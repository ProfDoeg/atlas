# Aby Warburg (1866-1929): research report

*Compiled for `aby_warburg.journey.json`. Queue entry: QUEUE.md line 358, "art historian of the Mnemosyne Atlas... the 1896 journey to the Hopi serpent ritual; the library that migrated whole from Hamburg to London."*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference, stated as such. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is printed and left standing rather than adjudicated.

Second verification pass. The first built the journey from the two Wikipedias, Gombrich, the German institutional pages and Kevin Dann's *Public Domain Review* essay with its WIA shelfmarks. This one added what the first lacked: **the primary text.** Warburg's Kreuzlingen lecture was read in the Cornell edition (trans. Michael P. Steinberg, 1995) through the OAPEN scan, so every quotation drawn from it is now page-verified, several against wrong pages carried in the pool. That access changed six items below and retired three weak or second-hand quotations.

Journey shape: **8 segments, 45 stops**, 18 carrying a quotation. Every scholarly hedge lives in `date_confidence`, never in the campa prose.

## 1. Hamburg origins, 1866-1885

**13 June 1866**, born in Hamburg, eldest of seven children of the Warburg banking family, house of M.M. Warburg & Co. **[A: both Wikipedias; ZKM biography]** *Gap:* no reachable source gives the street address. The pin 53.5636, 9.9908 is district-level (Rothenbaum/Harvestehude) and says so. **[A]** as a date, **[R]** as a place.

**Bad Ischl, 1874.** In the year of his mother's severe illness the boy devours "Red Indian" stories from a lending library and gorges on non-kosher sausages; he later called the pair "my inoculation against active cruelty." **[A: Gombrich, 19-20, via Dann]** *Flagged:* the 1874 maternal illness is uncited in German Wikipedia, so the journey uses it to date the stay, never as a cause.

**c.1879, the birthright** ceded to Max for a standing promise of books. **[A as family tradition]** The pool is explicit that this is a widely repeated anecdote with **no contemporary document cited**, and `date_confidence` now says exactly that rather than the first pass's shorthand "no document."

*New this pass, and why the stop now carries a quote.* Max Warburg's verdict, "was certainly the most careless [business decision] of my life," is verbatim in **Emily J. Levine, 'A Hamburg Friendship: Erwin Panofsky's Obituary on Aby Warburg'**, keydocuments.net. **Misattribution corrected:** the pool credited this, and the Alexandria dictum in section 5, to *Panofsky's obituary in translation*. Levine's piece is a **commentary about** the obituary; the obituary itself ran in the *Hamburger Fremdenblatt*, 28 October 1929, and was not consulted. Corroborated in Steinberg's afterword, Cornell ed., 76 and note 28 at 111 ("a very large blank cheque").

*Dropped:* the 1885 Abitur stop at the Realschule des Johanneums, cut to hold the file at 45 stops. It carried no event.

## 2. Student years, 1886-1893

**Bonn, 1886**, under Carl Justi and Henry Thode, and decisively **Hermann Usener**, historian of religion, whose comparative method he keeps for life; **Karl Lamprecht** teaches there in the same years. **[A: German Wikipedia; ZKM]** Pin 50.7339, 7.1022 inherited byte-identical.

**Munich, 1886-87 [R].** A term between universities; the pool itself tags it **[R]**, duration and content thinly documented. No stop.

**Florence, 1888-89: the most substantive correction, restated and sharpened.** The chronology lens had Warburg "studying at the Kunsthistorisches Institut in Florence" in 1888. **Anachronism:** that institute was founded only in **1897**, under Heinrich Brockhaus. What Warburg attended in the winter semester of 1888/89 was **August Schmarsow's private seminar in Schmarsow's own Florence apartments**, **nine students**, among them **Max J. Friedländer and Hermann Ulmann**. Schmarsow retrospectively called it the founding act of the KHI; no institute existed. **[A: khi.fi.it; de.wikipedia 'Kunsthistorisches Institut in Florenz'; Uffizi.it]** The campa prints the 1897 date so the correction cannot be silently re-lost. The first pass named Max Semrau on German Wikipedia's authority; the file now follows the institution's own history.

**Strasbourg, 1892.** Dissertation on the *Birth of Venus* and *Primavera* under **Hubert Janitschek**, defended 1892, printed 1893. **[A]**

**Berlin, 1892-93. Corrected.** The pool's geography lens dated the psychology lectures "c. 1889-1890," contradicting its own chronology lens. Both Wikipedias place the **two semesters after the doctorate**; in 1889-90 he was still between Florence and Strasbourg on the Botticelli work. `date_confidence` now reads "after the doctorate and not before it."

## 3. The American journey, 1895-1896

**Autumn 1895**, New York, for **Paul Warburg's marriage to Nina Loeb** **[A]**; then **Washington** and the Smithsonian ethnologists **James Mooney**, **Frank Hamilton Cushing**, **Jesse Walter Fewkes** and **Franz Boas** **[A: Freedberg; Gombrich]**. *Flagged:* the pool puts the Smithsonian weeks in **December 1895**, the first pass inferred November. The stop keeps November and names December as the rival reading. Neither is documented to a day; no source names the wedding venue.

**Santa Fe: [R] upgraded to [A], and a retraction.** The first pass treated Santa Fe as an inference, noting no source named it. Wrong, and the pool retracts it. Warburg's own lecture text records that on **10 January 1896, in room 59 of the Palace Hotel**, a kiva priest of Cochiti named **Cleo Jurino** and his son **Anacleto** drew the serpent-and-worldhouse cosmology for him. It is among the best-evidenced stops of the journey. **[A: Warburg, *Images from the Region of the Pueblo Indians*]** The stop is renamed for the event and dated to the day.

**Consequence: the Cochiti stop was removed.** The first pass had "Cochiti Pueblo, the corn dances," and the pool's geography lens located the Jurino drawings *at* Cochiti. **Both are wrong about the drawings:** the men were *from* Cochiti and drew *in Santa Fe*. He did pass through Cochiti, so the visit is not fabricated, but with its one firm event relocated the stop held nothing but generic pueblo colour.

**Added: Acoma and Zuni.** English Wikipedia states that "he stood on the plazas of Oraibi, Acoma, Zuni, Laguna." **[A]** Acoma is pinned at the corrected **34.8964, -107.5819**; the pool's claim gave 34.9903, right longitude and a latitude some 10 km north of the mesa. The mesa is **367 feet** high, not the pool's "three hundred and fifty." **Laguna** is documented only as a waypoint and was deliberately given no stop.

**Coordinates.** Cliff Palace **37.1667, -108.4703** (Wikidata/NPS; the first pass had -108.4728). Old Oraibi held at **35.8758, -110.6403** (English Wikipedia); the pool's lenses offered 35.8925/-110.6289 and 35.8794/-110.6393, and the more accurate figure already in the file was kept rather than reconciled downward. Walpi held at **35.8322, -110.3978**, the interlock lens's pin.

**The snake dance: the contradiction now resolves in one direction, and the file says so.** The first pass hedged. The pool is firmer, and this is the most consequential change of the pass. **Warburg never saw the Hopi snake dance.** It is danced in **August**; he was there in **spring**. He **acquired photographs of the Walpi snake dance taken by other men.** This is the hinge of Freedberg's 'Pathos at Oraibi'. **[A: Freedberg, Columbia]** The Walpi campa states it flatly. What he *did* watch, at Oraibi between **28 April and 1 May 1896**, was a **Hemis kachina dance**, with **H. R. Voth** as Mennonite intermediary. **[A: Freedberg; engramma.it]**

*Kept as an honest asymmetry:* the popular Warburg is built almost entirely out of a ceremony he did not attend. The Oraibi campa names Freedberg's charge without softening it.

**Mesa Verde [A as occurrence, R as duration]:** listed by Wikipedia, with him in Colorado in winter 1895-96; the length of the stop is disputed and the campa says so. **Voth, unresolved:** his ethnography was gathered in the course of a mission to end what it recorded, and no source shows this troubling Warburg at the time. Stated, not judged.

## 4. Florence again, 1897-1902

**1897**, marriage to **Mary Hertz**, painter and sculptor, against his father's wishes because she was not Jewish **[A]**. *Gap:* date and venue unverified beyond secondary biography.

**Florence 1898-1902**, Marietta born there 1899 **[A]**. **New stop: the Sassetti Chapel at Santa Trinita**, where Ghirlandaio painted the Piazza della Signoria and living Florentines into sacred narrative for the banker Francesco Sassetti **[A: en.wikipedia 'Sassetti Chapel']**. The **Tornabuoni choir at Santa Maria Novella** is the same argument in a second building and sits in `suggested_refs`. The attested **1899 Leonardo lecture at the Kunsthalle** was again left out for length and is recorded here so it is not lost.

**Return to Hamburg: a real contradiction, printed not resolved.** Wikipedia gives **1902**; warburg-haus.de gives **1904**. The pool notes the internal evidence, Max Adolph born in Florence 1902 and Frede Charlotte in Hamburg 1904, fits **1904** better. The first pass asserted 1902 flatly, which was too confident. The stop keeps 1902 as its pin date while `date_confidence` carries the conflict and names 1904 as the better-fitting reading: the one place where date and hedge deliberately disagree.

**Self-description: word order corrected.** The first pass printed "Amburghese di cuore, ebreo di sangue, d'anima Fiorentino." The attested order is **"Ebreo di sangue, Amburghese di cuore, d'anima Fiorentino"**, blood first, per **Gertrud Bing, *Rivista storica italiana* (1960), 113**, which engramma no. 2662 quotes and takes as its title. Source upgraded from Wikipedia to Bing.

**"Der liebe Gott steckt im Detail": dropped.** Carried at **[R]** with a long disclaimer (uncited in German Wikipedia, traceable to no primary Warburg text, also attributed to Flaubert and Mies van der Rohe). With page-verified Warburg available, an attribution that weak has no business in a quote field. The Archivio di Stato stop keeps its campa and loses its quote.

## 5. The library and the astrological turn, 1908-1918

**1908**, the Stephan Arndes calendar lecture and the astrological turn **[A]**. **1909**, the collection moves to **Heilwigstrasse 114** on the "good neighbour" principle **[A]**. **1912**, he declines a chair at Halle; the Hamburg Senate confers a **professor's title** instead **[A]**; in October he reads the Schifanoia paper at the **Tenth International Congress of the History of Art**, Rome **[A]**. **1914-1918**, the war-news archive **[A: Gombrich]**, start month undocumented.

**The 1919 professorship: corrected, and stated more strongly than in the first pass.** Both lenses framed 1919 as the year Warburg "finally accepts a professorship." He did not. **The title was conferred in 1912**, seven years before the University of Hamburg existed; 1919 is when his standing was folded into the new university. **[A: warburg-haus.de]** The word "honorary," used by the first pass, is dropped as unsupported by the German sources. **Panofsky arrives in 1920**, not 1919, and is not named in a 1919 circle **[A]**.

**Quotes here.** The Alexandria dictum, "Athens wants again and again to be reconquered afresh from Alexandria," sits at the astrology stop. **Provenance corrected:** it is **Warburg's own**, from the **Luther essay** ("Athen will eben immer wieder neu aus Alexandrien zurückerobert sein"), quoted in this wording by Levine and rendered by Steinberg (Cornell ed., 69, 88) as "Athens always wants precisely to be reconquered anew from Alexandria." Both renderings sit in `quote_source`. The same essay supplies the Schifanoia stop's line on the pagan augur, trans. David Britt (Getty, 1999), reached through HASTA and labelled second-hand in the citation itself.

## 6. Breakdown and Kreuzlingen, 1918-1924

**November 1918.** Severe psychosis amid the revolution; he threatens his family with a weapon. The werewolf hallucinations begin this year. **[A: Embden, in *La guérison infinie*, 88]**

**1919-1921.** Private clinics in and around Hamburg fail. *Gap, stated in the campa itself:* **their names are in no reachable source.**

**16 April 1921**, admitted to Sanatorium Bellevue, Kreuzlingen, under **Ludwig Binswanger**, **with a diagnosis of schizophrenia** **[A: American Journal of Psychiatry, 'Aby Warburg, 1866-1929']**. The admitting diagnosis is new this pass and sits in both campa and `date_confidence`, because it is what makes the 1923 revision matter.

**Kraepelin: corrected twice over.** The pool's geography lens attached **Emil Kraepelin** to the 1921 admission and had him merely "favouring the more hopeful diagnosis." Both wrong. He was brought in **only in 1923**, and **overturned the schizophrenia diagnosis in favour of a mixed manic-depressive state with a good prognosis** **[A: AJP]**. `date_confidence` names the 1921 misdating as the error it corrects.

**21 April 1923, the lecture**, delivered as **"Bilder aus dem Gebiet der Pueblo-Indianer in Nord-Amerika."** "Schlangenritual" and "A Lecture on Serpent Ritual" are later editorial titles of the 1988 German book and the 1939 *Journal of the Warburg Institute* translation; they sit in `suggested_refs`.

**The "condition of release" story: downgraded to [R], the myth named.** The pool's afterlife lens asserted **[A]** that the lecture was a condition Binswanger set for judging him fit to leave. Date and audience are solid; that framing is not. **Warburg proposed the lecture himself, and was not discharged for sixteen months.** `date_confidence` says so. The campa keeps the truthful half, that he set himself an examination, and drops the ultimatum the first pass implied.

**Quote replaced.** Max Adolph Warburg's marginal "One can only say 'hurrah'" (WIA GC/36870) gives way to Warburg's own page-verified sentence, **"The masked dance is danced causality"** (Cornell ed., 48).

**9 November 1923.** "Ludendorff should be kept in a lunatic asylum and Hitler should be hanged!", **WIA GC/37162**, written to Mary on the afternoon of the Beer Hall Putsch **[A: Dann, citing the shelfmark]**. Carried over unchanged and not re-verified this session.

**August 1924**, released after three years and four months, with **no record of a later relapse** **[A: ResearchGate, 'Aby Warburg, the Hopi Serpent Ritual and Ludwig Binswanger']**. *Quote dropped:* "in my fear, I may have hidden it" (WIA GC/36836, a mislaid Fortuna medallion, March 1923) sat oddly on a stop about discharge.

## 7. Mnemosyne and the last years, 1924-1929

**When does the Atlas begin? Contradiction printed, not resolved.** German sources date the work to **August 1924**, English sources to **December 1927**. Both get a stop, and December 1927 is read as the start of the **systematic panel campaign** rather than of the atlas, with `date_confidence` saying so.

**Panel counts: corrected.** The pool's chronology lens said the atlas was left "unfinished at roughly 40 panels," an outdated Wikipedia figure that contradicts the pool's own afterlife lens. The **final 1929 version comprises 63 panels with 971 images**, photographed after his death, and **his last written ordering is dated 19 October 1929, seven days before he died** **[A: Warburg Institute, 'Bilderatlas Mnemosyne, final version'; ZKM]**. The panel-campaign campa prints the divergent counts and explains the drift; the death stop now names 63 panels and the seven days.

**1 May 1926**, the purpose-built library at **Heilwigstrasse 116** opens, **with an inaugural address by Ernst Cassirer** **[A: warburg-haus.de]**. **Architect attribution corrected:** **Langmaack designed it**; **Fritz Schumacher recommended him but was not a co-designer**, as the pool had it. The first pass's unsourced "with Warburg at his elbow" is gone.

**1928-29**, nine months in Italy with **Gertrud Bing**, the Vatican Library and Roman churches worked for panel material **[A]**; pin 41.9029, 12.4534 inherited byte-identical. *Dropped:* the first pass's **[R]** "Ferrara, Schifanoia once more" stop, which admitted in its own campa that no source records a 1929 return. With attested stops available to replace it, an openly undocumented stop was cut rather than kept as filler.

**26 October 1929**, death by heart attack, aged 63 **[A]**.

**Burial: corrected, and the pool contradicted itself.** The afterlife lens placed him in **Friedhof Ohlsdorf**, the municipal cemetery of ~1.4 million graves, at 53.637/10.043. Wrong institution: Hamburg's Jewish burials are in the separate **Jüdischer Friedhof Ilandkoppel** (founded 1883), as the pool's own geography lens says. The file uses **53.6144, 10.0394** **[A: de.wikipedia; Wikidata Q60185]**.

## 8. The library's flight, 1933-1944

- **January 1933 [A]**, Hamburg bars the staff from lecturing; Saxl concludes work in Germany is impossible. No stop.
- **12 December 1933 [A]**, the library sails: **nearly 60,000 books and 25,000 photographs**, with staff and furniture, negotiated by **Saxl with Lord Lee of Fareham and Samuel Courtauld**. *Corrected:* the chronology lens named only Courtauld, omitting **Lee, generally reckoned the more important of the two**; both are now named. *Flagged:* sources differ on **one ship, named as the Hermia**, versus **two freighters**. `date_confidence` prints both and the stop name no longer asserts a number, where the first pass called it "two freighters for London." *Dropped as unsupported:* the claim that ~2,000 WWI-related books were ceded to Hamburg authorities as a condition of departure; no source reached mentions it.
- **1934 [A]**, installed on the ground floor of **Thames House, Millbank**, fitted out by the architect **Godfrey Samuel**. The first pass had Viscount Lee renting a bank building; the campa now follows the pool.
- **1937-1958 [A]**, Imperial Institute Buildings, South Kensington. *Corrected:* the pool's "1937-1944" implies departure in 1944. **1944 is the year of incorporation into the University of London**; the Institute stayed in South Kensington until **1958**.
- **28 November 1944 [A]**, incorporated as the Warburg Institute. **1958 [A]**, Woburn Square.

## 9. Afterlife, recorded but not given stops

**1939**, the lecture in English in the *Journal of the Warburg Institute*; **1988**, first German publication as *Schlangenritual*; **1995**, the Cornell edition trans. Steinberg, read this pass. **[A]** **1970**, Gombrich's biography, the standard account for decades **[A]**. *Note corrected:* the pool called Gombrich "a former student of the circle." **Warburg died in 1929; Gombrich joined the Institute in 1936, in London**, working from the archive as staff.

**Mnemosyne in print: corrected.** The pool gave a 1993 German edition followed by a 2000 facsimile. **No 1993 German edition of the panels surfaced in any source checked**, and the order is inverted: the German publication is **Gesammelte Schriften II.1, 'Der Bilderatlas MNEMOSYNE', ed. Martin Warnke with Claudia Brink, Akademie Verlag, Berlin, 2000** (study editions 2008, 2012). The 2020 Hatje Cantz / HKW *Bilderatlas Mnemosyne: The Original*, ed. Ohrt and Heil, is correct as given.

**Exhibitions.** Didi-Huberman's *Atlas: How to Carry the World on One's Back?* opened at the **Reina Sofía on 26 November 2010**, closed 28 March 2011, then travelled to **ZKM Karlsruhe (7 May - 7 August 2011)** and Sammlung Falckenberg. The pool dated it 2011; it is **2010-2011**. The pool's ZKM coordinates (49.0089, 8.4149) miss the Hallenbau by ~2.4 km, which is at **49.0014, 8.3836**. Neither is used: the journey has no exhibition stops. **1993/1995**, Hamburg reacquires and reopens the building as the **Warburg-Haus** **[A]**.

## 10. Interlocks: named, and refused

Naming another traveller in a campa requires a **direct or indirect personal relation**. Shared geography is not one, and the map already shows co-location. The lens returned fourteen candidates; **two** are named.

**`george_balanchine`**, at the Hamburg harbour stop, 12 December 1933. Ground: **family**. **Edward (Eddie) Warburg**, of the same banking dynasty, met Balanchine at the New York pier on **18 October 1933** and bankrolled the School of American Ballet **[A: george_balanchine.journey.json]**. The exact degree of kinship is **[R]** and unstated; the campa says only "kinsman."

**`freud`**, at the Kraepelin stop, 1923. Ground: **his own treating physician's correspondence**. Binswanger, who ran Bellevue and treated Warburg 1921-1924, is Freud's attested correspondent (letter of **15 October 1926**, quoted in the Freud journey at line 547). The chain runs through the doctor actually treating him.

**Refused, with reasons.** **`jung`** is the strongest temptation and the clearest refusal: Jung sat with Ochwiay Biano at Taos Pueblo in **January 1925**, drawing a comparative primitive/modern-mind argument out of Southwest ritual two years after Warburg's lecture did the same from Hopi material. The lens tags it **[A]**, but what it attests is a **parallel**, not a relation, with no meeting, correspondence or citation either way. **`frank_lloyd_wright`** (Arizona decades later, tagged **[R]** by the lens itself), **`sitting_bull`** (the salvage-ethnography project and the frontier campaigns as two faces of one closing, which is history rather than acquaintance), **`alberto_gerchunoff`** (a Hamburg port his family may or may not have sailed from, the record unresolved between Hamburg and Bremen), **`abraham`**, **`johannes_trithemius`**, **`virgil`** (survival-under-disguise as a theme; the Vatican Library as a shared address across five centuries): all refused as shared geography or thematic rhyme. The **Kaaba pin 21.4225, 39.8262** the lens offered belongs to the Abraham file and was not inherited.

**Queue note, not an interlock.** QUEUE.md 566-567 hold **Max** and **Paul Warburg**, both unresearched; they are named here as brothers, which is biography. `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` 309, 312 separately lists **Eddie** and **Felix Warburg**.

**Pins inherited byte-identical:** Bonn 50.7339/7.1022, Florence 43.7696/11.2558, New York 40.7128/-74.006, Washington 38.8891/-77.0269, Walpi 35.8322/-110.3978, Kreuzlingen 47.6486/9.1758, Vatican Library 41.9029/12.4534, London 51.4959/-0.1257, Hamburg 53.5511/9.9937.
