# Simon Magus, research report

*Slug `simon_magus`. Compiled 2026-08-30 from the verified research pool. **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, legend, or modern hypothesis.*

**The governing problem, stated once.** Simon Magus has one secure appearance in antiquity: fifteen verses of Acts 8. He is not dated, not described, not given a family, not given an exit. Everything else, the Roman statue, the flight, the fall, the philosophy, the woman, the succession of heretics, was written eighty to three hundred years later by people who wanted him to be the origin of something. The file keeps that asymmetry visible: seven stops carry Acts, thirty-seven carry what was built on it, each labelled in its own `date_confidence`.

---

## 1. Origin: the village nobody has found

- **[R]** Justin Martyr, *First Apology* 26 (c. 150-155), calls him "a Samaritan, of the village called Gitto," the only early statement of a birthplace. Justin was himself a Samaritan of Flavia Neapolis (Nablus), a few miles from the Sebaste of Acts 8, which is the likeliest reason he alone supplies the village. It makes him better placed, not more disinterested.
- **[R]** Gitta's site has never been identified. The file uses the Nablus-area landmark 32.2211, 35.2544 and says so: a region, not a village.
- **[R]** The parents Antonius and Rachel appear only in the Pseudo-Clementine *Recognitions* 2.7 and *Homilies* 2.22, fourth-century in their extant forms. **[A]** by contrast: Acts, Justin, and Irenaeus name no relative at all. Three centuries of gap.
- **Honest gap.** No source gives a birth year, a death year, or an age. The Catholic Encyclopedia's "about A.D. 37" **[R]** comes from the narrative sequence of Acts, not a datable event, and is used only as an ordering device.

## 2. Samaria: the only secure episode

All of this section is **[A]**, from Acts 8:4-24 (NRSVUE, via biblegateway.com).

- Simon has practiced magic in "the city of Samaria" a long time, amazing the population, saying that he is somebody great (8:9); the crowd, "from the least of them to the greatest," calls him "the power of God that is called Great" (8:10-11).
- Philip preaches; Simon believes, is baptized among the crowd, and then stays continually with him, amazed at the signs (8:12-13).
- Peter and John are sent down from Jerusalem, lay hands on the baptized, and the Spirit is given (8:14-17). Simon, seeing that it comes through the laying on of hands, offers money for the same power (8:18-19).
- Peter's rebuke, the gall of bitterness and the chains of wickedness, the order to repent (8:20-23). Simon asks Peter to pray for him (8:24). **The narrative then stops.** No death, no expulsion, no departure, no verdict.

**Contradiction with everything downstream, flagged not resolved.** The Simon of Acts is a baptized believer under discipline who asks for intercession. The Simon of Irenaeus and the *Acts of Peter* is an unrepentant rival claiming to be God. Irenaeus **[A]** supplies the bridge, saying that after the rebuke Simon "set himself eagerly to contend against the apostles" (1.23). Connective tissue, not a report.

**Geography note.** Acts names only "the city of Samaria"; the Sebaste identification is conventional, tagged **[R]**. Coordinate 32.2767, 35.1958, byte-identical to the pin in `apostle_peter.journey.json`; the pool's 32.2769, 35.1928 was corrected by its verifier.

## 3. Helena

- **[A]** Justin 26 and Eusebius quoting him (2.13): a Helena travelled with Simon, formerly a prostitute at Tyre, called by his followers "the first idea that proceeded from him."
- **[A]** Irenaeus 1.23: Simon "redeemed from slavery at Tyre, a city of Phoenicia, a certain woman named Helena"; she is his Ennoia, "the first conception of his mind, the mother of all," mother of the world-making angels, held down in successive bodies, one "that Helen on whose account the Trojan War was undertaken; for whose sake also Stesichorus was struck blind."
- **[R]** That any historical woman stands behind the doctrine. No independent attestation of Helena exists; everything is mediated by opponents. Tyre coordinate 33.2704, 35.1937, byte-identical from `apostle_paul.journey.json`; the pool's 33.2038 longitude was corrected by its verifier.

## 4. The Pseudo-Clementine biography

All **[R]**, and the label matters more than usual: the *Recognitions* and *Homilies* are fourth-century romances organized around set-piece debates, on an earlier "basic writing."

- Greek education, then Egypt to study magic; later tradition specifies Alexandria. **Not confirmed against the primary text**, and marked so in the file.
- The circle of thirty around John the Baptist, John's death during Simon's absence, Dositheus taking the leadership, Simon accepting second rank then displacing him. Helena is one of the thirty.
- The pursuit itinerary: Caesarea (*Hom.* 1-3), Tyre, Sidon, Berytus, **Byblus**, Tripolis (*Hom.* 4-11), Laodicea (*Hom.* 12-20), Antioch.

**Correction carried into the file.** The pool omitted Byblus; its verifier caught this against *Homilies* 7. Byblus is routinely dropped from summaries; the file restores it, at 34.1232, 35.6512. **Pins inherited here:** Caesarea 32.5, 34.8913 and Antioch 36.2047, 36.1817, byte-identical to `apostle_paul` and `apostle_peter`; Sidon 33.5638, 35.3688. The pool's Antioch coordinate is not used.

## 5. Rome

- **[A]** Justin 26, addressed to Antoninus Pius: Simon came to Rome under Claudius (r. 41-54) and "did mighty acts of magic, by virtue of the art of the devils operating in him." Same chapter: a statue was erected "on the river Tiber, between the two bridges," inscribed *Simoni Deo Sancto*, and almost all Samaritans plus a few others worship him as the first God.
  - **Correction, flagged.** Justin does **not** say "Tiber Island"; that is the modern inference from the two bridges being the Pons Fabricius and the Pons Cestius. Two of the pool's lenses mis-stated it as his own wording; the file marks the inference as inference.
- **[A]** Eusebius, *Church History* 2.13, quotes Justin in full, the channel through which most of the Middle Ages receives it.
- **[A]** A base inscribed SEMONI SANCO DEO FIDIO SACRVM, to the Sabine deity Semo Sancus Dius Fidius, was excavated on Tiber Island in July 1574 near the temple of Iupiter Iurarius; CIL VI.567, now in the Vatican's Galleria Lapidaria (Wikipedia "Semo Sancus"; Platner/Ashby).
  - Misreading is the standard explanation of Justin's claim. **It removes the monument as evidence of a Roman Simon cult. It does not by itself disprove a Roman visit**, and the file says both.
- **[R], the largest negative finding:** no Roman historian, inscription, rescript, or trial record corroborates any presence of Simon in Rome under Claudius or Nero. Everything traces to Christian apologetic and apocryphal writing of decades or centuries later.

## 6. The doctrine

- **[A] as report, [R] as Simon's own teaching.** Irenaeus 1.23: Simon taught that he appeared among the Jews as the Son, descended in Samaria as the Father, and came to other nations as the Holy Spirit.
- **[R]** The *Refutation of All Heresies* 6.9-18, ascribed to Hippolytus, quotes the *Apophasis Megale*, the Great Declaration: a Boundless Power likened to fire with a hidden and a manifest part; six roots in three male-female pairs (Mind and Thought, Voice and Name, Reason and Reflection); the Standing One, who "stood, stands, and will stand."
  - Modern scholarship treats this as a second-century product of the school, not an autograph. The file says so in the confidence field, not the prose.
- **[R]** M. David Litwa (2024) treats the *Declaration of Great Power* and *The Concept of Our Great Power* (Nag Hammadi VI,4) as early second-century Simonian compositions, likely Alexandrian, distinct from anything the historical Simon wrote. **Monograph not consulted.**
- **[A]** Menander, a Samaritan of Capparetaea, taught at Antioch that his baptized followers would not die. Justin is the earliest source and had met elderly followers; Irenaeus makes him the successor and starts the chain. The chain is a construction; Menander's existence is not.
- **[A]** Epiphanius, *Panarion* (c. 375-377): the Simonians are by then "almost extinct." **[R]** Origen a century earlier reportedly said their number was very small; **not refetched**, flagged in the file.

## 7. The deaths, which do not reconcile

Four incompatible endings survive. The file gives each a stop and adjudicates none of them.

1. **[A]** *Acts of Peter* (Greek c. 180-190, probably Asia Minor; sole substantial witness the Latin Vercelli manuscript): Simon rises over Rome and falls on the Via Sacra when Peter prays that he be disabled rather than killed. "And he fell from the height and brake his leg in three places. Then every man cast stones at him and went away home." He is carried to Aricia, then Terracina, to one Castor, where two physicians cut him and he dies.
2. **[R]** *Refutation* VI: Simon, having lost his following, sits under a plane tree and has his disciples bury him alive, promising to rise on the third day. "He remained in that grave until this day, for he was not the Christ."
   - **Correction, flagged.** The pool's first pass placed this "returning toward Gitta." The place is **not in the text**: the ANF translation carries a manuscript lacuna at exactly that word, "This man, ultimately repairing to ... (and) sitting under a plane tree," and the preceding narrative has Simon at Rome. "Gitta" is the Catholic Encyclopedia's editorial supplement. The file pins the stop at Rome and states the lacuna.
3. **[R]** *Acts of Peter and Paul*, later and distinct: the flight is staged before Nero from a wooden tower; Peter commands the sustaining spirits to release him; the body falls in four parts; Nero keeps the corpse three days to test the promised resurrection. **The pool's verifier could not confirm this against any primary or reliably excerpted source.** Kept with that flag in its `date_confidence`, because dropping it would silently remove a branch of the tradition.
4. **[R]** *Golden Legend* (Jacobus de Voragine, 1260s): the tower is on the Capitol, before Nero, and Simon dies of a broken neck. Site and injury both differ from the *Acts of Peter*. **Chapter text not retrieved**; work description from Wikipedia.

And **[A]**: Acts 8 reports no death at all. The oldest source is the one that says least.

**Related note.** The *Acts of Peter* is also the earliest source for Peter's inverted crucifixion. One literary object, two famous scenes, the same footing.

**[R]** David L. Eastman argues the apocryphal Simon is a composite of the Simon of Acts 8 and Elymas of Acts 13. **Original publication not consulted.** Paphos belongs to Paul and Elymas, already on `apostle_paul.journey.json`; the argument is cited in sources, never staged.

## 8. Josephus

- **[A]** Josephus, *Jewish Antiquities* 20.142: a Cypriot Jewish magician in the service of the procurator Felix persuades Drusilla to leave her husband Azizus and marry Felix, in the 50s.
- **Textual contradiction, flagged.** The critical Greek text (Niese) names him **Atomos**; the Latin version and the Epitome read **Simon**, which is what Whiston prints. The pool's geography lens said flatly that Josephus "names" him Simon, contradicting its own chronology lens. The file states both readings.
- **[R]** The identification with Simon Magus (Waitz and others) is a disputed minority hypothesis; the ethnicity does not match. The file gives it one stop and says the case never carried.

## 9. The name outlives the man

- **[A]** *Simony*, the buying or selling of ecclesiastical office, sacraments, or spiritual things, is formed from Acts 8. Gregory I (590-604) popularized "simoniac heresy"; conciliar prohibitions go back to Elvira (c. 305) and Nicaea (325).
- **[A]** Dante, *Inferno* XIX, opens the third bolgia of the eighth circle by addressing Simon by name.
  - **Correction, flagged.** The pool's quotation ("O forlorn disciples... brides of goodness... rapaciously for gold / And silver do adulterate") **matches no standard translation the verifier could locate**. The file quotes the verified Longfellow opening instead (Gutenberg #1001).
- **[R]** Medieval Irish tradition brings Simon into the orbit of the druid Mog Ruith, helped by Latin *magus* covering both magician and druid.
  - **Correction, flagged, direction reversed from the pool's first pass.** English Wikipedia says Simon "is said to have come to the aid of the Druid Mog Ruith," i.e. Simon assists him; the commonly retold version makes Mog Ruith his pupil, building his flying machine. The file states both.
  - **Coordinate warning.** The cited article names no place. Valentia Island, 51.9264, -10.3333, comes from **Mog Ruith's own tradition** and is unattested here; the file keeps the stop and labels the coordinate borrowed. Nothing claims Simon travelled to Ireland.
- **[A]** Relief of Simon Magus at the gate of Saint-Sernin, Toulouse; confirmed twice, by the "Simon Magus" image caption and the basilica's sculpture list ("Simon the magician, the demons and the birth of the vine").
- **[A]** Filippino Lippi, *Disputation with Simon Magus* paired with the *Crucifixion of St Peter*, Brancacci Chapel, Florence, completing Masaccio's cycle, c. 1482-1485. The pool's geography lens gave 1481-1482; its afterlife lens was right and is followed.
- **[A]** Pompeo Batoni designed a *Fall of Simon Magus* in 1746 for a St Peter's altarpiece to be worked in mosaic because of humidity damage; abandoned. A studio version, c. 1745-1750, is Cleveland Museum of Art 1983.217. The file pins the stop at the Vatican, where the commission was, not at the museum.
- **Coordinates corrected against the pool:** Saint-Sernin to the corpus pin 43.6084, 1.442 (the pool's landed near the Place du Capitole); Brancacci to 43.7686, 11.2445; Cleveland to 41.5085, -81.612 (the pool's was ~580 m off, on the Case Western campus).
- **[A]** Nag Hammadi Codex VI, dug up in Upper Egypt in December 1945, contains *The Concept of Our Great Power*, under no author's name.
- **[R]** G. R. S. Mead, *Simon Magus: An Essay on the Founder of Simonianism* (London, 1892), the first modern rehabilitation, from a Theosophical standpoint. gnosis.org **failed with a certificate error**; bibliographic data only.

### Dropped, and why

- **Santa Francesca Romana, Rome** (basalt knee-prints in the south transept). The tradition gives the knees to **Peter** alone: under the standing rule that is Peter's object, not Simon's stop, so it is carried as a `suggested_ref` on the Via Sacra fall stop. Correct coordinate for later use: 41.8912, 12.4889 (the pool gave two wrong ones).
- **Guido di Graziano, Siena, 1280s** and **Solimena, San Paolo Maggiore, Naples, 1689-90.** Both **[R]**, both unrefetched, both cut for stop budget rather than doubt. Recorded so the operator can add them if the attributions firm up.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Simon Magus is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. These relations surfaced in the interlock lens, are **not rooted in Simon's own life**, and all run **forward**, which the exceptions file does not license by default and no agent may grant. None was silently deleted; none was written into a campa.

- **Theodor Reuss, Lyon, 1919** (`theodor_reuss.journey.json`): the Ecclesia Gnostica Catholica given apostolic descent through a Syrian see. The Simon link is the corpus's own Gnostic framing; Reuss's file never names him.
- **René Guénon, Paris, 1908** (`rene_guenon.journey.json`): Léonce Fabre des Essarts of the Église gnostique, same neo-Gnostic revival. Guénon's file never names Simon.
- **C. G. Jung, Küsnacht, 1914** (`jung.journey.json`, Philemon, "pagan and Gnostic"): thematic only, a self-authorizing visionary authority of the kind heresiologists accused Simon of being.
- **Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn, Eranos 1938** (`olga_frobe_kapteyn.journey.json`): the lecture "Die gnostische Vorstellung der Sophia." That Sophia is the Valentinian figure, not Irenaeus's Helena-Ennoia.
- **Philip K. Dick** (`philip_k_dick.journey.json`), VALIS in Gnostic dress, and **Albert Pike** (`albert_pike.journey.json`), *Morals and Dogma* absorbing "the Gnostics" as a category. Both thematic, neither naming Simon.

To use any of it Simon would need a listing in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and it would be a **forward grant**, in the class of the Burroughs and Derrida entries, not the ordinary backward exception. Separately, not an apparatus case: **Ioan Petru Culianu**, unresearched in `QUEUE.md` (line 118), is the likeliest future file to interlock with this one directly.

**The standing negative.** No Roman historian, no inscription, no rescript, no trial record.
