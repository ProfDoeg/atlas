# Burnt Njal (Njall Thorgeirsson of Bergthorshvoll): research report

*2026-08-06. Subject slug: `burnt_njal`. Iceland, c. 935 to c. 1011 by the saga's own reckoning; the text that records him was written between 1270 and 1290.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, saga-internal tradition, or scholarly convention · *flagged* = a contradiction left standing, not adjudicated.

**What "attested" can mean here.** Njal exists in one substantial source: Brennu-Njals saga, composed roughly two hundred and sixty years after his death. Nothing in his life is documented in the sense the word carries for a charter or an annal. Here **[A]** means "the saga or a named modern authority says this, verbatim and checkably"; **[R]** means "the tradition assumes it, or a modern editor inferred it". The only external anchor is Clontarf, 23 April 1014. Everything Icelandic hangs off that pin and off the conversion of 999/1000. The journey file's register is a stance, not an evidentiary claim: the campa narrate the saga as Iceland narrates it, and every hedge sits in the `date_confidence` field of its stop.

---

## 1. Origins and family

**[R]** Njal is conventionally born in the mid-tenth century. No birth year exists anywhere. The c. 935 in the journey file is back-calculated from the burning and from the saga's references to him as an old man.

**[A, Wikipedia 'Njall Thorgeirsson']** His paternal grandfather fell out of favour with the king of Norway and planned to flee. He did not get away: "the king's errandmen came to him and took his life." The widow and children emigrated afterward. The pool recorded this as his having "fled Norway and been killed there", inverting the sequence.

***Flagged 1.*** The patronymic does not agree. Wikipedia makes Njal the son of **Thorgeir gollnir Ofeigsson**, putting **Ofeigr** in the grandfather's chair; Dasent ch. 20 reads "the son of Thorgeir Gelling, the son of Thorolf". Neither is adopted; the journey names both.

**[A, Dasent chs. 20-21, 25]** His mother is Asgerda. He holds Bergthorshvoll and a second homestead at Thorolfsfell, which has no verified coordinate here and is omitted rather than invented. He marries Bergthora Skarphedinsdottir; six children by her (Skarphedinn, Grim, Helgi, three daughters), plus a base-born son Hoskuld by Hrodny.

## 2. Bergthorshvoll: the farm and the man

**[A, Wikipedia 'Bergthorshvoll'; is.wikipedia same]** The farm stands in Vestur-Landeyjar, Rangarvallasysla, on the west bank of the Affall, at 63 deg 38'10 N, 20 deg 19'48 W = **63.6361, -20.33**.

***Flagged 2, the largest factual error in the pool.*** The geography lens gave 63.7561, -20.2419 across four entries (farm, burning, Kari's escape, memorial). That is essentially **Hvolsvollur** (63.750, -20.233), in Hvolhreppur rather than the Landeyjar, 13.5 km away. The pool's afterlife lens gave the correct coordinate, so the lenses contradicted each other outright.

**[A, Dasent ch. 20]** The saga's introduction is quoted verbatim in the journey file: wealthy, handsome, no beard on his chin, "so great a lawyer, that his match was not to be found". **[A, chs. 35, 41]** The beardlessness is a weapon: Hallgerda calls him "the beardless carle", and Bergthora's counter is on the record, that Thorwald "was not beardless, and yet thou plottedst his death".

## 3. Gunnar of Hlidarendi

**[A, Wikipedia 'Hlidarendi']** Hlidarendi is at **63.72481, -19.95067**. ***Flagged:*** the pool's 63.6989, -19.9528 is 2.9 km too far south, off the Fljotshlid slope and in the floodplain; and its "the farm's turf ruins are visited today" is wrong, the site carrying a church and churchyard and remaining an occupied farm.

**[A, Dasent chs. 21-22]** Gunnar comes to Njal for counsel on recovering Unnur's dower from Hrut; Njal's device carries the case at the Althing. This begins the central friendship.

**[A, Dasent ch. 69]** The most quoted line attributed to Njal: "with law shall our land be built up and settled, and with lawlessness wasted and spoiled." The pool cited ch. 70; in Gutenberg #597 and sagadb.org it is **ch. 69**. **[A, ch. 73]** Njal's warning that if Gunnar breaks the atonement "thou wilt be slain here in the land" is **ch. 73**, not 74.

**[A, Wikipedia 'Gunnar Hamundarson']** Riding into exile, Gunnar's horse stumbles; he looks back, is "so moved by the beauty of it that he changed his mind", turns home, and is killed when his bowstring breaks and Hallgerdur refuses him her hair.

***Flagged 3.*** The pool dated Gunnar's death to 990 and the stumble to c. 992, which is impossible. Both are placed at c. 990 and the conflict recorded. The pool also called the ford "above Hlidarendi"; at 63.68 it is 5 km south and downslope, and Gunnar was riding toward the coast.

**[R]** The pool's "Grjota (river crossing, Rangarvellir)" is unusable: Grjota is in **Fljotshlid** and is Thrain Sigfusson's farm, and chs. 92-93 narrate Thrain's killing on the ice of the Markarfljot, not ford ambushes. Its "Ranga eystri, drowning ford" cites the same wrong chapters for a composite with no named ford and no named dead; dropped.

## 4. The conversion, and the years abroad

**[A, Wikipedia 'Njals saga'; Islendingabok ch. 7]** Iceland adopts Christianity by law at the Althing, the only specific year the saga supplies. Sources give 999 or 1000; the divergence is left standing. **[A, Wikipedia 'Kari Solmundarson']** Kari rescues Helgi and Grim Njalsson on a trading voyage in the British Isles, c. 995, and brings them into Jarl Sigurd's service in Orkney; **[R]** he is said to have become at least nominally Christian at the conversion.

**[A, Wikipedia 'Keldur']** Keldur is at **63.82194, -20.07389**; the pool's 63.7756, -20.0206 is 5.8 km off. Its claim that Keldur "is not named as a site of specific events in the saga text" is false: the first recorded occupant, Ingjald Hoskuldsson, is a character in Njals saga. The dwelling's foundation is dated to the eleventh century and a jardhus tunnel to the farm stream was rediscovered in a 1998 excavation.

**[A, Landnamabok]** Kirkjubaer on the Sida carries a church-name from the Irish papar tradition. **[R]** Its role in Njal's story is saga embroidery.

## 5. Hoskuld Hvitanessgodi

**[A, Dasent ch. 111]** Njal's foster-son Hoskuld Thrainsson, for whom Njal contrived a godord, is killed in his own field by Njal's sons after Mord Valgardsson goads them. He dies asking God's forgiveness for his killers.

***Flagged 4.*** His title is **Hoskuld Hvitanessgodi**, godi of **Hvitanes**; the pool's chronology lens rendered it "holder of the Hvita district's godord", its geography lens correctly.

***Flagged 5.*** The chronology lens dates the killing to **1006**, the geography lens to **c. 1011**. Convention puts it in the spring before the Althing session and the autumn burning of 1011; the journey adopts 1011 and records 1006 as a live alternative.

**[R]** Ossabaer lies in the Landeyjar, a morning's ride from Bergthorshvoll. The pool's 63.85, -20.4 is 24 km north, which breaks the saga's geography. An approximate **63.66, -20.30** is used, flagged as unlocated.

**[A, Wikipedia 'Njals saga']** Njal offers self-judgment and generous compensation at the Althing; negotiations collapse over a cloak laid on the silver and taken as an insult. ***Flagged 6:*** dated 1010 in one lens, implied 1011 in the other. The session must follow the spring killing, so 1011 is used.

**[A, is.wikipedia 'Thrihyrningur (fjall)']** Thrihyrningur is at **63.7864, -19.9739**, 678 m. The pool's 63.66, -20.04 is 14 km too far south, and it described the mountain as a refuge for "Gunnar's kinsmen" in chs. 63-64. Its actual role is chs. 127-130: **Thrihyrningshalsar is where Flosi and the burners assemble and hide**.

## 6. The burning

**[A, Wikipedia 'Njals saga']** About a hundred men under Flosi surround a house holding about thirty. Unable to force the doors against Skarphedinn, the attackers fire it.

**[A, Dasent ch. 128]** Njal asks whether Flosi will take atonement or let men out, and refuses safe passage: "I will not go out, for I am an old man, and little fitted to avenge my sons, but I will not live in shame." Bergthora refuses with him: "I was given away to Njal young, and I have promised him this, that we would both share the same fate." They lie down with Kari's son Thord between them under an ox-hide and give their souls into God's hand. All of this is **ch. 128**, not 129 as the pool had it throughout.

***Flagged 7.*** The pool's chronology lens says "Njal, Bergthora, and about eleven others die", totalling thirteen; Wikipedia 'Njals saga' says **eleven people die** in all, as the pool's own afterlife lens states correctly.

***Flagged 8.*** **Helgi Njalsson did not die in the fire.** He was spotted leaving in a woman's cloak and beheaded by Flosi outside; only Skarphedinn of the two dies in the burning.

**[A, Wikipedia 'Njals saga']** Kari "escapes under cover of the smoke by running along the beam"; the pool's "breaks through the burning hall's roof timbers" changes the mechanism. **[A, Dasent ch. 131]** The bodies are found unburnt under the hide and Hjallti pronounces: "Njal's body and visage seem to me so bright that I have never seen any dead man's body so bright as this." **Ch. 131**, not 132.

## 7. Kari's vengeance, and Clontarf

**[A, Dasent chs. 138-145]** The case against the burners is argued for days, breaks into open fighting on the assembly field, and is settled by arbitration, outlawries, and fines rather than by verdict. **[R]** Widely read as the saga's commentary on the limits of law.

**[A, Wikipedia 'Kari Solmundarson']** Kari hunts the burners across Iceland, Orkney, and Wales. He beheads Gunnar Lambason mid-boast in Orkney; **Wales is where he kills Kol Thorsteinsson, the last of them**. ***Flagged 9:*** the pool reduced Wales to "a waypoint rather than a scene" at "a nobleman's court", understating it to the point of error, and its 51.4816, -3.1791 (Cardiff) coordinate is a placeholder.

**[A, Wikipedia 'Birsay']** Birsay is **59.1333, -3.3017**; the pool's -3.3383 falls 2 km offshore. Its claim that "Earl Sigurd's seat at Birsay is historically documented" is unsupported by Wikipedia 'Birsay' or 'Sigurd the Stout', and the saga puts the Yule feast on Hrossey. Downgraded to "traditionally associated".

**[A, Wikipedia 'Sigurd the Stout'; Annals of Ulster s.a. 1014]** The raven banner "will bring victory to the man it's carried before, but death to the one who carries it"; Sigurd dies under it at Clontarf. ***Flagged 10:*** the pool dated the Orkney muster to "Good Friday, 23 April 1014", the battle's date rather than that of a muster weeks earlier. The muster stop is dropped, its coordinate 58.9811, -2.96 also sitting off Orkney Mainland with no named site.

**[A, Annals of Ulster; Cogadh Gaedhel re Gallaibh]** The battle is fought 23 April 1014 north of the Liffey; Brian Boru is killed after the rout, and Sigtrygg had promised the kingship of Ireland to both Sigurd and Brodir. **[R]** Kari's presence is the saga's claim alone; it places him in Caithness, where Darrad sees valkyries weave the day's slaughter.

## 8. Rome, and the end of the feud

**[A, Wikipedia 'Kari Solmundarson']** "Flosi returned to Iceland after a pilgrimage to Rome." Both men make the pilgrimage.

***Flagged 11.*** Dates disagree between lenses: pilgrimage 1017 against c. 1015-1016, reconciliation 1018 against c. 1017. No firm date survives; the journey carries one hedged date across both stops.

***Flagged 12, a clean reversal.*** The chronology lens has **Flosi** shipwrecked near **Kari's** home. It is the reverse: **Kari** is wrecked at Ingolfshofdi near **Flosi's** home at Svinafell, which is how the reconciliation comes about. That both men made the pilgrimage is the likely source of the confusion.

**[A, Dasent ch. 159]** Flosi receives the stranger in the blizzard before recognizing him, then honours the recognition. Kari marries Hildigunn, Flosi's niece and Hoskuld's widow.

## 9. Historicity

**[A, Wikipedia 'Njals saga']** Scholarship treats it as reasonably certain that Njal and Gunnar were real people. **[A, Wikipedia 'Njall Thorgeirsson', quoting Gudbrandur Vigfusson]** The pool softened the key sentence: "Although it is certain, that Njall has existed, yet the Njall, which Njals saga describes, has never lived." Its paraphrase, that the depiction "differs from the historical man behind it", weakens a claim that the saga's Njal is a literary construct. Restored, and quoted in the journey file.

**[A]** The saga in its present form was composed between **1270 and 1290** from reshaped oral tradition. **[R]** No specific years survive for Njal's birth, his marriage, or the killing of Hoskuld; only the conversion of 999/1000 and the Clontarf synchronism anchor anything.

## 10. Afterlife: manuscripts, print, translation, spade

**[A, Wikipedia 'Njals saga', citing Einar Olafur Sveinsson]** Roughly 60 manuscripts and fragments survive, 21 medieval. Oldest vellum witnesses: Graskinna (GKS 2870 4to, c. 1300), Reykjabok (AM 468 4to, c. 1300-1325), Kalfalaekjarbok (AM 133 fol., c. 1350), classified into a stemma in 1953 underlying Sveinsson's 1954 edition. **[A, Wikipedia 'Modruvallabok']** Modruvallabok, written c. 1330-1370, found at Modruvellir in Eyjafjordur, given to Thomas Bartholin in 1684, passed to Arni Magnusson in 1691, **returned to Iceland in 1974**.

**[A]** First printed edition: Copenhagen, 1772, edited by Olafur Olafsson, printed by Johann Rudolph Thiele, based primarily on Reykjabok. **[A, Wikipedia 'George Webbe Dasent']** Dasent's *The Story of Burnt Njal*, 1861, published by Edmonston and Douglas of **Edinburgh**; ***flagged 13,*** the pool placed it in England. Later translations: Bayerschmidt and Hollander 1955; Magnusson and Palsson 1960; Cook, Penguin, 1997/2001.

**[A, Wikipedia 'Bergthorshvoll']** Sigurdur Vigfusson excavated at the site in the late nineteenth century; Matthias Thordarson dug in 1927, 1928, and 1931. **[R, gap]** Whether either campaign found burnt structural remains matching the saga's fire, as against ordinary occupation-layer burning, is **not established in any source reached**; the article is a stub. The pool's geography lens claimed "archaeology confirms Norse-period farm occupation on this exact mound" while its afterlife lens conceded no findings are published. The stronger claim is not carried into the journey.

## 11. Interlocks with the atlas corpus

**[A, `william_morris.journey.json`]** Morris stands at the Law-Rock at Thingvellir in July 1871 and records that "here Njall spoke and Gunnar was outlawed". The strongest intersection in the corpus. Named in campa.

**[A, `borges.journey.json`]** Borges, given the Volsunga Saga at ten, studies Old Norse all his life and reaches Iceland in **1971**, returning twice more; his Geneva gravestone carries a Viking longship. Njal is not named in his file, so **[R]** for that link. Named in campa.

**[A, `columbus.journey.json`]** Columbus, passing Iceland in 1477, hears from old men in the harbours of lands their grandfathers traded with. **[R]**, thematic only, two centuries after the saga's composition and no textual link to Njal or this saga specifically. Originally named in campa at the composition stop; removed under the house-style tightening of 2026-08-14, considered and not used.

**[A, census lines 366-367; QUEUE.md lines 121, 154, 181]** Snorri Sturluson sits adjacent to Burnt Njal in the census; he postdates the setting by two centuries and predates the composition by a generation, so Reykholt is inherited as a pin with the connection stated as literary. Leif Erikson, queued alongside, is a near-exact contemporary; San Brendan is queued next and paired with Leif. Neither has a journey file, so neither is named in campa.

**Inherited byte-identical**: Thingvellir 64.2559 / -21.1295, Reykjavik 64.1466 / -21.9426, Reykholt 64.6667 / -21.2833, Iceland general 64.9631 / -19.0208. **Deliberately not inherited**: the Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), the Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354), Paris (48.8566, 2.3522), all three flagged by the interlock lens itself as having no factual bridge to Njal. ## 12. Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. Grandfather and patronymic: Ofeigr (Wikipedia) against Thorgeir Gelling son of Thorolf (Dasent ch. 20).
2. Bergthorshvoll: 63.7561 / -20.2419 against 63.6361 / -20.33, between two lenses of one pool. The first is Hvolsvollur.
3. Gunnar's death 990 against the ford stumble "c. 992", reversing cause and effect.
4. Killing of Hoskuld: 1006 against c. 1011. 5. Collapse of the settlement at the Althing: 1010 against 1011. 6. Conversion of Iceland: 999 (saga) against 1000 (mainstream dating).
7. Death toll: eleven in all, against Njal and Bergthora "and about eleven others".
8. Helgi Njalsson: dead in the fire, against beheaded outside it by Flosi.
9. Direction of the shipwreck producing the reconciliation: Flosi near Kari against Kari near Flosi.
10. Rome pilgrimage 1017 against c. 1015-1016; reconciliation 1018 against c. 1017.
11. The exact site of the Logberg is unknown: Wikipedia 'Logberg' names Hallurinn, Almannagja, and Hestagja as candidates. The pool's "fixed by later tradition and geology" is too strong.
12. Chapter numbering in the pool's quote lens runs one or two high (70 for 69, 74 for 73, 129 for 128, 132 for 131), re-checked against Gutenberg #597 and sagadb.org.

## 13. Gaps, stated as gaps

- **No birth year for Njal exists.** The c. 935 is arithmetic, not evidence.
- **No independent attestation of Njal's death.** The pool asserts the deaths of Njal and Gunnar "are referenced in sources beyond the saga itself" but does not itemize them. Repeated as a claim, not adopted.
- **The Bergthorshvoll excavation results are unpublished** so far as any reachable source shows: two campaigns, four seasons, no findings described.
- **The Icelandic Saga Centre at Hvolsvollur could not be confirmed.** Its website failed DNS resolution: `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND sagacentre.is`. The journey does not stop there.
- **No monument or memorial at Hlidarendi or Bergthorshvoll, and no ghost stories or folk legends attached to either site, could be confirmed.** Gaps, not negations; saga sites commonly accrete such folklore, and the absence is likely an artefact of which pages were reachable.
- **Ossabaer and Thorolfsfell have no verified coordinates.** Ossabaer is placed approximately and flagged; Thorolfsfell, named in Dasent ch. 20, is omitted rather than guessed.
- **Konungahella** appears in the pool as a trade-route waypoint the saga does not narrate, 42 km off the real site (57.8452, 11.9397, modern Sweden). Dropped: a shipping lane is not a scene.
