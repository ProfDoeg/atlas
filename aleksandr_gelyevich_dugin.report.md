# Aleksandr Gelyevich Dugin (b. 1962): research report

*2026-08-31. Compiled for `aleksandr_gelyevich_dugin.journey.json`. Subject living; every
statement is dated to the evidence available at compilation, not to a settled record.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, named source reached and read. **[R]** = reconstruction,
tradition, movement folklore, or a claim recurring across secondary profiles without a
documentary anchor reached here. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Inaccessible sources
are named with the reason.

Governing caution: Dugin has a hostile literature, a sympathetic literature, and a self-narrating
literature, and the three disagree about his biography exactly where nothing documentary exists
to adjudicate. Where the disagreement is substantive it is recorded, not smoothed.

---

## 1. Moscow, 1962-1979

- Born **7 January 1962, Moscow** **[A]**, confirmed twice over by adversarial documents: the US
  OFAC designation record and the EU *Official Journal* L259I (2022) entry give the same date and
  city.
- Father **Geliy Aleksandrovich Dugin**, officer of Soviet military intelligence; mother **Galina
  Viktorovna Dugina, née Onufrienko**, physician; parents separated when he was three **[A:
  en.wikipedia]**. The GRU affiliation specifically is given as "probably" even by Dugin himself
  in Remnick's 2014 *New Yorker* profile **[R on the service, A on the career]**.
- **Gap:** no hospital, district, or address. The journey pins Moscow city centre and says so.

## 2. Moscow Aviation Institute, 1979-c. 1981

- **Entered 1979** **[A]**. Coordinates **55.8080, 37.5031** from the institute's own infobox,
  correcting a 1.2 km error in the research pool.
- **Departure c. 1981, cause disputed** **[R]**. ru.wikipedia says he left "after the second
  year", which puts it in 1981, not 1980 as some profiles have it. Three incompatible accounts:
  (i) expelled for anti-Soviet activity and forbidden literature, the movement's preferred
  version; (ii) academic failure; (iii) **he graduated after all** (Dunlop, Stanford FSI:
  "According to another account, he eventually managed to graduate from the institute").
  **Not adjudicated**; all three are in the journey's `date_confidence`.
- The pool's flat "left without a degree" has been **dropped as unsafe**: ru.wikipedia records a
  later **correspondence degree from the Novocherkassk Engineering-Reclamation Institute**,
  produced at his dissertation defence **[A]**.
- Street sweeper in the interval **[R]**: recurring anecdote, no record.

## 3. The Yuzhinsky circle, early 1980s

- Joined the underground circle of **Yuri Mamleev** and **Evgeny Golovin**, with **Geydar
  Dzhemal**; Guénon, Evola, Nietzsche, Heidegger, occultism **[A on milieu and reading list, R on
  the year of entry]** (Laruelle OP294; Dunlop; en.wikipedia).
- **Location corrected.** Yuzhinsky Pereulok is the 1929-1993 name of **Bolshoy Palashyovsky
  Pereulok**; coordinates **55.7641, 37.5985** (ru.wikipedia), correcting a 400 m error. The same
  article independently confirms the setting: a **six-flat wooden barrack** on the lane, one flat
  Mamleev's, where the circle met. The barrack is gone; the pin is the lane, not a door.
- **Qualification kept:** Dugin joined *after* Mamleev emigrated. The milieu continuing without
  its founder, not Mamleev's flat with Mamleev in it.
- **Lenin Library forged reader's card [R].** Widely repeated and undenied; **no card, register
  entry, or borrowing slip has ever been produced**. A rival, equally unverified story puts him in
  a KGB archive. Both unresolved; the journey keeps the stop and says so in `date_confidence`.

## 4. Samizdat Evola and Hans Zievers, 1981-1984

- **Translated Evola's *Heidnischer Imperialismus* into Russian, samizdat, 1981** **[R]**. The
  earliest nameable act of his intellectual life; the date rests on reconstruction, not on a dated
  artefact. No samizdat copy reached.
- **Recordings as "Hans Zievers" (Ганс Зиверс), 1981-1984** **[A on the range]**. *Correction:*
  en.wikipedia gives the range **1981-1984** and the Cyrillic transcription **Zievers**; the
  pool's single year of 1982 is undocumented. Alter ego after **Wolfram Sievers** of the SS
  Ahnenerbe, hanged at Nuremberg **[A]**.

## 5. Pamyat and the European right, 1988-1992

- **Pamyat under Dmitry Vasilyev, 1988, briefly on the central council, out in 1989** **[A]**.
  Whether he left or was expelled is not settled anywhere reached. No venue documented.
- **The Paris / de Benoist stop has been dropped: the report's largest editorial intervention.**
  The pool carried a c. 1989-1990 **Paris** stop tagged **[A]** for a de Benoist meeting. **No
  source reached places Dugin in Paris in those years.** The one documented Dugin-de Benoist
  encounter runs the other way: Dunlop places **de Benoist in Moscow** at the April 1992 General
  Staff roundtable, which the journey carries as fully attested. Staging a Paris scene on an
  unsourced attribution would break the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there.
  The relation survives, at the place where it is documented.
- **Brussels / Thiriart, c. 1990-1992** **[R, downgraded from the pool's A]**. What is documented
  is only that *Elementy* praised Thiriart's Euro-Soviet concept. **No meeting, no date.**
  Thiriart died 23 November 1992, fixing the window and nothing else. Kept as a stop, hedge in
  `date_confidence`, because the doctrinal debt is traceable in print even where the handshake is
  not.
- **Arktogeia, 1991** **[A: ru.wikipedia gives him as chairman "from 1991"]**, tightening the
  pool's 1990 / "c. 1990-1991". Center for Meta-Strategic Studies **[R]**. Collaboration with
  **Alexander Prokhanov** at the weekly *Den'* **[A]**.
- **April 1992, roundtable at the Military Academy of the General Staff** **[A: Dunlop, verbatim,
  with every name and rank]**: organized with Prokhanov, bringing **Alain de Benoist** and **Jean
  Lalou** to **Lt Gen Nikolai Klokotov** (head of strategy), **Lt Gen Nikolai Pishchev** (his
  deputy) and **Maj Gen Vladislav Iminov** (military history).
  - **Coordinate contradiction, flagged.** The Academy's infobox gives Vernadskogo Prospekt 100
    (**55.6498, 37.4740**); the same article's body cites **14 Kholzunova Lane** in Khamovniki,
    10 km away, which is where the pool's pin sat. No source says which building hosted the
    roundtable. The journey uses the infobox figure and states the ambiguity.

## 6. National Bolshevism, 1993-1998

- **NBP founded with Eduard Limonov and Yegor Letov, 1993** **[A: en.wikipedia; RFE/RL]**. Dugin
  supplied ideology, symbols and canon; Limonov the leadership and *Limonka*.
- **October 1993, the White House** **[A on alignment with the anti-Yeltsin camp; claims of a
  decisive combat role are explicitly undocumented]**. *Coordinate corrected* to **55.7549,
  37.5731**, a 350 m fix.
- **1995 Duma candidacy, 0.85 percent** **[A on the figure, unsourced on the district]**. Dunlop
  confirms the number verbatim and **names no constituency**; no source supporting the pool's **St
  Petersburg** district was found. The stop has therefore been **relocated from St Petersburg to
  Moscow** as a national/party event. This is a real loss of a pin, recorded as such.
- **1997, *Osnovy geopolitiki*, Arktogeia** **[A]**.
  - **Authorship contested, flagged:** Dugin credits **Klokotov** as "co-author and his main
    inspiration"; **Klokotov denies it**. **Colonel General Leonid Ivashov** is separately
    reported to have helped draft it.
- **1997, "Fascism: Borderless and Red"** **[A]**, carrying both the "by no means the racist and
  chauvinist aspects of National Socialism that determined the nature of its ideology" passage
  and, by **shared footnote [45]** on en.wikipedia, the Waffen-SS/Ahnenerbe "intellectual oasis"
  line. **That second attribution is an inference from a shared footnote, not from reading the
  essay**, and the journey says so.
- **1998, adviser on geopolitics to Duma speaker Gennady Seleznyov**, chairing the
  geopolitical-expertise section of a security advisory council **[R]**: much-repeated by Dunlop
  and Laruelle, no appointment record retrieved.
- **1998, split with Limonov** **[A]**. Cause given only as an ideological falling-out; the
  specific quarrel is undocumented.

  because it was: Rector **Sadovnichy** cancelled a reappointment order running to 2019; the press

  Following backlash over remarks urging killing in connection with the **2 May 2014 Odesa**
  violence. Not a clean single firing and not written as one.

## Contradictions between sources (not adjudicated)

1. **The MAI departure**: political expulsion / academic failure / graduated after all, the third
   carried by Dunlop.
2. **Klokotov and *Foundations of Geopolitics***: Dugin names him co-author and main inspiration;
   Klokotov denies it; Ivashov's drafting role is a third claim over both.
3. **The 1995 district**: 0.85 percent confirmed verbatim, district unnamed by the confirming
   source, St Petersburg attribution unsupported.
4. **The Rostov degree year**: 2000 defence vs 2001 registration, both in one record.
5. **Tsargrad TV**: 2015 (launch, Western profiles) vs 2016-2017 (ru.wikipedia).
6. **The General Staff Academy address**: Vernadskogo Prospekt 100 vs 14 Kholzunova Lane, 10 km
   apart, with no source fixing the April 1992 venue.
7. **Zakharovo to the blast site**: "about a kilometre" vs "a few kilometres".
8. **The intended target on 20 August 2022**: the strong version vs en.wikipedia's weaker
   rendering of the same WaPo report. The weaker one is used.
9. **The MSU departure**: cancelled reappointment vs contract expiry vs the press service's denial
   that any firing occurred. All three are true of the same summer.
10. **Two quotes were misattributed by the research pool**: the *Eurasian Mission* "individualism"
    line and the "Snake Island" line do **not** appear on en.wikipedia "Aleksandr Dugin"; both are
    verbatim on **English Wikiquote**. Source lines corrected in the journey.
11. **The Trump remark**: "I come to very sad conclusion: Donald Trump is totally mad." is on
    Wikiquote (New York Post, 5 August 2025). The trailing "It is the shame." appeared only under
    a leading prompt and **was not reproduced on a neutral re-fetch**. Not used.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Dugin is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** No apparatus stop, backward or forward, is in this
file. Three relations in the research pool were **not rooted in Dugin's own life**; per the
standing instruction they are surfaced rather than deleted, with the direction named:

1. **Backward, and not Dugin's.** The pool carried a **Carl Schmitt / Mircea Eliade** link
   (Schmitt telling Eliade, in the Cascais journal of 1943, that Guénon is "the most interesting
   person of the age"). That is a documented Schmitt-Guénon intersection belonging to
   `mircea_eliade.journey.json`, where it already sits. Dugin is not party to it. Not written
   here. It could legitimately be a *source* on a Dugin stop about his Schmitt reading; no such
   stop survived the cut.
2. **Backward, and not Dugin's.** The pool carried **Guénon's Buenos Aires reception, 1960**. That
   is Guénon's afterlife on Guénon's own file. Dugin's Traditionalism has no Buenos Aires
   component in anything reached. Not written here.
3. **Lateral, and negative.** The **Curtis Yarvin** interlock ("same pan-European illiberal-right
   speaking circuit") rests on no documented overlap, and the pool's own afterlife lens records a
   **negative finding**: no interview, joint appearance, or citation trail links the two. Not
   written here. The grouping of Dugin with Guénon, Evola, Land and Yarvin is **QUEUE.md's own
   framing note**, not evidence, and is recorded as such so no later pass mistakes it.

**A coverage gap in the other direction.** `nick_land.journey.json` contains **no "Dugin"
string**, despite the documented 6 October 2025 debate. Both men are living and the event sits
inside both lives, so this is an ordinary update rather than an exception question. This file
carries the debate as a Dugin stop naming Land. Whether Land's file gains a matching stop is the
operator's call, not a subagent's.

**Travelers named in campas**, each for a direct or one-directional relation of Dugin's own:
