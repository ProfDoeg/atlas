# Gaius Julius Caesar (100-44 BC): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `julius_caesar.journey.json`. Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or conventional modern identification. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Coordinates given are the ones written into the journey file; where the research pool's first pass gave a wrong pin, the correction and its source are recorded.*

Caesar is the rare ancient subject who is his own primary source: *Bellum Gallicum* 1-7 and the unfinished *Bellum Civile* 1-3 survive, continued by Hirtius and three anonymous hands **[A, corpus Caesarianum]**. Everything else runs through Suetonius, Plutarch, Cicero, Sallust, Appian, Dio. Campaign geography is therefore unusually well documented, private life is not, and every casualty figure in Gaul is the defendant's own testimony.

## 1. Birth and the Marian house (100-85 BC)

- Born at Rome, traditionally 12 or 13 July 100 BC **[A, Suetonius, *Divus Julius*, LacusCurtius; standard modern chronology]**. Some older reconstructions argued 102 BC on cursus-honorum grounds; not adjudicated.
- **Birthplace unattested.** The Subura link is **[R]**. Wikipedia, "Suburra" (fetched) gives the likeliest explanation: Caesar "lived in a family home (domus) in the Suburra until, in 63 BC, he was elected pontifex maximus," i.e. attested *residence*, not birthplace. **Pin corrected:** the pool's 41.8955, 12.4875 falls inside the Imperial Fora zone west of the quarter, which lay "on the slopes of the Quirinal and Viminal hills up to the offshoots of the Esquiline." File uses **41.8950, 12.4905** (Via Urbana).
- Father dies suddenly at Pisae while putting on his shoes; the son is about sixteen **[A, Pliny, *NH* 7.181, reported in dossier, not fetched]**. Pisae pin 43.7228, 10.4017 is settled geography.

## 2. Flamen Dialis, Cornelia, Sulla (85-81 BC)

- **Dating contradiction inside the pool, flagged.** Chronology lens: 87 BC. Geography lens: 87/86. Both precede the father's death in 85 and both contradict their own cited source. Suetonius 1 (fetched verbatim): "In the course of his sixteenth year he lost his father. In the next consulate, having previously been nominated priest of Jupiter, he broke his engagement with Cossutia... and married Cornelia." The next consulate after 85 is **84**. Some scholars allow 86/85. Stop dated 84, the disagreement stated in `date_confidence`. Whether he was ever fully *inaugurated*, as against nominated, is disputed and left open.
- Refuses Sulla's order to divorce Cinna's daughter; loses priesthood, dowry, property; hides in Sabine country **[A, Suetonius 1; Plutarch, *Caesar* 1, fetched: "he hid himself for some time, wandering about in the country of the Sabines"]**. **Gap:** no village, no house, no duration in any source; 42.15, 12.85 is a regional centroid, labelled as such.

## 3. Asia, Bithynia, pirates, schools (81-73 BC)

- Corona civica at the storming of Mytilene **[A, Suetonius 2]**. **Date corrected to 81**: chronology lens said 80, geography lens said "81/80", i.e. the lenses disagreed with each other. Wikipedia, "Mytilene" (fetched): the siege is 81 BC. Harmonized.
- Nicomedia. Suetonius reports the stay "under suspicion of improper relations" with Nicomedes IV **[A that Suetonius reports it; the allegation itself [R], unprovable either way]**.
- Service under Servilius Vatia against the Cilician pirates and the refusal to join Lepidus: **retagged [R]**. The pool tagged it [A] while its own source field read "independent confirmation not separately re-fetched, tagged R pending direct source check." No stop; folded into Pharmacusa's sources.
- **Two prosecutions, not one.** The chronology lens collapsed them. Dolabella **77 BC** (geography lens correct); Antonius Hybrida **76 BC**. Both lost **[A, Suetonius/Cicero tradition, not re-fetched]**. One stop dated 77, the split stated in campa and `date_confidence`.
- Pharmacusa: captured, held, ransomed, then crucified them **[A]**. **Discrepancy retained:** Suetonius 4 gives nearly forty days, Plutarch 2 gives thirty-eight.
  - Fitting out the pursuit fleet **at Miletus** is **[R]**, absent from the Suetonius and Plutarch text fetched. Kept in sources as an unconfirmed strand.
- Rhodes under Apollonius Molon; Plutarch 3 has him at "undisputed second rank" **[A]**.
  - **Interlock, direct:** Cicero studied under the same teacher a few years earlier. `cicero.journey.json` carries its own Rhodes stop at **36.4341, 28.2176** (line 97), inherited byte-identical. Cicero named in the campa.

## 4. The ladder of offices (73-59 BC)

- Pontiff 73, military tribune 71 **[A]**. No stops, for stop-count reasons; folded into later scenes and recorded here rather than dropped.
- Gades, 69: weeps before Alexander's statue; the dream of his mother read as the earth **[A, Suetonius 7; Plutarch 11, both fetched]**. Temple of Hercules Gaditanus site not fixed by the sources fetched; city-centre pin approximate. **Interlock, direct and one-directional:** Alexander, engaged here and at the Soma (`alexander.journey.json` 829).
- Funeral orations for Julia, widow of Marius, and for Cornelia, with Marian imagery displayed **[A, Suetonius 6]**.
- Aedile 65: Forum, Comitium, Capitol, restored Marian trophies, games, enormous debt **[A, Suetonius 10]**. No debt figure is in the pool and none is stated in the campa.
- Pontifex maximus 63, against Catulus and Isauricus, by lavish bribery **[A, Suetonius 13]**.
- **Catilinarian debate, 5 December 63: place corrected, coordinate kept.** The pool labelled the stop "Curia". The session met in the **Temple of Concord**, where Cicero delivered the Fourth Catilinarian (Wikipedia, "Catiline Orations", fetched). The pool's own 41.893, 12.4838 sits on Concord, c. 120 m west of the Curia Julia (c. 41.8929, 12.4853). Label changed, pin kept.
  - Caesar's speech survives only as Sallust's literary reconstruction (*Bell. Cat.* 51), **not a transcript**.
- Praetor 62, briefly suspended over Metellus Nepos, restored **[A]**. No separate stop. Bona Dea, December 62 **[A in the wider tradition; the specific chapters were not re-fetched, so well attested rather than verbatim-verified]**.
- Further Spain 61-60: Lusitani and Callaeci, the Atlantic, acclaimed imperator **[A]**. The **Brigantium / A Coruna** terminus is **[R]**: no ancient text fetched names the site.
- First consulship 59 with Bibulus; the private alliance with Pompey and Crassus formed 60 **[A; "First Triumvirate" is a modern label, never a Roman institution]**.

## 5. Gaul (58-51 BC)

All enslavement and casualty figures below are **Caesar's own**, published at Rome, unverifiable. Where the file uses one it says whose it is.

- Geneva, the Rhone bridge broken, 58 **[A, *BG* 1; Wikipedia "Geneva" (fetched) confirms his own use of Genava]**.
- Bibracte, 58 **[A]**. Wikipedia, "Bibracte" (fetched): the fighting was c. 26 km south of the oppidum, near Montmort. The pin marks the associated site, not the battlefield. Ariovistus, later the same year, is **[A as event, [R] as to place]**, no source fetched fixing a battlefield; **no separate stop**, folded into this campa and recorded here.
- **Sabis, 57: pin corrected.** Pool gave 50.0967, 3.5333, c. 16 km SSE near Le Cateau-Cambresis. Wikipedia, "Battle of the Sabis" (fetched) gives **50.23333, 3.44167** after Turquin (1955), west bank of the Selle near Saulzoir. **[R] stands**: the Sambre-near-Aulnoye identification is still live.
- Aduatuci, 57: surrender, night breakout, **53,000 sold** **[A, Caesar's own count; Namur-area site [R]]**.
- **Veneti, 56: pin corrected.** Pool gave 47.55, -3.12. Wikipedia, "Battle of Morbihan" (fetched) gives **47.50, -2.87**, likely near Quiberon Bay, and confirms Decimus Brutus in command and the hooked poles cutting the halyards.
- Rhine bridge in ten days, 55 **[A, *BG* 4]**; crossing point **[R]**, disputed, regional placement only. Britain 55 and 54 **[A]**, but **Deal and Brentford both [R]**, conventional identifications, contested, unconfirmed by any fetched source.
- Atuatuca, winter 54: Ambiorix destroys Sabinus and Cotta; Quintus Cicero besieged **[A, *BG* 5]**; Tongeren siting **[R]**.
- Avaricum, 52 **[A; Wikipedia "Bourges" (fetched): "all but 800 of its inhabitants"]**; the 40,000 total is Caesar's own. Gergovia, 52 **[A, Wikipedia "Gergovia" (fetched)]**, **no separate stop**, folded into Alesia.
- Alesia, September 52 **[A, Wikipedia "Battle of Alesia" (fetched)]**: dual lines, failed relief, the Mandubii expelled and refused passage, dying between the walls. **Later engagement, cited not staged:** Lionel Royer's 1899 canvas of the surrender is Royer's stop (`lionel_royer.journey.json` 301) and appears here only in `suggested_refs`, per the standing rule; historians hold the scene invented for effect.
- **Uxellodunum, 51: pin corrected.** Pool gave 44.8894, 1.7889, 6-7 km SE near Betaille. Wikipedia, "Uxellodunum" (fetched): **44.95, 1.68**, commune of Vayrac, Lot (the Puy d'Issolud summit itself c. 44.925, 1.751). Hands cut off the surviving defenders **[A, Hirtius, *BG* 8]**.
- **The cipher.** Suetonius 56 (fetched): letters to Gaius Oppius and Cornelius Balbus written so "that not a word could be made out," instructing the reader to "substitute the fourth letter of the alphabet, namely D, for A, and so with the others." Aulus Gellius, *Attic Nights* 17.9 adds that Valerius Probus wrote a treatise on the hidden meaning of these letters, and **the treatise is lost** **[A, via Wikipedia "Caesar cipher" citing Gellius]**.
  - **Gap:** no date, no place, no surviving specimen. The stop sits at Bibracte in the winter of 52/51, where Caesar is attested to have wintered, purely as an ordering device, and says so in `date_confidence`.

## 6. Civil war (49-45 BC)

- Rubicon, 10/11 January 49 **[A, Suetonius 31-32; Plutarch 32]**. The words are contested across sources *and* translations, and `quote_source` keeps all three visible: Suetonius's "iacta alea est", Plutarch's Greek imperative from Menander, and the Thomson/Forester rendering of the earlier line. The crossing point rests on the **1933 Italian government identification** of the Fiumicino with the ancient Rubicon **[R, administrative]**.
- Corfinium clemency, and Caesar's own surviving letter to Oppius and Balbus **[A, preserved because Cicero copied it into *Att.* 9.7C]**. One of very few pieces of his private prose.
- Brundisium: Pompey evacuates, Caesar has no ships **[A]**. Massilia, besieged by Trebonius and Decimus Brutus, surrenders in the same year **[A, Wikipedia "Marseille" (fetched)]**; **no separate stop**, folded into Ilerda.
- Ilerda, 49 **[A, *BC* 1; Wikipedia "Lleida" (fetched)]**.
- Dyrrachium, 48: lines broken, defeat admitted, withdrawal to Thessaly **[A, Wikipedia "Durres" (fetched)]**. Pin inherited from `cicero.journey.json`, **41.3231, 19.4414**, rather than the geography lens's 41.3133, 19.4458.
- Pharsalus, 9 August 48 (unreformed) **[A, Wikipedia "Battle of Pharsalus" (fetched)]**.
- Alexandria 48-47 **[A]**. On the Library: the evidence establishes a harbour fire spreading to dockside book warehouses and does **not** establish destruction of the main institution. Left as a gap.
  - **Pin corrected.** Pool gave 31.2089, 29.888, at the Pharos end of the Eastern Harbour. Wikipedia, "Alexandria" (fetched): the Royal Palaces filled "the northeast angle of the town and occupying the promontory of Lochias," which "has almost entirely disappeared into the sea." Lochias/Silsileh c. **31.209, 29.911**. The separate **Soma** stop uses the canonical atlas pin **31.2, 29.919**.
- Zela, 2 August 47 **[A, Wikipedia "Battle of Zela (47 BC)" (fetched)]**. **Contradiction retained:** Suetonius puts "veni, vidi, vici" on a placard in the Pontic triumph, Appian in a letter.
- Thapsus, 6 April 46 (unreformed) **[A, Wikipedia "Battle of Thapsus" (fetched)]**: archers against the elephants; the *Bellum Africum* author reports killing continuing past the point of orders.
- Temple of Venus Genetrix dedicated 26 September 46 **[A, Wikipedia "Temple of Venus Genetrix" (fetched)]**.
  - **Interlock, direct:** Aeneas. `aeneas.journey.json` line 543 has Anchises show the line running to "the Caesars." The relation runs from Caesar's side too: this is the descent he claimed aloud over his aunt's coffin in 69 and built in stone in 46.
- Julian calendar: 46 BC extended to 445 days, reform in force 45 BC, Sosigenes named by Pliny **[A]**. The file's `calendar` field is `julian`, this being a pre-1582 life.
- **Munda, 17 March 45: the pool's own account corrected.** Gnaeus Pompeius was **not** captured and killed at Munda. Wikipedia, "Battle of Munda" (fetched): he escaped and was "cornered during the Battle of Lauro and killed" weeks later; Sextus "remained at large"; Labienus and Attius Varus "died on the field and were granted a burial by Caesar." Both lenses carried the error; corrected in the campa.

## 7. Dictatorship, assassination, aftermath (44 BC)

- Dictator perpetuo **[A]**. Lupercalia, 15 February: the diadem refused and dedicated to Jupiter **[A, multiply attested: Suetonius, Plutarch, Dio, Nicolaus of Damascus]**. No reliable evidence establishes a settled plan to take the title of king; the conspirators' fear is a separate fact from the intention, and the campa keeps them separate.
- Assassination, Ides of March, Curia of Pompey **[A, Suetonius 81-82]**; Antistius found twenty-three wounds, one judged mortal.
- **Last words: contradiction retained, popular version rejected.** Suetonius says it is not known whether he spoke and reports that he merely groaned; he adds what "some authors relate." Thomson/Forester at Perseus (`Perseus:text:1999.02.0132:life=jul.:chapter=82`), verbatim: `although some authors relate, that when Marcus Brutus fell upon him, he exclaimed, "What! art thou, too, one of them! Thou, my son!"` Exclamation mark, not question mark; no Greek "kai su, teknon" in this translation. "Et tu, Brute?" is Shakespeare, not an ancient quotation. The file uses the line with the "some authors relate" framing in `quote_source`.
- Cremation in the Forum, the altar raised by the crowd and removed by the consuls, and the July comet read as the sidus Iulium **[A, Suetonius; Wikipedia "Temple of Caesar" citing Suetonius and Dio, since Suetonius 84-85 could not be fetched, the page truncating first]**.
- Temple of Divus Iulius on the cremation site, dedicated 18 August 29 BC **[A]**. **Pin corrected:** the afterlife lens gave 41.89253, 12.48611, c. 65 m north of the article's own 41.891943, 12.486246 and contradicting the geography lens's 41.8919, 12.4862 for the same building. File uses **41.8919, 12.4862**.
- Augustus is Caesar's **great-nephew** (grandson of his sister Julia), posthumously adopted; the afterlife lens said "nephew". Corrected. His own cipher, a right shift of one without wraparound, writing Z as AA, is confirmed verbatim.

## Honest gaps

Birth house and district, unattested, Suetonius's opening chapters lost · Sabine refuge, no village, no house, no duration · the cipher, no date, no place, no surviving specimen, Probus's treatise lost · Ariovistus battlefield, Rhine crossing point, Deal beach, Brentford ford and the Atlantic terminus in Spain, all unresolved or contested · the Alexandrian Library, harbour fire and burning warehouses established, destruction of the institution not established · all Gallic casualty and enslavement figures are Caesar's own, in a book written to be read at Rome.

**Tusculum portrait**, not used in the file so the error does not propagate: the pool called it Carrara marble, Wikipedia, "Tusculum portrait" (fetched) says "fine-grained marble". Its Turin coordinate 45.06983, 7.68566 lies c. 350 m south of the Museo di Antichita (c. 45.073, 7.685), unverifiable because the museum's article 404s.
