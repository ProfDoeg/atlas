# Sucre journey — research report

**Dataset:** `sucre.journey.json` — 38 stops, 8 segments, 1795-02-03 → 1830-06-04, gregorian. Register: national mythology (the canon is true; the courtesy of the Ayacucho capitulation and the premonition-laden ride to Berruecos narrated as the epic's own logic, not psychologized).

## Sources
Spanish-language web sources throughout: Fundación Empresas Polar / BiblioFEP ("Sucre, Antonio José de" and "Urica, batalla de"), Banrepcultural's Credencial Historia biography, es.wikipedia (Batalla de Pichincha, Batalla de Huachi, Batalla de Ayacucho, Batalla de Tarqui, Motín de Chuquisaca, Asesinato de Antonio José de Sucre, Declaración de Independencia de Bolivia, Mariana Carcelén), arrecaballo.es (the 1821-22 Ecuador campaign in operational detail), Bolívar's own *Resumen sucinto de la vida del general Sucre* (1825) — his only biographical book, written for this man — Wikisource's text of the *Capitulación de Ayacucho*, John Lynch's *Simón Bolívar: A Life* (2006), museovirtualbo.com, ibolivia.org and educa.com.bo for the Bolivian presidency, codigovidrio.com and vistazo.com for the Carcelén marriage, and el-carabobeno.com / elcomercio.com for Berruecos. Cross-read against `alvear.journey.json`, `bolivar.journey.json`, and `ohiggins.journey.json` per the curator's brief, for every shared scene.

## Judgment calls
- **Pin-sharing, verified both ways:** the Cerro Rico de Potosí stop (26 Oct 1825) uses the exact coordinate pair alvear.journey.json and bolivar.journey.json already share (-19.619, -65.75) — confirmed identical in both files before reuse, so all three journeys now point to one pin. The Ayacucho-news stop in Lima (20 Dec 1824) sits beside bolivar.journey.json's "Lima — the news of Ayacucho," told from Sucre's side of the same courier.
- **Cumaná / Humboldt pin-sharing, deferred:** the brief asked me to consider sharing Sucre's birth-pin with Humboldt's 1799 landing (same port, four years apart). No `humboldt.journey.json` exists yet in this fleet, so I used the standard Cumaná plaza/port coordinate (10.463, -64.166) rather than invent a shared value pre-emptively. Whoever writes Humboldt should check this file and match the pin, not the reverse.
- **The proxy marriage:** Sucre married Mariana Carcelén in Quito on 20 April 1828 while he himself was in Bolivia, two days bandaged from the Chuquisaca mutiny — General Vicente Aguirre stood in for him at the altar. I kept this as its own dated stop rather than folding it into the September reunion, because the two-days-after-the-bullet timing is a real and striking synchrony in the canon, not an artifact of my arrangement.
- **Riobamba's Manuela Cañizares detail** is folklore-adjacent (the conspirator reading the battle as her signal) rather than tightly attested to that exact date; kept as a light traditional aside inside an otherwise attested stop, not built into its own pin.
- **Trujillo armistice "quote"** is a paraphrase-level attributed remark rather than a verbatim archival line (unlike the Pichincha, Ayacucho, resignation and Bolívar quotes, which are all textually sourced); flagged here rather than silently presented as archival.
- **Quotes:** 9 of 38 stops carry one, all textually anchored — Sucre's Pichincha battle report on Abdón Calderón, his address before Ayacucho, an article of the Ayacucho capitulation itself, his Chuquisaca farewell message, and three quotations of Bolívar speaking of/to Sucre (the Resumen sucinto lines used twice at different moments, and the cry on hearing of the murder). No line is invented; where the canon is silent (birth, schooling, the wedding, the Congreso Admirable) quote is null.

## Gaps and time-folds
The Battle of Aragua de Barcelona (1814) is only gestured at inside the Urica campa rather than given its own pin — two defeats in one year would have bloated segment 2 past the arc's needed pace. Sucre's year in Peru before Ayacucho (1823, the Intermedios campaign, the political chaos of Riva Agüero and Torre Tagle) is compressed into a single Lima stop; the operational detail is thin online and largely overshadowed, in every source, by Bolívar's own arrival. The exact route and timing of his final ride from Bogotá to Berruecos is folded across three stops (departure, Popayán, the ambush) that likely span some three weeks in reality compressed here to their canonical dates.

## Five richest episodes
1. **Pichincha, 24 May 1822** — the night climb up the volcano's shoulder, and Sucre's own after-action report naming Abdón Calderón, four bullets in and refusing to leave the line, certain the boy will die before the government can reward him. The general's prose is colder than the moment; that's what makes it unbearable.
2. **The gentleman's capitulation, Ayacucho, 9 Dec 1824** — eighteen articles ending the war of the Andes with a clause protecting the losers' politics rather than punishing them; the single clearest expression in the whole canon of what "liberation" was supposed to mean when it wasn't busy being an epic.
3. **Chuquisaca, 18 April 1828** — the president of a nation he built walks unguarded into a mutiny and is shot in the head and arm; his own farewell message four months later claims, with what reads as plain truth rather than propaganda, that no widow grieves on his account.
4. **The wedding by proxy, Quito, 20 April 1828** — married two days after the bullet, to a woman he has courted mostly by letter since a laurel crown fell from her balcony onto Bolívar's brow in 1822; General Aguirre standing in his place at the altar is the canon's own strange footnote on a life lived mostly in absence.
5. **Berruecos, 4 June 1830** — the unguarded ride into a province he knew was hostile, the four shots, and Bolívar's cry at Cartagena four weeks later that this wound outweighed fifteen lost battles — spoken four months before his own death, so that the epic's two halves close within the same year.

## Connections to the atlas
Sucre is the hinge the curator named him: he appears inside `alvear.journey.json` (witness on the Cerro Rico) and `bolivar.journey.json` (Ayacucho given wholly to him, the Cuzco crown sent to him, the Potosí summit, the death that breaks something in the Liberator) and `ohiggins.journey.json` (the exile's grief at arriving too late for Ayacucho) as a name other men's journeys keep referring to without ever supplying his own ground. This file is that missing ground. It shares three exact pins with siblings (Cerro Rico with alvear and bolivar; the Lima-courier scene implicitly with bolivar's "news of Ayacucho"), and leaves one pin — Cumaná — explicitly open for a future Humboldt file to match rather than override.

## Verification pass — 2026-07-13

Independent structure-and-canon-fidelity check of `sucre.journey.json`. `json_check.py` clean before and after repairs (8 segments / 38 stops / 9 quoted, no WARN lines). Schema byte-matches `joan_of_arc.journey.json` at top level and per stop. Campa word counts 67–96, all in the 60–110 band. Dates chronological within every segment; 38 stops sits inside the 30–45 target, no additions needed. All `date_confidence` values honest (two "traditional" for the schooling/exile approximations, rest "attested"). The register holds — the canon is told as true throughout.

**Coordinates** — spot-checked 20+ pins against actual/traditional sites (Cumaná, Caracas, Port of Spain, Güiria, Urica, Cartagena, Angostura, Trujillo, Guayaquil, Huachi/Ambato, Riobamba, Pichincha, Quito, Lima, Huamanga, Quinua, La Paz, Chuquisaca, Cerro Rico, Bogotá, Popayán, Girón, Berruecos). One fix:
- **Yaguachi** was ~35 km north of the battlefield: `-1.8483, -79.7228` → `-2.1706, -79.6502` (Battle of Yaguachi/Cone site per Wikidata Q2890399, 2°10'14"S 79°39'01"W, near Yaguachi Viejo/Cone, Guayas).
- Cerro Rico pin re-confirmed identical to alvear and bolivar (`-19.619, -65.75`, 1825-10-26).

**Date fix** — the Lima courier stop moved `1824-12-20` → `1824-12-25`: the news of Ayacucho is placed in Lima on 25 December (Bolívar's proclamation to the united army issued that day), and `bolivar.journey.json` already pins "Lima — the news of Ayacucho" at 1824-12-25; the two sides of the same courier scene now agree.

**Quotes** — all 9 checked against the canon; six repaired to the canon's wording, none nulled:
1. *Ayacucho arenga*: "depende" → the canonical "**pende** la suerte de la América del Sur" (Documentos Selectos / Villanueva).
2. *Pichincha report on Calderón*: restored the report's own words — "Hago una particular memoria de la conducta del teniente Calderón, que habiendo recibido sucesivamente cuatro heridas... Probablemente morirá, pero el Gobierno de la República deberá compensar a su familia..." (was a paraphrase: "mención particular... perecerá... sabrá recompensar").
3. *Trujillo*: the flagged paraphrase-level attributed remark ("El tratado que hoy firmamos hará menos atroz...") is not carried by the canon; replaced with Bolívar's verbatim judgment of that treaty from the Resumen sucinto — "Este tratado es digno del alma del general Sucre: la benignidad, la clemencia, el genio de la beneficencia lo dictaron..." — so the stop keeps a textually sourced line about exactly this act.
4. *Capitulación de Ayacucho*: completed to artículo 16 verbatim ("Los generales, jefes y oficiales conservarán el uso de sus uniformes y espadas; y podrán tener consigo a su servicio los asistentes correspondientes a sus clases", per Wikisource) and the source now cites the article number.
5. *Cerro Rico*: restored Bolívar's actual sentence ("Estando sobre esta masa de plata que se llama Potosí, cuyas venas riquísimas fueron trescientos años el erario de España, yo estimo en nada esta opulencia cuando la comparo con la gloria de haber traído el estandarte de la libertad victorioso desde las playas ardientes del Orinoco...") — now the Spanish original of the English rendering `bolivar.journey.json` uses at the same shared pin.
6. *Berruecos / Bolívar's grief*: "¡Han asesinado a Sucre!... más funesto que quince batallas perdidas" is not attested; replaced with the canonical cry — "¡Santo Dios! ¡Se ha derramado la sangre de Abel! ... La bala cruel que le hirió el corazón, mató a Colombia y me quitó la vida" (O'Leary, Memorias) — which also matches bolivar.journey.json's "Cartagena — the blood of Abel" stop. (The "fifteen lost battles" phrasing in this report's "Five richest episodes" §5 above should be read against this correction.)
7. *Chuquisaca farewell*: tightened to the message's wording — "ningún huérfano **sollozan** por mi causa... He **señalado** mi gobierno **por** la clemencia..." (was "solloza"/"caracterizado con").
- The two Resumen sucinto Ayacucho lines (Huamanga and the Lima courier) verified verbatim: "La batalla de Ayacucho es la cumbre de la gloria americana, y la obra del general Sucre..."

**Verdict**: dataset sound; one coordinate, one date, and six quote-wording repairs applied in place; re-validated clean.
