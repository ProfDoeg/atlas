# Frank Herbert (1920-1986): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-16 for `frank_herbert.journey.json`. Second pass, written to close a gap flagged in the first: the original file covered all six novels' publication and never mentioned the Butlerian Jihad, the Orange Catholic Bible commandment, Mentats, or the Bene Tleilax.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested. **[R]** reconstruction or inference. **CONTRADICTION** sources disagree, not adjudicated. **GAP** the record is silent, nothing invented.

---

## 1. Tacoma and the peninsulas, 1920-1938

- Born **8 October 1920, Tacoma, Washington**. **[A Wikipedia, 'Frank Herbert'; Brian Herbert, *Dreamer of Dune* (2003)]**
- **CONTRADICTION, given names.** One lens has *Frank Patrick Herbert Jr.*, son of *Franklin Patrick Herbert Sr.*; the other has *Franklin Patrick Herbert Jr.*, son of *Frank Herbert Sr.* Exactly inverted, both tracing to Wikipedia. No primary record reached; the file uses *Franklin Patrick Herbert Jr.* and flags it.
- Mother **Eileen (McCarthy) Herbert**; a large Irish Catholic maternal family, several of whose sisters took charge of stretches of his upbringing and hoped to steer him toward the priesthood. **[A Brian Herbert; O'Reilly ch. 2; [R] as to finer detail, relayed second hand]**
- Boyhood largely on the **Olympic and Kitsap peninsulas**: hunting, fishing, boat handling **[A O'Reilly ch. 2]**; paternal grandparents joined the **cooperative colony at Burley** in 1905 **[A]**. **CORRECTED COORDINATE:** an earlier Burley pin (47.4712, -122.6082) lands about 6 km north, near Olalla; Burley is **47.4175, -122.6294** **[A]**. **GAP:** no childhood address is documented.

### The "eating the earth" remark

- **Version A [A Wikipedia, 'Dune (novel)']:** spoken by **Howard Hansen**, a friend raised on the **Quileute reservation**, in **1958**: *"White men are eating the earth. They're gonna turn this whole planet into a wasteland, just like North Africa."* **Version B [R]:** spoken in the **1930s** by an unnamed **Hoh** man among the outdoorsmen who mentored Herbert as a boy.
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** Same words, different speaker, twenty-odd years apart, with opposite implications: boyhood inheritance or mid-career trigger. Version A names a person and a year and is followed in the file. Neither version fixes the ground of the exchange, so the La Push pin is reconstruction either way.

## 2. The runaway newsman, 1938-1945

- **c. 1938:** at about eighteen, runs away from home to his aunt **Peggy "Violet" Rowntree** in **Salem, Oregon**. **[A]** **GAP:** no source states what he was fleeing.
- **1939:** graduates **Salem High School**, renamed **North Salem** only in 1954 **[A]**. **CORRECTED COORDINATE:** the school (765 14th St NE) is **44.9445, -123.0210**, not the 44.9526, -123.0480 carried earlier, about 2.4 km northwest of campus **[A Wikipedia]**.
- **1939:** lies about his age for his first newspaper job, the **Glendale Star**, California. **[A]** **CONTRADICTION, ordering:** one lens dates Glendale "c. 1938-1939," another squarely 1939, and graduation is also 1939. Whether the job preceded or followed the June diploma is unsettled; the file places it after.
- **1940:** back in Salem at the **Oregon Statesman**, several roles including photographer **[A]**. **1941:** marries **Flora Lillian Parkinson** in **San Pedro** **[A]**; daughter **Penelope** born **16 Feb 1942 [A]**; divorce **1943 [A]**. **GAP:** no address, no cause, no surviving correspondence located.
- **1942:** roughly **six months in the Navy Seabees** as a photographer; medical discharge for a head injury. **[A]** **GAP, duty station:** no source names where he trained or served. Seabee training then ran chiefly through Camp Peary and Port Hueneme, but **nothing ties Herbert to either by name**; the file pins Port Hueneme and states that the coordinate marks the depot, not a posting **[R]**.
- **Late 1940s:** reports for the **Oregon Journal**, Portland. **[A]** Undated; the file places it 1943 as a slot between discharge and the 1946 marriage. **[R as to year]**

## 3. Seattle and the long apprenticeship, 1946-1955

- **1946:** enrolls at the **University of Washington**, never completes a degree **[A]**; meets **Beverly Ann Stuart** in a creative-writing class, she having already sold a story to *Modern Romance*; marries her in **Seattle** **[A]**. The **20 June** date in the file is **[R]**. Beverly then set her own writing aside for advertising copywriting, became the household's earner, and edited his manuscripts **[A]**.
- **Sons: Brian** 1947, **Bruce Calvin** 1951 **[A]**. **GAP:** neither birth place given beyond "United States." Brian's is pinned to Seattle on the 1946 residence **[R]**.
- **1949:** family moves to California; **Santa Rosa Press-Democrat** **[A Wikipedia; O'Reilly dates it April 1949]**. **CORRECTION:** one lens carried **1950**, rejected against two statements of 1949. **April 1952:** first science fiction sale, **"Looking for Something," *Startling Stories*** **[A]**.
- **Guy Cordon, a two-way date problem.** Herbert worked in Washington, D.C. as campaign hand and speechwriter for Oregon senator **Guy Cordon**, and as research assistant at the **Library of Congress** **[A O'Reilly ch. 2]**. One lens dated it **1952**, another **1956**. **1956 is impossible**: Cordon's Senate service ran to **3 January 1955**. The file adopts **1954** and moves the locus from the Capitol to the **Library of Congress (38.8887, -77.0047)**.
- **November 1955:** *Under Pressure* serialized in *Astounding*; book form *The Dragon in the Sea*, 1956. **[A]** **GAP / CONTRADICTION, 1955 residence:** Wikipedia gives "about a decade" at the *San Francisco Examiner* with no start year; O'Reilly places him there only **from 1959**. Between 1949 and 1959 **no consulted source establishes employer or address**. The stop is a Bay Area placement with the uncertainty in `date_confidence`.

## 4. The sand, 1957-1965

- **1957:** travels to **Florence, Oregon** to cover a **USDA** program planting **European beach grass** to stabilize migrating dunes; the assignment becomes the seed of *Dune*. **[A Wikipedia, 'Dune (novel)']** Quote to his agent: *"could swallow whole cities, lakes, rivers, highways."* Coordinate **43.9612, -124.1088**, checked **[A]**. **CORRECTION:** an earlier phrasing had the article *"They Stopped the Moving Sands"* going "unsold"; Wikipedia is stronger, **never completed**, printed only in *The Road to Dune* (2005).
- **1959:** begins roughly five to six years of research, writing, and revision. **[A]** **CORRECTION:** one lens dated the San Francisco relocation and the start of the *Dune* work to **1960**; O'Reilly and Wikipedia both give **1959**. The **Marin County (Fairfax) residence** stays **[R]**, unconfirmed in this pass too, and it matters because the research reading is staged there.
- **The reading.** Herbert's own file records him reading the life of **Muhammad** and the wilderness narrative of **Moses** in these years, alongside desert ecology, drugs, prophecy, and the politics of a single scarce commodity; and it frames Bene Gesserit inherited memory as **Jung's** collective unconscious given a bloodline. **[A within the file's own text; the underlying reading lists are not independently reproduced, and the Jung link is conceptual, not a meeting]**

## 5. *Dune*, 1963-1966

- **1965:** after **more than twenty** rejections, accepted by **Chilton Book Company**, better known for auto repair manuals, on a **$7,500 advance**; published at **$5.95**; weak sales, tepid reviews **[A]**. **CONTRADICTION, publication month:** one lens gives **August 1965**, the first-pass file **1 October 1965**. - **Nebula:** the **inaugural** Best Novel award **[A]**, banquet March 1966, New York. **Hugo:** ties for Best Novel at **Tricon**, Sheraton-Cleveland, 1-5 September 1966 **[A]**. **GAP:** **no source places Herbert at either ceremony**, so neither city is a stop; the ceremonies are cited in `suggested_refs` on a stop pinned where he lived and worked.

## 6. The Butlerian Jihad: what is Frank Herbert's and what is not

The first pass omitted the theme entirely. The distinction below must not be collapsed.

### Unambiguously Frank Herbert's [A Wikipedia, 'Butlerian Jihad'; the six novels]

- Across all six novels he wrote himself, *Dune* (1965) to *Chapterhouse: Dune* (1985), he establishes and repeatedly references the **Butlerian Jihad**: a war roughly **10,000 years before** the saga, ending in the destruction of virtually all thinking machines, conscious robots, and computers. **He never narrates it.** It is deep backstory in every book, its causes and events left to speculation.
- The surviving law, epigraph from the **Orange Catholic Bible**: *"Thou shalt not make a machine in the likeness of a human mind."* **TAGGING CONTRADICTION:** one lens tags this **[A]** via *Dune*'s appendix and terminology material; another tags it **[R]** because every attempt to reach a primary-adjacent page carrying the text 404'd, paywalled, or returned nothing. The wording is uniform across secondary sources. The file uses the quote and states the limitation in `quote_source`.
- The **fullest in-text gloss he ever wrote** is *God Emperor of Dune* (1981), where Leto II calls the Jihad **"a semi-religious social upheaval"** born of human revulsion at how far machines had come to guide and control people. The **human substitutes** the prohibition forces into being: **Mentats** (humans trained as living computers), the **Bene Gesserit** disciplines, **Spacing Guild Navigators** (melange-induced prescience for folded space), the **Bene Tleilax** (genetic manipulation, Face Dancers, axlotl tanks, gholas).

### The name

- Generally understood **by critics** to derive from **Samuel Butler**, whose 1863 essay *"Darwin among the Machines"* (*The Press*, Christchurch; developed into the Book of the Machines in *Erewhon*, 1872) argued machines could evolve intelligence and displace humanity. **[R]**
- **CORRECTION.** An earlier phrasing stated this as fact and as Herbert's own account; Wikipedia presents it as **critical association**, not anything Herbert said. The essay's date and development into *Erewhon* are **[A]**. That **no Butler character exists in Herbert's six novels** is **[A]** and is the load-bearing observation. No `samuel_butler` file exists in the corpus.

### Not Frank Herbert's [A as to authorship, R as to derivation]

- **Sixteen years after his death**, Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson published the **Legends of Dune** trilogy: *Dune: The Butlerian Jihad* (2002), *The Machine Crusade* (2003), *The Battle of Corrin* (2004), dramatizing the war for the first time with invented characters including **Serena Butler**, the AI **Omnius**, and the robot **Erasmus**. **[A]**
- **CORRECTION.** An earlier summary called the **Titans** "machine armies controlled by Omnius." Wrong: they are **twenty formerly human, immortal, militant cyborgs** in cymek bodies, led by **Agamemnon**, who conquered the Old Empire and then accidentally ceded control to Omnius. Omnius commands **robots**; **Erasmus**, who kills Serena Butler's son and triggers the Jihad, is the other antagonist. **[A Wikipedia, 'Legends of Dune']**
- **Brian Herbert states** the trilogy draws in part on a **thirty-page outline** found among his father's papers **[A as to the claim being made]**. How much of the war narrative derives from that outline rather than the co-authors' invention is **disputed and unresolved by any source consulted** **[R, explicitly unadjudicated]**. The prequels also re-explain "Butlerian" as deriving from their invented Serena Butler rather than Samuel Butler: a naming discontinuity, not a resolution. The pool quotation *"With few ambitions, most people allowed efficient machines to perform everyday tasks for them..."* is likewise from the 2002 prequel and is **not used**.

## 7. Quotation audit

Only pool quotations are used in the file. Two needed correction; one is a hard rejection.

- **REJECTED, misattribution.** *"He who controls the spice controls the universe."* **Not in the novel.** It is **Baron Harkonnen's line in David Lynch's 1984 film** (Wikiquote). Open Library returns roughly 34 books for the sentence, all film adaptation, Lynch studies, or works citing the film, and **no edition of the novel**.
- **CORRECTED framing.** *"Highly organized research is guaranteed to produce nothing new."* Genuine wording, wrong presentation: Open Library shows *Dune* editions rendering it **lowercase, mid-sentence**, in the **Appendix I** ecology material on Pardot Kynes. Quoted in that form.
- **CORRECTED attribution.** *"From water does all life begin."* **Verbatim in *Dune*** (Open Library, multiple editions). But the **Orange Catholic Bible attribution is unsupported**: the adjacent formula is *"'Giudichar mantene': It is written in the Shah-Nama that water was the first of all things created"*, Fremen and Zensunni scripture. Cited accordingly.
- **Used, [A], two sightings each:** the Mohiam machine-thinking line; the **Litany Against Fear**. **Single-source [A]:** the **First Law of Mentat**; the Bene Gesserit strange-water proverb; the *Chapterhouse* self-accountability line.

## 8. The later years, 1969-1986

- **1969:** education writer, **Seattle Post-Intelligencer**; from **1970** also lectures at the **University of Washington**; *Dune Messiah* published. **[A]** **CORRECTION:** one lens placed the writing of *Dune Messiah* at **Port Townsend in 1969**. Wrong: he was at the *Examiner* until 1969 and in Seattle 1969-1972, and the peninsula house belongs to the 1970s.
- **1972:** social and ecological consultant in **Vietnam** and **Pakistan**; leaves journalism for full-time fiction **[A]**. **GAP:** no province and no city named anywhere; both coordinates are country-level placements.
- **1970s:** builds an **ecological demonstration project** near **Port Townsend** (solar, wind, methane, composting) **[A as to intent]**. **GAP:** no address or parcel.
- **1970s-80s:** a second home in **Hawaii** **[R]**. **CONTRADICTION:** Wikipedia confirms it **without naming island or town**; **Kailua, Oahu** is commonly cited but unsourced; other accounts say **Maui** or **Kauai**. Unfixed.
- **1974:** Beverly diagnosed with lung cancer **[A]**. **1976:** *Children of Dune* **[A]**. **1981:** *God Emperor of Dune* **[A]**. Writing locations for both are **[R]**.
- **7 February 1984: Beverly Herbert dies [A as to date and illness]**. **GAP:** sources **do not state the place**; one lens offers the Hawaiian residence, another the peninsula. The file defaults to Port Townsend and flags it.
- **1984:** *Heretics of Dune*; Lynch's *Dune* released. **1985:** marries **Theresa Diane Shackelford**; *Chapterhouse: Dune*, the last novel he writes. **[A]**
- **11 February 1986:** dies in **Madison, Wisconsin** of a **pulmonary embolism** while recovering from **pancreatic cancer** surgery, aged 65 **[A]**. The hospital name is an inference (Madison's major surgical centre), **not** independently confirmed **[R]**.
- **CONTRADICTION, place of death.** Hospital room or his son **Bruce**'s Madison house: **unresolved**; the biographies say only "while recovering from surgery." Both are on the map, a day apart, so the reader sees the pair rather than a false certainty. **GAP, remains:** **no reliable source for burial, cremation, or scattering was located.**

## 10. Interlocks

**Named in campas**, each on a direct one-directional relation of Herbert's own: **`muhammad`** and **`moses`** in the Fairfax research stop (he read the life of Muhammad and the Exodus wilderness narrative in the six years he was building Arrakis, the Fremen jihad, and Paul's prophecy plot); **`jung`** in the *Dune World* stop. **No Mecca or Sinai stop is created**: Herbert never went to either, and the canonical Kaaba pin (21.4225, 39.8262) and Sinai pin (28.539, 34.302) belong to those travelers' own files.

**Rejected.** **`philip_k_dick`**: three candidate crossings (both men's papers at the Pollak Library under Willis McNelly; both in Marin County 1959-1964; both in the same West Coast magazine scene) are all shared geography or milieu, **not a relation between the two men**, and no meeting is on record. **`frank_lloyd_wright`** and **`dirac`**: both pin Madison, Wisconsin, where Herbert dies in 1986, decades apart. **`nick_land`**: nearest thematic neighbour for the AI-prohibition material, with **no textual or biographical crossing whatever**.
