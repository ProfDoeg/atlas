# Benkos Biohó: research report
*Rey del Arcabuco; founder of the palenque tradition of the Montes de María; hanged and quartered at Cartagena de Indias, 16 March 1621.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named inline · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions between sources are flagged where found and are **not** adjudicated. Coordinates discussed below are those used in `benkos_bioho.journey.json`.

A note on the documentary situation, which governs everything else in this file. There is no archive of Benkos Biohó. There is one near-contemporary narrative source, Fray Pedro Simón's *Noticias historiales de las conquistas de Tierra Firme en las Indias Occidentales*, written a generation after the events and printed much later; everything else reachable in this pass is modern encyclopaedic summary (Spanish and English Wikipedia, which disagree with each other on ethnicity, dates and the treaty year), UNESCO's intangible-heritage documentation for the modern town, and 20th-century Colombian literature that has visibly fed back into the "historical" account. Where a claim below carries a date to the year, the year is usually the encyclopaedia's, not a document's.

---

## 1. Origin: the Bijagós

**[A, es.wikipedia "Benkos Biohó"]** Spanish-language sources identify him as of the **Bijago** ethnic group, as was his wife, called Reina Wiwa in the same tradition.

**[A, en.wikipedia "Benkos Bioho"]** The English article instead calls him "a Mandinka leader of royal lineage."

> **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Bijago (ES) versus Mandinka (EN). Both editions place the birth in or near the Bijagós Islands off present-day Guinea-Bissau. A third attribution circulating in the research pool that reached me, "Bran, from a Bioho region near Casamance," appears in **neither** source and could not be traced; it is treated here as unsupported and is not carried into the journey file.

**Birth year: a gap, not a fact.** EN gives only "late 16th century." ES gives a range of roughly c.1550-1599. No source states a birth year. The journey file pins a nominal 1570 inside that range and says so in `date_confidence`; **1550, which circulates widely, sits at the extreme early edge of the ES range and is not documented anywhere reachable.**

**Royal lineage [R].** Attributed by tradition on both sides; no contemporary document supports it, and nothing recorded at the time of his birth exists at all.

**Cacheu [R].** The Portuguese stockade at the mouth of the Cacheu river was the principal slaving depot serving the Bijagós and Casamance coasts in this period. **No source places Biohó at Cacheu by name.** It is included in the journey as the geographically necessary route, tagged as reconstruction in `date_confidence`, not as an attested stop.

## 2. Capture and sale

**[A, es.wikipedia]** The family is enslaved by **Pedro Gómez Reynel** and sold at Cartagena in **1596**.

**Correction carried from verification:** the date **1595** for the capture is in no cited source; ES puts the enslavement and the sale in the same year, 1596. The journey file does not use 1595 for the capture.

**[R]** Reynel was the holder of the Crown's **asiento**, the monopoly contract to supply African captives to the Indies, rather than a shipmaster personally transporting anyone. Describing him as the man who "captured" Biohó compresses a contract into a biography.

**[R]** The wife **Wiwa** and children **Sando** and **Orika** are Spanish-language tradition. They are named in the journey with that hedge; the family's presence aboard is not documented.

**[A, es.wikipedia and en.wikipedia]** At Cartagena he is bought by the merchant **Juan de Palacio** and transferred to Captain **Alonso de Campos** (also given as Alonso del Campo).

**Middle Passage: an honest coordinate gap.** No source records the vessel, its route, or any position during the crossing. The journey file carries one mid-Atlantic stop as a nominal marker and states in `date_confidence` that the pin corresponds to no documented position.

**Slave-market site [R].** Cartagena's market is conventionally placed at what is now the Plaza de los Coches. General historiography supports the location for the city's trade; **it is not attested for Biohó's sale specifically.**

## 3. The first escape

**[A, en.wikipedia]** A boat carrying him on the Magdalena sinks; he escapes in the confusion and is **recaptured**.

**Undated.** Neither Wikipedia edition assigns a year. The commonly quoted **1598 appears in no source** and is used in the journey only as a placeholder between the 1596 sale and the 1599 flight, flagged as such. Coordinates for the sinking are likewise a nominal point on the lower Magdalena; **no source locates the wreck.**

**Gap:** what was done to him after the recapture is nowhere recorded.

## 4. The flight of 1599 and the founding

**[A, Fray Pedro Simón, *Noticias historiales*, Tomo V, Séptima Noticia Historial, cap. XXII, p. 219 (Bogotá: Medardo Rivas, 1892; archive.org `tierrafirmeindias05simbrich`)]** The primary text was reached directly in verification, not only through Wikipedia's quotation of it. Exact wording:

> "AL Gobernador Don Pedro de Acuña sucedió en la ciudad de Cartagena, por los fines del año de 1599 ó principios del de 600, el Gobernador Don Jerónimo de Suazo Casasola, y en estos tiempos comenzó un alzamiento y retiro de ciertos negros cimarrones en aquella ciudad."

**Important correction to a widely circulated quotation.** The form "por fines de 1599 (...) comenzó un alzamiento y retiro de ciertos negros cimarrones" is a stitched ellipsis. Simón writes "por los fines del año de 1599 ó principios del de 600", and that date phrase attaches to **Suazo taking office**, not to the uprising, which Simón dates only as "en estos tiempos." The journey file quotes the second clause verbatim and nothing else.

**[A, EN and ES]** He escapes with a group traditionally numbered around **thirty** and moves into the marsh country southeast of Cartagena.

**[A, es.wikipedia]** Simón's characterisation of the man: "brioso, valiente y atrevido."

**La Matuna: contradiction flagged.** ES "San Basilio de Palenque" places the group's refuge in marshland near what is now the **Canal del Dique** (roughly 10.1 to 10.2 N) and says the community was first called *palenque de la Matuna*. ES "Benkos Biohó" instead puts La Matuna **south of Tolú** (roughly 9.67 N), about 70 km away. **The two Spanish-language articles contradict each other**; the journey pins the northern placement and records the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

**[A, en.wikipedia "San Basilio de Palenque"]** A first Spanish expedition of **twenty armed men** is defeated and its leader **Juan Gómez killed**. The location of the engagement is **not recorded by any source**; the journey uses a nominal Montes de María pin and says so.

**Founding date [R].** The town's own monument cuts **1603**. The documentary record does not date the founding.

**Name of the settlement: contradiction flagged.** EN: "first known as Palenque de San Miguel Arcángel." ES: initially called "palenque de la Matuna." Not reconciled.

## 5. The palenque as a polity

**[A, es.wikipedia "Benkos Biohó"]** Government by named offices: a **captain general, Lorencillo**, a lieutenant of war, a chief constable, a treasurer.

**[A, es.wikipedia]** Raiding targets named: **Cartagena's surroundings, Tolú, Mompós, Tenerife**, for arms, supplies, livestock, and to bring out the enslaved.

**Coordinate correction.** Tenerife, Magdalena is at 9.8989, -74.8589 per ES Wikipedia's own infobox (9°53'56"N 74°51'32"W). A figure of 9.9422, -74.8506 in circulation is about 5 km north of the town and is not used here.

**"Rey del Arcabuco": [R], and later than it looks.** The title is not in EN Wikipedia. In ES it traces to **Camilo Delgado's legend "El Rey del Arcabuco," published in 1913** in *Historia, leyendas y tradiciones de Cartagena*. It is a 20th-century literary coinage that has been absorbed into the historical narrative, not a contemporary or documentary title. The journey uses it and flags the provenance.

**"West African-style palisade fortification": [R], embellishment.** EN says only that the settlement was fortified with palisades, glossing *palenque* as Spanish for a wooden fence. **No source calls the construction West African in style.** Not asserted here.

## 6. The peace

**[A, EN and ES]** Governor **Jerónimo de Suazo y Casasola** opens peace negotiations on **18 July 1605**, offering recognition of the maroons' freedom.

**[R]** The 1605 offer is not implemented at once; conflict and negotiation continue for years. No document from the palenque's side of these negotiations survives; the entire record is the Spanish side's.

**Treaty year: contradiction flagged, three ways.**
- **EN Wikipedia: 1612**, under Governor Diego Fernández de Velasco.
- **ES Wikipedia: 1613**, same governor, same event.
- **Simón himself: "los años de 1612 ó 13."**

Not resolved. The journey pins 1613 and records all three readings.

**Treaty terms: a misattribution corrected.** The requirement that the cimarrones wear Spanish dress and carry a sword "as a mark of free status" is **not a treaty condition in any source.** The Spanish clothing and the gilded dagger come from Simón's description of how Biohó actually comported himself in the city. What EN does record as a condition is that his followers cease harbouring runaways. A further clause said to bar Biohó from using royal titles **appears in neither Wikipedia edition** and is not carried here.

**[A, Simón, Tomo V, cap. XXIII, pp. 223-224]** Verbatim:

> "salieron de una mala paz, que después se vino á hacer mejor y á darles licencia para que entrasen en la ciudad y saliesen de ella con su Capitán Dominguillo, como lo hacían á todas horas, y el Bioho andaba con tanta arrogancia, que demás de andar bien vestido á la española, con espada y daga dorada, trataba su persona como un gran caballero."

Two corrections to the version usually quoted: the licence was **granted by the Spanish** ("á darles licencia"), not held by the cimarrones as of right; and the ellipsis usually printed conceals "de ella con su Capitán Dominguillo."

## 7. Arrest and execution

**[A, EN and ES]** The Spanish break the peace in **1619**, seizing Biohó as he enters the city unguarded (ES: at the city wall). Governor **García Girón** justifies it by the extent of his influence over the African population.

**Custody 1619 to 1621: [R], pure inference.** No source documents a holding site, conditions, a trial, or any event between arrest and execution. This is an interval bounded by two dates and empty in between. Any narrative that fills it is invention.

**[A, EN and ES]** He is **hanged and then quartered on 16 March 1621** by order of García Girón.

**Contradiction flagged, and it is a serious one.** **Simón says Biohó was hanged in 1619**, by García Girón, not 1621. The encyclopaedic date of 16 March 1621 is the one in general circulation, but the earliest narrative source disagrees with it, which also collapses the two-year captivity described above into nothing. Not resolved here. Flagged in the journey's `date_confidence`.

**Execution site: [R].** **Neither Wikipedia edition names the square.** Identifying it as the Plaza Mayor and equating that with the present-day Plaza de la Aduana is unsourced inference. The journey uses the atlas's canonical Cartagena pin and states the site is not attested.

**Fate of the remains: a gap, stated as one.** Quartering existed to be displayed, and display at gates and roads was standard colonial practice. **No source names where Biohó's remains were set, for how long, or what became of them.** He has no known grave.

## 8. The town after him

**[A, en.wikipedia "San Basilio de Palenque"]** **1691**: royal decree guaranteeing the community's freedom on condition it stop harbouring new runaways. The population goes on growing because fugitives go on arriving.

**1713: contradiction flagged.** EN "Benkos Bioho" says San Basilio became the Americas' first free village **by royal decree in 1713**. EN "San Basilio de Palenque" describes the same instrument as an **"Entente Cordiale" signed by Bishop Antonio María Casiani** in January 1713, granting conditional land rights, with a count of **137 houses**. ES dates the decree establishing the **name** San Basilio to **1714**. The nature of the instrument and the exact year are unsettled across the very sources that assert the "first free town" claim.

Also corrected: the assertion that "the residents rejected the agreement's conditions" **appears in no source consulted**; EN says only that runaways kept arriving despite the condition. And the renaming to "San Basilio Magno" is not in the fetched EN text.

**[A, en.wikipedia]** **1772**: the town is incorporated into the jurisdiction of **Mahates**, under continuing restrictions on sheltering maroons.

**Founder attribution: the central contradiction, unresolved.** EN "Benkos Bioho" states there is **no direct documentary relationship** between him and the modern municipality of San Basilio de Palenque, only a palenque of the same tradition in the same hills. ES sources, Colombian popular memory, and the town's own plaza monument, which cuts 1603 into the base, treat him unambiguously as founder. Both positions are carried in the journey; neither is adjudicated.

## 9. Palenque now

**[A, UNESCO ICH element 00102, "Cultural Space of Palenque de San Basilio"]** Palenquero: Spanish lexicon, Bantu grammatical structure (chiefly Kikongo), described as the only such creole in Latin America. Social organisation by **ma kuagro**, age-based groups coordinating collective labour and life-cycle events. UNESCO's own documentation records that the community's distinctiveness draws **ethnic stereotyping and discrimination**, named there as a threat to the practice.

**Citation correction.** **Lumbalú and mapalé are not in the UNESCO element description.** Both are documented in EN and ES Wikipedia "San Basilio de Palenque": lumbalú as a nine-day funerary ceremony summoned by drums, mapalé as a paired dance of rapid hip and shoulder movement. Cited to Wikipedia here, not to UNESCO.

**[A, UNESCO]** Proclaimed a **Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2005**; formally inscribed on the **Representative List in 2008** at the Committee's third session. These are two administrative stages of one recognition, not two events, and sources cite them inconsistently without explaining the distinction.

**Monuments [A, ES].** A statue of Biohó in the town plaza, inscribed 1603; a second statue in **Parque Apolo, Cartagena**. **Gap:** sculptors and unveiling years for both are not established in any source reached, and no coordinates for Parque Apolo were available in the verified pool, so the journey pins it to the canonical Cartagena coordinate and says so.

**Literature.**
- **[A, ES]** Camilo Delgado, "El Rey del Arcabuco," in *Historia, leyendas y tradiciones de Cartagena*, **1913**. Earliest of the three, and the apparent origin of the epithet.
- **[A, ES, corrected]** Germán Espinosa, **"Orika de los palenques" (1991)** is a **short story**, listed under short fiction in his bibliography. It is **not** a novel; Espinosa's 1991 novel was *La tragedia de Belinda Elsner*, which is unrelated. Descriptions of a 1991 Espinosa *novel* about the palenque conflate the two.
- **[A, ES]** Antonio Prada Fortul, *Benkos, las alas de un cimarrón*, **2023**.

## Interlocks

The atlas contains a large number of travellers who passed through Cartagena de Indias: Bolívar (1812, 1830), Humboldt and Bonpland (1801), Sucre (1815), Policarpa Salavarrieta, Garcilaso de la Vega (c.1560), Griselda Blanco (1943), Maradona (2005), Hillary Clinton (2012), Chiachio (2011), Manuela Sáenz (1834), Tschiffely (1928), Alberto Hugo Restrepo (2023). There are also thematic rhymes with Marcus Garvey, Haile Selassie, Columbus, Pizarro and Che Guevara on marooning and Black sovereignty.

**None of these is a relation.** Not one met Biohó, corresponded with him, was influenced by him, cited him, or wrote about him. Shared geography and thematic resonance are not links, and the map already shows co-location. **No atlas traveller is named in any campa of this file.** The one figure who genuinely stands in a documented relation to Biohó is Fray Pedro Simón, who wrote about him; Simón is not an atlas subject, and he is named in the campas as a source rather than as an interlock.
