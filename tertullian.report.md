# Tertullian: research report

*Slug `tertullian`. Written 2026-08-30 from the verified research pool (gather pass plus adversarial verification). Companion: `tertullian.journey.json`, 8 segments, 43 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, convention, or tradition. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is printed and left standing.

The governing fact: the corpus is enormous and the biography nearly empty. Some thirty treatises survive, and from all of them we get a life consisting of Carthage, an unnamed Christian wife, an adult conversion, a writing career of roughly 196 to 212, and a late attachment to the New Prophecy. Everything else is Jerome writing 170 years later, or modern inference from prose style. The journey file keeps that asymmetry visible: the pins cluster in one city because that is the only city any source puts him in.

## 1. Origins and education (c. 155-170 to c. 190)

**Birth at Carthage, c. 155-160 [R].** No year, month, parents, or household in any source; c. 155-160 conventional, c. 150-170 defensible (Barnes). The birthplace rests on **Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 53 [A]**, and on nothing Tertullian says of himself. **The name [R]**: *Quintus Septimius Florens Tertullianus* is a **medieval manuscript-title tradition**, in no contemporary inscription; the file uses the safer *Septimius Tertullianus*.

**The education [R].** Forensic rhetoric, Roman legal vocabulary (*praescriptio* as a working instrument, not a metaphor), Stoic philosophy, Virgil, Cicero, Seneca, and Greek, all inferred from the writing. **No teacher, school, or date is named anywhere.** *Pool inconsistency flagged, not resolved*: its chronology tagged this **[A]** while conceding nothing is documented, its geography tagged the same claim **[R]**. The file follows R.

**Civic background [A for the city, R for the pins].** After Rome, among the largest and richest cities of the Latin West, with courts, temples, imperial cult, baths, theatre, circus, amphitheatre, schools of rhetoric, and a working double harbour (Lancel). Two backdrop stops carry this, both declaring themselves undated settings.

**Scillitan martyrs, 17 July 180 [A].** Twelve Christians tried at Carthage before proconsul Publius Vigellius Saturninus, offered thirty days to recant, then beheaded; the *Acta* is the **earliest precisely dated Latin Christian document**. **No source ties Tertullian to this trial.**

## 2. The wife and the conversion (before c. 196)

**Marriage [A].** *Ad uxorem*, two books to his wife: Book I asks her not to remarry if he dies first, Book II concedes that if she does she should marry only in the Lord. **No name, date, children, or later history**, and whether marriage preceded or followed conversion is unknown [R]. *Citation error caught*: the pool put both clauses in Book I; clause 1 is **I.1**, clause 2 **II.2**, and the file splits them between the marriage stop and the *De monogamia* stop.

**Conversion, c. 193-196 [R].** Dated only backward from the earliest datable works. **No account of event, church, baptizer, motive, or day**, and the martyr-inspired conversion story is a **later inference from *Apologeticum* 50**.

**The Roman jurist [R, probably false].** Eusebius, *HE* II.2, calls him versed in Roman law and distinguished at Rome [A for Eusebius, early 4th c.]. On that sentence older patrologies built a legal career and identified him with the jurist Tertullianus excerpted in Justinian's *Digest*. **Barnes and Rankin regard the identification as disproved or improbable**; no professional record links the two, and the *Digest* excerpts were never located. The file keeps a Roman Forum stop as a *documented legend*, tagged R.

**Negative finding, load-bearing [A].** No source places him in Rome, Greece, or Asia Minor in person; his knowledge of Roman church affairs must be framed as correspondence or traveller's report, and every Rome pin in the file is textual reach and says so.

## 3. First publications (c. 196-198)

***Ad martyras*** [A], to imprisoned Christians, before 197; prisoners unnamed, prison location unrecorded. ***Ad nationes*** [A], **after 19 February 197** by its own reference to Severus's defeat of Clodius Albinus as past. ***Apologeticum*** [A], later in 197, to the provincial governors, arguing condemnation for the name alone without lawful inquiry; opening verified. ***De testimonio animae*** [A], c. 197-198.

**The seed sentence [A, paraphrase flagged].** *Apologeticum* 50.13: "semen est sanguis Christianorum." The familiar "the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church" **supplies both "martyrs" and "Church"**, neither in the Latin. The file quotes the real sentence.

## 4. Rule, water, and arena (c. 198-206)

***De praescriptione haereticorum*** [A], ch. 7 verified verbatim. ***De baptismo*** [A], earliest surviving Latin treatise on baptism, whose recommended **delay** for small children is evidence that infant baptism was already being asked for. ***De cultu feminarum*** [A for text, R for date, disputed between "before 197" and the 198-203 cluster]: "You are the devil's gateway" verified, while **"a temple built over a sewer" is NOT securely located in the extant corpus** and is excluded from the file. ***De patientia*** [A], the only place in the corpus where he characterizes his own temperament; **truncation caught**, the pool cutting at "must of necessity sigh," where the sentence runs on, "sigh after... that health of patience which I possess not." ***De spectaculis*** [A], whose ch. 30 Last Judgement is eschatological fantasy, not evidence of any act. ***Adversus Iudaeos*** [A for the work, R for the date]: chs. 9-14 overlap *Adversus Marcionem* III so heavily that the second half's authenticity has long been doubted, **Dunn (2008) defending the whole as unified**; open. Its staged opening dispute is of contested historicity and no Jewish interlocutor is named.

**Perpetua and Felicity, 203 [A for the martyrdom, R for the day and the editorship].** Killed at the Carthage amphitheatre with Revocatus, Saturninus, Secundulus, and Saturus at military games for Caesar Geta's birthday. **"7 March" is the martyrological feast date, not a date in the *Passio*** (sources give only "c. 203"), his long-proposed editorship is **no longer regarded as established**, and **no personal meeting is documented.**

## 5. The New Prophecy (c. 204-212)

**Affiliation [A for the shift, R for its mechanics].** From c. 204, works with Montanist sympathies, including the lost *De ecstasi*, *De spe fidelium*, *De paradiso*. **No text records a ceremony of joining, an excommunication, or any self-description as "Montanist."** It shows only in changed rhetoric, the Paraclete cited as authority, and *psychici* as a term of contempt. **Pepouza [R]** is in the file as the movement's origin and explicitly not a place he reached. *Error caught*: the pool gave 38.35 / 29.75, "near Karayakup/Suhut," where Tabbernee and Lampe's site is **38.40806 / 29.46139**, near **Karayakuplu, Karahalli district, Usak province**; Suhut is in another province entirely and has been dropped.

***Adversus Marcionem*** [A], five books, dated by internal reference to Severus's fifteenth year, 207-208, and **his third version**, an earlier draft having been stolen and circulated uncorrected; Marcion had been dead some fifty years, so this attacks a legacy, not a rival. ***De anima*** [A], c. 208-212: the soul corporeal and traducianist, and **ch. 9 reports an unnamed woman of his congregation receiving visions during worship**, testimony he records and then uses to confirm his own theory. That chapter was never retrieved (the URL returned *Adversus Hermogenem*), so the content follows Waszink. ***De carne Christi*** and ***De resurrectione carnis*** [A], against docetic and Gnostic positions. ***De corona*** [A], c. 211, on the soldier who refuses the ceremonial crown and is detained while **other Christians call him rash**; the camp is unnamed, and the file's coordinate is a generic African placement that says so.

**"Credo quia absurdum" [A as a documented misquotation].** He never wrote it. *De carne Christi* 5 reads "credibile est, quia ineptum est" and "certum est, quia impossibile." **Browne reworded it in 1643; Voltaire recast it in 1767 and attributed his own version to Augustine.**

## 6. Ad Scapulam and the trinitarian vocabulary (212-213)

***Ad Scapulam*** [A], to the proconsul of Africa at Carthage; his title, the free-will passage, and the darkened sun over Utica all verified. **The conventional date, after the solar eclipse of 14 August 212, rests on tertullian.org's chronology and a modern back-calculation; the passage gives no calendar date**, and neither pass confirmed the eclipse from a primary source. *Coordinate error caught*: Utica given as 37.0833 / 10.0667, actual **37.05694 / 10.06194**.

***Adversus Praxean*** [A]. Father, Son, and Spirit as three *personae* "of one substance, and of one condition, and of one power" (ch. 2, verified): the **earliest surviving major Latin deployment of *trinitas***, with *persona*, *substantia*, *oeconomia*. Two hedges: it is **pre-Nicene**, with subordinating language, and must not be read as the Nicene settlement; and *una substantia, tres personae* does **not** appear verbatim there. Ch. 1 also reports that **a bishop of Rome had begun acknowledging the Phrygian prophets and sent letters of peace to Asia and Phrygia, until Praxeas pressed accusations and the letter was recalled** [A]. The bishop is unnamed, Eleutherus and Victor both proposed [R], and how the story reached Carthage is unrecorded.

## 7. The rigorist close, and the vanishing (after c. 212)

*De fuga in persecutione*, *De virginibus velandis*, *De monogamia*, *De ieiunio*, *De pudicitia*: all [A] as works, **none securely dated.** *De monogamia* hardens against *Ad uxorem*'s own counsel, treating remarriage after a spouse's death as fornication. ***De pudicitia*** [A for the text, unresolved for date and target] quotes and mocks a prelate's "peremptory edict" absolving adultery and fornication after penance, calling him *pontifex maximus* and *episcopus episcoporum*.

> **Source misattribution caught, left as a live contradiction.** The pool asserted "per tertullian.org/chronology.htm giving 222-223" and dated the work 222. **That page does not give 222-223**: it groups *De pudicitia* with the other post-breakdown treatises "around 213AD." Other reference works give 213-223. The file dates it 0213-12-01 **to match the cited source** and states the disagreement in `date_confidence`. The target, **Callistus of Rome (c. 217-222) or an unnamed Carthaginian bishop** (Monceaux), is unresolved and **materially affects both date and place**.

**The end [R, and thin].** No source records his last residence, health, office, trial, imprisonment, or death. **No tomb, epitaph, relic, shrine, feast day, or cult**; he is in neither the Roman Martyrology nor the Orthodox calendars, and c. 220, c. 225, c. 240 are estimates. **Barnes proposes death soon after 212, possibly as a martyr; no martyr act, cult, tomb, feast, or ancient witness supports it.** *Source failure caught*: the pool's sainthood claim cited the Catholic Encyclopedia entry, which **says nothing about sainthood or calendars** and asserts the opposite of part of the framing ("He became a priest, no doubt of the Church of Carthage"), so the file states the absence without hanging it on that page.

## 8. Afterlife

**Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 53, c. 392-393 [A as testimony, R as biography].** Presbyter until middle life; driven out by "the envy and reproaches of the clergy of the Roman church"; lapsed to Montanus; "is said to have lived to a very old age," which **marks his own hearsay as hearsay**. The presbyter claim is in tension with the lay standpoint of the late works and the silence of every African source; not adjudicated. *Series corrected*: the translation is **NPNF second series vol. 3**, not ANF, where Jerome does not appear.

**Cyprian's "da magistrum" [A as Jerome's report, R as fact].** An oral chain (an old Italian named Paul, who claimed to have met Cyprian's secretary at Rome) has Cyprian reading Tertullian daily and asking for the books by that phrase. **Cyprian never names him in any surviving work of his own.**

**Augustine, *De haeresibus* 86, c. 428 [A].** The Tertullianists at Carthage had dwindled, converted, and surrendered their basilica. Augustine adds that Tertullian broke with the church over Cataphrygian views and then with the Montanists too, gathering his own congregations; **that last is uncorroborated by any earlier source.**

**Manuscripts [A].** Five families: Cluniacensis, Corbeiensis, Trecensis, Agobardinus, Ottobonianus, of which the **Corbie group is lost**, surviving only in readings preserved by early printed editions. The **Codex Agobardinus** (BnF Latin 1622), 9th c., 204 folios, given by Agobard to the cathedral of Saint Stephen at Lyon, breaks off in *De carne Christi* ch. 10 and is unique witness for certain texts and titles. *Corrections*: tertullian.org prints "Agobard of Lyons (814-840)" but **he succeeded in 816**; the pool's "sole witness to several works, some now lost" is self-contradictory; the pin moved to **BnF Richelieu**.

**Rediscovery and print [A].** Poggio finds a manuscript around St Gall, 1416-17; Niccoli acquires an *Apologeticum* in 1424, transcribed by Traversari; **Benalio prints it at Venice before 1493** (the pool wrongly called that printing undated in its own source) and **Scinzenzeler at Milan in 1494**; **Rhenanus's editio princeps, Basel, 1521.** Then the **1584 Thevet engraving** [A]: **no ancient portrait, description, or tomb image survives.** *Fabricated quotation caught*: the pool's gloss "historical representation rather than contemporary evidence" **is not on the Wikipedia page**, which carries only a caption, and is nowhere quoted in the journey file.

**Modern scholarship [A].** Barnes (1971, rev. 1985) dismantled the Jerome-derived biography and compressed the chronology. Standard Latin text CCSL; standard English ANF vols. 3-4 (Thelwall and **Peter** Holmes, not "Philip" as Wikipedia has it). *Corrected*: the article gives **no CCSL volume numbers and no 1954 or 1885 dates**, which are correct but need another source.

## Contradictions carried, not resolved

**Whole-corpus chronology**: Barnes ends his activity c. 212, the standard chronology extends some works to 222-223, and every post-212 stop carries this. ***De pudicitia*'s date and target**: 213 (tertullian.org's grouping) vs 222-223 (the pool's claim, unsupported by the page cited) vs 213-223; Callistus of Rome vs a Carthaginian bishop. **Presbyter or layman**: Jerome says presbyter, the late works speak as a layman, the Catholic Encyclopedia says priest, and no African source attests an ordination. **Education tagging**: [A] in the pool's chronology, [R] in its geography, for one claim. ***Adversus Iudaeos* chs. 9-14**: unfinished, composite, or integral. **The Roman bishop of *Adversus Praxean* 1**: Eleutherus or Victor. **Perpetua's date**: "c. 203" in the sources, "7 March" a feast date.

## Honest gaps

No birth year, death date, death place, or cause; Carthage as deathplace is presumption from last-documented-setting. No teacher, school, or documented lawsuit, so the "lawyer" reading is stylistic inference. No wife's name, children, or household. No conversion narrative by anyone, including himself. No ceremony, date, or document for the Montanist affiliation, and no excommunication on record. No portrait, description, relic, tomb, or feast. Lost works: *De ecstasi*, *De spe fidelium*, *De paradiso*, and others known only from ancient lists. Primary texts neither pass could retrieve: *De anima* 9; *De carne Christi* 5 in context; the *Ad Scapulam* eclipse as a dated event; the *Digest* excerpts; Hippolytus, *Refutatio* 9.7.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Tertullian is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`**, so relations not rooted in his own life are kept and flagged here rather than deleted in silence, with the direction named. **Backward-direction apparatus relations: none found**, since he has no framework being applied backward to material he never treated; every stop is his own act, his own text, or a labelled civic backdrop at his own pin. **Forward-direction relations: none**, and nothing has been written into any earlier subject's file.

**Later-hands relations are kept as object-stops rather than other people's scenes.** Four closing stops concern events after his death (the Codex Agobardinus, the Basel *editio princeps*, the Thevet engraving, Augustine on the Tertullianists), each staged as **his own object or his own reception, at the pin the object occupies**, on the pattern of an artist's stop for the thing they made. Three decisions the operator may want to reverse: **Poggio's 1416-17 St Gall find got no stop**, being Poggio's travel, and is folded into the Basel campa (it could be pinned at 47.4245 / 9.3767); **the Catacomb of Callixtus was dropped** as doubly speculative by the pool's own account, with a "seat of the bishop Callistus" stop whose coordinate was in any case the Roman Forum; and **no Oxford stop was made for Barnes**, whose reconstruction is carried in `date_confidence` instead.

## Interlocks

**Named in a campa (direct or one-directional textual relation):**

- **`apostle_peter`, `apostle_paul`.** *De praescriptione* 36 grounds Rome's apostolic authority in Peter's crucifixion and Paul's beheading there, and he argues from Paul throughout (*De baptismo* 17; *Ad uxorem* II.2). **The Rome pin 41.9028 / 12.4964 is inherited byte-identical** from both files, and the stop states that his reach to Rome is citation, not travel. *Caveat*: attributing that argument to ch. 36 is [R], not refetched.
- **`saint_augustine_of_hippo`.** Augustine wrote substantively about Tertullian in *De haeresibus* 86, a one-directional relation across two centuries. Named at the Tertullianist-basilica stop as its witness, and once where Voltaire misattributes *credo quia absurdum* to him. **No Augustine scene is staged**; the pin is Carthage.
- **`justinian_i`.** The *Digest*, closed against commentary in 533, preserves a jurist named Tertullianus, and his identification with the theologian is a documented, now rejected tradition **about Tertullian**. Named once, at the Roman Forum, tagged R.

**Dropped as shared geography or theme only, and named in no campa:** **`hannibal`** and **`dido`**, same city four to ten centuries apart, which is continuity and not relation (the Byrsa pin **36.8528 / 10.3233** from `hannibal.journey.json` is nonetheless **inherited byte-identical** for the birth, tribunal, Ad Scapulam, De pudicitia, and death stops, and **36.8522 / 10.3239** from the interlock lens for the general-Carthage stops); **`flavius_josephus`**, thematic only, dead before Tertullian's birth; and **`simon_magus`**, where the heresiological genre link was flagged weak with **no Tertullian citation independently fetched** (if one surfaces, e.g. *De anima* 34, it belongs on the *De praescriptione* stop).

**Adjacent-subject gaps (no file yet):** **Cyprian of Carthage** (d. 258), his most consequential reader; **Perpetua** (d. 203), killed in his own city within his career; **Origen**, queued directly beneath him. Marcion, Praxeas, Montanus, Prisca, Maximilla, Scapula, and Callistus appear in campas as historical persons; none is an atlas traveler, so none is an interlock.
