# Torcuato de Alvear (1822-1890): research report

*Slug `torcuato_de_alvear`. First intendente municipal of Buenos Aires, 1883-1887; president of the Comisión Municipal 1880-1883. Son of General Carlos María de Alvear (atlas slug `alvear`), father of President Marcelo T. de Alvear.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named on the same line · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or inference. Contradictions are printed as contradictions and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason in §10.

**The shape of the evidence.** A thin-record subject with a dense middle. Nothing is attested of the first thirty-two years beyond the birth and the parentage, nothing of the twenty-six years between marriage and appointment, nothing of the three and a half years between the end of the term and the death. What *is* attested, in several places to the day, is a seven-year run of municipal acts from December 1880 to May 1887. The life as recoverable is an administration with a man attached to it. No dedicated biography was reachable (see §10). Both Wikipedia articles were; the English one is a stub.

---

## 1. Birth and family (1822)

- Born in **Montevideo**, then capital of the Provincia Cisplatina under Portuguese Brazil, fifth son of **General Carlos María de Alvear** and **Carmen de la Quintanilla**. **[A: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torcuato_de_Alvear]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved: the birth date.** es.wiki gives **21 April 1822**; en.wiki gives **12 April 1822**. Both agree on the year and the city. The journey file uses the es.wiki date for placement and records the conflict in `date_confidence`. **[A on both sides; the disagreement is the finding]**
- **Name.** es.wiki *Carlos María de Alvear* lists the son as **Torcuato Antonio de Alvear**; "Torcuato María" survives chiefly in the monument article's title. Prose here uses **Torcuato Antonio**, the monument keeps its own. **[A: es.wiki]**
- The parents had **ten children**, among them María Carlota, Emilio Marcelo, Camilo Urbelino, Diego, Juan Nepomuceno, León Gabino and Torcuato. **[A: es.wiki, Carlos María de Alvear]**
- The brother **Diego de Alvear** was founding president of the Club del Progreso in 1852. **[A: es.wiki Club del Progreso]** This matters below.
- The father's 1822 restoration of rank and property is attested; the family's consequent move to Buenos Aires with the infant Torcuato is **[R]**, inferred from the father's movements and placed by no document.

## 2. The first gap: 1822 to 1854

**No source documents anything at all** between the birth and the marriage: no schooling, no tutor, no travel, no first employment. **[R: gap]** Both Wikipedia articles jump straight from one to the other.

- The father was minister plenipotentiary to the United States. **Correction:** the pool said "from 1832"; es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_María_de_Alvear gives **two** missions, **23 December 1823 - 22 September 1824** and **30 June 1837 - 3 November 1852**. The long Washington posting is **1837-1852**. **[A: es.wiki, Carlos María de Alvear]**
- Whether Torcuato, aged fifteen to thirty, went north with him is **unattested in either direction**. The journey file's Washington stop is tagged as a reconstruction of possible family movement, not a claim that he was there. **[R: gap, flagged in `date_confidence`]**
- The father died in **New York, 3 November 1852**. **[A: es.wiki, Carlos María de Alvear]** Torcuato's presence or absence at that death is not recorded. **[R: gap]**

## 3. Marriage, children, and the second gap (1854-1880)

- Married **Elvira (María Elvira Dolores) Pacheco y Reinoso**, daughter of **General Ángel Pacheco**, in **1854**. **[A: es.wiki Torcuato de Alvear and Marcelo T. de Alvear]** Neither the day nor the church is given by any source; the city is inferred. **[R for place and day]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved: the number of children.** The pool asserted "nine children," citing en.wiki. That citation is false: the English article is a stub with no marriage or children data at all. The reachable sources give **two counts**:
  - **Ten**, each named individually in es.wiki *Torcuato de Alvear* (María del Carmen, Ángel Torcuato, Carlos Torcuato, Rómulo Torcuato Emilio, Torcuato Jr., Dolores Elvira, Leandro Urbelino Torcuato, Marcelo Torcuato, Luisa Elvira Carmen Dolores, Luisa).
  - **Seven**, three dead in infancy and four surviving (Carmen, Ángel, Carlos, Marcelo), in es.wiki *Marcelo T. de Alvear*.
  
  Both printed, neither adopted. "Nine" is in no reachable source and is used nowhere.
- **Marcelo Torcuato de Alvear** born **4 October 1868**, later **president 1922-1928**. **[A: es.wiki Marcelo T. de Alvear]** The residence address is unrecorded; the journey file's Barrio Norte coordinate is flagged as a reconstruction of location. **[R for coordinate]**
- **The second gap.** No source consulted documents any military service, legislature seat, diplomatic post or commercial office between 1854 and 1880. A Sociedad Rural Argentina founders list was checked and **does not include him**. **[R: gap]**
  - **Correction:** the pool claimed the English article "explicitly flags this as an omission." It does not; it is a stub containing nothing between the birth and the mayoralty. The gap is real, the self-flagging is not, and it is dropped.
- **Club del Progreso.** No source confirms Torcuato's membership; the searches that might have listed it all failed (see §10). The inference is stronger than a generic class inference, because the club's **founding president in 1852 was Diego de Alvear, his own brother**. **[R: inference; the brother's presidency is [A: es.wiki]]**
  - **Correction:** the pool placed the club at **Perú 375**, which matches none of its four seats. The only one in Alvear's adult lifetime is the **Palacio Muñoa at Perú y Victoria, 1857-1900**; the journey file uses it, at approximately **-34.6094, -58.3729**.
- **Freemasonry.** Recorded as a Freemason and a member of the **Partido Autonomista Nacional**. **[A: en.wiki Torcuato de Alvear]** No lodge, city or date for his own Masonic activity. **[R: gap]**
  - **Correction:** the pool tied this to a father "lodge-founder in Cádiz and London." Per es.wiki the father's stations are **Cádiz (Logia de los Racionales No. 3, c. 1810)** and the **Lautaro Lodge in Buenos Aires after 1812**; London is not in that article, and is dropped. Too thin to carry a stop, so the journey file makes no Masonic claim in prose.

## 4. The federalized city and the Comisión Municipal (1880-1882)

- **Buenos Aires federalized as national capital, 1880**, separated from the province. **[A: es.wiki Plaza Intendente Alvear; corroborated by en.wiki]** No month or day in the pool; the journey file defaults to January 1 and says so.
- **Proclaimed president of the Comisión Municipal, 4 December 1880.** **[A: en.wiki Torcuato de Alvear]**
- **Correction (it had propagated).** One lens gave his term as "intendente 1880-1887," conflating two offices. Per es.wiki and *Anexo:Intendentes*: **Comisión Municipal, 4 December 1880 - 10 May 1883**, then **intendente, 10 May 1883 - 10 May 1887**. No intendencia existed before the 1882 Ley Orgánica.
- **Recoleta cemetery commissioned.** As head of the Comisión he commissions **Juan Antonio Buschiazzo** to remodel the Cementerio de la Recoleta, **1880**. **[A: es.wiki Cementerio de la Recoleta]** The journey file places it in December as a declared placeholder, since he took that post on the 4th.
- **Works completed 1881**: brick perimeter wall, paved internal streets, neoclassical portico of **four Greek Doric columns**, *Requiescant in pace* outside and *Expectamus Dominum* inside, eleven allegorical symbols. The entrance plaque records **1822**, **1881** and **2003**. **[A: es.wiki Cementerio de la Recoleta]**
  - **Correction:** a lens gave the wall "English-bond joints," a detail in no consulted source; cut as invention. The same lens dated the commission 1881; commission 1880, completion 1881.
- **The Avenida de Mayo petition, 1882.** He petitions Interior Minister **Bernardo de Irigoyen** to have the projected avenue declared of public utility, so the properties in its line can be expropriated. **[A: es.wiki Avenida de Mayo]**

## 5. The intendencia (1883-1887)

- **Appointed first intendente municipal by President Julio Argentino Roca, 10 May 1883**, under the 1882 Ley Orgánica. **[A: en.wiki and es.wiki Torcuato de Alvear; es.wiki Anexo:Intendentes]**
  - **Correction:** the pool's venue, the "Casa de Gobierno," is anachronistic. Per es.wiki *Casa Rosada*, in May 1883 **Åberg's Casa de Gobierno (1882)** and **Kihlberg's Palacio de Correos (1878)** stood apart; **Tamburini's arch began in 1884**, the building inaugurated **1898**.
- **The Recova Vieja.** Built **1802-1804** by **Juan Bautista Segismundo**, dividing the Plaza de la Victoria from the Plaza 25 de Mayo. Demolished **in two stages by Alvear's order**: the **central arch from 9 August 1883, over eight days**; the **Anchorena-owned wings, 8 to 14 May 1884**, once expropriation concluded. **[A: es.wiki Recova Vieja]**
  - **Correction, twice:** both lenses dated this **25 May 1883**. No such demolition exists; replaced with the August 1883 / May 1884 sequence.
- **Hospital San Roque** inaugurated **12 August 1883**, by **Buschiazzo**, at **Urquiza 609, Balvanera**, on provisional foundations from the **1868 cholera epidemic**; renamed **Ramos Mejía in 1914**. es.wiki calls it *"el demorado Hospital San Roque"*: the delayed hospital. **[A: es.wiki Torcuato de Alvear and Hospital Ramos Mejía]**
  - **Correction:** the pool cited en.wiki (which lacks this) and a coordinate **1.1 km off**. Corrected to **-34.61766, -58.40961**.
- **Paseo / Parque de la Recoleta** laid out as a formal promenade during his tenure. **[A: es.wiki Plaza Intendente Alvear]** No founding date given; the journey file uses a declared 1883 placeholder.
- **Registro Civil created, 1884**, with guidance from **Dr Guillermo Rawson**. **[A: es.wiki Torcuato de Alvear]** The pool cited en.wiki, which mentions neither; reassigned. A dedicated civil-registry article returned **404**, so this rests on es.wiki alone. **[A, single-sourced]**
- **Law No. 1,583, 31 October 1884**, authorizing an avenue **at least 30 metres wide** from Plaza de Mayo toward the future Plaza del Congreso. Of **115 affected properties, 85** were donated in exchange for tax exemptions. **[A: es.wiki Avenida de Mayo]** *Open point:* the pool dates the law 31 October, some standard accounts 16 October. Flagged, not resolved.
- **Infrastructure.** Paved streets, water supply, electric lighting, transport and street lighting expanded across the term. **[A: en.wiki]** No single dated event; the waterworks coordinate is approximate.
- **Municipal territory enlarged**, annexing provincial land; the boundary then drawn was materialized only decades later as the **Avenida General Paz**, from **late 1936**. **[R for the attribution to his tenure; the General Paz dating is [A]: es.wiki]**
- **Parks: one identification plausible, one false, one excluded.**
  - **Parque Agronómico.** **[A: en.wiki]** for the attribution; the identification with the later **Facultad de Agronomía (UBA)** grounds is **[R]**, plausible only, since that faculty traces its site to **Roca's 1901 "Parque del Oeste"** authorization and a **1904 institute**, naming **no 1880s Parque Agronómico**. Coordinate corrected to **-34.5936, -58.4840**, barrio to **Agronomía**.
  - **Parque de la Convalecencia.** The pool identified this with the **Parque Rivadavia** in Caballito. **Wrong.** Per es.wiki *Parque Rivadavia*, that ground was **Plaza Lezica**, expropriated after 1908 and made a plaza by **Ordinance 2702 of 10 July 1928**. Alvear's park took its name from the **Convalecencia hospital lands** in the south (the later Hospicio de las Mercedes area). The journey file reduces the stop to "the park south of the city," marks the coordinate approximate, and names the false identification to kill it.
  - **Parque Centenario: excluded.** The repeated claim that Alvear created it is anachronistic: that park belongs to the **1910 centennial**. It is nowhere in the journey file.
- **The captured British colours** of **1806-1807**, including that of the **71st of the line**, were framed and preserved under his administration. **[A: Calzadilla, ch. V, 1891]** No date given; the journey file uses a flagged placeholder inside the term.
- **Term ends 10 May 1887**, four years to the day. **[A: en.wiki; es.wiki Anexo:Intendentes]** **Antonio F. Crespo** installed **14 May 1887**. **[A: es.wiki Anexo:Intendentes]**

## 6. The third gap, the voyage, and the death (1887-1890)

- **No source documents any public role** in the three and a half years between the end of the intendencia and his death. **[R: gap]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved: where he died.** es.wiki *Avenida de Mayo* has him dying **during a return voyage from Europe**; en.wiki has him dying **in Buenos Aires**; es.wiki *Torcuato de Alvear* says only *falleció en 1890*, with no place. All three agree on the **date, 8 December 1890**, and the **age, 68**. The journey file carries **both** accounts as separate stops, each naming the other, rather than silently choosing. No ship, port or itinerary was recoverable; the mid-ocean coordinate is a declared placeholder, and the Paris stop a convention, not an attested destination.
- **Correction:** the pool said the 1894 inauguration came "eight years after Alvear's death." **9 July 1894 is four years after 8 December 1890.** Corrected everywhere.

## 7. Afterlife (1890-1922)

- **Buried in the Cementerio de la Recoleta**, the ground he had walled, paved and gated a decade earlier. **[A: en.wiki]** The **burial day is given by no source [R: gap]**; the journey file uses the day after death as a declared placeholder.
- **The promenade renamed Plaza Intendente Alvear**, 1890. **[A: es.wiki Plaza Intendente Alvear]** Coordinate corrected from -34.5885,-58.3925 to **-34.5858, -58.3909**, about 330 m northeast.
- **Monument, 1900**, sculptors **A. Jons and Juan Lauer**: a **Carrara-marble Doric column** with a bronze *La Gloria* and a bust, and **three reliefs** showing the opening of the Avenida de Mayo, the paving of the city, and the Recoleta works. **[A: es.wiki Monumento al Intendente...]** The pool cited en.wiki; reassigned.
- **Family mausoleum, 1905**, by **Alejandro Christophersen** (slug `alejandro_christophersen`), holding Torcuato and his father **Carlos María de Alvear, repatriated from New York**. **[A: en.wiki; es.wiki Carlos María de Alvear]** The date and circumstances of that repatriation are given by no source. **[R: gap]**
- **Avenida de Mayo inaugurated 9 July 1894** under **Federico Pinedo** (in office 20 June 1893 - 20 August 1894), preceded by the demolition from **25 May 1888** of the oldest buildings on the Plaza de Mayo, the Casa de Policía and part of the **Cabildo** among them. **[A: es.wiki Avenida de Mayo and Anexo:Intendentes]**
- **Marcelo T. de Alvear president 1922-1928.** **[A: es.wiki Marcelo T. de Alvear]** No inauguration day in the pool; standard accounts say October 1922, and the journey file defaults and says so.
- **No literary corpus** and **no legend cycle.** No published editions, translations or collected writings by him were located, and no posthumous miracle, ghost or folk stories attached to his tomb, in contrast to the cycle around other Recoleta occupants such as Rufina Cambaceres. **[R: gaps]**

## 8. Quotations

The pool yielded **exactly two** usable quotations, both **about** Alvear rather than by him, and both from one contemporary chronicle. There are **no words of his own** in any reachable source, which is why the journey file carries two quotes against a corpus norm of about twelve.

- **Santiago Calzadilla, *Las Beldades de mi Tiempo*** (Buenos Aires: Casa Vaccaro, 1891; 1919 digitized ed. on es.wikisource.org), fetched and read directly.
  - **Ch. XV**, on the Recoleta quarter: *"Uno de los grandes servicios que se debe al mando superior en la Municipalidad, del Intendente señor don Torcuato de Alvear, es la completa transformación de aquel barrio."* Writing the year after Alvear's death, Calzadilla credits him with making the quarter *"el barrio higienico por excelencia."* **[A]**
  - **Ch. V**, on the British colours: *"sus banderas, entre las que estaba la del 71 de línea, que hoy se guardan colocadas en lujosos cuadros, merced al Intendente don Torcuato Alvear."* **[A]**

Both appear in the journey file with own translations and full attribution.

## 9. Atlas relations named in the journey prose

Three existing atlas travelers are named, each with a direct personal relation:

- **`alvear`** (Carlos María de Alvear): **father**. Named at the birth, the legation, the New York death, and the 1905 mausoleum that holds them both.
- **`julio_argentino_roca`**: **appointed him** first intendente, 10 May 1883. Named at the appointment.
- **`alejandro_christophersen`**: **designed the Alvear mausoleum** in which Torcuato lies. Named at the 1905 stop.

**Rejected on the shared-geography test:** Mario Palanti, Isabel and Eva Perón, Sarmiento, Rosas, Marta Ezcurra, Remedios de Escalada, János Czetz, Leloir, Victoria Ocampo, Matías Carbone, Schavelzon, Troiani, Leo Chiachio. The interlock lens offered every one as a Recoleta or Avenida de Mayo co-location; none has a documented relation to Alvear, and none is named.

Buschiazzo, Ángel Pacheco, Irigoyen, Rawson, Pinedo, Crespo, Diego and Marcelo T. de Alvear are named as actors in Alvear's own story; none has a journey file.
