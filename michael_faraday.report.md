# Michael Faraday (1791-1867): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-29 for `michael_faraday.journey.json`. 45 stops, 8 segments, calendar gregorian.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a pool claim no
fetched source confirms. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**; unreachable sources
are named with the reason. The file's hedges live in `date_confidence`, never in the prose.

---

## 1. Newington Butts to Blandford Street (1791-1812)

- **[A]** Born 22 September 1791, Newington Butts, now Southwark; third of four children of a
  journeyman blacksmith (rigb.org). Elementary schooling only, no grammar school and no university.
  **Gap:** no street address survives; the pin (51.4959, -0.0996) marks the parish. **[R]** The
  father's northern origin is carried by no fetched source, so the campa says only "come south
  for work".
- **[R]** Jacob's Well Mews, Marylebone, and the loaf divided into fourteen portions. The address
  appears in **neither** Wikipedia, the RI page, nor the DNB; the bread is early testimony, not an
  audited record, and the campa says so. **Contradiction, carried:** the pool's chronology dates
  the move **1796**, its geography the residence **c.1791-1804**; stop dated 1796-01-01.
  **Coordinates corrected:** the pool's 51.5171/-0.1533 sits ~150 m west, so the file uses
  **51.5169/-0.1512**.
- **[A]** Errand boy from c.1804, apprenticed to George Riebau for seven years **7 October 1805**
  (rigb.org). **Contradiction, carried and NOT resolved:** the chronology lens says **2** Blandford
  Street, the geography lens and **English Heritage's blue-plaque page** say **48**. 48 is the only
  number a fetched source supports, and the stop name carries **no number at all**.
- **[R]** John Tatum's City Philosophical Society, c.1810. **No verified meeting address was
  reached**; the pin (51.5145, -0.105) is an admitted central-London placeholder.
- **[A] Date corrected.** The pool dated the Abbott correspondence from **1811**; Epsilon
  (fetched) makes the first Faraday-to-Abbott letter **12-13 July 1812** (Faraday0003), Faraday0001
  (c.1811) being to T. Huxtable. **[A]** William Dance's tickets to Davy's last four lectures,
  1812, and the bound manuscript of ~300 pages sent to Davy; **gap:** no month, 1812-02-01 orders.

## 2. Chemical Assistant (1813)

- **[A]** Appointed **1 March 1813**, the vacancy arising from an assistant dismissed for fighting
  (rigb.org). **[R]** The twenty-five shillings, the candles and fuel are standard biography,
  itemised in no source fetched here; the campa keeps only "rooms at the top of the house".
- **[A]** The rules for a lecturer, verbatim from **Tyndall, DNB vol. 18 (1889)**. **Framing
  corrected:** the pool called this written "before he held any post at the Royal Institution", but
  Tyndall places the letters **after** Faraday joined Davy in the laboratory in March 1813. The
  false framing is cut.

## 3. The Continental tour (October 1813 to April 1815)

- **[A] Place corrected.** The pool gave "Dover to the Continent". The DNB gives departure from
  London **13 October 1813**, and the party sailed **by cartel from Plymouth to Morlaix** under a
  Napoleonic passport (Wikipedia, 'Humphry Davy'). Stop is Plymouth, 50.3714/-4.1422.
- **[A/R] Meetings reassigned.** The pool listed Ampère, Gay-Lussac, Humboldt and Volta together
  at "Paris, Geneva, Milan (various)". Per Wikipedia, 'Humphry Davy': **Ampère and Gay-Lussac at
  Paris**, late 1813 **[A]**, carried at the Institut de France pin 48.8567/2.3376; **Volta at
  Milan, June 1814** **[A]**, carried and **moved off Geneva**, where the pool wrongly put him;
  **de Candolle dropped**, since he held a chair at Montpellier until 1816. **Humboldt: not
  asserted, and flagged.** No fetched source places him at Geneva with the party, and none confirms
  a Paris meeting. `humboldt.journey.json` exists, so this is a live open question and the Paris
  stop carries it as a `suggested_ref`. **Not silently deleted.**
- **[A] Instrument corrected.** Florence: the diamond combustion used **the Grand Duke of
  Tuscany's great burning lens**, Faraday assisting. The pool's "Accademia del Cimento apparatus"
  is wrong; that body had been defunct since 1667.
- **[A]** Auguste de la Rive at Geneva and the lifelong friendship: the one element of the pool's
  Geneva stop that survives verification, so the stop is rebuilt around it, with the 1814 Geneva
  letter to Abbott on the "clouds, which intervened between me and the sun of prosperity".
- **[R] and demoted.** Montpellier, Nice, Genoa, Rome, Naples rest wholly on Bowers and Symons
  (1991), **not fetched** (see Sources); rather than five thin stops on one unfetched secondary
  source they are named inside the Florence and Milan campas. Faraday's resentment at Jane Davy's
  treatment of him is literature only and was cut with the Rome stop.

## 4. The chemist of Albemarle Street (1815-1825)

- **[A]** First paper 1816, 'Analysis of Native Caustic Lime of Tuscany'; work under W. T. Brande;
  alloy steels with James Stodart, 1815-1820. **Gap:** no month; defaulted to 01. **[A]** Married
  Sarah Barnard **12 June 1821** (rigb.org). Two corrections:
  (1) the pool collapsed wedding and confession of faith onto one date, where Wikipedia and the
  DNB both put the confession **a month after** the marriage (usually 15 July); separated in the
  campa. (2) **Venue doubt, flagged not resolved:** under the **Marriage Act 1753**, in force to
  1836, a Sandemanian meeting house could not solemnise a valid marriage, yet the pool staged the
  wedding at Paul's Alley. The file keeps that pin, where the congregation met and where the
  confession happened, but **does not assert** the wedding took place there.
- **[A] Title corrected.** 1821: **Assistant Superintendent of the House** (RI's own page). The
  pool's "Superintendent of the House and Laboratory" is a later title. **[A]** Continuous
  electromagnetic rotation, 1821, and the Wollaston/Davy priority dispute; **gap:** year only in
  the pool, September orders the segment.
- **[R]** FRS **8 January 1824**, one adverse ballot; the attribution of that ballot to Davy is
  traditional and unprovable, and the venue (Somerset House, the Society's premises to 1857) comes
  from general historical knowledge, **not re-fetched**. **[R] Babbage:** from the dossier only
  (K. K. Schwarz, 'Faraday and Babbage'), long friendship and support for the candidacy; named in
  the campa **with a hedge** and tagged [R], not independently confirmed.
- **[A]** Gas liquefaction and chlorine hydrate 1823; benzene from whale-oil gas residue 1825,
  named "bicarburet of hydrogen"; Director of the Laboratory 1825; Friday Discourses and
  Christmas Lectures founded (rigb.org).

## 5. The induction years (1827-1836)

- **[A] Dates corrected at both ends.** The RI's page gives **1830-1851** for the Woolwich chair
  and **1829** separately for the **Admiralty** advisership; the pool's "1829-1852 at Woolwich"
  conflated two appointments. **[A]** Induction ring **29 August 1831** (RI 'Iconic Objects'),
  quotation from Tyndall; the disc a few months later, **gap:** no day, 28 October orders.
- **[R] and inaccessible.** The first *Experimental Researches* paper reaching the Royal Society,
  November 1831: the **Philosophical Transactions page returned HTTP 403**. Folded into the disc
  campa, not given a stop.
- **[A] Tag upgraded.** Ordained **deacon 1 July 1832** (Wikipedia, 'Sandemanians'), lifting the
  pool's [R]. Same source: eldership 1840-1844 and 1860-1864, exclusion from the communion 1844,
  resignation as Elder **5 June 1864**. **Gap:** the circumstances of the 1844 exclusion are
  established by nothing reached here.
- **[A]** 1833: the laws of electrolysis, the terminology settled with Whewell, and the first
  Fullerian chair, held for life with no compulsory lectures.
- **Contradiction, carried and NOT resolved: the Melbourne pension.** The DNB puts the interview
  and the arrangement at **c.1835** ("humbug"; Melbourne apologised in writing); the RI page dates
  the **award** to **1836**. Both are in `date_confidence`. **Venue gap:** no source reached names
  where the interview happened, so the stop sits at Faraday's own RI pin rather than staging him
  at Downing Street.
- **[R]** The metal-lined room, 1836, [R] in the pool; the cube's dimensions and gauze are
  literature only, so the campa keeps them generic. **[A]** Trinity House adviser 1836-1865.
  **[R] and cut:** the refusal to work on a chemical weapon for the Crimean War is **not in the
  pool** and was not imported from memory.

## 6. The breakdown and the second harvest (1839-1845)

- **Contradiction, carried.** The pool dates the breakdown **1839**; the DNB dates it **1841** and
  gives it about three years. The stop is 1841 and the campa states the onset from about 1839.
- **[R] on cause, a real gap.** No medical cause is established (Larner 2021, see Sources: not
  fetched here). Mercury and exhaustion are both argued, neither proved. **[A]** The pencilled
  note on not bearing much talking (Tyndall, who saw the scraps).
- **[A] Tag upgraded and pin corrected.** The **1841 Switzerland journey with Sarah and her
  brother-in-law George Barnard** is explicitly in the DNB, lifting the pool's [R]. The pool
  pinned the trip at **Geneva** as an admitted placeholder, which no source supports for 1841;
  the file uses **Interlaken (46.6863, 7.8632)**, where the dated entry was written. **[A]** The
  cloutnail passage, **2 August 1841**: "I love a smith's shop... My father was a smith."
- **[A]** Haswell colliery **28 September 1844**, 95 dead; the Home Office inquiry with Charles
  Lyell on firedamp at a naked light, inadequate ventilation and suspended coal dust.
  **Contradiction, carried:** Wikipedia dates the **report** to 1846, the pool the **explosion** to
  1844. Both can be true; not adjudicated.
- **[A]** The Faraday effect **13 September 1845**, using the heavy glass left from the abandoned
  optical-glass project; diamagnetism the same year. **[R]** The list of substances hung between
  the poles (apples, meat, blood in popular versions) is carried by nothing fetched here, so the
  campa keeps it generic.

## 7. The public man (1848-1865)

- **Contradiction, carried.** Hampton Court: the pool dates the grant **1848** with residence
  delayed to 1858, but notes the **RI's own summary sometimes dates the provision to 1858**. Both
  are in `date_confidence`.
- **[A]** *The Chemical History of a Candle*, delivered 1848, published 1861 (Crookes); quotation
  verbatim from **Project Gutenberg 14474**, fetched. **Quotation corrected:** the pool's closing
  quote opened "I have now finished my subject...", which a grep of the text does not contain at
  all. Those words are **not Faraday's here** and are cut; the wish-clause is a `suggested_ref`.
- **[A] Tag upgraded, source replaced.** "Nothing is too wonderful to be true" was [R] in the pool
  as unlocatable. It is genuine: **Bence Jones (1870), vol. II, p. 248** (archive.org,
  fetched and grepped), laboratory book **19 March 1849**, Diary entry 10,040. Two further fixes:
  the circulating one-liner is only the **first half** of the sentence, so the **full** sentence is
  quoted; and the entry concerns **gravity and electricity**, not the gold-and-diamond work.
- **[A]** The reply to Lady Lovelace on being a Sandemanian (Tyndall, DNB); em dashes rendered as
  commas per house style, wording otherwise verbatim. **Gap:** Tyndall gives no date; Ada Lovelace
  died November 1852, so **1850 orders the segment and is not attested**.
- **[A]** The table-turning letter to *The Times*, **28 June 1853**, Epsilon Faraday2691 (fetched),
  signed "M. Faraday, Royal Institution, June 28", which is why the stop sits at the RI pin.
  **[R]** The Thames white cards, letter of **7 July 1855**: **not fetched**, only the RI record.
  The pin (51.5079, -0.1069) is a placeholder; **the exact vantage point is not recorded**.
- **[A]** Maxwell's *On Faraday's Lines of Force*, read to the Cambridge Philosophical Society and
  sent to Faraday (then 64) and to William Thomson, per `james_clerk_maxwell.journey.json`.
  **Gap:** the date it reached Albemarle Street is not in the pool; February 1856 orders.
- **[R] Trinity Buoy Wharf.** Wikipedia confirms he experimented there but **not** that the
  workshop stood above the Chain and Buoy Store, so that is dropped. **Coordinates corrected** to
  51.507722/0.008306, the pool's pin being ~190 m north; the standing tower is of 1864-66 and is
  not the tower of the earlier experiments.
- **[A] Date corrected.** Epsilon Faraday3732 is dated **25 February 1860**, **not 1859** as the
  pool had it, and its arithmetic is corrected too: adviser since 1836, so this summarises
  **twenty-four** years, not twenty-three.
- **[A] Tag upgraded.** Dungeness, first permanent electric light in any lighthouse, **operational
  1 February 1862**, decommissioned 1875. **Coordinate caveat carried:** 50.9147/0.9736 is near but
  not on the 1961 tower and the tower he lit was earlier, so the pin marks the site. **[A]** The
  congregation's move to Barnsbury Grove, 1862.
- **[R] and cut.** The stories that he declined the Presidency of the Royal Society and of the RI
  are **not in the pool** and are not asserted.

## 8. Death and afterlife

- **[A]** Died **25 August 1867** at Hampton Court Green (westminster-abbey.org, fetched).
  **Gap:** no certified cause in any source consulted. Dying in his chair at the window is [R].
- **[A]** Buried in the Sandemanian plot, **west side of Highgate Cemetery**; Sarah later with
  him. **Gap:** the pool gives only the month; 30 August **only orders the stop** after the death.
- **[R], flagged twice.** That he **refused an offer** of Abbey burial is repeated everywhere and
  documented nowhere; the Abbey's page records the 1931 memorial and gives no such narrative, and
  the campa states the absence of evidence rather than the story. Likewise the RI states he said he
  **would not accept** a knighthood but that **no evidence has been found one was ever offered**.
- **[A]** Memorial floor stone, Abbey nave, **30 September 1931**, "Buried elsewhere"; metal
  replacement from the Institution of Civil Engineers, 1976. Pin inherited byte-identical from
  `james_clerk_maxwell.journey.json`: **51.4993, -0.1273**. **[A] Material corrected:** the Faraday
  Memorial at Elephant and Castle is **stainless steel** housing a Tube substation, Rodney Gordon
  1959, built 1961, the intended glass cladding dropped over vandalism fears. The pool called it
  "a brutalist concrete monument".
- **[A]** Foley's statue at the RI, 1876, and its 1989 bronze copy at Savoy Place: `suggested_refs`,
  not stops. **[R]** Faraday Museum 1973 · the *Correspondence*, ed. James, 1991-2011 · the Series E
  £20 note. **[R], folklore:** the Gladstone "one day you will tax it" anecdote has **no secure
  contemporaneous source** and is unused. **Gap:** German editions exist but no translator names or
  dates could be extracted, so nothing is asserted about continental reception.

## Interlocks, and the rule applied

Pins inherited **byte-identical**: Royal Institution **51.509, -0.142** (from
`charles_babbage.journey.json`, also in `nikola_tesla` and `james_clerk_maxwell`); Westminster
Abbey **51.4993, -0.1273** (from `james_clerk_maxwell.journey.json`, same 1931 unveiling).

**Named in a campa**, each on a direct relation of Faraday's own: **`ada_lovelace`**, who wrote and
asked what he believed, his answer being the Sandemanian quotation; **`james_clerk_maxwell`**, who
read the *Experimental Researches* entire and sent Faraday the paper that mathematised his lines of
force (named at the 1856 stop and at the 1931 Abbey stone, where the two memorials were laid
together); and **`charles_babbage`**, exact contemporary, said to have supported the FRS candidacy,
named **with an explicit [R] hedge** because the friendship is dossier-only.

**Not named**, deliberately: `lord_kelvin` (Thomson received the same Maxwell paper, but no
Faraday-Thomson relation is attested in the pool, and the pool concedes the 1900 RI
venue-continuity link is its own inference) and `humboldt` (see §3). Both are `suggested_refs`.

**Somebody else's stops, not staged here:** Tesla shown Faraday's chair at the RI in 1892;
Einstein naming Faraday at the Royal Albert Hall in October 1933; Babbage lecturing at the RI in
1815. Each is on that man's own file, and each appears here only as a `suggested_ref`.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Michael Faraday is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. No framework of his has been applied
backward to material he never treated, and no such stop appears in the file.

Three relations in the pool are **not rooted in Faraday's own life**, and the direction caught in
every case is **forward**, which the exception never licenses: (1) **Nikola Tesla** (b. 1856, aged
eleven when Faraday died), whose rotating-field motors and transformers are engineering
descendants of the 1821 rotation and 1831 induction, genealogical only and [R] in the pool;
(2) **Albert Einstein** (b. 1879), the Faraday to Maxwell to Lorentz to Einstein lineage plus the
unproven detail of the three portraits in his study, which the pool itself calls "plausible and
widely repeated", not proven; (3) **the 1892 and 1933 visits themselves**, which are those men's
own travelling.

**None has been deleted.** All three are recorded above and carried in the file as
`suggested_refs` on Faraday's own Westminster Abbey stop, where posthumous reputation is already
the subject. They are not staged as scenes and not named in any campa. Carrying any of them
further is a ruling for `EXCEPTIONS.md` and a forward grant, not an agent's judgement call.
