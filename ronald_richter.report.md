# Ronald Richter (1909-1991): research report

*2026-08-23. For `ronald_richter.journey.json`. Austrian-Czech physicist; director of the Huemul Project, Isla Huemul, Lago Nahuel Huapi, 1949-1952; announced by Juan Perón on 24 March 1951 as having achieved controlled thermonuclear reactions "on a technical scale"; disproved by a technical commission in September 1952.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition, unconfirmed. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated; where the verification pass issued a correction, the correction is recorded and the superseded version named.

---

## 1. Bohemia (1909-1936)

- **Birth: two dates, one town. [A both traditions, unresolved].** en.wikipedia and the older Argentine tradition give **21 February 1909** at Falkenau an der Eger (Falknov nad Ohří from 1918, Sokolov from 1948). es. and cs.wikipedia, following Czech archival scholarship associated with **Milan Řípa** and **Jan Boukal**, give **11 October 1909**. es.wikipedia is not internally consistent. The file uses February to order the segment and states the conflict in `date_confidence`.
- **Prague. [A]** Natural Sciences Faculty of the German-language half of Charles University; **graduated 1935**; assistant while a student. The start of his studies is nowhere dated; the file's 1930 is a placeholder.
- **The doctorate: contested.** en.wikipedia flags the dispute itself. One tradition, the one Argentina later used against him, holds no degree was conferred because he read his own results as delta rays; the other cites the **doctorate register for 1931-1936**. *Gap:* that register was **not inspected** and no transcription was located.
- **Kurt Sitte's recollection. [A as a reproduced quotation]** *"cuando yo me desempeñaba como asistente del profesor Furth en el Departamento de Física Experimental"* (es.wikipedia; the professor is Reinhold Fürth). *Gap:* the source interview is not identified.
- **The Falkenau arc experiments, 1936. [A, tag raised from R].** The dossier tagged this [R] because Řípa's Czech originals were unread. Too weak: **cs.wikipedia** calls Richter author of the world's first plasma-fusion experiments, Czechoslovakia 1936, and places him at the chemical works after 1935; **karlovarska.drbna.cz** confirms Řípa's book documents those attempts at the Falknov nad Ohří works (now Synthomer Sokolov). Both fix on **1936**, narrowing "c.1935-1936." The world-priority claim itself remains unverified.

## 2. Germany, France, England (1939-1947)

- **The wartime years. [R]** es.wikipedia places him in Germany **1939-1943**. Von Ardenne and Steenbeck circle him in the dossier, but **no city, laboratory, employer, or rank could be confirmed in any source fetched**; Berlin is a declared placeholder. The largest single gap in the life. en.wikipedia adds six months on explosives development and commercial contracts, then travel to England and France, with no dates or places; folded into the same stop.
- **Kurt Tank. [A]** es.wikipedia: Richter met Tank, who later moved to Argentina; en.wikipedia dates the resulting invitation to **1947**. *Gap:* the venue and date of the London meeting are unrecorded; the London pin and 1946 date are placeholders.

## 3. Argentina: arrival, Córdoba, the tour (1948-1949)

- **Arrival, 16 August 1948. [A]** en.wikipedia, "Huemul Project."
- **The Pedro Matthies alias: misattribution, corrected.** That same article says Richter travelled under the alias "Dr. Pedro Matthies." It is the outlier: en.wikipedia's *Kurt Tank* and *Ronald Richter* both make it **Tank's** pseudonym, used from late 1946 at the Instituto Aerotécnico. Corrected in the campa, since the claim is widely repeated.
- **Second contradiction, unresolved.** es.wikipedia gives naturalisation in **September 1946**, awkward against a **1948** arrival. Nothing reached reconciles them; noted in `date_confidence`.
- **Introduction to Perón, 24 August 1948. [A]** es.wikipedia dates the presentation to 1948 without a day. **Eva Perón's remark [A as a reproduced quotation]**, "The General is very naive," is from Dujovne Ortiz, *Eva Perón* (1996), via en.wikipedia; not verified against the book.
- **Córdoba, Instituto Aerotécnico. [A]** First Argentine facilities, 1948-1949, inside Tank's aviation program alongside the Pulqui project.
- **The Córdoba fire: real, re-sourced.** The dossier cited es.wikipedia/Proyecto Huemul, which never mentions a fire (wikitext checked for *incendio*). It is at en.wikipedia: "in early 1949 a fire destroyed some of the equipment." No day. The sabotage charge is **Richter's own allegation [R]**.
- **The foreign tour. [A, tag raised from R].** The dossier said "not independently confirmed by any page I fetched." Incorrect: en.wikipedia states it plainly, "visiting Canada and perhaps the U.S. and Europe as well," early 1949. No Canadian city is named; Ottawa is a placeholder.
- **Lise Meitner in Vienna. [A, tag raised from R].** en.wikipedia carries the anecdote verbatim: a "strange Austrian with an Argentine visa" demonstrating a device he called thermonuclear and she dismissed as a chemical effect, on the early-1949 tour, narrowing "c.1949-1950." Retrospective testimony, not a laboratory record.

- **Site and construction. [A]** Colonel **Enrique P. González** selected the island; construction began **1949**. Coordinates **-41.1064, -71.395** from es.wikipedia, "Isla Huemul."
- **Coordinate defect corrected.** The dossier's afterlife lens twice gave Isla Huemul as **-41.061, -71.483**, about 8 km northwest of it in open water. The geography lens had it right.
- **Island size: unresolved.** magicasruinas.com.ar gives **200 hectares**, es.wikipedia **74**. Flagged in the ruins stop; the campa avoids the number.
- **Richter moves onto the island, March 1950. [R]** Not in any fetched source. Construction from 1949 and his direction of the site through 1952 are both [A].

- **Laboratorio 1 and the buried chamber. [R, both]** The twelve-metre cylinder with walls up to four metres thick, the holes bored through them, the crack, the demolition; and the fourteen-metre excavation later filled with concrete. All from the dossier's synthesis of press reporting on the ruins, chiefly a **WIRED** piece that **could not be fetched**.

- **The tilted-plate objection, 23 February 1951. [A as to date, secondhand as to content]** *Gap:* no transcript located; the technician is unnamed in anything reachable.

## 5. The announcement (March-July 1951)

- **Perón at the Casa Rosada, 24 March 1951. [A]** en.wikipedia.
- **The milk-bottle promise. [A, tag raised from R].** The dossier called this an unsourceable legend. en.wikipedia quotes it twice in its account of the announcement ("energy would be sold in packages the size of a milk bottle"), attributed to Perón. Only the "half-litre or litre" specificity is unsourced, and is unused.
- **The six-billion-dollar quotation: speaker corrected.** *"podía iniciar los trabajos atómicos por el procedimiento que siguen los norteamericanos, pero que para eso necesitaríamos unos seis mil millones de dólares"* is verbatim in Infobae (2021), but the dossier gave it to **Perón**. Infobae's sentence makes it **Richter's**, reported publicly by Perón in early 1951.
- **The "10,000 word interview": date corrected.** The dossier gave **28 March 1951**. en.wikipedia: "The next day Richter held another press conference," that is **25 March**. *Gap:* no transcript located, no venue named; the pin is a declared placeholder. **Medalla Peronista, 7 April 1951. [A]**
- **UBA honorary doctorate, 1951. [R]** From the dossier, **not located in any fetched source**; es.wikipedia's article on the university gave only the Rectorado address. Building coordinates [A], ceremony not. His claim to have returned medal and title comes from the 1984 interview; no record of either return exists.
- **Thirring and the reply. [A as to the exchange; title corrected].** Thirring's assessment ran in *United Nations World*, **May 1951**, Richter's signed reply there in **July 1951**. The dossier's "Argentina Has No **Atom** Bomb" is wrong by a word; it is **"Argentina Has No Atomic Bomb"**, per the citation list of **Mahaffey, *Atomic Adventures*** (Open Library full-text search; the dossier's wording returns zero hits). *Gap:* neither original was located, so **no interior passage from either is quoted**.
- **Roederer on the Argentine profession. [A as a reproduced quotation]** *Physics Today*, January 2003, via en.wikipedia; the original returned 403. **Teller's remark** ("Reading one line one has to think he's a genius. Reading the next line, one realizes he's crazy.") comes via ITER. *Correction:* ITER dates that exchange **January 2003**, not the dossier's "August 2003 and March 2004 letters."

## 6. The commissions and the closure (1952)

- **González's break, February 1952. [A, tag raised from R].** en.wikipedia: González "told Perón in February 1952 that either Richter departed or he would," Perón accepted the resignation and replaced him with **Navy Captain Pedro Iraolagoitía**, "who also protested and convinced Perón to investigate." es.wikipedia confirms the succession and his rank. *Gap:* the location of the resignation is recorded nowhere; the file uses the island pin and says so.
- **The inspection, 5-8 September 1952. [A]** Roster per es.wikipedia: **Balseiro, Mario Báncora, Manuel Beninson, Pedro Bussolini, Otto Gamba**; en.wikipedia also places **Iraolagoitía** with the team. Two corrections: the Jesuit is **Pedro** Bussolini, not the dossier's unsourced "Juan," and es.wikipedia gives Beninson **no rank**, so "Captain" is dropped. Balseiro was recalled urgently from England.
- **Findings. [A, quoted verbatim]** Three quotations carried into the file, two from es.wikipedia, one from Infobae: Balseiro on the lithium collision arithmetic making any control device impossible under the operating conditions; Báncora identifying the apparatus as **el arco cantante** discovered by Duddell fifty years earlier (source spells it "Dudell"; preserved, flagged in `quote_source`); Balseiro's verdict on the missing foundations.
- **Reports delivered, 15 September 1952. [A]**
- **The Gans-Rodríguez review: split verdict.** Reviewers and finding are **[A]**: en.wikipedia records **Richard Gans and Antonio Rodríguez** appointed to review the first report and Richter's rebuttal, and endorsing it. But the dossier's **16 October 1952 appears in no fetched source** and is dropped; the stop is dated 1952-10-01, day declared a placeholder.
- **Occupation and closure, 22 November 1952. [A]** es.wikipedia confirms closure that year without a day. The finding was technical and administrative: **no criminal conviction was published**, and none was found.
- **Cost. [A as approximate]** About 62.5 million pesos, roughly US$15 million of the day, per en.wikipedia's own approximate framing. Inflation-adjusted figures in the hundreds of millions circulate and are **not comparable across peso vintages**.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

Richter is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no apparatus relation of the licensable kind was found or needed: he has no theoretical framework to apply backward, only an apparatus made of carbon rods. The pool did contain **one-directional engagements by other people with Richter or his island**. Each is real and citable; none is his own travel, so none gets a stop. Direction caught in every case: **forward**, which the exceptions file does not license. All four go to `sources` / `suggested_refs`:

1. **Lyman Spitzer, Aspen and Princeton, 1951-1953.** He read of the announcement, judged the device unworkable, and conceived the stellarator on a ski lift; ITER calls that "the true beginning of the long, arduous and often frustrating road that eventually led to ITER." Richter's largest effect on the world, and **entirely Spitzer's travelling**. Cited on the announcement stop; the ski-lift stop is his.
2. **The 1955 Bariloche physics institute, Instituto Balseiro from 1962, and INVAP.** es.wikipedia/Proyecto Huemul states the causal connection outright, so the fact is **[A]**, correcting the dossier's [R], which rested only on ib.edu.ar returning a 404.
3. **Richter: Ópera documental de cámara (2003)**, Lorenzo and Buch. In `suggested_refs`, not a stop.
4. **Řípa's 2021 book launch at Sokolov Castle.** Řípa's stop; in `suggested_refs` on the birth stop. *Correction:* the archives were the **Sokolov Museum's**, credited to Michael Rund, **not Charles University's**.

**Travellers named in campa:** `juan_peron` and `eva_peron` only, for direct relations. Shared geography was not used as a reason to name anyone: `che_guevara` passes through Bariloche on 11 February 1952, while the island was an active secret project across the water, but had no relation to Richter, and the map already shows the co-location. The Tripoli pin is inherited byte-identical with no name-dropping. **Rejected:** the dossier's claim that **Heisenberg** alternated between crediting and dismissing the announcement could not be confirmed; `heisenberg.journey.json` never mentions Richter, Huemul, or Argentina, nor does `fermi.journey.json`.
