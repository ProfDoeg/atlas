# Plinio el Viejo (Gaius Plinius Secundus), c. AD 23/24 - 79
*Research report for the Atlas of Journeys. Compiled 2026-08-03 from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife), with the adversarial corrections folded in and flagged where they overturn a lens.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or scholarly proposal. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**Standing caution.** Almost everything biographical outside the Natural History comes from two letters of the nephew: *Epistulae* 3.5 (works and routine) and 6.16 (the death), the latter written c. AD 106 at Tacitus's request by a man who was seventeen in 79, stayed at Misenum, and saw none of it. Everything between those letters is modern prosopography, principally Syme's, contested at nearly every point.

---

## 1. Comum and the schools of Rome (AD 23/24 - c. 45)

- Born at **Novum Comum** on Lake Larius, modern Como, to an equestrian family with property around the lake **[A: Ep. 4.13 and 1.19, which call Comum the family home; Wikipedia; Livius.org]**.
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** Birth year **AD 23** (Wikipedia, most handbooks) versus **AD 24** (Livius.org). Neither comes from a dated document; both are back-calculations from the age at death. The journey dates the stop 0023 and carries the dispute in `date_confidence`.
- **CORRECTION to the chronology lens.** The parents' names, "Gaius Plinius Celer" and "Marcella", were tagged **[A]**. Wikipedia derives them from a fragmentary, disputed inscription, not from any literary source. **Retagged [R]** and kept out of the journey's prose as a positive claim.
- Hears the orator **Marcus Servilius Nonianus**; studies under the tragedian and grammarian **Publius Pomponius Secundus**, whose life he later writes in two books **[A: Wikipedia; Livius.org; Ep. 3.5]**.
- **Contradiction between lenses.** The geography lens said "no specific teacher is named in the surviving record" and tagged the schooling **[R]**; the chronology lens names both teachers with sources. Adopted: the *teachers* are **[A]**, the *location and any named school* stay **[R]**. The stop carries both halves.
- Witnessing the courts of Caligula and Claudius as a student is **[R: Livius.org]**: plausible for a young equestrian, attested nowhere.

**Gap.** From roughly AD 35 to the first posting the record is close to blank: nothing on family finances, patronage, or how a Comum equestrian reached Pomponius Secundus's circle.

## 2. The German years (c. AD 45 - 58)

- Leaves for the Rhine as a junior officer at twenty-one to twenty-three **[A]**. **Contradiction, unresolved:** departure dated **45** by Livius.org, **46** by Wikipedia.
- **Praefectus cohortis**, later tribune, under **Gnaeus Domitius Corbulo** in Germania Inferior, against the Chauci and Frisians **[A: Wikipedia, citing NH and Tacitus]**.
- Takes part in the **Fossa Corbulonis**, the Rhine-Meuse canal cut so shipping need not risk the open North Sea **[A: NH 16.5; Tacitus, Annals 11.20]**.
- No source names the fort of the Chauci operations. **Flevum** (Velsen) is the best-attested base in that theatre: a pin and an explicit **[R]** **[Tacitus, Annals 11.18-19]**.
- **The dream of Drusus.** Drusus appears in a dream begging to be saved from oblivion; Pliny begins the *Bella Germaniae*.
  - **CORRECTION to the chronology lens, citation.** The pool cited the NH preface. The passage is **Ep. 3.5.4**: *"astitit ei quiescenti Drusi Neronis effigies... orantis ut se ab iniuria oblivionis adsereret."* Verified at attalus.org/old/pliny3.html.
  - **CORRECTION to the geography lens, over-specification.** The pool placed the dream at **Castra Vetera**, in winter quarters, with Pliny already a cavalry officer, tagged **[A]**. Ep. 3.5.4 says only *"cum in Germania militaret"*: no fortress, no season, no rank. **Retagged [R]**; the journey pins Vetera as likeliest garrison and says so in `date_confidence`.
- **Coordinate correction.** The pool's Vetera pin, 51.6641 / 6.4478, is modern Xanten town. **Vetera I** stood on the Fürstenberg near Birten, c. **51.647 / 6.461**. Corrected.
- Tribune under **Pomponius Secundus** in Germania Superior against the Chatti; visits the Wiesbaden springs and the reputed Danube sources **[A: Livius.org; Wikipedia]**. No text names the post, so the Mainz pin is **[R]**.
- **Coordinate correction.** The pool's 49.993 / 8.2437 falls c. 1.5 km WNW of the fortress. Mogontiacum stood on the **Kästrich** plateau, c. **49.9975 / 8.2600**. Corrected.
- Witnesses the **naumachia on the Fucine Lake** **[A: Livius.org]** and records seeing **Agrippina the Younger** there in a war cloak of gold thread, as eyewitness **[A: NH 33.63]**. Dated AD 52, which puts him in Italy mid-service; no source explains the interruption.
- **Praefectus alae**, a cavalry wing of roughly 480 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
  - **CORRECTION to the geography lens, wrong object.** The pool claimed a "lead water-pipe inscribed with his name found at Vetera" that "confirms his cavalry command". There is no pipe. Wikipedia records a **phalera**, a harness disc, bearing his name. A harness disc also confirms nothing. Downgraded.
- Last commander in Germany, **Pompeius Paullinus**, c. 55-58 **[A: NH 33.143, where Pliny names him]**.
- Writes **De Jaculatione Equestri**, the two-book **Life of Pomponius Secundus**, and the twenty-book **Bella Germaniae** **[A: Wikipedia; Livius.org; Ep. 3.5]**, the last two dated c. 58. All lost.

**Contradiction, unresolved.** The pool dates both books to 58 and the return to 59 (Livius.org, "at thirty-six") while giving "Rome" as the place of composition. Whether the twenty books were finished on the Rhine or after the homecoming, no source says. Flagged in `date_confidence`.

## 3. Rome under Nero (c. AD 59 - 68)

- Returns to Italy c. 59 **[A: Livius.org]**. Avoids high office as dangerous under Nero **[A as the nephew's explanation: Ep. 3.5.5; the motive-reading is [R]]**.
- Writes **Studiosus** (three books, training an orator) and **Dubii Sermonis** (eight, on ambiguities of Latin usage) **[A: Ep. 3.5]**. Both lost.
- **CORRECTION to the chronology lens, split claim.** The pool asserted as **[A]** that he "witnesses the Great Fire of Rome and the subsequent construction of Nero's Domus Aurea". The two halves differ.
  - **Domus Aurea: [A]**. He writes about the Golden House and says he saw Nero's colossal statue with his own eyes **[NH 34.45-46, 36.111]**.
  - **The fire of 64: [R]**, downgraded. That he witnessed it is attested nowhere and he never says he was in Rome for it. The stop says only that he watches the Golden House rise over the burned city.

## 4. The procuratorships under Vespasian (c. AD 69 - 76)

Vespasian's accession opens the way back to office; the two had served together in Germany **[A: Wikipedia, consistent with Ep. 3.5.9]**.

This is the most heavily reconstructed stretch of the life. The modern sequence comes from Ronald Syme, "Pliny the Procurator," *HSCP* 73 (1969); the evidence is regional detail in the Natural History plus one sentence of Ep. 3.5.17. **Syme's article was not reachable for this run** and is used at second hand.

- **Gallia Narbonensis**, c. 70. **[R]**, Syme's proposal from regional detail. Competes with rather than complements the Africa reconstruction.
- **Africa Proconsularis**, c. 70-72. **[R]**.
  - **CORRECTION to the chronology lens, tag.** The pool tagged Africa **[A]**. No ancient source states he held it; it is Syme's inference from NH books 5 and 8, competing with Narbonensis for the same years. **Retagged [R]**, matching the tag the geography lens gave the identical claim. The two lenses were inconsistent.
- **Tacape** (Gabès): the oasis irrigation, one spring divided among the palm growers by timed sluices **[NH 18.188]**. Often read as eyewitness; he never says so. **[R]**.
- **Hispania Tarraconensis**, c. 73. The best attested of the postings **[A]**.
  - **CORRECTION to the geography lens, evidential.** The pool called this "the one provincial post he confirms in his own writing", citing NH 3.30 and 19.10. Those record Spanish detail; neither states he held the office. The attestation is **Ep. 3.5.17**, *"cum procuraret in Hispania"*. The post stands **[A]**; the self-confirmation does not.
  - **CORRECTION to the chronology lens, reversal.** The pool has Pliny "offering 400,000 sesterces to purchase the manuscripts of Larcius Licinius". The transaction runs the other way. Ep. 3.5.17: *"referebat ipse potuisse se, cum procuraret in Hispania, vendere hos commentarios Larcio Licino quadringentis milibus nummum."* **Larcius Licinius offered Pliny 400,000 for Pliny's own notebooks**, and Pliny did not sell. Verified at attalus.org/old/pliny3.html; Wikipedia's phrasing agrees.
  - **Coordinate correction.** The pool's 41.1147 / 1.2564 is the Tarragona **amphitheatre**. The provincial forum lies at c. **41.1167 / 1.2497**. Corrected.
- **Las Médulas** and the *ruina montium* passage, collapsing a gold-bearing mountain by flooding tunnels cut through it **[NH 33.66-78]**. Widely read as direct inspection. **[R]**: he does not say he was there.
- **Gallia Belgica**, c. 74-76. **[R]**, proposed on NH 17.3, a frost splitting the bark from vines among the Treveri.
- **Ostia** as embarkation point is **[R]** by general practice; no text places Pliny there.

**Gap.** The order of the procuratorships, and whether all four happened, is unsettled. Every `date_confidence` in the journey says the sequence is a modern reconstruction.

## 5. Vespasian's Rome and the thirty-seven books (c. AD 75 - 77)

- Calls on Vespasian before dawn while it is still night, then official duties, then his desk, reading or being read to and taking notes until dinner **[A: Ep. 3.5.7-9]**.
- Draws on the art in the **Templum Pacis** for the books on painting and sculpture, while conceding that much of what he says about where famous works stand in Rome is secondhand **[A: NH 34.84, 36.27]**.
- Publishes the **Naturalis Historia**, thirty-seven books, dedicated to Titus and naming his sixth consulship, AD 77 **[A]**. Composition thought to begin after 70 **[R]**.
- A command of the **vigiles** is **[R]** and uncertain; dropped from the journey for want of a distinct location, recorded here.
- **Standing caveat.** The Natural History still has no complete critical edition of the Latin **[A]**, so any claim about what Pliny "wrote" at a given line is a claim about one editorial tradition.

## 6. Misenum and the eruption (AD 77 - 79)

- **Praefectus classis** of the Misenum fleet, the empire's largest naval command; lives there with his widowed sister **Plinia** and her seventeen-year-old son **[A: Ep. 6.16.4]**.
  - **CORRECTION to the geography lens, wrong emperor.** The pool has **Titus** appointing him. **Vespasian** reigned until 23 June 79 and the appointment is dated c. 77-79, during Pliny's known close attendance on Vespasian (Ep. 3.5.9). Titus is the dedicatee of the book, not the appointing emperor.
- Writes of **Baiae** and its oyster beds with distaste, as a byword for extravagance **[A: NH 9.168-172]**.
  - **Coordinate correction.** The pool's 40.7893 / 14.0844 sits c. 3.5 km southeast of Baiae, on top of the Misenum pin two entries away. Baiae is at **40.8167 / 14.0697**. Corrected.
- **Vespasian dies 23 June 79** at Aquae Cutiliae **[A]**.
  - **CORRECTION, arithmetic, from Wikipedia.** The pool says Pliny outlived him "by four months". 23 June to 25 August is **two months**; Wikipedia's page gives both. Four months works only on an autumn date.
- **Contradiction, live and unresolved.** The eruption is traditionally dated **24 August 79**; a charcoal inscription found at Pompeii in 2018, dated to mid-October, has revived the case for **autumn**. The journey uses the traditional dates in `date`, as the schema requires, and carries the dispute in `date_confidence` on every eruption stop.

The sequence of 24-26 August is **[A: Ep. 6.16.4-21, with Ep. 6.20 for Misenum]** throughout, and is narrated stop by stop in the journey file rather than repeated here. Three points need flagging.

- **CORRECTION to the chronology lens, sequence.** The pool collapsed two separate orders into one, "orders the fleet's quadriremes launched to investigate and to rescue". The letter separates them: the **light Liburnian galley** first, for observation only; the **quadriremes** afterwards, once Rectina's messenger has arrived and the errand has changed. The geography lens got this right and the chronology lens did not.
- **Textual variant, unresolved.** Some manuscripts and editors read the name as a place, **Retina**, rather than as the woman **Rectina**. Neither her identity nor the site of the villa is settled **[R]**, and nothing records whether she got off that shore.
- **CORRECTION to the geography and afterlife lenses, date of recovery.** The pool gave "27 or 28 August", "three days after his death". Ep. 6.16.20 reads *"ubi dies redditus (is ab eo quem novissime viderat tertius)"*: the third day counting inclusively from 24 August as the first. That gives **26 August**, two days after the death. Standard translations render it "two days after the last day he had seen."

**Cause of death, unresolved.** The nephew infers a weak throat overcome by thick fumes **[A as his inference: 6.16.19]**. **Suetonius** transmits a rumour that Pliny, overcome by heat, begged a slave to kill him **[A as transmitted rumour; absent from 6.16]**. In 1859 **Jacob Bigelow** argued from the same text for apoplexy or heart failure **[A]**. Not adjudicated.

**Age at death. CORRECTION.** The pool gives "approximately 54 to 56". Born 23 or 24 and dead in 79, he was **55 or 56**; no reconstruction yields 54.

## 7. Afterlife

- **No tomb, no marker, no identified remains.** The body was recovered at Stabiae; what became of it went unrecorded. **[Stated as a gap: no source claims a tomb existed or names a site.]**
- The will names the nephew heir on condition of adoption; he takes the name **[A: CIL V 5262]** and endows Comum with a library **[A: Ep. 1.8]**, a school **[A: Ep. 4.13]**, and *alimenta* for freeborn children **[A: Ep. 7.18]**.
  - **CORRECTION to the geography lens, citation.** The pool cited **Ep. 5.7**, which concerns a bequest by Saturninus to Comum and has nothing to do with Pliny the Elder's will. Replaced with the above. The substance was sound; only the reference was wrong.
- The **Naturalis Historia** is the only one of his seven known major works to survive; the other six are lost **[A]**.
- Transmission runs through three families: 5th-6th century palimpsest fragments, the 8th-9th century *vetustiores* including the large-format **Leiden Pliny** produced at York in Bede's era, and the 9th-century *recentiores* behind the medieval vulgate. **No complete ancient copy survives** **[A: modern philological consensus]**.
- **Editio princeps**, Venice, **1469** **[A]**.
  - **CORRECTION to the afterlife lens, printer.** The pool credits "Johannes and Wendelinus of Speyer". Wendelin took over the press only after his brother's death in 1470 and cannot have co-printed the 1469 Pliny; the edition is generally credited to **Johannes de Spira** alone. Wikipedia uses both forms inconsistently. Venice, 1469, and 45.4408 / 12.3155 are correct.
- **Nicolas Jenson**, Venice **1472**, a copy at Wells Cathedral Library **[A]**; not in the journey. **Philemon Holland**, 1601, first full English translation **[A]**; **Bostock and Riley**, 1855, first complete scholarly one **[A]**.
- Como Cathedral's west front, **1457-1498**, carries statues of both Plinys as native sons **[A]**.
- **Villa Pliniana**, Torno, begun **1573** for Giovanni Anguissola, finished 1577, named for the intermittent spring the younger Pliny described **[A]**. Toponymic and promotional, not an ancient site **[R]**.
  - **Coordinate correction.** The pool's 45.8394 / 9.0967 falls in open water toward Cernobbio. Villa Pliniana is at 45 deg 51' 28" N, 9 deg 08' 22" E = **45.8578 / 9.1394**. Corrected.
- **"Plinian eruption." CORRECTION to the afterlife lens, and it inverts the claim.** The pool's opening sentence says volcanology "names a whole eruptive category after him", meaning the Elder. It does not: the term honours **Pliny the Younger**, who wrote the description, as the pool's own second half concedes. Rewritten so the honorand is the nephew. The coiner and first scientific use could not be pinned down. **Open gap.**
- That the death becomes the founding image of the naturalist as martyr to curiosity is **[R]**: reception history, with no ancient source stating the framing.

## 8. Interlocks with existing atlas travelers

Named in the journey's `campa` where the paths genuinely cross:

- Pompeii: **bulwer_lytton** (the 1833-34 novel), **johann_tischbein** (the half-cleared forum with Goethe, spring 1787).
- Vesuvius crater: no genuine crossing with Pliny found for **kircher** (lowered by rope, 1638), **humboldt** (repeated ascents with Gay-Lussac and von Buch, 1805), **bolivar** (beside him by tradition only, flagged **[R]**), **giordano_bruno** (Nola) or **hannibal** (Capua) — all share the mountain's coordinate at unrelated later or earlier dates with no citation tying any of them to Pliny himself; dropped from the campa in the 2026-08 house-style pass as coincidence of place, not documented relation.
- The Bay: **virgil** (buried on the Puteoli road at Posillipo), **aeneas** (the trumpeter Misenus buried at the cape that names Pliny's base), **cicero** (home through Puteoli, 75 BC). The reconstruction placing Siro's school at the Villa of the Papyri is **[R]** and treated as such.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical**: Vesuvius 40.8224 / 14.4289; Pompeii 40.7461 / 14.4989; Cape Misenum 40.7847 / 14.0894; Venice 45.4408 / 12.3155. The Villa of the Papyri pin, 40.8058 / 14.3488, is deliberately *not* reused: the Rectina-shore pin, 40.8058 / 14.3486, stays distinct as its own claim.

**Interlock gap, stated as a gap.** Before this file, **no atlas journey placed Pliny the Elder himself on the map**. The only anchors in the corpus were the QUEUE.md line and the census row, both reading "Roman naturalist, died at Vesuvius" with no itinerary. Como, the procuratorships, the Misenum command, and the rescue voyage are all new ground, sourced from outside the corpus.

## 9. Sources

**Reached and used directly**
- Pliny the Younger, *Epistulae* 3.5, 6.16, 6.20, Latin with J. B. Firth's 1900 English, attalus.org/old/pliny3.html and pliny6.html. The Larcius Licinius reversal, the Drusus dream citation, and the two-day recovery all rest on direct reading of these pages.
- Pliny the Elder, *Naturalis Historia*, trans. Bostock and Riley (1855), Gutenberg ebook 57493, book II; trans. Philemon Holland (1601), penelope.uchicago.edu/holland/, books VII and VIII.
- Wikipedia, "Pliny the Elder", "Natural History (Pliny)", "Plinian eruption".
- Livius.org, "Pliny the Elder" (Duane Roller / Jona Lendering).
- World History Encyclopedia, "Pliny the Elder".

**Cited at second hand, not reached**
- Ronald Syme, "Pliny the Procurator," *HSCP* 73 (1969). The whole procuratorship reconstruction depends on it; used only through Wikipedia's and the pool's summaries.
- Suetonius, the fragmentary life of Pliny and the rumour of the slave: only through later biographical tradition as relayed in the pool.
- Jacob Bigelow's 1859 medical analysis, widely cited in Pliny scholarship, not read.
- CIL V 5262 (Como) and the disputed inscription behind the parents' names: neither consulted in an edition.

**Open gaps, recorded rather than filled**
- Birth year 23 versus 24.
- Departure for Germany 45 versus 46.
- Whether the Bella Germaniae was finished in Germania or at Rome.
- Whether Africa and Narbonensis are alternatives or a sequence, and whether either happened.
- The eruption date, August versus autumn, live since the 2018 Pompeii charcoal inscription.
- Cause of death: fumes, apoplexy, or the Suetonian rumour.
- Burial place: unknown, and no source even claims one existed.
- First scientific use of the term "Plinian eruption": not established.
