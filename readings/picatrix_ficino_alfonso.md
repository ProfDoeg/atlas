# Ficino and Alfonso X under the sky: the electors' first direct look

nodus3, 2026-10-13. These are the day-dated acts in the two new dossiers
(`dossiers/marsilio_ficino.dossier.md`, `dossiers/alfonso_x_el_sabio.dossier.md`), cast with the
same method as `picatrix_election.py`: sky at 12:00 UT, Julian calendar, Mercury scored with
Lilly's dignity points plus Wednesday, and lunar mansions in both of `mansions.py`'s conventions.
Their journeys are not built yet. This was done by hand from the dossiers, so it is not in
`catalog_stops.csv` and the electors tool does not see it.

**The two dossiers give only nine day-dated acts between them.** Three of those nine
are not even chosen moments: a proclamation two days after a father's death, a banquet on Plato's
traditional day, and a printer's colophon. This is still too few to test.

## What it shows

- **Mercury is strong once in nine, and that one was not chosen:** Alfonso's proclamation fell
  two days after his father's death.
- **The books are written and printed under a weak Mercury.** Ficino dated his defence of astral
  magic, the *Apologia*, with Mercury in Virgo but retrograde and combust. That breaks the first rule of an
  election, the same kind of fact as Pico's *Conclusiones*. The *Epistolae* were printed with
  Mercury in both detriment and fall, on a Wednesday.
- **Alfonso's astrology book was begun on a Thursday, Jupiter's day, with Mercury under the beams.**
  If his astrologers chose that day, they did not choose it by Mercury. Jupiter, the planet of
  law and religion, is a possible alternative ruler for a royal commission, and this test does not
  check it.
- **Wednesdays:** 1 of 9, against 1.3 expected.
- **Correction (2026-10-13).** The first version of this file dated *Di Dio et anima* 1459,
  by "converting" a Florentine-style year. nodus1's verify stage showed that Treccani puts the
  MODERN year first and the Florentine one in brackets ("24 genn. '58 (stile fiorentino '57)"),
  so 1458 was already modern and converting it created the error. The corrected sky has no
  Mercury dignity, a Tuesday, and the Moon in Gemini, in the Gemini mansion al-Hanʿa by both
  conventions. That is a pretty fact for a book on God and the soul, and it proves nothing.

## The honest reading

At n = 9 the test could only detect someone electing on Mercury for more than half of
everything. So, as with the other electors, **this cannot tell whether they elected**. What we
have instead are three individual facts of the right kind: the *Apologia*, the *Epistolae* and
the *Libro complido*. Each shows a person who owned the doctrine acting on a day the doctrine
would call poor for a Mercurial work. Three such facts are still anecdotes. They become
evidence only if the hours turn up: whether the act was timed to a planetary hour or a rising
sign, which this method cannot see.

**What would settle it:** a record, in Ficino's letters or the Alfonsine chronicles, of
someone choosing a day or hour by the stars for a specific act. Neither dossier contains one.
Ficino's own birth hour is the only hour in either.
