# Pío Collivadino (1869-1945): research report

*Painter of the Riachuelo; director of Argentina's national art academy for twenty-seven years.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, with the source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions between sources are flagged where they occur and are **not** adjudicated. Gaps are stated as gaps.

A note on the base sources. Almost the whole documentary spine of this life runs through two Wikipedia articles (es and en), biografiasyvidas.com, and MNBA collection records. The scholarly literature that would settle most of the open questions, above all Malosetti Costa's *Collivadino* (El Ateneo, 2006/2007) and the 2013 MNBA catalogue, was **not reachable in full text**. Several corrections below come from reading the *other* institutions' own Wikipedia articles against the Collivadino articles, which is a weak instrument, and each is flagged as such.

---

## 1. Barracas and the Estímulo, 1869-1889

**Birth.** Born 20 August 1869 in Barracas, Buenos Aires, to an Italian immigrant family. **[A: es/en.wikipedia, 'Pío Collivadino'; the es article cites an ANBA necrology and Parker, *Argentinos de hoy*, 1920].** No source reached names the parents, the street, or the parish of baptism. **Gap.**

**First training.** Studies drawing at the Società Nazionale (the Italian-Argentine cultural society) in Buenos Aires under a maestro named only as **Luzzi**, then continues at the **Asociación Estímulo de Bellas Artes**. **[A: biografiasyvidas.com].** No years are given by any source; the range c. 1885-1889 is inferred backwards from the 1889 departure **[R]**. The society's premises are not documented and the journey pin is an approximation of central Buenos Aires. **Gap.**

**The Estímulo overlap, unresolved.** The Sociedad Estímulo de Bellas Artes was founded 23 October 1876 (the Sívori brothers, Schiaffino, Vaca Guzmán, Dormal and others) and **Ángel della Valle taught drawing there for eighteen years from the early 1880s** **[A: angel_della_valle.journey.json, from es.wikipedia]**. Collivadino's study therefore falls inside della Valle's tenure. **No source states that della Valle taught him**, and this report does not claim it: what is attested is the institution, the post, and the enrolment, overlapping in time. **[R on any personal connection.]**

**Departure for Europe, 1889.** **[A: en.wikipedia; biografiasyvidas.com].** The ports of embarkation and arrival are **not documented anywhere reached**; Genoa is entered in the journey file as a reconstruction from the standard Italy-River Plate route, on the same footing as the Genoa pin the corpus already carries for later Italian crossings. **[R].**

## 2. Rome, 1889-1896

**Admission to the Accademia di San Luca, 1891.** **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia].** Two years separate arrival in Rome from admission; what he did in them is undocumented. **Gap.**

**Cesare Mariani.** Trains under the painter Cesare Mariani (1826-1901), studying fresco. **[A: biografiasyvidas.com].** Mariani was an accademico di San Luca from 1863 and president of the academy 1888-1890, so the pairing is institutionally plausible.

**The Palazzo di Giustizia fresco claim: contradiction, not resolved.** biografiasyvidas has Collivadino collaborating with Mariani on frescoes at Rome's Palace of Justice; en.wikipedia's wording is "the Constitutional Court of Italy." Three problems, all flagged, none adjudicated:

1. **it.wikipedia, 'Cesare Mariani (pittore)'** lists Mariani's fresco commissions as San Paolo fuori le mura, Santa Maria di Loreto, Santa Maria in Trastevere, and the Palazzo delle Finanze (1878). **The Palazzo di Giustizia is not among them** **[A: negative evidence]**.
2. **it.wikipedia, 'Palazzo di Giustizia (Roma)'** attributes the Aula Magna fresco cycle *La Scuola di diritto di Roma* to **Cesare Maccari**, abandoned in 1909 after his paralysis and finished by Paride Pascucci through 1918, long after Collivadino left Rome. The near-identical names Mariani/Maccari are an obvious contamination risk. **[A].**
3. The **"Constitutional Court of Italy" had no Roman building in this period at all**: the Corte costituzionale was created by the 1948 constitution and seated only in 1955. The en.wikipedia phrasing is anachronistic on its face. **[A].**

What survives, at most, is generic decorative labour by a student on a large Roman building site (under construction 1889-1911 per it.wikipedia; the Collivadino articles' "1888-1910" is wrong). The journey file carries the stop **[R]**, with coordinates corrected to the building's own it.wikipedia position (41.9035, 12.4707) rather than the pool's 41.9057, 12.4667, which falls in the Prati street grid.

**The "seven-year course": arithmetic contradiction, flagged.** es.wikipedia says he completed a seven-year course at the Roman academy and returned in 1896 **[A for the return: "En 1896 regresó a Argentina"; en.wikipedia agrees]**. But 1891 + 7 = 1898. The seven years can only be counted from the **1889** departure. This report counts the San Luca period as **1891-1896, five years**, and flags the source's own figure as internally inconsistent. The pool's geography lens carried the error forward as a "1891-1898" Mariani period; corrected here.

**European travels.** Through France, Germany, Holland, Belgium, and England during the European years **[A: biografiasyvidas.com]**, but with **no dates, no cities, and no order**. The journey file places two stops (Paris, London) as **[R]** placeholders inside the 1890s and says so; nothing in any source names either city.

## 3. Return, the lithographs, and the Riachuelo, 1896-1903

**Return, 1896.** **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia].** He gains recognition for lithographic work in a romantic style **[A: es/en.wikipedia]**. Neither the printing house nor a single titled lithograph is named in any source reached. **Gap.**

**La Boca as working ground, c. 1896-1902.** **[R].** The subject matter is attested through the paintings; that his working base in these particular years was La Boca is an inference from the recurring geography of his work, not a documented studio address.

**'La hora del almuerzo', 1903.** Painted 1903; in the permanent collection of the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes. **[A: es.wikipedia works list; MNBA collection records].** The pool carried an alternate title, "La hora del reposo"; **no source consulted supports it** (es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia, biografiasyvidas, Wikimedia Commons all give only "La hora del almuerzo"). The alias is **deleted**, not carried as a variant.

## 4. The medals, 1903-1907

**Venice, 1903: gold medal** for 'La hora del almuerzo' **[A: es/en.wikipedia]**.

**Three Venice exhibitions, 1903-1907.** es.wikipedia: "Asistió a tres exposiciones internacionales en Venecia, de 1903 a 1907." **[A].** The pool's chronology lens collapsed all three onto 1903; corrected to the Biennale years 1903, 1905, 1907. Which works hung in 1905 and 1907 is **not stated anywhere reached**. **Gap.**

**St Louis, 1904: gold and silver** at the Louisiana Purchase Exposition. **[A: es/en.wikipedia].** The works exhibited are not named. **Gap.**

**Order of the Crown of Italy, 1905** **[A: es/en.wikipedia]**. Ceremony, venue and grade are undocumented; the Quirinale placement is **[R]** and labelled so.

**Accademia di Brera, honorary member: year unknown.** en.wikipedia's phrasing invites reading 1905 onto the Brera honour, but the es.wikipedia sentence attaches 1905 to the Order of the Crown only ("Honrado con la Orden de la Corona de Italia en 1905, Collivadino se convirtió en miembro honorario de la Academia de Brera"), and biografiasyvidas gives the honour with no year at all. **Membership [A]; year [R], undated, placed mid-range.**

## 5. Nexus and the Academy, 1907-1935

**Grupo Nexus, 1907.** Founding year **[A: es/en.wikipedia]**. Two corrections to the pool's version:

- **Role.** es.wikipedia says Collivadino "se incorporó al Grupo Nexus," a group "dirigido por los pintores Fernando Fader y el escultor Rogelio Yrurtia." He **joined** a group led by others; "co-founder" overstates the sources.
- **Membership, contradiction flagged.** en.wikipedia lists **Martín Malharro** among the members. es.wikipedia's own Malharro article (1865-1911) never mentions Nexus, and the rosters carried across es.wikipedia's articles on Carnacini, Ripamonte, Rossi and Dresco give: Fader, Collivadino, Carlos Ripamonte, Cesáreo Bernaldo de Quirós, Ceferino Carnacini, Alberto María Rossi, Arturo Dresco, Yrurtia. **The two language versions disagree; this report follows the es rosters and records the en attribution as unconfirmed, not as refuted.**

**Directorship of the Academia Nacional de Bellas Artes, 1908.** **[A: es/en.wikipedia; biografiasyvidas.com].** The school is the one nationalised in 1905 out of the Sociedad Estímulo workshops, at **Av. Las Heras 1749**; the address is attested for the institution **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes Prilidiano Pueyrredón']** but **no source reached places Collivadino's own office there**, and the identification is **[R]** on institutional continuity alone.

**Students.** Lino Enea Spilimbergo, Héctor Basaldúa, Benito Quinquela Martín **[A: biografiasyvidas.com]**; Raquel Forner appears at **[R]** only. He establishes specialised decoration workshops at the Ernesto de la Cárcova school **[A: biografiasyvidas.com]**, and is credited with founding the printmaking chair **[R]**.

**Quinquela Martín, dating corrected.** The pool put the mentorship in the 1920s-1930s. Quinquela Martín was born in 1890 and did his formative study c. 1907-1912; by the 1920s he was an established painter exhibiting internationally. The mentorship, if it sits anywhere, sits in the **1910s**. **[A for the relationship; R for any date.]**

**Troiano Troiani's chair, c. 1915.** Troiani teaches sculpture at the same Las Heras school through the decades of Collivadino's directorship. **[A for both posts, from separate sources; no document reached places the two men in a room together, and neither biography mentions the other.]** **[R] on any personal relation.**

**The 1910 Centenary Exhibition: venue corrected.** The pool placed him in Parque 3 de Febrero, Palermo. es.wikipedia's article on the Exposición Internacional del Centenario puts the **Bellas Artes section in the Pabellón Argentino on calle Arenales at Plaza San Martín**; Palermo held Agriculture/Livestock and Industry. **[A].** Pin moved.

**Teatro Colón.** Scenic director, later president of its board **[A: es.wikipedia]**, **undated in every source reached**. **Gap.**

**'La Higuerita'.** He creates a theatre of his own by this name. **[A: es.wikipedia, name and existence only].** Neither date nor address survives in anything reached. **Gap**, carried as **[R]** at the generic Buenos Aires pin.

**'Usinas', 1914** and **'El Riachuelo', 1916** **[A: es.wikipedia works list; MNBA for El Riachuelo]**. The specific power plant behind 'Usinas' is **not identified in any source**; the Dock Sud placement is **[R]**. 'Paisaje de Tandil' is attested as a work **[A]**; the trips that produced it are undated **[R]**.

**Retirement from teaching, 1935.** **[A: all three principal sources].**

## 6. The last posts, 1939-1945

**Prilidiano Pueyrredón, 1939-1944.** Interim director of the Manuel Belgrano preparatory school; helps organise the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes Prilidiano Pueyrredón and directs it until 1944. **[A: biografiasyvidas.com; es/en.wikipedia].** Note that the Las Heras school took the Pueyrredón name by decree only in **1940** **[A: es.wikipedia, Decree 72154 of 1940]**, so a 1939 appointment predates the name.

**The Museo de Calcos / Ernesto de la Cárcova: contradiction, unresolved.** Both Collivadino articles credit him with helping establish the Museo de Calcos y Escultura Comparada "en 1941," later called the Ernesto de la Cárcova. **es.wikipedia's own article on the Museo de la Cárcova** (Av. España 1701) says it was inaugurated in **1928**, opened to the public in **1932** under Ángel Guido, carried de la Cárcova's name from the outset, and **never mentions Collivadino at all**. Thirteen years apart, the "later renamed" clause also unsupported. **This report does not choose between them.** The journey file carries the stop **[R]**, at the museum's real coordinates (approx. -34.6222, -58.3560), not the pool's -34.585, -58.3945, which lands 5.5 km away in Recoleta.

**Forced retirement, 1944, with the year contested by the one literary source.** Both language versions give **1944**, under the military government of General Pedro Pablo Ramírez, described as hostile to European artistic influence. **[A].** The pool had flagged a 1943-vs-1944 disagreement between the two Wikipedias; **there is none, both say 1944**, and that phantom contradiction is deleted. Two real frictions remain:

1. **Ramírez was deposed on 24 February 1944.** A 1944 dismissal attributed to his government must fall in January or February; Edelmiro Farrell held power for the rest of the year. **[A: political chronology].**
2. **Geno Díaz's memoir dates the expulsion to the coup itself, 4 June 1943**, not to 1944 (quoted below). Memoirist and encyclopaedia are a year apart. **Not adjudicated.**

**Death.** Dies in Buenos Aires **26 August 1945**, six days after his seventy-sixth birthday. **[A: es/en.wikipedia].**

**Burial: an honest gap.** **No source reached names a cemetery, plot, or pantheon.** He does not appear in Recoleta's published lists of notable interments. Searched and not found; recorded as unknown rather than guessed. **[R: absence].**

## 7. Quotations available

Only two verbatim passages were secured, and **neither is by Collivadino**. No letter, diary entry, lecture, or interview in his own voice was reached, a substantial gap for a man who ran a national art school for twenty-seven years. Malosetti Costa's 2008 anthology *Cuadros de viaje* is said to print his own writings from the European years; **it was not obtainable**, and it is the obvious next source.

1. **Geno Díaz, *Genocidio II*** (Buenos Aires: Emecé, 1985), p. 20, quoted as a block quotation with footnote in es.wikipedia and verified against that article's raw wikitext:
   > El 4 de junio [de 1943], estalló la revolución, y expulsó al director de la escuela, don Pío Collivadino, un hombre de grandes conocimientos técnicos, un reconocido artista que había dedicado su vida a la Escuela. [...] Fue cruelmente reemplazado por uno de esos perversos incompetentes, y el ilustre maestro anciano murió de tristeza.

   **[A at the Wikipedia layer only.]** The 1985 Emecé volume was not consulted. Díaz is a memoirist and satirist; "murió de tristeza" is his framing, used in the journey file **as attributed opinion inside a quote field, never as narration**. Note the wording correction against the pool, which had "un hombre de gran conocimiento técnico" and elided the clause about his having devoted his life to the school.

2. **Laura Malosetti Costa, *Collivadino*** (El Ateneo, 2007), quoted in es.wikipedia, note 9, on what his canvases hold:
   > el perfume de otros tiempos, la nostalgia de lo apenas perdido

   **[A at the Wikipedia layer].** The monograph itself was not reachable.

## 9. Corpus adjacencies (not asserted as personal relations)

The Argentine-art cluster around this subject holds many co-locations and few documented relations.

- **Named in the journey file:** **Ángel della Valle**, teaching drawing at the Sociedad Estímulo through the years Collivadino studied there (stated as a fact of the institution, not a claim of tutelage); **Troiano Troiani**, holding the chair of sculpture at the Las Heras school through Collivadino's directorship of it.
- **Co-location only, deliberately not named in any campa:** Mario Palanti and Alberto Gerchunoff (same port, same decade); Nazareno Orlandi (Italian-born muralist, same city and era, no documented contact); Pedro Subercaseaux (Roman training, different masters); Leo Chiachio and Franco Fasoli (same school lineage, generations later); Xul Solar, Dalila Puzzovio and Auguste Rodin (works in the same museum).

2. What did he actually paint or decorate in Rome, if anything, and under whose contract?

4. What years did he serve at the Teatro Colón, and in which capacity when?
