# Archimedes of Syracuse: research report
*2026-08-29. Compiled for `archimedes.journey.json`. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or modern inference. Contradictions are flagged and left standing, not adjudicated.*

**There is no ancient biography of Archimedes.** Plutarch's *Life* is of Marcellus. What survives is three bodies of evidence that barely touch:

1. **His own treatises**, technical and almost impersonal, but whose *prefaces* are the only first-person material there is: they name his correspondents, record a quarrel over plagiarism, and dedicate a book to a prince.
2. **Three Roman narratives of the siege of 213-212 BCE** (Polybius, Livy, Plutarch), the earliest by a man born about a decade after Archimedes died.
3. **Anecdotes with no early attestation** (the crown and the bath; the lever epigram; the burning mirrors), each first appearing two to fifteen centuries after his death.

Nearly every famous thing about him is category 3; nearly everything he actually did is category 1, and is unquotable. The file keeps that asymmetry visible rather than smoothing it.

## 1. Birth, family, and the years before the record (c. 287 to c. 250 BCE)

- **Birth c. 287 BCE at Syracuse. [R]** No ancient source gives a birth date. The figure is modern arithmetic: **John Tzetzes** (12th c. CE, *Chiliades*) says Archimedes died at seventy-five, and the death year 212 is secure, so 287 falls out. The whole conventional chronology of his first seventy years rests on one late Byzantine sentence. MacTutor states this openly; the popular literature does not.
- **Birthplace neighborhood: gap.** No source places his birth in Ortygia or anywhere else within Syracuse; Ortygia is a convention, declared as such in `date_confidence`.
- **Father named Pheidias. [A, with two heavy qualifications]** The pool claimed Pheidias "measured the apparent diameter of the sun." **Overstated, and corrected.** *The Sand-Reckoner* names Pheidias only as the source of a **ratio** (the sun is twelve times the moon), in a list of figures alongside Eudoxus; the measurement described later in the same treatise is Archimedes' own. Second, the manuscript is corrupt at exactly that point, and the words "Pheidias my father" are **Blass's emendation**. The father is attested by an editor's repair of a damaged line.
- **Studied at Alexandria. [R]** No surviving ancient text says Archimedes went to Egypt. The inference rests wholly on his correspondence with Mouseion men and the collegial register in which he writes to them. MacTutor calls it "very probable"; it is not attested. Every Alexandria stop states in its confidence field that no voyage is on record. **Mother, wife, children, teachers, students: total gap**, not a name and not an anecdote.

## 2. The correspondence (c. 250 to c. 230 BCE)

The only phase where Archimedes speaks in his own voice about his own life.

- **Conon of Samos. [A] that he sent Conon propositions; [R] that he addressed treatises to him.** The pool claimed Archimedes "addressed mathematical treatises and problem-letters to Conon," citing the prefaces of *On the Sphere and Cylinder* and *Quadrature of the Parabola*. **Wrong, and corrected.** Both are addressed to **Dositheus**; *Quadrature of the Parabola* opens "Archimedes to Dositheus, greeting. When I heard that Conon, who was my friend in his lifetime, was dead..." What is attested is that he sent propositions to Conon during Conon's lifetime and says so **retrospectively, to a third party**. No surviving work of Archimedes is addressed to Conon.
- **The salted false propositions. [A]** *On Spirals*, preface: after Conon's death, unnamed mathematicians announced as their own the results Archimedes had circulated without proofs; he then planted false propositions in a later batch, so that anyone claiming universal proof would be exposed. The most personally revealing passage in the corpus, and uncontested.
- **Dositheus of Pelusium [A]**, recipient of *On the Sphere and Cylinder* I-II, *On Spirals*, *Quadrature of the Parabola*, *On Conoids and Spheroids*. **Eratosthenes of Cyrene [A]**, recipient of *The Method*, which survives **uniquely and incompletely**, in the Palimpsest and nowhere else. **Gelon, son of Hiero II [A]**, dedicatee of *The Sand-Reckoner*; Gelon predeceased his father (d. 216 BCE), the only firm terminus for that work.
- **Open question, left open:** whether any of this correspondence required physical travel. The Alexandria stops carry the doubt in their confidence fields.

## 3. The court of Hiero II and the machines (c. 250 to 215 BCE)

- **"Kinsman and friend" of Hiero II. [R]** Plutarch, *Marcellus* 14, 300+ years later. The degree of kinship is never specified and may be a later reader's rationalization of why a king kept a geometer.
- **The pulley and the laden ship. [R]** Plutarch, *Marcellus* 14, quoted verbatim in the file. Plutarch names only "the arsenal"; the placement at the small harbour rather than the Great Harbour is plausible but **unattested**, and says so in the confidence field.
- **"Give me a place to stand." [R], and the pool's verdict on it was itself wrong.** The pool contained a refutation saying the epigram could not be located in any primary text. **The verification pass overturned that**: the saying *is* attributed to Archimedes by **Pappus of Alexandria, *Collection* VIII** (ed. Hultsch 1878, p. 1060), c. 340 CE. What is genuinely unverifiable is the wording; there is no fixed Greek form and no fixed English one, and the five standard translations (Heath, Dijksterhuis, Thomas, Bartlett's, ODQ) all differ. Plutarch preserves a **differently worded** version of the same boast, and Tzetzes a Doric one. The file quotes Heath's rendering with Pappus cited. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: three ancient authors, three different sentences, none of them Archimedes.**
- **The *Syracusia*. [A] via Athenaeus quoting Moschion.** Stone-thrower, bilge screw, and windlass launch attributed to Archimedes, by authors writing long after the fact: this supports a *reputation* for large-scale engineering rather than documented sole authorship. The gift to Ptolemy, the grain cargo, and the renaming to *Alexandris* are **[A]**; **which** Ptolemy and the date are **[R]**. The pool tagged **Ptolemy III** and **c. 240 BCE** as attested; Athenaeus says only "King Ptolemy at Alexandria," so both are demoted.
- **The crown and the bath. [R], single-source.** **Vitruvius**, *De architectura* IX pref., two centuries later, is the *only* ancient source; no Archimedean text mentions it. Quoted verbatim in the file, where "heureka" appears **once**, not twice as popularly rendered. The physical objection, that displacement is too imprecise for a wreath-shaped crown, is old, and is why Galileo wrote *La bilancetta*.

## 4. The siege (213-212 BCE)

- **Date correction throughout. [A]** The pool dated several siege stops to **214 BCE**. **Corrected to 213**: 214 is the year of Hieronymus' assassination and Marcellus' consulship, while operations against the city run **spring 213 to autumn 212**.
- **Polybius, *Histories* 8 is the earliest and best source [A]**, and the technical content is coherent: artillery graduated by range; short-range "scorpions" behind hand-breadth loopholes; beams dropping stones "weighing as much as ten talents"; the crane-mounted **iron hand** on a chain that lifted ships by the prow and capsized them (8.6). The modern name "Claw of Archimedes" is a **later coinage**, not an ancient technical term.
- **Quote correction. [A]** The pool gave Polybius 8.3.3 in the singular ("he did not reckon with the ability of Archimedes"). The Loeb text reads **plural**; the subject is both Roman commanders, Appius Claudius and Marcellus. Corrected in the file's quote field.
- **The sambucae [A]**, paired ships carrying wall-height assault ladders raised by pulleys, neutralized by the dropped beams. **Eight months of failed assault, then blockade [A]** (Polybius 8).
- **The burning mirrors: attested absence. [R], negative.** Neither Polybius, Livy, nor Plutarch mentions them. The legend appears ambiguously in Lucian (2nd c. CE), is developed technically by **Anthemius of Tralles** (6th c.), then repeated as history by Zonaras and Tzetzes. It has its own stop in the file, framed as an absence, because the mirrors are the best-known thing about the siege and the worst attested.
- **Coordinate corrections applied.** Four pool pins sat in open water or in the wrong place: Castello Eurialo to **37.0912, 15.2228**; the Achradina sea wall west to **37.068, 15.292**; the Hexapylon east to **37.089, 15.271** (Scala Greca, not the western plateau); the Great Harbour walls and the *Syracusia* shipyard onto shoreline points, no ancient source fixing either.

## 5. The sack and the death (autumn 212 BCE)

- **The wall was never beaten. [A]** Livy 25.23-24: a festival of Diana, an unexpectedly low and unguarded stretch, night ladders, the Hexapylon postern forced from inside. The exact segment is modern inference.
- **Killed by a Roman soldier during the sack. [A]** This much is stable across all sources. **Everything else about the death is contradictory, and the file leaves it contradictory.** Four incompatible accounts:
  - **Livy 25.31 [A]**: "intentum formis quas in puluere descripserat **ab ignaro milite quis esset** interfectum." The ignorance belongs to **the soldier**, who did not know who he was. **The pool's claim that Livy says Archimedes did not know the city had fallen is false and was corrected**; that motif is Valerius Maximus'. Livy gives **no prior order** to spare him.
  - **Valerius Maximus 8.7 ext. 7 [R]**: Archimedes asks the intruder not to disturb "istum" (the diagram) and is **"obtruncatus"**, cut down, **not beheaded**. The pool's "beheaded" was corrected. Valerius *does* record the general's order of mercy.
  - **Plutarch, *Marcellus* 19 [A]**: three further versions, between which Plutarch declines to choose. Run through in anger for refusing to leave an unfinished problem; killed after asking for time to finish; killed by soldiers who took his instruments for valuables while he carried a problem to Marcellus.
  - **"Noli turbare circulos meos" [R]**: not securely attested verbatim anywhere early, a later compression of Valerius' plea and Plutarch's refusal. The file does not use it as a quote.
- **Marcellus' reaction. [A]** All sources agree he took the death hard, treated the killer as polluted, arranged burial, and protected the relatives. **Whether he had issued a prior order to spare Archimedes is contested**: Valerius Maximus and Plutarch say yes, Livy records no such order. The pool's "according to all the death narratives" was corrected; the contradiction is flagged in the stop's confidence field and left standing. **Burial place: gap**, no source records where the original grave was dug.

## 6. The objects and the text after him

- **The two spheres taken to Rome. [A]** Cicero, *De re publica* 1: a celestial globe placed in the Temple of Honos et Virtus outside the Porta Capena, and a hollow geared mechanism showing sun, moon, and five planets, kept privately by the Marcelli. **Correction applied**: the 166 BCE demonstration by **Gaius Sulpicius Gallus** took place at the house of **M. Claudius Marcellus, Gallus' colleague in the consulship of 166 and grandson of the captor of Syracuse**, not, as the pool twice stated, at the elder Marcellus' household; the elder Marcellus had been dead since 208.
- **Cicero and the tomb, 75 BCE. [A]** First-person testimony, *Tusc.* 5.64-66, written c. 45 BCE about a discovery thirty years earlier. **Correction applied**: the Latin is "ad portas **Agragantinas**," the **Agrigentine** gate on the western approach. "Gate Achradinae" is an artefact of Yonge's older English translation; Achradina is a different quarter on the opposite side of the city, and the two are **not** alternative names for one place. The pool's coordinate (37.071, 15.302, Achradina east coast) followed the bad translation and was dropped for the canonical atlas pin.
- **Grotticelle "Tomb of Archimedes." [R], misidentification.** Roman-period rock-cut columbarium; does not match Cicero's description; kilometre-scale pool coordinate error corrected to **37.0784, 15.2812** (OSM, Viale Teracati). In the file precisely *because* it is the wrong site people are shown.
- **The Palimpsest. [A]** Copied at Constantinople **c. 950** (the pool's chronology lens said 975 and its afterlife lens said 950; **corrected to c. 950**, removing the internal contradiction), from a 6th-century compilation associated with **Isidore of Miletus**. Overwritten as a euchologion in **1229** (colophon decoded only in 2002). Catalogued by **Papadopoulos-Kerameus** at the Jerusalem Metochion in 1899; identified and photographed by **Heiberg** at Constantinople in 1906. Sold at **Christie's, New York, 29 October 1998**, after the Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Jerusalem's suit failed chiefly on limitations grounds. Imaged at the **Walters, 1999-2008**, data release 29 October 2008, scholarly edition 2011: **the pool's "1999-2011" conflated imaging with publication and was split.** Walters coordinate corrected to **39.2964, -76.6165**.
- **Printed transmission. [A]** *Editio princeps* Basel 1544 (Herwagen and Venatorius); Heiberg's *Opera Omnia* 1880-81, revised 1910-15; Heath's *Works* 1897 with post-1906 supplement; Netz's series from 2004. **The Cattle Problem [R]** was published by **Lessing** in 1773 from a Wolfenbüttel manuscript (coordinate corrected to **52.164, 10.531**), and survives outside the main manuscript tradition, so its attribution is not secure.

## Interlocks used, and interlocks declined

**Named in a campa (2):**

- **`cicero`**, in "Syracuse, the column above the briers." He wrote substantively about Archimedes and is the sole witness to the monument, a real one-directional relation. **The stop is Archimedes' grave, not Cicero's visit**; Cicero's own file already owns the rediscovery scene and this file does not restage it.
- **`galileo_galilei`**, in "Syracuse, the crown and the bath," as the author of *La bilancetta*, the reconstruction of how the crown test must actually have been done. Cited on Archimedes' own stop for the episode, per the standing rule.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** the tomb stop uses **37.0774, 15.2735**, exactly as `cicero.journey.json` carries it; the generic Syracuse pin **37.0755, 15.2866** is exactly `plato.journey.json`'s, and matches the pool's own generic Syracuse. Existing files pin Syracuse four different ways (cicero 15.2735, plato 15.2866 and 15.2735, pindar 15.2933, simonides 15.2735); this file uses the two pairs above rather than adding a fifth.

**Declined, with reasons:**

- **`plato`, `pindar`, `simonides_of_ceos`, `diogenes_of_sinope`, `apostle_paul`, `empress_theodora`**: Syracuse or the Syracusan tyrant tradition only. Shared geography, no relation to Archimedes. **`hannibal`**: the link is Marcellus, a real actor in Archimedes' story but not a relation between Hannibal and Archimedes.
- **`justinian_i`**: the real link is **Isidore of Miletus**, who edited Archimedes and stands behind the Palimpsest's 6th-century ancestor. Isidore is named in the campa; Justinian is not, having no relation to Archimedes.
- **`vladimir_lenin`** (lever epigram in *What Is to Be Done?*), **`robert_fludd`** (perpetual-motion mill on an Archimedes screw), **`san_martin`** (Fray Luis Beltrán called "the Archimedes of the army"), **`edward_witten`** (Fields Medal iconography), **`frederick_law_olmsted` / `mark_lombardi` / `l_frank_baum`** (Syracuse, New York): later parties citing, using, or named after Archimedes. Lenin's and Fludd's are technically real one-directional relations, but each would put a modern sentence inside a third-century-BCE stop for no gain. Declined as name-drops and **recorded rather than deleted**, so the operator can overrule.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Archimedes is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`**, so no apparatus stops were written and none were needed: he has no theoretical framework being applied backward to material he never treated. No forward-reaching framework is applied anywhere in the file, and nothing was written into any other traveler's file.

One direction question is flagged rather than silently decided. **Direction caught: forward.** The final segment, "The Scraped Parchment" (8 stops, 950 to 1999 CE), and two stops in "The Objects That Outlived Him" are **posthumous**, dated long after this file's clock stops in 212 BCE. They are **object history**, the fate of manuscripts and machines he made, not anyone's framework applied, and they follow existing corpus practice (`abdelkader.journey.json` closes on the 1966 repatriation of his remains). Each says so in `date_confidence`. **If the operator reads the block rule in EXCEPTIONS.md as barring posthumous object stops on a subject's own file, that is the material to cut, and it is 8 of 43 stops.** It is not being cut on an agent's own judgement.

## Honest gaps

- No birth date, birthplace, mother, wife, children, teacher, or student is recorded anywhere. No ancient text places him outside Sicily; the Alexandria stops are inference from letters, and say so.
- The original grave's location is unrecorded, Cicero's monument is lost and cannot be relocated, and the site shown to tourists is the wrong one. The planetary mechanism is lost; its relation to the Antikythera mechanism is unestablished and is not asserted.
- **Not fetched in this pass:** the Latin of Valerius Maximus 8.7 ext. 7 (pool correction notes only); the *Patriarchate of Jerusalem v. Christie's* court record. The Berlin statue (Gerhard Thieme, 1969, Archenhold Observatory) and the Paruta/Agostini coin were verified in the pool but left out as too thin to carry a stop.
- **Not resolved:** the Palimpsest sale price, 2 million against 2.2 million US dollars across two sources. The file says "about two million dollars."
