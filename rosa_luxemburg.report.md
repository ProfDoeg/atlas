# Rosa Luxemburg (Rozalia Luksenburg), 1871-1919
*Research report for `rosa_luxemburg.journey.json`. 45 stops, 8 segments, calendar gregorian.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or unresolved hypothesis.
Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Coordinate fixes are tabled near the end.

---

## 1. Zamość and Warsaw, 1871-1889

**Birth**, 5 March 1871, Zamość, Lublin Governorate, Congress Poland, Russian Empire; fifth and
youngest child of the timber merchant Eliasz Luxemburg and Lina Löwenstein, a Jewish family
**[A: en.wikipedia]**. Birth house given as **ul. Staszica 37** **[A: de.wikipedia for the number;
en.wikipedia's Zamość article for the address]**, on encyclopaedic report, not a civil register.
*Coordinate spread, flagged:* three lenses gave three pins for the one birthplace, about 600 m apart;
the journey uses **50.7175, 23.2514**, the value tied to the street address.

**Move to Warsaw, 1873 [A: en.wikipedia]**. No street address for the family residence was found;
Warsaw-centre placeholder pin. **The hip. [R]** Around her fifth year, an ailment leaving a permanent
limp; the diagnosis is genuinely unsettled, the biographical tradition (Nettl, Frölich) carrying both
congenital dislocation and a misdiagnosed tuberculosis-related disease, and **no clinical record
survives**.

**Schooling. [A/R]** Second Girls' Gymnasium, a Russian-imperial school running a Jewish admissions
quota; attendance from 1884 **[A: de.wikipedia]**; **leaving certificate dated 14 June 1887**, German
Federal Archives, Nachlass Rosa Luxemburg NY 4002 **[A: weimar.bundesarchiv.de]**. The story that a
**gold medal was withheld for "oppositional" conduct is [R]**: repeated across biographies, no
surviving administrative document.

**Second Proletariat, 1887 [A: en.wikipedia]**, year only. **Flight, 1889. [A] for the fact, [R] for
the scene.** Fled to escape arrest, in Zurich by early 1889 **[A]**. The escape-under-straw-in-a-cart detail rests on later recollection, no police or border
record. **No source names the crossing point**; the pin near Aleksandrów Kujawski declares itself a
placeholder and is the weakest coordinate in the file.

## 2. Zurich, 1889-1898

**Matriculation. CONTRADICTION, flagged, one reading led.** en.wikipedia gives **1890**; de.wikipedia
**October 1889**, consistent with arrival in early 1889; pl.wikipedia has her in philosophy on
arrival, moving to *Staatswissenschaften* in **1892**. The journey leads with October 1889 and
records 1890 as the weaker reading. Day not recorded.

**Leo Jogiches [A]**. Met in the Zurich émigré milieu; roughly fifteen years of combined romantic and
political partnership. No precise start date exists; the stop is dated 1891 only to order the
segment. Jogiches kept out of print, and his organizational role is reconstructed from her letters.

**Zurich congress of the Second International, 6-12 August 1893 [A: de.wikipedia dates the
congress]**. The credentials fight with the Polish Socialist Party belongs **here**.

**SDKP and *Sprawa Robotnicza*, 1893. CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** The chronology lens founds both
among the **Zurich** exiles; the geography lens founds the paper in **Paris**. Both say 1893. The
journey keeps the Paris pin with a placeholder warning and states the conflict in both
`date_confidence` and the campa. Nothing reachable reconciles them.

**London 1896. ERROR IN THE ORIGINAL DOSSIER, corrected.** The dossier staged London as a *repeat
credentials battle*. It was not: en.wikipedia puts the mandate fight at **Zurich 1893** and records
London as passing a compromise resolution on the right of all nations to self-determination,
**without specific mention of Poland**. The stop was rewritten. Venue not confirmed.

**Doctorate, 1897 [A]**. *Die industrielle Entwicklung Polens*, **magna cum laude, May 1897**
**[A: de.wikipedia]**; the MIA translator's note calls it the first comprehensive economic history of
Poland ever published **[A: marxists.org]**. Day not recorded.

**Marriage to Gustav Lübeck, Basel, 19 April 1898 [A: de.wikipedia]**; divorced 1903.

## 3. Berlin and the German party, 1898-1905

**Arrival 12 May 1898, Cuxhavener Straße 2 [A: de.wikipedia]**.

**Upper Silesia, 1898 Reichstag campaign [A: en.wikipedia]**: no speaking site is documented, the pin
marks Oppeln as representative, month unknown. **Dresden, *Sächsische Arbeiterzeitung*, chief editor,
1898-1899 [A: marxists.org glossary; de.wikipedia]**, tenure cut short by conflict with colleagues,
start month not recorded.

***Sozialreform oder Revolution?*** **[A]**. The glossary dates it 1899; the MIA text of ch. 8 quoted
in the journey is the **1900** edition. Both dates are carried in `date_confidence`. Serialized in
the *Leipziger Volkszeitung*, for which she wrote 1899-1913 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Zwickau, 1904. ERROR IN THE ORIGINAL DOSSIER, corrected.** The dossier said "imprisoned roughly
three months," conflating **sentence** with **time served**. en.wikipedia: sentenced to three months
in **July 1904**, entered Zwickau **at the end of August**, released **15 October 1904** after about
**six weeks**. The stop is dated 31 August; the day of entry is not recorded.

## 4. 1905-1907

**Clandestine return to Warsaw, 28 December 1905, with Jogiches, on false papers [A:
en.wikipedia]**. **Arrest 4 March 1906 [A]**; Town Hall jail, then **Pawiak**, then **Pavilion X of
the Warsaw Citadel [A: en.wikipedia]**; the Town Hall jail is folded into the Pawiak stop. **Gap:**
no separate published coordinate exists for Pavilion X, so the pin is the Citadel perimeter.

**Release. CONTRADICTION, flagged, one reading led.** pl.wikipedia and the Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung
chronology give **28 June 1906**, on health grounds, bail pending trial. en.wikipedia gives **28 July
1906** with the well-sourced **3,000 roubles bail paid by her brother Józef**. The journey dates the
stop 28 June, keeps the bail detail, names both dates. (The dossier had picked the minority reading.)

**Kuokkala, Finland, 1906 [A]**: wrote *Massenstreik, Partei und Gewerkschaften*, met Russian
revolutionaries including Lenin **[A: en.wikipedia]**. *Political correction:* in 1906 Kuokkala lay in
the **Grand Duchy of Finland, inside the Russian Empire**, Finland becoming independent only in 1917,
which is why it worked as a refuge. Month approximate.

**Mannheim SPD congress, September 1906 [A for the political content]**; the **Rosengarten venue is
[R]**, conventional for the era, not confirmed for this session. **SPD Parteischule, Berlin,
1907-1914 [A: en.wikipedia; marxists.org glossary]**, producing the unfinished *Einführung in die
Nationalökonomie*; **gap:** the school's address was never confirmed, placeholder pin. **Stuttgart
congress, August 1907 [A]**: antiwar amendment worked out with Lenin and Martov.

## 5. 1907-1914

**Cranachstraße 58. THREE ERRORS IN THE ORIGINAL DOSSIER, corrected.** (i) coordinate, see table;
(ii) the district is **Berlin-Schöneberg**, not Friedenau; (iii) the span "c.1911-1919" is
**unsupported**: de.wikipedia records only a commemorative plaque there, and in January 1919 she was
lodging at Mannheimer Straße 27. The journey drops the span and dates the stop 1913 only to order the
segment. The **herbarium is securely dated 1913-1918 [A: Bundesarchiv]**.

***Die Akkumulation des Kapitals*, 1913 [A: marxists.org glossary]**, year only; no documented
address of composition, placeholder pin.

**Bockenheim speech, September 1913 [A: en.wikipedia]**, day not recorded. **Frankfurt trial,
February 1914 [A]**: defended by **Paul Levi**, sentenced to **one year**; a second prosecution over
army mistreatment of recruits was **adjourned** rather than heard. Day not fixed; en.wikipedia's
sequence is followed over the glossary's looser dating.

## 6. The war and the prisons, 1914-1918

**4 August 1914 [A: en.wikipedia]**: the SPD Reichstag delegation votes war credits under party
discipline; the Gruppe Internationale forms in opposition with Liebknecht, Mehring, Zetkin and
Jogiches. **Barnimstraße women's prison [A: de.wikipedia]**: from **18 February 1915**, released
**February 1916**.

**The Junius Pamphlet. ERROR IN THE ORIGINAL DOSSIER, corrected.** Writing and publication were
collapsed into one 1916 date. en.wikipedia dates the text to **1915**, written during the
Barnimstraße imprisonment; de.wikipedia specifies publication **June 1916**; marxists.org files it
under `/archive/luxemburg/1915/junius/`. The journey states both.

**Protective custody from July 1916 [A]**, held first in Berlin, then **Wronke**, then **Breslau**
**[A: Bundesarchiv; en.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** the Berlin facility for the first weeks is **not
identified** in any source reached; those weeks are folded into the Wronke stop, unpinned, rather
than a building being invented for them.

**Breslau [A]**. The buffalo letter to Sophie Liebknecht is dated only to **mid-December 1917**. The
unfinished manuscript on the Russian Revolution was written there in **1918**; **the original is
considered lost [A: Bundesarchiv]**, the text known through **Paul Levi's 1922 edition**, itself a
contested editorial act. The sentence usually rendered "Freedom is always freedom for the one who
thinks differently" is a marginal note in it and **was never a delivered speech or a constitutional
maxim [A: Bundesarchiv; germanhistorydocs.org]**.

**Release. ERROR IN THE ORIGINAL DOSSIER, corrected in two places.** The geography lens gave **8
November 1918** twice. en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia both give **9 November** for the release from
Breslau and **10 November** for the arrival in Berlin. The journey uses those, noting that some
biographies do carry 8 November.

## 7. Berlin, November 1918 to January 1919

**Die Rote Fahne from 10 November 1918 [A]**, co-edited with Liebknecht; office address not
confirmed, placeholder pin. **KPD founding congress, 30 December 1918 to 1 January 1919 [A]**,
programme speech **31 December** **[A: marxists.org]**. With Paul Levi she argued **unsuccessfully for participation in the National
Assembly elections**; the majority voted to boycott **[A: en.wikipedia]**, the documentary answer to
the later myth that she urged immediate armed seizure of power. **Gap:** venue not confirmed.

**January 1919 [A]**: Eichhorn's dismissal, the rising, the government's use of Freikorps troops.
'Ordnung herrscht in Berlin' in *Die Rote Fahne*, **14 January 1919**, her last article, closing "I
was, I am, I shall be!" **[A: marxists.org, fetched directly]**.

**Arrest 15 January 1919, about 20:30, Mannheimer Straße 43 (today 27), Wilmersdorf, by a citizens'
militia [A: de.wikipedia, 'Ermordung von Rosa Luxemburg und Karl Liebknecht']**. **Hotel Eden [A]**:
arrival around 22:00, struck at the main entrance with a rifle butt by the soldier **Otto Runge**;
Liebknecht taken out first and shot in the Tiergarten. Both killings were carried out **on Captain
Waldemar Pabst's order [A: Deutsche Biographie]**.

**The shooting. CONTRADICTION, flagged, not resolved.** de.wikipedia follows **Klaus Gietinger's**
reconstruction naming the naval officer **Hermann Souchon**, after the car had travelled about forty
metres; the **early proceedings centred on the transport officer Kurt Vogel**; no contemporaneous
conviction settled it. Both readings sit in `date_confidence`. Also softened: de.wikipedia puts the
disposal **between the Lichtenstein- and Corneliusbrücke**, so the journey says "between two bridges."

*Pin conflict, declared:* `herbert_marcuse.journey.json` pins its "Landwehr Canal / Tiergarten" stop
at **52.5008, 13.3325**, about 1 km off, an admitted placeholder. Not inherited; any reconciliation
belongs on that file.

## 8. Afterlife of the body, the grave, and the sentence

**Recovery [A], upgraded from the dossier's [R].** de.wikipedia: body found **31 May 1919** (some
accounts 1 June), identified by **Mathilde Jacob**, buried **13 June 1919**; the dossier's "could not
independently confirm" hedge is resolved. Identification went by clothing, personal effects and
physical characteristics, **not** by any method a modern forensic service would call conclusive.

**Leo Jogiches [A], upgraded from [R].** de.wikipedia: murdered **10 March 1919** in the
**Berlin-Moabit remand prison**, shot in the back of the head by criminal officer **Ernst Tamschick**,
after publishing his investigation of the January killings in *Die Rote Fahne* on **12 February
1919**. **Deliberately given no stop of his own**: three sentences inside the campa of her body's
recovery, and Moabit is not pinned.

**Monument to the November Revolution [A], upgraded from [R].** Built spring 1926 to **Mies van der
Rohe's** design, **unveiled 13 June 1926**, the seventh anniversary of the burial; **damaged in
February 1933**, **dismantled to the foundations in early 1935** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. *Two dossier
claims dropped:* "January 1935" is **not supported** there, and the "12 m by 6 m" dimensions come
from en.wikipedia alone.

**Rosa-Luxemburg-Platz [A: en.wikipedia]**: Bülowplatz, KPD headquarters from 1926, one of the last
mass anti-Nazi demonstrations in Berlin on 25 January 1933; then Horst-Wessel-Platz 1933-45,
Liebknechtplatz 1945-47, Luxemburgplatz 1947, Rosa-Luxemburg-Platz 1969, day not given.

**The 1988 banner. DATE CORRECTED, discrepancy flagged.** germanhistorydocs.org titles its document
"January 18, 1988," which the dossier took as the event date; de.wikipedia on **Stephan Krawczyk**
has him arrested on **17 January 1988** at that same SED demonstration, so the march fell on **Sunday
17 January** and the GHDI date is the **reporting document's**. GHDI confirms twenty to thirty people
encircled by some fifty Stasi officers, but de.wikipedia puts **arrests that day at 105**.

**The 2009 Charité hypothesis. [R], and it stays [R].** Michael Tsokos proposed that an unidentified
headless, handless, footless female cadaver held at the Charité might be her remains, saying he was
"90 percent" convinced pending DNA. **DNA never confirmed identity**, and some reference works now
call the Friedrichsfelde grave a **cenotaph**. **Access failure:** the Reuters and Guardian articles
of May 2009 could **not** be fetched, so this rests on the dossier's characterization of them plus
the en.wikipedia cemetery article. Carried inside the burial stop, with no stop of its own.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Rosa Luxemburg is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing was silently deleted.

**Direction caught: FORWARD in every case below.** All are later people engaging with her, never her
with them. Under the block rule none may be named in a campa here, and none is; where documented and
one-directional the relation is carried as a source or suggested_ref on her own stop.

1. **`herbert_marcuse.journey.json`, the strongest and only attested interlock.** That file names her
   directly and by date, 15 January 1919, "beaten, shot, and thrown into the Landwehr canal," and
   treats it as Marcuse's break with the SPD. She influenced him; he could not influence her. Held as
   a **suggested_ref on the Landwehr Canal stop**. Promoting it into a campa is the operator's call.
2. **`gustav_landauer.journey.json`**: the 1933 Munich removal of "Marxist revolutionaries'" grave
   monuments is the same regime and class of act as the 1935 demolition of the Mies monument over her
   grave. A parallel, not a relation to her person; the demolition is stated on her own stop from its
   own sources.
3. **`erich_muhsam`, `otto_gross`, `elsa_lindenberg`, `erwin_schrodinger`**: shared city or
   neighbourhood only. **Shared geography is not a relation**; none is named. The interlock pass
   flagged Schrödinger as its own weakest item, and I agree.
4. **`antonio_gramsci`** (the Comintern's 1931 condemnation of "Luxemburgism" and his prison dissent
   against the Third Period line, one disciplinary current, posthumous on her side) and
   **`georges_sorel`** (a theoretical parallel on the general strike out of the same 1905 wave, **no
   attested contact either way**). Neither is named.

**Named in campa, each a relation she herself had, each an earlier or contemporary figure:**

- **`karl_marx`**: *Die Akkumulation des Kapitals* was written to finish an argument left open in the
  second volume of *Capital*. A direct one-directional relation, and Marx died in 1883: old hash, new
  block.
- **`vladimir_lenin`**: **met at Kuokkala in 1906 [A]**, **drafted the Stuttgart antiwar amendment
  with him and Martov in 1907 [A]**, wrote against his government in the Breslau manuscript. Named at
  all three stops.
- **`leon_trotsky`**: the Breslau chapter on dictatorship, source of the journey's best-known quote,
  is addressed to **Lenin and Trotsky by name**. Named once.
