# Charles Fourier (1772-1837): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-18 for `charles_fourier.journey.json`. Slug: `charles_fourier`.
QUEUE.md row 147. Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction,
tradition, or a claim its own stated source does not carry.*

**Disambiguation first.** This is Francois Marie Charles Fourier, theorist of
passionate attraction and the phalanstery, not Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier, the
mathematician of the heat equation, who already has a file here. No relation
beyond the surname; QUEUE.md anticipates the confusion at row 146. The two
overlap in Paris in the 1820s, which is the kind of coincidence the map already
shows without prose help. No cross-link is written into the journey file.

## 1. Besancon, the draper's son (1772-1790)

- Born 7 April 1772 on the Grande-Rue at the corner of the old ruelle Baron,
  above his father's drapery shop. **[A: fr.wikipedia, citing Beecher, *Charles
  Fourier: The Visionary and His World*]**
- Father dies 1781, leaving Charles two-fifths of an estate reported at over
  200,000 francs. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- Educated "chez les ecclesiastiques du college de Besancon" until about sixteen.
  **[A for the schooling: fr.wikipedia]** **[R for the building, which no
  reachable source identifies; 47.234 / 6.023 is a placement in the old town]**
  **Correction:** the pool's geography lens wrote "Jesuit/ecclesiastical," but the
  Jesuits were suppressed in France in 1764, eight years before Fourier was born.
  "Jesuit" is struck.

## 2. Apprenticeship and Revolution (1791-1799)

- Rouen apprenticeship from c. 1791, then Lyon with a merchant named Bousquet
  **[A: en.wikipedia]**; returns to Besancon early 1793 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Siege of Lyon, 1793. Largest correction in the dossier.** The pool said he
  "is arrested and in real danger of execution." fr.wikipedia says the opposite,
  verbatim: *"il combat avec les federalistes lyonnais, echappe a l'arrestation
  et revient a Besancon."* He escaped arrest. **[A]** Nothing reached supports
  "danger of execution" and it is not written. A brief imprisonment does occur,
  attached to the conscription rather than the reprisals: *"Brievement incarcere,
  il est enrole, le 2 prairial an II"*, that is 21 May 1794. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- Roughly eighteen months with the Army of the Rhine in the Palatinate, 8th
  chasseurs a cheval; discharged 3 pluviose an IV, 23 January 1796.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **Coordinate correction:** the pool pinned "Mainz / the
  Palatinat" at 50.0009 / 8.2739, which is neither Mainz (49.9929 / 8.2473) nor
  Palatine, sitting across the Rhine in Hesse; Mainz was Kurmainz territory. No
  source reached names the garrison, so Speyer (49.3172 / 8.4311) is pinned
  instead, inside the Palatinate, with the gap in `date_confidence`. **[R]**
- Commercial traveller and correspondence clerk at Lyon under the Consulate;
  his own words for it, "the knavery of merchants" and "deceitful and degrading
  duties." **[A: en.wikipedia]**

## 3. The rice in the harbour (c. 1799)

- At Marseille he witnesses, or helps carry out, the dumping of a shipload of
  rice rather than sell it cheap into a glut. **[R]** Universally repeated,
  Beecher placing it c. 1799 at Marseille, fr.wikipedia carrying it without a
  hard date. No contemporary independent record found; written as tradition.
- Marseille pin **43.2965 / 5.3698**, inherited byte-identical from the corpus
  canonical value (`andre_breton.journey.json`, `adnan_khashoggi` line 706).
- Clerking and travelling through Paris, Rouen, Lyon, Marseille, Bordeaux.
  **[A: en.wikipedia]** The Bordeaux Place de la Bourse pin is a placement in the
  merchant quarter, not an attested address. **[R]**
- A short-lived post heading a Paris statistics office. **[R: en.wikipedia gives
  the post and nothing else, no year, address or duration; placed c. 1814 purely
  to order the segment.]**

## 4. Theorie des quatre mouvements (1808), and Muiron

- Published anonymously, 1808. **[A]** **Imprint correction:** the pool said
  "published anonymously in Lyon"; fr.wikipedia's works list gives the title page
  as *"...Leipzig, 1808"*, a false imprint on a book actually printed at Lyon.
  The file says "printed at Lyon under a Leipzig imprint." The rue Merciere
  printing address is confirmed by nothing reachable; the pool tagged it [A] and
  it is downgraded here to **[R]**, the file saying the shop is not identified.
- **The antisemitism.** The book denounces Jews collectively, identifying them
  with commerce, fraud, deceit and parasitism, calling them the leprosy and the
  ruin of the body politic, and proposing to put them to farm labour in the
  phalansteries. **[A: en.wikipedia quoting the text; text on French Wikisource]**
  Stated flat in its own stop in the journey file, not softened and not made
  resonant.
- Just Muiron, of Besancon, first disciple, after reading the book.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Just Muiron des 1814"]** The pool's geography lens said
  c. 1803, impossible for a man converted by a book of 1808; the chronology lens
  has 1814 and is right.

## 5. Talissieu, and the book he did not print (1815-1821)

- Winter 1815-16 at Talissieu in the Bugey. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
  **Household correction:** the pool put his sister Mme Parrat-Brillat at
  Talissieu; fr.wikipedia says he stayed *chez ses nieces de Rubat*, moving to
  Mme Parrat-Brillat only after a falling-out and staying to 1820. That address
  is not located by anything reachable, and is stated as a gap. **Coordinate
  correction:** pool 45.8333 / 5.6604; the Talissieu article gives
  **45.8680 / 5.7194**, four kilometres away and inside the commune.
- *Le Nouveau Monde amoureux* composed here c. 1816-18, unpublished in his
  lifetime. **[A for the fact and the non-publication; R for the window, which is
  Beecher's and Debout's dating, not a dated manuscript]**
- *De la deterioration materielle de la planete.* **Correction: the pool filed it
  as an 1836 Paris composition.** fr.wikipedia dates composition to **1821** and
  publication to **1847, posthumous, in La Phalange**: fifteen years out and
  misfiled as a lifetime work. The place of writing is not established.

## 6. Paris: the Traite, the rooms, the Thursdays (1822-1833)

- Arrives November 1822 with the *Traite*, lodging at 14 rue de
  Grenelle-Saint-Honore chez M. Saussol. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **Not his first
  Paris visit:** the earlier statistics post and his own four-apples anecdote both
  precede it, so the file says "arrives to launch the Traite." **Coordinate
  correction:** pool 48.8656 / 2.3315, apparently copied from the Saint-Roch pin
  950 m away; the street is the southern section of today's rue
  Jean-Jacques-Rousseau, merged 1868, at **48.8642 / 2.3439**.
- Hotel Saint-Roch, rue Neuve-Saint-Roch, 1823. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- Hotel de Hollande, 45 rue de Richelieu. **[A: fr.wikipedia, "le 15 decembre
  1825, il descend a l'hotel de Hollande, 45, rue Richelieu"]** The pool said
  January 1826, six weeks late.
- **The Thursday dinners. [R]** The pool described a "standing daily noon
  appointment," "the millionaire at noon." fr.wikipedia describes a weekly
  Thursday dinner, 1825 to 1835, at which he finally dined alone for ten years,
  flags the story as possibly fabricated, and credits its circulation to
  Beranger rather than to disciples' accounts. All four points are in the file.
- **Victor Considerant. Correction: wrong year, place and status.** The pool had
  a "young Polytechnicien" rallying in Paris in 1825. fr.wikipedia's Considerant
  article: won to Fourierism in **1824 at the lycee de Besancon** while preparing
  the Polytechnique exam, through **Clarisse Vigoureux**, herself brought in by
  Muiron; entered Polytechnique **1826**; met Fourier in Paris after. The
  Besancon conversion is not staged as a scene, Fourier not being there. **[A]**
- *Le Nouveau Monde industriel et societaire*, 1829. **[A]**
- *Le Phalanstere* launched 1832, and Albert Brisbane seeks Fourier out and
  studies with him in Paris. **[A for the journal and the study, the latter from
  Beecher and the American Fourierist historiography; R for the 5 rue Joquelet
  address, which comes from the pool and is confirmed by nothing reached]**
- **Conde-sur-Vesgre, 1832-33.** About 1,100 associates by autumn 1833, about 200
  after the winter. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **Correction: "fails entirely" is
  wrong.** The Conde-sur-Vesgre article dates the attempt from 1832 and records
  that La Colonie still functions on rules its members set in 1850 and houses
  about a dozen families. The phalanstery collapsed; the association did not.
  Both halves are written. **Gap:** no source says whether Fourier ever visited,
  so the stop is written as his disciples' trial of his system.

## 7. The last rooms, and the Palestine proposal (1834-1837)

- Final residence 9 rue Saint-Pierre-Montmartre from April 1834.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **Coordinate correction, and a deleted phrase.** The pool
  put this "at the foot of the Montmartre butte" at 48.8845 / 2.3436. The street
  was in the 2nd arrondissement, behind the Bourse, renamed **rue Paul-Lelong**
  in 1847; fr.wikipedia gives **48.8678 / 2.3422** and records number 9 as
  Fourier's place of death on 10 October 1837. The pool's pin is 1.9 km north.
  The butte phrase is deleted: the address is two kilometres from it.
- **La Fausse Industrie and the Jewish return to Palestine. [R]** Second largest
  correction. The pool asserted a Jewish monarchy, a flag, consuls, Jerusalem as
  named seat, and James de Rothschild imagined as king of Judea. en.wikipedia,
  the cited source, says only: *"By the end of his life, he advocated the return
  of Jews to Palestine with the assistance of the Rothschilds."* No mention of
  *La Fausse Industrie*, Judea, monarchy, flag, consuls, or a king. Silberner is
  behind JSTOR and was not reached. The file keeps the proposal and the family
  name and nothing else; the "six decades before *Der Judenstaat*" comparison is
  the pool's gloss. **The Jerusalem pin (31.7767 / 35.2345) is refused:** the pool
  conceded in the same entry that Fourier never travelled to Palestine, so the
  proposal is written at his desk in the 2nd arrondissement, where he wrote it.
- Dies 10 October 1837; buried at the cimetiere de Montmartre; never married.
  **[A: both Wikipedias]**

## 8. Afterlife

- **Considerant** organises the movement after 1837. **[A]** **Alphonse
  Toussenel**, disciple, publishes *Les Juifs, rois de l'epoque* (1845), turning
  Fourier's stereotype into a standalone tract that fed later French
  antisemitism. **[A: standard secondary literature, Silberner, not read direct]**
- **Brook Farm**, founded 1841 by George and Sophia Ripley, Fourierist
  constitution 1844, Phalanstery burned 3 March 1846 (two hours, 7,000 dollars,
  uninsured), closed by 1847, Hawthorne an early member. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **North American Phalanx**, Colts Neck Township, founded September 1843, mill
  fire 10 September 1854, terminated January 1856. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **La Reunion, Texas**: colonists arrived 16 June 1855, dissolution announced by
  Allyre Bureau 28 January 1857, so eighteen months and not the pool's "few
  years." **[A: Texas State Historical Association]**
- **All three American pins were wrong in the pool**, by 1.8, 3.7 and about 4 km
  respectively, and are corrected in the journey file's `date_confidence`.
- **Familistere de Guise**: Godin reads Fourier 1842, builds from 1858-59;
  Societe du Familistere 1880; Monument Historique 4 July 1991; Musee de France
  since 2010. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** Pool carries two longitudes for one site,
  3.6247 and 3.6253; the first is used, divergence noted.
- **Boulevard de Clichy monument**: Derre's bronze by public subscription,
  inaugurated June 1899 by Jean-Adolphe Alhaiza; melted under the Occupation;
  plaster replica 10 March 1969 by libertarian and situationist groups, removed
  within days; Franck Scurti's *La Quatrieme Pomme* on the pedestal, 2011.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **Correction:** the pool put the 1969 replica "during a
  period of general strike," but the general strike was May and June 1968, ten
  months earlier. Phrase deleted.
- **Debout edition** of *Le Nouveau Monde amoureux*, 1967; *L'Utopie de Charles
  Fourier*, 1978. **[A]**
- **Plaque at 2 rue Moncey, Besancon**, commemorating the birthplace.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia, Rue Moncey (Besancon)]** Coordinate corrected from
  47.2372 / 6.0243 to **47.2373 / 6.0258**, about 120 m. The rue Moncey was cut
  through in 1836-38 over the medieval ruelle Baron, so the street bearing the
  plaque post-dates the birth by sixty-four years and the work began while
  Fourier was alive in Paris.

## Quotes

Only quotes traceable to a named translation are used: ten across 44 stops.
**[A]:** three from 'Critique of the Revolutionary Ideals' and two from 'Fashion
and Parasitism', trans. Beecher and Bienvenu (Jonathan Cape, 1972) via
marxists.org; three from Julia Franklin, *Selections from the Works of Fourier*
(1901). Two more are verbatim-confirmed but wrongly sourced by the pool:

- "the commercial order is a temporary monstrosity..." is at Beecher and
  Bienvenu, *The Utopian Vision of Charles Fourier* (1971), p. 108. marxists.org
  credits *Manuscrits, Annees 1857-58*; the book's own Note on the Sources
  (p. 77) gives PM iii = *Publication des manuscrits, Annees 1853-1856*
  (OC XI, 78-86), while *Annees 1857-58* is PM iv. The file cites 1853-1856.
- "Nowhere is there more constancy and more unanimity among the French..." is not
  from *Theorie de l'unite universelle*. It is *Theory of Social Organization*
  (Somerby, 1876), p. 239, in the appended note on philosophers' undervaluing of
  women; Internet Archive `theoryofsocialor00four`, whose OCR reads "houor".

**[R], not used:** three Wikiquote attributions with no translator, no edition
and no primary check.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Charles Fourier is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing was silently
deleted. **The direction caught is forward in every case below:** all are later
people engaging with Fourier's texts after his death in 1837, which is not
licensable, so none is staged as a scene. Each is carried as a `source` or
`suggested_ref` on the stop for the Fourier object it concerns, and named in
prose only where Fourier is the thing they engaged.

1. **Andre Breton, Reno and the Hopi reservation, 1945.** fr.wikipedia's Breton
   article records the 1945 Reno divorce trip and the reservation visits
   *"emportant avec lui des ouvrages de Charles Fourier"*, the evidentiary link
   to the *Ode a Charles Fourier* (1947). **This is Breton's stop, not
   Fourier's**, and it is not staged here; one clause on the Clichy pedestal stop
   carries the Ode. The pool's Nevada pin at 40.8 / -117.0 is empty desert near
   Battle Mountain with no documented connection to anything, and is refused.
   **Note for the operator: `andre_breton.journey.json` contains no mention of
   Fourier, the Ode, or Fourierism** (grep, zero hits). A real missing interlock
   on Breton's own file, to be added there, not here.
2. **Walter Benjamin's Fourier convolute in the Arcades Project** and **Herbert
   Marcuse, *Eros and Civilization* (1955).** Carried as `suggested_refs`.
   Neither has a file in the corpus.
3. **Roland Barthes, *Sade, Fourier, Loyola* (1971)** and **Marx and Engels**
   classing Fourier among the utopian socialists (*Communist Manifesto*, 1848).
   Both have files. Each is named once, in prose, on a Fourier stop, because in
   each case the thing they engaged is Fourier.
4. **Guy Debord and the Situationists.** The pool repeats the brief's claim that
   the Situationists descend from Fourier's critique of alienated labour, and
   records zero mentions of Fourier in `guy_debord.journey.json`. Nothing
   verified attests Debord's engagement by name. fr.wikipedia does attribute the
   1969 plaster replica to libertarian and situationist groups, so the milieu is
   named and Debord is not. Left for the operator.
5. **James de Rothschild.** Fourier wrote about the Rothschilds in *La Fausse
   Industrie*, a one-directional engagement that licenses the name. Only "with
   the assistance of the Rothschilds" is verifiable, so the file names the Paris
   house and refuses the king-of-Judea assertion.
   `mayer_amschel_rothschild.journey.json` carries James's own Paris arc.
6. **Samuel Preiswerk** was proposed by the pool as a structural precedent for a
   proto-Zionist entry, and **Theodor Herzl** as the comparison. There is no
   relation of any kind between Fourier and either man, shared structure is not a
   relation, and Fourier died fifty-three years before *Der Judenstaat*. Neither
   is named and no lineage is written.

**QUEUE.md row 147 needs an edit.** It calls Fourier "coiner of feminisme."
Nothing reachable supports the coinage and it is not written into the file. He is
a major early advocate of women's emancipation, and the passage on the French
subordination of women is real and used; the coinage claim belongs struck or
hedged in the queue row. Flagged for the operator. No git action taken.
