# Ángel della Valle (1852-1903), research report
*Buenos Aires painter; eleven years in Florence under Antonio Ciseri; eighteen years teaching drawing at the Sociedad Estímulo de Bellas Artes; author of La vuelta del Malón (1892), the founding image of the Argentine frontier raid.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · **GAP** = no source found, stated as a gap.

**The evidence base is thin and almost entirely tertiary.** Every substantive biographical fact in this dossier comes from one Spanish Wikipedia article ("Ángel della Valle"), thinly corroborated by a second ("La vuelta del malón"), a third ("Sociedad Estímulo de Bellas Artes"), and a short English article. The museum that holds his best-known painting (bellasartes.gob.ar) returned HTTP 403 on every attempt, so no institutional text was ever seen. There is no monograph, no catalogue raisonné, no correspondence, and no auction record in reach. Treat the whole life as attested-to-Wikipedia rather than cross-verified, and read the tags below accordingly: **[A] here means "a source says so", not "a document proves it".**

## 1. Birth and family, 1852

- **Born Buenos Aires, 10 October 1852. [A: es.wikipedia, "Ángel della Valle"]**
  **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** Wikidata's description line reads (1852-1903), but its birth statement field has at one point returned **1855**. Spanish Wikipedia gives 1852 and the age-at-death arithmetic (fifty in July 1903) only works with 1852. The journey file uses 1852 and carries the conflict in `date_confidence`.
- **His father was a builder who raised buildings in Buenos Aires on commission from Juan Manuel de Rosas. [A: es.wikipedia]**, uncorroborated elsewhere. **No structure is named.** The journey's Palermo stop is therefore **[R]**: Palermo is the standard identification of Rosas's principal Buenos Aires seat, not a documented site of this family's work, and the stop's `date_confidence` says so.
- **He attends the Colegio San José, Buenos Aires. [A: es.wikipedia]**; **no years given [GAP]**.
  **Coordinate corrected.** The research pool placed the school at -34.6142, -58.3728, which lands in Monserrat/San Telmo, roughly 2.7 km wrong. The Colegio San José is at Azcuénaga 158, Balvanera; es.wikipedia gives 34°36′27″S 58°24′03″O. The journey uses **-34.608, -58.401**.
- **Pedro Lagleyze is called an "amigo de la infancia". [A: es.wikipedia]** He is **not** stated to have been a Colegio San José classmate. The pool's "schoolmate ... formed over nothing more than shared benches" was reconstruction dressed as attestation; the campa now says only "a friend of his childhood".

## 2. Italy, c. 1872-1883

- **He travels to Italy at about age twenty to study oil painting. [A as to the fact; R as to the year]** es.wikipedia says "a los 20 años" with no calendar date; c. 1872 is arithmetic on the 1852 birth.
- **He studies in Florence under Antonio Ciseri. [A: es.wikipedia]** No premises named; whether this was inside the Accademia di Belle Arti or Ciseri's private teaching studio is **[GAP]**. Coordinates differ trivially between the two research lenses (43.7761/11.2578 vs 43.7696/11.2558); both are central Florence and the first is used.
- **Departure port, arrival port, ship, and month: all [R], none named by any source.** Genoa is the standard nineteenth-century landfall for River Plate passenger routes and the Buenos Aires anchorage is the only plausible embarkation before Puerto Madero; both stops are tagged as reconstruction in `date_confidence`. This is honest scaffolding, not evidence.
- **Caballos de San Marcos, 1881. [A as title and date: es.wikipedia work list]** That it was painted in Venice before the basilica rather than from prints elsewhere is **[R]**, inferred from the title alone.

## 3. Return, studio, and the Buenos Aires art world, 1883 onward

- **"En 1883 volvió a Argentina". [A: es.wikipedia, corroborated by en.wikipedia]** The bare year is solid; the port and crossing narrated around it are **[R]**, and the journey stop is tagged accordingly. The research pool tagged this entry [A] while its actual content was unattested reconstruction; corrected here.
- **He installs his studio in his parents' house. [A: es.wikipedia]** **The address is nowhere given. [GAP]**
  **Correction of consequence:** the research pool invented the coordinate **-34.6158, -58.3731** for this house and then reused it, under an [A] tag, for six further stops (the Malón, Corrida de Sortija, Al borde del monte, Incendio en La Pampa, Apartando, El taller). Invented precision propagated across a quarter of the itinerary. The journey file drops that coordinate entirely and falls back to the canonical Buenos Aires pin **-34.6037, -58.3816** for every unlocated interior, with the fallback declared in each `date_confidence`.
- **His circle: Eduardo Sívori, Ernesto de la Cárcova, Augusto Ballerini, Lucio Correa Morales, Francisco Cafferata, Reinaldo Giudici (and, from the 1893 photograph, Eduardo Schiaffino, Martín L. Boneo, Américo Bonetti). [A as names]**
  **The pool's 1883 date for the association is not supported and is chronologically strained [flagged, not resolved]:** de la Cárcova (b. 1866) was seventeen in 1883 and left for Italy in 1888; Cafferata died in 1890. The only documented assembly of the group is the 1893 photograph. The journey dates the stop 1883 for narrative order and states the c.1883-1893 range in `date_confidence`.
- **Teaching: drawing at the Sociedad Estímulo de Bellas Artes, three sessions a week, for eighteen years. [A: es.wikipedia and en.wikipedia]**
  Two corrections to the pool: (a) the source says **three times a week**, not "three evenings a week"; the time of day was invented and asserted twice. (b) The pool built an entire "direct contradiction" around della Valle being named among SEBA's **1876 founders while simultaneously in Florence**. **That contradiction is fabricated.** The es.wikipedia founding list reads: Eduardo Sívori and his brother Alejandro, Santiago Vaca Guzmán, Eduardo Schiaffino, Alfred Paris, the architect Julio Dormal, the journalist Carlos Gutiérrez, and Giuseppe Agujari. Della Valle appears in a separate sentence about staff hired later: "Ángel della Valle y Reinaldo Giudici en Dibujo y Pintura". There is no conflict with his Florence years and nothing to flag. The 1876 founding (23 October) and Juan Camaña as first president are confirmed **[A]**.
  **The starting year of his teaching is [GAP]**; the journey infers 1884 from the 1883 return and says so.
- **The Roca portrait, via Pedro Lagleyze. [A that the Lagleyze connection produced access to Julio Argentino Roca; R as to place, year, and circumstance]** No source dates the sitting or places it at the Casa de Gobierno; the journey stop is tagged reconstruction. **This is the one documented personal link to another traveler in the corpus** and the only one named in a campa.

## 4. The work list

The complete list in the es.wikipedia raw wikitext, verified in place, runs: **Caballos de San Marcos (1881), Tren de La Pampa (1888), Anochecer en la laguna (1889), La diosa del Amor (1890), La vuelta del Malón (1892), Estación de Lomas de Zamora (1893), Corrida de Sortija (1893), Al borde del monte (1895), Incendio en La Pampa (1900), Apartando (1900), El taller (1900).** Eleven works. **[A]**

- **"El juego del pato" does not appear in the source** and was an invention of the research pass; dropped.
- **No source describes the content of any of these canvases** except La vuelta del Malón. Where a campa describes a scene (riders and cattle fleeing a grass fire; cattle cut from a herd; brushes and north light), that description follows the title and genre convention and is **[R]**; each such stop says so in `date_confidence`.
- **Whether he painted on site or in the studio is unrecorded for every work, including Estación de Lomas de Zamora, whose subject is a real, locatable platform. [GAP]** The pool tagged five studio-composition stops [A] while their own notes conceded the setting was "assumed" or "inferred"; all are retagged as reconstruction here.
- **Arithmetic error corrected:** the pool's Apartando entry said "Della Valle is fifty-eight" in 1900. Born October 1852, he was **forty-seven** for most of 1900 and turned forty-eight that October.

## 5. La vuelta del Malón, 1892-1893

- **Painted 1892, oil on canvas, 186.5 x 292 cm, Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, Sala 24. [A: es.wikipedia, "La vuelta del malón", infobox]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** della Valle's own es.wikipedia article gives the dimensions as **192 x 131 cm**. Both figures were verified in place in the verification pass. The journey quotes the painting-article figure and carries the conflict in `date_confidence`.
- **"Fue el primer cuadro pintado en gran formato que representó la temática indígena en Argentina". [A as a Wikipedia sentence, and only that]** The pool attributed this to the museum's own sourcing. It is an **uncited assertion in the body of the es.wikipedia article**, carrying no reference, and bellasartes.gob.ar returned 403, so no museum text was ever read. Attribution corrected in the journey's `quote_source`.
- **Shown at the Nocetti y Repetto bazaar, Buenos Aires, 1892. [A]** The source says only "en el bazar Nocetti y Repetto, ya que no existían aún en la ciudad salones ni galerías de arte". **"Calle Florida" was an unsourced addition** by the research pass, asserted in the title and prose while the note admitted the address was missing. Street dropped; the stop uses the city pin.
- **Shown in the Argentine section at the Chicago exposition, 1893. [A]** **No evidence that della Valle travelled with it [GAP]**; the campa says the canvas crosses alone, which is what the record supports.
- **Interpretive claims are not attested.** "He has never ridden with a malón, never seen one; he builds it entirely from research and invention" is an inference no consulted source states, though it is consistent with a life spent between Florence and one Buenos Aires room. The campas make the same point through placement (the studio stops, the Choele Choel stop) rather than asserting it as biography.

## 7. Death, 1903

- **"la muerte lo sorprendió el 16 de julio de 1903, mientras estaba dictando clases". [A: es.wikipedia]**; en.wikipedia: "died suddenly, while teaching in his classroom". **Age fifty [A]**, consistent with the 1852 birth.
- **The room is not specified** beyond the fact that he was teaching. Placing the death in a SEBA classroom is **[R]**, and the "three evenings a week" of the pool is corrected to three sessions.
- **The Thibon de Libián drawing is doubtful and is excluded from the journey prose.** es.wikipedia's della Valle article credits a death-scene drawing to him, but **Valentín Thibon de Libián was born in 1889**, making him thirteen in July 1903; his own es.wikipedia article never mentions della Valle, the drawing, or study at SEBA, and says he trained largely in Paris and Italy. Flagged, not resolved, and not narrated.
- **Burial: [GAP].** He appears in the notable-burials lists of neither the Recoleta nor the Chacarita articles, and no cemetery record was reached. **Do not assert a cemetery for him.** No stop in the journey claims one.

## 8. Afterlife

- **La vuelta del Malón hangs in Sala 24, MNBA, 19th-century Argentine collection. [A]**
  **Two corrections to the pool.** (1) **The MNBA was not founded by the Sociedad Estímulo de Bellas Artes.** A decree of President José Evaristo Uriburu created it in 1895, with Eduardo Schiaffino as first director; it opened 25 December 1896 in the Bon Marché on Calle Florida, moved to the Pabellón Argentino at Plaza San Martín in 1909, and reached the remodelled Casa de Bombas in Recoleta in 1932. SEBA founded a separate art academy in 1878, nationalised in 1905. Individual SEBA men drove the museum's creation; the society did not found it. (2) **Coordinate corrected** from -34.5875, -58.3931 (roughly 400 m south of the building) to **-34.584, -58.393**, matching es.wikipedia's -34.583986, -58.39297 and reconciling the two lenses, which had disagreed with each other.
  The pool's Bon Marché claim about the **1893 group photograph is wrong and anachronistic** and has been dropped: the caption names people only, and the Bon Marché housed the museum from December 1896, three years later. The caption's eight sitters are all restored to the campa.
- **Acquisition date of the canvas by the MNBA: [GAP].** No source consulted dates it; the journey stop uses 1932 (the building) and says why.
- **The 2021 Página/12 survey grouping della Valle with Monvoisin, Rugendas and Blanes. [R]** Outlet, title and date reached only through an infobae.com summary; **the original article text was never retrieved**, so no quotation from it appears anywhere.
- **No monument, statue, street or school named for him was found. [GAP, absence of evidence]** The Buenos Aires nomenclador urbano returned no hit and arteinformado.com's biography page 404s.
- **No editions, translations, print-reproduction history, or auction record found. [GAP]** sothebys.com returned 503, biografiasyvidas.com and the Commons category 404.

## 9. Interlock discipline

Fourteen candidate links were supplied by the interlock lens. **Only one survives the relation test and appears in a campa: Julio Argentino Roca**, whom della Valle painted, through the childhood friend who was Roca's ophthalmologist. That link is direct and documented in kind, if not in date. Roca also carries the Choele Choel stop (the campaign that emptied the ground the painting mythologises) and the 1941 monument stop (the same city still arguing about it), which are consequences of the same relation, not fresh ones.

**Rejected as shared geography or shared theme only, and named nowhere in the prose:**
- **Ceferino Namuncurá** and his father Manuel: della Valle never depicted, met, or wrote about either, and painting an imagined malón is not a link to a specific cacique. Same city, same decades, no relation.
- **Pedro Subercaseaux** (b. 1880), a continental parallel in national-history painting, with no contact of any kind.
- **Xul Solar** (b. 1887) and **Troiano Troiani** (arrived Buenos Aires 1910, seven years after della Valle's death): the same academy lineage is an institutional inheritance, not a relation between persons.
- The interlock lens's own note that **no corpus file places any traveler at the Kaaba or the Temple Mount in connection with this life** is correct and is recorded here as an absence; no such pin is inherited.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical** where used: Buenos Aires **-34.6037, -58.3816** (fifteen stops), Choele Choel **-39.2872, -64.75**, the Roca monument at Diagonal Sur y Perú **-34.6084, -58.3731**.
