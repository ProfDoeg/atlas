# Robert Falcon Scott (1868-1912): research report

*2026-08-22. Written for `robert_falcon_scott.journey.json` from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses). Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Calendar: gregorian.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or claim carried without independent corroboration.

The canon about Scott has been rebuilt twice in a century: hero (1913-1979), incompetent (Huntford onward), and a contested middle still moving. The register here is the 1913 canon, and this report marks where that canon rests on a document and where it rests on Leonard Huxley's editing of a dead man's notebook.

---

## 1. Plymouth and the Navy, 1868-1898

- **Birth, 6 June 1868, Outlands, Stoke Damerel. [A]** Third of six children of the brewer John Edward Scott and Hannah Cuming (Wikipedia, 'Robert Falcon Scott', fetched 2026-08-22; dossier agrees independently). The house is gone; the pin is the Milehouse district, **[R]**.
- **Stubbington House School. [R as to dates]** The crammer is attested; c. 1877-1881 is dossier-relayed local history, uncorroborated. **Coordinate corrected**: the lens fix sat 2 km southwest, toward Hill Head. Village and school: 50.826 / -1.212.
- **HMS Britannia, 1881 to July 1883. [A]** Passed out midshipman, seventh of twenty-six; the entry day (15 July 1881) is geography-lens only, **[R]**. **HMS Boadicea, South Africa, Oct 1883 [A]** is dropped for space, carrying no scene.
- **St Kitts, 1 March 1887. [A, corrected]** Markham watched Scott's cutter win the morning race. The ship was HMS *Rover* of the **Training Squadron**, not "the West Indies squadron" as the chronology lens had it, and Markham was RGS **Secretary** then, President only from 1893.
- **HMS Vernon torpedo course. [A, lens contradiction resolved]** The chronology lens said Scott *completed* the course in 1891; the geography lens gave 1891-1893. Wikipedia is explicit: in 1891 he *applied for and began* the two-year course, graduating with first-class certificates in 1893. The journey follows the geography lens and states the correction in `date_confidence`. Resolved rather than left standing, one side being a plain misreading of the source both cite.
- **Family collapse. [A in outline, R in detail]** Brewery sold, proceeds lost, family reduced by c. 1894; John Edward Scott salaried as a brewery manager at Shepton Mallet, dead of heart disease 1897; Archibald dead of typhoid 1898. Wikipedia carries the outline; Shepton Mallet is dossier-sourced biographical tradition, not re-verified.

**Shepton Mallet dropped as a stop.** It is his *father's* removal, and no reachable source puts Scott in the town, so the ruin is told at the Vernon stop instead.

**The 1889-1890 gap and the Huntford allegation. [R, flagged, not carried]** Huntford proposed that a documentary gap in Scott's naval record c. 1889 is explained by a scandal involving a married American woman; David Crane and others find no corroborating evidence. The pool tags it **R** and declines to drop it. It is not in the journey: no date exists for it that is not the disputed item, and asserting it in a campa would launder speculation into narrative. Adding it would need a London pin and a `date_confidence` calling the stop one biographer's conjecture.

## 2. Discovery, 1899-1904

- **The volunteering. [A, split from a conflation]** The chronology lens collapsed two events. The chance street encounter with Markham was early June 1899, usually dated the fifth; Scott volunteered at Markham's residence on **11 June 1899**, and the stop takes that date. Coordinate 51.4925 / -0.144 is Markham's Eccleston Square address, approximate to the square, **[R]**.
- **Discovery built at Dundee, launched March 1901. [A]** Last three-masted wooden ship built in Britain. The Royal Society / RGS quarrel over scientific versus naval command is attested and is the expedition's structural fact.
- **Departure: two dates, both true. [A]** Chronology lens: Cowes, 6 Aug 1901. `ernest_shackleton.journey.json`: East India Docks, 31 July 1901. Same voyage a week apart. The journey uses the East India Docks stop, folds Cowes into the campa, and **inherits 51.5083 / 0.0069 byte-identical from the Shackleton file**. The interlock lens *reported* that pin as 51.5074 / -0.1278, a generic London placeholder. **Where its coordinates disagree with the source file, the source file wins**; this happened three times.
- **Winter Quarters Bay, 8 Feb 1902. [A, claim corrected]** The geography lens called the shore hut "the expedition's shore headquarters." Wikipedia: Scott decided the men should "continue to live and work aboard ship," and left "the main hut to be used as a storeroom and shelter." The ship was the base. Surveyed Hut Point is -77.849 / 166.637; the atlas pin -77.8419 / 166.6381 is 800 m north, retained for consistency with the Shackleton file.
- **George Vince, 11 March 1902. [A]**
- **Farthest South, 30 Dec 1902, 82°17'S. [A on latitude, R on longitude]** Geography lens 163, interlock lens 164, Shackleton file **163.5**, which is inherited. That file also records a live contradiction, that some chronologies date the Farthest South to 14 January 1903, a date belonging to the return march; carried forward unresolved.
- **Shackleton invalided home, early 1903. [A on the fact, contested on the motive]** He wanted to stay. The "jealousy" reading is uncorroborated per the dossier and is not adopted; the campa says nobody outside that tent can settle it. The chronology lens says 1903 unqualified, the Shackleton file 2 March; the journey uses 1 March and flags the variance.
- **Homecoming, 10 Sept 1904. [A]** Portsmouth; Captain (London Gazette notice cited by the dossier); CVO. *The Voyage of the 'Discovery'*, 1905 **[A]**, dossier-sourced.

## 3. The interval, 1905-1910

- **Albemarle / Commonwealth collision, 11 Feb 1907. [R]** The weakest item in the journey; the pool's note reads "dossier, citing naval archival reassessment; not independently re-verified." Kept because the exoneration is part of the later polemical record on both sides. The pin is Portsmouth, the home port, not the collision's position at sea.
- **Kathleen Bruce, 1907; marriage 2 Sept 1908, Chapel Royal, Hampton Court. [A]**
- **The Rodin claim. [R, not carried]** The dossier asserts Kathleen Bruce trained "in the orbit of Auguste Rodin." The interlock lens checked `auguste_rodin.journey.json` and could not confirm it: that file names Camille Claudel and other 1906-1908 students, not her. So **Rodin is named in no campa**: the relation runs Scott to wife to alleged teacher and its middle link is unverified. The claim is stated as unconfirmed in the stop's `date_confidence`, so the file carries the doubt rather than the assertion.
- **Peter Markham Scott, 14 Sept 1909. [A]** **Coordinate corrected**: the geography lens gave 51.5074 / -0.1278, a Charing Cross placeholder; Wikipedia, 'Peter Scott', gives 174 Buckingham Palace Road, **51.4945 / -0.1472**. The Admiralty post (24 March 1909) and the departure from it are folded in. **Peter Scott's own afterlife is deliberately not told**: the WWF, the painting and the ornithology postdate his father's death.
- **The McMurdo territorial dispute, 1907-1908. [A]** Shackleton's written undertaking to land east of the 170th meridian; ice barring the eastern approaches; the hut at Cape Royds; Scott reading it as theft of ground (dossier lines 316-331, 1023-1029; `ernest_shackleton.journey.json`). **Cape Royds is not staged as a Scott stop**: he never went there. Relocated to London, see §8. The May 1907 date orders a year of correspondence, and `date_confidence` says so.

## 4. Terra Nova, 1910-1912

- **Cardiff, 15 June 1910. [A]** Scott left London 1 June on fundraising business and rejoined later; chronologies giving 1 June for "the departure" conflate the two. The **Cape Town rendezvous [R]** is dossier synthesis, folded into the Melbourne `date_confidence`.
- **Melbourne telegram, Oct 1910. [A in outline, R as to day]** The day the wire reached Scott is not fixed; defaults to the first.
- **Port Chalmers, 29 Nov 1910. [A]** The gale, choked pumps, bucket chain, bulkhead cut by hand, two ponies and a dog lost, then twenty days in the pack.
- **Cape Evans hut, 18 Jan 1911. [A]** Geography lens 4-17 January against chronology lens 18 January: a construction range against a habitability date, not a contradiction.
- **One Ton Depot, 17 Feb 1911, 79°29'S. [A]** Thirty-one geographical miles short of the intended 80°S, after six of eight ponies died. That Oates urged pushing the animals further and Scott refused is dossier-reported literature-standard, **[R]**.
- **Bay of Whales, Feb 1911. [A as event, relocated as stop]** Terra Nova found Fram moored there, Amundsen's camp building on the Barrier, the parties exchanging visits. **Scott was not present**, being out depot-laying. Relocated, see §8; the largest structural decision in the file.
- **Cape Crozier winter journey, 27 June to 2 Aug 1911. [A, relocated]** The chronology lens dated it loosely "1911"; the pool tightens it. A coordinate correction is recorded but unused, the journey having no Cape Crozier pin: the lens gave -77.45 / 169.2667, about 8 km offshore, where the cape is -77.517 / 169.400. Relocated, see §8.
- **Motor party start, 24 Oct 1911. [A, coordinate contradiction resolved]** The lens text said the party "sets out first from Cape Evans," matching Wikipedia, but its coordinate (-77.85 / 166.7) is the Hut Point area, 35 km south; the journey uses Cape Evans. **Scott's own departure, 1 Nov 1911 [A]**, is dossier chronology consistent with Wikipedia.
- **The Gateway / Shambles Camp, 9 Dec 1911. [A, coordinate corrected]** The ponies were shot at the Gateway, the pass between Mount Hope and Cape Allen: 83°31'S 170°58'E = **-83.517 / 170.967**. The geography lens gave -83.9 / 171, about 40 km too far south; the same error recurred on the Edgar Evans stop and is corrected in both. The Shackleton file pins the glacier *as a route* at -83.75 / 171, a different thing from its mouth and not in conflict. The dogs turning north on 11 December is folded in.
- **The five-man decision, 3 Jan 1912, 87°32'S. [A, major correction]** The geography lens placed the last supporting party's turnaround at "Lower Beardmore / Shambles Camp," -83.55 / 171, and attached Tom Crean's solo walk to it. Both wrong: Teddy Evans, Crean and Lashly turned back **on the plateau at about 87°32' to 87°35'S on 4 January 1912**, and Crean's walk was on the **Ross Ice Shelf near Hut Point, 18 February 1912**, 400 km away and six weeks later. Two events sited at a third place. The journey re-sites to about -87.53 / 159 (longitude approximate) and puts Crean's march in `suggested_refs`.
- **16 Jan 1912, the black flag. [A]** Dossier chronology; the entry is in *Scott's Last Expedition* vol. I ch. XVIII. Coordinate approximate, roughly thirty miles out.

## 5. The return march, 1912

- **Edgar Evans, 17 Feb 1912. [A on date, contested on cause]** No autopsy; frostbite, head injury and exhaustion all proposed, and the journey asserts none.
- **Oates, 16 or 17 March 1912. [A]** The journal hedges; his thirty-second birthday fell on 17 March. Coordinate extrapolated between One Ton Depot and the final camp; the body was never found, **[R]**.
- **The final camp. [A on fact, unresolved on day and distance]** Sources give 19 or 20 March, and eleven, eleven and a half, or twelve and a half miles short of One Ton Depot. Both spreads left standing; the journey uses 20 March and "eleven miles" and says in `date_confidence` that sources differ. **The coordinate is inherited, not computed [R by convention]**: geography lens -79.6833 / 169.5, interlock lens -79.87 / 169.28 (matching no file), and `james_matthew_barrie.journey.json` **-79.667 / 169.383**, "the conventional placement of that camp... not a surveyed point." The Barrie pin is inherited byte-identical across all three final-camp stops, disclaimer included.
- **29 March 1912, last entry and the Message. [A]** Manuscript at the British Library, Add MS 51024-51042, more authoritative for exact wording than Huxley's 1913 text; Max Jones's Oxford edition (2006) collates them, **[R]**.
- **12 Nov 1912, the tent found. [A]** Bodies, journals, letters, and some thirty to thirty-five pounds of Beardmore geological specimens; tent collapsed and cairned with a cross of skis.

## 6. The quotes

Every quote in the journey is verbatim from *Scott's Last Expedition*, vol. I, ed. Leonard Huxley (1913), via Project Gutenberg 11579 and Wikisource. Three corrections from the pool, all carried:

1. **"In the tent we find a record of five Norwegians having been here."** Verbatim, but the pool's date is wrong: it belongs to the **18 January** entry, which opens "Thursday morning, January 18." Not used as a `quote` field, but the campa follows the corrected sequence: Pole on the 17th, Polheim on the 18th.
2. **"Well, we have turned our back now on the goal of our ambition..."** The pool's punctuation is wrong; the actual text runs "...and must face our 800 miles of solid dragging--and good-bye to most of the daydreams!" Not used, but recorded so the error does not propagate.
3. **Message ordering.** The pool called "But if we have been willing to give our lives..." the Message's closing sentence. It closes the *penultimate* paragraph, and the journey's two Message quotes stand in the correct order relative to each other.

Cherry-Garrard's "give me Scott" line **[A]** (*The Worst Journey in the World*, 1922, Preface, PG 14363) is a participant's assessment, not Scott's words, so it is not a `quote` field; the book is in `suggested_refs`. One quote used is not Scott's text at all: "To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield," from Tennyson's *Ulysses*, sourced as the Observation Hill inscription.

## 7. Afterlife

- **Observation Hill cross, 22 Jan 1913. [A]** Nine-foot jarrah cross by ship's carpenter Francis Davies; HSM-20 since 1972. That Cherry-Garrard proposed the Tennyson line is widely repeated and unverified **[R]**, so the campa omits it.
- **Oamaru, 10 Feb 1913. [A]** The night landing and the refusal to identify themselves are standard in the accounts, **[R]** in detail.
- **St Paul's, 14 Feb 1913. [A]** King present, large crowds. The BFI film footage and the Mansion House figure of about £75,000 are dossier-sourced, not re-fetched, and flagged in `date_confidence`.
- **Waterloo Place statue, 1915. [A, upgraded from R]** Bronze by Kathleen Scott, unveiled 1915 by Arthur Balfour, subscribed by naval officers, Grade II listed 5 Feb 1970 (ref. 1357334), plaque naming the other four added 1923. The lenses give -0.1319 and -0.1321; the journey uses the latter. The **Portsmouth statue [A]**, "n.d." in the geography lens, is dated 1915 by Wikipedia, 'Kathleen Scott', and folded into the same campa.
- **Christchurch statue, unveiled 9 Feb 1917. [A]** Carrara marble carved 1916, toppled and broken in the Feb 2011 earthquake, restored on a seismic spring base and re-unveiled 26 Oct 2017. **Coordinate corrected to -43.5311 / 172.6336**; the lens's 172.6395 sat 480 m too far east, across the Avon. Dropped for space; if the file is expanded, this is the first stop to restore.
- **Scott Polar Research Institute, 1920. [A, coordinate corrected, lenses disagreed]** Founded on Frank Debenham's proposal out of the Terra Nova fund's surplus; Herbert Baker building on Lensfield Road, 1933-1934. Geography lens 52.2059 / 0.1132, about 1.3 km west of the site; afterlife lens 52.1997 / 0.1257, close. Wikipedia's fix is **52.1984 / 0.1262**, which the journey uses.
- **Amundsen-Scott South Pole Station, Nov 1956. [A]** **Terra Nova's wreck**, found off Greenland in July 2012 and surveyed again June 2025 **[A]**, is dropped: the ship's afterlife is the ship's.
- **The drifting tomb. [A on mechanism, R on schedule]** The shelf moves seaward continuously, so the final camp will eventually calve. The repeated projection of c. 2275 could not be confirmed from any source reached and is stated nowhere in the journey.
- **Huntford, 1979, and the 1985 dramatization. [A, one clause dropped]** *Scott and Amundsen* (1979) and the seven-part *The Last Place on Earth* (1985), Trevor Griffiths writing, Martin Shaw as Scott. **Not confirmed**: that Peter Scott publicly objected. Neither Wikipedia article mentions him or any objection, so the clause is dropped rather than repeated. Huntford appears once in the journey, in the SPRI stop's `suggested_refs`. **Sienicki's arXiv preprints 1011.1272 and 1109.5355 [R]**, arguing that Scott and Bowers falsified temperature records, are not carried: no peer-reviewed work supports them and they were not fetched.

**None found requiring a ruling.** Scott is not in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing in the pool needed his framework applied in either direction: he had no theoretical apparatus, only a transport plan. ## 9. Interlocks named in campa

- **`ernest_shackleton`**: shipmate on Discovery from 1901; with Scott and Wilson at the Farthest South; invalided home on Scott's decision in 1903; the McMurdo undertaking and its breach in 1908; discoverer of the Beardmore route on which Scott's party died. Named in five campas.
- **`james_matthew_barrie`**: godfather to Peter Scott, recipient of a tent letter. Named in two campas.

Deliberately **not** named: `auguste_rodin` (see §3). Amundsen is named constantly as a person but **is not an atlas subject yet**: `QUEUE.md` lines 393-394 carry him, framed around beating Scott to the Pole, with no `amundsen.journey.json`. When he is researched, the Bay of Whales meeting, the Melbourne telegram and Polheim become the reciprocal stops, and his file can carry the scene this one had to decline.
