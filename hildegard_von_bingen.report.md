# Hildegard von Bingen (1098-1179): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14. Slug: `hildegard_von_bingen`. Calendar: Julian for the life; the afterlife section crosses into Gregorian reckoning.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, hagiography, or a claim whose only witness has an interest in it. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Where a coordinate was corrected, the discarded value is recorded too, because it has already circulated.

---

## 1. Birth and family (1098)

**Born 1098, tenth child of a family of free noble rank in the Rhenish Palatinate. [A: Vita; standard biographies]**

The birthplace is **not settled**. Most modern sources give **Bermersheim vor der Höhe** (Alzey-Worms district); the *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1910) instead names **Böckelheim on the Nahe**. German Wikipedia is explicit that the *genauer Geburtsort ist umstritten* and that Bermersheim is the site of her **baptismal church**, not a documented birth house. The consensus is [A], the fact is [R]. **Contradiction left open**, carried in `date_confidence`; the pool's phrase "birth house site" is dropped.

**Coordinate correction.** The pool carried **49.7167, 8.1167**, roughly 7 km south, near Alzey. German Wikipedia gives 49° 47′ N, 8° 6′ E = **49.7792, 8.0981**. A third value in circulation, 49.6819, 8.2236, belongs to the *other* Bermersheim, near Worms, and must not be used.

**First vision at about age three. [R: autobiographical, unverifiable]** Hildegard reporting on Hildegard, not an externally attested event. Her fuller statement survives in the fragment quoted by the *Catholic Encyclopedia* (translator unnamed): "Up to my fifteenth year I saw much, and related some of the things seen to others, who would inquire with astonishment, whence such things might come."

## 2. Disibodenberg: the anchorage (1106-1150)

**Given into the care of the anchoress Jutta von Sponheim. [A: Vita]** The **age is contradictory**: the *Vita* says eight, yielding 1106; several modern scholars argue for about fourteen, since the enclosure did not occur until 1112. Both readings are in print; neither is preferred. The journey file dates the handover 1106 and carries the dispute in `date_confidence`.

**Formally enclosed with Jutta on All Saints' Day, 1 November 1112; vows received by Bishop Otto of Bamberg. [A: Vita]**

**Deduplication.** The pool carried a *separate* "c.1113, takes the Benedictine veil, professed by Bishop Otto of Bamberg" item. Same event: Wikipedia has "Their vows were received by Bishop Otto of Bamberg on All Saints Day 1112." Merged.

**Coordinate correction, applied in seven places.** The pool used **49.8083, 7.6472**, and once **49.8203, 7.6335**; the two disagree with each other and sit about 5 km and 7 km off. Wikipedia gives 49°46′36″N 7°42′04″E = **49.7767, 7.7011**, near Odernheim am Glan. All Disibodenberg stops use that.

**Jutta dies 1136; Hildegard elected magistra. [A: Vita]**

**Duration gap flagged.** Wikipedia's *Disibodenberg* article says the community relocated in **1147** and that she "lived in Disibodenberg for 39 years"; the mainstream date for the move is **1150**, and 1112 + 39 = 1151. Internal tension in one source, unresolved.

## 3. Scivias and the papal licence (1141-1151)

**1141: the vision commanding her to write. [A: Scivias, Protestificatio; Vita]** She dictates to the monk **Volmar**, with assistance from the nun **Richardis von Stade**. [A] From the Protestificatio, trans. Hart and Bishop (1990): "But I... refused to write for a long time through doubt and bad opinion and the diversity of human words... until, laid low by the scourge of God, I fell upon a bed of sickness," and then, "Cry out, therefore, and write thus!"

**c.1146-47: she writes to Bernard of Clairvaux. [A: her letter, excerpt on Wikiquote]** The translator is **not named** there, so the wording is attested only as *seen*; re-check against Baird and Ehrman in a citation-strict context. "Father, I am greatly disturbed by a vision which has appeared to me through divine revelation, a vision seen not with my fleshly eyes but only in my spirit."

**1147-48: Synod of Trier. Pope Eugene III has portions of the unfinished *Scivias* read aloud and sends her a letter of blessing and authorization. [A]**

**Citation correction.** The pool attributed Wikipedia's sentence to "footnote 60 citing scholarly sources." The raw wikitext shows a **primary-source citation**: Hildegard, "Letter 4," in *The Letters of Hildegard of Bingen*, trans. Baird and Ehrman (OUP, 1994-2004), pp. 34-35. The sentence is also **Wikipedia's editorial prose**, not a quotation from Eugene III or any chronicle, and must never be set in quotation marks as a medieval source.

**1151 (or 1152): *Scivias* completed.** [A, the year itself contradictory between two Wikipedia articles.] The illuminations were **produced under her direction, not by her own hand**. [A]

## 4. The Rupertsberg foundation (1148-1158)

**1148: she announces the move; the monks of Disibodenberg resist. [A]**

**Evidence downgrade.** The causal chain in the pool (Kuno objecting over prestige and dowries, Hildegard struck by a paralysis she reads as divine pressure, the abbot yielding) comes from the ***Vita***, written by monks with an interest in the miracle, and is **[R]**. Only the resistance and the release are **[A]**.

**1150: the move, about twenty nuns, Volmar as provost and scribe, backing from Archbishop Heinrich of Mainz. [A: Vita]**

**Coordinate correction, applied in seven places.** The pool used **49.9614, 7.8797** for Rupertsberg throughout, about 0.9 km southwest of the site. German and English Wikipedia agree on 49°57′57″N 7°53′21″E = **49.9658, 7.8892**.

**1151: *Ordo Virtutum*. [A]** Among the earliest surviving morality plays, and the only medieval musical drama whose text and music are both attributed to one named author.

**1152: the convent chapel consecrated by Archbishop Henry of Mainz. [A: Wikipedia, *Rupertsberg*]** **Tag correction, and a split.** The pool tagged this whole item **[R]**. The consecration is plainly attested and is **[A]**; only the claim that *Ordo Virtutum* was performed at the dedication is uncertain, the source hedging with "may have been performed." Separated: consecration [A], performance occasion [R].

**1158: Archbishop Arnold of Mainz finalizes the charters fixing Rupertsberg's property and legal status. [A] 1171: Archbishop Christian of Mainz grants tax relief. [A]**

## 5. The medical writing (undated, c.1150s)

***Physica*** (plants, animals, minerals) and ***Causae et Curae*** (physiology, disease, the humors). [A that they exist and are hers; [R] as to date] No colophon fixes their composition. The geography lens tagged them [A] at "c.1150-1158," the chronology lens [R] at 1155; the honest position is that the works are attested and the decade is a reconstruction. Quoted in the journey file, from *Causae et Curae*, c. 42 [A]: "It happens that certain men suffer diverse illnesses. This comes from the phlegm which is superabundant within them."

## 6. The four preaching tours (1158-1170)

The most corrupted region of the raw pool. **The chronology and geography lenses contradict each other about which tour is which**, the latter assigning Swabian houses to a "third tour" in 1160-1163 and Rhineland cities to a "second tour" in 1160. The ordering below follows the chronology lens, which matches the standard scholarly reconstruction.

| Tour | Year | Route | Stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1158 | up the Main | Mainz, Würzburg, (Kitzingen [R]), Ebrach, Bamberg [R] |
| 2 | 1160 | Moselle and Saar | Trier, Metz [R], Krauftal [R] |
| 3 | c.1161-1163 | down the Rhine | Boppard, Andernach [R], Siegburg, Werden [R], Cologne |
| 4 | 1170 | Swabia | Maulbronn, Hirsau [R], Kirchheim unter Teck [R], Zwiefalten |

**Attested frame [A]:** her biographer **Theodoric** records **twenty-one places** where she preached, **five of them cathedral cities**. Public preaching to clergy by a woman is without precedent for an abbess of the empire, and was possible only under the papal authorization of 1147-48 and her archbishop's licence.

**Corrections carried into the journey file:**

- **Cologne.** The anti-Cathar sermon belongs to the Rhine circuit, not a 1160 "second tour"; scholarship dates the sermon **1163**. Coordinate 50.9413, 6.9583 stands.
- **Werden.** 51.3853, 6.9781 corrected to **51.3881, 7.0047** (about 1.9 km off).
- **Maulbronn.** 48.9989, 8.8022 (the town, not the abbey) corrected to **49.0011, 8.8128**; reassigned to **1170**.
- **Hirsau.** The largest positional error in the pool: 48.6072, 8.7194 sits about 14.5 km south, near Nagold. Corrected to **48.7378, 8.7322**; reassigned to 1170.
- **Kirchheim, Zwiefalten.** Coordinates fine; reassigned to the fourth tour, 1170.
- **Metz, Krauftal.** Coordinates fine; reassigned from "1170" back to the **1160** circuit. Krauftal's addressee is disputed and its pin is an honest **regional estimate**, labelled as such in the stop.
- **Kitzingen and Bamberg** remain **[R]**. Kitzingen dropped for stop count; Bamberg kept as the eastern limit.

## 7. Frederick Barbarossa and Ingelheim (1150? 1154? 1163?)

**[R], and irreducibly so.** Sources disagree on the year of a meeting at the imperial palace at **Ingelheim**, giving 1150, 1154, or 1163, and at least one questions **whether a face-to-face meeting occurred at all**, the evidence being a letter of the emperor's whose authenticity is doubted (*sofern der Hinweis in einem angeblichen Brief des Kaisers an sie echt ist*). Not resolved. What is **[A]**: Barbarossa's court issues Rupertsberg an **imperial letter of protection**, and she corresponds with him, warning him against the antipopes of the schism.

**Coordinate correction:** the Kaiserpfalz is at **49.9775, 8.0715**, not the pool's 49.9744, 8.0561, about 1.1 km west.

## 8. Late books and the interdict (1163-1179)

- **1163-1174: *Liber Divinorum Operum*.** [A] Completion cited as 1172, 1173, or 1174.
- **1165: founds the second house at Eibingen.** [A] **Coordinate corrected** from 49.9722, 7.9236 (2 km south, wrong side of Rüdesheim) to **49.9925, 7.9281**, the verified position of Abtei St. Hildegard above Eibingen.
- **c.1167: an illness of about three years.** [R: secondary summary; undated]
- **1173: Volmar dies.** [A] Guibert of Gembloux later becomes her secretary. [A]
- **1175/1176: the letters to Guibert.** [A] The fullest first-person description of the visionary faculty: "From my early childhood, before my bones, nerves, and veins were fully strengthened, I have always seen this vision in my soul, even to the present time when I am more than seventy years old," and "The light which I see thus is not spatial, but it is far, far brighter than a cloud which carries the sun."
- **1178: the burial dispute and the interdict.** [A] The community buries a man who had died excommunicated; Mainz demands exhumation; she refuses; the archbishop's officials place Rupertsberg under interdict, forbidding Mass, the Eucharist, and the singing of the Office.
- **1179: she produces witnesses that the man was reconciled and absolved before death; the interdict is lifted shortly before she dies.** [A] Her letter to the Mainz prelates defends the burial and the sacredness of music.

**Three corrections.** (1) *Duration.* The pool said the interdict ran "roughly two years." Wrong: the *Catholic Encyclopedia* places the episode "in the last year of her life," so it ran **autumn 1178 to spring 1179, about six months**. (2) *Age.* The pool has her "in her eighties"; she was **80**. (3) *Place.* The pool filed the whole episode under **"Disibodenberg, monastery church."** The burial, the cemetery, and the interdict were all at **Rupertsberg**, as its own claim text says. Refiled, with **Mainz** as the secondary location of the appeal.

**17 September 1179: dies at Rupertsberg, aged about 81. [A]**

**Cross-lens contradiction, flagged rather than silently resolved.** The geography lens tags the **two luminous crosses of light over the convent** at her death **[A]**; the afterlife lens tags the same detail **[R]**, correctly, since it is a miracle report from the *Vita*. The [R] reading is adopted; both tags came from the same pool.

## 9. Cult, canonization, afterlife

**No medieval canonization was completed.** The *Catholic Encyclopedia* lists **four** processes under **four** popes: "Gregory IX (1227-41) and Innocent IV (1243-54) ordered a process of information which was repeated by Clement V (1305-14) and John XXII (1316-34)."

**Two errors corrected.** (1) The pool's anchors of **1227** and **"1227-1237"** are a **misreading of pontificate ranges as inquiry dates**. German Wikipedia dates the **first petition to 1228**, three Mainz clerics reporting favourably in **1233**, Innocent IV's attempt in **1244**. (2) The pool's "three separate 13th-century inquiries opened under Pope Gregory IX" is wrong on the count, the popes, and the century span.

**Qualification.** "No formal canonization" needs a footnote: a **confirmation of cultus by John XXII on 26 August 1326**. [A] The **Roman Martyrology** entry, dated 1584 in the pool, is flagged there as unconfirmed and kept [R]; the feast (17 September) is kept in Mainz, Trier, Speyer, and Limburg. [A] **2012: Benedict XVI extends her cult to the universal Church by equivalent canonization (10 May) and names her a Doctor of the Church (7 October). [A: Holy See]**

**Rupertsberg** was sacked and burned by Swedish troops in **1632** [A]; the remnants were sold and demolished in the nineteenth century, and the site now lies mostly under later development.

**Relics to Eibingen.** [A as to outcome, [R] as to chain of custody] German Wikipedia explains that they remained in the parish church because **the parish acquired the monastery buildings in 1831**, after the 1803 secularization. Coordinate corrected from 49.9709, 7.9309 (2.4 km south, in Rüdesheim) to **49.9925, 7.9281**. **Abtei St. Hildegard** was founded **2 July 1900**, occupied **17 September 1904** by twelve nuns [A]; coordinate corrected from 49.9773, 7.935 to the same value.

**The Rupertsberg Scivias codex** was held at Wiesbaden until the war, taken to **Dresden** for safekeeping, and **lost**. [A] **Production date corrected:** Wikipedia describes it as prepared "under her immediate supervision *or that of her immediate tradition*, being made around the time of her death," that is **c.1175-1180**, not "the 1160s-1170s." The **Eibingen facsimile**, hand-painted **1927-1933** [A], is the source of every colour reproduction now available.

**Translation history corrected.** The pool claimed Hart and Bishop (Paulist Press, 1990) was the **first** full English *Scivias*. It was not: **Bruce Hozeski, *Scivias* (Bear and Company, 1986)** precedes it by four years. Hart and Bishop is the **standard scholarly** translation, a different and defensible claim.

**Modern reception [A]:** Gothic Voices, *A Feather on the Breath of God* (Hyperion, 1982), and Sequentia's recordings drive the musical rediscovery. **"Hildegard-Medizin" [R]:** a German alternative-medicine movement of the 1970s, popularized by Gottfried Hertzka; its fidelity to the medieval text is disputed.

## 10. Doubtful and rejected material

- **"Woman may be made from man, but no man can be made without a woman."** The words **are** traceable, so this is **not** a fabrication, but the pool's sourcing was wrong twice over. It is not in an "Attributed" section (English Wikipedia has none; Wikiquote lacks the line); it carries a footnote to **Shawn Madigan, *Mystics, Visionaries and Prophets* (Augsburg Fortress, 1998), p. 96**. Madigan gives **no work, chapter, or line**, and the line remains untraced in Hildegard's corpus. **Not used as a quote in the journey file.**
- **A bronze statue of Hildegard in Bingen, and an annual "Hildegardisfest."** German Wikipedia lists a **Rochusfest** and a **Hildegard-Herbst**, names **no Hildegardisfest**, and mentions **no bronze statue**. The pool's coordinate (49.9647, 7.8709) is 1.8 km west of the centre, in Bingerbrück, and is not the Rochusberg in any case. **Dropped for want of verification.**

## 11. Interlocks with the existing corpus

Only **one** relation in the interlock pool is a genuine person-to-person tie rather than shared geography: **Bernard of Clairvaux** (`bernard_clairvaux`). [A] She wrote to him c.1146-47 asking him to judge her visions; he answered encouragingly; he taught Eugene III, the pope who then had *Scivias* read at Trier. Named twice in the journey prose, at the 1146 letter and at Ebrach, where she preaches to monks of his order.

Everything else in the pool is **co-location without relation** and is deliberately not name-dropped: Carl Jung of Mainz, Luther at Worms, Humboldt and Goethe at Mainz, Otto III and Charlemagne, Richard Lionheart, Trithemius at Sponheim. **Frederick Barbarossa** is a genuine correspondent [A] but has no journey file; he appears as himself, not an interlock.

**Canonical pin inherited byte-identical** from `carl_jung_of_mainz.journey.json`: Mainz Cathedral **49.99889, 8.27389**, used for the 1158 departure and 1179 appeal.
