# Leslie Wexner: research report
*2026-08-28. Leslie Herbert Wexner, b. 8 September 1937, Dayton, Ohio; living. Founder of The Limited / Limited Brands / L Brands; buyer and rebuilder of Victoria's Secret; from 1987 to 2007 the employer, client, and legal principal of Jeffrey Epstein.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a press figure with no primary document behind it. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Evidence base.** Nothing here rests on archival documents. The spine is Wikipedia compilations, a few named investigative pieces (NYT, Washington Post, ABC News, PBS NewsHour, Vanity Fair), Wexner's own 2019 "Letter from Les," and one long 2017 interview. The financial core, what Epstein was paid and what he took, has never been disclosed in audited form by anyone; every figure in that area is a press estimate and is tagged [R] whatever confidence the outlet projected.

## 1. Dayton, Bexley, Ohio State, 1937-1959

- **[A]** Born 8 September 1937 in Dayton to Harry and Bella Wexner (Wikipedia, "Les Wexner").
- **[A]** His mother was born Bella Cabakoff in Williamsburg, Brooklyn, into a Russian-Jewish immigrant family that moved to Ohio. Family origin, not a place Wexner is recorded at; **not staged as a stop**.
- **[R]** The family settles in Bexley in 1951 and runs a women's shop called Leslie's. Shop and suburb attested; **the year 1951 is not**, tracing only to the dossier and Britannica-derived summaries.
- **[A]** Attends Bexley High School, 326 South Cassingham Road (Wikipedia, "Bexley High School," which lists him among notable alumni, confirming attendance independently of his own article).
- **[A]** Enrolls at Ohio State 1955, Sigma Alpha Mu, business degree 1959.
- **[A]** Briefly attends the Moritz College of Law before leaving it. A draft had this **[R]**; it is attested and is **upgraded**. Drinko Hall has housed the college since 1958.
- **[R], dropped as a stop.** The dossier says he served in the Air National Guard and gives **no location**. A draft placed him at the Rickenbacker (Lockbourne) installation; that was an inference, and staging him at a base no source puts him on breaks the rule that nobody is placed where they never went. **No stop written. Gap.**
- **[A]** His own account of the motive: "I had decided quite subconsciously that I was going to prove to my dad that I had real worth and I could do something, but the only language he understood was the business he was in" (Academy of Achievement, 10 February 2017). The circulating shortening "I decided quite subconsciously..." is a misquote and is not used.

## 2. Kingsdale and the first company, 1963-1978

- **[A]** 10 August 1963: first Limited store at Kingsdale, Upper Arlington, on ~$5,000 from an aunt and $5,000 from a bank (Wikipedia, "L Brands"). First-year sales **$162,000** (FundingUniverse); the circulating "about $160,000" is less exact.
- **[A]** August 1964: second store, Columbus.
- **[A], the correction that matters.** 1969: The Limited goes public selling 47,600 shares at $7.25, **over the counter**. It was **not** listed on the NYSE until **1982** (Wikipedia, "Bath & Body Works, Inc."; FundingUniverse). Two drafts put a 1969 NYSE stop at 11 Wall Street: a place the company was not, in a year it was not there. The 1969 event is written at Columbus; the NYSE stop is dated 1982.
- **[A]** 1975: board chairman (EBSCO/company summary, corroborated by the company timeline).
- **Site problem, not resolved.** No source reached gives the company's 1975 address. Downtown Columbus (39.9612, -82.9988) is an acknowledged placeholder and the stop says so. The Morse Road headquarters (approx. 40.067, -82.960) dates from **1977** and has its own stop.
- **[A]** 1978: buys Mast Industries, Andover, Massachusetts, on significant debt, to build vertical sourcing. The coordinates are the town's, **not a corporate address. Gap, stated.**

## 3. The acquisitions, 1982-1992

- **[A]** 1982: NYSE listing. Lane Bryant acquired, ~207 stores for ~$105 million.
- **[A]** 1982: Victoria's Secret bought from Roy and Gaye Raymond for stock and ~$1 million; then five or six stores plus a catalog.
- **Not staged as a stop:** Raymond founded Victoria's Secret at the Stanford Shopping Center, Palo Alto, 12 June 1977. His site, not a place Wexner is known to have stood; carried as a source reference on Wexner's 1982 stop, not as a scene.
- **[R]** The retold legend that Wexner stole the company and drove Raymond to suicide. Raymond sold under financial strain, later failed with another venture, and died in 1993; **no evidence of coercion or fraud has been established.**
- **[A]** 1985: Henri Bendel (~$10 million) and Lerner Shops (~798 stores, ~$297 million).
- **[A]** 1988: Abercrombie & Fitch, ~25 distressed stores for ~$46 million; first Ohio State trustee term begins.
- **[A], date corrected.** A&F was **spun off as a separate public company in 1996** (Wikipedia, "Abercrombie & Fitch"; "Bath & Body Works, Inc."). A draft gave 1998, the year of the final tax-free distribution of the remaining stake, which **no source reached states**; that half is **[R]** and the stop is dated 1996.
- **[A] / [R] in one entry.** Bath & Body Works was founded 1990 in New Albany as a beauty line for Express; the first store opened September 1990 in a **Cambridge, Massachusetts** mall, not Ohio **[A]**. The New Albany Company / Jack Kessler formation in 1990 is **[R]**: Wikipedia's "New Albany, Ohio" mentions the company only in passing and names neither a founding year nor Kessler.
- **[A]** 1992: Victoria's Secret reaches roughly $1 billion in sales.

## 4. Philanthropy and the built legacy, 1983-2012

- **[A], conflation corrected.** The **Wexner Foundation** was established **1983**; the **Wexner Heritage Program**, created with **Rabbi Herbert A. Friedman**, launched **1985**. A draft merged them into one 1983 event; they are written separately.
- **[A]** 1989: the Wexner Israel Fellowship begins with Harvard's Kennedy School, up to ten Israeli public officials a year. Drafted [R]; **upgraded**.
- **[R]** October 2023: the Foundation ends the Harvard partnership, citing university leadership's response to antisemitism after 7 October. Dossier only, not fetched.
- **[A]** 17 November 1989: the **Wexner Center for the Arts** opens at 1871 North High Street; Peter Eisenman with Richard Trott, landscape by Laurie Olin, named for Harry Wexner; $25 million pledged of a $43 million cost.
- **[A]** c. 1990: a 30-room Georgian house on ~336 acres at New Albany, later valued ~$47 million; an annual equestrian competition for the Center for Family Safety and Healing ran there until 2018. **The exact address is not public**; coordinates approximate.
- **[A]** 1997: acquires Foxcote House near Ilmington, Warwickshire (Wikipedia, "Foxcote House": "sold by them in 1997 to the American billionaire Les Wexner").
- **[A]** 2011: pledges $100 million to Ohio State, announced 11 February; the board voted to rename the medical center on 10 February 2012.
- **Chronology inversion corrected.** A draft put the trusteeship after the 2012 renaming ("later served as an OSU trustee during the period university physician Richard Strauss abused students"). Reversed: trusteeship **1988-1997**, second term from December 2005, chairmanship 2009-2012; Strauss's abuse ran to 1998. The trusteeship **preceded** the renaming by ~15 years.

## 5. Jeffrey Epstein, 1986-2008

- **[A], introduction contradicted.** Wexner meets Epstein in 1986. The **dossier** names insurance executive **Robert Meister and his wife** and gives no location; **Wikipedia, "Jeffrey Epstein"** places it in **Palm Beach** through unnamed mutual acquaintances. **Not resolved.** The stop is pinned at Palm Beach and states both.
- **[A]** 1987: Epstein becomes his financial adviser and, within about a year, his de facto right-hand man.
- **[A]** His own wording: "I first met Mr. Epstein in the mid-1980s, through friends who vouched for and recommended him as a knowledgeable financial professional" (Letter from Les, 2019). A draft cut this mid-sentence without an ellipsis; the full sentence is used.
- **[A]** 1989: buys the unfinished Herbert N. Straus mansion, 9 East 71st Street (Horace Trumbauer, completed 1932), for $13.2 million.
- **[A], dates in conflict.** Epstein occupies the mansion and presents it as his own. Wikipedia dates possession to c. **1995**; other accounts give a **1998** purchase; **title did not pass to an Epstein-controlled entity until 2011**, for no recorded consideration. **Three dates, one building. Not resolved.**
- **[A]** July 1991: comprehensive power of attorney, authority to hire, sign checks, buy and sell property, borrow money, and execute binding transactions on Wexner's behalf. Confirmed via the Epstein article, not only the dossier.
- **[A]** 23 January 1993: marries the lawyer Abigail S. Koppel; he was 55, she was 31. **Venue contradiction:** one lens gives "probably New York area, exact venue not specified," the other inferred New Albany; neither is sourced. The stop sits at his New Albany residence and its confidence field says the venue is unestablished. The dossier's claim that Epstein arranged the prenuptial agreement and attended is **[R]**.
- **[A]** 1995: Southern Air Transport, CIA-owned until 1973, moves its world headquarters to Rickenbacker, Columbus; much of its Hong Kong-to-Columbus cargo was bound for The Limited. **[R]** The inference later writers draw from this, that the two men were intelligence assets, has **no public authenticated record** behind it; see section 7.
- **[A]** 1996: **Maria Farmer** traveled to Wexner's Ohio property in May 1996, was housed as an artist-in-residence in a 10,600 square foot guest home on the New Albany estate, and alleges Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell sexually assaulted her there; she reported to the NYPD Sixth Precinct in August 1996 and the FBI in September (Wikipedia, "Maria Farmer"; Washington Post, O'Connell and Ellison, through the dossier, not fetched). Drafted [R]; **upgraded to [A]** for the core allegation. **No finding establishes Wexner's own knowledge, presence, or participation, and he denies knowing Farmer or knowing of Epstein's abuse.**
- **[R], removed from the narrative.** The claim that the guest house "had been deeded to Epstein in 1992" appears in the dossier and could not be confirmed anywhere reachable. Not asserted in the journey.
- **[R]** 2003: Vicky Ward publishes "The Talented Mr. Epstein" in Vanity Fair; allegations from Maria and Annie Farmer were reportedly cut before publication under legal pressure (dossier only). Wexner's phrase from it, "excellent judgment and unusually high standards," reached this pass **only as relayed by PBS NewsHour** (February 2026); **Vanity Fair blocked direct fetch.**
- **[R]** 2007: Wexner says he decided to terminate the relationship and then discovered Epstein had misappropriated large sums. This rests on **his own later account** and reporting summarizing it; no independent contemporaneous documentation is reachable.
- **[R]** 2008: ~$46 million in securities transferred from Epstein-controlled entities to the YLK Charitable Fund, later cited by Wexner as recovery. Press-derived; no audited disclosure.
- **[A]** 2008 email from Wexner to Epstein, surfaced in released Epstein-files material and quoted by PBS NewsHour (February 2026): "Abigail told me the result… all I can say is I feel sorry. You violated your own number 1 rule … always be careful." **Seen only as relayed by PBS**, not as the scanned document; the ellipses are the outlet's.
- **[R]** Between 1999 and 2018 Epstein's firms are reported to have received roughly **$200 million** in fees managing Wexner's assets, including construction of the yacht Limitless (Forbes estimate 2025, via Wikipedia). A press aggregate, not audited.

## 6. The reckoning, 2019-2026

- **[A]** July 2019: Epstein charged with sex trafficking. 25 July 2019: the NYT publishes "How Jeffrey Epstein Used the Billionaire Behind Victoria's Secret for Wealth and Women" (Steel, Eder, Maheshwari, Goldstein). Summarized via the dossier; **not independently fetched.**
- **[A]** 8 August 2019: the Wexner Foundation publishes the "Letter from Les," which condemns Epstein, states he misappropriated vast sums, and says Wexner was deceived. Lines used verbatim in the journey: "I am embarrassed that, like so many others, I was deceived by Mr. Epstein"; "My heart goes out to each person who has suffered unthinkable pain"; "For 35 years, the work we have achieved together in this community has continued to be one of the great honors of my life."
- **[A]** February 2020: L Brands announces he will leave as CEO and chairman as Sycamore Partners moves on Victoria's Secret; the deal collapses in the pandemic shutdown. **[A]** May 2020: steps down after 57 years, becomes chairman emeritus; Andrew Meslow succeeds him.
- **[A], place corrected.** Shareholder derivative litigation alleging directors tolerated a culture of sexual harassment and failed to oversee Epstein-related conflicts was filed January 2021 and settled **30 July 2021 for ~$90 million**, without adjudicating the allegations. A draft staged it at the NYSE. **Wrong**: derivative litigation over an Ohio company, not an exchange event. The stop is at Columbus.
- **[A]** 3 August 2021: L Brands splits into Victoria's Secret & Co. and Bath & Body Works, Inc.
- **[R], verification failure named.** "Victoria's Secret: Angels and Demons," three parts, dir. Matt Tyrnauer, Hulu 2022; Wexner declined to participate. **Re-verification failed: Wikipedia 404, IMDb 403.** Carried at reduced confidence rather than dropped.
- **[A]** January 2026: the House Oversight Committee subpoenas him. **[A]** 18 February 2026: deposed behind closed doors in Ohio; calls Epstein a "world-class con man," says he was naive and duped, acknowledges the 1991 power of attorney, denies knowledge of or participation in sexual crimes. **The exact site within Ohio is unstated everywhere reached**; the stop is pinned at New Albany and says so.
- **[A]** April 2026: multiple alleged Epstein victims file lawsuits naming Wexner. No adjudication as of this report.
- **[A]** Tom Davies, spokesperson, in PBS NewsHour (February 2026): "It appears Epstein was furious that Mr. Wexner refused to meet with him years after Mr. Wexner terminated Epstein and cut off all ties with him following Mr. Wexner's discovery of Epstein's theft and criminal conduct."

## 7. Reputation and legend; there is no tomb

- **[A]** Wexner is living. There is no tomb, grave, or death; the brief's "tomb and its fate" does not apply, and afterlife here is reputational.
- **[A]** The 71st Street mansion sold March 2021 for $51 million to former Goldman Sachs executive Michael Daffey, proceeds to the Epstein Victims' Compensation Program; renovation completed 2025. **Not staged as a Wexner stop**: he did not sell it and was not there. It is a source reference on his 1989 purchase stop.
- **[R]** The post-2019 legend that Wexner effectively invented Jeffrey Epstein, supplying the money, mansion, aircraft, and contacts. Not confirmable as a discrete named legend outside the dossier's myth catalog; the underlying facts are tagged above, and **the legend is the shape those facts have been given.**
- **[R]** The conspiracy narrative that the two were handlers for Mossad or the CIA, drawing on Robert Maxwell's reported intelligence ties, Southern Air Transport's Columbus presence, and Wexner's pro-Israel philanthropy. **No public authenticated record substantiates this.** Recorded with its framing named, not dropped.
- **[A]** Wexner has authored no book. His first-person textual legacy is corporate: shareholder letters and SEC filings, 1969-2020, plus statements and interviews. The absence is the checkable fact.
