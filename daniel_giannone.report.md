# Daniel Giannone (b. 1964, Córdoba): research report
*2026-08-11. Prepared for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Daniel Giannone, Argentine embroiderer; one half of the duo Chiachio & Giannone, whose two hands have signed as one artist since 2003.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference not carried by any source reached · *gap* = looked for, not found, and left open.

**Method and its limits.** The evidentiary base is narrow and it should be said at the top. Reachable: the artists' own site chiachiogiannone.com (biography, CV in English and Spanish, work archive by year); the English and Spanish Wikipedia articles "Chiachio & Giannone"; the gallery CV at ruthbenzacar.com; two interview or press pages (The Textile Curator; Spectrum News). Nearly everything below therefore descends from the artists' own account of themselves, corroborated by an encyclopaedia that visibly draws on the same CV. Where the two disagree, the disagreement is recorded, not adjudicated. No archive, no catalogue raisonné, no scholarly monograph on the duo was found; none appears to exist.

**A structural caution.** Giannone has no individual documentary record after 2003: exhibitions, prizes, residencies and holdings are all in the joint name. The atlas convention of a single traveler is a slight fiction here, and what is attested of "Chiachio & Giannone" is not automatically attested of Giannone. Two things are his alone in the sources reached: his birth in Córdoba, and two sentences a journalist attributed to him by name in 2019.

## I. Córdoba, 1964 to the early 1980s

**Birth.** Daniel Giannone is born in the city of Córdoba, Argentina, in 1964. **[A: en.wikipedia and es.wikipedia, "Chiachio & Giannone"; the two agree on year and city]**. No month, no day, no hospital, no neighbourhood, no parents' names in any source reached. *Gap, and a large one: nothing whatever of his family or childhood household is documented.*

**The convent school.** The one piece of childhood detail that circulates comes from The Textile Curator interview, whose background answer reads: "Daniel learned to embroider as a child in a convent school and never returned to embroidery until he met Leo." **[A as to the sentence existing in that source; the sentence is third-person background inside a jointly answered interview, not a verbatim statement by Giannone]**. Two corrections to the version of this claim that circulates in press paraphrase:

1. The widely repeated wording "as a girl" is **[R]** and unsupported. The traceable source says "as a child," and no source using "as a girl" was located. It should not be printed.
2. The trailing gloss "that quiet, humble skill made him a great master of embroidery" appears in no reachable source. It is editorial and has been cut.

The school is not named, and its city is not named; Córdoba is an inference from his birthplace. *Gap.*

**Training as a painter.** The artists' own English CV names, for Giannone, no institution at all: only "Professors: Sergio Bazán, Teresa Lascano, Graciela Canero." The Spanish CV adds a fourth, Constanza Martínez. **[A: chiachiogiannone.com CV]**. The attribution of his training to the Universidad Nacional de Córdoba comes solely from ruthbenzacar.com **[A as to that gallery asserting it; R as to the fact]**.

Three problems attach to the university claim, all flagged rather than smoothed:

- **Anachronism.** There was no "Facultad de Artes" of the UNC in the 1980s. The unit was the Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes (1948), annexed to the Facultad de Filosofía y Humanidades in 1975, an independent academic unit only from 12 November 2011 and a full faculty from 28 June 2012 **[A: es.wikipedia, "Facultad de Artes (UNC)"]**. In the 1980s the correct name is the Escuela de Artes.
- **Date.** The decade "1980s" is nowhere sourced. It is arithmetic from a 1964 birth. **[R]**.
- **Coordinates.** Ciudad Universitaria, -31.43617, -64.18974 **[A: es.wikipedia]**, not Nueva Córdoba; an earlier file was 1.2 km wrong.

Graciela Canero taught both men, before they met **[A: CV]**.

**Between school and 2003.** Nothing. No exhibitions, no jobs, no addresses, no relationships, for roughly two decades of his life. *The single largest gap in the record.*

## II. Buenos Aires, 2003: the meeting

Giannone meets Leo Chiachio at a mutual friend's house in Buenos Aires in 2003, and the two begin working together that year **[A: en.wikipedia and es.wikipedia]**. Neither the friend, the neighbourhood, nor the address is given; the journey file uses the atlas's canonical Buenos Aires pin, -34.6037, -58.3816, as a city anchor rather than a claim about a house. *Gap.*

Chiachio is born in 1969 and trained at the Prilidiano Pueyrredón and Ernesto de la Cárcova **[A: en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia and the CV, in agreement]**; his teachers were Pablo Suárez, Tulio de Sagastizábal, Ahuva Szlimowicz and Graciela Canero **[A: CV]**. His birthplace is contested: en.wikipedia says Banfield, in Lomas de Zamora Partido; ruthbenzacar.com says only "Buenos Aires, Argentina, 1969." **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The journey file pins Banfield at -34.750, -58.3833 **[A: en.wikipedia]** and states the disagreement.

Both carried painting into thread: "We both trained in painting but decided to transfer our knowledge of painting to other disciplines including textiles," and the work is "a painting made with threads" **[A: The Textile Curator; joint answers, not separated by speaker]**.

## V. The flag years, 2019 to 2020

**The 108th Salón Nacional.** Premio Mejor Obra, Premio Adquisición Presidencia de la Nación, 108º Salón Nacional de las Artes Visuales, for "Splash criollo," **2019** **[A: CV Awards 2019; ruthbenzacar.com also lists 2019]**. English Wikipedia dates the prize to **2018**. **Contradiction flagged, not adjudicated: both years are recorded, and the journey file carries 2019 with the disagreement stated in date_confidence.**

**Venue rejected.** An earlier draft anchored the prize at the Palais de Glace. Es.wikipedia, "Palais de Glace," states the building is closed for restoration and that Salón Nacional exhibitions take place meanwhile at other venues **[A]**. The 108th was therefore not staged there, and the actual 2019 venue was not identified. *Gap.* The journey file uses the canonical Buenos Aires pin.

**MOLAA, Long Beach.** Artists in residence at the Museum of Latin American Art, 628 Alamitos Avenue, 33.7746, -118.1796 **[A: en.wikipedia for the address; CV Residencies 2019 for the residency]**. "Celebrating Diversity" produced a 120-foot textile flag carrying messages from more than 3,500 people in the LGBT community **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Date range corrected:** en.wikipedia dates the exhibition March to **August** 2019; an earlier draft had March to May.

**Pride at the Port, San Pedro.** The flag is shown at the Port of Los Angeles **[A: en.wikipedia]**, 33.7395, -118.2923.

**Santiago de Chile.** Solo "Genio doméstico," Isabel Croxatto Galería **[A: CV]**. **Comuna corrected:** the gallery is at Napoleón 3242, Las Condes, approx. -33.4165, -70.5905 **[A: isabelcroxattogaleria.com]**. Two drafts placed it in Vitacura, the wrong comuna, about 3.5 km away, and repeated the error for the 2022 show.

**January to March 2020, Encinitas.** Residency at the Lux Art Institute, whose format keeps the studio open to visitors **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**2020, San Diego and San Ysidro.** The LATINX Flag Project: workshops at the Museum of Contemporary Art San Diego, La Jolla **[A: en.wikipedia]**, and at The Front Arte & Cultura, San Ysidro, on the border **[R: en.wikipedia, thinly]**. Participants decorated cloth pieces later assembled into a mosaic textile.

**The only individually attributed speech in the record.** Spectrum News (Triad, North Carolina), "Celebrating Diversity," byline Sarah Pilla, reporting from Long Beach, quotes Giannone twice by name, apart from Chiachio: "This is very important for the message" (printed without terminal period) and "So we are talking about us, and then if you're talking about us, it's like a mirror with other people." **[A: both verbatim, both individually attributed]**. **Citation corrected:** the article's stated publication date is 9 May 2019, not the 25 April 2019 an earlier draft took from the URL path. Its only other quoted speakers are two exhibit visitors.

## VI. Buenos Aires institutions and the current work, 2022 to 2026

- **2022.** Solo "Fiesta," Colección de Arte Amalia Lacroze de Fortabat, Olga Cossettini 141, Puerto Madero, -34.599619, -58.364956 **[A: CV; coordinates es.wikipedia]**. Three draft entries carried a point about 1 km south; all reconciled.
- **2022.** Solo "Barbas y bigotes," Isabel Croxatto Galería, Las Condes **[A: CV]**.
- **2022, Bourges.** Residency at Transpalette, Centre d'Art Contemporain, part of Antre Peaux, formerly Emmetrop; the accompanying solo was "Hope will never be silent" **[A: CV Residencies 2022]**. **A claim is deleted here:** Bourges is not in "the historic tapestry region of central France." That region is Aubusson and Felletin, in the Creuse, 130 km south; the sentence had been imported from the Aubusson entry. Transpalette is at 26 route de la Chapelle, roughly 47.098, 2.392.
- **2023, Buenos Aires.** Solo at Ruth Benzacar Galería, curated by Leandro Martínez Depietri, titled **"Comechiffones"** **[A: CV; ruthbenzacar.com agrees]**. **A title error and the reading built on it are both struck:** an earlier draft gave the title as "Comechingones" and glossed it as invoking the indigenous people of Giannone's native Córdoba province. The real title is a bilingual pun grafting the French *chiffon*, rag or cloth, onto the Comechingones name; the misspelling erases the wordplay and then states the erasure as fact. Ruth Benzacar is in Villa Crespo, approx. -34.5983, -58.4432, not Puerto Madero as two drafts had it.
- **2024.** Anthological "Vivir sus vidas," Colección Amalita Fortabat **[A: CV]**.
- **2025.** Solo "Refugio," Ruth Benzacar **[A: CV]**. **A slip corrected:** a draft called 2025 "their fourth decade of collaboration." The partnership began in 2003; 2025 is its twenty-third year, inside the third decade.
- **2025.** Solo "El MUPI, Museo Piolin, en Colección Amalita," Colección Amalita Fortabat **[A: CV; note the CV spells it "Piolin," without accent]**.
- **2026.** "Morfología del amor," MARCO, Museo de Arte Contemporáneo de Monterrey **[A: CV]**.
- **2026.** "Fortune and Abundance," Wanås Konst, Sweden **[A: CV]**. Wanås is at 56.1860, 14.0454, in Östra Göinge Municipality west of Knislinge **[A: en.wikipedia, "Wanås Castle"]**; a draft carried 56.1667, 14.1333, about 6 km southeast in open country.

## VIII. The brief's premise, rejected

The research brief that generated the working pool asked for an afterlife: tomb, later legends, modern rediscovery. **There is no afterlife. Daniel Giannone is alive.** No death record, obituary, tomb or memorial was found in any source reached, and the duo's own site lists work through 2025 and exhibitions into 2026 **[A: chiachiogiannone.com work archive]**. Any tomb, grave or posthumous cult in this dossier would be fabrication. The absence was checked against es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia and the artists' site only; a fuller sweep of Argentine press was outside the session's tool budget, so this is an absence in the sources reached, not a proof.

No monograph by Giannone, and no translated edition of one, was located. Documentation is exhibition catalogue text and the site's chronological archive. *Gap.*
