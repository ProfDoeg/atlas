# Merce Cunningham (1919-2009): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Mercier Philip "Merce" Cunningham, dancer and choreographer; born Centralia, Washington; died Manhattan.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or standard biography not confirmed in any source fetched for this pass. Where two sources disagree, both readings are printed and the disagreement is left open. Where nothing was reachable, the gap is printed as a gap.

**Method note.** The research pool arrived adversarially verified, eleven entries carrying explicit correction notes. Those are honoured in full and printed rather than folded silently into the narrative, because several reverse a widely circulated claim: the 1952 date on the best-known Cunningham quotation, the "three continents" of the centennial, the Creative Commons framing of *Loops*.

---

## 1. Origins: Centralia, 1919-1937

Born **16 April 1919 in Centralia, Washington** **[A: Wikipedia, "Merce Cunningham"]**, a lumber and rail town in the southwest of the state. Parents, siblings and schooling are not itemised in the fetched sources. **Gap, stated as such.**

**The tap lessons of Mrs. Maude Barrett.** Wikipedia confirms the lessons but gives **no year** **[A for the fact, no date]**. The pool carried "1925"; the verification pass rejected it, noting de.wikipedia states he learned tap at her school *im Alter von 11*, c. 1930, not at age six. The file dates this stop 1930-01-01, carries the contradiction in `date_confidence`, and treats the tag as **[R]**. Tradition surrounding Barrett (circus background, her children dancing with her) is **[R]** and is not used in the campa.

## 2. Cornish, and the accompanist: Seattle, 1937-1939

**Cornish School, Seattle, from 1937** **[A: Wikipedia]**, studying dance and meeting Graham's technique. The pool does **not** name the teacher. Standard biography names **Bonnie Bird**, a former Graham dancer heading the department. **[R: not in any source fetched this pass; deliberately left unnamed in the campa.]**

**Meeting John Cage, 1938** **[A: mercecunningham.org]**, Cage then employed at Cornish as a dance-class accompanist. The campa adds that Cage began preparing the piano in the same building within two years (the 1940 *Bacchanale*): **[R]**, standard Cage biography, not in the pool.

## 3. The Graham years, 1939-1945

**Graham sees him dance and invites him into her company, 1939** **[A for the invitation: Wikipedia]**. **The place is contested and is the largest geographic problem in this subject.** The pool as drafted said **Seattle**; the verification pass rejected that, noting neither Wikipedia nor mercecunningham.org gives any location, and that David Vaughan's *Merce Cunningham: Fifty Years* places the encounter at the **Bennington School of the Dance summer session held at Mills College, Oakland, summer 1939**. **Neither location is attested in a source that was reached.** The file pins Mills College and says so in `date_confidence`. Vaughan was not obtainable and is **[R]** here.

**Moves to New York, joins the company as soloist, dances six years** **[A: Wikipedia]**. **First solo concert with music by Cage, April 1944** **[A: Wikipedia]**; no day of month in any fetched source, so the file uses 1944-04-01 and flags it.

**Appalachian Spring, the Revivalist, 30 October 1944** **[A: en.wikipedia, "Appalachian Spring"]**. The pool tagged this **[R]** and placed it in New York; the verification pass **upgraded it to [A] and corrected the place**. Premiere cast: Graham (Bride), Erick Hawkins (Husbandman), May O'Donnell (Pioneer Woman), Cunningham (Revivalist), at the **Coolidge Auditorium, Library of Congress, Washington, D.C.** The Copland commission is **[R]** at pool level.

**Leaves the Graham company, 1945** **[A: Wikipedia]**. The pool's separate "begins teaching and choreographing independently, 1947" is **[R]** by its own admission and is folded into the 1945 stop rather than given a stop on a date nothing supports.

## 4. Black Mountain, and the coin: 1948-1956

**Black Mountain College, summer 1948** arrives from inside the corpus, not from Cunningham research. `buckminster_fuller.journey.json` states that "Josef Albers calls him to Black Mountain College in the summer of 1948, into the same season as John Cage, Merce Cunningham and de Kooning" **[A]**, and `erik_satie.journey.json` carries Cage's staging of *Le Piege de Meduse* with Fuller as the Baron Medusa **[A]**. That Cunningham danced the mechanical monkey is **[R]**, in neither file.

**Black Mountain residency and formation of the company there in 1953, Cage as music director** **[A: mercecunningham.org; Wikipedia]**.

**Suite by Chance: an anachronism in the pool, corrected.** The pool said "the company premieres Suite by Chance." mercecunningham.org gives the premiere as **24 March 1953, University of Illinois, Urbana**, i.e. **months before the company existed**. The file places Suite by Chance at Urbana in March and the company's formation in the summer, in that order, and never says the company premiered it. Wolff's score and the founding roster (Carolyn Brown, Viola Farber, Remy Charlip, Paul Taylor) are **[R]**.

**Guggenheim Fellowships 1954 and 1959** **[A: Wikipedia]**, merged into single stops. **Suite for Five, 1956** **[A: mercecunningham.org]**, place given only as "USA"; the paper-imperfection method is **[R]**. **Rauschenberg as resident designer**: pool said "early 1950s"; corrected against en.wikipedia, "Merce Cunningham Dance Company," which gives **1954 through 1964** **[A]**.

## 5. The company on the road: 1959-1971

- **Crises with elastic bands, 1960**, and the **Dance Magazine Award, 1960** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Walker Art Center, Minneapolis, first performance 1963, relationship running 25 years**; **Field Dances and Story, 1963** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Rauschenberg assembling Story's set from objects found in each theatre is **[R]**.
- **Vexations, Pocket Theatre, Third Avenue, 9 September 1963** **[A: erik_satie.journey.json]**, Cage's relay with Tudor, Wolff, Cale, Rifkin. **Cunningham is not attested as present**; the crossing runs through Cage, and the campa does not put Cunningham in the room.
- **Cunningham Dance Foundation established 1964**; **first international tour, six months, 1964** **[A: Wikipedia]**, place given only as "multiple countries." The itinerary named in that campa (Western and Eastern Europe, India, Thailand, Japan) is **[R]** and hedged in the file.
- **Museum Event No. 1, 24 June 1964, Museum des 20. Jahrhunderts, Vienna.** The pool's "USA" was rejected as wrong and as inconsistent with the world tour in the adjacent entry. **Caveat carried forward: the Trust's Events page returned 404, so Vienna rests on standard literature, not a re-fetchable source.** Location tag is effectively **[R]**; only "USA" is positively excluded.
- **Changes: Notes on Choreography, 1968**; **RainForest with Warhol's Silver Clouds, 1968**; **Canfield, 1969**; **Second Hand, 1970**; **Westbeth from 1971, for four decades**. All **[A: Wikipedia]**. Oliveros's Canfield score, the *Socrate* basis of Second Hand, and the permission refusal that produced Cage's *Cheap Imitation* are **[R]**.

## 7. Cage's death, and the machine: 1992-1999

**John Cage dies 12 August 1992** of a stroke **[A: Wikipedia, "John Cage"]**; cremated, **ashes scattered in the Ramapo Mountains near Stony Point**, where he had earlier scattered his parents' **[A]**. The scattering is undated in the sources; the file places it 1992-09-01 and says so.

**The Bank Street problem, unresolved.** The pool asserted Cunningham and Cage shared **107 Bank Street** "for decades." The verification pass could fetch **no source for the address at all**: Wikipedia's Cage and Cunningham articles mention neither 107 Bank Street nor 101 West 18th Street, and the only dated Cage addresses there are 326 Monroe Street (c. 1946) and Gate Hill Cooperative, Stony Point (1954). The tradition, which the campa follows and the file flags **[R]**, is Bank Street c. 1971-1979, then the West 18th Street loft until Cunningham's death: **under a decade, not "decades."** The pool's coordinate sat about 100 m off; the file uses **40.7357, -74.0072**.

- **CRWDSPCR 1993**; **National Museum of Dance hall of fame, Saratoga Springs, 1993** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Ocean, 18 May 1994, Cirque Royal, Brussels** **[A: mercecunningham.org]**, correcting the pool's "USA." Music Andrew Culver (*Ocean 1-95*) and David Tudor (*Soundings: Ocean Diary*), design Marsha Skinner **[A]**. The Cage-origin claim is confirmed on that page. The figure of 112 musicians is **[R]** and is not used numerically in the campa.
- **Hand-drawn Spaces 1998; BIPED 1999** **[A]**, both given place "USA." BIPED's premiere is commonly placed at Zellerbach Hall, Berkeley: **[R]**, not fetched, so the file keeps a placeholder pin and flags the location as unestablished. Paul Kaiser and Shelley Eshkar are corroborated by openendedgroup.com.
- **Eightieth-birthday program with Baryshnikov, New York State Theater, 20 July 1999** **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 8. Last works, the Legacy Plan, and after: 2007-2019

- **XOVER 2007, final Rauschenberg collaboration** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Loops: the pool's worst mischaracterisation, corrected.** The pool claimed Cunningham "releases the choreography Loops under a Creative Commons license, making it freely available for others to perform." In fact **Loops as choreography is a 1971 solo for the hands** (mercecunningham.org, which mentions no Creative Commons release), and what was opened was **the OpenEnded Group's digital artwork of the same name**: per openendedgroup.com, "with the support of the Mellon Foundation, it was re-created in triptych form and its underlying code released as open source," dated 2007, covering **source code and motion-capture data, not performance rights**.
- **Nearly Ninety, BAM, on his ninetieth birthday, 2009** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Tagliabue's set and the rock score are **[R]**.
- **Legacy Plan announced June 2009** **[A: en.wikipedia, "Merce Cunningham Dance Company"]**: farewell world tour, digital documentation, creation of the Trust.
- **Death 26 July 2009 at home in New York, age 90** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Final performance 31 December 2011, Park Avenue Armory**; **final board meeting 15 March 2012** in the Westbeth studio, assets to the Trust, career total close to three thousand performances in over forty countries **[A: mercecunningham.org; en.wikipedia]**.
- **Merce Cunningham Trust, 130 West 56th Street** **[A: mercecunningham.org]**, licensing technique classes, teacher training, and reconstructions including RainForest (1968), Summerspace (1958), Fabrications (1987).
- **Night of 100 Solos, 16 April 2019** **[A: barbican.org.uk]**. **Two pool errors corrected.** "Three continents" is wrong: Barbican Theatre (London), BAM's Howard Gilman Opera House, and CAP UCLA's Royce Hall are **two continents**. "Some 75 to 100 dancers" is wrong: **exactly 75, twenty-five in each location**, 100 solos at each venue, 1 hour 30 minutes without interval. The pool's BAM coordinate sat about 190 m east of the Peter Jay Sharp Building; the file uses **40.6862, -73.9776**.

## Quotations: what survives verification

Three of the five candidate quotations were **spliced, misdated, or truncated mid-clause**. Only verbatim, correctly attributed text is used. The journey file therefore carries eight quotations across forty-four stops rather than the customary third; fabricating continuity was the alternative.

**1. "If a dancer dances..." is misdated by three years and spliced.** Circulated as a "1952 statement." Primary source located: Merce Cunningham, **"The Impermanent Art," *7 Arts* No. 3, pp. 69-77, Falcon's Wing Press, Colorado, 1955**, full text at mercecunningham.org, whose page header reads "Writings / 1955." A **1955 essay, not a 1952 statement**. The two famous sentences are separated by roughly four paragraphs, so joining them with a bare period fabricates continuity. The journey file quotes both verbatim with the cut marked by an explicit ellipsis, restoring "which is not the same as having theories about dancing" in the first and "What is meant is not license, but freedom" in the second.

**2. The I Ching passage: wording sound, source description wrong.** Wikiquote cites "A Lifetime of Dance (2000)" as a book. It is a **documentary film**, dir. Charles Atlas (PBS American Masters, broadcast December 2001; IMDb tt0316238), which is why it could not be "fetched." Spoken testimony, no timecode given by Wikipedia or Wikiquote. Verified against Wikipedia's raw wikitext at "Chance operations." Note "50s," not "50's."

**3. "I took this also for dance... scraps of paper" is a splice, and the most dangerous item in the pool.** "Well, I took this also for dance." is the **last sentence of one paragraph** about Cage and the 64 hexagrams; the scraps-of-paper material is a **separate following paragraph** about *Untitled Solo*. The journey file uses the second paragraph alone, verbatim, and never joins them.

**4. Jasper Johns, Newsweek 1968: genuine, but the circulating version amputates the final clause.** Tate's In Focus essay "Johns and Cunningham" gives it in full with the citation chain: Hubert Saal, "Merce," *Newsweek* 71, no. 22, 27 May 1968, p. 88; reprinted in Varnedoe (ed.), *Jasper Johns: Writings, Sketchbook Notes, Interviews*, MoMA 1996, p. 129. The circulating trim ends at "fascinating richness" and drops "and multiplicity of direction." The full text is used.

**5. Jasper Johns, "I met him around 1953..." (Tate Gallery publication, Winter 1993): not used.** Could not be fetched; the online excerpt already carries an internal ellipsis. **[R]**, excluded from the journey file.

## Contradictions, printed and not adjudicated

1. **Where Graham saw him dance, 1939.** Seattle (pool, unsourced) versus Mills College, Oakland (Vaughan, not fetched). Neither is in a source that was reached.
2. **When he learned tap.** 1925 (pool, unsourced) versus c. 1930 (de.wikipedia) versus no date at all (en.wikipedia, which attests the lessons and not the year).
3. **107 Bank Street.** Attested nowhere fetched; duration overstated as "decades"; tradition gives roughly 1971-1979.
4. **Museum Event No. 1.** Vienna, 24 June 1964 (standard literature) versus "USA" (pool). The Trust's Events page 404'd, so neither side rests on a re-fetchable source; only "USA" is positively excluded.
5. **BIPED's premiere venue.** "USA" (pool) versus Zellerbach Hall, Berkeley (general circulation, not fetched).
6. **Rauschenberg's start as resident designer.** "Early 1950s" (pool) versus 1954 (en.wikipedia). The later date is used.
7. **The Centralia College mural.** Tagged **[A]** in the pool on no evidence. centralia.edu carries **no mention** of Cunningham, a mural, or a gallery. **Dropped entirely from the journey file.**

## Gaps, stated as gaps

- **No grave, no marker, no recorded disposition of remains.** Unlike Cage, whose ashes are documented near Stony Point, Cunningham's remains are unaccounted for in any accessible source. A real absence, not a failed search.
- **Family, parents, siblings, childhood** beyond the tap lessons: nothing in the pool.
- **The years 1945-1953**, between leaving Graham and founding the company, are attested only as a generality. No venue, no roster, no dated concert survives in the fetched material. The journey file compresses them into one stop rather than inventing five.
- **The pool's geography section is empty.** Every coordinate derives from a place named in the chronology, from the afterlife section, or from a canonical pin. Several are city-centre pins standing in for unstated venues, and each such stop says so in `date_confidence`.
- **Tour itineraries** beyond "multiple countries" (1964) and "over forty countries" (career total) are undocumented. The 1964 route named in that campa is **[R]**.
