# Odoric of Pordenone: research report

*Slug `odoric_of_pordenone`. Friar Minor of the Patriarchate of Aquileia, c. 1280 to 14 January 1331. Author, by dictation, of the `Relatio`: the second great Latin account of the road to Cathay, and the source of the medieval West's most durable eyewitness-framed report of dog-faced men.*

**Legend. [A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or editorial inference. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

**Two cautions govern the file.** The `Relatio` carries **no calendar dates**; it counts elapsed travel time, so every year between Venice and Padua is a modern reconstruction, and the reconstructions differ by up to five years. That hedge lives in `date_confidence`, never in the prose. And the text is a **dictation, not an autograph**: roughly a hundred witnesses, no two agreeing on chapter order, chapter count, or the placement of the final attestation, which Yule arranges after the Farsetti and Bollandist texts while saying no manuscript has it.

---

## 1. Origins and the Friulian record (c. 1280 to 1318)

- **Birth. [R]** Treccani (Andrea Tilatti, *DBI* 79, 2013) says only Pordenone. **Villanova** is traditional attribution, not documentary; Tilatti hedges it and so does this file.
- **The birth year rests on a corpse. [A, forensic]** The 2002 examination of the partly mummified remains at Udine indicated a man of about fifty at death, supporting c. 1280 over 1265, 1274/75 and 1286. Retrospective reading, not a record; the Minor Planet Center's citation for asteroid 4637 still prints "Odorico Mattiussi (1265-1331)".
- **9 February 1296, Gemona del Friuli. [R]** An act names an "*Odolricus puer fratrum Minorum*"; Treccani confirms date, place and phrase verbatim, and Tilatti calls the identification **attractive but not certain**. *Pool contradiction:* chronology lens **[A]**, geography lens **[R]**; the claim's own text concedes the hedge, so **[R]**. The geography lens also gives **five** friar-witnesses: not in Treccani, omitted.
- **1316 Cividale, 1317 Castello di Porpetto, 1318 Portogruaro. [A, Treccani]** Three notarial appearances in three consecutive years, in three towns: against the later hagiographic woodland hermit, a friar embedded in the legal life of the province. Portogruaro is the **last securely dated European appearance**; between 1296 and 1316 no document was reached.

## 2. The outward road: Venice to Hormuz (after 1318)

- **Departure. [A as terminus, R as date]** Tilatti places it **after 1318**; the older 1316 and 1317 lack traceable authority. No ship, company or day documented.
- **Route. [R]** Yule-Cordier's sequence: Constantinople, Trebizond, Erzurum (Arziron), Tabriz (Tauris), Soltaniyeh (Soldania), **Cassan, Yezd, Comerum, Chaldaea**, Hormuz (Ormes). *Correction:* the pool attached **1319** to the Trebizond-Tabriz-Soltaniyeh stretch and tagged it **[A]**; the year has no textual basis and is removed, every date on this leg being an ordering device. The pool also omitted the four cities between Soldania and Ormes.
- **Three notes. [R]** The repeated 1317 arrival at Trebizond traces to d'Avezac and rests on nothing. Yule noticed years the chronology cannot account for and guessed they were spent in Franciscan houses along this corridor: his inference, not Odoric's. Persepolis and Baghdad are editorial identifications, and Tilatti says the Mesopotamian ordering is not secure.
- **Method of travel. [A, Tilatti]** Caravan roads, mendicant houses, Mongol imperial communications, merchant shipping out of Hormuz. No expedition, no escort, no funding body.

## 3. India and the martyrs of Tana (1321 to c. 1322)

- **The martyrdom at Thane. [A]** Four men killed at Tana after a confrontation before a Muslim judge, the **journey's firm terminus post quem**, since Odoric's account follows the killings. *Correction (a):* the date is **8 April 1321** (Wikipedia, "Thomas of Tolentino"; martyrologies give 9 April), not the pool's bare "April 1321".
- *Correction (b):* not "four Franciscans" but **three friars plus a layman**, Thomas of Tolentino, James of Padua, Peter of Siena, and **Demetrius of Tiflis, a lay interpreter**; Peter was killed three days after the others. The pool's geography lens said Odoric recovered three bodies and not Peter's, but Yule's ch. 13 is headed "How Fr. Odoric took up the bones of the **four** friars". All four are carried.
- **Malabar. [R]** Flandrina (Odoric's own form, which Yule identifies with Pandarani / Panthalayani Kollam, modern Koyilandy), Cranganore, Quilon, the shrine of **St Thomas at Mylapore**, Ceylon (Sillan) and Adam's Peak. *Tag correction:* the pool gave **[A]** with **1322**; English Wikipedia dates the arrival only to "soon after 1321 (with high probability in 1322)", so **[R]**, 1322 as an ordering year.
- **The structural limit of the book.** Every ethnographic notice from India eastward passes through interpreters Odoric never names, in languages he did not have; Yule flags the consequence on the Ceylon chapter, direct notice and inherited marvel lore mixed with no seam marked.

## 4. The islands, and the dog-faced men

- **Manuscript order. [R]** Yule runs Lamori and Sumoltra (ch. 20), Java (21), Thalamasyn (22), Champa (23), Nicoveran (24). The pool conceded that this order "does not form a navigationally coherent route" and still tagged the stretch **[A]** with **1323**. Both dropped: **[R]**, year as ordering device.
- **Lamori.** The pole star goes under the horizon, an observation only a man at sea makes; the same chapter reports nakedness, community of wives and cannibalism. *Citation correction:* the pool cited the cannibalism passage (p. 148) as "ch. 20 (Sumoltra)", but Yule's contents page it "cannibalism, 148; kingdom of Sumoltra, 149", so p. 148 is **Lamori**, as the file now cites it.
- **Nicoveran.** Yule's heading: "Of the island of Nicoveran, where the men have dogs' faces." Men and women with faces like great dogs, an ox worshipped and worn in gold on the forehead, a king with three hundred pearls and a ruby a span long, ransom or cannibalism for captives, a closing praise of the king's justice. Yule and Cordier call it an **anomalous jumble** compounding five sources: Nicobar's name (from Marco Polo), the conventional Andaman location of dog-heads, a Maabar forehead ornament, a Ceylon ruby, and the intra-group justice of Ctesias's cynocephali.
- **What the file asserts.** That the chapter is a documented part of the transmitted medieval text: **[A]**. That Odoric personally saw dog-headed humans, as against sailor report, interpreter-mediated ethnography and inherited monster geography: **not established**, said so in `date_confidence`.

## 5. Cathay (c. 1322/25 to 1328)

- **Itinerary. [A, Treccani]** Guangzhou (Censcalan), Quanzhou (Zaiton), Fuzhou, Hangzhou (Cansay), Nanjing, Yangzhou by canal, Khanbaliq. *Date correction:* the pool's flat **1324** tagged **[A]** is unsupported. Treccani dates the Khanbaliq residence to a presumed **1322-1325**, English Wikipedia to three years **between the opening of 1323 and the close of 1328**. The ranges do not reconcile; both are recorded.
- **The relics at Zaiton. [A for the deposit, R for the confirming letter]** Tilatti attests the deposit. The pool further called **Andrew of Perugia's 1326 letter** a confirmation of the relics' reception and "the firmest external date in his whole itinerary"; the letter is genuinely 1326 and from Zayton, but **no text of it reached mentions the Tana relics or Odoric**, and Treccani does not cite it for this. Downgraded to **[R]**.
- **Hangzhou. [A]** The longest, most circumstantial chapter, and its independent detail is a main reason historians accept the core author reached China. *Wording correction, and it matters:* the pool quoted the running text as "And 'tis the greatest city on earth", which belongs only to **Yule's chapter heading**; the narrative reads "the greatest city **in the whole world**". Corrected, ch. 32, pp. 192-193.
- **Khanbaliq. [A]** Hand-raised hill, dug lake, great jade jar, mechanical golden peacocks (ch. 37); three years' residence by his own statement. *Contradiction between the pool's lenses, left standing:* the chronology lens, citing Treccani, says he **met John of Montecorvino**; the geography lens says Montecorvino was "likely encountered but is **never named in the text**". Both go into `date_confidence`. Montecorvino's death in 1328 is secure.
- **The 20,000 baptisms. [R, Yule rejects it]** Later hagiography credited him with baptising more than twenty thousand "Saracens" in China. Absent from the `Relatio`; Yule judged it hagiographic expansion. Not a stop.

## 6. The return, and Tibet

- **Route. [R]** The land of Prester John, then Kansan (commonly read as Shaanxi), then a region called Tibet: felt tents, a religious superior compared to a pope, funerary dismemberment and skull vessels. The return chapters are arranged topically and cannot be laid out as a line.
- **Lhasa. [R, disputed]** Older reference works made him the first European into Lhasa; the text does not securely name it, and modern scholarship calls his presence plausible and unproved. The stop is pinned at Lhasa for want of a candidate and says so in `date_confidence`. He probably reached Italy in late 1329 or early 1330 **[R]**.

## 7. Padua, May 1330

- **[A, Treccani and Solagna's colophon]** At the Franciscan house of St Anthony, on the order of provincial minister **Guidotto**, Odoric dictated the report to **William of Solagna**, who states he wrote it as Odoric spoke it, without ornate Latin. No commissioning document survives, so whether it was for the provincial archive or for Avignon is unknown.
- **The attestation.** Sworn under obedience: everything written he either beheld with his own eyes or heard from men worthy of credit; where he did not see, the common talk of those countries bears witness; and he **left things out** for fear they would be judged too hard for belief.
- *Three citation corrections.* (a) The attestation **opens on p. 266**, not p. 267; the running header falls mid-sentence. (b) The pool dated it "shortly before his death in 1331", but Solagna's colophon dates the dictation to **May 1330 at Padua**. (c) The prologue is on **p. 97**, not p. 98, and the pool truncated at "harvest of souls" without noting that the Bollandist text continues "[and this I did with the leave of my superiors...]".
- **Pisa. [R, tradition only]** Later tradition sends him toward Pisa to sail for Avignon for reinforcements, stops him with illness, and adds a dream of Francis ordering him home to his nest. Wadding and Mark of Lisbon, centuries later, per Tilatti. No contemporary document.

## 8. Death, cult, and the body (1331 to present)

- **14 January 1331, Udine. [A]** Died at the convent of San Francesco, on a Monday (which checks against the Julian calendar). The 2002 examination attributed death to cardiac complications with respiratory insufficiency: retrospective, not a contemporary diagnosis. *Unverified details omitted from the campa:* the magistrate **"Conrad Bernardiggi"** intervening, the patriarch attending, a crowd cutting at the habit and hair. **None is in the Treccani text reached.**
- **5 April 1331. [A, re-sourced]** The commune paid two marks to **Friar James of Ireland**, "*Socio Beati Fratris Odorici, amore Dei et Odorici*". The pool credited Treccani, which mentions him only as a companion; the payment is in **Yule-Cordier vol. II, citing the public records of Udine**.
- **May 1331. [A]** Patriarch **Pagano della Torre** appointed a canon and a notary to take sworn miracle testimony. The heading advertises **seventy** miracles; the record enumerates **twenty-seven**. It imitates a canonization inquiry; no papal proceeding from that year survives.
- **1332. [A]** The commune funded a **stone** sarcophagus by the Venetian **Filippo de Sanctis**, and the body was elevated into it in a ceremony functioning as a local episcopal canonization. *Pool contradiction:* alabaster (chronology lens) against stone (geography lens); Treccani says "sarcofago di pietra". Its "May 1332" is unsupported, and Beatrice of Bavaria is not in the Treccani text reached.
- **1735 [R] · 1749 [A] · 2 July 1755 [A].** Relics translated to a new chapel; Roman inquiry into the immemorial cult opened; **Benedict XIV confirmed the cult *ab immemorabili***, commonly called equipollent beatification. He is *beato*, not canonized.
- **1771 [A] · 2002 [A] · 2006 [A].** After the Venetian suppression the relics moved with ceremony to the **Chiesa della Beata Vergine del Carmine**, Via Aquileia, Udine, where they remain. *Date correction:* the pool gave "1769", "1769-71" and "1771"; Italian Wikipedia dates the **transfer** to 1771, and **1769** is the **suppression**. The diocesan examination of 14 January 2002 opened a renewed canonization phase, closed positively in 2006, with no final canonization since. The pool's sarcophagus restoration at the sixth centenary is dated 1931 by its own inference **[R]**; not a stop.

## 9. The text after him

**Manuscript census. [A, and the authorities differ by a factor of three.]** English Wikipedia: c. 117 copies, 79 Latin, 23 Italian, 8 French, 5 German, plus Castilian and Welsh. Treccani: c. 80 Latin, 7 Italian, 2 French, 2 German, 1 Catalan-Castilian, 1 Welsh. Side by side, not merged. The pool's claim that the **oldest manuscript is at Assisi** could not be verified, and Italian Wikipedia names the **Biblioteca Riccardiana, Florence**. Neither is asserted.

**Editions. [A, Treccani]** Jean le Long 1351; BnF Français 2810, c. 1411, ff. 97r-115v, alongside Marco Polo **[R]**; Pesaro 1513; Ramusio 1574; Hakluyt 1599; *Acta Sanctorum* 1643; Yule-Cordier 1913; Van den Wyngaert 1929. **Absorption into Mandeville [R]:** the pool's own note says the item was **not** confirmed and should be checked against Higgins. Not a stop.

**The Bolinao legend. [R, rejected]** A modern tradition in Bolinao, Pangasinan holds that Odoric was blown ashore there in 1324 and said a Mass, baptising inhabitants two centuries before Magellan. His account names neither Bolinao nor Luzon, and **William Henry Scott rejects the story as a manufactured modern legend**; both supporting pages returned HTTP 403. **No Bolinao stop is written**: it would stage him where the evidence says he never was.

## 11. Interlocks

**Named in a campa: `marco_polo`, once.** Textual justification, not geographic: Yule and Cordier's notes on ch. 24 identify the Nicoveran chapter's great royal ruby as **borrowed or conflated from Marco Polo's Ceylon material**, and the name Nicoveran as an echo of Polo's Nicobar. Odoric's text takes from Polo's, a one-directional relation of the licensed kind, named at the Nicoveran stop and nowhere else. **Considered and rejected:** `rabban_bar_sauma` (same Trebizond, forty years earlier: shared corridor, no relation between the men); `kublai_khan` (Odoric describes buildings Kublai's builders raised, not the man, who died in 1294); **Jerusalem and Mecca**, which the interlock lens itself documents as rejected pins; **John of Montecorvino**, who has no journey file.

## 12. Apparatus relations for the operator

Odoric is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework may be applied backward onto his file as stops and nothing may reach forward out of it. Three relations surfaced that are not rooted in his own biography. None was silently deleted.

1. **Ibn Battuta's Barahnakar chapter (1345). Direction: FORWARD.** A close parallel to Nicoveran, flagged in Ibn Battuta's own file as contested (Gibb and Beckingham reject the Andaman/Nicobar identification), a caution applicable here too. But 1345 postdates Odoric's death by fourteen years: a forward pointer, not licensable, and **removed** from the Nicoveran `suggested_refs`. **If the operator wants it recorded, it belongs on Ibn Battuta's file pointing back at Odoric.**
2. **Ratramnus of Corbie on whether the cynocephali possess reason (c. 863-865). Direction: BACKWARD.** The corpus's doctrinal anchor for the question his chapter raises. No evidence Odoric read Ratramnus, so this is **not his own relation**; kept as a `suggested_ref` only, a background reference being apparatus rather than a staged scene. **If the operator judges that even a backward `suggested_ref` requires Odoric on the exceptions list, strike it.**
3. **Saint Christopher the dog-headed, Coptic and Ethiopian tradition (c. 250). Direction: BACKWARD.** Same status and reasoning; kept as a `suggested_ref` only.

## 13. Honest gaps

- Twenty undocumented years, February 1296 to 1316.
- No calendar date in the `Relatio` between Venice and Padua; every year in the middle six segments is an ordering device and says so.
- Andrew of Perugia's 1326 letter could not be read in a text mentioning the Tana relics or Odoric, so the "firmest external date" claim is not verified.
- The manuscript census cannot be settled, and the oldest manuscript's repository is contested between Assisi and the Riccardiana.
- No Latin critical edition reached; everything Latin here passes through Yule-Cordier or Treccani.
- Whether Odoric met Montecorvino: contradicted between two lenses both citing Treccani. Open.
- Whether Odoric entered Lhasa: disputed. Open, with the pin flagged.
- What he left out of the book as too hard for belief is nowhere specified.
