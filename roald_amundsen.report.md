# Roald Amundsen (1872-1928): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-22 for `roald_amundsen.journey.json`. 45 stops, 9 segments. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Gregorian.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting only on institutional biography. **[C]** arrived in the verified pool wrong and was corrected; every **[C]** is itemised in §12 and also written into the `date_confidence` of the stop it touches.

The pool came adversarially annotated: eleven entries carried explicit `CORRECTED:` notes, one of which (Cape Colborne) would have plotted the completion of the Northwest Passage some 450 km ahead of the ship.

---

## 1. Borge, Kristiania, a father lost at sea (1872-1893)

- Born 16 July 1872 at Tomta on the Hvidsten farm land, Borge, Ostfold, youngest of four sons of the shipowner and master mariner Jens Engebreth Amundsen and Hanne Henrikke Gustava Sahlqvist. **[A: Britannica; MiA; Wikipedia]** Pin to two decimals, which is all the pool supports.
- The family moves to Kristiania while he is an infant; he grows up in Uranienborg, reportedly playing there with Carsten Borchgrevink. **[A to the district; R to the acquaintance]** The move is undated everywhere reachable, so it sits in the birth campa; no childhood stop.
- 1886, the father dies aboard the steamship *Rollo* off Kristiansand. **[A: Fram Museum; MiA person record]** Month and day not established; dated 1886-01-01 and says so.
- 1890-1893, medicine at the Royal Frederick University; an indifferent student. **[A: Fram Museum; Britannica]** 1893, his mother dies, he sells the textbooks and signs on aboard the sealer *Magdalena*. **[A: Britannica and Wikipedia]** No *Magdalena* stop: the port of signing-on was not established, and inventing one is worse than folding it into the university campa. Mate's papers 1895 and master's licence 1900 **[A]** are placeless in the pool and get no stops.
- The Hardangervidda ski crossings, the failed attempt and the later one with his brother Leon. **[R]** In both the Fram Museum and MiA, with dating that varies between retellings, as the pool flags. Dated 1894-01-01 as a declared placement.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable documents the years between the father's death and university, or the household's finances. The popular detail of sleeping with the windows open to harden himself was kept out; no source reached carries it.

## 2. Belgica: the first winter in Antarctic ice (1897-1899)

- First mate under Adrien de Gerlache out of Antwerp, August 1897; the crew includes Frederick A. Cook. **[A: Britannica; Fram Museum]**
- Beset in the Bellingshausen Sea through the winter of 1898, the first wintering in Antarctic waters and involuntary; Cook and Amundsen use fresh seal and penguin against scurvy; free in 1899 after the crew saws and blasts a channel. **[A: Fram Museum; Wikipedia; the Belgica diary, 1999 edition]** Two stops on one pin (-70.5, -85.0), an approximate position in pack that had no fixed location. Neither month is fixed.
- Amundsen's lifelong defence of Cook after the North Pole claim collapsed is **[R]**: standard biography, no citable letter in the pool. What the pool gives instead is two verbatim sentences about Cook printed in *The South Pole* in 1912, after the claim had fallen apart. Both are used as quotes.

## 3. Gjoa: the Northwest Passage (1901-1906)

- *Gjoa* bought 28 March 1901, built 1872 **[A: Wikipedia, 'Gjoa']**; **[C]** her dimensions corrected (§12, row 1).
- Magnetic training in Hamburg under Georg von Neumayer. **[R]** No stop; carried in the departure campa. The Fram Museum corroborates the magnetic programme's purpose, not the studentship.
- Departure from Kristiania 16 June 1903 with seven men. **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]** The night escape from a creditor's lien is **[R]**: the campa says in its own words that the debt is documented and the midnight departure is Amundsen's telling.
- Godhavn (twenty dogs) and Beechey Island, summer 1903 **[A: Fram Museum itinerary]**, Godhavn folded into the Beechey campa for budget.
- Gjoa Haven 9 September 1903 to 13 August 1905, two winters, sustained Netsilik contact from late October 1903, source of the skin clothing, snow-house building and dog technique. **[A: Fram Museum; University of Oslo Historical Museum]** Two stops on one pin, the second dated 1904-01-01 because no single date fits a two-year relationship.
- **[C] Cape Colborne, 17 August 1905**, repinned to **68.9647, -105.2411** at the approach to Cambridge Bay, the water Collinson charted from the west (§12, row 2).
- Third winter at King Point; the overland dog run to Eagle; Gustav Juel Wiik dies 1 April 1906, the only death. **[A: Fram Museum]** The Eagle stop is dated 1905-12-05 as a flagged **placement**; the pool gives no date.
- Nome 31 August 1906. **[A]** **[C]** *Gjoa* stood in Golden Gate Park **1909-1972**, not the pool's "1906-1913." **[A: Wikipedia, 'Gjoa']**

**No San Francisco stop.** His presence at the donation is not established and the 1913 flight over the bay with Silas Christofferson was not verified. A stop belongs to whoever was there; the ship's fate rides on the Nome stop as a fact of the object.

## 4. The secret turn south (1908-1911)

- November 1908, the Arctic drift announced with Nansen's *Fram*, which Nansen gives up to him; 1909, Cook and Peary both claim the Pole and Amundsen secretly redirects south while still raising money for an Arctic voyage. **[A: Fram Museum]**
- **[C] The 1910 departures**, split into two stops (§12, row 4). The June pin is the Svartskog house on the Bunnefjord, declared a placement, not a documented mooring.
- **[C] Funchal.** *Fram* arrived **6 September** and Amundsen told the crew on **9 September**, telegraphing Scott "Beg to inform you Fram proceeding Antarctic." **[A: Wikipedia, 'Amundsen's South Pole expedition']**
- Bay of Whales 14 January 1911, Framheim finished 28 January, about 96 km closer to the Pole than the British base. **[A]** Coordinates approximate and declared: that ice front has since calved.
- The premature start of 8 September 1911 and Johansen's public criticism. **[A: Fram Museum]** Placed at the Framheim pin because the party turned back there; its furthest point is nowhere fixed.

## 5. Polheim (1911-1913)

- Departure 19 October 1911, five men, four sledges, 52 dogs, Johansen struck off. **[A]** Johansen's later fate is **not** carried: no verified account was reached, and it is exactly the detail that should not be written from memory.
- **[C] Butcher's Shop**, repinned to **-85.60**. **CONTRADICTION NOT RESOLVED:** Wikipedia gives **27** dogs killed with 18 going on, Amundsen's own text **twenty-four**. The file states both.
- South Pole 14 December 1911, Polheim, the letters for King Haakon VII and for Scott. **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia; *The South Pole*, vol. 1]** Some secondary sources printed 15 December through date-line confusion, noted in `date_confidence`. Pin **-90.0 / 0.0**, matching the **90.0 / 0.0** North Pole pin in `nicholas_roerich.journey.json`.
- **[C] The return.** 25 January 1912, 99 days, 11 dogs on two sledges, all correct; the distance is not (§12, row 7).
- Hobart 7 March 1912. **[A]** Scott reached the Pole 17 January 1912 and died on the return, the news reaching the world in 1913. That is Scott's; here it is only what Amundsen learns, never a scene at the Pole.
- **Buenos Aires, 25 May 1912.** *Fram*'s arrival is **[A: Fram Museum, 'the-polar-ship-fram']**. Whether Amundsen was aboard or ashore **was not established in this pass**, and the stop says so: it rides on the ship's movement and the patron's presence. This is the one stop where the "whoever was actually there" rule is under strain, and it is flagged rather than quietly resolved. **[C]** *Fram* never sailed for San Francisco (§12, row 8). Christophersen's patronage is **[R]**; the mountain named for him is unverified and absent from the campa. The pin is the canonical atlas anchor **-34.6037, -58.3816**, inherited byte-identically, not the geography lens's -58.3705.
- Philadelphia, January 1913, photographed with Ernest Shackleton and Robert Peary. **[A: Wikipedia]** Day not fixed. The one documented co-presence with an existing atlas traveller.

## 6. Maud: the Northeast Passage (1917-1921)

- **[C] Vollen, Asker** repinned to **59.7975, 10.4508**, the pool's own afterlife pin for Maud's 2018 return.
- *Maud* built by Christian Jensen, launched June 1917, named for Queen Maud. **[A: Fram Museum]** The block-of-ice christening was deliberately **not** written in: in the pool it belongs to the 2019 christening of *MS Roald Amundsen*, and letting it migrate backward would manufacture a fact.
- Vardo 18 July 1918, nine men, Sverdrup leading the science **[A]**; then the winter east of Cape Chelyuskin, with broken arm, polar bear, and carbon-monoxide poisoning on 10 December 1918. **[A for the poisoning, R for the injuries]** The Fram Museum says his health never recovered; the campa attributes that judgement to the museum rather than asserting it.
- Tessem and Knudsen vanish carrying records toward Dikson, 1919. **[R]** Month unknown, defaulted to 01; the pin is the destination, because neither route nor place of death is established, which is the content of the stop.
- Nome 28 July 1920, third navigation of the route after Nordenskiold's *Vega* and the Russian *Taymyr* and *Vaygach*. **[A]** The 1921 Seattle refit **[R]** folds into this campa; no Seattle stop.

## 7. Debts and aeroplanes (1922-1924)

- 1922, the Junkers flight from the New York area toward Seattle with Oskar Omdal, crashing in Pennsylvania **[R]**, departure field unconfirmed, the file using the pool's Long Island coordinates and declaring the imprecision; 1923, the Wainwright base and the failed transpolar attempt. **[A: Fram Museum/MiA aviation chronology]**
- September 1924, bankruptcy. **[A for the filing, R for the blame]** He blamed his brother Leon and the businessman Haakon Hammer; **no criminal or judicial finding against either exists in the archival record**, and the file says so rather than repeating the accusation.
- **[C] The two girls** (§12, row 10). **[A: Wikipedia]** The 1924 dating follows the bankruptcy year and inherits its uncertainty.
- Kristine Elisabeth "Kiss" Bennett, met in London 1907 **[A: Wikipedia]**, got no stop: the location is unspecified, and an undated relationship stop would have displaced a documented one. The file's coverage of his private life is thin.

## 8. The flying boats and the airship (1925-1927)

- **[C] The 1925 dates.** Landing **21 May 1925**, the day they left, after eight hours aloft; takeoff **15 June**. **[A: Fram Museum, N24/N25 page]** The pool's 6 June contradicted its own "over three weeks."
- The ice camp at 87 deg 43 min N: latitude attested, longitude not. The often-quoted tonnage of snow shifted was left out for want of a source.
- **Merged stop.** The Norge's departure from the Ny-Alesund mast rides inside the North Pole campa, to hold the file at 45; the 1925 stop already holds that pin.
- **[C] The Norge command structure** (§12, row 12). **[A: Wikipedia, 'Norge (airship)']** The pool's phrasing reversed the very dispute the 1927 autobiography was about.
- 11 May 1926 departure (09:55), North Pole 12 May, Teller 14 May after roughly 72 hours. **[A]** Byrd's 9 May claim is named as contested, the pool's own framing.
- July 1926 homecoming **[R]**; *Mitt liv som polarforsker* 1927, with its attacks on Nobile **[A: Fram Museum]**.

## 9. The Latham and the empty sea (1928)

- **[C] Italia** (§12, row 13). Nine survivors made the Red Tent; *Krassin* lifted the last on 12 July. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- "Make me a free man" is **[R]**: attributed in Fram Museum institutional history, not verified against a letter or court record. Used as a quote with that status written into `quote_source` itself, not buried.
- Tromso 18 June 1928 aboard Latham 47.02 with Rene Guilbaud, Leif Dietrichson and three other Frenchmen; radio contact lost within hours. **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia, 'Latham 47']** The prototype risk assessment is May and Lewis in *Polar Record*.
- **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED.** The pool's Fram Museum-derived chronology reads the recovered artifacts as indicating a crash **south of Bjornoya**. Wikipedia's 'Latham 47', where both are actually documented, draws **no conclusion** and says the aircraft disappeared without a trace. Float (Troms, 31 August) and tank (Haltenbanken, October) came ashore off mainland Norway, which is what drift produces. **The file records no crash location** and lets the last two campas end on that.
- The Hugin 1000 seabed searches of 2004 and 2009, which found nothing, close the last campa. **[A: Wikipedia]**

## 10. Interlocks with existing atlas files

One existing traveller is named in a campa, on a documented relation, not shared geography.

- **`ernest_shackleton`**, at the Philadelphia stop of January 1913, where the two were **photographed together with Peary** on Amundsen's lecture circuit. **[A: Wikipedia]** Direct co-presence, not co-location on a map. Shackleton's 1921 sentence, already verbatim in his file, names Amundsen's conquest of the Pole as the reason the trans-Antarctic crossing became the one great object left; it is cited as source and `suggested_ref` on Polheim and Philadelphia. The utterance stays his, and 1921 falls inside Amundsen's own life, so it is no forward reference under the block rule.
- **Robert Falcon Scott** is named at Funchal, Polheim, Hobart and Philadelphia. Queued with **no file yet**, so a named rival rather than an interlock slug; when his file comes, the Funchal telegram and the letter at Polheim are the hinges from his side.
- **Negative finding.** The atlas has files for **none** of Amundsen's circle: Nansen, Cook, Peary, de Gerlache, Nobile, Ellsworth, Riiser-Larsen, Sverdrup, Franklin. His is a new node, dense in its own geography and thin in cross-reference.

## 11. Apparatus relations for the operator

Amundsen is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework may be applied backward or forward on his file. Four pool relations are not rooted in his own life. None was silently deleted; all four are here, with the direction named.

1. **Miguel Serrano, hollow earth. Direction: FORWARD (later engagement).** Serrano treats the South Pole as an entrance to an inhabited Inner Earth and asks through the Caleuche legend whether the ghost ship will cross beneath its ice (`serrano.journey.json`, lines 215, 239). Amundsen's 1911 arrival closes exactly that terra incognita, but he is not on record on any of it and Serrano wrote decades later. **Not written into the file.** The convergence belongs on Serrano's file.
2. **Nicholas Roerich, Banner of Peace at both poles. Direction: FORWARD.** North Pole May 1988, South Pole January 2000 (`nicholas_roerich.journey.json`, lines 579-583, at the pole pins this file inherits). **Not written in.** Shared pins, other people's engagement.
3. **Rudolf Steiner's circuit "from Palermo to Kristiania." Direction: SIDEWAYS, same-city coincidence.** `steiner.journey.json` line 591 gives no date and no record of contact exists. **Not written in.**
4. **Alejandro Christophersen and the surname. Direction: SIDEWAYS, unverified family link.** The architect was schooled in Kristiania in the years Amundsen grew up there and built a church for Norwegian sailors by the Buenos Aires docks (`alejandro_christophersen.journey.json`, lines 28-32, 419). Separately, **Don Pedro Christophersen**, Norwegian shipowner of the River Plate, was Amundsen's patron and intervened in the 1924 bankruptcy. **Whether the two are related is not established**, and the interlock lens refused to carry it. The file names Don Pedro at Buenos Aires as Amundsen's own patron **[R]** and does **not** name the architect. Verify the relation and that stop becomes a real two-file thread instead of a surname match.

## 14. Afterlife, carried outside the file

The file ends in October 1928 with the last recovered fragment, as `ernest_shackleton.journey.json` ends at the grave. Nothing posthumous is a stop. Four attested facts ride in `suggested_refs` or the final campas: the **Amundsen-Scott South Pole Station**, built from November 1956 and named for both rivals; *Fram* at the Fram Museum on Bygdoy; *Maud*, sunk at Cambridge Bay in 1930, raised July 2016 and back at Vollen 18 August 2018; and the 2019 film *Amundsen*, received coolly at home. **[A: Wikipedia]** The archival turn on the debts, the brother and the two girls is recent: Ytreberg in hand would change §7.
