# Gita Galina (Guta Israiley; Gitta Galina, Guita Galina, Gita Galina Perlman)
*Research report, 2026-08-23. Yiddish stage and recording actress, c. 1907 Vitebsk to 6 January 1983, Israel. Riga, Vienna, Whitechapel, Warsaw, Buenos Aires, Johannesburg, Tel Aviv, Second Avenue.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named and reached directly · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim resting on a source that could not be opened. Contradictions are flagged and left standing.

---

## The shape of the evidence, stated first

Almost nothing about Gita Galina is documented as Gita Galina. The spine of her life reaches us through **her husband's biography**: Zylbercweig's *Leksikon fun yidishn teater* entry for Max Perlman, English translation at the Museum of Family History, which names her **exactly once**, for Vienna. Every other leg of the interwar tour is a Perlman sentence into which she has been read.

Three sources are hers in her own right: the **UBA** *Diccionario de musicos judios exiliados en Argentina*, vol. I pp. 195-196, her own entry, with birth name, arrival date and four dated Argentine performances **[A]**; the **Freedman** (Penn) and **JMI** catalogues, listing her as a named recording artist on two Makolit LPs **[A]**; and the **Bezikaron** grave record, giving her father's name **[A]**.

Everything else is a mention inside someone else's paragraph. **No birth certificate, marriage certificate, personal papers, autobiography, interview, death certificate or obituary was located.** Her mother is nowhere. This governs every tag below: where a claim reads attested for the event and reconstruction for her presence at it, that is the actual state of the record.

## 1. Origins: Vitebsk or Riga (c. 1907)

- Born **Guta Israiley** in **Vitebsk**, Russian Empire, now Belarus, **circa 1907**. **[A: UBA vol. I p. 195; Wikidata Q81939577, which also reads "circa 1907 in Vitebsk"]** A birth date of **1 April 1907** circulates attributed to yiddishmusic.jewniverse.info **[R]**: that site returned 403 in one pass and, in another, was found to carry **no biographical note on her at all**. See Contradiction 6.
- **DYTP** states she was "also from Riga" and had made a name in the theatre before their partnership. **[A as that source's statement; [R] as biography]** **Contradiction 1, unresolved:** Vitebsk (UBA, Wikidata) versus Riga (DYTP). Wikidata lists Latvia among her later residences but gives Vitebsk as birthplace, so it does not side with the Riga tradition.
- **Gap.** Nothing is known of her childhood, family beyond her father's given name Zalman (from the gravestone), training, or how she entered the theatre.

## 2. Riga and the Baltic circuit (late 1920s to 1934)

- Meets and begins performing with **Max Perlman** in Riga's Yiddish theatre. **[A for the meeting-in-the-theatre statement: DYTP]** The year 1932 is the inference already carried in `max_perelman.journey.json`, from joint touring preceding the 1934-35 London season.
- Perlman's Riga years (Rozovski's choir, the Peretz Club studio, the Nayer Yidisher Teater from 1928) and **Kovno**, Yidish Meutim Theatre, 1933-1934, are **[A: Zylbercweig; UBA frames the phase as 1928-1934]**. Galina is documented in none of it. **[R for her presence]**

## 3. The interwar European circuit (mid-1930s to 1939)

The whole itinerary is one paragraph of Zylbercweig: Vienna's Reklam Theatre (five months, director Goldflis), Czechoslovakia, London (director Fanny Blumenthal), Liverpool, Leeds, Glasgow, Manchester, Paris (about a year, under Lachtiger, Lazar and Lustik), Belgium (director Mendelevitsh), Warsaw's Skala, Poland; plus summers in Montevideo and 1945 in Chile.

- **Vienna, Reklam Theatre, five months, director Goldflis. [A]** The only leg naming her: "1933-1934 -- in Kovno's 'Yidish Meutim Theatre', later -- five months, together with his wife Gita Galina, guest-starring in Vienna's 'Reklam Theatre' (director Goldflis)". **No year is given for Vienna;** it must postdate the Kovno seasons.
- **All other legs: [R] for Galina.** The incoming dossier tagged the entire itinerary [A] for her; that overstates the source. Wikidata's residence list (Latvia, France, Belgium, Argentina, New York) is weak support for France and Belgium only.
- London venue: Zylbercweig names no theatre; DYTP names the **Pavilion Theatre, Whitechapel Road**, closed 1935, which bounds the visit. **[A for the name; [R] for identifying the two engagements]**
- **Translation caveat.** "together with his wife Gita Galina" is the **English translator's** wording. The Yiddish original was not inspected; it must not be quoted as Zylbercweig's literal text.

## 4. Argentina, first period (1939-1947)

- **Invited to Argentina 1939 by the impresario Adolf Mide**, with whose company they acted three years, then in a troupe at the **Excelsior**. **[A for Perlman] [R for Galina]** The dossier's reading, that the three years *were* the Excelsior residency, misreads the sentence.
- **7 June 1939: arrival at Buenos Aires aboard the *Oceania*, embarked at Trieste, both names.** **[A: UBA vol. I p. 195, vol. II p. 156]** The single day of her life fixed to the day by a document. One pass could not load the UBA PDF and carried it at [R]; another fetched it directly.
- **Compania Israelita de Operetas**; the **Teatro Mitre** and **Teatro Excelsior**. **[A: UBA]** Independently, Fiszman and Skura's footnote 4: "Su mujer, Guita Galina, tambien fue actriz y cantante del teatro idish, especialmente de los teatros Mitre y Soleil." **[A]**
- **9 May 1942, Teatro Excelsior**: Perlman and Galina, orchestra under **Leo Schwarz**, for the JKG. **[A: UBA]**
- **14 July 1943, Salon Union Tranviarios**, *Rund um die Operette*, Sociedad Cultural Israelita, conducted by Victor Schlichter, directed by Andre Laszlo, cast "Gita Galina, Max Perlmann and Philipp Wenning"; notices in *Argentinisches Tageblatt* 1 July p. 4 and *La Semana Israelita* 16 July p. 9. **[A: UBA vol. I p. 283]** Her name is printed first.
- **16 September 1943**: a photograph of Galina in the *Argentinisches Tageblatt*. **[A: UBA]** Not described, not retrieved.
- **Through 1947: Brazil, Uruguay, Chile.** **[A: UBA, "Hasta 1947 realizo espectaculos tambien en Brasil, Uruguay y Chile"]** No city, venue or date inside that span. Zylbercweig adds "during the summertime in Montevideo, Uruguay" and "1945 -- acted in Chile", both **[A for Perlman, [R] for Galina]**.
- **Geography corrected in the journey file.** The Excelsior stood at **Avenida Corrientes 3224, facing the old Abasto market** (1912, demolished 1961), not on the downtown Obelisco stretch; Ansaldo places Excelsior and Soleil in **Abasto**, the Mitre in **Villa Crespo**. Coordinates arriving with the dossier gave Buenos Aires and Montevideo **positive** latitudes.

## 5. Southern Africa and London (1948-1951)

- **1948, His Majesty's Theatre, Johannesburg**, Commissioner and Eloff, season brought out by **Sarah Sylvia**; reportedly 20,000 attendees in a fortnight, eight weeks. **[A that Perlman starred there in 1948 "with Guita Galina": ESAT] [R for run length and attendance, on Belling alone]** Some accounts date the first season 1949. **Contradiction 2, unresolved:** tour years 1948/49, 1951, 1953 (DYTP) versus 1948/49, 1951, 1972 (Belling).
- **8 April 1949, *South African Jewish Chronicle***: joint farewell letter signed "Max Perlman. Gita Galina", thanking audiences in the Union of South Africa and Southern Rhodesia and thanking Sarah Sylvia, announcing departure for London under **Meir Tzelniker**. **[R]** The archived PDF at ibali.uct.ac.za was fetched but returned image data only; wording rests on a dossier summary. This is the only document said to carry her own signature.
- **The Grand Palais, 133 Commercial Road, London**, seasons **1949, 1951, 1955**, titles *Here Comes Grandpa*, *The Shlemmazl*, *The American Litvak*. **[A: DYTP]** A photograph of her taken in London **5 July 1949** is said to be in the Jewish Museum London collection. **[R]** Page not loaded.
- **1951, Teatro Soleil, Buenos Aires.** Lidia Goldberg to Susana Skura, March 2001: "y al ano siguiente vinieron Perlman con Guita Galina al Teatro Soleil y ahi me contrataron otra vez." **[A: Fiszman and Skura PDF retrieved]** Goldberg's first appearance was at the Excelsior in 1950 aged eleven, fixing "al ano siguiente" as 1951. Cast around them: Alberto Ortiz, Pedrito Appel, a niece of Perlman's. **[R]**
- **1951, Johannesburg**: **Menora Zahav**, born in Paris to the Yiddish actress Niusia Gold, hidden in France through the occupation, in Johannesburg from 1950, first performs with Perlman at fifteen and a half and later takes the ingenue roles. **[A: DYTP]** The essay frames this as succession, not replacement. **No document records Galina leaving the company**; the 1973 Brazil item bills all three together.

## 6. Israel (1953-1960)

- **1953: first visit; programme no. 25 of the Li-La-Lo revue, *Zehirut ha-derekh be-tikun*, premiering 31 October 1953**, with Ann Crawley and Rosa Turkov. **[R: Rojanski, via dossier summary; the book was not opened in any pass]** Circumstantially supported: Perlman told reporters on 12-13 November they had been in the country "only two weeks", implying arrival about 29 October, and Li-La-Lo mounted 26 programmes, closing in February 1954.
- **Venue correction.** The dossier's "Matate/Li-La-Lo, political-satirical" fuses **two different Tel Aviv companies**. Per Hebrew Wikipedia: **HaMatate** (1928-1954) was the satirical, socialist-spirited theatre; **Li-La-Lo** (1944-1954, founded by **Moshe Valin**) staged escapist European-cabaret revues and was explicitly unlike HaMatate. Its home was the **Yasha Heifetz Hall, Simtat HaShoeva**, off Allenby. "Matate" and "political-satirical" dropped.
- **13 November 1953, about 2 a.m., Dizengoff Square.** After the Thursday night performance a car with the impresario driving, Perlman and Galina aboard, goes into the fountain; light scratches; the driver blames the darkness; Perlman jokes that they have been in the country two weeks and have already had "a nice and respectable bath at Dizengoff Square". **[A: Eli Alon retrospective, News1]** One pass reported this source unretrievable (403) and the story unconfirmed; a later pass retrieved it and confirmed it in detail. **That earlier verdict is withdrawn.** A photograph attributed to Hans Pinn. **[R]**
- **27 January 1954, *Maariv***: tour advertised to **Netanya, Jerusalem, Kiryat Motzkin**. **[R]** OCR at second hand; NLI page not loaded; no hall named for any stop.
- **1954: founded their own Yiddish company, Te'atron Komedya**, repertory *Der groyser fardiner*, *Shlimazl*, *Motl Mayers glikn*, *Der yidisher gazlen*, *Alts tsulib parnose*. **[R: Rojanski, not independently read]** **Contradiction 3, unresolved.** DYTP describes 1954 as a "grand debut" on a **nine-month visit**, with a Ramat Gan apartment from the mayor, and never mentions a company being founded. A nine-month visit is not a resident theatre; the dossier's claim that DYTP corroborates Rojanski here is wrong.
- **4 February 1955, *Jewish Chronicle* (London), p. 24**, review of *The Shlemazzel* signed A.B.L.: the only contemporary sentence describing her own performance. **[A: quoted with its footnote in DYTP]** **Contradiction 4, unresolved:** `max_perelman.journey.json` attaches the same sentence to the 1948 Johannesburg season.
- **14 December 1960**: joint feature "Max Perlman un Gita Galina" in *Tsanins ilustrirte velt*. **[R: Rojanski via dossier]** Evidence she kept independent billing.

## 7. The records (undated)

- **Makolit 12016, *A Khusn Mit Nadan***, recorded in Israel, eight tracks, singers **Max Perlman and Gita Galina**, conductor **Marcel Kreyzler** (Crysler at JMI, Krysler at Penn); tracks include "Efsher yo un efsher neyn", "Mazl Tov", "Alte Moyd", "Tsu Vos Arbetn". **[A: Freedman P-011(d), track ID 1200; JMI 2519/410, accession LP112]**
- **Makolit, *Hot A Yid A Vaybele***, carrying "Ikh Bin Nebekh An Almone". **[A: Freedman P-011(e), track ID 7111]**
- **No release date exists for either;** both catalogues say so. The 1958 and 1960 attached to these records in the dossier are **invented and dropped**; in the journey file they appear only as declared ordering placeholders. Penn does not record which voice sings which track, so attributing "Alte Moyd" to Galina rests on subject matter, not metadata. **[R]**

## 8. New York (1962-1969)

- **YIVO, Records of the Hebrew Actors' Union**, "Folder 423: Galina, Gita", 1962 and undated. **[A: finding aid]** No contents described. **9 July 1964, *Maariv***: the comic role **Kadeyndl**, an older unmarried woman, "roars of laughter" in her scenes with Perlman; production title not recovered. **[R]** NLI page not loaded.
- **17 October 1964 to 3 January 1965: *Good Luck*, 117 performances, Anderson Yiddish Theatre, 66 Second Avenue, Off-Broadway.** Galina as **Zisel**; with Fyvush Finkel, Jacob Jacobs, Miriam Kressyn, Seymour Rechtzeit, Bruce Adler and Perlman as Yosel Soloveitchik. **[A: Ovrtur 2896593]** The dossier called it Broadway; it was **Off-Broadway**.
- ***Hello Charlie*, Anderson Theater**, with Galina, Perlman, Jacob Jacobs, Miriam Kressyn, Leon Liebgold, Chayele Rosenthal, Seymour Rexite, Susan Walters. **[A: NYU 2451/54834]** **The record carries no date;** the journey file places it 1966 for ordering only.
- **1966 or 1967, Buenos Aires**: Perlman returns seeking a separate younger leading lady for romantic roles, per Lidia Goldberg. **[R: that recollection was not located in the retrieved Fiszman and Skura PDF]** UBA independently records Argentine engagements in 1955, 1966 and 1967 for Perlman. **[A]**
- **1968: *It's Never Too Late for Happiness*, a remake of *The Lithuanian Yankee***, with Leon Liebgold, Jacob Jacobs, Miriam Kressyn, Seymour Rexite, Bruce Adler, Fraydele Oysher, Diane Cypkin and Galina. **[A: NYU 2451/54807, which does carry the year 1968]** **Contradiction 5, unresolved.** NYU files it under the **Anderson Yiddish Theater**; `max_perelman.journey.json` puts it at the **Actors' Playhouse, Seventh Avenue South**. The 19 October 1968 date comes from a Museum of Family History biography of Fraydele Oysher, not fetched. **[R]**
- **1969: *Oh What a Wedding!*, Anderson Yiddish Theater**, music Murray Rumshinsky, lyrics Jacob Jacobs. **[A for the production, via `max_perelman.journey.json`] [R for Galina]** No cast list naming her found. That at sixty-two the ingenue parts had passed to Zahav and "Galina does not leave the company" is that file's statement, not a document's.

## 9. Brazil, and the ground at Holon

- **1973**: NLI Yehuda Gabay Theater Archive, a file on Jewish theatre in Brazil holding an item titled **"Max Perelman com Guita Galina e Menorah Zahav [1973]"**. **[R]** Catalogue page not loaded. **The last securely dated appearance of Gita Galina located anywhere.**
- **Death, 6 January 1983.** **[A: Wikidata Q81939577]** No place of death in any source. DYTP says she "passed away of leukemia" about two and a half years before Perlman's death of 23 July 1985, which computes to roughly January 1983 and **corroborates** the Wikidata date. **Leukemia is single-sourced. [R]**
- **Burial: Holon, southern Tel Aviv cemetery, block 23, plot 2, place 558, "Gita Galina Perlman, daughter of Zalman", Hebrew year 5743.** **[A: Bezikaron, which publishes 31.996624, 34.753914 for the plot]**
- **A non-contradiction, corrected.** The dossier read the grave as contradicting 6 January 1983 by giving "April 5743". Hebrew 5743 ran 18 September 1982 to 7 September 1983, and 6 January 1983 is 21 Tevet 5743, inside it. The record's own URL slug reads "na-5743", na meaning no date; the month shown is a placeholder for an unknown day. **The grave fixes the year and is silent on the day.**
- **Max Perlman is buried beside her**, 23 July 1985. **[A: DYTP]**
- **Afterlife.** **No dedicated biography, documentary or standalone encyclopaedia article exists.** Her afterlife is distributed across her husband's lexicon entry, Argentine exile-musician scholarship, Israeli press archives, cemetery databases and sound catalogues; the fullest standalone modern treatment is one paragraph inside a DYTP essay about Perlman. **[R, a synthesis judgement]** DYTP's "Wikidata, Yiddish Theatre Posters, and the World" proposes Perlman and Galina as a **hypothetical example** for mapping digitised poster metadata **[A]** and attaches no posters, dates or facts to her; the dossier's framing of it as an actual recovery overstates it. Wikidata calls her a stage, film, television and radio actress and singer **[A]**; no film or television credit was found anywhere else.

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Birthplace.** Vitebsk (UBA, Wikidata) versus Riga (DYTP).
2. **South African tour years.** 1948/49, 1951, 1953 (DYTP) versus 1948/49, 1951, 1972 (Belling).
3. **1954 in Israel.** Te'atron Komedya founded (Rojanski) versus a nine-month visit, no company (DYTP).
4. **The one review sentence about her.** *Jewish Chronicle*, 4 February 1955, London (DYTP footnote) versus the 1948 Johannesburg season (`max_perelman.journey.json`).
5. **1968 venue.** Anderson Yiddish Theater (NYU) versus Actors' Playhouse (`max_perelman.journey.json`).
6. **A phantom source.** The 1 April 1907 birth date, three repertory titles, the 1953 "repatriation" and the 6 January 1983 death are attributed in circulation to a note at yiddishmusic.jewniverse.info. **That site carries no such note.**
7. **1955 on two continents.** *Der Amerikanische Littwak*, 26 August 1955, Teatro Soleil (UBA) versus *The American Litvak*, Grand Palais London 1955 (DYTP).

## Honest gaps

- Childhood, education, training, family beyond her father's given name Zalman. Her mother is nowhere.
- How and when she entered the theatre, and when or why she took the name Galina.
- Whether she was in Kovno, Czechoslovakia, Britain, Paris, Belgium, Poland, Uruguay, Chile or Brazil in the 1930s and 1940s at all. She is named only for Vienna, and later for Argentina, South Africa, London, Israel, New York and Brazil 1973.
- Anything whatever between the 1973 Brazil item and her death ten years later. The day of that death, which the grave does not give.
- Any photograph identified and described. Two are attested by date (16 September 1943, Buenos Aires; 5 July 1949, London); neither has been seen.
- Any word she wrote or spoke. There is not one.
