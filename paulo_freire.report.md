# Paulo Freire (1921-1997): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-30 for `paulo_freire.journey.json`. Recife, Jaboatão, Angicos, Olinda, La Paz, Santiago, Cambridge, Geneva, Bissau, Maputo, São Paulo.*

Legend: [A] = attested, source named · [R] = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim not independently confirmed here. Where sources disagree, both readings are printed and neither is adjudicated unless the reasoning is shown. Keys: **IEP** = iep.utm.edu/freire/ · **IPF chronology** = angicos50anos.paulofreire.org/cronologia/ · **dossier** = the gathered research pool.

## 1. Recife and Jaboatão, 1921-1943

- Born Recife, Pernambuco, **19 September 1921**, youngest of four, son of Joaquim Temístocles Freire, officer of the Pernambuco Military Police, and Edeltrudes Neves Freire, called Tudinha. [A: en.wikipedia; IEP]
- Mango-tree literacy games in the family yard, c. 1925-1927. [R] Freire's own recollection relayed by IEP: a memoirist on his own childhood, not a document.
- Eunice Vasconcelos's private school, 1927. [R] In the dossier's chronology, consistent with IEP; no register, address or independent record located.
- Move to Jaboatão dos Guararapes after the 1929-30 crash, c. 1931. [A: IEP] The hunger and missed schooling of these years are his own repeated account: attested autobiography, not external documentation.
- Death of the father, Jaboatão, **31 October 1934**. [A: en.wikipedia] The pool tagged this [R] in its geography lens and [A] in its chronology lens; the [A] reading is correct and has been promoted.
- Colégio Oswaldo Cruz, Recife, **1937-1942**; director Aluízio Pessoa de Araújo arranged his attendance despite the family's poverty. Begins teaching Portuguese in the same school, **1942**. [A: IEP; pt.wikipedia]

> **Correction carried:** the school is **Oswaldo** Cruz, with a w, as at IEP and throughout the Portuguese sources; the pool had "Osvaldo" twice. pt.wikipedia has no article on it (404).

## 2. The Recife years, 1943-1962

- Law faculty, University of Recife, **1943**; philosophy and the psychology of language read alongside; never practised law as a career. [A: en.wikipedia; pt.wikipedia; IEP] Maritain and Mounier, Catholic personalism, named as formative. [R: IEP via the dossier] Neither is an atlas subject.
- Marriage to **Elza Maia Costa de Oliveira**, **1944**, elementary-school teacher, five children, collaborator in the literacy work. [A: en.wikipedia; IEP]
- Thesis *Educação e atualidade brasileira*, submitted for the chair in History and Philosophy of Education, **1959**. [R: Instituto Paulo Freire tradition via the dossier] Supplies material for *Educação como prática da liberdade*; folded into the Cultural Extension stop.
- Movimento de Cultura Popular, Recife, **1960**, under mayor Miguel Arraes. The pool's two lenses disagree on grade, [A] in geography and [R] in chronology. Its existence and Freire's participation are not in doubt; the date and his exact role are.
- Director, Service of Cultural Extension, University of Recife, **1961**. [A: en.wikipedia; IEP]
- Poço da Panela, pre-Angicos circle experiments, **1962**. [R: dossier synthesis only]

**Contradiction, not resolved: the mid-1940s post.** en.wikipedia gives *director of the Pernambuco Department of Education and Culture, 1946*; the dossier and IEP give *joins SESI, Pernambuco, 1947*, later directing its Division of Education and Culture [A: IEP]. Not the same claim, and nothing reachable reconciles them. The file carries SESI at 1947 and states the 1946 alternative in `date_confidence`.

**Contradiction, not resolved: the 1962 figure.** A "roughly 300 sugarcane workers literate in about 45 days" claim for 1962 sits in the Britannica-derived timeline inside en.wikipedia's Freire article. It is not the 1963 Angicos "forty hours". Sources vary on episode, duration and year. Both printed, neither picked.

## 3. Angicos, 1962-1964

- Planning meetings, **Rua Alfredo Coutinho, 79, Recife**, September 1962. [A: IPF chronology] Street and number attested, not geocoded.
- **18 January 1963**, opening session at Angicos, **380 participants** enrolled. [A: IPF chronology; corroborated by pt.wikipedia "Angicos", which also gives 380]
- Instruction begins **28 January 1963**; first generative word **belota**. [A: IPF chronology]
- **2 April 1963**, closing: **about 300 judged literate, 70% achievement** on the tests. President **João Goulart** attends with Governor **Aluízio Alves**. [A: IPF chronology; the pool's chronology lens had tagged Goulart's attendance [R], its geography lens [A]; the chronology settles it]
- Decree No. 53,465 institutes the **Programa Nacional de Alfabetização** with Freire coordinating, **21 January 1964**. [R: the dossier's summary of the Brazilian legal record; the text was not retrieved] Its projected scale, tens of thousands of circles and millions of adults, is a dossier figure and appears in the campa only in general terms.

> **Correction carried:** the pool said "roughly 300 rural workers gather", conflating enrolment with certification. 380 enrolled, ~300 certified. "Forty hours" is instructional time, not elapsed: the course ran eleven weeks.

## 4. The coup, the prison, the border, 1964

- The coup, 31 March to 1 April 1964. [A: en.wikipedia]
- Abolition of the National Literacy Program, **14 April 1964**. [A: IPF chronology]
- Arrest, Recife, **16 June 1964**, charged in substance with subversion. [A: IEP; IPF chronology]
- Imprisonment, army facility in the Olinda and Recife area, **about 70 days**. [A: en.wikipedia, Britannica-derived timeline, "Imprisoned 70 days as traitor following 1964 coup"] Other accounts give **72** or **about 75**: not resolved, and the campa prints all three. The anecdote of the officer who asked the prisoner to teach recruits to read is [R], recollection not transcript, marked as tradition in `date_confidence`.
- Bolivia, September 1964: asylum in La Paz, the altitude against him, President Víctor Paz Estenssoro overthrown that November, ending the refuge. [A: en.wikipedia; IPF chronology]

> **Correction carried:** the pool treated coup and abolition as one event dated 1964-04-01. They are two events fourteen days apart, and the interval is worth preserving, so they are two stops.

## 5. Chile, 1964-1969

- Arrival Santiago **November 1964**, departure **April 1969**. [A: freire.org short biography, explicit on both ends; corroborated by en.wikipedia]
- **Jacques Chonchol**, vice-president of INDAP, recruits him. [A: IEP; IPF chronology]
- **ICIRA**, the joint Chilean-FAO agrarian-reform institute, is his base 1965-1969. [A: IEP]
- *Educação como prática da liberdade*, **1967**. [A: en.wikipedia]
- *Pedagogia do oprimido* drafted **1967-1968, in Chile**. [A]

> **Contradiction resolved, reasoning shown.** freire.org says the book was composed "between 1967 and 1968, while living in the United States", and en.wikipedia echoes it. pt.wikipedia "Pedagogia do Oprimido" states plainly that he composed it during the Chilean exile. Freire did not reach the United States until April 1969 (IEP), after composition, so the United States reading is an error and is not carried.

**Contradiction, not resolved: publication.** First Spanish edition, Tierra Nueva, Montevideo, **1970** [R: dossier bibliography, not checked against a catalogue], against freire.org and en.wikipedia's *Pedagogy of the Oppressed*, both giving first Spanish publication as **1968**. English translation, Myra Bergman Ramos, **1970** [A: en.wikipedia]. Portuguese original, Portugal **1972**, Brazil **1974** [A: freire.org, corroborated by the Britannica-derived timeline]. **No Montevideo stop was written**: Freire did not travel there, and a publisher's act is not his journey.

## 6. Cambridge and Geneva, 1969-1979

- Harvard, visiting scholar, Center for Studies in Education and Development / Graduate School of Education, **April 1969 to February 1970**. [A: IEP for the span; en.wikipedia places it at 1969; the dossier gives 1969-70] Richard Shaull wrote the introduction to the English edition. [A: dossier, consistent with the printed book]
- World Council of Churches, Geneva, special consultant to the Office of Education, **February 1970 to 1980**. [A: IEP for the ten-year tenure; the February start is [R], on a WCC archival record the dossier cites and nobody re-fetched]
- **IDAC**, the Institute for Cultural Action, founded in Geneva with Rosiska Darcy de Oliveira, Miguel Darcy de Oliveira and Claudius Ceccon. [A: dossier synthesis of its founding and mission] Its stated founding worry was that Freire's practice would harden into a portable technique.
- Guinea-Bissau, **1975-1976**: advises the post-independence literacy campaign, corresponds with commissioner Mário Cabral, works in the shadow of Amílcar Cabral's anti-colonial theory. [A: dossier synthesis of *Cartas à Guiné-Bissau*, 1977, and its reception] Outcomes criticized by Linda Harasim and others for weak gains and reliance on Portuguese in a multilingual population; Freire acknowledged errors, naming the language question first.
- Mozambique, 1970s-1978. [A: dossier synthesis of the African literacy-project critiques] **Cape Verde, São Tomé and Príncipe, Angola, Tanzania**: country-level engagement only, [R] in every case, no visit, venue or date located for any. Named inside the Mozambique campa, given no stops: a stop implies a place he stood on a date.
- First return to Brazil, **1979**, amid the *abertura*; "relearning Brazil" is his own phrase. [A: en.wikipedia] The month, August, is [R] from the geography lens.

## 7. São Paulo, 1980-1997

- Permanent return, São Paulo, **1980**; joins the Partido dos Trabalhadores; supervises an adult literacy project. [A: en.wikipedia; IEP] King Baudouin International Development Prize, **1980**. [A: en.wikipedia]
- PUC-SP (Perdizes) and UNICAMP (Campinas), teaching through the 1980s. [A: IEP; pt.wikipedia] Both give only "1980s"; UNICAMP is folded into the PUC-SP stop.
- Death of Elza Freire and the UNESCO Prize for Peace Education, both **1986**. [A: en.wikipedia; IEP]
- Marriage to **Ana Maria Araújo Freire (Nita)**, **1988**, former pupil at Colégio Oswaldo Cruz, later his biographer and editor. [A: en.wikipedia; pt.wikipedia; IEP]
- Municipal Secretary of Education of São Paulo under mayor **Luiza Erundina**, **1 January 1989**; school councils, curriculum on generative themes, MOVA-SP. [A: IEP; pt.wikipedia]
- Resignation, **May 1991**, succeeded by Mário Sérgio Cortella. [R: dossier, not checked against a municipal record] Nothing consulted supports dismissal for cause.
- Instituto Paulo Freire founded, São Paulo, **1991**. [R: dossier; the institute's founding record was not retrieved]
- *Pedagogia da esperança*, 1992 [R]; honorary doctorates, Claremont 1992 and Illinois at Chicago 1993. [A: en.wikipedia]
- *Pedagogia da autonomia*, **1996**. [R] The fotobiografia cites the quotations to 1997; the wobble is a first-edition versus printing question, unresolved.
- **Omaha**, Second Pedagogy of the Oppressed Conference, **March 1996**: Freire and **Augusto Boal** share a public stage, reportedly for the first time, before about a thousand people. [A: ptoweb.org, "A Brief Biography of Augusto Boal"] University of Nebraska at Omaha honorary doctorate for Freire, 1996. [A: en.wikipedia]
- Death, heart attack, São Paulo hospital, **2 May 1997**, aged 75. [A: en.wikipedia] memorial.paulofreire.org names the hospital as Israelita Albert Einstein; the Associated Press obituary said only "a São Paulo hospital", so the naming is [R].
- Burial, **3 May 1997 at 12:55**, Cemitério da Paz, Rua Dr. Luiz Migliano 644. [A: pt.wikipedia, "O seu corpo foi enterrado no dia 3 de maio, sábado, às 12h55, no Cemitério da Paz, no Morumbi (zona sul de São Paulo)"] The municipal address places it in Jardim Vazani, Morumbi being the informal regional name; both appear in the campa.

> **Contradiction adjudicated, reasoning shown.** en.wikipedia's Britannica-derived line gives the Secretariat appointment as 1988; the dossier gives 1 January 1989. Erundina's term began January 1989 under the Brazilian municipal calendar: 1988 is the election, 1989 the term. The 1989 reading is carried, the Wikipedia line flagged as weaker.

> **Flagged, not carried as fact:** the dossier also claimed a *joint* honorary doctorate for Boal at Omaha; ptoweb.org's biography of Boal mentions no honorary degree. The campa credits the degree to Freire only and states the unconfirmed joint claim in `date_confidence`.

## 8. After

- Justice Ministry apology and reparation for the 1964 imprisonment, 2009 World Forum on Professional Education. [A: pt.wikipedia]
- Federal Law No. 12,612, **13 April 2012**, "Patrono da Educação Brasileira". [A: pt.wikipedia; the statute text was not fetched]
- Google Doodle, 19 September 2021 [A: doodles.google]; Correios stamps, 19 September 2024, and commonest name given to public schools across São Paulo, Minas Gerais and Paraná [A: pt.wikipedia].
- *Pedagogy of the Oppressed*: 20 languages, over 750,000 copies by 2000, called the third most cited book in the social sciences. [A: en.wikipedia]
- International Adult and Continuing Education Hall of Fame, 2008. [R: one line on en.wikipedia, no corroboration] No stop.
- Monument in Brasília, and mural at the Universidad del Bío-Bío, Chillán: each asserted by a pt.wikipedia photo caption only, no sculptor, date or building. [R, both] Neither given a stop. The pool's mural coordinate was Chillán's city centre; the faculty is at Campus La Castilla, roughly -36.615, -72.114 [es.wikipedia], 1.5 km away.

> **Two corrections carried, both fixing errors originating in the Wikipedia source itself.** "30th-anniversary edition, 2018" is arithmetically impossible: first publication is 1968/1970, so the 2018 Bloomsbury release is the **50th**-anniversary edition, the 30th being Continuum, 2000, Donaldo Macedo having introduced both. And the Open Syllabus finding is that in 2016 the book was the only Brazilian-authored title among the 100 most-assigned texts in **English-speaking universities**, not a Brazilian-authors list that also reached an international top 100.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Paulo Freire is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. One relation was found that is not rooted in his own life. Per the standing rule it has not been deleted; it is flagged here.

**Direction caught: backward, a later actor onto Freire.** From the 2010s, intensifying through **Jair Bolsonaro's** rise and presidency (2018-2022), Freire's statutory title as Patron of Brazilian Education became a culture-war target, with circulating calls to strip it and a supposed nationwide "Freirean method" blamed for the failures of Brazilian schooling. `jair_bolsonaro.journey.json` covers 2018-2022 in detail and **no stop in it names Freire or the patron-title controversy**.

Two problems, kept separate. **Attestation:** no independent primary source was located confirming that Bolsonaro personally made a specific anti-Freire statement on a specific date, and no bill number, sponsor or vote was pinned down; searches and fetches returned nothing or 404. The controversy as a public phenomenon is documented; the person-level link is not. **Structure:** even if attested, this is a later figure engaging a dead one, a real one-directional relation, citable on Freire's own stop, exactly the shape the standing rule permits, and no reason to write anything into Bolsonaro's file. Nothing has been.

**What was done:** the journey file carries a stop for the contest over the title, at the Brasília pin, describing the campaign without naming Bolsonaro, because attestation at person level is missing, not because the relation is illegitimate. If the operator wants him named, the campa needs one sentence and a source.

Nothing forward was found: no stop applies Freire's framework to material he could not have discussed, and no reference to Freire has been proposed for any earlier subject's file.

## Honest gaps

- Birth house, Eunice Vasconcelos's school, the SESI office, the Angicos schoolhouse, the Secretariat building and Rua Alfredo Coutinho: all city-pin or approximate. Street precision was not obtainable for any.
- Decree 53,465 was never read, only summarized; its signing location is inferred, so no Palácio do Planalto stop was written.
- The English wording of "Liberation is thus a childbirth, and a painful one" rests on Wikiquote; every English full-text mirror attempted returned 401, 403 or 404. The Portuguese is primary-verified in the dhnet.org.br PDF of *Pedagogia do oprimido*, ch. 1: "A libertação, por isto, é um parto. E um parto doloroso." **The journey file quotes the Portuguese**, removing the dependency.
- "Ninguém liberta ninguém, ninguém se liberta sozinho" was tagged [R] in the pool as merely widely attributed. It is [A]: Freire's own section heading in *Pedagogia do oprimido*, ch. 1, p. 29 of the Paz e Terra edition, confirmed from the table of contents in the primary dhnet.org.br PDF.
- Cape Verde, São Tomé, Angola and Tanzania have no visit, venue or date; sales, translation and citation figures for the book rest on a single Wikipedia paragraph.
