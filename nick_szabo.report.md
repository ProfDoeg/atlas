# Nick Szabo: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Nick Szabo, b. 5 April 1964; computer scientist and legal scholar; author of the smart-contract formulation (1994-1997) and of bit gold (designed 1998, published December 2005); perennial and always-denied candidate for the identity of Satoshi Nakamoto.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same sentence · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or anything verification could not re-confirm. Contradictions are flagged where left open and marked **RESOLVED** only where verification actually settled them. Gaps are stated as gaps. Verification overturned the gathering pass in both directions, and both kinds of error are recorded below rather than quietly fixed.

---

## 1. Origins, 1964-1989

**Birth: 5 April 1964 [A, Wikipedia, 'Nick Szabo']. Birthplace: GAP.** Wikipedia gives the date and no place; no source reached names a city, a state, a hospital, or a parent. The only further biographical detail anywhere is Nathaniel Popper's phrase, "a reclusive American man of Hungarian descent" **[A, Popper, NYT, 15 May 2015]**. The atlas cannot say where he was born.

**Coordinate contradiction, internal to the gathering pass: RESOLVED.** Two lenses gave two inconsistent pins for the same 1989 degree: geography 47.6565, -122.3096 (near the Quad, about 450 m off), afterlife 47.6553, -122.3035 (near the HUB, about 380 m off). ## 2. Amsterdam and DigiCash, the 1990s

**Szabo worked for David Chaum's DigiCash on the Ecash project, in Amsterdam, during the 1990s [R].** The standard account in cryptocurrency history, and the ordinary explanation of how he came to digital-cash design. Tagged **[R]** for an honest reason: verification introduced it as a correction and instructed that it be "sourced to a direct read of a DigiCash history rather than to the aggregator pages found here," and no such read was performed. **No start or end year is documented anywhere reached**; the journey's 1990 and 1992 pins are placement defaults inside "during the 1990s."

**What this replaced [flagged].** The gathering pass carried "First job in software after University of Washington, Seattle area (unlocated specific employer)" at 47.6062, -122.3321, conceding in its own note that this was "a structural inference... not a sourced fact." Struck.

**Consequence for the 1994 and 1996 essays [flagged, not resolved].** The gathering pass pinned both in Seattle and tagged them **[A]** while conceding the location was "reconstructed from his general Pacific Northwest base." Verification rejected both: nothing places Szabo in Seattle in the 1990s. The journey pins them in Amsterdam and marks the placement **[R]**. **This is one plausible location substituted for another, not a discovery.**

**Earliest essay in the archive: 1993 [A, Satoshi Nakamoto Institute author page, dating the collection 1993 to 2017].** Earliest title named: "Multinational Small Business."

**Cypherpunks mailing list [R].** His presence there alongside Hal Finney, Wei Dai, and Timothy May is asserted by the interlock survey and universally repeated, but no subscriber list or archived post was reached.

**Quote attribution errors [RESOLVED].** Two quotes arrived attributed to the 1996 Extropy essay and belong to the 1994 one:

- "A smart contract is a computerized transaction protocol that executes the terms of a contract." The opening sentence of **"Smart Contracts" (1994)**, which is what nakamotoinstitute.org/library/smart-contracts/ actually reprints. The 1996 essay defines the term differently ("a set of promises, specified in digital form, including protocols within which the parties perform on these promises").
- "There is ample opportunity in smart contracts for 'smart fine print': actions taken by the software hidden from a party to the transaction." Also **1994**. Its next sentence, dropped in the pool: "For example, grocery store POS machines don't tell customers whether or not their names are being linked to their purchases in a database."

The vending-machine quote is sourced to the szabo.best.vwh.net mirror of the 1996 essay and stays with the 1996 stop **[A]**.

**Extropy Institute attendance: NOT attested [R, flagged].** Wikipedia says Szabo "was associated with extropian life-extension concepts" in the 1990s; it does not say he attended any Extro conference. The gathering pass produced a Sunnyvale coordinate (37.3688, -122.0363) and flagged it "for verification or drop"; it was not verified. The journey keeps that pin explicitly as the journal's institutional home. Likewise First Monday's Chicago pin stands for the University of Illinois at Chicago library, which publishes the journal, not for a place Szabo visited.

**bit gold, 1998 [A].** Design date and the gold quotation attested by Wikipedia, 'Bit gold'. The writeup does not appear until December 2005. Wei Dai's b-money, the parallel 1998 proposal, is **not yet an atlas traveler**.

## 4. The bit gold post: December 2005, a contradiction that was resolved

The one place where the gathering pass reported an open contradiction and verification closed it. Wikipedia and the secondary press date the Unenumerated post "Bit gold" to December 2005, but a direct WebFetch of unenumerated.blogspot.com/2005/12/bit-gold.html returned a page timestamp of **27 December 2008**, and the afterlife lens proposed carrying this into the journey unreconciled.

**RESOLVED in favor of December 2005 [A]**, on three independent pieces of evidence:

1. The Satoshi Nakamoto Institute dates the essay **29 December 2005**, citing the same URL.
2. The Blogger permalink path is **/2005/12/bit-gold.html**, and Blogger assigns paths at first publish.
3. The Internet Archive holds a capture of that URL from **29 March 2006** (web.archive.org/web/20060329122942/), impossible if the post were created in 2008.

The live "27 December 2008" is a later timestamp bump by Szabo, which is also why the post no longer appears in Blogger's December 2005 archive index. **The journey states December 2005 as fact and carries the bump inside date_confidence, not as an open question.** The gathering pass's day, "27," was read off the 2008 bump; the journey uses 29 December 2005 per the Nakamoto Institute.

**Content of the post [A, Nakamoto Institute reprint].** The proof-of-work chain; the trusted-third-party framing; the credit to **Hal Finney's RPOW** as an implemented variant; and the defect Szabo names himself, that pieces minted in different eras cost different amounts of computer time and are not fungible. **RPOW's 2004 date [R]** comes from the interlock survey and Szabo's own credit, not independently re-verified.

**Quote correction, the 2011 post [RESOLVED].** The pool carried "...liked the idea [of bit gold / digital scarcity] enough to pursue it to any real degree until Nakamoto." Not what he wrote. The actual sentence: *"Myself, Wei Dai, and Hal Finney were the only people I know of who liked the idea (or in Dai's case his related idea) enough to pursue it to any significant extent until Nakamoto (assuming Nakamoto is not really Finney or Dai)."* The misquote swapped "significant extent" for "real degree" and deleted both parentheses, including the hedge treating Nakamoto as possibly Finney or Dai. The journey quotes the corrected sentence and attributes it to the Unenumerated post, not to Wikipedia.

**Source correction, the stylometry [date RESOLVED, source corrected].** December 2013 is corroborated across Wikipedia mirrors carrying "In December 2013, blogger Skye Grey linked Nick Szabo to the bitcoin white paper using stylometric analysis." But **the live Wikipedia article contains no such sentence**; it sits in an older revision, and the gathering pass cited the live article for phrasing it does not contain. The journey cites the mirrors and Grey's own posts. Day of month: gap.

**Date correction, the New York Times [RESOLVED].** The gathering pass gave 17 May 2015. The Wikipedia citation reads: Popper, Nathaniel, 15 May 2015, "Decoding the Enigma of Satoshi Nakamoto and the Birth of Bitcoin." The /2015/05/17/ in the URL is the print edition date, and the gathering pass also omitted the title entirely. Both fixed.

**Citation supplied, "direct precursor" [RESOLVED].** The gathering pass dated this loosely to "the period of heaviest Satoshi-identity press coverage" with no citation. Wikipedia attributes it to Martin O'Leary, Huffington Post, 8 May 2015.

**The identity question: open, and recorded as open [R].** No document, signature, or on-chain proof has ever confirmed or disproved it, and none of the evidence advanced (stylometry, timing, the resemblance to bit gold) is of a kind that could. The atlas states the accusations as attested events and the identification as unresolved.

## 7. Later work and honors

**Bloq, Chicago: board of advisors, 3 May 2016 [A, upgraded from [R]].** The gathering pass tagged this a reconstruction it "could not re-confirm today." Verification found the primary announcement: bloq.com's post of 3 May 2016 names Nick Szabo, "Cryptographer, Digital Currency Researcher and Smart Contracts Pioneer," on its board of advisors, and CCN's launch coverage independently lists him with Andrew Filipowski and Gavin Andresen. Founders Jeff Garzik and Matthew Roszak **[A]**. **Coordinate corrected:** the gathering pass used the Chicago Loop centroid 41.8781, -87.6298; Bloq was at the Merchandise Mart, about 41.8885, -87.6354, which the journey uses.

**"Money, Blockchains, and Social Scalability," 2017 [A as to existence and year-span placement].** Named on the Nakamoto Institute author page among the late essays inside the 1993-2017 span. Exact date: gap. Whether it is literally the last item is an inference from that span, so the journey says "the far end of the span the archive keeps," not "the last."

**Universidad Francisco Marroquín honorary professorship [A as to fact, GAP as to year].** Wikipedia states it; no year anywhere reached, and no source says whether the honor involved travel to Guatemala. Founder and founding date are right: Manuel Ayau, 1971 **[A]**.

**Coordinate contradiction, internal to the gathering pass: RESOLVED.** Geography gave 14.5891, -90.493 and afterlife 14.5951, -90.4859 for the same campus; both land southeast of it and disagree with each other by roughly 1 km. English and Spanish Wikipedia both give 14.6051, -90.5062; OpenStreetMap returns 14.6062, -90.5058 for the Zona 10 campus. The journey uses about **14.6055, -90.5060**.

**"Trust Machine: The Story of Blockchain" (2018) [R, flagged].** His participation is reported in general accounts and was **not** independently verified; the filming location for his segment is unknown and may not be a single place. Carried into the journey with the flag intact, since the film is one of the few non-textual records of him.

**The Nakamoto Institute archive [A].** Forty-seven essays, 1993 to 2017, reprinted without interpretive commentary. **Current residence: GAP**; no source reached states where he lives, and secondary characterizations placing him in Washington state cite nothing.

## 8. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Named in campa, pin inherited byte-identical where the atlas already had one:

- **ralph_merkle**: four crossings. (i) The 1989 degree stop dates itself against Merkle's arrival at Xerox PARC and his Alcor membership agreement. (ii) The 1999 patent-lapse stop inherits the canonical Washington patent-office pin **38.9186, -77.0367** and the atlas's framing of the nine-year gap between the expiry of US 4,309,569 and the Bitcoin white paper, naming bit gold and b-money as what falls into it. (iii) The 2008 white-paper stop inherits **37.4419, -122.143** exactly, with the Merkle file's caveat that the pin stands for where Merkle lives. (iv) The Alcor pin **33.6174, -111.911** is inherited for the RPOW stop and stands for Hal Finney.
- **diffie** and **hellman**: the Stanford Infobus stop inherits the canonical Stanford pin **37.4275, -122.1697** and names New Directions in Cryptography (Nov 1976) as the substrate his essays assume, on the campus now borrowing his vocabulary twenty-two years later.
- **rivest_shamir_adleman**: the blind-signature stop names them publishing from 545 Technology Square in 1978, since Chaum's blind signature is built on RSA. The atlas's pin for that address is **42.3626, -71.0913**; the interlock lens reported -71.0942, which does not match the file. The file value is authoritative.
- **fermi** and **niels_bohr**: the law-school stop inherits the canonical GWU pin **38.9007, -77.047** from `fermi.journey.json` and names the fifth Washington Conference on Theoretical Physics of January 1939, where Fermi and Bohr heard that a uranium nucleus had been split in Berlin.

Not used, and why:

- **hal_finney**: named repeatedly in campa (RPOW, the 2011 quote, the Nakamoto Institute shelf) but **not yet an atlas traveler**; QUEUE.md holds him beside Szabo. Not counted as an interlock slug.
- **Fort George G. Meade**: proposed as the shadow over open cryptography. The lens reported 39.1082, **-76.7419** while `diffie.journey.json` carries 39.1082, **-76.7719**. No Szabo event attaches to the pin, and inheriting a miscopied coordinate would propagate an error, so it was not used.
- **Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris, Buenos Aires**: checked against every cipher-wing file; no intersection. An absence, no pin assigned.
- **Ladislas Szabo** (census row 249, the Hungarian-Argentine journalist behind the 1947 Hitler-in-Antarctica claim): same surname, **no documented family or biographical relation whatsoever**. Flagged by the interlock lens to prevent a false positive, and deliberately absent from the journey file.

## 9. Standing gaps

**Birthplace**, and family background beyond one adjective in a New York Times article. **The years of the DigiCash employment**, and any primary document naming him there. **Where he was physically located** for essentially every essay from 1994 to 2017: the journey uses two placeholder pins (Amsterdam for the DigiCash years, 47.6062/-122.3321 for the undocumented American years) and hedges every one. **The JD year.** **The Universidad Francisco Marroquín year**, and whether he traveled to Guatemala. **Day of month** for the Skye Grey analysis, the 2009 blog post, and the 2011 post. **Current residence.** And **the identity question**, less a gap in the sources than a hole in the world: no signature exists on either side of it.
