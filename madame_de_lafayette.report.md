# Madame de Lafayette (Adrienne de Noailles, 1759-1807): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, working file. Compiled 2026-08. Slug: `madame_de_lafayette`. Paired in QUEUE.md with the consecutive row `Marqués de Lafayette` (lines 110-111), whose journey file does not yet exist.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim whose only carrier is an unsourced text. Contradictions are flagged and left open unless one source overrules another, and then the overruling is stated.

**A naming warning that shapes every catalogue search.** "Madame de Lafayette" in a library record usually means Marie-Madeleine Pioche de La Vergne (1634-1693), author of *La Princesse de Clèves*. A different woman, two generations earlier, and **not** unrelated: she took the name by marrying François Motier, comte de La Fayette, of the same house from which Gilbert du Motier descended **[R: inferred from the two married names; no VIAF or BnF authority record queried]**. Unrelated by blood, related by marriage into one family, which is why the conflation survives; the draft's flat "unrelated" was wrong.

## 1. The Hôtel de Noailles (1759-1774)

Marie Adrienne Françoise de Noailles is born **2 November 1759**, second daughter of Jean-Louis-Paul-François de Noailles, later Duc d'Ayen, and Henriette-Anne-Louise d'Aguesseau **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia, 'Adrienne de La Fayette']**. Her mother was born 12 February 1737 and married on 25 February 1755 **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Henriette Anne Louise d'Aguesseau']**; five daughters and two sons followed, among them Anne Jeanne Baptiste Louise, who will die beside her mother on the scaffold.

**The site.** The hôtel occupied what are now nos. 219-223 rue Saint-Honoré and was demolished in 1830 **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Hôtel de Noailles']**. The draft placed it 200 m west, past the rue de Castiglione. Corrected to **48.8658, 2.3300** (Nominatim: 221 rue Saint-Honoré = 48.86583, 2.33000).

**Marriage, 11 April 1774**, in the chapel of the hôtel **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Adrienne is fourteen and a half. **Presentation at Versailles** is biographical convention for her rank; no date confirmed **[R]**.

> **Contradiction, resolved against the source.** The French article calls Lafayette "dix-sept ans". He was born 6 September 1757 **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Gilbert du Motier de La Fayette']**, making him sixteen years seven months, not the seventeen of the French article, and settling the split between this dossier's own lenses.

## 2. The husband across the ocean (1775-1783)

- **Henriette, born 15 December 1775, died 3 October 1777 at twenty-two months** **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Adrienne de La Fayette', infobox]**.
  > **Contradiction, resolved.** French Wikipedia gives 1776-1778 for the same child; both pairings span the attested twenty-two months, but only the English dates are corroborated elsewhere. Adopted 1775-1777.
- Lafayette landed in South Carolina in June 1777 and did not return until February 1779 **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Marquis de Lafayette']**, so he is absent at Henriette's death on either dating.
- **Anastasie Louise Pauline, born 1777** **[A]**. The precise day (1 July, in one lens of this dossier) is **[R]**.
- **Lafayette's return, 15 February 1779**; Marie-Antoinette calls at the Hôtel de Noailles in person **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Georges Washington Louis Gilbert du Motier, born 24 December 1779** **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Georges Washington de La Fayette']**. Birth address unconfirmed.
- **Marie-Antoinette-Virginie, born 17 September 1782, died 23 July 1849** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
  > **Two corrections.** The draft dated the birth 24 September; the source gives the 17th. It also credited the name to Thomas Jefferson, who did not reach France until August 1784: **cut**, the name commemorates the state of Virginia.
- **Château de Chavaniac** as her residence in the American years, under Lafayette's aunt Madame de Chavaniac, is everywhere in Lafayette biography and confirmed by nothing fetched **[R]**. Corrected to **45.1572, 3.5792** (fr.wikipedia prints 45°09'26"N 3°34'45"E); the earlier 45.1719, 3.6167 sat 3.3 km out in open country.

## 3. Rue de Bourbon and the Revolution (1783-1792)

**1783: the family moves to the Hôtel de La Fayette on the rue de Bourbon, now rue de Lille, almost at the corner of the rue de Bourgogne** **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Adrienne de La Fayette' and 'Rue de Lille (Paris)', which puts the residence at no. 81]**. Corrected to **48.8606, 2.3186**; the earlier 48.8589, 2.3231 fell mid-street, 400 m southeast.

> **Embellishment cut.** The draft had her seeing her childhood home across the Seine from these windows and visiting it daily. Not in the French article, carried by nothing else.

**Fête de la Fédération, Champ de Mars, 14 July 1790.** Her attendance while her husband administers the oath is plausible and unconfirmed **[R]**.

**August 1792.** Lafayette flees, is taken by Austrian troops at the frontier of the Austrian Netherlands on 19 August, and begins more than five years at Wesel, then Magdeburg, then Olmütz **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Marquis de Lafayette']**. Georges goes into hiding in September **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Georges Washington de La Fayette']**.

## 4. The Auvergne years and the arrest (1792-1794)

The weakest-sourced stretch of the life, and the one the draft overstated worst.

- **Withdrawal to Chavaniac in 1792** was tagged [A] against "Wikipedia (en/fr), 'Adrienne de La Fayette'". The raw wikitext of both was fetched: **neither mentions Chavaniac, 1792, or the Auvergne at all.** Downgraded to **[R]**.
- **House arrest under the Law of Suspects, dated "1792-1793"** is anachronistic: the law was decreed 17 September 1793. Either the range narrows to 1793 or the citation drops. Kept **[R]**, narrowed.
- **Brioude**, then the departmental prison at **Le Puy-en-Velay**, are tradition only **[R]**; both pins are town centres (45.2989, 3.3831 and 45.0433, 3.885), not buildings.
- **Which Paris prison held her** is an open gap. Popular accounts name the Plessis, others La Force, and **no source fetched names any prison.** The journey uses a Paris pin and says so in `date_confidence`.

## 5. Thermidor, Picpus, and the Monroes (1794-1795)

**22 July 1794**, five days before 9 Thermidor, not four as the draft had it. Her mother the Duchesse d'Ayen, her grandmother Catherine de Cossé-Brissac, and her sister the Vicomtesse de Noailles are guillotined together and thrown into the common pit at Picpus **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Henriette Anne Louise d'Aguesseau' and 'Picpus Cemetery']**. Her mother had been arrested at the Hôtel de Noailles in May 1794 and held in the Luxembourg **[A]**.

The pit holds 1,306 people guillotined between 14 June and 27 July 1794 **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Picpus Cemetery']**. The scaffold stood at the Place du Trône-Renversé, now Place de la Nation (48.8483, 2.3958, confirmed).

**The Monroe intervention.** Elizabeth Monroe, wife of the American Minister, visits Adrienne in prison and announces publicly that she will return next day, after which the verdict is reversed.

> **The most-repeated story about Adrienne is the worst-sourced item in the dossier.** Its only carrier is the section "Imprisonment and planned execution" of en.wikipedia, 'Adrienne de La Fayette', **which the article itself flags Unreferenced** (verified in raw wikitext). It names no prison and cites nothing. Tagged **[R]**, attributed to Wikipedia and not to the record. The dating is unstable too, 1795 in one lens and 1794 in the other, and a scheduled execution in 1795 is hard to sustain when Robespierre fell 27 July 1794. Standard biography puts the visit in the winter of 1794-95 and the release on **22 January 1795**, which the journey follows, flagged.

**April 1795:** Georges is sent to the United States with his tutor Félix Frestel **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Georges Washington de La Fayette']**. Le Havre as the port, and Adrienne at the quay, are **[R]**. In 1796 he lives as George Washington's guest in Philadelphia and at Mount Vernon **[A]**, returning in 1798 **[A]**.

## 6. Olmütz: the voluntary captivity (1795-1797)

**The Vienna audience.** Tradition has her travelling to Vienna on an American passport got through Monroe and obtaining an audience with Francis II. The draft tagged this **[A]** against en.wikipedia, 'Adrienne de La Fayette', whose full raw wikitext contains **no mention of Vienna, Francis II, a passport, or Monroe securing one.** Downgraded to **[R]**; the Hofburg is the draft's own admitted assumption. Her letter of 6 February 1796 does say she wrote after "leaving Your Majesty's presence", so the audience is corroborated by the letter and the address is not.

**15 October 1795:** she arrives at Olmütz with Anastasie and Virginie and enters the captivity voluntarily **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Georges Washington de La Fayette']**.

**The letters** are the only place Adrienne speaks in her own voice. All are printed in M. MacDermot Crawford, *Madame de Lafayette and Her Family* (1907), read in the Internet Archive scan `madamedelafayett00craw`. **Folios sit at the foot of each page**, which produced a systematic off-by-one in the draft.

| Text | Verified (draft's page in brackets) |
|---|---|
| To Emperor Francis II, Olmütz, 6 Feb 1796 | **p. 282** [281] |
| To the Commander of Olmütz, 4 Apr 1796 | p. 282, unchallenged |
| To Dr Justus Erich Bollmann, 22 May 1796 | p. 333, unchallenged |
| "La femme Lafayette" | **pp. 2 and 202**, also 246, 330, 331; **nothing on p. 61** [61 and 202] |
| "Je suis toute a vous . . ." | **p. 318, in Crawford's own narrative, not the letter** [317] |
| "What a blessing! how happy I am to be yours!" | **p. 328**, in the letter to La Tour Maubourg [318] |
| Comte de Ségur on Adrienne | **Preface, p. vii** [p. 4, opening chapter] |
| "Let us adore the will of God..." | **p. 106** [107] |

Two further corrections. "I am wholly yours" as Crawford's rendering of "Je suis toute a vous" is invented: **that string occurs nowhere in the book**, so any English version here is the atlas's own. And p. 328 does resolve the order the draft called unresolved, the spoken exclamation first, then the pressure of the hand "which seemed still to mean" the French phrase. Adrienne's *Life of the Duchesse d'Ayen* is her own first-person memoir of her mother, written as a prisoner at Olmütz **[A]**; in the 1872 London edition Louis de Lasteyrie is the translator and Virginie de Lasteyrie the author of the companion Life, which the draft's "Louis (Mme) de Lasteyrie" conflates.

**Release, 19 September 1797.**

> **Chronological impossibility.** The draft tied the release to the Treaty of Campo Formio, **signed 17 October 1797** **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Treaty of Campo Formio']**, a month *after* the release; en.wikipedia, 'Georges Washington de La Fayette', compounds it by dating the treaty 18 October. The operative agreement was the **Armistice of Leoben, 18 April 1797**, with Bonaparte's pressure on Vienna. En.wikipedia, 'Marquis de Lafayette', says only "Napoleon Bonaparte secured his release in 1797" **[A]**.
>
> **Unresolved contradiction.** American Battlefield Trust states Lafayette "languished in Olmütz Prison until **1799**, when General Napoleon Bonaparte secured his freedom." **Not adjudicated.** The journey follows 1797 and flags it.
>
> **Internal split.** Chronology lens 18 September, geography lens 19 September. Most scholarship gives **19 September 1797**. Adopted.

The causal link between the damp of Olmütz and her death ten years later is universal in popular biography and rests on no medical or primary source reachable this session **[R]**.

## 7. The northern exile (1797-1800)

- **Hamburg as the place of exile is withdrawn.** De.wikipedia, 'Lehmkuhlen', has the manor at Lehmkuhlen rented in October 1797 by the Marquis de La Fayette, "who received support from Hamburg merchant John Parish". Hamburg held the family's **credit**, not the family **[A for Parish; the residence claim fails]**.
- **Lehmkuhlen, Holstein (54.2265, 10.3617)**, from October 1797 **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, 'Georges Washington de La Fayette']**. The draft's 54.15, 10.3833 matched neither Lehmkuhlen nor Wittmoldt. **They left in 1798**, not 1799.
- **The estate was not Madame de Tessé's.** Fr.wikipedia, 'Adrienne Catherine de Noailles', has the aunt at **Wittmoldt** (54.1742, 10.3519) from 1797, with "Adrienne de La Fayette habite à proximité, à Lehmkuhlen" **[A]**; de.wikipedia has Lafayette at Gut Wittmoldt only "für einige Tage". En.wikipedia places the family "near his aunt Madame de Montagu", but **Madame de Montagu was Adrienne's sister**, Anne Paule Dominique de Noailles. Flagged.
- **Vianen, Dutch Republic (51.993, 5.0932)**, 1798-1799, is **[R]**. The draft attached Mme de Tessé here too: **unsupported**, she is at Wittmoldt until her return to France in 1804.
- Georges returns from America in 1798 **[A]**. The return to France after 18 Brumaire is **[R]**; no re-entry point or date confirmed.

## 8. La Grange, and the death (1800-1807)

> **Two corrections to a much-repeated story.** The draft had Adrienne *buying* La Grange-Bléneau "using her own inheritance" from 1800. Fr.wikipedia, 'Château de La Grange-Bléneau', has the château coming through her family's holdings, "Adrienne de Noailles ... transmitted it to her husband", so by **inheritance and not purchase**, and gives Lafayette's residence "from **1802** until his death in 1834" **[A]**. Corrected to **48.6617, 2.9489** (the article prints 48°39'42.3"N 2°56'56"E); the earlier 48.6672, 2.9822 sat 2.5 km east.

**Death: 24 December 1807, at 24 rue d'Anjou, Paris, aged 48** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Coordinates **48.87125, 2.32175** (Nominatim), 185 m from the draft's 48.8721, 2.3197.

> **Contradiction, resolved against the source rather than left open.** En.wikipedia gives **25 December 1807** and "Auvergne, First French Empire". Both sit inside a section the article itself flags **Unreferenced** (verified in raw wikitext) and are corroborated by nothing, while the French article gives a street address. Mme de Tessé bought a rue d'Anjou house on returning from exile in 1804 **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Adrienne Catherine de Noailles']**. **Adopted: 24 December 1807, Paris.** The English version is an unsourced error, not a live dispute.

The dying words "Je suis toute à vous" are printed by Crawford at p. 318 in his own narrative voice, not as letter text. That they repeat her wedding vow is tradition with no carrier **[R]**.

## 9. Afterlife

- **Burial at Picpus [A]**, by her own wish, near the pit holding her mother, grandmother, and sister **[R for the motive, tradition rather than document]**. Coordinates **48.8440, 2.4004** (en.wikipedia prints 48°50'38"N 2°24'00"E; Nominatim 48.84401, 2.40040). **The prior dossier carried two different wrong Picpus pins**, 48.8467/2.3944 and 48.842/2.3963. Corrected once, everywhere.
- **Lafayette buried beside her, 1834**, his grave filled with soil carried from Bunker Hill **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Picpus Cemetery']**. The soil belongs to his tomb; the sources describe the tomb as shared.
- **The American flag over the grave**, renewed each Fourth of July by the Daughters of the American Revolution with the Society of the Cincinnati and US Embassy officials **[A]**.
- **No monument or statue is dedicated specifically to Adrienne** **[R: an absence, and absences are hard to attest]**.
- **The plaque.** A *Histoire de Paris* panel at **211 rue Saint-Honoré** records the 1774 marriage, but fr.wikipedia calls it "maladroitement situé au n. 211", misplaced onto the neighbouring Hôtel Saint-James & d'Albany **[A]**. The draft said the plaque marks the site. It does not. Panel 48.86524, 2.33117.
- **Virginie de Lasteyrie** wrote *Vie de Madame de Lafayette, précédée d'une Notice sur la vie de sa mère*, 2nd ed. 1869, on Gallica **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **André Maurois**, *Adrienne: The Life of the Marquise de La Fayette*, English 1961 **[A]**; French original c. 1960, date unconfirmed.
- **Anastasie** married Just Charles César de Faÿ de La Tour-Maubourg and lived to 1863 **[A]**. **Georges** and **Virginie** both died in 1849 **[A]**.
- Whether any 2007 bicentennial occurred could not be checked; the search budget was exhausted. **A gap, not a negative finding.**

## 10. Crossings with travellers already in the atlas

Each crossing is coincidence of place or date read out of the other traveller's file. **None of those files mentions Adrienne de Lafayette.** Their movements are **[A]**; the overlap is the atlas's inference **[R]**.

- **`miranda`.** Paris, spring 1792, maréchal de camp; Valmy on 20 September, the theatre Lafayette had commanded weeks before defecting. Acquitted by the Revolutionary Tribunal 16 May 1793, later held at La Force: the machine and the prison system that held Adrienne in 1794.
- **`mikhail_bakunin`.** Tried at Olmütz in 1850-51, condemned twice and reprieved, in the fortress town where Adrienne chose to be a prisoner fifty-five years earlier.
- **`napoleon`.** The file attests Campo Formio, 17 October 1797, and says nothing of Lafayette's release; see §6.
- **`san_martin`** and **`alvear`** cross only at a distance, the Bastille month and the Cádiz lodge of 1812; neither link is in either file, and neither is carried into the journey.
- **The canonical Paris pin, 48.8566, 2.3522**, shared across those files, is inherited byte-identical wherever no better address exists. **`marques_de_lafayette`**, the consecutive QUEUE.md row, has no file yet.

## 11. Open gaps, stated as gaps

1. Which Paris prison held her. No fetched source names one; Plessis and La Force both circulate.
2. The date and circumstances of her own arrest, as distinct from her mother's in May 1794.
3. The Vienna audience with Francis II. Her letter implies it; no article fetched names it, and the Hofburg is an assumption.
4. The Auvergne sequence 1792-1794, wholly tradition. Check against Bertière or Kramer.
5. Conditions at Olmütz, and which building in the fortress. Joint captivity is attested; the rest is not, and the pin is the city.
6. Georges's embarkation port in April 1795, and whether his mother was there.
7. The 1797 versus 1799 release date. Not adjudicated.
8. Any 2007 bicentennial activity. Search budget exhausted.
9. Her presence at the Fête de la Fédération, 14 July 1790.
