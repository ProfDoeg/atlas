# Claude des Armoises (Jeanne des Armoises, la fausse Pucelle)

*2026-08-15. Slug `claude_des_armoises`. In the record 20 May 1436 (Grange-aux-Hormes, near Metz) to February 1457 (remission of René d'Anjou). Birth, parentage and death unknown.*

**[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or late testimony. Contradictions are printed side by side and **not adjudicated**.

Nearly every document is printed by **Jules Quicherat, *Procès de condamnation et de réhabilitation de Jeanne d'Arc*, 5 vols. (Renouard, 1841-1849)**, in **t. V**, under *Documents sur la fausse Jeanne d'Arc qui parut de 1436 à 1440*, pp. 321-335. **A standing citation error runs through the popular literature and through the research pool: Quicherat is repeatedly cited as "vol. VI, p. 121ff". Quicherat has no volume VI** (vol. VI is Lanéry d'Arc's later supplement). Read every "t. VI" in the web literature as **t. V, pp. 321-335**.

---

## 0. What is not known

- **No birth, no parentage, no baptismal name.** "Claude" at Metz, "Jeanne du Lys / des Armoises" thereafter, "Jeanne de Sermaises" in 1457. The 1457 remission's "about forty-five" would put her birth c.1412, also Joan of Arc's conventional birth year. **No way to tell whether that is coincidence, a clerk's inference, or her own last piece of work.**
- **No death.** No burial entry, no widow's suit, no denunciation after February 1457.
- **No statement of motive**, including in her own 1440 confession, which explains her past and not her purpose.
- **The Metz chronicle manuscript was not consulted directly.** Four hands stand between event and page: **Père Jérôme Vignier**, who found it in 1645 and had a notarised extract made; his brother **Benjamin**, who printed the extract in the ***Mercure Galant*, November 1683, pp. 39-60**; **Dom Calmet**, in his *Preuves*; and **Quicherat**, t. V.

## 1. Before Metz: Rome (undated, before May 1436)

**[A] as testimony, [R] as fact.** In autumn 1440, on the marble table of the Palais in Paris, she said she had struck her mother, gone to Rome in penance, served in male dress among the soldiers of **Pope Eugenius IV**, and killed two men (**Journal d'un bourgeois de Paris**, summarized at *books.openedition.org/puc/7809*, hereafter PUC).

**Correction:** this is *not* a modern popular invention, as the pool's source line (The Vintage News / Mental Floss, "no contemporary primary citation found") implied; it is her own confession in a contemporary chronicle. What is unsupported is **the year 1435**; the confession implies only "before 1436", one summary "before 1434". The confession is at once the best source in the dossier and the least disinterested.

## 2. The Messin country, May to summer 1436

**[A] 20 May 1436**, Grange-aux-Hormes near Saint-Privay, south of Metz:

> Icelle année, le xx.e jour de may, vint la Pucelle Jehanne qui avoit esté en France, à la Grange-aux-Hormes, près de Saint-Privay; et y fut amoinnee pour parler à aucuns des seigneurs de Metz; et se faisoit appeller Claude.

(t. V, p. 321.) Quicherat's headnote flags the passage as the origin of the survival *paradoxe*, and he prints a **second manuscript redaction** at p. 323 calling the apparition an outright **supercherie**. Two redactions of one chronicle, in disagreement.

**[A] 21 May 1436**, Metz. Patricians meet her; gifts (a horse, weapons): "et fut recongneu par plusieurs enseignes pour la Pucelle Jehanne de France, qui amoinnat sacrer le roy Charles à Reims" (t. V, p. 322). **Correction:** the circulating modernization "plusieurs détails ... qui amena Charles à Reims" loses *enseignes* (tokens) and drops *sacrer*. In the chronicle this follows her private words to **sire Nicole Lowe**; the brothers' identification is a separate sentence.

**[A] Pierre du Lys, knight, and "petit Jehan" du Lys, squire**, brothers of Joan of Arc, come and accept her as their sister. The most load-bearing fact in the affair, and no source explains it. Through the summer she tours Lorraine and Luxembourg with them, armed and in men's dress, taking gifts from Joan's former partisans.

**[A] Marieulles**, three weeks with a noble family between Metz and Pont-à-Mousson (montjoye.net; jeannedomremy.fr). **Coordinate correction: 48.998 / 6.101**, not 48.9908 / 6.1478 (pool geography) nor 49.0333 / 6.1667 (pool afterlife), which also disagreed with each other. montjoye warns against confusing it with Marville.

## 3. North: Liesse, Arlon, Cologne, 1436

**[A] Notre-Dame de Liesse** (Aisne), Black Madonna shrine, on the road north. **Coordinate correction: 49.6103 / 3.805**, not 49.5142 / 3.8321, ~11 km off in open country.

**[A] Arlon**: received by **Elizabeth of Görlitz**, dowager Duchess of Luxembourg; there she meets **Robert, Count of Virneburg**, who takes up her cause. **Contradiction:** the pool's geography dates the arrival to **October 1436**, while montjoye records a **Cologne registration of 6 August 1436**, which requires an earlier arrival. Both cannot stand.

**[A] Cologne**: registered as **Pucelle de France**, given a cuirass. The inquisitor **Heinrich Kalteysen** cites her for male dress, bearing arms and magic, including the boast that she could make **Ulrich von Manderscheid** archbishop of Trier as she once made Charles king of France. She fails to appear, is **excommunicated for contumacy**, leaves under Virneburg's protection.

Two material corrections. **(1) Johannes Nider was not an eyewitness**: his *Formicarius* (c.1436-38) relays Kalteysen at second hand, and it is Kalteysen who described her dancing with men and eating and drinking "plus que ne le permettait son sexe" (fr.wikisource; PUC). The pool's "contemporary eyewitness account" was false. **(2) Virneburg falling in love with her and giving her a military company has no source** in Nider or the chronicle; his documented role is protection and safe conduct. Dropped.

**Date contradiction:** PUC puts the process in **1436**, montjoye in **1437-38**. The pool's separate [R] entry on a Rhineland excommunication is this same event, re-sourced to Nider via Kalteysen.

## 4. Orléans hears, August to October 1436

The Orléans accounts are the only serial, dated, disinterested source in the dossier, and where the pool's worst errors sat.

**[A] 9 August 1436** (jeudi, veille Saint-Lorens), **48 sous parisis** to the pursuivant **Fleur-de-lilz**: "pour ce qu'il avoit aportées lectres à la ville de par Jehanne la Pucelle; pour ce, 48 s. p." (t. V, p. 326). **Correction:** the pool carried this as a **1439** payment of "deux réaux d'or". Both wrong: the year is 1436, the sum 48 s. p.; "deux réaux d'or" is a secondary gloss (biblisem.net), not register language. **Quicherat's 1439 Orléans extract (pp. 331-332) contains no Fleur-de-Lys payment at all.**

**[A] 21 August 1436** (mardi), **12 livres tournois** voted to **Jehan du Lys**, 9 l. 12 s. p. disbursed: "ledit frère de ladicte Pucelle vint en la Chambre de la dicte ville requérir... d'aucun poy d'argent pour s'en retourner par devers sa dicte seur". **Two corrections:** the register says *tournois*, not *parisis*; and **"poy d'argent" is "a little money", not "a weight of silver"** ("poids d'argent" is a widely propagated misreading). Jean said the king had ordered him 100 francs of which only 20 were paid. **Duplicate collapsed:** the pool carried this one entry **twice**, as 1436 in parisis and as 1439 in tournois, as if two attestations; there is **no 1439 payment to Jehan du Lys** in Quicherat.

**[A] 18 October 1436**: a pursuivant paid for a six-week mission to **Arlon** to visit "the Maid" and carry letters on to the King at **Loches**; montjoye names him **Coeur-de-Lis**. The town did verify. **What the herald reported is not in the register.**

## 5. Madame des Armoises, October 1436 to 1439

**Contradiction, and a conflation the pool inherited:** jeannedomremy.fr dates the **marriage** to **3 October 1436 at Arlon**, while **6 or 7 November 1436** is the date of the **acte de vente** of the Haraucourt revenues, where Robert des Harmoises and "Jehanne du Lys, la Pucelle de France, ma femme" seal jointly (t. V, pp. 328-329, from Dom Calmet, *Preuves*, t. III, col. CCCV). The familiar "6 versus 7 November" quarrel is therefore **about the deed, not the wedding day**; the Metz chronicle gives **no month** ("là fut faict le mariage de messire Robert des Hermoises, chevalier, et de la dite Jehanne la Pucelle", t. V, p. 323).

**[A/R] Robert des Armoises**, widowed knight of a Lorraine house, born c.1388, so about 48 at marriage. The repeated "roughly twenty years her senior" depends on importing her birth year from Joan of Arc's, an assumption that should not be granted silently.

**[A]** Two sons, from her own 1440 confession ("mariée à un chevalier dont elle avait eu deux fils"). **[R]** that they were born at the **Château de Jaulny**: château tradition only. **Coordinate correction: 48.9683 / 5.8852** (Base Mérimée PA00106047), not 48.9394 / 5.7908, ~8 km off near Thiaucourt; the pool's afterlife entries used 48.9717 / 5.8875, contradicting its own geography entry. **[R]** Continued touring for about three years: composite secondary account.

## 6. The company, c.1439 (undated)

**[A] as document, [R] as identification.** Remission of **June 1441** to **Jehan de Siquemville**, esquire of Gascony, for pillage in Anjou and Poitou (Trésor des chartes J. 176 pièce 84; t. V, p. 333; given at **Saint-Denis**):

> dist à icellui suppliant qu'il vouloit aler au Mans et qu'il vouloit qu'il prinst la charge et gouvernement des gens de guerre que avoit lors une appelée Jehanne, qui se disoit Pucelle

Corrections: (1) she did **not** "serve under Gilles de Rais at Le Mans", **Le Mans was the objective**, and de Rais transferred to Siquemville the command of troops she then had; (2) the events are **undated**, inferred to c.1439 from "about two years earlier"; (3) the identification of this Jehanne with the Armoises woman is **explicitly conjectural** ("il n'est pas impossible que ce soit la Dame des Armoises"), so the pool's **[A]** on the Le Mans entry is **retagged [R]**. Textual fixes: "prinst la charge" not "prit en charge"; "lors" not "alors". Note that the crown writes "une appelée Jehanne, qui se disoit Pucelle" where Orléans writes "Jehanne la Pucelle" flat.

## 7. Orléans in person, July to September 1439

- **[A] 18 July 1439**: wine for "dame Jehanne des Armoises" on her arrival. **29 and 30 July**: further food and wine.
- **[A] 1 August 1439**: after deliberation of the town council, **210 livres parisis** "for the good that she did to the said town during the siege of 1429", paid at the farewell dinner; she departs hastily that evening. **Correction:** the pool's chronology dated this to **29 July**, contradicting its own geography entry, which correctly said 1 August (fr.wikisource: "1er août: dîner d'adieu, deux cent dix livres parisis"). The 29-30 July entries are food and wine only.
- **[A] 4 September 1439**: a *propine* marking her return. **Last entry naming her in the Orléans accounts.**
- **[A] 27 September 1439**: Tours pays a messenger carrying her letters to the King and to the bailli.
- **[R]** The supper she leaves early to avoid a tailor who had dressed the real Joan: Mental Floss, anecdotal, **no primary citation**; kept as a flagged legend. **[R]** Conjuring feats at Orléans: English Wikipedia, unattributed, **not used**.

## 8. The unmasking, 1440

**[A]** Brought to Paris and made to confess publicly on the **pierre de marbre** of the great court of the Palais, before the University and the Parlement: "elle n'estoit pas pucelle, et qu'elle avoit esté mariée à un chevalier dont elle avait eu deux fils". The entry closes: "Et quant elle fut à Paris, encore retourna en la guerre, et fut en garnison, et puis s'en alla." (Journal d'un bourgeois de Paris, §790, Tuetey ed., pp. 354-355; t. V, p. 335.)

**Corrections:** the pool framed that closing sentence as her passage through Paris *before* the collapse. It is the **close** of the entry, after the unmasking and the Rome material, and the **same paragraph** as the confession quote, so the two are **one source, not two**. Date: **August 1440** per the Journal, but **PUC puts the amende honorable in September-October and explicitly denies August**.

**[R] The king's secret.** The confrontation rests solely on **Pierre Sala**, *Hardiesses des grands rois et empereurs*, who had it from the chamberlain **Guillaume Gouffier** in **1480** and wrote it out about **1516** (t. IV, p. 281):

> Pucelle m'amye, vous soyez la très bien revenue, ou nom de Dieu qui sçait le secret qui est entre vous et moy.

Corrections: (1) "entre nous" must be **"entre vous et moy"**, the whole point of a two-party secret; (2) the words are **Charles VII's**, not Gouffier's, Gouffier being Sala's informant; (3) Sala did **not** write c.1480, he presented the work in 1516. Sala gives **no location**; PUC proposes **Bourges or Orléans**, autumn 1440, **not Paris**, where the pool's geography pinned it. The pool's afterlife entry tagging the confrontation **[A]** is therefore wrong: the Paris confession is [A], the confrontation [R].

## 9. After 1440: Sermaize, Saumur, silence

**[A] c.1449 and c.1452, Sermaize (Sermaize-les-Bains, Marne).** A woman still calling herself Jeanne la Pucelle, in men's dress, visits the **Voulton (Vouthon)** family, maternal kin of Joan of Arc, and dines with **Jehan du Lys**; she plays tennis against the curé and tells him he has now played against la Pucelle de France.

Two corrections, one serious. **(1) These are not depositions from the 1455-56 trial of rehabilitation**, as the pool stated: they come from a **separate inquest of 2-3 November 1476 before the notaries of Vitry**, into the descendants of the brother of Joan's mother. Witnesses: **Perinet de Voulton, 52; Simon Fauchard, curé, 53; Jehan le Montigueue, 70; Jehan Guillaume, 76.** **(2) Coordinates: 48.785 / 4.911**, not 48.7667 / 4.9167.

**Contradiction inside the testimony itself:** le Montigueue yields **1449**; Fauchard testified she came "twenty-four years ago", i.e. **c.1452**. Both are carried, on two separate stops. No etymological link exists between "Sermaize" and "des Armoises" despite the phonetic echo; a probable source of confusion in later retellings.

**[A] February 1457**, kingdom of **René of Anjou**: "Jeanne de Sermaises", after about three months at **Saumur** following a dispute with the Dame de Saumoussay, is released on a **five-year conditional remission** for having long called herself Jeanne la Pucelle and deceived many who had seen the real one at the siege of 1429, on condition she "bear herself honestly in dress, and in other matters, as a woman should do". **[R]** The same document calls her **about forty-five** and "at present wife of **Jean Douillet**". After this she disappears.

## 10. Afterlife, and one tomb

**[R] Pulligny-sur-Madon** (48.5333 / 6.15). Robert des Armoises died in **1450** at Jaulny and was buried in the parish church here. A plaque over the tomb, legible into the early twentieth century and gone by about 1909, named "haute et honoree dame Jehanne du Lis la Pucelle de France dame de Tichemont... qui trepassa l'an 1449 le 4e jour de may" (full text on the journey file's Pulligny stop). **Decisive correction:** the plaque was **affixed in 1690 by the des Armoises family**, in memory of Robert and his wife. It is a **seventeenth-century commemorative plaque, not a surviving fifteenth-century epitaph.** This dissolves the "contradiction" the pool flagged between Robert's 1450 burial and a 1449 epitaph for Jeanne: one tomb, two people commemorated, inscribed 240 years later, the wording ambiguous as to whom "trepassa l'an 1449" applies. **Still open:** a death on 4 May 1449 is incompatible with Sermaize c.1452 and Saumur 1457.

**[A] as objects, [R] as identification.** Two painted medallions at the **Château de Jaulny**, facing over a fifteenth-century fireplace, shown as Jeanne and Robert; uncovered under plaster in **1871**. **Corrections:** the mayor of Jaulny did not identify them at discovery, he recounted a **provenance story**, that his great-great-grandfather had hidden the portraits in the keep before the Revolution on the orders of M. des Armoises; and **Girault** holds they may well have been **painted in 1871 itself**. Pool tag [A] on the identification; retagged [R].

**[A] Andrew Lang. Correction:** the 1895 *Nineteenth Century* essay "The False Pucelle" was **not** reprinted in *The Valet's Tragedy and Other Studies* (1903); Lang's preface says those papers "are not the papers here presented", and the book's chapter IV is a different, rewritten piece, "The False Jeanne d'Arc". **[R]** The survivalist thesis (Ledru, Atten, Weill-Raynal; revived by Gorbenko c.2001) that Joan herself escaped the stake, remains possibly moved to **Cléry-Saint-André**, is carried by jeannedomremy.fr, which advocates it. **Not used.**

## 11. Carried forward unresolved, and one item cut

Seven contradictions are flagged in place above and carried in the journey file's `date_confidence` fields: the Arlon arrival, the marriage date, the year of the Cologne process, the month of the Paris amende honorable, the Sermaize year, the death date, and the two Metz redactions. **One more, not stated above.** The Île-aux-Boeufs donation of **28 July 1443** (not 29 July; t. V, pp. 212-213) has the duke of Orléans writing "en compaignie de Jehanne la Pucelle, sa soeur; avecque la quelle, jusques à son absentement, et depuis jusques à présent...", while a ducal cédule of the **same year** pays Pierre du Lys as "frère de **feue** Jehanne la Pucelle" (t. V, p. 214 n. 1). The pool's quote also rewrote the duke's own first person ("et de nous") as "et de Monsieur le Duc d'Orléans".

**Cut entirely.** The pool's [A] quote "à Isabeau, mère de Jehanne la Pucelle, pour don fait à elle", with its inference that the Orléans registers added "feue" only after September 1446. The wording appears only on jeannedomremy.fr; Quicherat (t. V, pp. 275-276) has the payments begin **7 July 1440** and read "pour avoir sa vie"; and the registers **already** write "feue Jehanne la Pucelle" for the obsequies of **1433, 1436 and 1439**.
