# Buenaventura Durruti: research report

*2026-08-22. José Buenaventura Durruti Dumange, b. León 14 July 1896, d. Madrid 20 November 1936. Mechanic, railwayman, deserter, expropriator, militia commander.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or contested attribution. Where sources or the two lenses of the research pool disagree, both readings are recorded and the disagreement left standing.

This life has two documentary registers. Wikipedia (en, es, fr, ca) carries the skeleton of dates and places and is firm on most of it. Everything interior comes from **Abel Paz, *Durruti en la revolución española***, a partisan biography by a man who fought in the CNT and the standard life for which no substitute exists. Every Paz quotation here is at one remove, from the pool's excerpting, not verified against a paginated copy: this file's largest weakness, not fixable without the book.

## Phase 1. León, 1896 to 1917

- Born **14 July 1896, barrio de Santa Ana, León**, to Santiago Durruti, railway worker, and Anastasia Dumange (Domingo). **[A: en./es.wikipedia]**
- **1903: the nine-month strike of the León *curtidores*; his father arrested.** **[A: es.wikipedia, which dates the strike and ties it to the family's hardship]** *Flagged:* the pool's "a boy of about six" is unsafe, since he turned seven on 14 July 1903. The campa says "six, or seven, depending which month of that long year the guards came."
- **1910: apprenticed to the mechanic Melchor Martínez, a socialist. April 1913: joins the UGT metalworkers' union.** **[A: both editions]** One stop.
- **c.1912-13: mineral-processing workshop at La Robla [A: Paz]. 1916: installs pumps at the Matallana coal workings, supports the striking miners, sours on the UGT leadership [A: es.wikipedia].** One stop. *Flagged:* Paz spreads La Robla and Matallana across c.1912-1917; the stop says so.
- **August 1917: in the revolutionary general strike; dismissed by the Northern Railway, expelled from the Unión Ferroviaria. December 1917: flees conscription, north and then to France.** **[A]**

**Gap.** No reachable source gives the sentence, if any, from the desertion proceedings.

## Phase 2. The deserter's circuit, 1918 to 1920

- **1918: arrested by the Civil Guard at Santiago de Compostela among fellow deserters. 1918-1919: court-martialed at San Sebastián; escapes into France.** **[A]** One stop; the sentence is nowhere stated.
- **January 1919: formally joins the CNT while working as a mechanic at La Felguera.** **[A: en.wikipedia]** The pool's 1918 dating is recorded on the stop alongside it, unreconciled.
- **By June 1919: mechanic in Paris**, moving between French and Occitan cities. **[A]**

*Sequencing problem, not resolved.* These cannot be confidently ordered, since no source gives months for the first two. The file orders them as listed and says the order is not fixed.

## Phase 3. Los Justicieros and Los Solidarios, 1920 to 1923

- **1920, San Sebastián: joins Buenacasa's branch, helps form Los Justicieros; the plot against Alfonso XIII is uncovered; flees to Zaragoza by freight train, works as a locksmith; backs Riotinto by sabotage from the north.** **[A]**
- **1921, Eibar: robs a company paymaster with Gregorio Suberviola and Rafael Torres Escartín.** **[A]** *Contradiction inside the pool:* chronology lens 1921, geography lens 1923, one robbery. The file follows 1921 because that account names the accomplices, and says so on the stop.
- **1922, Barcelona: co-founds Los Solidarios with Juan García Oliver, Francisco Ascaso, Ricardo Sanz.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** With Bilbao arms buying **[R]** and the c.6,000 grenades made with Eusebi Brau after March 1923 **[A]** folded in for place, the later date declared on the stop.
- **April 1923: arrested on the Calle de Alcalá, Madrid.** **[A]** *Flagged, unresolved:* the pool adds charges of armed robbery, attempted regicide and desertion with acquittal on the first two, which **neither Wikipedia edition says.** He is at liberty in Gijón by 1 September, implying a short detention, but the campa presents the charges as report, not fact. Needs Paz.
- **4 June 1923: Cardinal Juan Soldevila y Romero assassinated at Zaragoza; Los Solidarios widely accused.** **[R]** *Tag conflict:* chronology lens **[R]**, geography lens **[A]** while conceding in its own note that the sources establish attribution to the group, not authorship by Durruti. Carried as **[R]**.
- **1 September 1923: robs the Bank of Spain at Gijón of 650,000 pesetas; the manager bites and slaps him, a bullet grazes his neck. November: flees to Paris with Ascaso.** **[A]**

## Phase 4. Los Errantes, 1924 to 1926

- **1924, Paris: Renault mechanic in Belleville. November: leads a party at the Hendaye-Bera crossing, ambushed, two killed, forced back. December: embarks for Cuba with Ascaso on false passports; dockwork in Havana.** **[A]**
- **1925, Santa Clara: an employer killed during a cane strike, a note left signed "Los Errantes."** **[A]** Chronology lens 1925, geography lens 1924-1925; minor, noted.
- **1925: ashore in Yucatán by bribing Treasury officers; hosted in Mexico City by the CGT; joined by Alejandro Ascaso and Gregorio Jover (March); robs an office at Tecomán; lectures on rationalist education at Tampico.** **[A for all five]** The first, third and fifth are folded into the Mexico City stop; none is dated beyond the year.
- **July 1925, Valparaíso: robs a Bank of Chile branch** (the group in the city since May). **[A]** *Flagged:* "first anarchist bank robbery in Chilean history" is supported by neither Wikipedia edition; the campa states it as a claim whose citation has never been produced.
- **1925, Buenos Aires: works the docks; robs a bank at San Martín to fund the return after wanted posters circulate. February 1926: back in France.** **[A]** The only Argentine stretch of the life, and it matters downstream: **Argentina's extradition request in 1927 is what put him on hunger strike.** The pool's note that no sourced Durruti-Buenos Aires link existed was wrong; the canonical pin is used because he was actually there.

## Phase 5. La Santé and the stateless years, 1926 to 1931

- **25 June 1926: arrested in Paris after an informer betrays a plot against Alfonso XIII; held in La Santé. 7 October: tried at the Palais de Justice, confesses, justifies his acts by the repression of the dictatorship. 13 February 1927: hunger strike against extradition to Argentina. 8 July 1927: released after Louis Lecoin turns a majority of the Chamber of Deputies against extradition.** **[A]** Two stops; trial, strike and release folded into the second, dated at the release and declared as such.
- **1927, Ménilmontant: meets Émilienne Morin and Nestor Makhno at an anarchist bookshop.** **[R: no primary source reached]** Kept, because Morin becomes his companion for life and the mother of his child; the tag is on the stop.
- **1928, Berlin: sheltered by Augustin Souchy and Rudolf Rocker [A]; attends an IWA congress [R]. 1929: expelled after Centre Party objections [A]. 1929-1931, Brussels: metalworker; helps found *La Voz de Libertaria* [A].**

## Phase 6. The Republic that keeps arresting him, 1931 to 1936

- **14 April 1931: returns to Barcelona among the first exiles. 19 April: speaks at a CNT meeting on Montjuïc, calls the change of regime a step toward democratization and predicts civil war if working-class demands are ignored.** **[A; the geography lens gives 18/19 April]**
- **1 May 1931: the Civil Guard fires on demonstrators; Durruti calms the crowd from the bed of a truck.** **[A]** *Contradiction, left open:* the chronology lens places this at a May Day rally of over 100,000, the geography lens outside the Palau de la Generalitat after the shooting. Almost certainly one episode differently located. The stop uses the Generalitat pin and states the disagreement rather than choosing silently.
- **4 December 1931: birth of his daughter Colette to Émilienne Morin.** **[A for the date: fr.wikipedia, 'Émilienne Morin']** *Place unsupported:* the pool gave Paris, which no source supports and the itinerary contradicts, since he returned in April 1931 and is documented in Catalonia through December. The stop is placed in Barcelona and says plainly that Barcelona is inference. **The only pin in the file resting on inference.** The **December 1931 Girona arrest [A, month only]** is folded in.
- **January 1932: the Fígols / Alt Llobregat insurrection. 10 February: the steamer *Buenos Aires* sails from Barcelona with preventive prisoners, Durruti among them, for Bata in Spanish Guinea; the party is moved to the Canaries and returns in September. 13 December 1933: arrested in Barcelona that afternoon, after the anarcho-syndicalist insurrection of that month.** **[A: en./es.wikipedia]** The pool had fused these into one item dated 13 December 1933, two arrests twenty-two months apart. Split into two stops.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable documents where he lived, what he did for money, or how often he saw his daughter across 1932-1936.

## Phase 7. July 1936 and the Aragon front

- **19 July 1936: a principal figure in the anarchist defeat of the rising in Barcelona. 20 July: the CNT delegation, with Durruti and García Oliver, meets Lluís Companys at the Palau de la Generalitat and proposes a military committee rather than a government.** **[A]** *Flagged as not directly sourced:* **Ascaso's death at the Atarazanas barracks on 20 July** reached this file through the verification pass's correction note, not a source read for it. It is in the campa because Ascaso is the central relationship of the life, and the status is declared in `date_confidence`.
- **24 July: the column leaves Barcelona. 25 July: reaches Bujaraloz, HQ at la Casa de Rozas.** **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **Summer 1936: land collectivized in the villages taken along the Ebro.** **[A; Paz for Pina de Ebro]** The campa states that where a village did not want collectivization it was imposed, which the column's own accounts admit.
- **Autumn 1936: the line holds at Perdiguera, with no artillery to take Zaragoza. Emma Goldman visits the front and interviews him.** **[A: her own essay]** The essay fixes neither date nor place; the stop pins it at the column's HQ and says so.

## Phase 8. Madrid, 14 to 20 November 1936

- **November 1936: leads part of the column to Madrid; committed at the Ciudad Universitaria.** **[A]** Casualty figures differ between accounts; the campa says they differ rather than picking one.
- **19 November 1936: shot in the chest on the Calle de Isaac Peral, under two hours after an interview.** **[A for the wounding, R for the mechanism]**
  - *Source conflict:* es.wikipedia names Calle Isaac Peral; en.wikipedia's *Death of Buenaventura Durruti* **does not name the street**, placing the wounding near the Hospital Clínico. Both recorded.
  - **Competing accounts, none adjudicated:** enemy fire from the Hospital Clínico (the driver, **Julio Graves**); accidental discharge of Durruti's own *naranjero*, the open-bolt MP28 copy, its safety slipped (the secretary, **Jesús Arnal**, 1967, citing Ricardo Rionda); a fifth columnist; deserters he stopped; an NKVD killing (Robert Kern among those canvassing it). The pool had given Arnal's version to Graves, who reported the opposite. **Paz concluded the circumstances remain an enigma; Helen Graham calls the accident theory compelling without treating it as settled.**
- **20 November 1936, four in the morning: dies in the Hotel Ritz, converted to a blood hospital for the Catalan militias, of a 9 mm wound through the epigastrium damaging spleen and diaphragm.** **[A: es.wikipedia, citing the autopsy]**

## Phase 9. The funeral, the grave, the afterlife

- **23 November 1936: procession from Via Laietana to the Columbus Monument, 200,000 to 500,000 people, called the largest funeral in Spanish history. Four eulogists: Soviet consul Volodymyr Antonov-Ovseenko, President Companys, Justice Minister Garcia Oliver, Health Minister Montseny.** **[A: en.wikipedia, *Death of Buenaventura Durruti*]** The pool named only two. The omission is material: the same article records an NKVD theory of the shooting, and the Soviet consul was standing over the coffin.
- **24 November 1936: interment at Montjuïc, plot 69, delayed one day by rain. 1937: reinterred in agrupación 13a, beside Francisco Ascaso, near the cenotaph of Francesc Ferrer i Guàrdia. March 1937: the Agrupación de los Amigos de Durruti constituted by veterans of his column, two months before the May Days it then acts in.** **[A]**
- **Franco years: Communist propaganda circulated a counter-legend that his own comrades executed him at the Modelo on 21 November for opposing militarization.** **[R]** Contradicted by the documented Madrid wound and the burial timeline.
- **Afterlife in print and film [A/R]:** Paz, Enzensberger, Amorós, the Comolli and Paco Ríos documentaries, streets across Spain, songs. **None of it is staged as a stop:** the people who made it travelled to their own places, not to his.

## Quotations

**Verbatim [A].** Five lines come from **Emma Goldman, "Durruti Is Dead, Yet Living," *Spain and the World* (1936)**, read in full at theanarchistlibrary.org and quoting her interview at the Aragon front; two are used, at the Goldman stop and at Perdiguera.

**At one remove, via Abel Paz [A, unpaginated].** Six lines, used at León (twice), Matallana, Gijón, La Felguera and La Santé, none checked against the book. The letter to his sister Rosa and the enlistment-office line about Alfonso XIII carry the most weight and would most repay direct verification.

**Disputed [R], carried with the dispute stated in `quote_source`:** "We carry a new world here, in our hearts" and "No government fights fascism to destroy it," both attributed to the **Pierre van Paassen interview, *Toronto Star*, 24 July 1936**, wording and attribution disputed and fabrication suggested by some researchers, used at the 19 July and Generalitat stops; "only a hundred of us will survive, but with that hundred we shall enter Saragossa," attributed to a **Koltsov** interview and cited secondhand in **Hugh Thomas**, used at Bujaraloz; "The pickaxe and the shovel are as important as the rifle," cited in secondary literature to 3 October 1936 and confirmed verbatim nowhere reached, used at Pina de Ebro. The van Paassen material is the most famous thing Durruti is supposed to have said and the least secure; it is kept with the dispute attached rather than dropped silently.

## Interlocks: used and declined

**Used and named in a campa: `mikhail_bakunin`.** Durruti is on record judging Bakunin and Kropotkin against each other, "there is violence and radicalism in Bakunin, whereas one finds a practical element and the foundations of the free society in Kropotkin" (Paz). A reader citing a theorist is a real one-directional relation. It sits at the La Santé stop, whose `date_confidence` states that the remark is undated in Paz and placed in the prison years rather than attested to them. Nothing was written into Bakunin's own file.

**Declined.** The lens surfaced eighteen candidates; fourteen are shared geography or a shared calendar year, which the house rule excludes.

- **`lucia_sanchez_saornil`.** Same confederation (her CNT card, 1931), same rising, overlapping months in Barcelona from August 1936, but **no personal contact attested on either side.** The overlap is organizational and the operator should know it exists; it is not met, corresponded, influenced, or cited. Treating shared CNT membership as a relation would be a rule change, not a call for this file.
- **`antoni_gaudi`** (his workshop destroyed in 1936 in CNT-FAI Barcelona, but his file names no agents and Durruti has no attested relation to him or the building); **`che_guevara`** (trained under Alberto Bayo, a Republican regular officer, not CNT-FAI); **`albert_camus`** (*Révolte dans les Asturies* treats the 1934 Asturian rising: his relation to an event, not to Durruti); **`erich_muhsam`, `gustav_landauer`, `gusto_graeser`** (council-anarchist lineage, and Mühsam's link to Rocker is Rocker's, not Durruti's); **`jean_paul_sartre`, `simone_de_beauvoir`, `dion_fortune`, `mussolini`** (bare 1936-37 calendar coincidence; Italy's intervention against the Republic is a real antagonism, but nothing makes it a relation between the two men).

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found, in either direction.** Durruti is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing here needed the exception.

**No backward reach:** he has no theoretical apparatus of his own to apply to events he never treated, having been neither a theorist nor a writer. The doctrine in the file is Bakunin's and Kropotkin's, cited *by him*, an ordinary one-directional relation needing no licence. **No forward reach:** nothing applies his practice to anything after 20 November 1936. The three posthumous items, the Friends of Durruti (March 1937), the 1937 reinterment and the Franco-era counter-legend, happened to his name and his body at his own grave and in his own city, and are folded into the Montjuïc stop. **Nothing was silently dropped:** the declined interlocks are listed above by name with reasons. All are lateral, contemporaneous or later people with no relation to him, which is neither the backward direction the exception licenses nor the forward direction it forbids. If the operator disagrees with any call, the material is above and can be folded back without re-running research.
