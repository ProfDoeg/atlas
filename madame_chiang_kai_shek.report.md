# Madame Chiang Kai-shek (Soong Mei-ling), 1898-2003

*Research report for the Atlas of Journeys, compiled 2026-08-08.* **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim circulating without a traceable primary. Conflicts are printed, not resolved. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

## 1. Method, and what was reachable

Spine: English and Chinese Wikipedia, checked against three primary items fetched and read in full (section 12). **Not reachable:** Britannica's `Song Meiling` returned HTTP 403 and was never read; WebSearch quota was exhausted during gathering; Pakula, Hahn, Seagrave, Fenby and Chang were **not read**, so claims resting on them alone are marked [R] or dropped.

Two systematic defects in the incoming material, corrected throughout. **Fourteen of thirty-four geographic pins were wrong**, usually a municipal centroid substituted for a building, or a modern successor pinned instead of the historic structure. And **all six citations to *The Voice of China* were inflated by exactly +4 pages**, the signature of scan-leaf numbers read as printed pages.

## 2. Birth and family, 1898-1907

Born **Soong Mei-ling**, third daughter of Charlie Soong and Ni Kwei-tseng, Shanghai, **1898** [A, en.wiki]. Charlie Soong: Hainan-born, Methodist-trained in North Carolina, Bible printer, financier of Sun Yat-sen; siblings **Ai-ling**, **Ching-ling** (later Sun Yat-sen's wife), **T.V. Soong** [A].

**Birthplace disputed, not resolved.** en.wiki: the family house **Neishidi** in Chuansha, Pudong. zh.wiki: **Tongren Hospital**, inside the International Settlement. The pool's third candidate, the former French Concession, is supported by neither and is dropped. **Birth date disputed** likewise: her passport gives **4 March 1898**, other sources 5 March or 1897, from East Asian age reckoning. [A for both disputes]

**Wenchang, Hainan** is the ancestral village; Charlie left as a child and no child of his settled there, so the tie is genealogical, not lived [R]. Pool pin sat ~8 km north of Wenchang centre (~19.543, 110.753).

## 3. The American education, 1907-1917

Badly garbled in the pool; reconstructed from en.wiki's own timeline, which contradicts it at three points. **1907**: sent to the United States, private school at **Summit, New Jersey**. **1908**: eighth grade at **Piedmont College, Demorest, Georgia**. **1910**: **Fairmount College, Monteagle, Tennessee**. **1912**: **registered a freshman at Wesleyan College, Macon, at fifteen**. **1914**: transfers to **Wellesley** to be near T.V. Soong. **19 June 1917**: BA in English literature, **Durant Scholar**, **Tau Zeta Epsilon**; Wellesley's notable-alumnae gallery lists her without class year or plaque. [all A, en.wiki]

**Corrections.** The pool said she "joined her sisters at Wesleyan at about age 10" in 1908 and placed Piedmont in Tennessee. Both wrong: Piedmont is in **Georgia**, and she was too young for Wesleyan admission, attached to that campus only informally; Ai-ling had graduated in 1909, so only Ching-ling overlapped. Fairmount was 1910, not 1909. The Wesleyan campus was **College Street** (~32.839, -83.636), not the Forsyth Road campus of the 1928 move; the pool's 32.8407, -83.6534 matches neither. The pool began the Wellesley period a year early.

## 4. Return, courtship, marriage, 1917-1930

- Returns to China after ten years. That her Mandarin was inadequate rests on her own retrospective testimony. [R]
- **First meeting with Chiang: sources disagree and the disagreement stands.** en.wiki: **1920**. Biographical tradition: **Shanghai, 1922**, at Sun Yat-sen's residence. [R for both] The pool's **Guangzhou** setting is supported by neither and is dropped.
- **1 December 1927, Shanghai**, two venues in one day: a **private ceremony at the Soong house, 369 Seymour Road** (now Shaanxi North Road), ~31.235, 121.452; and a **public reception at the Majestic Hotel** (Gordon Road), ~31.2296, 121.4507, more than a thousand guests beneath a portrait of Sun Yat-sen. [A, zh.wiki] Pool coordinates were off by ~1.5 km and ~500 m.
- **Correction, substantive.** The pool stated Chiang "has converted to Methodism to marry into the Soong family." **He had not.** He promised only to study Christianity and was **baptised three years after the marriage**, in 1930, telling his mother-in-law that religion "needed to be gradually absorbed, not swallowed like a pill." [A, en.wiki]

## 5. The Nanjing decade, 1928-1937

- **1928**: Committee of Yuans; **Legislative Yuan 1930-1932**. **26 October 1931**: first *Time* cover. **1936**: **Secretary-General, Chinese Aeronautical Affairs Commission**. [A]
- **Residence, corrected.** The pool put the couple's home at the **Presidential Palace** (32.04528, 118.79222), which was the **seat of government and Chiang's office**, not their house. Their residence was **Meiling Palace**: completed 1934, presented on her birthday **4 March 1936**, occupied that summer, **Chiang's official residence from 1946**. [A] Coordinates **32.0456, 118.8464**; pool ~1.7 km off.
- **February 1934, Nanchang**: the **New Life Movement**. [A, founding documents, second hand] 28.682, 115.8579 accepted. **1934**: given **Meilu Villa** on **Mount Lu**, in the hill station of **Guling**. [A] Correct pin ~29.571, 115.980; the pool's 29.5936, 115.9967 is 3-4 km north of both the villa and Mount Lu itself.
- **April 1938**: a magazine cover styles her **"Deputy Commander of the Republic of China Air Force."** [R] Not otherwise confirmed.

## 6. The Xi'an Incident, December 1936

- **13 December 1936, Nanjing**: arrives having received Zhang Xueliang's assurance that Chiang is alive, and argues against the punitive expedition the Nanjing generals are preparing. [A, en.wiki; her own account, p.67] The pool filed her line "I decided to fight it..." under **place: Xi'an**. The text places it in **Nanking**: it follows directly on "as we found it when we reached Nanking on Sunday morning, December 13," and the "it" is the generals' expedition.
- **22 December**: flies to **Xi'an** with **T.V. Soong** and **Dai Li**. **24 December**: direct talks with **Zhou Enlai, Zhang Xueliang, Yang Hucheng**, producing agreements toward ending the civil war and legalising the Communist Party. [A]
- **25 December**: appeals to Zhang Xueliang; Chiang is released. [A] **Correction:** the party "took off around 5 pm on 25 December, arriving in Nanjing the following day," i.e. **26 December**, not the same day. [A]
- The seizure took place at **Huaqing Pool**, Lintong (34.3623, 109.2114) [A], but **no source places her there**, so the journey does not pin her at it.

## 7. War, 1937-1945

- **1937**: leads public appeals; helps establish women's battalions. **1937-1938, Hankou**: government relocates after the fall of Nanjing; refugee relief and air force work. [A] 30.5844, 114.2803 accepted.
- **3 January 1938**: *Time* "Man and Wife of the Year." **Correction to the pool and to en.wiki**, which both give **3 January 1937**, wrong by a full year: Time's archive shows the **19380103** cover, "General & Mme. Chiang, Couple of the Year," Person of the Year for 1937 being announced in the first 1938 issue. [A, content.time.com]
- **1938-1945, Chongqing**: based at the **Huangshan compound, Nan'an District**, south bank, ~29.52, 106.65. The pool's 29.563, 106.5516 is the **municipal centroid**. The journey file nonetheless pins Chongqing at **29.5556, 106.5764**, already canonical in the corpus (section 11). **1940, Repulse Bay, Hong Kong**: medical travel [R, fragmentary], not carried into the journey.
- **28 February 1942, Kunming**: dinner for the **American Volunteer Group**, Chennault replying; the speech survives verbatim. **1943**: third *Time* cover. **1945**: KMT Central Executive Committee. [A]

## 8. The American tour, 1943

- **February, the White House**: guest of the Roosevelts [A]; Eleanor Roosevelt's private irritation is second hand only, see section 10. **February, Waldorf Astoria**: New York fundraising base [A], 40.7566, -73.9741 accepted.
- **18 February**: addresses the **Congress**, first Chinese national and second woman. [A] *The Voice of China* heads the speech "An address to the United States **House of Representatives**." **Dropped as unsupported:** the pool's "delivered without notes."
- **2 March, Madison Square Garden.** [A] The pool pinned 40.7505, -73.9934, the **current** Garden, opened 1968. The 1943 rally was at the **third** Garden, Eighth Avenue between 49th and 50th, ~**40.7625, -73.9855**.
- **March, San Francisco City Hall** [R], not carried for want of a firmer date. **4 April, Hollywood Bowl**: crowds reported as large as **30,000**, the largest of the tour. [A] **June, Parliament Hill, Ottawa**: the Canadian House of Commons. [R, record not fetched]
- **November, the Cairo Conference**: she interprets. [A] **Correction:** the sessions were held **not at the Mena House** but at "a residence of Alexander Comstock Kirk, the American ambassador to Egypt, near the Giza pyramid complex," 22-26 November. [A] The pool's upgrade of her role to "effective co-negotiator ... shaping the postwar settlement" is editorial inflation and is dropped.

## 9. Defeat, Taiwan, New York, 1945-2003

- **28 August 1945**: **Mao Zedong** flies into Chongqing, forty-three days of face-to-face negotiation, their first meeting in eighteen years; she is resident in the capital throughout. [A, corpus `mao_zedong`] **1946-1949**: government returns to Nanjing; Meiling Palace is Chiang's official residence. [A]
- **28 November 1948**: departs for the United States. **Marshall, 3 December; Truman, 10 December**; aid refused. [A, zh.wiki] **Correction, twice over:** the pool's chronology lens called the 1948 trip an unverifiable gap, and its geography lens then placed her at **Songshan Airport in December 1949** with the evacuation. She was in America continuously from 28 November 1948 and reached Taiwan on **13 January 1950** (Songshan 25.0697, 121.5522, accepted); the claim that she "followed her husband to Taiwan in 1949" fails for the same reason. Mao entrained for Moscow on **16 December 1949** while the government she represented was being displaced. [A, corpus]
- **Shilin Official Residence, Taipei**, 1950-1975: Chinese and Western gardens, Chiang's "Victory Chapel." [A] Coordinates **25.09505, 121.53037**; pool ~500 m off. **Yangmingshan** retreats [R], not carried.
- **5 April 1975**: Chiang dies; black marble sarcophagus at **Cihu**, Daxi (**24.8422, 121.2993**), temporary pending burial at Fenghua. [A] The pool's geography lens gave 24.8264, 121.3011, contradicting **its own afterlife lens** by 1.8 km; the afterlife value, matching Wikipedia, is used.
- **1975**: breast cancer diagnosis; emigrates to **Lattingtown**, Long Island, the **36-acre** estate being her principal United States residence **to 2000**. The pool tagged this [R] as unconfirmed; it is named in two Wikipedia articles and is upgraded to **[A]**. Coordinates **40.89444, -73.59500**; pool ~2 km off.
- **1988**: returns to Taiwan after **Chiang Ching-kuo's** death. **July 1995**: Capitol Hill reception for the fiftieth anniversary, and her last visit to Taiwan around this time. [A]
- **2000**: sells Lattingtown, moves to **10 Gracie Square**. [A] **Correction:** the pool dated this residence from **1975** and called it a townhouse; it is a **cooperative apartment building**, occupied from 2000.
- **23 October 2003**: dies **in her sleep, in her Manhattan apartment**, aged 105. [A] **Correction:** the pool pinned the death at New York-Presbyterian / Weill Cornell and asserted that "sources disagree." **No source consulted places her in a hospital**; the hedge is retired. **November 2003**: crypt at **Ferncliff Cemetery, Hartsdale** (**41.0275, -73.83222**); the cemetery article does not say which of the three mausoleums. [A] The geography lens again contradicted the afterlife lens, by 1.4 km.
- **Afterlife.** The Ferncliff interment is **pending joint burial with Chiang on the mainland once cross-strait differences are resolved**; unmet. **28 February 2018**: the Cihu chamber is vandalised by pro-independence activists, the coffin thereafter viewed through glass. **Meiling Palace** is a house museum, its basement holding an exhibition of her paintings. Biographies by Hahn (1941), Seagrave (1985), Chang (2019), none read here; the 1997 Hong Kong film *The Soong Sisters*; in fiction a **passing mention** in Malinda Lo's *Last Night at the Telegraph Club* (2021), which the pool overstated as a character role, the story "Cooking for Madame Chiang" in Fiona Sze-Lorrain's *Dear Chrysanthemums* (2023), and a character in Jonas Jonasson's *The 100-Year-Old Man Who Climbed Out the Window and Disappeared*. [A] *Time*'s retroactive "Woman of the Year for 1937," 2019 [R, not checked against Time]. **[R], recorded not endorsed**: that her upbringing left her needing coaching for Chinese audiences; that her longevity was owed to royal jelly.

## 10. Quotations: verified text, corrected citations

Each checked against the full text of the cited item. **All six *Voice of China* page numbers in the pool were inflated by +4.**

**Address to the United States House of Representatives, 18 February 1943** (*The Voice of China*, 1944):

- "We in China, like you, want a better world not for ourselves alone but for all mankind, and we must have it." [A, verbatim, **p.87**; pool gave p.91, which falls inside the Madison Square Garden address of 2 March]
- "We of this generation who are privileged to help make a better world for ourselves and posterity should remember that while we must not be visionary we must have vision." [A, verbatim, **p.87**] The sentence in fact runs on about the scope of the peace; the submitted form is a clean truncation at a natural break.
- "Man's mettle is tested both in adversity and success. Doubly is this true of the soul of a nation." [A, verbatim, **p.88**], the closing sentence. The pool's side-note that later quotations render it "Twice is this true" **could not be verified and is dropped**.

**Speech to the American Volunteer Group, Kunming, 28 February 1942** (same volume; the printed heading confirms date, place, occasion):

- "You have flown across the Pacific in China's gravest hour on wings of hope and faith." [A, verbatim, **p.16**]
- "The whole of the Chinese nation has taken you to its heart and I want you to conduct yourselves in a manner worthy of the great traditions **that you have built up**." [A, **p.18**] The pool cut the final clause and added a full stop, converting "the great traditions" from the Flying Tigers' own record into a vague appeal to Chinese tradition. **Restored.**
- "War is not only a matter of equipment, artillery, ground troops or air force; it is largely a matter of spirit, or morale." [A, **p.19**] "I would like all of you to get up and drink a toast to the two great sister nations of both sides of the Pacific." [A, verbatim, **p.19**]
- "... as you soar into the skies you are writing in letters of flame on the horizon certain eternal truths for the world to see: first, the indomitable courage of the Chinese people; second, the indestructible spirit of the Chinese Army; and third, the deathless soul of the Chinese nation." [A, **p.19**] The pool promoted the mid-sentence clause to a sentence, capitalising "You"; the lead-in is restored.

**"Sian: A Coup d'Etat"** (*General Chiang Kai-shek*, 1937):

- "So, rightly or wrongly, I decided to fight it and strive for a quick, calm and bloodless settlement of the whole affair." [A, verbatim, **p.67**; pool gave p.91] **Place corrected from Xi'an to Nanking**, 13-14 December 1936.

**"China Emergent," *The Atlantic*, May 1942:**

- "Chinese socialism, **if you like to call it that**, seeks above all else to preserve the birthrights of the individual." [A, article read in full] The pool circulated it with the hedge deleted, turning a pointed disavowal of a Western label into a flat declaration. **Restored; tag moved from [R] to [A].**

**Attributed, and left attributed:**

- Eleanor Roosevelt: "She can talk beautifully about democracy, but does not know how to live democracy." [R] Genuine and attested, but the pool's citation chain was wrong. Verbatim in the *Guardian* obituary of 25 October 2003, sourced there to Seagrave's *The Soong Dynasty* (1985) and to the White House dinner at which Madame Chiang, asked how the Generalissimo would handle a coalminers' strike, drew a fingernail across her throat; the AP obituaries of the same day render it "... But **she** does not know." The Wikiquote attribution to Jonathan Fenby is a misread: on that page the Fenby reference belongs to a different quotation. **Tag stays [R]**: a private remark reported at second hand.
- "I came to school in the United States, and I will always consider America my second home." [R] Widely attributed, dated 1995; on Wikiquote without a traceable primary. **Not verified.**

## 11. Contradictions flagged, gaps stated, interlocks

**Not resolved**, each flagged in place above: birthplace (Neishidi vs Tongren Hospital); birth date (4 March 1898 vs 5 March vs 1897); the first meeting with Chiang (1920 vs 1922); the "Man and Wife of the Year" cover (en.wiki's 3 January 1937 against Time's own 19380103 archive entry); Cihu and Ferncliff, where the pool's two lenses disagreed with each other by 1.8 km and 1.4 km. On the death location no genuine dispute exists, and the pool's hedge is retired here so that it is visibly dropped rather than silently.

**Gaps.** *Taiwan, 1950-1975*: no detailed account of twenty-five years was found, so a quarter of her adult life is blank here. *Assassination attempts*: nothing confirmed, nothing asserted. *Britannica*: 403, never read. *The named biographies*: none read. *Her other writings*: only the two books above. *Chinese-language record*: zh.wiki only, and only for specific dates; Taiwanese archives untouched.

**Pins inherited byte-identical:** Chongqing 29.5556, 106.5764 and Republican Shanghai 31.2197, 121.4726 (`mao_zedong`); Washington 38.9072, -77.0369 (`fermi`); Cairo 30.0444, 31.2357 (`gustave_flaubert`); New York 40.713, -74.006 (`buckminster_fuller`).

**Travelers named in the campa**, where the paths genuinely cross: **mao_zedong** (Chongqing, August 1945, attested co-presence; the Moscow train of December 1949 against her New York exile), **matteo_ricci** (Zhalan, the far end of the mission lineage that ends in a Methodist Generalissimo), **gustave_flaubert** (Cairo), **buckminster_fuller** (New York, 1949), **fermi** (Washington). **einstein** (Shanghai, November 1922) was pulled from the first-meeting campa in house-style cleanup (2026-08-14): same port, unrelated year, no documented crossing with her, thematic coincidence only.

**Recorded and not inherited:** the Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris and Buenos Aires are multi-journey canonical pins here, but **no attested link places her at any of the four**. Left alone. All other pins were geocoded fresh, the corpus having no prior coverage of her American education arc or the Nanjing decade.
