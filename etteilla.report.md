# Jean-Baptiste Alliette, called Etteilla (1738-1791)

*Atlas of Journeys research report, 2026-08-09, from an adversarially verified pool. Slug `etteilla`. Companion file `etteilla.journey.json`: 7 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including anything the sources hedge or contradict. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved; gaps are stated as gaps.

**The shape of the problem.** This life is documented almost entirely by title pages: a birth certificate (year only), a marriage (year only), a death (full date), and about twenty imprints between 1770 and 1791. Nothing consulted cites a letter, an account book, a manuscript, a portrait, or an address for shop, school, society, deathbed, or grave. The rest is what he printed about himself, what a hostile occultist wrote sixty years later, and tarot literature repeating both. The verification pass produced more corrections than confirmations.

## 1. Origins: Les Halles, 1738 to c. 1760

**Birth. [A] year, [R] day, [R] parish, forename contested.**
Born in Paris in 1738 on the authority of the birth certificate, cited by en.wikipedia, whose body text says only "the birth certificate recording his birth in Paris in 1738" while its lead asserts "1 March 1738". **Wikidata carries P569 at YEAR precision and the death P570 at full day precision**: the project that models precision declines to assert a birth day. The journey file defaults the day and puts the warning in `date_confidence`.

**The forename is contested and not resolved here.** en and fr Wikipedia give **Jean-Baptiste Alliette**; de.wikipedia redirects "Etteilla" to **Jean-Francois Alliette**, and Wikidata's P735 resolves to Jean-Francois, keeping "Jean-Baptiste Alliette le Jeune" as an alias only. The file uses Jean-Baptiste and states the split in the opening campa.

**Parents. [A].** Father a *maitre rotisseur*, mother a *marchande grainiere*. Accounts of the father's trade conflict; flagged, not harmonized.

**Baptismal parish. [R].** The geography lens pinned the birth at **Eglise Saint-Eustache** (48.8639, 2.3447; Wikidata 48.86333, 2.34500, about seventy metres off, acceptable) and tagged it [A] while conceding that no baptismal register had been reproduced. Retagged **[R]**: the parish is what the family's Halles trades imply, and no source names it. *Lens disagreement recorded rather than silently fixed:* the afterlife lens pinned the same event at generic Paris (48.8566, 2.3522). The file follows geography and says so.

**Seed trade. [R], proposed dates invented.** fr.wikipedia writes only *"il aurait d'abord ete, comme sa mere, marchand grainier"*, in the conditional. The pool proposed 1750 (he would be twelve); the geography lens proposed "c.1755-1767". **No consulted source dates his entry into the trade.** Only the exit is dated, and hedged: *"environ 1768"*. The file pins 1755 with the invention flagged. **Gaps:** no school, master, apprenticeship, first employment, named companion, or address; nothing places him anywhere before 1757.

## 2. Rouen, 1757: the origin story he tells about himself

**[A] as his own printed testimony, [R] as event.** In the second cahier (1785) he writes that at Rouen in 1757 he met a Parisian named **Lecomte, called le Voyageur**, who found him at French card-fortunetelling and told him of a man who did as much with large cards. The trail leads on, in his telling, to **Lamballe** and a reader named **Alexis**, with the study running 1757 to 1765.

*The transcription:* the pool silently modernized the passage. What Steve Mangan actually posted reads *"Erant a Rouen"* (not *Etant*), *"sur ce qui'il me vis occupe"*, *"il me dis"*, *Francoise* unaccented. The journey file reproduces it **as posted** and notes that the cahier itself was not examined. *The "aged Piedmontese":* repeated everywhere in English tarot literature for his teacher, but the wording could not be traced to any verified translation. Tagged **[R]**, mentioned in campa as literature rather than quoted. The episode rests on one self-report published twenty-eight years later, inside a book selling the method it describes; no corroboration for meeting, man, or year.

## 3. Marriage and change of trade, 1763 to 1768

**Marriage. [A] event, [R] location.** He marries **Jeanne Vattier** in 1763 (en.wikipedia); it is said to last about five years. No parish, church, witnesses, month, or separation act. The geography lens pinned it at 48.856, 2.352, admitted this was a placeholder, and still tagged the pin [A]; retagged **[R]** for the location. **Correction carried:** the middle name **"Jeanne-Vincente"** appears only in that lens's prose and nowhere consulted, and was dropped.

**Prints. [A] with a hedged year, [R] on the site.** He becomes a *marchand d'estampes* around 1768 (fr.wikipedia, *environ*). The **Pont Notre-Dame / Quai de Gesvres** siting is reconstruction from the geography of the Paris print trade; no stall, lease, or tax record was found.

## 4. The first books, 1770 to 1773

**1770. [A] imprint; the "pen name" claim is WRONG and is corrected.**
*Etteilla, ou maniere de se recreer avec un jeu de cartes* (Amsterdam; Paris: Lesclapart, 1770). The pool said it appeared under a pen name formed by reversing his surname. It did not: **the title page reads "par M.***", anonymous asterisks**, as en.wikipedia's list gives it verbatim. "Etteilla" is in the TITLE from 1770; as a signature it comes later (1783: *l'auteur de la Cartonomancie*; the 1784 *Epitre* signs off *salut, Etteilla*). The afterlife lens repeated the error; corrected at both stops.

en.wikipedia credits this book with introducing **card spreads and reversed-position meanings**. The pool dated the reversals to 1783. **Reversals date to 1770**; what is attested by 1783 is the term *cartonomancie*. The claim was split accordingly.

**The Amsterdam imprint. [A] as type, [R] as interpretation.** French printers used foreign false imprints routinely, and no source places Alliette in the Dutch Republic in any year. The pin marks a line of type, not a stop on a road.

**Self-dating. [A] as printed claim.** The 1770 preface: *"j'ose avouer que depuis 16 ans j'ai ete le maitre de ceux & de celles qui ont fait le plus de bruit en ce genre"*, dating his practice to about 1754, three years before the Rouen meeting he later makes foundational. No independent record of it exists.

**1772 and 1773. [A].** *Le Zodiaque mysterieux, ou les oracles d'Etteilla* and *Etteilla, ou la seule maniere de tirer les cartes*, both through Lesclapart, held in one stop. **The 1770s practice, [R] entirely:** consultant and teacher, asserted only in general terms, with the **Palais-Royal** siting documented for the milieu and not for him. No lease, client name, fee, or address.

## 5. The Egyptian turn, 1781 to 1785

**Court de Gebelin, 1781. [A] event, [R] priority.** Volume eight of *Le Monde primitif* proposes the tarot as a fragment of ancient Egypt. Alliette absorbs the theory and disputes its ownership, claiming he reached Egypt independently and earlier. The priority is unresolvable from anything consulted.

**1782, and the professor of algebra. [A], one spelling fix and one upgrade.** The title is *Instruction sur la combinaison **hislerique**, extraite du Loto des Indiens*, not "hysterique"; both en and fr give *hislerique*. The pool called "professeur d'algebre" an undocumented self-designation; it is **better sourced than that**, printed in the 1782 title itself, with fr.wikipedia adding that he presented himself the same way inside his own Tarot. What stays open is whether any institution taught or certified him. **Correction:** the geography lens had this styling running across his title pages 1770 to 1791; two attestations are not a career-long practice, and the 1770 book was anonymous.

**1783. [A].** First and third cahiers of *Maniere de se recreer avec le jeu de cartes nommees tarots* (l'auteur; Merigot; Legras; Segault), plus *L'Homme a projets* and *Etteilla ou instructions sur l'art de tirer les cartes*. **1784. [A].** *Epitre a M. Court de Gebelin*, **the only imprint of his career dated 1784**.

**1785. [A], one title dropped.** Second and fourth cahiers, **chez l'auteur**; *Fragment sur les hautes sciences*; *Philosophie des hautes sciences* (six booksellers, Segaut among them). **Dropped: *L'Astrologie du Livre de Toth***, which appears in en.wikipedia only inside a garbled entry describing a **1993 Editions Tredaniel reprint** of the fourth cahier and is absent from fr.wikipedia.

**Contradictions here, flagged and open.** (1) *The span:* en.wikipedia supports 1783-1785, de.wikipedia 1783-1784, and the imprints show cahiers I and III in 1783, II and IV in 1785, with **no part of the work dated 1784**; the pool's filler entry dated 1784 was deleted. (2) *The printers:* Segault is on the 1783 imprints and among the 1785 *Philosophie* publishers, but the 1785 cahiers were published by *l'auteur*, so crediting the whole work to Segault flattens a documented shift to self-publication; **Legras appears on no imprint after 1783**.

**Corpus contemporaries, same city and years [R]:** the Mesmer commission at the Louvre (11 August 1784), Rose de Beauharnais at rue Thevenot from 1781, Rene Hauy across the span. No documented contact; see section 9.

## 6. Lodges, societies, the deck, 1786 to 1789

**1786, the Masonic denial. [A] in substance, [R] in date.** In the "Petit avant-propos pour le denier du pauvre" of *Les Sept nuances de l'oeuvre philosophique-hermetique* he denies being a Freemason, granting *"pour tout ce qui est de vraie Maconnerie, autant de respect que puisse en avoir un Frere"* and dismissing *"toutes les petites denominations de Loges et de grades"*. **Date precision overstated in the pool:** en.wikipedia gives the imprint as **s.l. [Paris], s.d. [1786]**, so 1786 is a cataloguer's bracket, not a printed year.

**1787. [A] by listing only.** Metoposcopy and palmistry works; no copy examined.

**1788. [A].** *Jeu des tarots, ou le livre de Thot*, issued by **Mad. veuve Lesclapart**, widow of the printer of the 1770 book. Same year, the **Societe des Interpretes du Livre de Thot**, a postal network rather than a physical lodge. **Gap:** no address, membership list, rule, or correspondence located.

**The deck, 1788-89. [A], date and hedge corrected.** Seventy-eight cards, a *melange* of the Tarot de Marseille and Egyptomane influences (fr.wikipedia), made for divination rather than play. *Date:* fr.wikipedia gives *"En 1788-89, il fit imprimer sa version du Tarot"* against 1789 flat in en and de, so it is presented as a span. *The pool's claim that "Grand Etteilla" is undocumented is wrong:* fr.wikipedia names three patterns, **Grand Etteilla** (78), **Petit Etteilla** (32), **Nouvel Etteilla** (54, credited to Gabryann Myrddin); hedge removed. *Superlative kept tight:* fr.wikipedia's "Cartomancie" names the *Jeu de cartomancie pour l'amusement des dames*, registered **15 April 1788**, as the first purpose-built divination deck, so his is the first **tarot** for divination, not the first divining deck. *Lens contradiction flagged:* the afterlife lens said the deck "breaks from" the Marseille pattern while fr.wikipedia says it blends with it; the file follows *melange*.

## 7. Revolution and death, 1789 to 1791

**1789. [A] for the city, gap for the man.** The Bastille falls a mile east of the print quarter. **No source records how, or whether, the Revolution touched his practice.** The pin uses the coordinate canonical in `marques_de_lafayette.journey.json`.

**1790. [A], one full date.** *Cours theorique et pratique du livre de Thot*, assigning planetary, zodiacal, and elemental correspondences and dividing the deck into what later writers call Major and Minor Arcana. On **1 July 1790** he founds the **Nouvelle Ecole de Magie**, with its prospectus. Address, enrollment, curriculum: gaps.

**1791. [A] by title only.** *Journal projetique et patriotique*; no issues located, contents a gap.

**Death. [A].** Paris, **12 December 1791**, full precision across sources and in Wikidata. **Gaps:** cause, house, street, will, inventory, attendants, estate. Nothing follows Jeanne Vattier after the separation.

**Burial. [R], the strongest gap in the file.** No cemetery, tomb, or grave in en.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia, data.bnf.fr, or Find a Grave memorial 270517479, which carries no burial location. The closure of old-regime parish cemeteries in these years is an explanation, not a record. Any claimed tomb site is unverified.

## 8. Afterlife

**Institutional survival. [A].** Copies of the manuals, cahiers, and Book of Thoth treatise are held through the royal and later national library on the rue de Richelieu, listed in data.bnf.fr. **No manuscript, letter, or account book of his is recorded there.**

**Egyptology arrives. [A] as events, [R] as juxtaposition.** Bouchard at Rosetta, **16 July 1799**, and Champollion's decipherment; the 1798 campaign and the *Description de l'Egypte*. Real Egyptology begins within a decade of his death and never finds his tarot in any Egyptian source. The causal reading is this file's.

**Lenormand. [R].** Later tradition credits Etteilla's method as her direct influence and sometimes makes her his pupil. No documented meeting, no record close to the events; the claim comes mostly from her own self-promotion. She was a teenager in Paris in his last years, which is the whole of it.

**Reprints and patterns. [A] for the decks, [R] for the chains.** *Petit Etteilla* manuals and decks keep printing under various publishers; the **Julia Orsini** manuals were not re-verified. **Correction:** the *Nouvel Etteilla* of Gabryann Myrddin carries **no date** in fr.wikipedia and nothing places Myrddin in the nineteenth century, so the pool's "Through the 19th century" framing was dropped.

**Eliphas Levi, where the pool needed most repair.** The three most-quoted English sentences proved misquoted, misattributed, or mis-edited:

| As circulated | Status | What the books say |
|---|---|---|
| "Etteilla or Alliette, an illuminated hairdresser, exclusively engrossed by his divinatory system, and the emolument he could derive from it..." | **Fabricated wording. In no English edition of Levi.** Checked against Waite 1896 and three copies of the revised Waite. | *Transcendental Magic*, Ritual ch. XXII, p. 358, reads: "Etteilla or Alliette, preoccupied entirely by his system of divination and the material profit to be derived from it, Alliette, formerly barber, having never learned French or even orthography..." |
| "This illuminated hairdresser, after working for thirty years, only succeeded in producing a bastard set, the Keys of which are transposed..." | **Words confirmed verbatim; the BOOK is wrong.** | Not *Transcendental Magic*. **The History of Magic**, trans. Waite, Bk VI ch. V. Verified in IA `historyofmagicin00lv` (1922), `eliphas-levi-the-history-of-magic`, `in.gov.ignca.7242`. |
| "The writings of Etteilla, now very rare, are obscure, wearisome and barbarous in style." | **Words confirmed; the EDITION is wrong.** | Waite's **revised** *Transcendental Magic*, Doctrine ch. XXI, p. 164 (Rider, 1923). The 1896 translation reads "obscure, wearisome, and in style barbarous." |

**The French line and its date. [R], contradiction unresolved.** fr.wikipedia has Levi describing him as *"un ancien coiffeur n'ayant jamais appris ni le francais ni l'orthographe"*, a **former** hairdresser, not "an old hairdresser". The wording the geography lens quoted, *"un vieux perruquier qui n'a jamais su ni le francais ni l'orthographe"*, **does not appear there at all**; it conflates that sentence with the separate, unattributed *"D'autres sources en font un perruquier."* The Levi line carries **no footnote**: no work, no year, no page. So **neither 1856 nor 1860 is supported by the cited source**, and the pool's two lenses contradicted each other. The file keeps two Levi stops, one per book, and states the contradiction rather than picking a winner.

**The trade contradiction, unresolved:** Levi's barber, the *perruquier* of "other sources", the print-seller of the imprints, the seed merchant of the family trade. Find a Grave calls the barber story unsupported legend.

**Museum. [A], coordinate and figure corrected.** Musee francais de la carte a jouer, 16 rue Auguste-Gervais, Issy-les-Moulineaux. Coordinate corrected from the geography lens's 48.8236/2.2705 to Wikidata's **48.82278/2.27333**, about two hundred metres. Holdings corrected: fr.wikipedia gives over 11,000 sets including more than 7,000 card games and more than 1,000 prints, drawings and posters, not "thousands" of prints. Etteilla is not named in the article.
