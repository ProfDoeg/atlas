# Jane Morris, nee Burden (1839-1914)

*2026-08-11. Slug `jane_morris`. Companion: `jane_morris.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction or inference; nothing reached this session supports it directly. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**The shape of the problem.** Jane Morris is one of the most reproduced faces of the nineteenth century and one of its thinnest first-person records. Nearly everything below is attested through somebody else's paper: her husband's letters, Rossetti's canvases, Georgiana Burne-Jones's memoir, Mackail's biography, her lover's diaries. Her own voice enters the record in bulk only in 2012.

## 1. Oxford origins, 1839-1857

- Born **19 October 1839** in Oxford to **Robert Burden**, a stableman, and **Ann Maizey**. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]**
- Birthplace given by the pool as **St Helen's Passage, off Holywell Street** (51.7539, -1.2517) **[A - same]**. The atlas already carries the family at **Brazier Passage, Holywell** (51.7549, -1.2503 / -1.2508). **Contradiction flagged:** two addresses about 150 m apart, both "off Holywell." The file inherits the atlas pin rather than adding a third coordinate to one quarter, and says so.
- Younger sister **Elizabeth ("Bessie") Burden**, born 13 December 1841 in the same passage. **[A - `elizabeth_bessie_burden.journey.json`]**
- Family poverty in a service quarter behind the colleges. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]** The stables at 65 Holywell Street come from the sister's file, carried **[R]**, the underlying document not re-checked.
- **October 1857:** Jane and Bessie attend a Drury Lane touring performance; **Rossetti** and **Burne-Jones**, in the city for the Union murals, notice her and ask her to sit. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris" and "Dante Gabriel Rossetti"; venue and date from `elizabeth_bessie_burden.journey.json`]**
- **Contradiction flagged:** `william_morris.journey.json` dates the discovery to **August 1857** at the Union itself; the sister's file gives **October 1857** at the theatre. One is wrong about the month, or the sighting and the first sitting are collapsed differently in each. The file orders the stops October then November.
- She sits for the murals, most often as **Guenevere** for Rossetti **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]**; Morris paints her as **La Belle Iseult** and inscribes it "I cannot paint you, but I love you" **[A - `william_morris.journey.json`]**.
- **Coordinate note.** The pool proposed 51.7548, -1.2544 for the Union, corrected to **51.7531, -1.2597**. The atlas pins it at **51.7527, -1.2585** in two files, inherited unchanged per the interlock rule.

## 3. Red House, 1860-1865

- **Red House, Bexleyheath**, by **Philip Webb**, occupied **1860-1865**. **[A - Wikipedia, "Red House, Bexleyheath" and "Jane Morris"]**
- **Worst coordinate error in the pool.** It gave 51.4477, 0.1486, about 1.4 km off, in open ground south-east of the house. The correct value, **51.4556, 0.1303**, is exactly the pin the atlas already carries. Inherited byte-identical.
- **Jane Alice ("Jenny") Morris** born **17 January 1861** at Red House **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]**, confirmed by Morris's letter of 18 January 1861 to Ford Madox Brown: "Kid having appeared, Mrs. Brown kindly says she will stay till Monday... Janey and kid (girl) are both very well." **[A - Mackail, *The Life of William Morris* (1899), Ch. V, via Wikisource]**
- The christening line, "Janey and I went together with a candle to look at the beds strewn about the drawing room for the men," is attributed by Mackail to an **unidentified guest**. **[A as to text - Mackail, Ch. V]** Not her speech; the file says so in `quote_source`. The **month** is not in Mackail; 1861-02-01 is an inference. **[R]**
- **Mary ("May") Morris** born **25 March 1862** at Red House, named for the Feast of the Annunciation. **[A - Wikipedia, "May Morris"]**
- **Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co.** founded **11 April 1861** at **8 Red Lion Square**, with Rossetti, Burne-Jones, Ford Madox Brown, Webb and P. P. Marshall; Jane and Bessie do the embroidery. **[A - both atlas files, identical date and pin]**
- The **Legend of Good Women** hangings at Red House, embroidered by the sisters, are pinned by the atlas at 1863. **[R as to year - the reachable sources do not date them]** **Distinction flagged:** these (1860s, Chaucer, unfinished) and the **Castle Howard panels** (1880s, three panels, Chaucer and Tennyson) are two commissions on a related subject. The pool conflated them under the single year 1880; Wikipedia gives only "the 1880s" for Castle Howard. The file keeps them separate and hedges the later date.
- Red House given up **1865**; the family moves to **26 Queen Square, Bloomsbury**, over the Firm's workshops. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"; pin 51.522, -0.123 inherited byte-identical]**

## 4. The Rossetti decade, 1865-1874

- **7 June 1865:** photographic session at **16 Cheyne Walk, Chelsea**, two exposures indoors and the rest in the garden, Rossetti directing poses, clothing and props. **[A - Wikipedia, "John Robert Parsons"]**
- **Correction.** The pool credited the photographs to **Rossetti as photographer**, pinned them at 51.4875, -0.1687, and hedged the location as unconfirmed. The photographer was **John Robert Parsons**; Rossetti directed and did not operate; 16 Cheyne Walk **is** confirmed; the coordinate is **51.4823, -0.1727**. All carried into the file.
- **1868: The Blue Silk Dress**; **1868: Pia de' Tolomei** begun, reworked until 1880. **[A - Wikipedia, "Dante Gabriel Rossetti"]** Pia is from **Dante**, *Purgatorio* V.
- A romantic relationship with Rossetti develops in the **late 1860s**. **[R - described by Wikipedia, "Jane Morris", but no document reached this session dates its beginning, and neither party recorded it]**
- **1869: Bad Ems**, for the waters. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]**
- **Correction.** The pool said "Rossetti paints her there." Rossetti **was not at Bad Ems**: the 1869 work is a drawing, made in England, of **William** Morris reading to Jane while she takes the cure, and it was William who went with her. Coordinate corrected to **50.3381, 7.7106**.
- **The dream passage**, Georgiana Burne-Jones, *Memorials of Edward Burne-Jones*, Vol. II (1904), chapter heading "1868-1871", verified against archive.org OCR: Jane dreams she is alone in a market place when a coach draws up and "there descended from it a little old woman with white hair, who was I ... and she said to me, 'They are all gone, Georgie' - and we wept together." **[A]**
- **Attribution corrected.** The pool read the line as spoken **to** Jane. The old woman is explicitly **Georgiana** ("who was I") and "Georgie" is her nickname, so the speaker is **Jane Morris**. These are among the very few recorded words of hers before 2012. Neither year nor place of the telling is given; the file pins it at Queen Square, 1869-01-01, inside the chapter's range. **[R as to date and place]**
- **Kelmscott Manor:** joint tenancy taken by Morris and Rossetti. **[A as to the fact - Wikipedia, "Kelmscott Manor"]** **Contradiction flagged:** Wikipedia's "Kelmscott Manor" and "Jane Morris" give **1871**; its "Dante Gabriel Rossetti" gives **1869**. The atlas carries 1871-06-01; the file uses 1871 and records the disagreement.
- **July 1871:** Morris's first Iceland voyage; Jane and Rossetti spend the summer at the manor **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris" and "Dante Gabriel Rossetti"]**, where Rossetti paints **Water Willow** with the manor behind her **[A - Wikipedia, "Kelmscott Manor"]**.
- **June 1872:** Rossetti's breakdown and laudanum suicide attempt; he comes to Kelmscott that autumn. **[A - Wikipedia, "Dante Gabriel Rossetti"]** **Factual error corrected:** the pool's 1872 entry asserted Morris was in Iceland. His **only two** Iceland journeys were **July 1871** and **July 1873**; he was in England in 1872, and the file says so.
- **November 1872:** Morris's letter to **Aglaia Coronio** from Queen Square. **[A - `elizabeth_bessie_burden.journey.json`, date and pin inherited]** The plainest contemporary statement of the triangle, and it is the husband's. Jane's side of 1872 is a **gap**.
- **23 January 1873:** Morris takes **Horrington House, Turnham Green**: "it is a very little house with a pretty garden, and I think will suit Janey and the children." **[A - Mackail, Ch. IX, verified verbatim via Wikisource, "very" italicised in the original]**
- **Source framing corrected.** The pool attached this line to **Kelmscott House, Hammersmith**. The full sentence shows the house is **Horrington House on the Turnham Green road**, occupied 1873-1878; the letter's "Hammersmith" is the **railway station**. Kelmscott House was taken only in 1878. **Coordinate 51.492, -0.256 is approximate**: the house is demolished and its frontage was not established. Flagged in `date_confidence`.
- **July 1873:** Morris's second Iceland voyage; Rossetti again at the manor, painting her. **[A - Wikipedia, "Dante Gabriel Rossetti"]**
- **Label corrected.** The pool titled the 1873 entry "Rossetti and Alexa Wilding sittings." Wikipedia, "Alexa Wilding" contains **nothing** placing her at Kelmscott in 1873; the attribution is dropped.
- **1870: Mariana**; **1874: Proserpine** **[A - Wikipedia, "Dante Gabriel Rossetti"]**, merged into one Chelsea stop at the later date, the merge declared in `date_confidence`. **July 1874:** Rossetti leaves Kelmscott abruptly and never returns, after Morris restructures the Firm. **[A - same]**

## 5. Italy and Hammersmith, 1876-1882

- **1876, Oneglia (now Imperia), Liguria:** Jenny suffers a severe epileptic seizure; Morris travels out from England. **[A - Wikipedia, "William Morris"]** No month given. **[R as to month]** The family travels on through **Venice, Padua and Verona**; order and months inferred. **[A as to cities, R as to sequence]**
- **Rome and Florence, c. 1880**, with her daughters, appear in the pool as tradition from secondary biography (Marsh). Nothing reachable confirms the itinerary. **[R]** **Both are omitted from the journey file**, deliberately, and recorded here.
- **October 1878:** the family takes 26 Upper Mall, Hammersmith, renamed **Kelmscott House**. **[A - Wikipedia, "Kelmscott House"]** **Contradiction flagged:** Wikipedia, "William Morris" gives **April 1879** for the same move. The pin 51.4905, -0.235 is inherited byte-identical from `william_morris.journey.json`, which also supplies the `edward_bellamy` interlock: *News from Nowhere* was written here as an answer to *Looking Backward*.
- **9 April 1882:** Rossetti dies at Birchington-on-Sea, Kent, of Bright's disease. **[A - Wikipedia, "Dante Gabriel Rossetti", lead and infobox]** **Internal contradiction flagged:** the same article's body says 10 April. The lead is followed. Village coordinate 51.3766, 1.3086 **[R - the bungalow was not located]**.
- **Causal sequence corrected.** The pool had Jane distancing herself **after** his death, on learning of the chloral. Wikipedia, "Jane Morris" has the discovery leading her away "although they stayed in touch until he died in 1882." The distancing **preceded** the death by years, and the file states it in that order.

## 6. Blunt, and the later work, 1883-1896

- **1883:** meets **Wilfrid Scawen Blunt** at a house party hosted by **Rosalind Howard**. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]** The venue is **not named** in any reachable source. **[R]**
- The Blunt stops are pinned at **Crabbet Park, Sussex (51.115, -0.155)**, the pool's corrected value; its original 51.0964, -0.1633 lay about 2 km off. **These are reconstruction pins.** Nothing consulted documents her at Crabbet Park or at **Newbuildings Place**, Blunt's residence after the 1906 separation and the likelier site of a late visit.
- **By 1887** the two are lovers; **1894** the sexual relationship ends and the friendship continues. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]**
- **1880s:** the three **Castle Howard** panels of Chaucer's and Tennyson's heroines, made with Bessie Burden. **[A as to decade and panels - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]** The pin 54.1214, -0.9058 and the year 1885 come from `elizabeth_bessie_burden.journey.json`; **the year is an atlas placeholder, not a documented date**, and is flagged as such.
- **14 June 1890:** May Morris marries **Henry Halliday Sparling** at Fulham Register Office. **[A - Wikipedia, "May Morris"]** Coordinate 51.479, -0.1978 is the civic offices, **approximate [R]**. The file's statement that the marriage lasted under five years is standard in the literature but was **not** verified. **[R]**
- **3 October 1896:** William Morris dies at **Kelmscott House**, Hammersmith. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"; `william_morris.journey.json`]** **Pin contradiction corrected:** the pool carried an entry titled "Kelmscott Manor (William Morris's death)" at the **manor's** coordinates while its own text correctly placed the death in west London.
- **6 October 1896:** burial at **St George's, Kelmscott**, coffin carried from Lechlade by hay wain, tomb by **Philip Webb**. **[A - `william_morris.journey.json`; Wikipedia, "Kelmscott"]**

## 7. Widowhood and death, 1897-1914

- **1903-1905:** Evelyn De Morgan paints a portrait study (1904) and works her likeness into *The Love Potion* (1903) and *The Hourglass* (1905). **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]** Sitting place unrecorded; generic London pin.
- **1913:** she buys **Kelmscott Manor** outright, for Jenny and May. **[A - Wikipedia, "Kelmscott Manor" and "Jane Morris"]** No month given. **[R as to month]**
- **26 January 1914:** dies aged 74 at **5 Brock Street, Bath**. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]** **Coordinate corrected**: the pool's 51.3841, -2.3634 falls south of the Circus toward Gay Street; Brock Street runs between the Circus and the Royal Crescent, near **51.3864, -2.3655**. **Cause of death: gap**, not stated in any source reached.
- Buried at **St George's, Kelmscott**, January 1914. **[A - same]** The **day** is not recorded; 1914-01-31 is an inference, flagged. **Whether her marker matches Webb's design for her husband's tomb is not stated anywhere reachable. Gap.**
- Repeated Bath cures through the 1890s and 1900s, proposed in the pool as a pattern, are **[R]**; only the final stay is attested, and no stop is given them.

## 8. Afterlife

- **1962:** after May Morris's bequest of the manor to Oxford University, conditional on preserving the contents and admitting the public, the university declines and ownership passes to the **Society of Antiquaries of London**. **[A - Wikipedia, "Kelmscott Manor"]**
- **2012:** *The Collected Letters of Jane Morris*, ed. **Frank C. Sharp and Jan Marsh** (Boydell and Brewer), the first scholarly edition. **[A - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]**
- **2013:** the National Portrait Gallery mounts **"Janey Morris: Pre-Raphaelite Muse."** **[A as to title and year - same]** **Not reachable:** the NPG page returned **HTTP 403**; dates, curator and contents unconfirmed, and the file says so.
- **April 2022:** the manor reopens after a restoration of roughly six million pounds funded by the National Lottery Heritage Fund and a public campaign; Thursday to Saturday in summer, about 26,000 visitors a year. **[A - Wikipedia, "Kelmscott Manor"]**
- **Astarte Syriaca** is **1875-1877**, not 1875-1879 as the pool had it. **[A, corrected]** No stop given.
- Modern reassessment: Marsh, *Pre-Raphaelite Women* (1987); Mancoff, *Jane Morris* (2000); Parkins, *Jane Morris: The Burden of History* (2013). **[A as a list - Wikipedia, "Jane Morris"]** **None was read this session.** Her face read as the emblem of Pre-Raphaelitism itself, rather than one woman's likeness, is later construction. **[R - no citation located]**
