# Benigno Benjamín Villanueva (Villanokoff), c. 1815 - 1872/1875
*Research report, 2026-08-12. Argentine duellist, Rosista cavalry officer, mercenary in the Mexican-American War, Gold Rush shopkeeper, Spanish observer at Sinop, and (as the tradition has it) general of the Russian Empire.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named and checked · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-chronicle claim with no independent support · every date given in the journey file carries its own hedge in `date_confidence`.

---

## 0. The evidentiary situation, stated before the biography

This is the rare atlas subject where the honest report has to be written before the life, not after it, because the life may not have happened.

- The **entire narrative** derives from one text: **Pastor S. Obligado, "Soldado argentino, general en Rusia," in *Tradiciones Argentinas* (Barcelona: Montaner y Simón, 1903)** **[A: verified at primary, Internet Archive item `tradicionesargen00obliuoft`, via search-inside; the four quoted passages sit at scan pp. 148, 152, 153, 154]**. Obligado is a *tradicionista*, a writer of national legends in the Palma manner, publishing at least thirty years after the last events he describes and an ocean from any Russian archive.
- Every later telling is downstream of Obligado: Estanislao F. Garay in *La Nación*, 26 March 1972 **[A: cited in es.wikipedia's bibliography; the article page itself not reached, see Sources]**; Isidoro Gilbert, *El oro de Moscú* (2011); the Spanish Wikipedia article "Benigno Benjamín Villanueva" that assembles them.
- **Isidoro Gilbert, who preserves the fullest Argentine version of the story, judges it legend rather than documented history** **[A]**.
- **Aleksandr I. Sizonenko, *Rodina*, 1995, nos. 3-4, p. 86, reports that he found no evidence in Russian archives that Villanueva ever existed** **[A]**.
- **Vicente Cutolo's *Nuevo diccionario biográfico argentino* calls the man "Benito" Villanueva, not Benigno** **[A: recorded at footnote 3 of the es.wikipedia article]**. The reference works do not agree on his given name.

So: the subject is carried in the atlas at the register the house uses for national mythology, with the canon told straight and the evidence stated plainly beside it. Nothing below should be read as a claim that the archives support this man. They do not. What the archives support is the *tradition*, which is real, dated, printed, and traceable to a single page range in Barcelona in 1903.

## 1. Buenos Aires: birth, schooling, and the duel (1815-1835)

- **Born 1815 in the barrio of San Nicolás, Buenos Aires city**, son of **Miguel de Villanueva** and **Rafaela Lozada y Reyes** **[R: es.wikipedia after Obligado; no baptismal entry produced in any consulted source]**. The father is said to have fought in the 1807 defence of Buenos Aires against the English invasions and later with the Army of the Andes **[R]**.
  - **Correction carried forward from the research pool:** an earlier draft placed the birth at **San Nicolás de los Arroyos** (-33.3333, -60.2167), a town 230 km up the Paraná. The source says *barrio* de San Nicolás, a neighbourhood of the capital, the same one that contains the Iglesia de la Merced of the 1835 duel. The journey file uses the canonical Buenos Aires pin.
- **Schooled at the establishments of Rufino Sánchez and of Acosta** **[R: es.wikipedia; no year given]**. An honest gap: nothing dates the schooling, and it matters only because the tradition makes the duelling opponent a schoolmate.
- **April 1835: at the old Café de los Catalanes, beside the Iglesia de la Merced, a quarrel over a cigar during a game of billiards ends in a sabre duel and the death of a former schoolmate** **[R: es.wikipedia after Obligado]**. The month, the café, the church, and the cigar are given; **the opponent's name is not**, and no criminal record has been produced.
  - **Correction:** one draft called the dead man a "rival officer." He was not; Villanueva was a civilian, which is the point of the sentence that follows.
- **Sentenced to serve as a common soldier** **[R]**. Rosas had resumed the governorship of Buenos Aires with the *suma del poder público* in March 1835 **[A: standard Argentine political chronology]**, which is the political weather of the sentence but not evidence for it.

## 2. The Rosista cavalry (1835-1842)

- **By 1839, cavalry lieutenant commanding the second squadron of the government escort** **[R]**. Four years from ranker to the governor's guard, with no promotion order, patron, or engagement named.
- **Combat of Tapalqué, 20 August 1839** **[R]**, frontier service on the line of forts.
- **Battle of Chascomús, 7 November 1839** **[A: the battle; R: his presence]**. The rising of the southern estancieros against Rosas, broken on the shore of the laguna. Villanueva is on the Federal side.
  - **Correction:** one draft dated Chascomús to **November 1847**, an eight-year error that also corrupted a stated "1839-1847" service range and would have placed him in Buenos Aires province while the same account has him in Mexico. Chascomús is 1839.
- **Battle of Rodeo del Medio, Mendoza, 24 September 1841**, serving under **General Ángel Pacheco** **[A: the battle; R: his presence]**.
- **Corrientes frontier service**, no date, no unit, no engagement **[R: es.wikipedia, unsourced within the article]**. Stated here as the gap it is.

## 3. The littoral and the siege of Montevideo (1843-1851)

- **Siege of Montevideo, 16 February 1843 to 8 October 1851** **[A: the siege]**. Villanueva is said to serve first with the besiegers, under **Nicolás Granada, Ángel Pacheco and Manuel Oribe**, then to change sides and serve inside the city under **General José María Paz** **[R]**. The sources record the switch without explaining it.
  - **Correction:** the siege is eight years and nearly eight months, not "nine years."
- **Corrientes, 1844, on Paz's staff** **[R]**, the setting of the only two remarks the tradition preserves in his own voice.
- **Contradiction, flagged and not resolved:** the same account keeps Villanueva in the Montevideo lines through the whole encirclement (to 1851) *and* has Santa Anna's agents hire him away to Mexico in 1846. Both statements stand in the same file with nothing between them.

### The two Corrientes quotes: attribution corrected

Both are commonly cited to **José María Paz, *Memorias póstumas*** (Emecé, 2000). **That attribution is wrong.** Both were traced to **Obligado 1903**:

1. **"La instrucción de este ejército se parece á la de un hombre que hubiese aprendido aritmética sin saber leer ni escribir."** Obligado 1903, scan **p. 152**, reported as Villanueva's own appraisal of Madariaga's army **[A at primary; modernized "a" for "á" in most modern citations]**.
2. **"joven de un talento muy despejado."** Obligado 1903, scan **p. 153**, one page later and part of the same passage: Obligado calls the man who made the arithmetic remark a young man of a very clear talent and adds that he was right **[A at primary]**. The circulating form drops the article: "joven de talento muy despejado."

Method note: every digitized edition of Paz's *Memorias póstumas* on Internet Archive was searched (`memoriaspstumas00pazgoog` 1917; `memoriaspstuma01/02/03pazj` 1892; `memoriaspstumas01pazgoog` 1855) and the phrases were not found. The 1892 vol. 3 and the 1855 edition **lack searchable OCR**, so **Paz is unverified rather than excluded**. es.wikipedia carries no footnote at all for either quote; it names Paz in unfootnoted running text and lists him only in the general bibliography.

## 4. Mexico (1846-1847)

- **Contracted by agents of Antonio López de Santa Anna to fight the United States invasion** **[R]**. No contract, muster, or passage document.
- **Havana** as a transit point is **this atlas's inference from the route**, not a source claim, and is marked as such in the journey file.
- **Battle of La Angostura / Buena Vista, 22-23 February 1847** **[A: the battle, two days; R: his presence]**. The one-day date "1847-02-22" that circulates is incomplete; the es.wikipedia article itself gives "22-23 de febrero de 1847."
- **Battle of the Sacramento, 28 February 1847** **[A: the battle; R: his presence]**. Mexican command was **General José A. Heredia**, with Governor **Ángel Trías** organizing the defence; **Pedro García Conde commanded only the cavalry**, and drafts naming him as overall commander are corrected here. Battlefield coordinates corrected to **28.87167, -106.18639**, the Sacramento river crossing about 15 miles north of Chihuahua city, from a drafted point that had drifted southeast toward the city.
- **Battle of Cerro Gordo, 18 April 1847** **[A: the battle; R: his presence]**. Coordinates corrected to **19.429, -96.688**, on the National Road near Plan del Río, from a drafted point 14 km too far west (effectively Xalapa).
- **Fall of Mexico City, 14 September 1847** **[A]**.
- **Unresolved physical problem, flagged:** Angostura (near Saltillo, Coahuila) on 22-23 February and the Sacramento (above Chihuahua) on 28 February are roughly 600 miles apart. Six days. No source addresses it.

## 5. California (1848 - c. 1852)

- **Opens a general store in the Sacramento valley and sells to miners rather than mining** **[R]**; the tradition says he grows rich.
- **Correction, chronological:** drafts dated the California arrival to **January 1848**. Impossible. Marshall found gold at Sutter's Mill on **24 January 1848** **[A]**; the news was not public even in San Francisco until **March 1848** **[A]**; the Latin American influx came in **late 1848 and mainly 1849** **[A]**. The arrival is redated to **late 1848 at the earliest**. Separately, the **city of Sacramento was laid out in 1849**, so a store "in Sacramento" in 1848 is anachronistic; "Sacramento Valley," the source's own wording, is the defensible form.
- **Departure via San Francisco, c. 1852** **[R]**, and **loses the American money gambling in Madrid** **[R]**.

## 6. Madrid, Prim, and the Eastern question (1852-1853)

- **Introduced by the poet Ventura de la Vega to General Juan Prim y Prats** **[R]**.
- **Attached to a Spanish military observation mission to the war between Russia and the Ottoman Empire** **[R]**.
- **Paris** appears as a waypoint with no date, address, or companion **[R]**.
- **Battle of Sinop, 30 November 1853 (18 November O.S.)**, Nakhimov destroying Osman Pasha's squadron at anchor **[A: the battle, independently attested]**. **His attendance is [R]**, resting on the single Argentine tradition. Drafts that tagged the *witnessing* as attested are corrected: the tags split.

## 7. Russian service (1854-1857)

- **France declared war on Russia 27 March 1854; Britain 28 March 1854** **[A]**. Drafts citing "28 March" for both are corrected: the single date names only the British declaration.
- **Villanueva leaves the Spanish observer role and takes service with Russia** **[R]**. Garay's explanation is chivalric (see quote below); es.wikipedia's own footnote undercuts it, noting that Prim had probably already returned to Spain, so the "resignada anuencia de Prim" may never have been sought.
- **Incorporated into the first regiment of the 31st cavalry division under Colonel Ponekkine** **[R]**. The es.wikipedia footnote 10 records the colonel's name variously as **Ponnekin, Ponekkine, Pouchkine, Pushkin**; this report does not standardize it silently.
- **Succeeds Ponekkine as colonel of that regiment on the colonel's death** **[R]**. Drafts saying he took command of the *division* overstate the source, which says regiment.
- **The Danube front and the sources' "Totorkan."** The identification of "Totorkan" with **Tutrakan** on the Danube (44.05, 26.61) is **this atlas's conjecture, not a source reading**, and is labelled as such in the journey file. The Danube/Silistra framing for spring 1854 is otherwise sound.
- **Sevastopol** is included as the emblematic ground of the war. **No source ties Villanueva to the siege lines by name**; this is stated in the file rather than smoothed over.
- **Crimean War ends with the Treaty of Paris, 30 March 1856** **[A]**.

## 8. Villanokoff: marriage, name, Khiva, and the two deaths (1857-1875)

- **1857: marries Ponekkine's widow, changes his surname to Villanokoff, is promoted general** **[R]**.
- **The padrino claim, corrected.** The source names **Luis Fernández de Córdoba y Ponce de León, Duke of Medinaceli, "embajador de España ante la corte del Zar."** The descriptor is **demonstrably false**: **Luis Tomás Fernández de Córdoba y Ponce de León, 15th Duke of Medinaceli (1813-1873), was a senator and Knight of the Golden Fleece with no recorded diplomatic post**, while **Spain's ambassador extraordinary at St Petersburg from 1856 to 1868 was Mariano Téllez-Girón, 12th Duke of Osuna** **[A]**. The claim is reported as the source makes it, with the false title flagged. Note also that **the source names no city** for the wedding; St Petersburg is this atlas's placement, on the ground that the Spanish embassy sat there.
- **Decorated by Tsar Alexander II** **[R]**; no orders named, no gazette produced.
- **Khiva campaign, corrected to 1873.** The Russian conquest of Khiva was the campaign of **1873**: Veryovkin's Orenburg column marched in late February 1873, the Aral force on 11 March, Kaufman left Jizzakh on 23 March, Khiva fell **10 June** and the khan was reinstated **14 June 1873** **[A]**. There was **no Russian assault on Khiva in 1872**, only Markozov's aborted probe in September 1872 and the Tsar's December 1872 decision to attack. The es.wikipedia article writes "1872-1873"; the flat "1872" in circulation is a compression. **Villanueva's presence is [R]** and no column or command is named.
- **Death: contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Most biographers give **1872**, with the rank of lieutenant general **[R]**. Obligado himself sets against this that **the Almanaque Gotha kept printing Villanueva's name on the Russian army rolls until 1875** **[A at primary: Obligado 1903, scan p. 148]**. The same accounts also have him campaigning against Khiva in 1873. **No burial place, tomb, monument, or grave is documented anywhere.**
  - **Textual note:** the 1903 original reads **"el Almanaque Gotha"**, without "de". A search of the 1903 scan for the exact string "Almanaque de Gotha" returns zero matches; es.wikipedia normalized the title when it wikilinked it.
- **Descendants said to be living in Moscow at the time of the 1917 Revolution** **[R]**. No names, no further trace.

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. **Montevideo to 1851 vs. Mexico from 1846.** Both in the same account, five years of overlap, unaddressed.
2. **Death 1872 vs. Khiva 1873 vs. Gotha rolls to 1875.** Three mutually exclusive terminal dates in one article.
3. **The name.** Obligado and es.wikipedia: Benigno (Benjamín). Cutolo: Benito.
4. **The colonel's name.** Ponnekin / Ponekkine / Pouchkine / Pushkin, per the article's own footnote.
5. **Six days, six hundred miles**, Angostura to the Sacramento.
6. **The padrino's office.** Source says ambassador; the ambassador was another man entirely.
7. **Garay's chivalry vs. the article's own caveat** that Prim had gone home and consented to nothing.
8. **Existence.** Sizonenko (archive) against Obligado (tradition), with Gilbert (the tradition's own custodian) siding with the archive.
