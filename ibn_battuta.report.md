# Ibn Battuta (Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Battuta), 1304-1369: research report
*slug: `ibn_battuta` · compiled 2026-08-02 · evidence base for `ibn_battuta.journey.json`*

Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Abdallah al-Lawati al-Tanji. Born Tangier 1304, dead in Morocco 1368 or 1369. Twenty-nine years of travel, Atlantic to South China Sea, Volga steppe to Niger bend, surviving in one dictated book: *Tuhfat al-nuzzar fi ghara'ib al-amsar wa-'aja'ib al-asfar*, the **Rihla**. Calendar: Julian throughout, the life ending 213 years before the Gregorian reform. Rihla dates are Hijri and appear here in their conventional Julian conversions.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, single-source, or contested · date hedges live in the journey file's `date_confidence`, never in campa prose.

**Method.** The pool arrives in five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife), a dozen entries carrying explicit `CORRECTED:` annotations from a verification pass. Those corrections are binding: where a lens entry and its own correction disagree, the correction wins and the disagreement is recorded rather than smoothed. Three defects recur and are handled by name: **invented placeholder dates carrying [A] tags**, **coordinates displaced from the named site**, and **overstated source claims**. All twenty-seven are listed in *Corrections applied*.

---

## I. Tangier and the training, 1304-1325

- **[A: Britannica; Encyclopedia.com]** Born at Tangier 24 February 1304 (703 AH) into a family of Maliki qadis.
- **[R]** Trained at Tangier for the qadi's office. The pool tags this [A] and dates it 1320 while its own note concedes that no precise year is given. A fabricated placeholder cannot carry an attested tag. The training is folded into the birth stop and no 1320 date is asserted anywhere.
- **[A: Britannica; ORIAS Berkeley]** Departs alone at twenty-one, 14 June 1325 (2 Rajab 725), on hajj and hoping to study under scholars met on the road. The Rihla's opening line: "I left Tangier, my birthplace, on Thursday, 2nd Rajab 725 [June 14, 1325]" **[A: Gibb 1929, via Fordham Internet History Sourcebooks]**
- **[A: Rihla, opening chapter]** The vow never to travel the same road twice.
- **[R]** The much-quoted "I set out alone, finding no companion to cheer the way with friendly intercourse, and no party of travellers with whom to associate myself" is attributed to Gibb everywhere and was not confirmed against the primary translation in this pass. It is not used as a `quote` field; its sense is carried in prose.

## II. The road east and the first hajj, 1325-1326

- **[A: Rihla, Tunis chapter]** Tunis: alone among strangers, weeping with homesickness, consoled by a fellow traveler, appointed qadi of the onward caravan. The first exercise of the office. Dropped from the file for stop budget, not for want of evidence.
- **[A: Rihla, Alexandria chapter; Wikipedia on the itinerary]** Alexandria 1326, some 3,500 km from Tangier, the Pharos already fallen on one side.
- **[R: Rihla]** Shaykh Burhan al-Din's prophecy, naming three brothers to be met in India, Sind, and China. **Place correction:** the pool labels the site "Alexandria, Pharos and tomb of Sheikh al-Murshidi." Al-Murshidi's hermitage was at Fuwwa in the Nile Delta, visited after Alexandria; his tomb is not in Alexandria. Split into its own stop.
- **[A: Gibb, via Wikiquote and Fordham]** Cairo: "mother of cities and seat of Pharaoh the tyrant, mistress of broad regions and fruitful lands, boundless in multitude of buildings, peerless in beauty and splendour, the meeting-place of comer and goer, the halting-place of feeble and mighty, whose throngs surge as the waves of the sea, and can scarce be contained in her for all her size and capacity." **[A: Rihla, Cairo chapter]** Twelve thousand water carriers, thirty thousand hired mules, lodging among the students of al-Azhar.
- **[R]** Jerusalem in 1326. The Rihla records the visit; no reachable source in this pool dates it, and it enters the file through the interlock lens alone.
- **[A: Rihla; Britannica; ORIAS]** Damascus 1326: the Umayyad Mosque, a marriage, brief study for hadith credentials, then the Syrian hajj caravan.
- **[R]** The visit to Ibn Arabi's tomb at the foot of Mount Qasiyun is recorded historically per the Rihla but is not in the chronology or geography lens; it reaches the file through the interlock lens.
- **[A: Rihla; ORIAS, "The Hajj: Medina to Mecca, 1326"]** Medina and the Prophet's tomb, then Mecca, first pilgrimage completed November 1326. "We immediately entered the holy sanctuary and began the rites of pilgrimage." **[A: Gibb 1929, via Fordham]**

## III. Iraq, Persia, and the Mecca years, 1326-1330

- **[A: ORIAS; Britannica]** Departs Mecca 17 November 1326 with a caravan for Iraq rather than turning home. The hinge of the life.
- **[A: Rihla, Iraq chapter]** Najaf: silver doors, carpets, lamps, a town living entirely off the shrine's endowments.
- **[A: Rihla]** Basra's decayed Arabic, Isfahan's Suhrawardi hospices, Shiraz and the tomb of Sa'di, all attested. Only Najaf and Baghdad reach the file; the rest are dropped for stop budget.
- **[A: Rihla, Baghdad chapter; Encyclopedia.com]** Baghdad 1327: two sides on the Tigris joined by a bridge of boats, the Mongol sack a lifetime old, the Ilkhanid Abu Sa'id's court on the move.
- **[R]** Roughly three years based at Mecca. **Contradiction carried:** Encyclopedia.com gives 1327-1330 with the second and third pilgrimages inside that span; World History Encyclopedia puts a third pilgrimage in the winter of 1330.

## IV. The Red Sea and the Swahili coast, 1331

- **[A: Encyclopedia.com; World History Encyclopedia]** Aden, then Mogadishu, "an exceedingly large city," then the Swahili coast including Kilwa, then north again by Oman and the Gulf.
- **[A: Hamdun and King, p. 13]** "Their food is rice cooked with ghee placed on a large wooden dish..."
- **[A: Hamdun and King, p. 380]** "The city of Kulwa is one of the finest and most substantially built towns..." **[A: Gibb, via World History Commons]** Of Sultan Abu'l-Muzaffar Hasan: "He used to devote the fifth part of the booty made on his expeditions to pious and charitable purposes, as is prescribed in the Koran"

## V. Anatolia, the Black Sea, and the Golden Horde, 1330/1332-1334

The worst-tangled phase in the pool. Nothing here is silently resolved.

1. **Date contradiction.** The chronology lens dates the leg 1332 and its note claims the sources disagree between "1332 or 1333-1334." The note misstates the disagreement: Wikipedia gives the Anatolia crossing as "the autumn of 1330 (or 1332)." The competing years are 1330 and 1332. The pool's own geography lens dates Konya c.1330-31, contradicting its chronology lens outright.
2. **[A: Rihla, Anatolia chapter]** Konya, the tomb of Jalal al-Din Rumi, and the akhi brotherhoods who lodge and feed strangers as a point of honor.
3. **[A: Rihla, Crimea chapter]** Genoese Kaffa, church bells heard for the first time. **Correction:** the common retelling has him climb a minaret and give the adhan himself. The Rihla has him and his companions go up to the top of their lodging, the call made by companions in some renderings, and local Muslims stopping them for fear of trouble.
4. **[A: Rihla, Golden Horde chapter]** The ordu of Sultan Muhammad Uzbek Khan. **Correction, and the label rejected:** the pool's "Sarai al-Jadid" at 46.5, 47.6667 matches neither capital. Sarai Batu (Selitrennoye) is about 47.18, 47.45; Sarai al-Jadid (Tsarev/Kolobovka) about 48.68, 45.34, some 80 km and 280 km away. His "al-Sara, known also as al-Sara Baraka, capital of sultan Uzbak" is conventionally Sarai Batu. The file uses 47.18, 47.45 and names the dispute.
5. **[A: Encyclopedia.com]** Constantinople with the khatun's retinue, one of very few Christian capitals he describes firsthand. **Date disputed: 1332 or 1334.**

## VI. Transoxiana to Delhi, 1333

- **[A: Rihla, Transoxiana chapter]** Bukhara in ruins a century after the Mongol conquest, its scholars scattered, the great minaret still standing. Samarkand likewise, dropped for stop budget; **[R]** Balkh entirely uninhabited, dropped; **[A]** Kabul on the winter road, dropped.
- **[A: World History Encyclopedia; Wikipedia]** Crosses the Indus **12 September 1333**. **Correction:** the pool's 30.1575, 71.5249 lies inside Multan, about 100 km east of the Indus and on the Chenab side. The file uses a point on the Indus itself and states that the ford is not identified.

## VII. Qadi of Delhi and the broken embassy, 1334-1342

- **[A: Britannica; Encyclopedia.com; Rihla, Delhi chapters]** Received by Muhammad bin Tughluq, granted a house, appointed qadi of the capital on a lavish salary.
- **[R]** Serves seven to eight years. The pool tags a 1338 midpoint [A] while its own note admits the year is invented. Retagged. The bracket 1334-1341/42 is standard and unchallenged.
- **Source overstatement corrected.** The pool cites the Delhi entry as "corroborated by contemporary Delhi chroniclers." This is false and inverted. He is conspicuously absent from the Delhi Sultanate chronicles, Barani among them, and the standard scholarly observation is that the Indian years lack contemporary non-Arabic corroboration. The clause is removed and the absence is stated as a gap.
- **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]** 1341: envoy to the Yuan court with gifts and a returning Chinese embassy. **[A: Wikipedia; World History Encyclopedia]** Attacked by bandits on the road to the coast, separated from his companions, robbed. The pool gives no coordinates for this, only "near Delhi," so it is folded into the departure stop rather than given a false pin.
- **[A: Rihla, embassy chapter]** Cambay in Gujarat, dropped for stop budget. Then Calicut: the fleet wrecked in a storm while he is ashore at Friday prayers, gifts, slaves, and companions lost. He does not return to Delhi. **[A: Wikipedia]** 1342: shelters under Jamal al-Din on the Coromandel coast.

## VIII. The southern seas, 1343-1345

- **[A: World History Encyclopedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Maldives: chief qadi, married into the ruling family. **Two contradictions carried.** (1) The geography lens asserts "about eighteen months" as [A]; the standard figure is about nine, arrival c. December 1343 and departure by August 1344, and the chronology lens itself hedges "nine months to roughly eighteen." (2) "Appointed by the island's ruling sultana" collides with the king-list, which dates Khadijah's first reign 1347-1362/63, years after the stay.
- **[A: Mahdi Husain]** "It was customary with me, when acting as qazi in the Maldive islands, that whenever I sent for one of the party in a suit I sent him a blank or filled-in notice."
- **[A: Rihla, Ceylon chapter]** Adam's Peak, ascended by a chain fixed to the rock, the footprint venerated by Buddhists as the Buddha's and by Muslims as Adam's. **[A: Mahdi Husain]** "When I entered the island of Ceylon I found that the inhabitants who were infidels respected the Muslim fakirs, gave them shelter in their houses and fed them."
- **[R]** Bengal, Sylhet, and the shaykh identified as Shah Jalal. **The pool's [R] tag is right and its reasoning is wrong.** The pool says the meeting rests on devotional tradition; it is narrated firsthand in the Rihla, an ascetic in the Kamaru mountains living beside a cave with one goat. What is uncertain is the identification: he names the shaykh "Jalal al-Din al-Tabrizi," and equating him with Shah Jalal of Sylhet is a scholarly reconstruction. The conversions and miracles come from later hagiography, the *Gulzar-i-Abrar* of 1613.
- **[A: Rihla, Bengal chapter]** Sonargaon and the verdict on Bengal as a hell full of good things, dropped for budget. **[A: Rihla, Sumatra chapter; Encyclopedia.com]** Samudera Pasai, a Shafi'i sultan, a fortnight of royal hospitality, a Chinese junk. **Coordinate correction and internal inconsistency:** the chronology lens gives 5.2, 97.05 and the geography lens 5.25, 97.1667 for the same place; Pasai lay about 15 km east of Lhokseumawe. Reconciled to 5.1667, 97.1667.

## IX. China, 1345-1346

- **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Wikipedia]** Landfall at Zaitun (Quanzhou), one of the largest harbors in the world, its waters crowded with junks, a Muslim quarter with its own qadi. **Claim corrected:** the pool calls it "the furthest point of his eastward travel," wrong on the pool's own terms, since Hangzhou at 120.16 E and Guangzhou both lie further east than Quanzhou at 118.68 E. Recast as his landfall.
- **[R]** Guangzhou, Hangzhou, and a claimed journey to Khanbaliq (Beijing). Scholars question the Beijing leg and, more broadly, how much of the China chapter is firsthand.
- **[R: Ross E. Dunn, *The Adventures of Ibn Battuta*, 1986]** Dunn concludes that nobody has made a fully convincing case against the China journey and calls the question probably unsolvable. That is the honest state of the field. Every China stop carries the dispute in `date_confidence`.
- **[R]** The passage on Chinese painters reproducing his portrait is widely attributed to Gibb, and the wording was not verified against the primary translation in this pass. Used with that hedge printed in `quote_source`.

## X. The plague road home, 1347-1350

- **[A: Encyclopedia.com]** Back via Sumatra to Malabar and Quilon, 1347, dropped for stop budget. **[R: World History Encyclopedia]** Baghdad again, January 1348, dropped.
- **[A: The Maydan; academic treatments of the plague chapter]** Damascus, 21 July 1348: the city ordered to fast three days and come out on Friday to implore God to raise the plague. He records two thousand dead a day at the worst, and Muslims, Christians, and Jews processing together. **[A: academia.edu, "A Two-year Journey under the Arrows of the Black Death"]** Then Mecca by Jeddah, more than four months, plague running through the Hejaz caravans.
- **[A: academic source citing the Rihla's own date, Sha'ban 750 AH]** Fez, November 1349, after roughly twenty-four years. **Framing conflict flagged:** the geography lens has him arrive at Tangier in 1349 and learn there of his mother's death from plague; the standard itinerary has him reach Fez first and go on to Tangier after. Both are kept, in that order.
- **Coordinate correction.** The traditional Tangier tomb on Rue Ibn Batouta is about 35.7811, -5.8139; the pool's 35.7873 is roughly 690 m north, a large error inside a dense medina.
- **[A: Wikipedia; academic summary citing the Rihla]** Granada under the Nasrids, 1350. **Overstatement corrected:** the pool has him "meeting scholars and officials at a court." He did not meet Sultan Yusuf I, who was ill; the sultan's mother funded his stay from her own purse; a reception inside the Alhambra is not established. He did meet Granadan scholars, among them Ibn Juzayy, his future scribe. The pool's closing line about seeing the frontier of the faith before it recedes is editorial and is dropped.

## XI. The Sahara and the Mali empire, 1351-1354

- **[A: ORIAS, "Journey to Mali, 1350-1351"]** Out from Fez across the Sahara; leaves Sijilmasa with a trading caravan, February 1352, the camels loaded with salt slabs to be traded slab for slab against gold.
- **[A: Rihla, Sahara-crossing chapter]** Taghaza, built of salt blocks, mosque included, brackish water, flies, inhabited only for the trade. **Coordinate correction:** Taghaza is 23.60, -5.00; the pool's 23.3333 is about 30 km south. Dropped for budget.
- **[A: ORIAS]** Walata, April 1352. **Quote correction, important.** The pool offers a bare "no good" glossed as a curt verdict on his lodging. Both the framing and the excerpt mislead. At Hamdun and King p. 37 the words sit inside a longer statement: sent a welcome gift of a sparse mixture of grain with honey and milk, he took it to mean he should "expect no good from the blacks," a judgment he later reconsidered. It is a reaction to a meagre gift of food, and the full wording is racially framed. The bare fragment is not used as a quote; the episode is described in prose with its context intact.
- **[A: ORIAS]** The court of Mansa Sulayman, 1352: seriously ill within days of arrival, recovering only after two months. **[A: Hamdun and King, p. 46]** His complaint about an inadequate hospitality gift opens "I have indeed traveled in the lands of the world..." **[A: Hamdun and King, p. 58]** His verdict on the Malians: "of all people they are the furthest from injustice"
- **[A: Yale Teachers Institute; Encyclopedia.com]** Timbuktu and the Niger towns, 1353; **[R]** the Rihla treats Timbuktu as smaller than the Malian capital. **[A: Hamdun and King, p. 66]** "The boy he gave me is still with me up till now," recording an enslaved boy given at departure. The Rihla names slavery as ordinary transactional fact and the quote is kept for that reason. **[A: Hamdun and King, p. 43]** On the Niger, of a boatman's caution: "He only did that for fear on your behalf of danger from the crocodile..." **[A]** Gao (Kawkaw), dropped for budget.
- **[A: ORIAS]** Leaves Takadda with a large caravan, 11 September 1353. **Coordinate correction:** Takedda is Azelik wan Birni at 17°31'06"N 6°46'59"E, that is 17.518, 6.783; the pool's 17.75, 7.05 is about 40 km northeast.
- **[A: ORIAS; academic summary citing the Rihla]** Fez again, January 1354, ending twenty-nine years.

## XII. The Rihla, the death, the tomb

- **[A: theconversation.com; Encyclopedia.com; Dunn; Wikipedia]** Abu Inan Faris commissions the account; Ibn Battuta dictates at Fez to Ibn Juzayy al-Kalbi of Granada, met in 1350, who shapes it into the Rihla and completes it in 1356. **Minor conflict carried:** some accounts date the finished written work to December 1355, the colophon to 756 AH. Ibn Juzayy dies in 1357. The work survives only through later copies: none in Ibn Battuta's hand, none in Ibn Juzayy's. A total documentary gap at the text's origin.
- **[R: Britannica]** Serves afterward as a qadi somewhere in Morocco. No posting, no year, no document.
- **[R]** **Death: a genuine three-way conflict, unresolved.** Year 1368, 1369, rarely 1377. Place: Britannica says only "Morocco"; Wikipedia and Wikidata give Marrakesh; other sources give Tangier, also the traditional burial site. The pool's afterlife lens says "most sources say in Fez," which overstates the consensus and contradicts the pool's own chronology lens. Fez is not privileged in the file.
- **[R: Atlas Obscura; Lonely Planet]** A whitewashed mausoleum in the Tangier medina on Rue Ibn Batouta is venerated as his tomb. No inscription or document confirms the remains are inside; the site is usually locked.

## XIII. Afterlife of the text

- **[A: Project MUSE, "The Colonial Adventures of Confiscated Arabic Texts"]** French officers recover five Arabic manuscripts at Constantine, Algeria, in the 1830s, two fuller than anything known before; removed to Paris, still the base of the standard text.
- **[A: Samuel Lee, London 1829]** First English rendering, from abridged manuscripts held at Cambridge, obtained via Burckhardt. **Correction:** the pool says Lee "predates the discovery of the complete manuscripts by decades." He does not; the Algerian manuscripts surfaced in the 1830s. What came decades later was the critical edition.
- **[A: Defremery and Sanguinetti, Societe Asiatique, 1853-1858]** First critical Arabic edition with French translation, four volumes, from the Constantine manuscripts. Base text for nearly every later translation.
- **[A: Hakluyt Society, Second Series]** Gibb: vol. I 1958, II 1962, III 1971; Beckingham completes vol. IV 1994. **Correction:** the pool credits Beckingham with the index and ends the project in 1994. The index is volume V, 2000, compiled by A. D. H. Bivar. The project spans 1958-2000.
- **[A: IAU Gazetteer; the-moon.us]** 1976: a lunar crater on Mare Fecunditatis named for him, replacing the provisional Goclenius A. **[A: Ibn Battuta Mall official site; Wikipedia]** 2005: Ibn Battuta Mall, Dubai, six courts styled Andalusia, Tunisia, Egypt, Persia, India, and China. **[A: Amazigh World News]** 10 March 2017: bronze sculpture unveiled at the Quanzhou Museum of Contemporary Art; the pool dates this to "the 2020s," the cited article gives 2017. **[A: Heykel.org; local Tangier reporting]** 2022: a museum in the Kasbah quarter of Tangier, plus a public statue in the city.
- **[R]** European rediscovery is a nineteenth-century event tied to the Algerian find and the Lee and Defremery-Sanguinetti translations. Before that the Rihla circulated as manuscript copies within the Islamic world and was not part of the Western canon of travel writing.

- **[R]** **Quanzhou, 24.8741, 118.6757** and **Hangzhou, 30.2741, 120.1551**, taken exactly from `marco_polo`. The atlas's two great medieval travelers now share both Chinese ports, forty years apart. `matteo_ricci` is a third China-anchored journey with no direct overlap, contextual only.
- **[A]** **Canton, 23.126, 113.259**, taken exactly from `keyserling`, who passed through in 1911. His **Colombo, 6.934, 79.843**, is deliberately **not** inherited: the Ceylon stop here is Adam's Peak at 6.8096, 80.4994, a different site, and a false pin-match would be worse than a named near-miss.

## Contradictions carried, not adjudicated

1. Anatolia crossing: autumn 1330 or 1332. The pool's own two lenses disagree with each other, and its note misdescribes the disagreement.
2. Constantinople: 1332 or 1334.
3. The Mecca years: continuous 1327-1330, or a third pilgrimage as late as winter 1330.
4. Maldives stay: roughly nine months or roughly eighteen.
5. Maldives appointment: the ruling sultana, against a king-list dating Khadijah's first reign 1347-1362/63.
6. The 1349 homecoming: Fez first and then Tangier, against the geography lens putting Tangier in 1349 as the arrival.
7. The China chapter: firsthand, compiled secondhand, or mixed. Dunn calls it probably unsolvable.
8. The Rihla's completion: December 1355 as a written work, against a 756 AH colophon read as 1356.
9. Death year: 1368, 1369, or rarely 1377.
10. Death place: Morocco unspecified, Marrakesh, or Tangier. Fez is asserted only by the pool's afterlife lens.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- **No autograph.** Neither the dictation nor Ibn Juzayy's fair copy survives. Every reading passes through later copies and, since 1853, through a French critical edition built on five confiscated manuscripts.
- **No contemporary external corroboration for the Delhi years.** Eight years as qadi of a capital left no trace in that capital's own historiography.
- **No dated record of the final Moroccan judgeship.** No town, no year.
- **No end date for the legal education at Tangier.** The pool's 1320 is invented and unused.
- **The Indus ford is not identified.** Only the date, 12 September 1333, is fixed.
- **The Jerusalem visit is undated in this pool.** It enters the file through the interlock lens alone.
- **The bandit attack has no coordinates**, only "near Delhi." Folded into the departure stop rather than given a false pin.
- **The Mali court has no coordinates in the chronology lens.** The quotes lens supplies 11.35, -8.7 for Niani; used with the site identification hedged.
- **No primary work was attempted.** No Arabic manuscript, no Defremery-Sanguinetti volume, no Hakluyt volume consulted directly. Everything here stands at one or two removes.
