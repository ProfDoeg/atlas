# Hanya Holm (1893-1992): research report
*2026-08-24. Slug `hanya_holm`. Born Johanna Josepha Eckert at Worms-am-Rhein; died New York City. Chief instructor of the Wigman school at Dresden, founder of its New York branch, one of the retrospectively named "Big Four" of American modern dance, Broadway choreographer, opera director, and for forty-three consecutive summers head of a dance school in Colorado.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim not corroborated. Contradictions are **flagged, not adjudicated**; where two lenses of the research pool disagreed, both readings are printed. Coordinate corrections are collected in §12 rather than repeated in the narrative. The reachable record is thin exactly where a biography wants thickness (childhood, marriage, son, emigration) and thick where an institution keeps files (opening dates, appointments, run lengths, award classes); the journey file follows that shape, and several stops say outright that nothing was recovered.

## 3. Dresden and the Wigman years (1920/21-1931)

**Seeing Wigman dance. [A for the fact; year unresolved.]** NYPL attributes the turn from music to movement to seeing Wigman perform. Sources give **1920 or 1921**; the pool leaves it open and so does the file. No venue named.

**14 January 1921, chamber group with the Dresden Philharmonic. [A]** `mary_wigman.journey.json` names Holm directly among the original members. Her documented entry into the Wigman lineage.

**The rise inside the school. [A]** NYPL, year by year: assistant instructor 1922, diploma and a place in the Concert Dance Group 1923, chief instructor 1924, European touring 1923-1928. Compressed into one stop dated to the diploma. The pre-1925 Bautzner Strasse premises are **approximate**: no street number was recovered. For 1925 the file uses the villa at number 107, where `mary_wigman.journey.json` has "Georgi, Kreutzberg, Holm carry the doctrine outward."

**Ommen, the Star Camps. [A for the role; dates corrected.]** NYPL specifies "summers 1927-1928"; the dossier had 1927-**1929**, and pinned the town while its own text located the gatherings at the **Eerde estate** 5 km southwest. The pin moves so text and point agree. The titles attributed to her, **The Bacchae** and **Plato's Farewell to His Friends**, are uncorroborated and stay [R].

**1929: The Miracle, L'Histoire du Soldat, the co-directorship. [R] on all three.** The pool tags it [R] and notes the NYPL biographical note as fetched does not carry all of it. Not re-verified anywhere. The performances need not have been in Dresden; the pin is her documented base.

**Munich, Totenmal, 24 June 1930. [A for the work; split evidence for Holm.]** `mary_wigman.journey.json` carries the congress and date but credits **only Wigman and Albert Talhoff**. Holm's role as assistant and dancer rests on the *Dance Research Journal* article (abstract only) and the dossier; the split is stated in `date_confidence`.

## 4. The crossing (1931)

**Not a flight. [A, legend corrected.]** The popular "fled Hitler" narrative is undercut by the dated record: she left under contract in September 1931, sixteen months before Hitler became chancellor, to open Wigman's authorized New York branch with Sol Hurok's backing (NYPL; en.wikipedia). The pool's hedge is honored: it describes the legend as a pattern in standard summaries, not one it witnessed in a fetched source. It is the **1936 renaming** that sources tie to distancing from Nazism.

**"25 September 1931" is wrong. [corrected]** A misreading of **26 September**, which en.wikipedia gives as the date the school **opened**, not a docking. No manifest located; the port is [R], Hamburg a labelled guess, since no source names a port or a ship.

**New York school, 26 September 1931. [A]** The inherited pin's own source note admits its address is inferred; no studio address was recovered on either pass. On Hurok: en.wikipedia's article on him mentions neither Wigman nor Holm, so corroboration comes from `mary_wigman.journey.json`, which has him bringing Wigman to the Chanin Theatre on 28 December 1930.

## 5. Bennington, Trend, the company (1934-1947)

**Bennington founding faculty, 1934. [A]** Named directly in **two** corpus files, `martha_graham` and `jose_limon` (1937). **Tenure corrected:** the dossier gave 1934-1937, but NYPL has her teaching "annually through **1940**" and en.wikipedia dates the school 1934-1942. "Big Four" is retrospective canonization tied to the 1934 roster, not a joint company or shared method.

**1936 renaming. [A for the fact; motive unresolved.]** en.wikipedia: Hanya Holm Studio from 1936, company founded the same year; Dance Teacher ties it to Wigman's political associations. Political distance, commercial necessity, or both: left disputed.

**Denver, Broadway Theater, 1936. [A]** First American concert as an independent choreographer (NYPL). The theatre is demolished, the pin approximate, and no programme survives.

**Trend: premiere versus debut. Contradiction flagged, handled openly.** en.wikipedia dates the first US performance flatly to **1937**, no venue; NYPL calls the **December 1937 Mecca Temple** performance her "historic New York debut", where the work took the New York Times award for best dance composition of the year. The dossier's two lenses both read as *the* premiere and contradict each other. The file splits them into **two stops**: a summer Bennington showing tagged **[R] as a reconciliation offered, not an attested fact**, and the December debut at [A]. "John Martin calls it the season's major dance composition" is **dropped** as an inference from the award.

**Metropolitan Daily: 1938, not 1939. [corrected]** NYPL dates it 1938; the dossier's geography lens said 1939 against its own chronology lens. NBC as network is attested; **30 Rockefeller Plaza as the origination studio is [R]**. The "first televised modern dance" superlative is reported with the pool's own hedge about what counts as experimental television.

**Naturalization 1939; Tragic Exodus and They Too Are Exiles 1939. [A]** Court, day and address named nowhere; the pin is a placement.

**Company's end: 1944 or 1947. Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Dance Teacher: folded **1944** after eight years, financial reasons. NYPL, stronger: dissolved **1947** after thirty original pieces. Verify ruled the dossier's [A]-tagged 1944 too strong. The stop sits at 1947 and prints both.

## 6. Colorado (1941-1983)

**Founding, 1941. [A]** NYPL; Colorado College Bulletin. Forty-three consecutive summers to 1983, by duration the most durable institution of her life.

**Alwin Nikolais. [A for the relation, [R] for the date.]** en.wikipedia: trained under Holm at Bennington, her assistant at the New York school, taught with her at Colorado College in the summers. No source dates the start of that assistantship; 1948 is a placement. **Nikolais has no journey file in the corpus**, noted in the stop. en.wikipedia, "Mary Wigman", credits Wigman's American legacy chiefly to Holm and through her to Nikolais and Joan Woodbury, and `mary_wigman.journey.json` carries the same lineage.

**The Ballad of Baby Doe, 7 July 1956. [A]** en.wikipedia: premiere at Central City Opera, directed by Hanya Holm and Edwin Levy. The composer is deliberately unnamed in the campa, since the pool names none.

**Honorary Doctor of Fine Arts, Colorado College, 1960. [A]** NYPL; commencement date is a placement.

## 7. Broadway (1948-1960)

**Kiss Me, Kate, 30 December 1948, New Century Theatre, 1,077 performances. [A]** en.wikipedia; the house was demolished in 1962.

**Copyright, 1952. [A, superlative narrowed.]** NYPL: the registration made her "the first choreographer of a Broadway show ever to receive such protection". The pool's afterlife lens carried the broader "first choreography ever copyrighted in the United States" and its own verify note ruled that too strong, so **the Broadway-scoped wording is what the file states**. The Copyright Office pin is a filing site; the stop claims no visit.

**The Golden Apple, 1954. [A for work and citation; venue a gap.]** No source reached names the New York theatre or the opening date. The pin is the theatre district, labelled provisional; the gap is stated inside the campa.

**My Fair Lady, 15 March 1956, Mark Hellinger Theatre. [A]** **Tony year corrected:** the nomination came at the **11th Tony Awards in 1957**, not 1956, and 1956 was the first year nominees were published at all, so no earlier Holm nomination can exist. Michael Kidd won for Li'l Abner.

**Vancouver 1959, Toronto 1960. [R] on both venues.** Productions attributed to NYPL via the dossier. The Queen Elizabeth Theatre (1959) and the O'Keefe Centre (1960) fit the dates, but neither booking was confirmed.

**Camelot, 3 December 1960, Majestic Theatre, 873 performances. [A]** **One dossier claim dropped as false:** that Camelot's run and tours outlasted every other show she worked on. Its 873 performances are fewer than Kiss Me, Kate's 1,077 and far fewer than My Fair Lady; the campa states the reverse.

## 9. Quotations: an honest near-total gap

**Exactly one verified Holm quotation exists in the pool**, at one remove:

> "You have a perfect right to branch out, if you have the stuff in you, if you discover your own richness, if you have something to say."

en.wikipedia attributes it to Walter Sorell, *Hanya Holm: The Biography of an Artist* (Wesleyan, 1969), pp. 102-103. **The Sorell text was not reached:** archive.org's scan (`hanyaholmbiograp0000sore`) is borrow-restricted and its search API returned 403. The wording was confirmed identical on two fetches of the Wikipedia page and nothing beyond. Used once, at the Nikolais stop, chain of custody in `quote_source`. One further quotation in the file is **not Holm's**: Wigman's studio credo, carried from `mary_wigman.journey.json` and attributed to her.

**Two quotations across forty-three stops is far below the house target of about a third.** A deficiency of access, not of effort; see §11. Unopened primary leads, any of which would likely yield verbatim Holm: her essays in *Dance Observer* (1935), *Magazine of Art* (1938) and *Dance Magazine* (1956); "The Mary Wigman I Know" in Sorell, ed., *The Dance Has Many Faces* (1966); the Tobi Tobias oral histories at NYPL (1974-75); *Liebe Hanya* (2003); Gitelman and Forster, *Dance Chronicle* 20:1 (1997). **A follow-up pass against these, with library or JSTOR access, is the highest-value work available here.**
