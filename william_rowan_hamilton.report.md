# Sir William Rowan Hamilton (1805-1865): research report
*Written 2026-08-23 for `william_rowan_hamilton.journey.json`. Dublin, Trim, Dunsink, one bridge.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition, or a claim reaching this file only through a secondary retelling · *corrected* = the pool's value was wrong and the correction is recorded, not applied silently.

The file has **9 segments and 44 stops**. Two atlas travelers are named in campa prose, both on a direct relation and not on shared geography: **arthur_cayley**, who crossed to Dublin in June 1848 to hear him lecture, and **lord_kelvin**, whose verdict on the quaternions the next generation repeated.

## 1. Dublin and Trim, 1805-1822

- Born at his father's house on Dominick Street Lower at midnight between 3 and 4 August 1805 **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**. Both dates circulate; he settled on the fourth, for a reason dated to 1835 (below). Number 29, later 36.
- Sent at about age three to his uncle the Rev. James Hamilton at Talbot's Castle, Trim, for an intensive classical and linguistic education **[A: Wikipedia, after Graves]**. *Corrected coordinate:* pool 53.5556, -6.7936 sits 450 m west, in the town; Talbot's Castle is in the St Mary's Abbey precinct at **53.55528, -6.78694**.
- **Contradiction, flagged not resolved.** The contest against the American prodigy Zerah Colburn: the Colburn article dates it **September 1813**, Hamilton aged eight **[A: Wikipedia]**; the dossier said "around 1818". The file uses 1813-09-01 and names the disagreement in `date_confidence`. **Gap:** the venue, so the pin is generic city centre.
- Much of Newton's *Principia*, with analytic geometry and calculus, "by around age sixteen" **[A: Wikipedia]**. The hedge is the source's; 1821 is approximate.
- 1822: Laplace's *Mecanique celeste*, and a supposed flaw in its demonstration of the parallelogram of forces; December 1822, three original papers shown to John Brinkley **[A: Wikipedia]**.

### The Brinkley remark: a double correction

*"This young man, I do not say will be, but is, the first mathematician of his age"* was tagged **[R]** and stays **[R]**:

1. **Graves is not the source.** Searching both volumes of Graves's *Life* returns zero hits for "mathematician of his age", "I do not say will be", or any Brinkley remark of the kind.
2. **The traceable printing disowns its wording.** The unsigned obituary (attributed to De Morgan), *The Gentleman's Magazine*, NS vol. 1 (= vol. 220), January 1866, p. 129, prints the sentence and then says that its writer knew Brinkley well and will "undertake to say that he went no further than 'If the young man goes on as he has begun, he will become,'" calling the famous version "the exaggerated expression of an admiring witness".

**Date conflict:** the obituary says "about 1828", MacTutor about 1822. The stop carries the remark as reported speech with the retraction inside the campa, both dates in `date_confidence`, and deliberately **not** as a `quote` field: no wording here can be stood behind.

## 2. Trinity, optics, and Dunsink, 1823-1827

- Entered Trinity July 1823 at seventeen; repeatedly first in classics and science, two rare *optime* judgments. Introduced to Maria Edgeworth at Edgeworthstown in 1824, beginning a long correspondence **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- *On Caustics* communicated to the Academy through Brinkley before the end of 1824 **[A: MacTutor]**; sent back for expansion, and the expansion became the *Theory of Systems of Rays*, whose mature first part was read **23 April 1827 [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**.
- On Brinkley's elevation to Cloyne, elected Andrews Professor of Astronomy **and** Royal Astronomer of Ireland at twenty-one, before a fellowship **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**. Only the **year** is attested; the file's June orders the segment.
- **Pin note.** All pre-1852 Academy stops sit at **Navigation House, 114 Grafton Street** (53.3407, -6.26) **[A: Wikipedia]**. Dawson Street is unused: its only pool event is Charles Graves's memorial address of 30 November 1865, Graves's own act.
- **Catherine Disney, and one downgrade.** Met at Summerhill, County Meath, August 1824; she married the Rev. William Barlow in 1825, and Hamilton wrote *The Enthusiast* (1826) about it **[A: Wikipedia]**. *Corrected twice:* (1) pool 53.4895, -6.7368 is 900 m northwest of Wikipedia's **53.481907, -6.73084**; (2) the pool called Summerhill House the Disney seat, where Wikipedia makes it the Langford/Rowley seat with no Disney connection. The *meeting* stands **[A]**, the *house as Disney seat* drops to **[R]**.

## 3. The poets and the refusals, 1827-1833

- **Rydal Mount, September 1827 [A: Wikipedia].** Wordsworth read the verse and advised that mathematics, not poetry, was the gift. *Corrected coordinate:* pool 54.443, -2.9787 falls 680 m off the property; Rydal Mount is **54.4487, -2.9824**.
- **Ellen de Vere refuses him, 1831 [A event, R place].** Wikipedia carries proposal and refusal; the Curraghchase placing is reconstruction, that article documenting the de Veres there but mentioning neither Ellen nor the proposal. *Corrected coordinate:* pool 52.6335, -8.8467 is 4.6 km off Curraghchase at **52.59444, -8.87083**.
- **Coleridge at Highgate, 1832 [R].** The pool tags this [A] on Wikipedia, but the verification pass flagged that it reached the dossier with no primary letter fetched. Kept, tagged [R], with Graves the outstanding check.
- **Marriage to Helen Maria Bayly, 1833 [A year, R day]:** the day, 9 April, comes from the pool and was not confirmed against a register. **Honeymoon at "Bayly Farm ... across the fields from the observatory" [R: MacTutor]:** no closer address exists, and 53.383, -6.335 is an approximation near Dunsink that says so, a gap rather than a located site.

## 4. Conical refraction and the honours, 1832-1836

- Third Supplement read **22 October 1832**, predicting external and internal conical refraction in biaxial crystals; Humphrey Lloyd observed the external cone in aragonite **14 December 1832** and reported the internal-cone verification to the British Association at Cambridge in 1833 **[A: TCD, Wilkins Hamilton archive, 'Conical Refraction']**.
- **A stop deliberately not written.** Lloyd's 1833 Cambridge appearance is *Lloyd's* travel: nothing places Hamilton there that year. It is cited on his own Trinity stop, never staged as a scene.
- *On a General Method in Dynamics*, Phil. Trans. 1834, and the Second Essay 1835 **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**. **Pin decision:** the pool put these in London, where they were printed; he did not travel, so the stop is at **Dunsink**, where they were written.
- **Cunningham Medal, 1834 [A: Wikipedia]**. *Corrected:* the pool called it the Academy's **first**; the medal dates from 1796. It was Hamilton's first of two, the second in 1848.
- **Royal Medal and knighthood, 1835 [A: Wikipedia; MacTutor; tcd.ie]**, the knighthood conferred while he was secretary of the Dublin British Association meeting. Months and days are absent, so both default to 01. The pool's Somerset House pin was [R] by its own admission and is dropped: he did not go to London.
- **Bristol, British Association, 1836 [A, hedge lifted].** MacTutor places him at Bristol "the following autumn" after 1835, Helen away at Bayly Farm ten months; the Association's sixth meeting was at Bristol in 1836, pinning the year the pool left approximate. **Gap:** the venue.
- **Armagh, visit to Thomas Romney Robinson [R, undated].** MacTutor gives no date, so **no stop was written**. Recorded so the operator sees it was found, not missed.

## 5. Algebra as the science of pure time, 1833-1841

- Complex numbers as ordered pairs read to the Academy **4 November 1833 [A: MacTutor]**.
- *Theory of Conjugate Functions ... Algebra as the Science of Pure Time*, Transactions of the RIA vol. 17 (1837) **[A: MacTutor; read in the D. R. Wilkins edition]**. Two passages read directly from that PDF are safe verbatim: p. 293 on the three schools of algebra, used inside the campa, and the Article II passage on Order in Time, used as the `quote`.
- **Children [A: Wikipedia].** William Edwin 10 May 1834; Archibald Henry **4 August 1835**; Helen Eliza Amelia 1840. *Corrected:* the pool gave only Archibald's year. The day matters: Hamilton fixed his own birthday on 4 August after this son was born on it.
- President of the RIA 1837-1846, M.A. from Trinity 1837 **[A: Wikipedia]**. Month unknown; the file's February orders the segment.

## 6. The sixteenth of October 1843

- The walk along the Royal Canal with Helen toward an Academy Council meeting, the four-term noncommutative solution, and the carving on a stone of Brougham Bridge **[A: Hamilton to his son Archibald, 5 August 1865, TCD Wilkins archive]**. Announced to the Academy **13 November 1843 [A: TCD; MacTutor; Wikipedia]**.
- Three verbatim `quote` fields here were confirmed on the TCD pages: "An electric circuit seemed to close" and "Nor could I resist the impulse", both from the 1865 letter, and "Less than an hour elapsed" from the **15 October 1858** letter to Tait. Exponents render there as `i2` for *i* squared, preserved and explained.
- **Gap, stated as a gap:** no trace of the original carving survives, confirmed independently in Wikipedia's 'Broom Bridge' and 'William Rowan Hamilton'. **Pin note:** the bridge carries two coordinates here, 53.3731, -6.30 for the 1843 carving and 53.3719, -6.2986 for the 1958 plaque, as the two lenses gave them, carried as found and declared in the stops.

## 7. The long defence, 1846-1859

- **February 1846:** at the Geological Society of Dublin he "made an exhibition of himself through his intoxication" **[A: MacTutor, whose wording that is]**. **Gap:** the venue, so the pin is a generic city-centre point.
- **June 1848, Trinity, the quaternion lectures.** Attested from the other side in `arthur_cayley.journey.json`, which carries the date, the venue, and Cayley's verbatim 1883 Southport rejection of the time-metaphysics. Hamilton lecturing at his own institution is his own act, so the stop is his; Cayley's crossing from London stays on his file. **Pin decision:** this stop keeps Cayley's byte-identical **53.3438, -6.2546** so the two files meet at one point, while Hamilton's other Trinity stops take the corrected **53.3444, -6.2577** (from `oscar_wilde.journey.json`; the older value sits 215 m from Front Square). The 2005 bicentenary stop uses it too, being the pool's value for the Hamilton Mathematics Institute.
- **1853, *Lectures on Quaternions* (Hodges and Smith) [A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**, pinned at Dunsink.
- **1853, Catherine Disney Barlow's pencil case: pool source upgraded [A: MacTutor].** The pool called this dossier synthesis "not independently re-verified". MacTutor carries it directly, with the inscription (verbatim in the stop's `quote` field) and with her dying **two weeks** after the visit; the pool's "November 1853" is not there and is *dropped*. **Gap:** where the visit happened is nowhere recorded, so the stop sits at Hamilton's own house and says the pin is not the site of the meeting.
- **Icosian Calculus 1856, presented with the game at the 27th British Association meeting, Dublin, August 1857 [A: TCD].**
- **Contradiction, corrected and left visible.** The pool dated the sale of rights to John Jaques and Son to **1859**. Wikipedia ('Icosian game') and puzzlemuseum both give **1857**, at John T. Graves's suggestion, for a 25-pound fee, with **marketing from 1859**. The file uses 1857 for the sale, names 1859 for the issue, shows both in `date_confidence`, and drops the pool's [R], puzzlemuseum having been fetched. Issued as *The Icosian Game* and *The Travellers Dodecahedron, or a voyage round the world*; commercial failure confirmed, a handful of originals surviving in the Hordern-Dalgety Collection.
- **The London/Jaques pin is dropped.** The pool's coordinates for Jaques's premises were unverified by its own account and Hamilton did not go to London; the sale is narrated inside the Dublin stop.
- **Tait, 1858: pool claim corrected.** The pool said Hamilton "meets" Tait in 1858, in Ireland. MacTutor's Tait biography has Tait reading the *Lectures* in 1853, dropping it, then **beginning to correspond in August 1858** after reading Helmholtz on fluid motion, writing on 7 December of "certain formulas I had admired years ago on page 610 of your *Lectures*." **No meeting in person is documented**; Wikipedia says only that Thomas Andrews introduced Tait to Hamilton and to quaternions. The file has a correspondence, drops the Ireland placement, and dates the stop to Hamilton's letter of 15 October 1858.

## 8. Last years, death, burial, 1860-1866

- The last six years at Dunsink went to *Elements of Quaternions*, which far outgrew its plan. Elected among the first foreign associates of the new US National Academy of Sciences, 1864, in absentia **[A: Wikipedia]**; pinned at Dunsink, since he never crossed the Atlantic.
- **5 August 1865, the letter to Archibald [A: TCD Wilkins archive]**, the first-person source for the whole discovery scene, written under a month before his death.
- **Died at Dunsink 2 September 1865, aged sixty [A: Wikipedia; MacTutor; tcd.ie/hamilton]**. *Corrected:* the pool added a "bronchial/respiratory illness" clause. Wikipedia and MacTutor give only "a severe attack of gout", and tcd.ie mentions no respiratory illness either. The clause is **dropped**, and the drop is recorded in the stop's `date_confidence`.
- **Buried at Mount Jerome, Harold's Cross [A: Wikipedia]**. *Corrected coordinate:* pool 53.3266, -6.2843 sits 210 m north of Wikipedia's **53.32472, -6.28417**, used here. **Real gap:** plot, headstone and condition are undocumented in anything reachable (dib.ie redirect error, Findagrave HTTP 403), and the **day of the funeral** is unknown, so the stop repeats the date of death to order the segment.
- *Elements of Quaternions* completed editorially by William Edwin Hamilton and published posthumously by Longmans, Green, 1866 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Gap closed:** Wikipedia's 'Charles Jasper Joly' confirms Joly edited the second edition, two volumes, **vol. I 1899, vol. II 1901**, converting the pool's [R] to **[A]**. It is a `suggested_ref`, not a stop: Joly's editing is not Hamilton's travel.

## 9. Afterlife

- Bronze plaque under Broom Bridge unveiled **13 November 1958** by Taoiseach Eamon de Valera **[A: Wikipedia]**. *Corrected:* the pool's quotation of the inscription stopped early, dropping both the formula i2 = j2 = k2 = ijk = -1 and the closing "& cut it on a stone of this bridge"; the full text is restored in the stop's `quote` field. The plaque was later moved higher and set behind the railing after wear and vandalism, on no stated date **[A: Wikipedia, 'Broom Bridge']**.
- Hamilton Walk annually on 16 October since 1989, Dunsink to Broom Bridge, nicknamed Broomsday; walkers have included Gell-Mann, Weinberg, Wilczek, Wiles, Penrose and Daubechies **[A: Wikipedia, 'Broom Bridge']**.
- Stamps 13 November 1943; a 10-euro silver proof coin, the Hamilton Mathematics Institute and the Hamilton Building at Trinity, all 2005; the Hamilton Institute at Maynooth; a lunar crater **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. Eclipse of quaternions by the vector analysis of Gibbs, Heaviside and Helmholtz from about the 1880s, and a late-twentieth-century revival for rotation computation **[A: Wikipedia, 'Quaternion']**.
- **Kelvin's remark, upgraded to [A] and un-truncated.** The pool carried it as [R] via MacTutor and cut it short at "touched them in any way". Primary printing: William Thomson to R. B. Hayward, December 1892, on Hayward's *Algebra of Coplanar Vectors and Trigonometry*, in Silvanus P. Thompson, *The Life of William Thomson* (1910), vol. 2, p. 1138, where the sentence ends "including Clerk Maxwell". The full text is in the stop's `quote` field; naming Maxwell is what makes it a shot in the vector-versus-quaternion war. A remark **about** Hamilton, not by him, and the `quote_source` says so.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Hamilton is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no backward apparatus stop was written. Two things were caught for the operator to rule on rather than have deleted silently:

1. **Forward, kept as one clause.** The bicentenary stop closes by saying that graphics engines, robot arms and spacecraft now compute rotations in quaternions because four numbers turn without gimbal lock. That is his algebra applied a century after his death, and the Burroughs and Derrida forward grants are subject-specific, so it is not covered. It is kept as reception history of the *object* (Wikipedia, 'Quaternion'), not a reading of later events through a dead theorist's framework; it runs one sentence, has no stop, segment or name of its own, and sits at a Trinity pin. If the operator calls it a forward reach needing a grant, it is one clause to cut.
2. **Forward, in the afterlife segment.** The plaque (1958), the walk (since 1989) and the named walkers are posthumous events at Hamilton's own sites, written as what happened to *his* bridge, *his* route and *his* formula. No later person is staged as a traveler in a scene; de Valera and the walkers appear as record, one clause each. Under a stricter reading the walk stop would go.

Relations found and deliberately **not** used, third-party bridges and shared geography not being reasons: **ada_lovelace**, linked only through Augustus De Morgan, who tutored her and, per the unverified dossier, wrote to Hamilton from 1841; **charles_babbage**, linked only through John Herschel, another unverified dossier claim, his file never mentioning Hamilton; **George Boole**, correspondence asserted only in the dossier and no journey file; **gauss**, no evidence of a meeting or a letter, Euler dead before Hamilton was born, and QUEUE.md's "gauss, euler already queued" being corpus structure rather than biography; the **Kaaba**, **Temple Mount** and canonical **Paris** pins, no Middle East or French travel being recorded.
