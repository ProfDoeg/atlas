# Henri Léon Lebesgue (1875-1941): research report

*Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys. Typographer's son from Beauvais; lycée professor at Nancy; author of the note of 29 April 1901 and of Intégrale, longueur, aire (1902); professor at the Sorbonne 1918-1921 and at the Collège de France 1921-1941.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named on the line. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition the reachable sources carry but do not document. Disagreements are printed and left standing. Every date hedge lives in that stop's `date_confidence` field, never in the campa prose.

**The shape of the problem.** Two documentary layers of very different quality. The first is thin and solid: MacTutor at St Andrews plus the English and French Wikipedia articles, giving a clean spine of years, posts, publications and honours and almost nothing else. That spine carries every `[A]` here.

The second is a dossier of archive-derived precisions: a Beauvais street number, exact days for a scholarship, a Sorbonne appointment and a divorce, a vote of one in thirty-five, a firing-tables commission, a burial at Gouvieux. Almost none of it could be re-fetched; it points at the Archives départementales de l'Oise, a paywalled Gispert article, Montel's 1941 obituary and a Collège de France catalogue, and the Beauvais scholarship dossiers themselves burned in June 1940. All of it is `[R]`.

## I. Beauvais, 1875 to 1889

**Birth. [A: MacTutor; fr.wikipedia.]** Léon Henri Lebesgue, **28 June 1875**, Beauvais, Oise. Father **Éloi Léon Lebesgue**, typographer; mother **Marie Estelle Amélie Leclerc**, seamstress. The address **29 rue de la Madeleine** is `[R]`, on an Oise civil-register citation not re-fetched; the house is not identified, and the pool gave two longitudes for it, neither on the named street.

**The deaths of 1878-79: the pool over- and under-hedges at once. [A: fr.wikipedia.]** Its chronology called tuberculosis "biographical tradition" and killed only the father; fr.wikipedia states it flatly and states more, the father **and both elder sisters** dying of it when Lebesgue was three. The precise 14 April 1879 is dossier-only, not used. **Contradiction, flagged, not resolved:** the same pool also has "his mother and elder sister" supporting the household through the collège years. The campa prints it and leaves it.

**Collège de Beauvais. [A: MacTutor.] Gap:** no start year anywhere reached; the file uses 1885 as a declared placeholder, and the pin is a town-centre approximation, the successor Lycée Félix Faure being the wrong building and deliberately not substituted.

**Bourse Chevalier, 24 May 1889. [R]** The pool tagged this **`[A]` in geography and `[R]` in chronology for the same claim**, its own note conceding the 1889 register was not located. Weaker tag taken. Chain of custody: printed council deliberations reported in 1991 by the Oise archive director; the originals burned in June 1940.

## II. The Paris lycées and the rue d'Ulm, 1891 to 1899

**Date error corrected.** The pool carried one entry, "Collège de Beauvais, then Lycée Saint-Louis and Lycée Louis-le-Grand," dated **1889** and placed in **Paris**, conflating the Beauvais years (1880s, Oise) with the Paris lycées (early 1890s). **Split into two stops**, placeholder declared as a placeholder. The schools themselves are attested (MacTutor; fr.wikipedia), as is **ENS entry in 1894**; both lycée pins were corrected. **Second scholarship [R]:** the 1893 award and the 1894 record of thanks are dossier-only, same printed deliberations.

**"Ranked fifth in his cohort": demoted. [R]** Tagged `[A]` in the pool and cited to MacTutor, which gives no entrance rank; kept out of the campa and named as tradition in `date_confidence`. Same for the unsourced Langevin and Montel cohort overlap.

**The agrégation: the pool's worst tag. [A for the 1897 diploma; R for the rank.]** MacTutor records the teaching diploma and **does not mention the agrégation at all**, yet the pool cited MacTutor for the year and tagged rank and year together `[A]`. Split; the rank is named as unsourced tradition in the prose.

**Library years, 1897-99. [A: MacTutor,** "studied in the school's library, reading Baire's work on discontinuous functions"**.]** The wider reading list is the pool's synthesis and `[R]`. **First paper, 1898 [A: en.wikipedia]:** Sur l'approximation des fonctions.

## III. Nancy and the note of April 1901

**Nancy, 1899-1902. [A: MacTutor.]** Professor at the Lycée Centrale; **October 1899** as a start month is inferred from the academic year and is more precise than any source reached.

**The note. [A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia.]** Sur une généralisation de l'intégrale définie, **29 April 1901**, Comptes rendus 132, 1025-1028. Placed at the Institut de France, with `date_confidence` noting that Lebesgue was posted at Nancy and it is not established he was in the room.

**The money-counting letter: upgraded by verification. [A: en.wikipedia, 'Lebesgue integration', citing Siegmund-Schultze 2008.]** The dossier treated it as a colleague's paraphrase; it is Lebesgue's own attested wording, in a letter to Montel, and is carried verbatim. **Gap:** no source reached dates the letter.

**Doctorate, 1902 [A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia]:** Intégrale, longueur, aire, printed at Milan in the Annali di Matematica. **Rennes, 1902-1906 [A: MacTutor]:** maître de conférences; the pool's coordinate was accepted.

## IV. The Peccot lectures and the marriage, 1903 to 1906

**Cours Peccot, 1903 and 1905. [A: MacTutor.]** No months or days for either series. **Marriage, 3 December 1903 [A: MacTutor]**, to **Louise Marguerite Vallet**, sister of a fellow student of the ENS (fr.wikipedia confirms the relation, with no date or place).

**Two problems, both printed, neither resolved.** A civil-register notation via the pool gives **16 December** against MacTutor's 3rd, possibly ceremony against registration; and no source reached puts the ceremony in the **11th arrondissement** or names **Geneva** as the bride's birthplace, so the stop takes the **canonical Paris pin** and says why.

**Geneva dropped as a stop.** The pool carried a Geneva entry for the bride's 1877 birth and conceded that Lebesgue never went there; a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, so the detail is cited on the marriage stop instead.

**Publications [A: MacTutor]:** Leçons sur l'intégration, 1904; Leçons sur les séries trigonométriques, 1906.

## V. Poitiers and the wait, 1906 to 1912

**Poitiers. [A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia.]** Chargé de cours 1906, professor of mechanics 1907, there to 1910. **The pool's "November 1906" and "the following November" are precisions MacTutor does not supply**; the file's months are inferred and flagged. The pin is the city centre, nothing reached identifying the faculty building of 1906; the modern campus is the wrong site and was **not** substituted.

**The son. [R]** Jacques Charles Lebesgue, Poitiers, **9 July 1908**, later a teacher at the Lycée de Caen. Dossier-sourced from records not independently checked; only the outline is corroborated, two children and Poitiers 1906-1910. **Sorbonne, 1910 [A for year and post: MacTutor]:** maître de conférences in mathematical analysis, and **the precise day, 16 November 1910, is not in MacTutor and could not be sourced elsewhere, so it is dropped**; the pool carried it under `[A]`.

**The 1912 ballot. [R]** "One of 35 votes" rests entirely on the pool's account of Gispert's archival research, unfetched; carried, tagged, and the sourcing said out loud in the campa itself. The **Prix Houllevigue** of the same year is separately `[A]`.

**ENS de Sèvres. [A for the teaching: MacTutor.]** Years not pinned down; the file uses 1925 as a declared placeholder, and the classroom manner comes from Félix's and Montel's testimony via the pool. **Foreign honours, on one deliberately Parisian stop.** LMS honorary member **1924**; Académie royale de Belgique **6 June 1931**; Officer of the Légion d'honneur **1932** [A: Wikidata Q206005], **the pool's "August" unsupported and dropped**; Royal Society **Foreign Member, 3 May 1934** [all A: MacTutor, with Wikidata for the 1934 date and for the correct category, ForMemRS rather than Fellow]. ## VIII. The last course and the death, 1941

**Tricycle, last course, dictated conics. [R]** Unable to walk, he hires a pedal tricycle to reach the Collège for a course on geometric constructions taken down by **Lucienne Félix**; from bed he dictates conics to **Mlle Beauvallet**, edited posthumously by **Montel**. All from the pool's synthesis of the Denjoy-Félix-Montel account (1957), unfetched. Split across two stops, the second's spring 1941 date existing only to order the segment.

**Death. [A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia.]** **26 July 1941**, Paris, aged 66. **Cause of death: a real gap, stated as one.** No certificate and no contemporary diagnosis located; tuberculosis is the recurrent attribution and the family history supports it, but it is not documented for him and is not asserted. The 11th-arrondissement precision is dossier-only.

**The grave: the weakest claim in the file, presented as weak. [R]** Wikidata Q206005 gives the Cimetière ancien de Gouvieux, Oise. Three problems, all printed on the stop: the reference attached to that claim, a page of L'Enseignement Mathématique vol. 38, **does not contain the word Gouvieux or any burial detail**; **Nominatim finds no such cemetery in the commune**; and the pool mis-transcribed the coordinate it was quoting. No funeral notice, register entry or photograph of a stone was found, and the burial date of 28 July is **invented purely to order the segment**.

**Centre and journal, two corrections.** The Centre's own site (**lebesgue.fr**) has it federating **six cities** in western France, not just Rennes and Nantes as the pool had it, and **its 2012 founding year is stated neither by the cited en.wikipedia page nor by the site**, so it is `[R]`. The **Annales Henri Lebesgue** are ENS Rennes-owned, Centre Mersenne-hosted and open access, but **the 2018 volume-one date is not stated on the about page**: the only year evidence is a copyright line, and the archive listing returned 404.

**Wiener, 1947. [A: en.wikipedia.]** He argued the Lebesgue integral had unexpected but important implications for the Gibbs foundations of statistical mechanics; original Wiener text not checked. **The persistent legend, recorded as legend [R]:** popular accounts credit Lebesgue with inventing measure and integration from nothing, where Jordan's content and Borel's measure came first.

## Interlocks

**Named in a campa (two).**

- **`joseph_fourier`**, on the 1906 Leçons sur les séries trigonométriques stop. Lebesgue's own relation and substantive: the second book applies the new integral to Fourier series. One-directional across eighty years, which the standing rule allows.
- **`norbert_wiener`**, on the 1947 afterlife stop. Wiener's engagement with the integral is documented three times in the pool: Hardy's Cambridge lectures of 1913-14, the 1920 Paris trip taken to get "the Lebesgue integral which I can get from it," and the 1947 Gibbs argument. Lebesgue influenced Wiener, a real relation in Lebesgue's direction. **Wiener's travelling is not staged here:** Cambridge and the 1920 trip stay on his own file, and only the 1947 argument, about the work rather than a place Wiener went, appears here, at Lebesgue's own pin.

**Considered and declined (four).** **`henri_poincare`**: the pool's link runs Poincaré to Borel to Lebesgue, which is Lebesgue-to-Borel plus Borel-to-Poincaré, not a relation between Lebesgue and Poincaré; the 1912 coincidence of Poincaré's death and Lebesgue's failed candidacy is coincidence of year and milieu. **`marie_curie`**: co-presence at the same faculty 1910-1921 and the 1911 Académie ballot set against Lebesgue's 1922 election, with no meeting, correspondence or citation either way; she was also cut from a draft of the rue Vauquelin campa, where the ESPCI radium connection would have been name-dropping via a building. **`evariste_galois`**: same institution sixty years apart, no engagement in the pool.

**`augustin_louis_cauchy`** is the near miss and is flagged as one. The Cauchy-Riemann integral is genuinely the ancestor of what Lebesgue generalised, and he very likely cited Cauchy in the historical opening of the 1904 Leçons, but **nothing in the verified pool attests that he did**, and the pool's own link is merely institutional. Declined rather than asserted on plausibility. **If a source turns up for Lebesgue citing Cauchy by name, this becomes a legitimate interlock and belongs on the 1904 stop.**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Lebesgue is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so nothing here was treated as licensed and nothing was silently deleted. **Direction caught: forward.** The whole "Afterwards" segment carries material postdating the 1941 death: Wiener's 1947 Gibbs argument, the abstraction of Lebesgue measure by Fréchet, Daniell, Radon and Carathéodory, a 1976 lunar crater, a 1996 asteroid, a 2012-ish research centre, a 2018-ish journal, a 2023 photograph of a plaque.

My reading is that this is **posthumous reception and commemoration, not apparatus applied forward**: documented facts about what happened to a dead man's work and name, each cited to a reachable source, rather than an interpretive extension of his framework onto material he never treated. It is nevertheless a file whose clock stopped in 1941 embedding artifacts from 1996 and 2023, so the operator may want to rule on where the line sits for afterlife segments generally. **The material is kept, not cut, and flagged rather than decided.**

No backward apparatus relation was found: Lebesgue's framework is mathematical rather than interpretive.
