# Leonardo da Vinci (1452-1519): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15. Slug `leonardo_da_vinci`. Companion file: `leonardo_da_vinci.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition the reachable record does not carry. Contradictions are flagged, never silently resolved. Calendar: Julian, Leonardo dying sixty-three years before the Gregorian reform.

## Method and its limits

The pool was gathered through five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife) and then verified adversarially, which corrected coordinates, dates, attributions, and one transcription error. Roughly a third of the first-pass pins were wrong by 300 m to 6 km. Where a correction exists, the journey file uses the corrected value.

No Leonardo manuscript was read in facsimile: the quotation layer rests on Richter's 1883 compilation and de Vere's 1912-15 Vasari, both read directly. Nothing here rests on an archive visited. See Gaps, below.

## 1. Vinci, 1452-1464

**[A]** Born 15 April 1452, out of wedlock, to Ser Piero da Vinci, a Florentine notary, and Caterina di Meo Lippi. His grandfather Antonio enters the birth in the family memorandum that night: "A grandson of mine was born April 15, Saturday, three hours into the night." (State Archive of Florence, via Kemp and Nicholl.)

**[R]** The *place* is not settled. The first pass tagged Anchiano [A]; English Wikipedia says it remains uncertain where he was born, and the Casa Natale is established only with *relativa certezza*. The pin is kept, the hedge moved into `date_confidence`.

**[A]** Baptised 16 April 1452 in Santa Croce at Vinci by the priest Piero di Bartolomeo Cecci, with ten godparents; the count comes from Antonio's memorandum via Nicholl and Kemp and is not in the Wikipedia article. **[A]** The catasto of 1457 shows the boy in Antonio da Vinci's household.

**[R]** The move to Florence. The chronology lens gave 1464; verification rejects the precision, since Wikipedia gives only "the mid-1460s" and 1464 is a conjecture tied to Antonio's death. Carried as inferred.

## 2. Florence, the bottega, 1466-1481

**[A]** Enters Verrocchio's workshop at about fourteen, is a formal apprentice by 1469, and is admitted to the Compagnia di San Luca as a master painter in 1472, while still working alongside Verrocchio (Vasari; guild records). **[R]** The bottega's exact address is not documented; the pin is flagged approximate.

**[A]** The earliest dated work is the pen-and-ink Arno landscape inscribed "5 August 1473," now in the Uffizi. The vantage point is not fixed by the inscription; the journey file pins the Vinci hills and says so.

**[R], and an internal contradiction.** Vasari's story that Verrocchio gave up painting on seeing Leonardo's angel in their joint *Baptism of Christ* is tagged [R] by the chronology lens ("apocryphal or exaggerated") and [A] by the geography lens. Verification rules [R] correct; the disagreement is recorded.

**[A]** April 1476: an anonymous *tamburo* denunciation names Leonardo and three others over the seventeen-year-old Jacopo Saltarelli. The Ufficiali di Notte open and drop the case twice for want of witnesses. (Atti degli Ufficiali di Notte, 1476, via Nicholl.)

**[A]** 1478: the San Bernardo altarpiece commission, Palazzo della Signoria. **[A]** 1480: the Anonimo Gaddiano puts him in the Medici circle and the San Marco garden. Neither is given a stop, for length.

**[R]** 1481: the Augustinians of San Donato a Scopeto commission the *Adoration of the Magi*, left in brown wash and underdrawing. The contract survives only through later scholars' citations of a lost archive, hence the [R].

## 3. First Milan, 1482-1499

**[A]** He goes to Ludovico Sforza around 1482. His draft letter (Codex Atlanticus; Richter 1340) lists nine military capabilities before mentioning, tenth, that he can also work in marble, bronze and clay and paint as well as any man. **[R]** Two arrival details are Vasari's alone: that Lorenzo de' Medici sent him partly as an envoy, and the silver horse-head lyre.

**Place-name correction [A].** The geography lens labelled the arrival "Milan, Castello Sforzesco (Corte Vecchia)" at 45.4707, 9.1791, conflating two buildings: the Corte Vecchia, where the studio and clay horse were, is the old ducal palace beside the Duomo. The journey file uses **45.4642, 9.19** for the studio stops and reserves 45.4707, 9.1791 for the Castello.

**[A]** 1483: the *Virgin of the Rocks* contracted by the Confraternity of the Immaculate Conception for San Francesco Grande. The valuation dispute runs a quarter of a century and produces two versions. The church was demolished in 1806; the pin approximates the site.

**[A]** From about 1489, systematic dissection and the right-to-left mirror hand, which most scholars attribute to left-handedness rather than secrecy. Richter item 796: "I have dissected more than ten human bodies... this I repeated twice, to learn the differences," and the warning that a student may be "deterred by the fear of living in the night hours in the company of those corpses, quartered and flayed and horrible to see."

**Quote correction [A].** "I wish to work miracles" was submitted as the *closing* line of item 796 and as earnest self-description. It is the **opening** line of the section headed "A general introduction," and Richter's footnote reads it as irony, the sentence running on into "those supreme fools, the necromancer and the enchanter." Not used as a stop quote.

**[A]** 1490: Pavia with Francesco di Giorgio Martini, to advise on the cathedral crossing tower; he studies the antique bronze Regisole there (cathedral fabric payment records). **[A]** 1493: the clay horse, over seven metres, unveiled for Bianca Maria Sforza's wedding; the bronze goes to cannon and French troops later shoot at the clay. **[A]** c.1494-1499: Vigevano, locks, sluices, irrigation, the Piazza Ducale layout (Codex Atlanticus hydraulic folios; Heydenreich 1954).

**[A]** 1495-1498: *The Last Supper*, oil and tempera on dry plaster, failing within his lifetime. Bandello recalls him standing before the wall half a day without touching it. **Canonical pin inherited:** 45.4664, 9.1706, as in `giulio_camillo.journey.json`. **[A]** 1498: the Sala delle Asse, mulberry branches knotted with gold cord, mulberry being *moro* and the duke il Moro. **[A]** 1499: the French take Milan; he leaves.

## 4. The road years, 1500-1503

**[A]** February 1500, Mantua: the chalk and pastel profile of Isabella d'Este, now in the Louvre. The promised painting is never begun, and she writes after it for four years. **[A]** March 1500, Venice: consulted on the Isonzo frontier against the Ottomans; proposes damming and releasing the river. Never built.

**[A]** 1501, Florence: housed by the Servites at Santissima Annunziata, he produces the Saint Anne cartoon instead of their altarpiece. Fra Pietro da Novellara tells Isabella d'Este that mathematics has made him impatient of the brush; Vasari describes two days of crowds.

**[A]** 1502: in Cesare Borgia's service as *architetto e ingegnere generale*, with a passport opening every fortress in the Romagna. The Imola map, now at Windsor, is an ichnographic plan drafted from measured survey rather than perspective convention, among the first of its kind in Europe.

**Date corrections [A].** Urbino: Borgia seized the city 21 June 1502, but Leonardo's Codex L notes there are dated **30 July 1502**; the first pass gave June. Cesena: his notebook entries are dated 6 August and the Borgia passport **18 August 1502**; the first pass gave July.

## 5. Florence again, 1503-1508

**[A]** 18 October 1503: he rejoins the painters' guild. Around now he begins the portrait of Lisa Gherardini, wife of Francesco del Giocondo. Vasari, the earliest source naming the sitter: "after toiling over it for four years, he left it unfinished." The four years are his claim alone, and there is no contract.

**Date correction and internal contradiction [A].** The *Battle of Anghiari*: the chronology lens gave 1504, the geography lens 1503-1506. Verification rules for **October 1503**, work continuing into 1506. Second correction: Michelangelo's *Battle of Cascina* reached cartoon stage and was never painted, so "companion" overstates what existed on the wall. Neither wall was finished.

**[A]** January 1504: the committee on Michelangelo's *David*. Leonardo argues for the sheltered Loggia dei Lanzi; the palace door is chosen (minutes ed. Milanesi). `michelangelo.journey.json` records the same meeting from the other side. **[A]** 9 July 1504: Ser Piero dies at eighty; Leonardo's note, Richter item 1372, gives the hour twice and is one of only two mentions of his father in the surviving pages.

**[A]** 1505: the *Codex on the Flight of Birds* and the Codex Atlanticus flight sheets. Richter item 1123: "Remember that your flying machine must imitate no other than the bat, because the web is what by its union gives the armour, or strength to the wings." **[R] the Monte Ceceri flight.** No contemporary document records a test flight. The first-pass provenance, nineteenth-century guidebooks recording Fiesole oral tradition, is wrong: Italian Wikipedia traces the story to Merezhkovsky's 1900 novel, which stages the crash in **Milan**, later fused with two genuine codex passages.

**Quote traced and retagged [A].** Submitted as unverified, the famous line is authentic. Italian original, **second cover recto** of the Codice sul volo degli uccelli: "Piglierà il primo volo il grande uccello, sopra del dosso del suo magno Cècero..." A draft on f. 18v names the mountain explicitly. The English "its great swan" is a gloss: *magno Cècero* is Monte Ceceri, and Florentines called swans *ceceri* for the chickpea knob on the beak, so the pun runs both ways at once. The journey file keeps "Cecero," flags the gloss, and carries the full sentence as the stop quote.

**[A]** 1507-1508: dissections at Santa Maria Nuova, including the old man he had spoken with hours before, opened "to see the cause of so sweet a death," and a child of two months.

## 6. Second Milan and Rome, 1506-1516

**[R]** 1506-1513: recalled by the governor Charles d'Amboise, serving Louis XII as *peintre et ingenieur ordinaire*, a title attested in royal correspondence of 1507. Tagged [R] for the loose range, not the substance.

**[A]** c.1507-1513: long stays at the Melzi villa at Vaprio d'Adda, tutoring Francesco Melzi and studying the Adda. **[A]** 1512, not 1511: the Trivulzio equestrian monument, abandoned amid war.

**[A]** 1513-1516, Rome: rooms in the Belvedere and a monthly allowance under Giuliano de' Medici, brother of Leo X. Optics, mirrors, botany in the Vatican gardens, the Pontine marshes scheme, and dissection until complaints to the pope close the hospital to him. Almost no painting.

**Contradiction, unresolved: the mechanical lion.** Chronology lens: 1517, in France. Geography lens: December 1515, Bologna. Verification: the mainstream attribution is **Lyon, July 1515**, at Francis I's entry, with **Argentan 1517** as a rival hypothesis. No original design survives. The Bologna meeting of king and pope is real and Leonardo was probably in Leo X's train; the lion is what does not belong to it. The journey file keeps the Bologna stop, states the dispute, and asserts no placement.

## 7. Clos Lucé, 1516-1519

**[A]** He crosses the Alps at sixty-four with Melzi, the servant Battista de Villanis, and three panels: the *Mona Lisa*, the *Saint John*, the *Virgin and Child with Saint Anne*. Francis I gives him Clos Lucé, a pension, and the title "first painter, engineer, and architect to the king."

**[R], reframed.** The first pass dismissed the underground passage to the château as local tradition. English Wikipedia states the manor "is 500 metres from the royal Chateau d'Amboise, to which it is connected by an underground passageway." The passage exists; what is unattested is that Francis I used it. The [R] flag belongs on the king's use, not the tunnel.

**[A]** October 1517: Antonio de Beatis, secretary to Cardinal Louis of Aragon, records the right arm paralysed, no more painting of that sweetness to be expected, drawing and teaching still possible. The only eyewitness physical description.

**[A]** 23 April 1519: the will, notarised by Guillaume Boreau. Papers and instruments to Melzi, who is also executor; garden and water rights to Battista de Villanis; the Santa Maria Nuova deposit to the half-brothers he had litigated against. **[A]** 2 May 1519: death at Clos Lucé, at sixty-seven.

**[R] the death scene.** Vasari: "his spirit, which was divine, knowing that it could not have any greater honour, expired in the arms of the King, in the seventy-fifth year of his age." The text is genuine Vasari, verified in de Vere; the event is not. He wrote decades later, never went to France, interviewed no witness, and his stated age is wrong by eight years.

**Correction to the counter-argument [A].** The first pass said Francis I was at Saint-Germain-en-Laye "that week" for his son's birth. Henry was born there on **31 March 1519**, a month before the death. The real objection is a **royal ordinance signed by Francis I at Saint-Germain-en-Laye on 3 May 1519**, the day after. The journey file uses the ordinance.

**[A]** Burial in Saint-Florentin inside the château grounds, per the will. The funeral day is fixed by no document read here; the traditional 12 August 1519 is carried from the standard biographies, flagged as such.

## 8. Afterlife

**[A]** Melzi carries the papers to Vaprio d'Adda and keeps them fifty years; his heirs disperse them by gift, sale, and dismemberment. The Codex Atlanticus, assembled from loose sheets by Pompeo Leoni, reaches the Biblioteca Ambrosiana in 1637. **Correction [A]:** the figure of roughly 13,000 pages is a modern scholarly estimate of what he left, of which about 7,000 survive, not a number Leonardo gave.

**[A]** Saint-Florentin falls in after the Revolution and is demolished 1802-1810 on Roger Ducos's orders; the grave marker is lost.

**[A/R]** In 1863 Arsène Houssaye excavates the site and reports a skeleton with slab fragments reading "EO... DUS VINC...". The bones are reinterred in 1874 in the chapel of Saint-Hubert under a slab reading *Ci-git Leonard de Vinci*. No test has confirmed the identification, and the 2016 "Leonardo Project" has published no genetic result.

**[A]** Not plotted, for length and geography: the Codex Leicester, bought by Thomas Coke in **1719** (not 1717, a first-pass error) and by Bill Gates at Christie's New York on 11 November 1994 for $30,802,500, its status as the only Leonardo manuscript in private hands being an unsourced commonplace; Pietro Magni's bronze in the Piazza della Scala, 4 September 1872; the Museo Leonardiano at Vinci, 1953; the 1911 theft of the *Mona Lisa* by Vincenzo Peruggia and its 1913 recovery in Florence.

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **The Verrocchio angel**: [R] in the chronology lens, [A] in the geography lens. Ruled [R]; the disagreement stands on the record.
2. **The mechanical lion**: Bologna December 1515, France 1517, Lyon July 1515, Argentan 1517. Not adjudicated.
3. **Vaprio d'Adda and Clos Lucé**: for each, two lenses gave two different wrong points for one place; each corrected to a third value.
4. **Battle of Anghiari start**: 1504 vs 1503-1506. Ruled 1503.
5. **The Houssaye excavation year**: 1863 in two lenses, about 1849 implied by the château's own article. 1863 is better attested.
6. **The Château d'Amboise pin**: the corpus canonical 47.4078, 0.9848 sits about 700 m from the verified 47.41368, 0.98603. The canonical value is kept for continuity with `abdelkader.journey.json` and the discrepancy recorded here.
7. **Vasari's age at death**: seventy-fifth year, against a documented sixty-seven.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- No manuscript, codex facsimile, or archival document was consulted directly. Everything primary arrives through a translation or a citation. The Codex on the Flight of Birds was reached only through the Italian Wikipedia article that quotes it; the Turin manuscript was not read.
- Caterina, his mother, is a near-blank: a name, "a peasant woman," nothing else. Recent scholarship proposing an enslaved Circassian origin is not in the pool and is not asserted here.
- The Verrocchio bottega has no securely documented address. The start of the *Mona Lisa* rests on Vasari plus the 1503 guild entry, not on a contract. The 1485 Hungary journey was dropped rather than plotted on a guess.
- No Spanish-edition check was performed on this file.
