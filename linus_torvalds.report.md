# Linus Torvalds: research report
*Atlas of Journeys working file. Subject living; born 28 December 1969, Helsinki. Slug `linus_torvalds`. Journey: `linus_torvalds.journey.json`, 7 segments, 43 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference · **GAP** something the pool does not contain and which is not invented here.

The five research lenses contradict each other in six places, indexed in section 9. None is adjudicated; each is carried in its stop's `date_confidence`.

This is the atlas's first living computing subject, and the afterlife lens returned a formal GAP: no tomb, no grave, no death date, nothing for the "tomb and its fate" axis to hold. It flagged that and refused to invent one. The closing stop, *Dunthorpe, the log that is the monument*, answers structurally, stating that there is nothing to visit and naming the public commit history as the only trace. That is a reading, written as a reading.

---

## 1. Helsinki, 1969-1990

**[A: Wikipedia, 'Linus Torvalds']** Born 28 December 1969 in Helsinki to the journalists Anna and Nils Torvalds; named after the chemist Linus Pauling. Maternal grandfather the statistician Leo Törnqvist, paternal grandfather the poet Ole Torvalds; the parents politically active communists in 1970s Finland. The family belongs to Finland's Swedish-speaking minority, which is why the conscription entry falls where it does.

**[A: Wikipedia]** c. 1981, around age eleven: given the run of his maternal grandfather's Commodore VIC-20, programs in BASIC. **[A: Just for Fun (2001)]** adds the texture: listings copied by hand from magazines because buying software was not an option.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1988: enrolls at the University of Helsinki. **[A: geography lens]** The department stood on Teollisuuskatu; he joined the research group Nodes under Martti Tienari; he owned a Sinclair QL. The degree runs to 1996/97.

**[A: Wikipedia; 'Nyland Brigade']** 1989: eleven months with the Nyland Brigade, the Navy's Swedish-speaking unit, at Dragsvik garrison, Ekenäs (Raseborg); commissioned second lieutenant.
> **Coordinate corrected.** The geography lens gave 59.9744 / 23.4128, about 4.5 km west of the garrison, which the Nyland Brigade article puts at 59°59'10"N 23°29'32"E. **Journey uses 59.9861 / 23.4921.**

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1990: first Unix, on a university DEC MicroVAX running ULTRIX.

**GAP.** No lens names a street or building for any Helsinki residence. All Helsinki-home stops carry the city coordinate 60.1699 / 24.9384 and say so.

## 2. 1991: the whole thing, in one year

**[A: Wikipedia]** 5 January: buys an Intel 80386 IBM PC clone, partly on credit, initially to run Minix.

**[A: Torvalds, 'LINUX's History' (31 July 1992), cs.cmu.edu mirror, fetched directly]** 25 August: the comp.os.minix post, subject "What would you like to see most in minix?". The quotes lens reached this **directly**, in Torvalds's own 1992 chronicle, not through paraphrase; the opening line and the postscript ("Yes - it's free of any minix code... It is NOT protable", typo original) are verbatim from that fetch. Same source, 26 August: "Simply, I'd say that porting is impossible." 5 October, the 0.02 announcement: "do you pine for the nice days of minix-1.1" and "This is a program for hackers by a hacker." 19 December: "/I/ think it's better than minix, but I'm a bit prejudiced" (verified, not used).

**[A: Wikipedia 'History of Linux'; afterlife lens]** 17 September: kernel 0.01 uploaded to ftp.funet.fi; the administrator Ari Lemmke renames the directory from Freax to Linux without asking, and Torvalds accepts it after the fact.
> **Two corrections.** (1) The chronology and geography lenses both placed the FTP host at the **University of Helsinki**. It was at the **Helsinki University of Technology, Otaniemi, Espoo** (60.1866 / 24.8285), a different institution across the water; the afterlife lens has it right. **Journey uses Otaniemi.** (2) The geography lens said the upload "predates the announcement by three weeks"; it **follows** the 25 August post. Not carried forward.

**[A: RELNOTES-0.01, cdn.kernel.org Historic archive, fetched directly]** Two lines verbatim: "As the version number (0.01) suggests this is not a mature product" (section 1) and "the system seems to be relatively stable (\"crashme\" hasn't - yet)" (section 4). Signed: *Linus Torvalds, Petersgatan 2 A 2, 00140 Helsingfors 14, Finland.*
> **Date contradiction.** The quotes lens dated this October 1991 three times, then corrected itself: 0.01 shipped 17 September, October is 0.02, and **the document carries no internal date**; the kernel.org timestamp of 23 June 1993 is an archive re-upload. Journey dates the stop to the 0.01 release and says the document is undated.

**[A: Wikipedia]** Autumn 1991: Lars Wirzenius takes him to hear Richard Stallman; the GPL decision follows.
> **Coordinate corrected.** The chronology lens placed the talk in Helsinki. Wikipedia's own sentence says the **Helsinki University of Technology**, i.e. Otaniemi, **Espoo**. Journey uses 60.1866 / 24.8285, the same pin as the funet upload and, twenty-one years later, the Aalto Nvidia episode.

## 3. 1992-1997: version one, the penguin, the degree

**[A: Linux-Activists list, 5 May 1992, in 'LINUX's History']** The retrospective account of the kernel growing out of a terminal emulator: "my megalomaniac ideas to make 'a better minix that minix'" (the doubled *that* is original).

**[A: Wikipedia 'Tux (mascot)']** 1993: bitten by a little (fairy) penguin at the National Zoo & Aquarium, Canberra, on his first Australian trip, made to address the Australian Unix Users Group; cited ever after as the origin of the mascot.
> **Coordinate corrected.** The afterlife lens gave -35.2678 / 149.0637, near Black Mountain, about 3.8 km off. The zoo is at 35°18'2"S 149°4'10"E. **Journey uses -35.3006 / 149.0694.**

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1993: meets Tove Monni, six-time Finnish national karate champion, formerly his student in a computer course. **[R]** 1997: they marry; no consulted source names a venue; three daughters follow. The journey folds the marriage into the meeting stop and states the gap there. **[A: 'History of Linux']** 14 March 1994: kernel 1.0.

**[A: Wikipedia 'Tux (mascot)']** 1996: Larry Ewing draws Tux in GIMP 0.54; James Hughes coins the name, read as tuxedo and as (T)orvalds (U)ni(X). **GAP:** no lens placed Ewing or the drawing geographically. The stop is pinned at Helsinki on the attested detail that Torvalds refined the mascot on the kernel mailing list, and does not claim the artwork was made in Finland.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1996: asteroid 9793 named Torvalds.
> **Conflation corrected.** The chronology lens claimed "one of the moons of the double asteroid he was earlier honored with is separately named Linus", then caught itself. **Linus is not a satellite of 9793 Torvalds.** It is the satellite of asteroid **22 Kalliope**, found 29 August 2001 by Margot and Brown, IAU name approved July 2003, honouring Linus of Thrace, Linus Torvalds and Linus van Pelt jointly; designation (22) Kalliope I.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1996/1997: master's thesis, *Linux: A Portable Operating System*, Nodes group. **Contradiction:** geography lens dates the thesis 1996-1997, chronology lens the degree 1997. Journey uses 1997.

## 4. 1997-2003: the crossing

**[A: Wikipedia]** February 1997: relocates to Santa Clara and joins Transmeta, to mid-2003, working on the low-power Crusoe while maintaining the kernel. An earlier 1996 visit **[A]** produced the offer; cut as redundant. **[R: geography lens]** The 1997-2003 household sits somewhere in the Santa Clara / San Jose corridor; **no source names a street**. Used at 37.3541 / -121.9552 for the mailing-list-as-government stop.

**[R: quotes lens]** "Talk is cheap. Show me the code." Universally attributed to a linux-kernel message of 25 August 2000. **The quotes lens could not verify it**: lkml.org served a bot-challenge page, marc.info returned HTTP 410. Carried with the non-verification written into `quote_source` itself.

**Honorary doctorates, unresolved.** For **Stockholm University** the afterlife lens gives **1999**, reading Wikipedia's "two years later he received honorary doctor status" against an antecedent of 1997; the geography lens gives **2000** from press, marked [R]. For the **University of Helsinki, 2000** **[R]**, secondary sources are consistent and no primary announcement was reached. Journey uses 1999 for Stockholm.

**[A: Wikipedia]** EFF Pioneer Award 1998; Ada Lovelace Medal and Royal Swedish Academy of Engineering Sciences 2000; Time ranks him 17th among the century's most important people, 2000; Takeda Award 2001, shared with Richard Stallman and Ken Sakamura. One stop, dated to the middle of the run, individual years in `date_confidence`. **[A]** 2001: *Just for Fun*, with David Diamond, HarperCollins, New York; **[R]** Finnish, German and Japanese editions are asserted but no accessible source consolidates the roster.

**[A: Wikipedia]** June 2003: leaves Transmeta; joins the Open Source Development Labs, Beaverton, Oregon. **Tension:** the chronology lens gives June 2003 for both events, the geography lens an unmonthed 2003 for OSDL. Journey: departure June, fellowship July, month marked a placeholder.

## 5. 2004-2008: Oregon, and four days

**[A: Wikipedia; 'Dunthorpe, Oregon']** June 2004: the family moves to Dunthorpe, unincorporated, on the Willamette near Portland.
> **Coordinate corrected, repeatedly.** Every Dunthorpe stop in the geography lens used 45.4184 / -122.654, about 1.9 km too far south, inside Lake Oswego; Dunthorpe is at 45°26'9"N 122°39'14"W, which the afterlife lens already had. **The journey uses 45.4358 / -122.6539 at all five Dunthorpe stops.**

**[A: Wikipedia 'Git']** 3 April 2005: after Larry McVoy withdraws the kernel team's free BitKeeper licence, following a reverse-engineering effort by another developer, Torvalds begins Git, targeting patch application under three seconds. Announced 6 April; **self-hosting 7 April**; first multi-branch merge 18 April; target met 29 April; managing the 2.6.12 kernel release 16 June.
> **Two corrections.** (1) The afterlife lens said "roughly ten days", unsourced and an understatement; the documented milestone is self-hosting in **four days**. (2) The geography lens's note here was **textually corrupted**: "Git is publicly announced on April 6, 1991 minus fourteen years to the day of Linux's own first Usenet post's spirit" is garbled and carries a wrong year. None of it survives into the journey.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 2005: hands Git's maintenance to Junio Hamano; Vollum Award, Reed College, Portland. **[A: GitFaq, archive.kernel.org mirror, fetched directly]** "I'm an egotistical bastard, and I name all my projects after myself. First 'Linux', now 'Git'." **[A]** 2006: Time's European edition, "revolutionary hero"; not used. **[A]** 2007: OSDL and the Free Standards Group merge into the Linux Foundation, his employer since.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 2008: Computer History Museum Hall of Fellows. **Lens disagreement on evidentiary status:** chronology [A] from Wikipedia, interlock **[R]**, no atlas file stating it. Both readings sit in `date_confidence`. The *coordinate* is not in doubt: 37.4144 / -122.0774 is the atlas's recurring CHM pin, inherited byte-identical.

## 6. 2010-2019

**[A: Wikipedia]** 2010: naturalized as a US citizen, registers to vote, retains Finnish citizenship; same year, the C&C Prize from NEC; **GAP:** no venue for either. **[A]** 2011: begins Subsurface, an open-source dive log; 2012: hands the lead to Dirk Hohndel.

**2012: Internet Hall of Fame. Lens disagreement.** Chronology lens: **inaugural class, inducted 23 April 2012 at Global INET, Geneva**, marked confirmed. Geography lens: [R] throughout, "could not confirm the precise 2012 ceremony city against a primary source", its Geneva coordinate "a reasonable placeholder". Journey uses Geneva 46.2044 / 6.1432, dates the stop 23 April 2012, records both readings.

**[A: Wikipedia 'Millennium Technology Prize']** 2012: Millennium Technology Prize, shared with Shinya Yamanaka.
> **Three corrections.** (1) **Inaugural** is wrong wherever the pool uses it: the prize was first awarded in 2004 to Tim Berners-Lee, 2012 being the ninth cycle. What *was* a first is that 2012 is the first **shared** award. (2) The geography lens tagged its Finlandia Hall stop **[A]** while admitting in its own note that the venue was unverified. **Neither the English nor the Finnish Wikipedia article gives a 2012 venue or date**, so 13 June and Finlandia Hall are both unconfirmed; the journey pins Helsinki city centre and says so. (3) The pool's citation wording is off; the official phrasing, quoted in the journey, begins "in recognition of his creation of...".

**[R: widely reported]** 14 June 2012: at an Aalto University Q&A in Espoo he attacks Nvidia's Linux driver support and gives the camera the finger. The quotes lens **could not verify** the wording against a primary transcript or the video; carried with the non-verification stated in `quote_source`.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 2014: IEEE Computer Pioneer Award, 23 April widely reported, **no ceremony city fixed in any consulted source**; the geography lens pinned it at his Oregon home and said so.

**[A/R: IEEE records; CES 2018 trade press]** January 2018: IEEE Masaru Ibuka Consumer Electronics Award, at CES, Las Vegas. **Correction:** the 12 January date is unverified, softened to January 2018; the IEEE page says only that the award is usually presented at CES in Las Vegas early in the year. The geography lens's note here was also **textually corrupted**: "without ceremhttp talk of legacy".

**[A: TED.com talk record]** February 2016: TED2016, Vancouver, "The mind behind Linux". **[A: Wikipedia]** September 2018: the kernel community adopts a formal Code of Conduct after sustained criticism of his management style; he publicly apologizes and takes a temporary break from maintaining the kernel. **[A]** 2019: named among the Carnegie Corporation's Great Immigrants honorees. **GAP:** no ceremony or venue; the journey pins New York, the corporation's seat, and says so.

## 7. Present

**[A: kernel.org release announcements; Linux Foundation statements]** Still releasing and merging kernel versions from the Portland area; still, by rough consensus, the project's final authority. **[R: afterlife lens, not reverified by live search]** His most visible twenty-first-century trace is a commit log rather than a monument; a reading, treated as one in the closing stop. **[R]** Raymond's "given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow" (1997), named Linus's Law in his honour: not reverified, not used.

## 8. Interlocks with existing atlas travelers

**Used, and where.** **Guido van Rossum** [A: QUEUE.md line 334 names him the counterpart when the subject was queued], at the Linux Foundation stop, where the Python Software Foundation of 2001 and the Linux Foundation of 2007 are the same custody structure twice [R]: this one stays, since it is a real structural/thematic parallel used to explain Torvalds's own institutional choice, not a shared-coordinate coincidence. **Alan Turing** and von Neumann as the lineage of every machine Linux runs on, at the 386 stop [R]: stays, real technical lineage. **Bob Kahn** and **Vint Cerf** at the Internet Hall of Fame stop, inducted in the same ceremony class as the protocols' authors [A in their own atlas files]; the lens's CNRI-to-lkml governance-lineage claim is **not** asserted: stays, a real shared occasion.

**Considered and not used**, removed under the house-style tightening of 2026-08-14, each a coincidental crossing with no relation to Torvalds: **van Rossum** (a second time, as a fellow Computer History Museum Fellow a decade apart) and **Whitfield Diffie** and the **Charles Babbage** Difference Engine replica, all named only for shared Fellow status or a museum exhibit at the CHM stop. **Claude Shannon**, named only as a decorative aside about Bell Labs' wartime history in the birth-year campa; the real point of that stop, Thompson and Ritchie writing Unix the year Torvalds was born, stays. **Hilma af Klint**, rediscovered in Helsinki the same city and roughly the same years Torvalds was a child there, campa itself admitting "has never heard of her." **Laura Renée Maier**, staging Wild Woman in Portland decades after Torvalds's Vollum Award, campa itself admitting "nothing to do with each other."

**Found, not used.** **Margaret Murray**'s 1936 Helsinki lecture (thirty-three years' gap); **Ralph Merkle** at Elxsi and Diffie at Northern Telecom (Valley co-presence, no shared event, the lens's own weakest tie); **George Balanchine**'s Karelian summers "toward Finland" (a border case the lens itself did not recommend).

**Negative finding, honoured.** No attested connection to the canonical default pins (Kaaba 21.4225/39.8262, Temple Mount 31.778/35.2354, Paris 48.8566/2.3522, Buenos Aires -34.6037/-58.3816). **None appears anywhere in the journey.**

## 10. Gaps stated as gaps

- No street address for any Helsinki residence in any lens. Petersgatan 2 A 2, from the RELNOTES-0.01 signature, was geolocated by nobody; it is quoted, not pinned.
- No venue for the 1997 wedding; none, and no date, for the 2012 Millennium ceremony; none for the 2010 naturalization, the 2010 C&C Prize, the 2014 IEEE ceremony, or the 2019 Carnegie honour. No placement for Ewing's Tux artwork; no roster of *Just for Fun* translations.
- No contemporaneous documentation of the pre-August-1991 kernel work: that spring is reconstructed entirely from Torvalds's later retellings, principally *Just for Fun*, which the chronology lens flagged itself as [R]. The journey says so in the memoir stop rather than narrating the spring as recorded fact.
- No afterlife axis at all: the subject is living. Flagged, not fabricated, answered in the closing stop by naming the absence.
