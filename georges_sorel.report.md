# Georges Sorel (1847-1922): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-22 for `georges_sorel.journey.json`: the Ponts et Chaussées engineer who resigned at forty-five and spent thirty years writing the philosophy of the general strike. 40 stops, 8 segments.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or inference. Where the gathering and verification passes disagreed, both readings are printed. Where two published sources disagree, the disagreement is printed and **not adjudicated**.

**Caution on the whole file.** Every reachable source for Sorel's *life*, as against his books, was an encyclopaedia article: English, French and Italian Wikipedia, plus a summary at laviedesidees.fr. Those three contradict each other on the engineering career, the burial, the reception of *Réflexions*, and Lenin. No monograph was reachable. The **quotations** are solid: Wikisource's Hulme translation of 1915, checked line by line. The file is strong on what Sorel wrote and weak on where he stood on any given day.

## 1. Cherbourg and the schooling, 1847-1869

- Born **Cherbourg, 2 November 1847** **[A, FR + EN]**.
- Father a merchant **in oils and carbonated waters**, "marchand d'huiles et d'eaux gazeuses"; mother "une mère très pieuse" **[A, FR Wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the gathering pass had the trade as *wine and mineral water*. Wrong, not carried.
- Cousin of the historian **Albert Sorel** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**. No correspondence was found; the campa asserts only the kinship.
- **Paris, 1864, Collège Rollin** **[A, EN Wikipedia: "he moved to Paris in 1864 to attend the Collège Rollin"]**. **Correction:** the gathering pass said "the family moves to Paris." No source says the family moved.
- **Anachronism corrected.** The gathering geography pinned the Collège Rollin at 48.8809 / 2.3406, the avenue Trudaine building. FR Wikipedia "Lycée Jacques-Decour" dates that move to **1876**, twelve years after Sorel arrived; in 1864 the school stood on the **rue des Postes**, today rue Lhomond, and the file uses **48.8428 / 2.3468**.
- **École Polytechnique, promotion X1865** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**. Coordinates corrected from the generic Paris centroid (48.8566 / 2.3522, ~1.3 km off) to the rue Descartes campus, **48.8465 / 2.3486**.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable on his rank leaving Polytechnique, his passage through the École des Ponts et Chaussées, the family's finances, or any politics before 1893.

## 2. The Corps des Ponts et Chaussées, 1869-1892

The phase where the gathering pass invented most and the record is thinnest.

- **1869, enters the Corps** **[R]**. EN Wikipedia: "In 1869 he became chief engineer with the Department of Public Works." **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** a man entering Polytechnique in 1865 leaves the Ponts school about 1869 at twenty-one, an *ingénieur ordinaire*, and FR Wikipedia attaches "ingénieur en chef" only to the terminal **Perpignan** post. The file says "state engineer" for 1869.
- **Corsica, until June 1871** **[A, EN Wikipedia: "Stationed in Corsica until June 1871"]**. Only the *end* is attested; the gathering pass's **1870** start is in no source, dropped. The stop's 1869-01-01 is a declared ordering device. **Gap:** no source names the Corsican town; Ajaccio stands in as the island's administrative seat.
- **Albi, Gap, Draguignan** **[R]**. EN Wikipedia says only that after Corsica "he was subsequently posted to various places in southern France", naming the three: **no dates, no ordering**. The gathering pass assigned "1873" and tagged it [A]; an invention. Defensible window **1871-1876**; all three tagged [R] with ordering dates.
- **Mostaganem, colonial Algeria, 1876-1879** **[A, EN Wikipedia]**. The one firmly dated posting. **Honest gap, a large one:** nothing records what he built there or what he thought of the administration he served. The campa states the silence instead of filling it.
- **Perpignan, ingénieur en chef du service hydraulique, to the 1892 resignation** **[A, FR Wikipedia for the title; EN Wikipedia for "the last years of his career until his retirement in 1892"]**. The gathering pass dated the appointment **1880**, in no source; the file gives c. 1879-1892. Coordinates corrected to **42.6986 / 2.8956** from 42.6886 / 2.8948, ~1.1 km south.
- **First books, 1889: *Le Procès de Socrate* and *Contribution à l'étude profane de la Bible*** **[A, laviedesidees.fr; FR Wikipedia]**. The firm anchor, replacing the gathering pass's "1886," manufactured out of "second half of the 1880s."
- **Influences.** The gathering pass listed **Aristotle, Taine, Renan**, sourced to FR Wikipedia, whose list is **Proudhon, Marx, Vico, Bergson**. The first triad is not carried; only the second appears, each name tied to an attested moment.
- **Légion d'honneur, 1891** **[A, EN Wikipedia]**. Decree date and Léonore dossier not reached.
- **Resignation, 1892, at forty-five** **[A, EN + FR]**.

## 3. Boulogne, the second life, 1892-1899

- Retires to the Paris region and settles at **Boulogne-sur-Seine** with **Marie Euphrasie David**, whom he never marries, reportedly because of his mother's opposition **[A, EN + FR]**. **Gap:** the street address is in no consulted source, nor her age, origin or cause of death.
- **Coordinate variance across the three lenses:** 48.8356 / 2.24, 48.8357 / 2.241, 48.8352 / 2.2405, all within ~200 m. The file uses **48.8357 / 2.241** throughout, so one house does not scatter.
- **Bergson's lectures at the Collège de France** **[A, EN Wikipedia, "whose lectures at the Collège de France he attended"; FR Wikipedia to the same effect]**. Coordinates corrected to **48.8492 / 2.3456** from 48.8459 / 2.3453, ~365 m south, nearer the Panthéon. **Gap:** no source gives the years or the courses; the date orders the segment only.
- **1893, public adherence to Marxism and socialism** **[A, EN + FR]**. No source names the article or occasion.
- **L'Ère nouvelle, Le Devenir social, L'Humanité nouvelle**, mid-to-late 1890s **[A, FR Wikipedia]**; the 1894 date is an ordering device **[R]**.
- **Marie David dies, 1897** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**; *Réflexions* is later dedicated to her **[A]**.

## 4. The crisis of Marxism and the syndicalist decade, 1898-1908

- **Takes Bernstein's side in the "crisis of Marxism"; reformist, Dreyfusard phase** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**, tied to the revisionism controversy of about 1898-1900 **[R for year]**.
- **Le Mouvement socialiste**, Lagardelle's Paris review, roughly **1899-1914** **[A, EN Wikipedia]**. Street address not found; the stop sits at the standing corpus Paris pin.
- **Édouard Berth** **[A as disciple; R as to when]**. The gathering pass tagged the alliance [A] while citing "general secondary literature on Sorel's circle", not a citation, and called Berth "eventual biographer of his ideas", which nothing supports. He is carried as attested: a polemicist in his own right, author of *Les Méfaits des intellectuels* (1914).

### The serialization tangle, resolved

The gathering chronology called this a **[R]** conflict, EN and FR Wikipedia "disagreeing by about a year." They do not disagree; they describe **two serializations**: **Il Divenire sociale**, Rome, in Italian, from **1905** **[A, EN Wikipedia]**, and **Le Mouvement socialiste**, Paris, in French, in **1906** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**. Book form **Paris, 1908**, with *Les Illusions du progrès* **[A, EN + FR]**. The afterlife lens compounded this by crediting the 1906 claim to EN Wikipedia, which says otherwise.

**Geographic error corrected.** The gathering geography put the 1908 book at **Perpignan's** coordinates. Sorel left Perpignan for good in 1892 and the book appeared in Paris; the stop is at the Paris pin.

**Decision: Sorel is never staged in Italy.** The Rome serialization, the Italian contacts and the Italian press of 1918-1922 are real **[A/R as marked]**, but no consulted source has him travelling to Italy. Under the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was there, all three sit at **his house at Boulogne**, with the Italian destination named in the campa. This costs the map its only Italian pin.

## 5. The detour through the right, 1909-1913

- **Action française, marginal and brief, 1909-1910** **[A, EN Wikipedia]**. How deep it went is disputed; the file states the dispute, does not settle it.
- **Article on Charles Péguy's Joan of Arc in *Action française*, 14 April 1910** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**. The only event in his intellectual life this pass could date to the day.
- **La Cité française**, planned with **Georges Valois** and **Édouard Berth**, short-lived **[A, EN Wikipedia, "Cercle Proudhon"]**.
- **Cercle Proudhon**: first meeting **17 November 1911**, founded **16 December 1911** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the gathering pass said Sorel "later disputes the extent of his connection." FR Wikipedia has him **hostile from the start**, fearing the Circle would render "les jeunes gens moins aptes à comprendre Proudhon." The dispute is over how sincere and sustained that hostility was, not over a later disavowal.
- **L'Indépendance, 1911-1913**, with Berth and Valois, left over the review's nationalist tone **[A, EN + FR]**. Sources do not order this against the Cercle's founding; the file dates it 1912 inside the window.

## 6. War, Bolshevism, the last years, 1914-1922

- **Opposes the Union sacrée, 1914** **[A, EN Wikipedia]**. Date of the statement not recorded.
- **Praises the Russian Revolution; calls Lenin "the greatest theorist socialism has had since Marx," 1917** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**.
- **After 1917, writes for a Soviet-aligned publication and for Italian papers defending the Bolsheviks** **[A, EN + FR]**. The gathering pass dated this **1918**, invented from a "post-1917" range; the file uses 1919 to order.
- **Correction, the most consequential here.** The gathering pass appended "while remaining hostile to Gabriele D'Annunzio and the rising fascist movement", tagged [A]. The cited source says otherwise: EN Wikipedia has Sorel describing **Mussolini** approvingly as "a leader of a consecrated battalion, saluting the flags of Italy with his sword," and notes that after his death "many former syndicalists welcomed the emerging fascism"; his attitude to Fiume is reported ambivalent, not hostile. The clause is dropped and the journey states the contradiction: both the Lenin sentence and the Mussolini sentence are his, and he died **two months before the March on Rome**.
- ***Matériaux d'une théorie du prolétariat*, 1919** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**.
- **Dies at Boulogne-sur-Seine, 29 August 1922, aged 74** **[A, EN + FR]**.

## 7. The grave, and what happened at it

- **Buried at Tenay (Ain), 1922** **[A, FR Wikipedia]**: "dans le caveau de son fils adoptif Jean-Baptiste David," resting "en face de sa compagne", opposite Marie David rather than beside her.
  - **Contradiction between lenses, resolved to the source.** One made Jean-Baptiste **Sorel's** adoptive son, another **Marie David's**. FR Wikipedia's wording is "son fils adoptif", i.e. Sorel's.
  - **Three lenses, three Tenay pins**, two wrong: 45.9436 / 5.5127 (~2.8 km north) and 45.9578 / 5.5093 (~4.4 km north, the largest coordinate error in the pool). The file uses 45.9186 / 5.5106 (FR Wikipedia, "Tenay"). Only the **year** is attested; the file's 1922-09-01 is a declared ISO default.
- **October 1932, the monument offered and refused** **[A, IT Wikipedia]**: "Il regime fascista, informato dello stato di abbandono in cui versa la tomba di Sorel, si offre di assumerne la cura e di elevarvi un monumento." The family refused, saying the grave's condition concerned only the family. **Correction:** the gathering pass named "the Italian ambassador to France, acting for Mussolini"; the source names **the regime**.
  - **Judgement for the operator.** This stop is posthumous; its actors are the Italian state and the family, not Sorel. It is kept, at his own grave, on the reading that a man's tomb is his own object and the regime's relation to him direct and attested. It is the only stop staging an event Sorel was not present at, and it is flagged rather than slipped past.
- **No monument was ever built** **[R, inferred from silence in EN/FR/IT Wikipedia]**.
- **Streets named for him** at Boulogne-Billancourt, Perpignan, Cherbourg-en-Cotentin, Le Port (Réunion), Saint-Cyprien **[A, FR Wikipedia]**, **undated**. The gathering chronology gave them **1950** while its own note admitted no date is in the source: a fabricated year inside an [A] entry. Carried **without a date**.

### Afterlife recorded but not made into stops

- **Translations.** First Italian, Antonio Sarno for Laterza, **1909**, introduced by **Benedetto Croce** **[A, IT Wikipedia]**. English by **T. E. Hulme**: **contradiction not resolved**, EN Wikipedia's "Reflections on Violence" gives **1912**, its Hulme article dates his translator's preface to *The New Age* XVII.24 (**1915**), the Internet Archive scan records Allen & Unwin, **1915**. The gathering pass asserted a bare **1914** as [A]; nothing supports it. Revised edition (Hulme and Roth) **1950**, Dover **2004**, Jennings's Cambridge edition **1999** **[A, EN Wikipedia]**.
- **Mussolini calls Sorel "our master"** **[A, IT/EN]**.
- **Lenin: quotation withdrawn.** The gathering pass had Lenin dismissing Sorel as **"a notorious scribbler"**, sourced to EN Wikipedia, which states no opinion of Lenin's and does not contain the word. IT Wikipedia has the substance in a footnote: "Lenin non aveva alcuna simpatia per Sorel, tacciato di confusionario." The journey says, unquoted, that Lenin thought him a muddler.
- **Sternhell**, *Ni droite ni gauche* (1983), reopened the fascism question **[R]**; the estimate that ~80 percent of fascism's early leadership passed through Sorelian syndicalism is his **[A, EN/IT Wikipedia]**, a suggested_ref only.
- FR Wikipedia's judgement that Sorel is "more known abroad than in France," claimed by Gramsci and Lukács on the left and Schmitt on the right **[A]**. Historiography, not an event.

## Quotations: verified and withdrawn

All fourteen quotations in the journey come from Wikisource's **Reflections on Violence, trans. T. E. Hulme (1915)**, checked verbatim. Two needed repair:

1. **"Working-class violence possesses an extraordinary efficacity in strikes."** Sorel's words, but a **mid-sentence clause dressed as a sentence**. The file restores the whole printed sentence, "It is becoming more and more evident every day that..." through "...compel them to give way." (ch. I)
2. **"I am more than ever convinced of the value of this philosophy of violence."** Verbatim, but the gathering pass cited a "Preface to the Third French Edition" that does not exist in the 1915 volume. The passage is in the front-matter note headed **"A Note to the Third French Edition"** (djvu p. 11); the file quotes it with its preceding sentence and the right heading.

**Withdrawn.** "Proletarian violence entirely changes the aspect of all the conflicts in which it intervenes," attributed to the introductory Letter to Daniel Halévy, reached the pool only through a secondary AI-summarised fetch; the Wikisource page returned 404 and the wording is unconfirmed. Tagged [R] in the pool, excluded from the journey, because a `quote` field must be verbatim.
