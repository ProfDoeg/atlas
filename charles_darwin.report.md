# Charles Darwin: the Beagle years in Argentina and Patagonia

*Research report, 2026-08-14. Slug `charles_darwin`. Brief as carried in QUEUE.md line 351: "the Beagle years in Argentina and Patagonia; the voyage that became the theory."*

**Legend. [A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or general knowledge not confirmed against a reachable record in this pass. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**; where the journey file had to pick one value, the pick is stated and the loser named in the stop's `date_confidence`.

**Weighting.** Three and a half of the five years were spent on and around South America, most of it on the Atlantic side. The file follows that: twenty-five of forty-five stops fall between Montevideo and the Strait of Magellan, three on the Galapagos.

---

## 1. England, 1809-1831

Born 12 February 1809 at The Mount, Shrewsbury, to Robert Darwin and Susannah Wedgwood, grandson of Erasmus Darwin **[A: Darwin's autobiography (1876/1958 ed.); Janet Browne, *Voyaging*, 1995]**.

**Coordinate corrected.** The pool gave 52.7069 / -2.7554, which is Shrewsbury town centre, about 900 m off. The Mount is 52°42′46″N 2°45′47.5″W = **52.7128 / -2.7632** (en.wikipedia.org, "The Mount, Shrewsbury"). Corrected pin used.

Edinburgh 1825-1827: medicine abandoned, taxidermy and invertebrate dissection with Robert Grant learned instead **[A: autobiography; Browne]**. Grant's open Lamarckism is standard in the literature but **[R]** here: the pool names Grant and the dredging, not the conversations. The campa says only that Grant talks of transmutation.

Cambridge 1828-1831: the BA, Henslow, the collecting habit **[A]**.

**Humboldt [A, textual].** Darwin reads the *Personal Narrative* at Cambridge and credits it, in the 1876 autobiography, with the zeal that put him aboard (cited inside `humboldt.journey.json` lines 562-570, 702). Not a meeting; they never met.

## 2. Atlantic crossing, Dec 1831 - Jul 1832

Plymouth 27 December 1831 under FitzRoy, Darwin aged 22 **[A]**. Tenerife 6 January 1832, no landing, cholera quarantine **[A]**; Fernando de Noronha 20 February **[A]**: both dropped from the file for length. Porto Praya 16 January 1832, the raised shell band in volcanic cliff **[A: *Journal of Researches*, Ch. I]**.

**Bahia landfall: date contradiction.** Chronology lens and the Beagle-voyage article give **28 February 1832**; the geography lens gives 29 February. File uses 28, conflict named in `date_confidence`. Content **[A: Ch. II]**: the first forest, and the revulsion at slavery.

Rio de Janeiro from 4 April 1832, cottage at Botafogo, three months ashore **[A]**.

## 3. The Plata and the bone beds, Jul-Oct 1832

Montevideo 26 July 1832, the ship's South Atlantic base **[A]**.

**5 August 1832 landing: place corrected.** The pool put it at Buenos Aires. It was **Montevideo**: officials and the British consul asked FitzRoy for help against a mutiny, the garrison held by Black troops loyal to Lavalleja **[A: Wikipedia, "Second voyage of HMS Beagle"]**. The Buenos Aires incident is separate and earlier, the Beagle fired on by an Argentine guard ship around 31 July / 2 August 1832. Not merged in the file.

**Punta Alta 22 September 1832 [A, from Darwin's diary]:** the conglomerate of shells with fossil teeth and bones in the bluff. **8 October 1832 [A]:** the *Megatherium* skull, more giants, glyptodont armour.

**Fossil list corrected, two errors.**
1. ***Toxodon* does not belong at Punta Alta.** Darwin's *Toxodon* skull was bought at Mercedes, November 1833 (en.wikipedia.org, "Toxodon"). Kept apart in the file.
2. **"Hoplophorus"** is a nineteenth-century name not supportable from reachable sources. File says "glyptodont armour".

Residual tangle, **not reconciled**: Darwin's own text names *Scelidotherium* ("of which I obtained a nearly perfect skeleton. It must have been as large as a rhinoceros") and *Mylodon Darwinii* among the Bahia Blanca material **[A: Wikisource; Gutenberg #944]**. Darwin's list and Owen's later taxonomy do not map cleanly and this report does not force them.

**The observation that matters [A: Wikipedia, "Charles Darwin"]:** giant extinct mammals lie beside modern seashells, recent extinction with no catastrophic upheaval.

**Edition corrected on every quote.** The pool labelled the Wikisource text "1839 first edition". It is the **1845 second edition** (1860 "tenth thousand" printing), which is why the same paragraph cites Owen's completed *Zoology*, published after 1839. All `quote_source` fields cite *Journal of Researches*, 2nd ed. (1845), by chapter.

Monte Hermoso, 19 October 1832, small extinct rodents in the same strata **[A]**. Dropped for length.

## 4. Tierra del Fuego, Dec 1832 - Feb 1833

First Fuegian landfall 18 December 1832 **[A]**, at Good Success Bay; the quotes lens attaches the "savage and civilized man" line to that meeting **[A for the quote, R for the attachment]**.

**Coordinate gap, stated as such.** No coordinate for Good Success Bay in the pool; the quotes lens carries a rounded -55 / -67, which is open water. The file uses **-54.8000 / -65.2167**, my own geolocation from the place name, **[R]**, flagged in `date_confidence`.

**Wulaia Bay 23 January 1833 [A]:** the three Fuegians and the missionary Richard Matthews landed; the station stripped within days. **Coordinate corrected twice.** The pool gave -54.9333 / -67.65 for both the 1833 landing and the 1834 revisit; that point is out in the Beagle Channel, roughly 40 km east. Bahia Wulaia is 55°02′53″S 68°08′58″W = **-55.0481 / -68.1494** (en.wikipedia.org, "Wulaia Bay"). Both stops corrected.

Beagle Channel 29 January 1833, first glaciers, "the beryl-like blue" **[A: Wikipedia, "Beagle Channel", quoting Darwin]**.

**Cape Horn Christmas gale: rejected, stop omitted.** The pool carried a violent gale off the Horn on 25 December 1832 as among the most alarming of the voyage. Christmas Day 1832 was spent at anchor in Wigwam Cove, Hermite Island (roughly 55.87S, 67.55W); the near-swamping gale Darwin calls the worst was **13 January 1833**. Neither the Beagle-voyage nor the FitzRoy article supports a Christmas gale. Omitted rather than carried at [R].

**Monte Darwin 12 February 1834: upgraded [R] to [A].** Monte Darwin, Cordillera Darwin, Chile, 54°45′S 69°29′W = -54.75 / -69.483, 2,438 m; FitzRoy names it for Darwin's twenty-fifth birthday (en.wikipedia.org, "Monte Darwin"). Carried as a stop, not as a commemoration.

**Wulaia revisit 5 March 1834 [A]:** Jemmy Button reverted, married, the goods gone, the offer of England refused **[A: Beagle voyage; FitzRoy; "Jemmy Button"]**. Orundellico, named for a mother-of-pearl button, c.1815-1864 **[A]**.

## 5. Falklands and Maldonado, Mar-Jul 1833

**Date contradiction.** Chronology lens says February 1833; geography lens says March. The geography lens is right: Port Louis in March and April 1833, the Beagle in Berkeley Sound at the start of March **[A: en.wikipedia.org, "Port Louis, Falkland Islands"]**. File uses 1833-03-01, conflict named. The second call, 16 March 1834 **[A]**, dropped for length.

Maldonado, roughly late April to mid-July 1833 **[A: Ch. V]**. **Rhea claim removed:** the geography lens put the *Rhea darwinii* specimen here. It was salvaged at **Port Desire in January 1834** (en.wikipedia.org, "Darwin's rhea"). At Maldonado he had the common rhea and hearsay only.

## 6. Rosas's frontier, Aug-Nov 1833

The overland ride from the Rio Negro toward Buenos Aires with gauchos from 8 August 1833 **[A]**; Carmen de Patagones 3-10 August **[A: Ch. VI]**; first hearsay of the *avestruz petise* on this route **[A: "Darwin's rhea"]**.

**Rosas's camp, 12 August 1833 [A]:** Darwin meets Juan Manuel de Rosas and gets a passport of safe conduct **[A: Beagle voyage; Ch. IV]**.

**Guardia del Monte: coordinate contradiction, NOT resolved.** Geography lens -36.0333 / -58.7833; quotes lens, correcting the placement of the "most just of all wars" remark, roughly -35.43 / -58.81 (San Miguel del Monte). File carries the geography lens value, since geography is that lens's remit, and names the conflict. Someone with a gazetteer should settle it.

**Two Rosas remarks, disentangled.** The pool merged them; the file keeps them apart.
- At the Colorado camp: "Every one here is fully convinced that this is the most just war, because it is against barbarians" **[A: Gutenberg #944 lines 3788-89]**.
- At the Guardia, on the last leg in: "I never saw anything like the enthusiasm for Rosas, and for the success of the 'most just of all wars, because against barbarians'" **[A: Gutenberg #944 lines 4519-22]**.

**Chapter attributions corrected.** Several quotes were labelled "Ch. VI (Buenos Ayres and St. Fe)". That parenthetical titles **Chapter VII**; Chapter VI is "Bahia Blanca to Buenos Ayres". Corrected throughout.

**Two quotes checked and then not used**, logged so the errors are not repeated: "their black hair blowing across their swarthy faces..." describes **Indians**, a party of Bernantio's friendly tribe, not gauchos **[A: Gutenberg #944 lines 4173-75]**; "the high sense of hospitality... nearly universal" continues "throughout these provinces" and is about an army lieutenant refusing payment at a posta, not about gauchos as a class **[A: lines 4281-84]**.

Punta Alta revisited 21 August 1833; articulated skeleton 1 September; gnawing tooth and mastodon bones on the Carcarana and Parana in October; fossil horse teeth in the Parana bluffs, striking because native horses were thought absent before the Spaniards **[all A]**. Buenos Aires September 1833 amid revolution **[A: Chs. V, VII]**, canonical pin **-34.6037 / -58.3816** inherited byte-identical.

**Mercedes, the bought skull: two errors and one open question.**
1. **Date off by one.** The Beagle article puts the purchase the day after 25 November, hence **26 November 1833**: "The next day, he visited a nearby house and bought a head of a Megatherium which must have been when found quite perfect for about two shillings."
2. **Two heads, not one.** The *Toxodon* article dates the celebrated Mercedes skull to 26 November 1833 for **18 pence**. The two-shilling *Megatherium* head and the eighteen-pence *Toxodon* head are **not merged**, and the file does not claim to know whether it was one transaction or two.
3. **Country unsettled.** Chronology lens: Mercedes, Argentina. *Toxodon* article: Mercedes, **Uruguay**. There is one in each country. File uses Mercedes, Soriano, Uruguay, -33.2524 / -58.0269, my own geolocation **[R]**, conflict stated. Unresolved.

## 7. Patagonia, Dec 1833 - Jun 1834

**Port Desire: two days, not one.** Put in **23 December 1833**; the guanaco was shot **24 December** **[A]**. The pool collapsed them; the file separates them.

**The lesser rhea, early January 1834 [A: "Darwin's rhea"].** Conrad Martens shoots it; it is cooked and mostly eaten before Darwin recognises the species from what is left, salvages head, neck, legs, one wing and feathers, and sends them to Henslow. The geography lens says Darwin shot it; the rhea article says Martens. **Martens used**, conflict named.

**Port St Julian, 9 January 1834: identification corrected.** Darwin's own words are "pieces of spine and a hind leg of some large animal, I fancy a Mastodon" **[A]**. Owen described it as *Macrauchenia patachonica*, a litoptern, in 1837 **[A]**. File says "bones he takes for a mastodon". **Date unsettled:** Beagle article 9 January 1834, *Macrauchenia* article 9 February 1834. January used, conflict named.

St Gregory's Bay 26 January 1834, the tall Tehuelche **[A]**; Port Famine and Mount Tarn, June 1834 **[A]**. Both dropped for length.

**Santa Cruz River: start date corrected** from the pool's 18 April to **19 April 1834** ("On 19 April, an expedition including FitzRoy and Darwin set off..."). Guanaco herds, gravel terraces, lava, rheas at a distance **[A]**.

**The load-bearing claim.** Darwin retrospectively treats the South American evidence, the Punta Alta extinctions and the geographic replacement of one rhea by a closely allied second with overlapping range, as what began to throw light on the origin of species, before the Galapagos material was processed **[A: Wikipedia, "Charles Darwin", citing his own retrospect]**. This rests on a Wikipedia summary of the retrospect, not on quoted notebook text. The emphasis is attested; the framing is interpretation.

## 8. Pacific side, Jun 1834 - Sep 1835

Chiloe from 28 June 1834, Osorno in eruption **[A]**. Valparaiso from 23 July 1834, shore base and a long unnamed illness **[A]**. Valdivia 20 February 1835, the earthquake **[A]**. Concepcion and Talcahuano early March 1835: mussel beds raised feet above the old tide line, taken as proof that continents rise gradually **[A: Ch. XIV]**. This is the Lyellian key that makes the pampas beds legible. Portillo Pass March-April 1835, marine shells and upright petrified trees at altitude **[A: Ch. XV]**. Copiapo and Lima **[A]**, dropped for length.

## 9. Galapagos and homeward, Sep 1835 - Oct 1836

**Landfall corrected.** The Beagle reached the archipelago **15 September 1835**, lay off Chatham from the 16th **[A]**; 17 September is at best Darwin's own landing. The file phrases it that way and does not present the 17th as the ship's arrival.

Floreana, the vice-governor's remark on tortoise shells, not acted on **[A: Ch. XVII]**. Santiago Island October 1835, heaviest collecting, specimens not labelled by island **[A]**.

Tahiti, New Zealand, Sydney (the "two distinct Creators" diary line, struck out), Cocos and the atoll subsidence theory, Mauritius, Cape Town and Herschel, St Helena, Ascension, FitzRoy's second crossing to Bahia for chronometer checks **[all A: Chs. XVIII-XXI]**. Sydney, Cocos, Falmouth kept in the file; the rest dropped.

Falmouth 2 October 1836, ashore the same night, mail coach to Shrewsbury **[A]**.

## 10. England, 1837-1882

***Rhea darwinii*, 1837 [A: "Darwin's rhea"].** Gould describes it before the Zoological Society; Darwin reads a paper on the eggs and the geographic distribution of the two species at the same meeting. Later *Rhea pennata*. **Month and day not fixed** in reachable sources; file dates it 1837-01-01 per the house default and says so.

**Owen's *Fossil Mammalia*: publication corrected.** Not a single 1838 event: issued **in numbers 1838-1840** as Part 1 of the *Zoology of the Voyage of H.M.S. Beagle*, the whole running February 1838 to October 1843 **[A]**.

**Taxonomy corrected.** The pool called *Toxodon* "glyptodont-related". *Toxodon* is a notoungulate; glyptodonts are armoured xenarthrans, a different lineage **[A: "Toxodon"]**.

**Publication history [A: "The Voyage of the Beagle"]:** May 1839 as vol. 3 of FitzRoy's *Narrative*; separate Colburn issue **15 August 1839**; Murray second edition 1845, copyright for **150 pounds**; illustrated Murray edition 1890 with Pritchett's engravings; short title *The Voyage of the Beagle* introduced by a **1905** republication.

Down House from 1842 **[A: Browne, *The Power of Place*]**; *Origin* 1859 **[A]**; death at Down House 19 April 1882, Westminster Abbey burial 26 April after a petition by colleagues and MPs, against his own wish for the parish churchyard **[A]**. **Adjacency to Newton's monument was not re-verified: [R]**, and the file words it as proximity rather than as a measured fact.

**Commemoration.** Museo Municipal de Ciencias Naturales Carlos Darwin, Punta Alta **[A: Wikipedia, "Punta Alta"]**, **undated in the source**; the file uses 2000-01-01 and says plainly in `date_confidence` that the year is a placeholder. The Beagle Channel is named for the ship's first survey, 1826-1830, before Darwin sailed **[A]**.

**Spanish editions [R].** *Viaje de un naturalista alrededor del mundo* is the usual title, but no first Spanish edition date or translator was confirmed. Gap.
