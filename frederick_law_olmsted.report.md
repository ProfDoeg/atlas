# Frederick Law Olmsted (1822-1903): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-21 from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife). **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the verifier could not confirm from a fetched source. Where lenses disagree, both values are printed and the disagreement left open unless the verifier adjudicated it. Verbatim quotes keep source punctuation, em dashes included; house style bars em dashes in authored prose only.*

## 1. Hartford and the boarding-out (1822-1840)

Born 26 April 1822 at Hartford to John Olmsted, a dry-goods merchant, and Charlotte Law Hull Olmsted, an eighth-generation Hartford mercantile family **[A: Wikipedia; NPS]**. Charlotte died before his fourth birthday, in 1826, of an overdose **[A]**; the chronology lens adds a family reconstruction suggesting laudanum and leaves accident versus intent unsettled, carried as **[R]**. John Olmsted remarried Mary Ann Bull, 1827 **[A]**.

Boarding placements, from Roper by way of the geography lens and no fetched primary source: c. 1829 with the Reverend Zolva Whitmore at North Guilford; c. 1832-1837, the longest, with the Reverend Joab Brace at Newington, remembered for severity and bullying. **[R: Roper, *FLO* (1973); village-center coordinates, houses not identified]** Phillips Academy, Andover, 1837-1838, surveying with Frederick Augustus Barton, whom he follows out. **[A]** The lenses disagree on the years (contradictions, item 1), and the chronology lens hedged to "near Phillips Academy" where the source says he attended it.

**The Yale question.** The chronology lens asserted, dated 1837 and placed at New Haven, that sumac poisoning ended his college plans and that he was never an enrolled Yale student. Most of that was struck: Wikipedia gives no year and no place for the poisoning, which happened while he boarded with Connecticut tutors, and "never at Yale" overstates the record, since Roper (1973) and Rybczynski (1999) put him at New Haven in 1842 as a non-matriculated auditor. The file carries one New Haven stop, 1842, **[R]**. Yale's archival page could not be fetched.

## 2. Clerk, sailor, farmer (1840-1850)

Clerk for roughly eighteen months from 1840 at the Beaver Street house of Benkard and Hutton, importers of French silks. Sails 24 April 1843 as apprentice seaman on the bark *Ronaldson*, Captain Warren Fox, for Canton by way of the Cape of Good Hope; Whampoa September 1843; back in New York 15 April 1844 after a passage of scurvy, overwork, and near-mutiny. **[R]**

**Source-attribution failure, the largest evidence problem in the dossier.** The geography lens credited the voyage to "Wikipedia: Frederick Law Olmsted (fetched)", an article that carries none of it: no *Ronaldson*, no Captain Fox, no sailing date, no Cape of Good Hope, only that he worked "as an apprentice seaman, merchant, and journalist". The details are real biography (Roper 1973) wrongly cited, and the same false attribution recurs on the Beaver Street, Whampoa, Cheshire, Geddes, New Orleans, Richmond, Founders' Rock, Bear Valley, and Mountain View entries. **All demoted to [R], sourced to Roper by way of the pool.**

Apprenticeships: scientific agriculture with his uncle David Brooks at Cheshire, 1844-1845, then George Geddes's farm near Syracuse, 1846-1847 **[R]**; a shore farm at Sachem's Head, 1847, too rocky to pay **[R]**; January 1848, 125 acres on the south shore of Staten Island, later Tosomock Farm, held to 1855 **[A]**. The "Woods of Arden" alias and the 1852 silver spoon for pears are **[R]**, not in the cited article; the campa keeps the prize and drops the alias.

## 3. England, 1850

Lands at Liverpool with John Hull Olmsted and Charles Loring Brace for a six-month walking tour; visits Paxton's Birkenhead Park, opened 1847, and calls it a People's Garden with nothing comparable in democratic America. **[A]**

**Quote withdrawn.** The geography lens attached "a scene of art superior to any I had before met with" to Birkenhead. That is not the phrasing the source carries, which is that he found "nothing to be thought of as comparable with this People's Garden", and the first could not be established against *Walks and Talks*. **The Birkenhead stop carries no quote field**; the People's Garden phrase appears in the campa as reported language.

One verbatim line from *Walks and Talks* Part 1 survives verification and is used at Liverpool: "every thing out of doors here soon gets toned down". **[A: PG #77164]** A Palace of Westminster stop (Commons session, Fourth of July dinner for Abbott Lawrence) exists in the geography lens **[R: Rybczynski 1999]**; cut for length, not for doubt.

## 4. The Southern journeys (1852-1861)

Commissioned by Henry J. Raymond of the *New-York Daily Times*, 1852. **[A]** Virginia and Richmond 1853-1854; New Orleans, where John Hull's tuberculosis turns him back; San Antonio 1854, where Olmsted secretly helps fund Adolf Douai's free-soil *San Antonio Zeitung*; a crossing into northern Mexico from Eagle Pass **[R: route reconstruction from *A Journey through Texas*, exact ford unconfirmed; the stop was cut on that ground and the ride folded into the San Antonio campa]**. Books: *Seaboard Slave States* (1856), *A Journey Through Texas* (1857), *Back Country* (1860), the 1861 abridgment *The Cotton Kingdom*. **[A]** Dix and Edwards goes insolvent in the Panic of 1857. **[A]**

**Quotes.** Five *Cotton Kingdom* passages were fetched directly from PG #72676, all **[A]**. Three had been silently altered in the dossier and are restored:

- "the mass of the citizen class of Virginia earn very little and are very poor" lost its opening clause, gained a capital, and had an em dash swapped for a comma.
- The "work like a nigger" passage had its semicolon and quotation marks changed and its continuing clause dropped. The slur is verbatim period source text, Olmsted reporting a Southern idiom to argue that slavery degrades the idea of labor. Not carried into the file.
- "The great mass, as they are seen at work..." dropped the initial "But" and closed with a period where the source has a semicolon and a clause calling the same people brethren and a part of ourselves. The journey quotes the whole sentence: contempt and claimed kinship sit inside it together, and citing half cites it falsely.

## 5. Central Park (1857-1861)

Superintendent of the works, 1857, under chief engineer Egbert Viele. **[R: not re-fetched from a primary parks source]** The Greensward plan of Olmsted and Calvert Vaux wins against 32 other entries, April 1858. **[A]** Architect-in-chief in May 1858 at $2,500 a year, Vaux consulting. **[R for the salary]**

**Seneca Village.** The city took the site by eminent domain and evicted the last residents, roughly 225 people, about two thirds Black, in October 1857, before Olmsted became architect-in-chief. He designed the landscape that replaced it; he did not initiate the taking. **[A: Central Park Conservancy, cross-checked against Rosenzweig and Blackmar (1992)]** **Gap:** nothing reachable shows him writing about the village, and the campa says so rather than inventing an attitude for him.

Marries Mary Cleveland Perkins Olmsted, his late brother's widow, 13 June 1859, Mayor Daniel Fawcett Tiemann officiating. **[A]** John Hull's death date and place are absent from the pool; the campa says only that he is dead of the tuberculosis that turned him back at New Orleans. European study tour, 1859: Alphand in Paris, Sir Richard Mayne in London. **[R]**

## 6. The Sanitary Commission (1861-1863)

Recruited by Henry Whitney Bellows as general secretary, 20 June 1861, the commission created 18 June. **[A]** Peninsula Campaign 1862: directs river steamers converted to hospital transports on the York and James, work Katharine Prescott Wormeley describes. **[A]** The chronology lens's "McLellan" is corrected to McClellan.

**Gettysburg, dropped.** The geography lens put Olmsted at Gettysburg in July-August 1863 directing relief, "cross-checked against Wikipedia (fetched)". That article carries no Gettysburg material at all; post-battle relief was run by Commission field agents; his presence is unattested in anything reachable, and he was in poor health that summer. A stop belongs to whoever was actually there, so **no Gettysburg stop exists**. Recorded so the operator can restore it if a USSC administrative history supports it.

Western tour by Cincinnati, Chicago, and St. Louis to Grant's encampment at Vicksburg, inspecting the semi-autonomous western branches he fails to unify, 1863. **[R, same attribution reason as section 2]** Resigns 1 September 1863 when the executive committee refuses centralized national control. **[R]**

**Two quotations corrected.** The dossier's "Olmsted was, in a fashion, a lay-Hildebrand" appears nowhere; the real entry is 11 March 1863, *Diary of George Templeton Strong*, vol. 3 (Nevins and Thomas eds.), verified against archive.org item gtsvol-3, and only "He is a lay-Hildebrand" is Strong's. The dossier's "System has ruled in the work of the Sanitary Commission." is likewise misworded and wrongly flagged as possibly not his voice; it sits in the connective narrative between the compiled letters, the compiler-editor's voice, and opens "Happily system has ruled...". **Residual uncertainty:** Gutenberg credits Olmsted as author, but the 1863 title page names none, only "Compiled and Published at the request of the Sanitary Commission". The quote_source says so.

## 7. California (1863-1865)

Crosses the Isthmus of Panama by rail, September 1863. **[A: NPS]** Manages the roughly 45,000-acre Mariposa Estate from Bear Valley, 1863-1865; ore values decline at depth, the indebted company cannot finance the works, the mines collapse in 1865. **[R]** Acreage checks out: Rancho Las Mariposas was 44,387 acres.

Mountain View Cemetery, Oakland, commissioned 1864, called in the pool his first largely independent executed landscape. **[R]** The cemetery was established in 1863, so 1864 belongs to the commission and not the founding, and "first independent" has no source of its own. Campus, park, and village for the College of California at Berkeley, 1865; fragments survive in Piedmont Avenue and the line of Strawberry Creek. **[R]** The unexecuted San Francisco plan of 1865-1866 is folded into the Berkeley campa rather than given a stop: it was delivered after he had gone east and no site landmark survives it.

Yosemite: Lincoln signs the Grant in 1864 **[A]**; Olmsted sits on the first California board of commissioners and writes the 1865 Preliminary Report, arguing that government must protect equal public access to exceptional scenery against private monopoly. Commissioners tied to the Geological Survey keep it from the legislature; it is not broadly published until 1952. **[A: NPS]**

## 8. Olmsted, Vaux and Company (1865-1874)

Resigns Mariposa, returns east, forms the firm, begins Prospect Park, 1865; design substantially complete 1873. **[A]** Buffalo's linked park and parkway system from 1868. **[A]** Riverside, Illinois, 1868-1869; the post-fire crash triggers default and the designers recover little of the fee. **[A]** Partnership dissolved for "mutual convenience" in 1872 **[R]**, cut as a stop and folded into the Capitol campa.

**Capitol dates corrected.** The dossier gave 1873 for the commission and June 1874 for a plan presented to Congress. The Architect of the Capitol gives **1874** for the commission and **1875** for the terrace design submission, and records no June 1874 presentation. The file uses 1874, drops the presentation, and keeps what AOC confirms: Summerhouse begun 1879 and finished late 1880 or early 1881, terraces 1882 through 1892.

Mount Royal, Montreal, commissioned 1874, inaugurated 24 May 1876. **[R for 1874, which the article does not give; [A] for 1876]**

## 9. Brookline and the Emerald Necklace (1878-1895)

Back Bay Fens from 1878. **[R]** Fairsted, 99 Warren Street, Brookline, from 1883: household and office, the first durable full-scale professional landscape architecture office in the United States, working under his successors until 1979. **[A]** Franklin Park from 1885, his last great individually composed public park. **[A: pool project table, cross-checked against Zaitzevsky (1982)]** Niagara Reservation created 1885 after the Olmsted, Vaux, and Charles Eliot Norton campaign; **lens disagreement**, tagged [A] by geography and [R] by chronology, carried as [R].

Stanford from 1886: Olmsted wants a foothill site, Stanford insists on the flat riding ground, and Olmsted answers with a low arcaded Mediterranean-Romanesque scheme with Shepley, Rutan and Coolidge. **[R]** Biltmore from 1888: Vanderbilt hires him while Richard Morris Hunt designs the chateau; Olmsted urges the damaged acreage be run as a working forest, and Gifford Pinchot becomes forester in 1892. **[A]** World's Columbian Exposition from 1890: lagoons and a wooded island with Burnham and Root, Olmsted preferring temporary colored pageantry against the architects' white ensemble. **[A]** Withdraws 1895; Sargent paints the portrait. **[A]**

## 10. Death and afterlife

Admitted to McLean Hospital, Belmont, 1898; dies there 28 August 1903 at eighty-one; buried at Old North Cemetery, Hartford. **[A]** The pool's best catch, confirmed against Wikipedia's "McLean Hospital": Olmsted "was consulted on the selection of the hospital site" but "there is no evidence that he was responsible for the design of the grounds", the engineer Joseph Curtis having supervised the landscape work. The repeated irony that he died in an asylum of his own design is unsupported, and the file says so.

Afterlife material given **no stop**, because the traveling in each case is somebody else's:

- **Robert Smithson**, "Frederick Law Olmsted and the Dialectical Landscape", *Artforum*, February 1973, reframing Central Park as unfinished material process and Olmsted as America's first earthwork artist **[A: Holt/Smithson Foundation]**: a suggested_ref on his own Central Park and burial stops. The **Seneca Village recovery**, 1990s to 2022 (Sara Cedar Miller's *Before Central Park*, Conservancy archaeology) **[A]**: sources on the Seneca Village stop.
- **Frederick Law Olmsted NHS**, established 12 October 1979, office wing "virtually unchanged" with roughly a million design records **[A]; [R] for the holdings**; **unresolved**, 60,000 versus 66,000 photographs. The **Papers of Frederick Law Olmsted Digital Edition** (Rotunda), roughly 60,000 items **[R]**, launch date not on the fetched page.
- **Olmsted Point**, Yosemite, named for Olmsted and his son **[A]**; nothing reachable puts him there, so it is a suggested_ref on the Yosemite stop. The **Seneca Park Zoo giraffe named Olmsted**, June 2022 **[A]**, is recorded here only. So is the **Captain Kidd treasure hoax**, Deer Isle: a joke of his daughter Marion's, written up by Franklin H. Head as a private pamphlet in 1898 and later reprinted as fact, which his son Rick called "a burlesque hoax" **[R: NPS]**. The pamphlet is Head's act, and Olmsted was at McLean by 1898.
