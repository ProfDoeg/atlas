# Cecilia Vicuña: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `cecilia_vicuna`. Chilean poet, visual artist, filmmaker and activist, born Santiago 22 July 1948, living at the date of this report.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference. *gap* = stated as a gap rather than filled.

**Living subject.** The house brief's "tomb and its fate" section does not apply and has not been invented. Any later claim of a grave, memorial, or posthumous cult is a contradiction to be checked against the gap recorded in §8.

## 1. Santiago, 1948 to 1971: the garden studio, the beach, the Tribu No

- Born **Santiago de Chile, 22 July 1948** **[A: en.wikipedia "Cecilia Vicuña"; MoMA artist biography]**. Sources agree on date and city. *Gap: no source names the clinic, street, or neighborhood of the birth.* The journey file uses the city pin.
- Childhood at **La Florida**, southern edge of Santiago in the Maipo valley; her father keeps a **garden studio** where she paints abstract works as a child, before any training **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Sources give only "the 1950s"; the journey file defaults to 1955 and hedges in `date_confidence`.
- Attends **St Gabriel's English School**, Santiago, from 1957 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Not carried as a stop: the attendance is attested, but the campus coordinate would have been a reconstruction, as the geography lens itself flagged.
- **1966, Concón**: begins the **precarios**, small assemblages of driftwood, feathers, string and beach debris left at the waterline **[A: ceciliavicuna.com, official artist site]**. Origin point of the whole later practice, and the only stop in the journey predating any institution.
- **1966**: enrolls at the **Universidad de Chile** for architecture, transfers to fine arts; writes 7,000 words a day for *El Diario Estúpido*. **1971**: **MFA**, same institution **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **1967**: founds **Tribu No**; the Mexico City bilingual quarterly **El Corno Emplumado** prints her first poem, at eighteen **[A: en.wikipedia; Poets.org "Cecilia Vicuña"]**.
  - **CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED NOT RESOLVED.** The pool's geography lens dated the *Corno Emplumado* publication **1966**; its own chronology lens dated it **1967**; **es.wikipedia** dates it **1968**. en.wikipedia and Poets.org both give 1967 ("In 1967 she founded the Tribu No and the Mexican magazine El Corno Emplumado published her first poem"). The journey file adopts **1967** as the two-source majority and records the 1966 and 1968 variants in `date_confidence`. Note that 1966 is not simply an outlier source: the inconsistency was internal to the research pass.
  - *Gap: the magazine's editorial address is undocumented; the city-center pin is **[R]**.*

## 2. London, 1972 to 1974: the award, the coup, the four hundred objects

- **1972**: **British Council Award** takes her to London for postgraduate study at the **Slade School of Fine Art** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Month unrecorded; defaulted to September (academic year), hedged **[R]**.
- **24 June 1973 to August 1974**: makes **over four hundred precarios** in London as political resistance objects, gathered as **A Journal of Objects for the Chilean Resistance**, now held by **Tate** **[A: en.wikipedia; Tate collection]**. en.wikipedia's day-level start ("Between June 24, 1973-August 1974") is unusually specific and is reproduced as attested.
- **11 September 1973**: the coup; Allende dies at La Moneda; she cannot safely return and stays in London **[A: en.wikipedia, which states the London exile, the 1975 Bogotá move and the 1980 New York move explicitly]**.
  - **TAG CORRECTION APPLIED.** The pool's afterlife lens tagged the exile **[R]**, hedging that "exact residency dates not independently verified in this pass." Unnecessary: every element is attested on en.wikipedia, including the day-level precarios dating. Promoted to **[A]**, hedge dropped, noted here so the change is not silent.
- **1974**: co-founds **Artists for Democracy**; helps organize the **Arts Festival for Democracy in Chile** at the **Royal College of Art**, Kensington **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 3. Bogotá, 1975 to 1980: the indigenous turn

- **1975**: leaves London for **Bogotá** to research indigenous art **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Works on stage designs with **Teatro La Candelaria** and the **Corporación Colombiana de Teatro** **[R: MoMA artist biography reports the collaboration without dates; the 1975 to 1980 span is inferred from arrival and departure years]**.
- **1975 to 1980**: research travel through **Venezuela and Brazil** into textile and oral traditions **[R: en.wikipedia states it only in general terms]**. *Gap: no source names a single town visited. The Caracas coordinate is a reconstructed anchor for an undocumented route.*
- **1979**: performs **El Vaso de Leche** in the street in Bogotá, against child deaths from contaminated milk **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **1980**: makes the film **¿Qué es para Usted la Poesía?** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
  - **PLACE ERROR IN THE POOL, CORRECTED.** The chronology lens attached the film to the New York relocation and gave it the New York coordinate. en.wikipedia is explicit: "In 1980 she made the film ¿Qué es para Usted la Poesía / What is Poetry to you? **while in Bogotá, Colombia**." The journey file splits the entry: the film is a **Bogotá** stop (4.711 / -74.0721), the marriage and relocation a **New York** stop.

## 4. New York, 1980 to 1998: downtown, QUIPOem, Cloud-Net

- **1980**: moves to **New York City**; marries the Argentine painter **César Paternosto** **[A: en.wikipedia; MoMA artist biography]**. New York is her base thereafter, with time divided between the city and Chile.
- **MoMA**: the 1980 Bogotá film enters the **Museum of Modern Art** collection **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
  - **CLAIM MISSTATED IN THE POOL, CORRECTED.** Both the geography and chronology lenses asserted MoMA **exhibited** her during the 1980s. No source found supports that; what is attested is a **collection holding** of the 1980 film. The stop is written as an acquisition, not an exhibition. *Gap: the accession year is undocumented; the stop is dated to the year of the work, hedge in `date_confidence`.*
- **1980s**: shown at the **Alternative Museum**, SoHo, and the **Center for Inter-American Relations** (later Americas Society) **[R: MoMA artist biography; venues attested, years and addresses approximate]**. Neither carried as a stop; both recorded so the downtown network is not lost.
- **1990**: solo exhibition **Precarious**, **Exit Art**, New York **[R: MoMA artist biography]**.
- **1991**: **Rockefeller Foundation residency**, Villa Serbelloni, **Bellagio** **[R: MoMA artist biography]**. A second Bellagio residency follows in **2003**, not carried **[R: same source]**.
- **1992**: **El Ande Futuro**, University Art Museum, **Berkeley** **[R: MoMA artist biography]**.
- **1997**: **Andy Warhol Foundation Award** for **QUIPOem** **[A: en.wikipedia awards list]**.
- **1997**: Catherine de Zegher's **The Precarious: The Art and Poetry of Cecilia Vicuña / QUIPOem**, Wesleyan University Press (Middletown, Connecticut) with the Kanaal Art Foundation (Kortrijk, Belgium) **[A: publisher record; ceciliavicuna.com bibliography]**.
  - **GAP CLOSED FROM WITHIN THE POOL.** The afterlife lens recorded the de Zegher volume as "unknown (pre-2010s)" with an empty place field. The answer was already in the same pool: the volume carries the **QUIPOem** title, and the chronology lens independently records the **1997** Warhol award **for QUIPOem**. Year set to 1997, place set to the publisher pairing. A cross-link and not a new source is what fixed it.
- **1998**: **Cloud-Net** travels **Hallwalls** (Buffalo) to **DiverseWorks** (Houston) to **Art in General** (New York) **[R: MoMA artist biography]**. Only the Buffalo opening is a stop; the other two legs are named inside its campa.

## 6. 2017 to 2019: documenta and the Disappeared Quipu

- **2017**: **About To Happen**, Contemporary Arts Center, **New Orleans** **[A: MoMA artist biography]**. Opening date within the year not established; defaulted, hedged.
- **2017**: **documenta 14**, in **Athens** (EMST, 8 April to 16 July) and **Kassel** (10 June to 17 September) **[A: en.wikipedia; documenta 14 record]**.
  - **TWO FRAMING ERRORS IN THE POOL, CORRECTED.** The geography lens described documenta 14 as "split across two **capitals**": Kassel is not a capital, it is a city in Hesse. The journey file uses the accurate formulation: documenta 14 was the first to give a second city a **co-equal full-scale presentation**. Also flagged: the pool's Athens coordinate sits roughly 400 m east of EMST's Kallirrois Avenue site, retained for consistency with the pool, offset stated in `date_confidence` rather than silently adjusted.

## 8. Afterlife, monuments, and the gap that must stay open

- She is **alive**. No tomb, gravesite, memorial or dedicated monument exists **[A by absence: nothing found across Tate, Guggenheim, Smithsonian and artist-site materials reviewed]**. Her only monumental works are **temporary**: the Turbine Hall commission and the museum quipu installations, all de-installed. Stated as a gap so that any later claim of a permanent monument registers as a contradiction, not a discovery.
- The **exile to national laureate** arc (stranded in London 1973, Premio Nacional 2023) is what later commentary is likeliest to mythologize. Recorded as an arc the dates support, not as a claim any source makes.

## 9. Interlocks with the existing corpus

Carried into the journey file with the tagging the interlock lens assigned:

- **`augusto_pinochet`** **[A for the events, R for the linkage]**: the coup of 11 September 1973 strands her; the Estadio Nacional fills on 12 September; DINA is constituted at **Londres 38** in November 1973, a Santiago address named for the city she is stranded in. Pinochet's own **arrest in London**, October 1998, puts him in her exile city a quarter-century after her. All four are named in campa.
- **`lautaro`** **[A for the pin, R for any thematic inheritance]**: Santiago del Nuevo Extremo, founded by Valdivia, is the same city. **A distinction the corpus must not silently collapse**: Lautaro's war is **Mapuche**, the quipu is **Inca and Andean**. Both concern indigenous Chilean memory against colonial erasure; they are not one tradition.
- **`ohiggins`** **[A that the source is cited, R for the inheritance]**: the O'Higgins journey cites **Benjamín Vicuña Mackenna**'s 1860 biographies repeatedly, embedding the surname in Chilean national historiography. *Gap: no genealogical link between Vicuña Mackenna and Cecilia Vicuña is established in-file.*
- **`pedro_subercaseaux`** **[A for the surname, R for the family line]**: Pedro Subercaseaux's father was **Ramón Subercaseaux Vicuña**. Surname match only; the campa writes it as a surname, not a descent, and says plainly that no document reached names blood between the houses.
- **`claude_levi_strauss`** **[R]**: his structuralist reading of indigenous South American sign systems is a precursor her quipu poetics engages and departs from. **Flagged distinction**: his fieldwork was **Brazilian** (Caduveo, Bororo, Nambikwara), not among Andean quipu-bearing peoples. The campa says so in those words.
- **`victoria_ocampo`** and **`leo_chiachio`** **[R]**: Buenos Aires. *Sur* built the transnational Latin American literary salon; Chiachio and Giannone work the same fiber-as-cosmology register. No direct crossing is recorded in any file, and the campa says so.
- **`arthur_bispo_do_rosario`** **[R]**: thread cosmologies, and the Venice Biennale honored both, him posthumously in 1995 and her alive in 2022. Named at the Venice stop.
- **Stated non-interlock**: no file carries the **Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262)** or **Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354)** coordinates in any way relevant to her. Those pins belong to the Islamic-history travelers. **No bridge was fabricated, and none should be.**
