# Georges Hébert (1875-1957): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-24 for `georges_hebert.journey.json`. Naval officer, inventor of the méthode naturelle, founder of La Palestra, author of "être fort pour être utile."*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or uncorroborated single source. Contradictions are flagged and left open.

Almost everything here runs through French and English Wikipedia. Three contemporary or primary items were reached at first hand: the 1902 *Tour du monde* narrative, the 1915 Plon *Dixmude*, and the 1905 scan of *L'Éducation physique raisonnée*. Two of the three served to **falsify** received claims, the most useful result of the pass. No archival or military record was consulted directly at any point.

## 1. Paris and the École navale (1875-1901)

- Born **27 April 1875, Paris 5e** **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**. No address, no family, nothing at all about the first eighteen years. The coordinate 48.8462, 2.3464 is the arrondissement centre, stated as such. **Gap.**
- **1893, entered the École navale**; graduated **60th of 72** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. That its seat was the hulk *Borda* at Brest is general naval history, **[R]** as applied to him: nothing places him aboard by name.
- **5 October 1896, made aspirant at Cherbourg** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the dossier gave the place as "France" and the *Dubourdieu* as 1896; fr.wikipedia gives Cherbourg and dates the ship to **1897**. Enseigne de vaisseau **5 October 1898** **[A]**, no place given.
- **1896-1903, South America, West Indies, North America** **[A for the range only]**: no ports, no dates, no ships. Carried as one labelled composite stop at Martinique.
- The "splendid bodies" sentence exists **only in English, only via McDougall, *Natural Born Heroes* (2016), p. 228, quoted on en.wikipedia** **[R]**. archive.org's copies are lending-restricted (403); **no French primary located**. Attributed in the journey as McDougall's rendering, not Hébert's words.

## 2. Saint-Pierre, 8 May 1902

The origin scene of the doctrine, and the part of the record most damaged by repetition.

- **His presence ashore in the ruins** **[A]**: both wikis, plus the *Tour du monde* narrator naming "l'enseigne de vaisseau Hébert, du Suchet."
- **CONTRADICTION, which ship.** fr.wikipedia: *D'Estrées*. en.wikipedia and *Tour du monde*: *Suchet*. *Correction:* the dossier stated this backwards, giving *Suchet* to the French article. Weight favours *Suchet*: en.wikipedia's article on that cruiser has her off Saint-Pierre from 6 May, taking 30 burned survivors and 25 off the SS *Roraima*, landing ~1,200 at Fort-de-France by 11 May, and gives *D'Estrées* no role.
- **The "700 rescued"** **[A that both encyclopedias carry the figure; R that he personally led it]**. *Correction in both directions:* the dossier first called it Hébertist tradition, then "explicitly contradicted by the scale of the disaster." Neither holds. Both wikis state the figure; the ~28,000-30,000 dead were inside the city, while the *Suchet* alone evacuated ~1,200. **The unverified element is his personal attribution, not the number.**
- **Sailors taken off the rocks toward Le Carbet** **[R]**: from the dossier's description of a *Tour du monde* passage not retrieved. Kept and flagged, being checkable.
- **"Dès lors ma voie était tracée..."**, attributed to *La Culture virile et les devoirs de l'officier combattant* via entrainement-naturel.org **[A as reported, R as collated]**; primary edition not opened.
- **The motto "être fort pour être utile"** **[R]**: universally quoted, **no dated first printing, page, or occasion located anywhere**, including on the movement's own sites. A real gap in the slogan the doctrine hangs on.

## 3. Lorient: the method (1903-1912)

- **École/Bataillon des fusiliers marins, Lorient, 1904**, gymnastics instruction from 1906 **[A]**; sources vary 1903/1904. **~1,000 fusiliers through 1912; ~800 apprentices aged 14-17 in 1908** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Cirque Molier** **[R]**. *Correction:* venue real and now located, at **6 bis rue Bénouville, Paris 16e**, built 1879, running 1880-1933, performers exclusively aristocratic amateurs, so ~48.8666, 2.2727; the dossier's 48.8686, 2.2775 is ~400 m off and was corrected. **His participation remains unconfirmed**: the article names only Molier and Blanche Allarty.
- **Publications.** *Correction:* *L'Éducation physique raisonnée* is **1907**, not 1905; the dossier claimed the fr.wikipedia bibliography corroborated 1905, and it lists 1907 (Vuibert et Nony). Then *Guide pratique* 1910, *Le Code de la force* 1911, *L'Éducation physique ou l'Entraînement complet* 1912 **[all A]**.
- **Navy adopts the method, 1909** **[R: fr.wikipedia]**: no decree, circular, or ministerial reference located. **Chevalier de la Légion d'honneur 1911, Officier d'académie 1914** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **The "final goal of physical education" quote** **[A as published English, R as Hébert's French]**. *Correction, twice.* (1) Its ellipsis spans **three paragraphs**, not a clause. (2) The ISBN resolves (Open Library OL28212006M) to Hébert and Philippe Til, *The Natural Method: Functional Exercises*, vol. 3, CreateSpace, 2015. Searching the 1905 French edition (archive.org b2808004x) for "êtres forts," "synthèse," "physique, virile" returned **zero hits**. A modern translator's English, uncollated.

## 4. Reims: the Collège d'Athlètes (1913-1914)

- **March 1913, demonstration at the International Congress of Physical Education, Paris** **[A for the event; R for the "peace army" press framing and for any venue]**. No period account retrieved.
- **1 April 1913, congé sans solde, détaché to Panhard et Levassor** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the dossier framed this as "just before the outbreak of war." It is sixteen months before, and is the **same date as the college's opening**, reading as the mechanism freeing a serving officer for a civilian post. Flagged as inference.
- **CONTRADICTION, did he direct the college?** fr.wikipedia's **'Collège d'athlètes de Reims' says it was founded by Édouard Redont and that Hébert did not direct it**, his methods merely being used there. Both Hébert articles make him technical director from April 1913. A probable origin of the confusion surfaced this pass: fr.wikipedia, 'Parc de Champagne', names **Redont and Ernest Kalas as architects of the post-1918 reconstruction**. A hypothesis, not a resolution; stated on the stop.
- **The site** **[A: 'Parc de Champagne']**: created 1909-1911 by the **Marquis de Polignac** for Pommery cellar workers, 21.6 ha on the Butte Saint-Nicaise, converted in 1913 to house France's first physical education college. Gymnasium 40 x 20 m, oval track, outdoor pool, boxing rings, fencing halls **[A]**.
- **COORDINATE CORRECTION.** fr.wikipedia gives 49° 14′ 19″ N, 4° 03′ 18″ E = **49.2386, 4.0550**. The dossier's geography lens gave 49.239, 4.0483 (~490 m west), its afterlife lens 49.2583, 4.0317 (central Reims, ~2.5 km off). All three Reims stops use the corrected pin.
- **19 October 1913, inaugurated by President Poincaré** **[A to the day: 'Parc de Champagne'; en.wikipedia gives only "October 1913"]**.
- **Jean Bouin.** *Correction.* fr.wikipedia, 'Jean Bouin': he **visited** in October 1913 at Hébert's invitation to be filmed in a four-minute short, **did not train there regularly**, and followed his own 1912 method. Killed **29 September 1914 at Xivray-et-Marvoisin, Meuse**, by shrapnel probably French. **The dossier's causal clause, that his death ended the college's era, is wrong and was dropped**: Reims was bombarded from September 1914 and the buildings went independently of him. **His death is not a stop on this file**: the Reims visit is Hébert's, the death is Bouin's.

## 5. Dixmude and the wound (1914)

- **Brigade de fusiliers marins raised 7 August 1914**, routed via Cherbourg and other ports to Ghent **[A]**. That article **never names Hébert**.
- **CONTRADICTION, when he took the company.** Dossier: 8th company, 2nd regiment, 249 men, leaving Cherbourg **28 August 1914** **[R]**. fr.wikipedia: commissioned captain of that company **14 October 1914** **[A]**, by which point the brigade had fought at **Melle, 9-11 October** **[A at unit level]**. The journey carries August with the conflict stated; his presence at Melle is inferred from his company's.
- **Diksmuide, 15 October to 10 November 1914** **[A at unit level]**. *Correction:* German strength **~30,000** under the Duke of Württemberg, not the dossier's 50,000. The rest checks: ~6,000 men in two regiments, Ronarc'h's order to "tenir au moins quatre jours," three weeks held, withdrawal 10 November.
- **FALSE CITATION CAUGHT.** "Soyons forts ! Les faibles sont des inutiles ou des lâches !" is routinely cited to **Le Goffic, *Dixmude* (Plon, 1915), pp. 89-91**. That citation is **false**. Full-text search of the 1915 Plon edition (archive.org `dixmudeunchapitr00legouoft`): "Soyons" 0 hits, "lâches" 0, "inutiles" 1 hit on p. 168 about "boucheries inutiles"; "Hébert" 8 hits (leaves 38, 131-134, 138, 169, 301), **none near pp. 89-91, none quoting any motto**. The 1916 English translation (Gutenberg 33929) mentions him five times with no such line. fr.wikipedia's real second reference is **Jean Mabire, *La Bataille de l'Yser***, unverifiable. Kept **downgraded to [R]**, falsification stated in `quote_source`.
- **The wounding** **[A that it happened at Dixmude and permanently disabled the left arm]**. **CONTRADICTION on the date:** the dossier, citing Philippe-Meden, gives **19 October 1914** with hospitalization in November; en.wikipedia gives **November 1914** for the wounding itself. Not reconciled. The bullet stopped by a manual and notebook is **[R]**, dossier-only, as is **Calais** as a waypoint (folded into the Lariboisière campa rather than given a stop).
- **Hôpital Lariboisière, 23 November 1914**: cachectic, hospital gangrene, purulent infiltration, severed radial nerve, erysipelas, complicated paraplegia **[A for the hospital's location; R for the date and clinical list, via the dossier's Philippe-Meden citation]**. The permanent disability is widely attested. **Parcours du combattant credited to his war experience [R]**.

## 6. La Palestra, the journal, the war on sport (1918-1938)

- **La Palestra, Normandy, 1918/1919**, residential college for women and children, Yvonne Moreau director **[A for the institution]**. **CONTRADICTION on the year:** 1918 and 1919 both appear; a 1991 federation documentary's "1925" is an outlier and was **dropped**. fr.wikipedia, 'Hébertisme', dates the community "entre 1918 et 1929," supporting 1918.
- **COORDINATE CORRECTION.** fr.wikipedia puts Tourgéville at **49.324, 0.065**. The dossier's 49.354, 0.093 is ~3.4 km north-east, effectively Deauville; its afterlife lens gave 49.3444, 0.0917 for the same death place. **Both wrong, both replaced.** Deauville-proper stops use 49.3591/0.0714 and 49.3667/0.0833.
- **CONTRADICTION, a postwar Reims directorship.** fr.wikipedia's Hébert article says he directed the college **from 1919**, in tension with its own 1913 entry. Distinct phase or dating error, unsettled. **No 1919 Reims stop was written**, the buildings being destroyed.
- ***L'Éducation physique féminine: muscle et beauté plastique*, 1919** **[A]**. *Correction:* the dossier claimed en.wikipedia's two articles disagree (1921 vs 1919). They do not. 'Georges Hébert', 'Méthode naturelle', and the fr.wikipedia bibliography all give **1919**; the alleged conflict was removed.
- **The journal *L'Éducation physique*, directed 1922-1940** **[A for the span; R for the May 1922 month, dossier-only]**. *Correction:* the dossier said "roughly three decades." It is eighteen years. **No editorial address located.**
- **CONTRADICTION, the marriage year.** A naval biographical register cited by the dossier gives **4 January 1923**, Paris 16e; en.wikipedia and the popular biographies give **1924**. The journey carries 1923 as the more precise and states the conflict. Yvonne Moreau: Demenÿ-trained, chief female instructor at Reims 1913, died 1975 **[A]**.
- **Winter Palestra, Riviera, 1923** **[A for year and region; R for all else]**. *Correction:* the dossier's 43.65, 7.0 was labelled "near Nice" but lies inland behind Cannes, 22 km from Nice and not coastal. The journey uses **Cannes, 43.5528, 7.0174**, declared an anchor rather than a site.
- **Condemnation of competitive sport after the 1924 Paris Olympics** **[R: no occasion or text given]**.
- ***Le Sport contre l'éducation physique* (Vuibert, 1925)** **[A]**. His definition of sport is **verbatim from fr.wikipedia and unfootnoted there**; the edition is **not on archive.org** and **Gallica's SRU endpoint returned 403**. **[R]**
- **The schooner *Alcyon* and the women's nautical school.** **CONTRADICTION carried live:** fr.wikipedia **1930**, en.wikipedia **1929**, both flat assertions. The dossier's "and boys under 14" is in **neither** wiki and was dropped. Mooring, ownership, itinerary undocumented.
- **1938, public separation from a "Groupement hébertiste"** **[R: no corroboration found]**.

## 7. Summa, stroke, death (1936-1957)

- ***L'Éducation physique virile et morale par la méthode naturelle*, tomes and fascicles 1936-1959** **[A]**; volume dates not collated. **1953, stroke affecting speech; relearned walking, speaking, writing** **[A]**. **1955, Commandeur de la Légion d'honneur** **[A]**.
- **Palestra destroyed by bombardment in the Second World War** **[R]**: en.wikipedia states it; no date, no action, no photograph. The journey dates it 1944 purely to order the segment and says so.
- **Died 2 August 1957, Tourgéville, Calvados, aged 82** **[A: both wikis, plus fr.wikipedia 'Tourgéville']**. The **heart attack is movement tradition [R]**; no certificate or medical record located. **NO GRAVE**: no reference work names a cemetery, tomb, or marker, and none was found. Stated as a gap, not as a claim that he lies unmarked.
- **The "natural method is not Hébertism" quote** **[R]**. *Correction:* the dossier said en.wikipedia cites Philippe-Meden 2018. **It does not.** The footnote is a website citation to `en.hebertisme.com/foire-aux-questions`, relaying **Marcel Defarges, revue *L'Éducation physique*, 2e trimestre 1958, p. 55** (posthumous), with **no Philippe-Meden layer**. That FAQ is unreachable and the archives blocked. Seen only in Wikipedia's blockquote, and cited that way.

## 8. Afterlife

- **The Reims site**: heavily damaged 1914-18, "n'est que très partiellement reconstruit," chiefly a pool and a diving board; the parc des sports Pommery is today the **Parc de Champagne**, architects of the reconstruction **Redont and Kalas [A]**.
- **Two named facilities**, the Deauville municipal stadium and a sports complex in northern Reims **[R]**: the only monument-equivalents found. **"Parcours Hébert"** survives as the generic French name for an outdoor obstacle course **[R]**, diffuse, no site.
- **Translations**: Til, vol. 1, 2014 **[A]**; an earlier *Practical Guide to Physical Education* by Pilou and Gregg, 1912 **[A as cited; publisher unverified]**. **MovNat**, from the method, founded by Erwan Le Corre **[A as cited; movnat.com 404]**.
- **Parkour**: en.wikipedia credits the "le parcours" pedagogy as an influence on parkour through **David Belle**, on **Sébastien Foucan's** freerunning, and on **John-Edouard Ehlinger** **[A as Wikipedia's assertion of influence]**. Transmission through **Raymond Belle**, trained in the method via the French military, is QUEUE.md line 102's framing, **[R]** as to any documented chain back to Hébert himself.

## Interlocks: checked and rejected

QUEUE.md places Hébert in the body-culture wing beside `delsarte`, `arnold_rikli`, `joseph_pilates`, `elsa_gindler`. **None produced a nameable relation and none is named in any campa.** Shared geography and shared period are not reasons to name-drop; the map already shows co-location.

- **Arnold Rikli**: died 30 April 1906, before the mature doctrine; his file mentions neither Hébert, France, nor physical education. Confirmed **non-link**, recorded so nobody invents contact later.
- **Joseph Pilates**: interned on the Isle of Man September 1914 to March 1919, almost exactly the span of Hébert's wounding and doctrinal rebuilding. Two founders on opposite sides of one war, each converting confinement or injury into a training philosophy. **Suggestive, not a relation.** Not named.
- **Émile Jaques-Dalcroze**: his file has a 1914-09-25 stop, "Geneva, news of the burning of Reims," the same destruction that took Hébert's college. **Same event, no relation between the men**, exactly the case the rule exists to prevent.
- **Auguste Rodin**: the dossier asserts "Rodin supported early physical-education initiatives." `auguste_rodin.journey.json`, checked stop by stop 1840-2022, has **no stop referencing physical education, Hébert, Demenÿ, or the college**. **Unconfirmed, not used.**
- **Rudolf von Laban**, **Alfred Dreyfus**, **Isadora Duncan**: shared milieu, officer corps, or city, no documented link, none named. **Kaaba / Temple Mount / Buenos Aires**: checked, no intersection, **no Argentine leg invented**.
