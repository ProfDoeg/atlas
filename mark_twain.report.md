# Mark Twain (Samuel Langhorne Clemens), 1835-1910: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-06 for the Atlas of Journeys. Register of the journey file: "national mythology: the canon is true." Queued at QUEUE.md line 122, "American writer, author of Joan of Arc memoir"; the 2026-08-02 census agrees at line 368. No mark_twain.journey.json existed before this run.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition or inference. Contradictions between the four lenses (chronology, geography, quotation, corpus interlock) are flagged where they fall and listed again at the end; none is silently resolved. Wrong coordinates are corrected with their authority, the wrong value kept on record so it is not re-imported.

## 1. Birth and Hannibal, 1835-1853

- Born **30 November 1835** at **Florida, Monroe County, Missouri**, sixth of seven children of John Marshall and Jane Lampton Clemens **[A: Wikipedia; Mark Twain House & Museum]**, two months premature **[A: Paine, *Mark Twain: A Biography*, 1912]**. Halley's Comet reached perihelion **16 November 1835**, two weeks earlier **[A: arithmetic on two attested dates]**.
- **Coordinate error, twice, differently.** Chronology lens 39.4739, -91.4432; geography lens 39.7075, -91.9950. Two passes producing two different wrong values is the signature of a guess. The village is **39.4931, -91.7906** **[A: Wikipedia, "Florida, Missouri"]**.
- **Hannibal** from 1839, the port refigured as St. Petersburg **[A]**. **McDowell's Cave** explored c. 1845-1852, model for the cave in *Tom Sawyer* **[R: Paine plus local tradition, no document fixes a date]**; the body preserved in one chamber is Hannibal tradition **[R]**, kept in the campa as such.
- **John Marshall Clemens dies of pneumonia, 1847** **[A]**, month a gap. School abandoned after fifth grade, apprenticed to a printer 1848, typesetter for Orion's **Hannibal Journal** from 1851 **[A]**. The master printer is in Paine, not re-fetched, so omitted.

## 2. Type case and pilot house, 1853-1861

- Itinerant printer in New York, Philadelphia, St. Louis, Cincinnati; **International Typographical Union** **[A]**.
- **Keokuk**, Orion's shop. The geography lens gave 1855-1857, too long and inconsistent with its own Cincinnati entry; corrected to **June 1855 to about October 1856**, then Cincinnati for the winter of 1856-57, Walnut Street lodgings and the Amazon scheme **[A/R: Paine, reconciled against the lens]**.
- Cub pilot under **Horace E. Bixby** from 1857, later licensed **[A]**; the licence year (1859 in most biographies) is **not** in this pool and is not stated in the journey. **New Orleans** the southern terminus **[A: *Life on the Mississippi*]**.
- **Henry Clemens: date error in the pool.** The chronology lens carried "dies of injuries" dated 1858-06-13. The *Pennsylvania*'s boilers exploded **13 June 1858**; Henry **died eight days later, 21 June**, at Memphis **[A]**. The stop is dated 21 June and names the explosion in prose. That Twain held himself responsible lifelong is universal in the biographies: **[R]**, no primary document fetched.
- **Marion Rangers**, summer 1861, second lieutenant, disbanded **[A]**. No lens gives a coordinate; the journey pins Marion County **[R for the coordinate]**.

## 3. Nevada, California, Hawaii, 1861-1866

- Stagecoach west with Orion, newly **secretary of Nevada Territory**; **Carson City**; the **Comstock Lode**, unsuccessfully **[A: *Roughing It*]**.
- **Lake Tahoe timber claim** burned by an untended campfire, Twain's own episode **[A]**. The pool's 39.0968, -120.0324 is the lake centroid, on the **California** side, contradicting its own label "Nevada shore"; about 39.24, -119.94 is usual **[R for the coordinate]**. The journey folds the episode into the Virginia City stop rather than pin a guess.
- **Territorial Enterprise** from 1862; **first use of "Mark Twain," 3 February 1863**, a leadsman's call meaning two fathoms **[A: Wikipedia; PBS/Ken Burns]**.
- **San Francisco** 1864-66, *Morning Call*, with **Bret Harte** **[A: Paine]**. **Angels Camp** 1865, source of **"The Celebrated Jumping Frog,"** printed in the New York *Saturday Press* **18 November 1865** **[A: Twain's notebook; Paine]**. **Hawaii** 1866 for the *Sacramento Union*, **Kilauea** by torchlight **[A: *Letters from Hawaii*]**; the lecture career that followed is standard biography, not in this pool, and the campa keeps it to a clause.

## 4. The Quaker City, 1867

This itinerary comes almost wholly from *The Innocents Abroad*, Twain's own account. It is attested as **what Twain published**, not quite as **what happened**: the book compresses, reorders and invents comic business. Flagged once here, not on every line.

Departs **New York 8 June 1867**, meeting Charles Langdon, who shows him a portrait of his sister Olivia. Then Horta, Gibraltar, Tangier; Marseille and Paris for the Exposition in July; Milan, Venice, Florence in passing, Rome, Naples, Pompeii; Athens, quarantined offshore, the Parthenon climbed by night; Constantinople, Sevastopol, an audience with Tsar Alexander II at Livadia, Odessa, Ephesus; Beirut and overland to Damascus (Street called Straight, a serious fever), Galilee, Jerusalem and the Holy Sepulchre, rejoining ship at Jaffa; Giza. *The Innocents Abroad* published 1869. Paris carries the canonical pin 48.8566, 2.3522, shared with muybridge (1881) and blavatsky (1884): pin only, no contact claimed.

## 5. Marriage, Hartford, Elmira, 1869-1889

- **Marriage place: the lenses contradict each other.** Geography lens, under Buffalo: "marries Olivia Langdon **in the city** on 2 February 1870." Chronology lens: Elmira. The wedding was at the **Langdon house, Elmira**; the couple then travelled to Buffalo, where Jervis Langdon had secretly bought them the Delaware Avenue house **[A: Wikipedia, "Olivia Langdon Clemens"]**. Elmira used; the *Buffalo Express* share stands. Son **Langdon** born 1870, dead of diphtheria at nineteen months in 1872 **[A]**.
- **Hartford arrival: the pool's date is wrong.** The chronology lens hung the move on the 1873 *Gilded Age*. The Clemenses **rented at Nook Farm from 1871**, **bought land 1873**, **occupied the house 1874** **[A: Wikipedia, "Mark Twain House"]**. The house: **Edward Tuckerman Potter**, High Victorian Gothic, home 1874-1891, **25 rooms**, not the "nineteen" of the geography lens. **Coordinates, two lenses:** geography 41.7658, -72.6976 against afterlife **41.7671, -72.7014**, which is right by 320 m **[A]**.
- **Quarry Farm, Elmira**: **octagonal study built 1874**, moved to the Elmira College campus 1952; most of *Tom Sawyer*, *Huckleberry Finn* and *Life on the Mississippi* drafted there **[A: Paine; Center for Mark Twain Studies]**. Two pool errors: the coordinate should be **42.1131, -76.7822**, the given latitude 2.5 km too far south, and "eight consecutive summers" understates roughly twenty **[A: Wikipedia, "Quarry Farm"]**.
- Children: Susy b. 1872 Elmira, d. 1896; Clara b. 1874 Elmira, d. 1962; Jean b. **26 July 1880 at Quarry Farm, Elmira**, d. 1909. **Place error in the pool**: the chronology lens gave Jean's birthplace as Hartford **[A: Wikipedia, "Jean Clemens"]**.
- Books: *Roughing It* 1872; *The Gilded Age* with **Charles Dudley Warner** 1873; *Tom Sawyer* 1876; *The Prince and the Pauper* 1881; *Life on the Mississippi* 1883; *Huckleberry Finn* 1884; *A Connecticut Yankee* 1889 **[A]**. **1878**: from **Heidelberg** with **Joseph Twichell**, the mock expedition across the **Gorner Glacier**, *A Tramp Abroad* 1880.

## 6. The machine, the failure, the world tour, 1889-1897

- **Paige Compositor**: roughly **$300,000** sunk 1880-1894 **[A]**; the famous parts count is not in the pool and is omitted. **June 1891**: Hartford closed, the family to Europe for cost, never to live there again **[A]**.
- **April 1894**: **Charles L. Webster and Company** fails, bankruptcy protection **[A]**. **H. H. Rogers** appears in this pool only as the recipient of a letter about *Joan of Arc*, so his reorganization of Twain's finances is not narrated. The refusal to settle for less than full payment is **[R]**; the pool attests only a tour "undertaken to pay off his debts."
- **"The tour opened in Sydney" is wrong.** It opened in North America, **first lecture Cleveland, 15 July 1895**, running west to British Columbia before the Pacific crossing; Sydney, mid-September, opened the **Australian leg** **[A]**. Corrected, with the Cleveland date in the Sydney campa. Legs attested by *Following the Equator*: Sydney Sept 1895, Melbourne Oct, Bombay Jan 1896, Benares Feb, Durban May.
- **Susy Clemens dies of spinal meningitis at Hartford, August 1896**, her parents abroad **[A for the death; the month is [R], the pool gives only the year]**.
- **London 1897**: the false report of his death, answered with the remark that a New York paper had exaggerated it **[A for the event: New York Journal dispatch, via Paine]**. **The wording is [R]**, kept out of any quote field; the familiar "greatly exaggerated" form is a later improvement and no verbatim text was fetched. *Following the Equator* 1897; **Vienna 1897-1899** **[A]**.

## 7. Personal Recollections of Joan of Arc

- Serialized **anonymously in Harper's Magazine from April 1895**, credited book from **Harper & Brothers, May 1896**; Twain wanted it judged without his name **[A: Wikipedia]**. Narrator the **Sieur Louis de Conte**, Joan's page, the book presented as a translation of his memoirs **[A: Gutenberg #2874]**. **Twelve years of preparation, two of writing**, by his own account **[A: Paine, ch. CXCV]**.
- **A fabricated quote was caught in the pool, recorded here so it is never reused.** The quotation lens supplied, tagged [A]: *"Consider what she did. Judged by any of the standards of his time or ours, it is still flawless..."* "Consider what she did." occurs **nowhere** in Gutenberg #2874 (zero hits, full-text grep), and "the standards of his time or ours" is invented. The real passage, pg2874.txt lines 183-188:

  > But the character of Joan of Arc is unique. It can be measured by the standards of all times without misgiving or apprehension as to the result. Judged by any of them, it is still flawless, it is still ideally perfect; it still occupies the loftiest place possible to human attainment, a loftier one than has been reached by any other mere mortal.

  Only "it is still flawless" onward was verbatim. The fetching stage did not compress the sentence, it stitched a new opening onto it.
- **A quote upgraded from [R] to [A].** The famous verdict, flagged unverified in the pool, is traced to **Paine, ch. CXCV** (Gutenberg #2988, lines 33504-33511), Twain's written note of **30 November 1908**, his seventy-third birthday:

  > I like the Joan of Arc best of all my books; & it is the best; I know it perfectly well. And besides, it furnished me seven times the pleasure afforded me by any of the others: 12 years of preparation & a years of writing. The others needed no preparation, & got none.

  Note **"the Joan of Arc,"** with the definite article, dropped by the circulating version and by the afterlife lens quoting Wikipedia as verbatim. Both readings recorded; the journey quotes Paine with ampersands expanded, says so in quote_source, and leaves the malformed "a years of writing" standing.
- To **H. H. Rogers**: he had "never done any work before that cost so much thinking and weighing and measuring and planning and cramming" **[A]**, a fragment because the source gives one. Twentieth-century critical dismissal of the book: **[R]**, synthesized from the source's framing.
- **Gap: did Twain ever stand at Domremy or Rouen?** The geography lens dates both "disputed" and states that no confirmed account places him at Domremy before writing, and that whether he saw Rouen or worked from Quicherat's transcripts alone is not established **[R]**. The journey therefore has **no Domremy stop and no Rouen stop**; the Joan material is pinned where he demonstrably was, at Hartford, Florence and Harper's.

## 8. Last years, death, burial, 1901-1910

- **Vice-president, Anti-Imperialist League, 1901** **[A]**. **Villa di Quarto**, Florence, leased 1903 for Olivia's health; **Olivia dies there 5 June 1904** after thirty-four years of marriage **[A: Paine]**. **Honorary D.Litt., Oxford, 1907** **[A]**, month not in the pool; that it was his last Atlantic crossing is **[R]**.
- **Stormfield**, Redding, built 1907-08 by **John Mead Howells**, first arrival **18 June 1908** **[A]**. **Coordinate error three times, three values**: 41.3037, -73.3968; 41.3037, -73.3948; 41.3037, -73.3765. Stormfield stood at **41.2911, -73.4011**, on Birch Spray Hill **[A: Wikipedia, "Stormfield"]**. All three are 1.7 to 2 km out; the corrected value is used for every Redding stop.
- **Jean Clemens dies 24 December 1909 at Stormfield** **[A]**. **Cause overstated in the pool**: not a flat "heart attack." She was found dead in her bath, the finding being death "not directly from drowning but more probably from strangulation due to epilepsy or from heart failure" **[A: Wikipedia]**. The campa follows the finding.
- **Death 21 April 1910 at Stormfield, heart attack**; Halley's perihelion **20 April 1910** **[A]**. From the 1909 dictation: "I came in with Halley's comet in 1835. It is coming again next year, and I expect to go out with it." **[A]** The afterlife lens hedged Redding as "supplied from general knowledge"; the place is documented and the hedge is removed.
- **Buried in the Langdon plot, Woodlawn Cemetery, Elmira, 24 April 1910** **[A]**. **Coordinates, three values**: chronology 42.0898, -76.8077 (city centre); geography 42.0942, -76.8027 (outside the cemetery); afterlife **42.1033, -76.8306**, correct **[A]**. **Child count**: the afterlife lens says "three of their four children" and elsewhere flags Langdon's inclusion as unconfirmed. Susy, Jean and Clara are named explicitly; infant **Langdon** (d. 1872) lies there too, making four, and the journey says four. Clara's **twelve-foot monument** is two fathoms **[A]**.

## 9. Afterlife

**351 Farmington Avenue** was under demolition threat until **Katharine Seymour Day**'s rescue in **1929** and the founding of the Mark Twain Memorial; **landmark 1962**, restoration from **1955**, museum from **1974** **[A]**. **Woodlawn** on the National Register from **2004** **[A]**. A **Sydney Writers Walk plaque** marks the 1895 visit **[A; installation date a gap]**. The **autobiography**, withheld a century by his own instruction, appeared as **volume one, over 700 pages, University of California Press, November 2010** (Mark Twain Papers, Berkeley), an unexpected bestseller **[A]**. Uncollected material keeps surfacing; the source gives the years **1995** and **2015**, no titles **[A for the years; substance a gap]**.

## 10. Corpus interlock: why Twain is in this atlas

- **joan_of_arc.journey.json and lionel_royer.journey.json do not mention Twain at all** (grep: zero hits). The thread is carried entirely by **pica_dossier.md** and **joffrey_bourlemont.journey.json** **[A]**. That is the warning against reading the graft as attested biography.
- **joffrey_bourlemont.journey.json**, stop "Neufchateau: Lois de Conte, two hundred years after," dated **1411-01-06**, grafts Twain's narrator into the Bourlemont line on the feast of the Kings. Twain's text gives de Conte's birth as **6 January 1410**, the corpus arithmetic 1411; the discrepancy is the corpus's, is **not** resolved, and the journey states it in a quote_source **[A: joffrey_bourlemont.journey.json; pica_dossier.md, Graft point 4]**.
- **pica_dossier.md**: de Conte's birth year coincides with Joan's and with the extinction of the Bourlemont male line (Pierre V, d. 1412), three calendars on one seam **[A]**; documentary basis in bourlemont_roster.md, Gen. 9. The Spanish corpus post of **2023-01-05** renders Joffrey's fairy-tree song as the material Twain's book draws on, chaining 1896 back to the **1455-56 rehabilitation testimony** in essay 218 **[A]**. Named in the 1883 Hartford campa.
- **borges.journey.json**: Twain named with Stevenson and Dickens among the English authors the child Borges read before *Don Quixote* in Spanish, in the library he called the chief event of his life **[A]**. Named in the *Huckleberry Finn* campa.
- **muybridge.journey.json**: Muybridge in San Francisco from 1856 into the 1880s, overlapping Twain's 1864-66 years; no textual link attested, co-presence is all the campa claims **[A for the overlap, R for any connection]**. Same discipline for **lionel_royer.journey.json**, whose 1889 Joan canvas sits beside Twain's manuscript in the Florence campa.
- **abdelkader.journey.json**: Abd al-Qadir settled in the Amara quarter of Damascus in December 1855 and taught in the Umayyad Mosque from 1856; Twain walked the Street called Straight in **September 1867**. Same city, no contact recorded by either, and the campa says none occurred **[A for both presences; R for any meeting]**. Found by matching abdelkader's Damascus dates against the Quaker City itinerary, not supplied by the pool.
- **Jerusalem**: canonical Temple Mount pin **31.778, 35.2354**, shared with abraham, adriano and al_kamil, inherited byte-identical **[A for the pin; atlas convention, not a claim about Twain]**. **tschiffely.journey.json**, proposed on the ground that his ride crossed the United States: no shared site or date **[R, rejected]**.

## Contradictions, not adjudicated away

1. Marriage place: Buffalo (geography) against Elmira (chronology, correct).
2. Coordinates, one site and several lenses disagreeing: Florida MO (two wrong values), Stormfield (three), Woodlawn (three), Mark Twain House (two), Quarry Farm (one, 2.5 km out).
3. Henry Clemens: explosion date carried as death date, eight days apart.
4. Hartford relocation: 1873 against rented 1871, land 1873, occupied 1874.
5. Jean Clemens: born Hartford in the pool, Elmira in fact; death called a heart attack against a finding that refused to choose.
6. World tour's opening city: Sydney in the pool, Cleveland in fact.
7. The Joan of Arc verdict: definite article or not, Paine against Wikipedia.
8. De Conte's birth year: 1410 in Twain, 1411 in the corpus graft.
9. Quarry Farm summers: eight in the pool, roughly twenty in fact; Mark Twain House rooms, nineteen in the pool, twenty-five in the source.
