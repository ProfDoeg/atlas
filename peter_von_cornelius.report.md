# Peter von Cornelius (1783/84-1867): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `peter_von_cornelius`. Companion file: `peter_von_cornelius.journey.json`, 8 segments, 43 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · contradictions are printed side by side, unresolved.

Cornelius is a subject whose institutional record is dense to the year and whose personal record is a handful of bare dates: three wives, at least one daughter, a mother whose refusal he wrote up sixty years later, none documented beyond a line. The file carries that asymmetry forward.

---

## 1. Dusseldorf, 1783/84-1809

**Birth.** Son of the painter and gallery inspector **Johann Christian Alois Cornelius** and **Anna Helena Corsten**, born at Dusseldorf **[A: de.wikipedia, which gives 23 September 1783]**. **Contradiction, unresolved:** the en.wikipedia infobox and the **1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica** both give **1784** **[A]**. No baptismal register was consulted, so nothing here adjudicates. The journey file carries 1783-09-23 with the conflict stated in `date_confidence`, never in the campa.

**Training.** At the Dusseldorf academy from boyhood, among his father's circle and successors **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. No matriculation date anywhere consulted; the file's 1798 is a placeholder **[R]**.

**Father's death and the goldsmith. Contradiction, unresolved:** en.wikipedia and EB1911 give **1799**; de.wikipedia gives the father's dates as **(1748-1800)**, i.e. **1800** **[A on both sides]**. Read as *c. 1799/1800*. The research pool claimed the sources "both concur" on 1799; that is true only of the two English sources and conceals the German one.

The episode is attested in Cornelius's own voice, in a letter to **Count Athanasius Raczynski** quoted in uncredited English translation in EB1911 vol. 7 **[A]**. Verified against the proofread Wikisource text of the page (Page:EB1911 - Volume 07.djvu/185), where it appears exactly as reproduced. **Gap:** the German original was not located, so the chain runs Cornelius, Raczynski, EB1911, here, with one uncredited translation inside it. Used as the quote on the 1799 stop.

**Weimar.** Competes in the prize competitions organised under Goethe's patronage, span **1803-1805** **[A: de.wikipedia]**; no source says he placed. The file inherits Goethe's Weimar coordinate (50.98/11.329).

**Neuss. Contradiction, unresolved and consequential:** en.wikipedia dates the choir decoration of **St Quirinus, Neuss** to **1803** **[A]**; de.wikipedia states that *1806/08 schuf er Wandmalereien im Quirinus-Munster von Neuss* **[A]**. The German dating is better supported, since he was at the Dusseldorf academy until about 1805, so the file leads with 1806 and prints the 1803 claim in the campa. It does **not** carry both as separate stops, which is what the raw pool did; that double-counted one commission.

*Coordinate corrected:* the Munster's own article gives 51°11'56.6"N, 6°41'35.5"E = **51.1991/6.6932**; the pool's two variants are both about 300 m off.

*Negative finding:* that article credits the surviving large choir paintings to **Franz Ittenbach (1863-64)** and never mentions Cornelius, so whatever he painted did not survive. Said so in the campa.

**Mother's death.** Anna Helena Corsten dies **2 June 1809** **[A: de.wikipedia]**.

## 2. Frankfurt, 1809-1811

Moves to **Frankfurt am Main in the autumn of 1809** and lodges with the publisher **Friedrich Wilmans** until he leaves for Rome **[A: de.wikipedia]**. The file sets the month to October as the ordinary reading of *Herbst*; month and day are **[R]** and flagged in `date_confidence`.

Begins the outline illustrations to **Goethe's Faust** at Frankfurt **[A: en.wikipedia]**; the finished cycle is twelve plates published as **Bilder zu Goethes Faust, 1816-1826** **[A: en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]**. de.wikipedia records the completed series appearing from **1816**, which is where the file puts the publication.

## 3. The road south, 1811: an honest blank

**No source consulted documents a single stage between Frankfurt and Rome.** What is attested: he travels with the painter **Christian Xeller** **[A: de.wikipedia]** and **arrives in Rome on 14 October 1811** **[A: EB1911]**. Everything between is **[R]**, and the file says so in campa and `date_confidence` both.

- **Innsbruck / the Tyrol**: the standard German artists' road to Italy **[R]**. Overbeck's party crossed the Brenner the year before, per `johann_friedrich_overbeck.journey.json`, where that stop is itself marked reconstruction.
- **Florence**: coordinate **inherited byte-identical (43.7696/11.2558)** from Overbeck's file, where the same waypoint is recorded explicitly as a gap. The pool proposed 43.7731/11.256; the corpus pin was preferred so two reconstructed passages down one road sit on one point.
- **Verona**, a third **[R]** transit stop in the pool, is dropped, to keep the reconstructed part of the route from outweighing the documented part.

## 4. Rome, 1811-1819

**The Lukasbund.** Joins the German painters at the disused Franciscan convent of **Sant'Isidoro a Capo le Case** and enters the **Lukasbund** **[A: de.wikipedia 'Lukasbund'; en.wikipedia 'Nazarene movement']**.

*Founding facts corrected:* the Lukasbund was founded at the **Vienna Academy in 1809 by six students** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, not four; the four usually named (Overbeck, Pforr, Vogel, Hottinger) are those who **moved to Rome in 1810**, Sutter and Wintergerst being the usual additions to the roster. **Cornelius is absent from the founding and joins only after his 1811 arrival** **[A]**.

*Four-to-six-year error corrected:* one pool item bundled "works at the Casa Bartholdy, befriends Overbeck, joins the Lukasbund" under **1811**. The Casa Bartholdy frescoes are **1816-17** (en.wikipedia, 'Nazarene movement'), with de.wikipedia dating the commission to **1815**. The file splits them: 1811 for arrival and brotherhood, 1816 for the wall.

*Communal phase shortened:* the pool gave 1811-1815 for the San Isidoro community, but **Pforr died in 1812** and **Vogel and Hottinger left Rome in 1813** **[A: de.wikipedia 'Lukasbund']**, so it had largely dissolved by 1813. The file's Pforr stop carries the dissolution; his day-date is **inherited from `franz_pforr.journey.json`**, the pool having the year only.

**Marriage.** Marries **Carolina (Carlotta) Grossi**, Roman-born, **1814** **[A: de.wikipedia]**; month and day unrecorded.

**Casa Bartholdy.** **Jakob Salomon Bartholdy**, Prussian consul general, rents rooms in the **Palazzo Zuccari, Via Gregoriana**, and commissions the Joseph cycle from **Cornelius, Overbeck, Philipp Veit and Wilhelm von Schadow** **[A: en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]**. The painter is **Wilhelm** von Schadow, not Rudolph; the pool carried that slip elsewhere.

*Coordinate:* the pool's 41.904/12.4845 is about 180 m off. The file uses **41.9056/12.4839**, which is both en.wikipedia's coordinate for the Palazzo Zuccari and, byte-identical, the pin already in Overbeck's file.

**Casino Massimo, the pool's largest error, kept as a contradiction.** en.wikipedia lists the **Villa Massimi** among his Roman decorations **[A]**; it.wikipedia ('Casino Massimo Lancellotti') states that *Philipp Veit dipinse il Paradiso sulla volta, invece Joseph Anton Koch realizzo l'Inferno e il Purgatorio sulle quattro pareti*, **1818-28** **[A]**. Cornelius was assigned the **Dante room** and left for Munich in 1819 without executing it. The stop is kept, says he did not paint it, and prints both attributions.

**Nibelungenlied.** The **Aventiure von den Nibelungen** designs, **1817** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. This part of the pool's Rome-decade item is sound.

**Naples [R].** Kept as one reconstruction stop saying plainly that no source consulted gives a date or an occasion and that the year is arbitrary within c. 1812-1818. The absence is itself informative: the Roman decade is documented by commissions and by nothing else.

## 5. Munich and Dusseldorf, 1819-1840

**Summons.** Called to Munich by **Crown Prince Ludwig of Bavaria** to plan the **Glyptothek** frescoes, **1819** **[A: en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia concurs]**.

**Dusseldorf directorship. Contradiction, unresolved:** de.wikipedia's **Kunstakademie Dusseldorf** article records him director from **1 October 1819 to 1825**, with **Mosler** interim 1825-26 **[A]**; en.wikipedia dates it **1821-1824** **[A]**. The pool's two lenses disagreed with each other as well. The file leads with 1819-10-01 and prints both readings.

*Coordinate corrected and flagged anachronistic:* the academy is at **Eiskellerstrasse 1, approx. 51.2288/6.7716**; the pool's 51.2251/6.7768 is about 500 m southeast. More to the point, the present building was **erected 1875-1879**, so no coordinate for it marks a place Cornelius stood. Said in `date_confidence` on both Dusseldorf academy stops.

**Glyptothek.** The **Gottersaal / Heroensaal** cycles, **1820-1830** **[A: de.wikipedia]**, executed largely by pupils under his cartoons **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Munich academy.** **Johann Peter von Langer** dies **6 August 1824** **[A: de.wikipedia]**; the sources give the transition as **1824-1825** **[A: EB1911; en.wikipedia]**. Formally appointed and **ennobled by Ludwig I in 1825**, taking the *von* **[A: de.wikipedia]**.

*Coordinate flagged:* the Akademie der Bildenden Kunste at Akademiestrasse 2-4 is at **48.1531/11.5803** (the pool gave 48.1518/11.582), but the Neureuther building there was **built 1876-1885 and occupied from 1886**, so it is not where he directed; `date_confidence` says so. **Gap:** the academy's premises in the 1820s were not located this session.

**Pinakothek.** Takes part in the decorative and iconographic planning for **Leo von Klenze's** building **[A: en.wikipedia]**, dated c. 1826-1836 in the pool without a precise start; the file's 1826 is a placeholder. Designs published posthumously as **Entwurfe zu den kunstgeschichtlichen Fresken, 1875** **[A: de.wikipedia]**.

**Carolina's death.** Wife and a daughter die **24 October 1834** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** the daughter is unnamed in every source consulted, and nothing else about the household reached this research.

**Ludwigskirche.** The **Last Judgment** over the high altar, **1836-1840** **[A: de.wikipedia, 'St. Ludwig (Munchen)']**, reported at roughly **62 feet by 38** **[A: EB1911]**; de.wikipedia calls it the second largest altar fresco in the world after Michelangelo's. The pool's vague "c. 1830s-1840" and its coordinate, about 140 m off, are both corrected (**48.1489/11.5814**).

## 6. Berlin, 1841-1848

**The break with Ludwig. Contradiction, unresolved:** de.wikipedia dates the quarrel and the move to **1841** **[A]**; en.wikipedia to about **1839-1840** **[A]**. Neither gives the occasion. **Gap.**

**Campo Santo.** **Frederick William IV** commissions the **Apocalyptic Riders** cartoons for a planned royal mausoleum beside the cathedral; the project dies after **1848** and Cornelius draws on to **1867** **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, both giving 1841-1867]**.

**London.** Crosses to England in **November 1841**, invited by the Fine Arts Commission considering fresco for the rebuilt Houses of Parliament **[A: en.wikipedia]**; day unrecorded. **Gesetzlose Gesellschaft:** joins the Berlin "Lawless Society", **1843** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** nothing of what that membership involved reached this research.

*Berlin residence coordinate rejected:* the pool used **52.517/13.3888** for a studio and final residence, generic ground near Potsdamer Platz matching no documented address in anything consulted. The file drops it; Berlin stops sit on the city pin (52.52/13.405) or the cathedral pin (52.5192/13.401), and none claims to mark his house. **Gap: his Berlin address is unknown to this research.**

## 7. Last years and death

**The Rome residence claim, flagged and kept.** en.wikipedia states he lived in Rome from **1853** until **1869** **[A]**, which is two years after the same article's own **1867** death date. The statements are mutually exclusive. The file keeps the stop, tags it unreliable in `date_confidence`, and says in the campa that the range is not usable. Neither silently dropped nor silently repaired.

**Second wife.** **Geltrude Ferretini** dies **June 1859** **[A: de.wikipedia, name and month]**. **The place is not attested:** de.wikipedia calls her Roman and does not say where she died, so the pool's "Berlin" was inference presented as fact. The file keeps the Berlin coordinate for want of another and says in campa and `date_confidence` that it is inference.

**Third wife.** Marries in **1861** a woman **from Urbino** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. **Gap: her name is not in the consulted source.** **Honorary citizen of Dusseldorf, 1862** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. **Death: Berlin, 6 March 1867** **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia; EB1911, agreeing]**.

## 8. Afterlife

**Burial.** de.wikipedia's wording is *Grabstatte auf dem Alten Domfriedhof der St.-Hedwigsgemeinde in Berlin*: the old cathedral cemetery of the **St Hedwig parish**, **Liesenstrasse, Berlin-Mitte** **[A]**. The pool's afterlife lens garbled the name and its geography lens put the burial in **Kreuzberg**; the two lenses contradicted each other and Mitte is correct.

*Coordinate:* St.-Hedwig-Friedhof I, Liesenstrasse 8, **52.5387/13.379**; the cathedral parish's Domfriedhof I is next door at no. 6. **Gap:** that cemetery's article does **not** list Cornelius among its notable burials, so the plot is unconfirmed and the pin marks a gate. Whether the grave survives or was cleared in a regrading is unresolved; the article was fetched under two title variants and 404'd both times. **The burial date is unrecovered** and the file's 1867-03-10 is labelled a placeholder.

**Monument, Dusseldorf.** The **Peter-von-Cornelius-Denkmal** by **Adolf von Donndorf**, unveiled **24 June 1879** as a *point de vue* where the Konigsallee meets the **Corneliusplatz**, **51.2271/6.7790** **[A: de.wikipedia]**; the pool's 51.2277/6.7828 is 280 m east.

**Ruhmeshalle bust, Munich.** Among the busts on the right-hand wall, identified as **MALER** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. *Coordinate corrected by about 2 km:* the Ruhmeshalle is at **48.1308/11.5457**; the pool's 48.1478/11.5379 lands in Neuhausen. **Gap: the installation date is unrecovered.** The first 74 busts date to 1853, with additions from 1868; the file's 1868 is labelled a placeholder.

**Corneliusgasse, Vienna.** Mariahilf names the street in **1867** jointly for the painter and his nephew the composer **Peter Cornelius** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. Coordinate 48.1958/16.3543 from the pool, unchecked. With month and day defaulted to 01 the stop sorts ahead of the burial although the naming followed the death; flagged in `date_confidence`.

**Academy portal.** His name has stood over the Kunstakademie's main portal **since the 1890s** **[A: de.wikipedia]**, on a building of 1875-79. Folded into the monument stop's campa. **Bartholdy frescoes to Berlin, 1886-87**, the Joseph cycle now in the **Alte Nationalgalerie** **[A/R: de.wikipedia 'Lukasbund', tagged [R] in the pool and carried at that strength]**.

**Gautier.** In **1855** Theophile Gautier wrote that Cornelius enjoyed "a celebrity such as few artists enjoy in their lifetime... as if he were already dead" **[A: en.wikipedia, quoting Gautier]**. **Not used as a quote in the file:** an ellipsed English fragment at second hand, no page, no original.

**Bibliography.** Ernst Forster, *Peter von Cornelius* (Berlin, 1874); Metropolitan Museum of Art, *German Masters of the Nineteenth Century*, catalogue, **1981**, nos. **17-21** **[A: en.wikipedia; the pool omitted the year]**.

**Negative finding [R]:** no source consulted documents any legend, miracle story, or folk tradition attached to him after death. He accreted only institutional commemoration: a monument, a street, a bust, a name over a door.

- **`johann_friedrich_overbeck`**: the spine. Sant'Isidoro 1811, the Casa Bartholdy walls 1816, the Munich directorship Cornelius takes and Overbeck refuses, the Stadel refusal of 1829, the two graves. Overbeck's file supplies the Cornelius quotation used on the Palazzo Zuccari stop.

## Sources

**Reached and used**

- de.wikipedia, *Peter von Cornelius*: the spine, and the only source for the 1783 day-date, the mother's death, the wives, the 1834 deaths, the ennoblement, the honorary citizenship, the burial ground.
- en.wikipedia, *Peter von Cornelius*: Faust, Glyptothek, London, Campo Santo, the Met catalogue and Forster bibliography. **Internally inconsistent** on the Rome residence, and at odds with de.wikipedia on Neuss, the Dusseldorf years, the Berlin move, and the Casino Massimo.
- *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, 11th ed. (1911), vol. 7, *Cornelius, Peter von*, by William Cave Thomas: the 14 October 1811 arrival, the fresco dimensions, the Raczynski letter, verified against the proofread Wikisource page.
- de.wikipedia *Lukasbund*; en.wikipedia *Nazarene movement*; it.wikipedia *Casino Massimo Lancellotti*; and, for coordinates and negative findings, de.wikipedia *Kunstakademie Dusseldorf*, *St. Ludwig (Munchen)*, *Quirinus-Munster*, *Ruhmeshalle (Munchen)*, *Peter-von-Cornelius-Denkmal*, *Hofgarten (Dusseldorf)*, *St.-Hedwig-Friedhof I*, *Johann Peter von Langer*.
- Corpus: `johann_friedrich_overbeck.journey.json`, `franz_pforr.journey.json`, `goethe_full.journey.json`, `johann_tischbein.journey.json`.

**Not reached, with the reason**

- The **German original of the Raczynski letter**, and Raczynski's *Geschichte der neueren deutschen Kunst*: not located, so the quote stands on an uncredited 1911 translation.
- **Ernst Forster, *Peter von Cornelius* (Berlin, 1874)**, the contemporary biography: known only as a bibliography line. The largest single gap here; what it holds about his household is probably not online at all.
- The **St Hedwig cemetery article** under the title the German biography uses: 404 under two variants, so the plot is unlocated. **Gautier's 1855 text** in French with a page: only an ellipsed English fragment at second hand.
- **Archival series** listed in `suggested_refs` throughout (Dusseldorf registers, Roman parish registers for 1814, Bavarian patents of nobility, Prussian building files on the Campo Santo, Fine Arts Commission reports): none consulted, named so the next pass knows where to go.

**Seen and rejected:** the **Wallraf commission** for the Neuss murals (unverifiable in anything consulted, dropped from the file under every tag); the **Berlin coordinate 52.517/13.3888** (no documented address, dropped); the **1811 dating of the Casa Bartholdy** (a four-to-six-year error, corrected); **"Casa Zuccari" as a site distinct from the Casa Bartholdy** (the Bartholdy rooms *were* an apartment in the Palazzo Zuccari, so one commission counted twice, merged to one pin); **Cornelius as decorator of the Villa Massimi** (kept only as a printed contradiction, never as a fact); the **Kreuzberg burial** (wrong, Mitte is correct).
