# Pytheas de Massalia: research report

*Slug: `pytheas_de_massalia`. Greek navigator, astronomer and geographer of Massalia (Marseille), fl. c. 330-320 BCE. Author of the lost `Peri tou Okeanou` (On the Ocean). Subject 503 in the census, queued with zero documents and no prior journey file.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference or tradition. Where the two conflict, both are printed and neither is adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**The governing difficulty.** Not one line of Pytheas survives in his own manuscript. Everything below is quotation, paraphrase or hostile summary in writers two to four centuries later, most of whom did not believe him, and at least one of whom (Strabo) probably never saw the original book. The standard fragment editions are Mette (1952) and Roseman (1994); a recent count puts the fragments at about thirty-nine. This report tags the *transmission* as often as the claim.

## 1. Massalia, and a man with no birth year (c. 350 BCE)

Pytheas is a citizen of Massalia, the Phocaean colony at the mouth of the Rhone, today Marseille. **[A]** Every ancient citation identifies him by that city, and the city is the only biographical fact the tradition agrees on.

No ancient source gives a birth year, a father's name, a death date, or a grave. **[R]** The conventional c. 350 BCE is inferred backward from the voyage, itself dated only by cross-reference to Timaeus and Dicaearchus. The chronology rests on two negative facts: he is later than Aristotle's generation and earlier than Timaeus's citation of him.

**Attested science at Massalia.** He determines his own city's latitude with a gnomon at the summer solstice, precisely enough that Hipparchus and, through him, Eratosthenes reuse it as a reference point. **[A]** This is the one Pytheas datum nobody in antiquity attacks: Strabo, who calls him a liar about everything else, does not dispute the shadow.

**What Massalia wanted.** That a merchant city had a commercial motive for northern reconnaissance, tin and amber reaching it only through Gaulish middlemen while Carthage held the Atlantic mouth, is modern reconstruction (Cunliffe 2001), not a statement in any fragment. **[R]** No source says who paid, and that hole becomes Polybius's weapon two centuries later.

## 2. The route out, disputed at the first step (c. 325 BCE)

Nothing in the surviving fragments states how Pytheas left the Mediterranean. Two reconstructions compete and neither is resolved here: the sea route past the Pillars of Hercules (the standard assumption, resting on his being a sailor), and an overland route down the Garonne and Gironde avoiding Carthaginian waters entirely **[R]**, associated with Mette's 1952 edition. The second exists because Carthage controlled the strait. Gadir (Cadiz) appears in most reconstructions by periplus convention, not evidence: **no ancient source places Pytheas at the Melqart sanctuary or anywhere on the Iberian Atlantic coast.** **[R]**

*Coordinate correction:* the Melqart-Herakles sanctuary of Gades is on the Sancti Petri islet at approximately 36.3796 N, -6.2202 W. A draft figure of 36.5836, -6.2059 falls inside the Bay of Cadiz near Puerto Real, twenty-two kilometres too far north.

## 3. The Atlantic coast of Gaul

**Corbilo on the Loire.** Strabo 4.2.1 mentions Corbilo as a former emporium on the Liger. **[A]** He does **not** call it a tin emporium, and no ancient source does; the "great tin-trading port" gloss is unsupported, demoted to **[R]**. What Strabo reports there is Polybius's anecdote that no Massaliot who spoke with Scipio could tell him anything about Britain worth recording, deployed *against* Pytheas. The site is unlocated; the Loire has moved.

**The Ostimii and the Cabaeum promontory.** Strabo 1.4.5 quotes Pytheas naming the Ostimii as the westernmost people of Gaul, with the promontory Cabaeum and the island Uxisame three days' sail out. **[A]** He quotes it to attack the distances, not, as is sometimes said, to mock measuring a coast by dead reckoning. *Terminological correction:* Pointe du Raz is in the **Finistère department of Brittany**; Cape Finisterre is a different headland, in Galicia. Draft material conflated the two.

## 4. Britain: tin, a promontory, and a circuit

**Belerion and Ictis.** Diodorus 5.21-22 describes the inhabitants of the Cornish tip as civilised by contact with foreign traders, gives a technical account of digging and smelting tin into knucklebone ingots, and describes an island reachable on foot at low tide where the Britons bring the tin on packhorses. **[A]** Diodorus names no source; the Pytheas-through-Timaeus chain is inference, and Ictis has never been identified (St Michael's Mount is one of two leading candidates). **[R]**

**Kantion.** The promontory at the south-east tip comes from Diodorus 5.21.3, and Strabo 1.4.3 attacks Pytheas's Cantium distance. **[A]** *Correction:* Pliny NH 4.102 does **not** name Cantium, contrary to secondary summaries; it gives the island's circuit, credited jointly to "Pytheas and Isidorus," at 4,875 miles.

**The circumference of Britain: four numbers that do not agree, printed unresolved.**

| Figure | Source | Attribution |
|---|---|---|
| more than 20,000 stadia (length) | Strabo 1.4.3 | Pytheas, quoted to be refuted |
| more than 40,000 stadia (coastline) | Strabo 2.4.1 | Pytheas, quoted to be refuted |
| 42,500 stadia | Diodorus 5.21.4 | **no source named by Diodorus** |
| 4,875 miles | Pliny NH 4.102 | Pytheas and Isidorus jointly |

The routine claim that Diodorus attributes 42,500 stadia to Timaeus is **false as stated**: Diodorus names nobody there. The Timaeus intermediary is modern inference (Walbank 1956, Mette 1952, Roller 2006), argued partly from Diodorus naming Timaeus in the Book 5 preface. Tag it **[R]**.

**The tide.** Pliny NH 2.99 (Loeb §217): "Pytheas of Massilia states that north of Britain the tides rise 120 ft." **[A]** 120 Roman feet equals 80 cubits, hence the cubit figure in older renderings (Bostock). *Correction:* Pliny locates this **north of** Britain, not in it.

**Orkney.** Pliny NH 4.103 lists forty Orcades but does **not** attribute them to Pytheas, who is credited with the northern tip of Britain, Orcas, via Diodorus 5.21; Ptolemy, Mela and Tacitus name the island group independently. The attribution of the count to Pytheas, common in popular accounts, is unsupported. **[R]**

## 5. Thule and the congealed sea

**Location and daylight.** "Thule, which Pytheas says is a six days' sail north of Britain, and is near the frozen sea" (Strabo 1.4.2). **[A]** *Correction:* that figure is Strabo's, not Pliny's, and is routinely misattributed. Pliny NH 4.104 supplies the other half, no nights at midsummer "when the sun is passing through the sign of Cancer," and puts the frozen Cronian sea **one** day's sail beyond Thule, not six. **[A]**

**Pytheas's own voice, as near as it survives.** The "barbarians showed us where the Sun goes to sleep" fragment, Geminus VI 9, quoted verbatim in the journey file. **[A]** *Correction:* the older English wording in circulation ("the place where the sun goes to rest ... rose again a short time after it had set") is not traceable to any named translator, and Wikipedia relays it with no edition in the footnote. Evans and Berggren (2006) is the citable text.

**The sea-lung.** Strabo 2.4.1, reporting Polybius reporting Pytheas: a substance "resembling a sea-lungs" in which earth, sea and all the elements are held in suspension, "a sort of bond to hold all together, which you can neither walk nor sail upon." **[A]** *Correction:* the passage is at 2.4.1, not 1.4.2 or 2.5.8 as often cited. Strabo preserves in the same place the most important methodological sentence in the corpus: "Now, as for this thing that resembles the sea-lungs, he says that he saw it himself, but that all the rest he tells from hearsay." **[A]** Pytheas, quoted by a man who calls him a liar, is drawing the eyewitness/hearsay line himself.

**Where Thule was.** Unresolved in antiquity and unresolved now. Candidates: Norway (Trondheim region, the island of Smøla), Iceland, Shetland, the Faroes, Orkney. **[R]** Solstice-daylight arguments point at roughly 64-66 N, a poor fit for Trondheim at 63.43 N; drafts reasoning to that band and then pinning Trondheim are inconsistent, and this report flags rather than repairs it.

**A verification failure, stated plainly.** The claim that Barry Cunliffe favours Norway or Iceland over Shetland **could not be confirmed in any reachable source**, including the Wikipedia articles on Pytheas, Thule and Cunliffe. Treat any Cunliffe-attributed Thule identification as unsourced until checked against the book itself.

## 6. The amber coast and the return

Pliny NH 37.35, trans. D. E. Eichholz (Loeb, 1962), quoted verbatim in the journey file: the estuary Metuonis, 750 miles long, the German Guiones on its shores, a day's sail on to the Isle of Abalus where amber arrives by spring currents, "an excretion consisting of solidified brine," and the inhabitants "use it as fuel instead of wood and sell it to the neighbouring Teutones." **[A]** Three corrections attach.

- **Translator.** Loeb Pliny volume X (books 36-37) is Eichholz, not Rackham; Rackham did the Book 4 volume, and the name gets carried over in secondary citation.
- **Text.** The Loeb reads *Metuonis* and *Guiones*; *Mentonomon* and *Gutones* are the older Bostock readings. Both should be printed.
- **Naming, reversed in most summaries.** Pliny NH 37.35-36 says **Pytheas** calls the island **Abalus** and **Timaeus** calls it **Basilia** (Xenophon of Lampsacus: Baltia). Drafts giving Basilia to Pytheas have it backwards. But Pliny NH 4.95 does read "Pytheas gives its name as Basilia," so **Pliny contradicts himself between books 4 and 37 and does not settle it.** Printed unresolved.

Localisation of the amber coast is modern inference throughout: Sambia near Kaliningrad, Jutland, Gotland and the Vistula mouth have all been proposed, and the ancient text fixes nothing. **[R]** Heligoland, offered in popular accounts as the Abalus candidate, is **not** in the standard reference treatment (Prussian Baltic coast per Smith 1854, Gotland, Jutland per Humboldt).

*A conflation to avoid:* the Ostimii/Ostiones are Strabo's Breton people at the western end of the route (1.4.5); the people on the Metuonis estuary are the Guiones/Gutones. Drafts placing "the Ostiaei" on the northern estuary have fused fragments from opposite ends of the voyage.

**The book.** He returns and writes it up as *On the Ocean* (*Peri tou Okeanou*), Geminus VI 9 being one of the few citations naming the work directly. **[A]** *Periplus* and *Periodos Ges* also circulate. **[R]** No manuscript survives; the text was gone by late antiquity.

## 7. The reception war: how a lost book gets transmitted by its enemies

- **Timaeus** repeats the amber island **[A]** and is the probable channel for the Cornish tin **[R]**. *Correction:* exiled from Sicily by Agathocles c. 315-312 BCE, he wrote over roughly fifty years in **Athens**; drafts placing him in Sicily or Italy are wrong.
- **Dicaearchus of Messana**, a near-contemporary Peripatetic, is reported not to have trusted the stories: the earliest recorded doubt. **[R]**, his text being lost.
- **Eratosthenes** builds the latitudes, including the Massalia solstice, into his map (Strabo 1.4.2-4, 2.5.8); **Hipparchus** uses the observations to fix a parallel between Massalia and Byzantium. Both **[A]** via Strabo.
- **Polybius** attacks on economic grounds: "it is incredible that a private individual, and a poor man too, could have travelled such distances by sea and by land" (Strabo 2.4.1). **[A]** About funding, not geography, and the most durable objection anyone raised.
- **Geminus** preserves the fragment above without ridicule, treating the non-setting sun as astronomically plausible. **[A]** *Correction:* his floruit is disputed (c. 70 BCE on one reading, as late as 50 CE on others), and "of Rhodes" is inferred from references to Rhodian mountains. The -70/Rhodes pairing is **[R]**.
- **Diodorus Siculus** carries the tin, the amber island and the northern promontory into a universal history, attributing none of it. **[A]** text, **[R]** attribution.
- **Strabo** supplies the largest body of *polemic*: "not only has the man who tells about Thule, Pytheas, been found, upon scrutiny, to be an arch-falsifier" (1.4.3), verbatim. **[A]** *Correction:* the claim that he is the largest source of *fragments* is contested and contradicts the parallel judgment that Pliny preserves more near-verbatim text.
- **Pliny the Elder** cites him across books 2, 4 and 37 as a source to be quoted rather than dismissed, and does not resolve the Pytheas/Timaeus contradictions he records. **[A]**

## 8. The afterlife of a name

Virgil, *Georgics* 1.30, sets "ultima Thule" as a figure for the edge of the world (c. 29 BCE); Procopius, *De Bello Gothico*, makes Thule a northern island of thirteen tribes, effectively Scandinavia (6th c.); Isidore, *Etymologiae* 14.6.4, fixes it as the world's northernmost island; Dicuil, *De mensura orbis terrae* (c. 825), records clerics who sailed to a northern island, read as Iceland, and saw the midnight sun. All **[A]**.

The Hyperborea fusion is not Pytheas's doing. **No surviving fragment shows him using the word.** **[R]** Hyperborea is older Greek myth (Pindar, Herodotus), welded onto Thule by later writers. *Correction:* Strabo 1.4.4-5 does **not** discuss the Hyperboreans; those passages are elsewhere (7.3.1, 11.6.2). The citation commonly given for "Strabo criticises the conflation" is wrong.

**Munich, 1918.** The Thule Society is dedicated on 18 August 1918 under Sebottendorff, taking the name as a lost Aryan homeland. **[A]** *Correction:* the Hotel Vier Jahreszeiten is documented as headquarters and meeting rooms, not an attested founding venue, and the Wikipedia article on the society does not mention it. Downgrade to **[R]**; the hotel (Maximilianstrasse 17) sits at roughly 48.139, 11.583.

**The Ahnenerbe.** Founded by Himmler on 1 July 1935 **[A]**, it sent **no expeditions in 1935**: Bohuslän and Karelia (1936), Val Camonica (1937), **Iceland (1938, three expeditions explicitly seeking Thule as birthplace of the Aryan race)**, Tibet (1938-39), Central Eurasia (1938). Drafts dating "1935 expeditions" are wrong twice over.

**Arrokoth.** NASA chose the nickname "Ultima Thule" on **13 March 2018** after a public campaign; the flyby was 1 January 2019. The object was **not renamed by NASA**: the IAU announced Arrokoth in November 2019, ceremony at NASA HQ on 12 November, with permission from Pamunkey elders of the Powhatan nation, after criticism of Thule's Nazi-era appropriation. **[A]** Drafts dating the nickname to the flyby are wrong.

## 10. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. Circumference of Britain: 20,000 vs 40,000 vs 42,500 stadia vs 4,875 miles (§4).
2. The amber island: Pliny NH 37.35 gives Abalus to Pytheas and Basilia to Timaeus; Pliny NH 4.95 gives Basilia to Pytheas. Pliny against himself.
3. Six days' sail: to **Thule** north of Britain (Strabo 1.4.2) versus to **Mictis/Ictis** where tin is found (Pliny 4.104, credited to Timaeus). Routinely conflated; two different claims.
4. The frozen sea beyond Thule: one day's sail (Pliny 4.104) against the looser "near the frozen sea" (Strabo 1.4.2).
5. Who preserves the most Pytheas: Strabo (most polemic) or Pliny (most near-verbatim text).
6. Lunar tidal theory: attributed to Pytheas by Aetius/Pseudo-Plutarch, *Placita* 3.17 alone. **Pliny NH 2.99 cites him only for tidal height, not the lunar cause**, and "first anywhere in the record" is contested against Seleucus of Seleucia (c. 150 BCE) and Posidonius.
7. Thule's identity: unresolved for two thousand three hundred years.

## 11. Gaps stated as gaps

- **No tomb, no grave, no burial tradition.** Not a lost tomb; there was never a tomb story to lose. **[R]** from absence in the corpus. His afterlife is entirely textual.
- **No birth year, no death year, no patronymic, no family. No statement of who funded the voyage**, the hole Polybius drives his attack through. **No route statement**: the exit from the Mediterranean is reconstruction throughout.
- **The "tidal observatory" at Massalia is invented framing.** No such site is attested; the phrase should not be reused.
- The Kuiper Belt object has no terrestrial coordinate; the journey file uses the Applied Physics Laboratory at Laurel, Maryland, as an operational proxy and says so. The Reykjavik coordinate for Thule is likewise a proxy, inherited for atlas continuity.
