# Roland Barthes (1915-1980): research report

*2026-08-02. Prepared for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Roland Gérard Barthes, French literary theorist, semiologist, essayist; census row #141, three corpus documents, queue description "French semiotician of The Fashion System."*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, with the source named in place · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or secondary biography carried without direct citation. Contradictions between sources are printed as contradictions. Where the verification pass overturned a claim from the gathering pass, the overturn is recorded here rather than quietly absorbed.

A general caution about this dossier: the reachable evidence is almost entirely encyclopaedic (English and French Wikipedia, with German Wikipedia used twice as a tie-breaker), plus Wikiquote and one full text of *Elements of Semiology* mirrored on marxists.org. The standard biographies (Louis-Jean Calvet, *Roland Barthes: A Biography*, 1994; Tiphaine Samoyault, *Barthes: A Biography*, 2017) were **not** reachable this session and are cited nowhere as [A]. Several details that "everybody knows" about Barthes therefore sit in this report as [R], including the rue Servandoni apartment and the sanatorium at Saint-Hilaire-du-Touvet.

---

## 1. Cherbourg and the dead father (1915-1916)

- Born **12 November 1915 in Cherbourg**, Normandy. **[A: en.wikipedia "Roland Barthes"; fr.wikipedia "Roland Barthes"]**
- The father, **Louis Barthes** (b. 1883, Marmande), **died 27 October 1916 aboard the trawler *Montaigne* in the North Sea**, sixteen days before his son's first birthday. **[A: fr.wikipedia, citing the Mémoire des Hommes death record: "BARTHES Louis, Mort pour la France le 27/10/1916"]**
- **Correction carried forward.** The gathering pass called Louis Barthes a "naval officer killed in action in World War I" and, in the geography lens, said he was "killed in a skirmish off Heligoland." Neither holds. He was an officer of the **merchant** marine (*officier de la marine marchande*) mobilised for the war as *enseigne de vaisseau*; and **Heligoland appears in no cited source**. The location is "the North Sea," nothing narrower. The Heligoland detail is struck.
- Coordinate note: the pool's Cherbourg pin (49.6337, -1.6222) sits about 620 m from Wikipedia's town point (49.6389, -1.6250). Both are inside Cherbourg. The journey file uses the Wikipedia point.

**Gap:** nothing reachable this session describes the family's circumstances in Cherbourg itself, or how long Henriette Barthes stayed there after the death. The move south is attested; its date is not.

## 2. The Basque country (1916-1924)

- Raised in **Bayonne** by his mother Henriette, an aunt, and a grandmother. **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**
- **Urt**, a village on the Adour above Bayonne, is where he is buried in 1980 beside his mother. **[A: fr.wikipedia, citing Dominique Noguez in *Libération*]**
- **Downgraded to [R].** The gathering pass asserted, with an [A] tag, that Urt held "the Barthes family's true anchor," his grandparents' house, and that he returned "through every school holiday of his childhood." **Neither cited Wikipedia article documents a family house at Urt or the holiday returns.** fr.wikipedia mentions Urt only as the burial place. The childhood-anchor framing is secondary-biography material and is carried in the journey file as **[R]**, flagged in the stop's date_confidence.
- Coordinate correction: the pool gave Urt as 43.5178, -1.2872 in two places (family house, burial) and 43.4947, -1.3244 in a third (afterlife lens). All three are wrong, by roughly 2.6 km, 2.85 km, and 2.85 km respectively, and the last two disagree with each other about the same grave. **Urt village is 43.4947, -1.28917 (fr.wikipedia); the cemetery is approximately 43.4922, -1.2887 (OSM, Chemin du Cimetière).** The journey file uses those two.
- Henriette's maiden name is **Binger**; her father was the explorer and colonial governor Louis-Gustave Binger. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**

## 3. Paris, the lycées, the first blood (1924-1941)

- Family relocates to **Paris, 1924**. **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**
- **Correction carried forward.** The gathering pass wrote that Barthes and his mother "keep house together" as a pair for fifty years. The household was not a dyad: fr.wikipedia records a **half-brother, Michel Salzedo**, who later authorised the posthumous publications, and de.wikipedia describes Barthes living "with his mother and stepbrother under difficult financial circumstances in their Parisian apartment." **[A: fr.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]**
- **Lycée Montaigne**, early secondary schooling. **[A: fr.wikipedia, "à Paris, où il étudie au lycée Montaigne"; category "Élève du lycée Montaigne (Paris)"]** No dates are given in the source; c. 1924-1930 is inference. Coordinate corrected from 48.8462, 2.3327 (inside the Luxembourg Gardens) to **48.84395, 2.33539** (17 rue Auguste Comte, OSM).
- **Lycée Louis-le-Grand**, final lycée years; **baccalauréat 1934**. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **1934: hemoptysis and a lesion of the left lung**, the first declaration of the tuberculosis. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **1937: exempted from military service.** **[A: fr.wikipedia, "En 1937, il est exempté du service militaire"]** **Correction:** the gathering pass framed this as exemption "from military service in World War II," which is anachronistic by two years. It was exemption from compulsory **peacetime** service; it is what subsequently kept him out of mobilisation.
- **Licence in classical letters, University of Paris, 1939**; **DES (MA-equivalent) 1941**, thesis on Greek tragedy under **Paul Mazon**. **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**
- **Lycée de Biarritz, 1939**; **lycées Voltaire and Buffon, Paris, 1940-1941**. **[A: fr.wikipedia, "puis aux lycées Voltaire et Buffon de Paris (1940-1941)"]**
- Coordinate corrections: Lycée Voltaire from 48.8593, 2.3822 to **48.8641, 2.38473** (101 avenue de la République, OSM); Lycée Buffon from 48.8404, 2.2967 to **48.84306, 2.31139** (16 boulevard Pasteur, fr.wikipedia), an error of about 1.12 km.
- **Internal contradiction in the pool, resolved against the gathering pass.** The Buffon entry closed by saying his later career ran through "the Sorbonne's own graduate structures." The EPHE, the EHESS and the Collège de France are not Sorbonne structures; the EPHE is an independent *grand établissement*, as the pool's own EPHE entry says ("outside the rigidities of the Sorbonne"). The two entries contradicted each other. The claim is struck.
- **Certificate in grammar and philology, 1943.** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**

## 5. Bucharest, Alexandria, and the turn to linguistics (1947-1952)

- Appointed **librarian at the Institut français, Bucharest, 1947**. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **Contradiction, flagged.** The gathering pass gave the Bucharest posting as **1947-1951**. That span appears to be an artifact: fr.wikipedia gives only the 1947 appointment, and separately notes eight articles he published in *Combat* "entre le 1 août 1947 et le 13 septembre 1951." de.wikipedia gives the actual posting as **1948-1949** ("Von 1948 bis 1949 arbeitete er als Bibliotheksgehilfe und unterrichtete am Institut français und der Universität Bukarest"), followed by Alexandria 1949-1950. On de.wikipedia's reading the "overlap" with Alexandria that the gathering pass flagged dissolves entirely. **fr.wikipedia's 1947 and de.wikipedia's 1948 are not reconciled here.** The journey file dates the stop 1947 and carries both readings in date_confidence.
- Coordinate corrected: Institut Français de Bucarest, 77 Bd. Dacia, is **44.4466, 26.1047** (OSM), not 44.4361, 26.0987.
- **University of Alexandria, teaching French, 1949 to June 1950.** **[A: fr.wikipedia]** Meets **Algirdas Julien Greimas** there; the friendship is credited with pushing him toward structural linguistics. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **1952: French Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then the CNRS.** **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]** *Stagiaire de recherche* 1953; *attaché de recherche* 1956-1960. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**

## 6. Degree Zero, the Mythologies, the first America (1953-1960)

- ***Le Degré zéro de l'écriture*, 1953.** **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**
- Short essays for **Maurice Nadeau's revue *Les Lettres nouvelles*, 1954-1956**, under the running title **"Petites mythologies du mois."** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **Correction.** The gathering pass called them "bi-monthly." fr.wikipedia's title says *du mois*: **monthly**. The gathering pass also contradicted itself, since its own CNRS geography entry said "written monthly."
- ***Mythologies*, 1957**, fifty-three essays closing with "Le Mythe, aujourd'hui." **[A: en.wikipedia "Mythologies (book)"]**
- **Correction.** The gathering pass gave the publisher as **Les Lettres nouvelles**. That is wrong, and the error traces to the infobox on en.wikipedia's *Mythologies (book)* article. *Mythologies* was published by **Éditions du Seuil, Paris** (BnF catalogue; consistent with fr.wikipedia, which identifies *Les Lettres nouvelles* as Nadeau's **revue**, not a book house).
- English translation history: **Annette Lavers, 1972** (Jonathan Cape / Hill & Wang), **abridged**, dropping twenty-five of the fifty-three essays and keeping "Myth Today" whole; a 2009 Vintage UK edition restores some pieces including "Astrology"; the complete English *Mythologies* appears only in **2012**, newly translated by **Richard Howard** with Lavers' "Myth Today" retained. **[A: en.wikipedia "Mythologies (book)"]**

### The Middlebury / New York date, adjudicated

The gathering pass left this open twice, in the chronology lens and again in the geography lens. It is resolvable and has been resolved.

- en.wikipedia: **Middlebury 1957**, meeting Richard Howard **1957**, citing Howard's own memoir, "Remembering Roland Barthes," *The Nation*, 20 November 1982: *"Mutual friends brought us together in 1957. He came to my door in the summer of that year, disconcerted by his classes at Middlebury (teaching students unaccustomed to a visitor with no English to speak of) and bearing, by way of introduction, a fresh-printed copy of Mythologies."*
- fr.wikipedia: **Middlebury 1958**, "puis à New York l'année suivante," i.e. New York 1959.
- **Adjudication:** Howard's first-hand account is a primary source and dates the encounter to summer 1957; *Mythologies* was published in 1957, which corroborates "fresh-printed." fr.wikipedia's 1959 is derived arithmetically from its own 1958. **The journey file takes 1957 and records fr.wikipedia's dissent in date_confidence rather than deleting it.**
- **Correction of a second kind:** the gathering pass wrote that Barthes "befriends Richard Howard" *at Middlebury*. Howard's account puts the meeting **in New York City**, at Howard's own door. The Vermont classes are what Barthes was complaining about when he arrived.
- Coordinate corrected: Middlebury College is **44.00889, -73.17722**, about 820 m to 1.06 km from the two pins the gathering pass supplied.

## 7. The semiologist (1962-1970)

- **Directeur d'études at the EPHE from 1962.** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **Contradiction, resolved against the gathering pass.** The pool's geography lens ran the EPHE posting "1962-1980" as "one seminar room under changing signage," calling the institutional sequence unresolved. fr.wikipedia's lead resolves it plainly: *"Directeur d'études à l'École pratique des hautes études jusqu'en 1975, puis à l'École des hautes études en sciences sociales (EHESS) et professeur au Collège de France."* The sequence is **EPHE 1962-1975, EHESS 1975-1977, Collège de France 1977-1980.** Running the EPHE to his death is wrong.
- ***Sur Racine*, 1963**, which provokes the quarrel with the Sorbonne traditionalist **Raymond Picard**. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- ***Éléments de sémiologie*: 1964 and 1965 are both right.** The essay first appeared in the journal *Communications* no. 4 in **1964**; the Seuil volume followed in **1965**. en.wikipedia dates the first, fr.wikipedia the second. **The gathering pass logged this as an unresolved discrepancy; it is not a discrepancy, it is two objects.** **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**
- **Johns Hopkins, Baltimore, 18-21 October 1966**, the colloquium *The Languages of Criticism and the Sciences of Man*, organised by **René Girard** and **Richard A. Macksey** for the new Humanities Center with Ford Foundation funding. Participants include Barthes, Paul de Man, Jean Hyppolite and Jacques Lacan; Derrida delivers "Structure, Sign, and Play"; Foucault is absent. **[A: en.wikipedia "Roland Barthes"; en.wikipedia "Structure, Sign, and Play"]** The gathering pass tagged this **[R]** on the grounds of exhausted search quota. **It is [A] and has been upgraded.** Coordinate 39.3299, -76.6205 matches Wikipedia's Homewood campus point to about 110 m and is kept.
- **Tokyo, May 1966**: first journey to Japan, at fifty, invited by **Maurice Pinguet** (1929-1991), director of the Institut franco-japonais, to lead a seminar on the structural analysis of narrative. *L'Empire des signes* follows in 1970. **[A: fr.wikipedia "L'Empire des signes"]** Coordinate corrected from 35.7101, 139.7183 to **35.6984, 139.7398** (15 Ichigaya-Funagawara-machi, Shinjuku; OSM). The pool's pin matched neither the Institut franco-japonais nor the separate **Maison franco-japonaise** (35.6441, 139.7157), which is a different institution and must not be conflated with it.
- **"The Death of the Author," 1967.** **Correction: the place is wrong in the gathering pass.** fr.wikipedia: the essay appeared **first in English, in *Aspen Magazine* no. 5/6, 1967**, and only in 1968 in French, in no. 5 of the Marseille review *Mantéia*. The 1967 first publication was an American magazine, not Paris. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** The journey file places the 1967 stop in New York, where *Aspen* was published, and names the 1968 Marseille printing in the campa.
- ***Système de la mode*, 1967**. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **Rabat, Morocco, 1969-1970.** **[A: fr.wikipedia]** The chronology matters: this is not the young lecturer of the Bucharest and Alexandria years but the author of *Mythologies* and *Système de la mode*, in his fifties. **Gap:** no reachable source names the institution or address in Rabat.
- ***S/Z* and *L'Empire des signes*, 1970.** **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**

## 9. The mother, the chair, the van (1976-1980)

- **Collège de France.** **Internal contradiction in the gathering pass, corrected.** One cannot be elected "in 1977" and deliver the inaugural lecture "that January." The **election is 1976**; the inaugural lecture, published as *Leçon*, is delivered **7 January 1977**; fr.wikipedia's bibliography catalogues the first course, *Comment vivre ensemble*, as "cours et séminaires au Collège de France 1976-1977"; the chair itself runs **1977-1980**. **[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** Coordinate 48.8482, 2.3459 is about 140 m off; the journey file uses **48.84917, 2.34556**.
- ***Fragments d'un discours amoureux*, 1977**, an unexpected popular success. **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**
- **Henriette Barthes dies 25 October 1977**, aged 85. **[A: fr.wikipedia for the date; en.wikipedia corroborates year and age]** *Journal de deuil* begins the following day.
- ***Le Roland Barthes sans peine*, 1978**, a satirical pastiche. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** In 1979 he plays **William Makepeace Thackeray** in *Les Soeurs Brontë*. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- ***La Chambre claire*, 1980**, published by Seuil two months before his death; Richard Howard's English *Camera Lucida* follows from Hill and Wang in 1981. **[A: en.wikipedia "Camera Lucida (book)"]**

### The accident: three errors in the pool

- **Attested:** struck by the van of a laundry firm (*camionnette d'une entreprise de blanchissage*) on the **rue des Écoles, Paris, 25 February 1980**. **[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia corroborates van and date]** The pin 48.8478, 2.3496 falls on rue des Écoles (OSM extent 48.8477-48.8496, 2.3448-2.3520) and is kept.
- **Direction reversed.** fr.wikipedia says he was struck *"alors qu'il se rend au Collège de France"*: walking **to** the Collège. The gathering pass's chronology lens wrote "while walking home... en route to the College de France," which is self-contradictory, and its geography lens wrote "returning on foot." Both are struck.
- **Sourcing overstated.** The **lunch with François Mitterrand** is **not** in fr.wikipedia's article on Barthes. It appears on fr.wikipedia in the article on **Laurent Binet's novel**. Attributing it to "fr.wikipedia, Roland Barthes" is a false citation. The journey file carries the lunch as **[R]** and says where the detail actually lives.
- **Unsourced embellishment struck.** "The driver, by most accounts, is going slowly, and the injuries do not at first appear fatal" appears in no cited source.
- **Dies 26 March 1980 at the Pitié-Salpêtrière**, of pulmonary complications, aged 64. **[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** The pool gave three different pins for the hospital (48.8388/2.3629; 48.8386/2.3655; 48.8391/2.3656). The journey file inherits the corpus's canonical Salpêtrière pin from `freud.journey.json`, **48.8382, 2.3661**, byte-identical, so that the two travellers land on the same point.

## 11. Relationships: one correction worth its own heading

The gathering pass wrote, dated 1970, that Barthes "maintained close intellectual relationships with figures including Susan Sontag and the writer Hervé Guibert."

- **The Sontag half is sound.** **[A: fr.wikipedia: "son amie Susan Sontag diffusera ses idées dans le monde intellectuel américain"]**, from his 1967 return to the United States.
- **The Guibert half fails on three counts.** Guibert was born in **1955** and would have been fifteen in 1970. fr.wikipedia places the acquaintance in **1977** ("C'est l'époque où il fait la connaissance d'Hervé Guibert"). And fr.wikipedia describes the relationship as *"exclusivement épistolaire"*, ending when Barthes made a preface conditional on Guibert sleeping with him. "Close intellectual relationship" misrepresents what the source says. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** The journey file does not carry a Guibert stop; the episode is recorded here.

**Quotation integrity**

Four of the thirteen quotations in the pool were altered in transmission. All are restored to source text in the journey file:

1. *Mourning Diary*, the icon passage: the pool dropped the second "where" and replaced an em dash with a comma. Restored: "In the corner of my room where she had been bedridden, where she had died and where I now sleep, in the wall where her headboard had stood against I hung an icon—not out of faith."
2. "From Work to Text": the pool deleted "(and not merely an acceptable)" from inside the final phrase without marking the cut. The full sentence is restored.
3. *The Pleasure of the Text*: the pool removed the parenthetical "(the language produced and spread under the protection of power)", which is the gloss that defines "encratic." Restored.
4. *A Lover's Discourse*, "Talking": the pool spliced the first sentence to the third with no ellipsis. The intervening sentence, "It is as if I had words instead of fingers, or fingers at the tip of my words," is restored.
