# Rashi of Troyes (Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki, 1040-1105): research report
*2026-08-13. Compiled from a verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotation, interlock, afterlife lenses), each entry re-checked against the source it names. Slug: `rashi`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Disagreements between reachable sources are printed and left standing.

**Structural warning.** The dossier's own lenses contradict each other on three points of substance: the city of Rashi's first teacher, the coordinates of Ramerupt, and the folio in Makkot where the commentary stops. Two of the three trace to a split between en.wikipedia 'Rashi' and the Encyclopaedia Judaica tradition (via Jewish Virtual Library and fr.wikipedia 'Rachi').

---

## 1. Birth and childhood at Troyes (c.1040 - c.1057)

- Born at **Troyes**, Champagne, **1040 [A: Wikipedia 'Rashi'; JVL 'Rashi'; fr.wikipedia 'Rachi']**. The traditional day, **22 February**, is a later calendrical reconstruction on no eleventh-century record **[R]**.
- Only child of **Yitzhak (Isaac), a winemaker [R: traditional, carried by Wikipedia 'Rashi']**.
- Mother is the **sister of the paytan Simon bar Isaac** of Mainz **[R: fr.wikipedia 'Rachi']**. **Chronological problem:** the chronology lens dated Simeon's Mainz rabbinate to "the 1040s," which is impossible; he lived c.950 - c.1020, dying two decades before Rashi's birth. Retained as **[R]**, re-dated c.1000-1020, and the "disciple of Rabbeinu Gershom" phrasing dropped: the article on Simeon calls him Gershom's **contemporary**, not his pupil.
- Brought to begin Torah on **Shavuot**, c.1045 **[R: traditional biography]**.
- Trained in trades alongside study: coinage standards, banking, soldering, weaving **[R: JVL, late biographical tradition]**. Corroborated indirectly by the commentaries, which handle vessels, tools and looms with working familiarity, and by the **laaz** glosses, Old French in Hebrew characters **[A: the glosses are in the text]**.
- Marries at about **seventeen**, c.1057. **His wife's name is preserved nowhere [A as a gap]**. That she stayed at Troyes is inferred from the attested thrice-yearly festival returns **[R: JVL]**.

**Birth legends, both [R], neither with medieval attestation.** (1) **The jewel cast into the sea**: Yitzhak refuses to sell a gem to buyers who would set it in an idol and drops it overboard; a promise follows of a son who will enlighten Israel. *Correction:* the geography lens said this is "not in Encyclopaedia Judaica or JVL," implying absence from mainstream reference works; **en.wikipedia 'Rashi' recounts it in full**, and its version has **no angel**, only a heavenly voice. (2) **The wall that opened at Worms**: his pregnant mother, threatened by two carts in a narrow lane, is sheltered when the wall recesses **[R: Wikipedia 'Rashi']**. The geographic incoherence stands: the attested birthplace is Troyes.

## 2. The Rhineland academies (c.1057 - c.1070)

**Majority reading, followed in the journey file:** c.1057-1064 at **Mainz** under **Yaakov ben Yakar**, pupil of Rabbeinu Gershom; **Yaakov dies 1064 [A: en.wikipedia 'Jacob ben Yakar', 990-1064]** and is buried in the Judensand **[A: de.wikipedia]**; Rashi stays at **Mainz** under **Isaac ben Judah**; then c.1065-1070 at **Worms** under **Isaac ben Eliezer HaLevi**, the final stage **[A: JVL, following Encyclopaedia Judaica, "Jacob b. Yakar and Isaac b. Judah at Mainz, and Isaac b. Eleazar ha-Levi at Worms"; fr.wikipedia 'Rachi']**.

**Dissent:** en.wikipedia 'Rashi' puts Yaakov ben Yakar's yeshiva at **Worms** and has Rashi reach Mainz second. The chronology lens followed English Wikipedia, the geography lens the Judaica tradition, so the dossier contradicted itself. **Not resolved.** Weight for Mainz: Yaakov was Gershom's pupil there and is buried there. *Sub-correction:* the geography lens said **JVL** places him at Worms. It does not; JVL says Mainz. English Wikipedia dissents alone.

Anchors of this phase:
- **Judensand, Mainz [A: de.wikipedia; UNESCO ShUM inscription 2021]**: oldest Ashkenazi cemetery in Europe, earliest stone 1049, holding Yaakov ben Yakar's grave and a Gershom memorial.
- **Worms synagogue, built 1034 [A: en.wikipedia 'Worms Synagogue']**. *Correction:* "the oldest such structure in Germany" misleads on fabric. The 1034 building was destroyed in 1096, rebuilt 1175, destroyed in 1938; the standing structure is a **1961 reconstruction**, and nothing Rashi stood in survives. The **Raschi-Kapelle** is later commemorative work; the circulating **1624** date is **not** in the cited article **[gap]**.
- **"Rashi's chair," a stone seat in the Worms synagogue [R: local heritage tradition, no medieval attestation]**.
- **The Rhine-Champagne circuit**: three returns a year, Days of Awe, Passover, Shavuot, and the standard judgment that he likely never went farther than "from the Seine to the Rhine" **[A: JVL]**. The route coordinates (49.0 / 6.5) are an approximate midpoint, not a landmark, flagged in that stop's `date_confidence`.

## 3. Return to Troyes: court, school, vineyard (c.1065 - c.1090)

- Returns at about **twenty-five**, c.1065 **[A: JVL]**; fr.wikipedia says only "before the age of 30" **[contradiction of precision, minor]**.
- **Failed claim.** "His mother dies shortly afterward" was attributed by the geography lens to JVL and fr.wikipedia. **Neither says it.** Dropped rather than carried as [R] on no authority.
- Joins the **beth din of Troyes [R]**. *Source correction:* JVL mentions neither Zerach ben Abraham nor the Troyes court. The Zerach material is from **en.wikipedia 'Rashi'**, which attests **only the succession** ("Upon the death of the head of the Bet din, Zerach ben Abraham, Rashi assumed the court's leadership"), not prior membership or the c.1065-1070 dating. Membership **[R]**; succession **[A]**.
- Founds a **yeshiva at Troyes**, c.1070, drawing students from France and the Rhineland and making Champagne a rival to Mainz and Worms **[A: Wikipedia; JVL]**.
- The Jewish quarter, later **la Brosse aux Juifs**, sits where rue Boucherat meets the road to Montier-la-Celle, with a cemetery on ground now under the town's media library **[A: fr.wikipedia 'Synagogue de Troyes']**. **The school's address is not recorded [gap]**; the quarter coordinates (48.2965 / 4.0715) are an estimate, the source giving street names, not a survey.
- **Vintner [R]**: tradition holds he lived from the vineyards, and fr.wikipedia says he "possessed vineyards." The supporting evidence is itself **[A]**: responsa apologizing for brevity during the grape harvest, and commentary vocabulary showing working knowledge of vat, press and cask. Rabbinic office was unsalaried in this period **[A: My Jewish Learning]**.
- **Comital protection**: the Counts of Champagne shielded the community, credited with the difference between Champagne's history and the Rhineland's **[A: fr.wikipedia 'Rachi']**. **No charter of privilege from his own lifetime is cited in any reachable source [gap]**.

## 4. The two commentaries

**Torah commentary [A].** Verse by verse across the Hebrew Bible, combining midrash with insistence on the plain sense. His method statement, from the introduction to the Song of Songs: "From one verse, emerge several explanations. [However] in conclusion, a verse does not depart from its literal sense and meaning."

*Citation correction:* that wording is the **Sefaria Community Translation of Rashi on Song of Songs 1:1:1**, not the Metsudah Five Megillot as the pool stated; the Metsudah text on Sefaria has **no content at all** for the introduction's segments. A second pooled quotation (Rabbi Akiva on Shir Hashirim) is verbatim Metsudah but sits **in the comment on the verse, 1:1:4**, not the introduction; it is not used.

Quotations used in the journey, all verbatim and Sefaria-verified: **Genesis 1:1** (why the Torah opens with Creation), **Genesis 33:4** ("His pity was aroused when he saw him prostrating himself so many times"), and **Deuteronomy 34:5** ("The Holy One, blessed be He, dictated this, and Moses wrote it in tears"), all **[A: Rosenbaum-Silbermann 1929-1934]**, plus **Song of Songs 1:1:1** **[A: Sefaria Community Translation]**.

*Two pooled quotation defects, neutralized:* the Deuteronomy 34:5 line "Is it possible that Moses died..." drops a word ("from here **and** onward"), and the Genesis 33:4 dispute text was a **paraphrase inside quotation marks**. Neither is used in a `quote` field; both are campa narration instead.

**Talmud commentary [A].** A running gloss on nearly the whole Babylonian Talmud, written to keep an unaided reader inside the text; unfinished at his death; still printed in the inner margin.

**Interlock of substance:** the objection raised against Maimonides's *Mishneh Torah* in Baghdad and Provence, that a reader might consult the ruling and never open the Talmud, is the exact inverse of Rashi's design goal **[A: maimonides.journey.json]**. This methodological relation is the only reason Maimonides is named in a campa.

## 5. The year 1096

- **Speyer, 3 May**: 12 killed, the rest sheltered by the bishop. **Worms, 18 May**: at least 800. **Mainz, 27 May**: at least 1,100 **[A: en.wikipedia 'Rhineland massacres']**.
- *Attribution correction:* the pool called these the work of "the People's Crusade," following loose usage in en.wikipedia 'Rashi'. The dedicated article attributes them chiefly to **Count Emicho's contingent** and distinguishes Peter the Hermit's band, which extorted rather than massacred. The journey says "armed bands" and names no leader.
- *Second correction:* the pool listed all three cities and then said "where Rashi had studied." **He never studied at Speyer**, and no source places him there. The journey keeps a Speyer stop but states the absence **[R]**.
- Among the Worms dead: the **three sons of Isaac ben Eliezer HaLevi**, his Worms teacher **[R: JVL]**.
- Rashi is at **Troyes** throughout; the massacres reach him as news **[R per JVL for Worms; A for his non-presence]**. He composes **selichot** for the dead **[A: Wikipedia; My Jewish Learning; JVL]**. **No individual poem is dated [gap]**.

## 6. Daughters, grandsons, and the unfinished page

- **Yocheved** marries **Meir ben Samuel** of Ramerupt, c.1078 **[A: Wikipedia 'Rashi'; JVL]**.
- **Miriam** marries **Judah ben Nathan**, c.1080 **[A: JVL]**, who writes early tosafot **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Rachel**, called Belle Assez, marries **Eliezer ben Shemiah**; the marriage ends in divorce **[A: JVL]**. **Nothing further recorded of her [gap]**.
- **He has no son [A]**. Transmission runs through the daughters and their husbands.
- Grandsons: **Rashbam**, **Rivam**, **Rabbeinu Tam**.
  - **Rashbam: c.1085 [A: en.wikipedia 'Rashbam'], "in the vicinity of Troyes," not specifically Ramerupt.** The chronology lens gave 1080 at Ramerupt on a family table in the Rashi article alone. **Contradiction flagged; the journey uses c.1085 and carries the dispute in `date_confidence`.**
  - **Rivam's 1090 rests on that same list, with no independent attestation [R]**; he gets no stop.
  - **Rabbeinu Tam, c.1100: confirmed [A]**, but the pool's "will found the Tosafist school" is **overstated**: he is its dominant figure, not its founder, the enterprise beginning with Rashi's sons-in-law and elder grandsons. The journey says "becomes the dominant figure."
- **Death, 29 Tammuz 4865 = 13 July 1105, Troyes, aged about 65 [A: Wikipedia 'Rashi']**.
- **The last page.** The commentary breaks off at **Makkot 19b [A: JVL, "died while writing the word 'pure' [tahor] in his commentary to Makkot, (19b)"; independently fixed at 19b by en.wikipedia 'Judah ben Nathan', "from 19b to the end"]**. *Correction:* the chronology lens put the break "at the discussion of the 613 commandments," which is at **Makkot 23b-24a, several folios later**; the geography lens had it right, so the lenses contradicted each other. The journey uses **19b and the word tahor**, tagging the mid-word death as embellishment on an attested break.
- **Judah ben Nathan completes Makkot after his death [A: JVL; en.wikipedia]**.

## 7. Afterlife

- **Burial at Troyes, 1105 [A]**. **Unresolved contradiction:** the site lost then rediscovered and monumented, versus a burial location simply unknown. Two reference works of comparable standing. **Not adjudicated; the journey stop states the split.**
- The rediscovery runs through **a Sorbonne professor's find of an old map** showing the cemetery under an open square **[R: en.wikipedia 'Rashi'; recovered local tradition, not a documented archival find]**.
- **Raymond Moretti monument [A]**: black and white globe pierced with Hebrew letters, base reading "Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki, Commentator and Guide." **Year not established in any reachable source [gap]**; the journey dates the stop 1990 with the uncertainty in `date_confidence`.
- **2005 plaque by Yisroel Meir Gabbai [A]**: "The place you are standing on is the cemetery of the town of Troyes."
- **First dated printed Hebrew book**: Rashi on the Torah, without the biblical text, by **Abraham ben Garton, Reggio di Calabria, 18 February 1475 [A]**. The qualifier matters: **first dated**, undated Hebrew incunabula being earlier. The geography lens dropped "dated" and was corrected.
- **Rashi script [A: en.wikipedia 'Rashi script']**: fifteenth-century Sephardic semi-cursive, taken up by Garton, the Soncinos and Bomberg to separate commentary from scripture. **He never wrote in it.**
- **Page layout.** The pool's "every edition of the Talmud since the first printings of the fifteenth century" is an **overstatement**: the layout comes from the Soncino tractates of the 1480s and was normalized by **Bomberg, 1520-1523**, fixing the foliation and inner-margin position still in use; many modern critical editions print the text without him. **Softened to "nearly every standard edition since Bomberg."**
- **More than 300 super-commentaries** written in later centuries **[R: My Jewish Learning]**; no list consulted.
- **Synagogue Rachi, 5 rue Brunneval [A: fr.wikipedia 'Synagogue de Troyes'; coordinates 48 deg 17' 47" N, 4 deg 04' 14" E confirmed exactly]**. *Date correction:* the pool's "rebuilt 1960/1966" is wrong. The French article gives **1960** (the diocese sold the community the presbytery of the Madeleine), enlargement **1986**, restoration begun **2011**, inauguration **4 September 2016**; **there is no 1966 event**. en.wikipedia 'Troyes' adds that the building replaces one destroyed in the Second World War, with Chief Rabbi Haim Korsia present in 2016 **[A]**.
- **Institut Universitaire Europeen Rachi, founded 1990 [A: fr.wikipedia 'Rachi']**; UNESCO-supported itinerant performance "Le tresor de Rashi," 2012 **[A: same]**, given no stop, having no fixed place.

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Mainz or Worms first?** Judaica tradition: Mainz (Yaakov ben Yakar), Mainz (Isaac ben Judah), then Worms (Isaac ha-Levi). en.wikipedia 'Rashi': Worms first, Mainz second. The journey follows the majority and prints the dissent in `date_confidence`.
2. **The grave.** Rediscovered and monumented, versus location unknown.
3. **Rashbam's birth.** c.1085 near Troyes versus 1080 at Ramerupt.
4. **Ramerupt's coordinates.** The chronology lens gave 48.5333 / 3.9333, open country some 26 km west near Marnay-sur-Seine; the geography lens gave 48.5328 / 4.3010, within about 1.5 km of the true 48.52 / 4.2931. **The journey uses 48.5328 / 4.3010.** A coordinate error repeated across three entries, not a source disagreement.
5. **Makkot 19b versus 23b-24a.** Resolved for 19b on two independent sources, recorded because the dossier was split.
6. **The uncle.** Simeon bar Isaac as maternal uncle is chronologically strained (died c.1020), and the "disciple of Gershom" label is contradicted by the article on Simeon.
7. **Return age.** "Around 25" (JVL) versus "before 30" (fr.wikipedia).

## Gaps stated as gaps

- **His wife's name**, nowhere preserved; **his mother**, beyond her brother's name, plus the failed claim of her death c.1065; **Rachel after the divorce**, nothing.
- **The address** of the yeshiva and of the house in the Brosse aux Juifs: the quarter is documented, the building is not.
- **The date of the Raschi-Kapelle** (the circulating 1624 is untraceable to the cited article) and **the year of the Moretti monument**.
- **Any comital charter of privilege** for the Jews of Troyes from his lifetime; **dating of individual selichot**.
- **Rivam**: birth year unattested outside one family list, nothing else recorded.

## Interlock discipline

The interlock lens supplied twenty-one candidates. **Nineteen were rejected as shared geography** (Bernard of Clairvaux at Mainz 1146, Richard the Lionheart through Speyer-Worms-Mainz, Trithemius at Mainz, Joan of Arc at Troyes 1429, the Council of Troyes 1129, the Bourlemonts in Champagne, Charlemagne's Isaac the Jew): each puts another person in a place Rashi also occupied, and none met, corresponded with, cited, or was influenced by him. **Two survive on relation:**

- **maimonides**, named in the Talmud-commentary campa: the *Mishneh Torah* objection is the methodological counterpart of Rashi's purpose **[A]**.
- **benjamin_de_tudela**, named in the final campa: the academies he records at Narbonne and Lunel sixty years after Rashi's death worked in a rabbinic culture shaped by the commentaries circulating out of Champagne **[R: the transmission is inference, not documented in Benjamin's text]**.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Troyes 48.2973 / 4.0744, Mainz 49.9929 / 8.2473, Speyer 49.3167 / 8.4333. From the geography and afterlife lenses: Judensand 50.0059 / 8.2498, Worms 49.6336 / 8.3664, Ramerupt 48.5328 / 4.3010, Reggio di Calabria 38.1113 / 15.6619, Brosse aux Juifs 48.2965 / 4.0715, Synagogue Rachi 48.2964 / 4.0706. The canonical Mainz pin was preferred for city-level stops over the lens's yeshiva coordinates (50.0009 / 8.2716); the Judensand keeps its own.

Two contrast pins offered by the lens, **Mecca 21.4225 / 39.8262** and **the Temple Mount 31.778 / 35.2354**, were **not used**: they were proposed as geography of absence, and a stop is a place a traveler went. The absence is stated in prose, in the road stop between the Rhine and the Seine.
