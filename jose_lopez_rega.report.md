# José López Rega (1916-1989): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, working file. Compiled 2026-08-09 from the verified research pool for slug `jose_lopez_rega`. Subject: Argentine police corporal, astrologer, Minister of Social Welfare 1973-1975, organizer of the Alianza Anticomunista Argentina, known as "El Brujo."*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or commonplace not traceable to a document. Where two sources disagree, both readings are printed and neither is adjudicated. Where nothing was reachable, the hole is stated as a hole.

**Method and its limits.** The evidentiary base is almost entirely encyclopedic (see Sources). No archival holding, no judicial file and no monograph was reachable in this run. For a subject whose central activity was clandestine by design, the distance between what is documented and what is repeated is unusually wide, and it is marked at every point below. The adversarial pass overturned several claims the gathering pass had accepted; those corrections are printed in place, with the error named.

---

## I. Birth and the police years, 1916-1962

Born in Buenos Aires, 1916, to Spanish immigrant parents, Juan López and Manuela Rega; the mother dies in or shortly after childbirth **[A: both encyclopedias]**. **Contradiction, unresolved:** en.wikipedia gives 17 November 1916, es.wikipedia 17 October 1916. The year is consistent, the month is not, and neither cites the civil register. The journey file carries 1916-10-17. **Gap:** the birth *barrio* is unconfirmed. Argentine press chronicle places him in Bajo Belgrano; that attribution appears in neither article and is not carried as fact.

Marries Josefa Flora Maseda Fontenla, 19 June 1943 **[A: es.wikipedia]**; daughter Norma Beatriz born 17 February 1945 **[A]**. Enters the Policía Federal Argentina shortly after, backed by comisario Filomeno Velazco **[A]**, and serves through Perón's first two presidencies **[A]**. In 1951 joins the guard at the Palacio Unzué, the presidential residence between avenida del Libertador, Austria, Agüero and Las Heras **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Palacio Unzué']**; it was demolished in 1958 and the Biblioteca Nacional stands on the ground, so the pin marks an absence. Sings on Radio Nacional in these years **[R: recorded without development]**, trivial except as the hinge of the next section.

Retires from the Policía Federal in 1962 at the rank of cabo primero **[A]**, one step above the bottom of the scale. In 1974 he is promoted from that rank to Comisario General, the top of the same scale, in a single act.

## II. The esoteric formation, 1950s-1965

Introduced by the journalist José María Villone into the house of "Doña Victoria" at Paso de los Libres, Corrientes, on the Brazilian border, where she led the rosicrucian circle Fraternidad Antigua **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Villone met him after hearing him sing on Radio Nacional in 1951. There he formed the syncretism that fed his books. **Correction:** the gathering pass carried a "Victoria Montero" and named no intermediary. That surname appears in no consulted source; es.wikipedia names only "Doña Victoria," and names Villone.

Publishes *Astrología Esotérica: secretos develados*, 1962 **[A]**, tying the zodiac to colors, plants and music. Es.wikipedia reports that the reviews it drew are the origin of the nickname *El Brujo* **[A as report; [R] as to the etymology, which no cited review corroborates]**. *Alfa y Omega: un mensaje para la humanidad*, 1963 **[A]**; *La Astroagenda*, 1965 **[A]**. *El Sabio Hindú* is dated **1972 in one line and 1977 in another within the same article [A that it exists; the date is a flat internal contradiction, not adjudicated]**. No translations and no re-editions after 1975 turn up **[R: absence in the consulted sources, not a positive finding]**.

Joins Propaganda Due, the Italian masonic lodge, February 1973, through Licio Gelli **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Placed here thematically; chronologically it belongs to section V.

## III. Isabel, and the road to Madrid, 1965-1966

The most heavily contested passage in the life. The sources do not converge.

**Reading 1 [A: es.wikipedia, 'Isabel Martínez de Perón'].** Isabel Martínez, in Argentina from October 1965 as Perón's personal delegate, meets López Rega at the Buenos Aires house of Lt. Col. Bernardo Alberte during that eight-month stay: *"Fue durante esa estadía en Argentina, que Martínez de Perón supuestamente conoció a José López Rega, en casa del teniente coronel Bernardo Alberte."* She then asks for a private meeting and offers him work as her secretary in Madrid. The corpus file `isabel_peron.journey.json` dates the introduction 1 May 1966.

**Reading 2 [R: es.wikipedia, 'José López Rega'].** First contact in 1965 through the esoteric Logia Anael, a route historians treat as unverified.

**Reading 3 [R: recurrent journalistic tradition].** López Rega, then a policeman, met Isabel Martínez while she danced in a Panama City cabaret, c. 1955. Unconfirmable in either article, and it contradicts Reading 1 as to place, date and circumstance.

**Correction.** The gathering pass placed the first meeting in Madrid. No consulted source puts the *first* contact there, and Madrid is dropped. **Not adjudicated:** 1965 versus 1966; Buenos Aires versus Panama; a lodge versus a serving officer of Perón's. The journey file carries the Alberte house at 1 May 1966 as the load-bearing stop and the Panama cabaret as a flagged legend at its own pin.

## IV. Puerta de Hierro, 1966-1973

Flies to Madrid beside Isabel, 9 July 1966, and is absorbed into the exiled Juan Perón's household at Puerta de Hierro **[A: corpus `isabel_peron.journey.json`; the period generally from both encyclopedias]**. Becomes Perón's private secretary and Isabel's secretary and bodyguard. En.wikipedia runs the period from 1965; es.wikipedia dates his relocation back to Argentina to 1971, closing the Madrid years earlier than the corpus does. **Not adjudicated.**

**Coordinate correction.** The gathering pass gave 40.4556, -3.7444 for Puerta de Hierro, which is the *gate monument*, not the Ciudad Residencial where Perón's villa stood some 1.5 to 2 km north-northwest. The Spanish article gives no coordinate for the urbanization and does not name the villa; the journey file inherits the corpus pin **40.462, -3.755**.

Eva Perón's embalmed body reaches the Madrid house, 3 September 1971 **[A: corpus `isabel_peron.journey.json`, corroborated by `eva_peron` and `juan_peron`; en.wikipedia, 'Eva Perón']**. He performs occult rites over the corpse and over Isabel, said to transfer Eva's spirit into the third wife **[R: es.wikipedia states the rituals without a primary citation; en.wikipedia repeats the transfer version]**.

**Correction, and it is a hard one.** The gathering pass and en.wikipedia date these rites to "the 1960s" or "c. 1966-1971," which is impossible: the body arrived in Madrid in 1971 and left in 1974, and López Rega relocated in 1973. The only possible window is **1971-1973**, and the journey file carries the window, not the decade.

## V. Return, ministry, and the Alliance, 1973-1975

Héctor Cámpora is elected 11 March 1973 and takes office 25 May 1973 **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Héctor José Cámpora', which gives 11 March for the elections and 25 May for the assumption]**. **Correction:** the gathering pass reported Cámpora *inaugurated* 11 March, which is the election date, and that error manufactured a phantom source conflict over the ministerial appointment. There is no conflict. Both encyclopedias give **25 May 1973** for the appointment as Ministro de Bienestar Social, held under Cámpora, Raúl Lastiri, Juan Perón and Isabel Perón in turn **[A]**.

Travels with Isabel to Beijing (8-18 May 1973) and Pyongyang (15 May), where Kim Il-sung receives the delegation **[A: es.wikipedia for the missions, corpus for the dates]**. Neither source records a word of what was said in either capital.

Masacre de Ezeiza, 20 June 1973 **[A: both encyclopedias]**; the homecoming aircraft carrying Perón, Isabel and López Rega is diverted to Morón air base **[A: corpus]**. That he personally organized the gunmen on the platform is **[R]**: both articles hedge it as an allegation, and so does the journey file. Perón-Perón ticket proclaimed at the Teatro Colón, 5 August 1973, elected 23 September with 61.85 percent **[A]**, López Rega having pushed hardest for Isabel's place on it **[A: corpus]**. The Peronist Superior Council declares a "state of war" against Marxist infiltrators, 1 October 1973 **[A: es.wikipedia]**.

Organizes the Alianza Anticomunista Argentina during 1973, out of the Ministry of Social Welfare and on its resources **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Triple A (Argentina)']**. En.wikipedia gives a founding date of **13 July 1973**; the Spanish article gives only a general 1973 organizing period and names no day. The precise date is uncorroborated and is not treated as established.

Ministry address: Avenida Hipólito Yrigoyen 370, Buenos Aires **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Ministerio de Bienestar Social (Argentina)']**. **Correction:** the gathering pass placed a weapons depot in a *microcine* on the second basement level, citing that article, which mentions neither cinema nor weapons. What *is* attested is the arsenal of 19 July 1975 (section VI); the microcine detail is dropped.

First signed Triple A attack: a bomb in senator Hipólito Solari Yrigoyen's vehicle, 21 November 1973, wounding him in the legs **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Alianza Anticomunista Argentina']**. The gathering pass framed this as an attack on his home; it was a car bombing, and his own article records **two** attempts, 1973 and 1975. Attack on Antonio Deleroni and Nélida Arana at San Miguel station, 27 November 1973 **[A]**. Travels to Libya for the Argentine government, 1974 **[A: mentioned with no agenda or interlocutors, a documented trip with no documented content]**. Promoted directly to Comisario General of the Policía Federal, 3 May 1974, skipping every intermediate rank **[A]**.

Father Carlos Mugica is shot dead outside the church of San Francisco Solano, Villa Luro, 11 May 1974, by a Triple A commando **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Triple A (Argentina)' and 'Carlos Mugica'; Rodolfo Eduardo Almirón named as the shooter]**. The gathering pass declared the church address undocumented; es.wikipedia gives it as calle Zelada 4771. The journey file uses the church coordinate, -34.6395, -58.5015, not the barrio centroid.

Juan Perón dies 1 July 1974; Isabel assumes the presidency; López Rega becomes the dominant figure in her government **[A: both encyclopedias]**. **Coordinate correction:** the gathering pass gave -34.4989, -58.6733 for the Quinta de Olivos, some 19 km west of it; the journey file uses the corpus pin **-34.5108, -58.4869**.

Alberto Villar takes operational command and forms "Los Centuriones," roughly 100 police officers **[A]**. In a three-month span, roughly **220 attacks, about 60 dead, 44 seriously injured [A]**. Regional arms: the Concentración Nacional Universitaria at Mar del Plata, 1974-1975, and the Comando Libertadores de América in Córdoba, with police participation **[A]**. **Total attributed dead:** **700 to 1,100** in one article and **700 to 1,500** in another. Neither reconciles the discrepancy and neither does this file.

Publishes *El idioma de la verdad*, 1975, an autobiographical defense **[A: es.wikipedia, which quotes the dedication and several passages]**. **Correction:** the gathering pass framed the book as written "from prison-adjacent exile pressure." That is invented: in 1975 he was a sitting minister until 11 July and an ambassador after, and was first detained in 1986.

## VI. The fall and the ten years' flight, 1975-1986

Resigns as Minister of Social Welfare, 11 July 1975, amid popular rejection of the Rodrigo economic plan (the Rodrigazo), and is sent to Spain as itinerant ambassador **[A: both encyclopedias]**. A weapons arsenal is discovered at the ministry, 19 July 1975 **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Alianza Anticomunista Argentina']**.

**Correction, causality inverted.** The gathering pass called the arsenal "the event that precipitates his political fall." It postdates the resignation by eight days and confirmed after the fact what the ministry had been. **Also corrected:** the reported ambassadorial term of 11 May to 18 June 1975 appears in no source. Es.wikipedia runs the posting **until 18 June 1976**, the day the junta requested his extradition; the timeline is continuous, not conflicting.

Resides near Jerez de la Frontera **[R: es.wikipedia, no address given]**; formally accredited at Madrid until June 1976 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. After the coup of 24 March 1976 and the extradition request of 18 June, flees to Switzerland and settles near Geneva **[A: es.wikipedia]**; en.wikipedia gives a broad 1975-1982 window between Switzerland and Italy without dating the moves. Located in Rome, December 1976, by AFP, protected by Italian freemasonry and assisted by Licio Gelli **[A]**. Flees to the Bahamas after an appeals court overturns an earlier absolution, 1983, alternating thereafter with Miami **[A: es.wikipedia supplies the legal trigger; en.wikipedia gives a looser 1982-1986 window and none]**, until a photographer finds and publishes him **[A]**.

13 March 1986, Miami International Airport **[A as to date and place, both encyclopedias]**. **Contradiction, unresolved, and not a small one:** es.wikipedia describes a **voluntary surrender** on a warrant issued by judge Samuel Smargon on 27 February, quoting him, *"Mucho gusto. Soy López Rega y vengo a entregarme."* En.wikipedia describes an **FBI arrest during an attempt to renew a passport**. The gathering pass wrote "El FBI lo arresta" while carrying the surrender quotation in the same entry, which is incoherent. The journey file prints both and prefers neither.

A search of his Madrid home is reported to have turned up masonic regalia and twelve volumes of Alice Bailey **[R]**. The press quotation is dated **1978** (*The Times of the Americas*, in en.wikipedia) and the search **1979** in the same article. Carried as a press report, not as an inventory.

## VII. Detention, death, afterlife, 1986-2016

Extradited to Argentina, 1986, on charges including asociación ilícita, secuestro, homicidio and financial crimes **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Held in preventive detention until his death, **without a completed trial and without a verdict [A: both encyclopedias]**. **Correction:** the gathering pass had him "awaiting the sentence in his trial for the murders of Rodolfo Ortega Peña and others." Both articles state there was no completed trial; the Ortega Peña specificity is dropped.

Dies 9 June 1989 in Buenos Aires, aged 72, of complications of diabetes mellitus, unconvicted **[A: both encyclopedias]**. **Gap:** neither the prison unit nor the facility in which he died is named in any consulted source; the journey file pins both to the city coordinate and says so.

Cremated at the Cementerio de la Chacarita by his own wish; ashes scattered in the Río de la Plata **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The gathering pass carried **two different coordinates for the same cremation**, neither matching the source; the journey file uses **-34.5908, -58.4597**, the pin the corpus already carries in `emin_arslan.journey.json`. The scattering point is **[R]**: the fact is attested, the coordinate is an approximation and is labeled as one.

2006: federal judge Norberto Oyarbide rules the Triple A crimes imprescriptible crimes against humanity; Rodolfo Almirón is captured at Torrente, Valencia, 23 December 2006; four civilians and one police officer are convicted in February 2016 **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Alianza Anticomunista Argentina']**. López Rega had been dead seventeen years when the acts he organized were declared unpardonable by time.

Three items carried as **[R]**: the era's violence in the 2009 film *El secreto de sus ojos* and the parody on *Peter Capusotto y sus videos*, with no documentary, exhibit or monument to him or to the Alliance's victims found in the consulted sources; the legend that the three A's encode three magnetic vertices of a triangle of human moral evolution, which en.wikipedia presents as lore, not as organizational rationale; and the "Rasputin" comparison, traceable to no named speaker and not used in the journey file.

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. Birth date: 17 October 1916 (es) versus 17 November 1916 (en).
2. First meeting with Isabel: Buenos Aires 1966 at Alberte's house (es) versus Logia Anael 1965 (es) versus the Panama cabaret of press tradition, c. 1955.
3. End of the Madrid period: en.wikipedia relocates him in 1971; the corpus and the ministerial appointment put the working return at 1973.
4. Triple A founding: 13 July 1973 (en) versus an undated 1973 period (es).
5. Death toll: 700-1,100 versus 700-1,500.
6. Miami 1986: voluntary surrender (es) versus FBI arrest at a passport renewal (en).
7. *El Sabio Hindú*: 1972 versus 1977, within one article.
8. The Alice Bailey search: quotation dated 1978, search dated 1979, same article.
9. Whether he directed the Ezeiza shooters: alleged in both, asserted by neither.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- Birth *barrio* unconfirmed; the Bajo Belgrano attribution is press chronicle only.
- No judicial file, no CONADEP or trial record, no monograph consulted. Everything rests on encyclopedic secondary text.
- The prison unit of the 1986-1989 detention and the facility of his death are unnamed everywhere consulted.
- The address of the Madrid villa is not given: es.wikipedia says only that Perón kept his villa in that urbanization.
- The Libya trip of 1974 has a destination and no content.
- No consulted source documents any translation or re-edition of his books. That is an absence in the search, not a positive negative.
