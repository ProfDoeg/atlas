# Manuela Sáenz (1797-1856): research report

*2026-08-12. Quito, Lima, Bogotá, Paita. Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys from a
verified research pool of five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock,
afterlife). Every pool item was re-checked and, where wrong, corrected in place; this
report carries the corrections forward, not the original claims.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or
contested. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Every doubt below is discharged
in the journey file into a `date_confidence` string, never into the narrative prose: that
is what the atlas register ("national mythology: the canon is true") requires, and this
report is the instrument that makes it honest.

---

## 1. Quito, birth and convent (1797-1817)

**Birth. [A: EN and ES Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Quito, 27 December 1797,
illegitimate daughter of Simón Sáenz de Vergara y Yedra, a Spanish merchant, and María
Joaquina Aizpuru (Aispuro).

**The mother's death: a live contradiction, not resolved.** ES Wikipedia says the infant
was sent to the hacienda Cataguango and that her mother "murió de fiebre puerperal,"
giving no year, implying death within weeks. Encyclopedia.com dates María Joaquina
Aizpuru's death to **April 1822**, the hinge of the standard account of Manuela's return
to Quito over the Cataguango inheritance. **Both cannot be true.** The pool asserted each
in turn without noticing. **[R]** on both sides; the journey file keeps the 1822
inheritance journey, which has the better-attested consequence, and flags the
puerperal-fever version in `date_confidence`.

**Convent. [A: EN and ES Wikipedia]** The **Monasterio de Santa Catalina de Siena**,
Dominican. **[R]** An earlier Conceptas stage, the date 1805, and the curriculum list
(embroidery, confectionery, French, English) are in the pool but in neither encyclopedic
source; the pool's **[A]** on that curriculum is withdrawn.

**Leaving. [R: Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com, contradictory]** Around age seventeen
(c. 1814), after an affair with a military officer, named in one account as Fausto
D'Elhuyar. **Sources disagree on whether she fled or was expelled.**

**Marriage to James Thorne. [A on the fact, [R] on the place]** She marries the English
physician-merchant, roughly twice her age, in **1817**. The pool put the ceremony at
Quito's Catedral Metropolitana / El Sagrario on 27 July 1817 and tagged it **[A]**; that
tag is withdrawn. **Neither EN nor ES Wikipedia names any city or church**, ES gives only
the year, and the pool item itself conceded that "some biographers place the wedding in
Lima instead." **A gap, stated as a gap.**

## 2. Lima, the conspiracy, the Order of the Sun (1819-1822)

**Arrival. [R: Murray (2008), via pool]** She and Thorne arrive by ship at **Callao**,
c. 1819.

**Conspiracy. [A: EN Wikipedia]** She is drawn into the conspiracy against Viceroy
**José de la Serna** and works to induce colonial troops, the **Numancia regiment**
named, to defect.

**Order of the Sun, date corrected. [R on the year]** San Martín invests her as
*caballeresa del Sol*. The pool dated this **1821** while its own source note read
"Encyclopedia.com (dated 1822)": the field contradicted its citation. The **Orden El Sol
del Perú was created by decree of 8 October 1821**, making 1821 the earliest possible
year; ES Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com both date the investiture to **1822**. **No source
gives a day.** The geography lens repeated the 1821 error and is corrected identically.

---

## 3. Quito 1822: the inheritance, the crown, the Liberator

**Return. [A: Encyclopedia.com]** 1822, over the Cataguango inheritance. (See section 1.)

**Pichincha. [R: Rumazo González (1944)]** She is in Quito for the **Battle of Pichincha,
24 May 1822**. **Her participation in the fighting is tradition, not documented fact.**
The pool's chronology item dated a combined Pichincha-Junín-Ayacucho presence to **1823**,
impossible for Pichincha, and pinned it at -11.15, -75.99, a null point matching none of
the three battlefields. Discarded and replaced by separate, correctly located stops.

**The meeting, 16 June 1822. [A on the meeting, [R] on the venue]** Bolívar enters Quito;
she throws a laurel crown from a balcony that strikes him; that night they dance at the
victory ball. Encyclopedia.com dates it precisely and places the ball at **the governor's
palace**; the geography lens placed it at the **Casa de los Larrea**, tagged **[A]**,
while admitting "the exact house is traditionally identified but not certainly
documented." **The attributions conflict and the tag was self-contradictory.** The journey
file pins the canonical Quito anchor and names no house.

**Leaving Thorne. [A: EN Wikipedia]** 1822. She becomes companion, archivist, and
eventually an officer attached to Bolívar's staff.

**Guayaquil, July 1822. [R]** Bolívar and San Martín confer alone on the quay, 26-27 July,
five to six weeks after the Quito meeting. **Her presence is plausible and undocumented**;
neither bolivar.journey.json nor san_martin.journey.json states it.

## 4. The campaigns (1823-1826)

**Lima, 1823. [A: Murray]** She rejoins Bolívar, who lands 1 September 1823 to take
command, and works as archivist of his papers. bolivar.journey.json states plainly that
"Manuela follows him south."

**Trujillo, 1824. [A: Murray; Rumazo González]** Campaign headquarters before the northern
sierra offensive.

**Junín, 6 August 1824. [R]** The cavalry action fought without a shot. **Her presence is
asserted in patriotic biography and absent from the contemporary dispatches** (contrast
drawn by Murray).

**Ayacucho, 9 December 1824. [A] on the battle, [R] on her.** Sucre destroys the royal
army of the Andes on the Pampa de Quinua. Biographers credit her with field hospitals and
supplies; undocumented. **Bolívar was not present**, having handed Sucre command on 7
October 1824, a detail the pool got right and which is often missed. **Coordinate
correction:** the pool gave both -13.1588, -74.2239 (the city of Huamanga) and -13.6608,
-74.1428 (some 68 km south of anything relevant); sucre.journey.json already carries the
battlefield at -13.0431, -74.1379, inherited byte-identical.

**The colonelcy: sources conflict on the year. [A on the rank]** The chronology lens gives
**December 1824**, Colonel of the Colombian army, on ES Wikipedia's authority. The
geography lens gives **1825** and inserts an intermediate rank, **Sub-Lieutenant of
Hussars**, that neither Wikipedia mentions, conceding that "the exact date and issuing
authority vary between sources." December 1824 is adopted, the disagreement flagged, the
Hussars stage treated as **[R]**.

**The letter from Juana Azurduy. [A: juana_azurduy.journey.json, sourced to
ideasfem.wordpress.com and EN Wikipedia]** From the recovered hacienda of **Cullco**, 15
December 1825, Juana Azurduy, newly confirmed colonel, writes to Manuela Sáenz, colonel:
gratitude for the rank, discomfort at royalist officers now serving beside the victors,
grief for the dead who did not live to be recognized. **The strongest documented interlock
in the atlas that does not run through Bolívar.**

**La Magdalena, 1826. [R: Murray]** She lives with Bolívar at the palace outside Lima. EN
Wikipedia gives the periods as the first months of the year and again February to
September, which do not obviously reconcile; not adjudicated.

## 5. Lima turns (1827)

**The Third Division mutiny. [R]** 25 January 1827. Murray has her confronting the
mutineers in Bolívar's absence; EN Wikipedia has her imprisoned in a convent for political
activity on his behalf. Both **[R]**.

**Arrest and expulsion. [A: EN Wikipedia; Murray]** Held by the Peruvian government in
February 1827 amid the breakdown between Peru and Bolívar's Colombia; **which convent held
her is not agreed**; released in March, expelled in April. She travels north toward
Bogotá, **[R]** by way of Guayaquil and **[R]** through Quito, both customary waypoints
rather than documented stages.

## 6. Bogotá (1827-1834)

**The masked-ball plot: probable conflation, not resolved. [R]** Encyclopedia.com mentions
her learning of a plot against Bolívar at a masked ball, and the pool dated a distinct
episode to **August 1828**. The ES Wikipedia article on the Conspiración Septembrina
contains **no such August 1828 plot**. The economical reading is that this garbles the
notorious **party of 10 August 1828 at which Sáenz staged a mock execution of Santander in
effigy**, a scandal rather than a foiled plot. **Not presented as a separate attempt on
Bolívar's life.**

**La Noche Septembrina, 25 September 1828. [A: EN and ES Wikipedia; Murray; Rumazo
González; O'Leary via bolivar.journey.json]** Conspirators break into the Palacio de San
Carlos. She wakes Bolívar, arms him, convinces him to go out the window ("lo convenció de
que escapara por la ventana"), confronts the conspirators, takes a blow to the forehead.
**Wording correction:** the pool's "pushes Bolívar out a window" overstates the sources;
he jumped, on her urging. **Coordinate correction:** the pool gave the generic Bogotá
point 4.711, -74.0721, some 12 km north of the scene; bolivar.journey.json carries the
palace at 4.5964, -74.0741, inherited byte-identical.

**Bolívar's resignation, date corrected. [A]** The pool gave January 1830 for the final
resignation. On **20 January 1830** he addressed the Congreso Admirable and offered to
step down; he **resigned definitively on 8 May 1830**, the congress having accepted in
late April, Domingo Caycedo interim president. Both dates are named in `date_confidence`.

**The public letter. [A: Encyclopedia.com]** 23 June 1830, to a Bogotá newspaper,
defending her loyalty and conduct. **The text was not reachable in this pool.**

**Bolívar's death. [A: EN Wikipedia]** 17 December 1830, Santa Marta, tuberculosis, at the
Quinta de San Pedro Alejandrino. **The pool asserted a source conflict between 10 and 17
December: that conflict is spurious and is deleted.** 10 December is the day he dictated
his will and issued his last proclamation.

**Who moved against her: anachronism corrected. [A]** The pool had "the Santander
government" acting immediately after Bolívar's December 1830 death. **Santander was in
European exile in 1830**, returning October 1832 and taking the presidency in 1832; the
government of the moment was Urdaneta's. Santander is correct for the **1834 expulsion**.

**Expulsion. [A, year contested]** Encyclopedia.com dates the expulsion from New Granada
to **1834**, EN Wikipedia's chronology implies **1835**. **Flagged, not resolved.** **[R]**
She leaves by way of Cartagena; **[A: Murray]** exile follows in **Kingston, Jamaica**.

## 7. Paita (1835-1856)

**Turned back. [A: EN Wikipedia; Murray]** 1835, attempting to return to Ecuador via
Guayaquil, her passport is revoked by President **Vicente Rocafuerte**.

**Paita. [A]** Barred from Ecuador and Colombia, she settles in the northern Peruvian port
for the rest of her life, selling tobacco and sweets, translating, and writing letters for
English-speaking whalers and illiterate neighbours.

**Thorne's murder, correction. [A]** 1847, murdered at his hacienda in **Pativilca**. The
pool said she was denied her share of "his roughly 8,000-peso estate." **The 8,000 pesos
was her dowry**: ES Wikipedia reads "siendo incapaz de cobrar ni siquiera los 8000 pesos
de la dote."

**Garibaldi, date corrected, tag raised. [A at 1851]** The pool's chronology tagged the
Garibaldi and Melville visits together as **[R]** at 1850; its geography lens tagged
Garibaldi alone **[A]** at **April 1846**. Both are wrong. **In 1846 Garibaldi was
fighting in the Uruguayan Civil War**; he returned to Italy in 1848 and reached Lima only
late in **1851**, leaving for Canton on 10 January 1852. ES Wikipedia's Paita article
dates the visit to 1851. The pool's coda that their conversations shaped his republican
convictions is **unsourced editorializing and is cut**.

**Melville. [R, and probably worse]** Widely repeated popular tradition. **The ES Paita
article does not mention him at all**; no consulted source documents the visit.

**Simón Rodríguez. [R: Rumazo González]** Said to have visited or corresponded during the
Paita years; undocumented in the encyclopedic sources.

**Death. [A]** 23 November 1856, in a diphtheria epidemic, aged 58 (EN) or "nearly 59"
(ES); both agree on the date.

**The grave, correction. [A]** The pool said "her body and belongings ... are burned."
**Her body was not burned.** EN Wikipedia: "Her body was buried in a communal, mass grave
and her belongings were burned." Corrected: papers and effects burned as a sanitary
measure, body in a common grave, the plot never identified.

**How much was lost. [R]** The pool claimed the burning destroyed "part of Bolívar's
correspondence and Gran Colombian papers." **This overstates it**: substantial
Sáenz-Bolívar correspondence survives and was published, and both Lecuna's 1945 *Papeles*
and the material behind the 1992 Quito museum rest on surviving documents. Reworded to
"an unknown portion of her papers."

## 8. Afterlife (1860-2013)

**The long neglect. [A: EN Wikipedia]** Between 1860 and 1940 only three Ecuadorian
writers address her life at all, typically negatively. **[A]** Vicente Lecuna publishes
*Papeles de Manuela Sáenz* in 1945, beginning the documentary rehabilitation.

**The museum and the diaries, a split verdict.** **[A]** Carlos Álvarez Saá founds the
**Museo Manuela Sáenz** in Quito's centro histórico in **1992** to house his documentary
collection. **[R]** His *Manuela: sus diarios perdidos y otros papeles* appeared in
**1995**, and **the authenticity of those "discovered" diaries is contested by
historians**; the pool presented the discovery as the settled basis of the museum.
**Separately:** no consulted source presents the Calle Junín building as her **birth
house**. The pool tagged it **[A]** as the birthplace while conceding that no deed or
parish record fixes the address, which is disqualifying. A traditional site only.

**Literature and activism. [A]** García Márquez's *El general en su laberinto* (1989)
carries her to a mass readership. On **24 September 1989** in Paita, Nela Martínez
organizes the "Primer Encuentro con la Historia: Manuela Sáenz."

**Ranks. [R]** Ecuador grants her the honorary rank of General in May 2007: **EN Wikipedia
gives 25 May, ES Wikipedia gives 22 May**, both tied to Pichincha commemorations. **Not
resolved.** **[A]** Venezuela promotes her to Division General in 2010.

**The Pantheon. [A]** 5 July 2010, a state ceremony under Hugo Chávez inters **symbolic
remains**, soil from her presumed burial site, in the **Panteón Nacional** in Caracas near
Bolívar; the coffin travels overland through Peru, Ecuador, Colombia and Venezuela.
**Coordinate note:** the pool's 10.4806, -66.9036 is the generic Caracas point, some
3.5 km off; miranda.journey.json carries the Panteón at 10.5123, -66.9146, inherited
byte-identical. **[A]** In 2013 a fourteen-metre sculpture, *Rosa Roja de Paita*, is
unveiled beside Bolívar's mausoleum.

**Monuments. [R]** Busts in the Parque Mujeres Argentinas, Buenos Aires (2010) and in
Domodossola Park, Miraflores, Lima (2020); the Lima coordinates are approximate. **[R]** A
Casa de Manuelita Sáenz museum operates in Bogotá with **no founding date attested in any
source consulted: a gap.**

## 9. Quotations: what is usable and what must not be written

1. **[A]** Her own account of the laurel crown, "Cuando se acercaba al paso de nuestro
   balcón, tomé la corona de rosas y ramitas de laureles ...", ES Wikipedia, "Obra y
   vida," footnoted to the underlying passage. **The archival source was not reached.**
2. **[A]** Bolívar at the same welcome: "Señora: si mis soldados tuvieran su puntería, ya
   habríamos ganado la guerra a España." Same secondary route.
3. **[A]** "Vivo adoré a Bolívar, muerto lo venero." ES Wikipedia, "Exilio y muerte."
   Underlying primary document not located.
4. **[A]** The San Carlos plaque, **transcription corrected**; the pool dropped the
   accents. Per ES Wikipedia citing the Fundación Empresas Polar *Diccionario de Historia
   de Venezuela*, "Atentado del 25 de septiembre de 1828": "DETENTE, ESPECTADOR, UN
   MOMENTO / Y MIRA EL LUGAR POR DONDE SE SALVÓ / EL PADRE Y / LIBERTADOR DE LA PATRIA /
   SIMÓN BOLÍVAR / EN LA NEFANDA NOCHE SEPTEMBRINA / 1828," rendering a Latin original
   given in the same entry. **Separate error in the pool's source line: the Palacio de San
   Carlos is not the Museo Manuela Sáenz site.** That gloss is dropped.
5. **RESTRICTED: "The Liberator is immortal."** The citation is real and verified via
   Crossref: Gerhard Masur, "'The Liberator is Immortal': An Unknown Letter of Manuela
   Sáenz," *HAHR* 29:3 (1949), pp. 380-383. **But the phrase is verified only as Masur's
   own English article title**; **the Duke University Press full text returned HTTP 403 and
   the letter was never seen.** It must not be given as a verbatim Sáenz utterance, and
   **must not be back-translated into an invented Spanish original**, which would be a
   fabricated quotation. Used only as the title under which Masur published the letter.
6. **RESTRICTED: "la amable loca."** Real and widely circulated for Sáenz (Chirinos,
   "Manuela, la «amable loca»"; *Manuela, mi amable loca*; García Brandt's radionovela
   *La Coronela, la amable loca del Libertador*; EN Wikipedia citing Mathew Lyons, "The
   Liberator's Saviour is 'Buried'," *History Today* 70:7 (2020), p. 26). **What could not
   be verified is the attribution to Bolívar**: every traceable use is a later writer's
   epithet. Best rendered "lovable madwoman."
7. **[A, about her not by her]** Rocafuerte, 1835: "It is the women who most promote the
   spirit of anarchy in these countries," cited to Sarah C. Chambers, "Republican
   Friendship: Manuela Sáenz Writes Women into the Nation, 1835-1856," *HAHR* 81:2 (2001),
   **via EN Wikipedia; Chambers herself was not reachable.**
8. **[R] "Libertadora del Libertador."** Ubiquitous, and **not verified as a direct
   quotation from any primary source in this session.** bolivar.journey.json carries "You
   are the Liberatrix of the Liberator" sourced to O'Leary's *Memorias* and to Sáenz's own
   accounts; the journey file uses that atlas-internal form and flags the status.

## 10. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Canonical pins inherited **byte-identical**: Quito -0.2202, -78.5124 · Guayaquil -2.1894,
-79.8891 · Lima -12.0464, -77.0428 · Pativilca -10.7, -77.7833 · Cartagena 10.4236,
-75.5253 · Kingston 17.9714, -76.793 · Santa Marta (Quinta de San Pedro Alejandrino)
11.2281, -74.1792, all from bolivar.journey.json; Bogotá centre 4.5981, -74.076 and the
Palacio de San Carlos 4.5964, -74.0741 (bolivar, with camilo_torres_restrepo reusing the
first); Callao -12.062, -77.146 (san_martin); Pampa de Quinua -13.0431, -74.1379 (sucre);
Panteón Nacional, Caracas 10.5123, -66.9146 (miranda).

**Correction to the interlock lens's own coordinates.** The lens reported the Quito
meeting at -0.1807, -78.4678, citing bolivar.journey.json:932. **That is not what the file
says.** bolivar.journey.json carries -0.2202, -78.5124 for "Quito, the wreath from the
balcony"; -0.1807, -78.4678 is humboldt.journey.json's Quito pin, some 6.5 km northeast of
the colonial city. The bolivar value is used throughout.

**Travelers named in campa**, where the paths genuinely cross: `bolivar` (throughout),
`sucre` (Pichincha, Ayacucho), `san_martin` (the Order of the Sun; the Guayaquil
interview), `juana_azurduy` (the Cullco letter of 15 December 1825),
`policarpa_salavarrieta` (shot in 1817 a few streets from the Palacio de San Carlos),
`humboldt` (Quito, January 1802, when Manuela is four: a place-and-time co-presence with
no documented personal link, named as such), `anita_garibaldi` (her widower reaches Paita
in 1851), `miranda` (the open cenotaph beside which her symbolic soil is laid in 2010).

**Absences flagged rather than forced.** No canonical pin at Mecca, the Temple Mount,
Paris or Buenos Aires touches her itinerary, and **Paita appears in no other journey file
in the atlas.**
