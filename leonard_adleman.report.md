# Leonard Adleman: research report
*2026-08-11. Leonard Max Adleman, b. 31 December 1945, San Francisco. The A of RSA; coiner of "computer virus"; founder of DNA computing. Living subject: no death, no tomb, no afterlife in the usual sense.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, anecdote, or retrospective retelling no contemporaneous record supports. Contradictions are recorded and left open. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**Method.** Chronology, geography, quotes and interlock lenses plus an afterlife lens, cross-checked against one another. The cross-check is where the value is: the lenses disagree in seven places, and in five the disagreement is a tagging or coordinate error rather than a factual one. The atlas corpus in `working/journeys/` was grepped for prior appearances of Adleman.

---

## 1. San Francisco and Berkeley, 1945–1976

**[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]** Born 31 December 1945 in San Francisco. Father an appliance salesman, mother a bank teller; a Jewish family whose forebears emigrated from the Minsk area of present-day Belarus. He grows up in the city.

**Gap.** No source names the hospital, the neighbourhood, or the high school. **[R]** Minsk is ancestral geography only: no emigration date found, and Adleman himself is not attested there. The journey carries the pin with the date marked a placeholder for a generation.

**[A: MacTutor]** He describes himself as a boy as "incredibly naive and immature," with no ambition toward mathematics, until an English teacher's reading of *Hamlet* showed him the beauty of ideas. That teacher sent him to Berkeley.

**[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]** **B.A. in mathematics**, UC Berkeley, **1968**. Wikipedia specifies B.A.; the geography lens's "BS" is corrected. MacTutor confirms the chemist-then-doctor-then-mathematician sequence (Mr. Wizard, then Kappa Nu fraternity brothers).

**Contradiction flagged.** The geography lens gives a 1963 enrollment year. Nothing supports it; MacTutor gives only the 1968 graduation. Not carried.

**[A: MacTutor]** Straight out of Berkeley, **computer programmer at Bank of America**. He is accepted to medical school in these years and declines. **Pin caveat:** MacTutor names the employer, not the building; the journey uses 300 Montgomery Street as a landmark, flagged approximate.

**[A for the fact, R for the year: MacTutor]** While at the bank he takes physics at **San Francisco State College** and quits: "I didn't like doing experiments, I liked thinking about things." No year in the source; "c. 1969" is inference. **Coordinate corrected:** the lens gave -122.4762, about 330 m east of campus across 19th Avenue; SFSU is at roughly 37.7241 / -122.4799.

**[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor; Mathematics Genealogy Project]** **Ph.D. 1976**, Berkeley EECS, thesis *Number Theoretic Aspects of Computational Complexity*, adviser **Manuel Blum**. His own reason: "I thought that getting a PhD in computer science would at least further my career."

**Contradiction flagged.** The geography lens pinned the doctorate to **Evans Hall, labelled "(EECS)"**. Evans houses Mathematics and Statistics; EECS is Cory Hall (and, from 1994, Soda). Its coordinate is also about 160 m north of Evans. The journey claims no building and uses the campus pin.

## 2. MIT, 1976–1980: the forty-second failure

**[A: MacTutor; ACM Turing citation]** Assistant professor of mathematics at MIT from 1976, straight out of the doctorate. **Rivest's office is next to his.**

**[A for the collaboration and year; R for the count: MacTutor; Council for Innovation Promotion]** Through 1977 Rivest and Shamir propose candidate systems and Adleman breaks them. The **forty-two failures before the forty-third** is a retrospective anecdote: MacTutor, later inventor-spotlight retellings, and Adleman's own memory in interview. No contemporaneous 1977 record carries it.

**Contradiction internal to the research pool.** The chronology lens tags this figure **[A]** at one index and **[R]** at another. Carried here as **[R]**. Adleman's own unhedged retelling makes it his memory, not a document; a memory is still [R].

**[A: CSO Online]** "I was the number theorist in residence. Ron and Adi were really more interested in public crypto than I was initially."

**[A: CSO Online]** The breakthrough is dated by Adleman to a night in April 1977: Rivest at a Passover dinner, a good deal of wine, a midnight telephone call about factoring large primes, and Adleman's answer on the same call: "Congratulations, you've done it!"

**Correction.** The chronology lens spread the invention across "1977"; the geography lens put it "that summer." Both wrong in the same direction. April 1977 is the night; the MIT/LCS memo follows in April, Gardner's column in August, the patent filing in December. The atlas already carries **1977-04-02** in `phil_zimmermann.journey.json`, and the journey inherits it.

**[A: MacTutor]** Offered co-authorship, Adleman first refuses: "Take my name off the paper. It's your work." He then reasons himself back on and down to third place: the paper will be the least important he is ever on, he will need vita lines for tenure, and he did break codes 1 through 42. Alphabetical order does the rest. The method is RSA and not RS because of that argument.

**[A: US Patent 4,405,829; cr.yp.to]** Filed **14 December 1977**: Rivest of Belmont, Shamir of Cambridge, **Adleman of Arlington**. Assigned to MIT, granted 1983.

**[A: CACM, February 1978]** "A Method for Obtaining Digital Signatures and Public-Key Cryptosystems" is published.

**[A: Scientific American, August 1977]** Gardner's "Mathematical Games" publicises the cipher and prints the RSA-129 challenge. **Terminology corrected:** the afterlife lens called the cryptogram "signed by" the three authors. It was *encrypted*, with a $100 prize, and *posed* by them. A digital signature is a different operation, and the slip matters in a file about cryptography.

## 3. Los Angeles, 1980–1985: the chair, the company, the word

**[A: USC Viterbi; AAAS]** **USC from 1980** as tenured associate professor; full professor **1983**; **Henry Salvatori Chair 1985**, later Distinguished Professor; a joint appointment in molecular biology later. He never moves again.

**[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia "RSA Security"; FundingUniverse]** He co-founds **RSA Data Security** with Rivest and Shamir. **Two corrections:** the chronology lens's founding year **1983** is wrong, three sources give **1982**; and the company began in Adleman's apartment, so **100 Marine Parkway, Redwood City** is the 1990s headquarters, not the founding address. **[R]** MacTutor alone says Adleman was "made president"; Bidzos joined 1 February 1986 and was president and CEO 1986–1999.

**Pin note.** The journey uses the atlas's canonical Redwood City pin **37.4852 / -122.2364**, inherited byte-identical from `hellman` and `phil_zimmermann`, not the geography lens's 37.5259 / -122.2434, itself 600–700 m southeast of Marine Parkway.

**[A: Annals of Mathematics 1983; MacTutor]** With **Rumely and Pomerance**, the primality-testing paper behind the Adleman-Pomerance-Rumely test.

**[R: MacTutor only]** He meets **Lori Bruce** at a singles dance and marries her about six weeks later. Sole source; no corroboration; **MacTutor names no city**. The chronology lens tagged this [A] and supplied "Los Angeles (per MacTutor account)," which the source does not support. The geography lens tags it [R] and is right.

**[A: Fred Cohen, "Computer Viruses: Theory and Experiments"; WeLiveSecurity; USC Viterbi]** November 1983: Cohen, Adleman's USC student, conceives a self-replicating program on **3 November** and demonstrates it on **10 November** at his weekly security seminar, taking a VAX 11/750 in under thirty minutes. Adleman supplies the name.

**[A: WeLiveSecurity, Adleman speaking]** "I would meet with Fred on a regular basis to discuss this, and I at the same time was doing research on HIV in a molecular biology lab. So viruses and how they worked were sort of much in my mind ... And so somewhere along the line during our discussions I started calling these things computer viruses."

**Correction, and a common error.** The chronology lens placed the demonstration at **Lehigh University**. It did not happen there. Several secondary sites (historyofinformation.com, EDN) carry Lehigh by conflating this with Cohen's later affiliation and the unrelated **1987 Lehigh virus**. The afterlife lens hedged the point as open; it is not open. Cohen's own account settles it, and the paper went to the 1984 IFIP security conference.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Adleman's explanation of the coining requires him to be in a molecular biology lab in 1983. The geography lens dates the wet-lab period to c. 1990–1993 **[R]**. Both cannot be exactly right, and neither is firm enough to correct the other.

## 4. The immune system, the movie, the test tube, 1986–1994

**[R: Stanford CS course profile]** With **David Wofsy of UCSF**, a theory of CD4-cell depletion in AIDS as failed homeostatic regulation. Getting HIV researchers to hear it is harder than the mathematics. **[R]** He enters a wet lab at USC to learn technique from the ground up, prompted partly by Watson's *Molecular Biology of the Gene*.

**[A: MacTutor]** The Adleman-Wofsy paper appears in the *Journal of Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndromes*, **1993**.

**[A: USC Laboratory for Molecular Science "Sneakers" page; theworld.com]** Mathematical consultant on **Sneakers** (1992); he writes "a breakthrough of Gaussian proportions," spoken by Dr. Gunter Janek.

**Correction.** The geography lens placed this at **Universal Studios**. Unsupported, and wrong twice over: Adleman's own account says he made "beautiful slides on my Mac" and that the scene was shot at a small college in Los Angeles, after which the production had the slides **redrawn by hand** because hand-drawn looked more realistic. So the lens also inverts the detail. **Gap:** no source names the college.

**[A: MacTutor, Adleman's account]** The founding insight arrives while he is ill in bed with a molecular biology textbook: "While lying there admiring this amazing enzyme, I was struck by its similarity to something described in 1936 by Alan Turing."

**[A: Science 266:1021–1024, 11 November 1994]** "Molecular Computation of Solutions to Combinatorial Problems": a seven-node Hamiltonian Path instance solved with oligonucleotide synthesis, PCR and affinity purification. **[A: MacTutor]** "I took a pinch (about 10^14 molecules) of each of the different sequences and put them into a common test tube ... Within about one second, I had the answer." DNA computing dates from this paper.

**[A: RSA Factoring Challenge histories]** The same year, **RSA-129**, the cipher Gardner printed in 1977, is factored by Atkins, Graff, Lenstra and Leyland over the early internet. Millions of years turns out to be seventeen. THE MAGIC WORDS ARE SQUEAMISH OSSIFRAGE.

## 5. Honours and the long tail, 1996–2026

**[A: ACM records]** The inaugural **Paris Kanellakis Award**, 1996, goes to **six** people jointly: Adleman, Diffie, Hellman, Merkle, Rivest, Shamir; presented March 1997. **Correction:** the chronology lens called it a three-way award; the amacad.org "shared with Rivest and Shamir" is an abbreviation, and `ralph_merkle.journey.json` already has the six-name version right.

**[A: Wikipedia; FundingUniverse]** RSA Data Security is acquired by **Security Dynamics in 1996**. **Contradiction on two axes, not resolved.** Value: $200 million (MacTutor) against approximately **$251 million** (FundingUniverse, four million shares). Date: July 1996 (Wikipedia) against April 1996 (FundingUniverse), probably announcement against close, but that is not confirmed.

**[A: Wikipedia; USC Today]** **NAE 1996**; **NAS 2006** (Section 34); **American Academy of Arts and Sciences 2006**. **Gap:** personal attendance at any induction ceremony is unconfirmed, and the journey's stops say so.

**[A: Wikipedia]** **2002**: his group reports a **20-variable SAT instance**, roughly a million candidates, solved by DNA computation.

**[A: amturing.acm.org; Notices of the AMS, July 2003]** The **2002 A.M. Turing Award** to Rivest, Shamir and Adleman. **Two corrections.** The citation reads "For their ingenious contribution **to** making public-key cryptography useful in practice"; both the chronology and afterlife lenses print "for making," a misquote of a formal citation. And the place is not New York: the banquet was in **San Diego on 7 June 2003**, opening day of FCRC 2003 at the Town and Country Resort. The geography lens has the venue right and the coordinate wrong (32.7627 / -117.1667 is about 700 m southwest, in the river corridor); Town and Country is at roughly **32.7679 / -117.1622**.

**[A: NIHF; PR Newswire]** **National Inventors Hall of Fame**, with Rivest and Shamir. Two days: **2 May 2018** illumination in the Gallery of Icons at the NIHF Museum inside USPTO headquarters, **Alexandria**; **3 May 2018** induction at the **National Building Museum**, Washington, emceed by Mo Rocca. **Correction:** the chronology lens gave Alexandria as the induction venue, which is the wrong evening; the afterlife lens gave the right venue with a coordinate 1.25 km east of it. The museum is at roughly **38.8977 / -77.0166**.

**[A: Wikipedia]** **ACM Fellow, 2021**.

**[A: USC Viterbi; adleman.usc.edu]** Still listed as **Henry Salvatori Distinguished Professor of Computer Science and Professor of Molecular Biology**. **[A: Wikipedia, as of 2017]** Working on a private theory he calls **strata**, and **[A: CSO Online]** on a book about the **compuverse**: "A first-world country with no computational infrastructure is a country with no economy, no food, no power, and ultimately not a country at all."

**[R]** An amateur boxer said to have sparred with James Toney. Wikipedia carries it undated and unplaced; no year, no gym, no corroboration. Recorded, not mapped.

## 6. Contradictions, not adjudicated

1. **Wet-lab dates against the virus anecdote.** 1983 coining depends on lab work then; the reconstructed immersion is c. 1990–1993.
2. **Sale value:** $200 million against approximately $251 million.
3. **Sale date:** July 1996 against April 1996.
4. **The presidency:** MacTutor alone; Bidzos held the title 1986–1999, and the pre-1986 years are unestablished.
5. **The forty-two failures:** tagged [A] and [R] within one lens. Carried [R].
6. **The doctorate's building:** Evans Hall against EECS in Cory. No building claimed.
7. **Berkeley enrollment 1963:** asserted, unsupported. Not carried.

## 7. Gaps

- Birth hospital, childhood neighbourhood, high school: **unnamed everywhere**.
- Emigration date and route of the Minsk-area forebears: **not found**.
- The small Los Angeles college where the *Sneakers* scene was shot: **not named**, including by Adleman.
- The singles-dance venue and city: **not in the source**.
- The boxing anecdote: **no date, no place, no corroboration**.
- Attendance at the NAE, NAS and American Academy inductions: **unconfirmed**. Those stops are institutional, not documented travel.
- Adleman is **alive**. No monument, gravesite, museum wing or relic collection exists, which is the expected result. The iconography is a citation, a plaque, three academy rolls, a faculty page.

## 8. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Adleman is already named in the corpus more often than most subjects are before their own file exists.

- **`phil_zimmermann.journey.json`**: 545 Technology Square at **42.3626 / -71.0913, 1977-04-02**; **Cheltenham 51.8994 / -2.1258 (1997)**, the GCHQ declassification; **San Francisco 37.7842 / -122.4016 (1991)**, Bidzos's first RSA Conference; **Bedford 42.4906 / -71.2762, 2000-09-06**, the algorithm released early. All inherited byte-identical.
- **`nick_szabo.journey.json`**: Chaum's blind signature built on the RSA function, citing the RSA file's own stop.
- **`hal_finney.journey.json`**: RSA over Diffie, Hellman and Merkle, and those over Gauss's modular arithmetic in the *Disquisitiones* of 1801; RPOW tokens are RSA-signed.
- **`ralph_merkle.journey.json`**: the six-name Kanellakis award at the canonical New York pin **40.7519 / -73.9686**, plus Merkle's 1978 quote naming Rivest, Shamir and Adleman.
- **`hellman.journey.json`**: Redwood City **37.4852 / -122.2364** and the patent fight.
- **`stephen_king.journey.json`**: names Diffie, Hellman and the RSA work in its late-1970s Orrington frame.
- **`diffie.report.md`**: a *New York Times Magazine* "Scientist at Work" piece its gathering lens attributed to Diffie (12 July 1994) does not exist under that byline; the actual column of that season, **13 December 1994**, ran on Adleman.

**A real problem, recorded not solved.** Five files (`hal_finney`, `nick_szabo`, `phil_zimmermann`, and two reports) cite **`rivest_shamir_adleman.journey.json`** by name, several citing stops inside it. **That file does not exist**, nor any `ron_rivest` or `adi_shamir` file; only scratch material sits in `atlas_tools/` (`_scratch_rivest.py`, `_scratch_rivest_lens.py`, `_rivest_oral_history.txt`). The corpus has been citing a source drafted into reference before it was written. This journey inherits the pins those citations record, which keeps the corpus consistent, but does not discharge the debt: `QUEUE.md` lists all three men as separate approved subjects.

**`nick_szabo.report.md`** documents a pin dispute over 545 Technology Square, the interlock lens's value against a reported -71.0942, ruling **-71.0913** authoritative. This journey uses **-71.0913** throughout; the -71.0942 that appears in this subject's own chronology and afterlife lenses for MIT is not used for the Technology Square stops.

**Not an interlock.** `solomon.journey.json` carries the Talmudic *shamir*, the rock-cleaving worm that cut the Temple stones without iron. It shares a surface name with Adi Shamir and nothing else. Flagged so nobody later mistakes the coincidence for a link.
