# Jules-Félix Coutan (1848-1939): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-13 for `jules_felix_coutan.journey.json`. Academic sculptor: Prix de Rome 1872, pensionnaire of the Villa Médicis, director of art works at Sèvres, professor at the École des beaux-arts, member of the Académie des beaux-arts from 1900. Author of the group over Grand Central Terminal and of three tombs in the Recoleta, and a man who on the evidence never left Europe.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including anything a single source asserts that no other source supports. Contradictions between sources are flagged and left standing.

---

## 1. Birth and formation (1848-1872)

- Born **22 September 1848** in the **ancien 11e arrondissement of Paris**. **[A, fr.wikipedia 'Jules Coutan']**
- The birthplace is a district that no longer exists. The old eleventh comprised the quartiers du Palais de Justice, de l'École de médecine, de la Sorbonne and du Luxembourg, plus the western part of the Île de la Cité, and was abolished by the law of 16 June 1859, effective 1 January 1860; the ground is now split between the modern 1st, 5th and 6th. **[A, fr.wikipedia 'Ancien 11e arrondissement de Paris']**
- **Coordinate correction.** The pool held two pins for this one birth, 1.3 km apart: 48.8462/2.3464, which sits in the Gobelins area of the old **12th**, and 48.8566/2.3522, the Hôtel de Ville on the **right** bank. Both are wrong. The journey uses **48.851/2.341**, a centroid of the Luxembourg and Sorbonne quarters, flagged as a centroid and not an address. **[R, pin]**
- Studied at the **École nationale supérieure des beaux-arts** under **Jules Cavelier**. **[A, fr.wikipedia]** No year of entry in any reachable source; the journey's 1866 is a placeholder. **[R]**
- **Prix de Rome in sculpture, 1872**, for *Ajax bravant les Dieux et foudroyé*. **[A, fr.wikipedia]** Month and day not recorded. **[gap]**

## 2. Rome (1873-1876)

- **Pensionnaire at the Villa Médicis, 1873-1876**. **[A, fr.wikipedia; Wikidata Q3183964]**
- What he studied, copied or sent back as *envois* is not recorded anywhere reachable. **[gap, carried into the campa as a gap]**
- A small bronze, **'Standing Cupid', 1875**, is credited to him by en.wikipedia alone and appears in **no** French works list. Its place tag in the pool, "Paris", is impossible for a man resident in Rome 1873-1876; the journey pins it at the villa and flags attribution and location as loose. **[R, single source]**
- The **return to Paris in 1876** and the opening of a studio are inference from the career pattern. **No address for any Coutan atelier appears in any source consulted, at any date.** The journey pins the studio in the Montparnasse and Plaisance sculptors' quarter as an explicit guess at a neighbourhood. **[R]** The pool tagged this same invented four-decimal pin **[A]** while conceding the address was unknown; downgraded here.

## 3. The Salon decades and the state posts (1881-1895)

- **Marriage, 1881**, to **Suzanne Émilie Desbeaux**, sister of the writer Émile Desbeaux. **[A, Wikidata Q3183964 citing French biographical records]** No month, parish or mairie. **[gap]**
- **Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur 11 July 1885; Officier 13 July 1889.** **[A, fr.wikipedia]**
- **'La Paix armée'.** The worst item in the pool put this bronze in the **parc Montsouris in 1887**, with a narrative of the figure standing watch over the Third Republic from that year. fr.wikipedia's article on the parc says instead: **conceived for the column of the square d'Anvers, made in 1888, removed in 1968, re-erected in Montsouris only in 1984**. The journey relocates the stop to the square d'Anvers at 1888. **[A, fr.wikipedia 'Parc Montsouris']** Two further pool errors dropped: Montsouris was inaugurated in 1869, not "barely a decade old" in 1887, and stands on disused limestone quarries, not a demolished bastion.
- **Exposition Universelle 1889, Champ de Mars**: the fountain group *La France apportant le flambeau de la civilisation*, also cited as *Fontaine du progrès*; **gold medal**. **[A, fr. and en.wikipedia]** No unveiling date for the fountain. **[gap]** It did not survive the fair. **[A]**
- **Sculpture on the Herbette family tomb, cimetière du Montparnasse.** fr.wikipedia gives it with **no date and no Salon**, so the pool's "presented first at the 1890 Salon" is unsupported; the journey carries 1890 with both year and Salon flagged uncorroborated. **[R]** Pin corrected from 48.8422/2.3266, about 430 m north on the boulevard, to 48.8383/2.3270.
- **'Le Manuscrit', marble, Bibliothèque nationale site Richelieu, 1891.** **[A, fr.wikipedia]**
- **Director of art works, Manufacture nationale de Sèvres, 1891-1894**, resigning for the professorship. **[A, fr.wikipedia; Wikidata]** Pin corrected from 48.8237/2.2131 (about 1 km off site) to the manufacture's own 48.8286/2.2225. **[A, fr.wikipedia 'Manufacture nationale de Sèvres']**
- **Monument to the soldiers of the Vienne killed in 1870, Poitiers, inaugurated 1895.** The pool guessed "likely the Jardin de Blossac"; its own cited source names the garden, *jardin public rue Magenta*. Upgraded to **[A, fr.wikipedia]**, coordinate approximated to that garden. **[R, pin only]**

## 4. The chair, the fairs, the Institut (1894-1905)

- **Professor of sculpture at the École des beaux-arts from 1894.** **[A, fr.wikipedia; Wikidata]**
  - **Contradiction, flagged not resolved.** en.wikipedia gives **1900**; fr.wikipedia gives **1894**, on the Sèvres resignation; fr.wikipedia's page on **Hippolyte Lefèbvre** names Coutan among his masters as early as **1882**. The economical reading is that en.wikipedia conflated the chair with the 1900 academy election, but no source says so, and `date_confidence` records the disagreement rather than adjudicating it.
- Pupils named across the sources: **Mahmoud Mokhtar, Marcel Gaumont, Raymond Delamarre, Louis Leygue** **[A, fr.wikipedia; Wikidata]**, **Hippolyte Lefèbvre** **[A, fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Rogelio Yrurtia.** en.wikipedia lists him as a pupil; es.wikipedia says he reached Paris **only in 1900**, was an *asiduo concurrente al taller* of Coutan, and first showed at the Salon in 1903. The journey says "frequenting the studio", not "student". **[A for association, R for pupil]** The pool's claim that he carried Coutan's line to Buenos Aires before Coutan's own work reached it is false on its own dates and is dropped. **[flagged, not carried]**
- **Rodin.** en.wikipedia records Coutan dismissing Auguste Rodin's work as *fumiste*, not serious, and criticising the sculptors following him. **[A, en.wikipedia]** The pool's label "Art Nouveau-adjacent" is rejected on the same evidence: an Institut academician in the Prix de Rome line who attacked Rodin publicly is not adjacent to the new style, and the one Art Nouveau object in the oeuvre is the 1900 Sèvres portique. **[R, recast as academic and neoclassical]**
- **Opéra-Comique, salle Favart. Corrected on two counts.** The pool put his caryatids on the **façade** and dated the house **1899**. fr.wikipedia gives the façade statues to **Denys Puech** and **Ernest Charles Guilbert**, while *dix cariatides de Jules Coutan* support the **boxes inside the auditorium**; Bernier's third salle Favart opened **7 December 1898**, after the fire of 25 May 1887. **[A, fr.wikipedia 'Opéra-Comique']** No date for the carving of the caryatids. **[gap]**
- **'Les Chasseurs d'aigles'.** fr.wikipedia's works list: *haut-relief en plâtre*, **1900**, now **musée d'Orsay**; **bronze executed 1900-1901** on the **galerie d'anthropologie (façade rue)** of the Muséum national d'histoire naturelle. **[A]** en.wikipedia gives **1893-1900** with installation in 1900. **[R, unsupported by the French source]** The pool's spliced "1893-1901" belongs to neither and is dropped; the journey carries the French dating and records the English one in `date_confidence`. The pool's "the plaster eventually leaves for the Musée d'Orsay" also inverts the sequence: the plaster is the original, the bronze the later cast.
  - **Pin gap.** fr.wikipedia's article on the *galerie de paléontologie et d'anatomie comparée* attributes that façade to **Allar and Frémiet**, no Coutan, so the pool's 48.8437/2.3588 is not anchored to a verified Coutan-bearing façade. The journey keeps it as "the museum grounds" and says the exact façade is unconfirmed. **[R, pin]**
- **Salon of 1900 in the Grand Palais: stop cut.** The Grand Palais in 1900 held the Exposition universelle; the annual Salon moved in **from 1901**, and fr.wikipedia's Coutan entry mentions no Salon showing. Both exhibition and venue are unsourced. An Orsay stop for the surviving plaster takes its place. **[flagged, not carried]**
- **Portique of the Sèvres pavilion, 1900, re-erected in the square Félix-Desruelles.** **[A, fr.wikipedia 'Square Félix-Desruelles']** Three corrections: the material is **grès émaillé**, not terra-cotta; it is a free-standing **portique**, not façade bas-reliefs; the architect **Charles-Auguste Risler** is **co-author**. Pin corrected to 48.8536/2.3344, the pool's being about 265 m too far west.
- **Grand Prix of the 1900 exposition.** **[A, fr.wikipedia; Wikidata]**
- **'La France de la Renaissance', pont Alexandre-III, 1900.** **[A, en. and fr.wikipedia]** Two pool errors corrected against fr.wikipedia's bridge article: the first stone was laid 7 October 1896 by **Tsar Nicholas II**, not by Alexander III, who was dead by 1894; and the four France groups stand at the pylon **bases**, under gilded *renommées*, and represent **epochs**, not centuries. Coutan's is the upstream left-bank pylon.
- **Elected to the Académie des beaux-arts, 1900**, succeeding **Alexandre Falguière** (died 19 April 1900) in *fauteuil* 4. **[A, fr.wikipedia 'Jules Coutan' and 'Alexandre Falguière'; independently es.wikipedia]** en.wikipedia's **1905** is an error, not a live alternative, and is not carried; it is recorded here so the discrepancy stays on file.
- **'La Porteuse de pain', Petit Palais.** In fr.wikipedia's works list with **no date and no material**. **[A for work and location, gap for the rest]** The pool credited it to en.wikipedia, which does not mention it, and called it a plaster model, which no source supports. The journey's 1900 is an explicit placeholder.
- **'La Science' and 'Le Travail', pont de Bir-Hakeim (then pont de Passy).** fr.wikipedia places the two bas-reliefs on **l'arche**, the central arch over the Île aux Cygnes, not across the piers. **[A]** No date anywhere; 1903-1905 is the only bracket. **[R, date]** Pin moved to the bridge midpoint, 48.8558/2.2874.

## 5. The transatlantic commissions (1904-1914)

Everything in this phase was made in Paris and installed by other hands. en.wikipedia states that **Coutan never visited the United States** **[A]**, and no source records any journey to Argentina.

- **Paz mausoleum, Cementerio de la Recoleta, commissioned 1904.** es.wikipedia's cemetery article says the commission honoured the family's son **José Jacobo Gainza Paz**, the father, founder of *La Prensa*, being interred there in **1912**. **[A]** The pool called this an "internal inconsistency"; it is not, since a tomb of 1904 receiving a later burial is ordinary. Flag withdrawn. Description confirmed: black granite cubic base, bronze door, two large white Carrara angels, one crowned, one with an anchor. **[A]**
  - **Live naming dispute, not resolved.** es.wikipedia's Coutan article calls the father **José Camilo Paz**; the Recoleta article calls him **José Clemente Paz**. The campa names only "the father, who founded the newspaper", and `date_confidence` records the dispute.
- **Avellaneda mausoleum, Recoleta, 1910**, entirely in Carrara marble, a woman in prayer beside the portrait, about 3 m by 2.5 m by 2.3 m. **[A, es.wikipedia 'Cementerio de la Recoleta']** A brief grouping it with the 1904 Paz commission is a separate account, not reconciled. **[flagged]**
- **Luis María Campos tomb, Recoleta.** Credited to Coutan by en.wikipedia ('La Recoleta Cemetery') and independently by es.wikipedia ('Jules Coutan'), so the attribution is firmer than the pool's "single source" caveat allowed; the **date remains entirely unknown**. **[A for attribution, gap for date]**
- **Pins.** The pool invented three four-decimal pins for three tombs inside one burial ground of 5.5 hectares. The journey uses the corpus's existing Recoleta pin, **-34.5875/-58.3936**, for all three. **[A, inherited pin]**
- **Grand Central Terminal group, 'Glory of Commerce'** (also 'Transportation'): Mercury flanked by Minerva and Hercules.
  - **Contradiction, flagged not resolved.** Wikidata gives the span **1907-1913**; en.wikipedia gives **1911-1914** for the same commission. Neither preferred; the studio stop is dated 1911 and both are recorded.
  - Modelled in his **Paris studio**, shipped to New York, carved at full size by the **John Donnelly Company**. **[A, en.wikipedia]** The pool's chronology lens calls the model **quarter-size**, its afterlife lens **full-scale**, which is self-contradictory and not credible for a 48-foot, 1,500-ton group; en.wikipedia gives no scale. The campa carries "a quarter of the size" as the only physically possible reading and claims no scale as attested. **[R]**
  - **Unveiled 1914**: about 48 feet high, 66 feet wide, some 1,500 short tons, called at the time the largest sculptural group in the world. **[A, en.wikipedia 'Glory of Commerce']** Day not recorded. **[gap]**
  - **Tiffany clock claim dropped.** en.wikipedia gives a 13-foot clock installed in 1914 by the **Self Winding Clock Company**, stained glass in a bronze frame, and names no Tiffany. **[flagged, not carried]**
- **Monument to Carlos Pellegrini, Plaza Carlos Pellegrini, Retiro, Buenos Aires, 1914**, Carrara marble and bronze, with figures of the Republic, justice and progress. **[A, es.wikipedia 'Plaza Carlos Pellegrini']**
  - **Arithmetic error corrected.** The pool called the sculptor "seventy six years old" in 1914. Born 22 September 1848, he was **65**, turning 66 that September.
  - **Pin.** The pool held two pins for one plaza, -34.5972/-58.382 and -34.5945/-58.3833, 350 m apart and both south of the site. The journey uses **-34.592/-58.381**, the head of the avenida Alvear near Cerrito and Arroyo, and flags the disagreement. **[R, pin]**

## 6. Death and afterlife (1939 onward)

- **Died 23 February 1939, 6th arrondissement of Paris, aged 90.** **[A, fr. and en.wikipedia]** Address not recorded. **[gap]**
- **Buried 25 February 1939, cimetière de Montmartre, division 25.** **[A, fr.wikipedia]** Pin corrected: the pool carried two pins for this grave (48.8887/2.3317, 48.8896/2.3312), both **outside the walls**. The journey uses the cemetery centre, **48.8878/2.3277**; the plot is unlocated. **[R, pin]**
- **Associate of the Sculpture Section of the Académie royale de Belgique since 1920**; death announced at the session of **2 March 1939**. **[A, Bulletin de la Classe des Beaux-Arts vol. 21 (1939) pp. 51-52, persee.fr]** No visit by Coutan to Belgium is recorded anywhere. **[gap]**
- **Small bronzes cast by Thiébaut Frères.** en.wikipedia supports only that the foundry produced them; the pool's "recurring presence on the art market" is unsourced market inference. **[R, foundry link only]**

## 7. Relations to other travellers in the corpus

Another traveller may be named in a campa only where **Coutan himself** has a direct or indirect relation to that person. Shared geography does not qualify; the map already shows co-location.

- **Auguste Rodin: qualifies, and is named.** Coutan publicly dismissed him as *fumiste* and criticised the sculptors following him. **[A, en.wikipedia]** A one-directional judgment on a named contemporary is a real link. Named in the professorship campa and again at the death.
- **Camille Claudel, Alejandro Christophersen: excluded.** Same school and Salon circuit, no attested contact.
- **Torcuato de Alvear, Troiano Troiani: excluded.** Alvear had the Recoleta surveyed and gated in the 1880s and Coutan's tombs stand inside that plan; Troiani worked the same city a generation later. Shared ground, nothing more.
- **Sarmiento, Julio Argentino Roca, Ceferino Namuncurá: excluded.** The links run through Coutan's *subjects*, Pellegrini and Avellaneda, not through Coutan. Avellaneda's presidency and the frontier war appear in that campa unnamed.
- **Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi: excluded, though nearest the line.** A French monumental sculptor of the same generation whose work also crossed to New York, but the pool tags the parallel **[R]** and records no meeting, letter or comment either way. **Agustí Querol Subirats**, queued, is excluded on the same test.

**Interlocks named in campa: `auguste_rodin` only.**

## 8. Quotes: used and not used

- **Used.** The Belgian academy's death announcement of 2 March 1939, verbatim French, fetched at persee.fr. Coutan is not the speaker; it is a contemporary record of the death and of a membership held since 1920.
- **Used.** Maxime Collignon, 1906, *Monuments et mémoires de la Fondation Eugène Piot*, on a cast seen "dans l'atelier de mon confrère et ami M. Jules Coutan". A dated primary sighting of him at work, and the only interior view of that studio anywhere reachable.
- **Used, headline only.** *The New York Times*, 14 June 1914, "WORLD'S LARGEST SCULPTURE GROUP FOR NEW YORK; Figures for Grand Central Station Are 50 Feet High". The **article body could not be fetched** (timesmachine.nytimes.com blocked here) and is not quoted. Note the headline's "50 Feet" against the 48 feet given elsewhere.
- **Not used.** Pascal Ory, *L'Expo universelle, 1889* (1989): verbatim text actually seen, but modern commentary rather than a period source. Recorded here instead, since Ory names the 1889 commission as the *Fontaine du progrès*.
- **No words of Coutan's own were found anywhere.** The *fumiste* judgment reaches us as reported speech in en.wikipedia, not as a locatable sentence, so it sits in prose and not in a `quote` field. This is the largest gap in the file: a ninety-year life with a chair, an academy seat and a factory directorship, and not one verified sentence in his own voice.

**Named and not reachable**
- **timesmachine.nytimes.com**, the 14 June 1914 Grand Central article (PDF 100320283): host blocked here. Headline verified through the English Wikipedia citation apparatus; body unread and unquoted.
- **Pascal Ory, *L'Expo universelle, 1889* (1989), p. 20f**: seen only as quoted in that same apparatus, not in the book.
- **No archival source was consulted at all.** Everything above rests on encyclopaedic compilations plus two persee.fr articles. No state archive, no Salon livret, no academy election record, no Sèvres administrative file, no civil-registry document was reached. For a man whose whole working life passed through documented state institutions, that is a large body of primary evidence left untouched, and every date here should be read against it.
