# Albert Pike (1809-1891): research report

*2026-08-28. Slug `albert_pike`. Written from the supplied verified research pool; no new pages were fetched at the write stage, so every "reachable / not reachable" note reports the state of the gather and verify lenses. The journey file has 8 segments and 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or legend. **CORR** a verify-lens correction to the gather lens, applied in the journey file.

Pike is four people sharing a body: a frontier poet who walked six hundred and fifty miles, a Little Rock editor and Supreme Court reporter, a Confederate brigadier who negotiated nine treaties with the Indian Territory nations, and the man who rewrote the Scottish Rite and wrote the book a century of conspiracy literature is built out of misquoting. The Masonic afterlife needs more flagging than everything before it combined.

---

## 1. Massachusetts, 1809-1831

Born Boston, 29 December 1809, to Benjamin Pike, cobbler, and Sarah Andrews Pike, one of six children **[A, Encyclopedia of Arkansas]**. Public schools at Byfield, Newburyport, Framingham **[A, EOA and TSHA]**, with no school, master, or anecdote named in either source. Passes the Harvard entrance examination at sixteen, cannot pay, does not attend **[A]**; receives an honorary A.M. in 1859 **[A]**. Later accounts routinely collapse the two into an attendance he never had, and the file states both facts and the confusion. Teaches at Newburyport, Gloucester, Fairhaven **[R]**, dossier-level only, so these are folded into the Cambridge stop rather than given pins. "Hymns to the Gods" published c. 1832 **[A]**. **CORR:** the gather lens gave one stop labelled "Newburyport (Byfield)" at Newburyport's coordinate, though Byfield is a village of Newbury about 8 km southwest. One pin is kept, at the corrected 42.8126, -70.8773, with all three towns named in prose.

## 2. The western journey, 1831-1832

March 1831, leaves Independence with traders bound for New Mexico; horse breaks away in a snowstorm; walks roughly 500 miles into Taos **[A, TSHA]**. Then Santa Fe, still Mexican. Trapping venture onto the Llano Estacado, across the Caprock and Oklahoma, ending at Fort Smith: about 1,300 miles, 650 on foot **[A, TSHA]**. *Prose Sketches and Poems Written in the Western Country*, Boston 1834, "the first book dealing with the region between Fort Smith and Santa Fe" **[A]**, folded into the Caprock stop since Pike was in Arkansas when it was printed. The Caprock coordinate is representative and no documented site exists. **CORR:** Belle Point is at roughly 35.3880, -94.4295, about 500 m northwest of the gather lens's value.

## 3. Arkansas: the Advocate, the bar, the war, the duel, 1833-1849

1833, anonymous "Casca" letters for Robert Crittenden; hired by Charles Bertrand at the *Arkansas Advocate*; sole owner 1835 **[A]**. 10 October 1834, marries Mary Ann Hamilton, whose family's resources capitalize him **[A]**; the marriage leaves almost no record for thirty years, which is a gap and not an interpretation. First reporter of the Arkansas Supreme Court from 1836 **[A]**. 1840, receiver for the failed Arkansas State Bank; 1842, *Arkansas Form Book* **[A]**. 29 July 1847, inconclusive duel with Lt. Col. John Selden Roane near Fort Smith, neither man hit, no prosecution **[A]**. 1849, admitted before the U.S. Supreme Court **[A]**; that stop's coordinate is the Court's documented 1849 seat, not a Pike-specific address, and is flagged.

- **Contradiction, not resolved.** Bar admission is 1836 in EOA and 1837 in TSHA, neither citing a document. Both years live in the stop's `date_confidence`.
- **CORR.** The court sat in the Old State House, 300 W. Markham St, roughly 34.7480, -92.2757; the gather lens used a point 1.1 km west-southwest. Corrected here and at the 1874 Brooks-Baxter reference.
- **CORR.** TSHA calls the 1846 unit "volunteer cavalry"; Wikipedia has him captain in an Arkansas **mounted infantry** regiment, which the file uses. And at Buena Vista, 22-23 February 1847, the gather lens's coordinate landed southeast of the field; La Angostura is 25.3369, -101.0464.

## 4. The degrees, 1850-1859

1850, raised a Master Mason, helps found St. Johns' College **[A]**; the lodge, traditionally Western Star No. 2, is **[R]** and sits in `date_confidence`, not in prose. 1851, Grand Chapter of Arkansas organized; 1853, Grand High Priest **[A]**. 20 March 1853, Albert G. Mackey communicates the 4th to 32nd degrees at Charleston **[R]**: city and date well attested, building not identified. 1853-1857, New Orleans, law practice and a legislative charter for a New Orleans-to-Pacific railroad never built **[A]**. 1856, walks out of the Know-Nothing national convention at Philadelphia with proslavery Southern delegates **[A]**. 2 January 1859, elected Sovereign Grand Commander, holding it until death **[A]**.

- **CORR, important.** The gather lens's 1853 entry gave the *place* as New Orleans, conflating the Charleston degree-receipt with the New Orleans railroad years; the file splits them. Wikipedia dates the 33rd degree to 25 April 1857 in Louisiana, stated inside the Charleston campa rather than pinned, no venue being known.
- **CORR.** The gather lens placed the 1859 election at **Little Rock**, contradicting its own chronology. The see was Charleston, no Little Rock session is documented, and Little Rock appears to be Pike's residence mistaken for the venue.

## 5. The Confederacy and the Indian Territory, 1861-1864

March 1861, commissioner to the Indian Territory by the Arkansas secession convention, then Confederate commissioner of Indian affairs **[A, OKHS]**. 7 October 1861, John Ross signs the Cherokee treaty at Tahlequah **[A]**, repudiating it in Washington within two years while Stand Watie fights on. 12 July 1862, resigns over Gen. Thomas Hindman's orders sending Indian Territory troops outside the Territory, accepted that November **[A]**. 3 November 1862, arrested at Warren, Texas, charges dropped **[A, TSHA]**, though the coordinate used, Warren in Tyler County, is unconfirmed. 1862-1864, retreat to Greasy Cove; 1864, associate justice of the Confederate-aligned Arkansas Supreme Court **[A]**.

- **CORR, substantive.** The gather lens listed seven nations. The Nebraska portal lists **nine** treaties: Creek; Choctaw and Chickasaw; Seminole; Comanches and other tribes and bands; Comanches of the Prairies and Staked Plain; Osage; Seneca and Seneca-Shawnee; **Quapaw**; Cherokee. The Quapaw treaty and the two separate Comanche treaties were both missing; the file names all nine.
- **Creek treaty, 10 July 1861.** Date **[A]**; the portal gives no signing site, so North Fork Town is conventional and **[R]**.
- **Comanche treaties, 12 August 1861. CORR and a deliberate departure.** The gather lens placed these at "Antelope Hills, 35.42, -99.35"; the hills are at 35.9042, -99.8846, the portal names no place, and the treaties are conventionally placed at the Wichita Agency in the Leased District. The file drops the Antelope Hills attribution rather than moving the pin, pins approximately at the Wichita Agency (35.07, -98.24), and says the place is undocumented. **Weakest coordinate in the file.**
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The brigadier's commission: EOA says **August 1861**, TSHA and Wikipedia say **22 November 1861**. Both stated; November orders the segment only.
- **CORR.** Fort Davis was **not** in the Cherokee Nation. It stood on the south bank of the Arkansas about 2.5 miles northeast of present Muskogee, in Creek country. Pin corrected to roughly 35.77, -95.33, flagged approximate.
- **Pea Ridge, 6-7 March 1862. CORR, substantive.** The gather lens gave the brigade as Cherokee, Choctaw-Chickasaw, and Creek regiments plus a Texas squadron. The American Battlefield Trust names the ~900 men as the **1st and 2nd Cherokee Mounted Rifles (Drew and Stand Watie) and Capt. Otis G. Welch's ~100-man Texas cavalry squadron**. No Choctaw, Chickasaw, or Creek regiment was engaged.
- **Foster's Farm.** At least eight fallen 3rd Iowa cavalrymen scalped and mutilated; the Trust states that **no eyewitness left a record**, and no perpetrator was named. Pike prohibits the practice in writing and court-martials a man for killing a wounded Federal **[A]**.
- **CORR.** The pool's lenses disagreed on Greasy Cove: 34.47, -93.88 in Montgomery County against 34.1, -93.7 in "Pike/Howard County", 45 km apart. Montgomery County is what the EOA chronology supports and what the file uses. The trunk of gold, the jayhawker raid, and the swim across the Little Missouri with the *Morals and Dogma* manuscript are **[R], explicitly legend**: no contemporaneous source, and the book appeared in 1871. Folded into that stop rather than pinned.

## 6. Pardon, Memphis, and the Klan question, 1865-1868

1865, New York then Canada, fearing arrest **[A, TSHA]**; what he did in Canada is a gap. 1867, Memphis, partnership with Gen. Charles W. Adams, editing the *Memphis Daily Appeal* against Congressional Reconstruction and Black suffrage, and on 16 April 1868 printing the editorial that proposes a secret white association for self-defence **[A]**.

- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The pardon. EOA and TSHA: amnesty **30 August 1865**. Wikipedia, after Brown (1997): applied **24 June 1865**, pardoned **23 April 1866**. Both carried; the earlier orders the segment.
- **The Klan claim is [R] and stays [R].** That Nathan Bedford Forrest made Pike the Klan's chief judicial officer or Arkansas Grand Dragon traces to Fleming's 1905 republication of John C. Lester's 1884 reminiscences, to Susan Lawrence Davis (1924), and to Stanley Horn (1939). None cites a contemporary membership record, appointment letter, or the Klan's own 1868 Prescript, and Brown (1997) is sceptical. Documented: the editorial and the politics around it. Not documented: the office. Neither file resolves it in either direction.
- **CORR.** The gather lens dated Pike's *Patriot* editorship in Washington to 1868 while dating his permanent move there to 1870, an internal contradiction. The file puts the editorship inside the post-1870 stop.

## 7. Washington and the book, 1870-1891

1870, moves to Washington permanently, edits the Democratic *Patriot*, practices with former senator Robert Ward Johnson to about 1880, and in 1874 backs Elisha Baxter in the Brooks-Baxter conflict **[A]**. 1871, *Morals and Dogma*, 861 pages, given to Southern Jurisdiction members until 1969 **[A]**. From about 1880, Sanskrit and the Rig-Veda, published posthumously as *Indo-Aryan Deities and Worship* **[A for the study; [R] for the 1930 Wood edition]**. 2 April 1891, dies at the Supreme Council aged 81 of a described progressive esophageal obstruction **[A for the death]**, with no surviving diagnosis or autopsy. Buried at Oak Hill Cemetery after Masonic rites, having asked to be cremated and scattered near acacia trees at the Supreme Council's home **[A for the burial, [R] for the request]**. Oak Hill's own reference material does not mention Pike, so even the burial rests on his biographers, and the day of interment is unrecorded.

- **CORR, and a standing caution.** The Supreme Council's pre-1915 headquarters, where Pike lived, worked, and died, was **not** the present House of the Temple at 1733 16th St NW, built 1911-1915. The gather lens put the 1891 death in that corridor, at 38.9072, -77.0369. The file uses 38.8934, -77.0163, an approximate placement near the Third Street NW premises, no source reached giving the street address.
- **Nine passages verified verbatim** against Project Gutenberg ed. #19447; eight are quoted in the journey file. Two locators corrected: **"Masonry is not a religion..."** to **Ch. X, Illustrious Elect of the Fifteen** (offset 379173), the gather lens's locator having been circular; **"[The Blue Degrees...]"** to **Ch. XXX, Knight Kadosh** (offset 1944652), there being no chapter on the Knights Templar.
- **CORR, the most consequential fix in the pool.** The gather lens said the book "does not clearly mark this bracketed sentence as Pike's own voice versus a source he is compiling." That is backwards. Pike's typography here is consistent: material translated unattributed from Eliphas Levi sits in **quotation marks**, his own interpolations in **square brackets**, and three other bracketed insertions in the same few pages follow the rule. So the most-quoted sentence Pike ever wrote is his own voice, and the borrowing charge holds for the surrounding Levi material but not for that sentence.
- **A contradiction inside the book, not resolved here.** "Masonry is not a religion. He who makes of it a religious belief, falsifies and denaturalizes it" stands against "Every Masonic Lodge is a temple of religion; and its teachings are instruction in religion." Both verbatim in the same volume, and Pike never notes the collision.
- **Late 1880s [R].** Gives Thornton A. Jackson of the Prince Hall-affiliated United Supreme Council a complete set of the rituals while refusing personal recognition of Black Masons as brothers. Dossier citing de Hoyos, *Heredom* 5 (1996); the file's 1887 is an ordering device.

## 8. Afterlife: the hoax, the forgery, the bronze

- **Taxil.** Leo Taxil (Gabriel Jogand-Pages) publicly confessed at Paris on **19 April 1897** that his years of writing on a Palladian Luciferian order directed by Pike from Charleston, and his own Catholic conversion, were fabrication **[A]**. The dossier's venue, the Société de Géographie, is unconfirmed, so the file uses the canonical Paris pin without naming the room. The cult is **[R]** fabrication built on genuine passages using "Lucifer" etymologically, which is why the verified 19th-degree quotation gets its own stop.
- **The Pike-Mazzini letter of 15 August 1871**, forecasting three engineered world wars: **[R], an unlocatable document.** No catalogue number, no manuscript image, no chain of custody at the British Museum or Library where it is said to sit; the three-war script postdates the events it claims to predict; it appears in no authenticated collection. Popularized by William Guy Carr, *Pawns in the Game* (1955). Staged at the Charleston pin because that is where the legend locates Pike.
- **The monument.** Authorized by Congress 9 April 1898, privately funded by Masons, sculpted by Gaetano Trentanove, dedicated **23 October 1901** at 3rd and D Streets NW, Pike in civilian dress and for a century the only outdoor statue of a former Confederate military officer in Washington **[A]**. **CORR:** the gather lens's coordinate was about 280 m north; corrected to 38.8946, -77.0157.
- Toppled, doused with accelerant, and burned **19 June 2020**, wreckage removed by NPS the next day **[A]**, after a 1992 D.C. Council petition, 2017 post-Charlottesville calls, and Norton's 2019 relocation bill. Restored and reinstalled late October 2025 after a presidential executive order. **Contradiction, not resolved:** the gather lens gave 25 October citing an NPS release, Wikipedia gives **27 October**, and the NPS URL returns **HTTP 404**. The file uses 2025-10-27 and states both.
- **Reception.** *Morals and Dogma* reprinted until 1969, superseded by Clausen (1974) and Hutchens (1988), restored **August 2011** as the de Hoyos annotated edition **[A]**. "The Old Canoe", long attributed to Pike and denied by him, is now credited to **Emily Rebecca Page [A]**; not staged as a stop.

## Interlocks used and refused

**Named in a campa**, each on a real relation. **`hiram_abiff`:** Pike took the Master Mason degree in 1850, which stages the Hiram legend with the candidate cast as the corpse, then rewrote thirty degrees and wrote on Hiram at length in *Morals and Dogma*. Named at the 1850 Little Rock stop and at Oak Hill, where the acacia he asked to be scattered under marks the grave in the legend. Canonical Charleston pin **32.7765, -79.9311** inherited byte-identical at both Charleston stops. **`prince_hall`:** Pike gave Thornton A. Jackson of the Prince Hall-affiliated United Supreme Council a complete set of his rituals while refusing to recognize Black Masons as brothers. The relation is to the body descended from Hall's African Lodge, not to Hall, who died two years before Pike was born, and the campa says so. Canonical House of the Temple pin **38.9126, -77.0361** inherited byte-identical.

**Cited only in `suggested_refs`**, the engagement being the other person's: `arthur_edward_waite` (a first draft narrated Waite entering the British Museum Reading Room inside Pike's campa, staging Waite's travel in Pike's file, and it was removed) and `giuseppe_garibaldi` (Mazzini is named in a Pike campa only as the addressee of a document that does not exist).

**Refused**, shared geography not being a relation: `blavatsky`, `rene_guenon` (and the Kaaba pin is deliberately not inherited, since Pike never went), `theodor_reuss` and `cagliostro` (the Yarker patent of 1902, organizationally separate and posthumous), and `john_brown` (an ideological mirror to Pike's 1868 "Southern Brotherhood", but nothing shows Pike writing about Brown by name). Named in campas but not atlas travellers: Mackey, Éliphas Lévi, de Hoyos, Thornton A. Jackson, John Ross, Stand Watie, Mazzini, Taxil.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found, and nothing deleted.** Pike is not in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing in the pool required applying a framework of his backward to material he never treated, or forward past 1891. His comparative method in *Morals and Dogma* reaches backward in the ordinary scholarly sense, but he applied it himself in a published book, so it needs no licence. Two items reach forward from Pike and are legal because they are the later party's engagement recorded as apparatus rather than staged as scenes: de Hoyos's 2011 annotated edition and the 1996 *Heredom* article, both in sources and `suggested_refs`. No stop stages a twentieth-century scholar as a traveller.

## Honest gaps

The Klan office is the largest and the one with the most riding on it: no primary record in either direction. Then the Supreme Council's Third Street NW address; the 12 August 1861 Comanche treaty site; the Warren, Texas identification; the day of interment at Oak Hill; the cause of death; the 1930 Wood edition; the Gloucester and Fairhaven teaching posts; the Taxil venue; and the marriage, thirty years leaving almost no record from the man who wrote about everything else.
