# Augustus Henry Julian Le Plongeon (1825-1908): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-22 for `augustus_henry_julian_le_plongeon.journey.json`. Queue row: QUEUE.md line 425.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · contradictions are flagged and left open, never adjudicated.

A caution governing the whole file. The reachable biographical record is thin and derivative: two encyclopedia articles plus the chacmool article, all resting heavily on Le Plongeon's own published self-account. The most colorful details (the shipwreck, the Fox Talbot tutelage) have no reachable independent corroboration and are tagged **[R]** even where the encyclopedia states them flatly. The primary texts are the exception: *Sacred Mysteries* (1886) and *Queen Moo and the Egyptian Sphinx* (1896) are public domain and were quoted verbatim.

## 1. Jersey and the wreck, 1825-1849

- Born **4 May 1825 on the Island of Jersey**, Channel Islands. **[A, Wikipedia "Augustus Le Plongeon"]** No parish, house, officiant, or mother's name reachable. Gap.
- At about nineteen he **sailed for South America and was shipwrecked off Chile**, settling at Valparaíso. **[R]** The encyclopedia states it flatly; no ship name, month, or position exists anywhere reachable. The file's coordinate (-33.5, -72.0) is an open-water approximation and says so.
- **Taught mathematics, drawing, and languages** at a college in Valparaíso. **[A]** Year from his stated age; no month.

**Flagged gap.** The QUEUE row calls him "French-American" and the interlock lens asserts that "he trained and lived in France before emigrating," offering the canonical Paris pin (48.8566, 2.3522). **Nothing in either research pool supports a French residence or training.** Birth is on Jersey; the next attested location is Valparaíso. No Paris stop was written, and the reason is recorded rather than passed over.

## 2. California and the camera, 1849-1862

- **San Francisco, 1849**, surveyor and medical apprentice during the gold rush. **[A]** No month.
- **Marysville town plan. [A, date corrected.]** The Le Plongeon article dates the survey **1851**; the Marysville article says "In 1850, Covillaud, Ramirez, Sampson, and Sicard hired Augustus Le Plongeon, a French surveyor, to create a plan for a town." **1851 is Marysville's incorporation year, not the survey year.** The file takes 1850 and flags the conflation.
- He **took his fee in land deeds**, which later funded the expeditions. **[A]** The load-bearing economic fact of the whole life, stated in one uncited sentence, and exactly the self-reported kind that wants a primary check.
- **The Great Exhibition and Fox Talbot. [R.]** Wikipedia gives **no year at all** for the England trip, and the Fox Talbot sentence is uncited, tracing to Le Plongeon's own telling. **1851** is defensible only because the Exhibition ran 1 May to 15 October 1851. Downgraded from [A].
- **Tropical photographic trials** at St. Thomas, Mexico, Australia, China, and the Pacific Islands, and a **daguerreotype studio in San Francisco** in the 1850s. **[A as to the fact, R as to every date and any itinerary.]** No notebook, no sequence, no years; the 1855 and 1856 dates order the segment and say so.

## 3. Peru, Bolivia, and the earthquake, 1862-1870

- **Lima, 1862**: photographic studio and alternative-medicine clinic. **[A]**
- **Eight years photographing ancient ruins including Tiahuanaco. [A as to the photographing, corrected as to geography.]** The pool's "eight years in Peru photographing ruins including Tiwanaku" **annexes a Bolivian site to Peru.** Tiwanaku (-16.5545, -68.6738) is on the Bolivian altiplano near Lake Titicaca. The file keeps the coordinate, names the right country, and flags the annexation.
- **The 1868 Arica earthquake. [R, downgraded from [A].]** Wikipedia says only that he "observed" it and documented the damage, and **the earthquake's own article does not mention him at all.** The pool had him "witnessing and later interviewing survivors"; both are unsupported embellishments and were cut. The file says the destruction reached his notice and that how close he stood is unrecorded.
- **Two anti-Jesuit books. [A, dates corrected.]** Not both 1867: *La religion de Jesus comparada con las ensenanzas de la Iglesia* (1867) and *Los Jesuitas y el Peru* (1869). The file separates them.
- **Lectures at the California Academy of Sciences, 1870. [A as to the event, coordinate corrected.]** The pool's 37.7793, -122.4193 lands near Franklin and Hayes and matches no Academy site in any period. Its first museum opened **1874 at California and Dupont (now Grant Avenue)**, approx. **37.7920, -122.4058**; Market Street 1891, Golden Gate Park only 1916. The file uses the downtown pin and records that the 1870 lecture room is unidentified.

## 4. London, Alice Dixon, the marriage, 1871-1873

- **British Museum, 1871**, Mesoamerican manuscripts. **[A]**
- **Meeting Alice Dixon in London, 1871. [A as to city and year; the PLACE is contradicted and left open.]** One account puts it at a **spiritualist seance**; another says "while he studied at the British Museum"; the Augustus article says only "Met Alice Dixon in London." The Museum as meeting place is an inference. The file writes both versions into the campa and declines to choose.
- **Alice Dixon born 12 December 1851 in London** to photographer Henry Dixon and Sophia Cook. **[A]**
- **Married in New York, 1873. [A]** The pool calls Alice "25 years his junior"; **the gap is 26 years**.

## 5. Yucatán: arrival, Uxmal, Chichen Itza, 1873-1876

- **Arrival in Mérida, 1873; Alice contracts yellow fever; both later learn Yucatec Maya. [A]**
- **Port of entry: [R].** No source names it. Progreso was Yucatán's working port in 1873 and is the file's declared guess.
- **First visit to Uxmal, 1873. [A, and a manufactured contradiction dismissed.]** The pool tagged this [R] on the ground that the two articles disagree, one dating the visit 1873 and the other 1875. **That disagreement does not exist.** The Alice Dixon article places the "short visit to Uxmal" immediately after the 1873 arrival and recovery, and neither article says 1875. The file dates it 1873.
- **Chichen Itza, 1875**, reached under **military escort** (the Caste War had run since 1847); systematic **wet collodion** documentation begun. **[A]**
- **The Chacmool excavated, November 1875. [A]** One real discrepancy: the Chacmool article says the statue was "buried beneath the Platform of the Eagles and the Jaguars," while both Le Plongeon articles say "near" it. Recorded, not resolved.
- **Coordinates at Chichen Itza, corrected.** The pool's 20.6843, -88.5678 for the Platform of the Eagles and Jaguars lands roughly 200 m ENE of it, nearer the Platform of Venus. The file puts the Eagles and Jaguars at **20.6836, -88.5696** (approximate: no per-structure coordinate is published) and reuses the pool's coordinate for the **Platform of Venus**, which is what it sits on.
- **El Caracol dropped.** The pool carried an [R] stop, "Alice conducting astronomical observations at the round tower known as the Caracol," at 20.6811, -88.5673. Two faults: the coordinate is about 400 m off (El Caracol is at 20.6792, -88.5707), and **no source consulted mentions the Caracol or any astronomical work by Alice.** That is invention, not reconstruction, and [R] does not license it. Removed rather than reworded.
- **Pyramid of the Magician, Uxmal, photographed. [R]**, dated 1876 to order the segment.
- **Centennial Exhibition, Philadelphia, 1876: permission to display the Chacmool refused. [A as to petition and refusal; his own presence on the grounds is NOT attested.]** The stop is kept on the model of `abdelkader.journey.json`'s Worcester handling, with `date_confidence` saying so.

## 6. The peninsula, the seizure, the search for money, 1873-1884

- **The secondary sites**: Izamal, Isla Mujeres, Cozumel, Cancún, Aké, plus Belize. **[A as a set.]** Wikipedia dates the whole group "From 1873 to 1884". **The pool gave every one of them an invented 1876-1878 window.** No per-site year exists in any reachable source. The file spreads them across the attested span in travel order, each `date_confidence` saying the year orders the segment and is not a claim.
- **Aké, coordinate corrected.** The pool's 20.9989, -89.6167 sits under a kilometre from Mérida cathedral. Aké is about **40 km east, in Tixkokob**, approx. **20.950, -89.300**: an error of roughly 33 km.
- **Cozumel, corrected and downgraded.** San Gervasio is at **20.5004, -86.8483**, not the pool's 20.3958, -86.9328 (about 14 km off). The source names only "Cozumel," so San Gervasio is an inference: **[R]**.
- **Cancún, corrected, approximate, downgraded.** The pool's 21.0819, -86.7716 falls in the northern hotel zone; El Rey stands at Blvd. Kukulcán km 17-18, approx. **21.052, -86.779**. No article publishes a coordinate, so this is unconfirmed against INAH and flagged. "El Rey" is likewise inferred from a generic "Cancún": **[R]**.
- **Belize / British Honduras, 1878. [A]**
- **The Chacmool seized, 1877. [A]** Yucatán took it to Mérida, then handed it to the federal government, which moved it to Mexico City.
- **Mexico City residence, dates corrected.** The pool gave 1877-1884; Alice's article says "From 1880 to 1884 the Le Plongeons spent time in Mexico City." **1877 is the seizure year, not the residence.**
- **Mexico City, coordinate corrected.** The nineteenth-century **Museo Nacional stood on Calle de Moneda beside the National Palace**, approx. **19.4340, -99.1306**. The pool's 19.4342, -99.1386 lies about 850 m west; the afterlife lens's 19.426, -99.186 is the **Chapultepec Museum of Anthropology, opened 1964**, a different institution.
- **Elected to the American Antiquarian Society, 1878. [A as to the election, [R] as to any travel, which no source records.]** Coordinate corrected to **42.2772, -71.8108**, Salisbury Street, but **that building was completed only in 1910**, so the pin is anachronistic for 1878, when the Society sat at Lincoln Square. Both facts are in `date_confidence`.

## 7. The books and the theory, 1881-1902

- ***Vestiges of the Mayas*, New York, 1881. [A as to place and date, gloss corrected.]** The pool called it "laying out his theory connecting Maya civilization to Ancient Egypt and Atlantis." **It does not.** It is a short pamphlet reporting the Yucatán fieldwork; the theory belongs to *Sacred Mysteries* (1886) and *Queen Moo* (1896). Gloss stripped.
- ***Sacred Mysteries Among the Mayas and the Quiches, 11,500 Years Ago*, New York, 1886**, same year as Alice's *Here and There in Yucatan*. **[A]**
- ***Queen Moo and the Egyptian Sphinx*, 1896. [A]** Title and date come from the standard bibliography by way of the interlock lens rather than an encyclopedia article, so the citation is weaker than for the other two; the text itself was quoted directly.
- **The Mu derivation. [R]** He took the name **Mu** from **Charles Étienne Brasseur de Bourbourg's 1864 misreading of the Madrid Codex** and placed the land in the **Atlantic**.
- **Direction of influence, corrected.** The pool says "Ignatius Donnelly and Helena Blavatsky both draw on Le Plongeon's research." **This is at least partly backwards.** The Mu article says he placed Mu in the Atlantic **"following Ignatius Donnelly in *Atlantis: The Antediluvian World* (1882)."** They were mutually citing contemporaries; **Blavatsky's use of him stands one-directionally.** The file writes Donnelly as a source he followed, Blavatsky as a reader who kept citing him.
- **Marginalization among archaeologists. [R]** Reported without dates; 1890 orders the segment.
- **Alice's *Queen Moo's Talisman*, New York, 1902. [A]**

## 8. Death and afterlife

- **Died Brooklyn, 13 December 1908, aged 83**, after a decline through that year. **[A]**
- **Burial: GAP.** No source documents a cemetery, grave, or marker. Nothing invented; the stop says the record does not say.
- **Alice: diagnosed with breast cancer February 1910, died 8 June 1910 at the New York Women's Hospital. [A]** **Age corrected: 58, not 59**, born 12 December 1851. The stray "age 59" contradicts the birth date in her own article.
- ***A Dream of Atlantis*, corrected.** Serialized in *The World Magazine* **1909-1911**, not 1909 alone; begun after his death, finished after hers.
- **The archive at the Getty Research Institute, Los Angeles. [A as to the holding; no acquisition date reachable.]** The closing stop's 1980 date is an ordering device and says so.
- **James Churchward, *The Lost Continent of Mu, the Motherland of Man* (1926)**, moving the continent from Atlantic to Pacific and recasting Mu as 64 million people in seven cities. **[A]**

## Interlocks written into the journey file

Two, both grounded in a relation Le Plongeon himself stands in, not in shared geography.

1. **`blavatsky`**, in "New York, the theory against the field." **[A]** She drew on his Maya material for Theosophy's lost-continent doctrine, and *Isis Unveiled* (1877) falls inside his working life. A real one-directional relation, contemporaneous, no block-rule problem.
2. **`ignatius_loyola_donnelly`**, in "New York, the name Mu." **[A]** Not merely a later borrower: **Le Plongeon followed Donnelly** in siting Mu in the Atlantic after *Atlantis: The Antediluvian World* (1882). This is the interlock QUEUE.md line 425 names. **Operator note: no `ignatius_loyola_donnelly.journey.json` exists yet**, so the slug is a dangling pointer; the campa names the man, not a file, so nothing breaks, but the pair wants re-reading once Donnelly is researched.

Named in campas correctly and not as interlocks, being people in his life rather than atlas travelers: **Alice Dixon Le Plongeon**, **Charles Étienne Brasseur de Bourbourg**, **William Henry Fox Talbot** ([R]), **James Churchward** (posthumous).

## Honest gaps

1. **Burial place.** Unknown. No cemetery invented.
2. **Any French period.** Asserted by the interlock lens, unsupported by every source consulted.
3. **The shipwreck** (no ship, no date, no position) and **the Fox Talbot tutelage** (uncited, self-reported, undated).
4. **Per-site dates for six of the seven secondary Yucatán sites.** Only the 1873-1884 span exists.
5. **Addresses.** The 1870 Academy lecture room, the Lima studio, the Brooklyn house, the church of the 1873 marriage, and the Getty acquisition date. None reachable.
6. **The Marysville land deeds** as funding source: stated once, uncited, load-bearing.
7. **Beneath or near.** Whether the Chacmool lay beneath or merely near the Platform of the Eagles and Jaguars: contradicted, left open.
