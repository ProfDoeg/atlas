# Alphonse Mucha (1860-1939): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-16 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Alfons Maria Mucha, born Ivancice, Moravia; died Prague. Journey file: `alphonse_mucha.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition not resolved to a document · *Flagged* sources disagree, recorded and not adjudicated.

The research pool arrived carrying its own verification pass, and many entries were marked CORRECTED. Those corrections are itemised below: several move a coordinate by tens of kilometres or a date by three years, and the pool's chronology lens and geography lens contradict each other in at least four places.

---

## 1. Moravia, 1860-1878

**[A: English Wikipedia, *Alphonse Mucha*; Mucha Foundation timeline]** Born 24 July 1860 at Ivancice, a Moravian market town in the Austrian Empire. Father a court usher, mother formerly a governess. **[A: Mucha Foundation]** A Crucifixion drawing survives from about 1868, made at eight, the earliest known work.

*Coordinate corrected.* The pool's birthplace pin (49.0958, 16.3776) falls about six hundred metres south of the town; Palackeho namesti is at approximately **49.1010, 16.3768**, which the file uses.

**[A: Mucha Foundation]** 1872: a choral scholarship takes him to the Gymnazium Slovanske in Brno, singing in the choir of Saints Peter and Paul.

*Correction, factual core.* The common tradition, repeated by The Art Story and by the pool's chronology entry (dated there to 1870), makes **Leos Janacek** the choirmaster who took Mucha in. English Wikipedia says the reverse: the choirmaster was **Pavel Krizkovsky**, who "was impressed by his talent, but was not able to admit and fund him, as he had just admitted another talented young musician, Leos Janacek." Janacek was a fellow chorister, not a teacher; the file follows Wikipedia and dates the Brno years 1872.

**[A: quoted verbatim on English Wikipedia, cited there to Tomoko Sato, *Mucha* (Taschen, 2015), p. 9]** "For me, the notions of painting, going to church, and music are so closely knit that often I cannot decide whether I like church for its music, or music for its place in the mystery which it accompanies."

*Source note.* The pool's quote lens claimed Wikipedia's citation for this line could not be traced. Wrong: the article carries `{{Sfn|Sato|2015|page=9}}` (ISBN 978-3-8365-5009-3, catalogued at Open Library as *Mucha*, so *The Artist as Visionary* may be a cover subtitle). **Gap:** the page was never opened, Google Books and HathiTrust being blocked, so the line is verified as Wikipedia's text only.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 1878: rejected by the Academy of Fine Arts in Prague. **[R]** The advice to find another career is reported, not documented.

*Dropped stop.* The pool carried a **Battle of Koniggratz** stop for 1866, its own note conceding that "near his hometown" is false: Hradec Kralove is roughly 150 km away, in Bohemia, and Mucha was five. Its coordinates were the city centre, not the battlefield at Chlum/Sadova. No stop is written: no source places the child anywhere during the Prussian advance.

## 2. Vienna and the patronage, 1880-1885

**[A: English Wikipedia; Mucha Foundation]** 1880: apprentice scenery painter with Kautsky-Brioschi-Burghart in Vienna (the pool spells the third name both ways; both circulate).

**[A: Mucha Foundation]** 8 December 1881: the Ringtheater fire kills close to four hundred people and destroys the firm's largest client. **[R]** on the chain to Mucha's dismissal: standard in every biography consulted, no employment record seen.

*Flagged: a date collision left standing.* Wikipedia dates the move to **Mikulov** to 1881, the same year as a fire of 8 December; both fit only if the move falls in the last weeks of the year. The file dates the stop 1881-01-01 and states the strain in `date_confidence`.

**[A: Mucha Foundation; English Wikipedia]** Patronage of Count Khuen Belasi, beginning with murals at **Emmahof**.

*Place and coordinate corrected.* The pool put Emmahof "near Hrusovany u Brna" at 48.9686, 16.4372. Emmahof (Czech **Emin zamek**) is not near Brno: it stands in forest on the Jevisovka about 4 km west of **Hrusovany nad Jevisovkou**, in **Sanov**, Znojmo district (Czech Wikipedia). Roughly 17 km off. The file uses **48.8283, 16.3569**.

*Flagged.* English Wikipedia calls him **Eduard** Khuen Belasi; the pool's geography lens and Czech sources call him **Karl**; the file writes no given name. The first Emmahof commission is 1882 in the Mucha Foundation timeline and 1883 in the pool chronology; the file uses 1882.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** The commission extends to **Gandegg Castle**, Tyrol. *Coordinate corrected:* Schloss Gandegg is in the Pigeno district of Eppan, South Tyrol, at 46°26'59" N, 11°15'11" E = **46.4497, 11.2531** (German Wikipedia). The pool's pin fell about 13 km short.

## 3. Munich, 1885-1887

**[A: English Wikipedia; Mucha Foundation]** September 1885: enrolled at the Munich Academy on Belasi's money; elected chairman of the **Skreta Group** of Czech and Slavic students.

**[A: quoted on English Wikipedia, cited to Philippe Thiebaut, *Mucha et l'Art Nouveau* (2018), p. 22]** "Here I am in my new element, painting. I cross all sorts of currents, but without effort, and even with joy. Here, for the first time, I can find the objectives to reach which used to seem inaccessible."

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 1886: commissioned to paint Saints Cyril and Methodius for a Czech immigrant church at **Pisek, North Dakota**. **[A]** He does not travel there.

*Coordinate corrected, and the stop reassigned.* Pisek is in Walsh County at 48.31056 N, 97.71028 W; the pool's pin was about 100 km south. More to the point, the stop does not belong at Pisek at all: Mucha was in Munich. The file places the commission there and puts the Dakota church in `suggested_refs`.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 1887: Belasi agrees to fund the move to Paris.

## 4. Paris, the poor years, 1887-1894

**[A: English Wikipedia; Mucha Foundation]** 1888 Academie Julian (Lefebvre, Boulanger, Laurens); lodgings at 1 rue Bara; 1889 Academie Colarossi; Belasi's subsidy ends about then and Mucha lives by magazine illustration. *Coordinate corrected (minor):* the Colarossi at 10 rue de la Grande-Chaumiere is at **48.8422, 2.3308**, 150 m east of the pool's pin, an address documented as the school's from 1879 into the 1930s.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 22 May 1890: Maupassant cover for *La Vie populaire*. 23 January 1892: Franco-Prussian War cover for *Le Petit Francais illustre*. 1894: four illustrations at the Paris Salon, medal of honour.

*Flagged, and resolved against the pool itself.* The pool's chronology dates the studio shared with **Gauguin** and the occult evenings with **Strindberg** to **1890**. English Wikipedia dates the studio to summer **1893**, on Gauguin's return from Tahiti, and the Strindberg friendship to late autumn **1894**; the pool's own geography lens also says 1893. The file uses 1893 and records the conflict.

## 5. Gismonda and the poster decade, 1894-1902

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 26 December 1894: Bernhardt's demand for a poster for the continuing run of *Gismonda* reaches Mucha at the Lemercier shop, the regular artists being away. 1 January 1895: it goes up and causes an immediate sensation; Bernhardt orders four thousand copies and signs him for six years.

*Flagged: two atlas pins for one theatre.* The pool's geography lens gives the Theatre de la Renaissance as 48.8687, 2.3572; the interlock lens, quoting `joffrey_rudel.journey.json`, gives **48.8706, 2.3554**. Per the inheritance rule the file uses the canonical pin, and the Gismonda-on-the-walls stop inherits the canonical Paris pin **48.8566, 2.3522** byte-identically.

**[A: English Wikipedia; Mucha Foundation]** 1896: studio on the rue du Val-de-Grace; *The Seasons* for Champenois.

*Corrected.* The pool's chronology dates the **448-work Salon des Cent retrospective to 1896**. Wikipedia dates his invitation to that gallery to 1896 and the retrospective to **1897**, as does the pool's geography lens. The file uses 1897 and folds in the 107-work Bodiniere show of the same year.

**[A: quoted on English Wikipedia, cited to Thiebaut (2018), p. 111]** "I had not found any real satisfaction in my old kind of work. I saw that my way was to be found elsewhere, little bit higher."

**[A: English Wikipedia]** January 1898: joins a lodge of the Grand Orient de France. 20 December 1899: *Le Pater* published in 510 copies, a work he considered among his most important; its three-page-per-petition structure is described by Mucha in a passage from Thiebaut p. 111, used verbatim as that stop's quote.

**[A: Thiebaut (2018), p. 111, quoted on English Wikipedia]** The critic **Charles Masson** on *Le Pater*: "There is in that man a visionary; it is the work of an imagination not suspected by those who only know his talent for the agreeable and charming." Carried in `suggested_refs`: Masson's reading is Masson's act.

*Corrected.* The **serpent bracelet** made with Georges Fouquet for Bernhardt is **1899**, not 1900 (1900 is the Cascade pendant). The **shop interior** at 6 rue Royale is completed 1901, dismantled 1923 **[A]**; *Documents Decoratifs*, 72 plates, 1902 **[A]**.

*Corrected: the wrong side of the river.* The pool put the **Bosnia-Herzegovina Pavilion** of 1900 on the **Champ de Mars**. It stood on the **Rue des Nations**, the row of national pavilions along the Quai d'Orsay (English Wikipedia, *Exposition Universelle (1900)*, which also names the architect Karl Panek and confirms Mucha's murals). The file uses approximately **48.8630, 2.3080**.

**[A: Sato (2015), p. 46, cited on English Wikipedia]** Bernhardt describes him publicly as "a Czech from Moravia not only by birth and origin, but also by feeling, by conviction and by patriotism."

**[A: Mucha Foundation]** 1902: travels to Prague and through Moravia with **Auguste Rodin**.

## 6. America, 1904-1910

**[A: English Wikipedia]** March 1904: first of five American trips, each five to six months, seeking funding and portrait commissions; Bernhardt's tours have already made him a celebrity there.

*Corrected twice on one entry.* The pool's **New York School of Applied Design for Women** stop used 40.7614, -73.9776 and added a friendship with President Theodore Roosevelt. (a) The school's address was 160 Lexington Avenue (40.7439, -73.9822), but that building went up in 1908-09 and the school occupied it from 1909, so the 1905 premises cannot be pinned; the file uses the Lexington coordinates and says so. (b) The Roosevelt friendship appears nowhere in the Wikipedia article and is dropped, not demoted.

*Corrected, register.* The pool wrote that "the couple relocates to Chicago" 1906-1909. Wikipedia describes a **visiting professorship** at the Art Institute during trips of five or six months: repeat visitor, not resident.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 10 June 1906: marries Marie (Maruska) Chytilova in Prague. 1908: three allegorical murals for the German Theatre, Irving Place, New York. 15 March 1909: daughter Jaroslava born in New York (Czech Wikipedia, *Jaroslava Muchova*).

*Flagged: when did Crane agree?* Wikipedia's Mucha article dates **Charles Richard Crane**'s agreement to fund the Slav Epic to **February 1910**; its *Slav Epic* article attaches it to **1909**. The file dates the stop 1910-02-01 and names the conflict rather than choosing silently.

## 7. The Slav Epic, 1909-1928

**[A: Mucha Foundation]** 1909: first sketches at Rosice, Moravia, between American trips.

**[A: quoted on English Wikipedia, cited to Sato (2015), p. 73]** "I will be able to do something really good, not just for the art critic but for our Slav souls."

**[A: English Wikipedia, *The Slav Epic*]** 1910-1928: studio at **Zbiroh Castle**, western Bohemia; canvases painted 1912-1926. *Coordinate corrected:* the castle sits just west of the town centre at approximately **49.8636, 13.7675**; the pool's pin was about 2 km north-west, in forest.

*Corrected: canvas dimensions.* The pool said the twenty canvases were "each roughly six by eight meters." That is the size of the **largest**; the cycle uses several formats. The file describes the hall as able to take a canvas six by eight, true of the room without overstating the set.

*Corrected: a room that does not exist.* The pool had Mucha at the Municipal House "working alongside composer Josef Suk's music room downstairs." Wikipedia's article on the building records no Suk room; its named halls are the Smetana Hall and the Lord Mayor's (Primatorsky) Hall. Cut.

**[A: English Wikipedia, *The Slav Epic*]** Research travel to Russia, Poland and the Balkans, "including the Orthodox Christian monasteries of Mount Athos." **[A: Mucha Foundation]** Moscow and Versailles, 1913.

*Stops dropped or folded.* The pool's **Dalmatian coast** stop for 1912 was tagged [R] and admitted that no port is named and the coordinates were simply centred on Dubrovnik; it is folded into the Mount Athos campa as part of the same Balkan travel, as is Versailles. Both are real trips and can be restored on expansion.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 12 March 1915: son Jiri Mucha born in Prague.

## 8. The republic, the Gestapo, the grave, 1918-1939

**[A: English Wikipedia; The Art Story]** 1918: designs the first Czechoslovak postage stamps and banknotes without payment, including the 100-koruna note carrying his portrait of **Josephine Crane Bradley** as Slavia.

*Corrected by subtraction.* The pool added the **state coat of arms**, which is not attested in the Wikipedia text fetched. Widespread, possibly true, not carried into the file: a live gap rather than a refutation.

*Corrected: a misleading understatement.* The pool called the 1919 Prague showing "the first lifetime exhibition" of the Slav Epic. Per English Wikipedia, the **Klementinum** exhibition showed the **first eleven canvases** of a cycle nine short of finished.

**[R: The Art Story]** The 1921 American tour is reported as drawing around 50,000 visitors a week at some venues, carried with that hedge. **Gap:** nothing establishes whether Mucha himself crossed for it, so no 1921 American stop is written.

**[A: The Art Story]** 1926: final canvas, *The Apotheosis of the Slavs*. **[A: English Wikipedia]** 1928: the cycle donated to Prague on the tenth anniversary of independence.

**[A: English Wikipedia; The Art Story]** 1931: stained glass of Saints Cyril and Methodius for St Vitus, funded by the Slavia insurance company. 1936: retrospective of 139 works at the Jeu de Paume, three Slav Epic canvases among them, and the unfinished triptych *The Age of Reason, Wisdom and Love* begun. March 1939: among the first arrested and interrogated by the Gestapo, as a figure of Slavic nationalism and Freemasonry. 14 July 1939: dies of pneumonia, ten days short of 79, and is buried at the **Slavin**, Vysehrad, in defiance of a ban on public gatherings.

*Flagged, minor.* The pool gives two Slavin coordinates about 70 m apart, 50.0645, 14.4192 and 50.0651, 14.4188; the file uses the first. No source gives the burial day; the file dates it 1939-07-16 and says so.

## 9. Afterlife of the work, not staged as stops

None of this is a place Mucha stood, so none of it is a stop. It sits in `suggested_refs` and in the final campa.

**[A: English Wikipedia, *The Slav Epic*]** The cycle is wrapped and hidden from Nazi seizure; after 1948 the regime dismisses him as decadent and bourgeois and shelves the promised pavilion. Some 25 years in storage, then to the chateau at **Moravsky Krumlov**, on display from **1963**. *Corrected:* the display ran until **2011**, not 2012; the chateau is at approximately 49.0500, 16.3086, about 900 m south of the pool's pin.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 2010-2012: Prague's campaign to recover the cycle, opposed by the Mucha Foundation. 2012-2016 at the Veletrzni Palace, then an Asian tour, back in Prague 2019, reported since at Moravsky Krumlov, with a permanent Prague home targeted for 2026.

**[A: English Wikipedia, *Mucha Museum*]** 1998: the Mucha Museum opens at Kounice Palace, a venture of Sebastian Pawlowski and the Foundation. May 2024: the Foundation cuts ties, alleging unpaid millions of koruna and conditions risking the paintings, and the Kounice displays are replaced with Ivan Lendl's collection. 14 February 2025: a new museum opens at **Savarin Palace**. Who may use the name in Prague is unresolved.

**[R]** The San Francisco psychedelic poster scene of 1966-1972 is universally credited with reviving Mucha's vocabulary, but the articles checked name only "Art Nouveau", never Mucha. Not written into the file.

## Interlocks

Named inside campas, on the rule that shared geography is never enough:

- **`paul_berthon`** **[A: `paul_berthon.journey.json`]**. His file states that Mucha's Bernhardt poster for *La Princesse Lointaine* "has already settled how the age will draw an actress" before Berthon paints the same subject in 1901: influence running one way, from Mucha to Berthon, and the atlas's only explicit named mention of him. Named in the Gismonda-on-the-walls campa.
- **`auguste_rodin`** **[A: Mucha Foundation]**. The 1902 journey through Prague and Moravia is Mucha showing Rodin his own country. Direct and mutual.

Deliberately **not** named, though the interlock lens surfaced them: `eugene_grasset`, `joffrey_rudel`, `jules_barbier`, `clotilde_sakharoff`, `george_balanchine`, `sergei_diaghilev`, `artaud`, `andre_breton`. Grasset (predecessor and rival in the same poster trade, with Jules Chéret, who has no journey file) is real but not co-location and not attested personal acquaintance, so per the rule above his name was correctly kept out of any campa; an earlier pass in this file's own drafting mistakenly claimed he'd been named in the Lemercier campa; corrected here. The rest are Bernhardt-adjacent or venue-name overlaps, several decades after Mucha's death, and the map already shows co-location.

## Honest gaps

1. **No Argentina connection.** QUEUE.md records "No documented Argentina work found" and nothing here contradicts it. Mucha enters as aesthetic ancestry; no link was manufactured.
2. **Sato and Thiebaut were never opened.** Their quotations are verified only as far as Wikipedia's rendering, with sfn page numbers recorded.
3. **The 1905 New York teaching address cannot be pinned** (section 6).
4. **Whether Mucha crossed with the Slav Epic in 1921** is unestablished.
5. **The 1918 coat of arms** is unattested in the source consulted despite being repeated everywhere.
6. **No Czech-language monograph was consulted**, including Jiri Mucha's biographies of his father, which underlie the anecdotal layer of every English account used here. The largest gap in this file.
