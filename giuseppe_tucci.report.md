# Giuseppe Tucci (1894-1984): research report
*2026-08-08. Orientalist, Tibetologist, archaeologist; founder and president of IsMEO. Born Macerata, died San Polo dei Cavalieri. The evidence dossier behind `giuseppe_tucci.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or route inference · *contested* = two reachable sources disagree and the disagreement is left standing.

**Method.** Two encyclopedic backbones were set against each other: the Treccani *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani* entry, and the Italian and English Wikipedia articles. Where they conflict on a year, both years are printed below and the conflict is carried into the journey file as a `date_confidence` string. Two of Tucci's own books were reached in full text on archive.org and quoted directly. The Italian archival material was not reachable and is listed as a gap. Where an earlier pass carried an error that verification caught, error and correction are both recorded under **Corrections**.

---

## 1. Macerata and the classical training, 1894-1919

- Born **5 June 1894 at Macerata**, in the Marche. **[A: Wikipedia (en/it) and Treccani DBI, same date.]**
- Enrolled 1907 at the **Liceo Classico Giacomo Leopardi**; diploma **25 June 1912**. **[A: it.wikipedia for the enrolment year, Treccani for the diploma date.]** The school is **in Macerata**, not Ascoli Piceno (C1).
- Self-taught in Hebrew, Chinese and Sanskrit before university. Published **Latin inscriptions in a German scholarly journal in 1911**. **[A: en.wikipedia.]** That article's "aged 18" is wrong: born June 1894, he was seventeen through most of 1911.
- **March 1913**, enrolled in classical philology at the University of Rome. **[A: Treccani.]**
- **12 July 1919**, doctorate with highest honours, thesis on the history of studies in oriental philosophy, the course interrupted by the First World War. **[A: Treccani for the exact date; en.wikipedia corroborates the year.]**
- Taught at the **Liceo-Ginnasio Stabili, Ascoli Piceno**. *Contested*: Treccani dates the post to 1920 and calls it a matter of months; it.wikipedia gives 1919-1921. Not adjudicated; 1920 used in the journey file with the range flagged. Married **Rosa Di Benedetto**, 1920. **[A: Treccani.]**

## 2. The first Roman decade, 1921-1929

- **January 1921**, secretary of the library of the Chamber of Deputies. **[A: Treccani; it.wikipedia gives the year only, with the range 1921-1930.]**
- **1921**, founds the journal *Alle fonti delle religioni*; **1922**, *Storia della filosofia cinese antica*; **1923**, *libera docenza* in Far Eastern languages and literatures, and the birth of his son **Ananda**; **1924**, teaching Indian and Far Eastern religions and philosophies at the University of Rome. **[A: Treccani for all four.]**
- **1925**: *Alle fonti delle religioni* is **absorbed into Raffaele Pettazzoni's** *Studi e materiali di Storia delle religioni*. **[A: Treccani for the merger; it.wikipedia for Pettazzoni as sole founder of the receiving journal, 1925.]** Tucci was **not** a co-founder of Pettazzoni's review (Corrections, C2).
- **1927**, placed on leave from the University of Rome and assigned to the Foreign Ministry. **[R: it.wikipedia only, no second source reached.]** Second marriage, to **Giulia Nuvoloni**, after annulment of the first, 1927. **[A: Treccani.]**
- **1929**, elected academician of the Royal Academy of Italy. **[A: Treccani, corroborated by it.wikipedia.]**

## 3. India and the Himalayan frontier, 1925-1930

- Departs for India with **Carlo Formichi**, **November 1925**; roughly five and a half continuous years in Asia. **[A: Treccani for the departure month, it.wikipedia for the 1925-1930 span.]**
- **Visva-Bharati, Santiniketan**: teaches Italian and Chinese, studies Buddhism, Tibetan and Bengali. Also teaches at **Dacca (Dhaka)**, **Benares** and **Calcutta**. **[A: en.wikipedia.]** The year assigned to each campus in the journey file is **inferred**: the sources give a circuit, not a timetable.
- **1929, Calcutta**: meets **Mircea Eliade** at the house of **Surendranath Dasgupta**. **[A: it.wikipedia.]**
- **Kashmir**, later 1920s; **Darjeeling**, February-March 1930; **Leh, Ladakh**, June-September 1930, second visit; **Gangtok/Sikkim**, two visits c.1926-1935. **[A: it.wikipedia for all four.]**
- **Kalimpong** and **Yatung (Chumbi valley)** are **[R]**: no consulted source names Tucci at either. Both are route inferences from the standard Sikkim-Tibet caravan traffic, and both are marked as reconstructions in the journey file.

## 4. Naples, IsMEO, and the western Tibet campaigns, 1930-1935

- Chair of **Chinese language and literature at the Naples Oriental Institute**, Italy's first. *Contested*: Treccani dates the appointment **1930**, en.wikipedia puts his return and the chair at **1931**. Left open.
- Expedition sequence. it.wikipedia: **July 1931, June 1933, 1935, 1937, 1939**, and numbers the July 1931 trip the **third** of his career (which is what makes the 1948 Lhasa journey the **eighth**). en.wikipedia instead groups the western Tibet and Nepal expeditions as **1931, 1933 and 1935**. Not reconciled (Corrections, C3).
- **Kathmandu valley**, four pre-war visits: 1929, November 1931, October 1933, 1935, Swayambhunath among the sites surveyed. **[A: it.wikipedia.]**
- **16 February 1933**, **IsMEO** founded in Rome by Tucci and **Giovanni Gentile**, Tucci managing vice-president. **[A: Treccani for the date; en. and it.wikipedia corroborate.]** Seat: **Palazzo Brancaccio** (C6).
- **Indo-Tibetica**, four volumes, 1932-1941. **[A: en.wikipedia and Treccani.]**
- **Toling** and **Tsaparang** (Guge), 1933 and 1935 **[A: en.wikipedia; Indo-Tibetica]**; **Tashilhunpo, Shigatse** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Sakya, 1935**: initiation conferred by the abbot **Ngawang Thutob Wangdrag**; he practises Buddhism afterwards and later describes himself as the reincarnation of a Tibetan lama. **[A: it.wikipedia.]**
- **Shalu, Narthang, Reting, Samye, Nagchu, Rutok, Gyantse, Kailash, Manasarovar**: all **[R]**. The pool tagged Shalu **[A]** on the strength of the en.wikipedia Shalu article, which does not mention Tucci (Corrections, C4). Only Samye and Kailash survive into the journey file, both as reconstructions; the rest were cut rather than dressed up.
- **Bogo-La glacier, June 1933**: it.wikipedia confirms a surviving photograph of Tucci with the captain-physician Ghersi on the Bogo-La during the fourth expedition. The pool's coordinates (29.5, 90.5) are round-number placeholders putting it 1,100 km east of where the 1933 expedition worked. **No stop was created**: a documented event with no usable location.

## 5. The regime, 1936-1944

- **November 1936 to January 1937**, official mission to **Japan as Mussolini's personal representative**; opening of the Italian-Japanese Institute in Tokyo; a **radio address in April 1937**; later on the editorial board of the propaganda magazine **Yamato**; speeches invoking racial purity. **[A: en.wikipedia.]** The pool's chronology lens dated the mission "1937" while its geography lens had it right (C5).
- **1938, the race question.** Reported service on a race-studies commission, and occasional claims that he signed the **Manifesto della Razza**, with **no documentary evidence of a signature produced**. The same scholarship credits him with helping the Jewish analyst **Ernst Bernhard** avoid deportation. **[R: it.wikipedia, reporting divided scholarly opinion.]** Left unresolved.
- **Central Tibet expeditions 1937 and 1939.** **[A: it.wikipedia for the dates; the specific monasteries visited are [R].]**
- **1941**, separation from Giulia Nuvoloni. **[A: Treccani.]**
- **1944**, with the Allied entry into Rome, IsMEO's activities cease. **[A: it.wikipedia.]** Gentile had been assassinated in Florence in April 1944.

## 6. Purge and reinstatement, 1944-1947

- **July 1944**, epuration proceedings over his ties to the regime and IsMEO's role in fascist cultural propaganda. **[A: Treccani.]** The commission's reasoning is not printed in any reachable source: a gap.
- **8 January 1946**, cleared and reinstated. **[A: Treccani; it.wikipedia gives 1946.]**
- **1947**, president of the reconstituted IsMEO; presidency to **1978**, honorary president thereafter. **[A: Treccani and it.wikipedia, in agreement.]**

## 7. Lhasa, 1948

- **Eighth and final Tibet expedition**: reaches **Lhasa**, received at the **Potala** by the young **fourteenth Dalai Lama**, travelling with the Sherpa mountaineer **Tenzing Norgay**. **[A: en.wikipedia; the ordinal "eighth" from it.wikipedia.]**
- **Jokhang, Drepung, Sera** and the rest of the Lhasa monastic circuit: **[R]**, inferred from the standard 1948 visitor itinerary and consistent with his documented stay. Only Drepung is kept as a stop, flagged as reconstruction.
- **1949**, *Tibetan Painted Scrolls* and *Theory and Practice of the Mandala*. *Contested*: en.wikipedia describes the *Painted Scrolls* in **three** volumes, Treccani in **two**. Left standing.
- **1950**, *The Tombs of the Tibetan Kings*. **[R: Treccani only.]**

## 8. The archaeological turn, 1952-1959

- **Nepal expeditions 1952 and 1954.** **[A: Treccani and it.wikipedia, in agreement.]** *Preliminary Report on Two Scientific Expeditions in Nepal*, 1956: the volume was reached in full text, so the publication is attested even though Treccani is the only bibliographic citation for it.
- **Swat valley, 1955**: reconnaissance under the patronage of the **Wali of Swat, Miangul Jahan Zeb**. **[A: Treccani; it.wikipedia for the patronage.]** **Butkara I was excavated 1956-1962 under Domenico Faccenna**, not by Tucci in person; Tucci directed the mission institutionally (C7).
- **Ghazni, Afghanistan, from 1956.** **[A: it.wikipedia.]**
- **1957**, proposes the founding of the national museum of oriental art in Rome, and publishes *Storia della filosofia indiana*. **[A: Treccani.]**
- **Iran, 1959-1978.** it.wikipedia supports **archaeological work in Iran** across those years and nothing narrower. The excavation was in **Sistan (Shahr-i Sokhta)**; IsMEO's presence at **Persepolis** was a **restoration** mission under **Giuseppe and Ann Britt Tilia**, and neither Persepolis article records an Italian excavation there (they credit Herzfeld, Schmidt and Godard). The pool's "Tucci directs Italian excavations at Persepolis" is wrong as stated (C8). The journey file keeps the Persepolis coordinate, the only verified one available for the Iranian work, and says in the stop text what that work actually was.

## 9. Emeritus, prizes, death, afterlife

- Retirement from the Roman chair. *Contested*: Treccani **1969** with emeritus 1970; it.wikipedia **1968** with emeritus 1970. Left standing. The pool's separate claim that he "teaches there until his death" is false on either reading (Corrections, C9).
- **1971**, third marriage, to **Francesca Bonardi**. **[A: Treccani.]**
- **Jawaharlal Nehru Award for International Understanding, 1976.** **[A: Treccani; en.wikipedia's list "Jawaharlal Nehru Award" gives 1976 Tucci, 1977 Tulsi Mehar Shrestha, 1978 Nichidatsu Fujii.]** The en.wikipedia Tucci article's "1978" contradicts that list, so this is a one-sided error and it is **resolved to 1976** (Corrections, C10), the only date in the dossier resolved rather than left open.
- **Balzan Prize for History, 1979**, *ex aequo* with **Ernest Labrousse**. **[A: en.wikipedia, Treccani, balzan.org.]**
- **Died 5 April 1984, San Polo dei Cavalieri**, aged 89; bibliography above 360 books and articles. **[A: en. and it.wikipedia, Treccani.]** **Gap: no consulted source states the burial place or the form of the tomb.**
- **2005**, the Museo Nazionale d'Arte Orientale at Palazzo Brancaccio formally named for him **[A: it.wikipedia]**; **2008**, a cultural centre bearing his name at San Polo dei Cavalieri **[R: en.wikipedia]**.
- **25 May 2010**, **Largo Giuseppe Tucci** inaugurated in Rome, with protests from Rome's Jewish community and from parliamentarians over his alleged support for the Manifesto della Razza. **[A: it.wikipedia, which carries the item and specifies the objection.]**
- **2012**, IsIAO (IsMEO merged with the Istituto Italo-Africano in 1995) put into compulsory liquidation by decree of 11 November 2011; Tucci's roughly 25,000-volume library absorbed into the **Biblioteca Nazionale Centrale di Roma**. **[A: it.wikipedia, IsIAO.]**
- **2016**, the museum absorbed into the **Museo delle Civiltà** and moved to the Palazzo delle Scienze in EUR. **[A: it.wikipedia.]** **Gap: sources do not say whether the Tibetan photographic and manuscript material moved with the art or stayed with the library.**
- *Indo-Tibetica* remains without a complete English translation. **[R: bibliographic consensus; no full translation located.]**

## Contradictions carried, not adjudicated

1. **Ascoli Piceno teaching post**: 1920, months only (Treccani) against 1919-1921 (it.wikipedia).
2. **Naples chair**: 1930 (Treccani) against 1931 (en.wikipedia).
3. **Expedition numbering**: five major Tibet expeditions 1931/1933/1935/1937/1939 with July 1931 counted third overall (it.wikipedia) against three western Tibet trips 1931/1933/1935 (en.wikipedia).
4. **Tibetan Painted Scrolls**: three volumes (en.wikipedia) against two (Treccani).
5. **Retirement**: 1969 (Treccani) against 1968 (it.wikipedia); emeritus 1970 in both.
6. **Fascism**: alleged race-commission service and an unproven signature on the Manifesto della Razza, against a documented act of protection of Ernst Bernhard. it.wikipedia reports the split and does not resolve it.
7. **San Polo dei Cavalieri coordinates**: the pool gave three figures for one small town (42.0006/12.81; 42.0136/12.8511; 42.0167/12.8333). Reconciled to it.wikipedia's **42.0167, 12.8333**, declared here rather than done silently.

## Corrections applied to the research pool

- **C1.** Liceo Classico Giacomo Leopardi is in **Macerata**, not Ascoli Piceno; the pool's 42.8536, 13.5750 was Ascoli, 55 km south. Those coordinates are correct only for the later Stabili teaching post, where they are used.
- **C2.** Tucci did **not** co-found *Studi e materiali di Storia delle religioni* (Pettazzoni, 1925); his own journal was merged into it.
- **C3.** July 1931 was not his first expedition (third, per it.wikipedia), and the pool's "four major Tibet expeditions" was followed by a list of five dates.
- **C4.** Shalu Monastery downgraded from [A] to [R], coordinates 2.4 km off. Cut from the journey file rather than carried on a bad tag.
- **C5.** The Japan mission is November 1936 to January 1937, not "1937"; the April 1937 radio address is separate and later.
- **C6.** IsMEO's seat is Palazzo Brancaccio (41.8955, 12.5033), not the Rome centroid 41.9028, 12.4964, which the pool reused for three distinct Roman locations. Corrected everywhere except the 2010 Largo Tucci stop, where no better coordinate was reachable and the centroid is retained knowingly.
- **C7.** Butkara I was dug under **Domenico Faccenna** from 1956; coordinates corrected to 34.7571, 72.3622.
- **C8.** Persepolis is the wrong site for Tucci's Iranian excavation (Sistan, Shahr-i Sokhta); IsMEO's Persepolis work was Tilia restoration. Stop text rewritten to say so.
- **C9.** He did not teach at La Sapienza until his death; pensioned 1968 or 1969, emeritus 1970.
- **C10.** Nehru Award is 1976, not 1978, on en.wikipedia's own list.
- **C11.** Gyantse Dzong (1.6 km), Reting (24 km), Samye (8.5 km), Drepung (1.2 km) and San Polo (3 km) coordinates in the pool were all off; surviving stops use corrected figures.
- **C12.** "Aged 18" for the 1911 Latin inscriptions is an en.wikipedia arithmetic slip; he was seventeen.

## Corpus interlocks

No existing journey file records a **documented meeting** between Tucci and any other atlas traveler. Every crossing below is geographic or thematic, and is written as such in the campa text.

- **matteo_ricci**: born in the same walled town, Macerata, 342 years earlier. Pin **43.3006, 13.4531** inherited byte-identically.
- **blavatsky**: Kashmir (**34.0837, 74.7973**) and Tashilhunpo/Shigatse (**29.268, 88.88**), both inherited exactly. Her 1868 Shigatse residence rests on her own word; Tucci's visit produced an inventory. **House-style pass, 2026-08-14:** the Shigatse campa named her; cut. Sixty-seven years apart, no citation either way, purely a same-coordinate coincidence, not an interlock. The Kashmir stop's mention of Blavatsky was outside this pass's flagged hits and is untouched. Pin unchanged.
- **keyserling**: Darjeeling. His corpus pin (**27.005, 88.288**) is used in preference to the pool's 27.0410, 88.2663, so the atlas keeps one Darjeeling node.
- **serrano**: Kailash, pin **31.067, 81.311** inherited exactly from his 1957 stop.
- **gurdjieff**: Lhasa 1901 against Tucci's Lhasa 1948. His corpus pin is 29.652, 91.172 (generic Lhasa); the Potala is a distinct site and keeps its accurate 29.6558, 91.1177. **House-style pass, 2026-08-14:** gurdjieff is no longer named in the campa. Forty-seven years apart, resting on Gurdjieff's own unverified word alone, no citation either way, purely a same-coordinate coincidence. Pin unchanged.
- **savitri_devi**: the Kashmir and Amarnath approach corridor, 1936. **swami_vivekananda**: Calcutta.
- **mussolini**: Tokyo 1936 and the 1938 race question. The Palazzo Venezia pin (**41.8961, 12.4817**) is inherited for the 1938 stop, in preference to the pool's unexplained 41.8719, 12.5674.
- **Not applicable**: the lens also surfaced pins for the Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), the Temple Mount and Buenos Aires. Tucci went to none of them and none is used.
- **xuanzang**, listed in QUEUE.md immediately before Tucci and the obvious Uddiyana counterpart, **has no journey.json in the corpus**. No interlock was written against a file that does not exist.
