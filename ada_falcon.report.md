# Ada Falcón: research report

*Aída Elsa Ada Falcone, "la joyita argentina," "la emperatriz del tango." Ituzaingó (Buenos Aires province), 17 August 1905 to Molinari (Córdoba), 4 January 2002.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, legend, or inference · **gap** a thing this research could not establish, stated as a gap rather than filled.

The documentary base is narrow. Two Wikipedia articles and two newspaper pieces (Sibila Camps, *Clarín*, 5 January 2002; Sandra Chaher, *Página/12*, January 2004) carry essentially everything below, and the Spanish article's footnotes rest on those same two pieces. Discogs, TodoTango's discography pages and the Télam archive were all unreachable, so the document a singer's file most wants, a discography, is missing entirely.

## 1. Birth and childhood (1905-1919)

**Born 17 August 1905 at Ituzaingó, Buenos Aires province, to Cornelia Boesio [A, es.wikipedia].** One lens names the property as the estancia "Los Paraísos"; it is not geolocated anywhere reached, and the pin (-34.6597, -58.6717) is modern Ituzaingó, not the house. **gap.**

**Paternity disputed [A that it is disputed].** Given as the landowner Miguel Nazar Anchorena, said to have died of cancer in France before the birth. The encyclopedia frames it as contested. Recorded, not adjudicated.

**Two older sisters, Amanda and Adhelma Falcone, singers of lesser renown [A].** No independent career documentation for either. **gap.** Adhelma reappears in 1989 as the object of a serious accusation (section 6).

**At four she asks to sing; her mother takes over the career and contracts Falcone to Falcón [A].** The year 1909 is **[R]**, arithmetic from her age; es.wikipedia gives no year.

**Debut as "la joyita argentina" at an event of the Sociedad de San Vicente de Paul [A].** No venue, no year, no programme. **gap.** One lens credited this to a TodoTango biography. **That attribution is wrong:** the URL used (creadores/biografia/210) is Miguel Cafre, an unrelated singer, so anything credited to "todotango biography" in the crawled dossier is void.

**Tonadillas at the Teatro Apolo, Corrientes [A].** **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the circulating date 1916 appears nowhere in the Spanish article, being back-computed from en.wikipedia's "a little girl of only 11 years old." es.wikipedia places the Apolo immediately before the 1919 film, so c. 1918, which the file uses. Pin corrected to Corrientes 1372 (approx. -34.6045, -58.3855), about 350 m from the dossier's.

**Film debut in the silent *El festín de los caranchos* at about fourteen [A].** **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** es.wikipedia 1919, en.wikipedia 1918. No shooting location anywhere, so the generic Buenos Aires pin is used rather than an invented studio coordinate.

## 2. The recording years begin (1925-1929)

**15 July 1925, begins work at the RCA Victor recording room with Osvaldo Fresedo's orchestra [A, verbatim to the day].** **"The third Argentine woman to record a disc" [A], not "the third to record tango" (correction):** es.wikipedia says «la tercera mujer argentina en grabar un disco», and the dossier added "tango," narrowing a superlative the source does not narrow. Rosita Quiroga and Azucena Maizani, both 1923, are the first two.

**1929, fourteen sides for Odeón with Enrique Delfino (piano) and Manuel Parada (guitar); mezzo-soprano register noted as unusual for the period [A].** No date within the year. **gap.**

**24 July 1929, begins the working relationship with Francisco Canaro's orchestra, opening with "La morocha"; roughly 180 pieces together [A, verbatim to the day].** **Correction:** the dossier attached the label Odeón to this session. es.wikipedia names no label here, the attribution is inference, and it is dropped from the stop name.

**Romance with Carlos Washington Lencinas, former governor of Mendoza, ending with his assassination; she composes the vals "Sueño con él" in his memory [A].** **Tag correction:** one lens of the pool marked this [R], "crawl-derived tradition." Wrong. es.wikipedia carries it directly, and its article on Lencinas fixes the killing to 10 November 1929 in Mendoza. Upgraded to [A]. **gap:** no source reached says Falcón was ever physically in Mendoza; the stop is pinned there because the man, the killing and the vals are, and `date_confidence` says so.

## 3. The peak (1930-1937)

**Roughly fifteen discs a month through the 1930s; radio debuts on Cultura, Stentor, Splendid, Argentina, Prieto, Belgrano and El Mundo [A].** Note the verb: she *debuted* on them; the dossier's "performed regularly on each" is not what the source says. **Roughly 200 songs; artistic peak 1930-1942 [A, en.wikipedia].**

**Four stations collapsed into one stop (structural correction).** The dossier carried Cultura, Stentor, Splendid and Belgrano as four stops on the *identical* placeholder coordinate -34.6037/-58.3816, positively wrong for Belgrano. One "radio circuit" stop now names all seven.

**Three-storey house in Palermo Chico, furs, jewellery, two automobiles [A].** **gap:** no street address in any source. **Pin inherited byte-identical** from `carlos_thays.journey.json` (-34.5824, -58.3919) rather than the dossier's -34.5786/-58.4064, about a kilometre away.

**1933, Canaro composes the vals "Yo no sé qué me han hecho tus ojos," inspired by her eyes [A].** Her 1995 recollection is quoted on that stop, postdating the vals by sixty-two years, and `date_confidence` says so. **1934, *Ídolos de la radio*, dir. Eduardo Morera, with Ignacio Corsini, Olinda Bozán, Dorita Davis and Tita Merello [A].** Second and last film.

**1935, decides she will make no further public appearances [A, «decidió que no realizaría más presentaciones en público»].** No cause given by the source and none supplied in the file.

**Radio El Mundo gives her the small "sala F," called "la sala Falcón" [A].** The room is «la sala ‹F›», not an official "Studio F." **Chronology strain flagged:** es.wikipedia dates the station's first broadcast to 29 November 1935, so a 1935 assignment fits only the last five weeks of the year, and the source dates *the decision* to 1935, not the room; the file dates the sala F to 1936. Pin moved to Maipú 555 (approx. -34.5995, -58.3760).

## 4. Withdrawal (1938-1942)

**28 September 1938, the collaboration with Canaro ends [A, to the day].** No statement by either party located. **gap.**

**The pistol anecdote [R].** Canaro's wife bursting into a rehearsal with a pistol: repeated in secondary press for decades, no contemporary account located, venue unknown. **Kept in the file and marked as legend in both campa and `date_confidence`,** because the legend is itself part of the record of what was said about her.

**Toward 1940 she sings from behind a curtain, hidden from her own musicians [A, «Hacia 1940, terminó cantando detrás de un cortinado escondida de sus propios músicos»].** **Correction:** one lens dated this 1939, contradicting the source. **gap:** no memoir by any musician who played behind that curtain was found.

**Devotion at the church of Pompeya [A, «sobre todo en la iglesia de Pompeya»].** The dossier called this crawl-derived and corroborated only in general terms; it is not, the church is named in the cited article, and it is upgraded to [A]. Pin moved to the basilica on Avenida Sáenz (approx. -34.6497, -58.4110), the dossier's being 1.2 km too far west. **What the source does not support, and the file does not claim:** that contemporaries read the devotion as a sign of what was coming. It says the devotion generated the *nun rumours*, a different claim.

**1942, final recordings: the tango "Corazón encadenado" and the vals "Viviré con tu recuerdo," both Canaro and Ivo Pelay [A].** **Not carried:** the interlock lens's claim that Ignacio Corsini duetted on "Corazón encadenado" was confirmed by nothing reached. **1942, sells the Palermo Chico house and two automobiles, distributes her possessions to friends, moves with her mother to Salsipuedes [A, es. and en.wikipedia].**

## 5. Córdoba (1942-1982)

**Lay Franciscan tertiary, not a professed nun [A, «tercera franciscana»].** **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** en.wikipedia calls her a "tertiary nun," collapsing the distinction the Spanish article draws; both are cited on the stop. The legend that she was cloistered, never left the house, or was impersonated on stage by a sister is [R].

**The task brief behind the original dossier called her "a cloistered Carmelite nun."** Nothing reached supports Carmelite, cloistered, or professed, and every source consulted contradicts it. Carried nowhere into the file, and flagged here rather than silently corrected.

**No further contact with her sister Adhelma after the withdrawal [A as to the fact].** No cause, no quarrel, no date. **gap.** The stop is dated 1960 purely to place it inside the retreat.

**1981, Cornelia Boesio dies past ninety; Falcón enters a casa de retiro as a Franciscan tertiary [A].** **Major correction:** the dossier merged this retreat house with the later Camillian home at Molinari and pinned it to Cosquín town (-31.2412, -64.4646). es.wikipedia names no town, congregation or month for it, and Molinari is Camillian, a different order some twenty kilometres away. The file keeps them separate and pins the 1981 retreat at her last attested address, `date_confidence` stating the true location is unattested. **gap.**

**March 1982 statement [A, verbatim, Camps, *Clarín*, 5 January 2002, quoted in full at es.wikipedia's reference 1]:**

> En plena juventud tuve riquezas y belleza, tuve una visión maravillosa del Señor y no vacilé un instante en dejarlo todo y recluirme en las sierras con mamita, en un convento franciscano, y vivir con humildad. Desde que nací, dormí junto a mi madre, y su muerte me destrozó.

**This is the most mishandled item in the research pool.** Two separate lenses quoted the sentence with the middle clause, «tuve una visión maravillosa del Señor», removed by a silent ellipsis, and one then filed a *separate* claim that the vision wording was unconfirmed tradition [R]. The gap was manufactured by the truncation: vision and renunciation are the same sentence in the same source. The file quotes it entire.

## 6. Two returns and the end (1989-2002)

**1989, returns to Buenos Aires, accuses Odeón of refusing to reissue her records, and accuses Adhelma of touring provincial towns as her and signing autographs in her name [A, verbatim].** **Correction:** the dossier tagged this "crawl-derived, primary press account not located"; it is directly in the cited article. Two dossier details are *not* in the source and are dropped: "for thirty years" (which belongs to her own quoted words) and "she offers no proof."

Her own words, 1989 [A, Camps, *Clarín*]:

> Durante treinta años cobró otra persona todos mis derechos; fue una venganza de una persona muy poderosa que ya murió, que me dijo que me iba a hacer morir de hambre.

**gap:** the powerful man is never named, and no case, judgment or counter-statement by Adhelma was found. The accusation stands in the file as an accusation.

**Last decades in the hogar de ancianos San Camilo at Molinari [A].** Corrections: the congregation is «Las Hijas de San Camilo» per es.wikipedia's Molinari article (the Falcón article says «las hermanas»); the locality lies **4 km** north of Cosquín, not 5; the home stands on Ruta Nacional 38; the coordinate is -31.1999/-64.4830, correcting a dossier pin 4 km off and an afterlife-lens pin whose latitude was truncated to a whole -31, 22 km off. The dossier's note that the facility was not locatable is wrong. The "1990s" dating is **[R]**.

**Filmed for *Yo no sé qué me han hecho tus ojos* [A, en.wikipedia: she had not allowed her picture to be taken for nearly sixty years and permitted the filmmakers to film her shortly before her death].** **Date corrected:** the dossier said 2002; she died 4 January 2002, so filming is 2001 or earlier.
The "No recuerdo" answer, unverifiable in Wikipedia alone, **is** verifiable in Chaher's *Página/12* piece and is quoted from there [A]. Wolf's gloss in the same piece is the most honest thing said about the interview: he believed she had senile dementia or something like it, notes that the sisters said she could not read while she read magazines he brought her perfectly, and calls it an indiscernible zone between memory's selection at that age and the illness [A].

**4 January 2002, dies of natural causes at ninety-six after long arteriosclerosis at Molinari; remains transferred to Buenos Aires and interred in the SADAIC pantheon at Chacarita near Canaro's plot [A, es. and en.wikipedia; Canaro's presence in the pantheon independently attested by es.wikipedia's article on him].**
**gap:** no source gives the date of the transfer, nor the plot or sector; the file dates it 2002-01-05, the day after death, to order the segment. **Coordinate reconciled:** the pool carried **three different pins for one cemetery**, all replaced by -34.5908/-58.4597, the es.wikipedia figure `angel_villoldo` and `carlos_thays` already use. «La emperatriz del tango» is attested for her, but **attributing it to the 2002 obituaries specifically is [R]**.

**"puta a los 20, monja a los 40" [A as a quotation, attribution contradictory].** *Clarín* gives it as an anonymous characterisation by the graphic press; *Página/12* attributes a near-identical phrase to Sergio Wolf. **Flagged, not resolved;** both attributions appear in the `quote_source`.

## 7. Posthumous (2003-2025)

- ***Yo no sé qué me han hecho tus ojos*, dirs. Lorena Muñoz and Sergio Wolf, released 4 December 2003, 64 min [A, es.wikipedia's article on the film].** **Award list corrected:** the dossier put Cóndor de Plata, Clarín, two Havana recognitions and BAFICI Best Documentary all in 2003. The Falcón article dates BAFICI to **2004**, and BAFICI **does not appear at all** on the film's own article, which lists a Cóndor de Plata and a nomination, Premio FIPRESCI, Mejor Película Documental, Tercer Premio Gran Coral and a mención especial. **Flagged, not resolved.**
- **2010, Telefé's *Lo que el tiempo nos dejó* dramatizes the Canaro romance, Julieta Díaz and Hilda Bernard playing her young and old [A].** **2013, the Salsipuedes house becomes the private Casona & Museo La Joyita [A, twice]**, address and operating status unconfirmed (**gap**). **2016, *Viviré con tu recuerdo*, dir. Wolf, prizewinner in BAFICI's official selection [A]**, its title reusing one of her own 1942 sides. **2025, *El alma del tango*, Vázquez de Gey and Zavala [R]**, from a crawled bibliography only, not used.

**A misattributed quotation, corrected.** The line «Ada Falcón vuelve a mí sin necesidad de que la convoque» is genuine and genuinely Wolf's, but it is **not** in the *Página/12* article the dossier credited: searches of that fetched text for "convoque" and "vuelve a mí" returned nothing. es.wikipedia references it to **Télam, October 2016**, a defunct domain, so it is verified only at one remove. Not used in the journey file.

## 8. Interlocks with the existing corpus

**Named in a campa, the only one: `angel_villoldo`.** Her first recording with Canaro, 24 July 1929, is **"La morocha,"** music by Enrique Saborido, **words by Ángel Villoldo**, attested by es.wikipedia's article on the song and already carried in `angel_villoldo.journey.json` at "Bar Reconquista, La Morocha written before dawn." A performer singing an earlier author's text is a genuine one-directional relation.

**Deliberately not named.** `max_glucksmann` signed Canaro in 1917, twelve years before Canaro brought Falcón in: Canaro's relation, not hers. `carlos_thays` laid out Palermo Chico in 1912 and she lived there twenty years later: shared geography only, so his pin is inherited byte-identical and his name appears in no campa. `osvaldo_pugliese` shares Chacarita and the milieu, no documented contact. `eva_peron` arrives in 1935 chasing radio work, no meeting on record. `raquel_liberman` was offered as a thematic contrast: real as a reading, not a relation.

**Real relations with no journey file in the corpus:** Canaro, Fresedo, Discépolo, Tita Merello, Ignacio Corsini, Delfino, Parada, Lencinas. All named in campas as her own people; none is an interlock, because none has a file yet.

**Unclaimed geography (negative finding).** A grep of all `*.journey.json` for "Salsipuedes," "Cosquín" and "Molinari" returned zero hits before this file. The Córdoba sierras had no pin to inherit; -31.1372/-64.2958 and -31.1999/-64.4830 are new to the corpus, from the towns' own es.wikipedia articles.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Ada Falcón is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`,** so nothing here is licensed as an apparatus stop. Per the standing instruction, relations not rooted in her own life are kept and flagged rather than silently deleted.

**Direction caught: forward, in every case.** All four are posthumous reception, other people engaging her after 4 January 2002: the 2003 premiere and award run of *Yo no sé qué me han hecho tus ojos*, the 2016 *Viviré con tu recuerdo*, the 2010 Telefé dramatization, and the 2025 biography. The first is handled as a `suggested_ref` and `source` on her own Molinari stop for the filming she was present at, which is legitimately hers, not staged as a scene. The other three appear in this report only, in no stop.

Two posthumous stops **were** kept, on the ground that they concern her own body and her own house rather than another person's travel: **Chacarita, the pantheon of SADAIC (2002)** and **Salsipuedes, the house opens as La Joyita (2013)**. Both are terminal in their segment and can be cut if the operator judges otherwise.
