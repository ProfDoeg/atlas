# Emiliano Kargieman (b. 1975): research report

*Compiled 2026-09-09 for `emiliano_kargieman.journey.json`.*

**[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a lead not confirmable from here. Where the pool and its correction pass disagreed, both readings are printed and the disagreement is left standing. Fixes against the pool are marked **CORRECTED**.

A living subject in the middle of his own events; the file's clock stops at the EY / La Nación interview of 21 August 2026, the last dated item in the pool.

Two documents carry most of the weight: **La Nación, "Emiliano Kargieman: explorador del universo," 14 June 2013**, with no second primary account of the early years, and **Alejandro Artopoulos, "El caso de Core Security Technologies," H-industri@**, for the Core decade.

## 1. Buenos Aires and Palermo, 1975 to c. 1990

- **Born 7 May 1975. [A: Fundación Konex b5038; Spanish Wikipedia]** Some profiles give 1977. **CORRECTED:** the pool defended 1975 with a June 2013 article calling him 36, which is backwards (a May-1975 birth makes him 38 that month). The corroboration that works is **Infobae, 3 March 2018, calling him 43. [A]**
- **Birthplace.** *Gap.* Konex states none; Buenos Aires rests on La Nación's Palermo childhood, so the pin is the city centroid.
- **Parents Alfredo and Ana María, both psychoanalysts; a twin sister, Lucila, later a doctor in electroneurophysiology. [A: Infobae 2018; La Nación 2013]** The pool tagged the twin **[R]** as a machine artefact clashing with profiles reporting no siblings. **CORRECTED to [A]:** a direct fetch confirms Lucila as La Nación's own reporting.
- **First computer. CORRECTED.** The pool had "about nine, a Sinclair-family machine, 1984." La Nación: *"la Sinclair 2068, la computadora que le regalaron apenas se asomó a la adolescencia,"* so roughly twelve or thirteen, c. 1987-88. `1988-01-01` orders the segment. **[A]** for model and circumstance, **[R]** for any year.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The 2026 EY interview has him programming at **nine and a half**, Spanish Wikipedia has him **repairing computers at ten**, and neither squares with a first machine at twelve unless the Sinclair was not the first machine he touched. Both readings are carried. The **modem at about twelve [R]** hangs on the same window, so its order relative to the Sinclair is a choice, not a finding.
- **Schooling. [A: La Nación 2013]** The **Cangallo Schule**, expelled from its secondary *"por irrumpir en la oficina del rector y empapelarla con papel higiénico"*; finished at the public **Colegio Nicolás Avellaneda**. **CORRECTED:** the pool read "Cangallo" as a street address; it is the school's name.
- **Contradiction, flagged and left standing.** Artopoulos says Core's founders came out of the **ORT technical schools**, which cannot be squared with Cangallo-then-Avellaneda for Kargieman. Neither source is dropped: the campa states Cangallo, `date_confidence` records the ORT problem.
- *Gap:* no address for either school, the flat, or the birth; every stop here sits on a centroid.

## 2. Hacked by Owls, 1992 to 1997

- **The bar at San José and Avenida de Mayo. [A: La Nación 2013]** A "mugriento bar" where the tables were earned by proving you had taken something hard; Matías Travizano is quoted on the etiquette.
- **Centro Cultural Recoleta payphone, 1994. [A: La Nación 2013] CORRECTED.** The manifesto the pool supplied, *"toda persona curiosa es un hacker potencial,"* is **not in the source** and echoes Loyd Blankenship's 1986 Hacker Manifesto; La Nación's text, used in the file, begins *"Esta conferencia sucks."* Caveat kept: a 2013 newspaper's report of a 1994 screen, not an archival document.
- **Llaneros solitarios, 1994. [A via La Nación, citing Bonsembiante and Roberti]** He appears at nineteen as **Logical Backdoor** and breaks off the interview. *Inaccessible source:* the book itself.
- **The DGI recruitment, 1994. [A: Artopoulos] CORRECTED from 1993, and the "two attributions" resolved into a chain.** Futoransky took silver at the International Olympiad in Informatics in Stockholm in 1994; Ricardo Cossio, DGI director under Menem, read the resulting press interview and offered him a post; Futoransky called in Altszul, Kargieman and Richarte. Fifteen were hired, all under twenty, all medallists, into the **Grupo de Proyectos Especiales**: catch taxpayers billing off the books with software, and secure the bureau's own files. Cossio (La Nación) and Futoransky (Artopoulos) are **both right, at different links**.
- **The AFIP myth. [R]** The caught-teenager-hired-by-his-victim formulation has no support in either primary account and is anachronistic: the DGI became AFIP only in **1997 [A]**.
- **University. [A for the facts, R for every date] CORRECTED.** The pool had mathematics "and, in other accounts, philosophy," as if the sources offered alternatives. La Nación describes a **sequence**: *"ya había largado su carrera de Matemáticas y cursaba la de Filosofía, que tampoco terminaría,"* and names *"los pasillos de Puán,"* where philosophy is taught, not Exactas. The 1994 anchor is unsupported; `1995-03-01` and `1996-03-01` order the segment against the academic calendar and nothing else.

## 3. Core, 1996 to 2005

- **Six founders, 1996: Iván Arce, Ariel Futoransky, Gerardo Richarte, Jonatan Altszul, Lucio Torre, Kargieman; first office in Arce's home. [A: Artopoulos, corroborated in outline by La Nación]** **Contradiction, not resolved:** **Konex** has him founding at **nineteen** (1994-95), and his own 2026 words are *"Yo arranqué la empresa con 18, 19 años"*; Artopoulos and the corporate record say 1996. The campa prints both. The founder count narrows in retelling from six to four to two as one face becomes public. **[A]**
- **CoreLabs publishing on Bugtraq. [A: coresecurity.com advisory archive]** **[R] for the year**, given only as about a year after founding.
- **CORE-SDI-04, 11 June 1998. [A to the day: seclists.org/bugtraq/1998/Jun/65]** Futoransky and Kargieman, the SSH1 insertion attack: the only item in the early life with a primary document readable in full.
- **1998-99. CORRECTED, both halves of a pool claim that failed.** The pool had a first US penetration test and an Endeavor selection, both 1998. Artopoulos puts the Endeavor recognition at **end-1999** and names **Altszul, not Kargieman**, entrepreneur of the year. The documented 1997 contract was with **Secure Networks Inc. (Canada)** for the **Ballista** scanner, and US-facing work followed **Network Associates' 1998 purchase of SNI**. *No source was found for a first US penetration test at all*, and it is dropped.
- **São Paulo, 2000. [A: Artopoulos] The largest omission in the incoming pool.** Core opened a Brazilian office and Kargieman moved there for eight months to run it. It failed: the Brazilian arm of Banco de Boston would not buy Core Force. Altszul meanwhile worked New York from his own flat, and the same source is explicit that **there was no US office**. The pool's "first US office, New York, 2000" is **CORRECTED** out; eight months in Brazil are restored as a stop.
- **CORE IMPACT, from Arce's 2001 presentation. [A: coresecurity.com]** Attribution flagged, not resolved: profiles credit Kargieman personally, the technical record credits CoreLabs collectively.
- **Boston headquarters, 2003, engineering staying in Buenos Aires. [A: Artopoulos]** *Gap:* no address.
- **"Foundations and Applications for Secure Triggers," 2005. [A: IACR ePrint 2005/284; ACM TISSEC 9:1]** With Futoransky, Carlos Sarraute, Ariel Waissbein. One version associates him with an **ITBA doctorate**, an enrolment and not a completed PhD: third abandoned degree, and no source explains any of the three.
- **Leaves at about thirty, 2005. [A]** *Gap:* **why he left** is in no reachable interview.
- **Three quote CORRECTIONS, all carried into the file.** (1) *"Core fue mi MBA"* is **not verbatim**; a targeted fetch returns NOT PRESENT for "MBA" in La Nación 2013, and the attested form, per Forbes Argentina and El Cronista, is *"una especie de MBA."* (2) The pool's *"Arranqué la empresa con 18, 19 años... 400 y pico de personas"* elides what makes the number a headcount. (3) *"Rompíamos sistemas..."* is **stitched across two turns** of the EY interview and its first sentence does not exist there. The file uses the printed text in all three cases.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Kargieman is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing in this file needed the exception.** Every stop is rooted in an event of his own life; no framework is applied backward to material he never treated, and nothing reaches forward into another subject's file.

Four relations the interlock lens surfaced were **kept but not staged**, since shared geography alone is not a reason to name a traveller. None is deleted; each is recorded for a ruling.

1. **Palermo, shared with Javier Milei's 1970 birthplace.** Direction: *neither*. Co-location decades apart, no documented connection as children, and `javier_milei.journey.json` never mentions Kargieman; the only trace is the inherited pin. Milei **is** named, but on the 2026 legislation, where the relation is real and one-directional.
2. **The DGI post read against Milei's 1992-93 BCRA internship.** Direction: *neither*, a structural echo the lens itself called constructed. Dropped from the prose.
3. **Cristina Fernández de Kirchner.** Direction: *neither*. Her governments' science ministry funded the first prototypes, which appears in `date_confidence` on the Satellogic founding stop. **No personal crossing is documented**, so she is named in no campa.
4. **Alex Karp / Palantir and Elon Musk / SpaceX.** Direction: *neither*, both **unverified as personal relations**. The Palantir-Satellogic tasking agreement is a dossier assertion that could not be confirmed, so it is **absent from the file** and flagged here as a lead. The SpaceX rideshare **is** attested for the Albania satellites, so the company is named in that campa; **Musk is not**, since the relation is to a launch provider.

Where a traveller **is** named in a campa the relation is Kargieman's own: he attended the program Kurzweil founded; his team named a satellite after Spinetta's song, thirteen months after Spinetta's death, an act entirely on Kargieman's side; his paper answers Thiel's katechon lectures; Altman supplied the statement for the letter of intent Kargieman's company signed; Milei's government drafted a bill traced to his paper, which Harari attacked in public.
