# Victor Hugo (1802-1885): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-16 for `victor_hugo.journey.json`. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Calendar: gregorian. 45 stops, 8 segments.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on a compiler (Wikiquote, popular pages) rather than a text checked at source. Disagreements between reachable sources are printed and left standing. Coordinate corrections carry the size of the error; several are large enough to move a pin off the building.

Hugo's difficulty is abundance. The life is documented to the day for decades, and the risk runs the other way: the anecdotal apparatus (red waistcoat, workman's passport, last line in the notebook) is so worn it enters biography uncited.

## 1. Childhood on the march (1802-1815)

- Born 26 February 1802, 140 Grande Rue, Besançon, third son of Joseph-Léopold-Sigisbert Hugo and Sophie Trébuchet. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** 47.2378 / 6.0241, both lenses agreeing. The opposed politics of the parents is **[A: Robb, *Victor Hugo* (1997)]**; reading it as the cause of Hugo's own migration is **[R]**.
- **Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** The journey to Avellino is dated **1807** by chronology (Britannica), **1808** by geography, **October 1807** by the verifier (en.wikipedia). The family is there across both years; file uses `1807-10-01`. The residence and the "glimpse of Vesuvius" are **[R]**, out of the campa.
- Feuillantines garden, roughly 1809-1813, 48.8412 / 2.3419. **[A: Britannica; Hugo's Feuillantines poems]**
- Convoy crossing into Spain at Fuenterrabía, March 1811, 43.3597 / -1.7936. **[A: Adèle Hugo, *Victor Hugo raconté* (1863), corroborated by Robb]** A family source written under Hugo's eye: itinerary [A], interior detail [R].
- Real Colegio de San Antonio Abad, Madrid, 1811-1812. **[A: attendance, multiple biographies]** Site approximate in the source.
- **Gap.** The parents' separation is undated anywhere in the pool **[R]**; the campa names it without a year.

## 2. Louis-le-Grand to Hernani (1815-1831)

- Pension Cordier and Lycée Louis-le-Grand, 1815-1818, 48.8462 / 2.3444; Académie française honourable mention, 1817. **[A: Britannica; school records per Robb]** The judges querying his age is **[R]**.
- Marriage at Saint-Sulpice, 12 October 1822, 48.8514 / 2.3342. **[A: parish and civil records per Robb]** **Contradiction corrected:** geography has him marrying "against his mother's old objections, now silenced by her death two years before." Sophie Trébuchet died 27 June 1821: fifteen months, not two years **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The brother's breakdown that night is **[A as event: Robb]**, with a **naming inconsistency in the pool**: geography says "his brother Eugène," the verifier "Eugène Foucher-related breakdown." The brother is Eugène Hugo; Foucher is Adèle's family name.
- Cénacle at Nodier's Arsenal apartment, roughly 1824-1827, 48.8508 / 2.3654 **[A: Robb]**, with *Cromwell* and its preface, 1827 **[A: Britannica]**, folded in: the preface is the document, the Arsenal is where the party that enforced it was assembled.
- **Coordinate correction, Comédie-Française.** Chronology gave 48.8656 / 2.3376, ~**290 m north** of the theatre. The verifier gives the Salle Richelieu as **48.8632 / 2.3363**, geography **48.8638 / 2.3364**: two "correct" values ~70 m apart, unreconciled. File uses the verifier's.
- *Hernani*, 25 February 1830, and the running battle in the pit. **[A: Britannica; Gautier's participant account]** Gautier's **red** waistcoat is **[R]** (the pool has only "flamboyant dress"); the campa drops the colour.
- *Notre-Dame de Paris*, 1831, **48.853 / 2.3499**, the canonical pin inherited byte-identical from the interlock lens. **[A: Britannica]** The March month is not in the pool, so `1831-01-01`.

**The ANANKE quote.** The quote lens claimed the preface sentence ends with "ANANKH" in the Gutenberg text, "confirmed directly." The verifier grepped the file: **zero hits** for ANANKH, ANANKE or ANAGKE. Gutenberg 2610 (Hapgood) prints the Greek **ἈΝÁΓΚΗ**. The file quotes only as far as "engraved by hand upon the wall:", with the Greek in `quote_source`. The inscription's authenticity is separately disputed, Hugo its only witness. **[R]**

## 3. Place Royale, Académie, peerage (1832-1848)

- 6 Place des Vosges, 1832-1848 **[A: the Maison de Victor Hugo]**. Geography 48.8555 / 2.3663 against afterlife 48.8549 / 2.3661, ~70 m; file uses geography. Museum inaugurated **30 June 1903**, not 1902: Paul Meurice's donation was 1902, the centenary year. **[A: fr.wikipedia; the pool's own flag on 1902 was well placed]**
- **Tag correction.** Juliette Drouet was tagged **[R]** on the grounds the onset was "reconstructed." It is not: fr.wikipedia gives "Hugo rend visite à Juliette la nuit du 16 février [1833], qui deviendra la date-anniversaire du début de leur relation." **[A]** Stop `1833-02-16`.
- **Académie française, 7 January 1841 [A]**, coordinate corrected: chronology gave 48.8534 / 2.3388, ~**420 m south** of the Institut. Verifier 48.8570 / 2.3373, geography 48.8579 / 2.3372, ~100 m apart: again two "correct" values. File uses geography's.
- Pyrenees with Juliette Drouet, August 1843, Cirque de Gavarnie 42.6928 / -0.0122. **[A: Hugo's *Pyrénées*]**
- **Léopoldine's drowning, 4 September 1843, is the worst-attested coordinate in the file.** Four values, none agreeing: chronology 49.5119 / 0.6764; geography (church of Villequier) 49.5219 / 0.6867, placed by the verifier well northeast of the village; afterlife 49.5167 / 0.6667; verifier's village centre **49.5136 / 0.6747**. Spread ≈ 2 km; file uses the village centre. **The site of the capsizing is fixed nowhere: a gap.** Geography also says "married four months earlier": she married Charles Vacquerie 15 February 1843, six and a half months before. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** The café-table newspaper is **[A: Robb]**; the town (tradition names Soubise) is not in the pool.
- **Peerage, 13 April 1845 [A: royal ordinance]**, with one coordinate error repeated twice: geography gives 48.8462 for the Palais du Luxembourg at both the 1845 peerage and the 1876 Senate stops. 48.8462 is the **Panthéon**, ~230 m south; the Palais is **48.8483 / 2.3372**. Both corrected. Léonie d'Aunet's arrest, 5 July 1845 **[A: en.wikipedia]**, folded into the peerage stop: immunity and offence are one fact.

## 4. Republic and coup (1848-1851)

- **Date correction, six weeks.** Hugo was **not** elected in the general election of 23 April 1848; he was elected to the Constituent Assembly on **4 June 1848**, in the complementary elections for the Seine. **[A: fr.wikipedia gives "4 juin 1848" explicitly]** Chronology's 1848-04-24 is unused.
- Peace Congress opening address, Paris, 21 August 1849. **[A as event]** The English wording quoted in the file is **[R]**: Wikiquote, translator unnamed, French original not reached; `quote_source` says so.
- The print campaign for Abdelkader's release. **[A: abdelkader.journey.json]** **Gap: no date anywhere.** The Amboise captivity runs 1848-1852; file uses `1850-01-01` and states the placeholder. Direction matters: Amboise (47.4133 / 0.9838) is the emir's pin on the emir's file; Hugo's stop is the Paris campaign, pinned where Hugo was.
- Coup of 2 December 1851, flight 11 December. **[A: Britannica]** The clandestine days, the omnibus, the posted appeals are **[A: Hugo's own *Histoire d'un crime* and *Choses vues*]**, attested by the participant and nobody else; any flattering detail is **[R]**. The passport name **Jacques-Firmin Lanvin** is famous and not in the pool; the campa says only "a false passport in a workman's name."

## 5. Exile: Brussels, Jersey, Guernsey (1851-1870)

- Brussels arrival 11 December 1851, Grand-Place 50.8467 / 4.3525 **[A: Robb]**. **Place correction:** chronology puts *Napoléon le Petit* on Jersey in 1852; it was written in Brussels and published at Brussels and London on **8 August 1852** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**, three days after Hugo reached Jersey. Given its own Brussels stop. The smuggling apparatus (tissue paper, hollowed busts) is **[R]**, excluded.
- **Coordinate correction, Marine Terrace, ~2 km.** Geography (49.1805 / -2.1055) and afterlife (49.1805 / -2.1085) put the house in Saint-Hélier town. It stood on the **Grève d'Azette, parish of Saint-Clément**: **49.1711 / -2.0850** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Rented from the shipowner Thomas Rose, occupied 16 August 1852 to 31 October 1855, demolished 1974. "Whitewashed tomb" is Hugo's own **[R]** phrase, paraphrased. *Les Châtiments*, 1853 **[A]**, folded in.
- **The séances.** Transcripts survive and the sessions happened **[A]**; Hugo's belief in their supernatural source is **[R]**, so tagged in the pool. The pool does **not** name Delphine de Girardin, who in standard biography brought the turning table from Paris in September 1853, so the campa says only "a visitor from Paris" and gives no month. `1853-01-01`.
- **Date correction, Jersey expulsion.** Chronology gave 1855-09; Hugo reached Saint-Pierre-Port **31 October 1855** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**, after **refusing a declaration of allegiance** following the *L'Homme* letter, not for signing it.
- **Coordinate correction, Hauteville House, ~550 m.** Geography 49.455 / -2.5335, afterlife 49.453 / -2.535; the house at 38 rue de Hauteville is **49.4513 / -2.5377** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Bought 16 May 1856, moved in 5 November. The pool's generic "Guernsey" pin (49.4553 / -2.5364) is Candie Gardens.
- **Tag correction.** The 1859 refusal of amnesty was tagged **[R]** as traditional attribution. It is a dated declaration: **18 August 1859**, "Quand la liberté rentrera, je rentrerai." **[A: fr.wikipedia]** File quotes the French and drops chronology's loose paraphrase.
- Waterloo battlefield, May 1861, 50.679 / 4.4118 **[A: Robb; the Guernsey notebooks at the BnF]**; the pressed poppy is **[R]**. **Double correction at the Hôtel des Colonnes:** geography gave 50.6167 / 4.45 (**Genappe, ~7 km south**) and 30 May 1861. The inn stood at Mont-Saint-Jean, ≈**50.681 / 4.409**; the dateline is **30 June 1861**, the month Hugo wrote that the book was done ("Je peux mourir"). **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- Letter to Captain Butler on the sacking of the Yuanmingyuan, 1861. **[A as document: en.wikipedia, 'Old Summer Palace']** Two hedges in `quote_source`: no translator named, and ellipses in the passage, so the joins are compressed. Month not in the pool; `1861-01-01`.
- *Les Misérables*, 1862, Brussels **[A: Britannica]**. The preface quote is **[A: English Wikisource, verbatim]**, em dashes replaced by semicolons per house style. Flaubert's hostility, the Index, the Confederate "Lee's Miserables" edition are **[R]**, not in the campa.
- *Les Travailleurs de la mer*, 1866 **[A]**; the island dedication and *pieuvre* entering standard French are **[R]**. Adèle Hugo dies at Brussels, 1868 **[A]**, year only; her burial at Villequier and Hugo's halt at the frontier are **[R]**.

## 6. Return, siege, the year of deaths (1870-1876)

- Return, Gare du Nord, 48.8809 / 2.3553. Chronology gives "1870-09", geography **5 September 1870**; file uses the day. **[A]**
- The siege: cannon cast from the receipts of his own work, zoo animals eaten. **[A/R: *Choses vues* and the contemporary press; geography tags the stop [R]]** The pin 48.833 / 2.41 is a general position on the eastern ramparts, not a documented site; `1871-01-01` is arbitrary inside a siege running September 1870 to January 1871.
- **Tag noted, not corrected.** Chronology tags the February 1871 double election (Gironde and Seine) **[R]** while citing fr.wikipedia for it. No stop of its own; it sits in the Bordeaux campa, dated by Charles Hugo's death **[A: en.wikipedia, 13 March 1871]**. His burial at Père-Lachaise on 18 March, the day the Commune rose, with the National Guard saluting the hearse, is **[R]**: repeated everywhere, not in the pool, and the file's largest unverified narrative detail.
- **Address correction, a cross-contamination worth naming.** Geography puts the May 1871 mob attack at the "Hôtel du Grand Miroir, Brussels," 50.848 / 4.3525. **That is Baudelaire's hotel, not Hugo's**, and the coordinates point at the Grand-Place. Hugo's address was **4 place des Barricades**, ≈**50.8535 / 4.3603** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Expulsion and departure toward Luxembourg stand. Contamination from a neighbouring biography in the same city and decade is a failure mode to remember.
- François-Victor Hugo dies 26 December 1873 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The eighteen-volume Shakespeare and the story that his father set him the task are **[R]**; Adèle's Caribbean years and her survival at Saint-Mandé are **[R]**, not in the pool, so the campa gives no dates. *Quatre-vingt-treize*, 1874 **[A]**, folded in.
- Senate, 30 January 1876 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**, coordinates corrected as above; amnesty campaign 1876-1880 **[A: Senate records]**. Mild stroke, 27 June 1878 **[A]**, no stop.

## 7. Apotheosis and death (1881-1885)

**The most tangled knot in the pool, and it appears twice.** Chronology and afterlife conflate three things: the **eightieth birthday**, **26 February 1882**; the **parade beneath his window on the avenue d'Eylau**, **27 February 1881**, marking his *entry into his eightieth year*; and the **June 1881 jubilee**, begun 25 June, in the course of which the avenue was renamed **avenue Victor-Hugo on 28 June 1881**. **[A: en.wikipedia]** Chronology compresses all three into "1881-06-28, Hugo's 80th birthday"; afterlife repeats it. The file gives one stop dated `1881-06-28`, states both real dates in the campa, and puts the correction in `date_confidence`. The address "à Monsieur Victor Hugo, en son avenue, à Paris" is **[R]**.

- **Coordinate spread for the avenue and the square, five values:** geography 48.8686 / 2.2822 (the house at 50 avenue Victor-Hugo), chronology 48.8683 / 2.2842, afterlife 48.8709 / 2.2836 and 48.8687 / 2.2837, and the interlock lens's **canonical Place Victor-Hugo pin 48.8676 / 2.2842** (`christian_dior`). File uses the house for both the parade/renaming stop and the death.
- Juliette Drouet dies, 1883 **[A]**, year only, so `1883-01-01`; stomach cancer is **[R]**. Her grave at Saint-Mandé, 48.842 / 2.416, is **[A: geography]** but **is not Hugo's stop**: too ill to follow the coffin, he did not go, so the file pins her death in Paris.
- Hugo dies 22 May 1885 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **An embellishment removed from the will:** afterlife says it "explicitly forbids any priest or crucifix" and that "the same instruction had governed his sons' burials." The wording is narrower: "Je refuse l'oraison de toutes les églises" and "Je donne cinquante mille francs aux pauvres"; the pauper's hearse is confirmed **[A: 'Funérailles de Victor Hugo']**. The claim about the sons is uncorroborated and is **dropped**, not downgraded.
- "aimer, c'est agir": **[A]** as a line in the notebook, **[R]** as a final testament, which the source itself calls disputed. Quoted with the dispute in `quote_source`.
- Arc de Triomphe, 31 May 1885, 48.8738 / 2.295 **[A]**. The two-million figure and "largest funeral France had seen" are **[A as reported, R as measured]**; nobody counted. The tradition that the brothels closed is **[R]**, not in the campa.
- Panthéon crypt, 1 June 1885, **48.8462 / 2.3464**, the canonical pin inherited byte-identical from the interlock lens (`braille.journey.json`). **[A]**

## Interlocks

Named inside a campa, both meeting the test of a real relation, not shared geography:

- **`hans_christian_andersen`**: Andersen called on Hugo in Paris on **14 August 1833**, during a stay attested to the day (10 May to 15 August), having already sought out Heine there on 25 June. **[A: hans_christian_andersen.journey.json]** A direct meeting, and the strongest interlock the pool produced. The stop is Hugo's because Andersen came to him.
- **`abdelkader`**: Hugo and Émile de Girardin campaigned in print in Paris for the emir's release while he was held at Amboise. **[A: abdelkader.journey.json]** One-directional in that the emir did not solicit it, but Hugo's own act and so Hugo's own stop, pinned in Paris.

Cited as source or `suggested_ref` only, never staged as a scene, because the other traveller did the travelling: **`luc_olivier_merson`**, who illustrated *Notre-Dame de Paris* decades after it was written **[A]**, sits in `suggested_refs` on Hugo's Notre-Dame stop, the edition year being a flagged gap in his own file; **`braille`**, reinterred in the Panthéon in 1952 among Voltaire, Rousseau and Hugo **[A]**, is Braille's own stop, no relation between the two men being attested, and appears only as a source line on the Panthéon stop.

**Rejected outright.** False positives on a different Hugo: Grotius (`vico`), Ball (`erik_satie`, `james_joyce`), Bergmann (`martin_buber`), Boss (`coco_chanel`). Shared-city or shared-era overlap with no textual link, excluded because the map already shows co-location: `camille_claudel`, `captain_alfred_dreyfus`, `gustave_eiffel`. Also excluded: `mikhail_bakunin`, where Hugo is a committee name and not in Geneva, and `jules_verne`, where Hetzel is "publisher of Hugo and Balzac," a shared publisher, not a relation.

## Honest gaps

1. **No French-language primary text was fetched this session.** Every fr.wikipedia citation comes through the verification pass; the two quotes verified at source are English translations.
2. **Ten of the fifteen pool quotes are Wikiquote entries with no underlying text located** (Villemain 1845, the Peace Congress address, four *Les Misérables* page citations, the Daelli letter, *William Shakespeare*, and three more). Only the Peace Congress line is used, hedged on its face; the rest are left out rather than laundered into campas.
3. **No stop for *La Légende des siècles*, *Les Orientales*, *Le Dernier Jour d'un condamné* or *L'Homme qui rit*.** All attested; none adds a place the file lacks. A real thinness in the poetry.
4. **The exact site of the Villequier capsizing is unknown** to every source in the pool. The pin is the village.
5. **The Paris campaign for Abdelkader has no date at all.** `1850-01-01` is a placeholder inside a four-year window.
6. **The Barrias statue's wartime fate** "could not be re-verified this session (search budget exhausted)," in the pool's words. Not in the file. **Sainte-Beuve** is absent from the pool, and so from the file, though he would carry the Place des Vosges years in any full biography.
7. **The 1980 musical** (Palais des Sports, 24 September 1980; the English version at the **Barbican** on 8 October 1985, reaching the Palace Theatre only that December, **not** a direct West End transfer as afterlife states) is posthumous reception, so no stop.
