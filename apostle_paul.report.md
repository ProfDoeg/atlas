# Paul the Apostle (Saul of Tarsus): research report
*2026-08-24. Slug `apostle_paul`. Journey file: `apostle_paul.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops, 13 verbatim quotes.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-witness narrative. Every date hedge lives in `date_confidence`, never in campa prose.

## The evidentiary situation, stated first

Paul left first-person documents. Two bodies of evidence exist and they do not agree.

1. **The undisputed letters** (Romans, 1-2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon): first-person, written for arguments other than biography and so incidentally reliable about whatever they mention in passing. They mention very little.
2. **Acts of the Apostles**: later, third-person, with a case to make about the unity of the early movement. Rich in itineraries, speeches and scenes, and unsupported by Paul's own hand on nearly every specific.

They conflict on the call, the Jerusalem visits, circumcision practice, and the temper of his relations with Jerusalem. **This report flags those conflicts and does not adjudicate them.** Rule throughout: **a claim resting on Acts alone is [R]**, however vivid. The received dossier violated that rule five times, logged below.

**One external anchor exists** and everything hangs from it: the Delphi/Gallio inscription **[A]**, nine fragments of a letter of Claudius dated 52 CE citing a report from Gallio, proconsul of Achaia. Gallio is in Corinth across 51-52; Acts 18:12-17 puts Paul before him. A weaker second: the ethnarch of Aretas at Damascus **[A, 2 Cor 11:32]**, Aretas IV dead in 40 CE.

## I. Tarsus and the Pharisee (birth to c. 33)

- **Tarsus, Cilicia; tribe of Benjamin; Roman citizenship [R, Acts 21:39, 22:3]**. Paul names neither birthplace nor citizenship in any letter. **No ancient source gives a birth year**; c. 5 BCE - 5 CE is back-calculation, and the file says so.
- **[A, Philippians 3:5-6]** Circumcised on the eighth day, a Hebrew born of Hebrews, as to the law a Pharisee, blameless. **[A, Galatians 1:14]** Advanced in Judaism beyond many of his own age, extremely zealous for the ancestral traditions.
- **Gamaliel's school at Jerusalem [R, Acts 22:3 only]**. Contested three ways: Paul never names Gamaliel or a Jerusalem boyhood; the verse's punctuation is ambiguous (does "brought up in this city" attach to birth or schooling?); no site exists.
- **Persecution of the assembly [A, Galatians 1:13, Philippians 3:6, 1 Cor 15:9]**, in his own hand, repeatedly, naming **no victim, no court, no charge, no death**. **Stephen's stoning, Saul minding the cloaks [R, Acts 7:58-8:3 only]**: Acts gives **no location**, and the traditional site is contested between the Kidron/Lions' Gate area and the church on the Nablus Road, so the file uses the generic Jerusalem pin.
- **The well and church of St Paul at Tarsus: dropped as a stop.** The "former Armenian Apostolic church" is not in the cited source, and the coordinate was the city point copied from the birth stop. Rather than invent one, the site is a `suggested_ref`.

## II. Damascus, Arabia, and the unrecorded decade (c. 34-43)

The sharpest Acts-versus-Paul conflict in the file, left standing. Galatians 1:15-24 gives one revelation with no setting, an immediate departure into Arabia explicitly instead of Jerusalem, and a first Jerusalem visit three years later, fifteen days with Cephas and James alone. Acts 9, 22 and 26 give a roadside light told three times with variant details, preaching in the Damascus synagogues at once, and an early Jerusalem visit where Barnabas introduces him to the apostles.

- **The call [A, Gal 1:15-16, 1 Cor 15:8]**: attested and undescribed. **The roadside scene [R, Acts]. The Kaukab site [R]**, late local tradition, absent from Acts.
- **Ananias and the house of Judas on Straight Street [R, Acts 9:10-19]**. Paul never mentions Ananias. Coordinate corrected (table); the 1921 excavation of a Byzantine church there is in the campa.
- **Arabia [A, Gal 1:17]**: attested that he went, and *nothing else*. Not destination, not duration, not activity. Probably Nabataea; the Petra coordinate marks the kingdom, not a stop.
- **The basket over the wall [A, 2 Cor 11:32-33]**, the second-best anchor in the life. **Discrepancy noted, not staged**: Acts 9:23-25 gives the same escape with "the Jews" rather than the ethnarch as the threat. Bab Kisan's window is **[R]**.
- **The Syria-Cilicia decade [A, Gal 1:21-24]**: attested as a fact, blank as a record. Ten years, no letter, no city, no congregation, and Acts skips it. **The largest gap in the life, treated as one rather than filled.**

## III. Antioch and the first circuit (c. 43-48)

- **The Antioch year and the coining of "Christians" [R, Acts 11:25-26 only]**: single witness; the letters never describe it.
- **Famine relief [R, Acts 11:27-30] versus the private hearing after fourteen years [A, Galatians 2:1-10]**. One trip or two is **unresolved and not resolved here**; the file gives them one stop whose `date_confidence` states the contradiction. Titus, a Greek, uncircumcised, is Paul's own [A].
- **Cyprus, Perga, Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe [R, Acts 13-14]**, uncorroborated in the letters, though **a stoning is [A, 2 Cor 11:25]** with no place attached. **Derbe**: coordinate corrected (table), on Ballance's 1956 reading of a 157 CE inscription at Kerti Huyuk, which the source calls contested; folded into the Lystra stop rather than pinned. Salamis and Attalia dropped for length, Salamis surviving inside the Paphos campa.

## IV. The council and the quarrels (c. 48-49)

- **Council of Jerusalem [R, Acts 15]**, c. 48-50: gentiles free of circumcision subject to four abstentions. **The split with Barnabas over John Mark [R, Acts 15:36-41]**.
- **The Antioch incident [A, Galatians 2:11-14]**: he opposed Cephas to his face, Barnabas was carried along, and **the letter never states the outcome**. The file supplies none.
- **Timothy circumcised at Lystra [R, Acts 16:1-3]**, on Acts' own sequence within a year of the council ruling circumcision unnecessary, and after Paul's account of refusing it for Titus. **Both stated, no conclusion drawn.**

## V. Macedonia, Achaia, and the fixed point (c. 49-52)

- **Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea, Athens [R, Acts 16-17]**. No prison structure at Philippi matches the narrative; the Areopagus speech is generally credited to the author of Acts, and whether Paul faced the council or spoke on the hill is disputed.
- **Corinth, eighteen months, Aquila and Priscilla [R, Acts 18:1-11]**. **Gallio [A, Delphi inscription]**: the firmest fact in the file.
- **1 Thessalonians.** Attested: only the co-sender list of 1 Thess 1:1 (Paul, Silvanus, Timothy). **[R]**: that it is the earliest surviving Christian text, that it came from Corinth, and its priority over Galatians, actively disputed. The campa hedges ("generally reckoned").
- **The Cenchreae vow [A, Acts 18:18]** as to haircut and vow; **[R]** as to its being Nazirite, which is inference.

## VI. Ephesus and the letters (c. 52-57)

- **The hall of Tyrannus, the magic books, the exorcisms [R, Acts 19]**. **The Demetrius riot [R, Acts 19:23-41]**; coordinate corrected (table).
- **1 Corinthians written from Ephesus [A, 1 Cor 16:8]**, referring back to **a letter that does not survive [A, 1 Cor 5:9]**. One and probably two Pauline letters to Corinth are lost. **Stated as a gap.**
- **The affliction in Asia [A, 2 Cor 1:8-10]**: crushed beyond strength, despaired of life, **cause never stated**. "Fought with wild beasts at Ephesus" (1 Cor 15:32) reads as metaphor. **The thorn [A, 2 Cor 12:7-9]** is **not resolvable from the text** and is carried as a quote, unglossed.
- **Romans [A]**, c. 56-58: Tertius the scribe (16:22), Phoebe of Cenchreae commended (16:1-2), the mission reported as far as Illyricum, the intention toward Rome and Spain (15:19-28).

## VII. The prisoner (c. 57-62)

- **Miletus farewell [R, Acts 20:17-38]**, the speech generally the author of Acts' own. **Two years at Caesarea under Felix and Festus, appeal to Caesar [R, Acts 24:27-25:12]**; the bribe motive imputed to Felix is the narrator's.
- **Arrest at the Temple [R, Acts 21:27-36]**. Citation corrected, not the pin. The dossier's 31.778/35.2354 is not from the Antonia Fortress article credited to it (which gives 31.78000/35.23417), but it **is the corpus's canonical Temple Mount pin**, already in `jesus.journey.json` and `egeria.journey.json`, so the file keeps it and re-cites it as such. That article's real contribution, the Josephus stairway parallel (JW 5:243) to Acts 21:40, is in `suggested_refs`; it stops short of identifying the fortress with the Acts barracks, and so does the file.
- **The voyage and the Malta shipwreck [R, Acts 27-28]**. Fair Havens carried **the worst positional error in the dossier**, 9.5 km (table). **St Paul's Bay [R]** is Maltese ecclesiastical tradition on the Acts narrative, not archaeology; the Rabat grotto likewise, folded into `suggested_refs`.
- **Rome, two years of guarded house arrest [R, Acts 28:16-31]**. **Acts ends without stating any outcome**, and the segment ends there.

## VIII. The silence after Acts (c. 62-67)

- **Philippians and Philemon written from confinement [A]**; **which** confinement is genuinely disputed among Rome, Caesarea and Ephesus. The file places them at Rome and says that this settles nothing.
- **Spain: unattested.** Romans 15:24, 28 prove intention only. 1 Clement 5's "the extreme limit of the west" may mean Spain or simply Rome seen from the east; claims of a completed mission appear only later, in the Muratorian fragment and after. **No Spanish community, inscription or letter confirms it.** The file gives it a stop so the hole is visible on the map, with the coordinate declared as marking the peninsula, not a stop.
- **The death.** Earliest witness: **1 Clement 5 [A], c. 96 CE**, saying he reached the extreme limit of the west, bore witness before the rulers, and departed from the world. **It names no city, no emperor, no manner of death.** Eusebius and Tertullian carry an early Roman martyrdom tradition under Nero. **The beheading, Aquae Salviae and the three springs are later development [R]**, said so in the campa too. Tre Fontane coordinate corrected (table).
- **The tomb.** Sarcophagus inscribed PAULO APOSTOLO MART beneath the papal altar of San Paolo fuori le Mura, 2.55 × 1.25 × 0.97 m, 1.37 m below it, excavation begun 2002. Announcement and radiocarbon dates both corrected (table). **Ulderico Santamaria of the Vatican Museums diagnostics laboratory said the dating neither confirms nor invalidates the attribution**; no forensic identification has ever been published. Both facts are in the campa.

## Interlocks used, and interlocks refused

**Named in a campa** (real relations, inside the canon's own timeframe):

- **`jesus.journey.json`**: the whole subject. Paul's apostleship rests on a resurrection appearance to himself (1 Cor 15:8), and Jesus is named in the Ananias and Thessalonica campas. Temple Mount pin **31.778 / 35.2354** inherited byte-identically.
- **`saint_thecla.journey.json`**: Iconium. Her file is built on Paul's first-journey route, and the *Acts of Paul and Thecla* is the source of the only physical description of Paul that exists. Her pins are inherited byte-identically: Iconium **37.8746 / 32.4932**, Pisidian Antioch **38.3061 / 31.1892**. Her scenes are **not** restaged; the campa says she sits at her window and that her story runs from there to the fire and the beasts, and points at her file. *Her flagged contradiction is not Paul's to resolve*: she puts the beast-fighting at Pisidian Antioch, `margarita_pelagia.journey.json` at Antioch on the Orontes. Paul's file only keeps the two Antiochs correctly assigned for Acts 13-14.

**Refused, since shared geography is not a relation:**

- **`cicero.journey.json`** (proconsul of Cilicia at Tarsus, gone decades before Paul's birth) and **`julius_caesar.journey.json`** (Cilician pirates): co-location only.
- **`strabo.journey.json`**: *Geography* 14.5.13 on the schools of Tarsus is a legitimate **source citation** in `suggested_refs` on the birth stop, but Strabo never wrote about Paul and Paul never cited Strabo, so he is not named in prose. The lens found 14.5.4 to concern Xenarchus and Rome, not Tarsus, and flagged the Tarsus-education claim as unverified against that file's stops. **That gap stands.**
- **`egeria.journey.json`**: her pilgrimage to Thecla's shrine at Seleucia is *her* stop on *her* file; Paul never went to Seleucia ad Calycadnum.
- **Buenos Aires (-34.6037/-58.3816)**: the lens proposed a reception-hub stop while admitting **no file places Paul or Pauline reception there**. Nothing invented. **Gap named**: it needs sourcing, and would belong on the Argentine reader's file.
- **Mecca and the Kaaba (21.4225/39.8262)**: the lens flagged, unverified, that later Muslim polemic casts Paul as corrupter of Jesus's monotheism, and **did not check `muhammad.journey.json`**. Paul never went to the Hejaz; the polemic belongs to its authors. **Gap named, not filled.**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Paul is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Three relations were found that are not rooted in his own life. **None was deleted.** All three were caught in the **forward** direction, later artifacts that could not have existed when this file's clock stopped, and forward reach is not licensable by an agent's judgement. They survive **only as `sources` / `suggested_refs` on Paul's own stops for his own objects**, as `abdelkader.journey.json` carries Vernet's 1845 canvas on its 1843 stop.

1. **Martin Luther on Romans and Galatians.** Forward (Luther 1483-1546; this file stops c. 65). A real one-directional relation: the 1515-1516 Wittenberg lectures on Romans and the 1531 on Galatians made these letters the trigger of the Reformation. **Kept as a `suggested_ref` on the "Cenchreae, Romans given to Phoebe" stop, named in no campa.** `martin_luther.journey.json` holds no Pauline stop (grep: two hits, both the church of St Peter and Paul at Eisleben). If the operator wants this visible, **it belongs on Luther's file**, and nothing should go into Paul's pointing at Luther.
2. **Marcion of Sinope, the *Apostolikon*, c. 144.** Forward. The earliest collected edition of Paul's letters and the first attempt to make a canon of him. A `suggested_ref` on the same stop; no Marcion file exists here.
3. **Erasmus, *Novum Instrumentum omne*, 1516.** Forward, and not carried into the journey file at all, since it attaches to the letters generally rather than to any stop; the corrected fact is in the table so it is not lost. **Shared-name trap flagged**: `saint_erasmus.journey.json` here is Erasmus of Formia.

The **2006-2009 excavation at San Paolo fuori le Mura** is not an apparatus relation and needs no grant: it is later engagement with **Paul's own grave at his own pin**, which the standing rule permits.

## Honest gaps

- **The birth year.** No ancient source gives one. **A decade in Syria and Cilicia** (c. 37-43): attested to have happened, wholly unrecorded. **What he did in Arabia**, for how long and why: blank by his own choice.
- **At least one and probably two letters to Corinth are lost** (1 Cor 5:9; the severe letter of 2 Cor 2:4). **The thorn in the flesh**: every proposal is guesswork.
- **The outcome of the Roman trial.** Acts stops without saying, and 1 Clement says almost nothing. **The manner and date of death**: everything picturable is later than the earliest witness. **Spain**: intention documented, arrival unverifiable.
- **`census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` was not reviewed line by line** for a prior Paul entry. QUEUE.md was grepped: every "Paul" hit is an unrelated first name (Klee, Berthon, Marcinkus, Singer). **A gap, not a confirmed absence.**
- **No `claudius.journey.json` exists** here, so the Claudian expulsion of Jews from Rome, the reason Aquila and Priscilla are in Corinth, cannot be interlocked from inside the atlas.
