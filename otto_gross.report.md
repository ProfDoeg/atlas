# Otto Gross (1877-1920): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-15 for the Atlas of Journeys. Psychiatrist and anarchist theorist of matriarchy and free love; son of the criminologist Hans Gross; patient and analyst of C. G. Jung; pushed out of Freud's movement, declared incapacitated on his father's motion, dead in a Berlin clinic at forty-two.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or a claim whose only carrier is contested. Where sources conflict, the conflict is printed and left standing.

**Method note.** The spine is the German Wikipedia article *Otto Gross*, the only continuous dated narrative reachable in this pass. It is a compilation, not a primary record, and it contradicts three other German Wikipedia articles (Frieda Schloffer's kinship, the Hattemer poisoning, the Mendrisio admission). English Wikipedia carries at least one date error corrected below. Hurwitz (1979), the congress volumes and Gross's own writings were **not** reachable as full text.

---

## 1. Origins and training, 1877-1900

- Born **17 March 1877** at Gniebing-Weißenbach, Styria, son of **Hans Gross**, founder of modern criminalistics. **[A: de.wiki, *Otto Gross*]**
- From **1881** the household is in **Graz** **[A: de.wiki]**.
- **Matura 1894**, second k.u.k. Staatsgymnasium, Graz. Begins in **zoology and botany**, switches to **medicine**, studying at Graz, Munich (summer semester 1897) and Strasbourg. **[A: de.wiki]** The usual motive, a father insisting on medicine, is **[R]**: the source records the change without a reason.
- **Dr. med., Graz, 1899.** **[A: de.wiki]**
- **1900**: ships as physician with the **Kosmos** line to South America; begins using **cocaine** on these voyages. **[A: de.wiki]** The port is **[R]**: the line called at several Chilean and Peruvian harbours and no landing is documented. Valparaíso stands in the journey file as a representative pin, labelled as such.

**Gap.** Nothing describes the ship years beyond the voyages and the onset of the drug. No log, no letter, no port.

## 2. Addiction, marriage, habilitation, 1901-1906

- **1901**: psychiatric assistant in **Munich** and **Graz**. **1902**: first addiction treatment at the **Burghölzli**, Zurich, under **Eugen Bleuler**. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **1902**: publishes ***Die cerebrale Sekundärfunction***, Vogel, **Leipzig**. **[A: de.wiki, Schriften list]** *Pool correction:* submitted as "Die zerebrale Sekundärfunktion," Graz. Both wrong; the year is right.
- **23 February 1903**: marries **Frieda Schloffer** (12 May 1876, Graz to 12 December 1950, Bosco-Gurin). **[A: de.wiki]**
  - **Contradiction, not resolved.** de.wiki *Otto Gross* calls her "eine Tochter des Chirurgen Hermann Schloffer." de.wiki *Hermann Schloffer* (1868-1937) names her as his **sister**. The daughter reading requires Schloffer to have fathered her at eight. Both are printed here.
  - "Niece of the philosopher **Alois Riehl**" is **[R]** by blood, **[A]** by marriage: Riehl's second wife Sophie née Reyer (1855-1928) "war eine Tante von Frieda Gross, geb. Schloffer" (de.wiki *Alois Riehl*).
- **1904**: **Wedekind** is said to model Karl Hetmann in *Hidalla* on Gross. **[R]**: de.wiki's literary-influence summary; the play premiered in Munich in 1905.
- **1905**: **submits** his Habilitationsschrift at Graz. **[A: de.wiki, "1905 reichte Gross dort seine Habilitationsschrift ein"]** The pool's "completes his Habilitation" overstates it; he lectures as Privatdozent für Psychopathologie from the winter semester 1906/07.
- **1906**: to **Ascona / Monte Verità** to detoxify. **[A: de.wiki]** The colony was founded in autumn 1900 by **Oedenkoven, Ida Hofmann, Karl and Gusto Gräser and Lotte Hattemer**, named Monte Verità in 1902; the pool named two of the five. **[A: de.wiki, *Monte Verità*]**
- **Lotte Hattemer**, 24 November 1876 (Berlin) to **19 April 1906** (Ascona). **[A: de.wiki, *Lotte Hattemer*]**
  - **Contradiction, not resolved.** de.wiki *Otto Gross* asserts flatly that at Ascona "stellte er jenes Gift bereit, mit dem sich die Siedlerin Paulette Charlotte Hattemer das Leben nahm." de.wiki *Lotte Hattemer* records the concluding police report (1909) as suicide, with Gross's part in a cocaine-and-opium poisoning appearing only "Gerüchten zufolge," by rumour. The flat assertion cannot carry an **[A]**; the journey file writes it as rumour.
- **1 September 1906**: assistant to **Emil Kraepelin**, Munich. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **Winter semester 1906/07**: lectures at Graz on "Über die Freud'sche Ideogenitätslehre." **[A: de.wiki]** He holds the Graz venia while working in Munich.

## 3. Munich, the sisters, the congress, 1907-1908

- **1907**: overlapping affairs with **Else von Richthofen (Jaffé)** and **Frieda von Richthofen (Weekley, later Lawrence)**. **[A as to the affairs: de.wiki; en.wiki, *Else von Richthofen*]** *Pool correction:* its two lenses said 1906 and 1907. 1907 fits Peter's birth at the end of that year and is the year used.
  - The **Wolfratshausen / Isar valley / Weber circle** setting is **[R]**. en.wiki says only: "She started an affair with Otto Gross with whom she had a fourth child, Peter (1907-ca. 1915)." The setting probably bleeds in from the 1912 Lawrence-Frieda period.
- **1907**: legitimate son **Wolfgang Peter Gross** born to Frieda Schloffer **[A: de.wiki]**; at the end of the year a son **Peter** to Else Jaffé, raised as Edgar Jaffé's **[R]**, birth and arrangement only.
- **1908**: daughter **Camilla Ullmann** born to the poet **Regina Ullmann**, his patient **[A: de.wiki]**.
- **26-27 April 1908**, Salzburg: Gross pushes psychoanalysis toward a politics and Freud will not follow. **[A: de.wiki, "Am 26. und 27. April 1908 fand in Salzburg der 1. Psychoanalytische Kongress statt"]**
  - **Name correction.** "First International Psychoanalytic Congress" is anachronistic: it was convened as the *First Congress for Freudian Psychology*, and the IPA was founded at Nuremberg in March 1910. en.wiki gives 27 April alone against de.wiki's two days; both printed.
- **Freud's own analysis of Gross at Berggasse 19: [R], and contested.** The pool submitted it as **[A]**; it cannot stand. de.wiki *Otto Gross* contains **no statement that Freud analysed Gross**. It says instead that Freud, having argued the opposite case in *Die kulturelle Sexualmoral und die moderne Nervosität*, "sorgte dafür, dass Gross aus der Psychoanalyse gedrängt und aus ihren Annalen getilgt wurde." No primary text was reachable; the journey file keeps the stop and writes the analysis as an open question.
- **6 May 1908**: admitted to the **Burghölzli** under **C. G. Jung** at Freud's urging; the treatment collapses into reciprocal analysis. **[A: de.wiki; en.wiki: "Jung claimed his entire worldview changed when he attempted to analyse Gross"]**
- **17 June 1908**: leaves before the treatment concludes; Jung's verdict is **dementia praecox**. **[A: de.wiki]** The garden-wall detail is **[R]**: the sources say only that he fled.

## 4. Ascona and the deaths, 1909-1911

- **1909**: publishes ***Über psychopathische Minderwertigkeiten***, Braumüller, Wien/Leipzig. **[A: de.wiki, Schriften list]** *Pool correction:* submitted as singular, 1903, Graz. Wrong by six years, in title and in place, and structurally: the error moves a mature monograph from before the marriage to after the Jung analysis.
- **October 1909**: **Sophie Benz**, lover and patient, becomes pregnant by him. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **1910**: travels to **Rab** with Benz; her psychosis develops. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **3 March 1911**: **Benz dies by suicide at Ascona.** **[A: de.wiki, "nahm sich am 3. März 1911 in Ascona das Leben"]**
  - **The poison clause is [R].** Two pool lenses said Gross supplied it. de.wiki's poison sentence attaches to **Hattemer**, not to Benz. Written as unattested in the journey file.
  - **Leonhard Frank**, who also loved Benz, fictionalises the triangle in *Links, wo das Herz ist*, Sophie under her own name and Gross as "Dr. Kreuz." **[A: de.wiki]**
- **6 March 1911**: to the **Casvegno** institution at **Mendrisio** for treatment. **[A: de.wiki, "begab sich ... zur Behandlung"]** *Corrections:* Casvegno, not Casvegna, and a voluntary admission, not a committal.
- **28 March 1911**: transferred to **Am Steinhof**, Vienna. **[A: de.wiki]**

## 5. Berlin, the warrant, the father, 1912-1914

- **1912**: a Berlin warrant for **murder and abetting suicide**, after he supplies poison to two women who then attempt suicide. **[A: de.wiki]**; the location within Berlin is unknown **[R]**.
- **February 1913**: moves to Berlin, lodges with the writer **Franz Jung** (no relation to C. G. Jung), joins **Pfemfert's** circle around ***Die Aktion***. **[A: de.wiki]**; the Wilmersdorf address is **[R]**.
- **9 November 1913**: arrested as a "dangerous anarchist," expelled from Prussia. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **late 1913**: **Hans Gross** has him shut into a private asylum at **Tulln**. **[A: de.wiki]** An international press campaign follows; **Mühsam**, **Pfemfert** and **C. G. Jung** are named among those intervening, **[R]** from the pool, the Jung signature unverified here.
- **1914**: the Graz court declares him **entmündigt**, legally incapacitated, appoints his father guardian, and he is moved to the **Landesirrenanstalt at Troppau** (Opava). **[A: de.wiki]** *Correction:* "legally insane" is wrong; the recorded ground is "Verschwendung und gewohnheitsmäßigen Gebrauch von Nervengiften," profligacy and habitual use of nerve poisons.
- **8 July 1914**: released from Troppau as recovered **[A: de.wiki]**.
- **9 July 1914**: aftercare under **Wilhelm Stekel** at Bad Ischl, where he meets the nurses **Nina Kuh** (1897-1955) and **Marianne Kuh** (1893-1948), sisters of Anton Kuh. **[A: de.wiki]** The pool named only Nina; Marianne bears his daughter in 1916, so the omission breaks the chronology.

## 6. War service and guardianship, 1915-1917

- **1915**: **Hans Gross dies**; **Anton Rintelen** becomes guardian and Gross contests it. Military physician at epidemic hospitals in **Ungvár** (Uzhhorod) and **Vinkovci**, Slavonia. Contributor to the anarchist-pacifist ***Die freie Straße***. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **1916**: daughter **Sophie Kuh** born to Marianne Kuh. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **c. 1915**: **Peter**, Else Jaffé's son by Gross, dies. **[R]**: en.wiki gives only "Peter (1907-ca. 1915)." It does **not** attest death at Wolfratshausen, gives no age, and does not name Max Weber as godfather; the pool claimed all three. He was seven or eight. The journey file flags the setting as reconstruction.
- **May 1917**: declared unfit for further service after repeated relapses. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **1917, Prague**: guest of **Max Brod** with **Kafka**, **Werfel** and **Anton Kuh**; they plan a journal, *Blätter zur Bekämpfung des Machtwillens*. **[A: de.wiki, "1917 war er in Prag – gemeinsam mit Franz Kafka, Franz Werfel und Anton Kuh – zu Gast bei Max Brod"]** *Pool correction:* the companion was **Anton** Kuh, not "Nina/Marianne Kuh." de.wiki dates Kafka's meeting with him to a night train from Budapest, July 1917, and links the influence to *Das Schloss*. Venue **[R]**: the source says Brod's house, the pool's coordinates land on Café Arco.
- **September 1917**: guardianship lifted; **December 1917**: reimposed in restricted form. **[A: de.wiki]**

## 7. Berlin, the last winter, 1919-1920

- **October 1919**: to Berlin, lodging with **Franz and Cläre Jung** in **Friedenau**, destitute **[A: de.wiki]**.
- **11 February 1920**: found ill and in withdrawal in a Berlin passageway near a friends' warehouse, the psychiatrist **Hans Walter Gruhle** among those present. **[A: de.wiki]**; the street is unnamed **[R]**.
- **13 February 1920**: dies in a clinic in **Berlin-Pankow**, pneumonia compounded by starvation and exposure. **[A: de.wiki; en.wiki adds he was found "in the street, near-starved and freezing"]**

**Gap, stated as a gap.** No burial place is recorded in anything reachable: no cemetery record, no grave, no Friedhof reference. Dying penniless in a strange city is consistent with an unmarked grave, but **no source located says so**, and the journey file asserts none.

## 8. Afterlife and rediscovery

- **11 January 1921**: **Anton Kuh** publishes "Die Lehre des Otto Groß," *Neues Wiener Journal*, p. 5. **[A]** *Pool correction:* the pool claimed publication "days after Gross's death." He died 13 February 1920; the essay ran eleven months later.
- **1921**: **Jung's *Psychological Types*** engages Gross's typology and credits him by name with priority on the extraverted type. **[A: CW 6, pars. 466 and 879]**
- **1976**: **Martin Green** on the Richthofen sisters, in German as *Else und Frieda*. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **1979**: **Emanuel Hurwitz**, *Otto Gross: Paradies-Sucher zwischen Freud und Jung*, the first modern biography, ending six decades of erasure. **[A: de.wiki]**
- **1980**: a selection of his writings appears, roughly 100 pages plus appendix. **[A: de.wiki, "1980 erschienen schließlich die 1923 von Franz Jung ausgewählten Schriften"]** *Correction:* Franz Jung made the selection in **1923**; it waited fifty-seven years for a publisher.
- **28-30 May 1999**: the **Internationale Otto Gross Gesellschaft e.V.** founded at its inaugural congress in the **Bauhaus-Archiv**, Berlin. **[A: de.wiki]** It **dissolved in 2017**, continuing as an informal working group (de.wiki *Otto Gross Gesellschaft*); the pool's present tense implied an ongoing society.
- **2011**: the eighth International Otto Gross Congress at **Graz**, 14-16 October **[A: de.wiki]**; **Vincent Cassel** plays Gross in **Cronenberg's** *A Dangerous Method* **[A: en.wiki]**.
- **[R]**: **Werfel** bases figures in *Die schwarze Messe*, *Schweiger* and *Barbara oder die Frömmigkeit* on Gross; undated.

### Verbatim quotations used

Reproduced exactly as printed, punctuation included.

1. Jung, *Psychological Types*, par. 466: "Gross deserves full credit for being the first to set up a simple and consistent hypothesis to account for this [the extraverted] type."
2. Jung, same work, par. 879, on Gross's manic-depressive versus paranoid dichotomy, which Jung reads as his own extraversion and introversion. Quoted in full in the journey file.
3. Maddox, *Freud's Wizard* (John Murray, 2006), p. 55, via en.wiki: Gross "talked all the time — even, if his portrait in D.H. Lawrence's novel Mr Noon is accurate, during sexual intercourse." *The pool replaced the em dash with a comma, garbling the sentence; the dash is restored.*
4. Kuh, *Neues Wiener Journal*, 11 January 1921, p. 5, quoting Gross: "Liebt euch ohne Gewalt, ihr Freien – und eure Kinder werden Geschwister sein!" *The pool substituted a comma for the dash and presented the line as Kuh's own; Kuh introduces it as Gross speaking.*
5. Kuh, same essay: "Er glaubte an die Herkunft alles Uebeln auf Erden von der Geschlechtsgewalt."
6. Franz Jung, *Grosz/Jung/Grosz* (Berlin, 1980), p. 105, on the death: "So ist er auch dann schnell gestorben, nicht im Kompromiß ..." Quoted in full in the journey file.

## 9. Contradictions between sources, not adjudicated

1. **Frieda Schloffer's kinship.** Daughter (de.wiki *Otto Gross*) versus sister (de.wiki *Hermann Schloffer*) of the surgeon. The first is impossible by arithmetic; both stay.
2. **Lotte Hattemer's death.** Poison supplied by Gross (de.wiki *Otto Gross*) versus suicide per the police report, the poisoning "Gerüchten zufolge" only (de.wiki *Lotte Hattemer*).
3. **Freud's analysis of Gross.** Asserted in parts of the secondary literature; absent from de.wiki, which records Freud engineering his expulsion instead.
4. **Salzburg 1908: one day or two.** de.wiki gives 26 and 27 April; en.wiki's IPA article gives 27 April.
5. **The Mendrisio admission.** Voluntary treatment (de.wiki) versus committal (the pool and much popular literature).
6. **Ascona coordinates inside the corpus.** Two pool lenses pinned Benz's death at 46.1567/8.7706 and 46.1547/8.7707; neither is Ascona centre. The journey file uses 46.1554/8.7736 for the town, keeping 46.1608/8.7658 for Monte Verità.
7. **Wedekind's *Hidalla*.** Pool gives 1904; the play premiered in 1905.

## 10. Gaps, stated as gaps

- **Burial place unknown.** See section 7.
- **Wolfgang Peter Gross**, legitimate son, born 1907: no adult biography, no death date, nothing beyond the birth. Searched in German and English Wikipedia without result.
- **Peter Jaffé/Gross**: birth late 1907 and death ca. 1915 are the whole record. Place, cause and the godfather claim are unsupported.
- **Camilla Ullmann**: birth only. **The ship years, 1900**: no port, no log, no correspondence.
- **Gross's own writings**: bibliographic list only. Every characterisation of his doctrine here comes from summary and from Kuh's essay, not from Gross's pages. **Hurwitz (1979)** and the congress volumes: not reachable. That is the standing gap in this life's evidence.
