## 2. The program, 1990-1993

**Written in Boulder 1990-1991**, RSA wrapped around Zimmermann's own symmetric cipher, **BassOmatic** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Pretty Good Privacy']**. **Name corrected:** the pool wrote "Ralph's Pretty Good Groceries", but Keillor's Lake Wobegon business is **Ralph's Pretty Good Grocery**, singular **[A]**.

**Motive.** His testimony ties the release to **Senate Bill 266 of 1991**, whose non-binding resolution would have forced trap doors into secure communications equipment **[A: Congressional Testimony, philzimmermann.com]**, and is quoted verbatim at that stop.

**Released June 1991**, free with source, via **Peacenet** and Usenet **[A: Wikipedia, 'Pretty Good Privacy']**. He says he cannot recall whether the postings began on the fifth or the sixth **[A]**; the journey uses `1991-06-05` and states the ambiguity. Overseas within weeks, a distribution he says he had no part in **[A]**.

**ViaCrypt.**

> **Year corrected against a primary source.** The pool dated the arrangement to 1994. The **New York Times of 21 September 1993** already names "Viacrypt, a Phoenix company that plans to sell a licensed version of P.G.P." among recipients of federal subpoenas **[A]**, and ViaCrypt PGP 2.4 shipped that November, so the journey uses `1993-11-01`. Phoenix coordinates confirmed.

**Contradiction left standing:** whether ViaCrypt's licence came from **RSA Data Security** (Wikipedia) or through **Public Key Partners**. Both are named in the stop.

**Interlocks here**, pins inherited byte-identical: **San Francisco, 37.7842, -122.4016, 1991** **[A: `rivest_shamir_adleman`]**, the first RSA Conference, about fifty cryptographers, the pairing against PGP's release year marked **[R]**; and **Redwood City, 37.4852, -122.2364** **[A for the company: same file plus `hellman`]**, RSA Data Security holding the MIT patent and, via Public Key Partners, the Stanford one. That Zimmermann's own unlicensed use put him against the same firm is **[R]**: the files document the Diffie and Hellman royalty fight, not his. Stop date `1991-09-01` is a flagged placeholder.

## 3. The criminal investigation, 1993-1996

**February 1993**, Zimmermann becomes the formal target of a federal criminal investigation for **munitions export without a licence**, run out of the **U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of California** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Pretty Good Privacy']**.

> **Venue flagged, not resolved.** The **San Jose** grand jury venue is repeated throughout the period press but **not** confirmed against any primary document. The journey pins it (37.3346, -121.8905) and says the venue is press-reported.

**Which agency.** Wikipedia frames it through the U.S. Attorney; his own testimony names the **US Customs Service** **[A]**. Compatible, investigating agency versus prosecuting office, but two names for one file, and both appear in the journey.

**Closed January 1996 without indictment, no public explanation** **[A: Wikipedia; his own testimony]**. Nothing was decided in law: no court ruled on whether publishing a cipher is protected speech.

**The book, 1995.** MIT Press publishes the complete source as the hardback **PGP: Source Code and Internals**, printed text carrying First Amendment protection that export-controlled software does not **[A: Wikipedia; philzimmermann.com]**.

> **Coordinate contradiction resolved.** The lenses disagreed on MIT Press: 42.3736, -71.1097 (2.3 km off, near Cambridge Common) versus 42.3601, -71.0942. The journey uses **42.3639, -71.0855**, 255 Main Street, and records the disagreement.

**PGPi.**

> **Causal claim corrected.** The pool said the International PGP lineage existed to route around the **RSA patent**, with a self-negating parenthetical ("distinct from the export-control problem, which was a patent issue"). Backwards, and struck. **Stale Schumacher**'s Norwegian effort supervised the **scanning and OCR of the printed MIT Press listing** because printed matter could leave the United States when software could not: the export problem, and the same workaround as the book **[A]**. Oslo 59.9139, 10.7522 confirmed.

**Gap:** the years of the scanning effort are not fixed anywhere reachable; the Oslo stop is dated 1995, the year of the book, and says so.

**Awards, 1995.**

> **Corrected twice.** Both lenses called him an early or inaugural EFF Pioneer Award recipient. The award began in **1992**; 1995 is the **fourth** class, with roughly sixteen honorees before him, Engelbart, Kahn, Baran, Cerf, Diffie and Hellman among them. Co-recipients: **Anita Borg and Willis Ware** **[A]**.

**Venue flagged:** the pool pinned the Pioneer Award to EFF headquarters, but EFF was at 1550 Bryant Street in 1995 and awards of that era were presented at the **Computers, Freedom and Privacy** conference. The journey keeps a nominal San Francisco pin (37.7825, -122.4204) and states the venue is not established. **[R]**

**Chrysler Design Award for Innovation, 1995** **[A: philzimmermann.com; Wikipedia]**. Venue undocumented; pinned to Auburn Hills (42.6875, -83.2341), flagged as an address. **[R]**

**Interlock:** the **Clipper Chip**, 1993, named and dated in `diffie.journey.json`, pinned there at Stanford **37.4275, -122.1697** **[A]**. That Zimmermann became its most visible opponent is **[R]** relative to that file, though documented externally.

## 6. After Snowden, 2013-2017

**Silent Mail, 9 August 2013.** In direct response to the forced shutdown of **Ladar Levison's Lavabit**, Silent Circle pre-emptively destroys its own Silent Mail servers, saying it could see the writing on the wall **[A: Wikipedia, 'Silent Circle (software)']**. **Gap:** where the decision was taken is undocumented; the journey pins the registered home, Nevis, and says so.

**Dark Mail Alliance, October 2013** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Phil Zimmermann']**, Zimmermann and Levison, for a next-generation secure email protocol. **Gap:** announcement venue not fixed in the pool; pinned to Washington, D.C. as a nominal American setting.

**The Snowden connection.**

> **Corrected: the pool had it inverted.** The afterlife lens claimed Snowden's first contact with **Greenwald** and **Poitras** "depended on PGP encryption to be secure". Snowden's approach to Greenwald **failed** because Greenwald did not install PGP; Snowden went to Poitras, who used it, and she later brought Greenwald in, at which point he set up the encryption, in late March or early April 2013. The first contact email dates to **1 December 2012**. PGP's role is real but negative space: the story is famous as much for the journalist who could not manage the software as for the one who could. The **broader post-2013 resurgence of interest in Zimmermann and PGP stands [A]**, and only that reaches the journey, in the Dark Mail stop.

**Blackphone, 2014** **[A]**, built by **SGP Technologies**, the Silent Circle joint venture with **Geeksphone**, running **PrivatOS**. The **Mobile World Congress, Barcelona** launch (41.3565, 2.1264) is trade knowledge, not independently verified. **[R]**

**Le Grand-Saconnex, 2014** **[A]**, headquarters from Nevis to a Geneva suburb, citing Swiss data-privacy law. Pin 46.2312, 6.1114.

**The 2016/2017 split.**

> **Conflation corrected.** The chronology lens put the end of hardware production and the return to the United States both in **2016**; the geography lens dated the move to **2017**, so the lenses contradicted each other. Wikipedia dates hardware's end to 2016 and says of the move: "Shortly after Smith became CEO, the company moved back from Switzerland to the United States." The journey **splits them**: Blackphone inventory losses end manufacturing in **2016**; **Gregg Smith** becomes CEO and headquarters return to **Washington, D.C.** in **2017**, which is where the stop sits. The split is recorded in `date_confidence`.
