# Frances Amelia Yates (1899-1981): research report

*Slug `frances_yates`. Compiled 2026-08-28 from the verified research pool. Legend: **[A]** attested, source named; **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source dossier claim not independently confirmed. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated.*

The spine is **J. B. Trapp, "Frances Amelia Yates 1899-1981," *Proceedings of the British Academy* 120 (2003), 527-554**, the memoir by her Warburg colleague: the richest source and the least checkable, since much of it (the audience of five in 1940, the aunt's bequest, the willow, the funeral address) exists nowhere else. Where a second source was reachable it was used, and where it disagrees with Trapp both are printed. The pool as delivered carried **eleven coordinate errors and five date errors**, corrected below, and its own lenses contradict each other on the place of her death.

## 1. Naval childhood, 1899-1918

Born **28 November 1899 at Southsea, Portsmouth**, youngest of four children of **James Alfred Yates** and Hannah Malpas Yates **[A: Wikipedia; Trapp p. 528]**. A secondary encyclopedia via the dossier gives 29 November; the one-day gap is recorded, not resolved. The pool's afterlife lens calls the father a **naval draughtsman**, conflicting with its own geography lens ("naval constructor") and with Wikipedia (a senior post overseeing dreadnought construction); the journey file uses "constructor to the Royal Navy." That same entry is chronologically impossible as written, placing the December 1903 Glasgow posting "before Frances was born … on 28 November 1899." Baptism at **St Anne's, Portsmouth Dockyard, February 1900** **[R: dossier lead only]**, no stop.

Moves, all **[A: Wikipedia]** unless noted: **Chatham 1902**; **the Clyde 1903** (Trapp's "December" is his alone, **[R]**), family house at **Kelvinside**, the father superintending Dreadnought-programme building on the **Govan** reach; **Laurel Bank School, Hillhead, 1910-1911**; then, after the 1911 retirement, **Ingleton, Llandrindod Wells, Ripon, Harrogate, Oxton** c.1911-1918. **9 Bellshaugh Road** and **Whitby 1911** **[R: dossier only, Warburg archive file titles]** get no stops; they survive in the last campa as fragment titles.

**Brother James ("Jimmy") Yates killed in action, 1915** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Trapp makes this the event that moved the family's whole expectation onto Frances **[A as Trapp's reading]**. The pool's place field is wrong twice over: a soldier killed in action in 1915 died abroad, not in "England," and the family was at neither Worthing (reached c.1919) nor Chatham (1902-03) that year. The stop is pinned at **Harrogate**, where the news plausibly arrived, and says so in `date_confidence`. **Gap:** no CWGC record was consulted; that would settle it. She then **failed the Oxford entrance examination her brother had passed** **[A: Trapp p. 529]**.

## 2. The external degree, 1919-1934

From **Worthing** **[R: Trapp only; the geography lens could not confirm it]** she prepared by correspondence for the **University of London external BA in French**, with classes at UCL: **first class, 1924**; **MA 1926** as an internal student under **Louis Brandin** and **F. Y. Eccles**, on French social drama of the sixteenth century **[A: Wikipedia; Trapp p. 529]**. Time in the **Loire valley** for spoken French **[R: dossier only; Wikipedia confirms annual French holidays, not the Loire]** gets no stop, and is folded into the Worthing campa in its attested form.

**1925: the family buys The New House, Coverts Road, Claygate** **[A: Trapp p. 529]**, chosen partly for rail access to the British Museum Reading Room and the Public Record Office; she lived there until weeks before her death. The same year, her **first article**, "English Actors in Paris during the Lifetime of Shakespeare," *Review of English Studies* vol. 1, from a PRO document, which the *TLS* praised for its "new and lively light" **[A: Trapp pp. 529-530]**. A 1927 follow-up using PRO finds and Montchrétien **[R: Trapp only]**.

**North London Collegiate School, French, 1929-1934** **[A: Wikipedia; the fonds says "late 1920s and 1930s"]**. The pool dated this "1930," arbitrarily; corrected. Until 1941 it was her only salaried work. The **aunt's bequest** is **[R: Trapp only, unverifiable]** and is carried as reported, not as established.

**1934: *John Florio*** (CUP, subsidized), praised by MacCarthy, Nicolson, Praz **[A: Trapp pp. 530-531]**. **Date corrected:** the pool put the **Rose Mary Crawshay Prize** in 1934; the British Academy awarded it in **1937** **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 3. The approach to the Warburg, 1935-1941

**Summer 1935: the Italian course at Girton** **[A: Wikipedia, verbatim]**. **Date corrected:** the pool dated it 1936 and bundled it with the Cornwall house party, a separate later event. **1936: *A Study of Love's Labour's Lost*** (CUP); Empson's review, *Life and Letters Today*, Sept. 1936, p. 204 **[A]**.

**Par, Cornwall: Dorothea Waley Singer introduces her to Edgar Wind** **[A: Wikipedia states it verbatim; only Trapp's "late 1936" dating is single-source]**. The pool's geography lens tagged this **[R]**; upgraded to **[A]** on its own correction note.

**Informal Warburg association from 1936/37** **[A]**. The library sat at **Thames House, Millbank** 1934-1937, then the **Imperial Institute buildings, South Kensington** 1937-1958 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Birkenhead High School, 1938** **[R: dossier lead only, duration unestablished]** gets no stop, and is a genuine hole in the employment record between Florio and the Warburg post.

**1940: first Warburg lecture** on the French Academies material, to fewer than half a dozen people in an unheated unlit wartime hall, at Saxl's and Wind's invitation **[R: Trapp p. 534 only]**. **1941: Saxl makes her Editor of Publications**, part-time, poorly paid **[A]**. **1944: incorporation into the University of London**; she keeps the post **[A]**.

## 4. The Institute, 1948-1957

***The French Academies of the Sixteenth Century***: title page 1947, actual appearance early 1948, delayed by paper and money shortages **[R for the slippage, Trapp p. 534; A for the book]**. Her preface names the London of 1940. **1950: Lecturer**, and **Gombrich puts Camillo's *Idea del theatro* into her hands**, "the book that made the difference" (Trapp p. 545) **[R for the Camillo episode; A for the lectureship]**.

**Family deaths. Contradiction, unresolved:** the dossier gives sister **Hannah Elizabeth Yates, 22 October 1951**, citing a certified death entry; Wikipedia gives "in March 1951 Hannah died of leukemia." Both are printed in the stop's `date_confidence`; March orders the segment. The **mother's death, October 1952**, is confirmed **[A]**. Wikipedia adds two dates the dossier omits and the journey file does not use: sister **Ruby** entered a nursing home March 1979 and died May 1980.

**September 1951: Italy, to Bruno sites** **[A for the journey: Wikipedia]**; **Campo de' Fiori** and **Nola** as specific stops are **[R]**, inferred from the general trip. One stop was written, at the Campo de' Fiori pin inherited from `giordano_bruno.journey.json`, its `date_confidence` saying both square and Nola are traditional rather than documented. **1954: "The Art of Ramon Lull," *JWCI* 17**, which Trapp calls the hardest task she ever undertook; **1960 sequel** proposing Erigena behind the Art **[A: Trapp pp. 540-542]**. **1952 and 1955 memory lectures**, and "The Ciceronian Art of Memory" in the Bruno Nardi volume **[R: Trapp p. 545 only]**, compressed into one stop. **1956 Reader; 1958 the move to Woburn Square; 1962 redesignated Reader in the History of the Renaissance** **[A: Trapp p. 529]**, the last without a stop.

## 5. The Hermetic books, 1959-1975

- **1959 *The Valois Tapestries***, the Uffizi series read as politique documents **[A: Trapp p. 538]**. **1964 *Giordano Bruno and the Hermetic Tradition***, written in under a year on decades of notes **[A: p. 527]**; first *NYRB* essay, on Kristeller, **19 November 1964** **[A: p. 549]**; **1965 London D.Litt.** by examination of published work **[A: p. 552]**.
- **1966 *The Art of Memory*** **[A]**, whose chapter XVI reads Fludd's engraved theatre as the Globe stage. **Spring 1966 American tour**: Johns Hopkins, Folger, Bryn Mawr, Columbia, Harvard, MIT, Newberry, Chicago, Northwestern, a week at the Institute for Advanced Study **[R: Trapp p. 551; no individual stop confirmed]**. One stop written (Princeton), the itinerary named in the campa. **July 1967 FBA, retirement, Honorary Fellow** **[A]**.
- **1969 *Theatre of the World*** **[A]**, contested because Fludd's diagram was printed in 1619, after the first Globe; carried in the journey file as contested. **1970 Ford Lectures, Oxford** on James I and the Palatinate, seed of the Rosicrucian book **[R: dossier only]**. **1972 *The Rosicrucian Enlightenment*; OBE** "for services to art history" **[A: pp. 546, 552]**. **1973 Senior Wolfson History Prize** **[A]**.
- **The Bouquet facsimile. Date contradiction:** the pool gives 1974, Open Library **1973**. The event is misdescribed everywhere too: **Charles IX's Paris entries took place in March 1571**, 1572 being the date of Bouquet's printed account, and Open Library's title reads "1572 [i.e. 1571]." The journey file uses 1973 and states the problem. **1975: *Astraea*, *Shakespeare's Last Plays* (Northcliffe Lectures, UCL), second edition of *The Valois Tapestries*** **[A: pp. 538, 547-548]**.
- **9 March 1974: the Clark Library seminar**, Los Angeles, testing the strong Yates thesis on magic and the Scientific Revolution; published as Westman and McGuire (1977) **[A for the seminar: Crossref]**. See "Stop ownership" below for why it is not a stop.

## 6. Last years, 1976-1981

- **February 1976: Smith College offers the Kennedy Professorship; she declines** **[A: Wikipedia]**. **1977: SRS lecture on Giorgi and Spenser; DBE** **[A: Trapp pp. 548, 552]**.
- **1978: cataract operations; Premio Internazionale Galileo Galilei, Pisa** **[A]**, the prize folded into the 1979 Claygate stop since travel to Pisa is unattested. **1979: *The Occult Philosophy in the Elizabethan Age***, the last book **[A: Trapp p. 548]**.
- **Summer 1981: Hungary** **[A for the country]**, **Budapest** an inference **[R]**; and the **last lecture, Manchester Cathedral, on John Dee** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **29 September 1981: death.** Date **[A]**. **Place contradiction, decided and printed:** both independent sources reached (Wikipedia infobox; arthistorians.info, "Died: 1981 in Surbiton, Kingston-upon-Thames") give **Surbiton**. The pool's geography lens asserts the Claygate house "though institutional records commonly give nearby Surbiton," inverting its own evidence, and its chronology lens says she "died at her Claygate home … a few weeks after leaving it," which cannot both be true. The journey file uses Surbiton and prints the disagreement. **19 November 1981: "An Alchemical Lear"** appears posthumously in the *NYRB*, seventeen years to the day after her first Review essay **[A]**.

## Contradictions, printed and not resolved

Five stand in the phases above, each carried in the relevant stop's `date_confidence`: **place of death**; **Hannah Yates's death**; **the Bouquet facsimile**; **birth date**; **the father's occupation**. Three more are corpus-level:

1. **Trapp's pagination.** The pool's quote lens cites the memoir at pp. 529, 531, 545, 547 for passages its own corrections place at 528, 532, 546, 548. The memoir begins at p. 527; corrected citations are used in the journey file.
2. **Warburg coordinates across the corpus.** `aby_warburg.journey.json` gives Thames House 51.4959,-0.1257 and Woburn Square 51.5236,-0.1289; the pool's geography lens gives 51.4928,-0.1259 and 51.5222,-0.1298; its afterlife lens 51.5225,-0.1296. Canonical pins inherited byte-identical, divergence recorded rather than averaged.
3. **`aby_warburg.journey.json` pins the 1944 incorporation at the Woburn Square coordinate**, though the Institute did not move there until 1958. Not fixed here, another subject's file. The Yates stop covering 1944 sits at South Kensington, where she was. **Flagged for the operator.**

**Corrections applied**, each also stated in the affected stop's `date_confidence`. Coordinates, per OSM: Southsea, Laurel Bank School, Oxton, North London Collegiate, Coverts Road (three pool variants), Par; the Clyde pin kept but relabelled **Govan**. Dates: Crawshay Prize to 1937 · Girton to summer 1935 · North London Collegiate to 1929-1934 · memorial meeting to January 1983 · Bouquet facsimile to 1973.

## Stop ownership

A stop belongs to whoever was actually there. Not written: the **1974 Clark Library seminar** (about her thesis; no source places her in the room, so it is a source on her 1964 stop); **Smith College 1976** (an offer declined by post); **Pisa 1978** (the prize attested, the journey not); **Mortlake**, which the interlock lens offered as a shared-geography pin for Dee and Yates, shared geography alone being no grounds for a stop or a name; and **Longwood, Saint Helena**, since the willow legend concerns a tree in her own garden and the stop is therefore at Claygate.

**One stop is reclaimed.** `robert_fludd.journey.json` carries **"London: Frances Yates and the memory theatre" (1966, lat 51.5074, lng -0.1278)**, staging her *Art of Memory* chapter as a scene inside Fludd's journey. By the standing rule that is **Yates's stop**, and it is written here at that exact inherited pin as "London, The Art of Memory." **For the operator:** Fludd's file should keep the `suggested_ref` to *The Art of Memory* ch. XVI and lose the staged scene. No edit was made to it in this pass. Conversely `john_dee.journey.json` **cites Yates nowhere**, though *The Occult Philosophy* (1979) and *Theatre of the World* (1969) are substantially about him: a citation gap in his file, not a stop for hers.
