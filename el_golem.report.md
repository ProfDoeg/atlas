# El Gólem, research report

*Compiled 2026-09-08 from the verified research pool, with the verifier's corrections folded in. Journey file: `el_golem.journey.json`, 9 segments, 42 stops.*

**Legend: [A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reported or reconstructed · **[N]** negative finding.

The traveler is not a person. It is a word that becomes a legal category, then a legend, a printed plot, a face on a screen, a machine's name, a pen name; its stops are where it was made, written, printed, filmed, searched for, or named. Where its only connection to a place is that someone else went there to talk about it, that person keeps the stop on their own file and appears here as a source: this applied three times, all to Borges (Geneva 1916, the Coliseo lecture of July 1977, the Israel visits of 1969 and 1971).

## Phase 1. The word (undated antiquity to the Babylonian academies)

Psalm 139:16 carries the noun *golmi*, "my unformed substance," its sole biblical occurrence in this form, joining the unformed body to a book in which the speaker's days are written **[A**, Masoretic text; Sefaria's Revised JPS 2023 translation fetched directly**]**. Composition and redaction of the Psalter cannot be dated or located **[N]**; the journey pins the canonical Temple Mount coordinate (31.778, 35.2354, inherited byte-identical from `aaron_the_levite`) and says in `date_confidence` that this marks liturgical centre, not authorship.

Sanhedrin 38b divides Adam's first day into twelve hours: dust in the first, an undefined *golem* in the second, limbs in the third, soul in the fourth, so golem there is a stage of Adam, not an artificial servant **[A**, Sefaria, fetched**]**. Sanhedrin 65b: Rava creates a man; Rabbi Zeira speaks to it, gets no reply, and tells it to return to its dust **[A]**. That passage contains no clay, no forehead, no *emet*, no Prague, no rampage, no defence of anyone **[N]**. Same page: Rav Ḥanina and Rav Oshaya produce a third-grown calf each Sabbath eve and eat it **[A]** for the text, **[R]** as to the event.

Locations for both scenes are conventional: Rava's seat at Mahoza is documented but the Talmud gives no place for the act, and Sura's site is disputed, so its coordinate is approximate. Both facts sit in `date_confidence`.

**Quote correction applied.** The pool's Rava quotation silently dropped the load-bearing sentence "Rabbi Zeira would speak to him but he would not reply." The journey carries Sefaria's full wording.

## Phase 2. The technique (931 to 1548)

- Saadia Gaon, head of Sura, completes an Arabic commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah* defending the 231-gate scheme while holding that human letter-manipulation is not creation *ex nihilo* **[R**, 931 is scholarly convention corroborated only by encyclopedia entries**]**. By then Sura's seat had effectively moved to Baghdad and both names are used for it; the file pins Baghdad and says so.
- Judah ben Barzillai of Barcelona writes a *Sefer Yetzirah* commentary preserving earlier creation traditions **[A]** for the work, **[R]** for the c. 1100 date, an ordering device.
- A commentary attributed to the circle of Eleazar ben Judah of Worms gives the earliest surviving explicit anthropoid procedure: two or three men, virgin mountain earth, running water, a body kneaded and circled while letter-combinations are recited, the sequence reversed to deactivate **[A]** for the text, **[R]** for the attribution. Eleazar's own dates float, c. 1165 or 1176 to 1230 or 1238.
- Abraham Abulafia, *Ḥayyei ha-Olam ha-Ba*, 1280: letter permutation, vocalisation, breath control and visualisation turned inward on the practitioner **[A]** for the work and date; British Library parchment Or. 10867.
- **Contradiction flagged (place of composition).** The geography lens placed this at Messina, which is anachronistic: Abulafia is in Patras, Trani and Capua in 1279, in Rome in 1280, in Messina only from 1281. No source gives a place of composition. The journey pins Rome and calls the place unattested.
- Moses Cordovero at Safed distinguishes *ḥiyyut* from *nefesh*, *ruaḥ* and *neshamah*: a human maker can animate without conferring a soul **[R]**, dating and doctrine both through standard scholarship, no primary read.

## Phase 3. The first named maker, and the lawyers (1583 to 1759)

- Elijah ben Aaron Judah, Ba'al Shem of Chełm, dies 1583 **[A]**, the first named historical figure to whom a creation story attaches **[R]**, by a family tradition written down more than a century later.
- Wagenseil prints Christoph Arnold's letter in *Sota* (Altdorf, 1674): a Polish rabbi "Elias" forms an anthropoid, it grows dangerous, the *aleph* of *emet* is erased leaving *met*, and the collapsing body kills its maker **[A]** for the printing, **[R]** for its contents, hearsay at two removes.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved (how the maker is hurt).** Scholem's Arnold has the golem grow beyond its maker's reach and crush him; the tradition descending from Emden's *Megillat Sefer* has the Name torn from the forehead and the falling clay only scratch his face. The pool attributed its quote to Arnold via Scholem, but verification traced the English to Dauber's SAPIR essay, which renders the Emden version. The journey prints Dauber's text with that attribution and names both endings as competing.
- Tzvi Hirsch Ashkenazi, *Responsa Hakham Tzvi* no. 93 (Amsterdam, 1712), asks whether a created man counts toward a minyan and rules that he does not, since Rabbi Zeira destroyed Rava's creature without consequence **[A]** for the responsum, **[R]** for the family tradition it invokes. A full translation of no. 93 read during verification holds no destruction narrative at all, which is why the scratched-face material cannot be sourced to it.
- Jacob Emden, *She'elat Ya'avetz* 2:82, compares the creature's performance to trained animal behaviour without understanding **[A]**. **Date corrected**: the pool gave 1750, but volume two belongs to the 1759 Altona printing. The journey uses 1759 and says why.

## Phase 4. Prague acquires a golem (1609 to 1919)

- **[N] The central negative finding**, and the spine of this phase. Judah Loew ben Bezalel dies in Prague on 17 September 1609. No work of his own, and no sixteenth-, seventeenth- or eighteenth-century Prague source of any kind, attributes a golem to him. The attribution is a nineteenth-century literary event.
- Achim von Arnim, *Isabella von Ägypten* (Berlin, 1812): a female golem-double, the bridge into German Romantic artificial persons **[A]**.
- Ludwig August Frankl, 16 November 1836: a brief entry reporting clay under the roof of the Altneuschul, known by the name Golem **[A]** to the day. **Source corrected**: the geography lens put it in Frankl's *Sonntagsblätter*, founded only in the 1840s. Dekel and Gurley (n. 17) identify it as an entry in the *Oesterreichische Zeitschrift für Geschichts- und Staatskunde* 92 (1836): 368, from which the journey quotes its opening sentence.
- Berthold Auerbach, *Spinoza* (Stuttgart, 1837): the earliest **widely acknowledged** attribution of a golem to Loew **[A]**. The pool called it the earliest, full stop; Frankl's notice precedes it by a year and already links golem, Maharal and Prague, so the file states the superseded priority instead.
- 1841: Klutschak in Prague's *Panorama des Universums*, Philippson in the *Allgemeine Zeitung des Judenthums*, Tendlau in his *Buch der Sagen* (1842) **[A]**. **Titles corrected**: Klutschak's is "Das Golam [sic] des Rabbi Loew," Philippson's simply "Der Golem"; the pool's "Der Golam" and "Der Golam, eine Legende" are both wrong. Weisel's "Der Golem" in Pascheles's *Sippurim* (1847) is then the mass-market carrier **[A]**.
- Yehudah Yudl Rosenberg, *Nifla'ot Maharal mi-Prag im ha-Golem* (1909), claims to reproduce a three-hundred-year-old manuscript from the imperial library at Metz. Scholarship treats it as his own composition, borrowing plot from Conan Doyle's "The Jew's Breastplate" read in Russian, and it consolidates the now-standard blood-libel-defender plot **[A]**. **Contradiction flagged**: records give Piotrków, a competing strand Warsaw; the journey pins Piotrków and leaves it open. The pool's "Podgórze near Kraków" gloss was dropped, that being a Kraków district unconnected to this imprint.
- Chayim Bloch, *Der Prager Golem* (Vienna, 1919-20), Germanises Rosenberg without consistently crediting him; his 1923 "Maharal letter of 1582" is regarded as a further forgery **[A]**.

## Phase 5. The bodies of the twentieth century (1915 to 1925)

- Meyrink's *Der Golem*, serialised 1913-14, book at Leipzig 1915: a spectral double, not an obedient guardian **[A]**.
- Šaloun's statue of Loew, 1914-1917 **[A]**. **Location corrected**: it stands at the Nová radnice, Mariánské náměstí 2/2 (50.086954, 14.417862), not the Novoměstská radnice 1.2 km south, where the geography lens pointed. The afterlife lens had it right.
- Wegener and Boese, *Der Golem, wie er in die Welt kam*, premieres at the Ufa-Palast am Zoo on 29 October 1920, sets by Poelzig, interiors by Marlene Moeschke, photography by Karl Freund **[A]**. The 1915 film is fragmentary, the 1917 one largely lost.
- The Old-New Synagogue attic: **[A]** that it has been searched repeatedly, **[N]** that anything anthropoid was ever found in it. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved (dates of the searches).** Kisch's entry is dated 1920, 1915 with publication in 1924, around 1910, and merely "the beginning of the 20th century" by four different sources. The geophysical survey is March 1984 in an unfetchable Radio Praga article, "the 1980s" in Prague tourism, 1985 in the afterlife lens. The journey uses 1920 to order the segment and lays the spread out in `date_confidence`.
- **Quote dropped.** The pool's "The attic was dark and dusty..." passage is carried only by an uncited tour-operator page, truncates before a clause whose slip into third person shows it is a paraphrase, and appears in no edition of Kisch. The stop states the fact in prose instead.
- H. Leivick, *Der Goylem* (New York, 1921); Habima in Hebrew at Moscow 1925, one source giving 25 December; a Yiddish production there in 1927 **[A]**. The Moscow staging is cited on the New York stop rather than pinned, the pool's Moscow coordinates being the Kremlin rather than Habima's premises.

## Phase 6. The golem in Spanish (mid-century Buenos Aires)

- The Idisher Folks Teater, Boulogne Sur Mer 549, is remembered as having staged Leivick **[R]**, with no register, dates, director or cast recovered. The 1950 date orders the segment only, and `date_confidence` says so.
- Borges and Guerrero, *Manual de zoología fantástica* (1957), expanded as *El libro de los seres imaginarios* (1967), carry the rabbinic and Prague material inside a bestiary **[A]**. Borges, "El Golem," *Davar* 77, pp. 3-5 **[A]**.
- **Contradiction flagged (month), and it is a live inconsistency between two atlas files.** The Borges Center bibliography gives 07/1958; `borges.journey.json` and some secondary sources give August 1958; one Wikipedia summary gives 1959. The journey follows the Borges Center and names the disagreement rather than harmonising it. Someone should decide which file is right.
- **Place corrected.** The pool labelled the pin "Villa Devoto," about 12 km west of the given coordinates, which fall on Retiro, at Maipú 994, where Borges lived from 1944 to 1985. The journey keeps the coordinates and renames the place.
- **Noted, not adjudicated.** "La cábala" at the Teatro Coliseo is dated 26 July 1977 by *Siete noches*, 22 July by radiosefarad.com, 20 July by `borges.journey.json`. It is cited on the poem stop, the evening being Borges's own.

## Phase 7. The golem of Rehovoth (1963 to 1981)

- WEIZAC, built 1954-55 at the Weizmann Institute, is decommissioned on 29 December 1963, the day its successor begins operating; the successor, first called WEIZAC2, is renamed Golem on Scholem's advice. Word length 75 bits, against ILLIAC II's 52 **[A]**. **Dates corrected**: the afterlife lens gave "operational from 1965" and a "1972 successor Golem II"; Golem A runs from 29 December 1963, Golem B from 1974 to 1983, and 1965 is the year of the address, not of operation.
- Norbert Wiener, *God and Golem, Inc.*, MIT Press, out of the Yale Terry Lectures of January 1962 and a Royaumont seminar in the summer of 1962; National Book Award **[A]**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved (year)**: his preface is signed 30 August 1963 and the text at simson.net carries a 1963 imprint, while MIT Press catalogues and the award record give 1964. The journey dates it 1964 and prints both.
- Gershom Scholem, "The Golem of Prague and the Golem of Rehovoth," 17 June 1965, published *Commentary* 41:1 (January 1966) **[A]** for occasion and date; the closing line's exact English rests on two agreeing secondary quotations, since *Commentary* and the NLI blog both returned 403. The pool's "do not destroy" was a normalisation; the journey prints "don't".
- Weizenbaum builds ELIZA at MIT, 1964-1966, in *CACM* 9.1 (January 1966): 36-45 **[A]**. **Citation corrected**: the pool's DOI 10.1145/3442188.3445922 is the 2021 reprint; the original is 10.1145/365153.365168, from the ACM numbering convention, since dl.acm.org returns 403.
- Lem, *Golem XIV*, Kraków 1981, partial form in *Wielkość urojona* 1973 **[A]**.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

El Gólem is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing was silently deleted; here is what I caught, with the direction named.

**Direction: forward.** Six stops connect the tradition to computing where no source in the pool has any participant invoking the golem: ELIZA (1966), Lemoine and LaMDA (2022), the ChatGPT release (2022), the model-welfare announcement (2025), and arguably the two Buenos Aires 2026 stops. Each carries the disclosure in its own `date_confidence` ("the link to the golem tradition is the compiler's"), on the `j_l_austin.journey.json` pattern. Two things make this weaker than an ordinary forward-reach problem. First, the subject has no death date: this is not a dead theorist's apparatus extended posthumously, and the file's final stops are the tradition still running. Second, three of the late stops are not interpretive at all, since Golem Aleph is *named* Golem, Wiener's book is *titled* God and Golem, Inc., and Lem's machine is *called* Golem XIV.

**Declined, and why.** The geography lens supplied five further machine-lineage pins with no golem attachment at all: Manchester 1950 (Turing's *Mind* paper), Dartmouth 1956, Toronto 2012 (AlexNet), Mountain View 2017 ("Attention Is All You Need"), and the GPT-2 and GPT-3 announcements. None is staged; each would be another person's stop, and the five subjects with atlas files (`alan_turing`, `geoffrey_hinton`, `yoshua_bengio`, `sam_altman`, `john_searle`) already hold or can hold the material. Turing's paper survives as a `suggested_ref` on the ELIZA stop, Searle's Chinese Room as one on the ChatGPT stop. Pinning that lineage instead would mean a forward grant in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, which is not my decision.

**Declined as shared theme or geography, not relation:** `mary_shelley` (Geneva 1816 and Geneva 1916 are a century apart, and the dossier's own instruction keeps the Promethean and letter-mystical genealogies distinct); `roger_bacon` and `albertus_magnus` (the brazen heads are a cousin legend family); `maimonides` (same Talmudic corpus, no engagement with the golem).

**Named in campa, direct relation:** `saadia_gaon` (commented on the golem's own textbook), `abraham_abulafia` (the same letter technique, turned inward), `judah_loew_ben_bezalel_the_maharal_of_prague` (the creature was attributed to him, and his file and this one record the same negative finding from opposite sides), `borges` (the poem and the bestiary entry), `norbert_wiener` (the book).

## Gaps, stated as gaps

**No `scholem.journey.json` exists**, and this is the largest structural hole. Scholem names the Rehovot machine, delivers the 1965 address, writes "The Idea of the Golem," is rhymed with the golem by Borges, and is reported to have met him in Israel in 1969 and 1971; here he can only be a source and a quotation.

Also open: Psalm 139's date and place; the place of composition of *Ḥayyei ha-Olam ha-Ba*; the IFT's Leivick production, with no register, programme or review recovered; whether any Prague Jew told a Maharal-golem story before November 1836; Kisch's German text, not located in any edition; Rosenberg's imprint city; and the identity of the signers behind the 2026 inscription.
