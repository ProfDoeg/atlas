# J. R. R. Tolkien — The Sub-Creator's Road: Bloemfontein to Wolvercote

**Span:** 1892–1973 (gregorian) · **36 stops · 9 segments · 25 attested quotes, 11 honest nulls**

## Register

Treated as *national mythology, the canon true* — but Tolkien is the rare case where the canon is threefold and the folds interlock: the **Letters** (Carpenter's edition), the **Biography** (Carpenter), and the **legendarium** itself. The life is narrated so the mythology reads as its true issue: the veldt becomes Rohan, the Somme becomes the Dead Marshes and Sam Gamgee, Edith dancing in the hemlocks becomes Lúthien, the fallen T.C.B.S. becomes the debt that drives the whole cycle. The invented events of Middle-earth (Fëanor devising the Tengwar, the Fall of Gondolin) are placed as *inferred* and narrated inside the fiction's own history, per the esoteric-cosmology rule.

## Judgment calls

- **Coordinates.** Real sites throughout — Bank House Bloemfontein, Sarehole Mill, King Edward's, Exeter/Pembroke/Merton, Roos, Wolvercote. Publication events (Hobbit, LotR volumes, Silmarillion) are pinned to **London** (Allen & Unwin), the point of issue, not to Oxford.
- **Dating.** Anchors are attested to the day where the record allows (birth 3 Jan 1892; Étaples 6 June 1916; Smith's death 3 Dec 1916; St Andrews 8 Mar 1939; Edith's death 29 Nov 1971; his own 2 Sep 1973). The blank-exam-page hobbit sentence is *traditional* (Tolkien's own recollection, which his sons gently disputed). The Welsh coal-trucks, and the in-world script-histories, are *traditional/inferred*.
- **Two Bloemfontein stops, two publication clusters** were kept distinct so the birth/christening-and-father's-death and the Fellowship/Return-of-the-King beats each get their own weight.
- **The grave letter** (no. 340) is quoted twice — at Roos and at Wolvercote — because it is the single document that fuses the life and the myth, and the register asks the miraculous be narrated as fact.

## Folds and gaps in the tradition

The great gap is Tolkien's own reticence: he loathed biography and insisted the work explain itself. The tradition therefore over-reads a few origin-anecdotes (the tarantula, the exam page) that he half-disowned. The legendarium's internal chronology is vast but fictional; I placed only its script-origins and the Fall of Gondolin, keeping the frame the *human* journey.

## The five richest episodes

1. **Roos, the hemlock glade (1917)** — Edith dances; Beren and Lúthien are conceived; the names later cut on their shared stone. The keystone.
2. **Souastre, the death of G. B. Smith (Dec 1916)** — Smith's last letter lays the charge on the survivor to "say the things I have tried to say"; the mythology becomes a debt to the dead.
3. **The Éarendel line of Cynewulf (1913)** — a single Old English couplet cracks open the whole world; *middangeard* becomes Middle-earth.
4. **The blank exam page at Northmoor Road (1930)** — "In a hole in the ground there lived a hobbit," written without intent, opens the door.
5. **St Andrews, "On Fairy-Stories" (1939)** — sub-creation and eucatastrophe named; the theology of the whole enterprise confessed aloud.

## How it connects to the atlas

Tolkien is placed as **a maker of scripts**: the Tengwar and Cirth stop deliberately faces **Barbier** and **Braille** (the fingertip alphabets, the night-writing) and, self-referentially, the quipu's knotted cords — an invented writing-system dreamed whole by one mind, exactly the atlas's recurring figure. His **North** — Beowulf, the Edda, the Kalevala, the sagas — faces William Morris and the Icelandic strand elsewhere in the collection. The **Somme** segment rhymes with the atlas's other war-forged visionaries (Jung's confrontation, the trench-born mythographers). And the whole is a **journey of exile and return** — veldt to Shire to Grey Havens — kin to the Goethe *Italian Journey* template that seeds the directory: a mythic itinerary whose last station is a single grave under two invented names made true.
