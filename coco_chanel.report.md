# Coco Chanel: research report
*Gabrielle Bonheur Chanel, 1883-1971. Saumur, Aubazine, Moulins, rue Cambon, the Ritz, Madrid, Lausanne. Researched 2026-08. Queue brief: the occupation years, Dincklage, Operation Modellhut, the clearance without prosecution, the fashion-house cluster already in the atlas.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a historian's characterization not grounded in a located primary document. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; this report does not adjudicate between biographers. Chanel revised her own account for fifty years, so nearly everything tagged [A] comes from paper she did not control, and nearly everything tagged [R] comes from her or from people repeating her.

---

## 1. Origin and removal (1883-1901)

**Born 19 August 1883 at Saumur**, in the charity hospital of the Sisters of Providence, to Eugénie Jeanne Devolle, a laundrywoman, and Albert Chanel, an itinerant market trader who was not present; the parents married in 1884. **[A: Wikipedia; Picardie 2010.]** The coordinate is a town-centre approximation, the ward not pinpointed anywhere reachable.

**The mother's death is the first hard contradiction, and it moves everything after it.** One strand of the pool dated it 1894; the French record dates it **6 February 1895 at Brive-la-Gaillarde**, of tuberculosis, and neither national article supports 1894. **[A: fr.wikipedia.]** Inside that sits a second: fr.wikipedia gives her age at death as **31**, en.wikipedia as **32**. Both are printed, neither corrects the other, and **the discrepancy is recorded and not resolved**. A third layer: one lens tagged Brive **[R]** because some biographers place the death elsewhere in the Auvergne. That hedge is over-cautious, and the journey file carries **[A]** noting the address within Brive is undocumented.

Because Aubazine is anchored to the death, correcting 1894 to 1895 shifts the whole block by a year. The journey file dates the orphanage stop **1895-03-01** and says in `date_confidence` that the month is a placement after the death, not an admission record. **No admission register is quoted in any reachable source**, and that gap sits under the most-repeated origin story in twentieth-century fashion.

**Aubazine, the Abbaye Saint-Étienne, c. 1895 to 1900 or 1901**, under the Congrégation du Sacré-Cœur de Marie. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Aubazine'; Picardie 2010.]** That the abbey's stonework and rose windows are the source of the house palette and the doubled C is **[A] as a house claim**, meaning it is attested that her own maison says so, and **[R] as a fact about her mind in 1895**; the journey file states it in the first register only. Departure year 1900 or 1901, not adjudicated.

## 2. Moulins, Vichy, and the name (1901-1906)

**1901**, a Catholic boarding house in Moulins; **1903**, seamstress work and cafe-concert singing at the garrison, the repertoire including "Qui qu'a vu Coco," from which the soldiers' name **[A: Wikipedia; Charles-Roux 1975]**. The venue is not consistently named and the coordinate marks the town. **1906, Vichy**: a failed attempt at a stage career **[A]**; the pump room's identification as **La Grande Grille** is **[R]**.

## 3. Balsan, Capel, the first shops (1906-1919)

- **1906-1909, Château de Royallieu near Compiègne**, mistress of Étienne Balsan; begins making hats. **[A for the liaison and the hats; R for the span and the footprint, the coordinate approximating the Royallieu quarter.]**
- **1908, 160 boulevard Malesherbes.** Balsan's apartment; Arthur "Boy" Capel becomes lover and first financier. **[A: Picardie 2010.]**
- **1910, Chanel Modes at 21 rue Cambon. 1912: Gabrielle Dorziat wears Chanel hats in *Bel Ami*. 1913, Deauville**, jersey sportswear. **1915, Biarritz**, a full couture house, profitable within a year, Capel repaid. **1918: 31 rue Cambon bought; 1919: registration as couturière**, numbers 23 to 31 held by 1927. All **[A: Wikipedia; Charles-Roux 1975]**.

**22 December 1919: Capel dies in a car crash near Fréjus. [A for the death and the day.]** Sources disagree on the stretch of road, some placing it nearer Cannes; **flagged, not resolved**. The night drive to the wreck is **[R]**. "In losing Capel, I lost everything" is **[A] as recalled by Paul Morand roughly twenty-five years later**, verified through Wikipedia's citation and **not against Morand's French text**, which was not obtained.

## 4. Stravinsky, No. 5, the Wertheimers (1920-1926)

**Two dates were conflated in the pool and are split here.** Diaghilev introduced Chanel to Stravinsky **in spring 1920**; the family lodged at **Bel Respiro, Garches, September 1920 to May 1921 [A]**. The affair is **[R]** and **disputed by the Stravinsky family**. The 300,000-franc guarantee of the *Sacre du Printemps* revival is **[A]**, 1920.

**Chanel No. 5 needed two corrections.** Ernest Beaux presented the numbered vials **in 1920**, not 1921; the perfume went on sale **5 May 1921** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Chanel No. 5']**. The samples ran **1-5 and 20-24**, not a single sequence. The claim that No. 5 was **the first perfume to carry a designer's name is false**, Poiret's Parfums de Rosine predating it by a decade, and the journey file says so rather than dropping it quietly. Whether the laboratory stood at **La Bocca or in Grasse** is disputed; the pool's coordinate (43.5486 / 7.0122) lands in Cannes proper and is moved to **43.5486 / 6.98**.

**1922, Longchamp: Théophile Bader introduces Chanel to Pierre Wertheimer. [A.] 1924, the Parfums Chanel agreement.** The pool gave the split as Wertheimers 70 / Chanel 10 and omitted the missing twenty. The full division is **Wertheimers 70%, Bader 20%, Chanel 10%**, her share being stock with withdrawal from operations. **[A.]** Dropping Bader makes the arithmetic look broken and hides why he mattered; the journey file carries all three.

**1923: the tweed suit. 20 June 1924: costumes for *Le Train Bleu*. 1926: American Vogue's Ford comparison for the little black dress.** All **[A]**. No Vogue editorial address is reachable, so the New York pin stands in for the garment trade.

## 5. Westminster, and four bad coordinates (1923-1932)

The affair with **Hugh Grosvenor, second Duke of Westminster ("Bendor")**, dated 1923 or 1923-1924 and running about ten years, is **[A]**; the site of the first meeting is **disputed** (casino versus yacht), and the journey file keeps the casino while saying so.

**This segment produced the densest cluster of coordinate errors in the pass, all four corrected in the journey file:** Achfary, 58.24 / -4.75 becomes **58.312 / -4.916**, about 13 km out; Eaton Hall, 53.113 / -2.8874 becomes **53.1403 / -2.8775**, about 3 km; Mimizan, 44.0968 / -1.1971 becomes **44.201 / -1.228**, about 11.5 km; La Pausa, 43.7639 / 7.5006 becomes **43.7631 / 7.4664**, about 2.8 km, the pool value landing near Menton.

**Mimizan also needed a tag upgrade.** The pass tagged Château Woolsack **[R]** as thin; en.wikipedia names the château, dates it 1911, names **Detmar Blow and Fernand Billerey** as architects, and names Chanel among the interwar guests. Carried as **[A]**. **Achfary keeps its [R]**, its tweed story being loosely sourced.

**1927-1929: La Pausa built with Robert Streitz, the staircase modelled on Aubazine. [A]** for both architect and model, and it sits against her lifelong denial of the orphanage.

**19 August 1929, Venice: Diaghilev dies and is buried on San Michele. [A.]** That **Chanel secretly paid for the funeral** was tagged **[A]** in the pool and is **downgraded to [R]**: en.wikipedia's *Misia Sert* article has Misia at the bedside and paying. **Both attributions are recorded; neither is adjudicated.**

**Hollywood was dated a year late.** Chanel travelled to California **in 1931** with Misia Sert and dressed Gloria Swanson in *Tonight or Never*; **Ina Claire's *The Greeks Had a Word for Them* is 1932**, the second commission **[A]**. The Goldwyn offer, reported at a million dollars, is **[A] as reported**, press-derived with no contract seen. "Infantile" and "vulgar" are **[A]** as quoted.

## 6. The occupation (1935-1944)

The queue brief's subject, and where sourcing quality varies most sharply.

**1935: Paul Iribe dies suddenly at Roquebrune. [A.]** That Chanel financed his ultra-nationalist, antisemitic newsletter **Le Témoin** is **[A]**. That she was by this year injecting morphine daily is **[A] as a characterization from biographical research (Vaughan)**, explicitly **not** a court or medical finding, and labelled so. The house then employed some 4,000 people **[A]**.

**1937: permanent residence at the Ritz. September 1939: the house closes**, some 4,000 women out of work, "not a time for fashion" **[A]**, the perfume boutique left trading. **May 1940: the Wertheimers transfer nominal control of Parfums Chanel to Félix Amiot**, a Christian industrialist, anticipating Nazi measures against Jewish-owned property; returned after the war **[A]**. The queue brief holds but for one word: she was questioned and released, never tried, so "cleared by a court" overstates it.

**1940: the Ritz is requisitioned in part for German officers; the relationship with Baron Hans Günther von Dincklage begins. [A.]** One correction: the afterlife lens dated his intelligence career **from roughly 1928**, while his own article puts his arrival at the German embassy in Paris in **spring 1933**. The 1928 date rests on Vaughan alone and is attributed to him rather than stated.

**5 May 1941: the Aryanization petition.** Chanel petitions to take Parfums Chanel from the Wertheimers, arguing the business "sont toujours la propriété de Juifs," that it had been legally abandoned, and demanding "réparation pour les préjudices subis pendant ces dix-sept années." **[A]**, seen through **French Wikipedia's citation of L'Express (2005)** and **not** the archival document. The petition failed.

**The Berlin trip is the file's sharpest internal contradiction.** The chronology lens asserted flatly, as **[A]**, that Chanel travelled to Berlin in 1943 to meet **Walter Schellenberg** at the RSHA. The geography lens tagged the identical event **[R]**, recording that **her own postwar statements and several biographers deny she made that leg personally**, giving it to Dincklage or intermediaries. **Two lenses of one pass contradicted each other on the same event.** The journey file adopts the hedge and states both positions. Schellenberg's stated reason for using her, "a person who knew Churchill sufficiently to undertake political negotiations," is **[A]** from postwar British interrogation records, **seen via secondary summary, not the transcript**.

**Late 1943 to early 1944: Operation Modellhut.** Madrid, the Ritz, Captain Walter Kutschmann as financial contact; **Vera Bate Lombardi** recruited as courier and told the trip concerned a boutique; the letter for Churchill carried to the British Embassy on the Calle Fernando el Santo; her denunciation of both as "Nazi spies"; collapse. **[A for the outline, per Vaughan (2011) on declassified British and French files; R for detail some later biographers treat as unresolved.]** Vaughan's conclusion that Chanel "committed herself to the German cause as early as 1941" is **[R]**, contested, and stated as fact nowhere in the journey file.

**The Préfecture de Police index entry** reads "Couturier and perfumer. Pseudonym: Westminster. Agent reference: F 7124. Signalled as suspect." It is **[A] as reported in secondary summary; the archive was not inspected.** **Its provenance was misattributed in the pool**: F 7124 comes from a Préfecture document **published in Vaughan's 2011 book**, three years before the **late 2014** declassification one lens credited with it, so crediting 2014 inverts the chronology. A related slip is corrected in passing: one lens gave Vaughan's subtitle as *Coco Chanel and the Secret War*, where the book is ***Coco Chanel's Secret War*** (Knopf, 2011).

**September 1944: interrogation by the Free French purge committee, release within hours for lack of documented evidence, the free No. 5 for American GIs, departure for Switzerland. [A.]** The building is **not consistently named** and the coordinate approximates. **"Churchill had me freed"** is **[A]** as reported by her grand-niece **Gabrielle Palasse Labrunie**, but the **causal claim** built on it, that Churchill instructed **Duff Cooper** to shield her, is **[R]**, called gossip by some historians, and **no order has been located**. The journey file states the absence of the document, not the presence of the theory.

## 7. Exile, settlement, comeback (1945-1955)

- **1945 to the early 1950s, Switzerland**, living part of it with Dincklage, who had fled to Lausanne in 1944. **[A.]** **St Moritz / Suvretta House** is **[R]**, repeated in popular biographies without a documented date range, and was cut rather than pinned.
- **Payments to Dincklage and Schellenberg**, read by biographers as purchasing silence, are **[A] as reported**. Scope correction: support for Schellenberg **can only run from his release in 1951 to his death in 1952**, funeral in **Turin** paid for, and the journey file bounds it. **Dincklage dies in Mallorca in 1974 [A]**, no exact date, the pool's coordinate (39.6953 / 2.9976) a generic mid-island point; folded into the Lausanne campa.
- **17 May 1947, the renegotiated Wertheimer contract.** Lump sum worth roughly 12 million dollars in 2022 terms, 2% of worldwide No. 5 sales, living expenses for life. **[A.]** No franc figure appears in the source.
- **1949: recalled for the Vaufreland war-crimes investigation; denies all accusations. [A.]** "I could arrange for a declaration to come from Mr. Duff Cooper." **[A.]**
- **1953: La Pausa sold to Emery Reves. 5 February 1954: the comeback at seventy**, financed entirely by Pierre Wertheimer; French press cold, citing the war, American and British press favourable. **March 1954: Marie-Hélène Arnaud in American Vogue**; Seventh Avenue copies the suit through 1954-1955 **[R: an industry, not an address]**.

## 8. Death and afterlife (1971-2023)

**10 January 1971, the Ritz. [A.]** One unadjudicated wrinkle: the quotation of her last words ("You see, this is how you die.") is dated **9 January** while the death is dated **10 January**; both are printed, and the journey file dates the stop to the death. **Funeral at the Madeleine**, Dalí, Dietrich and Saint Laurent attending; **burial at Bois-de-Vaux, Lausanne. [A.] Neither has a recorded day**, only January 1971, stated as a gap in `date_confidence`.

**The grave produced the file's worst coordinate problem: two lenses of one pass gave two different wrong values that also disagreed with each other**, geography 46.5138 / 6.6003 and afterlife 46.5182 / 6.6058. The correct value is **46.52139 / 6.59917** (en.wikipedia), plot **area 9, plot 129**, and both lenses are reconciled to it. The **five stone lions** are an attested carving; the **Leo reading** is interpretation framed as such.

**2020-2023: *Gabrielle Chanel. Fashion Manifesto*** at the Palais Galliera, travelling to Tokyo, Melbourne and the V&A (September 2023); the Met's 2005 Chanel show and 2023 Lagerfeld retrospective **[A]**. That these shows treat the occupation only glancingly is **[R]**, the researcher's characterization, not sourced to a critical study, as is the claim about the 2009 films, which were cut rather than pinned on an interpretive basis.

**2023: the ERIC network membership card.** Surfaced by **Justine Picardie**, disputed by **Guillaume Pollack**, who notes it was **issued in 1957**, shows **white-out alteration**, and has no corroborating wartime record. **[A that both positions were published; the authenticity is actively disputed and is not adjudicated here.]** No place of the find is stated anywhere reachable, so the canonical Paris pin stands in.

## Interlocks with journeys already in the atlas

The Ritz pin **48.8681 / 2.3286** and the Paris pin **48.8566 / 2.3522** are inherited byte-identical from the Dior and Perón files.

- **marcel_proust** and **eva_peron**: Proust worked the Ritz in the late 1910s and early 1920s, buying gossip from the maître d'hôtel Olivier, and Eva Perón received Dior, Fath and Rochas there in 1947, both **[A]** in their files. Chanel's residency from 1937 is the missing middle term, and her Swiss exile means the Ritz stood empty of her during exactly the Perón visit **[R]**. House-style pass (2026-08-14): both names cut from Chanel's campa as coincidental co-location, no documented Chanel-Proust or Chanel-Perón contact.
- **christian_dior**: the strongest textual link, since the Dior file cross-references a Chanel remark "recorded in cristobal_balenciaga.journey.json alongside a parallel remark of Coco Chanel." Dior also spent the war inside Lelong's house, open from 6 October 1941 and dressing German officers' wives, while Chanel closed her ateliers and sold scent. **The Chanel side of that comparison is [R]: outside knowledge, not in the Dior file.**
- **cristobal_balenciaga**: Chanel is quoted in his journey judging him twice, admiringly and hostilely, both **[A]** from V&A materials, both in the same postwar decade. **The atlas records them unresolved rather than reconciled, and so does this file.**
- **louis_vuitton**, **le_corbusier** and **hugo_boss**: all three inside the same collaboration structure in the same years, the first two pinned at Vichy. **[R] in every case: parallel milieu, matching calendar, no documented contact.** House-style pass (2026-08-14): the Vuitton/Corbusier mention cut from the Madrid Ritz campa as coincidental; the Hugo Boss mention elsewhere in the file was outside this pass's scope and left as found.
- **Buenos Aires** (**-34.6037 / -58.3816**) reached this subject only through a general inference about postwar clientele. **No specific Chanel-Buenos Aires fact was found in any atlas file**, and it is not pinned.

## Honest gaps

1. **No admission or discharge record for Aubazine** is quoted anywhere reachable; the origin story rests on biographers and the house's own account. **Departure year 1900 or 1901**, unresolved.
2. **The mother's age at death**: 31 in one national record, 32 in the other. Unresolved.
3. **The Berlin leg**: asserted and denied by different lenses of the same pass and by different biographers. Unresolved, tagged **[R]**.
4. **The Churchill intervention** rests on family recollection plus historians' inference; **no order, minute or instruction has been located**.
5. **The 1941 petition**, **the F 7124 card** and **Schellenberg's transcript** were read at one remove; none of the three archives was inspected. **No day is recorded for the funeral or the burial**, only January 1971.
6. **The 1944 interrogation building**, the **Moulins cafe-concert** and the **Vichy pump room** are not firmly identified; all three pins approximate and say so, and **the Longchamp coordinate** was supplied by this file, not by the pass.
