# Juan Eduardo Cirlot Laporta (1916-1973): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-13. Subject: Barcelona poet, art critic, sword collector, author of the* Diccionario de simbolos tradicionales *(1958).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or self-narrative at second hand. Contradictions are flagged; where a correction pass resolved one, the losing reading is still printed.

**Read first.** The brief that queued this subject calls Cirlot a "hermetic knight of the Order of Toledo." No source located supports that. See section IX.

---

## I. Birth and formation, 1916-1936

- Born in Barcelona, 9 April 1916, to Juan Cirlot and Maria Laporta. **[A** en.wikipedia, "Juan Eduardo Cirlot"**]** The street and house are not named in any source consulted; the birthplace is placed generically in the Eixample. **[R]**
- Schooled at the College of the Jesuits, Carrer de Casp. **[A** en.wikipedia; the Casp address from *Cuadernos Hispanoamericanos*, "Cirlot: el poeta y su mundo"**]** The dates (c. 1922-1932) are inferred from his age. **[R]**
- Begins musical training; studies composition under the maestro Fernando Ardevol for roughly eight years. **[A** en.wikipedia; Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes author-authority record**]**
- Works at a customs agency and then at the Banco Hispanoamericano. **[A** en.wikipedia**]** No source names the branch or gives start and end dates. **[gap]**
- **Correction.** The research pool tied the Manuel de Falla circle to the Ardevol years. Misdated: the affiliation is the **Circulo Manuel de Falla of Barcelona, joined 1948**, housed by the Institut Francais 1947-1955. Falla was in Granada and then Argentina and never crossed his path. The eight years under Ardevol stand. **[A]**

## II. Two mobilizations, 1936-1943

- The Civil War breaks out in July 1936, when he is twenty. **[A** context**]**
- **1937: mobilized to fight for the Second Spanish Republic. [A** en.wikipedia**]** No source names his unit, sector, or a single engagement; the Aragon front is inference only. **[R] [gap: the whole of his Republican service]**
- **Early 1940: mobilized again, this time by the Francoist side. [A** Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes**]** No source explains the reversal, and no letter or diary bearing on it has surfaced in this research. **[gap]**
- Stationed at Zaragoza, 1940-1943, he is taken up by the local intellectual circle, above all by **Alfonso Bunuel**, brother of Luis, whose library gives him his first Surrealism; in this period he translates Eluard, Breton, and Artaud. **[A** Cervantes Virtual; en.wikipedia**]** The individual translations are not dated within the span. **[gap]**
- Returns to Barcelona in 1943 and resumes work at the Banco Hispanoamericano. **[A** en.wikipedia**]**

## III. Return, marriage, Dau al Set, 1943-1951

- **December 1946:** writes *El libro de Cartago* (subtitle *Diario de una tristeza irrazonable*) at a cafe on the Ramblas in the last days of the year; the book originates in a dream he had in 1944. **[A** MACBA catalogue entry, collection ref. A13082; Vaso Roto facsimile apparatus**]**
- **11 August 1947:** marries Gloria Valenzuela. **[A** en.wikipedia; WebSearch biography summary giving August 1947**]** The church is not named in any source consulted. **[gap]**
- Works at the Argos publishing house and bookshop. **[A** en.wikipedia; arthistorians.info**]** **Correction:** the pool gave c. 1943-1948; both sources place Argos **after** the 1947 marriage, and from 1943 he was at the bank. Read **c. 1947-1951**, ending at Gustavo Gili.
- **September 1948:** *Dau al Set* publishes its first issue, run off Joan-Josep Tharrats's own press; founders Brossa, Arnau Puig, Ponc, Tapies, Cuixart, Tharrats. **[A** MACBA; Wikipedia, "Dau al Set"**]**
- **21 October 1948:** his composition *Preludio* premieres at the Ateneu Barcelones. **[A** en.wikipedia**]**
- **1949:** joins Dau al Set as a writer and pens a manifesto-text for the group. **[A** Dau al Set publication history; Cervantes Virtual**]** Herbert Read, who knew him, calls him the group's "leading theoretician." **[A** Read, Foreword to *A Dictionary of Symbols*, 2nd ed., p. ix**]**
- **1949:** publishes the monograph *Igor Stravinsky* and the *Diccionario de los ismos*. **[A** en.wikipedia; arthistorians.info**]**
- **1949:** daughter Lourdes born. **[A** en.wikipedia**]** Nothing further about her surfaced in this research. **[gap]**
- **1949, Paris:** Joan Miro introduces him to Andre Breton at the Place Blanche; the friendship holds. Breton later names him the only Spanish poet to answer the *L'Art Magique* questionnaire. **[A** en.wikipedia; Surrealismo Internacional, "Cirlot revisitado"; amediavoz.com**]** He dedicates the second *Lilith* to Breton the same year, engravings by Tapies and Cuixart. **[R]** It is his only recorded trip abroad.
- **Correction, Dau al Set exhibitions.** The pool had Cirlot exhibiting at the Institut Francais in 1949. Per MACBA, that **December 1949** show was organised by the **Cobalto 49** collective and hung three painters only: Tapies, Ponc, Cuixart. Cirlot took part instead in the group's **second and final joint exhibition, Sala Caralt, October 1951**, with Arnau Puig. **[A** MACBA**]**

## IV. Schneider, Gaudi, the swords, 1949-1955

- **1949:** begins a close working relationship in Barcelona with the German ethnomusicologist **Marius Schneider**, who introduces him to the study of symbology. **[A]**
  - **Contradiction, resolved but recorded.** 1949-1954 (en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia, Siruela: "Entre 1949 y 1954") vs 1949-1952 (blogspot "Enigmatico Cirlot I" alone). Schneider headed the Folklore Section of the Instituto Espanol de Musicologia (CSIC) in Barcelona from 1944, which supports the longer overlap. **1949-1954 adopted; the dissent printed, not deleted.**
- **1950:** publishes *El arte de Gaudi* (Ediciones Omega, Barcelona). **[A** arthistorians.info**]** **Correction:** the pool conflated this with *Introduccion a la arquitectura de Gaudi* (1966). They are two different books and should not be cited as one.
- No source consulted records a dated visit by Cirlot to the Sagrada Familia or Park Guell. The inference that he stood under them rests on the content of the Gaudi books alone. **[R, explicitly inferred]**
- **1951:** joins the publishing house of **Gustavo Gili**, where he remains until his death, and begins collecting antique and medieval swords, a passion the sources link to a family habit of Sunday visits to see the sword of Pere IV in Barcelona Cathedral. **[A** en.wikipedia; "El coleccionista de espadas," *Diario de Leon***]**
  - **Contradiction, flagged not resolved.** The firm's historic headquarters at Carrer del Rossello 87-89 was built 1954-1960, after Cirlot joined; where he worked in 1951-1954 is not established. **[gap]** **Coordinate correction:** the pool put Gili at 41.3948, 2.1596; Rossello 87-89 is in the Eixample Esquerra at approximately **41.390, 2.152**, a kilometre off. **[A** ca.wikipedia, "Editorial Gustavo Gili"**]**
- **Academia del Faro de San Cristobal. Correction on three counts.** The pool put it in **Madrid**, dated it 1954-1968, and called Cirlot a **founder**. It was founded by **Eugeni d'Ors in 1946** at the **Ermita de Sant Cristofol, Vilanova i la Geltru** (approx. **41.224, 1.725**, 45 km down the coast from Barcelona), where d'Ors died in 1954; Cirlot was a **younger member**, with Oriol Bohigas and Joan Perucho. **[A** vilanovaturisme.cat; endrets.cat; ca.wikipedia, "Eugeni d'Ors i Rovira"**]** A 1968 tribute volume to d'Ors under the Academia's name carries a Cirlot contribution. **[A]** Date of admission unknown. **[gap]**
- **c. 1950s, Sala Gaspar:** reads unpublished poems aloud at the Consell de Cent gallery, among them "Introduccion," "El caballero rojo," and the "Cuarto canto de la vida muerta." **[R** Cristina Zabala Adrada, *Las galerias de arte de Barcelona en los anos 40*; MACBA on Sala Gaspar; undated in both**]**
- **1953-1960:** said to study medieval art under Jose Gudiol. **[R** blogspot "Enigmatico Cirlot I" only**]**

## V. The dictionary, 1954-1963

- **1954:** publishes *El ojo en la mitologia: su simbolismo*. **[A** en.wikipedia**]**
- **Daughter Victoria born. Correction: 1955, not 1954.** The 1954 figure comes from the en.wikipedia article on the father, which contradicts its own sister page; both articles on **Victoria Cirlot** give 1955, es.wikipedia giving **9 April 1955**, her father's own calendar birthday. **[A]**
- **c. 1955:** by his own retrospective diagram "Biografia: Mitologia personal," he turns here from poetry and Surrealism toward the sustained study of symbology, under Schneider and Gifreda. **[R** Arsgravis (Universidad de Barcelona), "Vida-Arte-Simbolo en J. E. Cirlot"; this is Cirlot's self-periodization reported at second hand, not external biography**]**
- **1958:** first edition of the *Diccionario de simbolos tradicionales*, Barcelona, published by Luis Miracle. **[A** en.wikipedia; Editorial Renacimiento author page**]**
- **1960s (undated):** sustained correspondence with **Fernando Zobel**, encouraging the idea that becomes the Museo de Arte Abstracto Espanol at Cuenca, plus a monograph on Gustavo Torner, one of its artists. No source places Cirlot in Cuenca. **[R** March.es; the relationship is epistolary in every source consulted**]**
- **1962:** *A Dictionary of Symbols*, translated by Jack Sage with a foreword by Herbert Read, published in London by Routledge and Kegan Paul and in the United States by Philosophical Library. **[A** Cambridge Core review listing; en.wikipedia**]**
- **1962:** his father dies. **[A** en.wikipedia**]** No detail of illness, funeral, or burial. **[gap]**
- **1963:** severs ties with Antoni Tapies. **[A** en.wikipedia**]** No source consulted gives a cause. **[gap]**

## VI. Bronwyn, 1966-1971

- **1966:** sees Franklin Schaffner's *The War Lord* in Barcelona and returns to it repeatedly; Rosemary Forsyth as Bronwyn, rising from swamp water, becomes the germinal image of his late cycle. **[A** en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia; Cine Divergente; Zenda**]**
  - **Contradiction, resolved but recorded.** The pool carried 1965 from Luis Antonio de Villena, who dates the encounter by the film's US release (17 November 1965); en. and es.wikipedia both date **the viewing to 1966**, the Spanish release. **1966 adopted.** Second correction: the film is set among eleventh-century pagan villagers in Frisia and Normandy, so "Celtic" is **Cirlot's framing** of Bronwyn, not the film's.
- **1967:** publishes *Bronwyn*, opening a cycle of sixteen books that fuses Arthurian quest-symbolism, alchemy, Kabbalah, and Sufi material; across the poems Bronwyn transmutes into the Grail, Christ, Ophelia, the Shekhinah, matter, Venus, the universe. **[A** en.wikipedia; Medievalia/UAB summaries**]** He describes an interior world in which he lives in the High Middle Ages, loved by a Celtic maiden, dying of a lance wound. **[A** IVAM, "Mundo de Juan Eduardo Cirlot"**]**
- **1969:** publishes *Del no mundo: Aforismos*, and expands the dictionary for a second Spanish edition, adding entries including material on phonetic symbolism; the corresponding second English edition follows in 1971. **[A** en.wikipedia; BNE catalogue data**]**
- **July 1970:** the essay "Bronwyn (Simbolismo de un argumento cinematografico)" appears in *Cuadernos Hispanoamericanos*, published from the Instituto de Cultura Hispanica in Madrid. **[A** Los papeles de Don Cogito, reproducing the 1970 text**]** Whether he delivered it as a lecture in Madrid in person, as some accounts suggest of a 1969 talk, or submitted it by post from Barcelona, is **not settled by the sources consulted. [gap]**

## VII. Death, and what is not recorded

- **1971:** diagnosed with pancreatic cancer. **[A** en.wikipedia**]** No detail of treatment or hospital. **[gap]**
- **11 May 1973:** undergoes surgery and dies the same day at home on **Carrer d'Hercegovina** ("Herzegovino" in some sources). **[A** en.wikipedia**]** His widow keeps the house until her death in August 2008. **[A** Lluis Busse, "Close-up no 3: Victoria Cirlot"**]**
- **Burial site: unknown.** Searches of Montjuic and Sant Gervasi cemetery material returned no entry for him, and no alternative burial record surfaced. **[gap, stated as a gap: no source consulted names his grave]**
- **No commemorative plaque, street, or monument** to Cirlot in Barcelona was found in plaque directories or general sources. Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence. **[gap]**
- Undated: the retrospective diagram **"Biografia: Mitologia personal,"** organising his life into a central column of vocation flanked by family and by the work, as a Vida-Arte-Simbolo trajectory. **[A** Arsgravis; undated in the source**]** It is the closest documented thing to a self-invented order in the record.

## VIII. Afterlife

- **19 September to 17 November 1996:** IVAM stages *Mundo de Juan Eduardo Cirlot*, some ninety works by the Surrealist, Dau al Set, Informalist, and Abstract Expressionist artists he championed, with documentation of his poetry and music. **[A** ivam.es**]**
- **1997 onward:** Editorial Siruela (Madrid) keeps the *Diccionario de simbolos* continuously in print across some twenty reprintings, a tenth edition by 2003. **[R** siruela.com catalogue page**]**
- BNE records translations into Hebrew and Portuguese besides English. **[R** datos.bne.es, summarised**]**
- **September 2016:** *El libro de Cartago* in facsimile for the birth centenary. **Correction:** MACBA **holds the manuscript** (A13082) but did not publish it; the edition is **Vaso Roto Ediciones**, prepared and signed by Victoria Cirlot, 500 copies, reproducing the version sent to Carlos Edmundo de Ory and the copy illustrated by Julian Gallego in early 1947. **[A]**
- **22 September 2020:** New York Review Books issues *A Dictionary of Symbols: Revised and Expanded Edition* (ISBN 9781681371979): Jack Sage's translation with new work by Valerie Miles, Read's foreword, an afterword by Victoria Cirlot. **Correction: 2020, not 2022**, per NYRB's product page. The Philip Pullman introduction appears in ebook credits only.
- **Victoria Cirlot** is catedratica of Romance philology at Universitat Pompeu Fabra and directs its Institut Universitari de Cultura; she teaches Celtic religion at the Universitat de Barcelona and symbology at the Universitat Ramon Llull. **[A]**

## IX. The "Order of Toledo": an unverified attribution

The queue entry reads: "Barcelona poet-symbologist of the Diccionario de simbolos; hermetic knight of the Order of Toledo." **No source located attributes any Order of Toledo to Cirlot** as founder, member, or inventor; two independent passes searched and returned the same result.

What exists is **La Orden de Toledo**, a mock-chivalric avant-garde society **founded on San Jose's day, 1923, by Luis Bunuel**, condestable, with Pepin Bello as secretary; Garcia Lorca, Dali, Alberti, and Maria Teresa Leon were **members, not founders**. It ran until 1936. **[A** en.wikipedia, "Order of Toledo"; A*Desk, "The Order of Toledo: Bunuel and the occult versus the hegemonic"**]** (The pool itself misattributed the founding to "Garcia Lorca, Dali, and their circle".)

The one real thread runs through **Alfonso Bunuel**, Luis's brother, whose Zaragoza library gave Cirlot his Surrealism in 1940-1943. That is a relation to a Bunuel, not membership in an order.

**Recorded as an unresolved gap and probable conflation, not asserted either way.** The journey file carries a Toledo stop at the canonical pin (39.8628, -4.0273) whose prose states the negative.

## X. Interlocks used in the journey file

Named in campa prose, each on a documented or asserted relation to Cirlot himself:

- **andre_breton**: met at the Place Blanche in 1949 through Joan Miro, friendship maintained; translated him in Zaragoza; dedicated the second *Lilith* to him; named by Breton the sole Spanish respondent to the *L'Art Magique* questionnaire. **[A]**
- **jung**: archetype theory as the methodological spine of the *Diccionario*. Influence, not contact. **[R]**
- **joffrey_rudel**: *amor de lonh*, love for a woman never seen, as the inherited template of the Bronwyn cycle. Literary ancestry asserted by the interlock research; no source shows Cirlot naming Rudel. **[R]**

**Not** named, though in the interlock pool: Balenciaga, Borges, Cortazar, Maradona (shared Barcelona coordinates only); Rosenkreuz, Bruno, Trithemius, Waite, Dion Fortune, Blavatsky, Dante, Kyot, Roger Bacon, Ziryab (lineage resonance, no attested contact or citation). Shared geography is not a relation.

## XI. Contradictions logged

1. **Schneider apprenticeship**: 1949-1954 vs 1949-1952. Longer span adopted, dissent recorded.
2. **The War Lord viewing**: 1966 vs 1965 (Villena). 1966 adopted, dissent recorded.
3. **Victoria Cirlot's birth**: 1955 vs 1954. Wikipedia contradicts itself; 1955 adopted.
4. **Gustavo Gili**: employed from 1951, headquarters not built until 1954-1960. Unresolved.
5. **Argos bookshop**: c. 1943-1948 (pool) vs post-1947 (en.wikipedia, arthistorians.info). Narrowed to c. 1947-1951.
6. **Academia del Faro**: Madrid/1954/founder vs Vilanova/1946/junior member. Latter adopted, three sources.
7. **NYRB edition**: 2020 (NYRB) vs 2022 (retail listings). 2020 adopted.
8. **Cuadernos Hispanoamericanos 1970**: posted from Barcelona vs spoken in Madrid. Unresolved.
9. **The death house**: "Hercegovina" vs "Herzegovino". Both printed.
