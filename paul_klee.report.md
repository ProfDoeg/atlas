# Paul Klee (1879-1940): research report

*2026-08-04. Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or contested. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Coordinates given here are the ones carried into `paul_klee.journey.json`.*

Klee is a well-documented life on a treacherous documentary base. The diary he kept from 1897 to 1918 was edited by his son Felix, and that editing is itself an open scholarly problem. The one sentence every account of him quotes is disputed as to place and date. Several standard dates in circulation compress events that straddle two calendar years. The corrections below are the substance of this report.

The research pool supplied to this run had five lenses. **They contradict each other in four places.** Those internal contradictions are recorded here alongside the external ones.

---

## 1. Bern, 1879-1898: the music house

- Born **18 December 1879** at Münchenbuchsee, north of Bern, to Hans Klee, a German music teacher from Franconia, and Ida Frick, a Swiss singer. **[A, en/de.wikipedia "Paul Klee"]**
- **Coordinate corrected.** The pool gave 47.0169, 7.4658, which falls about 1.2 km east of the village in fields toward Moosseedorf. de.wikipedia gives **47.0167, 7.4500**, used here. **[A]**
- Family relocates to Bern, **1880**. **[A]** The Bern pin 46.9412, 7.4526 serves all Bern-childhood and parental-house stops: a city-centre coordinate, not a house address.
- Violin from about **1886** at the municipal music school; guest of the Bern Music Association as a boy. **[A]**
- **Diary opened 1897**, kept until 1918. **[A]**
- **Citizenship, flagged.** Klee was born in Switzerland holding **German** citizenship from his father. The pool's geography lens asserted he was "born a Swiss national"; that is false and is not carried forward. **[A, de.wikipedia]** It matters at both ends of the life: it is why Germany conscripts him in 1916 and why he dies waiting on a Swiss file.

**Gap:** the Klee family's Bern street addresses (the pool names the Kirchenfeld quarter without a street) are not established. The journey uses the city pin and says so in `date_confidence`.

## 2. Munich and Italy, 1898-1902

- **Correction, two stages collapsed into one.** The pool's chronology lens has Klee "begin art studies at the Academy of Fine Arts in Munich" in 1898 under Knirr "and later Franz von Stuck". Two separate events. In **1898** he went to Munich to study **privately** under Heinrich Knirr; he was **not admitted to the Academy** (Stuck's painting class) **until October 1900**. **[A, de.wikipedia]** The pool's geography lens has this right, so the two lenses contradict each other; resolved in favour of geography, which de.wikipedia supports.
- Two stops accordingly: Knirr's school (1898) and the Academy (October 1900). Kandinsky enters Stuck's class at nearly the same moment **[A]**; they are Bauhaus colleagues twenty years later and Dessau neighbours twenty-six years later.

### The Italian journey

- **Correction, date range.** The pool tags this "1901". It ran **22 October 1901 to 2 May 1902**, seven months across two years. **[A, de.wikipedia]**
- Companion: the Swiss sculptor **Hermann Haller**. **[A]**
- Itinerary per de.wikipedia: Milan, Genoa, Livorno, Pisa, Rome, Porto d'Anzio, Naples, Pompeii, Sorrento, Positano, Amalfi, Gargano, Florence. **[A]**
- Four are pinned (Milan, Rome, Naples, Florence); the rest are named inside campa text to stay within the stop ceiling. A deliberate compression, noted rather than hidden.
- Rome, winter 1901-02: the Forum and the Vatican collections. **[A, diary via en.wikipedia]**

**Gap:** the Italian diary passages are quoted secondhand through Wikipedia and Wikiquote. The Kersten critical edition of the Tagebücher was not reachable.

## 3. Bern and Munich, 1902-1910: the lean years

- Four years in his parents' house, playing violin with the Bernische Musikgesellschaft for money. **[A]**
- **Correction, date range.** The eleven zinc-plate etchings *Inventions* are dated **1903-1905**, not 1905 alone as the pool's chronology had it. **[A, en.wikipedia]**
- About **57** works scratched into blackened glass panels, from 1905. **[A]**
- First Paris visit **May-June 1905**. **[A]** He comes back unconverted.
- **Correction, place.** Klee married **Lily Stumpf on 15 September 1906 in Munich**, not in Bern as the pool's chronology lens stated. **[A, de.wikipedia]** The pool's geography lens has it right: second internal contradiction.
- Son **Felix Paul Klee** born 30 November 1907, Munich. **[A]**
- **Correction, date range.** The Feilitzschstrasse 3 apartment and studio in Schwabing is dated by de.wikipedia to **1908-1919**, not 1906-1919 as the pool's geography lens had it. **[A]** The journey opens that stop at 1908.
- First solo exhibition, Bern **1910**, travelling on through Swiss cities; Galerie Hans Goltz, Munich, 1911. **[A]**

**Under-sourced, flagged:** the widely repeated account that Lily earned the household income by teaching piano while Klee kept house and raised Felix is consistent with the reachable sources but not directly cited in them. **[R]**; the campa states it plainly and this flag stands behind it.

## 4. Der Blaue Reiter, 1911-1913

- Meets **Alfred Kubin**, January 1911, who encourages the *Candide* illustrations. **[A]**
- Meets **August Macke** and **Wassily Kandinsky**, Munich, 1911. **[A]**
- Joins the editorial circle of the **Der Blaue Reiter** almanac, 1911. **[A]**
- **Seventeen** graphic works in the second Blaue Reiter exhibition, February 1912. **[A]**
- Paris, **2-18 April 1912**; calls on **Robert Delaunay 11 April**; translates Delaunay's essay on light into German. **[A, en.wikipedia; Zentrum Paul Klee biography]**
- **Sonderbund**, Cologne, May-September 1912. **[A, Zentrum Paul Klee biography]**

**Atlas crossings, all [R] as to Klee.** Laban's 1910 Munich stop already places the atlas in Schwabing "where Kandinsky is dissolving the object"; Klee is in that quarter in those years but is not named in Laban's journey. Steiner lectures in Munich 1907, 1910, 1913, and Kandinsky attended; **Klee's own attendance is not attested and is not claimed.** Mary Wigman is in Munich 11 February 1914. Hilma af Klint's link runs through Kandinsky and the 1914 Baltic Exhibition at Malmö. The campas name these as neighbours, never as contacts.

## 5. Tunisia, April 1914: twelve days

The most quoted and least securely dated episode in the life.

- Travellers: Klee, **August Macke**, **Louis Moilliet**. **[A]**
- **Correction, departure date.** The pool gave a Marseille embarkation of **5 April 1914**, a date in neither reachable source. de.wikipedia dates the whole journey from **3 April 1914** (via Lyon and Marseille), arriving Tunis **7 April**; en.wikipedia's "Louis Moilliet" gives 7-22 April for the Tunisian portion and names no departure day. The journey uses 1914-04-03 and puts the uncertainty in `date_confidence`. **[A/R]**
- Itinerary per en.wikipedia "Louis Moilliet": Tunis, Saint-Germain, Carthage, Sidi Bou Said, Hammamet, Kairouan, return via Palermo, Naples, Rome, Milan. **[A]**
- **Correction, coordinate.** Saint-Germain is present-day **Ezzahra**, at **36.7439, 10.3083** (en.wikipedia "Ezzahra"), not the pool's 36.7333, 10.2833. The town was founded 10 September 1909 under the colonial name, so it existed as claimed in 1914. **[A]**
- **Correction, coordinate and label.** The pool pinned "Kairouan, Great Mosque of Uqba" at 35.6781, 10.0963, west of the city centre; the mosque stands in the north-east of the medina at about 35.6814, 10.1036. Rather than move the pin, the journey **relabels the stop as the city** and inherits the atlas's canonical Kairouan pin, **35.678, 10.096**, shared with Ziryab and Dihya. The mosque's true position is recorded here. **[A]**
- **Correction, overstated clause.** The pool's Naples-return entry said the war began "within weeks" of the late-April return. It began at the end of July, roughly three months later. Macke was killed at Perthes-lès-Hurlus on **26 September 1914**. **[A]**

### The disputed sentence

"Color has taken possession of me... Color and I are one. I am a painter" is **diary entry #926**, dated **16 April 1914**, in *The Diaries of Paul Klee 1898-1918*, ed. Felix Klee (UC Press, 1964), via Wikiquote. **[A as to text and edition.]**

Place and date are **contested [R]**:
1. Most scholarship places it at or near **Kairouan, 16 April 1914**.
2. Other accounts attribute the same words to **Hammamet**, a few days earlier.
3. The published diary was **edited by Felix Klee**, and the extent of that editing is a live question, which unsettles the evidential status of any single dated entry.

**Not adjudicated.** The journey pins the quote at Kairouan on 1914-04-16 as the majority reading, states the Hammamet alternative in the Hammamet campa, and carries the whole dispute in the Kairouan `date_confidence`.

**Atlas crossings, genuine.** Jung walks the Tunis medina in March 1920. Flaubert was at Tunis and Carthage in April 1858 for *Salammbô*. Foucault rents a house at Sidi Bou Said in 1967. Ziryab and Dihya sit on the Kairouan pin from the eighth and ninth centuries. None met Klee, and no campa implies it.

## 6. The war, 1916-1918

- Conscripted as a **Landsturmsoldat, 5 March 1916**. **[A]** Franz Marc had been killed at Verdun on 4 March 1916, the day before.
- **Correction, coordinate.** Oberschleissheim is at **48.250, 11.567** (en.wikipedia). The pool's 48.2153, 11.5497 lands about 4 km away in Munich's Feldmoching outskirts, clear of both village and airfield. Transfer date 20 August 1916 is correct. **[A]**
- Clerk to the paymaster of the Royal Bavarian flying school, **Gersthofen, from 17 January 1917**, to the armistice. **[A]**
- By 1917 the work sells well and German criticism names him among the leading figures of the new art. **[R, Wikipedia, unquantified]**

**Gap:** the pool tags the Landshut training stop **[R]**. No reachable source confirms Landshut as the depot; carried into the journey with `date_confidence` marking the place as reconstructed.

## 7. The Bauhaus, 1920-1931

- **The pool's flagged contradiction here is spurious and is dismissed.** It set "January 1921" against "the Bauhaus institutional history giving 1922". No source gives 1922 as Klee's joining year. Sequence: **appointed 29 October 1920; teaching from 10 January 1921; moved to Weimar permanently September 1921**. 1922 is the year **Kandinsky** joined. **[A, de.wikipedia]** Flag removed, and the reason recorded here rather than silently dropped.
- **Correction, two events merged.** The pool dated Kandinsky's arrival and the first Bauhaus exhibition both to 1922. Kandinsky joined **June 1922**; the first exhibition (Bauhaus Week) was **summer 1923**. **[A]**
- **Correction, place label.** Klee lived on the **street Am Horn** (number 39, later 53). Not the **Haus am Horn**, the separate model house built in 1923 for the exhibition. Relabelled in the journey. **[A]**
- **Die Blaue Vier** formed 1923 with Kandinsky, Feininger, Jawlensky. **[A]** Folded into the Paris 1924 campa, not given its own stop.
- First Paris showing **1924**; the Surrealists adopt him. **[A]** Breton renamed the 1912 *Zimmerperspektive mit Einwohnern*; Breton later cooled, Max Ernst did not. **[A]**
- **Correction, two dates confused.** The **Bauhaus moved to Dessau in 1925**. **July 1926** is when Klee occupied the Meisterhaus; the new school building opened **4 December 1926**. The pool's geography lens dated the institutional move to July 1926, contradicting its own chronology lens: third internal contradiction. **[A, resolved to 1925]**
- **Correction, coordinate.** The Meisterhäuser Dessau are at **51.8432, 12.2205** on the Ebertallee (de.wikipedia), not 51.8319, 12.2277, about 1.3 km off. **[A]**
- **Egypt, correction to a range and two stop dates.** The journey ran **17 December 1928 to 17 January 1929**. The pool used 17 December as the *arrival at Alexandria* (it is the departure date of the whole trip) and 17 January as the *date at Aswan* (it is the return date, by which he was back in Germany). Both corrected in `date_confidence`; Luxor and Aswan folded into the Cairo campa. **[A, de.wikipedia]**
- Grohmann's first major monograph, 1929. **[A]**
- Düsseldorf Academy professorship from **1 July 1931**. **[A]** The Dessau Bauhaus is closed by the Nazi-controlled council in 1932.

**Dropped for stop budget, recorded here:** the family holiday in Italy, **24 August to 29 October 1926**, the Hendaye summer with the Kandinskys, August 1929, and the nine-day Venice and Padua trip, October 1932. All **[A]**, all real, all omitted to stay under 45 stops.

## 8. 1933-1940: struck from the list

- **Correction, place.** The Gestapo searched **Klee's house in Dessau**, not Düsseldorf as the pool's chronology lens had it. de.wikipedia: "sein Haus in Dessau wurde durchsucht". He was still domiciled in the Dessau Meisterhaus while commuting to Düsseldorf. **[A]**
- Self-portrait **Von der Liste gestrichen / Struck from the List**, 1933, correctly attributed. **[A]** Dismissal confirmed 21 April 1933. **[A]**
- **Correction, two problems in one pool entry.** (a) en.wikipedia gives the London and Paris exhibitions as **"1933-34"**, not 1933; neither is pinned. (b) The **Picasso meeting is misdated**: the documented encounter is his visit to **Kistlerweg 6 in Bern, late 1937**. de.wikipedia records no 1933 meeting, only Klee's visit to a Picasso show at the Kunsthaus Zürich in October 1932. **[A]**
- Emigration to Bern, **24 December 1933**. **[A]** Kistlerweg 6, Elfenau, from **June 1934**. **[A]**
- **Scleroderma** from 1935; about **25** pictures in the whole of 1936. **[A]**
- **Entartete Kunst**, Munich, opens 19 July 1937: **17** Klee works shown, **102** confiscated from German public collections. **[A]**
- Output: roughly **264** works in 1937, **489** in 1938, **1,254** in 1939. **[A]**
- **Correction, naturalisation.** The pool's geography lens said Klee "never receives Swiss citizenship in his lifetime". The application was **approved 19 December 1939**, during his lifetime; only the conferral, six days after his death, was posthumous. **[A, de.wikipedia]**
- Clinica Sant'Agnese, Muralto, from **10 May 1940**; dies there **29 June 1940**, aged 60. **[A]**
- About fifty angel drawings in the last months; last finished painting **Tod und Feuer** (1940). **[A]**
- **Correction, coordinate, and the fourth internal contradiction.** The Schosshaldenfriedhof is at **46.9526, 7.4755** (de.wikipedia). The pool's geography lens gave 46.9497, 7.4699 and its afterlife lens gave 46.9445, 7.4681 for the same cemetery, disagreeing with each other and with the true position. Both discarded. **[A]**
- Epitaph chosen by Felix Klee from the 1920 Goltz catalogue text. **[A]**

## 9. Afterlife

- **Angelus Novus** (1920 oil-transfer monoprint) bought by **Walter Benjamin** in 1921; into exile 1933; left with **Georges Bataille** on the 1940 flight from Paris; passed via **Theodor Adorno** to **Gershom Scholem** per Benjamin's instructions. **[A, Wikipedia "Angelus Novus"]**
- Benjamin's 1940 "Theses on the Philosophy of History" reads it as the angel of history. **[A]**
- Held by the **Israel Museum, Jerusalem**. **[A]**
- In **2015** R. H. Quaytman found the monoprint pasted over a reused **1838 copperplate engraving of Martin Luther**. **[A]**
- **1997**: Livia Klee-Meyer donates about 690 works to Bern. **[A]**
- **Zentrum Paul Klee**, Renzo Piano, three undulating hills at Schöngrün, opened **20 June 2005**, holding roughly 40 percent of the pictorial output, about 4,000 works. **[A]**
- **Correction, coordinate.** The Zentrum is at **46.9494, 7.4739** (de.wikipedia: 46°56'57.9"N, 7°28'26.1"E), not 46.9394, 7.4787, about 1.1 km off. **[A]**
- **Dropped as unsourced.** The pool's "about 150 works every six months" rotation figure is in neither the German nor the English article on the museum. The light-sensitivity and the rotation are real; the number is not carried forward.

## Contradictions flagged and left open

1. **Where and when Klee wrote "color and I are one."** Kairouan, 16 April 1914 (majority) against Hammamet a few days earlier (minority), with Felix Klee's editing unsettling both. Not resolved; the journey states both.
2. **Four internal contradictions in the research pool**, chronology lens against geography lens: the 1898 Munich arrival, the place of the 1906 marriage, the year of the Dessau move, and two different wrong coordinates for one cemetery. Each is recorded above with its resolution and the reason.
3. **The London and Paris exhibitions**, 1933 in one place and 1933-34 in another. Not pinned.
4. **The 1933 Nazi press attack** is quoted on Wikiquote without a named newspaper. Tagged **[R]** in the journey's `quote_source`, which says so.

## Honest gaps

- **The diary in a critical edition.** Every diary quotation here runs through the Felix Klee edition of 1964 as relayed by Wikiquote. Kersten's 1988 unabridged critical edition of the *Tagebücher*, the text that would make the editing question tractable, was not reachable. The largest gap in the file.
- **Grohmann 1929**, the first monograph: not reachable.
- **The catalogue raisonné** (Paul Klee Stiftung, 9 vols., 1998-2004): not reachable. Work counts and titles here are Wikipedia's.
- **Sumpflegende (Swamp Legend, 1919)**, named by Wikipedia as a Nazi-era looting and restitution case with no claimant, holder, or resolution given. No provenance source reachable. Left as a gap; not pinned.
- **Bern street addresses before 1934**: not established. City pin used.
- **The newspaper** carrying the 1933 "Galician Jew" attack: not traced.
- **Landshut** as the 1916 training depot: unconfirmed.
