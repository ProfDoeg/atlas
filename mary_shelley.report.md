# Mary Wollstonecraft Shelley (1797-1851): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-14 for `mary_shelley.journey.json`. Documented travel 1814-1843: England, France, Switzerland, the Rhine, the Netherlands, Italy, Germany, plus the childhood in Somers Town and Skinner Street and the two Dundee stays.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or biographers' consensus that no document in reach carries. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; nothing is silently harmonised. Where the verified pool disagrees with its own corrections, both readings are printed.

---

## 1. The Godwin household, 1797-1814

- Born 30 August 1797 at 29 The Polygon, Somers Town, to William Godwin and Mary Wollstonecraft **[A: Wikipedia, 'Mary Shelley'; St Pancras parish records; Seymour, *Mary Shelley* (2000)]**. The Polygon is demolished; the site lies near present-day Werrington Street **[A: Seymour]**. Wollstonecraft dies of puerperal fever eleven days later in the same house **[A: same]**.
- Godwin marries Mary Jane Clairmont, December 1801 **[A: Wikipedia, 'William Godwin']**. **Correction carried:** the pool's chronology called Claire Clairmont a "stepdaughter" of Mary Jane. Claire was Mary Jane's biological daughter and became Mary's **stepsister** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Mary Shelley']**.
- Skinner Street, 1807-1814, over the Juvenile Library, with Fanny Imlay and Claire **[A: Seymour; Godwin's diary]**. The journey pins the move at 1807-01-01, month unknown.
- Ramsgate school, about six months, 1811 **[A: Wikipedia]**; no stop, dropped for length. Two Dundee stays with William Baxter's family, some sixteen months in all, from 1812 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Coleridge reading *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner* with Mary hidden behind the sofa: [R].** Universal in the biographies, in no document in the pool. Kept in the campa as tradition, worded "the family story has".

**Contradiction 1: the first meeting.** The chronology gives 1814 **[R]**, "exact date uncertain." The pool's own correction gives **November 1812**, Shelley dining at Skinner Street, with the consequential re-meeting before 30 March 1814. That correction adds the dinner fell "shortly before Mary's first Dundee stay," which conflicts with the same pool's chronology dating the first Dundee stay to 1812 (standard biography: north in June 1812, back in November 1812, north again June 1813). On the standard chronology November 1812 falls *between* the stays. **Not adjudicated**; the stop is dated 1812-11-01 and its `date_confidence` records the conflict.

**Contradiction 2: the graveside declaration.** Chronology: "Mary declares her love to Percy Shelley" at her mother's grave, 26 June 1814, tagged **[A]**. Geography, same site: "the graveside courtship scene itself is R." **The two lenses disagree on the tag for one event.** Documentary: a meeting on that day (Godwin's diary). Traditional: the declaration over the tombstone, built afterwards from the diary and Shelley's verse. The campa states the meeting and names the tradition as tradition.

## 2. The elopement and the Six Weeks' Tour, July-September 1814

Route, all **[A: *History of a Six Weeks' Tour* (1817), Gutenberg 52790]**: London (28 July, before dawn), Dover and a hired night crossing, Calais at dawn, a week in the rue Vivienne, Charenton, Troyes, Neuchâtel, Lucerne and Brunnen, down the Rhine, Rotterdam and Maassluis, Gravesend on 13 September.

- Mary Jane Godwin follows to Calais and fails to bring Claire home **[A: *Tour*; Claire Clairmont's journal]**.
- **Correction carried:** the donkey was bought after Paris, at Charenton; the mule replaced it when the ass proved unable to carry the baggage **[A: *Tour*, Part I]**. The pool's Paris entry had misplaced the purchase.
- **Quote dating corrected twice.** (a) Claire's "let us live here" passage was dated 28 July; the text places it after the Paris week, so 8 August. (b) The "Finding our ass useless" passage was dated 8 August; they reached Charenton about ten at night on the 8th and left "about nine o'clock" next morning, so **9 August**, the date used in the journey. The Claire quote is recorded here only.
- **Contradiction 3: the money.** Polidori's diary of 30 May 1816 has Shelley leaving Lucerne "with merely £26" **[A: Polidori, *Diary*, ed. W. M. Rossetti (1911), Gutenberg 55017]**; the biographies give £28, the figure used in the campa. **Not adjudicated.**
- **Polidori's 30 May entry is unreliable testimony.** The pool tagged it [A] and glossed it "in Shelley's own words." Rossetti, its editor, says the reverse: Polidori is "putting down (but not in the words of Shelley himself, who would assuredly not have said that he had 'seduced' Mary Godwin)" and the details "are far from accurate." Not used in the journey file.
- Landing at Gravesend, 13 September **[A: *Tour*]**. The boatman holding them aboard until Shelley fetched money from Harriet is **[R]**, standard biography.

## 4. Geneva, May to September 1816

- Shelley party at Geneva, 14 May 1816 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Contradiction 4, corrected:** the chronology fused two events under 25 May, "Byron arrives with Polidori and rents the Villa Diodati." Byron reached Geneva with Polidori on **25 May** and took Diodati only on **10 June**, on a lease to 1 November **[A: Wikipedia, 'Villa Diodati']**. For the first fortnight both parties were at the Hôtel d'Angleterre, Sécheron; the Maison Chapuis lease also begins in June. The journey splits arrival from villa.
- The cold wet summer, the confinement, the galvanism talk, Byron's proposal **[A: Mary Shelley's 1831 introduction, Gutenberg 42324; Wikipedia, 'Frankenstein']**. The Tambora link is **[A]** as reported, **[R]** as causation for any given day's weather.
- The waking dream is **[A]** as her own account (1831). Its dating to between 2 and 3 a.m. on 16 June 1816 is **[R]**, resting on Donald Olson's 2011 astronomical reconstruction, reached only through Wikipedia.
- Polidori's fragment becomes *The Vampyre* (1819) **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Chamonix and the Mer de Glace, July 1816 **[A: *Tour*; journal]**; the glacier is the setting of *Frankenstein* ch. 10 **[A: the novel]**.
- **Mary reading Rousseau's *Julie* on his lake: [R]**, from her 1816 reading and the party's deliberate retracing of *La Nouvelle Héloïse* by boat, whose letters she published in the *Tour*. This is the ground on which Rousseau is named in the campa.

## 5. Bath, Marlow, *Frankenstein*, 1816-1818

- Bath from September 1816, partly to hide Claire's pregnancy **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Contradiction 5, corrected:** the pool dated Fanny Imlay's death 10 October 1816. She took laudanum and died on the night of **9 October** at the Mackworth Arms, Swansea; the body was found on the 10th; Godwin and Shelley traced her on the 11th **[A: Wikipedia, 'Fanny Imlay']**. The pool's date is the discovery.
- Harriet Shelley drowned in the Serpentine, found 10 December 1816 **[A]**. Marriage at St Mildred's, Bread Street, 30 December **[A]**. Chancery denies custody, March 1817 **[A]**.
- **Contradiction 6, corrected:** the pool's geography put the winter 1816-17 composition at Marlow while dating the entry 1817. The Shelleys moved to Marlow in **March 1817**; most drafting was done at **Bath** through the previous winter, and the manuscript was finished at Marlow in **April/May 1817** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Frankenstein']**.
- Clara Everina born 2 September 1817 at Marlow **[A]**. *History of a Six Weeks' Tour* published November 1817, her first book **[A]**, unsigned.
- *Frankenstein* published 1 January 1818, anonymous, three volumes, about 500 copies **[A: Wikipedia; the imprint]**. **Correction carried:** the publisher is **Lackington, Hughes, Harding, Mavor and Jones**; "Lackington, Allen & Co." was the firm's superseded earlier style **[A: the 1818 imprint]**. Finsbury Square, the Temple of the Muses, is correct.
- **Percy Shelley's hand in the manuscript, the refusals by Ollier and Murray, Walter Scott's review: [R]** for this dossier. Well documented elsewhere (Bodleian notebooks; *Blackwood's*, March 1818), not in the pool.
- **Goethe's *Werther* as one of the creature's three books: [A]**, from the novel itself, with Plutarch and Milton. This is why Goethe is named in the Marlow campa: a citation inside her text, not a shared road.

## 6. Italy, 1818-1822

- Departure 12 March 1818 **[A]**; Milan in April **[A: journal]**.
- **Contradiction 7, adjudicated on the pool's own evidence.** The chronology says Allegra was handed to Byron "in Venice" with the party present; the geography says she "is sent from here to Byron in Venice" from Milan. The documented version is Milan: the child left on **28 April 1818** in the care of the nurse Elise Duvillard, the Shelleys not present. The journey follows Milan; the conflict is recorded.
- Bagni di Lucca, summer 1818 **[A: journal; Seymour]**. Its pool coordinates are about 1.5 km off; no stop assigned, so the error does not propagate.
- Clara dies at Venice, September 1818 **[A]**. That Shelley's summons brought mother and sick child onto the road is **[R]**, standard biography consistent with the journal.
- Naples, winter 1818-19 **[A]**. Elena Adelaide Shelley registered 27 February 1819 as daughter of Percy and Mary **[A, parentage tagged R]**; dies at Naples 9 June 1820 **[A]**. **That the registration records a birth on 27 December 1818 is [R]**, outside the pool. Who the mother was is left open.
- William dies of malaria at Rome, June 1819 **[A]**, on the 7th per the geography lens; buried in the Cimitero Acattolico by the pyramid of Cestius **[A: burial records]**. **That the exact grave was later lost, the plot proving to hold another body, is [R]**, standard biography.
- Percy Florence born at Florence, 12 November 1819 **[A]**.
- **Contradiction 8, corrected:** the chronology dated the move to Casa Magni "1822-summer" while the geography gave April 1822. The household was installed by **30 April 1822**, after the news of Allegra's death **[A: Wikipedia, 'Percy Bysshe Shelley']**.
- Miscarriage and ice bath, 16 June 1822 **[A]**. Shelley's visions in the following fortnight are **[R]**, from later accounts by Mary and Jane Williams.
- Shelley sails for Livorno 1 July and is lost on the return, 8 July, with Edward Williams and the boat boy **[A]**. The wreck position is not precisely known; the journey pins it offshore of Viareggio at 43.92/10.10 and says so.
- Body ashore near Viareggio, cremated on the beach 16 August by Trelawny, Byron and Leigh Hunt present **[A: Trelawny, *Recollections*; Seymour]**. **The Keats volume in the pocket, Byron swimming out, Hunt in the carriage: [R]**, all resting on Trelawny, whose memoirs are freely embellished and are the only source for most of the scene.

## 7. Genoa and the return, 1822-1826

- **Contradiction 9, corrected:** "about a year" with the Hunts at Genoa is long. She shared Casa Negroto at Albaro from late September 1822 until late July 1823: about **ten months** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Byron was at Casa Saluzzo nearby.
- **Contradiction 10, corrected:** the pool dated her return to England 23 July 1823. That is the **departure from Genoa**; she reached London in **late August**, lodging with Godwin in the Strand, and saw *Presumption* on 29 August, days later **[A: Wikipedia]**. The journey dates the arrival 25 August with the day flagged.
- *Presumption; or, the Fate of Frankenstein*, English Opera House, 29 August 1823 **[A]**. **T. P. Cooke in the part and the playbill's blank dash: [R]**, universally reported, not in the pool.
- *Posthumous Poems* (1824), edited by her, suppressed by Sir Timothy under threat to her allowance **[A: publication history; Seymour]**.
- **Rejected pool claim.** The interlock lens asserted, tagged [A], that *To a Skylark* was "the poem Mary Shelley herself first brought into print in her 1824 edition of his Posthumous Poems." **This is wrong**: the poem appeared in Shelley's lifetime, in the *Prometheus Unbound* volume of 1820. The Buenos Aires interlock built on it (Raúl Veroni's 1955 bilingual edition, tr. Wilcock) is **dropped**.
- **Contradiction 11, corrected:** the pool put her in Kentish Town in August 1823. She lodged in the Strand and moved to Kentish Town, near Jane Williams, in the summer of **1824** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- *The Last Man* (1826) **[A]**. **"The reviews are savage" is [R]**: the pool records only a cool reception and later scholarly recovery. The much-quoted "offspring of a diseased imagination" notice was not verified and is not used.

## 8. The London decades and the end, 1826-1852

- Godwin dies 1836, aged eighty **[A]**; the day, 7 April, is **[R]** here, the pool giving the year alone. Burial beside Wollstonecraft at St Pancras is implied **[A]** by the documented 1851 exhumation of both from that churchyard.
- Moxon commissions the *Poetical Works* for £500; published 1839 with her notes **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Sir Timothy's veto on a biography, the notes as a life in disguise, and Trelawny's charge of cowardice: [R]**, standard biography.
- *Rambles in Germany and Italy in 1840, 1842, and 1843* (1844), her last published book **[A: the book, Gutenberg 74280]**.
- **Contradiction 12, corrected:** Bad Kissingen was dated "1840-1842". It belongs to the **second** journey only, a month's cure in **1842** on the tour of June 1842 to September 1843; the 1840 trip ran Paris, Metz, the Rhine, Como, Milan, Geneva, and took in Baden-Baden **[A: Wikipedia, 'Rambles']**. Kissingen was dropped for length; Sorrento 1843 carries the late travel.
- Dies 1 February 1851 at 24 Chester Square, aged 53, suspected brain tumour **[A: death record; Wikipedia]**. **The paralysis from 1848, the blackmailers with Shelley letters, the 1844 baronetcy: [R]**, standard biography.
- Buried at St Peter's, Bournemouth, her parents' remains reinterred there from St Pancras **[A: Jane, Lady Shelley's account; Wikipedia]**.
- **Contradiction 13, standing.** The desk opened in February 1852 yielded hair, a shared notebook, and a copy of *Adonais* folded round a silk parcel of ashes and a calcified organ. The afterlife lens tags this **[A]**; the geography lens concedes the relic is **[R]**, and the correction agrees. It rests on Jane, Lady Shelley's account, and some biographers argue the organ was the liver, which resists burning. The campa states the find, names her as the source, and carries the objection.

## 10. Interlocks used and refused

**Named in campas**, each on a documented relation:

- `jean_jacques_rousseau`, Geneva. She read him on his own lake and published the 1816 letters describing the party's deliberate retracing of *La Nouvelle Héloïse* by boat.
- `ada_lovelace`, Geneva. Byron arrives weeks after the deed of separation from Annabella Milbanke, leaving the infant Ada behind. Mary lived four months in his company; Claire's daughter by him is Ada's half-sister.
- `dante_gabriel_rossetti` and `christina_rossetti`, Villa Diodati. Their uncle John William Polidori is the fourth in the room, and the diary he kept of that summer descended in the Rossetti household, printed in 1911 by their brother William Michael Rossetti.
- `goethe_full`, Marlow. The creature learns to be a person from *The Sorrows of Young Werther*: a citation inside her own text.

**Refused:** `raul_veroni_padre`, the connecting claim being false (section 7). `adam_smith`, `giordano_bruno`, `borges`, `hillary_clinton`: Geneva only, centuries apart. `napoleon`: a wrecked landscape she walked through, not a relation between persons. `plinio_el_viejo`, whose file says the Villa Pliniana hosted "Shelley" without saying which; the Milan campa keeps the failed Como lease and drops the name. Byron and Polidori are named freely as participants; neither has a journey file in the material supplied.

## 11. Honest gaps

1. **No archival work.** Printed and online sources only. The Bodleian *Frankenstein* notebooks and the Abinger papers were not consulted.
2. **The two standard scholarly editions were not reachable**: Bennett's *Letters* and Feldman and Scott-Kilvert's *Journals*. Every "journal" and "letters" citation here is at second hand, as is every citation of Seymour, which the pool quotes but never opens.
3. **Olson's 2011 dating** of the waking dream was not read, only reported.
4. **Elena Adelaide Shelley**: parentage unresolved and left unresolved. Nothing in reach settles whether the child was Elise Duvillard's, Claire's, or a foundling, or what Mary knew.
5. **Days unknown, defaulted to the first of the month**: the 1807 Skinner Street move, Godwin's funeral, the Moxon commission, the *Posthumous Poems* publication and suppression, the Bournemouth interment.
6. **Claire Clairmont's journals** were used for Calais only. She is the surviving witness who most contradicts the Shelley family's later account, and her testimony is otherwise absent here.
