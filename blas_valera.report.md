# Blas Valera (c. 1544/1545 - 1597, and, if the Naples papers are believed, 1618)
*Research report, 2026-08-12. Mestizo Jesuit, chronicler of the Incas in Latin, imprisoned by his own order, exiled, burned out of his manuscripts at Cadiz, and posthumously claimed as the secret author of another man's book.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried only by a contested document. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; nothing is adjudicated here.

Two source bases here cannot be merged. One is documentary: Jesuit province catalogs and correspondence, read most closely by Sabine Hyland, plus Valera's own words quoted by Inca Garcilaso de la Vega (1609). The other is the Miccinelli or Naples papers, surfacing in a private Neapolitan archive in the 1980s and 1990s, asserting the 1597 death was staged. Sections touching the second are marked. They give two men and two deaths.

## 1. Birth and the first mestizo generation (c. 1544/1545 - c. 1560s)

- Born at **Levanto, Chachapoyas region, northern Peru**, son of the Spanish captain **Luis Valera**, a conquest veteran, and an indigenous Andean mother. **[A: English Wikipedia, "Blas Valera"; Spanish Wikipedia, "Blas Valera"]**
- **Birth year contradiction, not resolved.** English WP gives 1544 or 1545; Spanish WP gives 1545. Both agree on Chachapoyas.
- **Coordinate correction.** The pool twice pinned the birth at -6.2298, -77.8683, which is **Chachapoyas city, not Levanto**. Levanto district is at roughly **-6.3083, -77.8992**, 10 km south. The journey file uses Levanto for the birth and keeps the city pin only for the mother's lineage. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Distrito de Levanto"; "Chachapoyas (Peru)"]**
- **The mother is a live contradiction, left as one.** English WP names her Francisca Perez, "an Andean woman and possibly a member of the Inca royal family"; Spanish WP, "posiblemente una noble de la corte de Atahualpa." Another strand of the pool made her a **Chachapoyas curaca's daughter**, a largely opposed tradition: the Chachapoyas were subject to Cusco and often hostile to it. **Presented side by side, not merged. [R for both]**
- Grows up speaking **Quechua**; schooled in Latin and Spanish at **Trujillo**, then Arts at **Lima**. **[A for the bilingual formation and the Lima studies; R for Trujillo, which Hyland infers from training patterns rather than from a document naming the town]**

## 2. The Society of Jesus in Peru (1568 - 1577)

- Enters the **Society of Jesus at Lima, 1568**, at the Colegio de San Pablo, among the first mestizos accepted for priesthood training in Peru while the province was still arguing whether to admit men of mixed parentage. **[A: English WP citing Hyland 2003; province catalogs]**
- Assigned early to the **Huarochiri** region for his Quechua. **[A for the assignment: English WP. R for the pin, placed at the modern town; no source gives a station coordinate.]**
- **Ordained priest 1573 at Cusco by Bishop Sebastian de Lartaun**, after roughly five years as a novice. **[A: Spanish WP names the bishop; English WP confirms the year]**
- **Internal contradiction in the pool, corrected.** A geography lens put the ordination at Lima. Spanish WP is explicit: "Ordenacion: 1573 en Cusco, por el obispo Sebastian de Lartaun." The journey file pins **Cusco**.
- **1576**: Latin teacher and preacher at Cuzco; joins the **Name of Jesus confraternity alongside Inca nobles**. **[A: English WP]**
- **1577**: the order tries to move him to Potosi and **the Inca nobles of Cuzco protest**. **[R: English WP]**
- **1577**: he is at **Juli**, the Aymara mission on Lake Titicaca. **[A: Spanish WP states this flatly; one lens of the pool over-hedged it as disputed, which the source does not.]** The "Rome of the Andes" nickname carried in the pool is **not corroborated** by es.wikipedia, "Distrito de Juli," and is dropped.
- **Unreconciled year.** English WP has him pushed toward Potosi in 1577; Spanish WP has him arriving at Juli in 1577. Both kept, as consecutive stops.

## 3. Potosi, Arequipa, and the years of gathering (1578 - 1582)

- **1578**: the contested transfer to Potosi takes place; he reportedly founds a confraternity. **[A: English WP]**
- **1582**: recorded at Potosi, still researching Andean custom and religion. **[A: Spanish WP]**
- **Correction.** A geography lens claimed "no primary Jesuit letter fixes a date for this stop" and tagged Potosi **[R]**. Both Wikipedias date him there (English c. 1578-1579, Spanish 1582). **Retagged [A]**, both datings kept.
- **Arequipa** appears only in later tradition. **[R: no primary source names it]**
- By **1579** he has written a history of the spread of Christianity among Andean peoples. **[A: English WP]** No location given, so it gets no stop.
- Across the Cusco years he assembles the lost five-volume Latin **Historia Occidentalis**. **[A for its existence and loss: Garcilaso, Book I ch. VI, "Father Bias Valera, who wrote a history of that empire in most elegant Latin"]**
- **Citation correction.** The pool cited "Anello Oliva, *Historia del reino y provincias del Peru* (1631)" as published. It was **composed** c. 1631 and not published then: manuscript for over two centuries, partially edited by Ternaux-Compans in 1857, first part at Lima in 1895. Cited as "comp. 1631; first publ. 1857/1895." **[A: es.wikipedia, "Giovanni Anello Oliva"]**

## 4. The Third Council of Lima and the ban (1582 - 1583)

- The **Third Provincial Council of Lima sat 15 August 1582 to 18 October 1583** under Archbishop Toribio de Mogrovejo, producing three trilingual manuals in Spanish, Quechua and Aymara: catechism, *confesionario*, *sermonario*. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Tercer Concilio Limense"]**
- **Valera takes part**, working on the Quechua and Aymara. **[A: Spanish WP; council records]** The pool dated him to 1583 in one lens and 1582-1583 in another; the sitting dates settle it at **1582-1583**.
- In the same years he argues at Lima that **Inca religion is compatible with Christianity**, contributing to the Jesuits' **1582 ban on mestizo priests**. **[R: English WP]**

## 5. The order against its own (1583 - 1592)

- **1583: imprisoned by his own order.** Roughly **four years' imprisonment plus six further years stripped of priestly duties**. **[A: English WP citing Hyland 2003, pp. 69-71, 176-177; Spanish WP]**
- **Cause: direct contradiction, not resolved.** The charge on paper is **fornication** (Spanish WP). **Hyland argues the real cause was his defense of Inca cosmology**, treated as potentially heretical (Hyland 2003 via English WP). Both stand.
- **Place of imprisonment: an honest gap.** The pool's chronology lens records the location as "not specified by either source." A geography lens nonetheless asserted a "Jesuit house of correction" at **Cusco, 1583-1588** as **[A]**, and an afterlife lens wrote "Jesuit cells in Cusco and Lima." **Neither Wikipedia names a city.** All city attributions are therefore **[R]**, and the journey file says so in `date_confidence` rather than asserting a prison in prose.
- A rival tradition places the scandal at **Chuquisaca (Sucre)**; later writers may have conflated separate incidents. **[R: Hyland 2003]**
- In prison: prayer, menial labor, weekly "mortifications" probably including flagellation; **permanently stripped of priestly duties and of language teaching**. **[A: Hyland 2003, pp. 69-71, 176-177, via English WP]**
- **11 April 1583: Father Andres Lopez departs for Spain** to argue for his dismissal. **[R: Hyland via English WP; departure point unspecified]**
- **c. 1588-1590: kept under restriction at Lima**; the manuscript stays in the order's custody. **[R: province catalogs; Hyland 2003]**
- Reports climb the chain of command to the **generalate at Rome**. **[R: standard practice; the Roman correspondence on his case is not published in full]** The pool labeled this place "Loyola / Society of Jesus generalate correspondence, Rome," conflating the Basque birthplace of Ignatius with the Roman curia, 1,500 km apart; the coordinates given were Rome's. **"Loyola" dropped.**
- **1592: General Claudio Acquaviva orders his exile**; he departs Peru with his manuscripts. **[A: Spanish WP]**

## 6. The crossing (1592 - 1596)

- **Departure year contradiction, not resolved.** Spanish WP: late 1592. English WP: still in Peru or newly at Quito as late as 1594.
- **Callao** as embarkation point: **[R]**. The pool tagged it **[A]** on "Jesuit province catalogs," but no source places Valera there; it is inferred from the fleet route exactly as the Panama and Caribbean legs are.
- **Panama isthmus, Cartagena, Havana**: standard Tierra Firme fleet itinerary. **[R for all; no personal account of any leg survives.]** The journey file keeps only Panama, rather than padding a route nobody documented.
- **Quito, 1594-1595**: roughly two years recovering from illness; writes the account of the ancient customs of the natives of Peru while petitioning Acquaviva. **[A: English WP]**
- **Arrival in Spain: contradiction, not resolved.** Spanish WP: **Seville, 1595**, then Cadiz as professor of humanities. English WP: **May 1596**, and **imprisoned on arrival**. Both kept as separate stops, in source order.
- Disembarkation by the **Sanlucar / Guadalquivir route** is the customary pattern, not documented for him. **[R]**
- **3 June 1596: Father Cristobal Mendez reports to Acquaviva that Valera has reformed**; he may teach humanities at Cadiz, but not languages and not confession. **[R: Hyland via English WP]**

## 7. Cadiz, the fire, and the death (1596 - 1597)

- **30 June 1596: Cadiz sacked and burned by an Anglo-Dutch fleet. Valera is injured and loses most of his writings, including the five-volume Historia Occidentalis.** **[A: Spanish WP for the day; English WP confirms sack and loss without it]**
- **Command correction, load-bearing.** The pool wrote that the raid was "led by the Earl of Essex and Francis Drake's fleet." **Drake had no part in it: he died of dysentery on 28 January 1596 off Portobelo, five months earlier.** The force of 30 June to 15 July was commanded by **Charles Howard, 1st Earl of Nottingham** (fleet) and **Robert Devereux, 2nd Earl of Essex** (land), Raleigh and Vere subordinate. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Francis Drake"; "Capture of Cadiz"]** The journey file names Howard and Essex, not Drake.
- Garcilaso is the nearest thing to a contemporary witness on the loss: "It was the misfortune of my native country...that his papers were lost in the ruin and pillage of Cadiz by the English in 1596. He himself died soon afterwards." **[A: Book I ch. VI, trans. Markham]**
- A **Jesuit college of Malaga** stop after the raid (via Jerez de la Frontera) and a period at the **Colegio de la Compania at Seville** are both inconsistently reported. **[R for both]**
- **Death: 2 April 1597, aged about 52.** **[A: both Wikipedias]**
- **Place of death: direct contradiction, not resolved.** **Spanish WP names Malaga. English WP names no city**, only that he was injured in the sacking and died 2 April 1597. The pool attributed a "Cadiz, died of sack injuries" reading to English WP; that is the researcher's inference, not the source's statement, and is **withdrawn**. **No marked grave and no burial record located in either city.**
- The interlock lens carried a stray "died officially at Valladolid/Cadiz 1597-98." **No source supports Valladolid**; recorded as an unsupported variant.

## 8. The fragments, and Garcilaso (1600 - 2011)

- **1600: Father Pedro Maldonado de Saavedra delivers the surviving fragments to Inca Garcilaso de la Vega at Cordoba.** Not an inference; Garcilaso's own dated first-person statement: "I received the fragments of papers which were rescued from the pillage... More than half were lost. I was presented with these papers by the Father Maldonado de Saavedra... who in the present year 1600 reads the Scripture in the city of Cordova." **[A: Book I ch. VI, trans. Markham]** The pool tagged this **[R]** with a "could not be retrieved" hedge; **the hedge is removed**.
- **1609: the Comentarios Reales printed at Lisbon**, citing "el Padre Blas Valera" and reproducing long passages Garcilaso says he translated literally from Valera's Latin "because of the great value of his authority." **[A]**
- The most consequential surviving Valera passage, for section 9: "They wrote them down distinctly by means of knots and threads of different colours which they kept for these purposes... Thus the laws that were established by their first kings, six hundred years ago, are now as well preserved in the memories of the people as if they had just been promulgated afresh." **[A: Book V ch. XI, trans. Markham]** Note what it does and does not say: the laws were **written down** by knots and threads. It does **not** claim, as the Naples papers do on Valera's behalf, that quipus encode syllables phonetically.
- **1945: Francisco A. Loayza publishes at Lima** the Quito text, *Relacion de las costumbres antiguas de los naturales del Piru*, with other fragments: the only Valera a reader can hold complete. **[R: the attribution of the Relacion to Valera is itself noted as disputed by the Spanish-language source]**
- **2003 and 2011: Sabine Hyland** publishes *The Jesuit and the Incas*, rebuilding the documented life from Jesuit archives rather than the Naples papers, then *Gods of the Andes*, translating the surviving Relacion. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Sabine Hyland"]**

## 9. The second death: the Naples papers (1980s - present)

Everything here rests on documents whose narrative content is rejected by most specialists. Tagged accordingly and kept separate.

- **In the 1980s and 1990s Clara Miccinelli announces a cache of seventeenth-century papers in a private Neapolitan family archive**: *Exsul Immeritus Blas Valera Populo Suo* and *Historia et Rudimenta Linguae Piruanorum*, published from the mid-1990s by Laura Laurencich-Minelli (University of Bologna, not Naples). **[A that the announcement happened]** The pool stamped the year as 1990 while conceding in its own note that the source gives only "the 1980s-90s"; the journey file uses a **range** and asserts no single year.
- The papers claim the **1597 death was staged** with the help of the Jesuit Giovanni Anello Oliva, and that Valera returned secretly to Peru. **[R]**
- **Second-death year, corrected.** One lens of the pool gave 1619, another 1618; en.wikipedia, "Quipu," reports the *Historia et Rudimenta* was allegedly written "sometime before 1618." The journey file uses **1618**.
- They further claim Valera **ghost-wrote Guaman Poma's *Nueva coronica y buen gobierno***; that **quipus were a true writing system**, Pacha Kamaq's name recurring in knots as phonetic script; and that **Pizarro's men poisoned the Incas**. **[R for all three]** The dedicated English WP article on Guaman Poma, checked directly, contains no discussion of the ghost-authorship theory.
- **Reception, stated precisely, because the pool got it backwards.** Adorno, Estenssoro and Alberdi Vallejo find the documents apocryphal. **Hyland's position is narrower and the opposite of what a geography lens attributed to her**: she "believes the documents are probably authentic and date from the 17th century, but that the claims in the documents are fictional and incorrect," representing some contemporary Jesuits' anti-colonial views rather than fact. **[A: English WP citing Hyland 2003, pp. 227-228]**
- **Urton and Mannheim** question both the authenticity and the quipu-as-writing claim, noting its resemblance to **Raimondo di Sangro's 1751 *Lettera Apologetica***. **[R]** The pool called that "an unrelated 1751 work." It is **not unrelated**: it was written precisely to argue the communicative capacity of the Peruvian knotted cords, in defense of Graffigny's *Lettres d'une peruvienne*. The shared subject is the entire force of the borrowing argument, which collapses if the 1751 text is called unrelated. **[A: it.wikipedia, "Raimondo di Sangro"]**

## 10. Absence

**No monument to Blas Valera has been located** in Chachapoyas or Levanto, no street, plaza or institution in Amazonas bearing his name, and no marked grave anywhere in Spain. **[R: absence of evidence only]** Spanish WP's Chachapoyas article does not mention him. This is not a claim that no monument exists; municipal records were not reachable. **Stated as a gap.**

## Interlocks with the existing atlas

Named in `campa` because the paths genuinely cross:

- **guaman_poma_de_ayala** - same viceroyalty and generation, both working from quipucamayocs; his manuscript surfaces in Copenhagen with no shipping record, an evidentiary gap of the same shape as the Naples papers, which claim Valera wrote his book.
- **cecilia_vicuna** - her journey says the Inca kept their reckonings in knotted cord and the Spanish burned them; Guaman Poma's file already names her.
- **pizarro** - the conquest generation Valera's father served in, and the man the Naples papers accuse of poisoning the Incas.
- **matteo_ricci** - novitiate 1571, three years after Valera; the same order sending men into a foreign sign system to read it as legible.
- **kircher** - interpreted non-European sign systems from field reports sent home to Rome, the exact move later made about Valera's cords.
- **giordano_bruno**, **tommaso_campanella**, **vico** - San Domenico Maggiore and the Jesuit schools nearby: the Naples in which the documents claiming Valera survived his death are set.

**Inca Garcilaso de la Vega** (QUEUE.md line 375, no journey file yet) is unavoidable here, since most of what survives of Valera survives inside his book. When his journey is written, the Cordoba 1600 and Lisbon 1609 stops should be shared.
