# Elizebeth Smith Friedman (1892-1980) research report

*Compiled 2026-08-30 for `elizebeth_smith_friedman.journey.json`. Queue entry: the cipher wing; wife and professional peer of William F. Friedman, who is queued but not yet researched.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried by one source only. Where reachable sources disagree, both are printed and the contradiction left open. Where nothing was reachable, that is said.

**File written from this material:** 9 segments, 44 stops, 9 quotes. Every pin is a place she was physically present, except four posthumous commemoration stops (Fort Meade 1999, Arlington 2017, Huntington 2021, Pascagoula 2025), each so marked in its `date_confidence`.

**Coordinate corrections** (each also stated in the stop's `date_confidence`): Wooster to **40.81167, -81.935494**; Hillsdale to **41.93306, -84.63361**; Newberry to **41.9000, -87.6306**; Riverbank, four stops, to the Fabyan Villa value **41.87111, -88.31194**; Munitions Building to **38.8925, -77.0430**; Plainfield to **40.6337, -74.4074**, the pool's two lenses having disagreed. Two offered pins were **not used**: the Gulf of Mexico, because she was not at the I'm Alone sinking, and Buenos Aires, because no source places her in South America.

## 1. Huntington, Wooster, Hillsdale, Wabash (1892-1916)

- Born **26 August 1892** near Huntington, Indiana, youngest of nine surviving children of **John Marion Smith**, Quaker dairy farmer and banker, and **Sophia Strock Smith**. **[A: Wikipedia; huntingtoncountyhonors.org]** *Correction:* the pool gave the mother as "Sopha Strock Smith". *Gap:* the farm site is not located; town-center pin.
- **1911-1913** at Wooster, Ohio; left when her mother fell ill. **[A]** *Flagged:* the school was then the **University of Wooster**, the College of Wooster name dating from 1915; Wikipedia uses the later name anachronistically and the campa uses the contemporary one.
- **1915**, graduated **Hillsdale College**, English literature. **[A]**
- **Fall 1915 to spring 1916**, substitute principal at a public high school in **Wabash, Indiana**. **[A]** *Gap:* building not identified; town-center pin.

## 2. The Newberry, the train, Riverbank (1916-1921)

- **Summer 1916**, visited the **Newberry Library**, Chicago, seeking a First Folio; a librarian connected her to **George Fabyan**, who recruited her that day. **[A as to substance: Wikipedia; Marshall Foundation]** *Caveat:* the visit survives only in interviews given fifty years later.
- **The Fabyan exchange.** The pool carried "Well! What do you know?" / "That remains, sir, for you to find out," sourced to the **1976 NSA oral history**. Wording and source are both wrong. The primary text is the **Marshall Foundation, "Tape #5," transcript, 6 June 1974, p. 2**: Fabyan's line is **"Well, what in hell do you know?"**, the scene is **on the train out to Geneva**, not at the library, and her reply is verbatim correct. **[A]** The file gives this its own stop, pinned at Chicago, dated 1916-06-02 only to order the segment.
- **1916**, put under **Elizabeth Wells Gallup** at **Riverbank Laboratories**, Geneva, Illinois, testing the claim that Francis Bacon hid a biliteral cipher in early printed Shakespeare typefaces **[A]**; met **William F. Friedman**, a plant geneticist who moved onto codes and ciphers after courting her **[A: Wikipedia, 'William F. Friedman']**. The photographic-enlargement detail rests on no document reached: narrated, not claimed as sourced.
- **Married May 1917, Chicago. [R as to the day.]** Wikipedia's biography gives May 1917; the caption in the article on William dates it only to 1917, and the repeated **21 May** is unconfirmed. No venue named; pin is Chicago city center.
- **April 1917**, Riverbank became an unofficial federal cipher bureau on United States entry into the war, solving forwarded intercepts and training Army and Navy officers. **[A]** **1918**, **Riverbank Publication No. 21, "Methods for the Reconstruction of Primary Alphabets,"** credited to **William and Elizebeth jointly** **[A]**; the pool hedged the authorship split, but the joint credit is on the pamphlet, so the hedge is dropped.
- **1921**, left Riverbank for the **War Department**, Washington. **[A]** *Not asserted:* William's 1919 demobilization, carried in the pool, was not confirmed.

## 3. Washington and the rum fleet (1921-1930)

- **1921**, War Department **[A]**; her office is undocumented and the campa says so. **1922**, briefly quit the government **[A]**; children Barbara **1923**, John Ramsay **1926** **[A]**.
- **Start of the Coast Guard work: contested, not resolved.** Wikipedia says the Coast Guard hired her **temporarily** after she quit in 1922, to clear a backlog; PBS dates her Coast Guard codebreaking to **1925**; the pool anchored it to **1927**, which is wrong as a personal date, 1927 being institutional (Treasury's joint Prohibition and Customs effort, folded into the Coast Guard 1927-1939). *In the file:* the backlog stop is dated **1925-01-01** with the disagreement in `date_confidence`, and the **1927** unit is a separate stop, labelled institutional.
- **June 1928**, went west to train **C. A. Housel**, later credited with roughly **3,300** messages in twenty-one months. **[A]** *Gap:* no source names the city or station; San Francisco is an approximate hub, low confidence.
- **22 March 1929**, the schooner **I'm Alone** sunk in the Gulf of Mexico, one crewman killed; her decryption established American ownership and supported the U.S. case in arbitration. **[A: Smithsonian; her "History of Work in Cryptanalysis, 1927-1930"]** The count of roughly twenty-three messages is **[R]**. The stop sits in Washington: she was not at sea.
- **October and November 1929**, Houston. **[A]** *Correction, twice over:* the pool had "October 1929" and "650 subpoenaed smuggling messages." Wikipedia has October **and November**, and **650 subpoenaed cases**, in the course of which she decrypted 24 coding systems. *Gap:* 201 Fannin St. is an approximation. **Galveston**, a further testimony site c. 1929-1931 **[R]** with neither date nor courthouse confirmed, is **dropped** and recorded here so it is not lost.
- **1928-1930**, she and one assistant credited with roughly **12,000** solved messages **[R:** institutional histories via the dossier; no primary count reached**]**. **1930**, memorandum proposing a permanent seven-person unit; approved the following year with her as civilian head, **Cryptologist-in-Charge**. **[A]**

## 4. The witness stand (1933-1939)

- **1933, New Orleans**, trial of twenty-three agents of **Consolidated Exporters Corporation**; she testified as expert witness. **[A: Wikipedia; Smithsonian names Col. Amos W. Woodcock as prosecutor]** *Attribution dropped:* the pool called the case **"United States v. Morrison"**; nothing fetched supports that name, and it is not asserted. *Quotation demoted:* the pool carried "without the work of the cryptanalysis unit and the expert testimony of Mrs. Friedman, the case would not have been won" as a **Woodcock quotation**, but on the Marshall Foundation page those words appear **without quotation marks**, with no underlying document named. The file renders it as reported speech, not a `quote`. Her own memoir line on the Bert Morrison indictments **is** a quotation and is used as one. **[A]**
- **1937, Vancouver**, opium network using enciphered Chinese-language code groups; she testified for the Crown at the trial of **Gordon Lim** and others. **[A]** *Tag corrected upward:* the pool had **[R]**, but Wikipedia states it directly. *Still [R] and hedged:* the January 1938 conviction date, the RCMP as requesting body (Wikipedia says "the Canadian government"), the arms-trafficking element, and 750 Hornby St. as venue.
- **Honorary Doctor of Laws, Hillsdale. Contested, not resolved.** Wikipedia gives **1938**, Hillsdale's own current lists **1939**, and nothing reached adjudicates. Dated 1938 with the disagreement in `date_confidence`.
- **The turn toward German networks: re-anchored.** The pool said **1940**, supported by nothing fetched. PBS dates the move to her **transfer to the Navy in December 1941**, the South American work running **1942-1944**; Wikipedia ends the Treasury arrangement at **1939**. *In the file:* a transition stop dated **1939-01-01**, labelled a reconstruction asserting no documented event, then a hard-dated December 1941 stop.

## 5. The German circuits (1941-1946)

- **January 1941**, William hospitalized in the neuropsychiatric section at **Walter Reed** for roughly two and a half months, the breakdown attributed to the strain of **PURPLE**. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **December 1941**, transferred to the Navy. She headed **Unit 387**, later **OP-20-GU**, working clandestine Latin American radio including several intelligence-service Enigma systems, in liaison with **Intelligence Service Knox** at Bletchley Park; the unit decoded **4,000 messages on 48 circuits**. **[A: Wikipedia; PBS]** The ISK liaison is **[R]**, dossier-carried.
- **March 1942, Brazil**, an FBI-directed roundup used verbatim decrypts prematurely and too freely, compromising sources and prompting the Germans to change systems. **[A per the unit's own 1943 "History of OP-20-GU" as reported in the dossier, that history not itself fetched]**
- **March 1942**, her unit read German agent traffic reporting the movement of **RMS Queen Mary** near the Brazilian coast; the voyage completed safely. **[A]** **The "8,000 lives saved" tradition is [R], flagged inside the campa itself:** the record establishes detection and a safe passage, not a proven counterfactual rescue.
- **Operation Bolivar** and **Johannes Siegfried Becker**, code name **SARGO**, out of Argentina. **[A that Becker = SARGO: Wikipedia]** **No source places Elizebeth in South America.** See §7.
- **1944**, **Velvalee Dickinson**, the "Doll Woman," using doll transactions as an open code for naval shipping; Friedman's analysis contributed to the guilty plea and ten-year sentence. **[A]**
- **The credit dispute.** **"How the Nazi Spy Invasion Was Smashed"** ran in **The American Magazine**, alongside the film **The Battle of the United States**; neither mentioned her or the Coast Guard. **[A as to title and venue: Wikipedia, citing Fagone 2017 and Haynes, *Time*, 2021]** *Not confirmed, not asserted:* the **September 1944** date and **Hoover as byline author**, Wikipedia placing the campaign at the war's **end**.
- **Retirement 1946, not 1945.** Smithsonian states flatly that she retired in 1946; Wikipedia gives no year; the pool had 1945. Corrected, which puts retirement and the IMF consultancy in the same year.

## 6. After the vault, and after her (1946-2025)

- **1946 onward**, **Consultant in Communications** to the **International Monetary Fund** (pool span 1946-1949). **[A]** *Gap:* the Fund's offices of those years are not confirmed to have stood at the present 19th St. NW site. **1955**, Folger prize with William for **"The Cryptologist Looks at Shakespeare."** **[R]**
- **1957**, **The Shakespearean Ciphers Examined**, Cambridge University Press, 303 pages. **[A: Wikipedia; Ashby's 1958 review]** The concealed message **"I did not write the plays. F. Bacon."** is **[A]** per Wikipedia, but the pool's afterlife lens tagged it **[R]**, having failed to confirm the wording: **contradiction flagged, not resolved.** *Placement:* published in Cambridge, England, where **she is not documented to have gone**; the stop is pinned at the Washington home where it was written.
- **26 June 1959**, NSA personnel visited the home and removed or reclassified material previously held as unclassified, including First World War documents. **[R:** dossier lead, unconfirmed against any fetched source.**]** Retained with the hedge in `date_confidence`, since the consequence, the Marshall gift rather than a federal one, is attested.
- **5 August 1962**, Washington Post piece on the **Voynich manuscript**. **[A: cited in Wikipedia, 'Voynich manuscript']** *Correction:* the pool's title was wrong; the citation is **"The most mysterious ms. - still an enigma," Washington Post, 5 August 1962, pp. E1, E5**, and her line **"doomed to utter frustration"** is quoted from it.
- **2 November 1969**, William died. **[A]** **The headstone: agency corrected.** The pool had William inscribing his own tomb; per elonka.com/friedman, **Elizebeth designed the stone** and chose the Baconian epitaph, whose serif and sans-serif letterforms encode a biliteral cipher. **[A]** The authorship is hers; the earlier Riverbank cipher photograph is a separate artifact, not conflated with it.
- **1971**, supervised the transfer of the library, papers, desk, and a small safe to the **Marshall Research Library**, Lexington, following the truck by car; donated her own papers. **[A]** **June 1974**, five-tape interview series there with **Tony Crawford** and **Lynn Biribauer** **[A]**; the 1973, 1975, and 1976 interviews are attested but **their venues are not confirmed** **[R]**.
- **31 October 1980**, died of arteriosclerosis at **Abbott Manor Nursing Home**, Plainfield, New Jersey, aged 88; cremated, ashes placed at William's grave. **[A: Wikipedia; Post obituary, 2 November 1980, not re-fetched]**
- **1999**, **NSA/CSS Hall of Honor**, inaugural group, first woman honoree, with an auditorium renamed for both Friedmans; **2002**, OPS1 dedicated the **William and Elizebeth Friedman Building**; **2014**, ATF named its headquarters auditorium for her (no stop, cut against the 45-stop ceiling). **[A: Wikipedia]** *Reattributed:* the pool credited the NSA items to a Smithsonian article which, fetched in full, **mentions none of them**. **c. 2008**, declassification **[A]**; **17 April 2017**, **Elonka Dunin** solved the headstone cipher **[A]**, the same year bringing Fagone's *The Woman Who Smashed Codes* **[A: Time]**.
- **Two legends, both [R], both flagged.** That the gravestone secretly reads *FRANCIS BACON IS SHAKESPEARE* (Francis Bacon Society) inverts the only concealed message the Friedmans are documented to have planted; the file states it as a claim. That her codebreaking convicted Al Capone is a misattribution, his conviction being for tax evasion; not carried into the file.
- **USCGC Friedman (WMSL-760).** **[A: Wikipedia]** Named **21 December 2018**; fabrication begun **11 May 2021** at Huntington Ingalls, Pascagoula; **cancelled 5 June 2025**, never completed or commissioned. *Correction:* the pool gave the announcement as 7 July 2020, wrong by eighteen months.

## 7. Interlocks: used, and refused

**Named in campas**, because she herself stands in a real relation:

- **`francis_bacon`**, at the 1916 Riverbank stop and the 1957 book stop. She was employed for years to test Bacon's biliteral cipher under Gallup, and she and William later demolished the Baconian claims in print: the one-directional relation the standing rule licenses.
- **`herbert_yardley`**, at the December 1941 Navy stop. `herbert_yardley.journey.json` states that **William Friedman opposed Yardley's June 1941 appointment** to head Canada's Examination Unit, and Yardley's unit then worked the identical target, Abwehr traffic between South America and Germany, that hers was working the same year. **Flagged as the weakest of the three:** her own relation to Yardley is undocumented, and if the standard is "she herself," cut it.
- **`al_kindi`**, in the New Orleans courtroom campa where she explains letter-frequency analysis to a jury. Methodological descent, not contact; `al_kindi.journey.json` calls the same technique "the first known description of cryptanalysis by frequency analysis." The campa claims inheritance, not acquaintance.

**Refused:** **`alan_turing`**, because the lens's own note says "Turing himself is not documented as having any personal connection to Elizebeth" and shared institution is not a relation (the file names **Dilly Knox's section**, the unit hers liaised with; Knox has no journey file). **`ian_fleming`**, institutional proximity only. **`betty_shannon`**, offered as a negative counterpart to the Friedmans' two-cryptanalyst marriage, which is a comparison a reader may draw, not a relation she had. **The Buenos Aires pin**, since no source places her there: the SARGO stop sits at her Washington desk and names Buenos Aires as the origin of the traffic, and the pin belongs on a future Becker or Operation Bolivar entry. **`william_f_friedman`** is named throughout but has **no journey.json** (checked under both slugs); he is a QUEUE.md row, so the interlock cannot be cross-checked. Not counted.
