# Johann Faust: research report

*2026-08-04. Subject: the historical Johann (Georg) Faust, itinerant astrologer and self-styled necromancer of the German lands, documented c. 1506 to c. 1540; and the printed legend that swallowed him after 1587.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; this report adjudicates nothing it cannot document.

**Method.** Four lenses (chronology, geography, quotations, corpus interlock) were cross-checked against each other; surviving corrections appear below as flags, not silent edits. The documentary man and the legendary man barely overlap: a dozen archival entries across three decades on one side, a chapbook of 1587 on the other. This report keeps them separate and does not let the second explain the first.

---

## 1. The cradle nobody can fix

No baptismal record survives. No birth date, no birthplace, no parents are documented anywhere. **[A: gap noted across Baron, Ruickbie and the standard summaries]** Everything below is competing tradition.

**Three birthplace claims, none reconciled:**

- **Knittlingen**, duchy of Württemberg. Local tradition, adopted by later biographers and institutionalized by the Faust-Museum and Faust-Archiv. **[R]** Melanchthon, transmitted by Manlius, names "Kundling," which the tradition identifies with Knittlingen. **[A for the Manlius text; R for the identification]**
- **Helmstadt** near Heidelberg. Baron's identification, resting on the form "Georgius Faustus helmstet" in Kilian Leib's Rebdorf weather diary of 1528. **[A for the diary form; R for the town]**
- **Roda / Stadtroda** in Thuringia. Also attributed to Melanchthon via Manlius. **[R]**

German reference material adds **Simmern** and **Salzwedel**. The lens calling Knittlingen "one of two towns" understates the field: three serious claimants, five names in circulation. **Flagged, not resolved.**

**Correction inside the birthplace question.** One lens attributed the epithet "Helmstet" to Mutianus Rufus's letter of 1513. Wrong: the 1513 letter reads *Georgius Faustus, Helmitheus Hedelbergensis*, and the "helmstet" form belongs to Kilian Leib's diary of 1528. Only the second supports a Helmstadt reading. **Flagged.**

**Birth year.** Roughly 1466 or roughly 1480/81. **[R: Baron 1992, Ruickbie 2009]** The earlier date makes Faust a contemporary of the Heidelberg matriculant Georg Helmstetter, the later a generation younger. Nothing decides it.

**The Weisert document.** In 1934 Karl Weisert made a pencil transcription of a document he dated 1542, purportedly confirming Knittlingen. The original was destroyed in the war; the transcription is now the only witness and can be collated against nothing. **[R]** The clearest gap in the dossier: the strongest-looking claim rests on a copy made four centuries later, of a paper that no longer exists.

**Heidelberg and Kraków.** A Georgius Helmstetter matriculates at Heidelberg in 1483, baccalaureus 12 July 1484, magister artium 1 March 1487. **[A: university records]** Whether he is Faust is disputed. One interlock note asserts a Faust matriculation here in 1509; no reachable source supports that date and it is not carried forward. **Flagged as an internal inconsistency in the pool.** Manlius, relaying Melanchthon, has Faust learning magic at Kraków. **[R]** No matriculation record survives; the claim feeds the Polish Pan Twardowski legend.

## 2. The first name in the record, 1506 to 1513

**Gelnhausen, 1506.** A wandering performer calling himself Faust appears offering tricks and horoscopes: the first documentary trace of anyone using the name. **[A: municipal record cited by Baron]**

**Würzburg, 20 August 1507.** Johannes Trithemius writes to the astrologer Johannes Virdung of Hasfurt warning him against a vagrant styling himself *Georgius Sabellicus, Faustus junior, fons necromanticorum, astrologus, magus secundus, chiromanticus, agromanticus, pyromanticus, in hydra arte secundus.* **[A for the letter; R for the exact Latin, which reaches this report only through secondary literature]**

The letter reports three further things. The man held a schoolmaster's post at Kreuznach through the patronage of Franz von Sickingen, and fled after being accused of sodomy with boys in his charge. He boasted he could reproduce all the miracles of Christ at will. Trithemius calls him a fool, not a philosopher. **[A for all three]**

**Correction carried into the artifacts.** One lens described Trithemius as "abbot of Sponheim" at the time of writing. He was not: he resigned Sponheim in 1506 and was abbot of the Schottenkloster St. Jakob at Würzburg by August 1507. Date, recipient and self-styling are otherwise correct. **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia; en.wikipedia]** The journey file pins the stop at Würzburg.

**Erfurt, 1513.** Conrad Mutianus Rufus, writing to Heinrich Urbanus, describes overhearing a chiromancer at an inn calling himself *Georgius Faustus Helmitheus Hedelbergensis*, and dismisses him as a braggart and a fool. **[A for the letter; R for the German rendering quoted in the journey file, seen only via de.wikipedia and never collated against the original]**

**The two-men hypothesis.** Some source-critical scholarship holds that the records of 1507 to 1513, naming a *Georgius* Faustus of Heidelberg, describe a younger and different man from the *Johann* Faustus of the 1520s and 1530s: that "Faust" conflates two itinerant magicians. **[R: discussed by Baron]** This report neither adopts nor rejects it. It is why the journey file keeps the early and later segments distinct.

## 3. The doctor paid in gulden, 1516 to 1532

**Maulbronn, traditionally 1516.** A seventeenth-century household register, Zeiter's *Fürstlich Württembergisches Dienerbuch*, calls Abbot Johann Entenfuß "Dr. Fausten ... Collega." **[R]** No sixteenth-century document confirms the visit.

**Bamberg, 23 February 1520.** The episcopal court chamber pays ten gulden to a Doctor Faustus for casting a nativity for Prince-Bishop Georg III Schenk von Limpurg. **[A: Bamberg Hofkammerrechnung, cited by Baron 1978]** The clearest attestation that Faust was employed and paid at a princely court. The German text quoted in the journey file is **[R]**: de.wikipedia's footnoted excerpt, not the archival record.

**Rebdorf, 5 June 1528.** Prior Kilian Leib records that "Georgius faustus helmstet" claimed that when sun and Jupiter stand in the same degree of the same sign, prophets are born. The same entry has Faust claiming the rank of a Johanniter. **[A]**

**Ingolstadt, 17 June 1528.** The council orders one calling himself "Dr. Jörg Faustus von Heidelberg" to spend his pennies elsewhere: an expulsion, apparently for fortune-telling. **[A: Ingolstadt Ratsprotokoll]** **Correction:** English summaries give 27 June; the German minute reads **17 June**, the Wednesday after St. Vitus. The chronology lens carried the transposition, the geography lens the correct date. The artifacts use 17 June. **Flagged.**

**Nuremberg, 1532.** The junior mayor notes that safe conduct is refused to "the great sodomite and nigromancer Doctor Faustus." **[A: Staatsarchiv Nürnberg, Nürnberger Ratserlasse Nr. 810]**

**Correction to the German text.** The transcription in the pool was corrupt and wrongly credited to de.wikipedia. The archive reading is *Doctor Fausto, dem grossen Sodomitten und Nigromantico zu furr, glait ablainen. Burg[ermeister] Ju[ni]or.* Against the pool's version: dative *Fausto*, *zu furr* not *in furt*, and a closing subscription that is the only thing identifying the junior mayor. The English gloss is genuine but is a translation, not the record. **Flagged and corrected.**

**Erfurt again, c. 1520s.** Later tradition, from the Melanchthon circle through Manlius, has Faust lecturing on Homer, conjuring Homeric heroes, and being warned off by the Franciscan Konrad Klinge. **[R]** No contemporary Erfurt record supports it. Franconian tradition likewise has him practicing alchemy for knights around **Boxberg and Bad Mergentheim**. **[R]** No documentary base, and no stop in the journey file.

## 4. The last years on the record, 1534 to 1540

**The Welser horoscope.** Philipp von Hutten, writing from Coro in Venezuela on 15 January 1540, recalls that "the philosopher Faust" had nearly predicted the disastrous year the expedition endured. **[A]** **Correction:** one lens placed the casting at Bad Mergentheim in 1534, but no source states where or when it was cast. The Augsburg attribution in the journey file is likewise an inference from the Welser house's location and is tagged **[R]** there; the 1534 date is inferred from the expedition's departure. **Flagged.**

**Münster, 25 June 1535.** Faust is reported present during the suppression of the Anabaptist kingdom, among the last dated sightings. **[R: cited in modern scholarship; the underlying record was not reachable and no lens supplied coordinates]** The journey file pins the Prinzipalmarkt and says so.

**Tübingen, 1536.** Joachim Camerarius writes to Daniel Stibar about Faust's astrology. **[A]** **A direct contradiction inside the pool:** the chronology lens claimed Camerarius treated Faust as a genuine, respected astrologer; the geography lens claimed the opposite and is right. German summary calls the judgment *nichtigsten Aberglauben*, the most worthless superstition. Camerarius treats Faust as a known figure whose predictions his correspondent follows, not as an authority. "Tübingen/Wittenberg circle" is also wrong; the chair was Tübingen, 1535 to 1541. **Flagged; the artifacts follow the geography lens and say so.**

**Korbach, 1536.** Daniel Prasser's regional chronicle has him passing through. **[R]** Uncorroborated, and flagged as such by later scholarship.

**Worms, 1539.** City physician Philipp Begardi publishes in his *Index Sanitatis* a complaint that many patients were deceived by Faust, his promises great and his results deceptive. **[A]** The last securely dated notice of the man alive is a consumer complaint.

**Staufen im Breisgau, c. 1540 to 1541.** He dies. **[A for the death in the lordship of Staufen; R for everything else.]** **The largest correction in the dossier.** Two lenses attributed to the *Zimmerische Chronik* an alchemical explosion at the inn Zum Löwen and a mutilated corpse. The chronicle says neither: Zimmern records only that Faust died *ellengclichen*, miserably, in the lordship of Staufen around the time of the Reichstag, and that many believed the evil spirit took him. The explosion is later town tradition; the corpse turning its face to the earth belongs to Gast's *Sermones Convivales* of 1548. The journey file splits these into separate stops with separate tags. **Flagged and corrected.**

**Two gaps.** Year of death: 1540 or 1541, with no primary record to fix it. Burial: no tomb, marker or interment record has ever been found, and the Staufen inn inscription marks a reputed death site, not a grave.

## 5. The witnesses who wrote him down, 1548 to 1602

**Basel, 1548.** Johann Gast publishes *Sermones Convivales*, claiming to have dined with Faust and reporting a dreadful death, a vanishing dog and a corpse that would not lie face up. **[A for Gast's text; nothing corroborates it]**

**Wittenberg, 1563.** Johannes Manlius publishes the *Locorum Communium Collectanea*, recording Melanchthon's table talk: he knew a Faustus of Kundling, a small town not far from his homeland, who learned magic at Kraków. Elsewhere Melanchthon calls him a *turpissima bestia et cloaca multorum diabolorum*. **[A]**

**Two corrections.** The date: English summaries give 1562, German 1563 for the Basel edition, with a German edition in 1565; the artifacts use 1563. Worse: the German wording quoted in the journey file comes from the **1565 German edition**, while the 1563 *Collectanea* is in **Latin**, so citing it to the 1563 volume is a mismatch. The transcription had also been silently modernized, commas replacing early-modern virgules; the virgules are restored. **Flagged and corrected.**

**Nuremberg, 1602.** Philipp Camerarius, in *Operae Horarum Subcisivarum*, claims to have heard firsthand accounts from people who knew Faust. **[A for the claim]** The pool gives no site; the journey file pins the jurist's base, marked as reconstruction.

## 6. The book that replaced the man

**Frankfurt am Main, 1587.** Johann Spies prints the anonymous *Historia von D. Johann Fausten*. **[A]** It fixes the legendary architecture: the pact, the twenty-four-year term, the flight over Christendom and the Levant, the dismemberment at midnight. None of it corresponds to the record, and it is the version that survives. The Faustbook also asserts a Wittenberg doctorate in theology **[R]**, corroborated by no register. The doctorate that gives "Doctor Faustus" his name is, so far as the archives go, unearned.

**London, 1592.** *The Historie of the Damnable Life and Deserved Death of Doctor John Faustus*, translated by "P.F. Gent.," reaches Christopher Marlowe. **[A]** **Correction:** one lens said the English translation appeared "within a year of the German original." It did not; the earliest surviving English Faust Book is dated 1592, and the lens's own source line already said so. A lost earlier printing is posited by some scholars but none survives. **Flagged.** This chapbook is the only source in the dossier that puts extended words in Faust's mouth, and the journey file labels them fiction where it quotes them.

Widmann's expanded *Historien* (1599) and Pfitzer's revision (1674) each add moralizing episodes absent from 1587, at no fixed site. **[A]**

**Wittenberg, 1683.** Johann Georg Neumann publishes *Disquisitio historica de Fausto praestigiatore*, the first scholarly attempt to establish that a historical Faust existed at all, against contemporaries who by then doubted it. **[A]** Faust becomes an object of historical inquiry ninety-six years after he became a chapbook.

## 7. Monuments raised on tradition

Every site below rests on tradition rather than a contemporary document, and each is tagged accordingly in the journey file.

- **Knittlingen**, Faust-Museum and Faust-Archiv. **[A for the institution; R for the birthplace claim]** The circulated pin 49.0053, 8.7492 falls 1.7 km south of the Altes Rathaus; the artifacts use approximately 49.0197, 8.7561.
- **Bad Kreuznach**, a Faust-Haus reportedly built in 1492. **[R]** Local history doubts the date, and Trithemius's letter, the only evidence for Faust at Kreuznach, names no house. One lens tagged this **[A]**; it should be **[R]**. **Flagged and corrected.**
- **Maulbronn**, the Faustturm, named on the strength of the Zeiter register alone. **[R]** **Wittenberg**, a plaque at Collegienstraße 31, installation date not established. **[R]** **Leipzig**, Auerbachs Keller with its painted panels of the barrel ride. **[R: Faustbuch, 1587]**
- **Prague**, the Faust House on Karlovo náměstí. **[R: civic legend attached to the building's earlier alchemical tenants]** **Staufen im Breisgau**, the Gasthaus zum Löwen inscription, marking a reputed death site. **[R]**

Against all of this stands the modern critical recovery: Baron (1978/1992) and Ruickbie (2009), working the archives back out from under the chapbook. **[A]**

## 8. Coordinate corrections applied

Five pins were wrong by over a kilometre: **Gelnhausen** (49.199 N was off by a full degree of latitude, about 111 km, landing in the Odenwald) corrected to 50.2010, 9.1876; **Helmstadt** (17 km east) to 49.319, 9.000; **Kloster Rebdorf** (2.3 km past Eichstätt) to 48.8884, 11.1668; **Vienna Hofburg** (0.6 km, nearer Stephansplatz) to 48.2065, 16.3657; **Knittlingen** as above. Sub-kilometre shifts at Ebernburg and Maulbronn also applied.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical**, as the interlock lens found them: Würzburg 49.7913 / 9.9534, Nuremberg 49.4521 / 11.0767, Basel 47.5596 / 7.5886 (paracelsus); Wittenberg 51.866369 / 12.637947, Heidelberg 49.4102 / 8.7089 (martin_luther); Paris 48.8566 / 2.3522 (barbier, giordano_bruno); Temple Mount 31.7784 / 35.2354 (dante). The Kaaba and Buenos Aires pins correspond to no Faust site and are not used; inventing stops to consume them would have been fabrication.

## 9. Where the paths cross the atlas

Four travelers are named in the campa. In each, the corpus stop is **[A]** and the Faust overlap **[R]**.

- **paracelsus.** Trithemius, Faust's first accuser, teaches Paracelsus alchemy at Würzburg in 1508, a year after the letter; Paracelsus signs his adopted name at Nuremberg in 1529, the council that refuses Faust safe conduct in 1532, and flees Basel in 1528, where Gast later claims to have dined with Faust.
- **martin_luther.** Melanchthon, source of nearly every hostile Faust anecdote, is at Wittenberg across four stops of the Luther journey. Luther matriculates at Erfurt in 1501 and enters the cloister there in 1505, the town where Mutianus overhears Faust in 1513; he is at Heidelberg in 1518.
- **giordano_bruno.** Bruno teaches at Wittenberg 1586 to 1588 as the *Historia* goes to press, and is burned at Rome in 1600 on the square the Faustbuch had sent its magician flying over.
- **goethe_full.** At Frankfurt in 1769 Goethe reads the alchemists under Susanna von Klettenberg and builds a furnace to seek the stone; he drinks at Auerbachs Keller as a Leipzig student and visits Erfurt in 1808.

The queue plans to join Faust with Trithemius, Dee, Fludd, Trismosin and the Rosicrucian legend. **[A: QUEUE.md]** Agrippa, the census's Trithemius pupil, is the figure most often conflated with Faust in sixteenth-century legend. **[R]**

**Open questions left open.** Is Georg Helmstetter of Heidelberg the same man as Faust? Is "Faust" one man or two, the *Georgius* of 1506-1513 and the *Johann* of 1520-1540? Birthplace: Knittlingen, Helmstadt, Roda, Simmern, or Salzwedel? Birth year c. 1466 or c. 1480/81? Death year 1540 or 1541? Where and when was the Welser horoscope cast (Augsburg is inference only)? Where is he buried? Every one of these is undecided, and the last has no record at all.
