# Anton Fugger (1493-1560): research report

*2026-08-31, for `anton_fugger.journey.json`: 44 stops, 7 segments.*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or dossier lead not confirmed against a fetched source. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated** unless a fetched source settles them, in which case that source is named. Hedges live in each stop's `date_confidence`, not in campa prose.

Backbone: the **Neue Deutsche Biographie**, "Fugger, Anton" (GND 118694170), fetched and read. Several pool claims were mis-cited *to* it and are re-cited below: it contains none of the words "Fuggerei", "Waltenhausen" or "Glött".

## 1. The third son, 1493-1523

- Born **10 June 1493, Augsburg**, third and youngest son of Georg Fugger and Regina Imhof **[A: NDB, en.wiki]**; birth building undocumented, pinned at the Fuggerhäuser. Georg dies **1506** and the boy of thirteen passes to his uncle **Jakob Fugger the Rich** as guardian and employer; **1512**, partnership agreement among the nephews **[A: NDB]**.
- **Apprenticeship by rotation, c. 1512-1523**: NDB names Nuremberg, Breslau, Buda, Italy, Tyrol **[A]**. No building documented at any; city landmarks used and flagged. Years undocumented, set only to order the segment.
- **Rome, c. 1518-1523 [A: NDB]**: residence, papal court, curial finance, and the detail the segment turns on, that Anton **helped introduce the theologian Johann Eck at the papal court**. Eck's Roman business in 1520 was procuring *Exsurge Domine* against Luther; that date is general knowledge, and NDB does not date the introduction. **Gap**: no year for it, no letter or act in Anton's hand from the Roman years.

## 2. The uncle's choice, 1523-1527

- **1523**: Jakob sends Anton, his cousin Ulrich and the factor Georg Hörmann to Tyrol with legal power to bind the company **[R: NDB carries the sending; the dossier's added Rattenberg smelting is unconfirmed]**. *Correction*: the pool's Rattenberg coordinate is off by about 2.5 km, and de.wiki's Rattenberg article mentions **no** Fugger works. **Rattenberg gets no pin**; the posting is staged at Schwaz, confirmed as the centre of Fugger silver under sovereign lease (habsburger.net).
- **22 December 1525**: Jakob's revised testament gives Anton decisive managerial responsibility, Raymund and Hieronymus staying partners without command **[A for substance: NDB; R for the date]**.
- **30 December 1525**: Jakob dies, Anton takes over at thirty-two **[A: NDB]**. En.wiki gives inherited assets of **2,032,652 gulden**, which compresses company and estate accounting and is not one man's cash; the journey repeats it with that warning. **1526**: reconciliation restoring the Fugger-Thurzo Upper Hungarian copper enterprise with the crown; **1527 inventory**, capital about 1.6 million gulden **[A: NDB]**.
- **Portraiture [A]**: a Hans Maler portrait dated **1525** (Karlsruhe), a second Maler panel at the **Louvre** (RF 2002 28), a **Burgkmair** silverpoint in Berlin, a drawing **attributed to Dürer** at Veste Coburg, the NDB itself calling that attribution uncertain. The sitting is placed at Schwaz because Maler worked there: an inference, labelled one.

## 3. The Emperor's purse, 1527-1534

- **1527**: Roman factoría closed after the Sack **[A: NDB; R for the fuller narrative]**.
- **1527**: marriage to Anna Rehlinger von Horgau **[A: NDB, en.wiki, de.wiki]**. **Contradiction, not resolved**: the dossier gives a contract of 5 February 1527 and notes sources differing between late February and early March for the wedding. The stop takes the contract date and records the divergence.
- **1529**: Fugger credits and arms deliveries assist the Habsburg defence at the siege of Vienna **[A: NDB]**. **No evidence Anton was there**; the stop pins where the money went, on a coordinate inherited byte-identical from `joseph_oppenheimer.journey.json`.

**1530, the Reichsgraf: three dates for one honour, not resolved.** NDB says 1526 ("Seine Erhebung in den Reichsgrafenstand (1526)"). German Wikipedia says 1530, twice and independently, in its Anton Fugger and Babenhausen articles. A **third, separate act** is documented for **20 June 1535**, Ferdinand I's privilege permitting the style *Grafen von Kirchberg, Weißenhorn und Marstetten*. The pool's lenses disagreed, chronology 1526 against geography 1530. **1530 is followed** as the majority reading, with the NDB variant and the 1535 privilege both in `date_confidence`. Jakob's own elevation was 1514, so 1526 is not a carry-over.

- The **Diet-of-Augsburg financing** pressure over Ferdinand's election as King of the Romans is **[A: NDB]**; the dossier's figure for it (275,333 gulden) is **[R]**, unused. The **Cologne coronation financing of 1531** is **[R]**, no evidence Anton travelled there, **folded into the Augsburg 1530 campa rather than given a pin**.
- **1531**: Charles V grants concessions on the Pacific coast of Peru and Chile; Anton lets them lapse by c. 1533 **[A: NDB, en.wiki]**. Placed at Augsburg, where the decision not to act was taken, since Anton never crossed the Atlantic. The **1532** partnership renewal is folded in. **1534**: imperial coinage privilege **[A: NDB]**; February 1534, Georg von Logau dedicates an edition of Ovid to him **[A: de.wiki]**.
- **Erasmus of Rotterdam [R: dossier]**: gold cup, thank-you letter of 7 July 1529, an offered stipend to settle in Augsburg, declined. One campa clause. Not an interlock: `saint_erasmus.journey.json` is the martyr.

## 4. Land instead of money, 1533-1538

Some **seventeen lordships between 1533 and 1557 [A: Historisches Lexikon Bayerns, "Fuggersche Herrschaften"]**.

- **Glött, 1533 [A: Lexikon]**. *Source correction*: "Glött" does **not** appear in the NDB, to which the pool mis-cited it. **Oberndorf am Lech** and **Kleinkissendorf**, both 1537: *tag upgraded R to A*, the Lexikon itemises both, the pool omitted Kleinkissendorf. Both folded into this campa.
- **Hirbishofen, 1534.** *Tag upgraded R to A*, the Lexikon itemises it. **The pool's coordinate (48.1697, 10.4436) is not shipped**: it carries the same spurious eastward offset as the pool's Babenhausen pins, and Hirbishofen is a hamlet of Kirchhaslach next to Babenhausen. It is named in that campa with no coordinate. **Gap, for a later pass.**
- **Donauwörth, from 1536 [A: Lexikon, "Dazu kam seit 1536 der Pfandbesitz der Reichspflege Donauwörth"]**. *Tag upgraded R to A.* A **pledge of an imperial office**, not a purchase, and tied by the Lexikon to **no French campaign**; the dossier's link to the 1536 French campaign is dropped as unsupported.
- **Babenhausen. Year disputed three ways, not resolved.** Lexikon "1536/39" (staged); de.wiki 1538; en.wiki 1539. Written as staged 1536-1539, with 1538 used only to order the segment. **The 1541-1546 rebuilding is confirmed verbatim** by the Lexikon.
- **Weißenhorn, 1533 to c. 1536 [A: NDB for the withdrawal amid conflict with Augsburg's Zwinglian civic government; Lexikon for its role as administrative centre]**. The dossier's further claim of a **brief detention of Anton by the Augsburg reformers** is inconsistently reported by its own compiler's account and **is asserted nowhere in the journey [R, dropped]**.
- **Raymund Fugger dies 3 December 1535 [A: NDB for the year, de.wiki for day and place]**. *Place correction*: the pool said "Augsburg (Fugger lands)". He died at **Mickhausen**, itself a Fugger lordship he bought in 1532; 48.2408, 10.6600. **Hieronymus dies 1538 [R]**, as is the family's admission to the Augsburg patriciate that year; neither is asserted in a campa.

## 5. The Schmalkaldic War, 1543-1547

- **1543**: Fuggers and Welsers raise 300,000 ducats for the French and Ottoman wars **[A: NDB]**, transacted Augsburg to Antwerp. **No evidence Anton ever went to Antwerp [R]**; the business ran through resident factors, **Veit Hörl** and later **Matthäus Örtel**. **1546**: capital at its documented maximum, about five million gulden **[R: NDB, a scholarly summary rather than a transcribed balance]**.
- **Donauwörth 1546, the secret disclosure. [R, flagged, not dropped.]** The dossier has Charles V secretly informing Anton there that imperial resources would be turned against the Schmalkaldic League. The verification pass found this in no reachable source, NDB included. Kept as a stop whose campa and confidence field both say so.
- **The move to Schwaz [A: NDB, route corrected].** NDB: "zog sich F. mit der Firmenleitung auf neutralen bayerischen Boden, später nach Schwaz in Tirol zurück". First to neutral Bavarian soil, **only later** to Schwaz; the pool compressed it into one move, and the journey restores both.

**The bonds torn or burnt, 1546.** *Attested*: a memorandum by **Dr. Holtzapfel** in the Fugger archives, printed by **Ehrenberg (1928), 118-119**, in which Anton says he had no means in Germany "save certain bills of His Majesty, which he had torn up or burnt". Ehrenberg found this more credible than the folkloric versions and dated it after the fall of Ingolstadt in 1546. *Rejected as legend* **[R]**: the cinnamon-scented fire, which the **family's own archive** (fugger.de) says has no contemporary evidentiary basis, is traceable no earlier than the late seventeenth century, and was told of other rich merchants. Sources disagree whether the protagonist is Jakob or Anton, the catalogue of Becker's 1866 painting of the scene (**Alte Nationalgalerie**, SMB 959700) favouring Anton.

**Ulm, the Fußfall, early 1547.** *Tag upgraded R to A*: NDB names "der berühmte Fußfall in Ulm" and dates the return to Augsburg to "anfangs 1547". **Caveat preserved**: NDB's construction is "nicht allein durch den berühmten Fußfall", the kneeling being one instrument among diplomatic, financial and personal intervention, so it is not staged as the whole of the mediation. **Quotation year corrected**: the Peutinger report is on Ehrenberg p. 101 as the pool had it, but dated there **26 February 1546**. It is **1547**, since Ehrenberg gives no year and his chronology runs from the Emperor mastering South Germany "at the end of 1546" to a loan concluded at Wittenberg on 15 May.

## 6. Winding up, 1547-1553

- **1547**: Hungarian exposure ended, liquidations begun **[A: NDB]**; no third Buda pin.
- **25 March 1548**: Anna dies at Schwaz, reportedly following childbirth, aged forty-two, buried at Babenhausen **[A for death and burial: NDB, giving her dates 1505-1548; R for the exact day and the childbirth circumstance]**.

**31 July 1548, three foundations on one charter date.** All three were mis-cited to the NDB and **none appears in that article**; all are re-cited to the **Historisches Lexikon Bayerns, "Fuggersche Stiftungen"**.

1. **Fuggerei charter renewed in explicitly Catholic form [A]**. Lexikon: "Am 31. Juli 1548 erneuerte Anton Fugger... den Stiftungsbrief von 1521", the Catholic clause "nochmals bekräftigt und präzisiert". Afterlife **[A]**: still running on essentially original terms at a symbolic rent, now 0.88 euro a year, after two thirds of it was bombed flat in 1944 and rebuilt.
2. **Holz- und Blatternhaus [A, attribution corrected]**. Anton was **not** the sole founder: the Lexikon attributes the two additional places created in 1548 to a co-endowment by **Veit Hörl**, separately named as a Fugger factor at Antwerp. A real internal interlock; the journey names Hörl.
3. **Waltenhausen almshouse [A, numbers corrected]**. Lexikon: "jeweils 25 Frauen und Männern", i.e. **twenty-five women and twenty-five men, fifty places**, not twenty-five in total as the pool implied. **Coordinate corrected** from 48.0453, 10.2367, about 20 km too far south-west, to **48.1900, 10.3639**.

- **1549-1550 [A: NDB]**: Anton separates his personal interest from the Spanish business, considers dissolution, drafts a second testament. Ehrenberg (100-101) quotes it, dated **22 March 1550** with a codicil of 1560, for his wish to bring the firm "to an end and retire" since his nephews showed no disposition to continue as merchants.
- **28 May 1552, the Asiento of Villach [A: NDB]**. **No evidence Anton was there**; the pin marks the instrument.
- **Hans Dernschwam sent east: date corrected to 1553/55 [A: NDB, "Die Entsendung des Altfaktors Hans Dernschwam 1553/55 in die Türkei"].** NDB calls him *Altfaktor* and **does not mention Ogier Ghiselin de Busbecq**; that embassy link is real in the wider literature but not carried by the cited source, so the journey omits Busbecq.

## 7. The creditors are many, 1553-1560

The late years survive in Anton's own voice more richly than any earlier phase, entirely through **Ehrenberg (1928)**. Six letters and orders of 1553-1557 are carried verbatim in the journey's `quote` fields, from pp. 109, 109-110, 110 twice, 115 and **280**, that last **corrected from the pool's 276-277**. **Two verified quotations were left unused**: "it is a great interest, if it be so" (p. 276), on Mary I borrowing at 13%, and "The creditors are many. A man might shudder to think of them" (p. 115), which has a **date problem**, the source printing "1568", eight years after Anton's death and almost certainly an error for 1557 or 1558, no corrected edition being reachable.

- **1542-1553**: Maestrazgo lease renounced, Guadalcanal silver mines given up **[A: en.wiki]**. **Almadén [R]**: securities for Spanish asientos from 1525 included the Maestrazgo revenues and access to the Almadén mercury complex. **No evidence Anton visited Spain.** The convict-labour regime at Almadén began in **1566, six years after his death**, and is not attributable to him.
- **1557 [A: NDB]**: Örtel makes unauthorised deals with Philip II, Spanish authorities seize Fugger silver transports, Anton dismisses him and cuts Spanish investment. **Spain's first state bankruptcy the same year damaged the firm and marks the start of its decline [A: fugger.de]**. Dossier claims of chronic illness impairing his management and the Weißenhorn textile wind-down are **[R]**, as is the 1555 report that Ferdinand lodged in the Fugger palace.

**26 July 1560, the third will: contradiction resolved, resolution named.** The dossier dated the final provisions to 26 July 1560, before the 14 September death; the **Fugger Archive catalogue at paperinmotion.org gives 1 November 1560**, after it. The **Historisches Lexikon Bayerns independently dates the Schneidhaus endowment to 26 July 1560**, purpose "zur Operation von Blasensteinen", so the November date is read as **probate or registration, not composition**. Totals (57 legacies, 10,900 gulden, plus 1,000 for alms) agree across both sources.

- **14 September 1560**: death at Augsburg after a long illness, aged sixty-seven **[A: NDB ("Nach langem Leiden verschied F. am 14.9.1560 zu Augsburg"), en.wiki, de.wiki]**.
- **Buried at Babenhausen, not at St Anna [A: NDB, "Beigesetzt in Babenhausen"]**. De.wiki's **St. Andreas (Babenhausen)** states the church became *Hauptkirche und Grablege* after Anton took the patronage rights; the year it gives, 1538, sits inside the three-way dispute above. That the move followed St Anna's passing into Protestant hands is the **dossier's framing [R]**, written as juxtaposition rather than assertion.
- **Coordinate correction applied throughout.** Babenhausen (Schwaben) is **48.1428, 10.2533**. The pool carried **two different wrong** values, 48.1706, 10.4106 and 48.1794, 10.4108, both roughly 12 to 13 km too far east.

## Interlocks

**Named in a campa, a direct or one-directional relation existing:**

- **`jakob_fugger_the_rich`**: uncle, guardian, employer, testator, named in two campas. All shared sites (Fuggerhäuser, Schwaz, Weißenhorn, Buda, Nuremberg, Almadén, St Anna) take their pins **byte-identical from Jakob's file**, the same firm and the same houses.
- **`martin_luther`**: via **Johann Eck**, whom Anton helped introduce at the papal court and whose Roman business was the bull against Luther. The Luther file separately names the Fugger townhouse on the Maximilianstrasse as the site of Cajetan's examination of Luther in October 1518, Anton's own family house.
- **`paracelsus`**: adversarial. His file records the 1529 Nuremberg attack on the **Fugger guaiac-wood monopoly** as a fraud on the poor, and the suppression of his syphilis tracts by a "Fugger-fearing faculty", four years into Anton's tenure and about a monopoly he ran. Placed on the **Holz- und Blatternhaus** stop, where Anton endows a house for the poor sick of the disease the wood was sold to cure.

**Refused, shared geography or century only**: `giulio_camillo` and `thomas_more` (network-level, via Erasmus), `johannes_reuchlin` (Augsburg 1513-14 overlap predating Anton's succession; his file does not mention the Fuggers), `pedro_de_mendoza` (that file mentions neither Fuggers nor Welsers).

**Standing gaps in the record itself**

- **Anton Fugger wrote no book.** The only writing of his own that survives is the **Titelbuch**, a chancery formulary for addressing princes and prelates, in manuscript, never printed. What exists instead are records issued under his authority: the 1527 inventory, the three testaments, the foundation charters. His voice here reaches us entirely through letters other people preserved and Ehrenberg printed.
- The **exact house of his birth** is undocumented, and the **Fugger premises in Rome and Breslau** appear in no reachable source, so those stops use city landmarks.
- **Whether Anton was ever personally at Vienna, Antwerp, Cologne, Villach, Almadén or in Spain**: no evidence found for any. Each such stop states in `date_confidence` that it pins the money or the instrument, not the man.
