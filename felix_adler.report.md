# Felix Adler (1851-1933): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Founder of the Ethical Culture movement and of the first free kindergarten in New York City, founding chairman of the National Child Labor Committee, professor at Columbia.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including anything resting on a single unreachable page. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Coordinates are claims like any other: an unsourced pin is a fabrication whatever tag sits beside it.

**Two cautions.** *Most of Adler's stops have no address*: the books, the journal, the committee, the Urban League board. The journey pins these to a declared Manhattan placeholder (40.7128, -74.006) and says so in each `date_confidence`. *And the most quotable secondary source is gone*: Gerhard Holzer's 2014 dedication speech for the Alzey plaque (aeu.org) **now returns 404**, so everything resting on it alone is **[R]** here.

## Phase 1. Alzey and the crossing (1851-1870)

- Born **13 August 1851 at Alzey**, Grand Duchy of Hesse, second son of Rabbi **Samuel Adler** and **Henrietta Frankfurter**. **[A: Wikipedia; Jewish Encyclopedia; Encyclopedia.com]**
- Birth house at the corner of **Schlossgasse and Amtsgasse**. **[R: Holzer only, now 404]**
- Grandfather **Isaak Adler (1753-1822)**, Torah scholar of Worms, buried in the rabbis' section of the **Heiliger Sand**. **[R: Holzer only.]** The German article on the cemetery calls that section the *Rabbinental* and does not list him among its notable graves. Coordinate corrected to **49.6297 / 8.3556**; the inherited 49.6321 / 8.3639 sits some 650 m northeast, in the town.
- **Emigration to New York, 1857**, after Samuel Adler is called as head rabbi of **Temple Emanu-El**, then on **East Twelfth Street** and there until 1868. **[A: Jewish Encyclopedia]** Holzer specifies February 1857, the boy five and a half. **[R: Holzer only]**
- **Port of embarkation unknown.** Le Havre and Bremen were the standard Rhineland routes; the journey pins Le Havre and labels it a reconstruction. **[R, declared gap]**
- Schooled in New York public and private schools including **Columbia Grammar School** **[A for attendance: Jewish Encyclopedia]**; the 1860s building is located by nothing consulted **[gap]**. The inherited pin 40.7736 / -73.9757 points near West Seventy-second Street at nothing relevant. Dropped rather than guessed; stop folded into the Columbia College entry.
- **Graduates Columbia College, 1870, with highest honors**, at the Forty-ninth Street and Madison Avenue campus. **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia]** Month and day not given. **[gap]**

## Phase 2. Berlin, Heidelberg, and the sermon (1871-1873)

- Studies at the **Hochschule für die Wissenschaft des Judenthums** in Berlin, preparing for the rabbinate. **[R]** The first hard error the pool carried under **[A]**: the Jewish Encyclopedia and the JTA obituary date it **1871**, but **the Hochschule was not established until 1872** (twelve students in its first year; en.wikipedia.org). Earliest possible attendance 1872-73, and Adler is not among its listed notable alumni. Demoted to [R], date moved to 1872. The Tucholskystrasse coordinate (52.5279 / 13.3956) is **anachronistic**: that house, now the Leo-Baeck-Haus, held the school only **from 1907**. Street pin dropped for Berlin at large.
- Philosophy and economics at the **University of Berlin**, 1872. **[R: Jewish Encyclopedia]**
- **Doctorate in philosophy (Semitics), summa cum laude, Heidelberg, 1873.** **[A: Jewish Encyclopedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Month not given. **[gap]**
- **The trial sermon at Temple Emanu-El**, dated **11 October 1873** by Encyclopedia.com. Wikipedia describes it, remembered as "The Judaism of the Future," as abandoning the personal God of the liturgy for an ethic of conduct and ending his prospects at his father's synagogue. **[A]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Holzer places the godless sermon in **1876** and has the Society founded out of it, compressing three events: sermon c. 1873, lecture May 1876, incorporation February 1877. The journey follows 1873; the rival dating is recorded.

## Phase 3. Cornell, and the lecture of May 1876

- **Nonresident Professor of Hebrew and Oriental Literature, Cornell, 1874.** **[A: Wikipedia; Jewish Encyclopedia; Encyclopedia.com]**
- **1876: Cornell declines to continue funding the chair**, the reason reported as his unorthodox religious views. **[A: New World Encyclopedia]**
- **15 May 1876: the founding lecture of Ethical Culture.** **[A: Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com; Ethical NYC]** At **Standard Hall**, Forty-second Street and Broadway **[R: hall and site rest on the Society's own history page]**. **Contradiction flagged:** Ethical NYC dates the Society to this lecture, Wikipedia and New World Encyclopedia to the **February 1877** incorporation. Both carried, as two stops.
- **February 1877: the Society incorporated**, with help from the banker **Joseph Seligman**. **[A: Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Day not given. **[gap]** Articles for *Johnson's Universal Cyclopedia*, 1876, are **[R: Jewish Encyclopedia only]** and get no stop.

## Phase 4. The works of the Society (1877-1890)

- **District Nursing Department**, nurses sent into tenement homes. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** Wikipedia **1877**, Jewish Encyclopedia **1878**. **Lineage claim rejected:** the inherited text called it "the seed of what becomes the Visiting Nurse Service of New York," but no consulted source supports the descent, and the VNSNY traces itself to **Lillian Wald's Henry Street work in 1893**. Clause removed.
- ***Creed and Deed*, 1877**, G.P. Putnam's Sons. **[A: Encyclopedia.com; full text at Project Gutenberg 37697]** The only Adler book read directly; every quotation in the journey comes from it or from the 1876 founding address.
- **1878: the first free kindergarten in New York City.** **[A for the year: Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Three inherited problems, corrected. (1) **The street is unsourced and the atlas contradicted itself:** chronology gave **West 64th Street** (40.7735 / -73.9819), geography **West 44th Street** (40.758 / -73.9855); no source gives either, and the 64th Street figure is the **1910 Meeting House** contaminating backward. Both dropped for a declared placeholder. (2) **"First free kindergarten in the United States" is false:** Peabody's Boston kindergarten (1870) and Blow's St. Louis kindergarten (1873) precede it. (3) **"Alfred Wolff," a converted dance hall, eight children on the first day:** search-derived, uncorroborated, kept out.
- **The 1878 / 1880 "contradiction" dissolves and is not carried as one.** The Jewish Encyclopedia's "1880: Established free kindergarten" is a compression: **1878 is the kindergarten, 1880 is when elementary grades were added and the school named the Workingman's School**, renamed **the Ethical Culture School in 1895**. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ethical Culture Fieldston School']**
- **Marriage to Helen Goldmark, 1880.** **[A: JTA obituary; Wikipedia]** Chronology says **Brooklyn**, geography says New York City generally; Brooklyn followed and hedged. Sisters **Josephine** and **Pauline Goldmark** are reformers in their own right. Five children: **Waldo, Eleanor, Lawrence, Margaret, Ruth [A: JTA obituary]**.
- **Tenement House Building Company, 1885**, limited-dividend model housing; six buildings on the Lower East Side by 1887 at about $155,000. **[A: Wikipedia]** **International Journal of Ethics founded 1890**, later *Ethics*. **[A: Encyclopedia.com]**

## Phase 5. The movement travels (1891-1901)

- **Summer School of Applied Ethics, Plymouth, Massachusetts, 1891**, with William James and John Dewey named among participants. **[R: Encyclopedia.com; roster reported, not documented]**
- **German Society for Ethical Culture, Berlin, 1892**, founded by **Wilhelm Foerster** and **Georg von Gizycki**, spreading into Switzerland and Austria. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ethical movement'; Holzer]** No source places Adler there. **[R for any personal presence]**
- **The Einstein claim is rejected.** Holzer has the young Einstein meeting Adler's ideas through this society in his **Zurich** years. Nothing consulted connects Einstein to Ethical Culture in Zurich (1896-1900); his documented association is Berlin and later American. Dropped.
- ***The Moral Instruction of Children*, 1892.** **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Wikipedia works list]**
- **Carnegie Hall.** Sunday meetings move through **Chickering Hall** (Fifth Avenue and Eighteenth Street) to **Carnegie Hall** across the 1880s and 1890s. **[R: Ethical NYC only, dated by decade]** This carries the atlas's strongest interlock, the Stebbins school of 1893; see Interlocks below.
- **British ethical societies** multiply through the 1880s and 1890s; four London societies federate as the **Union of Ethical Societies in 1896**, carried over by intermediaries such as **Stanton Coit**. **[R: Wikipedia, 'Ethical movement'; nothing documents Adler in London]** Folded into the Lowell stop, no pin of its own.
- **Lowell Institute, Boston, 1896-97**, six lectures on "The Ethics of Marriage." **[A: Wikipedia]**

## Phase 6. Columbia and the children in the mills (1902-1918)

- **1902: chair of Political and Social Ethics at Columbia**, created for him, held until his death. **[A: Wikipedia; Jewish Encyclopedia; JTA obituary]**
- ***Life and Destiny***. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** Encyclopedia.com **1903**, Britannica-derived summary **1905**. The journey carries 1903 with the disagreement stated in the stop.
- **1904: founding chairman of the National Child Labor Committee.** **[A: Wikipedia; Jewish Encyclopedia]** Address unknown. The inherited geography lens gave the founding office as 40.7484 / -73.9857, the Empire State Building site, under an **[A]** tag; that pin was invented and is removed.
- ***The Religion of Duty*, 1905** and ***The Essentials of Spirituality*, 1905.** **[A: Wikipedia works list; OpenLibrary first-publish year 1905 for the latter, James Pott and Co.]** The pool's **1908** for *Essentials* is wrong by three years, taken from Wikipedia's error-ridden prose rather than its bibliography. For *The Religion of Duty*, 1905 and a Britannica-derived 1906 both circulate; 1905 followed.
- **1908: exchange professor at Berlin**, holding the **Theodore Roosevelt Chair of American History and Institutions**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** JTA obituary **1908**, Holzer **1909**.
- **1908: the Committee hires Lewis Hine.** **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **1910: the Meeting House at 2 West 64th Street**, architect **Robert D. Kohn**, Secessionist facade, later landmarked. **[A: Ethical NYC]** **Canonical pin 40.7711 / -73.98 inherited byte-identical from robert_oppenheimer.journey.json.** The atlas has carried three coordinates for this one building; the interlock pin wins by rule, and the funeral stop reuses it.
- **1910s: executive board of the National Urban League.** **[A for membership: Wikipedia]** The year he joined is nowhere given **[gap]**; the journey pins 1911, the League's founding year, as a declared placeholder.
- **1917: the Civil Liberties Bureau**, forerunner of the ACLU. **[A: Wikipedia; Jewish Encyclopedia]**
- ***An Ethical Philosophy of Life*, 1918.** **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Wikipedia]**

## Phase 7. Last decade (1923-1933)

- **May 1923: the Hibbert Lectures.** **[A: JTA obituary for the year; Wikipedia works list for the rest]** Two corrections: delivered at **Manchester College, Oxford**, not the university at large, and published as ***The Reconstruction of the Spiritual Ideal*** (D. Appleton, OCLC 607376), whose title page names college and month. They were **not** published as *An Ethical Philosophy of Life*, which appeared in 1918.
- ***Reconstruction*: publication year unsettled.** Wikipedia's works list gives the Appleton first edition **1923**, OpenLibrary **1924**; the **1925** figure appears only in Wikipedia's prose with no edition behind it. Best statement: lectures May 1923, published 1923-24.
- **1928: the Fieldston campus opens in Riverdale, the Bronx.** **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Ethical NYC]**
- **1928: president of the Eastern Division of the American Philosophical Association.** **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **1932: an operation for intestinal illness**, cited a year later as contributing to his death. **[A: JTA obituary]** Folded into the death stop.
- **24 April 1933: dies at Mount Sinai Hospital, aged 81.** **[A: JTA obituary; Wikipedia]** The inherited geography lens hedged the death as **[R]** at a generic Upper West Side pin (40.7831 / -73.9712) for want of a residence; the obituary names the hospital. Hedge removed, pin moved to 40.7903 / -73.9527.
- **27 April 1933: funeral at the Meeting House, 2 West 64th Street**, addresses by **Dr. John Elliott** and **David S. Muzzey**. **[A: JTA obituary gives "Thursday at 10 a.m."; the Thursday after Monday 24 April 1933 is the 27th]**
- **Buried at Mount Pleasant Cemetery, Hawthorne, New York**, under **a large uncut boulder** carrying no cross, star, or religious symbol. **[A for the burial: Wikipedia; R for the boulder: Holzer only]** Three coordinates circulated: **41.1004 / -73.7929**, **41.1067 / -73.7936** (about 1.5 km north, probably outside the grounds), and OpenStreetMap's **41.0930 / -73.7900**, which the journey uses. Findagrave's **memorial number 11** for Adler is **[R]**: the page returned 403.

## Phase 8. Afterlife

- **1933 onward: the ethical societies of Germany and Austria are banned** under the National Socialists; the American movement continues. **[R: Holzer only]** Adler died that April.
- **Horace L. Friess**, Adler's son-in-law and a Columbia philosopher, wrote ***Felix Adler and Ethical Culture***, published **posthumously in 1981** (Friess died 1975). **[A: Wikipedia]** The chief modern biography, and **not consulted for this report.**
- **2 November 2014: a bronze relief dedicated on the birth house**, the second Adler monument in Alzey after his father's on Hospitalstrasse. **[R: Holzer only]** Holzer's framing: Adler is "scarcely known" in his birth town.
- **The motto.** The attested phrasing is **"deed, not creed"** (Wikipedia, New World Encyclopedia; it matches the 1877 title *Creed and Deed*). Holzer's **"Deed is more important than faith"** looks like a re-translation from the German text of his speech and should not stand as a direct quotation under **[A]**.

## Quotations: verified, corrected, excluded

All quotations in the journey come from the **1876 Founding Address** (American Ethical Union text) or from ***Creed and Deed*, 1877**, read in full at **Project Gutenberg 37697**. Three errors in the pool were caught by reading that text line by line.

1. **"not in the creed but in the deed! In the deed is the pledge of the sacredness of life..."** Verbatim, but attributed to Discourse I, "Immortality." It is in **Discourse VII, "Our Consolations"** (line 2891).
2. **"Virtue can have no recompense, save as it is its own recompense, and vice its own punishment."** A genuine misquote; the second clause is absent from Adler. Actual, lines 479-480, Discourse I, which *is* the right discourse: "...and vice can receive no real punishment save as it is its own avenger."
3. **"...the new the Eternal within ourselves."** Two words dropped. Actual, lines 1574-76, Discourse III: "the new the Eternal **that is** within ourselves."

**Excluded:** "The bloom of human life is morality; whatever else we may possess, health," a fragment cut mid-sentence. **Carried with a caveat:** "They compel me to do nothing which I was not previously resolved upon" is **Spinoza's** reply to the herem as **Adler retells it** in Discourse VIII, used at the sermon stop because it is Adler's own rendering in his own book; the `quote_source` says so.

## Honest gaps

- **The Friess biography (1981) was not consulted.** Most of what follows is probably answered in it.
- **No address for** the 1878 kindergarten, the 1880 Workingman's School, the Child Labor Committee, the journal, the Society's offices, or Adler's own residences in New York.
- **No month or day** for most events. ISO dates default unknown month and day to 01, and each `date_confidence` says so.
- Unknown: the **port of embarkation (1857)**, the **Columbia Grammar building of the 1860s**, the **year he joined the Urban League board**.
- **No primary Adler manuscript, letter, or diary was read.** The base is two encyclopedia entries, one obituary, one history page, one dead speech, one book text.
- **Nothing was found** on the internal politics of the Society, on Adler and Stanton Coit, or on how a free school for working people's children became one of the most expensive private schools in the country. That last is the largest hole here.
