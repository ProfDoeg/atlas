# Gustav Landauer (1870-1919): research report

*2026-08-21. Written from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses). Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, paraphrase, or reachable only at second remove. Contradictions are flagged, not silently resolved; where the journey file had to pick one reading to place a pin or a date, the choice and its reason are stated here and in the stop's `date_confidence`. Subject: German anarchist, translator of Meister Eckhart and Kropotkin, editor of Der Sozialist, founder of the Sozialistischer Bund, commissar for enlightenment in the first Bavarian Council Republic, killed at Stadelheim on 2 May 1919.*

## 1. Karlsruhe and the universities, 1870-1891

**[A: Wikipedia; de.wikipedia; nsdoku.de]** Born 7 April 1870 at Karlsruhe, third child of Hermann Landauer, shoe merchant, and Rosa née Neuburger (English sources spell it *Neuberger*; variant noted, not resolved). Attended the Bismarck-Gymnasium, 49.0134 / 8.3949, about 1.5 km from the birth pin at 49.0094 / 8.4044; the German article gives no years for it.

**Contradiction, adjudicated for pin placement.** The English chronology's 1888 entry names "Heidelberg, Strasbourg, and Berlin" but attaches the Berlin coordinate. The German article gives a sequence: Heidelberg 1888, Berlin spring 1889 (where he meets **Fritz Mauthner**), Strasbourg c. 1890-91. One compiled biography says "Heidelberg 1888-1891", which cannot be right, overlapping the other two matriculations. The file follows the German sequence and narrows Heidelberg to 1888-89; the 1888 Berlin coordinate is treated as an error. No doctorate, no academic post, ever, and nothing reachable explains why he broke off.

## 2. Der Sozialist, the congresses, the prisons, 1891-1899

**[A: de.wikipedia]** April 1891 joins the Freie Volksbühne; February 1892 the Verein Unabhängiger Sozialisten and the editorial board of *Der Sozialist*. **[A: Wikipedia]** 1893, the novel *Der Todesprediger*.

**[A: prabook; Horrox at theanarchistlibrary.org]** August 1893, anarchist delegate to the Zurich congress. Bebel's exclusion motion carries, the German delegation ejects its own anarchists by force, the expelled hold a rump meeting where Landauer speaks. **[A: de.wikipedia; Horrox]** October 1893, arrested for an article urging disobedience of the law; imprisoned at **Sorau** in the Niederlausitz (now Żary, Poland; 51.6425 / 15.1378). Two months, plus nine more in December for incitement.

**Contradiction: the marriage to Grete Leuschner.** English Wikipedia gives "late 1891 / early 1892"; German Wikipedia gives **October 1894**, as does the geography lens independently. The file follows the German date, being the specific one and consistent with the household chronology; the disagreement is recorded in the stop, not erased.

**[A: de.wikipedia]** 1 October 1895, the cooperative **Befreiung** opens at Kottbusser Straße 11 (52.4975 / 13.4185). Same year, *Der Sozialist* relaunched and *Ein Weg zur Befreiung der Arbeiterklasse* published, both by year only. **[R: Wikipedia]** October 1896, exposes the police spy Commissar Bösel; arrested, acquitted. Carried as a clause inside the Friedrichshagen campa, having no distinct place beyond "Berlin". **[A: Wikipedia]** 1896, the London congress, arguing for peasant cooperatives. Year only, no venue, no text of his intervention.

**Correction: Friedrichshagen, and the order of events.** The English chronology says he "separates from his first wife Grete and moves to the Friedrichshagen literary colony", inverting the causality. Per Friedrichshagen local research (ptgustavlandauer) and the German article he moved there in **October 1897 with Grete and both daughters**; the break came **4 May 1899**, after Grete found a love letter to Hedwig Lachmann. Coordinate corrected: one lens gave 52.4453 / 13.6247, in the Müggelsee about 900 m south of the settlement. The file uses 52.4530 / 13.6280.

## 3. Hedwig Lachmann, Tegel, England, 1899-1902

**Correction: the meeting with Lachmann is an anachronism in two lenses.** Both place it at the **Neue Gemeinschaft**, the Hart brothers' circle at Schlachtensee, founded in **1900** and running to about 1904. It did not exist in early 1899. Per the German article on Lachmann they met **28 February 1899 at a reading in the house of Richard Dehmel**. Schlachtensee is a real Landauer connection for 1900-02, and where Erich Mühsam belongs, but the wrong setting here.

**Correction: the Tegel sentence is misdated in two lenses.** Both give March 1899. Per socialisme-libertaire.fr and the Jewish Virtual Library the term ran **18 August 1899 to 26 February 1900**, and the Eckhart translation and the Mauthner editing belong inside that window. Coordinate corrected: one lens gave 52.5896 / 13.2891, about 900 m northeast of JVA Tegel at 52.5828 / 13.2836.
**Correction: three errors in one England item.** The geography lens reads "London, England, 1899-09/1902-06". All three parts are wrong. Dates: the stay ran **September 1901 to 1902**; he was in Tegel until February 1900. Place: they lived at **Bromley, Kent** (c. 51.406 / 0.015), near Kropotkin, not central London. Daughter: Gudula was **not born in England** but on **15 August 1902 at Berlin-Hermsdorf**, after the return. A fourth follows from it: the lens describes the January 1901 Lützowplatz sublet as taken "back from England", but that address precedes the stay, which begins September 1901.

## 4. Hermsdorf, Skepsis und Mystik, the Bund, 1902-1914

**[A: gustav-landauer.org, "Wohnorte in Berlin: 1902-1917"]** The Berlin addresses in order: Lützowplatz 14, sublet, January 1901 (52.5049 / 13.3505); Schloßstraße 17, Hermsdorf, June 1902 (52.6247 / 13.2996), the only one carrying a plaque today; Fellbacher Straße 26, then Kaiserstraße, June 1908 (52.6255 / 13.305); Oggenhausener Straße 2a, then Tresckowstraße, June 1914 (52.626 / 13.307). **[A: hdbg.eu; hohenemsgenealogie.at]** Daughters by Lachmann: **Gudula**, 15 August 1902; **Brigitte**, 1906.

**[A: Wikipedia; de.wikipedia]** May 1903, divorce finalised and marriage to Lachmann; same year *Skepsis und Mystik: Versuche im Anschluss an Mauthners Sprachkritik*. 1907, *Die Revolution*; January 1907, *Volk und Land: Dreissig sozialistische Thesen*. **[A: `martin_buber.journey.json`]** Buber, editing *Die Gesellschaft* for Rütten und Loening from 1906, commissions Landauer by name (with Simmel, Sombart, Tönnies); *Die Revolution* is the volume delivered. **[A: Wikipedia; libcom.org]** June 1908, founds the **Sozialistischer Bund** on the Twelve Articles; Buber and Erich Mühsam are drawn in.

**Correction: the Ascona date, and a partisan source downgraded.** The chronology lens dates Monte Verità to 1906 and frames it through Gusto Gräser, citing gusto-graeser.de. Per the German article the visit was **summer 1908**, the occasion on which he met the settlers and **Margarethe Faas-Hardegger**. No primary source reachable here places Landauer with Gräser; [R], **not carried into the file**. **Canonical pin inherited:** the stop uses **46.1608 / 8.7658**, byte-identical to the pin in `gusto_graeser.journey.json` and `otto_gross.journey.json`, not either lens figure.

**[A: Wikipedia]** January 1909, *Der Sozialist* revived as the Bund's organ. 1911, *Aufruf zum Sozialismus*, and the pamphlet *Die Abschaffung des Kriegs durch die Selbstbestimmung des Volks* confiscated by the Berlin police.

## 5. The war, Krumbach, Düsseldorf, 1915-1918

**[A: Wikipedia]** April 1915, *Der Sozialist* ceases. **[A: de.wikipedia]** 1916, promotes the opening of the **Jüdisches Volksheim**, Max-Beer-Straße 5, Scheunenviertel (52.5265 / 13.4085). **[R: Wikipedia]** 1916, the Aufbruchkreis with Kurt Hiller and Ernst Joel, and lectures on Goethe, Hölderlin and Shakespeare carrying veiled political criticism; rightly tagged [R], since no lecture text is cited anywhere reachable and the "veiled criticism" reading is a summary judgement, not a document. **[R: Wikipedia]** December 1916, open letter to Woodrow Wilson urging a postwar congress. Reported, not reproduced.

**Correction: the Krumbach move.** It was **May 1917**, not "early 1917": Lachmann's mother died in February, leaving the Hürben flat empty. Krumbach is loosely called "Hedwig Lachmann's home town"; she was born at **Stolp in Pomerania, 29 August 1865**, and spent seven childhood years at Hürben, now part of Krumbach. Only Gudula and Brigitte went; **Charlotte stayed in Berlin** minding the Hermsdorf flat. "Roughly twenty-five years in the capital" understates it: he was in Berlin from 1889, about twenty-eight.

**[A: Wikipedia; de.wikipedia]** 21 February 1918, **Hedwig Lachmann dies** at Krumbach of influenza and pneumonia, aged 52. **Gap: the Düsseldorf dramaturgy.** "End of 1918" is the whole record, with no contract, month or production attached to his name. The thinnest stop in the file, dated 1918-10-01 purely to order the segment before the Munich move.

## 6. Munich, November 1918 to May 1919

**Correction: two Munich items misdated by a month.** The chronology lens gives **October 1918** for Eisner's call, impossible with Eisner not yet in power; the letter is dated **14 November 1918** ("dass Sie durch Vortragstätigkeit an der Umbildung der Seelen mitwirken") and Landauer arrived **15 November**. The same lens dates the council memberships to December; he entered both councils **on arriving**.

**[A: Wikipedia; de.wikipedia]** 7 November 1918, Eisner proclaims the Free State of Bavaria. 21 February 1919, **Eisner assassinated**, the same calendar day on which Lachmann had died a year earlier. 7 April, the first Räterepublik proclaimed and Landauer made **Beauftragter für Volksaufklärung**. 13 April, a Communist-led second Räterepublik displaces the first; **16 April**, he resigns every office.

**Correction: Wittelsbacher Palais coordinate.** One lens gave 48.1436 / 11.5799. The palace stood at **Brienner Straße 20, 48.1442 / 11.5722**, about 600 m off. Delegates met there 5 April and resolved on the Räterepublik.

**[A: de.wikipedia; Lunn]** 1 May 1919, arrested at **Großhadern** (48.1089 / 11.4661). Two accounts of one arrest: the German article names a house in Großhadern, Lunn the house of Kurt Eisner's widow; taken here as the same house, though **no source states that identification outright**. Held overnight at **Starnberg** (47.9997 / 11.3438), then Stadelheim the morning of 2 May. Beaten and shot; wounds recorded to the left eye socket, right forehead and left chest. Among the assailants **Eugen Digele**, who took Landauer's watch; tried March 1920 and given **five weeks, for assault and for receiving stolen goods**, never for the killing. **Franz Freiherr von Gagern** court-martialled September 1919, fined 500 Mark.

**Correction: Stadelheim coordinates, wrong in all three lenses.** The figures given were 48.0836 / 11.5814, 48.0781 / 11.6069 and 48.0967 / 11.6053, between about 1 km and 2.6 km out. The prison stands at **Stadelheimer Straße 12, 48.0997 / 11.5919**.

## 7. Quotations: what is verbatim and what is not

**Fetched and read directly [A]**, from theanarchistlibrary.org's translation of "Schwache Staatsmänner, schwächeres Volk!" (*Der Sozialist*, 15 June 1910): the "state is a social relationship" passage (used), plus two others verified and not used. **One remove [A as citation]**, via Wikiquote citing Gabriel Kuhn's *Revolution and Other Writings*: "Anarchic Thoughts on Anarchism" (p. 87), "Tucker's Revelation" (p. 249). **Second remove, not seen in a Landauer primary edition**: the two letters to Buber (1901, means and ends; 1914, Tolstoi and abstention from violence), via Wikiquote citing Friedman, I.251-52. All used, with the chain named in `quote_source`.

**Correction: the Buber-review sentence carries a typo in circulation.** The libcom / Muraskin text reads "Judaism is **a not** an external contingency … into a single community", an error of the printing rather than of Landauer. The cleaner published reading is Mendes-Flohr and Reinharz, eds., *The Jew in the Modern World*: "Judaism is not an external contingency, but an inalienable inner property which transforms a number of individuals into a community." Horrox renders it differently again ("not an external accident, but a lasting internal quality … within a Gemeinschaft"). The Mendes-Flohr reading is used and the "sic" dropped. Occasion: the **1908 review of Buber's *Die Legende des Baalschem***.

**Correction: placement of "I've not betrayed you".** The pool placed these words at the arrest on 1 May. The **wording is confirmed verbatim** (Toller's eyewitness account in *I Was a German*, quoted in full in Lunn p. 338; also Liptzin and Cherniavsky), but Lunn's sequence puts them in the passage at **Stadelheim, morning of 2 May**, as he was driven out with rifle butts. The file places them there.

**Three [R] quotations, all used with the doubt stated in `quote_source`.** The last words: two English forms circulate, the fuller "Kill me then! To think that you are human!" and libcom's shorter "To think that you are human"; the German is "Erschlagt mich doch. Daß ihr Menschen seid." "Socialism is possible at all times, if enough people want it": a circulating rendering of the *Aufruf*'s thesis, unconfirmable against a full text (HTTP 403). The Whitman line: given in libcom as paraphrase of his school programme, not a cited document.

**About him, not by him [A]**: Buber's tribute, and the FAUD obelisk inscription, which is Landauer's own text quoted on the stone. Both used where another party's act is the occasion. **[A: Friedman I.254] "Now comes death, now one must hold one's head high!"** runs as reported speech in the Stadelheim passage campa, its `quote` slot taken by the betrayal line. **Not used:** "That blasted carrion has nine lives", attributed to a killer; [R], unverified beyond one retelling.

## 9. Honest gaps

Richard Dehmel's address in February 1899 was not established, so that stop sits on the Berlin city pin. The Düsseldorf dramaturgy is "end of 1918" and nothing more. The Wilson letter and the wartime lectures are reported in summary and neither is reproduced, so both are [R] in the file. The London 1896 intervention is a year with no venue and no text. The 1895 Swiss settlement attempt, [R] with "exact Swiss location not specified", is **dropped from the journey file** for want of a place to pin it and recorded here instead. The 2017 unveiling is framed as 2017 by PM Press with no date and nothing independent reachable. One lens calls Peter Kühn a musician of a "Martin Buber Society"; no source supports it, and per raete-muenchen.de he is a musician and systemic counsellor from Flemlingen, so the description is **dropped**.

**Cemetery coordinates.** Neuer Israelitischer Friedhof: two lenses disagree in the fourth decimal, 48.1821 / 11.6008 against 48.1815 / 11.6038, while a third figure, 48.1073 / 11.5442, is about 9 km off in Sendling and is discarded. The file uses 48.1815 / 11.6038; the residual 200 m is unresolved and probably reflects two points inside one cemetery. Waldfriedhof: two lenses gave 48.12 / 11.49 and 48.1103 / 11.4933, both north of the cemetery at 48.1028 / 11.4936, corrected.
