# Alexandra David-Néel (1868-1969): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `alexandra_david_neel`. Companion file `alexandra_david_neel.journey.json`: 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested; a bare **[A]** means both the English and French Wikipedia articles on the subject carry it, and anything else is named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or the traveler's own undocumented narrative. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**, except where one side is impossible (a dead man, an arithmetic slip). Where a pin forced a choice, the pick is stated here and the loser is printed in that stop's `date_confidence`.

**The subject.** Louise Eugénie Alexandrine Marie David: Paris-born, Brussels-raised, opera soprano as Alexandra Myrial, anarchist pamphleteer, self-declared Buddhist from 1889 with no teacher and no sangha, cave hermit in Sikkim, first Western woman known to have entered Lhasa (February 1924, disguised, on foot), author of some thirty books, dead at Digne-les-Bains six weeks short of a hundred and one.

---

## 1. Origins and the Belgian childhood, 1868-1891

- Born **24 October 1868 at Saint-Mandé**, to a Huguenot father in republican and anarchist circles and a Belgian Catholic mother. **[A; Foster & Foster, *The Secret Lives of Alexandra David-Néel*, 1998]**
- **1871, Père-Lachaise.** Her father shows her the wall where Communards were shot. **[A as an anecdote in Foster & Foster and FR Wikipedia; the causal reading into her later politics is theirs, not a document]**
- **1873-1874.** The family emigrates to Belgium, settling at **Ixelles, Brussels**. **[A: FR Wikipedia]**
- **1883, aged fifteen.** Absconds from a family holiday at **Ostend** toward **Vlissingen (Flessingue)**, meaning to embark for England; turns back for lack of money. **[A]**
  - **Correction.** The pool had her going "by bicycle." Neither edition mentions one; the bicycle belongs to a later solo ride into Spain. Struck.
- **c. 1886-1888.** Studies "at Madame Blavatsky's Theosophical Society." **[A as to the fact; dated only by her age, "at 18"]**
  - **Correction.** The pool placed this at "Paris/London" in 1886. Blavatsky was resident at **Ostend** in 1886 and reached London only in **May 1887**; Paris is unsupported. The journey pins London and states the range in `date_confidence`.
- **1889.** Reads in the **British Museum** library, moves in London Theosophical circles, converts to Buddhism, keeps the diary later published as *La Lampe de sagesse* (1896). **[A; Foster & Foster]**
- **1891.** First India journey on a small inheritance: the Theosophical headquarters at **Adyar** **[A: Foster & Foster]**, then at **Varanasi** the ascetic **Swami Bhaskarananda Saraswati** **[A]**.
  - **Gap.** The site of the Varanasi meeting is not fixed across sources; the Manikarnika Ghat pin is explicitly approximate and the journey carries the flag.

## 2. Alexandra Myrial: opera and anarchism, 1895-1904

- **1895-1897.** First soprano in French Indochina as **Alexandra Myrial**, singing Violetta and Marguerite at **Hanoi**. **[A]**
  - **Flag raised here, not in the pool.** The pool pins this to the **Hanoi Opera House (Nhà hát Lớn)**, built 1901-1911 and therefore postdating her seasons. The coordinate is kept as a city-of-performance marker and the anachronism stated in `date_confidence`.
- **1898.** *Pour la vie*, an individualist tract. **1899**, a treatise on women's rights prefaced by **Élisée Reclus**, later in five languages. **[A]**
- **Nov 1899 to Jan 1900.** Engagement at **Athens**. **[A: FR Wikipedia; the theatre no longer stands]** Folded into the Tunis stop.
- **July 1900, Tunis.** Meets **Philippe Néel de Saint-Sauveur**, distant cousin, chief engineer of the Tunisian railways. **1902**, leaves the stage, briefly directs the Tunis casino. **4 August 1904**, marries him. **[A]**
- The Montmartre pin for her 1898-1900 journalism is **[R]**: the writing is well attested, the address is not documented.

## 3. The third departure and the Sikkim years, 1911-1916

- **9 August 1911.** Departs alone, telling Philippe she expects about **nineteen months**. The absence runs **fourteen years**. **[A]**
  - **Gap.** No source consulted names the port of embarkation; pinned on the Paris node, and the stop says so.
- **15 April 1912.** Audience with the **thirteenth Dalai Lama** in exile at **Kalimpong** (Bhutan House); he blesses her study and promises to answer doctrinal questions. **[A]**
  - **Contradiction, not resolved.** A minority of sources put the meeting at **Darjeeling**. Kalimpong is the more commonly cited site and is used; the alternative is printed in `date_confidence`.
- **May 1912, Lachen.** Meets the **Gomchen of Lachen**, receives the name **Yeshe Tome**, "Lamp of Wisdom," and lives intermittently to 1916 in a hermitage above the village at roughly 4,000 m. **[A]**
- **1912-1914, Gangtok.** Close to the Sikkimese royal house, above all the crown prince **Sidkeong Tulku**, dead suddenly in December 1914. **[A: FR Wikipedia]**
- **1914.** Meets **Aphur Yongden**, aged fifteen, at a monastery in Sikkim. **[A]**
  - **Correction.** The pool dated this **October 1912, near Darjeeling**; both editions give **1914, in Sikkim, aged fifteen**. Wrong by two years and wrong on the place; the superseded reading is recorded in `date_confidence`.
- **13 July 1916.** Crosses into Tibet without permission with Yongden and a monk, against the British no-entry edict. **[A]**
- **19 July 1916, Tashilhunpo (Shigatse).** Received by the **ninth Panchen Lama**, granted honorary titles. **[A]**
  - **Correction.** The pool called him the **sixth**, who was Palden Yeshe (1738-1780), dead 136 years before the visit; the lama at Tashilhunpo in 1916 was **Thubten Choekyi Nyima, the ninth**. An older Tibetan reckoning does number him sixth, but the pool gave no such caveat, so the standard numbering is used.
- **1916.** British authorities issue a **deportation notice**. **[A as to the year only; the journey places it after the July return and says the month is inferred]**

## 4. Across China, and Kumbum, 1917-1921

- **1917.** Unable to reach wartime Europe, she travels through **Japan, Korea, Beijing**, then crosses China east to west through the **Gobi and Mongolia**. **[A]**
- **1918-1921.** Roughly three years at **Kumbum Monastery (Ta'er Si)** in Amdo, translating portions of the **Prajnaparamita** with Yongden. **[A]**
  - **Correction.** The pool gave 36.5075, 101.5661; Ta'er Si, Huangzhong, Qinghai surveys at approx. **36.4958, 101.5750**, about 2 km away.

## 5. The road to Lhasa, 1923-1924

- **1923.** Final approach staged from the **Yunnan-Tibet borderlands near Atuntze (Deqin)**, disguised as an *arjopa*, a poor pilgrim, with Yongden posing as her son. **[A: *My Journey to Lhasa*, 1927; FR Wikipedia]**
- **The river crossings are the weakest geography in the file, and deliberately so.** She obscured her route in print to protect the villagers who sheltered her, so biographers reconstruct it. Two problems:
  1. **"Salween crossing near Markam" is internally contradictory. [R, flagged]** The pool's 29.6833, 98.6167 is essentially the Markam county town, in the **Mekong/Jinsha** corridor; at that latitude the Salween runs near **97°E**, some 150 km west. Either the river name or the coordinate is wrong and the sources do not settle which. The journey **keeps the pin, drops the river from the stop name**, and states the contradiction in `date_confidence`.
  2. **"Dokar La" pass. [R]** Named only in her own narrative, with no surveyed coordinate confirmed under that name. 29.6, 96.8 is a reading off the text, labelled a reconstruction.
- **Chaksam ferry over the Yarlung Tsangpo. [A as event; coordinate corrected]** The pool gave 90.4954; the historic ferry lies by **Qüxü (Chushul)**, approx. **29.35, 90.74**, about 24 km east.
- **February 1924.** Reaches **Lhasa** with the crowds gathered for **Monlam**, the first Western woman known to enter the city. **[A]**
- **1924, about two months in the city.** Visits **Drepung, Sera, Ganden** and **Samye** in disguise; attends the **Tsomchö Sertreng** near the Potala on **4 April**. **[A: FR Wikipedia; *My Journey to Lhasa*]**
  - **Flagged.** Samye lies some 150 km southeast of Lhasa across the Tsangpo, so calling it part of a circuit "around Lhasa" is loose. The canonical atlas pin (`giuseppe_tucci`, 29.35 / 91.42) also sits west of the surveyed monastery at **29.3255, 91.5037**; it is retained for globe consistency and the discrepancy printed in `date_confidence`.
- **1924.** Nearly unmasked because she **washes daily at the river**, which no beggar does; denounced to the governor **Tsarong Shapé**, she leaves before the authorities move. **[A]**
- **May 1924, Gyantse.** Reveals her identity.
  - **Contradiction, not resolved, and one conflation undone.** FR Wikipedia: at Gyantse "elle est accueillie par **Frank Ludlow**," head of the English school. EN sources credit **David Macdonald**, British Trade Agent at **Yatung**, who lent her **500 rupees** and arranged her papers with his son-in-law Captain Perry. The roles are distinct and both plausible; the pool merged them into "Macdonald sheltered her at Gyantse." The journey names both and declines to choose. **Coordinate corrected** to Gyantse Dzong, approx. **28.9253, 89.5947**, 1.4 km north of the pool's figure.
- **10 May 1925.** Lands at **Le Havre**, met by Philippe Néel. **[A on the date. The pool tags its geography entry for Le Havre [R] while its chronology entry for the same event is [A]; noted, not resolved.]**

## 6. Fame, Samten Dzong, the disputed book, 1925-1936

- **1925-1928.** Settles a few years at **Toulon** **[A: FR Wikipedia]**, folded into the Le Havre stop. **1927**, *Voyage d'une Parisienne à Lhassa* / *My Journey to Lhasa* is published in Paris, London and New York, bringing both fame and immediate scepticism about *tummo* heat, trance-running and levitation. **[A]**
- **1928.** Buys the house at **Digne-les-Bains**, enlarged from 1932, named **Samten Dzong**, "fortress of meditation," described as the first Lamaist hermitage and shrine in France. **1929**, *Mystiques et magiciens du Tibet* and the **legal adoption of Aphur Yongden**. **1930**, *Initiations lamaïques*. **1939**, *Buddhism: Its Doctrines and Its Methods*. **[A]**
- **By 1936** the 1929 book exists in English under **two titles**, *Magic and Mystery in Tibet* and *With Mystics and Magicians in Tibet*. **[A: EN Wikipedia]**

## 7. The second Asia, 1937-1946

- **1937.** At sixty-nine she departs via **Brussels** and **Moscow** on the **Trans-Siberian**, meaning to study Taoism in China, arriving into the **second Sino-Japanese War**. Route by **Beijing** and **Wutai Shan** (1937-1938), then **Chengdu** (1938). **[A: FR Wikipedia]**
- **1938-1944, Tachienlu (Kangding).** A five-year wartime retreat with Yongden, working on *Sous des nuées d'orage*. **[A as to the retreat]**
  - **Contradiction, not resolved.** EN Wikipedia: **4 June 1938**. FR Wikipedia: **4 juillet 1938**. The journey carries the June date and prints the conflict.
- **1941.** Learns of **Philippe Néel's death**. **[A as to the year]**
  - **Correction.** The pool said "by letter in **February** 1941." No source consulted gives a month; FR Wikipedia says only "la mort de son mari en 1941." The month is dropped.
- **June 1946.** Leaves Asia **by air from Calcutta** with Yongden, aided by French Consul **Christian Fouchet**. **1 July 1946**, arrives Paris; returns to Digne in **October**. **[A]**

## 8. Last years, death, afterlife, 1952-2006

- **1952.** *Textes tibétains inédits*, including erotic poems attributed to the **sixth Dalai Lama**. **[A]**
- **7 October 1955.** **Yongden dies** suddenly of kidney failure at Samten Dzong, aged fifty-six. **[A]**
  - **Correction.** The pool, following FR Wikipedia, said she was left alone "at age 87." Born 24 October 1868, she was **86**, turning 87 seventeen days after his death. The French article's "à 87 ans" is an arithmetic slip and is not copied.
- **April 1957.** Leaves for **Monaco**, hotel to hotel, rheumatic: "I walk on my arms." **[A]**
  - **Contradiction, unresolved and unresolvable in the sources at hand.** One EN source has her resident at Samten Dzong **only 1925-1957**, which cannot be squared with her dying there in 1969, and **no source describes a return**. Recorded as a gap; the atlas does not invent a journey home.
- **June 1959.** Engages **Marie-Madeleine Peyronnet** as secretary, who stays to the end and becomes her heir. **1961**, *Immortalité et réincarnation*. **1964**, *The Secret Oral Teachings in the Tibetan Buddhist Sects*, with Lama Yongden on the title page nine years after his death. **[A]**
- **May 1968.** Renews her **passport** at the Digne prefecture, saying she means to return to Tibet. **[A]**
  - **Correction, an internal contradiction in the pool resolved by weight.** One chronology entry dated this **1969** and hedged it as unverified tradition; three other entries, including FR Wikipedia directly ("À presque 100 ans, en mai 1968, comptant retourner au Tibet, elle renouvelle son passeport"), give **May 1968** plainly. Retagged **[A]**, dated 1968-05. The office within the prefecture is not documented.
- **8 September 1969.** Dies at **Samten Dzong**, aged 100. **[A]**
- **1972.** **Jeanne Denys**, *Alexandra David-Néel au Tibet: une supercherie dévoilée*, argues she never entered Lhasa and that the photograph was a montage; **Jacques Brosse** and others reject it as made in obvious bad faith. **[A that the charge and the rebuttal both exist. The photograph is NOT re-verified here and the atlas does not claim to have done so.]**
- **1973.** Peyronnet carries her ashes and **Yongden's** to **Varanasi** and immerses them in the Ganges. **[A: FR Wikipedia; Foster & Foster]**
- **Honors. [A]** Samten Dzong now operates as the **Fondation Alexandra David-Néel** museum; streets in Montpellier, Bondy, Lille, Massy, Rennes; a **lycée at Digne**; a **tram stop on Paris T3a**.
  - **Two corrections.** (1) The pool put the tram stop at 48.8322, 2.3708, near Place d'Italie in the 13th; it is in the **12th**, between Montempoivre and Porte de Vincennes, approx. **48.844, 2.411**, some 4.5 km off. No dated stop exists for it in the journey, but the error is recorded so it does not propagate. (2) "More than 40 books" in the pool; EN Wikipedia says "over **30** books."
- **Priscilla Telmon** retraces the route alone on foot (Vietnam, Yunnan, Tibet via Lhasa, Sikkim, India) and makes a documentary.
  - **Contradiction, not resolved.** EN Wikipedia: **2006**, film *Au Tibet Interdit*. FR Wikipedia: expedition **2002**, film *Voyage au Tibet interdit*, released 2005-2006, walked at 27 over six months. The journey carries 2002 and prints both readings.
- **Cultural reach. [R, single tertiary source]** Cited as an influence on Kerouac, Ginsberg, Alan Watts, Ram Dass and Benjamin Creme, with a 1991 opera titled *Atlas* said to draw on her life. One Wikipedia section, no Beat-studies corroboration. **Not used in the journey file.**

## Quotations: verification status

Every quote carried into the journey was checked against the underlying scan. Line numbers refer to the raw djvu OCR. Chapter attributions in the pool were wrong in several places.

- **"Ever since I was five years old…"** Verbatim, archive.org `dli.pahar.2325`, lines 204-207. **Correction:** the pool cites ch. 1; it is the **Introduction** (running heads at 195/239/284, CHAPTER I begins at 678). Cited as Introduction, p.16. The pool's filename `2325_djvu.txt` 404s; the correct file is `1927 My Journey to Lhasa by David-Neel s_djvu.txt`.
- **"…I took an oath that in spite of all obstacles I would reach Lhasa…"** Verbatim, lines 602-606, exclamation mark included. **Correction:** Introduction, not ch. 1.
- **"I had chosen to travel as an arjopa…"** Verbatim, lines 1517-1522. No correction.
- **"but, and why should I not confess it…"** Verbatim, lines 1523-1527. **Correction:** the pool turned an **authorial question mark** into a comma, believing it an OCR artifact; it is not, and what the OCR dropped is the closing dash. **Not used in the journey.**
- **"This time we have won,"** (line 13728) and **"Lha gyalo. De tamche pam! We are at Lhasa!"** (lines 13899-13901, spoken by **Yongden**). Both verbatim. **Correction:** ch. **VII**, not the "closing chapter," since CHAPTER VIII begins at 15891.
- **"I was in Lhasa. No doubt I could be proud of my victory…"** Lines 13903-13905, ch. VII, with **one word restored**: the scan reads "= not yet over," an OCR artifact, and "was" is grammatically forced. Restoration flagged in `quote_source`.
- **"I shut myself in [retreat] and proceeded to perform…"** Verbatim, *Magic and Mystery in Tibet*, archive.org `2015.107735.-magic-and-mystery-in-tibet`, lines 16128-16132, ch. VIII. **Correction:** the pool's ellipsis after "other rites" is **spurious**, nothing being omitted. The `[retreat]` bracket is honest; the raw OCR reads "esams" for Tibetan *tsams*.
- **"the primary recommendation… is: \"Doubt!\"…"** Verbatim by direct PDF extraction, *The Secret Oral Teachings*, p.15. No correction.
- **"When I adopted the principles of Buddhism…"** and the Gomchen's **"You have seen the ultimate and supreme light…"** Both verbatim on tricycle.org. The first episode is **undated in the source**, which is why it gets no stop of its own and sits on the 1889 London stop as a retrospective; the second reaches us through a 1995 magazine article rather than a manuscript, reporting a c. 1914 conversation.

Three further quotes were verified and left unused: two remaining tulpa passages (lines 16162-16166 and 16182-16185), one such quote per stop being enough, and the *Secret Oral Teachings* p.9 passage on false Buddhisms, paraphrased into campa rather than quoted.

## Interlocks with the existing atlas

Five atlas travelers are named in campa where the paths genuinely cross.

1. **`blavatsky`**. Turned back at the **Darjeeling frontier** by the British in 1856, where David-Néel gets through the same corridor in 1924. She told followers she **lived at Shigatse in 1868**, the year David-Néel was born, and the atlas holds her at 29.2666, 88.88 **on her word alone**; David-Néel reaches those coordinates in 1916 with a date and an audience. Adyar and the London Theosophical circle are shared nodes, but the pool flags **[R]** that no atlas file records a meeting or correspondence between the two women, and none is asserted. Cut by name from the Shigatse campa on the house-style pass of 2026-08: shared institutional membership in Theosophy without documented personal contact falls short of the bar, and the stop now contrasts David-Néel's verified date against unnamed travelers' claims instead.
2. **`nicholas_roerich`**. In **December 1923**, one year before her Lhasa entry, the Roerichs take **Talai Pho-Brang** at Darjeeling, the villa the exiled thirteenth Dalai Lama had used, and stage five years of expedition from it. Their party is **halted near Nagchu for five months in the winter of 1927-28**, with valid passports and five dead, the mirror image of her undetected entry. **Helena Roerich dies at Kalimpong in 1955**, where David-Néel was received by the Dalai Lama in 1912.
3. **`giuseppe_tucci`**. Assembles **all eight** Tibet expeditions (1930-1948) out of Darjeeling, Gangtok and Kalimpong; studies the **mandala plan of Samye** in 1937; reaches **Lhasa in 1948** and is received at the Potala by the fourteenth Dalai Lama, aged thirteen, twenty-four years after her. His file also carries the **1950-1951 closure** of the plateau, falling inside her late writing years at Digne.
4. **`gurdjieff`**. Followers relay a claim that he spent years in **Lhasa around 1901**, held by the atlas **on his word alone**. Hers rests on a published narrative with dates and streets. Was named at the Potala stop as the counterweight; cut by name on the house-style pass of 2026-08 as a same-city, different-era, no-citation pairing, with the contrast preserved impersonally.
5. **`keyserling`**. Climbs above **Darjeeling in March 1912** to see Kanchenjunga at dawn, the season she is first working the Sikkim frontier. A gateway coincidence, presented as one.

**Deliberately not used:** `savitri_devi`. The pool offers the **Amarnath cave, Kashmir, 1936** as a shared "northern approach corridor," a corridor-level abstraction with no contact of any kind. Using it would be interlock inflation.

## Honest gaps

- **Port of embarkation, August 1911.** Not named anywhere in the pool.
- **The Varanasi meeting site, 1891.** Not fixed across sources.
- **The Paris address of her 1898-1900 journalism.** No document; Montmartre is a placement, tagged **[R]**.
- **The route between Atuntze and Chaksam, 1923-1924.** She obscured it on purpose. Two of the three intermediate pins are reconstructions and one is internally contradictory (§5). This is the condition of the record, not a failure of searching.
- **Whether she returned from Monaco to Digne, and when.** No source describes a return, yet she died at Digne.
- **The Lhasa photograph.** Denys alleged montage in 1972, Brosse rejected the allegation, and neither the photograph nor the montage claim was examined here.
- **The Beat Generation influence claim.** Single tertiary source, uncorroborated, excluded.
- **1968 vs 1969 for the passport, June vs July 1938 for Tachienlu.** Live disagreements between the language editions; values picked to place pins, losers printed in `date_confidence`.

**Standing caution.** The largest single body of evidence for the Lhasa journey is the traveler's own book, written for publication two years after the fact by a woman who had spent four months lying to everyone she met. The register of this atlas is *national mythology: the canon is true*, and the canon here is *My Journey to Lhasa*. The report's job is to say where the canon is the only witness: the Dokar La, the river crossings, the interior of the disguise, and the two months inside the city. It is corroborated at both ends, by a documented departure, a documented arrival at Gyantse in rags, and a documented five hundred rupees from a British trade agent who had no reason to invent her.
