# Antoinette de Saint Léger (1856-1948): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-04 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: the woman who bought two
uninhabited rocks in Lago Maggiore in 1885, planted the garden that is now the Parco
Botanico del Cantone Ticino, received Segantini, Leoncavallo, Rilke, Kessler and Joyce
on them, and died sixty-three years later in the Intragna poorhouse.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or
inference. Contradictions between sources are flagged and left open. Sources that could
not be reached are named together with the reason.

---

## 1. Saint Petersburg: everything before Naples is contested

**Born 20 June 1856 in Saint Petersburg [A, de.wikipedia, "Antoinette de Saint Léger"].**
The date and the city are the only two facts in this phase that no consulted source
disputes. Even the tourism-register biography hedges the city: Silvano Pezzoli's ticino.ch
note (reproduced on stleger.info) writes «naît en 1856 (peut-être à Saint-Pétersbourg)»,
"perhaps at Saint Petersburg", with the hedge in the original **[A for the wording]**.

**Her surname is not agreed on [A, contradiction between de.wikipedia and fr.wikipedia].**
German-language sourcing gives **Bayer** (also spelled Beyer in the afterlife pass).
French-language sourcing gives **Tzikos**. Neither is documented against a register.
The atlas does not adjudicate; both are carried.

**The tsar's daughter [R, en.wikipedia, "Brissago Islands"].** Tradition, repeated across
four language editions and by the Springer chapter below, makes her an illegitimate
daughter of **Tsar Alexander II** and adds that she was ordered out of Russia within
forty-eight hours. English Wikipedia hedges it twice ("believed to be", "said to have
been"); Andreas Fischer writes "supposedly". No primary document is named by anyone. The
atlas carries it as tradition.

**The Smolny Institute [A, de.wikipedia].** German Wikipedia places her at the Smolny
Institute in Saint Petersburg, studying piano, with later study under **Liszt**. The
research pool had misplaced her musical education at Naples; it belongs here. **Gap:** no
source says where or when the Liszt tuition happened, so there is no Weimar stop and no
date, only a hedge in the Smolny campa.

## 2. Naples and Portici: two husbands, and a son who does not fit

**Sent to Naples [A, en.wikipedia].** Verbatim: sent "to Naples for health rehabilitation
and to learn Italian". One language, not "languages"; the research pool's plural was
corrected against the source text.

**Married Friedrich Stolte at Portici, 1873 [A, de.wikipedia].** German Wikipedia is
precise where English Wikipedia is blank: «1873 heiratete sie in erster Ehe in Portici bei
Neapel den deutschen Generalkonsul Friedrich Stolte († 1878).» The geography lens had
asserted that "his name is not given in any consulted source"; that assertion is **false**
and is corrected here. The wedding place is **Portici** (40.8156, 14.3350), some eleven
kilometres southeast of Naples proper, not Naples.

**A son by Giulio Ewald Jaeger, 1876 [A, de.wikipedia; chronology unresolved].** German
Wikipedia records a son born of a union with Giulio Ewald Jaeger in 1876, while Stolte is
still alive (he dies 1878). No source explains the overlap. **Flagged, not resolved.** No
place is given for the birth; the atlas pins it at Naples and marks the coordinate a
placeholder.

**Stolte dies 1878; she marries Richard Fleming 1881 [A, de.wikipedia].** No place is
given for either event. Fleming is variously **Fleming, Flemyng, Fleming de Saint Léger**;
Anglo-Irish, an officer, and in some renderings a banker.

**The Stresa placeholder [R, dropped].** The geography lens proposed a Stresa stop for a
paraphrase saying she frequented the lakes before 1885, with the town unnamed by any
source. A pin invented to fill a sentence is not a pin; it stays out of the journey.

## 3. The title: a barony that is not in the peerage

**[A that the style was used; [R] at best that it was ever conferred.]** English Wikipedia
says Fleming inherited a title from an uncle and that she thereafter styled herself
Baroness Antoinette de Saint Léger. German Wikipedia is more careful and carries the
disclaimer the English article drops: Fleming bore «die - angeblich von Wilhelm dem
Eroberer verliehenen - Titel eines Grafen von Doneraile und Barons von Saint Léger»;
*angeblich*, allegedly.

The check runs against her: **Doneraile is a Viscountcy in the Peerage of Ireland**, held
continuously across the relevant years by Hayes St Leger, 4th Viscount (d. 1887), Richard
Arthur St Leger, 5th Viscount (d. 1891), and Edward St Leger, 6th Viscount (1866-1941).
**No Richard Fleming appears in the succession.** The baronial style rests on a claim, not
a creation. The atlas gives her the title she used and calls it pretended.

## 4. The islands, 1885

**Purchased 1885 with Fleming's family inheritance [A, en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia].**
Both islands, Isola Grande (San Pancrazio) and the smaller Isola di Sant'Apollinare
(also Isolino, Isola Piccola, Isola dei conigli).

**Coordinate [A, corrected].** The afterlife lens gave 46.1338, 8.7247, which falls
roughly 830 m WNW of the islands, in open water. The correct fix is **46.1330, 8.7355**
(fr.wikipedia infobox 46.133025 / 8.735504; it.wikipedia 46°07'58"N 8°44'09"E). Every
island stop in the journey uses it.

**Commune [A, corrected].** The islands belong administratively to **Brissago**
(it.wikipedia infobox, "Suddivisione3 = Brissago"), not to Ronco sopra Ascona as the
afterlife lens stated.

**The convent was not a recent ruin [A, en, it, fr Wikipedia concur].** The building she
rebuilt was a **13th-century Umiliati house, suppressed by Pope Pius V in 1571**, its
property assigned to the hospital at Locarno; the islands then stood uninhabited for over
three centuries. The ruins of the Umiliati church stand on the **smaller** island, which
the geography lens wrongly said received no mention of its own.

**The garden [A, isoledibrissago.ti.ch; de.wikipedia].** Soil, exotic plants and gardeners
brought over by boat; paths laid; eucalyptus and palms planted. Dated **1886 onward** in
de.wikipedia and the isoledibrissago material, not 1885 as the chronology lens had it.

## 5. "Die grosse Zeit", 1886-1914

**[A, de.wikipedia]** names the span 1886-1914 as "die grosse Zeit der Antoinette de
Saint Léger". Guests attested across en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia:

| Guest | Bound |
|---|---|
| Daniele Ranzoni, painter | died 1889; painted her portrait in oil, 1886, on the islands |
| Filippo Franzoni, painter | no visit dated |
| Giovanni Segantini, painter | died 1899, outer bound |
| Ruggero Leoncavallo, composer | no visit dated |

**No single visit in this table is dated by any consulted source.** The atlas assigns each
a plausible year inside the attested span and says so in `date_confidence`. Scaffolding,
not evidence.

**"La sirena" [A, swissinfo.ch, Gerhard Lob, 25 July 2017].** «Venne soprannominata «la
sirena».» Reported by the 2017 article as a contemporary Ticino nickname, not sourced there
to an archival document.

**Fleming withdraws to Naples, 1897 [A, de.wikipedia; it.wikipedia].** Reported cause: her
financial dealings and her personal conduct. English Wikipedia softens this to his finding
her too impetuous, and adds that he took a post at the British consulate. He never returns
to the lake.

**The daughter who leaves by boat [R, en.wikipedia].** English Wikipedia states, as a bare
fact with no surrounding chronology and no date, that the couple's daughter left the island
with a gardener's help, escaping by boat. No other source consulted mentions a daughter at
all. Carried as [R], undated.

## 6. The other end of the lake: what the atlas already holds

Saint Léger's islands sit four kilometres down-water from the hill that the atlas already
knows well. None of the following is a meeting; all of it is proximity, and the journey
says so.

- **Engelmann** founds his vegetarian settlement at Monte Trinità above Locarno, 1898-1920
  (`engelmann.journey.json`).
- **Ida Hofmann** and Henri Oedenkoven hold the founding meeting at **Munich** in October
  1900 and buy Monte Verità from **Alfredo Pioda** that December (`ida_hoffmann`). Pioda
  appears independently at `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` line 286.
- **Rudolf von Laban** runs the dance farm on Monte Verità 1913-1917; **Mary Wigman** and
  **Suzanne Perrottet** arrive July 1913; **Elsa Gindler** is at the same hill that year.
- **Hermann Hesse** climbs Monte Verità in 1907 and settles at **Minusio** in April 1919.
- **Charlotte Bara** makes Ascona her home from 1920; the Teatro San Materno is raised for
  her in 1928.
- **Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn** opens Eranos at **Ascona-Moscia** in August 1933, and **Jung**
  lectures there from that first year.
- **Otto Gross** is named in `QUEUE.md` line 259 as part of the same circle; no journey yet.

**Explicit gap [R].** No consulted source records Saint Léger visiting Monte Verità,
meeting anyone from its colony, or being visited by any of them. In 1927 she moves to
Moscia, the shore where Eranos will open six years later, and still nothing links the two
circles. The proximity is geographic fact; contact is an unfilled gap.

**Two interlock claims are rejected.** The lens carried, flagged [R], traditions of a
**Buenos Aires** birth and of **Paris** society years. Neither appears in any source
consulted here, and Buenos Aires contradicts the one fact every language edition agrees
on. Both are recorded and given no pin.

## 7. Joyce, 1919

**Joyce is on the same shore before he ever crosses to her [A].** He convalesces at the
Pension Daheim, Locarno, from 5 November 1917 after Ernst Sidler's iridectomy
(`james_joyce.journey.json`).

**The visit [A, en.wikipedia; Art Ascona dates it May 1919].** In Locarno, working on
*Ulysses*, Joyce hears that the baroness of Brissago keeps wall scrolls painted with
scenes from the Odyssey, and crosses to the island.

**Correction: she was sixty-two, not sixty-three.** Born 20 June 1856, she turns 63 only
in June. If the visit is May 1919, "sixty-three-year-old" is off by one. **Flagged.**

**The trunk [A, verified this pass, upgraded from R].** The Oxford Figshare record
"Baroness St Leger" (portal.sds.ox.ac.uk article 22892909) returns 403 as HTML but is
readable through the Figshare API at `api.figshare.com/v2/articles/22892909`. Its
description states that Joyce "accepted from her a trunk of erotic books and letters,
which he intended to use in the writing of 'Circe'". The record credits **William
Brockman**; the earlier guess at Budgen or Ellmann is dropped.

**Locarno coordinate corrected.** The geography lens gave 46.1667, 8.8, about 600 m
southeast of the town centre and out toward the water. The atlas uses **46.1712, 8.7994**,
the canonical Locarno pin already carried by `james_joyce.journey.json`.

**Rilke and Kessler [A, en.wikipedia].** Received on the islands after the First World
War, 1918-1919; a second wave distinct from the pre-war circle.

## 8. The collapse, and the date of the sale

**The ruin [A, de.wikipedia].** Speculative investments named across sources:
**Transcaucasian railway shares and Romanian tram companies**. Failed business ventures
compound them.

**Smuggling [R, en.wikipedia only].** The claim that she used the islands as a base for
smuggling between Italy and Switzerland appears in the English article and in no other
consulted source. The atlas carries it as [R]. The Cannobio stop is explicitly a
reconstruction: no source names a crossing point, and Cannobio is the nearest Italian
shore town, chosen to represent an unattested route.

**Dolls [R, de.wikipedia].** «Sie stellte Puppen von berühmter Schönheit her.» The research
pool attributed this to an "unnamed biographical summary surfaced via web search"; it is in
fact the same German Wikipedia article cited throughout. **Gap:** no museum or collection
holding these dolls has been located, so [R] stands.

**THE PRINCIPAL CONTRADICTION: 1926 or 1927.** English Wikipedia gives the sale to
**Max Emden** as 1926, in two places. German, Italian and French Wikipedia all give 1927.
swissinfo.ch (25 July 2017) is more precise still: «fine 1927», the end of 1927. The atlas
uses **1927** as the headline date on the weight of three language editions plus the news
article, and states the English outlier in `date_confidence`. **This is not a resolution;
it is a majority, recorded as such.** Emden, a Hamburg department-store magnate and art
collector, builds the 24-room Villa Emden on the site.

## 9. Ascona, Intragna, the poorhouse

**Casa Moscia, Ascona, 1927 [A, de.wikipedia].** She moves there directly after the sale.
Moscia is the same shore where Eranos opens in 1933.

**San Donato, Intragna, November 1940 [A, de.wikipedia].** The nursing home, opened 1929.

**Died 24 January 1948 at Intragna [A, de.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia, GND 137493398].**
swissinfo.ch describes her dying "in the poorhouse at Intragna".

**Age corrected.** The research pool stated she died "at age 92 (per the 1856 birth year)
or reported elsewhere as 91". The arithmetic is inverted: 20 June 1856 to 24 January 1948
is **91 years and 7 months**. 91 is the figure consistent with the 1856 birth year; 92 is
the erroneous one.

**Intragna coordinate corrected.** de.wikipedia "Intragna TI" gives 46°10'39"N 8°42'00"E =
**46.1775, 8.7000**, elevation 369 m. Both lenses were off, the geography lens by about
600 m north and the afterlife lens by about 1.2 km southwest.

**The quote [A for swissinfo, R for the ultimate wording].** Shortly before her death she
is quoted as saying **«Tornerò a essere milionaria»**, "I shall be a millionaire again",
in a *Corriere della Sera* interview headlined «Dalla corte dello Zar all'ospizio dei
poveri». Both the sentence and the headline were read verbatim in Gerhard Lob's
swissinfo.ch article of 25 July 2017. **The original Corriere issue was not located.**
Verification stops one remove short of the newspaper.

## 10. Afterlife

**The estate auctioned [A, swissinfo.ch, 2017].** After her death the Locarno bankruptcy
office auctions her estate, including **Daniele Ranzoni's 1886 oil portrait of her**,
painted on the islands. The portrait passes through the Giacomo Jucker collection into a
private Italian collection.

**The islands sold on, 1949 [A, de.wikipedia for the price; it.wikipedia for the buyer].**
Max Emden dies in 1940; his son Hans Erich Emden sells in 1949 for roughly **700,000
francs**. The buyer was **not the Canton alone**: it.wikipedia gives «un consorzio che
comprendeva il Canton Ticino, i comuni di Ascona, Brissago e Ronco sopra Ascona». Full
cantonal ownership comes only in **2019**.

**THE SECOND CONTRADICTION: the grave.** The afterlife lens recorded an explicit gap: no
accessible source naming her grave or tomb. The chronology lens recorded the opposite:
fr.wikipedia "Îles de Brissago" states verbatim «Sa dépouille fut ensuite transférée sur
la Grande île le 12 août 1972.» **The lenses contradict each other.** The transfer is
attested and the gap is not, so the atlas carries the transfer. What stays open is **where
she lay between 1948 and 1972**, which no source consulted says.

**The portrait returns, July 2017 [A, swissinfo.ch].** Ninety years after she left, the
Ranzoni portrait is exhibited in the Sala Rossa of Villa Emden on Isola Grande, an
initiative of Milan's Galleria Maspes (Elena Orsenigo), running to mid-October 2017.

**The garden [A, it.wikipedia].** Her planting is the direct root stock of the **Parco
Botanico del Cantone Ticino**, roughly 1,700 species, over 90,000 visitors a year. She has
no personal monument. The institution is the monument.

**The diaries [A for their existence, de.wikipedia; gap for their fate].** More than sixty
volumes of her personal diaries are known to have existed. No institution is recorded as
holding them; no edition or translation exists. **Their fate after 1948 is undocumented.**

**The one archival trace [A, Archivportal-D].** Correspondence from her to **Leon Hirsch**
survives in the **Deutsches Exilarchiv**, in the Leon Hirsch Nachlass. It is the only
located archive holding of her own writing, unpublished, untranslated, and catalogued
undated; the journey pins it at Frankfurt and calls its year a placeholder.
