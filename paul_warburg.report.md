# Paul Moritz Warburg (1868-1932): research report

*2026-08-31, written for `paul_warburg.journey.json`. The Hamburg-born Kuhn, Loeb partner who argued the United States into a central reserve system, sat on the first Federal Reserve Board, was driven off it by wartime suspicion of his birth, warned about the market seven months before October 1929, and became sixty years after his death the central figure of an American conspiracy literature he never read.*

Legend: [A] attested, source named · [R] reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-only · inaccessible sources named with the reason. Journey file: **44 stops, 9 segments**. Every hedge below lives in the file's `date_confidence` fields, never in campa prose.

---

## 1. Hamburg, 1868-1891

[A] Born 10 Aug 1868, Hamburg, to Moritz Warburg and Charlotte Oppenheim (en/de.wikipedia "Paul Warburg"). Third son: Aby b. 13 Jun 1866 (eldest), Max b. 5 Jun 1867, then Felix and Fritz. The dossier's ordering implied Aby was younger; he was not, and the 1879 "bargain with Max" in `aby_warburg.journey.json` is what fixes Paul's position as the surplus son.

[R] Birth address. The dossier claimed a specific Rotherbaum house; unconfirmable. The file takes the canonical family pin **53.5636, 9.9908**, inherited byte-identical from `aby_warburg` and `max_warburg`.

[A] Realgymnasium, graduating 1886, no university; school unnamed in any reachable source. [A] Apprenticed at **Simon Hauer**, Hamburg, from 1886 (address unknown, stop approximates the merchant district). [A] **Samuel Montagu & Co.**, City of London, 1889-90. [A] Paris agency of the **Banque Russe pour le Commerce Etranger**, 1890-91. Firms and dates confirmed, offices not; Paris takes the **canonical atlas pin 48.8566, 2.3522** rather than an invented building. [A] Joins M. M. Warburg 1891, partner 1895.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: 1893.** Encyclopedia.com's chronology says "Travelled worldwide to study international finance." de.wikipedia says he was made **Prokurist** that year. English Wikipedia mentions no tour. The Encyclopedia.com URL now 301-redirects to a 404 and **could not be re-read**; other accounts place his Asian travel at 1891-92. The campa states the conflict and declines to decide it.

[A] Ferdinandstrasse 75 is at **53.5524, 9.9978** (de.wikipedia); the dossier's 53.5474, 9.9968 fell ~550 m south. Site continuity back to 1891 *is* documented: the bank moved onto that plot in 1868 and rebuilt on it in 1911, resolving the dossier's own doubt in its favour.

## 2. The Loeb marriage and the crossing, 1895-1905

[A] Marries **Nina Jenny Loeb**, daughter of Kuhn, Loeb cofounder Solomon Loeb, New York, **1 Oct 1895**; venue unidentified.

[A, interlock] `aby_warburg.journey.json` carries this event from the other side: a stop dated 1895-09-01, "New York, his brother Paul's wedding," at **40.7128, -74.006**, the errand that opened Aby's Pueblo journey west. Paul's wedding stop **inherits that pin byte-identical**. Strongest cross-file interlock in the subject.

[A] **Hamburgische Bürgerschaft, 1900-1902**, one of its first Jewish members (de.wikipedia), upgrading the dossier's [R]. It matters: the standard American framing of Warburg as a foreign banker with no political past is wrong on the record, and the correction exists only in German. [A] Permanent relocation autumn 1902, general partner at Kuhn, Loeb 1903-1914.

**Address contradiction, resolved.** The dossier put the 1902 arrival at **52 William Street**, coordinates 40.7043, -74.0089. Two errors: the firm was at **27 Pine Street** from 1894 and moved to William Street only in **1903**; and 52 William Street is at the William/Pine corner near 40.7071, -74.0086, while the dossier's pin landed several blocks south near Hanover Square. The file names Pine Street, takes 40.7071, -74.0086, and states the one-block discrepancy.

[A] Institute of Musical Art opens 11 Oct 1905, former Lenox Mansion, Fifth Avenue and 12th Street, founded by Frank Damrosch, endowed by **James Loeb**, Nina's brother. [R] Paul and Nina's patronage is dossier-only.

**Identity trap caught, stop dropped.** en.wikipedia "Temple Emanu-El of New York" lists a "**Paul F. Warburg**" among notable members. That is **Paul Felix Warburg, the son, b. 1904**, not Paul Moritz. The dossier's Temple Emanu-El stop rested on that list and would have staged the son's membership as the father's, so **it is not in the journey file**. Its building dates were wrong too (sold Jan 1926, demolished 1927, current building consecrated 1930).

## 3. The reform campaign, 1907-1912

[R] "**Defects and Needs of Our Banking System**," New York Times, 1907. Attested in the dossier chronology and Federal Reserve founding summaries, but **the 1907 text was not fetched** and the day is unpinned. The Medici comparison, that American banking had reached about the stage Europe reached "at the time of the Medicis," reaches this file through the dossier's report of the article, not the article. It is the one place an atlas traveler outside the family is named on a one-directional citation across centuries: `cosimo_de_medici.journey.json` is in `suggested_refs`, and Cosimo is named in the campa because Warburg invoked the period himself.

[A] Panic of 1907; clearinghouse cooperation and J. P. Morgan's private coordination avert collapse. The pool fixes only the year, so the stop is dated 1907-10-01, October being the Knickerbocker suspension.

[A] "A Plan for a Modified Central Bank," **12 Nov 1907**, vol. I p. 23. [A] Letter to Senator Nelson Aldrich, **31 Dec 1907**, Appendix Two, vol. I pp. 555-556. All three quoted passages verified verbatim against archive.org `federalreservesy0001paul`.

[A] *The Discount System in Europe*, 1910, for the National Monetary Commission. **Upgraded from the dossier's [R]** via the archive.org catalogue record; FRASER returns **HTTP 403**, so archive.org is the citation of record. [R] Director of **Wells Fargo & Co.**, Feb 1910, widely reported but not re-fetched; no stop of its own, folded into the 1914 confirmation campa where the resignation from it is the load-bearing fact.

**Jekyll Island** [A]. Departure from **Hoboken Terminal**, evening of **22 Nov 1910**, private car, first names only, duck-shooting cover (en.wikipedia "Jekyll Island Club"); about a week at the club, producing the outline of the Aldrich Plan. Undisputed: Aldrich, A. Piatt Andrew, Henry P. Davison, Frank A. Vanderlip, Warburg, and Aldrich's secretary **Arthur Shelton**, whom the file names rather than leaving anonymous. **Disputed and not asserted:** Benjamin Strong and Charles D. Norton, whom the Wikipedia article names but whose attendance is contested; the campa says so and settles nothing. **Source-line error corrected:** the dossier called the participant list "documented in the National Monetary Commission's published report." It was not and could not have been, the trip being secret.

[A] Naturalized 1911, month and day unrecorded, defaulted to 01. [A] Commission's final report and draft bill of **59 sections**, **9 Jan 1912**; **contradiction resolved**, the dossier gave 8 January. [A] The 1912 Democratic platform opposes "the Aldrich plan or a central bank"; Wilson's election kills the plan while its mechanics survive into Glass-Owen. [R] 7 Dec 1912, at Henry Morgenthau Sr.'s request Warburg prepares a draft compatible with Democratic commitments; dossier chronology only.

## 4. The Act and the first Board, 1913-1918

[A] House Committee on Banking and Currency, **7 Jan 1913** (vol. I p. 55). Attested *through Warburg's own 1930 recollection*, **not** the transcript, which was not reached. The caveat matters: the man summarising the testimony is the man defending his record seventeen years later.

[A] Act signed **23 Dec 1913** (38 Stat. 251). Warburg's presence at the signing is not established by anything reached and is asserted nowhere in the file.

[A] Testimony before the **Reserve Bank Organization Committee, Jan 1914**, Appendix Twenty-Eight, vol. I **p. 759**, not vol. II p. 745 as the dossier had it (p. 745 is Appendix Twenty-Five, a different document).

[R] Senate confirmation: **Senator Joseph L. Bristow** of Kansas questions him roughly a day and a half on Kuhn, Loeb ties, interlocking directorates, and alleged Rothschild agency; Warburg denies the agency. **federalreservehistory.org returned HTTP 403** and the Senate record was not reached, so the 1 and 3 Aug 1914 dates are unconfirmed.

[A] Takes his seat **10 Aug 1914**, his forty-sixth birthday. [R] That the Board's first offices were rooms inside the Treasury is general institutional history, unconfirmed by anything fetched. **Coordinate correction applied at four stops:** the dossier used **38.8934, -77.0342** for the Treasury Building, a pin falling on the Ellipse ~470 m south; en.wikipedia gives **38.8976, -77.0343**, which every Board stop now uses.

[A] Twelve Reserve Banks open **16 Nov 1914**. [A] **Vice governor from 10 Aug 1916**, again his birthday; the substance of his gold-pooling and rate-uniformity argument is [R]. [R] Twin City Bankers' Club, St. Paul, 22 Oct 1915, and ABA convention, Kansas City, 29 Sept 1916, both surviving in the dossier bibliography as a title and a date only, neither venue identified; **St. Paul was cut** under the 45-stop ceiling, Kansas City kept.

[A] Letter to Wilson, **27 May 1918**, Appendix Thirty-Two, vol. I pp. 802-803; three passages verified verbatim. *Note correction:* the dossier said brothers **Max, Fritz and Felix** all remained German nationals. Felix did not; he emigrated, naturalized, and became a Kuhn, Loeb partner. Warburg's own sentence reads "I have two brothers in Germany who are bankers," meaning Max and Fritz. *Citation corrections:* the dossier placed all three 1918 passages in vol. **II**; they are in vol. **I**, and one spans pp. 802-803 rather than 803-804.

[A] Board service ends **9 Aug 1918**.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: resignation or expiry.** Board membership tables record a four-year term run to its end. en.wikipedia "Warburg family" calls it a "resignation in August 1918." Warburg's own May letter asks only that his name not be resubmitted *if* a fight would damage the system, which supports neither reading cleanly. The campa states all three positions and asserts none.

## 5. The acceptance years, 1919-1927

[A] **American Acceptance Council** founder and first chairman, 1919 (en.wikipedia; upgrades the dossier's [R]).

[R, thin] **Amsterdam, Oct-Nov 1919**, private banker meetings with Gerard Vissering, Fred I. Kent, Raphaël-Georges Lévy and **John Maynard Keynes**, feeding into the 1920 Brussels conference. Asserted **only in the dossier**; no other source reached, and `john_maynard_keynes.journey.json` has **no Amsterdam stop and does not mention Warburg**. Independently attested is only the setting: Keynes resigned from the Treasury delegation in June 1919 and wrote *The Economic Consequences of the Peace* that autumn. The file keeps the stop, names Keynes, and says all of this in `date_confidence`. If a harder standard is wanted, this is the stop to cut.

[A] **International Acceptance Bank** founder and first chairman, 1921 (en.wikipedia, corroborated by the JTA obituary). [A] **Council on Foreign Relations** incorporated 29 Jul 1921, Warburg a director 1921-1932; the dossier's note was too pessimistic, since the CFR article's list of Root, Davis, Cravath and Gay is an officer list rather than an exhaustive founding-director roster, and the directorship is sourceable from en.wikipedia "Paul Warburg" alone. CFR had no premises until Harold Pratt House in 1945, so no 1921 address is used.

[R] **Federal Advisory Council**, New York district, through 1926, presiding from 1924. Rests on Encyclopedia.com's chronology, **whose page now returns 404**; no roster reached.

[R] Trustee of the **Institute of Economics** from 1922, continuing after the merger. [A] The merger: Institute for Government Research, Institute of Economics and Robert Brookings Graduate School consolidate into the **Brookings Institution on 8 Dec 1927**. The trusteeship stays [R] though the merger date is [A]; the 1922 address is unconfirmed, Jackson Place used as an approximation.

[R] Pasadena rest period, 1927-28. The **year** is dossier-only; the **fact** that the first draft was prepared at Pasadena is attested by the first sentence of Warburg's own Preface.

## 6. The warning, the book, the death, 1929-1932

**The March 1929 warning: two things the dossier got wrong.** It attributed to Time (18 Mar 1929, time.com/archive/6743142) Warburg quotations about "orgies of unrestrained speculation" and about the Fed having "lost control of the money situation." **Neither is in that article**, which was fetched. It contains [A] investment trusts as "incorporated stock pools", [A] Federal Reserve rates as "grotesquely impotent and out of line", and **no date** for the annual report.

The "orgies" sentence *is* genuine Warburg, but it circulates through Galbraith's *The Great Crash*, not Time. The file quotes it from its real source: **Appendix Thirty-Seven, vol. I p. 824**, headed "Remarks of the Author as Chairman of International Acceptance Bank, Inc., made in his Annual Report March 7, 1929", verified verbatim. *Date contradiction resolved:* the dossier's chronology said **9 March**, its geography lens **7 March**; Warburg's own heading says 7 March, which the file uses while stating the conflict. *Volume correction:* the appendix is in vol. **I**, not II, per the Preface (p. xi) and the title page, which reads "IN TWO VOLUMES / VOLUME ONE".

[A] **Aby Warburg dies of a heart attack in Hamburg, 26 Oct 1929**, two days after Black Thursday and three before Black Tuesday; the dossier's month-resolution dating is unnecessarily coarse. [R] Paul's presence in New York that day is inferred; the stop is his, at a New York pin, because the event in *his* life is receiving the news. [R] The claim that Paul helped fund Aby's library could not be verified and is hedged in the campa.

**The Manhattan Company merger: demoted, then dropped.** The dossier claimed [A] that in 1929 the International Acceptance Bank combined with the Bank of the Manhattan Company and that Warburg chaired the reorganized institution. **No fetched source supports this.** en.wikipedia "40 Wall Street" lists the bank only as one of four Manhattan Company subsidiaries tenanting the 1930 tower, mentions no merger, and does not mention Warburg. **The 40 Wall Street stop is not in the journey file.**

[A] *The Federal Reserve System: Its Origin and Growth*, Macmillan, New York, **1930**, two volumes, confirmed via the Arno Press 1975 reprint record; the dossier's **April** month is unsupported and dropped. [A] The "national monument" passage is verified verbatim but sits in the **Introduction, p. 10**, not the Preface as the dossier said (the Preface is separately paginated in roman, pp. ix-xi). Its ellipsis honestly elides "and that all must feel an equal degree of concern and responsibility for its welfare."

[R] **Carl Schurz Memorial Foundation**, 1930, Warburg as treasurer. Dossier plus an unread JTA listing. **Cut under the 45-stop ceiling**, and a real loss to the shape of the life: the man attacked for German birth spending his last years funding German-American exchange. The operator may want it restored in place of a weaker stop.

[A] JTA dispatch **15 Jan 1932**, condition "causing extreme anxiety." [A] **Dies 24 Jan 1932**, New York, pneumonia following a slight stroke about a month earlier, aged 63 (en and de.wikipedia, independently). The dossier hedged to "mid-to-late January" because the JTA obituary is dated 26 January; **that is the report date, not the death date**. [R] The home address appears in nothing reached; the two death stops use an Upper East Side placeholder (40.7736, -73.9566).

[A] Buried at **Sleepy Hollow Cemetery**, Westchester. **Coordinate reconciliation:** three pairs circulated in the pool, 41.0862/-73.8646 (geography lens), 41.0906/-73.8629 (afterlife lens), and the cemetery article's own 41°5'48"N 73°51'41"W. The file uses **41.0967, -73.8614**. `max_warburg.journey.json` uses a fourth pair, 41.0954/-73.8603: **the two files disagree by roughly 150 m and the operator may want them reconciled.** The dossier's "other prominent New York banking families" is also loose; the other major family presence there is the **Rockefellers**, whose money is Standard Oil.

## 9. Honest gaps

- **The primary record is largely unread** (see the table below for why): not the 1907 New York Times article, so the Medici sentence is second-hand; not the Senate confirmation record, so Bristow's questioning rests on the dossier and Time's retrospective; not the Yale Warburg Papers, MS 535, whose call number, extent and dates stay unconfirmed. *(The pool calls their user "Ron Lowenstein"; *America's Bank*, 2015, is by **Roger** Lowenstein.)* Encyclopedia.com's page, sole support for the 1893 world tour and the Advisory Council service, went to a 404 during the work itself.
- **No translated edition of any Warburg title could be confirmed**, in any language including German. For a Hamburg-born native German speaker who published his major work only in English, that absence is worth noting rather than glossing.
- **No specific address survives** for the Realgymnasium, Simon Hauer, Samuel Montagu's office, the Paris agency, the American Acceptance Council, the International Acceptance Bank, the wedding venue, the Kansas City hall, the Pasadena residence, or Warburg's own New York home. Nine stops sit on city-centre or district placeholders and say so.
- **"Daddy Warbucks."** The claim that Harold Gray modeled the character on Warburg is **unsupported**: the character's own Wikipedia article is silent on it and on any biographical inspiration. Not in the journey file.
