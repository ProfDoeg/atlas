# Betty Shannon (Mary Elizabeth Moore Shannon), 1922-2017: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-05 for the Atlas of Journeys. QUEUE.md line 113 queues her as the direct pair to `claude_shannon`; census rows 324-325 list the two adjacent.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in the same sentence. **[R]** reconstruction or inference beyond what any source states. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; inaccessible sources are named with the reason.

**Standing caution.** Everything known about Betty Shannon is known because of her husband. Every substantial source consulted is a source *about Claude Shannon* in which she appears: an obituary of him, a biography of him, a profile by his biographers. There is no monograph, no finding aid, no memoir of her own. The one document unambiguously hers, the 1949 memorandum signed Mary E. Shannon, is cited by everyone and read by nobody in this pass. That asymmetry is the record, not a gap in a corner of it.

---

## 1. New York and New Brunswick, 1922 to the early 1940s

- Born **Mary Elizabeth Moore, 14 April 1922, New York City**, to Vilma Ujlaky Moore and James E. Moore. **[A: Wikipedia, "Betty Shannon", which breaks out no citation for the date]** Borough and hospital: **not recorded**; the pin uses the general Manhattan coordinate. **[Gap]**
- Attends the **New Jersey College for Women** (later Douglass, Rutgers) **on a full scholarship**; graduates **Phi Beta Kappa in mathematics**. **[A: Wikipedia; Scientific American, Soni and Goodman, 24 July 2017]**
- **No source gives a matriculation or graduation year.** The journey's 1940 and 1944 are placeholders. **[R]**

## 2. Bell Telephone Laboratories: the human computer, early 1940s to 1948

- Joins **Bell Telephone Laboratories as a "computer"** in the mathematics department, one of the women performing calculation by hand for the engineers; works as a **numerical analyst on microwaves**, then **transfers to the radar research group**. **[A: Wikipedia; Scientific American]**
- **Site contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** The first pass placed this work, and the 1948 meeting, at **Murray Hill**. Against that: Wikipedia's Bell Labs article says headquarters was "officially relocated to Murray Hill" only in **1967**, and Scientific American has the couple in 1948 splitting time between "his West Village apartment and hers on East Eighteenth Street," which only works with a Manhattan workplace. Mathematics research sat at **463 West Street**. **[A for the 1967 date and the two apartments; R for the inference that all her 1940s work sat there]** Complicating it, the radar group is at **Whippany**, neither site, and gets its own stop. And `claude_shannon.journey.json` pins the 1949 marriage at Murray Hill while admitting the marker "follows her workplace, not a document": inherited unchanged for interlock integrity, contradiction stated rather than silently corrected.
- **Alan Turing spent about two months at 463 West Street in early 1943**, taking tea with Claude Shannon in the cafeteria. **[A: `claude_shannon.journey.json`]** Six years before the marriage, attested for Claude alone; no source places Betty there. **[Gap]**
- **The cryptography question.** No source locates Betty Moore in a cipher unit distinct from the numerical-analysis and radar computing described by Wikipedia, MacTutor, Scientific American and MIT News. Her own account of the courtship is about music. Any claim of cipher work is **[R]** and unsupported. QUEUE.md line 237 threads Shannon into the atlas cipher wing beside al-Kindi, Trithemius, Diffie and Hellman: **Betty inherits that lineage by pairing, not by attested work.** Those four names were removed from the campa text on the house-style pass of 2026-08-14: no documented relation to Betty herself, only her husband's committee membership.

## 3. The courtship and the marriage, 1948 to 1949

- **Meets Claude Shannon at Bell Labs in the fall of 1948** by one account **[A: Scientific American]**; Wikipedia says the first date was 1948 and that they dated nightly after **[A]**. She keeps an apartment **on East Eighteenth Street**; he keeps rooms in the **West Village**. **[A: Scientific American]**
- On the music: *"I played piano and he played clarinet, and we'd come home from work, and we found some books of music that had two parts, and we'd enjoy playing together."* **[A: Betty Shannon in Scientific American]**
- **Marriage: two published dates, contradiction flagged.** **27 March 1949** per MacTutor **[A]**; **22 March 1949** per Scientific American **[A]**. Both say 1949, they differ by five days, neither shows its working, and no marriage record was reachable. The journey carries **1949-03-27** to match the existing Shannon pin and names the disagreement in `date_confidence`. **No adjudication.**
- On the proposal: *"not very formal."* **[A: Betty Shannon in Scientific American]** **The wedding venue is unrecorded in every source consulted.** **[Gap]**
- **Christmas 1949: she gives him a unicycle.** *"He had been a gymnast in college, so he was better at it than you might have thought."* **[A: Betty Shannon in Waldrop, MIT Technology Review, 2001]** The house is not named. **[Gap]**

## 4. The collaboration, 1949 to the mid-1950s

- **15 November 1949: John R. Pierce and Mary E. Shannon co-author BTL Technical Memorandum MM-49-150-29, "Composing Music by a Stochastic Process."** **[A: memorandum cited in Wikipedia]** **The memorandum was not read in this pass**; every statement about it here comes through citation. **[Gap]**
- Yu and Varshney call it **"exceptional"** and the co-authorship a **"significant and unusual achievement for a woman to get her name on a research report."** **[A: IEEE Information Theory Society Newsletter, December 2017, as quoted in Wikipedia]** The newsletter was reached only through that quotation. **[Gap]**
- **c. 1950: Theseus, the maze-solving mouse.** Betty helped design and build it **[A: Wikipedia]**; Scientific American is specific: *"it was Betty, not Claude, who completed the wiring for Theseus the mouse."* **[A: Soni and Goodman]**
  - **Countervailing note, kept:** Shannon's own 1952 paper does not name her as co-builder. **[A on the absence; R that the credit is therefore weaker]** Against that, the Scientific American piece is by the biographers working from family material, stronger than an ordinary retrospective without being contemporary.
- **Her hand in the manuscripts.** *"Some of his early papers and even later papers are in my handwriting...and not in his, which confused people at first."* **[A: Betty Shannon in Scientific American]** Wikipedia's "main research collaborator" is a characterization, not a joint credit: **[A]** for its existence, **[R]** for any co-authorship read into it.
- On his habit, framing herself as the exception: he *"wouldn't go out of his way to collaborate with other people."* **[A: Betty Shannon in Scientific American]**
- **Early 1950s: parlor experiments on the redundancy of English**, with Claude, Bernard Oliver and Oliver's wife: one supplies opening letters, the others guess the continuation. **[A: IEEE Spectrum]** The first pass pinned this at a fabricated midpoint in Union Township, at no address associated with anyone in the story; the journey moves it to the Murray Hill pin. **[R as to location]**
- **May 1951: Claude serves on the Special Cryptologic Advisory Group in Washington.** **[A: `claude_shannon.journey.json`]** **No source places Betty on that trip.** The journey gives it a stop only to state the absence. **[Gap]**

## 5. The children

- **Three per Wikipedia and MIT News: Robert James, Andrew Moore, Margarita ("Peggy").** **[A: Wikipedia; MIT News obituary]** **Four per MacTutor: "Robert, James, Andrew Moore, and Margarita."** **[A: MacTutor]** MacTutor appears to split "Robert James" into two children. **Flagged, not resolved:** three sources say three, one says four, and the four-name list contains no name absent from the three-name list.
- **Robert James Shannon dies in 1998 at 45.** **[A: Wikipedia]** That puts his birth in **1952 or 1953**, in New Jersey or New York, before the 1956 move. **[R: arithmetic from an attested death age, not a stated birth date]** Genealogy aggregations giving "born 1952 in Massachusetts" are wrong on the state and are not used.
- Birth dates for Andrew and Margarita: **not recorded.** **[Gap]** They get no stops.

## 6. Winchester: Entropy House, 1956 to 1978

- **1956: Claude joins MIT as visiting professor; the family settles on Mystic Lake in Winchester** **[A: MIT News]**, in the house called **Entropy House**, occupied more than thirty years **[A: IEEE Spectrum]**. The three children are raised there. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Summer 1961: the Las Vegas casino test of the wearable roulette computer.** The first pass tagged Betty's presence as an unconfirmed gap. **Thorp's own account refutes it: the device was operational in June 1961 and "in the summer of 1961 the Shannons and Thorps met in Las Vegas for a casino test," both wives taking part.** **[A: Thorp]** **Interlock friction, flagged:** `claude_shannon.journey.json` places the roulette work in the **Winchester basement** and the interlock lens records Betty's role in the device itself as unstated, so the journey keeps the two apart: **the building of the machine, where her role is [R]**, and **the casino test, where her presence is [A] on Thorp.**
- **The workshop.** Claude keeps building: a talking chess machine, a motorized pogo stick, a penny-matching "mind-reading" device, a Hex machine, a juggling W. C. Fields figure. **[A: IEEE Spectrum]** Betty's household is the base of operations. **[R as framing; the gadgets are attested]**
- **Investing.** Her daughter: *"Their work in the stock market was completely a team effort...It was always a joint project."* **[A: Margarita "Peggy" Shannon in Scientific American]** Testimony **about** Betty, not **by** her. The investing positions the family for early Silicon Valley stakes. **[A]**
- **An overreach corrected out.** The first pass had Betty "dropping in on" seminars of Claude's MIT circle. **No source says this**; IEEE Spectrum places her in home-based experiments, not on campus. Dropped. **[Gap]** Separately, his endowed chair is the **Donner Professorship, 1958 to 1978**, not a 1956 appointment. **[A: MIT News]**
- **1969 at MIT: Peter Elias hands Martin Hellman Shannon's 1949 "Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems."** **[A: `claude_shannon.journey.json`; `hellman.journey.json`]** The paper is from the first year of the marriage, the period in which, by her account, his papers came out in her handwriting. **Whether her hand touched that one is not stated by any source.** **[R]**
- **Weaving.** Member of the **Weavers' Guild of Boston**, **Dean 1976 to 1978**, recipient of its **Distinguished Achievement Award**. **[A: Wikipedia]** **The award is undated:** the first pass invented "c. 1978"; the journey places it at 1978 and discloses the invention in `date_confidence`. **[Gap]** No source connects the weaving to the mathematics. **[A on the absence]**
- **1978: Claude retires from the MIT faculty.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Claude Shannon"]**

## 7. The decline and the deaths, 1993 to 2001

- **By mid-1993 the family confirms that Claude Shannon has Alzheimer's disease; later that year they place him in the Courtyard Nursing Care Center, Medford.** **[A: Waldrop, MIT Technology Review, 2001; facility named by MIT News]** The first pass left this undated and called Betty a "caregiver-adjacent survivor"; the same source shows her **actively caretaking and representing him publicly**, and the phrase is struck.
- On his late work: *"He still built things! One was a...figure of W. C. Fields that bounced three balls on a drumhead."* **[A: Betty Shannon in MIT Technology Review]**
- **Gaylord, Michigan: two commemorations, contradiction flagged.** Waldrop says that **in 1998** his hometown marked fifty years of information theory by unveiling a bust in a city park and that **Betty Shannon thanked the town in his stead** **[A]**; no month is given, and the first pass's "1998-09" is dropped. But the documented Gaylord monument is a **bronze by Eugene Daub dedicated 6 October 2000** **[A: HMDB; University of Michigan ECE; WCMU]**. Two events, or one misremembered, possibly conflated with the 1998 Lucent symposium. **Not adjudicated.** The journey keeps 1998, since Betty's presence is attested only in that account.
- **1998: Robert James Shannon dies at 45.** **[A: Wikipedia]** No cause, place, or date. **[Gap]**
- **24 February 2001: Claude Shannon dies at 84** at Medford. **[A: MIT News]** The obituary lists Betty first among survivors, with Andrew Moore of Brookline, Margarita (Peggy) of Cambridge, a sister, two granddaughters. **[A]** On the aftermath: *"The response to his death has been overwhelming. I think it would have astounded him."* **[A: Betty Shannon in MIT Technology Review]**
- **Burial: a disagreement inside the atlas itself, flagged.** MIT News states "Services and burial are private." **[A]** The first pass could not confirm a cemetery and surfaced Wildwood Cemetery and the Winchester Cemetery Company as candidates; **those are struck, since no source places either Shannon there.** The corrected finding: **both Shannons are buried at Mount Auburn Cemetery, Cambridge, his headstone carrying the entropy formula** **[A: Mount Auburn burial listing; Find a Grave, HTTP 403 on fetch, reached through corroborating published accounts]**. **However**, `claude_shannon.journey.json` titles its stop "the grave that is claimed and not confirmed." **The two atlas documents disagree**, and this report does not resolve it: the journey uses the canonical pin and says in `date_confidence` that the claim rests on aggregators, not on a cemetery record read directly.

## 8. Lexington, death, and reassessment, 2001 to 2017

- Widowed, she lives at **Brookhaven, a senior living community in Lexington.** **[A: Wikipedia]** **The date of the move is not recorded**; the first pass's "2001-2017" span is an inference. **[R]**
- **1 May 2017: Mary Elizabeth Moore Shannon dies at Brookhaven, age 95.** **[A: Wikipedia; Boston Globe obituary as cited there]** The Globe and Legacy.com obituaries were reached **only as search-engine snippets**. **[Gap in direct access]**
- **18 July 2017: Soni and Goodman publish "A Mind at Play"; six days later the same authors publish "Betty Shannon, Unsung Mathematical Genius" in Scientific American.** **[A]** The article is **not an independent reassessment**; it is the book's Betty material in a magazine. The first pass called the book's treatment of her unresolved on the strength of a Wikipedia plot summary; **that reading is refuted** by the companion article, which quotes her on the duets, the handwriting and the Theseus wiring. **[A]** Both appear within eleven weeks of her death; whether she saw either is **not recorded**. **[Gap]**
- **December 2017: Yu and Varshney publish a retrospective on her in the IEEE Information Theory Society Newsletter.** **[A: via Wikipedia]**
- **The 2019 documentary "The Bit Player"** contains no documented Betty content per its Wikipedia summary. **[A on the summary; R about the film, which was not viewed]**
- **Commemoration, narrowed.** A grave marker for her exists, in the family plot at Mount Auburn. Absent is **any monument or commemorative object naming her independently of her husband.** **[A on the absence across sources checked; not an exhaustive survey]**
- **Translation history.** No Spanish edition of any Betty-authored or co-authored technical work was located. **[R: a negative finding limited by an exhausted search quota, not a researched conclusion]**
