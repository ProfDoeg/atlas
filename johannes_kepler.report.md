# Johannes Kepler (1571-1630) - research report

*Colegio Invisible, Atlas of Journeys. 2026-08-23, against the verified research pool for slug `johannes_kepler`. Companion: `johannes_kepler.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or aggregator trace · **[C]** correction applied to the dossier · **[G]** gap, stated and not filled.

Geography: Weil der Stadt, Leonberg, Adelberg, Maulbronn, Tübingen, Graz, Benátky, Prague, Linz, Leonberg and Güglingen again, Ulm, Sagan, Regensburg. None of the four canonical world-pins appears in his life **[A, absence check]**.

## 1. Childhood, 1571-1584

- Born Weil der Stadt 27 December 1571, about 2:30 p.m. **[A, Britannica; MacTutor]**; the hour is from Kepler's own retrospective horoscope of himself, not a register **[A, en.wikipedia]**. Premature and weak; parents Heinrich Kepler, mercenary, and Katharina Guldenmann. Leonberg from 1576, German then Latin school **[A, MacTutor]**.
- **Calendar [A/G].** The birth date is Julian. The file is set `"calendar": "gregorian"` because the bulk of the life postdates 1582; the Julian status sits in that stop's `date_confidence`, unconverted, as in the pool.
- **Comet of 1577 [A, en/de.wikipedia].** At about six, taken by his mother to high ground; he recalled it in writing. Year firm; the file's November comes from the known apparition, not from a source dating his viewing **[R on the month]**.
- **Eclipse of 1580 [A on the event; C on the parent].** The dossier had his *father* call him outdoors. Both de.wikipedia ("Keplers Mutter weckte schon früh sein Interesse für Astronomie") and en.wikipedia give the **mother** for both childhood sky events, and the same woman is tried for witchcraft forty years later. **[G]** No source dates the eclipse to a day.

## 2. The Württemberg scholarship ladder, 1584-1594

- Adelberg 1584 **[A, MacTutor]**; Maulbronn 1586 **[A, Britannica]**; Tübinger Stift September 1589 for the ministry, Magister Artium 1591 **[A, MacTutor; Britannica]**. Distinction alongside illness and loneliness is attested in general terms; the campa's beatings and fevers are characterisation from that, **[R]** as to detail.
- **Mästlin teaches Ptolemy publicly and Copernicus privately [A, MacTutor; en.wikipedia].** The hinge of the life, attested rather than reconstructed.
- **The blocked lunar disputation, c. 1593 [R].** An exercise describing the heavens as seen from the moon, said to have been refused by Veit Müller and floated as the seed of *Somnium*. The **only** source is the Wikipedia *Somnium* article itself. Kept, status in `date_confidence`.
- **[G]** Kepler's Eucharistic dissent, the refusal to sign the Formula of Concord that cost him communion at Linz and any Württemberg chair, is standard biography but appears **nowhere in the pool**, so it is in neither artifact. It is the best explanation of why a man who wanted a pulpit never got one.

## 3. Graz, 1594-1600

- Nominated to the Graz Stiftsschule before finishing theology, accepts reluctantly, arrives April 1594; also provincial mathematician, carrying calendars and prognostications **[A, MacTutor; en.wikipedia]**. On **19 July 1595**, drawing a class figure, he conceives the five-solids model, attested to the day from his own account **[A, en.wikipedia]**.
- *Mysterium Cosmographicum* 1596 **[A, MacTutor]**, the first substantial published defence of Copernicanism by an astronomer after Copernicus. The pool gives "Tübingen, printed for Graz"; the stop is pinned at **Graz**, where he held the post and did the work. **[G]** Whether he travelled to supervise the press was not established, so there is no Tübingen stop. Second edition 1621 **[A; C]**: the dossier said "nearly twice the length", the source says "half as long again".
- Marries Barbara Müller von Mühleck 27 April 1597, her father objecting to his poverty **[A]**; residence Schloss Mühlegg, Gössendorf **[A, de.wikipedia]**. **[G]** Coordinates are the municipality to two decimals, the castle not separately located.
- Ferdinand's Counter-Reformation edict September 1598, brief exemption for his usefulness, then the post lost; leaves permanently 30 September 1600 rather than convert **[A]**.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The dossier's chronology lists the Graz departure (1600-09-30) *before* the Benátky arrival (1600-02-04), incoherent as written. The file adopts the standard reconstruction and says so: Benátky in February, return to Graz, expulsion in autumn. **No pool source states the return trip**; it is inferred from two attested dates, tagged **[R]** in `date_confidence`.

## 4. Prague under Rudolf II, 1600-1612

- Benátky nad Jizerou 4 February 1600 **[A, en.wikipedia; MacTutor]**. Tycho held twenty years of data; Kepler wanted the Mars observations, autonomy, and pay; Tycho was reluctant on all three.
- **Appointment [A; C].** The dossier gives 1601-10-24 twice. **That is Tycho's death date.** en.wikipedia: "two days after Tycho's death, Kepler was appointed his successor as imperial mathematician." Corrected to **26 October 1601**. Ownership of the observations stayed disputed with the heirs.
- *De fundamentis astrologiae certioribus* 1601 **[A on existence; R on the quotation, §8]**. *Astronomiae pars optica* 1604 **[A, MacTutor; C on place]**: the dossier said Prague, MacTutor lists "Frankfurt, 1604". Written at Prague, printed at Frankfurt; the stop is pinned at Prague and says so.
- SN 1604 observed October 1604; *De stella nova* 1606, used against Aristotelian incorruptibility **[A]**. *Astronomia nova* 1609, first two laws from Mars, **printed at Heidelberg, work done at Prague [A]**. **[G]** No source says whether he travelled to Heidelberg, so there is no Heidelberg stop.
- *Dissertatio cum Nuncio sidereo* 1610, publicly backing Galileo without a comparable instrument **[A, Stanford]**. Friendship with Wackher von Wackenfels c. 1601-1611 **[A]**; that he personally brought the Jupiter news fits it but is **[R]**. *Dioptrice* 1611 **[R, Stanford; the pool tags this R]**. Rudolf II dies January 1612; Matthias keeps the title but permits removal to Linz **[A]**.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: the deaths of 1611.** The dossier conflated two deaths under one date **[C]**:

| | dossier | corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Friedrich, 6, smallpox | folded into 1611-07-03 | **January 1611** (de.wikipedia) |
| Barbara | 1611-07-03, "shortly after" | **3 July 1611** (en.wikipedia) **or 3 August 1611** (Stanford) |

About five months separate them, not "shortly after". **The July/August conflict is left open**: both dates are in `date_confidence`, July orders the stop, nothing here adjudicates.

## 5. Linz, 1612-1626

- **Relocation date [C].** Dossier geography says **August 1612**; de.wikipedia says **April 1612**; MacTutor puts the acceptance late 1611; Stanford gives bare "1612". **No reached source supports August.** The file uses 1612-04-01 and states the spread.
- **Canonical pins.** Linz **48.3069, 14.2858** from `robert_fludd`; Prague Castle **50.0909, 14.4016** and general Prague **50.0875, 14.4213** from `john_dee`, `edward_kelley`, `giordano_bruno`, all byte-identical per the lens. The dossier's own Linz pin (48.3058, 14.2864) is overridden.
- **Calendar reform [R].** The dossier places him at Regensburg in 1613 for imperial calendar deliberations, backing the Gregorian calendar astronomically while refusing it on papal authority. The visit could not be confirmed, so **no Regensburg 1613 stop was made**; the substance folds into the Linz-arrival campa.
- Marries Susanna Reuttinger 30 October 1613, fifth of eleven candidates in his own written survey, the day chosen to fall on a lunar eclipse **[A]**. *Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum* 1615 **[A, MacTutor]**; the gauger-at-the-wedding origin is reported rather than documented **[R]**.
- **Third law, 15 May 1618 [A, en.wikipedia; Stanford; Kepler's own narration in *Harmonice Mundi* V].** Conceived 8 March, miscalculated, rejected as false, returned 15 May. Attested to the day by the subject.
- *Harmonices mundi libri V* 1619 **[A, MacTutor]**: third law in Book V, attack on Fludd's monochord cosmology in an appendix. **Dating strain flagged:** Book V narrates a discovery it dates to 1618 and the pool cites the work as both years; its Wikiquote correction resolves publication as **1619**. Both are in `date_confidence`.
- *Epitome astronomiae Copernicanae*, installments 1618-1621 **[A, MacTutor]**, placed on the Index **[R: the pool sources this to a dossier synthesis cross-checked against MacTutor, not a decree]**. **[G]** No decree or Index edition reached; 1619 orders the stop and nothing more.

## 6. The trial of Katharina Kepler, 1615-1622

- Ursula Reinbold accuses Katharina at Leonberg of causing chronic illness with a bitter drink, 1615; Kepler removes his mother to Linz, December 1616 **[A, en.wikipedia 'Katharina Kepler']**.
- Arrest and transport to Güglingen 7 August 1620 **[A]**. **[G]** de.wikipedia's Güglingen article did **not** itself carry the detention; it reaches the pool via the Leonberg article. Coordinates are low-precision (49.0667, 9.0) in the source and carried as given.
- **Territio verbalis [A; C on the date].** Dossier geography said September 1621; both en.wikipedia ("court hearing: 20 August 1621") and de.wikipedia ("Folterandrohung: 20. August 1621") give **20 August 1621**, with release in **October 1621**, about six weeks later, not "the following month".
- Released after roughly 405 days of pretrial detention, acquitted; the case had cost her son over 900 gulden. Dies at Roßwälden 13 April 1622, about 74, never convicted or sentenced **[A]**.
- **[R]** The campa's "dozens of counts" generalises the dossier; the often-quoted 49 counts is **not** in the pool and is not stated. **[R]** The *Somnium*-circulation tradition keeps the pool's own qualification: **no trial record names *Somnium* as the case's initiating cause**, so it appears nowhere in the journey file and only here.

## 7. Ulm, Sagan, Regensburg, 1626-1630

- Siege and fire of Linz June 1626: press and type for the Tables destroyed, manuscript surviving. Ulm from November 1626, printing with Jonas Saur **[A]**; **[G]** his shop address unconfirmed, old-town coordinates used. *Tabulae Rudolphinae* printed September 1627, one thousand copies **[A]**.
- **The arrears [C].** The dossier put ~12,000 gulden at the 1628 Prague visit. en.wikipedia gives the printing-period figure as **6,299 florins owed by the Imperial Court, of which Kepler recovered 2,000**, paying the rest himself; the **~12,000 gulden** is the arrears at his **death in 1630**, which is what took him to the Regensburg diet. Both are now in their correct stops.
- Sagan (Żagań) under Wallenstein's patronage from 20 July 1628 **[A]**. **[C]** The dossier called Jacob Bartsch a son-in-law in 1629; he married Kepler's daughter Susanna in **March 1630**. The Sagan press and the 200-plus notes to *Somnium* are otherwise sound.
- Rides for Regensburg 8 October 1630 **[R, Stanford; the pool tags this R]**. Dies 15 November 1630 after a fever developed on the journey, no contemporary diagnosis **[A, Britannica]**.
- **Death house [A on the museum; C on coordinates; R/G on the identification].** Keplergedächtnishaus opened 1961 **[A, de.wikipedia]**. Coordinates corrected from 49.0195/12.0989 to **49.0211, 12.0938**, a ~450 m fix. The dossier's attribution of the identification to Carl Woldemar Neumann is **not** on that page and has been **dropped**.
- **Burial and lost grave [A; C].** Buried 19 November 1630 in the Petersfriedhof outside the walls **[A, de.wikipedia]**, which dates the razing to **1632-1633**, not specifically October 1633. The stop's coordinates (49.0134, 12.1016) are **labelled a city-centre placeholder**: the site is unrecoverable, which is the point of the stop.
- Monuments **[A]**: d'Herigoyen's at Regensburg 1806-1808; Kreling's bronze at Weil der Stadt, 1870. **[C]** The dossier's coordinates for the latter (48.7539, 8.8747) sit 600-700 m off; the correct point is the birthplace pin. *Somnium* published posthumously 1634 at Frankfurt **[A]**, carried as a `suggested_ref` on the Sagan stop, not staged as a scene.

## 8. The quotations

Six used, four of them corrected.

- **[A]** *Harmonice Mundi* V.3, the 15 May 1618 discovery narrative, trans. Aiton, Duncan & Field (1997) p. 411, on MacTutor with the translation named; truncated at a sentence boundary.
- **[R; C]** Longomontanus dedication line: three words restored, "arrived **just at the time**". *Gesammelte Werke* III via Voelkel (1999) p. 66 via Wikiquote; Voelkel not opened.
- **[R; C]** "Discover the force of the heavens O Men…": the dossier attributed it to *both* *Mysterium Cosmographicum* (1596) and *De fundamentis* (1601). **The Mysterium attribution is unsupported and dropped**: Wikiquote's raw wikitext carries it once, only under *De fundamentis*, capital O in "Once recognised", printed here in full. No named translator; the cited pages are the whole treatise.
- **[C]** Bryant's gravity sentence: the dossier's "…towards union." invents a sentence boundary. Verified in the full text and printed entire.
- **[R; C twice]** "Geometry is one and eternal…": verbatim in Wikiquote raw wikitext, cited to Field (1987) p. 154. **Book/chapter dropped** (Wikiquote says Book III, but that is the music-theory book, the metaphysics is Book IV) and **the year is 1619, not 1618**. Field not opened; the Latin never located.
- **[A as seen; R as to edition]** Bernegger letter (March 1629), the Susanna line, the eclipse-wedding line. **[C]** The pool carried a Wikipedia typo ("whick"); the file prints "which".

**Dropped.** "If the earth should cease to attract its waters, they would flow to the moon" is a compression existing in no source: Bryant reads "…to attract its waters **to itself** all the waters of the sea would be raised and would flow to **the body of** the moon". Two further lines, on the ellipse and on geometry co-eternal with the divine mind, are Wikiquote aggregations never checked against a primary translation.

## 9. Interlocks

**Named in a campa, 2 slugs**, both resting on an act of Kepler's own inside his own lifetime.

- **`robert_fludd`.** Kepler attacks Fludd's monochord cosmology in the 1619 *Harmonices mundi* appendix from Linz; Fludd replies from Frankfurt in 1621; Kepler answers again in 1622 **[A, `robert_fludd.journey.json`]**. Fludd's file already dramatizes the exchange with Kepler as antagonist, so Kepler's file does **not** duplicate that scene: it carries his own Linz act and points at Fludd's file.
- **`giovanni_pico_della_mirandola`.** Pico's *Disputationes adversus astrologiam divinatricem* (begun 1493) is a text "Kepler is still answering a century on" **[A, asserted in Pico's file on the Stanford Encyclopedia's authority]**. A one-directional text-to-text relation of the kind the house rule allows, on the 1601 *De fundamentis* stop, at Kepler's own Prague pin.

**Named, not a corpus slug.** Galileo Galilei, in the 1610 *Dissertatio* campa: a direct relation and Kepler's own act, but `galileo_galilei` is queued with no file, so it is not listed as an interlock. When that file exists, the *Dissertatio* stop anchors it from Kepler's side.

**Not named, shared geography only.** `john_dee`, `edward_kelley`, `giordano_bruno`: Rudolf II court continuity with no personal contact (Dee expelled 1586, Kelley dead 1597, Bruno burned February 1600, all before Kepler reached Prague). The map already shows co-location, so none appears in any campa; their canonical pins **are** inherited, which is how the corpus registers the shared court. Same for `judah_loew_ben_bezalel` (queued, no file): in Prague for Kepler's first eight years there, no source that they interacted **[R/G]**.

## 10. Apparatus relations for the operator

Kepler is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so the relations below are **not written into the journey file in any form, not even `suggested_refs`**. They are recorded rather than silently deleted, so the operator can decide whether to add him. **All four are forward, not backward**, the direction `EXCEPTIONS.md` says is never licensable by an agent's judgement.

1. **`kircher`.** Summoned to Vienna in November 1633 specifically to succeed the dead Kepler as court mathematician; a storm blows his ship to Italy and he never takes the post **[A, `kircher.journey.json`]**. It is the hinge of Kircher's own scene, where it already sits.
2. **`edmond_halley`, twice.** He presents the inverse-square derivation he got "out of Kepler's third law" to the Royal Society on 24 January 1684, and frames his 1716 transit-of-Venus paper against Kepler's unsolved problem of the solar system's size **[A, `edmond_halley.journey.json`]**.
3. **`wolfgang_pauli`.** Pauli's Eranos lecture at Ascona, c. 1948, argues an inherited trinitarian archetype shaped Kepler's astronomy **[A, `wolfgang_pauli.journey.json`; *Naturerklärung und Psyche*, 1952]**.
4. **The Kepler/Fludd/Pauli/Jung node.** Rascher Verlag's 1952 *Naturerklärung und Psyche* binds Pauli's Kepler essay to Jung's synchronicity essay and casts Fludd as antagonist of Pauli's half **[A, from both files]**. It belongs on those two files.

A 1630 block cannot embed a 1633, 1684, or 1948 hash. If the operator wants any of it, Kepler needs a **forward grant** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, which is the author's call.

Posthumous material *about his own works* is a different case and **is** carried, in `suggested_refs` and a clause or two of campa, on the model of `abdelkader.journey.json`'s closing stop: the 1634 *Somnium*, Rosen's 1967 translation, Donahue's 1992 *New Astronomy*, the monuments. That is the reception of his own objects, not another traveller's scene.

**Attested but unused.** The Kepler conjecture, stated in *Strena seu de nive sexangula* (1611), unproven until Hales's 1998 proof, Flyspeck-verified and accepted 2017; and NASA's Kepler telescope, decommissioned 15 November 2018, the 388th anniversary of his death, a coincidence that is the verifier's own observation, **[R]** as to intent. The pamphlet has no stop because the count was at its ceiling; a revision should give it a Prague stop with the proof as a `suggested_ref`.
