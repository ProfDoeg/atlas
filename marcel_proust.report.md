# Marcel Proust: research report

*2026-08-02, for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Marcel Proust (1871-1922), French novelist. QUEUE.md row 3. Corpus presence: named in 3 documents (1 essay, 2 Instagram posts), ranked #126 of 523 named real persons in `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, critical tradition, or local legend. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Sources checked and not reached are named with the reason.

Proust is heavily documented, but the documentation is contaminated by the novel: biographical writing about him imports scenes from *A la recherche du temps perdu* as though they were events, and the gathered pool did so in several places. Section 7 itemises those, because they are the substance of the verification pass rather than an appendix to it.

## 1. Auteuil and Malesherbes, 1871-1881

Born 10 July 1871 in the Auteuil house of his great-uncle Louis Weil, to Adrien Proust, physician, and Jeanne Clemence Weil **[A: Wikipedia, "Marcel Proust"; Tadie, *Marcel Proust: A Life*]**. The family had left central Paris for the suburb during the siege; the birth falls two months after the Commune's fall **[A: Tadie]**. Baptized 5 August 1871 at Saint-Louis-d'Antin **[A: Wikipedia]**. Brother Robert born 1873 **[A: proust-ink.com]**. The father is professor of hygiene and inspector-general of sanitary services, architect of the European quarantine cordon **[A: Tadie]**. The mother never converts; the Dreyfus years make that a public fact **[A]**.

First serious asthma attack "around age nine," after which he is sickly for life **[A: Wikipedia]**. The pool gives 1880; that year is back-calculated from the birth, so **[R]** for the precise year. The journey stop is dated 1880-01-01 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

Illiers receives him for Easter and summer at the house of his aunt Elisabeth Amiot, the physical model for aunt Leonie's house at Combray **[A: Musee Marcel Proust; Tadie]**. **Gap:** no source consulted fixes a single Illiers visit to a date.

## 2. Condorcet, Orleans, the degrees, 1882-1895

Lycee Condorcet from age eleven, 1882, schooling broken by illness, strong in literature **[A: Wikipedia]**. Society column in *Le Mensuel*, 1890; co-founds *Le Banquet*, 1892 **[A: Wikipedia]**. One year with the 76th infantry at the Coligny barracks, Orleans, 1889-1890; fellow conscripts remembered an odd, courteous recruit **[A: Wikipedia; Tadie]**.

**Education, corrected.** The pool claimed degrees in philosophy and law taken in 1893 at Sciences Po and the Sorbonne **[claimed A, proust-ink.com]**. French Wikipedia contradicts both: he left the Ecole libre des sciences politiques in 1890 from the diplomatic section *non diplome*, and took the *licence es lettres* in philosophy in **March 1895** **[A: fr.wikipedia.org]**. A *licence en droit* around 1893 stands as **[R]**, standard tradition not independently verified here.

## 3. The salons and the coasts, 1891-1898

Frequents the salons of Mme Straus, Madeleine Lemaire, Mme Arman de Caillavet **[A: Wikipedia]** and the comtesse Greffulhe on the rue d'Astorg **[A: Tadie; Painter]**. Their transposition into the Verdurin and Guermantes salons is attested in general **[A: Wikipedia]**; the assignment of specific traits to specific sitters, Straus's wit and Greffulhe's bearing into the Duchesse, Montesquiou into Charlus, is **[R]**, standard critical reading and not documentary.

Reveillon, summers of 1894 and 1895, with Reynaldo Hahn; the relationship is described in the sources as close and romantic **[A: Wikipedia; Tadie]**. Dieppe, August 1895, a shared house with Hahn **[A: Tadie; Carter]**. Belle-Ile and Beg-Meil, September 1895; at Beg-Meil he drafts *Jean Santeuil* **[A: Tadie]**. First book *Les plaisirs et les jours*, 1896, preface by Anatole France **[A: Wikipedia]**, illustrated by Lemaire **[A: Tadie]**. *Jean Santeuil* abandoned and published posthumously **[A: Wikipedia]**; the 1952 date is **[R]**, not verified in this pass.

**Dreyfus.** The pool's interlock states Proust was a signed Dreyfusard active in Zola's 1898 petition **[R: outside-corpus, so tagged in the pool itself]**. No corpus file connects Proust to the affair. The journey carries one stop at the canonical Paris pin, tagged as reconstruction, whose campa names Lionel Royer, in the atlas drawing Dreyfus in his cell in January 1895 **[A: `lionel_royer.journey.json`]**.

## 4. Ruskin, Italy, the Low Countries, 1899-1906

Evian-les-Bains, September 1899, Hotel Splendide, a cure against asthma and nerves **[A: Tadie]**. Venice with his mother, May 1900, at the Danieli; Saint Mark's with *The Stones of Venice* in hand; an excursion to the Scrovegni chapel at Padua **[A: Tadie; Painter]**. The pool tagged **[A]** the claim that the kitchen maid nicknamed Giotto's Charity "is born directly of this visit." That is critical inference; downgraded to **[R]** here and in the journey.

October 1902, Belgium and Holland with Bertrand de Fenelon, a Ruskinian pilgrimage **[A: fr.wikipedia.org]**. At the Mauritshuis he sees Vermeer's *View of Delft* **[A: Tadie; Painter]**; the patch of yellow wall reappears in Bergotte's death **[A as a fact of the text; the causal link to this visit is [R]]**.

Ruskin translations: *La Bible d'Amiens* 1904, *Sesame et les lys* 1906 with the preface *Sur la lecture* **[A: Wikipedia]**. The pool's "a French he could barely read" is a garble: it was **English** **[A: Wikipedia, "imperfect command of English"; "I don't claim to know English; I claim to know Ruskin"]**. The work was a group effort, sketched by his mother, revised by Proust, revised again by Marie Nordlinger, polished by Proust **[A: Wikipedia]**.

Father dies November 1903; mother dies September 1905; he wears mourning for life **[A: Wikipedia]**. Month attested in both cases, day not established anywhere consulted.

## 5. The cork-lined room, 1906-1919

Moved into 102 boulevard Haussmann on **27 December 1906** **[A: fr.wikipedia.org]**. The pool gave only "1906" and tagged the move **[R]**; the exact date upgrades it to **[A]**.

Cork lining, nocturnal schedule, the self-description as an owl **[A: proust-ink.com; Wikipedia]**. The pool dated the cork to 1910; nothing consulted pins the year, so the journey folds the cork into the arrival stop rather than asserting a date. **Gap stated as a gap.** Sustained work on the novel begins 1908, with the *Contre Sainte-Beuve* fragments feeding in **[A: Wikipedia]**.

Cabourg, the Grand Hotel, every summer 1907-1914 **[A: proust-ink.com chronology]**; the hotel as model for the Grand-Hotel de Balbec **[A: Tadie; Grand Hotel Cabourg institutional history]**. Villa Les Fremonts at Trouville, repeated guest of the Finaly family across the 1890s and 1900s **[A: Tadie]**; not a journey stop, the range being too loose to place.

Agostinelli enters the household in 1913 as secretary and chauffeur, leaves, and dies in an airplane accident in 1914 **[A: Wikipedia for the relationship]**. The reading of the loss into the Albertine volumes is **[R]**, and the pool tags it so.

*Du cote de chez Swann*, Grasset, at the author's expense, November 1913, after rejections by Fasquelle, Ollendorff, and the NRF, where Gide was the reader **[A: Wikipedia; Wikipedia, "In Search of Lost Time"]**.

Rite of Spring, 29 May 1913. Proust's attendance is **[R]**: widely reported tradition, not documented, as the pool's own interlock note concedes. Hedge carried in `date_confidence`.

Police raid on Albert Le Cuziat's male brothel, 11 January 1918, Proust identified as a customer **[A: Wikipedia]**. The folding of the episode into Jupien's establishment is **[R]**.

## 6. Goncourt, the last rooms, death, 1919-1922

Evicted from boulevard Haussmann on the building's sale; 8bis rue Laurent-Pichat May to October 1919 **[A: Tadie]**; then 44 rue Hamelin until his death **[A: Wikipedia; Tadie]**. *A l'ombre des jeunes filles en fleurs*, Gallimard, 1919, Prix Goncourt; *Le Cote de Guermantes* in two parts 1920; *Sodome et Gomorrhe* 1921-1922, the last volume in his lifetime **[A: Wikipedia]**. Death at 44 rue Hamelin, 18 November 1922, aged 51 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** The cause of death is given two ways inside the pool, both cited to the same encyclopedia: "pneumonia and a pulmonary abscess" (chronology) against "pneumonia following untreated bronchitis and asthma" (geography, Saint-Pierre-de-Chaillot entry). The journey gives the first and states in the same campa that sources differ and what the other says. Not adjudicated.

Man Ray's deathbed photograph: **20 November 1922**, at Jean Cocteau's request **[A: fr.wikipedia.org, "le surlendemain de la mort de l'ecrivain, a la demande de Jean Cocteau"]**. The pool dated it 18 November. Funeral and burial: **21 November 1922**, Saint-Pierre-de-Chaillot with military honours, then Pere-Lachaise, division 85, family vault **[A: fr.wikipedia.org]**. The pool gave 22 November in three places and called the funeral four days after the death; corrected to 21 November and three days.

Posthumous volumes: *La Prisonniere* 1923, *Albertine disparue* 1925, *Le Temps retrouve* 1927 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 7. Corrections applied to the gathered pool

Each is a place where the pool asserted as **[A]** something wrong or overstated.

1. **Mazarine.** Pool: appointed June 1896, "never actually worked," 1896-1900. Correct: examination sat **29 June 1895**; he "y fait quelques apparitions pendant les quatre mois qui suivent et demande finalement son conge," so he did appear, repeatedly, for about four months, then took leave **[A: fr.wikipedia.org]**. Range corrected to 1895-1900.
2. **The duel with Jean Lorrain.** Pool: 1897, in Paris. Correct: **6 February 1897, dans les bois de Meudon**, outside Paris **[A: fr.wikipedia.org]**. The journey pin (48.7930, 2.2300) is an approximate centre of the Bois de Meudon; the exact spot is unrecorded anywhere consulted.
3. **The Ruskin translation.** English, not French. See section 4.
4. **The degrees.** See section 2.
5. **The madeleine at Illiers.** The pool stated as **[A]** that "the boy tastes a madeleine dipped in lime-blossom tea" at the aunt's house. That is a scene from the novel presented as biography; in *Contre Sainte-Beuve* the trigger is dry toast. Downgraded to **[R]** and rewritten so the madeleine stays inside the fiction.
6. **Hotel des Reservoirs, Versailles.** Pool: September 1914, a flight of days from German bombardment. Correct: **August to December 1906**, over five months, after leaving rue de Courcelles and while waiting on boulevard Haussmann; he wrote to Rene Peter almost daily. In September 1914 he was at Cabourg. War framing removed.
7. **Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam.** Pool latitude 48.352 lands in rural Champagne; correct value 52.3600. Beyond the typo the venue is doubtful: fr.wikipedia frames the October 1902 trip as a Ruskinian pilgrimage with Fenelon, and the Amsterdam stop is usually tied to the 1902 Rembrandt exhibition. **Stop dropped rather than re-pinned on a guess.**
8. **Scott Moncrieff.** Pool: 1922-1931. Correct: his six volumes ran **1922-1930**; he died in 1930; **Stephen Hudson** translated the seventh as *Time Regained* in 1931 **[A: Wikipedia, "In Search of Lost Time"]**.
9. **Penguin translation.** Pool: a team of seven "led by Lydia Davis," 2002. Correct: general editor **Christopher Prendergast**, seven translators of whom Davis was one, published **1995-2002** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
10. **The Vinteuil quote.** The pool opened "Scarcely had the little pianist begun to play," acknowledging a dash normalisation but hiding two changes: the clause "But to-night, at Mme. Verdurin's," dropped without ellipsis, and "scarcely" silently capitalised. The journey uses the **full sentence** from Project Gutenberg 7178, line 8447, including the source's double hyphen.
11. **The Woolf quote.** Pool: a 1922 diary entry via Tadie p. 611. Correct: **The Letters of Virginia Woolf, 2:525** (Nicolson and Trautmann, Harcourt, 1976-77). A letter, not a diary; the Tadie p. 611 citation belongs to the Gide letter and appears copied onto the Woolf item in error.
12. **"One can say anything."** Wording verbatim on Wikipedia. Citation corrected to **Michael Lucey, "Proust's Queer Metalapses," in *Never Say I* (Duke, 2006), p. 218**, not "Lucey-15." The pool's "circa 1913-1914" is unsupported and has been **dropped, not replaced**: Wikipedia gives no date and frames the remark as a reply to Gide's criticism, an exchange conventionally placed at Gide's 1921 visits.

## 9. Honest gaps

- No day established for the father's death (November 1903) or the mother's (September 1905). Both stops default to the first of the month.
- The year the cork went up at boulevard Haussmann is not fixed by any source consulted. The pool asserted 1910; the journey does not repeat it.
- The exact spot of the Meudon duel is unrecorded.
- The Amsterdam venue for October 1902 is unresolved. Dropped rather than guessed.
- Proust's presence at the Rite of Spring premiere remains tradition.
- The Sur-Proust link in Buenos Aires rests on general literary history: no corpus file, no primary citation checked.
- Belle-Ile-en-Mer (47.3167, -3.15, September 1895) and Villa Les Fremonts, Trouville (49.3667, 0.0833) are attested legs the journey omits for stop-count reasons. Not doubtful, only absent.
- *Jean Santeuil*'s 1952 publication and Hahn's biography beyond the pool are carried as **[R]**, unverified in this pass.
