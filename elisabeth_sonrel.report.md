# Élisabeth Sonrel (1874-1953): research report

*Tours, Paris, Sceaux, Brittany. Symbolist and Art Nouveau painter and illustrator, at the Salon des Artistes Français from 1893 to 1941, forgotten at her death and rediscovered from 1974.*

**Method.** Assembled from a verified research pool built on four reachable bodies of evidence: French Wikipedia's *Élisabeth Sonrel* (whose own load-bearing reference is Renate Treydel's entry in De Gruyter's *Artists of the World*, 2023), the English article, Wikidata Q4428845, and Clara Erskine Clement's *Women in the Fine Arts* (1904), the only period source reached directly. Dates and coordinates that failed an adversarial pass are marked corrected or dropped, with the reason.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including this pass's own. Contradictions are flagged and left standing.

---

## 1. Tours: birth and first training (1874-1892)

- Born **Élisa Marie Stéphanie Adrienne Sonrel, 17 May 1874, at Tours**. **[A: fr.wiki; Wikidata Q4428845]** Her father, **Nicolas Stéphane Sonrel**, a **military physician and amateur painter**, gives her her first lessons. **[A: both encyclopedias]**
- The **location of the father's studio is not recorded**. The pool carried a precise coordinate for it (47.3958, 0.6874) while the claim text itself conceded the address was unknown. That coordinate is an invention, **dropped**; the stop is pinned to the Tours centroid and tagged **[R]**.
- **"L'Hiver" (Winter), 1890.** **[A: fr.wiki gallery list]** Month and day unknown.
- **"Pax et Labor", 1892**, her terminal exam piece, held by the **Musée des Beaux-Arts de Tours**. **[A: both encyclopedias]** **Coordinate corrected**: the museum was pinned at 47.3931, 0.6912, roughly 450 m southwest of the building; 18 place François-Sicard, the former episcopal palace beside the cathedral, is at **47.3950, 0.6944**.

**Gap.** Nothing is recorded of the childhood beyond the date of birth, the father's profession, and the fact of the lessons. No schooling, no siblings, no household detail was reachable.

## 2. Paris: the Académie Julian and the first Salons (1891-1895)

- She studies at the **Académie Julian, in the atelier of Jules Lefebvre**. **[A: fr.wiki]** Verbatim: "Elle part à Paris parfaire sa formation artistique à l'Académie Julian dans l'atelier de Jules Lefebvre."
- **The French Wikipedia prose gives no year for this.** The commonly repeated span **1891-1892 comes from Wikidata Q4428845, where it is attached to a different institution, the École des Beaux-Arts (Q337480), not to the Académie Julian.** **[A: Wikidata entity data]** Splitting an undated period into two dated rows, as an earlier pass did, manufactures a chronology no source contains; the hedge sits in `date_confidence`.
- **Contradiction, and its resolution.** English Wikipedia and Wikidata name the **École des Beaux-Arts**; French Wikipedia names the **Académie Julian**. This is not a standoff to be left open: **women were not admitted to the École des Beaux-Arts until 1897** ("Les femmes y sont progressivement admises... à partir de 1897", fr.wiki, ENSBA), so study there in 1891-1892 is impossible. **The Julian reading wins** **[A: fr.wiki ENSBA]**; the English-language record is in error. Wikidata compounds it by naming as her teacher **Gustave Boulanger (Q433973), dead since 1888.**
- **Tony Robert-Fleury**, named as a second teacher in one lens of the pool, is **unsupported by any cited source**: fr.wiki and en.wiki name only Lefebvre. **Dropped. [R, and rejected]**
- **Salon des Artistes Français, 1893: honorable mention.** **[A: Clement, *Women in the Fine Arts*, 1904]** Verbatim: "SONREL, MLLE. E. Honorable mention, Paris, 1893; third-class medal, 1895; bronze medal, Paris Exposition, 1900."
- **Age corrected**: an earlier pass made her nineteen at this Salon; born 17 May 1874, she was **eighteen** at its May opening. **Venue coordinate corrected**: the Palais de l'Industrie was pinned 380 m west of itself, at 48.8656, 2.3095; it stood at **48.8669, 2.3142**.
- **She begins working professionally in Paris, 1893.** **[A: French Wikipedia]** The street she lives on in these first years is **not named in any reachable source**. Gap.
- **"Âmes errantes" at the Salon, 1894**, and **"Vierge à l'enfant", 1894** **[A: both encyclopedias]**; **third-class medal, 1895** **[A: Clement, 1904]**. No press reception was reachable for any of them.
- **Fabricated source removed.** An earlier pass cited "La Croix, 18 June 1895 (cited in French Wikipedia)". **No such citation exists.** The complete fr.wiki wikitext was read: its only references are Treydel (2023), Bénézit B00172277, bretagne.com, and an unsourced footnote on the 1974 Drouot sale.
- **Sceaux, 1895:** she establishes her own studio, her home until her death. **[A: fr.wiki; Wikidata]**

## 3. Italy and the making of the manner (1890s, undated)

- A journey to **Florence and Rome** exposes her to Quattrocento painting and turns her decisively toward Renaissance idealism. **[A as to fact and effect: both encyclopedias, Style sections. R as to date: no source fixes a year, both place it loosely in the 1890s.]**
- **What she saw in Rome is not specified.** The pool's earlier Rome coordinate, 41.9029, 12.4534, is the **Vatican City centroid**, 3.5 km west of the city centre, silently committing the entry to the Vatican collections its own prose called unspecified. Corrected to **41.9028, 12.4964**.
- The reception category that follows her, "the Pre-Raphaelite of French Symbolism", is a **critical framing** carried by both Style sections **[A as reception]**. The painters she is measured against, **Dante Gabriel Rossetti** and **Edward Burne-Jones**, are the comparison by art-historical convention, not by any document tying her to them **[R]**. No source records Sonrel meeting, corresponding with, or writing about any member of the Brotherhood. These are the only two atlas travellers named in the journey's prose.

## 4. Sceaux and the public career (1895-1905)

- **"Le Sommeil de la Vierge", 1895.** **[A: both encyclopedias]**
- **"Les Rameaux" (Palm Sunday), 1897**, later in the **Ashmolean Museum, Oxford**, accession **WA2020.16**, watercolour on laid paper, 52.7 x 90.8 cm. **[A: Ashmolean]**
- **Contradiction flagged.** fr.wiki's gallery caption gives only the year for *Les Rameaux* and **does not say it hung at the Salon**. The Salon attribution comes from the Ashmolean and Commons records, which in the same breath place the 1897 Salon "at the Grand Palais", a building not open until 1900. The exhibition claim is **not carried**; the work is dated, not placed.
- **Maison Neyret, Saint-Étienne, 1899:** woven **silk pictures** after her designs, four of them held by the **Musée d'Art et d'Industrie de Saint-Étienne**, including *Flore*, black and white on a white ground. **[A: French Wikipedia]** An earlier pass called these textile "sold by the meter"; they are framed woven pictures. Neyret was a Saint-Étienne ribbon and picture firm, not a "Lyon-region silk house". Whether Sonrel travelled there or posted the design is **not stated**. Gap.
- **"Les Esprits de l'abîme" at the Salon, 1899.** **[A: both encyclopedias]**
- **Venue unresolved, and left unresolved.** The 1899 Salon cannot have been at the Palais de l'Industrie: fr.wiki has the Palais serving the Salons "entre 1857 et 1897", and it was demolished in 1896-97 for the 1900 Exposition site. The Société only moved into the Grand Palais from 1901. The 1898-1900 Salons were held elsewhere, most likely the Champ-de-Mars, but **no reachable source names the 1899 venue**, so the stop is pinned to the Paris centroid.
- **Exposition Universelle, 1900: bronze medal for "Le Sommeil de la Vierge".** **[A: both encyclopedias; corroborated by Clement, 1904]**
- **Two awards, kept separate.** fr.wiki: "Lors de l'Exposition universelle de 1900, elle obtient une médaille de bronze pour sa toile Le Sommeil de la Vierge, ainsi que le prix Henri Lehmann de 3000 francs par l'Académie des beaux-arts." The bronze is the Exposition award, tied to that canvas. **The Lehmann prize is an Académie des Beaux-Arts prize, nowhere stated to have been awarded at the Exposition or for that painting.** Earlier passes merged them; they are separated here, and the Lehmann prize gets **no coordinate**, since no source places its award.
- **Clement's list ends at 1900**, recording no distinction after the Exposition bronze. Noted, not adjudicated.
- **"Jeune femme au chaperon" / "Young Woman with a Headdress", 1904.** **[A: en.wiki gallery; Commons record]** **No source records a Salon showing**; an earlier pass invented one, with two unsupported arithmetic claims. Dropped.
- **Featured in *Women Painters of the World*, 1905.** **[A: fr.wiki]** The **place of publication is not given**; the journey pins London as an **[R]** inference and says so.

## 5. Brittany, in two waves (c.1900 and from 1910)

- **From about 1900 she travels regularly to Brittany**, including the forest of **Brocéliande** (Paimpont). **[A: en.wiki]** **First-wave towns, per fr.wiki: Concarneau, Plougastel, Pont-l'Abbé, Loctudy.** **[A]**
- **Second wave, from 1910, is a different list**, verbatim: "à partir de 1910, elle réalise également des vues des villes côtières de Plougastel-Daoulas, Pont-l'Abbé, Le Faouët et Concarneau." **[A: French Wikipedia]** An earlier pass scrambled the two lists. Corrected: **Loctudy belongs to c.1900, Le Faouët to the post-1910 list** (and Le Faouët is inland, despite fr.wiki calling the group coastal).
- **Coordinates corrected.** Plougastel-Daoulas, pinned at 48.3608, -4.3611, about 1.6 km southeast of the bourg, is **48.3733, -4.3694**. Concarneau, pinned 160 m off, is **47.8761, -3.9178**.
- **Unsourced quotation removed.** An earlier pass attributed to Sonrel, "by her own later account passed down through biographers", the view that her Breton sitters were friendly, honest and self-possessed. **This appears in neither Wikipedia edition nor in Clement, with no source given.** Deleted.
- **"Jeune dentellière de Pont-l'Abbé"** is attested **[A: fr.wiki gallery]** but **undated**, so its placement in either wave is **[R]**.
- **"Jeune fille du Cap Sizun"**, watercolour, **undated** **[A as a work: fr.wiki gallery]**. The **Pointe du Van** location is the earlier pass's inference from the toponym, and its coordinate (48.0369, -4.5719) was about 12 km off; the Pointe du Van is **48.0644, -4.7150**. Corrected coordinate used, inference stated as such. **[R]**
- **"Le Goareva": stop dropped.** The earlier pass placed this marine at a hamlet in Cléden-Cap-Sizun (48.045, -4.5967). **There is no Goareva there.** Goaréva is on the Île-de-Bréhat, roughly 250 km northeast; fr.wiki lists the work among her marines **with no location attached**, and nothing places Sonrel on Bréhat. Removed rather than relocated on a guess.
- **Le Faouët** holds a lasting collection of her work at its **musée**. **[A: fr.wiki]**

## 6. The late decades (1916-1941)

- **"Virgin and Child, Between Saint Geneviève and Joan of Arc", 1916.** **[A: en.wiki]**
- **"Notre-Dame du Cerfeuil" / "Our Lady of the Cow Parsley", 1923**, watercolour with bodycolour over pencil, 45 x 38 cm, private collection. **[A: English Wikipedia; Commons record]** **No evidence of a 1923 Salon showing**; the earlier claim is dropped, with its "unbroken Salon record", which is false (below).
- **A villa built at La Baule, in the 1930s.** **[A: French and English Wikipedia; fr.wiki sourced to bretagne.com]** Verbatim: "Dans les années 1930, elle se fait construire une villa à La Baule." **No source fixes a year**; the earlier "1930" is false precision, and `date_confidence` carries the decade.
- **"Missel de Lourdes, dédié aux fidèles serviteurs de Marie", Turnhout, 1932.** **[A: French Wikipedia]** The **publisher is not named in the source**; the attribution to **Brepols** is an inference from the city, plausible but unsourced **[R]**. The journey names the town, not the house. Earlier devotional work: the **"Missel des Saintes Femmes de France"** (Tours, c. 1900), plus postcards, almanacs and periodicals. **[A]**
- **"Vase d'anémones", 1941** **[A: French Wikipedia]**, and the **last recorded Salon participation, 1941**, at 67, in occupied Paris **[A: both encyclopedias, "entre 1893 et 1941"]**.
- **The career span is not a run.** 1893 to 1941 is 48 years, but the **Salon was suspended during the First World War** ("sauf pendant la première guerre mondiale où le Salon a été interrompu", fr.wiki, *Salon des artistes français*). Every earlier phrase describing an unbroken record is **false and reworded to a span**, at 1904, 1923, and 1941 alike.

## 7. Death, burial, and the rediscovery

- **Dies 9 February 1953 at Sceaux, Hauts-de-Seine, aged 78.** **[A: fr.wiki; Wikidata]**
- **Buried in the Cimetière de Sceaux.** **[A: fr.wiki category "Personnalité inhumée au cimetière de Sceaux"]** She is **not** named in that cemetery article's own list of notable burials.
- **Coordinate reconciled.** Two lenses of the pool disagreed: 48.7797, 2.2894 and 48.7798, 2.2838. The cemetery, 174 rue Houdan, is at **48.7797, 2.2838** (Wikidata Q2972561 agrees); the second figure is used.
- **Grave marker: gap.** No description of plot, stone, or condition located; Find a Grave and Google Maps returned HTTP 403. **[R]**
- **The forgetting.** fr.wiki, verbatim: "Malgré son succès toute sa vie, la peintre est rapidement oubliée et redécouverte seulement à partir de 1974." **[A]** The prose names no mechanism; the 1974 date hangs on an **unsourced footnote**.
- **Hôtel Drouot, 25 November 1974:** "Les Esprits de l'abîme" resurfaces and sells high, the event credited with the rediscovery. **[A: fr.wiki, citing the Drouot sale in an unsourced footnote, so weakly attested]**
- **Charlotte Foucher, "Élisabeth Sonrel (1874-1953): une artiste symboliste oubliée", *Bulletin des Amis de Sceaux*, no. 25, 2009.** **[A: fr.wiki bibliography; text not reachable]**
- **Musée international de la parfumerie, *Regards sur les collections*, Grasse, 2018**, Quiquempois (dir.), Silvana Editoriale, pp. 182-183. **[A]**
- **Renate TREYDEL, "Sonrel, Élisabeth", *Artists of the World*, De Gruyter, 2023.** **[A]** Misspelled "Trydel" in one lens of the pool. Not marginal: it is the principal source of the fr.wiki article, so the whole chain rests at base on **one modern reference entry that was not directly read**.
- **Ashmolean holding, attribution corrected.** The Ashmolean's *Les Rameaux* is real, but **must not be sourced to Wikidata**: the Q4428845 entity data carries **no "collection" statement naming the Ashmolean**. The correct citation is the museum's own record. The "13 sitelinks / 69 statements" figures could not be confirmed and are volatile; dropped.

## 8. The Argentina question: an open gap, not a negative

QUEUE.md groups Sonrel with **Eugène Grasset** and **Paul Berthon** as an unresolved-Argentina cluster of French Art Nouveau figures, distinguishing them from Mucha, Klimt, Mackintosh, and Gaudí, whose Argentina links **were** checked and came back negative. **[A: QUEUE.md, lines 878-909]** This pass **could not close the question**:

- No mention of Argentina, Buenos Aires, or any Latin American exhibition, collection, or collector appears in either Wikipedia or in Wikidata Q4428845. **[A as absence within those three]**
- All institutional holdings found are European: Ashmolean, Musée des Beaux-Arts de Tours, Musée d'Art et d'Industrie de Saint-Étienne, musée du Faouët, Frick Art Research Library artist files. **[A]**
- **The session's web-search budget was exhausted** before auction records, South American museum catalogues, or digitized period press (*Caras y Caretas*, for instance) could be searched. **[A as to the tool failure]**

**This is a gap, not a confirmed absence.** If the pattern that held for the four checked Art Nouveau names holds here too, no connection exists, but that is an unverified extrapolation. Two speculative bridges are recorded as speculation only and appear nowhere in the journey: **Victoria Ocampo**, whose own journey file mentions no Sonrel, and **Xul Solar**, QUEUE.md's own name for Argentina's esoteric-Symbolist wing, one generation later, with no documented crossing. **[R, both]**

## 9. Contradictions flagged, not resolved

1. **Institution, teachers, and date of study.** Académie Julian (fr.wiki) vs École des Beaux-Arts (en.wiki, Wikidata); Lefebvre vs Boulanger, dead in 1888, vs the unsourced Robert-Fleury; undated in prose vs 1891-1892 in Wikidata, attached to the wrong institution. Resolved on the admissions evidence, but the divergence stands in `date_confidence`.
2. **Salon venue, 1899.** Palais de l'Industrie demolished 1896-97; Grand Palais not in use by the Société until 1901. Venue unknown.
3. **Exhibition claims for dated works.** *Les Rameaux* (1897), *Jeune femme au chaperon* (1904), *Notre-Dame du Cerfeuil* (1923): dated by gallery captions, **not** attested as Salon exhibits, though earlier drafts asserted showings for all three.
4. **The two awards of 1900**, Exposition bronze vs Académie Lehmann prize, merged in earlier drafts and separated here.
5. **The 1904 painting.** One lens of the pool asserts her best-known 1904 work is **"The Damsel of the Sanct Grael"**, an archaic-spelling Grail title close to Rossetti's 1857 Oxford Union bay "Sir Lancelot's Vision of the Sanc Grael". **No other lens, and no reachable source, names such a painting**; the 1904 work in the record is *Jeune femme au chaperon*. Flagged, uncarried, unresolved.
6. **Cemetery coordinate.** Two figures in the pool, reconciled to the Wikidata-agreeing one.
