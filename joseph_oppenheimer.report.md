## Folds and gaps in the tradition
The trial itself is the great fold: a death sentence that **names no capital crime** — "usurpation of office, fraud, lèse-majesté, high treason" heaped up to justify a verdict fixed in advance by the Estates. Mintzker's four narrators (inquisitor, eyewitness, visiting court-Jew, first biographer) contradict one another on the very facts of his last days, so the "canon" here is deliberately plural. The lechery charge was **dropped to protect noblewomen's honour** — a gap the record itself confesses. The afterlife inverts twice: Feuchtwanger redeems, Harlan weaponizes.

## The five richest episodes
1. **Ludwigsburg, 12 Mar 1737** — the duke dies at 9:30 p.m. and the shield vanishes in an hour; arrest that same night.
2. **The Galgenberg, 4 Feb 1738** — throttled in the iron cage crying the Shema, refusing baptism to the last breath.
3. **The Herrenhaus, 30 Jan–4 Feb** — the relentless conversion pressure, the return to faith, the eve-of-execution Jewish visitor.
4. **Vienna, 1703** — Samuel Oppenheimer's fall, the scapegoat-template laid a generation early.
5. **Berlin, 1940** — Harlan's film disinters the murdered man to serve genocide; the cage's longest shadow.

## Connection to the atlas
A **martyr/scapegoat** node linking to Hypatia (mob-murdered scholar), Joan of Arc (show-trial, burned, faith-to-the-end), and Falconetti/Jesus (the trial-and-execution arc). As a **Court Jew** tragedy it faces its own opening (Samuel Oppenheimer, Vienna) and the wider German-Jewish thread the atlas carries. Geographically it overlaps the Swabian/Rhineland world of the Merovingian and German journeys (Stuttgart, Ludwigsburg, Frankfurt, Mannheim). Its unique contribution is the **afterlife-of-a-slander** coda — a life whose mythologization (Hauff → Feuchtwanger → Harlan) becomes part of the canon itself, the only journey in the atlas whose final stops are the retellings of the earlier ones.
