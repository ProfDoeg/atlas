# Thomas More (1478-1535): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-23 from the verified research pool for slug `thomas_more`. Lawyer, under-sheriff of London, Speaker of the Commons, Lord Chancellor of England, author of Utopia; beheaded on Tower Hill 6 July 1535, canonized 1935.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or later elaboration. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**; sources checked that gave nothing are recorded as gaps. Pre-1582 life, so the journey file is stamped `julian` and dates are Old Style as the sources give them.

---

## 1. Origins and schooling, 1478-1496

- **[A]** Born in the City of London, 7 February 1478, to John More, lawyer of Lincoln's Inn, and Agnes Graunger (thomasmorestudies.org; Wikipedia, 'Thomas More'). **[R]** Milk Street is tradition only; Joanne Paul's archival work (dossier-cited, not reached) treats the birthplace as disputed. **Only London is secure.** The journey pins Milk Street because that is where the tradition puts it, and says so.
- **[A]** St Anthony's School, Threadneedle Street. *Correction:* the point-date 1485 in Wikipedia and Luminarium compresses a range. thomasmorestudies.org gives "c. 1484-1489 Attends St. Anthony's School, London (7-12)"; the journey uses the range.
- **[A]** Page in John Morton's household at Lambeth. *Contradiction, unresolved:* Wikipedia 1490-1492; thomasmorestudies.org "c. 1489-1491 Page for Archbishop and Chancellor Morton (12-14)". Rendered c.1489/1490-1492. **[R]** Morton's prediction over the boy at table is Roper sixty years later, a retrospective motif; used as a quote because it is verbatim Roper, with the confidence field saying so.
- **[A]** Oxford c.1491/1492 to c.1493, under Linacre and Grocyn. *Gap:* Canterbury College or St Mary Hall, unsettled by any reachable source.
- **[A]** New Inn c.1493/1494; admitted to Lincoln's Inn 1496. *Contradiction:* called to the bar 1501 (Luminarium) or 1502 (Wikipedia). Both recorded, neither preferred.
- **Dropped for lack of any source.** The dossier attached "reader 1511; treasurer 1512" to Lincoln's Inn. The Lincoln's Inn Wikipedia article does not mention More at all and thomasmorestudies.org has no such entries. Not asserted in the journey.

## 2. The Charterhouse, the bar, the first marriage, 1501-1511

- **[A]** Public lectures on Augustine's *City of God* at St Lawrence Jewry, 1501 (Stanford Encyclopedia chronology; Roper). No text survives; the gap is stated in the campa.
- **[A/R] The dossier carried the Charterhouse episode twice**, at chronology[8] (1503, [A]) and chronology[40] (1504, [R]): same events, two dates, two tags. **Merged** at c.1501-1504 per thomasmorestudies.org; Wikipedia gives 1503-1504. A second defect in chronology[40] is corrected: its text had him "lectures on and tests a monastic vocation at the London Charterhouse", conflating the Charterhouse with St Lawrence Jewry, where the lecturing happened. **[R]** Hair shirt and scourge come from Erasmus (to von Hutten, 23 July 1519) and Roper, both friends writing for the record; the negative fact stands, he did not take the habit.
- **[A]** Parliament for Great Yarmouth, 1504. **[R]** Leading the opposition to Henry VII's subsidy, and the king's reprisal against his father, is Roper family tradition, disputed by modern scholars, no writ produced. The seat is documented; the episode is not.
- **[A]** Marriage to Jane (Joanna) Colt at Netherhall, Roydon, Essex, 1505. **[R]** The preferred-sister anecdote is Roper-derived and uncheckable. The Netherhall pin was the worst coordinate error in the pool; see the table below.
- **[A]** Bucklersbury household from 1505; Lucian translations with Erasmus published 1506, the "friendly contest" framing [R] from the humanist correspondence. **[A]** Jane Colt dies 1511; More remarries the widow Alice Middleton within weeks.

## 3. Utopia and the civic years, 1510-1517

- **[A]** *The Life of John Pico, Earl of Mirandola*, 1510 (Stanford Encyclopedia chronology). Under-Sheriff of London from 1510, held through Evil May Day. *History of King Richard III* begun c.1513, English and Latin, both unfinished. Royal commercial embassy to Flanders 1515, Bruges then Antwerp, Book II of Utopia drafted in the adjournment.
- **[A]** First edition of Utopia, Louvain, December 1516, printer **Dirk Martens**, Erasmus supervising. *Correction:* the dossier credited the woodcut map and frontispiece to Ambrosius Holbein, but those belong to the **1518 Basel** edition (Wikipedia, 'Ambrosius Holbein': "Frontispiece to the third edition of Thomas More's Utopia, 1518"); the 1516 editio princeps carried a different, anonymous map. The dossier's uncertainty about the printer is dropped: Wikipedia, 'Dirk Martens' confirms him.
- **[A]** Evil May Day, 1 May 1517, More involved as a London officer. **[R]** The "Strangers' Case" speech is from the Elizabethan play *Sir Thomas More*, roughly seventy years later. Dramatic reconstruction; no contemporary record. Flagged in the campa, not printed as a quote.

## 4. Royal service, 1514/1518-1523

- **CONTRADICTION, AND THE DOSSIER CONTRADICTED ITSELF.** It asserted 1518 for Master of Requests and Privy Council membership while citing Wikipedia, 'Thomas More', which dates both to **1514**; others give 1517 or 1518. Unfixed across 1514-1518. The journey dates the stop at the late end and states that the range is unresolved and the dossier's own citation did not support its date.
- **[A]** Field of the Cloth of Gold, 7-24 June 1520, More's attendance confirmed: Wikipedia has "Included among the English guests were Thomas More and Anne Boleyn's mother and sister." The dossier pinned Guines town rather than the Val d'Or in Balinghem; see the table below.
- **[A]** Knighted, made Under-Treasurer of the Exchequer, 1521; diplomacy at Calais and Bruges (coordinate tightened, see table).
- **[A]** *Responsio ad Lutherum*, 1523, under the pseudonym Gulielmus Rosseus. **DATE CORRECTION, Bishopsgate:** the dossier's "Crosby Hall leased 1523" inverts the sequence. Wikipedia, 'Crosby Hall, London' has More **leasing in 1519**, purchasing 1523, and in that same year selling the remainder of the lease to Antonio Bonvisi. 1523 is the year he disposed of it. The dossier's caution that a property interest is not continuous residence is well founded and kept.

## 5. Chelsea and the great offices, 1523-1532

- **[A]** Speaker of the Commons, 15 April to 13 August 1523, with the petition for freedom of speech in Parliament; Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster 31 December 1525; Lord Chancellor 26 October 1529, succeeding Wolsey, *A Dialogue Concerning Heresies* published the same year. **[A/contradiction]** The move to Chelsea: thomasmorestudies.org 1524, Wikipedia 1525. Rendered c.1524-1525, dated to the earlier reading, both named.
- **[A]** Holbein's individual portrait, 1527, now in the Frick Collection. *Gap:* the Frick's provenance page 404'd; attribution and date rest on the Wikipedia cross-reference plus standard art history.
- **[A]** The lost Holbein household group, c.1527, destroyed in an 18th-century fire. *Attribution the dossier omitted:* the Nostell Priory copy is **Rowland Lockey, 1592**, family-commissioned, held most faithful to the original. Nostell and the Frick are cited in `suggested_refs` on More's own Chelsea stop, not staged as scenes: neither is a place he went.
- **[A]** South chapel of Chelsea Old Church rebuilt 1528 as his private chapel, the date carved on a chancel pillar capital.
- **[A]** Resignation 16 May 1532, the day after the Submission of the Clergy, citing ill health. That the real reason was the break with Rome is the universal reading, not a documented statement by More, who gave no reason but his health.

**Cambrai. [R], flagged.** The dossier placed More at the 1529 peace negotiations on thomasmorestudies.org alone. **Not independently confirmable:** Wikipedia's 'Treaty of Cambrai' names no English envoys, 'Cuthbert Tunstall' credits Tunstall alone. The stop is kept and says it rests on one source. **ANACHRONISTIC POLITY LABEL CORRECTED:** the dossier said "Cambrai, France"; in 1529 it was "the center of the principality-bishopric in the Netherlands (now the French department Nord)". Stop renamed, treaty date 5 August 1529 added; coordinates were correct.

## 6. The oath, the Tower, the scaffold, 1533-1535

- **[A]** Refusal to attend Anne Boleyn's coronation, 1 June 1533 (**[R]** the twenty pounds from three bishops for a gown is Roper's). **[A]** Questioned over Elizabeth Barton, the Holy Maid of Kent, by Cromwell's agents, 1534.
- **[A]** Before the commissioners at Lambeth 13 April 1534; refuses the oath appended to the Act of Succession while offering to swear to the succession itself; committed to the Tower 17 April.
- **[A]** Fifteen months in the Tower; *A Dialogue of Comfort against Tribulation* written there, *De tristitia Christi* unfinished. **[R]** The Bell Tower as the cell is tradition, not a documented assignment.
- **[A/contradiction]** Trial at Westminster Hall. Wikipedia and the Paris Newsletter give **1 July 1535**; famous-trials.com carries a conflicting **May** date. The journey follows 1 July because two independent sources give it, and states that the discrepancy is unexplained. Rich's testimony, the two witnesses who heard nothing, and conviction in about fifteen minutes are attested.
- **[A]** Beheaded on Tower Hill, 6 July 1535, aged 57.

### "I die the King's good servant, but God's first" is not printed as a quote

The most-quoted sentence attributed to the subject **appears in no primary source that could be reached.** "good servant", "king's good servant" and "God's first" are **all absent from the full CTMS text of Roper's *Life***. The **Paris Newsletter** of 4 August 1535, the only contemporary account, gives **indirect speech only**: "He then besought them earnestly to pray to God to give the King good counsel, protesting that he died his faithful servant, and God's first" (CTMS transcription; the 1882 *Letters and Papers* translation reads "but God's first"). Two drifts have occurred: **and** to **but**, and **faithful servant** to **good servant**. The journey does **not** carry it in the `quote` field; the campa calls it a third-person report that later became a first-person quotation, and `date_confidence` sets out the chain and names both renderings.

## 7. Body, head, and cult

- **[A]** Body buried in the Chapel Royal of St Peter ad Vincula inside the Tower, unmarked grave, 6 July 1535.
- **[A]** Head parboiled and displayed on London Bridge. **STRUCTURE CORRECTED, anachronistic by four decades:** the dossier put it on the **southern gatehouse**, but Wikipedia, 'Old London Bridge' has heads on the **drawbridge tower** mid-span, the Southwark stone gate taking that function only after the drawbridge tower was replaced in **1577-1579**. **[R]** Margaret's recovery: famous-trials.com confirms only that it stood "for some Months" and that she "bought it"; the bribed bridge-keeper and the Thames rescue are later elaboration.
- **Two incompatible burial traditions for Margaret Roper, flagged, not resolved.** geography[27] has her at **Chelsea Old Church** with her father's head, tagged **[A]**; geography[28] and Wikipedia have her bringing it to the Roper tomb at **Canterbury**, tagged [R]. The journey carries Canterbury, tags it [R], says the sources disagree, and treats the Chelsea [A] as an error. On the body: **that More lies at Chelsea is unsubstantiated local tradition**; the accepted burial is St Peter ad Vincula.
- **DATE CORRUPTION.** The dossier's "1997 vault opening" appears to be a corruption of **1978**: the only scholarly account is H. O. Albin, "Opening of the Roper Vault in St. Dunstan's Canterbury...", *Moreana* (1979), which describes fragmentary remains, not an intact skull. **1997 appears nowhere in the journey**, nor do the dossier's unconfirmed "2025 exhumation proposal", 2035 target, and GBP 50,000 figure.
- **[A]** Beatified by Leo XIII **29 December 1886** (day added, from the decree under which Wikipedia, 'Forty Martyrs of England and Wales' records eleven beatifications that day; the dossier gave the year only); canonized by Pius XI **19 May 1935** jointly with John Fisher (the dossier's two lenses disagreed in precision, both now carry the day).
- **[A]** Patron of statesmen and politicians, John Paul II, 31 October 2000. **TITLE CORRECTED:** the incipit is **"E sancti Thomae Mori"**, not "Ex Sancti Thomae Mori". Verified against vatican.va, which carries the header, the date, and "I establish and declare Saint Thomas More the heavenly Patron of Statesmen and Politicians."
- **[A]** Chelsea Old Church hit by a parachute mine **14 April 1941**; the More Chapel took the least damage of the standing fabric; reopened 1950, chancel and Lawrence Chapel May 1954, church reconsecrated May 1958. Statue by Leslie Cubitt Bevis outside, facing the river.
- **[R], left out.** The 1918 "Obelisk of Revolutionary Thinkers", Alexander Garden, Moscow. The obelisk, its Soviet repurposing and Lenin's approval of the nineteen names are confirmed; **whether More is one of the nineteen is not**. No stop: not a place he went, and unconfirmed. Recorded so it is not lost.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Thomas More is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. The relations below, found by the interlock lens, are therefore **not in the journey file** and are recorded here rather than silently deleted. **Every one is FORWARD in direction:** a later subject reaching back to More, which the block rule does not license and which no grant covers for him.

- **Francis Bacon** (1561-1626): *New Atlantis* descends from Utopia's island form; also held More's office, tried in the same hall, held in the same Tower. Postdates by 91 years, and the office and prison overlaps are additionally bare co-location.
- **Tommaso Campanella** (1568-1639): *La citta del sole*, same genre, same travelogue frame.
- **Giordano Bruno** (1548-1600): executed by the church whose supremacy More died defending. The "inverse mirror" is thematic, not documented.
- **John Dee** (1527-1608): Tower ward birth, London/Antwerp trade-and-court world. Shared geography alone is not a reason.
- **H. G. Wells** (1866-1946): *A Modern Utopia* carries the genre-name More coined.
- **Edward Bellamy** (1850-1898), QUEUE.md line 388, and **George Orwell** (1903-1950), line 389, "the dystopian counter-current to thomas_more's just queued utopia". The queue notes are editorial apparatus, not facts of More's life.

**Operator decision needed only if any of these should be named inside More's campas**, which requires adding him to the forward-grant table in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.

Three relations **were** legal and **are** in the journey, each backward or contemporary and each a real engagement by More himself rather than shared geography:

- **`giovanni_pico_della_mirandola`** [A]: More translated and adapted Pico's life and letters into English, printed 1510. Named in that stop's campa.
- **`plato`** [A on the citation]: Utopia Book I cites Plato by name and argues against him on whether a philosopher should serve a king. Named in the Louvain campa.
- **`martin_luther`** [A]: More wrote the *Responsio ad Lutherum* (1523) against Luther under a pseudonym, in a controversy both men lived through. Named in that stop's campa.

**Not named, deliberately.** Erasmus appears in the campas as a person but creates no interlock: `saint_erasmus` is an unrelated subject. **Absence of evidence, flagged:** no existing journey file states a documented meeting or exchange with More.
