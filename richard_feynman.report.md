# Richard Feynman (1918-1988): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-04 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `richard_feynman`, physics wing.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or attribution not verified against an original. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Standing caution.** Most of the popular Feynman record descends from two books of taped anecdotes, *Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!* (1985) and *What Do You Care What Other People Think?* (1988), assembled by Ralph Leighton decades after the events. They are testimony, not documentation, and claims resting only on them are marked as doing so.

## 1. Queens, 1918-1935

Birth 11 May 1918, son of Lucille (Phillips) and Melville Arthur Feynman **[A, Wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction, unresolved: the birthplace.** Wikipedia gives **Queens**; Britannica and Gleick give **Manhattan**. The pool's geography lens pinned the birth at **Far Rockaway**, wrong on a third count, since the family moved there only in 1927: Wikipedia, verbatim, "Four years later, Richard's sister Joan was born and the family moved to Far Rockaway, Queens." The journey pins a Queens centroid and states the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

Brother Henry Phillips born 1923, dead four weeks later; sister Joan born 1927; move to Far Rockaway **[A]**. Far Rockaway High School 1931-1935, the algebra team, self-taught calculus, the radio repair trade **[A as memoir]**; no school record was consulted.

**Gap.** Melville's pedagogy (the bathroom tiles, the refusal to let a name count as an explanation) is told entirely by Richard, late in life, about a dead man. Kept in restrained form as filial memory, not record.

## 2. MIT and Princeton, 1935-1942

MIT 1935-1939; bachelor's degree and Putnam Fellowship 1939; published before graduating **[A, Wikipedia; Gleick]**. Ballistics at Frankford Arsenal, 1941 **[A]**, given no pin because the arsenal's coordinates could not be verified against a survey point here; carried in the campa of the doctorate stop. PhD, Princeton, 1942, under John Archibald Wheeler **[A]**; month not fixed, defaulted to June and hedged.

**Coordinate note.** The geography lens gives Palmer Physical Laboratory at 40.3459/-74.6577; the journey inherits the corpus Princeton pin instead (see the interlock table), and the few hundred metres of offset are noted in `date_confidence`.

**Interlock, geographic only [R].** Einstein at the Institute 1933-1955; Dirac in the old Fine Hall from 1934; von Neumann one of the founding six since 1933. **No direct Einstein-Feynman meeting is attested in any corpus file.** The story of Einstein and Pauli at Feynman's first Princeton seminar is not in the verified pool and is **not** asserted. The campa says only that these men were in the same square mile in the same years.

Marriage to Arline Greenbaum, 29 June 1942 **[A]**. **Correction:** they were **not married on the ferry**. Wikipedia, verbatim: "On June 29, 1942, they took the ferry to Staten Island, where they were married in the city office." The pool compounded this with a "Municipal Building, Manhattan" pin at 40.7127/-74.0059, which is not the Municipal Building but the generic New York City centroid. Corrected to approximately 40.6432/-74.0759.

## 3. Los Alamos, 1943-1945

Departure 28 March 1943 **[A]**. Group leader under Hans Bethe **[A]**.

**Correction: the recruiter.** The pool's chronology has Feynman "recruited by Robert Oppenheimer." Wikipedia, verbatim: "Feynman was recruited by Robert R. Wilson, who was working on means to produce enriched uranium." Oppenheimer directed the site; he did not recruit Feynman. The journey names Wilson.

**Safecracking [A as his own account, *Surely You're Joking*; Wikipedia]**. No security log confirming an individual opening was reachable, and later retellings inflate the number and difficulty beyond his own telling **[R]**; the journey keeps the method and the notes left in drawers and puts no number on the safes.

Oak Ridge Y-12, uranium handling and criticality hazards, 1943-1944 **[A as memoir]**; no plant records reachable. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 36.0103/-84.2696 is the **town**; Y-12 is at **35.9883/-84.2547**, 2.7 km SSE.

Bohr at Los Alamos from 30 December 1943 as Nicholas Baker **[A, `niels_bohr.journey.json`]**. That Bohr sought out Feynman as the one junior man who would say an idea was bad is his own undated account **[A as memoir]**; the stop carries Bohr's arrival date and says so.

The IBM computing group and the implosion calculations **[A, Gleick; memoir]**. Fermi in F Division from 1 September 1944 **[A, `fermi.journey.json`]**; von Neumann at Los Alamos 1943-1945 for the lens and height of burst **[A, `von_neumann.journey.json`]**. That Feynman crossed both is established outside the corpus but **is not stated in the corpus files**, which nowhere name him.

Arline dies 16 June 1945, Presbyterian Sanatorium, Albuquerque **[A]**; the clock and the dress in the Oak Ridge shop window are from *What Do You Care* **[A as memoir]**.

Trinity, 16 July 1945 **[A]**. **Correction: the vantage point.** The pool said "Los Alamos's viewing distance," which is meaningless: Trinity Site lies in the Jornada del Muerto, roughly 200 miles south of Los Alamos. Feynman watched from **Base Camp, some 10-20 miles from ground zero**, through a truck windshield, reasoning the glass would stop ultraviolet. That he alone saw it unprotected is his own claim **[A as memoir, unverified]**.

## 4. Cornell and the diagrams, 1945-1950

Cornell from October 1945 **[A]**. Melville Feynman dies of a stroke 8 October 1946 **[A]**. The letter to Arline, 17 October 1946 **[A, *Perfectly Reasonable Deviations*, 2005, via news.lettersofnote.com]**, quoted in the journey; his location that day is **inferred** from the Cornell appointment **[R]**.

Shelter Island Conference, 2-4 June 1947 **[A]**. **Corrected twice.** (1) The pool's 41.0709/-72.3251 sits on the main island; the **Ram's Head Inn**, 108 Ram Island Drive, is at **41.0797/-72.2942**, 2.8 km NE. (2) The pool's claim that Feynman "presents his path-integral approach to renormalization" overstates him. Wikipedia's conference article says he "gave an informal presentation about his work on quantum electrodynamics" and gave "a more formal, and less successful, presentation on QED at the Pocono Conference next year." The deflationary version is followed.

Pocono Conference, 30 March to 2 April 1948, Pocono Manor Inn; Bohr and Dirac both challenged him **[A, Schweber, *QED and the Men Who Made It*]**. **Coordinate correction:** pool 41.1145/-75.3049; the village is at **41.1001/-75.3588**, 4.8 km SW.

Oldstone Conference, 11-14 April 1949, and Dyson's equivalence proof **[A, Schweber]**. **Location correction:** the pool placed it at Croton-on-Hudson. Oldstone-on-the-Hudson stood in **Cortlandt Manor / Peekskill**, approximately **41.27/-73.93**; Croton is roughly 10 km south.

## 5. Brazil, 1949 and 1951-1952

Several weeks in Rio, July 1949, and a sabbatical year at the Centro Brasileiro de Pesquisas Físicas, 1951 **[A]**. Portuguese lectures, the frigideira, the samba school, and the closing address to the ministry on teaching by memorised definition **[A as memoir]**; the last is kept because it is the argument of "Cargo Cult Science" twenty-three years early.

**Coordinate correction.** The pool's -22.9068/-43.2295 is a mangled Rio centroid landing in Tijuca/Maracanã. CBPF is in **Urca**, 22°57'14"S 43°10'25"W = **-22.9540/-43.1737**, about 8 km off.

**Dropped.** The pool's Copacabana pin, tagged **[R]** ("beach visits not independently documented"), is folded into the CBPF stop: a pin implies a located event, and general leisure is not one.

## 6. Caltech, the second marriage, Gweneth

**Contradiction not resolved: the Caltech start.** The pool's chronology says **1952**; its geography lens gives the span **1950-1988**. The journey dates the stop 1952-01-01 and records the disagreement.

Mary Louise Bell, Boise, 28 June 1952; separation 20 May 1956; interlocutory decree 19 June 1956; divorce final 5 May 1958 **[all A]**. The complaint's language about calculus while driving is widely reprinted and was **not** checked against the filing **[R]**.

**Correction: the first Japan visit.** The pool dates Kyoto **September 1955**. Feynman's first visit was for the International Conference of Theoretical Physics, Kyoto and Tokyo, **14-24 September 1953**, under IUPAP auspices, proceedings 1954. The journey uses 1953 and states the disagreement; Kyoto coordinates are kept. Las Vegas **[A as memoir]**: no trip is dated, so 1955 is an explicit placeholder within the decade.

Gweneth Howarth met at the Atoms for Peace Conference, September 1958 **[A, Wikipedia: "On the beach at Lake Geneva, he met Gweneth Howarth"]**. **Correction:** the pool claimed they met in **Altadena**, where she was "an English au pair." They met at Lake Geneva; she arrived in Altadena in **June 1959** as his housekeeper, and the Altadena pin is relabelled as continuation, not origin.

Marriage at the Huntington Hotel, Pasadena, 24 September 1960; Carl born 1962; Michelle adopted 1968 **[A]**. **Correction:** the pool's household pin, "Pasadena, Alameda Street," 34.1445/-118.1273, has the wrong town: they lived in **Altadena** from 1960 until his death. The pool's own note adds that "a house he designed with her" is unsupported; dropped, as is the pool's CERN pin (46.2333/6.05, "no primary itinerary verified"), **[R]** on its own account.

## 7. The lectures, the prize, the 1968 charge

The Feynman Lectures delivered from 1961 **[A]**; the pool's afterlife lens gives the course span 1961-1964. Translated widely, Spanish among them, still in print **[A]**.

Nobel Prize 1965 with Julian Schwinger and Shin'ichirō Tomonaga, ceremony 10 December **[A, Nobel Foundation]**. The pool gives Konserthuset variously as 59.3341/18.07 and 59.3293/18.0686; the journey inherits the corpus Stockholm pin instead (see the interlock table). The predawn telephone call and the consideration of refusing are his own repeated telling **[R as to wording]**; the journey states the call and puts no dialogue in his mouth.

**1968: allegations of sexism toward women at Caltech [A, Wikipedia].**

**Honest gap, and a large one.** Nothing reachable gives complainants, substance, process, or finding; that one sentence is the whole attested record available here. The journey records the accusation because it was made, says in `date_confidence` that no particulars exist, and resolves it in neither direction. Related but distinct: the topless bar he worked in and defended in court is from *Surely You're Joking* **[A as memoir]**, placed in the same stop and offered as evidence about nothing.

## 8. The van, the cancer, Tuva, 1970-1985

"Cargo Cult Science," Caltech commencement 1974 **[A, Caltech Archives, fetched directly]**, quoted; ceremony date unconfirmed, June is the commencement month, hedged. The Feynvan, 1975, Long Beach, plate QANTUM, bought and preserved by Seamus Blackley in 2012 **[A]**; month unrecorded, coordinates are the city, hedged.

Liposarcoma, 1978 **[A, Wikipedia: "Surgeons removed a 'very large' tumor that had crushed one kidney and his spleen"]**. **Tag correction:** the pool tagged this **[R]**; it is **[A]**. Two details in the pool's phrasing are wrong and corrected: "the size of a football" is Gleick's phrasing, not Wikipedia's, and the tumour **crushed** the kidney and spleen rather than being removed with them. Surgery at UCLA.

Esalen **[R]**: attested in his own writing but undated, so 1970 is an explicit placeholder. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 36.1372/-121.7847 is **in the Pacific**, 13 km offshore. Esalen, 55000 Highway One, is at 36°07'37"N 121°38'30"W = **36.1270/-121.6416**.

Tuva **[A as campaign, Leighton, *Tuva or Bust!*, 1991]**: ten years of correspondence, never reached. The Moscow pin stands for the bureaucratic route, not a visit, and 1980 is a placeholder; the Kyzyl pin is a destination never arrived at, carrying his death date with a `date_confidence` saying exactly that. **Contradiction not resolved:** accounts put the Tuvan invitation variously days and weeks after his death, and the campa says "two days after he dies, or a few weeks after, depending on which account is followed."

*Surely You're Joking* and *QED*, both 1985 **[A]**; QED from the Alix G. Mautner Memorial Lectures **[A]**. The taping sessions are undated, so the stop takes the publication year at the Altadena household.

## 9. Challenger and Appendix F, 1986

Challenger breaks apart 73 seconds after launch, 28 January 1986; seven dead; O-ring seal failure **[A, Rogers Commission Report]**. Appointment to the commission, 1986 **[A]**; exact date unfixed, 3 February taken from the commission's formation and hedged.

**Correction: where the hearings were held.** The pool offered a "Department of Transportation Building" pin (38.8853/-77.0246) and a "National Academy of Sciences auditorium" pin (38.8927/-77.0505). **No source places these hearings at the Department of Transportation.** The public hearings, 11 February included, were in the **Dean Acheson Auditorium, Department of State, 2201 C St NW**, approximately **38.8940/-77.0487**. The NAS coordinates fall 250 m away, the two being neighbours on Constitution Avenue, but the label names the wrong institution.

**Correction: the ice-water rehearsal.** The pool pins one at "Feynman's kitchen, Pasadena." **Unsupported**, and doubly wrong: the documented account has him buying a C-clamp and pliers near his **Washington hotel** the night before, and the home was in **Altadena**. The pin is deleted and the hardware store folded into the hearing stop.

The demonstration, 11 February 1986 **[A, Rogers Commission Report; broadcast footage]**. Appendix F, June 1986 **[A, fetched from nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v2appf.htm]**. That Feynman threatened to remove his name unless it was included is widely reported and **[R]** here: no primary document of the threat was reached.

**Quotation discipline.** One Appendix F quotation in the pool begins silently mid-sentence and drops a closing clause. Printed, the sentence reads: "Because of this, obvious weaknesses are accepted again and again, sometimes without a sufficiently serious attempt to remedy them, or to delay a flight because of their continued presence." The pool's truncation is **not** used; the three Appendix F lines that are used are quoted whole.

## 10. Death and afterlife

Died 15 February 1988, aged 69 **[A, Wikipedia; NYT obituary]**, at **UCLA Medical Center**, Westwood, 34.0669/-118.4452; the pool gave 34.0522/-118.2437, the **downtown Los Angeles centroid**, corrected. Immediate cause a ruptured duodenal ulcer and kidney failure, dialysis refused; a second cancer, Waldenström's macroglobulinemia, also present **[A]**.

**The last words** ("I'd hate to die twice. It's so boring.") are universally reported and **not** traced to a primary document **[R]**. They appear in the campa as reported speech, never in a `quote` field.

Buried at Mountain View Cemetery and Mausoleum, Altadena **[A]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 34.1897/-118.137 is the town centroid; the cemetery at 2400 N Fair Oaks Ave is at approximately **34.1861/-118.1481**, 1.1 km west. No reachable source fixes the burial **date**; 18 February is an inference, hedged.

Asteroid 7495 Feynman; the Foresight Institute's Feynman Prize in Nanotechnology, for the 1959 talk "There's Plenty of Room at the Bottom"; the Physics World 1999 poll of 130 physicists ranking him seventh; Gleick's *Genius* (1992) as the engine of the posthumous revival **[all A]**.

**[R], and excluded from the journey: the "Feynman Technique."** No source fixes when or by whom the label was coined. The practice traces to his study habits; the branding is a later, undated accretion of internet pedagogy culture.

## Addendum, 2026-08-11: the Froebel line, one stop added

An enrichment pass, not a rewrite. Nothing above is amended; one stop is added to segment 1 between the 1918 birth and the 1927 move, and this section records what it rests on.

**The stop.** "New York, the paper weaving and the mother trained to teach it," 1923-09-01, coordinates 40.7282/-73.7949 inherited byte-identical from the birth stop. Applied identically to `es/richard_feynman.journey.json`; `lat`, `lng`, `date` and `date_confidence` are byte-identical across the two editions, per house convention.

**[A] Feynman's own kindergarten weaving.** "We had weaving in those days. They've taken it out; it's too difficult for children. We used to weave colored paper through vertical strips and make patterns. The kindergarten teacher was so amazed that she sent a special letter home to report that this child was very unusual, because he seemed to be able to figure out ahead of time what pattern he was going to get, and made amazingly intricate patterns." Source: "What Is Science?", his address to the fifteenth annual meeting of the National Science Teachers Association, New York, 1966, printed in *The Physics Teacher* 7:6 (1969), 313-320. **This corrects a "not reachable" entry in section 10 above**, which recorded the 1966 address as unopened; it was fetched directly at feynman.com in this pass, and its one usable line is now quoted at the new stop. The five Wikiquote attributions named in that same paragraph remain **[R]** and remain unused.

**[A] Paper weaving is a Froebel occupation.** Weaving and paper interlacing stand in the standard list of Froebel's occupations beside perforating, sewing, folding and cutting (Kate Douglas Wiggin's manuals; Froebel Trust materials). Feynman's own aside that the weaving was later taken out of the curriculum dates his kindergarten to the period when the Froebelian occupations were still in general American use. `froebel.journey.json` carries the Gifts and the occupations at Bad Blankenburg, 1838.

**[A] Lucille Phillips attended the Ethical Culture School.** *Dictionary of Scientific Biography*, "Feynman, Richard," via encyclopedia.com, verbatim: "Feynman's mother was born into a well-to-do New York family and attended the Ethical Culture School but did not go to college thereafter." Independent of the essay that prompted this pass. Her birth date, 22 March 1895, from genealogical sources **[R]**, puts her at the school in the years before the 1917 marriage. `felix_adler.journey.json` already establishes the descent: Adler's free kindergarten of 1878, Froebelian, becomes the Workingman's School in 1880 and is renamed the Ethical Culture School in 1895.

**[R], and left at [R]: that she trained there as a kindergarten teacher.** The claim originates with Gleick, *Genius* (1992), cited in passing by the thinkingwiththings.com essay "Richard Feynman and Froebel Kindergarten," which is the only source that names the page. **The text of *Genius* could not be reached in this pass**: the archive.org copies are lending-restricted or withdrawn (HTTP 403; one public upload returns "Item not available"), and the book is absent from the Open Library full-text index, where a control search on "Melville Feynman" returns Gribbin, Krauss, LeVine and Goodstein but not Gleick. The campa therefore says "is said to have trained there as a kindergartner" and the `date_confidence` field states the remove. **Anyone who can open *Genius* should settle this line and, if it holds, upgrade the campa.**

**Not asserted.** Brosterman's reply to the essayist, that a trained kindergartner "you can be 100% certain" used the gifts and the system with her own child at home, is inference about a private household and appears nowhere in the journey. The stop claims only what is documented: she went to that school, he did that weaving, and the weaving is Froebel's.

**Interlocks named in the campa.** `felix_adler` and `froebel`, both by name, both by a real institutional route rather than a thematic one. No new coordinate is introduced. Five are used, each at the stop that is its own occasion; the truncated "Obvious weaknesses" line is excluded on the grounds in section 9. The five **[R]** attributions appear nowhere, not in a `quote` field and not paraphrased into a campa as if they were his words. The journey therefore quotes at five stops out of forty-three rather than the dozen a fuller pool would allow, and that density is set by the evidence.
