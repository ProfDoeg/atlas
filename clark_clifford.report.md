# Clark Clifford (1906-1998): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Clark McAdams Clifford: counsel to Truman, chairman of the President's Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board, ninth Secretary of Defense, and chairman of a Washington bank that turned out to belong to the Bank of Credit and Commerce International.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference from office and date rather than a document placing the man in the room. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and not adjudicated; gaps are stated as gaps.

**Method.** The report works from a verified pool assembled in five lenses (chronology, geography, quotation, interlock, afterlife). Where the verifier corrected the pool's first pass, the correction is reproduced as a correction rather than folded in: the shape of the error is evidence about which sources are load-bearing.

---

## 1. Fort Scott and St. Louis, 1906-1943

Clark McAdams Clifford is born at Fort Scott, Kansas, on 25 December 1906, son of Frank Clifford, a traveling auditor for the Missouri Pacific Railroad, and named for his maternal uncle Clark McAdams **[A: Wikipedia, "Clark Clifford"]**. The family settles in St. Louis, where he grows up **[A: same]**. He is initiated into the Beta Theta chapter of Kappa Alpha Order at Washington University in St. Louis in 1924 and takes a BA and an LLB there **[A: Wikipedia]**. He practices law in St. Louis from 1928 to 1943 **[A: Wikipedia]**, and marries Margery Pepperell "Marny" Kimball on 3 October 1931; three daughters follow, Margery ("Gery"), Joyce Clifford Burland, and Randall Clifford Wight **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 2. The Navy and the White House, 1944-1946

Clifford serves as a U.S. Navy officer between 1944 and 1946, reaching Captain, and is assigned to the White House in 1945 as assistant naval aide to Truman, then promoted to naval aide **[A: Wikipedia]**.

*Gap, and a substantial one:* nothing reachable establishes where he served before the White House posting. Training stations, ships, naval districts: none are named. The journey file opens his war at the White House door, which is where the record opens it.

*Contradiction, not resolved:* the sources disagree on the title of the office he holds from February 1946. See item 2 below.

## 3. Counsel to the President, 1946-1950

- **The Clifford-Elsey Report, 1946 [A: Wikipedia].** With George Elsey, a top-secret report to Truman cataloguing Soviet treaty violations, hardening Cold War policy alongside Kennan's "X Article." *Hedge:* year only, so month and day are defaults and date_confidence says so.
- **The National Security Act, 1947-1949 [A: Wikipedia].** Clifford works the legislative campaign producing the 1947 Act and its 1949 amendments. *Hedge:* the Capitol pin marks the seat of the process, not an itemized presence on named dates, as the geography lens flags.
- **The 1948 campaign strategy [A: Wikipedia].** Key architect of Truman's reelection strategy, the "Clifford Memo" approach. *Hedge:* the source dates the approach to 1948 and not the memorandum, and nothing here supplies a date.
- **Recognition of Israel, 14 May 1948 [A: Wikipedia].** Clifford, with David Niles, argues for prompt recognition against Secretary of State George Marshall's strong objection; Truman recognizes Israel the day it declares independence.
- **Philadelphia, Democratic National Convention, July 1948 [R].** Venue and his role as strategist are documented; his attendance is not verified. Kept [R].
- **Two stops dropped.** *St. Louis whistle-stop, September 1948 [R]*, reconstructed from the campaign route and his authorship of the strategy: it puts a specific man on a specific train with no source that does so. *Key West, 1948 [A as to the photograph, R as to the season]*: he is photographed with Truman at the Little White House, but "Spring 1948" is unverified, Truman's stays there having run to a winter rhythm, and the pool's 24.5551,-81.7994 lands in Old Town, 800 m from the house at 24.5563,-81.8069.

## 4. The superlawyer and the intelligence board, 1950-1967

Clifford leaves the White House on 31 January 1950 and opens a Washington practice whose office, in his own account, "overlooked the White House" **[A: Wikipedia]**. *Gap:* the street address is unestablished; the pin approximates Lafayette Square.

- **Kennedy transition, 1960 [A: Wikipedia].** Clifford, already Kennedy's lawyer, heads it after the election.
- **Bay of Pigs review, 1961 [R].** A general mention with no date detail, tagged a gap. **Dropped:** real enough to name in a report, too thin to pin.
- **PFIAB, appointed May 1961; chairman 23 April 1963 to 29 February 1968 [A: Wikipedia].** He succeeds James Killian and leaves the chair the day before taking over Defense.
  - *Correction.* The pool's first pass had the board "renamed the President's Intelligence Advisory Board" during Clifford's tenure. That is a forty-year anachronism: the renaming came on 29 February 2008, under EO 13462 **[A: Wikipedia, "President's Intelligence Advisory Board"]**. Clifford chaired the PFIAB throughout, and the parenthetical is deleted.
- **USS Liberty, 1967 [A: Wikipedia].** As PFIAB chairman he oversees the investigation into the Israeli attack on USS Liberty, calls it "a flagrant act of gross negligence," and presses Johnson to hold Israel accountable. *Hedge:* year only; the journey file pins June, the month of the attack, and says so in date_confidence.

## 5. The Clifford-Taylor mission, July-August 1967

Wikipedia confirms that Clifford travels with General Maxwell Taylor to South Vietnam **[A]**. The rest of the itinerary is the "more flags" mission to the allied governments, reconstructed by the geography lens and partly upgraded by the verifier:

- **Saigon [A: Wikipedia; FRUS 1964-1968 Vol. V, at second hand].**
- **Canberra [A after correction].** FRUS Vol. V, Doc. 270 records a full day's visit including private talks with Prime Minister Harold Holt on raising the Australian contingent from 6,500 toward 9,000. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool's -35.3081,149.1244 is the new Parliament House, opened 1988; Old Parliament House, the seat of government in 1967, is at -35.3019,149.1297.
- **Bangkok [A after correction].** The same document records two lengthy meetings with the Thai Prime Minister and Cabinet, agreement on the need for more Thai troops, and a shortage of officers to lead them. *Coordinate corrected:* Government House is at 13.7659,100.5133, not the pool's 100.5203.
- **Wellington [R]**, **Manila [R]**, **Seoul [R].** General historical record only. Wellington and Seoul are carried as [R] pins with the hedge in date_confidence; Manila is dropped for length, not for doubt.

## 6. Secretary of Defense, 1968-1969

- **Appointed 19 January 1968; sworn in 1 March [A: Wikipedia].** Ninth Secretary of Defense, succeeding Robert McNamara.
- **The Wise Men, 25-26 March 1968 [A after correction].** The chronology lens tagged this [R], "exact meeting dates are not supplied"; the verifier found the opposite problem, that the dates are documented. The Senior Advisory Group on Vietnam (Acheson, Bohlen, Harriman, Kennan, Lovett, McCloy, Omar Bradley and others) reconvened on those days, Clifford having got them re-briefed and re-summoned, and most concluded the war was not winnable.
- **31 March 1968 [A: Wikipedia].** He supports a partial bombing halt above the 20th parallel, and his counsel helps move Johnson to deny Westmoreland's request for 206,000 more troops. Johnson announces the same night that he will not seek reelection.
- **31 October 1968 [A: Wikipedia].** He supports complete cessation of the bombing of North Vietnam.
- **20 January 1969 [A: Wikipedia].** His term ends with the administration, and Johnson awards him the Presidential Medal of Freedom with Distinction the same day.

*Quotation flagged as unverified.* "I found out that we couldn't win the war with the limitations that we had, which I thought were correct limitations," attributed to a 1981 WGBH oral history on Vietnam. The transcript was not fetched. Carried as **[R]**, doubt stated in quote_source.

## 7. Elder statesman and the approach of BCCI, 1977-1981

- **December 1977, first meeting with Agha Hasan Abedi [A: Clifford's 1991 Senate testimony, S. Rep. 102-140, Ch. 13].** In his account a brief social meeting about Third World banking; he found Abedi "pleasant and a man of importance."
- **1978, the introduction [A: Kerry-Brown Senate report, via Wikipedia].** Clifford and Altman are introduced to BCCI by Bert Lance, former director of the Office of Management and Budget, and the relationship runs 1978 to 1991, predating the formal 1982 acquisition.
  - *Contradiction, not resolved:* December 1977 against 1978 as the start. See item 3 below. The journey file pins the earlier claim and names the later one in the same campa.
- **1980, special presidential emissary to India for Carter [A: Wikipedia].** *Gap:* no New Delhi venue is given; the pin marks the seat of the Indian government.
- **1980, Iran hostage diplomacy [R: Wikipedia].** Clifford is reported to have threatened Khomeini's government with war over its intransigence. Location unspecified; the State Department vicinity stands in for it.
- **23 April 1981, Federal Reserve hearing [A: S. Rep. 102-140, Ch. 13].** Asked what role BCCI would play in First American's affairs: "None. There is no function of any kind on the part of BCCI ... I know of no present relationship. I know of no planned future relationship that exists."

## 8. First American Bankshares, 1982-1991

- **1982-1991 [A: Wikipedia, "Clark Clifford" and "BCCI"].** Middle Eastern investors acting secretly as nominees for BCCI acquire Financial General Bankshares, renamed First American Bankshares. Clifford, called adviser to five presidents in the source, chairs it for nine years to allay regulators' concerns; the board includes former Senator Stuart Symington. *Gap:* the headquarters address is unconfirmed; the pin approximates downtown Washington.
- **1986 [A: S. Rep. 102-140, Ch. 13].** Clifford and Altman buy CCAH holding-company stock with BCCI-arranged loans: "We got to a point where we knew we were over the hump," and "I wanted to own some stock in my own company." He later says he made about six million dollars on bank stock bought with an unsecured BCCI loan **[R: Wikipedia]**.
- **1987 [A: S. Rep. 102-140, Ch. 13].** An anonymous employee's letter warns: "Your basic error has been BCCI. This association is 'on the street' and as soon as this becomes known (with BCCI reputation) decent accounts fly." Not pinned, for length.
- **1990 [A: Wikipedia, "BCCI"].** A Price Waterhouse audit exposes that BCCI secretly and illegally owns roughly 60 percent of First American, having seized shares pledged as collateral by defaulting investor-shareholders who had borrowed from it.

*Institutional pins.* Two BCCI addresses enter the journey file as inherited atlas pins, not as places Clifford stood: Calle 50, Panama City (8.9833, -79.5167) and Leadenhall Street, London (51.5133, -0.0831), byte-identical from `manuel_noriega.journey.json`. Their campas say so; what is shared is the institution, not the room.

## 9. The reckoning, 1991-1998

- **1991, Senate subcommittee testimony [A: S. Rep. 102-140, Ch. 13].** "In all these years, we didn't encounter a single suspicious circumstance. ... Were we deceived? Apparently, we were deceived." And: "We have not violated any law. We have not been guilty of any impropriety." *Hedge:* year only, so date_confidence states that the stop's position within 1991 is not fixed.
- **1991, the memoir [A: Wikipedia].** *Counsel to the President*, with Richard Holbrooke, published as the scandal breaks; he discusses it on C-SPAN's Booknotes in July.
- **29 July 1991 [A: Wikipedia, "BCCI"].** Morgenthau discloses that BCCI secretly owns First American and indicts BCCI, Abedi, and Swaleh Naqvi.
- **14 August 1991 [A: UPI Archives].** Clifford and Altman resign; the wire copy carries the line that has followed him ever since.
- **1992 [A: Wikipedia; Kerry-Brown report].** Both men are indicted for fraud, conspiracy, and bribery; Clifford's assets are frozen in New York. Abdur Sakhia, a former BCCI regional officer, has told the subcommittee it is "very hard to believe, very, very hard to believe, almost impossible to believe" that they did not know **[A: S. Rep. 102-140]**.
- **1993 [A: Wikipedia, "BCCI"].** Altman is tried in Manhattan and acquitted, and separately accepts a de facto lifetime banking ban in a civil settlement with the Federal Reserve. Charges against Clifford are dropped and he is never tried.
  - *Contradiction flagged, not resolved:* charges "dropped due to his advanced age and deteriorating health," or Clifford "severed from the case as physically unable to stand trial." Different events, same result; the journey file uses language covering both.
  - *Coordinate corrected:* the geography lens pinned this at 38.8977,-77.0365, the White House, which has nothing to do with a prosecutor's decision. The pin is now the E. Barrett Prettyman federal courthouse, 38.8929,-77.0163.
- **1998 [A: Wikipedia].** A five-million-dollar settlement with the Federal Reserve closes the civil litigation. Clifford dies at Bethesda, Maryland, on 10 October, aged 91. *Gap:* home or hospital is not specified; city coordinates are used.
- **Burial, Arlington National Cemetery [A as to the cemetery; tertiary as to the plot].** The first pass recorded the plot as a gap, Arlington's own page having returned an error. The verifier closed it with **Section 7-A, Grave 35**, from Find a Grave corroborated by Wikidata and WikiTree, and moved the coordinate south of the Memorial Amphitheater to about 38.8767,-77.0703. Three crowd sources agreeing is weaker ground than it looks; the file takes the coordinate and names them for what they are.

## 10. Afterlife

- **1969 [A].** Presidential Medal of Freedom with Distinction. **1978 [A].** Golden Plate Award, American Academy of Achievement; not pinned.
- **1995 and 2002 [A].** Tony Goldwyn plays Clifford in HBO's *Truman*, Donald Sutherland in HBO's *Path to War*. Both dramatizations are built on the pre-BCCI career.
- **1997 [A].** His last public appearance is in the PBS documentary *Truman*.
- **The nickname.** *Correction.* The afterlife lens called "Bank of Crooks and Criminals International" a press coinage adopted after the 1991 collapse. The timing runs the other way: investigators used the epithet during the 1980s, of BCCI's clientele of arms dealers and drug traffickers, and it reached general press usage only afterward. Upgraded [R] to [A], direction reversed.

## Contradictions between sources (not adjudicated)

1. **Four presidents or five.** The career framing says "adviser to four presidents" (Truman, Kennedy, Johnson, Carter); the BCCI-era sources say five. The journey file avoids the count and names the presidents.
2. **White House Counsel vs Special Counsel.** The chronology lens gives **White House Counsel**, 1 February 1946 to 31 January 1950; the geography lens, working the same article, gives **Special Counsel to the President**, 1946-1950. Both **[A: Wikipedia]**. In later usage these are different offices. The journey file uses "Special Counsel," the title the administration itself used.
3. **When BCCI began.** December 1977 (his testimony, the first Abedi meeting) vs 1978 (Kerry-Brown, the Bert Lance introduction) vs 1982 (the acquisition and chairmanship). All three are attested and describe different events; the risk is a summary picking one and calling it the beginning.
4. **"I have a choice of either seeming stupid or venal."** Three placements circulate. The quotation lens first sourced it to a New York Times report of his 1991 Senate testimony, tagged [R]; the verifier could not substantiate that attribution and traced the line to UPI's 14 August 1991 wire story, which has him saying it "to a reporter in April," with the same wording in the Deseret News that day and the Los Angeles Times of 19 April 1992. The geography lens attaches it instead to the 1993 dropping of charges. The journey file pins it to the resignation with the April provenance in quote_source.
5. **The mechanism of the 1993 outcome.** Dropped vs severed as unable to stand trial. See section 9.
6. **Lens against verifier.** The Wise Men ([R] "dates not supplied" against a documented 25-26 March 1968), the PFIAB renaming (contemporaneous against 2008), the BCCI nickname (post-1991 against 1980s), and the Arlington plot (a gap against Section 7-A, Grave 35 on tertiary sources). The verifier is followed in all four, and the disagreements are recorded.
7. **Coordinates corrected.** Key West, Canberra, Bangkok, the 1993 charges, Arlington. The Canberra error put a 1967 meeting in a building opened in 1988. See sections 3, 5, 9.

## Honest gaps

- Addresses: birth or hospital at Fort Scott, childhood home in St. Louis, wedding venue, the St. Louis firm, the law office that "overlooked the White House," and the headquarters of First American Bankshares. Every one of these pins is therefore a town or district approximation, labeled as such in date_confidence or the campa.
- Graduation year for the BA and the LLB.
- Every naval posting between 1944 and the 1945 White House assignment. The largest hole in the file.
- Dates and substance of his Bay of Pigs advisory involvement; the specific Key West visit of the Truman photograph.
- Whether Clifford died at home or in a hospital in Bethesda; the funeral date at Arlington, only the year being given, so the burial stop is pinned to the date of death.
