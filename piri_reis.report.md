# Piri Reis (Muhiddin Piri, c. 1465 to 1553) research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for `piri_reis.journey.json`. Corsair, Ottoman captain, cartographer of the 1513 world map and the Kitab-i Bahriye, admiral of the Indian Ocean fleet, executed at Cairo.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a pin placed by inference. Contradictions are printed and left open.

**Method.** Built from a verified research pool assembled across five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife), each checked adversarially, with corrections recorded inside the pool. Nothing was fetched fresh; the pool's reachability limits are inherited and stated at the end. The backbone is Wikipedia, "Piri Reis," "Piri Reis map," and "Kitab-i Bahriye," footnoted in turn to the monographs listed below and marked relayed where cited through those footnotes.

---

## 1. Gelibolu and the corsair apprenticeship (c. 1465 to 1492)

**Birth. [A Wikipedia; Soucek]** Born Muhiddin Piri at Gelibolu on the Dardanelles, son of Haci Mehmed. **No exact birth date exists**: the sources give only c. 1465 to 1470. Every later statement of his age inherits that, which is why the execution accounts say "roughly eighty-four" when the honest range is 83 to 88.

**To sea, c. 1481. [A Wikipedia, citing the Kitab-i Bahriye preface tradition]** After his parents' death he begins sailing with his uncle Kemal Reis, a corsair captain of the same town. The year belongs to the tradition, not to a document. In **1487 [A]** Kemal Reis's squadron bombards the forces besieging Malaga in support of the Emirate of Granada, Piri aboard.

**The western Mediterranean, c. 1487 to 1492. [A Wikipedia; Kitab-i Bahriye]** Six years raiding the Spanish and French coasts, Corsica, Sardinia, Sicily, the Balearics. Eleven comrades killed in an action near Mallorca.

- **Correction.** Geography pinned his Balearic observations at Palma. Per Dierksmeier and Cespa (2022), pp. 361 to 366, he recorded the salt loading and the "Turkish and Arab captives in this salt mine" at **Ibiza**, the fig-tree spring in the **Gulf of Mahon** on Menorca, Mallorca's rivers and olive oil, Cabrera's rabbits. **Nothing is recorded at Palma.** Repinned to Ibiza.

**The 1492 evacuation. [A as event; R as to place]** Bayezid II orders Kemal Reis's ships to ferry Muslim and Jewish refugees from Spain to North Africa. **No source names a port.** Geography pinned Almeria as [A]; downgraded to **[R]**, coordinate corrected to 36.8411, -2.4719 (the supplied pin fell 0.8 km southeast of the fortress).

## 2. The sultan's fleet (1495 to 1510)

**Formal service begins in 1495, not "after 1495." [A Pedani (2015), pp. 319 to 320, relayed]** Kemal Reis was jailed for piracy in 1495 and the sultan answered with a navy commission instead of punishment; Piri Reis wrote that 1495 began their formal service.

**Zonchio, 12 to 25 August 1499. [A Wikipedia, "Piri Reis" and "Battle of Zonchio"]** He fights as a captain under his uncle off Sapienza, in the first naval engagement decided by shipboard cannon. **Contradiction, unresolved:** the two articles give **270 Ottoman against 150 Venetian** and **260 against 95**. Both stand in the campa.

**Modon and Navarino. [A Wikipedia]** Both fall in 1500; Venice retakes Navarino that December, and it is finally seized in 1501.

**The Spanish captive and the Columbus chart. [A Wikipedia; the map's marginalia, Section V]** In an action off the Spanish coast the Ottomans take a sailor claiming to have served under Columbus; from him Piri Reis obtains, by his own account, a chart of the Americas that had belonged to Columbus, with parrot feathers and black stones.

- **Lens disagreement, printed rather than hidden.** Chronology dates it **1501**, near Valencia; geography dated it **1502**. Per Mikaberidze (2011), p. 468, and McIntosh (2000), pp. 9, 69 to 75, he raided that coast in 1501 and the map legend names Valencia and 1501; **1502 is the year of his return to Constantinople**. Filed as 1501. McIntosh's identification of the captive as an officer or pilot of at least the third voyage is a suggestion only. **[R]**
- **The famous quotation is a paraphrase.** "The infidel of Genoa, whose name was Colombo, was the one who discovered these lands," repeated throughout the Hapgood-derived literature, is **not** in the primary translation. Wikisource, Section V, trans. Akcura (1935), reads "A Genoese infidel, his name was Colombo, he it was who discovered these places," and the file uses that.

**Return to Constantinople, 1502. [A]** **Tripoli, 1510. [A]** The last meeting of uncle and nephew; Spain takes the port the same year.

**The death of Kemal Reis. [A Wikipedia]** Drowned in a storm near Samos while carrying aid toward Egypt; news reaches Cairo by February 1511. **Contradiction, unresolved:** chronology gives 1510, geography records **1510 and 1511 alike**, and the February 1511 news is the only fixed point. His eulogy for his uncle is quoted from Isom-Verhaaren (2022), p. 98, relayed.

## 3. The 1513 map (1511 to 1517)

**Gelibolu, 1511 onward. [A]** He returns after his uncle's death and turns to cartography, beginning both the world map and an early Kitab-i Bahriye. **The map, Muharram 919 AH / March 1513. [A Wikipedia, "Piri Reis map"]** Gazelle-skin parchment; the surviving fragment covers the Atlantic, the coasts of Europe and Africa, and South America, roughly a third of the sheet, the eastern half lost with no copy known.

**The sources legend. [A marginalia, Sections V and VI, trans. Akcura (1935), via Wikisource]** He names his own sources: about twenty older charts and mappae mundi, eight "Jaferiyes" (Ja'fariyya or Geographia-type works), an Arabic map of Hind, four new Portuguese maps, a map by Colombo. Three passages are carried into the file as quotes.

**The southern coastline. [A Wikipedia, reporting scholarly consensus]** The mainstream reading is that it "is most likely a version of Terra Australis," the theoretical southern continent posited by Ptolemy and drawn on most maps of the period.

- **Correction.** Chronology added that the coast is "a distorted South American coastline bent to fit the shape of the gazelle skin," a popular-level explanation **not carried by the cited scholarship**; neither Wikipedia nor McIntosh offers it. Dropped.

**The Barbarossa timber ship. [A Isom-Verhaaren (2022), p. 109; Soucek (2013), p. 142, relayed]** Barbarossa sends a captured timber-laden ship to the capital under Piri Reis's command as an overture to Selim I; it earns two galleys and a robe of honor. **Date disagreement:** chronology said 1516, geography and the monographs say **c. 1515**. Filed as 1515.

**Egypt, 1516 to 1517. [A]** He commands ships blockading Alexandria during the conquest of Mamluk Egypt; Cairo falls in 1517. **The map is presented to Selim I. [A as event; R as to place]** Geography pinned this at Topkapi Palace, the interlock lens at **Cairo** during the campaign season, and **nothing in reach settles it**. Filed at Cairo. **Gap: the map's whereabouts from 1517 to 1929 are unknown.**

## 4. Mutiny, Rhodes, and the two Kitab-i Bahriye (1518 to 1528)

**Chios, February 1518. [A Marino Sanuto, relayed; Pedani (2015), pp. 321 to 322]** He and other captains defect, land 800 men, take a castle, and seize "400 of the sultan's subjects."

- **Correction.** Chronology said the rebellion "collapses by May." It did not: **in May he is still out**, raiding Euboea and the coast near Athens. Kurtoglu Muslihiddin Reis received a fleet command in 1518, but his own relatives were among the rebels, so "command passing" as a response to the revolt is unsupported and is dropped. **Gap:** no motive, no terms of return, no punishment recorded, and he is back in service before 1522.

**Kitab-i Bahriye, first version, 1521. [A Wikipedia]** Roughly 130 chapters offered to the newly enthroned Suleiman with advice for taking Rhodes. **No response of any kind is recorded.**

- **Correction.** The afterlife lens called it "less artistic but more practical" in the judgment of contemporaries. That is **Soucek's modern assessment** (1992, p. 275), and attributing it to contemporaries inverts the evidence. Recast as later scholarship.

**Rhodes, 1522. [A]** He serves in the siege he had argued for in writing.

**The 1524 commission. [A Kitab-i Bahriye epilogue; Soucek (2013), pp. 137 to 138; Casale (2010), pp. 34 to 37, relayed]** Ibrahim Pasha, aboard the flagship Piri Reis commands while sailing to Egypt against Hain Ahmed Pasha's rebellion, asks about the ship's charts and commissions the expanded atlas. **Correction:** geography pinned the conversation at the Golden Horn, but **it happened at sea, on the voyage to Egypt**; repinned to the Egyptian destination, no position at sea being recorded.

**Second version, 1526. [A Wikipedia, "Kitab-i Bahriye"]** About 210 chapters, richer illumination, a longer verse preface, the first detailed Ottoman account of the Indian Ocean, incorporating Vasco da Gama's route to India. **[A]** More than 40 manuscript copies survive from the late 1500s to the 1700s, over 5,700 maps between them, 26 in Istanbul.

**The 1528 world map, 935 AH. [A Wikipedia; McIntosh (2015), p. 313]** More cautious: Greenland, Newfoundland, Florida, Hispaniola set correctly against Cuba, blank parchment where he lacks reliable information. Only a fragment survives.

## 5. The Mediterranean command (1532 to 1546)

All from Wikipedia, "Piri Reis," the only reachable narrative here. **[A]** throughout.

- **1532.** An eight-vessel squadron against pirates in the Adriatic. After Andrea Doria seizes Coron, he besieges the Spanish garrison with ten galleys and five fustas; his galley is struck by return fire and he withdraws.
- **1536.** Seizes the Venetian galley Contarina near Cyprus and refuses to return its precious-stone cargo, claiming Muslim prisoners had been aboard. **Position unrecorded**; the pin is an offshore approximation.
- **1537.** With the Third Ottoman-Venetian War he pursues Venetian shipping and serves under Barbarossa at the siege of Corfu.
- **1546.** Attacks the Venetian ship Liona, killing twelve sailors and wounding twelve; Rustem Pasha finds for him in the dispute.

## 6. Hind Kapudani: the Indian Ocean (1547 to 1552)

- **1547. [A]** Sells his house, garden, and vineyard before departing. **No source gives a reason;** reading it as a man who did not expect to return is later readers', not the record's. **[R]**
- **29 October 1547. [A]** Promoted Hind Kapudani after the death of Yahudi Sinan; departs Suez with about sixty ships. On **26 February 1548** he lands beyond the range of Aden's cannon, puts artillery on the hills above the port, brings the fleet into the crescent bay, and the citadel falls.
- **January 1549. [A]** Burns a Portuguese relief attempt and takes captives; rewarded with a timar reportedly worth 100,000 silver coins. **Engagement site unrecorded.**
- **April 1552. [A]** Departs Suez for Hormuz, Bahrain as secondary objective: twenty-five galleys, five sailing ships, about 850 soldiers. **Correction:** geography made Rustem Pasha's dismissal a condition of the launch, but per Pedani (2015), p. 324, he was dismissed "well after the fleet had left Suez." The lost ally is a consequence during the campaign, not a precondition.

## 7. Hormuz, Basra, and the execution (1552 to 1554)

- **August 1552. [A]** Takes Muscat after a month; the Portuguese garrison is set to row the captured galleys.
- **September to 9 October 1552. [A, with the Portuguese governor's letter of 31 October 1552]** Enters the city of Hormuz but cannot take the fortress; bombards for weeks, runs low on powder, asks Kubad Pasha at Basra for reinforcements that never come, lifts the siege on warning of a counterattack. **Correction:** the relief fleet of forty ships was **organized by** Afonso de Noronha, governor of India, but **led by his nephew Antao de Noronha** (Ozbaran 2009, pp. 110 to 111).
- **October 1552. [A]** Loots Hormuz and plunders Qeshm, carrying off with the earlier haul well over a million gold pieces. **Bahrain [R]** was named in the orders and bypassed; that pin reconstructs a route, not a visit, and its coordinate is corrected to 26.2336, 50.5206 (the supplied pin fell 3.9 km north, offshore).
- **1553. [A]** Reaches Basra; Kubad Pasha refuses galley rowers; he leaves most of the fleet and continues with two ships. A Venetian letter of **15 November** already reports him charged with "having raised the siege of the fortress of Hormuz because of bribery" and replaced, and court histories fault the plundering of Qeshm; Pecevi records that although the charge was implausible, the sultan chose to believe it. **[A Venetian correspondence and Ibrahim Pecevi, both relayed]**

**The execution. [A Wikipedia; Pedani (2015), p. 324; Soucek (2013), p. 143 n. 16]**

- **Correction affecting two lenses.** Chronology and geography both said the beheading was ordered by **the Ottoman governor of Egypt**. It was ordered by **the sultan**: "The sultan had him beheaded in Cairo in late 1553," and Pecevi says the sultan "issued an order of execution."
- **Date contradiction, unresolved.** 16 December 1553 is **Idris Bostan's** identification, relayed via Soucek; older histories give **1554**, and Turkish Wikipedia still prints 1554. **Age at death: 83 to 88**, a function of the unknown birth year.
- **The grave. [A tr.wikipedia]** "Mezarinin yeri bilinmemektedir": the location of his grave is not known. No tomb, no shrine. The execution site within Cairo is also disputed.

**1554. [A]** Seydi Ali Reis tries to bring the remaining fleet home from Basra; the Portuguese intercept it and the ships are captured or destroyed.

## 8. Afterlife (1929 to 2008)

- **1929. [A Wikipedia, "Piri Reis map"]** During the conversion of Topkapi Palace into a museum, a tour by Gustav Adolf Deissmann prompts a library search, and director Halil Edhem Eldem finds "a disregarded bundle of material containing an unusual parchment map." Paul E. Kahle identifies it as Piri Reis's, citing the marginalia's reference to a Columbus map.
- **1929 to 1935. [A / R]** Ataturk takes a personal interest and initiates facsimiles and further research; chronology tagged this **[R]** and afterlife **[A]**, from the same text. A Turkish Historical Society facsimile of the second Kitab-i Bahriye appears in 1935, a four-volume colour facsimile in 1988.
- **1956, 1966. [R as claim, A as publication]** Arlington H. Mallery re-projects the map onto a grid of his own; Charles Hapgood, *Maps of the Ancient Sea Kings*, then argues the southern coastline shows an ice-free Antarctica surveyed by a lost pre-classical civilization, the drawn islands being the ice-covered mountains of **Queen Maud Land**, on the disproven pole-shift hypothesis. **Kept [R].**
- **The rebuttal. [A Wikipedia, citing geologist Paul Heinrich]** Hapgood ignores post-glacial rebound; the 1949 Norwegian-British-Swedish seismic survey (cited first by Mallery) "could not measure even one percent of the area drawn"; Drewry (1983) finds no similarity with the subglacial coastline. **Correction affecting two lenses:** Wikipedia cites a 2024 study putting the ice sheet's formation at about **34 million years ago**, not "roughly ten million" (chronology) or merely "millions of years" (afterlife). Von Daniken and Hancock repeat the claim regardless; the map reaches Eckert's *The HAB Theory* and the Assassin's Creed games.
- **2008. [A]** Piri Reis University founded at Tuzla, Istanbul, 8 February 2008. **Coordinate corrected** to 40.8261, 29.3222 (the supplied pin fell 2.6 km north).
- **Ships and monuments. [A]** RV K. Piri Reis. TCG Pirireis (S-343), a hull number used twice: ex-USS Mapiro (SS-376), transferred 1960, then ex-USS Tang (SS-563), leased 1980, decommissioned 2004; **not** TCG Piri Reis (S-330), commissioned 2024. Statue at Karaman, bust at Gelibolu, coin 1995.

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

Zonchio fleet strengths, 270 vs 150 against 260 vs 95 · the Columbus chart, 1501 against 1502 · Kemal Reis's death, 1510 or 1511 · the Barbarossa timber ship, c. 1515 against 1516 · where the 1513 map was presented, Topkapi Palace against Cairo, neither documented · the execution date, 16 December 1553 against 1554 · age at death, anything from 83 to 88 · the tag on Ataturk's interest, [R] in one lens and [A] in another from the same text.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- **No birth date.** A five-year window with no document behind it.
- **1502 to 1510, and 1528 to 1532.** Years nearly empty of movements.
- **The 1518 revolt.** No motive, no terms of return, no punishment recorded.
- **The map, 1517 to 1929.** Four hundred and twelve years with no sighting or inventory entry. The eastern half is lost with no copy known, so anything said about it is inference from what survives.
- **Three unrecorded sites:** the Contarina seizure (1536), the 1549 Portuguese engagement, the 1554 interception. All three pins are approximations and say so.
- **The grave.** Unknown, the execution site within Cairo disputed, and no reason stated anywhere for the 1547 sale of his house.

## Interlocks with existing atlas files

One traveler is named inside a campa, because Piri Reis stands in a direct documentary relation to him: **columbus**, whose chart he says he obtained from a captured Spanish sailor and whom he names in his own marginalia. The relation runs one way across a generation and is attested in his own hand.

Every other interlock in the pool is **co-location only** and is left unnamed in the prose: **leo_africanus** (Cairo and Alexandria 1517, capture off Djerba 1518, held at Rhodes 1518, no documented contact); **ibn_battuta** (Alexandria and Cairo two centuries earlier); **evliya_celebi** (Topkapi, Jerusalem, Basra a century later, nothing showing him writing about Piri Reis); **kircher** (Malta 1637); **serrano** and **pytheas_de_massalia** (Greenwich Island 1948, linked to the tradition that appropriated the map, not to the man); **cervantes** (queue entry, unresearched).

Canonical pins inherited byte-identical: Cairo **30.0459, 31.2626**; Alexandria **31.2001, 29.9187**; Rhodes **36.4447, 28.2247**; Topkapi **41.0115, 28.9833**; Basra **30.5085, 47.7835**; Rio de la Plata **-34.6037, -58.3816**; Greenwich Island **-62.479, -59.656**. Geography supplied competing pins for three (Cairo Citadel, Qaitbay Citadel, Palace of the Grand Master); the canonical pins win.
