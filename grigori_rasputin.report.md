# Grigori Rasputin (1869-1916): research report

*2026-08-31. Against the verified research pool for `grigori_rasputin`. Companion: `grigori_rasputin.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source hostile testimony · **[C]** contradiction, flagged, not resolved.

A structural note first. Rasputin is the rare subject where the *police* are the best source: the translated Okhrana journal (1 January 1915 to 10 February 1916) is a ledger, not a polemic. Almost everything else divides into two mirrored literatures, monarchist hagiography and the pornographic denunciation of Iliodor, Novoselov and the 1912-1916 press campaign, and where both agree it is usually because both quote the same memoirist. Rule applied here: a claim resting on Yusupov, Iliodor or Vyrubova alone is [R], however often repeated.

## Calendar

Russia ran on the Julian calendar until 1918. The journey declares `calendar: gregorian` and gives New Style dates **where the pool supplied a New Style equivalent**. Otherwise the Old Style date stands as printed and `date_confidence` says so and gives the offset (12 days in the nineteenth century, 13 in the twentieth). Converting dates myself would manufacture precision the sources do not carry.

One live collision, resolved: the pool's chronology gave the departure from Gorokhovaya as 1916-12-16 (O.S.) and the killing at the Moika as 1916-12-30 (N.S.), putting one night two weeks apart. The journey makes both New Style, 29-30 December 1916.

The **Okhrana journal's own dates carry no stated calendar.** Police records of these years are normally Old Style, which would push every entry 13 days later, but the page does not say. Left open, flagged on all seven stops drawn from it.

## Village years, 1869-1897

- Born 9 January 1869 O.S. (21 January N.S.) to the state peasants Yefim and Anna Rasputin at Pokrovskoye, Tobolsk governorate. **[A**, parish baptismal register via the Tyumen regional state archive.**]** Baptized next day, named for Gregory of Nyssa, whose feast falls 10 January. Of roughly seven children, three survive: Dmitri 1895, Matryona 1898, Varvara 1900. **[A]**
- Met Praskovya Fyodorovna Dubrovina on pilgrimage at or near Abalak; married February 1887. "Probably 1886" per the dossier, 1886 outright per en.wikipedia; no register fetched for either event. **[R]** shading to **[A]** on the year. Praskovya and Dmitri dispossessed and deported north in 1930, both dead in that decade: **[R]**, dossier reconstruction, no Soviet-era record checked.
- Farm and carting work on the Tyumen-Tobolsk road; rudimentary adult literacy, all later writing dictated. **[A]** Childhood-visions and wonder-child stories current elsewhere are **omitted**: legendary retrospection, no contemporary source.

## Pilgrimage and formation, 1897-1904

Everything here is **[R]**: the least documented and most retrospectively narrated period, sourced to his own dictated memoir plus biographers reconstructing backward.

- Verkhoturye, Monastery of St Nicholas, c. 1897, and the elder Makary at the nearby Aktai skete. Standard across the literature, no primary source fetched. **Motive is disputed and left disputed**: religious crisis, vow, grief, or flight from a horse-theft accusation.
- Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, late 1890s-1900s: dossier chronology table, from later biography rather than a dated itinerary.
- Mount Athos, St Panteleimon, variously c. 1900, 1903, or later: explicitly the **weakest** journey in the file, no independent source located. Kept with that status in `date_confidence` rather than dropped, since the reported journey is itself a datum about how far his reputation travelled ahead of his feet.
- Kazan patronage c. 1903-1905, opening the Petersburg academy door: dossier only. Year of the first journey to the capital: **1903, 1904, or winter 1904-1905, unresolved [C]**, placed at 1904-01-01 and labelled a placement.

*Zhitie opytnogo strannika* dates itself internally to May 1907 and is treated by the pool as substantially authentic though dictated and tidied by literate followers. Four quotations from it are used. **Gap:** omolenko.com names neither translator nor date, so the English wording is of unknown provenance even where the Russian is genuine.

## Court access and the Khlyst file, 1904-1912

**The first imperial meeting is the pool's sharpest internal contradiction. Resolved here, with the reasoning stated.**

- Nicholas II's diary, 1 November 1905 O.S. (14 November N.S.): "We became acquainted with a man of God - Gregory from Tobolsk province." **[A]**, Russian text at ru.wikipedia; Smith 2016, p. 65 for an English rendering.
- **[C] Place.** The pool's chronology said Peterhof; its geography and quotes lenses said Tsarskoye Selo; en.wikipedia says "the Peterhof Palace." Ru.wikipedia quotes the diary's preceding sentence: the couple drove at four o'clock to **Sergievka**, the Leuchtenberg estate at Old Peterhof, and took tea with Militsa and Stana. The journey follows Sergievka (59.881, 29.863) and records all three readings. Tsarskoye Selo is rejected outright: 40 km from Peterhof, irreconcilable with either alternative.
- Further presentations 18 July and October 1906 O.S., the July occasion the first at which he meets the imperial children. **[A]**, Old Style only. The icon of St Simeon of Verkhoturye reportedly brought: **[A]**, Smithsonian.
- Alexei's haemophilia and Alexandra's turn to his prayers, commonly dated 1907: **[R]**, no primary document fixes it. Attested instead: recurring audiences at Tsarskoye Selo 1906-1916, and that he performed no documented medical procedure and was never the boy's physician.
- **Tobolsk consistory Khlyst case: opened 6 September 1907 O.S. on a denunciation of 1903, closed 7 May 1908 with the bishop declaring him "a Christian man spiritually disposed and seeking the truth of Christ." [A]**, ru.wikipedia. The pool carried only the opening. **The acquittal is the load-bearing half**, the fact most consistently omitted in the polemics; the journey gives it its own stop. Reopened in 1912 after Novoselov's confiscated pamphlet **[R]**, ending the same way.
- December 1911, Hermogenes and Iliodor assault him. **[A]** in outline. **[C] Place:** the pool put the scene in central Petersburg (59.9343, 30.3351); en.wikipedia says "a monastery on Vasilyevsky Island," 4 km west. The journey follows Vasilyevsky Island with an approximate pin, no confirmed address existing. Also from en.wikipedia: the weapon was a crucifix; Hermogenes and Mitya Kozelsky were expelled to Zhyrovichy **without trial**. A castration detail in later retellings is **omitted as unverified**.

## Jerusalem, 1911

- The pilgrimage produced the dictated *Moi mysli i razmyshleniya* (1915). **[A]**, but the documentary anchor is Rachel Mairs's study and **the verifier could not fetch it**: both journal and repository returned bot-protection blocks. The pilgrimage is well attested across the wider literature independently.
- The Mount of Olives / Gethsemane stop present in the pool is **dropped**. No source names it for Rasputin; the pool's own note calls it a plausible stop on a standard pilgrim circuit and nothing more. Recorded here rather than silently deleted. **Port of embarkation is named by no source**: Odessa is used as the standard Russian departure for Jaffa, with the coordinate flagged as not from the pool.

## The knife, the war, the flat, 1912-1915

- Spała, October 1912: the doctors give Alexei almost no chance on 6 October O.S.; Vyrubova telegraphs; Rasputin replies; the boy recovers. **[A]** as to the crisis and the reply's arrival, **[R]** as to the *wording*, which is rendered from Spiridovich's French memoir of 1929 with no original telegram located. Spała's coordinates are village-level and not from the pool.
- Apartment 20, 64 Gorokhovaya Street, from May 1914. **[R]** on the tenancy date. **Coordinate correction, ~1 km:** OSM resolves it to 59.92320, 30.33097. The pool's 59.927, 30.3183 was wrong *and so was its reasoning*: no. 64 stands at the Fontanka end, not near the Sadovaya crossing.
- **Unsupported detail dropped:** the pool asserted he assaulted the porter's wife on the stairs on 2 February 1916, but its own note concedes the printed entry records only a 9.30 a.m. return.
- 12 July 1914 N.S. (29 June O.S.): Khionia Guseva, 33, of Iliodor's circle, stabs him in the abdomen outside his home at Pokrovskoye. **[A]**, en.wikipedia; the British Newspaper Archive account cited by the dossier returned HTTP 403.
- Surgery and prolonged recovery at Tyumen **[A]**; the hospital is named in no source reached, coordinates city-level. Antiwar telegrams from the bed are **[R]**: the position is consistent across accounts, the wording varies by edition, no original located. An archival study traces the wartime recoding of his pacifism into the German-agent charge and finds no evidence of treason: **[A]**.
- The Yar, Moscow, 26 March 1915 O.S. **[R]** and contested. The police report of exposure, boasts of access to the empress, and a fight with staff cost Dzhunkovsky his post; witnesses later recanted and some writers call it manufactured. **The verifier did not fetch the Leiberov and Margolis material on which the entire narrative rests.** **Coordinate correction, ~1.5 km, plus an anachronism:** OSM puts the 1910 Yar building at 55.7848, 37.5694, and in 1915 that address was the **Petersburg Highway**, "Leningradsky Prospekt" not existing until 1924.
- Nicholas II assumes supreme command 23 August 1915 O.S. = **5 September N.S.** The pool's "1915-09" was imprecise; corrected.

## The surveillance year, 1915-1916

All from the translated Okhrana journal, **[A]** as to what the agents wrote, under the calendar caveat above.

- 12 January 1915: 250 roubles a head charged to petitioners for pardons, one sect conviction and one forgery conviction. 9 September 1915: a violent confrontation with his elderly father, bruises, bystanders separating them, nothing further recorded anywhere. 8 February 1916: five unidentified women arriving at 8.15 p.m., singing until morning. 14 January 1916: home at seven "completely overcome with drink."
- Villa Rode and Restaurant Donon among his regular haunts (Donon entries 8 December 1915, 29 January 1916). **Coordinates for both are weak**: the journal names venues, not addresses. Villa Rode's pin is an approximate Kamennoostrovsky placement; Donon stood at Moika 24 (~59.940, 30.321) and the pool's pin lay ~1 km east. The journey merges them into one stop at the Villa Rode pin and says so.
- **Two citation corrections to the pool's quote lens:** (1) "Last year, when I was lying in hospital..." sits under **20 July 1915**, not 20 June, confirmed on two fetch passes; the 20 June entry is an unrelated item about cucumbers and wine. (2) The Vyrubova telegram ("God Himself keeps him in heavenly joy...") sits under **13 January 1916**, not 10 January 1915; the report begins 1 January 1915 and has no 10 January 1915 entry at all.
- 1 November 1916 O.S.: Milyukov's "Stupidity or Treason?" speech **[A]**, pinned in the journey at **Rasputin's own flat, not the Tauride Palace**, since he was not present: the speech is about him, not by him. That November Nicholas tells Alexandra the ministers are his to choose and asks her not to "drag our Friend into this" **[A]**, from letters the verifier did not re-fetch.

## The murder, December 1916

- Night of 16-17 December 1916 O.S. = 29-30 December N.S. Lured from Gorokhovaya to the Moika Palace on the pretext of meeting Princess Irina, who was in the Crimea. **[A]** as to the fact, **[R]** as to everything narrative: the sole source is Yusupov's *Lost Splendor* ch. XXIII, the murderer's self-justifying memoir written decades later.
- **[C] Forensics against memoir, flagged, not merged.** Byard 2024, via PMC: a **contact gunshot wound to the mid forehead**, muzzle imprint abrasion on the lower and right margins, no stippling; **no trace of poison**; two further lethal wounds, liver and kidney; drowning irrelevant, the head wound being immediately fatal and pre-immersion. Byard also notes Rasputin avoided sweet food out of superstition, an argument against the cakes.
- **Correction to the pool's own wording:** the **original autopsy report does not survive.** Byard reasons from Kossorotov's findings, Zharov's 1993 review, and the postmortem photographs, so "the autopsy record shows no trace of poison" overstates what exists. The journey says so.
- Body dropped from the **Bolshoy Petrovsky Bridge** over the Malaya Nevka, recovered ~200 m downstream. **Coordinate correction, ~1.4 km:** 59.9648944, 30.2547472, replacing the pool's 59.9633, 30.2789, which was not on the bridge. No trial ever held; Yusupov exiled to Kursk, Grand Duke Dmitri to the Persian front. **[A]**

## Burial, fire, afterlife

- Buried 21 December 1916 O.S. beside a chapel under construction on Anna Vyrubova's Tsarskoye Selo property, Alexandra and her daughters attending; wife, mistress and children excluded. **[A]** **[C] resolved:** the pool carried both 2 and 3 January 1917 N.S., but 21 December plus 13 days is **3 January 1917** and Wikipedia's "2 January" is an arithmetic slip, not a competing tradition. Chapel coordinates remain an approximate Alexander Park placement; no source pinpoints the site.
- March 1917: exhumed on Kerensky's orders and burned to stop the grave becoming a monarchist shrine. **[A]** as to the act; **site, lorry-breakdown story, and fate of the ashes all disputed and unresolved.** The journey's pin (60.01, 30.38, Lesnoye) is **the weakest-grounded in the file**. The corpse sitting up in the flames is **[R] folklore** with no contemporary source, kept in the campa as a story that rests on nothing.
- 1980: the Pokrovskoye house dismantled "on the eve of Olympics-80" by regional party order. **[A]**, ru.wikipedia, with a Tyumen bibliographic index calling it deliberate erasure. **Coordinate correction, ~56 km:** the pool's 57.75, 66.53 replaced with the museum's 57.240391, 66.791346. Museum founded 1990 by the Smirnovs, Sovetskaya Street 78, in a **different house across the road**: sharper than the pool's "reassessment from 1988-1990." The Gorokhovaya flat is likewise a museum by appointment, **[A]** rasputin-spb.ru, which gives **no opening date**, so the journey's 1990-01-01 is a placement.
- Reception, **[A]** as reception and none of it a stop, since none of it is his own travelling: the 1934 MGM libel judgment (Irina Yusupova's event, not his); Boney M's "Rasputin" (1978) and its 2021 TikTok resurgence; Beevor's 2026 biography, **[C]** on detail, titled ***Rasputin: The Downfall of the Romanovs*** and its 14 April 2026 date unconfirmed.
- Canonization dispute, **[A]** with a **date correction**: the pool dated the church's rejection to February 2003, but Patriarch Alexy II's denunciation quoted by *The Moscow Times* (5 Feb 2003) was made in **December 2001**: "What believer would want to stay in a church that equally venerates murderers and martyrs, lechers and saints?"
- Editions marketed as "Rasputin's Diary" or "Prophecies of Rasputin," including the prophecy of the dynasty falling within two years if nobles killed him, are **[R] posthumous commercial products**, not used. The St Petersburg "preserved relic" is **[R] legend**, omitted.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Rasputin is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework-applied-backward stops are licensed and none were written. Nothing was silently deleted. What was caught, and in which direction:

**Forward direction. None taken as stops.** Five posthumous items sit in the pool's afterlife lens: Byard 2024, the 1934 MGM judgment, the canonization dispute, Boney M 1978, Beevor 2026. A 1916 file cannot embed a 2024 hash as an artifact of his own travelling. Handled as the block rule permits: Byard's findings are **sources and `date_confidence` material on Rasputin's own murder and bridge stops**, the forensic reading being of *his* body at *his* place; the rest sit only in `suggested_refs` and `sources`, never staged as scenes. **No operator action needed unless you disagree with the Byard placement.**

**Backward direction: none.** Rasputin has no theoretical apparatus to apply to anything.

**Co-location-only relations dropped from the campas** (the standing rule, not the exception). The interlock lens offered Lenin (same city, 1905 and 1917), Diaghilev (same court, 1909), Gurdjieff (parallel healer-hypnotist), Houdini (anti-spiritualist campaigner), Ahad Ha'am (same Jerusalem pin). **Every one is shared geography or typology with no documented contact, correspondence, influence, or citation in either direction**, as the pool concedes on each ("my inference, not stated by either file"; "there is no documented contact between Houdini and Rasputin"). None is named in a campa and `interlocks` is empty.

One **canonical pin inherited byte-identical**: Jerusalem, **31.778, 35.2354**, from `ahad_ha_am.journey.json`, over the Holy Sepulchre's own 31.7783, 35.2297.

## Honest gaps

1. Praskovya's and Dmitri's fate in Soviet exile: reconstruction only, no Soviet-era record consulted.
2. The omolenko.com translations: translator and date unnamed. All four Rasputin self-quotations inherit this.
3. **Eight cited sources were unreachable or unfetched**, listed with reasons under Sources. Three carry weight alone: Leiberov and Margolis (the whole Yar narrative), Mairs (the Jerusalem pilgrimage), tumentoday.ru (the baptismal register).
4. Three coordinates are weak by their own admission: the Aktai skete, Villa Rode, and above all the March 1917 cremation site, which no source fixes.
5. The last ten months of his life are far more thinly documented than the preceding thirteen: the surveillance journal stops on 10 February 1916 and nothing replaces it.
