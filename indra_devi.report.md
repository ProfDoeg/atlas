# Indra Devi: research report

*Eugenia Vasilievna Peterson · Eugenie Strakaty · Indra Devi · "Mataji". Riga, 12 May 1899, to Buenos Aires, 25 April 2002. Compiled 2026-08-31.*

**[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, institutional testimony, or single unverifiable retelling. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason. The pool's own verification pass corrected its first pass in twenty-two places, several of them cases where two lenses over the *same* sentence tagged one fact [A] and [R]; those appear below as lens conflicts.

## 1. Riga and Russia, 1899-1920

- **Birth [A].** Riga, Governorate of Livonia, 12 May 1899 (en.wikipedia; Hinduism Today; Live History India). Father **Vasili Peterson**, Swedish bank director; mother **Aleksandra Labunskaia**, a Russian noblewoman who acted at the **Nezlobina Theatre**. Baptised Russian Orthodox (Hinduism Today). Coordinates 56.9496, 24.1052 confirmed. **[R]:** that she kept the baptismal cross all her life is dossier only.
- **Petrograd gymnasium, 1917, gold medal [A]** (en.wikipedia). **Lens conflict:** the geography lens tagged this identical fact [R], "dossier lead only," while citing the same page. It is [A].
- **Moscow drama school [A in outline, R in detail].** en.wikipedia: she "briefly attended drama school in Moscow." The **Komissarzhevsky** attribution and his supposed prediction of stardom are unconfirmed.
- **Flight, 1920-1921.** [A] Fled the revolution, in Berlin by 1921 (en.wikipedia); Hinduism Today gives 1920 for leaving Russia. [R] The Warsaw leg and travelling with her mother: dossier only. **Gap:** no school record, transit document or parish register located, so this section rests on encyclopaedia summary plus one obituary.

## 2. Berlin and the forgotten call, 1921-1928

- **Berlin from 1921 [A]** (en.wikipedia). **Der Blaue Vogel [R]:** she is everywhere said to have toured with the Blue Bird Russian cabaret. livehistoryindia, cited for it at [A] in the first pass, **does not mention the company**.
- **Krishnamurti: a live contradiction, not resolved.** es.wikipedia, verbatim: "Conoció a Jiddu Krishnamurti en 1926, durante una de sus charlas en Ommen, Holanda" **[A]**. livehistoryindia instead places it in **Berlin** in the 1920s and attaches the *same* recollection to it **[A]**. Neither is dropped: the file puts the stop at Ommen, which fixes a year, and states the Berlin variant in `date_confidence`. The pool had that one quote doing duty in two stops; the duplication is removed.
- **Nomenclature [A].** In 1926 the body was still the **Order of the Star in the East**, renamed in June 1927; Krishnamurti dissolved it at Ommen on 3 August 1929 before Besant and some three thousand members (en.wikipedia).
- **Hermann Bolm [R].** Engagement, the condition that she see India first, the funded voyage, the returned ring: her own retrospective account via the dossier and *Vokrug Sveta*. Bolm's papers have never been consulted by anyone.
- **First India voyage, 1927 [A for the year]** (livehistoryindia; Dawn). The sailing date, route and duration are her own telling.

## 3. India, the name, the marriage, 1928-1937

- **Adyar and Nehru [R, downgraded].** The dossier placed her at the Theosophical Society's Adyar headquarters, dancing and meeting Nehru. **Neither cited source supports it:** Time mentions neither Nehru nor Adyar, and the Hinduism Today obituary names Nehru nowhere. Her entry into that network after 1926 is attested in general; this ground is not. Her phrase *влюбленной дружбой* ("an amorous friendship") for Nehru appears only in *Vokrug Sveta*, and is carried as her own words, not as a documented relationship.
- **Stage name, 1930 [R].** en.wikipedia gives 1930 and *Sher-e-Arab*, and that is the whole support. Time mentions no film and no stage name; IMDb tt0241947 returned **HTTP 403**; the director attribution to **Bhagwati Prasad Mishra** is corroborated by nothing.
- **Marriage to Jan Strakaty, 1930 [A].** en.wikipedia: "Czechoslovak commercial attaché in Bombay." He was a *commercial attaché*, not a consular official; Time does not mention him; "roughly forty years old" is unsourced.
- **The illness [R].** The heart trouble preceding her turn to yoga is known from her memoir alone, which Goldberg treats as partly self-mythologising.

## 4. Mysore: Krishnamacharya, 1937-1938

- **Study under Tirumalai Krishnamacharya [A].** en.wikipedia dates it 1938 and calls her his first foreign woman pupil; Hinduism Today puts her at "the Maharaja of Mysore's palace" with **Iyengar** and **Pattabhi Jois** as fellow students. Sources differ between **1937 and 1938**; not adjudicated.
- **Correction, the building.** The dossier pinned the school at **Amba Vilas**, the main palace (12.3052, 76.6552). The yogashala opened 11 August 1933 in the **Jaganmohan Palace**, 200 m west. The file uses both: Amba Vilas for the patronage, **12.3062, 76.6522 inherited byte-identical from `pattabhi_jois.journey.json`** for the school.
- **Correction, a quotation that was not one.** The pool had his students carrying his "classical postures" west. That phrase **does not appear** on the cited page.
- **The Maharaja's intervention [R].** That **Krishnaraja Wodeyar IV** personally overruled Krishnamacharya's refusal to teach a foreign woman is repeated everywhere and confirmed by nothing fetched. Attested, from `pattabhi_jois.journey.json`: the patronage itself, from 1926.

## 5. Shanghai and the war, 1939-1947

- **Classes at Soong Mei-ling's house, 1939 [A].** en.wikipedia calls them among the first yoga classes taught in China, at Madame Chiang Kai-shek's house; Hinduism Today independently names the house. "First yoga school in China" is narrowed to **modern postural yoga**.
- **Occupation years [R].** Continuity of teaching follows from the 1939 start and 1947 departure; nothing documents the intervening years, and the **free orphanage classes** are dossier only.
- **Death of Jan Strakaty, 1946 [A for the fact; gap for the place].** **No source gives a location.** The pool's "Europe (traveling)" is unsupported; the stop sits at the household's Shanghai pin and says in its confidence field that this is not an attested place of death.
- **The first book [A, upgraded].** *Yoga: The Technique of Health and Happiness*, Allahabad: Kitabistan, 1948, bylined "Eugenie Strakaty (Indira Devi)": confirmed against the **Bauman Rare Books** listing, which the first pass had marked unfetched. The **1946 composition date is unsourced**. **Contradiction:** Open Library dates the same title to **1984**.
- **Sailing to San Francisco, 1947 [A]** (Time, Dawn; Time gives six thousand dollars in savings). The ship name, **USS General W. H. Gordon**, is from en.wikipedia, unchecked against a manifest.

## 6. Hollywood, 1948-1959

- **8806 Sunset Boulevard, 1948 [A, superlative flagged].** en.wikipedia: a dedicated studio, first in Los Angeles. Dawn says first in the **United States**, which the dossier itself calls promotional overstatement and Goldberg disputes. Street number unverified. **Students [A]:** Swanson, Garbo, Gabor (es.wikipedia).
- **The Huxleys [A].** Time attests the visits to Aldous and Maria Huxley, the talk of diet and pesticides, her later quoting of Huxley on nonattachment, and the Bernardine Fritz circle.
- **Elizabeth Arden [R, negative].** That Arden's salons took up Devi-related yoga **could not be confirmed at all**: Arden's own Wikipedia page mentions neither Devi nor yoga.
- **1953 [A].** *Forever Young, Forever Healthy* and marriage to **Dr. Sigfrid Knauer** (en.wikipedia; Open Library, four editions). Open Library records **no publisher**, so the **Prentice-Hall** attribution is dropped. **1959 [A]:** *Yoga for Americans*, five editions plus an ebook.
- **Citizenship and legal name [A as mid-1950s only].** en.wikipedia says only "in the mid-1950s". **The pool's "1955" is not in the source.** The stop uses 1955-01-01 as a placeholder inside the attested range and says so.

## 7. Moscow, Tecate, Puttaparthi, 1960-1984

- **USSR, 1960 [A for the visit; nearly all detail fails].** en.wikipedia attests the visit and meetings with ministers **Gromyko** and **Kosygin**. Unsupported by anything fetched: a Leningrad leg, a Finland routing, a companion named Balu Krishna, ambassador K. P. S. Menon, and the Hotel Sovetskaya reception, whose pooled coordinates were also ~900 m off. Time's Moscow material concerns a **1990s** trip and was mis-cited for this one.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** Hinduism Today: the 1960 conference "led to the granting of legal status for the teaching of yoga in Russia." The dossier and modern scholarship call this greatly exaggerated: Soviet yoga stayed restricted until perestroika. Both carried.
- **Tecate, 1961-1977 [A, upgraded].** en.wikipedia confirms both ends. **Unlocated:** price, deed, enrolments. **Bangalore, 1977 [A, upgraded]:** the 1983 end of that period is inferred from es.wikipedia's Sri Lanka departure.
- **Sathya Sai Baba, 1966 [A].** en.wikipedia and Hinduism Today both date the devotion from 1966 and the reframing as **"Sai Yoga"**. Ashram coordinate corrected to **14.1652, 77.8117**, 1.3 km east of the pool's rounded figure. Salon's 2001 reporting fixes the location and **does not mention Devi**.
- **Knauer's death, Sri Lanka, 1984 [A, upgraded].** en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia and Hinduism Today agree; the secondary misdating to 1977 is contradicted by all three and set aside. **Gap:** no source says where on the island; the stop uses Colombo explicitly as a country marker.

## 8. Argentina, 1982-2002

- **First visit, 1982 [A].** Hinduism Today confirms it and that she "fell in love with the country and its people." **Lens conflict:** the geography lens tagged the stop [R] against its own note. Only the "extraordinary public demand" framing is unverified.
- **Permanent settlement [A, year disputed].** en.wikipedia gives **1985**; Hinduism Today implies it followed directly on the 1984 death. Both stand. The three-countries formulation, Russia by birth, India by spirit, Argentina by love, is Hinduism Today's.
- **International Yoga Federation, 1987 [A, city on one source].** en.wikipedia: "1987 (Montevideo, Uruguay)". es.wikipedia corroborates year and country but **not the city**. SciELO adds that the Federación was founded by Devi with **Swami Maitreyananda** and **Vayuananda**. Organisation names differ in every institutional source.
- **Panama [R, the weakest material in the file].** [A] Roberto Díaz Herrera, retired June 1987, broke with the Panamanian Defense Forces command and said he acted "at the behest of Sathya Sai Baba" (en.wikipedia). [R] Devi as connector, via the singer **Piero**, at Defense Forces expense: dossier only, citing a Princeton ephemera folder and a CIA FOIA request, neither fetched. **Documented gap:** `manuel_noriega.journey.json` never mentions Díaz Herrera, Piero or Devi. **F-2010-01317** was filed by Goldberg *seeking* records: research effort, not a file.
- **Fundación Indra Devi [A; founders disputed].** Indra Devi International gives **1988** (not "1987-1988") and records that it ran **until 2020**. **Contradiction, both carried:** Hinduism Today names **David Lifar** as co-founder, **SciELO** credits **Maitreyananda** and never mentions the Lifars. The pool's "still operates" is wrong: the successor entity does.
- **First Argentine Yoga Congress, late 1980s [A for the event; city inferred].** SciELO records Devi presiding with Maitreyananda, treats it as pivotal for Argentina's yoga lineages, and credits her with insisting yoga is "a method for physical, mental and spiritual development," not a religion. **It does not say where the congress met**; the Buenos Aires pin is an assumption.
- **Riga and Moscow, 1990 [R].** The Riga return is dossier only. Time attests a Moscow trip **in the 1990s**, with **no year** and **no programme named**; Molchanov's *Do i posle polunochi* is dossier material. The pool's [A] on "1990" overstated its source.
- **United Nations, June 1992 [R]** and **Argentine Congress, 8 March 1999 [R].** Both are fetchable verbatim at indradevi-international.net.ar, which the first pass wrongly called "not independently located"; that URL is now cited. [R] stands for both: no UN programme and no congressional record was found. **Lens conflict:** the afterlife lens tagged the 1999 event [A], two others [R]. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's -34.6083, -58.3712 is the **Plaza de Mayo**, 1.9 km off; the file uses **-34.6096, -58.3926**.
- **Centenary, May 1999 [R].** The "more than three thousand" attendance is the foundation's own figure.
- **Death, 25 April 2002 [A].** Hinduism Today, Dawn and Time all report death in Buenos Aires at 102, with a **stroke in February 2002** and a **three-day vigil** before cremation, following her teaching that the soul takes three days to detach. **Contradiction:** Dawn's dateline gives **26 April**. No official cause was disclosed.
- **Ashes [A, coordinate reconciled].** Hinduism Today: "her body was cremated and her ashes scattered in Río de la Plata." The pool carried **two coordinates ~35 km apart** for one event; the shore-adjacent **-34.55, -58.35** is used, once. **No monument [R]:** no tomb, marker, statue or plaque was located anywhere, and a search of Latvian and Mexican local sources was not possible.

## 9. Historiography

- **Michelle Goldberg, *The Goddess Pose*** (Knopf, 2015) is the first major modern biography; not read this pass.
- **YogaCityNYC, "Breaking Myths," 20 June 2016 [A, corrected].** An **interview with Goldberg, not a review**. It supports the substance (she "never let anything build up around her") but **does not name Iyengar or Jois** as the comparison. The first pass's `/postings/` URL 404s.
- **The self-mythologising critique [R, softened].** The pool claimed Goldberg and "an academic review" puncture a "first Western woman to teach yoga in India" superlative. **Neither cited page carries that critique**, and the review is not among the sources cited.
- **Bibliography contradiction.** Against the pool's four Spanish titles with years, the cited source lists **five**, **omits *Sai Baba y yo***, attaches no "Sai Yoga" subtitle, and **gives no years**. Those years are not carried into the journey file.

## 10. Quotations: provenance

- **Five come from one popular Russian magazine [A]**, *Vokrug Sveta* 7175, which names no primary source: "Йога! Индия!…", "Там мой дом", "влюбленной дружбой" (single-source for Nehru), the mother's dialogue and Rau's line. Two carry corrections: «Индия… Кто у тебя там?» / «Никого» is a **two-turn dialogue**, not one string, and is set in **Petersburg**, impossible for a 1928 departure, as the stop says; and "Вы и так все это уже знаете" is **spoken by Enakshi Rama Rau, not Devi**.
- **Five come from Hinduism Today (2002) [A].** "I do not belong to any religion…" is in the **main text**, not the "In Her Own Sweet Words" box as the pool had it, and BrainyQuote is downstream of it. Four are from that box: the "art and science of living" passage (its trailing ellipsis unnecessary, so the full text is used), "Movements in yoga are harmonious…", "Nonviolence is one of the keys…", and "We women must listen to our inner voice…", the last **upgraded** from [R] since the full passage is there and not aggregator-only.
- Also [A]: "…a forgotten call…" via Elliott Goldberg (2016) pp. 338-339, whose pages were not reached; two from Time; one habitual remark from YogaCityNYC; the foundation's "Mataji abandonó su cuerpo físico…". [R]: "I try to live in the eternal now…", Time, from Goldberg. And "Like water which can clearly mirror the sky…" **[R]**, aggregator-only and **untraceable** to any page of her books, whose Internet Archive scans are access-restricted; carried as tradition, stated in `quote_source`.

## 11. Interlocks used, and interlocks refused

Named in a campa, because Devi had a real relation to the person: **`jiddu_krishnamurti`**, whom she heard speak and to whom she dated the turn of her life, Ommen pin **52.5233, 6.4225**; **`annie_besant`**, named once at Adyar as president of the Society whose Order Devi followed to Ommen, an **institutional, not personal, relation**, since no source puts the two women in a room together; **`pattabhi_jois`**, fellow pupil under the same teacher in the same years, pins **12.3062, 76.6522** and **12.3052, 76.6552**; **`madame_chiang_kai_shek`**, in whose house Devi taught, pin **31.2304, 121.4737**. All inherited byte-identical.

Refused, because shared geography is not a relation, though their pins are still inherited byte-identical: **`swami_vivekananda`** (the lens's Mysore-dynasty link joins Mysore to the West, not Devi to Vivekananda, whom she never cited); **`blavatsky`, `alexandra_david_neel`, `keyserling`** (all at Adyar, decades apart); **`max_perelman`, `gita_galina`, `benzion_witler`** (the Riga Yiddish stage circuit, "geographic and generational, not social"); **`manuel_noriega`**, pin **8.9824, -79.5199** (her relation runs to Díaz Herrera); **`victoria_ocampo`**, pin **-34.6037, -58.3816**, used at every Buenos Aires stop without naming her.

**Paris** and the **Kaaba/Temple Mount** canonical pins were checked and no attested intersection exists; left open rather than populated.

## 12. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Indra Devi is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no apparatus relation was found that would need her added.** She is not a theorist; nothing in the pool read a later apparatus back onto her, or hers forward onto anything.

Two near-misses are recorded so nothing is deleted silently, and both resolve as ordinary sourcing. **Backward-facing, legal as a source:** the later commentators (both Goldbergs, SciELO, YogaCityNYC) sit on her own stops as `sources` and `suggested_refs`; none is staged as a scene, and no stop was created for anyone else's visit or research trip. **Forward-facing, refused:** the Mysore-dynasty two-exports argument belongs to `pattabhi_jois.journey.json` and is not re-staged here.

Direction caught: **none requiring a licence.** No addition to `EXCEPTIONS.md` is proposed.
