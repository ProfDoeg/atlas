# Sir Francis Baring, 1st Baronet (1740-1810): research report

*2026-08-31. Prepared for `francis_baring.journey.json`. Devon serge merchant's son; founder of the house that became Baring Brothers & Co.; East India Company director 1779-1810 and chairman 1792-93; MP 1784-1806; pamphleteer on tea duties and paper money; the credit behind the Louisiana Purchase bonds.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim at one remove from a source I could not open. Contradictions are flagged and left open, not adjudicated.

## 1. Devon, 1740-1755

**Birth, 18 April 1740, at Larkbeare, near Exeter.** [A on date and parentage; R on siting.] Son of Johann Baring, a Bremen wool merchant naturalized as John, and Elizabeth Vowler Baring (Wikipedia, following Orbell's ODNB life).

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: which Larkbeare.** Two candidates: Larkbeare in Talaton parish, about eleven miles north-east of Exeter (Talaton centre 50.783 / -3.317), and Larkbeare House on Exeter's southern edge, commemorated by Larkbeare Road, EX2. Wikipedia's 'John Baring (merchant)' calls it the father's "country estate just outside Exeter", ODNB-derived accounts "near Exeter"; neither fits Talaton at eleven miles. The incoming coordinate 50.781 / -3.369 matches *neither*, falling in open country 3.7 km west of Talaton. **It is not published**: the file falls back to the Exeter city pin, 50.7184 / -3.5339. Exeter Memories returned 404; an open gap.

**Death of John Baring, 30 October 1748.** [A] Francis is eight; Elizabeth Vowler Baring manages the business thereafter.

## 2. London, early 1750s-1762

**Two schools instead of a university.** [A on the schools; R on the year.] Mr Fargue's French school at Hoxton, then Mr Fuller's academy in Lothbury. **The hard date 1751 is unsourced** and has been dropped: the sources say only "the early 1750s", and the Hoxton stop is dated 1751-01-01 purely to order the segment. **Coordinate correction:** the incoming 51.534 / -0.085 lands about 500 m WNW of Hoxton proper; corrected to 51.5324 / -0.0783. Lothbury 51.5147 / -0.0894 unchanged.

**Apprenticed to Samuel Touchet, 1755, seven years, premium reported at £800.** [A] Touchet was a Manchester cotton manufacturer turned London merchant and MP, with West India business and slaving voyages.

**The slave-trade origin legend.** [R] Wikipedia carries it flatly: "It is said he made his fortune from the slave trade while only 16 years old." The rebuttal is Dresser and Hann, *The Slavery Connections of Northington Grange* (Historic England, 2013), which reportedly found Baring absent from Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database ownership records and judged investment by an apprenticed clerk "extremely unlikely". **historicengland.org.uk returned repeated HTTP 403, so the rebuttal is known only through paraphrase and stays [R].** Peter Fryer's *Staying Power* (1984) is named as the origin of its modern circulation; not checked. **Date correction:** Touchet's *Lydia* sailed in **1757**, not 1756, making Baring seventeen; the *Favourite* sailed 1761.

**Apprenticeship completed Christmas Day 1762**, articles signed the same day founding John & Francis Baring & Co. (London) and John & Charles Baring & Co. (Exeter). [A]

## 3. The City, 1763-1778

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: where the house opened.** The incoming chronology and geography both put the opening on **Mincing Lane, 1 January 1763**. Wikipedia, 'Barings Bank' says the firm "started in offices off Cheapside" and "moved to Mincing Lane within a few years", and dates the founding to 1762. **So the 1763 opening did not happen at Mincing Lane, and the precise date is unsourced.** The file opens the house off Cheapside (51.5138 / -0.0950), dates it 1763-01-01 as an ordering device, and states both problems; Mincing Lane (51.5108 / -0.0817) gets a later stop. The **6 Mincing Lane** street number of the residence is corroborated by nothing fetched and is not asserted [R].

**Marriage to Harriet Herring at St John the Baptist, Croydon, 12 May 1767.** [A] Twelve children, ten to adulthood. Archbishop Thomas Herring, a relative of the bride, is buried in the same church: a coincidence, not a claimed link.

**Court of the Royal Exchange Assurance, 1771-1780** [A], with marine underwriting at Lloyd's, then housed in the same building [A for the location; the underwriting is ODNB-derived and no individual risk is itemized anywhere consulted]. **First North American correspondent account, 1774** [A], opening the network that ran through Willing, Morris, and Bingham. **Dissolution of the interlocking partnerships, 1777** [A], after disputes over brother Charles's management of the Exeter house.

**Two quotes upgraded from [R] to [A]-secondary.** The incoming lens carried "wild, strange, incoherent" and "almost monstrous" as untraceable. Both are traceable and both fragments were incomplete: Charles's speculations were "of a wild, strange, incoherent description" (Northbrook MSS D1), and Francis was scandalized by his "almost monstrous management of the original and chief dependence of the Baring family" (D7). **Cite as archive-quoted-in-ODNB, never as primary letters read directly.**

## 4. Leadenhall Street and Westminster, 1779-1793

**East India Company director from 1779 to his death, chairman for the 1792-93 term including the 1793 charter renewal.** [A]

**Two more quotes upgraded to [A]-secondary.** On the India Bills of North and Fox: "India is not a colony and God forbid that it ever should be" (Northbrook MSS N4.5). On the Court of Directors during the chairmanship: "The more I work, the greater the degree of jealousy and difficulty I have to encounter amongst the directors who are in general composed of either knaves or fools" (Baring to Lord Lansdowne, DEP193.17.1). Both after Orbell's ODNB; the original reads "either knaves or fools", against the incoming fragment "knaves or fools". Neither is a speech or a publication: both are manuscript utterances and must be cited as such.

**Confidential commercial adviser to Shelburne from 1782 [A]; presence at Bowood [R].** **No source consulted places Francis physically at Bowood House**, and the sobriquet "Prince of Merchants" reaches us through the dossier only; the stop says so.

**Grampound, 1784, reportedly about £3,000** [A for the return; gap on presence]. Whether Baring set foot in the borough, as against buying the seat through agents, is not established. **Ilchester, 1790** [R]: a failed contest there, on ODNB authority, but **Wikipedia's Ilchester article says nothing of a 1790 election and History of Parliament Online returned HTTP 401**, so it is carried as attributed, not established; coordinate corrected to 51.0011 / -2.6825. **Later seats** [A]: Chipping Wycombe 1794-96 and 1802-06, Calne 1796-1802, both Lansdowne boroughs.

**The Commutation Act pamphlet, 1786.** [A] **Edition correction:** archive.org item `principleofcommu00bariuoft` is the **second** edition (London: J. Sewell, 1786), not the third. Verified quotes: "The Author of these Sheets writes neither from party views, nor upon party principles" (p. 5), and, facing the balance table, "the Publick purchase their Tea for £2,055,462. less than was formerly paid by them" (pp. 13-14; the original spells it *Publick*).

**The cochineal corner, 1787-88.** [A for the operation; R for Baring's presence in Amsterdam.] Roughly £450,000 deployed with Hope & Co. through agents from Cadiz to St Petersburg; prices did not move; the partners renounced a year's profit share. **Coordinate corrected:** 52.3702 / 4.8859 is Amsterdam's Dam, not the Keizersgracht address its own label named; Hope & Co. stood at Keizersgracht 444-446, approx. **52.366 / 4.887**.

**Devonshire Square, 1793** [A on the year]. The incoming "11 Devonshire Square" is corroborated by nothing fetched; the square is carried without a number. **Baronet, 29 May 1793.** [A]

## 5. The American business, 1795-1804

**Barbary loan, 1795.** [R] Up to $800,000 in United States six-percent stock marketed toward negotiations with Algiers, Tunis, and Tripoli, funds advanced ahead of the sale. Corroborated only in outline; no primary document opened. **The Maine lands** [A, corrected]: Alexander Baring purchased **over a million acres in late 1795 for £107,000**, the incoming "early 1796" and lower bound of £90,500 being unsupported.

**Louisiana, 1803-04.** [A] Francis Baring & Co., on Francis's authorization and credit, contracted with Hope & Co. to buy and distribute the $11.25 million tranche of United States six-percent bonds; negotiations concluded in Paris at the end of April 1803; 10 million francs followed in July 1803 and a further 40.35 million in April 1804, discharging the obligation.

**Stop-ownership ruling applied, three times.** Alexander went to Maine and Francis did not, so there is **no Maine stop**. The **Bingham marriages** (1798 and 1802) happened in the United States, so they are reported at Francis's London desk. **Alexander Baring and Pierre César Labouchère negotiated Louisiana in Paris; Francis never travelled there**, and the incoming geography lens rightly holds no Paris entry for him, so there are **no Paris stops**. In all three the business sits at Francis's own London pin.

**Retirement and the firm's name.** [A, with a correction.] Francis began surrendering his profit share in 1803 and retired as an active partner in 1804, leaving roughly £70,000 of capital in the business. **The incoming chronology dates the renaming to 1806 and names two entering sons. Both are wrong.** The firm became Baring Brothers & Co. in **1804**, when **Thomas, Alexander and Henry** joined as partners, a name kept until 1890. **The 1806 move to 8 Bishopsgate is a separate event**, with no 1807 component. No Bishopsgate stop is carried: no coordinate for the address exists in the verified pool and I decline to invent one.

## 6. Money and paper, 1797-1810

**The 1797 suspension and the pamphlets.** [A] *Observations on the Establishment of the Bank of England, and on the Paper Circulation of the Country* and *Further Observations*, both 1797, read from archive.org item `bim_eighteenth-century_further-observations-on-_baring-francis-sir_1797`, the copy inscribed "To Jeremy Bentham". **Two quote corrections, both material:**

- "the Bank **were** not insolvent, they had more than failed, and had passed beyond the line of bankruptcy": the printed page reads **"was"** (*Further Observations*, p. 16, leaf n17). The variant "the Bank were not insolvent" does occur, in a different sentence on p. 15.
- "the very bitter pill which the **Nation** has been forced to swallow": the printed page reads **"the public"** (p. 16, above FINIS). The archive.org OCR truncates the line to "the N has been forced to swallow", almost certainly where "Nation" came from: an OCR artifact already hardened into a quotation.

**The 1801 answer to Walter Boyd** [A], item `observationsonpu00bariuoft`. Verified: "The Bank of England is, to the Agriculture, Commerce, and Finance of Great Britain, its sun"; "The ignorant, and more particularly the half-informed, are too much disposed to place confidence in a name". **Bullion Committee testimony, 1810** [R]: no primary transcript opened, and likewise the Fouché peace feelers reaching Baring through Labouchère, which are a dossier synthesis of Hidy and the Baring Archive. Both [R] in the stop.

## 7. Land, family, and death, 1796-1810

**Manor House, Lee, Kent, 1796.** [A, three corrections.] Price **£20,000 flat**, the range "£20,000 to £27,000" being unsupported. Vendor **Mrs Angerstein, formerly Mrs Lucas**, not Joseph Paice as trustee for Thomas Lucas, who was the 1770s developer. Coordinate: 51.446 / 0.014 is about 1.2 km south of the site, corrected to **51.456 / 0.006**, the same correction applying to the death stop.

**Stratton Park, Hampshire, 1801.** [A, corrected.] Bought from the Russell heirs in 1801, firm; the "circa 1800-1802" hedging is dropped. Dance remodelled the house 1803-06 with a stone Doric portico, and **Humphry Repton laid out the park from c.1803**, missing from the incoming chronology. **The £150,000 is not a purchase price:** it is cumulative expenditure by 1803. Coordinate corrected from 51.131 / -1.2445 to **51.156 / -1.225**.

**Hill Street, Mayfair, 1802** [A], 51.5069 / -0.1498. **Lloyd's Patriotic Fund, chairman from 1803** [A]. **Estate at death approximately £606,000** [A], including a Dutch and Flemish Old Master collection. **Harriet Baring dies at Bath, 4 December 1804** [A on the fact; gap on presence]: whether Francis was there is not established, and his remembered words about her "superior" mind reach us through the ODNB-derived account only, so the phrase is **not** used as a quote field.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: the London Institution.** The biography gives Baring the presidency "from 1805 until his death"; Wikipedia's 'London Institution' dates the body's establishment to **18 January 1806**, at 8 Old Jewry at £350 a year. 1805 is at best a subscription year. **Site correction:** the incoming 51.5155 / -0.0922 is Coleman Street, i.e. the **post-1812** building (the Institution moved there in 1812 and to Finsbury Circus in 1815, both after Baring's death). The stop uses 8 Old Jewry, approx. **51.5142 / -0.0919**, dated 1806-01-18.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: the date of death.** Most modern sources give **11 September 1810** at Lee, Kent; the historic Hansard person index gives **12 September**. Both are stated; neither is chosen. The *Gentleman's Magazine* obituary also gives his age as 74, which does not agree with a birth date of 18 April 1740 (he was 70). **Burial, 20 September 1810, St Mary the Virgin, Micheldever** [A], coordinate 51.1486 / -1.2656; the afterlife lens's 51.1425 / -1.2478 is about 1.4 km out and inconsistent with its own geography lens.

**The "first merchant in Europe" quotation: attribution corrected.** [A on the words; the attribution is the DNB's.] Primary source located, *Gentleman's Magazine*, vol. 80 pt. 2 (September 1810), p. 293: "At his death, he was unquestionably the first merchant in Europe; first in knowledge and talents, and first in character and opulence." **This is the magazine's own anonymous obituary. Lord Erskine is not named in it, nor anywhere in the September or October 1810 issues in connection with Baring.** "In the words of Lord Erskine" is the DNB's phrase (1885-1900). The journey file quotes the magazine and notes the misattribution in `quote_source`.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Francis Baring is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Several relations surfaced by the research are not rooted in his own life and are **kept here and excluded from the journey file rather than silently deleted**. In every case the direction caught is **forward**, which the exceptions file never licenses on an agent's own judgement.

**Direction: FORWARD (not licensable without the author's say-so).**

1. **Four posthumous merchant-banking parallels:** Nathan Rothschild's 1825 bullion rescue of the Bank of England, paired with Baring's 1810 Bullion Committee testimony; James Rothschild's 1812 specie-running inside Napoleon's blockade; David Sassoon at Bushehr, 1829; the Sassoon-Rothschild marriage in Paris, 1887. New hashes in an old block, every one.
2. **Nick Leeson and the 1995 collapse of Barings Bank**, offered as a circulating conflation with Francis Baring's biography. No independently fetched source performing the conflation could be located, and Wikipedia's Baring article does not mention 1995. Doubly excluded: forward by 185 years, and unverified as a legend.
3. **The 2024 scholarly wave.** Mark Edward Hay, *Transatlantic Finance in the Age of Revolutions* (Springer, 2024), confirmed via Open Library. Larry Neal, *The Forgotten Financiers of the Louisiana Purchase*, found in neither Open Library nor Google Books (429); [R], not to be carried as a published title without a catalogue check.
4. **The 1963 demolition of Stratton Park** by John Baring, 7th Baron Ashburton, leaving only Dance's portico (listed 1983; park Grade II 1984). Afterlife of an object Francis commissioned, so carried as a `suggested_ref` on his Stratton stop, not as a scene. The incoming "four centuries of family ownership" is out by an order of magnitude: 162 years.

**Wrong owner, moved off this file entirely.** **The Grange, Northington, Hampshire**, where the incoming afterlife lens anchored the slave-trade legend. **Alexander Baring, 1st Baron Ashburton, bought it from Henry Drummond in 1817, seven years after Francis died.** Francis never owned or visited it, so the place belongs to Alexander; the legend stays on Francis's file at Touchet's countinghouse, where Francis actually was. (The incoming coordinate 51.1633 / -1.1633 was also about 5 km out; Wikipedia gives 51.1220 / -1.1990.)

**Shared geography only, deliberately not named in any campa.** Miranda in Paris 1792; Lafayette in Paris from 1789; Franklin at Passy from 1777; Adam Smith in Paris 1765-66; Jefferson and Adams in Amsterdam 1788. Same-city, same-period echoes with no documented contact, and the map already shows co-location. **Adam Smith is the closest call**, Shelburne having been patron to both men on the same subject, but nothing consulted documents a Smith-Baring connection.

**Named in campas, on the strength of a real relation.** `william_paterson`: Baring's 1797 pamphlet is titled *Observations on the Establishment of the Bank of England*, and in 1810 he testified before the committee examining that Bank's paper. He wrote substantively about the institution Paterson founded in 1694: a one-directional relation across a century. `thomas_jefferson`: Baring's house bought and marketed the bonds that paid for Jefferson's purchase of Louisiana. `napoleon`: those francs went to the French treasury under Napoleon, the same transaction from the other end.
