# Oscar Wilde: research report
*2026-08-20. For `oscar_wilde.journey.json`. Oscar Fingal O'Fflahertie Wills Wilde, b. Dublin 16 October 1854, d. Paris 30 November 1900.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or standard biography that no reachable source confirmed in this pass. Contradictions are printed and not adjudicated. Every coordinate in the verified pool was checked against Wikipedia's own point value; the eighteen corrections are summarised in §11 and the reason for each is written into its stop's `date_confidence`. File: **45 stops, 8 segments**, calendar `gregorian`, register `national mythology: the canon is true`.

## 1. Dublin and the Irish childhood, 1854-1874

Attested backbone, all **[A, Wikipedia "Oscar Wilde"]**: born **21 Westland Row, 16 October 1854**, second son of Sir William Wilde and Jane Francesca Elgee ("Speranza"); **1 Merrion Square North** from 1855, site of her salon; **Portora Royal School, 1864-1871**; **Trinity College Dublin, 1871-1874** under Mahaffy and Tyrrell, **Berkeley Gold Medal in Greek**, paper "Aesthetic Morality" to the University Philosophical Society; **Sir William dies 19 April 1876**. The insolvency of the estate and Lady Wilde's removal to London are **[R]**, standard biography not in the pool, flagged in the stop. **Moytura House, near Cong, Co. Mayo** **[A, mayo-ireland.ie; Buildings of Ireland]**, coordinates approximate **[R]**.

**Isola Wilde dies aged nine.** The pool placed the death in Dublin; that is wrong. The standard account, following Ellmann, puts it at **Edgeworthstown, Co. Longford**, at her uncle the rector's house. Wikipedia confirms only "died at the age of nine of a febrile illness", with no date and no place. The date **23 February 1867** is standard biography and **could not be re-confirmed from any fetchable source in this pass** **[R]**. The coordinate is a village-centre value, not the rectory.

## 2. Oxford, 1874-1878

**Magdalen on a demyship of about £95 a year**, **double first in Mods and Greats, 1878**, **Newdigate Prize for "Ravenna"** **[A]**. The **Sheldonian recitation on 26 June** follows the customary form of the ceremony and was not re-fetched **[R for day and venue]**. **Papal audience with Pius IX, Rome, 1877** **[R]**: attested in the scholarship the pool cites but not re-fetched, month and day placeholders.

**Ruskin's road-menders: village corrected, date left contested.** The diggings were at **North Hinksey (Ferry Hinksey)**, not South Hinksey; Wikipedia names the gang, Wilde in it alongside Milner, Rawnsley, Collingwood and Toynbee, and quotes Wilde's own recollection **[A]**. The pool's South Hinksey coordinate lies 2.7 km away across the flood meadow. *Date, NOT resolved:* Wikipedia dates the project to **1874**, Wilde came up in **October 1874**, and the pool gave **1875-1876**. These cannot all be right; the stop is dated 1875-03-01 as a placeholder inside the overlap and says so.

## 3. America and Paris, 1882-1883

**New York, 2 January 1882** **[A]**. **Chickering Hall, 9 January 1882, "The English Renaissance of Art"** **[A, Wikipedia]**, upgraded from the pool's [R]; the **141-lecture total** is confirmed by oscarwildeinamerica.org. **Salt Lake City and San Francisco, 1882** are **[R]**, venues unconfirmed, and get no stops; they are named inside the Chickering Hall campa as the shape of the western circuit. **Leadville, April 1882** **[A, Colorado Springs Gazette; Irish America]**, the Cellini lecture subject **[R]**. **Paris, 1883**: meets Victor Hugo, writes *The Duchess of Padua* **[A]**, lodging unconfirmed. ***Vera*, New York, August 1883** **[R]**; the Union Square venue is approximate in the pool and unconfirmed.

**"I have nothing to declare except my genius." [R, legend]** No contemporary newspaper or customs record attests it. The pool credited the earliest appearance to Frank Harris (1916); that is wrong. Quote Investigator traces it to **1910, *The Oscar Wilde Calendar* compiled by Stuart Mason (Christopher Sclater Millard)**, then Ransome and Jerrold (both 1912), Ross possibly supplying the line to both. Carried as a `quote` whose `quote_source` says so, never as something Wilde is recorded saying.

**Whitman at Camden, 18 January 1882: the pool's worst error, and a live contradiction between two Wikipedia articles.** "Walt Whitman" says Whitman moved in 1873 to his brother George's house at **431 Stevens Street**, bought the Mickle Street house only in **1884**, and received Wilde at Stevens Street. "Walt Whitman House" says "Oscar Wilde visited Whitman at this home", i.e. Mickle Street, presumably how the error and the museum coordinate entered the dossier. The file uses **431 Stevens Street, approx. 39.947/-75.113, demolished**, and prints the conflict **[A for the visit, R for the coordinate]**. The erotic reading is **[R]**.

## 4. Marriage, Tite Street, and the prose, 1884-1891

**Marries Constance Mary Lloyd, St James's, Paddington, 29 May 1884**, **upgraded from the pool's [R] to [A]** on Wikipedia "Oscar Wilde" §6 and "Constance Lloyd". Cyril born 5 June 1885, Vyvyan 3 November 1886 **[A]**. **16 (now 34) Tite Street, January 1885 to 1895** **[A, Wikipedia; Historic England]**; Godwin's decorative scheme **[R]**. **The Woman's World, 1887-1889** **[A]**; the pool has no coordinate for the Cassell's office, so the canonical London pin is used. **"The Decay of Lying", January 1889**, revised for *Intentions* (1891) **[A]**, quotations verified against Project Gutenberg. ***Dorian Gray***: censored in *Lippincott's*, July 1890, expanded from thirteen to twenty chapters with a new Preface, April 1891 **[A]**. **1891**: *Intentions*, *Lord Arthur Savile's Crime*, *A House of Pomegranates*, "The Soul of Man under Socialism" **[A]**, quotations verified against marxists.org.

**The three-way Tite Street coordinate conflict, reconciled.** The pool carried **three values** for one street: 51.4875/-0.1653 at geography index 10, the same wrong value again at index 37 (the blue plaque), and a **third**, 51.4875/-0.1699, at afterlife index 7. Wikipedia's point is **51.4854/-0.1603**, used for every Tite Street stop here. The duplicate plaque entries were merged, the 1954 plaque and the 1895 auction carried inside the Tite Street and Wandsworth stops rather than as a separate pin.

## 5. Douglas, Salome, and the West End, 1891-1895

**Meets Lord Alfred Douglas, 1891**, probably through Lionel Johnson, intimate by 1892-93 **[A]**; the **Café Royal Domino Room** habit is **[R]**, widely attested, not re-fetched. ***Lady Windermere's Fan*, St James's, 20 February 1892**, Alexander **[A]**. ***A Woman of No Importance*, Haymarket, 19 April 1893**, Beerbohm Tree **[A]**. ***An Ideal Husband*, Haymarket, 3 January 1895** **[A]**, *attribution corrected*: the pool credited Tree with this one too, but Tree was in America and it was managed by **Lewis Waller**. ***Earnest*, St James's, 14 February 1895**, with *An Ideal Husband* still running **[A]**; the snow and Queensberry's bouquet of vegetables are **[R]**.

***Salomé*: composition date corrected.** The pool dated the writing to 1892. Wikipedia §15 dates it to Wilde's **October 1891** Paris visit, and "Salome (opera)" refers to "the 1891 French play Salomé by Oscar Wilde". What happened in **June 1892** was the Lord Chamberlain's examiner halting the Palace Theatre rehearsals under the prohibition on biblical characters on the public stage. The file separates the two: composition 1891-10 in Paris, the ban narrated as the following June.

## 6. The trials and the prison, 1895-1897

**Backbone [A, Wikipedia; The London Archives; famous-trials.com]:** Queensberry's card at the Albemarle Club, **18 February 1895**; **libel trial 3-5 April**, Carson's cross-examination, Queensberry acquitted with his plea of justification found proven; **first criminal trial opened 26 April** before Justice Arthur Charles, hung jury 1 May, bail 7 May; **conviction 25 May**, Justice Alfred Wills, two years with hard labour, the statutory maximum. Wills's "totally inadequate for a case such as this" is **[R]**, widely quoted but not re-fetched. **Bankruptcy November 1895**, **discharge only in 1906** **[R, as tagged in the pool]**.

**The arrest: sources conflict, NOT resolved.** famous-trials.com puts it on the **evening of 5 April 1895**, warrant issued at 5:00 p.m.; Wikipedia §19/§21 states flatly "On 6 April 1895, Wilde was arrested for 'gross indecency'". The file prints both and adopts the biographers' reconciliation: taken at the Cadogan on the evening of the fifth, held overnight at Bow Street, **charged before the magistrate on the sixth**. The britishnewspaperarchive.com post the pool cites returned **HTTP 403** and is its only support for 6 April, which is why the conflict stands.

**"The love that dare not speak its name": trial attribution corrected.** The wording is verified verbatim against the transcript at famous-trials.com/wilde/342-wildetestimony, elided middle sentence included, but the pool assigned it to the **second** criminal trial. **C. F. Gill prosecuted the first**; the second (20-25 May) was Lockwood's. The answer belongs to the **first criminal trial, 30 April 1895**.

**Reading, November 1895, prisoner C.3.3.** Month secure, **day contested, 13 or 23 November**, as the pool itself flags; the file uses the thirteenth and prints the conflict. **The chapel fall and the ear injury**: the pool places the collapse at **Reading**, several biographies at Wandsworth; the file follows the pool and the divergence is flagged here. ***De Profundis*, 1897**: Ross's abridgement 1905, Vyvyan Holland's 1949 text still full of transcription errors, the first accurate complete text in **Hart-Davis, 1962**, from a manuscript held under a **fifty-year restriction Ross himself imposed** **[A]**.

**Release: date and mechanism both corrected.** The pool said "released from Reading Gaol, 18 May 1897, crosses to France the next day". Wikipedia §22 says "released from prison on **19 May 1897**": moved to **Pentonville** on the evening of the eighteenth, released there on the morning of the nineteenth, sailing the **same night**. Wikipedia's own summary line ("incarcerated from 25 May 1895 to 18 May 1897") contradicts its explicit release sentence; that contradiction is printed, not smoothed.

## 7. Exile, 1897-1900

**Dieppe, 20 May 1897**, met by Ross and Reginald Turner **[A, Oscar Wilde Society chronology]**. **Sebastian Melmoth**, from Saint Sebastian and Maturin's *Melmoth the Wanderer*, is **[R]**: standard in the biographies, no primary document such as a signed register located. **Chalet Bourgeat, Berneval-le-Grand**, 1897, *The Ballad* **begun** here and **completed at Naples** **[A, Wikipedia]**; the jubilee party for the village children **[R]**. **Rouen reunion with Douglas: month corrected** from the pool's September to **August 1897** (Wikipedia §23/§24), carried inside the Naples stop. **Posillipo, Naples**, autumn 1897, separation by December **[A]**, villa address unconfirmed. ***The Ballad*, Smithers, 13 February 1898**, byline C.3.3. **[A, University College Oxford]**; the 800-copy print run comes from the dossier, **was not reverified**, and is not stated in the file. **Second Daily Chronicle letter, "Prison Reform", 23 March 1898**: the pool dated the quotation "1897/1898", **corrected to 1898**, verified verbatim on Wikisource **[A]**. **Constance dies at Genoa, 7 April 1898** **[A]**; no Genoa stop, see §9.

**Wilde calls on Alfred Jarry, Paris, May 1898** **[A, fr.wikipedia "Alfred Jarry", via `alfred_jarry.journey.json`]**; canonical Paris pin inherited byte-identical from Jarry's file, where the meeting is already staged from the other side, the unrecorded substance of the conversation stated as a gap in the campa. **Hôtel d'Alsace, 13 rue des Beaux-Arts**, 1898 to death, **ear operation 10 October 1900** **[R, as tagged in the pool]**; Dupoirier's forbearance over the bill **[R]**. **Conditional baptism by Fr Cuthbert Dunne, 29 November 1900** **[R]**: Dunne and Ross report it, no third witness, assent disputed, printed as a disputed question. **Death 30 November 1900**, cerebral meningitis, aged 46 **[A]**. **"Either that wallpaper goes, or I do." [R, legend]**: no bedside witness records it as his last words, and it most likely began as an earlier joke about the room. Named as a legend inside the death stop, with no stop of its own.

## 8. The tomb and the afterlife

**Bagneux, 3 December 1900**, a **temporary concession** taken for want of funds, Ross paying **[A, Crowell, branchcollective.org]**. **Reinterred at Père Lachaise, 1909**, **Ross's ashes in the tomb, 1950** **[A]**. *Coordinate conflict inside the pool, NOT resolved:* the geography lens gives **48.8595/2.3936** (division 89), the afterlife lens **48.8631/2.3934**, 400 m apart. The file uses the geography value throughout the segment and says so.

**The Epstein monument: unveiling year corrected.** Crowell gives a commission announced in **December 1908**, £2,000 from an anonymous donor later revealed as **Helen Carew**; the sculpture **arrived in Paris in August 1912** and was at once covered and put under guard; it was **not unveiled until August 1914**, Epstein refusing to attend. The pool gave 1912 as the unveiling. On the covering, the pool said plaster with a bronze fig-leaf in 1914, while Wikipedia describes a **bronze butterfly-shaped plaque**; the file says bronze butterfly and leaves the plaster claim unverified. **Genitals chiselled off 1961**; **restoration and glass panels November 2011** **[A]**.

**Danny Osborne's memorial sculpture, Merrion Square, unveiled 1997** by Merlin Holland for the Guinness Ireland Group **[A]**. **British Museum Reading Room ticket revoked 15 June 1895; British Library reinstated a pass in his name October 2025** **[R, press release not re-fetched]**, carried inside the Wandsworth stop rather than as its own pin. ***Salome* (Strauss), Dresden, 9 December 1905** and **Hambling's Adelaide Street bench, 30 November 1998**, both **[A]**: neither given a stop, see §9.

## 9. Stops declined under the "who was actually there" rule

Kept in the record, not staged as scenes:

1. **Dresden 1905, the Strauss opera.** Wilde was five years dead and never went to Dresden; that is Strauss's scene, on Strauss's file if he ever gets one. Carried as a `suggested_ref` on Wilde's own Salomé composition stop.
2. **Adelaide Street 1998, the Hambling bench.** A memorial to Wilde at a place with no recorded connection to him. Carried in the Merrion Square stop's `suggested_refs`.
3. **Genoa, 7 April 1898.** Constance's death, and Constance's place: Wilde was in Paris and the pool records no visit by him to the grave, so the death is narrated in his own Paris stop.
4. **Père Lachaise and Isadora Duncan.** Her ashes went to the same columbarium in 1927, with no meeting and no mention of Wilde in her file. **Shared geography only**, so she is named in no campa.
5. **Aleister Crowley at the 1914 unveiling, and later removing the butterfly plaque.** Crowley's act at Wilde's grave, fourteen years after the death. It stays in Crowell's account as a source; the tomb stop narrates the covering and uncovering without staging him.

## 10. Apparatus relations for the operator

Wilde is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework of his is applied backward or forward here and nothing in the file needs the author's ruling. Recorded so nothing is deleted silently:

- **Direction caught: forward, repeatedly, all of it ordinary afterlife material.** Every posthumous item in §8 postdates his death. Each one kept is attached to **his own body, his own house, or his own square**, which is how the corpus already handles graves and reinterments (compare `abdelkader.journey.json`, closing on the 1966 repatriation), and none applies anyone's theoretical apparatus to anything. The two forward items **not** anchorable to a place of his own, Dresden 1905 and Adelaide Street 1998, are the two declined in §9. Staging either as a Wilde stop anyway would be a judgement about afterlife scope, not an exceptions question.
- **No backward apparatus relation was found**, and none was constructed.
