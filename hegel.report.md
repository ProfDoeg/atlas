# Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: G.W.F. Hegel, 1770-1831. Slug: `hegel`. Companion artifact: `hegel.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

**Evidence key.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim not tied to a citable source. Contradictions are printed and left standing; in the journey file the hedge lives in `date_confidence` and never in the campa prose.

**Method.** Four lenses (chronology, geography, quotations, afterlife) plus a pass over the atlas corpus for intersections with travelers already in the globe. A verification pass re-checked each claim against de.wikipedia, en.wikipedia, SEP, Deutsche Biographie and the marxists.org Hegel Archive, and returned eighteen corrections. Each is folded in below and marked CORRECTED, so the earlier reading stays visible.

---

## 1. Stuttgart and the Stift, 1770-1793

**Birth, 27 August 1770, Stuttgart, capital of the Duchy of Württemberg** [A: en.wikipedia, de.wikipedia]. Father Georg Ludwig Hegel, secretary at the ducal Rentkammer; mother Maria Magdalena. Birth house Eberhardstraße 53, a sixteenth-century building [A: de.wikipedia, "Hegel-Haus"].

*CORRECTED, coordinates.* Three pins came out of the lenses for one building: 48.7758 / 9.1775 (geography) and 48.7735 / 9.1804 (afterlife, twice). De.wikipedia gives 48° 46′ 23″ N, 9° 10′ 37″ E, that is **48.7731, 9.1769**, used everywhere in the journey. The discarded pins were 260 to 300 m off.

**Schooling** [A: en.wikipedia]: German School 1773, Latin School 1775, then from 1776 the Stuttgart gymnasium. Sophocles, Epictetus, the diary of extracts [R: en.wikipedia, uncited there]. *CORRECTED, name:* the chronology lens called the 1776 school the Eberhard-Ludwigs-Gymnasium, which is anachronistic. In 1776 it was the **Gymnasium illustre**, founded 1686; the ducal name came later [A: de.wikipedia].

**Mother's death, 1783, bilious fever, Hegel thirteen** [A: en.wikipedia]. Month and day not established.

**Tübinger Stift from 1788** [A: en.wikipedia, "Tübinger Stift"]; Master of Arts 1790 [A]; theological certificate 1793 [A]. The examiners' note of middling promise in philosophy is [R: Deutsche Biographie as relayed; the record itself was not seen].

**The chamber shared with Hölderlin and Schelling** [A: en.wikipedia, and corroborated inside the corpus by `schelling.journey.json`], one of the few facts attested from two directions at once.

*Contradiction, left standing.* En.wikipedia dates the entry to 1788; `schelling.journey.json` puts it at **18 October 1790**, Schelling then fifteen. Both are carried: a 1788 entry stop and a separate 1790-10-18 chamber stop on the canonical Schelling pin.

**The Tree of Liberty, 14 July 1793, a meadow by the Neckar** [contested]. `schelling.journey.json` has it as **[A]**, the three dancing round it and swearing fidelity to freedom. The verification pass calls it **[R]**: no contemporary evidence, absent from the de.wikipedia Stift article, and the geography lens's own text concedes "by later legend". The stop is kept, the register of this atlas being national mythology, and marked `traditional` with the disagreement spelled out.

## 2. The Hofmeister years, 1793-1801

**Bern, house tutor to Captain Karl Friedrich von Steiger, 1793-1796** [A: en.wikipedia; SEP]. Kant and political economy in his own hours [R].

**Tschugg, the Steiger country estate above the Bielersee, summers 1793-1796** [A: en.wikipedia]. *CORRECTED, coordinates.* The lens gave 47.068 / 7.148, about 9 km northeast of the village near Gals. De.wikipedia "Tschugg" gives 47° 1′ 38″ N, 7° 4′ 37″ E, that is **47.0272, 7.0768**. Kept detail: the former Landgut Steiger has housed the Klinik Bethesda since 1889 [A].

**Frankfurt am Main, 1797-1801, tutor to the wine merchant Johann Noe Gogel**, the post arranged by Hölderlin [A: de.wikipedia; Deutsche Biographie]. "The Spirit of Christianity and Its Fate", 1799, unpublished in his lifetime [A: en.wikipedia].

**Father's death, January 1799, Stuttgart** [A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia has 1799]. *CORRECTED, tag.* The chronology lens tagged this [R] on "standard biographical tradition (Pinkard)". It is attested, and the geography lens already had [A]. The two lenses disagreeing on a tag for the same event is recorded rather than quietly averaged.

## 3. Jena, 1801-1807

**January 1801, arrival at Schelling's urging; *De Orbitis Planetarum*; unsalaried Privatdozent** [A: en.wikipedia]. First book the same year, *The Difference between Fichte's and Schelling's System of Philosophy* [A: SEP; en.wikipedia].

**The *Kritisches Journal der Philosophie*, with Schelling, 1802-1803** [A: SEP; en.wikipedia]. *CORRECTED, title:* the chronology lens wrote "Kritische Journal"; the geography lens had it right.

**Extraordinary professor, 1805, still unsalaried** [A]. Schelling has left for Würzburg [A].

**Napoleon in Jena, 13 October 1806** [A: en.wikipedia; the Niethammer letter of that date]. *CORRECTED, conflation:* the chronology lens filed the entry and the battle together under 14 October. Napoleon entered on the **13th**; the battle was the **14th**. The geography lens had already split them; the journey carries two stops.

*CORRECTED, quotation.* The geography lens had Hegel writing that he saw "the world-spirit on horseback". He did not: the letter reads "diese Weltseele", **this world-soul**, riding out of the city on reconnaissance. "Weltgeist zu Pferde" is a later popularization. The verbatim line is quoted in the journey; the campa names the popular version as the phrase the century remembers instead.

**Phenomenology finished as the battle opens, 14 October 1806** [A as to general timing: SEP; en.wikipedia]. Whether the last pages went to the printer before, during or after the battle is disputed among biographers; recorded in `date_confidence`, not adjudicated. Published 1807 by Joseph Anton Goebhardt, credited to Bamberg and Würzburg [A: en.wikipedia].

**Ludwig Fischer born 5 February 1807 to Christina Charlotte Burkhardt, born Fischer, Hegel's landlady** [A: de.wikipedia]. *CORRECTED, date and name.* The chronology lens gave February **1806** and "Christiana"; the geography lens gave 1807-02 and flagged the split. De.wikipedia gives 5 February 1807 and the correct name, en.wikipedia's body text now also gives 1807, and the corroboration is that the boy joins the household in April 1817 "aged about ten". The journey uses 1807-02-05 and names the rejected date.

**Leaves Jena, March 1807**, enrolment collapsed [A: Deutsche Biographie]. Folded into the Bamberg stop, not pinned.

## 4. Bamberg and Nuremberg, 1807-1816

**Editor of the *Bamberger Zeitung*, 1807-1808**, on Niethammer's recommendation [A: en.wikipedia; Deutsche Biographie].

**Rector of the Ägidiengymnasium, Nuremberg, eight years** [A: en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]. *Contradiction on the month:* chronology lens **November 1808**, geography lens **December 1808**; de.wikipedia supports November. Both are printed in `date_confidence`; the stop is dated 1808-11-01. The historic school stood at Egidienplatz 10, about 49.4557 / 11.0797; the lens pin 49.4544 / 11.0784 is some 175 m off and is kept, the alternative being the 1911 successor building on the Sulzbacher Straße.

**Marriage to Marie Helena Susanna von Tucher, 1811** [A: en.wikipedia], September per the geography lens. Venue not established; the pin is central Nuremberg and the campa names no church.

**Science of Logic**, three volumes, 1812, 1813, 1816 [A: en.wikipedia]. **Sons Karl, 1813, and Immanuel, 1814** [A]; months and days not established.

**Brother Georg Ludwig dies as an officer in Napoleon's Russian campaign, 1812** [A: en.wikipedia]. Not pinned: the source gives only "Russia", too vague for a coordinate. Carried in the Logic stop's campa; a deliberate omission.

## 6. Berlin, 1818-1831

**Chair of philosophy from October 1818 until his death** [A: SEP; en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]. *CORRECTED, tag:* the afterlife lens had [R], "not independently re-verified"; attested three ways, retagged [A]. *CORRECTED, institution:* in 1818 it was the **Universität zu Berlin**, Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität only from 1828 and Humboldt-Universität from 1949 [A: de.wikipedia]. Fichte, first rector 1811/12, held the chair before him [A].

**Residence Am Kupfergraben** [A]. *CORRECTED, tag:* the geography lens marked it [R] for want of a citable source; de.wikipedia carries the memorial plaque in Berlin-Mitte. Retagged [A], cited to the plaque. The **house number remains unknown**, so 52.5195 / 13.3958 is approximate and the journey says so.

**Elements of the Philosophy of Right, published 1820** [A: en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]. *CORRECTED, year:* both lenses said 1821. En.wikipedia: "published in 1820, though the book's original title page dates it to 1821." SEP's loose 1821 is the title-page date. The journey uses 1820 and names the title page in `date_confidence`.

**Travel.** Weimar, to Goethe [A: en.wikipedia; Deutsche Biographie]. *CORRECTED, frequency:* "repeated visits" overstates it; en.wikipedia gives **two trips**, the second ending in their last meeting. The Netherlands 1822, Dutch painting feeding the aesthetics lectures, with Brussels on the same journey [A]. Vienna and Prague 1824 [A]; the Rossini enthusiasm is widely repeated and not re-verified [R]. Paris 1827, the one recorded trip, with the Louvre [A]; dates within the year not established. Brussels and Prague are named inside campa rather than pinned, to hold the stop budget.

**Rector, elected October 1829, term to September 1830** [A: en.wikipedia]. The Augsburg Confession tricentennial address, June 1830 [R: Deutsche Biographie as relayed; text not reached]. **Order of the Red Eagle, third class, 1831** [A], carried in campa, not pinned. **Ludwig Fischer dies with the Dutch army at Batavia, 1831, shortly before his father** [A], pinned at Batavia, the farthest point of the journey and the only stop Hegel never went near. **Cholera and Kreuzberg, August 1831; return to Berlin, October 1831** [A].

**Death 14 November 1831, aged 61** [A: en.wikipedia; Deutsche Biographie]. Cholera per the attending physicians; a different gastrointestinal illness per later scholarship. Both are in the campa and neither is chosen.

**Burial 16 November 1831, Dorotheenstädtischer Friedhof, beside Fichte and Karl Wilhelm Ferdinand Solger, at his own request** [A: en.wikipedia]. *CORRECTED, coordinates:* the geography lens gave 52.527 / 13.382, some 275 m off and at odds with the afterlife lens's 52.5296 / 13.3835 for the same ground. The entrance is Chausseestraße 126. The verification pass recommended **52.5293, 13.3839**, used here for both the burial and the 1889 reinterment; the remaining 40 m disagreement is recorded, not hidden.

## 7. After

**The Verein von Freunden des Verewigten produces the first collected edition** [R]: span and volume count unverified, the search budget having run out before a bibliographic source was reached.

The institutional facts are verifiable and upgraded to [A]; only the edition's start date and volume count stay [R].

Not pinned.

## 8. Quotations

Verified verbatim against the marxists.org Hegel Archive; the starred six are used in the journey.

- \* "The owl of Minerva takes its flight only when the shades of night are gathering." [Philosophy of Right, Preface, trans. Dyde 1896]
- \* "When philosophy paints its grey in grey, one form of life has become old, and by means of grey it cannot be rejuvenated, but only known." [same]
- \* "The truth is the whole." [Phenomenology, Preface. The fetched page credits no translator, so the attribution of that web edition to Baillie is [R] though the text is [A].]
- "Everything depends on grasping and expressing the ultimate truth not as Substance but as Subject as well." [Phenomenology, Preface]
- \* "Self-consciousness exists in itself and for itself, in that, and by the fact that it exists for another self-consciousness; that is to say, it is only by being acknowledged or 'recognized'." [Phenomenology, Lordship and Bondage, para. 178]
- \* "I saw the Emperor, this world-soul, riding out of the city on reconnaissance." [letter to Niethammer, 13 October 1806]
- \* "Reason governs the world and has consequently governed its history." [Introduction to the Philosophy of History, trans. Hartman 1953]
- "It sets the passions to work for itself, while that through which it develops itself pays the penalty and suffers the loss." [same, the cunning of reason]
- "the slaughter-bench at which the happiness of peoples, the wisdom of states, and the virtue of individuals have been sacrificed." [same]

*CORRECTED, one quotation.* "What is rational is real and what is real is rational" is in Dyde, but not as one flowing clause: the page reads "What is rational is real; And what is real is rational." Quote it with the semicolon or not at all. The older flag stands: Knox and others render *wirklich* as "actual", and that dispute is not resolved here. Not used in the journey.

**Two famous lines that are not what they are said to be, both kept out of the quote fields:**

1. "Only one man ever understood me, and he did not understand me either." *CORRECTED.* The chronology lens cited Kaufmann and called it untraceable. It is traceable: Heinrich Heine, *Zur Geschichte der Religion und Philosophie in Deutschland* (1834), "nur Einer hat mich verstanden", then "und der hat mich auch nicht verstanden". Pinkard (2000) states the story is fictional, an anti-Hegelian legend grown up after 1831, and in Heine's telling Hegel is not on a deathbed at all. Tag stays [R], source line replaced with Heine 1834, reported as apocryphal.
2. "a flat-headed, insipid, nauseating, illiterate charlatan." *CORRECTED.* Not invented but misattributed as to work: that English wording is Popper's, *The Open Society and Its Enemies* II, ch. 12. The German is Schopenhauer's and attested at de.wikiquote ("ein platter, geistloser, ekelhaft-widerlicher, unwissender Scharlatan"). Neither the *Parerga* page nor the *On the Will in Nature* preface could be pinned down, so that part of the earlier source line is dropped. Tag stays [R] for the primary location.

## 9. Atlas interlocks

Travelers already in the globe, named inside campa where the crossing is genuine: **schelling** (the chamber, the Tree of Liberty, the call to Jena, the *Kritisches Journal*, the 1841 summons to Hegel's chair); **kant** (read by candlelight in the chamber; `kant.journey.json` has him declining a Jena chair in 1770, the year of Hegel's birth); **fichte** (the Berlin chair before him, the Jena plateau, the grave beside him); **goethe_full** (Weimar, and Goethe's hand in the Jena appointments); **napoleon** (13 and 14 October 1806); **schiller** (the Jena chair of history, 1789); **novalis** and **froebel** (the Jena of 1799); **karl_marx** (matriculating 1836); **humboldt** (in the audience at Schelling's 1841 inaugural, per both `schelling.journey.json` and `humboldt.journey.json`).

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Stift chamber 48.521 / 9.058 and Neckar meadow 48.519 / 9.055 from `schelling.journey.json`; Berlin university 52.5179 / 13.3939 from `karl_marx.journey.json`; Berlin 52.517 / 13.394 from `schelling.journey.json` for the 1841 stop.

**Gap.** The Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), the Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354) and Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816) are canonical atlas pins with no biographical link whatever to Hegel. Not inherited.

**Gap.** Hegel has no attested Paris stop in the corpus, though he made one trip in 1827. The canonical Paris pin 48.8566 / 2.3522 belongs to Marx's 1843 exile; the Louvre stop uses 48.8606 / 2.3376 and does not claim it.

**Gap.** Hölderlin, the third man in the chamber and the one who found Hegel his Frankfurt post, has no journey file. He is named in campa as a person, not as a traveler.
