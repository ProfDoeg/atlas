# Justinian I (Petrus Sabbatius Iustinianus), c. 482-565

*Research report, 2026-08-28, from the verified research pool. Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the pool could not push back to a primary text.*

**Method note, load-bearing for the journey file.** Justinian left the neighbourhood of Constantinople almost never; the one excursion the sources place him on personally is the Thracian ride of 559, where Agathias shows him working diplomacy rather than commanding. Every African, Italian and Mesopotamian stop is therefore a stop of the *reign*, not of the emperor's body: the campaign he ordered, paid for and read dispatches about, and the campas keep that visible. Where a place enters only because a *later* traveller went there, it is not a stop but a `suggested_ref` on Justinian's own stop.

---

## 1. Dardania and the uncle, c. 482-518

Born Petrus Sabbatius, son of Sabbatius and Vigilantia, at **Tauresium near Scupi** in Dardania. Birth year conventionally **482**, with **483** also proposed; the traditional 11 May is **not securely attested**. **[A: Wikipedia 'Justinian I' after Evans; *Buildings* 4.1 for the site.]**

*Coordinate correction carried.* The geography lens gave 41.9773 / 21.5644, in Skopje's southern outskirts; the afterlife lens gave a *third* value for the same village, 42 / 21.4, which is Skopje proper. Wikipedia 'Taor' gives **41.898 / 21.611**, 20 km southeast on the left bank of the Vardar. Three coordinates for one birthplace inside one dossier is a warning about the dossier.

Brought to Constantinople by his uncle **Justin**, adopted, educated for imperial service. **No ancient source fixes the date. [R]** The pool tags this inconsistently: chronology lens [R], "exact date unknown"; geography lens [A], "c. 500s-527". The file follows the chronology lens.

**Justin I proclaimed emperor 9 July 518. [A]** A late tradition holds that the Excubitors offered the throne first to Justinian, who refused in his uncle's favour **[R:** later chroniclers; modern historians read it as retrojection**]**. Kept as tradition, marked in `date_confidence`.

## 2. The rise under Justin, 519-527

- Ended the **Acacian Schism**, 519; *comes domesticorum*, then *magister militum praesentalis* after the murder of **Vitalian**, 520; patrician, *nobilissimus* **[A: World History Encyclopedia; Britannica-derived chronology]**. His own part in the killing is disputed and not resolved here.
- **Consul 521**, with games of conspicuous extravagance marking him as heir **[A: Marcellinus Comes s.a. 521]**.
- **Marriage to Theodora, c. 525**, after **Codex 5.4.23** lifted the bar on senators marrying repentant actresses **[A: *Secret History* 9]**. *Contradiction with the sibling file, flagged not resolved:* `empress_theodora.journey.json` dates it **c. 524-525** at the old Hagia Sophia; the chronology lens says 525 flatly. The file uses 525, states the range in `date_confidence`, and inherits the sibling's Hagia Sophia pin (41.0086 / 28.9802).
- **Crowned co-emperor 1 April 527. [A/R]** Tagged [R] by the chronology lens (Wikipedia 'Justin I') and [A] by the geography lens (*Chronicon Paschale*). The pool disagrees with itself; recorded as such.
- **Justin I dies 1 August 527 [A]**.

## 3. The law, 529-534

- First **Codex Iustinianus** promulgated **7 April 529** by *Summa rei publicae*, commission of ten under John of Cappadocia, surviving only in fragments **[A]**. *Correction carried:* the geography lens says it "takes effect 7 April", conflating promulgation with force. In force **16 April 529**.
- **Deo auctore, 15 December 530**, putting Tribonian over the Digest commission; **Institutiones** 21 November 533; **Digesta**, fifty books, **16 December 533**; **Codex repetitae praelectionis** 16 November 534. **[A]**
- **The Athenian schools, c. 529. [R:** Malalas 18.47, with the pool's caveat.**]** The causal link is disputed, and "Justinian closed Plato's Academy" overstates a continuous institution that had not existed for centuries. `plato.journey.json` already stakes out **Codex I.11.10.2 (529)**; this file points at it rather than duplicating it.

## 4. Persia and the fire, 530-532

- **Dara, June 530. [A:** *Wars* 1.13-14.**]** *Coordinate correction:* the pool's 37.1783 / 40.9761 falls east of the ruins in open country. Wikipedia gives 37.1778 / 40.9411 and 37.18 / 40.955; the file uses **37.178 / 40.945**, at Oguz in Mardin province, Turkey, not on the Syrian border strip as the chronology lens loosely says.
- **Callinicum, 19 April 531. [A:** *Wars* 1.18.**]** *Correction carried:* the geography lens calls it "Belisarius's outnumbered force", contradicting the pool's own chronology lens. Wikipedia gives Roman and allied 20,000-25,000 against Sasanian 15,000-20,000.
- **The Endless Peace, 532**: 11,000 pounds of gold to Khosrow I **[A: *Wars* 1.22]**. *Correction:* the geography lens sites it in the **Chrysotriklinos**, built a generation later under Justin II. The room name is dropped.
- **Nika riots, 13-18 January 532 [A]**, the old Hagia Sophia and the senate house burning; **Hypatius and Pompeius executed** without trial, property confiscated and later partly restored **[R: *Wars* 1.24; Malalas 18]**. **The death toll**, roughly 30,000 in the sources, is **not independently verifiable** and is given as a source's figure.
- **Theodora's speech against flight. [R]** The council is plausible and the episode likely real; the wording is Procopius's composition, not a transcript. "The purple makes a fine winding-sheet" is a paraphrase from secondary literature; Dewing's Loeb reads *"royalty is a good burial-shroud."* The file quotes Dewing and states in `date_confidence` that the words are Theodora's.

## 5. The building programme, 532-565

- **Hagia Sophia**: broken ground **23 February 532**, dedicated with Patriarch Menas **27 December 537**, under Anthemius of Tralles and Isidore of Miletus **[A: *Buildings* 1.1]**. **"Solomon, I have surpassed you"** is a **later reported saying**, unattested by any contemporary **[R]**; both the geography and afterlife lenses flag it and neither located a primary origin. Carried as tradition.
- **Justiniana Prima, founded 535**, an archbishopric on or near his native ground. **[A: *Buildings* 4.1, Novel 11.]**
- **Saint Catherine's, Sinai, c. 548-565**: fortress walls round the Burning Bush site and a chapel attributed to Helena **[A: *Buildings* 5.8]**. Three other atlas files carry the same attribution with a shared pin of **28.5563 / 33.975**, inherited byte-identically.
- **The shrine of Saint Pantaleon at Nicomedia, rebuilt. [A:** *De aedificiis*, via `saint_pantaleon.journey.json`.**]** Outside the pool's list of principal projects, recovered from the interlock lens. Pin and placeholder date (40.7654 / 29.9408; 0550-01-01) inherited exactly, with that file's correction that the testimony points at Nicomedia.
- **A martyrium of Saint Acacius at the Heptaskalon**, said to have been restored under Justinian **[R]**: `saint_acacius.journey.json` marks it unattested with no source gathered, so it is **not used**, recorded here rather than dropped silently. The **Column of Justinian**, raised **543 [A]**, gets no stop for space and is carried in the burial stop.

## 6. Africa, 533-534

Ad Decimum **13 September 533**, Carthage **15 September**, Tricamarum **15 December 533**, Gelimer's surrender spring 534, triumph 534. **[A: *Wars* 3.18-21, 4.2-3, 4.9.]**

*Two corrections carried.* (1) The chronology lens has Belisarius "made consul on return" in 534. He was consul *ordinarius* for **535**, entering office 1 January 535, after the 534 triumph. (2) Tricamarum's 36.7833 / 10.0333 sits south of the Medjerda, whose course at that longitude runs near 36.85-36.90; the file uses **36.87 / 10.03**.

**The Temple spoils.** *Wars* 4.9 has the triumph display treasures taken from the Temple by Titus in 70 and from Rome by the Vandals in 455, and has Justinian send them on toward Jerusalem **[R]**: the pool could not fetch the text, and the Solomon and Aaron files establish the Temple furnishings without mentioning the spoils. One Jerusalem stop carries the canonical Temple Mount pin **31.778 / 35.2354**, tagged reconstruction, because the object moved at Justinian's order though he never stood there.

## 7. Italy, 535-554

Sicily 535; **Naples 536** through an aqueduct channel, then sacked; **Rome 9 December 536**; the **siege under Witigis 537-538**; **Milan 539**, destroyed amid friction between Belisarius and Narses; **Ravenna 540**, entered after Belisarius feigned acceptance of the western crown. **[A: *Wars* 5.8-6.30.]**

- **Procopius's 300,000 adult male dead at Milan is not credible as arithmetic**, as the pool flags itself. Not used as a number, and Milan gets no stop. **No triumph for Ravenna**, unlike Africa; whether Justinian read the feigned usurpation as loyalty or threat **is not settled by any source**, and the campa leaves it open.
- **Rome falls to Totila, 17 December 546.** *Correction carried:* the geography lens puts this at the **Salarian Gate**, 41.9145 / 12.5044, three kilometres from where it happened. Wikipedia has the Goths entering by the **Porta Asinaria**, opened by Isaurian defectors. The file uses **41.886 / 12.506** for both 536 and 546, so one gate carries one pin. Rome recovered 547, lost again 550.
- **Taginae, July 552**; **Mons Lactarius, October 552. [A: *Wars* 8.32, 8.35.]** *Correction:* the pool locates Mons Lactarius on "the slopes of Vesuvius" at 40.7614 / 14.4939, on the Sarno plain. It is the **Monti Lattari**, a distinct range; Teia camped at Vesuvius first, then withdrew. File: **40.66 / 14.55**.
- **Casilinum / the Volturnus, autumn 554. [A: Agathias 2.4-9.]** *Internal contradiction:* the chronology lens says 552, the geography lens 554. **554 is correct.** No stop, for space.
- **Pragmatic Sanction, 13 August 554.** *Two corrections:* issued **at Constantinople**, to Narses and the prefect Antiochus, not at Ravenna; and "exarchal administration" is anachronistic by thirty years, the Exarchate first attested c. 584. Italy 554-584 was a praetorian prefecture. **Spania, from 552**, gets no stop either; a gap noted here.

## 8. Plague and the empress, 540-548

- **Antioch sacked by Khosrow I, 540**, its people deported to a new city near Ctesiphon **[A: *Wars* 2.5-14]**. **John the Cappadocian brought down, 541**, by a trap laid by Antonina and Theodora **[A: *Wars* 1.25; *Secret History* 17, with the pool's caveat on hostile embellishment]**. **The annual consulship ended, 541 [A]**.
- **Plague, 541-542**; **Justinian catches it and recovers**, and during his incapacity the eastern generals discuss the succession and Buzes is arrested **[A: *Wars* 2.22-23; *Secret History* 4]**. Ancient DNA has identified *Yersinia pestis* in graves of the period; **total mortality is disputed** and no figure is stated as fact.
- **Theodora dies 28 June 548. [A: *Wars* 2.30; Malalas 18.104.]** *Correction carried:* the geography lens stages her death **at the mausoleum**, conflating death with burial, and gives 41.0181 / 28.9503, disagreeing with the pool's own afterlife lens and with `empress_theodora.journey.json`, both of which use **41.0197 / 28.9497**. The file separates the two and uses the sibling's pins.
- **San Vitale, consecrated 547**, its apse panels setting Justinian and Theodora haloed into the sanctuary **[A: Agnellus of Ravenna]**. **Justinian probably never visited Ravenna**, and the campa says so.

## 9. The Three Chapters and the last years

- **Pope Vigilius** in Constantinople from 547, around the **Palace of Hormisdas**, dragged from an altar, changing position four times under threat **[R: *Liber Pontificalis*; Victor of Tunnuna]**. The **Second Council, 5 May to 2 June 553**, condemns the Three Chapters without him; the Miaphysites are not reconciled and northern Italy goes into schism for 150 years. **[A]**
- **Hagia Sophia**: quakes 553 and December 557; on **7 May 558** the **eastern portion of the dome and the eastern semi-dome** fall into the sanctuary, destroying ambon, altar and ciborium; Isidore the Younger rebuilds higher and it is **rededicated 24 December 562 [A: Agathias 5.9]**. *Correction:* both pool lenses say "the first dome collapsed". It was the eastern portion.
- **Melantias, March 559. [A: Agathias 5.11-25.]** *Correction:* the pool's 41.045 / 28.585 is roughly 33 km west of Hagia Sophia, out toward Silivri; Melantias sat about 20 km west, near modern Yarimburgaz. The file uses **41.02 / 28.76**. He then goes into **Thrace in person** and breaks the invasion by diplomacy. **No source shows him in a pitched battle, ever**, and this is the only journey of his adult life beyond the capital.
- **Fifty-Year Peace, 562 [A: Menander fr. 6.1]**, and the same year the **bankers' conspiracy**: Belisarius implicated, confined, restored in 563 **[A: Malalas 18.129; Theophanes]**, unverified against a primary text. **The blinded, begging Belisarius is medieval invention** and is not used.
- **Aphthartodocetism, January 565**: bishops reportedly ordered to hold Christ's body incorruptible from the Incarnation, Eutychius deposed for refusing **[R: Evagrius 4.39]**. **The edict does not survive.**
- **Death, night of 13/14 November 565**, aged about 83; buried at the **Holy Apostles [A]**. **Callinicus** reported that the dying emperor named Justin II **[R]**: private, uncheckable, reaching us only through Corippus.

## 10. Afterlife

Not a segment; carried in the last two stops and their `suggested_refs`. All **[A]**: the tomb plundered in the **Fourth Crusade, 1204** (Nicetas Choniates); the church demolished **1461** for the Fatih Mosque; the column statue losing its orb **1422-1427**, falling in the **1509 earthquake**, its fragments melted for cannon; the **Digest** taught in northern Italy from **c. 1070** via the *Littera Florentina*; **Hagia Sophia** museum 1935, mosque again 2020; the Orthodox feast with Theodora on **14 November**. The **"Upravda" legend** is **spurious [R]**, kept because the pool attached a wrong coordinate to it (see §1).

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found requiring a ruling.** Justinian I is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no relation turned up that would have needed the exception. Two things were checked against the block rule and disposed of without deletion.

1. **Backward, and legitimate without any exception.** His engagements with figures long dead are ordinary attested acts, not apparatus: he rebuilt Pantaleon's shrine at Nicomedia; his commission's compilation is the vehicle by which Constantine's 321 Sunday-rest law survives (**Codex Justinianus 3.12.2**, as `constantino_el_grande.journey.json` cites it); his legislation of 529 acted on the school Plato founded; the Solomon material, the dedication cry and the Temple spoils, is his own speech and his own order. These are named in campas, and no exception is needed, because the man himself did the thing.
2. **Forward, and therefore not written.** The interlock lens supplies a large body of later engagement: Charlemagne's agents stripping Ravenna columns for Aachen; Hesse, Klimt, Yeats, Mackintosh, Rivera and Bernhardt under the San Vitale apse; Le Corbusier and Rabban Bar Sauma inside Hagia Sophia; Luther's father buying him a Corpus Juris at Erfurt in 1505; Paul the Deacon carrying Eutropius into Justinian's age; Dante making him the sole speaker of *Paradiso* VI. **None is staged as a scene and none is named in a campa**, per the block rule: a sixth-century file cannot embed a later artifact. Each survives as a plain-language `suggested_ref` on Justinian's own stop. **Recorded here so the operator sees what was moved, rather than meeting it as an absence.**

Two leads, neither usable now. **Dante's *Paradiso* VI**: `dante.journey.json` has no stop for the canto in which Justinian speaks, a gap in **Dante's** file and the licensable direction. **The Ghassanid thread**: Justinian's ally **al-Harith ibn Jabalah** and the bishop **Jacob Baradaeus**, consecrated through Theodora's and al-Harith's patronage, built the Syriac Miaphysite hierarchy, but searches for those names and 'Ghassanid' returned nothing across the journey files. **No atlas anchor exists to link against**; a queue candidate, not a connection.

**Quotations, verbatim, from a reachable text.** Dewing's Loeb *Anecdota* (1935) and *Buildings* (1940), via LacusCurtius. Three needed correction. *Anecdota* 8 reads **"I would say that he resembled Domitian, son of Vespasian, very closely"**, not "He resembled..."; the first person matters, since Procopius advances the comparison as his own judgement, and the trailing clause about the Romans chopping Domitian's body to pieces is what makes it an accusation. *Anecdota* 18.1 reads **"as has been suggested"**, not "as has been indicated". At *Anecdota* 18 the pool spliced two figures of different scope: **"a myriad myriad of myriads perished"** is the **empire-wide** estimate, and the Libyan figure sits inside a conditional whose point is that five hundred myriads **understates** the dead. Neither is used as a number. Only two quotations reached the journey file: the *Buildings* line to the architects, and Theodora's burial-shroud line, marked as hers.
