# János Czetz / Juan Fernando Czetz (1822-1904): research report

*Slug: `janos_czetz`. Gidófalva (Háromszék, Transylvania) to Buenos Aires: Habsburg cadet, honvéd general at twenty-six, ten years of European exile, forty-four years as an Argentine surveyor and founding director of the Colegio Militar de la Nación.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or inference, with the inferential step stated. Contradictions are flagged in place and again in section 8; none is adjudicated here.

**Standing caution on the source base.** Almost the whole biography reaches this report through three Wikipedia articles (hu, en, es) that do not agree with each other and that, for the most quotable material, footnote a single amateur secondary page. Two primary texts by Czetz were reached in full and machine-searched: *Bem erdélyi hadjárata 1848-49-ben* (Pest, 1868) and *Emlékezéseim* (ed. Kedves Gyula, Budaörs, 2001), both digitized at MEK. What could be pinned to those two books is pinned; the rest is labelled as encyclopedia.

---

## 1. Origin: Gidófalva and the Armenian question (1822)

**Born 8 June 1822 at Gidófalva (Ghidfalău), Háromszék, Transylvania, son of a Habsburg cavalry officer. [A: hu.wikipedia, "Czetz János"]**

Coordinate correction: **Ghidfalău is at 45.9044 / 25.8411 [A: ro.wikipedia]**. An earlier working pin of 45.8494 / 25.8264 falls about 6.3 km south of the village, in open country nearer Sfântu Gheorghe.

**The Armenian descent is an attribution, not a document. [R]** The Hungarian National Library (OSZK) catalogues him as *örmény származású*, of Armenian origin, and the Embassy of the Republic of Armenia in Argentina reportedly gives his Armenian name as **Hovhannés Tzetzian**. Neither the catalogue record nor the embassy page was fetched directly in this pass; both reach the report through the research brief. Family tradition adds a genealogy: Armenian settlers surnamed **Vártán** reaching Transylvania around **1672**, the name entered as **Czetz** in Szamosújvár civic records in **1715** **[R: hu.wikipedia genealogical note]**. No parish register, charter or family tree was seen. Two institutions of standing assert Armenian origin and no primary document confirms it; the journey file carries the claim as attribution and names the gap inside the campa.

This is why the subject sits in the corpus at all: `QUEUE.md` line 314, the entry for **Arshag Shorjian**, states that Shorjian "joins janos_czetz already queued in the corpus's Armenian-diaspora material" **[A: QUEUE.md]**.

## 2. The cadet ladder (1829-1846)

- **c. 1829, Kézdivásárhely military preparatory institute**, entered at about seven after his father, who had broken family tradition by taking a commission, died early. Latin, German, geography, surveying. **[A: hu.wikipedia]** Coordinate correction: Târgu Secuiesc is at **45.9969 / 26.1406 [A: en.wikipedia]**, not the working notes' 45.8484 / 26.0827, which lands some 17 km south-southwest of the town.
- **Theresianum, Wiener Neustadt**, first in his class. **[A: hu.wikipedia]** Pin 47.8121 / 16.2422, within about 250 m of the academy.
- **1842, commissioned into the 62nd Infantry Regiment as *hadnagy*. [A: hu.wikipedia]** *Hadnagy* is **lieutenant**, not captain; the working note mistranslated it. The captaincy comes later: joining the honvéd in July 1848 he is a *főszázados*, senior captain.
- **1844, Pest: *Magyar hadnyelvtan*** (Trattner-Károlyi Ny.), a Hungarian military language for officers of the imperial-royal army, arguing that troops be commanded in their mother tongue. **[A: hu.wikipedia works list]** The working notes dated this **1842** and placed its writing at Wiener Neustadt as a grammar for German-speaking cadets. Both wrong: 1844, published at Pest, and a military-language manual. The 1842 date belonged to the commission and had been collapsed into the publication.
- **1844**, weighed for the Hungarian Academy of Sciences alongside János Korponay and Lázár Mészáros; not elected. **[A: hu.wikipedia]**
- **1846, Geographical-Cartographic Institute, Vienna. [A: hu.wikipedia]** The hinge of the life: this surveying discipline is what he sells in Argentina fourteen years later.

## 3. The revolution (July 1848 to May 1849)

- **July 1848**, leaves Vienna for the organizing honvéd army. **[A: hu.wikipedia, date only]** The enlistment site is **not documented [R]**; Pest stands in the journey file as the ministry's seat and probable point of transfer, stated as such in the campa.
- **September 1848**, War Ministry, southern (délvidék) campaigns under Lázár Mészáros. **[A: hu.wikipedia]** No towns are named anywhere reachable; the Bácska pin (45.25 / 19.6667) is a regional placeholder **[R]**.
- **30 October 1848**, promoted *őrnagy* and made temporary commander of the roughly ten thousand Hungarian troops in Transylvania. **[A: hu.wikipedia]** **Headquarters unknown [R]:** Kolozsvár is the obvious guess and probably wrong, since it fell to Wardener in mid-November and the force retreated west to Csucsa. The journey file uses Kolozsvár for the appointment and Csucsa for the holding action, saying in both campas that the seat is inferred.
- **The Csucsa pass action is Czetz's own [A, attribution corrected].** An earlier note credited the halting of Puchner at Csucsa to Bem with Czetz as chief of staff. The sequence runs the other way: Czetz held the line as temporary commander in November, **before** Bem arrived; Bem then reorganized the army already standing there and launched the counteroffensive from it in December.
- **1 December 1848, Bem takes command in Transylvania. [A: hu.wikipedia, "Bem József"]** The date **29 November 1848** belongs to Bem's appointment and to Czetz becoming chief of staff, not to Bem's assumption of command in theatre; the two had been compressed into one entry.
- **Bem's first address to the officer corps, Szilágysomlyó. [A: Czetz 1868, printed p. V]** Verified by direct extraction from mek.oszk.hu/09600/09664. Hungarian: "Uraim, föltétlen engedelmességet kívánok önöktől; a ki nem engedelmeskedik, azt agyonlövetem. Én tudok megjutalmazni, de tudok büntetni is. Elmehetnek!" Corrections: the page is **V**, not VII; and Czetz writes that Bem spoke *lengyel hangnyomatu német nyelven*, in German with a Polish accent, so the Hungarian is Czetz's own rendering. Flagged and not silently applied: *kívánok* is closer to "I require" than "I demand"; the journey file carries the pool's translation as it stands.
- **18 December 1848, the counteroffensive out of the Csucsa pass. [A: hu.wikipedia]**
- **"Nincs kegyelem!!" on the Marosvásárhely road. [A: Czetz 1868, printed p. VIII]** Page confirmed. Roles inverted in the working note: the two hussar NCOs who disobeyed were the **condemned**, and two of their comrades, then two officers of the same company, pleaded for them; Bem lifted the sentence only after Kiss Sándor interceded. The **date 1848 is unsupported**: the passage is undated and sits in a character sketch, and Marosvásárhely came under Bem's control in January 1849. The journey file dates it 1849 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
- **12 February 1849, promoted *ezredes* (colonel) by Bem. [A: hu.wikipedia]**
- **2-3 March 1849, Medgyes (Mediaș). [A, reframed]** On 2 March Czetz repulsed Puchner's advance on the town: *"Március 2-án Czetz verte vissza Puchnernek a város felé törő seregét"* **[A: hu.wikipedia, "Medgyesi csata"]**. But the engagement ran two days, **Bem was present**, and on 3 March he evacuated Medgyes after a bayonet action. The working claim, a victorious battle led by Czetz in Bem's absence, is wrong on both counts.
- **11 March 1849, Nagyszeben (Sibiu) taken; Bem recommends Czetz for general. [A]** **8 April**, Hungarian Military Order of Merit, 3rd Class **[A]**. **May**, promotion approved **[A]**. **29 May**, riding accident while acting Transylvanian commander during Bem's Banat campaign, ending his combat role **[A: hu.wikipedia]**; the **place of the accident is nowhere documented [R]**, and the journey file pins it to Kolozsvár as presumed seat of command and says so.
- **Bem's characterization of Czetz** ("Relentless severity, impartiality, harshness in punishment, but magnanimity in reward…"): the Hungarian is on hu.wikipedia and was string-matched there, **but the working attribution to *Emlékezéseim* is false. [R]** The footnote is to Jakab Erzsébet, "Csapatnévadónk: Czetz János…", rmcssz.ro, a scout troop page archived 2012-01-30. A full-text search of the digitized *Emlékezéseim* for *Kérlelhetetlen*, *pártatlanság*, *jutalmazásban* and *bosszúáll* returns nothing.
- **Two quotations describe scenes Czetz did not witness**, since he was out of command from 29 May and in hiding: the ten officers of the 37th battalion bringing their banner to Bem at Déva, August 1849 (p. 183), and **Inczédy Samu** answering Schlick at Arad after Kazinczy's surrender (**printed p. XXI, not p. IX**; verified verbatim, with the shoulder-pat and the twelve-year fortress sentence confirmed in context). Both are stops in the journey file, and both campas say he records rather than witnesses.

## 4. The decade of exile (1850-1859)

- **1850**, evades the Habsburg authorities inside Hungary and escapes to Germany **[A: hu.wikipedia]**; the route and the months underground are a **gap**.
- **1850, Hamburg**, publishes *Bem's Feldzug in Siebenbürgen in den Jahren 1848 und 1849*. **[A: hu.wikipedia]**
- **1850s**, exile across Paris, the Ottoman Empire and Switzerland, with engineering work on the Mont Cenis railway. **[A: hu/en.wikipedia]** No dates or addresses for the Paris interval **[R]**, no worksite named within the Mont Cenis project **[R]**; both pins are regional and both campas say so.
- **Crimean War era, Istanbul**: with **György Klapka**, negotiations over a Hungarian legion against Russia; the plan collapses when Austria stays out of the war. **[A: hu.wikipedia]**
- **11 July 1859, Armistice of Villafranca**: Czetz withdraws the legion he had organized in Italy. **[A: hu.wikipedia]** His physical presence at Villafranca is **not established [R]**.
- **Seville, c. 1859, marriage to Basilia Ortiz de Rozas**, daughter of General **Prudencio Ortiz de Rozas**, brother of Juan Manuel de Rosas. **[A: en.wikipedia]** He met Prudencio in Spain, and the marriage is what convinced him his future lay in Argentina, so it **precedes** the 1860 emigration. An earlier entry putting the marriage in Buenos Aires in 1860 is wrong and contradicts the geography.

## 5. Argentina: the surveyor (1860-1875)

- **1860, Buenos Aires**, arrival; takes the name **Juan Fernando Czetz**. **[A: hu/en/es.wikipedia]** Argentine regulation reserving general's rank for the native-born means he serves as **colonel**. **[A: hu.wikipedia]**
- **1861**, revalidates his land-surveyor's licence; surveys in the south of Buenos Aires province. **[A: es/en.wikipedia]**
- Taken into the army's engineering section, reportedly through **Lucio V. Mansilla**; draws the official border maps with Paraguay and Brazil. **[A: en/es.wikipedia]** An institute name ("Geographic-Military Institute") circulates with **no dates and no address [R]**. **1865**, promoted colonel during the War of the Triple Alliance, with illness confining him to bed for months. **[A]**
- **1866**, lays out the town plan of **Rojas**; plans rail and road routes in the **Santa Fe** region, including toward the colony of **Esperanza**. **[A: en/es.wikipedia]** The Esperanza dating is loose **[R]**.
- **1869, the frontier line. [A: es.wikipedia, "General Villegas"]** Sarmiento's government charges *"el Cnel. de ingenieros Juan Czetz y al coronel Antonio Benavides, jefe de la Frontera Sur de Santa Fe"* with surveying the new line: **Fortín N.º 8 República, Fuerte Coronel Gainza, Fortín Díaz**. Three corrections: the year is **1869**, not 1868 (Sarmiento took office 12 October 1868); **Benavides was a colonel and head of the Santa Fe southern frontier, not an engineer**; and the fortín line lies in what is now **northwestern Buenos Aires province**, not southern Córdoba. Only en.wikipedia's loose phrasing puts it in Córdoba, and `QUEUE.md` line 312 repeats it. **Flagged, not resolved.** Coordinate correction: General Villegas is at **-35.0333 / -63.0167**, not the working notes' -34.9167, which sits about 13 km north. The town is not founded until an **1888** decree naming Arbolito as seat, confirmed as Los Arbolitos in **1896**: the survey precedes the settlement by nearly two decades. **[A]**
- **1875 onward, Paraná**: chief of the topographic department of Entre Ríos, producing *"los planos catastrales y topográficos de esa provincia completa."* **[A: es.wikipedia]** The working phrase "the first complete provincial survey leaving no unmapped zones" is an **embellishment** and is dropped.

## 6. The Colegio Militar de la Nación (1869-1875)

- **1869, Buenos Aires**: Czetz's own account, under the *Emlékezéseim* heading "A Colegio Militar megszervezése. 1869," of being offered the directorship of the Ferrocarril Central Norte by Minister **Vélez Sarsfield** on **Sarmiento's** behalf, and declining because he had already promised Minister **Gainza** he would found a Colegio Militar. **[A: Czetz, *Emlékezéseim* (2001), p. ~125, verified in the digitized MEK PDF]**
- **11 October 1869**, the academy established by law under Sarmiento, housed in the former Rosas *caserón* at **San Benito de Palermo**. **[A: es.wikipedia]** **Correction, and a good story lost:** the working entry had the building reaching Czetz "through his marriage into the Rosas family." That must go. The property had belonged to Rosas and was **expropriated by the state after 1852**; no source names a donor. The date and the location survive; the donation and the Seville loop do not.
- **22 June 1870**, Colonel Juan Czetz designated **first director**; first courses begin **19 July 1870**; he holds the post about five years. **[A: es.wikipedia]** The academy moves to **El Palomar in 1937**, which is where his remains go in 1969.
- **A treatise on permanent and temporary fortification [A: en.wikipedia]**, undated in the source: a **gap**.

## 7. Old age, death, afterlife

- **The pension offer. [A, figures corrected]** hu.wikipedia: *"Széll Kálmán miniszterelnök 1920 koronáról 4000 koronára emelte fel a nyugdíját, majd később már 8000 korona évi díjat is kész lett volna juttatni."* Széll **raised** it from **1,920 to 4,000** korona and **would later have been willing** to grant 8,000. The working claim, "from 1,920 to 8,000," collapses the offer into the raise. Széll was prime minister 1899-1903, which dates the refusal to **c. 1900-1903, not the 1890s**.
- **The letter to Albert Deáky [A: hu.wikipedia wikitext, string-matched]**: *"…Én már öregember vagyok, hazámtól már oly rég távol, és mégis a honvágy hatása folytán még mindig sajog a szívem ama tudattól, hogy idegen porban kell megnyugodnom…"* Grounds confirmed in the fuller passage: doctors' advice and his daughter **Krisztina's** serious lung disease. **Provenance caveat:** footnoted, like Bem's characterization, to the same rmcssz.ro scout page.
- **1895**, retires after thirty-five years. **[A: hu.wikipedia]** The working note cited en.wikipedia, which does not mention it; the citation is moved. A second overreach dropped: **"professor" is unsupported**.
- **6 September 1904**, dies in Buenos Aires at eighty-two **[A]**; buried in the **Cementerio de la Recoleta**, in the **Ortiz de Rozas family vault**, not a tomb of his own **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **1969**, remains moved to the chapel of the Colegio Militar at El Palomar during the institution's centennial (created 11 October 1869, so 1969 is the centenary). **[A: hu/es.wikipedia]**
- **The tomb inscriptions. [R: hu.wikipedia]** The original Spanish plaque named him only as an Argentine colonel and the academy's first director, with no mention of Hungary; after requests from Argentina's Hungarian community a second inscription was added stating that a Hungarian general and Transylvanian commander sleeps his eternal sleep there. **No date is given for the second inscription: a gap.**
- **Statues, corrected. [A: hu.wikipedia]** In front of the Argentine academy stands a **life-size standing bronze** (*életnagyságú bronzszobor*), **not equestrian**. The **equestrian** statue is in Budapest, unveiled **4 October 2019** at Lékai Bíboros tér, **Békásmegyer**. A **bust** is inside the academy **[R]**. **Czetz János utca** runs in Budapest District III (Óbuda) with plaques at its entrance **[A]**. At Gidófalva there is a **memorial plaque on the town hall wall**, **not a monument**. A **relief** stands in the courtyard of the Armenian Catholic church at **Gyergyószentmiklós** (46.7236 / 25.5951) **[A]**. The engineering unit he organized is said still to wear the dress uniform he designed **[R, uncorroborated]**.

## 8. Contradictions and open questions (not adjudicated)

1. **Córdoba or Buenos Aires province.** en.wikipedia and `QUEUE.md` line 312 place the frontier advance in **southern Córdoba**; the documented fortín line lies in **northwestern Buenos Aires province**. The journey file follows the fortines and says the sources disagree.
2. **Armenian descent.** Institutional attribution (OSZK, Armenian Embassy) against an empty archive; neither page fetched directly in this pass.
3. **The El Palomar coordinate.** Three values circulate: es.wikipedia's **-34.6008 / -58.6031**, the working note's -34.6062 / -58.6152 (about 1.2 km off), and the corpus's own `juan_peron.journey.json` pin at **-34.5977 / -58.606** (about 400 m off). The journey file uses **-34.6008 / -58.6031**; the divergence from the Perón file is flagged and left standing.
4. **Provenance of the two most-quoted sentences.** Both trace on hu.wikipedia to rmcssz.ro, not to an edition; the Bem characterization is demonstrably **absent** from *Emlékezéseim*, to which it had been misattributed.
5. **Medgyes.** A victorious battle led by Czetz in Bem's absence, against hu.wikipedia's own two-day engagement with Bem present and the town evacuated on day two.
6. **"Hadnagy."** Lieutenant, not captain, in 1842.
7. **Undated items**, all left as gaps: the fortification treatise; the second tomb inscription; the months of the 1850 escape; the July 1848 enlistment site; the Bácska towns; the site of the 29 May 1849 riding accident; the installation dates of the Gyergyószentmiklós relief, the Óbuda street naming and the Gidófalva plaque.
