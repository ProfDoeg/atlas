# Charles Koch: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-23 for slug `charles_koch`. Subject living (b. 1 November 1935, Wichita, Kansas). Coverage 1935 to August 2026.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, dossier-only, or contested. Disagreements are printed and left open, never adjudicated by preference.

## 1. Wichita and the household, 1935 to 1953

- **Birth [A].** Charles de Ganahl Koch, 1 November 1935, Wichita, second of four sons of Fred Chase Koch and Mary Clementine Robinson Koch. Forbes (refetched 2026-08-23) gives age 90 in August 2026, fixing the year; day and month from en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Koch. No hospital or address established.
- **The chores [A].** Farm labor from about age six: dandelions, milking, manure, fence, in a wealthy household kept deliberately austere. Wikipedia, with the family's own telling behind it.
- **Eight schools, Culver among them [R].** Dossier's family-history citation only. A fetched Culver Academies page confirms the school and does not mention Koch; the expulsion-for-drinking-on-a-train story is dossier-only.
- **The father's politics [A, Fred's].** Fred Koch was a founding member of the John Birch Society, 1958, when Charles was twenty-two and at MIT. Household background, never a scene.
- **Ancestry, none of it his.** Grandfather Hotze (later Harry) Koch, born Workum, Friesland, 1867 [R], emigrated 1888, settled at Quanah, Texas, merged two papers into the Quanah Tribune-Chief [A, Texas Observer]. Fred Chase Koch born Quanah 1900 [A]; Rice Institute **1917 to 1919** [A, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_C._Koch], not "the 1920s" as the pool had it. **None of these places is a stop on this file.**
- **Winkler-Koch abroad [A, Fred's].** Fifteen oil-cracking units in the Soviet Union 1929 to 1932; 1934 cracking technology for a refinery near Hamburg later used by Nazi Germany (Time, dossier citations, not refetched). Koch Industries and Mayer dispute how far the refinery depended on that contract; the contract is documented. Charles was born in 1935.

- **The Freedom School [R].** Robert LeFevre's school at Larkspur, Colorado, circa 1961, later trusteeship: dossier-only. A fetched LeFevre article confirms the school there 1956 to 1973 and does not mention Koch.

## 3. The company, 1961 to 1969

- **The return [A].** At his father's request, back to Wichita at twenty-five to run Koch Engineering alongside the oil and refining business (Schulman, *Sons of Wichita*, via dossier).
- **The presidency [R, contested].** Dossier: **1966**. Wikipedia: **1967**. Forbes: "Chairman of Koch, Inc. since 1967." No fetched source supports 1966. **The pool's [A] tag is unearned**; demoted, both years printed in `date_confidence`.
- **Chairman and CEO [A].** Fred Koch died 17 November 1967, heart attack while duck hunting near Ogden, Utah; David Koch's "magnificent shot" account at en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_C._Koch. Charles, thirty-two, took a company of about three hundred employees. The exact hunting site is undocumented, **it is Fred's location and not Charles's**, and no source places Charles on the trip. **There is no Utah stop**; the succession is staged at Wichita.
- **The name [A, predecessor disputed].** Renamed Koch Industries, Inc., 1968, in honor of Fred. Dossier says the predecessor was Rock Island Oil and Refining; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch,_Inc. says Wood River. The journey names neither and flags the split.
- **Pine Bend [A].** 1969: Koch's 35 percent in Great Northern Oil combined with J. Howard Marshall II's 15, reaching 50, blocking Union Oil, later buying Union out (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch,_Inc.; the pool's [R] was over-cautious). Charles's presence at the plant is undocumented.

## 4. The institutions, 1961 to 1999

- **Institute for Humane Studies [A institution / R Koch's dates].** Founded by F. A. Harper at Menlo Park, 1961; Koch later chairman emeritus, $12.4 million 2008 to 2012. His presence at the founding is not claimed; the stop is dated 1970-01-01 to order the segment. Pin 37.453, -122.1817, as in `murray_rothbard.journey.json`.
- **Cato Institute [A, and the pool's own lenses disagreed].** The chronology lens claimed a 1974 predecessor called the Charles Koch Foundation, renamed Cato in 1977; the geography lens gave a plain January 1977 San Francisco founding. **The geography lens is right.** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Institute: founded January 1977 in San Francisco by Ed Crane, Murray Rothbard and Charles Koch, named at Rothbard's suggestion after *Cato's Letters*, with no predecessor and no renaming. cato.org and Britannica returned 403, so the 1974 story is **dropped**. Pin **37.7749, -122.4194**, inherited byte-identical from `murray_rothbard.journey.json`.
- **Rothbard removed, 1981 [A].** The board, Crane-controlled with Koch backing, removed him after the Clark-campaign quarrel; Cato moved to Capitol Hill the same year. "Kochtopus" is attested in circulation from the 1980s, though its own Wikipedia article cannot source the coinage. **Coinage unresolved, persistence attested.**
- **The 1978 essay [A words / R primary].** "The Business Community: Resisting Regulation," Libertarian Review, 1978; libertarianism.org returned HTTP 429 every attempt. The words reach this file through *Mother Jones*, March 2015: "Our critics rightfully claim that we want socialism only for the rich." **The pool carried the four-word fragment alone; the full sentence is used.**
- **Mercatus and IHS, Arlington [A].** The Center for the Study of Market Processes begins at Rutgers in the late 1970s under Richard Fink, moves to George Mason in the 1980s, and is renamed Mercatus in 1999, sharing 3434 Washington Boulevard with IHS. Koch's own visits are undocumented.
- **George Mason funding [A, via one study].** Over $100 million across twenty-one grants 1988 to 2016, about 57 percent of his university giving in the period, per an academic tax-filing study (link.springer.com, 10.1007/s41996-020-00063-0), not refetched but named directly rather than through the dossier's paraphrase. Honorary doctorate from GMU, undated.

## 5. The brothers' war, 1980 to 2001

Anchored on **Koch v. Koch Industries, Inc., 203 F.3d 1202 (10th Cir. 2000), No. 98-3223**, fetched in full.

**1980 [A]** Bill Koch, with Frederick Koch and J. Howard Marshall III, attempts a boardroom takeover; it fails and Bill is removed from employment. **June 1983 [A]** Buyout of William, Frederick and the Simmons family, roughly 47.8 percent, at about $200 a share, group total approximately $1.1 billion. **1985 [A]** The bought-out shareholders sue over concealed material information, notably Pine Bend, seeking over $2 billion. **1998 [A]** After an eleven-week trial a jury finds information about the Pine Bend expansion was withheld but immaterial; verdict for defendants on all claims, no damages. **2001 [R]** Global settlement of most remaining family litigation, per the dossier and consistent with the case record.

**Quotation error, corrected.** The pool attributed two trial answers to Charles Koch: "Yes, Bernie Paulson had been talking about expanding the refinery to that number for many years" and "Well, it was a long-term objective, yes." **The words are genuine and the speaker is wrong.** In 203 F.3d 1202 the witness in both exchanges is **David Koch**. The pool's citation was wrong twice besides: there is no *Sterling v. Koch Industries*, and the year is 2000, not 1998 (98-3223 is a docket number). **Neither quotation appears in a `quote` field.** The exchange is narrated in the 1998 stop with David named as the witness.

## 6. Spills, measurement, and the criminal docket, 1996 to 2001

- **Lively, Texas, 24 August 1996 [A].** An eight-inch Koch Pipeline LPG line ruptured, leaking butane; the cloud ignited when two teenagers drove a pickup toward it to report the smell. Both were killed. A Texas jury awarded $296 million in 1999 (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch,_Inc.). CBS News, "Blood and Oil," fetched, does not mention Charles Koch personally. **Ages:** the pool gave Danielle Smalley as eighteen and Jason Stone as seventeen; **no reachable source gives ages**, the cited UPI archive returned 403, and Smalley is generally reported as seventeen. **The ages are dropped, the names kept.**
- **False Claims Act verdict, December 1999 [R].** 24,587 false claims by Koch Oil in measuring crude bought from federal and Native American leases, roughly $553,504 in royalty underpayments (57 F. Supp. 2d 1122, dossier citation, not refetched). Koch's testimony, "There's always a problem of accuracy in the oil field in measurement," from CBS News [A].
- **EPA settlement, 13 January 2000 [A].** $30 million civil penalty, then the largest civil environmental fine under federal law, over more than 300 oil spills across six states 1990 to 1997 (epa.gov, fetched).
- **Corpus Christi, 2000 to 2001 [R].** 97-count indictment over benzene controls, hazardous waste and false statements at the West Plant refinery; April 2001 plea to one false-statement count on wastewater compliance, $10 million in fines plus $10 million in environmental projects, most counts and all individuals dismissed. Both DOJ releases are dossier citations; the 2001 one returns **403**.

## 7. Network, purchases, houses, 1999 to 2013

- **Donor seminars, 2003 [A year / R headcount].** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Together: "In 2003, Charles Koch began hosting annual meetings of business leaders and philanthropists." **Two corrections:** the pool's "about seventeen donors" is in no reachable source and is dropped, with Mayer's roughly fifteen attributed to her by name in the campa; and the seminars were **annual** at first, not semiannual.
- **Charles Koch Arena [A, dates split].** A $25 million renovation across 2002 to 2003, enabled by a $6 million endowment, the arena taking his name in **2003** (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Koch_Arena); the Charles Koch article dates the gift to **2002**. The pool collapsed both into 2002 in two lenses. **Donation 2002, renaming 2003.**
- **Purchases [A/R].** Georgia-Pacific 2005, $48 a share, $13.2 billion equity, about $21 billion enterprise value (the dossier's SEC release returned 403; the Wikipedia article confirms). INVISTA from DuPont 2004 [A]; Molex for $7.2 billion 2013 [R, dossier only]. His presence at any of these headquarters is undocumented.
- **Cato shareholder suit [A, dates tightened].** Niskanen died October 2011; Charles and David filed in Kansas in **March 2012**; settled June 2012 with Crane's retirement, an enlarged board, and abolition of the shareholder structure. The pool's "2011 to 2012" is tightened. The Kansas division is unidentified, so the stop uses the general Wichita pin and says so.
- **Residences [A].** Two houses at The Vintage Club, Indian Wells, one reportedly $10 million in 1999; a residence in Aspen; principal residence Wichita. Both non-Wichita pins are area approximations, no property confirmed.
- **Honours, all undated [A].** Honorary doctorates from Washburn, Babson and George Mason, and a distinguished citizen award from Kansas State, all attested in the Charles Koch article, **none carrying a date in any reachable source**. The journey assigns ordering dates and says so in each `date_confidence`.

## 8. The late refashioning, 2014 to 2026

- **"I'm Fighting to Restore a Free Society" [A].** Wall Street Journal, **2 April 2014**: "I believe that cronyism is nothing more than welfare for the rich and powerful, and should be abolished," preceded by his acknowledgement that the company opposes such favors "even when we benefit from them." Reprinted at joedigenova.org, corroborated by AEI, 25 May 2022. **The pool's source line, "circa 2012, brainyquote.com," is replaced.**
- **Washington Post, 18 February 2016 [R].** Publicly agreeing with Bernie Sanders that corporate welfare rigs a two-tier economy. Not refetched.
- **Stand Together, 2019 [A].** The Seminar Network rebranded 20 May 2019, after his January 2019 statement that he was shifting away from partisan politics toward poverty, addiction, criminal justice and education.
- **David Koch, 23 August 2019, Southampton [A].** Retired from the company in 2018 for health; his 42 percent passed to Julia Koch (Forbes). Frederick Koch died February 2020 [A, dossier]. **Southampton is David's place of death**; the loss is staged at Wichita.
- **"Boy, did we screw up! What a mess!" [A].** **Written**, not spoken, in *Believe in People* (November 2020, with Brian Hooks), first reported by the Wall Street Journal, 13 November 2020. wsj.com is unfetchable here; corroborated by Business Insider and the Daily Mail the same day and Salon on 24 November. **Punctuation:** the pool had a period on the second sentence; it is an exclamation.
- **The $5.3 billion transfer [A, two sub-claims stripped].** Forbes, refetched: $5.3 billion in nonvoting Koch, Inc. stock, 2020 to 2022, to "a pair of nonprofits with fewer restrictions on lobbying and politics than traditional charities," nearly a tenth of his 42 percent stake, voting power retained. Forbes gives neither the $4.3 billion Believe in People figure nor the "largest publicly disclosed 501(c)(4) donation" claim. **Both stripped.**
- **Leadership and succession [A / R].** Dave Robertson co-CEO and vice chairman, Jim Hannan president and COO, 2 March 2023 (kochinc.com, fetched). Voting shares to pass to Chase Koch, with Chase and Elizabeth already holding equal nonvoting quantities, October 2023 [R, Forbes/Durot, not refetched]; four quotations from that interview are carried as [A] against the named article.
- **Milton Friedman Prize, 2025 [A recipient and year; the rest stripped].** Both the Cato and the prize articles list Charles Koch as the 2025 recipient; biennial, $250,000, previous recipient Jimmy Lai. **Neither gives a 1 May 2025 date, a venue, or a protest, and cato.org returns 403.** The pool carried the protest in two lenses, once pinned to a confirmed building. **Date and protest removed from prose, recorded as unverified in `date_confidence`.**
- **Books [A].** *Market Based Management* (2006/2007), *The Science of Success* (2007), *Good Profit* (2015), *Believe in People* (2020); *Becoming a Principle-Driven Leader*, with Chase Koch, 5 May 2026 [R, dossier only]. **Net worth [A]:** Forbes, $73.8 billion at age 90.

## 9. Gaps, and things that are not his

- **There is no tomb.** The subject is alive; the afterlife brief presupposes a death that has not occurred. No mausoleum, statue, coin or street name was found, and the only structure carrying his personal name is Charles Koch Arena. A hard gap, and nothing was invented to fill it.
- **The MIT Koch Institute is David's.** It came from David H. Koch's $100 million gift in 2007, dedicated 4 March 2011, and is routinely misattributed to "the Koch brothers." Its address is 500 Main Street, Cambridge MA 02142 (Building 76), not the "77 Massachusetts Ave" mailing convention the pool followed. Not a stop on this file.
- **Fred Koch has no corpus file.** No journey treats him; a grep for "Winkler-Koch" returns nothing. The 1929 to 1935 Soviet and German work needs Fred's own file.
- **Unverified leads, not carried.** A $150,000 Koch foundation grant to Berkeley Earth and Richard Muller's contrary findings; a Bloomberg-sourced Koch-Glitsch bribery lead involving a French compliance officer. Neither confirmed, neither in the corpus.
- **Negative finding.** `david_rockefeller.journey.json` mentions neither Koch, Koch Industries, nor Cato, and nothing was invented. The two donor networks are contemporaneous and structurally analogous and are not shown to intersect.
