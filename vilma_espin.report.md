# Vilma Espín Guillois (1930-2007): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `vilma_espin`. Companion file: `vilma_espin.journey.json`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim carried without independent confirmation. Contradictions are flagged and left standing, never averaged; unreachable sources are named with the reason. The research pool behind this file contradicts itself in several places, and in at least two the geography lens is right where the chronology lens is wrong. Every correction below is stated, never applied silently.

---

## 1. Santiago de Cuba, 1930-1948

**Born 7 April 1930 in Santiago de Cuba [A, English Wikipedia and EcuRed, agreeing on date, place and parents].** Father **José Espín**, a lawyer of means; mother **Margarita Guillois**.

**Contradiction, siblings, unresolved.** English Wikipedia gives four: Nilsa, Iván, Sonia, José. EcuRed gives five, adding **Liliana** and calling the last **José Alejandro**. Either EcuRed added a sibling or Wikipedia dropped one. No civil register was reached.

Education, all **[A, EcuRed and English Wikipedia]**: primary at the **Academia Pérez-Peña**; secondary at the **Colegio Sagrado Corazón**; ballet and singing at the **Asociación Pro-Arte Cubano**; **bachillerato en ciencias, 1948**. EcuRed describes the household as built on "ethics, austerity, and culture," the vocabulary a revolutionary state uses about a heroine's childhood, repeated here as characterisation **[R]**.

**Gap.** No street address for the Espín house, and no coordinate for the Pro-Arte building. The journey file pins the birth and the later safe-house use of the residence at the corpus's Santiago coordinate.

## 2. The Universidad de Oriente, 1950-1954

**Enrolls in chemical engineering at the newly founded Universidad de Oriente, 1950 [A, English Wikipedia and EcuRed].** Sings in the university choir; captains the volleyball team **[A]**.

**Takes part in student demonstrations against Batista after the coup of 10 March 1952 [A, Spanish Wikipedia and EcuRed].** No source reached names a specific march, date, or arrest.

**Degree in industrial chemical engineering, 14 July 1954, one of the first two women in Cuba to hold it [A, EcuRed: "El 14 de julio realiza el último examen"].**

**Contradiction internal to the dossier, the most consequential here.** Its chronology lens dated the degree **14 July 1955**, its geography lens **14 July 1954**; EcuRed supports 1954. Same day and month, one year apart: a transcription slip, not a source conflict. **1954 is used**, and the American year moves back to 1954-55.

---

## 3. Frank País and Acción Revolucionaria Oriental, 1953

**Meets Frank País García through the university revolutionary group and becomes his collaborator [A, English Wikipedia, Spanish Wikipedia, EcuRed].** País later folds the group into the 26th of July Movement.

**Name and place corrections.** The chronology lens called the group "Acción Nacional Revolucionaria (later Acción Revolucionaria Oriental, ARO)"; English Wikipedia has only **Acción Revolucionaria Oriental**, and no sourced predecessor by the other name exists in anything reached, so the two-stage name is dropped. The same lens gave the place as "Santiago de Cuba / Havana"; Spanish Wikipedia places the meeting in **Santiago** shortly after Moncada, and Havana is dropped.

**Moncada, 26 July 1953.** The Espín family house shelters attackers fleeing the failed assault **[R, Spanish Wikipedia, no document behind it]**. **She was not at the barracks**: she was a twenty-three-year-old engineering student who had joined nothing. The journey file places this stop at the family house, cites Moncada, and does not stage her at it.

## 4. Cambridge and Mexico City, 1954-1956

**Postgraduate study in chemical engineering in the United States, one semester only [A that she went; R that it was MIT].**

**Single-source flag.** English Wikipedia names the **Massachusetts Institute of Technology** and "one semester." EcuRed says only "concluir un curso de postgrado en Estados Unidos," naming no school. The MIT identification rests on one source and is carried as **[R]**, not **[A]**. That her father sent her north hoping distance would end her politics is likewise English Wikipedia's framing **[R]**.

**Mexico City, 1956: meets Fidel Castro and Raúl Castro, formally joins the 26th of July Movement [A, English and Spanish Wikipedia].** Serves thereafter as courier between the exile leadership and País's Santiago underground, using fluent English for international contacts **[A, English Wikipedia]**. No individual journey is dated and no message survives in anything reached.

## 5. The Oriente underground, 1956-1958

**30 November 1956: takes part in the Santiago uprising timed to cover the Granma landing [A, Spanish Wikipedia and EcuRed].** The rising fails to divert enough of Batista's forces; the landing goes badly regardless. **Her house serves as a Movement operational address [R, English Wikipedia, no document trail].**

**February 1957, the Herbert Matthews interview. Sources disagree and are not reconciled here.** EcuRed places her among those who guided the *New York Times* correspondent to Fidel Castro for the dispatch establishing that Castro was alive. English Wikipedia instead has her interpreting at a Castro press interview and arranging an intelligence contact. Both put her between the guerrilla and the foreign press; they differ on which episode and in what capacity.

**Location problem, unresolved.** The geography lens pinned "Comandancia de La Plata," 19.9958 / -76.9928. The Comandancia was Castro's *permanent* headquarters, established in **1958**, whereas in February 1957 the column was mobile with no fixed base; and the coordinate matches no published value, EcuRed placing the Comandancia in Bartolomé Masó, **Granma province**. The journey file uses the corpus's regional Sierra Maestra pin and records the site as undetermined, tag **[R]**.

**20 July 1957: named Provincial Coordinator for Oriente [A, EcuRed: "El 20 de julio de 1957, Vilma Espín asciende a la dirección de la lucha como coordinadora provincial del MR-26-7"].**

**Causal correction, important.** Both lenses of the dossier had her appointed *following* País's assassination, one dating it 31 July and one August 1957. EcuRed dates the appointment to **20 July, ten days before País was killed on 30 July**: it did not result from his death. The chronology lens also mis-dated the killing to 31 July.

**30 July 1957: Frank País killed by Batista's police [A, Spanish Wikipedia]**, in the **Callejón del Muro**, alongside Raúl Pujol, after a beating on calle San Germán. **The dossier's "San Agustín neighborhood" has no source and is dropped.** No coordinate for the Callejón del Muro was verified; the journey file uses the city pin and says so.

**1957-58, the CIA meeting.** In a meeting arranged by Bacardi executive **Pepín Bosch**, she tells CIA Inspector General **Lyman Kirkpatrick** that what the rebels want is "what you Americans have: clean politics and a clean police system." **[A as text, one remove from primary]**: the wording is Tom Gjelten's, *Bacardi and the Long Fight for Cuba* (Penguin, 2008), reached only through the English Wikipedia citation. **Gjelten's book could not be opened.** The date is given only as 1957 or 1958.

## 6. The Second Eastern Front, June-December 1958

**Six months in the Segundo Frente Oriental "Frank País" under Raúl Castro's command, where their relationship develops [A, all three sources].** The front was founded 11 March 1958 at Piloto del Medio, and it operated in the **Sierra Cristal**, not the Sierra Maestra, which the dossier conflates in two places.

**22 June 1958: English interpreter for the Second Front command during the seizure of American servicemen and technicians at the Moa and Nicaro nickel installations [A, EcuRed].** No coordinate for the plants was verified and **the outcome of the affair is in no source reached**; the journey file pins the command seat and states both gaps.

**Retrospective, paraphrase only [R].** EcuRed carries her late reflection on the difference between guerrilla combat and hiding underground, preferring the former. The primary wording was not verified, so it appears in no quote field in the journey file.

## 7. Havana, 1959

**1 January 1959.** Fidel Castro addresses Santiago from the city hall balcony on Parque Céspedes late on the night of 1-2 January **[A, Spanish Wikipedia]**. **Espín's personal presence in the square is not attested in anything reached [R]**; only her presence in the city is secure.

**26 January 1959: marries Raúl Castro Ruz [A, English Wikipedia, article "Raúl Castro"].** The geography lens called the date a genuine gap; it is not, only the **venue** is. Four children: **Deborah, Mariela, Nilsa, Alejandro Castro Espín [A]**.

**Contradiction, city of the wedding.** The geography lens pins Havana while stating the venue unknown; the interlock lens places the ceremony in **Santiago de Cuba**. Neither is documented. **Left standing.** The journey file pins Havana and flags the alternative.

**16 February 1959: counted as de facto First Lady from this date [A, English Wikipedia]**, the same date as Fidel Castro's swearing-in as prime minister. The role is a reference-work convention; Cuba has no such office and no Cuban source reached uses the term.

**1959: leads the Cuban delegation to the First Latin American Congress on Women and Children, Santiago de Chile [R, Spanish Wikipedia].** Proceedings not located.

**The agrarian reform draft, and a source that had to be re-attributed.** A declassified item in the CIA Reading Room (**CIA-RDP75-00149R000200580013-6**, read directly via archive.org) lists Espín among five drafters of the agrarian reform bill alongside Ernesto Che Guevara, Raúl Castro, Antonio Núñez Jiménez and Pinos Santos. **It is not a CIA assessment**: it is a clipping of **Drew Pearson's syndicated "Washington Merry-Go-Round"**, headlined "CIA Data Cited Against Castro," filed in the agency's press series, so Pearson is reporting what a CIA report allegedly said. The visible 1999 date is the archive.org upload, and **the column's own date could not be established**. The same clipping calls her "the sister of a noted French Communist and apparently a Communist herself," a period allegation of doubtful accuracy carried in the journey file as a hostile characterisation **of** her, never as fact.

## 8. The Federation, 1960-1976

**23 August 1960: founding president of the Federación de Mujeres Cubanas, held until her death [A, all three sources].** Forty-six years and ten months. The FMC raises emergency medical brigades within months **[A]**. **Venue [R]**: the founding hall is named nowhere reached, and the Habana Libre is the dossier's suggestion.

**1961-1965: the círculos infantiles and literacy / retraining programmes directed at former sex workers [R, Spanish Wikipedia].** Enrolment figures, budgets and programme records were not reached.

**1965: Central Committee of the Communist Party at its founding [A, all three sources]. 1967-1971: chairs the Commission for Social Prevention [A]. 1969: Director of Industrial Development, Ministry of Food Industry [A]**, with nothing recorded anywhere about what she did in it. **1971: president of the Institute of Childcare [A, English Wikipedia for all three.]**

**1975: the Family Code [A, English and Spanish Wikipedia].** She is its principal architect and public champion; it obliges shared male participation in housework and childcare and makes the obligation enforceable, among the first such provisions in Latin America. No promulgation date was carried in the pool, so the journey file defaults month and day.

**23 August 1975: the Orden "Ana Betancourt" [A, EcuRed]**, on the FMC's fifteenth anniversary. The chronology lens called it a **medal**; EcuRed calls it an **order**. Corrected.

**1976: deputy in the first legislature of the National Assembly [A, EcuRed]** and member of the **Council of State [A, English Wikipedia]**.

## 9. The conferences and the Politburo, 1975-2001

Spanish Wikipedia's summary of her international representation names the conferences and **not one word of what she said at any of them**. That is the whole reachable record for this phase: she leads the Cuban delegation to the world conferences on women at **Mexico City 1975, Copenhagen 1980, Nairobi 1985 and Beijing 1995 [A]**. She is also **a UN General Assembly delegate on women's and social issues, "1970s-1980s" [R, English Wikipedia; no session, no year, no speech]**, and the journey file's date for that stop is an explicit placeholder.

**1980: alternate member of the Political Bureau [A, Spanish Wikipedia and EcuRed]. 1986: promoted to full member, until 1991 [A, EcuRed].**

**Year correction.** The dossier dated the promotion to **1985**; EcuRed attaches it to the **Third Congress**, which met in **February 1986**, with 1991 and the Fourth Congress as terminus. The geography lens further blurs both steps into one "1980-1991" span; they are two, alternate 1980-1986 and full 1986-1991.

**23 March 2001: Venezuela's Order of Francisco de Miranda, First Class [A, EcuRed].**
**2 December 2001: Heroína de la República de Cuba [A, EcuRed].** The dossier called this "the state's highest civilian honour"; the title is conferred for **both civil and combat merit**, and the phrasing is corrected. Note that 2 December is the anniversary of the Granma landing.

## 10. Death and afterlife

**18 June 2007: dies in Havana at 4:14 p.m., aged 77, after a prolonged illness widely reported as cancer [A, English Wikipedia citing Cuban state media].** National mourning runs from 8 p.m. on the 18th to 10 p.m. on the 19th **[A, Spanish Wikipedia]**.

**Vigil at the Teatro Karl Marx [A, English Wikipedia].** Raúl Castro attends; **Fidel Castro is absent and no source reached explains why.** His withdrawal from public life by 2007 is the obvious answer and is not a stated one. Recorded as open.

**Cremated; ashes interred at the Mausoleo del Segundo Frente Oriental "Frank País" [A, English and Spanish Wikipedia].**

**Factual slip corrected. Frank País is not buried in the mausoleum that carries his name.** He lies in the **Santa Ifigenia cemetery** in Santiago, with José Martí and, from 2016, with Fidel Castro. The dossier's phrase "alongside the man whose death set the course of her revolutionary life" implies co-burial and is not used.

**April 2013: an elementary school in Havana named for her [A, English Wikipedia]. 2012: Pathfinder Press publishes *Women in Cuba: The Making of a Revolution Within the Revolution*, by Espín, Asela de los Santos and Yolanda Ferrer [A, pathfinderpress.com]**; the dossier gave the title as *Cuban Women*, **wrong, corrected**, and a Farsi edition (2021) is in the same catalogue. The FMC still publishes *Mujeres*, the magazine she founded **[A]**. Condolence letter from the Colombian ELN, signed by its full central command **[R]**.

**Stated gap.** No statue or freestanding monument surfaced in any source reached, in contrast to the Frank País and Celia Sánchez memorials in Santiago; her commemoration is institutional. **This is an absence in the material accessed, not a demonstrated absence in the world.**

## 12. Corpus interlocks

**Correction to the task framing.** The interlock lens asserted that `celia_sanchez.journey.json` "does not yet exist in working/journeys." **It exists**, as do `fidel_castro.journey.json` and `che_guevara.journey.json`. Only Espín herself was missing.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Santiago de Cuba 20.0247, -75.8219, Havana 23.1136, -82.3666 and Moncada 20.0264, -75.8192 (`fidel_castro`); Sierra Maestra 19.95, -76.85 (`che_guevara`); Habana Libre 23.1392, -82.3828 (`celia_sanchez`); MIT / Cambridge 42.3601, -71.0942 (`adi_shamir`); Santiago de Chile -33.437, -70.65 (`augusto_pinochet`). The interlock lens's prose proposed 42.3634, -71.0899 for MIT; the file on disk says otherwise, and the file on disk wins.

**Travelers named in campas, with the relation that earns it.** **`fidel_castro`**: met in Mexico City in 1956; she joined the movement he led, organised the Santiago rising meant to cover his landing, became his sister-in-law, and he was absent from her funeral. **`che_guevara`**: named at one stop only, on the strength of the Pearson clipping listing them among five drafters of the agrarian reform bill, a working relation on a weak source, and the campa says so in the same breath.

**Not staged:** **Celia Sánchez**, since although the two leading women of the same revolution near-certainly knew each other well, no source in this pool documents a meeting, letter or joint action, and shared movement plus shared geography is not enough. **Herbert Matthews**, whose trip into the Sierra is his own episode, cited in her stop rather than staged. **Raúl Castro**, husband and commander throughout but holding no journey file.
