# Henri Oedenkoven (1875-1935): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-28. Subject: the Belgian who bought Monte Monescia above Ascona in 1900, ran the Sanatorium Monte Verità for twenty years with Ida Hofmann, and died in São Paulo after two failed Brazilian sequels.*

**Legend. [A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, or a claim carried only by secondary compilation. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

---

## 1. Antwerp: origin and money (1875-1898)

- **Born 1875 in Antwerp**, son of a wealthy industrialist, filed as a Belgian merchant "sans confession". **[A]**: HDS 049204 (Schwab, 2009); de.wikipedia gives the identical bare fact. **No day, no month, no address**, no civil record reached: **[R]** for anything finer than the year. The pin is the Cathedral of Our Lady (51.2206, 4.4006, corrected from a figure ~330 m off), the city's landmark and **not** a birth site.
- His paternal grandfather, **also Henri Oedenkoven (d. 1871)**, was an industrialist and **former mayor of Borgerhout**, then a municipality in its own right (annexed to Antwerp only in 1983), and **uncle and guardian of the writer Georges Eekhoud**, orphaned 1865. **[A]**: nl.wikipedia, "Georges Eekhoud", which names him "industrieel en oud-burgemeester van Borgerhout" and sharpens earlier compilation's vague "Antwerp industrialist and politician". Borgerhout coordinates corrected to 51.2167, 4.4333; the earlier figure sat ~1.4 km SE, outside the district.
- **The candle factory is [R].** Belgian secondary writing locates the fortune in a candle works; no trade register or company record was found. The firm, the ownership share and how the son came to have free disposal of capital in 1900 are **an open gap**, as is everything **between 1875 and 1899**: twenty-four years with no reachable record.

## 2. Veldes: the cure and the meeting (1899)

- **Summer 1899, Arnold Rikli's natural healing institute at Veldes (Bled)**, then Austria-Hungary: he meets **Ida Hofmann**, a Viennese piano teacher eleven years his senior, and **Karl Gräser**, an ex-Habsburg officer. **[A]**: HDS 049204; en.wikipedia, "Monte Verità"; and decisively Hofmann's 1906 memoir, quoted verbatim in `ida_hoffmann.journey.json` from the Internet Archive scan (MonteVeritaWahrheitOD, p. 4): *"Im Sommer 1899 ... gewann mich Henri Oedenkoven für das in grossen Zügen geschilderte Zukunftsunternehmen."* The same page gives what brought him there: *"Krankheit bis zum Grabesrande führte ihn auf den Läuterungsweg des Vegetarismus."* The illness is not named.
- **Shore corrected.** en.wikipedia, "Arnold Rikli": Bled from 1854, institute 1855, villa and baths 1895, promenade 1899. Straža Hill and the surviving Vila Rikli lie **south/southeast** of the lake, so earlier compilation's "north shore" is unsupported. No source gives coordinates; the journey uses the corpus's Veldes pin (46.3599, 14.1076).

## 3. Munich and the road south (1900)

- **October 1900, Munich: the founding meeting**, at which the record calls the scheme "Oedenkoven's plan". Present: Oedenkoven, Hofmann, her sisters Jenny and Lilly, Karl and Gusto Gräser, Lotte Hattemer, Ferdinand Brune. **[A]**: en.wikipedia; corpus `ida_hoffmann`, `karl_graser`, `gusto_graeser`.
- **The Reformehe**, a free union refusing priest and registry on doctrinal grounds. **[A]** for fact and doctrine (de.wikipedia; the 1906 book, p. 18); **[R]** for place and date, since de.wikipedia gives 1900 and no place and Munich is used because the group had not yet reached Ticino. **The overland route** through Oberammergau and Cadenabbia is likewise **[R]**, a corpus reconstruction; **[A]** is only that six founders travelled through northern Italy and southern Switzerland that autumn (aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch).
- **Autumn 1900: purchase of Monte Monescia from Alfredo Pioda**, a Ticinese theosophist who had announced an unbuilt lay monastery, "Fraternitas", on the ground. **[A]**: en.wikipedia for Pioda as seller, de.wikipedia and HDS for the family capital.
- **SOURCE FAILURE FLAGGED: the price.** Earlier compilation gives 150,000 francs citing www4.ti.ch/can/oltreconfiniti. That page was fetched: **one line**, "Dal Belgio il primo proprietario del Monte Verità di Ascona", no price, no purchase sentence, no Pioda. **The figure is unsourced** and is not asserted in the journey.
- **Coordinates.** Survey gives 46.1589, 8.7629 (de.wikipedia and it.wikipedia). The journey instead inherits the **corpus's canonical pin, 46.1608, 8.7658**, byte-identical from `ida_hoffmann` and the other Ascona files, so shared events land on one point; the divergence is stated in the confidence field.

## 4. The cooperative collapses into a business (1901-1903)

- **First winter:** terraces cleared, timber air huts, sun and air bathing. **Mid-February 1902: the first sun bath**, from Hofmann's diary, one of the few dates in the first years fixed to a week. **Early spring 1902: the name**, "because our undertaking was founded by truth seekers", and in the same year the enterprise begins trading as **Sanatorium Monte Verità**. **[A]**: aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch; en.wikipedia.
- **1901: Gusto Gräser expelled** over the turn to fees; grotto below the meadow, over a decade. **December 1901: Karl Gräser leaves** for the same reason, settling with Jenny Hofmann under a rule that nothing may be accepted from anyone. **[A]**: corpus `ida_hoffmann`, `gusto_graeser`, `karl_graser`. **DROPPED ATTRIBUTION**: earlier compilation hung this split on the aboutswitzerland sun-bath sentence under an **[A]** tag, and that page never mentions the Gräser brothers. Kept and re-sourced; the false attribution is not reproduced.
- **DROPPED CLAIM: the orthography.** Earlier compilation credited Oedenkoven with "developing a distinctive orthography", tagged **[A]** to literaturkritik.de/id/6486. That review contains no such credit and no source reached supports it. **Not carried.**
- **1903: the promotional statement.** Two passages circulate via the Fondazione Beatrice Trussardi essay "Monte Verità", both confirmed in that page's raw HTML. (1) *"We make judicious use of all the tools made available by science and modern hygiene, we lack nothing."* Attributed there to Oedenkoven, 1903: **[A]** as a quotation from the essay, **[R]** as to the underlying document, unfootnoted and unseen. (2) *"We want everyone ... to have everything they want at their fingertips, without the help of servants..."* **ATTRIBUTION CORRECTED**: earlier compilation had this continuing the 1903 statement, but an authorial sentence intervenes and it carries **no attribution and no date**. Used with the speaker inferred and the date withheld.

## 5. The houses, the cure, the critics (1904-1913)

- **Casa Anatta**, residence and reception house: rounded corners, double timber walls, sliding partitions, domed ceilings, large windows. **[A]**: monteverita.org/history, verbatim. **DATE DISPUTED**: traditionally 1904, inaugurated 1905, but de.wikipedia reports newer scholarship placing construction **after 1907-08**, the house appearing in no earlier report or photograph, and states the **architect is unknown**. Earlier compilation's "personally directed the building" overstates and is not reproduced. Sigfried (not Siegfried) Giedion's *"a perfect example of liberated living"*, 1929, is from monteverita.org; de.wikipedia has him calling it "das originellste Holzhaus der Schweiz" in the *Schweizerische Schreinerzeitung*, 1934. Both may be genuine; the journey names which it uses.
- **BUILDING MISATTRIBUTION CORRECTED.** Earlier compilation put Mary Wigman's dancing and Hofmann's Wagner playing in **Casa Centrale**. monteverita.org assigns them to **Casa Anatta**: "Mary Wingman danced, Bebel, Kautsky and Martin Buber discussed, Ida Hofmann played Wagner". Casa Centrale it describes only as built for the community, planned for maximum daylight, yin-yang symbols in windows and balconies, demolished 1948. Scenes moved accordingly. The claim that "his friend Walter Hoffmann" built it is in **no source reached** and is dropped, and no construction year was found.
- **1905: Provisorische Statuten der vegetarischen Gesellschaft Monte Verità**, the one text most securely his own. **No printing, edition, translation or holding located**, known only through later quotation. **[R]**, and the sharpest bibliographic gap here.
- **The brochure on the vegetarian cure**, quoted in French on ymago.net, footnote 12, confirmed verbatim in raw HTML. **[R] as to authorship**: it is an unsigned institutional brochure, and the article does not disambiguate his wording from Hofmann's, whose 1905 pamphlet is cited nearby. **CITATION CORRECTED**: footnote 12's "op. cit." resolves to **R. Landmann, *Monte Verità, Die Geschichte eines Berges* (Berlin, 1930), p. 151**, not the "Ascona: Monte Verità" title earlier compilation gave.
- **1905: Erich Mühsam** stays and publishes *Ascona* (Locarno, 1905), the first insider account, favourable to Karl Gräser's practice rather than Oedenkoven's institution: *"Hotelpension mit ethischem Firmenschild"*, "ein Sanatorium wie andere auch". **[A]**: literaturkritik.de/id/6486; corpus `erich_muhsam`.
- **19 April 1906: death of Lotte Hattemer**, one of the seven founders. Rumour implicates a colony physician; **nothing adjudicated**, no finding named anywhere reached. **[A]** death, **[R]** rumour.
- **1906: Otto Gross** comes to break a morphine dependency, meeting Mühsam and Johannes Nohl. **April 1907: Hermann Hesse** climbs up from a Locarno cure; photographs place him with Hofmann and Oedenkoven. **[A]**: corpus `otto_gross`, `hermann_hesse`, `ida_hoffmann`.
- **1908: the Capri visit to Karl Wilhelm Diefenbach. [R]**, unconfirmed by any fetched source. **BUILDING CORRECTED**: en.wikipedia puts Diefenbach in the **Villa Giulia, Anacapri**, 1899 to his death in 1913, not the Casa Rossa. Pin moved to Anacapri (~40.5556, 14.2178).
- **1909: Villa Semiramis**, by the Turinese architect Anselmo Secondo, Piedmontese Jugendstil with triangular shutters: growth into fee-charging tourism. **[A]**: monteverita.org. aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch carries, **[R]** for sourcing, a period recollection with **no named author**: guests slipping to a village at night for salami and wine, Oedenkoven holding up the cheese rinds "as if it was poison".
- **Summer 1913: Rudolf von Laban opens his School for Art** on Hofmann's and Oedenkoven's ground, bringing Mary Wigman and Suzanne Perrottet. **[A]**: mediaguide.monteverita.org/en/the-history/1913-1918; corpus `laban`, `ida_hoffmann`.

## 6. The war years (1914-1918)

- **From 1914 he takes part in sessions of the Ordo Templi Orientis. [A]**: HDS 049204, plainly. No account of what he did or believed there was found.
- **August 1914: the colony scatters** as members become enemy nationals; the sanatorium stays open through the war. **c.1914-1916:** the partnership with Hofmann ends as a conjugal one while she stays in the household; he takes up with, and later marries in law, the English dancer **Isabelle Adderley**, born in India, of the Laban circle. Three children. **[A]**: de.wikipedia; corpus `ida_hoffmann`. No marriage date or children's names located.
- **15-25 August 1917: Theodor Reuss's Anational Congress** on the hill, sessions on cooperation, women, education and esoteric themes, closing with a ritual dance to the sun from sunset to sunrise. **[A]**: en.wikipedia; mediaguide.monteverita.org.
- **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED: the expulsion of Reuss.** en.wikipedia, "Monte Verità", says Oedenkoven "promptly threw Reuss out" over midnight rituals, calling him *"a charlatan and devourer of women"*. But en.wikipedia, "Theodor Reuss", has Reuss **resident and hosting** in August 1917 and leaving "some time before November 1918", no cause given; and HDS 049204 has Oedenkoven attending O.T.O. sessions from 1914. **The expulsion cannot be dated to 1917**, as earlier compilation had it. The journey places it at 1918-06, a placeholder inside the only window both accounts leave open. `theodor_reuss.journey.json` carries the same split.

## 7. Departure, Spain, and the deed (1920-1926)

- **1920: he leaves Monte Verità** with Adderley, the children and Hofmann; the founding era ends. **[A]**: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia; literaturkritik.de; corpus `ida_hoffmann`. Earlier compilation gave "January 1920": **no source reached is more precise than the year**.
- **Spain.** HDS 049204: *"tenta d'établir des colonies végétariennes en Espagne et au Brésil"*. **[A]** for the attempt, **blank for the geography**: no province, town, purchase, date or outcome anywhere reached. The journey pins a country-level placeholder and says so.
- **1923: made over to a Berlin group** to run as an artists' hotel: **Werner Ackermann, Max Bethke und Hugo Wilkens** (de.wikipedia); it.wikipedia lists four, adding William Werner. **CORRECTED**: earlier compilation **omitted Bethke**, and its citation, the www4.ti.ch page, does **not** contain the sentence attributed to it.
- **1926: Eduard von der Heydt buys the hill for 160,000 francs**, converting Casa Anatta to a private house for his collection; Mies van der Rohe approached, hotel built with Emil Fahrenkamp in 1929. **[A]**: de.wikipedia, it.wikipedia, monteverita.org. **CONFLICT FLAGGED**: aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch gives **1927** and 1928. 1926 has three backers and leads; the dissent is recorded, not dropped.

## 8. Brazil (c.1921-1935)

- **Monte Sol, Palmital region, Santa Catarina**, founded "sometime between 1921 and 1923, two decades after the Swiss colony", attributed by the source to **Ida Hofmann**. **[A]**: SciELO, *História, Ciências, Saúde-Manguinhos*, "Em busca do novo Éden no século XX".
- **Failure within about four years**, causes verbatim: the property "which proved relatively insalubrious" and "the lifestyle of those who went to live there". With a 1921-1923 start the collapse falls **c.1925-1927**, bracketed by Hofmann's death at São Paulo on 12 July 1926.
- **CORRECTED: "near Joinville" is not in the source**, which says only "the Palmital region of Santa Catarina". The purchase from "Empresa Industrial Agrícola Palmital Ltda." and the Christol 2015 citation likewise did not surface in the fetched text. Downgraded to **[R]**; the pin is the corpus's Brazilian placeholder, labelled as such.
- **Catalão, Goiás:** "a new attempt in the municipality of Catalão, Goiás, **in the 1930s**", also unsuccessful. **[A]** for the fact, **decade only** for the date; earlier compilation's flat "1930" is not a record. What he did there, who went, how long it lasted: **not recorded in any source reached**.
- **Death: 1935, São Paulo. [A]**, double-sourced: HDS 049204 and de.wikipedia. **Upgraded from the [R]** of earlier compilation, which treated HDS as unreachable; it is reachable. **No day, month, cause, address or burial located.** Hofmann's 1926 death in the same city puts the household demonstrably there.

## 9. Afterlife: no grave, and a hill belonging to somebody else

- **No monument, plaque, headstone or gravesite for Oedenkoven exists at Monte Verità or is documented anywhere.** The site's commemorative structures are the Elisarion pavilion ("Chiaro mondo dei beati", for Elisar von Kupffer's 1923 painting) and Hans Arp's *Roue Oriflamme*, 1962. Oedenkoven is not on the list. **[A] as positive confirmation of an absence**: de.wikipedia, commemorative-structures section, read directly. **The absence is the finding.**
- **1948: Casa Centrale demolished** for a restaurant, only the curving steps remaining. **[A]**: monteverita.org, verbatim. Its "1904, commissioned by Oedenkoven" gloss in earlier compilation is **not** on that page and is not carried.
- **1979/1981: Casa Anatta reactivated** for Harald Szeemann's exhibition and, since 1981, the site's History Museum (monteverita.org). The 1978 Ascona exhibition travelled to Zurich, Berlin, Munich and Vienna, and the Fondazione Monte Verità was founded in 1989 (de.wikipedia). The bulk of Szeemann's archive is at the **Getty Research Institute**, as the **Harald Szeemann papers, 1836-2010**, not the "1800-2011" of earlier compilation.
- **2003: Andreas Schwab, *Monte Verità: Sanatorium der Sehnsucht*** (Orell Füssli), showing from guest registers and business records that the house ran for two decades on "unternehmerischen Kriterien" (literaturkritik.de/id/6486). **2014: *Freak Out!* (Carl Javér)**, popularizing the counterculture-precursor framing, **[R]**: the cited luganolac.ch listing returned HTTP 403. **2021: *Monte Verità: Der Rausch der Freiheit* (Stefan Jäger)**, Michael Finger as Oedenkoven, Julia Jentsch as Hofmann, built around an invented protagonist, the photographer Hanna Leitner: historical fiction, not biography. **[A]**: de.wikipedia, which also corrects the runtime to **116 minutes**, not 110.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found.** Oedenkoven is not on the EXCEPTIONS.md list, and this research surfaced no relation requiring the exception in either direction: he propounded no framework a later hand applies backward, and nothing applies his own thinking forward past 1935.

Two adjacent judgements are recorded rather than made silently, so the operator can overturn them:

1. **The posthumous segment ("The Hill Without Him", 1926-2021) is kept**, because its stops are the fate of **his own ground and buildings**: the hill he bought, Casa Anatta which he designed, Casa Centrale which he had built. Szeemann, von der Heydt and the 2021 film act on his property and his name and are never given a scene of their own travel. The segment is separable if the operator disagrees.
2. **Charlotte Bara and Suzanne Perrottet were dropped from the campa text**, though both are in the interlock pool. Bara reaches him only through Szeemann's 1978 exhibition, forty-three years after his death; Perrottet only through Laban's school on ground he leased out. Neither is an attested relation. Recorded, not silently deleted.
