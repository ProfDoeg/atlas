# Francisco Salamone (1897-1959): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-16 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Francesco / Francisco Salamone, Sicilian-born architect and civil engineer, builder of the monumental civic program of Buenos Aires province under Governor Manuel Fresco, 1936-1940.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, with the source named in brackets. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or researcher's inference, including any date or coordinate that no source actually prints. Contradictions between sources are flagged and left open; they are not adjudicated here. Where a source is unreachable, it is named with the reason.

**Standing caution.** The bibliography is thin and circular: nearly every popular account derives from two encyclopedia articles plus Juan Forn's 2002 newspaper essay, and the two articles disagree on the graduation year, the burial, the building count and the university sequence. The detailed inventory (`Anexo: Obras de Francisco Salamone`) is more reliable than either and repeatedly corrects the English one. Several circulating coordinates are wrong by ten to nearly two hundred kilometres; those are itemised below.

---

## 1. Sicily and the crossing (1897 - c. 1905)

- Born **5 June 1897 at Leonforte**, province of Enna, Sicily, son of the architect and builder **Salvatore Salamone** and **Antonia D'Anna**, one of five children. **[A, es.wikipedia and en.wikipedia, "Francisco Salamone"]**
- Coordinate note: two passes give Leonforte as 37.6389/14.3903 and 37.6497/14.3947 **[R]**; the journey file uses the second, the difference being under two kilometres.
- The family **emigrates to Argentina while Francisco is a small child**. **[A]** The year is **not attested**: en.wikipedia gives only "between 1903 and 1906". The 1905 used in the journey file is the **midpoint of that window and appears in no source [R]**. An earlier research pass tagged this **[A]**, which is not supportable and has been corrected.
- **Gap:** no ship, no port of embarkation, no arrival document located. The interlock lens proposed inheriting the corpus's canonical **Genoa** pin (44.4056/8.9463) used for Gianotti, Colombo and Collivadino. **Rejected:** no source attests Genoa, or any port, for this crossing, and inheriting the pin would manufacture attestation.

## 2. Schooling and titles (c. 1910 - 1920)

- **Escuela Técnica Otto Krause**, Buenos Aires; graduates **maestro mayor de obras**. **[A, es.wikipedia, which also lists him among the school's distinguished graduates]**. The **year is not attested [R]**; an earlier pass asserted 1910, which is in no source.
- School address and coordinates: Av. Paseo Colón 650, Monserrat, **-34.6153 / -58.3686 [A, es.wikipedia]**. An earlier pass gave -34.6118/-58.383, about 1.4 km off; **corrected**.
- **Universidad Nacional de La Plata**: **contradiction, open.** es.wikipedia places a La Plata stage between Otto Krause and Córdoba; **en.wikipedia names only the Universidad Nacional de Córdoba** and omits La Plata entirely. Neither gives a year. Recorded in the journey file as **[R]** with the contradiction stated in `date_confidence`.
- **Two medals in 1919**, for designs exhibited at **Milan** and **Barcelona**. **[A, es.wikipedia]**. Neither exhibition is named, neither design described, and no prize catalogue has been located. **Gap.**
- **Degrees in architecture and civil engineering, Universidad Nacional de Córdoba.** **[A]** **Contradiction, open: es.wikipedia gives 1920, en.wikipedia gives 1917.** Nothing reachable adjudicates. The journey file dates the stop 1920 and carries the contradiction in `date_confidence`.

## 3. Córdoba: practice, politics, first public works (1920s - 1936)

- **Private houses and minor commissions in Córdoba province through the 1920s.** **[A, es.wikipedia]** for the decade; **no year is given [R]**. An earlier pass printed 1922 as attested, which is fabricated precision.
- **1923: loses an internal Unión Cívica Radical primary** for a provincial senate seat. **[A, es.wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the contest was in **Córdoba province, for the senate seat of the Punilla department**, not in Buenos Aires. One research pass pinned it at -34.6037/-58.3816, putting a Córdoba primary in the federal capital; the journey file pins it at Córdoba (-31.4201/-64.1888).
- **1928: marries Adolfina Croft**, of Austrian descent; four children, **Ricardo, Roberto, Ana María, Stella Maris**. **[A, es.wikipedia and en.wikipedia]**
  - en.wikipedia adds that Adolfina was **the daughter of the Austro-Hungarian vice-consul stationed at Bahía Blanca [A]**, but neither edition says the **wedding** happened there; siting it at Bahía Blanca is **[R]** inference, which one research pass made while tagging it attested. The journey file uses the canonical Buenos Aires pin (-34.6037/-58.3816, inherited byte-identical from victoria_ocampo.journey.json) and gives the reason in `date_confidence`.
- **Las Varillas, Córdoba: municipal building, May 1935 to 22 March 1936.** **[A, `Anexo: Obras de Francisco Salamone`]**. **Correction:** an earlier pass gave the range as 1934-1936; that range belongs to the **Villa María** works, not Las Varillas.
- **Villa María, Córdoba: municipal slaughterhouse, 1934-1936.** **[A, Anexo]**

## 4. The Fresco commission (1936-1940)

- **Manuel Fresco assumes the governorship of Buenos Aires province on 18 February 1936**; his term ends **7 March 1940**. **[A, es.wikipedia "Manuel Fresco"]**
- The program is financed through a **public works bond law**, aims to **"dignificar el perfil oficial y paisajista de la región"**, and runs under the motto **"Dios, Patria y Hogar"**. **[A, Forn 2002]**, though Forn attributes neither phrase to a decree or letter. **Flag.**
- **A personal friendship between Fresco and Salamone is unverified.** es.wikipedia's Fresco article states that the claim **"no ha podido ser comprobada"**, yet popular accounts repeat it as fact. **[R, flagged as unproven by the source reporting it]**
- **Scale.** **[A]** for "more than 60 buildings across some 25 rural districts, 1936-1940", a figure in **both** editions. **Correction of an earlier pass:** the 60-versus-70 discrepancy is **not** a Spanish/English disagreement; en.wikipedia's works section says more than 60, while **70 appears elsewhere in the same English article**. An **internal inconsistency**, left open.
- The English article's flat list of towns is less reliable than the `Anexo`; three failures are documented below.

### 4b. Errors in circulating accounts, corrected here

1. **"Carlos Pellegrini" is wrong.** The town is **Pellegrini**, seat of Pellegrini partido, at **-36.2667 / -63.1333** (es.wikipedia; Anexo: "Pellegrini, Municipalidad 1937"). The name comes from en.wikipedia, collides with a town in Santa Fe, and drags coordinates (-37.89/-62.69) **about 185 km off, into open country**.
2. **Chillar cannot have a municipal palace.** It is a locality of **Azul partido**. The Anexo lists "Delegación de Chillar, 1937". The "palacio municipal" phrasing is en.wikipedia's flattening of the list.
3. **Carhué's "matadero" and the Villa Epecuén matadero are one building.** es.wikipedia "Carhué" puts it "en el acceso a las ruinas de Villa Epecuén"; the Anexo lists it under Villa Epecuén. Earlier passes pinned two. **Merged.**
4. **There is no Salamone cemetery portal at Carhué.** The Anexo lists only the cross. Corrected.
5. **Coordinates corrected** against each town's own es.wikipedia article: Saldungaray -38.20/-61.7667 (two passes, 10 and 17 km off); Villa Epecuén -37.1315/-62.8101 (16-18 km); Salliqueló -36.75/-62.9167 (65 km); Alberti -35.0167/-60.2667 (20 km); Vedia -34.4979/-61.5423 (38 km); Otto Krause -34.6153/-58.3686 (1.4 km).
6. **Pilar is in Greater Buenos Aires**, about 50 km from the capital, not "in the provincial interior" as one pass had it. This matters, because it is also the district where he is buried.
7. **The Laprida cross superlative is unstable.** The Salamone article calls it "la segunda obra religiosa más alta de Sudamérica, después del Cristo Redentor"; the article on **Laprida** claims only the **second highest cross in South America, 30 metres**. No measurement source supports either; the journey file gives both as circulating claims.

## 5. Tucumán, Uruguay, and the return (1943-1945)

- **1943: a legal case over a paving contract at San Miguel de Tucumán**; on his lawyer's advice he leaves the country while it is pending. **[A, es.wikipedia]**. Charges, court and parties are described nowhere reachable. **Gap.**
- **1943-1945: lives in Uruguay.** **[A]** for Uruguay. **Montevideo is [R]**, a researcher's assumption; es.wikipedia names only the country. One research pass asserted Montevideo as attested; corrected.
- **1945: wins his claim against the state over unpaid professional compensation and returns to Buenos Aires**; the charges are afterward dismissed. **[A, es.wikipedia]**

## 6. After the program (1945-1959)

- Directs **urban paving works**; designs **two Rationalist buildings and a private residence**, and by en.wikipedia's account further **Rationalist condominiums through the 1950s**. **[A]** for the work, **[R]** for any year: nothing is dated. The 1950 in the journey file is a placeholder inside the decade.
- **Gap:** none of these post-war buildings has been identified by address in any reachable source.
- **Dies in Buenos Aires, 8 August 1959, aged 62.** **[A, both editions]**
- **Burial: contradiction, open.** es.wikipedia buries him at the **Cementerio Jardín de Paz, Pilar**; en.wikipedia buries him **first at Recoleta**, **transferred to Jardín de Paz later**, undated. One pass asserted the Pilar burial as **[A]**; that half stays **[R]** until the transfer question is settled. The pin (-34.4587/-58.9142) is the **Pilar town point**, not the cemetery, which could not be located exactly.

## 7. Afterlife and rediscovery

- **2001:** his works declared **cultural patrimony of Buenos Aires province**. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- **2014:** several buildings classified as **national historic monuments** and national historic-artistic assets, including the Carhué Palacio Municipal and its associated matadero. **[A, es.wikipedia "Francisco Salamone" and "Carhué"]**
- **1992:** **Alberto Bellucci, "Monumental Art Deco in the Pampas: The Urban Art of Francisco Salamone"**, **not a book** but an **article in *The Journal of Decorative and Propaganda Arts*** (es.wikipedia bibliography). An earlier pass took it for a monograph with a translated title; the title is English because the journal is. **[A]**
- **2007:** Tom Shaw and Edward Shaw, *Salamone, La Consagración*. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- Two documentaries circulate, **"Las minas del rey Salamone"** and **"Salamone Superstar"**. **[A]** for the titles; **directors and release years not established. Gap.**
- **2017:** a **Rome exhibition** presents him as **"el Gaudí de las pampas"**; ambassador **Tomás Ferrari** is quoted saying the work "representa también un claro ejemplo de la integración que tuvo la inmigración italiana en la Argentina". **[A, Infobae, 21 December 2017]**. **Citation correction:** the real headline is "Una muestra en Roma homenajea la obra del arquitecto Francisco Salamone, 'el Gaudí de las pampas'". Both quotes verify at the cited URL.
- **1985:** the flooding of **Villa Epecuén** on **10 November 1985** submerges the resort for roughly a quarter of a century, leaving Salamone's slaughterhouse at the entrance to the ruins. **[A, es.wikipedia "Villa Epecuén"]**

## 8. Style, and what he never said

Salamone **left no theoretical writing, no manifesto, and no interview** that survives in the reachable record. **This is the largest single gap in the subject.** Everything said about his intentions is said by other people:

- **Prof. Mario Sabugo** coined **"Futurismo Populista Bonaerense"** for the style, precisely because Salamone explained nothing. **[A, Forn 2002]**
- **Alejandro Novacovsky** calls the Azul cemetery complex an **"obra total"**. **[A, Cutuli 2015]**
- The encyclopedia's own characterisation of the Carhué palace blends **Art Deco, Italian Futurism, and Bauhaus-inflected functionalism**. **[A as a characterisation, es.wikipedia]**; it is a stylistic ascription by later writers, **not evidence that Salamone read Marinetti or met anyone in the Futurist circle [R]**. The journey file names Marinetti once, at Carhué, explicitly as what the catalogue-writers say is in the building.
- Two popular sayings from the building years, both from Forn: **"Lo que Fresco dispone lo construye Salamone"** and its corrective, **"No se mueve un ladrillo sin que lo diga Bustillo"** (crediting Alejandro Bustillo instead). **[A as sayings; undated beyond c. 1936-1940]**
- The Laprida story, in which the intendente **Martínez** stops the train carrying the disassembled portal at gunpoint ("El cementerio se queda acá"), rests **entirely on Forn 2002** and is corroborated nowhere else. **[A to Forn; uncorroborated]**

## 9. Interlocks considered, and what was rejected

Sixteen candidates were returned by the interlock lens. Almost all are **shared geography or structural parallel, not relation**, and the map already shows co-location without help from the prose. **Rejected as campa material:** the Italian-immigrant architect cohort (Palanti, Colombo, Gianotti, Collivadino, Buschiazzo, Troiani), no attested contact in either direction; **Christophersen's** 1920s debate with Virasoro, to which Salamone was not a party; **Catelin** as provincial works chief a century earlier, a structural parallel only; **Perlotti's** monuments on the same small-town circuit; the towns already in the corpus (Azul, Carhué, Balcarce, Pergamino) as pure place coincidence, with Balcarce a **name coincidence** besides; the **Collivadino "fresco" wordplay**, rejected outright; and **Gaudí**, whose name reaches Salamone only through 2017 exhibition publicity and is therefore carried in the journey file **as a quote and never as prose**. **Manuel Fresco** has no journey file and is not a corpus traveler, so he is named freely as the commissioning patron.

**The one interlock accepted in prose:** **F. T. Marinetti**, named once, at Carhué, as the source of the Futurist idiom the cataloguers identify in that building. An influence ascription made by the sources about the work, tagged as such above.

**Standing gaps, stated as gaps.** No writing by Salamone of any kind. No year for the Otto Krause title, the Córdoba houses, or the post-1945 Rationalist buildings. No arrival record for the emigration, only a three-to-four-year window. Graduation year unresolved (1917 vs 1920); burial history unresolved (direct to Pilar, or Recoleta first); building count unresolved inside one source (60+ vs 70+). The Fresco friendship unproven and flagged as such by the source reporting it. And nothing at all about the household: Adolfina Croft has a father's title and no recorded words.
