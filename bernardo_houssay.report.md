# Bernardo Alberto Houssay (1887-1971): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Argentine physiologist, first Latin American laureate in a science Nobel category, founder of IBYME and first president of CONICET.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference not carried by a source actually read. Contradictions are printed as contradictions and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason they failed.

**Standing caveat on the source base.** The two obvious authorities for this life, the Nobel Foundation's biographical page (nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1947/houssay/biographical/) and the Encyclopaedia Britannica biography, both returned HTTP 403 Forbidden on 2026-08-11. Several claims below carry "Nobel Prize biographical note (nobelprize.org)" as their source string because that is how the gathering pass recorded them, but the page was not readable here. Those claims rest in fact on the English and Spanish Wikipedia articles, plus one peer-reviewed biography (Singapore Medical Journal 2016, PMC4728695) that settles one date the Wikipedias disagree on. This is the largest weakness in the dossier.

## 1. Birth and schooling, 1887-1904

**[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia, "Bernardo Houssay"]** He is born in Buenos Aires on 10 April 1887, son of Albert Houssay and Clara Laffont, French immigrants. No source consulted names the house, the street, or the parish. The city is all there is.

**[R: Spanish Wikipedia, plus the school's institutional address]** He completes secondary schooling at the Colegio Nacional de Buenos Aires around 1900, at roughly thirteen. The address Bolívar 263 comes from the institution, not from the biographical source, which names the school and not the street.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** He is admitted to the School of Pharmacy of the University of Buenos Aires in 1901, at fourteen. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]** He graduates from the School of Pharmacy and Biochemistry in 1904, at seventeen. The two editions agree on age but reach it from opposite ends. **[R]** The faculty building he attended is not named by any source read; the journey file's coordinate is the historic university precinct near the Manzana de las Luces, a reconstruction and not an attestation.

## 2. Medicine, the physiology bench, the thesis, 1904-1911

**[A: English Wikipedia]** He enters the Faculty of Medicine in 1904 and studies there until 1910, taking a post as research and teaching assistant in the physiology department while still a student. By 1908 he is assistant lecturer in physiology, still an undergraduate.

**[A: English Wikipedia and Spanish Wikipedia]** He receives the medical degree with honours at twenty-three and publishes a doctoral thesis on the physiological actions of extracts of the pituitary gland. English Wikipedia dates the thesis 1911; Spanish Wikipedia frames the degree as taken at twenty-three, which given an April 1887 birth fits late 1910 or 1911. **The one-year spread is not resolved here.** The subject is not in doubt: the anterior pituitary is his from the first document he signs and still his when Stockholm calls thirty-six years later.

**[A: Anales de la Sociedad Científica Argentina, Tomo 75, p. 346, Wikisource table of contents]** A paper by Houssay with L. Giusti and C. Maag carries the verbatim title "Acción de los solutos hipofisiarios i de su principio activo sobre la secreción láctea". The table of contents was confirmed directly; the article's full text could not be reached. The old orthography ("i" for "y") is preserved as printed. This is the only verbatim quotation the research pool produced, and it is a paper title rather than anything he said. **Gap flagged: no letters, no speeches, no notebooks, no Nobel lecture text were reachable.** A life of six hundred papers yielded one line of quotable primary text.

## 3. Chairs, hospitals, and the founding of an institute, 1910-1920

**CONTRADICTION, flagged and not silently resolved.** The date of Houssay's appointment as professor of physiology at the University of Buenos Aires veterinary school:

- **1910.** Singapore Medical Journal 2016 (PMC4728695), peer-reviewed: "Upon his graduation in 1910, at the age of 23, he was made Professor of Physiology at the university's School of Veterinary Medicine." English Wikipedia concurs, placing the appointment immediately after the doctorate; the standard Nobel biography is reported to give the tenure as 1910-1919.
- **1912.** Spanish Wikipedia, alone.

The 1910 reading is carried by more sources and by the only peer-reviewed one; 1912 comes from the outlier. The journey file dates the stop 1910 and records the dissent in date_confidence. Neither reading is presented as settled.

**[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia]** In 1913 he becomes chief of clinical services at the Hospital Marcelo Torcuato de Alvear. **Coordinate correction applied:** the gathering pass gave -34.6027, -58.4547; Nominatim places Hospital Alvear on Avenida Warnes, La Paternal, at -34.5971, -58.4747, roughly 1.9 km away. **[R]** The hospital later became a psychiatric institution, so the coordinate is its current site, not a confirmed unchanged building.

**[A: English Wikipedia gives 1915; Spanish Wikipedia gives 1915-1919]** He is named chief of experimental pathology at the National Public Health Laboratories, the bacteriological institute later called the Instituto Malbrán. **Coordinate correction applied:** claimed -34.6339, -58.3719; Nominatim gives Avenida Vélez Sarsfield, Barracas, at -34.6426, -58.3898, again about 1.9 km off.

**[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia]** In 1919 he takes the chair of physiology at the Faculty of Medicine and founds its Instituto de Fisiología, which he directs until 1943.

**[A: Spanish Wikipedia]** In 1920 he marries María Angélica Catán and co-founds the Sociedad Argentina de Biología. **Correction to the gathering pass:** it asserted the society's founding premises were not named in the source. They are: Spanish Wikipedia says the founding took place "en la Asociación Médica Argentina". That gap was not real. **[R]** The marriage venue genuinely is one: no church, registry, or date within 1920 is given anywhere read.

## 4. Recognition from abroad, 1940-1944

Four foreign elections in five years, none of them evidence that Houssay travelled.

- **[A: English Wikipedia]** 1940, member of the United States National Academy of Sciences.
- **[A: English Wikipedia]** 1941, International Honorary Member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences. **Correction applied:** the gathering pass put the Academy in Boston at 42.3736, -71.119, near Harvard Yard. It stands at 136 Irving Street, **Cambridge**, Massachusetts, geocoding to 42.3807, -71.1103. City and coordinate both corrected.
- **[A: English Wikipedia, cross-checked against the Wikipedia list of Fellows elected in 1943]** 1943, Foreign Member of the Royal Society. One of only two foreign members that year, with Victor Moritz Goldschmidt.
- **[R: English Wikipedia]** 1944, International Member of the American Philosophical Society. Tagged R: one source only.

**[R]** For all four, presence at the electing body is unattested and, in the Royal Society case in wartime, improbable. Each stop's date_confidence says so: the election is attested, the travel is not.

## 5. The dismissal, the private institute, the textbook, 1943-1945

**[A: English Wikipedia and Spanish Wikipedia]** In 1943 the military government dismisses Houssay from his University of Buenos Aires posts for his liberal, pro-democracy political views. Spanish Wikipedia specifies that the dismissal followed his signing of a pro-democracy petition; English Wikipedia gives only the political stance. The two are compatible but not identical, and no source read reproduces the petition or names its other signatories. **Gap.**

**CORRECTION, and a date split.** The gathering pass folded the founding of IBYME into the 1943 dismissal entry. It does not belong there. **[A: ibyme.org.ar and Spanish Wikipedia]** The Instituto de Biología y Medicina Experimental was founded on **14 March 1944**, with Eduardo Braun Menéndez, Oscar Orías, Juan T. Lewis, and Virgilio G. Foglia, designed from the start to be independent of government control. The two events are separate stops in the journey file.

**Coordinate contradiction inside this same research pass, flagged.** Three values for one building, IBYME at Vuelta de Obligado 2490, Belgrano:

| Source | lat | lng |
|---|---|---|
| geography lens, as gathered | -34.5556 | -58.4472 |
| afterlife lens, as gathered | -34.5548 | -58.4457 |
| OSM Nominatim, "IBYME, 2490, Vuelta de Obligado, Belgrano" | -34.5579 | -58.4588 |

The Nominatim value is used, as the only one traceable to a geocoder. The disagreement between the two lenses is recorded because it is evidence about the gathering method, not only about the building.

**[A: Spanish Wikipedia]** Rockefeller Foundation support helps sustain the institute. **[R]** The grants, amounts, and years are not enumerated in anything read.

**CORRECTION on the textbook.** The gathering pass dated the treatise 1950. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]** "En 1945 publicó el tratado Fisiología humana, que sería traducido a las principales lenguas." The first edition is 1945; the 1950 figure belongs to the successive Houssay / Braun-Menéndez / Covián editions that ran from that decade on. **[R]** The source asserts wide translation in general terms and enumerates neither languages nor foreign editions. **Gap.**

## 6. Stockholm, 1947

**[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia]** The 1947 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine goes to Houssay, jointly with Carl and Gerty Cori, for his discovery of the role of the anterior pituitary hormones in the regulation of glucose metabolism. He is the first Latin American laureate in any science category.

**[R]** The ceremony and banquet venues in the journey file are the Foundation's standard venues for the era: the Concert Hall on 10 December, the Golden Hall of the City Hall for the banquet. His attendance as laureate is attested; no banquet-specific or itinerary detail for him individually was found. The Konserthuset stop inherits the atlas's Stockholm pin, 59.333 / 18.064, byte-identical with Dirac 1933, Fermi 1938, Bose 1956, Feynman 1965.

**[A: Spanish Wikipedia]** He returns to Buenos Aires still barred from his chair. The prize alters his international standing and does not restore his post; he keeps directing IBYME.

## 7. The private decade and the Pan-American network, 1945-1955

**[A: English Wikipedia]** He is awarded the James Cook Medal in 1948. **Gap:** the source names medal and year, not the place of award or whether he collected it in person.

**[A: Spanish Wikipedia]** In 1950 he begins publishing Acta Physiologica Latinoamericana from Buenos Aires.

**[R]** Regional histories describe Houssay lecturing and receiving honours at the Universidad de Chile, and describe him as a frequent visitor to Montevideo cultivating the Uruguayan school. Neither was pinned to a primary source in this pass; both are carried as tradition, dated only "c. 1940s-1950s". They are in the journey file because the Pan-American network is central to his career, and tagged R because the specific visits are undocumented here.

**CORRECTION on the Harvard claim.** The gathering pass stated that no source names Harvard among his honours. One does. **[A: English Wikipedia]** Houssay "won many distinctions and awards from the Universities of Harvard, Cambridge, Oxford, Paris, and 15 other universities." The honour is attested, undated, and unaccompanied by any evidence of a visit to Cambridge, Massachusetts. **[A]** as to the honour, **[R]** as to any travel.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** He mentors a generation of Argentine physiologists, Eduardo Braun-Menéndez and Miguel Rolando Covián among them. A general characterisation, undated in the source.

## 8. Reinstatement, CONICET, and the last years, 1955-1971

**[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia]** After the fall of Perón in 1955 he is reinstated to the chair of physiology and resumes direction of the Instituto de Fisiología, holding the post until his death.

**CONTRADICTION, flagged and not resolved.** His CONICET presidency:

- English Wikipedia dates his directorship from **1957**.
- Spanish Wikipedia dates his presidency **1958-1971**.

No third source was reachable to adjudicate. The journey file uses 1958 and states the dissent in date_confidence. Both editions agree he was the first president and held the post until his death.

**[R: English Wikipedia]** He receives the Dale Medal of the Society for Endocrinology in 1960. Whether it was presented in person in London is not stated.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** Over his career he publishes more than six hundred papers and several specialised books.

**[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia, independently confirmed by Wikidata Q237160]** He dies in Buenos Aires on 21 September 1971, aged eighty-four, and is buried in the Cementerio de la Chacarita. Wikidata's independent record of the burial place disposes of the gathering pass's speculative hedge about La Recoleta, which is dropped. **Coordinate correction applied:** Spanish Wikipedia gives -34.5908, -58.4597 and Nominatim -34.5911, -58.4583; the geography and afterlife lenses of this pass gave -34.5975, -58.4553 and -34.5975, -58.4539, both about 800 m off and disagreeing with each other. The Spanish Wikipedia value is used. **Gap:** no source gives the section or panteón, so the plot itself is unlocated.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A: English Wikipedia]** April 10, his birthday, is observed nationally in Argentina as the Día del Investigador Científico.

**CONTRADICTION on the prize that bears his name, flagged.** Two distinct awards share the name Premio Bernardo Houssay and the gathering pass conflated them:

- **[R: English Wikipedia]** An Organization of American States prize created in **1972**, for biomedical researchers across the Americas. One source, unconfirmed here. Downgraded to R.
- **[A]** An Argentine national award created by Law 25467, article 25, in **2001**, first awarded in **2003**, presented by the President of Argentina.

They are not the same prize and are not treated as one.

**[A: Spanish Wikipedia, "Plaza Houssay"]** Plaza Houssay, between Recoleta and Balvanera, opened on 9 July 1980 on the former grounds of the Hospital de Clínicas of 1877, whose chapel, the Capilla de San Lucas, was kept as the plaza's centrepiece. A bronze bust of Houssay stood there and was stolen during the 1990s. **Gap:** the source gives only the decade and does not say whether the bust was replaced.

**[A: Spanish Wikipedia, "Instituto de Biología y Medicina Experimental"]** IBYME survives him under dual affiliation with the University of Buenos Aires and CONICET, with roughly 384 staff across endocrinology, neuroscience, biotechnology, oncology, and immunology, its director as reported being Victoria Lux. **Caveat:** headcount and directorship sit on a page with no clear as-of date. Provisional.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** A lunar crater named Houssay carries a 2009 designation; asteroid (2550) Houssay also bears his name.

**[A: English Wikipedia; coordinate corrected]** Hospitals bearing his name operate in Florida, partido de Vicente López, and in Mar del Plata. Nominatim gives Hospital Municipal Prof. Dr. Bernardo Houssay, 1757 Presidente Hipólito Yrigoyen, Florida, at -34.5223, -58.4897, about 1 km from the claimed value, and supplies the street address the gathering pass called a gap. The Mar del Plata hospital is attested by city only.

**CORRECTION on the species.** The gathering pass recorded "a species, Yeneen houssayi" as undated and undescribed. **[A]** Yeneen houssayi is a titanosaurian sauropod described in 2026 by Filippi, Bellardini, Carballido, Méndez and Garrido, from the Bajo de la Carpa Formation at the La Invernada site, Neuquén. Its etymology honours "Bernardo Alberto Houssay, a recipient of the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, who was involved in promoting the creation of the National Scientific and Technical Research Council (CONICET)." It returns zero GBIF hits only because the backbone taxonomy has not ingested a 2026 taxon; a search on "houssayi" returns other honorifics instead, including Mesungulatum houssayi (Bonaparte and Soria, 1985).

**[R: English Wikipedia]** A Google Doodle of 8 April 2013 is described as marking Houssay's 126th birthday. **Contradiction flagged:** 8 April does not match the 10 April birthdate every other source gives, and nothing read explains the two-day gap. Not silently corrected.

**[R: English Wikipedia]** In 1996 the Prefectura Naval Argentina acquires a CONICET research vessel and renames it Dr. Bernardo Houssay (MOV 1). Home port not specified.

**[R: English Wikipedia]** His work on the pituitary-adrenal axis is sometimes cited as background to the cortisol treatment given to a sitting United States president for adrenal insufficiency diagnosed in 1947, the same year as the Nobel. The source frames this as legacy attribution, not a traced chain from Houssay's papers to a physician's decision. Tradition, not proof.
