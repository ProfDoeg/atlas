# Roger II of Sicily (1095-1154): research report

*Slug `roger_ii_of_sicily`. Journey file: 8 segments, 42 stops, Julian calendar,
register "national mythology: the canon is true".*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference
(including inferred pins where the record gives no place). Contradictions are flagged and left
standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

---

## 0. The governing decision

Roger's geography is small and his reach is enormous: born in Calabria, raised in Sicily, barely
moving after 1140, yet under his warrant fleets took Corfu, Thebes, Corinth, Tripoli, Mahdia, Sousse
and Sfax. So the largest decision here is negative: **no African or Greek conquest is staged as a
Roger stop.** Every source confirms those expeditions were led in person by George of Antioch, later
Philip of Mahdia, not by the king **[A: 'Kingdom of Africa'; 'George of Antioch']**. They appear as
two Palermo stops for what the king directed from the palace. Same reasoning removed **Galluccio,
22 July 1139**: the pope was captured by Roger's *son* **[A: 'Treaty of Mignano']**, so it is
reported inside the Mignano campa.

**Four coordinate corrections to the pool**, all **[A]** against the linked articles, all applied:
Mileto 38.5833, 16.0817 to **38.617, 16.067** (4 km, 'Mileto'); Nocera battlefield 40.7333, 14.6417
to **40.7563, 14.6416** (2.56 km, 'Battle of Nocera'); Rignano battlefield 41.7667, 15.7 to
**41.683, 15.583** (13.45 km, 'Battle of Rignano' and 'Rignano Garganico'); Mignano 41.4167, 13.9333
to **41.400, 13.983** (4.5 km, 'Mignano Monte Lungo'). The Rignano pool value landed off the Balcone
delle Puglie escarpment where the battle was fought. At Mileto the Norman town and castle were
destroyed by the 1783 earthquake and the comune rebuilt nearby, so *any* pin there is approximate to
the castle site **[R]**.

## 1. Birth and minority, 1095-1112

- Born **Mileto, Calabria, 22 December 1095**, to Count Roger I and Adelaide del Vasto **[A:
  'Roger II of Sicily']**.
- Roger I dies at Mileto **22 June 1101**; half-brother Simon succeeds under Adelaide's regency
  **[A]**. Simon dies **1105**; the nine-year-old becomes count, Adelaide continuing **[A]**.
- **1109**: Alexios I Komnenos grants him the Byzantine dignity *protonobilissimos* **[A]**.
- **1110**: Sigurd Jorsalfare of Norway visits Sicily. The claim that he conferred royal recognition
  is **[R]**, later Norse saga tradition; Roger was a count and stayed one until 1130. Carried as a
  saga and labelled one in `date_confidence`.
- **12 June 1112**: a charter describes him as newly knighted and count of Sicily and Calabria,
  beginning personal rule **[A]**. **Gap:** the charter text was not independently retrieved.

**Gap.** Nothing survives of the infancy or schooling. The picture of a boy raised among Greek and
Arabic secretaries is inference from the attested character of the chancery **[R]**; the campa
states it about the chancery, not about his tutors.

## 2. Count into duke, 1117-1129

- Marriage to **Elvira** of Leon-Castile. **Contradiction flagged:** the pool gave a flat 1117; the
  Elvira article gives "1117 or 1118" **[A: 'Elvira of Castile, Queen of Sicily']**. The stop is
  dated 1117-01-01 and `date_confidence` says the year is not fixed. Her mother Isabella is herself
  disputed, possibly Zaida of Seville **[R]**.
- **Place gap.** No source documents where any of the three weddings took place. All three Palermo
  pins are inferences from the court's residence and each declares it.
- **1122**: Duke William II of Apulia cedes rights in Sicily and part of Calabria for cash and ~600
  knights **[A]**. Place given only as "Apulia/Sicily"; pin inferred to Palermo **[R for the pin]**.
- **July 1127**: William dies childless; Roger claims the duchy; Honorius II excommunicates him and
  organises baronial resistance **[A]**. **Pin inferred**: sources give only "Apulia", so Salerno,
  the ducal capital, is used and flagged.
- **August 1128**: Honorius invests him Duke of Apulia at **Benevento** **[A]**.
- **September 1129**: mainland cities and barons recognise his ducal authority **[A]**. Sources give
  only "southern Italy", so this is folded into the Benevento campa rather than pinned. Melfi is
  conventional in the wider literature, is not in the verified pool, and is not asserted.

## 3. The crown, 1130-1132

- **27 September 1130**: antipope **Anacletus II**, needing Roger's arms in the schism, issues the
  bull creating him King of Sicily, Calabria and Apulia **[A]**.
- **25 December 1130**: crowned in **Palermo Cathedral** **[A]**. **Limitation:** Britannica was
  unreachable (HTTP 403) to cross-check the cathedral specifically.
- **1131**: Amalfi reduced by George of Antioch's blockade and land forces **[A]**. Naval base on
  **Capri** **[R]**, thinly attested, carried as tradition. **Cefalù Cathedral** founded as intended
  dynastic mausoleum **[A]**; the storm-and-vow origin is transmitted *by the source itself* as
  tradition **[R]**; porphyry sarcophagi placed c. 1145 **[A]**.
- **1132**: **Cappella Palatina** commissioned on an older chapel; mosaics unfinished by 1143 **[A]**.
- **24 July 1132**: defeat at **Nocera** **[A]**.
  - **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The Roger II article gives 25 July; the Battle of
    Nocera and Ranulf II articles both give 24 July. The file uses 24 July and records the split.

## 4. The reversal years, 1133-1137

- **AH 528 / 1133-34**: the red silk **tiraz mantle**, its Kufic band dating the piece and praising
  the treasury rather than the king by name **[A]**. It cannot be the actual 1130 coronation robe
  despite later imperial coronation use **[A/R]**; now in the Schatzkammer, Vienna **[A]**. The
  file's first quote.
- **6 February 1135**: Elvira dies in a court epidemic; Roger falls gravely ill and secludes himself
  so completely that rebels circulate reports of his death **[A]**.
- **Naples, 1135-1136: correction to the pool.** The pool had a single failed siege in July 1135.
  The Sergius VII article gives a Pisan fleet arriving April 1135 with 8,000 men, full revolt, and a
  **naval blockade through spring 1136**, the inhabitants starving, lifted only as Lothair's relief
  army approached **[A]**. **No evidence Roger conducted it in person.** Recast as a blockade dated
  April 1135.
- **Late 1135**: return to **Messina** as the coalition gathers **[A]**. **June 1137**: Lothair III,
  with Innocent II, Robert of Capua and Ranulf, takes **Bari** **[A]**, then withdraws over the
  Alps. **August 1137**: an investiture recorded at **Mercato San Severino** **[R]**, detail thin,
  no independent confirmation.
- **1137**: landing at **Tropea** **[R]**; devastation of **Capua** and **Alife** **[A]**, and of
  **Avellino** and **Pozzuoli** **[R]**.
  - **Editorial choice, declared.** Avellino and Pozzuoli were dropped from the file: both [R], and
    four consecutive devastation pins flattened the segment. Recorded for restoration if wanted
    (Avellino 40.9147, 14.793; Pozzuoli 40.8236, 14.1197). Sources give only "1137" for Capua and
    Alife, so the file's November and December dates are ordering choices and say so.
- **30 October 1137**: **Rignano**, his worst defeat; Duke Sergius VII of Naples killed at his side;
  flight to Salerno **[A]**.

## 5. The pope in the net, 1138-1140

- **January 1138**: Anacletus II dies, ending Roger's papal cover **[A]**.
- **April 1139**: Innocent II excommunicates him **[A]**; in the same month **Ranulf II dies of fever
  at Troia** **[A]**. The exhumation-and-reburial story is **[R]**: a hostile *chronicle* tradition
  with no royal record behind it, flagged as such by the sources themselves.
- **25 July 1139, Treaty of Mignano**: the captive pope recognises Roger as King of Sicily, Duke of
  Apulia and Prince of Capua for 600 schifati a year; signed in Mignano castle **[A]**.
- **1139**: Bari surrenders; Jaquintus executed, reportedly by hanging; the city spared a general
  sack **[A]**.
- **1140, Ariano**: reform of the coinage, silver ducat introduced **[A]**.
  - **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The **Assizes of Ariano** are *traditionally* dated to
    this assembly, but whether the 44-clause collection was promulgated here or compiled across the
    1140s is disputed, and **Falco's contemporary account mentions the coinage rather than a
    complete code** **[A]**. The campa says the law "is dated by tradition to these same days";
    `date_confidence` carries the dispute.
- The pool's **5 November 1139 return to Palermo to plan the legislation** is **[R]** and thin;
  dropped, recorded here.

## 6. The court and the two seas, 1143-1148

- **1143-1151**: George of Antioch founds the **Martorana**; its mosaic shows Roger crowned directly
  by Christ, inscribed **ROGERIOS REX** in Greek letters **[A: 'Martorana']**. The file's second
  quote. The image states sacral kingship bypassing papal coronation; the campa describes the
  picture and lets it interpret itself.
- **c. 1139-1154**: **al-Idrisi**, born in Ceuta, works at the court fifteen to eighteen years
  **[A]**. The scene of king and geographer conferring rests on Idrisi's own preface taken at face
  value **[R for the scene, A for the preface]**.
- **1146-1148, Ifriqiya**: Tripoli, then Mahdia, Sousse, Sfax; Arabic gold dinars struck in Roger's
  name **[A]**. Directed from Palermo; see §0.
- **1147, Byzantine Greece: contradiction flagged, not resolved.**
  - The **Roger II** article lists **Athens**. The **George of Antioch** article does not: it has the
    fleet taking Corfu, sailing into the Saronic Gulf "between Athens and the Peloponnese", raiding
    Monemvasia, then sacking **Thebes** and **Corinth**. Two articles contradict each other. The
    file names Thebes and Corinth only; `date_confidence` records Athens as disputed.
  - **Corfu was occupied and garrisoned, not sacked** **[A]**; recovered by a Byzantine-Venetian
    coalition in 1149 **[A]**, not a Roger stop since he never went there.
  - **Silk weavers**: in the Roger II article, absent from the George of Antioch article. The file
    attributes the deportation to the Thebes and Corinth sack, where the sources carrying it place
    it **[A/R]**.

## 7. The narrowing house, 1148-1154

- **Duke Roger, the heir, dies May 1148. Two corrections to the pool.** The date is disputed, "2 or
  12 May 1148", and the article says he "died an unknown death in an unknown location", so the
  pool's "Sicily" is **not attested** **[A: 'Roger III, Duke of Apulia']**. The file uses 12 May,
  records the split, and says the Palermo pin is the court's, not a claim about where he died.
  Second, "only surviving legitimate adult son" overstates it: William was about seventeen and was
  crowned co-king at Easter 1151, so "succession crisis" is too strong. The campa says the
  succession narrowed; it does not say it broke.
- **1149** Sibylla of Burgundy, **1151** Beatrice of Rethel **[A to the year, R for the place]**.
- **AH 548 / 1153-54**: al-Idrisi completes the *Book of Roger*, seventy regional maps across seven
  climates **[A]**.
- **The silver planisphere: two contradictions, both left standing.** (i) **Weight**: the al-Idrisi
  article cites S. P. Scott for 450 lb, the Tabula Rogeriana article gives 300 lb (140 kg); both are
  named in `date_confidence`, neither picked. The ~2 m diameter is confirmed by both.
  (ii) **Destruction**: the Tabula Rogeriana article states **flatly** that the disc "was destroyed
  in 1160 during a rebellion led by Matthew Bonnellus", but that revolt is conventionally dated
  **March 1161**, so the safer form is **1160/61**, and no object or inventory supports the
  destruction at all. **[R]**: the disappearance is certain, the destruction story is not.
- **26 February 1154**: dies at Palermo aged 58 **[A]**. A few medieval traditions give 27 February;
  26 is accepted. "Falcandus" attributes the death to exhaustion; **no medical cause is
  recoverable** and none is guessed.
- Buried in a **porphyry sarcophagus, Palermo Cathedral**, right aisle, near Constance, Henry VI and
  Frederick II, among the earliest free-standing secular tombs in the medieval West **[A]**. In
  **1215** Frederick II moved the Cefalù sarcophagi to Palermo **[A]**. William I succeeds without a
  succession war **[A]**.

## 8. Interlocks

**Named in a campa: one.** **`bernard_clairvaux`**, in the coronation stop. A *direct antagonist
relation*, not shared geography: Bernard championed Innocent II against **Anacletus II** through the
schism of 1130-1138, and Anacletus is the pope whose bull made Roger a king. Bernard's file covers
Étampes (1130) and Anacletus's death (1138) and never names Roger **[A:
`bernard_clairvaux.journey.json`, lines 289-357]**. The campa states the antagonism and does not
inflate it into a meeting.

**Cited as source, not name-dropped: one.** **`benjamin_de_tudela`**, whose Palermo notices (c. 1170)
record Theban silk workers in the royal workshops, matching the 1147 raid **[A: his file, lines
94-159, 532-549]**. His file credits the later Norman kings, and he never met or wrote about Roger,
so this sits in `suggested_refs` and `sources` on Roger's own stop for the raid, not in the campa.

**Declined**, all shared geography or institution only, which the map already shows a viewer:
`richard_lionheart`, `ramon_llull` (Messina, long after his death); `william_the_conqueror` (died
before Roger was born); `thomas_aquinas`, `al_kamil` (relations to Frederick II, not Roger);
`ibn_arabi` (etymological echo, [R] by the lens itself); `maimonides` (a hometown shared with
al-Idrisi); `leo_africanus`, `piri_reis` (cartographic inference across centuries); `justinian_i`
(a keyword match on "kaaba").

**Canonical pins offered and deliberately NOT inherited:** the Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262) and Paris
(48.8566, 2.3522). **Roger went to neither.** The Mecca justification offered, that al-Idrisi's
sheets covered Arabia, is a fact about a book, stated in the Book of Roger stop.

**Negative finding, confirmed.** A grep across all `*.journey.json` for "Cappella Palatina",
"Martorana" and "Cefalù" returns no hits, so Roger's monuments were unclaimed and this file takes
them outright.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

Roger II is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework may be applied backward on his file and
nothing may reach forward. Nothing was silently deleted. Three items were caught; direction given.

1. **FORWARD, retained, low-risk.** The tomb stop (1154) closes on Frederick II moving the Cefalù
   sarcophagi in **1215**, and the Cefalù stop (1131) says they "stand empty here for eighty years".
   Both are afterlife-of-the-object clauses on Roger's own stops for objects he commissioned, and
   neither points at another file or slug. Precedent: `abdelkader.journey.json`, whose final campa
   carries the 1966 repatriation. If the block rule is read strictly enough to exclude even this,
   these two clauses are what to cut.
2. **FORWARD, excluded, recorded here instead.** The whole afterlife lens postdates the file's clock
   and is kept out: the planisphere's destruction in the Bonellus revolt, 1160/61 [R]; the
   manuscripts (BnF Arabe 2221; Bodleian Pococke 375) [A]; the Rome 1592 Arabic printing and the
   Paris 1619 *Geographia Nubiensis* [A]; Jaubert 1836-1840 [A]; the Factum Foundation's 2017-2019
   reconstruction [R, no display location named]; the UNESCO inscription of 2015 [A, though its text
   does not name Roger]; and Szymanowski's *Król Roger*, Warsaw, 19 June 1926, revived Covent Garden
   2015 [A]. All are other people's stops if they are anyone's.
3. **Not an apparatus relation, flagged for completeness.** A modern popular legend invents a direct
   genealogical line from **Rollo of Normandy** to Roger II and calls the silver map a surviving
   object **[R: no instance of the Rollo claim was independently sourced]**. Inventions *about*
   Roger, not relations *of* his; excluded, recorded because the brief asked about later legends.

## 10. Quotes

**Four placed, all verbatim.** The tiraz mantle inscription (1133) **[A]**, commissioned in Roger's
name by his own workshop and naming no author, so it speaks for the treasury and not for the king;
translation via Wikipedia citing Qantara. "ROGERIOS REX" (Martorana, 1143) **[A]**, the
Greek-lettered Latin inscription on the surviving mosaic. Al-Idrisi on his patron (1144) **[A]**
from the archived Aramco World 28:4 (1977); **limitation**, not traceable to a named translator or
a page in the Nuzhat al-mushtaq, so the edition is uncertain though the quotation is real. Ibn
al-Athir on the Frankish dominion (1148) **[A]** via 'Kingdom of Africa' citing Abulafia p. 35;
**limitation**, the volume itself was not seen.

**Verified but not placed**, so they are not lost. Only one quote fits the single Ifriqiya stop.

- Ibn al-Athir on the **wāli of Gabès** and the **Banū Matrūh** (p. 34), and on the **Rūm**
  frequenting Tripoli "[for the sake of commerce]" (pp. 36-37). Both real, and the **diacritics
  matter**: quote fields are verbatim and exempt from house style, so if either is ever placed it
  must carry the macrons and the grave accent the pool stripped, and the second must keep the
  "[Tripoli]" gloss the pool dropped.
- Ibn al-Athir on Sfax, "were treated humanely" (p. 35): too short for a stop.
- Ibn Abi Dinar, "the accursed one [the king of Sicily] imposed the toughest conditions" (p. 33
  n. 35): in the Africa stop's `suggested_refs`, echoed by the campa's "the accursed one".
- Romuald of Salerno, "cum triumpho et gloria", on **Philip of Mahdia's** welcome at Palermo: about
  the admiral, not the king; in that stop's `sources` rather than as a quote.

## 11. Honest gaps

No wedding place for any of the three marriages, so all three pins are inferred and declared. Duke
Roger's place of death unknown, and the file says so. No recoverable medical cause for Roger's own
death. The Factum Foundation page named no display location. Mercato San Severino rests on one thin
summary line, [R]. Whether Roger conducted the Naples blockade in person is unevidenced, and the
file does not claim he did. Unreachable sources are listed below.
