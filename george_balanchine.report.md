# George Balanchine (1904-1983), research report

*Compiled 2026-08-05 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject slug: `george_balanchine`. Georgiy Melitonovich Balanchivadze, born Saint Petersburg, died Manhattan; dancer, then ballet master of the Ballets Russes, then founder of the School of American Ballet and the New York City Ballet.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the reachable sources contradict each other about. Where the research pool asserted something the verification pass overturned, the overturned version is printed too, because the error will otherwise be re-imported the next time somebody reads the same Wikipedia article.

The dominant source is the English Wikipedia article "George Balanchine" and its satellites. Bernard Taper's *Balanchine: A Biography* (1984) stands behind most of the anecdotal material and was **not** read in this pass; it reaches this report at second hand throughout.

---

## 1. Saint Petersburg, 1904-1921: the school he did not want

- Born **22 January 1904** (9 January Old Style) in Saint Petersburg to the Georgian composer **Meliton Balanchivadze** and **Maria Nikolayevna Vasilyeva**. **[A: Wikipedia, "George Balanchine"]** The street is not recorded in any reachable biography. **Gap, stated as a gap.**
- Summers before the Revolution at a rented dacha on the **Karelian Isthmus**. **[R: Taper's anecdotal chapters; Solomon Volkov, *Balanchine's Tchaikovsky*]** The journey file pins Lounatjoki (see the coordinate table). The pool also said Finnish independence (December 1917) made the isthmus foreign; the border was fixed by the **Treaty of Tartu, 1920**. Both corrected.
- Accepted into the **Imperial Theatre School on Rossi Street** at age nine, **1913**, at his mother's insistence, reportedly crying through the entrance audition. Teachers **Pavel Gerdt** and **Samuil Andrianov**. **[A: Wikipedia; Taper]**
- First choreography, the pas de deux **La Nuit** to Anton Rubinstein, **1920** **[A: Wikipedia]**; a version of Cocteau and Milhaud's **Le Boeuf sur le toit** the same year **[R: Wikipedia, thinly carried]**.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The pool placed the earliest pieces at the **Mikhailovsky Theatre, 1920-1921**, tagged **[A]**, cited to the Wikipedia Balanchine article. That article's raw wikitext contains **no mention of the Mikhailovsky at all**; the connection attaches in the literature to the **Evenings of the Young Ballet from 1923**. The journey file carries one Mikhailovsky stop dated 1923, venue tagged **[R]**, and places La Nuit at the school.
- Graduates the (renamed) school **1921**, joins the corps of the former Mariinsky, enrolls at the **Petrograd Conservatory** (piano, theory, counterpoint, harmony, composition), graduates 1923. **[A: Wikipedia; Taper]**

## 2. The marriage to Tamara Geva: three sources, three years

This is the sharpest unresolved conflict in the file. Geva was born **17 March 1906**. The Wikipedia Balanchine infobox says **1921**, making her 15; the Wikipedia Tamara Geva article says **1924**, making her 18; Taper says **1922**, making her 16.

The pool tagged 1921 **[A]** and glossed it "at age seventeen," which is true of **Balanchine** in 1921 and pins a year the sources do not agree on; a second lens said "sixteen-year-old," which fits only 1922. Divorce **1926** is the one figure all three carry. The journey file dates the stop 1922-01-01 with the three-way disagreement stated in `date_confidence`, tagged **[R]**. **Not adjudicated.**

## 3. Out of Russia, 1924

Route, in order, as reconstructed: **Stettin → Berlin → Wiesbaden → Bad Ems → London → Paris**.

- The four are **Balanchine, Tamara Geva, Alexandra Danilova, Nicholas Efimov**, billed as the Soviet State Dancers and later the Young Ballet, touring under a state contract for hard currency. **[A: Wikipedia]** The **Stettin** crossing is **[R: Taper's route reconstruction]**, not independently documented.
- Berlin: poor reception, money short. **[A: Wikipedia, following Taper]**
- Wiesbaden and Bad Ems: the spa circuit, Geva's account of dark rooms, small houses, skipped meals, and on one night an audience of mental patients. **[A: Wikipedia, Geva memoir as summarised]** **Correction:** the pool placed Wiesbaden in the **Rhine Province**; it was capital of **Hesse-Nassau**. Bad Ems was in the Rhine Province, so the phrase was moved there.
- London: a two-week engagement met with "dead silence" (Geva). **[A]**
- Paris, the émigré quarter around the **Cathedral of Saint Alexander Nevsky, 12 rue Daru**. **[A for the quarter]**

## 4. Diaghilev, 1924-1929

- **November 1924**: Diaghilev auditions the arrivals and hires Balanchine, renaming him within weeks. **[A on the hiring and the renaming; R on the month, which the pool itself tagged R]**
- Chief choreographer from **1925**, after Diaghilev tires of **Bronislava Nijinska**. Collaborators: **Prokofiev, Stravinsky, Satie, Ravel**; designers **Picasso, Rouault, Matisse**. Roughly ten major ballets. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **The football injury.** A shattered kneecap in an informal match during a London season, ending any performing career. **[R]** The pitch is unrecorded, the year varies between accounts (c. 1927 used here), and the sources are Taper and general biography rather than any document.
- **Apollon musagete**, Stravinsky, **Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt, Paris, 12 June 1928**. **[A]**
- **The Prodigal Son**, Prokofiev, **1929**. **[A: Wikipedia; no day fixed by the pool]**
- **Diaghilev dies August 1929**; the company goes bankrupt. Balanchine takes work for **Charles B. Cochran** and **Oswald Stoll**, staging revues at the **London Coliseum**. **[A: Wikipedia]**

## 5. The itinerant years, 1930-1933

- **1930**: guest ballet master, **Royal Danish Ballet**, Copenhagen. **[A]**
- **Contradiction flagged; the pool contradicted itself.** One lens said hired **1931** "under Denham, Blum and de Basil"; the other said **1932-1933** under **Blum and de Basil**. The second is right: the company was founded **in 1932 by René Blum and Colonel Wassily de Basil**, and **Serge Denham was not a founder**, heading the rival **Ballet Russe de Monte Carlo from 1938**. The first lens reproduces a Wikipedia error. Works made: **Cotillon**, **La Concurrence**, for the "baby ballerinas" **Toumanova, Baronova, Riabouchinska**, alongside **Leonide Massine**; out within a year. **[A for the works and dancers]**
- **Les Ballets 1933**, with **Boris Kochno**, financed by **Edward James**; **Théâtre des Champs-Élysées, June 1933**; **Mozartiana** and **The Seven Deadly Sins** with **Kurt Weill**, **Bertolt Brecht** and **Lotte Lenya**. Folds within months. **[A]**
- **Savoy Theatre, London, July 1933**: **Lincoln Kirstein** in the audience, invitation to New York. **[A: Wikipedia; Kirstein biography and NYCB historiography]**

## 6. America, 1933-1946

- **SS Olympic**, arrival New York **18 October 1933**, met by Kirstein and **Edward M. M. Warburg**. **[A]**
- **School of American Ballet opens 2 January 1934 at 637 Madison Avenue with 32 students.** **[A: Wikipedia, "School of American Ballet"; date, address and count all exact]**
- **Serenade**, Tchaikovsky, first danced by students at **Woodlands**, the Warburg estate, **10 June 1934**. **[A]** The rehearsal accidents folded into the choreography are repertory tradition. **[R]**
- **6 December 1934, Avery Memorial, Wadsworth Atheneum, Hartford.** **[A for date and venue]** **Correction:** the pool called this the American Ballet's first public performance. Hartford was billed as the **Producing Company of the School of American Ballet**; the **American Ballet**'s debut was **1 March 1935, Adelphi Theatre, New York** (Wikipedia, "Serenade (ballet)").
- **1935-1938**: American Ballet resident at the **Metropolitan Opera House**; a badly received 1936 season of new commissions; residency not renewed. **[A]** **On Your Toes** (Rodgers and Hart), **1936**, the first Broadway show credited "Choreography by George Balanchine" rather than "Dances staged by", with **La Princesse Zenobia** and **Slaughter on Tenth Avenue**. **[A]** **Gap: no venue or day in the pool**, so it sits inside the Metropolitan Opera campa rather than taking a pin.
- **1938**: Hollywood, a rented house on **North Fairfax Avenue** shared with the rehearsal pianist **Nicholas Kopeikine**; **The Goldwyn Follies**; meets and marries **Vera Zorina** (divorced 1946). **[A]**
- **1941**: **American Ballet Caravan** tours South America under a State Department Good Neighbor program, then folds. **[A for the tour and the program; R for the venue-level stops]**
- **1944-1946**: resident choreographer, **Ballet Russe de Monte Carlo**. **[R, hard correction]** The pool said "under Blum and Massine." **René Blum was deported 23 September 1942 and murdered at Auschwitz that month**; he cannot have run a company in 1944. The engagement was under **Serge Denham**, in New York. Wikipedia's "Blum & Massine's new iteration" is an anachronism. Cut from the journey file rather than carried with a bad attribution.
- **1946**: **Ballet Society** formed with Kirstein; **The Four Temperaments**, score commissioned from **Paul Hindemith**, premieres **20 November 1946**. **[A]** Marries **Maria Tallchief** (annulled 1952). **[A]**
- **1948**: **Orpheus**, with **Stravinsky** and **Isamu Noguchi**. **[A]**

## 7. New York City Ballet, 1948-1967

- **1948**: Ballet Society becomes resident at the **New York City Center of Music and Drama**, West 55th Street, and is renamed **New York City Ballet**. **[A]**
- **1950**: London debut at the **Royal Opera House, Covent Garden**. **[A: NYCB touring historiography]** Critical hostility to the abstraction, and to **Maria Tallchief** read as cold.
- **Early 1950s**: **Edinburgh International Festival** appearances. **[R]** Exact year and venue not reverified; folded into the Covent Garden campa rather than given a pin of its own.
- **1952**: marries **Tanaquil LeClercq** (divorced 1969). **[A]**
- **1954**: his **Nutcracker** for NYCB, dancing **Drosselmeyer** himself; becomes an annual fixture. **[A; no day fixed by the pool]**
- **1962, October-November**: Cold War exchange tour to the USSR. **Moscow (Bolshoi)** **[R]**, **Leningrad (Kirov, i.e. the Mariinsky)** **[A]**, **Tbilisi** **[R]**. A **Kiev** stop appears in fragmentary secondary accounts and is not confirmed anywhere reachable; it is recorded as a gap in the Tbilisi campa rather than given a pin.
- **1964**: NYCB moves to the **New York State Theater**, Lincoln Center, designed by **Philip Johnson** in consultation with Balanchine on stage depth, sightlines and rake. **[A]** Renamed the **David H. Koch Theater**.
- **1967**: **Jewels** (**Emeralds**, **Rubies**, **Diamonds**). **[A; no day fixed by the pool]**

## 8. Decline and death

- **1978**: begins losing balance while dancing and rehearsing; angina pectoris; heart bypass surgery; declining equilibrium, eyesight and hearing. **[A]** The illness is identified as **Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease** only after death. **[A]**
- **1975** Legion of Honour (France); **1978** Kennedy Center Honors; **1980** Austrian Decoration for Science and Art; **1983** Presidential Medal of Freedom, posthumous. **[A]** The Kennedy Center and Austrian awards are folded into the 1978 campa rather than given pins.
- **1982**: largely incapacitated. **[A]**
- **Dies 30 April 1983, aged 79, at Roosevelt Hospital, Manhattan.** **[A]**
- **Buried, Oakland Cemetery, Sag Harbor, New York**, after a Russian Orthodox rite. **[A]** **Alexandra Danilova**, who crossed the Baltic with him in 1924, is buried in the same ground. **[A: Wikipedia, "Oakland Cemetery (Sag Harbor, New York)"]**

## 9. Afterlife

- **George Balanchine Way**, West 63rd Street between Columbus Avenue and Broadway, dedicated **June 1990**. **[A]**
- **The George Balanchine Trust** (1983) and the **George Balanchine Foundation** license and steward the ballets; the Foundation's **Video Archives** record coaching sessions with dancers who worked under him. **[R: balanchine.org, the institution's own site]**
- **Balanchine (crater)**, Mercury, 38 km across, Raditladi quadrangle, its blue rays said to recall the tutu palette of Serenade. **[A]** No pin, since a Mercury coordinate would break a terrestrial globe; carried in the closing campa as prose. A **Balanchine archive** is at Harvard's **Houghton Library**. **[A: Wikipedia external links]**
- **A monument at the Tbilisi Opera and Ballet Theatre.** **[A]** The pool glossed it as "honoring the choreographer's birth city," which is **wrong**: he was born in Saint Petersburg and never lived in Georgia. Tbilisi is ancestral, not natal.
- **Nikolai and the Others** (Richard Nelson), Mitzi Newhouse Theater, 2013. **[A]** **Suki Schorer on Balanchine Technique** (1999) and **The Balanchine Essays** (2013, ten DVDs, Merrill Ashley and Suki Schorer, produced by Catherine Tatge, executive producer Barbara Horgan) fill the vacuum left by the technique "dictionary" he never wrote. **[A: Wikipedia, "Balanchine technique"]**
- **465 ballets.** **[R]** Repeated everywhere, including by the Foundation, traced to no primary catalogue. Carried as an explicitly unsourced number inside the Jewels campa.

## 10. Quotations: what survived checking and what did not

Nine quotes are carried into the journey file. Three of them needed correcting first.

- **"Apollo I look back on as the turning point of my life."** **[A]** Wikipedia gives "turning point **in** my life," cited to "Fisher (2006), p. 27", which has **no matching entry in that article's reference list**. The preposition is **of**, verified through Open Library search-inside in McDonagh's *George Balanchine*, Kirstein's *Program Notes*, and Balanchine's own essay in Walter Sorell (ed.), *The Dance Has Many Faces*.
- **"The ballet is a purely female thing; it is a woman, a garden of beautiful flowers, and man is the gardener."** **Re-tagged R to A.** The pool called it aggregator-only. It is **Balanchine, "To Get into the Act," *Life*, 11 June 1965, p. 97**, the citation Douglas Crimp gives in *Before Pictures*, and is also in Schorer, Simpson's *Contemporary Quotations*, Kelly's *Ballerina*, and Hanna's *Dance, Sex and Gender*.
- **Maria Tallchief, "I always thought Balanchine was more of a musician even than a choreographer..."** **Source corrected.** Wikipedia credits Sarah Halzack's obituary, *Washington Post*, 12 April 2013; it comes from a **1996** profile in the same paper, "Maria Tallchief: Lithe, Fantastic," 8 December 1996, a phone interview with a living subject.
- Carried unamended: **"They are my people... I am Georgian myself."** (Kisselgoff, *NYT*, 2 December 1974); Tallchief's **battement tendu** line and **"Maria, I would like you to become my wife."** (Tallchief memoir, via Wikipedia); **Kirstein's 1946 letter** (via Macaulay, *NYT*, 4 May 2007); **Suzanne Farrell** on how he made ballets (BOMB, Fall 2003, via Wikipedia). Carried as tradition: **"But first, a school."** **[R]**
- **Not used.** **"God creates, I do not create. I assemble and I steal everywhere to do it..."** **[R upheld, source note corrected]**: no dated interview located, but the line is in Simpson's *Contemporary Quotations*, *Webster's II New Riverside Desk Quotations*, Winokur's *True Confessions*, and Dufresne's *The Lie That Tells a Truth*, all predating quote sites, so BrainyQuote is the wrong thing to blame. Also unused: **Clive James** on Balanchine ruling NYCB "as a fiefdom, with the 'droit du seigneur' among his privileges" (*Cultural Amnesia*, 2007, p. 46), a judgement rather than a document.

## 11. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Direct, named, document-stated: **one**. `joseph_pilates.journey.json` records Balanchine sending injured NYCB dancers to Pilates' gymnasium at **939 Eighth Avenue** from **1941**, including **Romana Kryzanowska**. That file notes its own correction from an impossible 1931 date, since Balanchine did not reach the United States until October 1933. It is the only existing journey that names him.

Everything else is shared ground rather than personal overlap, named in the campas as such. Four canonical pins are inherited byte-identically: the **Mariinsky** (59.9251, 30.2961) from `tchaikovsky`, the **Théâtre des Champs-Élysées** (48.8656, 2.3033) from `marcel_proust`, the **Teatro Colón** (-34.601, -58.3831) from `victoria_ocampo`, and **939 Eighth Avenue** (40.765, -73.982) from `joseph_pilates`.

`tchaikovsky` gives the Sleeping Beauty of 1890 and the Nutcracker of 1892 in the house he trains in, the score he reworks in 1954, and the Serenade for Strings danced on the Warburg lawn in 1934. `marcel_proust` and `gurdjieff` give the 1913 Rite of Spring and Gurdjieff's December 1923 demonstrations in the hall where Les Ballets 1933 opens. `victoria_ocampo` puts Ocampo on the Colón stage speaking Perséphone in May 1936, five years before the Caravan plays it. `isadora_duncan` puts Duncan before Diaghilev, Fokine and Pavlova in January 1905, eight years before he enters the school; `suzanne_perrottet` puts Diaghilev at Hellerau in June 1912; `mary_wigman` stages Stravinsky's Sacre in Berlin in 1957, shared composer and city, no contact.

**QUEUE.md** lists **Nijinsky**, **Diaghilev**, **Stravinsky**, **Debussy** and **Picasso** as approved but unresearched. His employer, his predecessor as the company's choreographer, his lifelong collaborator and two of his designers are all outside the corpus at the time of writing. When those files exist, the Ballets Russes segment will need re-reading against them.

## 12. Honest gaps

1. **The birth address.** Not in any reachable biography.
2. **The dacha.** Traditional, not documented; the pool's own pin was 40 km wrong.
3. **The football pitch and the year of the knee injury.** Accounts differ; no document.
4. **The Ballet Society premiere venue** for The Four Temperaments: not City Center, but the pool fixes no hall.
5. **The Kiev leg** of the 1962 tour: fragmentary secondary accounts only.
6. **Weston, Connecticut**: address and years of tenancy uncertain. **The 465-ballet figure**: no primary catalogue reached. **The Sag Harbor cemetery choice**: anecdotal in every account.
7. **Bernard Taper's biography was not read.** The single most important source for this life, and everything credited to it here is at second hand.
