# Troiano Troiani (1885-1963): research report

*Friulian-born sculptor, portraitist and medallist; naturalized Argentine; author of seven of the twenty-six caryatids of the Buenos Aires Legislature, of the fifty-one artistic lamps of the Plaza de los Dos Congresos, and of El Arquero at La Plata.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · contradictions are flagged, never adjudicated silently.

---

## 1. Friuli and the Italian schools (1885 - c.1909)

- Born **1885 in Buja, province of Udine, Italy** **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Troiano Troiani'; corroborated by Wikidata Q110846062]**.
- **Coordinate correction.** Buja is at **46.2167 / 13.1333** (es.wikipedia, 'Buja'). The afterlife lens proposed 46.1544 / 13.0742, roughly 8 km SSW of the town, out on the plain; the geography lens carried the correct figure. **The two lenses contradicted each other and are reconciled here** to 46.2167 / 13.1333.
- **Wikidata birthplace ambiguity [A as a data fact]:** Q110846062 records the birthplace at both province level (Udine) and town level (Buja). Spanish Wikipedia specifies 'Buja, provincia de Udine'. The town reading is preferred; the ambiguity is recorded, not resolved.
- **Studies sculpture in Venice under Aníbal de Lotto [A: es.wikipedia]**. The article names the city and the master and nothing else: no academy, no address, no years. The Accademia di Belle Arti di Venezia (45.4307 / 12.3265) is a landmark coordinate only, **not confirmed as his school [R]**.
- **Continues in Florence, Rome, and Austria [A: es.wikipedia]**, meeting the European currents of the early twentieth century without adopting the avant-garde movements. The same problem recurs at each stop: the city is attested, the institution is not. Florence is pinned at the Accademia di Belle Arti (43.7757 / 11.2589), Rome at a historic-centre point (41.9057 / 12.4823), both **[R]** as sites.
- **Austria is named only as a country [A for 'Austria']**. Vienna and the Akademie der bildenden Künste (48.2016 / 16.3652) are **[R]**, the natural next stop after Venice, Florence and Rome. No source reached names any Austrian city or teacher.

## 2. The crossing (1910)

- **Arrives in Buenos Aires in 1910 under a scholarship scheme (sistema de becas) run by the Argentine government to bring European-trained artists to the country [A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **Port of departure: unresolved [R]**. No source names the port, the vessel, or the date. Genoa (44.4056 / 8.9463, correctly on the waterfront) is the great northern-Italian embarkation point of the period; Trieste is closer to Friuli. **Hard correction:** the geography lens titled this 'Genoa or Trieste, Italy'. **Trieste was not in Italy in 1910**, being the principal port of Austria-Hungary until its annexation in 1920. Friuli lay on that frontier, which sharpens the alternative, but the label must read Austria-Hungary. The contradiction is left open.
- **Landing point: [R], not [A]**. The Hotel de Inmigrantes at Retiro is at **-34.584889 / -58.372753** (Av. Antártida Argentina 1355), not the -34.5875 / -58.3762 proposed, a miss of about 440 m. The complex was built 1906-1911 and pavilion 6 opened in 1911, so a 1910 arrival used an earlier facility on the same stretch of port. **No source places Troiani at Retiro at all.** The geography lens tagged this [A] while its own note conceded the reconstruction; corrected to [R].
- **The 1910 Centennial [R as a link to Troiani]**. His arrival falls in the year of the Centennial exhibitions in the Palermo parkland (-34.573 / -58.4159), and the scholarship belongs to the same civic programme. Whether he set foot on the grounds is not recorded; the Centennial is context, not an itinerary stop.
- **Naturalized as an Argentine citizen [A: es.wikipedia]**. Date and registering office are in no source reached; an open gap.

## 3. The commissions of the commercial city (1916-1927)

- **1916, Bolsa de Comercio de Buenos Aires [A: es.wikipedia]**, executed with the sculptor **Riganti** (Rodolfo Riganti per the afterlife lens). The building is Alejandro Christophersen's, built 1913-1916 **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Bolsa de Comercio de Buenos Aires']**.
- **Coordinate correction, both lenses wrong and wrong differently.** The Bolsa is at **Av. Leandro N. Alem 356 / 25 de Mayo 367, -34.60389 / -58.370722**. The geography lens gave -34.6082 / -58.3714 (about 490 m south); the afterlife lens gave Sarmiento 299, -34.6076 / -58.3737 (about 450 m southwest). Both discarded.
- **Contradiction flagged:** the Bolsa's own article names no sculptors. The Troiani/Riganti credit rests on the biography page alone.
- **Teatro Gran Splendid, decorative sculptural ensemble [A: artedelaargentina.com, Troiani page]**. This began the session as an open gap, since es.wikipedia's 'El Ateneo Grand Splendid' credits only Nazareno Orlandi's 1919 oil dome and names no sculptor. **The gap closes:** artedelaargentina.com, which is reference :2 of the Spanish Wikipedia article on Troiani, credits him with a sculptural ensemble for the Grand Splendid, listed beside the Bolsa commission. Building facts confirm: Av. Santa Fe 1860, opened May 1919, architects Peró y Torres Armengol, at -34.596111 / -58.394167. **No date attaches to Troiani's work there [gap].**
- **1927, Banco Tornquist [A for year and commission: es.wikipedia]**. The building and address are **[R]**, placed in the Microcentro (-34.6011 / -58.3714) on the strength of the firm's known location, not on any source naming the structure.

## 4. The chair (c.1910s - 1940s)

- **Teaches sculpture at what is now the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes 'Prilidiano Pueyrredón', forming several generations of artists [A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **Anachronism corrected; it recurs in two lenses.** The school carried no Pueyrredón name until **Decree 72154 of 1940**. Lineage: workshops founded 1878 by the Sociedad Estímulo de Bellas Artes, **nationalised in 1905 as the Academia Nacional de Bellas Artes**, renamed 1940 **[A: es.wikipedia, school article]**. Troiani's classes from 1910 fell under the national academy, not under the Sociedad Estímulo, and not under the Pueyrredón name for most of his tenure; the geography lens's hedge is itself off by one institution.
- **Coordinate correction:** Av. Gral. Las Heras 1749 is at **-34.591715 / -58.391314**, not -34.586 / -58.3931, a miss of about 660 m.
- **Contradiction flagged:** the school's own article **does not name Troiani**. The post rests on the biography alone. **No student is named [gap]**; 'several generations of artists' is the whole of the record.
- **Successful participation in the Salón Nacional and in individual exhibitions [A: es.wikipedia]**, in general terms. **No medal, year, or exhibited piece is named [gap].**
- **Venue reconstruction weakened.** The Palais de Glace is at **-34.585722 / -58.389361**, not -34.5885 / -58.3893 (a drift of 310 m), and housed the Salón **only 1932-1954 and again from 1960 [A: es.wikipedia, 'Palais de Glace']**. Troiani exhibited from the 1910s, so for the first two decades the venue was categorically elsewhere. The entry is narrowed to his post-1932 participation.

## 5. Iron, light, and water

- **Ornamental lamps (candelabros) for the Plaza de los Dos Congresos [A: es.wikipedia works table; artedelaargentina.com]**.
- **The detail is far richer than the summary [A: artedelaargentina.com]:** **fifty-one artistic lamps of one to five arms**, in wrought iron, **cast under Troiani's direction in the Champagne region of France**. The foundry town is not named **[gap]**.
- **Date deleted, not corrected.** The works table says 'c. 1920' with no citation; secondary summaries commonly say 1929; **no source reached gives any year**, and the dedicated articles on the Plaza de los Dos Congresos and the Palacio del Congreso mention neither Troiani nor a date. The field is emptied rather than filled with either candidate.
- **Consequence for the interlock lens:** several proposed resonances are keyed to 1929 (Le Corbusier's October 1929 lectures; Querol's 1927 monument 'two years before'; the Alvear presidency 'immediately preceding'). With 1929 gone they collapse. A further internal error: the geography lens's 'his cariátides wrap the porteño legislature two years later' is backwards even on its own dating, the Legislatura work having begun in **1926**.
- **Canonical pin inherited:** the corpus pins the Plaza de los Dos Congresos at **-34.6095 / -58.3924** (Cristina Fernández de Kirchner, Javier Milei, Isabel Perón files). Reused byte-identical rather than recomputed.
- **Fuente de Tritón [A: es.wikipedia works table; weboeba.com MOA inventory no. 298]**. The stated gap is smaller than claimed: **the corner is documented.** Inventory record 298 places the Tritón at **Avenida Julio A. Roca and Perú, Montserrat**, credits Troiano Troiani, and describes a granite fountain crowned by a bronze Triton spout acquired by the former Municipality. Coordinates tightened to **-34.6097 / -58.3746**. **Only the year remains unknown [gap].**
- **Contradiction flagged:** the Legislatura article does not mention the fountain, though it stands at the building's corner.

## 6. The Legislature (1926-1931)

- **Palacio de la Legislatura**, Perú 130 / Av. Pdte. Julio A. Roca 575, at **-34.609444 / -58.374444** (not -34.611 / -58.373, about 220 m off; the afterlife lens's -34.6117 / -58.3728 is further off still). Cornerstone **18 November 1926**, construction **1926-1931**, tower 97 m, cost m$n 4,000,000, twenty-six caryatids, **architect Héctor Ayerza** **[A: es.wikipedia, Legislatura article]**. The architect is absent from the raw claim set and is added here.
- **Attribution corrected; the single most consequential correction in the file.** Troiani did **not** make all twenty-six caryatids. He made **seven**: *Navegación*, *Protección a la Infancia*, *Orden Social*, *Vigilancia y Providencia*, *Poder y Derecho*, *Ciencias Médicas y Químicas*, *Comercio* **[A: es.wikipedia, Legislatura article, naming him directly]**. The other nineteen are by **Juan Carlos Oliva Navarro** (*Mercados*, *Mataderos*), **Luis Perlotti** (*La Música y la Historia*), **Torcuato Tasso** (*Arquitectura*), **Héctor Rocha** (*Atletismo y Pintura*), **Juan Bautista Leone**, **Roberto Juan Capuro**, **Pedro Tenti** and **Santiago Berna**.
- The error's origin is identifiable: the biography's phrasing, *esculturas de Troiano Troiani, incluyendo 26 cariátides*, reads the building's total as his own share. **That phrasing is not followed.**
- **Inaugurated 3 October 1931 [A]**; first ordinary session **1 March 1932 [A]**. **Sculptures photographed on the Perú street facade [A: Wikimedia Commons, Category:Troiano Troiani]**.

## 7. El Arquero (1924, installed 1970)

- **Created 1924, inspired by Bourdelle's Hércules Arquero, executed with a modelling technique associated with Rodin [A: es.wikipedia, 'Plaza Moreno']**.
- **Two corrections to the afterlife lens.** (1) *Carves* is wrong for a bronze: El Arquero was **modelled and cast**. (2) *For Plaza Moreno in La Plata* is wrong: it was made as an **independent work** in 1924 and only **acquired by the La Plata Municipality in 1970**, forty-six years later **[A: artedelaargentina.com; es.wikipedia, 'Plaza Moreno']**. It was not a commission for that plaza.
- **Installed in Plaza Moreno in 1970 [A]**, at **-34.9214 / -57.9542**. Troiani had been dead seven years.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the works table lists El Arquero flatly as 'c. 1924, Plaza Moreno, La Plata', with no sign of the forty-six-year gap the Plaza Moreno article states explicitly. The two articles are not cross-referenced.
- **A copy stands in Recoleta [A: artedelaargentina.com]**; exact placement unspecified **[gap]**, pinned approximately at -34.5844 / -58.3924 **[R]**.
- **The Freemason legend [R, and explicitly refuted]**: a local tradition holds that Freemasons aimed the archer at the cross of the La Plata cathedral. **Wikipedia's Plaza Moreno article labels the account refuted.** Recorded as a tradition the record debunks, not as a fact and not as a suppressed one.

## 8. Bronzes, medals, museums, and the end

- **Small-format bronzes across roughly two decades, c.1920-1940 [R: es.wikipedia works table]**. No individual piece and no collection is named **[gap]**. The biography separately attests numerous small bronzes of childhood scenes. **Titles recovered this session [A: artedelaargentina.com]:** *Figura alada* and *Humus*.
- **Works in the collection of the Museo Nacional de Arte Decorativo [A: Wikidata Q110846062]**. **Coordinate correction:** the museum is at Av. del Libertador 1902, **-34.582 / -58.4015** (the former Palacio Errázuriz, built 1911-1917 by René Sergent), not -34.5804 / -58.3936, about 750 m off. **Works also in situ or in the collections of the Bolsa de Comercio and the Palacio de la Legislatura [A: same]**.
- **A holding at the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes is [R] and unconfirmed**, proposed by the geography lens with no source. Left as an inference, flagged.
- **Medallist [A: es.wikipedia infobox and lead]**; **the Casa de Moneda link is [R], unconfirmed inference**. **Coordinate correction:** the proposed -34.628 / -58.3707 points at neither mint. The modern Casa de Moneda (Av. Antártida Argentina 2085) was inaugurated **27 December 1944**, after most of his career; the period-correct mint stood at **Defensa and México in San Telmo** (opened 1881, 1914 annex), around **-34.618 / -58.372**, and the entry is anchored there. No medal, die or commission ties him to either **[gap]**.
- **Bust of Dante Alighieri in 'los jardines de Palermo' [A for the bust; wrong garden in both lenses]**. The Jardín Botánico Carlos Thays article catalogues over thirty sculptures with named authors and contains **neither a Dante bust nor any Troiani**. The bust stands in the **Jardín de los Poetas at El Rosedal de Palermo, Parque Tres de Febrero**, which holds twenty-six busts of poets and **explicitly includes Dante [A: es.wikipedia, 'Rosedal de Palermo']**. Moved to **-34.5706 / -58.4173**.
- **The date is dropped.** The works table's 'c. 1921' is uncited, and its own rationale is broken: the bust is said to be donated *con motivo del IV centenario del poeta*, but 1921 was the **sixth** centenary of Dante's death (d. 1321); a fourth would fall in 1721. **Flagged; no year asserted.**
- **Dies in Buenos Aires, 24 May 1963 [A: es.wikipedia; year corroborated by Wikidata]**, aged seventy-eight.
- **Burial: an open gap.** Wikidata's place-of-burial field is empty and no cemetery record was locatable. Chacarita and Recoleta are plausible for a naturalized professor and public sculptor; **none is named in any record reached, and none is guessed here.**

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. **Twenty-six caryatids vs seven.** The biography implies the whole cornice; the building's article names seven for Troiani and eight other sculptors for the rest. Resolved in favour of the building article, with the biography's phrasing recorded as the origin of a widely circulated claim.
2. **The Congress lamps' year.** 'c. 1920' (uncited works table) vs 1929 (secondary summaries, unsupported) vs no year at all (artedelaargentina, which supplies every other detail). Left empty.
3. **The Dante bust's year.** 'c. 1921' uncited, and its stated occasion is arithmetically impossible for that year. Left empty.
4. **El Arquero.** Works table: made for Plaza Moreno, c. 1924. Plaza Moreno article and artedelaargentina: independent 1924 work, acquired 1970.
5. **Birthplace granularity.** Wikidata gives both Udine (province) and Buja (town).
6. **Port of departure.** Genoa vs Trieste (Austria-Hungary in 1910). Neither confirmed.
7. **Single-source attributions.** The Bolsa, the Banco Tornquist and the teaching post are each carried by the biography page alone; the Bolsa's and the school's own articles name no sculptor and no Troiani respectively.
8. **Internal lens conflict on coordinates.** The lenses disagreed on Buja, on the Bolsa and on the Dante garden; each is reconciled above with the discarded value named.
