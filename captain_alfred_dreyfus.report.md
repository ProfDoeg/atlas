# Captain Alfred Dreyfus (1859-1935): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for `captain_alfred_dreyfus.journey.json`. Queued (QUEUE.md line 425) for the interlock with Theodor Herzl and with the queued Samuel Preiswerk proto-Zionist current.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a party's own retrospective claim · **[C]** contradiction between sources, flagged and not adjudicated.

**Method.** Written from a verified research pool assembled across five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife). The pool carries its own adversarial correction layer: an English-Wikipedia first pass checked line by line against fr.wikipedia, against Project Gutenberg full texts, and against coordinates. Roughly a third of the first-pass claims came back wrong on date or position. Where the correction layer disagrees with the first pass, both readings are given below and the journey file's choice is stated.

---

## 1. Alsace, and the province that changed countries (1859-1873)

- Born **9 October 1859 in Mulhouse**, youngest of nine children of Raphaël Dreyfus, a prosperous Jewish textile manufacturer; first family home on the **Rue du Sauvage**. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Alfred Dreyfus']**
- A later move to a mansion on the **Rue de la Sinne**. **[R: so tagged in the pool; no year beyond "1860s"]** Not given a stop in the journey file.
- Franco-Prussian War and annexation. First pass: displacement **1870**. **CORRECTED:** Raphaël Dreyfus opted for French nationality in **1872**; the household went first to **Basel**, where Alfred attended secondary school, and to **Paris in 1873**, where he entered the collège Sainte-Barbe. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** The file follows 1872 and 1873.

The load-bearing fact of the life: a family that chose France when the choice cost them a house, producing a son France convicted of selling itself to Germany.

## 2. The making of an artillery officer (1878-1889)

- **École polytechnique, 1878**, received at nineteen, 182nd of 235. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **[C]** The pool's chronology lens carried **1877**, from en.wikipedia's "after turning 18 in October 1877"; its own geography lens carried 1878. Two lenses of one pool contradicting each other. File follows 1878.
- **Commissioned sous-lieutenant on leaving Polytechnique, 1880, 128th of 235.** **[A: fr.wikipedia]** The first pass attached the commission to the end of Fontainebleau instead.
- **École d'application de l'artillerie, Fontainebleau, 1880-1882.** **[A]**
- **Lieutenant 1 October 1882**, posted to the **31st Artillery at Le Mans**; leaves for Paris late 1883. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **[C]** en.wikipedia gives a bare "1885", an implausible nine-year gap after 1880. Not followed. The geography lens's "Le Mans, 1880 onward" likewise corrected.
- **Late 1883: horse batteries of the 1st Cavalry Division, Paris.** **[A: fr.wikipedia]** First pass said 1885.
- **1889: adjutant at the Établissement de Bourges, promoted captain.** **[A]** Uncontested. The ordnance experience is what later made his access to artillery secrets plausible to accusers.

## 3. Marriage, the War College, the General Staff (1890-1894)

- **Married Lucie Eugénie Hadamard 18 April 1890** (Lucie: 23 August 1869 - 14 December 1945). **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **[C]** en.wikipedia gives 18 April **1891**. The French date is internally consistent with the War College admission two days later. File uses 1890 and carries the conflict in `date_confidence`.
- **Admitted to the École supérieure de guerre 20 April 1890.** **[A: fr.wikipedia]** First pass: 1891, and "three days" after the wedding; it was two.
- **Graduated November 1892, 9th of 81, mention "très bien".** **[A: fr.wikipedia]** First pass gave 1893, conflating graduation with the staff posting. One examiner marked him down on the stated ground that Jews were unwanted on the staff; the ranking stood.
- **General Staff traineeship taken up 1 January 1893**, Ministry of War, rue Saint-Dominique; the only Jewish officer there. **[A]**

## 4. The bordereau, the court-martial, the degradation (1894-1895)

- **Arrested 15 October 1894** at the Ministry of War, summoned on a pretext. **[A]**
- **The bordereau**: a torn, unsigned covering note recovered from a wastebasket at the German embassy in **September 1894** by the housekeeper Marie Bastian. **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Dreyfus affair']**
  **[C], the most important correction in the pool.** The first pass said the bordereau "was later shown to be forged and to match the handwriting of another officer, Esterhazy", which is self-contradictory and wrong. **The bordereau was authentic and written by Esterhazy.** The forgery is the **faux Henry**, fabricated by Major Hubert-Joseph Henry in **November 1896**, two years after conviction. The journey file keeps the two documents strictly apart.
- **Cherche-Midi military prison from 15 October 1894.** **[C]** First pass ran the confinement to 17 January 1895; corrected: after the 5 January degradation he moved to the **prison de la Santé**, and it was from there he was transferred on 17 January. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **Secret court-martial 19-22 December 1894.** Unanimous conviction on the bordereau plus a secret dossier passed to the judges and never shown to Dreyfus or his counsel. Degradation and deportation for life. **[A]**
- **Degradation, 5 January 1895, courtyard of the École militaire.** **[A]**

**Two renderings of what he shouted, not the same sentence.**
- "Soldiers, they are degrading an innocent man. Soldiers, they are dishonoring an innocent man. Vive la France, vive l'armee!" **[A: Dreyfus, *Five Years of My Life* (1901 English trans.), ch. V]**
- "I swear that I am innocent. I remain worthy of serving in the Army. Long live France! Long live the Army!" **[A as secondary: en.wikipedia, footnote 3; primary text not seen]**

The file uses the memoir's wording and carries the variant in `quote_source` rather than choosing silently. The chant "Death to Judas, death to the Jew" is secondary only (en.wikipedia, 'Dreyfus affair', footnote 83) and is attributed to a crowd, not a speaker.

**Other memoir quotations used in the file** **[A: *Five Years of My Life*, 1901 English trans.]**: "Take my keys, open everything in my house; I am innocent." (arrest, ch. II) · "I trust in God's justice. In the end truth must prevail. My conscience does not reproach me." (letter to Lucie, 5 December 1894, ch. III) · "My despair knew no bounds; the night which followed my condemnation was one of the bitterest of my embittered existence." (ch. IV). A fifth, "Show me at least the proofs of the infamous act you pretend I have committed" (ch. II), went unplaced.
**Caveat:** fetched from Internet Archive OCR of the 1901 translation via an AI summarizer; wording should be spot-checked against the printed book. Live gap.

## 5. The island (1895-1899)

- **17 January to 21 February 1895: Citadelle de Saint-Martin-de-Ré**; a last visit from Lucie through prison bars. **[A]**
- **21 February 1895: embarked on the *Ville de Saint-Nazaire*.** **[R]** The quay near La Rochelle is not stated in any source consulted; the La Pallice pin is an inference and is flagged in the stop.
- **Île du Diable, 13 April 1895 to 9 June 1899.** Stone hut, armed guard, conversation forbidden, letters copied by the administration. **[A]** The **double boucle** (night ankle shackling) imposed **September 1896** after escape rumours. **[A]**
- **[C] Coordinates and size, both wrong in the first pass.** Geography lens: 5.2828 / -52.5967, about 1.6 km southwest, in open water. Afterlife lens: 5.2833 / -52.5833, about 1.1 km south. **Correct: 5.2936 / -52.5831 (fr.wikipedia).** The first pass also called the island "scarcely half a hectare"; it is **14 hectares**, off by a factor of about 28. The file uses the corrected pin and says nothing about acreage.

## 6. The Affair in France while he is on the rock (1896-1899)

- **August 1896:** Picquart matches the bordereau to Esterhazy's hand, reports upward, is told the matter is closed, transferred, then arrested. **[A]**
- **November 1896:** the faux Henry fabricated. **[A]** · **January 1898:** Esterhazy acquitted within minutes. **[A]**
- **13 January 1898: "J'Accuse...!"** across the front page of *L'Aurore*, edited by Clemenceau, addressed to President Félix Faure; roughly 300,000 copies sold. **[A]**
- **7-23 February 1898:** Zola tried for libel at the Cour d'assises de la Seine, convicted 23 February, leaves for England, returns June 1899. **[A]**

Quoted in the file from *The Trial of Emile Zola* (Project Gutenberg #68561) **[A]**: "I accuse, finally, the first council of war of having violated the law by condemning an accused person on the strength of a secret document, and I accuse the second council of war of having covered this illegality, in obedience to orders, in committing in its turn the judicial crime of knowingly acquitting a guilty man."

## 7. Rennes, the pardon, the years of neither (1899-1906)

- **7 August to 9 September 1899:** second court-martial in the salle des fêtes of the **lycée de Rennes** (today lycée Émile-Zola), chosen because it faced the military prison holding him. Guilty again, "with extenuating circumstances", ten years. **[A]** **[C]** First-pass coordinate 48.114 / -1.675 sits about 600 m north; corrected to **48.1086 / -1.6747**.
- **19 September 1899: pardon signed by President Loubet**, accepted, leaving the verdict formally standing. **[A]** Dreyfus: "The government of the Republic has given me back my freedom. It is nothing for me without my honour." **[A as secondary: en.wikipedia, footnote 2]**
  The pool also carries "I want the whole of France to know that it was an innocent man that I saved," tagged **[R]** and loosely attached to the pardon. On its face it belongs to whoever did the saving, not to Dreyfus. **Not used.**
- **House arrest at Carpentras with a sister, later at Cologny near Geneva, until July 1906.** **[A: en.wikipedia]** First pass windowed Carpentras at 1899-1900, six years short.
- **12 July 1906: the Cour de cassation annuls the Rennes verdict without referral.** Full exoneration. **[A]**

## 8. The second uniform (1906-1918)

- **13 July 1906: reinstated as chef d'escadron.** **[A]**
- **Legion of Honour, chevalier, at the École militaire: 20 July (fr.wikipedia) or 21 July (en.wikipedia) 1906. [C, not adjudicated.]** The first pass said "the day after" the exoneration; it was not. File dates the ceremony 1906-07-20 and carries both readings.
- **1906: command at Vincennes; 15 October 1906: command at Saint-Denis; 1907: retirement at 48.** **[A]**
- **4 June 1908:** shot in the arm by the journalist **Louis Gregori** at the transfer of Zola's ashes to the Panthéon; Gregori acquitted. **[A]**
- **1914: recalled.** Serves at **Verdun** and near the **Chemin des Dames**, both dated 1917 by the geography lens. **[A]** **26 September 1918: lieutenant-colonel [A]** · **November 1918: Officer of the Legion of Honour [R]**.

## 9. Death and afterlife (1935-2026)

- **Died in Paris 12 July 1935, aged 75. [A]** The address of the last residence is in no consulted source. **Gap.** The death pin is the nominal Place de la Concorde coordinate the pool gave for both death and cortège.
- **14 July 1935: cortège across the Place de la Concorde on Bastille Day; buried at Montparnasse.** **[A]** **[C]** The first pass placed the grave in a "Jewish section"; en.wikipedia's 'Montparnasse Cemetery' places him "in the south of the cemetery" and the Jewish enclosures are the 5th and 30th divisions. Framing dropped, coordinates corrected to **48.8381 / 2.3269**.
- **The Tim statue**, by **Louis Mitelberg**: **cast 1985** (Clementi foundry, Meudon) after the army refused it the École militaire courtyard; **inaugurated in the Tuileries 9 June 1988**; **moved 16 October 1994** to the place Pierre-Lafue at rue Notre-Dame-des-Champs and boulevard Raspail; **resin copy 2003** for the MAHJ. **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Hommage au capitaine Dreyfus']** First pass read 1985 as a dedication and left it standing on the Raspail.
- **Relocated to the Cour de cassation, Île de la Cité. [C]** Geography lens: **June 2026**. Afterlife lens: **12 July 2026**. Not adjudicated; the file uses the July date.
- **2021:** museum of the Affair inaugurated at **Médan**, at Zola's house. **[A]**
- **Posthumous promotion to général de brigade. [C]** First pass: "18 November 2025", matching no step. fr.wikipedia: Assemblée nationale **2 June 2025**, Senate unanimously **6 November 2025**, law promulgated **17 November 2025** (used).
- **12 July declared an annual day of commemoration** "for the victory of justice and the truth against hatred and antisemitism". **[A]**

## 10. The Herzl question, stated as a question

**Attested.** Herzl was Paris correspondent of the *Neue Freie Presse* **1891 to 1895**, returning to Vienna as literary editor. **[A]** He wrote in 1899: "The Dreyfus case embodies more than a judicial error; it embodies the desire of the vast majority of the French to condemn a Jew, and to condemn all Jews in this one Jew. Death to the Jews! howled the mob, as the decorations were being ripped from the captain's coat...." **[A: verified verbatim, Project Gutenberg #25282, lines 811-816]** *Der Judenstaat* appeared **February 1896**; the **First Zionist Congress** met at the Basel Stadtcasino **29-31 August 1897**. **[A]**

**Four things the correction layer will not let stand.**

1. **He did not cover the affair "from 1894 to 1906".** He had left Paris before Rennes and was dead (3 July 1904) before the exoneration.
2. **His presence at the 5 January 1895 degradation is not confirmed** by en.wikipedia, which records he may have heard "Death to the traitor" rather than "Death to the Jews". The pool tagged the presence **[A]**; that is generous, and the journey file treats it as **[R]**, his own later account, and says so in the stop's `date_confidence`.
3. **The passage the first pass attributed to Alex Bein is Louis Lipsky's.** "He was present at the trial of Alfred Dreyfus in December, 1894..." sits at line 264 of Gutenberg #25282, inside Lipsky's Introduction; the Bein-based biography begins around line 450. The sentence is doubtful anyway: the December 1894 court-martial sat **in camera**.
4. **The chronology of *Das neue Ghetto* undercuts the legend.** Written **October-November 1894**, before the degradation; premiered January 1898. The first pass dated it 1895 and read it as reflecting the affair.

**Counter-current.** Kornberg, as summarized on Wikipedia, argues the retrospective account should be treated with caution: some evidence suggests Herzl initially believed Dreyfus guilty and foregrounded the case only once it became an international cause célèbre, with **Karl Lueger's antisemitic rise in Vienna in 1895** weighing at least as heavily. **[R]** *Der Judenstaat*'s own text draws more on Vienna than on France. The journey file states the connection as Herzl's claim and leaves the Vienna counterweight standing beside it. **Gap:** Kornberg was not read directly, and the pool could not source the "Death to the traitor" chant within budget.

## 11. Interlocks inside the corpus

## 13. Gaps stated as gaps

- The five *Five Years of My Life* quotations are OCR-derived and unverified against the printed book.
- The address where he died is in no consulted source; the death pin is nominal.
- The quay of embarkation for Guiana is unspecified; the La Pallice pin is inferred.
- The **Sainte-Barbe and La Santé pins are this compiler's own geolocation** of the rue Valette and rue de la Santé buildings, not coordinates from the pool. Flagged in both stops.
- No consulted source records Dreyfus's own later views of the Affair, of Herzl, or of Zionism. The file puts no position in his mouth.
- **A short list of contextual facts in the campas comes from general knowledge of the Affair, not from the pool**, and should be checked before anyone leans on them: Henry's suicide at Mont-Valérien; the shooting of Maître Labori during the Rennes retrial; the Rennes verdict as five judges to two; Picquart at the Ministry of War by 1907; the Île du Diable as a former leper station; the crossing taking about a fortnight; Picquart's own stated dislike of Jews.
- **Lucie Hadamard has no independent research pass.** She carried the case in Paris for four and a half years while her husband was on a rock in the Atlantic, and the pool gives her two dates and a marriage.
