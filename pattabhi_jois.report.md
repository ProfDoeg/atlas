# Krishna Pattabhi Jois (1915-2009): research report
*2026-08-08. Prepared for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: the Mysore Sanskrit professor who fixed the Ashtanga vinyasa sequence and exported it; the manuscript he said it came from; the accusations published after his death.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, lineage tradition, or inference · *gap* = searched and not found, recorded as absence of evidence rather than evidence of absence.

The spine of the attested record is the English Wikipedia articles **K. Pattabhi Jois**, **Ashtanga (vinyasa) yoga**, **Tirumalai Krishnamacharya**, **Sharath Jois**, **Larry Schultz**, and **Yoga Korunta**, which are themselves compilations. Where those articles disagree with each other, the disagreement is printed below and left standing. Reached directly: the 1995 letter to *Yoga Journal* (aylibrary.blogspot.com), the 1994 *Yoga International* interview (ashtangidiary.blogspot.com), and Open Library full-text search for the two most-quoted maxims. **Not** reached: the *New York Times* pieces of 8 November 2019, the 2018 *Yoga Journal* mea culpa, Anneke Lucas's 2016 YogaCityNYC post, and Stern's 2002 English *Yoga Mala* in full. Those are cited below at second hand, and marked as such.

---

## 1. Kowshika, or Bettadapura (1915-1927)

- Born **26 July 1915**, on the full-moon day of Guru Purnima, into a Brahmin household; father an astrologer, priest, and landholder. **[A: Wikipedia, K. Pattabhi Jois]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** Wikipedia gives the birthplace as **Kowshika**, a village near **Hassan**. A second tradition circulating in Ashtanga-lineage accounts gives **Bettadapura**, Periyapatna taluk, **Mysore district**, 82 km from Mysuru city. Neither claim carries a citation trail the other lacks. Both are recorded; the journey file pins both.
  - Coordinate note: no source gives a coordinate for Kowshika itself. The journey uses Hassan town (13.0033, 76.1004) as the district pin and says so in the campa. For Bettadapura the correct figure is **12.4539, 76.1161** (12°27′14″N 76°06′58″E); an earlier draft coordinate of 12.343 / 76.083 was ~14 km off and is discarded.
- Instruction in Sanskrit and Brahminical ritual from age five: **[R]**, not [A]. Wikipedia says only that the father was an astrologer, priest, and landholder. The "from age five" detail is lineage narrative with no cited source.
- A competing origin story has Krishnamacharya demonstrating at the boy's **village school when he was five**, i.e. c. 1920, years before the Hassan meeting. **[R: institute oral tradition]**. The two first-encounter stories cannot both be first. Neither is footnoted to a contemporary record. Left open.
- **1927, Hassan, Jubilee Hall.** At twelve he attends a Krishnamacharya lecture-demonstration and presents himself as a student the next day. **[A: Wikipedia]** He then practices under Krishnamacharya in and around his own village through **1927-1929**. **[A]**

## 2. The road to Mysore (1930-1933)

- **1930.** He leaves home for Mysore with **two rupees**, against his family's wishes, to study Sanskrit. **[A: Wikipedia]**
  - **Corrected from the research pool.** (a) The age is not "thirteen or fourteen": born 26 July 1915, he left in 1930, so he was 14, or 15 after his July birthday; Wikipedia's summary says 15. (b) The departure is **1930**, not 1927; 1927 is the Hassan demonstration, three years earlier, and one draft collapsed the two.
  - The road itself is a *gap*: no itinerary, no waystation, no date within the year. Hassan to Mysore is about **120 km**, not the 150 of one draft. The journey pins a midpoint and says in the campa that no source places him there.
- He enrolls at the **Maharaja's Sanskrit College**, Mysore (12.3068, 76.6553). **[R as to the exact enrollment date; A as to the Sanskrit-study motive: Wikipedia]**
- **1932.** Reunited with Krishnamacharya in Mysore; formal yoga study resumes. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Context, not biography: Krishnamacharya had been engaged at the **Mysore Palace** since **1926** by Maharaja **Krishnaraja Wodeyar IV**. Whether Jois attended any palace session before the Jaganmohan yogashala opened is **not established** in anything reached here. **[R / gap]** The palace pin is inherited byte-identically from `swami_vivekananda.journey.json` (**12.3052, 76.6552**), where it marks Chamarajendra Wodeyar's December 1892 reception of Vivekananda and his offer to pay the monk's passage to America. Chamarajendra was Krishnaraja IV's father: one dynasty funds both journeys, forty years apart. **[A for each stop separately; R for the dynastic framing, census line 495]**
- **June 1933.** Marries **Savitramma** ("Amma"); three children follow, Saraswathi, Manju, Ramesh. **[A: Wikipedia]** Other secondary accounts give other years without citation. Flagged, not resolved.

## 3. The yogashala years (1933-1956)

- **11 August 1933.** The Jaganmohan Palace yogashala opens under Krishnamacharya, granted by the Maharaja. Jois trains here into the early 1950s: Yoga Sutras, Hatha Yoga Pradipika, and the vinyasa sequence method. **[A: Wikipedia, Krishnamacharya and Jois]**
- **1937.** Takes up the yoga teaching post at the **Sanskrit College**, Mysore. Holds it to **1973**. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **3 August 1940.** Krishnaraja Wodeyar IV dies (Bangalore). Patronage for the yogashala begins to fail. **[A: general historical record; Wikipedia, Krishnamacharya]**
- **1948.** Founds the **Ashtanga Yoga Research Institute** at his house in **Lakshmipuram**. **[A: Wikipedia]**
  - Coordinate contradiction inside the research pool itself: geography gives **12.2965, 76.648**, afterlife gives 12.3021, 76.6469 (about 700 m north), OSM's node is 12.2960, 76.6456. The journey adopts **12.2965, 76.648** for every Lakshmipuram stop.
- **1950.** The Jaganmohan yogashala is closed by order of Mysore's first post-independence Chief Minister. **[A: Wikipedia, Krishnamacharya]**
- **1952 or 1953.** Krishnamacharya leaves Mysore for **Madras** (lecturer, Vivekananda College). Wikipedia's Krishnamacharya article gives **1952**; the Jois article's "1932-1953 study period" implies 1953. **Contradiction flagged.**
  - A draft narrative said the two men then had "fifty-six years remaining" each. Wrong: Krishnamacharya died in 1989 (about 36 years), Jois in 2009 (56). Corrected in the journey.
- **1956.** Made **vidwan**, senior professor, at the Sanskrit College. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- ***Yoga Mala*, Kannada original: 1958 or 1962.** Wikipedia's **K. Pattabhi Jois** article says 1958; Wikipedia's **Ashtanga vinyasa yoga** article says 1962. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The journey file carries the hedge in `date_confidence`.

## 4. The first foreigners (1964-1978)

- **1964.** Hall extension built behind the Lakshmipuram house. **[A]** Same year, **André Van Lysebeth**, Belgian pranayama teacher, studies with Jois for **two months**. **[A: Wikipedia; the duration is sourced, better than the [R] one draft assigned]**. Which of his books carries the account is *not* confirmed; that part stays **[R]**.
- **c. 1971-72.** **Norman Allen**, generally described as the first Western student, reaches Mysore after seeing a Manju Jois demonstration. **[A as to precedence; R as to year]**. A draft placing Allen in 1973 alongside Williams is wrong on both counts.
- **1973.** **David Williams** and **Nancy Gilgoff** arrive. **[A]** They, not Allen, sponsor the 1975 California visit. Gilgoff's arrival is better fixed than the "mid-1970s" of one draft.
- **1973.** The Sanskrit College post ends after 36 years. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **1974.** First trip outside India: **South America**, a Sanskrit lecture at an international yoga conference. **[A as to the fact and year: Wikipedia]**. Country, city, conference name, and month are a *gap*: the source names the continent only.
- **1975.** Four months teaching in **Encinitas, California**, with Manju. **[A]** Treated as the origin point of American Ashtanga.
- **1976-1978.** Honorary Professor of Yoga, **Government College of Indian Medicine**, Mysore. **[A]**

## 5. The method exported (1982-2002)

- **1982.** **Larry Schultz** meets Jois at a US workshop organized by **Stan Hafner**; city not named. Studies seven years in India and the USA, returns to San Francisco 1989, founds **It's Yoga** in **1991**, teaching the sequence loosely. **[A: Wikipedia, Larry Schultz]**. A draft claimed a "1985 Mysore photograph"; the Wikipedia caption reads only "K. Pattabhi Jois with Larry Schultz, mid 1980s".
- **1990.** **Sharath Jois**, grandson, begins formal study at nineteen. **[A: Wikipedia, Sharath Jois]**
- **November 1995.** Letter to *Yoga Journal* against "Power Yoga". **[A: reproduced in full on aylibrary.blogspot.com, fetched directly]**. Three phrases from it are quoted in the journey file: "Power is the property of God. It is not something to be collected for one's ego."; the Yoga Sutra II.28 gloss; and "the precious jewel of liberation in the mud of ignorant body building."
- **1999.** *Yoga Mala* in English, trans. **Eddie Stern**, published by **Patanjali Yoga Shala / Ashtanga Yoga New York** (Broome Street), **not** by a trade house. **North Point Press (FSG)** issues its edition in **2002**; Open Library records printings **2010** and **2017**. **[A: Open Library]**. A draft asserting a "1999 North Point edition, revised 2012" is wrong on both counts; no 2012 revision is traceable.
- Translations beyond Kannada and English: a **Spanish** edition (El hilo de Ariadna) and a **Chinese** edition (*Yu jia Mala*, Taiwan, 2015) exist. **[A: Open Library]**. This partly closes a gap the research pool had left open.
- Lino Miele's companion manual, *Astanga Yoga: Including the Benefits of Yoga Chikitsa*, was published in **Rome in 1994**, not 2003. **[A: Open Library]**
- **2002.** New, larger school opens in **Gokulam**, Mysore. **[A]**
  - Coordinate contradiction inside the pool: geography gives 12.3273, 76.6403; afterlife gives **12.3255, 76.6297**; OSM's Gokulam node is 12.3261, 76.6283. The geography figure lands ~1.3 km east of the neighbourhood. The journey adopts **12.3255, 76.6297**.

## 6. Death and afterlife (2007-2024)

- **2007.** Health decline; **Sharath Jois** becomes director. **[A]**
- **18 May 2009.** Dies in Mysore, aged 93. **[A]** Cause of death, funeral, and cremation site: *gap*. No source reached names a tomb, samadhi shrine, statue, museum, stamp, or centenary monument. Searched and not found; recorded as an open gap.
- **2009.** The institute is renamed the **K. Pattabhi Jois Ashtanga Yoga Institute (KPJAYI)**, in the year of his death, not vaguely "the 2000s". **[A: Wikipedia legacy section; the [R] tag one draft gave this can be upgraded]**
- **The Yoga Korunta.** Krishnamacharya said he found the text in the **National Archives of India**, written in **Nepali** (elsewhere "the Gurkha language"), attributed to **Vamana Rishi**, his only copy later **eaten by ants**. **[A as to what Wikipedia reports him claiming]**. The Calcutta-palm-leaf version circulating in the lineage is one variant among several, not the canonical account; a draft presenting it as *the* lineage story overstated it. **[R]**. Mark Singleton treats its existence as unverified; a *Yoga Journal* editor (Fernando Pagés Ruiz) counted **at least five mutually inconsistent** discovery stories; Krishnamacharya cites it in neither *Yoga Makaranda* (1934) nor *Yogasanagalu* (c. 1941). Wikipedia files the article under "Hoaxes in India". **[A: Wikipedia, Yoga Korunta]**
- **Abuse allegations.** Multiple women who studied with Jois allege he sexually assaulted them during hands-on adjustments. Wikipedia states he "systematically sexually abused some of his female and male yoga students, both in Mysore and during his travels, until his death in 2009." **[A: Wikipedia, K. Pattabhi Jois]**
  - **Timeline corrected.** The wave is usually dated 2018-2019, but **Anneke Lucas** published her account on **YogaCityNYC.com in 2016**, describing an assault at a Jois workshop at the **Puck Building, 295 Lafayette Street, Manhattan** (40.7247, -73.9950). *Yoga Journal*'s "Sexual Assault in the Ashtanga Yoga Community: A Mea Culpa" ran in **2018**. The *New York Times* coverage ran on **8 November 2019**, naming **Karen Rain** and **Jubilee Cooke**. A draft dating the NYT to 29 January 2019 is wrong.
  - **Not directly read this session:** the NYT pieces, the *Yoga Journal* piece, and Lucas's post, all cited at second hand. No standalone Wikipedia article on the scandal exists and there is no page for Karen Rain; both 404. That is a gap in Wikipedia, not in the record.
- **2019.** Sharath Jois's public statement: "It brings me immense pain that I also witnessed him giving improper adjustments". **[A: quoted in Wikipedia; the original Instagram post not opened]**
- **11 November 2024.** **Sharath Jois dies** of a heart attack at 53, hiking with students near **Humpback Rocks**, Blue Ridge Parkway, Virginia, during a University of Virginia visit. **[A: Wikipedia, R. Sharath Jois]**
  - Coordinate corrected: Humpback Rocks (Milepost 5.8, Augusta County) is approximately **37.9728, -78.8995**; the pool's 37.9975, -79.0175 lies ~11 km west of the ridge. Warning for future compilers: English Wikipedia's "Humpback Rocks" article is a **different feature**, a rock group off South Georgia.
- **Ongoing.** Saraswathi Rangaswamy and her daughter Sharmila run a separate school in Mysore; Manju Jois teaches in the United States. Authority has fragmented across family branches. **[A: Wikipedia]**

- **`joseph_pilates`**: same body-culture wing; the atlas already cross-links these journeys to each other (Pilates's file cites `johann_jahn.journey.json`). - **Indra Devi**: trained under Krishnamacharya at the same yogashala in the 1930s. **[R: census line 493]**. No journey file yet, no stop built on her, not named in the campa. Recorded as the strongest unbuilt interlock.
- **Giuseppe Tucci**: queued, thematically adjacent, **no documented contact with Jois**. Not used.
- **Explicitly NOT inherited:** Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354), Paris (48.8566, 2.3522), Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816). No attested link exists. The 1974 South America trip is real but its city is unknown, and Buenos Aires is not a permissible guess.
