# Clotilde Sakharoff (Clotilde von Derp), 1892-1974: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14. Slug: `clotilde_sakharoff`. Dancer, born Clotilde Margarete Anna Edle von der Planitz in Berlin; stage name Clotilde von Derp; from 1919 half of the duo billed as Les Sakharoff.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or an inference the cited source does not make. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. A date or coordinate supplied by a compiler rather than by a source is marked **[unsourced date]** or **[unsourced pin]** and left visible.

**Standing warning.** The documentary base is two Wikipedia articles per person (English and German) plus the Neue Deutsche Biographie entry by Frank-Manuel Peter. The Dansmuseet Stockholm page "The Sakharoffs," which several tour claims rest on, returns **404**; everything resting on it alone is **[R]**. No monograph, periodical run, or archival finding aid beyond one Houghton link was opened.

---

## 1. Names and birth dates

- Born **5 November 1892, Berlin**, as Clotilde Margarete Anna Edle von der Planitz **[A: English and German Wikipedia; corroborated by the NDB, "5.11.1892 Berlin"]**. Father **Hans Edler von der Planitz (1863-1932)**, Prussian major; mother **Margarete von Muschwitz (1868-1955)** **[A: German Wikipedia]**. Minor Vogtland nobility.
- **Contradiction.** English Wikipedia's *Alexander Sakharoff* article says in body text: "He married the German dancer Clotilde von der Planitz (**1893**-1974)." This is often called an infobox error; it is not, since the English Clotilde von Derp infobox reads `birth_date = 1892-11-05`. It is a stray in a sister article. Recorded, not deleted.
- The stage name **Clotilde von Derp** is in use by her 1910 debut **[A: English Wikipedia]**. No source explains "Derp." **Gap.**
- English Wikipedia spells the husband **Sakharoff**, German Wikipedia **Sacharoff**; the French billing was **Les Sakharoff** **[A]**.
- **Alexander's birth day is contested [A, unresolved].** English Wikipedia: **13 May 1886**, Mariupol, to a Jewish family. German Wikipedia: **14 May Julian / 26 May 1886 Gregorian**, youngest of seven sons of Maria Polianskaja and Simon Zuckermann, born Alexander Zuckermann. The English date matches neither leg of that conversion (13 May Julian is 25 May Gregorian). The "Jewish family" descriptor is English Wikipedia's alone.

## 2. Munich childhood, c. 1900-1909

- Parents separate; the mother moves the family to **Munich, Schwabing**, and founds a **music institute for children**, dated 1900 **[A: German Wikipedia]**.
- **She wanted first to be a violinist [A: English Wikipedia only]**; German Wikipedia does not carry it, and no year attaches to it. She works as an **artist's and sculptor's model**, German Wikipedia naming **Wilhelm Krieger [A]**; no year in either edition, so the 1905 in circulation is **[unsourced date]**.
- Ballet from **Julie Bergmann and Anna Ornelli** of the **Munich Opera [A: English Wikipedia]**, with **no year** given: "c. 1908" is **[unsourced date]**, and the upgrade of "Munich Opera" to a court ballet school is **[R]**. The Nationaltheater sits at 48.13944, 11.57944; the pin in circulation was some 160 m off and is corrected in the journey file.

## 3. Munich debut and the expressionist circle, 1909-1913

- **1909:** she begins giving public dance performances in Munich **[A: German Wikipedia]**, vaguer and earlier than the English account.
- **25 April 1910, Hotel Union, Munich:** professional solo debut as Clotilde von Derp **[A: English Wikipedia]**; German Wikipedia names no venue. Both readings are carried as separate stops.
- **Rudolf von Delius**, in *Masken: Wochenschrift des Schauspielhauses Düsseldorf*, vol. 5, 23 May 1910: *"Diese Clothilde ist endlich einmal eine Tänzerin, die völlig eigenes Erleben formt, die sich von keinerlei historischem Kostüm gängeln lässt, die aus dem Reichtum ihres kleinen Herzens eine neue Welt entfaltet."* **[A: quoted with citation in German Wikipedia]**. **No verified published English translation was found**, so the journey file carries the German verbatim.
- By c. 1910 she is received in Munich as one of the first "modern" dancers, praised for grace and naturalness against ballet convention **[A: German Wikipedia]**. English Wikipedia adds that "her style was said to be elegant and more modern than that achieved by Isadora Duncan" **[A]**, the only measurement against a named contemporary in the reachable record.
- She moves in the circle of the **Neue Künstlervereinigung München** and in the **Blaue Reiter** around **Kandinsky**, admired by **Rilke** and (German Wikipedia) **Yvan Goll** **[A]**. **Correction:** the NKVM was founded in **January 1909**, and German Wikipedia dates *her* involvement with it to the joint programmes after 1913; only the Blaue Reiter belongs to 1911.
- **Reinhardt's *Sumurun*.** The 1912 dating in circulation is wrong on the source's own authority: the **NDB**, cited by English Wikipedia, has her in it **from September 1910 in Munich** with her *"größte Erfolge"* in **London in 1911**, and Wikipedia's *Grete Wiesenthal* article independently dates the London run to 1911, New York to 1912 **[A]**. **Unresolved:** English Wikipedia gives her "the title role," which Wikipedia elsewhere assigns to **Grete Wiesenthal** (Berlin 1910) and **Camilla Eibenschütz** (Broadway 1912). **No source names the London venue**; the Coliseum is an **[unsourced pin]**.
- **1913, London:** photographs of her by **Rudolf Dührkoop** exhibited at the **Royal Photographic Society [A: English Wikipedia]**.

## 4. Alexander Zuckermann becomes Alexander Sakharoff

- Born Mariupol 1886 (disputed day, §1) **[A]**.
- **St Petersburg** schooling **[R: Dansmuseet, 404]**; dropped from the journey file.
- **Paris, 1903-1905:** law alongside painting; **Académie des Beaux-Arts** and **Académie Julian**; German Wikipedia adds that seeing **Sarah Bernhardt** turned him from the easel **[A: English Wikipedia for the schools, German for dates, law and Bernhardt]**. The Académie Julian's principal address was the Passage des Panoramas, rue du Dragon a branch atelier.
- **Munich, 1909:** joins the **NKVM**, the circle around Kandinsky and **Thomas von Hartmann** (German Wikipedia records a synaesthetic collaboration); **Jawlensky** and **Werefkin** paint him, remarking on his androgynous appearance **[A: both editions]**. **Honest negative:** the Wikipedia article on the NKVM does **not** name him among performers at its exhibitions. The **Moderne Galerie Thannhauser** pin in circulation is **[R]**, since no source puts his joining or the sittings there.
- **Munich Tonhalle, 2 June 1910:** solo debut, in a piece drawing on mythological motifs and Renaissance painting **[A: German Wikipedia, "Alexander Sacharoff," verbatim]**. The German sentence is sometimes credited to Dansmuseet; that attribution is **false**. The Tonhalle (Kaim-Saal, Türkenstraße 5, destroyed 1944) stood at 48.14498, 11.57233; the pin in circulation was about 415 m north.

## 5. Meeting, war, marriage, 1913-1921

- **1913, Munich Press Ball:** they meet **[A: German Wikipedia]**, and from this year appear on shared programmes as a duo and as two soloists **[A]**.
- **1914:** the pair relocate to **neutral Switzerland** **[A: English Wikipedia]**. **No city is named**; the journey file pins Zurich and says so.
- **1916:** **Georg Kolbe** writes to her: *"Zweifellos sind sie für mich die erste Tänzerin Deutschlands."*
  - **Two corrections and a downgrade.** German Wikipedia frames it *«…», schrieb ihr später der Bildhauer Georg Kolbe im Jahr 1916*: he wrote it **to her**, and *sind sie* is the formal second person, so the sense is "Without doubt **you** are, for me, the foremost dancer in Germany," not "she is." It is a private letter, not a public remark. **The sentence carries no footnote at all** in the German wikitext. **Downgraded to [R]**, hedge in `date_confidence`.
- **Marriage, 25 January 1919, Zurich**, witness **Marianne von Werefkin**; German Wikipedia calls it a *Zweckehe* undertaken to ease an American tour **[A: both editions on Clotilde, supported by the NDB genealogy]**.
  - **Internal contradiction.** German Wikipedia's own *Alexander Sacharoff* article gives **25 July 1919**. This is Wikipedia against itself; 25 January is better supported. Both recorded.
- **Metropolitan Opera, New York**, backed by **Edith Rockefeller**, "without any great success" **[A for the event]**. **No source gives a year**; the "17 February 1920" in circulation is **fabricated** and is not used, and the expansion to **Edith Rockefeller McCormick** is **[R]**.
- **1921:** a **poster by George Barbier** "was seen as showing a mutually complementary androgynous couple united in dance" **[A: English Wikipedia quoting Lodder, Kokkori and Mileeva, *Utopian Reality*, Brill 2013, 33-35]**. It is a **commercial poster**, not an illustration, and **no source places it in Paris** **[unsourced pin]**.

## 6. Les Sakharoff between the wars

- They live in **Paris** as Les Sakharoff until the Second World War **[A: English Wikipedia]**, which gives **no start year**; the **NDB dates the Paris base to 1919**. The 1922 in circulation has no source. German Wikipedia adds **London and Monaco** without years **[A]**.
- **Costume:** "Their outrageous costumes included wigs made from silver and gold coloured metal, with hats and outfits decorated with flowers and wax fruit," and "Both Sacharoff and Clotilde were known for their crossdressing costumes" **[R: English Wikipedia's own unquoted editorial prose, footnoted to Karl Toepfer, *Empire of Ecstasy* (University of California Press, 1997), 219-222]**.
  - **Correction.** Both are widely relayed as **quotations from Toepfer**. They are not: the raw wikitext shows them unquoted with a bare `<ref name=empire/>`, and the wording would not retrieve from the open-access UC Press edition (publishing.cdlib.org, docId ft167nb0sp). Neither appears as a quote in the journey file. Wikipedia's template also misspells the author "Toepler, Karl."
- **1933: Émile Vuillermoz** publishes a study of their collaborations with composers **[A: English Wikipedia]**. Not read.
- **1934:** they tour **China and Japan**; the earlier tour "was so successful that they returned again in 1934" **[A]**. **No city, no venue** in any source: Shanghai, Beijing, Tokyo and Osaka come from the **404 Dansmuseet page [R]**, and the Tokyo Imperial Theatre is an **[unsourced pin]**.
- **Americas:** "The couple and their extravagant costumes visited both North and South America" **[A]**, **no year**; the 1935 in circulation is **[unsourced date]**. Havana, São Paulo, Montevideo and Buenos Aires come from the same dead page **[R]**, and Havana was additionally filed under a "South American itinerary," a plain category error. Havana, São Paulo and Beijing were dropped from the journey file.

## 7. Exile: Spain and Buenos Aires

Contradicted three ways and **not** resolved.

- **English Wikipedia:** "moved to Spain when France was invaded by Germany… returned to South America making a new base in Buenos Aires until 1949." **No year for the flight**; 1940 follows from the invasion **[R]**. The word *returned* points back to the earlier tours.
- **German Wikipedia** dates the Buenos Aires period **from 1938 [A]**.
- **German Wikipedia, "Alexander Sacharoff":** he emigrated **in 1941**, and the couple **separated but reunited for artistic projects [A]**. The separation is **not** dated by the source, and English Wikipedia mentions none.
- Only the country is named for Spain; **Madrid is an [unsourced pin]**.
- **1948:** he returns to Europe **[R: Dansmuseet, 404]**.
- **1949, Buenos Aires:** *Reflexiones sobre la danza y la música* (Emecé; OCLC 637742007), from which English Wikipedia quotes: **"Clotilde Sakharoff and I did not dance with music or accompanied by music: we danced music"** and **"Our aim was to transpose the sense expressed by the music of the sounds, to the music of the movements"** **[A as quoted; the book was not consulted, and whether this is the first publication is unconfirmed]**. The only first-person sentences about her dancing in the reachable record are her husband's.

## 8. Rome and Siena, 1950-1963

- **c. 1950:** they settle in Italy on the invitation of **Count Guido Chigi Saracini**; he teaches at the **Accademia Musicale Chigiana**, Siena, developing the theory of *danced music*, and they **tour Italy in 1950 [A: English Wikipedia]**. **Dansmuseet gave 1952 for the Rome move [R, unreachable]**; not reconciled.
- They **open their own dance school in Rome [A]**, in the same run of sentences as the Chigi Saracini invitation and with **no year attached**. The "1956" in circulation for the school is **fabricated** and conflicts with the source's narrative order; the journey file dates it 1950.
- **Her last performance.** German Wikipedia: **Rome, 1954**, after which she only teaches **[A]**. English Wikipedia: they "stopped dancing together in **1956**," infobox `years_active 1910-1956` **[A]**. **Contradiction carried as two stops, not resolved.**
- **25 September 1963:** he dies in **Siena [A]**; buried at the **Cimitero Acattolico**, Rome (41.8764, 12.4799), the ground that holds Keats and Shelley's ashes **[A]**.
- **11 January 1974:** she dies in **Rome**, aged 81, and is laid in the **same grave**; German Wikipedia carries a photograph of it captioned *Grab der Eheleute* **[A]**.

## 9. Estate and afterlife

- **1973, Stockholm:** the **Dansmuseet** has held **23 Sakharoff costumes since 1973 [A: English Wikipedia]**. The claim that **Clotilde sold** them the year before her death is **not in the source**, which names no seller. **Downgraded to [R]** and restated in the journey file as a holding.
- **Papers of Alexandre and Clotilde Sakharoff, 1922-1970**, **Houghton Library**, Harvard (42.3732, -71.1159) **[A: finding aid linked from German Wikipedia]**. No accession date anywhere; the journey stop takes the closing year of the span and says so.
- **1997, Cologne:** the **Deutsches Tanzarchiv** (MediaPark 7, approx. 50.9497, 6.9424) buys **65 costumes, hundreds of set and costume designs, 500 photographs [A]**. The "over 300 costume designs" in circulation is over-precise and drops the set designs; two inconsistent pins for this one institution were also in circulation.
- **Undated:** she "gave and sold many of their writings and costumes… to museums and auctions," eventually selling the **1909 Jawlensky portrait of her husband [A: English Wikipedia, which misnames the painter "Alexander Jawlensky"]**. Where or when, no source states.
- **2002-2003:** ***Die Sacharoffs: Zwei Tänzer aus dem Umkreis des Blauen Reiters***, at the German Dance Archive, Cologne, and the **Museum Villa Stuck**, Munich (48.1407, 11.5996) **[A: German Wikipedia]**; catalogue edited by **Frank-Manuel Peter and Rainer Stamm [A]**. **Horst Koegler** is credited in circulation with catalogue essays; German Wikipedia's Weblinks in fact list **two Koegler pieces, both reviews [A]**, neither fetched. Kandinsky trained under Franz von Stuck **at the Munich Academy around 1900**, not at the villa, so reading the venue as a closed loop overstates it **[R]**.
- **No public monument, statue or street name** for either Sakharoff was found **[R: absence in English and German Wikipedia, no further searching possible]**. An argument from silence.

## 10. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Her birth year:** 1892 (both her articles, NDB) vs 1893 (English Wikipedia's *Alexander Sakharoff*, body text).
2. **His birth day:** 13 May 1886 (English) vs 14 May Julian / 26 May 1886 Gregorian (German). Neither converts into the other.
3. **Marriage:** 25 January 1919 (both articles on her, NDB) vs 25 July 1919 (German article on him).
4. **The *Sumurun* title role:** Clotilde vs Grete Wiesenthal vs Camilla Eibenschütz, all per Wikipedia.
5. **Buenos Aires:** from 1938 (German) vs after the 1940 invasion (English) vs his emigration in 1941 (German on him). Only "until 1949" is common ground.
6. **End of her stage career:** 1954, Rome, her last performance (German) vs 1956, end of the duo (English).
7. **Rome relocation:** 1950 (English) vs 1952 (Dansmuseet, unreachable). **Paris base:** 1919 (NDB) vs no year at all (English); the 1922 in circulation has no source.

## 11. Gaps stated as gaps

- **No writings by Clotilde herself were located.** Not a book, an essay, or a letter in print. Her recorded voice here is zero sentences long: every quotation about her dancing is by a man writing about her. What manuscript material exists appears to sit unedited at Houghton and in Cologne, and no critical edition or translation of either Sakharoff's writings was found.
- **The Dansmuseet page is dead (404)**, removing the only itinerary source for the Far East and South American tours; every city on those tours is an unverified relay.
- **Toepfer, 219-222** could not be retrieved, so the two costume sentences remain unchecked against the book. **Vuillermoz's 1933 study**, the Lodder/Kokkori/Mileeva volume and the 2002 catalogue were cited but not opened.
- **No venue is documented** for the London *Sumurun*, the Tokyo appearance, the Buenos Aires performances or the Spanish stop of 1940. Four pins in the journey file are stand-ins and say so.
- **The web search budget was exhausted** during gathering (DuckDuckGo CAPTCHA, Mojeek HTTP 403), so no corroboration outside Wikipedia, the NDB and one Harvard finding aid was possible.
