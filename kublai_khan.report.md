# Kublai Khan (1215-1294): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-09. Slug `kublai_khan`. Companion artifact: `kublai_khan.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on one interested witness. Contradictions are flagged in place and **not adjudicated**. Where verification caught an outright error in a repeated claim, the correction is given with its authority.

The journey file carries the register *national mythology: the canon is true*. That governs the campa prose, not this report. Hedging lives here and in each stop's `date_confidence`.

## 1. The sources, and what they cannot do

Four bodies of evidence, overlapping badly. **The Yuan Shi** (compiled 1370 under the Ming from Yuan court records) supplies offices, campaigns, edicts, succession; it is also the source that says the Mongols took Pagan. **Rashid al-Din's *Jami al-Tawarikh*** (Ilkhanid Persia, c. 1300-1310) supplies the steppe material, written by a vizier of Kublai's own kinsmen for Chinggisids about Chinggisids. **Marco Polo's *Travels*** (dictated c. 1298-1299) is the only extended eyewitness portrait of the court and the one no Chinese record corroborates; see §7. **Wikipedia and World History Encyclopedia** are what the research pass could reach at volume; both were caught in errors itemized below, and where they contradict each other or their own dedicated articles that is recorded, not resolved.

**Not reached.** Rossabi's *Khubilai Khan* (1988), the standard modern biography, was **not read**, and neither were the Asian chronicles listed in §13; they appear in the journey's `sources` arrays because the intermediary cites them. Read that as a dependency, not a credential.

## 2. Steppe childhood, 1215-1242

Born **23 September 1215**, second son of Tolui and Sorghaghtani Beki **[A** Wikipedia, 'Kublai Khan'**]**, with the caveat that some sources give only the year. **The birthplace is not recorded in any primary source**; the Kerulen pin (48.0 / 108.5) is inferred from Tolui's appanage. **[R]**

**1224, the first hunt on the Ili.** Genghis anoints the boy's thumb with the fat of his first kill and praises his words as full of wisdom. **[R** Rashid al-Din**]** The only childhood anecdote in the record, retrospective, by an author employed by the dynasty.

**1227, Genghis dies. [A]** The arithmetic in circulation is wrong: Wikipedia says "aged 12", but born 23 September 1215 with Genghis dead in August 1227 he was **eleven**, turning twelve the following month. The atlas carries the death at Liupanshan, 18 August 1227, following `genghis_khan.journey.json`.

**1232** Tolui dies and Kublai marries Tegulen Khatun; **1233** she dies; **1234** he marries Chabi **[A]**, no months recorded. **1236**, the Hebei appanage of 10,000 households **[A]**, pinned at Xingzhou, 37.0682 / 114.5048.

**1242, Haiyun at Karakorum. [A]** Contradiction flagged: Wikipedia's 'Kublai Khan' puts Zhenjin's birth in the same year; its article 'Zhenjin' gives **8 July 1243** (d. 5 January 1286). The journey states the disagreement in `date_confidence` instead of choosing.

## 3. Viceroyalty and the southwest, 1251-1258

**1251**, Mongke becomes Great Khan and gives Kublai the Chinese territories **[A]**, based near Jingzhao / Xi'an, 34.3416 / 108.9398, through 1259.

**1253, the Jinsha crossing and Dali.** Pin corrected: the pool gave 26.7853 / 99.8329, but Shigu at the First Bend of the Yangtze is at roughly **26.867 / 99.955**, so the pool's point sits about 17 km southwest, in the hills off the river. Dali falls in 1253 but King Duan Xingzhi flees to Shanchan and resistance runs to about 1256 **[A** Wikipedia, 'Dali Kingdom'**]**. Phagpa enters the entourage on this campaign. **[A]**

**1257, the audit**: 142 regulatory breaches found among Kublai's northern administrators, and he is stripped of his commands **[A]**. **1257 as a campaign year alongside Mongke is therefore wrong**, though World History Encyclopedia gives it. Mongke restored him and gave him the Eastern Army in **1258**; he took the field in **1259**. Corrected in the journey.

**1258, Uriyangkhadai takes Thang Long. [A]** Executed by a general out of Kublai's Dali command, **not by Kublai in person**; the pool's phrasing is easily misread as presence.

## 4. The succession war, 1259-1264

The densest cluster of date-conflation in the pool. Three items had to be pulled apart.

**Mongke dies 11 August 1259 at Diaoyucheng. [A** Wikipedia, 'Mongke Khan'**]** Pin corrected: the pool gave 29.92 / 106.268; the fortress stands on Diaoyu Mountain east of Hechuan at roughly **29.99 / 106.29**, 8 km north.

**The Ezhou siege, the concealed death and the truce with Jia Sidao are 1259, not 1258.** The pool's chronology lens conflated the *grant* of the Eastern Army (1258) with the *campaign* (1259); its own geography lens had 1259 right.

**15 April 1260**, proclaimed Great Khan by a kurultai he convened himself; **5 May 1260**, crowned. **[A]** Place corrected: both happen at **Kaiping**, renamed **Shangdu only in 1264**. "Crowned at Shangdu" is an anachronism, however common. Also **1260**, the Jiaochao paper currency; **1260-1264**, the Toluid Civil War, with Karakorum starved of grain and changing hands more than once; **1263**, the kheshig organized; **21 August 1264**, Ariq Boke surrenders. **[A]**

**"Capital moves to Xanadu in 1263": wrong twice.** World History Encyclopedia gives 1263; Kaiping was renamed and made **summer capital in 1264** (Wikipedia, 'Shangdu', which the pool's own afterlife lens dates correctly, contradicting its chronology lens). And it was **never the sole capital**: Dadu becomes the principal seat in 1272.

## 5. Building Dadu, 1264-1272

**1264, the Jade Island decision. [A]** Substantive correction: Wikipedia's 'Khanbaliq' calls the Jin pleasure palace on Jade Island the **Daming Palace**, which was the **Tang** complex at Chang'an, a thousand kilometres away and four centuries earlier; the Jin building here was the **Daning Palace**. Wikipedia propagates the error; the atlas does not. Coordinates 39.925 / 116.39 are sound. **Walls from 1264, great palace from 1274, city complete 1293 [A]**: all three check out.

**18 December 1271**, the regime is renamed Dai Yuan; **1272**, Dadu proclaimed capital. **[A** Wikipedia, 'Khanbaliq'**]** These are **different years**. The pool's chronology lens ran them together as one 1271 event; its geography lens made the mirror error, writing "1272, the year he also names his dynasty Yuan." Both wrong in the same joint; the journey gives them separate stops. **1270**, Phagpa's square script and his promotion to Imperial Preceptor **[A]**, folded into the Dai Yuan stop since the pool supplies no location.

## 6. The conquest of the Song, 1268-1279

**Xiangyang, 16 October 1268 to 14 March 1273. [A]** Duration corrected: the pool called it a five-year siege; it ran **four years and five months**. The Persian counterweight trebuchets are attested; **Marco Polo's claim that his family supplied the design is chronologically impossible**, since he had not yet arrived, and Yule prints it anyway **[R]**. Bayan of Baarin takes command for the final drive, 1273. **28 March 1276, Lin'an surrendered [R** World History Encyclopedia; day not independently confirmed here**]**.

**19 March 1279, Yamen. [A]** Contradiction flagged, and unusually resolvable. Wikipedia's 'Kublai Khan' says **Emperor Gong** jumped into the sea; Gong had been taken alive at Lin'an in 1276 and later became a monk in Tibet. The boy who drowns at Yamen is **Zhao Bing**, carried into the water by Lu Xiufu. The pool's geography lens calls him eight; Wikipedia's 'Battle of Yamen' says **seven**, and the journey uses seven. Coordinates 22.2667 / 113.0833 match the article exactly. That same lens states Kublai died "at seventy-nine", which is wrong (§9) and is not repeated.

## 7. Marco Polo: testimony, not record

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical** from `marco_polo.journey.json`:

| Stop | lat / lng | date |
|---|---|---|
| Khanbaliq, Niccolo and Maffeo before the Great Khan | 39.9042 / 116.4074 | 1266-01-01 |
| Shangdu, arrival at the summer court | 42.3597 / 116.1858 | 1275-05-01 |
| Khanbaliq, taken into service | 39.9042 / 116.4074 | 1275-09-01 |
| Yangzhou, the governorship | 32.3947 / 119.4143 | 1279-01-01 |
| Karajang (Yunnan), the mission | 25.0389 / 102.7183 | 1283-01-01 |
| Zaiton (Quanzhou), departure with Lady Kokotchin | 24.8741 / 118.6757 | 1291-03-01 |

These differ from the coordinates the geography lens produced independently (Shangdu 42.35802 / 116.18516, Quanzhou 24.9139 / 118.5858). **The canonical pins win**, so Kublai's stops and Polo's land on the same points.

**Dates disputed.** Wikipedia gives seventeen years of service, World History Encyclopedia gives 1275-1292; the start year is soft. **No Chinese source names Marco Polo at all**: not the Yuan Shi, not a gazetteer, not a tax roll. That is the standing objection to the whole account, recorded rather than argued away. **The Yangzhou governorship [R]** is uncorroborated and long doubted; the office he describes maps onto no Yuan post in the city. Carried because the atlas already carries it, with the doubt in `date_confidence`.

**The quotations: three of four citations in the pool were materially wrong.** All five were verified verbatim against the fetched Project Gutenberg #10636 text of Yule-Cordier 1903. The **wording** is genuine in every case; the **provenance** was not.

1. **"Welcome is he too."** Pool said Vol. I, Book I. It sits **before** the "BOOK I." heading, in **Vol. I, Prologue, ch. XIV**.
2. **"How would you have me to become a Christian? ... then I will receive baptism."** Pool said Book I, framed as the Khan's words on the brothers' first visit. It is in **Vol. I, Book Second, ch. VI, inside Yule's editorial Note 1**, which opens "Here Ramusio has a long and curious addition." It is therefore **not** Polo/Rustichello narrative but **Ramusio's sixteenth-century recension** quoted in an annotation. A real downgrade of status, and the journey's `quote_source` says so.
3. **"I have conquered you by force of arms..."** Pool cited a "chapter on the Great Kaan's ordinances against gambling". **No such chapter exists**; the description was invented. The passage is in **Vol. I, Book Second, ch. XXXIV**, on the religion and customs of the Cathayans.
4. **"I had far liever hearken about the strange things..."** Pool said Book I; it is in the **Prologue, ch. XV**, and Yule ends it with a semicolon.
5. **"Do as you think right."** Citation stands (Vol. I, Book II, on Ahmad Fanakati), but **the exchange is undated in the source**; 1280-01-01 is editorial and says so. Ahmad's own line, "Such an one deserves death, for he hath done this or that against your imperial dignity", is reported speech *by the minister*, so it sits in the campa rather than the `quote` field.

**Rabban Bar Sauma [A, misdescribed in the pool].** He travelled 1287-1288 as ambassador **of** the Ilkhan Arghun, not as Kublai's ambassador "alongside" him. He was a **Yuan-born Nestorian monk from Dadu**, the real atlas link. No usable coordinate, so **no stop was created**: a deliberate omission.

## 8. The wars beyond the frontier, 1266-1293

**1266, the letter to the King of Japan. [A]** Original preserved at Todai-ji, Nara. **Only the opening and closing lines were verified; the body is unread**, taken on Turnbull's authority. **1274, the first invasion [A]**: Tsushima 4 November (Komoda Beach, 34.2377 / 129.2889), Iki 13 November, Hakata 19-20 November, about 900 ships. Iki was dropped for stop count, not on evidence.

**1281, the second invasion. [A]** Roughly 140,000 men in two fleets. The **Nagato diversion of 300 ships is firmly dated 25 June 1281**, but the pool's pin (34.1 / 131.1833) sits **inland in the Yamaguchi basin**; the coast is near 34.37 / 131.18. Rather than carry a corrected but thin stop, Nagato was folded into the Hakata 1281 campa. **The pool gives no day for the main disaster**; August is the standard placing, flagged as a placing. **1277, Ngasaunggyan [R]** was dropped for stop count.

**1282-1283, Champa. [A]** Pin corrected: Cha Ban / Do Ban, the Vijaya citadel, is at **13.92869 / 109.07507**; the pool's 13.9694 / 109.135 is about 8.5 km northeast. **1284 Tagaung; 1287 Pagan [R]**, a live dispute: the Yuan Shi and Marco Polo say the city was taken, **Michael Aung-Thwin argues the column halted some 160 km short**. The atlas carries both and resolves neither. **1285 and 1287, the second and third invasions of Dai Viet [A]**, after which tributary relations follow; the Bach Dang stake stratagem is carried via intermediaries, not from the *Toan Thu*.

**Java, 1292-1293. [A, three corrections.]** The pool dates the sailing from Quanzhou to **January 1292**, which **predates Kublai's own order** of March 1292; the fleet sailed late in 1292 and arrived January 1293. The pool puts casualties at "over 3,000", but Wikipedia's 'Mongol invasion of Java' gives **12,000-18,000 Yuan dead, around 60% of the force**, and both figures appear in the campa. And the target was **not Singhasari**: Kertanagara was already dead and his kingdom absorbed by Kediri before the fleet arrived, which is why the Yuan fought Kediri alongside Raden Wijaya, who then turned on them.

**Tibet: Drikung Kagyu revolt 1285, crushed by Buqa-Temur in 1291 at a reported 10,000 dead. [A]** No coordinates in the pool, so no stop; folded into the Sangha campa. A gap, stated as one. **Cambodia, 1283-1295 [R]**: tribute demanded, no invasion, and Zhou Daguan's embassy postdates Kublai's death. Dropped as outside the life.

## 9. Death and burial, 1281-1294

**1281** Chabi dies; **1283** marriage to Nambui; **5 January 1286** Zhenjin dies. **[A]** **1287, Sangha introduces the Zhiyuan Chao [A]**: year corrected, since the pool said 1286 and Wikipedia dates the currency to 1287.

**18 February 1294, death at Dadu. [A]** Age contradiction, resolvable. Wikipedia says 78, World History Encyclopedia says 79-80, the pool's geography lens wrote "seventy-nine" as fact. Born 23 September 1215, dead 18 February 1294, he was **78**. The journey uses 78 and records the variance. Succeeded by his grandson Temur Khan, 1294. **[A]**

**Burial: unknown. [R]** The Yuan Shi says only that the body was sent north. Interment near Burkhan Khaldun in the Ikh Khorig precinct follows the Genghisid pattern and the Great Taboo, both attested **for the dynasty generally**; the claim for Kublai's own grave is tradition, not a located site, and no tomb has ever been found. Pinned 48.75 / 109.0333.

## 10. Afterlife and transmission

**1279, Araniko's White Stupa. [A, pin corrected.]** The pool gave 39.9236 / 116.3833, which falls between two different monuments and nearer Beihai. Araniko's stupa is at **Miaoying Temple, 39.9240 / 116.3569**. **The White Dagoba in Beihai Park is a separate Qing structure of 1651.** Two monuments conflated; corrected, and "Beihai" dropped from the place name. His **1294 posthumous portrait [A]** became the standard image in the Yuan imperial portrait albums, whose **present repository is not stated in any reachable source**. A gap.

**1298-1299, Rustichello takes the dictation in Genoa; the autograph is lost. [A]** About 150 manuscripts survive in six textual traditions per Philippe Menard: Pipino's Latin *Iter* (1302, 70 MSS), Ramusio's Venice edition (1559), Frampton's English (1579, from Santaella's 1503 Castilian). Sources disagree on counts and groupings; that variance is intrinsic to the tradition, not a resolvable error.

**1369** Shangdu sacked by Ming forces; **1872** Stephen Bushell documents the ruins; **2002** preservation work; **2012** UNESCO inscription **[A** Wikipedia, 'Shangdu'**]**, compressed into one stop. The **1375 Catalan Atlas [A]** was dropped for stop count; its BnF repository is general knowledge, not stated in the fetched extract.

**1797, Coleridge at Ash Farm. [A, pin corrected, date contested.]** The pool gave 51.2039 / -3.5586, which lands on **Porlock village**, about 6 km east-southeast of Ash Farm near Culbone Church at roughly **51.2225 / -3.6290**. The date is disputed: the **Crewe manuscript says autumn 1797**, the **preface says summer 1797**, **some scholars argue May 1798**. The opium dream and the person from Porlock come from Coleridge's own preface and read as a literary frame. Published 1816 at Byron's urging.

## 11. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Named in campa (mutual gaze): **`genghis_khan`** twice, at **Liupanshan, 18 August 1227** (35.6 / 106.2, canonical pin inherited), and at **Zhongdu, June 1215** (39.867 / 116.333), the Jin capital his grandfather starved into surrender **three months before Kublai was born**, on the ground where Dadu was later laid out beside the ruin; that stop was cut for count and the fact carried into the "Dadu, the walls begin" campa. **`marco_polo`**: six inherited pins, four stops naming him, three quotations (§7). **`matteo_ricci`** and **`mao_zedong`**: both in one Beijing stop (39.9158 / 116.3908, Ricci's canonical arrival pin, 24 January 1601), tagged as geographic echo rather than intersection.

**Found and deliberately unused.** Polo's Bukhara (39.7747 / 64.4286), Hormuz and Jerusalem stops carry real Chinggisid links, Bukhara most of all, since the Polos waited three years there because of the Toluid succession wars. They belong to Polo's journey; using them would put Kublai pins in cities he never saw. **The Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris and Buenos Aires probes returned nothing connected to Kublai.**

## 12. Honest gaps

1. **No birthplace**: the Kerulen pin is inference from an appanage. **No grave**: the Burkhan Khaldun pin is tradition, no tomb, no excavation.
2. **No independent witness to Marco Polo.** Every court scene, every quotation and the Yangzhou governorship rest on one book with no autograph and six divergent manuscript families.
3. **The standard biography and the Asian chronicles were not read**; their presence in the journey's `sources` arrays reflects the intermediary's citation, not direct consultation.
4. **The Ahmad exchange has no year in Polo** and is placed at 1280 editorially. **The 1266 Japan letter is unread in the middle.** **Tibet has no pin**: the 1285 revolt and the 1291 suppression of 10,000 people arrived without coordinates.
5. **The month is unknown for most of this life.** Twenty-eight of forty-five stops default an unknown month or day to 01, and each says so in `date_confidence`.
