# Pasquale Simonetti, "Pascalone 'e Nola" (1926-1955): research report

*2026-08-10. Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: a Campanian guappo of the postwar produce markets, shot dead on Corso Novara in Naples eighty days after his wedding; the killing that made his widow, Assunta "Pupetta" Maresca, a national figure.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same sentence. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or interpretive framing, including anything a single outlet asserts uncorroborated. Contradictions are flagged in place and left standing.

**Warning that governs the whole file.** There is no primary-document layer here. Nothing rests on a trial transcript, a police file, a parish register, or an archival act consulted directly. Every claim comes from two Wikipedias, from Italian news and true-crime outlets, or from one scholarly work seen only through a Wikipedia citation. The Neapolitan press has retold this story continuously since 1955 and the retellings feed each other. Where three outlets agree, that may mean three witnesses or one witness copied twice.

---

## 1. Palma Campania and the Nolano, 1926-1943

**[A: English Wikipedia, "Pasquale Simonetti"; italiamistero.it]** Pasquale Simonetti is born 26 February 1926 in Palma Campania, a market town in the Nolano hinterland at the foot of Vesuvius. His father is a carter, his mother a housewife; the family is poor.

**[A: contemporary witness quoted in Felia Allum, *The Neapolitan Camorra* (Brunel PhD thesis, 2000), p. 370, via English Wikipedia]** He grows tall, and the height becomes the whole of his public image: "a tall man, nearly two meters, a gigantic man who always had a way of behaving, a 'guappaesica' way of doing things, a self-assured man."

**[R]** The nickname Pascalone 'e Nola, "big Pasquale of Nola," attaches him to Nola rather than to his birthplace. Sources use the two towns interchangeably as his home ground. No source explains when or by whom the name was first used.

---

## 2. The black market, 1943-1948

**[A: English Wikipedia; italiamistero.it]** His criminal career begins with the falsification of *annonarie*, wartime ration books, resold on the Naples black market. Both sources date this only to "the 1940s." No precise year exists in anything consulted.

**[A: English Wikipedia, exact wording]** On the cigarette trade the article says: "Soon a thriving trade smuggling cigarettes from Marseille, Gibraltar, Nice and Tangiers by highly organised and structured foreign criminal groups (the Marseillais, Corsicans, Sicilians) developed, employing local guappi to distribute the cigarettes."

**Attribution correction, important.** An intermediate pass read that sentence as Simonetti's own smuggling route and produced four geography entries claiming he ran cigarettes from those ports. **Wikipedia says no such thing.** The ports belong to the Marseillais, Corsican and Sicilian organisations; the local guappo was the distribution end inside Naples. The journey file states the ports as the trade's, not as his itinerary, and no stop claims he travelled to any of them.

**[R]** Contraband on that route landed on the Torre Annunziata and Castellammare coast in this period. **No source names any landing point for Simonetti specifically.**

---

## 3. President of prices, c. 1948-1952

**[A: internapoli.it, "Pascalone 'e Nola, così morì un guappo"; English Wikipedia]** After the war Simonetti moves into mediating the fruit-and-vegetable trade at the Corso Novara market in Naples, becoming a *presidente dei prezzi*, a "president of prices," taking roughly **100 lire on every quintal of potatoes sold** across a circuit of towns: Nola, Pomigliano, Giugliano, Scafati, Pagani and Marano.

**[A: internapoli.it]** At the height of the potato trade, with some 40,000 quintals moving daily, sources describe him netting three to four million lire a day.

**[A: English Wikipedia; terranostranews.it]** He is one of three rival price presidents: himself, Antonio Esposito ("Totonno 'e Pomigliano") of Pomigliano d'Arco, and Alfredo Maisto of Giugliano in Campania.

**[R: English Wikipedia vs internapoli.it, unresolved]** Wikipedia calls Esposito a business partner who *becomes* a rival. internapoli.it presents him as a competing price president with his own faction from the outset. **The chronology of a partnership turning into a rivalry is undated in both sources**, and the two framings are not reconciled here.

**[A: internapoli.it, with OpenStreetMap]** The Corso Novara market sits in the Rione Vasto, east of Naples Central Station. An intermediate pass tagged this [R]; it is not a reconstruction but an administrative fact, carried in OSM's own address hierarchy for the street. Retagged [A].

**[R: internapoli.it, single source]** Local tradition holds that one Francesco Antonio Tuccillo mediated informally between Simonetti and Esposito as the rivalry escalated. Undated and uncorroborated.

**[R: English Wikipedia; internapoli.it tone]** Simonetti is remembered as a classic postwar guappo: imposing, charismatic, an informal arbiter of disputes rather than a builder of a modern hierarchical organisation. An interpretive characterisation repeated across secondary retellings, not a documented event.

## 4. The war with Maisto, and prison, 1952-1954

**[A: internapoli.it; terranostranews.it]** In 1952 Simonetti's group fights Alfredo Maisto's men in a street gunfight at Melito di Napoli over produce mediation. Both describe Maisto sustaining two armed confrontations that year against Simonetti and an associate, Raffaele Baiano. **Some derivative summaries place a similar incident in 1951; the two Italian sources checked here both say 1952.**

**[A: English Wikipedia]** He is convicted of attempted murder over the shootout and sentenced in 1953 to **8 years and 3 months**. The court is not named beyond Naples in anything found.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** He is released after roughly two years, at **Christmas 1954**. No exact date is given; the journey file uses 25 December 1954 and says so in `date_confidence`.

**[A: napolitan.it; English Wikipedia]** On release he finds that Esposito has taken over their joint business and kept the profits, in breach of their agreement. This is the proximate motive for the conflict that kills him.

**Gap, stated as one.** **No source consulted places Simonetti in Poggioreale specifically.** Poggioreale is documented for Maresca, not for him. The journey file carries a Poggioreale stop for his imprisonment tagged as inference, with the gap named in `date_confidence`.

## 5. The marriage, 27 April 1955

**[A: English Wikipedia; internapoli.it; it.wikipedia "Assunta Maresca"]** Simonetti marries Assunta "Pupetta" Maresca on 27 April 1955 at Palma Campania. She is a former beauty queen from a Castellammare di Stabia family of the local underworld, the "Lampetielli," born 19 January 1935 and so twenty years old, not the eighteen of the retellings, and she is already pregnant. After the ceremony the couple go to the Pompei sanctuary, where Simonetti hands her his pistol as a votive promise to change his life. Roughly 500 guests attend, rivals Maisto and Esposito among them; the whole town takes part, guests offering the customary envelopes of money.

**[A but thinly sourced]** Antonio Esposito is said to have stood as witness, *compare d'anello*, at the wedding, a detail that sharpens the betrayal three months later. It appears in secondary Italian press summaries only.

## 6. Corso Novara, 16 July 1955

**[A: English Wikipedia; terranostranews.it]** Eighty days after the wedding, Simonetti is shot in broad daylight on Corso Novara, near the produce market, by **Carlo Gaetano Orlando**, called "Tanino 'e Bastimento," acting on Esposito's orders. The motive is control of the fruit-and-vegetable market. The eighty-day figure is exact and is itself the best evidence on the date: 27 April plus 80 days is 16 July.

**[A: internapoli.it, single source]** The immediate trigger: Orlando fails to return the customary greeting, a breach of guappo etiquette; the confrontation escalates and Orlando shoots Simonetti in the abdomen. **Only internapoli.it gives this account.**

**Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** The date of death splits three ways. English Wikipedia (both articles) and italiamistero.it give **16 July 1955**, shooting and death the same day; it.wikipedia "Assunta Maresca" gives **15 July**; internapoli.it has him shot **16 July** and dying of peritonitis at the **Ospedale degli Incurabili on 17 July**, after surgery. The 80-day arithmetic favours 16 July for the shooting and says nothing about the death. The journey file dates the shooting 16 July and the death 17 July, and states the split in `date_confidence` at both stops.

**[A: Gigi Di Fiore, *Il Mattino*, 16 July 2005, republished at internapoli.it]** Maresca on the hospital: "Corsi subito all'ospedale Incurabili dove l'avevano portato. L'avevano operato, ma era subentrata la peritonite. Mi rincuorava e mi fece il nome di Esposito come mandante della sparatoria. Pascalone era il mio principe azzurro e io volevo che si facesse giustizia, ma la giustizia voleva prove, prove, prove."

**Gaps.** No source found describes Simonetti's **funeral** (route, church, date) or names his **burial place**. The journey file carries both as [R] stops with the absence declared in `date_confidence`. Presumption of burial in Palma Campania or Nola rests on custom alone.

## 7. The widow's answer, October 1955

**[A: English Wikipedia "Pupetta Maresca"; ilmattino.it]** Six months pregnant and dressed in mourning, Maresca takes a taxi with her brother Ciro to the Bar Grandone, which stood on Corso Novara and not in Piazza Garibaldi, and shoots Antonio Esposito dead with a Smith & Wesson .38 held in both hands. The vendetta and the murder happen on the same street.

**Contradiction, partly resolved, residue flagged.** The date splits four ways: **4 October 1955** (it.wikipedia, napolitan.it, English Wikipedia "Pasquale Simonetti", storienapoli.it, Il Mattino), **4 August 1955** (internapoli.it, English Wikipedia "Pupetta Maresca"), and a stray **16 September**. Italian accounts describe the two killings as eighty days apart, and 16 July plus 80 days is 4 October exactly; only 4 October fits an arrest on 13-14 October and her stage of pregnancy. The journey file uses **4 October 1955** and names the 4 August variant in `date_confidence` rather than suppressing it.

**Detail in dispute.** The famous "29 shots" figure circulates alongside investigators' finding that the shots came from **at least four pistols**. Both are in print; neither is stated flatly in the journey file.

**[R: English Wikipedia "Pupetta Maresca"]** "I was afraid I would miss," she said later of holding the revolver two-handed. Wikipedia sources it to *The Times* (20 April 1959), Fiandaca (ed.), *Women and the Mafia* (2007), p. 12, and Clyde Haberman, *New York Times*, 4 September 2022. **John Dickie is not cited for it**, contrary to an intermediate note; the Dickie reference (*Mafia Republic*, 2013, pp. 93-95, not *Blood Brotherhoods*) sits in the Simonetti article's source list and was never opened. Tagged R for that reason and because it is her later gloss, not contemporaneous testimony.

**[A: it.wikipedia; English Wikipedia]** Maresca is arrested and taken to Poggioreale on 14 October 1955; sources give either 13 or 14 October.

**[R]** She gives birth to Simonetti's posthumous son, Pasquale ("Pasqualino"), in a prison infirmary. **No source gives a date**, and napolitan.it does not name the facility beyond "prison." The 1956 year is inferred from an October arrest at six months; naming Poggioreale is inference on inference, and the journey file says so.

## 8. The trial, 1959, and the pardon, 1965

**[A: English Wikipedia; *New York Times*, 7 April 1959, "'Crimes Of Honor' Debated By Italy"]** The trial opens before the Court of Assizes in Naples in April 1959 and runs 33 hearings. Maresca declares in court, "I would do it again!" **The NYT archive page itself was not opened**, only Wikipedia's citation of it.

**[A: napolitan.it; it.wikipedia]** Verdict delivered **16 May 1959** at 23:15 after twelve hours' deliberation: **Gaetano Orlando 30 years; Assunta Maresca 18 years and 6 months; her brother Ciro Maresca 12 years and 4 months.** An intermediate pass gave her sentence as "18 years." On appeal it is reduced to **13 years and 4 months** plus lifetime disqualification from public office (it.wikipedia); the date of that reduction is not given anywhere consulted.

**[A: napolitan.it]** Maresca is pardoned and released on **17 April 1965**, after "oltre dieci anni" of detention. internapoli.it and English Wikipedia give the year only; the day is single-sourced, flagged.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A: English Wikipedia]** Francesco Rosi directs *La sfida* (1958), with José Suárez as the Simonetti figure, renamed Vito Polara, and Rosanna Schiaffino as the Maresca figure. **The release year was not re-verified against a filmography.**

**[R: English Wikipedia]** A song, "La legge d'onore," is composed celebrating the revenge killing. No composer, date, or recording is named.

**[A: English Wikipedia; it.wikipedia]** In 1974 Pasqualino Simonetti, by then an adult in the Camorra underworld, is abducted and murdered; his body is never recovered. Both sources give the year and neither gives a date or place.

**[A: it.wikipedia]** The miniseries *Pupetta - Il coraggio e la passione*, with Manuela Arcuri, airs in 2013.

**[A: ilmattino.it]** From 2016 the journalist Giovanni Terzi records dozens of hours of interviews with Maresca at Castellammare di Stabia, later published as *Madame camorra* (Vallecchi).

**[A: ANSA; Fanpage]** Maresca dies at Castellammare di Stabia on **29 December 2021**, aged 86. Her funeral, set for the morning of **1 January 2022** at Sant'Antonio di Padova, is barred that day by the Prefect and Questore of Naples, who authorise only a blessing at the cemetery. About ten women go to pray anyway; her lawyers object publicly that calling her a *camorrista* is defamatory. The death and the ban are two dated events, not one, and an intermediate draft folded them together.

**[A: reportdifesa.it]** In 2023 the French author Philippe Vilain publishes a novel centred on Maresca, covering the meeting with Pascalone, the marriage, the murder and the revenge; it is presented at the Institut français de Naples.

## 10. Coordinates, and the atlas interlock

**Coordinates.** Eight drafted pins were wrong, two of them (Palma Campania, Corso Novara) carrying two and three mutually disagreeing values across the research lenses. Errors ran 0.6 to 3.6 km, enough to move Melito into Mugnano, Poggioreale past the cemetery complex, and Corso Novara off its own street. Corrected values are carried in the journey file: Palma Campania 40.8667/14.5500, Corso Novara 40.8557/14.2712, Melito 40.9167/14.2333, Poggioreale 40.8600/14.2789, Pagani 40.7499/14.6133, Castellammare 40.6952/14.4830, Nice 43.7102/7.2620, Tangier 35.7595/-5.8340. Correct as drafted: Marseille, Gibraltar, the Pompei sanctuary, Nola.

**Charles "Lucky" Luciano, Naples 1946-1962.** `charles_lucky_luciano.journey.json` puts Luciano ashore at the Port of Naples (40.8388, 14.2555) on 28 February 1946, in a bombed-out port city where the black market is the economy: the economy that is Simonetti's first racket. His shop on Via Tasso (40.834, 14.228) opens in 1948; police question him at the Questura (40.8395, 14.2497) on 9 June 1951; *soggiorno obbligato* confines him to Naples in November 1954, eight months before Simonetti is shot. **No source ties the two men to each other**, and the journey file states the crossing at that strength and no more. Under house style, three campa stops that had leaned on the Luciano crossing to carry the sentence (Port of Naples/1946, the Questura/1951, and Capodichino/1962) were rewritten under this cleanup pass to stand on Simonetti's own attested facts instead; the Via Tasso and *soggiorno obbligato* stops still note the adjacency at the strength described above.

**[R]** Vito Genovese, who visits Luciano in Havana (23.1136, -82.3666) on 22 December 1946, had run black-market and currency operations out of occupied Naples in 1943-44. Inferred adjacency, not a documented link.

**[R]** `mussolini.journey.json` supplies the contrast: the regime's 1920s campaign against the Sicilian mafia has **no recorded Camorra equivalent anywhere in the atlas**, consistent with the Camorra's resurgence under Allied occupation that produces Simonetti's power base.

**Canonical Naples pin.** 40.8518, 14.2681 is already shared by ten atlas travelers, Virgil, Giordano Bruno, Jacques de Molay and Luciano among them. Simonetti's Naples stops inherit it byte-identically where the stop is the city rather than a named street.

**Empty interlocks, reported as empty.** Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris and Buenos Aires were checked against this subject and **no connection was found**. None appears in the journey file; forcing them would be fabrication.

**Structural note.** Assunta Maresca has no journey file; she is a QUEUE.md entry with docs count 0, framed there as joining the atlas's organised-crime cluster from its Italian rather than its American side. Half of this file's later stops are hers, held here until she has her own.

## 11. Corrections applied to the verified pool

Errors carried by the research pass, corrected here and named so they are not re-imported.

- **Allum citation.** Credited to *Percy* Allum, "Camorristi and Politicians in Post-War Naples." It is **Felia** Allum's Brunel thesis, p. 370; the title conflated it with her 2006 book.
- **Smuggling ports.** Read as Simonetti's own route. Wikipedia assigns them to the Marseillais, Corsican and Sicilian groups.
- **"Prezzo presidente."** Not Italian. The term is *presidente dei prezzi*.
- **The year 1948** for his market ascendancy was invented; no source gives a year.
- **Maresca's birth** was given as 27 April 1935, the wedding date carried onto it. She was born 19 January 1935, so twenty at the marriage, not eighteen.
- **Wedding venue** given as Pompei in two entries and Naples in a third. It was Palma Campania; Pompei was a votive visit afterwards.
- **"Three months pregnant"** at the wedding is impossible against six months in October.
- **The Incurabili quotation** credited to napolitan.it. It is at internapoli.it, reproducing Gigi Di Fiore in *Il Mattino*.
- **Bar Grandone** was placed in Piazza Garibaldi. It stood on Corso Novara.
- **Maresca's first-instance sentence** was given as 18 years. It was 18 years and 6 months.
- **Death and funeral ban** folded into one date. They are 29 December 2021 and 1 January 2022.
- **The Rione Vasto** was tagged [R]. It is an administrative fact, [A].
- **Eight coordinates**, listed in section 10.
