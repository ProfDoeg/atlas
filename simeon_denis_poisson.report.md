# Siméon Denis Poisson (1781-1840) - research report

*Compiled 2026-08-29 for `simeon_denis_poisson.journey.json`. 44 stops, 9 segments.*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim at one remove from
the document. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Gaps are stated as gaps.

The difficulty is not scarcity but channel. Much of what is repeated about Poisson comes from
**François Arago's eulogy of 1850**: the nail and the swaddling bands, Vanneau, Billy, the
Palais-Royal dinner, the saying about what a life is good for, *grande aisance*. It reaches
modern accounts through summarisers who never opened Arago either, so it is [R]. The [A] spine
is narrower: MacTutor, the two Wikipedias, Kosmann-Schwarzbach's 2022 SIGMA survey.

---

## 1. Pithiviers and the failed trades (1781-1798)

- **Born at Pithiviers, 21 June 1781**, son of a former soldier turned local administrator whom
  the Revolution raised to the presidency of the district. **[A - MacTutor; en.wikipedia]**
  *Contradiction, unresolved:* **21 June** (MacTutor, en.wikipedia, after Arago) against
  **27 June** in a later secondary source; the file carries 21 June and records it. The **birth
  house is unlocated**.
- **The wet nurse and the nail. [R - Arago 1850, at one remove.]** Family tradition, no
  document; the nurse's house is unrecovered.
- **The Fontainebleau surgical apprenticeship failed**, poor manual coordination given as the
  reason. **[A - MacTutor]** **No year is fixed by any source reached**; the file's 1793 orders
  the segment. The variant in which a bled patient died is unverified lore.
- **1796, enrolled at the École centrale de Fontainebleau. [A - MacTutor, verbatim.]**
  *Correction to the pool:* it tagged **Vanneau and Billy [A] and cited MacTutor. MacTutor
  names neither.** Both trace only to Arago and are **[R]** here. The La Fontaine pun on the
  surname is fr.wikipedia's, as tradition. **[R]**

## 2. The École Polytechnique (1798-1806)

- **1798, entered the school**, reported to have placed first. **[A for entry - MacTutor,
  en.wikipedia; the placing is reported, not documented]** The collection said to have been
  taken up by classmates is **[R]**: no subscribers, no sum.
- **c. 1799-1800, memoir on the number of complete integrals of a finite-difference equation.
  [A - MacTutor: it "attracted Legendre's attention"]**
- *Correction to the pool:* it credited "Legendre **and Lacroix** ... in the **Recueil des
  savants étrangers**" to MacTutor. **MacTutor names Legendre alone and never mentions the
  Recueil.** Lacroix and the Recueil are Arago's; the file splits the attribution and marks the
  second half **[R]**. The 1799 memoir on Bézout's elimination is likewise **[R]**, folded into
  the 1800 stop.
- **1800, finished without the usual final examination, répétiteur in analysis on Laplace's
  recommendation; 1802, deputy professor; 1806, chair of mechanics**, vacated by Fourier's
  departure for Grenoble. **[A - MacTutor, en.wikipedia]**

## 3. Arcueil and the Laplacian programme (1806-1812)

- **Société d'Arcueil, 1806-1822**, Sundays at the neighbouring houses of Laplace and
  Berthollet, Poisson among the attendees with Biot, Malus, Thénard, Gay-Lussac, Arago and
  Humboldt. **[A - fr.wikipedia; `pierre_simon_laplace.journey.json`]**
- **1808, the first printed account of Fourier's heat theory, Bulletin de la Société
  Philomatique, signed "P". [R]** MacTutor corroborates only the general shape of his activity
  then, and **the Bulletin issue was not opened**. This matters: it is the hinge of the Fourier
  quarrel, and if the signature is not his the quarrel reads differently.
- **1808, attached to the Bureau des longitudes as astronomer. [A - MacTutor, en.wikipedia]**
  **The Bureau's own premises are not established** by anything reached; the Observatory, over
  which it held authority, is the best-attested site.
- **1809, the bracket expression**, in the memoir on the variation of arbitrary constants.
  **[A - Kosmann-Schwarzbach, p.11]**, which also establishes that "Poisson brackets" was **not
  standard until Whittaker's history of 1910**, that it was called simply an expression before
  that, and that one author in 1872 wrote of "Poisson's notation".
- **1809, professor of rational mechanics, Faculté des sciences de Paris [A - MacTutor,
  fr.wikipedia]; 1811, Traité de mécanique, 2 vols. [A - MacTutor]**
- **1812, elected to the physics section of the Institut** after an earlier failed candidacy.
  **[A - MacTutor, en.wikipedia]** **No month recorded**, so the order against the 9 March
  memoir is unknown; 1812-01-01 is an ordering device. **9 March 1812, the memoir on
  electricity over conducting surfaces. [A - MacTutor]** That this is the origin of the modern
  Poisson equation is a **synthesis**, not stated in those words by anything reached.

## 4. Heat, light, and the quarrels (1815-1819)

- **1815, Fourier complains to Laplace** that Poisson and Biot added nothing to his results
  while claiming a better exposition. **[R]** as to the letter, not opened; **[A]** as to the
  dispute itself. Modern historians give Fourier the theory and the series while allowing some
  of Poisson's technical criticisms. **The file does not resolve it in Poisson's favour.**
- **1815, examiner for the École Militaire (Saint-Cyr); 1816, examiner of the final
  examinations at the École Polytechnique in Lacroix's place, held until death. [A - MacTutor,
  en.wikipedia]** *Correction to the pool:* the geography lens flagged "1815 vs 1816" as an
  unresolved contradiction. **It is not one.** Both sources list two separate posts a year
  apart; the flag is dropped and the file carries two stops.
- **1817, married Nancy de Bardi**, an orphan born in England to émigré parents; four children.
  **[A - en.wikipedia, citing standard biographical dictionaries]** *Two gaps:* no source
  reached identifies the church or civil registry, and **no verified list of the children's
  names and dates was recovered**.
- **March 1818, Fellow of the Royal Society [A - MacTutor; en.wikipedia dates it to March]** and
  **1832, Copley Medal for the capillarity [A - MacTutor, en.wikipedia]**. **No evidence was
  found that Poisson ever travelled to England**; both are in absentia, so the Somerset House
  pin marks the conferring body and the file says so.
- **The diffraction prize.** Poisson sat on the commission with Laplace, Biot and Arago and
  deduced from Fresnel's own equations the bright point at the centre of a small disk's shadow;
  Arago tested it with a disk about 2 mm across and the spot appeared; Fresnel took the prize.
  **[A - MacTutor; en.wikipedia "Arago spot"]** Prior sightings by **Delisle (1715) and Maraldi
  (1723)** confirmed by the same article.
- *Correction to the pool:* it dated **Arago's experiment** to March 1819. Unsupported:
  en.wikipedia gives only "1819", and the timeline (entries due 1 August 1818, prize 1819) puts
  the experiment in the commission's deliberations of **late 1818**. **March 1819 is the award,
  not the experiment.**
- **The theatrical version is not carried:** Poisson declaring the result impossible and being
  humiliated is a later narrative of wave optics. The record supports only that he derived a
  consequence he took to be absurd, and that the algebra was right.

## 5. The offices of the state (1820-1827)

- **26 July 1820, councillor of the University [R - en.wikipedia]; from 1822, treasurer of the
  Royal Council of Public Instruction [R]**, corroborated only across MacTutor's account of his
  administrative posts and en.wikipedia's list of councillorships. The advocacy of mathematics
  for humanities students and of a unified agrégation is Arago's.
- **Foreign elections: Edinburgh 1820 [A - MacTutor]; American Academy 1822 and Royal Swedish
  Academy 1823 [A - en.wikipedia].** All in absentia. Rather than pin him in three cities he
  never entered, the file gives these one stop at the generic Paris pin and says why.
- **The barony**, offered and never taken up by letters patent. **[A as to the offer and the
  non-use]** *Contradiction, unresolved:* **1825** (en.wikipedia) vs **1821** (other
  biographical sources); the file uses 1825 and carries it. **No government office was
  identified.**
- **11 April 1827, succeeded Laplace as géomètre of the Bureau des longitudes. [R as to the day
  - en.wikipedia; MacTutor gives the succession without a day-date]**

## 6. Molecules, fibres, films (1827-1832)

- **The elastic constants and the ratio.** *Sur l'extension des fils et des plaques élastiques*
  is of **1827**, Annales de chimie et de physique; the elasticity work runs to 1831.
  **[A - Kosmann-Schwarzbach, pp.5-6]**
- *Contradiction, unresolved.* Kosmann-Schwarzbach says Poisson announced a universal value of
  **1/2**; the standard elasticity literature has the uni-constant theory giving **1/4**, the
  equal-Lamé-parameters case en.wikipedia still calls a "Poisson solid", 1/2 being the
  incompressible limit. A source-level error in a peer-reviewed survey, not a dossier
  invention. The file carries **both numbers**.
- *Correction to the pool:* it wrote that the ratio's variability "was only firmly established
  experimentally by engineers in the 1970s". The SIGMA text says the reverse: *until* the 1970s
  it **had only** been established experimentally, known empirically and ungrounded
  theoretically.
- **1831, Nouvelle théorie de l'action capillaire. [A - MacTutor]**
- **1829, Jacobi's first visit to Paris**, meeting Legendre, Fourier and Poisson.
  **[A - MacTutor, de.wikipedia, and `carl_gustav_jacob_jacobi.journey.json`, whose 1829-08-01
  date and pin the file matches byte-identically]** Month and day unrecorded.

## 7. Galois, the July Monarchy, the Chamber (1830-1837)

- **1830, the Palais-Royal dinner arranged by Arago**, at which Louis-Philippe recognised him
  publicly. **[R - Arago's 1850 notice, a friend's retrospective anecdote]** No month or day.
- **17 January 1831, Galois submits the memoir on solvability by radicals**, written at
  Poisson's own urging; Lacroix and Poisson named referees. **[A - MacTutor, en.wikipedia, and
  `evariste_galois.journey.json`, whose date and pin the file matches byte-identically]**
- *A guard against a false interlock:* the two earlier lost Galois submissions went to **Cauchy
  (1829) and Fourier (1830), not Poisson**, as `evariste_galois.journey.json` confirms
  independently. No Poisson stop is staged for them.
- **4 July 1831, the Lacroix-Poisson report:** the argument "neither sufficiently clear nor
  sufficiently developed to allow us to judge its rigour", with a recommendation that Galois
  publish the whole work. **[A as to substance - en.wikipedia citing R. Taton 1947; MacTutor]**
  **The exact date is [R]**, from the dossier, flagged unverified there and in
  `evariste_galois.journey.json`. **The legend in which Poisson calls the memoir
  incomprehensible and rejects a genius is not supported by the report**; Ehrhardt notes that
  many Academy submissions got none at all, so an adverse one was engagement.
- **1835, Théorie mathématique de la chaleur, with an 1837 Supplément. [R]** Dossier
  bibliography via MacTutor; neither volume opened.
- **1837, Recherches sur la probabilité des jugements. [A - MacTutor]** The limiting rare-event
  law **had already appeared in an 1829 memoir on the proportion of newborn girls and boys**,
  printed in 1830, and reappears at p.206 of the 1837 book. **[A - Kosmann-Schwarzbach,
  pp.6-7]** **De Moivre reached related limiting mathematics earlier**, so "discovered by
  Poisson" is conventional attribution.
- **1837, peer of France and member of the Royal Council of the University. [A - MacTutor,
  en.wikipedia]** No month for either, so their order within 1837 is unknown.

## 8. Sceaux and the grave (1839-1840)

- **1839, Recherches sur le mouvement des projectiles dans l'air. [R]** Bibliography alongside
  MacTutor's account of the final years; volume not opened. The projected **Traité de physique
  mathématique** is known only as an unfinished plan.
- **November 1839, Cauchy elected to the Bureau des longitudes and not seated** for refusing
  the oath. **[A - en.wikipedia, MacTutor]** That **Biot and Arago backed him and Poisson
  opposed** comes through the dossier's citation of a Bureau historical project which
  `augustin_louis_cauchy.journey.json` also carries **without having opened it directly**; the
  caution is inherited, not upgraded.
- **25 April 1840, died at Sceaux, aged 58. [A - MacTutor, fr.wikipedia]** **No source reached
  gives a medical cause**, and no well-supported evidence points to addiction, psychiatric
  crisis, suicide, accident or violence. Stated as a gap, not filled.
- **The estate inventory opened the same day in the minutes of the notary Hippolyte Lejeune
  [R - the French archival notice, via the dossier]** does **not** enumerate the assets.
  Arago's *grande aisance* is second-hand; no basis was found for calling him impoverished or
  wealthy.
- **Buried at Père-Lachaise, division 19**, sepulchral chapel on the chemin Camille Jordan,
  line 3; inscription naming him peer of France and member of the Institut. **[A - fr.wikipedia;
  Wikimedia Commons]** **Present condition unknown**: photographs of 2004 and 2016 show it
  standing, nothing since.
- *Two dossier claims corrected.* (1) **Base Mérimée PA00086780 does not designate the tomb:**
  the notice identifies the whole cemetery, and individually protected tombs carry their own
  acts, Poisson's not among them. (2) **An 1839 issue of Marty's "Les principaux monuments
  funéraires du Père-Lachaise" cannot illustrate the grave of a man who died in April 1840**;
  the work appeared in fascicles and any Poisson plate postdates the death.

## 9. The name afterwards

- **The Pithiviers statue**, lifesize, installed **ten years after his death**, the brass
  **melted down during the German occupation in the Second World War**, leaving only the square.
  **[A - Kosmann-Schwarzbach, p.1, near-verbatim]** **The dossier's sculptor "Deligand", founder
  "Charnot" and date 15 June 1851 could not be confirmed and are not carried**; the file's 1851
  is an ordering date.
- **Rue Denis-Poisson, Paris 17e, named 1864 [A - fr.wikipedia]**, proposer not recorded ·
  **Eiffel Tower frieze, POISSON at position 17, southeast face, 1889 [A - en.wikipedia]** ·
  **lunar crater "Poisson" (IAU 1935) and asteroid (12874) Poisson [A - fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Not staged as a stop:** the 2014 exhibition at the Université Pierre-et-Marie-Curie, then at
  Illinois and Berkeley **[A - en.wikipedia]**. No travel by Poisson.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Poisson is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. These were **kept, not silently deleted**, and
are flagged with their direction so the operator can rule. All four are **forward** reaches:
reception of his own results by people who came after him. None is a framework of his applied
to material he never treated, and none stages another person's travel as a Poisson scene. Each
is **one or two sentences closing a stop otherwise entirely his own**, at his own pin, the
scope the `EXCEPTIONS.md` forward grants use.

| direction | stop | what reaches forward | traveler named |
|---|---|---|---|
| **forward** | "the brackets of 1809" | Whittaker fixes the name 1910; **Dirac** 1925 finds quantum quantities obey the same algebra | **`dirac`** |
| **forward** | "Jacobi comes from Königsberg" (1829) | **Jacobi** in 1850 rediscovers the theorem in the brackets, "truly prodigious" | **`carl_gustav_jacob_jacobi`**; the stop itself is an in-life meeting, licensed on its own |
| **forward** | "the ratio" (1828) | **Lakes**, 1987, a foam with negative Poisson ratio | no |
| **forward** | "the probability of judgments" (1837) | **Bortkiewicz**, 1898, horse-kick counts | no |

Only the first puts a later atlas subject's name in a campa on purely posthumous grounds. It
survives because the prompt licenses naming a traveler whom the subject **influenced**, and
because `dirac.journey.json` already names the Poisson bracket. **On a strict reading of the
block rule (an 1840 file may not embed a 1925 hash), that is the sentence to cut**; the
Whittaker clause alone carries the stop. Nothing was written into any other file.
