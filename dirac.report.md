# Paul Dirac (1902-1984): research report

*2026-08-02. Atlas of Journeys, slug `dirac`. Companion file: `dirac.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim whose primary source could not be reached. Contradictions are recorded where found and **not adjudicated**.

Dirac is a well-documented subject with a thin *quotable* record: the chronology is solid nearly everywhere, the geography arrived with a large crop of bad coordinates, and the quotations are almost entirely [R]. Verification corrected fourteen coordinates, two dates, one place, one reversed sequence and five quotation texts, all listed below.

---

## 1. Bristol, 1902-1923

- **Birth**, 8 August 1902, 15 Monk Road, Bishopston, second child of Charles Dirac, a Swiss teacher of French from Saint-Maurice in the Valais, and Florence Holten **[A: Wikipedia; Farmelo, *The Strangest Man* (2009), ch. 1]**. *Coordinate corrected* from 51.4699, -2.5905 (~1.1 km off) to **51.4795, -2.5949** **[A: Nominatim]**.
- **The French rule**: the children had to address their father in French, and Dirac attributed his reticence to it **[A: Farmelo, ch. 1]**. Farmelo's fuller domestic account is outside the verified pool and is not asserted in the campa.
- **Bishop Road Primary School [A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. The year is a **gap**. The pool's "1910" was rejected as unsupported: Wikipedia names the school with no year, and the pool's own geography lens dated the attendance 1907-1914. The file uses `1907-09-01`, hedged in `date_confidence` **[R]**. *Coordinate corrected* 51.472, -2.591 to **51.4804, -2.5939**.
- **Merchant Venturers' Technical College** from 1914, his father teaching French in the same building; science and technical drawing, no Latin or Greek **[A: MacTutor; Nobel biographical note]**. *Coordinate corrected* to **51.4534, -2.6005** (~330 m error).
- **Naturalisation** as British subjects, 22 October 1919 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **University of Bristol**: first-class B.Sc. in electrical engineering 1921, first-class B.A. in mathematics 1923, no engineering work in the slump, mathematics taken free of charge **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. Coordinate 51.4571, -2.6037 unchallenged.
- **Flagged [R]**: the campa line that engineering taught him every equation is an approximation. His own repeated account, but uncited in the pool.

## 2. Cambridge, 1923-1926

- **St John's College** on the DSIR scholarship under Ralph Fowler, 1923; the 1851 Research Fellowship from 1925 **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- **Heisenberg's proof**, September 1925 **[A: Wikipedia]**. The Sunday-walk-and-Poisson-bracket detail in the campa is the standard account and is **[R]**: the pool attests the proof, not the walk.
- **Ph.D., June 1926**, thesis titled *Quantum Mechanics*, the first on the subject accepted anywhere **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Cavendish colloquia** as a research student, 1923-1926 **[A: MacTutor]**.

**Coordinate contradiction, unresolved and deliberately inherited.** Three figures for the Cavendish: pool geography lens 52.2043, 0.1192 (Free School Lane, correct on the map); `robert_oppenheimer.journey.json` **52.2018, 0.0919**, about 1.9 km west; corpus Cambridge pin 52.2039, 0.1187. Atlas convention inherits interlock pins byte-identical so paths converge, so the file carries **52.2018, 0.0919** with the discrepancy in `date_confidence`. A known bad geographic coordinate held for corpus consistency; correct it whenever the Oppenheimer file is corrected.

**Mutual gaze.** Oppenheimer was at the Cavendish under Thomson and Rutherford in 1925, the year Dirac was a research student there **[A: `robert_oppenheimer.journey.json`]**. That file names the Cavendish and Rutherford but **not Dirac**, so co-presence is documented and acquaintance is not. The campa says so.

## 3. The continental year, 1926-1927

- **Copenhagen**, fellowship at Bohr's institute, transformation theory unifying matrix and wave mechanics **[A: MacTutor; Farmelo]**.
- *Place and date corrected.* The pool's chronology put this work at Cambridge in 1926. Both halves fail: it was done in Copenhagen (the pool's own geography lens says so), and the paper was received 2 December 1926 and published in *Proc. R. Soc. A* in 1927.
- *Coordinate contradiction, inherited.* Blegdamsvej 17: pool 55.6997, 12.5654, verified to 55.6969, 12.5717 (Wikipedia); `niels_bohr.journey.json` **55.6989, 12.5658**, which the file carries, the ~500 m gap noted.
- **Göttingen, February 1927**: Born, Franck, Tamm, Oppenheimer **[A: MacTutor]**. Pool 51.5335, 9.9354 flagged ~680 m off, 51.5277, 9.9323 proposed, `robert_oppenheimer.journey.json` **51.5413, 9.9158** inherited. Three figures, no agreement.
- **Date conflict.** The Oppenheimer file has Dirac in the Göttingen seminars with Pauli, Fermi and Teller **[A]** but dates it autumn 1926; MacTutor dates the stay to February 1927. **Flagged, not resolved**; the file follows MacTutor.
- **Leiden**, several weeks with Ehrenfest, 1927 **[A: MacTutor]**. No month.
- **Como, September 1927**: the Volta centenary congress is documented, **Dirac's attendance is not [R]**. Kept as an [R] stop with the inference stated in the campa. *Coordinate corrected* 45.8081, 9.0852 to **45.8189, 9.0654** (Villa Olmo).
- **Fifth Solvay Conference, Brussels, 24-29 October 1927 [A: Wikipedia; `einstein.journey.json`]**. Canonical pin **50.8466, 4.3528** inherited from the Einstein file, which names Dirac among the attendees; the pool's 50.8393, 4.3777 is set aside.

## 4. The equation and the circumnavigation, 1928-1931

- **The Dirac equation, 1928 [A]**; **the positron and monopole paper, 1931 [A: Wikipedia]**. Anderson's 1932 confirmation is indexed in the corpus but narrated by no corpus file **[A: QUEUE.md; census]**.
- **FRS 1930 [A]**; **The Principles of Quantum Mechanics, 1930 [A]**. The Oxford pin 51.7548, -1.2544 is the publisher's address, standing in for an event with no site.
- **Wisconsin, spring 1929 [A]**. *Coordinate corrected* 43.0722, -89.4008 (downtown Madison) to **43.0803, -89.4310** (campus). **Ann Arbor 1929 [A: MacTutor]** is attested, cut as a stop for length, named inside the Wisconsin campa.
- **Yellowstone: dropped.** The pool's Yellowstone stop carried the dancing anecdote. Verification found the anecdote misplaced and misworded, belonging to the Pacific voyage to Japan, and Dirac's presence in Yellowstone unestablished by any source consulted.
- *Quotation corrected.* The pool's "shake hands with a woman without the trouble of steps" is not the recorded wording. Documented exchange: Dirac asks why Heisenberg dances, Heisenberg says it is a pleasure when there are nice girls, Dirac replies **"But how do you know beforehand that the girls are nice?"** **[R: Heisenberg's recollection, placed on the summer 1929 voyage by the Japanese Wikipedia article; the primary memoir passage was not reached]**.
- **Port of embarkation: a gap.** San Francisco stands for the West Coast crossing point, no pier named **[R: Farmelo, ch. 11]**.
- **Japan, 1929 [A: MacTutor; Farmelo]**. Yokohama and Tokyo merged for length. The pool's Tokyo coordinate, 35.7128, 139.762, falls in the northeast of the city and reads as a transposed figure; not used.
- **Trans-Siberian return via Moscow, 1929 [A: MacTutor]**. **Sixth Solvay, October 1930 [A]**, Brussels pin reused.

## 5. The Lucasian years, 1932-1939

- **Lucasian Chair taken up 1 October 1932, held thirty-seven years [A: Wikipedia, "Lucasian Professor of Mathematics"]**. *Age corrected*: the pool said "at twenty-nine"; he turned 30 on 8 August 1932.
- **Interlock pin.** The corpus fixes Cambridge at **52.2039, 0.1187**, dated 1932-10-01 for the Lucasian chair, and it is used for all generic Cambridge stops in preference to the pool's Senate House 52.2043, 0.1183.
- **Mutual gaze [R].** Bohr's 1911 Carlsberg fellowship and Oppenheimer's 1925 Cavendish stint put both men in Dirac's eventual home town at other times. Neither file names Dirac there; the overlap is of place only, and the campa says so.
- **Nobel Prize, Stockholm, 10 December 1933**, shared with Schrödinger, Heisenberg receiving the deferred 1932 prize the same day **[A: Nobel ceremony record; `heisenberg.journey.json`]**. Canonical pin **59.333, 18.064** inherited; the pool's 59.3339, 18.0632 is ~80 m away and equally defensible. *Age corrected in the campa*: the draft said two of the three were thirty-one. Dirac was thirty-one on the day; Heisenberg, born 5 December 1901, had turned thirty-two five days earlier; Schrödinger was forty-six **[A: birth dates, Wikipedia]**.
- **Contradiction flagged.** The pool attached here the story that Dirac considered declining the prize until told refusal would draw more attention. The **cited source does not support it**: the ceremony record says nothing of the kind, and the story is usually credited to Rutherford without a traceable citation. Kept in the campa as canon, but **[R]**.
- **Princeton, 1934-1935 [A: MacTutor]**. *Building corrected*: the IAS then occupied the 1931 Fine Hall, now Jones Hall (~40.3487, -74.6588); the present Fine Hall tower dates from 1969, and the pool's figure matches neither.
- **Meeting Margit Wigner at the Annex, Nassau Street, 1934 [A: Wikipedia, citing Farmelo]**. The corrected Fine Hall pin and the Annex pin sit ~40 m apart; both kept.
- **Marriage, London, January 1937 [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no venue, no day, stated in `date_confidence`.
- **Children.** Stepchildren Judith and Gabriel **[A]**. Daughters Mary Elizabeth and Florence Monica: **birth years not given** in the fetched sources **[R]**. Mary Elizabeth died 20 January 2007 **[A]**.
- **Soviet visits** in 1928, 1929, 1930, 1932, 1933, 1935, 1936, 1937 **[A: MacTutor]**. *Anachronism corrected*: the pool attached a 1928-1937 range to the Institute for Physical Problems, founded only in the mid-1930s. The file anchors one Institute stop at 1937 and lists the years in the 1929 Moscow campa. *Coordinate corrected* 55.7033, 37.5822 to **55.7091, 37.5767**; the rejected latitude duplicated the pool's Moscow State University figure, suggesting a copy error upstream.
- **Cut as unverifiable:** Mount Elbrus and Yalta, both **[R]** with no specific Dirac citation, Yalta explicitly "traditional rather than documented".
- **Large numbers hypothesis 1937 [A]**; **bra-ket notation 1939 [A]**; **Royal Medal 1939 [A]**.

## 8. Afterlife

**Burial, Roselawn Cemetery.** Three coordinates for one cemetery appear in the pool and **contradict each other**: geography lens 30.4166, -84.2445; afterlife lens 30.438, -84.2782; Nominatim **30.4866, -84.2662**. The spread reaches about 8 km; the pool contradicts itself. The file uses the geocoded figure and states the disagreement in `date_confidence`. **The plot was not confirmed by any source consulted.** *Order corrected*: the pool said he was "buried beside the wife he later joins there", inverting it. - **ICTP Dirac Medal**, Trieste, annually on 8 August since 1985 **[A]**; *coordinate corrected* 45.6495, 13.7768 to **45.7037, 13.7186** (Miramare). The **Institute of Physics Paul Dirac Medal** (Hawking, Bell, Penrose) **[A]** has no fixed site and is folded into the same campa.

## 9. Quotations: an honest accounting

**Every quotation in this dossier is [R].** No primary text could be fetched: nobelprize.org 403, britannica.com 403, the May 1963 *Scientific American* article unretrievable, archive.org's *Physics and Beyond* restricted, the 1984 paper not located. Verification worked from Wikiquote, Kragh, Farmelo and OpenLibrary.

1. **Einstein to Ehrenfest, 23 August 1926.** The pool carried Wikiquote's "I have trouble with Dirac", which names no translator and no page. The file uses Kragh's translation, **"I am toiling over Dirac. This balancing on the dizzying path between genius and madness is awful,"** *Dirac: A Scientific Biography* (CUP, 1990), p. 82. Two English versions of one German letter exist; Kragh's has a page and a named translator. **Flagged, not adjudicated.**
2. **Pauli at Solvay, 1927.** The pool inserted a first name Pauli did not use. Corrected to the authorized translation: **"Well, our friend Dirac, too, has a religion, and its guiding principle is 'God does not exist and Dirac is His prophet.'"** *Physics and Beyond* (1971), **p. 87**, one page not pp. 85-87. Farmelo, p. 138, gives it without "Paul" and notes it recycles an 1850s *Punch* joke.
3. **Einstein on the "most logically perfect presentation".** The pool quoted a compressed rendering traceable to Pais, *Subtle Is the Lord* (1982), p. 441. The file quotes Einstein's own sentence, from *James Clerk Maxwell: A Commemoration Volume 1831-1931* (CUP, 1931), pp. 72-73.
4. **Feynman.** The pool spliced out "He made a breakthrough, a new method of doing physics" and truncated before "and to try to interpret it afterwards", removing his point. Restored in full. *Date corrected*: the **1986** Dirac Memorial Lectures, published 1987.
5. **The dancing exchange.** See section 4.

Used with no correction available and no primary confirmation, all **[R]**: "never to start a sentence without knowing the end of it" (attributed reply to Bohr); "Physical laws should have mathematical beauty" (Moscow blackboard); the science-and-poetry remark on Oppenheimer, whose **two competing wordings** (Wikipedia's, and Jungk, *Brighter Than a Thousand Suns* (1958), p. 22) **are not adjudicated**; "getting beauty in one's equations" (*Scientific American*, May 1963); "very dissatisfied with the situation" (1975, no citation detail); "searching for a Hamiltonian" (1984 paper); "the strangest man who ever visited my institute" (Bohr via Gottfried 2010 and Farmelo's title). Unused: "God is a mathematician of a very high order..." Twelve of the forty-five stops carry a quote.
