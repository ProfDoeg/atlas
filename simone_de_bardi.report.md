# Simone de' Bardi: research report

*2026-08-28. Subject: **Mone (Simone) di Geri di Ricco di Bardo de' Bardi**, Florentine magnate knight, c.1260 to after 1315 (possibly 1321, possibly 1330). Husband of Bice di Folco Portinari, the Beatrice of Dante's Vita Nova and Commedia.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or reported-but-unverified. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Inaccessible sources are named with the reason.

---

## 0. What kind of subject this is

Simone de' Bardi wrote nothing **[A: D'Addario, Enciclopedia Dantesca]**. No letter, speech, deposition, poem, or account book in his hand or voice survives or is known to have existed. The whole corpus attached to his name consists of instruments drafted by other men about his property and his obligations: conveyances, sureties, procurations, appointments to office, and one will that is not his own.

Two consequences. First, nearly every biographical claim passes through **Isidoro Del Lungo**, *Beatrice nella vita e nella poesia del secolo XIII* (Hoepli, Milan, 1891) **[A: Commons scan, publisher/year/pages confirmed]**, and through **Arnaldo D'Addario**'s Enciclopedia Dantesca notice of 1970, which summarizes him. Second, a 2024-2026 wave of claims by **Domenico Savini** (with Samuele Masi) is reshaping the popular account and none of it is critically published. The two strata are kept separate.

---

## 1. The identification problem

The Bardi produced many contemporaneous Simoni: **Simone di Nello di Simone, Simone di Giuliano di Ricco, Simone del fu Cino di messer Iacopo, Simone di messer Iacopo** **[A: D'Addario 1970, summarizing Del Lungo]**. Del Lungo separates "our" Simone by the consistent attachment of the honorific **dominus / messer**. The dispute, with one correction the verification pass caught:

- Del Lungo **first** assigned the biography to **Simone di Iacopo**, then revised to **Simone di Geri** on the *dominus* criterion.
- **Randi** argued for Simone di Giuliano; **Passerini** conflated several Simoni.
- **Scartazzini did NOT dissent.** D'Addario: "Lo Scartazzini cita come marito della Portinari Simone di Geri". The gathering pass listed him among dissenters; wrong, corrected here.

Consequence: **every date in this file is conditional on the attribution holding.** The 1329 San Miniato entry (§6) is where it visibly strains.

## 2. Origins and the first family, to 1280

- **Birth: no record** **[R: D'Addario 1970, stating the inference as an inference]**. c.1260 is worked backward from adult property acts in 1280 and reported widowerhood. Month and day unknowable.
- **Father Geri di Ricco di Bardo, dead by February 1281** **[R]**. Attested only by implication: the 1281 conveyance styles Simone "del fu Geri". Source is the Savini/Masi transcription of the Strozzi copy, not a critical edition.
- **Brother Cecchino di Geri** **[A: D'Addario]**. Co-purchaser 1280, buyer 1281, procurator 1305; the most persistent other person in the file.
- **First wife Gemma dei Pulci; daughter Bartola** **[R]**. Death reported 25 September 1280 by Savini and Masi from ASFi, Notarile Antecosimiano, Bonaiuto di Nolfo, vol. 2950. **Not corroborated**; that article carries corrupt dates elsewhere ("1235", "1203").
- **Residence: Via de' Bardi, Oltrarno, near Santa Lucia dei Magnoli** **[A for the family, R for Simone's own house]**. Italian scholarship: the houses stood "sulla via de' Bardi in Oltrarno, in particolare a palazzo Canigiani, originariamente palazzo de' Bardi". No deed names his house.

**Coordinate correction.** The gathering pass used 43.7669 / 11.2607 for Santa Lucia dei Magnoli in four items; that lands near Piazza dei Mozzi, 450 m east. The church is at **43.7655, 11.2568**, which the journey file uses.

## 3. The al Buco documents and the marriage, 1280-1281

- **17 June 1280:** Simone and Cecchino buy land and a house from their cousin Bartolo at **al Buco**, parish of San Lorenzo, pieve of Ripoli **[R]**.
- **26 February 1280 Florentine style = 26 February 1281 modern:** Simone sells that property on to Cecchino **with the consent of his wife Bice**. Notary **Giunta di Spigliato di Brunetto** of Santa Lucia dei Magnoli; witnesses **Gualterotto and Ruggero, sons of the late Rosso**, and **Maffeo di Tommasino di Gualterotto de' Bardi** **[R]**.

**Tag dispute, recorded not resolved.** The gathering pass tagged the 1281 act **[A]**; the verification pass downgraded it to **[R]**: the original is lost, the evidence is a c.1635 Carlo Strozzi transcription (BNCF, Magliabechiana cl. XXV n. 591), and the only publication is a non-peer-reviewed 2026 news article. **The downgrade is followed.** Content was confirmed verbatim against that transcription, so the *reading* is sound and the *evidentiary status* weak. The folio "p.310" is not in the article and is dropped; the article's date is **17 July 2026**, not 25 August; and 2026 is not the rediscovery moment, Savini having presented the material at **Villa Bertelli, Forte dei Marmi, 1 March 2024** **[A: event page]**.

**Marriage date: contradictory, left open.** Zanoboni's DBI has "sembra debba risalire al **1280 almeno**" **[A]**; Savini and Masi argue a narrow **January-February 1281** window after Gemma's reported death of 25 September 1280 **[R]**. Incompatible if Gemma really died that September. The journey file dates the stop 1280 to order the segment and states the conflict in `date_confidence`. **The only firm floor is 15 January 1288** (Folco's will).

**Quoted:** "l'unico atto esistente in cui compare in prima persona Beatrice Portinari" **[A: adhocnews.it, 17 July 2026, verbatim]**.

## 4. The marriage documented, and the offices, 1288-1290

- **1288: podestà of Volterra** **[A: D'Addario]**. Year only.
- **15 January 1288: Folco Portinari's testament**, rogated by ser **Tedaldo di Orlando Rustichelli**, leaves his daughter Bice, "wife of dominus Simon de Bardis", fifty lire in small florins beyond the dowry already given; unmarried sisters get about 800 lire each **[A]**. The most secure document in the file. It does not name Simone's father.
- **1290: captain of the people at Prato** **[A: D'Addario]**. Year only.

### Quote attributions, corrected

The gathering pass attributed four early quotations to a Treccani entry "Bardi, Simone"; the verification pass reports **that entry does not exist**. **Unresolved internal contradiction in the research pool**: other verified items quote that same page with confirmed Italian text, so it *is* fetchable and *does* carry the biography. **Flagged, not resolved.** In the journey file, quotations are cited to the sources the verification pass identified; unquoted facts to D'Addario.

| Quotation (incipit) | Corrected source |
|---|---|
| "Item domine Bici etiam filie sue, et uxori domini Simonis de Bardis..." | Del Lungo 1891, appendix (ASFi, Diplomatico, R. Acquisto Manni, 1288 gennaio 15); also Toynbee **[A]** |
| "e fu moglie d'un cavaliere de' Bardi, chiamato messer Simone" | Boccaccio, *Esposizioni sopra la Comedia*, literal exposition of Inferno II, 1373-74 **[A]** |
| "era stata anima nobile di mona Biatrice figliuola cheffu..." | chiosa to Inf. II, MS Firenze BNCF II.I.39, printed by Rocca, *Giornale dantesco* XI (1903) **[A]** |
| "ipsa domina erat anima generose domine Beatrice condam domini..." | Graziolo de' Bambaglioli, Latin commentary on Inf. II, 1324, quoted in Curtius **[A]** |

Two further corrections: (a) the Folco clause's orthography is medieval (`domine / filie / sue`, lowercase `l` = 50), not the classicized form the gathering pass gave; (b) the English Wikipedia transcription is **corrupt** (`uxoris` for `uxori`, `reliquite` for `reliquit`) and is **dropped**, not offered as corroboration.

**Bambaglioli / Lancia attribution: unresolved.** The gathering pass claimed an Andrea Lancia autograph gloss (c.1341-43), citing D'Addario, who does not name Lancia; the verification pass nonetheless reports that current scholarship assigns the *volgarizzamento* chiosa to Lancia, 1341-43, not to Bambaglioli. Lancia may be right about the text and wrong about the citation. **Left open**: the journey file quotes the chiosa from Rocca 1903 and attributes it to the manuscript, not to a named commentator.

## 5. The death and burial of Bice, 1290

- **Death 1290, aged about twenty-four** **[A for the year]**. Cause, place, hour unrecorded.
- **Day contested, not resolved:** Zanoboni gives **19 June 1290**, Santagata calculates **8 June 1290**, `dante.journey.json` carries **1290-06-08**. The discrepancy is calendrical and textual, the date being encoded numerologically in the Vita Nova rather than recorded, and is not evidence of two deaths.
- **Simone's whereabouts and conduct at her death: nowhere recorded** **[A as a negative finding]**. He held the Prato captaincy that year; nothing connects the two.

**Burial: three rival sites, no evidence, no resolution.** (1) **Santa Margherita de' Cerchi**, the tourist tradition; Zanoboni calls the modern marker **unreliable**, Italian scholarship calls burial here "assai improbabile", since Folco and Monna Tessa lie there but a woman married into the Bardi belongs in the Bardi sepulchre **[R]**. (2) **Bardi ground at Santa Croce**, Savini 2008 **[R]**. (3) **Santa Lucia dei Magnoli or Santa Maria Sopr'Arno**, Santagata, on parish custom **[R]**. A plaque at Santa Maria Nuova is likewise flagged unreliable.

**Coordinate correction:** Santa Margherita de' Cerchi is at **43.7712, 11.2573**, not 11.2596. That is also the canonical `dante.journey.json` pin and is inherited byte-identical.

## 6. Surety, faction, property, and the last decade, 1293-1315

- **1293, Ordinances of Justice.** Simone's subjection is attested **indirectly**, through Del Lungo's finding that he stood surety for Andrea de' Mozzi "under the Ordinances" **[A: via D'Addario]**.
- **17 July 1295: guarantor of the peace between the Mannelli and Velluti** at **San Piero Scheraggio** **[A: D'Addario, summarizing Del Lungo]**.
- **1295: surety for the condottiere Inghiramo of the counts of Biserno** **[A, upgraded]**. Verbatim in D'Addario ("fu mallevadore nello stesso anno per il condottiero Inghiramo de' conti di Biserno"), contra the gathering pass. The Bargello as site remains inference.
- **1300: the Donati / Black Guelf conspiracy** **[R, the weakest consequential claim in the file]**. Santagata names Simone among conspirators planning outside armed intervention (Guido di Battifolle) after the May Day violence; plot discovered, Simone and Guido fined, Corso Donati condemned to death in absentia. **The judicial roll was not located**, the claim is **absent from D'Addario**, and the Corso Donati literature does not name Simone. Unresolved.
- **1304: the expedition against the castle of the Stinche**, a Cavalcanti possession in the Val di Greve **[A, upgraded]**, stated plainly in D'Addario. Distinction from the later Florentine prison confirmed: the prison, begun 1299 at what is now the Teatro Verdi, took its name from the castle when its 1304 captives became the first inmates. **Coordinate correction:** the castle stood south of Greve between Panzano, Lamole and Radda, around **43.545, 11.343**, not 43.575, 11.33 (Greve town).
- **1305: the Mozzi surety comes due.** Simone had stood surety for **Andrea, son of messer Tommaso de' Mozzi**, condemned for beating a popolano; he paid and sought recourse, appointing Cecchino procurator on **22 May 1305** to take possession of the assets ceded **[A, upgraded on both counts]**, both in D'Addario.
- **Moriano:** bought with other holdings from Vanni de' Mozzi **20 April 1306** **[A]**; **leased out some months later** **[A, upgraded]** ("altri possessi che alcuni mesi dopo dava in fitto"); **sold 7 December 1307 to the inquisitor of heretical depravity** **[A]**, who is named only by office.
- **1308, and again 1310: operations against Arezzo** **[A]**. Years only. **1310: captain of the people at Orvieto** **[A]**. D'Addario gives captaincy and campaign in the same year and does not reconcile them. **Flagged, not resolved.**
- **1313: among the Florentines firmly opposing Emperor Henry VII** **[A]**. The Palazzo della Signoria as site is inference.
- **1313: the marriage of Francesca** **[R, citation corrected]**. **Not in the Enciclopedia Dantesca.** The real source is **Savini** (Villa Bertelli, 1 March 2024: "un documento del 1313 che riguarda il matrimonio della figlia di Simone, Francesca"), who calls her Simone's **daughter**; the gathering pass's "or niece" hedge and its Cecchino mediation are **not in the source** and are dropped. Known children: **Bartola** (by Gemma), **Gemma** (married Ardingo de' Medici), **Francesca**. Zanoboni on the Beatrice marriage: "dal quale non risulta siano nati figli". Descent of any known child from Beatrice is **not established**.
- **29 August 1315: in arms at Montecatini** against Uguccione della Faggiola **[A: D'Addario; battle date corroborated]**. **His personal outcome is not recorded.** **Coordinate correction:** the gathering pass labelled the pin Montecatini Alto (43.8964, 10.7894) while giving Montecatini Terme on the plain (43.8802, 10.7735), where the battle was fought; coordinate kept, **label** corrected.

**Note on the upgrades.** The gathering pass repeatedly called material "not present in the Enciclopedia Dantesca text I fetched" that the verification pass then found there verbatim. Its negative findings about that source are discarded wholesale.

### The two endings, in direct contradiction

| Ending | Evidence | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| **Death 7 September 1321**, deathbed codicil dictated in a Florentine hospital under Bardi patronage, dead within days, a week before Dante's death at Ravenna | Savini and Masi, adhocnews 2026, whose table of contents promises "il codicillo e ultime volontà di Messer Simone di Geri de' Bardi" while **the text supplies no date, notary, hospital, or shelfmark**; no image or edition exists | **[R]** |
| **Alive to at least 1330**: "dominus Symon de Bardis" made captain of the people of San Miniato **18 December 1329**, six-month term from 10 February 1330 | ASFi, Provvisioni 25, fol. 88v, cited by Del Lungo, reported by D'Addario, who treats the attribution as **conditional** owing to homonymy | **[R]** |

**Not resolved, and it should not be.** The 1321 date is symbolically loaded, warranting suspicion until the codicil is published with a shelfmark; the 1329 entry is documentarily real but may name a different Bardi. Del Lungo believed the documented life ended after 1315.

**No death date, no burial place, no tomb for Simone is recorded anywhere** **[A: negative finding, in D'Addario and in the UVa / IATH Florentine Sepoltuario, whose entry for him carries genealogy and a manuscript reference (ins. 593/II, 23) and no burial data]**.

## 7. Myths flagged and kept out of the journey file

1. **"Simone the banker."** The Compagnia dei Bardi's greatness is documented; **Simone's personal role in it is attested nowhere**, not as partner, factor, or account-holder **[A as a gap]**. **Coordinate correction:** the gathering pass sited the company at the Mercato Vecchio (43.7707, 11.2545), which no source supports; the Bardi were an Oltrarno house and the journey file sites the stop at 43.7658, 11.2578.
2. **"The obstructive husband."** Earliest mass staging: **Caserini's 1913 silent film *Dante e Beatrice*** (S.A. Ambrosio, 50 min; restored 2007), which invents a reciprocal-love ending with Beatrice dying in Dante's arms **[R as legend, A as film fact]**; it recurs in Elijah Guo's 2015 play *Beatrice* (Producers' Club, New York) **[A]**. Reception, not biography; neither is staged as a stop (see §8).
3. **"Simone the chapel patron."** Giotto's Bardi Chapel frescoes at Santa Croce are family patronage a generation after Beatrice's death; nothing attributes them to Simone. Carried only as a `suggested_ref`.
4. **"Dante and Simone."** **No document records any meeting, correspondence, dispute, or relationship between them**, and Dante never names Simone or the Bardi in the Vita Nova or the Commedia **[A: negative finding, D'Addario and Santagata both noting the silence]**. The journey file states the silence rather than filling it.

## 8. Apparatus relations for the operator

Simone de' Bardi is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. These relations surfaced in research, are **not rooted in his own life**, and were kept out of the campas rather than silently deleted. All are **forward** in direction, which `EXCEPTIONS.md` does not license in general and never by an agent's judgement.

1. **`cosimo_de_medici`, FORWARD.** Cosimo married Contessina de' Bardi in 1415 into Simone's own consorteria, at Via de' Bardi; that file already narrates the Bardi bank's rise and 1345 collapse. House link direct, person roughly a century later. **Not named in any campa**; the company and its 1345 failure appear as the family's own history.
2. **`dante_gabriel_rossetti`, FORWARD.** Rossetti painted Beatrice for forty years under four faces: his own engagement with Simone's wife, belonging on his file. **Not named, not staged.**
3. **`saint_francis` / `al_kamil`, FORWARD and non-personal.** Both already use "Giotto, The Trial by Fire before the Sultan, Bardi Chapel, Santa Croce" as a suggested_ref. The chapel is Simone's family's, the frescoes are not his. Carried as a `suggested_ref` only, neither traveler named.
4. **Del Lungo, D'Addario, Zanoboni, Savini, Caserini, Guo.** Modern engagements with the record; none has a journey file, all carried as `sources` / `suggested_refs` on Simone's own stops. Del Lungo's Milan pin, Savini's Forte dei Marmi pin, Caserini's Milan pin and the New York theatre pin were **dropped as stops** for exactly this reason.

**Gap worth naming, not apparatus:** **Corso Donati** has no atlas file, though he is the connective tissue between Simone's reconstructed 1300 conspiracy and Dante's exile and is named repeatedly across `dante.journey.json`.

## 9. Interlocks used, and canonical pins

Only **`dante`** is named in a campa, on three grounds, each a real relation rather than shared geography: Dante wrote the book that is the only reason Simone is remembered, about Simone's own wife (one-directional but real); the two were contemporaries on opposite sides of the 1300 factional quarrel, if Santagata's attribution holds; and they took opposite sides on Henry VII in 1313.

Canonical pins inherited byte-identical: the Portinari quarter and Santa Margherita de' Cerchi at `43.7712, 11.2573`, from `dante.journey.json`, "Florence, the death of Beatrice"; Santa Croce at `43.7686, 11.2626`, from the `al_kamil` / `saint_francis` suggested_refs.

`cosimo_de_medici.journey.json`'s Via de' Bardi pin (43.7674, 11.2593) was **not** inherited: it sites a fifteenth-century marriage, whereas this file's Oltrarno stops sit on the corrected Santa Lucia dei Magnoli coordinate (43.7655, 11.2568) and the Via de' Bardi houses (43.7658, 11.2578).

## 10. Honest gaps

No birth date, death date, burial, tomb, portrait, description, or age at any event. No writing of any kind, so no editions and no translations. No word of his about his wife, her death, her poet, or anything else. The dowry figure is unrecorded, only that it was paid before January 1288. **al Buco cannot be located**, the pieve of Ripoli having held sixteen suffragan churches. **Moriano's exact site is not established**; the coordinate is approximate. **The 1300 judicial roll was not located.** **The 1321 codicil has no published shelfmark, notary, hospital, image, or edition.** **Whether the 1329 San Miniato appointment names the same man is unanswerable**, by D'Addario's own admission.
