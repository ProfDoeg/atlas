# Las Valientes de Potosí — research report

## Sources
The canon is **one chronicle**: Bartolomé Arzáns de Orsúa y Vela, *Historia de la Villa Imperial de Potosí* (written 1705–1736), año 1653, the chapter "En que se cuentan los hechos de dos doncellas nobles naturales de esta Villa, y lo demás que sucedió durante el corto gobierno de don Luis Pimentel." I recovered most of the chapter **verbatim** via full-text search inside the Internet Archive copy of the Hanke–Mendoza edition, vol. II (`archive.org/details/historiadelavill02arzn`, pp. 169–175) — including the recorded dialogue of the duel. Secondary: Mónica Navia Antezana, "Las damas travestidas de Charcas," *Estudios Bolivianos* 27 (2017), which quotes and paginates the episode; Padden's *Tales of Potosí* (Brown UP, 1975) contains the English translation; Badass of the Week / Rejected Princesses carry the modern folk layer (Ana as street orphan, the goring). Arzáns himself names four streams: Juan Pasquier's lost eyewitness *Historia de Potosí*, three venerable elders, don Juan de Itulaín (met in Chayanta), and the negro fiel's tales relayed through Diego Melgarejo.

## Judgment calls
- **Calendar: the brief said julian; I wrote `gregorian`.** Spanish America used the Gregorian calendar from 1582; every date in Arzáns is Gregorian. Flip the field if the pipeline needs the label as briefed, but conversion-as-julian would shift every date by 10 days.
- The canon differs from the folk version in ways I followed the **canon** on: Ana is not a street orphan but the daughter of don Pedro Urinza (Andalusian) and doña Plácida Lezama, orphaned at four into the Souza house; the brother don Juan dies at 15 *of a jug of cold water drunk while fencing*; the fatal bullfight is a **fall from a horse corriendo toros in Lima**, not a goring in Potosí (I let the campa nod to the tradition's horn). The "master persuaded / secret lessons" is the tradition's elaboration of the canon's self-training ("aprender a disparar las de fuego y jugar las otras"); I kept it, flagged traditional.
- Anchor date: the whole episode sits under **1653** (Pimentel's four-month government, Nestares Marín presiding). The famous duel is datable inside the canon to the **night of the víspera de San Juan** → 1653-06-23/24. Everything else is back/forward-counted from the chronicle's own intervals (15 months, two months abed, three months, eight days, one month in La Plata, five years away, return at age 20, two-plus years in La Plata, four months) → births c. 1640, return 1658, Ana's death early 1661, Eustaquia's ~May 1661. Marked traditional/inferred accordingly.
- Coordinates: Potosí micro-sites (Munaypata entrance, plaza del Gato, the bridge over the Ribera, valle de Mataca) are approximate — the colonial toponyms are real but not all survive on modern maps; noted `inferred`/`traditional` on dates, and the report flags Mataca (a Río/Cadena de Mataca exists in Potosí dept.) as the loosest pin.
- The two quotes used are genuinely recorded speech (via Melgarejo, an eyewitness Arzáns cites): Ana's "Deteneos allá, barbados…" and Eustaquia's "Levántate, hermana, que nos va la honra." All other stops take `null` — the deathbed declaration is indirect speech in the canon.
- The hornero coda is marked as **corpus**, dated 1736 (the chronicle closing) so the final stop carries the image without breaking in-segment chronology; Belgrano's Potosí rides in the opening (Cerro) and closing (coin/escudo) stops; Joan at the first crossing of dress and at the graves.

## Gaps in the canon
No birth or death dates as such; no burial site named; Melgarejo's later life unknown; the portraits (with their inscribed feet, "peruanas de Potosí") are lost; doña Paula, the Seville healer, the fencing master, the esclava and her son are all unnamed; the Lima years are summarized in two sentences ("otros sucesos, que por no tener certeza de ellos no los refiero" — Arzáns's own honesty about the limits of his testimony).

## The 5 richest episodes
1. **The San Juan's Eve duel** (pp. 171–172): full costume inventory, the challenge dialogue verbatim, Ana's pistol shot, the back-to-back fight, the alférez Mendoza dead, the wound tally, the retreat under the bridge arch binding each other's wounds.
2. **The escape** : father finds the suits, beats and locks them; the slave's rope from the camarín window, the drop in petticoats at 7 pm, the mules from Mataca eight days later.
3. **The portraits of Itulaín**: the friend from Lima undeceived, the sick Ana rising to dress as a man one last time, the inscriptions — the pair's only "epitaph."
4. **The double death**: Ana of the ring-wound after two-plus years; Eustaquia four months later "de sólo un corrimiento en el pecho," grief given a physician's name; both declaring they die doncellas; the estate to the poor, the esclava freed with silver to return to Potosí.
5. **The jug of water**: the brother killed at fifteen by a drink between fencing passes — the canon's own hinge from embroidery to steel.

## Verification (2026-07-05)
Independent structure-and-canon pass against the OCR full text of the Hanke–Mendoza edition (`historiadelavill02arzn_djvu.txt`, downloaded from archive.org).

**Structure.** JSON parses; 34 stops in 8 segments, every stop carries the same 10 keys (name, lat, lng, date, date_confidence, campa, quote, quote_source, sources, suggested_refs); all dates Gregorian AD and strictly ascending after repair; all campa 60–110 words (final range 96–110), present tense throughout, the great episodes (duel, retreat under the bridge, bullfight, double death) fully voiced.

**Quotes.** Both quotes verified **verbatim** in the OCR: Ana's "Deteneos alla barbados, y si no, experimentaréis la audacidad de este rayo" (l. 21670) and Eustaquia's "Levántate, hermana, que nos va la honra" (l. 21706–07). The Melgarejo eyewitness attribution is the text's own ("según contó Diego Melgarejo después que presente se halló a todo esto"). No other stop claims a quote; nothing to null.

**Coordinates.** Web-checked 12+ stops against the actual sites; all within ~100 m: Cerro Rico summit (−19.6183, −65.7497 vs file −19.619, −65.75), San Lorenzo de Carangas / plaza del Gato area (−19.5869, −65.7548), Convento San Francisco Potosí (−19.5911, −65.7528), Plaza 25 de Mayo Sucre (−19.0480, −65.2596) for the four La Plata stops, Plaza de Armas Cusco (−13.5167, −71.9788), Plaza Mayor Lima (−12.0458, −77.0306), Plaza de Armas Trujillo (−8.1118, −79.0286), Chayanta town (−18.4167, −66.5167 — exact). The flagged loose pins (Munaypata, plaza del Gato, bridge arch, valle de Mataca) are all genuine Arzáns toponyms (confirmed in the OCR: Munaypata ×15+, "plaza del Gato" ×4, "valle de Mataca" ×5, Mataca "distante de esta Villa ocho [leguas]"); positions plausible, left as placed. No coordinate fixes needed.

**Canon-fidelity spot checks (all verbatim in OCR).** Urinza/Lezama household in Munaypata (l. 21328–29); the jug of water ("sudando con la fatiga pidió un jarro de agua," l. 21384); víspera de San Juan at 10 o'clock (l. 21584ff); the escape with doña Paula, the mules, the merchant's train to Lima; Itulaín and the portraits in Chayanta ("La valerosa doña Ana de Urinza, peruanas de Potosí," l. 21946–64); the deaths ("poco más de dos años… murió de aquel achaque doña Ana, y a los cuatro meses… apesarada de su querida compañera"); the freed esclava returning to the Villa with silver (supports the coda placement).

**Repairs made (in place, re-validated).**
1. **Segment 5 reordered to the canon's sequence.** The chronicle reads: two years in Lima → Trujillo and the other cities → *return to Lima* → news of the father's death there ("Al cabo, pues, de dos años que estuvieron en Lima fueron a Trujillo… hasta que después tornaron a Lima… Estando en aquella ciudad tuvieron noticias de la muerte de su padre"). The file had bullfight (1656-07) → father's news (1656-09) → Trujillo (1657-03), which inverted this — and the Trujillo campa itself already said "they turn again to Lima." Fixed: Trujillo circuit moved before the bullfight and re-dated 1656-02-01 ("al cabo de dos años" from the Jan-1654 arrival); bullfight re-dated 1657-07-25 (second Lima stay, where the canon's "fiesta y regocijos de plaza" sentence sits, immediately before the news); father's news re-dated 1657-09-01. Return at the gates of Chuquisaca stays 1658-10 — "al cabo de cinco años de la salida" from the Oct-1653 escape, age 20, both intact.
2. **Viceroy anachronism in the Lima-arrival stop.** The campa named the Conde de Alba de Liste at a January-1654 arrival, but Alba de Liste reached Lima only in 1655; the viceroy in 1654 was the Conde de Salvatierra. Now reads "the viceregal court — Salvatierra's now, Alba de Liste's soon," which covers the whole stay.
3. Trujillo and news campa endings lightly rewritten to carry the displaced "five years" line and stay inside the 110-word budget (final counts 103 and 108).

**Not changed.** Calendar stays `gregorian` — the researcher's call is correct for post-1582 Spanish America; flip only if the pipeline hard-requires the briefed label. Stop count 34 is inside the 20–35 target and the canon's Lima years are, by Arzáns's own admission, summarized ("por no tener certeza de ellos no los refiero"), so no stops added.
