# Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah al-Ghafiqi: research report

*Wali of al-Andalus (interim, 721; substantive, 730-732). Killed on the road between Tours and Poitiers, October 732. Prepared 2026-08-03 for the Atlas of Journeys. Companion file: `al_ghafiqi.journey.json`, 39 stops in 9 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Gaps are stated as gaps.

One warning governs the dossier. Relative to his fame, al-Ghafiqi is among the least documented men in the atlas: no Arabic biography, no birth year, no burial place, no eyewitness from his own side, and everything outside one October afternoon fits on a page. The source with the most detail, the *Chronicle of 754*, was written in al-Andalus by a Christian a generation later. The journey runs heavily **[R]** and says so on each stop, in `date_confidence` rather than in prose.

## 1. Origins: the Tihamah, the Hijaz, and the Maghreb (no dates at all)

- Born into the Arab tribe of **Ghafiq**. **[R: Wikipedia; genealogical tradition in Ibn Idhari and al-Maqqari, both centuries later]** No birth year, no town, no parents beyond the patronymic.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: where.** English Wikipedia places the Banu Ghafiq in the **Tihamah**, the Red Sea coastal lowland. Arabic Wikipedia says he was born in the **Hijaz** and files the Ghafiq under the tribe of **Akk**. Both are in the campa; neither is chosen. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's pin, 14.1951 / 43.3151, is **Zabid in Yemen**, a town not founded until roughly 819. Discarded for a generic Tihamah point, **15.0 / 43.0**, naming no settlement.
- Moves to **Ifriqiya** and the Maghreb; becomes acquainted with **Musa ibn Nusayr** and his son **Abd al-Aziz ibn Musa**. **[R]** **Kairouan** carries this phase as the administrative centre of the Umayyad Maghreb, but **no source places al-Ghafiqi in the city**: the milieu is assigned by pattern, not attestation.
- **The crossing into al-Andalus is unrecorded.** **[R]** The pool pinned it at Tangier, 35.7873 / -5.8137, which is 3.5 km off the town (35.7595 / -5.8340) and the wrong crossing: the columns went **Ceuta to Gibraltar and Tarifa across the narrows**. Moved to **Ceuta, 35.8894 / -5.3198**.

## 2. Toulouse and the rout of 721

- **Al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani**, wali of al-Andalus, campaigns into Aquitaine from **Narbonne**, Umayyad since 719 and the caliphate's only outright possession in Gaul. **9 June 721: Odo of Aquitaine relieves Toulouse and routs the army; Al-Samh is mortally wounded.** **[A: Chronicle of 754 (Continuatio Hispana); Ibn Idhari; Wikipedia, "Battle of Toulouse (721)"]**
- **Retagged [A] to [R].** The pool tagged "al-Ghafiqi serves under Al-Samh and takes part in the campaign" **[A]**. English Wikipedia places him only among those who **"managed to escape"** after the defeat. No source attests his rank or role; officership is inferred backward from later prominence, which the pool's geography lens concedes.
- **Al-Samh dies at Narbonne after the retreat.** **[A: Ibn Idhari]** That **al-Ghafiqi directed the withdrawal** is **[R]**.
- **Interim governorship, 721**, after a command in eastern al-Andalus. **Duration corrected:** the pool gave 721-722 and "roughly a year," but Al-Samh died 9 June 721 and Anbasa was appointed later that same year, so the interim ran **months, in 721**.

## 3. The nine blank years (721-730)

- **Anbasa ibn Suhaym al-Kalbi supersedes the interim.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"; "Anbasa ibn Suhaym al-Kalbi"]** **Date corrected by a year:** the pool said **722**, both English Wikipedia articles say **721**. He was relieved the same year he took interim command.
- **Command in "eastern al-Andalus."** **[A for the phrase, R for all else]** No city is named; the journey pins **Zaragoza** and flags it as inference. **The gap:** between 722 and 730 there is **nothing**, no action, office, residence, or year. The largest hole in the dossier; the campa states it rather than filling it.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: how Anbasa died.** The pool said **killed** campaigning in southern Gaul, 726. English Wikipedia says he **"died of natural causes in 726 during his campaign in Aquitaine."** Some Arabic traditions have him mortally wounded in a rearguard action. Both are carried, not merged into "killed." The pin is his Septimanian base, not a recorded death site. After him, **a run of short-tenured governors**; the pool gives no count and the journey invents none. **[A: succession lists]**

## 4. The second governorship (730-732)

- **Caliph Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik appoints him wali a second time, 730**, on a reputation as intelligent, eloquent, a capable administrator. **[A: Wikipedia; Ibn Idhari; Akhbar Majmu'a]** Those three adjectives are the entire surviving character sketch of the man.
- **Causal claim removed as chronologically impossible.** The pool gave as the reason for the 730 appointment "his record suppressing the Cerdanya revolt." That revolt is dated **731** by the pool's own lenses and by Wikipedia's "Munuza." The suppression is an **act of** the second governorship, not a **qualification for** it.
- **Uthman ibn Naissa, called Munuza in Frankish sources**, Berber deputy in the Cerdanya, allies with Odo and marries his daughter, then revolts, 730-731. **[A: Chronicle of 754; Fredegarii Continuationes]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: how Uthman died.** The Chronicle of 754 has him killed in the fighting; Ibn Idhari and al-Maqqari have him go off a cliff rather than be taken. The pool's own source notes the contradiction. **Site:** the fortress is **not pinpointed** by either primary source; **Llivia** is a traditional identification, and the pool's **42.4633 / 1.9724** is kept and flagged as such.

## 5. The campaign of 732: muster, passes, Aquitaine

- **Contradiction inside the pool itself: where the army mustered.** Geography 12 puts the muster at **Cordoba** and cites the *Chronicle of 754*, which contains **no Cordoba mustering scene**. The same pool's chronology 10 and geography 13 name **Pamplona**, after English Wikipedia's "The wali assembled troops in Pamplona." Both kept: a Cordoba stop demoted to **[R]** for the summons and levy, the muster proper at **Pamplona, 42.8185 / -1.6432**.
- **Composition:** Arab heavy cavalry, Berber light cavalry, troops from other caliphal provinces. **[A: New World Encyclopedia, "Battle of Tours"]** **Recruitment from Yemen and the Levant** is reported and **flagged dubious by modern historians** in the pool's own source. **[R]** Carried as reported speech.
- **The Pyrenean crossing is attested; the pass is not.** **[A crossing, R Roncesvalles]** Primary sources say only that the army crossed.
- **Sequence corrected: Bordeaux before the Garonne.** The pool inverted the order in **two separate items**. Wikipedia, "Battle of the River Garonne": "following the victory at Bordeaux, Abdul Rahman engaged Odo's forces on the Garonne." Odo beaten outside Bordeaux, the city sacked, then a **second** Odo army destroyed at the Garonne. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's 44.6 / 0.2 is about 85 km upstream near Marmande and on the **wrong side of the prime meridian**; Wikipedia locates the engagement at Bordeaux, **45.0414 / -0.6067**. Used, still **[R]**, since the exact point on the river is unrecorded.
- **Saint-Seurin is tradition only.** **[R]** No contemporary source names any burned church.
- **Angouleme is an inferred waypoint.** **[R]** Not in either chronicle; it exists so the line of march is a line and not a jump, and its `date_confidence` says that. **Odo's appeal to Charles Martel [A]** is carried in a campa, not as a stop: the journey follows al-Ghafiqi's own geography.

## 6. Poitiers, Tours, and the seven days

- **Poitiers is named by the Chronicle of 754 as within the army's path.** **[A]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: Saint-Hilaire-le-Grand.** Three accounts of one building. French Wikipedia, "Eglise Saint-Hilaire-le-Grand de Poitiers": the site **was devastated**, "d'abord par les musulmans conduits par Abdiraman en 732." Medieval Frankish hagiography: the church was **spared** by miracle. The *Chronicle of 754*: **no episode at Poitiers at all**. The pool framed this as a miraculous sparing, which contradicts the source it cites. All three are stated; none is reconciled. **Coordinate note:** the pool gave 46.5769 / 0.3397, the church's French Wikipedia value is 46.5775 / 0.3325; the journey uses neither, inheriting **46.576 / 0.335** from `charles_martel` so the two travellers land on one dot.
- **Tours, the basilica of Saint Martin, is the declared objective.** **[A: Chronicle of 754]** He never arrives; the stop is an objective and says so.
- **Almost seven days of mutual observation before the engagement.** **[A: Chronicle of 754]** Charles Martel's dispositions, a Frankish, Gallic, and Germanic force avoiding open cavalry ground and intercepting on a wooded plateau. **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of Tours"]**

## 7. The battle and the death

- **Date contradictions, three, flagged and not resolved.** English Wikipedia: **10 October 732**. Arabic Wikipedia: **27 Sha'ban 114 AH = 21 October 732**. The atlas's own `charles_martel`: **0732-10-25**. The journey uses 10 October and carries all three in `date_confidence`.
- **Site contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** `charles_martel` pins the field at **46.721 / 0.54**, a placeholder rather than a located point. Modern scholarship places the engagement at **Moussais-la-Bataille, 46.7811 / 0.5406**, about 7 km north, while English Wikipedia still calls the exact location unclear from the surviving sources. The journey does both: four battle stops on the inherited pin, Moussais as its own stop. **Bad coordinate discarded:** the pool's afterlife lens twice gave 46.7167 / 0.6167, about 7 km south and 6 km east, across the Vienne near Bonneuil-Matours.
- **The infantry square holds against repeated cavalry charges.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of Tours"]** **The camp raid:** a portion of the Umayyad force breaks off to defend captured booty, disordering the line. **[A: Fredegarii Continuationes]** The camp includes camp followers, wives, and concubines. **[A]**
- **Overstatement corrected: how he died.** The pool claimed "both western Christian and Muslim historical traditions agree that al-Ghafiqi died while trying to halt the Umayyad retreat and became surrounded." They do not agree; the Latin source does not say it. The *Chronicle of 754* says only that the Austrasians **"killed the king, Abd ar-Rahman, when they found him, striking him on the chest."** The **rallying** and the **arrow** both come from the Arabic tradition ("he attempted to reorganize Muslim ranks... but fell mortally wounded"). Claiming convergence manufactures agreement the record does not supply. The journey states the traditions separately and says outright that they are usually welded together and should not be.
- **The night withdrawal.** With no agreed successor, divided Arab and Berber contingents strike nothing and go; the Franks find the camp empty at dawn. **[A: Fredegarii Continuationes; Chronicle of 754; Wikipedia]**
- **Numbers.** Army estimates run **20,000 to 80,000**. **[R]** Some medieval Christian chronicles claim **375,000** Muslim dead, treated as implausible and likely duplicated from earlier separate battles. **[R]** No number is asserted in the journey.
- **Scale disputed.** Historians disagree whether this was an invasion aimed at conquest and settlement or a large **raid in force** aimed at plunder. **[R]** Both readings survive in the journey's language.

## 8. Afterlife

- **There is no grave.** No source, English, French, or Arabic, records where the body went or whether any marker was raised. **[A as an absence, across all three Wikipedias]** The journey leaves the hole open and refuses to invent a tomb stop. **He left no writings** either: no letters, no documents, no attributed work. **[R, as an absence]**
- **Balat al-shuhada.** Arabic historiography names the battle for the road beside it and remembers him as a martyr and one of the greatest governors of al-Andalus. **[A: Arabic Wikipedia]** This is the whole of his positive afterlife. **Septimania outlasts him by twenty-seven years:** the pool's "until the 750s" is corrected, since Pepin's siege of Narbonne began **752** but the city fell **759**. **[A]**
- **The Haut-Quercy legend.** A Lot tradition holds he was not killed at Poitiers but fled toward Narbonne and was overtaken and killed at **Louchapt**, at the foot of the **Sangou cliff**, in **733**; **Pierre Riche** has proposed the epithet Martel was earned there rather than at Moussais. **[R: French Wikipedia, "Bataille de Poitiers (732)", "Martel (Lot)"]** **Coordinate correction:** the pool gave Louchapt and the town of Martel the **same point**, 44.9333 / 1.6167, which is Martel duplicated; French Wikipedia places Loupchat about 3 km away. The journey uses **44.953 / 1.63** for Louchapt, flagged approximate, and **44.9378 / 1.6097** for Martel.
- **The town of Martel** credits Charles Martel with founding it. Its own history dismantles that: first documented mention in the **cartulary of Aubazine, 1142**; settlement created in the **eleventh century** around a salt market; **no manuscript links Charles Martel to it before the sixteenth century**. **Correction: seven towers, not seven anvils.** French Wikipedia calls it the **"ville aux sept tours,"** a seventeenth-century designation, and its arms bear **martels**, hammers.
- **Item dropped.** The pool's "Cinq Morts" tradition at Vouneuil-sur-Vienne, with a priory dated c. 945, is **not carried**: its stated source was "a DuckDuckGo-aggregated search summary," which is not a source, and the commune's French Wikipedia article has no such locality and no priory of that date. Its coordinate for the commune was also wrong (46.7178 / 0.5408, not 46.6667 / 0.5333).
- **A gap, not a negative.** A search for streets, mosques, schools, or institutes named after him **returned nothing**. That is a gap in the search, not evidence of absence; the journey claims nothing either way.
- **Nineteenth-century French art.** **Theodore Gechter**, bronze, **1833**, *Le combat de Charles Martel et d'Abd al-Rahman, roi des Sarrasins*, Musee du Louvre. **[A: French Wikipedia]** **Charles de Steuben**, history painting, **1837**. **[A: English Wikipedia]** This is the mechanism of his memory in the West: he survives as the defeated antagonist in somebody else's founding myth. From Gibbon onward the victory and his death mark the farthest north-westward reach of Umayyad expansion. **[R]**

## 9. Atlas interlocks and pin discipline

Pins inherited **byte-identical**: Cordoba **37.8882 / -4.7794** (`ziryab`, `ibn_arabi`); Damascus **33.514 / 36.277** (`dihya`); Toulouse **43.6045 / 1.4442** (`clovis`); Pamplona **42.8185 / -1.6432** (`charlemagne`); and from `charles_martel`, Narbonne **43.184 / 3.004**, Bordeaux **44.838 / -0.579**, Poitiers Saint-Hilaire **46.576 / 0.335**, Tours Saint Martin **47.394 / 0.685**, the field of Tours-Poitiers **46.721 / 0.54**, Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois **47.152 / 0.654**.

**Three pin conflicts inside the atlas, flagged and not silently resolved:**

1. **Cordoba.** `maimonides` uses **37.879 / -4.7794**; `ziryab` and `ibn_arabi` use **37.8882 / -4.7794**. The pool wanted the Mezquita value. This journey follows the two-to-one majority.
2. **The battlefield.** `charles_martel` at **46.721 / 0.54** against Moussais at **46.7811 / 0.5406**, 7 km apart, with different dates. Both pins are in the journey.
3. **Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois.** `charles_martel` at **47.152 / 0.654**, `joan_of_arc` at **47.157 / 0.6545**, roughly 500 m apart. This journey takes the Charles Martel value, since the chain starts with his sword.

**Mutual gaze, travellers named in campa:** `charles_martel`, `joan_of_arc`, `clovis`, `charlemagne`, `dihya`, `ziryab`, `maimonides`, `ibn_arabi`.

The pool also proposed the Kaaba, Ibn Arabi's 1240 Damascus pin, and Cordoba in Argentina. None is carried: no source puts al-Ghafiqi in Mecca, `dihya`'s caliphal Damascus pin serves better than the thirteenth-century one, and the Argentine Cordoba is a toponymic echo with no historical link, as the pool concedes.
