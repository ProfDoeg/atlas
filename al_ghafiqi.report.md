# Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah al-Ghafiqi: research report

*Governor of al-Andalus (721; 730-732). Killed on the field between Tours and Poitiers, October 732. Prepared 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and are **not adjudicated**. Gaps are stated as gaps.

A warning governing the whole file: al-Ghafiqi is among the least documented men in the atlas relative to his fame. What the sources say about him outside one October afternoon fits on a page. No Arabic biography, no birth year, no burial place, no eyewitness from his own side; the Latin chronicle with the most detail, the *Chronicle of 754*, was written in al-Andalus by a Christian a generation later. The journey therefore carries a high proportion of **[R]** stops and says so on each one.

---

## 1. Origins: the Tihamah and the Maghreb (no dates)

- Born into the Arab tribe of **Ghafiq**, whose homeland is the **Tihamah**, the Red Sea coastal lowland of Arabia. **[R: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"]** No birth year, no town. The first pin marks the tribal region at 15, 43; it is a region, not a birthplace, and cannot honestly be given decimals.
- Relocates to **Ifriqiya** and the wider **Maghreb**, where he becomes connected to **Musa ibn Nusayr** and Musa's son **Abd al-Aziz**. **[R: same article]** **Correction:** the pool called both men "the early Umayyad governors of al-Andalus." Musa governed **Ifriqiya**; only Abd al-Aziz governed al-Andalus, 714-716, handed the province before Musa returned to Syria. **[A: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Aziz ibn Musa"]**
- **Kairouan** is the era-landmark for the seat of Umayyad Ifriqiya. No source places al-Ghafiqi at the Great Mosque. **[R]** Coordinate **35.6814, 10.1039**; the pool's 35.6785, 10.1005 is 450 m off and discarded.
- **Ceuta** as the crossing point is pure route reconstruction. **[R: general knowledge of the conquest route]** No source records his crossing at all. A gap, and the stop says so.
- **Cordoba before 721.** The pool tagged "serves in the administration ... at Cordoba" **[A]**. Not attested: the article jumps from the Maghreb straight to his eastern command after Toulouse. **Retagged [R]**.

## 2. Septimania and the rout at Toulouse (719-721)

- **Al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani**, governor of al-Andalus, takes **Barcelona** and **Narbonne** and eliminates the remaining Visigothic strongholds in Septimania, 719/720; he mints the first purely Arab coins struck in al-Andalus, 720, in a fiscal reform. **[A: Wikipedia, "Al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani"]**
- **9 June 721, Battle of Toulouse.** Duke **Odo of Aquitaine** returns with reinforcements, encircles the besieging army and routs it; al-Samh is mortally wounded and dies shortly after at Narbonne. **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of Toulouse (721)"; "Al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani"]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Some authors call Toulouse 721 the *Balat al-Shuhada*, the pavement of the martyrs; others attach that name only to the battle of 732. **[R: Wikipedia, "Battle of Toulouse (721)", which states the dispute in those terms]** The pool asserted the 721 attribution as **[A]**; retagged **[R]**.
- **Al-Ghafiqi is among the officers who escape the rout.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of Toulouse (721)"]** The exact point of the battle line is not attested; the Basilica of Saint-Sernin, used as landmark, postdates 721 by centuries.
- **July 721: elected interim governor by the army in the field; August 721: replaced by Anbasa ibn Suhaym al-Kalbi.** The first term lasts about a month. **[A: Wikipedia, "List of Umayyad governors of al-Andalus"]**

## 3. The years between the two terms (721-730)

- Al-Ghafiqi takes command of **Eastern al-Andalus** in 721, immediately after Toulouse, and is briefly relieved when Anbasa is appointed. **[A: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"]** The pool had him commanding there continuously 721-730; no source supports that. The command is attested for **721 only**, and **722-730 is a blank** the journey states rather than fills.
- **Zaragoza** stands as the seat of that eastern command (the later Upper March). The source says "Eastern Andalus" without naming a city. **[R: inference]** No period Islamic monument survives there, so the city centre stands in.
- **Seven governors, not six**, hold office between the terms; the pool said "six" then listed seven. Corrected, all **[A: governors list]**: Anbasa ibn Suhaym al-Kalbi (Aug 721 - Jan 726); Udhra ibn Abd Allah al-Fihri (Jan - Mar 726); Yahya ibn Salama al-Kalbi (Mar 726 - Jun 728); Hudhaifa ibn al-Ahwas al-Ashja'i (Jun - Dec 728); Uthman ibn Abi Nis'a al-Khath'ami (Dec 728 - Apr 729); al-Haytham ibn Ubayd al-Kilabi (Apr 729 - Feb 730); Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Ashja'i (Feb - Mar 730).
- **Narbonne in these years.** Umayyad capital of Septimania, base for raids into Aquitaine. Whether al-Ghafiqi held a post there is **not stated in any source consulted**. A gap; the journey's Narbonne stops carry al-Samh and the Septimanian afterlife, not his residence.

## 4. The second governorship (March 730 - October 732)

- **Second term, March 730 to October 732**, succeeding Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Ashja'i. **[A: governors list]** He is described as intelligent, eloquent, an accomplished administrator. **[A: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: who appointed him.** The al-Ghafiqi article says Caliph **Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik** appointed him from Damascus. The governors list marks the second term **without** the asterisk denoting caliphal appointment, i.e. appointed from **Kairouan**. The dependent-governor system of 711-756 favours Kairouan; the two pages plainly conflict. **Retagged [R]**.
- **Uthman ibn Naissa, called Munuza**, Berber deputy governor in Cerdanya, seals a peace with Odo of Aquitaine by marrying Odo's daughter **Lampegia**, 730; he revolts in 731, and al-Ghafiqi marches against him, defeats him in a brief action and has him executed. **[A: Wikipedia, "Uthman ibn Naissa"]**
- **Llivia**, Cerdanya, coordinate **42.4644, 1.9808**. The pool's 42.4653, 1.9711 is discarded (about 800 m west of the town).
  - **Framing corrected.** Wikipedia, "Llivia," does **not** call the town Munuza's stronghold. It says the town "may also have been the scene of the siege by which governor Abdul Rahman Al Ghafiqi ... rid himself of the Moorish (Berber) rebel Uthman ibn Naissa." The journey uses that weaker claim. The pool's "by some accounts by suicide" is in neither cited article and is dropped.
  - **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Some Christian chronicles report a Munuza killed near **Trubia** or **La Felguera** in Asturias. Wikipedia attributes those to a probably **different** Munuza.

## 5. The campaign of 732: muster, passes, Aquitaine

- **Pamplona is the muster point.** He "assembled troops in Pamplona, called for recruits from Yemen and the Levant," and that army "went through all places like a desolating storm." **[A: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"]** The pool's "drawing on garrisons from Pamplona" misreads this: Pamplona is where the army gathers, not where the troops come from.
- **Crossing of the Pyrenees into Gascony, 732.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of Tours"]** The **specific pass is not named** in any source. The journey uses the **Ibaneta pass at Roncesvalles (43.0186, -1.3183)** as the best-attested western crossing of the era. **[R: route reconstruction]**
- **Huesca, Bayonne, Dax, Saintes, Angouleme** are **[R]** route reconstructions on the known Roman and Ebro-valley roads. **No chronicle names any of them.** They exist so the line of march is a line and not a jump, and each stop says it is not attested.
- **Bordeaux: Odo defeated outside the city, the city sacked, 732.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of Tours"]** The precise point of the battle or sack is not specified; the Basilica of Saint-Seurin, a genuine fourth/fifth-century foundation standing through this period, is the landmark.
- **Battle of the River Garonne: Odo defeated a second time.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"]** The **coordinate is not attested**. The pool's 44.8378, -0.5792 puts the battle inside Bordeaux, which no source does; the engagement is conventionally placed at a river crossing outside the city, exact site unrecorded. **Coordinate retagged [R]**, a stand-in for a crossing on the lower Garonne.
- **Quote at the Garonne:** "God alone knows the number of the slain."
  - **Attribution corrected and upgraded.** The pool called this untraceable; it is fully citable. It is the *Chronicle of 754* on the Garonne. Latin: *Solus Deus numerum morientium vel pereuntium recognoscat.* English from **Wolf, 1990, p. 144**, the citation attached to that sentence in the current "Battle of Tours" wikitext. The al-Ghafiqi article's looser "western chroniclers" attribution is not followed; the phrase is **not** in the "Battle of the River Garonne" article.
- **Charles Martel's numbers and whereabouts.** The **Danube** campaign appears **only** in the al-Ghafiqi article, following David Levering Lewis; "Battle of Tours" places him in Gaul. **[R]**, single-sourced. "Roughly 30,000" is one estimate among many: "Battle of Tours" gives 15,000 to 80,000, Davis and Hanson 20,000-30,000, Schoenfeld calling anything above 50,000 logistically impossible.
- **Poitiers and the burning of Saint-Hilaire.** A later Latin hagiographic tradition, absent from the Arabic sources and from the *Chronicle of 754*. **[R]** The atlas carries it as an **[A]**-tagged stop in charles_martel.journey.json dated 0732-10-10; that internal disagreement is flagged below.
- **Tours, the Abbey of Saint Martin as the objective.** **Upgraded from the pool's framing.** "Battle of Tours" says the Chronicle records a desire to "despoil Tours" and plunder the Abbey of Saint Martin, so the shrine as objective is chronicle-supported, not merely later tradition. Still **[R]**, because the army never arrives. Coordinate **47.3941, 0.6848**, inherited byte-identical.

## 6. The battle and the death (October 732)

- **The site is unknown.** "Battle of Tours" places the engagement only generally between Tours and Poitiers, near Moussais-la-Bataille, and says the exact location remains unclear. **[R]** The pool tagged the same coordinate **[A]** in one lens and **[R]** in another; the journey uses **[R]** throughout.
- **Karl Ferdinand Werner** proposed the hamlet of **Moussais**, commune of Vouneuil-sur-Vienne, and dated the engagement **2 October 732**. **[A: French Wikipedia, "Bataille de Poitiers (732)"]** Werner's coordinate, from "Battle of Tours": 46°46'52" N, 0°32'26" E = **46.7811, 0.5406**. The pool's 46.667, 0.517 is 13 km off and discarded.
  - **Internal inconsistency flagged.** Taking Werner's *site* while keeping the traditional **10 October** *date* mixes two incompatible reconstructions. The journey dates the death 10 October, states Werner's 2 October in date_confidence, and gives Moussais a separate afterlife stop rather than pretending the two agree.
- **Seven days of skirmish before the general engagement.** **[R: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"]** **The Frankish infantry square repels repeated cavalry charges.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of Tours"]** **A contingent breaks off the line to protect the plundered baggage train, disordering the ranks**, and **al-Ghafiqi is killed rallying his men, 10 October 732**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"]** for the death and the date; **[R]** for the place.
- **Quote, the death:** "The people of Austrasia, greater in a number of soldiers and formidably armed, killed the king, Abd ar-Rahman, when they found him, striking him on the chest." *Chronicle of 754*, tr. Kenneth Baxter Wolf, as quoted in Wikipedia, "Battle of Tours." **[A]**
  - **Misattribution corrected.** The pool's chronology and afterlife lenses both rendered this as "the Franks killed the king." The chronicle says **the people of Austrasia**; the chest wound is genuine. Wolf's translation was seen only through Wikipedia's citation of it, not in the book.
- **The Chronicle of St. Denis** claims Martel "slew in that battle 300,000 men, likewise their king by name Abderrahman." **[A for the words, on the Fordham page]** The 300,000 is chronicle exaggeration, not a count. The pool's transcription silently spliced out the interior clause "as history bears witness"; restored here and in the journey quote.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: the date of death.** Western reckonings give **10 October 732**; Werner gives **2 October 732**; an Arabic-tradition date of **27 Sha'ban 114 AH** converts to **21 October 732**; the atlas's own charles_martel.journey.json dates the pitched battle **0732-10-25**. Four dates, no reconciliation offered. The journey carries the disagreement in date_confidence.
- **Withdrawal.** The surviving commanders cannot agree on a successor, divided by tribal and ethnic rivalry, and the army withdraws over the Pyrenees. **[A: Wikipedia, "Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi"]** The pass is **not named**; the journey reuses the outbound Ibaneta pin and says so.
- **Abd al-Malik ibn Qatan al-Fihri succeeds him at Cordoba**, 732, installed in December per the geography lens. **[A: Wikipedia, "List of Umayyad governors of al-Andalus"]**

## 7. Afterlife

- **No burial place is recorded anywhere.** No source, Latin or Arabic, names a grave, a marker, or a relocation of remains. **[A as an absence, across the Chronicle of 754, Ibn Idhari and later chronicles]** The largest gap in the dossier; the journey leaves it open rather than inventing a tomb stop.
- **Balat al-Shuhada.** Ibn Idhari al-Marrakushi records in *Al-Bayan al-Mughrib* that al-Ghafiqi and many of his men found martyrdom at what Arabic tradition calls the pavement of the martyrs. **[A]**
  - **Dating corrected.** Wikipedia puts Ibn Idhari in the late thirteenth / early fourteenth century and *Al-Bayan al-Mughrib* at about 1312, so "early fourteenth-century" replaces the pool's "13th-century." The stop sits at **Marrakesh**, where the historian worked, not on a placeholder on the Tours-Poitiers axis.
  - **Meaning disputed.** Some scholars read *balat* as pavement or causeway, implying a Roman road; others as palace, implying proximity to the Abbey of Saint-Martin at Tours. The disagreement drives rival theories of where he fell. **[A: French Wikipedia, "Bataille de Poitiers (732)"]**
- **The Haut-Quercy legend.** A regional tradition holds that al-Ghafiqi survived the rout and was killed the following year, **733**, at **Louchapt**, near the Sangou cliff. **[R: French Wikipedia, "Bataille de Poitiers (732)"]**
  - **Location corrected.** The tradition belongs to the **Lot** (Haut-Quercy is the northern Lot), not the Dordogne. The pool's 45.233, 0.417 lies about 120 km northwest of the Lot. Corrected to approximately **44.75, 1.75**, flagged as approximate.
- **Élisabeth Carpentier** on the battle: "Il s'agit d'« une bataille très mal connue. On n'est sûr ni du lieu ni de la date ni de l'importance des effectifs »" **[R as quoted in French Wikipedia, "Bataille de Poitiers (732)"]**
- **Gibbon's counterfactual**, 1788: "Perhaps the interpretation of the Koran would now be taught in the schools of Oxford." **Citation corrected:** the passage is chapter 52 of *Decline and Fall*, which falls in **volume V**, not volume VI. Volumes IV, V and VI all appeared in 1788, so the year stands.
- **The national narrative.** Charles Martel as saviour of Christendom against al-Ghafiqi's army hardened chiefly in the **nineteenth century** and has been reused since, from anti-clerical Republican historiography to contemporary identity politics. **[A: French Wikipedia, "Bataille de Poitiers (732)"]** This is the mechanism of the afterlife: he persists as the defeated Other in somebody else's founding myth.
- **The modern site.** An open-air installation, "732, la bataille," stands at **Pied Sec** in **Vouneuil-sur-Vienne**, with a sonorised orientation table and enamel panels showing Eudes, Abd al-Rahman and Charles Martel together; roughly 25,000 visitors a year. **[A: same]** It is the nearest thing to a monument naming al-Ghafiqi at the battle site.
  - **Coordinates corrected.** Vouneuil-sur-Vienne town is 46.7178, 0.5408; the Moussais / Pied Sec site sits north of it at about **46.7811, 0.5406**. The pool's 46.6667, 0.5167 is 6 to 14 km too far southwest.
- **Septimania.** Umayyad footholds persist there roughly twenty-seven more years, to about 759, though major northward expansion ends with al-Ghafiqi's death. **[R: Wikipedia, "Battle of Tours"]**

## 8. Atlas interlocks and pin discipline

Pins inherited **byte-identical** from journeys already in the atlas: Poitiers / Saint-Hilaire **46.5802, 0.3404**, the field of Tours-Poitiers **47, 0.5** and Tours / Saint Martin **47.3941, 0.6848** from charles_martel.journey.json; Cordoba **37.8882, -4.7794** from ziryab, maimonides and ibn_arabi; Paris **48.8566, 2.3522** from clovis; the Umayyad Mosque of Damascus **33.5117, 36.3064** from abdelkader.journey.json.

**Two pin conflicts, flagged and not silently resolved:**

1. **Cordoba.** The geography lens wanted **37.879, -4.7794**, the Mezquita. Three atlas journeys use **37.8882, -4.7794**, and the journey follows the atlas so the stop lands on the dot the other travellers land on. The Mezquita value is recorded here as the more precise landmark.
2. **The battlefield.** charles_martel.journey.json pins the field at **47, 0.5**, a placeholder rather than a located point; Werner's Moussais at **46.7811, 0.5406** lies about 30 km south. The journey does both: the battle stops inherit the atlas pin, Moussais gets a separate afterlife stop. Neither is presented as settled.

**Mutual gaze, travellers named in campa:** charles_martel, clovis, charlemagne, joan_of_arc, ziryab, maimonides, ibn_arabi, dihya, yolande_aragon. Also carried without a stop: the sword charles_martel.journey.json leaves at **Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois**, sent for by name by Jehanne d'Arc in 1429. No verified coordinate for Fierbois was reachable this pass, so it is named in a campa instead.
