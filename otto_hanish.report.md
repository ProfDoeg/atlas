# Otto Hanish (Otoman Zar-Adusht Ha'nish), 1856-1936: research report

*2026-08-07. Ernst (or Erich) Otto Haenisch, born Posen, died Los Angeles; founder of Mazdaznan. For the Atlas of Journeys.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, hostile testimony, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**The central problem.** Two biographies exist for the same man and they do not overlap at one point. The first is a Prussian parish and civil record: a grocer's son born in Posen in 1856. The second is the movement's hagiography, written by his emissary David Ammann: a Russian diplomat's son born in Tehran in 1844 and raised by a secret Zoroastrian order in the Iranian mountains. The journey carries both, because the subject's whole public career consists of maintaining the second account against the first.

---

## 1. Posen, 1856: the documented birth

- Born **Ernst Otto Haenisch** (en.wikipedia) or **Erich Otto Haenisch** (de.wikipedia), son of **Heinrich Ernst Haenisch**, grocer or provisions merchant, and **Anna Dorothea Schmidt**, in Posen (Poznan), Prussia, **19 December 1856**. **[A: both Wikipedias, "Otoman Zar-Adusht Ha'nish"]** **Contradiction flagged:** Ernst versus Erich. The pool's afterlife lens introduced a third form, "Otto Hanisch," in neither cited article; dropped. The claimed alternative birthday is also 19 December, twelve years earlier: the legend keeps the calendar day and moves the year and the continent.
- **Baptized evangelical, 28 December 1856**, in Posen. **[A: de.wikipedia]** **Corrected:** the pool located this at the Evangelical-Reformed church of St Peter on Plac Wiosny Ludow, at 52.4066, 16.9282. Neither holds. de.wikipedia says only "evangelisch getauft" and names no building; per pl.wikipedia the square is at 52.405556, 16.930388, and the church that stood there from 1841 until its destruction in 1945 was at the Krysiewicza and Polwiejska corner, roughly 52.4048, 16.9305. The journey uses the corrected square coordinates and calls the church attribution **[R]**, conjecture offered as conjecture.

## 2. Tehran and the Alborz, 1844: the claimed birth

- Ammann's follower biography holds that he was born 19 December **1844 in Tehran**, son of a Russian diplomat and a German noblewoman, with a congenital heart defect, and that as a child he was given to a **secret society of Zoroastrians in the mountains of Iran** who trained him in breath and diet. **[R: en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia, reporting Ammann]**
- **Corrected:** the "25 years of training" figure is in neither Wikipedia; en.wikipedia says only that he was sent as a child. Dropped. No Persian, Russian, or Prussian record supports any element, and no evidence places Haenisch on Persian ground at any point. Tehran (35.6892, 51.3890) and the Alborz highlands (35.9563, 52.1090) are pinned as **[R]** legendary geography, marked so in stop prose and `date_confidence`.

## 3. The crossing, undated

He emigrates at an unlocated date. **No manifest, port of entry, or year has been found.** en.wikipedia says only that he settled in the United States; de.wikipedia that he was living there "spaetestens seit 1890," Chicago first. **[R]** The journey pins the Port of New York (40.7033, -74.0170) as a **stated gap**, with `date_confidence` recording the 1880 date as a placeholder.

## 4. Illinois: Sinclair's version

- **Upton Sinclair**, *The Profits of Religion* (1918), pp. 278-281, places him in **Mendota, Illinois**, as sheep herder, typesetter, mesmerist, spiritualist. **[R: en.wikipedia, hedged as "According to Upton Sinclair"]**
- **Corrected twice.** (a) The pool dated this 1880 and tagged it **[A]**. No year is in the source, and the account is uncorroborated hostile testimony from a polemic about religious fraud; retagged **[R]**, date marked a placeholder in an undated pre-1890 window. (b) The pool attributed Sinclair to "the Chicago press"; it is a book, and he places the trades in Illinois generally, not in Mendota specifically.
- Mendota is at 41.5470, -89.1179. The pool's geography lens gave 41.5495, -89.1201 (acceptable); its afterlife lens gave 41.5486, **-89.3345**, 18 km west in farmland. The journey standardizes on **41.5495, -89.1201**.

## 5. The church memberships

- Reported to have joined the **LDS Church**, the **Christian Catholic Apostolic Church**, and **James Brighouse's Mormon splinter**. **[R: en.wikipedia, same undated Sinclair-derived passage]** **Corrected:** the pool dated this 1885 and tagged it **[A]**; no date is in the source. Retagged **[R]**, undated.
- **Partly recovered against the pool's own hedge:** the Christian Catholic Apostolic Church **is** John Alexander Dowie's, run out of Zion City, Illinois (42.4469, -87.8340). The pool treated the Dowie link as unconfirmed; the membership is sourced. What is not sourced is any physical presence at Zion, and the journey separates the two. Zion City was not founded until 1901, so the pin marks the institution, not a visit.

## 6. Chicago: the ten-year contradiction

**de.wikipedia:** living in the USA by 1890 at the latest, Chicago first. **en.wikipedia:** "From 1900 Hanisch lived in Chicago." **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Nothing consulted closes the ten years. **Corrected:** the pool claims in three separate lenses that he founded a "**Mazdaznan Peace Centre**" here in 1890. That name appears in neither Wikipedia article on Ha'nish nor in either on Mazdaznan. It is unsourced and **dropped from the journey entirely**; de.wikipedia supports residence, not a founding.

## 7. The temple at 3016 Lake Park Avenue

- "For many years Hanisch maintained a temple at 3016 Lake Park Avenue, Chicago." **[A: en.wikipedia]** The address is attested; **no start year is**. The pool asserted 1900, an inference from the separate sentence dating his Chicago residence. The journey gives a 1900 pin with `date_confidence` recording the start as undetermined and the temple as first *documented* in the 1912 Lindsay proceedings.
- **Coordinates corrected:** the pool used 41.8390, -87.6067 across three stops, a point in Burnham Park off the street grid. Nominatim gives **41.8400, -87.6121**, which the journey uses.
- The building is the former **Farragut Boat Club** house (1886, Robert Rae Jr.) per non-Wikipedia sourcing. **[R: not independently confirmed]** The pool attached the founding of the "Mazdaznan Temple Association of Associates of God" to this address; that body was founded in **Los Angeles in 1917**, so the clause is deleted.
- He claims a medical degree and lectures on vegetarian diet and intestinal cleansing, c. 1900. **[R: de.wikipedia reports the claim without verification]** No institution or diploma identified. Folded into this stop, not pinned separately.

## 8. 1902: the name, the journal, and New York

- **Adopts the name Otoman Zar-Adusht Ha'nish**, 1902 **[A: en.wikipedia]**, and **launches the monthly *Sun-Worshiper*, February 1902, "in the Chicago area"** **[A: en.wikipedia, "Mazdaznan"]**. The pool located the launch at 3016 Lake Park Avenue; no source does. The address attribution is removed and the stop pinned to Chicago generally.
- **de.wikipedia relocates his base to New York City in 1902.** This overlaps en.wikipedia's continuous Chicago temple. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** No New York address survives in either.

## 9. *Inner Studies*

Dated **1902** in the en.wikipedia Mazdaznan bibliography **[A]**; the archive.org text used for the quotations reads **1904** (identifier `inner_studies`). **Discrepancy flagged.** Four quotations were verified verbatim against that text and are all **[A]**: one from Lesson One (the body as the greatest of mechanisms) and three from Lesson Two (clean bodies and clean ideas, the pure fountain, the constipated person). Two are used in the journey.

**Corrected quotation:** the pool gave "Woman is the greatest factor in all life manifestations, and in a perfect state the true presentation of nature." The source (Lesson Three, pp. 34-35) continues "...the true presentation of nature **and nature's God**." Truncating converts a theological claim into a naturalistic one. Not used in the truncated form.

This is the book read aloud in a Chicago courtroom in 1912 and named in a Saxon expulsion order in 1914.

## 10. Europe: the Ammanns, not the founder

- **David and Frieda Ammann** carry the teaching into Europe **from 1907**, founding lodges in **Leipzig, Dresden, Chemnitz, Weimar, Hamburg**. **[A: de.wikipedia]** **Corrected:** the pool dated all five to 1907; de.wikipedia says "von 1907 an," an open period. **Diaetetik und Kochbuch**, Ammann's translation, is the first German edition, 1908, alongside the Leipzig magazine *Mazdaznan*. **[A]** The pool also called the Ammanns "former California farmers"; de.wikipedia records David Ammann born 12 October 1855 in **Honnavar, India**. Dropped.
- **Leipzig central office, Schulstrasse 1, in the former Masonic lodge Minerva zu den drei Palmen, opened 25 September 1910.** **[A: de.wikipedia, "David Ammann"]** Coordinates could **not** be confirmed: Nominatim resolves that address only to the Moellkau suburb at 51.3313, 12.4440. The journey uses 51.3406, 12.3747 and labels it approximate.
- **Two later Leipzig addresses in the pool are misdated by six and seven years.** Sophienplatz 4 is the office for **1916-1919**, Weststrasse 6 **post-1919**. Both fall after Ammann's May 1914 expulsion and cannot belong to a pre-1914 expansion narrative. Neither is pinned, and the Weststrasse coordinates are additionally doubtful.
- **Hanish's own travel: Europe once in 1911, then regularly 1923-1932.** **[A: de.wikipedia]** No city is named for any of it. The pool pinned the 1923-1932 visits to Leipzig, inventing an itinerary its own text admits does not survive. Both journey stops state the placement as inference.

## 11. Los Angeles, 1908 or 1917

- **en.wikipedia:** from 1908 he lives in Los Angeles and gives free Sunday morning lectures. **de.wikipedia:** he settles there in 1917. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved;** both are carried as separate stops.
- **1917: founds the Reorganisierte Mazdaznan Tempel-Vereinigung der Verbuendeten Gottes.** **[A: both Wikipedias]** The pool's rendering, "Temple Association of God," drops "Verbuendeten"; the sense is "of the Associates of God." **Corrected:** the pool placed this founding at **1159 South Norton Avenue**, which nothing sources. en.wikipedia attaches Norton Avenue only to the **February 1930** suit. The 1917 stop is pinned to Los Angeles generally.

## 12. The charges

- **January 1912, the Billy Lindsay case.** Elizabeth Lindsay sent her twelve-year-old son Billy to travel with Hanish; the uncle sued for custody and passages of *Inner Studies* describing tantric sexual practice were read into the record. **[A: en.wikipedia; courtroom detail from contemporary press relayed by secondary sourcing, not independently verified]** **4 March 1912:** temple raided; convicted of sending obscene literature through the mail; fined $2,500; six months. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **4 June 1918:** a wanted poster circulates in Los Angeles. **[R: en.wikipedia, "Mazdaznan"; carried as reported]** **14 June 1918:** a grand jury votes ten indictments for sexual abuse of minors, several complainants children aged 11 to 15. **[A: en.wikipedia]** **Early 1920:** arrested in Chicago and sent to Los Angeles to answer them. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **Corrected:** the pool says of the arrest that "the charge and disposition are not specified." The charge is specified. What is missing is the **disposition**: no verdict, acquittal, sentence, or dismissal was found. That is the real gap, the journey states it, and it sits under the most serious charge of his life.

## 13. 1930: the divorce suit

**25 February 1930:** Herman R. Huber, a draftsman, sues, alleging his wife had become "fanatical, idiotical, and unreasonable by following the repugnant rituals" of the cult; Hanish, then 74, is named as "Ottoman, czar of Adusht, master mind Hanisch of the Mazdazans"; the temple address is given as 1159 South Norton Avenue. Cited to the *Kansas City Star*. **[A: en.wikipedia]** **Corrected:** the pool says "the details of the suit itself are not preserved." They are preserved in full in the cited article.

## 14. Death and afterlife

- **Died of pneumonia in Los Angeles, 29 February 1936.** **[A: both Wikipedias]** No hospital, street, or physician named. **No burial record found**: neither article gives one, and Find a Grave returned HTTP 403. **Stated as a gap.**
- **Contradiction flagged:** a Mazdaznan document announces a Summer Gahanbar at the Norton Avenue temple, 27 June to 4 July **1937**, "led by Dr. O. Z. A. Hanish," sixteen months after the attested death. **[R: search results only]** Either the name was used posthumously or a date is wrong. Not decided here.
- **Nazi bans, staged:** provisional 1935, literature prohibited 1938, complete ban 1941. **[A: de.wikipedia]** **Corrected:** the pool says the bans came "in the years following his death" while dating the entry 1935. The 1935 ban precedes the February 1936 death, and the journey places it in his lifetime. Charges carried alongside them include child abuse and antisemitic and white-supremacist elements, the latter reflected in the 1933 compilation *Mazdaznan: Die Rassenlehre*. **[A that the sources carry them; not adjudicated, and the regime is not a court]**
- **1959: Neuzeitliche Diaet- und Lebensschule founded at Bringhausen am Edersee.** **[A: de.wikipedia]** **Coordinates corrected:** the pool gave 51.2167, 8.9333, in forest; de.wikipedia gives Bringhausen (Edertal, Hesse) at **51.1715, 8.9972**. The 1980 move to Encinitas and the 1960 Norton Avenue listing are **[A]** and named in the closing campa without pins.

## 15. The Herrliberg coordinate problem

Three points for one village appear in the pool: 47.2936, 8.6006 (Aryana-Heim); 47.2939, 8.5989 (Ammann's supposed death); 47.3067, 8.5928 (afterlife lens). Herrliberg's centre is 47.287, 8.615. **The atlas already holds a Herrliberg pin:** `itten.journey.json` at **47.2906, 8.6125**. Per the inheritance rule the journey uses that value for both Herrliberg stops, so the two files sit on one point.

Related: **David Ammann died at Frankfurt am Main on 20 February 1923** and was buried at Herrliberg on 27 February. **[A: de.wikipedia, "David Ammann"]** Both the pool's chronology and its afterlife lens put the death at Herrliberg. The journey pins it to Frankfurt (50.1109, 8.6821) and flags the disagreement.
