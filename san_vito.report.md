# San Vito (Saint Vitus): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Vitus of Lucania, called San Vito, Sankt Veit, Svaty Vit, Sveti Vid; traditionally a Sicilian child martyr of the Diocletianic persecution, patron of dancers, actors, dogs and epileptics; eponym of Saint Vitus's Dance and of Vidovdan.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, hagiographic tradition, or claim carried only by devotional literature. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Sources checked and failed are named with the reason.

---

## 0. The shape of the problem

Vitus is a saint with no biography and an enormous afterlife. The atlas subject is therefore not a man but a trajectory: a legend written down in the seventh century, a body dispersed from the eighth, a feast day that keeps colliding with European history.

**[A]** English Wikipedia states that "the dates of his actual life are unknown," and that Modestus and Crescentia are "probably fictitious." The narrative is described in the scholarship as purely legendary.

**Correction 1.** The research pool dated the earliest Passio to "the 6th-7th century." Italian Wikipedia dates it flatly to the seventh ("Secondo una passio del VII secolo il fanciullo siciliano Vito...", BHL 8711); English Wikipedia gives no range at all. The wider bracket was the researcher's own and is dropped. **[A, it.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vito]**

**Correction 2.** The pool posited "a later redaction" distinct from a sixth-century original. No cited source supports a two-stage textual history: Italian Wikipedia treats the seventh-century passio as *the* text, and it names the arresting official *il preside Valeriano*, that is *praeses*, a provincial governor, not a prefect. Both the phantom redaction and the wrong office title are removed. **[A]**

## 1. Sicily: birth and boyhood

**[R]** Vito is born at Mazara (Mazara del Vallo) to a pagan senator, named in the Italian tradition as Hylas, under Diocletian; conventionally placed around 290. *Wikipedia, "Saint Vitus"; Italian Wikipedia, "San Vito."*

**[R]** He is baptized and raised as a secret Christian by his nurse Crescentia and her husband Modestus, his tutor. **[A]** for the Breviary wording: "Vitus while yet a child was baptized, unknown to his father."

**[R]** Hagiography splits on his age at martyrdom, seven in some tellings and twelve in others. An unresolved split, not a range.

**[R]** As a boy he heals the blind and others, drawing the notice of the Sicilian governor, and refuses his father's demand that he sacrifice.

**[A]** The Golden Legend preserves the father's first tactic in Caxton's English: the household filled with music and company to soften him. *Textual note:* the pool's transcription reproduced a scraping artifact. christianiconography.info prints Caxton's "did do come maidens" immediately followed by an unbracketed modernizing gloss "had maidens come," so flattened scrapes double the phrase. The full Caxton reading is restored in the journey file.

**[R]** San Vito Lo Capo, on Sicily's northwestern cape, places a landing of the three fugitives there before the crossing. **[A]** for the modern observance: every 15 June the town restages *lo sbarco dei santi*, three performers coming ashore by boat at sunset.

## 2. Lucania

**[R]** The three flee by boat to Lucania, a name then covering parts of modern Basilicata and Campania, and settle beside the river Sele. Vito lives there for years and is credited with miracles before Rome hears of him. *Wikipedia, "Saint Vitus"; Vatican News, "San Vito, martire in Lucania"; Cathopedia.*

**[R]** The *preside* Valeriano has him arrested, imprisoned, and, in the telling, freed by an angel. First instance of the pattern that structures the whole passio: ordeal, then intact release.

**[A]** Golden Legend, Vito to Valerian: "I may well heal thee in the name of my Lord Jesu Christ."

---

## 3. Rome

**[R]** Summoned to exorcise a demon from the son of Diocletian; succeeds; is accused of sorcery; refuses to sacrifice; is tortured.

**[A]** Caxton calls the possessed boy "Diocletian, son of the emperor," and has the spirit say it will not leave "till the child of Lucca named Vitus was come." "Lucca" is a corruption of *Lucania*. The artifacts keep Caxton's wording verbatim in the quote field and flag the corruption in prose.

**[A]** Two further Golden Legend lines are used: "My child, take counsel in thy works and do sacrifice unto our gods to the end that thou die not an evil death," and "Alas! alas! a child hath overcome me."

**[R]** Torture sequence: cauldron of boiling pitch or molten lead, survived unmarked; lions that lie down; the gibbet. **[R]** Tradition puts the prison in the Mamertine and popular devotion puts the lions in the Colosseum. Neither is attested for Vito by any source reached, and historians dispute that the Colosseum staged Christian executions at all. Recorded as devotional geography.

**[R]** An angel frees the three and carries them back to Lucania.

## 4. Death and burial: the contradictions

**[R]** The three die beside the Sele on 15 June, the Italian tradition giving 303.

**[A]** The Roman Martyrology entry for 15 June is quoted in full in the journey file: "In Basilicata, near the river Silaro, the birthday of the holy martyrs Vitus, Modestus, and Crescentia, who were brought thither from Sicily..."

**Contradiction, unresolved.** Cathopedia carries a strand in which the matron Fiorenza buries the three not at the Sele but at *Marianus*, identified with Marigliano near Naples. The sites are about forty kilometres apart and cannot both be the grave. The Breviary names the burier "a noble lady named Florentia" and no place. **[A]** for the Breviary wording, **[R]** for both site claims.

**Contradiction, unresolved.** Marigliano claims a martyrial basilica over the burial; Polignano a Mare holds an arm bone and a knee patella; Omegna claims the body; Prague holds an arm plus the relics of 1355. Mutually exclusive, with no chain of custody joining them. A gap, not a sequence.

*Corrections applied.* The afterlife lens called Marigliano a "sealed tomb"; Italian Wikipedia says only "la chiesa di San Vito, costruita su una basilica martiriale altomedievale." The same lens gave one coordinate pair (40.8917, 14.5111) for both Marigliano and Polignano a Mare, towns 230 km apart; both are re-pinned (40.9333, 14.45 and 41.000, 17.217).

## 5. Earliest cult evidence

**[A]** The earliest documented veneration is the Martyrologium Hieronymianum line, "In Sicilia, Viti, Modesti et Crescentiae."

*Correction, and it moves the dating.* The pool placed this at "450." The Hieronymianum was compiled in northern Italy, probably Aquileia, in the 430s or 440s, but the surviving text, the one carrying the Vitus entries, is a Gallic reworking made probably at Auxerre around 600, and the oldest manuscripts are eighth-century. Dating the earliest documented veneration to 450 overstates what survives by roughly a century and a half. The atlas pins this at Auxerre, c. 600, and says why. **[A, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum]**

**[R]** The line itself was seen only secondhand in the Catholic Encyclopedia, never in a critical edition. Flagged R for that reason.

**[A]** Pope Gelasius I, in correspondence of 492-496, mentions a shrine to Vitus in Rome. Its location is given by no source reached: a gap.

## 6. The relics move north

**Contradiction, unresolved.** In 755, per the Italian tradition, King Aistulf brings relics to Pavia, to the church of San Marino, which he founded and in which he was buried. In 756, per English-language sources, Abbot Fulrad brings relics to Saint-Denis. Year and agent both differ.

*Correction:* the geography lens claimed "no source reached confirms which Pavia church held them." Italian Wikipedia names it, "nella chiesa di San Marino." The false gap is removed. What remains genuinely thin is the 1355 transfer onward to Prague.

**[A]** In 836 relics pass from Saint-Denis to Corvey in Westphalia, which becomes the distribution point for the northern cult; Vitus becomes patron of the Saxons. *Correction:* the two lenses gave Corvey different and both wrong coordinates; the artifacts use 51.7789, 9.4094.

**Contradiction, unresolved.** 925 for Henry I's gift of the hand or arm relic to Wenceslaus, 930 for the founding of the rotunda. Carried in one stop, with the disagreement in date_confidence.

## 7. Prague

**[A]** 1060: Spytihněv II replaces the rotunda with a Romanesque basilica.

**[A]** 21 November 1344: John of Bohemia lays the foundation stone of the Gothic cathedral, coinciding with Prague's elevation to an archbishopric. Charles IV intends coronation church, dynastic crypt and reliquary.

**[A]** 1355: Charles IV brings relics from Pavia.

**[A, corrected]** Matthias of Arras dies 1352. Peter Parler arrives 1356, aged twenty-three, and directs the work until his death on 13 July 1399. The pool gave "1352 to 1397," wrong at both ends, silently erasing the four-year gap. *en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Parler.*

**[A]** 12 May 1929: consecration, timed to the millennial jubilee of Wenceslaus. **[A]** 1997: the dedication is widened to Wenceslaus and Adalbert beside Vitus. The 1997 widening sits inside the 1929 stop's campa rather than taking its own pin.

## 8. The dance

**[A]** The naming runs: a German custom of dancing before Vitus's statue on his feast; chroniclers recording that dancers ended processions at his chapels and that episodes clustered around 15 June; the affliction taking his name; the name surviving in medicine as the folk term for what Thomas Sydenham described, now Sydenham's chorea. **[R]** The 1686 date is conventional and unconfirmed by anything reached.

**[A]** 24 June 1374, Aachen: one of the first great outbreaks, spreading through the Rhineland and Low Countries.

**Contradiction, unresolved.** Maastricht: the dancing-mania literature has two hundred dancers collapsing a Meuse bridge in 1278, survivors healed at a nearby Vitus chapel; Maastricht's civic record dates a collapse to 1275 and blames a procession. *Correction:* the pool pinned the Sint Servaasbrug (50.849, 5.691), which is the replacement, built north of the old Roman bridge and funded by indulgences. The bridge that fell is gone. The artifacts pin approximately and say so.

**[A, corrected place]** 1428: a monk dances himself to death at Schaffhausen and women at Zürich are seized. The pool's stop paired "Ulm and Schaffhausen" at 47.7996, 9.4995. Wikipedia's "Dancing mania" never mentions Ulm, and those coordinates match neither Schaffhausen (47.6961, 8.6349) nor Ulm nor Zürich; they land near Bad Waldsee. Pairing and pin discarded; re-pinned on Schaffhausen, with Zürich named in prose.

**[A]** July 1518, Strasbourg: the best-documented outbreak, one woman first, fifty to four hundred within weeks, several reportedly dead of exhaustion. *Two corrections.* (1) The name Frau Troffea is **not** in the cited source; it comes from John Waller's popular account. The journey leaves her unnamed and says why. (2) The afterlife lens put Strasbourg in "France"; in 1518 it was a Free Imperial City, not annexed until 1681. The chronology lens has it right and the two lenses contradicted each other.

**[A]** Seventeenth century, Drefelhausen: Gregor Horst records women making an annual pilgrimage to a Vitus chapel to dance all day and night, one for twenty years, another for thirty-two. *Correction:* Drefelhausen is in Württemberg, near Weissenstein in the territory of Ulm, not Bavaria; the pool's coordinates (48.3667, 10.3167) land near Günzburg, roughly sixty kilometres off. The artifacts use approximately 48.69, 9.80.

**[R]** Waller and other modern historians read the epidemics as mass psychogenic illness or stress response, not curse. Recorded as interpretation.

## 9. Vidovdan

**[A]** 15 June (Julian) 1389: the Battle of Kosovo falls on the feast. Murad I and Lazar both die. The day becomes Vidovdan. *Correction:* Gazimestan is at 42.6906, 21.1236, not the 42.7203, 21.1875 the pool gave twice, an error of six to seven kilometres; and the monument itself stands five to seven kilometres from the presumed battlefield, so the artifacts pin the monument and say so.

**[A]** 15 June 1876: Serbia declares war on the Ottoman Empire on Vidovdan.

**Correction, which kills the claim's point.** The pool asserted the Austro-Serbian Alliance of 16 June (Julian) 1881 coincided with Vidovdan. Wikipedia calls it "the day after Vidovdan." The stop is kept because the near-miss belongs to the pattern, and is stated as a near-miss.

**[A]** 28 June 1914: Princip shoots Franz Ferdinand on Vidovdan. *Correction:* not on the Latin Bridge. He fired from the pavement in front of Schiller's delicatessen where the motorcade turned off the Appel Quay and the car stalled. Wikipedia gives 43.8581, 18.4289. (The pool attached this correction, by a copy error, to the Prague cathedral entry's note field.)

**[A]** 28 June 1921: the Vidovdan Constitution is proclaimed. *Correction:* not at the House of the National Assembly, dedicated 18 October 1936 with construction suspended 1920-1926. Where in Belgrade it happened is established by nothing reached: a gap, stated as one, pinned on central Belgrade.

**[A]** 28 June 1989: Milošević addresses hundreds of thousands at Gazimestan on the six-hundredth anniversary.

## 10. The scattered body, the feast, the calendar

**[A]** Polignano a Mare holds an arm bone and a knee patella. **[A]** Omegna keeps in the Chiesa di Sant'Ambrogio "l'urna col corpo di san Vito martire, patrono di Omegna," a claimed whole-body relic; he is the town's adoptive patron, feasted on the last two Sundays of August. The geography lens understated this as relics "without further detail," and its coordinates are corrected to 45.8781, 8.4069.

**[R]** Marola in the Reggio Emilia Apennines likewise keeps relics under his name, undated and unexplained. The pool's coordinates were about fifteen kilometres too far north; correct is 44.4827, 10.4856. *Cut from the journey file for thinness; recorded here only.*

**[A]** San Vito Romano appears as *Castrum Sancti Viti* in the Regesto Sublacense in 1085, granted to Subiaco; its arms show the saint armed, palm in hand, dog at his side. Pool coordinates were about four kilometres west; corrected to 41.8833, 12.9833.

**[A]** Forio on Ischia has him as town patron; its mother church became a basilica in July 1988.

**[A]** Mazara del Vallo names him co-patron beside the Santissimo Salvatore in 1614 and keeps a silver statue in the Chiesa di San Michele. *Correction:* the geography lens said the statue is processed "each autumn," contradicting its own neighbouring entry; Italian Wikipedia moves the festino, once the four days before 15 June, to "tra la penultima e l'ultima settimana di agosto." The artifacts say August.

**[A]** Iconography: cauldron, rooster, lion, martyr's palm. **[A]** Patronages: dancers, actors and comedians, dogs, epileptics, the oversleeping; invoked against snakebite and storms.

**[A]** 1969: the General Roman Calendar drops Modestus and Crescentia, keeping Vitus alone on 15 June at reduced rank. **[A]** The traditional Latin propers for the day, read directly as PDF, still name all three; Collect and Postcommunion are quoted in the journey file.

## 12. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. Burial at the Sele versus at Marigliano.
2. 755 Aistulf to Pavia versus 756 Fulrad to Saint-Denis.
3. 925 relic gift versus 930 rotunda foundation at Prague.
4. Age at martyrdom, seven versus twelve.
5. Maastricht, 1278 dancers versus 1275 procession.
6. Whole-body claims at Omegna and Marigliano against dispersed relics at Polignano, Prague, Corvey.
7. Vierzehnheiligen date and coordinates differ between two existing atlas files.

## 13. Gaps stated as gaps

- Where in Rome the bones were kept between the fourth century and 756.
- Where the Gelasian shrine of the 490s stood.
- Where in Belgrade the Vidovdan Constitution was proclaimed.
- Whether the 1355 Pavia to Prague transfer is documented independently of later retellings. It is not, in anything reached.
- The Hieronymianum line was seen only secondhand, never in a critical edition.
- No landing story for Ischia to match San Vito Lo Capo's, though Forio claims him as patron.
