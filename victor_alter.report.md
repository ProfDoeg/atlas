# Victor Alter (Wiktor Alter), 1890-1943 - research report

*2026-08-29, from the verified research pool for slug `victor_alter`. Companion file: `victor_alter.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named in the same line - **[R]** reconstruction or dossier material that could not be refetched. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated; gaps are stated as gaps. Names carry no diacritics, matching the journey file, which is plain ASCII.

The dossier contained **one fabricated citation** (section 8) and **six wrong coordinates** (own section below), all caught on refetch; the journey file follows the corrections. Short names: **CJC** = Congress for Jewish Culture Yiddish lexicon, "Alter, Viktor"; **enwiki / plwiki / ruwiki** = the Wikipedia articles on Alter unless another title is given; **BN** = Polish National Library catalogue; **Glotzer** = Albert Glotzer, "The Murder of Ehrlich and Alter," March 1943, marxists.org.

---

## 1. Mlawa and Warsaw, 1890-1905

**[A]** Born 1890 in Mlawa, Plock Governorate, Russian Poland, to Yisroel Alter of a wealthy Hasidic family; **youngest of fourteen children** (CJC; enwiki).

**Contradiction, unresolved - birth date.** English-language works give 1890 with no day; plwiki gives **20 December 1890** and names the mother Syma nee Horn; the dossier's **7 February 1890** appears in nothing refetched. The file uses 7 February to place the stop and records the other. Neither is settled.

**[R]** The father as a **timber merchant** (CJC says "wealthy" and names no trade), his death when Wiktor was about one, and the 1891 Warsaw move: all uncorroborated, kept here and not in the campa.

**[A]** The mother remarried **Israel Wolf Gliksman, "a maskil and early Zionist"** (CJC): the future Bundist grew up between a Hasidic patrimony and a Zionist-leaning stepfather, and took neither.

**[R]** Attendance at the private secular **Wojciech Gorski Gymnasium**. **[A]** In 1905, at fifteen, he joined a school strike, was **briefly arrested**, and **joined the Bund** (plwiki; CJC).

**Contradiction, unresolved - the expulsion.** CJC says he lost his place at the school; plwiki's article on the gymnasium itself lists Alter among its **graduates**. The dossier's [A] tag on the expulsion is withdrawn and both versions stand.

## 2. Belgium, c. 1906-1912

**[A]** Engineering study in Belgium (enwiki, plwiki, ruwiki, CJC). **Which city, which degree, which year is a live conflict:** a polytechnic at **Liege**, electromechanical, **1910** (CJC, refetched); the **University of Ghent**, mechanical, **1912** (enwiki, ruwiki); or both cities, unspecified (plwiki).

The dossier's two lenses contradicted each other here, each tagging a different version [A]. Both tags are withdrawn and the file gives **two stops**, each stating the other exists. The **1906** start date is in no fetched source: inferred from CJC's sequence (arrest 1905, degree 1910), marked approximate.

**[R]** Marriage to **Melanie Lorraine**, a Belgian woman: CJC confirms the marriage and the name and gives no date. The 1911-1912 dating, the Brussels residence, and her death there in **1974** come from an unverified family correction in the dossier. Brussels is a placement.

## 3. Underground, Siberia, London, 1912-1917

**[A]** Return to Warsaw, a post with a Jewish cultural organization, continued illegal work (CJC). **[R]** The 1913 joint workers'-insurance electoral committee with the SDKPiL and PPS-Lewica (unrefetched). **[A]** **Arrested April 1913**, exiled to Siberia (plwiki, "aresztowany w kwietniu 1913"; enwiki; CJC).

**Gap - Narym is supported by nothing.** CJC says only "imprisoned and exiled to Siberia" and no Wikipedia article names a place. The stop is kept at the corrected coordinate but declares that it may not belong on the map at all. The escape is likewise tradition.

**[A]** From 1914 in Britain: laborer, then factory engineer; organizing Yiddish-speaking workers through **Veker**; writing for **Arbeter-Vort**; campaigning against conscription; contact with Labour's anti-militarist wing (enwiki, plwiki, CJC). No street address survives, so the London pin is the standard East End coordinate.

## 4. Revolution, 1917-1918

**[A]** Return after February, a brief Petrograd stay, **Kyiv** among his 1917 stops (CJC). **[R]** That Kyiv was the Bund's **Southern Conference**, in August, with Alter speaking for the Internationalists (dossier).

**[A]** **December 1917, Petrograd: elected to the Bund Central Committee** (plwiki, "grudzien 1917 - czlonek KC Bundu"; enwiki; CJC). **Correction:** the dossier called this the **Eighth Congress**. No fetched source uses it; the gathering is normally counted the Bund's Eighth **Conference**. Dropped from the stop name, with the reason stated rather than silently applied.

**[A]** The Bund Central Committee office in **Minsk**, the daily **Der Veker**, departure as German forces approached in 1918 (CJC).

**[R]** Moscow, July 1918: **Cheka** arrest of delegates at a non-Bolshevik workers' conference, threatened execution, release through Bolshevik intervention (dossier accounts; CJC places him in Moscow but was not seen to carry the arrest). **[R]** The essay **"Marks un di visnshaft fun geshikhte"** in *Zum ondenk fun Karl Marks* (1918), dossier bibliography. Merged into one Moscow stop.

## 5. Warsaw and Moscow, 1919-1924

**[A]** Return to Warsaw and the Polish Bund Central Committee (CJC). **Minor contradiction:** enwiki says he resided in independent Poland **from 1918**, CJC frames the Warsaw period as **1919-1939**. The file uses 1919 and warns against attributing that year to enwiki, which the dossier did.

**[A]** **Warsaw City Council 1919-1934** (CJC, verbatim) and **lawnik**, city executive, **1927-1936** (plwiki), which the dossier had tagged [R].

**[A]** **5 August 1921**: Moscow arrest over Socialist-Revolutionary correspondence, **nine-day hunger strike**, release (CJC). **[R]** The pseudonym **"M. Lorman"**, the Comintern affiliation mission, and **Sylvia Pankhurst** as addressee: dossier only, CJC carries none of it.

**Correction - two events compressed into one.** The dossier dated the Comintern congress and the arrest together, but the **Third Congress sat 22 June to 12 July 1921** (enwiki), three weeks before the arrest, so the file splits them. One find survives the split: the delegate table records the **Bund in Poland sending three representatives**, corroborating "three Warsaw delegates."

**[R]** From 1923, the central trade-union commission and the chairmanship of the national council of Jewish unions in Poland (unrefetched).

**[R]** The 1924 journey to **Mandatory Palestine** and the 1925 **Der emes vegn Palestine**. The book is real (BN); the itinerary is not, since **no source reached names a city he visited**. Jerusalem is a placement.

## 6. The alderman's decade, 1927-1938

**[A]** **1929, United States**: CJC records "Traveled to United States (1929)." The trip is attested, **the city is not**: the dossier's only trace was a 1929 press notice giving a San Francisco address, unrefetchable. The pin moves to **New York**, seat of the Bund's American representation, with the gap declared.

Bibliography, corrected against BN; the dossier's list was both misdated and incomplete.

**[A] BN catalogue:** Der emes vegn Palestine (1925) - Der sotsyalizm in kamf (1927, reissued 1930) - **Socjalizm walczacy** ("Di Welt", **1934**, and **enwiki's 1926 is an error**) - Gdy socjalisci dojda do wladzy...! (Nowe Pismo, 1934) - Hiszpania w ogniu, with Julius Deutsch (1937) - Czlowiek w spoleczenstwie / Der mentsh iz der gezelshaft (1938). **Absent from the dossier bibliography entirely**, and also held by BN: Grund-printsipien fun der proletarisher kooperatsye (1921), Tsu der yidn-frage in Poyln (1925), Arbeter-Fragn (1928), "Jednosc" i "plan" (Nowe Pismo, 1935).

**[R]:** Comment realiser le socialisme? (Paris, 1932) and Antysemityzm gospodarczy w swietle cyfr (1937), dossier only. **Esquisse d'un programme economique socialiste** (Paris, 1934) is in **neither BN nor OpenLibrary** and is unverifiable.

**[R]** The 1935 unity resolution, the failed talks with the Communist Party of Poland, and **Mysl Socjalistyczna** (1935-1938), unrefetched.

**[A]** **1936: "elected head of Warsaw Jewish community administration from 1936"** (CJC). The dossier understated this as board membership and tagged it [R].

**[R]** The 1937 journey to **Republican Spain**: the book is attested by catalogue, but **where he went inside Spain is not established**, so Madrid is a placement.

## 7. September 1939 and the Soviet prisons

**[R]** The leadership's flight from Warsaw, the Central Committee's reversal, and Alter learning of it on **9 September** (dossier).

**[A]** **Lublin**: a single wartime issue of the **Lubliner Shtime** under bombardment (CJC), the 14 September date being the dossier's. **[A]** **Kowel, 17 September 1939**, the day Soviet forces entered eastern Poland, and NKVD arrest there (CJC; enwiki; plwiki).

**Contradiction, unresolved - the arrest date.** plwiki **26 September**, enwiki **29 September**. The stop is dated to the arrival and carries both.

**[R]** Transfer through **Luck** before Moscow: confirmed by nothing reached, kept and flagged as the thinnest leg in the record.

**[A]** **Moscow prisons, September 1939 to September 1941** (CJC, verbatim), in **Butyrki from 9 December 1939** (plwiki); the dossier pinned this at the Lubyanka, 3.4 km away. **[R]** The prison essay on modern physics, **Di naye fizik**, is in nothing fetched and is never staged as a scene.

**[A]** **20 July 1941: sentenced to death** by the Military Collegium of the USSR Supreme Court (plwiki; CJC).

**Gap - the venue.** No fetched source says where sentence was pronounced, and the parallel cuts against Moscow: **Erlich's death sentence was handed down at Saratov** (enwiki, "Henryk Erlich"). The dossier cited Glotzer, who gives neither date nor place. The stop sits at Alter's known place of confinement and says outright that this is not an attested courtroom.

**Contradiction, unresolved - commutation.** plwiki **22 July 1941**; other sources **30 July**. Both recorded.

**[A]** **Released 13 September 1941** under the Sikorski-Mayski amnesty (plwiki, enwiki), with a formal apology, a Moscow hotel, clothing and medical care, and the offer of an international Jewish anti-Hitler committee, Erlich chairman and Alter secretary (Glotzer).

**[R]** Work with ambassador **Stanislaw Kot** on relief and on Jewish recruitment into **Anders's** army, from the two men's memoirs, unrefetched. **They are said to disagree on Alter's position regarding segregated Jewish units**, and the disagreement is carried, not resolved.

## 8. Kuibyshev, 1941-1943

**[A]** Evacuation with the Polish embassy to **Kuibyshev** (Glotzer). **Contradiction:** the dossier has the two sharing a hotel room with **Lucjan Blit** under surveillance; enwiki, "Henryk Erlich", has them **residing at the Polish embassy** when re-arrested. Blit and the surveillance appear in nothing fetched, and the campa keeps both versions.

**[R]** The joint letter of **31 October 1941** endorsing a single non-segregated army (unrefetched).

**Correction - the re-arrest date.** The dossier gave **3 December 1941**, tagged [A] on Glotzer, who says only that in December 1941 the two "were called to pay a visit to the Commissariat of the Interior. They never came back!" - no date, no twenty-hour wait. **Four independent pages** (enwiki and plwiki on Erlich, plwiki and ruwiki on Alter) give **4 December 1941**, which the file uses; the twenty-hour wait is demoted to tradition.

**[R]** **Erlich's death in custody, night of 14-15 May 1942**, recorded in post-Soviet files as suicide by hanging (Rudnicki and Mochalova, via the dossier). Whether Alter was told is recorded nowhere.

**[A]** **Shot at Kuibyshev, 17 February 1943, without trial** (plwiki: "17 lutego 1943 ... rozstrzelany w wiezieniu w Kujbyszewie bez sadu"; ruwiki).

**The fabricated citation.** The dossier attributed that date to **CJC**. Refetched, **CJC says the opposite**: both men were "arrested in Kuibyshev and shot December 1941 (initially reported as December 1942)." The date is genuine, the citation was not, and CJC turns out to be a **witness for the old Soviet story**.

**[A]** **23 February 1943**: ambassador **Maksim Litvinov** tells AFL president **William Green** the two had been tried and executed (enwiki, "Henryk Erlich"; Glotzer). **Correction - the order of the false accounts.** The dossier had Litvinov giving December 1942 first and a later message moving it to December 1941. **Glotzer, writing at the time, records the reverse**: reports had circulated of an execution "more than a year ago," and Litvinov's letter corrected this to "only four months previous." The campa asserts no direction of travel.

**Quote used.** "spying for Hitler", the Soviet 1943 charge in enwiki's English rendering. The underlying Soviet statement **could not be reached** (see Sources), so `quote_source` declares it a tertiary transcription.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A]** **17 April 1988**: a symbolic grave for Erlich and Alter unveiled at the **Okopowa Street Jewish Cemetery** against the wishes of the Communist government, driven by **Marek Edelman** with people from **Solidarity**, before more than three thousand people (enwiki and plwiki on both men). The dossier had this [R]; it is [A].

**Quote used, with two corrections.** The inscription reads *"Leaders of the Bund, Henryk Ehrlich, b. 1882, and Wiktor Alter, b. 1890. Executed in the Soviet Union"* - spelled **Ehrlich**, verified character-for-character against the raw wikitext of enwiki "Henryk Ehrlich" via Special:Export. The dossier's claim that **two** Wikipedia articles reproduce it identically is **false**: only the Ehrlich article carries it, citing **Victor Erlich, *Child of a Turbulent Century* (2006), pp. 93-97**, a memoir by Henryk Ehrlich's son. Two caveats in `quote_source`: **no photograph or independent transcription of the stone was located**, and the inscription is presumably in Polish or Yiddish, making the English an **unattributed translation**.

**Contradiction, unresolved - rehabilitation.** plwiki: **13 November 1990**, **Prosecutor's Office of the USSR**. enwiki: **8 February 1991**, attributed to **Yeltsin**. Both dates verified as reported, so the conflict is real. The English attribution is separately **anachronistic**: Yeltsin was not elected President of Russia until 12 June 1991, and a February 1991 act would have been Soviet. The file uses 13 November 1990, records the other date, and refuses the Yeltsin attribution.

**Posthumous editions, against the dossier's "none found".** OpenLibrary shows the memorial volume **"Henrik Erlikh un Viktor Alter"**, 1943 (Di Bundishe grupe, and a J. S. Hertz edition for the Bund's American Representation) and again 1951 (Unzer Tsayt), plus **"The case of Henryk Erlich and Victor Alter"** (1943).

**Gaps.** No street, plaque, gallery, or monument to Alter alone, beyond the shared cenotaph, was found: an absence of evidence in a small set, not a finding about the world. Modern rediscovery events could not be checked, and the YIVO **RG 1459** collection **could not be confirmed**.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Alter is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stop is licensed here, and nothing was silently deleted. Two relations in the pool are not rooted in his own travel, and both run **forward**, which the exception never licenses:

1. **Litvinov's announcement, Washington D.C., 23 February 1943.** The pool gave it a coordinate in two lenses, one noting in its own text that Alter "himself was never in Washington for this." It is Litvinov's act at Litvinov's location. **Not staged as a stop**; folded into the campa and `suggested_refs` of Alter's own execution stop.
2. **The Buenos Aires memorial volume**, Jose Horn's *Henrik Erlikh un Viktor Alter* (1943), for which the pool offered the corpus-standard Buenos Aires pin. Alter never went to Argentina and was dead before the book existed. **Not staged as a stop**; if it is to be mapped, it belongs on a file for whoever published it.

Also dropped as milieu, not relation: the pool's `max_perelman` item, which its own note calls "adjacent milieu, not a documented interlock."

## Interlocks: who is named in a campa, and why

Shared geography is never a reason to name another traveler: the map already shows co-location. Two names survived.

- **Vladimir Medem** (`vladimir_medem.journey.json`), at the December 1917 Petrograd stop. Not because both were in Warsaw: Medem is the Bund's theorist of **national cultural autonomy**, got it adopted as party policy at Warsaw in 1912, and sat on the Central Committee Alter joined, and Alter then administered that doctrine for twenty years. No fetched source documents a personal meeting and the campa claims none.
- **Vladimir Lenin** (`vladimir_lenin.journey.json`), at the June 1921 Comintern stop. Not because both were in Moscow: Alter traveled there **to negotiate the Bund's dissolution into Lenin's International on Lenin's conditions** and carried home the refusal. That is a relation, not a coincidence of place.

Deliberately **not** named, though the pool offered all four: **Rosa Luxemburg**, **Leon Trotsky**, **Rudolf Rocker**, **George Orwell**. Each shares a city, a prison, a milieu, or a war with Alter and nothing else the record documents.

Pins inherited byte-identical: **Warsaw 52.2297, 21.0122** - **Paris 48.8566, 2.3522** - **Butyrka 55.78444, 37.59389** - **New York 40.7128, -74.006**.
