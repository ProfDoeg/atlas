# Isabelle Eberhardt (1877-1904): research report

*Slug `isabelle_eberhardt`. Compiled 2026-08. Approved on QUEUE.md line 355 as "Geneva to the Sahara as Si Mahmoud: conversion, initiation, death by desert flood."*

Born illegitimate in Geneva, raised by an anarchist ex-priest in six languages and boys' clothes; in Algeria from twenty as a convert and Qadiriyya initiate travelling as Si Mahmoud Saadi; sabred at twenty-three; expelled, readmitted by marriage; intelligencer to Lyautey; dead at twenty-seven in a flood; published by a man who put his own name beside hers.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved; circulating claims shown to be wrong are named and corrected with a source.

## 1. Geneva, 1877-1897

**Birth, 17 February 1877**, Grottes quarter, registered as the illegitimate daughter of Nathalie Moerder, née Eberhardt **[A: EN and FR Wikipedia]**. FR Wikipedia, "Les Grottes" locates it: *rue de la Pépinière, dans la villa Fendt, détruite par la suite* **[A]**. The raw coordinate (46.2081, 6.1421) was ~200 m off; **corrected to 46.2094, 6.1400** (OSM Nominatim).

**The household.** Around **1871** Nathalie left her husband Pavel de Moerder for **Alexandre Trophimowsky**, the Armenian ex-Orthodox-priest-turned-atheist-anarchist she had hired as family tutor **[A: EN Wikipedia]**.
- *Correction:* the circulating description of de Moerder as a **"civil-service general" is unsupported**. EN Wikipedia calls him a Russian general some forty years Nathalie's senior and does not qualify the rank. Encyclopedia.com omits him entirely and calls Nathalie "a Prussian aristocrat," a second, unreconciled account of her origins. **Flagged, not resolved.**
- Brother **Augustin**: EN Wikipedia "around 1872"; FR Wikipedia's list of the nine children gives **1871**. **[A for existence, contradictory for the year]**; carried as "c. 1871-1872" and never pinned.

**Education.** Home-schooled, no school and no church, and raised in male clothing from early childhood, unopposed **[A]**. The language list is *inconsistent between sources*: EN Wikipedia gives French, Russian, German, Italian, Latin, Greek and classical Arabic; FR Wikipedia adds Turkish. The journey follows EN and does not claim Turkish.

**1888.** Half-sister Nathalie marries against Trophimowsky's wishes and is cut off from the household **[A: EN Wikipedia]**.

**The Letord correspondence.** EN Wikipedia: "Sometime **prior to 1894**, Eberhardt began corresponding with Eugène Letord, a French officer stationed in the Sahara who had placed a newspaper advertisement for a pen pal" **[A]**.
- *Correction:* the raw chronology dated the *start* to 1894. 1894 is a **ceiling**. The journey pins 1893 with the ceiling stated in `date_confidence`. The personal ad is attested and kept.

**1894.** Augustin, in debt and entangled with Russian revolutionary circles, flees Geneva and enlists in the **Foreign Legion**, posted to Algeria **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. His garrison is **[R]**: Sidi Bel Abbès was the Legion's principal Algerian depot, but no consulted source names his posting; the pin is tagged as reconstruction.

**1895.** First story in *La Nouvelle Revue Moderne* as **Nicolas Podolinsky**, at eighteen **[A: FR and EN Wikipedia]**. **Louis David** takes the sailor-uniform portrait **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. No month for either; the journey's months are declared placeholders.

## 2. Bône, 1897-1899

**May 1897.** Eberhardt and her mother move to **Bône (Annaba)**, lodging first with the Davids, then renting an Arabic-style house on the **rue Moreau** outside the European quarter **[A: EN and FR Wikipedia]**. She dresses as a man, converts, and takes the name **Si Mahmoud Saadi** **[A]**.

**November 1897.** Nathalie dies of a heart attack and is buried as a convert under the name **Fatma Mannoubia**; Trophimowsky arrives after the burial and shows his daughter no sympathy **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. *Gap:* the cemetery plot is unlocated; city-centre coordinates used.

*No source dates the adoption of the name* to a day or month; it belongs to the 1897-1899 window and is carried as traditional. *Spelling, unresolved:* sources alternate **Saadi** / **Essadi** and **Slimène** / **Slimane**; the journey is internally consistent, which is an editorial choice, not an adjudication.

## 3. The Aurès, Geneva, Paris, 1899-1900

- **1899, Batna**, in the Aurès, frequenting Muslim mystical circles **[A: FR Wikipedia]**.
- **May 1899, Geneva**: returns with Augustin as Trophimowsky dies of throat cancer **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. The estate is contested, sold in **1901**, and legal costs leave her nothing **[A]**.
- **1899**: briefly engaged to **Riza Bey**, an Armenian diplomat; it ends **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. No month, no reason given.
- **Paris and the de Morès commission.** *Correction:* the raw chronology's **"1900-01" is invented precision.** EN Wikipedia gives no date for the Paris arrival or the commission, only "While in Paris Eberhardt met the widow of Marquis de Morès" and "She returned to Algeria in July 1900." The journey pins 1900-01-01 and declares the month a placeholder for "early 1900." The commission itself, investigating the Marquis's murder in the Sahara for his widow, is **[A: EN Wikipedia]**.

## 4. El Oued and Béhima, 1900-1901

**July 1900**, settles at **El Oued** in the Souf **[A]**. *Correction:* the geography lens had her there from **1899**, which is impossible; in 1899 she was at Batna, then Geneva for the May death, then Paris. Window: **1900-1901**.

**Slimène Ehnni**, non-commissioned officer of the spahis and fellow Qadiriyya member, met at El Oued in 1900 **[A: EN Wikipedia]**, no month. **Qadiriyya initiation** by **Sheikh Hussein ben Brahim**, without the usual formal examination **[A: EN Wikipedia]**, no date beyond 1900.

**Lalla Zaynab.** From Algiers she visits the **Rahmaniyya** zawiya at El Hamel, describing Zaynab as a woman of great simplicity with sadness behind the intelligence, and reports feeling rejuvenated **[A: EN Wikipedia, "Lalla Zaynab"]**.
- *Correction:* raw coordinates (35.3333, 4.3167) were **~32 km wrong and on the wrong side of Bou Saâda**; FR Wikipedia "El Hamel" gives **35.133, 4.083**, 10 km south-west. Corrected.
- *Kept:* this lodge is **Rahmaniyya**, not the Qadiriyya she joined; the journey does not blur the orders. *Gap:* the visits are dated only to the c. 1900-1903 window.

**Touggourt** is an explicit **[R]** waypoint: plausible on her Souf itinerary, confirmed by nothing.

**29 January 1901, Béhima.** Sabred by **Abdallah ben Mohammed** at a Qadiriyya gathering where she was seeking money from the marabout **Si Lachmi**; superficial head wound, left arm cut to the bone **[A: EN and FR Wikipedia]**. FR Wikipedia gives 29 January; EN Wikipedia only "late January 1901." **Flagged; FR preferred, both recorded.**
- *Correction:* raw coordinates (33.28, 6.77) put Béhima **south-west** of El Oued. FR Wikipedia "Hassani Abdelkrim" gives 33°28'38"N, 6°53'42"E = **33.477, 6.895**, **east** of the wilaya capital. Corrected.
- *Caveat:* the FR article on Hassani Abdelkrim does **not** itself say the commune was formerly Béhima; that equivalence comes only from the Eberhardt articles. Declared in `date_confidence`.
- **Motive genuinely disputed:** EN Wikipedia records a rival-marabout-order theory and a French-instigation theory and confirms neither. **Not resolved.**
- **Sentence:** life, commuted to ten years after she advocated leniency **[A: Encyclopedia.com]**; EN Wikipedia says only "a life sentence." Compatible if the commutation followed; both named.

**February 1901**, recovery at the El Oued military hospital, Qadiriyya members treating her survival as a miracle and funding her **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. **May 1901**, ordered out of North Africa without explanation **[A]**.

## 5. Marseille and Constantine, 1901-1902

- **May 1901**, Marseille: stays with Augustin, works the docks disguised as a man **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. **June 1901**, Constantine: testifies, says she bears her attacker no grudge, is expelled again **[A: EN Wikipedia]**.
- **17 October 1901**, Marseille: marries **Slimène Ehnni**, acquiring French legal status **[A]**. FR Wikipedia gives the precise date, EN Wikipedia only "October 1901." **Flagged; FR preferred.**
- **February 1902**: Ehnni discharged, the couple return to **Bône** **[A]**.

## 6. Al-Akhbar and the coast, 1902-1903

- **March 1902**: joins **Al-Akhbar** under **Victor Barrucand** **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. **July 1902**: to **Ténès**, where Ehnni is a translator **[A]**.
- **1902-1904**: chronic malaria, probable syphilis, heavy hashish and tobacco use, chosen poverty **[A: EN Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]**. An ongoing condition, not a dated event; attached to the Ténès stop with no claimed date.
- **August 1903**: *Trimardeur* begins its serial run **[A]**.

## 7. The Moroccan frontier, 1903-1904: the largest error in the pool

**The raw chronology had her reporting on the Battle of El-Moungar on 2 September 1903 and meeting Lyautey there. Both halves are wrong.**

1. EN Wikipedia says Barrucand dispatched her to report on the **aftereffects** of the 2 September battle; she arrived after the fact **[A]**.
2. **Lyautey was not yet in the region.** EN Wikipedia, "Battle of El-Moungar": as a direct consequence of the losses, "the commander of the Ain Sefra military region was replaced and Brigadier General Hubert Lyautey was appointed in his place" **[A]**. He took the command *after* 2 September.
3. The **first meeting** is dated "late 1903, arranged through journalist Victor Barrucand's introduction" (Encyclopedia.com) and **November 1903 at Béni Ounif** (FR Wikipedia) **[A]**. Compatible; the journey uses the latter and names both.

**El-Moungar coordinates: an honest failure.** The raw pin (32.62, -1.35) is ~200 km out: El Moungar lies in the **Zousfana valley** of Béchar province near Taghit (30.917, -2.029, OSM Nominatim), not in the Aïn Séfra to Béni Ounif corridor. **EN Wikipedia's own infobox coordinate (35°26'N, 4°03'E) is also wrong**, landing ~700 km from the frontier. **No reliable coordinate was locatable.** The stop is kept at Taghit and declared a reconstruction: the weakest pin in the file.

**Béni Ounif coordinates.** Raw pin (32.6167, -1.3833) was ~63 km wrong; FR Wikipedia "Beni Ounif" gives 32°03'N, 1°15'W = **32.05, -1.25**. Corrected.

**Kenadsa.** The stay at the **Ziania** zawiya, basis of her longest late piece of writing, rests on **her own published Saharan writing**; the EN Wikipedia article on Kenadsa does not mention her, and no independently dated source was reachable. Carried as **[R]**.

**1904, the marabout mission.** Lyautey sends her into the mountains to meet an influential marabout, and she returns weakened by fever: **[A]** for the mission (EN Wikipedia), **[R]** for the location, named nowhere reachable. The Ksour-range coordinate is a declared placeholder.

## 8. Aïn Séfra, October 1904

- **October**: admitted to the military hospital, leaves against medical advice **[A]**, no day given. **20 October**: reunited with Ehnni, renting a small mud house **[A]**.
- **21 October**: a flash flood strikes; the house collapses and she is crushed under a supporting beam, aged 27 **[A]**. *Contradiction kept open:* biographers disagree on whether she tried to flee or accepted her fate (EN Wikipedia leaves it unresolved). The journey settles nothing.
- **27 October**: the body is recovered, six days on **[A: FR Wikipedia only; EN Wikipedia gives no recovery date]**.
- **Late October**: Lyautey orders troops to search the ruins; manuscript fragments are recovered from the mud and handed to **Barrucand** **[A: Encyclopedia.com, EN Wikipedia]**. *Gap:* **no source gives a page count**, saved or lost.
- **Burial** in the Muslim cemetery of **Sidi Boudjemâa** under a marble stone commissioned by Lyautey, engraved with her adopted Arabic name and her birth name in French **[A]**.
  - *Correction:* the afterlife lens's coordinate (32.7509, -0.5747) sits ~1 km **east** of the centre, contradicting both Wikipedias, which place the cemetery **west**, and the geography lens's own 32.745, -0.595. Harmonised on the **westward** offset, declared approximate.

## 9. Afterlife

**First posthumous book, *Dans l'ombre chaude de l'Islam*.** *Contradiction resolved, working shown:* EN Wikipedia says 1906, FR Wikipedia 1921, Encyclopedia.com November 1905, Open Library 1908. The **BnF catalogue** lists Eberhardt & Barrucand (Fasquelle) editions of **1906 (first)**, 1908, 1911, 1921, 1923. **1906 is the first**; FR Wikipedia's 1921 is a reissue, Encyclopedia.com's 1905 unsupported, Open Library incomplete **[A: BnF]**.

**Barrucand's editing.** He credited himself as co-author and did not distinguish his additions and rewrites from her text, and was criticised for it at the time **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. The central textual problem of her corpus, stated rather than smoothed over.

**Later run [A: FR Wikipedia]:** *Notes de route* (1908), *Mes Journaliers* (1923), *Au Pays des sables* (1925, first compiled as *Contes et souvenirs*), *Pages d'Islam* (1932, préface Barrucand), *Amours nomades* (2003, Losfeld, from Aix-en-Provence archives). *Correction:* the raw chronology capped the run at **1925**; Barrucand's sequence runs to **1932**, publication to **2003**.

**Mackworth.** *Correction:* **1951**, not 1954. Open Library records *The Destiny of Isabelle Eberhardt*, Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1951 (later editions 1975-1986); EN Wikipedia's 1954 is an error the raw research inherited. The biography prompted **Paul Bowles** to translate her into English **[A: EN Wikipedia]**.

**Commemoration.** Streets in **Béchar**, **Algiers** and the **Grottes** quarter of Geneva, and a memorial garden on the **Avenue Foch**, Paris 16e, between nos. 46 and 50 **[A: EN and FR Wikipedia]**. None is dated by any source; the journey's date there is a declared placeholder.
- *Correction:* the Geneva street coordinate (46.2072, 6.1367) was ~460 m off; OSM Nominatim gives **46.21137, 6.13683**. FR Wikipedia is **internally inconsistent** here: its "Les Grottes" street list omits the street its Eberhardt article asserts. OSM preferred, the disagreement recorded.

**Adaptations [A]:** Bayer (1976), Wertenbaker's *New Anatomies* (1981), Kobak's biography (1988), Pringle's film (1991, Mathilda May and Peter O'Toole), Mazzoli's opera *Song from the Uproar* (March 2012, The Kitchen, New York), Greiner (2018).
- *Correction:* **Ferrandez's *Carnets d'Orient* did not begin in 1992.** FR Wikipedia dates the initial narrative to 1986 in *Corto Maltese*, the series to 1991 in *(À suivre)*, and **names no Eberhardt volume**. Its status as an adaptation is **[R]**; the journey omits it.

**Legacy reading [R].** Scholarship recasts her as an early feminist and anti-colonial figure while questioning the contradiction between those sympathies and her material dependence on **Lyautey**, who paid her, buried her and recovered her manuscripts (EN Wikipedia, synthesising unnamed commentary). Tagged **[R]** because no historian is named. The journey states the arrangement and resolves nothing.

## 10. Quotes

**Verified [A], used:** four diary lines from *The Nomad: The Diaries of Isabelle Eberhardt*, trans. de Voogd, ed. Kabbani (Virago 1987), seen verbatim on Goodreads, GradeSaver, MagicalQuote and QuoteCatalog. **Caveat: aggregator pages, not the printed book; no page numbers, no entry dates.** Also "It is impossible for me to do otherwise" (Abdel-Jaouad 1993, p. 110); "God wished it; God still wishes it" (Mackworth 1977, p. 111); the "drank more than a Legionnaire" line and the "peters out into a wasteland" street description (Blanch 2010, pp. 266, 271), all cited on EN Wikipedia.

**Retagged R to A.** Lyautey's "a rebel" line, circulating uncited and misworded, traces to Jamie James, *The Glamour of Strangeness* (FSG, 2016), which supplies the provenance: **a letter from Lyautey to Barrucand after her death**. It ends "what attracts me most in **the world**: a rebel," not "in this world."

**Corrected before use.** "Soon, the solitary, woeful figure that I am will vanish from this earth" **truncates the sentence**, inviting a death-premonition reading. Per Emma Garman, *The Paris Review*, 11 February 2019, it continues "**where I have always been a spectator, an outsider among men.**" The full sentence is used and the framing "written shortly before her death" dropped as unsupported.

**Rejected [R], unused:** "It is the inescapable chain of events..." (Literary Ladies Guide, uncited); "two lives, one that is full of adventure..." (Women in Exploration timeline, uncited).

## 11. Interlocks

**Named in a campa (one): Emir Abdelkader** (`abdelkader.journey.json`), at the El Oued initiation. The link is the **order**, not the map: she enters the Qadiriyya, whose Algerian shaykh-line is Abdelkader's family, his father heading its zawiya on the Wadi al-Hammam. **[R]** in the pool, taken here as an indirect relation through a named institution.

**Found and not named,** being shared geography, century or rhyme, which the map already shows: **Guenon**, **David-Neel**, **Rousseau**, **Flaubert**, **Saint-Exupery**, **Ibn Battuta**, **Dihya**, **Emin Arslan**, and **Bakunin**, whose Geneva emigre circle the lens attaches to Trophimowsky on no documented contact, and which concerns her guardian rather than her.

**The Kaaba pin (21.4225, 39.8262)**, inherited by Abdelkader and Guenon: **no hajj by Eberhardt is attested**, the lens itself calls it "unattested," and **no stop is placed there**.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Geneva 46.2044, 6.1432; Paris 48.8566, 2.3522; Constantine 36.365, 6.6147; Tangier 35.7595, -5.834; Algiers 36.7538, 3.0588.

## 12. Gaps, stated as gaps

No usable El-Moungar coordinate exists anywhere reachable, and EN Wikipedia's own is wrong. Augustin's Legion garrison is unknown, Sidi Bel Abbes being an institutional inference. Nathalie's grave plot at Annaba and the Sidi Boudjemaa plot are unlocated. The 1904 marabout mission has no named destination. The Kenadsa visit rests on her own writing alone; the Lalla Zaynab visits have only the c. 1900-1903 window; the Paris months of 1899-1900 are undetailed; the streets are undated; no page count exists for the manuscripts saved or lost.

**No primary text was consulted:** no printed diary edition, no archival material, no monograph read directly. Every quotation from her own writing reaches this report through a translated edition seen only on aggregator pages, and every citation to Mackworth, Kobak, Abdel-Jaouad or Blanch through Wikipedia's footnotes. That is the largest limitation here.
