# John Dewey (1859-1952): research report

*2026-08-29. Prepared for `john_dewey.journey.json`. Legend: **[A]** attested, source named; **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or uncorroborated dossier claim, carried rather than dropped.*

The spine is one document: **Barbara Levine, comp., *Chronology of John Dewey's Life and Work*, Center for Dewey Studies, SIU Carbondale, 2016** (`deweycenter.siu.edu/_common/documents/chrono.pdf`), cited as **CDS** with its own entry keys (`1884.06.05`). It is a dated register built on letters, newspapers, regents' proceedings, death certificates, and church rolls, printing its sources per line. Where the dossier and CDS disagree, CDS wins and the disagreement is recorded, not smoothed. Several dossier coordinates were wrong by hundreds of metres to thousands of kilometres; corrections are listed below.

---

## 1. Burlington and Vermont (1859-1882)

Born **20 October 1859**, 186 South Willard Street, to Lucina Rich and Archibald Sprague Dewey [A 1859.10.20]; the house was bought that August [A 1859.08.20]. An elder brother John Archibald, b. 1856, died **18 January 1859**, and the name was reused nine months later [A 1859.01.18, citing the *Daily Free Press*]. *Dossier correction:* it sourced the dead brother to 1859.08.20, which is the house purchase, not the death. The nine-month interval itself checks out.

**1864**, the family moves to Cumberland, Virginia, which Jane Dewey called "the last winter of the war" [A 1864, citing correspondence 1933.07.29 and Coughlan]. No month, no house-level site. **September 1867**, District School No. 3, later North Grammar School, the family on George Street to 1876 [A 1867.09, 1867-1876]. **September 1875**, enters the University of Vermont at fifteen, philosophy privately with Torrey [A 1875.09]; **25 June 1879**, A.B., oration *Limits of Political Economy*, Phi Beta Kappa [A 1879.06.25, *Burlington Free Press Times* 26 June 1879].

**1879 to June 1881**, assistant principal at Oil City High School, Pennsylvania [A 1879-1881.06, citing Ryan and Coughlan]. No arrival month is given, and the journey file dates that stop September 1879 to hold travel order, saying so in `date_confidence`. **1881-1882**, principal at Lake View Seminary, Charlotte, Vermont, weekends with Torrey [A 1881-1882, *Gazetteer of Chittenden County* 1882-83]. Roberta Lowitz, his second wife, was born at Oil City in 1904 [A 1946.12.11], twenty-three years after he left.

## 2. Baltimore (1882-1884)

Graduate student at Johns Hopkins, lodging at 66 and later 91 Saratoga Street, among Morris, G. Stanley Hall, and C. S. Peirce [A 1882-1884]. **5 June 1884**, Ph.D. [A 1884.06.05, *Johns Hopkins University Circulars* 3, no. 31: 119]. The dissertation, **The Psychology of Kant, is lost**: no published text survives [A: CDS's own note, verified negatively against the *Early Works*].

## 3. Michigan and Minnesota (1884-1894)

**19 July 1884**, instructor in philosophy at Michigan [A 1884.07.19, *Proceedings of the Regents* June 1884, 482]. **2 November 1884**, joins the First Congregational Church of Ann Arbor, the roll recording him "dismissed without letter" on 30 March 1898 [A 1884.11.02, Membership Roll 1847-1906]. **28 July 1886**, marries Harriet Alice Chipman at the Fenton house of her grandparents the Riggses [A 1886.07.28, *Ann Arbor Courier* 4 Aug 1886]. **1887**, *Psychology*, and on 19 July son Frederick Archibald born at Fenton [A 1887, 1887.07.19].

**February 1888 to 1889**, chair of Mental and Moral Philosophy and Logic at Minnesota, with daughter Evelyn Riggs born Minneapolis 5 March 1889 [A 1888.02, 1888-1889, 1889.03.05]. **19 April 1889**, accepts the Michigan chair after Morris's death [A 1889.04.19]. **10 April 1891**, his father dies of heart failure in Ann Arbor [A 1891.04.10, county death certificate, *U. of M. Daily* 22 April 1891]. **Summer 1891**, Adler's summer school of applied ethics at Plymouth, with William James and Dewey both on the roster [R: `william_james.journey.json` states the co-presence, `felix_adler.journey.json` corroborates the roster while flagging both men's participation as reported rather than documented].

**Contradiction corrected (dossier error).** The pool claimed Frederick Archibald died in childhood at about eight. False: CDS 1967.07.28 has him dying in New York at seventy-nine (*NYT*, 29 July 1967, 25). The son who died at about eight was **Gordon Chipman Dewey** (b. Chicago 1896, typhoid, hospitalised at Ballinasloe, died in Ireland 10 or 11 September 1904, memorial at the Hull-House Theatre on 1 October) [A 1896.05.29, 1904.09.10, .10.01].

## 4. Chicago (1892-1904)

**Hull House, 800 South Halsted Street.** CDS lists Dewey with the board of trustees at **1889**, at board meetings in July and October **1897**, resigning the board on 14 April **1903** for a year on the advisory council, and in 1930 speaking "representing early Hull-House Trustees" [A 1889, 1892.01.21, 1897.07.27, .10.12, 1903.04.14, 1930.05.10].

- **Contradiction, unresolved.** The dossier claimed a formal 1897 election to a "Hull-House Association" board. That phrase occurs **nowhere** in CDS, and there is no 1897 election entry, only the two attendances. The trustee tie runs from 1889, the year Hull House opened and a year Dewey lived in Ann Arbor: either the listing is retrospective or he was a non-resident trustee from the start, and CDS does not say which. Stated in `date_confidence`.

**19 March 1894**, accepts the head of the combined department of philosophy, psychology, and pedagogy [A 1894, 1894.03.19].

- **Contradiction corrected.** The dossier attached a **$5,000 salary** to Chicago. The only $5,000 in CDS is the **Columbia** offer of 1904.05.02; no Chicago figure appears anywhere. Removed from the Chicago stop.

**January 1896**, the University Elementary School opens, sixteen pupils, Alice principal [A 1896; DePencier]. **July 1896**, "The Reflex Arc Concept in Psychology" [A 1896]. **1897**, "My Pedagogic Creed" [A 1897, verified at Wikisource]. **1899**, *The School and Society* and the APA presidency [A SEP, CDS]. **5 April 1904**, resigns effective 1 July after the Laboratory School was merged with Francis Parker's school and Alice forced out, her own resignation effective 1 October [A 1904.04.05, *University Record* May 1904, 35].

- **Interpretation flagged, not adjudicated.** The standard "Harper fired his wife" telling omits documented administrative disorder on several sides; the journey file states merger, exit, and resignation, and awards no blame.

## 5. New York and Columbia (1904-1918)

**2 and 9 May 1904**, offered and accepts the Columbia chair at $5,000, from 1 February 1905 [A 1904.05.02, .05.09, *Journal of Philosophy* 1 (12 May 1904): 280]. **1910** *How We Think*, **1916** *Democracy and Education*, **1925** *Experience and Nature* from the 1922 Carus Lectures [A SEP, CDS]. **February 1913**, Columbia's welcome gift to Henri Bergson is a bibliography of his writings introduced by Dewey [A `henri_bergson.journey.json`, corroborated by Dewey's dossier, which also places a New York meeting that month]. The dossier's further claim of a 1913-1920 lecture series grouping James, Russell, and Bergson is [R].

## 6. Japan and China (1919-1921)

**23 January 1919**, leaves San Francisco on the *Shinyo Maru*; **9 February**, Yokohama; the Imperial Hotel, the Hoashi interview, then Nitobe's house; Kyoto and Kobe in April, then the *Kumano Maru* for China [A 1919.01.23, .02.09, .02.10-11, .04.22-28]. **30 April**, Shanghai, four days before the May Fourth demonstrations, lecturing at the Kiangsu Educational Association building near the old West Gate [A 1919.04.30, *Millard's Review* 8 (3 May 1919): 365; 1919.05.03-04]. **9 May**, dinner with Hu Shih, Jiang Menglin, and Sun Yat-sen [A 1919.05.09, citing Zou, "The Dewey Fever," 46].

- **Contradiction, unresolved.** CDS gives **no location** for that dinner. "In Hangchow" is an inference from the 5-9 May window. Against it: CDS puts him in Shanghai on 11 May, records a second, separate dinner with Sun Yat-sen on 12 May, and Sun was resident in Shanghai through 1919. The journey file keeps the Hangzhou pin and states in `date_confidence` that the location is inferred and the Shanghai reading is live.

Then Nanking (nineteen lectures from the Sophists to Plato), Peking from 30 May 1919 in the YMCA compound and later 135 Morrison Street, Tientsin, Mukden, Canton, and the farewell address of June 1921 [A 1919.05.14, .05.30, .07.28, .09.04, .11.02-09, 1920.04.04-06, 1921.04.28-05.09, 1921.06]. **28 September 1921**, back in New York after two years and two months [A 1921.09.28]. Reception: Mr. Democracy, Mr. Science, the American Confucius [A Wikipedia]. The later arc, Maoist denunciation after 1949 and post-Mao revival, is **[R]**: not retraceable to a campaign document within budget. One sentence of it, the part inside his lifetime, sits in the campa for the 1921 return.

## 7. The surveys and the late books (1924-1938)

**Turkey, summer 1924**: Constantinople, Bursa, two weeks in Angora, the congress of the Union of Teachers [A 1924.06, .07, .08.01-02, .08.22; Wolf-Gazo, *JAST* 3 (1996): 19]. **Mexico, July to August 1926**: two courses at the National University summer school, minister Puig Casauranc, writing on the Calles-era church-state conflict; Cuernavaca, where Alice has a heart attack; Lake Chapala [A 1926.07.05-08.21, .08, .08.23]. **14 July 1927**, Alice Chipman Dewey dies [A 1927.07.14, *Notable American Women*, 466].

- **Contradiction, unresolved.** CDS gives 14 July and arteriosclerosis; other reference works give 13 July and cerebral thrombosis. Both day and cause are unsettled.

**Hubbards, Nova Scotia**, summers recurring 1927-1948 [A 1927.07.23, 1929.09, 1937.07.15-09.14, 1948.07.15, among many]. **USSR 1928**: Berlin, then Leningrad and the Hermitage 3-9 July with daughter Evelyn, Moscow 13-21 July [A 1928.06.28-07.09, .13, .18, .21]. **Edinburgh, 15 April to 17 May 1929**: Roxburghe Hotel, the Gifford Lectures published as *The Quest for Certainty* [A 1929.04.15, .04.17-05.17, .05.15]. **1930**, retires from Columbia with eleven books still to come [A 1930, SEP]. **1934**, *Art as Experience* and *A Common Faith* [A SEP, CDS]; Barnes met Dewey at a Columbia seminar in 1917, and the Foundation's "a school rather than a typical museum" identity carries the Deweyan model [A Wikipedia].

**South Africa 1934** [A CDS 1934.05.26 through .08.06]: Gibraltar on the *Rex*, Capetown 14 June and Table Mountain the same day, Durban 16-21 June, the New Education Fellowship conference 2-13 July ("The Need for a Philosophy of Education"), Johannesburg 15-27 July with an honorary degree on the 24th, Victoria Falls on the 28th and the Zimbabwe Ruins on the 31st, Pretoria 4 August, departure on the 6th. CDS credits the degree to "Johannesburg University," the Witwatersrand name appearing only for the 3 August address to its Philosophical Society. That Dewey wrote little afterward against the conference's segregated setting is an observation of later scholarship, not a CDS entry, and `date_confidence` says so.

**19 March 1937**, agrees to head the Commission of Inquiry into the Charges Made against Leon Trotsky [A 1937.03.19]; **10-17 April**, thirteen sessions at Avenida Londres 127, Coyoacan [A 1937.04.10-17, which says only "Mexico City"; venue and session count from the transcript].

- **Two dossier errors corrected.** (1) The house was **not** "Diego Rivera and Frida Kahlo's": it was the Kahlo family house, lent to Trotsky and Sedova, resident January 1937 to April 1939, while Kahlo and Rivera were at San Angel, Rivera moving in only in 1941 [A Wikipedia, "Frida Kahlo Museum"]. (2) Coordinate: see below.
- **Beals** put his question at the afternoon session of 16 April and resigned the next morning; the transcript files the resignation under the **twelfth** session, so "after the eleventh" is loose and is not used. The December 1937 finding of not guilty rests on the commission's published report, not on CDS.

**1938**, *Experience and Education* and *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* [A SEP, CDS]. The first annual Felix Adler lecture in 1938 is **[R]**: a dossier claim, absent from `felix_adler.journey.json` and from the CDS excerpt consulted. Carried with the hedge, not dropped.

## 8. Last years, death, afterlife (1946-1972)

**21 September 1946**, "Slipped . . . and dislocated my left shoulder" [A 1946.09.21]. **CDS does not say where**; the next entry reads "Got back yesterday to NY after returning from CAPE BRETON," while the dossier's coordinate sat on mainland Nova Scotia, 350 km away. The journey file folds the fall into the Hubbards stop and says the chronology does not locate it.

**11 December 1946**, marries Estelle Roberta Lowitz Grant (b. 1904, Oil City) in his apartment at 1158 Fifth Avenue, 10:00 a.m., Jerome Nathanson officiating [A 1946.12.11, *NYT* 11 and 12 December 1946]. **1 June 1952**, dies of pneumonia [A 1952.06.01, control no. 09541]; CDS does not say he died in the apartment, and Wikipedia's "at his home in New York City" supports it [A for day and cause, R for the room]. **2 June 1952**, cremated at Fresh Pond Crematory, Middle Village, Queens [A 1952.06.02].

**26 October 1972**, programme commemorating the interment of the ashes of John and Roberta Dewey at the University of Vermont [A 1972.10.26, citing Dykhuizen, 322]; the memorial stands north of the Ira Allen Chapel, the only known grave site on that campus [A Wikipedia]. **The pool tagged this [R]; the tag was wrong and is raised to [A].** He had given the Founder's Day address in that chapel on 1 May 1939 [A 1939.05.01]. The pool left the stamp's date and place unknown; CDS has it issued 21 October 1968, first-day ceremonies in Burlington, after an announcement of 25 April 1965 [A 1965.04.25, 1968.10.21], and records a separate Grenada Dewey stamp of 8 May 1971 [A 1971.05.08].

## Quotation integrity

Two pool quotations were **truncated in a way that changed the grammar** and are repaired against the primary texts: the Creed sentence runs on past "in the home" to "in the neighborhood, or on the playground," and "The right to a hearing before condemnation is such an elementary right in every civilized country" halves a "such...that" construction, so the full First Session sentence is quoted instead. Three pool quotations are **[R]**, known only through a standard citation, and two of those are used with the hedge written into `quote_source`: the audacity-of-imagination line and the democracy-born-anew line. The shadow-cast-by-big-business line is not used.

- **The dissertation.** *The Psychology of Kant* (1884) is lost. No text.
- **The twenty years of the ashes.** Cremated 2 June 1952, interred 26 October 1972. No source reached explains the interval.
- **Jane Addams and Hull House have no atlas file**, the largest coverage gap around Dewey: fifteen years of documented relationship with nothing to interlock against. No file either for **C. S. Peirce or G. S. Morris**, his Baltimore teachers, for **B. R. Ambedkar**, or for **F. M. Alexander**, both of the latter dossier claims and unverified here.

- **"Learning by doing"** as a Dewey coinage is a modern myth per the dossier's account of Knoll's research, which was not reached [R]. **No feature-length biographical film** centred on Dewey was found [R: a negative]. **The mid-century "Dewey destroyed American education" polemic** could not be sourced to a specific controversy or text [R].
