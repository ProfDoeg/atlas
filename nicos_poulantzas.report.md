# Nicos Poulantzas (1936-1979): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-21 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `nicos_poulantzas`. Companion: `nicos_poulantzas.journey.json` (8 segments, 44 stops).*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named in the same sentence · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-biographer claim not independently confirmed · **[gap]** the record is silent and is left silent.

**Two structural warnings.** First, most of the standard Poulantzas biography descends from **one book**: Bob Jessop, *Nicos Poulantzas: Marxist Theory and Political Strategy* (Macmillan, 1985), the only detailed life, written with family consultation. It was never opened on this pass; every "Jessop-only" below reaches this file through a dossier summarising him, and is tagged **[R]** however confidently it circulates. That is not scepticism about Jessop but a refusal to launder one unread source into an attestation by repeating it. Second, the **Greek popular biographical web** (mixanitouxronou.gr, andro.gr, ertnews.gr) and the **English and French encyclopaedic web** disagree on several dates and on the circumstances of the death; the disagreements are catalogued below rather than smoothed.

## 1. Athens, 1936-1957

Born Athens, **21 September 1936** [A: uniform across ertnews.gr, poulantzas.gr, and Stuart Hall's obituary essay, *New Left Review* I/119; no primary vital record located]. Parents **Aristides Poulantzas** and **Angeliki Kariophili** [A: mixanitouxronou.gr, andro.gr, independently].

Childhood address **Veranzerou Street, near Vathis Square** [**R**: Jessop-only; no fetched source names the street]. Kept despite the tag because it yields a checkable coincidence: the Nikos Poulantzas Institute, founded 1997, sits at **Stournari 57**, which Nominatim puts in the **same Πλατεία Βάθης quarter**. **Coordinate corrected**: the pool's 37.9838 / 23.7278 falls at Omonia; Nominatim gives **37.9857 / 23.7273**.

Schooling at the **Peiramatikon Gymnasion** with the **Institut Français d'Athènes** [A: mixanitouxronou.gr]. **Coordinate corrected**: the pool's 37.9793 / 23.7332 sits on Panepistimiou; the school is at Skoufa 43, Kolonaki, **37.9797 / 23.7385**, the Institut at Sina 31 a block away. One pin covers both.

**Baccalauréat redated.** The pool gave 1954; el.wikipedia gives **1953**, consistent with entering the Law School that year [A]. The **1954 Salzburg Festival** episode (a press card from *Eleftheria*, pieces on the festival, on intellectuals, and on municipal socialism) is a *separate* event, **[R]**. The two were conflated in the pool and are separated here.

Law School **1953-1957**, graduating "excellent" [A: mixanitouxronou.gr]. Entry **without the entrance examination** under a French-Greek exchange is **[R]**. **Coordinate corrected**: the pool labelled it Solonos Street at 37.9808 / 23.7328, which is the Propylaea; Nominatim puts it on **Sina**, **37.9809 / 23.7362**.

**Over-tagging corrected.** The pool tagged the 1953 entry [A] on Hall's authority, but Hall says nothing about 1953, the Law School, or an exchange waiver. What he attests, narrowly, is that Poulantzas was "active in the Greek student movement in the 1950's, when he joined the Greek Democratic Alliance (eda)" [A, verbatim]. **That name is Hall's own loose rendering** of EDA, Ενιαία Δημοκρατική Αριστερά, the United Democratic Left; the journey file uses the correct expansion and marks the phrasing as Hall's. A **brief arrest at a Cyprus demonstration, 1955** [**R**]: no charge, no conviction, site unknown.

## 2. Navy, Germany, Paris, 1957-1965

Naval service 1957-1960, translation service in Athens, reassigned to **Crete** after going absent without leave, registered with the Athens bar afterwards and never practised [**R** throughout].

**Heidelberg redated.** Confirmed as a place of study [A: en.wikipedia; poulantzas.gr, "Athens, Heidelberg and Paris"]. The pool's "late 1950s" cannot stand against el.wikipedia's dating of the Munich and Heidelberg seminars to **1960**, after the degree and the service. **Munich** is [A: en.wikipedia names LMU; mixanitouxronou.gr and andro.gr independently], but the month-long stay, the letter to his father, and the stated reason that Nazi ideas remained too influential are **[R]**; Jessop is himself reported to call it "the pretext".

**Contradiction on the Paris arrival, not resolved.** en.wikipedia: moved 1960, with a Panthéon-Sorbonne assistantship the same year. poulantzas.gr: settled **1961**. el.wikipedia: doctorate **1961-64**, assistantship **1962-1965**. The journey file takes 1961 and states the conflict. The **chargé de cours** post is **[R]**: the poulantzas.gr text cited for it in the pool does not mention such a post at all.

Mémoire, *La renaissance du droit naturel en Allemagne après la Seconde Guerre mondiale*, 161 pages, **October 1961** [A: bijus.eu record via the dossier]. Doctorate *Nature des choses et droit*, completed 1964, published LGDJ 1965 with a preface by the Thomist jurist **Michel Villey** [A]. The pre-Marxist Poulantzas, in print under conservative Catholic patronage, buried until Verso's 2008 *Reader*.

## 3. Les Temps Modernes, CNRS, marriage, 1963-1966

**The Sartre circle is [R].** That Poulantzas joined the younger contributor circle around *Les Temps Modernes* alongside Annie Leclerc and Georges Perec, as Beauvoir records in her memoir, and contributed 1964-66, rests on the dossier's citation of a memoir that was not fetched. The atlas's Sartre and Beauvoir files corroborate the journal and its Gallimard address, which corroborates the milieu, not his place in it.

**Meeting Leclerc, downgraded.** The pool tagged "meets Annie Leclerc, 1963" [A] on Hall's authority. **Hall's obituary, fetched in full, never mentions Leclerc.** The meeting is **[R]**. Only the **marriage** is documented: **6 December 1966** [A for the year: andro.gr, mixanitouxronou.gr, en. and el.wikipedia; the day is Jessop's]. Ceremony venue [gap].

**Althusser** is **[R]**: regular Paris meetings, close interlocutor, never a formal student, dossier-general with no dated meeting located. It is nonetheless the most consequential intellectual relation of the Paris years, carried at the corpus rue d'Ulm pin with the tag stated.

**CNRS, conflict named.** andro.gr 1965-1972; en.wikipedia **1966-1972**. The file takes 1966 and says why. Deputy editor, *Archives de philosophie du droit*, 1966-69 [A]. The quai Anatole-France pin is a period placeholder ~250 m south of no. 15. **Athens, May 1966**, the "Second Week of Contemporary Thought" [**R**; cited bibliographically in *Marxismos kai Episteme*, 1966]; venue [gap], and the Zappeion placeholder is labelled as such.

## 4. 1967-1968: the colonels, the split, the book

The coup, 21 April 1967. Founding member of the **anti-dictatorship committee of Greeks in Paris**, writing under the pseudonym **N. Skyrianos** [**R**]. Hall supplies the attestation for the party: "After the internal split, in the wake of the Colonel's coup, he joined, and remained, a member of the Greek Communist Party of the Interior" [A].

**Contradiction on the cause of the split, not resolved.** The pool asserted he sided with KKE (Interior) *after the Soviet invasion of Czechoslovakia split the KKE*. That sequence is unsafe: the split was formalised at the **12th Plenum, February 1968**, months before the **August** invasion, and Hall places the break in the coup's aftermath. But en.wikipedia's KKE (Interior) page does say the split followed the invasion. The journey file dates the stop to the plenum and lets both accounts stand.

***Pouvoir politique et classes sociales*, Maspero, 1968** [A: en.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia; kathimerini.gr notes the May coincidence]. The two details that always travel with it, that it appeared **days before** the Sorbonne occupation and that **several thousand copies** were bought by students, are **[R]**: no fetched source gives the book's month, let alone its sales.

## 5. Vincennes, 1969-1979

Among the first sociology faculty at **Vincennes**, until his death [A].

**A fabricated quotation removed.** The pool attributed to the Verso/Löwy memorial piece the words "where he worked with Michael Löwy from 1969-1977... classes were packed to the rafters". **Neither phrase is in the source.** What Löwy actually says (versobooks.com, 5 March 2015, fetched) is that he became Poulantzas's assistant in **1969**, replacing **Emir Sader**, and that "Our little duo lasted for some years". The journey file uses the real wording. This is the most serious defect found in the pool and it is recorded rather than quietly fixed.

**Coordinate.** The pool carried two Vincennes points 1.1 km apart, neither verifiable; fr.wikipedia says only that the centre occupied 40,000 m² of the Bois de Vincennes, took students from January 1969, and was razed 27-29 August 1980 with "no trace remaining on the site". The file **inherits the canonical pin, 48.8434 / 2.4415**, byte-identical with `michel_foucault`, `gilles_deleuze`, `felix_guattari` and `jacques_lacan`.

**The Miliband debate.** *NLR* I/58 (1969) and I/95 (1976), both fetched. Miliband has no atlas file, so this is a one-directional citation carried as a source on Poulantzas's own stop. **No source confirms he ever visited the *NLR* office**; Carlisle Street is the publication venue and the file says so.

**Contradiction on Ariane's birth, not resolved.** en.wikipedia: **1970**, no day. el.wikipedia: **1971**, name Ariadne. andro.gr and mixanitouxronou.gr: **1971**. Jessop, on family consultation: **2 July 1970**. No fetched source carries the day. The pool's [A] overstates it; the exact date is **[R]**, and the stop is dated 1970-07-02 for ordering only.

*Fascisme et dictature*, Maspero, 1970; *Political Power and Social Classes*, trans. O'Hagan, New Left Books, 1973; "On Social Classes", *NLR* I/78, 1973 [fetched]; *Les classes sociales dans le capitalisme aujourd'hui*, Seuil, 1974. All [A] on publication record.

**Two quotations restored to full length.** The pool's quote lens truncated two sentences in ways that reversed them. "One can be unconsciously and surreptitiously contaminated by the very epistemological principles of the adversary" drops the qualifier; restored, it opens "**In the extreme case**". And "...defined by its place in the ensemble of the division of labour" cuts exactly the clause separating Poulantzas from the economism he attacks; restored, it ends "the division of labour **which includes political and ideological relations**".

**EHESS.** poulantzas.gr calls him "Director of Studies at the Ecole Pratique des Hautes Etudes" [A for the title]. **The start year is unsupported**: poulantzas.gr gives none, the pool's "from 1974" is an inference, and Jessop says only that he "began teaching". Open discrepancy.

## 6. 1974-1977

The Estado Novo and the junta both fall in 1974; he writes for *Avgi*, *Ta Nea*, *To Vima*, *Anti* [**R**: Jessop-only, unverified against the papers]. *La crise des dictatures*, Maspero 1975, second edition 1976 [A]. Visiting professor at **Panteios**, 1975 [**R**]. **Coordinate corrected**: the pool's 37.9636 / 23.7325 lands in Neos Kosmos ~1.2 km east; Nominatim gives 136 Andrea Syngrou, Kallithea, **37.9596 / 23.7193**. *La crise de l'État*, PUF 1976 [A].

**The Castoriadis exchange, misattributed in the pool and corrected.** Poulantzas opens in *Avgi*, **1 January 1977**, calling Castoriadis "a revolutionary from the OECD" [A: Verso blog, fetched]. The pool then claimed "Castoriadis and others reply in *Avgi* and *Eleftherotypia* through the month". Per the same source, **Castoriadis did not reply that month at all**: the replies came from **Erifili Kartali** (*Avgi*, 12 January) and **Chr. D. Loukopoulos** (*Eleftherotypia*, 25 January), and **Castoriadis answered only in 1978, in *Libre***.

Name on the KKE (Interior) list at the general election in an unelectable position; **Mélusine** co-founded with Barret-Kriegel, Birnbaum, Buci-Glucksmann, Clément, Debray, Fossaert, Joxe, Lindenberg, Motchane, Vincent and Weber. Both **[R]**, Jessop-only; venue [gap].

## 7. 1978-1979

***L'État, le pouvoir, le socialisme*, PUF, 1978** [A]. The state as "a material condensation of a relationship of forces among classes and class fractions"; **authoritarian statism**. "Towards a Democratic Socialism", *NLR* I/109, 1978 [A, fetched]. One of its most quoted lines, "the sweeping replacement of 'formal' representative democracy by the 'real', direct democracy of workers' councils", is Poulantzas **characterising a position in order to reject it**; quoted out of context it inverts him, and the journey file warns against exactly that.

**April 1979**, the British Sociological Association at Coventry, and the meeting with **Bob Jessop** [**R**, which is to say the biographer is his own source for how he met his subject]. Canonical Warwick pin, 52.38 / -1.56167. **Autumn 1979**, "L'État, les mouvements sociaux, le parti", *Dialectiques* 28 [A: Viewpoint Magazine, fetched]; reprinted in *Repères* (1980).

**Death: 3 October 1979, Paris, by suicide, aged 43** [A: en.wikipedia; ertnews.gr; Hall, *NLR* I/119]. Date, city, manner and age are secure; **everything else about the scene is not**, and the dispute is catalogued below. Löwy [A, fetched]: Poulantzas "had been depressed for over a year", had an "accident" in 1978 some suspected was a prior attempt, and **Constantine Tsoucalas**, reported present, recalled him throwing his books from the window saying the work was worthless. Löwy resists reading a lifelong depressive backward from the death, remembering "a Marxist full of joy".

## Contradictions, not adjudicated

1. **Father's profession.** Lawyer of Maniot origin (el.wikipedia, Greek press) vs professor of forensic graphology (en.wikipedia). 2. **Paris arrival.** 1960 vs 1961 vs a 1962-65 assistantship. 3. **CNRS start.** 1965 (andro.gr) vs 1966 (en.wikipedia). 4. **Cause of the 1968 split.** Coup aftermath and the February plenum vs the August invasion. 5. **Ariane's birth.** 2 July 1970 vs 1970 undated vs 1971. 6. **EHESS start year.** "From 1974" (inference) vs no year at all in the source supplying the title.

7. **The site and floor of the death**, the largest, now with **four horns and two arrondissements**:
   - **13th floor of his own apartment building** (andro.gr, mixanitouxronou.gr);
   - **22nd floor of the Tour Montparnasse** (English-language tradition, corroborated by nothing fetched);
   - **29th floor of a Paris apartment building** (a contemporaneous Greek notice preserved by Kathimerini, whose page returned a fetch error);
   - **50th floor of the Tour Montparnasse, in the 13e arrondissement** (fr.wikipedia), **internally incoherent**, since the tower stands in the 15e.

   Neither poulantzas.gr nor en.wikipedia names a building or floor at all. **A hypothesis recorded without adoption**: "13th floor" may be a corruption of "13e arrondissement". The pool plotted the death at 48.8421 / 2.3219, **exactly the Tour Montparnasse**, staking the map on the least-supported variant. **The journey file refuses that** and uses a neutral central-Paris point, describing the dispute in the campa.

## Honest gaps

- **No grave, tomb, cemetery or burial site is documented anywhere.** poulantzas.gr, mixanitouxronou.gr and the Löwy piece all cover the death in detail and none says where he was interred; funeral arrangements likewise. Written into the journey file rather than omitted.
- **No police report or contemporaneous French newspaper account** of the death was located, which is why the floor dispute cannot be closed.
- **Venues unknown**: the 1966 Athens conference, the 1955 demonstration, the Mélusine meetings, the 1966 marriage ceremony, and the 1971 Latin American conference behind "Las clases sociales", which is named by no source with a city and gets no stop at all. The rest are placeholder pins, labelled as such.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Poulantzas is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no backward application of his framework is licensed and none was written. Nothing was silently deleted; what was caught is listed for a ruling.

**Direction caught: forward, in every case.** Everything in the pool not rooted in his own life is *a later person engaging with him*, never his apparatus reaching into an earlier century:

- **Stuart Hall's obituary essay** (*NLR* I/119, 1980), **Michael Löwy's memorial piece** (Verso, 2015) and the Tsoucalas account it relays: carried as **sources and suggested_refs** on Poulantzas's own death and Vincennes stops, never staged as their scenes. Likewise **Jessop's 1985 biography**: the April 1979 meeting *is* a Poulantzas event and gets a stop, the writing of the book afterwards does not.
- **The Institute (1997), the ERT documentary (1989), the Verso *Reader* (2008), the 2019 reissue, the transform! anniversary essay (2019).** Posthumous reception, given a short afterlife segment at Athens and London pins per standing corpus practice for monuments and editions bearing the subject's name; each stop states in `date_confidence` that it postdates him.

**Relations found and declined, with the reason:**

- **The Gladio rumour**, an unsupported internet claim (Portuguese and Spanish Wikipedia-derived material, a Reddit thread citing an uncited thesis) that Poulantzas was a casualty of NATO's stay-behind network rather than a suicide. The atlas carries a Gladio thread through `william_colby.journey.json` (Scandinavia, early 1950s), and **Colby's work has no documented connection of any kind to a 1979 death in Paris.** Named here, given no stop, no campa and no cross-reference; it is not an evidentiary alternative of equal weight.
- **Coventry / Warwick.** `nick_land` (1987) and `mark_fisher` (1990) use the same pin a decade or more later. Same place, different decades, no relation. The pin is inherited; **neither man is named in any campa**.
- **The Montparnasse cluster.** `jean_paul_sartre`, `simone_de_beauvoir` and `julio_cortazar` hold graves and haunts at 48.83-48.84 / 2.32-2.33. Since the Montparnasse death account is the weakest of the four, plotting there would have manufactured an overlap out of the shakiest reading in the file. Declined.
- **Cyprus, Munich, Heidelberg.** The atlas's Cyprus pins are pre-modern, and the 1955 arrest happened **in Athens, about Cyprus as a cause**. Munich and Heidelberg are heavily used pins with dozens of unrelated occupants. Coincidental reuse only; no traveler named at any of the three.

**Travelers named in campa, each on a relation of his own:** `jean_paul_sartre` and `simone_de_beauvoir` (the *Les Temps Modernes* circle he was recruited into and wrote for); `karl_marx` (his lifelong subject, and the object of a quoted sentence on Marx's silence about the state); `mussolini` (Italian Fascism, one of the two regimes analysed in *Fascisme et dictature*).
