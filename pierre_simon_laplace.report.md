# Pierre-Simon Laplace (1749-1827): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `pierre_simon_laplace.journey.json`. 43 stops, 8 segments, gregorian, register "national mythology: the canon is true".*

**Legend. [A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim whose source cites no primary record. Contradictions are flagged, never adjudicated; unreachable sources are named with the reason. Each stop's `date_confidence` carries the full apparatus; this report gives the evidence by phase and the corrections applied to the research pool.

Two structural findings. **(1)** The pool's two geographic lenses contradicted each other on four pins (Beaumont-en-Auge, rue du Bac, Saint-Julien-de-Mailloc, Malmaison); in each case one was demonstrably wrong. **(2)** Its chronology lens and its afterlife lens told two different burial stories, and the afterlife lens is right: three graves, not two.

## Phase 1: Normandy, 1749-1768

- Born **23 March 1749** at Beaumont-en-Auge to Pierre Laplace, cider trader and parish syndic, and Marie-Anne Sochon; non-noble, comfortable, intended for the Church. **[A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia]**
- School of the **Benedictine priory**, about age seven to sixteen, **c.1756-1765**, an uncle teaching in it. **[A schooling, R endpoints: MacTutor; JAHH 12(2) (2009), p. 93]** *Correction:* the pool dated the schooling to **1765**, the year he **left**. Priory founded 1060, dependent on Saint-Ouen of Rouen; its church of Saint-Sauveur is now the parish church **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **University of Caen** at sixteen, matriculated in theology; **Gadbled** and **Le Canu** turned him to analysis **[A: MacTutor; fr.wikipedia]**. **Left without a degree** for Paris at about nineteen **[A: MacTutor]**; no month, 1768 defaults to 01-01.

**Gap.** No parish register, matriculation record or degree register consulted. The phase rests on two tertiary biographies plus one peer-reviewed article touching only the schooling.

## Phase 2: Paris and the Academy, 1768-1774

- **D'Alembert** ignores the recommendations, is won by an unsolicited memoir on mechanics, finds him a post. **[A pattern: MacTutor, en.wikipedia. R scene]** No letter is cited anywhere; d'Alembert's famous reply is reported as tradition and is **not** placed in a quote field.
- **Ecole Militaire**, professor of mathematics, **1769-1776**. **[A: MacTutor, en.wikipedia; span confirmed JAHH 12(2), p. 93]**
- **28 March 1770**, first paper to the Academie, on maxima and minima; thirteen papers in under three years; failed candidacies 1771 and 1772; **elected adjoint 31 March 1773** at twenty-four. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **1771**, paper on integral calculus and finite differences in the Turin *Miscellanea*, opening the correspondence with **Lagrange** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Written in France; pinning it at Turin, the place of publication and of the correspondent, is a compiler's decision.
- **1773**, begins the work on the invariability of the planetary mean motions and the Jupiter-Saturn great inequality (near 5:2 resonance) **[A: MacTutor]**. No month; the file's 1773-06-01 only orders the stop after the March election.
- **1773/1774**, *Memoire sur la probabilite des causes par les evenements*, read 1773, published 1774; the Laplace distribution derived 1774. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- The **Lexell** remark on his wanting to pronounce on everything at the Academy is **[A at one remove]**: on MacTutor, dated c.1780-81, original not consulted, wording provisional.

## Phase 3: Heat, cadets, a marriage, 1783-1793

- **1783**, ice calorimeter and *Memoire sur la chaleur* with **Lavoisier** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, the pool giving the span 1782-1784. **1784**, examiner of the Royal Artillery Corps **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **September 1785**, examines the sixteen-year-old **Napoleon Bonaparte**. **[R, on narrower grounds than the pool assumed]** en.wikipedia's *Napoleon* article states the examination by Laplace as **fact**; MacTutor gives it as tradition. Neither cites a register and none was located, so the [R] rests on the missing document, not on doubt that the two men were in the room.
- **Marriage 1788: contradiction standing. en.wikipedia 15 March; MacTutor 15 May.** The file uses 15 March and records the other. *Second-order error caught:* the pool's **geography** lens printed this attribution **backwards** while its **chronology** lens had it right. en.wikipedia also gives the bride's age as **eighteen**, of **Besancon**; the pool says only "about half his age".
- Children: **Charles-Emile**, b. 5 April 1789 (later artillery officer, lieutenant-general, hereditary marquis, senator under Napoleon III, died childless); **Sophie-Suzanne**, b. 1792. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **Weights and measures commission 1790-1793**; **removed December 1793** by the Committee of Public Safety for insufficient republican confidence, a political dismissal with no charge. **[A: MacTutor; fr.wikipedia]** *An [R] inside the [A]:* the names of the others struck off (Borda, Coulomb, Brisson, Delambre) are standard in the literature but **not verified against a primary list**, so the campa names only the men who sat with him.

## Phase 4: Terror and reconstruction, 1794-1799

- **1794**, withdraws to **Melun**, fifty km southeast, returning after Thermidor; never arrested. Lavoisier guillotined 8 May 1794. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **20 January to 19 May 1795**, the **Ecole normale de l'an III** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Two corrections: the lectures were in an amphitheatre of the **Museum national d'histoire naturelle at the Jardin des Plantes**, not at the pool's pin near the Pantheon (the *later* rue d'Ulm school of 1808); and Laplace shared the **mathematics** chair with Lagrange, the probability material being a strand of those lectures, not the chair.
- **6 July 1795**, founding member of the **Bureau des Longitudes** as geometre with Lagrange, Mechain, Cassini and Delambre; law of 25 June 1795, first meeting in a room of the **Petit Luxembourg** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the pool seated this at the Institut de France, which fr.wikipedia contradicts, adding that the Bureau was not originally seated at the Observatory either.
- **1796**, *Exposition du systeme du monde*, nebular hypothesis in the final book **[A: en.wikipedia]**, and the dark-star argument, with a mathematical proof published **1799** in German in *Allgemeine Geographische Ephemeriden* vol. 4 **[A: Montgomery, Orchiston & Whittingham, JAHH 12(2) (2009), 90-96]**. *Correction:* the pool traced the modern rediscovery of the Michell-Laplace dark star to **Israel 1987**; MOW's own introduction cites **Schaffer, JHA 10 (1979), 42-43**, eight years earlier, and the file credits Schaffer with Israel as elaboration.
- **1799**, volumes **I and II** of the *Traite de mecanique celeste* **[A: en.wikipedia's volume list, 1799, 1799, 1802, 1805, 1825]**. *Correction:* the pool dated volumes III and IV **jointly to 1805**; III is 1802, IV is 1805.

## Phase 5: The Empire, 1799-1812

- **12 November 1799**, Minister of the Interior; **removed 25 December**, replaced by Lucien Bonaparte, and placed in the **Senat conservateur** the same day **[A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the pool attached the ministry to the Palais du Luxembourg, which housed the **Senate**; in the file that pin carries the Senate half only and the ministry is deliberately left unlocated. Napoleon's "spirit of the infinitely small" verdict is **[A at one remove]**, from the *Memorial de Sainte-Helene* as quoted on MacTutor, the *Memorial* not consulted.
- **Paris Observatory** under the **oversight** of the Bureau des Longitudes, 1795-1854 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the pool's justification read "Paris Observatory is the documented institutional seat of the Bureau", the **reverse** of the source; the influence claim survives, the justification does not. Delambre's remark against putting a geometer at the head of an observatory is **[A at one remove]** from MacTutor, original not consulted.
- **Legion d'honneur: chevalier 12 October 1803, grand officier 14 June 1804 [A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the pool gave 1805.
- **8 August 1802, Malmaison**, the most heavily corrected item, every correction reducing the claim. Documented: **Herschel's Paris diary**, in Lubbock, *The Herschel Chronicle* (1933), **p. 310**, has Napoleon asking who is the author of all this, and Laplace wishing "to shew that a chain of natural causes would account for the construction and preservation of the wonderful system"; Lagrange present. **Not** in the diary: "I had no need of that hypothesis" **[R]**, which traces to **Rouse Ball, ch. XVIII, pp. 417-418** (verified verbatim, `ashortaccounthi01ballgoog`) and carries **no "Sire"**; **Antommarchi (1825), i. 282** does not name Laplace; **Faye (1884), pp. 109-111**, says flatly Laplace never said it and reports through **Arago** that Laplace tried to have it suppressed. The pool's paraphrase needed two fixes, "a *continuous* chain" and the gloss "[of the heavens]". **Verification limit:** Lubbock reached only through a citing source quoting p. 310, **archive.org returning HTTP 503 on every Herschel/Lubbock volume attempted**. Single-source; the file quotes nothing from the diary.
- **Societe d'Arcueil**, founded **1806**, running to **1822** and ending with Berthollet's death; Sunday meetings at the two neighbouring houses; Biot, Poisson, Malus, Thenard, Gay-Lussac, Arago, **Humboldt** **[A: both Wikipedias]**. *Correction:* the pool gave "c.1805-1817". **Contradiction standing:** house purchase **1806** (en.wikipedia) vs **1801** (fr.wikipedia).
- **21 December 1807**, sits with Lagrange, Monge and Lacroix on the committee that refuses **Fourier's** heat memoir over the trigonometric series **[A, via `joseph_fourier.journey.json`, sourced there to MacTutor and en.wikipedia]**.
- **Comte de l'Empire. Contradiction standing: 24 April 1808 (fr.wikipedia) vs 1806 (en.wikipedia).** The file uses 1808 and records both.
- **1807, Brunswick:** a 2000-franc French war levy on **Gauss**; Olbers and Laplace independently offer to pay it, Gauss refuses both **[A, via `gauss.journey.json`]**. Laplace's ranking of Gauss above Pfaff reaches that file from a nineteenth-century biographical dictionary at second hand and is **[R]**.

## Phase 6: The two books on probability, 1812-1814

- **1812**, *Theorie analytique des probabilites*, dedicated to **Napoleon-le-Grand** **[A: MacTutor]**. That the dedication was dropped later is a commonplace **not verified against the second and third editions**; **[R]**, flagged in the stop.
- **22 September 1813**, **Sophie-Suzanne dies following childbirth**, aged twenty-one, the child surviving. **[R: en.wikipedia, which the pool also tagged R]** **Permanent gap:** nothing survives from Laplace about it, no letter, no dedication, no line in the Arcueil memoirs, very likely a consequence of the **1925 fire** (Phase 8).
- **1814**, *Essai philosophique sur les probabilites*, expanded from the second edition's introduction and rooted in the 1795 lectures. **[A: MacTutor]**

**Quotation verification.** The 1902 Truscott and Emory translation was read in full on archive.org (`philosophicaless00lapl`, 412,536 characters) and cross-checked on Wikisource. The **"intelligence" passage** and **"Present events are connected with preceding ones..."** (both Ch. II) are verbatim **[A]**; Laplace never wrote "demon", a later coinage. Three pool quotations needed repair. **(1) Classical definition of probability** (Ch. II, pp. 6-7): the pool misquoted the opening, which reads "The ratio of **this number** to that of all the cases possible...", referring back to the preceding sentence; the pool substituted "the number of favorable cases", a gloss. **(2) The sunrise bet** (Ch. III, p. 19), unused in the file but recorded because **the pool's own correction note is wrong**: the numeral needing repair is the **first**, "182623 days" being a compositor's error for **1,826,213**, while "1826214 to one" is correct as printed. **(3) "If man were restricted to collecting facts..."**: **wrong work entirely.** Grepping the full 1902 text returns **zero** occurrences of "nomenclature". The sentence is from the ***Exposition du systeme du monde*** (verified, `expositiondusys06laplgoog`), the published English being **J. Pond, *The System of the World* (1809), i. 205** (verified, `systemworld01laplgoog`), which is what the file uses. The pool's English matches no locatable translation.

## Phase 7: Restoration, 1814-1826

- **April 1814**, as senator votes for **Napoleon's deposition** and adheres to the Restoration (day not recorded); **4 June 1814**, **peer of France**. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **1815**, withdraws from Paris during the **Hundred Days** **[R: MacTutor]**. **No location and no date in any source reached**, so the stop uses the generic Paris pin as an explicit compromise and its date only orders the segment. That he did not sign the Acte additionnel is inference from silence.
- **December 1815**, as peer votes for the **death sentence on Marshal Ney** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the pool called it a court-martial, reversing the point: Ney was tried by the Chamber of Peers **because** he had escaped trial by court-martial. Verdict 6 December, execution 7 December; the pool gives only the month, so the days are flagged in the stop as not pool-sourced.
- **11 April 1816**, elected to the **Academie francaise** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; **1817**, **marquis** **[A: both Wikipedias]**, with no day or month, so folded into the 1816 stop rather than given a placed date of its own.
- **1825**, volume **V**, the last, of the *Mecanique celeste* **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **1826**, declines the Academie protest for **freedom of the press**, alienating the circle around **Arago** **[R: en.wikipedia]**, no primary record located and none cited by the source.

## Phase 8: Death and the three graves, 1827-1932

- **5 March 1827**, a Monday: dies at **108 rue du Bac**, aged seventy-seven; the Academie cancels its meeting as an exceptional mark of respect and the seat stays unfilled for months until Puissant's election **[A: MacTutor]**. Volta died the same day. *Nuance:* fr.wikipedia's entry for No. 108 says a plaque records that Laplace **lived** there, not that he died there; the death address is the pool's synthesis.
- **The brain. [R]** en.wikipedia's Death section: removed by his physician **Francois Magendie**, kept for years, eventually shown in a roving anatomical museum in Britain, reportedly smaller than average. Two corrections: the pool attributed this to **fr.wikipedia**, which does not carry it, and its afterlife lens called it untraceable to any source, overstating the gap. The narrower real gap, on which the [R] rests, is that **no museum catalogue, accession record or measurement can be traced**.
- **Three resting places, not two. The largest correction in the file.**

  | period | place | source |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1827-1878 | Pere-Lachaise, 25th division | fr.wikipedia infobox; napoleon-empire.org |
  | 1878-1888 | cemetery of **Beaumont-en-Auge**, his birthplace | fr.wikipedia infobox |
  | 1888 on | **Saint-Julien-de-Mailloc**, tombeau des Colbert-Laplace | fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia |

  **Contradiction, flagged not resolved.** en.wikipedia and napoleon-empire.org compress this into **one** 1888 transfer and never mention Beaumont. The chronology lens followed the simpler account; the afterlife lens caught the three stages. The file carries all three because one source has them and none denies them, and says the middle grave rests on a single source.
- The tomb stands beside the sixteenth-century chapelle Notre-Dame-de-la-Delivrande at the **Chateau de Mailloc**, which **Laplace bought in 1813** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **In 1925 the chateau burns and the family archive with it** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; the dossier did not mention the fire, and it is why several gaps here are permanent.
- **1889**, one of the **72 names** on the Eiffel Tower **[A: MacTutor; fr.wikipedia]**. **3 July 1932**, bronze at Beaumont-en-Auge by **Robert Delandre**, inaugurated by **Marshal Franchet d'Esperey**, medallions of **Galileo and Newton** on the pedestal **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Posthumous editions** stay in `suggested_refs` and are not staged as stops, postdating him and belonging to their own authors: **Bowditch's** translation of vols I-IV, in manuscript by 1818, published from 1829 **[A: en.wikipedia]**; **Harte's** Dublin *Exposition*, 1830 **[A: MOW 2009]**; the *Oeuvres completes*, 14 vols, 1878-1912 **[A via archive.org; HathiTrust returned HTTP 403]**.

### Apparatus relations for the operator

Laplace is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stop is licensed here. One relation was caught and is reported rather than deleted.

- **`lord_kelvin`: FORWARD direction.** That file records Thomson entering Peterhouse in **October 1841** already reading Fourier and Laplace. Laplace died in **1827**. Writing it into Laplace's file would put a new hash into a block already closed, the direction the exception **never** licenses without the author's separate say-so. **Nothing about Kelvin was written into this file, in campa, sources or suggested_refs.** The relation is real, one-directional, and already sits where it belongs. Flagged in case the operator wants a forward grant; without one it stays out.

No backward-direction relation needing the exception was found. **Gap in another file, not fixed here:** `humboldt.journey.json` has **no Arcueil stop and no Laplace stop**, though Humboldt's membership of the Societe d'Arcueil is attested. **No Argentina, Kaaba or Temple Mount thread:** none found, none plausible, Laplace being an aesthetic-and-scientific-ancestry entry in the mathematics wing.
