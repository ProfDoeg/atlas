# Nabucodonosor II (Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon), c. 642 - 562 BC

*Research report for `nabucodonosor_ii.journey.json`. 2026-08-15.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, legend, or modern
inference not carried by a primary document. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and left
open. Dates BC are proleptic Julian, as in the journey file.

**The documentary situation governs everything below.** Three bodies of evidence describe this reign
and they barely overlap: the **Babylonian Chronicle series** (ABC 3-5), contemporary, terse, and
broken off after 594 BC; the **royal building inscriptions**, voluminous, first-person, and almost
entirely undated; and the **Hebrew Bible plus Josephus**, written from the receiving end. For the last
thirty-two years of a forty-three-year reign there is no chronicle at all, and any continuous
narrative of those decades is a modern splice.

---

## 1. Before the throne (c. 642 - 606 BC)

- Son of **Nabopolassar**, by his own inscriptions: "the son of Nabopolasser, king of Babylon"
  (East India House Inscription). **[A]**
- Birth at **Uruk**, c. 642 BC. **[R]** No Babylonian birth record survives for any king of this
  dynasty; the year is a modern back-calculation.
- An office connected with the **Eanna temple of Ishtar** before he was made crown prince. **[R]**
  Modern reconstruction relayed by Wikipedia, fixed by no dated inscription.
- **Harran, 610 BC. Pool tag corrected from [A] to [R].** The pool credited his presence to the
  Chronicle. The Livius translation of **ABC 3 (Fall of Nineveh Chronicle)**, years 16-17 of
  Nabopolassar, names only "the king of Akkad" at Harran; no crown prince appears. The Chronicle
  series **first names Nebuchadnezzar in the 607 BC entry**.
- **607 BC. Place corrected.** The pool put this campaign in the "Zagros borderlands". ABC 3 /
  Wiseman has him follow the Tigris in the month Elul up to the mountains of **Bit-Hanunia in the
  district of Urartu**, the northern highlands, not the country east of Babylonia. **[A]** for the
  campaign; **[R]** for any coordinate, since Bit-Hanunia is unidentified. The pin is labelled as
  approximate in `date_confidence`.

## 2. Carchemish and the accession (605 BC)

- **Carchemish**: as crown prince he destroys **Necho II**'s army. **[A]** ABC 5, independently
  cross-dated by **Jeremiah 46:2** to the fourth year of Jehoiakim.
- **Hamath**: the remnant is annihilated in pursuit. **[A]** ABC 5, "not a single man escaped to his
  own country."
- **The accession dates. Corrected, and a live disagreement with the existing atlas file.** The pool
  read an accession on 15 August followed by a coronation on 7 September. ABC 5 records that
  **Nabopolassar died on 8 Abu (15 August 605)** and that Nebuchadnezzar **"sat on the royal throne in
  Babylon" on 1 Ululu (7 September 605)**. So 15 August is the father's death, not an act of the
  son's; 7 September is the enthronement, not a later ceremony. **[A]** on both.
  **Flagged, not resolved:** `nabucodonosor.journey.json` places the accession on 15 August and a
  separate coronation on 7 September, and states in its own `date_confidence` that readings conflating
  1 Ululu with 7 September are in error. This file follows ABC 5 as above. The two files disagree.

## 3. The west, first years (604 - 599 BC)

- **Ashkelon**, Kislev of the accession year: taken, plundered, made a ruin heap, its king carried
  off. **[A]** ABC 4/5, with an excavated **burn layer** (Stager, Leon Levy Expedition).
  *Coordinate corrected:* the pool's 31.6656, 34.5664 is the modern city; Tel Ashkelon is 31.6639,
  34.5458, which the journey file uses.
- **Jerusalem**, c. 604-603: **Jehoiakim becomes his servant three years**. **[A]** 2 Kings 24:1.
  **Gap:** no Babylonian text names Jerusalem in this window; the date is inferred from Ashkelon.
- **601 BC, the Egyptian frontier**: an invasion ends in a mutually costly, indecisive battle, the
  Chronicle recording heavy losses on both sides. **[A]** ABC 5. The site is unnamed; the Pelusium
  pin is **[R]**.
- **599 BC, the Arabs of Kedar**: a raid for flocks, gods, and people. **[A]** ABC 5;
  **Jeremiah 49:28-33** narrates it as judgment. **[R]** for location: no place-name survives in the
  cuneiform, and Dumat al-Jandal is reconstructed from the biblical text.
- **Sippar, Ebabbar. Pool tag corrected as to date.** The pool dated the rebuild to c. 599 BC **[A]**.
  The **Sippar Cylinder of Nabonidus** says only that a former king rebuilt Ebabbar and that after
  **forty-five years** the wall sagged. The year is arithmetic done on that interval; nothing is dated
  to a regnal year of Nebuchadnezzar. **[A]** for the fact of the rebuilding, **[R]** for the date.

## 4. The first taking of Jerusalem (597 BC)

- **2 Adar of the seventh year**, 15/16 March 597: the city taken, the king seized. The one dated day
  where the Babylonian and Hebrew records touch and agree. **[A]** ABC 5 (BM 21946); 2 Kings 24:10-17.
- **Quotation problem, resolved by choosing one translation.** The pool's quotation was a composite:
  "encamped against" is **Grayson (1975)**; "Addaru ... seized the city and captured the king" is the
  **livius.org** reproduction. The spliced string matches neither. The journey file quotes the
  **Livius wording unaltered**, and says so in `quote_source`. Grayson's reading, recorded here for
  anyone who prefers it: "He encamped against the city of Judah and on the second day of the month
  Adar he captured the city (and) seized (its) king."
- **Jehoiachin deported; Mattaniah installed as Zedekiah.** **[A]** 2 Kings 24:12-17.
  **Correction:** the pool attached "after an eighteen-month siege" to the 597 capture. False.
  Jehoiachin reigned three months (2 Kings 24:8) and ABC 5 gives the capture flatly on 2 Adar. The
  eighteen months belong to the **second** campaign, from 10 Tevet (2 Kings 25:1). Duration deleted.
- **Ezekiel** goes out in this column and dates his visions from the captivity. **[A]** Ezekiel 1:1-2.
- **Weidner ration tablets**, Babylon, c. 592: oil and barley for **"Yaukin, king of the land of
  Yahud"** and his sons. **[A]** Excavated by Koldewey, published by Weidner. The best
  Babylonian-side corroboration of a biblical captivity narrative.

## 5. Elam, the revolt, and the end of the record (596 - 594 BC)

- **596 BC, Elam. Corrected.** The pool's chronology claimed he campaigned against Elam, "subduing the
  kingdom". ABC 5, ninth year, says the opposite: the **Elamite** king "was afraid and, panic falling
  on him, he returned to his own land". No engagement, no subjugation. The pool's own geography lens
  had this right, so the two lenses contradicted each other; the journey file follows the Chronicle.
  **[R]** for Susa as objective, which the text does not name.
- **595 BC, tenth year: a revolt inside Babylonia**, put down with the king killing many of his own
  army. **[A]** ABC 5. The pool had misdated this to 595 as a Levantine campaign; corrected.
- **594 BC, eleventh year:** campaigning in the west, and then **ABC 5 breaks off**. **[A]**, and the
  break is a documentary fact.
- **The gap is the central fact of this life.** 594 to 562 BC: no chronicle tablet. Everything after
  is Hebrew Bible, Josephus quoting lost historians, undated brick, or one broken fragment.

## 6. The second siege, the burning, the aftermath (588 - 582 BC)

- **Zedekiah rebels** with Egyptian encouragement. **[A]** 2 Kings 24:20; Jeremiah 52:3.
- **Siege opens 10 Tevet of Zedekiah's ninth year**, about eighteen months long. **[A]** 2 Kings 25:1;
  Jeremiah 52:4.
- **The 587 / 586 problem, flagged and left open.** No Chronicle tablet survives for these years. The
  pool is internally inconsistent: it opens the siege in 588 and burns the city in 586, roughly thirty
  months, while calling the siege eighteen months. The journey file **keeps both dates as given and
  states the contradiction in `date_confidence` at both stops** rather than sliding a year to make the
  arithmetic close. Standard chronologies run 588-587 or 587-586; nothing reachable supports 588-586.
- **Lachish** falls in the same campaign. **[A]** destruction layer II (Starkey, Ussishkin) and
  **Lachish Letter IV**, which reports watching for signal fires and no longer seeing Azekah.
- **7-10 Av: Nebuzaradan burns the Temple, palace, and city and breaks up the bronze.** **[A]**
  2 Kings 25:8-17; Jeremiah 52:12-23. Nebuchadnezzar is not at the wall; the narrative has him at
  Riblah.
- **Zedekiah** flees, is taken **on the plains of Jericho**, brought to **Riblah**, sees his sons
  killed, is blinded and fettered. **[A]** 2 Kings 25:4-7; Jeremiah 39:4-7.
- **Riblah coordinate: contradiction flagged, canon kept.** The pool notes Riblah is at 34.4333,
  36.5500 and that its own value is ~4 km off. But **34.4667, 36.5333 is the canonical shared pin**
  already in `ezekiel.journey.json`, and this file inherits it byte-identical rather than fragmenting a
  shared location.
- **Gedaliah made governor at Mizpah.** **[A]** 2 Kings 25:22-23. *Coordinate corrected:* Tell
  en-Nasbeh is 31.8851, 35.2164; the pool's 31.9214, 35.1953 sits 4.5 km away in open country.
- **Jeremiah** is handed to Gedaliah under the only order in the Hebrew Bible attributed verbatim to
  Nebuchadnezzar: "Take him, and look well to him, and do him no harm." **[A]** Jeremiah 39:11-14.
- **582 BC:** campaign against **Ammon and Moab** **[A]** Josephus, *Antiquities* 10.181-182; **third
  deportation**, 745 people **[A]** Jeremiah 52:30. Josephus is the sole witness to the Transjordan
  campaign; there is no cuneiform trace of it.

## 7. Tyre, the cedar, and Egypt (586 - 568 BC)

- **Thirteen-year siege of Tyre** under King Ithobaal III. **[A]** Josephus, *Against Apion* 1.21,
  quoting **Menander of Ephesus**, whose text survives only inside Josephus.
- **The outcome, [R] and honestly so.** Negotiated submission, not storm; Tyre keeps its king.
  **Ezekiel 29:18** concedes the army "had no wages" from Tyre, the strongest independent evidence
  that the city was never plundered. No Babylonian inscription mentions Tyre at all, and some
  historians dispute the whole reconstruction on that ground.
- **Wadi Brisa (Wadi esh-Sharbin):** twin rock reliefs and long inscriptions on the cedar road.
  **[A]** Da Riva (2012). Undated within the reign.
  *Pool-internal coordinate contradiction, resolved toward the geography lens:* geography gives
  34.4385, 36.3138; the afterlife lens gave 34.3936, 36.3867, ~8 km away. The journey file uses the
  former.
- **Nahr el-Kalb:** his stele among those of Ramesses II and every later conqueror. **[A]** Factum
  Foundation documentation. Erosion has destroyed the text beyond name and titles. That is a **gap**,
  not an uncertainty.
- **The Wadi Brisa quotation is carried flagged unverified.** "On that day, Lebanon, the mountain of
  cedars..." is widely reproduced but could not be checked against Da Riva's edition, which was not
  reachable. The reservation is stated inside the journey file's `quote_source`.
- **568/567 BC, thirty-seventh year: Egypt.** **BM 33041**, badly broken, records a campaign and names
  **Amasis**, then breaks. Whether troops entered the Delta is **not established**. **[R]**
- **Tahpanhes (Tell Defenneh):** Jeremiah's prophecy of a throne set on hidden stones at Pharaoh's
  door. **[A]** as text (Jeremiah 43:8-13); **[R]** as event, since no Babylonian or Egyptian source
  says it happened. *Coordinate corrected:* Tell Defenneh is 30.8606, 32.1714; the pool's longitude
  put the pin ~14 km east.

## 8. The king in brick (undated within the reign)

The inscriptions are expansive about what was built and silent about when.

- **Esagila** rebuilt and enlarged. **[A]** East India House Inscription and other building texts.
  **Undated.** The pool's -590 is invented and has been removed, with the removal stated in
  `date_confidence`.
- **Etemenanki**, raised so its head should "rival heaven". **[A]** his building texts and Koldewey's
  excavation of the foundation. **Undated**; the pool's -580 likewise removed. Later identified with
  the Tower of Babel, an identification popularised in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries (Andrew
  George, AfO 51, 2005). The pool's two lenses gave different coordinates (32.5407 vs 32.5361); the
  file inherits the canonical pin.
- **Ishtar Gate and Processional Way**, principal phase c. 575 BC. **[A]** Koldewey; dedicatory
  inscriptions in situ. *Contradiction flagged:* Wikipedia gives the gate site as 32.5433, 44.4222,
  about 740 m from the canonical atlas pin at 32.5423, 44.4206, which is kept for consistency with the
  other Babylon files.
- **Borsippa, Ezida** and its ziggurat. **[A]** the Borsippa cylinder.
- **The "Median Wall". Pool tag corrected from [A] to [R].** The pool cited **Xenophon, *Anabasis*
  2.4.12**, who writes two hundred years later and **never names Nebuchadnezzar**. No contemporary
  Babylonian text attributes that line to him. The pool's geography lens tagged the identical claim
  **[R]**; the journey file takes the cautious reading.
- **The Hanging Gardens. [R], and disputed at the root.** No inscription of his reign mentions a
  garden. The tradition runs through **Berossus in Josephus, *Against Apion* 1.19**, repeated by
  Diodorus and Strabo centuries later. **Stephanie Dalley** (2013) argues the wonder belongs to
  Sennacherib's Nineveh, a century earlier, in a different empire.
- **The queen. Corrected.** The pool named **Amytis**, daughter of **Cyaxares**. Josephus at *Against
  Apion* 1.19 says only that the wife was "brought up in Media" and gives no name. Berossus, through
  Josephus and Abydenus, makes the bride a daughter of **Astyages**; "Cyaxares" is a modern emendation
  adopted because Astyages was too young to have a marriageable daughter c. 614 BC. The journey file
  states the emendation as an emendation.

## 9. Madness, death, afterlife

- **Daniel 4, the seven "times" of madness. Pool tag corrected from [A] to [R].** The pool tagged it
  attested while its own note conceded there is no Babylonian attestation, which is the definition of
  a legendary item here. No chronicle, inscription, ration list, or dated contract knows of any
  interruption in the reign.
- **4Q242, the Prayer of Nabonidus**, Qumran: a Babylonian king afflicted seven years at Teima. **[A]**
  as a manuscript. Many scholars hold the tradition was originally told of **Nabonidus** and
  transferred to Nebuchadnezzar in Daniel. **Not settled**; recorded as a live disagreement.
- **Death at Babylon, forty-third year, 562 BC.** **[A]** administrative date formulas and the
  Babylonian King List; **Berossus via Josephus** 1.20 adds the illness.
- **No tomb has ever been located.** Nearly two decades of excavation over the palace and temple
  precincts identified nothing as a burial. A **gap in the record**, not a documented negative finding.
- **Amel-Marduk** releases **Jehoiachin** in his accession year. **[A]** 2 Kings 25:27-30, with
  tablets naming Amel-Marduk as king.
- **Berlin. Figures corrected.** The pool said "roughly 800 crates" and a display date "in the 1930s".
  Wikipedia says only "hundreds"; detailed accounts give about **500**, shipped after the First World
  War, arriving from **1926**, assembled from **1928**, the gate **opened in 1930**.
- **London**: the **East India House Inscription**, British Museum since **1938**. **[A]**
- **Babylon 1987-88, Saddam Hussein. Quotation corrected.** "Built by Saddam, son of Nebuchadnezzar,
  to glorify Iraq" **is not the excavated brick text**. The documented stamp reads: "In the reign of
  the victorious Saddam Hussein, the president of the Republic, may God keep him, the guardian of the
  great Iraq and the renovator of its renaissance and the builder of its great civilization, the
  rebuilding of the great city of Babylon was done in 1987." The journey file carries the shorter
  wording explicitly as journalism's paraphrase, not as the brick.
- **Langdon. Corrected.** *Die neubabylonischen Königsinschriften* (VAB 4, 1912) was written by
  **Stephen Langdon in English** and rendered into German by **Rudolf Zehnpfund**, named on the title
  page. It is superseded chiefly by **RINBE**, not by Da Riva's Brisa volume alone.

## Travellers named in campas, and the ones deliberately not

Named, because a real relation exists in each case:

- **Solomon** (`solomon`): the Temple burned in Av is his, and the bronze pillars broken up for
  transport are his work. One-directional across four centuries, which the brief permits.
- **Ezekiel** (`ezekiel`): deported in Nebuchadnezzar's own column of 597, and prophesies about him by
  name, including the concession that the army got no wages out of Tyre.
- **Cyrus** (`cyrus`): takes Babylon and returns the identical Temple vessels Nebuchadnezzar carried
  off. The relation runs through specific objects and a specific act reversed.
- **Saddam Hussein** (`saddam_hussein`): stamps his own bricks into Nebuchadnezzar's courses and
  claims descent from him in print.

**Not** named, though the interlock lens offered them, because the only link is shared ground or
shared centuries: **Alexander**, **Diogenes of Sinope**, **Gurdjieff** (whose Sarmoung claim is about
the city, not this king), **Benjamin de Tudela**, **Maimonides**, **Rashi** (institutional descent
from the exile, real history but not a relation to the man), **Dido**, **Sheba**, **Jesus**.
