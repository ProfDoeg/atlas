# Fred Rogers (1928-2003): research report

*2026-08-02. For the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Fred McFeely Rogers, Presbyterian minister, composer, puppeteer, host of "Mister Rogers' Neighborhood." Slug `fred_rogers`. Queue item 3; census entry #128, three corpus documents, two contexts.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in place. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim whose only carrier is a secondary quotations page. Coordinates carry their own tag: in this dossier they were the weakest part of the pool by a wide margin.

## Method

The pool arrived in five lenses and went through adversarial verification, which overturned fourteen items, eleven of them coordinates. Every correction is carried into `fred_rogers.journey.json` and restated below with its reason. Where the pool contradicted *itself* across lenses, the contradiction is printed and left standing; where an outside source settled it, the settlement is named. The life is small in space, eleven of forty-five stops sitting on one address; the base above Wikipedia is thin, King's *The Good Neighbor* (2018) being cited throughout the pool and never fetched; the quotations, by contrast, are unusually well attested.

## 1. Latrobe, 1928-1946

- Born 20 March 1928 at 705 Main Street, Latrobe **[A, Wikipedia "Fred Rogers"]**. Coordinate **[A]** 40.3189, -79.3772 (Nominatim); the pool's 40.3167, -79.3781 is ~250 m off, rejected.
- Father James Hillis Rogers is president of the McFeely Brick Company **[A]**. The pool credited the fortune to "his grandfather's brick works"; the firm carries his *mother's* family name and his *father* ran it. Corrected.
- "Only child" is wrong as written: the Rogerses adopt his sister Elaine when he is about eleven, c. 1939 **[A]**. He is their only biological child.
- 737 Weldon Street, three-storey brick **[A]**. Coordinate **[A]** 40.3182, -79.3766; the pool's 40.3145, -79.3760 is ~410 m south, rejected. Asthmatic, overweight, bullied as "Fat Freddy"; piano from about age five **[A]**.
- A vacation house outside Latrobe with a pool where he learns to swim **[A]**. **Gap:** no address anywhere consulted, so it is folded into the Weldon Street stop rather than pinned.
- Childhood winter trips to Florida **[A]**. The pool placed them in Jacksonville at 30.3322, -81.6557; Wikipedia supports only "Florida," and Jacksonville enters this life as Joanne Byrd's hometown. **Rejected as overspecified.**
- Latrobe High School 1946: student council president, National Honor Society, yearbook editor **[A]**. Coordinate 40.3163, -79.3822 **[R]**, approximate; the 1940s building may not be the present campus.

## 2. Hanover, Greensburg, Winter Park, New York, 1946-1952

- One year at Dartmouth before transferring **[A]**. The pool's geography entry said "1946-1948" while its own prose said "roughly a year on the Hanover campus"; the journey uses 1946 with transfer by 1948 and says so in `date_confidence`. Coordinate 43.7044, -72.2887, the Green **[A]**.
- Registers for the draft at Greensburg in 1948, aged twenty, classified 1-A; reclassified unqualified after an Armed Forces physical on 12 October 1950 **[A]**. Greensburg coordinate is city-level **[R]**; **no source places the 1950 physical anywhere at all**.
- Rollins College, Winter Park: Bachelor of Music in composition, magna cum laude, 1951; meets fellow music student Sara Joanne Byrd of Jacksonville there **[A]**. Coordinate **[A]** 28.5926, -81.3483 (Nominatim, 1000 Holt Avenue); the pool's 28.5978, -81.3492 is ~580 m north of campus, rejected.
- Joins NBC in New York shortly after graduating; floor director and assistant producer on "Your Hit Parade" and "The Kate Smith Hour" **[A]**. Coordinate 40.7587, -73.9787 **[A]**.
- Marries Sara Joanne Byrd 1952, married until his death **[A]**. **Contradiction inside the pool, left standing:** chronology gives the place as Jacksonville, geography correctly records the ceremony site as undetermined, and no source names a wedding location. The journey pins Jacksonville and declares the pin the bride's hometown standing in for an unknown church.

## 3. Pittsburgh: WQED, McFarland, the seminary, 1953-1963

- Leaves NBC for Pittsburgh in 1953 to help launch WQED **[A]**.
- **The most consequential coordinate fix here.** WQED, 4802 Fifth Avenue, geocodes to 40.4467, -79.9447 (Nominatim), confirmed by the 2018 USPS release giving the Fred Rogers Studio address as 4802 5th Ave, Pittsburgh PA 15213 **[A]**. The pool's 40.4444, -79.9508 is ~600 m southwest and was replicated across seven entries. All eight WQED-family stops now use the corrected pair.
- Co-creates "The Children's Corner" with Josie Carey from 1954, working the puppets from behind the set; Daniel Striped Tiger, King Friday XIII and X the Owl originate here **[A]**. Year only; month and day defaulted to 01.
- Studies child development under Margaret McFarland at the University of Pittsburgh **[A]**. Coordinate 40.4444, -79.9528 **[R]**: carried from the pool ungeocoded, no source naming a building. The one uncorrected soft pin in the Pittsburgh cluster.
- Bachelor of Divinity magna cum laude, Pittsburgh Theological Seminary, 1962, studied part-time from the mid-1950s **[A]**. Coordinate **[A]** 40.4674, -79.9209 (Nominatim, 616 North Highland Avenue); the pool's 40.4664, -79.9280 lands ~600 m west, off campus; rejected.
- Ordained by the Pittsburgh Presbytery in 1963 with a charge to serve children through television rather than a pulpit **[A]**. **Gap:** the church or chapel is not given anywhere consulted; the journey pins downtown Pittsburgh as a declared placeholder. (The pool's chronology lens lists the 1962 degree *after* the 1964 Toronto entry: sequencing artefact, corrected in ordering.)

## 4. Toronto, 1963-1967

- The CBC contracts Rogers in 1963 to develop and host "Misterogers," fifteen minutes, black and white, running 1963-1967; his first appearance on camera **[A]**. Works on "Butternut Square" with puppeteer Ernie Coombs, 1964 **[A]**. Acquires the rights and returns to Pittsburgh, 1967 **[A]**.
- **Contradiction withdrawn.** The pool's note claimed one Wikipedia passage dates the CBC work from 1961 against another giving 1963-1967. Verification found no 1961 date anywhere in the current article. The contradiction is not reconciled, it is deleted, because it was never there.
- Coordinate **[A]** 43.6635, -79.3773 (Nominatim, 354 Jarvis Street); the pool's 43.6629, -79.3733 is ~330 m off, rejected.

## 5. The Neighborhood, 1967-1975

- Premieres nationally 19 February 1968 from WQED, distributed by National Educational Television; 895 episodes eventually **[A, Wikipedia "Mister Rogers' Neighborhood"]**.
- Testifies before Senator John Pastore's Subcommittee on Communications, 1 May 1969, defending twenty million dollars of federal funding **[A; transcript at Speakola and Current]**. Coordinate 38.8926, -77.0067, the Russell Senate Office Building **[R]**, reasonable for 1969, but **no source names the room**.
- Father dies 1970; becomes pescetarian **[A]**, and vegetarian in the early 1980s **[A]**. The pool's specific year 1982 is **rejected**: Wikipedia says only "the early 1980s."
- Chairs the White House Conference on Children and Youth, 1970 **[A]**. Coordinate is a Washington placeholder **[R]**; no venue given.
- Forms Family Communications, Inc., 1971 **[A]**. Stops producing new episodes 1975, reruns continuing **[A]**.

## 6. Return, honours, retirement, 1978-2001

- "Old Friends ... New Friends," thirty-minute adult interview programme, twenty episodes, from 1978 **[A]**. Resumes production 1979 **[A]**; the pool called this "after a five-year hiatus," but Wikipedia's hiatus runs 1975 to 1978, roughly four years. **Arithmetic corrected.**
- **Attested but placeless, therefore unpinned:** Outstanding Rollins Alumnus 1970; Peabody 1992; "Fred Rogers' Heroes" 1994; the acting role on "Dr. Quinn, Medicine Woman" 1996; Television Hall of Fame 1999 **[A]**. None carries a place in the pool and none gets an invented coordinate.
- Lifetime Achievement Award, 24th Annual Daytime Emmy Awards **[A]**. **Contradiction between transcript hosts, resolved against the pool's own citation:** Speakola labels its transcript "14 September 1997, Pasadena Civic Auditorium," which is the 49th Primetime Emmys, a different ceremony. American Rhetoric and Wikipedia both give 21 May 1997 at Radio City Music Hall, and Wikipedia names Rogers as the recipient. Journey uses 1997-05-21 and Radio City; the pool's **[R]** for the venue is upgraded to **[A]**. Coordinate 40.76, -73.98 is coarse but lands on the building.
- Hollywood Walk of Fame star, 1998 **[A]**. **No stop:** no source gives the star's block, and the journey will not invent a Hollywood Boulevard coordinate.
- Final episode taped at WQED 1 December 2000; final original episode airs 31 August 2001, closing a thirty-three-year run at seventy-three **[A]**.
- "You are an important person just the way you are. You can make healthy decisions." **[A]** Rogers under oath in *Universal City Studios v. Sony Corp.* (C.D. Cal. 1979), quoted at *Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios*, 464 U.S. 417, 446 n.27 (1984). The pool had it **[R]**, primary source unlocated, and truncated the second sentence. Both fixed. Year only; the pool's "1979-09" is unverified.

## 7. The last year, 2002-2003

- Final commencement address at Dartmouth, 9 June 2002 **[A; transcript via SingjuPost]**. Presidential Medal of Freedom, East Room, 9 July 2002 **[A]**, coordinate 38.8977, -77.0365 **[A]**.
- Stomach cancer diagnosed October 2002; surgery 6 January 2003 **[A]**. **Contradiction inside the pool, resolved:** the afterlife lens said "diagnosed only weeks earlier," the chronology lens gave October 2002, and October to February is about four months. The afterlife phrasing is rejected.
- Dies at his Pittsburgh home 27 February 2003, aged 74, Joanne present, after last rites from Archabbot Douglas Nowicki **[A]**. **Contradiction inside the pool, resolved by arithmetic:** geography said "one month after his seventy-fifth birthday," afterlife said "one month before." Born 20 March 1928, died 27 February 2003: three weeks *before*, at 74. Geography is wrong.
- Coordinate 40.4359, -79.9231 **[R]**: Wikipedia says only "his Pittsburgh home," so the pin is a Squirrel Hill centroid, not an address. The afterlife lens gave 40.4406, -79.9959 for the same event, which is downtown at roughly Heinz Hall and contradicts its own prose; rejected.
- Private funeral at Unity Chapel, Unity Cemetery, 1 March 2003 **[A]**. Interred in a mausoleum **owned by his mother's family** **[A]**; the afterlife lens called it "the Rogers family mausoleum." McFeelys, not Rogerses.
- **Three coordinates for one cemetery.** Geography gives 40.2953, -79.3567 (~5.5 km out in open country), afterlife gives 40.3115, -79.3765 (~3.6 km east), Nominatim gives 40.3084, -79.4189. Journey uses Nominatim for funeral and burial alike.
- Public memorial at Heinz Hall, 3 May 2003, about 2,700 people **[A]**. Coordinate **[A]** 40.4426, -80.0017; the pool's 40.4407, -79.9958 is ~550 m southeast. Performers: Perlman, Yo-Yo Ma **by video**, organist Alan Morrison **[A]**. The pool's "Perlman and Yo-Yo Ma perform" overstates a video appearance.

## 8. Afterlife

- Fred Rogers Center, now the Fred Rogers Institute, at Saint Vincent College **[A, Wikipedia "Saint Vincent College": the college houses the Rogers archives and an exhibit; the institute cites over 22,000 items]**. Pool tag **[R]** (a 404) upgraded. **Three coordinates again:** geography 40.3402, -79.4033 and afterlife 40.3193, -79.3903 are 5.3 km and 3.1 km out, in the wrong township, against Wikipedia's 40.2928, -79.4021, which the journey uses. Founding year 2003 is **not confirmed**, and "served for years as a trustee" is **unsourced** and dropped.

- USPS Forever stamp, first day of issue Friday 23 March 2018, 11 a.m. ET, at WQED's Fred Rogers Studio **[A, USPS pr18_018, read directly]**. The release does **not** describe the stamp art, so "with King Friday XIII" rests on other sourcing, **[R]**.
- "Won't You Be My Neighbor?" (2018) among the highest-grossing biographical documentaries in American box-office history **[A as relayed from Focus Features data and 2018 trade reporting, not fetched, so the superlative is reported, not audited]**. ## 9. Corpus interlock: essay 174

- Essay 174, "Make Believe," names Fred Rogers and groups him with **Schelling, Pestalozzi, Froebel and Steiner** as pedagogues who made play and imagination "crucial elements in the formation of a national subject" **[A, `essays/174_make_believe.md`]**. All four already have atlas journeys. This sentence is why the subject is queued at all.
- The same text is duplicated as Instagram raw material, EN and ES **[A, `essays/instagram/2023-07-31-sin-titulo.md`; `.../2023-07-31-el-pais-de-la-fantasia-mantiene.md`]**: one claim, three carriers, which is the census's three-document count. Census #128 lists him with one journey and two contexts, from the same cluster as Mesmer, Traute and Terry Gilliam **[A, line 134]**. Mesmer has a census entry but no journey.json, so he is named here and in no campa.
- **Canonical pins inherited byte-identical into segment 1:**
  - Schelling, the Tübinger Stift attic with Hegel and Hölderlin, 1790-10-18, 48.521 / 9.058 **[A, `schelling.journey.json`]**. The lens rendered the latitude 48.52; the file wins.
  - Froebel, the Universal German Kindergarten founded at Bad Blankenburg town hall, 1840-06-28, 50.6795 / 11.2723 **[A, `froebel.journey.json`]**. **The lens transcribed the longitude as 50.813, which would drop the pin into the Black Sea**; it flagged its own error.
  - Itten at Hofwil, 1904-10-01, 47.0213 / 7.4617 **[A, `itten.journey.json`]**, absorbing "the kindergarten method of Friedrich Fröbel ... in the reform tradition of Pestalozzi," the stop that chains both into the Bauhaus Vorkurs.
  - Steiner before Édouard Schuré's circle, Paris, 1906-05-25, 48.8566 / 2.3522 **[A, `steiner.journey.json`]**. Blavatsky holds the identical pin on 1884-03-28 **[A, `blavatsky.journey.json`]**, named in that campa as the pin's other tenant.
- **Two canonical pins deliberately declined.** The lens offered the Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262) and the Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354) as threshold pins rhyming with the trolley tunnel, the shoe change, the sweater, tagging its own offer **[R]** because no source ties Rogers to either. This journey does not take them: a structural echo is not a stop.
- **Reich.** Essay 174 names "Wilhelm Reich's make-believe science of orgone" in the same cluster **[A]**. Reich's journey moves him toward a federal prison in Pennsylvania **[A, `reich.journey.json`]**, but the lens is right that this is coincidence. Reich is named once, in segment 1's closing campa, on the essay's authority and not the map's.
- **The negative result, as a gap.** No journey.json contains "Presbyterian," "ordained minister," or "children's television" as Rogers-biography content; the only Pennsylvania hits (Muybridge's Penn studio, Reich's prison transfer, a Pittsburgh dissertation cited for Giulio Camillo) are incidental, and every seminary or puppet-theatre hit (Schelling's Stift, Hesse's Maulbronn, Goethe's puppet theatre) is structurally analogous and attested to nothing **[A, grep across `working/journeys/*.journey.json`]**.

## Contradictions and gaps, stated as such

Five contradictions are carried forward unresolved and are flagged where they arise: the wedding location (section 2), the Emmy date and venue between transcript hosts (section 6), the cardigan's origin, the Fred Rogers Center's founding year, and the statue's completion date against its listed date (section 8). The gaps: the vacation-house address outside Latrobe. The wedding church. The ordination chapel. The Senate hearing room. The Squirrel Hill street address. The dedication date and exact site of the North Shore statue. The date of WQED's studio renaming. The location of the Latrobe marker. The date of deposit for the Nesbitt Room archive. The translation count. The 1950 Armed Forces physical has a date and no place.
