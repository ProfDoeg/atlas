# Eugène Grasset (1845-1917): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject slug: `eugene_grasset`.*

Swiss-born decorator, illustrator, poster designer, glass and mosaic cartoonist, type
designer and teacher; naturalized French 1891. The working life is spent entirely in Paris
and its southern suburb; the only journeys outside France in the record are the Swiss
childhood, one year in Egypt, and one probable exhibition trip to London.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or
inference, including a source's own hedged language. Contradictions are flagged, not
adjudicated. Where verification overturned a gathering pass, both versions are recorded,
because the overturned one circulates.

---

## 1. Lausanne and the Swiss formation (1845-1870)

- Born **Eugène Samuel Grasset, 25 May 1845, Lausanne**, son of a cabinetmaker and
  sculptor. **[A: fr/en.wikipedia, 'Eugène Grasset']** The house is not identified anywhere
  reachable; the pin is the historic city centre. **[R for the coordinate]**
- Draws under the Lake Geneva painter **François-Louis David Bocion**, c. 1859-1863.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia; Wikidata Q672079, whose student list names Grasset and Steinlen]**
  No start or end date is documented; the range is the article's.
- Studies **architecture at the Zurich Polytechnicum**, 1861, without finishing.
  **[A: en/fr.wikipedia]** Matriculation, term dates, reason for leaving: **gap**.
- **Travels to Egypt, 1866.** **[A: fr.wikipedia]** No city, route, duration or companion is
  named anywhere reachable; the Cairo pin (30.0444, 31.2357) is a convention, labelled as such
  in the journey. **[R for the location]** fr.wikipedia asserts the trip's imagery recurs in
  his later ornament but names no work; that causal claim is the source's own. **[R]**
- **Theatre decorator and sculptor for the Lausanne theatre**, c. 1869-1870.
  **[A: en/fr.wikipedia]**

### Contradiction 1: the Viollet-le-Duc citation

en.wikipedia states that Grasset met **Eugène Viollet-le-Duc** in the Lausanne period and
that the meeting was formative. An earlier pass attached **Wikidata Q524154 P1066 ('student
of')** as corroboration. **That citation is wrong.** P1066 on Q524154 has the single value
**Q672079 = François Bocion**; Viollet-le-Duc does not appear in the property at all.

The meeting therefore rests on **en.wikipedia alone, unsourced there**, is carried as **[R]**
with the hedge in `date_confidence`, and the P1066 citation is reassigned to the Bocion stop.
The doctrine is stated in the journey as inherited argument, not conversation.

## 2. Paris, the trades of ornament (1871-1889)

- **Settles in Paris, 1871**, year of the siege, the Commune and the Semaine sanglante.
  **[A: en/fr.wikipedia]** Designs furniture, wallpaper, fabric, tapestry, ceramic, jewelry.
- Marries **Euphrasie Charlotte Charrotton**, born Gibraltar 15 April 1848.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]**

**Contradiction 2 (fabrication retracted).** An earlier pass dated the marriage to 1871,
"the same year" as the move to Paris. fr.wikipedia reads *"Il épouse, à une date
inconnue..."*. The 1871 year is a fabrication and is deleted; name and Gibraltar birth stand
**[A]**, the wedding date is a **gap**.

- Teaches at the **École de la rue Thévenot** from 1875, drawing for jewelers' apprentices.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]** The street has been absorbed into the redrawn 2nd arrondissement;
  the coordinate is approximate. **[R for the coordinate]**
- Illustrated postcards and estampes for income, c. 1877. **[R: en.wikipedia]**
- **1880:** decorates and furnishes the **rue Madame** townhouse of the printer and
  collector **Charles Gillot**. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** Gillot's photorelief process is the
  technical precondition of the later career; calling the partnership the hinge is this
  report's reading. **[R]**
- **1883:** illustrates for **Paris illustré** **[A: fr.wikipedia; Wikidata Q524154 P108]**
  and the **Histoire des Quatre Fils Aymon** **[A: fr.wikipedia works list]**. The magazine's
  address is not given; the pin marks central Paris. **[R for the coordinate]**
- **1887:** begins the collaboration with master glassmaker **Félix Gaudin** on glass and
  mosaic; Gaudin is later named executor of the 1904 will. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**

## 3. The decade of the poster (1890-1899)

- **1890:** redraws the **Larousse** house mark. **[A: en/fr.wikipedia]**

### Contradiction 3: who designed the Semeuse

The popular account, and an earlier pass, credit Grasset with **designing** the sower.
fr.wikipedia is narrower: *"La version de Grasset, sur une idée de Georges Moreau,
cofondateur de Larousse, y ajoute une nymphe (souvent nommée « semeuse »)"*. He worked from
co-founder **Georges Moreau's** idea and **added the nymph to a logotype in use since 1876**.
The journey states the narrower version; the 1890 date and the mark's continued use stand
**[A]**. Coordinate: the Larousse premises on the rue du Montparnasse are at approximately
**48.8434, 2.3269**; an earlier pass gave 48.8422, 2.321, about 440 m west.

- **1890-1903:** holds the chair of **decorative composition at the École Guérin**, rue
  Vavin / rue Bréa. **[A: en/fr.wikipedia]** en.wikipedia gives 1890, other atlas material
  1890/1891; the one-year spread is **left open** in `date_confidence`.
- **1890s:** studio at the **Cité fleurie**, boulevard Arago, built from salvaged pavilions
  of the 1878 Exposition. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** No year of taking or leaving: **gap**.
- **1891:** poster *À la Place Clichy*. **[A: works list]**
- **22 June 1891:** naturalized French by decree, after twenty years in France as a Vaudois.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **1892:** posters *Chocolat Mexicain* (Masson) and *Encre L. Marquet*, the latter held by
  the **Rijksmuseum**. **[A: fr.wikipedia works list; Wikidata SPARQL over P170/P195]**

**Contradiction 4.** An earlier pass dated the Marquet poster **1895**; the works list reads
*"Encre L. Marquet la meilleure de toutes les encres, 1892"*. Corrected to **1892**.

- **Christmas 1892:** cover for **Harper's Magazine**, New York. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1894:** *The Wooly Horse* and *The Sun of Austerlitz* for **The Century Magazine**, New
  York. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

**No source places Grasset in the United States at any point.** Both are Paris-executed
commissions and the journey pins mark the **clients**, saying so: Harper & Brothers at
Franklin Square (40.7106, -74.0028) and The Century Co. at 33 East 17th Street (40.7370,
-73.9903). An earlier pass put both at **40.7484, -73.9857**, the Empire State Building, not
built until 1931.

**Contradiction 5 (a manufactured conflict, retracted).** An earlier pass reported a
**1892 / 1893 / 1894** spread for the Grafton Galleries showing of French decorative artists.
**It is not reproducible:** en.wikipedia gives one year, **1893**, and fr.wikipedia surfaces
no Grafton entry. The journey uses 1893 and records the failed verification rather than a
hedge with no source behind it.

### Contradiction 6: the *Jeanne d'Arc* poster (date and venue)

The most consequential correction in the file. An earlier pass dated *Jeanne d'Arc* for Sarah
Bernhardt to **1889-1890**, attaching it to the **Théâtre de la Renaissance** in one place
and, by atlas cross-reference, to **Jules Barbier's 1890 production at the Théâtre de la
Porte-Saint-Martin** in another. The fr.wikipedia works list gives *"Jeanne d'Arc - Sarah
Bernhardt"*, **1894, two versions**; and Bernhardt did not take over the Renaissance until
**1893**, so an 1889 poster for her production there is anachronistic. The journey adopts
**1894, two versions** at the Renaissance, with the conflict in `date_confidence`. **The
Porte-Saint-Martin and Barbier attribution is carried into no campa**, since the dating that
produced it is contradicted; it is a superseded reading, not an equal alternative.

- **April 1894:** shows at the **Salon des Cent**, rue Bonaparte, run by *La Plume*.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **1894:** mosaics and glass, *La Vie de saint Joseph*, **Église de la Madeleine, Troyes**.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia works list]**
- **1894-1895:** enamel mosaics and windows for the **Église Saint-Étienne de Briare**,
  tesserae by the local Bapterosses works, glass by Gaudin, church built 1890-1895.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]** Coordinate: **47.6328, 2.7389**; an earlier pass gave 47.641, 2.746,
  about 1 km off in open ground.
- **7 May 1895:** **Chevalier de la Légion d'honneur**. **[A: fr.wikipedia; Wikidata Q524154
  P166 with date qualifier]** An earlier pass rendered this as a broken, self-contradicting
  sentence about nationality. Clean: **naturalized 22 June 1891, chevalier 7 May 1895.**
- **1896:** ***La Plante et ses applications ornementales***. **[A: fr.wikipedia works list]**
- **11 April 1897:** lecture ***L'Art nouveau*** before the **Union centrale des arts
  décoratifs**. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **Venue correction:** an earlier pass placed it at the
  **Pavillon de Marsan**, which the Union centrale did not occupy until **1905**. The source
  names the body, not the room; the journey drops the venue for a central-Paris point flagged
  approximate. The room is a **gap**.
- **1897-1904:** illustrates the **Nouveau Larousse illustré**, seven volumes; **1899**,
  poster *Cycles et automobiles Georges Richard*. **[A: fr.wikipedia works list]**
- **7 October 1897:** the typeface is **patented**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

### Contradiction 7: the year of the typeface

fr.wikipedia gives **1897** for the design for the **Fonderie G. Peignot et Fils** and
**1898** for the issue; en.wikipedia gives the **patent as 7 October 1897**; an earlier pass
asserted **1898 as the design year**, conflicting with both. Carried with the disagreement
stated: **drawn and patented 1897, issued by Peignot 1898, shown at the Exposition 1900**.

## 4. Sceaux, the type, the teaching years (1900-1913)

- **1900:** typeface and the tapestry *Fête de printemps* at the **Exposition Universelle**,
  Champ de Mars. **[A: en/fr.wikipedia]**
- **1900:** moves to **Sceaux**, into a house formerly of the family of **Eugène Curie**,
  father of Pierre Curie; the street is renamed rue Pierre-Curie in 1907. **[R:
  fr.wikipedia; the house is not identified and no address or lease survives]**
- **1903-1904:** **École d'art graphique**, rue Madame, dates loose. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1904:** postage stamps in the **Type Grasset** for **French Indochina**. **[A:
  fr.wikipedia works list]**
- **1904-1913:** **Académie de la Grande Chaumière**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **École Estienne**, boulevard Auguste-Blanqui: listed among the school's *professeurs
  notoires* (gravure et imprimerie), teaching lettering there until his death. **[A for the
  post]** **No source gives a start year; 1904 is inferred.** **[R]** Coordinate: **48.8312,
  2.3514** (OpenStreetMap relation 1211305); an earlier pass gave 48.8291, 2.3538, 290 m
  south-east.
- **1905:** ***Méthode de composition ornementale***, Librairie centrale des Beaux-Arts.
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **1905:** illustrations for the **Petit Larousse illustré**, no stop of its own.
  **[R: fr.wikipedia works list; no source seen names the plates]**
- **20 October 1911:** **Officier de la Légion d'honneur**. **[A: fr.wikipedia; Wikidata

## 5. Death, burial, dispersal (1917-1918)

- **Dies at Sceaux, 23 October 1917, aged 72.** **[A: fr/en.wikipedia; Wikidata Q524154
  P20 = Q370498 = Sceaux, Hauts-de-Seine]**

### Contradiction 8: the death place (a hallucination retracted)

An earlier pass reported that machine-summarized reads of the Wikidata entity gave
**Suresnes** or **"Varennes-Saint-Saule"** as place of death, and carried the conflict
forward as unresolvable. **No such conflict exists.** P20 resolves to Q370498, Sceaux, and
the rendered page displays *place of death: Sceaux*. The dissenting towns were labels from
summarizing a raw Q-id, not readings of the record. The hedge is deleted rather than carried,
since carrying it misrepresents Wikidata as dissenting.

- **Buried in the cemetery of Sceaux, 26 October 1917, in a concession décennale**, the
  ten-year temporary grade of plot. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** Coordinate: **48.7798, 2.2838**
  (OpenStreetMap way 21817335); earlier passes used the town-centre point, about 650 m off.
  The gloss "cheapest and least permanent grade" is editorial, not source language.
- **c. 1927:** fr.wikipedia states it **probable** (*"il est probable que..."*) that the
  concession was reclaimed and the remains moved to the ossuary for expired concessions.
  **[R: the source hedges; nothing later settles it]** The present location of the remains is
  a genuine **gap**.
- **March 1918:** studio contents and collection (Japanese prints, Greek vases, Tanagra
  figurines, medieval antiquities) dispersed in **four sales at the Hôtel Drouot**; the 1904
  will names **Félix Gaudin** executor. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**

## 6. Afterlife and holdings

- **2011:** retrospective ***Eugène Grasset: l'art et l'ornement***, **Musée cantonal des
  Beaux-Arts**, Lausanne. **[A: fr.wikipedia, catalogue citation]**

### Contradiction 9: the holding institutions

fr.wikipedia names **Musée d'Orsay**, **Musée des Arts décoratifs**, **Petit Palais**,
**National Gallery of Art**. **Wikidata Q524154 P6379** (15 values) confirms the last two but
**does not list Orsay or the Petit Palais**, and adds nine the Wikipedia list omits, among
them Nelson-Atkins, the National Galleries of Victoria and of Canada, MoMA, the Stedelijk,
Cooper Hewitt, the Van Gogh Museum, the Rijksmuseum and the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes,
Buenos Aires. The lists are **not reconciled here**; the journey names only institutions
attested in one or the other.

- **No street, square or public monument** bearing his name is documented in the French
  biography. **[R: absence of evidence after targeted extraction, flagged as a gap, not
  asserted as proven non-existence]**

### The open Argentina question

P6379 includes the **Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, Buenos Aires**, sourced to
bellasartes.gob.ar, whose JavaScript-driven collection search could neither confirm nor deny
it from here. Targeted checks (English and French Wikipedia, Internet Archive creator and
title searches for "Grasset, Eugène") found **no mention of Argentina, Buenos Aires, or Latin
American exhibition or reception** in the biography or in his writings. With **WebSearch
unavailable this session**, the correct statement is **"not found in the sources checked"**,
not "proven absent". That distinguishes Grasset from Mucha, Klimt and Mackintosh, for whom
the question was closed negative; here it stays **open**. The journey carries one stop for
it, framed as an unconfirmed catalogue line and pinned at the museum (-34.5875, -58.3931),
not the canonical city point (-34.6037, -58.3816).

## 7. Quotations: authenticity and truncation

Ten French sentences were gathered from the OCR text of ***Méthode de composition
ornementale***, vol. I (1905), Internet Archive identifier **mthodedecomposit01gras**.
Verification against the printed text found **no fabricated quotations**, but **five silent
truncations**, four cutting the clause that carries the point, and **two misattributed
locators**. Below, the restored clause in bold and the true locator; full corrected text is
carried verbatim in the journey file.

1. *"Le but de l'Art Ornemental... d'orner les objets fabriqués* **qui, de nus qu'ils sont en
   construction pure, deviennent comme habillés pour le plaisir de l'œil.**" *Principes
   généraux*, p. 1. The pass stopped at *fabriqués* with a terminal period.
2. *"La composition de cet ornement abstrait est la clef de toute autre composition
   artistique,* **quelle qu'elle puisse être.**" *Principes généraux*, c. pp. 5-6.
3. *"L'ornement primitif... est composé presque uniquement des éléments géométriques
   simples,* **ceux que chacun connaît sans avoir nécessairement fait de la géométrie une
   étude spéciale.**" *Principes généraux*, c. pp. 6-7.
4. *"...c'est la façon personnelle d'exprimer des idées connues qui constitue le talent* **et
   l'originalité.**" Introduction, sidenote *Innovation*. The cut clause was the point.
5. *"Le vrai talent n'a guère de concurrence à redouter, et celui qui en est doué peut
   travailler au grand jour* **sans crainte d'être égalé.**" Introduction, c. pp. xi-xii.
   Truncated at both ends.

Used unaltered, no correction raised: the Introduction sentences beginning *"L'ornement est
né du désir..."*, *"Il est comme une manière..."*, *"La faculté de composition de l'ornement
est donc un don poétique..."*, and *"Nous ne voulons voir ici que le jeu ingénieux..."*.

**Not used:** *"Innover, c'est conserver en modifiant."* Its own gathering note flags it as
OCR-derived and **unchecked against a page image**, and no verification pass confirmed it in
print. Recorded so it is not quietly picked up later as verified.

## 8. Interlocks with other atlas subjects

One connection is a **relation between persons** rather than shared geography:

- **Paul Berthon** (`paul_berthon`): direct pupil at the **École Guérin**, rue Vavin, where
  Grasset held the chair; en.wikipedia calls Grasset "a far greater influence on him" such
  that "he may be regarded as his pupil". Berthon also died at **Sceaux**, 27 February 1934,
  where Grasset lived from 1900 and died in 1917. **No source connects the two residences**;
  the journey states the coincidence without asserting a link. Named in two campas.

Rejected as name-drops, so the reasoning is auditable: **Gustave Eiffel** (shared
fairground); **Mark Twain** (shared publisher, three years apart); **Bartholdi** (both made
youthful Egypt journeys); **Luc-Olivier Merson** (shared Salon criticism, where the common
name is Puvis de Chavannes, not each other); **Lionel Royer** (has an entry *in* a Larousse
Grasset illustrated, not a relation between men); **Jules Barbier** (rested wholly on the
superseded poster dating); **Saint-Exupéry** and **Mario Palanti** (Buenos Aires geography
only, against an unconfirmed holding).
