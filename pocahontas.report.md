# Pocahontas (Matoaka, Amonute; baptized Rebecca Rolfe), c. 1596-1617
*Research report, 2026-08-31, for `pocahontas.journey.json`.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source-and-contested. Contradictions are flagged and left standing unless verification actually settled them, in which case the resolution and its reason are stated.

## The evidentiary situation, stated first

Every word about this person was written by an Englishman. No writing in her hand, no dictated statement, no Powhatan document of any kind. The corpus is four authors (Smith, Strachey, Hamor, Purchas) plus three documents: Argall to Hawes (1613), Rolfe to Sandys (1617), and the St George's Gravesend burial register. Most of it was written to sell something: a colony, an investment, or the author's own importance. The two most quoted passages, the rescue and the Brentford speech, exist only in the book Smith published in 1624, seven years after she was dead and sixteen after he printed a different account of the same days. That is the central fact of the source base, and in the journey file it lives in `date_confidence`, not in the prose. The finished file has **9 segments and 42 stops**, register `national mythology: the canon is true`, calendar `gregorian`.

## 1. Tsenacommacah, c. 1596-1607

**Birth. [R]** Daughter of Wahunsenacawh (Powhatan); her mother is unnamed in every source. Private name **Matoaka**, also **Amonute**, household nickname **Pocahontas**. *(Smithsonian, "The True Story of Pocahontas"; Strachey c. 1612.)*

**Birth year. [R]** Never recorded, only back-calculated. Smith gives "a child of tenne yeares old" (1608) and "twelve or thirteene yeeres of age" (letter to Queen Anne, on 1607); the van de Passe engraving of 1616 gives twenty-one. c. 1596 is the reconciliation, not a datum.

**Birthplace and childhood residence. [R]** No document names a birthplace; Werowocomoco is convention, inferred from where her father held court. Nor does any text place her childhood: Spelman's account of Powhatan maternal custody implies she was raised chiefly in her mother's town. Both left unresolved and declared as such at the pin. **Coordinates corrected:** Werowocomoco **37.4122, -76.6489** (excavated site, Gloucester County, identified 2003). The pool's afterlife lens carried 37.3168, -76.7897, about 15 km southwest of the real site; the geography lens was right and the file uses its figure throughout.

## 2. The English arrive, 1607-1609

**Jamestown founded, May 1607. [A]** She is not present; the file's landing stop sits in her father's country, framed as news reaching him. **Smith captured and brought to Werowocomoco, December 1607 [A]**: feasting, questioning, release. *(Smith, A True Relation, 1608.)*

**The rescue. [R], contested.** Head on stones, clubs raised, the king's daughter across him. Appears in the c. 1616 letter to Queen Anne and the 1624 *Generall Historie*; **absent from Smith's own 1608 account of the same days.** Proposed explanations: embellishment, invention, misread adoption rite (Rountree and others). Smith told structurally similar rescue stories about other elite women. The contradiction between his two books is flagged and left standing.

**The word-list. [A], with corrections.** The pool carried "Tell Pocahontas to bring me three baskets" as a documented December 1607 exchange at Werowocomoco. **Date and place wrong:** it is an entry in the Powhatan vocabulary Smith printed in *A Map of Virginia* (1612), compiled across his whole Virginia stay; this correction is firm. **Wording is a paraphrase:** the primary reads "Bid Pokahontas bring hither two little Baskets, and I will give her white beads to make her a chaine" - two, not three. A full transcription of the 1612 vocabulary was **not reachable** (see gaps), so the basket count is not independently confirmed.

**1608-1609. [A]** Visits, provisioning, and captive exchanges are attested (Smith 1608; Encyclopedia Virginia); the file reads them as Powhatan tribute diplomacy in which she is her father's warrant. **The night warning of 1609 [R]** is Smith only, uncorroborated. **His gunpowder injury and departure, October 1609 [A]**, are followed by the Starving Time and the war; she leaves English writing for four years.

## 3. The Potomac years, c. 1610-1613

**Marriage to Kocoum. [A], single source.** Strachey, c. 1612: married to "a private Captaine," wording confirmed. One sentence. No children named, no residence, no duration. The manuscript was not printed until **1849**, so for 237 years this marriage was invisible to everyone writing about her. **Location [A], county corrected:** Passapatanzy, **38.2972, -77.3142**. The pool labeled it **Stafford County**; it is **King George County**, and its own correct coordinates contradicted the label. The confusion likely comes from the commemorative marker near the Potomac Creek bridge on US 1 in Stafford.

**Ka-Okee. [R], tribal tradition.** Modern Patawomeck genealogy holds Kocoum was killed and a daughter, Ka-Okee, raised among the tribe, with a living descent line. No seventeenth-century document names her. Kept and labeled as living tribal memory. **Argall's seizure, April 1613 [A]**, with the weroance Japazaws and his wife; Hamor records a copper kettle as the price. Summaries differ slightly on the day; 13 April used, variance noted.

## 4. Captivity, conversion, marriage, 1613-1614

**Held at Jamestown, then Henricus. [A]** About a year in captivity; instruction from **Alexander Whitaker**.

**Baptism. [A] as fact, [R] as to date and place. Corrected.** The pool's chronology lens headlined 1613 while its own note conceded no date is recorded. No source consulted gives one; the only firm bound is that it preceded the marriage of 5 April 1614. The file dates it **c. early 1614** and states that Henricus is inference from where she was held. Hamor's promotional letter says she "renounced publicly her country idolatry," language written to show investors their money is buying souls; no account survives from anyone with no stake in the colony, so belief versus coerced accommodation **cannot be established from the record**.

**Matchcot, March 1614. [A]** Dale takes an armed force up the Pamunkey with the hostage aboard; gunfire is exchanged; she sends word she wishes to see her father and, per Hamor, says he valued her less than old swords, pieces, or axes. Powhatan does not come; two brothers do. **Coordinates loosened:** Matchcot's site is not independently fixed, so the modern West Point area (37.5436, -76.8053) is used and precision declared.

**Rolfe's petition to Dale, 1614. [A], quotation corrected.** Hamor 1615, p. 63. The pool misquoted the first word: the primary reads "...**whose** education hath bin rude, her manners barbarous, her generation accursed...", not "her education". The letter's other quoted phrases were verified as printed.

**Marriage, 5 April 1614. [A]** James Fort church, with Dale's and Powhatan's approval; Powhatan sends an uncle. The traditional officiant **Richard Buck** is thinly documented and is not named. The **Peace of Pocahontas [A]** follows and holds until 1622.

## 5. Varina, 1614-1616

**Household location. [R], genuine contradiction, flagged.** Encyclopedia Virginia and NPS materials put the household at **Varina**, across the James from Henricus. Against that: the place-name **Varina is first documented in 1632**, after Rolfe's death, and at least one reference work calls the association a mid-nineteenth-century legend. **Not resolved.** The file uses the Varina pin (37.3819, -77.3361) and states the contradiction in the campa as well as in `date_confidence`.

**Thomas Rolfe's birth. [A] as to year and country, [R] as to the day. Corrected.** The pool tagged the whole item [R] and allowed "possibly England." England is **impossible**: Thomas is documented as an infant aboard the spring 1616 outbound voyage from Virginia. **30 January 1615, James Cittie, Virginia** is the descent-line date; Encyclopedia Virginia is the outlier in leaving a 1615-1617 window open. The [R] hedge is kept on the exact day only, and the birthplace inherits the Varina problem.

**Dale's request for a second daughter, 1615. [A]** Powhatan refuses; one marriage is enough. *(Hamor 1615.)* **Placement:** the exchange occurred at Powhatan's town and she is not recorded present, so per the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, the file pins it at her own household and dates it only to order the segment.

## 6. England, 1616-1617

**Sailing, spring 1616. [A]** The Rolfes, infant Thomas, Dale, **Uttamatomakkin**, and about a dozen Powhatan people, on Argall's ship, financed by the Virginia Company as promotional exhibition.

**Landfall at Plymouth. Contradiction, resolved against the pool's reading.** **September 1616** is Encyclopedia Virginia's timeline, which the researcher followed; **12 June 1616** comes from the Pocahontas entry, corroborated by the John Rolfe entry ("Arrival in England: June 1616") and by the Tomocomo entry, which starts the notched-stick census "upon arriving at Plymouth." **June is used**: three sources against one, and September is very hard to square with an April sailing. Encyclopedia Virginia is named as the outlier rather than suppressed. **Coordinates corrected:** the pool's 50.3658, -4.1298 sits on the Coxside side of Sutton Harbour; tightened to **50.3670, -4.1355** for the Barbican.

**London. [A] in general, gap in detail.** Received as Lady Rebecca. **Gap:** lodgings, daily movements, and most of the people she met are not itemized anywhere; Purchas is the only eyewitness who wrote about her. The **van de Passe engraving [A]** of 1616 is the only portrait from life. The **Whitehall Twelfth Night masque, 6 January 1617 [A]**, before James I and Anne of Denmark, is the most firmly documented hour of her time in England.

**Bishop John King's entertainment. [A], coordinates corrected.** Purchas: "with festival state and pomp." The pool's 51.5074, -0.0877 lies near Monument/Eastcheap, about 900 m southeast of St Paul's, contradicting its own note about the St Paul's precinct. The residence was **London House, St Paul's Churchyard**; the file uses **51.5138, -0.0984**. Verification also dated the entertainment to 1617 and confirmed King held the see 1611-1621.

**Brentford. [A] for the residence, [R] for the speech. Date corrected.** The pool's chronology lens dated Smith's visit to **1616**; its geography lens said "winter 1616-1617," and the chronology lens was the looser of the two. The residence is dated 1616-1617, Smith came after a long delay, and the party left in March 1617, so the visit falls in **early 1617**. The speech is Smith's retrospective composition, printed 1624; most historians treat the **encounter** as real and the **words** as his.

**Heacham. [R]** Norfolk tradition of a visit to Rolfe's family seat and a mulberry planted there; no contemporary document secures it. Heacham Hall burned in 1941, so the coordinates are the site, not a building.

## 7. Gravesend, March 1617

**Taken ill and put ashore. [A]** *(Rolfe to Sandys, 1617.)* **Cause of death [R], undetermined:** pneumonia, tuberculosis, smallpox, dysentery all proposed, none confirmed by any surviving medical record. Stated as a gap.

**The last words. [R], wording corrected.** The circulating "All must die. Tis enough that the child liveth" is a modernized paraphrase. The original was traced: Rolfe to Sandys, June 1617, in Kingsbury, ed., *Records of the Virginia Company of London*, vol. III, p. 71 (archive.org, `recordsofvirgini03virg`): "My wifes death is much lamented; my childe much desyred, when it is of better strength to endure so hard a passage, whose life greately extinguisheth the sorrow of her loss, **saying all must die, but tis enough that her childe liueth**." The manuscript wording is used, and **the syntax is ambiguous about who is speaking**: Rolfe's "saying" is usually read as reporting her, but reads equally well as his own consolation. Attributed as Rolfe's report, never as a verified deathbed utterance.

**The burial register, 21 March 1616/17. [A], transcription corrected.** Verified against Wyndham Robertson, *Pocahontas, alias Matoaka, and her Descendants* (Richmond, 1887), p. 29 (archive.org, `cu31924029771130`):

> 1616 - March 21. Rebecca Wrolfe, wyffe of Thomas Wrolfe, gent. A Virginia lady borne, was buried in the chauncel.

The pool garbled three details: "Thomas **Wrolfe**," not "Thomas Rolfe"; "**A Virginia** lady borne," not "Virginian"; "in the **chauncel**," not "in ye chancell"; and the year stands as **1616**, Old Style. Its substantive note stands: the forename should be John, and Thomas was the son's name. Coordinates **51.4441, 0.3685** verified exactly.

**The fire. [A], hedge removed.** The pool wrote "burned in 1727 (or 1731 per some accounts)." **There is no source dispute.** Destroyed by fire in **1727**, rebuilt under an Act of Parliament dated **7 May 1731**; 1731 is the rebuild, not a variant fire date. **Thomas left in England [A]** in the care of Sir Lewis Stukley, later his uncle Henry Rolfe; sources place the handover variously at Gravesend and Plymouth, and the variance is noted.

## 8. Afterlife

**The 1624 printing. [A]** *The Generall Historie* puts the rescue into print for the first time and fixes the national fable. **Chapman's *Baptism of Pocahontas* [A]**, commissioned 1837 and installed 1840 in the Capitol Rotunda, is **not staged as a stop**: she was never in Washington, and the painting is Chapman's act in Chapman's place. It appears as a `suggested_ref` on **her own** Henricus baptism stop.

**Stamp and statue. [A], contradiction resolved.** The 1907 Jamestown Exposition five-cent stamp, engraved from van de Passe, is usually cited as the first US stamp honoring a named Native American. On the Partridge bronze the pool's chronology lens said installed at Jamestown in 1907; **its afterlife lens had it right.** Commissioned c. 1906 for the **1907 exposition, held at Norfolk**; loaned to the Corcoran Gallery; **dedicated at Jamestown 3 June 1922**. The Gravesend replica date, **5 October 1958**, is correct.

**The Racial Integrity Act, 1924. [A]** The one-sixteenth carve-out, the "Pocahontas exception," inserted so that First Families of Virginia claiming descent from her would not be reclassified under an otherwise absolute one-drop rule. Placed at her own household pin, not the Richmond capitol, with the reason in `date_confidence`.

**Descent. [A] for the documented line, [R] for the rest.** The only English-documented descent runs Thomas Rolfe to his daughter Jane to Robert Bolling to John Bolling; **Edith Bolling Wilson** traced her line this way. Gary Boyd Roberts records that **Thomas Rolfe's own wife is unknown**, and numerous online trees attach unsupported lines. **The 2017 quatercentenary [R] as to programme detail:** arithmetic and general pattern sound, specific events not re-fetched.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Pocahontas is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Relations in the research pool that are not rooted in her own life have **not been silently deleted**. Direction is given for each.

**Forward-direction (not licensable without an author's grant; none used):**

1. **Thomas Jefferson** (`thomas_jefferson.journey.json`). A Randolph/Bolling First-Families-of-Virginia connection to her documented descendants. **Forward** (b. 1743), and the pool itself flags the genealogy as unproved. Dropped; the Bolling descent appears only in its own right.
2. **John Locke** and **William Paterson** (`john_locke.journey.json`, `william_paterson.journey.json`). Whitehall as the seat of colonial policy, and Locke's Board of Trade administration of the colony Rolfe's tobacco built, about 75 years after her death. **Forward.** Institutional succession only, no personal link; the pool's own lens recommended dropping both.
3. **Sitting Bull** and **Low Dog**. **Forward**, and curatorial rather than biographical: QUEUE.md names them as the corpus's existing Native American geography. A note about the map, not a relation.

**Same-period, shared-geography only:** **Francis Bacon** (`francis_bacon.journey.json`), Attorney General 1613-1617, the same Jacobean court in the same years. No primary source places them in the same room, and co-location is what the map already shows a viewer.

**Backward-direction (licensable in principle, but not a relation):** **John Dee** (`john_dee.journey.json`) came ashore at Gravesend on 2 December 1589; she was put ashore at the same small Thames port and died there in March 1617. Direction **backward**, so it could legally be embedded, but it is shared geography and nothing else. It would stay out even if she were on the exceptions list, because the exception licenses an apparatus, not a coincidence of harbours. Recorded in case the operator wants it as a map-level annotation.

**No canonical pin inherited.** The interlock lens checked the canonical pins (Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris, Buenos Aires) and grepped every `*.journey.json` for Jamestown, Werowocomoco, Henricus, Varina, the Potomac towns, and Gravesend, finding **no other traveler at any of her sites** beyond incidental mentions of "Virginia" in unrelated Founding-era campas. Reported as a gap rather than papered over with a forced pin.
