# Baha al-Din Naqshband: research report

*Muhammad Baha al-Din Naqshband Bukhari, c. 1318 to 1389. Born and buried in one village
outside Bukhara; eponym of the Naqshbandi tariqa. Compiled 2026-08-09 from a verified
research pool (gather, adversarial verification, correction pass).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, hagiographic
tradition, or route inference. Contradictions between sources are flagged where they
occur and are **not adjudicated** unless a source explicitly resolves them. Sources that
could not be reached are named with the reason.

**The evidential shape of this life.** Almost everything narrated about Naqshband
descends from within the order he named: the *Rashahat 'Ayn al-Hayat* of Fakhr al-Din
'Ali Safi (early 16th c.), the *Anis al-Talibin*, and the *Maqamat-i Bahauddin*, all
compiled by disciples or disciples of disciples. There is no independent contemporary
chronicle of him. The most rigorous modern account, Hamid Algar's *Encyclopaedia Iranica*
entry, was inaccessible (see Sources). Partly substituting for it is the Turkish TDV
*İslam Ansiklopedisi* entry, the only source in this pool that both dates episodes and
assesses the attributed corpus critically. Where TDV disagrees with the order's own
devotional literature, both readings are recorded below.

---

## 1. Birth and the third day (c. 1317/1318)

- Born in the village of **Qasr-i Hinduvan**, near Bukhara, in Muharram 718 AH.
  **[A: Wikipedia, "Baha-ud-Din Naqshband Bukhari"; ghayb.com]**
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** Wikipedia and ghayb.com give 14 Muharram 718 AH =
  March 1318 CE. The order's own Golden Chain page (naqshbandi.org) gives Muharram
  717 AH / 1317 CE, one year earlier. Neither source acknowledges the other. **[R]**
- Ethnically **Tajik and Persian-speaking**, from the settled population of the oasis
  rather than a nomadic Turkic lineage; Wikipedia also cautions that texts claiming
  descent from the Prophet should be treated with reserve. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Corrected in verification.** "Weavers of gold thread" is a folk etymology back-formed
  from *naqshband*. Wikipedia states plainly that the earliest texts do **not** explain
  the epithet; *naqsh* as the imprint of God's name on the heart is a later, retrospective
  reading. **[A for the gap; R for the imprint reading]**
- **Corrected in verification.** The village lies about **10 km** from Bukhara, not "a
  day's walk," and sits at **39.8000, 64.5369**, not the 39.8567, 64.4931 of the first
  pass, an error of roughly 7.5 km. The same correction applies to the death place and to
  the shrine complex (39.8014, 64.5372). **[A: Wikipedia, "Kasri Orifon"; Wikipedia,
  "Bahoutdin Architectural Complex"]**
- Three days after his birth the travelling Khwajagan master **Muhammad Baba as-Samasi**
  passes through, blesses the infant, and adopts him as a spiritual son.
  **[R: Wikipedia; ghayb.com; naqshbandi.org]**
- **Corrected in verification.** The prediction attributed to Sammasi in the first pass,
  that the child would become "the leader of the time," is not what the order's own page
  says. naqshbandi.org's Muhammad Baba as-Samasi page gives: *"Allah's name is going to
  be engraved (Naqsh) on his heart. And the Order will take its name from this
  engraving."* **[A for the wording as the order transmits it; R as event]**

## 2. First discipleship: Sammasi (c. 1336 to c. 1354)

- At about **eighteen** he is sent to serve Baba Muhammad Sammasi in the village of
  **Samas**. **[A: naqshbandi.org Golden Chain; ghayb.com]**
- **Corrected.** The first pass tied this to his marriage; no source attests it. Dropped.
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** TDV states Sammasi "died before Naqshband's adulthood,"
  which cannot be squared with a service beginning around 1336 and the death date of
  755/1354 given by the devotional sources. Recorded as open.
- **Coordinate correction.** naqshbandi.org locates Samas as "a village in the suburbs of
  Ramitan." Romitan is **northwest** of Bukhara at 39.9333, 64.3833; the first pass put
  Samas northeast at 39.9, 64.55. Approximate corrected placement: **39.93, 64.38**.
- His grandfather, not his parents, brought him to live near Bukhara in his youth, and the
  devotional passage *"I would arise early, three hours before Fajr prayer"* is presented
  on naqshbandi.org as the **grandfather's** instruction, not a routine set by Sammasi.
  The first pass attributed it to Sammasi. **[A for the attribution; R as biography]**

## 3. Amir Kulal (c. 1354 to 1370)

- After Sammasi's death, his designated successor **Sayyid Amir Kulal** (d. 1370), a
  potter's son who heads a madrasa at **Vabkent** north of Bukhara, becomes Naqshband's
  primary teacher. **[A: Wikipedia, "Amir Kulal"; ghayb.com]** Vabkent (Vobkent):
  **40.0181, 64.5283**.
- Kulal instructs him in **nafy va ithbat**, negation and affirmation, a method joining
  breath control to silent remembrance, and teaches him silent dhikr privately.
  **[A: ghayb.com, quoting Naqshband's own account; naqshbandi.org]**
- **Corrected.** The "roughly twelve years with Amir Kulal" of the first pass is
  unsupported. The Wikipedia article gives no duration; TDV says only *uzun yıllar*,
  many years. The twelve-year figure belongs to **Halil Ata** (below) and appears to have
  been transposed onto the wrong teacher. **[R]**
- Kulal dies in 770/1370 at **Sokhar (Suhari)** near Ramitan and is buried there.
  **[A: Wikipedia, "Amir Kulal"]** Village coordinates approximate; the tomb was not
  pinpointed independently.
- Kulal's designated successor in the Naqshbandi chain is **Naqshband himself**. The
  first pass called Mawlana 'Arif Deqqarani "successor to Amir Kulal"; this is wrong and
  is corrected here. **[A]**

## 4. Khalil Ata and the Chagatai court (to 748/1347)

This is the phase that most clearly breaks the tidy chronology, and the break is left
standing.

- Naqshband recognizes a further master, the Turkic shaykh **Khalil Ata** (Darwish Khalil
  Ata, also given as Khalil Ghirani), from a dream, and remains in his company.
  **[A as tradition: naqshbandi.org]**
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** naqshbandi.org gives **six years**. TDV gives *on iki
  yıl kaldı*, **twelve years**, running until Khalil's fall in **748/1347** in Chagatai
  Khanate territory. The two figures are not reconcilable.
- **Conflation flagged.** TDV treats Halil Ata as **one** figure, a Yasawi shaykh who
  became a ruler; the first pass split him into two, a "Turkic shaykh Khalil Ghirani" and
  an "amir Khalil Ata." TDV's single figure is the better reading; the split is recorded
  because it is what the devotional summaries produce.
- **Location unsupported.** No source gives a location for the court; TDV says only that it
  lay in Chagatai territory. The **Samarqand** pin (**39.6542, 66.9597**, the atlas's
  existing canonical pin, shared with baha_ud_din_walad) is retained in the journey as the
  region's capital with the hedge carried in `date_confidence`.
- **Chronological collision, unresolved.** Twelve years ending in 1347 places this period
  at roughly 1335 to 1347, overlapping the service under Sammasi said to begin around 1336.
  The sources do not sequence these. Both stand; neither is adjusted to fit.
- The claim that he followed Khalil Ata's affairs as far as **Konye-Urgench** in Khwarazm
  is hagiographic and undated. **[R]**

## 5. The other teachers, and the uwaysi bond

- **Mawlana 'Arif ad-Dikgarani** (Dikgerani, Dik Karrani). **[A: naqshbandi.org]**
- **Serious conflation, corrected.** The first pass rendered this teacher as "Arif
  Riwgari" and gave seven years under him. **Khwaja Arif ar-Riwakri died 636 AH / 1239 CE**
  and was Ghujdawani's own direct successor (naqshbandi.org, "Arif ar-Riwakri"), a
  13th-century figure who predeceased Naqshband's birth by about eighty years and cannot
  have taught him. TDV names Dikgarani with only a "brief association." **[A]**
- **Mawlana Kuthum Shaykh (Qutham Shaykh)**, followed for many years.
  **[A as tradition: naqshbandi.org]**
- No source consulted places Naqshband personally at **Nasaf (Qarshi)**, where the first
  pass sent him to meet these two. The stop is retained in the journey but downgraded to
  **[R]**. Qarshi: **38.8611, 65.7847**.
- **The uwaysi transmission.** Tradition holds he received training directly from the
  Prophet and from **'Abd al-Khaliq al-Ghujdawani** through dreams and visions, without
  living contact. **[R: ghayb.com]** Ghijduvan (the burial town): **40.1, 64.6667**.
- **Corrected.** "A master two centuries dead" is wrong arithmetic. Ghujdawani died 1179;
  Naqshband was born c. 1318. The interval is about **140 years**. Note also that the
  uwaysi bond is a visionary relationship, not evidence of a physical visit to Ghijduvan,
  so the geographic anchoring there is **[R]**, not **[A]**.
- **The eleven principles.** Tradition credits Naqshband with adding three principles
  (temporal awareness in dhikr, numerical counting, heart-centered concentration) to
  Ghujdawani's eight. **[R: ghayb.com]**
- **The silent dhikr, corrected.** The first pass treated the abandonment of vocal,
  communal dhikr as Naqshband's innovation. Wikipedia's "Naqshbandi" and "Abdul Khaliq
  Ghijduwani" articles credit Ghijduwani (12th c.) with "placing stress upon the purely
  silent remembrance of Allah," and say Naqshband **revived** a lapsed hidden dhikr rather
  than originating the break. **[A]** His teaching also insists on strict adherence to the
  Sunnah and a sober, non-ecstatic style, rejecting *sama*. **[A: islam.wiki]**

## 6. The journeys out of Bukhara

- **TDV closes a gap the first pass declared unclosable.** TDV records **three journeys**
  away from Bukhara, including **two hajj pilgrimages**, and a visit to **Herat** where he
  meets a Khorasani shaykh. **[A: TDV]** Herat: **34.3419, 62.2031**. Kaaba: **21.4225,
  39.8262** (the atlas's canonical pin, shared with nine other journeys).
- The first pass reported a "direct contradiction" between hagiography affirming the hajj
  and a Persian Wikipedia summary silent on it. That is an **argument from omission**, and
  a better source overturns it. The contradiction is withdrawn.
- **Location error.** TDV puts the encounter at **Herat**, not **Taybad**. The Taybad stop
  is retained as **[R]**. Taybad: **34.7408, 60.7792**.
- **A problem inside TDV itself, flagged.** TDV names the shaykh as Zayn al-Din al-Khafi,
  who died in 1435, decades after Naqshband; the intended figure is almost certainly
  **Zayn al-Din Abu Bakr Taybadi** (d. 1389). Not adjudicated.
- **Route inference, not attestation.** Merv (37.6608, 62.1897), Sarakhs (36.5442,
  61.145), Nishapur, and the Amu Darya crossing near Chaharjuy (39.0833, 63.5667) are
  reconstructions from standard 14th-century Bukhara-to-Khorasan caravan geography. No
  source names Naqshband at any of them. **[R, weakest tier]**
- **Baghdad, the shrine of 'Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani.** Later Naqshbandi telling places him
  at the tomb; not found in any core biographical summary. **[R, weakest tier]** The first
  pass also mis-sited the shrine, which sits in the Bab al-Sheikh quarter of eastern
  Rusafa; the atlas pin **33.3364, 44.4076** (abdelkader.journey.json) is inherited here.
- **Balkh** (36.755, 66.897) circulates in popular accounts and may be a conflation with
  other Khwajagan masters. **[R, disputed]**

## 7. Bukhara, the following, and the succession

- Known to the people of Bukhara as **Khwaja Bala-gardan**, the master who turns away
  calamity, and regarded as the city's patron saint; his following is urban and
  predominantly **Tajik-speaking**, and the order later spreads among Turkic populations
  across Central Asia. **[A: Wikipedia]** Bukhara: **39.7747, 64.4286** (canonical pin).
- **The order as institution.** At least one source holds that the Naqshbandiyya becomes an
  institution mainly **after** his death rather than being founded in his lifetime; small
  communities are attested across Mawarannahr and Khurasan by the mid-15th century, about
  sixty years on. **[R: ghayb.com]**
- Successors: **Sayyid 'Ala al-Din al-Bukhari al-Attar**, recipient of the transmitted
  secret **[A: Wikipedia; naqshbandi.org]**, and **Khwaja Muhammad Parsa**
  **[A: naqshbandi.org]**. 'Ala al-Din 'Attar is tied by tradition to the **Chaganiyan**
  region (approx. 38.28, 67.83), a regional placement only. **[R]**

## 8. Death and afterlife

- Dies at **Qasr-i Hinduvan**, renamed **Qasr-i Arifan**, the Palace of the Gnostics, in
  his honor, and is buried in his garden there. **[A: Wikipedia; naqshbandi.org]**
- **Date contradiction, now partly resolved.** Wikipedia and ghayb.com give 2 March 1389
  (791 AH). naqshbandi.org gives "Monday night, the 3rd of Rabi'ul-Awwal, 791 H.," which it
  converts to **1388**. The Hijri year is consistent across all sources; only the Gregorian
  conversion differs. TDV confirms **3 Rabi' al-Awwal 791 = 2 March 1389**, so the "2nd or
  3rd" ambiguity resolves to the 3rd and naqshbandi.org's 1388 is a conversion error. **[A]**
- **Age, corrected.** The first pass said "roughly 71 lunar years." The units are inverted:
  1389 minus 1318 is 71 **solar**; 791 minus 718 is 73 **lunar**.
- The tomb becomes the core of the **Bahoutdin Architectural Complex** (**39.8014,
  64.5372**), roughly 10 km from Bukhara: mosques, khanqah, madrasah, minaret, and a
  central *dahma* in marble latticework. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Corrected.** What Khan 'Abd al-Aziz built in **1544-1545** was the **khanqah**, "the
  largest building in the complex," not a tomb erected over the grave. The grave
  structure predates it; the Shaybanid work expanded an existing shrine into a complex.
  **[A]**
- Minaret **1885**, year recorded in a Nasta'liq inscription; visiting **forbidden under
  Soviet rule**; asphalt road from Bukhara to the shrine **1951** (Wikipedia, "Kasri
  Orifon"); renovation **1993** for the 675th anniversary of his birth; gate inscriptions
  by the calligrapher **Habibulloh Solih** **2003**; Sufi museum and research center
  **2004**; plaque **2009**; further restoration **2010**; UNESCO Tentative List **18
  January 2008**, folded into the transnational **"Silk Roads: Zarafshan-Karakum
  Corridor"** inscription **2023**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Bahoutdin Architectural Complex"]**
  Note that a 675th anniversary in 1993 counts from 1318, quietly adopting one of the two
  contested birth years.

## 9. The attributed corpus

- **Corrected and largely closed.** The first pass reported an unclosable gap here. TDV
  assesses the corpus directly: the *Awrad* (el-Evrad al-Bahaiyya), a cycle of litanies,
  may genuinely be his; *Silk al-anwar*, *Hadiyyat al-salikin*, *Tuhfat al-talibin*,
  *Hayatnama*, and *Dalil al-ashiqin* are attributed but, in TDV's words, very likely not
  his. **[A: TDV]**
- **Attribution probably wrong.** TDV lists **no Maktubat** among his works. The famous
  Sufi *Maktubat* is **Ahmad Sirhindi's**; the first pass looks like a conflation. **[A]**

## 10. Quotations: what survives verification

This is where the first pass fared worst, and the results are worth stating plainly.

- **"The aim in dhikr is that the heart be always aware of al-Haqq, for its practice
  banishes inattention."** Traced to **J. Spencer Trimingham, *The Sufi Orders in Islam***
  (Oxford), via Open Library full-text search across three editions. Three corrections:
  the verb is *banishes*; the citation given in the first pass (aalequtub.com) is
  **fabricated**, that page was fetched and contains no occurrence of "al-Haqq," "Yad
  Kard," or "inattention"; and Trimingham gives *Yad kard* as one of **Ghujdawani's
  eight** principles, so it must not be attributed to Naqshband. **[A: Trimingham]**
- Five sentences attributed in the first pass to the *Rashahat* ("Our way is
  companionship..."; "This path is built on two things..."; "The intention of the seeker
  must be pure..."; "Deriving benefit from the Shaykh..."; "We prefer silent dhikr...")
  were **verified verbatim on aalequtub.com**, so they are not invented, but **Open
  Library full-text search returns no match for any of them**, and the site supplies no
  edition, translator, or page. Where used in the journey they are cited as **rendered by
  Aal-e-Qutub, aalequtub.com, citing *Rashahat 'Ayn al-Hayat***, never as quotations from
  the *Rashahat* itself. **[R for provenance]**
- **"Dil ba yar o dast ba kar"** (heart with the Beloved, hands at work). The order's most
  famous motto. Encountered only in informal secondary mentions (a video description, an
  audiobook blurb). Could not be traced to the *Rashahat*, the *Anis al-Talibin*, or the
  *Maqamat-i Bahauddin*. **[R: unverified tradition]**

## 11. Where this life touches the atlas

Bukhara was already four times pinned before this journey. Naqshband inherits, he does not
establish, this geography.

- **genghis_khan** sacks Bukhara in 1220 and speaks in the Friday mosque; **marco_polo**'s
  father and uncle trade there three years from 1264; **ibn_battuta** finds it in rubble in
  1333; **gurdjieff** hunts dervish lineages in the same city in 1898. The **Kalyan
  minaret**, spared in 1220 and standing when Ibn Battuta passes, is the monument at the
  center of the city Naqshband knows; the atlas pins the Po-i-Kalyan complex at **39.7736,
  64.4205** (gurdjieff.journey.json). **[A for the four journeys; R for continuity of
  place]**
- **abu_yazid_al_bistami** at Bastam (36.4864, 55.0091) is credited by Naqshbandi tradition
  with a secondary uwaysi transmission; **attar** kneels at that tomb gathering sayings,
  and **rumi**'s Konya scene stages Bistami's *shath* as a live doctrinal problem.
  Naqshbandi sobriety is framed historically as a reaction against that register.
  **[R: doctrinal, not biographical]** Kept in the campa: Bistami himself, since the
  Naqshbandi uwaysi transmission claim is a genuine (if doctrinal, not biographical) chain
  to Naqshband specifically. Cut on the house-style pass of 2026-08: **attar**'s own kneeling
  at the same tomb, which is attar's relation to Bistami and not to Naqshband.
- **baha_ud_din_walad**'s household passes through Samarkand in 1212 with the child Rumi.
  **[A]** Cut by name from both the Samarqand and Nishapur and Balkh campas on the same
  pass: real events in their own right, but no documented crossing with Naqshband beyond
  the shared coordinate a century and more apart.
- **junayd_al_baghdadi** and **abd_al_qadir_al_jilani** hold the Baghdad pin; **abdelkader**
  kneels at Jilani's tomb in 1826 at the coordinates this journey inherits. **[A for the
  pins; R for any Naqshband visit]** Kept: al-Jilani himself, since later Naqshbandi
  tellings make a specific doctrinal claim of lineage-binding at his grave. Cut: abdelkader,
  whose 1826 kneeling has no connection to Naqshband beyond the same stone at a different
  date for a different reason.
- QUEUE.md states the intended interlock itself: Naqshband "joins the same Sufi cluster
  (junayd_al_baghdadi, abd_al_qadir_al_jilani just queued)." **[A: QUEUE.md]**

## 12. Standing gaps

1. **Encyclopaedia Iranica**, Hamid Algar, "BAHĀʾ-AL-DĪN NAQŠBAND, ḴᵛĀJA MOḤAMMAD B."
   Exists, would be the most rigorous account available; every fetch of iranicaonline.org
   returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden**. Not consulted.
2. **The primary hagiographies were never read directly.** The *Rashahat*, the *Anis
   al-Talibin*, and the *Maqamat-i Bahauddin* reach this report only through summary and
   through one website's unsourced renderings.
3. **The sequence of teachers is not fixed.** Sammasi, Kulal, Khalil Ata, Dikgarani, and
   Qutham Shaykh are each attested by name; no source orders all five with dates that
   agree, and the Khalil Ata period (ending 1347) overlaps the Sammasi period (from
   c. 1336).
4. **Sammas and Sokhar** have no independently confirmed modern coordinates; both are
   placed approximately within the Bukhara oasis and flagged as such.
5. **No marriage, no children, no household** is documented anywhere in this pool. The
   silence is total and is reported as silence.
6. **The "hidden years" framing collapses.** The first pass posited two unrecorded decades
   between Kulal's death and Naqshband's prominence, dated 1354-1370. Kulal did not die
   until 770/1370, so that window is the period spent **with** Kulal. Any obscurity must be
   moved after 1370, where it is much shorter.
