# Loïe Fuller (1862-1928): research report

*Marie Louise Fuller of Fullersburg, Illinois: dancer, lighting inventor, patent-holder, film director. Compiled 2026-08-14 for `loie_fuller.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference not confirmed against a named source in this pass. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**Method and its limits.** The documentary spine here is thin and doubled. Wikipedia in two languages supplies most of the chronology and contradicts itself as often as it contradicts the other edition. Against that sits one first-person document of weight: Fuller's memoir, 1908 in French and 1913 in English, read here in the Project Gutenberg text of the Jenkins edition (#72257). The memoir is the source of nearly every anecdote later told about her, so most secondary accounts are her own account with the seams sanded off; where it is the only witness, this report says so. No archive, no monograph, and no contemporary press was reached.

---

## 1. Illinois and the American circuit, 1862-1889

Born Marie Louise Fuller on a farm in the settlement of Fullersburg, west of Chicago **[A, English Wikipedia]**. Fullersburg was the mill hamlet around Graue Mill on Salt Creek, today in Oak Brook rather than Hinsdale **[A, 'Graue Mill']**: the coordinate in general circulation sits about 2 km too far west, and the journey uses 41.8200, -87.9276.

**Birth date: contradiction, flagged.** English Wikipedia gives 15 January 1862 at Fullersburg. The French infobox gives *15 janvier 1862*, agreeing; the French running text gives 22 January 1862 at Hinsdale. The discrepancy is **internal to the French article**, not a French-versus-English split, and framings of it as the latter are wrong. Not resolved.

The family moved to Chicago when she was about two and opened a boarding house; she attended the Progressive Lyceum, a free-thinkers' Sunday school **[A]**. The toddler recitation of "Mary Had a Little Lamb" is reported only *per her memoir*, with **no year attached**; any dating of it is inference **[R]**. Formal debut at the Chicago Academy of Music in *Was He Right?*, c. 1866 **[A]**; the venue is demolished and its address unconfirmed, so the pin marks the Loop theater district **[R for the coordinate]**.

Toured with the Felix A. Vincent Company in a pantomime of *Aladdin*, 1878; joined Buffalo Bill Cody's touring act "around age nineteen," c. 1881 **[A, which gives the age, not a date]**; played *Davy Crockett*, 1882, and *Twenty Days, or Buffalo Bill's Pledge*, 1883 **[A]**. Beyond these titles the sources name no towns and no other parts: the 1880s are a decade-shaped hole with a working actress inside it. **Gap.** London, 1889: she mounted and starred in *Caprice*, a critical and financial failure, then understudied at the Gaiety Theatre and met the English skirt dance there **[A]**.

**The 1889 Exposition claim: ANACHRONISM, excluded.** A widely repeated claim has Fuller struck by the Palais de l'Électricité at the 1889 Paris Exposition. That building belonged to the **1900** fair; 1889 had only a small private electricity pavilion on the avenue de Suffren, demolished immediately after **[A, 'Exposition Universelle (1889)' and '(1900)']**. The 1889 Champ de Mars luminous fountains are genuine; the Palais is not. The episode is **excluded from the journey** rather than downgraded, since the error cannot be repaired without inventing what she saw.

## 2. The Serpentine, 1891-1892

**Holyoke.** Performing in *Quack Medical Doctor* at Holyoke, Massachusetts, 16 October 1891, she improvised in flowing white silk and the audience called her a butterfly, then an orchid; French Wikipedia treats the night as the origin of her style **[A, French Wikipedia, for date, place, and framing]**. Her memoir tells it without a date: the silk was sent by two English officers bound for India, and the decisive experiment came privately afterwards **[A, memoir, Ch. III]**. Compatible but not identical: the memoir describes a process, the French article a night.

**Casino and Madison Square.** Rudolph Aronson of New York's Casino Theatre named the dance "The Serpentine" and hired her to perform it between the acts of *Uncle Celestin*, 1891; she then succeeded at the Madison Square Theatre too **[A, 'Serpentine dance']**. The manager is **Rudolph**, not Rudolf; the misspelling travels widely. The explanation usually attached to the move, a quarrel with Aronson, appears in **neither** relevant article and is carried as tradition only **[R]**. The second theatre stood at 24 West 24th Street, about 40.7434, -73.9903.

**Premiere date: contradiction, flagged.** French Wikipedia dates the premiere of the developed Serpentine Dance to **15 February 1892, Park Theatre, Brooklyn**. English sources place the first performances at the **Casino Theatre in 1891**. Both are entered in the journey as separate stops; neither is dropped, neither preferred.

**Fuller v. Bemis, 1892.** She sued the imitator Minnie Renwood Bemis; the U.S. Circuit Court denied an injunction, ruling that the dance told no story and was therefore outside copyright **[A, English Wikipedia]**. The Copyright Act of 1976 later extended protection explicitly to nondramatic choreographic works, a change legal historians trace to this precedent **[A]**. She invented a form, could not own it, and watched it become common property within a season.

## 3. Paris and the Folies Bergère, 1892-1896

**Departure and arrival: soft contradiction.** English Wikipedia has her leaving America in **June 1892**; the memoir places the Paris arrival **after** failed engagements in Berlin, Altona, and Cologne, where she danced in a circus between an elephant and a donkey, and dates it to **October 1892** **[A, memoir, Ch. V]**. No source states a reconciliation, so the stop carries both dates. Her arrival point is unrecorded; the Gare Saint-Lazare pin is a guess and labeled one **[R]**.

**The engagement.** She became a star of the Folies Bergère, presenting the Fire Dance among other works, one of the highest-paid entertainers in the world and a fixture of Art Nouveau and Symbolist Paris **[A, English and French Wikipedia; 'Folies Bergère']**. **No source consulted gives a debut date**: English Wikipedia says only "the early 1890s," and French Wikipedia names the artistic director Édouard Marchand without a date. The November 1892 in the journey is **[R]** and marked so.

**Coordinate note.** English Wikipedia gives 48.8742, 2.3449 for 32 rue Richer; the journey instead inherits **48.8752, 2.3444**, the pin already used for this venue in the corpus, so the two travelers' stops coincide on the map. The 110 m discrepancy is recorded, not silently reconciled.

**The audition, from the memoir.** She arrived to find the house already booked with an imitator, bought out that contract, and went on under the imitator's billing; the exchange ran through her interpreter Mr. Stein, and Marchand, she learned later, spoke English perfectly **[A, memoir, Ch. V]**. His explanation, quoted in the journey, is usually circulated with its mid-sentence attribution silently dropped; the journey restores the printed form.

**Fire Dance.** A glass floor lit from beneath with colored gels, producing the illusion of flame **[A for the mechanism]**. **No year is attached to it in any source consulted**; the 1895 in circulation is unsupported. The journey places it in 1893 and calls the placement reconstruction.

**Patents.** US 513102 "Mechanism for the Production of Stage Effects," US 518347 "Garment for Dancers," US 533167 "Theatrical Stage Mechanism" **[A, citing patent records]**, plus, per French Wikipedia, roughly ten patents and deposits covering costume, phosphorescent compounds of her own devising, and revolving color-gel lamps **[A]**. The numbers fall in the **1894-1895** issue range, so the 1893 in circulation is wrong; the journey uses 1894. The Patent Office Building stood at F Street NW at 8th, about 38.8979, -77.0230, not on the Mall.

**Film, by others; and science.** Edison Studios filmed *Annabelle Serpentine Dance*, 1895, one of several hand-tinted films simulating her colored-light effect and made by imitators; the Lumière brothers produced their own version, 1896 **[A, 'Serpentine dance']**. The West Orange coordinate is approximate **[R]**. She was a member of the Société astronomique de France **[A, English Wikipedia, no year; French Wikipedia states *dès 1895*]**; accounts placing this "in the 1900s" are both vaguer and wrong.

## 4. Gab Sorère, the Fullerets, and 1900

**Gabrielle Bloch.** English Wikipedia places the meeting in the **mid-1890s** with no start year for the partnership; the only hard date it gives is **1898** for cohabitation **[A]**. The frequent "1895" is manufactured precision and is not used. Bloch took the name **Gab Sorère** in 1920 **[A]**; the partnership ran over thirty years **[A]**.

**Neuilly.** A house and later a dance school in the western suburbs, training a company called the Fullerets. **[R]**: general accounts only, address unknown, pin approximate. At the **Exposition Universelle of 1900** she operated her own théâtre-musée on the Rue de Paris attractions strip, an Art Nouveau structure built around her image **[A, French Wikipedia; footprint [R]]**.

**Sada Yacco: correction.** English Wikipedia says only that Fuller **supported** performers "such as Sada Yacco"; French Wikipedia says she **introduced** Sada Yacco to French audiences at her 1900 theatre **[A]**. "Mentored," common in later accounts, overstates: Sada Yacco was an established international star, not a protégée. The journey says "presents her to Paris."

**Rodin at the Place de l'Alma.** General accounts make Fuller an early advocate for exhibiting Rodin independently near the 1900 fairgrounds; **not confirmed against a named source** **[R]**, so no stop is built on it. Rodin enters the journey only at his 1908 letter.

## 5. The 1902 tour, Marie of Romania, the Curies

**Isadora Duncan.** In 1902 Fuller invited Duncan to tour with her, carrying her across Europe and launching her continental career **[A, 'Isadora Duncan'; French Wikipedia]**. **Framing contradiction, flagged:** one account presents Fuller as mentor; French Wikipedia says she "contributed to making known" Duncan, who then eclipsed her. Duncan's atlas file records the partnership collapsing within the year into an unexplained coldness. Both framings are kept; the rupture is stated and not explained, because no source explains it.

**Tour geography.** Vienna, Budapest, and Berlin are attested at the level of city only. **No venue is confirmed**: the Carltheater and the Wintergarten come from general accounts, and the English Duncan article names no cities at all. The journey marks the venue question **[R]** and inherits the corpus pin 52.5200, 13.4050 for Berlin. Munich, also in circulation, is dropped for want of a named source.

**Marie of Romania.** Met Crown Princess Marie during a performance in Bucharest, 1902; a long friendship followed, with extensive correspondence and the predictable rumors **[A, English Wikipedia]**. The article on Marie does **not** corroborate the meeting; it does confirm that Fuller "quickly put together a committee that supported Marie's tour of America" **[A]**. Bucharest coordinates in circulation are about 3 km off; the journey uses 44.4325, 26.1039.

**The Curies.** She approached Pierre and Marie Curie for help with a radium-based phosphorescent costume; they declined **[A, French Wikipedia]**. A Fire Dance variant is called by some sources *la danse du radium*, tribute rather than literal use **[R]**. Anatole France's introduction confirms the closeness, reporting her talking "about Curie, Mme. Curie, Auguste Rodin and other geniuses" **[A]**; memoir Ch. XI describes an April 1902 visit to Rodin's Meudon studio with great chemists including a husband "since died under tragic circumstances, having been run over by a carriage," almost certainly the Curies unnamed **[R, internal evidence]**. The rue Lhomond address and the date of first contact are **[R]**. The story that radium from the Curies burned her hands is supported by **no source consulted** and is entered in the journey explicitly as legend **[R]**, since it circulates widely enough that silence would read as assent.

## 6. Books, films, museum, 1908-1927

- *Quinze ans de ma vie*, Paris, F. Juven, 1908, introduction by Anatole France **[A]**; English edition, *Fifteen Years of a Dancer's Life*, London, H. Jenkins, 1913, translator uncredited **[A]**.
- Rodin's letter, Meudon, 19 January 1908, reproduced verbatim in the memoir and quoted in the journey **[A, memoir, Ch. XI]**. The separate widely quoted line, that Fuller "has paved the way for the art of the future," is attested only as an undated Wikipedia attribution **[R]** and is not used.
- **Maryhill, 1917: date correction.** The turning point at Sam Hill's unfinished Columbia River mansion "came in 1917 when dancer Loie Fuller visited" and persuaded him to make it "a museum for the public good, and for the betterment of French art in the far Northwest of America" **[A, 'Maryhill Museum of Art']**. The **1920** in circulation matches **no event**: commissioned 1914, Fuller's visit 1917, dedicated by Queen Marie 3 November 1926, opened 13 May 1940. Coordinates in circulation are about 3.5 km off; the journey uses 45.6778, -120.8633.
- Gabrielle Bloch takes the name Gab Sorère, 1920 **[A]**. *Le Lys de la vie*, 1921, co-directed with Sorère from a story by Queen Marie, featuring René Clair **[A]**.
- *Visions des rêves*, 1924 **[A]**. Légion d'honneur, 1924 **[R]**: general accounts only, ceremony location unknown, pin approximate. *Les Incertitudes de Coppélius*, 1927 **[A]**.

## 7. Death and afterlife

**Death: contradiction, flagged.** Died of pneumonia in Paris at 65, two weeks short of her 66th birthday **[A]**. English Wikipedia gives **1 January 1928** and the French infobox agrees; the French running text gives **2 January 1928** at the Plaza Athénée, 25 avenue Montaigne, 8th arrondissement. As with the birth date, the contradiction is **internal to the French article**. Not resolved.

**Cremation.** Remains placed in the columbarium of Père-Lachaise, site number 5382 **[A]**. **Gap:** no inscription for the niche is documented anywhere consulted. The coordinate in circulation sits about 300 m north of the Formigé crematorium building; the journey uses 48.8592, 2.3947 **[R for the precise pin]**.

**Image.** François-Raoul Larche's gilt bronze lamp ("Dance of the Lily") is dated **c. 1901**, not 1900 **[A]**; examples are at the Museum Wiesbaden and the National Gallery of Australia **[A]**, while a commonly asserted MoMA holding is **not** mentioned there and could not be verified (moma.org returned HTTP 403), so it is dropped. Toulouse-Lautrec, Chéret, Rodin, Gérôme, Franz von Stuck, Maurice Denis, and Mallarmé are named among those who engaged with her **[A]**, but the dates "Lautrec 1892" and "Moser 1901" come from **image captions rather than article prose**, and the Moser caption is corrupt, printed as "2901." The journey uses the canonical 1893 Lautrec lithograph.

**Archives and scholarship.** The Jerome Robbins Dance Division, New York Public Library, holds manuscripts and related material **[A]**; not consulted. Lista's *Loïe Fuller, danseuse de la Belle Époque* is cited by Wikipedia as Hermann, 2006, **second edition** (first 1994), and the "680-page" figure in circulation appears in no source consulted. Garelick's *Electric Salome* is cited as Princeton 2009, though the original hardcover appears to be 2007; **unresolved**. Neither book was read.

**Popular memory.** French Wikipedia states she was "practically forgotten by the general public after her death" **[A]**, while remaining fixed in dance history and Art Nouveau scholarship: a documented gap between popular memory and specialist canon. *La Danseuse*, directed by Stéphanie Di Giusto with Soko as Fuller and Lily-Rose Depp as Duncan, premiered at Cannes 13 May 2016 and released in France 28 September 2016 **[A]**.

## Corpus interlocks

Named inside campa text, on the ground of a real relation rather than shared geography:

- **isadora_duncan** **[A]**. Duncan saw Fuller's theatre at the 1900 Exposition; Fuller invited her to tour in 1902 and paid for the concerts that made her continental name; the partnership went cold within the year; Duncan eclipsed her. Duncan's journey file quotes Fuller's memoir directly.
- **thomas_edison** **[A for the appropriation, R for any contact]**. Edison Studios filmed *Annabelle Serpentine Dance* in 1895, a hand-tinted imitation of Fuller's colored-light effect danced by another woman: one-directional and industrial. **No meeting between the two is attested in any reachable source**, and the campa does not imply one.

**Rejected** as name-drops, geography only: **paul_berthon**, whose 1896 Folies Bergère poster of Liane de Pougy shares Fuller's venue and nothing else; and **stebbins** with the Delsarte lineage, a shared movement-culture milieu, structural rather than documented.

**Corpus gap.** No journey file exists for Marie Curie, Pierre Curie, Sada Yacco, Marie of Romania, or Auguste Rodin, and neither QUEUE.md nor the 2026-08-02 census names Fuller, the Curies, the Folies Bergère, or the Serpentine Dance. Fuller's densest documented relationships point at people the corpus does not yet carry.

**Pins inherited byte-identical** from the corpus: Paris 48.8566, 2.3522; Exposition Universelle 48.8566, 2.2945; Berlin 52.5200, 13.4050; Folies Bergère 48.8752, 2.3444 (section 3).

## Open contradictions, collected

Seven, each flagged in place above and none adjudicated: the birth date, the death date, the Serpentine premiere, the Duncan framing and the 1902 rupture, the 1892 departure, Garelick's imprint year, and the cause of the Madison Square move.

## Gaps

- The American touring decade, roughly 1878-1889: titles without towns, dates, or wages.
- No debut date for the Folies Bergère engagement, the most consequential booking of her life; no year for the Fire Dance or the glass stage floor; no year for the first "Mary Had a Little Lamb" recitation.
- No inscription recorded for niche 5382 at Père-Lachaise.
- No address for the Neuilly house and school, no roster of the Fullerets, no venue confirmed for any stop on the 1902 tour.
- Nothing archival: the NYPL Dance Division holdings, the patent files, and the Larche museum records all left unread.
