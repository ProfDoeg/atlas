# Federico García Lorca (1898-1936) : research report

*2026-08-24, for `federico_garcia_lorca.journey.json`. 9 segments, 45 stops, 10 quotes.*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on
secondary summary not pushed back to a document in this pass. Contradictions are flagged in
place and **not adjudicated**. Gaps are stated as gaps.

The hard problem here is not the poetry. The killing has no body, no grave, no trial record,
and three competing dates from three serious historians. The file is written so that the
disagreement survives into the data.

## 1. The Vega (1898-1909)

**Birth [A]**: about midnight, 5 June 1898, 4 Calle de la Trinidad, Fuente Vaqueros; baptized
six days later Federico del Sagrado Corazón de Jesús; father a landowner enriched by sugar
beet, mother the village schoolmistress (Centro Lorca; en.wikipedia). A brother Luis, born
1900, dead of pneumonia at two, is **[R]**, not refetched, and is not used as a stop.

- **Move to Asquerosa/Valderrubio: CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** es.wikipedia says **1905**;
  Centro Lorca says *entre 1906 y 1907*; en.wikipedia's chronology hedges 1905/1906-07. Stop
  dated 1906-01-01 only to order the segment, spread stated in `date_confidence`. The
  renaming of Asquerosa to Valderrubio on 15 August 1943 is attested.
- **Almería: TWO CONTRADICTIONS.** *Date*: chronology lens 1908, geography lens 1908-1909;
  es.wikipedia has him with Antonio Rodríguez Espinosa from 1906, covering the **1907-1908**
  school year plus July and August 1908, illness ending it before Granada schooling in 1909.
  Stop dated 1907-09-01. *Tagging*: the same episode was **[A]** under geography and **[R]**
  under chronology; treated as [A] for the stay, [R] for the dates and for whether the
  illness was an abscess or a phlegmon. **Gap**: the boarding house is documented nowhere
  reachable, so the pin is central Almería, an approximation, said so in the file.

## 2. Granada, piano and university (1909-1918)

**[A]**: six years of piano with **Antonio Segura Mesa**, 1911-1916; Arts and Law at Granada
from 1915, degree 1923, never practised; teachers Domínguez Berrueta and Fernando de los
Ríos; *Impresiones y paisajes*, April 1918, printing paid by his father, day defaulted to 01.
Segura Mesa's 1916 death as the hinge from music to writing is the account all the
biographies carry, not a documented statement by Lorca; the campa gives it as sequence.

- **Study journeys [A, partial]**: Universo Lorca *Trips 1916* confirms **Baeza** (Antonio
  Machado) and **Salamanca** (Miguel de Unamuno). **Gap flagged**: the dossier's fuller
  itinerary (Córdoba, Ronda, El Escorial, Ávila, Santiago de Compostela, A Coruña, Lugo,
  León, Burgos, Segovia, Madrid) is not on the fetched page. Not asserted, no stops there.

## 3. The Residencia (1919-1928)

**[A] and uncontested**: the Residencia de Estudiantes from October 1919 (Juan Ramón Jiménez,
Salinas, Moreno Villa, Pepín Bello, Buñuel, from 1922 Dalí); *El maleficio de la mariposa*,
Teatro Eslava, 22 March 1920, withdrawn after four performances; the Concurso de Cante Jondo
with Manuel de Falla, 13-14 June 1922; the Huerta de San Vicente, bought 27 May 1925 and used
from 1926, where *Así que pasen cinco años*, *Bodas de sangre*, *Yerma* and the *Diván del
Tamarit* were written or advanced; *Mariana Pineda*, Teatro Goya, Barcelona, 24 June 1927,
Xirgu, sets by Dalí.

- **Cadaqués [A stay, R Dalí's account]**: 1925, read *Mariana Pineda* aloud to the Dalí
  family. That Dalí refused him is **Dalí's own later testimony**, one-sided, decades after.
- **Galeries Dalmau [R]**: the June 1927 drawings show rests on en.wikipedia alone, folded
  into the Goya campa rather than given a stop.
- **Seville, Góngora tercentenary [A event, R venue]**: the December 1927 homage that named
  the Generation of 1927 is documented; the Ateneo de Sevilla as the building is dossier-only
  and unverified. *Romancero gitano* (1928, Revista de Occidente) is folded into this stop,
  no place being attested for the publication itself.

## 4. New York and Cuba (1929-1930)

**[A], Centro Lorca bio-1929-1930**: Southampton and the **RMS Olympic**, 19 June 1929, with
Fernando de los Ríos; Eden Mills, Vermont, August 1929, guest of Philip Cummings; the
Institución Hispano-Cubana de Cultura's invitation, the train to Tampa in March 1930, Havana
lectures on lullabies, Góngora's imagery and cante jondo, Lydia Cabrera, Nicolás Guillén and
José Lezama Lima, further lectures at Caibarién, Cienfuegos and Santiago de Cuba; home on the
**Manuel Arnús**, sailing 12 June and ashore at Cádiz on the 30th.

- **Gap, Paris**: the dossier's Paris transit is uncorroborated by anything fetched, so **no
  Paris stop was written**, though the corpus's canonical pin (48.8566, 2.3522) was there to
  inherit. The pin is confirmed corpus-wide; the travel claim is not.
- **Columbia [A, primary blocked]**: Furnald Hall room 617, then John Jay Hall room 1231.
  Columbia RBML's *Dormlife in 1929* **returned access-denied on direct fetch**; the claim
  rests on Centro Lorca's corroboration, and the file names the block.
- **Bushnellsville [A stay, coordinates unreliable]**: Ángel del Río's Catskills house, late
  August 1929. The circulating pin is about 7 km off and en.wikipedia's *Shandaken*
  replacement (roughly 42.148, -74.362) is itself approximate, so rather than plant a stop on
  a coordinate nobody can vouch for, the stay is named inside the Eden Mills campa and in
  `suggested_refs`. **Deliberate omission, recorded.**
- **Wall Street [A presence, R scene]**: that he was in New York during the crash and that it
  fed *Poeta en Nueva York* is attested; that he stood in the financial district watching men
  come out of the buildings is his own later lecture account.
- **Gap, Cuba**: no reachable source dates the individual towns. Tampa, Caibarién and
  Cienfuegos are named inside campas rather than given stops, to stay inside the stop ceiling.

## 5. The Republic and La Barraca (1931-1933)

- **La Argentinita discs [A]**: five 78-rpm discs, 1931, Lorca at the piano. **No recording
  of his speaking voice is known.** The studio is identified nowhere reachable, so the stop
  uses the corpus's canonical Madrid pin (40.4168, -3.7038).
- **La Barraca: DATE SPREAD, both kept.** en.wikipedia dates the directorship to **1931**,
  the working chronology dates the first tour to **July 1932**. Compatible; the file says so
  rather than picking one.
- **Bodas de sangre: VENUE ERROR CORRECTED.** The working material put the 8 March 1933
  premiere at the **Teatro Español** and reused that theatre's coordinates, merging two
  venues. en.wikipedia's *Blood Wedding* gives the **Teatro Beatriz**, Calle Claudio Coello
  41. Separated in the file. The Níjar killing of 1928 as partial source is **[R]**.

## 6. Buenos Aires and Montevideo (1933-1934)

The best-sourced stretch outside Granada, because Argentina's Biblioteca Nacional has
published a chronology of it. **[A], from bn.gov.ar and Universo Lorca, both fetched**:
sailed from Barcelona on the **Conte Grande**, 29 September 1933, invited by **Lola
Membrives** and her manager-husband **Juan Reforzo**; arrived 13 October, lodging at the
**Hotel Castelar**, Avenida de Mayo 1152; *Bodas de sangre* ran to roughly **150
performances** at the Teatro Avenida, his first economic independence; Montevideo and the
Hotel Carrasco in January 1934 with Díez-Canedo, Amorim, Mora Guarnido and Juana de
Ibarbourou, no day-level dates in either source; home on the **Conte Biancamano**, 27 March
1934.

- **CORPUS CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED NOT RESOLVED.** `mario_palanti.journey.json`, covering the
  same building as the Hotel Excelsior, records that the Lorca association was *"unsourced in
  the consulted pages and has been dropped rather than hedged."* The Biblioteca Nacional page
  supports the residency directly. Both statements are carried in the Lorca stop. **Palanti
  is not named in any campa**: Lorca had a relation to the building, not to its architect.
- **Juego y teoría del duende [A]**: delivered in Buenos Aires, 1933; the two-voice lecture
  on **Rubén Darío** with **Pablo Neruda** likewise. **Gap**: no venue or day for either, so
  the stop uses the corpus's generic Buenos Aires pin (-34.6037, -58.3816) and says so.
  **Rosario [R]**: a December 1933 visit is dossier tradition only, **dropped as a stop**.
- **The Gardel legend [R], named as legend.** `carlos_gardel.journey.json`, grepped for 1933
  and 1934, carries Paris, Joinville, the Astoria Studios and the Buenos Aires house with
  **no Lorca mention anywhere**. The campa names Gardel and says no document from 1933 or
  1934 records the meeting.
- **Checked and unsupported**: `victoria_ocampo` puts *Sur* and Editorial Sur at the centre
  of Buenos Aires letters through exactly those months, board including Borges, Mallea and
  Girondo; `borges.journey.json`, grepped for 1933, 1934, Generation of 27 and Neruda,
  returned nothing. Overlap real, contact not attested. Neither is named in any campa.

## 7. The last two years (1934 to July 1936)

**[A] and uncontested**: *Yerma*, Teatro Español, 29 December 1934, Xirgu, amid success and
organized right-wing hostility; the *Sonetos del amor oscuro*, composed 1935-36, kept out of
the censored 1953-54 Aguilar *Obras completas*, essentially unpublished until 1983-84,
**manuscripts never located**.

- **Sánchez Mejías [A]**: gored at Manzanares 11 August 1934, died in Madrid on the 13th,
  prompting the *Llanto*. The two dates are the standard record, **not refetched**. The stop
  is pinned to Madrid, where he died and the elegy was written, not to Manzanares.
- **Doña Rosita [A per secondary source]**: Barcelona, 13 December 1935, from the dossier
  citing Cervantes Virtual, not refetched. The venue was the Teatro Principal Palace and its
  coordinates were **not verified**, so the stop uses a central Barcelona pin and says so.
- **Seis poemas galegos [A for the book, dropped as a stop]**: published 1935 in Santiago de
  Compostela under Blanco-Amor's editorial care. **No source reached places Lorca in Santiago
  for it.** A stop would have staged the publisher's location as the poet's.
- **Bagaría interview [A per secondary source]**: El Sol, 10 June 1936, cited by the dossier
  to the Universitat Oberta de Catalunya catalogue, **not refetched**. The line *"As I was not
  worried about being born, I don't worry about dying"* is **[R]**, circulating everywhere
  with this citation but never reached in the original.
- **La casa de Bernarda Alba: CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** Completed 19 June 1936. *Where*
  is contradicted inside the working material: the chronology lens says **Madrid**, the
  geography entry for the Huerta says **there**. He left Madrid for Granada on 13 July, which
  favours Madrid, but the Huerta entry is not dismissed. Stop in Madrid, contradiction stated.
- **The Bergamín packet [R]**: leaving the *Poeta en Nueva York* manuscript at Bergamín's
  office on 13 July 1936 is the standard reconstruction, uncorroborated by any primary source
  here. The friends' warnings and his saint's-day answer are memoir.

## 8. Granada, August 1936

**[A], from Universo Lorca, *Civil Government (1933-1944)*, fetched**: return to the Huerta
mid-July 1936; Granada fell on 20 July and the killing of officeholders, unionists, teachers
and suspected leftists began at once; on 9 August he moved into the Rosales house, a
prominent Falangist family's; on 16 August a party led by former CEDA deputy **Ramón Ruiz
Alonso** arrested him and held him in a room of roughly four square metres on the Gobierno
Civil's second floor, with a table, an armchair, two chairs and a barred window over the
Botanical Garden; killed with him were schoolteacher **Dióscoro Galindo González** and
banderilleros **Francisco Galadí Melgar** and **Joaquín Arcollas Cabezas**. The Falangist
search of the Huerta on 6 August and the holding at **La Colonia** are **[R]**, from Gibson
and the biographical literature, not refetched.

- **Fernández-Montesinos: SOURCE ERROR, NOT A LIVE DISPUTE.** Universo Lorca has Granada's
  Socialist mayor **Manuel Fernández-Montesinos**, Lorca's brother-in-law, shot in the
  provincial prison on **16 August 1936**, the same day as the arrest, which is the
  consensus; en.wikipedia's compressed chronology puts both on 19 August. The working
  material proposed preserving an even contradiction. It is not one. The file states
  16 August and records en.wikipedia's placement as a source error.
- **Angelina Cordobilla: DATES CORRECTED.** Food and tobacco on **17 and 18 August**, finding
  him in a brand new suit and refusing to eat; on her third visit, the morning of **19
  August**, she was told he was gone. The working material compressed this to 18 August alone.

**THE DATE OF THE KILLING: THE CENTRAL UNRESOLVED CONTRADICTION.** Three reconstructions,
none adjudicated:

| historian | date | basis |
|---|---|---|
| Eduardo Molina Fajardo | dawn, **17 August 1936** | the Rosales brothers and Ruiz Alonso |
| Gabriel Pozo | **2 a.m., 18 August 1936** | a letter |
| Ian Gibson | **19 August 1936** | Angelina Cordobilla's testimony |

*All three per Universo Lorca, fetched;* en.wikipedia gives 19 August. The stop is dated
1936-08-19, with `date_confidence` saying that orders the segment and naming all three.
Fuente Grande / Ainadamar as the ground is **[R]**, traditional identification, no grave.

**The Trescastro boast: WORDING CORRECTED.** The working material had *"le hemos dado dos
tiros"*. Every attested printing (Gibson, Preston's *El holocausto español*, Francisco Martín
Moreno) has the first person singular: *"Acabamos de matar a Federico García Lorca. Yo le
metí dos tiros en el culo por maricón."* **Juan Luis Trescastro** is claiming he personally
fired, not a collective act. Corrected wording used, still **[R]**: Gibson's text was not
fetched directly and the attribution runs through witnesses to a café boast.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A] and uncontested**: *Poeta en Nueva York* posthumous in 1940, near-simultaneous Mexico
and New York editions; *La casa de Bernarda Alba*, world premiere **8 March 1945, Teatro
Avenida, Buenos Aires**, by Margarita Xirgu's company; the Hotel Castelar room refitted as a
memorial by the City of Buenos Aires in 2003, the peña Signo basement now a bathhouse.

- **Poeta en Nueva York: DATE ERROR CORRECTED.** The working material attached 2013 to the
  manuscript's *rediscovery*. Per es.wikipedia it was lost for nearly fifty years,
  **resurfaced in 1999** with Manuela Saavedra y Moreno de Aldama, was **acquired by the
  Fundación García Lorca in 2003**, and was **exhibited in New York in 2013**.
- **The 1965 police report [R]**: 9 July 1965, two pages, after Marcelle Auclair's inquiries,
  admitting arrest and execution by rebel forces while labelling Lorca a socialist, a
  freemason of the Alhambra lodge, and citing homosexual practices. Dossier cite: RTVE,
  23 April 2015. **rtve.es and elpais.com both blocked WebFetch**; the document was not read,
  and **no lodge register naming him has ever been produced**.
- **The bust in the Jardín de los Poetas [A]**: bronze on concrete by **Eduardo Carretero**
  (1920-2011), inventory **MOA no. 2477**, inaugurated **19 August 1986**, the fiftieth
  anniversary, plaque crediting the Provincial Delegation of Granada and the Argentine
  Republic (weboeba.com, fetched). Corrects circulating claims of a 2014 date or a Takutaro
  Yabushi design, which belong to other Rosedal objects. `rabindranath_tagore.journey.json`
  could not confirm the garden's 26-bust roster; **for Lorca that does not arise**, the
  inventory having a record for this bust.
- **The searches [A]**: University of Granada excavation October-December 2009 by Fuente
  Grande, about 70,000 euros, bedrock at 40 cm, no bones, cloth or shell casings; a 2014 dig
  under **Miguel Caballero Pérez** half a kilometre away, inconclusive; a 2016 well theory,
  unsubstantiated. **BODY COUNT CORRECTED**: the working material gave "roughly 132" bodies
  in the Barranco de Víznar; es.wikipedia gives **166 recovered and seven identified as of
  2025**. **None is Lorca's. No grave, body, or remains have ever been located.**
- **Legends kept as legends, no stops [R]**: that he was shot in the buttocks or sexually
  tortured for his homosexuality, no body recovered and the witness chains late and
  inconsistent, distinct from his killers' better-attested slurs; and that a recording of his
  voice survives in a Plata archive, none produced.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Lorca is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. One relation surfaced that is **not rooted in his own
life**, reported rather than deleted.

**Direction caught: FORWARD. Juan-Eduardo Cirlot (1916-1973).** No documented meeting or
collaboration exists; Cirlot was twenty when Lorca was killed. The connection is a **postwar
reception line**: shared surrealist imagery, and later scholarship comparing their treatments
of Hamlet and of modernism. `juan_eduardo_cirlot.journey.json` confirms this from the other
side, carrying no direct Lorca acquaintance. Forward references are not licensable, so
nothing about Cirlot's reading of Lorca went into this file; if the operator wants it in the
atlas, it belongs on **Cirlot's** file. The one backward-facing item was used: Cirlot's file
documents the **Orden de Toledo**, founded by Buñuel in 1923 with *"Lorca and Alberti and
Dalí among the caballeros"*, a real Lorca circle of his Residencia years, cited as a
`suggested_ref` and `source` on Lorca's Residencia stop and never staged as a scene.

No backward-direction apparatus relation was found, and none was invented.
