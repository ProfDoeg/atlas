# Michel Foucault (1926-1984): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02. Slug `michel_foucault`. Companion artifact: `michel_foucault.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, single-witness relay, or an inference the sources do not make. Where the pool disagrees with itself the disagreement is printed and left standing; the journey records which value was adopted, and the hedge lives in that stop's `date_confidence`. Coordinates are in section 10.

## 1. Poitiers, 1926-1945

- Born 15 October 1926 at Poitiers to Paul Foucault, surgeon, and Anne Malapert, herself a surgeon's daughter **[A: Wikipedia, 'Michel Foucault'; Eribon, *Michel Foucault* (1989)]**. Family house 10 rue Arthur-Ranc **[A: Eribon]**. The father's intention that the boy inherit the trade is standard in the biographies, **[R]** as phrased in the campa. Lycee Henri-IV of Poitiers from 1930, entered two years early **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Contradiction, College Saint-Stanislas.** Chronology lens: 1939, "during the Nazi occupation". Geography lens: 1940-1943. Correction: 1940 (EN Wikipedia "Enrolled 1940"; FR Wikipedia ties it to a threatened repeat year). The occupation began June 1940, so 1939 cannot be "during" it. **1940 adopted [A: EN/FR Wikipedia via correction]**; the 1939 reading recorded, not resolved.
- Baccalaureat in philosophy 1943 **[A: Wikipedia]**, folded into the Saint-Stanislas stop.
- **Gap:** nothing reachable says what he did under the occupation; the campa's closing line compresses his later remarks, **[R]**.

## 2. Paris and the rue d'Ulm, 1945-1955

- Paris 1945, boarding at Lycee Henri-IV under Jean Hyppolite **[A: Wikipedia]**. ENS September 1946, ranked fourth **[A: Wikipedia]**. The geography lens adds "on his second attempt" **[A: Eribon, Macey]**, which the chronology lens does not mention; both printed, neither harmonized. B.A. philosophy 1948; diplome d'etudes superieures and licence in psychology 1949 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Depression and a suicide attempt at the ENS **[A: Eribon, Macey]**. The campa's detail (the school doctor filing the misery under homosexuality, Althusser's protection) exceeds the pool's summary line: **[R]**.
- Agregation failed 1950, PCF joined the same year, agregation passed 1951, Fondation Thiers, ENS psychology instructor, thesis abandoned and Specialist Diploma in Psychopathology 1952 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **No Fondation Thiers coordinate in the pool**; that stop inherits the ENS pin.
- Sainte-Anne 1952-1955, at the clinical presentations with no defined status **[A: Eribon]**; coordinate contested three ways (section 10). Lille 1953 **[A: Wikipedia]**, with **no coordinate in any lens**.
- August 1953, Nietzsche's *Untimely Meditations* on holiday in Italy **[A: Wikipedia]**. The pool gives the country and nothing else, so **no pin**; carried inside the Lille stop. Biographies name Civitavecchia; the pool does not.
- **Contradiction, Jean Barraque.** Chronology lens: begins 1953, ends "the following spring". Correction: mid-1950s to spring 1956 (EN Wikipedia), FR Wikipedia starting it 1953-1954. Two years apart, no place attached, so the journey **omits it**. *Maladie mentale et personnalite* and the Binswanger introduction, 1954 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 3. The northern posts, 1955-1960

- Uppsala 1955, reader in French and director of the Maison de France, first of five years as cultural diplomat **[A: Wikipedia]**; works the university library's medical holdings for the history of madness **[A: Eribon, Macey]**. The collection's size is not in the pool, so the campa says "thousands of volumes".
- Warsaw, October 1958, director of the Centre francais **[A: Wikipedia]**; leaves abruptly after entrapment by the security services exploiting his homosexuality **[A: Eribon, whose lens notes that sources differ on the details]**.
- **Contradiction, Hamburg.** Chronology lens 1958; geography lens 1959-1960; correction cites FR Wikipedia for a directorship of 1 October 1959 to 30 September 1960, arrival early 1959. He was in Warsaw through 1958. **1959 adopted [A: FR Wikipedia via correction]**; the chronology lens's 1958 recorded as that lens's error.
- Thesis complete 1960 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 4. Clermont-Ferrand and the thesis, 1960-1966

- Clermont-Ferrand from October 1960, commuting from Paris, taking over Jules Vuillemin's position in 1962 **[A: Wikipedia]**. Daniel Defert is dated only "c. 1960s" and tagged **[R]**; placing the meeting at Clermont is **[R]**, flagged in `date_confidence`. The geography lens's clause "hires Gilles Deleuze's replacement post there" is garbled and inverted: in 1966 Foucault pushed for Deleuze's appointment and was overruled; Deleuze never took it **[A: correction]**. The campa uses the corrected version.
- **Contradiction, the 20 May 1961 soutenance [A: Wikipedia].** The geography lens says the jury was "led by Georges Canguilhem"; the correction establishes **Henri Gouhier presiding**, Canguilhem as rapporteur and sponsor **[A: correction]**. Corrected version used. The secondary thesis on Kant's *Anthropology from a Pragmatic Point of View* is **[A]** biographically, tagged **[R]** in the interlock lens only because `kant.journey.json` holds no Foucault string.
- *Folie et deraison*, Plon, May 1961; *Naissance de la clinique* and *Raymond Roussel*, 1963; *Les Mots et les choses*, April 1966, a bestseller **[A: Wikipedia]**. Reviews of the thesis by Blanchot and Barthes: **[R]**, not in the pool.
- **Correction, the English translation.** Both lenses say 1965; the correction gives **1964, Howard's abridgement, Tavistock, London**, Pantheon New York 1965 **[A: EN Wikipedia, 'History of Madness']**. The afterlife lens's "omits roughly two-thirds" is also wrong: the abridgement keeps roughly 40%. Complete translation 2006, Murphy and Khalfa **[A]**. **No pin**: no publisher coordinate in the pool, so the 1964 abridgement is named inside the soutenance stop.
- **Brazil, dropped, and this is a hole.** Sao Paulo ("1965; 1973; 1976") loses its 1973 anchor to the correction, which puts *A verdade e as formas juridicas* at PUC-Rio, May 1973. Salvador de Bahia (October 1976) is sourced to *O nascimento da medicina social*, delivered in **Rio, UERJ, October 1974**, so the source does not support the pin. No Rio coordinate exists in the pool; both omitted, so the atlas shows no South American pin.

## 5. Tunis, 1966-1968

- Chair at Tunis from September 1966 **[A: Wikipedia; Eribon for the motive]**; villa at Sidi Bou Said 1966-1968, where *L'Archeologie du savoir* is drafted **[A: Eribon, Macey]**.
- **Contradiction, the crackdown.** Chronology lens: anti-government riots June **1967**, Foucault present and assisting militant students **[A: Wikipedia]**. Geography lens: the repression that shook him, June **1968**. Correction: **March 1968**, the student movement, with June 1967 the earlier disturbances. **March 1968 pinned**, contradiction stated in `date_confidence`. The eight-to-fourteen-year sentences in the campa are Eribon and Macey, **[R]**.

## 6. Vincennes and the College de France, 1968-1971

- Head of philosophy at the experimental Paris VIII, 1968 **[A: Wikipedia]**. No month in the pool; the department was constituted in December 1968, so 1968-12-01 with the month marked as reconstruction. Hires Deleuze and others **[A: Eribon]**; Badiou and Ranciere, in every account of the department, are **not** in the pool and were cut. *L'Archeologie du savoir* 1969 **[A]**.
- **Contradiction, the chair, the pool's worst.** Chronology lens: "elected to a chair in the history of systems of thought, November 1969". The College's own page: the chair was **created** November 1969, Foucault **elected 12 April 1970 by 24 votes of 39** **[A: college-de-france.fr]**. The Stanford Encyclopedia's loose "1969" repeats the slip. **12 April 1970 pinned**, conflation printed in `date_confidence`.
- Inaugural lecture December 1970 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the usual date of 2 December is not in the pool, so the day defaults to 01. The title *L'Ordre du discours* and the lecture's argument are **[R]** against a pool recording only that it happened. Visiting professor at Buffalo 1970 **[A: Wikipedia]**, with **no coordinate in the pool**, narrated inside the Attica stop.

## 7. The prison years, 1971-1975

- GIP founded February 1971 with Pierre Vidal-Naquet and Jean-Marie Domenach **[A: Wikipedia]**. The registered address in Foucault's flat and the unedited printing of prisoners' answers are **[R]**, standard in Eribon.
- La Sante 1971-1972 **[A: Eribon]**; no exact dates in the pool, so 1971-05-01 is declared a placement. Djellali Ben Ali November 1971 **[A: Wikipedia]**, with Sartre, Genet and Mauriac on the committee **[R]**. Toul 1971-1972 **[A: Eribon]**; Dr Edith Rose's report **[R]**.
- **Correction at Attica (April 1972).** The geography lens's claim that he discussed the visit "on radio with Noam Chomsky's interlocutors" is false: the Chomsky debate was 22 October 1971 at Eindhoven, months earlier, and Attica was not its subject. His account is the 1972 interview with **John K. Simon** **[A: correction]**; the clause is dropped. The 29 prisoners and 10 hostages killed in the retaking are **[R]**.
- New York City, "1972; 1975 onward" **[A: Macey, Miller]**, dropped for want of a dated event; the pin 40.7484 / -73.9857 remains available. Arrest with Jean Genet at the Mohammad Diab protest December 1972, and Agence de Presse-Liberation with *Liberation* in 1973 **[A: Wikipedia]**, folded into one stop. *Surveiller et punir* 1975 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 8. California, Japan, Iran, 1975-1979

- Zabriskie Point, LSD with Simeon Wade and Michael Stoneman, May 1975 **[A: Wikipedia for the fact]**. Everything atmospheric in the campa comes from Wade's *Foucault in California* (2019), one interested witness at forty-four years' distance: **[R]**.
- San Francisco bathhouses 1975-1983, from Miller: **[A]** as a source, contested as interpretation, so the campa says biographers dispute it and does not join the dispute. Berkeley across 1975, 1979, 1980, 1983 **[A: Macey, Eribon]** is folded in here for the 1970s; the 1983 arrangement has its own stop **[A: Stanford Encyclopedia]**.
- *La Volonte de savoir* 1976 **[A]**. Croissant demonstration and fractured rib 1977 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the extradition was November 1977, undated in the pool, so that month is a placeholder.
- **Correction, the Zen temple.** The geography lens names "Seitai-ji"; the chronology lens spells it **Seionji**, which the correction confirms, at Uenohara in Yamanashi. Some accounts place the retreat near Kyoto, and that alternative stays in `date_confidence`. Tagged **[R]** in the pool. Tokyo lectures April 1978 **[A: *Dits et Ecrits* III]**.
- Tehran and Qom, September 1978, for *Corriere della Sera* after Black Friday **[A: Foucault's dispatches]**. Jaleh Square and "political spirituality" are his **[A]**; the Qom clerics' explanation of religious government is **[R]** in detail, as is the clause about the later attacks on the Iran writings. At Neauphle-le-Chateau the contact claim is **[R]** and disputed among biographers in the pool, and the campa keeps that framing; the month is inferred from Khomeini's arrival.

## 9. Last years, death, afterlife

- Warsaw convoy of medical and printing aid for Solidarity, January 1982 **[R: Eribon, tagged [R] by the geography lens]**. Berkeley annually from 1983, and *L'Usage des plaisirs* and *Le Souci de soi* 1984 **[A: Stanford Encyclopedia]**.
- 289 rue de Vaugirard: FR Wikipedia confirms 289 as the plaque address, so the geography lens's "exact address disputed" hedge is withdrawn. Only the coordinates were wrong, twice, in opposite directions (section 10).
- **Death 25 June 1984 at the Pitie-Salpetriere**, complications of AIDS, at 57, the first prominent French figure whose death is widely understood as belonging to the epidemic **[A: Eribon; Wikipedia; *Le Monde*]**. Edmund White's report that Foucault doubted the disease was real is one named witness, uncorroborated **[R: fr.wikipedia relaying White]**, and the campa attributes it to him. Burial 29 June 1984 at Vendeuvre-du-Poitou, beside the family house at 17 route de Poitiers **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- AIDES founded by Defert 1984 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**, Guibert's novel 1990, the College de France courses published from the 1990s, and *Les Aveux de la chair* ed. Gros 2018 **[A]**, gathered into one closing stop at the Paris pin.
- **Unresolved, the BnF archive.** The afterlife lens dated the acquisition of the working papers to "the early 2020s" and flagged it unverifiable; its correction dates the major acquisition to **2013**, after classification as a *tresor national* in 2012. Neither reached a live source (section 13). **Left [R], kept out of the journey.**

## 12. Honest gaps

1. **No South American pin.** Both Brazil entries collapsed under their own corrections and the pool holds no Rio coordinate (section 4). **No Italy pin** for the 1953 Nietzsche reading: the pool gives the country only.
2. **No Lille, Fondation Thiers or Buffalo coordinate** in any lens: the first uses a city-centre geocode, the second the ENS pin, the third no pin at all.
3. **The Barraque relationship is omitted**, its two datings two years apart and no place attached.
4. **The BnF acquisition and *Dits et ecrits*** could not be verified against a live source.
5. **Nothing on the family after 1926**: no material on the mother's survival, the siblings or the estate.
6. **The 1979 controversy over the Iran dispatches** is compressed into one clause and is **[R]**; the pool holds nothing on the reception.
