# Alberto Gerchunoff, research report

*Slug: `alberto_gerchunoff`. Born Abraham Gerchunoff, Proskurov (Podolia, Russian Empire), 1883 or 1884; died Buenos Aires, 2 March 1950. Forty years at* La Nación*; author of* Los gauchos judíos *(1910), the founding book of Jewish-Argentine literature.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction or inference, reason given. Contradictions are left standing unless a majority of independent sources decides them, and the losing reading is still named.

Artifact: `alberto_gerchunoff.journey.json`. 9 segments, 45 stops, 8 quoted, register `national mythology: the canon is true`, calendar gregorian.

---

## 1. Podolia, 1883 to 1889

Born Abraham Gerchunoff in Proskurov, Podolia governorate, inside the Pale of Settlement; the town is later renamed Khmelnytskyi and is now in Ukraine. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia, both giving 1 January 1883.]** **Contradiction, unresolved:** the Jewish Virtual Library and biografiasyvidas.com give **1884**, one of them moving the birth to **Tulchin**. The journey plots Proskurov at 49.423 / 26.9871, dates the stop 1883-01-01, and carries the dispute in `date_confidence`. On 1884, the ages twelve (1895 move) and sixty-seven (death) each shift a year.

Father: Rab Gershon ben Abraham Gerchunoff, a rabbi with traditional Talmudic training. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia.]** Nothing surfaced on the mother or siblings; his stop is dated 1886-01-01, a labelled placeholder.

**Gaps.** No birth house, no street, no civil or rabbinical record located. The Podolian phase is two attested facts with a placeholder between them.

## 2. The emigration of 1889

The family emigrates to Argentina in 1889, fleeing antisemitic persecution under the tsarist regime, sponsored by Baron Maurice de Hirsch's Jewish Colonization Association (JCA). **[A: English Wikipedia; Spanish Wikipedia, "Los gauchos judíos".]**

**The route is entirely reconstruction.** Spanish Wikipedia states the absence flatly: *"no hay una fecha ni el nombre de un barco"*. **[A, as a statement of absence.]** All five transit stops are tagged **[R]**, inferred from the ordinary JCA route of that year:

- **Volochysk**, the Russian and Austrian frontier crossing used by Podolian emigrants.
- **Bremerhaven**, the Norddeutscher Lloyd halls. The Moisés Ville founding cohort sailed from Bremen aboard the **Weser** in August 1889; that ship belongs to the colony's tradition, and nothing puts the Gerchunoffs on it.
- **Hamburg / Veddel**, the competing port. **Contradiction left open:** both ports are plotted.
- **Buenos Aires, Dársena Sur**, the standard JCA entry point, no arrival record located; and **Rosario** on the Paraná, the competing transfer for the Santa Fe colonies. **Second contradiction left open:** both plotted.
- **Palacios station**, nearest railhead to Moisés Ville, last stretch by cart.

**Brody** and **Basavilbaso** were drafted and cut: pure route tradition, no personal attestation, and four unattested transit stops is the limit before reconstruction outweighs record. Spanish Wikipedia also relays a family tradition that the father called Argentina a promised land where they would farm "like the ancient Jews of the Bible" **[R: paraphrase, not a document quotation]**, carried as such in the Palacios campa.

## 3. Moisés Ville and the murder, 1889 to 1891

The family settles at **Moisés Ville**, Santa Fe, a JCA colony founded that same year. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia.]** Coordinate **-30.7167 / -61.4833** (en.wikipedia); one lens gave -30.7 / -61.4667, 2.4 km northeast, and the corrected figure is used.

**The murder: a contradiction resolved against the pool.** The chronology and afterlife lenses put the father's killing in **Rajil, Entre Ríos**, after a relocation dated 1890. The geography lens put it in **Moisés Ville**, before any relocation, and is right: *"Allí fue donde su padre... fue asesinado por un gaucho matrero"*, the tombstone in the Moisés Ville cemetery reading *"12 de febrero de 1891"*, and only then *"la familia se trasladó a... Colonia Rajil"*. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, citing the tombstone.]** The 1890 relocation date is therefore also wrong. Both corrections are in the journey, and the rejected placement is named in the cemetery campa.

The pool twice added that the killing happened "before Passover", citing *Entre Ríos, mi país* (Plus Ultra, 1973, p. 23). Spanish Wikipedia says only *la fecha exacta se desconoce* and cites no memoir. Both dropped as unsupported.

## 4. Entre Ríos, 1891 to 1895

The household relocates to **Colonia Rajil**, near Villaguay, another JCA settlement **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia; 1891 or later, from the tombstone]**. The boy works as an agricultural laborer and **boyero**, oxherd, and drives carts **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]**.

**Coordinate gap.** No source gives Rajil a position of its own; every account locates it by naming Villaguay. The journey plots -31.9 / -58.98 and says so. **A second coordinate contradiction is printed rather than averaged:** the geography lens gives Villaguay **-31.85 / -59.0167**, the quotes lens **-31.9414 / -59.0128**, roughly 10 km apart. The journey uses the Wikipedia figure and names the other in the campa. **Villa Domínguez**, hub of the Colonia Clara group in which Rajil sits **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]**, is plotted **[R]**: no source places Gerchunoff personally in the town.

The one piece of his own prose attached to this landscape comes from a book printed thirty-five years later: *"¡Oh, Villaguay de mis lares, oh, pueblo dulce entre los pueblos!"* **[A: El hombre que habló en la Sorbona, Gleizer, 1926, p. 55.]** See section 8.

## 5. Buenos Aires, the apprentice years, 1895 to 1907

*"En 1895, se trasladó a Buenos Aires"*. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia.]** He is **twelve**. The chronology lens called this a move made "as a young man... to begin work as a journalist"; the arithmetic refutes the first half and no source supports the second. No address survives; the pin -34.6083 / -58.3712 is a stand-in. On acquiring citizenship he changes Abraham to Alberto **[A: English Wikipedia]**, undated everywhere, so 1901-01-01 is a placeholder. Vice director of the bulletin of the Ministry of Public Instruction, 1904, and active in the Progressive Democratic Party against Yrigoyen's caudillismo **[A: biografiasyvidas.com, single source, the politics undated]**.

**The teaching, a correction.** He taught literature at the **Colegio Nacional Belgrano** and the **Colegio del Pilar**, vice director of the latter **[A: biografiasyvidas.com; no dates]**. The geography lens had recorded an unnamed university faculty on the phrase *profesor universitario*, tagged **[A]**; no source consulted names any university, and the attested teaching is secondary-level. The stop was reframed and its coordinate -34.5991 / -58.3831 labelled a placeholder.

## 6. *La Nación* and *Los gauchos judíos*, 1908 to 1914

The Jewish Virtual Library dates his joining to **1908** and gives the association more than forty years, eventually as chief editor. **[A: JVL.]** **Contradiction, unresolved:** Spanish Wikipedia says only *muy joven*; both readings sit in `date_confidence`. The address **San Martín 350** comes from the geography lens; pin -34.6063 / -58.3745.

Stories that will form the book begin appearing in *La Nación* from 1908 **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]**. *Los gauchos judíos* is published at **La Plata** in 1910 for the centennial of the May Revolution, **24 stories** **[A: Spanish and English Wikipedia]**; publication there does not prove travel there. The *Encyclopaedia Judaica* calls it the first work of literary value written in Spanish by a Jew in modern times, and the National Yiddish Book Center later ranks it 35th among the hundred greatest works of modern Jewish literature **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, citing both; neither read directly]**. Perla Sneh's phrase, rendered by English Wikipedia as *"an improbable Gaucho literature of the exile"* from her *"gauchesca de un desterrado"*, is the quote on this stop **[A as to the Wikipedia text; Colihue 2007, p. 21, would not load]**.

**Madrid, 1914.** He lectures at the **Ateneo de Madrid** during travels in Europe **[A: biografiasyvidas.com, single source]**. The geography lens asserted that "no source consulted places Gerchunoff in Europe as an adult" while the same pass cited this very page for the lecture. Adult travel to Europe is attested, thinly.

## 8. *El hombre que habló en la Sorbona* (Gleizer, 1926), the only text read directly

The one volume whose prose this research reached, through the unrestricted archive.org scan of item **`elhombrequehablo0000albe`**; everything else is known by citation only. Six verbatim quotations were pulled from the OCR, and **five had wrong page numbers in the pool**. Corrections verified against printed folios:

| Quotation (opening) | Pool | Correct | How fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| *"Grecia ha creado en la humanidad..."* | ~124 | **124** | stands |
| *"La patria no es el territorio..."* | ~90 | **30** | leaf 31, printed folio "30" on the page |
| *"¿Por qué no soñamos en lengua extranjera?..."* | ~90-91 | **30** | same page, a few lines below the preceding |
| *"¡Oh, Villaguay de mis lares..."* | ~50 | **55** | leaf 56; offset printed = leaf minus 1, fixed by leaves 50-52, 61 |

The wording of all six is exact as printed; only diacritics were restored where the scanner misread them, and each truncation ends where the printed sentence ends. Four have their own stops, dated 1926-01-01. ## 9. SADE, 1928: a claim corrected against the traveler

The **Sociedad Argentina de Escritores** is founded in 1928 and Gerchunoff sits on its first *comisión directiva* **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, "Sociedad Argentina de Escritores"]**. The Jewish Virtual Library calls him **SADE's first president**, and the geography lens recorded this as an unresolved conflict. It is not: Spanish Wikipedia's SADE article names **Leopoldo Lugones** first president and Gerchunoff a founding board member only. The JVL claim is an error, the afterlife lens's "founds and leads SADE" a stronger form of it. The journey follows Wikipedia and names Lugones.

## 10. The turn, 1933 to 1948

After Hitler's rise, Gerchunoff, who had earlier emphasized Jewish assimilation into Argentine rural life, turns to Jewish cultural preservation and Zionism **[A: English Wikipedia; JVL]** and joins antifascist groups including **AIAPE** **[A: English Wikipedia categories]**. Plotted at the Once pin -34.6037 / -58.4085, an activity zone and not an address. *Los gauchos judíos* is reissued in 1936 with two further stories, twenty-six in all **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]**. Between roughly 1945 and 1948 he campaigns among Latin American leaders for Jewish statehood, work credited with contributing to Latin American support at the UN **[A: JVL]**. **Cut for budget:** the 1936 PEN Congress at the Teatro Colón, which happened, though his attendance is **[R]**, attested nowhere.

**The United Nations, 1947.** English Wikipedia has him advocating for the state of Israel "before the United Nations" in 1947, without distinguishing personal appearance in New York from lobbying run out of Buenos Aires; neither does the journey, and the stop is tagged reconstruction. The pool's coordinate **40.746 / -73.8283** matches neither named site: Flushing Meadows-Corona Park is 40.74583 / -73.84472, with that point outside its eastern boundary, and Lake Success 40.77028 / -73.71333, 9 km east. The journey uses **40.7458 / -73.8447**.

## 11. Death and afterlife

Buenos Aires, **2 March 1950**, aged sixty-seven. **[A: Spanish and English Wikipedia.]** The age holds only on the 1883 birth date, and some catalog metadata gives the death year as **1949**; flagged. No source names the neighbourhood, residence, or hospital, and the city-centre pin stands in. The Borges assessment is quoted on this stop: *"Fue un indiscutible escritor, pero el estilo de su fama trasciende la de un hombre de letras..."* **[A as to the text: verbatim in the Spanish Wikipedia wikitext, which gives no citation, so the original Borges essay is untraced.]**

**Burial, an honest gap.** No source consulted records where Gerchunoff is buried. The journey plots the **Cementerio Israelita de La Tablada** **[R: Ashkenazi burial custom for figures of his generation]** and states in campa and `date_confidence` that no record confirms it. The father's grave is documented down to the tombstone; the son's not at all.

- ***Entre Ríos, mi país***, autobiographical, **1950** **[A: JVL]**.
- ***Retorno a Don Quijote***, **1951**, posthumous **[A: Spanish Wikipedia bib.; Open Library]**. Two corrections: biografiasyvidas.com gives the title as *El retorno de Don Quijote*, wrong, and calls it a novel, which no source supports; his Quixote writings are essays, from *Nuestro Señor don Quijote* (1913) onward.
- **Gran Premio de Honor** of SADE, **1952**, the only posthumous award in the prize's history **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]**.
- ***The Jewish Gauchos of the Pampas***, English translation, **1955** **[A: English Wikipedia; JVL]**.
- **The film**, directed by **Juan José Jusid** with Pepe Soriano and China Zorrilla. **Not a contradiction, despite two lenses reading it as one:** Spanish Wikipedia gives production year **1974** and Argentine premiere **22 May 1975** (New York 1976, Germany 1980). The two dates are shooting and release. The journey uses 1975-05-22.

## 12. The dubious claim, flagged and not plotted

English Wikipedia asserts that Gerchunoff **"collaborated with Wilhelm Reich on a version of his orgone box"**, tied to a 1948 volume *Héroes de los intersticios* and sourced only to a Reich biography (*Passion of Youth*, 1994). Not carried into the journey: it appears in no Spanish-language source, no academic study, and not in the Spanish Wikipedia article or the JVL entry, and both the geography and afterlife lenses flagged it as likely vandalism. The atlas already walks **Reich** (`reich.journey.json`), which makes the refusal more necessary: an interlock built on a vandalized sentence would corrupt two journeys. A second negative, an open gap and not a finding: searches for **streets, plazas, schools, or museums named for Gerchunoff** returned nothing confirmed, and the search budget ran out partway through.

## 13. Interlocks with the atlas

Named in campa where the paths genuinely cross:

- **`emin_arslan`**, the only direct textual link. Arslan's weekly *La Nota* (founded 14 August 1915, offices at 25 de Mayo 294) lists Gerchunoff among its regular contributors, beside Lugones, Ingenieros, Rodó, Rubén Darío and Alfonsina Storni. **[A: `emin_arslan.journey.json`, verified in the file text.]** At the *La Nación* stop.
- **`borges`**, quoted at the death stop **[A]**, and for his argument that Argentine writers stand toward European culture as Jews stand toward Western culture, which Gerchunoff had staged as one identity two decades before **[R]**. At the 1910 and 1950 stops.
- **`martin_buber`**. Buber's Hasidic material comes from Podolia, the governorate Gerchunoff was born in, and Buber never travelled there to collect it. **[A in-file for Buber; R for the link.]** At the Rab Gershon stop and the 1945 campaign stop: Buber in Jerusalem, Gerchunoff in Buenos Aires counting votes. Cut from both campas on the house-style pass of 2026-08: no documented contact or citation between the two men, only overlapping geography and theme.
- **`taras_shevchenko`**, the same southwestern imperial borderlands a generation earlier **[R]**. At Volochysk.
- **`julio_argentino_roca`**, schooled at Concepción del Uruguay in Entre Ríos, the province whose JCA colonies raised Gerchunoff; the Conquista del Desierto opened land later parcelled to colonists **[A for Roca's stops; R for the link]**. At Moisés Ville and Martín Fierro.
- **`victoria_ocampo`**, whose *Sur* launched from San Isidro in 1931, gathering the establishment Gerchunoff moved through at *La Nación*. No direct textual link exists in any file consulted; the campa says so. At the Montevideo stop.
- **`freud`**, for the assimilated-yet-marked immigrant-child persona **[R, thematic only]**. At the *El cristianismo precristiano* stop. Cut from the campa on the house-style pass of 2026-08 as a thematic echo with no documented citation between the two.

Considered and **not** named: `samuel_oppenheimer`, `joseph_oppenheimer` (thematic only); `reich` (section 12); the QUEUE.md rows for **Félix Weil**, **Raquel Liberman**, **Golda Meir**, queue entries and not yet journeys.

**Canonical pin inherited byte-identical:** Buenos Aires **-34.6037 / -58.3816**, already shared by Borges, Victoria Ocampo, Emin Arslan, San Martín and others; used unaltered on 18 of the 45 stops. The five other Buenos Aires pins (Dársena Sur, Plaza de Mayo, *La Nación*, Once, the teaching placeholder) are deliberately separate, each labelled as what it is.
