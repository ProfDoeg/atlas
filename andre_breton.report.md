# André Breton (1896-1966): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `andre_breton`. Legend: **[A]** attested · **[R]** reconstruction or inference. A bare [A] means the andrebreton.fr chronology, the spine of this pool; other sources are named. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. Sections 1 to 9 are the first pool; section 10 audits a second, independently gathered pool against it.*

andrebreton.fr, maintained around the Breton estate and the 2003 inventory of 42 rue Fontaine, is the densest reachable source and the most quoted second-hand, so its narrow entries get inflated in transmission. Several corrections below consist of catching a widely repeated claim at the point where the entry behind it says something smaller.

## 1. Normandy and the war hospitals (1896-1919)

Born at Tinchebray, Orne, to a police officer and a mother from a family of Breton sailors and seamstresses; the family leaves within months and he keeps no memory of the town [A: Wikipedia; Polizzotti, *Revolution of the Mind*, 1995]. **Contradiction:** andrebreton.fr gives **19 February 1896**, Britannica and the Salvador Dalí Museum **18 February**; month and year agree everywhere.

Lycée Chaptal; early interest in painting, the Bernheim galleries, Moreau, Bonnard, Vuillard [A, 1912]. Medical studies from 1913 [A: Britannica]; meets Paul Valéry [A, 15 March 1914]. Conscripted into the 17th artillery regiment as a military nurse, 26 February 1915 [A]. At Nantes he meets Jacques Vaché in a neurological ward; the "umour" and total detachment are named ever after as a founding influence [A; Britannica]. Vaché dies of an opium overdose in a Nantes hotel room, January 1919 [A: Polizzotti]. At the Saint-Dizier neuropsychiatric centre under Dr Raoul Leroy, July 1916, he tests psychoanalytic method on shell-shocked soldiers [A]; transcribing delirious speech feeds the later automatic writing [A: Polizzotti].

**CORRECTION.** The Apollinaire meeting is commonly attached to the 8 January 1917 posting to the 22nd division, or to Val-de-Grâce. Both wrong: that entry covers only the posting. Breton began corresponding with Apollinaire in December 1915 and first visited him on **10 May 1916**, the day after Apollinaire's trepanation [A; Bloomsbury *Timeline of Surrealism*]. Reverdy is a separate strand. Apollinaire's 1917 coinage of "surrealist" stands [A: Wikipedia]. **Val-de-Grâce, 1 September 1917:** meets Louis Aragon [A]. **Gap:** no reachable source names the Paris hospital of the May 1916 visit.

## 2. Littérature, automatism, Vienna (1919-1923)

**19 March 1919:** *Littérature* founded with Aragon and Philippe Soupault [A]. The group gathers nightly at the Café Certa in the Passage de l'Opéra until the arcade comes down in 1925; Aragon elegizes it in *Le Paysan de Paris* [A: Aragon 1926; Polizzotti]. **May 1919:** *Les Champs magnétiques* composed with Soupault, published May 1920 [A]; he lodges at the Hôtel des Grands Hommes, later a landmark of *Nadja* [A: *Nadja*; Polizzotti]. **May 1921:** the mock trial of Maurice Barrès, Breton presiding [A: *Procès Barrès*, *Littérature* no. 20, trans. Alex Stein, *Cabinet* no. 45]; **gap:** the transcript preserves editorial stage directions but not his own speech, so no quotation of his words there exists.

Meets Simone Kahn June 1920 [R: andrebreton.fr, tagged R in the pool]; marries her 15 September 1921, Valéry as best man [A]. **10 October 1921:** meets Freud at Vienna, an encounter he calls a disappointment [A]; Berggasse 19 sits at 48.2191, 16.3632 in the atlas's Freud file. **1 January 1922:** moves to 42 rue Fontaine, his address until death [A; Centre Pompidou]; door-plate "1713," digits chosen to draw A and B [R: Le Nouveau Neuf]. **25 September 1922:** the sleep sessions begin with Desnos, Péret, Crevel [A].

## 3. The manifesto, the Bureau, Nadja (1924-1929)

**11 October 1924:** the Bureau de recherches surréalistes opens at 15 rue de Grenelle [A; Wikipedia]; Artaud installed as director 27 January 1925 [A: `artaud.journey.json`]. **15 October 1924:** *Manifeste du surréalisme* with *Poisson soluble* [A; text at theanarchistlibrary.org, Seaver/Lane]. **1924:** after Klee's Paris show Breton renames a 1912 Klee canvas to fit his own argument [A: `paul_klee.journey.json`]. **26 November 1926:** the group, Breton presiding, expels Artaud at the Café Le Prophète [A: `artaud.journey.json`]. **14 January 1927:** joins the PCF [A]. **25 May 1928:** *Nadja*, 44 photographs in place of description, NRF; revised 1963 [A; Wikipedia].

**4 October 1926, the encounter. Contradiction, left open:** the novel narrates it on the **rue Lafayette**, which this pool's geography pass followed (while pinning it wrongly, section 7); fr.wikipedia "Léona Delcourt" documents the site as the **rue de Chéroy** in the 17th, by the stage door of the Théâtre Hébertot, two kilometres away. Narration and record are not the same claim and are not merged.

**CORRECTION, name and dates.** The woman behind Nadja was **Léona Camille Ghislaine Delcourt** (not "Delacourt"), born 23 May 1902 at Saint-André-lez-Lille; taken to the Infirmerie spéciale of the Préfecture **21 March 1927**, then Sainte-Anne, then Perray-Vaucluse for a year, then Bailleul (Nord) from 16 May 1928, where she died **15 January 1941** [A: fr.wikipedia "Léona Delcourt"; Mélusine-surréalisme]. "Institutionalized until her death" is correct; the common claim that she died at Sainte-Anne is false.

**CORRECTION, the Second Manifesto.** Not January 1930: it appeared **15 December 1929** in *La Révolution surréaliste* no. 12 (pp. 1-17); the reworked book edition came from Éditions Kra in 1930, with no month on andrebreton.fr's own work record. The expelled answer with the pamphlet *Un Cadavre*. The Café Cyrano on the Place Blanche is the daily table through these years and the site of formal expulsions [A: Polizzotti; Le Nouveau Neuf].

## 4. Objective chance, the party, Mexico (1930-1939)

**1934, Saint-Ouen flea market:** with Giacometti, Breton finds the mask and spoon-slipper analysed in *L'Amour fou* as the model case of objective chance [A]. The concept runs across *Nadja* (1928), *Les Vases communicants* (1932), *L'Amour fou* [A: fr.wikipedia "Hasard objectif"]. **Contradiction between passes:** the afterlife pass dated *L'Amour fou* 1938; the chronology pass, following andrebreton.fr, gives **2 February 1937**, which is what the site states. 1938 is dropped.

**CORRECTION, two expulsions kept distinct.** Britannica says Breton was "expelled from the Communist Party" in 1933. The andrebreton.fr entry for **3 July 1933** reads "expelled from the A.E.A.R.," the Party's writers' organisation, not the Party. The **definitive break with the PCF is 2 July 1935** [A]. Britannica conflates the two bodies; both episodes are recorded, neither folded into the other.

**29 May 1934:** meets Jacqueline Lamba at the Place Blanche; marries her 14 August 1934, Giacometti and Éluard as witnesses; Aube born 20 December 1935 [A]. **March 1935:** Prague, at the Czech group's invitation [A: Wikipedia; Czech Surrealist Group archives]. **May 1935:** Santa Cruz de Tenerife with Péret [A: Wikipedia; Domingo Pérez Minik]. **June 1935:** barred from speaking at the International Writers' Congress after slapping Ilya Ehrenburg [A]; René Crevel kills himself on 18 June, during the congress [A]. **May 1936:** co-curates the Exposition surréaliste d'objets at the Charles Ratton gallery [A].

**CORRECTION, two events fused.** Breton did not lecture in a diving suit in London. The International Surrealist Exhibition opened at 3 p.m. on **11 June 1936** at the New Burlington Galleries with an introductory address by Breton to over 1,150 people. The diving-suit lecture was **Dalí's** ("Authentic Paranoiac Fantasies," 1 July 1936), during which he nearly suffocated and was freed with a spanner [A: National Galleries of Scotland; en.wikipedia].

**Mexico, 1938.** Arrives 18 April on a conference mission, welcomed by Diego Rivera [A]. **CORRECTION, place:** he landed at **Veracruz**, not Mexico City [A: Revista BiCentenario]; the wrong tag misplaces him by 400 km. In May he meets Trotsky at Coyoacán [A] and stays with Rivera and Frida Kahlo at the Casa Azul [A: Wikipedia; `frida_kahlo.journey.json`]; per the Kahlo file she says she "has not been" a surrealist and he declares her one anyway. **25 July 1938:** co-signs *Pour un art révolutionnaire indépendant* with Rivera [A]; Trotsky wrote much of Rivera's half and kept his name off it [A: `diego_rivera.journey.json`]. **November 1938:** his catalogue essay for Kahlo's Julien Levy show supplies "a ribbon around a bomb" [A: `frida_kahlo.journey.json`]; he is in France, so the essay travels, not the man. **1939:** Kahlo sails for Paris on his promise of an exhibition and finds nothing arranged, paintings in customs, the household broke; Duchamp hangs the show, *Mexique*, at the Galerie Renou et Colle in March. She calls the surrealists "coocoo lunatic sons of bitches" in a letter home [A: same file].

## 5. The crossing, the exile, the return (1939-1966)

**CORRECTION, place.** The chronology pass tagged the 2 September 1939 mobilization to **Antibes**; andrebreton.fr reads "mobilized; reports to **Nogent**." Antibes is where Jacqueline and Aube went. **Gap:** which Nogent is unspecified. **1 August 1940:** demobilized from the flying school at Poitiers [R: andrebreton.fr, tagged R in the pool]. **Winter 1940-41, Villa Air-Bel, Marseille:** refuge in the house run for Varian Fry's rescue operation, with Victor Serge, André Masson and others waiting for visas [A; Sullivan, *Villa Air-Bel*, 2006; Fry, *Surrender on Demand*, 1945]. Arrested 3 December 1940, released after four days; the "Jeu de Marseille" devised there [A].

**24 March 1941:** the family boards the **Capitaine Paul-Lemerle** at the Vieux-Port [A; fr.wikipedia "Capitaine-Paul-Lemerle"]; fr.wikipedia's Breton article gives **25 March** for the same sailing, and the split is left open. Fellow passengers include Claude Lévi-Strauss, Victor Serge, Anna Seghers, Germaine Krull, Wifredo Lam, Kantorowicz, Jacques Rémy [A: fr.wikipedia; `claude_levi_strauss.journey.json`, which names Breton]. **CORRECTION:** 222 passengers, not "some three hundred fifty." **CORRECTION: André Masson was not aboard** and does not belong in the Martinique episodes, though he does belong at Villa Air-Bel.

**20 April 1941:** docks at Fort-de-France; Vichy interns the refugees at the **Lazaret**, a former leper station [A: fr.wikipedia; Guernica]. **CORRECTION, duration:** neither "roughly a month" nor "a week"; he left Martinique 16 May 1941, so about **three weeks**. In a mercerie he enters **alone**, to buy a ribbon for Aube, he finds the first issue of *Tropiques*; the haberdasher is the sister of the editor René Ménil and arranges the introduction to Aimé and Suzanne Césaire, met the next day [A: Potomitan.info; Montray Kréyol; Salvador Dalí Museum]. The claim that Masson and Lam were with him in the shop goes with Masson. The en.wikipedia Césaire article dating this meeting to 1940 in New York is impossible on the sailing dates and is treated as an error. **Gap:** no file in `working/journeys` names Aimé Césaire, so the meeting is attested from one side only.

**CORRECTION, the Césaire preface.** Not a "1944 bilingual edition in *Fontaine*": "Martinique charmeuse de serpents. Un grand poète noir" first appeared in the New York review **Hémisphères in 1943** and prefaced the **1947** editions, Brentano's at New York in January and Bordas at Paris in March [A: Encyclopædia Universalis; Érudit]. **CORRECTION, a misattribution.** "We belong to those who say no to darkness" is not Breton's: the Sulfur reprint reads "'We belong,' Césaire proclaimed, 'to those who say no to darkness,'" Breton quoting Césaire's "Présentation" opening *Tropiques* no. 1 [A: webdelsol.com; bib.ens.psl.eu].

**CORRECTION, the New York landfall.** "May 1941" is the departure from Martinique, not the arrival. andrebreton.fr gives "early July 1941," fr.wikipedia **14 July 1941**; Lamba's thank-you letter to Fry is dated 24 June 1941. The exact day is left open. He works on the French broadcasts of the Voice of America and refuses to learn English [A: Wikipedia; Polizzotti]. **June 1942:** founds *VVV* with Max Ernst and Marcel Duchamp [A]; the afterlife pass flagged that it had not re-verified *VVV* details independently. **14 October 1942:** opens "First Papers of Surrealism," with Duchamp's mile of string, at the Whitelaw Reid Mansion, 451 Madison Avenue [A]: a separate venue from Peggy Guggenheim's Art of This Century at 30 West 57th Street, which opened the same season and is often conflated with it [A: Guggenheim, *Out of This Century*, 1946]. **August 1944:** at Percé on the Gaspé writes much of *Arcane 17* [A].

**Contradiction, Reno.** The geography pass dated the divorce from Lamba to 1943 and was corrected to **1945**, just before the marriage to Elisa Claro on **31 July 1945**, several accounts saying he married the day after the decree; 1943 is the year Lamba left him for David Hare [A: renodivorcehistory.org; en.wikipedia "Elisa Breton"]. Polizzotti places the decree itself in 1943, and the journey file follows him. Both readings stand; the Reno venue of the ceremony is [R] either way. **22 August 1945:** visits Hopi and Zuni reservations [A]; **gap:** no source names the villages. **4 December 1945:** Haiti; meets Wifredo Lam and Hector Hyppolite; the lectures are credited with helping spark the unrest that brings down President Élie Lescot in January 1946 [A; Wikipedia; René Depestre]. The causal line is how the sources report the sequence, not a demonstrated cause.

**26 May 1946:** returns to Paris [A; Polizzotti]; fr.wikipedia gives the landing at Le Havre on 25 May. **7 June 1946:** speaks at the Homage to Artaud at the Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt, twenty years after the expulsion [A: `artaud.journey.json`]; **13 January 1947:** in the audience with Gide, Camus and Paulhan at Artaud's Vieux-Colombier reading [A: same]. **7 July 1947:** opens "Le Surréalisme en 1947" at the Galerie Maeght [A]. **1951:** buys a house at Saint-Cirq-Lapopie above the Lot and summers there for life, saying he ceased there to wish to be elsewhere [A: Wikipedia]; stones from the Lot end on the rue Fontaine wall [R: Centre Pompidou magazine]. **1 September 1960:** co-signs the "Manifesto of the 121" against the Algerian war [A]; other sources give 6 September as the declaration's publication date.

**28 September 1966:** dies aged seventy after a long bronchial illness [A: Wikipedia]. **Contradiction, left open:** the geography pass has him dying in the rue Fontaine apartment; fr.wikipedia has him taken ill at Saint-Cirq-Lapopie and dying of respiratory failure at the Hôpital Lariboisière. Buried in the **Cimetière des Batignolles**, 8 rue Saint-Just, 17e, division 31, epitaph "Je cherche l'or du temps" [A: JeSuisMort.com; Landrucimetières; Wikimedia Commons].

**Afterlife.** The wall held roughly 255 objects, the apartment on the order of 10,000 [R: Centre Pompidou magazine]; other accounts put the 2003 sale at more than five thousand lots. **Auction, corrected:** neither "1 to 18 April" nor a standoff with "7-17 April" survives; the Calmels Cohen sales ran **7 to 17 April 2003** at Drouot-Richelieu, Drouot's retrospective being titled "17 jours historiques à Paris," and totalled over **46 million euros**, some 50 percent above estimate, of which the quoted "$50.1 million" is a plausible conversion [A: drouot.com; WSWS.org]. Over 400 paintings and roughly 1,500 photographs were dispersed; the Pompidou acquired a fragment of the wall [A]. **2009:** Place André Breton inaugurated near no. 42 [A: Le Nouveau Neuf]. Howard's *Nadja*, Grove Press, 1960; Polizzotti's, NYRB, 2025, reviewed by Suleiman, NYRB 2026 [A]. That the sale and the retranslation form a "rediscovery" is [R], stated by no single source.

## 8. Quotations

**Verified by direct reading of a full text.** From the *Manifesto of Surrealism* (1924), trans. Seaver and Lane, at theanarchistlibrary.org: the dictionary definition of SURREALISM; "I believe in the future resolution of these two states, dream and reality..."; "There remains madness, 'the madness that one locks up'..."; "These people are honest to a fault..." (of the asylum inmates); "Beloved imagination...". From "A Great Black Poet," Sulfur reprint at webdelsol.com: "Aime Cesaire's voice, beautiful like nascent oxygen"; "To sing or not to sing..."; "All the grimacing shadows were torn apart...".

**Verified only by secondary quotation [A, secondary]:** the opening of *Nadja* ("Who am I? If this once I were to rely on a proverb...") and its closing line ("Beauty will be convulsive or will not be at all"), both Howard's translation; the scanned primary page on archive.org was access-restricted.

**Not verified [R]:** the pistol sentence of the *Second Manifesto*, attributed by wide citation only; "Let us not mince words: the marvelous is always beautiful...," not found on the 1924 full-text page fetched.

**Withdrawn.** "Love is always before you. Love. / L'oeil existe a l'etat sauvage" was a composite of two unrelated Wikiquote entries. The first half is attributed variously to *L'Amour fou* and *Poisson soluble* (Pléiade I, p. 384) and could not be traced to a citable page. "L'œil existe à l'état sauvage" is genuine, the opening sentence of *Le Surréalisme et la peinture* (1928); Simon Watson Taylor's English (1972) reads "The eye exists in its savage state," singular, not Wikiquote's plural gloss. "We belong to those who say no to darkness" is Césaire's; see section 5.

## 10. Second-pool audit: corrections this report did not already carry

A second research pool (chronology, geography, quotations, interlock, afterlife lenses, then an adversarial audit) was worked against French Wikipedia, Encyclopedia.com, Polizzotti, and the Seaver/Lane Manifesto text on archive.org. Most of its findings duplicate sections 1 to 9. What follows is what it adds, and the two places where its tagging differs from this report's.

**Childhood, an unreconciled pair.** Polizzotti has the family move to the Paris region in Breton's infancy and settle at the working suburb of **Pantin [R, no year given]**, with childhood *summers* on the Breton coast near **Lorient [R]**. Encyclopedia.com instead has him **raised in Brittany by his maternal grandfather**, dated loosely to 1900 and tagged **[A]** in that pool. These are compatible only if "partly" is doing heavy work. Both are carried as stops in the journey file with the split stated in `date_confidence`; neither is adjudicated.

**Sequence, the war hospitals.** French Wikipedia puts Breton at the **Nantes** hospital as a nurse from **July 1915**, meeting Vaché in **early 1916**, and only in the **summer of 1916** at **Saint-Dizier**. Several English summaries date both postings to 1916 and put Saint-Dizier first. Nantes precedes Saint-Dizier by a year, which also means Vaché precedes Freud. See section 6 for how this parts from andrebreton.fr's dating of the Nantes posting.

**The 1913 conflation.** Encyclopedia.com folds medical studies, early verse, and friendships with Valéry, Apollinaire, and Reverdy into a single 1913 entry. Apollinaire and Reverdy belong to 1916 and 1917 respectively, after mobilization; the PCN certificate (with Théodore Fraenkel) is c. 1913-14.

**Tzara, a year early.** One chronology dated Breton's close association with Tzara and the formation of Paris Dada to **1919**. Tzara did not reach Paris until **January 1920**; *Littérature* (March 1919) predates him by ten months.

**Lam, not met in Martinique.** A claim that Breton collaborated with Wifredo Lam "whom he had met en route through Martinique" is false. Per en.wikipedia "Wifredo Lam" they met in **Paris in 1939**, and the *Fata Morgana* drawings were made at **Marseille in 1940-41**, before the crossing. They sailed as existing collaborators, which is how the journey file puts it.

**The Vichy phrase, kept out of the quotation count.** "The very negation of the national revolution" is **[R]**. En.wikipedia carries it with an inline reference to **Franklin Rosemont, *André Breton and the First Principles of Surrealism* (1978, ISBN 0-904383-89-X)**; that is Rosemont's English rendering, and no primary decree or dated gazette was traced. The journey file paraphrases rather than quoting.

**Two tag divergences with this report, left open.** (1) This report treats "the greatest lyrical monument of our time" as **[A]**, having read the Sulfur reprint of "A Great Black Poet"; the second audit, which never reached the essay, tagged the phrase **[R]** as tertiary-only. The direct reading is the stronger evidence and the **[A]** stands, with the second pool's caution recorded. (2) The second pool tagged the *Capitaine Paul-Lemerle* **event** as **[A]** on en.wikipedia "Wifredo Lam" plus fr.wikipedia "André Breton", leaving only the **ship's name** at **[R]**; this report reaches fr.wikipedia's article on the ship itself and so holds the name too.

**Two further datings.** Breton was in Mexico from **April to roughly August 1938**, and the manifesto was drafted in **July**, so an April-to-May window is too narrow. And the monument at Batignolles is an **octaèdre étoilé**, a starred octahedron, not the "plain octahedron" of one lens.

**Gaps this audit could not close:** the year Breton left the Lycée Chaptal; the exact publication day of *Nadja* in 1928; the French original of the "It is Jacques Vaché to whom I owe the most" line, used in the journey file from an English rendering on en.wikipedia and flagged there as such.
