# Louis XVI (1754-1793): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `louis_xvi`. Companion: `louis_xvi.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

**Method.** Written from a research pool in five lenses (chronology, geography, quotations, interlock with atlas travelers, afterlife), then passed through adversarial verification that corrected about a fifth of the entries. Where it corrected the pool, both readings are printed and the journey carries the corrected one, with the correction named in `date_confidence`. Where two lenses contradict each other, the contradiction is printed and left standing rather than smoothed.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested; a bare **[A]** in sections 1 to 9 means Wikipedia, 'Louis XVI', the pool's spine source, and any other source is named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition. **[A/corr]** attested, but the pool carried a wrong date, place or coordinate that verification fixed. Standard historiography carried in the journey but not itemized by the pool is collected in one list near the end.

---

## 1. Birth and childhood, 1754-1770

- Born at Versailles, third son of the Dauphin Louis and Marie-Josèphe of Saxony, titled duc de Berry. **[A: with Britannica]** Geography gives 1754-08-23, chronology only the year; the journey uses the full date.
- Sent with his elder brother the duc de Bourgogne to Bellevue at Meudon, 17 May to 27 September 1756, on the advice of the Genevan physician Théodore Tronchin, as a sickly child. **[A: fr.wikipedia, quoted verbatim in the pool]** Verification restored Tronchin and the brother, both dropped in the first pass.
- Father dies 20 December 1765; the eleven-year-old becomes Dauphin. **[A]** *Contradiction, not resolved:* the pool places the death at Versailles, standard biography at Fontainebleau with burial at Sens. The journey keeps the Versailles pin and flags the conflict in `date_confidence`.
- Mother dies of tuberculosis 13 March 1767 at Versailles. **[A]**

**Gap.** Nothing in the pool between March 1767 and the marriage negotiation: no tutors sourced, no episode, no reading. Three years are missing, and the journey has no stop in them.

---

## 2. The Austrian marriage, 1770

The pool's worst tangle: chronology dates the marriage **1770-04-19** at **Versailles**, geography has the wedding at the Chapelle Royale on **1770-05-16**. Verification found the chronology entry sites a Vienna ceremony at Versailles. Proxy marriage **19 April 1770, Augustinian church, Vienna**; wedding in person **16 May 1770, Chapelle Royale, Versailles**. **[A/corr: en. and fr.wikipedia]** The journey splits them; the Vienna pin is compiler-supplied.

Route between the ceremonies, all **[A: fr.wikipedia]**: 14 May, first meeting at the edge of the forest of Compiègne, then the château; 15 May, the Carmel de Saint-Denis, convent of the king's daughter Madame Louise, then a family supper at la Muette where the Dauphine is presented and sees Madame du Barry; 16 May, the wedding. Compiègne and the Carmel are folded into neighbouring stops for length, not dropped for doubt. La Muette was corrected twice: the pool called it a gathering "to present the fifteen-year-old Dauphin's household", where the source has the supper; and the pin moved.

## 3. The reign before the crisis, 1774-1788

- Louis XV dies of smallpox 10 May 1774. **[A: with Britannica]** *Pool error:* the geography lens called him "the twenty-year-old Dauphin". Born 23 August 1754, he was **nineteen**.
- Coronation at Reims, 11 June 1775. **[A]**
- Recall of the parlements, with the remark "It may be considered politically unwise, but it seems to me to be the general wish and I want to be loved." **[A as attribution: fn. 29, primary citation not traced]**
- Treaty of Alliance with the American colonies, 1778. **[A]** *Precision gap:* the pool gives the bare year; the journey defaults to 1778-01-01 and notes in `date_confidence` that signature was 6 February 1778.
- Children, all **[A: children list]**: Marie-Thérèse-Charlotte 1778-12-19; Louis-Joseph 1781-10-22; Louis-Charles 1785-03-27; Sophie-Hélène-Béatrix 1786-07-09, dead 1787-06-19.
- Rambouillet bought from the duc de Penthièvre, 1783 **[A: 'Château de Rambouillet']**; Fontainebleau the fixed autumn residence **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Choisy-le-Roi and Marly are undated **[R]** residence entries, no stops.
- Saint-Cloud bought 1785 **by Marie Antoinette in her own name**, 6,000,000 livres, from the duc d'Orléans; the family summers there in 1790. **[A/corr: 'Château de Saint-Cloud']** The pool's "bought for Marie Antoinette" inverted the point. No stop, cut for length.
- Cherbourg, the ninth cone of the breakwater sunk, his only major provincial journey as king. **[A/corr: fr.wikipedia, 'Rade de Cherbourg']** Verification dated it 22 June 1786 and moved the pin out of open water.
- Edict of Versailles, 7 November 1787, civil status for non-Catholics. **[A]**
- Convocation of the Estates-General, 8 August 1788. **[A]**

**Gap.** No Turgot, Calonne, Notables, grain policy or ministries anywhere in the pool. The fiscal history that produced 1789 survives in it as the phrase "fiscal crisis", so the journey's reign segment is one of houses, children and a single edict.

## 4. The year 1789

- Estates-General convenes for the first time since 1614. *Contradiction inside the pool:* chronology **1789-05-01**, geography **1789-05-05**. Verification: 1 May was named in the convocation, the body convened **5 May** at the Salle des Menus Plaisirs **[A/corr: 'Estates General of 1789']**. The chronology entry was also out of order, after the 4 June death of the Dauphin.
- Dauphin Louis-Joseph dies at Meudon, 4 June 1789, aged seven. **[A/corr]** The pool's framing, "days before the Estates-General crisis intensifies", is wrong: the body had opened a month earlier. Recast as a month in, sixteen days before the Tennis Court Oath.
- Tennis Court Oath, 20 June 1789. **[A]**
- Storming of the Bastille, 14 July 1789. **[A]** The exchange with La Rochefoucauld-Liancourt: **[A: 'Storming of the Bastille', fn. 55, after Chaussinand-Nogaret]**.
- Women's March and removal to Paris. The pool tagged it **[R]**, dating unconfirmed. Verification reversed that: march the morning of **5 October**, family escorted to Paris about one in the afternoon of **6 October**, installed at the Tuileries. **[A/corr: 'Women's March on Versailles']**

## 5. The constitutional monarchy, 1790-1791

- Fête de la Fédération, Champ de Mars, 14 July 1790; the king swears to uphold the constitution. **[A: pool geography lens, "standard historical record", corroborated by the Lafayette journey]**
- **18 April 1791**: a crowd blocks the royal carriages, preventing Easter at Saint-Cloud with non-juring clergy; the king refuses to have the way cleared by force. **[A/corr: fr.wikipedia, 'Fuite de Varennes']** *Pool error:* pinned at Saint-Cloud, where they never arrived. The blockade was in the Tuileries courtyard and the journey moves the pin there.
- The refusal quoted in the pool's correction note, "Je ne veux pas que le sang coule pour moi", is in fr.wikipedia but **not** in the quotation lens, so the journey paraphrases rather than quoting it.

## 6. The flight to Varennes, 20-25 June 1791

Stops in travel order, all **[A: 'Flight to Varennes']**: Tuileries, departure near midnight 20 June; Fromentières, the king talking to peasants at a horse change; Chaintrix, the queen leaving silver dishes, with Châlons, where townspeople recognize and applaud the party without stopping it, folded in; Pont de Somme-Vesle, where Choiseul's hussars have already withdrawn; Sainte-Menehould, Drouet recognizing him from an assignat; Varennes, arrest at the procureur Sauce's house about 11 p.m., 50 km short of Montmédy; Tuileries, return 25 June through a crowd silent with hats on.

Somme-Vesle was **[R]** in the pool and is corrected to **[A]**, its pin 8.5 km wrong; the Varennes pin was 2.8 km wrong. Bondy, Meaux, Clermont-en-Argonne and Dormans are **[R]** traditional route accounts and are cut.

## 7. Constitution to fall, 1791-1792

Three pool entries were tagged **[R]**, "not independently confirmed in sources consulted for this brief". Verification reversed all three: these are among the best-attested dates of the Revolution, and the tags were an artifact of the gathering pass.

- **Constitution of 1791.** Pool "1791-09", [R]. Corrected: completed 3 September, accepted in writing 13 September, sworn 14 September. **[A/corr: 'French Constitution of 1791']**
- **War on Austria, 20 April 1792**, formally against "the king of Bohemia and Hungary". Pool [R], corrected to **[A]**; date and substance stood.
- **Temple imprisonment.** Chronology "1792-08" tagged [R] against geography "1792-08-13" tagged [A]. Confirmed **13 August 1792**. **[A/corr: 'Temple (Paris)']**

Also: 10 August 1792, the Tuileries stormed, refuge with the Legislative Assembly, the king's written order for the Swiss Guards to ground arms **[A: '10 August (French Revolution)']**; 21 September 1792, monarchy abolished, the citizen Louis Capet **[A]**.

## 8. The trial, December 1792 to January 1793

All **[A: 'Trial of Louis XVI'; fr.wikipedia, 'Procès de Louis XVI'; the Wikisource trial pamphlet]**. 3 December, the Convention votes to try him. 4 December, Barère presents the indictment. 6 December, the form is decreed. 10 December, the "Rapport sur les crimes imputés à Louis Capet". 11 December, **first interrogation** on thirty-three charges **[A/corr]**. 25 December, he dictates his will. 26 December, de Sèze with Malesherbes and Tronchet delivers the defense. 15 January, roll call on guilt, 683 guilty. 16-17 January, penalty roll call, death proclaimed in the early hours of the 17th, 361 of 721 for death without condition **[A/corr]**. 20 January, reprieve rejected 380 to 310, notification at the Temple **[A/corr]**.

Two pool errors, corrected: it fused the indictment and the first interrogation on 4 December, where the interrogation began **11 December**; and it dated the death sentence **20 January**, which is the refusal of the reprieve. The tally, 361 of 721, matches the source and stands.

## 9. Execution and afterlife

- Guillotined at the Place de la Révolution, 21 January 1793, about 10:22 a.m., between the Champs-Élysées and the pedestal of the toppled statue of Louis XV; spectators dip pikes and handkerchiefs in the blood, the head is shown to a crowd crying "Vive la Nation", the body goes into a quicklime pit at the Madeleine cemetery, head at the feet. **[A: 'Execution of Louis XVI', citing eyewitness and vicar accounts]**
- 1793: the Convention empties the royal tombs at Saint-Denis; 158 monarchs end in a crypt ossuary. **[A: 'Basilica of Saint-Denis']**
- 21 January 1815: remains identified as the king's and queen's exhumed and reinterred at Saint-Denis, Marie-Thérèse present **[A]**; 1816, Louis XVIII commissions the funerary monument **[A/corr]**. The pool fused the monument with "simple black marble slabs mark the actual tomb"; those slabs date from **1975** and the journey drops them.
- 1826: Chapelle expiatoire inaugurated on the cemetery ground, Fontaine architect, three million livres, funded by Louis XVIII and the duchesse d'Angoulême; annual mass on the Sunday nearest 21 January; monument historique 1914. **[A: 'Chapelle expiatoire']**
- 8 June 1795: Louis-Charles dies in the Temple at ten. 2000: mitochondrial DNA on the heart kept since 1795 confirms his identity and refutes Naundorff, buried at Delft as Louis XVII; roughly a hundred claimants had come forward after 1814. **[A: 'Louis XVII']**
- Marie-Thérèse-Charlotte, the only child to reach adulthood, dies at Frohsdorf, Austria, 19 October 1851 **[A]**, and the Valois statue of 1829 goes from Montpellier to Louisville, Kentucky in 1966, installed 1967, in storage since September 2020 after vandalism **[A: 'Statue of Louis XVI']**. Neither has a stop: the journey was at its ceiling.
- Two afterlife entries are **[R]** and carried nowhere: the Freemason's cry invoking Jacques de Molay at the scaffold, apocryphal in the source itself, and the "gourd of Louis XVI" blood relic, whose 2013 genetic study the pool records as unreachable.

## Quotations

Eleven attributed sayings in the pool, eight used in the journey. **The last words are contested and the journey does not resolve them.** Three versions, all **[A]** as attributions. From the abbé Edgeworth, *Memoirs* (London, 1815), in Wikipedia, 'Execution of Louis XVI': "I die innocent of all the crimes laid to my charge; I Pardon those who have occasioned my death; and I pray to God that the blood you are going to shed may never be visited on France." From the executioner Sanson's letter of 20 February 1793, printed in the *Thermomètre du Jour* the next day, two shorter ones: "My people, I die innocent!" and "Gentlemen, I am innocent of everything of which I am accused." The journey sets Edgeworth as the stop `quote` and names the Sanson variants inside `quote_source`, so the disagreement travels with the artifact.

**Two Wikisource citations in the pool were wrong.** "Oh, the murderers! the murderers!" and "Do they think me base enough to destroy myself?" were cited to Part 3 of the trial pamphlet. Both are verbatim in **Part 2**, which carries the execution narrative; Part 3 holds only the will, and the word "murderers" is absent from it. The journey cites Part 2.

Three attributions rest on untraced Wikipedia footnotes and are labelled so in the journey: the parlements remark (fn. 29), "must always consult public opinion; it is never wrong" (fn. 30, a fragment lacking subject and verb as quoted), and "Any news of La Pérouse?" (fn. 46).

## Honest gaps

- **1767-1770**: nothing at all.
- **Government**: no Turgot, Calonne, Notables, grain policy or ministries.
- **Marie Antoinette**: in the pool only as bride, mother, and the woman who left silver dishes at a relay; her trial and execution appear only as a date inside the Lavoisier interlock. The non-consummation and the marital crisis of the 1770s are absent and not carried.
- **The gourd relic**: the 2013 genetic study could not be reached in session; unverified, not in the journey. **Frohsdorf**: no coordinates for the place where the last of the four children died.
- **Scholarship**: nothing by Petitfils, Price, Hardman or Tackett was reachable. The base is Wikipedia in two languages, one Wikisource pamphlet and Britannica, thin for a life this well documented.
