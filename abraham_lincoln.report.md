# Abraham Lincoln (1809-1865): research report
*Atlas of Journeys working file, 2026-08-10, from the verified research pool for slug `abraham_lincoln`.*

**Scope note.** The pool is unusually weighted: about a third of its chronology and *all fourteen* of its verified quotations concern money. The biography is present in outline, the currency in detail. The journey follows that weighting and gives the money its own eight-stop segment.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, standard consensus, or an inference the sources do not draw. Where the pool tagged an item [A] but its own note undermines the tag, it is retagged here.

---

## 1. Kentucky and Indiana, 1809-1830

Born 12 February 1809, one-room cabin, **Sinking Spring Farm**, to Thomas Lincoln and Nancy Hanks **[A: Wikipedia, *Abraham Lincoln*; NPS, Abraham Lincoln Birthplace NHP]**; site 37.5311, -85.7375 **[A: NPS]**. Move to **Indiana, 1816**, "reportedly over disputed land titles": **[A]** move, **[R]** motive, hedged by the source itself. **Nancy Hanks Lincoln dies of milk sickness, 5 October 1818 [A]**. Thomas remarries **Sarah Bush Johnston, 2 December 1819 [A]**, who "encourages his reading"; the affectionate detail is Herndon-derived tradition, **[R]**. Sister **Sarah Lincoln Grigsby dies in childbirth, 20 January 1828 [A]**.

**County anachronism, corrected.** The pool says "LaRue County." Sinking Spring lay in **Hardin County** in 1809; LaRue was carved out of Hardin in **1843**. Wikipedia and the NPS give the modern county, not the 1809 one. The journey says Hardin and lets the campa note the later line.

**Contradiction: the schooling figure.** The pool carries, tagged [A], "formal schooling totals less than twelve months," dated **1820**. Two problems, both conceded in its own note. (1) *The tag is wrong*: no attendance records survive and the figure descends from Lincoln's retrospective sketches, as the hedge "commonly cited" concedes. Retagged **[R]**. (2) *The date is wrong in kind*: the formulation is "less than 12 months by age 15," cumulative to roughly 1824, not an event in 1820. **Flagged, not resolved.** No stop of its own; it sits in the 1818 campa with the status in `date_confidence`.

## 2. Illinois, 1830-1854

**Macon County**, March 1830 **[A]**. Clerk at a New Salem store, 1831 **[A]**; the Offutt flatboat voyage, **[R]**. **1832**: captain of Illinois volunteers in the **Black Hawk War**, plus a first, failed candidacy **[A]**; that he saw no combat, **[R]**. **1833**: co-owner of the **Lincoln-Berry store**, which fails into debt **[A]**. **1834**: elected to the **Illinois House**, first of four terms for Sangamon County, 1834-1842 **[A]**; that the legislature sat at **Vandalia** until the 1839 removal is **[R]**, and the journey pins the 1834 stop there on that basis. **Bar admission, 9 September 1836 [A]**; circuit riding, **[R]**. **Lyceum address, 27 January 1838 [A]**: "political religion of the nation" is genuinely from it, but the pool supplies **no verified text**, so it is paraphrased and carried in no `quote` field.

Marries **Mary Todd**, 4 November 1842 **[A]**. **Robert** born 1843, "the only of his four children to survive to adulthood" **[A]**, month and day not given. Partnership with **William Herndon**, 1844 **[A]**. **Eddie** born 1846 **[A]**, dies 1 February 1850 **[A]**; **Willie** born 21 December 1850 **[A]**; **Tad** born 4 April 1853 **[A]**. Single term in the **U.S. House**, 7th district, **1847 [A]**, year only; the Spot Resolutions and his Mexican War opposition are **not in the pool** and are asserted nowhere in the journey.

**Ordering anomaly in the pool, not a factual contradiction.** The chronology array lists the 1838 Lyceum address before the 1836 bar admission, and Willie's death (1862-02-20) after the preliminary Proclamation (1862-09). Array-order errors, not date disagreements. Corrected in the journey's travel order, noted so the correction is visible.

## 3. The question reopens, 1854-1860

**Kansas-Nebraska Act**, May 1854 **[A]**. **Peoria Speech**, October 1854 **[A]**; duration, the pairing against Douglas, the arguments themselves, **[R]**. **1856**: joins the forming Republican Party, supported for the vice-presidential nomination at its first national convention **[A]**; the pool's place field, "Bloomington, Illinois / Philadelphia," conflates the state and national conventions, so this is folded into the 1858 campa rather than given a stop with an ambiguous pin. **1858**: seven debates against **Stephen Douglas**, Democrats hold the legislature, Douglas returned **[A]**, **year only**; debate sites and dates are **not in the pool**, so the journey pins Ottawa, the first site, and says so, with format and crowd sizes **[R]**. **Cooper Union**, 27 February 1860 **[A]**; Brady photograph, **[R]**. **Elected 16th President**, November 1860 **[A]**; vote share and the four-way split, **[R]**.

**The Damascus rifle, and its date wrinkle.** The only place the atlas already names Lincoln is `abdelkader.journey.json`, stop "Damascus, the letter to Bishop Pavy and the honors of the world," dated **1860-09-01**, which names **a gold-inlaid rifle sent by Abraham Lincoln from Washington [A]** among the honors sent after the July 1860 rescue. At that date Lincoln was neither president nor president-elect but a private citizen in Springfield, two months from election. Either the gift is later than September, the honors having arrived over many months, or the attribution is loose for a gift from the United States government. **No source reached here resolves it.** The journey keeps the atlas's date, inherits the Washington pin **38.8977, -77.0365** byte-identical, and puts the problem in `date_confidence`.

## 5. The money: greenbacks and national banks

- **July 1861**: **Demand Notes**, approx. $50 million authorized, "later called greenbacks for the green ink printed on their reverse" **[A: Wikipedia, *Greenback (money)*]**. **December 1861**: banks suspend **specie payments**, driving Chase toward paper: **[A]** suspension, **[R]** causal framing, per the pool's hedge, which cites "Federal Reserve History background (via secondary synthesis)," a page not directly read.
- **25 February 1862**: **First Legal Tender Act**, **$150 million** in United States Notes, legal tender for most debts, unbacked **[A: Wikipedia, *Greenback (money)* / *Legal Tender Act of 1862*; U.S. Statutes at Large, 37th Cong.]**. **Contradiction, not resolved:** the pool's note reads "figure appears elsewhere as $60 million in first tranche per Chase's account; sources vary on the initial authorized amount." The campa uses $150 million, the figure both pool entries carry; `date_confidence` records the disagreement, unadjudicated. Second act **July 1862 [A]**, third **March 1863 [A]**, months only.
- **25 February 1863**: **National Currency Act (National Banking Act)**: federally chartered banks, notes secured by Treasury bonds, the **Office of the Comptroller of the Currency**, at least one third of capital in government bonds **[A: Wikipedia, *National Banking Act*; OCC agency history]**. Revised act **3 June 1864 [A]**; **10 percent tax on state bank notes** enacted **1865**, effective 1866 **[A]**.
- **Depreciation [A: same]**: December 1862, gold at roughly a **29 percent** premium; July 1864, **258 greenback dollars per 100 in gold**, the wartime worst; **December 1878**, par. Postdating him, *Hepburn v. Griswold* (1870) strikes down legal tender for pre-Act debts under **Chief Justice Chase**, who built it as Treasury Secretary, and the **Legal Tender Cases** (1871) reverse that 5-4 **[A]**. No stops, recorded as the policy's verdict.

**Contradiction: Chase's portrait.** The pool carries, tagged [A], that Chase appears on the one-dollar greenback, "the only Treasury official so depicted on that denomination." The **portrait is real [A]**: engraved by Joseph P. Ourdan, $1 United States Note, Series 1862-63, per Wikipedia *Greenback (money)* and *United States Note*. The **superlative is unsourced**: neither source makes it, and the $1 later carried Washington. **Dropped.** What survives is the irony: the sitting Treasury Secretary put his own face on the first federal dollar bill while seeking the presidency from the man who appointed him. His 1864 candidacy is **[R]**.

**Contradiction: a quotation that is not a sentence.** The pool's fifth quotation reads: *"The organization of banking associations, under a general act of Congress, well guarded in its provisions, to such associations the Government might furnish circulating notes, on the security of United States bonds deposited in the Treasury."* **This is not a sentence Lincoln wrote.** It splices two consecutive sentences of the Second Annual Message (1 December 1862), amputating the head of the first and joining them with a comma. The actual text:

> "I know of none which promises so certain results and is at the same time so unobjectionable as the organization of banking associations, under a general act of Congress, well guarded in its provisions."
> "To such associations the Government might furnish circulating notes, on the security of United States bonds deposited in the Treasury."

("None" answers a preceding rhetorical question about how a safe and uniform currency might be secured.) The journey uses **both sentences whole, with the break preserved**, at the National Banking Act stop. The pool's framing, that this is the proposal which became the 1863 act, is accurate and kept.

All other pool quotations were checked against the Wikisource texts of the three Annual Messages and are used verbatim. **Six of the journey's forty-five stops carry a quote**, all from those messages and all in this segment: below the usual third of stops, and a consequence of the pool's shape.

## 6. Death, and the body

Shot by **John Wilkes Booth** at **Ford's Theatre** during *Our American Cousin*, 14 April 1865; dies next morning at the **Petersen House** across 10th Street NW **[A: contemporary Washington press; NPS, Ford's Theatre NHS]**. Booth's knowledge of the play and the absent door guard: **[R]**. Stanton's deathbed line is given in campa as disputed between "the ages" and "the angels."

**Coordinates corrected.** The pool gave 38.8973, -77.0247: roughly 130 m off, mid-block rather than on the building. Ford's Theatre, 511 10th St NW, is **38.8967, -77.0258**; the Petersen House **38.8969, -77.0259**. Both used, on separate stops, the shooting and the death having happened in different buildings.

**Funeral train [A: NPS itinerary via Wikipedia, *Funeral and burial of Abraham Lincoln*]. Distance corrected**: the pool said "roughly 1,700 miles"; the documented figure is **1,654 miles**, seven states, thirteen days, thirteen stops, departing Washington 21 April, Springfield 3 May, burial 4 May. That it retraced his 1861 inaugural route and that Willie's coffin travelled with him: **[R]**. **Buried at Oak Ridge Cemetery, 4 May 1865 [A: Illinois Historic Preservation Agency]. Coordinates corrected, four times**: the pool gave 39.8203, -89.6501 on four separate afterlife items, roughly 600 m off and outside the tomb grounds. The Lincoln Tomb is **39.8233, -89.6558** (Wikipedia, *Lincoln Tomb*).

**1876 body-snatching [A: Secret Service case records and Speer, *The Lincoln Conspiracy Trial*, both at second hand in the pool, neither verified here]**: a ring under **Big Jim Kennally** tries to take the body on 7 November 1876 to ransom it for a jailed engraver; agents hidden in the tomb foil it. The engraver's name, the prison, the cash figure and the timing: **[R]**. **1901 reinterment [A]**: 26 September, on Robert Todd Lincoln's authority, the coffin set in a steel cage under roughly ten feet of concrete; the twenty-three witnesses, **[R]**.

**Contradiction: seventeen movings.** The pool carries, tagged [A], that custodians moved the coffin "by some counts as many as seventeen times." **Retagged [R].** Documented relocations 1865-1901 number closer to half a dozen (receiving vault May 1865, temporary vault December 1865, crypt 1871, sarcophagus 1874, basement 1876, subterranean vault during the 1899-1901 restoration, final vault 1901), and the claim's own "by some counts" concedes the problem. The campa states the documented pattern as fact and names seventeen as what popular telling makes it. **Not adjudicated:** nothing reached here establishes where seventeen came from.

## 7. Afterlife: coin, statue, memorial, mountain

- **Lincoln cent**, 2 August 1909, centennial issue, **Victor David Brenner**, first circulating U.S. coin to depict a real historical person **[A: U.S. Mint]**; the initials controversy, **[R]**. **No coordinate in the pool**; the journey pins the third Philadelphia Mint building, in service in 1909, and says so.
- **Manchester statue. Place and date were mismatched in the pool.** George Grey Barnard's Lincoln was presented by Mr and Mrs Charles Phelps Taft in **1919** and erected in **Platt Fields Park**; it moved to what is now **Lincoln Square** only in **1986**, so a 1919 entry cannot carry Lincoln Square coordinates. The journey dates it 1919, pins **Platt Fields Park, roughly 53.4497, -2.2214**, and names the 1986 move in campa. The pool's "relocated more than once" overstates it: **one** relocation. The cotton-famine dedication is **[A]**; the 1862 address and Lincoln's reply, **[R]**.
- **Lincoln Memorial** dedicated 30 May 1922 **[A: NPS]**; segregated seating, **[R]**. **Mount Rushmore**, Borglum, 1927-1941 **[A: NPS]**; the Six Grandfathers name and the broken Fort Laramie treaty are **[R]** here, stated in campa as fact because uncontested elsewhere.
- **Basler's *Collected Works*. Corrected.** The pool said "nine-volume," 1953. It is **eight volumes** (Rutgers, **1953-55**), plus index and a 1974 supplement; "nine" counts the index. That it superseded Nicolay-Hay is standard but unstated in the source. No stop, as with ***Team of Rivals*** (2005) and the 2012 film **[A]**.
- **The premonitory dream [R: Lamon, *Recollections*, 1895, posthumous]** and **the White House ghost [R: White House Historical Association]**, the latter including a Churchill anecdote with no primary documentation. **No stops**: a stop would dignify them past their evidence.

## 9. Honest gaps

- **The pool's `geography` array is empty.** No geographic lens ran. Coordinates come from the pool's own notes (Sinking Spring, Ford's Theatre, Petersen House, Lincoln Tomb, Lincoln Memorial, Mount Rushmore, Manchester); from inherited atlas pins (Washington 38.8977, -77.0365); or from standard published site coordinates supplied here for the remaining Indiana, Illinois, Washington, New York, Gettysburg and Philadelphia locations. That third group is noted in `date_confidence` and is approximate to a few hundred metres.
- **No verified text** of the Lyceum address, either Inaugural, or the Gettysburg Address.
- **Month or year only** for most of the pre-presidential chronology and much of the war, including the Indiana move, New Salem, the Black Hawk War, the 1834 election, the term in Congress, the 1858 debates, the habeas corpus suspension, the currency legislation of 1861-63 and the preliminary Proclamation. All defaulted to `-01`, every default stated in `date_confidence`.
- **Absent from the pool entirely**, therefore absent from the journey: the Spot Resolutions; the 1860 Chicago convention; Fort Sumter; every battle and every general by name except McClellan as an electoral opponent; Hampton Roads; the Richmond visit of April 1865; the Thirteenth Amendment; the conspirators' trial. **The war is present here almost entirely as a fiscal event**, which is what the pool made possible.
