# La Difunta Correa (Deolinda Correa): research report

*2026-08-14. The folk saint of San Juan, Argentina: a woman said to have died of thirst in the travesía of Caucete in the 1840s while following her conscripted husband, her infant son found alive at her breast. Her grave at Vallecito is now the largest unofficial pilgrimage site in the country.*

**Legend: [A] = attested, with the source named · [R] = reconstruction, tradition, or inference.**

The governing condition of this file: **there is no documented life here.** Every biographical claim below is oral tradition written down long after the fact, and the only primary document either encyclopedia produces is a single 1865 newspaper advertisement (§3). The Archdiocese of San Juan opened a formal investigation in May 2026 to find out whether she existed at all (§7). This report separates what the sources say from what can be shown, and does not adjudicate between conflicting versions.

---

## 1. Origins: Angaco, and the dates that do not hold

**Birth.** No birth record survives, and none of the reachable sources gives a birth date, a birth year, or a birthplace. es.wikipedia says only that she was born *durante las Guerras de Independencia* **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Difunta Correa']**. The Argentine wars of independence run 1810-1818, so the journey file places her birth at the midpoint of that window (1815) as a **placeholder, not a finding**, with the hedge carried in `date_confidence`.

> **Correction.** The pool gave "born in San Juan province... 1800." Both halves fail. 1800 *predates* the wars of independence the same entry cites as its setting; and neither Wikipedia names San Juan as her birthplace, only that the family **lived** in Angaco. Birthplace is recorded here as **unknown [R]**.

**Name.** Deolinda Correa; es.wikipedia also records **Dalinda Antonia Correa [A]**, without explaining the variance.

**Residence.** The family lived in the **departamento de Angaco**, San Juan **[A: es.wikipedia, "Vivían en el departamento de Angaco... junto a la familia"]**.

> **Coordinate correction.** The pool gave two Angaco points, -31.1973 / -68.1218 (geography lens) and -31.35 / -68.28 (afterlife lens), ~22 km apart. The first is a Nominatim centroid for the whole department polygon and lands on uninhabited desert. The inhabited Angaco oasis is the department seat **Villa del Salvador, -31.4533 / -68.4031**, 23 km northeast of San Juan city **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Departamento Angaco']**. The journey file uses **-31.453 / -68.403**. Where a rural family plausibly lived; not a documented address.

**Husband.** **Clemente Bustos [A: es.wikipedia, "su marido, Clemente Bustos, fue reclutado forzosamente hacia 1840"]**. Marriage date **unknown [R]**; placed generically before the conscription.

> **Attribution correction.** The pool credited the name Clemente Bustos to English Wikipedia, which never names him: it says only that "the husband of Deolinda Correa was forcibly recruited around the year 1840." The name comes from **es.wikipedia**; La Capital independently uses the surname Bustos **[A]**.

## 2. The conscription and the crossing

**Conscription.** Bustos is forcibly recruited into montonera forces marching toward La Rioja, *hacia 1840* **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia 'around 1840']**. La Capital: *"alrededor del 1840 lo reclutaron a Bustos para ir a pelear en el enfrentamiento de unitarios y federales"* **[A: 17 Feb 2021]**. He is later said to have sickened on the march and been abandoned by the troop **[A: es.wikipedia]**. **No muster roll or period document supporting any of it has been produced by any source. [gap]**

**Provisions.** Bread, charqui, two water containers, the nursing infant **[A: es.wikipedia]**. La Capital names the containers: *"dos 'chifles' de agua"*, hollowed cattle horns **[A]**.

**Death.** Thirst and exhaustion beneath an algarrobo in the desert of Caucete **[A: es.wikipedia; Argentina.gob.ar, "fallece por deshidratación"]**.

> **Date correction.** The pool dated the death "c. 1840." es.wikipedia says *"en algún momento de la década de 1840"*, sometime in the 1840s, deliberately vague. **1840 is the year of the recruitment, not of the death**, and the two are conflated in most retellings. The journey file uses 1841 as an explicit placeholder.

**Discovery. SOURCES CONFLICT, NOT RESOLVED.**

| | es.wikipedia | en.wikipedia |
|---|---|---|
| Who found her | **arrieros** (muleteers) | **gauchos** |
| When | *"al día siguiente"*, the next day | "days later" |
| Milk | *"de los cuales aún fluía leche"*, still flowing | described as a miraculously ever-full breast |
| The child | *"se llevaron consigo al niño"*, they took him with them | not stated |

The Spanish account is the fuller one and draws on Chertudi and Newbery, *La Difunta Correa* (1978), the standard folkloric study. The journey file follows **es.wikipedia (arrieros, next day)** and states the conflict in `date_confidence`. **The pool carried this contradiction internally**: its chronology said "the day after," its afterlife lens "days later," in the same pass. Flagged, not resolved.

Argentina.gob.ar, the official state tourism page, states the miracle flatly: *"Fue hallada sin vida, pero con su hijo aún amamantando"* **[A]**.

**Burial.** The **arrieros who found her buried her at the spot**, which took the name **Vallecito [A: es.wikipedia: "Los arrieros la enterraron en el paraje conocido hoy como Vallecito"]**.

> **Correction.** The pool's chronology had "local gauchos" bury her as a group separate from the finders, and added "a simple cross" as a burial marker. Neither holds: es.wikipedia has the *same* arrieros bury her, and the cross in the sources is the **hilltop cross of the pre-1940s shrine [A: en.wikipedia]**. The journey file gives a mound and a place-name, no cross.

## 3. The child, and the only document

Oral tradition names the surviving son **Baudilio Bustos Correa [R]**.

**The 1865 advertisement.** A notice in the San Juan newspaper **El Zonda** names a Baudilio Bustos Correa living near the **Plaza 25 de Mayo**, selling property before a move to Córdoba **[A: both encyclopedias]**. **This is the only primary-source document either produces bearing on the existence of the Correa/Bustos family.** It confirms none of the legend's other details, and the identification of this Baudilio with the child is **tradition, not proven genealogy [R]**.

The 1865 issues belong to **Sarmiento's 1862 revival** of *El Zonda*; the paper he founded in 1839 ran only six numbers **[A]**. The single surviving trace of her family is therefore printed in a sheet founded by the province's most documented son. That is the one link to another traveller in the corpus resting on something other than shared geography, and the only one named in the campa prose.

> **Correction.** The pool created a waypoint called "El Zonda newspaper archive, San Juan city." **No such archive is located by any source; the place is fabricated.** Its coordinate is in fact Plaza 25 de Mayo, where Baudilio Bustos Correa is documented to have lived; the journey file re-labels the stop accordingly, at -31.5371 / -68.5250.

**Gap.** Nothing else is known of the child: whether he reached Córdoba, whether he had descendants, whether he ever spoke of his mother. No reachable source addresses any of it.

## 4. The grave becomes a shrine

**The water offering.** Travellers begin leaving water at the grave. en.wikipedia: *"A common votive offering is bottles of water 'to calm her eternal thirst'"* **[A]**. Argentina.gob.ar: *"los devotos le llevan botellas con agua como símbolo"* **[A]**. es.wikipedia gives the order of adoption: *"Los arrieros primero, y posteriormente los camioneros"*, muleteers first, truckers afterwards **[A]**, with **no date attached to either stage**.

> **Two corrections.**
> 1. **Piled stones.** The pool asserted stone-piling at the grave and along the roads. **The practice appears in neither Wikipedia article**; both describe bottles of water only. It is dropped from the journey file rather than sourced to nothing. It may well be real, but nothing reachable attests it: **unsourced claim [gap]**.
> 2. **"Plastic bottles from 1930."** The pool anchored trucker offerings of *plastic* bottles to 1930. Commercial PET beverage bottles date from the 1970s; the anchor is anachronistic. es.wikipedia gives no date at all and says only *botellas de agua*. The journey file drops the 1930 anchor and the word "plastic."

**Miracles claimed.** en.wikipedia: *"Every year since its origins in 1840, miracles are said to have occurred at the shrine"* **[A]**. This dates the shrine's origin to 1840, at odds with es.wikipedia's "sometime in the 1840s" for the death.

**First chapel.** Built over the grave by an **arriero named Zeballos**, in adobe **[A: es.wikipedia]**. **No date is given by any source [gap];** the journey file uses 1890 as an explicit placeholder.

**Location.** The sanctuary is **63 km east of the city of San Juan [A: es.wikipedia]**, in Departamento Caucete, at **-31.7345386 / -67.9825390** (Nominatim: Santuario de la Difunta Correa, Ruta Nacional 141, Vallecito, Caucete, San Juan) **[A]**.

> **Coordinate correction, twice over.** The pool's afterlife lens gave -31.7167 / -68.15 in three separate entries: ~29 km WNW of the real site and only ~41 km from San Juan city, which **contradicts the same entry's own "63 km" figure**. Its geography lens had it right; all Vallecito stops use -31.7345 / -67.9825. The pool also called Vallecito a shrine "on the outskirts" of Caucete town. It is ~30 km east of that town, in Caucete *department*.

## 5. Vallecito becomes a town

- **From the 1940s** the shrine grows *"from a hilltop cross into a small town with a network of votive chapels... full of offerings"* **[A: en.wikipedia, quoted verbatim]**.
- **Seventeen votive chapels as of 2005 [A: en.wikipedia]**. No later count found.
- **A life-size recumbent statue**, on her back, face to the sky, child at her breast **[A: both encyclopedias]**. **They disagree on its location:** es.wikipedia puts it in the chapel said to hold her remains, en.wikipedia in the hilltop chapel. **Not resolved**; stated in `date_confidence`.
- **Attendance: ~1,000,000 pilgrims per year [A: es.wikipedia, tied explicitly to 2005: "el promedio (año 2005)... es de 1.000.000 de personas por año"]**. Argentina.gob.ar, undated: *"convoca a más de un millón de peregrinos cada año"* **[A]**.

> **Date correction.** The pool dated the million-a-year figure to 2015. es.wikipedia dates it to **2005**. Re-dated.

- **Peak crowds: 200,000 during Holy Week [A: en.wikipedia]**, up to **300,000 [A: es.wikipedia]**, and around the **Day of Souls, 2 November [A]**. Neither figure is tied to a measured year; both are reported as a recurring pattern.
- **Cabalgata de la Fe**, an annual mounted pilgrimage in April-May **[A: es.wikipedia]**. No founding year given **[gap]**.

## 6. The spread: how far the roads carry her

The devotion is concentrated in central and northern Argentina and has spread to **Uruguay and Chile**, and southward into **Chubut and Santa Cruz via migrant families following petroleum work [A: es.wikipedia]**.

Beyond that, the diffusion is documented in this pass only by **map data**: shrines, streets, neighbourhoods, and bus stops bearing her name, via **nominatim.openstreetmap.org [A as locations; undated]**. Twenty-one such points were found, from Tierra del Fuego to Misiones and from the Atacama to the Maule. Each is entered with the hedge that **the coordinate is attested while the date is not**; those years are placeholders and should not be quoted.

> **Distance correction.** The pool put the Río Grande shrine "some 3,000 kilometers from the algarrobo tree." Great-circle from Vallecito to Río Grande is **~2,455 km**; the figure works only as road distance (~3,200 km). The journey file states both and labels which is which.

## 7. The Church, the cause, and the 2026 investigation

**Status.** She is **not canonized and never has been [A: both encyclopedias]**, and the Archdiocese of San Juan has historically regarded the cult with suspicion **[A: en.wikipedia]**. La Capital puts it bluntly: *"la mítica santa popular -que en realidad no es santa por no haber sido aceptada por la iglesia"* **[A]**.

**The beatification petition. DATE CONTRADICTORY, NOT RESOLVED.** es.wikipedia states that *en enero de 2020*, during **World Youth Day in Panama**, members of the Fundación Vallecito handed Pope Francis a letter asking that a beatification process be opened **[A as a claim]**. The claim is impossible as stated: **WYD Panama was held 22-27 January 2019**, and no Panama edition took place in 2020. The news item cited is dated 2 January 2020 with a forward-looking headline; it now returns 404 and **no Wayback copy could be retrieved [inaccessible]**. Either the year is 2019 or the occasion is not WYD. The journey file dates the stop 2020-01-01 and carries the contradiction in `date_confidence`. **No cause has been opened [A].**

**May 2026: the investigation.** The **Archdiocese of San Juan** and the **Instituto de Historia Regional y Argentina "Héctor Domingo Arias," Facultad de Filosofía, UNSJ**, launch a joint investigation into whether Deolinda Correa existed **[A]**; **Los Andes** adds that it runs **three years [A]**. Coordinator: **presbítero José Juan García [A]**.

> **Correction.** The pool flagged this as "could not be cross-verified against a second independent source." It **did**: **Diario de Cuyo, Estela Maris Ruiz, 19 May 2026** and **Los Andes, 21 May 2026**, both fetched and read directly. The pool also named the partner loosely as "the National University of San Juan"; it is the institute above. Caveat removed, partner corrected.

García's three statements are the most valuable living testimony here, coming from the institution with the most to lose either way:

- On the limit of the archive: *"En el peor de los casos, que no tuviéramos finalmente documentos en mano que prueben fehacientemente su existencia, tampoco estaríamos habilitados para decir que Deolinda Correa no existió, simplemente estaremos habilitados para decir 'no encontramos'"* **[A]**.
- On the devotion: *"Es una expresión religiosa muy fuerte, arraigada, profunda y siempre respetable"* **[A]**.
- On what the story transmits: *"Es una tradición oral que se ha transmitido muy fuerte y conlleva muchos valores morales también. Estaremos hablando de la fidelidad en el matrimonio al marido de parte de Deolinda, del cuidado del hijo, de la comunión en familia"* **[A]**.

## 8. Interlocks, and the ones deliberately not taken

The pool proposed several links to other travellers. Most are **shared geography or structural parallel, not relation**, and do not earn a name in the campa prose. Recorded here instead:

- **Gauchito Gil [R: parallel, not relation].** The corpus's other Argentine *santo popular*: unrecognized by Rome, no cause opened, no documented life, roadside shrines, diffusion along truck and migrant-labour routes, an ex-voto economy matching the votive economy at Vallecito. A precise structural parallel and **not a relation**: the two never met and neither cult cites the other. Not named in prose.
- **Eva Perón [R: parallel, not relation].** The other mass Argentine folk canonization outside the Church. Same reasoning; not named.
- **Domingo Faustino Sarmiento [A: relation, named in prose].** The one link that is not co-location: the **1865 advertisement that is the only documentary trace of her family was printed in *El Zonda*, the San Juan paper Sarmiento founded in 1839 and revived in 1862.** That is a documentary connection to the object at the centre of the historical question. Named once, in the Plaza 25 de Mayo campa. The pool also offered his San Juan childhood, his 1840 beating by the mazorca, *Facundo*, and the killing of El Chacho Peñaloza; all four are **shared time and place only**, and were declined.
- **Juan Manuel de Rosas [R: context, not relation].** The unitario/federal war that took Bustos is the war of the Rosas era. Structure, not acquaintance. Not named.
- **Pope Francis [A as an event, no corpus file].** The petition was put into his hands, which is a direct relation. He has no journey file, so no slug is claimed; he is named in the campa as the recipient, a fact of the event rather than an interlock.
- **Inherited pins [A].** The Kaaba (21.4225 / 39.8262), byte-identical from `gauchito_gil.journey.json`, carried as the pilgrimage-shrine comparator against which a million-a-year unofficial shrine is measured; and the canonical Buenos Aires pin (-34.6037 / -58.3816) for the single "devotion reaches the capital" stop. Named shrines keep their own mapped coordinates.

## 9. What could not be reached

- **Chertudi and Newbery, *La Difunta Correa* (1978)**, the standard folkloric monograph and es.wikipedia's underlying authority. **Not consulted directly**; everything attributed to "the fuller Spanish account" ultimately rests on it, unverified.
- **The 1865 *El Zonda* advertisement itself**, known only through Wikipedia's citation. No facsimile, transcription, or issue number located. **The single most important document in the file has not been seen by anyone in this research chain.**
- **The 2 January 2020 news item on the beatification petition**, HTTP 404. no Wayback copy retrieved. This is the reason the 2019/2020 contradiction stands unresolved.
- **Parish and notarial records of Angaco and Caucete**, not searchable remotely. These are the records the 2026 UNSJ/Arzobispado project has gone into; its findings do not exist yet.
- **Foundation dates for any of the twenty-one mapped shrines**. OpenStreetMap gives location, not history. Every roadside stop in this file is undated for that reason.
- **Any period document (1830s-1860s) naming Deolinda Correa or Clemente Bustos.** None exists in any reachable source. That is not a research failure; it is the subject of the file.
