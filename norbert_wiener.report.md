# Norbert Wiener (1894-1964): research report

*2026-08-28. Slug `norbert_wiener`. Written from the verified research pool; every correction the verify lens issued is carried into the journey file and recorded here.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim not independently corroborated. Per-claim citations live in each stop's `sources` and the full form of every hedge in its `date_confidence`; this report carries what the file cannot: corrections, contradictions left standing, gaps, and unreachable sources named with the reason.

The spine is Irving Ezra Segal's NAS *Biographical Memoir* (1992), PDF from nasonline.org: a first-person memoir by a mathematician who knew Wiener, strong on mathematics and personal detail, weak on dates, with four hard date or venue errors caught this pass. MacTutor (St Andrews), fetched directly, is the second spine. Where they disagree, both readings stand.

## 1. Origins (1894-1909)

**[A]** Birth at Columbia, Missouri, 26 Nov 1894; Leo Wiener's 1880 emigration; the infant deaths of 1900 and 1911 and the surviving siblings Constance (1898, later m. the MIT mathematician Philip Franklin), Bertha (1902), Frederick (1906); the Cambridge school year of 1901 and the withdrawal for home instruction; Ayer High School, graduated 1906 at eleven; Tufts 1906-09, A.B. mathematics *cum laude* at fourteen, no Phi Beta Kappa. *(Segal 390-393; MacTutor; Wikipedia.)*

- **CORRECTED.** Segal puts the utopian colony in "Central America"; Wikipedia names **British Honduras (Belize)**. The file says Belize.
- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** Segal and MacTutor put Leo Wiener on the **University of Missouri** faculty at Norbert's birth, losing it within a year; Wikipedia's Leo Wiener article **never mentions Missouri** (Kansas from ~1892, Harvard **1896-1930**). The Missouri post rests on Segal and MacTutor alone, and the Cambridge footing dates from 1896. The stop is dated 1896.
- **CORRECTED.** The pool made **Leon Lichtenstein** first cousin to Norbert's *paternal grandmother*. Wikipedia: "His cousin, Leo Wiener, was the father of MIT mathematician Norbert Wiener." Cousin to the **father**, the only chronologically possible reading (b. 1878 against Leo's 1862). Too small for a stop; in the birth stop's `sources`.
- **[R]** Family tradition, from Wiener's own autobiography, claimed descent from **Moses Maimonides**. Named as tradition in the birth campa; no demonstration exists anywhere reachable.
- **Gap.** No address for the Columbia boarding house or the Cambridge home; both pins approximate.

## 2. Zoology to a doctorate at eighteen (1909-1913)

**[A]** Harvard zoology, defeated inside a year for want of manual dexterity; Cornell's Sage School 1910-11 under Thilly, Hammond, Albee, and the "black year" of 1911 (phrase via MacTutor, from Wiener); Royce's health failing, the thesis passing to **Karl Schmidt of Tufts**, and the Harvard Ph.D. of 1913 on the algebra of relatives. *(Segal 392-393; MacTutor.)* **[R]** The 1912 M.A. under Huntington, Palmer, Royce, Santayana is Segal-only.

## 3. The traveling fellowship (1913-1915)

**[A]** Cambridge, England, for logic with **Russell**, but **Hardy** the decisive influence and the lifelong model; **Göttingen** for Hilbert, Landau, Husserl, cut short by the war; a second year at **Columbia** with **Dewey**. *(Segal 393-395; MacTutor.)* The Bowdoin Prize the same year is crawl-only, **[R]**.

- **CORRECTED, and promoted [R] to [A].** The **ordered pair** was wrong in year and document: not the 1913 dissertation but "**A Simplification of the Logic of Relations**," **1914**, (a,b) := {{{a},null},{{b}}}, superseded by Kuratowski's {{a},{a,b}} of 1921. *(Wikipedia, "Ordered pair," citing van Heijenoort 1967.)* Read to the Cambridge Philosophical Society, so the stop sits in England. **Gap:** the reading date 23 Feb 1914 was **not** confirmed, and van Heijenoort itself was not consulted.
- **Pin note.** Göttingen is **51.5413, 9.9158**, inherited byte-identical from `john_von_neumann.journey.json`, which declares it canonical; it disagrees with the geography lens's 51.5347/9.9357 and the corpus's commoner 51.534/9.935. Recorded in both Göttingen stops, **not resolved**.

## 4. Odd jobs and the war (1915-1919)

**[A]** Harvard 1915-16, Plattsburg 1916, Cambridge ROTC 1917, Orono 1916-17, GE turbines at Lynn 1917, Encyclopedia Americana at Albany 1917-18; the Jan 1918 letter to his father, quoted verbatim from Segal 395-396; **Veblen**'s ballistic-table unit at **Aberdeen Proving Ground**, civilian then enlisted private, discharged Feb 1919, with Princeton hoped for and never offered. *(Segal 395-396; MacTutor.)*

- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED: the Boston Herald firing.** The crawl has him refusing to write up conditions in the Lawrence mills as better than they were; Wikipedia's wikitext has him fired "for his reluctance to write favorable articles about a politician the newspaper's owners sought to promote." Nothing reachable adjudicates and nothing supports the Lawrence version. The stop is **[R]** and names both.
- **Cut for stop budget:** Plattsburg, Cambridge ROTC, and **Albany** (a real loss; the Encyclopedia Americana desk is named inside the Lynn campa).

## 5. Function space (1919-1933)

**[A]** MIT 1919 on **W. F. Osgood**'s recommendation, held to his death; the Aug 1920 Paris letter to his sister Constance on **Frechet** and "integration in function space" (verbatim, Segal 397) and the **Strasbourg ICM**; Wiener measure and the rigorous theory of Brownian motion, the function-space problem from I. A. Barnett and the Brownian motion from Russell, with a general solution of the **Dirichlet problem** and the **Wiener criterion** in the same years and Wiener's retrospective on Einstein, Smoluchowski and Perrin carried verbatim from Segal 411; marriage to **Margaret Engemann** 1926; *Generalized Harmonic Analysis* 1930, *Tauberian Theorems* 1932, a share of the **Bocher Prize** 1933. *(Segal 396-400, 411; MacTutor; MIT News; Wikipedia.)* **[R]** Daughters Barbara 1928 and Margaret 1929, birthplace inferred.

- **Background, not a pool claim:** the exclusion of the defeated powers from the 1920 Strasbourg congress comes from the congress's own history, flagged as such in `date_confidence`.
- **[A] Guggenheim 1926**, datable precisely from the Foundation's own record (gf.org): awarded 1926 in Mathematics, "Appointed for researches on Bohr's almost periodic functions, on haphazard motion, on periodogram analysis, and other topics," ten months from 1 April 1926.
- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED, and a stop deliberately dropped.** Segal splits that year between **Göttingen** (via **Max Born**) and **Copenhagen** with **Harald Bohr**; MacTutor's account runs **Cambridge (Hardy) then Göttingen** with no "Copenhagen" in it. The pool also caught "Robert Schmidt in Copenhagen" inside that item: Schmidt, whose Tauberian work Wiener synthesized, was German. **No Copenhagen stop is written**, which may omit a real leg.

## 6. Hardy, Hopf, Paley, Vienna (1931-1934)

**[A]** The Cambridge year near **Hardy** and *The Fourier Integral and Certain of Its Applications* (1933), with Wiener's judgement on Hardy and Littlewood carried verbatim (Segal 401); **Eberhard Hopf** brought to MIT and the **Wiener-Hopf equation**, which happened at MIT and not Cambridge, as the campa says, because MacTutor pairs the two; **R. E. A. C. Paley**, his death, and the completion of *Fourier Transforms in the Complex Domain* (1934), the division of labour quoted verbatim (Segal 400); the continental circuit of **Blaschke**, **Karl Menger**, **Artin**, **Godel**, **Hahn**. *(Segal 400-401; MacTutor.)*

- **Gap.** The **manner** of Paley's death is not specified in anything reached. The campa says "killed in an accident" and stops. The widely repeated skiing-avalanche account was **not** in the pool and is not written.
- **Naming caution observed.** The Vienna figure is **Karl** Menger the mathematician (1902-1985), not **Carl** Menger the economist (1840-1921), the separate atlas subject `carl_menger`. Named in full, distinction stated in `date_confidence`.

## 7. Peiping, the predictor, Mexico (1935-1946)

**[R]** Tsing Hua 1935-36, the Chinese fluency and the analytic publications, Segal 404 alone; the **Yuk-Wing Lee** tie and the later refugee placement for Lee, **Zygmund** and others through the China Aid Society and the Emergency Committee are crawl-only. **[A]** NDRC antiaircraft prediction with **Julian Bigelow**, noise as a stationary Gaussian process and pilot-plus-aircraft as one black box (Segal 405-406; crawl citing Galison for the black box and the Benzedrine); **Kolmogorov**'s independent prediction theory, unknown to Wiener until Segal mentioned it (Segal 405, first person); and the work with **Arturo Rosenblueth** at the **Instituto Nacional de Cardiologia** from 1945.

- **CORRECTED.** The pool paired the China year with **Shannon** "taking his MIT doctorate during this same broad period." Shannon was a Michigan undergraduate through 1936, MIT graduate work from 1936, master's 1937, Ph.D. 1940. The file makes no Shannon claim at that date.
- **CORRECTED.** *Extrapolation, Interpolation, and Smoothing of Stationary Time Series* is **1949**, not 1950 (OpenLibrary: Technology Press of MIT / Wiley; the 1964 MIT Press reissue explains the later dates in circulation).
- **CORRECTED, coordinates.** The pool's 19.287/-99.1508 is the institute's **Tlalpan campus, inaugurated 17 Oct 1976**, twelve years after Wiener's death (es.wikipedia.org). From its 1944 founding it stood on **Avenida Cuauhtemoc, Colonia Doctores**: the stop uses **19.4118, -99.1530**, 14 km away.

## 8. Cybernetics and the refusals (1946-1954)

**[A]** The postwar MIT seminar (Bigelow, Y. W. Lee, Rosenblueth, **McCulloch**, **Rosenblith**, **Wiesner**, **Pitts**); the **Macy conferences** with McCulloch, Pitts, **Mead**, **Bateson**, **von Neumann**; the autumn 1946 refusal of Boeing, published as "**A Scientist Rebels**," *The Atlantic*, Jan 1947; *Cybernetics* 1948, with **Cannon** and **Gabor** independently near it, the MIT/Wiley edition appearing **simultaneously** with the **Hermann et Cie** French one, "Actualites Scientifiques et Industrielles" no. 1053; and the 13 Aug 1949 letter to **Walter Reuther**. *(Segal 407-409; MIT News; archive.cybsoc.org; libcom.org.)*

- **[R]** on the Macy meeting number (crawl citing asc-cybernetics.org, not refetched). **Gap:** the Manhattan venue is unconfirmed and the pin is midtown.
- **No quotation from "A Scientist Rebels" is carried**, because no verbatim text of the letter survived into the pool; the campa paraphrases and says why.
- **[A]** Segal frames **Shannon**'s information theory as the clearest concrete exemplification of cybernetic philosophy (407), which is the basis for naming `claude_shannon`. **Stated honestly:** the relation is one of ideas, framed by Wiener's memoirist; neither the pool nor `claude_shannon.journey.json` records a meeting or correspondence between the two men.
- **CORRECTED.** The pool's Reuther excerpt dropped an opening clause. Full sentence: "**In the hands of the present industrial set-up,** the unemployment produced by such plants can only be disastrous." Wiener conditions the disaster on the ownership arrangement, not on automation as such, which is the whole point of writing to a union. The file quotes the full sentence.
- **CORRECTED, edition.** Two famous lines are routinely cited to *The Human Use of Human Beings* (1950) and are not in it: the full OCR of the 1950 Houghton Mifflin text (archive.org item `the-human-use-of-human-beings`) returns zero hits for "whirlpool," "abides," "perpetuate themselves," "ever-flowing," "Progress imposes," or "new restrictions," and has no chapter "Organization as the Message." Both belong to the **1954 revised second edition** (Doubleday Anchor; Da Capo 1988): the whirlpool passage ch. V, p. 96 (page cite via Bynum, arXiv:1802.02029v2), the progress line ch. II, pp. 46-47. The wording also **drops an article**: "not **stuff** that abides." The file gives 1954 its own stop with the corrected text.
- **[R] Not used:** "To live effectively is to live with adequate information," and the "secrete thought as the liver does bile / Information is information, not matter or energy" pair. Wikiquote only.

## 9. The circuit of the world (1951-1960)

**[A]** The 1951 Fulbright at the **College de France**; **UCLA** summers with prediction lectures in 1953; three **TIFR** lectures and the **Bhabha** dinner at which he met **Nehru**, plus lectures at the **Institute of Science**, Mumbai, and **Bengal Engineering College**; about seven months at the **Indian Statistical Institute**, Baranagar, at **Mahalanobis**'s invitation, late 1955-1956, with **Kallianpur**, **Masani**, the Second Five Year Plan and a photographed mango planting; and the 1960 **Moscow** IFAC congress, the hall full to the aisles, *Voprosy Filosofii* seeking an interview after having helped denounce the field. A Russian *Cybernetics* appeared **1958** from **Sovetskoe Radio**, ed. **G. N. Povarov**, second edition 1968. *(MIT South Asia exhibit and Wikipedia, "Cybernetics in the Soviet Union," fetched; Russian Wikipedia, single source; the Fulbright and UCLA legs crawl-only.)*

- **No stop written, recorded as gaps:** **[R]** a **Madrid** leg on the 1951 circuit, venue never identified; **[R]** a **Japan** leg on the return from India, with no city or institution named in any source (the pool's Tokyo coordinates were an explicit placeholder). **Cut for stop budget:** the Institute of Science, named instead inside the Bombay campa, and Bengal Engineering College.
- **CORRECTED, date and city.** The pool placed the **41st Indian Science Congress** in "December 1953" at Kolkata coordinates. The 41st was **Hyderabad, 1954** (S. L. Hora presiding, theme "Give scientists a chance"); the 40th was Lucknow 1953, the 42nd Vadodara 1955, and these sessions convene in early January. The MIT exhibit confirms attendance but names no city, which is probably how the error entered.
- **CORRECTED, anachronistic venue.** The pool's TIFR coordinates pointed at the **Colaba campus**, "inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on 15 January 1962," eight years after the lectures; in 1953-54 TIFR was near **Apollo Bunder**, where the corpus's canonical Bombay pin **18.922, 72.8347** falls. The pool's 18.949/72.8258 for the Institute of Science is likewise 2.6 km off (OSM: 18.9263/72.8300, Kala Ghoda, Fort).

## 10. Last lectures and death (1958-1964)

**[A]** The **Terry Lectures** at Yale 1962, "Prolegomena to Theology," out of which, with **[R]** a Royaumont presentation the same year, came *God & Golem, Inc.*; the **National Medal of Science** (1963 award) from **Lyndon Johnson** at the White House in January 1964, the NSF citation fetched and carried verbatim; death in Stockholm of a heart attack on **18 March 1964**, aged sixty-nine, the collapse taking place, in Segal's phrase, "fittingly enough, in a speech laboratory," Margaret accompanying him; burial at **Vittum Hill Cemetery**, Sandwich, New Hampshire. *(Segal 408-409; NSF recipient page; Wikipedia.)*

- **[R], no stop written:** **Varenna** 1958 (Villa Monastero, lecturing toward *Nonlinear Problems in Random Theory*) and **Naples** in the last years, both venue-unconfirmed and cut for budget; and a **Norwegian** leg with no city or venue, so Norway is named only inside the Amsterdam campa as itinerary, which is all the sources support.
