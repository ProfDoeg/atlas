# José Limón (1908-1972): research report
*2026-08-15. Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys. Culiacán, Sinaloa, to Hunterdon County, New Jersey: the dancer who carried Doris Humphrey's fall-and-recovery into a technique that outlived them both.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · contradictions are flagged and left open, never silently adjudicated.

The spine of this file is the English Wikipedia article "José Limón," the only continuous biography reached in this pass, and a thin one: several of its dates are internally ambiguous, its infobox leaves the place of death blank, and Wikidata contradicts it on both death place and cause. Corrections caught during verification are carried in the body rather than in footnotes, because three of them change the map.

---

## 1. Culiacán and the crossing north (1908-1928)

- **Born 12 January 1908 in Culiacán, Sinaloa**, birth name José Arcadio Limón. **[A**: en.wikipedia infobox; Wikidata Q785199 P19, Britannica-derived**]**
- **Eldest of twelve children**; his father a bandmaster who conducted theater orchestras. **[A]**
- **The parents' names are NOT sourced.** Florencio Limón and Francisca Traslaviña appear nowhere in en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia, or Wikidata (P22 and P25 both empty); a search for "Traslaviña Limón Florencio" returns zero hits. They presumably derive from *An Unfinished Memoir*. **[R** at best; not stated in the journey file**]**
- **1915: the family emigrates, fleeing the Mexican Revolution, and settles in Los Angeles.** **[A]** No month, port of entry, or address is recorded. Limón is seven.
- **Graduates from Lincoln High School, Lincoln Heights.** **[R]** School attested; year not pinned in anything reached, placed by arithmetic on the birth year alone.
- **c. 1926: enrolls at UCLA as an art major, intending to be a painter.** **[R]** Attendance and major are in en.wikipedia; the 1926 start year is unsourced.
  - **Correction carried:** the campus is the old **Vermont Avenue** campus, now Los Angeles City College, at **34.0870, -118.2929** (en.wikipedia, *Los Angeles City College*: the site "was the original campus of the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) in 1919," relocated to Westwood in 1929). An earlier pin at 34.0742, -118.2965 sits 1.5 km south, near Vermont and Beverly.

**Gap.** Nothing is recorded about what he painted, who taught him, or why he left without a degree. The turn from painting to dance is documented only by its result.

## 2. New York, and the end of painting (1928-1933)

- **1928: moves to New York to study painting at the New York School of Design.** **[A]** The address 2 West 13th Street is **[R]**, commonly cited, unconfirmed in anything reached.
- **1929: sees Harald Kreutzberg and Yvonne Georgi dance and abandons painting.** **[A]** Venue unrecorded.
  - **Attribution contradiction, flagged.** The famous passage attached to this evening ("What I saw simply and irrevocably changed my life...") is confirmed **verbatim** on the Pacific Northwest Ballet repertory page. But **PNB names neither Kreutzberg nor Georgi**: it frames the passage as his reaction to *the first dance program he saw after arriving in New York in 1928*. Two separate attestations, conventionally welded; kept adjacent here, not welded.
- **1929: enrolls at the Humphrey-Weidman School under Doris Humphrey and Charles Weidman.** **[A]** Studio address **[R]**, placed in Chelsea.
- **1930: first Broadway appearance; first choreography, "Etude in D Minor," a duet with Letitia Ide.** **[A]** Debut theater unrecorded.
- **c. 1930: forms "The Little Group."** **[A** for the fact, **R** for the year**]**
  - **Correction carried, twice.** (a) en.wikipedia reads "Limón recruited Ide and schoolmates Eleanor King and Ernestine Stodelle": **Letitia Ide belongs in the list** and had been dropped. (b) The source places the recruitment in the same 1930 passage as *Etude in D Minor* and **never states 1931**.
- **1932-1933: two more Broadway appearances, in *Americana* and in Irving Berlin's *As Thousands Cheer*, the latter choreographed by Weidman.** **[A]**
  - **Correction carried:** one 1932 date for both is wrong. *Americana* opened October 1932; *As Thousands Cheer* opened **30 September 1933 at the Music Box Theatre**. Two years, two houses.

## 3. Bennington, Mills, and the war (1937-1946)

- **1937: named a Bennington Fellow.** **[A]**
  - **Correction carried:** the summer school was **founded in 1934 by Martha Hill** and ran to 1942; Limón's fellowship is **1937**, not a span from 1934. Graham, Humphrey, Weidman and **Hanya Holm taught there in their own right**.
  - **Canonical pin inherited:** Bennington College at **42.9247, -73.2367**, byte-identical to `martha_graham.journey.json`. An earlier 42.8967, -73.2179 sits in Bennington village, 3.5 km off campus.
- **1939: *Danzas Mexicanas* premieres at the Bennington Festival, held that year at Mills College, Oakland.** **[A]** His first major independent choreography.
- **1940: dances in Balanchine's *Keep Off the Grass*.** **[A]**
  - **Correction carried, and it re-orders the life.** The show opened **23 May 1940** at the Broadhurst and ran 44 performances (en.wikipedia, *Keep Off the Grass*). The Limón article's ambiguous "After five years, however, Limón would return to Broadway...," following the 1939 Mills paragraph, produced a spurious 1944 reading in earlier passes. 1940 **precedes** *Rosalinda*.
- **1941: leaves Humphrey-Weidman; works briefly with May O'Donnell**, then on the West Coast. **[A]**
- **3 October 1941: marries Pauline Lawrence.** **[A** for the date: en.wikipedia, citing Limón 1998, p. 108**]**
  - **Correction carried:** the wedding was in **San Francisco**, not New York (en.wikipedia, *Pauline Lawrence*: "Marriage: October 3, 1941 in San Francisco to José Limón"). Pin 37.7749, -122.4194.
- **1943: final Broadway appearance, in Balanchine's *Rosalinda*.** **[A]**
- **April 1943: drafted into the U.S. Army**, in part with Special Services. **[A** for draft and year; **R** for the branch; **a flat gap for the postings**: no camp, unit, or theater of service was found. The journey file pins Washington, D.C. as an admitted placeholder.**]**
- **1946: attains American citizenship.** **[A]**

## 4. The company (1946-1958)

- **1946: forms the José Limón Dance Company**, with Humphrey as artistic director and himself as principal dancer and choreographer. **[A]** The star subordinates his authority to his teacher's for twelve years.
- **1946: formal debut at Bennington College.** **[A]** Program unrecorded.
- **1947: New York debut at the Belasco Theatre**, dancing Humphrey's *Day on Earth*, made for him after arthritis ended her performing; the New York Times calls him the finest male dancer of his time. **[A]**
- **1948: first appearance at the Connecticut College American Dance Festival, New London.** **[A]**
- **17 August 1949: *The Moor's Pavane* premieres** there, set to Purcell, drawn from *Othello*; it takes a Dance Magazine Award and never leaves the repertory. **[A]**
- **1950: the company tours Paris, the first American modern dance company to perform in Europe**, sharing the engagement with Ruth Page's company. **[A** for fact and year; **the theater is a gap.** An earlier pass placed it speculatively at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées; unconfirmed, so the canonical Paris pin is used instead.**]**
- **1951: invited by Mexico City's Instituto Nacional de Bellas Artes; makes six new works.** **[A]**
- **1951: joins the Juilliard dance faculty**, then at 120 Claremont Avenue until the 1969 Lincoln Center move. **[A]**
  - **Canonical pin inherited:** **40.8122, -73.9614**, byte-identical to `jennifer_muller.journey.json`.
- **1956: choreographs *The Emperor Jones*, after O'Neill.** **[A]** Premiere venue unrecorded. His 1958 defense of the blackface used in it was cross-checked on two independent fetches of the Wikipedia article, identical wording both times, with a citation marker.
- **1957: second Dance Magazine Award.** **[A]**
- **29 December 1958: Doris Humphrey dies; Limón becomes sole artistic director.** **[A]** *Day on Earth*, *Night Spell*, *Ruins and Visions* and *Lament for Ignacio Sánchez Mejías* stay in repertory with Limón as steward.
- **1958 onward: works made in close partnership with the dancer Pauline Koner.** **[A]**

## 5. Institutions and the last stage (1962-1972)

- **1962: the company opens the New York Shakespeare Festival's public season in Central Park.** **[A]**
  - **Correction carried:** the **Delacorte opened 18 June 1962**, the same year, with *The Merchant of Venice*; the appearance falls in its **inaugural season**, not four years after its building. en.wikipedia also says the company "returned" to Central Park, implying undocumented earlier Festival appearances. Pin 40.7801, -73.96877.
- **1963: Jennifer Muller enters the company at eighteen**, principal through 1971. **[A**: `jennifer_muller.journey.json`, citing a 1981 New York Times profile and the company's own posting**]** Contradiction inherited: her biography says "nine years"; 1963-1971 is eight. Not adjudicated.
- **1964: Capezio Dance Award; appointed artistic director of the American Dance Theater at Lincoln Center.** **[A]** How long that body lasted is a gap.
- **1965: an NET special, *The Dance Theater of José Limón*, airs.** **[A]** Broadcast date not recovered.
- **1967: performs at Washington National Cathedral; the company receives an NEA grant.** **[A]**
- **1967: performs at the White House for Lyndon Johnson and King Hassan II of Morocco.** **[A]** Program unrecorded.
- **1968: the José Limón Foundation is incorporated.** **[A]** (The Limón Institute, 1985, is a separate later body. **[A]**)
- **1969: final onstage performances, *The Traitor* and *The Moor's Pavane*, Brooklyn Academy of Music.** **[A]**
- **1970: diagnosed with prostate cancer.** **[A]**
- **16 July 1971: Pauline Lawrence Limón dies** at the converted dairy farm near Stockton, New Jersey, bought in 1948. **[A**: en.wikipedia, *Pauline Lawrence***]**
- **1971: founds the José Limón Philadelphia Dance Theater**, "little-known... originally intended to become a second company." **[A]**
  - **Two corrections carried.** (a) Lawrence died the **same year** as the founding, not the year before; only the 1970 diagnosis precedes it by a year. (b) The claim that "the Philadelphia venture outlives him by only a matter of years" is **unsourced speculation** and is cut. Its fate is a gap.
- **2 December 1972: dies, aged 64.** **[A** for date and age: en.wikipedia lead, "January 12, 1908 – December 2, 1972"; "In December 1972, at the age of 64, he died of cancer"**]**

### The place of death: an open contradiction

| Source | Place | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| en.wikipedia, *José Limón* | infobox **blank** | cancer, unspecified in lead |
| Wikidata Q785199 | **New York City** | kidney disease |
| es.wikipedia | **Flemington, New Jersey** | prostate cancer |

Compounding it: the couple's home from 1948 was the farm **near Stockton** (40.4059, -74.9706), where Lawrence died in 1971. **Flemington** (40.5086, -74.8599) is a different Hunterdon County town, 13 km northeast, so "died at his home in Flemington" conflates two places. Wikidata's New York City / kidney disease pair is Freebase- and IMDb-derived and the weakest of the three.

**What this file states:** died 2 December 1972 in Hunterdon County, New Jersey; Flemington per es.wikipedia; the farm near Stockton; no place at all in en.wikipedia. The stop is pinned at Flemington with the conflict written into its date_confidence.

## 6. Afterlife (1973-present)

- **1973: the José Limón Collection goes to the New York Public Library Dance Collection**, Lincoln Center; a retrospective of the material follows there in **1996**. **[A]**
- **1988: the José Limón national dance award is established.** **[A]**
  - **Correction carried, and it puts the stop on the map.** The award is **Mexican**: Wikidata Q33327007, "Premio Nacional de Danza Contemporánea José Limón," country Mexico, inception 1988. en.wikipedia's own citation for the date is the **Instituto Sinaloense de Cultura in Culiacán** announcing the 28th edition to Cecilia Appleton (2015). Administered from his birth state, so pinned at Culiacán rather than left placeless and implicitly American.
- **1997: inducted into the National Museum of Dance's Mr. and Mrs. Cornelius Vanderbilt Whitney Hall of Fame.** **[A]**
  - **Correction carried:** the museum is in Saratoga Spa State Park at **43.0661, -73.7903**; an earlier 43.0831, -73.7846 is downtown Saratoga Springs, 2 km north.
- **1998: *José Limón: An Unfinished Memoir*, Wesleyan University Press / UPNE, ed. Lynn Garafola.** **[A** for 1998**]**
  - **Edition-year contradiction, flagged.** en.wikipedia's prose says the memoir "appeared in edited form in 1999," while **its own citation for the marriage date is {{sfn|Limón|1998|p=108}}**. Wikidata and OpenLibrary give 1998; a Jacob's Pillow essay cites **2001**. **1998 is used.** Garafola's editorship is stated by secondary sources, not by Wikipedia.
- **2001: the documentary *José Limón: A Life Beyond Words*.** **[R]** Release date not recovered. The Bill T. Jones fragment quoted from it ("man of color who was grappling with the European hierarchy of beauty and importance") is a **single-fetch, uncross-checked clip** and may be truncated; not used as a stop quote.
- **2003: named one of America's "Irreplaceable Dance Treasures" by the Dance Heritage Coalition.** **[A]**
- **2008: the Foundation receives the National Medal of Arts.** **[A]**
- **2012: featured on a U.S. postage stamp.** **[A** for the fact; series name a gap**]**
- **Present: the Foundation archive, 466 W. 152nd Street, Floor 2, New York**: images, audio, playbills, posters, choreographic notes, scores, being digitized. **[A**: limon.nyc/archives**]**
- **The company today:** **Dante Puleio**, sixth artistic director; **Logan Frances Kruger**, associate artistic director (joined 2009, rehearsal director 2017, appointed 2021). **[A**: limon.nyc**]** Carla Maxwell's tenure dates (roughly 1978-2016 by general dance history) were **not verified live** and are not stated as fact.
- **Boulevard José Limón, Culiacán.** en.wikipedia lists the street but **does not say it commemorates the dancer**. **[R]**, and left out of the journey file.
- **Honorary doctorates** from Wesleyan, North Carolina, Oberlin and Colby. **[A]** No years recorded.

## 7. Lineage and interlocks

The line the corpus keeps returning to runs **Delsarte to Stebbins to St. Denis to Humphrey to Limón to Muller**, and onward, per QUEUE.md, toward Buenos Aires and Emilia Massacesi.

- **[A]** Limón trained under **Doris Humphrey** and **Charles Weidman** from 1929 and built his technique on Humphrey's fall-and-recovery principle. `jennifer_muller.journey.json` states it downstream: Limón "dances Doris Humphrey's line and teaches a body that falls and recovers."
- **[A]** Humphrey and Weidman came out of **Denishawn**, the company of **Ruth St. Denis** and Ted Shawn, so St. Denis stands one teacher upstream of his teachers. Qualification carried: `saint_denis.journey.json` does **not** use the word "Humphrey"; that link comes from QUEUE.md line 537.
- **[R]** The **Delsarte and Stebbins** rungs are the queue's framing, cited by filename in `jennifer_muller.journey.json`, not a claim Limón is recorded making. Named once in the journey file, in that register.
- **[A]** **Isadora Duncan and Martha Graham are named by Limón himself**, the only two atlas travelers he is documented as invoking: "I view myself as a disciple and follower of Isadora Duncan, and the American impetus as exemplified by Doris Humphrey and Martha Graham" (Selma Jeanne Cohen, ed., *The Modern Dance: Seven Statements of Belief*, Wesleyan, 1966).
- **[A]** **Jennifer Muller** was his principal 1963-1971: direct, documented, and reciprocal.
- **Not interlocks.** Humphrey, Weidman, Pauline Lawrence, Pauline Koner and Louis Falco have no journey files; they are named in the prose because they belong to his life.
- **Not claimed.** Limón has no documented connection to Emilia Massacesi, who is queued only. The Buenos Aires end of the line is Muller's stop, on Muller's file.

## 8. The quotations, and what had to be cut

Three memoir and statement quotations are carried into stop quotes and two are repaired:

- **"Early in the year nineteen hundred and twenty-nine I was born at 9 East Fifty-Ninth Street, New York City."** (*An Unfinished Memoir*, p. 1, via racheloriol.wordpress.com, 23 March 2016.) He dates his own birth to the year he began to dance. **The trailing ellipsis carried in earlier passes is spurious**; the sentence is complete.
- **"The choreographic genius of Doris Humphrey was an affirmation of order and clarity."** (*Memoir*, p. 55.) **Also not truncated**; earlier ellipsis removed.
- **The 1965 written statement** ("I reach for demons, saints, martyrs, apostates, fools, and other impassioned visions"), University of Washington Department of Dance faculty page.

**One quotation is cut.** The Humphrey line "In the other arts, you can hide behind words and facial expression. But it is impossible to deceive with the dance" is **only half traceable**. The source reads: "In the other arts, she told us, 'you can hide behind words and facial expression.'" The second sentence is the reviewer's own paraphrase, not quoted matter. Only the first clause is usable, and only as Humphrey's words reported inside Limón's memoir.

**One citation is repaired.** Shelly C. Berg's sentence about Limón embedding his ethnicity in American modern dance **is** a marked verbatim quotation in the Jacob's Pillow essay, not that essay's paraphrase; the earlier hedge was wrong. Berg's text is in Patricia Seed, ed., *José Limón and La Malinche: The Dancer and the Dance* (Texas, 2008).

James Moreno's counter-reading is carried alongside it without adjudication: "even though the transient quality of Limón's identity could be seen as a move that expanded racialized boundaries and diversified mid-century U.S. modern dance, his work did not break down or synthesize the prevailing black/white racial boundary" (*The Oxford Handbook of Dance and Politics*, via Jacob's Pillow).
