# Manuel Antonio Noriega Moreno (c. 1934-2017): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, Colegio Invisible. Compiled 2026-08-10 from the verified research pool for slug `manuel_noriega`, checked against en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia, OpenStreetMap, the American Presidency Project, Time of 15 January 1990, and the atlas's own journey files.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or contested. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Where the pool's claim was found wrong, the correction is printed rather than the error.

---

## 1. Birth and childhood (c. 1934 to c. 1953)

**[A]** Born in a poor district of Panama City. His mother dies of tuberculosis while he is a child; a godmother raises him. (Wikipedia, "Manuel Noriega," citing Britannica.) **[A]** He attends the Instituto Nacional, Panama's most prestigious public secondary school and a traditional incubator of the political and military elite. (Kempe; Dinges.)

**[R] Contradiction, not resolved: the year.** Sources give 1934, 1936 and 1938. The May 2017 obituaries report his age at death as 83, which fits 1934. The atlas pins 1934 and calls the year unsettled in `date_confidence`.

**[R] Contradiction, not resolved: the place.** His memoir and most reference works say Panama City; press profiles of 1988-1990 (AP, Miami Herald) say La Chorrera, 30 km west. No primary document decides. The atlas gives both towns a pin.

**[A] Correction.** The pool placed the Instituto Nacional at 8.9848 / -79.5245 on Avenida Cuba. es.wikipedia gives 8.9576 / -79.5430, in Santa Ana at the foot of Cerro Ancón, fronting Avenida de los Mártires. The pool's pin was about 4 km off, in El Cangrejo.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable documents what he did between leaving the Instituto and arriving in Lima. The years c. 1953-1958 are blank in every source consulted and are left blank.

## 2. Recruitment and commission (1955-1962)

**[A]** As a teenager he receives his first payment from US intelligence, a reported $10.70; federal prosecutors later documented thirty-one years of payments. He is an informant before he is a cadet.

**[A]** He trains at the Escuela Militar de Chorrillos in Lima from 1958, studying engineering. Declassified CIA and DIA payment records released in the 1990s, and Dinges, place the formal recruitment in these years. Commissioned second lieutenant in the National Guard, 1962.

## 3. Torrijos, the coup, military intelligence (1966-1981)

**[A]** He takes counterintelligence training at the US Army's School of the Americas at Fort Gulick in the Canal Zone. **[A]** October 1968: he supports Torrijos in the National Guard coup against President Arnulfo Arias, and posted to Chiriquí suppresses resistance and secures the province; the reward is military intelligence (Kempe). **[A]** 1970: promoted lieutenant colonel and made chief of the G-2, compiling files on dissidents, businessmen and foreign officials while drawing a CIA retainer (Dinges; Kerry Committee, 1988). **[A]** 1971: the CIA puts him on the regular payroll, and the relationship survives multiple administrations.

**Contradiction, not resolved: the Fort Gulick year.** The pool's chronology lens says 1966, its geography lens 1967. Neither cites a document. The atlas pins 1967 and records the discrepancy.

**[A] Correction.** Fort Gulick was given as 9.3167 / -79.9167. en.wikipedia gives 9°19'N 79°52'W, that is 9.317 / -79.867. The pool's longitude was about 5.5 km off, toward the water on the Colón side.

**[R]** George H. W. Bush, as DCI in 1976, is said to have personally approved his continued asset status (Dinges; FOIA correspondence released in the 1990s). Carried as [R]: that material was not re-fetched here.

**[A]** Torrijos dies in a plane crash in the Coclé highlands, 31 July 1981. **[R]** That the crash was sabotage, and that Noriega engineered or exploited it, is contested tradition (Kempe; Panamanian press). The recorded cause remains weather and no investigation has proved otherwise.

**Contradiction: the marriage.** The pool dated the marriage to Felicidad Sieiro to 1960. en.wikipedia dates it to the late 1960s and no source gives 1960; the pool is wrong by about eight years. The atlas carries no marriage stop and names Felicidad only where a source supports it, in the letter of 3 January 1990.

## 4. De facto ruler: the isthmus for rent (1982-1987)

**[A]** 12 August 1983: he becomes de facto ruler as commander of the Panama Defense Forces, the National Guard's successor, after maneuvering past rivals. He never takes the presidency.

**[A]** He holds accounts at the Bank of Credit and Commerce International, Agha Hasan Abedi's bank, later shut by regulators across seventy countries. Amjad Awan, head of the Panama branch, handles the relationship with Syed A. Hussain, and investigators trace drug proceeds and covert payments through the same accounts. BCCI's London head office on Leadenhall Street runs the correspondent network until the 1991 collapse. (Wikipedia, BCCI; Kerry Committee BCCI report, 1992; US v. Noriega trial record.)

**[A]** The Colón Free Trade Zone's currency controls and container traffic make it a laundering point for cartel proceeds (US v. Noriega; DEA reporting in the Kerry Committee report). **[A]** May 1984: Panama's first presidential election in sixteen years, and Noriega manipulates the count to install Nicolás Ardito Barletta over the opposition's apparent winner. **[A]** Panamanian territory and Noriega's cooperation figure in the Contra resupply network run partly through Ilopango in El Salvador (Kerry Committee, 1988; Iran-Contra hearings, 1987).

**[R]** Associates of Pablo Escobar's Medellín cartel are reported to have met Panamanian intermediaries close to Noriega at Torrijos's farmstead at Coclesito, to arrange transshipment and refining protection. Source: DEA debriefings in the Kerry Committee report; denied in the memoir.

**[A] Correction.** Coclesito was pinned at 8.95 / -80.2 "in Coclé province." OpenStreetMap puts it at about 8.82 / -80.54, in the Omar Torrijos Herrera district of **Colón** province: about 40 km off, and the wrong province.

**[R]** The Darién Gap serves as a corridor for cocaine allegedly moving under Noriega's protection. The route is broadly attested; waypoints inside it are reconstruction.

**[A]** September 1985: Hugo Spadafora, a former Torrijos-government official who has publicly accused Noriega of protecting traffickers, is detained crossing from Costa Rica. His decapitated body is later found in a post office bag. **[A]** 26 June 1987: an estimated 100,000 march in Panama City against his rule.

**[A] Three corrections on Spadafora.** (1) en.wikipedia says "post office bag," not a US Postal Service bag; the "U.S." is dropped for want of a primary source. (2) The body was found on the **Costa Rican** side of the border, not inside Panama as the pool's place field implied. (3) The pool said "convicted in absentia years later"; the Wikipedia text as fetched places that conviction under Endara's government, which began only on 20 December 1989. No year could be verified, so the atlas says "in absentia, after his fall" and invents no date.

**[A] Quotes.** An NSA-intercepted call during the arrest carries Luis Córdoba's "We have the rabid dog" and Noriega's reply "And what does one do with a dog that has rabies?", both per Koster and Sánchez Borbón cited in Wikipedia. Prosecutors read the reply as a tacit order.

## 5. The break with Washington (1988-1989)

**[A]** February 1988: federal grand juries in Miami and Tampa indict him on drug trafficking and racketeering, alleging payments to let Colombian cocaine, including Medellín shipments, cross Panama. **[A]** May 1989: the opposition wins decisively and Noriega voids the result; footage of vice-presidential candidate Guillermo Ford beaten by pro-Noriega paramilitaries circulates internationally. **[A]** 3 October 1989: Lieutenant Colonel Moisés Giroldi Vera leads a coup attempt with junior PDF officers, which is crushed, the captured rebels tortured and executed. **[A]** 16 December 1989: US Marine First Lieutenant Robert Paz is shot dead at a PDF roadblock; Bush cites the killing, and the harassment of a US naval officer and his wife, as justification for invasion.

**[A] Two corrections on May 1989.** (1) The date. en.wikipedia's 1989 Panamanian general election article gives the election as 7 May, annulment 10 May, and the beating of Endara and Ford as "the next day," 11 May, Endara struck by an iron club and taking eight stitches. The pool's 10 May was off by a day. (2) The place. Neither that article nor the Guillermo Ford article names Vía España; Ford's says only "after a rally." The atlas keeps the coordinates as a central-city approximation and flags the street as unverified.

**[A] Correction.** The pool's geography lens called Giroldi a Major; its own chronology lens had the rank right. He was a Lieutenant Colonel.

**[A]** In the autumn before the invasion a Vietnam Veterans Against the War delegation travels to Panama to observe the standoff and sit with Panamanians opposed to intervention. Pete Zastrow of the VVAW national office is documented in Panama at the end of November (*The Veteran*, Summer 1990), and a Panamanian eyewitness, Lamoin Werlein-Jaen, thanks VVAW for standing with them beforehand.

**[R] Correction, the atlas correcting itself.** The pool tagged Arthur Ben's membership of that delegation [A]. `arthur_ben.journey.json` states that his presence is family testimony, not press-verified, and that no published source names him on it. Tagging it [A] contradicts the file it cites; it is carried here as **[R]**. The pool's further claim that the delegation met Panamanian officials is unsupported by that file and is dropped.

**[A]** 15 December 1989: the PRD-dominated National Assembly declares a state of war between Panama and the United States and names Noriega "Maximum Leader" and head of government.

**[R] Correction: a quotation attributed to the wrong document.** The pool carried "Noriega declared his military dictatorship to be in a state of war with the United States" as a paraphrase of the Assembly's resolution. It is verbatim George H. W. Bush from the address of 20 December 1989, already in the pool as a Bush quotation. The Assembly declared Panama in a state of war arising from US aggression; Bush's formulation is his administration's characterization of that act, and presenting it as the Assembly's own language is the inversion that makes the casus belli look self-declared.

## 6. Operation Just Cause (20 December 1989 to 3 January 1990)

**[A]** The invasion opens at 12:46 a.m. on 20 December with over 27,000 troops and 300 aircraft, the plan renamed from "Blue Spoon" for its public-relations value. Navy SEALs assault Punta Paitilla Airport in the first minutes to disable Noriega's jet, taking heavy casualties (DoD after-action reports; Kempe). US forces bombard and burn the Comandancia in El Chorrillo, and the wooden tenement quarter around it catches fire, killing an unknown number of civilians and leaving thousands homeless (Physicians for Human Rights, 1991; NYT and Miami Herald, December 1989). Guillermo Endara is sworn in as president at Fort Clayton, a US base, before Noriega's fate is known. Noriega's residence is raided within hours; pornography, ritual paraphernalia and cash are found and publicized. He has already gone.

**[A] Correction.** The residence was pinned at 9 / -79.5, rounded to whole and half degrees and outside Altos del Golf, which sits near 8.99 / -79.51. The atlas uses neighbourhood-level coordinates and says so.

**[A]** He evades capture for days under a $1 million reward, then takes refuge in the Apostolic Nunciature of the Holy See on 24 December. US forces besiege the compound and play loud rock-and-roll music day and night. After ten days he surrenders on 3 January 1990 and is flown to the United States.

**[A] Correction.** The pool's afterlife lens tagged the music siege [R], "not independently re-verified." en.wikipedia's invasion article states it directly. Promoted to [A].

**Contradiction NOT resolved: where the Nunciature is.** The pool gave the same building three coordinates: 8.9958 / -79.5175 (geography lens), 8.9887 / -79.5057 (afterlife lens), and OpenStreetMap's present-day nunciature at 8.9937 / -79.5578 in Ancón, up to 1.3 km apart. Nothing reachable establishes where the 1989 building stood. The atlas uses **8.9937 / -79.5578** in both stops and states the uncertainty.

**[A] Correction: a misattributed quotation.** The pool gave "a broken man" to "Sgt. Scott Geist, U.S. paratrooper who received Noriega at the Nunciature gate." Time, 15 January 1990: "On the evening of Jan. 3, Noriega, described by Panama's Archbishop Marcos McGrath as 'a broken man,' emerged from his room in a crisp tan general's uniform with four stars." The speaker is **Archbishop Marcos McGrath**. No US sergeant or paratrooper appears in that article at all.

**[A] Correction: a non-quotation.** "Outwitted" was carried as a Laboa self-description. It is the Time reporter's narration: "Laboa, who had outwitted his adversary, said that would be fine." Removed as a quote. Laboa's genuine line from the same sentence, "I'm better at psychology," is kept.

**[A]** Just Cause formally concludes 31 January 1990. Pentagon figures: 23 US dead, 325 wounded; Panamanian losses officially about 314 soldiers and 202 civilians.

**Contradiction, not resolved: the Panamanian dead.** Independent and Panamanian estimates run up to about 3,000. The gap between official and independent counts is carried, not closed.

## 7. America's prisoner (1990-2010)

**[A]** The flight lands at Homestead Air Force Base on 4 January 1990. He is held at Miami's federal facility with prisoner-of-war status, furnished with television and exercise equipment, which draws public criticism (Miami Herald, 1990-1992). **[A]** April 1992: a Miami jury convicts him on eight of ten counts of drug trafficking, racketeering and money laundering after a seven-month trial; on 10 July 1992 he is sentenced to 40 years. **[A]** 2007: his reduced sentence reaches its scheduled completion date, but he is not released; the US holds him for extradition proceedings sought by France and Panama.

**[A] Correction: one facility counted twice, and two wrong years.** The pool listed "Metropolitan Correctional Center, Miami" (25.7325 / -80.2211) and "Federal Correctional Institution, Miami" (25.6178 / -80.3556) as two places. Per en.wikipedia they are the **same site**, called MCC-Miami in the late 1980s and renamed FCI-Miami in June 1995, at 25.6169 / -80.4119; both pool coordinates were wrong, one by about 20 km. The pool also gave his years there as "1992-2007"; Wikipedia places him there from roughly 1990 to 2010, since he stayed in US custody until the April 2010 extradition. The atlas uses one pin.

**[A] Correction: the direction of an extradition.** The pool said "France extradites and convicts Noriega." Backwards. The **United States extradited him to France** in April 2010; France, which had already convicted him in absentia in 1999, then convicted him of laundering Colombian drug proceeds through French banks and sentenced him to seven years.

**[A]** 1997: *America's Prisoner: The Memoirs of Manuel Noriega*, with Peter Eisner, Random House, ISBN 0679432272. The catalogue record was verified via Internet Archive (`americasprisoner00nori`) and Open Library (OL3283543W); **the body text was not accessible**, so nothing is quoted from it anywhere.

## 8. Paris, El Renacer, the end (2010-2017)

**[A]** He serves the French sentence at La Santé in central Paris; a Paris court confirms the conviction in July 2010 (AFP, Le Monde; French judicial records). In December 2011 France extradites him to Panama, where he is imprisoned at El Renacer near the canal at Gamboa, serving sentences handed down in absentia for the Spadafora murder and other crimes of his rule (Panamanian judicial records; Reuters). **[A]** July 2014: he sues Activision in Los Angeles over his depiction in *Call of Duty: Black Ops II* as a kidnapping target and "enemy of the state"; dismissed 28 October 2014 on First Amendment grounds.

**[A]** He dies 29 May 2017 at Hospital Santo Tomás, Panama City, aged 83, of cerebral haemorrhage following brain tumour surgery, still formally a prisoner of the Panamanian state. The body is cremated privately: no public funeral, no marked tomb, the ashes handed to his widow Felicidad Sieiro de Noriega in an urn (es.wikipedia).

**[A] Correction: the diagnosis date.** The pool said the tumour was "diagnosed in March 2017." es.wikipedia dates the **surgery** to 15 February 2017, and a diagnosis cannot postdate its own operation. The atlas pins the operation, not the diagnosis.

**[A] Correction: the hospital, given three ways.** The geography lens gave 8.9836 / -79.5312, the afterlife lens 8.9797 / -79.5321 for the same building. OpenStreetMap puts Hospital Santo Tomás at **8.9698 / -79.5324**, Calle 36, Calidonia; that value is used in both stops. The pool's claim of a hospitalization running "2017-01 to 2017-05-29," five months, is supported by nothing and is dropped.

**Gap.** No public grave exists to visit, and the later disposition of the ashes is undocumented in anything reached.

**[R]** He is popularly known as "Cara de Piña," Pineapple Face. Common in the Spanish-language press; not found in the Wikipedia text fetched. **[A]** Afterlife in fiction, per es.wikipedia: *Die Hard 2* (1990), whose General Ramón Esperanza is a thinly veiled caricature, and *Noriega: God's Favorite* (2000), with Bob Hoskins.
