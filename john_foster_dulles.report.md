# John Foster Dulles (1888-1959): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for `john_foster_dulles.journey.json`. Subject: United States Secretary of State, 21 January 1953 to 22 April 1959; head of Sullivan & Cromwell; brother of Allen Dulles, Director of Central Intelligence 1953-1961.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried only by a secondary paraphrase. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method.** Written against a pre-verified research pool already through an adversarial correction pass. Where that pass overturned a claim, the correction is recorded in the body rather than silently applied: several of the overturned claims are the ones in widest circulation about this man.

---

## 1. The Watertown manse, 1888-1911

- Born **25 February 1888, Washington, D.C.** **[A: Wikipedia, "John Foster Dulles"; history.state.gov]**. Grandson of **John Watson Foster**, Secretary of State 1892-1893; nephew by marriage of **Robert Lansing**, Secretary of State 1915-1920. Father **Allen Macy Dulles**, Presbyterian minister.
- Raised at **Watertown, New York**, taught at home; siblings include **Allen Welsh Dulles** (b. 1893) and **Eleanor Lansing Dulles** **[A]**. Boyhood summers at the Foster retreat near **Henderson Harbor** **[R: Pruessen (1982), not directly consulted]**; *not made a stop, being [R] and a duplicate of one on Allen's file.*
- **Second Hague Peace Conference, 15 June to 18 October 1907**, at nineteen, as secretary on his grandfather's staff **[A]**. **Correction:** John W. Foster attended **not on a United States delegation but as retained counsel to the Chinese delegation** under **Lu Zhengxiang** **[A: Wikipedia, "Hague Conventions of 1899 and 1907"]**. The usual framing of this as his first exposure to *American* diplomacy is wrong; it was an exposure to a former American Secretary of State working for a foreign government on a fee.
- **Princeton, 1908, Phi Beta Kappa**; **American Whig-Cliosophic Society** **[A]**. Allen enters the same college and society six years behind him **[A: allen_dulles.journey.json]**.
- **George Washington University Law School**, then **Sullivan & Cromwell, 1911** **[A]**; he stays with the firm forty-two years.
- **Married Janet Pomeroy Avery, 1912** **[A for the year; the 26 June date is secondary only, R]**, at Auburn, New York.

**Gap.** Janet Avery Dulles is nearly absent from the reachable record as anything but a spouse in a delegation photograph. Nothing found supports a fuller account, and none was invented for her.

## 2. The uncle's errands, 1917-1924

- **1917:** rejected for the Army over poor eyesight; sent by his uncle, then Secretary of State, to advise on **Costa Rica (Tinoco), Nicaragua (Chamorro), Panama**; commissioned a **major on the War Trade Board** **[A]**.
- **1919:** legal counsel to the American delegation at the **Paris Peace Conference** under Lansing, on **Bernard Baruch's** reparations work **[A]**. Both brothers are on the delegation at the **Hotel de Crillon**, their first joint assignment **[A: allen_dulles.journey.json]**.
  - **Correction applied.** The pool carried a separate **Versailles, Hall of Mirrors** stop. The coordinate is right for the palace but the stop is misplaced: his work was done in Paris at the Crillon, and the Hall of Mirrors was only the signing venue of 28 June 1919. The two are **merged** on the Crillon pin.
- **1924: the Dawes Plan** **[A]**. Sullivan & Cromwell places on the order of **a billion dollars** of foreign bonds through the decade, heavily German **[A for the aggregate, dated generally to the 1920s]**.

## 3. The German practice, 1926-1939

- **1926:** Allen resigns from the Foreign Service and joins the firm **[A]**.
- Repeated Berlin travel for the German practice **[A]**, including work bearing on **I.G. Farben** and an international nickel cartel **[A as to the firm]**. **Correction applied:** the pool dated this span "1920s-1939"; the Berlin office closed in **1935** and the file's span ends there.
- **1935: the firm leaves Germany. Correction, direction of agency reversed.** The pool had Dulles "forced by mounting Nazi-era concerns to end" the business. The record runs the other way: **Allen**, back from Germany and alarmed at the treatment of German Jews, led the push to close Berlin and Frankfurt **over John Foster's opposition**, Foster being the sole partner voting against **[A: Wikipedia, "Allen Dulles"; "Sullivan & Cromwell"]**. The firm then **backdated the closure announcement to 1934** **[A: allen_dulles.journey.json]**.

**Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** The same encyclopedia that records Foster's lone dissent states elsewhere that the firm under him "assisted Nazi Germany's arms buildup effort by incorporating I.G. Farben into an international nickel cartel." No primary account reconciling the two was located. Both are recorded on the stop; neither settles the other.

## 4. The churchman, 1940-1949

- **December 1940:** appointed to and chairs the Federal Council of Churches' **Commission on a Just and Durable Peace** **[A]**.
- **March 1943:** publishes **"Six Pillars of Peace,"** arguing for a federal framework above states **[A]**.
- **1945:** delegate at the **San Francisco Conference**, adviser to Senator **Arthur Vandenberg**, works on the **UN Charter preamble** **[A]**.
- **1944, 1948:** chief foreign-policy adviser to **Thomas E. Dewey** **[A]**.
- **7 July 1949:** appointed by Dewey to a vacant New York Senate seat; four months; loses the November special election to **Herbert Lehman** **[A]**.

**Noted because it shapes the file.** The distance between the 1943 churchman arguing that the moral unit of politics is not the nation and the 1955 Secretary calling neutrality immoral is the sharpest internal contradiction in this life. No source locates an explanation in Dulles's own words. The file states both and declines to bridge them.

## 5. Japan, 1950-1952

- **1950:** special adviser on the Japanese settlement and its principal architect **[A]**; publishes **War or Peace** (Macmillan), attacking containment and arguing for "liberation" **[A]**.
- **1 September 1951: ANZUS** signed at San Francisco **[A]**. **8 September 1951: Treaty of San Francisco** at the **War Memorial Opera House**, in force 28 April 1952 **[A]**; the **bilateral U.S.-Japan Security Treaty** the same day but at the **Presidio**, not the Opera House **[A: Wikipedia, "Treaty of San Francisco"]**.
  - **Correction applied.** The pool collapsed all three into one 8 September event at the Opera House. The file separates them: a Presidio stop dated 1 September carrying ANZUS and the bilateral treaty, an Opera House stop dated 8 September carrying the peace.
- **1955, on Taiwan:** "Japan merely renounced sovereignty over Taiwan" **[A as a quotation of a 1955 statement, via Wikipedia, "Treaty of San Francisco"]**. The continuation, that the United States "cannot, therefore, admit that the disposition of Taiwan is merely an internal problem of China," is in the same source but **not confirmed as one unbroken sentence**; the file quotes only the first clause.

## 6. Secretary of State, 1953-1959

### Entry on duty: a live contradiction

**history.state.gov** gives appointment and entry on duty as **21 January 1953** **[A]**; **allen_dulles.journey.json** has the oath on **26 January**, and the pool's geography lens agreed with it. **Flagged, not resolved**: the file uses 21 January and records the conflict in `date_confidence`. Related error: Allen became DCI only on **26 February 1953**, having been Deputy Director. The "two brothers sworn in together" line is off by five weeks.

### Iran, 1953

Dulles set in motion the operation against **Mohammad Mosaddegh**; his brother's agency executed it as TPAJAX **[A]**, the street phase on **19 August 1953** **[A: mohammad_mosaddegh.journey.json]**. **Dulles was not in Tehran**, as the pool's geography lens says explicitly, so the stop sits in **Washington**.

### Massive retaliation, Dien Bien Phu, Geneva, 1954

The speech was delivered **12 January 1954 to the Council on Foreign Relations in New York**, not Washington as the pool had it **[A: Wikipedia, "Massive retaliation"]**, and **he never says "massive retaliation."** He says **"massive retaliatory power"**; the shorter phrase is the press's. The file quotes the passage verbatim and says so.

**Operation Vulture, corrected twice.** The pool called it "the U.S. Air Force's proposed" plan and dated it April. It came from a **French request** through **Admiral Radford** in **March 1954**: up to **98 B-29s** from Okinawa and the Philippines plus about **150 carrier fighters**, with an option for **up to three small atomic weapons** **[A: Wikipedia, "Operation Vulture"]**. Dulles's own stop is therefore the **London mission** to recruit Churchill and Eden, where he was rebuffed; Eisenhower then cancelled it.

**Geneva, corrected by two months.** He was there **26 April to 3 May 1954 only**, replaced by **Walter Bedell Smith** **[A: Wikipedia, "1954 Geneva Conference"]**, not through July as the pool had it. The 17th-parallel partition and the 1956 election terms were settled **21 July**, after he left, and the refusal to sign was **Bedell Smith's to deliver**. The refusal to shake **Zhou Enlai's** hand is documented and belongs to late April or early May **[A]**.

### Guatemala, SEATO, and Taipei, 1954

**PBSUCCESS**; Arbenz resigns 27 June **[A]**. Dulles had represented **United Fruit** at Sullivan & Cromwell, as had Allen **[A]**. The claim that he **publicly attributed the bombing to the Guatemalan air force** survives only as a **paraphrase** in Wikipedia's "1954 Guatemalan coup d'état", **no press briefing or cable having been located** **[R]**; used in the file and labelled there as reconstruction. The **Manila Pact** was signed **8 September 1954** **[A]**. And the pool put the **Sino-American Mutual Defense Treaty** at **Taipei**: it was signed at **Washington on 2 December 1954**, in force 3 March 1955 **[A]**, the documented Taipei stop being his later visit of 20-23 October 1958.

### Neutrality, June 1955

"Neutrality has increasingly become obsolete and, except under very exceptional circumstances, it is an immoral and shortsighted conception" **[A as a quotation; date contested]**. Wikipedia dates it **9 June 1955**; other secondary sources give a **June 1956 Iowa State College commencement**. **Not resolved**, no primary transcript located, so the place, **Ames**, is only as good as the contested date. Recorded in `date_confidence`.

### The Baghdad Pact, 1955: a stop deliberately not made

The pool's geography lens carried a **Baghdad** stop for 1955, tagged [A] on "standard Cold War diplomatic histories." Dulles **sponsored** the northern tier, the United States **did not join**, and **no source in this pass places him in Baghdad in 1955**, so the file **omits it**. His **May 1953 Middle East tour**, which did include Baghdad, lies outside the verified pool and was likewise not used.

### Suez, 1956

**Corrected, wrong city twice.** The **Aswan withdrawal of 19 July 1956** was announced **in Washington**, to Ambassador **Ahmed Hussein**, not in Cairo; Nasser's answering nationalization speech of **26 July** came at **Alexandria**, not Cairo either **[A: Wikipedia, "Suez Crisis"]**. **Corrected, date:** the pool put his turn against Nasser and the arms-sale obstruction in **1958**; it belongs to **1955-1956**, after the September 1955 Czech arms deal **[A]**. He attended the **London Conference, 16-23 August 1956**, opposed the invasion, and pressed with Eisenhower for withdrawal **[A]**. In **November** the first colon-cancer operation, with a bowel perforation, took him out of decision-making at the peak of the crisis **[A]**.

### The Taiwan Strait, 1958, and brinkmanship

**Corrected, overstated.** The pool had him "authorizing the U.S. Air Force to state publicly" a readiness to use nuclear weapons over Quemoy and Matsu. **No support was found for any such public authorization.** Documented instead: the crisis ran **23 August to 2 December 1958**, and Dulles met the **Joint Chiefs on 2 September**, where nuclear use in Kinmen's defence was weighed **internally**, conventional means first **[A: Wikipedia, "Second Taiwan Strait Crisis"]**. A separate March 1955 item, "Dulles stated publicly that the U.S. was seriously considering a nuclear strike," is **explicitly a paraphrase** and the verbatim statement was **not located** **[R]**; not used. **Taipei, 20-23 October 1958** **[A]**, with the communique renouncing force as the principal means of return to the mainland.

The word itself comes from **Shepley, "How Dulles Averted War," Life, 16 January 1956, p. 70ff.**: "The ability to get to the verge without getting into the war is the necessary art" **[A, confirmed against both Wikipedia's "Brinkmanship" and Wikiquote, matching wording]**. The noun was coined derisively by **Adlai Stevenson**.

### The domino theory, attributed away

The pool carried a 1954 [A] entry crediting Dulles with articulating it. **Wikipedia's "Domino theory" does not credit him with originating or first stating it**; the "falling domino" articulation is **Eisenhower's**, at his news conference of **7 April 1954**, with Truman-era antecedents **[A]**. Dulles was a leading proponent inside the administration, which is a different claim. **No stop was made for it.**

## 7. Illness, death, burial, 1959

- **February 1959:** further abdominal surgery at **Walter Reed**, the cancer recurred and spread **[A]**. **Resigned 15 April 1959**, formal termination recorded **22 April** **[A: history.state.gov]**. **Died 24 May 1959 at Walter Reed**, aged 71 **[A]**. **Funeral 27 May, Washington National Cathedral**, Eisenhower present with allied delegations including West German officials; **burial at Arlington** the same day **[A]**.
- **Correction, an anachronism.** He received the **Medal of Freedom** (Truman's 1945 civilian award) posthumously. The **Presidential** Medal of Freedom is a different, higher decoration **created by Kennedy in 1963** and could not have been awarded to him **[A]**. The **Sylvanus Thayer Award** (1959) is correct.
- **Gap.** The grave's **section and lot** could not be confirmed: arlingtoncemetery.mil and Find a Grave both blocked automated retrieval.

## 8. Afterlife

- **John-Foster-Dulles-Allee, West Berlin, 1959** **[A]**, formed that year by merging the Zeltenallee with the Schlieffenufer and Spreeweg, past the Kongresshalle toward the Reichstag. **Christian Herter** attended the dedication **[A per the pool, not re-verified]**. **The pool contradicts itself on the coordinate:** geography lens 52.5145, 13.3501 (inside the Tiergarten, about 1.1 km southwest of the street) against afterlife lens 52.5186, 13.3639. The corrected de.wikipedia value **52.5176, 13.3624** is used, and the disagreement recorded rather than quietly averaged.
- **Washington Dulles International Airport**, dedicated **17 November 1962** by Kennedy with Eisenhower present; site chosen by Eisenhower in 1958, the name proposed by his aviation adviser **Pete Quesada** **[A]**. **Correction, place:** it is in **Dulles, Virginia**, straddling Loudoun and Fairfax; **Chantilly**, which the pool gave, is a separate community. **Gap:** a **statue or bust in the terminal**, commonly assumed to exist, has **no confirmable source** and is asserted nowhere in the file.
- **John Foster Dulles Library of Diplomatic History**, Firestone Library, Princeton **[R, downgraded from A]**. The **1962 date could not be verified**: Princeton's finding aids returned **403 and bot-verification**, and Wikipedia's Firestone article **does not mention the Dulles Library at all**. The pool contradicts itself here too (40.3467, -74.6577 against 40.3467, -74.6591); both wrong, Firestone is **40.34942, -74.65747**.
- **Reassessment.** Declassification of the Iran and Guatemala records from the 1990s, and popular history, chiefly **Kinzer's *The Brothers***, have complicated the statesman image the airport and the library memorialize. **No organized renaming campaign targeting Dulles memorials was confirmed** **[R]**.

**Real relations, NOT named in campa, with reasons**

## 12. Shape of the journey file

**44 stops in 9 segments**, gregorian, register "national mythology: the canon is true." Fifteen of the forty-four stops are in Washington, D.C., eight of them at the State Department building itself. That is not a defect of the file. It is what a Secretary of State's geography looks like: the coups in Tehran and Guatemala City were ordered from a desk, and the man who ordered them stayed at the desk.
