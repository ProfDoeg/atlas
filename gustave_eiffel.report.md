# Gustave Eiffel: research report

*Slug `gustave_eiffel`. Alexandre Gustave Bonickhausen dit Eiffel, Dijon 15 December 1832 to Paris 27 December 1923. Engineer, contractor, aerodynamicist. Compiled 2026-08-05 for the Atlas of Journeys.*

**Legend.** `[A]` = attested, source named on the spot. `[R]` = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on one unverifiable relay. Contradictions are flagged and left standing, not adjudicated. Gaps are named as gaps.

**On the shape of this subject.** Eiffel is barely a traveller: the record confirms two border crossings (Egypt 1866; Vevey 1906, to sign a will). His pins scatter over four continents because what travels is the crated iron and the drawing on file at Levallois-Perret. The journey pins the works as well as the man, and says in the campa where he was not present.

---

## 1. Origins and schooling, 1832-1855

- Born at Dijon, 15 December 1832, to Alexandre Bonickhausen dit Eiffel, officer of military supply, and Catherine-Mélanie Moneuse, coal merchant. The doubled surname comes from an ancestor of the Eifel region. `[A: fr.wikipedia]`
- Collège Royal de Dijon from 1843; Collège Sainte-Barbe, rue Valette, Paris, from 1850. Fails the École Polytechnique; enters the École Centrale des Arts et Manufactures, graduating 1855 thirteenth of eighty, trained as a chemist. `[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]`

**Correction.** The pool put the École Centrale at 48.8636 / 2.354 and hedged that the building is unconfirmed. Both halves are wrong: the school occupied the Hôtel Salé, 5 rue de Thorigny, 1829-1884. Journey uses **48.8598 / 2.3626**. `[A: fr.wikipedia, "École centrale Paris"]`

*Gaps.* The Dijon birth address is not pinned anywhere reachable; the journey uses the city centroid and says so. The family quarrel that closed the expected career at an uncle's vinegar works is tradition without a document `[R]`.

## 2. Apprenticeship and the Garonne, 1856-1862

- Charles Nepveu, builder of metal railway equipment, takes him on in 1856; Nepveu goes bankrupt, the business passes to the Compagnie Belge de Matériels de Chemin de Fer, and Eiffel stays on to head the research department. `[A: fr.wikipedia]`

**Contradiction, not resolved. Bordeaux.** fr.wikipedia: Eiffel "directs his first major project", the Garonne railway bridge (about 510 m), in **1858, at 26**. en.wikipedia: works run **1857-1860**, and he takes site management from Nepveu only in **March 1860, at 27**. The likeliest reading is assistant from 1858, director from 1860, but no reachable source states that; the journey pins 1858 and spells the conflict out in `date_confidence`. Eiffel diving in after a fallen workman is tradition. `[R]`

**Correction.** The pool gave 44.8478 / -0.5522 and 44.8425 / -0.5501 for the Passerelle Eiffel; both land well north, the first near the Pont Chaban-Delmas. Journey uses **44.8322 / -0.5522**, per fr.wikipedia.

- Marriage to Marguerite Gaudelet, 17, at Dijon, 7 April 1862; Claire born 1863, Édouard 1866. `[A: fr.wikipedia]` The full list of five children rests on compiled genealogies and is not enumerated. `[R]`

## 3. The independent firm, 1866-1877

- **Egypt, 1866.** A locomotive contract takes him there; he sees the Suez Canal under construction under Ferdinand de Lesseps. `[A: en.wikipedia]` The hinge of the biography: the man whose ditch he admires in 1866 hires him for Panama in 1887 and stands trial with him in 1893.
- **Levallois-Perret, 1866.** Borrows capital, acquires the Ateliers Pauwels, founds his own company; these sheds are the origin of nearly every non-Paris pin in the file. **Eiffel et Cie**, partnership with Théophile Seyrig, 6 October 1868. `[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]`

**Correction. Gare de Verdun.** Not "completed" on 14 April 1870, as the pool said. fr.wikipedia: built **1868** to Eiffel's plans, in service **14 April 1870**, inaugurated **1 July 1870**. Coordinates to **49.1656 / 5.3794**.

**The export trade,** 1869-1882, all `[A: Wikipedia, "Gustave Eiffel"]` and single-source: lighthouses at Burullus and Ras Gharib, gasworks at La Paz, the Tacna cathedral frame, a Moquegua fountain, bridges at Ungheni, Viana do Castelo, Cubzac, Szeged and Saigon (`[R]`). Only La Paz and Arica are pinned.

**Tag downgraded. Arica.** The pool tagged the Catedral de San Marcos `[R]`. es.wikipedia states without hedging that it was commissioned from Eiffel's workshops, prefabricated for Ancón near Lima, and redirected to Arica after the 1868 earthquake. Arrived **1875**, inaugurated **1876** (the pool's "1875" is the arrival). Coordinates to **-18.4790 / -70.3207**. The residual doubt attaches to every prefabricated Eiffel building.

- Marguerite dies at Levallois-Perret, 8 September 1877, aged 32. Tagged `[R]` in the pool; fr.wikipedia gives it plainly with date and place. He never remarries; Claire, 14, takes over the household. `[A: fr.wikipedia]`

*Gap.* Nothing reachable describes what the bereavement cost him; the two months after her funeral contain the Budapest and Douro openings. The journey says the record is silent instead of inventing grief.

**Corrections. Budapest Nyugati.** Coordinates from generic Budapest (47.4979 / 19.0402, 1.9 km off) to **47.5108 / 19.0575**. en.wikipedia credits the **design to the architect Auguste de Serres**, Eiffel's company as **builder**; the pool's "designs and builds" is wrong. Opened **28 October 1877**.

**Corrections. Maria Pia bridge, Porto.** The pool dated win and construction start to 1875-05. Eiffel & Cie won the competition in 1875 with the lowest bid (965,000 francs), but construction ran **5 January 1876 to 1 October 1877** and the bridge opened **4 November 1877**. `[A: en.wikipedia]` Coordinates to **41.13972 / -8.59722**.

*Contradiction, not resolved.* Authorship of the Douro arch. Seyrig and Eiffel quarrelled over the credit and the partnership dissolved over it in 1879. Nothing reachable adjudicates.

## 4. Garabit, the Statue, and the name, 1878-1886

- Stephen Sauvestre first works with Eiffel on the Paris Gas Company pavilion at the 1878 Exposition `[A: Wikipedia]`; he later makes Koechlin's pylon legible as a monument.
- **1879, three things at once.** Seyrig leaves and the firm is renamed Compagnie des Établissements Eiffel; Maurice Koechlin joins; the Garabit contract is awarded. `[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]`

**Correction, three times over. Garabit.** The pool gave three different and all wrong pairs: 44.9989 / 3.1039, 44.9319 / 3.1653, 45.0964 / 3.1685 (13 km out). en.wikipedia gives **44.97528 / 3.17750**, used everywhere in the journey. The 165 m span is right; the height is **124 m** (elsewhere 122.5 m), not 122 m; and the arch closed in **1884** while the line opened in **November 1885**. Léon Boyer laid out the crossing.

- A decree of 1879 and a civil judgment of **15 December 1880** authorise the family to use Eiffel alone. `[A: fr.wikipedia]` The relation between the two acts is not spelled out anywhere reachable.

**Brackets restored.** The pool carried as verbatim Eiffel a sentence on his surname's "consonance allemande". fr.wikipedia n. 3 reads *« ...qui inspire [des] doutes sur [sa] nationalité française... de nature à [lui] causer... »*, the brackets marking words the editor substituted into a presumably first-person original. Stripping them turns an edited paraphrase into a false verbatim. The journey restores them and notes the primary document was not located.

**Contradiction, not resolved. When Bartholdi engaged Eiffel.** en.wikipedia "Statue of Liberty": **1880**, the year after Viollet-le-Duc's death on 17 September 1879. en.wikipedia "Gustave Eiffel": **1881**. Two further pool errors: Viollet-le-Duc was an architect serving as chief engineer, not a sculptor; and the rue de Chazelles assembly was complete by July 1884 and dismantled from January 1885, so "1881-1885" is loose.

**Contradiction, not resolved. Who designed the skeleton.** fr.wikipedia credits Koechlin with originating it in 1881 under Eiffel's firm; en.wikipedia writes as though it is Eiffel's. The same question as the Douro arch and, shortly, the tower: it recurs three times in one career. The journey states both attributions side by side.

*Gap.* Whether Eiffel crossed for the 28 October 1886 dedication. The pool's geography lens says accessible sources do not confirm it either way. Carried as a gap in the campa.

## 5. The tower, 1884-1889

- **Patent, 18 September 1884**, for a metal tower exceeding 300 m, with Koechlin and Nouguier; Eiffel buys out his engineers' rights. `[A: toureiffel.paris; en.wikipedia]` The name follows the patent, not the drawing.
- **30 March 1885**, paper to the Société des Ingénieurs Civils making the public case, quoted in the journey. `[A: en.wikipedia]`
- **8 January 1887**, convention with the State and the City: 1.5 million francs of subsidy, a twenty-year concession, then demolition. `[A: fr.wikipedia]`

**Correction, and an internal contradiction in the pool flagged. Foundations.** The pool's chronology lens gave **26 January 1887**, its geography lens **28 January 1887**. Both encyclopedias give the 28th, which the journey uses. The official "2 years, 2 months and 5 days" back from 31 March 1889 is what tempts sources toward the 26th: an arithmetic artefact.

- **14 February 1887, Le Temps.** The Committee of Three Hundred protest and Eiffel's reply run the same day; signatories reportedly include Maupassant, Dumas fils, Gounod. `[A: en.wikipedia; French Wikisource]`
- **Platforms.** First floor 1 April 1888 `[A: toureiffel.paris]`; second floor 14 August 1888, `[R]`, not pinned.
- **31 March 1889.** Topped out; tricolour raised at 2.35 p.m. to a twenty-five-gun salute; Legion of Honour conferred at the summit `[A: toureiffel.paris]`. **15 May 1889**, opens to the public as centrepiece of the Exposition Universelle for the Revolution's centenary `[A: fr.wikipedia]`.

## 6. Panama, Münchenstein, the trial, 1887-1893

- Eiffel's company contracts to build the lift locks for the French canal under de Lesseps, each about eleven metres of lift. `[A for the contract: en.wikipedia; R for the exact scope, per the pool's own flag]` *Gap:* whether Eiffel went to the isthmus. The pool says plainly it is unconfirmed. So does the journey.

- **14 December 1888.** The Compagnie Universelle du Canal Interocéanique suspends interest payments, precipitating the scandal. `[A: en.wikipedia]` The journey pins this at Colón and notes in `date_confidence` that the corporate act occurred in Paris.

**Two corrections. Münchenstein, 14 June 1891.** Death toll **73 killed and 171 seriously injured**, not 71 `[A: en.wikipedia]`. And the pool said the bridge was built by "Eiffel's former associates": it was not, the contract went to **Eiffel & Cie itself**, which built it in 1874-75, which is why the disaster hurt his reputation. Coordinates to **47.5177 / 7.6183**.

- **9 February 1893.** Convicted of misuse of funds, fined 20,000 francs, sentenced to two years. `[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]` The Court of Cassation quashed the conviction **on prescription grounds, not on the merits**, a distinction the pool's afterlife lens gets right and its geography lens blurs. Waldeck-Rousseau was among his counsel; the Legion of Honour council threatened mass resignation rather than strip his cross.

**Sequencing corrected.** The pool's geography lens says he resigned "before the verdict". Half right: he announced the intention before the trial, but resigned at the general meeting of **14 February 1893, five days after** the verdict. `[A: en.wikipedia]`

- Street names removed in several French cities during the scandal, later restored. `[A: fr.wikipedia; restoration date not given: a gap]`

**Dispute re-described. Casa de Fierro, Iquitos.** The pool said the rival attribution was to "another Parisian iron-house manufacturer". es.wikipedia credits the design to Eiffel (c. 1860) and the fabrication to the **Belgian** works Forges d'Aiseau. Shown at the 1889 Exposition, it reached Iquitos in **1890**, so "1889-1892" should be 1889-1890. Coordinates about **-3.7497 / -73.2439**. `[R]`

**Long Biên, Hanoi.** Vietnamese tradition credits Eiffel; the documented design credit is Daydé & Pillé, from a competition his company did not win. `[R]` Not pinned. With the Casa de Fierro, the clearest case of the name outrunning the firm.

## 7. The second career, 1898-1913

- **Meteorology at the summit, 1898.** `[R]` in the pool, fr.wikipedia only; carried with that hedge. The concession expires in 1909 and the tower is then to be scrapped, so the instruments open the campaign to make it indispensable.

**Correction, year replaced. Wireless.** The pool claimed a permanent TSF transmitter in **1901**. No such thing exists. Per fr.wikipedia: Ducretet's experiment from the summit to the Panthéon, **5 November 1898**, then Ferrié's permanent military station, **1903**, which saved the tower when the concession lapsed in 1909. The journey pins 1898 and 1903 separately.

- **Aerodynamics.** Laboratory and first wind tunnel at the Champ de Mars, 1909; relocation after noise complaints to 67 rue Boileau, Auteuil, 1912, where the tunnel still runs. `[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]` Publications on air resistance 1907 and 1911 `[A: en.wikipedia, which gives no title or publisher: a documented gap]`.
- **Langley Medal, 1913**, presented by Alexander Graham Bell, who observes that Eiffel's writings on air resistance have become classical. `[A: en.wikipedia]`

*Gap.* The Paris venue of the ceremony: the pool tags it `[R]` and calls it unconfirmed. The journey pins the Institut de France and calls it in `date_confidence` a placeholder for an unlocated address. Separately, the pool gave Auteuil twice, 48.8467 / 2.2637 and 48.8459 / 2.2637; the journey uses the second, with the rue Boileau address.

## 8. Death and afterlife

- **27 December 1923**, at his residence on the rue Rabelais, Paris 8e. `[A: fr.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]`

**Three-way disagreement inside the pool, flagged not resolved.** Chronology lens **48.8566 / 2.3522** (the Paris centroid, 3 km off); geography **48.8721 / 2.3121**; afterlife **48.8698 / 2.3092**. The chronology lens's own note asserts "approximately 48.8703 / 2.3095" and claims the other two give it correctly, which neither does. The journey uses 48.8698 / 2.3092.

- **The Beethoven detail.** en.wikipedia carries the tradition that the andante of the Fifth was playing; some sources print it as fact, others as anecdote. The journey states it as tradition, in those words.
- **31 December 1923.** Funeral at the Église Saint-Philippe-du-Roule (48.8737 / 2.3103) `[A: fr.wikipedia]`; burial the same day in the family vault at the Cimetière de Levallois-Perret `[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]`.

**Correction.** The pool gave the cemetery as 48.8935 / 2.2833 and 48.8935 / 2.2868, both about 0.8 km short; fr.wikipedia places it at **48.9000 / 2.2889**. Those figures collide with the pool's figure for the **workshops** (48.8935 / 2.288), a separate site: the two were conflated. The journey keeps the workshops and moves the cemetery.

- **2 May 1929.** Bourdelle's bust unveiled at the base of the north leg. `[A: en.wikipedia]`
- **Heritage.** Passerelle Eiffel listed 2010 after campaigning by the Association of the Descendants of Gustave Eiffel `[A: en.wikipedia]`. Auteuil laboratory 1997, Garabit 2017, UNESCO candidates from 2018: fr.wikipedia alone, not independently verifiable.
- **Testament** dated 3 August 1906 at Vevey, codicils to 1922, at the Archives Nationales (microfilm MC/MI/RS/1166). `[R: fr.wikipedia only; shelfmark unchecked]`

## 9. Quotations: what is real and what is not

Four bodies, all `[A]`: the reply to the artists, Le Temps, 14 February 1887 (French Wikisource, three passages); English renderings on toureiffel.paris of that reply and of the line on the tower's curvature, French originals not collated against them here; the 1885 paper and the 14 February 1893 statement, in English translation on en.wikipedia; and the Committee of Three Hundred protest, labelled in the journey *not by Eiffel*.

**Rejected.** *"Méfions-nous des grands hommes."* Widely attributed in popular accounts of the 1887 controversy, cited without primary sourcing, untraceable to a signed document or dated interview. `[R: tradition]` Not used. On the bracket-restored Bönickhausen quotation see section 4.

## 10. Intersections with existing atlas travellers

**Named in the campas.** Two atlas files already mention the tower in their own text `[A]`: **`claude_levi_strauss`** (Musée de l'Homme, Palais de Chaillot, 1949, the tower named as the view from his post) and **`jules_barbier`** (Passy, the tower named as the landmark orienting the grave). Both appear in the funeral campa. **`luc_olivier_merson`** took the 1889 gold medal for painting under "a new tower of iron" that French painters had signed a letter against `[A]`; named at the 1867 Galerie des Machines and at the 1889 opening. **`isadora_duncan`** and **`laban`** walk the 1900 Exposition on the same ground without naming the tower `[R: inferred]`; named at the 1889 opening. **`mikhail_bakunin`** (1861), **`muybridge`** (1875) and **`tschiffely`** (1928) are named in the Colón campa, all `[R]`.

**Recorded, not named.** **`juan_peron`** adds nothing the other three do not; **`pizarro`** and **`columbus`** touch Panama four centuries early, coincidence rather than crossing; **`marcel_proust`**, **`suzanne_perrottet`**, **`tchaikovsky`** and **`dannunzio`** are Paris-in-general links with no tower mention; **`hegel`** is a format donor for city pins only.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical.** Champ de Mars **48.8556 / 2.2986**; Paris **48.8566 / 2.3522**; Colón **9.3547 / -79.9**; Panama locks **9.08 / -79.68**; Statue of Liberty **40.6892 / -74.0445**.
