# Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi (c. 718 - 786/791)

*Basra, Iraq. Founder of 'ilm al-'arud; author of Kitab al-'Ayn, the first dictionary of Arabic; reformer of the script's vowel-marking; author of the lost Kitab al-Mu'amma.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, anecdote, or inference. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and are **not** adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Standing caution.** This life is documented almost entirely in tabaqat written two to six centuries after his death: Ibn al-Nadim's *Fihrist* (from 987), Ibn Khallikan (13th c.), al-Dhahabi (14th c.). No work securely from his hand survives intact: *al-'Arud* is lost, *Kitab al-Mu'amma* is lost, *Kitab al-'Ayn* survives in a student's redaction. His largest attested presence is as a quoted authority inside somebody else's book. That is the evidentiary floor. Second: the reachable tertiary sources (en.wp, ar.wp, Britannica) **disagree with each other** on birthplace, death date, and the nature of his diacritical innovation. Where they conflict, both readings are printed.

---

## 1. Origins and birth (c. 718 / 100 AH)

**Tribe.** Of the Azd, and of the Farahid clan, whence the nisba **al-Farahidi**, said to derive from an ancestor named **Furhud**, "young lion" **[R - traditional genealogy, no document]**. The Azd trace their origin to **Ma'rib in Yemen** before the collapse of the Ma'rib dam scattered Azdi clans toward Oman and southern Iraq **[R - the dam-collapse migration is itself a contested tradition, not fixed archaeologically to one event]**. Tribal genealogy, not personal itinerary, and flagged as such in the journey file.

**Year.** 718 CE / 100 AH is consensus **[A - en.wp, ar.wp, Britannica]**. A wider 700-718 range appears in some secondary literature **[R]**.

**Place: an open contradiction, not resolved.**

- **Oman.** En.wp opens "Born in 718 in Oman" and Britannica gives Oman **[A as to what these sources say; the en.wp sentence carries that article's own verification-needed flag]**. No town is ever named, so the journey pin (22.9333, 57.5333) marks the historic interior heartland, **not** a birthsite.
- **Basra.** Ar.wp states flatly that he was born in Basra in 100 AH and demotes Oman to "some sources say he was born in Oman and moved to Basra as a child" **[A as to what that source says]**, adding that his father was among the first men after the Prophet's time named Ahmad **[R]**.

**Flag.** The split runs along a language line: the **English-language** reference tradition favours Oman, the **Arabic** tabaqat tradition favours Basra. Neither quantifies itself, so "most sources" claims are unsupported in both directions. **The year is [A]; the place is [R].**

**Coordinates.** All lifetime stops use **30.3919, 47.7011**, Old Basra beside modern az-Zubayr. The city was **relocated to the Shatt al-Arab in the late 900s**, more than a century after his death **[A - en.wp 'Basra']**, so the modern pin (30.5085, 47.7835) is ~17 km off for his lifetime. Only the 1965 statue uses it.

## 2. Formation in Basra (730s-740s)

Basra in this period is, with Kufa, the leading centre of Arabic linguistic and religious scholarship **[A - Britannica]**. Teaching happened in circles at the congregational mosque (founded 14 AH / 636 CE); that al-Khalil studied in such circles is **[A]**, that any given session met there is **[R - inferred from documented practice]**.

**Teachers named in the sources [A, all undated]:** **Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala'**, canonical Qur'an reader and leading Basran grammarian, named as his primary teacher in philology (Britannica); **'Isa ibn 'Umar al-Thaqafi**, grammarian (ar.wp teacher list); **Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani**, traditionist (Britannica, en.wp), see below; **'Asim al-Ahwal** and **al-'Awwam ibn Hawshab** (en.wp, after Ibn Khallikan / Ibn al-Nadim).

**The Ibadi question, with a chronological defect flagged.** The tradition holds that al-Khalil was raised in or drawn to Ibadi doctrine, reflecting Oman's Ibadi character, and was persuaded to Sunni orthodoxy by **Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani** **[A as to the account: Britannica and en.wp both carry it; how far it is documented rather than deduced from his tribal background is disputed]**.

The further claim that he was a **companion of Jabir ibn Zayd, "the founder of Ibadism,"** fails twice **[R, corrected]**: (1) Jabir was not the founder but "one of the founding figures," who "took leadership of the denomination after the death of Abd-Allah ibn Ibadh"; (2) Jabir died c. 93 AH (711/12), 103 AH (721) at the outside, so a man born in 718 cannot have been his companion in any ordinary sense. En.wp asserts it anyway. **Carried as a claim about milieu, not personal association.**

**Al-Mirbad.** The camel market and literary gathering-ground on Basra's outskirts, where philologists collected Bedouin speech as the standard, is background for the whole Basran school **[A as to the institution]**; al-Khalil's presence there is **not individually documented [R]**. Pin 30.3805, 47.715 is approximate. No source locates the coppersmiths' market, so that stop takes the city pin rather than duplicating this one.

## 3. Manner of life

- Lives in a **small reed house** in Basra, on **falconry** and an **inherited garden**; refuses lavish gifts from rulers **[A - en.wp]**. The house is attested for his **adult** life; no source makes it a family home of the 720s-730s.
- Performs the **Hajj annually** **[A - en.wp, Britannica]**, the only travel his documented life contains. No individual year is recorded.
- **Medina** appears in the journey file as an explicit **[R]** inference from ordinary hajj-route practice. **No source consulted places al-Khalil there.** A stated gap.
- **Refusal of a governor's stipend**, answered with a saying and with verse **[R - the governor is never named and the wording varies between tellings]**.

## 4. Prosody: fifteen meters, five circles

**The anecdote.** On returning from pilgrimage he overhears the hammering of a **blacksmith's anvil** and derives the meters from it **[R - en.wp]**. Arabic Wikipedia instead names the **Suq al-Saffarin**, the **coppersmiths'** market, "where the distinctive sound of their hammers striking inspired the concept of Arabic meter" **[R - ar.wp]**. **Two incompatible versions; both printed.** Note that Suq al-Sagha, which circulates in some retellings, is the goldsmiths' market, not the coppersmiths'.

**The system.** Fifteen meters on **five circles**, described in his treatise **al-'Arud**, **no longer extant** and known through citation **[A - en.wp 'Arabic prosody']**.

**The sixteenth meter: attribution contested.** En.wp states "Later Al-Akhfash al-Akbar described a 16th meter, the mustadarik." But **al-Akhfash al-Akbar** (d. 177 AH / 793) was **Sibawayh's teacher** and al-Khalil's contemporary, and his own article credits him with **no metrical work**. Much specialist literature assigns the mutadarik instead to **al-Akhfash al-Awsat** (d. c. 830), Sibawayh's student. **Contested; "his student al-Akhfash" is an error either way.**

**The three unattested meters.** "Three meters were unknown to Pre-Islamic Arabia, suggesting that al-Farahidi may have invented them" **[A as a statement; the inventorship inference is [R]]**. The source states three flatly and **names none of them**. Invention versus systematization is left open.

## 5. The script

He introduces **distinct shapes** for the vowel diacritics, replacing the earlier ambiguous **dot**-based notation (Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali's coloured dots), and creates the **small shin** marking **shadda**. The system, first intended for poetry, comes to be applied to the Qur'an **[A - en.wp]**.

**A common inversion, flagged.** Summaries in circulation credit him with *devising the dot-based* vowel marks. That reverses the innovation: fatha, damma and kasra are **miniature letter forms** (alif, waw, ya), and what he replaced was the dots.

## 6. Kitab al-'Ayn

- The **first dictionary of Arabic** and the oldest to survive **[A - en.wp 'Kitab al-Ayn']**.
- Ordered not alphabetically but by **point of articulation**, from pharyngeal **'ayn** to labial **mim**, in twenty-six books; al-Khalil held 'ayn's throat origin to mark it the essential, self-representing sound **[A - en.wp, paraphrasing secondary scholarship]**.
- Built on **permutation of root letters** to enumerate the possible and mark the actual **[A]**.
- **Dictated to al-Layth ibn al-Muzaffar ibn Nasr**, who completed it after al-Khalil's death **[A as to the outline - ar.wp, en.wp]**. The **division of labour between master and student is debated and unresolved** in anything reachable here **[R]**; some hold the surviving redaction is substantially al-Layth's.
- In circulation: "It is said that al-Farahidi's Kitab al-'Ayn did for lexicography what his student Sibawayh's al-Kitab did for grammar" **[A as a hedged encyclopedic comparison, attributed to no named author]**.

## 7. Students

- **Sibawayh** (c. 760 - 796), of Shiraz, studies under him in Basra and is "more indebted" to him than to any other teacher **[A - en.wp]**. He cites al-Khalil **608 times** in al-Kitab, more than any other authority **[A - exact figure]**.
  - **Two circulating errors rejected.** (a) Study "during Harun al-Rashid's reign" is **impossible**: Harun reigns 786-809 and al-Khalil dies in 786, so it falls under al-Mahdi or al-Hadi. (b) **Yunus ibn Habib** was Sibawayh's *teacher* and al-Khalil's contemporary, not a fellow student.
  - The window is roughly **c. 775-786** **[R - from Sibawayh's birth c. 760 and al-Khalil's death in 786]**. Any range beginning in the 760s has Sibawayh studying in the decade of his own birth.
- **Al-Asma'i**, later philologist and transmitter of Bedouin poetry **[A - Britannica student list]**.
- **Al-Nadr ibn Shumayl**, who carried Basran philology to Khurasan and who transmits the "reed hut / not two coins" report **[A - Britannica; ar.wp for the saying, via Ibn Khallikan]**.
- **Al-Layth ibn al-Muzaffar ibn Nasr** (above).

## 8. Cryptology

**Attested.** He writes **Kitab al-Mu'amma** ("Book of Cryptographic Messages"), containing the **first known use of permutations and combinations** to enumerate all possible Arabic words with and without vowels; modern historians call it the first work of its kind by a linguist **[A - en.wp 'History of cryptography'; ar.wp corroborates]**. The treatise is **not extant**, known through later citation **[A]**.

**The al-Kindi link, citation corrected.** That al-Khalil's combinatorial method influenced **al-Kindi** (c. 801-873), author of *Risala fi Istikhraj al-Kutub al-Mu'amma*, which introduces **frequency analysis**, is sourceable, but **not** to 'History of cryptography', which discusses both men separately and asserts no connection. The linkage is in **en.wp 'Al-Kindi'**: "Building on the work of al-Khalil (717-786), Al-Kindi's book ... gave rise to the birth of cryptanalysis." Al-Kindi's dates, service under al-Ma'mun and al-Mu'tasim, and treatise title are confirmed **[A]**.

**Dropped.** Frequency analysis as "the single most significant cryptanalytic advance before World War II" appears in **neither** article; it traces to Kahn and Singh popular histories, and is not laundered through a Wikipedia citation here.

**The Byzantine cryptogram: rejected as told, kept as legend.** A tradition credits al-Khalil with deciphering a Byzantine coded message for **al-Ma'mun's** court by permutation. It is **not located** in en.wp (al-Khalil, History of cryptography, al-Kindi), ar.wp, or Britannica **[a gap, stated as one]**, and it is **chronologically impossible as told**: al-Ma'mun reigns **813-833**, at least 27 years after al-Khalil's death **[A - en.wp]**. Most probably a conflation with al-Kindi, who did serve al-Ma'mun and did write the foundational treatise. A supporting assertion often attached to it is also overstated: the House of Wisdom is not simply "a project of al-Ma'mun's reign." En.wp traces its foundation to Harun al-Rashid's collections or al-Mansur's private one, has al-Ma'mun only "turn it into a public academy," and notes the institutional narrative "has been challenged by some historians." **Before any future upgrade:** the *Fihrist* directly, and Mrayati, Alam and al-Tayyan.

## 9. Death

**Secure:** he dies in **Basra** **[A - all traditions agree]**.

**Disputed:** the year. The traditions give **170 AH**, **173 AH** and **174 AH**. En.wp prints "786 or 791"; ar.wp prints 174 AH as primary with 170 and 173 as alternatives.

**Arithmetic correction, because the AH-to-CE glosses in circulation are wrong.** 170 AH = 786/787, 173 AH = 789/790, 174 AH = 790/791. Much repeating literature pairs 173 with 791 and 174 with 789, both swapped. Corrected, en.wp's "786 or 791" is simply **170 AH or 174 AH**, so the English-versus-Arabic disagreement is narrower than it looks: same candidate set, different preference. **An unresolved range, 786-791, not adjudicated.**

**The pillar.** Absorbed in devising a system of accounting to keep his maidservant from being cheated by the greengrocer, he walks into a mosque pillar and is fatally injured. Transmitted by **al-Dhahabi** (ar.wp), the greengrocer detail supplied by en.wp. **Tagged [R], not [A]:** a single 14th-century biographer on an 8th-century death is tradition, and the maidservant motive is elaboration on top of it.

**Burial.** In Basra **[R - general consensus]**. **No consulted source names a tomb, quarter, or gravesite. A gap.**

**Corrections applied to this section:**

- The **862/863** date is specific in the source; ranges beginning c. 856 are wrong at the low end.
- **Ibn al-Nadim was a Baghdadi, not a Basran.** Born in Baghdad c. 320/932, worked in the family bookshop there, died there c. 995; he travelled to Basra, Kufa, Aleppo and Mosul hunting material, but Baghdad was his base.
- **Anastase-Marie al-Karmali did not discover or edit the text.** The discovery "was announced in 1914" in *Lughat al-Arab*, the Arabic-language Baghdad periodical he founded and edited; he was the publicist, and calling this a reintroduction to Western scholarship misplaces the venue. **Who found the manuscript, and which manuscript, is not stated in any reachable source. A gap.**
- **University of Nizwa.** 22.9333, 57.5333 is Nizwa **town**; the campus is at **22.9108, 57.6722**, in Birkat al-Mouz, 20 km northwest. The named centre is **single-sourced**: the university's own article lists no research centres.

## 11. Interlocks named in campa

A traveler may be named in a campa only where **al-Khalil himself** has a direct or indirect relation to that person. Shared geography does not qualify. Two names pass:

1. **al_kindi** - en.wp 'Al-Kindi' states that al-Kindi built on al-Khalil's work: one-directional intellectual descent, explicitly asserted in a source. Named at the Baghdad stop.
2. **saadia_gaon** - Saadia's Hebrew lexicographic project (the Agron) is framed against "the grammarians of Basra and Kufa" who did the same for Arabic, and al-Khalil is the founding figure of that school. One-directional influence across a century and a half. Named at the al-Andalus stop.

**Deliberately not named**, because the only connection offered is co-location or contemporaneity, which the map already shows: rabia (born c. 717-718, same city and generation, no attested contact of any kind), hallaj, ibrahim_ibn_adham, junayd_al_baghdadi, ibn_battuta, ziryab, evliya_celebi, benjamin_de_tudela, david_sassoon, abd_al_qadir_al_jilani, maslama_al_qurtubi, ahmad_al_majriti, aristotle. Also not named: claude_shannon, alan_turing, diffie, adi_shamir and the rest of the cryptography cluster, since Shannon's 1945 memorandum frames itself against **al-Kindi** and the link back is second-hand.

**Sibawayh**, the most consequential relation in the life, has no journey file and therefore no slug to name.
