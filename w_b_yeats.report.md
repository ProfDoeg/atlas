# W. B. Yeats (1865-1939): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the atlas journey `w_b_yeats`. **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or standard biography not confirmed in the sources reachable this pass. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; coordinate fixes are marked **[coord]**. Foster's *W. B. Yeats: A Life* (I 1997, II 2003) is the spine of the pool but **was not read directly**: every "Foster" tag is a citation trail through Wikipedia and the research lenses. The poems were fetched verbatim (poets.org, Wikisource) and are the firmest material here.*

## 1. Sligo and Sandymount, 1865-1877

- Born 13 June 1865 at Georgeville, Sandymount Avenue, Co. Dublin, eldest child of the painter John Butler Yeats and Susan Pollexfen. **[A: Wikipedia]** **[coord 53.3305, -6.2185 checks out]** **Corrected:** a lens called the house "his maternal grandparents' rented house"; the Pollexfens lived at Merville in Sligo, and Georgeville was rented by his father. The ownership claim is dropped.
- Family to London, 1867, for the father's art career; summers at Merville, the Pollexfen house in Sligo, c.1868-1880, Sligo named as his real home for life. **[A: Foster I; Yeats, *Reveries*]**
- Rosses Point: pilot boats, drowned sailors, fairy lore, later printed as *The Celtic Twilight* (1893). **[A: Yeats; Foster I]** The pool's "c.1872-1880s" is a span, not a date; the pin says so.
- Benbulbin: horizon of the childhood, named in "Under Ben Bulben" (1938). **[A]** **[coord corrected: lens pin 54.381, -8.4402 sits ~2.3 km NE of the summit, off the mountain; use 54.367, -8.467]**
- Godolphin School, Hammersmith, 1877-1880; a poor and unhappy student. **[A: Foster I]** Folded into the Bedford Park stop.

## 2. Bedford Park and the return to Dublin, 1877-1886

- The family settles in Bedford Park, the new artists' suburb at Chiswick. **[A: Foster I]** **[coord corrected: lens pin 51.4949, -0.2777 is ~1.3 km SW of the estate; Bedford Park is 51.5001, -0.2617]**
  - **Corrected (address):** 8 Woodstock Road only in 1879-1881; from 1888 the family was at **3 Blenheim Road** (the plaque address). The lens's separate "studio" pin at 51.4949, -0.2789 is a fabricated split from that house; one coordinate serves both stops.
- Erasmus Smith High School, Harcourt Street, from October 1881. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Corrected:** the pool dates the Dublin return to 1881; the family returned at the **end of 1880** and schooling resumed October 1881.
- Metropolitan School of Art, Kildare Street, from 1884; meets George Russell (AE). **[A: Wikipedia]**
- June 1885: Dublin Hermetic Society founded; a Dublin Theosophical lodge opens with Mohini Chatterjee lecturing. **[A: Wikipedia; Yeats, *Autobiographies*]** Day not recorded.
- 1886: *Mosada*, 100 copies, his first solo publication. **[A]** No stop.

## 3. London, Theosophy, and the Golden Dawn, 1887-1901

The weakest part of the pool and the most hedged part of the file.

- Blavatsky at 17 Lansdowne Road, Holland Park, from 1887; *The Secret Doctrine* published from it, October 1888. **[A: `blavatsky.journey.json`]** **[coord 51.5099, -0.2044, inherited byte-identical]**
- **Yeats's own Theosophy is [R].** The introduction to Blavatsky, membership of the Esoteric Section (usually dated 1888) and departure after demanding experimental tests **could not be confirmed** here: the Theosophical Society article carries no Yeats dates, Britannica returned HTTP 403. One lens tagged it **[A]** while its own chronology flagged it unconfirmed.
- 1888: writes "The Lake Isle of Innisfree" after hearing a fountain in a London shop window; published 1890. **[A: Yeats, *Autobiographies*]** No adult visit to the islet is documented: the poem fixes the place, not a journey.
- 1889: *The Wanderings of Oisin*; meets Maud Gonne **[A: Wikipedia, Maud Gonne]**, the day 30 January being **Foster's**, standard but second-hand. Proposals: at least four, 1891-1901, a last in 1916, then to Iseult Gonne, all refused **[A]**. "The troubling of my life began." **[A]**
- 7 March 1890: initiated at Isis-Urania, motto **Demon Est Deus Inversus**. **[A for the year: Wikipedia; A via R. A. Gilbert, *The Golden Dawn Companion*, for the day]**
  - **Contradiction (venue):** one lens gives Mark Masons' Hall, 86 **Great Queen Street**; the Waite file gives Mark Masons' Hall, **33 Golden Square, Soho**. The pin follows Great Queen Street (51.5155, -0.1235) and records the disagreement.
- Order lineage (grade ladder cut from the SRIA, the *Fama*'s vault as ceremonial furniture, *Zanoni* behind both): see the interlock table. 1894: visits Mathers in Paris, avenue Duquesne, watching trance evocations **[R: Foster I]**, no stop.
- 1895-1919: 18 Woburn Buildings, renumbered 5 Woburn Walk, one door under two numbers the lenses never reconciled; the pool's 1896 start is wrong. **[A: Wikipedia, Woburn Walk]** **[coord 51.5265, -0.129, ~80 m out]**
- June 1896: the Kelmscott Chaucer finished at Morris's press, 14 Upper Mall, with Burne-Jones's 87 woodcuts. "Yeats calls it the most beautiful book of the age." **[A: `edward_burne_jones.journey.json`, quoting Yeats]** **[coord 51.4908, -0.2321, inherited]**
- **1900-1901, the crisis. Two corrections.** (a) The pool dated the pamphlet *Is the Order of R. R. & A. C. to Remain a Magical Order?* to 1900; Wikipedia is explicit that Yeats printed it in **1901**. The **revolt against Mathers is 1900**, triggered by his initiating Aleister Crowley at the Paris temple on 16 January 1900; the pamphlet is aftermath. (b) He did not resign in 1900: he stayed active and remained in **Stella Matutina until 1921**. **[A: Wikipedia, Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn]**
- Waite, initiated at Isis-Urania in January 1891, ten months after Yeats, kept the Isis-Urania name for his Independent and Rectified Rite and closed it in 1914; Stella Matutina and Alpha et Omega survived. **[A: `arthur_edward_waite.journey.json`]** Personal Yeats-Waite opposition over ritual authenticity: **[R]**.

## 4. Coole, the theatre, and the nation, 1894-1916

- Lissadell House: visits to the Gore-Booth sisters from 1894, remembered in the 1927-29 elegy. **[A]** Cut from the journey for space, corrected here for later use: **[coord 54.350, -8.583, not the lens's 54.3363, -8.5347]**, the house is Greek Revival not Georgian, and the "gazelle" is a simile for one of the sisters.
- 1896: introduced to Lady Gregory by Edward Martyn; summers at Coole for the rest of her life; initials on the autograph copper beech beside Martyn, Shaw, Synge, O'Casey. **[A: Wikipedia, Coole Park]** **[coord corrected: lens pin 53.0847, -8.8264 is ~1.4 km SE of the demesne centre, 53.0965, -8.835, and does not reproduce the attested three miles to Ballylee]**
- 1899: co-founds the Irish Literary Theatre with Gregory, Martyn, George Moore **[A]**. 1903: Maud Gonne marries Major John MacBride in Paris **[A for the year]**; February is the standard dating but the pool carries the year alone, so the pin defaults and says so.
- **27 December 1904: the Abbey opens.** **[A: Wikipedia, Abbey Theatre]** **Corrected:** the pool named two plays. The bill was **three**: Yeats's *On Baile's Strand* and *Cathleen Ni Houlihan*, and Lady Gregory's *Spreading the News*. *On Baile's Strand* was omitted.
- 1907: the *Playboy* riots, faced from the Abbey stage. **[A: Foster I via the geography lens]** The words he used there are unverified and not quoted.
- April 1907: Ravenna with Lady Gregory and Robert Gregory; the San Vitale mosaics. **[A: Foster I; Yeats letters 1907]**
- North American lecture tours: **five**, not four. 1903-04, 1911, 1914, 1920, and 1932-33 (for the Irish Academy of Letters). **[A]**
  - **Gap:** no source consulted places Yeats at **Carnegie Hall**; the lens coordinate is Carnegie Hall's, standing in for "New York." The journey drops the American pins rather than carry an unsupported venue. The tours are good material for a later revision; the venue is not.
- 1909: meets Ezra Pound in London. **[A]**
- 1913-1916: winters at Stone Cottage, Coleman's Hatch, Pound as secretary, Fenollosa's Noh papers; drafts *At the Hawk's Well* January 1916. **[A]**
- 1916: "Easter, 1916," the same year as the last proposal to Maud and the proposal to Iseult. **[A]** Standard editions date the poem 25 September 1916; the pool has the year only, so the pin defaults and says so.

## 5. Marriage, the Instructors, and the tower, 1916-1921

- Purchase of the Ballylee tower for **35 pounds**. **Contradiction:** Wikipedia says "purchased it in 1916 or 1917." One lens asserted 1917 flat; the other kept the disjunction. Price firm, year not; the pin carries 1917 with the disjunction stated.
  - **Corrected [coord]:** Thoor Ballylee is **53.1032, -8.7748**. The lenses gave 53.1024, **-8.8721** (~6.5 km west, open country) and 53.1244, -8.7847 (~2.4 km north): both wrong, mutually inconsistent. All four Ballylee stops use the corrected figure.
- 20 October 1917: marries Georgie (George) Hyde-Lees at Harrow Road register office, Pound a witness. **[A]**
- 24 October 1917: the automatic writing begins; the "Instructors"; the script becomes the basis of *A Vision*. **[A: Wikipedia for the substance; the 24 October date via widely cited secondary sources, Wikipedia giving only the year]**
- Restoration under the architect William A. Scott, renamed Thoor Ballylee. **Contradiction, unresolved:** Wikipedia dates the Scott work **1921-1923**, Foster and most scholarship **1917-1919**, and **Scott died in 1921**, which strains the later dating from inside. The pool's flat "1918" is unsupportable on the authority cited.
- Occupancy as a summer residence. **Contradiction, unresolved:** the chronology lens says 1919-1929, the afterlife lens 1921-1929, both quoting the same Wikipedia sentence ("from 1921-1929"). The pin uses 1921 and states the disagreement.
- 1919: leaves 5 Woburn Walk; takes 4 Broad Street, Oxford, 1919-1922; "The Second Coming" written January 1919, published in *Michael Robartes and the Dancer* (1920); Stella Matutina affiliation runs to 1921. **[A]**

## 6. Senator and Nobel laureate, 1922-1928

- 82 Merrion Square, 1922-1928; a bomb in the street and gunfire outside during the Civil War. **[A: Foster II]** 28 March 1923: the "certain leisured class" speech. **[A: Wikiquote]**
- **11 December 1922:** appointed Senator of the Irish Free State; reappointed 1925; term ends 1928. **[A]** **[coord corrected: lens pin 53.3403, -6.2513 lands ~215 m east of Leinster House, on the Merrion Square side, though the claim names the Kildare Street entrance; use 53.3405, -6.2545]**
- **Nobel Prize, 1923**, "for his always inspired poetry, which in a highly artistic form gives expression to the spirit of a whole nation." **[A: Nobel citation]**
  - **Contradiction resolved by distinction:** announced 14 November 1923, conferred at Stockholm 10 December 1923. One lens dated the Stockholm event to November, the other to December. If the place is Stockholm the date is December; the journey uses 1923-12-10.
- 1924: chairs the coinage committee, wanting designs "elegant, racy of the soil, and utterly unpolitical." **[A]** The animals themselves (horse, salmon, hare, woodcock, bull) are **[R]**, standard numismatic history unverified this pass; the campa names them, the designer is not named.
- **11 June 1925: the Seanad divorce debate.** Two further quotations from it (marriage sacred but not a sacrament; a southern Ireland governed by Catholic ideas alone will never get the North) are **[A via Foster II, p. 294]**.
  - **Corrected (truncation):** "we are no petty people" is a fragment. The full sentence, via Garratt, *Modern Irish Poetry* (1989): "We against whom you have done this thing, are no petty people. We are one of the great stocks of Europe." The fragment drops the accusation; the journey quotes the whole.
  - **Inaccessible:** the Oireachtas record (Seanad 1925-06-11) returned **HTTP 403**; the wording rests on Foster and Garratt, not the primary record.
- *A Vision*: privately published by T. Werner Laurie, title page 1925, published January 1926; revised 1937. **[A]** "I dare say I delude myself in thinking this book my book of books," to his publisher, early 1924 **[A via Foster II, p. 245]**; the pool's citation to "note 77" is imprecise and corrected.
- 1925: the Cappella Palatina, Palermo, on a trip taken partly for his health **[A: Foster II]**. 1926: writes "Sailing to Byzantium"; the 1931 BBC draft calls Byzantium "the centre of European civilization and the source of its spiritual philosophy" and says it is "right for an old man to make his soul." **[A]** **Corrected:** it first appeared in **October Blast (1927), a Cuala Press collection of his own poems**, not a periodical; republished in *The Tower* (1928).
- 1928: *The Tower*; retirement from the Senate on health grounds; last stay at Ballylee, the family gone for good in 1929. **[A]**
- He never visited Constantinople; Byzantium in the poems is wholly symbolic. **[R, a negative claim]** Pin **41.0086, 28.9802**, inherited byte-identical from `constantino_el_grande`.

## 7. Last years, death, and the disputed grave, 1928-1965

- Winters at Rapallo, 1928-1934, where Pound had settled; much of *The Winding Stair* written there, *A Vision* revised **[A: Foster II]**. 6 April 1934: the Steinach operation, by Norman Haire, London **[A]**. 1938: *The Ten Principal Upanishads*, *Autobiographies*, the Abbey premiere of *Purgatory* **[A]**.
- 1935: Mallorca with Shri Purohit Swami for the principal Upanishads. **[A]** The pool names the island only; the pin sits at **Palma (39.5696, 2.6502)**, residence unverified.
- **28 January 1939: dies of heart failure at the Hotel Ideal Sejour, aged 73.**
  - **Contradiction, unresolved:** Wikipedia's Yeats article puts the hotel in **Roquebrune-Cap-Martin**; its Roquebrune article says he died "in the neighbouring town of **Menton**." The lenses give coordinates ~1 km apart, and the geography figure falls on the Menton side under a Roquebrune label. The journey uses 43.7629, 7.4986 and states the ambiguity.
  - "The Black Tower" as his last completed poem: **[R]**, unverifiable this pass, not used. Letter to Lady Elizabeth Pelham, 4 January 1939: "Man can embody truth but he cannot know it." **[A: Wikiquote]**
- Buried at Roquebrune, 30 January 1939, in a grave meant to last a year before reburial at Drumcliff. **[A]** **[coord corrected: lens pin 43.7649, 7.4989 is ~2.8 km east toward Menton; the commune centre is 43.7628, 7.4631]**
- **The ossuary. Chronology corrected, tag downgraded to [R].** The remains went to the communal ossuary when the concession lapsed in **1946**; the reassembly of a skeleton by a French doctor belongs to **1948**, when the Irish request arrived and the authorities said they could no longer locate the actual remains. One lens tagged the episode **[A]** while its next entry flagged the same doubt **[R]**. It rests on a single 2015 find in declassified French diplomatic papers plus press reporting and is **disputed by the Yeats family**. Whether Drumcliff holds his own bones is unresolved; **no DNA identification has ever been undertaken**.
- September 1948: repatriation aboard the corvette LE Macha; reburial at St Columba's, Drumcliff, under the epitaph from "Under Ben Bulben." **[A]** **[coord corrected: the geography lens gave 54.3403, -8.4986, ~1.3 km north of the churchyard; use the afterlife figure 54.3308, -8.4936]**
  - **Corrected (genealogy):** the rector of Drumcliff was Yeats's paternal **great-grandfather**, the Rev. John Yeats, not his grandfather (Wikipedia, Drumcliff).
- 20 June 1965: Thoor Ballylee opened as a museum by Padraic Colum on the birth centenary; Kiltartan Society founded by Mary Hanley 1961; flood 2009; Failte Ireland restoration from February 2012; reopening June 2015 for the 150th; Storm Desmond damage that December; open every summer since. **[A]**
- Not used, all **[R]**: Gillespie's Sligo bronze (pool says 1989, his catalogue 1990, siting unconfirmed); Moore's *Knife Edge*, St Stephen's Green, 1967; the Yeats Summer School, Sligo, from 1959.

## Honest gaps

1. **Blavatsky and the Esoteric Section:** join and departure dates and the expulsion story unconfirmed. Foster or Ellmann would settle it; encyclopedia stubs do not.
2. **The Isis-Urania hall:** Great Queen Street versus 33 Golden Square.
3. **The tower:** restoration 1917-19 versus 1921-23 (with an architect dead in 1921 inside the later dating), first summer 1919 versus 1921.
4. **Place of death:** Roquebrune versus Menton, two Wikipedia articles in flat contradiction.
5. **Whose bones lie at Drumcliff:** open, untested, likely to stay so.
6. **The American tours:** five attested, no venue. The Carnegie Hall pin was dropped rather than dressed up.
7. **Foster unread:** both volumes cited at second hand throughout.
