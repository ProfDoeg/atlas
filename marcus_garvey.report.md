# Marcus Mosiah Garvey (1887-1940): research report

*Atlas of Journeys working file, compiled 2026-08-11 for `marcus_garvey.journey.json` (45 stops, 9 segments).*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or secondary claim without primary support. Contradictions are printed as contradictions; where one side is demonstrably wrong the demonstration is shown, otherwise the disagreement stands.

**Method note.** About one claim in four in the research pool needed correction: wrong places (Kingston for St Ann's Bay, Whitechapel for Southwark, Bloomsbury for Fetter Lane, Foley Square for Park Row), wrong years (1907, 1917, 1964), a wrong building, a wrong verb inside a famous sentence. No coordinate was accepted without a check against the street address. Adjudicating spine: **Grant, *Negro with a Hat* (2008)**, with Cronon (1955), Martin (1983), Hart (1967), Elkins (1972), and Hill (ed.), *The Marcus Garvey and UNIA Papers*.

## 1. St Ann's Bay and the trade, 1887-1910

- Born 17 August 1887 at St Ann's Bay **[A: Grant p.8; UNIA Papers vol. 1]**; marked birthplace 32 Market Street **[A: Jamaica National Heritage Trust]**; town centre 18.436, -77.203, the atlas's canonical pin for the bay.
- **Father corrected.** The pool gave "Marcus Garvey Sr.", a later convention. The biographies give **Malchus Garvey**, stonemason **[A: Grant p.8]**. Mother Sarah (Jane) Richards, domestic servant **[A]**. **"Eleventh child" rejected [R]**: Grant p.9 has six children by two previous wives and four with Sarah, Marcus the youngest, two dead in infancy.
- **Apprenticeship 1901, place corrected.** Year and age right; place wrong. Apprenticed to his godfather **in St Ann's Bay**, at the **Port Maria** branch in 1904, in **Kingston only from 1905** at P. A. Benjamin **[A: Grant pp.13-16; en.wikipedia, "Early career in Kingston: 1905-1909"]**. The godfather's name, given elsewhere as Alfred E. Burrowes, is **not in Grant or Wikipedia [R]**. Father's private library **[R: Amy Jacques Garvey, a widow's memoir]**. Mother's death March 1908 **[A]**.
- **Strike: year and union both corrected.** **November 1908**, not 1907, and Garvey was vice president of the compositors' section of the **Printers' Union**, not a "Kingston Typographical Union", a name absent from the sources **[A: Grant p.18; Alexander 2004]**. 1907 is the earthquake year, the likely source of the slip. Strike broken, Garvey sacked and blacklisted, work found with a government printer **[A]**.
- National Club assistant secretary April 1910 **[A]**; *Garvey's Watchman*, three issues, 1910 **[A]**, **office address not attested [R]**.

## 2. Central and South America, 1910-1911

- United Fruit, Limon Province, 1910 **[A: UNIA Papers vol. 1; Garvey, "The Negro's Greatest Enemy" (1923)]**, his own account read as self-narration.
- **The Limon paper: name and year both wrong.** Not *La Nacionale*, 1910. It was **Nation / La Nación**, bilingual, **spring 1911**, folded when the press broke **[A: Martin p.16; Grant p.30]**. ***La Prensa* is a separate, later paper founded at Colón**; the pool conflated the two.
- Colón and the canal camps, 1911 **[A: UNIA Papers vol. 1]**. **Bocas del Toro [R]**: tradition, absent from the Papers itinerary; no stop. Return to Kingston, ill, 1911 **[A: Cronon]**.
- **Ecuador, split verdict.** Travel through **Ecuador is attested [A: Hart p.220; Grant p.31]**; the **Guayaquil quay pin (-2.1894, -79.8891) is unsupported [R]**. Country attested, city guessed, so no stop.

## 3. London, 1912-1914

- **Not the East End.** The pool lodged him in Whitechapel (51.5152, -0.062). He rented on **Borough High Street, Southwark**, approx. **51.5020, -0.0916**, about 1.9 km southwest, and laboured in the docks by the piece **[A: Grant pp.36, 38; Martin p.18]**. **He did not initially live with a sister**: Indiana joined him only in **August 1912 [A: Grant pp.35-36, 45]**. Add House of Commons visits and Speakers' Corner speeches **[A: Grant]**; no stop, the dates are not recorded.
- African Times and Orient Review under Dusé Mohamed Ali, 1913-1914 **[A: UNIA Papers vol. 1; Duffield]**.
- **Birkbeck: the worst geographical error in the source tier, and Wikipedia carries it.** The pool put the evening classes in **Bloomsbury (51.522, -0.1308)**, the modern campus, 1.7 km off. Birkbeck occupied the Breams Buildings on **Fetter Lane** until the Malet Street move in **1952 [A: en.wikipedia, "Birkbeck, University of London"]**. Corrected to **51.5157, -0.1094**, two minutes from the Fleet Street office. **Subject: law only**; "philosophy" is in **neither Cronon p.7, Martin p.19, nor Grant p.45 [R]**. SS Trent for Jamaica, June 1914 **[A]**; no stop, no coordinate that is not invented.

## 4. The Association founded, 1914-1917

- **UNIA founded July 1914; the 1 August date is not supported [A: Martin pp.27-28; Grant p.53].** Original name in the founding constitution correct **[A]**. **"A meeting on Orange Street" overstates the premises**: run out of Garvey's **hotel room**, with headquarters secured afterwards by Amy Ashwood on her father's credit **[A: Grant p.56]**.
- Ashwood met August 1914 at the Queen Street Baptist Literary and Debating Society **[A]**. **The 1915 secret engagement is [R]**, carried by Wikipedia without further citation. Landing 23 March 1916, SS Tallac **[A: UNIA Papers vol. 1]**.
- **The Harlem address is misdated in the pool.** 1916 lodging was with a Jamaican expatriate family; **238 West 131st Street belongs to c.1918-19 [A: Grant pp.72-73, 165]**. Soapbox speaking at 135th Street is **1917 onward [A: Grant p.90]**.
- **Two events, two months.** New York division launched **May 1917**; the platform shared with **Hubert Harrison** at the Liberty League's inaugural meeting was **June 1917 [A: Cronon p.41; Grant pp.87, 93]**, a month later than the pool has it. **The hall is not named in the sources**, so the stop uses a general Harlem pin. Speaking tour of thirty-eight states, 1916-1917 **[A: UNIA Papers vols. 1-2]**; folded into the Harlem stops.

## 5. The paper and the line, 1918-1921

- ***Negro World* from April 1918, not 1917 [A: Grant p.135; Cronon p.46].** Circulation near 10,000 within a year, backed in part by Madam C. J. Walker **[A]**. Black Star Line incorporated 1919 at 56 West 135th Street, shares sold door to door **[A: UNIA Papers vol. 2]**. **Chicago arrest, September 1919**, under Illinois Blue Sky Laws, some $50,000 raised by then **[A]**; no stop, carried in the prosecution campa.
- **Liberty Hall dedicated July 1919 [A: Cronon p.49; Grant pp.197-198]**: a half-built church at **114 West 138th Street**, named for Liberty Hall in Dublin, capacity about 6,000. **Coordinate corrected** from the pool's 40.8149, -73.9422 (290 m southwest) to **40.8170, -73.9400**.
- **The shooting, 14 October 1919: wrong building in the pool.** George Tyler entered the **UNIA and Black Star Line office at 56 West 135th Street**, not Liberty Hall **[A: Grant; NYT reporting]**. Leg and scalp wounds correct. "Dies in custody days later" is loose: he went over the third-tier rail of the Harlem jail almost at once. **Why he came was never established [A]**.
- **SS Yarmouth / Frederick Douglass [A: UNIA Papers vol. 2].** Thirty-year-old tramp, launched 31 October 1919, $11,000 of repairs within weeks, scrapped in 1922. **The maiden voyage called at Cuba first, then Jamaica**; the pool's "to Kingston" drops Havana. Marriage to Amy Ashwood 25 December 1919, some 3,000 at Liberty Hall **[A]**.
- **SS Shady Side: deposit confirmed, month not.** $10,000 down **[A: Cronon p.57; Grant p.233]**, but **July 1920 is when Garvey sacked secretary Smith-Green and captain Cockburn**. Carried as first half of 1920. SS Kanawha, 1920-1921 **[A: UNIA Papers vol. 3]**; no stop.
- **Convention, August 1920 [A: UNIA Papers vol. 2].** Twenty-five thousand **[A: Grant p.245]**; Declaration of Rights adopted; Garvey named Provisional President of Africa. **The pool has the wrong Garden**: this was **Madison Square Garden II** (1890-1925, Madison Avenue and 26th Street), not Garden III, which opened in 1925 at 50th and Eighth. The coordinate 40.742, -73.9877 is right; only the label was wrong.

## 6. The prosecution, 1922-1927

- **Arrest and indictment are different events.** Arrested and charged **January 1922** over shares advertised in the SS Orion, a ship the line did not own, bail $2,500; **indicted February 1922 [A]**. The pool called January the indictment. Edward Young Clarke met at Atlanta, June 1922 **[A]**.
- **Baltimore, July 1922, and a divorce never conceded.** "Divorces Amy Ashwood" overstates it: the **New York court refused** him a divorce; he took one in **Jackson County, Missouri**, whose validity **Ashwood contested for life [A: Grant p.239]**.
- **Trial venue anachronistic by thirteen years.** **Foley Square opened in 1936.** In May 1923 the SDNY sat in the old **Federal Building and Post Office at Broadway and Park Row**, approx. **40.7118, -74.0060**; the pool's 40.7143, -74.0021 is the 1936 building. **Judge Julian Mack presided; Garvey defended himself [A: UNIA Papers vol. 5]**. Convicted 18 June 1923, five years and $1,000; bailed at $15,000 by Judge Manton, September 1923; held in the Tombs **[A]**, no stop.
- **USP Atlanta, February 1925 to November 1927 [A: UNIA Papers vol. 6].** Dates right, **coordinate badly wrong**: the prison is at 601 McDonough Boulevard SE, approx. **33.7031, -84.3453**; the pool's 33.7627, -84.3327 lands 6.7 km north.
- **The 1927 against 1928 contradiction is resolvable, and the demonstration is shown.** Coolidge commuted the sentence **18 November 1927**, to expire at once, conditioned on deportation **[A: Elkins p.76; Grant pp.410-411]**. History.com's "1928" is the **Geneva trip**, which happened **after** deportation.
- **The voyage, corrected.** About a thousand supporters saw him onto the **SS Saramaca** at New Orleans on **3 December 1927**, not 2 December **[A: Grant p.411]**. The ship called at **Cristóbal**, where he was **refused permission to land**; he transferred to the **SS Santa Maria** for Kingston **[A: Grant p.413]**, arriving 10 December **[A]**. The date of that call is not given and is marked inferred.

## 7. Jamaica and Europe, 1928-1935

- **Edelweiss Park from 1928 [A: Cronon; Martin]**, **foreclosed and auctioned in 1934**, not held to 1935 **[A: Grant p.433]**; he sailed in March 1935 having already lost it. His play ***Coronation of an African King*** was staged there in **August 1930 [A]**.
- **1928 tour [A: Grant pp.422-425]:** England from April, **Royal Albert Hall 6 June**, Paris, then the League petition, received with **no League action [A]**. **Palais Wilson coordinate corrected to 46.2149, 6.1510** (the pool's is 450 m southwest). An earlier **five-member delegation went to Geneva in 1922 [A: Grant pp.340-341]**, so 1928 is the second approach.
- **People's Political Party, 1929 [A: Grant p.428]**, Jamaica's first modern party. **Contradiction recorded: Wikipedia 1929, History.com 1928**; 1929 followed on Grant.
- **The 1930 mechanism is wrong in the pool.** He was not "disenfranchised by his own conviction". The PPP lost because the franchise ran on **property and income qualifications** that excluded his base. The conviction cost him his **Kingston city council seat**: he pled guilty to demeaning the judiciary over the plank on impeaching corrupt judges, and served **three months at Spanish Town with a £100 fine [A: Hart p.224; Grant p.429]**.

## 8. London and death, 1935-1940

- **West Kensington from 1935; Talgarth Road only from 1937 [A: Grant pp.438, 444].** The pool gave "53 Talgarth Road, 1935-1940". From 1935 he rented in West Kensington with headquarters on **Beaumont Avenue and Crescent**. Coordinate corrected from 51.4936, -0.2075 (640 m northeast) to **51.4908, -0.2157**. **Blue plaque 2005**, "lived and died here" **[A]**. The London monthly was ***The Black Man*** **[A]**.
- **Toronto, 1937 and 1938 [A: Grant pp.442, 444]**, with the School of African Philosophy. **Two corrections**: Toronto was **not** the UNIA's headquarters after 1937, which stayed in **London**; and **Massey Hall is unverified**, so the attribution is dropped and the stop pinned to the city. **Cape Breton, 1937 [R]**; no stop.
- **The death is a five-month decline.** Stroke **January 1940**; premature obituaries in **late May**, on rumours traced to George Padmore, one calling him dead "broke, alone and unpopular"; a **second massive stroke on the second day of reading them**; death **10 June 1940 [A: Grant pp.1-2, 448-450]**. **The causal role of the obituary is a narrative inference, not a medical fact [R]**; the journey states the sequence without the cause. He died at **53 Talgarth Road** per the plaque, **not** at Beaumont Crescent, the office, where the pool put it.
- **Burial [A: Grant p.451]: St Mary's Roman Catholic Cemetery, Kensal Green, 51.5280, -0.2336**, distinct from the general Kensal Green Cemetery at 51.5286, -0.2241, which the pool pinned. "Plain, unmarked-for-years grave" is wrong: a **vault in the catacombs of the cemetery chapel**.

## 9. Afterlife

- **Reburial 22 November 1964 [A: Hart p.218; Grant p.454]**, at what was then **King George VI Memorial Park**, after lying in state in Kingston's Catholic cathedral. **The pool's two lenses disagreed**, one giving 13 November (History.com), one an undated November; **both are wrong**. **Coordinate corrected to 17.9825, -76.7887**; the pool's candidates land 3.4 km and 1.7 km north. Monument by G. C. Hodges: a tomb at the centre of a platform in the shape of a black star, with an Alvin Marriott bust on the ground since 1956 **[A]**.
- **National Hero 1969, not 1964 [A: Jamaica Information Service; Grant].** 1962 is when the independent government began hailing him; 1964 is the reburial; the Order was conferred in **1969**. Dating the honour to 1964 is what generated the conflict inside the pool. "The Right Excellent" and first National Hero are correct **[A]**.
- Mount Morris Park renamed Marcus Garvey Park, 1973 **[A: NYC Parks]**. St Ann's Bay statue **[R]**. Liberty Hall Multimedia Museum, Kingston, 2011 **[R]**. UNIA-ACL still active **[A]**.
- **Rastafari [R].** The "look to Africa, when a Black king shall be crowned" prophecy is **not verified in Garvey's writings or speeches**; Chevannes (1994) treats it as apocryphal. **It is not used as a quote anywhere in the journey file**; the 1930 stop states the play and the coronation without asserting it.
- **Ghana's black star [R], contradiction left standing.** Nkrumah credits general Garveyite influence; the flag-design link is questioned by later scholarship, including Sherwood. **"A people without the knowledge of their past history..." [R]**: not traced to a primary text, not used.

## Quotations: what survived checking

Checked against the Internet Archive scans of *Philosophy and Opinions of Marcus Garvey* (Vol. I, 1923, `philosophyopinio01garv`; Vol. II, 1925, `philosophyopinio0000amyj`). **Five circulating citations failed:**

1. **"Power is the only argument that satisfies man."** Verbatim, **page wrong**: Chapter II, "Power", is **p.22**, not p.14.
2. **"The Negro must have a country and a nation of his own..."** **Wrong word, wrong volume.** The verb is **"discommode"**, not "dislodge" (the Vol. I scan's garbled "discdthode" resolves in Vol. II), and there is no comma after "country". Source: **"An Appeal to the Soul of White America" (2 October 1923), Vol. II (1925), pp.5-6**.
3. **"Should we not, therefore, turn our eyes towards Africa..."** No comma after "home" in the source. **Location wrong**: it closes **"The Slave Trade", Chapter IV, p.46**; "Shall the Negro Be Exterminated?" begins at p.63 and lacks the line.
4. **"If 400,000,000 Negroes can only get to know themselves..."** Verbatim. **Location wrong**: **Chapter III, "Man Know Thyself", p.39**, not p.17.
5. **"We hear among Negroes the cry of 'Africa for the Africans'."** **The opening clause is not Garvey's.** The source reads *Day by day we hear the cry of "AFRICA FOR THE AFRICANS."* **Location wrong**: Chapter I, "Epigrams", **p.14**, not Chapter V pp.93-94.

Clean and used as printed: the Chapter I epigrams at pp.11-12 and the corrected p.14 epigram; "I know no national boundary..." (Ch. III, p.37); "Statement on Arrest" (Ch. V, p.100); and the five corrected citations above in corrected form.

Canonical Harlem pin **40.8116, -73.9465** inherited byte-identical for the convention's opening stop; Finley named in that campa.
- **`columbus`: inferred [R].** The 1503-1504 marooning pins the bay Garvey was born on. Canonical bay pin **18.436, -77.203** inherited byte-identical for the birth stop; Columbus named there. `columbus.journey.json` does not mention Garvey.
- **`milton_blahyi`: inferred [R].** Blahyi's ground is Cape Mesurado, taken by the Colonization Society in 1822, the Liberia the Black Star Line meant to settle a century later. **The Finley stop records that no source has Garvey setting foot in Liberia**, so there is no Monrovia stop; the tie is made in the Black Star Line campa.
- **`prince_hall`: inferred [R]**, the first Black lodge chartered in America as precedent for the UNIA's halls; named in the Liberty Hall campa. **`mussolini`: inferred [R]**, through the 1935 invasion that made Rastafari reverence for the exiled Selassie global; named in the 1930 Coronation campa. **`muammar_gaddafi`: inferred [R]**, "King of Kings of Africa" at Benghazi in 2008 against the 1920 title; named in the Garden campa. None of the three files names Garvey.
- **`sheba`: offered [R]**, via the Solomonic descent Selassie claimed. Two removes; recorded here, not carried into any campa. **Haile Selassie** is a QUEUE.md entry, not yet a journey file.
