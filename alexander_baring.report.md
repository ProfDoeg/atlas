# Alexander Baring, 1st Baron Ashburton (1774-1848)

*Research report. Slug `alexander_baring`, written 2026-08-31 from the verified pool,
against `francis_baring`, `thomas_jefferson`, `napoleon`, `empress_josephine`.*
**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a dossier synthesis
no independently fetched page confirmed. Where sources disagree, both are printed and
neither is chosen.

---

## 1. Birth and baptism: the year that will not settle

**Born 27 October 1774, London, second son of Francis Baring. [A]** History of
Parliament member 6533, the Hansard people page, and the 1885 DNB headline "BARING,
ALEXANDER, first Baron Ashburton (1774-1848)".

**Contradiction, flagged.** The 1773 variant is real but the pool mis-attributed it: it
comes from landedfamilies.blogspot.com and English Heritage's Northington Grange page
("1773-1848"), **not** from the DNB, which gives 1774. "Older sources including the DNB
give 1773" is an error introduced in the dossier, not in the literature. **Mincing Lane is
an inference [R]**: no source gives a street address of birth, and the stop's
`date_confidence` says so.

**Baptism, St Gabriel Fenchurch, 25 November. [R], doubly compromised.**
1. The sole source reads "baptised at St. Gabriel, Fenchurch St., London, 25 November
   **1773**". The dossier silently moved it to 1774 to sit after the attested birth,
   producing a date no source states. The stop carries 1774-11-25 for sequence only and
   names the conflict.
2. **The church did not exist in the 1770s.** It burned in the Great Fire of 1666 and,
   alone among the ruined City churches, was never rebuilt; the parish was united with
   St Margaret Pattens and the site became roadway. At most there is a register entry of
   the united parish. Pin corrected to the parish site, **51.5108, -0.0817**, from the
   pool's 51.5121, -0.0824 (150 m north).

**Lineage [R]:** Johann Baring of Bremen settled at Exeter early in the eighteenth
century. That predates Alexander's life. **No Exeter stop**; it appears in the birth campa.

## 2. Amsterdam, c.1792-1794

**He joined Hope & Co in 1792, not "around 1794". [A]** Wikipedia, "Hope & Co.", also
records Henry Hope's flight from Hellevoetsluis on **17 October 1794**; by then the Hopes
were relocating to London. The pool both understates and mis-dates the episode.

**Labouchère was his brother-in-law, not a colleague. [A]** A Hope partner from January
1790, he married Dorothy Baring in 1796. Every later Labouchère-Baring transaction,
Louisiana included, runs along a family line as well as a commercial one.

**Pin inherited byte-identical: 52.366, 4.887**, Hope & Co's Keizersgracht 444-446 house,
from `francis_baring.journey.json`, whose own stop there is caveated as agent-run. Francis
never went; Alexander did, which is the point of the pair.

## 3. The American errand, 1795-1801

**Sent at about twenty-one as first resident agent. [A]** Baring Archive timeline, 1795.

**Coordinates corrected.** Baring Plantation, Washington County, Maine is at **45.1344,
-67.3156**, not the pool's 45.1305, -67.4759 (12 km west, unincorporated land). Alexander,
Maine is at **45.0922, -67.4675**, not 45.1893, -67.4934 (11 km north).

**Namesake framing corrected, then dropped. [A]** Wikipedia attributes both place-names
to Baring **as the Webster-Ashburton negotiator**, commemorating 1842, not the 1790s land
speculation, and gives Alexander, Maine no tie to any brother-in-law's tracts. Neither
town gets a stop: a commemorative place-name is nobody's travel. The 1842 naming is cited
on the treaty stop. The **2 February 1796 memorandum to Bingham on alien landholding [R]**
was not re-fetched and its composition site is unknown; it is a source on the Maine stop,
not a scene.

**Marriage to Ann Louisa Bingham, Philadelphia, 23 August 1798. [A]** landedfamilies
locates it "at Blackpoint, Philadelphia", a locality this pass could not geocode; central
Philadelphia used and flagged. The $900,000 dowry figure is an estimate and is not
repeated. `francis_baring.journey.json` has a London desk-stop for the same event saying
the weddings "happen in America; he is at his desk in London for both". **The wedding is
Alexander's scene, dated to the day; the London reaction stays Francis's.**

## 4. Louisiana, 1803-1804

- **30 Jan 1803, Labouchère to Baring, the financing structure. [R]** Known through Larry
  Neal's 2024 chapter; letter not re-fetched. The correspondence contemplates inducements
  to officials on both sides but **establishes no completed payment to any named
  recipient**, and the file says exactly that.
- **April 1803, Paris, with Livingston, Monroe and Barbé-Marbois. [A]** Baring Archive. No
  building given; general Paris coordinates, flagged. **Where the bankers met
  Barbé-Marbois, probably the Treasury, is an open gap.**
- **30 April 1803: 100 million francs argued down; treaty at 80 million ($15m), of which
  60 million francs ($11.25m) in US six-percent stock. [A]** Baring Archive.
- **May 1803, Britain declares war. [A, general history]** Not re-fetched.
- **16 Dec 1803, Addington forbids remittances to France on the Louisiana debt, without
  clearly forbidding onward sale of bonds already acquired. [A via the dossier's reading
  of the exhibition text]** The page itself was not re-fetched.
- **21 Oct 1803, Washington, letter to Sir Francis [A, verbatim]; 16 Jan 1804, first
  tranche delivered in Washington [A].**
- **April 1804, London prospectus and discharge. [A for the prospectus; R for the
  compressed schedule]** The further 1.65 million franc discount is Neal via the dossier,
  unconfirmed.

**Interlocks in this material.**
- `francis_baring.journey.json` has a four-stop Louisiana arc, 1798-1804, all at
  Devonshire Square (51.5175, -0.0787), narrating Alexander's Paris work from London.
  **Division adopted: Paris scenes are Alexander's at the Paris pin; the London
  authorization stays Francis's.** Alexander's own London stops here reuse the Devonshire
  Square coordinate byte-identical, because that is where he physically worked.
- `thomas_jefferson.journey.json` pins the purchase to Washington (38.9072, -77.0369),
  names no banker, and never mentions the bond mechanism: its line is "Fifteen million
  dollars for the drainage of two rivers doubles the country". The Paris stop complements
  it rather than duplicating it.
- `napoleon.journey.json` contains **no** Louisiana, Barbé-Marbois, Talleyrand or 1803
  financing material at all. A gap in the atlas, not a connection. Nothing written there.
- **Legend corrected. [R]** Barings did not buy Louisiana and resell it. The treaty
  conveyed the territory directly from France to the United States; Baring and Hope
  bought and marketed the US bonds used to pay France.

## 5. Firm, Bank, Commons, 1804-1820

- **The renaming is contradictory and is not resolved. [A/A]** Wikipedia's "Barings Bank"
  dates Baring Brothers & Co from **1804**, on Sir Francis's retirement; its Ashburton
  article gives **1807**; the Baring Archive timeline has no renaming entry;
  `francis_baring.journey.json` uses 1804. The stop is dated 1807 **to preserve sequence
  only** and says so.
- **Bank of England director 1805-1817 [R]**, records not re-fetched. **MP for Taunton,
  Nov 1806 to June 1826 [A].**
- **First Hansard contribution, 24 Feb 1808, "Committee on Trade and Navigation".
  [A, verbatim]** ***An Inquiry into ... the Orders in Council*, 1808 [A as bibliography]**
  is folded into the same stop: text not re-fetched, no composition site documented.
- **Sir Francis dies 1810 [A, DNB]; Hope & Co's London offices merge into Baring Brothers
  1811 [A].** One stop.
- **Post-Waterloo indemnity loans, 1817-1818. [A]** Oosterlinck, Ureche-Rangau and Vaslin,
  *JEH* (2014), per dossier; meeting locations unconfirmed. **The "six great powers" bon
  mot [R]**, attributed to Richelieu and sourced by Wikipedia to Gibbs's *Complete Peerage*
  (1910), has **no first printed source and no verbatim original wording**; carried as
  tradition inside prose, never in a `quote` field.
- **London Merchants' Petition, 8 May 1820. [A, upgraded this pass]** Hansard, Commons,
  "COMMERCIAL RESTRICTIONS - PETITION OF THE MERCHANTS OF LONDON", cols 165-974, opening
  with his own words. Drafted by Thomas Tooke; the pool's bare "1820" is now exact.

## 6. Houses, and one loan to Buenos Aires

- **The Grange, bought 1817 from Henry Drummond. [A]** **Coordinate corrected to
  51.1220222, -1.1989667** (Wikipedia), which the pool's afterlife lens had right; its
  geography lens gave 51.0989, -1.1878, 2.7 km off and missing the house.
- **Cockerell conservatory date corrected.** "January 1823" is unsupported. Wikipedia:
  design concluded **by June 1823**, construction beginning **the following March**
  (1824); English Heritage dates the Cockerell work to **1823-25**. Dimensions, about 80
  by 50 feet with an Ionic portico, confirmed. Jones & Clark's kitchen-garden metalwork
  (6 Nov 1819 [A]) and Smirke's west wing (1820 [A]) are folded into the same stop.
- **Bath House, 82 Piccadilly, 1821. [A]** Coordinate corrected to **51.506558,
  -0.143524**; the pool's 51.5049, -0.1469 sat 300 m southwest, inside Green Park.
- **Buckenham Tofts, c.1821-22 from Lord Petre. [A]** Coordinate corrected to **52.5203,
  0.7075** from 52.5152, 0.7326. Held until 1869; hall **demolished 1946** after Stanford
  Battle Area damage, not vaguely "later demolished". Carried inside the Thetford stop.
- **Bay House, Alverstoke. [A]** Not acquired: **built new for him in 1838 by Decimus
  Burton**. Coordinate corrected to **50.7867, -1.1647** from 50.7889, -1.1435. Ann
  Louisa's death on 5 December 1848 is confirmed, but **the source does not say she died
  there**, and her death is her stop, not his.
- **Rudhall Manor c.1830 [R]**, dossier only, and **Melchet Court 1835 [R]**,
  landedfamilies only: unresolved property claims, no stops.
- **Buenos Aires, 1824. [R]** Barings issued the first external bond loan for Buenos
  Aires. **No evidence places Alexander himself there**; it was transacted from London.
  The file gives it a **London** stop at the Devonshire Square pin and says the money
  crossed and the banker did not. No atlas file exists for the 1824 loan or any Argentine
  counterparty; it is the corpus's clearest Río de la Plata thread in his record, and thin.

## 7. The property question, 1823-1831

Stated flat, per house rule.

- **15 May 1823, Buxton's motion. [R for Hansard, A for the printed speech]** He accused
  Wilberforce and Buxton of exaggerating colonial conditions and argued for gradual change
  managed by the colonial legislatures. Printed separately, London 1823.
- **The April 1826 petitions were two sittings, not one. [A, corrected]** The pool's
  chronology merged them under 20 April; its geography lens had the dates right. **14 April
  1826**, "SLAVERY IN ANTIGUA - MEMORIAL OF COUNCIL AND HOUSE OF ASSEMBLY", which he said
  had been placed in his hands though he had no connection with the island; **20 April
  1826**, "PETITION OF WEST-INDIA MERCHANTS FOR PROTECTION OF THEIR PROPERTY", where the
  "moderate and well-digested measures" formula is his. Both quoted verbatim in the file.
- **1828: he defended the property right in an enslaved person. [R]** **April 1831:
  immediate emancipation would destroy West Indian capital and force Britain to buy
  slave-produced foreign sugar. [A per the dossier's Hansard account, not re-fetched]**
  One stop, 1831-04-15.
- **He never went to Antigua or any colony.** All London stops.
- **Modern reassessment. [R, access-blocked]** UCL's Legacies of British Slave-ownership
  and related National Gallery-partnered research have reconstructed Baring firm plantation
  mortgages, enslaved collateral, and compensation interests. Both sites returned **HTTP
  403**: real, named projects, passages not read. Cited, never asserted as read.

## 8. Boroughs, Reform, office, barony

- **Callington, June 1826 to July 1831 [A]**; **Thetford, May 1831 to Dec 1832 [A]**, both
  patron-controlled boroughs, while he opposed Grey's bill.
- **May 1832: agreed in principle, after hesitation, to take the Exchequer if Wellington
  could form a government; Wellington failed. [R]** The "Past Chancellors" list confirms
  only that he never held it. **The "thousand devils" anecdote is unverified and appears
  nowhere in the journey file.** **North Essex, Dec 1832 to April 1835 [A]**, his last
  Commons seat, is folded into the same stop; no town anchors the county division.
- **President of the Board of Trade, 15 Dec 1834 to 8 April 1835 [A]; Master of the Mint,
  23 Dec 1834 to 8 April 1835 [A].** Two offices, two start dates; the pool's single
  1834-12-15 covers only the first. Two stops, two pins.
- **Created Baron Ashburton, 10 April 1835 [A]**, taking John Dunning's vacant title.
- **British Museum trustee from 1829 [R]; National Gallery trustee 1835 to death [R].**
  The Gallery's trustee page returned an access error. One stop.

## 9. Washington again, 1842-1843

- **Arrived 4 April 1842. [A]** State Department, Office of the Historian.
- **Correction: four months, not ten.** The pool repeats Wikipedia's "roughly ten months",
  irreconcilable with its own arrival and signing dates. **4 April to 9 August 1842 is four
  months**; the ten-month figure presumably counts from the announcement in late 1841.
- **Ashburton House, 1525 H Street NW [A]**, rented by Webster, a National Historic
  Landmark since 1973 for this residency.
- **Treaty signed 9 August 1842. [A]** Boundary, extradition, joint naval squadrons
  against the slave trade. **Oregon left unresolved**; impressment and the Creole handled
  by understanding, not by article.
- **Thanks voted, Lords 11 April 1843, Commons after partisan debate. [A per dossier, not
  re-fetched]** Palmerston among the accusers. **Two verbatim Lords quotes carried [A]:**
  3 April 1843 and 11 April 1843, the latter in Hansard's indirect third person, which the
  `quote_source` states.

## 10. The currency, and the end

- **9 Feb 1843, Lords, "Distress of the Country". [A, pronoun corrected]** The pool
  printed "we owed it to the protection of the law". Hansard reads "**they** owed it to
  the protection of the law that we were not deluged with wheat from abroad." The
  corrected form is what the file quotes.
- **The contradiction is left standing.** The man who presented the free-trade Merchants'
  Petition in 1820 defends the Corn Laws in 1843 and attacks the rigidity of the currency
  statute in 1847. Not smoothed.
- ***The Financial and Commercial Crisis Considered* (Murray, 1847), 40 pp, at least three
  English editions. [A]** Two verbatim quotes from the archive.org first-edition scan,
  pp. 8 and 27. **The German translation is unverified [R]:** *Beleuchtung der jetzigen
  finanziellen und commerciellen Krisis*, Cotta, Stuttgart and Tübingen, 1847, with **no
  catalogue record reachable this pass.**
- **2 Dec 1847, Lords, "The Currency: Commercial Distress". [A, verbatim]** "The cast-iron
  rule which bound down the Bank in its issue of notes ought to be abrogated."
- **Died at Longleat, 12 May 1848, aged 73. [A]** Hansard's member record and History of
  Parliament give 12 May; **the 1885 DNB gives 13 May.** Flagged, not reconciled.
  **Coordinate corrected to 51.1857, -2.2743**; the pool's two lenses gave 51.1653,
  -2.1965 and 51.1969, -2.1897, both about 6 km out in different directions. Five sons and
  four daughters (DNB). No cause of death in any source consulted. The DNB records a Lords
  tribute from Lansdowne, Brougham and Derby, wording not verified verbatim.
- **The burial is contradictory and no stop is written.** The pool's geography lens asserts
  St John the Evangelist churchyard, Northington, on a not-re-fetched "parish memorial
  record", with coordinates 4.2 km south of the village; its afterlife lens states flatly
  that the DNB gives no burial place and no accessible source located one. **Both cannot
  stand.** The file ends at Longleat and its `date_confidence` records the gap.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found, in either direction.** Baring is not in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing in the
pool required a backward application of his framework: he had no theoretical apparatus.
The modern work that engages him is other people's work about him, handled the standard
way, **cited as sources and `suggested_refs` on Baring's own stops, never staged as a
scene**: Larry Neal's 2024 chapter on the 1803 Amsterdam and 1804 London stops; the Baring
Archive's Louisiana exhibition on the 1803-1804 stops; UCL's Legacies of British
Slave-ownership on the 1823 and 1831 Commons stops; Lawrence's c.1810 portrait on the firm
stop and Healy's 1842-43 portrait on the mission stop. Neither painter gets a scene.

No forward reference was written, and nothing was written into `francis_baring`,
`thomas_jefferson`, or `napoleon`.
