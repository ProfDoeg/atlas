# George H. W. Bush (1924-2018) - research report

*2026-08-11. Compiled for `george_hw_bush.journey.json`. Milton, Massachusetts, to a grave at Texas A&M, by way of Chichi Jima, Odessa, Beijing, Langley, and Kuwait.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the pool itself flags as unconfirmed. Where the lenses disagree, both readings are printed and the disagreement is left open. Where the pool carried an error and corrected itself, the correction is followed and the original recorded, so the change stays auditable. The pool ran in four lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock) plus an afterlife lens; its corrections are not cosmetic, five moving a pin by more than a kilometre, three a date, two an attribution.

---

## I. Greenwich and Andover, 1924-1942

Born 12 June 1924 at the family house on Adams Street, Milton, second son of Prescott Bush and Dorothy Walker Bush, named for his maternal grandfather **[A - Wikipedia; Bush Library biography]**.

**Coordinate contradiction, flagged not resolved.** The geography lens gives 42.2495, -71.0662 for "173 Adams Street", which is the Milton town centroid; `prescott_bush.journey.json` pins the same house at 42.262, -71.055. The father's pin is inherited so the two births share a point; the divergence is recorded, not adjudicated.

Family to Greenwich, 1925 **[A]**. The lens labels the pin "Bush family home, Grove Lane" at 41.0262, -73.6282 and then corrects itself: that is the town centroid, 15 Grove Lane lying about 1.4 km north-northwest. The correction allows moving the pin or dropping the street name. **The second is taken**, because 41.0262, -73.6282 is also the Greenwich pin of `prescott_bush.journey.json`, and keeping it makes the pin shared. Greenwich Country Day through the eighth grade **[A - Bush Library]**, narrated inside that stop. Phillips Academy 1937-1942, captain of baseball and soccer, senior class president **[A - Wikipedia; Phillips Academy archives]**; pin corrected from the Andover town centroid (42.6584, -71.1367) to the campus (about 42.6465, -71.1305).

**Date contradiction inside the chronology lens, resolved by its own correction.** One entry at 1943-06-09 reads "Enlists in the Navy on his 18th birthday and is commissioned an ensign ... at Naval Air Station Corpus Christi". Two events are welded together: enlistment 12 June 1942, his eighteenth birthday and the day of Andover commencement; commissioning 9 June 1943 **[A]**. The journey file separates them.

Meets Barbara Pierce at a Christmas dance in Greenwich, 1941 **[A]**; engaged 1943 **[A]**. The pool names no venue. The stop is pinned at 41.0682, -73.6465, the Round Hill Club coordinate carried by `prescott_bush.journey.json`. **The venue is tradition, not attestation, and is flagged as such in `date_confidence`.** The lens's separate **[R]** entry for boyhood summers at the Pierce house in Rye is not pinned.

## II. The Pacific, 1942-1945

Preflight at Chapel Hill, 1942; primary training at Wold-Chamberlain Field, Minneapolis, 1942-1943 **[A - Naval History and Heritage Command]**; both narrated inside the Corpus Christi stop rather than pinned. Commissioned ensign and designated naval aviator at NAS Corpus Christi, 9 June 1943, three days short of nineteen **[A]**. First combat mission over Wake Island from USS San Jacinto, May 1944 **[A]**; lieutenant junior grade 1 August 1944 **[A]**. **The carrier is not a place**: the geography lens pins it at 15, 150, tags it **[R]**, and notes "the carrier had no single fixed position". The pin is inherited exactly and the caveat repeated.

Shot down over Chichi Jima; both crewmen killed; rescued by USS Finback **[A - NHHC; Wikipedia]**. **Date contradiction, not resolved**: the chronology lens gives September 1944 with no day, the geography lens 2 September 1944. The later is used and the disagreement stands. DFC and Air Medal three times **[A]**; back aboard San Jacinto November 1944 **[A]**. Married Barbara Pierce at Rye, 6 January 1945 **[A]**.

Released from active duty September 1945 after 58 combat missions and 1,228 flight hours **[A]**. **Two problems.** The lens dates the release 1945-09-02, which is the date of the Japanese surrender aboard Missouri, not of his release; its correction gives only the month. And the landing count is 128 in the lens against "126-128" in the correction; the journey file narrates the lower bound and flags the range.

## III. Yale and the Permian Basin, 1945-1959

Yale on the accelerated postwar schedule, economics in two and a half years; DKE president, baseball captain, Phi Beta Kappa **[A - Wikipedia; Yale archives]**; Skull and Bones **[A - geography lens citing Yale archives]**, the society his father entered in 1916 **[A - `prescott_bush.journey.json`]**. George Walker Bush born in New Haven, 1946 **[A, year only]**; the date 6 July 1946 and the pin 41.3083, -72.9279 are inherited byte-identical from `prescott_bush.journey.json`. West Texas from 1948 as an equipment clerk for a Dresser subsidiary; postings through Odessa, Ventura, Bakersfield, Compton, Midland **[A]**. That Prescott Bush sat on the Dresser board comes from the atlas file, not this pool, and is cited accordingly.

Robin Bush born 20 December 1949 **[A]**. **Place contradiction, resolved by the pool's own correction**: the chronology lens says "Texas"; the correction, citing Wikipedia on Pauline Robinson Bush, says Compton, California. The correction is followed; the pin is the centre of Compton, no address being given. She dies of leukemia in New York City, 11 October 1953 **[A - same correction]**, pinned at 40.7641, -73.9568, the Memorial Hospital coordinate `prescott_bush.journey.json` uses for the grandfather's own death in 1972. **The building is not attested by this pool**, which names only the city; the borrowing is flagged in `date_confidence`, as is the seven-month treatment figure, which is traditional.

Bush-Overbey, Midland, 1951 **[A]**; Zapata Petroleum with the Liedtke brothers, 1953, drilling the Jameson field **[A]**; Jeb Bush born Midland 1953 **[A]**; president of Zapata Offshore 1954 **[A]**; discharged from the Naval Reserve as a lieutenant, October 1955 **[A]**; to Houston 1959, befriending James Baker **[A]**.

## IV. Harris County to Langley, 1963-1977

Harris County Republican chairman, February 1963 **[A]**. Senate race against Ralph Yarborough lost 56-44, 1964 **[A]**; **ordering note**, the lens lists it after the 1966 stock sale and the file restores calendar order. Texas's Seventh with 57 percent, 1966, first freshman on Ways and Means since 1904 **[A]**; unopposed 1968 **[A]**; Senate lost to Lloyd Bentsen 54-46, 1970 **[A]**.

Ambassador to the UN 1971-1973, through the Resolution 2758 vote **[A - Wikipedia; UN archives]**. RNC chairman 1973-1974, defending Nixon then urging resignation in August 1974 **[A]**; the 1625 Eye Street pin is tagged **[R]** at source ("per period press accounts; the committee later relocated"). Chief of the Beijing Liaison Office 1974-1975 **[A]**, pin city-level. Director of Central Intelligence 30 January 1976 to 20 January 1977 **[A]**; the lens adds that "Wikipedia notes the U.S. national security apparatus actively supported Operation Condor operations during his tenure" **[A as a statement of what the source says]**. Out of office 1977: First International Bank of Houston, teaching at Rice **[A]**; narrated, not pinned.

## V. The ticket and the vice presidency, 1980-1989

Iowa won 31.5 to 29.4, New Hampshire lost after a disputed debate format, withdrawal in late May **[A]**. **Not pinned**: the pool gives no coordinates for either, and a statewide contest has no honest single point. Running mate at Detroit, 1980 **[A]**; elected with 50.7 percent and 489 of 538 electoral votes **[A]**.

**Interlock near-miss, recorded as a near-miss.** `william_casey.journey.json` reports that its own pooled research claimed Casey brokered the alliance that put Bush on the ticket, and that the file dropped the clause because the cited source did not support it. Twice refused, therefore **[R]**, and not repeated.

**Tag error corrected.** The chronology lens tags the vice presidency itself **[R]** because the Wikipedia fetch was truncated. Its correction is right: a truncated fetch is a gathering artifact, not an evidentiary grade, and 1981-1989 is **[A]**. The correction further warns that "a period spanning the Iran-Contra affair" does improper work and that any claim about his role needs its own citation.

**What the pool does and does not support on Iran-Contra.** Supported: North ran the covert-supply operation from an Old Executive Office Building desk from 1981, and McFarlane flew to Tehran with a cake and an inscribed Bible in May 1986 **[A - `oliver_north.journey.json`]**; Khashoggi was the arms middleman, interviewed by the Tower Commission in February 1987 **[A - `adnan_khashoggi.journey.json`]**; Bush said publicly he was "out of the loop -- no operational role", and his diary of 4-5 November 1986 reads "I'm one of the few people that know fully the details ..." **[A - National Security Archive EBB 210, ellipsis the Archive's own]**. Not supported: the Walsh report finding that Bush knew of the arms-for-hostages transfers, which the pool's correction names as the stronger available claim and says needs its own citation. **It is therefore not asserted.** The proximity of Bush's office to North's desk is tagged **[R]** by the interlock lens ("inferred proximity; the file does not itself state Bush's office location"); the stop is built on that inference and says so.

## VI. The presidency, 1989-1993

Sworn in 20 January 1989, Quayle vice president **[A - Miller Center; Wikipedia]**; inaugural quotes verified against the Avalon Project transcript. State visit to Beijing February 1989, weeks before Tiananmen **[A]**, narrated rather than pinned. Malta summit aboard the Maxim Gorky, 2 December 1989 **[A]**. Operation Just Cause ordered 20 December 1989, invasion at 00:46, Noriega surrendering 3 January 1990 **[A - Miller Center; `arthur_ben.journey.json`]**; Bush directs from Washington and does not travel to Panama during the fighting **[A]**, and the Panama City pin is inherited byte-identical (section IX). ADA, Immigration Act, Clean Air Act amendments, all 1990 **[A]**; the tax pledge reversed by signature, 1990 **[A]**.

**Contradiction inside one entry, split.** The lens files "Appoints David Souter and Clarence Thomas" under a single 1990 date. Correction: Souter nominated July 1990, confirmed October 1990; Thomas nominated 1 July 1991, confirmed 52-48 on 15 October 1991, sworn in 23 October. One stop, dated to the Thomas confirmation, narrates both.

Gulf War: Kuwait taken 2 August 1990; air campaign 16 January 1991 **[A - Miller Center transcript]**; occupation ended 27 February 1991 **[A]**. Loses to Bill Clinton November 1992 after negotiating NAFTA, ratified after he leaves **[A]**. Baidoa, New Year's Eve 1992, visiting Operation Restore Hope troops **[R - Wikipedia plus contemporaneous news accounts]**; carried as **[R]**.

**Quote flagged [R].** "This will not stand, this aggression against Kuwait" is carried by the quotes lens as **[R]**, "frequently cited in secondary sources but not fetched from a primary transcript by me in this session", dated 5 August 1990. Used with the tag preserved. **Quote corrected**: "I want a kinder, gentler nation" is a misquote by one word, the American Presidency Project transcript of 18 August 1988 reading "Well, I am moved. I want a kinder and gentler nation."

## VII. Afterlife, 1993-2018

Kuwait as a private citizen, April 1993; investigators later conclude Iraqi intelligence plotted to kill him during the trip **[A]**. Houston's airport renamed effective 2 May 1997, not 1 October as the afterlife lens first had it **[A per its own correction]**; not pinned.

**Library dedication, 6 November 1997: two errors corrected.** (1) The geography lens's 30.6096, -96.3344 and the afterlife lens's 30.6086, -96.355 are both wrong; the site is at 30.5966, -96.3534, and all three atlas values are collapsed onto it. (2) "Bush and four other presidents" overstates: four in total (Bush, Clinton, Carter, Ford), Reagan absent and represented by Nancy Reagan, and the pool flags even this as needing a citation.

CIA headquarters designated the George Bush Center for Intelligence, 26 April 1999 **[A]**. **Mechanism corrected**: designated by Congress in the Intelligence Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 1999, not on DCI Tenet's recommendation; Tenet presided. CVN-77 commissioned 10 January 2009 **[A]**; not pinned.

Birthday jumps at 75, 80, 85 and 90 **[A]**; the 90th is placed at 43.3812, -70.4767 by the afterlife lens, while Walker's Point is at 43.3433, -70.4594 after correction of the geography lens's 43.345, -70.446, which fell in the Atlantic 1.1 km offshore. The estate pin is used, inherited from `prescott_bush.journey.json`. **Ownership chain flagged**: George Herbert Walker bought the point in 1902; Bush owned it only from his 1977 inheritance.

Not used, and why. The colorful socks, tagged [R] at source as obituary anecdote without primary attestation. The 1963 memo legend **[R]**, which the pool corrects to an FBI memorandum signed by Hoover on 29 November 1963, surfacing in 1988 through Joseph McBride in *The Nation*, the Agency answering that the reference is to George William Bush, a junior employee who so testified by affidavit: a debunked legend rather than a stop, recorded so the decision stays visible. Bibliography corrections: *All the Best* (1999) is not posthumous; *Heartbeat* is 2001, not 2015.

## VIII. Death and funeral, 2018

Died at home in Houston, 30 November 2018, aged 94, of vascular parkinsonism, seven months after Barbara (d. 17 April 2018) **[A]**. The "Mission complete" photograph of the service dog Sully: **date corrected**, posted by spokesman Jim McGrath on 2 December while the casket lay in Houston, not 5 December as the lens had it, since by the fifth the casket was at the National Cathedral and the entry as dated was impossible. Lies in state in the Rotunda 3-5 December, twelfth president so honored **[A - Architect of the Capitol]**.

State funeral, Washington National Cathedral, 5 December **[A]**. **Contradiction between lenses, resolved in favour of the afterlife lens.** The geography lens names three eulogists and omits Jon Meacham, who gave the principal eulogy; the afterlife lens has all four, and the geography lens's own correction concedes it: "the afterlife lens has this right and the geography lens does not." Coordinates differ, 38.9295, -77.0706 against 38.9315, -77.0709, both within about 100 m; the file uses 38.9305, -77.0708 and records the spread.

Second service, St. Martin's, Houston, 6 December **[A]**; **coordinate spread flagged**, 29.7565, -95.4602 (geography) against 29.746, -95.4573 (afterlife), about 1.3 km apart, the geography value used. **Funeral train, route corrected**: the geography lens has it leaving Houston and pins 30.1795, -95.885, west of any plausible route near Waller County, where the departure was **Spring, Texas** (30.0799, -95.4172), the first presidential funeral train since Eisenhower in 1969. Buried on the library grounds beside Barbara and Robin, whose remains came from Putnam Cemetery, Greenwich, in 2000 **[A]**; coordinate corrected as above.

## X. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **The Walsh report** was never fetched. Bush's knowledge of the arms-for-hostages transfers is unresolved here; the file says only what the diary and the public statement say.
2. **The Noriega 1976 sign-off** is flagged inside `manuel_noriega.journey.json` as tradition, not FOIA-confirmed. Carried as tradition.
3. **1980 Iowa and New Hampshire** have no coordinates in the pool and are narrated, not pinned. So too **the RNC street address** (press-derived, tagged [R] at source) and **the Beijing liaison office address** (unconfirmed, pin city-level).
4. **Bohemian Grove** membership is tagged [R] ("specific visit dates are not part of the official presidential record"). Not pinned.
5. **The Walker South Carolina plantation** entry is an admitted placeholder on downtown Charleston, over 150 km from Duncannon Plantation in Barnwell County. The correction says to source Duncannon and move the point or drop the entry. **Dropped.** Memoir **translation** data is likewise unconfirmed.
6. **Quotes cluster.** Every verbatim quote the pool verified falls between 1986 and 1991, plus one 2018 caption. Seven stops of forty-five carry quotes, all in that window. There is no verified quotation from the Navy years, the oil years, the UN, the RNC, Beijing, or Langley, and none has been invented to fill the space.

**Context supplied outside the pool**, listed for audit: Prescott Bush's Dresser board seat and his 1916 Skull and Bones tapping (`prescott_bush.journey.json`); the Berlin Wall having opened three weeks before Malta; Noriega being an American prisoner by 1999 (`manuel_noriega.journey.json`); the seven-month duration of Robin's treatment, traditional and flagged.
