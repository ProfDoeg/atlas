# Hal Finney (1956-2014): research report

*Harold Thomas Finney II. Games programmer, main author of PGP 2.0, builder of the first cryptographic anonymous remailer, author of RPOW, the first person besides Satoshi Nakamoto to run Bitcoin and the recipient of the first bitcoin transaction. Died of ALS at Phoenix, Arizona; cryopreserved by Alcor at Scottsdale. No grave.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or synthesis without a dated primary source. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Sources that could not be read are named with the reason.

---

## 1. Origin and schooling (1956-1979)

- Born **4 May 1956 at Coalinga, California**, to Virginia and Harold Thomas Finney, a petroleum engineer **[A: Wikipedia, "Hal Finney (computer scientist)"]**.
- **Gap flagged.** The pool carried a marker titled "Coalinga District Hospital." No consulted source names a birth venue; Wikipedia says only "born in Coalinga, California," and the Coalinga article lists no such hospital. The venue is dropped; the coordinates 36.1397, -120.36 survive because they are the town centroid.
- Graduated **Arcadia High School, 1974** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Month not recorded; June is convention.
- **Coordinate correction.** The pool pinned the school at 34.1367, -118.0353, seven hundred metres north of campus in residential streets. Arcadia High School, 180 Campus Drive, is at 34.130563, -118.036852 per its Wikipedia infobox; the journey uses 34.1306, -118.0369.
- **Error corrected.** The pool's prose called Arcadia "the town where he will later spend his final years." It was not: Finney lived at **Temple City**, an adjacent but distinct city, and died at Phoenix.
- **BS in electrical engineering, California Institute of Technology, 1979** **[A: Wikipedia]**. The 1974 start is inferred from the high-school date plus a four-year course; it is not attested.

## 2. The games years (1978-1983)

- **[A: Wikipedia, "Intellivision"]**: "Hal Finney of APh contributed sound and music processing routines to the Exec." The Exec is the console's onboard operating software; its design phase is 1978. Finney was an employee of **APh Technological Consulting**, the outside contractor Mattel used, **not of Mattel**.
- **Error flagged.** The pool claimed Finney entered the games industry in **1979** and programmed four titles "for Mattel's Intellivision console." Employer and year are both wrong, and the titles are not one console: **Astrosmash** (1981), **Space Attack** (1981), **Armor Ambush** (1982) and **Adventures of Tron** (1983) span Intellivision and Atari 2600. No title dates to 1979.
- **Gap.** No reachable source gives Finney's role on any single cartridge, his job title at APh, his years there, or APh's address. The journey's Hawthorne pin marks **Mattel's Intellivision site**, with campa and date_confidence both saying he worked for the contractor and that APh's address is unknown.

## 3. Cypherpunk (1991-1995)

- **PGP 1.0 released by Phil Zimmermann, 1991, Boulder, Colorado** **[A: Wikipedia, "Pretty Good Privacy"]**: handed to friends for uploading to the Internet. Zimmermann came under federal criminal investigation as an arms exporter **[A: via interlock material and the standard account; the case file itself was not fetched]**.
- **Finney is the main author of PGP 2.0 and of the core crypto libraries of later versions** **[A: Wikipedia, "Cypherpunk"]**, corroborated by the Nakamoto Institute ("developed some of the earliest PGP code") and by Finney: "I got my start in crypto working on an early version of PGP, working closely with Phil Zimmermann."
- **Cypherpunks list founded 1992** by Eric Hughes, Timothy C. May and John Gilmore, meeting monthly at Gilmore's company Cygnus Solutions **[A: Wikipedia, "Cypherpunk"]**. Finney active on the list from 1992 and running **two anonymous remailers** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **First cryptographically based anonymous remailer** **[A: Nakamoto Institute biography; Finney's own claim]**.
- **Citation corrected.** The pool sourced two quotations to "Why Remailers?, Extropy no. 9." The piece is titled **"Why Remailers I"**, dated 15 November 1992, originally at finney.org/~hal/why_rem1.html, with no Extropy credit on it. Correct citation: https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/why-remailers-i/
- **"Protecting Privacy with Electronic Cash", Extropy no. 10, 1 January 1993** **[A: archived, Nakamoto Institute]**. This attribution, unlike the previous one, holds.
- **Location gap, flagged hard.** The pool put the early-1990s remailer at Finney's Temple City house. **His Temple City residency is documented only from about 2004** (Greenberg, 2014: about ten years). No consulted source gives his 1992 address. The journey pins the remailer, PGP 2.0, the Extropy essays and the Extropians at a regional Los Angeles marker (34.0522, -118.2437), calling the placement a convention each time.
- **Extropians [R]**. Widely reported in profiles; no consulted source gives a date, a location or a posting record. The pool tagged this [R] and it stays [R], dated 1994 by convention, because it is the antecedent of the Alcor arrangement twenty years later.

## 4. The export war (1994-1995)

- **[A: Wikipedia, "Damien Doligez"]**: "In 1994, Hal Finney issued a challenge on the cypherpunk mailing [list] to read an encrypted SSLv2 session."
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The pool dated the Netscape export-cipher work to **1995**. Wikipedia's Finney article carries **no year at all** and cites only a 1996 Washington Post column; the 1994 date comes from a different article. Both appear in the journey, the challenge at 1994 and the break at 1995, each flagged in date_confidence.
- **The break**: Damien Doligez scanned half the forty-bit keyspace in eight days on machines at Inria, the ENS and the Ecole polytechnique, **finishing about two hours behind the winning team** **[A: Wikipedia, "Damien Doligez"]**. The journey says so rather than crediting Doligez with the win. **Gap**: the winning team is not named in anything reached, and the month is not attested (the journey's August is a convention).

## 5. The company years (1997-2011)

- **December 1997**: Network Associates acquires PGP Inc.; Zimmermann and team become NAI employees **[A: Wikipedia, "Pretty Good Privacy"]**.
- **October 2001**: NAI announces it is selling the PGP assets and suspends development **[A: same]**.
- **August 2002**: the PGP assets are purchased out of NAI **[A: same]**.
- **Error corrected.** The pool said "former PGP team members, presumably including Finney, form PGP Corporation." **PGP Corporation was established in June 2002 by Jon Callas and Phil Dunkelberger (CEO), backed by Doll Capital Management and Venrock** **[A: Wikipedia, "PGP Corporation"]**. Finney was **not a founder**: "When Phil decided to start PGP Corporation, I was one of the first hires."
- **Error corrected.** The pool called Finney "PGP's second developer" employed **1991-2011**, sourced to "Bitcoin and Me." The post says no such thing. There is **no attested start date** at any PGP company before 2002; only the 2011 retirement is documented. The 1991 front end is dropped.
- **Coordinate corrected.** The pool pinned PGP at 37.5485, -122.3122, the city of **San Mateo**. Wikipedia has PGP Corporation **based in Menlo Park** (approx. 37.4530, -122.1817), thirteen kilometres southeast. Menlo Park sits in San Mateo County, so the pool's county label survived while its pin was on the wrong city. The journey uses 37.453, -122.1817.
- **June 2010**: Symantec acquires PGP Corporation **[A: Wikipedia, "Pretty Good Privacy"]**. **August 2019**: Broadcom announces its purchase of Symantec's enterprise security division, the PGP line included **[A: same]**.
- **Early 2011**: retirement. Finney's own words: "fatigue and voice problems forced me to retire in early 2011" **[A]**.

## 6. RPOW (2004)

- **RPOW, Reusable Proofs of Work**, announced to the cypherpunks list, timestamped **Sun, 15 Aug 2004 10:43:09 -0700** **[A: Nakamoto Institute library copy; Wikipedia dates the first reusable proof-of-work system to 2004]**.
- Finney's own description **[A]**: "This system receives hashcash as a Proof of Work (POW) token, and in exchange creates RSA-signed tokens which I call Reusable Proof of Work (RPOW) tokens." And: "The RPOW server is running on a high-security processor card, the IBM 4758 Secure Cryptographic Coprocessor, validated to FIPS-140 level 4."
- **Error corrected.** The pool had Finney "hosting the server from his own house." No source says this, and the design rests on the coprocessor's **remote attestation** rather than on where the box sat. Dropped.
- **Attribution corrected.** The pool made RPOW a descendant of Adam Back's Hashcash alone. The Nakamoto Institute calls it "a prototype for a digital cash based on Nick Szabo's theory of collectibles." Both lineages are in the journey.
- **Correspondence with Wei Dai and Nick Szabo [A as fact, date invented].** "Bitcoin and Me": "I was lucky enough to meet and extensively correspond with both Wei Dai and Nick Szabo." The pool dated this **2008**; the post gives no date, and RPOW (2004) already builds on Szabo, so the exchange precedes 2008. The journey states it without a date.
- **Claim dropped entirely.** The pool had Finney following b-money and bit gold on cryptography lists in 1998, sourced to "Bitcoin and Me." The post says nothing of the kind. Wei Dai's b-money circulated in **November 1998**, but Szabo's **bit gold was not published until a December 2005 blog post**, so it was not on any mailing list in 1998. The 1998 Temple City placement was anachronistic besides.

## 7. Bitcoin (2008-2009)

- **31 October 2008**: the white paper posted to the cryptography list at metzdowd.com **[A]**.
- **7 November 2008**, Finney's reply **[A: metzdowd archive, 2008-November/014827.html]**: "Bitcoin seems to be a very promising idea. I like the idea of basing security on the assumption that the CPU power of honest participants outweighs that of the attacker."
- **Quotation repaired.** The pool truncated a third quotation mid-sentence and closed it with a fabricated full stop. The archive reads: "...does not clearly specify the various rules and verifications **that the participants in the system would have to follow**." The journey paraphrases rather than reproducing the cut version.
- **13 November 2008** **[A]**: "solving the global, massively decentralized database problem is arguably the harder part."
- **January 2009**: Finney runs the software. His own words **[A: "Bitcoin and Me"]**: "I think I was the first person besides Satoshi to run bitcoin. I mined block 70-something, and I was the recipient of the first bitcoin transaction, when Satoshi sent ten coins to me as a test." He stopped because "it made my computer run hot, and the fan noise bothered me."
- **Manufactured contradiction removed.** The pool flagged a conflict between Wikipedia's 12 January 2009 and en.bitcoin.it's 9 January 2009. There is none: **9 January is his first mined block**, **12 January is the transaction**. The pool also wrote Wikipedia's date as "January 12, **1909**", its own typing error. Each event gets its own stop.
- **Gap.** Which block Finney mined is not established; he says "70-something" and no source consulted resolves it against the chain.

## 8. Illness, publicity, death (2009-2014)

- **August 2009**: ALS diagnosis, while in peak physical condition and training for marathons **[A: "Bitcoin and Me"; en.bitcoin.it]**. "In August, 2009, I was given the diagnosis of ALS, also called Lou Gehrig's disease, after the famous baseball player who got it."
- **5 October 2009**: public announcement in the LessWrong post "Dying Outside" **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **2010**: rediscovery of the 2009 coins, by then carrying real value; moved to an offline wallet for his heirs **[A: "Bitcoin and Me"]**. No month attested.
- **19 March 2013, 08:40:02 PM**: "Bitcoin and Me (Hal Finney)", Bitcointalk topic 155054 **[A]**. "Today, I am essentially paralyzed. I am fed through a tube, and my breathing is assisted through another tube." Closing: "I'm comfortable with my legacy."
- **Error corrected, the ugliest in the pool.** Two lenses rendered Finney's "It's very slow, probably **50 times slower** than I was before" as working "at roughly **50 times his former speed**." That inverts the sentence and turns a description of catastrophic disability into a boast. The journey quotes him directly.
- **Error corrected.** The pool put the eyetracker years at "Santa Barbara / Phoenix area" and the January 2009 node at Santa Barbara (34.4208, -119.6982). He was at **Temple City** throughout, a hundred and fifty kilometres away. The journey uses the Santa Barbara pin for one thing only, the marathon fundraising, which belongs there.
- **Claim demoted.** The pool called "Bitcoin and Me" his **final post**. It is not: he went on replying in the same thread afterwards. The journey calls it his most-cited document and nothing more.
- **2013**: extortion demands and a swatting incident against Finney and his wife Fran **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Gap**: no month, no case detail, no arrest or outcome in anything reachable.
- **6 March 2014**: Newsweek's cover story by Leah McGrath Goodman names Dorian Satoshi Nakamoto, who lived a few blocks from Finney at Temple City **[A: via Wikipedia, "Dorian Nakamoto"]**.
- **March 2014**: Juola and Associates, commissioned by Andy Greenberg of Forbes, find Finney's writing **the closest stylistic match yet found** to the Nakamoto texts among candidates tested. Greenberg's fuller investigation, using private email, wallet histories and an interview, concludes the opposite, and that Finney **did not know** Dorian Nakamoto. Finney denied being Satoshi **[A as reported; Forbes not re-fetched, reaching this report via Wikipedia, "Satoshi Nakamoto"]**.
- **Contradiction left standing.** The stylometry points at Finney; Greenberg's document review points away. The journey states both and adjudicates neither. The general "Finney was or assisted Satoshi" tradition is tagged **[R]** throughout, as the pool itself tagged it.
- **28 August 2014**: died at **Phoenix, Arizona**, of complications of ALS, aged 58 **[A: Wikipedia, citing Nathaniel Popper's New York Times obituary]**.
- **Claim dropped.** The pool asserted he had "relocated near Alcor's facility in his final months so that cryopreservation could begin within minutes of legal death." No reachable source establishes that motive, and Alcor's own case pages return **HTTP 403**. The journey records the death at Phoenix as fact and says the reason for being there is not on the record.

## 9. Alcor, and the absence of a grave

- Cryopreserved by the **Alcor Life Extension Foundation, 7895 East Acoma Drive Suite 110, Scottsdale, Arizona**, per arrangements made in advance **[A: Wikipedia; en.bitcoin.it]**.
- **Gap, stated as a gap.** Whether the preservation is **whole body or neuro** is not established by anything reachable. The pool's chronology lens flagged this as open; its geography lens then wrote "his neurally preserved remains" anyway. Alcor's pages return HTTP 403. The journey asserts nothing here.
- **Coordinate reconciliation.** The pool carried **three points** for one building: 33.6119, -111.9075; 33.5722, -111.9114 (five kilometres south, in central Scottsdale); and 33.6174, -111.911. Wikipedia's Alcor infobox gives 33.61737, -111.91093. The journey inherits **33.6174, -111.911** byte-identical from ralph_merkle.journey.json.
- **No grave [R]**. No burial, headstone or memorial site appears in any source consulted, and none is expected. The absence is recorded as the fact rather than as a research failure.

## 10. Interlocks with travelers already in the atlas

- **ralph_merkle**: Merkle signed his Alcor contract in 1989, in good health; Finney was preserved at the same address in 2014. Merkle's 1992 "The Technical Feasibility of Cryonics" coined **information-theoretic death** **[R as to its bearing on Finney's reasoning; he is not recorded citing Merkle]**. Named at two stops; coordinates inherited from his file.
- **diffie**, **hellman**, **rivest_shamir_adleman**: PGP's public-key layer is RSA; behind RSA stands the Diffie-Hellman-Merkle patent 4,200,770. That patent dispute ran through the same years PGP's export was a federal criminal matter. Named together at the PGP 2.0 stop.
- **gauss**: modular arithmetic over finite fields, set out in the Disquisitiones (1801), is the machinery under both the RSA inside PGP and the elliptic-curve signatures that carried Nakamoto's ten coins to Finney. Same campa.
- **richard_feynman**: Feynman held his Caltech professorship from 1950/1952 to 1988; Finney was an undergraduate there 1974-1979. **[R]** as a crossing: no source puts them in a room, and the journey says so. The Pasadena pin 34.1377, -118.1253 is inherited byte-identical from richard_feynman.journey.json, shared there with einstein.journey.json and robert_oppenheimer.journey.json.
- **Queued but not yet researched, so not named as travelers**: Phil Zimmermann, Nick Szabo, Satoshi Nakamoto. They appear in the campa as historical persons, not as cross-references.
