# José Martí (1853-1895), research report

*2026-08-24. Written from the verified research pool supplied by the pipeline. Companion file: `jose_marti.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops, 12 quotes.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or unconfirmed · **CONTRADICTION** recorded, not adjudicated.

The pool arrived with a corrector's second pass over the first researcher's findings, overturning eleven items, several of them coordinate errors of one to eight kilometres. Those overturns are treated here as the current record, and every corrected or reconstructed pin says so in its own `date_confidence`.

## 1. Havana and the colony, 1853-1871

- Born José Julián Martí Pérez, 28 January 1853, Havana, to Mariano Martí (Valencia, colonial police) and Leonor Pérez (Canary Islands). **[A: en.wikipedia, corroborated across every consulted source]**
- The house: Calle Leonor Pérez, formerly Paula, no. 314, between Egido and Picota, barrio of San Francisco de Paula; built c. 1810 and numbered 41; family resident until 1856. **[A: EcuRed]** *Coordinate gap:* EcuRed gives no decimals, and the pool's 23.1372, -82.3538 lands a kilometre north near Plaza del Cristo. The file uses 23.131, -82.348, reconstructed from the street address beside the Estación Central. **Not gazetteer-checked. [R on the pin]**
- Baptism, 12 February 1853, Iglesia del Santo Ángel Custodio. **[A: es.wikipedia on the church, which states the date and gives 23.1417, -82.3558, about 600 m from the pool's approximation]**
- Hanábana, the boy travelling with his father in Matanzas and seeing enslaved labour treated as it was. **[R: the pool itself calls it an episode "polished by retrospective patriotic narration"]** *Cut for the stop ceiling, not for falsity;* if restored it goes at roughly 22.5, -81.42, flagged as tradition.
- Mendive's school, 1865; Mendive later arrested and deported. **[A: en.wikipedia]** Building unconfirmed, pin approximate **[R on the pin]**. Poem in *El Álbum* of Guanabacoa, April 1868 **[R]**; *Abdala* in the single issue of *La Patria Libre*, 1869 **[A]**; Ten Years' War opens 10 October 1868 **[A]**.
- Arrested 21 October 1869 at sixteen over a letter found in Fermín Valdés Domínguez's house; convicted of *infidencia*; six years at hard labour, 1870. **[A: en.wikipedia, plus an independent summary of the standard timeline]** The shackle injury in the San Lázaro quarries: imprisonment and hard labour **[A]**, the specific groin and leg injury **[R]**, flagged by the first researcher as unverified against any primary source. The injury is the physical hinge of the whole iconography and rests on secondary literature only.
- Isla de Pinos, 13 October 1870, the Sardá estate, then commutation to deportation. **[A: es.wikipedia]** Pin is Nueva Gerona town centre, estate site unconfirmed. **[R on the pin]** Sails for Cádiz on the *Guipúzcoa*, January 1871. **[A]**

## 2. Spain, 1871-1874

- Cádiz landing and an article in *La Soberanía Nacional*; *El presidio político en Cuba*, Madrid, July 1871; lodging at Calle Desengaño 10. **[A]**
- Registration at the Universidad Central de Madrid, March 1871 **[R: carried as reconstruction, not a matriculation record]**. The Cuban flag hung from the balcony on Calle Concepción Jerónima, February 1873 **[A]**. Licentiate in Civil and Canon Law, Zaragoza, June 1874, then Philosophy and Letters **[A: both Wikipedias]**; pin approximate to the historic university quarter **[R on the pin]**.
- Paris, late 1874, and Le Havre in December. Passage **[A]**; the translation of Hugo's *Mes fils* **[A: dossier bibliography]**; the meeting with Hugo **[R]**, which the pool calls thinly documented and which no fetched source supports.

## 3. Mexico and Guatemala, 1875-1878

- Veracruz, 8 February 1875. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- Mexico City, *Revista Universal*, first article 2 March 1875 **[R: carried as reconstruction]**; friendship with Manuel Mercado begins, and he meets Carmen Zayas-Bazán in December 1875 **[A]**. Guatemala under Justo Rufino Barrios, heading the literature faculty by 29 May 1877 **[A]**; the alias Julián Pérez and the Yucatán passage **[R]**.

**CONTRADICTION 1, the marriage.** en.wikipedia: 1878, on Havana's Calle Tulipán. EcuRed and es.wikipedia: 20 December 1877, Mexico City. The corrector judged en.wikipedia wrong, Tulipán being where the couple lived *after* returning to Cuba in 1878. The file follows the two Spanish-language sources and states the contradiction in the stop's confidence field. I did not open either page myself; this is the corrector's finding taken on trust.

- María García Granados dies, May 1878. **[A]** That she died of love: **[R]**, a legend generated by *Versos sencillos* IX, written thirteen years later. No medical record is cited anywhere in the pool in either direction. Site unconfirmed, pin approximate to Guatemala City.

## 4. The Zanjón interval and the second deportation, 1878-1880

- Return under the amnesty; blocked from practising law under his own name; organizing with Juan Gualberto Gómez; son José Francisco born 22 November 1878. **[A]** Second arrest September 1879, deported to Spain. **[A: en.wikipedia timeline; the day, 17 September, from the pool's geography lens]**
- New York, January 1880; Steck Hall; Calixto García's committee. **[A]** The address, 11 East 14th Street, comes from period directories and was not re-verified. **[R on the pin]** Caracas, 1881, *Revista Venezolana*, two issues, departure forced by Guzmán Blanco. **[A: es.wikipedia]**

## 5. New York, 1881-1891

- The Miyares-Mantilla boardinghouse. **[A]** Addresses unpinned, coordinates approximate to Manhattan **[R on the pin]**. The disputed paternity of María Mantilla is left in the campa at the width the evidence supports.
- *Ismaelillo* and the start of the *La Nación* (Buenos Aires) correspondence, 1882; *Amistad funesta* as "Adelaida Ral," 1885; the *Ramona* translation, 1888; *La Edad de Oro*, four numbers from July 1889. **[A]**
- Haymarket coverage, 1886-1887, shifting from suspicion toward sympathy. **[R]** Attested in the dossier; neither pass located the chronicle text. The file keeps the stop but pins it in New York, where the writing happened, not Chicago, and says so in the confidence field.
- First International American Conference, Washington, 1889-1890, attended as a journalist. **[A]** The Argentine consulship in New York, 1890-91, is dossier-derived only, unchecked against any diplomatic archive, and is load-bearing for the Buenos Aires stop. **[A per the dossier, R as evidence]**
- Catskills, 1890. **[R]** Rests on the dossier and the martiano literature; the usually named site, Twilight Park at Haines Falls, is not tied to him by any fetched source. Coordinates corrected to 42.19583, -74.09694 (2.5 km off in the pool); month is a placeholder.
- *Nuestra América*, Revista Ilustrada de Nueva York, 1 January 1891, reprinted in *El Partido Liberal* 30 January. **[A]**

**CONTRADICTION 2, the date of *Versos sencillos*.** Both Wikipedias give October 1891, EcuRed August 1891. Left open. It matters because his wife and son sailed on 27 August 1891 **[A]**, and the book postdates that departure only on the later date.

## 6. The party, 1891-1895

- Ybor City, November 1891, "Con todos y para el bien de todos" and "Los pinos nuevos," at the invitation of the Club Ignacio Agramonte. **[A: es.wikipedia]** *Patria* founded, March 1892. **[A]**
- Key West, January 1892, the clubs and the drafting of the party bases. **[A]** The day, 5 January, is in the pool but **not** supported by the San Carlos source, which says only "beginning in January 1892." **[R on the day]** Coordinates corrected to 516 Duval Street, 24.555279, -81.802801 (2.3 km off in the pool).

**CONTRADICTION 3, the Delegado election.** Wikipedia dates it 8 April 1892 at Tampa; other sources give 10 April 1892. Same week. Not adjudicated; the file folds the election into the Ybor City stop and records the variance.

- The Tampa illness, 16 December 1892, recovery in the Pedroso household. Illness and recovery **[A]**; the poisoning by Spanish agents **[R]**, with no perpetrator, no forensic finding and no prosecution documented anywhere. A contemporary suspicion consolidated into patriotic legend, and the file says so.
- Rubén Darío met at Hardman Hall, 24 May 1893 **[A]**; "Mi raza," *Patria*, 1893 **[A]**; Montecristi, meeting with Máximo Gómez, 3 June 1893 **[A]**. *Gap:* no `ruben_dario.journey.json` exists, so a documented, citable relation has nothing to link to. Queue candidate.

**CONTRADICTION 4, Fernandina.** The pool's chronology lens says seized January 1895; EcuRed dates the failure of the Plan de Fernandina to 25 December 1894, with the seizure of the *Lagonda*, *Amadís* and *Baracoa* following in mid-January 1895. The file dates the stop 1895-01-12, inside the seizure window, and says in the confidence field that it marks a process rather than a day.

- Order for the rising signed with Enrique Collazo and José María Rodríguez, 29 January 1895; risings across the island 24 February 1895. **[A]**

## 7. The last campaign, 1895

- Manifiesto de Montecristi, 25 March 1895. **[A]** The last letter to Leonor Pérez the same day: **[A per the dossier]**, corroborated by the standard chronology, unchecked against the manuscript. Departure from Montecristi and Cap-Haïtien, 1 April 1895 **[A]**; *cut for the stop ceiling*, Cap-Haïtien has no pin of its own and the crossing is carried inside the landing campa.
- Landing at Playitas de Cajobabo, 11 April 1895, with Gómez, Ángel Guerra, Francisco Borrero, César Salas and Marcos del Rosario. **[A: EcuRed, which confirms all six names]** The pool's 20.08, -74.72 sat inland, about 10 km west-northwest of Imías, on the wrong side of the municipality; EcuRed places the site roughly 17 km east of Imías on the south coast. File uses the reconstructed 20.035, -74.475. **Not survey-checked. [R on the pin]** Major general, 15 April, a political rather than a military act. **[A]**
- La Mejorana, 5 May 1895, with Gómez and Maceo. **[R on the content]**: no agreed minutes; his own diary records disagreement; every account of what was said is a partisan reconstruction. Pin approximate.
- Letter to Manuel Mercado begun 18 May 1895, unfinished **[A: es.wikisource]**. Death at Dos Ríos 19 May 1895, Spanish column under Colonel José Ximénez de Sandoval, wounds to face and chest **[A: Adelante.cu]**; coordinates reconstructed from es.wikipedia's relative description (100 m northeast of the Contramaestre, 2.1 km southeast of the present village), not a stated figure **[R on the pin]**.
- Remanganaguas, 20 May 1895, shallow burial beneath a Spanish soldier's body, then exhumation and examination by Dr. Valencia Fortún. **[A: Adelante.cu]** Pin from relative geography only. **[R on the pin]**
- Reburial at Santa Ifigenia, May 1895. Month and year **[A]**; the day, 27 May, **[R]**, unconfirmed against a burial register. Coordinates corrected from 20.0043, -75.8306 (repeated four times in the afterlife lens, 3.6 km wrong) to 20.0359, -75.8407.

## 8. Afterlife

- Mausoleum: Benavent and Ibarra with Mario Santí; built 1947-1951; inaugurated 30 June 1951; *lucernario* admitting direct sun onto the sarcophagus, keyed to the image of dying facing the sun; national monument 10 October 1978; conservation prize 2003; honour guard since 2002. **[A: es.wikipedia]** *Distinction the file preserves:* 1978 belongs to the **mausoleum**; the **cemetery** was declared a monument 7 February 1937, ratified 20 May 1979.
- Guantanamera: *Versos sencillos* set to Joseíto Fernández's guajira; Seeger 1963; the Sandpipers 1966, Billboard no. 9. **[A]** A `suggested_ref`, not a scene: the recordings are other people's stops.
- UNESCO: the José Martí Pérez fonds inscribed on the Memory of the World **International Register in 2005**, on a Cuban nomination of 2004. **[A: unesco.org]** The dossier's 2023 is wrong; the first researcher's "2002 regional plus 2005 international" is half wrong, the 2002 half not being on the UNESCO page; the 2,418-document count is on no fetched page **[R]**. A `suggested_ref` only.
- Central Park equestrian by Anna Hyatt Huntington, cast 1959, dedicated 1965, bronze on Barre granite, a copy of the Havana version. **[A]** *Location corrected:* the source places it at Central Park South opposite Avenue of the Americas, the **Artists' Gate**, with the Bolívar and San Martín equestrians; **Merchants' Gate (Columbus Circle) is wrong** and appears in two of the pool's lenses. **Not a stop:** Martí never saw it, and it belongs to Huntington.
- Jardín de los Poetas bust, Palermo: bronze by Alberto Lescay Merencio, donated by the Cuban Embassy, authorized by a city law of 7 November 2002, decree 1600 of 29 November. **[A: defensoria.org.ar]** The Rosedal was inaugurated 24 November 1914, monuments restored 1994-96 and 2008; **no source gives an installation date for the bust itself** (gap). *Coordinate mess:* the geography lens carried +34.5755, off Morocco; the afterlife lens -34.5711, -58.4172; the corrector -34.5706, -58.4173; the interlock lens -34.5729, -58.4132 from `carlos_thays.journey.json`. The file uses that last, byte-identical, per the inheritance rule.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Martí is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops were licensed. Nothing was silently deleted. Three relations in the pool are not rooted in his own life. **All three point forward** (new hash, old block), the direction the exceptions file licenses under no circumstances.

1. **Fidel Castro toward Martí.** `fidel_castro.journey.json` already carries all of it: the Pico Turquino bust standing since the 1953 centenary, Castro reading Martí with Marx and Lenin on the Isla de Pinos, the *Generación del Centenario*, the 1953 courtroom defence naming Martí the intellectual author of Moncada, the urn and the ashes at Santa Ifigenia, and the airport. **Direction: forward.** All real and citable, and every scene of it belongs where it already sits. I named Castro in no campa and put *La historia me absolverá* in no `suggested_ref`, because on the block rule a `suggested_ref` is a hash and this file stops in 1895. If the author wants the Santiago burial stop to point at the 1953 defence delivered in the same city, that is his ruling and it is one line.
2. **Huntington's Central Park equestrian and `calvert_vaux.journey.json`.** Place-inherited across time; the statue postdates both men. **Direction: forward.** Recorded above, not written in.
3. **The Palermo bust with `carlos_thays.journey.json` and `sarmiento.journey.json`.** Thays's file confirms the chain: Rosas's confiscated ground, Sarmiento's 1875 park, Thays's 1892-1913 expansion, the 1914 Rosedal, the 2002 bust. Worth writing somewhere, but it is shared geography, not a Martí relation, and the bust postdates all four men. **Direction: forward.** The Palermo stop stays, justified in its campa by his own thirteen years of *La Nación* correspondence and his Argentine consulship, naming neither Thays nor Sarmiento.

**The one backward relation, and the only interlock used:** Victor Hugo. Martí translated *Mes fils* and published it in Mexico in 1875; Hugo died in 1885. A translator's engagement with the man he translates is a real one-directional relation, so `victor_hugo` is named in the Paris campa, the reported meeting flagged as unconfirmed tradition in the confidence field.

**Interlocks named in campa: `victor_hugo` only.**

## Gaps, stated as gaps

- **His closest circle is absent from the corpus.** No file exists for Antonio Maceo, Máximo Gómez, Juan Gualberto Gómez, Manuel Mercado, the Pedrosos, Fermín Valdés Domínguez, Calixto García or Rubén Darío. Every one is named in this journey with nothing to link to. That is the largest structural gap around this subject.
- **The Haymarket chronicles.** Text not located by either pass. `lucy_parsons.journey.json` covers the same Chicago events in the same window and never mentions Martí; the connection is inferred from shared event and date, so it was **not** named in any campa. If the chronicle is found and he wrote about Lucy Parsons herself, that becomes a real one-directional relation and the stop should be revised.
- **The Argentine consulship (1890-91)** is dossier-derived and unchecked against a primary archive, and it is doing real work in the Palermo stop.
- **The shackle injury** rests on secondary literature only.
- **Three unverified interlock leads, none written in.** (a) Sarmiento's much-quoted 1887 judgement on Martí's Spanish: absent from the pool; if real it is a **contemporary two-directional** relation between two subjects who both have files, and the strongest interlock available here. (b) Martí's chronicle on the 1883 New York memorial for Karl Marx, a clean backward relation to `karl_marx.journey.json`; two es.wikisource URLs returned 404. (c) The 1893 speech in honour of Bolívar, a clean backward relation to `bolivar.journey.json`. All three failed because the session's web-search budget was exhausted (200 of 200) before this pass. Each is worth one fetch later.
- **Pins that are reconstructions rather than sourced figures:** birth house, Mendive school, Instituto, San Lázaro quarries, Sardá estate, Zaragoza quarter, Calle Moneda, Mantilla boardinghouse, Guatemala death site, Pedroso house, Playitas de Cajobabo, La Mejorana, Dos Ríos, Remanganaguas.
