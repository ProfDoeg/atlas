# Megasthenes: research report

*2026-08-27. Greek envoy and ethnographer, of unknown city and parentage; author of the lost Indika in four books; the first European to describe the Mauryan empire from inside it.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction, convention, or tradition. Contradictions are printed side by side and left open. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Almost nothing about the man is attested, and almost everything about the book is.** No birth date, birthplace, family, death date, death place, tomb, inscription, coin or portrait. What survives is roughly forty quotations of his work in other men's books, most of them hostile, plus two biographical clauses. This is therefore mostly a report on the transmission of a text and on a chronological dispute still live.

---

## 1. Origins: nothing

Modern reference works give a birth of **c. 350 BCE** and an origin in **Ionia or Asia Minor**, sometimes near Smyrna. **[R]** No ancient testimonium supplies any of it; the pool's review found the tradition circulating and could not trace it to an ancient text, and independent checking gave the same negative. Both are handbook conveniences, carried in the file as flagged convention with the Smyrna coordinate marked a placeholder for "Ionia".

**Gap, stated as a gap:** parents, city, education, how he came east, and what language competence he had in India are all unrecorded. No fragment says whether he worked through an interpreter.

## 2. Arachosia and the household of Sibyrtius, c. 324-316 BCE

- **Sibyrtius appointed satrap of Arachosia and Gedrosia, early 324 BCE. [A** Successor chronology, in Bosworth, "The Historical Setting of Megasthenes' Indica", *Classical Philology* 91.2 (1996)**]**
- **Megasthenes "lived with Sibyrtius, satrap of Arachosia, and says that he often visited Sandracottus, king of the Indians." [A** Arrian, *Anabasis* 5.6.2, manuscript reading, from McCrindle 1877, read directly**]** One of only two biographical statements about him in all of antiquity. No rank, no office, no duties, no length of residence.
- The satrapal seat is identified with **Alexandria in Arachosia at Old Kandahar. [R** for his residence; **A** for the city, Wikipedia "Alexandria Arachosia", which gives the excavation record: 1970s and 2008-2009 digs yielding coins, inscriptions and graves, no confirmed Greek building**]**
- Alexander dies at Babylon in 323; Sibyrtius is retained at **Babylon (323)** and **Triparadeisus (321)**, and after the defeat of Eumenes is confirmed a third time by **Antigonus (c. 316)**, reinforced with a contingent of the **Silver Shields**. **[A** Bosworth 1996**]** Megasthenes' presence across these years is inference from the Arrian clause. **[R]**
- **Adjacent, not evidence:** the bilingual Greek-and-Aramaic **Ashoka inscription at Chehel Zina** near Kandahar, found 1958, c. 260 BCE, shows a substantial Greek-speaking population still in Arachosia seventy years after Alexander **[A** Wikipedia, "Kandahar Bilingual Rock Inscription"**]**. It postdates him and belongs to Ashoka; cited as context, never staged as a stop.
- **Coordinate discrepancy.** Three values for Alexandria in Arachosia appear in the pool: geography 31.5822 / 65.6672, interlock 31.6289 / 65.7372, afterlife **31.60222 / 65.65889** (Wikipedia). Verification corrected the first to the third and the file uses it throughout; the interlock value was an approximation attached to a negative finding, not authority.

## 3. The chronological dispute, not settled here

The central problem of the subject. Both positions are carried in the file's `date_confidence` fields, not resolved.

**The conventional reconstruction. [R]** Megasthenes is Seleucus I's envoy to Chandragupta after the Seleucid-Mauryan settlement, usually dated **c. 303-298 BCE**. The embassy is attested: "King Sandrocottus to whom Megasthenes was sent on an embassy" **[A** Strabo 15.1.36, read directly**]**. The *date* is not; it is a modern synthesis anchored on the settlement of 305.

**Bosworth's reconstruction. [R** Bosworth 1996, read in full via archive.org**]** A mission out of Arachosia under Sibyrtius, through the Punjab, reaching Chandragupta around **320-318 BCE**, before full Mauryan control of the northwest. His grounds: Arrian's word is that Megasthenes visited Sandracottus **often**, a habit and not one embassy **[A** *Anabasis* 5.6.2**]**; the received text of *Indika* 5.3 has him say he met "Sandracottus, the greatest of the Indian kings, and Porus, even greater than he was", which editors since the eighteenth century have emended or excised as impossible on the conventional dating **[A** for the manuscript wording, confirmed against the Fordham translation**]**; and the Indika claims Alexander alone invaded India in arms, which nobody writes after Seleucus crossed the Indus in 305, hence composition c. **310 BCE [R]**.

**The hinge is Porus' death, and the pool's figure needed correcting.** The pool gave a flat "c. 318 BCE". The substance is right, the precision wrong: Porus was killed by **Eudamus** (Diodorus 19.14.8 has him obtain his elephants by assassinating the king), but the date is loose, **reportedly between 321 and 315**, with Bosworth placing Eudamus' arrival in Susiana in 317. The file states the range, not a year. **[R]**

**Not adjudicated.** Both datings could hold, since "often visited" permits an early journey and a later formal embassy. The file is built that way, early road as main narrative and the 303 embassy as a distinct late stop, but that is a compositional decision, labelled as one in the confidence fields.

## 4. The journey east

Every waypoint between Arachosia and Pataliputra is **[R]**, route inference; no ancient text places him anywhere on the road. Pins are the obligatory crossings: **the Indus below Ohind**, **Taxila** (33.746 / 72.787), **the Hydaspes** (32.933 / 73.733) and **the Hyphasis** (31.55 / 75.1), those three inherited byte-identical from `alexander.journey.json`, plus **Mathura** and **Prayaga**. Two corrections: the geography lens' Beas pin (31.5083 / 75.0912) falls near Kartarpur, off the river line, and is superseded by the canonical 31.55 / 75.1; Mathura is corrected from 27.5046 / 77.6733 to **27.4924 / 77.6737**.

**A misquotation caught and not repeated.** The pool carried "Arrian judged that Megasthenes had seen more of India than any of Alexander's own companions". Arrian's sentence is primarily deflationary: "But not even Megasthenes, so far as I can see, travelled over any large part of India; yet a good deal more than the followers of Alexander son of Philip did." **[A** *Indika* 5**]** The file quotes it whole.

## 5. Palibothra

- He reached **Pataliputra (Palibothra)** at the Ganges-Son confluence near modern Patna. **[A** Strabo 15.1.36; Arrian, *Anabasis* 5.6.2**]** Arrian's **Erannoboas** = the **Son** is a modern equation, not his words. **[R]**
- **The city description. [A]** Strabo 15.1.36, verified verbatim: eighty stadia by fifteen, a parallelogram, "surrounded by a wooden wall that is perforated so that arrows can be shot through the holes", with a trench in front "used both for defence and as a receptacle of the sewage that flows from the city"; Megasthenes named at 15.1.35-37. Arrian, *Indika* 10 gives the same 80 by 15 and adds **570 towers, 64 gates**, ditch six plethra wide and thirty cubits deep. The pool had both tagged **[R]** on a failed fetch; verification located both and upgraded them, which changes the weight of the most checkable thing in the fragment corpus.
- **Archaeology, with a conflation removed. [A** Wikipedia "Kumhrar", "Pataliputra"**]** The pool merged two sites. **Waddell** dug **Bulandi Bagh** in 1895 and found the wooden palisade, since traced over several kilometres; **Spooner** (1912-13) and **Jaiswal** (1951-55) dug **Kumhrar**, producing an eighty-pillared hall in eight rows of ten, one whole monolith of 9.75 m and the pits of seventy-nine more. They lie about 350 m apart and get separate stops: **Bulandi Bagh 25.602 / 85.185**, **Kumhrar 25.59889 / 85.18472**, the latter corrected from the geography lens' 25.592 / 85.169, about 2 km off.
- **The seven divisions. [A** Arrian, *Indika*: "The Indians generally are divided into seven castes"**]** Modern scholarship, Bosworth included, reads this as a Greek analytical grid, not a rendering of varna **[R]**: the report is secure, its accuracy is not. **Brachmanes and Garmanes / Sarmanes [A** Strabo 15.1.59-60**]**.
- **Court dining, from book 2. [A** Athenaeus 4.153 section 39, verified verbatim**]** The pool called this the only fragment tied to a numbered book. **Overstated, and softened:** Clement, *Stromateis* 1.15, cites "the third of his books", so the file says "one of only a handful".
- **The royal camp: a factor-of-ten error, corrected. [A** Strabo 15.1.53: "although the number in camp was forty thousand"**]** The geography lens carried **400,000**, from the older Bohn / Hamilton-Falconer rendering misreading the myriad count, contradicting the pool's own chronology entry. The figure is **40,000**; the point is that reported thefts never exceeded two hundred drachmae in a camp that size.

## 6. Contents of the Indika, and its contradictions

**[A]** here certifies that an ancient author names Megasthenes as the source, never that the claim is true.

- **Elephants:** capture, taming, veterinary treatment, royal ownership **[A** Strabo 15.1**]**; Trautmann (2021) reads this as the environmental logistics of Mauryan power, wet east supplying elephants, dry northwest horses **[R]**. **Tigers among the Prasii, nearly twice the size of lions [A** Strabo 15.1.37**]**; **fifty-eight navigable rivers [A** Arrian, *Indika***]**; the **Ganges** a hundred stadia at medium breadth, twenty fathoms at least depth **[A** Strabo 15.1.35**]**.
- **Contradiction, open:** "no Indian uses slaves" **[A** Strabo 15.1.54**]** stands in the same sentence as Onesicritus' contrary report that slavery is peculiar to the Indians in the country of Musicanus. Strabo chooses neither; so does the file.
- **Contradiction, open:** the claim that no outside army ever mastered India except those of Heracles, Dionysus and the Macedonians **[A** Strabo 15.1.6**]** is contradicted by evidence of Achaemenid control in the Indus valley. **Attribution corrected:** Arrian, *Indika* 5 names Sesostris, Idanthyrsus and Semiramis and **does not include the Persians**, who belong to the Strabo branch. The pool gave them to Arrian.
- **Dionysus in India. [A]**, sources split, where the pool had a vague "compilation". **Diodorus 2.38-39** supplies the heat, the sickness, the withdrawal into the hills, the name Meros, the cities, laws, wine, and "after he had reigned over all India for fifty-two years he died of old age". **Arrian, *Indika* 7-8** supplies the thigh-etymology and the reigns, and has **nothing** about the heat.
- **The Indian Heracles and his daughter Pandaea**, given a southern kingdom with 500 elephants, 4,000 horse, 130,000 foot **[A** Arrian, *Indika*; Pliny**]**. Pandaea = the historical Pandya kingdom is a later identification Arrian never makes. **[R]**

## 7. The marvels, and Strabo's verdict

- **Dog-headed peoples. [A** Pliny, *NH* 7.2.22-23, explicit attribution**]**, with a correction that matters. The pool's quote ran Nulus and the dog-heads together and closed with "he says that they numbered more than 120,000 when he published his work". In Pliny's Latin that census clause is credited to **CTESIAS**. The 120,000 is **dropped from the journey file** and recorded here as a misattribution to avoid. The pool also credited the English to Bostock and Riley; it is Rackham.
- **Astomi**, mouthless, hairy, living on scent, near the Ganges source **[A** Pliny 7.2.25; parallel Strabo 15.1.57**]**; **Enotocoetae**, ears to the feet, and **people five and three spans tall, some with no nostrils [A** Strabo 15.1.57**]**; **pygmies at war with cranes [A** Strabo 2.1.9**]**.
- **Gold-digging ants among the Derdae. [A** Strabo 15.1.44, verified verbatim, so the researcher's "could not confirm" caveat is dropped**]** **Geographical contradiction, open:** Strabo puts the Derdae "towards the east and in the mountains", while the coordinate in use, **Gilgit (35.9208 / 74.3144)**, is far northwest and was chosen on the Dardistan etymology; the file's confidence field states the conflict. Herodotus 3.102-105 has the story earlier.
- **Taprobane** cut in two by a river, its people the Palaeogoni, richer in gold and pearls than India. **[A** Pliny 6.81**]** No evidence he went; content relayed by informants.
- **Strabo's verdict. [A** *Geography* 2.1.9, read directly**]** He ranks the writers on India as fabricators, names **Deimachus** worst and Megasthenes next, says no faith whatever can be placed in either, and lists the men who sleep in their ears, without mouths, without noses, with one eye, with long legs, with fingers turned backward, and the cranes. In Book 15 he quotes Megasthenes at greater length than anyone else in antiquity does. **The contradiction is Strabo's own and is nowhere smoothed over.** Deimachus, the later envoy to Bindusara, gets no stop here: **there is no evidence the two men ever met**, and his embassy is his own.

## 8. Death: a negative finding

No ancient source records **when, where or how** he died. No tomb, epitaph, burial, coin, portrait, papyrus or inscription names him. **[A** as a negative finding, checked across Arrian, Strabo, Clement, Pliny and Athenaeus, and against Wikipedia "Megasthenes", which records only "died c. 290 BCE"**]** The **c. 290** date is convention counted forward from a birth date that is also convention. **[R]** The death stop reuses the Palibothra coordinate and states in its confidence field that this is not a claim about where he died.

## 9. Transmission

- **Quoters, all verified this pass:** Strabo (attacks and excerpts), Pliny the Elder (turns it into data), Arrian (rebuilds a connected account), Athenaeus (book 2), Clement (book 3, plus the Seleucus clause). Diodorus' link is the **demonstrable Dionysus material at 2.38-39**, not the pool's "probably adapted large portions". **Eratosthenes** used Megasthenic measurements: Strabo 15.1.11 shows them agreeing on India's length. **[A]**
- **Dating of the two chief vehicles. [A]** Pliny's books 6 and 7 fall in the first ten, **published 77 CE**, the whole work left unrevised at his death in 79, so the file says "the books containing the Megasthenes material". Strabo's *Geography* was revised roughly 7 BCE to 23 CE.
- **Modern editions:** Schwanbeck, Bonn **1846**, base of the fragment numbering; McCrindle's English, Calcutta **1877**, read directly **[A]**; Stoneman (2022) **[R]**, see Sources.
- **Sir William Jones, 1793**, identifies Sandracottus with Chandragupta Maurya, the synchronism that aligned Indian and Graeco-Roman chronology; the reign is still called "the sheet anchor of Indian chronology". **[A** Wikipedia, "Sandrocottus"**]** **Quotation corrected:** the pool put "of vital importance" in quotation marks and gave it to Sushma Jansari; the phrase is not in the quoted material, so the file paraphrases her.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Megasthenes is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing here needs him added.** He has no theoretical apparatus to apply in either direction, so the exception is not engaged. Three things are flagged rather than silently deleted, each being a relation not rooted in his own life.

**1. Reception material. Direction: backward, from later people onto him.** The pool's afterlife lens supplied fully coordinated stops for Strabo at Amaseia, Pliny at Rome, Clement at Alexandria, Arrian at Nicomedia, Schwanbeck at Bonn, McCrindle and Jones at Calcutta, and *Wonders of the East* at the British Library. **None was staged as a scene here**, under the standing rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there. Each is carried instead as a `source` or `suggested_ref` on Megasthenes' own stop for the material, and the closing stop names the quoters and editors in prose at his last pin.

**2. `plinio_el_viejo.journey.json` has no Megasthenes stop; the join does not exist on Pliny's side.** The interlock lens checked the file directly: Pliny's military and administrative career and the Vesuvius death, no India, cynocephalus or *Natural History* ethnography anywhere. Pliny citing Megasthenes by name is a real one-directional relation, and Pliny is named in two campas here, but nothing points back. **Operator decision needed:** whether to add a stop to that file for the Megasthenic material in Books 6 and 7 when it is next revised. Nothing was written into it from here.

**3. The cynocephalus thread was cited, not annexed.** `san_cristobal`, `saint_mercurius`, `odoric_of_pordenone`, `ibn_battuta`, `rimbert` and `ratramnus_of_corbie` all carry dog-headed-peoples material, and the interlock lens confirmed by keyword search that **none names Megasthenes**: the chain reaching them runs through Ctesias, Pliny and Solinus. A shared motif several removes downstream is not a personal relation, so none is named in a campa; they appear only in the `sources` list of the dog-headed stop. `QUEUE.md` has **Ctesias** (line 86) and **Herodotus** (line 164) queued and framed as joining this thread; when written, the joins on their side will be direct.

**No pin was claimed off anyone else's file.** Taxila, the Hydaspes and the Hyphasis are inherited byte-identical from `alexander.journey.json`; Pataliputra and Alexandria in Arachosia existed nowhere in the corpus. Travelers named in campas, each for a real relation: **alexander** (Megasthenes writes substantively about his invasion and makes it the only successful one), **strabo** (quotes him at greater length than any other ancient author, and delivers the surviving verdict on him), **plinio_el_viejo** (cites him by name for the Indian marvels). None is named on shared geography.
