# Arthur Scherbius (1878-1929): research report

*2026-08-30, for `arthur_scherbius.journey.json`. Frankfurt am Main to Berlin-Wannsee: the engineer
who patented the rotor cipher machine, signed the patents away for a minority holding, and died with
no fortune and, now, no grave.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or a claim resting on a single
unverifiable relay. Contradictions are printed, not resolved; unfixable places are called gaps.

**The spines.** Biography descends from the Deutsche Biographie / *Neue Deutsche Biographie* entry
(GND 127822089) or from **Claus Taaks, "Scherbius and the Enigma," HistoCrypt 2023, pp. 170-180**, an
archival reassessment. Where they disagree, Taaks is document-bearing and the NDB is the popular
chronology; both are printed. Technical dates come from the Crypto Museum patent index and the DPMA.

## 1. Frankfurt, 1878-1899

- **Born 30 October 1878 [A: Deutsche Biographie; de.wikipedia]**, Jacob Ludolf Arthur Scherbius. A
  rival **16 October** circulates in institutional popularizations; unsupported, not adopted.
- **Parents [A: NDB genealogy].** Father Gustav (1836-1907), "V Gustav, Kaufm. in F.", a **merchant**:
  the dossier's "financial broker" is **wrong and corrected in the file**. Mother Hedwig Nacken
  (1854-1954), traced by the NDB to the banker **Ludolf Camphausen (1803-1890)**, source of the
  middle name.
- **Schooling 1885-1898 [A]:** primary school, then an *Oberrealschule*, the modern non-classical
  track. Abitur 1898.
- **A year in a machinery factory, 1898/99 [A as to fact, R as to place].** The NDB states it plainly,
  but **the firm is unidentified and the source does not say the factory was in Frankfurt**. The pin
  is an assumption and `date_confidence` says so.

## 2. Studies, 1899-1904: an unresolved sequence

**[A: de.wikipedia, on university records]** TH **Hannover** 1899-1903, "interrupted by two semesters
at TH München (1901-1902)", no Zurich; against **[A: Deutsche Biographie]** **Munich** from 1899, then
institutions including **Zurich**, then Hannover for the diploma. Both agree on the
**Diplom-Ingenieur at Hannover, 1903**. The file follows the record-based account and prints the
conflict in `date_confidence`. **Zurich gets no stop**: staging him in a city he may never have
studied in would invent geography for one source.

**Dr.-Ing., TH Hannover, 14 July 1904 [A: NDB; en.wikipedia]**, dissertation on an **indirectly acting
water-turbine governor**. One of very few exact early dates.

## 3. Industry, 1903-1914

- **Felten & Guilleaume-Lahmeyerwerke, project engineer, from 1903 [A].** Month unattested; the pin
  follows the firm's Frankfurt works.
- **Siemens-Schuckertwerke, brief post, 1904 [A]; independent from 1905 [A].** **No address
  attested.** The dossier's "Siemensstadt" coordinate (52.5341, 13.2028) falls in **Spandau
  Altstadt**, and in 1904/05 SSW was **not yet concentrated at Siemensstadt**. The file uses a generic
  Berlin pin: a deliberate loss of false precision.
- **Brown, Boveri & Cie., Baden, c. 1906-1912 [A].** Slip-power recovery for adjustable-speed
  induction motors, the **Scherbius cascade**: the work that made his professional name.
- **Berlin from 1912 [A].** Mercury-vapour rectifiers, consulting for Siemens, high-voltage direct
  current, an ambitious DC machine **never finished**.
- **Ceramic radiators, 1914.** Dossier tagged [R]; **upgraded to [A]**: Taaks has him deciding "to
  contribute to the war effort" with a ceramics manufacturer. Motive is **volunteering**, not metal
  shortage. Manufacturer and works unidentified.

## 4. War service and the commission, 1915-1918

- **Telegraph troops from October 1915 [A: NDB].** Posting [R]: Taaks says only "probably he was
  assigned to its directorate at the Grand Headquarters (GHQ)". **Coordinate replaced:** the dossier's
  50.5, 6.5 is open country in the Eifel and corresponds to nothing; German GHQ in October 1915 stood
  at **Charleville-Mézières (49.7628, 4.7161)**, and the file flags the "probably". Fenner's
  recollection [A: TICOM DF-202, p. 9, via Taaks p. 170], "the talented engineer Dr SCHERBIUS of the
  Supreme Army Command", is the stop's quote.
- **WuMBA, 1 February 1917 [A: Taaks, exact]**, deputy head of a department, probably electrical
  machines and equipment. **Coordinate gap:** the premises are not established anywhere reached, and
  the dossier's 52.5145, 13.376 sits inside the Tiergarten; the file uses an approximate War Ministry
  pin.
- **The commission, spring 1917 [A as to fact, R as to content].** Taaks: the order came from the War
  Ministry, and "the order itself and the specifications have not yet been found." The dossier's
  specification (polyalphabetic, changing after each keystroke) is a **modern inference read back out
  of the machine**. Dated to spring, not to a day.
- **Elisabeth Adelaide Taaks [R, weaker than the dossier claimed].** Taaks's paper **never mentions
  Elisabeth** (zero hits in the extracted text) and the NDB carries **no marriage information**;
  de.wikipedia gives "(1896-1945)", making her **eighteen** years his junior, not nineteen. The
  Partenkirchen meeting survives as [R], the Frankfurt engagement gets no stop, and the Bremen wedding
  of 10 November 1917 rests on one line of transmission.

## 5. The 1918 patents and demonstrations

- **DE416219 filed 23 February 1918 [A: Crypto Museum; DPMA].** Rotating wired wheels, keyboard, lamp
  panel. **Content corrected:** the dossier's "up to ten exchangeable rotors" is not in the filing.
  Taaks on the demonstrator: "a square of 5x5 letter keys, the result was indicated by 5x5
  glow-lamps." The **ten rotors were a naval demand after April 1918**, and go in the naval stop.
- **Spa, 15 April 1918 [A as to the letter].** His letter says the demonstration will be at Great
  Headquarters (at Spa from spring 1918), then before the Imperial Navy Office. **The forecast is the
  only evidence for the demonstration**; the file says so.
- **Reichsmarineamt, 1918 [A: Taaks, citing BArch RM 5/3566].** Two seven-rotor machines built to the
  naval demand **failed on unreliable contacts**. **Coordinate corrected:** the dossier's 52.5145,
  13.3628 lands in the Tiergarten; the office occupied the new Königin-Augusta-Straße building from
  1914, **52.5069, 13.3614**. Month unattested, dated May to follow the letter.
- **DE416833 filed 2 June 1918 [A].** Hydraulic or pneumatic movement of cipher elements. Taaks adds
  the afterlife: no German machine, but built in Britain 1925-26 and in Czechoslovakia as the first
  Štolba machine in 1930.
- **Reichspatentamt coordinate corrected in five places.** The dossier's 52.5074, 13.3904 is
  Spittelmarkt, ~1.1 km off; the office has stood at **Gitschiner Straße, Kreuzberg (52.4985,
  13.3977)** since 1905. All patent stops use it.

## 6. Scherbius & Ritter and the state that would not buy, 1919-1922

- **Reichsverwertungsamt and the Army Peace Treaty Commission, 1919 [A].** Seven rotors cut to three
  or four; a turn toward a printing machine with a line-transposition stage.
- **Scherbius & Ritter oHG founded 1 April 1920 [A: Taaks, exact]**, for cipher machines and Birka
  thermostats. Partner Ernst Richard Ritter, whose earlier firm (1911) probably built the 1918
  demonstrator. **Address wrong in the dossier:** "Schiffbauerdamm" appears **nowhere** in Taaks
  (grepped: zero hits), while de.wikipedia and gazette-berlin.de put the firm in Wannsee, "in der
  Königstraße 40" (**52.4178, 13.1457**). Schiffbauerdamm 30 discarded.
- **The Reichspost refusal, 13 August 1920 [A: BArch R 4701/8665, via Taaks p. 172]:** "the government
  is not in a position to spend funds on the purchase of your patents... Consequently, the use of your
  invention is herewith released for foreign countries as well." The hinge of the life. A companion
  letter the same year [A] warns that foreign inventors are moving and "it can only be recommended
  that you speed up your work".
- **DE425147 filed 26 September 1920 [A].** Taaks describes it more fully than the dossier: a
  **writing machine with line-by-line scrambling for pneumatic machines**.
- **December 1920 [A: Taaks].** Demonstration 2 December, agreement reportedly concluded 19 December,
  after which **the project vanishes from the administrative trail** with no evidence of completed
  procurement.
- **Gewerkschaft Securitas, 21 November 1921 [A].** Ten patents and applications transferred for a
  **minority interest** and manufacturing cooperation. Securitas's offices are not located.
- **DE385682 filed 10 May 1922 [A].** Taaks: the process "the Reichswehr wanted to keep secret and the
  Reichspost had demanded for international use, was made public. This made the machine unusable for
  the military... it had failed for the second time."

## 7. Chiffriermaschinen AG, 1923-1925

- **Schauffler, 27-29 June 1923 [A: PA AA, via Taaks p. 174].** Report on "die Scherbius'sche
  Maschine (Frage der Sicherheit)": attackable "on the basis of theoretical considerations and
  possibly with the help of a 'counter-machine'", though the effort might still allow practical use.
  **His own presence is not attested**; the stop follows the machine and says so.
- **ChiMaAG founded 9 July 1923, Steglitzer Straße 2 [A: NDB; Crypto Museum].** He is the
  indispensable inventor; the legal founders and financiers are a broader group.
- **General assembly, 24 September 1923, Vossstraße 18 [A: Landesarchiv Berlin A Rep 342-02, via
  Taaks p. 175].** The lawyer for the "capitalists' group": the contract "makes any litigation with
  the inventors unnecessary for the company." **Occasion corrected:** the dossier placed this at the
  *preliminary discussion*; Taaks has it after the *General Assembly* resolutions, passed against the
  inventors' protest. Building not located; the pin approximates the street.
- **"Enigma" Chiffriermaschine, *Elektrotechnische Zeitschrift* 44, nos. 47/48, pp. 1035-1036,
  29 November 1923 [A].** His fullest public statement. **No translation, no reprint, text not
  obtained.**
- **Stockholm, UPU Congress, 1924 [A].** Venue unidentified; the coordinate marks the city centre.
  Taaks's delivery list is used instead: two Enigma B to the Swedish General Staff, twelve commercial
  machines to Šentel in Prague, ten Funkschlüssel C to the naval command.
- **The Wannsee house, September 1924 [A: de.wikipedia; gazette-berlin.de].** **The dossier has house
  and works exactly swapped across two items: the largest geographic error in the pool.** Lindenstraße
  5/6 is the plot where "sich Scherbius und Ritter Häuser für ihre Familien bauten". So **Königstraße
  40 = the works** (52.4178, 13.1457), **Lindenstraße 5/6 = the houses** (52.4257, 13.1573). Both
  dossier coordinates were wrong too, by up to 1.9 km.
- **Barmat-Höfle, 10 February 1925 [A].** The Post Minister is arrested; the network touches ChiMaAG's
  dealings, including a loan tied to the businessman Hermkes, who resigns eleven days later. The
  Reichstag committee airs it on 15 July 1925 and ruins the first corporate structure. The same year's
  **separation of property [R]** (Elisabeth takes the house and two accounts, and it shields nothing)
  is known only by year, so it is folded into that stop.
- **DE416219 published 8 July 1925 [A]**, seven years and four months after filing.

## 8. Orders, a child, a wall, 1925-1929

**The naval order is the weakest procurement claim in the pool, flagged not resolved:** dossier
**fifty** ordered 1925; Taaks **"10 machines Funkschlüssel C to the naval command"** around 1924-25;
Crypto Museum, purchase **in 1926**, quantity unstated. Nothing supports the fifty. The file carries
the ten and prints the rest.

- **Thirteen printing Enigmas ordered April 1926, cancelled in June [R].**
- **400 Enigma I ordered by the Army, 17 May 1927 [R].** Plausibly the turning point, but date and
  quantity rest on one unverified summary.
- **ChiMaAG management board, 1927-1928 [R].** Accumulated losses mean no dividend: **no personal
  fortune from Enigma at any point in his life.**
- **Late patents [A].** US 1,875,765 "Vacuum Switch", priority 1 July 1927, granted **posthumously**
  6 September 1932; DE541702, electromagnets for printing, filed 30 January 1929; US 1,657,411
  "Ciphering Machine", priority 11 February 1922, granted 1928 to ChiMaAG.
- **The child, January 1929 [A: de.wikipedia]:** the couple took in a *Heimkind* and **Elisabeth
  completed the adoption alone in 1930**. **Day not attested** (the dossier's 15 January is
  unsourced); **the name "Rainer Scherbius" is unattested anywhere reached and is not used**; the
  institution is unlocated.
- **Death, 13 May 1929, aged 50 [A].** **The mechanism in the dossier is backwards and is corrected.**
  It had a runaway carriage pressing him against a wall as a bystander; the sources have him
  **driving**. gazette-berlin.de: he struck a wall with his own carriage and died of internal
  injuries; de.wikipedia, "nachdem er die Kontrolle über einen Pferdewagen verloren hatte"; NDB,
  "(Unfall) 13.5.1929 Berlin". The street is not established.
- **Suicide claim [R, contradicted].** Attributed to Bengt Beckman via Friedrich L. Bauer.
  **Beckman was not accessed at all, and Bauer sits behind a Springer login wall**; the claim is named
  in `date_confidence` as a circulating variant and not adjudicated in prose.

## 9. Afterlife

- **Buried, Friedhof Lindenstraße, Berlin-Wannsee [A: gazette-berlin.de].** Funeral date unrecorded;
  the stop carries the date of death. **The grave is gone [A]:** "sein Grab existiert nicht mehr."
- **Obituary [A: Heinrich Meyer-Delius, *Elektrotechnik und Maschinenbau* 47/28, 14 July 1929, p.
  610f; trans. Jim Rawlinson, epigraph to Taaks 2023]:** "Luck rarely helped this man in his search
  for new paths..." Carried in full as the quote on the burial stop.
- **Death notice, *Hannoverscher Kurier*, 17 May 1929 [R].** **The digitized PDF could not be opened
  here**. A lead, given no stop: a document about him, not a place he stood.
- **ChiMaAG taken over by Heimsoeth and Rinke, 1935 [A: Crypto Museum].** The successor firm becomes
  the principal Enigma supplier to the German forces, and his name recedes behind its brand.
- **The open question [A: NDB].** His role in the Enigma's further development "ist bislang nicht
  vollständig geklärt": a standard reference saying so is the best evidence that this is a live
  research problem, which is what Taaks 2023 addresses.
- **The Koch legend [R, rejected as told].** An NSA brochure and popular histories have Hugo Koch
  inventing the rotor machine and Scherbius acquiring or copying it. Crypto Museum confirms ChiMaAG's
  **1927** purchase of Koch's Dutch patents and names **R. P. C. Spengler and Theo A. van Hengel** as
  conceiving the rotor principle by **1915**. The honest shape is **multi-national simultaneous
  invention**, not a theft; the file names no thief.
- **Objects.** The Science Museum Group holds a "Three-ring Enigma cypher machine in oak wood transit
  case" against an authority record naming Scherbius as designer. **No 1918 prototype is known to
  survive.** The nearest relic is DE416219 itself, via DEPATISnet; he never went to the DPMA, so that
  is a `suggested_ref`, not a stop.
