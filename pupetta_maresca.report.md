# Assunta "Pupetta" Maresca (1935-2021): research report

*2026-08-11. Atlas of Journeys. Companion: `pupetta_maresca.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** [A] attested, source named · [R] reconstruction, tradition, single uncorroborated source, or atlas inference. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; where the atlas takes a reading it says so and prints the dissent. Per-stop hedges live in the journey's `date_confidence` fields.

**Structural warning.** The two Wikipedia editions disagree with each other, and the English one disagrees with *its own sister article* on the central date of the life. it.wikipedia, which cites *la Repubblica*, *Il Mattino* and JSTOR, is the stronger witness here, not a decisive one.

## 1. Castellammare di Stabia, 1935-54

Born 19 January 1935, Castellammare di Stabia [A: it.wikipedia "Assunta Maresca"; English Wikipedia; italyonthisday.com]. Father Alberto Maresca, cigarette smuggler. Family nickname "Lampetielli", glossed by English Wikipedia as switchblade skill [A as nickname; gloss English-only].

Three English-article assertions fail checking:

1. "Only daughter among four brothers": it.wikipedia names two, Pasquale and Ciro "Lampetiello". Four is English-only. **[R]**
2. "Uncle Vincenzo, a known Camorra boss": it.wikipedia has him only as sentenced to seven years for murdering his brother Gerardo. **[A** sentence; **R** rank]
3. Spelling: "i Lampatielli" (English) vs "Lampetielli" (Italian). Unresolved; the Italian is used.

Teenage wounding of a schoolmate, complaint withdrawn **[R: italyonthisday.com, single source, undated]**. In neither Wikipedia edition.

The beauty contest **[A for the fact only]**. it.wikipedia: "vinse un concorso locale di miss". "Miss Rovigliano" is English-only, as is the gloss calling Rovigliano "a suburban village of Naples": it is a rocky islet ~400 m offshore at the Sarno mouth, administratively part of Castellammare di Stabia [A: it.wikipedia]. **1954 is inferred from "about 19" and is in no source [R].**

**Gaps.** No address for the family house; no route, boat, or commodity beyond cigarettes; no school record.

## 2. The marriage, and eighty days, 1955

Pasquale Simonetti, "Pascalone 'e Nola," born Palma Campania 26 February 1926 [A: both Wikipedias]. Ration-book forgery in the war, then control of the Corso Novara wholesale exchange [A: English Wikipedia], held jointly with Antonio Esposito of Pomigliano d'Arco [A: it.wikipedia].

Marriage 27 April 1955; ~500 guests including the rivals Maisto and Esposito [A: it.wikipedia]. **Esposito served as wedding witness, testimone di nozze [A]**, the load-bearing detail an earlier pass omitted.

> **CONTRADICTION 1: the wedding venue. Unresolved.** An earlier pass gave the Pontifical Shrine of Pompei, supported by no Wikipedia edition; withdrawn. Il Mattino: an old church outside Castellammare di Stabia, banquet on the Pozzano hillside, ~300 guests. The Simonetti file found three sources for Palma Campania; the atlas inherits that and prints Il Mattino beside it.

Simonetti shot in Corso Novara near the wholesale market by Gaetano Orlando on Esposito's orders [A: English Wikipedia; internapoli.it; it.wikipedia]. Gaetano Nuvoletta as accomplice **[R]**; the failed-greeting trigger is **internapoli.it alone [R]**. Maresca six months pregnant [A].

> **CONTRADICTION 2: the date of his death. Unresolved.** **16 July 1955**: both English Wikipedia articles, terranostranews.it, italiamistero.it, confirmed by the 80 days from 27 April. **15 July**: it.wikipedia (outlier). **17 July**, peritonitis at the Ospedale degli Incurabili the day *after* the shooting: internapoli.it alone separates the events, the others collapsing both into the 16th. The atlas follows the Simonetti file.

**Gap.** Simonetti's funeral is described by no source consulted: no date, church, route, or mourners. His burial place is unnamed; custom alone suggests Palma Campania or Nola.

## 3. Corso Novara, 4 October 1955

She travels to Naples with her brother Ciro and kills Antonio Esposito outside the Bar Grandone on Corso Novara, by the wholesale market near the central station [A: it.wikipedia; English Wikipedia "Pasquale Simonetti"; Il Mattino, "davanti al bar Grandone in corso Novara"; storienapoli.it].

> **CONTRADICTION 3: the date of the killing. Weighed, not silently resolved.** **4 October 1955**: it.wikipedia, English Wikipedia "Pasquale Simonetti", napolitan.it, storienapoli.it, Il Mattino, consistent with the arrest ten days later. **4 August 1955**: English Wikipedia "Pupetta Maresca", contradicting its own sister article, and internapoli.it. **16 September**: a stray third date. The atlas takes 4 October and prints the August reading rather than deleting it.

> **CONTRADICTION 4: the weapon. Unresolved, and physically constrained.** English Wikipedia: up to 29 rounds from a Smith & Wesson .38 said to be her husband's wedding gift. it.wikipedia: shots from at least four pistols. Twenty-nine rounds cannot come from a six-shot revolver, and the wedding-gift provenance is uncorroborated **[R]**. Four pistols implies she was not alone, and **no source names who else fired**.

The San Giovanni Rotondo detour **[R]**: one English-language account sends her to Padre Pio first, no Italian source carries it. Mapped as tradition.

Arrested 14 October 1955 [A: both Wikipedias agree on the 14th; some sources give the 13th].

Gives birth in prison to a son, Pasquale [A: italyonthisday.com; English Wikipedia]. **No source gives a date [gap]**, and the account naming the birth says only "prison": **naming Poggioreale is a second inference on the first [R]**. The journey uses 1956-01-01, counted forward from an October arrest at six months.

## 4. The trial, 1958-65

Rosi's *La sfida* (1958), Schiaffino in the role drawn from her, reaches cinemas while she awaits trial [A: English Wikipedia]. Trial before the Corte d'Assise di Napoli, April 1959 [A: both English Wikipedia articles; corroborated by *New York Times* coverage of 7 April 1959]. International press; "the Diva of Crime" [A as press epithet].

> **[R] flag on the courthouse.** No source names the building; Castel Capuano is the atlas's inference from its function as seat of the Naples criminal courts. An earlier pass tagged it **[A]** while admitting the site was only "likely." Corrected to **[R]** here and at the 1975, 1985 and 1986 court stops.

Thirty-three hearings [A via the Simonetti file]. Verdict 16 May 1959 at 23:15 after twelve hours [A: napolitan.it; it.wikipedia, hour included].

> **CONTRADICTION 5: the sentence.** English Wikipedia and theweek.in: 18 years. The Simonetti file: 18 years 6 months. All agree on reduction on appeal to 13 years 4 months, homicide with mitigating circumstances. **The date of that judgment is supplied by nothing consulted [gap].**

Pardoned 1965 after roughly ten years [A on the year: internapoli.it; both Wikipedias]. The day, 17 April 1965, is **napolitan.it alone [R]**.

**Quotes.** "I would do it again!", April 1959, cheered by the courtroom **[A as an English rendering; the Italian original not located, a gap]**. Her testimony ("I had killed for love... That is, I shot in self-defence") survives **only in Newland's translation [A in translation]**; an Italian page with near-identical content **returned HTTP 403**. "I was afraid I would miss", on her two-handed grip **[A as attributed by English Wikipedia; its date inherits Contradiction 3]**. The trial fed the national argument over delitto d'onore [A].

## 5. Shops, film, Ammaturo, 1965-1975

Two clothing shops in Naples [A: it.wikipedia; italyonthisday.com]. **No source names a street [gap];** the Via Chiaia pin is the atlas's inference from the period's shopping district **[R]**.

*Delitto a Posillipo*, also *Londra chiama Napoli* (1967), dir. Renato Parravicini [A: it.wikipedia]. She plays the lead, "Pupetta Amoruso", a part it.wikipedia calls only "vagamente ispirato alla sua vita", not the film of her own life an earlier pass made of it. Dubbed by Rita Savagnone, but sings her own composition "'O bbene mio" in her own voice [A].

Ammaturo; relationship from 1970; twins Roberto and Antonella [A: it.wikipedia, "nel 1970 si innamorò del camorrista Umberto Ammaturo, dal quale ebbe due gemelli, Roberto e Antonella"]. Place and date of the twins' birth: **gap**.

> **CONTRADICTION 6: marriage or not. Weighted, not left symmetrical.** theweek.in describes a marriage; italyonthisday.com says they never married; it.wikipedia ("una relazione amorosa") and English Wikipedia describe a relationship only. An earlier pass treated this as evenly open. It is not: no Italian source supports a marriage.

**The Fuorigrotta invention, corrected in place.** it.wikipedia mentions Ammaturo's father's wine shop only as where *he* worked as a boy; an earlier geography pass pinned the start of the relationship there. No source makes the connection. The stop is kept and labelled as corrected invention rather than deleted.

Pasqualino killed, 1974 [A: both Wikipedias]. Eighteen, born in Poggioreale. Abducted and murdered, never solved; body never recovered, reportedly weighted and thrown into the sea **[A as report]**. Motive per it.wikipedia: he had rejected his mother's relationship with Ammaturo and threatened him. Ammaturo jailed, then acquitted for insufficient evidence in April 1975 [A: it.wikipedia].

> **CONTRADICTION 7: did she suspect him? Unresolved, and consequential.** English Wikipedia has her publicly suspecting Ammaturo. it.wikipedia: "Pupetta non accettò mai del tutto questa ipotesi." Opposite claims about her position on her son's death; an earlier pass carried the English reading as fact.

**Gaps.** Neither the place of the ambush nor the date within 1974; no court named for the 1975 acquittal. Recorded but not mapped: Ciro shot and surviving, 1978, and threatened by Cutolo's men, 1982 **[both R: English Wikipedia, uncorroborated]**.

## 6. Cutolo, Semerari, the courts, 1981-1986

Ciro Galli, of Cutolo's Nuova Camorra Organizzata, killed 1981; Maresca accused of being the *mandante* as a *vendetta trasversale*; prosecution sought life; acquitted for lack of evidence 1985 [A: it.wikipedia].

> **Tag correction.** An earlier pass tagged this **[R]**, uncorroborated (theweek.in), and phrased the charge as killing Galli. Both wrong: it.wikipedia carries the episode, so the tag moves to **[A]**, and the charge is ordering, not committing.

Press conference, 13 February 1982 [A: it.wikipedia]. Mid-war between the NCO and Nuova Famiglia, she threatened Cutolo publicly.

> **CONTRADICTION 8: the wording. Both printed.** it.wikipedia, used verbatim in the journey: *"Se per Nuova Famiglia si intende tutta quella gente che si difende dallo strapotere di quest'uomo, allora mi ritengo affiliata a questa organizzazione."* laRegione.ch, citing Il Mattino and Metropolis, opens *"Sentite, se voi per Nuova Famiglia dite..."* and ends at *"mi ritengo affiliata."* An intermediate draft produced a third version, in no source, by altering "si intende tutta quella gente" and "strapotere". Withdrawn.

English Wikipedia's gloss that the conference concerned a demanded "tax" on cigarette smuggling is **English-only [R]**; pairing it with a 1982 separation from Ammaturo is **unsupported [R]**, it.wikipedia dating the rupture to 1975.

Aldo Semerari murdered 1 April 1982, decapitated body in a Fiat 128 at Ottaviano [A: it.wikipedia]. An earlier pass dated it 1981, which is wrong.

Arrested with Ammaturo in a Naples apartment shortly after the conference; charges of extortion, drug trafficking, and the Semerari killing [A: it.wikipedia]. Address **[gap]**; date not given, 1 May 1982 a placeholder.

> **CONTRADICTION 9: the outcome of the 1982 case. Unresolved and important.** it.wikipedia: acquitted of the Semerari murder, of attempted extortion of a bank, and of drug trafficking. English Wikipedia and theweek.in: convicted, about four years served, single-sourced. Neither taken.

*Il caso Pupetta Maresca* (1982), RAI, dir. Marisa Malfatti and Riccardo Tortora; broadcast blocked after she objected [A: it.wikipedia]. **Alessandra Mussolini's casting is English-only [R]**; it.wikipedia cites a 1994 *Repubblica* piece headlined "MUSSOLINI ATTACCA," attesting her connection to the film but not the casting.

> **A quotation that is not a quotation.** "An affront to her honour" circulates as Maresca's words. Newland reads: *Maresca, calling the casting decision an "affront" to her honour, took the makers to court*. **The only quoted word in the source is "affront."** The journey renders it as narration.

1986: the Tribunale di Napoli declares her an affiliate of Nuova Famiglia and confiscates her assets [A: it.wikipedia], a preventive measure requiring no conviction, landing after a run of acquittals. Venue unnamed **[R]**.

## 7. Retirement, afterlife, death, 1989-2023

Bellizzi Irpino [A]. it.wikipedia states as fact, not allegation: "La donna gestiva feste cui partecipavano magistrati e alte personalità," citing *la Repubblica* of 18 March 1989, the only date anchor for that imprisonment. **Otherwise a gap.**

Ammaturo arrested in Peru 1993 with Yohanna Valdez; collaborates "pochi mesi dopo" [A: it.wikipedia]. **The June 1993 date drafted in an earlier pass is unsupported**: no source names the month. His confession to the Semerari beheading ("Gli tagliai io la testa... era un traditore") comes from a May 2010 interview; the judges disbelieved him and the men he named were acquitted [A].

Her remark after that arrest, *"Per me Umberto non esiste più; resta solo il padre dei miei figli, che gli vogliono bene e lo rispettano come è loro dovere"*, is verbatim in it.wikipedia [A]; **undated in every source reached [gap]**. An earlier pass sourced an English translation to laRegione.ch; that page was fetched in full and carries no Ammaturo quotation.

1994: the Tribunale civile di Roma rules the 1982 film must air uncut, as not injurious to its subject's reputation [A: it.wikipedia, citing *la Repubblica* of 29 June 1994]. The broadcast is a court defeat, not a delay that expired. The Rai Tre channel is **English-only [R]**.

2004: her former Naples apartment becomes a Comune di Napoli social-services office [A: it.wikipedia, citing *la Repubblica* of 26 February 2004]; an earlier pass mis-sourced this to English Wikipedia.

> **CONTRADICTION 10: where she lived in old age. Unresolved.** it.wikipedia: "Chiusi i negozi di Napoli, si ritirò a Castellammare di Stabia." English Wikipedia: she "lived alone in Sorrento." An earlier pass synthesised these into "dividing her time between" the two, which no source says; withdrawn. The journey pins Sorrento for retirement, Castellammare for the death.

*Pupetta, il coraggio e la passione* (2013), Canale 5, dir. Luciano Odorisio, with Manuela Arcuri; criticised for glamorizing organized crime [A: it.wikipedia]. Her reply of 7 June 2013: *"Ho pagato con lacrime e affanno le mie scelte. La prima volta perché l'uomo a cui ho sparato avrebbe fatto lo stesso con me. Cosa dovevo fare, farmi uccidere? Ero incinta."* [A: interris.it]

Died 29 December 2021, Castellammare di Stabia, aged 86 [A: ANSA; Fanpage; both Wikipedias; theweek.in; WION].

**The funeral.** Questura and Prefect forbade a public funeral; a service planned at Sant'Antonio di Padova did not take place, about ten women gathering there to pray, and only a private blessing of the coffin was authorized [A: Il Fatto Quotidiano, 1 Jan 2022]. Her lawyers called "camorrista" in the justification defamatory **[R: single source]**.

> **CONTRADICTION 11: the funeral date.** Il Fatto Quotidiano places the blessing on 31 December 2021; the Simonetti journey file records ANSA, Fanpage and Il Fatto Quotidiano reporting the ban on 1 January 2022, the morning the rite had been scheduled. Both printed.

**Her burial place is named by no source consulted. Stated as a gap, not filled by inference.**

**Borrelli's letter [A, re-sourced].** *"We are seeing on social media a glorification of this woman who is a symbol of the Camorra in our neighbourhood... The mythologising of bosses is to be avoided at all costs."* Francesco Emilio Borrelli, Europa Verde councillor, to the Naples police. **Traced to the primary source: The Guardian, 31 December 2021**, corroborated by Lucie-Smith. An earlier pass credited Newland's Substack, which does not contain it.

**The literature.** Marmo, "La rima amore/onore di Pupetta Maresca. Una primadonna nella camorra degli anni cinquanta," *Meridiana* 67 (Viella, 2010), 113-143, JSTOR 23204928 **[A; subtitle and issue dropped in an earlier pass]**. Longrigg, *Mafia Women* (1998); Fiandaca, ed., *Women and the Mafia* (2007); Nadeau, *The Godmother* (2022) [all A]. Vilain, *Pupetta*, Gremese, trans. Ferrini: **Italian edition, not a French first [A]**; date contradicted, 21 April 2023 in listings, 15 April on Amazon, 24 April in an earlier pass. Tisci (2014) **[R]**.
