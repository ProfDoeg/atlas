# Immanuel Kant (1724-1804): research report

*2026-08-02. Königsberg, Prussia (Kaliningrad, Russia since 1946). Queued as the corpus's paradox: a philosopher of pure reason and of cosmology whose only journeys were journeys in thought. Named in 3 corpus documents (census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. "First pass" means the raw research pool this file corrects.

**Governing difficulty.** Kant is the one traveler in this atlas with no itinerary: after August 1754 everything happens inside a few kilometers. The record offers a densely dated bibliography, three or four documented country addresses from the tutoring years, and a large body of legend repeated without documentation.

---

## 1. The saddler's quarter, 1724-1740

- Born 22 April 1724 in the Vorstadt, fourth of nine children (six reaching adulthood) of Johann Georg Kant, harness and saddle maker, and Anna Regina Reuter. **[A: Wikipedia, "Immanuel Kant"; Kuehn, *Kant: A Biography* (2001)]** Baptized Emanuel, the spelling changed later after he learned Hebrew. **[R: tradition via Wikipedia]**
- **The birth house is a gap.** It stood near the Hospital of the Holy Ghost, does not survive, and its plot is reconstructed from parish and city records rather than marked. The first pass carried it **[A]** at 54.704 / 20.515; demoted to **[R]** at quarter-level precision, the point falling in the right quarter but fixing no address. Which parish register holds the baptism is disputed between biographers, so the church is left unassigned. **[R: Vorländer, *Immanuel Kants Leben* (1911)]**
- Enters the Collegium Fridericianum under Franz Albert Schultz at about eight and stays roughly eight years, reacting strongly against its demand for a datable conversion and taking refuge in the Latin classics. British bombing destroys the building in August 1944 and the school closes in January 1945. **[A: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Kant"; Kuehn; Klemme (1994)]**
- **Mother's death, corrected.** Anna Regina died 18 December 1737, Kant thirteen; some accounts blame a physician's advice. **[A: de.wikipedia; Borowski (1804); Kuehn]** The chronology lens had this **[R]**, hedging that the year was unconfirmed, and so contradicted its own geography lens (already 1737-12); hedge struck, tag promoted. Kant credits her with awakening his feeling for nature and his moral seriousness. **[A: Borowski (1804), an eyewitness]**
- Matriculates at the Albertina at sixteen, September 1740, under Teske and above all Martin Knutzen, who introduces him to Newton. He studies and teaches at no other university. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP; Kuehn]**

## 2. The interrupted student and the tutor's road, 1745-1754

- *Thoughts on the True Estimation of Living Forces*, written 1745 on the vis viva controversy, printed 1749 **[A: Wikipedia]**; calling that controversy the Cartesian/Leibnizian quarrel over the measure of force is standard context, **[R]** as to Kant's own framing. The father, struck down in 1744 by Kuehn's relayed chronology, dies March 1746, and the lost income ends Kant's studies without a degree. **[A: Wikipedia; Kuehn]**
- **Judtschen (now Vesyolovka, Kaliningrad Oblast), 1747-1750**, tutoring the sons of the Reformed pastor Daniel Ernst Andersch; parish records list Kant as godparent to village children, and the restored house became a small Kant museum in 2018. **[A: Vorländer (1911); de.wikipedia, "Wessjolowka"]**
- **Groß Arnsdorf (now Jarnołtowo, Poland), 1750-1753**, on the estate of Major von Hülsen near Mohrungen **[A: Kuehn, citing Borowski]**, with three corrections. (1) **Coordinate wrong by about 22 km**: pl.wikipedia "Jarnołtowo" gives 53°53′48″N, 19°39′03″E = **53.8967 / 19.6508**, against the first pass's 53.905 / 19.98. (2) **Region misnamed**: Kreis Mohrungen is historic Oberland, Prussia proper, not Warmia (Ermland); the modern Warmian-Masurian voivodeship name is the likely source of the slip. (3) **Employer's given name is a live contradiction, unresolved**: Kuehn as relayed has "Bernhard Friedrich von Hülsen," pl.wikipedia "major Karl Friedrich von Hülsen." Local commemoration: plaque 1994, fire-station museum 2012, sculpture 2013. **[A: pl.wikipedia]**
- **Keyserlingk household: disputed, left disputed.** Borowski and Jachmann (1804), both personal acquaintances, describe a live-in third tutorship **[A as testimony]**; Kuehn ch. 4 denies it was a tutorship at all **[A as scholarship]**; de.wikipedia "Capustigall" supports Kuehn, saying Kant "lived but never in the place, but came for day visits from nearby Königsberg." **Coordinate corrected** from 54.9 / 21.6 (Rautenburg, 80 km northeast) to roughly **54.69 / 20.42**, Schloss Waldburg-Capustigall west of the city. Tag stays **[R]**.
- Returns permanently to Königsberg in 1754, August traditional **[A as to the year: Wikipedia; SEP; Kuehn]**. **The never-travelled claim is [R] throughout.** The geography lens appended to this attested return a sentence about his never leaving the vicinity again, smuggling the legend into an [A] item while tagging the same commonplace [R] in two other lenses. What the record supports is narrower: **no journey after 1754 is attested in any source reached**, an argument from silence, labelled as one.

## 3. Paid in student coin, 1755-1769

- **1755.** *De Igne* in April, bringing the Magister degree nine years late; *Nova dilucidatio* in September, bringing the venia legendi and an unsalaried Privatdozent's life paid by attending students. **[A: Kant (1755); Wikipedia; Kuehn]**
- **1755.** *Allgemeine Naturgeschichte und Theorie des Himmels*, anonymous: a solar system condensed from a rotating cloud (later the Kant-Laplace nebular hypothesis), the Milky Way one island universe among many. Publisher bankrupt, copies undistributed, no notice taken for decades. **[A: Kant (1755); SEP]**
- **1756-1757.** Three papers on the causes of the Lisbon earthquake, early scientific seismology; then the physical geography lectures, pioneering the discipline in Germany and continued "for over three decades." **[A: Wikipedia]** The papers were not read, so their mechanism as summarized is **[R]** as to wording, and the number of course iterations is a **gap**.
- **1758-1762.** Russian occupation: oath to Empress Elizabeth, Russian officers in his lecture room, better income, and a December 1758 petition for the chair of logic and metaphysics that goes to another candidate. **[A: Wikipedia; Kuehn]**
- **1762-1764.** *The False Subtlety of the Four Syllogistic Figures*; *Negative Magnitudes* and *The Only Possible Argument*, neither given a stop and both recorded so the bibliography is not thinned; *Observations on the Feeling of the Beautiful and Sublime*, which makes him a widely read popular author; and the prize essay placing second to Moses Mendelssohn at the Berlin Academy. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **1766-1772.** Assistant sublibrarian at the castle library (54.7096 / 20.5117), his first salary and only institutional income before the chair; *Dreams of a Spirit-Seer* the same year; Erlangen declined 1769 and Jena 1770, both without traveling. **[A: SEP; Wikipedia; Kuehn]**

## 4. The chair and the silence, 1770-1780

August 1770: appointed ordinary Professor of Logic and Metaphysics at forty-six after fifteen unsalaried years; the inaugural dissertation *De mundi sensibilis atque intelligibilis forma et principiis* already contains the appearance/thing-in-itself distinction. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP; Kant (1770)]** Then 1770-1781, the "silent decade" **[R: standard scholarly characterization, SEP summary]**: the phrase is the historians' and not Kant's, and the period's content is reconstructed from the result. **Gap:** the Marcus Herz correspondence, the principal primary evidence for its internal chronology, was not consulted, and no claim in the journey file rests on it.

## 5. The three Critiques, 1781-1790

- **1781.** First *Critique*, sent to Johann Friedrich Hartknoch in Riga; Kant never goes, and everything passes by letter and courier. **[A: Kant (1781); Kuehn]** **Anachronism corrected:** the geography lens placed the writing at the Prinzessinstraße house, bought only in 1783 by that same lens two entries later. The file anchors the book in rented rooms (54.706 / 20.51), whose sequence biographers dispute and none of which survive. **[R: Vorländer (1911)]**
- **1783.** Buys the Prinzessinstraße house, his first property, and lives there until his death; demolished 1893, the site built over. **[A: de.wikipedia, residence section, citing Vorländer]** The *Prolegomena* appears the same year.
- **1784.** *An Answer to the Question: What is Enlightenment?* with "Sapere aude" **[A: Wikisource, fetched]**; *Idea for a Universal History*, whose Sixth Proposition carries the crooked-timber image **[A: de Quincey, Wikisource, fetched]**.
- **1785-1790.** *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* **[A: Abbott, Gutenberg 5682, fetched]**; *Metaphysical Foundations of Natural Science*, Reinhold's popularizing letters, a first rectorate and election to the Prussian Academy by post, all 1786; the revised second edition of the first *Critique*, 1787; *Critique of Practical Reason* and a second rectorate, 1788; *Critique of the Power of Judgment*, 1790. **[A: Wikipedia; Kuehn; Kant (1788), (1790)]**

## 6. The censor and the disciples, 1791-1799

- **4 July 1791: Fichte's interview**, cold, after which he writes *Attempt at a Critique of All Revelation* in weeks to earn Kant's regard; Kant arranges its anonymous publication in 1792 and Germany mistakes it for Kant's fourth Critique. **[A: fichte.journey.json]** Canonical pin 54.71 / 20.51 inherited byte-identical.
- **1792 block and 1793 publication, corrected and split.** The Berlin censorship commission stops the second Piece of *Religion within the Bounds of Bare Reason* in 1792; Kant routes all four Pieces through the philosophy faculty at Jena and the book appears complete in 1793. **[A: en.wikipedia]** The first pass attached a royal reprimand to 1792. Wrong: the royal order followed the **second edition of 1794** ("When he published a second edition in 1794, the censor was so irate that he arranged for a royal order").
- **1794-1798.** October 1794, the cabinet order under Friedrich Wilhelm II forbidding further writing or lecturing on religion, with which Kant complies while framing the pledge narrowly as personal to the king **[A: Wikipedia; cabinet correspondence cited in Kuehn]**; 1795 *Perpetual Peace* **[A: Wikisource, fetched]**; 1796 retirement at seventy-two after forty-one years in one institution **[A: SEP]**; 1797 *The Metaphysics of Morals*; 1798 *The Conflict of the Faculties*, whose preface prints the royal order and his reply **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **1799, split from 1798.** The open letter denouncing Fichte is **1799**, the *Erklärung in Beziehung auf Fichtes Wissenschaftslehre* of August 1799: en.wikipedia states that "Kant opposed these developments and publicly denounced Fichte in an open letter in 1799." The first pass merged the two events into one year.

## 7. Three traditions, all [R]

Kept in the journey file because suppressing them would misrepresent what this figure is in general memory. None is documented.

1. **The clock-setting walk** along the rampart later named the Philosophengang, traceable to Heine (1834), thirty years posthumous. *Internal contradiction now fixed:* the chronology lens tagged it **[A]** while the geography and afterlife lenses tagged the identical claim **[R]**. **[R]** stands.
2. **The single missed walk.** Sources disagree whether the cause was Rousseau's *Emile* or the news from France. **Not resolved**; both recorded.
3. **The ten miles, and Pillau.** That he never went more than about ten miles from Königsberg, and never the forty kilometers to Pillau to see open sea, after four decades lecturing on physical geography. No source confirms or denies a coastal visit; the claim rests on the silence of the letters and the biographers. **[R: popular tradition, e.g. Geier (2003)]**

## 8. Death and afterlife, 1800 to the present

- From about 1800 the faculties visibly decline; he works to the end on the manuscript published posthumously as the *Opus Postumum* **[A: SEP]**. He dies at the Prinzessinstraße house on **12 February 1804**, attended by his servant and a few friends, last coherent words reported as "Es ist gut" **[A: Wasianski (1804), an eyewitness]**. **Coordinate corrected** from the afterlife lens's 54.7104 / 20.4522, four kilometers west of the historic city in the modern port area, to **54.7085 / 20.5095**, the geography lens's own value; that erroneous longitude also sat on every quotes-lens entry and is used nowhere in the file.
- Lying in state and a public procession contemporaries call the largest Königsberg had given a private citizen **[A: Wasianski; newspapers cited in Kuehn]**; burial 28 February in the Professorengewölbe, the faculty vault at the cathedral's northeast corner **[A: de.wikipedia, "Königsberger Dom"]**. **Gap:** the sixteen-day interval is arithmetic from two attested dates and nothing reached explains it.
- **The Rauch statue: a flagged gap now closable.** Cast **1857** by Hermann Gladenbeck in Berlin, the year Rauch died; unveiled **18 October 1864** on the Kantberg at Prinzessinnenstraße and Altstädtische Bergstraße; moved **1885** to the Paradeplatz; in **1944** carted to the park of Schloss Friedrichstein and buried ahead of the Red Army, and **lost ever since despite intensive postwar searches**, not melted for scrap. **[A: de.wikipedia, Königsberg Kant monument article]**
- **1880:** the remains go out of the cathedral into a neo-Gothic chapel at the northeast corner **[A: en.wikipedia, "Königsberg Cathedral"]**, which was **not** the Stoa Kantiana, a name belonging to the 1924 colonnade, and whose claimed motive, the centenary of the first *Critique*, nothing reached confirms. **1924:** Friedrich Lahrs's open colonnade in Rochlitz porphyry replaces it for the bicentenary **[A: de.wikipedia, "Königsberger Dom"]**. RAF raids of August 1944 destroy the cathedral's spire and roof and burn the museum's Kantiana collection; the Soviets leave the colonnade standing through the demolition of the old city, and reconstruction begins in the early 1990s **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Open question, flagged:** de.wikipedia calls the 1924 structure a *Kenotaph* and records that the remains "wurde mehrfach umgebettet," so the first pass's "the sole point on earth Kant is verifiably known to occupy after death" cannot stand; the file claims only the 1924 reinterment, at the northeast corner, choir end (de.wikipedia's northwest corner of the choir building is the same spot from another reference point).
- **1992-2018.** On 27 June 1992 a replica by **Harald Haacke**, commissioned by Marion Gräfin Dönhoff with the ZEIT-Stiftung and Friedrich Wilhelm Christians, is erected beside the New Albertina; the first pass's 54.7104 / 20.5111 sits about 400 m south of the university main building at Universitetskaya Street 2, put by en.wikipedia at **54.71389 / 20.51000**, and its "cast from surviving German molds" needs checking, the sources describing a replica. In 2005 the university is renamed **Immanuel Kant State University of Russia**, announced by Putin and Schröder at Königsberg's 750th anniversary, the first pass dropping "of Russia"; in 2010 it becomes Immanuel Kant Baltic Federal University; in 2018 tomb and statue are vandalized with pink paint amid objection to naming the airport after Kant. **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**

## 9. Quotations

Eight quotes appear in the journey file, all verbatim from a fetched translation. Three corrections to the pool. (1) The humanity formula had lost its leading "So," restored from Abbott (Gutenberg 5682, Second Section): *So act as to treat humanity, whether in thine own person or in that of any other, in every case as an end withal, never as means only.* It sits on the 1797 *Metaphysics of Morals* stop with quote_source naming the 1785 Groundwork, an offset stated in the field. (2) The starry-heavens passage is **promoted [R] to [A]** from Abbott, Gutenberg 5683, Conclusion, line 6330, which reads "ever new and increasing admiration and awe," "the oftener," and "the starry heavens above and the moral law within" without "me"; the circulated wording, part Gregor and Pluhar, matches no single translation. (3) Crooked timber is **Isaiah Berlin's English, not Kant's**: de Quincey's *Idea of a Universal History*, Sixth Proposition, has *Out of wood so crooked and perverse as that which man is made of, nothing absolutely straight can ever be wrought*, so the file quotes de Quincey and names Berlin in the campa. "Es ist gut" is reported speech recorded by Wasianski, who was present. Verified but unused: the universal-law formulation; "Enlightenment is man's release from his self-incurred tutelage"; Preliminary Article 3 and the First Definitive Article of *Perpetual Peace*.

## 10. Interlocks

Kant travels nowhere, so every crossing is somebody coming to him or reading him. Slugs named in campa: **fichte**, the only physical crossing, 4 July 1791, canonical pin inherited byte-identical, plus the 1792 *Attempt* and the 1799 repudiation; **schelling** and **hegel**, the Tübinger Stift chamber of 18 October 1790 shared with Hölderlin, pin 48.5216 / 9.0576 from schelling.journey.json; **novalis** and **schiller** at Jena from October 1790, Reinhold lecturing on Kant, pin 50.9271 / 11.5892 from novalis.journey.json, reused for the 1793 Jena publication stop; **steiner** at Wiener Neustadt, the Critique bound inside a history textbook, pin 47.8151 / 16.2437, sharing that stop with **nietzsche** (Leipzig by night), **jung** (Basel by night) and **niels_bohr** (the Copenhagen Friday talk running "from Kant to the physiology of respiration"); **felix_adler** at the Groundwork stop and his ethical manifold; **descartes** at the 1754 return, the atlas's inverse traveler. Given the lifetime radius the journey clusters on one pin cluster rather than a route **[R: inference, as in the pool]**, and the three non-Königsberg stops are stops of the books.
