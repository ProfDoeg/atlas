# Viktor Sulčič (1895-1973): research report
*Slovene architect of the Mercado de Abasto and La Bombonera. Compiled 2026-08-16 for `viktor_sulcic.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or single uncorroborated witness · contradictions are flagged and left standing.

**Summary.** A farmer's son from a Slovene village above Trieste is conscripted out of trade school in 1914, studies sculpture and then architecture in two Italian academies, works a season on Meštrović's house in Zagreb, and sails for Argentina in 1924. Within two years he wins the largest architectural competition in the country, on drawings he was not legally permitted to sign. He builds the first raw reinforced-concrete building on the continent and the most famous football ground in South America, and in 1953, after the accidental shooting of his twelve-year-old son, stops building and spends his last twenty years painting mountains and self-publishing three books.

---

## 0. The state of the evidence

Four bodies of evidence of unequal weight, disagreeing on roughly a third of the datable facts. **slovenska-biografija.si, entry `sbi629661`**, carries **two** lexicon entries, the older **SBL** and the later, fuller **PSBL** (by Irene Mislej): edited scholarly articles citing archive material collected by **Josip Regali**, and the best witnesses reached. **es.wikipedia** is good on building chronology, loose on his life. **sl.wikipedia** carries family material found nowhere else and is **wrong on at least three datable points** the lexicons can check. **en.wikipedia** is a stub with a different birth date, wife's name and sons' names.

The error pattern in the uncorrected research pool is systematic: **the Wikipedia articles were weighted equally with the lexicon entries**, giving date errors of up to thirteen years, and **coordinates were repeatedly set at city centroids or the wrong city**.

**Not reached:** any Argentine archive, the Boca Juniors records, the Banco Hipotecario dossier, the 1927 *Revista de Arquitectura*, the 1972 *Meddobje* article, the 1939 *Naša sloga* articles, any copy of his books. See §9.

## 1. The borderland, 1895-1922

**Born 2 August 1895 in Križ pri Trstu (Santa Croce), on the karst above the Gulf of Trieste [A: SBL and PSBL independently; corroborated by es.wikipedia].** **en.wikipedia gives 14 January 1895** and cites nothing; flagged in `date_confidence`, not treated as an equal reading.

**Ethnonym: there is no dispute.** The brief called him "Croatian-born"; every source reached, **including en.wikipedia** ("an architect of Slovene ethnic origin"), calls him **Slovene**. The pool's cross-edition disagreement is an artifact.

**Parentage: two errors corrected.** Both lexicons give *"kmetu Jožefu in Mariji r. Bogatec"*, a **farmer named Jožef** and **Marija née Bogatec** [A]; sl.wikipedia gives a **fisherman named Josip** [R, contradicted]. And sl.wikipedia's "died roughly nine months before the birth" refers to **the younger brother's** birth; the pool transposed it. The aunt **Marjana Sulčič** as guardian is sl.wikipedia's alone [R].

**Coordinate correction, the largest in the set.** The pool carried 45.666/13.769 and 45.6167/13.85, roughly **9 km and 14 km** away. **Used: 45.7342 / 13.6925**, per it.wikipedia's 45°44′03″N 13°41′33″E for Santa Croce.

**Finishes the *obrtna srednja šola* in Trieste, 1914, and is conscripted at once [A: PSBL]. Gap: no unit, front, rank, wound or discharge anywhere.** The four war years are blank.

**Florence: sculpture first, architecture second [A: PSBL].** Both Wikipedia articles compress this to "studied architecture in Florence and Bologna", which is not what happened. He enrols at the **Accademia di Belle Arti** in **sculpture** under **Domenico Trentacoste**, switches after the first year, and PSBL dates the letter announcing the switch, to **Veno Pilon**, to **September 1920**; he continues alongside Pilon in **1921**. **The pool's "c. 1917-1920" is impossible**, since he was in uniform from 1914 and PSBL puts the Florence years *"Po prvi svetovni vojni"*. **Corrected: c. 1919-1921.**

**Graduates in architecture at Bologna, 1922 [A: PSBL].** Coordinate corrected from 44.4949/11.3426, about 800 m WSW, to **44.4966 / 11.3527**.

## 2. Riva, Zagreb, and the crossing, 1922-1924

**Riva del Garda, 1922-1923, looking for architectural work [A: PSBL, single witness; absent from both Wikipedias]. Gap: no office, employer, commission or building.** Kept as a stop because a lexicon puts him in a named town for two years, the single-source status flagged.

**Zagreb, 1923: works with the architect Harold Bilinić on the renovation of Ivan Meštrović's house on Mletačka street [A: PSBL, verbatim].** The pool flagged the address as inferred; it is not. **The street is documented**, and only the house number 8 is inferred. Coordinate corrected from 45.8131/15.9741, about 280 m off, to **45.8157 / 15.9737**. **The bronze head of Josip Juraj Strossmayer**, a gift from Meštrović, is sl.wikipedia's alone [R].

**Emigrates 1924 [A].** Two motives, not in conflict: the **push** of fascist pressure on Slovenes in the Julian March, es.wikipedia placing him in *"la segunda ola de inmigración eslovena"*; and the **pull** of PSBL's detail that the **engineer Luis Migoni**, already in Argentina, urged him to come. **Gap, complete: no port, line, ship, month or landing date**, and the Trieste departure and Dársena Norte arrival pins are both **[R]**.

## 3. The drawing board and the Banco Hipotecario, 1924-1928

**Eight months as a draughtsman in an unnamed Buenos Aires office, where he meets the geometer Raúl Bes [A: PSBL].** Generic city pin.

**Bragado municipal building, second prize. Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** es.wikipedia dates it **1928** and names no collaborator; PSBL narrates it **before** the March 1926 Banco Hipotecario episode, so **c. 1925 to early 1926**, and names the engineer **Migone**. **The file uses c. 1925 and records both.** Whether that Migone is the "Luis Migoni" of the emigration is **stated by no source** and **not asserted**.

**The Banco Hipotecario competition, and why Delpini is in the firm's name [A: PSBL, the fullest passage in either lexicon].** In **March 1926** Sulčič persuades Bes to work up an entry for the bank's central building on Diagonal Sur; with the drawings finished they discover **neither holds an Argentine-nostrified diploma** and neither may submit; they recruit the engineer **José Luis Delpini** to sign, and he agrees **on condition that all three produce a second variant**, signing both. Two entries go in, **ICATU** and **IMPROBUS**, against **46 competitors** [A: SBL]. On **31 December 1926** the jury awards first prize to **ICATU**, and the three constitute **"Delpini, Sulcic, Bes, Ingenieros, arquitectos"** *"takoj"*, immediately; plans published in the **Revista de Arquitectura**, 1927 [A: SBL].

**Two corrections follow.** The firm was founded **end-1926 or early 1927, not 1928** as es.wikipedia has it; and **Delpini is a party to the winning entry, not a later addition**, where the pool had him joining afterward, inverting why he is there at all.

**1928: construction suspended by the change of government, which es.wikipedia attributes to Yrigoyen's accession; fees recovered by court award [A: es.wikipedia and PSBL]. Gap: no source says what became of the site, or whether any part was executed.**

## 4. Luján, the Salon, and the Abasto, 1928-1937

**Luján cemetery portico [A for the commission, single-sourced for the year].** Both lexicons list it; **the year 1928 is es.wikipedia's alone.** The pool flagged the commission itself as unverified; it is verified, and only the date is not.

**Biblioteca Florentino Ameghino is in LUJÁN, not Buenos Aires**, the worst geographic error in the pool. PSBL: *"...v Lujanu, knjižnico Florentino Ameghino v istem kraju"*, in the same place; SBL's parenthetical *"vse B. Aires"* produced the mistake. Tag upgraded **[R] to [A]**.

**Mercado Vélez Sársfield**, likewise upgraded **[R] to [A]**: both lexicons list it, and PSBL's apparatus cites *Meddobje* (XII/1972, p. 77) carrying photographs. Coordinate corrected from -34.6037/-58.44, about 3.5 km NE, to **-34.6318 / -58.4917**. **Gap: no year**; the file's 1930 is a placeholder.

**Watercolours from 1929, shown at the Salón Nacional [A: es.wikipedia].** Every summary account gets the shape of the life wrong here: **the painting is not a retirement hobby.** It begins the year the Abasto is drawn and runs concurrently with the whole architectural career.

**The Mercado de Abasto: two live problems.** **(a) The opening date. Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** es.wikipedia: *"fue reinaugurado el 24 de marzo de 1934"*. PSBL: first phase opened **26 May 1934**, President **Justo** visiting **the day before**, second phase **1935**. The file uses 26 May, PSBL being the fuller entry and the only one accounting for the Justo visit; the March date is **not refuted**. **(b) The area figures are unusable as stated.** es.wikipedia gives **both 44,000 m² and a 58,000 m² total** in one article; PSBL gives **site 21,259 m²** with **12,161 m² built**. **The file quotes PSBL and states 44,000 nowhere.**

**Confirmed without change [A]:** designed 1929; cornerstone **28 December 1931**; five naves, central **20.60 m by 30 m high**, four side naves at **14.50 m**; a **115 m** facade; escalators rated **6,000 persons/hour**, the **first in South America**; the municipal **first facade prize for 1937**; a gold medal to the three partners. SBL dates the start of building to **1930**, a minor discrepancy left standing. **The superlative was understated, not overstated:** PSBL makes it **the first building on the continent** in raw reinforced concrete without plaster.

## 5. The Slav institutions, 1931-1943

***Novi list*: wrong by twelve years in the pool.** sl.wikipedia puts his presidency of the consortium in **1945**, alongside postwar refugee aid. PSBL: *"1933 je bil na čelu konzorcija, ki je začel izdajati tednik Novi list (izh. 1933-36)"*, and he serialised his own articles, **"Priseljenčeva hiša"**, in it in 1933-34 [A]. **The 1945 refugee-aid framing is supported by neither lexicon**; what PSBL records postwar is a concept sketch for the seat of the Slovene community.

**Also attested [A: PSBL and SBL]:** deputy leader of the Slovene **Sokol**, 1931; repeated chairman of the **Jugoslovanski Dom** at Dock Sud; **general secretary of Yugoslav National Defense** during the war, undated (the file's 1942 is a placeholder); the **Order of Saint Sava, Fifth Class**, from **Dr Izidor Cankar**, 1939.

**Hogar Yugoslavo at Dock Sud: wrong by a decade in the pool, and its open question is answerable.** PSBL: **Cankar** **commissioned** it, so c. 1937-38; Sulčič **published the design himself in *Naša sloga* in 1939**; and *"Dom je bil v okrnjeni obliki odprt 1943"*, **opened in reduced form in 1943**. It was built, but **not as drawn**. **Gap: which parts were cut, by whom, why.** Coordinate corrected from -34.6417/-58.3478, about 1 km NW, to **-34.6489 / -58.3406**.

**Marriage: the sources fork and are left forked.** sl.wikipedia gives the wife as **Anica**, Orthodox, from Vojvodina, sons **Todor** and **Igor**; en.wikipedia gives **Anna Kiselicki**, a piano teacher from **Vranjevo near Novi Bečej**, sons **Fedor** and **Héctor Igor**. **Vranjevo is in Vojvodina**, so the origin is assertable; **the elder son's name is not**. **Neither Slovene lexicon mentions the marriage. Gap: no date, city or church**; the file's 1935 is a placeholder.

## 6. La Bombonera, 1937-1940

**Attested and uncontested [A: es.wikipedia, *Estadio Alberto J. Armando*]:** the **1937** competition, won with a reinforced-concrete proposal; the foundation stone **18 February 1938**, club president **Camilo Cichero** and President **Justo** present; construction begun **6 August 1938** under the German contractor **GEOPÉ**; inauguration **25 May 1940**, Boca beating San Lorenzo **2-0**, **Ricardo Alarcón** scoring in the **13th minute**; three curved sides and one straight, dictated by a plot too tight for a bowl.

**Correction [A: PSBL]: 1940 is not the completion.** *"1940 so odprli prvo fazo in naslednje leto dokončali"*. Only the **first phase** opened. Later remodellings in 1941, 1953, 1996 and since are by other hands [R].

**The chocolate-box anecdote [R], told backwards in the pool.** es.wikipedia presents it as tradition rather than record, which is right, but its version is: *"una amiga le regaló una caja de bombones, que Sulčič comenzó a llevar a las reuniones... ya que su forma era casi exactamente igual a la del estadio"*. **A female friend gave him the box**, and only then did he carry it to meetings. The pool had him supplying it himself and dropped the gift, the hinge of the story. The verbatim Spanish is the `quote` on the competition stop. **Rejected:** the looser claim that the stadium was "designed in 1932 and finished in 1940".

## 7. Aluminé, the withdrawal, and the last twenty years

**Visits Ivan Benigar at Aluminé, Neuquén, 1943 [A: PSBL]**, having heard of him from the Salesian **Ludvik Pernišek**. Benigar reached Argentina in 1908, married a Mapuche woman and spent thirty years recording Mapuche language and law. **SBL calls him Janez**; **Ivan** is the Argentine form, and the one on the title page. The visit is the origin of the 1970 book, for which the pool had neither year nor origin.

**Iglesia del Sagrado Corazón de Jesús, San Justo: wrong by over a decade in the pool.** PSBL: **built 1941-42**, called **the summit of his architectural output**, with the entire interior furnishing his as well; the pool's 1930 is supported by nothing. Minor discrepancy left standing: SBL mentions an original concrete church there *"stekla 1939"*. **Coordinate gap:** the two lenses gave -34.6833/-58.55 and -34.6889/-58.5636, both approximations; the file uses the first and says so.

**The death of Igor [R, sl.wikipedia alone].** The younger son dies at twelve when a neighbour's firearm discharges accidentally, and the death prompts the abandonment of practice. **No date, place or name anywhere**; neither lexicon mentions it. The file's 1952 is inferred only from having to precede the 1953 withdrawal. **The causal link is sl.wikipedia's, not the lexicons'.**

**Leaves practice, 1953 [A: PSBL]**, a change of profession and not the start of the painting; see §4. **Exhibitions [A: PSBL]:** Galería Witcomb, Florida street, **1954** and **1959**; **Punta Arenas, 1955**; **Belgrade, 1956**. **Gap: no catalogue, notice, sale or work list**, and whether he travelled to Punta Arenas is not stated.

**The 1956 journey to Europe is real, and the pool left it open.** SBL: *"Sredi 1956 se je S. mudil v Evropi (Trst, Lj., Opatija, Dalmacija)"*; PSBL adds Belgrade. It is his **only documented return to Europe after 1924**.

**Anti-seismic housing. Contradiction flagged; the pool's date dropped.** es.wikipedia says only *"durante los años 1940"*, tying it loosely to San Juan and the January 1944 earthquake. PSBL places it **after his retirement**, so post-1953: prefabricated, fireproof, earthquake-resistant houses for the poorest, published in the **Asociación Sarmientina** bulletin and **donated to the city of Buenos Aires**, with **no mention of San Juan**. **The file drops the San Juan pin.**

**The books [A: SBL and PSBL], with one correction.** ***Luces y sombras***, poetry, **1965**, not 1968: both lexicons give 1965, and **only sl.wikipedia says 1968**, apparently contaminated from *La olla*. PSBL adds three poems in Slovene, self-illustration, and that **all three books were self-published**. ***La olla***, **1968** [A: sl.wikipedia; undated in both lexicons]. ***Ivan Benigar, el sabio que murió sentado***, **1970** [A: PSBL].

**Death 9 September 1973, Buenos Aires, aged 78 [A]. Gap: no cause, hour or address.** **Burial in the Bóveda de la Asociación Eslava, Cementerio de la Chacarita [A: es.wikipedia, single article]**; the article **on the cemetery**, fetched separately, **does not mention Sulčič or the vault**. **Gap: no burial date**; the file's 10 September is an inference.

**Posthumous retrospective in the hall of the municipal palace.** Upgraded **[R] to [A] for the event**, which PSBL states flatly. **The year is [R] and open**; PSBL gives none, and the pool's "1973-1974" probably derives from its apparatus citing *Glas SKA* for October-November 1973 and January 1974.

**The Abasto's afterlife [A: es.wikipedia]:** fire **1952**, Delpini reinforcing the structure; wholesale trading ends **14 October 1984**; cultural-patrimony declaration **1985**, stopping demolition; **IRSA** purchase **1995**; reopening as the **Abasto shopping centre in 1998**, not sl.wikipedia's 1999.

## 8. Interlocks, and why the list is empty

**No existing journey file mentions Viktor Sulčič, the Bombonera's architect, the Abasto's design, the Luján portico or the San Justo church by name** [A: grep across all `*.journey.json`]. Everything the interlock lens surfaced is a corpus-side inference, and **no atlas traveler is named in any campa**.

- **Diego Maradona** signed for Boca at La Bombonera in 1981 and spoke his farewell there in 2001. That is **Maradona's relation to Sulčič's building, not to Sulčič**, who was eight years dead when Maradona first played there. **A stop belongs to whoever was actually there:** those are Maradona's stops, on his own file. The engagement is citable, so `diego_maradona.journey.json` sits in **`suggested_refs` on the 1940 inauguration stop**, never as a scene.
- **Palanti, Gianotti, Christophersen, Colombo, Collivadino, Buschiazzo, Alvear, Xul Solar, Schavelzon**: cohort or era overlaps in Buenos Aires. **No source puts Sulčič in a room with any of them, in correspondence, or citing any of them.**
- **The people Sulčič actually stood beside** are named freely in the campas: Meštrović, Pilon, Trentacoste, Cankar, Benigar, Delpini, Bes, Bilinić, Migoni, Pernišek. **None has a journey file**, so none is an interlock. **False match blocked:** La Difunta Correa's shrine is at **Luján de Cuyo, Mendoza**, not **Luján, Buenos Aires province**.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identically:** Buenos Aires **-34.6037 / -58.3816** (ten address-less stops); La Bombonera **-34.6356 / -58.3648**, from `diego_maradona.journey.json`; Chacarita **-34.5908 / -58.4597**, from the Ángel Villoldo file.
