# Nadia Boulanger, 1887-1979: research report

*Written from the verified research pool for this run (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock and afterlife lenses, with the verifier's CORRECTED notes folded in). Nothing below was fetched fresh; where the pool says a source could not be read, that is recorded as a gap.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim reachable only through a secondary summary. Contradictions are flagged and left standing.

---

## 1. Origins and the Conservatoire, 1887-1904

- **Birth [A].** 16 September 1887, **30 rue La Bruyère, Paris 9e** (9ème Histoire; Encyclopedia.com). The pool said "Montmartre" and pinned the butte. In the older biographies "Montmartre" means the Nouvelle Athènes quarter of the ninth, not the hill; the label produced an 800 m error.
- **Parents [A, one clause downgraded].** Ernest Boulanger (1815-1900), composer, professor of voice at the Conservatoire; Raïssa Myshetskaya (1856-1935), singer, formerly his pupil. Her descent from a **Russian princely family is a family claim repeated by biographers, not a documented fact [R]**; the pool's "a Russian princess" is softened accordingly.
- **The fire bell [R].** At about five, per her own retelling in Monsaingeon, *Mademoiselle* (1985), p. 20, whose preface warns that some passages are reconstructed from memory. Neither street nor exact year recorded; Paris centroid.
- **Lili [A].** Marie-Juliette Olga born 21 August 1893, the only surviving sister.
- **Conservatoire entry [A on the fact, contradictory on the year].** Wikipedia: 1896. Britannica's training span: 1897-1904, and the pool's geography lens also carried 1897. **Not resolved**; dated 1896 with the split recorded. The Conservatoire was then on the **rue du Conservatoire**, moving to rue de Madrid only in 1911.
- **Teachers and prizes [A].** Composition with Fauré, organ with Widor. Harmony prize 1903; organ, accompaniment and fugue 1904. Meets **Raoul Pugno** in 1904.

## 2. The Wednesday class and the composer she stopped being, 1904-1918

- **Teaching [A].** From autumn 1904 at **36 rue Ballu** (now 3, place Lili-et-Nadia-Boulanger); the Wednesday class runs about seventy-five years.
- **Prix de Rome [A].** Second Grand Prix 1908 for *La Sirène*; never the First. Same year, *Les Heures claires* with Pugno.
- **A Conservatoire post from 1909 [R, and contradictory].** Encyclopedia.com has her as assistant/instructor "for decades" from 1909; every other source treats the 1946 chair as her first appointment there. **Not resolved.** No 1909 stop is staged.
- **Conducting debut [A on the fact, no venue].** April 1912, Société des Matinées Musicales. The pool placed this at 36 rue Ballu, wrong for a public concert. No hall named anywhere reachable; Paris centroid.
- **Lili's Prix de Rome [A].** 1913, first woman to win the First Grand Prix. Standard accounts date the announcement 5 July; the pool gives the year only.
- **La ville morte [A, via SFCV].** Opera written 1909-1913 with Pugno on a d'Annunzio libretto. Opéra-Comique premiere planned for 1914, aborted by Pugno's death on 3 January 1914 and the war; the orchestration was destroyed when the Normandy warehouse holding it was bombed, leaving only the piano-vocal score. First performance and world-premiere recording: Goren, Catapult Opera, Talea Ensemble, 2023-2024, Pentatone 2024. The pool had this as "a lost opera connected to Boulanger's circle" dated "2020s".
- **War work [A, redated].** The **Comité Franco-Américain du Conservatoire National de Musique et de Déclamation**, with Blair Fairchild. Standard accounts place the founding in **1915**, not 1914; Wikipedia frames it as 1914-1918 war work generally.
- **Lili's death [A, twice corrected].** 15 March 1918 at **Mézy-sur-Seine**, not Paris. **Intestinal tuberculosis** is the contemporary and still standard attribution (Wikipedia, "Lili Boulanger"; NAC biography); **Crohn's disease is a modern retrospective re-diagnosis [R]** and is named as such, not stated flat. Nadia effectively stops composing from this month.

## 3. Fontainebleau and the American pipeline, 1919-1939

- **École normale de musique [A, ownership corrected].** She **joined** the faculty in 1919; she did **not** co-found it, the founders being **Alfred Cortot and Auguste Mangeot**. The pool's geography lens asserted co-founding. Address 114 bis bd Malesherbes.
- **Fontainebleau [A].** 1921, professor of harmony in the Louis XV wing, first director Widor. **Aaron Copland** is in the first intake and stays three years.
- **The pupil list [A, two names removed].** Copland, Glass, Carter, Piazzolla, Quincy Jones, Barenboim, Virgil Thomson, Piston, Harris, Diamond, Markevitch, Lipatti, Gardiner. **Darius Milhaud removed**: contemporary and colleague, not pupil. **Leonard Bernstein removed**: he sought her out and consulted her but never formally studied with her. Neither is in Wikipedia's list.
- **The two totals are not rival estimates [A].** ~1,200 is the career total (La Poste; JAMS); "over 250" counts *notable* students. The pool's "sources disagree on the exact total" is a false framing, dropped.
- **First American tour [A, conflation split].** Sailed **24 December 1924** from Cherbourg on the **RMS Aquitania**, arrived New York **31 December**, returned **28 February 1925**. The Copland *Symphony for Organ and Orchestra* premiere is a separate event: **11 January 1925, Aeolian Hall**, under Damrosch, Boulanger at the organ, and it was the **world** premiere, not the American one. Split into two stops.
- **Cherbourg, anachronism removed.** The **Gare Maritime Transatlantique did not exist in 1924** (built 1928-1933). Passengers were tendered out from the older quay; the file names the port, not the terminal.
- **Gershwin [R, redated].** Wikipedia's bare "1927" is the outlier: his Paris stay was **March-June 1928**, and his own letter to her refers to meeting "two years ago", c. 1926. Sources report she declined, saying she could give him nothing he did not already have. The pool's rendered line about unlearning what already worked is **not an attested quotation**; dropped. **[R]**, dated 1928, Paris centroid.

## 4. The podium years, 1936-1939

- **London [A, venue R].** 1936, first woman to conduct the **London Philharmonic**; November 1937, first woman to conduct a complete **Royal Philharmonic Society** concert. Queen's Hall is tagged [R] in the pool for 1936; no programme reachable. **Monteverdi [A]:** 1937, six HMV discs of madrigals, place given only as "Paris/London".
- **Boston [A, tour length corrected].** 1938, first woman to conduct the **BSO**, taking the baton from Koussevitzky. Tour ran **February to May 1938**, four months, not three. Counts conflict: 40 concerts and 60 lectures in one account, "over 100 lectures" in another. **Both approximate.**
- **Dumbarton Oaks [A, date supplied].** She conducted the premiere of Stravinsky's Concerto in E flat, Washington, 1938. Pool gives the year only; standard accounts give 8 May 1938. Flagged in `date_confidence`.
- **1939 [A].** First woman to conduct the **New York Philharmonic**, the **Philadelphia Orchestra** and the **National Symphony**; 102 lectures in 118 days. The pool wrongly put these "before year's end" in 1938; they are 1939. The NSO venue (DAR Constitution Hall) is **inference [R]**: no source names a hall, though it was the orchestra's home.

## 5. Exile, 1939-1946

- **Gargenville, September 1939 [A].** Fontainebleau closed by the war; she shelters at Les Maisonnettes, where the Stravinsky family are also in the village.
- **The escape [A, one leg R].** **Barcelona**, October 1940, where **Ethel Thurston** joins her; **Madrid**; **Lisbon**, November 1940, where Thurston talks her off a Greek liner and onto the American **SS Excambion**; the Greek ship never reaches America **[R]**. Luggage travelled separately under Thurston's safer papers. *Teaching Stravinsky* (Oxford Academic), ch. "The War".
- **New York [A].** 6 November 1940, temporary visitor's permit. **Peabody [A]:** 1942, Baltimore. **Return [A]:** January 1946.
- **Longy [A, address corrected].** 1940-1945 (Longy's history page). The pool's coordinate pointed at the **present** campus at 27 Garden Street; the wartime address was **1 Follen Street**.

## 6. The room reopens, 1946-1977

- **Conservatoire chair [A].** Professor of accompaniment, 1946-1957. **Michel Legrand** studies with her seven years from 1946 without a first prize; his own account is that the pupils she meant to keep were the ones she gave nothing to.
- *Location note for the operator.* The interlock lens inherited **48.8723, 2.3468** (rue du Conservatoire) for this 1946 stop. That building is right for 1896-1904 and wrong for 1946: the Conservatoire moved to **14 rue de Madrid in 1911**. The file keeps the old pin for the childhood stops (shared with `erik_satie.journey.json`) and uses **48.8785, 2.3207** here. **A deliberate departure, recorded so it can be overruled.**
- **Fontainebleau directorship [A, four candidate years].** 1948 (afterlife lens), **1949** (French Wikipedia; robertcasadesus.com: Casadesus ceded the Resident Director post that year, held "pendant trente années jusqu'à sa mort"), 1950 (Britannica), 1953 (English Wikipedia, for the whole School, music plus fine arts). Ruling followed: **1949 for the Conservatoire américain, 1953 for the combined school, Britannica's 1950 dropped.** **Ordered, not resolved.**
- **Piazzolla, 1954 [A on the event, R on the words].** He arrives on a French grant, hides the bandoneón and the cabarets, is told she cannot find him in his classical scores, and is pushed back toward tango. Two strands render her reaction incompatibly and **must not be fused**: (1) his own recollection via Judd, thelistenersclub.com, 17 Mar 2021, where he plays "some bars of a tango of my own" and she says **"You idiot, that's Piazzolla!"**, naming no piece; (2) the strand naming **Triunfal** (CSU Northridge thesis; Wikipedia), which gives **no quotation**, or renders her as "This is Piazzolla! Don't ever leave it!" The file quotes strand 1 and names no piece; Gorin is cited **without a page**.
- **Monaco [A].** 19 April 1956, music for the Rainier-Kelly wedding at Saint Nicholas. The dead-composers-only detail comes from LA Phil, "Get to Know: The Boulangers".
- **1962 [A].** Turkey with İdil Biret; invited to the White House by President Kennedy. **1964-1966 [A]:** Philip Glass on a Fulbright at 36 rue Ballu.
- **Moscow, 1966 [A, one framing dropped].** Jury of the International Tchaikovsky Competition; also teaching at the Yehudi Menuhin School, the Royal College and the Royal Academy. The pool's claim that the Great Hall was "built for the composer whose name the prize carries" is **wrong**: the hall dates from 1901, Tchaikovsky died in 1893.
- **Honours [A].** Grand Officer of the Légion d'honneur, 9 February 1977; ninetieth birthday at Fontainebleau, 13 August 1977.

## 7. Last years, death, afterlife

- **The film [A, internal inconsistency flagged].** Monsaingeon's *Mademoiselle: Nadia Boulanger*, his first, shot in the late 1960s and early 1970s and re-cut for 1977, preserves a Wednesday lesson on Schumann's *Davidsbündlertänze*. The pool says she was "in her late eighties" during the shoot; **she turned 80 in 1967**, so age and shooting dates cannot both stand. Not resolved; the file dates the apartment stop to the 1976 filming Encyclopedia.com describes and asserts no age.
- **Death and burial [A].** 22 October 1979, Paris, aged 92, brought back from Fontainebleau by ambulance weeks earlier. Cimetière de Montmartre, **division 33**, family tomb with Ernest and Lili (Find a Grave 13917567; Alex Ross, 2012). **Burial date not documented**; placed days after the death and marked inferred.
- **The apartment and the square [A, arithmetic corrected].** Bequeathed to the Institut de France. Place Lili-Boulanger from 5 May 1970; **Place Lili-et-Nadia-Boulanger from 19 November 2021**. The pool's "fifty-one years after Nadia's death" is wrong: 2021 is 42 years after 1979; fifty-one is the gap from the 1970 naming. **Les Maisonnettes [A]:** bought by Gargenville 1998, **Maison des Illustres 2014**, not 2015.
- **Archives and editions [A / R].** BnF "Fonds Boulanger, Nadia". Thirty-three letters, 1942-1977, at BAnQ **[R]**, known only through a Sherbrooke paper. Francis, *...and the Stravinskys*, Rochester, **2018** (not 2016).
- **The Thomson line [A, date-stamp dropped].** The "one-woman graduate school ... a five-and-dime and a Boulanger pupil" bon mot, quoted in full on the burial stop. Attribution confirmed (JAMS review). The pool's 1979 date-stamp is arbitrary; the line predates her death.
- **Stamps [A on one, R on the rest].** French stamp issued **18 September 2017**. The 1983 Gargenville philately campaign, its causal link to the 2015 heritage status, and the Monaco stamps of 1985 and 2005 are single-sourced to a town page **whose fetched text does not contain them**; all three excluded.

## Contradictions between sources, not adjudicated

**(1)** Conservatoire entry year, 1896 vs 1897. **(2)** A Conservatoire post from 1909 (Encyclopedia.com) vs everyone else's 1946; not staged. **(3)** Fontainebleau directorship, 1948 / 1949 / 1950 / 1953, four sources, at least two different offices; ordered, not resolved. **(4)** 1938 tour arithmetic, 40 concerts and 60 lectures vs "over 100 lectures". **(5)** Total pupils, ~1,200 career vs "over 250" notable: not competing figures. **(6)** Lili's cause of death, intestinal tuberculosis (contemporary) vs Crohn's (modern retrospective). **(7)** Monsaingeon film chronology: "late eighties" during a late-1960s shoot is impossible, left standing as a source problem. **(8)** Piazzolla's words, two incompatible renderings, not fused.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Boulanger is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no apparatus of hers is applied to anything. But the interlock lens surfaced relations **not rooted in her own life**; per the standing rule they are reported, not silently deleted. None is written into the journey file as a stop or named in a campa.

**Backward.** **Erik Satie** at the Conservatoire on the rue du Conservatoire, 1879-1886, the same building she entered in 1896: shared geography only, no source connects the two people, dropped from the campa, pin retained. **Diaghilev commissions *The Firebird*, 1910**: reaches her only through Stravinsky, third-party.

**Contemporary but not hers.** **Coco Chanel** and the Stravinskys at Garches from 1920; **Victoria Ocampo**, Stravinsky and *Perséphone*, 1932; **Balanchine's *Apollon musagète*, 1928** and **Balanchine at the Teatro Colón, 1941**; **Stravinsky buried on San Michele, 1971**. Shared city or shared Stravinsky, none within her own life.

**Forward (not licensable, not written).** **Osvaldo Pugliese and Piazzolla's sextet at the Koninklijk Theater Carré, Amsterdam, June 1989**, ten years after her death. Pugliese's stop and Piazzolla's, on their own files.

The two atlas travellers named in campas are **John F. Kennedy** (he invited her to the White House in 1962) and **Tchaikovsky** (she juried the competition carrying his name, in the conservatory where he taught harmony). Everyone else in the prose, Copland, Gershwin, Glass, Piazzolla, Legrand, Stravinsky, Pugno, Fauré, Widor, Cortot, Damrosch, Koussevitzky, Biret, Thurston, has a direct documented relation to her and **no journey file in the atlas**.

## Gaps, stated as gaps

- **No venue for the April 1912 conducting debut**; Paris centroid.
- **No venue and no exact date for the Gershwin refusal**; only his Paris stay, March-June 1928.
- **No hall named for the 1939 National Symphony concert.** Constitution Hall is inferred from the orchestra's home address.
- **No quay named at Cherbourg in 1924**, and the terminal that would later serve that traffic did not exist.
- **No Buenos Aires stop.** The interlock lens flagged the missing outbound half of the Piazzolla crossing. It stays missing: she never went to Argentina, and staging his departure here would put her where she never was. That scene belongs on a Piazzolla file the atlas does not have.
- **No `gershwin`, `astor_piazzolla`, `aaron_copland`, `philip_glass`, `igor_stravinsky` or `michel_legrand` journey file** exists. Six one-directional crossings live only inside this file.
- **The Gorin memoir page was not read**; cited without a page. **The Gargenville stamp campaign and the Monaco stamps** could not be corroborated; excluded. **Burial date not documented**; inferred. **1890s household addresses** (fire bell, Lili's birth) not documented; both at the Paris centroid.
