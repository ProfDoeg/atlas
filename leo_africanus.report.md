# Leo Africanus (al-Hasan ibn Muhammad al-Wazzan al-Fasi): research report

*Granada c. 1485/1494 to Tunis c. 1554, both ends disputed. Compiled 2026-08-14.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and **not** adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method note.** His own book can be quoted directly: the 1600 John Pory translation, reprinted by the Hakluyt Society in 1896 in three continuously paginated volumes, is on the Internet Archive in full text. Every quotation below was checked against the scan rather than against a secondary quoting it, which overturned four incoming citations (see **Quotation audit**). His *biography*, by contrast, rests on two reference articles and one modern monograph (Davis, *Trickster Travels*, 2006) that could not be read. The asymmetry is the main thing to know here: **the words are verifiable and the life is not.**

---

## 1. Granada and the flight (c. 1485–c. 1500)

- Born **al-Hasan ibn Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Wazzan al-Fasi al-Gharnati**, in Granada. **[A]** (Wikipedia; World History Encyclopedia)
- **Birth year contradicted, flagged, not resolved. [R]** Wikipedia and most reference works give **c. 1494**; World History Encyclopedia gives **c. 1485**. Wikipedia is explicit that the year "can be estimated from his self-reported age at the time of various historical events." No version is documentary.
- **A dating error propagated through the pool and is corrected here.** Several summaries pair a c. 1494 birth with "the final years of Nasrid rule" and a flight "after the fall of Granada in 1492." Granada fell on 2 January 1492, so a child born c. 1494 is born under **Castilian** rule and cannot flee a conquest that preceded him. English Wikipedia says "Kingdom of Castile" and dates the move "soon after his birth." **The two chronologies do not mix:** either 1485 with a 1492 flight, or 1494 with a flight in the later 1490s. Both are carried; neither is chosen. **[A for the fall; R for either reconstruction]**
- No source places the birth in a **named quarter**. The Albaicin is used in the journey file with an explicit hedge, but it is conjecture. **[R]**
- The family relocated to **Fez**. **[A]** (both sources) The **uncle who served the Wattasid sultan as a diplomat** rests on a single source (World History Encyclopedia, which names Sultan Muhammad al-Shaykh) and is therefore **[R]**.

## 2. Fez: the schools (c. 1500–1510s)

- Educated through neighborhood Qur'anic schools, madrasas, and the **University of al-Qarawiyyin**. **[A]** (English Wikipedia: he "studied at the University of al-Qarawiyyin"). Caution: the al-Qarawiyyin article lists him among associated scholars without asserting attendance, so confirmation runs one way only.
- **The pool's date range is wrong at both ends.** It gave "1492–1507" for his education. He cannot have begun school two years before the birth year the same entry uses, and his schooling did not end in 1507, since the Timbuktu mission falls c. 1510 and he was a student-notary shortly before it. Read **c. 1500 to the 1510s**. **[R]**
- He worked as a **notary at a hospital in Fez** to fund his studies. **[A]** (World History Encyclopedia, sole source)
- **The Safi anchor is not sourced where the pool said it was.** That he was a boy of about twelve when the Portuguese took Safi, widely used to date his birth, could **not** be found in the English Wikipedia article cited for it; it descends from Davis. The occupation is moreover dated **1508**, not 1507 (Wikipedia, "Safi, Morocco"). Retained as **[R]** with the correct year and the sourcing problem stated.

## 3. The caravan south (c. 1509–1512)

- He accompanied an uncle on a **diplomatic mission for the Wattasid sultan of Fez** that reached **Timbuktu**, then under Songhai rule, c. 1510. **[A]** (both sources) Further, apparently more independent journeys across West Africa, North Africa, Egypt, Portugal and Ottoman territory follow over roughly a decade. **[R]** (World History Encyclopedia)
- **Sijilmasa: the pool's characterization is wrong.** It called the town a "ruined but still-used entrepot." Sijilmasa was **abandoned in 1393**, and Leo's own observation, quoted in Wikipedia's Sijilmasa article, is that former residents had moved out into outlying villages and castles. He found it destroyed. And **no source routes his caravan through it**. **[R]**
- **Taghaza: the pool's coordinate was wrong by about 57 km and its hedge too strong.** Wikipedia places the salt mines at 23°36'N 5°00'W (23.600, -5.000) in the Taoudenni region; the pool's 23.68, -5.55 lands in empty desert to the west. Wikipedia also states Leo **visited around 1510 and spent three days there**, complaining of the brackish well water. Coordinate corrected; **[A for the visit; R for how much of the salt-trade account is eyewitness]**.
- **Gao: the pool's coordinate did not point at the landmark it named.** The Tomb of Askia is at 16°17'23"N 0°02'40"W (16.2897, -0.0444); the pool's 16.2661, -0.0454 sits 2.6 km south, in Gao town. Corrected. Askia Muhammad I (r. 1493–1528) is right for the date. Whether Leo went to Gao himself or folded an informant's report into his own voice is **disputed and left open**. **[R]**

## 4. The Maghreb circuit (1510s)

Stops here come from **his own book**, which describes the places, not from external records placing him at them. Every tag reads **[A for the description; R for the visit]** unless noted.

- **Marrakech** (ruined palaces, reduced population under the Kutubiyya), **Tlemcen** (Zayyanid seat), **Tunis** (Hafsid capital, Zaytuna), and **Taza** and **Oujda** on the road east from Fez, the last two placed by him in the Book of the Kingdom of Fez. **Oran**, Spanish since **1509** **[A for the seizure; R for his presence]**.
- **Bejaia / Bougie**, taken from Sultan Abdelaziz by Pedro Navarro in **1510** **[A for the seizure]**. The pool cited a "Book on the Kingdom of Bejaia," which **does not exist**: the work has seven regional books (Marrakesh, Fez, Tlemcen, Tunis, Numidia, the sub-Saharan regions, Egypt), and Bejaia falls inside the Tunis book. His presence in a Spanish-held port is also **not established**, so "documents firsthand" drops to **[R]**.
- **Sale / Rabat: the pool's label conflated two cities.** The Kasbah of the Udayas is in **Rabat**, opposite Sale (34.0306, -6.8356). The pool's coordinate (34.0347, -6.8362) is itself in Rabat, 4 km from Sale's centre. The journey file **retitles the pin to Rabat** rather than moving it.
- **Sousse (Susa), Tunisia.** Newly identified here. The passage about staying four days "in regarde of the danger of the time" sits in the chapter "Of the towne of Susa," before "Of the towne of Monaster." The pool had it as "town unspecified." It is Sousse. **[A]** The journey file's coordinate (35.8256, 10.6369) is the modern city centre, **not** from the source; flagged there.

## 5. East, in the year of Selim (1517–1518)

- **Diplomatic mission to Constantinople** for the sultan of Fez (named as Muhammad al-Burtughali). **[A]** (Wikipedia)
- He was in **Rosetta** during the Ottoman conquest of Egypt, and travelled through **Cairo** and **Aswan**. **[A]** (Wikipedia)
- **Alexandria is not attested.** The pool listed it [A] as part of "his eyewitness account," but Wikipedia's 1517 itinerary names Rosetta, Cairo and Aswan and **not** Alexandria. Downgraded to **[R]**. Note the crosswind: his own Rosetta passage says Selim was "returning this way from Alexandria," which puts the sultan there, not Leo.
- **Red Sea crossing, Arabia, and the hajj: inference, not record. [R]** The pool tagged this [A] while its own wording conceded "is believed to have performed." No source narrates the rite. **French Wikipedia complicates the route**, saying his ship was attacked *revenant de La Mecque*, against the Cairo-departure version in World History Encyclopedia. Both carried; neither chosen.
- **Egypt superlative removed.** The pool called the Egypt section "the fullest Book" of the work. Egypt is one of seven regional books and is not the longest. Cut.

## 6. Capture, Rome, baptism (1518–1520)

- **Captured at sea by Christian corsairs**, summer sailing season, 1518. **[A]** Location contradicted: **Djerba** in Pory's chapter heading, **near Crete** in Wikipedia ("more probably near Crete"). **Flagged, not resolved.** The corsairs are called Italian (Pory), Spanish, and probably Venetian (Brown, 1896). No two agree.
- **Held on Rhodes** by the Knights Hospitaller before being sent to Rome. **[A]** Coordinate correction: the Palace of the Grand Master is at about 36.4447, 28.2247, some 380 m from the pool's pin.
- The **transfer route through Italy is undocumented.** A southern Italian landfall is an inference from ordinary corsair practice and nothing more; it is dropped rather than pinned. **Stated as a gap.**
- **Presented to Pope Leo X**, detained at **Castel Sant'Angelo**. **[A]**
- **"Freedom on condition of conversion" is a narrative gloss and is dropped. [R]** Neither English, French nor Italian Wikipedia states that manumission was conditioned on baptism. The journey file gives the sequence without the bargain.
- **Baptized 6 January 1520, Feast of the Epiphany, in St Peter's Basilica**, as **Johannes Leo de Medicis**. **[A]** Two corrections: the **day** comes from World History Encyclopedia, not Wikipedia, which gives only the year; and the pope was **namesake and patron**, not godfather. The godfather of record is **Cardinal Egidio da Viterbo** (Wikipedia, "Egidio da Viterbo": "a Muslim convert who was a godson of Giles of Viterbo").

## 7. The Roman and Bolognese years, and the book (1520–1527)

- Papal patronage, Italian and Latin, teaching Arabic in the papal circle, including to **Egidio da Viterbo**. **[A]** **Date correction:** the pool ran "the court of Leo X" to 1523; Leo X died **1 December 1521**, so read 1520–1521. The Egidio connection extends past the pope's death (Leo revised a Latin Qur'an for him in **1525**), so the two must not share one range.
- **Departure amid suspicion under Adrian VI: date wrong, motive interpretive. [R]** Adrian VI was elected 9 January **1522** and did not reach Rome until August 1522, so a 1521 departure "under Adrian VI" is impossible. Wikipedia says only that after Leo X's death he left and spent "three or four years travelling in Italy."
- **Bologna**, fragments of an Arabic grammar and a medical vocabulary, collaboration with Jewish scholars. **[A]** c. 1522–1525.
- **Returned to Rome in 1526 under Clement VII. [A]** The pool's "1523–1526" for a Roman residence under Clement is wrong: Clement's papacy begins November 1523 but Leo was in northern Italy until c. 1525. Read **1526–1527**.
- **Manuscript completed 1526 [A]**, in Italian, titled *Cosmographia et geographia de Affrica*, later printed as *Descrittione dell'Africa*. A variant gives **1527 [R]** (World History Encyclopedia).
- **Sack of Rome, 1527.** The documentary record goes dark at or shortly after this event. **[A]**

## 8. After Rome: three theories, no record

The sources carry **three incompatible reconstructions**, all **[R]**, and this file carries all three: (1) **Tunis, with reversion to Islam**, dying c. 1554, the most cited, resting on later Arabic biobibliography rather than any contemporary record; (2) **Rome, dying c. 1550**, never having left Christendom; (3) **Tunis until Charles V took the city in 1535, then Morocco.**

- **The reversion is better evidenced than "argument from silence."** The pool called it pure inference "from the total silence of the post-1527 record." That is wrong and self-contradicting: English Wikipedia records that **Johann Albrecht Widmannstetter planned (and failed) to travel to Tunis to meet Leo, who had since reconverted to Islam**, a sixteenth-century report by a named near-contemporary. Still **[R]** as to the fact, since he never made the journey.
- **No date, place or manner of death is recorded, and no tomb is documented.** French Wikipedia: *il n'existe aucune information fiable sur la date et le lieu de la mort*. **[A]**

## 9. Afterlife

**Venice, 1550:** Ramusio first prints the work in Italian within *Delle navigationi et viaggi*; repeatedly reprinted. **[A]** **1556:** French and Latin translations. **[A]** **London, 1600:** **John Pory**, *A Geographical Historie of Africa*, made from the error-prone **Latin** rather than the Italian original. **[A]** **c. 1603:** scholars have suggested Shakespeare drew on Pory while preparing *Othello*. **[R]** His account of Timbuktu's gold and scarce salt shaped the European myth of the city as an African El Dorado for three centuries. **[A]** (Wikipedia, "Timbuktu") **1986:** Amin Maalouf, *Léon l'Africain*, a fictionalized first-person autobiography, becomes the most widely read modern popularization. **[A]** (French Wikipedia) **2006:** Davis, *Trickster Travels*, the major modern scholarly reconstruction, arguing for the return to Islam; **[A]** for its existence and thesis, contents **not read directly**. **2011:** BBC documentary *Leo Africanus: A Man Between Worlds*, presented by Badr Sayegh, directed by Jeremy Jeffs. **[R]**

## Quotation audit

Every quotation was verified against Internet Archive scans of the Hakluyt 1896 Pory (items `historyanddescr01leogoog`, vol. III, and `india.history.resource.88579`, vol. I). Four incoming citations were wrong:

1. **The "one of Granada / an African" sentence.** The submitted text was a **hybrid** of two renderings. Primary location is the translation itself at **vol. I, p. 190** ("I wil affirme", with commas after "of" and "discommended"). Brown quotes it in his Introduction at **p. v**, not p. iii, rendering it "affirme my self". **[A]**
2. **The Timbuktu book-trade sentence.** The pool truncated it on a false premise: the closing clause "than any other merchandize" **is** present, and the earlier full-text search failed only because the OCR interposes a marginal-note marker between "written" and "bookes". Restored in full. Citation **vol. III, pp. 825–826**, not vol. II p. 140. **[A]**
3. **The Selim passage.** Not a fragment. The OCR interleaves a marginal note ("John Leo was at Rasid the same time when Turke passed this way"), which made it look cut off. The city is **Rasid (Rosetta)**, not Cairo, and the event is **Selim I returning from Alexandria**, not the conquest. Citation **vol. III, p. 866**. **[A]**
4. **"The rich king of Tombuto hath many plates and scepters of gold, some whereof weigh 1300. poundes."** **Wrongly rejected in the incoming pool.** The earlier search failed on spelling alone: the 1600 text prints **"scepters"**, not "sceptres". Verified at **vol. III, p. 824**. Upgraded from [R] to **[A]**.

Also verified: the **Gerbi capture line** (a chapter **title** in Pory, not a marginal note; vol. III, pp. 733–734, and Djerba is treated in the book on **Tunis**, not Numidia); the **Abu Bacr sirnamed Pargama** sentence (vol. II, p. 143); the **Susa "fower daies"** sentence (vol. III, p. 727); and Ramusio's **"E indusse a farsi Christiano..."** (Brown's Introduction, vol. I, p. xlii n. 2), left untranslated per the verbatim rule. All **[A]**.

**Rejected outright:** the modern paraphrase beginning *"In Timbuktu there are numerous judges, doctors and clerics, all receiving good salaries from the king..."* circulates online in that exact wording but does **not** match Pory. It probably derives from a later translation (possibly Épaulard, 1956), which could not be accessed. It covers the same ground as the two verified Pory sentences and is **not** used. **[R, not quoted]** **Rejected as primary:** Robert Brown's sentence about "some Christian (probably Venetian) corsairs off the famous island of Djerba" is the **1896 editor's synthesis**, frequently requoted as though sixteenth-century. **[R]**

## Interlock note: no atlas traveler is named in any campa

The interlock lens returned a long list of adjacencies and **not one survives the naming rule**: Maimonides in Fez three and a half centuries earlier, Ibn Battuta in Timbuktu 170 years earlier, Ramon Llull dying on the Barbary coast, Ibn Arabi and Ziryab belonging to the al-Andalus whose collapse produced the Wazzan exile, Abd al-Qadir's hajj crossing the same Fez-Tunis-Cairo-Mecca string, Cervantes a structural parallel in Mediterranean captivity. **Every one is shared geography, shared tradition, or structural rhyme.** No reachable source shows Leo Africanus meeting, corresponding with, citing, or writing about any of them, and the map already shows co-location. So the campas name none. Canonical pins **are** inherited byte-identical: Kaaba 21.4225 / 39.8262; Fez 34.0656 / -4.9731; Timbuktu 16.7666 / -3.0026; Granada 37.1773 / -3.5986; Tunis 36.8065 / 10.1815; Rome 41.9028 / 12.4964. The one link that *would* have qualified is one-directional and its subject is not in the atlas: **Shakespeare reading Pory while preparing *Othello***, itself only **[R]**.

## Honest gaps

1. **No birth record, no death record, no grave.** Both ends of the life are later inference.
2. **Roughly 1528 to 1554 is empty**, apart from Widmannstetter's report that he was in Tunis and had returned to Islam. Twenty-six years, one sentence.
3. **The transfer route from Rhodes to Rome is undocumented.** No Italian landfall is pinned.
4. **Which parts of the *Description of Africa* are eyewitness and which are compiled from informants is unsettled**, affecting perhaps a third of the geographic stops. Where the book describes a place but no external source puts him there, the file says so in `date_confidence`.
5. **Davis (2006) was not read.** Several circulating claims (the age-twelve-at-Safi anchor, the conversion-for-freedom bargain, the Naples landfall) appear to originate with it and are carried as [R] with that provenance stated rather than dropped. The 1526 manuscript was likewise not consulted, so all dating of its completion is secondhand.

**Named and not reached**
