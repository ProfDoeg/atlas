# Alfred Jarry (1873-1907): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-15 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `alfred_jarry`. Companion file: `alfred_jarry.journey.json` (9 segments, 44 stops).*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried by biographers without a document · **[C]** = contradiction between sources, flagged and not resolved.

The pool was gathered through five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife) and then adversarially checked. The check found errors in every lens, mostly coordinates: eleven stops carried figures missing their own named building by 150 metres to 1.35 kilometres, one building drew three different coordinates from three lenses, and one point landed in open country matching neither town in its own claim. All corrections are tabulated below and repeated in the `date_confidence` field of the stop concerned, which is where the hedge lives. The campas state facts; the doubt sits in the metadata.

---

## 1. Laval and Saint-Brieuc, 1873-1888

**[A: fr.wikipedia, Alfred Jarry]** Born at Laval (Mayenne) on 8 September 1873 to Anselme Jarry, a merchant, and Caroline Quernest, from a Breton family of magistrates. Baptized at Laval cathedral 8 June 1874. An older sister, Charlotte, survives him and is his last close family tie.

**[C] Birth address.** The plaque address, 15 rue Charles-Landelle, is attested (fr.wikipedia), but **the biographers do not agree which house the birth room was in**, and the pool's own note concedes the street number is unconfirmed. Left open, in the campa as here.

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** In 1879 the family relocates to Saint-Brieuc in Brittany after Caroline leaves her husband; Alfred is schooled there until 1888.

**[R: Fell (2005)]** The disintegration of the household in Laval before that move, and Caroline's motive (unwillingness to depend on a failing merchant), are biographers' reconstruction. No date, no address. The journey file dates that stop 1877-01-01 as an explicit placeholder inside the 1874-1879 span.

**Gap.** Nothing in Jarry's own hand survives from the Laval or Saint-Brieuc years. The characterization of the schoolboy, already given to mockery and wordplay and a private mythology, is recollection set down decades later, after Ubu made him worth recollecting. Treated as [R] even where the biographies state it flatly.

## 2. Rennes, 1888-1891: Pere Heb and the Morins

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** October 1888, enters the rhetoric class at the Lycée de Rennes and encounters the physics master **Félix-Frédéric Hébert** (1832-1917), whose ridiculed classroom persona is the germ of Ubu.

**[C: authorship of *Les Polonais*].** The pool's chronology lens claimed Jarry "writes and helps stage the schoolboy puppet play Les Polonais," and the geography lens said he "co-writes" it. **Both are wrong and are corrected.** en.wikipedia (*Ubu Roi*) states that in 1888 Jarry *encountered* "a brief farcical sketch, Les Polonais, written by his friend Henri Morin, and Henri's brother Charles," which already ridiculed Hébert as king of an imaginary Poland; Jarry took it over and developed it. The journey file says *inherits* and *adapts*. In the pool's wording, the most-repeated fact about the origin of Ubu gave Jarry credit for someone else's invention.

**[R]** The name **Théâtre des Phynances** attaches to the boys' marionette performances but cannot be independently dated to 1888. The Morin house location within Rennes is approximate; the fact of the home performances is well attested (Arnaud 1974).

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** 1890, passes the second part of the baccalauréat with distinction. Folded into the Paris stop in the journey file rather than given a stop of its own.

## 3. Paris, 1891-1895: the school that refused him

**[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** 1891, to Paris to prepare for the École Normale Supérieure, at the Lycée Henri-IV, under Henri Bergson. Friendships with Léon-Paul Fargue and Albert Thibaudet. **He is never admitted to the École.**

**[C: number of attempts].** The geography lens said he sat the exam **twice**, in 1893 and 1894, and gave a coordinate for an "École Normale Supérieure entrance exam venue." All three claims are unsupported. fr.wikipedia: "il échoue par trois fois et successivement au concours d'entrée à l'École normale supérieure," plus two failed attempts at the licence. **Three, not two**; the years appear nowhere reached; and no single venue can be pinned. The stop is merged into the Latin Quarter lodgings and states three refusals.

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** 1893, two prose prizes at *L'Écho de Paris* (May and July), 250 gold francs; his first published texts, already carrying the Ubu figure. December 1893, joins the staff of *L'Art littéraire*.

**[R: en.wikipedia]** 1893, Jarry contracts influenza; Caroline falls ill nursing him and dies of it. fr.wikipedia corroborates the death in 1893 but not the sequence.

**[C: where Caroline died].** The geography lens flags Fell (2005) against Arnaud (1974): Paris in one, Laval in the other. **Not resolved.** Carried into the campa as an open disagreement, which is the only honest way to state it.

## 4. 1894-1895: Pont-Aven, the Mercure, the barracks

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** May 1894, Pension Gloanec at Pont-Aven; meets Paul Gauguin, Armand Séguin, Charles Filiger, Roderic O'Conor.
**[A]** 1894, reads an early *Ubu Roi* at the home of Alfred Vallette and Rachilde of the *Mercure de France*.
**[A]** October 1894, *Les Minutes de sable mémorial*, with ten of his own wood engravings.
**[A]** 1894, co-edits *L'Ymagier* with Remy de Gourmont; the partnership sours inside two years.

**[C: the regiment].** Chronology said the **149th** Infantry Regiment at Laval; geography said the **101st**. The 101st left the Paris garrisons for Laval in 1877 and was garrisoned there through the period (fr.wikipedia, *101e régiment d'infanterie*); the 149th was an Épinal regiment. **The 101st is carried.** The running joke about uniform sizes is [A: en.wikipedia] in substance, [R] in every detail of its telling.

**[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** 1895, Anselme Jarry dies; a small inheritance, quickly spent; medical discharge, officially for chronic biliary lithiasis. **[A: en.wikipedia]** 1895, *César-Antéchrist*, the first substantial appearance of Père and Mère Ubu in print.

## 5. 1896-1898: the premiere and its aftermath

**[C: publication date].** Chronology gave **10 June 1896** for the Mercure de France edition. fr.wikipedia gives only *juin 1896*; the day is unsupported and is defaulted to 01, with month precision noted in the stop. The fr.wikipedia article on *Ubu roi* records an earlier printing in Paul Fort's *Le Livre d'art* on **25 April 1896**, which the chronology lens missed entirely.

**[A: en.wikipedia, Ubu Roi]** 10 December 1896: premiere at the **Nouveau-Théâtre, 15 rue Blanche, Paris 9e**, hired by Aurélien Lugné-Poé for the Théâtre de l'Œuvre. Firmin Gémier plays Ubu. Riot in the house. Three lenses gave three different coordinates for this one building; see the table below.

**[R, demoted from [A]]** "Roughly fifteen minutes of uproar." Neither Wikipedia carries any duration; they say only that a riot broke out. The number is dropped from the prose, and the demotion is recorded in the confidence field.

**[A: en.wikipedia, Ubu Roi]** Jarry's curtain speech before the premiere, quoted in full at that stop.
**[A: W. B. Yeats, *The Trembling of the Veil* (1922)]** Yeats was in the audience and wrote the "After us the Savage God" passage about that night. This is the one interlock in the file where a named atlas traveler was physically present at a Jarry event.

**[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** 20 January 1898, puppet *Ubu roi* at the **Théâtre des Pantins**, 6 rue Ballu, co-founded with Franc-Nohain and Claude Terrasse, sets and costumes by Pierre Bonnard.

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** May 1898, Oscar Wilde, in Paris exile, meets Jarry. **Gap:** nothing of what passed between them is recorded anywhere reached.

## 6. The river years, 1897-1906

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** 9 July 1897, buys the mahogany skiff *L'As* at **Alfortville** and restores it with Vallette near Corbeil.

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** November 1897, evicted from the boulevard de Port-Royal, moves to the "deuxième étage et demi" at **7 rue Cassette**, about 25 m², ceiling too low to stand under; nicknamed the *Grande Chasublière*.
**[C, resolved in one direction]** Rachilde (1928) and later accounts place this apartment on the boulevard de Port-Royal instead. fr.wikipedia's dated November 1897 move supersedes that: **Port-Royal is the lodging he was evicted from**, not the half-storey.

**[R: fr.wikipedia]** 1897, meets the physician **Jean Saltas**, later a source for biographers and co-editor of the posthumous *La Dragonne*.
**[A]** 1897-1898, drafts *Gestes et opinions du docteur Faustroll, pataphysicien*.
**[A: Rachilde 1928; Arnaud 1974]** The riverside years near Corbeil and Le Coudray-Montceaux; the revolver fired in the garden. **[R]** The pistol reply survives only through Rachilde and is reworded in every retelling; en.wikipedia gives a different English version. Both wordings are recorded at that stop.

**[C: La Revue blanche].** The geography lens dated the association to **1901** and called Jarry "a kind of secretary" to the Natanson brothers. fr.wikipedia records that the review published him **from May 1896 to April 1903**, a span rather than an event, and **the secretary role is not attested in anything reached**. Downgraded from [A].

**[A: Shattuck (1955)]** Friendship with Henri Rousseau, whose studio was in the Plaisance quarter. Note the coincidence the sources do carry: **Rousseau was born in Laval**, as Jarry was. Rousseau's portrait of Jarry survives only mutilated, and accounts of the mutilation vary, so the details are left out of the prose.

**[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** *L'Amour absolu* (1899), *Ubu enchaîné* (1900), *Messaline* (1901), *Le Surmâle* (1902).

**[C: the Tripode date].** The pool dated the Corbeil plot and the cabin to 1905. fr.wikipedia puts the acquisition and the order for the cabin at **the end of 1904**, with an enlargement of the holding in July 1905. Dated 1904-12-01 in the journey file.

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** 1904, stays with Claude Terrasse at Le Grand-Lemps in the Isère and begins the unfinished *La Dragonne*.

## 7. The last eighteen months, 1906-1907

**[R: fr.wikipedia]** 28 May 1906, writes to Rachilde from Laval describing himself as exhausted, his failing body compared to a machine running down. **[A]** May 1906, severe delirious fever at Laval, last rites, writes his own will, survives. **[R]** That he took refuge specifically with his sister Charlotte comes from the same account of the correspondence and is not separately documented.

**[C, partly demoted].** June 1906, sells the Corbeil land to pay creditors: **[A]**. That **Charlotte** was the buyer and that **Octave Mirbeau and Thadée Natanson** arranged the money is **not attested anywhere reached**, and is dropped; the campa says the record does not name them.

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** July 1906, the last known photograph: fencing with Félix Blaviel at Laval.
**[A]** 1906, the *Vers et prose* evenings at La Closerie des Lilas organized by Paul Fort, with Apollinaire among the younger generation there.
**[A]** April 1907, sells most surviving manuscripts to the autograph dealer **Victor Lemasle**; publishes his last book, a memoir of the poet **Albert Samain**.
> *Drafting error, caught and fixed:* a first draft of that campa put Samain's death six months earlier. He died in 1900. Nothing in the pool dates it, so the campa says only "dead some years."

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** October 1907, found in advanced deterioration at the rue Cassette room, unable to walk, taken to hospital.
**[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia for cause]** **1 November 1907, 4.15 p.m., Hôpital de la Charité, 47 rue Jacob and 45 rue des Saints-Pères, Paris 6e, tuberculous meningitis**, worsened by years of alcohol and, when he could not afford alcohol, ether.

**[R: the toothpick].** The sentence "It is recorded that his last request was for a toothpick" exists verbatim in en.wikipedia, and **the pool's citation for it was wrong**. It carries no footnote; footnote [12] attaches to the *preceding* sentence, about ether, and is Mark Ford, "The King of Charisma," *New York Review of Books* 59:8, 10 May 2012, an article that was fetched and **does not mention a toothpick**. The anecdote is conventionally traced to Shattuck and to Rachilde; neither page could be verified. It is carried as an oft-repeated anecdote, never as a documented deathbed fact.

**[A: fr.wikipedia]** November 1907, buried at the **Cimetière parisien de Bagneux**, 23rd division, 5th row, 5th plot. Mourners: **Vallette, Rachilde, Mirbeau, Valéry, Léautaud** (confirmed verbatim).
**[C: the mourner list]** The geography lens added **Apollinaire and Léon-Paul Fargue**. Neither is attested in anything reached; both are dropped.
**Gap:** no day is recorded for the funeral. The journey file defaults it to 01 and says in the confidence field that this is therefore not the true date.

## 8. Afterlife

**[A: en.wikipedia]** Picasso acquires Jarry's revolver after his death, later collects a number of his manuscripts, and draws his portrait. **No date is given for any of the three**; the stop's 1908 is a declared placeholder.
**[A: fr.wikipedia]** 1911, *Faustroll* published posthumously by Fasquelle.
**[A: artaud.journey.json; andre_breton.journey.json]** 1926, Artaud, Roger Vitrac and Robert Aron found the **Théâtre Alfred Jarry** at the Théâtre de Grenelle; June 1928, the Strindberg *Dream Play* evening at which Breton is barred at the door and the Artaud-Breton friendship ends.
**[A: en.wikipedia, Collège de 'Pataphysique]** 1948, the Collège is founded under the motto *Eadem mutata resurgo*; the Oulipo begins as a subcommittee, Queneau among its members; the Collège suspends public activity 1975-2000, calling the interval its "occultation."
**[A: fr.wikipedia]** 1966, Zadkine's bronze at Laval; 1972, the Pléiade edition begins; 2012-2022, the six-volume Classiques Garnier critical edition.
**[R: fr.wikipedia, citing cemetery documentation]** The burial concession was never renewed, yet the remains were not exhumed.
**[C]** Popular accounts say the tomb was lost outright and the body swept into a common grave. This contradicts the cemetery documentation. **Not resolved**; both are stated at the closing stop, which is where the file ends.
**[R]** The Morgan Library exhibition *Alfred Jarry: The Carnival of Being* (2020) is [R] in the pool and is not used as a stop.

## Interlocks

Named in a campa, with the relation that justifies it:

- **w_b_yeats**: present in the audience at the Ubu Roi premiere, 10 December 1896, and author of the "After us the Savage God" passage about that night. Direct, physical, documented.
- **artaud**: named his 1926 theatre the Théâtre Alfred Jarry; his first stage role came from Lugné-Poé, who staged Ubu Roi; his own journey file chapters a section "Le théâtre et son double: cinéma, Jarry, Cruauté." One-directional influence across a nineteen-year gap, which is a real link.
- **andre_breton**: barred at the door of the Théâtre Alfred Jarry in June 1928; his formative meeting with Jacques Vaché, "a disciple of Alfred Jarry," is already in his own file.

Deliberately **not** named, though the interlock lens surfaced them: **guy_debord**, **erik_satie**, **sergei_diaghilev**, **f_t_marinetti**, **juan_eduardo_cirlot**, **jacques_lacan**, **laban**, **james_joyce**. Every one rests on a shared city, a shared decade, or a lineage claim no document supports. The Debord link is flagged as inference by the queue itself and is recorded in the Collège stop's confidence field instead.
