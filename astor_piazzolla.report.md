# Astor Piazzolla: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-22 for `astor_piazzolla.journey.json`. Astor Pantaleón Piazzolla, Mar del Plata 1921 to Buenos Aires 1992: bandoneonist, composer, arranger, called the assassin of the tango for thirty years.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source in the bracket. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim without corroboration. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Nine inherited pins were re-geocoded against OSM/Nominatim and corrected in the journey file: Rivadavia 2527 (1.1 km out), the Beaux-Arts Apartments (1.6 km, the old pin sat in Times Square), Alberti 1561 (830 m), the Facultad de Derecho (230 m), Sala Planeta (165 m), Teatro Regina (1.05 km), Ambroise Pare (1.5 km), Jardin de Paz (2.6 km), and Wembley (800 m, no stop written). The rue Ballu, Carre, Teatro Colon and canonical Buenos Aires and Paris pins are inherited byte-identical. The file carries **45 stops in 8 segments**; every hedge below has a matching `date_confidence` field.

## 1. Mar del Plata and New York, 1921 to 1936

- **Birth [A].** 11 March 1921, Rivadavia 2527, about two in the morning, midwife attending; only child of Vicente "Nonino" Piazzolla and Asunta Manetti, named for the violinist Astor Bolognini. A gallery now stands on the site with two plaques (English Wikipedia; La Capital de Mar del Plata).
- **Emigration [A, year contested].** English Wikipedia gives 1925 for the Lower East Side, Spanish Wikipedia 1924; Vicente worked as a barber [A]. No building address; neighbourhood pin.
- **The bandoneón [A, year and price contested].** English Wikipedia and Wise Music Classical give 1929 for the pawnshop instrument; Spanish Wikipedia gives 1927 and eighteen dollars.
- **Depression round trip [A, corrected].** Back to Mar del Plata at the beginning of **1930**, back to New York at the beginning of **1931**, so the Little Italy resettlement belongs to that return and the bandoneón precedes it by two years, not one (lacapitalmdp.com; acercandonoscultura.com.ar).
- **Béla Wilda [A, address gap].** 1933: study with the Hungarian pianist, a former Rachmaninoff pupil heard practising through an open window, and Bach fugues transcribed for bandoneón (Todotango.com, 1976 interview reprint). **Gap:** no studio address anywhere.
- **Gardel [A, corrected].** Gardel moved in January 1934 into the **Beaux-Arts Apartments, 310 East 44th Street**, penthouse on the eighteenth floor, and the thirteen-year-old was sent there with his father's carved wooden gaucho and met at the door by the pianist Alberto Castellano (fundacioncarlosgardel.org; La Nación; Diario Norte). *Flagged:* one strand says 48th Street against the better-sourced 44th, and the film's 1935 release is why some timelines date the meeting then.

## 2. Return, Corrientes, Troilo, Ginastera, 1936 to 1950

- **Return, 1936 [A for the year, R for the address].** **Alberti 1561** rests on one Mar del Plata outlet, not cross-checked. **Buenos Aires, 1938 [A]:** arrival at seventeen, work under Francisco Lauro and Gabriel Clausi, with no venue named anywhere, so the stop takes the canonical pin.
- **Troilo [A, year contested].** Spanish Wikipedia dates the orchestra debut to **December 1939** and English Wikipedia agrees on the year; several general accounts, the dossier included, give 1938.
- **Ginastera [A, chain corrected].** "Through Rubinstein's mediation" is wrong: Rubinstein's Buenos Aires encounter was **1940** and he referred Piazzolla to **Juan José Castro**, who declined and passed him to **Alberto Ginastera**, whose first private pupil he became, studying **1941 to 1946** (English Wikipedia; Caras y Caretas; Fundación Juan March). *Contradiction:* Wise Music Classical starts them in 1940. No address for the lessons; canonical pin.
- **Marriage to Odette "Dedé" Wolff [A, contested but resolvable].** Interlude, "Tango Passion", gives a civil ceremony at the **end of October 1942** with a church ceremony two days later, and Spanish Wikipedia agrees; English Wikipedia's 1943 is the single dissenter. Diana born 1943 [A]; Daniel born 1945 [R]. *Tagging note:* this stop and the Ginastera stop share one bare canonical pin, which the working pass tagged A on one and R on the other; both are treated alike here.
- **Leaving Troilo [A].** 1944, departure with Francisco Fiorentino; 1946, his own Orquesta Típica for Odeón. **Disbanding [A, year contested]:** English Wikipedia 1950, the dossier 1949.

## 3. Sevitzky, Paris, Boulanger, 1951 to 1955

- **The Sevitzky prize and premiere [A, place corrected].** The dossier's 1951 is the **composition** date of *Buenos Aires (Tres movimientos sinfónicos)*. Prize and premiere both fall on **16 August 1953** and both happened **in Buenos Aires, not the United States**: Sevitzky conducted the Radio del Estado orchestra in the auditorium of the Facultad de Derecho, UBA, with two bandoneóns added, and the evening ended in fistfights among outraged "cultured" listeners (La Nación; historiadelasinfonia.es). "Contest judged in the United States" is deleted.
- **Paris and Boulanger [A for the fact, R for the words].** 1954, French government scholarship, study with Nadia Boulanger at **36 rue Ballu**, conducting under Hermann Scherchen. *Contradiction flagged, not resolved:* four incompatible wordings of Boulanger's verdict circulate for one episode. Two are from the Gorin memoir via thelistenersclub.com, "You say that you are not pianist. What instrument do you play, then?" and "You idiot, that's Piazzolla!"; a third is in a Staveacre interview at maramarietta.com; a fourth at pleasekillme.com cites no document. The episode is attested, the wording is not, and since the second already sits on Boulanger's file the first is used here. Some accounts name the tango as *Triunfal*, others name none.
- **Fontainebleau, unresolved.** English Wikipedia places part of the study at the **Conservatoire Américain** rather than or besides rue Ballu; nothing disentangles them, and no stop is written because no source puts a datable event there. **The tango *Nonino* [A]** was written in Paris the same year, and its lyrical section is the material reworked five years later (English Wikipedia, "Adiós Nonino").

## 4. Nuevo tango and the assassin label, 1955 to 1960

- **Octeto Buenos Aires [A].** Founded 1955, dissolved 1958; eight players, cello and electric guitar among them, no singer.
- **The law faculty concert [A, date corrected].** The documented Octeto concert there is **1956**, not 1957, fixed by two acetates found by the collector Ezequiel Lodeiro in mid-2021 and digitised by the Instituto Nacional de Musicología "Carlos Vega": the only live recording of the Octeto and of *Tango Ballet*, the one work written expressly for it. The working chronology dated it 1957 and the working geography 1956; it is one event and 1956 is sourced. No source calls it a **first public performance**, INMCV calling it "uno de los grandes (e infrecuentes) éxitos", and no month is given, so the working "1956-10" claimed precision nothing supports.
- **"Asesino del tango" [R].** The label is widely reported, but its best-known illustration, the taxi driver who refuses him the ride, traces to Laura Escalada's recollection in later journalism, not a dated first-hand account. Carried as tradition (El País; pleasekillme.com).
- **New York, 1958 to 1960 [A].** Relocation, arranging work, apartment on East 92nd Street. **Gap:** that address is documented for 1959 only; the file uses it for both stops and says why.
- **The father's death and *Adiós Nonino* [A, place corrected].** Vicente died in **Mar del Plata** in October 1959 while Piazzolla was in **San Juan, Puerto Rico**, on the Club Flamboyán engagement; he flew back and wrote the piece in about forty-five minutes in the **New York apartment on East 92nd Street** a few days later, reworking the 1954 Paris *Nonino*. Death place and composition place are different and are not merged (English Wikipedia; Infobae, 20 June 2021). *Duplicate removed:* the working **Club Flamboyán 1960** entry is the same engagement misdated, merged into the 1959 stop, and the unsourced Condado pin is dropped for a city-level one. *Contradiction:* Spanish Wikipedia dates the composition to 1962, against the 1959 death date it gives itself.

## 5. Quinteto, Ferrer, the operita, 1960 to 1972

- **First Quinteto Nuevo Tango [A].** 1960: bandoneón, violin, piano, electric guitar, double bass. **Philharmonic Hall, Lincoln Center [A]:** 1965, issued as a live record (Strings Magazine), and often misattributed to Carnegie Hall, which the file corrects.
- **Ferrer contract [A, figure corrected].** 1967, five-year exclusivity with Horacio Ferrer. "Over 200 tango songs" is off by an order of magnitude: Ferrer's own *Quereme así piantao (1967-1971)* gathers about **twenty-eight**.
- **María de Buenos Aires [A, address located].** Premiered **8 May 1968** at the Sala Planeta with Amelita Baltar, about a hundred performances into the first week of September (La Nación). The address the working note called unfindable is Galería de las Américas, **Suipacha 927**.
- **Balada para un loco [A for 1969 in substance].** *Contradiction:* Spanish Wikipedia dates it 1964, three years before the Ferrer contract the same timeline lists for 1967.
- **Las cuatro estaciones porteñas [A, dating corrected].** Not composed in 1965: only *Verano Porteño* dates from that year, with the other three across 1969 and 1970 and the complete set premiered **19 May 1970 at the Teatro Regina** (English Wikipedia's timeline). The file gives the span as 1965 to 1970 and treats the Regina date as a premiere, not an unresolved "early performance".
- **Teatro Colón with Troilo, 1972 [R].** The only evidence located is a Wikimedia Commons photograph captioned for that year, provenance unverified beyond Commons metadata, no month; the campa says the evidence is one photograph and its caption.

## 6. Italy, the dictatorship, the late tours, 1973 to 1989

- **Heart attack and Italy [A].** 1973, managed by Aldo Pagani, most of five years abroad. **Libertango** recorded in Milan, May 1974; **Summit** with Gerry Mulligan, September 1974. **Gap:** no studio named for either, so the file folds both into one Milan stop.
- **A dossier claim that does not survive.** The dossier says *Libertango* entered the Latin Grammy Hall of Fame in 2001; independent search names **Adiós Nonino** for that induction instead, so the file attaches it there and makes no Hall of Fame claim for *Libertango*. **Verify against latingrammy.com before promotion to canon.**
- **The Videla dinner, 1976 [A, materially understated in the dossier].** Not a gala at which he happened to be seated near the general: it was a **dinner with Jorge Rafael Videla**, and his own memoir account is that he was summoned, "They sent two men in black with an envelope and a letter saying President Videla was expecting me." In the surrounding *Radiolandia* and *Gente* interviews he called the musicians he had recruited "egomaniacs, drug addicts and leftists", which Martín Graziano notes came close to a death sentence in those years. **Julio Cortázar broke off the friendship**, and his daughter Diana said she never forgave him (Perfil; cabezademoog.blogspot.com). The dossier's "later expresses regret" is **not what the record shows**.
- **Second Quintet [A].** Formed 1978 with Pablo Ziegler, eleven years touring, *Tango: Zero Hour* recorded 1986 and called by him his greatest record. **César [A]** 1986 for *Tangos, el exilio de Gardel* (Solanas), ceremony date unverified. **Montreux with Gary Burton [July 1986], Central Park [6 September 1987], Tokyo with Milva [26 June 1988], quadruple bypass [1988], all [A].**
- **Marriage to Laura Escalada [contested].** English Wikipedia gives divorce and remarriage in **1988**, other accounts date the partnership's start to **1976**; the file follows 1988 and flags the conflict.
- **Amsterdam, Carré, June 1989 [A, day contested].** His sextet shared a bill with Osvaldo Pugliese's orchestra. English Wikipedia gives 26 June in its Piazzolla article and 29 June in its Pugliese article; `osvaldo_pugliese.journey.json` preserves that discrepancy and this file preserves it identically.
- **Five Tango Sensations [A, place narrowed].** Recorded and mixed with the Kronos Quartet at **Power Station, New York**, November 1989, released by Nonesuch 25 January 1991; the working entry gave only "USA" (Discogs; kronosquartet.org).

## 7. Athens to Pablo Nogués, 1990 to 1992

- **Last concert [A].** Athens, 3 July 1990, Orchestra of Colours; the hall is not named in any source.
- **The hemorrhage [contradiction, unresolved by construction].** English Wikipedia gives **4 August 1990**, but the accounts describing him walking to Sunday mass at Notre-Dame require **5 August**, because 4 August 1990 was a Saturday (verified by calendar). Both cannot stand; the file follows the Sunday framing, dates the stop 1990-08-05, and states the contradiction rather than printing both silently.
- **Hôpital Ambroise Paré, Boulogne-Billancourt [R].** Hospital name and irreversible-damage diagnosis came through a Spanish-language search summary, not a page read directly; the caveat is preserved.
- **Death [A for city and date, R for the clinic].** Buenos Aires, **4 July 1992**, aged seventy-one, after twenty-three months in and out of coma. The "sanatorium in Palermo" comes from an aggregated summary, unverified.
- **Burial [A].** Cementerio Jardín de Paz, Ingeniero Pablo Nogués, **Sector 4, Manzana 3, Parcela 16**, not Chacarita, correcting a misconception repeated in some reference works. A plain marble plaque flush with the ground, no sculpture, no portrait, no bandoneón motif; a visiting journalist reported cemetery staff not knowing whose grave it was (Perfil; Find a Grave 1711911). *Corrected:* Ingeniero Pablo Nogués is in the **Partido de Malvinas Argentinas, not Pilar**, the Spanish Wikipedia category naming Pilar being a different site of the same chain. The "forgotten" framing is one journalist's impression in an opinion column, so it does not enter the campa as a claim.

## Interlocks used, and interlocks refused

Named in a campa, each a direct relation: **`nadia_boulanger`**, his teacher in 1954, whose file carries the same meeting from her side with the complementary quotation; **`carlos_gardel`**, met in New York 1934 and cast in *El día que me quieras*, though Gardel's own file does **not** attest the meeting and records only the Astoria filming; **`osvaldo_pugliese`**, sharing the Carré bill in June 1989, already staged from his side; and **`julio_cortazar`**, a friendship ended by Cortázar over the 1976 Videla dinner.

Refused, each being **shared geography or shared period only**: **`angel_villoldo`**, dead 1919, no crossing; **`enrique_santos_discepolo`**, same milieu, no reference either way; **`juan_peron`**, whose 1955 fall merely coincides with the Octeto's founding year; **Cementerio de la Chacarita**, not reused because **Piazzolla is not buried there**; **Café El Nacional**, Pugliese's debut venue, named by no Piazzolla source; and the **Teatro Colón** as a stage shared with Pavlova, Sakharoff and Py, where the pin is inherited but nobody else is named.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Piazzolla is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are written. Per the duty not to delete silently, the relations found that are not rooted in his own life are named here; **all run forward**, the direction the exception never licenses. Nothing was deleted, and upgrading any of the first four into a stop would require adding Piazzolla to `EXCEPTIONS.md` with a forward grant, which no agent may do on its own judgement.

1. **Yo-Yo Ma on Piazzolla.** The verified line is "There's a quote to the effect that Piazzolla is the Ellington of Argentina, and in a way it's true" (thelistenersclub.com, 2021); the working pool truncated it in a way that changed the speaker, since Ma is repeating someone else's formulation. His 1997 *Soul of the Tango* is his own engagement, for his own file.
2. **The 2021 centenary.** The Google Doodle of 11 March 2021, the Centro Cultural Kirchner and Fundación Astor Piazzolla programming culminating in a free Obelisco concert, and the 1995 Konex de Honor: posthumous third-party acts, carried only as `suggested_refs`.
3. **The rediscovered acetates.** Lodeiro's 2021 find and the INMCV digitisation are named inside the 1956 law-faculty campa, as facts about *that recording* made *at that concert* rather than a scene of Lodeiro travelling. If the operator judges this over the line, the sentence to cut is that campa's last.
4. **Monuments and namings.** The 2011 Benavídez bronze on the Rambla Playa Bristol, Astor Piazzolla International Airport, asteroid 12102 Piazzolla, and the 2024 Mercury crater: honours to the subject rather than other people's journeys. One clause of the burial campa names three, on the pattern of `abdelkader.journey.json`, whose final stop carries the 1966 repatriation.
5. **The standard biography.** Azzi and Collier, *Le Grand Tango* (OUP 2000; expanded 2017). Correction: Azzi's own account is **seven years** of research, not four; rights reverted to her after Collier's death, which is why the 2017 edition is not an OUP imprint. Do not conflate the book with Piazzolla's unrelated 1982 piece of the same name.

## Honest gaps

- **No address** for Wilda's studio, Ginastera's teaching room, the Club Flamboyán, the Milan studio, the Athens hall, or the Buenos Aires sanatorium.
- **No month** for twelve events: the 1925 emigration, 1929 bandoneón, 1936 return, 1938 arrival, 1941 Ginastera start, 1950 disbanding, 1955 Octeto, 1956 law-faculty concert, 1965 Lincoln Center concert, 1967 Ferrer contract, 1969 *Balada*, 1973 move to Italy. All default to `-01-01`.
- **Not verified:** the Commons provenance of the 1972 Colón photograph; the Ambroise Paré identification; the Palermo sanatorium; the 1986 César ceremony date; the Hall of Fame question above.
- **Deliberately no stop:** the 1963 Hirsch Prize [A]; **Conjunto 9** [A, September 1971 per Wise Music Classical against 1970 per English Wikipedia], carried in the Colón stop's `date_confidence`; Wembley, 30 June 1989 and the BBC Bristol session, June 1989, both [A] but thin tour-list items with an unconfirmed pin and a venue demolished in 2006.
- **Single-sourced, carried as such:** the Alberti 1561 address; Daniel Piazzolla's birth year; the taxi-driver anecdote; the hospital and clinic names.
