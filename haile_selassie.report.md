# Haile Selassie I (Tafari Makonnen), 1892-1975: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `haile_selassie`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · **[C]** = contradiction between sources, flagged and left open.

The pool was gathered in five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife) then verified adversarially. Several corrections were substantial: a speech misdated by seven weeks, a birthplace misplaced by 26 km, a battle series misdated by four months, three inconsistent coordinates for one house.

---

## 1. Harar and the apprenticeship, 1892-1911

Born Lij Tafari Makonnen, 23 July 1892, at Ejersa Goro in the hills east of Harar; father Ras Makonnen Wolde Mikael, governor of Harar and cousin of Menelik II **[A: Wikipedia, "Haile Selassie"]**.

*Correction.* The first pass gave 9.25, 42.2333. Wikipedia's Ejersa Goro article gives **9.483, 42.233**: latitude wrong by about 26 km, longitude fine **[A]**. The first pass also claimed "no structure survives that can be pointed to as the birth house"; Haile Selassie later built the church of Kidane Mihret at the site **[A]**.

Childhood inside Jugol, the walled old town, tutored by Abba Samuel, an Ethiopian Orthodox monk, and by Dr Vitalien, a Guadeloupean physician at the Harar court **[A: Marcus, *Haile Selassie I: The Formative Years*; Wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no reachable source dates the tutelage, so that stop defaults month and day to 01.

Dejazmatch of Gara Mulatta at thirteen, 1 November 1905; father dies 1906 and Selale passes to him; Sidamo 1907; brought into Menelik's Gebbi in this period **[A: Marcus; Wikipedia]**.

**Harar, 1910 or 1911. [C]** The first pass stated a confident 1910. Wikipedia says verbatim: *"Tafari was made governor of Harar in 1910 or 1911."* The file pins 1910 only to preserve travel order and says so in `date_confidence`. Do not quote 1910 as settled.

Married Menen Asfaw of Ambassel, 3 August 1911 **[A]**. **Gap:** the pool gives the place only as "Ethiopia". The stop sits at Harar, where the couple then lived, with the gap declared.

## 2. The regency, 1916-1928

- Lij Iyasu deposed; Tafari proclaimed Crown Prince and Regent Plenipotentiary as Ras Tafari, 27 September 1916 **[A]**.
- Battle of Segale, 27 October 1916 **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of Segale"]**; Negus Mikael of Wollo defeated largely by Fitawrari Habte Giyorgis's troops.
- Formal investiture as Ras Tafari Makonnen, 11 February 1917 **[R]**; folded into the regency stop, not given its own.
- Ethiopia admitted to the League of Nations, 1923 **[A]**. The day (28 September) is conventional and was **not** re-verified **[R for the day]**. **Gap:** which hall hosted the vote is unknown; the Assembly met at the Salle de la Reformation before 1937.

*Correction.* The first pass placed Segale at 9.3667, 39.05. Wikipedia locates it "40 miles north of Addis Ababa", i.e. approximately **9.6, 38.75**; the original sat north-east of the capital at two-thirds the distance **[A for bearing and distance; R for the point]**. The first pass also wrote that Tafari "emerges from the battle as regent"; he had been proclaimed Regent a month before it **[A]**. The file inverts that: the appointment came first, the battle made it real.

## 3. The grand tour of 1924

**[A]** Wikipedia gives the itinerary verbatim: *"In 1924, Ras Tafari toured Europe and the Middle East visiting Jerusalem, Alexandria, Paris, Luxembourg, Brussels, Amsterdam, Stockholm, London, Geneva, Gibraltar and Athens."*

*Correction, twice.* The first pass dropped Luxembourg, Brussels, Amsterdam, Stockholm and Gibraltar, and built a full stop around **Cairo**, which appears nowhere in the source. The Egyptian city is **Alexandria** (31.2001, 29.9187) **[A]**; Cairo is deleted. The file gives stops to six cities and recites all eleven inside the Athens campa so nothing is lost.

**Two items dropped.** Port Said, proposed on "standard shipping-route reconstruction" alone, with no document placing him there **[R]**; and the detail that at Jerusalem he arranged support for orphans at an Armenian monastery, traced to no primary document **[R]**. The Deir Sultan visit itself, to the Ethiopian community on the roof of the Holy Sepulchre, is **[A]**.

**Dating. Gap.** The tour is attested as a list of cities, not a schedule; no arrival dates found. All six stops carry `1924-01-01` with the gap declared, and their order follows the source's own listing.

## 4. Crown, constitution, prophecy, 1928-1933

- Crowned Negus by Zewditu, 7 October 1928 **[A]**.
- Battle of Anchem, 31 March 1930; Ras Gugsa Welle defeated **[A]**. **Gap:** the battlefield is unlocated in reachable sources; the stop uses an approximate 10.05, 38.85 and says so.
- Zewditu dies of paratyphoid fever and diabetes complications, 2 April 1930 **[A]**.
- Crowned Negusa Nagast as Haile Selassie I at the Cathedral of St George, 2 November 1930 **[A]**.
- First written constitution, 16 July 1931 **[A]**.
- Jimma absorbed, 12 May 1932 **[A: Wikipedia, "Kingdom of Jimma"]**.

*Correction.* The first pass called Jimma a **Sultanate**. It was a **Kingdom**, rulers titled *Moti*; the year was right, and the absorption dates precisely to 12 May 1932, when some 400 soldiers and a team of administrators arrived after the death of Abba Jifar II **[A]**.

**Rastafari: the date is the correction.** The first pass dated the Jamaican proclamation of divinity to **1930**. Wikipedia's Rastafari article dates the movement's development to *"during the 1930s"* and names Leonard Howell chief among the preachers who proclaimed it *after* the coronation; it gives **no** 1930 proclamation **[A for the framing]**. Howell's preaching is conventionally placed c. 1932-33 **[R]**. The file splits the trigger (the coronation stop) from the proclamation (a Kingston stop at 1933-01-01, whole hedge in `date_confidence`).

**Garvey.** The "look to Africa" prophecy is **[R]**: the pool records its wording and date as disputed, with no exact dated quotation found. QUEUE.md states that the title Ras Tafari "directly inspired the Rastafari movement, which drew on marcus_garvey's ... look to Africa prophecy" **[A: QUEUE.md line 230]**. The campa names Garvey as supplier of the prophecy and notes he rejected the reading, without quoting it.

**Aksum.** A stop at the coronation date, coordinates 14.1211, 38.7269 inherited from `sheba.journey.json` **[A: interlock]**. No journey to Aksum at this date is recorded anywhere; the pin marks the dynastic claim, and `date_confidence` says so in its first clause. The 225th-monarch count is traditional **[R]**.

## 5. The Italian war, 1934-1936

- Welwel Incident, 5 December 1934 **[A]**; coordinates approximate (desert wells).
- Mussolini declares war from the Palazzo Venezia balcony, 1935 **[A: mussolini.journey.json]**. **[C] for the day:** the interlocking file gives the *year only*. The file uses 1935-10-02, the conventional date, and flags the day as unverified in this pool.
- Mustard gas, banned by treaty since 1925, dropped on troops and villages on Mussolini's own written authorization **[A: mussolini.journey.json]**.
- General mobilization, 3 October 1935 **[A]**.
- **Northern battles: corrected by four months.** The first pass dated the Tembien / Amba Aradam / Shire series to October 1935. They fell in **early 1936** **[A: Wikipedia, "Second Italo-Ethiopian War"]**: First Tembien concluded 24 January; Amba Aradam 10-19 February; Second Tembien concluded 29 February; Shire 28 February into early March. The corrected chronology sits in the Dessie stop.
- Dessie headquarters abandoned, 20 February 1936 **[A]**.
- Korem, 3 March 1936; the decision to give pitched battle rather than disperse into guerrilla resistance **[A]**; folded into Maychew.
- Battle of Maychew, 31 March 1936 **[A]**. Troop figures (roughly 30,000-35,000 against some 40,000) are Wikipedia's ranges, and estimates.
- Lalibela entered, 13 April 1936 **[A]**. **Gap, and the campa says so in its own text:** what he did there is not recorded.
- Were Ilu, 22 April 1936 **[R]**; no stop. Departure for French Somaliland, 2 May 1936 **[A]**.
- Badoglio enters Addis Ababa 5 May; Victor Emmanuel III proclaimed Emperor; Mussolini proclaims the empire from the same balcony 9 May 1936 **[A: mussolini.journey.json]**.

## 6. The Geneva address: the largest error in the pool

**[C] Three dates circulate; one is disposed of and two remain live.**

1. **12 May 1936.** A misparse of the sentence that "the League of Nations allowed Selassie to address the assembly", which is about the League granting him a hearing, not about the speech. Also **physically impossible**: he sailed from Djibouti only on 4 May and travelled via Haifa, Jerusalem and Gibraltar, reaching England in early June **[A]**.
2. **7 June 1936.** The heading on the Wikisource text of the address itself.
3. **30 June 1936.** Wikipedia's *Timeline of the Second Italo-Ethiopian War*: *"30 June 1936: Emperor Haile Selassie addresses the League of Nations."*

The file **uses 30 June** and prints the whole contradiction, including the Wikisource heading, in `date_confidence`. Readings 2 and 3 both stand; only reading 1 is disposed of, on the impossibility ground above.

**[C] Coordinates.** Geography gives 46.2016, 6.1466 (Salle de la Reformation); interlock and quotes give 46.2044, 6.1432. The file uses the geography figure for the 1923 admission and the interlock figure, byte-identical per the canonical-pin rule, for the 1936 speech.

**Quote wording, corrected.** The pool read "the most painful of the duties of a Head of State". Wikisource reads *"the most painful of the duties of the head of a State."* The file quotes the corrected wording.

## 7. Exile, 1936-1941

- Boards HMS Enterprise at Djibouti, 4 May 1936 **[A]**.
- Haifa landfall and passage to Jerusalem, May 1936 **[A for the month]**. **Gap:** the day is nowhere fixed; the file uses 1936-05-15 as an explicit placeholder inside the attested month.
- Gibraltar, Rock Hotel **[R]**; no 1936 stop. The 1924 Gibraltar stop carries the 1936 passage in its campa, keeping the claim visible without a dated stop resting on reconstruction.
- **Fairfield House, Bath [A, coordinates corrected twice].** Bought 1936, resident to 1941, given to Bath in 1958 with the Freedom of the City **[A: Wikipedia, "Fairfield House, Bath"]**. The pool held **three inconsistent coordinates** for one house: 51.386/-2.3717 (geography), 51.394/-2.383 (afterlife), and the correct **51.3902/-2.3998** (Wikipedia); all reconciled to the last. The street is **Kelston Road**, not "Newbridge Hill". The "fourteen-room" figure is **unsupported** and is dropped.
- Warne's Hotel, Worthing, and Parkside, Wimbledon, before Bath **[A]**.
- Christmas broadcast to America, 25 December 1937, with a fractured knee from a traffic accident **[A]**.
- Princess Romanework dies an Italian prisoner, 1941 **[A, with the pool's own note that some sources place her death differently within the exile period; not resolved]**. Princess Tsehai dies in childbirth, 1942 **[A]**.

## 8. Return and reign, 1941-1974

- Khartoum forward base, 1940-1941 **[A for the range; no month]**.
- Um Iddla crossing, 18 January 1941 **[A]**; coordinates approximate. Addis Ababa re-entered 5 May 1941, five years to the day **[A]**.
- Slavery abolition confirmed with penalties up to death, 27 August 1942 **[A]**.
- Shashamane: 500 acres designated 1948, conveyed to the Ethiopian World Federation by letter in 1955, settlers from 1955 **[A: Wikipedia, "Shashamane"]**. Population fell from over 2,000 to under 300 by 2004 after post-1975 confiscation **[A per Wikipedia; no land registry checked]**.
- **White House state visit, May 1954 [R]**; "standard biographical timelines" only, not re-verified. **No stop.**
- OAU founded at Africa Hall, 25 May 1963, first chairmanship the same date **[A]**. **[C] Signatory count:** Wikipedia gives 32 states in one place, 33 signatory governments in another; not resolved, the file uses 32 and flags it. *Coordinates corrected* to **9.0147, 38.7620**, not 9.0197, 38.7614 (about 550 m off).
- UN General Assembly address, 4 October 1963 **[A]**; the "until the philosophy" passage set by Bob Marley as "War" in 1976 **[A]**. **[C] minor:** geography pins New York at the UN (40.7489, -73.968), quotes at a city point (40.7128, -74.006); the file uses the UN pin.
- Jamaica, 21 April 1966 **[A for day and commemoration; R for the year]**. Wikipedia confirms 21 April and Grounation Day, but the *year* was not explicit in the fetched passage; 1966 is what every secondary source reports. "Liberation before repatriation" is Rastafari **oral tradition [R]**, marked as such inside an attested visit. Second OAU chairmanship, 5 November 1966 **[A]**; no stop.
- Deposed by the Derg, 12 September 1974 **[A]**. The causes named in the campa (concealed famine, army pay revolts, the students) are general context, **[R]** with respect to this pool.

## 9. Death and afterlife

**Died 27 August 1975 at the Jubilee Palace [A for date and place].**

**[C] Cause, left open.** The Derg's 1975 statement gave circulatory failure following prostate surgery; later testimony, including from a former Derg official, alleged smothering or strangulation on Mengistu Haile Mariam's order. The campa states both without adjudicating.

*Correction to the publicity chronology.* Two pool versions were wrong in opposite directions. "Not publicly acknowledged by the Derg until 1994" is **impossible**, since the Derg fell in 1991; Wikipedia says the killing "was revealed to the public" in 1994, under the successor government, during the Red Terror proceedings **[A]**. "Not made public until 1991" is **wrong**: the Derg announced the *death* in 1975 as natural causes, and what stayed hidden was the *killing* **[A]**.

**[C] Jubilee Palace coordinates.** Geography gives 9.025, 38.7469; afterlife gives 9.0227, 38.7615, for the same building. Wikipedia's Jubilee Palace article **404s**, so neither could be verified. The file picks 9.0227, 38.7615 and marks it approximate.

Buried in secret on the palace grounds, reportedly beneath a slab under a latrine **[R: widely repeated in press accounts, unverified against a primary source]**. Remains located in the 1992-94 Red Terror investigations **[A]**.

**Reburial, 5 November 2000, Holy Trinity Cathedral**, north transept, beside Empress Menen **[A]**. *Coordinates corrected* to **9.0308, 38.7667**, not 9.018/38.758 (geography) or 9.0184/38.7598 (afterlife), both about 1.6 km off. Some Rastafari organizations reject the funeral, holding that he cannot have died **[R: a theological position, not a factual dispute]**.

**Writings.** *Hiywotenna ye'Ityopp'ya 'Irmijja* ("My Life and Ethiopia's Progress"), two volumes, Addis Ababa 1973 and 1974, published while he reigned **[A]**. Volume one (1892-1937) translated by Edward Ullendorff, OUP 1976; volume two (1936-1942) translated by Ezekiel Gebissa, edited by Harold G. Marcus, English 1994 **[A]**.

**The Wimbledon bust.** Hilda Seligman's bronze in Cannizaro Park, 1957, a London Rastafari pilgrimage site; destroyed 30 June 2020 by a crowd of about 100 reported as connected to unrest in Ethiopia after the killing of Hachalu Hundessa **[A: Wikipedia, "Cannizaro Park"]**.
