# Fyodor Yevtishchev / Fedor Adrianovich Jeftichew (1868? - 1904)
### "Jo-Jo the Dog-Faced Boy": research dossier

*2026-08-02. Prepared for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject listed at QUEUE.md row 132 and census row 382 as "Russian sideshow performer, JoJo the Dog-Faced Boy."*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions between sources are printed side by side and left open; they are not adjudicated. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**A note on the name.** The subject appears in the record under at least six forms: Фёдор Адрианович Евтихиев (Russian Wikipedia), Fedor Jeftichew (English Wikipedia), Fédor Jeftichjew (French Wikipedia), Fedor Jeftichjew (Popular Science Monthly, 1874), "Theodore Petroff" (his own European billing, c. 1883), and "Jo-Jo the Dog-Faced Boy" (P. T. Barnum, from 1884). The atlas slug follows the Russian transliteration. Nothing in the reachable record shows what he called himself.

---

## 1. Origins: a birth that two encyclopedias place 1,000 km apart

The single largest unresolved problem in this life is where and when it began.

- **[A - English Wikipedia, "Fedor Jeftichew"; French Wikipedia, "Fédor Jeftichjew"]** Born 1868 in Saint Petersburg, Imperial Russia, with congenital hypertrichosis.
- **[A - Russian Wikipedia, "Евтихиев, Фёдор Адрианович"]** Born "1868 или 1870" (1868 or 1870) in the village of **Berezniki**, near Manturovo, Kostroma Province.

**Contradiction 1, not resolved.** Saint Petersburg and Berezniki are roughly 900 km apart, and the year is given flat by two national Wikipedias and hedged by a third. A stop tagged simply "1868, Saint Petersburg" overstates what the record supports. Both readings are carried into the journey file as separate stops, and the date_confidence field on each carries the hedge.

**Contradiction 2, not resolved: whose son is he.**

- **[A - English Wikipedia]** Adrian Jeftichew is his father, and also has hypertrichosis.
- **[A - Russian Wikipedia]** Adrian Evtikhiev is his **приёмный отец**, adoptive father. The two men are natives of neighbouring villages near Manturovo: Adrian of **Korovino**, Fedor of **Berezniki**. Fedor's own birth surname is given as **Petrov**.

This second version has a consequence the English-language literature has not absorbed. If Russian Wikipedia is right, then "Theodore Petroff", the name under which he is touring Europe around 1883 and under which Barnum's agent finds him, is not an invented stage name at all. It is his own surname, the only name in the whole file that may be his.

**[R]** The genuine gap in the Russian period is not the outline, which Russian Wikipedia supplies (two villages, an adoption, a surname). The gap is confirmation at the level of a parish register. No such document has been located, in any language, for either birthplace.

**[R - kickassfacts.com, reprinting thehumanmarvels.com; Russia Beyond, "The tragic story of the Russian dog-faced boy"]** Adrian, covered in fine ash-coloured hair, is said to have fled his village and lived as a hermit in the forest until showmen found him. This hermit story is the seed of the later "Wild Man from the Kostroma Forest" billing, and it should be read as billing material rather than biography.

**Coordinate correction (carried into the journey file).** An earlier research pass pinned the Kostroma origin at 58.7667 / 44.75, which is about 48 km north of Manturovo in empty forest, and elsewhere at the city of Kostroma, 57.7665 / 40.9269, which is about 230 km south-west of the family's actual district. **Manturovo, Kostroma Oblast, is at 58.333 / 44.767.** That is the honest pin for the origin stops.

**Medical context.**

- **[A - Wikipedia, "Hypertrichosis"]** Only about fifty cases of congenital hypertrichosis lanuginosa have been recorded since the Middle Ages.
- **[A - Wikipedia, "Hypertrichosis"]** Petrus Gonsalvus (1537-1618), of Tenerife, is the earliest recorded case, and the origin of the court-and-sideshow exhibition line into which historians later place the Jeftichews.

## 2. The European exhibition years, 1873 - 1883

- **[A - English Wikipedia]** From 1873 Adrian tours Europe billed as the "Wild Man from the Kostroma Forest", with the small boy exhibited beside him.
- **[R - kickassfacts.com, after thehumanmarvels.com]** Before leaving the empire, at four or five years old, Fedor debuts on the Russian fair circuit as the "son of the Wild Man". No town is named in any source. The itinerary of this earliest touring is undocumented.
- **[A - Un monsieur de l'Orchestre, "Les Coulisses de l'Homme-Chien", *Le Figaro* no. 290, 17 October 1873, pp. 3-4]** A Paris paper sends a correspondent backstage to the dog-man exhibition and prints an eyewitness account of Adrian and the boy.
- **[A - French Wikipedia, "Fédor Jeftichjew"]** The Paris venue is named, against an earlier claim in this file that it was not: the **Vauxhall de Paris**, also called the Tivoli-Vauxhall, where the pair were shown in the 1870s as "L'Homme chien et son fils Fédor". It stood on the rue de la Douane, now rue Léon-Jouhaux, in the 10th arrondissement near the Place de la République, roughly 48.869 / 2.366.

**Correction of kind, not just detail.** English Wikipedia's phrase "French circuses" is wrong about what sort of place this was. The Tivoli-Vauxhall was a pleasure-garden exhibition hall, not a circus. The circus proper enters this life only in 1884, in America.

**The lost decade.**

**[R]** Between the Paris notice of October 1873 and the reappearance as "Theodore Petroff" around 1883 there is nothing. English Wikipedia gives the reappearance as "~10 years after 1873", which is arithmetic rather than a source, and this dossier accordingly tags 1883 **[R]**, not [A], correcting an earlier pass that tagged it attested. Where the pair toured, where they lived, whether Adrian was still alive, and when Fedor began performing without him are all unknown.

**[R - Russia Beyond]** Berlin is named, together with Paris, as a city the pair played before the Atlantic crossing. No date and no venue are given.

- **[A - thehumanmarvels.com, via the kickassfacts reprint]** In 1884 an agent working for P. T. Barnum sees him in **Liverpool** and recruits him for America. No hall is named. The docks pin used in the journey file is a plausible landmark for the era's chief transatlantic embarkation port and nothing more; it is flagged [R].

## 3. Barnum, 1884: the renaming

- **[A - English Wikipedia]** Barnum's agent brings the teenager to the United States in 1884 and Barnum rebrands him **Jo-Jo the Dog-Faced Boy**.
- **[A - English Wikipedia, from Barnum promotional material, flagged there as fabrication]** The publicity story: a hunter near Kostroma tracks Fedor and his father to a cave and captures them living wild as savages. Barnum's copy casts Adrian as uncivilizable and stresses the boy's supposed canine appearance. He is coached to bark and growl on cue.
- **[A - English Wikipedia]** He speaks Russian, German and English.

The two facts in the last two bullets sit against each other and the atlas leaves them there. The multilingual young man is paid to bark.

- **[R]** No document names his point of entry to the United States. Castle Garden was New York's immigrant landing depot until 1890, which makes it the near-certain gate, but this is inference from practice, not from a manifest.

**Correction to an earlier pass.** An earlier version of this file dated the Barnum-Bailey merger to 1887 and cited Wikipedia's "Barnum & Bailey" for it. The cited source does not say that. Barnum and Bailey agreed to combine their shows on **28 March 1881**; the name "Barnum & Bailey Greatest Show on Earth" dates from **1888**, when the partners rejoined after a split (Wikipedia, "P. T. Barnum"). 1887 corresponds to neither event and has been removed.

## 4. The American years, 1884 - 1897

What is documented here is thin, and what is usually printed is thick. The distinction matters.

**Documented, [A]:**

- **[A - Rosemarie Garland Thomson, ed., *Freakery: Cultural Spectacles of the Extraordinary Body*, New York University Press, 1996, p. 317]** At the **Globe Museum** in New York, Jeftichew's manager works him **twenty-three shows in a twelve-to-fourteen-hour day.** This is the single hardest fact in the American section and it was missing from the earlier pass, which offered a generic "likely continued with Barnum" reconstruction in its place. The Globe Museum's exact street address is not established in the source; it belonged to the Bowery dime-museum district.
- **[A - English Wikipedia, "Charles Eisenmann"; Wikimedia Commons category "Fedor Jeftichew"]** He sits for **Charles Eisenmann** at 229 Bowery, the photographer who turned nearly every sideshow performer of the age into a cabinet card. A credited Eisenmann portrait survives.
- **[A - Wikimedia Commons category "Fedor Jeftichew"]** Also photographed by Fred Park Swasey (active 1888-1896), and, circa 1875, by the Lumière studio alongside his father. Sixteen images survive in the Commons archive.

**Reconstructed, [R]:**

- Madison Square Garden. The Barnum show's spring residency at the Garden is well documented; Jeftichew's personal appearance there in any named year is not separately sourced. **Correction:** an earlier pass gave the residency at the "original building" as 1884-1891, which is impossible. The 1879 Garden was demolished in 1890 and Stanford White's second Garden opened 6 June 1890. The honest span for the first building is 1884 to 1889/90. Its coordinates are 40.7428 / -73.9856, at the north-east corner of Madison Avenue and East 26th Street; the earlier 40.7412 sat about 180 m south, inside Madison Square Park.
- **Bridgeport, Connecticut**, the circus's winter quarters. Well documented for the show, never documented for him by name.
- **Philadelphia, Chicago, Boston, Brooklyn, Washington.** Standard stops on the Barnum & Bailey route. No source places Jeftichew personally at any of them. They are included as circuit reconstruction and tagged as such, because a map of a touring performer that shows only the four places a newspaper happened to mention would be a falser picture than one that admits the route.
- **[R - Russia Beyond, citing an unnamed 1886 Kentucky newspaper]** A Kentucky paper calls him "one of the most interesting freaks alive". The source names neither the paper nor the city, and wire syndication means the item need not reflect an appearance in Kentucky at all. Louisville is a placeholder for the state.

## 5. The European tour, 1897 - 1902, and Salonica

- **[A - Wikipedia, "Barnum & Bailey"]** Bailey takes the Greatest Show on Earth to Europe for a five-year tour beginning December 1897.
- **[R]** London, Paris, Berlin and Vienna are reconstructed stops on that tour. Wikipedia attests the tour and its dates. It does not name Jeftichew on it, and Vienna is not named for the tour at all. The Vienna stop is the most speculative point in the entire journey file and is flagged there.
- **[R - kickassfacts.com biography, combined with Wikipedia's 1902 tour-end date]** He "briefly returned to the United States to join up with the Barnum and Bailey's Circus" before going abroad again. **Tag corrected from [A] to [R]:** the only [A]-grade element is the tour ending in 1902. Neither source names a venue, a date, or a ship.

**The death, and Contradiction 3, not resolved.**

- **[A - English Wikipedia]** Died of pneumonia **31 January 1904** at Salonica, then Ottoman, while apparently still touring.
- **[A - French Wikipedia; Russian Wikipedia]** Both give **1 February 1904.**

Place (Salonica, 40.6401 / 22.9444), cause (pneumonia), age (about thirty-five) and the Ottoman rather than Greek attribution are agreed across all three. Only the day differs, and the two-against-one split is left standing in the journey file's date_confidence field.

**Correction to an earlier pass.** That pass reported the conflict as Wikipedia's 1904 against Grokipedia's 1903. Grokipedia returned HTTP 403 and could not be read, so its alleged 1903 was itself unverified. The real, checkable discrepancy is the one printed above.

**[A by absence]** No circus, hall, hotel or lodging is named for him at Salonica. No burial record, cemetery, plot or marker has been found in any source consulted, including Find A Grave-type indexes. Whether a marked grave ever existed is unknown. This is the file's most complete silence.

## 6. Afterlife

- **[A - Wikimedia Commons category "Fedor Jeftichew"]** A Popular Science Monthly illustration of 1874 ("PSM V04 D465 Fedor Jeftichjew") and an 1888 dermatological treatise plate both reproduce him as a specimen of hypertrichosis. The medical literature and the sideshow poster use the same face and, in the American case, the same word: dog-faced.
- **[A - Wikipedia, "Hypertrichosis"]** Reference works list him among the small set of historically documented cases, dates given 1868-1904.
- **[A - Wikipedia, "Fedor Jeftichew", popular culture section]** Referenced in the 1940 film *Strike Up the Band*; recreated by an edited photograph in the 1995 *X-Files* episode "Humbug"; depicted in *The Greatest Showman* (2017).
- **[R - All That's Interesting]** A modern popular-history claim holds that his face influenced the design of Chewbacca. No primary Lucasfilm design source attests this. Unverified tradition.
- **[R - Ringling Bros. & Barnum & Bailey Circus catalog, citing Taschen, *The Circus: 1870s-1950s*, 2008, p. 132]** A real-photo postcard of Jo-Jo is reproduced in the Taschen volume.
- **[R - Russia Beyond]** Twenty-first-century Russian-language rediscovery pieces reintroduce him to a Russian readership largely unaware of the Kostroma performer's American fame.
- **[R]** No critical edition, collected letters, or scholarly monograph on Jeftichew exists in English or Spanish. **Corrected from an earlier pass** which described the coverage as encyclopedia entries and "popular clickbait history sites": the Garland Thomson volume (NYU Press, 1996) is an academic collection and treats him directly. Scholarly coverage is thin, not absent.

## 7. Interlocks with the existing corpus

Checked against the atlas's own files rather than asserted.

- **[A - san_cristobal.journey.json]** The San Cristóbal dossier already carries the cynocephali tradition: the Passion of Marmarica in Egypt, and Ratramnus of Corbie's letter on baptizing the dog-headed peoples of the north. Yevtishchev is the corpus's modern, historical instance of that theme. **House-style pass, 2026-08-14:** the naming of San Cristóbal/Ratramnus and Ibn Battuta was cut from the campa itself. Neither figure has any documented relation to Yevtishchev; it was a thematic echo (dog-headed-man motif) spanning centuries with zero citation either way, not a real interlock. The corpus fact above stands, but the journey file no longer names them.
- **[A - ibn_battuta.journey.json]** Ibn Battuta's dossier carries the Barahnakar dog-faced-people tradition. Same lineage, fourteenth century. **Cut from campa, same pass and same reason as above.**
- **[R - muybridge.journey.json]** Muybridge opens his Zoopraxographical Hall on the Midway Plaisance at the 1893 Columbian Exposition, 41.788 / -87.597. The Midway's human-curiosity and ethnological exhibits are general historical record, not in the Muybridge file, and no source places Jeftichew on the Midway. The stop is a shared ground, not a meeting. **House-style pass, 2026-08-14:** the Muybridge name was cut from the campa; the stop now describes the human-curiosity trade in general rather than a coincidental same-fairground crossing with no citation.
- **[A - joseph_pilates.journey.json]** Pilates works the English circus and music-hall economy from 1912, including Blackpool piers, and London in 1913. The overlap with Yevtishchev's earlier English circuit is inferred, not stated in the file.
- **[R - steiner.journey.json]** Steiner addresses seven thousand Berlin printers in "the great circus building" in Berlin on 24 June 1900, 52.522 / 13.402, during the years of the Barnum & Bailey European tour. **House-style pass, 2026-08-14:** the Steiner name was cut from the campa for the same reason as Muybridge above (coincidental same-building crossing, no citation either way).
- **Ruled out, recorded so the keyword sweep is not repeated.** Eiffel's 1887 "circus" is the Place de l'Étoile roundabout. Borges's "national circus" and Nietzsche's "bourgeois circus" at Bayreuth are both figurative. None is a connection.
- **[R - corpus-wide grep, no file]** No file in the corpus names Barnum, Ringling, or the American Museum. Every Barnum fact here is sourced from outside the atlas.
