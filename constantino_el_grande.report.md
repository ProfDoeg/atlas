# Constantine the Great: research report

*2026-08-02. Flavius Valerius Constantinus, c. 272 to 22 May 337. Born Naissus (Nis), died Achyron near Nicomedia (Izmit), buried Constantinople.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on a hostile or much later witness. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**Warning for the whole file.** The two fullest narratives are in open conflict of interest. Eusebius's *Vita Constantini* is a panegyric by a bishop who owed the emperor his church's freedom and his own rehabilitation after Nicaea; it omits the deaths of Crispus and Fausta entirely. Zosimus's *Historia Nova* is pagan polemic written a century and a half later by a man who blames Constantine for the fall of Rome. Lactantius is contemporary and written to show God killing persecutors. Where all three agree the fact is usually solid; where two disagree, this report says so and stops.

---

## 1. Birth and the eastern court (c. 272 to 305)

Born at **Naissus** in Moesia Superior, son of the officer Constantius Chlorus and **Helena**. **[A: Origo Constantini 2; Eutropius 10.2]** The year is given as 272 or 273 and 27 February comes from later commemorative calendars, not from any contemporary record; the journey carries 0272-02-27 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

**Correction (site and coordinate).** The pool pinned the birth at **Mediana**, 43.308 / 21.956. Wrong twice: Mediana is a villa Constantine himself built in a suburb of Naissus *during his reign*, so it did not exist in 272, and it sits at 43.3103 / 21.9489 while Naissus centre is 43.3209 / 21.8958. The journey uses the Naissus centre. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Mediana', "born and raised in Naissus"]**

**Helena's status is disputed and unresolvable** here: wife or concubine, family unrecorded. **[R]** Her birthplace is claimed by **Drepanum**, and also by Naissus, Edessa and Trier. **[R: Procopius, Aed. 5.2; Sozomen 2.2]** **Correction:** the pool gave Drepanum at 40.728 / 29.586; the site is Hersek, Altinova, at **40.72339 / 29.50224**, about 7 km west.

Sent east to **Diocletian's court at Nicomedia**, educated there, effectively a hostage for his father's loyalty. **[A: Lactantius, DMP 18; VC 1.19]** **Date correction:** the chronology lens dated this to **284**, wrong by roughly a decade, since Diocletian became emperor in November 284 when Constantine was about twelve. The pool's own geography lens gives c. 293 to 303. The journey anchors 293; no source dates the arrival exactly. **[R for the year]**

Danube campaigns c. 296. **[R: Britannica]** Dropped as a stop: no source names a place, and a pin would have been invented. Recorded as a gap.

**Persian war staged out of Antioch**, Constantine a tribune of the first order. **[A: Origo 2; VC 1.19]** **Correction, twice:** the chronology lens called it "Diocletian's Persian campaign" and the geography lens gave the command jointly to Diocletian and Galerius. The war was **Galerius's**; Diocletian was in Egypt in 297 to 298 against Domitius Domitianus. The years are 297 to 299, not 296 to 297.

**Alexandria, 297 to 298:** the pool tags Constantine's presence **[R]**, noting it is "not independently attested for Constantine." Dropped: it would have been a pin resting on an inference.

Present at Nicomedia when the persecution opens. **[A: Lactantius, DMP 12-13]** **Correction:** the pool dated the **first edict** to 23 February 303. Lactantius puts the **demolition of the church** on the 23rd and the **edict on the 24th**. The journey separates them.

**Honest gap.** Nothing indicates what Constantine thought of the persecution while it ran, and nothing he did between 303 and 312 registers as objection. Eusebius places him at Diocletian's side and has an interest in doing so. The silence is real and is not filled.

## 2. The flight, Britain, and the purple (305 to 307)

Flees or is released from Galerius's court and crosses from **Gesoriacum (Boulogne)** to Britain. **[A in outline: Origo 2; Pan. Lat. VI(7).7]** The hamstrung post horses are panegyric, and the journey presents them as such.

Campaigns **beyond Hadrian's Wall against the Picts**. **[A as an event]** **Date correction:** the pool gave 305. Constantius crossed in 305, but the campaign is normally placed in the **summer of 306**, immediately before his death on 25 July. **Honest gap:** no source names the ground. The journey pins the Wall itself (55.0125 / -2.3311) and says in `date_confidence` that the pin is a stand-in, not a location.

**Constantius dies at Eboracum, 25 July 306; the troops acclaim Constantine.** **[A: Eutropius 10.2; Origo 4; Zosimus 2.9]** **Correction:** the pool named the acclaiming body "the Sixth Legion Victrix," a modern gloss; the sources name Constantius's troops led by the Alamannic king **Crocus** (Epitome de Caesaribus 41.3). **Also corrected:** the army acclaimed him *augustus*, but **Galerius recognised him only as caesar**, a refusal dressed as a concession. Both are in the journey.

Defeats the Frankish kings **Ascaric and Merogais** and throws them to the beasts in the Trier amphitheatre. **[A: Pan. Lat. VII(6).10-12]** **Date correction:** the pool gave 306; the invasion falls in the winter of 306 to 307 and the executions in **307**. Marries **Fausta** at Trier the same year. **[A: Livius.org; Britannica]** **Coordinate correction:** the pool gave 49.7565 / 6.6414, in the Hauptmarkt; the Aula Palatina is at **49.7561 / 6.6442**.

## 3. Gaul, Maximian, and the invasion of Italy (309 to 312)

**Two pool claims dropped.** "Constantine becomes consul and constructs a bridge over the Rhine near Cologne," 309, fails in both halves. **309 is not among his attested consulships** (307, 312, 313, 315, 319, 320, 326, 329) and was a disputed consular year with no agreed western pair. The **Divitia/Deutz bridge** is dated 310, construction from about 308. Neither half survives as stated, and the bridge alone did not justify a stop.

**Maximian's second revolt and death at Massalia, 310.** **[A: Lactantius, DMP 29; Pan. Lat. VI(7).20]** The suicide by hanging is Lactantius, writing for the winning side; the journey says so.

**The Apollo vision at Trier, 25 July 310.** **[A as a speech: Pan. Lat. VI(7).21]** **Correction:** the pool called this his **decennalia**. It was his **quinquennalia**, the fifth year from 306; the decennalia fell in 315 at Rome, which the pool's geography lens has correctly.

**Segusio, Turin, Verona**, spring to late summer 312. **[A: Pan. Lat. XII(9).5-9; Zosimus 2.15-16]** Months approximate, and flagged.

**The Battle of the Milvian Bridge, 28 October 312. [A for the battle and the date.]** The vision is the central contradiction of the life, and is flagged, not resolved:

- **Eusebius, VC 1.28** gives a **daylight sky-sign**, a cross of light above the noon sun seen by the whole army, with a Greek legend Richardson renders "Conquer by this," plus Christ in a dream afterward. Eusebius says Constantine told him so himself, under oath, years later: a single-witness report of a single-witness memory.
- **Lactantius, DMP 44**, contemporary, gives **only a dream** and no words in the sky: *"Commonitus est in quiete Constantinus, ut caeleste signum dei notaret in scutis atque ita proelium committeret."* An instruction in sleep to mark the heavenly sign of God on the shields, and nothing more.
- **"In hoc signo vinces" is in neither.** The pool carried it twice as a quotation, once attributed to Lactantius. That attribution is false and was rejected; the Latin is a later formulation and the English "In this sign thou shalt conquer" is its traditional rendering, not a quotation from any ancient text. The journey quotes Eusebius with the VC I.28 citation and states the disagreement.
- **Both sources do agree on the marking of the shields.** That is the hard core of the tradition.

**Coordinate correction, twice.** The pool's geography lens gave Ponte Milvio at 41.9389 / 12.4661 and its afterlife lens at 41.9439 / 12.4664, about 370 m and about 1 km north of the bridge. The bridge is at **41.93556 / 12.46694**.

## 4. The two augusti, Milan, and the first Licinian war (312 to 321)

The Rome statue holding the cross-shaped spear, with the inscription Eusebius records. **[A: VC 1.40]** The wording is deliberately unspecific about which divinity is meant, as is the Arch inscription three years later.

**The Milan meeting, February 313:** marriage of Constantia to Licinius and a religious settlement. **[A for meeting, wedding and month: Lactantius, DMP 48]** **Substantial correction:** there was **no Edict of Milan**. The two agreed a toleration policy at Milan; the surviving instrument is a **rescript published by Licinius at Nicomedia in June 313**, and many modern historians reject the traditional label outright. The pool tagged the claim [A] under the traditional name. The journey states the correction and cites the quoted text to Eusebius, *HE* X.5, where the surviving translation of the promulgated text sits. Coordinate note: the pool gave 45.461 / 9.1859, San Lorenzo Maggiore is 45.4583 / 9.1817; the journey uses the latter, and the difference is immaterial.

**The Arch of Constantine, dedicated 25 July 315**, largely spolia recut from Trajanic, Hadrianic and Aurelian monuments, crediting *instinctu divinitatis*. **[A: CIL VI.1139]** The **Colossus of Constantine** (acrolithic, roughly 12 to 13 m, Basilica of Maxentius, c. 312 to 315, fragments rediscovered 1486) **[A: Wikipedia]** was folded into this stop's `suggested_refs` to hold the stop ceiling.

**Cibalae, first war with Licinius.** **[A for the battle: Origo 5.16-17; Zosimus 2.18]** **Date contradiction, unresolved:** the standard date is 8 October 316, but a competing scholarly chronology puts the battle in **314**. **Sirmium** as a recurring Danubian base c. 313 to 324 **[A: Origo 5.21; archaeology]** is folded into the Cibalae campa.

**Serdica, "my Rome."** **[R: attributed remark transmitted only in later Byzantine tradition]**

**The Sunday law. [A for the law in 321.]** **Contradiction flagged:** the codices give **3 March 321** (CJ 3.12.2) and **3 July 321** (CTh 2.8.1), and the pool separately carried 7 March. The place of issue is not agreed. The journey pins Serdica with the hedge stated openly; that pin is a placement of convenience, not a finding.

## 5. The last war and the founding of the city (322 to 325)

Fleet and harbour works at **Thessalonica**, 322 to 323, and the Danube crossing that gives Licinius a grievance. **[A in outline: Zosimus 2.22]**

**Correction, and it matters.** The chronology lens folded **Adrianople and Chrysopolis into one entry under 18 September 324**, misdating Adrianople by two and a half months. They are two battles: **Adrianople on the Hebrus, 3 July 324**, and **Chrysopolis, 18 September 324**. The journey separates them; the geography lens had this right.

**The Hellespont, Crispus against the eastern fleet.** **[A: Zosimus 2.23-24]** **Name contradiction, flagged not resolved:** Zosimus names the admiral **Abantus**; the form **Amandus** reached the pool and some later accounts. The journey names Abantus and records the variant.

**Byzantium besieged; Licinius surrenders at Nicomedia, is spared at Constantia's intercession, executed the following year.** **[A: Zosimus 2.23, 2.26-28. Eusebius, VC 2.18 records the surrender and omits the execution, which is characteristic.]** Folded into the Chrysopolis campa.

**Foundation rites at Byzantium, 8 November 324. [A for the date.]** The spear-traced wall line and the answer "until the one who walks ahead of me stops" are **[R: Philostorgius 2.9, a foundation legend]**, presented in the journey as legend told in the canon's register.

**The First Council of Nicaea, opening May 325.** **[A: VC 3.10-13; Socrates 1.8]** **Correction:** the pool dated the close to **19 June 325**, at best the date of the creed's subscription. Sessions ran May to end of July; the council "closed in the first weeks of July, with the bishops invited to attend Constantine's celebration of his twentieth anniversary on the throne on 25 July," and **25 August** is another traditional close. The journey gives the opening as attested and states all three competing closes.

## 6. The killings of 326, and Helena's provinces (326 to 330)

**Crispus executed at Pola, 326.** **[A for year and place: Origo 6.30; Zosimus 2.29; Wikipedia, 'Crispus', "apparently without trial"]** **Correction:** the pool gave **February 326**; no ancient source gives a month, and modern reconstructions put the death around **May 326**, on the road to the July Rome visit. The journey gives 0326-05-01 and marks the month a reconstruction.

**Honest gap, the largest in the life.** No source supplies a charge the other sources accept. Constantine never issued an explanation. Crispus's name was struck from inscriptions across the empire, and Eusebius, writing eleven years later, narrates the reign without mentioning that Constantine had an eldest son. The record was destroyed deliberately, and this report does not speculate about what it held.

**Fausta dies at Rome, 326. [R, a downgrade from the pool's [A].]** The episode rests on hostile or much later sources: Zosimus 2.29 for the frame, the overheated bath later still. Eusebius omits the death entirely and Constantine's own record is silent.

**The Rome visit of July 326 and the refusal on the Capitol. [R for the refusal: Zosimus 2.29, hostile and disputed.]** **Correction:** the pool called the visit his **tricennalia**. It was his **vicennalia**: the twentieth year opened 25 July 325, which is why the Nicene bishops were invited to it, and closed with the July 326 festivities. The tricennalia fell in 335 to 336 and were kept in the East. What is certain about 326 is a negative: Constantine never enters Rome again.

**Helena's pilgrimage, 326 to 328. [R as tradition: Wikipedia, 'Helena (empress)']** The **finding of the True Cross is later tradition**; Eusebius reports the Golgotha excavation and not the discovery of the wood.

**The three Palestinian foundations: Holy Sepulchre, Nativity, Eleona. [A: VC 3.25-43]** **Coordinate correction:** the pool gave the Nativity at 31.7043 / 35.2033, about 400 m west of the church and short of Manger Square; the correct point is approximately **31.7042 / 35.2076**. The Mount of Olives stop was folded into the Bethlehem campa and its pin recorded in `suggested_refs`.

**Helenopolis**, the refounding of Drepanum in his mother's name. **[A for the refounding; R for the reason, her birth there being tradition]**

**Helena's death and burial on the Via Labicana. [A for the burial]** **Date contradiction:** the pool gives 330, other reckonings 328 to 329; the year is not fixed. The porphyry sarcophagus in the Vatican, carved with cavalry and captives, is generally thought to have been cut for Constantine and reassigned. **[R]**

## 7. The city, the last years, and the death (330 to 337)

**Dedication of Constantinople, 11 May 330. [A: Chronicon Paschale]** **The Column of Constantine**, dedicated 330 with erection begun 328, originally near 50 m overall, crowned with a bronze Constantine as Helios-Apollo; shaft today 34.8 m. **[A: Zosimus 2.31; Wikipedia]** **Coordinate contradiction inside the pool, settled by measurement:** the geography lens gave 28.9689, the afterlife lens **28.9711**; the monument stands at 41 00 31 N, 28 58 16 E, so **41.0086 / 28.9711**.

**Canonical pin inheritance, recorded for a later editor.** The corpus carries two established Constantinople pins: **41.0086 / 28.9802** (saint_barbara, hypatia, pierre_bourlemont, blavatsky, san_cristobal, gustave_flaubert) and **41.0082 / 28.9784** (marco_polo, ibn_battuta, san_antonio_abad, miranda, paracelsus, emin_arslan). Six files each, an exact split. The journey inherits **41.0086 / 28.9802** byte-identically for general city stops, because the interlock lens cited it for "the City of Constantine" in saint_barbara. This is a convention, not a finding, and can be reversed knowingly. Reims (49.2583 / 4.0317) and the Holy Apostles / Fatih site (41.0192 / 28.9497) are inherited exactly.

**The tricennalia, 335. [R:** the pool's own note reads "general knowledge of Constantine's reign, not independently confirmed in fetched sources this session."**]** Eusebius's tricennalian oration is attested as a text.

**"I also am a bishop." [A as a text, undated as an event: VC IV.24]** **Correction to the wording.** The pool submitted "I myself am, as it were, appointed by God overseer of those outside the church" and credited it to Richardson. That is not Richardson's sentence. Richardson reads: *"You are bishops whose jurisdiction is within the Church: I also am a bishop, ordained by God to overlook whatever is external to the Church."* The journey uses the verified sentence and states in `quote_source` that the remark is undated. The familiar "bishop of those outside" phrasing must be cited to Cameron and Hall (Oxford, 1999), not to NPNF, and was kept out entirely.

**The letters to Antony in the Egyptian desert. [A: Athanasius, Vita Antonii 81; corroborated by san_antonio_abad.journey.json at c. 337]** The letters do not survive; the reply is transmitted only through Athanasius.

**The last illness, the springs, and Achyron. [A for the illness and withdrawal: VC 4.61]** **[R for the site: Achyron has never been securely identified.]** Pinned at 40.75 / 30.03 and marked as such.

**Baptism by Eusebius of Nicomedia and death 22 May 337. [A: VC 4.61-64; Socrates 1.39]** The baptizer was an **Arian**, of the party the emperor's own council condemned at Nicaea twelve years earlier. The later Western legend of baptism at Rome by Sylvester exists to replace this fact and is **[R]**, and forged.

**Burial in the mausoleum at the Church of the Holy Apostles. [A: VC 4.58-60, 4.70-71]** The church was unfinished at his death and completed by **Constantius II**, who performed the interment; the month is not fixed. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's afterlife lens gave 41.0294 / 28.9497, about 1.1 km north of the mosque precinct in Carsamba; the correct point is **41.0192 / 28.9497**, which the geography lens had.

## 8. Afterlife

**Sack of 1204:** crusaders loot the imperial tombs at the Holy Apostles. **[A: Wikipedia]** Whether Constantine's own sarcophagus was opened is not separately documented.

**Demolition and the Fatih Mosque. [A, with two corrections.]** The pool said the church "briefly serves as the Ecumenical Patriarchate's seat before being demolished" and dated the mosque 1462. Both wrong: Gennadius Scholarius held the church from 1453 and moved to the **Pammakaristos in 1456**, so the patriarchate left *before* the demolition rather than because of it; the church came down in **1461** and the mosque was built **1463 to 1470**.

**The Donation of Constantine. [R as a document:** a forgery of roughly the eighth century, possibly linked to Stephen II's negotiations with Pepin the Short. **A** that Leo IX cites it in 1054.**]**

**Valla's exposure, 1439 to 1440. [A** for the treatise and the philological argument, including the anachronistic feudal term *fief*.**]** **Correction:** the pool pinned 41.8719 / 12.5674, a generic Italy centroid east of Rome. Valla wrote the *Declamatio* **at Naples**, at Alfonso of Aragon's court, Alfonso's quarrel with Eugene IV being the occasion. The journey pins **Naples, 40.8518 / 14.2681**, and notes the treatise was not printed until 1506.

**The York bronze, 1998**, by Philip Jackson, in Minster Yard over the legionary principia; the plinth reads "Constantine by this sign conquer." **[A: Wikipedia]**

## 9. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Named in campa where the crossing is real: **san_antonio_abad** (Constantine and his sons write to Antony; Antony dictates a reply) **[A]**; **virgil** and **dante** (Constantine reads Eclogue 4 aloud as prophecy of Christ, which is why the church keeps Virgil and why Dante inherits him) **[A]**; **clovis** and **clotilde** (Tolbiac, then "a new Constantine" at the Reims font) **[A]**; **charlemagne** (Frankfurt "as under a new Constantine") **[A]**; **otto_iii** (a pope who takes the name Sylvester on purpose) **[A]**; **saint_barbara** (relics translated to the city in the sixth century) **[A]**; **marco_polo** and **ibn_battuta** (both pass through the city and past the vanished mausoleum) **[A]**; **pierre_bourlemont** (1179, into the Golden Horn, marvelling at porphyry emperors) **[A]**; **napoleon** ("I would have returned to Paris by way of Constantinople") **[A]**.

Found by the lens and **not** named, with reasons. **merlin**: Geoffrey of Monmouth's British King Constantine is a different figure, a name-echo correctly flagged in the pool. **Constantine, Algeria** on the ibn_battuta manuscript route: the city is named for the emperor and is not the emperor, a second name-echo. **charles_martel, san_cristobal, paracelsus, blavatsky, miranda, emin_arslan**: real Bosphorus pins, but the lens tags most **[R] inferred**, meaning the corpus file names the city and not Constantine. Naming them all in one campa would have produced a list rather than a sentence.

## 10. What is not in this file

No Danube stop (296): no place named by any source. No Alexandria stop (297 to 298): a presence the pool itself declined to attest. No consulship-and-bridge stop (309): both halves failed verification. No separate Mount of Olives, Sirmium, Byzantium-siege or Colossus stop: attested, but folded into neighbouring campas and `suggested_refs` to hold the 45-stop ceiling. Two verified quotations left unplaced: VC I.29 (Christ appearing with the same sign) and VC II.64 (the letter to Alexander and Arius); both checked against Richardson and available to a later editor, neither with a stop where it could sit without displacing a better one. No claim about Constantine's inner belief: the sources reporting it are the ones with an interest in it.
