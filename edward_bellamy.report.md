# Edward Bellamy (1850-1898): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-05 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Edward Bellamy, novelist and journalist
of Chicopee Falls, Massachusetts; author of* Looking Backward, 2000-1887 *(1888), the only
American novel of its century that produced a national political movement inside a year of
publication.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference.
Contradictions are printed and left open. Unreached sources are named with the reason.

**Caution about the base source.** English Wikipedia, "Edward Bellamy," is the spine of most of
what follows and is wrong in four checkable places, each corrected in place below: the date of
the law study, the year of *Miss Ludington's Sister*, *The Duke of Stockbridge* called
unfinished, and the abandonment of journalism in 1878.

---

## 1. Chicopee Falls (1850-1867)

**Birth [A].** 26 March 1850 at Chicopee Falls (Wikipedia, "Edward Bellamy"), confirmed
independently by the Fairview Cemetery obelisk inscription (§8). Father Rufus King Bellamy,
Baptist minister, descended from Joseph Bellamy, the Great Awakening theologian. Mother Maria
Louisa Putnam Bellamy, daughter of a Baptist minister who left his Salem pulpit over objections
to his Freemasonry. **Not born in the landmark house [A]:** 91-93 Church Street was built in
1852, two years after his birth (Wikipedia, "Edward Bellamy House"), and his parents moved in
while he was an infant. The journey file uses the village centre for the birth stop.

**Family [A].** First cousin of Francis Bellamy, later author of the Pledge of Allegiance. The
strongest interlock in the atlas, already written into `francis_bellamy.journey.json` from the
other side.

**Schooling [A for the fact, R for the place].** He attends the public school of Chicopee
Falls. No schoolhouse, teacher, or curriculum is named anywhere reachable; the coordinate
(42.159, -72.582) is a village-centre estimate. **[R]** That the household's religious severity
lies behind the later moral fervour is inference, not a statement by Bellamy; the journey file
puts it as juxtaposition, not causation.

**West Point [A, upgraded from the gathering lens's "tradition"].** Three sources date the
application to 1867 and the rejection to physical frailty: Sadler, "One Book's Influence,"
*New England Quarterly* 17 (1944); "Rose Culture and Social Reform," JSTOR 20719136 ("In 1867
Bellamy had applied to West Point, but failed the physical examination"); Encyclopedia.com.
**Coordinate correction [A]:** the lens gave 42.3608, -73.9721, in Columbia County, about 107
km north of the Academy. Correct: 41.3913, -73.9659 (OpenStreetMap).

## 2. Union College, Dresden, the law (1867-1871)

**Union College [A].** Enrolled 1867, two semesters, no degree (Wikipedia). **Coordinate
correction [A]:** the lens gave 42.8163, -73.9264 and called it the Nott Memorial; the Nott
Memorial is at about 42.8175, -73.9295, roughly 285 m northwest. **Spencer reading [R]:**
standard in the literature, not confirmed from a primary document.

**Germany [A for the country, R for the city].** Wikipedia, "Edward Bellamy" dates the stay
1868 and names only Germany. Wikipedia, "Edward Bellamy House," following Bowman's chronology,
is more precise: "from 1868 to 1869 he lived in Germany with a cousin." Encyclopedia.com
places the stay at Dresden. The city level is therefore one source deep.

**The law [A, and Wikipedia is wrong].** Wikipedia has him studying law in the "late 1860s" and
never practising. Encyclopedia.com, corroborated by swiftpapers: he read law in a Springfield
firm and **was admitted to the bar in June 1871**, then took a single case and left. Two
corrections. The date is 1871, and he practised once, so "never practised" is wrong too.

## 3. Journalism (1871-1878)

**New York Evening Post [A].** Position taken 1871 (Wikipedia). **[R]** No numbered address for
his desk was found; the coordinate marks the Park Row newspaper district. **Springfield Union
[A]:** leaves New York 1872, editorial writer and literary critic. **[R]** Address not fixed;
coordinate marks Court Square.

**Tuberculosis [A].** Develops at about age 25, so around 1875, and kills him in 1898.

**Hawaii [A].** About a year in the Hawaiian Islands, 1877-1878 (Wikipedia). **[R]** No Honolulu
lodging or street is named anywhere reachable; coordinate marks the downtown waterfront. **San
Francisco [R]:** no ticket, passenger list, or letter places Bellamy there. That stop is routing
only, inferred from 1870s trans-Pacific steamship service, and labelled a reconstruction.

**"Abandoned journalism in 1878" [A, overstated].** He contributed to the *Springfield Daily
Union* through 1881 (Encyclopedia.com; swiftpapers) and founded a newspaper in 1880. The correct
shape is a gradual shift toward fiction across 1878-1881.

## 4. The Homestead years (1878-1887)

**Six to One: A Nantucket Idyl [A publication, R visit].** Published 1878. Nothing reachable
establishes a visit; the stop is a reconstruction from the setting.

**The Duke of Stockbridge [A, and "unfinished" is wrong].** The novel was completed and ran as
a serial in 1879 in the **Berkshire Courier of Great Barrington, Massachusetts**
(Harvard/Belknap 1962 John Harvard Library edition, ed. Joseph Schiffman; first-edition
catalogue records). The 1900 Silver, Burdett book was edited by Bellamy's cousin, who altered
the text so heavily that the 1962 scholarly edition went back to the serial. Correct formula:
**serialised 1879, issued posthumously in an edited book text in 1900.**

**Great Barrington, not Stockbridge [A / R].** The *Berkshire Courier* was published at Great
Barrington [A]. A Stockbridge research visit is undocumented [R], and Morgan held the novel's
town is modelled on Chicopee, which weakens it further. The journey file places the stop at
Great Barrington and drops the proposed Stockbridge stop entirely.

**Springfield Penny News [A, gathering account wrong on two points].** A tri-weekly founded in
1880 by Edward and his brother Charles, which became the *Springfield Daily News*. (1) It did
**not** begin from a downtown Springfield office: "When Bellamy helped found the Penny News
(later the Springfield Daily News), it began publication in the house" at Chicopee Falls
(Wikipedia, "Edward Bellamy House," citing Morgan p. 127). (2) It did not fold. The journey
file places the stop at the Homestead. *Dr. Heidenhoff's Process* [A] follows the same year.

**Marriage [A for the year, CONTRADICTION on the day].** Emma Augusta Sanderson, 1882
(Wikipedia). The geography lens gave 31 May; Hampden County genealogical aggregations
(Familypedia, WikiTree) give **30 May 1882 at Chicopee**. Both weak. The journey file uses the
30th and prints the disagreement in `date_confidence` without settling it. **[A]** Sanderson
had been a ward of Rufus Bellamy's household since 1874 (Wikipedia, "Edward Bellamy House,"
citing Bowman and Morgan p. 54); obelisk dates 12 February 1861 to 4 September 1956.

**Children [A, gathering hedge dropped].** The lens wrote that the sources do not give their
names. They do: **Paul** and **Marion** (edwardbellamyhouse.org). **Residence [A]:** not
continuous, the couple living next door for part of 1886 while expecting a second child and
returning after Rufus Bellamy died that November.

**Miss Ludington's Sister [A, and Wikipedia is wrong].** Wikipedia dates it 1885. It is
**1884**: Boston, James R. Osgood and Company, 1884 on both title and copyright pages (Library
of Congress catalog item 06011696; archive.org `missludingtonssi00bell`; WorldCat OCLC
13138074). The gathering lens contradicted itself here, chronology giving 1885 and geography
1884.

## 5. Looking Backward (1887-1889)

**Composition and first edition [A].** Planned through the 1880s, completed 1887, submitted to
Benjamin Ticknor, published by Ticknor and Company in January 1888 to modest sales (Wikipedia,
"Looking Backward").

**Publisher coordinate correction [A].** The lens placed Ticknor at 42.3567, -71.0603 and
justified it by the Old Corner Bookstore. That conflates two firms. Benjamin Ticknor's
**Ticknor and Company** (1885-1889, formed out of the second J. R. Osgood and Company) was at
**211 Tremont Street**; it is not the Ticknor and Fields of the Old Corner Bookstore, which
merged into Houghton in 1878 (Wikipedia, "Ticknor and Fields"). The journey file uses 42.3524,
-71.0637.

**Houghton Mifflin reissue [A].** September 1889, minor revisions; this is the edition that
turns local sales into an international phenomenon.

**Sales [A].** Past 400,000 by 1897; more than 235,000 in the United Kingdom 1890-1935;
532,000 in the United States by mid-1939 (Wikipedia, "Looking Backward").

**CONTRADICTION in the rankings.** Wikipedia, "Looking Backward," citing Daniel Immerwahr, ranks
its early sales second in the century behind *Uncle Tom's Cabin*; Wikipedia, "Edward Bellamy,"
calls it the third best-selling American novel of the century. These may measure different
windows, but nothing reachable says so. Both are printed in the journey file, neither
adjudicated. **Coordinate note:** Houghton Mifflin's Park Street address (42.3568, -71.0631) is
the firm's historic address, not confirmed for this transaction.

## 6. Nationalism (1888-1896)

**First Nationalist Club [A for the fact, R for the venue].** Organised at Boston, October
1888; Bellamy is not its founder. Tremont Temple is a plausible reform hall proposed by the
gathering lens and **not confirmed**; the journey file keeps the venue and keeps the flag
visible in both the stop text and `date_confidence`. Approximately 162 clubs by 1891 [A].

**The New Nation [A].** Founded 1891, published from Boston, ceased 1894 for want of money.
**[R]** Street address not established; coordinate marks the downtown publishing district.

**People's Party [A].** Bellamy works to influence the platform and urges the clubs toward
united action (1891). By 1892 club activists are largely absorbed into the party apparatus and
the Nationalist name fades.

**Wisconsin 1896 [A for the fact, R for the coordinate].** A residual Nationalist Party runs
three candidates. The sources give the state, no town, no names, no result. The Milwaukee
coordinate (43.0389, -87.9065) is a state-level marker and documents nothing. Likewise the
162-clubs stop has no single place: the journey file uses the geographic centre of the
contiguous states (39.8283, -98.5795) and says in the stop text that it is a marker.

## 7. Equality and the end (1897-1898)

**Equality [A].** 1897, extending the argument into women's suffrage, reproductive rights,
animal welfare, and wilderness preservation. Never approaches the reach of the first book.
Several short stories follow in 1898 [A].

**Colorado [CONTRADICTION, unresolved].** Some accounts have him travelling west for a climate
cure in the last months; Wikipedia describes his death at Chicopee Falls and mentions no trip.
Nothing reachable settles it. The journey file keeps the Denver stop and prints the
disagreement.

**Death [A].** 22 May 1898, tuberculosis, Chicopee Falls, age 48. Confirmed against the grave
inscription. **Coordinate correction [A]:** the afterlife lens gave 42.1758, -72.5762 for both
the death place and the house, about 2.3 km north of it. Correct: 42.1560, -72.5839
(OpenStreetMap, Edward Bellamy House museum), matching NRHP 42.1558, -72.5844 and the NHL
listing's 42.1561, -72.5841. The journey file uses the NHL figure throughout; the alternatives
are within about 30 m.

## 8. Burial

**Fairview Cemetery [A, upgraded from R].** The lens tagged this tradition because Find a Grave
returned 403 Forbidden and the NPS/NHL nomination PDF was unreadable. It is confirmable without
either: Waymarking WMRK6V / wmRK5B describes "a family brownstone obelisk in Fairview Cemetery,
Chicopee, MA" marking the grave, at **N 42 08.389 W 072 35.416**, base inscribed:

> EDWARD BELLAMY MARCH 26, 1850 MAY 22, 1898 / EMMA S. BELLAMY FEBRUARY 12, 1861
> SEPTEMBER 4, 1956

**Coordinate correction [A].** The geography lens gave 42.1493, -72.5735, about 1.9 km
northeast and outside the cemetery. Fairview Cemetery is at 42.1390, -72.5895 (OpenStreetMap
way 29906454, bbox 42.1372-42.1426 N, 72.5921-72.5867 W). The afterlife lens's 42.1391,
-72.5899 is correct and is what the journey file uses.

## 9. Afterlife

**The house [A].** 91-93 Church Street, built 1852, designated a National Historic Landmark 11
November 1971. The Edward Bellamy Memorial Association acquired it in 1974 and restored it
through the 1970s and 1980s; part operates as a museum, the rest is rented for income.

**Translation [A, partly overstated].** "At least thirteen languages" is **not earned by its
cited source**: Wikipedia, "Looking Backward" says only "several languages." The individual
items hold: Bulgarian (1892), Chinese, German, French, Russian;
the Russian edition banned by Tsarist censors (Bowman, *Edward Bellamy Abroad*, pp. 70-78,
cited at second hand). The journey file says "several" and asserts no count.

**Chinese serial [A].** Timothy Richard's abridged translation, serialised 1891-1892 in *Wanguo
Gongbao* as *Huitou kan jilue*, later *Bainian Yi Jiao* ("A Sleep of 100 Years"), called the
first Western science fiction published in Qing China. **[R]** The Shanghai coordinate (31.2304,
121.4737) is city-level; no editorial address is fixed.

**Bulgarian adaptation [A].** Iliya Yovchev produced an adapted rather than literal version,
relocating the story to Sofia, with Bellamy's personal approval; Wikipedia treats this as a
separate category from the standard 1892 Bulgarian translation, and the journey file preserves
the distinction. **[R]** The Sofia coordinate marks the city the adaptation moved the book
into, not a printing address.

**British Edward Bellamy Society [R, not used].** Aggregated search results describe a society
formed in Britain in his lifetime, nine branches, his endorsement. Not confirmed against any
primary British record. A lead, not a fact, and **not** in the journey file.

**Also [A].** Erich Fromm: "It is one of the few books ever published that created almost
immediately on its appearance a political mass movement." Mack Reynolds, *Looking Backward from
the Year 2000* (1974); Roger Lee Hall, *Bellamy's Musical Telephone*, premiered Emerson College
1988. Bellamy Loop, Co-op City, the Bronx, named July 1967; Bellamy Road, Toronto.

## 11. Honest gaps

- **No manuscript, letter, or diary was read.** Every claim comes through published secondary
  literature or reference works. Bowman (1958, 1962) and Morgan (1944), the standard monographs,
  reach this report only through citation.
- **Dresden is city-level tradition**, carried by Encyclopedia.com alone.
- **No address is fixed** for the *Evening Post* desk, the *Springfield Union* office, the *New
  Nation* office, the Honolulu lodging, or the First Nationalist Club's hall. Five district-level
  coordinates in the journey file are approximations, each flagged in its own stop.
- **Unresolved and printed as such:** the Colorado trip; the marriage date, off by one day
  between weak sources; the two sales rankings.
- **The Wisconsin ticket of 1896** has no town, no candidate names, and no result. **The
  thirteen-languages figure** is unsupported by its cited source and is reduced to "several."
