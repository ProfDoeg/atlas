# Dante Gabriel Rossetti (1828-1882)

*Compiled 2026-08-13. Legend: **[A]** attested, source named; **[R]** reconstruction or single-source claim not independently confirmed. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.*

Rossetti enters the corpus late and surrounded. Eleven travelers already in the atlas cross his path: Christina Rossetti, Hunt, Millais, Overbeck, Ruskin, William Morris, Jane Morris, Bessie Burden, May Morris, Burne-Jones, and Dante Alighieri. The research problem is not scarcity but overlap: almost every well-known Rossetti anecdote is already told from someone else's side in an existing journey file, and several pins the atlas carries for those shared places disagree with the best available coordinates. Both problems are recorded rather than smoothed over.

## 1. The house of the Italian exile (1828-1845)

- Born 12 May 1828 at 38 Charlotte Street, Portland Place, second child of Gabriele Rossetti and Frances Mary Lavinia Polidori; full name Gabriel Charles Dante Rossetti. **[A: Wikipedia; English Heritage]** The street was renumbered and renamed: 38 Charlotte Street is now 110 Hallam Street. Fitzrovia's surviving Charlotte Street (W1T) is a *different* street about 550 m east, and the coordinate confusion between them is real. **[A]**
- Family moves to 50 Charlotte Street in 1836, staying until 1851, then to 38 Arlington Street. No. 50 is demolished. **[A: Rossetti Archive, *Family-Letters*]**
- King's College School, in the basement of King's College on the Strand, where his father taught Italian. **[A: Victorian Web chronology]** *Date contradiction:* the chronology lens gives entry 1837, the geography lens 1836. Not resolved; the journey file carries 1837 and says so in `date_confidence`.
- Henry Sass's Drawing Academy, Bloomsbury, from 1841; the street number is **not** confirmed in any source reached **[gap]**. **[A: Victorian Web]**
- 1844: translates Bürger's "Lenore" and parts of the Nibelungenlied. c. 1845: completes the translations later published as *The Early Italian Poets*. **[R: Victorian Web only; no primary manuscript seen]**

**Royal Academy Schools, an inverted sequence.** The claim in circulation ("probationer 1846, later a full student in the Antique School") reverses the order. Correct: probationer 1844, full student December 1845, Antique School July 1846. **[A: Rossetti Archive; Marillier, *Rossetti*, Gutenberg]** Wikipedia gives 1845 for the Antique School, so the cited source supports neither the received date nor its sequence. The journey file uses July 1846 and records the disagreement.

## 2. The Brotherhood (1848-1849)

- March 1848: writes to Ford Madox Brown asking to be taken as a pupil; Brown accepts and sets him to painting pickle jars. The arrangement ends fast, the friendship lasts to the grave, where Brown designs the headstone. **[A: Doughty; Wikipedia]** Studio coordinates are approximate. **[R]**
- 1848: shares a studio with William Holman Hunt at Cleveland Street; begins *The Girlhood of Mary Virgin* beside him, Christina Rossetti modelling the Virgin. Hunt's journey records that Rossetti sought him out after seeing *The Eve of St Agnes* at the Academy. **[A: william_holman_hunt.journey.json; Rossetti Archive]**
- September 1848: the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood founded at the Millais family house, 83 Gower Street: Rossetti, Hunt, Millais, William Michael Rossetti as secretary, James Collinson, Thomas Woolner, F. G. Stephens. **[A: Fitzwilliam Museum; john_everett_millais.journey.json]** The atlas's Overbeck journey records the deliberate echo of the Lukasbund of 1809. **[A: johann_friedrich_overbeck.journey.json]**
- March 1849: *The Girlhood of Mary Virgin* at the Free Exhibition, Hyde Park Corner, the first picture publicly exhibited with the initials P.R.B. **[A: Rossetti Archive]** January to April 1850: four issues of *The Germ*, carrying the first finished text of "The Blessed Damozel" and seven poems by Christina, William Michael editing; *Ecce Ancilla Domini!* is exhibited to hostile notices, including in The Times, and the Brotherhood begins dissolving as a formal body. **[A: Victorian Web; christina_rossetti.journey.json]**

## 3. Paris and the Flemish circuit (autumn 1849)

Rossetti and Hunt tour the Louvre in September, then Belgium in October: Brussels, Antwerp, Ghent, Bruges. From Bruges on 25 October he writes that the "miraculous works of Memling and Van Eyck" are "by far the best of all" he has seen, and later writes a sonnet on Memling for *The Germ*. The Ghent Altarpiece is seen in situ at St Bavo's. **[A: Victorian Web, "D.G. Rossetti's Trip to Paris and Belgium"; letter of 25 October 1849, rossettiarchive.org]**

*Coordinate error, corrected:* the Brussels pin in circulation (51.51, 4.3572) carries a **London latitude** and lands in the Netherlands about 75 km north of the city. The Musées Royaux des Beaux-Arts are at approximately 50.8417, 4.3582. Antwerp is dropped from the stop list for length, not for doubt.

## 4. Chatham Place and Elizabeth Siddal (1850-1862)

- Walter Deverell spots the shop assistant Elizabeth Siddal in a back room of a milliner's in Cranbourne Alley; she sits first as Viola for him, then for the circle, then almost exclusively for Rossetti. **[A: COVE chronology; Allingham]** The year is given as 1849 in some sources and 1850 in others. Not resolved.
- 1851: lodgings and studio at 17 Red Lion Square; Morris and Burne-Jones take the same rooms from 1856. **[A: Survey of London; Mackail, ch. 4]**
- 1852: sets up at 14 Chatham Place; Siddal is installed there and the two are understood to be engaged, though the status and date of the engagement are reported inconsistently **[gap]**. In 1853, with pictures not selling, he inquires after a post as a telegraph operator with the Great Northern Railway. **[R: Britannica summaries; Victorian Web only]**
- 1854: Robert McCracken of Belfast buys *Dante Drawing an Angel* and introduces Rossetti to John Ruskin, who becomes his patron. **[A: Victorian Web]** Ruskin teaches drawing unpaid at F. D. Maurice's Working Men's College alongside Rossetti, Brown, and Burne-Jones, whose testimony ("He taught me practically all I ever learnt") the journey file carries as a quote. **[A: john_ruskin.journey.json; edward_burne_jones.journey.json]**
- 23 May 1860: marries Siddal at St Clement's, Hastings Old Town, no family present; she is carried the short distance to the church. Honeymoon in Paris and Boulogne, not carried as a stop for length. **[A: Hastings Online Times; parish history; Allingham]**
- 1860-61: contributes painted furniture and murals to Red House, Bexleyheath, with Siddal, Burne-Jones, and Brown. **[A: National Trust; Art Fund]** *Coordinate correction:* Red House is at 51.4556, 0.1303, not 0.149, which lands about 1.3 km east.
- 11 February 1862: Siddal dies of laudanum, not long after a stillbirth; the verdict is accidental death. **[A: Wikipedia; Victorian Web]** Rossetti places the manuscript of his unpublished poems in her coffin before burial at Highgate West. **[A: Castleton; Connotations]**

## 5. Oxford, the Union, and Jane Burden (1856-1857)

- 1856: meets the Oxford undergraduates William Morris and Edward Burne-Jones and recruits them for the Oxford and Cambridge Magazine. **[A: Victorian Web]** His February 1857 letter to William Bell Scott naming "Morris and Jones ... both men of real genius" is verbatim in Mackail, ch. 4, and is carried as a quote in the journey file. **[A]**
- August to November 1857: leads a volunteer team painting Malory subjects on the upper walls of the Oxford Union debating hall: Morris, Burne-Jones, Arthur Hughes, Val Prinsep, Spencer-Stanhope, Pollen. His own subject is *Sir Lancelot's Vision of the Sanc Grael*. Distemper on unprepared brick; the murals begin failing within months. Work breaks off in mid-November when Rossetti is called to Siddal, ill at Matlock. **[A: Wikipedia; Ashmolean; william_morris.journey.json]**

**Two contradictions, neither resolved.**

**(a) The Woodward meeting.** The chronology in circulation places it at Oxford in July 1855, with Siddal; the Rossetti Archive places it in **August 1857**, immediately before the murals. A separate 1855 contact exists, an invitation to decorate the Oxford Museum which Rossetti declined. The journey file carries the 1857 sequence.

**(b) Who spotted Jane Burden.** The Rossetti Archive dates the theatre encounter to **September 1857** and names **Rossetti and Morris**. The Ashmolean and the Burne-Jones biography name **Rossetti and Burne-Jones**. The atlas's jane_morris and elizabeth_bessie_burden journeys carry **October 1857** and the Burne-Jones version; the journey file inherits that date and pin for continuity and states the disagreement in `date_confidence`. Bessie Burden was present and goes unnoticed in the record. **[A: elizabeth_bessie_burden.journey.json]**

## 6. The Firm, Tudor House, and the exhumation (1859-1870)

- 1859: *Bocca Baciata*, modelled by Fanny Cornforth, turns his practice toward close-up single-female oils. **[A: Victorian Web]**
- 11 April 1861: founding partner of Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co. at 8 Red Lion Square with Morris, Burne-Jones, Brown, Philip Webb, P. P. Marshall, Charles Faulkner. Hunt is pointedly not among them. **[A: william_morris.journey.json]** The same year Ruskin finances *The Early Italian Poets*, reissued 1874 as *Dante and His Circle*. **[A: Rossetti Archive]**
- 24 October 1862: takes up residence at Tudor House, 16 Cheyne Walk, sharing with Swinburne, George Meredith, and William Michael. **[A: Rossetti Archive: "24 October 1862: 16 Cheyne Walk. Rossetti takes up residence."]** *Flagged:* the chronology in circulation says November.
- The Cheyne Walk menagerie: wallabies, armadillos, a raccoon, peacocks, a zebu, kookaburras, and the wombat "Top", acquired September 1869 and mourned in verse and drawing. **[A: Public Domain Review, "O Uommibatto"]** Lord Cadogan, the ground landlord, inserted a clause forbidding peacocks; the version in which the ban enters *all future leases* is embellishment. **[R]**
- 7 June 1865: Rossetti stages a day-long photographic session of Jane Morris at Cheyne Walk, posing and dressing her himself while John Robert Parsons works the camera. **[A: jane_morris.journey.json]** Summer 1869: Penkill Castle, Ayrshire, home of Alice Boyd, at William Bell Scott's invitation. **[A: williambellscott.org]**
- 5 October 1869: the grave at Highgate is opened at night on Charles Augustus Howell's prompting, under a Home Secretary's licence with a solicitor and doctor as witnesses; Rossetti does not attend. On 15 October he writes that the book is in a "disappointing state but not hopeless", with a "dreadful smell" and "a great worm-hole" through many pages, including every page of "Jenny". **[A: W. M. Rossetti correspondence, cited by the Cambridge English Faculty]**
- March 1870: rests his eyes at Barbara Bodichon's Scalands Gate, writing to Burne-Jones on 13 March; Jane Morris is nearby at Hastings. **[A: Burne-Jones Catalogue Raisonné]** In April, *Poems by D. G. Rossetti*, built from the exhumed manuscript, carries the first installment of *The House of Life*. **[A: Rossetti Archive]**

## 7. Kelmscott, the Fleshly School, and the break (1871-1874)

- **Date correction, load-bearing.** The lease date of 1869 in circulation is wrong. Morris and Rossetti agreed the joint Kelmscott lease on **20 May 1871**, from the farmer Charles Hobbs. **[A: Rossetti Archive; Kelmscott Manor Trust; Historic England]** The geography lens and the william_morris journey both have 1871 correctly.
- Summer 1871: Morris leaves for Iceland; Jane Morris, Rossetti, and the daughters Jenny and May live at the manor together, and he paints Jane repeatedly there. **[A: jane_morris.journey.json; mary_may_morris.journey.json]**
- October 1871: Robert Buchanan, as "Thomas Maitland" in the Contemporary Review, attacks Rossetti as "the fleshly school of poetry"; Rossetti replies in the Athenaeum; the pseudonym collapses. The expanded **pamphlet** follows in **May 1872**. **[A: Wikipedia]** *Flagged:* the afterlife material dates the pamphlet 1871, conflating article and pamphlet.
- **Place correction, load-bearing.** The 8 June 1872 laudanum overdose did **not** happen at 16 Cheyne Walk. Rossetti had been removed to Dr Thomas Gordon Hake's house at **Roehampton**, was left alone at night, and was not found until the following afternoon. **[A: Rossetti Archive; Marsh]** The journey pin is a district approximation. **[R]**
- 21 June 1872: taken north by Hake and his son George; about nine weeks at Urrard House, Killiecrankie. **[A: Rossetti Archive]** September 1872: returns to Kelmscott under Jane Morris's care, on chloral with whisky nightly. Morris's letter to Aglaia Coronio of November 1872, the plainest surviving statement of the arrangement, is not carried as a stop, Rossetti not being present. **[A: jane_morris.journey.json]**
- **Sequence correction.** The received version has Morris reorganizing the Firm and thereby driving Rossetti out. The order is the reverse: Rossetti left Kelmscott for good in **July 1874**, Morris moved to restructure in **August 1874**, and the Firm was dissolved and reconstituted as Morris & Co. under Morris alone on **31 March 1875**, with Marshall, Rossetti, and Brown each paid GBP 1000 while Burne-Jones and Webb stayed on unpaid. **[A: Wikipedia; BIFMO; edward_burne_jones.journey.json]**

## 8. Bognor to Birchington (1875-1882)

- 18 October 1875 into 1876: Aldwick Lodge near Bognor Regis, working on *Astarte Syriaca*, a new *Blessed Damozel*, and portraits of his mother and Christina. He visits Blake's Cottage at Felpham, where William Blake lived 1800-1803. **[A: Rossetti Archive, from the family letters]** *Coordinate correction:* the Felpham pin in circulation falls in the English Channel; the cottage on Blakes Road is at 50.7887, -0.6524 (Historic England 1353792).
- 16 September 1881: *Ballads and Sonnets*, Ellis and White, printed at the Chiswick Press, completing *The House of Life*. **[A: Rossetti Archive]** *Claim corrected:* it was **not** edited with William Michael Rossetti. Rossetti issued it himself with F. S. Ellis. W. M. Rossetti's editorial role is posthumous: *The Collected Works* (1886) and the *Works* (1911).
- 11 December 1881: mild stroke, dimmed sight, left-side paralysis. **[A: Wikipedia]** 4 February 1882: travels to a bungalow at Birchington-on-Sea lent by the architect John P. Seddon; its precise footprint is not confirmed **[gap]**. **[A: Birchington Heritage Society]**
- Dies of Bright's disease. **Date contradiction, not resolved:** the Victorian Web chronology gives **9 April 1882**; Wikipedia gives **10 April** and calls it Easter Sunday. Easter Sunday in 1882 fell on 9 April, and jane_morris.journey.json already carries 9 April. The journey file uses 9 April and records the dispute.
- Buried at All Saints, Birchington, under a Celtic cross designed by Ford Madox Brown; Historic England 1094682. His mother, Christina, William Michael, Watts-Dunton, and Hall Caine follow the coffin. **[A]** His documented 1876 wish for cremation was disregarded **[A]**; the explanation that he wanted a clifftop site overlooking the sea sits oddly beside that wish and is weakly sourced **[R]**.
- Two windows inside All Saints, both executed by Frederic Shields for Rossetti's mother: the left adapted from his *The Passover in the Holy Family*, the right showing Christ leading the blind man **out of** Bethsaida, not "healing the blind man outside the gates" as the wording in circulation has it. **[A: Visit Thanet]**

## 10. Gaps, stated as gaps

The street number of Sass's Academy is unconfirmed in every source reached. Brown's Fitzrovia studio address is approximate; Hunt's Cleveland Street pin is inherited from his own journey. Dr Hake's Roehampton house is placed at the district centroid, no street address having been recovered. The footprint of the Birchington bungalow is unconfirmed. The atlas carries no stop for Gabriele Rossetti's grave at Highgate West, though the interlock lens flags it; this journey adds Highgate stops for the burial and the exhumation but not for the father's grave.

## 11. Quotations

**Verified and used.** Twelve quotations appear in the journey file, each with its source in `quote_source`: five verse texts from Representative Poetry Online and Wikiquote, three lines confirmed verbatim in the Wikisource text of Mackail ch. 4, the drawing caption inherited from jane_morris.journey.json, the Burne-Jones testimony inherited from his own journey, the letter of 15 October 1869, and the brainwork line below.

**Corrected in wording and source:** the brainwork line. It is **not** in Hall Caine's *Recollections* (1882); a full-text search of Gutenberg #25574 finds no "brainwork" or "brain-work". It is in Hall Caine, *My Story* (Heinemann, 1908), ch. 2.4: "Conception, my boy, FUNDAMENTAL BRAINWORK, that is what makes the difference in all art." Note "the difference in all art", not the usual "all the difference in art".

**Not used, all [R]:** "one of the finest little fellows alive ..." (only a search-engine summary of the Rossetti-Allingham correspondence was reachable); "If any man has any poetry in him, he should paint ..." (Mackail flags it as paraphrase); "I am an image, Chiaro, of thine own soul within thee." (the text of "Hand and Soul" was not retrieved); "In all illumination and work of that kind, he is quite unrivalled ..." (confirmed in Mackail, attribution to Allingham unverified).

## 12. Afterlife claims corrected

The pairing of the 2009 BBC Two drama *Desperate Romantics* with Tate Britain's *Pre-Raphaelites: Victorian Avant-Garde* as a single 2009 wave collapses on inspection: the Tate show ran **12 September 2012 to 13 January 2013**, then toured to Washington, the Pushkin, and Tokyo through spring 2014. The rediscovery is a 2009-2013 arc. **[A]**

The Red Lion Square plaque does **not** commemorate the founding of the Brotherhood. Its LCC text of 1911 marks Rossetti's tenancy of 1851 and the Morris and Burne-Jones tenancy of 1856-1859. The Brotherhood was founded at 83 Gower Street.
