# Donna Haraway (b. 1944): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-31 for `donna_haraway.journey.json`. The subject is living: no death, no tomb, no closed record.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, first-person recollection, or inference. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. **HLAL** = *How Like a Leaf: An Interview with Thyrza Nichols Goodeve* (Routledge, 2000), which is Haraway talking about herself. Nearly every intimate fact below (the dolls, the convent, the divorce without lawyers, the Sonoma land, the deaths in the household) rests on it alone, and everything from it is tagged **[R]** on principle, not from doubt.

---

## 1. Denver and the Catholic formation, 1944 to c. 1962

**Birth.** Donna Jeanne Haraway, 1944, Denver **[A, UCSC finding aid, *Donna Jeanne Haraway Papers*, ark:/13030/c8b283b2]**. The day, 6 September, is from the ISFDB entry **[A as bibliography, [R] as vital record]**; no certificate reached.

**Formation.** Taught by nuns; the eucharist shapes her later insistence on the linkage of figure and matter **[A as to the claim's presence in Wikipedia, citing HLAL and Muriel Lederman's *Isis* review; [R] as to the causal story, which is hers]**. High school at **St. Mary's Academy, Cherry Hills Village** **[A, Wikipedia, citing HLAL 8-9]**, a separate municipality from Denver. The Mass-for-dolls, the confessions in a closet, and the considered convent vocation are HLAL only **[R]**.

**Mother's death.** Dorothy Haraway dies of a heart attack when her daughter is sixteen **[A as to fact and age, Wikipedia citing HLAL 6-7; the *Simians* dedication carries her dates]**. **The year is not attested:** born 6 September 1944, she turned sixteen in September 1960 and seventeen in September 1961. The journey file dates the stop 1961 to order the segment and says so.

## 2. Colorado College and Paris, c. 1962 to 1967

**Scholarship and degree.** Full-tuition **Boettcher Foundation** scholarship **[A, Wikipedia citing HLAL 12, 175]**; B.A. in **zoology** with minors in **philosophy and English**, 1966 **[A, Wikipedia]**, the most load-bearing fact in the life. **The matriculation year is an inference:** no source reached gives one, and 1962 is back-calculated from the degree **[R]**. Colorado College awards her an honorary DSci at Opening Convocation, 1986 **[R, recipient list cited in the pool, not fetchable here]**.

**Paris on a Fulbright.** Wikipedia: "After college, Haraway moved to Paris and studied evolutionary philosophy and theology at the Fondation Teilhard de Chardin on a Fulbright scholarship" **[A]**, with **no year**. The **UCSC finding aid independently adds a second institution**, "Fulbright Fellow at Universite de Paris and Fondation Teilhard de Chardin" **[A]**. 1966-67 is inferred from the degree **[R]**. That she first met the Vietnam and Indochina war through French politics shortly after Algerian independence is her own recollection, relayed by the dossier **[R]**.

**Pin note.** Both Paris stops use the canonical Paris coordinate; no source fixes the Fondation's premises. The Collège de France pin is **not** inherited from `norbert_wiener.journey.json`, since Wiener's 1951 Fulbright year in Paris is his stop.

## 3. Yale, 1967 to 1972

**Entry** to the biology doctoral programme, 1967 **[R, Wikipedia; the pool itself tags this [R], no registrar record reached]**. **Woods Hole:** summer course at the **Marine Biological Laboratory** on **tunicate regeneration**, 1968 **[R, UCSC finding aid; HLAL]**. **Doctorate** in biology, 1972: *The Search for Organizing Relations: An Organismic Paradigm in Twentieth-Century Developmental Biology*, adviser **G. Evelyn Hutchinson** **[A, UCSC finding aid; Yale record]**. **Marriage** to **Jaye Miller**, 1970 **[R, HLAL]**; no civil record, and the New Haven placing follows the Yale milieu.

**The cybernetics thread.** The strongest connective claim in the interlock material is Haraway's own line, in an interview with Nicholas Gane, that Hutchinson's circle at Yale was deeply interested in cybernetics, herself included. **[R]**: neither the pool nor this session fetched the Gane interview (academia.edu/4724092), so the journey file uses the claim without quotation marks.

## 4. Hawai'i, 1970 or 1971 to 1974

**Contradiction, unresolved.** Wikipedia (citing the European Graduate School): **1971-1974**. Encyclopedia.com: **1970-1974, assistant professor of general science, University of Hawaii at Honolulu**. The journey file pins 1971 and states the disagreement. The **General Science** teaching is corroborated by Encyclopedia.com independently **[A]**; the move followed Jaye Miller's appointment **[A, Wikipedia]**.

**Separation.** They separate c. 1973, later divorcing in Hawai'i without lawyers **[R, HLAL]**. Miller was gay; the household reorganized rather than dissolved, and Miller and his partner Bob Filomeno stayed inside her kin network **[R]**.

## 5. Johns Hopkins and Sonoma County, 1974 to 1980

**Appointment.** Johns Hopkins, assistant professor in the **History of Science**, 1974 **[A, Wikipedia]**, held to the 1980 Santa Cruz move. **First book:** *Crystals, Fabrics, and Fields*, Yale University Press, 1976, a revision of the dissertation **[A, Wikipedia]**; the journey file pins it at Baltimore, where she lived and taught, not New Haven, where the press sat.

**Rusten Hogness.** Partnership from **1975** **[A, Wikipedia infobox]**. That Hogness first audited one of her courses is HLAL only **[R]**.

**The Sonoma land.** Haraway, Hogness, Miller, and **Nick Paulina** jointly buy roughly thirty acres outside **Healdsburg** with a collapsed house on it, which they rebuild, c. 1977 **[R, HLAL; terms and year in no public record reached, no deed consulted]**. The pin is the town, not the parcel.

## 6. Santa Cruz and the manifesto, 1980 to 1991

**The chair.** UC Santa Cruz, **History of Consciousness**, 1980, in a post UCSC's own material calls probably the first tenured professorship in feminist theory in the United States **[A, library.ucsc.edu/reg-hist/haraway; Wikipedia]**. That superlative is the university's claim about itself.

**The manifesto.** "A Manifesto for Cyborgs," *Socialist Review* no. 80 (1985), pp. 65-108, commissioned by the journal's West Coast collective after Reagan's election **[A]**. **No source dates the drafting**; the 1983 commission stop is an ordering device.

**Quotations verified this session** at archive.org, item `anarchy_Cyborg_Manifesto_Harroway`, file `Cyborg_Manifesto_Harroway_djvu.txt`, all **[A]**: the cyborg definition; the closing "I would rather be a cyborg than a goddess"; "Blasphemy has always seemed to require taking things very seriously"; the "monstrous and illegitimate" sentence, whose tail the pool had flagged as possibly reconstructed and which is now retrieved whole; the irony sentence, of which the pool had only the first clause; and the "informatics of domination" sentence. Exact texts are carried in the journey file's quote fields.

**The Foucault sentence.** "Michael Foucault's biopolitics is a flaccid premonition of cyborg politics, a very open field." **[A**, with a spelling note: the transcription reads "Michael"; the philosopher is Michel Foucault.**]** This is the *documented* Haraway-to-Foucault link, converting what the interlock lens offered as inference into an attested one-directional citation.

**Situated Knowledges.** *Feminist Studies* 14, no. 3 (1988), pp. 575-599 **[A, JSTOR record stable/3178066]**; full text paywalled. **The pool's [R] on the "splitting, not being" quotation is upgraded to [A]:** verified against the reprint in *Simians, Cyborgs, and Women* (Routledge, 1991), ch. 9, p. 193, archive.org item `harawaydonnajsimianscyborgsandwomenthereinventionofnature`, running head "Situated Knowledges 193".

**The collection.** *Simians, Cyborgs, and Women*, Routledge, 1991 **[A]**, reprinting the 1985 essay as "A Cyborg Manifesto" and carrying the dedication to Dorothy.

## 7. Primates, witnesses, prizes, 1986 to 2000

**Deaths in the household.** **Bob Filomeno** dies of AIDS, 1986 **[R]**; **Jaye Miller** of AIDS-related illness, 1991 **[R]**. Both HLAL. **No source gives a place for either death.** The journey file uses one stop pinned to the shared Sonoma household and says plainly that the location is undocumented. This is the weakest pin in the file.

**Books and prizes.** *Primate Visions*, Routledge, 1989 **[A]**, awarded the **Robert K. Merton Award** by the ASA's Science, Knowledge and Technology section, 1992 **[A, asanet.org]**. *Modest_Witness@Second_Millennium.FemaleMan(c)\_Meets\_OncoMouse(TM)*, Routledge, 1997, with images by **Lynn Randolph** **[A]**, awarded the **Ludwik Fleck Prize**, 4S, 1999 **[A, 4sonline.org]**. **J. D. Bernal Prize**, 4S, lifetime contribution, September 2000 **[A, 4sonline.org]**. **No source says where any of the three was conferred**; the journey file consolidates them into one stop at her own campus and declares the pin as such.

**Reception, unadjudicated.** Reviewers of *Primate Visions* split between praising the archival reach and charging partisanship and relativism **[R, composite of secondary commentary reported in the pool; individual reviews not pulled]**.

## 8. Companion species, 2003 to 2007

**The second manifesto.** *The Companion Species Manifesto*, Prickly Paradigm Press, 2003 **[A]**. Four quotations verified against archive.org item `thecompanionspeciesmanifestodogspeopleandsignificantothernessdonnaharaway`, all **[A]** and carried in the journey file: "We are, constitutively, companion species. We make each other up, in the flesh." / "Dogs are not surrogates for theory..." / "There cannot be just one companion species..." / "Run fast; bite hard!" Cayenne Pepper is named in the earthlysurvival.org material **[A]**; breed and agility training come from the manifesto's own text. *The Haraway Reader*, Routledge, 2004 **[A]**.

**When Species Meet, and a real date contradiction.** The Minnesota Press catalogue gives **26 November 2007** for the Posthumanities paperback, ISBN 9780816650460 **[A]**. The book is nonetheless widely catalogued and cited as **2008**, the Vatican included: *Laudate Deum*, note 41, cites "D. J. HARAWAY, *When Species Meet*, Minneapolis, 2008, pp. 205-249" **[A, vatican.va]**. **Both dates are real.**

## 9. The Chthulucene, 2015 to 2018

**The film.** Fabrizio Terranova, *Donna Haraway: Story Telling for Earthly Survival*, 90 minutes, shot in her California home with Cayenne, mixing green-screen projection, archival material, and fabulation; premiered 2016 at the **Kunstenfestivaldesarts, Brussels** **[A, earthlysurvival.org]**. UK premiere at **Tate Modern**, 5-6 April 2017 **[A, tate.org.uk]**: first night a live conversation among Terranova, Haraway, and Filipa Ramos, second night a video introduction by Haraway. **Rule note:** the journey file gives Haraway **one** stop, the filming at her own home; Brussels and London are cited in that stop's sources rather than staged as scenes, since the Tate listing is ambiguous about whether she appeared in person or by video.

**Books.** *Staying with the Trouble: Making Kin in the Chthulucene*, Duke, 2016 **[A]**. *Manifestly Haraway*, Minnesota, 2016, binding both manifestos with a conversation with **Cary Wolfe** **[A]**. *Making Kin Not Population*, coedited with **Adele E. Clarke**, Prickly Paradigm, 2018 **[A]**.

**The Chthulucene dispute, live and unresolved.** Haraway's disavowal, *Staying with the Trouble* p. 173, was verified verbatim this session from Sophie Lewis, "'Cthulhu plays no role for me,'" *Viewpoint*, 8 May 2017, and is quoted in full in the journey file: Lovecraft's Cthulhu "plays no role for me," she writes, although it did for Gustave Hormiga, who named the spider. Lewis's counter is that the disavowal is strained, since *Pimoa cthulhu* was itself named after Lovecraft's creature **[R, an interpretive dispute, not adjudicated]**.

## 10. Late honours, the archive, and the present, 2017 to 2026

**Wilbur Cross Medal, 2017.** Yale GSAS, "Yale Graduate School honors four alumni with Wilbur Cross Medals," 24 October 2017 (live URL now dead, archived 11 October 2022) **[A]**.

**Laudate Deum, October 2023.** Pope Francis cites *When Species Meet* for "contact zone" **[A, vatican.va]**; Haraway commented in a *National Catholic Reporter* interview **[A as reported in the pool, not fetched]**. **The journey file does not stage her in Rome:** the stop sits at her own campus pin with the Vatican text as a source, per the rule that a later reader's engagement belongs as a source on the author's own stop for the work.

**Golden Lion, 2025.** La Biennale di Venezia announces the Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement on 8 April 2025 and presents it at **Ca' Giustinian** on 10 May, curator **Carlo Ratti** **[A, labiennale.org]**. The retrieved citation calls her one of the most influential voices in contemporary thought. **Her presence is unattested:** no source says whether she attended, and the stop declares the pin as the venue, not a Haraway location.

**Erasmus Prize, 2025.** Praemium Erasmianum names her the 2025 laureate under the theme "The pursuit of what binds us," 150,000 euros, presentation set for autumn 2025 **[A, erasmusprijs.org]**. **The laureate page names no venue**, and no source confirms a ceremony date or her attendance. A portrait film by **Emily Cohen Ibañez** was commissioned with the prize **[A]**. In her acceptance speech she recounts tripping while picking lemons at home, falling, striking her head, and months of imbalance and tinnitus **[R; no date or place given]**.

**The archive, 2025.** *Donna Jeanne Haraway Papers*, UA 42: **1966-2026**, 27.6 linear feet, **52 boxes**, **70.2 GB / 161,959 digital files**; six gifts (2005, 2014, 2018, 2021, 2023, 2024); processed by **Maureen Carey** in 2011 and comprehensively by **Annika Berry** with **Alix Norton** in 2025 **[A, oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8b283b2/, every figure checked]**. Special Collections is inside **McHenry Library**, 36.9957, -122.0588.

**El País, 24 July 2026.** She calls herself pro-child and non-natalist rather than anti-natalist, and discusses artificial intelligence by way of a new Lynn Randolph painting joining a cyborg figure to Anthropic's Claude **[R, not fetchable; single-sourced through the dossier]**. Used as the file's last stop with that status declared.

**Spanish reception.** *Manifiesto para ciborgs* (Cátedra, 1991); *Ciencia, cyborgs y mujeres* (Cátedra, first edition standardly 1995, only evidenced edition catalogued 2000); *Testigo_Modesto@Segundo_Milenio* (UOC, 2004); *Manifiesto para cyborgs* (Puente Aéreo, **Buenos Aires**, 2014, es.wikipedia only); two Sans Soleil titles, 2015 and 2016.
