# Johann Heinrich Wilhelm Tischbein (1751-1829): research report

*2026-08-02, for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `johann_tischbein`. Queue line: "German painter of Goethe in the Roman Campagna."*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named in place · **[R]** reconstruction, inference, tradition, or a representative pin standing in for an unrecorded location. Contradictions are printed as contradictions and left open. Gaps are stated as gaps.

One warning governs the whole file. This subject is unusually exposed to **fabricated precision**: the life is documented in outline and thin in detail, so single years get attached to spans, cities to undated stops, and the one famous picture pulls every loose fact toward itself. The first research pass produced at least fourteen invented or displaced dates, each corrected below and marked, so that no later pass reintroduces it.

---

## 1. Haina, Kassel, Hamburg (1751-1773)

**Birth [A].** 15 February 1751 at Haina (Kloster), Hesse, in the village around the former Cistercian abbey; son of the carpenter Johann Conrad Tischbein, who worked the abbey grounds (en. and de.wikipedia). Pin 51.0125, 8.9636.

**Kassel [A].** From 1765, aged fourteen, under his uncle **Johann Heinrich Tischbein the Elder**, court painter to the Landgrave of Hesse. Year attested; month and day are not, and the journey defaults to 01-01.

**Hamburg [A as to persons, gap as to dates].** Then under a second uncle, **Johann Jacob Tischbein**, and afterwards with his cousin **Johann Dietrich Lilly**, described by de.wikipedia as "in Hamburg als Kunsthändler, Kopist und Restaurator tätig," who "Tischbein an die Historienmalerei heranführte." Two corrections: **1770 for the Hamburg stay is fabricated** (neither edition dates it; honest bracket **c. 1766-1771**), and the first pass's account of him learning burgher portraiture there is backwards. Lilly was a dealer, copyist and restorer, not a painter-teacher, and the training under Johann Jacob was in **landscape**, which de.wikipedia says he left because "ihn das Gebiet der reinen Landschaftsmalerei jedoch nicht interessierte."

**Holland [A that it happened, [R] as to route, CONTRADICTED as to date].** de.wikipedia: Holland **1771**, Bremen on the return in **1773** ("kehrte nach einem kurzen Aufenthalt in Bremen 1773 nach Haina zurück"). en.wikipedia: Bremen **1771**, Holland **1772-1773**. The editions invert the leg. **Not resolved.** The journey follows de.wikipedia and carries the conflict in `date_confidence`. No Dutch itinerary survives; the Amsterdam pin is **[R]**, representative only. His elder brother made the same circuit, which may be one root of the confusion.

**Return [A as to fact, [R] as to city].** de.wikipedia has him back at **Haina** in 1773, not Kassel. en.wikipedia gives the commissions as a span with no city: "Between 1773 and 1775 he completed many portrait commissions with his brother." Kassel is inferred from where the brother lived and tagged **[R]**. Persistent slip corrected: the "Younger" (1742-1808, Inspector of the Kassel gallery from 1775) was the **elder** brother, "der ältere Bruder von Johann Heinrich Wilhelm."

## 2. Berlin, first Rome, Zurich (1777-1782)

**Berlin [A].** 1777, at the Berlin court, mediated by **Landgräfin Philippine**. 1778, admitted to the lodge **Zur Eintracht**.

**First Roman period [A, with an [R] pin].** 1779, on a Kassel academy stipend; the manner shifts from Rococo toward Classicism. The **Piazza di Spagna** pin (41.9057, 12.4823) is **[R]**: no address is given anywhere, and the quarter is cited only as the standard lodging district of the northern artists.

**Zurich [A, one claim struck].** 1781, out of Rome for money; into the circle of **Lavater** and **Bodmer**; first contact by letter with **Goethe**. **Struck:** the first pass had him "collaborating on Lavater's *Physiognomische Fragmente*," which is **chronologically impossible and not claimed by de.wikipedia**. The *Fragmente* appeared 1775-1778, three years before he reached Zurich; de.wikipedia says only that Lavater changed his manner and turned him toward physiognomic theory.

## 3. Second Rome, and Goethe (1783-1787)

**The Gotha stipend [A].** 1783, 100 ducats a year from **Duke Ernst II of Saxe-Gotha-Altenburg**, procured on Goethe's recommendation.

**Goethe's departure [A, place CORRECTED].** He did not slip out of Weimar in September 1786. He had gone openly to the spa at **Karlsbad** in July and left Karlsbad on **3 September 1786** (de.wikipedia, "Italienische Reise"): pin 50.2306, 12.8720, not Weimar 50.9795, 11.3235. The journey omits the stop, since Tischbein is not there, but the error is logged.

**The lodging [A, date CORRECTED].** Goethe reaches Rome 29 October 1786 and moves into **Via del Corso 18 on 30 October**, the day after arriving (de.wikipedia, "Casa di Goethe"). Not November. Flatmates: **Tischbein, Friedrich Bury, Johann Georg Schütz, Johann Heinrich Lips, Heinrich Meyer**. Goethe stays to February 1787, returns after Naples and Sicily, remains through spring 1788.

**Five Rome pins circulate for one address:** 41.9038/12.4809 and 41.9020/12.4789 (first pass, both near Piazza Colonna, about 700 m wrong); 41.9090/12.4772 (Casa di Goethe, right); 41.9106/12.4762 (afterlife lens, museum); and **41.903, 12.483, the atlas's canonical Rome pin** (`goethe_full.journey.json:518-519`). The journey resolves this deliberately: **stops where Tischbein's path crosses the corpus's Goethe path inherit 41.903, 12.483 byte-identical**, since a shared coordinate is what makes the crossing legible; stops about the house as a building use the corrected pin. A convention, not a claim about topography, stated so no later pass "fixes" it back.

**Portrait start date: CONTRADICTION, resolvable.** en.wikipedia: December **1787**. de.wikipedia quotes Tischbein's letter to Lavater of **9 December 1786**: "Ich habe sein Porträt angefangen und werde es in Lebensgröße machen, wie er auf denen Ruinen sitzet" (*Goethe und Lavater, Briefe und Tagebücher*, Weimar 1901, p. 364). Goethe's diary of 29 December 1786 and 17 February 1787 both describe it in progress. The English date is an error, and the atlas takes 9 December 1786 as [A] while recording the error rather than ignoring it.

**The composition, from the sitter [A, verbatim].** Entry of 29 December 1786: he is to be drawn "of the size of life, enveloped in a white mantle, and sitting on a fallen obelisk, viewing the ruins of the Campagna di Roma." Everything later read into the pose is set out plainly by the man being posed, before the picture exists.

## 4. The road south, and the parting (1787)

**Departure [A].** 22 February 1787: "Am 22. Februar 1787 reisten die beiden Männer gemeinsam nach Neapel" (de.wikipedia). Road stops **Velletri** and **Fondi** are attested in "Italienische Reise"; only Velletri is carried into the journey, for stop economy. **Naples [A, approximate day]:** about 25 February 1787, inheriting the canonical **pin 40.839, 14.253** from `goethe_full.journey.json:535-544`.

**Vesuvius [A as to Goethe's report, [R] as to Tischbein's conduct].** Early March 1787. Goethe's line about the mountain being "not content with being hateful" is verbatim; Tischbein's holding back from the rim is tradition in secondary literature.

**Pompeii, Paestum, Caserta [R].** de.wikipedia lists Goethe's spring excursions as "Pompeji, Caserta, Capua, Herkulaneum und Paestum." **Tischbein's presence at any of them is unattested.** He is the constant companion, so the inference is reasonable, but it stays [R]. The first pass had Caserta as an undated 1787-1799 inference from the Bourbon appointment; in fact a **March 1787** visit is documented, by Goethe, with Tischbein unproven. Only Pompeii is pinned; the other two are recorded here and left off the map.

**Hamilton introduction: DOWNGRADED to [R] and dropped from the map.** The first pass had Tischbein introducing Goethe to Sir William Hamilton at the Palazzo Sessa, and called him "already an engraver of Hamilton's vase collection in earlier years." Both halves fail: en.wikipedia dates the vase engravings to **the 1790s**, and de.wikipedia's "Italienische Reise" credits Goethe's entry into elegant Neapolitan society to **Gaetano Filangieri**, mentioning the Hamilton dinners without naming Tischbein.

**29 March 1787 [A, CORRECTED].** Goethe sails for Sicily with **Christoph Heinrich Kniep**. Tischbein neither goes nor stays: "von wo aus Goethe am 29. März nach Sizilien aufbrach, während Tischbein nach Rom zurückkehrte." He returns to Rome.

**CONTRADICTION, unresolved.** it.wikipedia's Tischbein article says he "later went to Sicily." de. and en.wikipedia place him in Rome and then Naples throughout. The atlas follows the two against the one and prints the dissent.

**February versus July 1787: NOT a contradiction.** The first pass flagged a clash between the February travel date and en.wikipedia's July relocation. de.wikipedia reconciles them: February the joint trip, late March the return to Rome, **early July 1787 the permanent removal**. The [R] tag is dropped; it is [A].

**The canvas [A, CORRECTED].** He did not carry the picture south. He "ließ sich das noch unvollendete großformatige Gemälde 1788 aus Rom nach Neapel nachsenden": shipped after him in **1788**, and never finished.

**The Hector [A, date CORRECTED, residual tension flagged].** Not 1786 but **August 1787**. Goethe, *Italienische Reise*, report of August 1787: David's *Horatier* had tipped the balance toward the French, and "Tischbein wurde dadurch veranlaßt, seinen Hektor, der den Paris in Gegenwart der Helena auffordert, lebensgroß anzufangen." **Residual tension, not resolved:** the trigger is the French Academy exhibition in Rome at the end of August, by which date he had moved to Naples in early July. Either he was back in Rome that month, or Goethe reports a resolution formed at a distance. No source settles it.

**The cooling [A, with a better mechanism].** Not "incompatible temperaments." de.wikipedia gives a documented cause: **no picture for three years** to Duke Ernst II, the patron Goethe had procured, and Goethe's complaint to Herder of **2 March 1789**, calling him "Hasenfuß, ... faul, unzuverlässig." Physical separation from 29 March 1787; rupture in feeling from 1789.

## 5. Director in Naples (1789-1799)

**Directorship [A, date resolved].** de.wikipedia: "Vom Herbst 1789 bis 1799 ... war Tischbein Direktor der ... Accademia di Belle Arti." en.wikipedia gives 1789; it.wikipedia gives 1787-1788. Resolves to **autumn 1789**; the Italian date is the outlier.

**False-precision pin removed.** The first pass tagged the directorship to the academy's **Via Costantinopoli** building, 40.8518/14.2529. That building dates from **1864**. The journey pins the city and says so.

**Vases and Homer [A, "1791" struck].** en.wikipedia dates the vase etchings and the Homer illustrations to **the 1790s** in Naples, says the vase engravings were "probably intended for a supplementary fifth volume of Sir William Hamilton's *Collection of Engravings from Ancient Vases*," and names the Homer plates as *Homer nach Antiken gezeichnet*. **1791 is unsupported** and was invented in the first pass.

**Geographical correction.** The first pass placed *Homer nach Antiken* in **Hamburg, 1801-1808**; en.wikipedia puts it in **Naples in the 1790s**. Hamburg is where he lived afterwards, not where the plates were made.

**1799 [A].** French invasion, Parthenopean Republic, end of the post. He leaves, and leaves the unfinished canvas behind in the city.

## 6. Itinerant years, and Hamburg (1799-1808)

**Kassel, Hanover, Göttingen [A that they happened, gaps as to dates].** en.wikipedia lists all three after Naples, **undated**. The first pass's **Hanover 1801** is unsupported and probably displaced, since de.wikipedia has him in Hamburg from 1801. Honest placing: **c. 1799-1800**, before Göttingen, where he founds a drawing academy **for women** from 1799. The journey pins Hanover at 1799 with the hedge in `date_confidence`; Kassel, the thinnest of the three, is recorded here and left off the map.

**The refused school [A as to fact, date INVENTED and struck].** de.wikipedia gives **no year**, only that the Hamburg Senate refused to fund the planned Kunstschule and that Tischbein therefore accepted Peter I's offer in 1808. The first pass's **1805 is invented**; the refusal brackets only to **1801-1808**. Worth keeping, because it is where Tischbein touches the next generation: **Philipp Otto Runge** and **Friedrich Overbeck** approached him about the plan.

**Marriage: CONTRADICTION inside one source.** de.wikipedia's *Leben* section implies **1801**; its *Familie* section gives **1806** for the marriage to **Anna Martha Koetting**, five daughters and one son. **Not resolved.** The journey uses 1806 and flags it.

## 7. Eutin (1808-1829)

**The appointment [A, title CORRECTED, date resolved].** 1808, an offer from **Peter I of Oldenburg**, at that date **Prince Regent of the Duchy**, not Grand Duke; the grand-ducal title came later. it.wikipedia's 1809 is the outlier against de.wikipedia's plain 1808. Peter also **bought Tischbein's picture collection outright** as part of the deal.

**He did not live in the castle [CORRECTED].** Schloss Eutin was the duke's **summer** residence. Tischbein had his own house, the surviving **Gartenhaus at Stolbergstraße 8-10** (54.134, 10.617), now carrying his plaque. He taught **the duke's sons** and local society, not "the ducal children." The first pass's castle coordinates (54.1339, 10.6156) are also about 400 m southwest of the actual Schloss at **54.1372, 10.6197**.

**Autobiography [A, year CORRECTED].** *Aus meinem Leben*, "niedergeschrieben seit **1810**." Not 1811. **Unpublished in his lifetime.**

**Goethe again [A].** Correspondence renewed 1817; illustrated idyll sketches 1821.

**Stoves [A, MISTRANSLATION corrected].** de.wikipedia: numerous **Öfen** survive from the Niemann manufactory with tiles designed by Tischbein, and "**43 von ihnen** stehen im Eutiner Schloss." The antecedent of *ihnen* is *Öfen*: **43 stoves**, not 43 tiles. Cited to *Lübecker Nachrichten*, 18/19 April 2021, p. 16, and Wolfdieter Schiecke, *Wilhelm Tischbein und die Eutiner Öfen* (Eutin 2021).

**Death and burial [A].** Died Eutin **26 June 1829**, aged seventy-eight; buried **30 June 1829** per the parish register (Kirchenbuch Nr. 97/1829). Grave in the cemetery on the **Plöner Straße**.

**"Weimar des Nordens" [R, supporting cast corrected].** de.wikipedia attributes the epithet to Peter I's patronage and names **Klopstock** and **Franz Anton von Weber** alongside Tischbein. The first pass named **Johann Heinrich Voss**, an Eutin figure but not the name the passage uses, and cited the wrong article.

**Gap, stated.** No source documents any legend of a lost, desecrated or relocated grave, of the kind that attaches to other Grand Tour figures, and nothing was found on whether the original stone survives. Genuine absence, as far as this pass can tell, rather than suppressed tradition.

## 8. Afterlife of one picture

**Memoir printed [A].** *Aus meinem Leben*, C. A. Schwetschke & Sohn, Braunschweig, **1861**.

**1956 edition [A, significance claim struck].** de.wikipedia calls it a "Neuausgabe, hg. v. Kuno Mittelstädt, Henschelverlag, Berlin 1956": a **new edition**, not a critical edition, and no source frames it as a scholarly rediscovery. Not carried into the journey.

**The Städel [A].** Donated **1887** by **Adèle von Rothschild**. Provenance runs from the Naples studio through the **Heigelin family** and **Carl Mayer von Rothschild**; the picture reaches Frankfurt **58 years after Tischbein's death**. The first pass claimed he "carried it out of Naples"; he left it behind there in 1799. en.wikipedia links the donation to cultural nationalism in the new Empire seeking Goethe and Schiller as unifying icons: **that causal claim is the article's framing, not a documented motive of the donor, and is [R]**.

**Casa di Goethe [A].** The apartment opens as a museum **30 May 1997**, after an earlier memorial room (Freie Deutsche Hochstift, 1973-1982) closed for lack of funds. First floor acquired 1990, second 2009, expanded facility inaugurated 20 September 2012.

**Print Quarterly [A, author CORRECTED].** Fulton, **Lesley** (2023), "Johann Heinrich Wilhelm Tischbein's Album of Prints in the British Museum," *Print Quarterly* 40 (June 2023), 150-169. The first pass gave "M. Fulton." The article was **not consulted**, only the en.wikipedia citation, which is why the stop is not carried into the journey.

**Gap, stated [R].** Before this file, **no stop in any atlas journey gave Tischbein a biographical pin**. Every occurrence was the Goethe-Rome stop or a `suggested_refs` line inside someone else's. He existed in the atlas as an attribute of Goethe's journey.

## 10. Sources

**Reachable and used**

- en. and de.wikipedia.org, "Johann Heinrich Wilhelm Tischbein" (the German is fuller and resolves most conflicts)
- de.wikipedia.org, "Goethe in der Campagna" (Provenienz; quotes the Lavater letter of 9 December 1786); en.wikipedia.org, "Goethe in the Roman Campagna"
- de.wikipedia.org, "Italienische Reise"; en.wikipedia.org, "Italian Journey"; de.wikipedia.org, "Casa di Goethe" and "Eutin"
- it.wikipedia.org, same subject, used only as a dissenting witness: it is the outlier on Sicily, on the Accademia dates, and on the Eutin year
- **Goethe, *Letters from Switzerland and Travels in Italy*, trans. A. J. W. Morrison, Bohn's Standard Library (London: George Bell & Sons, 1881), Project Gutenberg eBook #53205.** All ten journey quotes come from this file, the only primary text read directly.

**Citation caveat.** The first pass credited the translation to "A. J. W. Morrison and Charles Nisbet." eBook #53205 names only **"THE REV. A. J. W. MORRISON, M.A."**; Nisbet is dropped from every quote source string. One pool quote had also been silently re-cut into a false full sentence, and the journey restores the whole line from under "*Rome, Feb.* 21, 1787": "That Tischbein, who possesses as glorious an eye for nature as for art, is to accompany me on this journey, is to me the subject of great congratulation." The Monte Cavallo line belongs to the entry headed "Rome, November 3, 1786," recalling the previous day's ceremony, not to an entry of 2 November.

**Named and not reached**

- **Kuno Mittelstädt (ed.), *Aus meinem Leben*, Berlin 1956**, and the Braunschweig 1861 first printing. **The autobiography, the single most important primary source for this life, was not read in any edition.** Every statement here that appears to come from Tischbein's own memory is relayed through Wikipedia's summary of it. This is the largest gap in the research.
- **Wolfdieter Schiecke, *Wilhelm Tischbein und die Eutiner Öfen* (Eutin 2021)** and *Lübecker Nachrichten* 18/19 April 2021: cited by de.wikipedia, not consulted; the 43-stoves fact rests on that relay.
- **Lesley Fulton, *Print Quarterly* 40 (2023), 150-169**: paywalled, not consulted.
- ***Goethe und Lavater, Briefe und Tagebücher* (Weimar 1901)**: the 9 December 1786 letter is quoted from de.wikipedia's citation of p. 364, not from the volume.
- **Casa di Goethe archives; Ostholstein-Museum Eutin holdings**: no online catalogue consulted.

**Structural gaps left open**

1. The Hamburg years (c. 1766-1771) are undated in every reachable source.
2. The Dutch itinerary is unrecorded; the Amsterdam pin is representative only.
3. Bremen 1771 or 1773: English and German editions contradict.
4. Sicily: the Italian edition contradicts the other two.
5. The marriage year is contradicted within a single Wikipedia article.
6. The August 1787 Hector sits awkwardly against the early-July removal to Naples.
7. The survival of the grave marker at Eutin is unknown.
8. No reachable source counts how many portraits he actually painted. This matters: the whole shape of his reputation is that one canvas out of hundreds became the picture of a nation's poet, and the denominator of that sentence is a guess.
