# Walt Disney (1901-1966): research report

*2026-08-27. For `walt_disney.journey.json`: 44 stops, 8 segments, register "national mythology: the canon is true".*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-carried claim not corroborated in this pass. Where sources disagree, both are printed and neither is preferred. Short names: **d23** = d23.com/about-walt-disney/ · **SHSMO** = historicmissourians.shsmo.org/walt-disney/ · **WDFM** = the Walt Disney Family Museum blog · **wiki:X** = the en.wikipedia.org article X. Full source list at the end.

## 1. Chicago, Marceline, Kansas City, France, 1901-1919

- Born **5 December 1901, Chicago [A: d23]**, at **1249 North Tripp Avenue** (renumbered 2156 North), **Hermosa [A: wiki:Walt Disney]**, fourth son of Elias and Flora Call Disney. Elias building the house himself, Flora's schoolteaching: **[R]**.
- Farm outside **Marceline, Missouri, 1906**; Park Elementary **[A: SHSMO]**. The nickel from a neighbour doctor for a drawing of his horse, the orchard, the Santa Fe line: Disney's own retellings, **[R]**. The Marceline-to-Main-Street link is **[A]** as a claim the Marceline article and town museum make, **[R]** as biography. **Gap:** no source reached gives the farm's acreage or the terms of its sale, and the file invents no figure.
- Kansas City from **1911**; paper route **at nine**; **Benton Grammar School**; Saturday classes at the **Kansas City Art Institute [A: SHSMO]**; classmate **Walter Pfeiffer** and the vaudeville houses **[A: wiki]**. That Elias paid his sons nothing: **[R]**. Chicago from **1917**: **McKinley High School**, evenings at the **Chicago Academy of Fine Arts [A: SHSMO]**, cartoons for *The Voice* **[A: wiki]**.
- **16 September 1918** accepted into the **American Red Cross Ambulance Corps**, birth year falsified to 1900, actual age sixteen **[A: WDFM, "Over There"]**. Ships **18 November**, arrives **4 December 1918**; drives in the post-armistice occupation, cartoons on the ambulance, caricatures sold to soldiers **[A: same]**. The influenza that kept him off the first sailing and the doctored-helmet sideline: **[R]**.
- **Contested pin.** The dossier places him at **Neufchâteau, Vosges**. Wikipedia confirms the service and the November arrival but **not the base**. The stop is **[R]** and its pin is declared as the Vosges country, not a documented billet.

## 2. Kansas City again, 1919-1923

- **1919** Pesmen-Rubin; meets **Ub Iwerks [A: WDFM, "How Walt Started"]**. The Iwerks-Disney partnership and the Christmas layoff: **[R]**.
- **1920** **Kansas City Film Ad Service**, first animation work, Iwerks follows **[A: same]**. **21 March 1921** **Newman Laugh-O-Grams**, $150 and further commissions, plus the **E. G. Lutz** textbook and the borrowed camera **[A: same]**.
- **22 May 1922** **Laugh-O-Gram Films** incorporated, McConahay Building, **1127 East 31st Street**; staff Iwerks, Harman, Ising, Freleng, Carman Maxwell; the broken **$11,100** distributor promise; **July 1923** bankruptcy **[A: wiki:Laugh-O-Gram Studio]**. Union Station washing, the can of beans, the Greek restaurant's credit: **[R]**.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The departure for Hollywood: **d23**, the company's own archive, says **August of 1923** with $40; **wiki** says **July 1923**. Both are printed in the stop's `date_confidence` and in the campa. The **$40** is confirmed by d23.
- **Dropped stop.** Admission to the **Order of DeMolay** Mother Chapter, March 1920, as its 107th member. It rests on a **Scottish Rite** page that **returned 403**, so it is dossier-carried **[R]**, and that page's statement that Disney was **not** a Freemason could not be verified verbatim. Restorable if the operator wants it.

## 3. Los Angeles, 1923-1928

- **1923** Walt and Roy pool **$290**, borrow **$500**, set a camera stand in their uncle's garage **[A: d23]**. The uncle is **Robert Disney**, **4406 Kingswell Avenue**: **[A]** as arrangement, **[R]** as address.
- **16 October 1923** the **Margaret J. Winkler** contract for the **Alice Comedies**, the date the company treats as its founding **[A: wiki]**. **1924** **Lillian Bounds** joins as inker/painter: **[R]**. **13 July 1925** marries her at her brother's house, **Lewiston, Idaho [A: d23; wiki]**; the honeymoon and the abscessed tooth **[R]**. **1926** **2725 Hyperion Avenue**, demolished 1940 **[A: wiki]**.
- **Oswald, corrected.** The dossier called **Poor Papa** the first cartoon. Per **wiki:Oswald the Lucky Rabbit** it was the first **produced** and was **rejected by Universal** for quality; **Trolley Troubles**, **5 September 1927**, was the first **released**.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** Per-short fee: **silentfilm.org** gives **$2,250**, the Oswald article **$500**. The campa says the sources cannot agree and names neither; both sit in `date_confidence`.
- **Correction to the New York terms.** The dossier had Mintz offering a reduced rate **contingent on Disney becoming an employee**. Nothing reached supports the employee clause; the sources describe a demanded **20% budget cut**. Clause **dropped**. The **February 1928** trip, Universal's ownership of the copyright, the refusal, and the loss of most of his staff: **[A: Oswald article; wiki]**.

## 4. The mouse, 1928-1932

- **The train.** Disney's own 1948 essay: the mouse "popped out of my mind onto a drawing pad" **[A: WDFM, "The Birth of a Mouse"]**. **Diane Disney Miller's counter-account**, that Iwerks made the drawing and Lillian the name, is in the **same source** and is **not adjudicated**; the campa carries both.
- **Plane Crazy, 15 May 1928** and **The Gallopin' Gaucho**, no distributor **[A: same]**. **Steamboat Willie, 18 November 1928**, **Colony Theatre**, later the Broadway Theatre on 53rd **[A: wiki:Steamboat Willie]**; the failed Los Angeles recording session, the bouncing visual cue, Carl Edouarde's orchestra: **[R]**.
- **Disney's own contradiction, preserved.** In 1933 he gave Mickey's birthday as **1 October 1928**, the date the first picture was started; archivist **Dave Smith** later fixed the debut at the **18 November** premiere. Both are in the same museum source; the stop prints both and chooses neither.
- **1929** the **$300** hotel-lobby licence for a children's writing tablet, the first merchandising deal, predating **Kay Kamen [A: WDFM, "Selling Mickey"]**. First **Silly Symphonies** 1929: **[R]**. **1931** collapse, ordered rest, **Havana** and the **Panama Canal**: **[R] throughout**, popular biography, no primary source reached, and both stops say so.
- **Flowers and Trees, 30 July 1932**, first commercially released three-strip Technicolor film, won at the Fifth Academy Awards **[A: wiki:Flowers and Trees]**. **Category corrected**: it was **Short Subjects (Cartoons)**; "Best Animated Short Subject" is the post-1971 name and anachronistic here.
- **Gaps.** Daughter **Diane Disney** (b. 18 Dec 1933) and **Flora Call Disney's** death by carbon monoxide poisoning (26 Nov 1938) are both **[R]**, uncorroborated. Neither got a stop: the first has no documented place beyond "Los Angeles", the second could not be verified and is too grave to stage on an unverified pin.

## 5. Snow White, the letter, Burbank, 1937-1940

- **21 December 1937** *Snow White* at the **Carthay Circle Theatre**, cost about **$1,499,000 [A: d23]**; the theatre at 6316 San Vicente **[A: wiki]**. "Disney's Folly" and the audience standing: **[R]**.
- **7 June 1938** rejection letter to **Mary V. Ford**: **[A] as a document** (Letters of Note). **Signed by staffer Mary Cleave on the company's behalf, not by Walt personally**, and the file says so in both campa and `quote_source`.
- **Place corrected.** The dossier filed the letter under **Burbank**, but Burbank was not completed until **1940 [A: d23]**, so the stop is pinned at **Hyperion**, where it was typed.
- **1940** Burbank complete, over 1,000 staff, *Pinocchio* and *Fantasia* **[A: d23]**.

## 6. The strike, 1941

Four dossier claims corrected against **wiki:Disney animators' strike**. **Babbitt's dismissal** was not the day of the walkout but **February 1941**, with **sixteen other Screen Cartoonists Guild members**, and that termination is the identified trigger. **Duration** was not "five weeks" but **29 May to 21 September 1941**, three months and twenty-six days; five weeks at most describes the picketing before federal intervention, and the campa says so outright. **"A federal mediator rules in the guild's favor on every issue"** is overstated and **dropped**: what the sources say is that the **NLRB** asked Disney to sign and he agreed, and the **U.S. Conciliation Service** brokered reinstatement of employees fired before the strike, pay equalization, a clearer salary structure, and a grievance procedure, which is exactly what the campa lists. **"President of the studio's employee guild"** is unsupported by anything checked and is dropped; the file calls Babbitt a leading organiser.

## 7. The Good Neighbour tour, 1941-1942

The tour of **Brazil, Argentina, Chile** is **[A] in outline** (d23 a-to-z "South America"; the strike article notes his absence cooled tensions and that the union threatened pickets in South American cities on his itinerary). The **Rio** and **Buenos Aires** legs' specific sites are not confirmed to landmark precision, and **Santiago** was drafted and cut, its content folded into the Rio campa as itinerary rather than staged at an unverified pin. **Date corrected**: the dossier's afterlife entry said **1942**, but the **trip** was **1941**, and *Saludos Amigos* premiered in **Rio, August 1942**, reaching **US release February 1943**. The file splits trip year from film year. Both South American stops carry months that are **ordering devices only** and say so.

## 8. HUAC, 1947

Friendly witness in **October 1947 [A: wiki:Hollywood blacklist; wiki:Hollywood Ten]**, naming **Herbert Sorrell** and others as Communists behind the 1941 strike **[R: dossier citing the govinfo.gov transcript, not refetched]**. **Gap**: the dossier's **24 October** could not be corroborated, neither article giving **per-witness dates**, so the stop keeps the R tag and its campa says plainly that no source reached fixes the day. The **Cannon (then Old) House Office Building** caucus room is congressional record; the exact room was not re-fetched. **Structural note**: in the dossier's chronology this entry sat between the 1952 and 1954 entries, out of order, and is restored to place.

## 9. The park, 1950-1964

**Carolwood Pacific Railroad**, named for **355 North Carolwood Drive [A: wiki]**; the flowerbed tunnel and the guests' waiver **[R]**. **WED Enterprises, 1952** and **ABC's *Disneyland*, 1954**: both **[R]**; the television stop is pinned at Burbank and declares it, the dossier recording only a broadcast with no place. **Disneyland opens 17 July 1955**, **$17 million**, live broadcast **[A: d23 for the cost; date corroborated by wiki]**; the opening-day chaos is **[R]** and says so. **1960 Squaw Valley** pageantry chairmanship: **[R]**, public record of the Games, not re-fetched. ***Mary Poppins*, 1964**: **[R]**; what **is** attested is the **pitch letter to P. L. Travers [A: WDFM]**, on which the stop is built. The **1964-1965 World's Fair** pavilions and the industrialising of Audio-Animatronics: **[A] in outline**, not re-fetched. **14 September 1964 Medal of Freedom**, noon, **East Room**, presented by **Johnson [A: presidency.ucsb.edu]**; the other recipients are not listed in the file, the roll not being itemised this pass.

## 10. Florida, EPCOT, death, 1965-1966

- **Correction to the announcement.** The dossier credited **Walt** with disclosing the land assembly. Per **wiki:Walt Disney World**, the **15 November 1965** Orlando announcement was made by **Roy O. Disney** with Governor **Haydon Burns**; **27,443 acres** had been assembled through **dummy corporations from April 1964**. The file names Roy and the Governor, puts Walt beside them, and does not give him the microphone.
- **EPCOT film, 27 October 1966**: **[R]** on the date, though the film is well documented as his last on-camera appearance. **Quote corrected**: the dossier truncated the line mid-sentence with no ellipsis, and the full sentence is restored on the stop. **Single-source caveat, in the file**: all three EPCOT lines rest on one Google Sites transcript, and no independent source reproducing them was found.
- **Death.** The **November 1966** removal of the cancerous left lung and the decades of heavy, often unfiltered smoking: **[R]**. **Admission 30 November**, **death 15 December 1966**, **St. Joseph Hospital, Burbank [A: wiki; d23]**. **Acute circulatory collapse** on the certificate: **[R]**. The **ceiling-tile** scene is tradition **[R]** and is labelled inside the campa as Roy's telling.
- **Cremation 17 December 1966**, ashes at **Forest Lawn, Glendale [A]**. The sub-claim that the marker bears Walt's name **alongside Lillian's** is **unverified** (the article lists him among those interred with no marker detail) and is **not in the file**.
- **Cryonics.** No signed instruction, payment, or preservation record has ever surfaced, and the cremation is documented **[A]**. Attribution of the rumour to **Bob Nelson's 1972 press claim** is **[R]** and unverifiable here, **LA Magazine and Snopes both being blocked**. Carried as a `suggested_ref`, not as fact.
- **Magic Kingdom opens 1 October 1971 [A]**. Roy's death "later the same year" is **[R]**, uncorroborated, and is **not** stated in the file.

## Afterlife material carried as `suggested_refs`, not as stops

A stop belongs to whoever was actually there. All verified **[A: wiki]** and all about Disney, but he was at none of them, so each is cited on his own relevant stop rather than staged as a scene: **Partners** (1993) and **Storytellers** (2012) on the park opening and the 1923 arrival; the **Walt Disney Family Museum**, founded by Diane Disney Miller, opened 1 Oct 2009, on Forest Lawn; the **Hometown Museum** on Marceline, with **no dedication date in any source reached, stated as a gap**; **Steamboat Willie** entering the US public domain 1 Jan 2024 on the Colony Theatre.

***Destino* is a real gap.** The 1945-46 collaboration with **Salvador Dalí**, completed posthumously in 2003, is a working relationship of Disney's own, unlike the statues, and would justify a stop at the Hyperion or Burbank pin. It is absent because the museum exhibition page was **not re-fetched** and no date or place is fixed in the pool. **Recommended for a second pass.**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Walt Disney is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Relations found in the research that are **not rooted in his own life** are kept here rather than silently deleted, with the direction named.

1. **Frank Lloyd Wright, Broadacre City applied to EPCOT. Direction: BACKWARD** (a 1932 scheme read onto a 1966 plan). Neither the Wright file nor any source reached documents contact or influence; the comparison is the research pass's own. **Not written into a campa.** It survives only as a `suggested_ref` on the EPCOT stop, as "the nearest American precedent for a totally designed community", a statement about planning history rather than influence. To make it explicit, Disney would need adding to the exceptions list, or a source found for his having read Wright.
2. **Hans Christian Andersen to the Disney adaptations. Direction: FORWARD.** *The Little Mermaid* (1989) postdates Walt by twenty-three years and belongs to the company, not the man. **Dropped entirely**, nothing written into either file, flagged only so the operator knows it was considered.
3. **Larry Ellison, Skydance-Paramount, 2025. Direction: FORWARD** by fifty-nine years, and to a rival studio. **Dropped.**
4. **Antoni Gaudí.** The Gaudí file carries a visitor's line calling the Sagrada Família "Disney whimsicality raised to the level of soaring nobility". That is the visitor's engagement with **Gaudí's** building, not Disney's with anything, and it belongs where it already sits. **Nothing written into Disney's file.**
5. **William Rowan Hamilton and "Catherine Disney".** **False lead, confirmed.** An unrelated Anglo-Irish family; the surname is the only connection.

**Declined for shared geography alone**: **John Cage**, **Fritz Lang**, **George Balanchine**, **Carmen Miranda**, **L. Frank Baum**, none with documented contact in the atlas files or the pool. Baum is the closest call, having modernized fairy tales for an American market and made the same Chicago-to-Hollywood move thirteen years earlier. Still declined: parallel, not relation.

## Pins

**Inherited byte-identical**: **Chicago 41.8781, -87.6298** (canonical, from `l_frank_baum`, `carmen_miranda`, `fritz_lang`) on the Red Cross enlistment; **Hollywood 34.098, -118.3267** on the 1923 arrival and Mary Poppins; **Flushing Meadows 40.7466, -73.8459** from `robert_moses.journey.json`, over the geography pass's 40.7429, -73.8448. The **birthplace** does **not** take the canonical Chicago pin: it is a specific address with its own geocode, and the city pin would put the birth house 5 km away. The canonical downtown Los Angeles pin is unused; no stop falls downtown.

**Corrected.** Fifteen pool pins were wrong and were re-geocoded against OSM. Each stop's `date_confidence` names its own correction and the error it fixes, so the values are not re-listed here. The worst offenders: **Benton Grammar School** and the **Kansas City Film Ad Company**, both over 2 km out; **Laugh-O-Gram Studio** and **Hyperion Avenue**, 1.4 km each; and the **Kingswell office**, whose pool pin fell south of Kingswell Avenue entirely.

**Forest Lawn is the worst of all.** The pool carried two different wrong pins for the same cemetery, 34.1381, -118.2851 in the geography lens and 34.1425, -118.2551 in the afterlife lens; the first is 3.9 km off and lands near Forest Lawn **Hollywood Hills**, a different cemetery. Both are replaced by **34.1245, -118.2455**. Separately, the corrected Burbank studio and hospital pins put them **directly across Buena Vista Street from each other**, which the uncorrected pins obscured and which the death stop now says.

**Declared, not sourced.** Three pins no source supplied, each declared in its own `date_confidence`: **Pesmen-Rubin** (generic downtown Kansas City), **Manhattan, February 1928** (generic midtown; Mintz's office address undocumented), and the **White House East Room** (38.8977, -77.0365). The Mickey-on-the-train material is pinned at **Hyperion**, not on the train: a train has no coordinates, and nobody may be staged where they were not.

**The Mojácar legend** has a stop, dated to the birth so it sits beside the claim it contests, with `date_confidence` stating flatly that it is town lore and that the Chicago birth is documented. Its content is corrected: **mojacar.es** does **not** say agents destroyed local records. It says no birth certificate exists for Walt in Chicago nor for José in Spain though records survive for his siblings, names the mother **Isabel Zamora**, adds a CIA-pressure claim, and records **Disney's own dismissal** of the story as a confusion with a studio employee named Zamora, which is kept.
