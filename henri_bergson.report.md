# Henri Bergson (1859-1941): research report

*2026-08-23. From the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife). Companion: `henri_bergson.journey.json`, 43 stops in 8 segments.*

**[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on one unverifiable relay. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

---

## 1. Origins and schooling (1859-1878)

- Born **18 October 1859**, rue Lamartine, Paris 9e, to **Michał (Michel) Bergson**, a Warsaw-descended Jewish composer and pianist, and **Katherine Levison**, an Englishwoman of a Yorkshire Jewish family. **[A]** Wikipedia, SEP, Collège de France. No house number is reachable; the pin is the street.
- **1863**, the family moves to **Geneva** for Michel's Conservatory post; Henri, four, begins school there. **[A]** Wikipedia's Henri and Michał articles. The archival account behind it is Louis M. Greenberg (1976), cited in the dossier and **not fetched**: a named, checkable scholar, but a relay.
- **1868**, enrolled at the **Lycée Fontanes**, later **Condorcet**, through 1878, while the rest of the family relocates to **London**. **[A]** Wikipedia, IEP. The scholarship secured with help from Geneva's chief rabbi **Joseph Wertheimer** is a dossier lead via Greenberg: **[R]**.
- **Concours général**: first prizes in rhetoric 1875, philosophy 1876, **mathematics 1877**, the last solution printed in the **Nouvelles annales de mathématiques** (1878, ser. 2 vol. 17, p. 268). **[A]** SEP plus the journal issue via numdam. The master's lament that he would become "a mere philosopher" is **[R]**, traceable to no document.
- **Naturalization**: Wikipedia gives no year ("Before he was nine..."); SEP's placing of it in 1878 is unsupported, and the journey does not date it.

## 2. ENS, the provinces, the doctorate (1878-1898)

- **1878**, enters the **ENS, 45 rue d'Ulm**, in the promotion of **Jean Jaurès**; influences claimed are **Lachelier**, **Ravaisson**, and the working-through and against of **Herbert Spencer**. **[A]** SEP, Collège de France.
- **CONTRADICTION, LEFT OPEN.** SEP: "accepted at École Normale in 1878 alongside Jean Jaurès and Émile Durkheim." Wikipedia's Durkheim article: he "entered the École normale supérieure in 1879, on his third attempt." Both cannot be right about a shared cohort. The journey takes the correction while preserving the overlap and states the conflict in date_confidence.
- **1881**, second in the **agrégation**, then the **lycée d'Angers**. **1883-1888**, **Clermont-Ferrand**: Lycée Blaise-Pascal plus university faculty, the school Lucretius, and in **1886** *De la simulation inconsciente dans l'état d'hypnotisme*. **[A]** SEP, Wikipedia.
- **CORRECTED FROM THE POOL.** The pool dated the Paris return to 1889. English Wikipedia: "After settling in Paris in 1888" he taught at the Collège Rollin; SEP dates the submission of both theses to 1888. So Paris and Rollin from **1888**, defense **1889** (*Essai sur les données immédiates de la conscience* with the Latin *Quid Aristoteles de loco senserit*), Lycée Henri-IV shortly after, "where he remained for eight years." **[A]**
- **Marriage to Louise Neuburger. CONTRADICTION, LEFT OPEN**: Wikipedia gives **1891**; IEP, following the civil-record-based French chronology, gives **7 January 1892**. The journey uses 1892-01-07 as a sortable value and states the split. **Marcel Proust**, her cousin, as witness: **[A]** Wikipedia.
- **Jeanne Adèle Bergson**, born **16 March 1893**, 16e, deaf, later a student of **Antoine Bourdelle**. **[A]** French Wikipedia, with the Musée Bourdelle directory giving 16/03/1893 independently. Some English summaries give **1896**; noted, not resolved. **1896**, *Matière et mémoire*, Alcan. **[A]** IEP.

**Publication stops.** The pool pinned four publications (1896, 1922, 1932, 1934) at one coordinate described as Félix Alcan's. It is not his: Alcan was at **108 boulevard Saint-Germain, ~48.8524, 2.3406**. The journey anchors each book where Bergson was, putting only *Durée et simultanéité* at that address.

## 3. The Collège de France and the boom (1898-1914)

Uncontested and jointly attested by Wikipedia, SEP, IEP and the Collège de France biography, so listed without further apparatus **[A]**: the 1898 ENS appointment (folded into the 1900 stop); entry to the **Collège de France** in 1900 in the chair of Greek and Latin philosophy, succeeding **Charles Lévêque** after losing the modern philosophy chair to **Gabriel Tarde**, with *Le Rire* the same year; the 1901 election to the **Académie des sciences morales et politiques**; *Introduction à la métaphysique*, 1903; the succession to Tarde, 1904; *L'Évolution créatrice* and the **élan vital**, 1907; and the boom of c. 1907-1914, with its press caricature of the audience as *bergsoniennes* and *snobinettes* (Emily Herring).

**Contradiction inside an existing atlas file, flagged for the operator.** `georges_sorel.journey.json` places Sorel's attendance at Bergson's lectures **at the Collège de France in 1893**. Bergson did not enter the Collège de France until **1900**; in 1893 he was a master at the Lycée Henri-IV. Sorel's own date_confidence concedes that "No source gives the years or the courses; the date orders the segment only." The attendance is attested; the year-plus-venue pairing is not. The Bergson journey names Sorel without asserting a year. **The operator may want to fix the date or the venue in Sorel's file.**

## 4. England, America, the Index (1911-1914)

- **1911**: Oxford, *The Perception of Change*, plus honorary DSc; the **Huxley Lecture** at Birmingham; four lectures at the **University of London**. **[A]** No months are recorded for Birmingham or London.
- **February 1913**, Columbia: the visit, the lectures, and the **bibliography introduced by John Dewey** are **[A]**. The **first traffic jam** claim is **[R]**, and the pool's sourcing was backwards: SEP does not flag the anecdote, English Wikipedia never mentions it, and **1914-1918-online asserts it flatly as fact** ("generated New York City's first traffic jam", note *New York City*, not "Broadway"). A reference work asserting a superlative **without evidence** is not one labelling it folklore. The [R] tag stays; the attribution to SEP is dropped.
- **May 1913**, presidency of the **Society for Psychical Research**. **[A]** Wikipedia. The address *Fantômes de vivants et recherche psychique* is **[R]**, via psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk.
- **1913-14**, **Gifford Lectures**, Edinburgh. The **"eleven lectures"** count and the **"May 1914"** date rest on the dossier alone: neither Gifford archive gives a number, and both date the series "1913-14". **[R]** on count and month. Both supply a detail the pool lacked, now in the journey: the text is lost because **Bergson's 1930s will instructed that all his papers be destroyed**.
- **1 June 1914**, works placed on the **Index Librorum Prohibitorum**. **[A]** SEP, with the dossier citing Bruno Neveu. **Stop placement**: the decree issued in **Rome**, from a body he never appeared before, while he was in Paris, so it is **not a Vatican stop on his file** and sits at his own canonical Paris pin (48.8566, 2.3522).

## 5. The war (1914-1918)

- **Elected to the Académie française 12 February 1914**, seat of **Émile Ollivier**; **received 24 January 1918**, with **René Doumic delivering the response**, which the pool implied he did not. **[A]** Académie française's own records.
- **4 November 1914**, *La force qui s'use et celle qui ne s'use pas*, Bulletin des Armées; **December 1914**, the presidential address *La signification de la guerre*, English as *The Meaning of the War*. **[A]** Wikipedia. **Pool error corrected**: the geography lens carried a separate **"August 1914" ASMP address** whose citation in fact belongs to the November article. No reachable source supports an August address; that stop conflated two or three utterances under one date and is **dropped**.
- **1916**, Spanish mission, **Ateneo de Madrid**, received by **Alfonso XIII**. **[A]** on one documentary line only, a memoir cited in the dossier plus a Cambridge Core chapter. Thinner than any other [A] here.
- **January 1917**, sent to Washington as a **special envoy** to meet **Woodrow Wilson**. **Causal order corrected**: the pool had him travelling "as part of the French diplomatic mission led by René Viviani." 1914-1918-online says the government "sent the bi-lingual Bergson to the United States as a special envoy" in January 1917 to meet Wilson, i.e. **before** American entry, which was the point of the trip; Wikipedia says only that he "was there when the French Mission under René Viviani paid a visit in April and May 1917 **after America's entry**," and **says nothing about a Wilson meeting at all**. The meeting is cited to 1914-1918-online and **Somsen (2020)**.
- **May-September 1918**, a second, longer American stay. **[A]** The pool attached it to the Viviani network, a year out; dropped. Bergson's Viviani preface (1917) belongs to the earlier trip.

## 6. Relativity, Geneva, illness, the prize (1920-1930)

- **June 1920**, Cambridge honorary doctorate; the Collège de France relieves him of teaching in the same period. **[A]** Wikipedia. No venue is named; the journey's Senate House pin is **inferred, not sourced**.
- **6 April 1922**, Société française de philosophie session on relativity with **Einstein** present, alongside **Langevin, Hadamard, Cartan, Painlevé**. **[A]** as to the event; the exact date comes from the dossier's citation of the Société's records and Jimena Canales, **not independently re-verified**. **1922**, *Durée et simultanéité*: criticized by physicists, withdrawn from his standard corpus, though a 1924 letter still calls relativity "a very great advance." **[A]**
- **ICIC. Discrepancy resolved, not preserved.** The pool offered 1920-1925 against 1922-1925. Wikipedia's ICIC article: established **August 1922**, first session **1 August 1922**, Bergson **inaugural chair 1922-1925**, seat the **Palais Wilson 1922-1937**. The committee **did not exist in 1920**, so this is an error rather than a disagreement. **Einstein** and **Marie Curie** as members: **[A]**.
- **Mid-1920s onward**, severe **rheumatoid arthritis**, repeated failed treatments including clinics and thermal spas. **[A]** Kahn, PubMed 20729121. **Gap**: no clinic or spa is named anywhere reachable. The pool pinned this at Clermont-Ferrand, a leftover from an earlier stop; the journey anchors it at his house.
- **Nobel Prize in Literature**: designated laureate for **1927**, but the prize was **reserved and conferred in 1928** (IEP: "When he received the Nobel Prize in 1928"). Both years are right about different things and the journey says so. **Illness kept him from Stockholm**, so the stop sits at his own house, for the same reason the Index sits in Paris. **1930**, **Grand-Croix de la Légion d'honneur**, folded in. **[A]**

## 7. The last decade (1932-1941)

- **1932** *Les Deux Sources de la morale et de la religion*; **1934** *La Pensée et le mouvant*. **[A]**
- **7 February 1937**, the **testament**. **[A]** Wikipedia quotes the will directly; the JTA printed a contemporary translation on 26 September 1941, and the journey quotes it as printed, awkward English and all ("more and more closer"). Louise Bergson's own report of his words is independently in SEP, and is the quote on the Garches stop.
- **Autumn 1940**, refusal of the exemption for "exceptional scientific merits" under the statute of **18 October 1940**, and registration as a Jew under the **Statut des Juifs of 3 October 1940**. The refusal is **[A]** on a **contemporary JTA report of 9 December 1940**. The visit to the Passy commissariat and the wording of the form ("Academic. Philosopher. Nobel Prize winner. Jew.") are **[R]**: tradition, never produced from an original.
- **Death. CONTRADICTION, LEFT OPEN, POOL ATTRIBUTION CORRECTED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS.** **3 January 1941**: English Wikipedia (bronchitis), French Wikipedia, SEP, **and the Académie française's own biographical page**. **4 January 1941**: IEP (pneumonia), **Valéry's memorial address on the Académie's own site** ("congestion pulmonaire"), and 1914-1918-online. The pool said the Académie "commonly" gives 4 January; it does not uniformly, and **contradicts itself between its index page and Valéry's address, so it cannot be the authority for either day.** The journey writes the death as the night of 3-4 January and takes 1941-01-03 as a sortable value only.
- **The cold-queue story** (that he caught the fatal illness waiting in line to register) is **[R]**, and SEP calls it a rumor in those words. **The yellow-star claim is chronologically impossible** and is not in the journey: some modern accounts say Bergson refused exemption from wearing the star, but the German order making it compulsory in occupied France took effect in **1942**. **[A]** Wikipedia, *Yellow badge*.
- **Burial at Garches**, with a Catholic priest saying prayers at his own request and no conversion. **[A]**

## 9. Afterlife, carried as sources and refs, not as stops

Everything below postdates 1941 and gets **no stop of its own**, travelling instead in `sources` and `suggested_refs` on Bergson's own stops.

- **Panthéon inscription. Upgraded [R] to [A] and dated.** The pool called it a tradition. French Wikipedia's *Liste des personnes citées au Panthéon de Paris* lists Bergson under inscriptions in the nave and cites the instrument: **Décret du 21 février 1967**, Journal officiel no. 46, 23 February 1967. He was **inscribed, not interred**, which is why no Panthéon article lists him among those transferred.
- **Jeanne Bergson** preserved the study and left the archives to the **Bibliothèque littéraire Jacques Doucet**. **[A]** French Wikipedia.
- **Gilles Deleuze, *Le Bergsonisme* (1966)**, English 1988, reopened the reading. **[A]** In `suggested_refs` on the 1932 stop, **not named in any campa**: Deleuze's engagement is Deleuze's stop. Likewise **Bergsoniana** (2021), **Emily Herring, *Herald of a Restless World*** (2024), and **Valéry's memorial address**, a source on the death stop rather than a scene.
