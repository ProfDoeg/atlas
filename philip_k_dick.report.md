# Philip K. Dick (1928-1982): research report

*2026-08-15. Slug: `philip_k_dick`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named in line · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition not
carried by a fetched source · *gap* = stated absence, not filled by guesswork. The pool arrived
in five lenses with a correction pass already applied to about a quarter of the items; where
that pass overturned something, the original and the correction stand side by side below.

---

## 1. Chicago and the twin (1928-1929)

- Philip Kindred Dick born 16 December 1928 in Chicago to Dorothy Kindred and Joseph Edgar
  Dick, with a twin sister, Jane Charlotte Dick, born the same day, six weeks premature.
  **[A: Wikipedia, "Philip K. Dick"]** The hospital is not named anywhere reachable. *gap.*
- Jane dies 26 January 1929, about six weeks old, of malnutrition. **[A]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The geography lens first gave "malnutrition after a
  hospital feeding error"; the correction pass replaced this with Sutin's account, the twins
  underfed at home by an inexperienced mother who lacked milk, Jane additionally burned by a
  hot-water bottle, both rushed to hospital, Jane dying on the way. Wikipedia supports neither
  mechanism, only the word malnutrition, and the journey file declines to state one. **[A for
  the death; R for every mechanism on offer]**
- Jane is buried at Fort Morgan, Colorado, in the Kindred family plot, under a stone carved
  with both children's names and a blank left for Philip's death date. **[A: Wikipedia
  confirms the shared headstone and the plot, section K, block 1, lot 56]** That Fort Morgan
  was Dorothy Kindred's hometown appears only in the reconstruction layer; her birthplace is
  not stated in any reachable source **[R, probably droppable]**, and whether the six-week-old
  Philip was taken to the burial is nowhere stated. The journey file's 1929 Fort Morgan stop
  is written around the stone, not around an infant's attendance. *gap, left open.*

### The cemetery coordinate: a three-way disagreement

Three lenses pinned Riverside Cemetery three ways: 40.2456, -103.8064 in the geography lens for
both burials, about 1.8 km SSE of the site and in open ground; 40.2503, -103.7863 in the
afterlife lens, very nearly Fort Morgan town centre; and 40.2600, -103.8132 in OpenStreetMap.
**The journey file adopts the OSM value for both Fort Morgan stops [A]**, and records the others
as superseded, not averaged.

## 2. Westward, and the divorce (1929-1938)

- The family relocates to the San Francisco Bay Area after Joseph Edgar Dick is transferred by
  the federal government. **[R: Sutin, via the geography lens, dated 1929 there]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The chronology lens dates relocation and divorce to 1933,
  the transfer taking Joseph to Reno and Dorothy retaining custody **[A: Wikipedia]**, against
  the geography lens's 1929. Reconcilable in principle as an early move west and a later second
  transfer, but no reachable source states the reconciliation. The journey file carries one
  California stop dated 1933 and records the earlier date in `date_confidence`. Philip is not
  documented as having gone to Reno, so the stop stays in California and names Reno only as
  where the transfer pointed. **[A for transfer and custody; R for whereabouts]**
- 1936-1938: lives with Dorothy in Washington, D.C., where she takes government work, and
  attends grades two through four at John Eaton Elementary. **[A: Wikipedia, dates and grades
  both confirmed]** **Coordinate correction, adopted:** the lens gave 38.9297, -77.0687; the
  school stands at 3301 Lowell St NW, Cleveland Park, 38.9330, -77.0658 per OpenStreetMap.
- June 1938: mother and son return to California and settle in Berkeley. **[A: Wikipedia]**

## 3. Berkeley (1938-1958)

- Reads his first science fiction magazine, *Stirring Science Stories*. The chronology lens
  dates this 1940. **[A for the magazine; the date is impossible]** **Correction, adopted:**
  the magazine did not exist in 1940. First issue February 1941, edited by Donald A. Wollheim
  for Albing Publications, after an announced December 1940 date that was not met. **[A:
  Wikipedia]** The journey file dates the discovery 1941.
- 1947: graduates Berkeley High School in the same class as Ursula K. Le Guin, whom he did not
  know there. **[A: Wikipedia]** That Le Guin did not remember meeting him and that the two
  corresponded decades later is the geography lens's **[R]**.
- May 1948: marries Jeanette Marlin; the marriage lasts months. **[A]** The geography lens
  ends it that November. **[R]**
- 1948 onward: record retail on Telegraph Avenue, at University Radio and TV Supply and then
  Art Music. **[A for Art Music; R for University Radio and for the 1952 endpoint]**
- September 1949: enrolls at UC Berkeley (history, psychology, philosophy, zoology, German),
  withdraws by mid-November. **[A]**
- **Contradiction, not adjudicated.** The chronology lens gives two reasons, anxiety and the
  mandatory ROTC requirement; the geography lens gives only ROTC. Wikipedia carries the
  compound version, and so does the journey file.
- 14 June 1950: marries Kleo Apostolides. **[A]**
- 1951: sells his first story, "Roog," at twenty-two. **[A]** The geography lens names *The
  Magazine of Fantasy and Science Fiction* as buyer **[R]**; the story's publication date is in
  no lens. *gap.*
- 1955: FBI agents visit over Kleo's left-wing associations ("socialist ties" is the geography
  lens's own wording), and *Solar Lottery*, his debut, is published as an Ace Double. **[A]**
  No month for the book; the journey file dates it 1955-01-01 and says so.

## 4. Point Reyes Station (1959-1965)

- 1 April 1959: marries Anne Williams Rubinstein, moves into her West Marin house; 25 February
  1960, daughter Laura Archer Dick born. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Tag correction:** the geography
  lens filed the birth **[R]** on Sutin with a bare year; Wikipedia gives the full date.
- Through the 1960s he writes some twenty-one novels in relative poverty, and in January 1963
  the Scott Meredith Literary Agency returns his unsold mainstream novels. **[A]**
- 1963: wins the Hugo for *The Man in the High Castle*. **[A]** That it was completed at Point
  Reyes Station is the geography lens's **[R]**.
- **Contradiction, resolved only as far as sources allow.** The chronology lens dated to 1965
  one compound event: a violent confrontation with Anne, her committal, and a suicide attempt
  by driving off a road. Wikipedia separates them, with Dick telling neighbours in 1963 that
  his wife was trying to kill him and having Anne involuntarily committed for two weeks, the
  drive off the road coming after he filed for divorce in 1964 with a passenger in the car, and
  the divorce final in October 1965. The journey file follows the corrected sequence and notes
  the compound dating in `date_confidence`.
- **Claim withdrawn, not used.** The geography lens has Dick "call[ing] police to report Anne
  as a Communist threat." No source carries it; it reads as a blend of the 1963 committal with
  the 1955 FBI visit over Kleo.

## 5. San Rafael and Santa Venetia (1965-1972)

- October 1965: the divorce is final; he leaves Point Reyes for the San Rafael area. 6 July
  1966: marries Nancy Hackett. 15 March 1967: daughter Isolde Freya Dick, later the producer
  Isa Dick Hackett, born. **[A]**
- 1966-1971: the couple live at Santa Venetia, an unincorporated Marin community with a San
  Rafael postal address, through *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?* (Doubleday, 1968, later
  retitled after the film) and *Ubik*, with heavy amphetamine use. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- November 1971: the house is burgled, a fireproof safe or file cabinet blown open, personal
  papers taken. Culprits and motive never established; Dick offered competing explanations for
  the rest of his life. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Place correction, adopted.** Chronology and afterlife lenses both said San Rafael;
  Wikipedia says Santa Venetia. Journey file uses Santa Venetia, 38.0033, -122.5177.
- **Tag correction, adopted.** The afterlife lens filed the burglary **[R]**, "a widespread
  later legend ... not confirmed in the Wikipedia pages fetched." It is on the same Wikipedia
  page cited throughout the pool, so **[A]**. What remains disputed is who did it.

## 6. Vancouver (February-April 1972)

- February 1972: attends a Vancouver science fiction convention as Guest of Honour and gives
  the address later published as "The Android and the Human." **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Venue withdrawn.** The geography lens placed the talk at the University of British
  Columbia, pinning 49.2606, -123.246. Nothing supports it: Wikipedia's VCON article records
  VCON 2 on 19 February 1972 with Dick as Guest of Honour and names no venue, and UBC's SF
  society only co-sponsored the 1971 event. The journey file pins central Vancouver, 49.2827,
  -123.1207, and flags the venue as undocumented. *gap.*
- **Claim withdrawn.** The same item said "he does not return to California afterward." He was
  in Fullerton within weeks. The journey file says he does not return to Marin.
- 23 March 1972: attempts suicide by potassium bromide overdose. **[A]** Address unknown. *gap.*
- Late March or April 1972: enters X-Kalay, a residential drug rehabilitation program run by
  ex-addicts. **[A for the program; R for the confrontational-therapy characterization]**

## 7. Orange County and 2-3-74 (1972-1976)

- April 1972: relocates to Fullerton at the invitation of Cal State Fullerton professor Willis
  McNelly, entering a circle including K. W. Jeter, James Blaylock and Tim Powers. **[A]** That
  the Pollak Library holds the Philip K. Dick Science Fiction Collection is **[A]**; that
  McNelly arranged the deposit is **[R]**.
- 18 April 1973: marries Leslie "Tessa" Busby. 25 July 1973: son Christopher **Kenneth** Dick
  born. **[A]**
- **Name correction, adopted.** The geography lens called him "Christopher Kindred Dick."
  Kindred was Dorothy's maiden name and Philip's own middle name; the chronology lens is right.
- 20 February 1974: after an impacted wisdom tooth is extracted under sodium pentothal, a
  delivery woman bringing Darvon wears a gold fish pendant; light off it strikes Dick as a
  beam of pink. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Tag correction:** the geography lens filed the dental
  surgery **[R]** on Sutin, but Wikipedia states the pentothal extraction and the Darvon
  delivery directly.
- February and March 1974: geometric patterns, images of Jesus and ancient Rome, a parallel
  existence as "Thomas," a first-century Christian persecuted by Rome, and the names Zebra,
  God and VALIS for the perceived intelligence. **[A: stated on the Wikipedia article itself]**
  **Tag correction:** the chronology lens filed this **[R]** as resting on Dick's own accounts;
  it is **[A]** as a record of what he reported.
- The Koine Greek, and the assignment of "the Empire never ended" to those weeks, come from the
  geography lens. The refrain is verbatim *VALIS* text **[A]**; its 1974 assignment is **[R]**.
- 1974: the infant Christopher's undetected right inguinal hernia, diagnosed by a voice or
  vision and confirmed by doctors. **[A for the episode, Wikipedia, "VALIS"]** No month, no
  clinic named. *gap.*
- 1974: writes to the FBI alleging Fredric Jameson and others are foreign agents and that
  Stanisław Lem is a pseudonym for a Communist committee. **[A]** No month. *gap.*
- 1974: *Flow My Tears, the Policeman Said* published, first novel after a block. **[A]** The
  chronology lens attached the John W. Campbell Memorial Award to 1974; it was presented in
  1975, the award honouring the previous year's book. **Correction adopted.**
- 1974 onward: begins the Exegesis, some eight thousand handwritten pages and roughly a
  million words, continuing until his death. **[A]**
- 1976: writes *Radio Free Albemuth*, published posthumously 1985. **[R for the 1976
  composition, Sutin; A for the 1985 publication]**

### On Jung, Ezekiel, and Zoroaster

Three interlocks meet the relation test, Dick himself having engaged with the other party even
where that party could not possibly engage back. Each is named in exactly one campa. All three
links are **[R]**: the other side is **[A]** in its own journey file, but Dick's engagement
rests on his published citations, not on anything in those files.

- **Carl Gustav Jung** (slug `jung`). *Answer to Job* (Küsnacht, 1951-52) is, by Dick's own
  repeated account in the Exegesis, the largest single influence on his reading of 2-3-74, and
  Jung's autonomous inner teacher-figure Philemon, met in 1916, the closest documented
  precursor to the VALIS contact. The corpus holds three Jungs, so the campa names the Zurich
  psychiatrist in full.
- **Ezekiel** (slug `ezekiel`). Dick cites the Chebar-canal vision, wheel within a wheel with
  eyes in the rims, in the Exegesis and in *VALIS* as a prior instance of the same contact.
- **Zoroaster** (slug `zoroaster`). The dualist vocabulary of light against adversary that Dick
  borrows for the Black Iron Prison set against VALIS, Zebra, Firebright.

Twelve further interlocks are **not** named in any campa, each resting on shared geography,
period, or theme alone with no engagement by Dick: Theodor Reuss, René Guénon, Eadweard
Muybridge, Fritz Lang, Jorge Luis Borges, Maimonides, Michel Foucault, Robert Oppenheimer, Otto
Gross, W. B. Yeats, Jacob Boehme, Hermann Hesse. Lang is closest and was still declined:
*Metropolis* is a plausible ancestor of the replicant, but nothing in the pool records Dick
citing Lang, and the interlock note itself marks the line as inferred.

## 8. Santa Ana and the end (1976-1982)

- 1976: after the marriage to Tessa ends, he moves within Orange County to a Santa Ana
  apartment and spends his last years there. **[R: Sutin]** Address not confirmed. *gap.*
- 1977: publishes *A Scanner Darkly*, about amphetamine addiction and its cost to his circle,
  which he later called his best work. **[A]**
- 1981: publishes *VALIS* (Bantam), fictionalizing 2-3-74 through Horselover Fat, intended as
  the first volume of an unfinished trilogy. **[A: Wikipedia, "VALIS"]**
- **Correction, adopted.** The chronology lens called *The Transmigration of Timothy Archer*
  (1982) "the last novel published in his lifetime." It was the last novel he **wrote**,
  published shortly after his death; *VALIS* was the last published while he lived.
- January 1982: shown early special-effects test footage from *Blade Runner*, reportedly
  telling the production the Los Angeles cityscape matched his visions. **[R: widely reported;
  accounts differ on screening versus videotape]**
- 17 February 1982: telephones his therapist about failing eyesight, is told to go to
  hospital, does not go. **[A]** **Date correction:** the geography lens put the stroke on 17
  February and the discovery on 18 February, duplicating the stroke across two days. Wikipedia
  puts the stroke on 18 February, the day he was found unconscious on the floor.
- 25 February 1982: a second stroke in hospital, then brain death. 2 March 1982: taken off life
  support, dies aged 53. **[A]** The geography lens said he died "five days before he would
  have turned fifty-four"; born 16 December 1928, that birthday was nine and a half months off.
- **Coordinate correction, adopted.** Western Medical Center Santa Ana, 1001 N Tustin Ave, now
  Orange County Global Medical Center, is at 33.7540, -117.8330 per OpenStreetMap, not the
  lens value 33.7748, -117.8483, about 2.4 km off.
- March 1982: ashes buried at Riverside Cemetery, Fort Morgan, beside Jane, section K, block
  1, lot 56, under the stone carved in 1929. **[A]**
- *Blade Runner* opened 25 June 1982. "Four months after his death" (chronology, afterlife) and
  "three months" (geography) both circulate; the journey file gives the date, not a rounding.

### Posthumous items deliberately not staged as stops

These belong to other people's travel and are cited as sources on Dick's own stops rather than
dramatized inside his journey. All **[A]**: the Philip K. Dick Award, established
1983 by Thomas Disch with Hartwell, Williams and Brown, sponsored by the Philadelphia SF
Society and, since 2005, the Philip K. Dick Trust, first won by Rudy Rucker's *Software*;
Machover's *VALIS* opera at the Centre Georges Pompidou, 1987; *The Exegesis of Philip K.
Dick*, 2011, ed. Jackson and Lethem; the 2007 Library of America induction, the first for a
science fiction writer; Sutin's *Divine Invasions*, 1989; and Grant Morrison crediting *VALIS* for
*The Invisibles*. The correction pass adds that
Norwescon first hosted the award in 1984 at Norwescon VII, so "presented annually at Norwescon"
from 1983 is overstated.

## 9. Quotes

Ten verbatim quotes go into the journey file's `quote` fields, all **[A]** via Wikiquote unless
noted: five from *VALIS* (ellipses in the fight-the-Empire passage are internal to the source),
one from *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?*, two from *A Scanner Darkly* including the
Author's Note, the "invasion of my mind" line from the Charles Platt interview via Wikipedia,
and the fictionalizing-philosopher line, which the pool carried truncated after "novelist" and
sourced vaguely to letters and which Wikiquote places in *In Pursuit of VALIS*, ed. Sutin, 1991.

Not used: "The two basic topics which fascinate me are 'What is reality?' and 'What constitutes
the authentic human being?'" **[R]**, widely given as the opening of the 1978 essay but
unverifiable this pass; "This is a sign used by the early Christians," attributed to the
delivery woman **[R]**, well documented as an anecdote but not cross-checked against a
transcript, so paraphrased in campa; and Carrère on his own biographical method **[A]**.

## 10. Standing gaps

Unfilled, and left unfilled: the Chicago hospital of the twins' birth; Dorothy Kindred's
birthplace, and so whether Fort Morgan was her hometown; whether the infant Philip was taken to
Fort Morgan in January 1929; the publication date of "Roog," and confirmation of *F&SF* as its
buyer; the venue of the February 1972 Vancouver convention; the Vancouver apartment of the
overdose; the X-Kalay address; the Fullerton apartment and dental office; the clinic that
confirmed Christopher's hernia and the month within 1974; the month of the letter to the FBI;
the Santa Ana apartment; and whether the *Blade Runner* footage came by screening or tape.

One further pin disagreement, recorded rather than resolved: the Fullerton home is 33.8704,
-117.9242 in the geography lens and 33.8703, -117.9253 in the afterlife lens. The journey file
uses the geography value throughout.
