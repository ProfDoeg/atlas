# Paul Elliott Singer: research report

*2026-08-15. Subject: Paul Elliott Singer (b. 22 August 1944, Teaneck, New Jersey), founder of Elliott Associates L.P. / Elliott Management / Elliott Investment Management; principal of NML Capital Ltd. and Kensington International Ltd. Living subject: no funerary geography, no closed record.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference. Contradictions between sources are flagged where they occur and **not** adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Standing caveat.** Nearly everything below reaches this report through Wikipedia's articles on Paul Singer, Elliott Investment Management, Argentine debt restructuring, Aptiv and The Washington Free Beacon. Almost no originals were reachable from this host, and the pieces behind the quotations were verified only at the Wikipedia layer, meaning the quoted words were confirmed verbatim in Wikipedia's wikitext and no further. Court records in every jurisdiction named are cited from secondary description. This is a two-remove file and the journey's register should not disguise that.

## 2. The Peruvian paper, 1995 to 2000

The rehearsal, and the pool's sequence was wrong in three places.

**[A]** Elliott buys defaulted Peruvian commercial bank debt, roughly $20 million face, for $11.4 million.
**Contradiction, not resolved:** "Elliott Investment Management" dates the purchase **1995**, "Paul Singer (businessman)" dates it **1996**. One encyclopedia disagreeing with itself. The journey anchors 1995.

**[A] Instrument correction.** The pool labelled Lima "origin of the defaulted Brady bonds". This inverts the case: Elliott bought **unrestructured commercial bank loan paper guaranteed by Banco de la Nación** and pointedly refused the Brady exchange. Refusing Brady is the whole mechanism of the position. "Brady bonds" struck.

**[A]** Judgment of $58 million (principal plus past-due interest), 1998, S.D.N.Y. The pool had collapsed purchase, judgment and collection into one 1996 entry.

**[A] Doctrinal correction.** The pool framed the recovery as won "under the pari passu principle". Anachronistic. Peru was won by persuading the **Brussels Court of Appeal (26 September 2000)** to block settlement of Peru's payment through Euroclear, freezing its ability to pay anyone. The ratable-payment reading Griesa later adopted for Argentina is a separate and later instrument. Peru settles in full in **October 2000**.

**[R]** The Fujimori episode: that with Fujimori fleeing corruption charges, the firm confiscated his jet and offered him passage in exchange for the treasury paying the judgment. Tagged [R] in the pool, no year within the flight period, no primary citation in Wikipedia. Placed at 2000-11. **It may be legend.**

## 3. Kensington and the Congo, c. 2001 to 2008

**[A]** Kensington International, Elliott's Cayman vehicle, buys Republic of Congo debt with a face value of **$32.6 million** for under $20 million. The pool's "roughly $30 million" is loose.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** The pool's chronology dates the purchase **1998**; its own afterlife lens dates the campaign to **"the 2000s"**. The English High Court judgments run **2002 to 2003**, favouring the later reading. The journey anchors c. 2001 and records the disagreement. Neither entry cites a document.

**[A]** English High Court judgments against the Republic of Congo, London, 2003, then a worldwide chase of Congolese oil revenue. **[A]** Undisclosed settlement, 2008; reporting since puts it near $90 million, carried in the pool without citation and therefore soft.

**[A] as words, [R] as reach.** Brice Mackosso, Congolese transparency campaigner: *if it were not for funds like Elliott*, "we would not know any facts about the way our country's wealth is being taken away." The conditional is the entire point and the pool's submission had amputated it. Source: Lydia Polgreen, "Unlikely Ally Against Congo Republic Graft", *New York Times*, 10 December 2007, via Wikipedia. **NYT unreachable; Wikipedia layer only.**

## 4. Argentina: default and holdout, 2001 to 2010

**[A] Amount corrected.** Default **26 December 2001**. The pool said "roughly $100 billion" twice. Wikipedia, "Argentine debt restructuring" gives **US$93 billion of external debt, roughly $81.8 billion of it in bonds**. The round number is press shorthand and is not used.

**[A]** NML Capital Ltd., a Cayman affiliate, buys defaulted bonds on the secondary market: **$49 million paid for a claim of $832 million by 2014**. That ratio, not any doctrine, is what the word vulture is about.

**[A]** First exchange, 2005, at roughly 25 to 30 cents; about 76 percent accept, NML refuses. **[A]** Second exchange, 2010, lifts acceptance to roughly 93 percent; NML refuses again and sues on the New York-law pari passu clause. That figure cuts both ways in the mythology: it grounds the Argentine complaint that one foreign creditor forced a national default, and Singer's answer that he is one of the few who never consented.

## 5. The pari passu doctrine in court, 2011 to 2014

**[A] Date corrected.** *NML Capital Ltd v Republic of Argentina* [2011] UKSC 31 was handed down **6 July 2011**, not 29 June (supremecourt.uk, UKSC 2010/0040; Lords Phillips, Walker, Mance, Collins, Clarke). Pin corrected to Middlesex Guildhall, 51.5001, -0.1281.

**[A] Date corrected, real ambiguity flagged.** Griesa's holding that Argentina may not pay restructured bondholders without ratably paying the holdouts issued **23 February 2012**. The pool's "2012-11" belongs to the **amended injunction** operationalizing it after the Second Circuit's 26 October 2012 remand. Two different judicial acts, conflated by the pool.

**[A]** Second Circuit affirms 26 October 2012 and, after remand, **23 August 2013**. Certiorari denied **June 2014**. Argentina enters technical default on the restructured debt **30 July 2014**.

**[A] and troubling.** ProPublica later reported Singer provided free private-jet travel to Justice Samuel Alito for a 2008 Alaska fishing trip, and that Alito did not recuse when the Court considered *Republic of Argentina v. NML Capital*. **Gap:** nothing establishes whether Singer was at the lodge himself, only that he furnished the travel. The stop hedges accordingly.

**[A]** Attachment attempts against Argentine central-bank deposits at the New York Fed, 2011 to 2013, mostly defeated on immunity grounds. *Coordinate correction:* 33 Liberty Street is 40.7081, -74.0089, not the pool's -74.0021. **Stop dropped for economy.**

## 6. The ARA Libertad, October to December 2012

**[A]** On **2 October 2012**, on an order obtained through Ghana's courts at Accra, the Argentine navy's sail-training ship is detained at Tema as security for NML's judgment.

**[A] Two corrections.** The pool said "roughly 200 cadets and crew stranded for weeks". Reporting puts roughly **300 aboard**, the great majority repatriated within days, about **45** remaining. The pool also called her a frigate; the Navy styles her *fragata*, but she is a full-rigged Class A tall ship and "frigate" misleads in English.

**[A]** ITLOS Case No. 20, Order of **15 December 2012**: Ghana "should forthwith and unconditionally release the frigate ARA Libertad". This is the only verbatim primary quotation in the file that attaches to the Argentine campaign, and it comes from the tribunal that ruled against the seizure. Released **19 December 2012**, at Mar del Plata **9 January 2013**.

**[A] Internal coordinate contradiction.** ITLOS appeared three times in the pool with three pins: 53.5628/9.8610, 53.5461/9.9878 (about 9 km out) and the correct 53.5511/9.8508, which the journey uses.

**[R]** Argentine legend holds that Singer personally ordered or watched the detention. Nothing places him in Ghana; the attested record is a routine attachment executed by local counsel. The gap between folk narrative and process record is the interesting object and is flagged rather than smoothed.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** The Cristina Fernández de Kirchner buitres quotation is placed by the pool's quote lens at **Buenos Aires**, undated; the pool's own gap note ties the line to **9 January 2013**, the day the ship reached Mar del Plata. The journey uses the Buenos Aires canonical pin with that date and states the disagreement. Source: Spanish Wikipedia, "Fondos buitre"; **original speech unverified.**

## 7. The settlement, 2016

**[A]** Macri, elected November 2015 on a promise to end the standoff, replaces Fernández de Kirchner. **[A]** Agreement in principle **29 February 2016** in New York under Special Master **Daniel Pollack**, not in Buenos Aires; the pool had staged this at the Casa Rosada. The Manhattan pin (40.758, -73.9855) is the pool's own and the exact office is **not confirmed**.

**Contradiction, not resolved, and important.** Three figures circulate and are not the same quantity: **$4.653 billion** (settlement with the four lead holdouts), **$6.5 billion** (Argentina's broader offer of 5 February 2016), **roughly $2.4 billion** (Elliott/NML's share alone). The pool quoted the third as the settlement in one place and the second in another. All three are named in the stop.

**[A] Sequence corrected.** Griesa vacated the injunctions **19 February 2016** conditional on repeal of the Lock Law and Sovereign Payment Law and on payment; Congress repealed in **March 2016**; the Second Circuit affirmed the vacatur **13 April 2016**; the injunctions lifted **22 April 2016** on payment. The pool assigned the April action to Griesa's court.

## 9. Politics, philanthropy, iconography

**[A]** Regional finance chair and senior policy adviser to Giuliani's 2007-08 campaign, jet included. **[A]** Honorary US delegation to Israel's sixtieth anniversary, May 2008. **[A]** Over $4 million to Republican candidates in 2010; $1 million to Restore Our Future (Romney), 2011; $5 million to Trump in 2024 after funding the 2016 Stop Trump effort.

**[A] Attribution corrected.** Our Principles PAC was **not Marco Rubio's**: founded January 2016 by Katie Packer, a former Romney deputy campaign manager, affiliated with no campaign.

**[A] Date corrected.** The $1 million to Trump's inaugural committee was given after the election, December 2016 or January 2017, disclosed in the committee's April 2017 FEC filing.

**[A]** American Unity PAC, 2012, pushing Republican support for same-sex marriage; his son Andrew had married Corey Morris in Massachusetts in 2009. **Gap:** the town is unspecified in anything reachable, and Singer's own attendance, though obvious, is not attested. **The Massachusetts stop was dropped** and the wedding carried inside the American Unity campa.

**[A] but never to be quoted in fragments.** Wikipedia's full sentence: Singer stated that same-sex marriage promotes "family stability" and that in a time when "the institution of marriage in America has utterly collapsed", the fact that gay couples want to marry "is kind of a lovely thing and a cool thing and a wonderful thing". The pool submitted the two halves as two separate quotations, which would double-count one utterance and let the collapse clause stand alone, where it reads as a social-conservative lament and inverts his meaning. Citation: BusinessNewsDaily, 25 April 2012. **The live URL now serves a rewritten article; only the 16 December 2012 capture carries the text, and archive.org is unreachable. Wikipedia layer only.**

**[A] Founding corrected, causal chain deleted.** The Washington Free Beacon launched **7 February 2012** under Michael Goldfarb, Aaron Harison and Matthew Continetti. Singer is its principal backer, **not its founder**, and 2015 is when it retained Fusion GPS, not when it was created. That engagement ran October 2015 to May 2016 and ended when Trump took the nomination; Fusion GPS was separately retained by Perkins Coie for the Clinton campaign and DNC in April 2016. The Beacon states it "had no knowledge of or connection to the Steele dossier"; Singer says he learned of it when BuzzFeed published it in January 2017. **The pool's claim that the Beacon's research "is later expanded into what becomes the Steele Dossier" is deleted, not softened.** Sequential clients of one firm is not one project.

**[A]** Start-Up Nation Central, Tel Aviv, founded and funded from 2012, roughly $20 million. **[R] and dropped:** the pool's Aspen Institute stop was sourced to "Aspen Institute program materials", a placeholder; what the sources support is a house in Aspen. **[R]** Rochester honorary doctorate 2017 and the Harvard Singer Prize, unverified beyond Wikipedia. **[A]** Headquarters to West Palm Beach, 2020.

**[A]** Singer is alive as of 2026, so the afterlife here is reputation, not tomb. The nearest monument in Argentina is the ARA Libertad, still in service. The nearest in law is the pari passu holding, which pushed the IMF, the UN General Assembly and the bond markets toward collective-action clauses designed to make a future holdout campaign impossible. That last claim is tagged **[R]** in the pool and stays [R]: policy synthesis, not a dated document.
