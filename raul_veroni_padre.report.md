# Raúl Veroni (Raoul Veroni), the elder, 1913-1992

*Research report for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `raul_veroni_padre`. Compiled 2026-08-12 from the verified research pool.*

Milanese by birth, Argentine by every other measure. Engraver (*artista grabador*), lithographer, publisher, printer. Founder of **Ediciones Urania**, Buenos Aires, 1943; later trading as **Raúl Veroni Editor** and the **Estudio Gráfico de Raúl Veroni**. Died Buenos Aires 1992. Father, by every indication and no document, of the **ral_veroni** already in the atlas queue.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, single-witness listing, or tradition. Contradictions are flagged and left open.

**A structural warning about this whole file.** Roughly two-thirds of the spine below rests on **antiquarian bookseller listings (AbeBooks)**: commercial descriptions of objects a dealer has in hand, which makes them decent evidence that a book exists with a given collation and poor evidence for dates, authors' names, and attributions of individual plates. Several corrections here exist because a listing was wrong. No copy of any Veroni edition was examined. Where a listing is the only witness, the claim is tagged **[R]** even when it is probably true.

## 1. Milan, 1913: birth

**Born 8 August 1913, Milan, Italy. [A]** Library of Congress Name Authority File, record **no2023125659**, heading `Veroni, Raúl, 1913-1992`, carrying `mads:birthDate 1913-08-08` and `mads:birthPlace Milan (Italy)`, sourced in the record's own note to *Arte de Argentina*, consulted 13 November 2023. The same record assigns the occupations **Artists** and **Lithographers** and the descriptor **Argentines**.

**Correction carried.** The pool gave only "Italy," citing the very LoC record that specifies Milan to the day. The precise data was in the cited source and was being under-read. Milan is now a stop.

**Gap: family, schooling, training.** No parents' names, no school, no master, no academy, no Milanese workshop, no Italian work of any kind. The first thirty years are documented by one date and one city.

## 3. Buenos Aires, 1940-1943: the hired hand

**1940, La Virgen de la Asunción y su Oratorio, R. de Lafuente Machain, Buenos Aires, illustrated by Veroni. [R]** AbeBooks listing filed under Raúl Veroni. Single witness, no copy examined. The earliest traceable work of his life.

**1943, "italiano, artista grabador, de treinta años de edad." [A]** `edicionesurania.blogspot.com`, publisher's introduction. The closest thing to a self-description that survives, and it is a trade description rather than a biography: nationality, craft, age.

## 4. Ediciones Urania, founded 1943

**Founded 1943 in Buenos Aires; self-financed; debut publication Rafael Alberti, *Tres Recuerdos del Cielo*, 24 copies, 5 etchings by Veroni, on artisanal Fabriano Perusia paper. [A]** Source: the house blog's own history.

**Correction carried, and it is the most important one in the file.** The pool's claim said Veroni "set in movable type and printed" the book. The same blog says otherwise, in terms: *"compuesto por Alejandro Zampieri con tipos móviles de plomo, impreso por Emilio Colombo e ilustrado con 5 aguafuertes de Veroni"*, produced at **Don Francisco Colombo's** printing house under the care of the poet **Basilio Uribe**. Veroni **financed, commissioned, and etched**. He did not compose and he did not press. The atlas splits this into two stops so the correction is visible rather than buried.

The network established in this one book (**the Colombo press**, **Basilio Uribe**) recurs for twenty years: Colombo prints the 1944 Salomé and the 1950 *Flor de la Rosa* and shares the 1951 Leopardi imprint; Uribe translates that Leopardi and is the probable author of the 1959 *Los Días*.

**The name.** The blog does not explain it. Urania is the muse of astronomy. See §9.

## 5. The bibliophile editions, 1944-1965

Ordered by date; every entry names its evidential status.

| Year | Work | Veroni's role | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1944 | Wilde, *Salomé*, 2 vols, Francisco A. Colombo | **one etching only** | [A] |
| 1945 | Marechal, *El viaje de la primavera*, Emecé | one etching | [R] |
| 1947 | Milosz, *Miguel Mañara*, Emecé | illustrations | [R] |
| 1950 | Devoto, *Cancionero llamado Flor de la Rosa*, Colombo | four engraved plates | [R] |
| 1951 | Leopardi, *L'Antico Amor*, Colombo / Urania / Raul Veroni | six etchings, imprint | [A] |
| 1955 | Shelley, *A una alondra / To a Skylark*, La Cabellera | editor, engraving | [A] |
| 1965 | Carducci, *Poemas*, Raúl Veroni Editor | signed lithograph, imprint | [R] |

**1944 Salomé, correction carried.** The pool claimed Veroni made the **wood engravings** and gave the run as "328 copies." Both are wrong. OpenLibrary **OL58008884M** reads: *"scholarly version by Aníbal Bargas Nigoul, woodcuts by Constante Orlando Paladino, and an etching by Raúl Veroni."* The woodcuts are **Paladino's**; Veroni contributed **one aguafuerte**. The "328" is a sum somebody computed, not a stated run: *"trescientos ejemplares numerados"* on Polar paper in two fascicles **plus** 28 special copies on Croxley with Fabriano and Guarro covers, numbered I-XXVIII. Two states, not one run.

**1947 Milosz, correction carried.** The author is **O. V.** de Lubicz Milosz, i.e. **Oscar Vladislas** (OpenLibrary authority), not "O. W." The work is *Miguel Mañara* (1935). Neither the 1947 date nor Veroni's illustration could be confirmed beyond one bookseller listing, so the stop is **[R]**.

**1951 Leopardi, the fullest surviving description of a Veroni edition. [A]** 55 pp; **82 numbered copies in two paper states, 27 on Fabriano Magnani and 55 on Ingres**; six etchings including a vignette; frontispiece signed by him in pencil; glassine wrappers; cartonné box. Spanish translation by **Basilio Uribe**. The pool said only "on Fabriano paper," which flattens the object.

**1955 Shelley. [A]** Colección de Poesía "La Cabellera," bilingual, translated by **Juan Rodolfo Wilcock**, one engraving by Veroni, **63 copies on papel Ingres Écoles**; the described copy is *"Ejemplar nº 16 de 63 ejemplares."*

> **Anachronism removed.** The pool's 1955 claim included a binding by **Sol Rebora**. The blog gives **no binding year**, and Rebora is a living contemporary Argentine fine binder. Her *Estructura Francesa* goat-leather binding with reversible Japanese-paper *escartivanas* is a **later artisanal rebinding of a copy** of the 1955 edition. It is split out of the 1955 stop and placed in the posthumous segment, with the placeholder date declared in `date_confidence`. **No year for the rebinding is attested. [gap]**

**1965 Carducci. [R]** Imprint "Raúl Veroni Editor"; signed original lithograph; 125 copies, 2 on Rives and 123 on Ingres. Bookseller listing only.

## 6. The Emecé covers, 1954-1969

Emecé Editores is the constant of the second half of his working life: at least nine books between the 1945 Marechal etching and the 1969 Peltzer vignette. All of the following rest on **bookseller listings alone [R]**, except where noted.

- **1954** Molinari, *Días Donde la Tarde es un Pájaro*: cover vignette and a drawing.
- **1955** *La Niña de la Rosa*: illustrations. **Correction carried:** the author is **Fermín Estrella Gutiérrez** (1900-1990), Spanish-born, resident in Argentina, not "Estrella Fermín Gutiérrez." OpenLibrary records the title under the correct name; es.wikipedia gives the dates. **[A]** for author and year.
- **1955** Gustavo J. Franceschi, *La Democracia Cristiana*, Ediciones Criterio: cover design. Franceschi edited *Criterio*; 1955 is the year of the Church-Perón rupture, the Plaza de Mayo bombing and the Revolución Libertadora. **Nothing in any reachable source records a political opinion of Veroni's, and none is imputed here.**
- **1959** Pedro Miguel Obligado, *Los Altares*: illustrations.
- **1959** Basilio Uribe, *Los Días*: cover vignette. **[R]**, flagged as such in the pool.
- **1960** Betina Edelberg, *Imposturas*: cover illustration.
- **1964** León Benarós, *El Río de los Años*: cover vignette.
- **1964** Horacio Moine Macías, *Poemas entre dos Eras*: cover vignette. **Nothing whatever is recoverable about this poet [gap].**
- **1967** Clara Bottini, *Caza Mayor*: cover illustration; the book took the **Premio Emecé 1967**. The prize is the novel's, not the engraver's.
- **1968** Rodolfo E. Modern, *Levántate y Canta*: cover vignette. **[R]**, flagged in the pool.
- **1969** Federico Peltzer, *La mi Muerte*: cover vignette. Last Emecé item in the record.

**[R] researcher background, not from the verified pool:** Betina Edelberg co-wrote a study of Leopoldo Lugones with **Jorge Luis Borges** in 1955. This is a thread to Edelberg, **not** to Borges. It is used once in the journey, hedged, and marked in that stop's `sources`.

## 7. The Estudio Gráfico, 1967-1978

**Cuadernos de Arte, Mendoza I, 1967, 200 copies. [R, with correction]** Presented by the **Centro de Medios Audiovisuales de la República Argentina**, **printed at** the Estudio Gráfico de Raúl Veroni. Featuring Roberto Azzoni, Federico Céspedes, **Zdravko Ducmelic**, Dardo Retamoza, Juan Scalco. **Two corrections:** the pool dated this **1966** (the listing says 1967) and called Veroni its **publisher** (he printed it; the Centro presented it).

**Cuadernos de Arte, Córdoba I, 1967, 200 copies. [R]** Same studio, same run. **Mendoza II, 1969, 50 copies. [R]** A quarter of the first run, and **no source explains the reduction. [gap]**

**1971, *El Andén*, Pedro Miguel Obligado / Estudio Gráfico Raúl Veroni. [A for the imprint, and posthumous]** *"Con una reproducción de un grabado de Raúl Veroni."* **Correction carried:** Obligado **died in 1967** (es.wikipedia, 1892-1967). The volume is a **posthumous issue** and must not be narrated as a living collaboration. The pool's claim omitted the death.

**1971, Horacio Esteban Ratti, *La Luz que Más Me Vale*, Nexo. [R]**

**1976, Oscar R. C. Vogliano, *Hospital de Niños*, second edition, 136 pp, 16 x 23.5 cm, 200 copies, engravings by Veroni. [R, with correction]** The pool dated this **1969**; the AbeBooks record says **second edition 1976**. Seven years is too large to carry silently, so the later date stands and the discrepancy is logged.

**1976, Antonio de la Torre, *El Regalo, Cuentos y Narraciones*, Cooedit, San Juan, 128 pp, drawings by Veroni. [R, with correction]** The pool said the book was "published dedicated to Veroni." It was not. **One copy** is *"inscribed and signed by the author... al artista Raúl Veroni"*: a manuscript inscription on a single copy, not a printed dedication. The only stop in this journey outside Buenos Aires after 1940.

**1978, Antonio Requeni, *Hojas de Poesía No. 30*, edición del autor. [R]** The **last dated work in the reachable file**.

**Gap: 1978-1992.** Fourteen years. No exhibitions, no editions, no interviews, no obituary. Nothing.

## 8. The Urania backlist claim

The blog's exhibition notice reads: *"con poetas de la estatura de Enrique Banchs, Alfonsina Storni, Silvina Ocampo, Tulio Carella, Shelley, T. S. Eliot, Edgar Allan Poe, Leopardi, Safo entre muchos otros."*

**Correction carried.** The pool rendered this as Veroni "collaborating with poets including Enrique Banchs, Alfonsina Storni and Silvina Ocampo." That misreads the sentence: it lists **authors published**, and the same list contains **Shelley, Eliot, Poe, Leopardi and Sappho**. **Alfonsina Storni died at Mar del Plata in 1938**, five years before Urania existed, so any Storni edition is necessarily posthumous. **Tulio Carella** was dropped from the pool's claim and is restored.

Of the nine names, only **Banchs**, **Silvina Ocampo** and **Carella** were alive and in the city during Urania's run, and **no reachable source records Veroni in conversation with any of them. [gap]**

**No catalogue of the Ediciones Urania backlist exists in any reachable source. [gap]** The house's output is known only through scattered dealer listings and this one sentence.

## 9. Contradictions, flagged and left open

1. **"Milanese fine-press printer" versus "Buenos Aires founder of Urania."** `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` line 281 describes a "Raoul Veroni" as a **Milanese** fine-press printer and publisher born 1913; `QUEUE.md` line 308 has him founding Urania **in Buenos Aires**. The LoC record reconciles the two *by biography* (born Milan, died Buenos Aires) and the atlas narrates it that way. **But the census entry as written asserts a Milan printing-trade identity, and no Milanese press activity is attested anywhere.** Recorded, not adjudicated. If that census line came from a source really placing him at a press in Milan, the source has not been seen here.

2. **Urania, Buenos Aires, versus Urania, Milan.** The pool's afterlife section suspected the Argentine imprint might be a phantom generated by confusion with **Mondadori's Urania**, the Italian science-fiction line founded in **Milan in 1952 by Giorgio Monicelli**. **The suspicion is wrong and the inference is reversed here.** Ediciones Urania was founded in Buenos Aires in **1943**, nine years earlier. The Mondadori imprint remains a real **search hazard**: it dominates results for the name. The genuine Milan connection is **biographical, not editorial**: Veroni was born there. The journey keeps this as a Milan stop so the hazard is on the map.

3. **Hospital de Niños: 1969 versus 1976**, and **Cuadernos de Arte Mendoza I: 1966 versus 1967.** Pool dates versus bookseller dates. Both resolved in favour of the bookseller, who has the object, and both logged.

4. **The father-son relation itself is not documented. [R]** The 2012 documentary features a living **Raúl Veroni** who keeps the Casa-Taller. That he is the **son** rather than a grandson or a nephew rests on the shared name and the workshop continuity, and nothing else. The pool flagged this itself; the atlas carries the hedge into `date_confidence` on the relevant stop rather than asserting the kinship in prose.

## 10. Death and afterlife

**Died Buenos Aires, 1992. [A]** LoC no2023125659: `mads:deathPlace Buenos Aires (Argentina)`, deathDate 1992, from *Arte de Argentina*. **No month and no day are recorded anywhere. [gap]**

**Cemetery, grave, plot, monument: total gap.** No funerary record of any kind surfaced. The pool's afterlife entry declared the whole afterlife unreportable; that was **overstated** and is corrected. What is genuinely missing is the **grave and nothing else**. No cemetery name or burial coordinate is invented here.

**2012: the retrospective. [A]** *"EL LIBRO COMO ARTE, Raoul Veroni y las ediciones de bibliófilo,"* **Museo del Libro y de la Lengua, Av. Las Heras 2555, Buenos Aires**, 9 August to 15 December 2012; opening Thursday 9 August at 7 pm; hours Tuesday to Sunday, 2 to 7 pm. Shown: typographic compositions, xylographic blocks, colophons from the **Casa-Taller**.

**The address is the only geolocatable anchor in the entire afterlife**, and the pool's version of this claim omitted it. The journey's coordinates for this stop (-34.5845, -58.393) are **derived from the street address**, not taken from a gazetteer entry for the museum, and that derivation is stated in the stop's `date_confidence`.

**The documentary. [A]** Cast, corrected upward from the pool's partial list: papermaker **Alejandro Geiler**, printer **Patricio Gatti**, engraver **Lorena Pradal**, editors and printers **Leandro Jacob** and **Martín Artagaveytia**, and the son **Raúl Veroni**.
