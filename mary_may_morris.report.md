# Mary "May" Morris (1862-1938): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `mary_may_morris`. Companion: `mary_may_morris.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops, 11 quotes.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction or inference, reasoning given. Source conflicts are printed side by side and **not adjudicated**. Coordinates the pool supplied wrongly are corrected here with the reason; the corrected value is what the journey file carries.

**The problem with this subject.** Nearly every fact about May Morris is recorded in an article about somebody else: her father's biography, her mother's, the Firm's company history, two art schools' institutional histories. Her own working life survives as year-ranges and three city names. That asymmetry is the finding.

---

## 1. Red House and Queen Square (1862-1876)

**Birth. [A: Wikipedia, 'May Morris'; Wikidata Q449758 P569 = +1862-03-25]** Born 25 March 1862 at Red House, Bexleyheath, second daughter of William Morris and Jane Morris née Burden. Named Mary for the Feast of the Annunciation; May from infancy.

- *Coordinate corrected.* Chronology gave 51.4508, 0.1444, ~1.1 km southeast of the house. Red House is at **51.4556, 0.1303** (Wikipedia, 'Red House, Bexleyheath'), the atlas's existing pin in three files.
- *Strays, not used.* One pool summary gave only "March 1862"; an interlock entry gave "11 March 1862". Wikipedia, Wikidata and `jane_morris.journey.json` all give 25 March.

**Queen Square. [A: atlas pins in `jane_morris` and `elizabeth_bessie_burden`]** Red House given up autumn 1865; the family takes 26 Queen Square, above the Firm's workshops.

**Learning the needle. [A as to the fact, [R] as to the date]** The V&A feature states she learned embroidery as a child from Jane Morris and her aunt Elizabeth "Bessie" Burden, and **gives no date**. The file places it at 1868 and flags the year as a placement.

**Kelmscott Manor. [A: Wikipedia, 'Kelmscott Manor'; atlas pin June 1871]** Joint lease with Dante Gabriel Rossetti, June 1871. May is nine.

- *Coordinate corrected, three ways.* The pool gave **three different** values for one house: 51.6919, -1.6497 (chronology), 51.6825, -1.6539 (afterlife), 51.688, -1.6382 (geography). The last is correct and matches the atlas; the afterlife value is 1.2 km southwest of the house, the chronology value 900 m northwest in open fields.

**Italy 1876. [A as to journey and seizure: Wikipedia, 'William Morris'] [R as to months]** Jane Morris takes both daughters to Oneglia; Jenny suffers a severe epileptic seizure; William Morris crosses Europe to reach them; the family goes on to Venice, Padua and Verona. **No source gives a month for any leg.** Oneglia is carried at 1876-01 and Venice at 1876-04, placements inherited from `jane_morris.journey.json`, Oneglia at **43.8833, 8.0333** rather than either lens value (43.8836, 8.0208 / 43.8873, 8.0257).

**Gap.** No letter, diary or drawing by May from this journey has surfaced; everything known of her presence is in her father's biography. Verona carries no May-specific detail and is folded into the Venice campa rather than pinned.

## 2. Training, and the Upper Mall (1878-1884)

**National Art Training School. [A as to enrolment: V&A feature] [R as to month]** Enrolled 1878, South Kensington, forerunner of the Royal College of Art. Coordinate 51.4966, -0.1722; chronology gave 51.4988, -0.1749, with no source preferring either.

**Kelmscott House. [A: Wikipedia, 'Kelmscott House'; atlas pin 1878-10]** 26 Upper Mall, Hammersmith, from October 1878. Coordinate **51.4905, -0.235** from the atlas rather than the lens's 51.4905, -0.2354.

**Merton Abbey: a contradiction, rejected.** The pool summarises the V&A feature as May beginning formal work for the firm in **1880** at the "Merton Abbey / Oxford Street workshops". **Impossible as stated**: the dyeworks did not move from Queen Square to Merton until **June 1881** [A: Wikipedia, 'Morris & Co.'; atlas pin in `william_morris` and `elizabeth_bessie_burden`]. Further, **the 1880 start year is itself unattested**; the only documented milestone is the 1885 directorship. The file carries a Merton stop at the attested works date and says in `date_confidence` that the 1880 claim is unsupported and the Merton attribution anachronistic: neither used nor silently dropped.

**Gap.** How the embroidery trade divided between the Merton sheds and the Oxford Street showroom is undocumented in anything reachable; the geography lens says as much itself.

## 3. Director of embroidery, marriage, the book (1885-1895)

**Directorship. [A, twice]** "May became the director of the embroidery department in 1885, when she was in her early twenties" (Wikipedia, 'Morris & Co.'); "aged 23" ('May Morris'). No month recorded.

- *Coordinate corrected.* Chronology gave 51.5154, -0.142 (Oxford Circus, ~800 m east of the showroom); geography gave 51.5154, -0.1489 (~300 m east). **449 Oxford Street**, near the Marble Arch end, is approximately **51.5145, -0.1533**, used and flagged approximate.
- *Institutional interlock.* `elizabeth_bessie_burden.journey.json` records Bessie holding no institutional post after 1877 while embroidering for the Firm into 1901; the niece directs from 1885 the department the aunt worked in.

**Socialist League. [A as to the League and coach house] [R as to her attendance]** The Hammersmith branch met in the coach house behind Kelmscott House; Shaw was a regular, Sparling the secretary. **That May personally attended is not attested**; it is reconstructed from the household.

**Bloody Sunday, 13 November 1887. [A as to the event, from `william_morris.journey.json`] [R as to May]** Morris marched; Alfred Linnell died of injuries. **No source places May in Trafalgar Square.** The stop is kept because the atlas already pins the day and her household is the subject; the [R] is explicit in `date_confidence`.

**Shaw. [R throughout]** Three claims, all reaching this report through search summaries and not the underlying documents: an attachment forming c. 1885 (Sally Peters, JSTOR); a hand-painted Valentine of 14 February 1886, Shaw's diary noting "Got a handsome Valentine" (The Guardian, 2017); renewed feeling within the marriage c. 1893 (Peters). **The JSTOR article was not read; the Shaw diaries were not consulted.**

**Marriage. [A: Wikipedia, 'May Morris'; V&A]** Henry Halliday Sparling, Fulham Register Office, 14 June 1890. Coordinate **51.479, -0.1978** inherited from `jane_morris.journey.json` rather than the lens's 51.4795, -0.1958.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** `jane_morris.journey.json` says of this marriage "The marriage lasts under five years." Wikipedia gives the divorce as **1898**, eight years after the wedding. Both are carried into `date_confidence` on the marriage and divorce stops. They might both be true in different senses (cohabitation ending c. 1894, decree 1898), but **no retrieved source says so** and the reconciliation is not invented here.

**Decorative Needlework. [A: upgraded from the pool's [R]]** The pool flagged 1893 as [R] for want of direct confirmation. It is confirmed: Wikipedia, 'May Morris' states "Decorative Needlework (Joseph Hughes & Co., 1893)", OpenLibrary gives first publication 1893, and the full text was verified against Internet Archive `decorativeneedle00morriala`. **Retagged [A].**

**The South Kensington research claim. [R: split off]** The geography lens asserted she drew on the museum's collections while researching the book. **No retrieved source supports this.** It is kept as a separate stop explicitly tagged [R], so publication ([A]) and research method ([R]) are not conflated.

**Quotations.** Nine passages verified verbatim against the Internet Archive OCR. Two needed punctuation restored, both Ch. VII, c. p. 89: "Be definite before everything — let every form you put on paper be something" and "In the later work — and I am careful to speak of late Renaissance ... all its own — we have the convention". The pool had commas; **the em dashes are original**. House style bans the em dash in **authored** text only, so they stand.

## 4. Inheritance and the teaching circuit (1896-1908)

**William Morris dies. [A]** 3 October 1896 at Kelmscott House; buried at St George's, Kelmscott, 6 October, under a tomb by Philip Webb (Wikipedia, 'Kelmscott'; 'William Morris'). Dates and coordinates from `william_morris` and `jane_morris`.

- *Coordinates corrected.* Chronology gave Kelmscott House as 51.4881, -0.2286, ~550 m southeast, on the wrong side of the Upper Mall; afterlife gave the churchyard as 51.6839, -1.6512, ~1.1 km southwest. The atlas values (51.4905, -0.235 and 51.6924, -1.641) are used.

**Advisory role, and divorce. [A: Wikipedia, 'May Morris']** After 1896 she moves from directing the embroidery department to an advisory capacity. Divorced 1898, reverting to Morris; no month, and see the contradiction in section 3.

**Central School of Arts and Crafts. [A as to dates, corrected as to place]** Teaching from **1897**; head of the embroidery department **1899-1905**; Visitor until **1910** (Wikipedia, 'May Morris').

- *Place corrected.* Both lenses attached the **Southampton Row** address (51.5155, -0.1233 / 51.5177, -0.1198) to the 1897 start. **Anachronistic**: the school was founded by the LCC in 1896 in Morley Hall, rented from the Regent Street Polytechnic, and "in 1908 it moved to purpose-built premises in Southampton Row" (Wikipedia, 'Central School of Art and Design', giving 51.5189, -0.1207). The file splits it into **two stops**, 1897 at Regent Street (51.5175, -0.1425, flagged approximate) and 1908 at Southampton Row. **Gap:** no source gives Morley Hall's address, or says whether she taught in the new building.

**Birmingham, Leicester, Hammersmith. [A as to the cities, [R] as to all else]** Wikipedia, 'May Morris' names art schools in these three places and **gives no years, no syllabus, no terms**. The file places them at 1900, 1902, 1903, each flagged as a placement inside the 1897-1910 decade; the geography lens's "1899-1910" range is editorial inference. *Coordinate corrected:* Leicester was given as 52.6369, -1.1398, generic city centre; the school occupied the **Hawthorn Building, The Newarke**, approximately **52.6294, -1.1394**.

**Women's Guild of Arts. [A: Wikipedia, 'May Morris']** Founded 1907 with Mary Elizabeth Turner, assisted by Mary Annie Sloane, Ethel Fanny Everett, Mabel Esplin and Letty Graham, because the Art Workers' Guild would not admit women. No month; the coordinate (51.521, -0.1204) marks the Art Workers' Guild's Bloomsbury district and is **not** a documented Women's Guild address.

## 5. The American tour (1909-1910)

**Arrival. [A: Textile Society of America, "The Reluctant Reformer: May Morris' United States Lecture Tour of 1909-1910", digitalcommons.unl.edu]** A five-month tour beginning with an October 1909 arrival in New York. Day, ship and pier not named.

**Hull House. [A: Jane Addams Hull-House Museum blog, "The Hidden Legacy of May Morris"; corroborated by the UNL paper]** About a month based at 800 South Halsted Street, Chicago; precise dates not given. Coordinate 41.8716, -87.6497; chronology's 41.8724, -87.6501 differs by under 100 m and is immaterial.

**Glessner House. [A: Glessner House archive blog]** A lecture on "Design in Costume" to Frances Glessner's Monday Morning Reading Class at 1800 South Prairie Avenue, in a house papered with Morris & Co. goods including May's own "Arcadia". Exact date not recorded.

**Detroit. [R]** An Archives of American Art item records the Detroit Society of Arts and Crafts bringing her to lecture at the Detroit Museum of Art. **The citation could not be re-fetched; the lecture is unconfirmed.** The name is right for 1909-1910 (renamed Detroit Institute of Arts only in 1919). *Coordinate corrected:* the lens gave 42.3336, -83.0467, near Hart Plaza; the museum stood at **704 E. Jefferson Avenue** until 1927, approximately **42.3345, -83.0395**.

**Return. [R]** March 1910. **No source names the ship or either port.** The return is reconstructed arithmetically from the five-month duration and October 1909 start. The pin (50.0, -20.0) is a mid-ocean placeholder and says so in both campa and `date_confidence`.

## 6. The Collected Works, and Kelmscott (1910-1938)

**Editorship. [A: Wikipedia, 'May Morris']** The Collected Works of William Morris, 24 volumes with her introductions, Longmans, Green, 1910-1915. No months recorded.

**Kelmscott freehold. [A: Wikipedia, 'Kelmscott Manor'; 'Jane Morris']** "His wife Jane purchased the freehold in 1913", "a few months before her death ... to secure it for her daughters' future."

**Claim rejected. [R, and rejected]** The pool claimed May "took up residence at Kelmscott Manor following her mother's purchase" in 1913. **No source supports it.** Jane "did not return to the house after having purchased it" (Wikipedia, 'Jane Morris'), and 'Kelmscott Manor' gives only an undated succession. The single dated anchor for May living there is **1917**. Residence moved to 1917; the 1913 stop is confined to the purchase.

**Jane Morris dies. [A, with a caveat]** 26 January 1914 at 5 Brock Street, Bath; buried at Kelmscott. The pool flagged this as standard biography **not re-fetched in this session**; `jane_morris.journey.json` carries the same date, place and coordinate (51.3864, -2.3655), used rather than the pool's 51.3811, -2.359.

**Mary Lobb. [A: Wikipedia, 'May Morris']** From 1917 to 1938 she lives at Kelmscott Manor with Mary Lobb, a Women's Land Army volunteer. **The sources call the relationship a companionship and go no further.** Nothing is asserted here beyond the dates, the shared house and Lobb's inheritance of her effects. **Negative finding:** searches for the two travelling abroad together, to Egypt or Italy as is sometimes said, **turned up nothing**. A gap, not a denial.

**Jenny dies; the manor passes. [A: Wikipedia, 'Kelmscott Manor']** 1935. No month.

**William Morris: Artist, Writer, Socialist. [R]** The two-volume study, commonly cited as Basil Blackwell, Oxford, 1936. **This session did not confirm publisher, place or date directly.** Kept [R], Blackwell's Broad Street coordinate flagged approximate.

**The letter to Shaw. [R]** "I'm a remarkable woman, always was, though none of you seemed to think so." From Peters via search summary, dated late in her life **without a year or month**; the 1936 placement says so.

**Death. [A]** 17 October 1938 at Kelmscott Manor, aged 76. Wikidata Q449758 gives P570 +1938-10-17 and P569 +1862-03-25; the age is consistent.

**Burial. [A: upgraded from the pool's [R]]** Both lenses tagged the burial as inference from village custom. It is not inference. **Wikidata Q449758 carries P119 place of burial = Q114737519, "St. George's Churchyard", Kelmscott**, P625 51.69255, -1.64088; Jane Morris is likewise "interred in the churchyard of St. George's Church in Kelmscott" (Wikipedia, 'Jane Morris'). **Retagged [A]**, and the pool's open question about whether Jane and May lie in the same ground as William is closed rather than left standing. Coordinate **51.6924, -1.641**, the atlas pin. The **day** of the funeral is nowhere recorded; 1938-10-20 is a flagged placement.

**Bequest. [A: Wikipedia, 'Kelmscott Manor']** Left to Oxford University in 1938 on condition the contents be preserved and the public admitted; the University found it unworkable and transferred the property to the Society of Antiquaries of London in **1962**, which holds it still. Reopened April 2022 after a £6 million restoration, drawing roughly 26,000 visitors a year.

**Recovery. [R]** Anna Mason et al., *May Morris: Arts & Crafts Designer* (Thames & Hudson, 2017), published alongside a William Morris Gallery exhibition of the same name, and Jan Marsh, "Feminist, socialist, embroiderer: the untold story of May Morris" (2017); **exhibition dates and venue not independently confirmed**. Londraville (1997) and Hulse (2017) reconstruct her correspondence with the collector John Quinn; neither was read here.

**Single-source claim, flagged. [R]** Wikipedia's live 'May Morris' article states "In 2026 there was an exhibition of her work in the Lady Lever Art Gallery". **Not cross-checkable against a second source**; possibly a recent unreviewed edit. Reported in the final campa as reported, not as settled.
