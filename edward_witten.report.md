# Edward Witten: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject living; no terminus.*

**Slug:** `edward_witten` · **Years:** 1951 to present · **Calendar:** Gregorian throughout.

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, tradition, or a placeholder coordinate standing in for an unlocated event. Contradictions are flagged and left open; gaps are stated as gaps.

A caution about the dossier. Its spine is the English Wikipedia article on Witten, with MacTutor as a second reading of the same career, Wikiquote for the quotations, and a few near-primary pages. That is a thin base for a living man of enormous public record. Nothing here rests on an archive, an interview, or a manuscript. Where a claim is marked [A], a named published source says so; a document was not inspected. Section 10 tables every correction this pass applied to the material it was handed.

## 1. Baltimore, 1951 to 1968

Edward Witten is born 26 August 1951 in Baltimore, Maryland, to a Jewish family. His father, Louis Witten, is a theoretical physicist working on gravitation; his mother is Lorraine Witten. **[A: Wikipedia, "Edward Witten"; MacTutor]** Four children: Edward, Celia, Matthew, Jesse. The count is confirmed; the birth order is stated by no reachable source and is left out of the journey. **[R]**

**The year in Israel.** Wikipedia states that the family lived in Israel for one year during the 1960s, naming neither city nor year. The stop is dated 1960-01-01 with the hedge in `date_confidence`, and the coordinate is Jerusalem (31.7683, 35.2137), used openly as a placeholder for the country. **[R: decade only, city unknown]**

**Park School of Baltimore**, graduation 1968. **[A: Wikipedia]** The school at 2425 Old Court Road, Baltimore MD 21208 sits at **39.3928, -76.6761**; it moved to that campus in 1959, so graduating from it in 1968 is right.

**A coincidence, not a connection.** On 18 October 1966 the structuralism colloquium at Johns Hopkins convened in Baltimore, an event the atlas already carries in four files (see section 11). Witten was fifteen and living in the same city. Nothing in any source connects him to it, and the campa says plainly that he was not in the room. **[R: coincidence of geography only]**

## 2. Brandeis, journalism, the McGovern campaign, 1968 to 1972

**Brandeis.** B.A. 1971, major in history, minor in linguistics. No undergraduate physics or mathematics. **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**

**The magazine pieces.** Witten published in *The New Republic* and *The Nation* in the **late 1960s**, while an undergraduate. **[A: Wikipedia]** Where they were written is unknown. The New York pin (40.7128, -74.0060) is at best half right, since *The Nation* was published from New York and *The New Republic* from Washington, D.C. throughout the 1960s. It is a placeholder, tagged **[R]**.

**McGovern.** About six months as a staffer on George McGovern's 1972 presidential campaign. **[A: Wikipedia]** No posting, office, or city is given; Washington, D.C. (38.9072, -77.0369) is a placeholder and his actual station is a gap.

**Michigan.** Enrolls as a graduate student in economics at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, and drops out. **[A: Wikipedia]** No duration beyond "briefly."

## 3. The turn to physics: Princeton, 1973 to 1976

- Enrolls at Princeton in applied mathematics in 1973 and shifts into physics. **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**
- Summer student at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory, 1974. **[A for the observatory, R for the site.]** Wikipedia names the NRAO but not which site; the summer program historically ran at Green Bank, West Virginia (38.4331, -79.8397), and that is the pin.
- Ph.D. in physics 1976 under David Gross, dissertation *Some Problems in the Short Distance Analysis of Gauge Theories*. **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**

**The building.** Witten's department is physics, housed since 1970 in Jadwin Hall. The atlas already holds its earlier address: the 1931 Fine Hall, now Jones Hall, pinned at **40.3487, -74.6588** in `dirac.journey.json`, where Dirac spent 1934 to 1935, Wigner held his joint appointment, and the IAS kept its first borrowed rooms. The journey includes one stop at that pin as an inherited address, not as an attested Witten location. **[R]**

## 4. Harvard, Oxford, the Society of Fellows, 1976 to 1980

- Postdoctoral fellow, Harvard University, from 1976. **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**
- Visits Oxford, 1977, the geography lens giving 1977 to 1978. **[A: Wikipedia]** The Oxford pin 51.7590, -1.2568 was not independently checked.
- Junior Fellow, Harvard Society of Fellows, 1977 to 1980. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Marries Chiara Nappi, a theoretical physicist who later becomes a Princeton professor, 1979. **[A for the marriage and the year: Wikipedia]** MacTutor has Witten at Harvard until 1980, and Nappi was likewise at Harvard before Princeton, so Cambridge, Massachusetts is the placeholder and the journey says so. **The ceremony's actual location is a gap.**

**Children.** Three: Ilana B. Witten, neuroscientist at Princeton; Daniela Witten, biostatistician at the University of Washington (Stanford Ph.D. 2010 under Robert Tibshirani); and Rafael. **[A: Wikipedia]** None of this enters the journey, which follows the traveler and not the household.

## 6. The Institute, 1987 to 1994

Witten becomes a professor in the School of Natural Sciences at the Institute for Advanced Study in 1987. **[A: Wikipedia; IAS records]** He receives the Charles Simonyi Professorship of Mathematical Physics only in **2005**, when the chair is created out of Simonyi's $25M donation; the journey carries the two separately.

**Coordinate discipline.** Three pins circulate for the IAS: **40.3298, -74.6672**, what the atlas actually uses (from `robert_oppenheimer.journey.json`); **40.3301, -74.6688**, what this run's interlock lens wrongly reported as canonical; and **40.3317, -74.6678**, the geographically correct Fuld Hall position from Wikipedia's infobox. The journey uses the first at every IAS stop, because coherence with the four IAS travelers outranks 200 metres of accuracy. It is not a claim about where Fuld Hall stands.

**1988 and 1989.** Witten coins the term "topological quantum field theory," then shows that the Jones polynomial of knot theory is obtainable from Feynman path integrals in Chern-Simons theory. **[A: Wikipedia]**

**1990, the Fields Medal, Kyoto.** Witten receives the Fields Medal at the International Congress of Mathematicians, August 1990, the only physicist ever to have received it. **[A: Wikipedia, "Edward Witten"; "Fields Medal"]** Two problems attach, both flagged, neither resolved.

*Who wrote the citation.* The chronology gathered in this run attributes it to **Ludwig Faddeev**; the quotations gathered in the same run attribute the identical sentence, "his ability to interpret physical ideas in mathematical form is quite unique," to **Michael Atiyah**. Both come from the same pass and cannot both be right. Atiyah did write an appreciation of Witten's work for the 1990 proceedings; Faddeev presided over parts of that Congress. The journey carries the quotation with the disagreement in its `quote_source`.

*The venue coordinate.* The Kyoto International Conference Center at Takaragaike is at 35.0611, 135.7831. The journey uses **35.0116, 135.7681** instead, the canonical Kyoto city pin already in `richard_feynman.journey.json`, so the atlas's Kyoto node holds together. Stated in `date_confidence`.

**1993**, honorary degree, Hebrew University of Jerusalem, from MacTutor's list (Brandeis 1988, Hebrew University 1993, Columbia 1996, USC 2004, Johns Hopkins 2005, Harvard 2005). **[A: MacTutor]** Which campus conferred it is not stated, so the Mount Scopus pin (31.79444, 35.23889) is **[R]**.

**1994**, Seiberg-Witten theory, with Nathan Seiberg at the IAS. **[A: Wikipedia]**

## 7. Eleven dimensions, 1995 to 1998

**Strings '95.** Speaking at the Strings '95 conference at the University of Southern California in March 1995, Witten proposes that the five superstring theories are limiting cases of a single eleven-dimensional theory, and names it M-theory. **[A: Wikipedia, "M-theory," quoted verbatim in the pass]** USC pin 34.0203, -118.2856.

**Minor planet 11349 Witten.** Discovered 3 May 1997 by Eric W. Elst at La Silla Observatory, Chile (-29.2567, -70.7367); a Vesta-family main-belt object roughly 3 km across. **[A: asteroid catalogue data]** The date of the naming, as against the discovery, was not established; the stop is dated to the discovery and says so.

**Santa Barbara.** Witten was a frequent visiting researcher at the Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics during major string-theory programs. **[R]** This comes from the interlock lens, outside the atlas and outside the verified pool; no source is named and no dates given. The stop is dated 1998-01-01 by pure convention, the hedge is in `date_confidence`, and the pin (34.414, -119.8489) is inherited byte-identical from `diffie.journey.json`. The Diffie juxtaposition is a shared campus and nothing more.

## 9. Afterlife, in a living subject

Witten is alive. There is no tomb, no grave, no death, no funerary iconography. The available material is institutional: prizes, an asteroid, memberships, translations, reputation.

**The "smartest living physicist" poll.** At a 1990 cosmology conference an informal poll of attendees is said to have named Witten, by count of mentions, the smartest living physicist, and the framing has recurred in profiles since. Origin and rigour could not be established beyond the Wikipedia citation. **[R: legend-grade]** It gets no stop; this atlas does not pin rumours.

**Translations.** Green, Schwarz and Witten's two-volume *Superstring Theory* (1987) became the field's standard reference. A Chinese edition is catalogued as 超弦理论 on Chinese book platforms; publisher and year unconfirmed **[R]**. No Spanish translation of any Witten technical work was located; the search was not exhaustive, so this is unresolved, not "none exists." No exhibit, statue, bust, or physical memorial was located either: a gap in the search, not a confirmed negative.

**The physicist father.** Louis Witten worked in general relativity, and retrospective profiles frame Edward's career as a continuation of a family line. That framing is **[R]**: it appears in later profiles rather than contemporaneous sources.

## 11. Interlock: where this journey touches the atlas

Every one of the following is **[R] from Witten's side.** No existing atlas file mentions Edward Witten. The connections are institutional and geographic and the journey says so. Named in campa:

- **`einstein`, `von_neumann`, `robert_oppenheimer`, `claude_shannon`**: the Institute for Advanced Study, pin inherited byte-identical, 40.3298, -74.6672. Einstein and von Neumann among the founding six; Oppenheimer director 1947 to 1966; Shannon a National Research Fellow at Fuld Hall in 1940. Witten arrives in 1987 and is still there. Shannon returns at Kyoto: he took the first Kyoto Prize in Basic Sciences in 1985 at pin 35.0459, 135.7825, and Witten takes the same prize at the same pin in 2014.
- **`dirac`**: old Fine Hall, now Jones Hall, 40.3487, -74.6588, the Princeton address of the 1930s emigres.
- **`richard_feynman`**: the path integral, worked out as Wheeler's student at Princeton in the early 1940s. Witten's 1989 derivation of the Jones polynomial runs a Feynman path integral over connections. Intellectual descent, not a meeting; no meeting is attested anywhere reached.
- **`niels_bohr`**: Bohr and Wheeler on uranium fission at Princeton, January 1939. Institutional overlap only.
- **`roland_barthes`, `gilles_deleuze`, `michel_foucault`, `nick_land`**: the Johns Hopkins colloquium of 18 October 1966, in the Baltimore where Witten was fifteen. Pin inherited byte-identical, 39.3299, -76.6205.
- **`edgar_allan_poe`**: Baltimore itself, 39.2904, -76.6122, inherited byte-identical.
- **`diffie`**: UC Santa Barbara, 34.414, -119.8489, inherited byte-identical. CRYPTO every August there in `diffie.journey.json`; the KITP string programs on the same campus here.
- **`keyserling`**: Kyoto, through the atlas's recurring Kyoto node.

Named by the interlock lens but not carried into campa: `barbier` (a 1790s Baltimore landfall) and `fermi` (whose Princeton connection is oblique).

**The negative finding, stated as such.** None of the four canonical hub pins the atlas watches for (Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris, Buenos Aires) appears in Witten's documented geography, and the journey inherits none of them, because inheriting one would be a fabrication. The childhood year in Israel is not Jerusalem in any sourced sense; that stop's Jerusalem coordinate is a placeholder for a country, not the Temple Mount pin.
