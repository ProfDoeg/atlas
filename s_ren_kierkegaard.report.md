# Søren Aabye Kierkegaard (1813-1855): research report

*Slug `s_ren_kierkegaard`. Written 2026-08-29 against the verified research pool
(chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses, verifier's CORRECTED notes
folded in). Journey file: `s_ren_kierkegaard.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops,
15 quotes.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or
inference · contradictions flagged, never adjudicated silently.

Register note. Most of what is "known" about Kierkegaard comes from a man writing about
himself in a private journal, for effect. That class is tagged **[A]** as to the existence
of the entry and **[R]** as to what the entry reports, here and in `date_confidence`.

## 1. Nytorv, 1813-1830

- Born **5 May 1813** on **Nytorv**, seventh child of the wool merchant Michael Pedersen
  Kierkegaard and Ane Sørensdatter Lund. **[A, sorenkierkegaard.org; Stanford
  Encyclopedia of Philosophy]**
- Baptized **3 June 1813**, **Helligåndskirken**. **[A]**
- **Borgerdydskolen**, Klareboderne, from **1821**. **[A]** The nickname *Gaffelen*, the
  fork, and the old-fashioned clothes are **[R]**, from schoolfellows' memoirs gathered
  decades later in Kirmmse.
- Confirmed **20 April 1828**, **Vor Frue Kirke**, by Bishop **Jacob Peter Mynster**.
  **[A]** Not in the dossier; found during verification.
- Matriculated in theology **30 October 1830**. **[A]**

**Correction.** The pool's single entry for 27 October 1831, "top marks in classics,
mathematics, and philosophy", conflates two exams. sorenkierkegaard.org separates them:
**25 April 1831**, magna cum laude in languages and history; **27 October 1831**, summa
cum laude in philosophy alone. Both are in the file.

**Coordinates corrected** (pool, then used): Nytorv 2, 55.6774/12.5701 to
**55.6775/12.5726**, the pool fell on Gammeltorv ~190 m west; Helligåndskirken,
55.6795/12.5747 to **55.6790/12.5769**, the pool landed on Strøget; Vor Frue Kirke,
55.6786/12.5729 to **55.6794/12.5728**, ~95 m south, a bad pair repeated at four stops.

**Gap.** The Wikipedia articles on Nytorv and on the Church of Our Lady do not mention
Kierkegaard at all, and no plaque at either site turned up: an absence in the record, not
proof no marker exists.

## 2. The earthquake and the idea, 1834-1838

- Mother dies **31 July 1834**; two of seven children survive. **[A]** Her near-total
  absence from the journals is a documented feature of the papers, written into the campa
  as a hole rather than filled.
- **The great earthquake.** sorenkierkegaard.org dates it 1835 and states the content
  directly: "discovered father's curse on God." The pool's claim that the content was
  "never specified in his surviving papers" **overstates the mystery** and is rewritten.
  What is disputed is the **dating**, pushed by some scholars as late as 1838, and how
  literally to read the entry. **[R]** as to date and content, **[A]** that the entry
  exists (II A 805, **no translator named**).
- **Gilleleje, summer 1835. [A]** for the stay, **[R]** for the spot, which tradition puts
  at **Gilbjerg Hoved**, where a memorial stone carries the line *Hvad er sandhed andet
  end en leven for en idé*. The stone is an upgrade on the dossier and supplies the stop's
  quote. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's 56.135, 12.2934 sat ~700 m offshore; used
  **56.1290, 12.2869**.
- **Meets Regine Olsen**, Rørdam household, Frederiksberg, **May 1837**
  (sorenkierkegaard.org narrows to 8-12 May). **[A]** **Factual correction:** the pool
  called her eighteen; born **23 January 1822**, she was **fifteen**. **Coordinate [R]
  and poor:** 55.6786, 12.5309 sits well north of Frederiksberg proper (~55.667, 12.533),
  and the house is not identifiable.
- **First book, 1838**, *From the Papers of One Still Living*, on **Hans Christian
  Andersen**'s *Only a Fiddler*. **[A]** for book and year; the Andersen-critique framing
  was flagged in the pool as dossier-based rather than fetched and is **not checked
  against the text here**. That the two avoided each other afterwards is **[R]**.
- **Father dies 9 August 1838**, buried **14 August**, Assistens. **[A]** The five-day gap
  between the pool's two figures is ordinary burial delay, not a contradiction. The death
  read as a sacrifice for the son is his own reading: **[A]** as testimony, **[R]** as
  fact.

- Theological examination **3 July 1840**. **[A]**
- **Jutland journey, 19 July to 6 August 1840. [A]** for journey and dates
  (sorenkierkegaard.org **names no village**). **Major geographic correction:** the pool

## 5. The Corsair, 1845-1846

- **The sharpest correction in the pool.** The chronology attached 27 December 1845 to
  **Møller's review**; it belongs to **Kierkegaard's reply**. en.wikipedia: Møller
  published in *Gæa* on **22 December 1845**, and the counter-attack, "The Activity of a
  Traveling Esthetician", ran in *Fædrelandet* on **27 December 1845**. Both are in the
  file.
- **First *Corsaren* caricature 2 January 1846. [A]** **Meïr Aron Goldschmidt** founded
  the paper in 1840 and sold it in 1846, so his editorship covers the caricatures. **Tag
  corrected:** the pool marked the stop [A] while its own note conceded the office address
  was a reconstruction. Placement is now **[R]**.
- ***A Literary Review*, 1846. [A]** for the year only; day and month defaulted, so its
  position relative to the January caricatures is approximate, not sequence.
- ***Concluding Unscientific Postscript*, 27 February 1846**, with the signed declaration
  claiming the pseudonymous authorship. **[A]**
- **Fourth Berlin trip, 2-16 May 1846. [A]** *Correction:* the geography lens gave
  "May-June 1846" and called it the least certain of the four datings; its own chronology
  lens had it right, and that note is dropped.

## 8. Assistens and afterlife

- **Funeral, Vor Frue Kirke, 18 November 1855. [A]** A largely student congregation, and
  his brother Peter Christian in the pulpit, are **[R]**.
- **Henrik Lund's graveside protest**, prosecution, fine. **[A]** Which passages he read
  aloud is **[R]**. ***Coordinate contradiction, not resolved:*** Assistens is 55.6913,
  12.5514 in the geography lens, 55.6908, 12.5494 in the afterlife lens; geography used.
- ***Factual error in the pool, corrected.*** The afterlife lens had **Regine dying in the
  Danish West Indies**. en.wikipedia 'Regine Olsen' gives **18 March 1904, in Copenhagen,
  aged 82**, buried at **Assistens** alongside Kierkegaard and Schlegel. The West Indies
  belong to another part of her life; Schlegel was governor there from 1855. The note's
  open question about the burial is settled: same cemetery.
- **The will** left the estate to Regine on the ground that an engagement is as binding as
  a marriage; she refused it, then accepted only as trustee, to give to the poor. **[A]**
- **Hasselriis statue, Royal Library Garden, 1879. [A]** No unveiling account or
  description: a gap.
- **Reception.** *Øieblikket* into German by **1861**, again **1896**; by **1889** an
  encyclopedia calls him "the most original thinker and theological philosopher the North
  ever produced", "steadily growing since his death". **[A]** His standing climbed through
  the later nineteenth century, not manufactured by twentieth-century existentialism;
  **"father of existentialism"** is itself a **[R]** label, written into no campa.
- **Editions.** *Søren Kierkegaards Skrifter*, **55 volumes, 1997-2013**, free online.
  **[A]** ***Two corrections from the verifier:*** Lowrie's 1941 title is ***Training in
  Christianity***, not *Practice in Christianity* (the Hong title of 1991); and the
  Princeton *Kierkegaard's Writings* series is listed by its publisher as **27 volumes**,
  not 26, so **no number is given** in the campa.

## Quotations

Fifteen of forty-five stops carry a quote, all verbatim from the pool. Most are
**Hollander's 1923 *Selections from the Writings of Kierkegaard***, via Wikisource, named
every time. The *Stages* line on woman and "the joke" is **a pseudonym's banquet speech
(Constantin Constantius), not Kierkegaard in his own name**, and the quote_source says so.
The **great earthquake** entry (II A 805) and the **"everyone spat at Christ"** entry come
from sorenkierkegaard.org with no translator named; both fields ask for a cross-check
against SKS.

**The backwards/forwards aphorism.** The entry is real: *Journals* 1843, **Pap. IV A 164
(= JJ:167)**, Danish attested as *"Det er ganske sandt, hvad Philosophien siger, at Livet
maa forstaaes baglænds. Men derover glemmer man den anden Sætning, at det maa leves
forlænds."* The familiar English "Life can only be understood backwards; but it must be
lived forwards" is a **popular condensation** in at least three inconsistent forms with no
edition cited anywhere. The file uses the **literal rendering** with the citation and says
the condensed wording is not attributable to Hollander, Dru or Hong.

## Interlocks

Named in a campa, on a real relation:

- **`schelling`**: Kierkegaard sat in his Berlin course, filled a notebook, quoted him in
  the journals, then broke with him.
- **`hegel`**: named at the dissertation and at the *Postscript*. The authorship is built
  against Hegel's System and the Danish Hegelianism transmitted through Martensen and
  J. L. Heiberg. **They never met and Hegel never went to Copenhagen**; the relation is
  intellectual reception, one-directional, and is written that way.
- **`hans_christian_andersen`**: Kierkegaard's first book is a hostile review of Andersen's
  novel, and Andersen read it.

Deliberately **not** named in any campa:

- **`mikhail_bakunin`, `humboldt`** (and, off-atlas, Engels and Burckhardt), all in the
  audience of Schelling's 1841-42 lectures, with four existing journey files staging that
  hall. The pool says plainly: **co-attendance only, no documented personal meeting**. A
  shared lecture hall is shared geography, so the fact survives only in the stop's
  `suggested_refs` and `date_confidence`. Naming them is the operator's ruling to make.
- **`kant`**: a thematic parallel only, one city walked in an afternoon as the shape of a
  philosophy. No relation of the kind the rule requires.
- **`alexander_hamilton`**: Regine's husband Schlegel became governor in the Danish West
  Indies, where Hamilton's Christiansted pin already sits. Shared geography between two
  third parties, neither of them Kierkegaard, at a place he never went. That coordinate is
  **not** inherited.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Kierkegaard is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. One relation here is not rooted in his
own life. It is **not silently deleted**; it is reported with its direction named.

- **`theodor_adorno`, direction: FORWARD.** Adorno's Frankfurt habilitation (1929-31,
  sponsored by Tillich) studies Kierkegaard's aesthetics and appears as *Kierkegaard:
  Construction of the Aesthetic* on **23 March 1933**: a substantive one-directional
  engagement by a later atlas subject, which the task's rule permits citing as **a source
  on Kierkegaard's own stop**. It sits in `suggested_refs` and `sources` on the
  ***Sickness Unto Death*** stop, appears in no campa, and Adorno's Frankfurt scene is
  **not** staged here: that already sits in `theodor_adorno.journey.json`.
  **The tension to rule on:** by the block rule in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, a 1933 artifact is a
  new hash in an old block, and Kierkegaard's file stopped in 1855. To remove it, strip one
  `suggested_refs` entry and one `sources` entry from that one stop.
- **No backward apparatus relation was found**, and none is needed. Kierkegaard applies
  his own apparatus, in his own decades, to Abraham and to Socrates, and both appear as
  *his* material inside stops he was present for. No stop stages him at Moriah or in
  Athens.

## Honest gaps

1. **The mother.** Ane Sørensdatter Lund is nearly absent from the journals. The largest
   hole in the record, written into the file as a hole.
2. **The Rørdam house.** No street address; only the district is certain, and the pool's
   coordinate for it is bad.
3. **Berlin.** No fetched source names the lecture room or building, and the
   Gendarmenmarkt lodging is dossier-only.
4. **Reitzel's shop, the *Corsaren* office, the *Fædrelandet* office.** Three
   reconstructed placements: six publication stops and three newspaper or periodical stops
   rest on two approximate pins. Historic **Nørregade** numbering likewise does not map
   onto the modern street. A Copenhagen street directory of the 1840s would fix all of it;
   none was available.
5. **Cause of death.** Unresolved, no autopsy.
6. **No plaque or memorial documented** at Nytorv 2 or Vor Frue Kirke, and no unveiling
   account or description of the 1879 statue.
7. **The Sweden crossing, 1851.** Unconfirmed in every particular; not in the file.
8. **Niels Andreas Kierkegaard's death at Paterson, New Jersey, 1833**, tagged **[R]** in
   the pool and confirmed by no primary source. Not in the file, and even if confirmed it
   is not Kierkegaard's stop: the brother travelled, Søren did not.
9. ***Stages on Life's Way*, 30 April 1845.** Relayed from the bibliography, not
   re-verified.
