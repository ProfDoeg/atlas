# Strabo of Amaseia: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-18 for `strabo.journey.json`. Register: national mythology: the canon is true. Calendar: julian.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, passage named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition · **[gap]** nothing reachable, stated as nothing.

Almost everything known about Strabo's life comes out of his own book, in scattered first-person asides. There is no ancient biography, no letter to or from him, no epitaph, no contemporary who names him. The evidentiary base is one autobiography-by-accident embedded in a 17-book geography, plus modern reconstruction laid on top. Several citations in general circulation are wrong; this pass corrected six.

---

## 1. Family prehistory

The maternal line is the one Strabo tells. He never mentions his father at all. **[gap]**

- Dorylaus "the Tactician," military specialist to Mithridates V Euergetes, sent to Crete to enlist mercenaries, stranded there when Euergetes is murdered at Sinope, elected general by the Cnossians against Gortyn, marries a Macedonian woman named Sterope, fathers Lagetas and Stratarchas on the island. Strabo saw the son Stratarchas "when he was an extremely old man," the only place his own eye touches the pre-history. **[A, 10.4.10]**
- **Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** 12.3.33 calls the Tactician "my mother's grandfather." 10.4.10 makes Strabo's mother's mother the *sister of Lagetas*, i.e. daughter of the Tactician, putting him a generation further back. The gather pool said great-grandfather; verify corrected to grandfather on 12.3.33. Both passages are Strabo's. The Cnossus stop's `date_confidence` states the discrepancy instead of picking a side.
- A second Dorylaus, son of Philetaerus, nephew of the Tactician, foster-brother of Mithridates VI Eupator, receives "all the greatest honours" and "in particular the priesthood of Comana," then is caught trying to make the kingdom revolt to the Romans, is overthrown, and "the family was cast into disrepute along with him." **[A, 12.3.33; foster-brother relation at 10.4.10]**
  - **Correction.** The pool cited 10.4.10 for the priesthood; 10.4.10 does not contain it. Also dropped: "hereditary." Strabo says the priesthood was *conferred* by Eupator.
  - **Second contradiction, flagged.** At 10.4.10 the Loeb renders the same fall with **Lagetas** as the man caught. 12.3.33 names Dorylaus son of Philetaerus. Not adjudicated; the campa says so outright.
- Moaphernes, "my mother's uncle," returns to distinction just before the dissolution and is tied to Colchis. **[A, 11.2.18, 12.3.33]** No evidence Strabo went there. **[gap]**
- During the war with Lucullus, Mithridates puts to death Tibius, cousin of the maternal grandfather, and Tibius's son Theophilus; the grandfather then takes pledges from Lucullus and causes **fifteen garrisons** to revolt, on great promises. Pompey supersedes Lucullus, treats his beneficiaries as enemies, the Senate refuses to ratify, and the grandfather gets nothing. **[A, 12.3.33]** No place is given for the killings; the file pins them at Cabeira and says in `date_confidence` that this is inference. **[R]**
- The whole arc is read by modern scholarship not as transparent archive but as active reshaping of family memory for a Roman world. **[R, Kuin 2017]**

## 2. Birth and home region

- **Birth year contested, left contested.** 64/63 BC is modern chronographic reconstruction, not an ancient datum; Roller (2014) argues no later than c.60 BC; Pothecary (1999) holds the traditional date less secure than usually assumed. **[R for all three]** Unreconciled in current scholarship and unreconciled here; the file carries `-0064-01-01` with the dispute in `date_confidence`.
- **Birthplace is inference.** Strabo calls Amaseia "my city" (12.3.39) and "my fatherland" (12.3.15) **[A]**, and nowhere writes that he was born there. He describes it fully at 12.3.39: deep valley, the Iris, fortified rocks, palaces and royal monuments inside the circuit, underground water channels, bridges.
  - **Correction, and an internal disagreement in the pool.** The chronology section cited 12.3.15 and 12.3.39 for the *same* "my city" phrase and verify ruled 12.3.15 wrong; but the quotes section carries a distinct verified 12.3.15 line, "flowing past the very wall of Amaseia, my fatherland." Resolution used: **12.3.39 is "my city," 12.3.15 is a separate "my fatherland."** Two passages, not one claim doubled.
- Cabeira: palace, water-mill, park, hunting grounds, mines. **[A, 12.3.30]** **Correction:** the pool cited 12.3.31, which is **Kainon Chorion**, a sheer rock with a summit spring where Mithridates kept his treasures, whose location is disputed and is *not* Niksar. The stop carries both, separated.
- Zela: the mound, Anaitis, Omanus, Anadatus, oaths sworn there **[A, 11.8.4, 12.3.37]**; Sinope and Amisus elaborated **[A, 12.3.11, 12.3.16]**. Description only, no personal-visit statement for any. **[gap]**

## 3. Education

- Studied under **Aristodemus of Nysa**, grammarian and rhetorician in a Homeric-interpretation line **[A, 14.1.48]**, date of attendance not stated **[R]**; and connected to **Xenarchus of Seleucia**, Peripatetic, who taught at Alexandria, Athens, finally Rome **[A, 14.5.4]**.
- **Tyrannion of Amisus**, carried west after Lucullus's campaigns, is named from Amisus **[A, 12.3.16]**; that Strabo *studied* under him is standard modern reconstruction, not a flat statement. **[R]**
- **Athenodorus of Tarsus** is routinely named as his teacher; the ancient evidence for a formal teaching relationship is thinner than the textbook claim. **[R, weaker than usually presented, Roller 2014]** *No Tarsus stop was created:* no attested Strabo presence there, and a stop belongs to whoever was actually there. The relation sits in the Rome/Xenarchus stop's `date_confidence`.
- Strabo identifies with the Stoa: "our Zeno," "our school" against the Peripatetics. **[A, e.g. 1.2.34]** Byzantine tradition calls him "Strabo the Stoic philosopher" **[R]**; no primary Byzantine text was verified in this pass, see Sources.

## 4. Rome

**The most-corrected item in this dossier.** The standard claim is that Strabo "places himself in Rome in the time of the elderly Publius Servilius Vatia Isauricus (d. 44 BC)," cited to 5.3.7 or 5.3.9 and often extended to "he was in Rome the year Caesar was assassinated." Book 5 chapter 3 contains **no mention of Servilius Isauricus at all**. The passage is **12.6.2**, in the account of Isaura: "in particular to Publius Servilius, surnamed Isauricus, with whom I was acquainted." **[A]** He says only that he knew the man: not where, not when, no Rome, no Caesar **[gap]**. What survives is a terminus ante quem **[A]**; the rest is reconstruction **[R]**, and the stop is hedged accordingly.

**Selurus**, called the son of Aetna, sent up to Rome, set on a tall scaffold built to resemble Aetna and rigged to collapse, dropping him into cages of wild beasts. **[A, 6.2.6]** **Two corrections.** Strabo names the venue, a gladiatorial combat in the **Forum**; the pool's Circus Maximus was speculation presented as fact and is dropped with its coordinates. And he gives **no year**: "recently, in my own time." The c.35 BC date is conjecture, carried only as an ordering device.

## 5. The Aegean and the stated range

- **Gyaros**, 29 BC: barren island, a fisherman sent to petition Octavian over an unpayable tax, taken aboard Strabo's own vessel. **[A, 10.5.3]** The year follows from his own statement that Caesar was at Corinth "on his way to celebrate the Triumph after the victory at Actium"; Corinth is named as where Octavian was, with no claim that Strabo went ashore. **[A, 10.5.3; gap for the landfall]**
- **Stated range**: west to "the regions of Tyrrhenia opposite Sardinia," east to Armenia, north-south from the Black Sea to the frontier of Ethiopia. **[A, 2.5.11]** He names **no town** at either end; Populonia and Artaxata are representative points, and both stops say so.

## 6. Egypt: the best-documented journey of his life

- Accompanied **Aelius Gallus**, prefect of Egypt, c.25-24 BC, and states in his own voice that he "ascended the Nile as far as Syene and the frontiers of Ethiopia." **[A, 2.5.12]** **Correction:** the pool attached this quotation to 17.1.50, which is the wagon passage to Philae.
- Three stops carry explicit first-person testimony: **Heliopolis**, where "the houses of the priests and schools of Plato and Eudoxus were pointed out to us" **[A, 17.1.29]**; the **Colossi of Memnon**, where he was present with Gallus "and his crowd of associates, both friends and soldiers" and heard the noise "at about the first hour," allowing it may come from the stones or from the people at the base and declining to choose **[A, 17.1.46]**; and **Philae**, reached from Syene by wagon "through an exceedingly level plain, a distance all told of about one hundred stadia" **[A, 17.1.50]**, the southern limit of the travelled itinerary.
- **Giza / Memphis**: described at 17.1.33-34 but **not** marked with a statement of seeing, unlike the stops that are. **[R for autopsy]** The file keeps the distinction because Strabo keeps it.
- **Alexandria**: extended residence is a strong reconstruction from the density of firsthand Book 17 material **[R, Roller 2014, Dueck 2000]**. There is **no evidence he worked in the Library**; that detail is refused. **[gap]**
- **Arabia Felix: he was not there.** The Gallus campaign is narrated at 16.4.22-24 entirely on Gallus's authority and other testimony, with no claim of presence; the common secondary-literature claim of an eyewitness account is wrong. The farthest point reached is "a city called Marsiaba, which belonged to the tribe of the Rhammanitae, who were subject to Ilasarus" (16.4.24). **Strabo never writes "Marib"**; the equation is a disputed modern conjecture. **No Marsiaba stop was created**, since Gallus was there and Strabo was not; the material sits in the Arsinoe stop's campa and `suggested_refs`.

## 7. The works

- The **Historika Hypomnemata**, roughly 47 books. Strabo cites "the sixth book of my Historika Hypomnemata," also calling it "the second book of my continuation of Polybius." **[A, 11.9.3; reconstruction per Lindsay 2014]** Almost entirely lost: of about 64 known books, 17 survive. **[R for the arithmetic]**
- The **Geography**, 17 books, historical-cultural rather than technical-cartographic, region by region, Anatolia far past its share. **[A]** Place of composition unknown.
- **Dating of the final form**: the latest securely datable event is the death of Juba II, AD 23/24, treated as very recent. **[A, 17.3.7, corroborated 17.3.9]** This anchors a substantially **Tiberian** final form against older assumptions of an Augustan c.7 BC edition. **[R, Roller 2014]** **Correction:** the pool cited 17.3.25 as a second Juba passage; it is not one. The corroboration is 17.3.9.
- Retirement to Amaseia is tradition, not record **[R]**, so the six methodological stops in segment 6 sit at Strabo's own Amaseia pin, dated only to order the segment, stated in each `date_confidence`. The pool's suggested Athens pin (37.9838/23.7275) and Pataliputra pin (25.61/85.14) were **declined**: he went to neither, and both would read on a map as travel.

## 8. The cynocephali question, against the pool's own headline

Posed as a research task, answered wrongly by the gather stage, caught by verify. Stated straight, because the wrong version appears three times in the pool:

- **Strabo does name dog-headed men.** At **1.2.35**, defending the poets against charges of geographical ignorance: no one charges Hesiod with ignorance "when he speaks of men who are half-dog, of long-headed men and of Pygmies; ... nor **Aeschylus when he speaks of dog-headed men**, or of men with eyes in their breasts, or of one-eyed men." **[A, verified verbatim, Loeb via LacusCurtius]**
- Correct formulation: **no cynocephali in Book 15's India material; the dog-headed men are in Book 1, attributed to Aeschylus, as an example of deliberate poetic marvel-making.**
- In Book 15 the nearest thing is the **Monommati**, "with dog's ears, with the eye in the middle of the forehead, with hair standing erect, and with shaggy breasts," relayed from Megasthenes with the mouthless Astomi, the Ocypodes, and the Enotocoetae who sleep in their own ears; framed by "But Megasthenes, going beyond all bounds to the realm of myth," and closed a section later by "Now these statements of Megasthenes are mythical and refuted by many writers." **[A, 15.1.57-58]** Dog's **ears**, not a dog's **head**: different creatures, different books, kept apart in the journey file.
- The list of unreliable India-writers at 1.2.35 names Deimachus, Megasthenes, Onesicritus, Nearchus, with Ctesias among earlier offenders: that Ctesias is suspect on monstrous races is **Strabo's own flag**, not a modern judgement. **[A]**

## 9. Method

- **Amazons**: "marvellous and beyond belief," and not discarded. He sets the writers against each other and checks rivers, mountains and neighbouring peoples for a real population under the tradition. **[A, 11.5.1-4, quote at 11.5.3]**
- **Typhon**: myth and geology on the same page. **Correction:** the pool labelled these "Cilicia/Syria"; neither is Cilician. **13.4.6 is Lydia**, the Catacecaumene, where subterranean fire is offered as cause and "they do not hesitate to lay there the scene of the mythical story of Typhon." **16.2.7 is Syria**: the Orontes formerly called Typhon, the lightning-struck dragon cutting the river bed. **[A]**
- **Pytheas**: attacked by name as a falsifier (1.4.3, 2.4.1) and preserved in the act of refutation, including where Pytheas distinguishes what he saw from what he was told. **[A]** See §11.

## 10. Death, and the gaps

- No year, place or manner of death is attested. **[gap]** "c. AD 24" is arithmetic run backward from the Juba notice **[R]**; the "aged 87" printed alongside it is arithmetic laid on a birth year that is itself disputed, and is refused as illusory precision. Retirement to Amaseia is tradition. **[R]**
- No wife, children or household is attested anywhere, in his text or in any ancient testimony; no tomb, epitaph, funerary monument, ancient biography or obituary. The silence is total, not thin, and is evidence of nothing. **[gap]**
- **No authenticated ancient portrait** was ever identified; Renaissance engravings and the modern Amasya statue are imaginative. **[A as a confirmed absence]** The name Strabo, the Squinter, is real; whether he squinted is unverifiable. **[gap]**
- Transmission: Latin translation Rome c.1469, before any printed Greek; Greek editio princeps Venice 1516; Casaubon 1587; Diller (1975) on the manuscripts; Jones's Loeb 1917-1932; Roller 2014 and 2018. **[A]** All in the last stop's `suggested_refs`, not as stops: Strabo was not in Venice in 1516. Two further bibliography claims were dropped as unverifiable, see Sources.

## 11. Interlocks: named and refused

**Named in a campa (one): `pytheas_de_massalia`.** A direct, one-directional textual relation: Strabo reads Pytheas, calls him an arch-falsifier at 1.4.3 and 2.4.1, denies the northern voyage, and in refuting him transmits most of what survives of the man. Pytheas's file already carries an Amaseia stop for this encounter; the reciprocal engagement now sits at Strabo's own pin, in segment 6.

**Refused, with reasons:**

- **`cicero`**: three touchpoints found by the lens (*De Imperio Cn. Pompei*, 66 BC; the Ides of March, 44; Philo of Larissa fleeing "Mithridates' war," 87). Same war and decades as Strabo's family catastrophe, no relation between the two men, and Strabo never cites Cicero.
- **`virgil`**: offered as thematic and contemporary parallel, explicitly tagged R, "not a documented meeting." **`saint_blaise`**: Pontus as a Roman administrative unit three centuries later, geographic anchor only.
- **`hypatia`**: same city four centuries later, same Museion lineage. Shared geography only. Her Alexandria pin **was** inherited byte-identical, which is the right use of an interlock: a shared coordinate, not a shared sentence.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Amaseia **40.6499 / 35.8353** (`pytheas_de_massalia`), used for every Amaseia stop over the pool's 40.657/35.8347 · Alexandria **31.2001 / 29.9055** (`hypatia`) · Rome, the Forum **41.8925 / 12.4853**, consistent with the Rome cluster in `cicero` and `virgil`.

**Atlas gaps, stated as gaps:** no existing journey file names Aelius Gallus, Tyrannion, Aristodemus, Athenodorus, Gyaros, Comana Pontica or Syene in connection with Strabo. Absences, not overlooked intersections; nothing was invented to fill them.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found, and nothing was silently deleted.** Strabo is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no stop here required the exception.

- No **backward** application: every methodological stop in segment 6 rests on a passage he wrote, cited by section.
- No **forward** application: nothing points at an artifact postdating him. The 1469 translation, the 1516 editio princeps, Casaubon, Diller, the Loeb, Roller and the modern Amasya statue appear only in `suggested_refs`, plus one closing campa clause about the statue, on the same footing as the 1966 repatriation clause in `abdelkader.journey.json`. If the operator reads even that as a forward hash, the clause to cut is the last sentence of the final stop.
- Two stops were **declined rather than written**, recorded here so the decision is visible: a Marsiaba/Marib stop (Gallus was there, Strabo was not) and a Tarsus stop (Athenodorus's city, no attested Strabo presence). Both survive as `suggested_refs` and `date_confidence` text on stops that are genuinely his.
- One stop is deliberately weak and flagged rather than dropped: **Ephesus**. No first-person Ephesus statement survives anywhere in the Geography; it stands on the shape of the roads between Nysa and Rome, and its campa says so. For a file with no inferred travel at all, this is the stop to remove.
