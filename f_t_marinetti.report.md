# F.T. Marinetti (1876-1944): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 for `f_t_marinetti.journey.json`. Adversarially verified pool; every claim carries a tag.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on Marinetti's own later telling. Contradictions are printed side by side and left open; corrections are stated rather than silently applied.

---

## 1. Alexandria and Paris, 1876-1902

**Birth. [A: Wikipedia EN and IT]** Filippo Tommaso Emilio Marinetti, Alexandria, Egypt, 22 December 1876, son of **Enrico Marinetti**, lawyer, of Voghera in Piedmont, and **Amalia Grolli**.

- **Correction.** Amalia Grolli was the **daughter**, not granddaughter, of a Milanese professor of literature. EN Wikipedia: "Amalia Grolli (daughter of a literary professor from Milan)." The pool's two lenses contradicted each other on this.
- **[A: Wikipedia IT]** The parents **never married**; they lived together *more uxorio*. Stated in the journey file.
- **[R, dropped]** The much-repeated "came to Egypt in 1865 at the invitation of Khedive Isma'il Pasha" appears in **neither** article fetched this session. Unsourced as given.

**Schooling. [A: Wikipedia IT]** Jesuit **Collège Saint-François-Xavier**, Alexandria, from 1888.

- **Correction.** One lens gave "Collège des Frères de Saint-François-Xavier". "Frères" denotes the Christian Brothers, a **different congregation**; the school was Jesuit.
- **[A: Wikipedia EN]** At about seventeen (1893) he founds the magazine **Papyrus** and circulates Zola in it; the fathers threaten expulsion. He is **not** recorded as expelled, and some accounts treat the expulsion story as embellished. **Gap:** campus site unverified, coordinates approximate the European quarter.

**Baccalaureate. [A, year contested]** EN: **1894**, Sorbonne. IT: **1893**, Paris. Unresolved; the stop is dated 1893-01-01 with the disagreement in `date_confidence`. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 48.8462, 2.343 sat about 270 m south of the Sorbonne; the file uses **48.8486, 2.343**.

**Law degree, 1899. [A as to year; city contested, left open]**

- EN Wikipedia: **Pavia**. IT Wikipedia: **Genoa** (IT also has him starting law at Pavia in 1893).
- Nothing fetched adjudicates. The file plants **one** stop, Genoa (44.4148, 8.9266, via Balbi), and carries the Pavia claim in `date_confidence`; two stops would have implied a resolution the record does not support. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 44.4056, 8.9463 sits near Piazza De Ferrari, about 1.9 km off.

**1902. [A: Wikipedia IT]** His mother dies; the same year **La Conquête des Étoiles** is published in Paris, in French. Months of neither event established. **Gap.**

**D'Annunzio, first phase. [R: Wikipedia IT]** *D'Annunzio intime* (1903), *Les Dieux s'en vont, D'Annunzio reste* (1908): real titles, contents not read, used only to ground the Fiume campa.

**Abbaye de Créteil. [R, downgraded from A]** EN Wikipedia: founded **October 1906** (not 1907) by **Gleizes, Arcos, Barzun, Mercereau and Vildrac** (the pool dropped the last two); closed **28 January 1908**. Of Marinetti it says only that he and Brâncuși "were visitors there": no date, no reaction recorded. The pool's line that Créteil "teaches him what to reject" was the researcher's invention.

## 2. The manifesto, 1905-1910

**Poesia. [A: Wikipedia IT]** Founded and directed from Milan, 1905; organ of Futurism after 1909. **[R]** The Corso Venezia address, the newsroom-style household and the paternal funding come from standard historiography (Berghaus, Poggi). Street number **not verified; gap**.

**The crash. [R]** Sources agree on an accident outside Milan in 1908 and on its mythologization, but not on the month (December usual, October in secondary accounts); the site was **never precisely documented**, and the ditch imagery survives only in Marinetti's own prose. The 45.489, 9.234 marker is a point on the Monza road, not a located site.

**Publication sequence of the Manifesto. [A: Wikipedia EN]** The pool's most repeated error, present in three lenses:

1. **January 1909**: as preface to a volume of Marinetti's poetry, Milan.
2. **5 February 1909**: front page, *Gazzetta dell'Emilia*, Bologna. **First newspaper printing, not first publication.**
3. **20 February 1909**: front page, *Le Figaro*, Paris.
4. **April 1909**: Spanish translation in *Prometeo*, Madrid; dedicated issue of *Poesia*.

The claim that the Bologna text was "a shorter version" is **unsupported** and was dropped. **Gap:** the paper's address is unknown, city-centre coordinates used.

**Le Roi Bombance. [A: EN and IT]** Paris premiere, April 1909, whistled down; duel with a hostile critic follows. Day and theatre **not established; gap**.

**Mafarka il futurista. [A: Wikipedia IT]** **Correction.** The pool said "tried and cleared". Acquitted at first instance in **October 1910**, he was convicted on appeal, and the **conviction was upheld by the Court of Cassation**.

**Boccioni, Carrà, Russolo. [A: Wikipedia IT]** Adhere in 1910.

## 3. The serate, 1910

The weakest-sourced material here, and the reason this segment carries three stops rather than six.

- **Trieste, Politeama Rossetti, January 1910. [A/R]** The date rests on **Berghaus alone**; the theatre's own article does not mention a 1910 serata, so the pool's [A] is optimistic. **Coordinate correction:** 45.6524, 13.7846 (Viale XX Settembre 45), 1.2 km east of the pool's figure.
- **Milan, Teatro Lirico, February 1910. [R]** Same problem. **Coordinate correction:** 45.4615, 9.1918 (Via Larga 14), 440 m south of the pool's figure.
- **Turin and Naples. [R, dropped]** Teatro Chiarella was offered as "a plausible but unverified candidate"; Naples had only a city pin and a four-year range. Both **dropped rather than pinned**.
- **Venice, Contro Venezia passatista. [A: Wikipedia IT]** **Correction.** The leaflet action is standardly placed at the **Torre dell'Orologio**, not the Campanile; the pool's two lenses contradicted each other. IT Wikipedia dates the **text** to 27 April 1910 and names **Marinetti, Boccioni, Carrà and Russolo** as authors. The July date of the drop is conventional.

## 4. The wars of the correspondent, 1911-1914

**Tripoli. [A: EN and IT]** Lands 12 October 1911 for the Paris *L'Intransigeant*; the dispatches become *La battaglia di Tripoli*, 38,000 copies.

**Sciara Sciat. [A for the battle; R for his participation]** Ambush of 23 October 1911, counterattack of 26 October in which he claims to have taken part. **Biographers dispute how much combat he saw**, and his account does not separate witness from reconstruction. The campa states his claim as his claim.

**Technical Manifesto of Futurist Literature. [A: Wikipedia IT]** 11 May 1912, Milan. *Parole in libertà.*

**Adrianople. [A, duration corrected]** The pool said "winter of 1912 into 1913". His trip to the Bulgarian lines was **autumn 1912**, weeks long; the **siege** ran 3 November 1912 to 26 March 1913. *Zang Tumb Tumb*, Milan, **1914**.

**Fondo Marinetti, Pavia. [A, date corrected by three decades]** The pool had the 42 manifestos entering the university library **after his death in 1944**. IT Wikipedia describes the fondo as a **donation made between 1913 and 1916**: a gift from the living Marinetti, with seven first editions and works by Folgore, Buzzi, Papini, Palazzeschi and Govoni. The figure holds; the direction of the story was reversed. **Coordinate correction:** 45.1867, 9.1559 (Strada Nuova 65), about 250 m off.

**Russia, 1914. [A: Wikipedia IT]** Moscow and St Petersburg; meets the Russian Futurists. Venues, audiences and months **not established; gap**. One Moscow stop; Petersburg is named inside its campa rather than pinned.

## 5. War, the Fasci, and the break, 1914-1920

**Flag-burning arrest. [A as event; month corrected]** IT Wikipedia records the Piazza del Duomo arrest **without a month**. The pool's "August 1914, weeks after Sarajevo" is wrong twice: the episode is conventionally dated **mid-September 1914**, eleven weeks after Sarajevo. Stop dated 1914-09-01, hedged.

**Volunteer cyclists. [A: Wikipedia EN]** Lombard Volunteer Cyclist Battalion, 1915, Trentino front near Lake Garda; disbanded as unsuited to mountains. Month approximate.

**Isonzo, 1917. [A: EN and IT]** Bronze **Medal of Military Valour** for **Monte Kuk, 12-14 May 1917**; wounded in the same period with an artillery unit. **Exact place and date of the wounding not specified; gap.** Gorizia stands as a representative point.

**Vittorio Veneto. [A: EN and IT]** 28 October 1918, driving a **Lancia 1Z armoured car**. **[A: Wikipedia EN]** The **Futurist Political Party**, founded Milan 1918, merges into the Fasci in 1919; no separate stop, stated inside the San Sepolcro campa.

**Piazza San Sepolcro. [A: IT and EN]** 23 March 1919: among the founders of the **Fasci Italiani di Combattimento**; co-writes the programme with **Alceste De Ambris**.

**15 April 1919. [A: Wikipedia IT]** Leads arditi and Futurists against a socialist and anarchist march near the Duomo; **three young workers killed**; the *Avanti!* offices sacked and burned the same day, at an address **not verified; gap**.

**Fiume. [A: Wikipedia IT; corroborated by `dannunzio.journey.json`, which independently names "the futurists" among the city's population]** Joins **D'Annunzio's** occupation and is asked to leave within days. Exact dates **not established**; stop dated 1919-09-01 and hedged. A **relation** (rivalry, pamphlets naming him, a visit to his city), not a coincidence of place.

**The break. [A as to substance and month; city contested]**

- IT Wikipedia: at the **second congress of the Fasci**, **May 1920**, he argues for *svaticanare l'Italia* and against the monarchy, and breaks with the movement's politics by year's end. EN Wikipedia: he **walks out of a Fascist congress** in 1920 and withdraws for roughly three years.
- The interlock pass placed that congress at **Florence**, 24 May 1920; the gathering pass at **Milan**. **Not resolved.** The file pins Milan and prints Florence in `date_confidence`.
- **[A]** He does not abandon Fascism as a cultural allegiance. **Dropped as unverified:** "elected to the Fascist Party's first central committee", found in none of the sources opened this session.

**Mussolini** is named in four campas (flag-burning, San Sepolcro, *Avanti!*, walkout). Justified by relation: co-founder, antagonist in 1920, and the man who later appoints him to the Academy.

## 6. The academician, 1921-1936

**Tattilismo. [A, date corrected by two years]** The pool dated the tactile works **1923**, contaminated by the marriage year. IT Wikipedia dates *Il Tattilismo* to **1921**, first presented in **Paris, January 1921**; that presentation drew the cool reception.

**Marriage. [A: EN and IT]** Marries **Benedetta Cappa**, painter and novelist, pupil of **Giacomo Balla**, met 1918; daughters **Vittoria, Ala, Luce**. The Brazil-tour motive is EN Wikipedia's. Ceremony date and place **not specified; gap**.

**Bologna, 30 March 1925. [A: Wikipedia EN]** Gentile cites Marinetti's "artistic" and "creative" ideas as foundational to Fascism; Marinetti signs the Manifesto of Fascist Intellectuals the same year. The regime's preference for imperial-Roman classicism is the standing tension of his last two decades.

**Buenos Aires, 1926. [A: via `xul_solar.journey.json` and general historiography]** A real South American tour, coincident with the Amigos del Arte modernist show. **No personal relation to Xul Solar is documented**, so he is not named in the campa. Months and venues **not established; gap**.

**Accademia d'Italia. [A for the appointment; "newly founded" corrected]** EN Wikipedia: decree of **7 January 1926**; first thirty members, Marinetti included, approved **13 March 1929**; inaugurated **28 October 1929**. The institution was three years old when he entered it. Plumed hat and ceremonial sword are accurate to its regalia. Coordinates approximate the Palazzo Corsini seat, 155 m south.

**Aeropittura. [A: IT and EN]** Manifesto of 1929 after a flight over the Gulf of La Spezia; Benedetta Cappa a leading exponent. Month **not established**.

**Futurist Cooking. [A: Wikipedia EN]** *Gazzetta del Popolo*, Turin, **28 December 1930**: pasta as a cause of "lassitude, pessimism, and lack of virility", rice in its place, food eventually replaced by pills.

**Ethiopia. [A, date and rank corrected]** The pool gave "1936" and the rank *centurione*. IT Wikipedia's decorations list places him at **Passo Uarieu, 21 January 1936** and at **Uork Amba and Debek Amba, 27-28 February 1936**, implying departure in **1935**, with the rank **seniore**, 2nd CC.NN. Division "28 Ottobre". The pool's claim that these engagements are poorly documented is **false**. **Coordinate correction:** they were fought on the northern front in Tigray, roughly **13.2 N, 39.0 E**, not Addis Ababa (9.032, 38.7469), 470 km south.

## 7. The last years, 1938-1944

**Degenerate Art. [A: Wikipedia EN]** 1938: he persuades Mussolini to bar the Nazi travelling exhibition from Italy. Italy's own Racial Laws pass that November.

**Artecrazia. [A, and the pool's reading inverted]** The most serious substantive correction here. The gathering pass claimed he published articles **opposing antisemitism and the Racial Laws** in 1938. EN Wikipedia says instead that in the **11 January 1939** issue of *Artecrazia* he condemned attacks on modern art "by noting that Futurism was both Italian and nationalist, not foreign, and stating that there were no Jews in Futurism."

That is **1939**, not 1938, and it is a defence of Futurist art made **on antisemitic terms**: "there were no Jews in Futurism" is a plea of racial cleanliness, not opposition to the Racial Laws. The journey file states the defence and states that it concedes its own premise. The regime closed the journal anyway.

**Eastern Front. [A, "Stalingrad" removed]** 1942 into 1943 with **ARMIR**, at sixty-five. The pool said "near Stalingrad"; the Italian Eighth Army held the **middle Don**, 200-300 km northwest, and was destroyed there. Unit movements **not documented; gap**: the Don coordinate stands for the sector, not for a place he is known to have stood.

**RSI. [A: Wikipedia IT]** Adheres to the Italian Social Republic after 1943; dictates several memorial works.

**Death. [A: EN and IT; NYT obituary 3 December 1944]** Cardiac arrest, **Hotel Excelsior Splendide, Bellagio, 2 December 1944**, aged 67, having just completed *Quarto d'ora di poesia della X Mas*. **[A: Wikipedia IT]** A death mask is cast by **Spartaco Di Ciolo**; **present whereabouts not established; gap**.

**Two funerals. [A: Wikipedia IT]** The pool's chronology lens carried only the second.

1. **3 December 1944**: funeral, **Basilica di San Giacomo, Bellagio**.
2. **5 December 1944**: **state funeral, Chiesa di San Sepolcro, Milan**; burial at the **Cimitero Monumentale**.

**Benedetta Cappa** died **15 May 1977** in Venice and was later interred with him. Tomb inscription, design and any later damage **not found; gap**.

**Afterlife. [A: Wikipedia, "Italian Futurism"]** Guggenheim, New York, 2014: *Italian Futurism, 1909-1944: Reconstructing the Universe*, the first comprehensive US survey. The **Estorick Collection**, London, is a principal custodian outside Italy; **coordinate correction** for future use: 51.5439, -0.1000 (39A Canonbury Square). Bellocchio's *Vincere* (2009) uses Futurist visual language.

## 8. Quotations: verbatim, mistranslated, misattributed

Nine quotes across 43 stops, all Flint's 1909 manifesto except one 1911 essay. Three findings:

- **Misattribution, removed.** "I declare that it is far from my idea to encourage anything like a state art." These are **Mussolini's** words, Novecento Italiano exhibition, **1923**; in EN Wikipedia's Marinetti article they sit inside an unfootnoted quotation *of Mussolini*. **Not used anywhere in the journey file.**
- **Translation collision.** "There is no longer any beauty except the struggle" is genuine but is **Doug Thompson's** point 7 (*Critical Writings*, 2006), not Flint's, whose point 7 begins "Except in struggle, there is no more beauty." Since the rest of the file is Flint, the Thompson wording was **not used**.
- **Compression, expanded.** "Prepare the identification of man with the motor" is a paraphrase; the journey file carries the full traced sentence from *Multiplied Man and the Reign of the Machine* (1911), via Berghaus (2000), p. 318, located through Wikiquote. Its `quote_source` records that it was **not verified against the printed page**. The file's weakest citation.

Two items offered as "quotes" in the pool were **Wikipedia's own prose about Marinetti**. Both are used as facts, neither as quotation.

## 9. Open contradictions, not adjudicated

1. **Baccalaureate:** 1893 (IT) vs 1894 (EN).
2. **Law degree city, 1899:** Genoa (IT) vs Pavia (EN). One degree, two universities.
3. **Second congress of the Fasci, May 1920:** Milan (gathering pass) vs Florence, 24 May (interlock pass).
4. **The 1908 crash:** December vs October; and whether the ditch happened in anything like the described form, since Marinetti is the only witness on record.
5. **Trieste January 1910, Milan February 1910:** in Berghaus, absent from both theatres' own articles.

## 10. Named gaps

- Birth house in Alexandria; campus of the Collège Saint-François-Xavier.
- Street number of the *Poesia* office; addresses of the *Gazzetta dell'Emilia* and of *Avanti!* in 1919.
- Venues and dates of the Turin and Naples serate; venues and months of the 1914 Russian and 1926 Argentine lectures.
- Exact place and date of the Isonzo wound; unit movements on the Don.
- Whereabouts of the Di Ciolo death mask; the tomb itself.

- Corpus files read for interlock: the `dannunzio`, `mussolini`, `guido_keller`, `sergei_diaghilev`, `xul_solar` and `alexander` journey files, plus `QUEUE.md`.

**Cited at second hand, not opened this session**
