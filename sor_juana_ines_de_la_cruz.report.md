# Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz, research report

*Juana Ramírez de Asbaje, in religion Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz. Born San Miguel Nepantla, 1648 or 1651; died Convento de San Jerónimo, Mexico City, 17 April 1695. Poet, dramatist, theologian, Hieronymite nun.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source anecdote. Where sources disagree, both readings are printed and neither is adjudicated. Rejected coordinates are tabled at the end.

The structural fact governing this life: **from 24 February 1669 until her death on 17 April 1695 she does not leave one building.** Every place after 1669 on her map is either a room inside San Jerónimo or a place her writing went without her. The journey file preserves that asymmetry.

## 1. Birth and the disputed year (1648 / 1651)

**[A: Wikipedia (Spanish), 'Juana Inés de la Cruz', section Nacimiento]** She is born at the Hacienda de San Miguel Nepantla, near modern Tepetlixpa, State of Mexico, illegitimate daughter of Isabel Ramírez, a criolla, and Pedro Manuel de Asbaje, a Basque captain from Vergara (Bergara) in Guipúzcoa who never marries her mother. Her mother could not write her own name **[R: Paz]**.

**CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED: the birth year.** Two dates compete and the sources cross each other:

- **1648.** A register at Chimalhuacán Chalco, found by Guillermo Ramírez España and published by Alberto G. Salceda in 1952, dated **5 December 1648**, names *Inés, hija de la Iglesia*, granddaughter of Pedro Ramírez and Beatriz Ramírez, no father named **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The documentary side of the case; it agrees with the convent recording her dead at forty-six.
- **1651.** Calleja's *Vida* (1700) gives **13 November 1651** **[A as to Calleja's text; R as to the fact]**: a biographer's date, written by a man who never met her.

**Pool error, corrected before drafting:** the pool had this **inverted**, calling 1651 the register date and 1648 the traditional one. No register names Juana in 1648 and none names Inés in 1651; the 23 July 1651 entry names a **María**, identified by Guillermo Schmidhuber as a younger sister **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The dispute lives in `date_confidence`.

**Gap.** No reachable source identifies the chapel or font of the 1648 baptism. The stop is pinned at the modern town center of Chalco (historic Chimalhuacán Chalco), **19.2647, -98.8975**, and the campa says so.

## 2. Childhood at Panoaya (c. 1651-1656)

**[A: Respuesta a Sor Filotea de la Cruz, 1691]** She tells this whole phase herself, forty years later, in a document written under duress. That is the evidentiary situation for the entire childhood: autobiography written as legal defense, every anecdote doing argumentative work.

- **[R: Respuesta]** At about three she follows an older sister to the *amiga*, the informal girls' school, tells the mistress her mother has sent her, and learns to read. She hides it, expecting punishment.
- **[R: Respuesta]** She gives up cheese because she has heard it makes people stupid. The pool garbled this into a sentence about stopping "the moment she is told learning is what she wants," which is not what the Respuesta says. Corrected.
- **[R: en.wikipedia]** By five she is said to keep household accounts; by eight to have written a *loa* for the Blessed Sacrament and won a book for it.
- **CONTRADICTION INSIDE THE POOL.** The same age-eight Eucharist poem is dated **1656** in one entry and **1658** in another; neither fits both birth years (age eight is 1656 on 1648, 1659 on Calleja's 1651). The entries were **collapsed into one stop**, the arithmetic stated in the campa rather than hidden.
- **CUT.** Latin at age three is a conflation. Latin came later, about twenty lessons from the *bachiller* **Martín de Olivas** in her Mexico City adolescence **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The pool credited those lessons to her confessor Núñez de Miranda; corrected.
- **[A: es.wikipedia]** Her grandfather **Pedro Ramírez** dies (1656); the household breaks up and she is sent to relatives.

**Gaps.** The Amecameca schooling is **regional tradition only**, no document naming a classroom or teacher **[R: Amecameca municipal history]**; kept as a stop, labelled memory rather than archive. Yecapixtla is likewise **[R: Calleja tradition]**, its years called undocumented in the campa.

## 3. Mexico City and the viceregal court (c. 1659-1667)

- **[R: es.wikipedia chronology]** Sent to live with her aunt **María Ramírez** and uncle **Juan de Mata** in Mexico City, for roughly eight years. The house is not identified; the stop is pinned at the historic center generally and the campa says so.
- **[R: Respuesta]** She asks to be dressed as a boy and sent to the **Real y Pontificia Universidad**, which does not admit women. Refused; she never enters as a student. The anecdote survives only in her own retelling.
- **[A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]** In **1664** she enters the household of Vicereine **Leonor Carreto, Marquesa de Mancera**, as lady-in-waiting.
- **[R: Calleja, 1700]** At the viceroy's instigation some **forty** theologians, jurists, philosophers and poets examine her publicly; she answers all of them; Calleja compares her to a royal galleon among small boats.

**TAG DOWNGRADE.** The pool tagged the forty-examiners episode **[A]**. It is not attested in any ordinary sense: no minutes, no list of examiners, no recorded question, and the only source is Calleja's hagiographic *Vida*. **Downgraded to [R]** and marked as tradition in `date_confidence`. Kept because it is canonical, but labelled.

**Geography.** The pool put the viceregal palace on the north side of the Zócalo. It is the **east** side; the north side is the cathedral. Corrected, with the coordinate (see table below).

## 4. The two convents (1667-1669)

- **[A: convent records via en/es.wikipedia]** **14 August 1667**: enters the Discalced Carmelites of **San José** as a postulant, and leaves within months, before profession, the rule's austerity having broken her health.
- **[A: standard consensus]** **24 February 1669**: professes among the Hieronymites at the **Convento de Santa Paula (San Jerónimo)**, taking the name Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz. She chooses the order for its looser rule: private cell, personal property, servants, visitors at the grille.
- **[A: attributed, en.wikipedia]** Her stated reason: "to have no fixed occupation which might curtail my freedom to study." A longer variant circulates from the Respuesta. The shorter is used in the journey file, with its source named.

**Gap.** The colonial footprint of the Carmelite house of San José is not fixed by surviving maps; the coordinate (**19.4326, -99.137**) is approximate and the campa says so.

**The confessor.** Antonio Núñez de Miranda, SJ, urged her toward religious life **[R: es.wikipedia]**: confessor and promoter of the vocation, not Latin tutor. See §2.

## 5. The cell, the library, the locutorio (1669-1690)

- **[A: en.wikipedia; the figure is the encyclopedia's, not an inventory]** A private library described as one of the largest in the Americas, commonly given as about **4,000 volumes**, with scientific and musical instruments. **No shelf list survives.** The figure is repeated, not documented, and the campa says so.
- **[A: correspondence and dedications in her printed works]** The *locutorio*, the visiting parlor with its grille, is for two decades the nearest thing New Spain has to a salon.
- **[A: Respuesta]** The kitchen passage: the egg in oil versus in syrup, sugar and its stages, "had Aristotle cooked he would have written a great deal more." The short form quoted in the journey file ("One can perfectly well philosophize while cooking supper") is **attributed**, and per en.wikipedia echoes an Aragonese poet and St Teresa of Ávila; sourced as attributed, not as her verbatim sentence.
- **[A: es.wikipedia chronology]** **1673**: the Manceras depart; Leonor Carreto dies en route to Veracruz.

**CONTRADICTION FLAG.** The pool gives 1673 for Carreto's death; other accounts give 1674. Not resolved; the stop carries a year-only hedge. **No coordinate for the death site was available**, so it sits at San Jerónimo, where the news reaches her, rather than at an invented point on the Veracruz road.

## 6. The productive decade (1676-1692)

- **[A: Villancicos, 1676-1691]** Villancico sets for the cathedrals of Mexico and Puebla across fifteen years, carrying Nahuatl and the voices of enslaved Africans and indigenous worshippers.
- **[A: Neptuno alegórico, 1680]** The allegorical program for the triumphal arch at the entry of the Marqués de la Laguna. The text survives; the arch, built for one procession, does not.
- **[A: Paz, 1982]** **María Luisa Manrique de Lara, Condesa de Paredes**: the central friendship and the decisive patronage. She carries the manuscripts to Spain.
- **[A: dated performance record]** **4 October 1683**: *Los empeños de una casa* staged for celebrations around the birth of the Count of Paredes' son.
- **[A: en.wikipedia]** **1689**: *Inundación castálida*, Madrid, press of Juan García Infanzón.
- **[A: en.wikipedia]** **1689**: *El divino Narciso*, the auto sacramental whose loa argues an Aztec rite of the god of seeds into a figure of the Eucharist.

**CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED: the premiere of *Amor es más laberinto*.** The main biographical articles in both languages give **11 February 1689**; the es.wikipedia article on the play gives **11 January 1689**, staged for the accession of the Conde de Galve. The journey file uses **1689-01-11** and states the disagreement in the campa rather than silently picking. Also corrected: the occasion is a **new viceroy's accession**, not "the inauguration of a new viceroyalty."

**CORRECTION: the Segundo volumen.** The pool placed it at **Barcelona, 1693** in two separate lenses. The first edition is **Sevilla, Tomás López de Haro, 1692** **[A: es.wikipedia]**; Barcelona 1693 is a reprint. This matters because **Primero sueño first appears in print in that 1692 Seville volume**.

**On *Primero sueño*.** **[A]** Composed around 1685, printed 1692; near a thousand lines; she describes it as the only thing she wrote for her own pleasure rather than on commission.

**FLAG, a claim NOT in the verified research pool.** The *Primero sueño* campa names **Athanasius Kircher** as a source she reads and mines. This is standard Sor Juana scholarship (Paz treats her Kircherian sources at length), but **it appears nowhere in the pool supplied here** and was not verified in this pass. It is the only intertraveler link in any campa, named for a relation she had to a body of writing rather than shared geography. Treat as **[R]** pending Paz.

## 7. The Carta Atenagórica and the Respuesta (1690-1691)

- **[A: en/es.wikipedia]** A private critique of a decades-old sermon by the Portuguese Jesuit **António Vieira** on the greatest *fineza* of Christ.
- **[A: Carta de Sor Filotea de la Cruz, Puebla, 1690]** **November 1690**: Bishop **Manuel Fernández de Santa Cruz** prints it without her consent as the *Carta Atenagórica*, prefacing his own letter signed **"Sor Filotea de la Cruz,"** praising her intellect and urging her from secular to sacred study.
- **[A: the letter survives and is widely anthologized]** **1 March 1691**: *La Respuesta a Sor Filotea de la Cruz*.
- **[A: Autodefensa Espiritual, 1690, Tapia Méndez edition 1993]** "But who has prohibited women private and individual studies? Do they not have a rational soul like men?"

**Note.** She never travels to Puebla; the crisis reaches her as a printed book. The Puebla stop is pinned at the episcopal seat (**19.0428, -98.1984**) as the place the pressure originates, and the campa says she is not there.

**Gap.** The bishop's own sentence urging her toward sacred study exists in the pool only as **the encyclopedia's paraphrase**, so it is **not used as a quote** anywhere in the journey file.

## 8. The renunciation (1692-1694)

- **[A: en/es.wikipedia]** **1692**: flood, grain shortage, riot and famine in Mexico City. By **1693** she has effectively stopped producing new work, amid mounting church pressure and the loss of her protectors.
- **[A: en.wikipedia, the sourcing itself flagging the dispute]** **1694**: she sells or surrenders the library and the instruments, proceeds to the poor. **Whether this was voluntary or coerced is disputed in the sources and left open here.**
- **[A: surviving convent documents]** **1694**: she signs a renewal of vows and a document of self-condemnation. The phrase circulates in two forms: **"Yo, la peor de todas"** (en.wikipedia) and **"yo, la peor del mundo"** (es.wikipedia). Both are printed in the journey file's quote source; neither is adjudicated.

**CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED: the blood signature.** The pool held two entries calling the blood apocryphal and its own correction notes overturned both. The honest position: the **document survives** (the convent's *Libro de Profesiones* / *Protesta de la fe* of 1694), and en.wikipedia carries an image of a manuscript page captioned as one **"which Sor Juana signed in ink and her own blood."** The detail is attached to an **extant artefact**, not floating free. Contested are **the medium** and **the degree of coercion**, not the document. Tagged **[R]** and written that way.

## 9. Death and burial (1695)

- **[A: es.wikipedia]** **17 February 1695**: Antonio Núñez de Miranda dies.
- **[A: en/es.wikipedia, agreeing]** **17 April 1695**, about four in the morning: she dies, having taken an illness while nursing sisters during an epidemic in the convent.
- **[A: es.wikipedia]** Buried the same day; **Sigüenza y Góngora** delivers the eulogy.
- **[R: en.wikipedia]** More than a hundred works are referenced in period records; only a fraction survive.

**Gap.** No marked tomb was preserved and the interior was altered over later centuries **[A: pool's afterlife lens]**.

## 10. Afterlife

- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED:** *Fama y obras póstumas del Fénix de México* (Madrid, Ruiz de Murga), carrying Calleja's *Vida*, is dated **1700** by the Spanish sources and **1701** by the English ones. Not adjudicated; the file uses 1700 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
- **[A: es.wikipedia; identification explicitly uncorroborated]** **1978**: excavation at the former convent uncovers bones and a medallion resembling those in her portraits, presented as her remains. "Nunca pudo corroborarse su autenticidad." **Disputed identification, not established fact.**
- **[A: en.wikipedia, citing New York Times coverage; primary article not consulted in this pass]** **14 November 1995**: Margarita López Portillo returns the medallion to Congress after a deputy demands it.
- **[A: en.wikipedia]** **1979**: the former convent becomes the **Universidad del Claustro de Sor Juana**. **April 1995**: her name inscribed in gold on the Mexican Congress's wall of honor.
- **[A: en.wikipedia]** Currency: 1000-peso note (1978-1992), 200-peso note (1994-2020), 100-peso "G" series since 2020. Village renamed **Nepantla de Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz**. **2022**: approved for the Episcopal Church's liturgical calendar, feast day 18 April.
- **[A: es/en.wikipedia]** Monuments in Chapultepec (**19.4204, -99.1813**, the park, not the monument), in Madrid (site and date not established), and before the Claustro.
- **[A: en.wikipedia]** **1982**: Octavio Paz, *Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz o las trampas de la fe*, credited with re-establishing her centrality; feminist retranslation of the *Respuesta* from the 1980s onward.

## 11. Interlocks: what was rejected, and why

The interlock lens returned seventeen candidates. **Sixteen were rejected** under the rule that shared geography or shared century is not a relation. Recorded so the rejections are visible:

- **Cortés, Diego Rivera, Frida Kahlo, Humboldt, Guamán Poma, Louis XIV, Juana Azurduy, Manuela Sáenz, Policarpa Salavarrieta**: co-location in Mexico City or the Spanish-American colonial world, or a shared given name. No relation to her person or her reading. Rejected.
- **Giordano Bruno, Tommaso Campanella, Pico della Mirandola, Matteo Ricci**: structurally parallel cases, religious-order intellectuals colliding with church authority, the convent-for-library bargain. Illuminating parallels, and the map already shows them. No evidence she read or knew any of them. Rejected as name-drops.
- **Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262) and Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354)**: the lens itself flagged these as non-connections for a nun who never left New Spain. Not inherited, and no stop was invented to carry them.
- **Athanasius Kircher: ACCEPTED**, named in one campa. She read him and used him; the relation runs through the books. Flagged in §6 as a claim outside the verified pool.

Legitimate interlocks with **no existing traveler file**: Sigüenza y Góngora, Núñez de Miranda, María Luisa Manrique de Lara, Fernández de Santa Cruz, Vieira, Calleja. Named in campas as historical persons, not cross-references.
