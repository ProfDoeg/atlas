# Emmy Noether (1882-1935): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-24 for `emmy_noether.journey.json`.
Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, later recollection,
or a claim carried from the dossier without a primary fetch. Contradictions are flagged and
**not adjudicated**.*

Spine: MacTutor (St Andrews) and the University of Göttingen memorial page, cross-checked
against en. and de.wikipedia and, for every coordinate, against Nominatim. Four texts were
fetched whole and are quoted directly: Weyl's funeral address of 18 April 1935, Einstein's

## 1. Erlangen: the professor's daughter (1882-1900)

Born Amalie Emmy Noether at Hauptstraße 23, Erlangen, 23 March 1882, into an assimilated
Jewish family **[A MacTutor]**. Elementary school on Fahrstraße, then from 1889 the Städtische
Höhere Töchterschule on Friedrichstraße: languages, arithmetic, music, domestic subjects, and
not the Gymnasium curriculum that opened a university **[A MacTutor]**. The family moves to a
larger apartment on Nürnberger Straße in 1892 **[A MacTutor]**. In 1900 she passes the Bavarian
state examination to teach English and French in girls' schools, graded very good overall,
weakest in classroom delivery, and never takes a teaching post **[A MacTutor]**.

**Correction to the dossier.** Max Noether came to Erlangen in 1875 as *ausserordentlicher*
professor and was named ordinary professor only on 16 April 1888 **[A MacTutor,
Biographies/Noether_Max]**. The dossier's flat "professor from 1875" is wrong in rank and is
not used.

**Gaps.** No source names the building the 1900 examination was sat in; that stop uses the
corpus canonical Erlangen pin and says so. A family recollection of her solving a logic puzzle
at a children's gathering is **[R]**, a later story with no contemporaneous record, and is not
staged.

## 2. The credential won twice (1900-1907)

- 1900-1902: audits at Erlangen by each lecturer's individual permission, one of two women
  among roughly a thousand students **[A MacTutor]**.
- 14 July 1903: passes the external matriculation examination at a Realgymnasium in Nuremberg
  **[A MacTutor]**. *Gap:* which Realgymnasium is unstated; the pin is city centre.
- Winter 1903-04: audits at Göttingen under Hilbert, Klein, Minkowski, Schwarzschild and
  Blumenthal **[A MacTutor]**.
- 24 October 1904: matriculates at Erlangen once Bavaria permits it **[A MacTutor]**.
- 13 December 1907: doctoral oral summa cum laude under Paul Gordan, on invariants of ternary
  biquadratic forms; the published thesis enumerates 331 covariants by Gordan's computational
  methods **[A MacTutor]**.
- Of the thesis and the invariant-theory papers after it she used one word: "crap"
  **[A Dick, archive.org OCR]**. Only the word is verbatim Noether; the framing sentence is
  Dick's, and the journey's `quote_source` says so.

## 3. Seven years without salary (1908-1915)

Unpaid work at Erlangen's Mathematical Institute from 1908, publishing, supervising, and
substituting in her father's courses; habilitation closed to women by law and custom
**[A MacTutor]**. Addresses the Deutsche Mathematiker-Vereinigung at Salzburg in 1909 on
invariant theory and joins the society that year **[A MacTutor]**. Lectures to the DMV in
Vienna in 1913 on rational function fields and visits the aging Franz Mertens **[A MacTutor]**.

On Ernst Fischer: "Above all I am indebted to Mr E Fischer from whom I received the decisive
impulse to study abstract algebra from an arithmetical viewpoint, and this remained the
governing idea for all my later work" **[A MacTutor, verbatim on the biography page]**.
*Gap:* MacTutor does not date the remark and its original context was not located; the journey
dates that stop 1911 purely to order the segment and states this in `date_confidence`.

## 4. Göttingen: Invariante Variationsprobleme (1915-1919)

- Spring 1915: Hilbert and Klein bring her to Göttingen for the invariant-theoretic problems
  thrown up by general relativity and Hilbert's variational formulation of gravitation. No
  position, no pay **[A Göttingen memorial page]**.
- May 1915: her mother Ida Amalia Kaufmann Noether dies suddenly **[R]**. Attributed by the
  dossier to Dick; the exact month was not independently confirmed.
- 20 July 1915: the habilitation petition. Hilbert and Klein support it; the Philosophical
  Faculty declines on grounds of sex, and Hilbert thereafter advertises courses under his own
  name with her lecturing **[A Göttingen memorial page]**.
- 26 July 1918: Klein reads *Invariante Variationsprobleme* into the record of the Royal Society
  of Sciences, she being without venia legendi **[A Wikipedia for the date; A arXiv
  physics/0503066 for the publication record, Gött. Nachr. 1918, pp. 235-257]**.
- 1919: habilitation accepted after the revolution removes the bar **[A Göttingen memorial page
  for the year]**. The precise 4 June 1919 and a May oral are **[R]**, dossier-only.

**The two famous lines of 1915, both hedged, neither used as a `quote`.**
(a) Hilbert's "we are a university, not a bathhouse" survives through later recollection;
Wikipedia notes his exact words were not preserved.
(b) The soldiers objection. The dossier's fragment "at the feet of a woman" is too short to
quote. Full reported wording, from an unnamed faculty member at a joint Göttingen
philosophical-faculty meeting in early 1915: "What will our soldiers think when they return to
the university and find that they are required to learn at the feet of a woman?" Citable to
Clark Kimberling, "Emmy Noether and Her Influence," in Brewer and Smith (eds.), *Emmy Noether:
A Tribute to Her Life and Work* (Marcel Dekker, 1981), p. 14, and Lederman and Hill, *Symmetry
and the Beautiful Universe* (2004), p. 72. **Correction:** the dossier's attribution to Dick is
wrong. The phrase does not occur in Dick's full OCR; Dick p. 32 renders a different objection,
about a Privatdozent becoming a professor and entering the University Senate. No primary
transcript of either line survives.

## 5. The Noether school (1919-1932)

- Winter semester 1919-20: lecturing in her own name, still unpaid **[A MacTutor]**.
- 1921: *Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen*, the ascending chain condition made central; the eponym
  Noetherian ring descends from it **[A MacTutor]**.
- 13 December 1921: Max Noether dies at Erlangen **[A MacTutor]**. (The coincidence with the
  calendar date of her 1907 oral is arithmetic on two attested dates, not a source's claim.)
- 1922: the unsalaried title *nichtbeamteter a.o. Professor* **[A MacTutor]**.
- 1923: *Lehrbeauftragte für Algebra*, a small fixed stipend, her first paid position, at 41
  **[A Göttingen memorial page]**. **Correction:** the dossier's added clause that this enabled
  her to supervise doctoral examinations is unsupported by anything checked and is dropped. The
  1922 title carried standing; the 1923 Lehrauftrag carried money.
- 1924: *Abstrakter Aufbau der Idealtheorie in algebraischen Zahlkörpern* **[A MacTutor]**.
- Seminars run at the blackboard; students include Grete Hermann, Max Deuring, Hans Fitting,
  Ernst Witt and Chiungtze Tsen **[A MacTutor]**.
- September 1928: invited sectional address at the ICM in Bologna **[A Göttingen memorial page;
  A MacTutor]**. *Gap:* congress hall unconfirmed; the pin is the corpus canonical Bologna pin.
- Winter 1928-29: visiting professor at Moscow State University, working with Alexandrov,
  Pontryagin and Chebotaryov **[A MacTutor; A Wikipedia for the 1928-1929 span]**.
  **Correction:** the dossier's "1928" alone is wrong.
- 1930: visiting teaching at Frankfurt **[A MacTutor]**.
- September 1932: first woman to give an ICM plenary lecture, at Zürich; next is Uhlenbeck,
  58 years later **[A Göttingen memorial page; A MacTutor]**.
- 1932: shares the Alfred Ackermann-Teubner Memorial Prize with Emil Artin **[A same]**. Weyl's
  failed 1932 attempt to have her elected to the Göttingen Academy is **[R]**, dossier-only.

## 6. The law of the seventh of April (1933)

**The one place the pool conflated two events; the report keeps them apart.**

- 30 January 1933: Hitler becomes chancellor **[A MacTutor]**.
- April 1933: notice under paragraph 3 of the Law for the Restoration of the Professional Civil
  Service of 7 April, a Beurlaubung **[A Wikipedia, quoting the Prussian Ministry letter]**.
- **13 September 1933:** the Lehrbefugnis finally withdrawn **[A de.wikipedia: "wurde Noether am
  13. September 1933 die Lehrbefugnis endgültig entzogen"]**. *Contradiction flagged, not
  resolved:* the pool's geography lens gave 2 September 1933 and cited the Göttingen memorial
  page, which gives no date at all. The eleven-day discrepancy is a dossier artifact;
  13 September is used and the disagreement is stated in the stop.
- To Helmut Hasse, 10 May 1933: "Many thanks for your dear, compassionate letter! I must say,
  though, that this thing is much less terrible for me than it is for many others. At least I
  have a small inheritance (I never was entitled to a pension, anyway) which allows me to sit
  back for a while and see." **[A Dick, OCR fetched and confirmed verbatim]**
- Summer 1933: seminars continue in her own apartment. Sole source is Weyl's 1935 funeral
  address: "Even those in their brown shirts were welcome; never for a second did you doubt
  their integrity." **[A Weyl]** A memorial recollection two years after the fact, not a
  contemporaneous record; the apartment address was not located, so the stop sits at the
  canonical Göttingen pin.
- October 1933: sails aboard the SS Bremen **[A MacTutor]**. The port is **[R]**: Bremerhaven is
  inferred as North German Lloyd's terminal and the Bremen's standard port, named in no fetched
  source.
- 7 November 1933: arrival in New York **[R]**, dossier-only; no pier documented.
- **[R]** The Bryn Mawr offer: a one-year research professorship at 4,000 dollars, arranged by
  president Marion Edwards Park with the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced German
  Scholars, dated 4 August 1933. Dossier-only; see the unreachable-sources list.

## 7. Bryn Mawr and Princeton (1933-1935)

- Winter term 1933-34: first seminar, three students and one staff participant, working through
  van der Waerden's *Moderne Algebra* **[A MacTutor; A Wikipedia]**.
- February 1934: weekly lectures at the Institute for Advanced Study on Flexner's and Veblen's
  invitation, with Albert, Vandiver, Brauer and Weyl attending **[A MacTutor; A Wikipedia]**.
  The IAS had no building of its own until 1939 and used old Fine Hall; the pin is inherited
  byte-identical from `dirac.journey.json`.
- **[R]** The "men's university, where nothing female is admitted" remark. The IAS institutional
  history page returned **HTTP 403** on fetch, so it could not be confirmed; it is reported as
  attributed in prose and is not used as a `quote`.
- Summer 1934: last return to Germany. She sees her brother Fritz, visits Göttingen, meets Emil
  Artin at Hamburg **[A MacTutor]**. Natascha Artin's underground anecdote (*Ideale, Gruppen,
  Führer*) is **[R]**, recorded decades later.
- 14 April 1935: surgery for a pelvic mass, three normal days, then on the fourth circulatory
  collapse, unconsciousness, and a temperature of 109 F (42.8 C). Dies at Bryn Mawr Hospital,
  cause given cautiously as probable postoperative infection **[A Wikipedia for the 109 F;
  A MacTutor for the four-day course]**.

## 8. The ashes in the cloister (1935 and after)

- 18 April 1935: small service at president Park's house; Weyl and Brauer come from Princeton;
  Weyl delivers the graveside address **[A St Andrews]**. His long published memorial address is
  a separate text from these remarks, and the distinction is kept.
- Cremation, urn interred under the walkway around the cloister of the Old Library, the former
  M. Carey Thomas Library **[A en.wikipedia; A de.wikipedia]**.
- 1 May 1935 written at Princeton, 4 May published: Einstein's letter to the *New York Times*
  **[A St Andrews, fetched verbatim]**. **Correction:** the dossier's note that the page renders
  an umlaut-less "Fraulein" is wrong; the raw HTML reads "Fräulein Noether."
- **26 April 1935, Goodhart Hall: contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Neither the date nor the
  venue could be confirmed against a Bryn Mawr record; what is confirmable is only that Weyl's
  graveside speech was 18 April. The stop is kept with the doubt carried in `date_confidence`.

## Further contradictions and corrections

1. **Fritz Noether.** Mis-tagged **[R]** by the dossier and wrong in its details. Documented
   **[A Wikipedia, "Fritz Noether"]**: emigrated to the USSR and took the Tomsk chair in 1934;
   arrested by the NKVD at Tomsk November 1937; sentenced to 25 years 23 October 1938; sentenced
   to death 8 September 1941; **shot at Oryol 11 September 1941** in the Medvedev Forest
   massacre, not at Tomsk; rehabilitated 22 December 1988. This postdates her death by six
   years, so it gets no stop: one clause in the Hamburg campa, where she actually saw him, plus
   a `suggested_ref`.
2. **The slogan.** "Underlies every conservation law in physics" is an overreach **[R, Nina
   Byers, UCLA CWP]**: the first theorem applies to systems with a finite-dimensional continuous
   symmetry group and a Lagrangian formulation. The journey states what the theorems prove.
3. **"Einstein's tutor,"** the title of Lee Phillips's 2024 trade biography, is a later metaphor
   **[R]**. No tutor-pupil relation is documented; the channel ran through Hilbert and Klein.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Emmy Noether is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are licensed. Per the
adversary's duty nothing is silently deleted, and two candidates are recorded here:

- **Forward, caught and withheld.** Her first theorem is the standard modern derivation of
  conserved currents in gauge field theory and underwrites conservation bookkeeping in the
  Standard Model, work entirely postdating her 1935 death and which she never discussed.
  Reaching forward would need an author's grant of the kind recorded for Burroughs and Derrida.
  The file therefore says only what the 1918 paper proves and what the eponyms are named after.
  Flagged for the operator to rule on.
- **Backward, none found.** Her work is not a reading of any earlier corpus subject's material.
  The nearest candidate, her axiomatization of Dedekind-type ideal theory, is a real
  one-directional citation relation, but there is no `dedekind` file to interlock against.

**Shared geography deliberately not written as interlocks.** The interlock lens surfaced
Göttingen and Princeton overlaps with Heisenberg, Bohr, Oppenheimer, Dirac, von Neumann, Pauli,
Schrödinger and Gauss. Every one is co-location only: same institute, adjacent years, no
attested meeting, correspondence or citation with Noether in anything consulted. None is named
in a campa; the map already shows a viewer that the pins coincide. Only **Felix Klein** and
**Albert Einstein** are named, both on a real relation: Klein recruited her to Göttingen, argued
for her habilitation, and personally read the 1918 paper into the Society's record; Einstein's
general theory is the problem her theorems were commissioned to solve, and he wrote her
obituary. Weyl, Werner Weber and Grete Hermann all have substantive relations to her and no
journey file here, so no interlock exists either way.
