# Gilles Deleuze (1925-1995): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-03. Slug `gilles_deleuze`. Companion: `gilles_deleuze.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim the sources do not carry. Contradictions are flagged, never adjudicated. Corrected coordinates are given with the source that fixes them.

**Method.** Nothing re-fetched here. Worked from the pipeline's verified pool, whose verifier corrections override the original claims and are reproduced below rather than applied silently.

---

## 1. Birth and the Occupation, 1925-1944

- Born 18 January 1925 in Paris to Louis Deleuze, engineer and veteran of the first war, and Odette Camaüer; comfortable, conservative household **[A: EN and FR Wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no source names the building, so 48.8848/2.3122 is a centroid for the 17th arrondissement, not an address.
- **Lycée Carnot**, 145 boulevard Malesherbes **[A: EN and FR Wikipedia; listed among Carnot alumni on the school's own FR page]**. **Coordinate corrected** to **48.8844/2.3081** (48°53'04"N 2°18'29"E); the pool's 48.8836/2.3095 was 130 m off.
- **Dating problem, flagged.** The pool anchored Carnot to 1942 and the Guy Môquet classmate detail to 1941, after Deauville. Neither holds: Môquet (1924-1941) was arrested October 1940 and shot October 1941, so any overlap falls **before** the Normandy year. **No source checked gives any year for Deleuze at Carnot.** The stop is dated 1939-09-01 with the reconstruction declared in `date_confidence`. **[R]**
- 1940-1941 schooled at **Deauville** **[A: SEP]**, where a teacher first turns him to literature. **Conflict:** FR Wikipedia names **Pierre Halbwachs** at Deauville, the IEP **Pierre Vial** at Carnot the year before. Both carried. **[A for the year away, R for the teacher]**
- Older brother **Georges** arrested for Resistance activity, dies during deportation **[A: SEP, IEP, EN Wikipedia]**. Three open problems:
  1. **No year is attested.** All three sources omit it; the pool's "1944" was flagged by the verifier as an inference by the research model, not a sourced date, and downgraded to **[R]**. The file uses 1944-01-01 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
  2. **Manner of death contradicted.** SEP and IEP: **on the train** toward Auschwitz. FR Lycée Carnot page: a Deleuze alumnus "Résistant, déporté et mort en camp de concentration", i.e. **in the camp**. Not adjudicated.
  3. **Drancy is not documented for him.** The transit stop rests on the standard routing of Paris-region convoys **[R]**. **Coordinate corrected** to **48.9197/2.4550** (Cité de la Muette); the pool's 48.9187/2.4306 was 1.8 km west.

## 2. Formation, 1944-1948

**The largest unresolved contradiction in the file: where was the khâgne?** SEP and IEP say **Lycée Henri IV** (IEP dating completion 1945); FR Wikipedia says **Louis-le-Grand**, with Beaufret's Henri-IV courses attended separately. The verifier downgraded the Henri IV version to **[R]** and moved the date to 1945; the two schools stand four hundred metres apart on one hill. The file puts the khâgne at Louis-le-Grand (48.8478/2.3444, confirmed for 123 rue Saint-Jacques) with a separate Henri-IV stop (48.8459/2.3478), and states the conflict in `date_confidence`. **Not resolved.**

- **Sorbonne**: history of philosophy under **Alquié, Canguilhem, Gandillac, Hyppolite**, with Bachelard and Wahl in the faculty; Claude Lanzmann a fellow student; DES thesis 1947; **agrégation 1948, placed eighth** **[A: EN and FR Wikipedia]**.
- **Correction applied.** The geography lens attached those four teachers to the khâgne; the verifier flagged them as his **Sorbonne** teachers, as the chronology lens has it. Moved accordingly.
- **Pin inheritance.** Sorbonne pinned **48.8489/2.3435**, byte-identical to `claude_levi_strauss.journey.json`, not the geography lens's 48.8496/2.3435. Lévi-Strauss took his licence and agrégation in the same building twenty years earlier, and is named in the campa **[A: interlock lens]**.

## 3. The lycée years and the first book, 1948-1957

**Correction, load-bearing.** The pool gave "from 1948 to 1957 he teaches at various lycées". The 1948 start is **unsupported**; EN Wikipedia says only "until 1957". FR Wikipedia gives Louis-Thuillier, Amiens 1950-1952; Pothier, Orléans 1952-1955; Louis-le-Grand 1955-1957 **[A]**. **The years 1948-1950 are unaccounted for by any source checked [Gap]**, stated in the Amiens campa.

- *Empiricism and Subjectivity* (Hume) 1953 **[A: SEP]**, then silence for nine years.
- He later calls **1953-1962** "a hole in my life" and contracts the chronic respiratory illness then **[A: SEP]**. Carried as reported speech in a campa, not as a `quote`: no verbatim primary text was supplied.
- Marries **Denise Paule "Fanny" Grandjouan**, translator of D. H. Lawrence, 1956 **[A for fact and year: EN Wikipedia]**. **Neither source names the arrondissement or the ceremony [Gap]**; the pool called its central-Paris coordinate a placeholder and tagged the item **[R]**. Folded into the Louis-le-Grand return stop rather than given an invented pin.
- 1957: **assistant at the Faculté des Lettres**, University of Paris **[A: FR Wikipedia]**.

## 4. Lyon, the CNRS, Foucault, 1960-1968

- **Correction, place.** The pool put the 1960 CNRS fellowship in Paris. It was **Lyon**: "En 1960, il est nommé attaché de recherche du CNRS à Lyon", and there he becomes a friend of **Michel Foucault** **[A: FR Wikipedia]**.
- **Correction, coordinate.** The pool's 45.7620/4.8348 lands near Cordeliers in the 2nd arrondissement, 1.3 km north. The FR quai Claude-Bernard page gives **45.7500/4.8353**, used for both Lyon stops.
- Son **Julien** born Lyon **May 1960** **[A; R for the site, no clinic named]**. Daughter **Émilie** born **1964** **[A: FR Wikipedia]**.
- **Correction, rank.** The pool called him a professor at Lyon from 1964; FR Wikipedia gives **chargé d'enseignement**, 1964-1969. **[A for the French rank; the professorship is R and contradicted.]**
- Books, all **[A: SEP]**: *Nietzsche and Philosophy* 1962, *Kant's Critical Philosophy* 1963, *Proust and Signs* 1964, *Bergsonism* 1966.
- **1966, Clermont-Ferrand.** Foucault tries to have Deleuze appointed beside him; the university overrules it, wanting neither man **[A: michel_foucault.journey.json]**. Canonical pin inherited byte-identical: **45.7772/3.0870**.
- **1966, Baltimore.** The Johns Hopkins colloquium (18 October) convenes Barthes, Lacan and Derrida; Foucault conspicuously absent **[A: roland_barthes.journey.json]**. **Deleuze's absence is [R]**: no source records an invitation either way, the interlock lens offering the parallel as extendable to him rather than documented. He is habitually named in the period's "structuralism" cluster, though the Foucault file does not name him **[R]**. Canonical pin: **39.3299/-76.6205**.
- **1968.** Theses defended: *Difference and Repetition* and *Expressionism in Philosophy: Spinoza* **[A: SEP]**. Tuberculosis diagnosed **[A: EN Wikipedia]**.
- **Correction, over-claim.** The pool put the post-thoracoplasty convalescence at **Saint-Léonard-de-Noblat**, tagged **[A]**. FR Wikipedia says only "en convalescence dans le Limousin avec sa femme": the region, not the town, which is documented **only as the burial place**. Downgraded to **[R]**; the campa says the Limousin and admits the borrowed pin.

## 5. Vincennes, Guattari, the collaboration, 1968-1980

- **Correction, the seminar's start.** The pool had weekly seminars from 1969. The 1969 **nomination** is confirmed **[A: EN Wikipedia, FR Paris VIII]**, but the FR Vincennes page says Foucault "sollicite d'abord Gilles Deleuze, **qui ne viendra à Vincennes que deux ans plus tard**". Seminars therefore begin circa 1970-1971 **[A for the delay; R for the first term]**. Split into a 1968-12 nomination stop and a 1971-01 seminar stop carrying the hedge.
- **Pin inheritance.** Vincennes pinned **48.8434/2.4415**, byte-identical to `michel_foucault.journey.json`. The geography lens's 48.8288/2.4392 was flagged as an unsourced estimate: no source gives coordinates for the campus in the Bois de Vincennes.
- **Where did he meet Guattari? Open.** EN Wikipedia and IEP: **Vincennes**, after May 1968. FR Wikipedia: the **Limousin convalescence** the same year. **[R]**; pinned to canonical Paris (48.8566/2.3522) as a declared placeholder.
- **La Borde.** Guattari has worked at the Cour-Cheverny clinic since 1955 and it is routinely called the ground of the collaboration. **Neither the clinic's FR page nor the Guattari page states that Deleuze ever visited [R; the stop records the gap].**
- **Collège de France, a documented absence.** Foucault elected 1970, Lévi-Strauss holding a chair from 1959; **Deleuze never receives one** **[R]**. Canonical pin inherited: **48.8479/2.3447**.
- **GIP.** Founded from Foucault's flat February 1971; Deleuze a co-signer and participant with Foucault, Sartre and Genet, **though the Foucault file's GIP stop names only Foucault, Vidal-Naquet and Domenach [R]**. That file also records **Luis Alberto Spinetta**, twenty-one, in Paris in March 1971 reading Foucault and **Artaud**, the current Deleuze's Artaud belongs to **[R]**. Both named in the campa.
- ***Anti-Oedipus***, 1972, Éditions de Minuit **[A: SEP]**. **Coordinate corrected** to **48.8531/2.3317** for 7 rue Bernard-Palissy; the afterlife lens's 48.8534/2.3339 was 165 m east and disagreed with the geography lens.
- **Cerisy-la-Salle, partly resolved.** "Nietzsche aujourd'hui" is now **confirmed and dated to 1972** **[A]**, but **Deleuze's participation, the paper "Pensée nomade" in the 1973 proceedings, remains unconfirmed [R]**. **Coordinate corrected** to **49.0219/-1.2886**; the pool's was 4.5 km off.
- **The single trip abroad**, 1975, United States **[A: SEP]**. **No source names the city, host or occasion [Gap]**; New York (40.7128/-74.0060) is marked in the campa as the commonest guess and nothing better. Foucault's 1975 Zabriskie Point trip is the contemporaneous crossing **[R]**.
- *Kafka: Toward a Minor Literature* 1975; *A Thousand Plateaus* 1980 **[A: SEP]**.

## 6. Saint-Denis and the last books, 1980-1991

- **Coordinate corrected, against the pool's own inconsistency.** The 1980 relocation to Saint-Denis, between the rue de la Liberté, the avenue Lénine and the avenue de Stalingrad, and the razing of Vincennes 27-29 August 1980, are **[A: FR Paris-VIII]**. The geography lens gave 48.9367/2.3543 (1.2 km short) and the afterlife lens 48.9459/2.3609 for the same campus; FR Wikipedia gives **48.944722/2.363333**. The file uses **48.9447/2.3633**.
- Publications, all **[A: SEP]**: *Francis Bacon* 1981, *Cinema I* 1983, *Cinema II* 1985, *Foucault* 1986, *The Fold* 1988, *What Is Philosophy?* with Guattari 1991.
- **Foucault dies at the Pitié-Salpêtrière 25 June 1984 [A: michel_foucault.journey.json]**; **Barthes died at the same hospital 26 March 1980** after being struck near the Sorbonne **[A: roland_barthes.journey.json]**. Canonical pin inherited: **48.8385/2.3648**. The interlock reading that all three Parisian termini sit within a few kilometres is **[R]**, an observation and not a document.
- Retirement 1987 **[A: SEP, EN and FR]**; the month is inferred from the academic calendar.

## 7. The last years, the death, the afterlife

- *Postscript on the Societies of Control*, October 59 (1992) **[A]**. Severe respiratory illness, lung surgery, writing become laborious physical effort **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. Final apartment **84 avenue Niel, 17th arrondissement**, the district of his birth **[A: FR Wikipedia]**.
- **Two coordinate and address problems.** For the same avenue Niel address the geography lens gives **48.8815/2.2960** and the afterlife lens **48.8807/2.2947**, about 130 m apart, neither carrying a correction; the file uses the geography value. Separately, interlock item 16 says he was "born 1925 at rue Daubenton, died 1995 at rue de Bizerte": **neither address appears anywhere else in the pool**, and both conflict with the Wikipedia birth district and avenue Niel death. **Not adjudicated; the file follows Wikipedia.**
- 'Immanence: A Life' 1995, part of the unfinished *Ensembles and Multiplicities*; the announced Marx book *La Grandeur de Marx* never written **[A: EN Wikipedia]**.
- **Death 4 November 1995 by suicide, from the avenue Niel window, aged seventy [A: EN and FR Wikipedia]**; FR gives the hour as about 12:30, and both tie the act to the illness. **A later reading treats the defenestration as consistent with his philosophy of immanence; no primary source records anyone close to him framing it that way [R].**
- Buried at **Saint-Léonard-de-Noblat** **[A: EN Wikipedia]**. **Coordinate corrected** to **45.8383/1.4914**; the afterlife lens's 45.8339/1.4867 was 600 m off and disagreed with the geography lens. **The plot is not given [Gap].** Tributes in *Libération* from Agamben, Badiou, Derrida, Lyotard, Nancy and Faye **[A: FR Wikipedia]**.
- **Foucault's epitaph line** "One day, perhaps, this century will be called Deleuzian" is **[A: EN Wikipedia]**, but **the apparatus is not**: nothing fetched confirms the 1970 date, the journal *Critique*, the title "Theatrum Philosophicum", or that it reviewed both theses **[R]**.
- **L'Abécédaire, a contradiction that cannot be papered over.** FR Wikipedia confirms the Arte broadcast and roughly 80,000 DVD copies **[A]**. The reported condition that it screen **only after his death** cannot stand beside a **January 1995 broadcast, ten months before he died [A: FR Wikipedia]**; EN Wikipedia dates it 1994-1995 and resolves nothing. The campa states the contradiction. **Boutang's direction and the 1988-89 recording dates were not confirmed [R].**
- **2007 citation study**: eleventh most-cited author in English-language humanities publishing, between Freud and Kant **[A: EN Wikipedia, which cites but does not name the study]**. Translation lags **[A/R]**: *Difference and Repetition* 1994, twenty-six years late; *The Logic of Sense* 1990; *Nietzsche and Philosophy* 1983. **No plaque or monument** is recorded at the address, the lecture halls or the grave **[R, argument from silence]**.
- **Two details retagged upward by the verifier.** The long fingernails, kept for want of normal fingerprints, are **[A]**: EN Wikipedia cites James Miller, *The Passion of Michel Foucault* (1993), p. 196, quoting Deleuze. The alcoholism and his stopping are **[A]**, from his own on-camera testimony in *L'Abécédaire*. The fingernails are unused; the drinking is in that campa.

## 8. Quotes: verbatim and not

Seven of 45 stops carry a `quote`; the pool supplied fourteen candidates.

**Four are verbatim from a fetched text [A]:** the *Anti-Oedipus* opening line (p. 1, from the PDF); "We're tired of trees..." (Massumi, Rhizome, p. 15); "The key thing may be to create vacuoles of noncommunication..." ('Control and Becoming'); and "There is no need to fear or hope, but only to look for new weapons." **in its corrected form**, the circulating "There's no need" being a Wikiquote rendering, the Joughin text confirmed in section 1, not at the essay's end.

**Three carry their limitation in `quote_source` [R]:** the Tomlinson *Nietzsche and Philosophy* line on evaluations and "A book is a small cog..." (1977), both via Wikiquote and unconfirmed against a fetched copy; Foucault's "Deleuzian" line, quotation confirmed and apparatus not.

**Two are rejected as non-verbatim.** "A rhizome has neither beginning nor end, but always a middle from which it grows." is in no source; Massumi reads "It has neither beginning nor end, but always a middle (milieu) from which it grows **and which it overspills**". And "A leftist government doesn't exist, because being on the left has nothing to do with governments." is a Wikiquote compression of two statements, checked against the Stivale transcript. Neither is used, nor paraphrased inside quotation marks anywhere. Five further verbatim lines, from the *Postscript*, *Anti-Oedipus* p. 2 and the Negri conversation, are attested and unused.
