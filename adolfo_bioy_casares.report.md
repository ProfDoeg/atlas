# Adolfo Bioy Casares (1914-1999): research report

*2026-08-31. Written for `adolfo_bioy_casares.journey.json` (9 segments, 44 stops).
Argentine novelist; author of `La invención de Morel` (1940); lifelong collaborator
of Borges; husband of Silvina Ocampo; Premio Cervantes 1990.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition,
anecdote, or an inference I could not carry back to a document. Contradictions are
flagged where they sit and are **not** adjudicated; unreachable sources are named
with the reason.

---

## I. Family and birth (1914)

Born **15 September 1914** in Recoleta, only child of **Adolfo Bioy Domecq** and
**Marta Ignacia Casares Lynch** **[A: Biblioteca Nacional Argentina; both
Wikipedias]**. The mother's family owned **La Martona**, founded 1889 by **Vicente
Casares (1844-1910)** in what is now the locality of Vicente Casares **[A:
es.wikipedia]**. Casares is given as Bioy's **maternal grandfather**; I saw no
civil record for the exact degree, and the pool's burial lens made the opposite
error of calling him Bioy's *father*. A tradition attributed to **Narciso Binayán
Carmona** traces the paternal line to the conquistador **Domingo Martínez de
Irala**, and so to remote Guaraní ancestry **[R: unverified]**.

**Pin correction.** The pool's birthplace coordinate (-34.5875, -58.3931) sits
inside the Cementerio de la Recoleta, under forty metres from the grave, and was
reused a third time for Losada. The file offsets the birthplace to -34.5890,
-58.3960, moves Losada to Monserrat, and uses the canonical cemetery pin from
`sarmiento` / `jose_hernandez` for the burial.

## II. Childhood travel and schooling (1924-1929)

Family tours of Europe in **1924** and **1928**, the second reaching **Egypt and
the Near East** **[R: Instituto Cervantes chronology via the dossier]**. Not one
city, ship, address, or duration is named for either; the file uses **Paris** and
**Cairo** as declared stand-ins and says so in each `date_confidence`. Around 1928
he drafts **"Vanidad o una aventura terrorífica"** **[R: same, "around this
time"]**.

Secondary school at the **Instituto Libre de Segunda Enseñanza**, Libertad 555,
founded 1892 after the Colegio Nacional faculty walked out over rector Adolfo
Orma's dismissal **[A: ilse.esc.edu.ar/historia, fetched]**. **1929**, aged
fifteen: **Prólogo**, 300 copies paid for by his father **[A: Martino;
es.wikipedia]**. **1933**: *17 disparos contra lo porvenir* as **Martín
Sacastrú**; **1934**: Revista Americana prize for *Caos* **[A: Martino]**.

## III. Villa Ocampo, and the degrees abandoned (1932-1937)

**Borges**, met at **Villa Ocampo, San Isidro**, **1932** **[A: both Wikipedias'
Bioy articles; `borges.journey.json` carries the same year and pin]**. Two pool
errors corrected rather than carried:

the pool calls Borges "seven years his senior" when he was **fifteen** years older
(b. 24 Aug 1899 vs. 15 Sept 1914), a figure its own geography lens got right; and
its Villa Ocampo coordinate is about **1.8 km off**, at San Isidro town centre, so
the file inherits **-34.4579, -58.5184** from `borges` and `silvina_ocampo`.

**Contradiction, open**: en.wikipedia's *Borges* article dates the introduction to
**1935**; both *Bioy* articles say **1932**.

**Silvina Ocampo**: the pool says **1934** while citing es.wikipedia, which says
the opposite (its *Silvina* article: met in **1932**; its *Bioy* article: the same
Villa Ocampo gatherings). `silvina_ocampo.journey.json` says **1933**. Three
dates, no document; the file uses 1932 and flags all three. The **eleven-year age
gap is [A]** (Silvina b. 28 July 1903).

**University**: law abandoned, then Philosophy and Letters from **1933**, left for
good in **1934** with Borges and Silvina urging it **[A: Cervantes chronology]**.
The pool's coordinate is ~500 m off; the faculty then met at **Viamonte 430**, and
the file inherits `julio_cortazar`'s pin (-34.5996, -58.3737). The Puán campus
dates from 1988 and must never be used for this period.

**Rincón Viejo, Pardo**: he tries to run the family holding himself and fails
within the year **[A]**. Two problems, both flagged in the file: the pool's own
lenses date it **1934** and **1935**; and its cited source (diarioeltiempo.com.ar)
describes an **almacén de ramos generales**, a general store, not administration of
the estate. That source does confirm the name from 1872 and the railway's arrival
four years later.

**Destiempo** (1936), three issues with Borges, nominally directed by **Ernesto
Pissavini**, in fact the caretaker of the building **[A: Martino; Sur-circle
history via dossier]**. **La Martona pamphlet**: the pool left a 1935-vs-1937
conflict unresolved, which es.wikipedia's *La Martona* article settles at **1937**,
commissioned by Bioy's uncle **Miguel Casares**, then vice-president **[A]**. The
pool put its coordinate in Cañuelas town; the factory stood in the locality of
**Vicente Casares**, and the file moves the pin.

## IV. The island (1937-1941)

Composition of **La invención de Morel** at Rincón Viejo, by some accounts nearly
three years, with draft titles **La invención de Gopar** and **La invención de
Guerin** **[A: Martino's manuscript research]**. **The Wells link is [R]**: Bioy
associates Morel with Wells's Doctor Moreau, but the draft sequence complicates a
single direct allusion and `h_g_wells.journey.json` contains no reference to Bioy
or Morel, so the file names Wells and hedges in `date_confidence`.

**Anachronism removed.** The pool staged Bioy writing *Morel* in room 11 of the
**Gran Hotel Avenida, Las Flores, 1937-1940**; its own cited source
(lanacion.com.ar, nid1726164) says the hotel **has operated only since 1943**. The
stop is dropped, the composition put at Rincón Viejo. *Recorded so the operator can
see what went and why.*

**Marriage**, Las Flores, **15 January 1940**, Borges a witness **[A:
lasflores.gob.ar with photograph; both Wikipedias; `silvina_ocampo`]**; **Enrique
L. Drago Mitre** and **Óscar Pardo** as the other two **[A: culture ministry
account via dossier]**. The house-trailer honeymoon reaching Rosario and Córdoba
before failing is **[R]**.

**Morel**, Losada, **1940**, cover by **Norah Borges**, prologue by Borges: *"no me
parece una imprecisión o una hipérbole calificarla de perfecta"* **[A: text
fetched at literatura.us]**. Same year, the first **Antología de la literatura
fantástica** with Borges and Silvina, Bioy writing the theoretical prologue **[A]**.
**First Municipal Prize, 1941** **[A: es.wikipedia]**.

## V. Bustos Domecq and Suárez Lynch (1942-1948)

**1942** *Seis problemas para don Isidro Parodi* as **H. Bustos Domecq**, the
barber solving from cell 273 **[A: both Wikipedias; Martino]**. **1945** *Plan de
evasión*; adviser at **Emecé**; with Borges launches **El Séptimo Círculo** **[A:
en.wikipedia; Cervantes]**. **1946** *Un modelo para la muerte* as **B. Suárez
Lynch**, 300 copies, illustrated by **Xul Solar** **[A: `xul_solar.journey.json`,
matching the dossier on pseudonym and imprint]**; and *Los que aman, odian* with
Silvina at Mar del Plata, the only book they sign jointly **[A: both Wikipedias;
Martino; `silvina_ocampo` for the place]**. **c. 1947** **"La fiesta del
Monstruo"** with Borges, rewriting Echeverría's *El matadero* in a Peronist
demonstrator's voice **[A for the story and its suppression; R for the 1947 year,
given without a document]**; first printed in Montevideo's **Marcha, 30 September
1955** **[R: as reported; the issue not seen]**. **1948** *La trama celeste* **[A:
en.wikipedia]**, given no stop, cut for budget.

## VI. Paris, Garro, and the household (1949-1959)

Meets **Octavio Paz** and **Elena Garro** in Paris; the relationship with Garro
runs about two decades and leaves **91 letters, 13 telegrams and 3 postcards** in
Princeton's Garro papers **[R as to the counts: catalog.princeton.edu is behind bot
verification, so the figures come through the dossier]**. **Contradiction, open**:
Cervantes gives **1949**, Infobae a **summer 1950** meeting at the **Hôtel George
V**; both agree on Paris and Paz's presence. Two letter excerpts survive in
Infobae's 2024 piece, one used as a `quote` **[A as journalism quoting the letters;
manuscripts not seen]**. **Helena Paz Garro's testimony** that her mother became
pregnant by Bioy and aborted after Paz threatened to claim the child is **[R]**:
memoir and interviews only, no documentation located.

**1954**: *El sueño de los héroes* **[A: en.wikipedia]**; birth of **Marta Bioy
Ocampo** to **María Teresa von der Lahr**, raised by Silvina and Bioy as their own
**[A: es.wikipedia, 8 July 1954]**. **Marta's birthplace is a four-way
contradiction, left open**: Pau (Infobae) / Buenos Aires (Cervantes) / New York
(genealogical summaries) / the United States (`silvina_ocampo.journey.json`).
**Posadas 1650, Recoleta**, over the Plaza San Martín de Tours, is the principal
home and the scene of the dinners with Borges later published as *Borges* (2006),
ed. Martino **[A: infobae for street and overlook; number from the dossier;
`silvina_ocampo` for address and pin, inherited byte-identical]**.

**Photography** from about **1958-1959**, subjects including Silvina, the Borges
circle, **Julio Cortázar**, Mujica Láinez and Pizarnik **[R as to start year:
exhibition catalogues vary]**. **Café La Biela** and the lovers kept to different
hours is **[R]** and undated, printed by Infobae explicitly as anecdote; the file
dates it 1950 as a placeholder and says so.

## VII. Brazil, and the rented Peugeot (1960-1970)

- **1960** PEN congress at **Rio**, on through **São Paulo** and the months-old
  **Brasília**, written up as *Unos días en el Brasil* **[A: agenciabalcells.com,
  fetched]**. The **Praça dos Três Poderes** is **inferred** from Brasília's single
  central square; no Bioy source names it. *São Paulo cut for budget.*
- **1962** death of his father; *El lado de la sombra* **[A: Cervantes]**. *No
  stop; a gap, recorded here.*
- **1967** solo drive in a rented **Peugeot** through France, Britain, Switzerland,
  Germany, Austria, Italy and Andorra, letters to Silvina later edited as *En
  viaje* **[A: agenciabalcells.com, fetched]**. Only **Le Touquet** (La Nación) and
  **Andorra** survive as named points; Britain and Switzerland are countries with
  no city, so the pool's London and Geneva stops stood in for nothing and went.
  *Le Touquet cut for budget, not for doubt.*
- **1970** months in **Pau**; *Memoria sobre la pampa y los gauchos* **[A:
  Cervantes]**.

## VIII. The dictatorship years (1968-1986)

- **1968** begins *Diario de la guerra del cerdo* at **Mar del Plata**; published
  **1969** with the screenplay of **Invasión** (Borges, Hugo Santiago) **[A:
  en.wikipedia; film record via dossier]**. **1973** *Dormir al sol*; **1975** SADE
  Gran Premio de Honor **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **21 May 1976**, near **San José and Hipólito Yrigoyen**: men in civilian clothes
  and soldiers chase and shoot a man; a green Ford Falcon passes with its siren;
  Bioy is warned to say nothing; his diary calls it a *fusilamiento* and records his
  fear of having been seen **[A: enlacecritico.com, fetched, quoting the diary,
  which also has Borges and Sábato lunching with Videla two days earlier]**.
- **1977** *Nuevos cuentos de Bustos Domecq* **[A]**. **El héroe de las mujeres**
  is a **live conflict**, **1978** (en.wikipedia) vs. **1979** (Cervantes); the
  pool's confident [A] 1978 is not safe. *No stop given.*
- **1980** petition in **Clarín** with Borges on the disappeared **[A: petition
  record via dossier]**. **1981** Légion d'honneur **[A]**, *no stop*. **December
  1983** diary entry calling the outgoing regime authoritarian, unscrupulous and
  criminal, with hope in **Alfonsín** **[A: diary via dossier]**.
- **1982-1985** *La aventura de un fotógrafo en La Plata* **[A for composition and
  publication; R for the dictatorship reading, which is reception, not his stated
  intention]**. **23 September 1985**, **Uriarte 2472**: an ESEADE event with
  Borges and Alberto Benegas Lynch (h) **[A: enlacecritico.com]**.
- **14 June 1986** **Borges dies in Geneva**, liver cancer, aged 86 **[A:
  en.wikipedia, fetched]**, ending fifty-four years. **That Bioy did not travel is
  [R]**: no source states it either way. Same year: **Ciudadano Ilustre**, honorary
  PEN membership, *Historias desaforadas* **[A]**.

## IX. The prizes (1984-1995)

From the Instituto Cervantes prizes page and the Balcells agency page, both
fetched, all **[A]**: **1984** Mondello (Palermo); **1985** Premio del Instituto
Latinoamericano de Roma for *Historias de amor*; **1986** Premio Lilia del
Instituto Italo-Latinoamericano de Roma; **1988** Chieti doctorate and Capri prize;
**1992** Grinzane Cavour; **1993** Grenoble doctorate; **1995** Prix Roger Caillois
(Reims). Two corrections carried in: the pool gave **both** Roman honours to the
IILA, but the source names two bodies; and it framed Reims 1995 as "two years
before Silvina's death," when she died in **December 1993**.

**Premio Cervantes** announced **15 November 1990** **[A: en.wikipedia, fetched]**,
address delivered at **Alcalá de Henares, 23 April 1991** **[A: argentina.gob.ar,
fetched, quoting his remarks on first reading Don Quixote]**. **The Paraninfo venue
is [R]**: that article names *no* venue. Sources differ on calling him the 1990 or
1991 laureate.

## X. The losses, and the death (1993-1999)

- **14 December 1993**, **Silvina Ocampo** dies after progressive Alzheimer's **[A
 , institutional records and standard bibliographies, against a competing report
  of 15 December]**. **Place contradiction, open**: `silvina_ocampo.journey.json`
  says the **San Isidro house**, the dossier says **Buenos Aires**. The file pins
  Posadas 1650 and states both.
- **4 January 1994**, **Marta Bioy Ocampo** killed on the sidewalk by two colliding
  automobiles, twenty-one days after her mother **[A: contemporary reporting via
  dossier]**; no street is named in any source reached. Same year, *Memorias*,
  prepared with **Marcelo Pichon-Rivière** and **Cristina Castro Cranwell** **[A]**.
- **1998** legal recognition of **Fabián Bioy Demaría**, b. 1963 to **Sara Josefina
  "Fina" Demaría Madero**, with travel to Paris **[A: succession record via
  dossier]**. Fabián's death in Paris, 11 February 2006, rests on the dossier alone
  and is **[R]**.
- **8 March 1999**, death in Buenos Aires at 84, cardiac and respiratory
  complications; *De las cosas maravillosas* the same year **[A: both Wikipedias,
  fetched]**. The building is not named; the file pins his own address and says so.
- **9 March 1999**, burial in the **Casares vault, Cementerio de la Recoleta**,
  after his express refusal of a wake: ~100 mourners, his son Fabián and
  grandchildren, secretary **Amelia Francisco**, a priest **José Ignacio Ferro**
  saying only the Lord's Prayer, no speeches **[A: lanacion.com.ar, "Despidieron en
  la Recoleta los restos de Bioy," fetched]**. The pool called the vault "his
  father's"; it carries **Vicente Casares**, the maternal line. Its **five o'clock
  hour** and **Drago Mitre's presence** are **not** in the article, which names
  **Sergio Renán** and **Ernesto Schoo**; both dropped.

## XI. Afterlife, and what was kept out of the file

Kept as a stop, the objects being his own: **2017**, the Bioy-Ocampo library
bought and donated to the **Biblioteca Nacional Mariano Moreno** after years of
succession litigation, announced at ~17,000 volumes in 330 boxes and later
catalogued at **15,860** **[A as to the acquisition; the two figures are an
estimate against a later count, not missing books; the pool's news links returned
404, so the numbers rest on the dossier and es.wikipedia]**.

Kept only as `suggested_refs` on his own stops: the Simms translation (NYRB, 2003,
out of print), **Margaret Jull Costa's *Morel's Invention*, NYRB, 13 October 2026**
**[A: NYRB page; the pool gave only the bare month]**, Emidio Greco's
**L'invenzione di Morel (1974)**, and the 1973 French translation by **Armand
Pierhal** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

Recorded with **no stop**: **L'Année dernière à Marienbad (1961)**, read as drawing
on *Morel* but **[R]** and disputed (Robbe-Grillet read and reviewed the novel yet
denied adaptation, Resnais said he did not know it, and the film has no recording
machine); the **1994** and **Stewart Copeland 2017** operas **[A as listed by
en.wikipedia, no detail located]**; the **2008 *Lost*** reference and US sales spike
**[A: es.wikipedia]**; the street beside Posadas 1650 named for him on **22
September 2011** and one in **La Gavia, Madrid** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

atlas file and Robbe-Grillet denies it, so it is recorded rather than written in.
