# Emanuel Swedenborg (1688-1772): research report

*2026-08-28, from the verified research pool plus the Colegio Invisible dossier behind it. Journey file: `emanuel_swedenborg.journey.json`, 9 segments, 43 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on a single interested or posthumous source. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated, unless a fetched source settles them, in which case the evidence is shown.

**Structural note.** Almost everything interesting about Swedenborg is reception, and under the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, none of it could become a stop here. It is carried as `sources` and `suggested_refs` on his own stops and named in four campas; every drop is itemized under **Interlocks**.

## 1. Stockholm and Uppsala, 1688-1709

**Birth**, Stockholm, to Jesper Swedberg, court chaplain, and Sara Behm, **29 January 1688 Old Style** [A: swedenborg.com; Wikipedia]. **Corrected:** New Style is **8 February**, not swedenborg.com's 9 February; the 1688 offset was ten days and sv.wikipedia states the conversion outright. Old Style is the sort key. **Gap:** the birth house is nowhere recorded, so 59.3251, 18.0711 is a Gamla Stan anchor near Stortorget, not an address.

**Move to Uppsala, 1692** [R, dossier only]. **Mother's death, 1696** [A: dossier citing Acton, *Letters and Memorials*; not reverified]. **Matriculation at eleven, 1699** [A: swedenborg.com]. **Dissertation/edition, 1709**, *Selectae sententiae L. Annaei Senecae et Publilii Syri mimi* [A: swedenborg.com; Wikipedia].

**Father to Skara 1702; the Benzelius household 1703-1709.** **Corrected:** the chronology lens fused two facts and dated the second a year early. Jesper's elevation to Skara is 1702 [A: sv.wikipedia], but en.wikipedia dates Emanuel's residence with **Erik Benzelius the Younger** to **1703-1709**. The geography lens compounded it by running one continuous residence from the 1692 arrival, which no source says. The file splits them.

**Dropped stop, recorded as a gap.** The geography lens supplied **Skara / Brunsbo, from 1702** [R]. Dropped: nothing in the pool attests that **Emanuel** was ever there. The entry is about his father's career, and his father is not the subject. Restorable if the operator has a source placing him at Brunsbo.

## 2. The grand tour, 1710-1715

**Departure 1710, wartime crossing, London then Oxford, the Netherlands, France and German lands, return c. 1715** [A: Wikipedia].

**Newton** [R for the lectures]. The Swedenborg Society's own biography says he "attended Newton's lectures": an affiliated source, uncorroborated, and the dossier itself calls claims that he studied under Newton unsupported. That he read Newton is [A], which is what the campa rests on. **Flamsteed and Greenwich** [R]: the Observatory and Flamsteed's tenure are [A: Wikipedia], but Swedenborg's approach to him for lunar observations comes from the dossier's reading of the Benzelius correspondence, unconfirmed against a primary document.

**Oxford, c. 1711-1714** [R]. The dossier hedges its own claim ("and perhaps Cambridge"). **Dropped from the file** as the weakest leg, recorded here so it is not lost. **Amsterdam and Paris** [A at city level: dossier]: no lodging or press addresses established, and the unbuilt machines (submarine, air pump, flying machine) belong to his notebooks of this period in the standard biographies, not to a Paris document.

## 3. The Board of Mines, 1716-1747

**Lund, 1716**: Charles XII, assessor extraordinary of the Bergskollegium [A: Wikipedia]. Coordinate is Lund Cathedral; no meeting site is named anywhere. **Daedalus Hyperboreus, 1716-1718**, six issues, generally called Sweden's first scientific periodical [A: swedenborg.com; Wikipedia]. **Gap:** no printing address in either source and the pool gives only "Sweden," so it is folded into the Lund stop rather than pinned to an invented press.

**Trollhättan, September 1718, Emerentia Polhem** [R]. *Swedenborg Epic* ch. 7, resting on a document produced to a Stockholm society **in 1789** on Gabriel Polhem's late testimony. The canal work is well attested; the courtship is tradition.

**Fredriksten, 1718** [R for his role, A for the king's death]. The overland ship transport is engineering fact inflated by later retelling into a solo feat. Charles XII killed November 1718 [A: Wikipedia]; month only, day defaults to 01. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's 59.1194, 11.3861 sits 400-500 m west of the ramparts; the file uses **59.1181, 11.3925**, per en.wikipedia.

**Ennoblement, 1719** [A: swedenborg.com; Wikipedia, which also confirms the Riddarhuset coordinate]. **Uppsala chair declined, 1724** [A: Wikipedia]; the speech impediment is a **later report**, not his own statement. **Opera philosophica et mineralia, Dresden and Leipzig, 1734** [A: same], city level, presses not established. **Resignation, June 1747** [A: swedenborg.com for the petition, Wikipedia for the month]: honourable release at half salary, against the later story of dismissal.

**Oeconomia regni animalis.** **Corrected:** the chronology lens gave "London, England / Amsterdam" and contradicted its own geography lens. swedenborg.com gives Amsterdam, two volumes, 1740-41. **London does not belong in the imprint.** **Academy of Sciences, elected 1740** [A: sv.wikipedia]. **Coordinate contradiction inside the pool, resolved by dating rather than preference:** the geography lens reused the generic Gamla Stan point; the afterlife lens gave 59.3697, 18.0559, the Frescati campus, **not built until 1915**. In 1740 the Academy had no purpose-built home. The file uses a central Stockholm point for 1740 and 1772, Frescati only for the 2005 UNESCO stop, and says so in each `date_confidence`.

## 4. The crisis, 1743-1745

**Departure 21 July 1743; itinerary Ystad, Stralsund, Hamburg, Bremen, the Netherlands, London** [R], from the journal's dated entries via newchristianbiblestudy.org and the dossier, not checked against a scholarly edition. Stralsund, Hamburg and Bremen **dropped** as bare waypoints with no dated event; the departure is pinned at Ystad.

**The Delft crisis, 6-7 April 1744.** **The two lenses contradicted each other and the geography lens was wrong.** It placed the crisis in **London**, 51.5074, -0.1278; the chronology lens correctly had the Netherlands; the standard reading of the journal puts the vision of Christ at **Delft**, and the file uses 52.0116, 4.3571. Also corrected: "Easter weekend." Gregorian Easter Sunday 1744 fell on **5 April**, so 6-7 April is Easter Monday into Tuesday.

**Regnum animale, 1744, The Hague / London** [R: Wikipedia]; seventeen parts projected, three published. **The London commission, 1745** [R]: the fullest early source is **Carl Robsahm's memoir**, written after Swedenborg's death from later conversations, so it documents his narrated self-understanding rather than a verified event. **Gap:** the inn is unnamed, so the coordinate is central London. **Journal of Dreams first published 1859** [R]: dossier claim; the afterlife lens confirmed only that an archive.org scan of an early English edition exists.

## 5. Publication, 1749-1771

**Arcana Coelestia, London 1749-1756**, eight quarto volumes, 4,559 pages, anonymous, at his own cost, poor sales [A: swedenborg.com; Wikipedia]; the printer is **not** established. **Heaven and Hell and four other titles, London 1758** [A: same]; quotes verified against Project Gutenberg ebook 17368, the file using section 59. **Divine Love and Wisdom (1763), Divine Providence (1764), Apocalypse Revealed (1766), all Amsterdam** [A: swedenborg.com], compressed into one stop. **Conjugial Love, Amsterdam 1768** [A: swedenborg.com, which calls it the first book bearing his name]; quotes verified against ebook 11248.

**Vera Christiana Religio.** **Corrected twice.** The pool collapsed journey and publication: he travelled to Amsterdam **July 1770** [A: Wikipedia], but swedenborg.com gives the first Latin edition as **Amsterdam, 1771**. And "under his own name for the first time" is wrong: **Conjugial Love (1768) already carried it.**

**The Gothenburg Consistory, 1768-1771.** **End date corrected.** Complaint filed **27 September 1768**; the Royal Council's final order, that Beyer and Rosén be treated "with mildness and Christian charity, without reproaches for the past," is **7 December 1771**, after which the case passed to the Göta Court of Appeals and stalled [A: swedenborgdigitallibrary.org/ES/epic41.htm]. The pool said 1770; the April 1770 decree was an **interim** step. Participants per the same source: Bishop Eric Lamberg, Dean Olof Ekebom, prosecutor **Anders Aurell**. The target was the professors. **Petition to the king, 25 May 1770** [A: same source, quotation verbatim].

## 6. The wonders: fire, receipt, queen

All three are [R] in the file.

**The Stockholm fire, Gothenburg, 19 July 1759.** The pool called this "a genuine bibliographic tangle, not resolved here." **It is resolvable: two facts were being confused.** (1) The fire is documented independently of Swedenborg as the **Mariabranden**, out **19 July 1759**, extinguished the evening of the 20th [A: sv.wikipedia; en.wikipedia gives "Thursday, 19 July 1759"]. (2) Kant's letter to Charlotte von Knobloch, read directly at en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dreams_of_a_Spirit-Seer/Appendix_2, says "In the year 1759, towards the end of September, on Saturday at four o'clock p.m., Swedenborg arrived at Gottenburg from England." Kant's **year agrees with the record**; only his **month** is wrong, a slip in a letter reconstructing second-hand report years afterwards. The file dates the event 19 July and treats the month as Kant's error, not as a rival of equal weight. Two further corrections against Kant's wording, both in the campa: four p.m. is his **arrival**, not the alarm; the eight o'clock exclamation is "Thank God! the fire is extinguished." The dossier's attribution of "19 July" to Kant is wrong too: **Kant never says July.**

**The Marteville receipt, c. 1760** [R]. **Corrected:** "accounts differ over whether Dutch or French" overstates it. Kant is unambiguous: the widow of the **Dutch** ambassador, dunned by **Croon, a goldsmith**, for a silver service. **Kant gives no date**, so 1760 is inference. **Quote citation corrected:** the Bayle wording is **not** in the 1841 Tafel edition the pool cited, whose Marteville pages say only that Swedenborg "informed her the next day where it was." It is from the **1847 Tafel edition, Part II ch. XIV, p. 94** (archive.org `documentsconcern00tafe`), chain per Tafel's footnote: **Thiébault (1804)**, relaying Chamberlain d'Hamon. The 1875-77 edition (Doc. 274) retranslates it as "had **received** the receipt," so the wording is edition-specific.

**Queen Louisa Ulrika, c. 1758-61** [R]. **Misattached quote corrected.** The pool filed "he could not disclose his errand in the presence of witnesses" under the Marteville widow; it belongs to the **queen's** episode, where he returned days after her request about her dead brother, found her at cards, and asked for a private audience (1847 Tafel, Part II ch. XIV, **p. 95**). She then posted Senator von Schwerin at the door, and the 1875-77 edition (Doc. 275) renders the line differently. **A finding, not a hedge:** the content of what he told her was **never written down by anyone**, so the story is unfalsifiable by construction.

**Riksdag memorial on currency, November 1760** [R]. Dossier via newchurchhistory.org; not refetched.

## 7. Death and afterlife

**Great Bath Street, Coldbath Fields, 1771** [A for the Shearsmith lodging: Wikipedia]. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's 51.527, -0.1107 lands in Amwell, 350-400 m north of the historic Coldbath Fields near Mount Pleasant. File uses **51.524, -0.1105**; the street no longer exists.

**Stroke, December 1771** [A: Wikipedia; swedenborg.com]. **Deliberately omitted:** the famous deathbed exchange in which Ferelius asks whether he wishes to retract and he answers that everything is true. Not in the verified pool. Ferelius administering communion **is**, and is what the campa carries. **Death 29 March 1772** [A: swedenborg.com, Wikipedia, Swedenborg Society, agreeing]; no autopsy documented, and stroke complications are the sources' inference. **Burial 5 April 1772, Swedish Church, Princes Square, Shadwell** [A: Swedenborg Society]. Wikipedia calls the same ground Wapping, a neighbouring designation rather than a competing claim; the renaming of the square is carried as report, uncorroborated from a fetched source.

**Reinterment: the largest error in the packet, and a false dilemma.** The pool offered 1910 (Swedenborg Society) against 1912/1913 (en.wikipedia, "the 140th anniversary") and called it unresolvable. **Neither is right.** Both transfer and reinterment occurred in **1908**: sv.wikipedia, "År 1908 flyttades kvarlevorna," to the Bielke-Liewenska vault in Uppsala Cathedral, ceremony **19 May 1908**, and independently en.wikipedia's *Uppsala Cathedral* has the remains "transported to Uppsala from England in 1908." The Society's 1910 is an error and the anniversary arithmetic works for neither offered year. The file uses 1908 and drops the manufactured controversy.

**The skull "returned from Britain to Sweden in 1978"** [R, weaker still]. Asserted **only** in the dossier; the afterlife lens found no independent source. **Open gap**, recorded rather than written into a campa.

**Summerhouse to Skansen, 1896** [A, single-source: swedenborg.com gives the date, Wikipedia confirms the building but no date]. **Manuscripts to the Academy 1772; UNESCO Memory of the World 2005** [A: UNESCO record, confirmed verbatim, roughly 20,000 pages]. **Swedenborg Society founded London 1810** [A: Wikipedia]; **not a stop here**, being the readers' institution, thirty-eight years posthumous, but for later use the pool's 51.5194, -0.1211 is some 350 m off Swedenborg House, 20-21 Bloomsbury Way, roughly 51.5173, -0.1245. **New Church constituted 7 May 1787; first General Conference, Great Eastcheap, April 1789** [A: Wikipedia]. He ordained no clergy and wrote no constitution, so "founder of the Swedenborgian Church" is genealogically loose.
