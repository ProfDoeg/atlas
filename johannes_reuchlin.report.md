# Johannes Reuchlin (Capnion), 1455-1522: research report

*2026-08-28, for `johannes_reuchlin.journey.json`. 9 segments, 44 stops, 13 quoted stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same sentence. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim not confirmed against a page reached here. Conflicts are printed, not resolved. All hedging in the journey file lives in `date_confidence`; this report holds the reasoning behind it. Reuchlin is famous for a lawsuit whose nineteenth-century German literature is mostly out of reach, so what circulates descends through two Wikipedia articles that disagree with each other.

### Coordinates corrected

Nine pins were corrected against the circulating synthesis, each stated in its own stop's `date_confidence`: Denkendorf **48.6920 / 9.3152** (the circulating value was **6.5 km** off, in open country), Speyer Cathedral **49.3172 / 8.4424** (~720 m), Bad Liebenzell **48.7742 / 8.7314** (~1 km), Pforzheim Stadtgarten **48.886 / 8.6975** (~700 m, outside the park), the Stuttgart Dominican cloister **~48.772 / 9.178** (the circulating value is the Altes Schloss), plus smaller fixes at the Pforzheim cloister, Tuebingen, the Leonhardskirche and the Worms Lutherdenkmal.

## 1. Pforzheim and the schools, 1455-1481

**Birth [A]** (both language editions): Pforzheim, son of Georg Reuchlin, property administrator to the Dominican house, and Elissa Erinna Eck, at the ninth hour after noon.

*Date contradiction, unresolved.* en.wikipedia gives **29 January 1455**; older works, notably the Jewish Encyclopedia, give **22 February 1455**, which the crawl misattributed to Wikipedia. Both are printed in `date_confidence`; the file uses 29 January. The circulating place-name, "site now Schlosskirche/St. Michael quarter," is a conflation: the cloister's church was St Stephan, destroyed 1689.

**Freiburg, 1470 [A]**, both editions: matriculated in arts at fifteen, took no degree. The day (19 May) is **[R]**, from the crawl only.

**Paris, 1473: unresolved ordering.** de.wikipedia puts Paris in **1473** with Basel following in 1474; en.wikipedia orders it the other way, "1477: Left Basel for Paris." The file follows the German ordering and states the conflict. Content is solid on both sides **[A]**: Greek under **Georgios Hermonymos of Sparta**, entry into the circle of **Johannes Heynlin von Stein**.

**Basel, 1474-1477 [A]**: baccalaureate 1475, **master of arts 1477**. *The Vocabularius breviloquus must be split off from the magisterium*: the crawl fused them, but de.wikipedia's works list gives "Vocabularius breviloquus, **Basel 1478**" and en.wikipedia dates first publication **1475-76**. The file does not pin the dictionary to 1477; Amerbach as printer is **[R]**.

**Orleans and Poitiers [A]** for the degrees, both divergences stated in the file: Orleans 1478 (en.wikipedia) against 1479-1481 (de.wikipedia); the Poitiers licentiate July 1481 against 14 June 1481.

## 2. Wuerttemberg service and the Italian missions, 1481-1494

**Tuebingen, December 1481 [A]** (de.wikipedia: 9 December): retained by Count Eberhard im Bart as interpreter, secretary, legal adviser, envoy. **First Italian mission, 1482 [A]**: Florence and Rome as second orator, on business separating the University of Tuebingen from the collegiate church of St George. **Linz, 1492 [A]**: ennobled with arms by Frederick III, and began Hebrew with **Jacob ben Jehiel Loans**, the emperor's Jewish physician; his Latin tribute to Loans (De Rudimentis Hebraicis p.3, in Barham 1843 p.54) is quoted on the stop, with no English rendering claimed. **Basel, 1494 [A]**: *Liber de verbo mirifico* and the five-letter **Pentagrammaton** YHShWH.

**Doctorate and first marriage, c. 1484. [R]** throughout: de.wikipedia dates both to 1484/85 and identifies the wife only as a daughter of Haenslin Mueller. *The two marriages do not sequence*: it gives the first wife married ~1484 and dead ~1500, and **Anna Decker married "after 1496"** and dead ~1519. Those overlap, so the clean widower-remarries story cannot be right as printed. Both dates reported, no ordering asserted. Ditzingen gets no stop, the estate tradition being genealogical only; it is named in the campa as what "the local genealogies" say and nowhere treated as attested.

**Second Italian journey, 1490: the crawl's biggest overclaim.** The circulating item asserts, tagged [A], that Reuchlin moved among **Ficino** and **Poliziano** and encountered **Elijah del Medigo**. Not supportable: en.wikipedia's 1490 entry names **only Pico della Mirandola**, Ficino and Poliziano appear in no 1490 account on either edition, and while de.wikipedia supports del Medigo loosely, **en.wikipedia 'Elia del Medigo' does not mention Reuchlin at all** and has him leaving Italy for Crete about then. The stop is built on Pico; the other three are **[R]**, named in the campa as older-biography embroidery. *The Careggi stop was dropped*: it pinned the encounter at Ficino's villa, and the Ficino contact is exactly the part that fails.

## 3. Heidelberg, Rome, the grammar, 1496-1506

**Heidelberg, 1496 [A]** (en.wikipedia): fled Stuttgart after Eberhard's February death; service with Elector Palatine Philip, in Bishop Johann von Dalberg's circle, and the comedies *Sergius* and *Henno*. **Pforzheim, 1506 [A]**: *De rudimentis Hebraicis libri III*, printed by **Thomas Anshelm**, whose de.wikipedia article lists "Rudimenta hebraica (1506)" among his Pforzheim output; his shop address is undocumented, so the pin is the town.

**Rome, 1498: corrected to a two-year span, with the intermediary named.** The circulating item gives one year and no introducer, against **en.wikipedia 'Obadiah ben Jacob Sforno'**: Reuchlin "was in Rome during **1498-1500**," **Cardinal Domenico Grimani** advised him to apply to Sforno, and Sforno taught him across the two years. **[A]** *Aldus Manutius at Venice, dropped*: the meeting appears on none of the pages fetched, **[R]**, and no stop was staged on one unsourced sentence.

**The 1501 trilingual stone: promoted [R] to [A].** de.wikipedia attests it: made 1501, Hebrew *olam ha-hayyim*, Greek *ANASTASIS*, Latin dedication; originally in the Stuttgart **Dominikanerkloster**, moved to the Leonhardskirche in 1955. Only the coordinate was wrong.

**Swabian League bench, 1502: an internal contradiction, printed as such.** The crawl's chronology lens places the court at Tuebingen, its geography lens at Stuttgart. **Neither wiki article gives a seat**; de.wikipedia says only "Judge, Swabian League (1502-1513)," en.wikipedia hedges the start as approximately 1500-1502. The file pins the Altes Schloss and states that the seat is undocumented. Succession from **Johannes Vergenhans (Nauclerus)** is **[A]**.

**Denkendorf** stays **[R]**: the monastery article does not mention Reuchlin, and de.wikipedia corroborates the quarantine and the preaching manual **without giving a year**, so 1502 is unconfirmed.

## 4. The Jewish book controversy, 1509-1516

**Frankfurt, 1509 [A]**: Maximilian I's mandate, and **Johannes Pfefferkorn**'s confiscations beginning in the Judengasse; the September dating and Kunigunde of Austria's role come from the Bodian page. **Ratschlag, 6 October 1510 [A]**: the opinion against wholesale destruction. **Cologne, 1511-1512 [A]**: Pfefferkorn's *Handspiegel*, the faculty's propositions, imperial restriction. **Confiscation order 7 October 1512 [A]**; **resignation from the bench, January 1513 [A]**, narrated at one pin because the court's seat is undocumented. **Paris, August 1514 [A]**: the faculty condemned the *Augenspiegel*.

**Augenspiegel, autumn 1511 [A]: the "presumably" can go.** de.wikipedia 'Augenspiegel (Reuchlin)' states it was printed **autumn 1511 in Tuebingen by Thomas Anshelm** and published in German at the **Frankfurt autumn book fair**, and confirms the motto verbatim: **"verbrennt nicht, was ihr nicht kennt!"** Quoted in the original, untouched.

**Mainz, 15 September 1513 [A]: datable to the day, venue corrected.** The circulating item gave "Mainz/Cologne." **en.wikipedia 'Jacob van Hoogstraten'**: on that day Hoogstraten summoned Reuchlin before the ecclesiastical court of **Mainz**, within six days. Cologne is the seat of Hoogstraten and of the condemning faculty, a separate strand.

**Speyer, 29 March 1514: promoted [R] to [A].** The crawl's chronology lens tagged it unlocated; its geography lens found it. **en.wikipedia 'Jacob van Hoogstraten'**: "On 29 March 1514, the Bishop of Speyer announced that the Augenspiegel contained nothing injurious to the Catholic Faith." de.wikipedia has the commission going jointly to **Worms and Speyer**.

**Augsburg, 1513-1514 [R]: a divergence recorded, not staged.** The German ADB places part of the defence activity at **Augsburg** alongside Speyer and **does not mention Mainz at all**. Found independently, absent from the dossier. One uncorroborated ADB line is thin ground for a scene, so it gets no stop, only the Speyer stop's `date_confidence`.

**Epistolae obscurorum virorum, 1515.** Principally **Crotus Rubeanus**, with **Ulrich von Hutten** behind the second series: **[A]** in outline, but **no dedicated page on the satire was pulled** and the printing place is disputed, so it is staged where Reuchlin was.

**Rome, July 1516: "quashed" is too strong.** en.wikipedia gives "Case quashed by papal court in Rome," and the Jewish Encyclopedia has the Lateran Council deciding in his favour; but **en.wikipedia 'Jacob van Hoogstraten' describes the same moment as the pope postponing the trial indefinitely**. The file makes it a suspension favourable in practice, not an acquittal, which is what leaves room for 1520. Date **[A]**, victory **[R]**.

## 5. The Reuchlinists and the reversal, 1517-1520

**De arte cabalistica, 1517.** Three-day dialogue among Simon the Kabbalist, Philolaus the Pythagorean and Marranus the Muslim, dedicated to **Leo X**: work, cast and dedication **[A]**. The **Hagenau** printing place is **[R]**, from the dossier; I could not verify Anshelm's press had moved there by 1517. **Rome, 23 June 1520 [A]**: Leo X condemned the *Augenspiegel*, imposed silence and ordered costs, in Reuchlin's absence.

**Luther's theses, 1517: the quote is [R] and stays [R].** "Thanks be to God, at last, they have found a man who will give them so much to do that they will be compelled to let my old age end in peace." Widely attributed, circulated through twentieth-century compilations (Wikipedia citing Rines 1920), **traced to no primary letter or contemporary source**. The campa itself says no surviving letter carries it.

**Wittenberg, 1518: chair corrected, stop moved to where Reuchlin was.** en.wikipedia (Reuchlin) says he was appointed professor of Hebrew and Greek at Wittenberg and sent Melanchthon instead; **en.wikipedia 'Philip Melanchthon'** specifies what Melanchthon took: "became professor of **Greek** there in 1518 at the age of 21," on his great-uncle's recommendation. Kinship confirmed: his grandmother **Elizabeth Reuter** was Reuchlin's sister. **[A]** *Reuchlin never went to Wittenberg*, so the scene is staged at **Stuttgart** and the Leucorea pin appears in `suggested_refs` only.

## 6. The last chairs and the death, 1519-1522

**Stuttgart 1519 [A]** (war, robbery, famine, plague; Anna's death), marriage-date incoherence re-flagged. **Ingolstadt by November 1519 [A]**; **professorship February 1520 [A]** under Duke Wilhelm IV. **Tuebingen spring 1521 [A]**, plague probable but not documented as the reason for leaving Ingolstadt. **Liebenzell spring 1522 [A]**: the bath visit and the fatal illness, called jaundice or "yellow fever," a term that does not map onto a modern diagnosis.

**The death place: a real contradiction, sourced backwards in the crawl.** The date, **30 June 1522**, is agreed everywhere; the place is not. Modern consensus gives **Stuttgart** (en.wikipedia's infobox, "Died: 30 June 1522 (aged 67), Stuttgart, Duchy of Wuerttemberg"; de.wikipedia agrees). Nineteenth-century works give **Liebenzell**: the **ADB**, "er starb in Bad Liebenzell am 30. Juni 1522," and **jewishencyclopedia.com**, "at Liebenzell, near Hirschau." That is modern encyclopaedias against nineteenth-century ones, not two coequal traditions as the crawl framed it. Not adjudicated; the stop is pinned at the **Leonhardskirche**, where the burial is not in dispute.

## 7. Afterlife

**Leonhardskirche [A]**: severely damaged in the raid of **22 August 1944**, rebuilt **1948-1954** under **Rudolf Lempp**; the gravestone survived and hangs on the choir wall beside an **1871** medallion. *Contradiction printed*: de.wikipedia 'Johannes Reuchlin' gives **1955** for the stone's move into the church, while the church's own article dates the rebuilding to 1954.

**Reuchlinpreis, 1955 [A]** (de.wikipedia 'Reuchlinpreis'): founded by Pforzheim on the five-hundredth anniversary of the birth, awarded with the Heidelberg Academy; laureates include **Gershom Scholem (1969)** and **Hans-Georg Gadamer (1971)**. Scholem's reported remarks crediting Reuchlin with helping begin the *Wissenschaft des Judentums* are secondhand: **[R]**, not quoted. **Barlach medal, 1922 [A]**, for the four-hundredth anniversary of the death. The crawl mis-cited both: **en.wikipedia 'Johannes Reuchlin' has no legacy section at all**, so the medal, the Goethe phrase and the Worms monument were re-cited to German Wikipedia.

**Worms Lutherdenkmal: Reuchlin is a statue, not a relief. [A]** de.wikipedia 'Lutherdenkmal (Worms)' places him as a **full standing statue on the surrounding wall**, with Melanchthon, Frederick the Wise and Philip the Magnanimous, Luther central and the forerunners below. **No date for the figure was established**, so the file's 1868 is an ordering placeholder, not an assertion. The separate Friedrichsbad relief at Baden-Baden got no stop.

**Reuchlinhaus, 1957-1961 [A]**: Stadtgarten, to a 1953 design by **Manfred Lehmbruck**, on the site of a hall of 1900-1945 destroyed in the bombing; now the Schmuckmuseum, named for him and holding no Reuchlin exhibit. *Statue conflict inside German Wikipedia, printed*: 'Johannes Reuchlin' puts the **Matthias Daempfle** statue in the **Stadtgarten**, 'Pforzheim' refers to one **am Schlossberg**.

**Goethe: promoted [R] to [A], with a named text.** de.wikipedia 'Johannes Reuchlin' gives the "Wunderzeichen" line with its source, so it is not a floating civic slogan: **"Reuchlin! Wer will sich mit ihm vergleichen, zu seiner Zeit ein Wunderzeichen."** A couplet from the ***Zahme Xenien*, book V**, quoted verbatim on the final stop.

**Legend kept as legend [R].** That Reuchlin "single-handedly saved the Talmud" is heroic simplification, and the file never says it.

## Interlocks used, and interlocks refused

Named in a campa, with the direction stated:

- `giovanni_pico_della_mirandola`, Florence 1490: they met, and Pico's Christian Kabbalah redirected his work. Pico's own file already names Reuchlin as his heir in the tradition.
- `heinrich_cornelius_agrippa`, Basel 1494: one-directional and textual. Agrippa's first public lecture, Dole 1509, was **on Reuchlin's De verbo mirifico**, and he was denounced the next year partly for it.
- `martin_luther`, Stuttgart 1517 and 1518: Reuchlin's reported reaction to the theses, and Melanchthon, his great-nephew and pupil, called to Wittenberg by Luther on his recommendation.
- `goethe_full`, Pforzheim 1961: Goethe wrote the Zahme Xenien couplet about him. One-directional, three centuries later.

**Coordinates inherited byte-identically.** Paris **48.8566 / 2.3522**, Florence **43.7757 / 11.2544**, Rome **41.9028 / 12.4964** and Basel **47.5596 / 7.5886**, from `giovanni_pico_della_mirandola.journey.json`; the Basel pin is that file's 1601 Opera omnia stop, itself the stop naming Reuchlin. Wittenberg Leucorea **51.864722 / 12.650278** from `martin_luther.journey.json`, in `suggested_refs` only.

**Refused.** `johann_faust` and `johannes_trithemius`: the connection runs only through **Franz von Sickingen** (defended Reuchlin; his patronage put the historical Faust in a Bad Kreuznach schoolmaster's post in 1507) and **Ulrich von Hutten** (Reuchlin's defender, Sickingen's guest). Sickingen and Hutten are Reuchlin's own and are named as people; Faust and Trithemius are not, Reuchlin having no attested relation to either. `borges`: thematic lineage only, no textual chain in `borges.journey.json`, not named.

**Atlas gaps.** **Johannes Pfefferkorn**, **Jacob van Hoogstraten**, **Philipp Melanchthon** and **Desiderius Erasmus** have no journey file (the only `erasmus` file is the unrelated martyr). All four are load-bearing here and are carried only as named persons. `martin_luther.journey.json` documents Melanchthon at length and **never traces his surname or education back to Reuchlin**; a grep for "Reuchlin" there returns nothing. That is a `suggested_ref` for a future editor of Luther's file to add. **Nothing was written into it by this pass.**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Reuchlin is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no backward apparatus application was licensed and none was performed. Two items were kept rather than silently deleted, per the adversary's duty:

1. **Goethe's Zahme Xenien couplet, named in a campa. Direction caught: forward.** Goethe (1749-1832) wrote about Reuchlin (d. 1522): real, one-directional, citable. It sits on an **afterlife stop at Reuchlin's own Pforzheim pin**, quoted verbatim, staging no scene of Goethe travelling, the same class of move as the 1601 Basel stop in Pico's file and the 1858 bronze-door stop in Luther's. If the operator reads the block rule strictly enough that no posthumous person may be *named* in an afterlife campa, this line and the `goethe_full` reference should move into `sources` and `suggested_refs`. Flagged, not decided.

2. **Gershom Scholem's 1969 Reuchlinpreis, deliberately not staged.** Scholem receiving the prize is **Scholem's** stop, not Reuchlin's, and he has no file. He is named in the prize stop as the fact that gives the prize its shape; his own remarks about Reuchlin are **[R]** and are not quoted. If a Scholem file is ever written, the 1969 acceptance belongs there.

No forward application of a framework was attempted, and nothing was written into any other traveler's file.
