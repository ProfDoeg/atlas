# Haydée Tamara Bunke Bíder, "Tania la Guerrillera" (1937-1967)
*Research report, 2026-08-12. Buenos Aires, Stalinstadt, East Berlin, Havana, Prague, La Paz, Ñancahuazú, Vado del Yeso, Santa Clara.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim no second reachable source carries · contradictions are printed, not adjudicated.

Almost every claim below runs through four reachable secondary sources: English, Spanish, and German Wikipedia, and EcuRed. No archive behind them was reachable in this pass. EcuRed is the most detailed on 1963-1967 and also the most committed: it is the memorial literature of the state that dispatched her, and its day-exact dating derives from Cuban intelligence accounts, above all Ulises Estrada's *Tania: Undercover with Che Guevara in Bolivia*, not fetched. The disagreement between the four is the substance of this report.

## 1. Buenos Aires, 1937-1952

Born **19 November 1937 in Buenos Aires**, barrio of Once, to Erich Bunke and Nadia Bíder, both German communists carrying party cards **[A: EN and ES; EcuRed]**. The birth address is given as the corner of Corrientes and Pasteur, with a later move to Sarmiento 2106 **[R: ES; no street-level corroboration]**.

**Contradiction, the mother's origin.** EN calls Nadia Bíder a Jewish communist from Russia; ES calls her Polish. Not adjudicated. **[A both, mutually exclusive]**

**Contradiction, the year of flight from Germany.** EcuRed gives 1933; EN and DE both give 1935. Two of three for 1935, recorded as the likelier without deleting EcuRed **[A both]**. The research pool for this file contradicted itself here, heading one item 1933 while its geographic lens said 1935.

Primary schooling at the **Escuela Cangallo**, at what is now Teniente General Juan Domingo Perón 2169 **[A: ES, which also attests that her father taught there]**. The repeated detail of German classes at a party circle after hours is **[R]**: the cited source does not carry it. **Coordinate correction:** Perón 2169, Balvanera, is at about -34.6035 / -58.4005; a circulating -34.6053 / -58.4064 is 600 m southwest.

Secondary schooling at **Escuela Normal N.9 [R: ES for the school; no verified building, a gap]**. The family's involvement with the German émigré club **Vorwärts in Quilmes**, Erich Bunke on its board, is **[R]**, and the club's address was not verified.

**Contradiction, the youth organization.** EN has her joining the Free German Youth at about fourteen, dated 1951, in East Germany: impossible as printed, since the family did not leave Argentina until 1952 on that same article's account. ES, for the same year, has her in Buenos Aires joining an Argentine communist youth federation. The journey file places FDJ membership after the 1952 arrival and flags the Argentine claim **[R]**.

## 2. The German Democratic Republic, 1952-1961

The family emigrates and settles in **Stalinstadt**, later Eisenhüttenstadt, the republic's first planned socialist city, on the Oder **[A: EN and DE; EcuRed]**. **Contradiction, year of the move:** EN and DE give 1952, EcuRed 1951 at age twelve **[A both]**.

Joins the **Socialist Unity Party at eighteen, 1955 [A: EN, ES, and EcuRed, all agreeing]**. **Abitur 1956**; **matriculation at Humboldt University, East Berlin, 1958**, at the **Romanistisches Institut [A: DE]**.

**Contradiction, the field of study.** EN says philosophy; ES says Faculty of Letters; DE says Romance languages, 1958. The German account is the most specific and the only one consistent with the 1956 Abitur, and is preferred in the journey file; the disagreement stands. A start date of 1952 is arithmetically impossible: she was fourteen. **Contradiction, the languages:** ES says five, EN lists four **[A both]**.

DE Wikipedia carries the detail that the **Stasi foreign intelligence arm opened a file on her as a Perspektivagentin, prospective agent, number 430/60 [A: DE alone]**. The most consequential GDR-era claim here, and no BStU citation was reached.

**Vienna, 1959**: travels with an Argentine delegation to the VII World Festival of Youth and Students **[A: ES]**. **Leipzig, 1960**: interprets for a Cuban trade delegation and meets **Ernesto Guevara**; also interprets for **Alicia Alonso**, whose invitation decides her on Cuba rather than a return to Argentina **[A: EN and ES; ES narrows the meeting to December 1960]**.

Two of her letters survive in quotation, both via Lois Pérez Leira in *Diario 16*, 2 September 2017, cited by ES. 1956: "¿Podéis escribirme sobre el trabajo del Partido y en especial de la juventud allá en la Argentina?" 1957: "me vuelvo a más tardar a comienzos del 59" **[A at one remove: the article was not fetched, the originals not located, the recipient named only as a friend]**.

## 3. Havana, 1961-1963

**Arrives 12 May 1961** at the National Ballet's invitation, weeks after the Bay of Pigs **[A: EN and ES; EcuRed]**. Works for the Ministry of Education, ICAP, and the Federation of Cuban Women; enrolls in journalism at the University of Havana **[A: same three]**.

From an autobiographical note published 2005, p. 152, quoted by EN Wikipedia: she "became directly involved in revolutionary activities" and "grassroots organizations" shortly after arriving. **Note on the quotation:** two separate quoted fragments joined by "and" in the source; a rendering as one clause with a bracketed "[in]" circulates and fabricates syntax the source does not have **[A at one remove; the note not fetched]**.

**Coordinate correction.** The University of Havana is at 23.1343 / -82.3820, in Vedado; a circulating 23.1358 / -82.3672 lands in Centro Habana, 1.5 km off.

## 4. The making of Tania, 1963-1964

The phase most garbled in circulating summaries, which present the training locations as competing claims. They are a sequence, not a contradiction.

- **Havana, from May 1963**: training with the Dirección General de Inteligencia under Ulises Estrada, with whom she also forms a relationship **[A: DE for the phase, EN for the relationship]**. The safe house is named nowhere reachable.
- **First phase completed 20 February 1964**, then transfer to **Cienfuegos [A: EcuRed]**, where she takes the war name **Tania [A: ES]**, for the Soviet partisan **Zoya Kosmodemyanskaya [A: EN]**. Dating Cienfuegos to 1963 inverts EcuRed's own sequence.
- **Departs Havana 9 April 1964** on a passport in another name **[A: EcuRed]**.
- **Prague, spring 1964**: training with the Czechoslovak StB **[A: EN for the StB, DE for the season]**. The curriculum is described nowhere reachable. This is her second Prague passage; the first, 1961, was a transit stop on the bloc corridor to Havana **[A: DE]**.
- **Frankfurt am Main, 5 August 1964**: arrives under the passport identity "Haydée Bider González" and completes the transition into **"Laura Gutiérrez Bauer"**, Argentine ethnologist **[A: EcuRed]**.

**Source-attribution correction.** The Frankfurt arrival, its date, and the passport name are often cited to ES Wikipedia, in which the word "Frankfurt" does not appear; the material is EcuRed's. The reciprocal error also circulates, citing the Prague StB training to ES Wikipedia, in which "Praga" does not appear.

## 5. La Paz, 1964-1967: Laura Gutiérrez Bauer

**Contradiction, the arrival in Bolivia.** EN and DE give **October 1964**; EcuRed gives arrival in **Peru on 5 November 1964** and a later border crossing, a distinction lost in summaries that pin 5 November to La Paz **[A both]**. The journey file uses October.

October 1964 to January 1965 she builds a network reaching government, diplomatic, and military circles, including **General Alfredo Ovando** and **President René Barrientos [A: EcuRed; EN]**, working as German tutor, folklore researcher, and freelance journalist, and taking a post as secretary to the presidential palace press spokesman **[A: ES]**.

**20 January 1965: permanent residency and a Bolivian passport [A: EcuRed]**. ES names a marriage to **Mario Martínez**, electrical engineering student, but gives it **no date and no city**; the 20 January date belongs to EcuRed and to the residency. Summaries dating the wedding to it, or alternatively to 1966, weld two sources together: **[A]** on the marriage, **[R]** on its date.

Further milestones, all **EcuRed** unless noted and all single-sourced: new communication codes, January 1966; a contact called "Mercy," 1 January 1966 **[ES]**; clandestine meetings in **Mexico**, April 1966 (miscited to ES, in which the trip does not appear); return to La Paz, May 1966; safe houses around **Camiri**, 10 July 1966. ES reports admission to the **Cuban Communist Party** around 1966, carried by no other source **[R]**.

**December 1966**: she conducts **Mario Monje**, general secretary of the Bolivian Communist Party, to the camp at Ñancahuazú **[A: EcuRed]**. The Monje-Guevara meeting over command fractures Bolivian communist support before the campaign begins.

## 6. The column and the blown cover, 1967

**March 1967**: she drives **Régis Debray** and **Ciro Bustos** toward the camp, her last support trip **[A: EcuRed]**. In the same month her vehicle is discovered and the cover collapses **[A: DE, "March 1967: her vehicle discovered, cover blown"]**. EN Wikipedia's account: a captured Bolivian communist revealed a safe house where the jeep stood with her address book in it.

**Two corrections.** Dating the jeep to 1966, as some chronologies do, puts the consequence a year before its cause. And **EN Wikipedia names neither Camiri** as the safe house nor the captured communist; that placement, and the story of plates traced within days, are later constructions. The journey file pins the stop at Camiri with the location hedged in its source line.

Guevara's diary, **27 March 1967**: "zwei Jahre guter und geduldiger Arbeit verloren," two years of good and patient work lost **[A at one remove: seen only in DE Wikipedia's German rendering of *El Diario del Che en Bolivia*, p. 341; the Spanish original was not fetched]**.

She joins the column as a combatant, the only East German in a force of roughly sixty **[A: EN]**. **20 April 1967, Muyupampa**: the column splits when Debray and Bustos are extracted; Tania is among the seventeen of **Joaquín's** rearguard under Juan Vitalio Acuña Núñez **[A: EN; also attested inside che_guevara.journey.json, which names her at this stop]**.

**April to August 1967**: cut off, she suffers a leg injury, fever, and a chigoe flea infestation, and serves as radio monitor, encryptor, and food distributor in the Bella Vista area **[A: EN; no coordinate for Bella Vista was verified and the journey uses the Masicuri corridor, flagged]**.

## 7. Vado del Yeso, 31 August 1967

Joaquín's column, nine including Bunke, is ambushed while fording the **Río Grande at Vado del Yeso**, near Puerto Mauricio. She is shot in the arm and lung and dies in the crossing, aged twenty-nine **[A: EN and ES; EcuRed]**. ES Wikipedia attests the guide: "fue guiada junto a su grupo por Honorato Rojas" **[A]**.

**Correction, the army unit.** The claim that the Eighth Division mounted the ambush is unsourced and probably wrong: neither EN nor ES names a unit; the 8th Division is the Vallegrande formation of Guevara's October capture, while Vado del Yeso is associated with Captain Mario Vargas Salinas of the Manchego Regiment, under the Camiri-based 4th Division. The journey file names no division.

**Correction, the coordinates.** The ford is pinpointed in no reachable source, and two approximations circulate, -18.9667 / -63.9667 and -18.6 / -63.85, about 45 km apart. Rather than publish a third guess, the journey file inherits the canonical pin from che_guevara.journey.json, **-18.617 / -63.983**, byte-identical.

An unfinished letter is found in her rucksack, quoted by Cordt Schnibben in *Der Spiegel*, 23 September 1996, via DE: "Ich weiß nicht, was aus mir werden soll. Wahrscheinlich nichts. Ich versuche mich daran zu erinnern, wie es ist, wenn man Courage hat. Ich bin ein Nichts. Ich bin nicht einmal mehr eine Frau, kein Mädchen, nur noch ein Kind." **[A at one remove: the Spiegel article was not fetched, no verified English translation exists, the date of composition is unknown]**

**6 September 1967**: the body is recovered **[A: EN; EcuRed]** and taken to **Vallegrande**, buried in unmarked ground **[A]**. A tradition that local women intervened to secure a Christian burial comes from local testimony via EN Wikipedia **[R]**. Guevara's body reaches Vallegrande on 9 October 1967, thirty-nine days after the ambush: about five weeks, not the "more than two months" some accounts give.

## 8. The poem

EN Wikipedia carries a quote box cited to Jon Lee Anderson, *Che Guevara: A Revolutionary Life* (1997), p. 689, titled only "Leave a Memory":

> So I must leave, like flowers that wilt? / Will my name one day be forgotten / And nothing of me remain on the earth? / At least, flowers and song. / How then, must my heart behave? / Is it in vain that we live, that we appear on the earth?

Three cautions, all honored in the journey file by placing the lines in the afterlife rather than in her own voice at a Bolivian campfire.

1. The Spanish title "Deja un recuerdo," widely attached to these lines, is **attested in no reachable source [R, probably an invention]**.
2. The attribution rests solely on Anderson via Wikipedia. Page 689 was not fetched, so whether she composed the lines or copied them into a notebook is **unresolved**.
3. The lines closely echo the *Cantares Mexicanos* poem attributed to **Nezahualcóyotl**: "¿He de irme como las flores que perecieron? ... ¡al menos flores, al menos cantos!" This could not be checked (see Sources). Until Anderson p. 689 is read, the lines should not be called her original composition.

No date or place of composition is attested; the usual placement in "Bolivia, final period" is inference.

## 9. Afterlife, 1967-2014

- **November 1995**: retired general **Mario Vargas Salinas** reveals that Guevara's body was never cremated, opening the search that locates the guerrilla graves **[A: EN]**. **1997**: Guevara's remains located **[A]**.
- **13 October 1998**: her remains found in a small pit at the periphery of the Vallegrande army base **[R on the exact date]**. ES gives only "1998", DE dates the identification to **September 1998**: contested, not adjudicated.
- **29 December 1998**: interred with nine other guerrillas in the **Che Guevara Mausoleum, Santa Clara [A: Wikipedia, "Che Guevara Mausoleum"]**. The vault holds Guevara plus twenty-nine combatants, thirty in all, in four groups between 1997 and 2000; the repeated "roughly 28 others" is wrong. **Coordinate correction:** 22.40222 / -79.97944, not the circulating 22.4041 / -79.9658, 1.4 km east.
- **GDR commemoration**: more than 200 schools, FDJ brigades, kindergartens, and a Berlin youth club carried her name, nearly all renamed after 1989-90; a memorial stone at **Dabel, Mecklenburg**, 1984, for a Democratic Women's League group carrying her name since 1977 **[A: DE]**. **Coordinate correction:** Dabel is at 53.667 / 11.900; a circulating 53.6167 / 12.2833 lands 25 km east in open country.
- **Minor planet 2283 Bunke**, discovered 26 September 1974 **[A: EN and DE]**; ***Desmopachria taniae***, a diving beetle described from Bolivia in 1999, and Heidi Specogna's documentary ***Tania la Guerrillera***, 1991 **[A: EN]**; Hospital Materno Norte "Tamara Bunke," Santiago de Cuba, and tribute songs by **Alí Primera** and **Patricio Manns [R, all undated]**.
- **2014**: Cuba Sí transfers part of her papers to ICAP in Havana; her estate is catalogued at HTW Berlin under Oliver Rump **[A: DE]**. A tradition holds that unknown hands leave flowers each 31 August where her body was found: "Desde entonces, cada 31 de agosto, manos disconocidas llenan de flores aquel lugar donde se encontró su cuerpo" **[R: EcuRed, undated]**.

## 11. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. The **Havana DGI safe house** of May 1963 is unnamed anywhere reachable; the pin is a general Havana location. The **StB course content** at Prague, 1964, is described nowhere: only presence and season are attested.
2. **Bella Vista** has no verified coordinate, and the **ford at Vado del Yeso** has never been surveyed in the reachable literature.
3. **Escuela Normal N.9's building** and the **Club Vorwärts address** in Quilmes were not verified.
4. **Anderson p. 689** was not read, leaving the poem's authorship open. The **recipients of the 1956 and 1957 letters** are unnamed and the originals were not located.
5. Whether the **marriage to Mario Martínez** was one of convenience alone is settled by no reachable source, and the **month of the 1998 identification** is contested across the three Wikipedia editions.
