# Patrice Émery Lumumba (1925-1961): research report
*Atlas of Journeys. Compiled 2026-08-11 from the verified pool for `patrice_lumumba`.*

**Method.** Every line carries a tag. **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, estimate, or the atlas's own framing. Where verification overturned the gathering pass, both readings are printed and the fix is marked **CORRECTED**; where two sources disagree and neither wins, the disagreement stands. Coordinates the pool itself calls estimates are tagged [R] even where the event is [A]. Unreachable sources are named with the reason at the end. This report is where the hedges live, so the campa does not have to carry them.

---

## 1. Onalua and the Sankuru country (1925-1944)

- Born 2 July 1925 in Onalua, Katako-Kombe region, Kasaï province, Belgian Congo, to a Tetela family; birth name Isaïe Tasumbu Tawosa. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Patrice Lumumba']**
- **CORRECTED (geography).** The gathering pin -6.05, 23.6 is essentially Mbuji-Mayi in Kasaï-Oriental, some 300 km south of the birthplace and in Luba country. Katako-Kombe's territorial centre is -3.40, 24.42 (English Wikipedia); the journey uses approximately **-3.4, 24.4**, tagged [R], since no source in the pool fixes the village. **[R: coordinate estimate]**
- **CORRECTED (place-name, name).** "Tshilenge lands" is wrong: Tshilenge is Luba territory near Mbuji-Mayi; Onalua is Sankuru, and the article notes the birthplace was split off around 2022 to form the new city of Lumumbaville. The middle name is Émery, not "Hemery". **[A: Wikipedia, 'Katako-Kombe']**
- Educated at a Protestant primary school, a Catholic missionary school, and a government post-office training school; fluent in Tetela, French, Lingala, Swahili and Tshiluba. **[A]**
- Which mission station, and in what order the two schoolings fell, is unresolved; no source names the building. The journey stop keeps [R] and the same Sankuru pin. **[R]**
- Clerked for a mining concern in Maniema / Sud-Kivu around 1943-1944, with Kindu (-2.9469, 25.9333) as the regional anchor; no source names the compound. **[R: French Wikipedia]** Report only.

## 2. Stanleyville: the post office and the associations (1945-1957)

- 1945: married Henriette Maletaua; traveling beer salesman in Léopoldville, then postal clerk in Stanleyville, eleven years on the great bend of the river. **[A]**
- 1947: divorced, married Hortense Sombosia. 1951: divorced, married **Pauline Opango**, then around fourteen; she remained his wife until his death. **[A]** **CORRECTED (spelling):** the gathering pass wrote "Opangu".
- 1952: assistant to the sociologist Pierre Clément; co-founded the ADAPÉS alumni association. 1955: regional head of the Cercles network; joined the Liberal Party of Belgium. **[A]**
- 1956: wrote an autobiography; arrested on embezzlement charges tied to postal funds after a study tour in Belgium; convicted 1957, twelve months and a fine. **[A]** The pool carries two framings and resolves neither: peculation by a man living beyond a clerk's salary, or a colonial state making an example of an African too visible in associational politics. **[R: both recorded]**

## 3. Léopoldville, the MNC, and Accra (1957-1959)

- Released, moved to Léopoldville, sales director for the Brasserie de Léo et du Bas-Congo. **[A: French Wikipedia]** Month not fixed; the journey dates it 1957-07-01 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
- 5 October 1958: co-founded the Mouvement National Congolais, the first nationwide, non-ethnically-based Congolese party, and led it. **[A]**
- December 1958: All-African Peoples' Conference in Accra; met Kwame Nkrumah; returned pushing the MNC toward outright independence. **[A]**
- October 1959: arrested for inciting an anti-colonial riot in Stanleyville in which about thirty people were killed. **[A]**

## 4. Prison, the Round Table, and 30 June (1959-1960)

- **CONTRADICTION, carried.** The gathering pass had the trial opening 18 January 1960 and release before the Round Table. Verification found no support for 18 January; fr.wiki gives **sentencing 21 January** (six months) and **release 26 January 1960**, while the conference had opened in Brussels on **20 January**, so his release fell six days into it. The journey follows fr.wiki and says so in `date_confidence`.
- 27 January 1960: independence fixed for 30 June 1960. **[A]**
- 11-25 May 1960: the MNC won a plurality in the first national elections. **[A]**
- 21 June: named formateur. 23 June: the 37-member government formed, approved by the Chamber 74-5-1 that night and the Senate 60-12-8 early on 24 June. **[A]**
- **CORRECTED.** He became prime minister on **24 June 1960**, on parliament's approval; English Wikipedia gives the premiership as 24 June to 5 September 1960. He was therefore already prime minister at the independence ceremony rather than becoming prime minister at it. **[A]**
- 30 June 1960: the unscheduled speech before King Baudouin. **[A]** **CORRECTED (geography):** the Palais de la Nation is at approximately **-4.3025, 15.28** (fr.wiki: 4°18′09″S, 15°16′48″E); the gathering pin sat about 5 km southeast.

## 5. Ten weeks of a republic (July 1960)

- 5 July: Force Publique mutiny. 6 July: dismissed the Belgian commander Émile Janssens. 8 July: renamed the force the Armée Nationale Congolaise, Victor Lundula general, Joseph Mobutu chief of staff. **[A]**
- 10 July: Belgium dispatched roughly 6,000 troops without the government's consent. 11 July: Moïse Tshombe declared Katanga's secession. 14 July: Lumumba broke relations with Belgium and cabled Khrushchev for support. **[A]**
- 24-26 July: met Dag Hammarskjöld in New York. 27 July: met US officials in Washington, Christian Herter among them. **[A]**
- 28 July: the TASS interview, source of two of the sharpest quotations in the pool. **[A: Lumumba Internet Archive, marxists.org]**
- 29 July: Ottawa, requesting Canadian assistance. **[A]** Report only.
- Early August: toured Tunisia, Morocco, Guinea, Ghana, Liberia and Togo, and reached an agreement with Nkrumah envisioning a "Union of African States". **[A]** **CONTRADICTION, carried:** the chronology lens says "signed a secret agreement"; the geography lens says "never signed, never ratified". The campa states only that the two men discuss it and that nothing is ratified.
- Days within the August circuit are not fixed beyond the month; the journey assigns working days and marks them `date_confidence: "month attested, day inferred"`. **[R]**

## 6. The unmaking (August-November 1960)

- 9 August: state of emergency. 16 August: a special military regime announced for six months. **[A]**
- Late August: troops into South Kasai; the ANC reached Bakwanga on 27 August; roughly 3,000 Luba civilians died before the army withdrew in September. **[A]**
- **CORRECTED.** The South Kasai secession did **not** end in 1961. Per English Wikipedia, 'South Kasai', it collapsed on 5 October 1962, when central government troops entered Bakwanga after the coup of 29-30 September and Kalonji fled into exile. **[A]**
- July 1960 onward: Belgian officials in Brussels discussed plans to "get rid of" Lumumba. **[A: 'Assassination of Patrice Lumumba']**
- August 1960: Eisenhower authorized a CIA operation to assassinate him; Allen Dulles had called his removal "an urgent and prime objective". **[A: Church Committee findings via Wikipedia]**
- September 1960: CIA chemist Sidney Gottlieb delivered botulinum toxin to station chief Larry Devlin, for Lumumba's food or toothpaste; Devlin never carried it out, and in November officer Michael Mulroney refused the same plot, preferring to see Lumumba delivered into hostile custody. **[A]** Days not given; the journey dates the vials 1960-09-01 with month-only confidence.
- 5-6 September: Kasa-Vubu announced the dismissal by radio, order countersigned 6 September at 16:00; Lumumba announced Kasa-Vubu's dismissal in turn. **[A]**
- **CORRECTED (two points).** The Chamber's 60-19 vote of 7 September annulled **both** dismissals, not only Kasa-Vubu's of Lumumba. The Senate's confidence vote of 8 September was **49-0 with 7 abstentions**. **[A: English Wikipedia]**
- 14 September: Mobutu announced a "peaceful revolution", neutralizing Kasa-Vubu, Lumumba and Parliament together. **[A]** That Lumumba went to Camp Léopold II that evening seeking Mobutu is [R] and stays [R].
- October 1960: Belgian minister for African affairs Harold d'Aspremont Lynden cabled for the "définitive elimination" of Lumumba. **[A]** Day not fixed.
- Late September to November: confined at the Kalina residence, a UN cordon outside and Congolese soldiers inside. 24 November: the UN General Assembly seated Mobutu's delegates in place of his government's. **[A]**

## 7. Capture and captivity (November 1960 - January 1961)

- 27 November: slipped past the guards and left Léopoldville in a convoy, east toward Stanleyville, where Gizenga's loyalists held the province. **[A]**
- 30 November: the roadside stop at Mweka, where he is said to have paused to address a crowd and lost the margin that cost him his freedom. **[R: De Witte, *The Assassination of Lumumba*; tradition, not settled fact]**
- 1 December: captured at a Sankuru crossing near Lodi, Ghanaian UN peacekeepers nearby not intervening; Pauline and their young son seized with him, and he reportedly surrendered fearing for their safety. **[A for the event]** **CORRECTED (tagging):** the pool concedes the coordinates are an estimate, so the pin -4.75, 22.35 is **[R]**, not [A].
- 2 December: held briefly at Port-Francqui (Ilebo), flown to Léopoldville, beaten before cameras on arrival. **[A]**
- December into January: Camp Hardy, Thysville, on deliberately poor rations. **[A]**
- 4 January 1961: the letter to A. M. Dayal, UN Special Representative. **[A: Lumumba Internet Archive]** **CORRECTED (quotation integrity):** the pool's version splices two non-contiguous passages. In the source, "at no time have we been permitted to leave them" is followed by the passage on the two bad meals a day and the denied requests for fruit and evening walks, before the clothing and shoes sentences. The journey prints the quotation **with an explicit ellipsis**.
- The letter to Pauline is undated in the archive and circulated after his death as his "last letter". The journey dates it 1961-01-13 for placement only and says so. **[R for the date; A for the text]**
- 13 January: discipline among the Camp Hardy guards faltered, prompting Congolese and Belgian officials to arrange the transfer to Katanga, understood to be likely fatal. **[A]**

## 8. Katanga: the killing and the disposal (17-21 January 1961)

- 17 January, afternoon: a DC-4 carrying Lumumba, Maurice Mpolo and Joseph Okito landed at Luano, Élisabethville; the three were beaten on the tarmac. Evening: held at the requisitioned farmhouse of Lucien Brouwez while Belgian officers and Katangan ministers, Munongo among them, decided their fate. **[A]**
- Night: shot by three successive firing squads near Shilatembo on the Mwadingusha road, under orders of the Belgian officer Julien Gat, Katangan officials present. A Belgian commissioner's datebook records "9:43 L Dead". **[A]**
- **CORRECTED (geography).** The afterlife lens placed the execution at -11.6609, 27.4794, which is Lubumbashi itself; the killing was some 50 km out in the bush. The journey uses the geography lens's **-10.85, 27.32**. **[A for the event; R for the precision]**
- 18 January: first exhumation, the bodies moved toward Kasenga near the Northern Rhodesian border. **[A]**
- 21 January: second exhumation on Munongo's order; dismembered and dissolved in sulfuric acid supplied by Union Minière du Haut-Katanga, over two days, by the Belgian police officer Gerard Soete and his brother Michel. **[A]** The clearing is not fixed; the pin -11.5, 27.4 is an estimate, **[R]**.
- 13 February: Munongo announced the death publicly, falsely claiming the three had escaped and been killed by villagers. **[A]**

## 9. Afterlife (1961-2024)

- 22 February 1961: the Moscow university founded 5 February 1960 was named Patrice Lumumba University. **[A: English Wikipedia, 'Patrice Lumumba Peoples' Friendship University']** **CORRECTED:** the name was **removed 5 February 1992** and **restored only in March 2023**; the gathering text compressed a 31-year gap out of existence. Coordinates 55.651, 37.505.
- 1966: Mobutu consecrated Lumumba as a national hero and gave his name to the former Boulevard Léopold II, erecting a statue at its head. **[A: French Wikipedia]** **CORRECTED:** the gathering text put the statue on Boulevard du 30 Juin, the former Boulevard Albert I, a different artery. The monument stands at the Limete interchange, approximately -4.3747, 15.3456. Fr.wiki also dates a monument installation there to 17 January 2002. **[R: erection date and sculptor not established]**
- 1962: *La pensée politique de Patrice Lumumba*, assembled by Jean Van Lierde, preface by Sartre. **[R: bibliography unconfirmed, see Sources]**
- 1975: the Church Committee confirmed the CIA had conspired to assassinate him, while finding no direct US hand in the killing itself, carried out by Belgian and Katangan actors. **[A]**
- 1999: Soete's admissions about the remains. **[R, downgraded]** The afterlife lens says he displayed a tooth on Belgian television and kept "a tooth and two finger bones"; Dutch Wikipedia says he wrung **two incisors** from the jaw and attaches 1999 chiefly to De Witte's *De Moord op Patrice Lumumba*, for which Soete gave his account. One incisor was recovered in 2016 at his daughter's residence. **Contradiction carried.**
- **CORRECTED (geography).** Soete did not live in Uccle. Dutch Wikipedia has him moving to Bruges in 1972 and living in Sint-Kruis until his death on 9 June 2000. The journey uses approximately **51.21, 3.25**; the gathering pin was off by roughly 250 km. **[A]**
- 2001: a Belgian parliamentary commission concluded that Belgium bore moral responsibility. 2002: the government issued a formal apology. **[A]** Day of the apology not given.
- 14 May 2000: Raoul Peck's *Lumumba*, with Eriq Ebouaney, premiered at Cannes, shot in Zimbabwe and Mozambique because filming in the DRC was not possible. **[A]** Report only.
- 10 September 2020: a Belgian court ordered the tooth handed to judicial authorities. 20 June 2022: Belgium handed it to his children at Egmont Palace, De Croo apologizing. 30 June 2022: interred in a new Kinshasa mausoleum amid three days of national mourning. **[A]**
- **CORRECTED (two points).** 30 June 2022 is the **sixty-second** anniversary of independence, not the sixty-first; and the mausoleum is in **Limete**, approximately -4.3778, 15.3481 (fr.wiki), not central Gombe. **[A]**
- 18 November 2024: the mausoleum was vandalized; the tooth reportedly remained secure. **[A]**

## Contradictions carried, not resolved

1. **January 1960.** Trial opening 18 January (unsupported) versus sentencing 21 January and release 26 January (fr.wiki).
2. **The Nkrumah union.** "Signed a secret agreement" versus "never signed, never ratified", between two lenses of the same pool.
3. **The Soete relics.** A tooth and two finger bones shown on television in 1999, versus two incisors described to De Witte with one recovered in 2016.
4. **The 1956 case.** Ordinary embezzlement versus a colonial prosecution.
5. **The Limete statue.** Erected 1966 under Mobutu, or installed at the Place de la Reconstruction on 17 January 2002.
6. **Camp Léopold II, 14 September 1960.** That Lumumba went to the camp seeking Mobutu appears in one lens only, uncorroborated elsewhere.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- **Myth, prophecy and folk material about his afterlife: none found.** The Wikipedia legacy sections returned truncated or empty results within the session's access limits. A stated gap, not a documented absence: an oral corpus almost certainly exists in Kinshasa and the Sankuru and was not reachable here.
- **The mission school** is unlocated (Tshumbe or another Sankuru station). **Onalua** has no coordinate in the pool; the pin is the territorial centre. **The Lodi crossing** and **the clearing of the acid barrels** have no fixed coordinates.
- **The letter to Pauline** has no date of composition; **the 2002 apology** has no day; **the August 1960 circuit** has no days, only a month and an ordering of capitals.
