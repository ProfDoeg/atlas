# Amir Khusrow Dehlavi, 1253-1325: research report

*2026-08-09. Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Abu'l Hasan Yamin ud-Din Khusrau, called Tuti-e-Hind, the Parrot of India. Born at Patiyali, buried beside his shaikh in the Nizamuddin Dargah. Court poet to seven sultans of three dynasties; the first major writer of Hindavi whose name we have.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested in a named, reachable source. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or attribution repeated without a documented origin. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; none is adjudicated.

---

## The governing problem

Khusrow is the opposite of the usual atlas difficulty. He wrote an enormous amount himself, much of it dated, and his texts are the primary source for the politics of the Delhi Sultanate between 1289 and 1325. The trouble is threefold.

**The works tables disagree with the chronologies.** Wikipedia carries both a narrative chronology and a table of works, giving different years for the same books (Miftah ul-Futuh, Duval Rani, Tughlaq Nama). The pool inherited both and presented each as settled; four dates are marked split.

**The attributions are porous.** Wikipedia itself warns that for Khusrow "the authenticity of works ascribed to him [cannot] be determined with any confidence" **[A]**. Books, riddles, dohas, musical forms and instruments accreted to his name for seven centuries.

**Most corrections below are [A] tags on claims the cited source does not support.** Three quotations were fabricated or half-fabricated. One entry made a prose chronicle a masnavi. One put a prince in a province he would not govern for two more years. Five coordinates were wrong by 2 to 28 km.

## Phase 1: the refugee line and Patiyali (before 1230 - 1271)

**Ancestry.** The father's family originates at **Kesh (Shahrisabz)** in Transoxiana, flees the Mongol advance, passes through Balkh, and is granted refuge and land by Sultan Iltutmish **[R, Wikipedia]**. No year is given. The atlas holds **genghis_khan**, whose 1220s campaigns are the displacement in question; parallel, not a link either file makes.

**Birth, 1253, at Patiyali** in Kasganj district, on his father's fief **[A, Iranica (Schimmel, 1989); Britannica; Wikipedia]**. Father **Amir Sayf al-Din Mahmud**, a Turkish officer; mother Indian, daughter of Imad ul-Mulk. *Coordinate corrected ~26 km:* the pool gave 27.9508, 79.0464, open country north of the town; Patiyali is **27.717, 79.017**.

**Father's death, 1260** **[A]**; Khusrow is raised by his maternal grandfather **Imad ul-Mulk, called Rawat Arz**, who dies in 1273 at a reported 113 **[R, household tradition]**.

> **Two errors corrected.** (1) *Arithmetic:* born 1253 and orphaned in 1260 makes him **seven**, not "about eight or nine" (Wikipedia says eight, itself a stretch). (2) *Doubled grandfather:* Imad ul-Mulk **is** Rawat Arz, one man with a title; the pool's phrasing implied two.

**Tuhfat us-Sighr, 1271** **[A]**, the first divan, verse of about ages sixteen to eighteen.

**The shaikh at sixteen, c. 1269** **[R]**. By his own account in the preface to Wast ul-Hayat, Khusrow was sixteen when he found Nizam ad-Din Auliya. **Contradiction, unresolved:** the commonly cited date of formal discipleship is **1310**, when he would be fifty-seven. Both are carried as separate stops, each stating the conflict in `date_confidence`. The preface was not consulted; this reaches us through secondary summary only. Nizam ad-Din Auliya has **no journey file yet**, so he is named in campa as a person, not counted as a file interlock.

## Phase 2: the princes of the house of Balban (1272 - 1285)

**Malik Chhajju, c. 1272** **[R]**, nephew of Balban, first court position, on secondary biography only. **Nasir al-Din Bughra Khan, 1276** **[A]**, son of Balban. **Wast ul-Hayat, 1279** **[A]** for the date. The strongest error in the incoming set is here.

> **Anachronism corrected.** The pool had Khusrow in Bengal in 1279 in Bughra Khan's service, finishing the divan there. Bughra Khan was **not in Bengal in 1279**: Balban marched there in **1281** to crush Tughril Tughan Khan, took his son with him, and installed him at Lakhnauti only after the rebel was killed. The governorship runs **1281-1287** (Wikipedia; Banglapedia). Either the date or the place has to go. **This file keeps 1279 for the divan at Delhi and moves the Bengal stop to 1281 [R].**

**Multan, 1281-1285** **[A]**, court poet to **Khan Muhammad**, Balban's eldest son and heir, governor of Multan. *Residual problem:* the corrected Bengal date and the Multan start collide in 1281 and no source reached orders them. Stated in `date_confidence`, not resolved.

**9 March 1285, the death of Khan Muhammad** **[A for the date]**. Three corrections:

1. **Place.** The engagement is conventionally the **Battle of the Beas River** in the Punjab, not a raid on Multan Fort; pinning the death at Multan misplaces it by hundreds of km. No battlefield coordinate was reached, so the file pins the city he rode out from and says so.
2. **Commander.** Wikipedia gives the Chagatai commander as **unknown**. The pool's "Timur Khan" is unsupported.
3. **Captivity.** "Taken captive, later escaping" is **[R]**: a few days (Habib), two years at Balkh (Shibli Nomani), ransom rather than escape (others). Tag downgraded.

The two elegies are attested, and one line is verified in Losensky and Sharma: *"People shed so many tears in all directions that five other rivers have appeared in Multan."* **[A]**

## Phase 3: Qaiqabad and the first masnavi (1287 - 1289)

**Court poet to Muiz al-Din Qaiqabad, 1287** **[A]**. **The meeting of father and son**, Bughra Khan out of Bengal against Qaiqabad out of Delhi, recorded by Khusrow as eyewitness in **Qiran us-Sa'dain, 1289** **[A for the masnavi and date]**.

> **Place and year unsupported; tag downgraded to [R].** The pool put the meeting on the **Sarayu bank in Awadh in 1288**, at 26.7996, 82.1998. That coordinate is simply modern Ayodhya, a stand-in with no attestation. Wikipedia's "Muiz ud din Qaiqabad" places it "in Northern Bihar" and names no river and no year; the year is bracketed only by the poem's completion in 1289. **This is the weakest pin in the file, labelled as such in its own `date_confidence`.**

## Phase 4: the Khalji ascendant (1290 - 1300)

**Jalal al-Din Firuz Khalji, 1290** **[A, Iranica]**; Khusrow granted the title **Amir** and the office of **Mushaf-dar** **[A]**. **Ghurrat ul-Kamal, 1294** **[A]**, third divan.

**Miftah ul-Futuh** **[A for existence]**, on the Ranthambore campaign. **Date contested:** the works table gives **1290**, the pool's chronology **1291**; the siege was fought 1290-91 and failed **[A, Wikipedia, "Ranthambore Fort"]**. Both years stand. *Coordinate corrected ~2 km:* the fort is at **26.0203, 76.4550**.

**Kara-Manikpur, c. 1294-96** **[R]** for Khusrow's presence in Ali Gurshasp's provincial court; the pool's hedge is kept. *Coordinate corrected ~26 km:* Kara is at **25.700, 81.350** in Kaushambi district; the pool's figure landed south of the Yamuna, the wrong side of the river.

**19 July 1296: Alauddin murders Jalal al-Din at Kara and takes the throne** **[A]**. Khusrow is kept on; Iranica notes without comment that he was the favourite of both **[A]**.

**Khamsa-e-Khusrau, 1298** **[A, Iranica, "Khamsa of Amir Khusrow"]**, five masnavis written as a *nazira* against **Nizami Ganjavi's** Khamsa. nizami.journey.json already holds Nizami (1141-1209, Ganja): two poets who never meet, one answering the other across a century **[A, cross-file]**.

**Mother and a brother die, 1300** **[A]**. No further detail anywhere reached: no name for the brother, no cause.

## Phase 5: the chronicle of the conquests (1301 - 1312)

> **Two errors on Khaza'in ul-Futuh, the pool's worst.** **(1) Genre:** it is a **prose chronicle**, not a masnavi; the chronology lens said masnavi while the geography and afterlife lenses said prose, contradicting each other. The verse treatment of the same campaigns is a separate work, the **Tarikh-i-Alai**. **(2) Date:** completed **c. 1311-1312**, since it narrates the Ma'bar campaign of 1311. The "1296" in the works table is Alauddin's accession year, not a composition date.

| Campaign | Date | Tag | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ranthambore taken | 1301 | [A] | Khusrow with the royal camp per summary sources |
| Chittorgarh | 28 Jan - Aug 1303 | [A] | the Padmini legend is in no early account, his own included |
| Malwa | 1305 | [R] | presence versus dispatches not established |
| Devagiri | 1307 or 1308 | [A/R] | see below |
| Siwana | Aug-Sept 1308 | [A] | defender Sitala Deva |
| Warangal | 1310 | [R] | presence disputed; the Koh-i-Noor is tradition |
| Dwarasamudra | 1311 | [R] | sharp-stones detail argues eyewitness, no proof |
| Ma'bar / Madurai | from 10 Mar 1311 | [R] | march date is Khusrow's own |

> **Devagiri: half right, conclusion flipped.** Khusrow *does* date the invasion to March 1307 and *does* narrate it after Siwana. But Siwana was fought August-September 1308, so **his own text is internally inconsistent**. Kishori Saran Lal, following Barani, holds 1308 correct. The pool gave 1307 as the event date; it is given here as Khusrow's disputed dating against a scholarly 1308. The campaign was led by **Malik Kafur**, not Alauddin. *Siwana coordinate corrected ~28 km:* **25.633, 72.4167**, not 25.8833.

**Capital pin corrected.** The pool reused **28.5245, 77.1855** (Qila Rai Pithora, Mehrauli, the Mamluk city) for every Delhi stop from 1260 to 1320, flattening three capitals into one. From 1303 the Khalji seat is **Siri** (**28.5265, 77.2215**); from 1321, Tughlaqabad. The file uses all three in their periods.

## Phase 6: Ghiyaspur (undated within his life)

> **Dargah coordinate unified.** Three lenses disagreed on the same site (geography 28.5885, 77.2384; afterlife 28.5905, 77.2432). The Nizamuddin Dargah is at **28.5914, 77.24197**; the journey file uses **28.5914, 77.2420** at every shrine stop.

**Qawwali** **[R]**. Widely credited to Khusrow as founder, fusing Persian, Arabic and Turkic modes with Indian ones at the khanqah. **No contemporaneous 13th or 14th-century document names him as its inventor**; the attribution solidifies in later hagiography and is then repeated as settled. That his verse dominates the surviving repertoire is not in doubt.

**Instruments** **[R, disputed]**. The sitar (a *tritantri* cut down and renamed *seh-tar*) and the tabla are both popularly his. **Wahid Mirza, his own biographer, could confirm neither.** The *tritantri* and *saptantri* predate him, and one theory hands the sitar to a **different, later Amir Khusru of the eighteenth century**; sources contradict each other on whether the two men are distinguished at all.

## Phase 7: the last sultans (1316 - 1320)

**Alauddin dies 4 January 1316** **[A]**.

> **A whole reign compressed out.** The pool had Mubarak Shah succeeding his father directly. He did not: **Malik Kafur installed the child Shihabuddin Omar and ruled as regent**, and only after Kafur's murder did **Qutb ud-Din Mubarak Shah** depose Omar, in **April 1316** **[A]**.

**Duval Rani wa Khizr Khan** **[A for existence]**, two errors: (1) *Date:* the works table gives **1316**, the pool's chronology **1315**; split, unresolved. (2) *Patronage inverted:* it was **not** commissioned by Alauddin but written at **Khizr Khan's own request**, and since the tragic ending was added under Mubarak Shah, the finished work postdates the sultan the pool credited with ordering it.

**Baqia-Naqia, 1317** **[A]**. **Nuh Sipihr, 1318** **[A, works table]**, on Mubarak Shah's reign, with the ethnographic praise of India. *The pool gave no composition date for it at all; that gap is filled.*

**Afzal ul-Fawaid, 1319.** **Tag downgraded from [A] to [R].** Date and prose genre match the works table, but the attribution is not secure: Wikipedia's caution about ascribed works applies, and the securely attributed *malfuzat* of Nizam ad-Din Auliya is **Amir Hasan Sijzi's Fawa'id al-Fu'ad**. The search budget ran out before the dispute could be checked, so this is flagged rather than proven.

**1320: Mubarak Shah assassinated by Khusrau Khan; Ghiyath al-Din Tughlaq takes Delhi; Tughlaq Nama** **[A]**. *Date:* works table and chronology both give **1320**; the geography lens said "around 1321," contradicting its companion entry and probably borrowing the fort's construction date. *Coordinate ~1 km:* Tughlaqabad Fort is **28.51194, 77.26083**.

## Phase 8: the two graves (1325)

**Nizam ad-Din Auliya dies 3 April 1325 at Ghiyaspur** **[A]**. Khusrow is by tradition away on a Tughlaq campaign in the east **[R]**, returns to find him buried, and recites the Hindavi doha at the grave.

> **The doha, corrected in both directions.** The pool said no primary verification existed and that even Khusrow's authorship was disputed; that **overstates the doubt**, since Losensky and Sharma print it as what he recited on hearing of the death. But the pool's *text* is the popular qawwali variant. The critical reading is *gori sove sej par mukh par dare kes / chal Khusrau ghar apne sanjh bhai(n) sau des*; the submitted *"rain bhayi chahun des"* is an oral variant, real in print but not in this selection. It is a **doha (couplet)**, not a quatrain.

**Khusrow dies at Delhi, October 1325**, about six months after his pir, and is buried beside him **[A, Wikipedia; Iranica gives 725 AH]**. Death by grief is devotional tradition **[R]**.

## Phase 9: afterlife

- **1334**: **Ibn Battuta** reaches Delhi and is made qadi of the capital for seven or eight years **[A, ibn_battuta.journey.json; pin 28.5244, 77.1855 inherited byte-identical]**. The poet is nine years dead.
- **1485**: a **Khamsah** miniature attributed to **Bihzad** at Herat, now Chester Beatty Library, Dublin; further folios at the Met **[R on the attribution, A on the holdings]**. **No likeness made in Khusrow's lifetime survives.**
- **1605**: the present marble tomb with jali screens, built under Jahangir by **Khwaja Imad ud-Din Hassan** **[R]**. Nothing of the 1325 marker remains.
- **1949 / 1953**: **Wahid Mirza**'s editions, *Nuh Sipihr* (OUP) and *Khaza'in ul-Futuh* (Asiatic Society, Calcutta) **[A]**. *Correction: the pool gave 1950 for Nuh Sipihr; the Internet Archive record (in.ernet.dli.2015.335188) gives **1949**.* Translations: Khazain, Lahore 1975 **[A]**; *India as Seen by Amir Khusrau*, Jaipur 1981 **[A, unchecked]**.
- **2001**: **Jahan-e-Khusrau** founded by Muzaffar Ali at Arab ki Sarai **[A]**. *Correction: the pool hedged "2000/2001"; Wikipedia is unambiguous on 2001. Three days, February or March, **not held in 2008**.*
- **2011**: **In the Bazaar of Love**, trans. Losensky and Sharma **[A]**, source of most verified quotations here.
- **Ongoing**: the urs at the dargah, and the *paheliyan* in circulation **[R]**.

## Quotations: three fabrications found and removed

The most serious problem in the pool, worth stating plainly because the fabricated text carried real page numbers in a real 1871 edition.

1. **Nuh Sipihr, Elliot and Dowson III, p. 559.** Submitted: *"...and mounds of slain lay in the streets and lanes of the city."* The full volume was downloaded and grepped: **"mounds of slain" and "streets and lanes" appear nowhere in it.** Only the opening clause is real, and even that reads "to the gate." The pool's note was wrong too: this is **Khusrau Khan's** Arangal campaign under Mubarak Shah, not Malik Kafur's under Alauddin. **Dropped from the journey file.**
2. **Nuh Sipihr, p. 563, on the Vedas.** Submitted: the four books "contain all sorts of information and rules of their religious tenets." The verified text **reverses the sense**: they "contain stories of their gods, but little advantage can be derived from their perusal." The submitted version credits the Vedas; the translation disparages them. The verified wording is what the journey file quotes.
3. **Khazain ul-Futuh on Somnath**, Habib trans. via Sarkar. Submitted: *"...its high place was made low, the abode of idolatry became the qibla of true believers."* Half fabricated; Habib's real continuation has the temple bow, jump into the sea, and take a bath after its prayers. Verified wording used.
4. **Upgrade in the other direction.** *"If honey is useful, vinegar too has its buyers,"* submitted as untraceable [R], is genuine: Losensky and Sharma, quoting the conclusion of the **Hasht Bihisht**. Used at the Khamsa stop. Still untraceable and unused: *"Khusrau darya prem ka, ulti wa ki dhaar..."*

## Honest gaps

- **The Northern Bihar meeting has no geolocation**, and **no Beas battlefield coordinate** was found; both pins are admissions of ignorance and say so.
- **Khusrow's own prefaces were not read.** The age-sixteen claim rests on secondary summary. **The Afzal ul-Fawaid dispute was not searched**; budget exhausted.
- **Whether Khusrow travelled with Malik Kafur's southern armies is unresolved.** Four stops (Malwa, Warangal, Dwarasamudra, Ma'bar) are [R] on his presence and [A] on the campaign.
- **The Hindavi corpus cannot be sorted.** How much of the doha, riddle and folk-song material is his is not answerable from anything consulted, and the sources repeating it do not raise the question.
- **No lifetime portrait exists**; every painted face is at least a century posthumous.
