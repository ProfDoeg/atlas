# Empress Joséphine: research report
*Marie-Josèphe-Rose Tascher de La Pagerie, vicomtesse de Beauharnais, Empress of the French (1763-1814). Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys from the verified pool for slug `empress_josephine`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named on the spot. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or consensus not pinned to a document in the sources actually reached. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and are **not** adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

A structural note first. Joséphine is among the most written-about women of the nineteenth century and among the least documented in her own voice. Hall's introduction records that the Collection Didot holds 228 letters from Napoleon to Joséphine, 70 from Joséphine to Hortense, and **two** from Joséphine to Napoleon, apparently the only two of unquestioned authenticity in existence **[A: Hall, ed., *Napoleon's Letters to Josephine 1796-1812*, Gutenberg 37499]**. The edition does not print those two. Every `quote` field in the journey file is therefore her husband writing at her, and none is her writing back.

## 1. Martinique: birth, baptism, and the contested birthplace (1763-1779)

Born **23 June 1763** **[A: en.wikipedia, "Empress Joséphine"; fr.wikipedia, "Joséphine de Beauharnais"]**, to Joseph-Gaspard Tascher de La Pagerie, a minor plantation-owning noble, and Rose-Claire des Vergers de Sannois **[A: same]**. Baptized **27 July 1763** in the parish church of Les Trois-Îlets **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The pool's first chronology entry led with "probably at Paix Bouche, Babonneau, or on the family plantation near Les Trois-Îlets" and tagged the dispute [A]. That inverts the weight of evidence. Mainstream scholarship and both Wikipedia articles place the birth at **La Pagerie, Les Trois-Îlets** **[A]**; the French article on the commune calls it flatly the "lieu de naissance de Joséphine de Beauharnais" **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Les Trois-Îlets"]**. **Paix Bouche** and **Babonneau** are Saint Lucian localities belonging to a **minority local tradition [R]**, not co-equal candidates. The file places the birth at Trois-Îlets and names the Saint Lucian claim in the campa as a rival tradition. It is not dismissed, it is ranked.

Sister **Catherine-Désirée** born 1764, died 1777 before the intended Beauharnais marriage, Rose proposed as substitute bride **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Convent school with the Dames de la Providence at Fort-Royal from about 1773 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Embarkation aboard the flûte *Île de France*, **late August 1779** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Gap, stated as a gap.** No source consulted names the **port of disembarkation in France** in autumn 1779 **[R: absence confirmed against both Wikipedia articles]**. The file has no landing stop and names the gap in the embarkation campa rather than inventing a Brest or a Nantes.

## 2. The vicomtesse de Beauharnais (1779-1790)

Married **Alexandre, vicomte de Beauharnais**, at **Noisy-le-Grand, 13 December 1779** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The couple lodged at the marquis de Beauharnais' town house on the **rue Thévenot**, 1780 **[A]**. **Eugène** born 1781, **Hortense** born 1783 **[A: en.wikipedia]**, year only in the fetched sources, so the file defaults month and day to 01 and says so. The marriage was troubled and the couple separated **[R: consensus, not independently dated in the fetched source]**.

**Contradiction flagged.** The pool dated her entry to the **abbaye de Penthemont** to 1783. French Wikipedia gives **1784** for the entry and **March 1785** for the court-ordered separation **[A]**. The file uses 1784 and records the conflict. Note the orthography: French usage is **Penthemont**, not "Pentemont". The geography lens coordinates (48.8556, 2.3212) are correct for 106 rue de Grenelle and are kept.

Three years at or near **Fontainebleau, 1784-1787** **[R: fr.wikipedia, undated arrival]**. Return to **Martinique, June 1788 to November 1790** **[A]**, ending with a landing at **Toulon** from the frigate *La Sensible* **[A]**.

## 3. The Terror (1793-1794)

Refuge at the house of Madame de Hosten-Lamotte, **Croissy-sur-Seine**, 1793-1794 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Alexandre arrested **2 March 1794** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction flagged twice, and both times the pool contradicted itself.**

1. **Her imprisonment.** The pool gave **18 April 1794**; no source supports it. French Wikipedia: "inculpée le **19 avril** et enfermée à la prison des Carmes" **[A]**. English Wikipedia gives **21 April** **[A]**, as did the pool's own geography lens. The file uses 21 April and prints the conflict in `date_confidence`.
2. **Her release.** The pool said **28 July 1794**, "days after Robespierre's fall". French Wikipedia: "libérée le **6 août 1794**" **[A]**, ten days after 9-10 Thermidor, matching the geography lens. English Wikipedia's 28 July appears to be an error. The file uses 6 August.

Alexandre guillotined at the **Place de la Révolution, 23 July 1794** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. No coordinates for the square are in the pool; the file uses the standard Place de la Concorde point (48.8656, 2.3212), a **[R]** placement of a place rather than of an event. The role of **Tallien and Thérésa Cabarrus** in her release is **[R]**, absent from the fetched extracts.

## 4. Rue Chantereine, the Directory, and the second marriage (1795-1796)

**Contradiction flagged, and the pool's own source undercuts it.** The pool placed her post-prison house at "**50 rue Chantereine (now rue des Victoires)**", January 1795, citing the French Joséphine article, which does print that string. The dedicated street article contradicts it twice over **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Rue de la Victoire (Paris)"]**: the street is the **rue de la Victoire**, singular, renamed from Chantereine on **28 December 1797**; and the house stood at what became **nos. 58-60**, demolished 1858, not no. 50. The same source dates the lease to **late 1795**. The file keeps the coordinates, uses the period street name, and carries the hedge in `date_confidence`.

The **Merveilleuses** circle and the connection to **Paul Barras** are **[R]**: tradition, not precisely dated. Met Bonaparte in society, **1795** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; proposal **January 1796** **[R]**. Children's schooling at **Saint-Germain-en-Laye**, 1795-1796 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Civil marriage **9 March 1796** at the **Hôtel de Mondragon**, town hall of the 2nd municipality **[A]**; both falsified their ages, she downward about four years, he upward **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Coordinate contradiction flagged and deliberately not resolved.** The geography lens corrects the Hôtel de Mondragon to **48.8683, 2.3325** (3 rue d'Antin) **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The corpus pins the same event at **48.8697, 2.331**, three hundred metres away. Canonical pins are inherited byte-identical, so the file uses 48.8697, 2.331 and states the discrepancy. If the atlas reconciles its Paris coordinates, this stop and the napoleon file should move together.

**Contradiction flagged, badly wrong in the pool.** The pool dated Napoleon's departure for Italy to **10 May 1796** while calling it "days after the wedding". He left **two days after the wedding**, about 11 March **[A: en.wikipedia]**; 10 May is the Battle of Lodi. No departure stop is carried.

## 5. The Italian campaign (1796-1797)

Joined Napoleon at **Milan, 9-10 July 1796** **[A]**, after delaying for weeks **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Then, in the order the French article gives: **Brescia, 26 July**; **Verona, July**, lodging in a house lately occupied by the exiled **Louis XVIII**; **Peschiera del Garda, 29 July**, refusing to leave the fortress under Austrian advance; **Castelnuovo del Garda, 30 July**; **Lucca**, 1796; **Genoa, November 1796**. All **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The affair with **Hippolyte Charles** is **[R]**, widely reported and not in the fetched extract; the hostility of Letizia Bonaparte and the sisters is **[R]** and undated.

**Contradiction flagged and coordinates corrected.** The pool placed the **Château de Mombello** at 45.6127, 9.253. The headquarters was the **Villa Crivelli Pusterla** at Mombello, frazione of **Limbiate**, at about **45.607, 9.128** **[A: it.wikipedia, "Mombello (Limbiate)"]**, nine to ten kilometres away. The same article records Napoleon resident **16 May to 18 November 1797** and the marriages of Elisa and Paolina in the chapel on **14 June** **[A]**. The pool's "roughly eighteen months" cannot describe a six-month occupancy. The file corrects both.

## 6. Malmaison, the Consulate, and the crowns (1798-1805)

**Toulon, May 1798**, as the Egyptian expedition prepared to sail **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Her intended following voyage did not happen and **no source consulted explains why**. **Malmaison purchased April 1799** for 325,000 francs **[A: en.wikipedia]**, the geography lens giving 21 April. Balcony collapse at **Plombières-les-Bains, 20 June 1799**; installed at the **Tuileries from 19 February 1800** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; escaped the **rue Saint-Nicaise** bomb, **24 December 1800** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Heated orangery for some 300 pineapples, 1800; nearly 200 new plant varieties and the patronage of **Pierre-Joseph Redouté** by 1803 **[A]**. Proclaimed Emperor **18 May 1804** **[A]**; crowned at **Notre-Dame, 2 December 1804**, Pope Pius VII present **[A]**, the religious marriage having been performed quietly at the Tuileries by Cardinal Fesch the day before **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction flagged.** The pool dated the Italian crown to **23 May 1805**. Napoleon was crowned King of Italy with the Iron Crown at Milan on **26 May 1805** **[A: en.wikipedia, "Iron Crown of Lombardy"]**; the Kingdom had been proclaimed 17 March. The pool's geography lens gave 26 May, contradicting its own chronology. Further: **Joséphine was not herself crowned in Milan**, holding the consort title only, and the French article on the Kingdom describes the ceremony **without mentioning her [R]**. The file uses 26 May, inherits the corpus pin, and flags her attendance as unconfirmed.

Two [R] items are **not** carried in as stops, neither confirmable: the **Aachen** cure on the autumn 1804 Rhineland tour, and the presence at **Genoa** around its June 1805 annexation.

## 7. Divorce, Navarre, death (1809-1814)

Childlessness as the stated ground for annulment is **[R]** as a dated event: the fact is consensus, the date is not a date. Told of the political necessity, **30 November 1809** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction flagged and split.** The pool dated the dissolution to **10 January 1810** in one entry and to 1809 in the afterlife lens. French Wikipedia: "Le divorce est signé le **15 décembre 1809** et prononcé par un sénatus-consulte le 16 décembre" **[A]**, the **religious annulment following in early January 1810** by the Officialité de Paris **[A]**. The corpus pins the ceremony at 1809-12-15 **[A: napoleon.journey.json]**. The file uses 15 December and names the January act in the campa. She retained the title and rank of Empress **[A]**.

Created **Duchess of Navarre**, granted the **Château de Navarre** near Évreux, retaining Malmaison in her own right, pension of 5 million francs a year, **April 1810** **[A]**. Napoleon married **Marie Louise of Austria** in 1810 **[R as to the date in the fetched sources]**; the corpus dates the Compiègne marriage 2 April 1810 **[A: napoleon.journey.json]**.

**Contradiction flagged.** The pool dated the birth of Napoleon II to **11 March 1811**. He was born **20 March 1811** at the Tuileries **[A: en.wikipedia, "Napoleon II"]**, and the corpus pins that stop at 1811-03-20. Nine days of error, corrected.

Reception of **Tsar Alexander I** and other allied sovereigns at Malmaison, **spring 1814** **[A]**. **Died of pneumonia there, 29 May 1814** **[A]**.

**Coordinate contradiction flagged.** The afterlife lens gave Malmaison as 48.8798, 2.1878; the geography lens gave **48.8708, 2.1669**, matching Wikipedia's 48.87083 N, 2.16694 E **[A]**. The afterlife point lies 1.6 km northeast, in built-up Rueil. The file uses 48.8708, 2.1669 throughout.

## 8. Afterlife: the tomb, the house, the statue

**The tomb.** Buried in the **église Saint-Pierre-Saint-Paul de Rueil-Malmaison**, June 1814 **[A]**. Remains kept in the presbytery cellar for over a decade, installed in the finished tomb **28 September 1825** **[A: fr.wikipedia, church article]**. Carrara marble by **Pierre Cartellier** with the architect **Louis-Martin Berthault**, kneeling in an orante pose echoing David's *Sacre* **[A]**. Hortense died in 1837, her remains in a crypt excavated in 1856, and the mausoleum facing her mother is a **cenotaph by Jean-Auguste Barre, 1858** **[A]**. The pool's "interred beside her" overstated this.

**Coordinate contradiction flagged.** Three values for the church: geography lens 48.8779, 2.1808; afterlife lens 48.8746, 2.1806; the correction from the French article on the church itself, **48.8764, 2.1814** **[A]**. The file uses the third.

**The house.** Eugène inherited; his widow sold in 1828 to a Swedish banker; sold on in 1842 to Queen Marie-Christine of Spain; bought by Napoleon III in 1861; ransacked by the Prussians in 1870, then a barracks; the parkland sold to developers by 1877; bought in 1896 by **Daniel Iffla, called Osiris**, restored with Pierre Humbert, bequeathed with his Napoleonic collection to the state in 1904, opened as a national museum in **1905** **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia, "Château de Malmaison"]**.

**The statue.** Marble Joséphine commissioned by Napoleon III, installed **1859** in **La Savane**, Fort-de-France **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Repeatedly vandalized from **1991**, decapitated and spattered with red paint, tied by critics to the **1802 law reinstating slavery**, left unrepaired with fresh paint most years **[A]**. Torn down and destroyed in **July 2020**, after the killing of George Floyd **[A]**. **No replacement is confirmed** in any source checked **[R]**.

That she personally procured the 1802 restoration of slavery is **[R]**: a Martinican tradition and a critics' charge, not a documented act. What is documented is the coincidence of her family's planter interest, the Consulate's law, and a hundred and sixty years of a marble empress standing over a colonial capital. **Napoleon's death:** tradition holds his last words in 1821 included her name, and the English article flags the detail as unverified **[R]**.

## 9. The quotations

All nine quotes were checked against the Hall edition, Project Gutenberg ebook 37499, re-downloaded at 617,548 bytes and byte-identical to the cached copy. Eight are **[A]**, one **[R]**. Three needed correction:

- **"My waking thoughts are all of thee."** Wording **confirmed verbatim [A]**: Series A, Letter No. 1, opening line. **The pool's date was wrong.** The letter is **undated** here; "February 23rd. Bonaparte made Commander-in-Chief of the Army of Italy" is the chronology heading above it, not the letter's date. Hall's Note to Series A (p. 198): "'7 A.M.' Probably written early in March." The `quote_source` now says undated, with the editor's early-March dating.
- **"Every moment separates me further from you..."** Series A, No. 2, "Chanceaux Post House, March 14, 1796" **[A]**. **The pool truncated it without an ellipsis**, inventing a full stop and dropping the qualifier that carries the sense: "...and every moment I have less energy **to exist so far from you**." Restored in full.
- **"I truly loved my Joséphine, but I did not respect her."** **[R]**: attributed to Napoleon at Saint Helena, cited at Wikipedia footnote 31, likely from Las Cases, *Mémorial de Sainte-Hélène*. **The Mémorial was not fetched or read**, and the `quote_source` says so.

Six more were verified as printed and used unchanged, all **[A: Hall, Gutenberg 37499]**: the letters of 3 April, 7 April, 15 June, 6 July, 17 July, and 19 November 1796. In the first of these the Gutenberg text prints an em dash where the file uses a semicolon per house style, and the `quote_source` declares the substitution.

## 11. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **The landing port in France, autumn 1779.** Not named in either article. No stop.
2. **Her own writing.** Two authenticated Joséphine-to-Napoleon letters exist; Hall does not print them and no other edition was reached. Every quotation here is his voice.
3. **Why she never sailed to Egypt** after the Toulon farewell of May 1798.
4. **Her attendance at the Milan coronation of 26 May 1805.** Widely stated, not confirmed in the source read.
5. **The Aachen cure, autumn 1804, and the Genoa visit, June 1805.** Tradition only; both omitted from the file.
6. **The current state of the La Savane site** after the 2020 destruction.
7. **The 1802 restoration of slavery.** Her personal role is asserted by Martinican critics and unproven in any document reached. Flagged [R] wherever it appears.
8. **The day of her burial in June 1814.** Month attested, day defaulted with a note.
