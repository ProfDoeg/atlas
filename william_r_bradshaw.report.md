# William R. Bradshaw (1851-1927): research report

*Compiled 2026-09-01 for `william_r_bradshaw.journey.json`. William Richard Bradshaw, born
County Down, died Flushing, Queens: magazine editor, decorative-arts journalist,
anti-vivisection lecturer, author of one novel,* The Goddess of Atvatabar *(1892).*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or
single-witness self-report · inaccessible sources named with the reason.

---

## 0. The evidentiary situation, stated first

Almost everything claimed about Bradshaw's first thirty-two years comes through **one
document**: his own entry in *Who's Who in America*, vol. 2 (1901-1902), p. 124, which he
supplied himself. It was read in the Internet Archive OCR text (`whoswhoinamerica02marq`).
The birth date, County Down, the Belfast education, the South Kensington course, Denis
Leonard, the 1877 gold medal, the 1878 commission, the Dublin marriage, the 1883
emigration, and the Chicago editorship are **all and only** in that paragraph. Wikipedia,
the *Encyclopedia of Science Fiction*, and the 1927 obituaries copy it or descend from it.

That is no reason to reject it, only to tag it **[R]**, which the journey file does stop
by stop. Where the pool tagged such items **[A]** because Wikipedia "corroborates" them,
the tag is downgraded here: Wikipedia is downstream of the same page.

Two independent islands carry the file's **[A]** claims: **the book** (Project Gutenberg
32825, the 1892 first edition) and **the 1920 anti-vivisection pamphlet**
(`CUHSLVIV_M113`, Colorado Anschutz), which prints Bradshaw's affidavit, Woglom's rebuttal
and Belais's retraction verbatim. The *Who's Who* residence line ("57 Locust St., Flushing,
N. Y.") is usually dropped when the entry is quoted, and is load-bearing: it ties that man
to the "Dr. William R. Bradshaw of Flushing, N.Y." of the 1920 affidavit. The file restores
it with an ellipsis.

## 1. Ireland, 1851-1883

**Birth [R].** 14 January 1851, County Down, "of Anglo-Irish ancestry," his own words. No
town, parish, or townland, and no baptismal record; pinned at county level deliberately
(54.3306, -5.7136). **The infancy-migration error [A, as correction]**: the widely
circulated claim that Bradshaw was carried to America as an infant is called "mistaken"
outright by John Clute's *Encyclopedia of Science Fiction* entry, and the internal evidence
agrees, with Irish schooling, an 1877 prize, an 1878 appointment and Dublin marriage, and
Belfast births in 1878 and 1881.

**Education [R].** "ed. Belfast, Ireland": four words, no school. Then "took scientific
course South Kensington, London," no college, syllabus, or qualification named; the
institutional history of South Kensington is **[A]**, his attendance **[R]** and
unverified. Then languages under private tutors, and the art of reading under **Denis
Leonard**, "Irish comedian and lawyer," unidentifiable in any source reached, though
dramatic reading is the only training he claims that plainly paid off. **Irish journalism
[R]**: he says he wrote for the Irish press on Irish character and travel, and **not one
byline has been traced**.

**The gold medal, 1877 [R].** Church of Ireland Young Men's Association, Belfast, prize
essay "The Ulsterman." No copy, no publication, no award notice. **The venue is
undocumented**: the file pins the Ulster Hall (corrected to 54.5947, -5.9305 from the
pool's 54.5959, -5.9316) and calls it a placeholder for the customary Belfast civic hall,
not a confirmed site.

**Commissioner for taking affidavits, 1878 [R].** For Belfast and County Antrim: a
legal-administrative commission, **not** evidence of practice as barrister or solicitor,
a distinction later summaries blur. Appointing instrument not located.

**Marriage [R]. Contradiction, not resolved.** Emma Douglas (or Douglass), Dublin. His own
entry says **1878**, later genealogical indexes **1877**, and the civil register entry has
not been located by anyone. The file shows 1878 and says the contradiction stands open.

**Children born in Ireland [R].** **Mary Christobel / Christabel**, b. 14 June 1878,
Strandtown, per a Belfast birth index naming William R. Bradshaw as father; later surname
Hansel; d. 1970. Coordinate corrected to 54.6008, -5.8797 (the pool's 54.5975, -5.86 lay
1.1 km southeast). **Reginald Douglas / Douglass**, b. 11 October 1881, Belfast;
d. 7 January 1980.

**Emigration, 1883 [R].** Age thirty-two, self-reported. **No passenger manifest found**,
no month, port, or vessel, and it is unknown whether the family crossed together. The
Castle Garden pin (40.7033, -74.017) is labelled a reconstruction.

## 2. Chicago and Brooklyn, 1886-1889

**Literary Life [R]. Tag downgraded from the pool.** The pool tagged this **[A]** on
Wikipedia's corroboration, but Wikipedia says only "magazine editor for Field and Stream,
Literary Life, and Decorator and Furnisher": **no city, no dates**. Chicago and 1886-87
rest solely on the *Who's Who* entry. The file tags it **[R]** and reuses the standing
Chicago pin (41.8796, -87.6237, byte-identical from `alphonse_mucha.journey.json`).

**William Clanmorna Bradshaw [R], the weakest item in the file.** Six births recorded,
five children surviving in 1927; the sixth is inferred to have died in childhood. **No
death record, notice, or burial was found.** The stop is kept because the arithmetic is
real, and its confidence field says date, place, and fact are all reconstruction.

**Wilmar Hereward Bradshaw [R].** b. 2 April 1889, Brooklyn; d. 28 March 1975. A Friends
of Maple Grove page attributes him to a different, earlier-dead William R. Bradshaw
(1854-1888): documented conflation, not used.

## 3. The Decorator and Furnisher, 1890-1896

**The editorship [A].** Issue front matter prints "W. R. Bradshaw, Editor"; the 1890-1896
span is independently attested. The office street address was not found, so the file uses
a generic Manhattan publishing-district pin (40.7359, -73.9911) and says so.

Seven signed pieces of 1890-1895 are used, all **[A] as catalogued bylines** with no
issue independently retrieved; they are named stop by stop in the journey file.

**Correction: the Pompeia is not the Canfield Casino.** The pool pinned it at
43.081, -73.7846, the Canfield Casino in Congress Park, an unrelated 1870 gambling house.
The Pompeia was **Franklin W. Smith's** Pompeian-revival hall on Broadway, 1888-1889,
modelled on Bulwer-Lytton's *Last Days of Pompeii*. Its exact site is **not established**;
the file uses an approximate Broadway coordinate (43.0831, -73.7852) and says so. **Villa
Zorayda** moves from 29.8938, -81.3145 to 29.8917, -81.3151 (83 King Street); Smith built
that too, so one builder accounts for two of Bradshaw's articles.

**Correction: the French Benevolent Society pin was fabricated.** The pool used
40.7484, -73.9857, the Empire State Building site, reused as a generic Fifth Avenue pin
and again for the Anti-Vivisection Society. No evidence puts the Société Française de
Bienfaisance on Fifth Avenue in 1890; its known seat was the French Hospital on the West
Side. **The stop was kept but moved to the editorial-office pin**, the confidence field
stating the address is unestablished, since a wrong pin is worse than an honest generic
one. For the same reason **"Decoration of a Fifth Avenue Mansion" gets no stop**.

## 4. The Goddess of Atvatabar, 1891-1892

**Imprint [A], primary.** Title page: "J. F. Douthitt, 286 Fifth Avenue, New York";
copyright notice 1891; title page dated 1892; introduction by Julian Hawthorne, all
re-fetched from the Gutenberg images edition. Publisher coordinate corrected to
40.7466, -73.9863 (the pool's 40.7477, -73.9877 was about 150 m north).

**Correction: the illustration count.** The pool says "forty-five plates" in two lenses,
following booksellers. **The book's own list of illustrations has 32 entries**
(frontispiece plus 31), credited chiefly to C. Durand Chapman with Rattray, Gutherz,
Harold Haven Brown, de Longpre, Davis, Dunk, and Doggett. **Contradiction not resolved:**
the *Encyclopedia of Science Fiction* says the first issue of the 1892 printing *lacked*
the pictures and that they appear in the second, against the booksellers' "first edition,
profusely illustrated." The lwcurrey.com listing carrying that collation now 404s. The
file states both positions and resolves neither.

**Source of the premise [A, doctrinally].** The polar aperture is John Cleves Symmes Jr.'s,
and the *Morning Call* review of 5 June 1892 names "Symmes' Hole" outright. **No statement
by Bradshaw naming Symmes has been located**: the descent is visible in the text and the
reviews, not in an authorial admission.

**Reception [R], relayed not refetched.** *St. Paul Daily Globe*, 5 May 1892 (Verne and
Munchausen); the *Sun*, New York, 4 June 1892 (denies servile imitation of Haggard's *She*
while proposing *She* as its model); *San Francisco Morning Call*, 5 June 1892. None was
refetched from the digitised originals, and the reception stop says in its confidence
field that the reviewing was done elsewhere by other hands.

**Quotations [A].** Six passages were checked verbatim against Gutenberg 32825 and carried
into the file's `quote` fields. **"Professor Starbottle, Astronomer" [A]** is confirmed in
the same text (ch. V); see §8 for the Nabokov thread, which is not Bradshaw's.

## 5. Flushing, 1896-1910

**The move [A].** Left the magazine in 1896, lived in Flushing until his death. **The Art
Amateur series [R]**: "The House. The Salon." (Oct and Nov 1898, Jan 1899) and "Carpets and
Rugs" (Feb 1899), catalogued, issues not retrieved.

**Addresses [R].** The Bennett Building (93-99 Nassau Street, built for James Gordon
Bennett Jr.) is **[A]**; Bradshaw's tenancy is **[R]**, with no room, firm, partner, or
lease found. **Republican district captain [R]**, from the obituary alone: no year, term,
ward, candidacy, or office located.

**Field and Stream [A for the article, R for the editorship].** "Hunting for Gold at
Porcupine Lake," April 1910, is a signed byline; the obituary calls him a *former editor*
and **no issue naming him as editor was found**. Whether he travelled to the Ontario
goldfields or compiled the piece is unestablished, and the file's stop says it marks the
article's subject, not a documented journey. **Gap, 1899-1910**: between "Carpets and Rugs"
and this piece, no traced byline of any kind.

## 6. Anti-vivisection, 1908-1923

**Correction: he was not president of the New York Anti-Vivisection Society.** The pool
asserts this twice, once at **[A]**. **Diana Belais founded** the society at a Carnegie
Hall meeting in **February 1908** and was its president for thirty years, spanning the
whole of Bradshaw's involvement and outlasting his death; Wikipedia's Bradshaw article
says only that he *lectured for* it. The presidency is struck from the file; he is a
**spokesman and lecturer [R]**, and no society role can predate February 1908.

**The legislative fight [R].** The Frederic Schiller Lee papers at Columbia are described
as documenting a 1909-1923 dispute in which Bradshaw speaks for the anti-vivisection side.
**The Columbia finding aid returned an access-denied page.** The Albany Capitol pin is
labelled a reconstruction standing for "the New York legislature"; no dated appearance by
Bradshaw there was identified. **Buffalo, 11 April 1911 [R]**: "The Uselessness of
Vivisection," *Buffalo News*, catalogued, not retrieved, and whether he spoke there or the
paper printed supplied text is unknown.

**The Belais presentation copy [R].** Inscribed 3 October 1911; two independent
bookseller listings describe the object, neither examined, one now dead. **The place of
inscription is not recorded**, so the stop sits at Bradshaw's own Flushing pin rather than
at an invented Manhattan address.

**Correction: the 1913 lecture notice is locatable.** The pool had an undated "March 1913
notice." It is the *Elmira Star-Gazette*, "He Will Lecture in Opposition to Vivisection,"
**15 March 1913, p. 12**. The venue and city of the lecture are still unnamed, so the stop
sits at Flushing; staging him in Elmira would be invention.

**"Naturopathy, the Medicine of the Future" [R]. Tag downgraded, and it may not exist.**
The pool tagged it **[A]**, but it appears in **no bibliographic database reached**: Open
Library returns nothing for the title and nothing like it among Benedict Lust's 22
catalogued works. The only support is a bare Wikipedia mention, and the file says so.

**The Woglom episode**, the best-documented event in his life and the one that goes
against him. **4 March 1916 [R]**: he attends a lecture by Dr. William H. Woglom of the
Crocker Research Laboratory at the Brooklyn Academy of Music, and **the only evidence he
was there is his own affidavit four and a half years later**; no transcript or press
report was found. **5 October 1920 [A]**: he swears an affidavit in Queens County reciting
Woglom's alleged words, a million and a half animals sacrificed with no cause or cure
found, "and if any man says he has discovered a cure for cancer through animal
experimentation, that man is a liar," printed verbatim in the pamphlet. **Coordinate
corrected**: the pool's 40.7484, -73.9302 sits in Sunnyside, 1.2 km from the Long Island
City Courthouse at 25-10 Court Square, 40.7458, -73.9431; the file uses the corrected pair
and still calls the notarisation site undocumented. **The "M.D." [R] is unresolved**: the
affidavit opens "Dr. William R. Bradshaw of Flushing, N.Y." and is signed "Wm. R.
Bradshaw, M.D.," and nothing in his documented education supports a medical degree.
**The rebuttal [A]**: Woglom's letter of 31 May 1920 gives his own version and calls the
pamphlet text a gross and deliberate distortion; Belais withdraws the disputed paragraph
while defending Bradshaw as "most trustworthy in every particular." The file presents both
sides and adjudicates neither.

## 7. Death and afterlife

**19 July 1927 [A].** Died at home, 37 Locust Street, Flushing, after "a brief illness";
survived by wife, two sons, three daughters. **Contradiction flagged:** the obituary gives
his age as **75**, against **76** from a January 1851 birth. Not resolved.

**Burial [R].** Find a Grave memorial 198372969 attributes burial to Flushing Cemetery.
**Find a Grave returned HTTP 403 and was unreachable through a web archive**, and no
cemetery register has been checked by anyone. The pool's two lenses disagreed on the
coordinate (40.758, -73.8129 vs 40.7517, -73.7994); the file uses **40.7517, -73.7994**.

**No monument.** No plaque, street, named building, or marker found in any source. The
nearest institutional trace is **Houghton Library SF-251** at Harvard, on Wikimedia
Commons: 21 files, cover, title page, frontispiece, 16 interior plates and a studio
portrait. (The pool said 17 interior plates; it is 16 plus a frontispiece.)

**Publication afterlife.** *Forgotten Fantasy* (Nectar Press, Hollywood) serialised the
novel; the magazine ran **five** bimonthly issues, October 1970 to June 1971, not the four
the pool reports. Upgraded **[R]** to **[A]**. Arno Press 1975 reprint **[R], unverified**:
ISFDB 403s. Project Gutenberg eBook 32825, **15 June 2010** **[A]**. LibriVox audiobook
read by Nigel Fisher **[A via sffaudio.com]**; librivox.org 403s.

## 8. Apparatus relations for the operator

Bradshaw is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing has been silently deleted. Three
relations are not rooted in his own life; all are **FORWARD**, which the exceptions file
does not licence, and all are kept as citations rather than staged as scenes.

1. **Nabokov / Matthew Roth (FORWARD).** A note proposing that Bradshaw's "Professor
   Starbottle" lies behind Professor Starover Blue in *Pale Fire*. Two corrections: the
   paper is **"Three Allusions in Pale Fire" by Matthew Roth**, *The Nabokovian* 58
   (Spring 2007), **not by Brian Boyd**, who has an unrelated "Annotations to Ada" in the
   same issue; and a search snippet points the allusion at **Bret Harte's Colonel
   Starbottle**, so the Bradshaw link may be a conflation. academia.edu still 403s.
   **Kept** as a `suggested_ref` on the "invented tongue" stop, error and doubt inside
   the ref.
2. **The modern reception community (FORWARD).** SFFaudio Podcast #681, the LibriVox
   recording, the Wikipedia articles. **Kept** in `sources` and in the final stop's
   confidence field, given no scenes.
3. **Houghton Library SF-251 (FORWARD).** **Kept** as a `suggested_ref`, not a stop:
   Bradshaw never went to Cambridge, Massachusetts.

Promoting any of these to stops is an `EXCEPTIONS.md` decision, not mine.

**Checked and dropped for shared geography only**, so they are not lost: **H. G. Wells**
at South Kensington from 1884, after Bradshaw emigrated; **John Ruskin**'s 1884 Slade
resignation over a vivisection laboratory, 25 years early and across an ocean; **Robert
Moses** in Flushing Meadows from the 1930s; **Edgar Allan Poe**'s *Pym*, the same
Symmes-derived opening with nothing showing Bradshaw engaged with Poe. **H. Rider
Haggard** has no file and appears only as the *Sun* reviewer's comparison.

**Kept, and why.** Three travellers are named in campas, each on a documented
one-directional relation of Bradshaw's own. **`symmes`**: the novel takes its premise
whole from Symmes's polar-aperture doctrine, a descent the 1892 *Morning Call* names
outright. **`jules_verne`** and **`bulwer_lytton`**: named by Julian Hawthorne inside
Bradshaw's own front matter as the lineage the book is read against, and Bulwer-Lytton is
also the source of the Pompeia, which Bradshaw wrote up in 1890.

## 9. Gaps, stated as gaps

No Irish primary record of any kind: no baptism, school register, marriage register entry,
newspaper byline, copy of "The Ulsterman," or appointing instrument for the 1878
commission. **Denis Leonard** unidentified. No passenger manifest for 1883. No run of
*Literary Life*, and no evidence outside the self-report that it was in Chicago. No office
address for any of the magazines or for the French Benevolent Society, and no room number
in the Bennett Building. No issue of *Field and Stream* naming Bradshaw as editor. No
record of William Clanmorna beyond the arithmetic. Nothing written by Bradshaw traced
after April 1910. No photograph later than the Houghton portrait. No monument, marker, or
verified grave.
