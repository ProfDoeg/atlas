# Alexander Hamilton (c. 1755-1804): research report

*2026-08-10. Slug `alexander_hamilton`. Register: national mythology, the canon is true.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or disputed · **[A/corr]** attested but corrected against the pool, correction stated in full.

The verified pool arrived with fourteen explicit corrections logged against it. They are reproduced rather than absorbed, because several (the Beekman and Cruger start, the 1 March 1777 commission, Philip Hamilton's duel site, the 2016 dollar-bill reversal) change the shape of the story and not merely a digit.

## 1. The islands, c. 1755-1772

**Birth**, Charlestown, Nevis, to Rachel Faucette Lavien and James Hamilton, out of wedlock **[A: Smithsonian; LOC Hamilton Papers timeline; Chernow (2004)]**. Site 17.1412, -62.6296 **[A: Museum of Nevis History marker]**. **Relocation to St. Croix, 1765 [A: American Battlefield Trust; biographers' consensus]**.

**The year is open and stays open.** 1755 rests on a 1768 St. Croix probate document giving his age as 13; Hamilton himself claimed 1757 **[A, both]**. The journey carries 1755-01-11 with the dispute in `date_confidence`. *Not adjudicated.*

**Abandonment [A/corr].** The pool dated it 1765. James Hamilton *brought* the family in 1765 and left in 1766; Chernow ch. 2 and Discovering Hamilton separate the events by about a year. Whether he left for lack of money or to spare Rachel a bigamy charge is unresolved in the sources and stated as such.

**Beekman and Cruger [A/corr].** The pool put the clerkship after February 1768. The earliest known Hamilton documents are witness signatures on the firm's papers of **April and August 1767**, before his mother's death. The geography lens's "1771-1772" is likewise corrected to **c. 1767-1772**; 1771-1772 is only the five months he ran the house alone during Cruger's absence **[A/corr: Chernow ch. 3; the letterbook]**.

**Rachel's death, 19 Feb 1768 [A/corr: LOC; probate in Chernow ch. 2].** Date holds; "yellow fever" does not. Accounts and probate papers describe only a violent fever striking mother and son in the same room. The journey names no disease: a gap in the record, not in the reporting.

**The hurricane letter, 1772 [A: American Battlefield Trust; Mount Vernon].** *No month or day in the pool* for the storm or the printing, so 1772-01-01, stated as such. Commonly repeated dates (storm 31 August, letter 6 September, printing 3 October) were not in the pool and are not asserted.

## 2. The passage north, 1772-1775

**Boston, Oct 1772 [A/corr: LOC; Chernow ch. 4].** Coordinate corrected: the pool's 42.3601, -71.0589 is downtown Boston's centroid, not the harbour it was labelled. Journey uses Long Wharf, **42.3590, -71.0500**. **Elizabethtown, late 1772-1773 [A: Chernow ch. 4]**, Francis Barber's academy; no month in the pool, so 1772-11-01 with the approximation flagged. **King's College, 1773 [A: Columbia; americanhistorycentral]**, Park Place 40.7143, -74.0093 **[A: Chernow ch. 5-6]**, with the **1774 pamphlets [A: americanhistorycentral]**, year only, folded in.

## 3. The artillery company, 1776-1777

- **Long Island / Brooklyn Heights, 27 Aug 1776 [A: Chernow ch. 7]**.
- **Harlem Heights, 16 Sept 1776 [R, tag corrected downward].** Presence disputed: Revolutionary War Journal finds no record of his artillery in the action and places his documented work in Putnam's retreat of 15 Sept; other accounts put his guns on the heights. Coordinate corrected: the pool's 40.8115, -73.9532 sits in the Harlem flats about 1 km east of the battlefield, at c. **40.8070, -73.9640**. Both problems carried.
- **White Plains / Chatterton Hill, 28 Oct 1776 [A: Chernow ch. 7]** and **Fort Washington, Nov 1776 [R: Chernow ch. 7, presence inferred from unit movements]**. No stops given, for length; recorded here.
- **McConkey's Ferry, 25-26 Dec 1776**, and **Trenton, 26 Dec 1776 [A: Chernow ch. 7]**.
- **Princeton, 3 Jan 1777 [R]**. The Nassau Hall cannonball is tradition; Chernow doubts it. The pool's 40.3468, -74.6699 contradicts its own label (that is the university generally; state park c. 40.3336, -74.6800, Nassau Hall c. 40.3487, -74.6593). Since the claim carried is the cannonball, the journey uses the Nassau Hall pin, which is also the corpus's existing Princeton pin.

**Valley Forge, winter 1777-1778 [A: Chernow ch. 9]**. The HMDB discrepancy is recorded and left open.

## 5. Bar, bank, convention, 1782-1787

- **Philip born, 1782**, Albany **[A: PBS American Experience]**, and **admitted to the New York bar, 1782 [A: NY Courts Historical Society]**. Year only for both, so they share one Albany stop: two stops on the same defaulted date would be false precision.
- **Continental Congress for New York, 1782-1783 [A: Constitution Center; Chernow ch. 13]**. No stop; recorded here. **Bank of New York, 1784 [A: American Battlefield Trust; Mount Vernon]**, year only.
- **Annapolis Convention, Sept 1786 [A: americanhistorycentral; Chernow ch. 15]**. Maryland State House, 38.9787, -76.4911, the corpus's existing Annapolis pin.
- **Constitutional Convention, 18 June 1787 [A: Constitution Center; Chernow ch. 16]**. A speech reported at roughly six hours, proposing a quasi-monarchical national government; not adopted. Signs 17 September as New York's only delegate then present.

## 6. Publius, 1787-1788

Eighty-five essays as Publius from October 1787; Hamilton writes more than half, put at 51 of 85, with authorship of essays 18-20, 49-58 and 62-63 contested between Hamilton and Madison and editions disagreeing **[A: LOC, "Full Text of The Federalist Papers" and "Bibliography of the Federalist Papers"]**. The 1788 bound edition is J. and A. McLean's; the first to attribute by name is Jacob Gideon's of 1818, revised by Madison and therefore not neutral **[A]**. *Left open.*

Four quotes carried, all fetched. **Federalist No. 1**, 27 Oct 1787 **[A: Avalon, direct fetch]**; **Federalist No. 15**, 1 Dec 1787 **[A: The Founders' Constitution, direct fetch]**; then two corrections:

- **Federalist No. 78**, 28 May 1788 **[A/corr]**. The pool dropped two words from inside the second sentence with no ellipsis and compressed the first clause. Avalon reads "The interpretation of the laws is the proper and peculiar province of the courts." Corrected text carried; citation moved from Wikiquote to Avalon, which carries the essay in full.
- **Poughkeepsie, 21 June 1788 [A/corr, the most serious correction].** The pool printed "It has been observed that a pure democracy if it were practicable would be the most perfect government" as Hamilton's own view. The primary text reads "It has been observed **by an honorable gentleman**..." and the next sentence is "Experience has proved, that no position in politics is more false than this." He is restating an opponent to demolish him; dropping four words reverses the sense. The journey carries the full sentence *and* the sentence after it, cited to Teaching American History's "First Speech of June 21".

**Ratification, July 1788 [A: NY Courts Historical Society; Chernow ch. 18]**, against Clinton's majority, by three votes.

## 7. The Treasury, 1789-1795

- **Nominated and confirmed the same day, 11 Sept 1789 [A: U.S. Treasury]**. Federal Hall; journey uses **40.7071, -74.0104** from `george_washington` (the pool gave 40.7072, -74.0102; metres apart, inheritance wins).
- **First Report on the Public Credit, 9 Jan 1790 [A: the 1790 report]**: redemption at face value, assumption of state debts. **Compromise of 1790, June 1790 [A: americanhistorycentral; Constitution Center]**, month only.
- **Second Report on Public Credit, Dec 1790 [A: home.treasury.gov]**, same month and place as the cabinet quarrel with Jefferson **[A: `thomas_jefferson.journey.json`]**. One stop, at **39.9494, -75.15**.
- **First Bank chartered, 1791 [A: americanhistorycentral]** and **Reynolds affair begins, 1791 [A: Britannica]**, year only for both; the account of the first approach comes from Hamilton's own 1797 pamphlet, uncorroborated. **Report on Manufactures, 5 Dec 1791 [A: The Founders' Constitution, direct fetch]**.
- **Whiskey Rebellion.** Bower Hill climax July 1794 **[A: Gilder Lehrman]**; Hamilton rides west in October and oversees interrogations near Bedford, 40.0176, -78.5042 **[A: Mount Vernon; Chernow ch. 28]**. *No coordinate for Bower Hill in the pool, and none is invented.* **Resignation, 31 Jan 1795 [A: U.S. Treasury]**, folded into the next stop rather than pinned.

The Philadelphia Treasury office address is **[R]**: Chernow ch. 20-25 places it near Independence Hall at c. 39.95, -75.149, and the pool notes the address was not independently confirmed.

## 8. Out of office, 1796-1804

- **Farewell Address, 19 Sept 1796 [A: `george_washington.journey.json`]**. Drafted mostly by Hamilton over Madison's 1792 outline; printed, not delivered. *Coordinate discrepancy:* the interlock lens reported 39.9494, -75.15; the Washington file carries **39.9526, -75.1652**. The file wins.
- **Reynolds Pamphlet, Aug 1797 [A: HISTORY.com]**. **Inspector general, 1798-1800 [A: americanhistorycentral]**: a range, no stop.
- **The deadlocked election, Feb 1801 [A: americanhistorycentral; Constitution Center]**. *Geographic hedge:* the pool places it at Washington, D.C., where the House balloted; Hamilton's instrument was correspondence and the pool does not attest his presence in the chamber. Journey pins the Capitol at **38.8899, -77.0091** and states the hedge.
- **New-York Evening Post, Nov 1801 [A: Wikipedia, "New York Post"]**, on roughly $10,000 of Federalist money.
- **Philip Hamilton's duel, 23 Nov 1801, died 24 Nov [R, tag corrected downward].** Site genuinely disputed: Gilder Lehrman's Hamilton Education Program and PBS say **Paulus Hook** (Jersey City); Chernow and Wikipedia say **Weehawken**. The pool's flourish that Philip died on the ground where his father later fell is unsupported (the Weehawken duel of the day before belongs to Eacker's meeting with Richard Price, 22 November) and is dropped from both lenses that carried it. *Not adjudicated:* the journey pins Weehawken and names Paulus Hook in the confidence field.
- **The Grange, 1802-1804 [A: NPS]**. *Internal conflict in the pool:* geography gives 40.8214, -73.9472, afterlife 40.8236, -73.949, for the same estate; the journey uses the geography value and flags it. The house moved in 1889 to 287 Convent Avenue and on 7 June 2008 into St. Nicholas Park, so no single modern pin is honest about all three positions. The pool's "38-day relocation" is corrected: the 2008 move took about six hours, and the multi-year figure belongs to the restoration, which ended with reopening on 17 Sept 2011.

## 9. Weehawken and the grave

- **Letter to Elizabeth, 4 July 1804 [A/corr]**. Verbatim, confirmed against Gilder Lehrman's manuscript presentation and Founders Online 01-26-02-0001-0248. *Place wrong in the pool:* the letter is headed only "July 4. 1804 / Mrs. Hamilton" with no dateline, and Hamilton was in New York City at the Society of the Cincinnati dinner. The pool's Weehawken coordinates belong to the duel a week later.
- **The duel, 11 July 1804 [A: HISTORY.com; LOC; NPS]**, with the statement of 10 July on throwing away his first fire **[A: Wikiquote, direct fetch]**. Whether he fired wide deliberately is disputed **[R: Chernow ch. 32; Gilder Lehrman]**, and the pool flags the principled reading as a later moral simplification. *Coordinate corrected twice over:* geography gave 40.7645, -74.0134, off the Palisades ledge; afterlife gave 40.7698, -74.0113, in the Hudson. HMDB marker 7334 gives **40.7700, -74.0172**.
- **Death, 12 July 1804**, at William Bayard Jr.'s house **[A on fact and date; R on site]**, c. 40.7359, -74.0075, approximated by Chernow. Folded into the burial stop.
- **Burial, 14 July 1804**, Trinity Churchyard, 40.70833, -74.01167 **[A: Trinity records; NHHC; NPS]**. Inscription genuine **[A: City Journal]**. *Date corrected:* the pool dated the monument 1804 and conflated two markers. Trinity put up a marker soon after the burial, but the surviving pyramid-topped marble is attributed to a later erection associated with the Society of the Cincinnati. The journey states the inscription without dating the stone. Elizabeth is buried beside him, d. 1854 **[A: Trinity FAQ]**.

## 10. The long afterward

- **Syrett edition**, 27 vols., Columbia UP, 1961-1987 **[A/corr: NHPRC]**. The pool gave coverage 1777-1804; vol. 1 opens with the West Indies material including the 1769 letter to Edward Stevens, so the range is **1768-1804**. **The musical**, from Chernow, opens 2015 **[A: New Statesman; Variety]**.
- **Central Park statue, 22 Nov 1880**, Westerly granite, Carl H. Conrads, donated by John C. Hamilton, 40.7794, -73.9632 **[A/corr: NYC Parks]**. The pool's "1778 bust by Giuseppe Ceracchi" is impossible: Ceracchi reached America in 1791 and cut the marble c. 1794, and that is the model. **Treasury statue, 17 May 1923**, Fraser's bronze on a Henry Bacon base **[A: Clio]**, at the corpus Treasury pin.
- **The ten-dollar bill [A/corr, twice].** Lew announces the redesign on **17 June 2015**; the pool's 2015-06-18 is the press reaction. The reversal is not the next day: on **20 April 2016** Lew announces Hamilton stays on the $10, Tubman replaces Jackson on the front of the $20, and the $10 reverse honours the 1913 suffrage march. The pool collapsed two announcements ten months apart into one.

## Contradictions carried, not resolved

1. **Birth year.** 1755 (1768 probate, most scholars) v. 1757 (his own claim).
2. **Harlem Heights.** His guns present v. absent, his documented action being the 15 Sept retreat. Tag [R].
3. **Philip's duel site.** Weehawken (Chernow, Wikipedia) v. Paulus Hook (Gilder Lehrman, PBS).
4. **Federalist authorship.** Essays 18-20, 49-58, 62-63 contested; the 1818 attribution is Madison-revised.
5. **The thrown-away shot.** Deliberate abstention v. accident v. a shot simply low.
6. **The Grange coordinate.** 40.8214, -73.9472 (geography lens) v. 40.8236, -73.949 (afterlife lens).
7. **Yorktown redoubt.** HMDB 37.2292, -76.4975 v. the corpus's 37.2384, -76.5074. Corpus pin used.
8. **Farewell Address pin.** Interlock lens 39.9494, -75.15 v. the Washington file's 39.9526, -75.1652.
9. **Queue bookkeeping.** `adam_smith.journey.json` and `william_paterson.journey.json` exist on disk at 572 lines each while QUEUE.md lines 213-214 mark both "0". Flagged, not reconciled.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- **No month or day** for: the 1772 hurricane and its printing, the 1774 pamphlets, Philip's 1782 birth, the 1782 bar admission, the 1784 Bank of New York, the 1791 bank charter, the 1791 start of the Reynolds affair. All default to 01, hedge in `date_confidence`.
- **The disease that killed Rachel Faucette Lavien is not established.** "A fever" is all the record supports.
- **The Philadelphia Treasury office address** [R] and **the Bayard house on Jane Street** [R] are both approximated; no confirmed addresses.
- **Bower Hill** has no coordinate in the pool; the Whiskey Rebellion appears only at Bedford.
- **Hamilton never crossed the Atlantic as an adult.** No attested stop in Paris, London, or Calais, so the corpus's European banking-thread pins (Paterson's London, Smith's Calais and Paris, Lafayette's Paris) are *not* reused. The interlock lens raised the possibility; it is declined.
- **No Hamilton material at the Kaaba, the Temple Mount, or Buenos Aires.** A grep across all `*.journey.json` returns no co-occurrence. A negative finding; those pins are not force-fitted.
- **Two name collisions.** `william_rowan_hamilton` (QUEUE.md line 493, quaternions) shares only a surname. And the corpus's William Paterson is the Bank of England founder, *not* the William Paterson of New Jersey who authored the New Jersey Plan at the same Convention. The Convention stop's `date_confidence` says so, so the reader cannot slide between them.
