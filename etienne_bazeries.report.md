# Étienne Bazeries (1846-1931): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-30 for `etienne_bazeries.journey.json`. Port-Vendres 1846 to Noyon 1931. Chef d'escadron of the train des équipages; cryptanalyst of the Bureau du Chiffre; inventor of the Bazeries cylinder; the man who read the Great Cipher of Louis XIV.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the dossier carries alone. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

The most productive source in this pass was the **Légion d'honneur service record, Léonore LH//150/51, matricule 68**. Where the journey file departs from Wikipedia and from the dossier, Léonore is usually why.

---

## 1. Origin and enlistment (1846-1863)

- Born **21 August 1846 at Port-Vendres** **[A: en.wikipedia and fr.wikipedia, independently fetched, agreeing on date and place; the town's plaque repeats both]**.
- **Enlisted 26 August 1863**, five days after his seventeenth birthday, as **engagé volontaire, soldat de 2e classe, 4e Escadron du Train des Équipages Militaires** **[A: Léonore, which prints unit and day]**. The dossier's caveat that only the year was corroborated is now removable.
- **Place of enlistment is not attested.** Léonore gives Port-Vendres as *family domicile* only; the squadron depot lay elsewhere. The file pins the stop there as a **domicile proxy** and says so. **[R as to place]** No schooling of distinction is recorded anywhere.

## 2. The Franco-Prussian War (1870-1872)

Wikipedia is weakest here and Léonore strongest.

- **Sous-lieutenant, 23 October 1870** **[A: Léonore, which enters the date twice on the same sheet]**. **Wikipedia's 13 October is wrong by ten days.** The record names **no place**.
- **Captured at the Metz capitulation, 27 October 1870**, with the 2nd Corps of the Army of the Rhine **[R: the dossier's reading of LH//150/51, unverified against any fetched source]**.
- **Escaped disguised as a bricklayer or mason** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Month, route, and site unrecorded; the file dates it 1870-11-01 as an ordering device. **Army of the Loire, winter 1870-71** **[R: dossier only]**, found in no page fetched, carried with the gap stated rather than deleted.
- **Lyon, interior duty, 30 April to 1 May 1871** **[A: Léonore campaigns block, "À l'Intérieur, Lyon: du 30 Avril 1871 au 1er Mai 1871"]**. **Two days, not two weeks.** The dossier's Commune framing also fails on the calendar: the Semaine sanglante ran 21 to 28 May, after he had gone. The file corrects both.
- **Lieutenant en 2e, 19 January 1871**, then **"Remis Sous-Lieutenant par décision du 29 février 1872 de la commission instituée par la loi du 10 Août 1871"** **[A: Léonore]**. The dossier missed this: his wartime lieutenancy was revoked and he had to earn it again, **confirmed lieutenant 26 April 1874** **[A]**. The revocation stop sits at the **canonical Paris pin (48.8566, 2.3522)** because that commission sat in Paris; Léonore does not say where Bazeries was, and `date_confidence` says so.

## 3. Africa, marriage, garrison (1875-1889)

- **First African tour, 8 May 1875 to 9 February 1876** **[A: Léonore; both Wikipedia editions confirm the years]**; posted to the 3e Compagnie du 9e Escadron the day after returning **[A]**.
- **Algiers is a staging proxy**: the file names no Algerian garrison for either tour. Coordinates **snapped from the dossier's 36.7538, 3.0588 to the standard centroid 36.7325, 3.0872**, the pool's largest deviation at 3.47 km.
- **Married 26 April 1876 to Marie-Louise-Élodie Berthon, "alors domiciliée à Châteauroux, département de l'Indre"** **[A: Léonore état civil block, with ministerial authorisation of 1 August 1876]**. **This is the dossier's largest geographic error: the wedding was not in Roussillon but some 700 km away, at Châteauroux (46.8103, 1.6911).** The Indre tie is corroborated in the same file by the artillery detachments of 12 January and 9 February 1877 **[A]**.
- **Captain, 12 January 1877** **[A: Wikipedia career timeline]**, the same day as the first detachment.
- **Second African tour, 18 May 1878 to 6 June 1882** **[A: Léonore]**. The dossier's start date of 19 March 1878 is **wrong by two months**; the end date is exact. The "five campaigns" credit is **not supported**: the file lists two tours and names no Algerian town, so Constantine stays a guess. **Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur, 28 December 1888**, as a captain of the train **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 4. Nantes and the cylinder (1890-1891)

- **Read the official French military transposition cipher without the key** before General Charles-Alexandre Fay at Nantes, then read the strengthened replacement before it entered service **[A as to the solution of the official system: en.wikipedia; R as to the year 1890 and Fay's role]**.
- **Kerckhoffs** published the six desiderata in the army's own *Journal des sciences militaires* in 1883, seven years earlier, and both men built careers on embarrassing the same cipher bureaucracy. No meeting or correspondence was found: the influence is **[R]**, the two dated facts **[A]** from their own files.
- **Gendron's handover of the Louis XIV dispatches. Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** bibmath and en.wikipedia's "Man in the Iron Mask" both date it **1890**; cryptiana places it **at Nantes in 1891**. The dossier's 1891 is internally inconsistent, giving two years rather than the "roughly three" every account needs to reach the 1893 publication. **The file dates it 1890 and states the conflict in `date_confidence`.**
- **The Bord challenge cryptograms, January 1891** **[R: dossier only; the name Bord appears in no source fetched]**.
- **The cylinder: twenty removable disks, each with a mixed 25-letter alphabet, 1891** **[A: cryptomuseum's M-94 page]**. **The date 12 February 1891 and the submission to the Ministry of War are [R]**: cryptomuseum says only that in 1891 Bazeries independently developed a similar device, nothing of a submission or a date.
- **The same device had been described by Thomas Jefferson in 1795** and left unread among his papers for a century **[A: en.wikipedia's M-94 article]**. One object invented twice; the campa says outright that Bazeries did not know of the American's version. The **M-94** itself, from Parker Hitt and Mauborgne in 1917, was adopted **1921 (cryptomuseum) or 1922 (en.wikipedia)**: **sources conflict and the dossier chose the minority year.** Both go in a `suggested_ref`; the M-94 is the U.S. Army's object, not a stop here.
- **Édouard Lucas presented the cylinder to the twentieth AFAS congress at Marseille, 19 September 1891** **[A as to the presentation: cryptiana; R as to the congress citation, unlocated]**. **No source places Bazeries in Marseille**, so this is a `suggested_ref` on his own cylinder stop, not a scene.

## 5. The Great Cipher and the Iron Mask (1891-1894)

- **Placed at the disposal of the Bureau du Chiffre, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, August 1891** **[A: en.wikipedia; bibmath]**. **Chef d'escadron, 10 July 1892** **[R: dossier; Wikipedia confirms the progression, not the day]**.
- **The anarchist Gronsfeld solution, 1892**, which he rated his best **[R: dossier]**. The Gronsfeld belongs to the Vigenère family, and his own 1901 book surveys that tradition **[A]**.
- **The break, 1893** **[A: bibmath, cross-checked against cryptiana]**: the groups stood for **syllables**; **124-22-125-46-345 read as les-en-ne-mi-s**; a nomenclator of **587 values**; roughly three years' work.
- **Le Masque de fer: Révélation de la correspondance chiffrée de Louis XIV**, with Émile Burgaud, Firmin-Didot, 1893 **[A: bribes.org names authors and title; bibmath describes the argument]**. The identification of **General Vivien de Bulonde** as the masked prisoner rests on **one pair of groups, 330 309, read as *masque***.
- **The quotation in the research pool was a back-translation and is corrected in the file.** The authentic wording, cited by fr.wikipedia to Burgaud and Bazeries 1893, differs materially: *citadelle de Pignerol* not *forteresse*, *Monsieur de Bulonde* not *le général*, *avec un 330 309* not *en portant un*.
- **Counter-evidence, flagged not resolved.** Bulonde's arrest was publicised at the time and he was **released by royal order on 11 December 1691**; his death is recorded in **1709**, not the 1708 the dossier gives in one place while giving 1709 in another, and the masked prisoner died **19 November 1703** **[A: en.wikipedia, citing Du Junca's Bastille register]**. bibmath objects further that it is aberrant for a six-hundred-value military code to carry *masque* and lack *ennemis*, and fr.wikipedia notes recent analysis reading group 330 as ***gardien***, a guard, which would dissolve the identification.
- **Kahn, "THE MAN IN THE IRON MASK, ENCORE ET ENFIN, CRYPTOLOGICALLY", *Cryptologia* 29/1 (2005), 43-49** **[A as to existence: Crossref; cryptiana names it independently]**. **The dossier's page range 43-94 is wrong and its title truncated.** Reprinted in *How I Discovered World War II's Greatest Spy* (2014), 421-426. Kahn's report that the archival letter could no longer be located **remains unverified**.
- **Tables chiffrantes et déchiffrantes, ou Chiffre Bazeries**, A. Hermann, 1893, and the **third African tour and Constantine command, late 1893 to 3 July 1894** **[R: dossier only, both]**.

## 6. The Dreyfus affair (1894-1899)

The contested centre of his reputation, treated as contested throughout.

- **Placed again at the Foreign Minister's disposal, 29 September 1894** **[R: dossier only]**, plausible given the confirmed 1891 attachment. **Dreyfus arrested at the Ministry of War sixteen days later, 15 October 1894** **[A: `captain_alfred_dreyfus.journey.json`]**.
- **The Panizzardi telegram.** bibmath's dedicated page, fetched directly: **sent 2 November 1894**; around **6 November** a fragmentary text readable as incriminating; by **13 November** the complete and exonerating message **[A]**. **The dossier's "2-10 November" is wrong; the correction landed on the 13th.**
- **Whether Bazeries worked on it is unresolved.** **Kahn** found no record proving his participation **[R]**; **Andrew** attributes the erroneous ending to him **[R: 1984, not fetched]**; **neither bibmath, nor fr.wikipedia's "Affaire Dreyfus", nor its "Télégramme de Panizzardi" names him at all**, the last crediting only *l'équipe du chiffre du Quai d'Orsay*.
- **Who received the false version is contested too.** fr.wikipedia attributes it to **Hubert Henry**, reconstructed *de mémoire* in 1898 for the Cour de cassation, not to the 1894 court-martial. The file asserts neither.
- **The Fashoda misreading of 30 September 1898**, and Andrew's portrait of him as poorly educated, without languages, alcoholic, a compulsive gambler, *un singulier bohème* **[R: Andrew 1984; the portrait rests on archives this research could not open]**.
- **The Orléanist Chevilly traffic and the Haute Cour testimony, 13 December 1898 onward** **[R: dossier only]**.
- **Napoleon's field ciphers of the 1813 Hamburg campaign**, 57 pages, first in *L'Abeille de Fontainebleau*, September 1896 **[R: dossier only, unlocated on BnF or Gallica]**.

## 7. Retirement, the book, the limits (1899-1907)

- **Retired from the active army, 1899** **[A as to year; R as to 18 March and Paris]**. **Final pencil-and-paper cipher rejected by the War Ministry as insufficiently secure under heavy traffic, 19 April 1899** **[R: dossier only]**.
- **Les Chiffres secrets dévoilés, 1901** **[A]**. **Imprint corrected to E. Fasquelle, Paris, 282 pages** (BnF and OpenLibrary OL2914915M agree, as does apprendre-en-ligne, which the dossier itself fetched). "Charpentier et Fasquelle" reflects the Bibliothèque-Charpentier series Fasquelle had absorbed, not the imprint of record.
- **The cylinder key-phrases**, pages 204-205: *Allons enfants de la patrie*, *Dieu protège la France*, *Évitez les courants d'air*, *J'aime l'oignon frit à l'huile* **[A: transcribed with page citation at bribes.org, cross-referenced to Friedman, *Military Cryptanalytics* II.2, 444]**. **Not read from the page image**: Gallica 403s on the 1901 ark. The file quotes the last.
- **The Japanese diplomatic code, 1904**: a week's work, a verdict of impossible, then commissaire **Haverna** and one assistant reconstructing most of the codebook in two months **[R: Andrew 1984]**. The sharpest check on the infallible-cryptanalyst legend, and unverified.
- **Paid Foreign Ministry work at Versailles, c. 1899 to the 1920s**, and **officier of the Légion d'honneur, 30 December 1907** **[R: dossier only; Léonore notice 22690 returned a search-page header]**.

## 8. The last war and afterward (1914-1931)

- **Worked on German ciphers during the First World War, not as head of the Section du Chiffre**, which by then included younger men such as Georges Painvin **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **What he actually solved is itemised nowhere reachable**, so the plaque's claim that he broke the German codes is civic memory, not verification.
- **Fully retired from cryptanalytic work in 1924, aged 78** **[A]**. Where he lived after that is unrecorded; the stop sits at the canonical Paris pin standing for the employing ministry, as `date_confidence` states.
- **Died at Noyon, Oise, 7 November 1931, aged 85** **[A: both Wikipedia editions]**. **Why Noyon is unexplained in every source reached.**
- **The grave is a documented gap.** No cemetery register, headstone, vault, or burial record in fr.wikipedia, en.wikipedia, bibmath, or cryptiana; the Léonore notice would not load. The file carries a stop stating the absence rather than filling it. A municipal search at Noyon is the next step, not possible here.
- **The Port-Vendres plaque** on the monument aux morts **[A: fr.wikipedia, citing monumentsmorts.univ-lille.fr, consulted 19 October 2019, now behind a 301 not re-fetched]**. **The date of erection is unrecorded**; the file's ISO date is the consultation date, used only to place the stop last, and says so.
- **Afterlife material carried as citations, not stops**: Candela (1938) **[R]**; Kahn's *The Codebreakers* **[R as to Panizzardi; A as to the p. 244 quotation]**; the 1983 Harvard microfilm of the 1901 book **[A: OpenLibrary]**; Commons' category, holding a portrait, a full PDF of the book, and a National Cryptologic Museum photograph of the cylinder illustration **[A]**. **No modern documentary, exhibit, or rediscovery was located** beyond these **[R: a gap, not proof of absence]**.
- **Two Kahn phrasings circulate**: "one of the greatest natural cryptanalysts the science has seen" (en.wikipedia, citing Kahn 1996, 244) and "one of the finest natural cryptanalysts that science has known" (fr.wikipedia). The first is the death stop's quote.

## Open contradictions, left standing

1. **Gendron's handover**: 1890 (bibmath, en.wikipedia, and the arithmetic of "three years") against Nantes 1891 (cryptiana). File dates it 1890, flags the conflict.
2. **M-94 adoption**: 1921 (cryptomuseum) against 1922 (en.wikipedia). Both given.
3. **Bulonde's death**: 1708 against 1709; the dossier gives each once. File uses 1709.
4. **Panizzardi authorship**: Kahn (unproven) against Andrew (Bazeries) against the French sources (nobody named). File states all three.
5. **Who received the false decrypt**: the 1894 court-martial (dossier) against Henry's 1898 reconstruction (fr.wikipedia). File asserts neither.
