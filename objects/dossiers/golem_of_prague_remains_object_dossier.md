# The Golem of Prague's claimed remains: Object Dossier

**Proposed filename:** `the_golem_of_pragues_claimed_remains_object_dossier.md`

## Scope and evidentiary conclusion

The object examined here is not a museum artifact, archaeological find, or scientifically authenticated relic. It is an alleged deposit of clay—the deactivated body, fragments, or dust of the Prague Golem—traditionally located in the attic of Prague’s Old-New Synagogue. No accession number, inventory record, verified specimen, photograph, laboratory sample, or authenticated eyewitness description establishes that such an object exists or ever existed.

The earliest located printed assertion about the remains dates not from the lifetime of Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel, the Maharal of Prague, but from **16 November 1836**, more than two centuries after his death. Ludwig August Frankl described “a piece of trunk-like clay” preserved beneath the roof of Prague’s oldest synagogue and called “Golem.” This is the earliest securely datable textual attestation presently identified for the alleged relic itself. It is a folkloric notice, not an inventory, deposition, or authenticated inspection report.

The subsequent record is the history of an unstable identity claim:

- **1836:** one visible, trunk-like piece of clay allegedly remains in the attic.
- **1847:** Leopold Weisel says the Golem collapsed and crumbled into pieces, which “can be seen” there.
- **1909:** Yudl Rosenberg’s literary fabrication converts the fragments into an intact, recumbent body, deliberately deactivated and concealed beneath worn sacred textiles and books.
- **Twentieth-century tradition:** the body either remains hidden under the attic gravel, has disintegrated into indistinguishable dust, or was stolen, unsuccessfully reanimated, placed in a coffin, and buried in the old Jewish cemetery at Žižkov.
- **Physical searches:** Egon Erwin Kisch’s inspection in or around 1920, Ivan Mackerle’s geophysical survey in March 1984, and photographs made during attic work in 2014 disclosed no identifiable remains.

The strongest evidence therefore supports the following limited conclusion: **the claimed remains constitute a legendary relic without an authenticated physical referent.** The Old-New Synagogue attic is real, historically accessible to authorized persons, and repeatedly inspected. The relic’s descriptions are literary and mutually inconsistent. No demonstrated chain joins any physical material now present there—or at Žižkov—to Rabbi Loew or to a sixteenth-century artificial body.

## 1. Identification and Physical Description

### 1.1 Object class

**Class:** legendary bodily relic; alleged deactivated artificial anthropoid; alternatively a heap or fragments of worked clay.

The claimed object is sometimes treated as:

- the intact but inert body of the Golem;
- the broken clay remnants of that body;
- a trunk-shaped mass without limbs;
- clay or dust buried beneath the attic floor;
- a heap covered by discarded sacred objects in a *genizah*;
- or a corpse-like object removed from the synagogue and buried at Žižkov.

These are not compatible descriptions of one documented artifact. They are successive variants in the legend.

### 1.2 Names

Relevant forms include:

- **Hebrew:** גולם מפראג, *Golem mi-Prag*, “Golem of Prague.”
- **Hebrew:** שרידי הגולם, *śeride ha-golem*, “the Golem’s remains”; הנשאר ממנו, *ha-nish’ar mimennu*, “what remained of it/him.”
- **Yiddish:** דער פראגער גולם, *der Prager goylem*.
- **German:** *der Prager Golem*; *die Überreste des Golems*; *das Golam des Rabbi Löw* in Franz Klutschak’s 1841 spelling.
- **Czech:** *pražský golem*; *pozůstatky Golema* or *Golemovy ostatky*.
- **Later personal names:** Josef, Joseph, Jossile, Jossele, or Yossele; “Yossele the Silent.” No personal name appears in the earliest 1836 notice or Weisel’s 1847 narrative. “Josef/Yossele” belongs principally to the expanded Rosenberg cycle.

The Hebrew noun גֹּלֶם, *golem*, denotes something unformed or incomplete. Its sole biblical occurrence is גָּלְמִי, *golmi*, “my unformed substance/body,” in Psalm 139:16. The biblical word is not the name of an artificial monster. Its later use for an artificially formed anthropoid emerged through rabbinic and mystical interpretation. [Psalm 139:16 and commentaries](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.139.16?with=Commentary); [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Golem”](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6777-golem).

### 1.3 Physical descriptions by source

| Date and source | Description of alleged remains | Evidentiary status |
|---|---|---|
| 1836, Ludwig A. Frankl, “Vaterländische Sagen und Legenden IV” | A piece of clay, compared to a trunk or torso, retaining its “primeval form and color,” under the roof of the Altneuschul. | Earliest located printed claim; folkloric report, not documented inspection. |

Frankl’s earliest description is especially important. In the modern English translation it reads: “Under the roof of the oldest synagogue in Prague … there is preserved, in its primeval form and color, a piece of trunk-like clay, which is known by the name ‘Golem.’” Nothing in the notice establishes dimensions, weight, manufacture, inscriptions, anatomical detail, or the identity of anyone who examined it. [Posen Library transcription](https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/ancestral-tales-and-legends); [Dekel and Gurley, “How the Golem Came to Prague”](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~kazhdan/Shneider/Dekel%2CGurley.Golem.pdf).

### 1.4 Alleged materials and construction

Across the traditions, the body is made from:

- clay;
- mud or loam;
- earth and clay taken from the bank of the Vltava/Moldau;
- or, in Weisel’s more general formulation, clay or possibly carved wood—although the particular servant of Rabbi Loew is said to be clay.

Rosenberg’s 1909 version specifies a human figure molded on the Vltava bank on 20 Adar 5340, corresponding to 1580 in his chronology. Rabbi Loew and two assistants walk around it seven times while reciting letter combinations; fire, water, air, and earth are ritually combined. Hair and nails emerge, the body is clothed, and the three recite Genesis 2:7. The being is activated by sacred words rather than by firing, mechanical construction, or ordinary sculpture. [Hebrew excerpt from Rosenberg](https://tarbutil.cet.ac.il/literature/%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9A-%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%90-%D7%9E%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%9C-%D7%90%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%9D/).

No clay sample has been recovered, chemically characterized, petrographically compared with Vltava sediments, thermoluminescence-dated, or radiocarbon-dated. No residue of a parchment *Shem*, inscription, armature, clothing, or tool mark has been documented.

### 1.5 Inscriptions and activation devices

The sources disagree:

- Weisel places a **Shem**, a written or spoken divine-name formula, in the Golem’s mouth.
- Many later popular versions place אמת, *emet*, “truth,” on its forehead; erasing א, *aleph*, leaves מת, *met*, “dead.”
- Rosenberg emphasizes combinations of sacred letters and names and a deactivation ritual performed in reverse order.
- Modern films often replace the written formula with a conspicuous metal or stone tablet inserted into the chest. That chest-mounted device is a cinematic design, not an early Prague textual detail.

No such inscription or device survives as an authenticated relic.

### 1.6 Current condition

**Documented condition:** no object identifiable as the Golem’s remains is known.

**Traditional condition:** dormant, disintegrated, buried, concealed, or awaiting possible reanimation.

**Cataloguing:** none. The Jewish Museum in Prague discusses the remains explicitly as legend. It does not publish an accession number or claim custody of a physical body or fragments. [Jewish Museum in Prague](https://www.jewishmuseum.cz/en/explore/sites/old-new-synagogue/).

## 2. Origin, Manufacture, and Earliest Attestation

### 2.1 Background: golems before Prague

The conceptual prehistory is much older than the Prague relic. Psalm 139:16 uses *golem* for an unformed body. Rabbinic texts describe Adam’s initially unformed state and sages creating a man or calf through sacred knowledge. Medieval *Sefer Yetzirah* traditions developed procedures involving Hebrew letters and divine names.

Those traditions prove that Jewish artificial-human speculation predates Prague. They do **not** prove that Rabbi Loew created a Golem or that clay remains were deposited in the Old-New Synagogue.

Before the Prague attribution crystallized, a golem legend was associated particularly with Rabbi Elijah Ba‘al Shem of Chełm. An anonymous seventeenth-century account links an artificial servant with Elijah; later versions describe destruction through altering אמת to מת. Edan Dekel and David Gurley characterize the golem story as mobile or “ecotypified”: storytellers could attach it to different rabbis and towns before Prague became its fixed literary center. [Dekel and Gurley](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~kazhdan/Shneider/Dekel%2CGurley.Golem.pdf).

### 2.2 Absence from contemporary Maharal evidence

Judah Loew ben Bezalel died in 1609. Neither his extensive surviving writings nor known contemporary records identify him as the creator of a Golem. David Gans, who knew and praised him, does not record such a creation. His epitaph does not mention it. The silence is not mathematical proof that no private experiment occurred, but it leaves the sixteenth-century claim unsupported.

Joseph Davis’s study of the Maharal’s pre-Golem legendary reputation concludes that the first written text connecting him with the Prague Golem appears only in 1836. [Joseph Davis, “The Legend of Maharal before the Golem”](https://www.ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=92375).

### 2.3 Earliest attestation of the claimed relic: 1836

The earliest identified printed attestation is Ludwig August Frankl’s short German contribution, “Vaterländische Sagen und Legenden IV,” published in issue 92 of the *Oesterreichische Zeitschrift für Geschichts- und Staatskunde* on 16 November 1836, page 368.

What it asserts:

- the location is beneath the roof of Prague’s oldest synagogue, called the Altneuschul;
- workers avoid disturbing it for fear of misfortune;
- a trunk-like clay object remains in its original shape and color;
- it is called “Golem”;
- its maker is a learned rabbi whom Jews call “High-Reb Leb.”

What it does **not** supply:

- a date of manufacture;
- a named eyewitness;
- dimensions or a drawing;
- a synagogue inventory;
- evidence that Frankl entered the attic;
- evidence that the clay was tested;
- the blood-libel-defense story;
- the name Yossele;
- a Vltava-bank creation scene;
- an intact body concealed beneath sacred books.

Frankl’s notice is therefore both the object’s earliest textual “birth certificate” and a weak one: a nineteenth-century folkloric assertion framed as local belief.

### 2.4 Early growth, 1837–1847

Berthold Auerbach incorporated a Maharal-Golem story into his historical novel *Spinoza* in 1837. Franz Klutschak published “Das Golam des Rabbi Löw” in Prague’s *Panorama des Universums* in 1841. Abraham Tendlau and Gustav Philippson issued further versions in 1841–1842.

Leopold Weisel’s 1847 “Der Golem,” printed in Wolf Pascheles’s *Gallerie der Sipurim*, became the standard nineteenth-century form. Weisel says Rabbi Loew molded a clay servant, animated it by placing a *Shem* in its mouth, and employed it in domestic and civic labor. When the rabbi forgot to remove the formula before Sabbath, the servant ran wild, demolishing houses, uprooting trees and throwing stones. Loew removed the formula before the Sabbath had formally begun in the Old-New Synagogue; the Golem collapsed into pieces. Weisel ends by asserting that pieces remained visible in the attic. [Digitized *Sippurim* collection](https://sammlungen.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/freimann/content/structure/412134); [English translation in Dekel and Gurley](https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/20471505/how-the-golem-came-to-prague-jewish-quarterly-review-/5).

This version supplies a causal origin for the “remains”: accidental destruction. It does not yet describe a carefully preserved whole body.

### 2.5 Rosenberg’s 1909 manufacture narrative

Rabbi Yehudah Yudl Rosenberg published ספר נפלאות מהר״ל עם הגולם, *Sefer Nifle’ot Maharal ‘im ha-Golem*—“The Wonders of the Maharal with the Golem”—at Piotrków in 1909. He represented it as an old eyewitness manuscript by the Maharal’s son-in-law, Isaac ben Samson Katz, supposedly preserved in the Royal Library at Metz and obtained through a manuscript dealer.

In this narrative:

1. recurring ritual-murder accusations threaten Prague’s Jews;
2. Rabbi Loew receives heavenly authorization to create a Golem;
3. Loew, Isaac Katz and Jacob Sasson the Levite fast and purify themselves;
4. they gather clay beside the Vltava;
5. they form an anatomically complete man;
6. successive circuits and letter combinations introduce fire, water and air into earth;
7. Genesis 2:7 completes the animation;
8. Loew names the creature Josef or Yossele and commands it to protect Prague’s Jews;
9. after ten years of service, it is ordered to sleep in the synagogue attic;
10. the creation procedure is reversed;
11. the inert body is covered with worn prayer shawls and sacred books;
12. access to the attic is forbidden.

Rosenberg’s book is enormously influential but not a sixteenth-century witness. Shnayer Leiman documents chronological errors: Rosenberg makes Rudolf II king and emperor in 1573, although Maximilian II then held those positions; he invents a Cardinal Johann Sylvester unattested in Prague or elsewhere; and his account conflicts with the documented date of the Maharal’s meeting with Rudolf. Leiman concludes that the supposed Metz manuscript was a modern literary hoax authored by Rosenberg. [Leiman’s full study](https://leimanlibrary.com/wp-content/texts_of_publications/93.%20The%20Adventure%20of%20the%20Maharal%20of%20Prague%20in%20London%20R.%20Yudel%20Rosenberg%20and%20the%20Golem%20of%20Prague.pdf); [National Library of Israel discussion](https://blog.nli.org.il/prague_golem/).

Rosenberg did not invent the Maharal-Golem association or the attic-remains claim; both predate him. He did invent or consolidate much of the detailed biography, physical construction, blood-libel mission, name, assistants, dates, and deliberate attic interment now commonly mistaken for ancient tradition.

## 3. Provenance and Custody History

Because no authenticated object is available, two chains must be kept separate.

### 3.1 Legendary custody chain

#### Creation and initial control, traditionally 1580

Rosenberg assigns creation to Rabbi Loew, assisted by Isaac Katz and Jacob Sasson, on the Vltava bank. Loew is sole commander and keeper of the animating formula. The Golem is then housed or employed in the rabbi’s household and Prague’s Jewish quarter.

**Status:** 1909 literary account, not sixteenth-century documentation.

#### Deactivation and deposit, traditionally 1590

In Rosenberg’s version Loew directs the Golem to sleep in the Old-New Synagogue attic on Lag ba-Omer 5350/1590. Loew and his assistants reverse the creative ritual. The clay body remains prone, covered with worn sacred objects. Loew orders that the public be told the servant disappeared and prohibits attic access.

**Status:** literary narrative. Earlier versions usually say the Golem collapsed after the *Shem* was removed; they do not supply this formal burial.

#### Alleged continuity in the attic, 1590–1836

No contemporary or seventeenth-century synagogue record documents the body. There is therefore a documentary gap of approximately 246 years between Rosenberg’s purported deposit date and the earliest printed assertion in 1836.

The claim that the legend “survived for centuries” may refer to presumed oral transmission. It cannot itself fill the custody gap.

#### Frankl’s claimed attic object, 1836

Frankl says a trunk-like piece of clay is preserved under the roof. He does not say who legally owns it, who guards it, when it was last handled, or that he personally saw it.

#### Weisel’s claimed fragments, 1847

Weisel says fragments remain visible in the attic. This differs physically from Frankl’s singular trunk-like mass and Rosenberg’s later intact body.

#### Ezekiel Landau tradition

A tradition published in Rabbi Chaim Noach Levin’s notes to Meir Perles’s *Megillat Yuḥasin* in 1864 says Rabbi Ezekiel Landau, chief rabbi of Prague from 1755 until his death in 1793, prepared by fasting, immersion, prayer shawl and phylacteries before ascending—or attempting to ascend—to see the remains. He returned disturbed and renewed the prohibition.

The story was recorded after Landau’s death and after the Golem legend was already in print. Versions disagree on whether Landau fully entered the room, merely put his head through the opening, or retreated at the threshold. It is not a documented inspection, and Landau left no identified description of what he saw. [Leiman](https://leimanlibrary.com/wp-content/texts_of_publications/93.%20The%20Adventure%20of%20the%20Maharal%20of%20Prague%20in%20London%20R.%20Yudel%20Rosenberg%20and%20the%20Golem%20of%20Prague.pdf).

#### Removal of genizah material, 1883

A later Czech account reports that old *shemot*, books, papers and other sacred refuse were removed from the attic for hygienic reasons in 1883 and buried in a cemetery. This is plausible genizah practice, but the available report does not identify an inventory, removal register, or physical item recognized as the Golem.

The Old-New Synagogue was restored in 1883 under architect Josef Mocker. Later popular assertions that “no Golem was found during the restoration” are reasonable in the limited sense that no authenticated discovery was reported. They do not establish that every floor cavity was excavated or that all removed material was individually examined.

#### Alleged nineteenth-century theft and Žižkov burial

Kisch reports learning a story that three men removed the body from the attic during the nineteenth century, took it to a house in the former ghetto, and attempted to revive it. After failing, they placed it in a coffin and buried it in the Jewish cemetery at Žižkov.

No names, dates, grave number, police record, burial register, coffin, or exhumation confirm this story. It functions as an explanatory legend for why searches of the attic find nothing.

The old Žižkov Jewish cemetery was founded in 1680, used regularly from 1787 to 1890, and originally contained about 40,000 burials. Much was converted to Mahler Gardens in the 1960s and further damaged during construction of the Žižkov Television Tower in the 1980s. Thus the alternative legend transfers the remains to a real but heavily disturbed site where recovery is now practically difficult. The Prague Jewish Community does not list a Golem grave. [Prague Jewish Community cemetery history](https://www.synagogue.cz/he/hrbitovy?p=24).

#### Kisch’s authorized inspection, approximately 1920

After obtaining permission from Prague’s Jewish community, reporter Egon Erwin Kisch climbed into the attic. He observed structural beams, vault ridges, gravel, bricks, a chimney pipe, fungi, birds and a bat but no Golem.

Kisch did not excavate the gravel. He concluded rhetorically that if a clay creation were buried there, removing it might require collapsing the synagogue vault. His report therefore establishes authorized entry and absence of an obvious body, not absence of every possible clay deposit. [Kisch’s German text](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/egon-erwin-kisch/books/geschichten-aus-sieben-ghettos/chapter/13/); [Prague City Tourism summary](https://prague.eu/en/spiritual-prague/the-old-new-synagogue-resting-place-of-the-mythical-golem/).

#### Chabad witness traditions, early twentieth century

A letter attributed to Menachem Mendel Schneerson records that he personally heard his father-in-law, Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, say he had seen “what remained of” the Golem in the attic. Related Chabad tradition describes Sholom Dovber Schneersohn or a member of his party approaching the attic, turning pale, and declaring entry impermissible.

The versions are not uniform about who ascended or what was seen. No photograph, independent witness statement, physical description, date-stamped synagogue permission, or recovered specimen accompanies the claim. It is important as a living Hasidic testimony, but it cannot be converted into an authenticated material provenance. [Hebrew presentation of the Chabad tradition](https://chabadpedia.co.il/wiki/%D7%94%D7%92%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%9D_%D7%9E%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%92).

#### Nazi-period tales

Modern retellings sometimes say:

- Nazi or Gestapo officers feared to enter the attic;
- one officer entered and died;
- the Golem awakened to kill an intruder;
- or the Nazis spared the Old-New Synagogue because of the Golem.

No officer has been reliably named and no German administrative, police, military, medical, or synagogue record has been identified for such a death. These are late survival or curse narratives, not established wartime events. That the synagogue survived Nazi occupation is documented; supernatural causation is not.

#### Ivan Mackerle’s survey, March 1984

Czech mystery researcher Ivan Mackerle obtained access with assistants and geophysical equipment. He reported an easily surveyed attic about 15 metres long and 10 metres wide, without niches or concealed corners. Radar recorded the underlying vaults; the floor or masonry cover was said to be under approximately 40 cm thick. A suspected anomaly proved to be building fabric. No body or unusual deposit was identified. [Radio Prague interview with Daniel Mackerle](https://espanol.radio.cz/ivan-mackerle-el-cazador-de-misterios-8684001); [Mackerle’s illustrated account](https://www.kpufo.eu/veas/htm/s_gol.htm).

The survey was not a controlled archaeological excavation and apparently produced no peer-reviewed technical report, calibration record, raw radar data, or sampling program. Its negative result is nevertheless material evidence against an intact human-sized body hidden in the accessible floor structure.

#### 2014 access during building work

An Israeli film crew reportedly photographed the attic while renovation or construction work was occurring in August 2014. The published photographs show roof timbers and a largely empty structural space. No Golem object was reported. This was not a formal archaeological publication. [Contemporary photograph post](https://thepartialview.blogspot.com/2014/08/pictures-inside-atic-of-altneu-shul-in.html).

### 3.2 Documented custody chain

The documented chain is extremely short:

1. The Old-New Synagogue and its attic physically exist.
2. The synagogue has served Prague’s Jewish community for more than seven centuries.
3. The attic has at various times held ordinary architectural material and possibly genizah refuse.
4. Authorized visitors and workers have entered it.
5. No catalogued object has been identified, transferred, conserved, or accessioned as the Golem’s remains.

There is consequently no object-level chain of title or custody. The Prague Jewish Community controls the building and access to its attic; that does not prove it possesses the alleged relic.

## 4. Authenticity and Identity Disputes

### 4.1 Does the named object exist?

No authenticated physical candidate has been produced. The present identity claim lacks the minimum archival features ordinarily expected for a relic:

- continuous or even intermittent inventories;
- a fixed physical description;
- dated photographs;
- recorded measurements;
- material samples;
- conservation files;
- an accession or relic authentication;
- witness continuity;
- or a marked repository location.

The descriptions also evolve from one clay trunk to broken fragments to a complete body covered in genizah material.

### 4.2 The strongest affirmative case

The affirmative case consists of cumulative tradition:

- Frankl’s 1836 notice asserts a visible clay object.
- Weisel’s 1847 version says fragments remained visible.
- The Landau story places an eighteenth-century rabbinic authority at the attic threshold.
- Chabad testimony says a respected religious leader saw what remained.
- Restrictions and physical difficulty of access may have allowed material to survive undisturbed.
- Clay concealed among dust, gravel, and decaying sacred refuse might be indistinguishable from ordinary building material.

This case depends almost entirely on testimony transmitted after the alleged events. None of its witnesses provides a testable description or authenticated sample.

### 4.3 The strongest negative case

The negative case is documentary and material:

- no contemporary Maharal source mentions creation of a Golem;
- no seventeenth- or eighteenth-century inventory identifies the body;
- the earliest claim appears in the Romantic folklore environment of 1836;
- the story resembles older, mobile golem legends associated with other rabbis;
- physical descriptions contradict one another;
- the most detailed narrative, Rosenberg’s 1909 book, is based on a fictitious manuscript provenance and contains demonstrable historical errors;
- authorized twentieth-century examinations found no body;
- no scientific finding supports a sixteenth-century artifact.

The negative evidence does not prove that no unidentified clay ever lay in the attic. It does show that no evidentiary basis currently permits identifying any clay as the Golem.

### 4.4 Rosenberg’s manuscript fabrication

Rosenberg claimed his text derived from an eyewitness manuscript by Isaac Katz found in Metz. He printed or described a bill of sale connected with a dealer named Chaim Scharfstein. Researchers have not located the alleged original manuscript, its Metz catalogue entry, the original bill of sale, or a verifiable documentary trail.

Mendel Eckstein challenged the work shortly after publication. Gershom Scholem, Joseph Dan, Eli Yassif, Shnayer Leiman and other scholars have treated the supposed ancient provenance as untenable. Leiman identifies incorrect rulers and dates, a fictitious cardinal, and other anachronisms. The National Library of Israel summarizes the text as an influential modern literary creation whose pseudo-documentary apparatus misled readers. [National Library of Israel](https://blog.nli.org.il/prague_golem/); [Leiman](https://leimanlibrary.com/wp-content/texts_of_publications/93.%20The%20Adventure%20of%20the%20Maharal%20of%20Prague%20in%20London%20R.%20Yudel%20Rosenberg%20and%20the%20Golem%20of%20Prague.pdf).

The proper conclusion is not that Rosenberg invented every Prague Golem tradition. The attic-fragment claim predates him by at least seventy-three years. He supplied the pseudo-historical narrative that made those fragments into the remains of a named protector created for a specific blood-libel campaign.

### 4.5 Rival present locations

Two principal location claims survive:

1. **Old-New Synagogue attic:** institutional and tourist tradition; earliest attested location.
2. **Old Jewish cemetery at Žižkov:** secondary removal-and-reburial legend reported by Kisch.

A peripheral tradition places a golem in an attic at Poznań/Posen. This reflects the mobility of golem narratives and should not be treated as an independently proven transfer of the Prague object.

### 4.6 Scientific examinations

No scientific examination of a claimed specimen has occurred because no specimen has been isolated.

Mackerle’s radar survey examined the architectural setting rather than the relic. Its negative result is not chemical or chronological authentication. There has been no:

- archaeological excavation directed specifically at the claim;
- sediment-stratigraphic publication;
- thermoluminescence dating;
- mineralogical provenance study;
- organic-residue analysis;
- DNA testing;
- facial reconstruction;
- or comparison with sixteenth-century Prague ceramics.

### 4.7 Authority to authenticate

Authority has shifted between:

- nineteenth-century folklorists, who published local assertions;
- Prague rabbis and synagogue attendants, who controlled access;
- Hasidic leaders, whose testimony carries religious authority within their communities;
- Prague’s Jewish Community, which controls the synagogue;
- museums and historians, which classify the account as legend;
- and physical investigators, who can report only that they found no identifiable object.

No church-like canonical process, rabbinical court ruling, state heritage declaration, or museum acquisition has authenticated the relic.

## 6. Ritual, Ceremonial, and Symbolic Function

### 6.1 Function while animated

In Weisel’s version, the artificial servant:

- chops wood;
- carries water;
- sweeps streets;
- performs unpaid, tireless labor;
- and must be deactivated for Sabbath.

In Rosenberg’s expanded version, its primary function becomes collective defense: exposing ritual-murder plots, preventing blood libels, protecting Jews, locating evidence, and acting as the Maharal’s physical agent.

This defensive role is a late development. The earliest Prague forms concern an artificial servant who becomes dangerous, not a superhero created principally to fight antisemitism.

### 6.2 Deactivation rite

The body’s alleged transformation into “remains” is ritual:

- removal of the divine-name formula;
- erasure of a Hebrew letter in later variants;
- reversal of the creation circuits and formulas in Rosenberg;
- withdrawal of speech or breath;
- covering with sacred textiles and texts;
- prohibition of further entry.

The residue is therefore not merely broken sculpture. Within the legend it is matter from which sacred animation has been withdrawn but might conceivably be restored.

### 6.3 Sabbath and synagogue liturgy

A Prague tradition connects the Golem’s rampage with the recitation of Psalms 92 and 93 at the beginning of Sabbath. Because Rabbi Loew supposedly interrupted the service to deactivate the Golem and then began again, the Old-New Synagogue is said to repeat the relevant psalm.

The custom may be real while its Golem explanation is etiological—that is, a story devised to explain an existing local liturgical practice. No sixteenth-century record supplied here demonstrates that the practice originated in a Golem incident. Prague City Tourism presents it explicitly as legend linked to continuing custom. [Prague City Tourism](https://prague.eu/cs/staronova-synagoga-misto-odpocinku-mytickeho-golema/).

### 6.4 Taboos and access

The taboo includes:

- no ordinary person may climb into the attic;
- disturbing the remains brings injury or death;
- only properly prepared religious authorities may approach;
- building workers risk falling;
- and the body must not be reanimated unless the community again requires protection.

The attic is in fact difficult to reach. Its elevated external door and incomplete or high-set access system visibly reinforce the legend. Restricted public access is consistent with safety, conservation, security and religious control; it is not independent proof of a supernatural occupant.

### 6.5 Symbolic function of the remains

The remains serve as:

- a latent protector who may return;
- proof, within believing traditions, of the Maharal’s sacred knowledge;
- an explanation for the sanctity and danger of the attic;
- a material anchor for a story otherwise lacking artifacts;
- a symbol of Prague Jewish survival;
- and a tourist landmark connecting the real medieval synagogue with the legendary Rudolfine city.

Unlike a public reliquary, the object’s power depends upon non-display. Its inaccessibility both protects the claim from inspection and magnifies it.

## 7. Myths and Legends Attached to the Object

### 7.1 The dangerous servant

**Earliest developed printed form:** Weisel, 1847, with antecedents in Frankl and other early nineteenth-century versions.

Rabbi Loew creates an obedient clay servant. Every Friday he removes the *Shem* so it will remain inactive during Sabbath. One Friday he forgets. The servant rampages through Prague, throwing stones, uprooting trees and damaging houses. Loew extracts the formula; the figure collapses into pieces. Those pieces remain in the attic.

**Type:** early attested Prague literary legend.

**Probable narrative sources:** older golem-servant stories and the Romantic “sorcerer’s apprentice” motif. Weisel’s text explicitly invites comparison with Goethe’s poem.

### 7.2 The Jewish defender

**Principal source:** Rosenberg, 1909.

Prague’s Jews face systematic blood libels. The Maharal constructs Yossele to obtain evidence, defeat conspirators and prevent violence. When the danger passes, he deactivates the protector but preserves the body in case future generations need it.

**Type:** modern literary elaboration presented as early-modern history.

**Historical problem:** the detailed political setting, dates and named cardinal are demonstrably defective. The blood-libel mission is absent from the earliest Prague accounts.

**Function:** it converts a dangerous servant into a Jewish communal guardian and answers modern anxieties about persecution and powerlessness.

### 7.3 The attic burial and future awakening

The inert body lies under prayer shawls, books or synagogue dust. It will remain there until Prague’s Jews again face mortal danger, the Messiah comes, or a qualified master restores the divine name.

**Type:** late nineteenth- and twentieth-century elaboration.

No uniform early prophecy says precisely when it will awaken. The open-ended possibility is central to later retellings.

### 7.4 Rabbi Landau’s forbidden inspection

Landau fasts, immerses, dons ritual garments and ascends. He returns pale or trembling and forbids anyone else to enter. Some versions say he saw the body; others say nothing about what he saw; others say he did not fully enter.

**Earliest located publication:** 1864.

**Type:** transmitted rabbinic legend.

The story establishes religious rather than empirical authentication: Landau’s silence becomes evidence of awe.

### 7.5 The fatal workmen

Workers who alter or repair the synagogue roof supposedly fall and die. A roofer named Vondrejc is said to have fallen in the 1880s. These deaths are linked variously to:

- disturbing the Golem;
- violating the Maharal’s prohibition;
- or altering a synagogue whose stones angels delivered from Jerusalem.

The causal attribution is legendary. The overlapping synagogue-foundation and Golem traditions show how a pre-existing architectural taboo could absorb the relic claim.

### 7.6 Theft and reburial at Žižkov

Three men steal the Golem, carry it to a house, and try to reactivate it. When they fail, they place it in a coffin and bury it in the Žižkov cemetery.

**Earliest located reporter:** Kisch’s twentieth-century account of a story he heard.

**Type:** secondary removal legend.

**Narrative function:** explains why the attic is empty while preserving physical survival elsewhere.

### 7.7 Nazi intruder and supernatural survival

A Nazi officer enters the attic and dies, or the Golem prevents the synagogue’s destruction.

**Type:** post-Holocaust accretion; sometimes presented without a source.

No officer, date, unit, death record, or contemporary testimony has been established. The story sacralizes the building’s survival and adapts the protector legend to the twentieth century.

### 7.8 Clay mistaken for dust

Some believers answer negative searches by arguing that centuries of decay reduced the body to dust indistinguishable from attic fill. This is physically unfalsifiable unless a stratified sample with a demonstrable artificial composition is recovered. It also differs from the 1836 and 1847 claims that recognizable material could be seen.

### 7.9 “The attic has never been entered”

This popular claim is false. Landau’s entry is itself legendary, but Kisch and Mackerle obtained documented access, workers have repaired the roof, and a film crew photographed the interior in 2014. What remains true is that the attic is not part of ordinary public access.

## 8. Current Status and Location

### 8.1 Claimed principal location

**Traditional site:** attic of the Old-New Synagogue, Červená Street, Josefov, Prague 1, Czech Republic.

The synagogue was built in the last third of the thirteenth century by masons associated with the royal workshop working at the nearby Convent of St Agnes. It was originally called the New or Great Shul and later the Old-New Synagogue. It remains an active synagogue and the main historic house of worship of Prague’s Jewish community. [Jewish Museum in Prague](https://www.jewishmuseum.cz/en/explore/sites/old-new-synagogue/).

The Jewish Museum’s current description says that “legend has it” the attic is home to the clay remains. It does not say the museum or community possesses an authenticated relic.

### 8.2 Precise status

- **Existence:** unverified and unsupported by material evidence.
- **Visible display:** none.
- **Accession number:** none published.
- **Conservation record:** none for a Golem relic.
- **Scientific authentication:** none.
- **Public access:** the synagogue interior is publicly visitable through the Jewish Town circuit, subject to religious and visitor regulations; the attic is not part of ordinary admission.
- **Building controller:** Prague Jewish Community; the Jewish Museum in Prague currently incorporates the synagogue into its visitor circuit.
- **Heritage regime:** the synagogue is a protected Czech cultural monument; one official heritage proceeding identifies it as national cultural monument register no. 38867/1-452. [Czech heritage record](https://iispp.npu.cz/mis_public/documentDetail.htm?id=738475).

### 8.3 Alternative current location

The Žižkov theory places the body beneath the former old Jewish cemetery, possibly under or near the present Žižkov Television Tower and Mahler Gardens. No grave is identified. The cemetery’s extensive twentieth-century destruction makes the claim difficult to investigate, but difficulty is not evidence for it.

### 8.4 Serious present conclusion

No known physical object currently stands in for the remains. The operative “relic” is the attic-site association itself. Visitors see:

- the Old-New Synagogue;
- the high attic opening and exterior access;
- the Maharal’s traditional seat;
- tourist reproductions of the Golem;
- and the Maharal’s grave elsewhere in Prague.

The site receives the relic’s identity even though the supposed body is neither shown nor authenticated.

## 9. Modern Reception

### 9.1 Literature

The nineteenth-century German-language publications transformed a mobile Jewish mystical legend into a specifically Prague story. Key stages include:

- Ludwig A. Frankl, 1836;
- Berthold Auerbach, *Spinoza*, 1837;
- Franz Klutschak, 1841;
- Gustav Philippson, 1841;
- Abraham Tendlau, 1842;
- Leopold Weisel, 1847;
- Ludwig Kalisch, 1864;
- Leopold Kompert, 1882;
- Moritz Bermann, 1883;
- and Yudl Rosenberg, 1909.

Gustav Meyrink’s novel *Der Golem*, serialized in 1913–1914 and published as a book in 1915, cemented the association between the Golem and a dreamlike occult Prague. Meyrink’s spectral Golem is not simply Rosenberg’s clay defender, but public reception often merges the two.

H. Leivick’s Yiddish dramatic poem *Der Goylem* further transformed the being into a tragic figure torn between violence, obedience and humanity.

### 9.2 Film and visual form

Paul Wegener’s German films—especially *Der Golem, wie er in die Welt kam* (1920)—gave the creature its massive expressionist body. Film replaced textual ambiguity with a recognizable humanoid costume and helped internationalize the Prague story.

The Czech film *Císařův pekař – Pekařův císař* (*The Emperor and the Golem*, 1951–1952), starring Jan Werich, created the familiar Czech image of a monumental clay figure animated through a device in its chest. That image heavily influences Prague souvenirs. It is not derived from Frankl’s trunk-like clay piece or Weisel’s scattered fragments.

### 9.3 Replicas and commercial substitutes

Prague shops sell innumerable Golem figurines in fired clay, ceramic, resin, metal, wood and chocolate. These are openly modern souvenirs rather than rival authentic remains. Their designs usually descend from cinema, children’s illustration, or generic robot imagery.

The Jewish Museum Berlin’s *GOLEM* exhibition catalog notes that the myth remains visible in Prague storefronts, the Maharal’s grave, and rumors surrounding the inaccessible synagogue attic. [Jewish Museum Berlin](https://www.jmberlin.de/en/online-catalog-golem-chapter-4).

### 9.4 Tourism and site economy

The claimed remains give the Old-New Synagogue an additional identity beyond its architectural and religious significance. Official Prague tourism material recounts the body-in-the-attic story while explicitly stating that no traces have been found. [Prague City Tourism](https://prague.eu/en/spiritual-prague/the-old-new-synagogue-resting-place-of-the-mythical-golem/).

This is not an officially authenticated relic cult. It is heritage interpretation in which institutional sources maintain a clear “according to legend” qualification.

### 9.5 Political and cultural mobilization

The Rosenberg version made the Golem a defender against antisemitic violence and ritual-murder accusations. Later works have used the figure as:

- an emblem of Jewish self-defense;
- a warning about uncontrolled power;
- a prototype robot or artificial intelligence;
- a figure for labor without agency;
- an image of diaspora memory;
- and a symbol of Prague’s Jewish presence after the Holocaust.

The dormant remains support a distinctive political image: protective force preserved but deliberately restrained by ethical or religious authority.

### 9.6 Modern hoaxes and pseudo-history

The most consequential pseudo-documentary object is not a clay body but Rosenberg’s alleged manuscript. Its claimed Metz provenance, bill of sale and eyewitness authorship gave modern fiction the appearance of archival history.

Modern claims that photographs show the body, that Nazi records document a supernatural death, or that radar detected a buried giant have not been substantiated in the sources reviewed.

## 10. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### 10.1 Major approaches

**History of Jewish magic:** Gershom Scholem, Moshe Idel and Peter Schäfer place golem-making within traditions of *Sefer Yetzirah*, sacred language and anthropogenesis. Their work explains the conceptual possibility of a golem but does not authenticate Prague’s remains.

**Folklore migration:** Edan Dekel and David Gurley show how a mobile story attached itself progressively to Prague and Rabbi Loew. Their recovery and analysis of Frankl’s 1836 notice moves the first printed Prague attestation earlier than the previously emphasized 1841 Klutschak version.

**Czech-Jewish cultural history:** Hillel Kieval treats the Prague Golem as an “invented tradition” emerging amid nineteenth-century transformations in Jewish cultural identity and German-language folklore.

**Maharal biography and reputation:** Joseph Davis emphasizes that the Maharal possessed a legendary reputation before the Golem story attached to him, but contemporary and early posthumous evidence does not make him a golem-maker.

**Rosenberg criticism:** Mendel Eckstein, Gershom Scholem, Joseph Dan, Eli Yassif and Shnayer Leiman identify *Nifle’ot Maharal* as modern composition rather than recovered eyewitness history.

### 10.2 Dating dispute

Older scholarship sometimes gave:

- 1841, Klutschak, as the first Prague Golem text;
- 1837, Auerbach, as the first Maharal attribution;
- or 1847, Weisel, as the decisive Prague version.

Dekel and Gurley identify Frankl’s 1836 notice as earlier and explicitly tied to both the Old-New Synagogue attic and “High-Reb Leb.” It is consequently the earliest located printed attestation of the relic claim.

### 10.3 Oral tradition

Early authors sometimes represented their stories as oral Jewish tradition. Tendlau stated that his knowledge rested on oral accounts. The recurrence of similar material by 1836–1847 makes some pre-publication oral circulation plausible.

What cannot be established is how old that oral tradition was. Claims that it extends continuously to 1609, or even to the eighteenth century, exceed the evidence. The correct boundary is:

- **plausible inference:** oral narratives circulated before their publication;
- **unresolved:** their date of origin;
- **unsupported:** a continuous sixteenth-century chain.

### 10.4 State of the primary evidence

Surviving relevant evidence includes:

- Frankl’s 1836 journal notice;
- Auerbach’s 1837 novel;
- Klutschak’s 1841 article;
- Tendlau and Philippson’s early texts;
- Weisel’s 1847 printed tale;
- Levin’s 1864 notes containing the Landau tradition;
- Rosenberg’s printed 1909 editions;
- Kisch’s report;
- photographs and descriptions of the attic;
- building-restoration history;
- and modern institutional presentations.

Missing or unlocated evidence includes:

- any sixteenth-century creation record;
- any Maharal manuscript mentioning his Golem;
- any early synagogue inventory of the body;
- Rosenberg’s alleged Metz manuscript;
- Rosenberg’s original bill of sale;
- a burial record at Žižkov;
- contemporary documentation of Landau’s ascent;
- records of a Nazi officer killed in the attic;
- a scientific survey report identifying anomalous clay;
- or a specimen available for testing.

### 10.5 Discredited but persistent theories

The following continue in popular circulation despite inadequate evidence:

- that Rosenberg merely edited a genuine sixteenth-century manuscript;
- that the Golem was unquestionably created in 1580 and deactivated in 1590;
- that “Yossele” is an early traditional name;
- that the attic remained unopened from the Maharal’s time;
- that a Nazi officer’s death is documented;
- that the 1984 radar survey found a buried body;
- and that the synagogue’s access restrictions themselves authenticate the remains.
