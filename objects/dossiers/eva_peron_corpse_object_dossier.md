# Eva Peron's embalmed corpse: Object Dossier

**Proposed filename:** `eva_perons_embalmed_corpse_object_dossier.md`

## Evidentiary note

Eva Perón’s preserved body unquestionably existed and is documented photographically, medically, administratively, and through multiple witnessed transfers. The principal uncertainty is not whether the body existed but what happened during the secret interval between its seizure from the Confederación General del Trabajo (CGT) in November 1955 and its clandestine burial in Milan in May 1957. That interval was concealed by the Argentine state, reconstructed largely through later testimony, and subsequently overlaid by journalism, partisan memory, Rodolfo Walsh’s fiction, Tomás Eloy Martínez’s deliberately hybrid novel *Santa Evita*, and screen adaptations.

The following labels are used throughout:

- **Documented:** supported by contemporary records, official archives, photographs, medical documentation, cemetery records, or mutually corroborating testimony.
- **Retrospective testimony:** a participant’s later account, potentially accurate but not equivalent to a contemporary record.
- **Scholarly inference:** a reconstruction supported by evidence but not directly documented.
- **Institutional account:** the position of a museum, archive, government, cemetery, or custodial body.
- **Allegation/disputed:** reported but contested or not independently verified.
- **Legend or literary invention:** a story demonstrably shaped or created by fiction or later folklore.
- **Unresolved:** available evidence cannot establish the claim.

## 1. Identification and Physical Description

### Object identity

The object is the chemically preserved human corpse of **María Eva Duarte de Perón** (1919–1952), commonly called **Eva Perón** and **Evita**. It is a whole-body political and funerary relic produced through long-term anatomical preservation after her death from cervical cancer in Buenos Aires on 26 July 1952.

Relevant names include:

- **Spanish:** *el cadáver embalsamado de Eva Perón*; *el cuerpo de Evita*; *los restos mortales de Eva Perón*; *la momia de Evita*.
- **Italian burial identity:** **Maria or María Maggi, vedova de Magistris**—“Maria Maggi, widow of De Magistris.” The grave identity was false and created to conceal the corpse.
- **Official Argentine designation:** documents generally use *los restos mortales de la señora doña Eva Perón* or *María Eva Duarte de Perón*.
- **Political epithets transferred to the corpse:** *Abanderada de los Humildes*, “standard-bearer of the humble”; *Jefa Espiritual de la Nación*, “Spiritual Leader of the Nation”; and, in popular devotion, *Santa Evita*. These are titles or devotional usages, not object catalogue names.

“Embalmed” is conventional but incomplete. Pedro Ara’s procedure combined chemical fixation, immersion, dehydration, solvent replacement, and paraffin impregnation. The result is more accurately described as an anatomically preserved or paraffinized body. “Mummy” is common in journalism and fiction but can misleadingly suggest ancient desiccation or evisceration.

### Physical substance

The surviving object consists principally of Eva Perón’s human tissues and skeleton chemically fixed and impregnated with preservative agents. A technical history of long-term embalming, drawing on Ara’s published methods and case record, reconstructs the process as follows:

1. Initial arterial and local fixation with a mixture including approximately 10 percent formaldehyde, high-strength ethanol, water, and thymol.
2. Injection through a carotid artery and treatment of visceral cavities and subcutaneous tissues.
3. Immersion in a tank holding approximately 150 litres of a solution containing ethanol, glycerin, potassium salts—reported as acetate and nitrate—and thymol.
4. Progressive dehydration with increasing concentrations of ethanol.
5. Replacement of tissue alcohol with paraffinic organic solvents, including turpentine oil and gasoline.
6. Paraffin impregnation, producing translucent, wax-like, relatively stable tissues.
7. Repeated local cosmetic and preservative treatment of the face, hands, and fingers.

The technical summary appears in the peer-reviewed *Journal of Plastination*. It describes Ara’s method as an improvement on earlier European paraffinization procedures and places his work on Eva’s body between July 1952 and November 1955, including the subsequent period of maintenance at the CGT. [Technical review of Ara’s procedure](https://journal.plastination.org/articles/embalming-techniques-for-long-term-preservation-of-bodies/)

Contrary to the recurring claim that the body was “hollowed out,” the available technical description says that the organs were preserved in place. Contrary to another shorthand, the blood was not simply “replaced with glycerin” in a single operation: fixation, immersion, dehydration, solvent exchange, and paraffin impregnation were separate stages.

### Appearance

Contemporary photographs show a markedly emaciated adult woman lying supine, with closed eyes, arranged hair, and a cosmetically composed face. Ara described his aesthetic objective as an appearance of tranquil sleep rather than a conventional dried mummy. The body’s waxen or translucent surface led observers to compare it with sculpture. That comparison later encouraged stories that the corpse was itself a wax effigy.

Published dimensions and weight for the finished preparation have not been found in an authoritative medical or conservation record. Eva reportedly weighed very little at death after advanced cancer, but estimates of her terminal weight describe the living patient and cannot safely be treated as the object’s measured post-preservation weight.

### Incisions and medical alterations

The body necessarily carried marks from cancer surgery and terminal medical treatment. Ara also made access openings required by his preservation technique. A later letter by a medical colleague states that, after the 1955 coup, samples were taken from a fingertip pad and an earlobe for histological examination to demonstrate that the figure was a genuine human body rather than a wax model. [Contemporary professional recollection published in *El País*](https://elpais.com/diario/1982/09/29/opinion/402102013_850215.html)

A modern claim holds that Eva underwent a pre-death frontal lobotomy. It rests principally on interpretations of reproductions of skull radiographs said to have been taken in 1955; the original films are missing. Critics note that the apparent openings could correspond to procedures described by Ara and that the physician alleged to have performed the operation, James L. Poppen, did not record a 1952 visit in his own account. No exhumation or direct modern imaging has tested the claim. It remains **disputed and unverified**, and concerns an alleged intervention before death rather than the embalming itself. [Critical review of the lobotomy claim](https://elhistoriador.com.ar/eva-peron-y-la-version-sobre-una-lobotomia-practicada-en-1952/)

### Damage

When the body was delivered to Juan Perón in Madrid in September 1971, it showed damage attributed to handling, storage, and burial. Reports variously mention:

- compression or flattening of the nose;
- damage to an ear;
- broken or damaged fingers;
- a missing piece or entire segment of one finger;
- injuries to a foot, sometimes attributed to prolonged upright storage;
- cuts or punctures;
- superficial marks and fractures in the paraffinized surface.

The evidentiary levels differ. Ara inspected the returned body and authenticated it. The Eva Perón Museum says his report recorded blows and mutilations consistent with careless transfers. Eva’s family and conservator Domingo Tellechea believed the injuries were more extensive and interpreted some as deliberate abuse. [Museo Evita institutional account](https://museoevita.org.ar/el-derrotero-del-cuerpo-de-eva/)

Claims of rape or necrophilic assault appear in testimony, documentaries, journalism, and fiction but have not been established by a surviving public forensic report. They must be treated as **allegations**, not documented physical findings.

### Containers and associated objects

The corpse has occupied several containers:

- its state-funeral coffin or funerary bier in 1952;
- the glass-topped display arrangement used during public mourning;
- the apparatus and tank used for preservation at the CGT;
- one or more transport crates during secret military custody, one reportedly labelled as radio equipment;
- a metal or silver-coloured inner coffin with a viewing panel, enclosed within a wooden transport casing by the time of the 1971 exhumation;
- the coffin used for its return to Argentina and eventual entombment.

Descriptions of a “silver coffin” recur in 1971 reporting. The wooden outer casing at Milan was reportedly deteriorated, whereas the sealed inner coffin and body remained substantially intact. [Contemporary 1971 report](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C909988%2C00.html)

The present coffin has not been opened for publicly documented scientific inspection in recent decades. Its current internal condition is therefore unknown.

### What survives

The body is believed to survive substantially intact in Buenos Aires. No publicly accessible accession number exists because it is not a museum specimen. There is no published modern DNA test, radiocarbon study, CT examination, toxicological analysis, or recent conservation survey. Its identity rests on custody documentation, contemporary recognition, medical inspection, cemetery records, and the absence of a credible rival body.

## 2. Origin, Manufacture, and Earliest Attestation

### Biological death and commencement of preservation

Eva Perón died in Buenos Aires at 20:25 on 26 July 1952. The government announcement described her as *Jefa Espiritual de la Nación*. Her death transformed a human body into a state-managed funerary object almost immediately.

Pedro Ara Sarriá, a Spanish anatomist and specialist in preservation, was summoned to undertake long-term conservation. He had worked on anatomical preparations and had preserved the composer Manuel de Falla. Ara’s own book, *El caso Eva Perón*—later republished as *Eva Perón: la verdadera historia contada por el médico que preservó su cuerpo*—is the indispensable participant account. Its chapter sequence covers the death, the first sixteen days, the technical and administrative process, custody at the CGT, the 1955 coup, official inspections, and removal on 23–24 November 1955. [CEDINPE catalogue record for Ara’s account](https://www.cedinpe.unsam.edu.ar/content/ara-pedro-eva-per%C3%B3n-la-verdadera-historia-contada-por-el-médico-que-preservó-su-cuerpo)

Ara began temporary fixation very soon after death, before the full year-long paraffinization process. The state funeral and preservation therefore overlapped: the visible corpse shown to mourners was already being treated but was not yet the finished long-term preparation.

### Commission and purpose

Juan Domingo Perón’s government intended the body to remain visible and eventually to lie in a monumental funerary complex. Law 14,124, enacted on 4 July 1952, authorized a monument to Eva Perón. The project developed out of Eva’s earlier proposal for a Monument to the *Descamisado*—the “shirtless” worker—and was recast as her memorial during her final illness. [Official Argentine legislative record](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-14124-296468)

The planned monument was to combine colossal architecture, political iconography, a museum or commemorative space, and a crypt or funerary chamber. The preserved corpse was intended to act as a permanent visual and material focus of Peronist memory. The exact final display mechanism was never completed.

A frequently repeated fee of US$100,000 for Ara’s work appears in later accounts. The existence and precise terms of the alleged contract are not demonstrated by the open official sources consulted here. The figure should be treated as **widely reported but not independently verified in this dossier**.

### Manufacturing site

After the lying-in-state, the body was transferred to the headquarters of the Confederación General del Trabajo at Azopardo Street, Buenos Aires. A laboratory was established on the second floor. Ara continued immersion, dehydration, paraffin treatment, and finishing there for approximately a year.

The body was thus both a corpse and an intentionally manufactured political relic: the biological remains supplied the substrate; Ara’s chemical and anatomical work produced the durable object known thereafter as “the embalmed body of Evita.”

### Earliest visual and documentary attestations

The funeral was extensively photographed and filmed. An official colour documentary, *¡Y la Argentina detuvo su corazón!*, directed with the participation of cinematographer Edward Cronjager, recorded the funeral and was released on 17 October 1952. It is a primary visual source for the body’s presentation, state ceremonial, crowds, flowers, procession, and institutional staging. [Archivo Prisma record](https://www.archivorta.com.ar/asset/y-la-argentina-detuvo-su-corazon-1952/)

The Archivo Nacional de la Memoria catalogues contemporary photographs of the funeral at the CGT and of Juan Perón and officials beside the bier. [ANM photograph: CGT funeral](https://catalogo.jus.gob.ar/index.php/fotografia-del-funeral-de-maria-eva-duarte-de-peron-2) [ANM photograph: Perón at the funeral](https://catalogo.jus.gob.ar/index.php/fotografia-del-funeral-de-eva-duarte-de-peron)

## 3. Provenance and Custody History

### 26 July–August 1952: death and state funeral

After death, the body passed into combined presidential, governmental, medical, and ceremonial custody. It lay in state first at the Ministry of Labour and Welfare. It was later taken to the National Congress and then, after an enormous procession, to the CGT headquarters.

The funeral lasted approximately two weeks. Contemporary records establish a dense sequence of public viewing, Catholic rites, military honours, labour-union participation, processions, floral offerings, and mass mourning. The corpse functioned simultaneously as human remains, state body, political image, and projected relic.

The funeral images should not be confused with the finished embalming. Ara’s durable preservation continued after the public ceremonies.

### August 1952–1953: preservation laboratory at the CGT

At the CGT building the body was placed under Ara’s direct technical custody. The second floor operated as a restricted anatomical laboratory. Ara recorded administrative conflicts over access, money, responsibility, and ownership. Although the state and Peronist institutions controlled the site, Ara treated himself as professionally responsible for the physical integrity of the body.

By approximately the first anniversary of Eva’s death, the major treatment was complete. The body remained at the CGT pending construction of the monument. Public accounts sometimes say it remained “on display,” but ordinary continuous public access is not well established. It was kept in a prepared room and could be shown ceremonially or to authorized visitors.

### September–November 1955: regime change and contested custody

The September 1955 military revolt overthrew Juan Perón. The body remained in the CGT building through the change of government. Ara continued to guard and maintain it while officials of the new regime debated its identity and fate.

The problem for the self-designated *Revolución Libertadora* was political as much as sanitary or legal. Destroying the corpse risked creating martyrdom; leaving it at the CGT allowed it to remain a focus of Peronist mobilization; handing it openly to the family could enable a public shrine.

Military authorities inspected the object. The examination and reported tissue sampling were intended to settle whether it was truly Eva’s body rather than a wax figure.

### 23–24 November 1955: state seizure

On the night of 23 November 1955, Lieutenant Colonel **Carlos Eugenio Moori Koenig**, head of the Army Intelligence Service, and officers under his authority removed the corpse clandestinely from the CGT. The Museo Evita and CEDINPE describe the act as a seizure or kidnapping of the body. It was conducted without family consent and outside an ordinary transparent funerary transfer. In object-provenance terms, it was a **political confiscation and disappearance**. [CEDINPE/Diccionario del Peronismo account](https://diccionarioperonismo55-69.ar/desaparicion-del-cadaver-de-eva-peron/)

The body was first taken to Army Intelligence premises, commonly identified as Viamonte 1816, Buenos Aires. From that point until its foreign burial the chain becomes less secure.

### Late 1955–1956: the undocumented Buenos Aires itinerary

Later accounts place the corpse at some combination of:

- Army Intelligence headquarters;
- a military truck or van;
- a naval or military installation;
- private houses belonging to officers;
- the attic or premises associated with Major Eduardo Arandía;
- behind the screen or in a storeroom of a cinema;
- Moori Koenig’s office, with the coffin or crate stored upright;
- other military properties used as temporary hiding places.

The CEDINPE account says various testimonies support transfers among Army Intelligence, private officers’ homes, and military locations, but warns that the itinerary generated numerous incompatible versions and myths. Some researchers argue that much of the supposed wandering consisted of false trails created to frustrate Peronist searches. The only prudent conclusion is that the state retained the body in covert custody and moved it at least once; the complete itinerary is **unresolved**.

The often-repeated story that candles and flowers mysteriously appeared wherever the hidden body was parked belongs to legend unless a particular incident can be tied to a named contemporary witness. The claim that Moori Koenig displayed the body to visitors, including filmmaker María Luisa Bemberg, rests on retrospective testimony.

The story that Major Arandía, fearful that Peronists would seize the corpse, accidentally shot and killed his pregnant wife after mistaking her for an intruder is widely repeated. The death itself is separable from the claim that the body was physically in the house at that moment. Its place in the corpse’s exact custody chain remains uncertain.

### 1956–1957: transfer from Moori Koenig’s control

President Pedro Eugenio Aramburu removed Moori Koenig from responsibility. Colonel **Héctor Eduardo Cabanillas** and other officers were assigned to find a lasting solution. Francisco Manrique, head of the presidential Military Household, participated in discussions.

Options reportedly considered included destruction, cremation, burial at sea, or secret burial. The government ultimately chose clandestine but nominally Christian burial abroad.

### April–May 1957: Operación Traslado

The plan is generally known as **Operación Traslado**, “Operation Transfer.” Later testimony associates it with Cabanillas, Lieutenant Colonel Alejandro Agustín Lanusse, the Argentine priest Francisco “Paco” Rotger, and members of the Catholic Company of Saint Paul. Accounts differ about the precise knowledge and authorization of the Vatican and Pope Pius XII. Ecclesiastical assistance at organizational level is well supported; direct papal authorization is a **reported but insufficiently documented claim** in accessible sources.

False civil and funerary papers identified the corpse as **Maria Maggi de Magistris**, supposedly an Italian woman who had died in Argentina. The body was shipped across the Atlantic aboard the Italian liner **Conte Biancamano**, arriving at Genoa in May 1957. Italian accounts and later press reconstructions agree on the ship and Milan destination, although dates given for arrival and burial vary between 13, 14, and 17 May. [Italian reconstruction and driver testimony](https://milano.corriere.it/notizie/cronaca/23_aprile_02/la-salma-di-evita-peron-era-nascosta-al-cimitero-musocco-di-milano-io-l-ho-trasportata-a-madrid-ma-non-sapevo-fosse-lei-8629ef76-d0a2-11ed-8952-10f6bf0a23fa.shtml)

### May 1957–September 1971: Milan

The coffin was buried in Milan’s **Cimitero Maggiore**, commonly called **Musocco**, at **Campo 86, Giardino 41**—variously translated as field or lot 86, garden/grave 41.

The grave marker carried the false name Maria Maggi de Magistris. The concealment worked because the cemetery’s records and visible memorial supported the invented identity.

**Giuseppina “Pina” Airoldi**, a consecrated laywoman associated with the Company of Saint Paul, tended the grave and brought flowers. Accounts differ on whether she knew who was buried there. The Museo Evita says she believed the fabricated story; other later versions give greater knowledge to clergy, especially Father Giulio Madurini. [Museo Evita account](https://museoevita.org.ar/el-derrotero-del-cuerpo-de-eva/)

The body remained underground for approximately fourteen years. This is the longest clearly documented stable custody in its post-1955 history.

### 1970: corpse used as counter-hostage

In May 1970 the Montoneros abducted former president Aramburu. Their accusations included responsibility for Eva’s disappearance. After killing him, they announced that his body would be returned only after Eva Perón’s remains were restored “to the people.”

The communiqué is primary evidence of political action performed in Eva’s corpse’s name. [Digitized Montoneros communiqué, 15 June 1970](https://backend.educ.ar/refactor_resource/get-attachment/22807) [CeDeMA transcription](https://website.www.cedema.org/digital_items/231)

Aramburu’s abduction did not immediately recover Eva’s body, but it intensified the state’s need to settle the problem.

### September 1971: exhumation and transfer to Madrid

Under President Alejandro Agustín Lanusse, the Argentine government decided to return the body to Juan Perón, then exiled in Spain. A man using the identity **Carlos Maggi**, supposedly the dead woman’s brother, secured authorization to exhume and transport “Maria Maggi.”

The grave was opened on or about 1 September 1971. Cemetery workers found the outer wooden casing decayed but the internal coffin and corpse well preserved. A Milan funeral firm transported the coffin by road to Spain. A surviving driver later recalled that he did not know the passenger’s real identity. [Corriere della Sera interview](https://milano.corriere.it/notizie/cronaca/23_aprile_02/la-salma-di-evita-peron-era-nascosta-al-cimitero-musocco-di-milano-io-l-ho-trasportata-a-madrid-ma-non-sapevo-fosse-lei-8629ef76-d0a2-11ed-8952-10f6bf0a23fa.shtml)

On 3 September 1971, the body was delivered to Juan Perón at his residence **17 de Octubre**, in Puerta de Hierro, Madrid. Present or closely involved were Isabel Martínez de Perón, Cabanillas or government representatives, and later Pedro Ara.

Perón and Ara recognized the body. Ara’s inspection is the crucial authentication link between the corpse under his custody in 1955 and the object returned in 1971.

### 1971–1974: Puerta de Hierro

Eva’s body remained at Perón’s Madrid residence. It was not returned when Perón went back to Argentina in 1972 or when he resumed the presidency in 1973. Reports say it was kept in an upstairs room or private chapel-like setting. Access was tightly controlled.

Accounts that José López Rega performed occult rites intended to transfer Eva’s essence into Isabel Perón belong to retrospective testimony and political-occult folklore. López Rega’s presence in the household and involvement in the later repatriation are documented; the exact rites are not.

Ara died in September 1973, ending the possibility of further firsthand technical supervision by the original embalmer.

### 1974: restoration in Madrid

The body was treated by Argentine conservator **Domingo Tellechea**, associated with the restoration laboratory of the Argentine Federal Police Museum. His work reportedly repaired fingers, facial damage, and the paraffinized surface. His *Informe sobre la conservación y restauración del cadáver momificado de la señora Eva Perón* was published by the Argentine state press in 1975, but a complete open digital copy was not located.

The restoration is documented, but popular summaries often merge Tellechea’s observations with allegations of deliberate torture. Without the full report, the precise distinction between handling damage, burial deformation, sample removal, and intentional mutilation cannot be definitively reconstructed.

### 16–17 November 1974: repatriation to Argentina

After Juan Perón’s death on 1 July 1974, President María Estela Martínez de Perón ordered Eva’s return. On 16 November, an Aerolíneas Argentinas special flight left Madrid carrying the coffin, accompanied by Social Welfare Minister José López Rega and Argentine ambassador José Campano. The Argentine Foreign Ministry’s annual record documents the official Spanish send-off. [Foreign Ministry historical record](https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/userfiles/ut/memoriamrec_1974.pdf)

Isabel Perón announced the return in a national radio address. She said the remains would rest provisionally beside Juan Perón at the presidential residence in Olivos until the projected Altar de la Patria could be completed. She presented the corpse as a “pledge of peace and unity.” [Archived presidential broadcast, 16 November 1974](https://www.archivorta.com.ar/asset/cadena-nacional-maria-estela-matinez-de-peron-anuncia-la-llegada-de-los-restos-de-evita-2/)

The body arrived in Buenos Aires on 17 November and was taken from Aeroparque to Olivos amid public homage. A contemporary issue of *La Razón* catalogued by the Library of Congress of Argentina announced that Eva’s remains rested beside her husband’s. [Biblioteca del Congreso archive record](https://archivos.bcn.gob.ar/index.php/informationobject/browse?collection=459&page=174&sf_culture=pt&sort=endDate&sortDir=asc&topLod=0&view=table)

### 1974–1976: presidential residence at Olivos

Eva’s coffin was kept with Juan Perón’s in or near a chapel at the Quinta Presidencial de Olivos. The projected Altar de la Patria was never realized.

The pairing of the two coffins was politically deliberate. It reunited the foundational couple in material form under the authority of Isabel Perón’s government. It also made the bodies vulnerable to seizure amid escalating political violence.

### October 1974: theft of Aramburu’s corpse

Montoneros stole Aramburu’s body from Recoleta Cemetery and again connected its return to the repatriation of Eva. Since Eva arrived the following month, the two corpses became part of a macabre political exchange, although no simple simultaneous “swap” occurred.

### March–October 1976: military custody and transfer to the Duarte family

The military coup of 24 March 1976 placed Olivos and both presidential bodies under junta control. The authorities transferred Juan Perón to Chacarita Cemetery and delivered Eva to her sisters for private burial.

A surviving inhumation act records that at 18:00 on **22 October 1976**, with Recoleta Cemetery closed to the public, a coffin containing the embalmed remains of María Eva Duarte de Perón was brought from the presidential residence and placed in the vault registered to Julia Elisa Duarte de Arrieta:

- **Cemetery:** Recoleta
- **Section:** 16
- **Tablón:** 22
- **Graves/lots:** 4–6

The act was signed by Eva’s sisters Erminda Luján Duarte de Bertolini and Blanca Amelia Duarte de Álvarez Rodríguez and cemetery or military officials. [Study reproducing the inhumation record](https://es.scribd.com/document/731252502/Dialnet-EvaPeronDelColosoALaBovedaFamiliarEnRecole-240420-003703)

### 22 October 1976–present: Recoleta

The body has remained in the Duarte family vault. No subsequent lawful exhumation is publicly documented.

## 4. Authenticity and Identity Disputes

### Is the Recoleta body the same body preserved by Ara?

The evidence strongly supports continuity:

1. The corpse was publicly photographed and medically treated in 1952.
2. Ara retained technical custody through November 1955.
3. Military authorities verified that the body was human and identified it as Eva’s.
4. The object seized from the CGT was clandestinely exported under a documented false burial identity.
5. The Milan grave was exhumed through the same secret network that had created it.
6. Juan Perón and Pedro Ara inspected and recognized the body in Madrid.
7. Tellechea restored that body.
8. Argentine diplomatic and governmental records document its 1974 transport.
9. The 1976 inhumation act identifies the coffin arriving from Olivos as containing Eva’s embalmed remains.

There is no substantiated rival corpse and no evidence of a substitution after 1971.

### Limitations

There has been no publicly reported modern DNA comparison with maternal relatives. The coffin has not been opened for an independent modern forensic audit. Absolute biological identification by current laboratory standards is therefore absent.

Nonetheless, identity does not depend solely on laboratory evidence. The converging documentary and witnessed provenance is unusually strong from Milan onward and substantial before Milan.

### The “wax body” suspicion

Because the paraffinized corpse looked sculptural, the post-coup authorities reportedly suspected that Ara had substituted an effigy. Histological samples taken from an ear and finger helped establish that the object was actual human tissue. This was an early authenticity test, though surviving laboratory slides or a full test report have not been identified.

### Duplicate bodies

*Santa Evita* famously describes three full-scale copies in wax, vinyl, or fibreglass deployed as decoys. Tomás Eloy Martínez later acknowledged that he invented this element for the novel, although he drew on scattered rumours, including remarks attributed to Eva’s mother. In interviews, military participants reportedly denied that such replicas existed. The Washington Post’s contemporary profile explicitly records Martínez’s admission. [Martínez on the invented copies](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1996/12/24/evita-the-body-of-a-novelists-work/6b16b5a9-76a0-4aef-aad8-e5dea7ec7c06/)

Thus:

- full-scale funeral sculptures or modelling studies may have existed in connection with the monument;
- rumours of decoy corpses circulated;
- the elaborate system of three indistinguishable travelling bodies is **literary invention, not established provenance**.

### Abuse and mutilation

Three evidentiary positions should be separated:

- **Documented:** the body had physical damage by 1971 and required restoration.
- **Plausible inference:** some damage resulted from repeated movement, upright storage, sampling, pressure, and fourteen years of burial.
- **Alleged:** intentional sexual abuse, stabbing, burning, or systematic torture of the corpse.

The family and Tellechea interpreted some marks as deliberate. Ara’s more restrained assessment reportedly emphasized mishandling. No publicly available modern forensic examination resolves the disagreement.

### The Milan alias

“Maria Maggi de Magistris” was an officially supported false identity, not a rival authentic claimant. Sources disagree over how the name was chosen and whether it borrowed details from a real Italian immigrant. Symbolic etymologies—*Maria* from Eva’s first name, *Maggi* from May/Plaza de Mayo, *de Magistris* from “master”—are later speculation rather than demonstrated facts.

### Authority to authenticate

Authority moved through several regimes:

- 1952–1955: Pedro Ara’s anatomical expertise and the Peronist state.
- 1955–1957: military intelligence and government-appointed medical examiners.
- 1957–1971: secret military and ecclesiastical custodians plus Milan cemetery documentation.
- 1971: Ara’s renewed medical recognition and Juan Perón’s personal recognition.
- 1974: Tellechea’s conservation assessment and the Argentine executive.
- 1976 onward: the Duarte family, Recoleta Cemetery, and Buenos Aires municipal burial records.

## 6. Ritual, Ceremonial, and Symbolic Function

### State funeral object

The body’s first function was as the centre of an extraordinary state funeral. Its arrangement made the political leader visually present after biological death. The bier, lighting, flowers, clothing, guard rotations, military honours, labour delegations, and mass passage of mourners produced a national rite extending beyond ordinary Catholic burial.

The government’s film and photographic apparatus multiplied the body’s presence. Sandra Gayol’s scholarship interprets the funeral as helping construct a specifically Peronist “emotional community”: the suffering body, popular grief, flowers, cries, music, rain, and mass participation linked individual mourning to political identity. [Review of Gayol’s *Una pérdida eterna*](https://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?pid=S0185-26202025000100293&script=sci_arttext)

### Projected secular-political relic

Preservation was not only a sanitary treatment. It made indefinite visual access conceivable. The intended monument would have presented Eva as permanently available to “her people,” combining features of:

- a national mausoleum;
- a labour shrine;
- a museum of Peronism;
- a monumental political temple;
- a funerary crypt;
- a Catholic-adjacent cult of bodily incorruptibility.

The parallel with Lenin’s displayed body was widely recognized, but Eva’s visual language also drew upon Catholic relic traditions: intact saintly bodies, recumbent effigies, votive flowers, intercession, and pilgrimage.

### Legitimation

Possession of the corpse repeatedly served political authority:

- Perón’s government planned it as a monument to social transformation and loyalty.
- The *Revolución Libertadora* confiscated it because its continued accessibility threatened the erasure of Peronism.
- Montoneros demanded its return to “the people,” linking revolutionary legitimacy to recovery of the absent body.
- Lanusse returned it to Perón during negotiations over political normalization.
- Isabel Perón repatriated it as a symbol of national unity and placed it beside Juan Perón.
- The 1976 junta depoliticized it through private family burial, though the grave soon became a public site of memory.

### Devotional practice

The current vault attracts flowers, letters, photographs, prayers, political insignia, and tourist visits. Some visitors treat Eva as a secular or popular saint; others approach her as a political leader or historical celebrity. These practices are real regardless of whether formal canonization occurred.

Campaigns for Eva’s canonization and descriptions of her as *Santa Evita* circulated after her death. The Catholic Church has not canonized or beatified her. Her “sainthood” is popular, literary, and political rather than canonical.

### Access rules

Access has varied radically:

- 1952: mass public viewing under state supervision.
- 1952–1955: restricted laboratory and commemorative access.
- 1955–1971: state secrecy; even the name and country of burial were concealed.
- 1971–1974: private household access in Madrid.
- 1974–1976: secured presidential custody.
- Since 1976: public access to the exterior of the Duarte vault during cemetery hours; no public access to the coffin or body.

## 7. Myths and Legends Attached to the Object

### The incorruptible body

**Early basis:** Ara deliberately produced an exceptionally lifelike, long-lasting preparation. Photographs and witnesses described apparent sleep.

**Later legend:** the body resisted decay through Eva’s sanctity or will.

**Assessment:** preservation is scientifically explicable through intensive chemical treatment. The belief in incorruptibility nevertheless shaped the body’s religious-political reception.

### The body that summoned flowers

**Legend:** wherever the military hid the corpse, candles and flowers appeared, as though the people or the dead woman herself revealed its location.

**Earliest influential form:** oral and journalistic accounts later amplified by *Santa Evita*.

**Assessment:** isolated offerings may have appeared after a hiding place leaked, but a repeated supernatural pattern is unverified. The story expresses the proposition that Peronist memory could not be suppressed.

### The identical doubles

**Legend:** Ara or sculptors created three indistinguishable replicas that travelled through Argentina as decoys.

**Modern invention/elaboration:** central to Tomás Eloy Martínez’s 1995 novel.

**Assessment:** Martínez admitted inventing the replicas. They should not be inserted into the chain of custody.

### Moori Koenig’s obsession

**Story:** the intelligence chief kept the body upright in his office, displayed it, spoke to it, or developed an erotic fixation.

**Evidence:** later testimony and Walsh’s literary treatment support a historical core—that Moori Koenig possessed secret knowledge and was psychologically consumed by the assignment. Specific scenes are difficult to separate from literary construction.

Rodolfo Walsh’s “Esa mujer,” written from encounters with a colonel identifiable as Moori Koenig, never names Eva. The story transforms the hidden corpse into an absent centre of Argentine politics: everyone’s speech and desire circles an object that cannot be seen. [CEDINPE record for “Esa mujer”](https://cedinpe.unsam.edu.ar/content/walsh-rodolfo-esa-mujer)

### Sexual violation

**Allegation:** officers sexually abused the corpse.

**Sources:** later family statements, documentary testimony, journalism, and fiction.

**Evidence:** publicly described injuries do not conclusively prove sexual assault; no accessible contemporaneous forensic report establishes it. The allegation must remain explicit but unresolved.

### Eva as the first *desaparecida*

This description became powerful after Argentina’s later state disappearances. Literally, Eva was a publicly known dead person whose corpse was secretly removed and concealed, not a living detainee disappeared under the practices of 1976–1983. Analogically, however, the state seizure, denial of location, false identity, transnational burial, and family’s inability to recover the body anticipate mechanisms later used against the disappeared. The formulation is a political-historiographical analogy, not a claim that the cases are legally identical.

### The curse of the custodians

Later retellings connect the corpse with misfortune affecting Moori Koenig, Arandía, Aramburu, and others. The events invoked often have factual cores, but their arrangement as supernatural punishment is a retrospective curse narrative.

### Occult animation of Isabel Perón

**Story:** López Rega placed Isabel in contact with the corpse, combed or treated the body, and performed rites intended to transfer Eva’s charisma or soul into Isabel.

**Assessment:** the body was at Puerta de Hierro; López Rega was present and interested in esoteric practices. Specific rituals remain dependent on retrospective and hostile testimony. They are best classified as **unverified occult-political tradition**.

### Secret substitution at Recoleta

Rumours occasionally claim that the vault contains a duplicate, an empty coffin, or a later substitute. No documentary or physical evidence supports them. The 1971 authentication and 1974–1976 official chain make substitution unlikely, though modern DNA confirmation has not been performed.

## 8. Current Status and Location

The body is presently believed to lie in the **Duarte family vault**, Recoleta Cemetery, Buenos Aires:

- **Section 16**
- **Tablón 22**
- **Lots/graves 4–6**
- Registered in 1976 to **Julia Elisa Duarte de Arrieta**

The Buenos Aires municipal description identifies the vault as Art Deco, with pilasters, a pediment framing an ornamented bronze door, stylized flowers and leaves, and a brazier symbolizing eternity. It confirms that Eva’s remains were placed there in 1976. [Official Buenos Aires cemetery description](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cementerio_de_la_Recoleta) The same municipal tourism service lists Eva’s tomb among Recoleta’s most visited sites and publishes current public-access hours. [Buenos Aires City cemetery page](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/gcaba_historico/gobierno/gestion-comunal/cementerios-de-la-ciudad/cementerio-de-la-recoleta)

The vault exterior is publicly accessible during cemetery opening hours. The underground chamber, coffin, and body are not open to visitors. Stories that the coffin lies five or more metres below ground behind elaborate steel protections are widespread, but detailed engineering plans or an authoritative security survey have not been located. It is safe only to say that the burial was intentionally made secure against further theft.

### Ownership and legal regime

Human remains are not straightforward museum property or commercial chattels. Practical control is divided among:

- the Duarte family or its legal successors as holders of the vault;
- the cemetery administration and Buenos Aires municipal authorities;
- Argentine funerary, cemetery, health, heritage, and criminal law.

No active, credible repatriation dispute exists because the body is in Eva’s country and family tomb. There is no published current demand for exhumation or scientific testing.

### Present condition

Unknown. The last widely documented direct treatments occurred in 1974. The coffin was sealed before burial in 1976. Claims that the body remains “perfectly preserved” are projections from its 1971 condition, not recent observation.

## 9. Modern Reception

### Photography and film

The funeral documentary *¡Y la Argentina detuvo su corazón!* fixed the state-authorized visual grammar: the sleeping leader, the sea of flowers, the disciplined procession, and national suspension of ordinary time.

The documentary *Evita: la tumba sin paz* or *Evita: The Unquiet Grave*—directed by Tristán Bauer and written by Miguel Bonasso—assembled interviews, participant testimony, photographs, and disputed evidence of damage. It substantially shaped late twentieth-century popular understanding of the body.

### Literature

The corpse became one of the central objects of modern Argentine literature:

- Jorge Luis Borges, **“El simulacro”** in *El hacedor* (1960): a provincial counterfeit funeral, emphasizing political representation and substitution rather than the real corpse’s itinerary.
- Rodolfo Walsh, **“Esa mujer”** (written 1961–1964; published 1965): an interview narrative about the hidden body, never naming Eva.
- David Viñas, **“La señora muerta.”**
- Néstor Perlongher, **“Evita vive”** and poems on Eva’s corpse.
- Tomás Eloy Martínez, ** *Santa Evita* ** (1995): a hybrid historical novel that made the body itself the protagonist and deliberately blurred testimony, archival research, fantasy, and invention.
- Numerous later novels, plays, poems, television productions, and adaptations.

Viviana Plotnik’s *Cuerpo femenino, duelo y nación* maps the corpse’s central role across Borges, Walsh, Viñas, Perlongher, Martínez, Copi, and other writers. [CEDINPE bibliographic record](https://www.cedinpe.unsam.edu.ar/content/plotnik-viviana-cuerpo-femenino-duelo-y-naci%C3%B3n-un-estudio-sobre-eva-per%C3%B3n-com-personaje)

### *Santa Evita* and contamination of the historical record

Martínez’s novel is indispensable reception history but unsafe as an unmarked factual source. Its invented replicas, uncanny repetitions, and scenes of eroticized custodianship have migrated into journalism and online summaries. Martínez described the novel as reconstructing the marks left by the myth in Argentine consciousness and openly acknowledged fictional invention. [Interview with Martínez](https://www.penguinlibros.com/us/revista-lengua/entrevistas/tomas-eloy-martinez-santa-evita)

The novel’s greatest historiographical effect was to make uncertainty itself part of the object’s identity: the corpse became simultaneously evidence, relic, missing person, political weapon, fetish, and text.

### Political mobilization

The body has been invoked in:

- Peronist anniversaries and processions;
- labour-union commemoration;
- Montoneros propaganda;
- debates over political violence and state disappearance;
- memorial comparisons with Argentina’s *desaparecidos*;
- feminist and gendered readings of women’s bodies in national politics;
- arguments over Peronism’s religious or “theological” political language.

The grave receives flowers and political messages, particularly near 7 May, 26 July, and 17 October.

### Tourism and commerce

The Duarte vault is among Recoleta’s most visited tombs. Guided tours, books, films, postcards, photographs, and branded “Evita” tourism turn the resting place into part of Buenos Aires’s international heritage economy. Visitors can reach only the vault exterior; the physical relic itself remains inaccessible.

### Replicas and representations

Wax museums, stage productions, film props, funeral reconstructions, photographs, and television sets have produced many visual substitutes. None has an authenticated place in the corpse’s chain of custody. The most influential “copies” are conceptual: photographs of the embalmed face and literary descriptions have circulated far more widely than the hidden body.

## 10. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Core primary sources

1. **Pedro Ara, *El caso Eva Perón***: participant diary/memoir and technical-apologetic account. Essential but self-defensive.
2. **1952 funeral photographs and film**: primary evidence for presentation and ceremony.
3. **Law 14,124**: legal context for the projected monument.
4. **Military and intelligence testimony**: crucial for the 1955–1957 disappearance but generally retrospective.
5. **Milan cemetery and transport records**: establish the alias, grave, exhumation, and transfer.
6. **1971 contemporary press**: records public disclosure and return to Perón.
7. **Tellechea’s 1975 restoration report**: central for physical damage; not readily accessible online in full.
8. **Argentine Foreign Ministry and broadcast records**: document the 1974 repatriation.
9. **1976 Recoleta inhumation act**: anchors the current location.

### Major debates

#### Why preserve the body?

One interpretation emphasizes Juan Perón’s personal mourning and a practical decision made during crisis. Another emphasizes the deliberate creation of a permanent Peronist relic modelled partly on Lenin. The monument project and extended treatment demonstrate institutional intent, even if the initial decision had personal and improvised elements.

#### Was preservation religious or secular?

It was both and neither exclusively. The technical procedure came from anatomy; the projected display resembled a modern political mausoleum; the public reception drew on Catholic idioms of incorrupt bodies, martyrdom, intercession, and pilgrimage. Scholars disagree over whether “religion” is literal, metaphorical, or part of Peronism’s political theology.

#### How complete is the secret itinerary?

Popular histories give a confident sequence of vans, houses, offices, cinemas, and military bases. Archival caution requires a broken chain between Army Intelligence custody and the final export operation. Named episodes should not be accepted merely because they recur in accounts derived from the same memoir or novel.

#### Was the body deliberately tortured?

Damage is documented. Intent is disputed. Ara, the family, Tellechea, military witnesses, and later filmmakers framed the marks differently. No published modern forensic re-examination has resolved the matter.

#### Did the Church authorize the concealment?

Catholic intermediaries and the Company of Saint Paul participated. Direct authorization by Pius XII is frequently asserted but requires Vatican documentation not produced in the accessible sources reviewed here.

#### Was the body “the first disappeared”?

The analogy illuminates bodily seizure and state secrecy; it can also collapse important differences between a stolen corpse and clandestinely detained living victims. Scholarship uses the phrase as an interpretive bridge rather than a literal chronological classification.

#### How reliable is *Santa Evita*?

As evidence for the 1990s myth and for the narrative power of the corpse, it is primary and indispensable. As evidence for particular events in 1955–1957, it must be corroborated independently. Its fictional doubles are a proven example of invention becoming pseudo-history.

### Archival gaps

Important materials remain inaccessible, dispersed, lost, or unpublished:

- complete military orders for the 1955 seizure;
- an unbroken transport log through 1955–1957;
- original identity-testing radiographs and full histology documentation;
- complete Vatican or Company of Saint Paul files;
- Ara’s complete laboratory documentation and financial contract;
- the full original Tellechea conservation file with photographs;
- modern condition reports for the Recoleta burial;
- definitive records establishing which custodians knew the Milan grave’s true identity.
