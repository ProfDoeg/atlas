# The Seal/Ring of Solomon: Object Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary finding

**Object class:** legendary signet ring; divine or magical seal; late-antique relic claimant; subsequently a family of talismanic signs, amulets, rings, pentacles, manuscripts, and state emblems.

**Central conclusion:** No surviving artifact can be documented as a ring owned by the biblical Solomon, and no continuous physical chain of custody exists from Iron Age Jerusalem. The Hebrew Bible does not give Solomon a magical ring. The earliest securely dated witness relevant to the tradition is Josephus, writing in Greek in **93/94 CE**, who describes an exorcist using a ring and a Solomonic root and incantations—but does **not** say that the ring had belonged to Solomon. The first extensive object-biography appears in the composite Greek *Testament of Solomon*: God sends Solomon, through the archangel Michael, a small seal-ring set with an engraved precious stone, by which demons are stamped, bound, interrogated, and forced to build the Temple. Its surviving manuscripts are medieval and early modern; the work’s layers and date remain disputed.

By approximately **381–384 CE**, Christian pilgrims at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem were shown a physical object identified as “Solomon’s ring.” That relic was handled or contemplated during Good Friday ceremonies beside the True Cross and the horn said to have anointed Israel’s kings. Its provenance before that display is unrecorded, its material and inscription are not reliably described, and its later fate is unknown.

Rabbinic and Islamic narratives independently reshape the object. In the Babylonian Talmud, Solomon possesses a ring and chain engraved with the divine name; Ashmedai obtains and swallows the ring while overthrowing him. In Qurʾanic exegesis—not in the Qurʾan itself—the ring becomes the repository or instrument of Solomon’s sovereignty, is stolen by a demon, cast into the sea, swallowed by a fish, and recovered by Solomon. Medieval Islamicate and Western magical traditions then make “the Seal of Solomon” a reproducible diagram rather than a unique heirloom. Both the pentagram and hexagram bear that name, alongside more elaborate “seals,” divine names, planetary figures, and demon sigils.

Accordingly, “the Seal/Ring of Solomon” is not one demonstrably continuous material object. Its fullest reconstructable history comprises:

1. a hypothetical royal signet that is never documented in biblical accounts;
2. a late-antique literary ring with variable descriptions;
3. a late-fourth-century Jerusalem relic claimant, now lost;
4. rabbinic and Islamic narrative versions with incompatible custody histories;
5. multiple reproducible talismanic diagrams and manufactured objects bearing the name;
6. modern replicas, occult instruments, commercial jewelry, and political emblems.

## 1. Identification and Physical Description

### 1.1 Names

| Language | Form | Transliteration | Meaning and usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | Seal of Solomon; Ring of Solomon; Solomon’s signet | — | May mean the legendary ring, its engraved device, or any talisman derived from it. |
| Greek | δακτυλίδιον; σφραγίς; σφραγὶς Σολομῶνος | *daktylidion*; *sphragis*; *sphragis Solomōnos* | “Little ring,” “seal,” “seal of Solomon.” The *Testament* distinguishes the ring from the seal or engraving it carries, although translations often collapse them. |
| Hebrew | חוֹתָם שְׁלֹמֹה; טבעת שלמה | *ḥotam Shelomoh*; *ṭabbaʿat Shelomoh* | “Seal of Solomon”; “ring of Solomon.” These are conventional later labels, not the wording of a biblical inventory. |
| Jewish Babylonian Aramaic | גושפנקא | *gushpanka* | Signet or seal-ring in the Talmudic story; described as bearing the divine name. |
| Arabic | خَاتَم سُلَيْمَان; خَاتِم سُلَيْمَان | *khātam Sulaymān* / *khātim Sulaymān* | “Solomon’s seal/signature-ring.” The Arabic root خ ت م concerns sealing or ending; *khātam* can mean a seal or signet-ring. |
| Persian | خاتم سلیمان | *khātam-e Soleymān* | The Arabic expression in Persian construction; applied to the ring, its figure, and talismanic diagrams. |
| Latin | *sigillum Salomonis*; *annulus Salomonis* | — | “Seal of Solomon”; “ring of Solomon.” *Sigillum Salomonis* could also designate geometric ornaments or magical figures. |
| Geʿez/Amharic tradition | “Seal of Solomon” in translated royal and devotional usage | — | Connected principally with Solomonic dynastic identity, decorations, seals, and modern Ethiopian imagery. |

“Seal” is intrinsically ambiguous. It may denote:

- the complete signet-ring;
- the bezel or stone;
- the engraved device;
- the impression made by the device;
- a diagram believed to reproduce that device;
- a text or invocation functioning as a “seal”;
- a title for a talisman, pentacle, or occult manual.

### 1.2 The biblical absence

The books of Kings and Chronicles describe Solomon’s wisdom, throne, palace, Temple, vessels, wealth, and control of a large labor force. They do **not** inventory a magical ring and do not say that demons constructed the Temple. Ecclesiastes 9:7–10 and related verses later became attached to the ring in folklore, but no biblical passage identifies them as its inscription.

Thus no historically secure description—metal, dimensions, weight, stone, maker, or inscription—comes from a text contemporary with the traditional tenth-century-BCE Solomon.

### 1.3 Josephus’s ring, 93/94 CE

Josephus says Solomon composed incantations and methods for expelling demons. To demonstrate their continuing efficacy, he reports seeing a Jewish exorcist named Eleazar work before Vespasian, his sons, officers, and soldiers. Eleazar held to a possessed man’s nostrils **a ring containing beneath its seal one of the roots prescribed by Solomon**; he drew out the demon through the nostrils, recited Solomonic incantations, and commanded the spirit to overturn a vessel of water.

Physical description:

- object: a ring with a seal or bezel;
- contents: a medicinal/magical root placed under the seal;
- metal: unspecified;
- stone or figure: unspecified;
- inscription: none reported;
- ownership: Eleazar’s or otherwise unrecorded;
- connection to Solomon: its root and ritual instructions were attributed to Solomon, not necessarily the ring itself.

It is therefore inaccurate to say that Josephus explicitly exhibits “Solomon’s own ring.” His passage is the earliest securely dated evidence for a **ring used within Solomonic exorcistic technology**. [Josephus, *Antiquities* 8.45–49](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-8.html)

### 1.4 The ring in the *Testament of Solomon*

The principal Greek description reads:

> δακτυλίδιον ἔχον σφραγῖδα γλυφῆς λίθου τιμίου

Literally, a “small ring having a seal consisting of the engraving of a precious stone.” The ring is a divine gift transmitted by Michael. The precise mineral is not named in the principal recension; translations or retellings that specify emerald, sapphire, brass, iron, or a composite set of jewels generally draw upon later traditions.

In variants of the Greek manuscript tradition, the engraved device is expressly called a **pentalpha**—a five-alpha figure, normally understood as a pentagram. This detail is recension-dependent and cannot safely be projected into every form or chronological layer of the work. The critical problem is visible in McCown’s edition: manuscripts differ materially in their seal lists, figures, order, and narrative content. [Greek text and translation](https://www.greekdoc.com/DOCUMENTS/pseudepigrapha/t-solomon.html) [McCown’s critical edition](https://storage.lib.uchicago.edu/pres/2006/pres2006-0534.pdf)

Properties and operations in the narrative include:

- the ring is small enough for a boy to carry and throw;
- striking a demon’s chest with it leaves the demon subject to Solomon;
- Solomon seals demons individually;
- the ring or its seal can close a demon inside a wineskin;
- Solomon seals written commands or his testament with it;
- its authority derives from God, not merely from Solomon’s personal skill;
- demons recognize and fear its seal;
- its operation often includes a spoken divine name or command.

The text does not provide usable dimensions, weight, workshop, alloy, gem species, or a realistic manufacturing account.

### 1.5 The Jerusalem relic claimant

The pilgrimage text conventionally associated with Egeria/Silvia describes a Good Friday rite at the Anastasis/Holy Sepulchre in which a deacon held:

- the ring of Solomon; and
- the horn with which Israelite kings were said to have been anointed.

Pilgrims kissed the horn and “attended to,” looked closely at, or touched the ring, depending on the textual reconstruction and translation. No secure physical description survives. The object was already being presented as a relic, not merely as a diagram. Modern analysis interprets it as an ecclesiastically authenticated materialization of Israelite kingship and Solomonic power, rather than an artifact with a documented Iron Age provenance. [Pilgrimage text](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c5/The_library_of_the_Palestine_Pilgrims%27_Text_Society_%28IA_cu31924091167860%29.pdf) [modern ritual analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/290C5E647A96B32675263F1514FC1A78/9781009355544c5_167-199.pdf/objects_and_ritual_in_egerias_fourthcentury_pilgrimage_the_props_of_my_faith.pdf)

### 1.6 Rabbinic description

In Babylonian Talmud, *Gittin* 68a–b, Solomon gives Benaiah son of Jehoiada:

- a chain engraved with God’s name;
- a ring or signet engraved with God’s name;
- wool and skins of wine.

The ring’s metal, stone, size, and geometric form are not stated. The operative feature is the **divine name**, not a pentagram or hexagram. Ashmedai later persuades Solomon to remove the chain and surrender the ring, then swallows the ring and casts Solomon four hundred parasangs away. [Hebrew/Aramaic and English text](https://www.sefaria.org/Gittin.68a.6-68b.2)

### 1.7 Islamic descriptions

The Qurʾan never mentions the ring. It does present Solomon as commanding winds, birds, humans, and jinn by God’s permission; Qurʾan 38:34 says God tested Solomon and placed a “body” upon his throne, after which Solomon repented. Later exegetes supplied the stolen-ring narrative.

Islamic descriptions vary:

- sovereignty is “in” or authenticated by the ring;
- its bezel bears the Greatest Name of God;
- it may have descended from heaven;
- some later compilations give it four stones or jewels delivered by angels, corresponding to command over spirits, winds, animals, or elements;
- some distinguish an iron portion for rebellious spirits from a brass or gold portion for obedient jinn;
- pentagrams, hexagrams, grids, letters, divine names, or “seven seals” may be called its impression.

These are separate traditions and should not be synthesized into a single manufactured object.

### 1.8 Geometric forms

The figure now marketed as “the Seal of Solomon” is most often:

- a **hexagram**, two interlaced or superimposed triangles; or
- a **pentagram/pentalpha**, a five-pointed star drawn in one or intersecting lines.

Neither geometry is uniquely Solomonic. Both predate their association with Solomon and occur independently in many cultures as decoration, makers’ marks, mathematical figures, and religious signs.

Gershom Scholem concluded that medieval Arabic magical usage applied “Seal of Solomon” to several potent figures rather than one exclusive design. Both five- and six-pointed stars received the name. Jewish magical texts subsequently adopted related terminology, while the hexagram’s later career as the *Magen David* or Star/Shield of David became increasingly distinct. A modern claim that the historical Solomon’s bezel certainly bore a hexagram is therefore unsupported. [Scholem’s study](https://www.kabbalah.info/eng/content/view/full/3923)

### 1.9 Surviving physical instantiations

These are not the legendary original, but material witnesses to the tradition:

1. **Late-antique inscribed rings.** Ittai Gradel and Nils Arne Pedersen have assembled ten Roman-period rings or ring-stones bearing variants of a Greek apotropaic formula interpreted as “Solomon says: Watch out!” Several entered scholarship through the antiquities market. The Kaufmann example reportedly has the strongest find-history: discovery by Bedouins at Abu Mina in Egypt’s Western Desert in the nineteenth century. The authors date the group principally to the third century and connect it with a circulating Solomonic demon narrative. Market provenance and the proposed literary reconstruction require continued scrutiny; none is claimed to be Solomon’s personal ring. [Book preview and catalogue discussion](https://cdn.mohrsiebeck.com/73%2C675cf1963b46b3a913e03f63170044.pdf)

2. **Metropolitan Museum talismanic scroll, accession 1978.546.32.** An eleventh-century block-printed ink-on-paper amulet containing Qurʾanic material and a six-pointed star catalogued as the “Seal of Solomon.” It is not a ring and is not on view. [Metropolitan Museum catalogue](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/452893)

3. **British Museum AF.1022.** A gold signet-ring/amulet with a massive hoop, oval bezel enclosed by an ouroboros-like serpent, and a pentagram with letters. It is a post-antique magical object associated by curatorial comparison with figures used by John Dee, not an Iron Age royal signet. [British Museum catalogue](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_AF-1022)

4. **Science Museum Group A665506.** Bronze hexagram pendant, one point broken, 55 × 47 × 3 mm, broadly dated 1701–1920, European perhaps, from the de Mortillet/Wellcome collections and historically described as a “Seal of Solomon.” [Collection record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co102976/amuletic-pendant-in-form-of-bronze-hexagram)

5. **Burrell Collection hand-palm amulet.** A hexagram with a central dot is interpreted institutionally as the seal or ring of the prophet Sulayman and as an empowering/protective device. [Museum With No Frontiers catalogue](https://islamicart.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?id=object%3BISL%3Buk%3BMus04%3B40%3Ben)

These objects demonstrate the material career of the name, not continuity of identity.

## 2. Origin, Manufacture, and Earliest Attestation

### 2.1 Traditional Iron Age origin

**Tradition:** Solomon ruled Israel in the tenth century BCE and possessed a divinely empowered signet.

**Documentary status:** Unestablished. No excavated ring, contemporary inscription, biblical text, royal inventory, or ancient Near Eastern administrative document identifies such an object. Signet-rings were normal instruments of authority, so a historical monarch could plausibly have possessed one; that general plausibility is not evidence for this legendary ring.

The Hebrew Bible’s nearest conceptual background is broader:

- seals and signets signify identity and delegated power;
- Solomon receives wisdom from God;
- Solomon builds the Temple;
- later interpretation magnifies his knowledge of plants, animals, spirits, and hidden things.

The magical object is a post-biblical development.

### 2.2 Josephus: earliest secure textual horizon

Josephus completed *Jewish Antiquities* in 93/94 CE. He attributes anti-demonic incantations and knowledge of roots to Solomon and reports a contemporary exorcism using a ring. This establishes that by the late first century:

- Solomonic exorcistic expertise circulated;
- rings could serve as instruments in that practice;
- Solomon’s name authenticated living ritual knowledge.

It does **not** establish:

- that Solomon owned the demonstrated ring;
- that the ring bore a star or divine name;
- that Solomon’s sovereignty resided in it;
- that it was used to build the Temple.

### 2.3 The *Testament of Solomon*: first full origin story

The *Testament* is pseudepigraphic: it speaks in Solomon’s voice but was not written by the biblical king. Its literary growth is complex. Proposed dates range broadly from the early Roman period into late antiquity, with substantial agreement that the received text is composite and Christian elements are integral to at least major recensions.

The oldest surviving manuscripts are many centuries later than the proposed composition. McCown’s 1922 edition collated manuscripts in Mount Athos, Bologna, Holkham Hall, Jerusalem, London, Milan, Paris, and Vienna. D. C. Duling’s modern English translation used McCown’s eclectic reconstruction and argued that the work’s native language was Koine Greek, though some sources or names may have Semitic backgrounds. [Duling translation and introduction](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/THEOL264/James%20H.%20Charlesworth%20The%20Old%20Testament%20Pseudepigrapha%2C%20Vol.%201%20Apocalyptic%20Literature%20and%20Testaments%201983.pdf)

Its creation sequence is:

1. The demon Ornias attacks a favored Temple workman’s son, taking half his wages and vitality.
2. Solomon prays to the God of heaven and earth.
3. The Lord Sabaoth sends Michael.
4. Michael brings a small ring with an engraved precious-stone seal.
5. The angel instructs Solomon that it will subject male and female demons and enable completion of Jerusalem and the Temple.
6. Solomon gives the ring to the boy.
7. The boy throws it against Ornias’s chest while ordering him to come before Solomon.
8. Ornias is compelled to obey.
9. Solomon uses Ornias to summon Beelzeboul, then extends custody of the seal through controlled demon-to-demon summons.
10. The ring becomes the administrative instrument of a coerced supernatural labor force.

No mortal smith, mine, quarry, or lapidary is named. Manufacture is divine and unobservable.

### 2.4 A Christian relic is manufactured institutionally

By the late fourth century Jerusalem’s church custodians were displaying a ring as Solomon’s. The evidentiary seam is sharp:

- literary demon-ring traditions existed;
- a Christian pilgrimage complex possessed an object assigned that identity;
- no record connects its acquisition to an excavation, treasury inventory, royal inheritance, or Temple deposit.

Raʿanan Boustan and Michael Beshay argue that Solomonic demon sealing, Christian baptismal/exorcistic controversies, and Jerusalem relic practice converged in the third and fourth centuries. The displayed object gave physical form to a Christian typology: Solomon’s provisional authority over demons anticipated Christ’s decisive victory. [Boustan and Beshay](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/arege-2014-0008/html?lang=en)

Thus the fourth-century relic’s “manufacture” cannot be reconstructed materially, but its **relic identity** can be: ecclesiastical presentation and ritual use made an otherwise undocumented ring into “Solomon’s ring.”

### 2.5 Rabbinic origin

The Babylonian Talmud reached redaction over late antiquity, conventionally around the fifth to sixth centuries, though its traditions vary in age. *Gittin* supplies no celestial manufacture. Solomon already has a divine-name ring and chain when he sends Benaiah to capture Ashmedai. The source does not specify who engraved them.

This is not simply the *Testament* retold:

- Benaiah, not a victimized boy, first carries the implements;
- the chain and ring both bear God’s name;
- the immediate objective is obtaining the *shamir*, needed for stonework;
- Ashmedai later takes the ring and displaces Solomon;
- the detailed demon catalogue of the Greek *Testament* is absent.

### 2.6 Islamic origin traditions

The Qurʾanic Solomon/Sulayman is a prophet whose dominion is granted directly by God. Qurʾan 2:102 explicitly says Solomon did not disbelieve and associates the teaching of magic with devils. Qurʾan 27 and 34 describe his command of jinn and wind without assigning that power to jewelry. Qurʾan 38:34–40 narrates a test, repentance, and renewed grant of exceptional sovereignty—but no ring.

Exegetical narratives added the ring as an answer to several questions:

- How could a “body” occupy Solomon’s throne?
- How could a demon counterfeit royal identity?
- What visible token mediated divinely permitted sovereignty?
- How was Solomon exiled and restored?

Al-Ṭabarī (d. 923) preserves multiple reports rather than a single authoritatively harmonized account. In one, the demon obtains the ring, occupies the throne, and eventually throws it into the sea. A fish swallows it; Solomon receives or opens the fish, finds the ring, puts it on, and recovers his royal splendor and command. [Al-Ṭabarī on Qurʾan 38:34](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura38-aya34.html)

Later commentators sometimes reject or qualify the story. Ibn Kathīr labels its objectionable elements material received from the People of the Book and rejects claims compromising prophetic dignity. [Ibn Kathīr on Qurʾan 38:34](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/katheer/sura38-aya34.html)

## 3. Provenance and Custody History

Because no single continuous object is documented, the custody history must be divided by source.

### 3.1 Documentary/material chain

#### Before 93/94 CE

No documented physical ring of Solomon.

#### 93/94 CE: Eleazar’s exorcistic ring

- **Holder/operator:** Eleazar, a Jewish exorcist.
- **Place:** before Vespasian, Titus, Domitian, officers, and soldiers; Josephus does not securely identify the location in the passage.
- **Use:** a Solomonic root under the seal is placed at a demoniac’s nostrils.
- **Transfer:** none recorded.
- **Fate:** unrecorded.
- **Identity:** not said to be Solomon’s own ring.

#### Approximately 381–384 CE: Jerusalem relic

- **Keeper:** deacons or clergy of the Anastasis/Holy Sepulchre.
- **Place:** Jerusalem, within the Good Friday relic ceremony.
- **Companion objects:** True Cross, titulus/inscription, royal anointing horn.
- **Access:** closely controlled; pilgrims passed in sequence.
- **Use:** veneration, inspection, and possibly touch.
- **Prior provenance:** absent.
- **Later provenance:** absent.
- **Fate:** unknown.

The chain breaks completely after its late-antique liturgical attestation. There is no defensible basis for identifying any modern ring with the Jerusalem relic.

#### Third-century-type “Solomon says” rings

These constitute separate amulets rather than one royal relic. Their custody histories are individual:

- some were previously published;
- five additional pieces surfaced in the antiquities market;
- the Kaufmann ring was reportedly found at Abu Mina in the nineteenth century and remained in the Kaufmann family before modern study;
- another specimen or stone is associated with the Istanbul Archaeological Museums;
- the group was assembled analytically by Gradel and Pedersen, not as fragments of one object.

Their formula may preserve material reception of a Solomonic story. Their existence does not authenticate the *Testament*’s narrated ring.

### 3.2 Custody chain in the *Testament of Solomon*

1. **Lord Sabaoth/God** — ultimate maker or donor.
2. **Michael the archangel** — heavenly courier.
3. **Solomon** — primary recipient.
4. **The workman’s boy** — temporary authorized bearer; throws it against Ornias.
5. **Solomon** — receives the subdued demon and resumes custody.
6. **Ornias** — temporarily carries or is subjected through the ring while bringing Beelzeboul, depending on recension.
7. **Solomon and servants** — repeatedly deploy it to summon, stamp, seal, and confine demons.
8. **Temple/Jerusalem operations** — the ring remains an instrument in Solomon’s administration.
9. **End of narrative** — Solomon’s apostasy is narrated, but the principal text does not provide a clear, uniform disposal history for the ring.
10. **Final fate** — unrecorded.

This chain ends inside the story. It does not connect textually to the later Holy Sepulchre relic.

### 3.3 Custody chain in *Gittin* 68a–b

1. **Solomon** possesses the divine-name ring and chain.
2. **Benaiah son of Jehoiada** receives them from Solomon, with wine and wool, to capture Ashmedai.
3. **Ashmedai** is bound with the chain and brought to Jerusalem; the ring authenticates or enforces divine authority.
4. **Solomon** retains or regains custody while questioning Ashmedai.
5. **Ashmedai** requests release from the chain and asks for Solomon’s ring as the condition for demonstrating demonic strength.
6. **Solomon** voluntarily gives it to him.
7. **Ashmedai** swallows the ring, expands cosmically, and throws Solomon four hundred parasangs.
8. **Afterward:** Solomon wanders declaring himself king. The text associates his fearful later life and guarded bed with the episode.
9. **Recovery:** unlike later harmonized retellings, the Talmudic passage does not clearly narrate the familiar fish-mediated recovery of the ring. Claims that the Talmud itself contains that full recovery often import Islamic or later folklore.

### 3.4 Islamic exegetical custody chain

A common composite version runs:

1. **God/heavenly donor** grants Solomon the ring or the sovereignty it represents.
2. **Solomon** wears it; beings recognize his authority.
3. **Amīna**, a trusted concubine or servant, temporarily holds it when Solomon washes, enters the privy, or approaches a wife.
4. **Ṣakhr**, Ḥabīqīq, Āṣaf-like impostor, or another named demon assumes Solomon’s appearance.
5. **Amīna** gives the ring to the disguised demon.
6. **The demon** sits on Solomon’s throne and exercises authority for forty days in many versions.
7. **The true Solomon** is rejected and works among fishermen or wanders in poverty.
8. **The demon**, exposed or frightened by recitation of scripture and the court’s suspicions, casts the ring into the sea.
9. **A fish** swallows it.
10. **Fishermen** catch the fish.
11. **Solomon** receives it as wages, alms, or food.
12. **Solomon or a household cook** opens the fish and finds the ring.
13. **Solomon** puts it on; his radiance, recognition, and dominion return.
14. **The demon** is captured, sealed into stone, iron, lead, or a chest, and cast into the sea.
15. **At Solomon’s death:** sources diverge. Some leave the ring with him or omit its fate; the *Arabian Nights* “Bulukiya” cycle puts it on Solomon’s finger in his remote tomb, creating a treasure-quest custody extension.

Variants differ on the demon’s name, whether he could wear the ring, whether he had access to Solomon’s wives, the cause of Solomon’s punishment, the number of fish, and the method of the demon’s final confinement. Al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī preserve extensive forms. [Al-Baghawī](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/baghawy/sura38-aya34.html) [Al-Qurṭubī](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/qortobi/sura38-aya34.html)

### 3.5 *Arabian Nights* custody extensions

#### The Fisherman and the Jinni

Solomon’s ring itself does not enter the fisherman’s hands. Its **impression** survives on lead stoppers sealing brass vessels:

1. Solomon or his agents imprison a rebellious jinni in a copper/brass vessel.
2. Lead closes the mouth.
3. Solomon stamps the closure with his signet.
4. The vessel is cast into the sea.
5. Centuries later a fisherman nets it.
6. Removing the seal releases the jinni.
7. The fisherman later tricks the jinni back inside; the seal’s authority again prevents escape.

The tale turns the unique ring into a technology capable of producing many imprisoning impressions. [Primary tale](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Book_of_the_Thousand_Nights_and_One_Night/The_Fisherman_and_the_Genie)

#### The City of Brass

The expedition recalls traditions that Solomon imprisoned jinn in brass bottles, poured lead over them, and sealed them with his signet. The ring is absent; its replicated impressions populate a landscape of archaeological wonder. [Primary tale](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Arabian_Nights_%281909%29/The_Story_of_the_City_of_Brass)

#### Bulukiya

This tradition relocates the ring after Solomon’s death:

1. Solomon is placed in a coffin or tomb beyond seven seas.
2. The ring remains on his finger.
3. ʿAffān plans to retrieve it and gain command over creation.
4. Bulukiya assists in the journey.
5. The attempted theft fails; the ring remains with the enthroned or entombed Solomon.

This is a late literary custody claim, not evidence for an actual burial. [“Adventures of Beloukiya”](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Book_of_the_Thousand_Nights_and_One_Night/The_Adventures_of_Beloukiya)

### 3.6 Ethiopian dynastic variant

A modern-recorded Ethiopian tradition says Solomon gave the Queen of Sheba a ring engraved with a lion, intending it for their son. It then passed through rulers to the modern Solomonic dynasty. This appears to be a distinct legitimating ring tradition, not clearly identical to the demon-binding ring. No verified chain of individual artifacts runs from antiquity to Haile Selassie. A twentieth-century stone seal stamp in a teaching collection illustrates how that legendary ring became dynastic iconography. [Kenyon College study](https://vrc.kenyon.edu/blog/medieval-aksum-and-the-queen-of-sheba-the-iconography-of-a-twentieth-century-ethiopian-seal-stamp/)

## 4. Authenticity and Identity Disputes

### 4.1 Did a historical original exist?

**For plausibility:**

- ancient monarchs used seals and signets;
- royal authentication required portable instruments;
- biblical literature is familiar with signet imagery;
- Solomon may represent a historical ruler or royal memory.

**Against identification:**

- no contemporary source mentions this ring;
- no candidate has an excavated inscription naming Solomon with secure tenth-century-BCE context;
- the supernatural properties appear in much later literature;
- surviving descriptions contradict one another;
- the object’s most distinctive features correspond to late-antique exorcism and medieval talismanic systems.

**Finding:** A historical ruler could have owned an ordinary signet, but the legendary object cannot be established as a surviving or even historically described artifact.

### 4.2 Is Josephus describing the legendary ring?

Older summaries often say yes. Strict reading says no: Josephus describes a ring used by Eleazar, containing a Solomonic root. He attributes the exorcistic science to Solomon but never states that Solomon manufactured, wore, or transmitted this particular ring.

The passage is best treated as a precursor: it links Solomon, exorcism, root lore, incantation, and a ring without yet supplying the later custody identity.

### 4.3 Date and religious setting of the *Testament*

Competing positions include:

- an early Jewish core subsequently Christianized;
- a Hellenistic Jewish or Jewish-Christian composition;
- a predominantly Christian work of the third or fourth century;
- a layered dossier incorporating older demonological materials.

Boustan and Beshay emphasize third- and fourth-century Christian innovation, baptismal-exorcistic debate, and Jerusalem relic culture. Gradel and Pedersen argue that third-century inscribed rings support an earlier circulating narrative and suggest at least a second-century origin for a proto-*Testament*; reviewers have cautioned that the leap from ring formulae to one reconstructed Alexandrian literary community remains hypothetical. [Critical review](https://www.apocryphicity.ca/2026/08/04/book-review-the-lost-novel-of-king-solomon-and-the-demons/)

No surviving autograph exists. The manuscript tradition’s lateness and fluidity prevent reconstruction of a single pristine “original description.”

### 4.4 Was the late-antique Jerusalem relic genuine?

No evidence supports continuity from the tenth century BCE. The ring was institutionally authenticated through its location, clerical custody, and liturgical use. Modern historians can establish that pilgrims encountered an object **presented as** Solomon’s ring; they cannot authenticate it as Solomon’s.

No published metallurgical, epigraphic, radiocarbon, or provenance analysis is known. Nor can such testing now be undertaken unless the object is rediscovered and securely identified.

### 4.5 Pentagram versus hexagram

No source supports the proposition that one of these was universally and originally Solomon’s device.

- A pentalpha appears in some *Testament* witnesses.
- Medieval Islamicate tradition uses both pentagrams and hexagrams.
- The “seven seals” and more complex figures could also bear the name.
- Western grimoires multiply planetary pentacles rather than preserve one unique bezel.
- Modern jewelry often selects whichever star its maker or market recognizes.

Any seller claiming to reproduce “the exact original Seal of Solomon” is making an unverifiable claim unless the product is explicitly a replica of a named manuscript illustration.

### 4.6 The ring and the Star of David

The hexagram long existed as a general geometric ornament. Its designation as the Seal of Solomon and its later designation as the Shield/Star of David have overlapping medieval histories, but they are not evidence of an unbroken Solomonic royal emblem. The hexagram’s adoption as a broad Jewish communal and eventually Zionist symbol belongs largely to a later historical trajectory.

### 4.7 Physical claimants

No museum presently catalogs an object as the authenticated biblical Ring of Solomon. Objects may be catalogued as:

- “Seal of Solomon” amulets;
- rings invoking Solomon;
- star-shaped pendants;
- Solomonic pentacles;
- magical rings previously attributed to ancient or medieval periods;
- tourist or commercial replicas.

Their labels describe iconography or attributed function, not direct identity with the legendary original.

### 4.8 Alleged discoveries and online claims

Periodic reports identify newly found rings in Turkey, Jerusalem, or private collections as “Solomon’s ring.” Unless supported by secure excavation, contextual dating, legible contemporary inscription, and peer-reviewed analysis, such claims remain publicity, misidentification, or modern folklore. An ancient ring found in Jerusalem is not thereby Solomon’s, and a hexagram alone cannot establish Jewish, Solomonic, or magical identity.

## 6. Ritual, Ceremonial, and Symbolic Function

### 6.1 Royal authentication

The ordinary signet’s function—making a legally or administratively valid impression—underlies every supernatural development. In the legends, creation recognizes the divine “signature” rather than merely Solomon’s person. This explains why:

- demons obey a child carrying the ring;
- an impostor wearing it can occupy the throne;
- loss of it produces political nonrecognition;
- recovery restores kingship instantaneously;
- impressions on vessel stoppers remain effective after Solomon’s death.

### 6.2 Exorcism

Josephus provides the earliest concrete procedure:

1. place a prescribed root beneath the ring’s seal;
2. hold it at the possessed person’s nostrils;
3. draw the demon out through the nostrils;
4. recite Solomonic incantations;
5. order the demon never to return;
6. require a physical demonstration, such as overturning water.

This is described as an actual contemporary performance, although its supernatural explanation is Josephus’s interpretation.

### 6.3 Demon capture and forced labor

The *Testament* gives a different procedure:

1. strike or stamp the demon with the ring;
2. invoke God’s name and Solomon’s summons;
3. compel the demon to identify itself, its activities, and the angel who frustrates it;
4. seal or bind it;
5. assign it labor in the Temple project.

The ring functions simultaneously as warrant, weapon, interrogation device, chain of command, and labor seal.

### 6.4 Building the Temple

In the *Testament*, demons quarry, transport, weave, burn, lift, and otherwise labor under compulsion. In *Gittin*, the ring and chain capture Ashmedai so that Solomon may learn the location of the *shamir*. These are related explanations of how sacred stone could be worked without forbidden iron noise, but their mechanisms differ.

### 6.5 Late-antique Christian veneration

At the Holy Sepulchre, the ring appears in a carefully staged relic sequence. Its presence beside the anointing horn and the Cross made it a bridge among:

- Israelite kingship;
- Solomon’s Temple;
- Christian episcopal and imperial authority;
- Christ’s power over demons;
- Jerusalem as the physical center of salvation history.

The clergy’s control of presentation and touch also transferred authority to authenticate the ring from legendary monarch to church institution.

### 6.6 Islamic sovereignty

In exegetical legend, the ring functions as the visible form of *mulk*, divinely granted dominion. Its theft allows a demon to imitate political authority; its return makes the true sovereign recognizable. The story also limits the object’s autonomy: Solomon’s power remains ultimately God’s gift and can be withdrawn during a test.

### 6.7 Amuletic protection

In Islamicate material culture, the “Seal of Solomon” appears on:

- paper scrolls;
- metal pendants;
- bowls;
- architectural surfaces;
- weapons and armor;
- talismanic shirts and caps;
- seals and administrative stamps;
- manuscript diagrams.

Its functions include warding off jinn, evil eye, illness, thieves, dangerous travel, childbirth hazards, and hostile magic. Actual use is evidenced by wear, folding, suspension holes, placement on garments, and museum provenance, although the efficacy attributed to it is a religious or magical belief.

### 6.8 Western ritual magic

The *Clavicula Salomonis* or *Key of Solomon* is not the ancient king’s ring and is not identical with the *Testament*. It is a fluid family of medieval and early-modern magical manuals. The tradition directs practitioners to manufacture and consecrate:

- circles;
- knives, swords, wands, and staffs;
- rings;
- parchment pentacles;
- planetary seals;
- divine-name inscriptions.

The Latin text appears to have circulated by the later thirteenth century; Pietro d’Abano refers to the title around 1310. The earliest surviving substantial medieval witness commonly cited is an Italian manuscript dated 1446, BnF MS ital. 1524. A recent study of a newly identified Hebrew *Clavicula* manuscript dates it paleographically and by watermark to the sixteenth century, probably on Ferrara paper, undermining simple claims that all Hebrew witnesses are late translations or that the extant Latin family descends straightforwardly from a biblical Hebrew original. [Recent Hebrew-manuscript study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/upon-the-magicians-escritoire-a-recently-discovered-hebrew-clavicula-salomonis/BE07843BA2B2F3415B75BAC2F3B80F90)

The late-seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* or *Lesser Key of Solomon* multiplies demon seals. Each spirit receives a graphic sigil, while the operator uses additional protective pentacles. Modern popular culture often calls any of these “the Seal of Solomon,” erasing their manuscript-specific identities.

### 6.9 State symbolism

Morocco’s 1915 flag decree added a green five-pointed interlaced figure to the red field. The decree’s explanatory tradition calls it the “Seal of Solomon,” while its political purpose was to distinguish the Moroccan flag from other plain red flags. This is a documented modern state appropriation of the symbol, not evidence for the ring’s physical survival. [Historical discussion and decree text](https://abdelazizbenabdallah.org/oeuvres/docs/Oeuvres_Millenaire_Flotte_Marocaine.pdf)

The Ethiopian imperial Order of Solomon’s Seal, founded in the nineteenth century and developed under the Solomonic monarchy, invoked the dynasty’s claimed descent from Solomon and Makeda. Again, this is symbolic succession, not custody of the legendary ring.

## 7. Myths and Legends Attached to the Object

### 7.1 The demon-master’s ring

**Earliest full attestation:** *Testament of Solomon*, in a layered late-antique Greek textual tradition.

**Story:** A Temple worker’s boy is being drained by Ornias. Solomon prays; Michael delivers the ring. The boy hurls it at Ornias, who is dragged before Solomon. Through Ornias, Solomon summons Beelzeboul and then a taxonomy of demons. He questions their names, celestial associations, diseases, and thwarting angels, stamps or seals them, and uses their labor to complete the Temple.

**Historical function:** The narrative converts demon lore into a hierarchy subordinate to the biblical God. It also presents exorcistic knowledge as a catalogue authorized by the paradigmatic wise king.

**Factual core:** Late-antique use of Solomon’s name in exorcism and amulets is documented. Supernatural capture and Temple construction are matters of tradition, not archaeologically verified events.

### 7.2 Solomon and Ashmedai

**Source:** Babylonian Talmud, *Gittin* 68a–b.

**Story:** Solomon wants the *shamir* for Temple construction. He sends Benaiah with a divine-name chain and ring. Benaiah traps Ashmedai by replacing his water with wine, binds him, and brings him to Jerusalem. Later Ashmedai asks Solomon to remove the chain and give him the ring. Once free, he swallows it, expands between earth and heaven, and hurls Solomon far away. Solomon wanders, declaring his kingship, while others disbelieve him.

**Variants:** Later retellings add a sea-cast ring, fish, recovery, and restored throne. Those details should not automatically be attributed to the Talmudic text.

**Interest served:** The story dramatizes the fragility of worldly kingship, the danger of curiosity and pride, and the superiority of the divine name over both ruler and demon.

### 7.3 The stolen ring, false king, and fish

**Early Islamic framework:** Qurʾan 38:34’s enigmatic body on Solomon’s throne.

**Exegetical elaboration:** Solomon entrusts the ring to Amīna. A demon takes Solomon’s form and receives it. Solomon loses his radiance and recognition; the demon rules for forty days. Scripture or wise courtiers reveal the imposture. The demon casts the ring into the sea; a fish swallows it; Solomon receives the fish and retrieves the ring. His dominion returns, and he imprisons the demon.

**Variants:**

- demon named Ṣakhr, Ḥabīqīq, or otherwise;
- idol worship by a wife causes the trial;
- Solomon’s own lapse or unfulfilled oath causes it;
- demon does or does not approach Solomon’s wives;
- one fish or two;
- the ring is wearable or unbearable to the demon;
- the demon is confined in stone, iron box, lead, or a sealed vessel.

**Reception and criticism:** Muslim exegetes transmitted the material but did not uniformly endorse it. Ibn Kathīr treats morally compromising details as unreliable Israelite lore. Later rational or theological commentators reject the possibility that a demon could exercise prophetic authority.

### 7.4 Bottled jinn

**Attestation:** medieval Arabic narrative tradition, prominently the *Thousand and One Nights*.

**Story:** Solomon imprisons rebellious jinn in brass or copper vessels, seals them with lead bearing his signet impression, and casts them into seas. The seal remains active centuries later.

**Object transformation:** The ring itself becomes absent but productive. A potentially unlimited series of authentic seal impressions can outlive it.

**Cultural effect:** This story is the principal ancestor of the popular “genie in a bottle” motif and of modern depictions in which Solomon’s seal is a universal anti-jinn lock.

### 7.5 Solomon’s tomb beyond the seven seas

**Source:** Bulukiya cycle.

**Story:** Solomon’s embalmed or miraculously preserved body remains seated or interred with the ring on his finger. The ring would grant universal command, but supernatural geography and guardians prevent its theft.

**Tier:** Literary treasure legend. No archaeological evidence identifies such a tomb.

**Function:** The inaccessible burial preserves the ring from the closure problem that affects other traditions: it still exists, but cannot be reached.

### 7.6 Four jewels and four realms

Later Arabic writers describe four angels giving Solomon four jewels, subsequently united in one ring. These govern winds, animals, earth or water, good jinn, and rebellious spirits in varying arrangements. Some versions assign different metals to commands addressed to good and evil spirits.

**Tier:** Medieval elaboration. It is absent from Josephus, the Hebrew Bible, and the principal early *Testament* account.

### 7.7 “This too shall pass”

A widespread modern story says Solomon asked for a sentence that would sober him in happiness and console him in sorrow; a ring was inscribed “This too shall pass.” Versions also connect it to Persian Sufi lore.

No ancient biblical or late-antique source cited above gives the ring that inscription. Its attachment to Solomon is a later moral tale and should not be used to reconstruct the legendary demon-seal’s bezel.

### 7.8 The exact hexagram

Modern art and commerce often portray the ring as gold with a large hexagram. That standardized appearance is a late convention. Some Greek textual witnesses favor a pentalpha; rabbinic tradition specifies the divine name; Islamic diagrams vary; Western grimoires use many pentacles. The supposed exact hexagram is therefore a modern simplification of multiple traditions.

## 8. Current Status and Location

### 8.1 The original

**Status:** Legendary/unverified; no authenticated surviving object.

**Location:** Unknown because historical physical existence is unestablished.

**Legal owner or custodian:** None demonstrable.

**Scientific examination:** None possible without a securely identified candidate.

### 8.2 The Holy Sepulchre relic

**Last securely reconstructable location:** Church of the Holy Sepulchre/Anastasis, Jerusalem, late fourth century.

**Present status:** Lost or unidentified.

**Possible fates:** removal, destruction, concealment, relabeling, theft, or incorporation into another treasury are all conceivable; none is established. It should not be conflated with ordinary Jerusalem pilgrim rings or unrelated archaeological finds.

### 8.3 Late-antique rings invoking Solomon

The newly assembled corpus is dispersed among museums, private collections, and market-derived holdings. At least one stone is associated with the Istanbul Archaeological Museums; the Kaufmann example retains a nineteenth-century Abu Mina find tradition. These are authentic or proposed Roman-period talismans invoking Solomon, not the king’s legendary signet.

### 8.4 Museum surrogates

Examples presently catalogued include:

- Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York, **1978.546.32**, eleventh-century talismanic scroll; not on view.
- British Museum, London, **AF.1022**, gold magical signet-ring/amulet; collection object, not Solomon’s own ring.
- Science Museum Group, **A665506**, bronze hexagram pendant; one point broken.
- Burrell Collection, Glasgow, hand-palm amulet incorporating a dotted six-pointed seal.

These institutions authenticate date, material, provenance, or catalog identity of the **surrogates**, not the legendary prototype.

### 8.5 Access and living use

The symbol remains openly reproduced in jewelry and paper talismans, while particular ritual objects may be privately consecrated or restricted. No central Jewish, Christian, or Islamic authority controls the name or design. Commercial products vary widely and ordinarily lack scholarly provenance.

### 8.6 Current disputes

Active questions include:

- whether the “Solomon says: Watch out!” rings prove a second-century proto-*Testament*;
- whether their inscriptions belong to one workshop, region, or religious community;
- the balance of Jewish and Christian formation in the *Testament*;
- whether “Seal of Solomon” first became a stable geometric label in Arabic or Jewish magical discourse;
- how to catalogue pentagrams and hexagrams without imposing later religious identities;
- how museums should describe amulets acquired through colonial or antiquities-market networks;
- whether modern national or commercial usage inaccurately presents a specific star as the one original seal.

There is no serious museum-level authenticity contest between rival “original Rings of Solomon,” because no claimant possesses a supportable Iron Age provenance.

## 9. Modern Reception

### 9.1 Printed occultism

S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s 1889 *Key of Solomon the King* selected and normalized manuscript material for English readers. Its printed pentacles became far more visually stable than their manuscript ancestors. The effect was paradoxical: a highly variable textual family came to look like a standardized ancient system.

The 1904 Mathers–Crowley *Goetia* and later editions of the *Lesser Key* likewise popularized:

- seventy-two spirit seals;
- a “secret seal of Solomon”;
- protective circles and hexagrams;
- purpose-built rings and lamens.

Modern occult shops frequently merge these unrelated diagrams under “Solomon’s seal.”

### 9.2 Ceremonial magic

Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, Thelemic, neo-grimoire, and independent ritual-magic practitioners use Solomonic rings, pentacles, and seals. Their designs are reconstructed from particular early-modern manuscripts or devised through modern synthesis. Consecration materials may include gold, silver, brass, iron, parchment, wax, or engraved gemstones.

These are real ritual objects with individual makers and custody histories. They are not copies of an archaeologically known original.

### 9.3 State and dynastic imagery

- **Morocco:** the green pentagram on the national flag is regularly identified as the Seal of Solomon. Its state adoption dates to the 1915 decree, not antiquity.
- **Ethiopian monarchy:** the Order of Solomon’s Seal and imperial heraldry invoked claimed Solomonic descent.
- **Jewish symbolism:** the hexagram’s development as the Star/Shield of David partially overlaps with the medieval Seal of Solomon but acquired a distinct communal, political, and memorial history.

### 9.4 Literature

Important examples include:

- *One Thousand and One Nights*: sealed jinn vessels and the Bulukiya treasure quest;
- Rudyard Kipling, “The Butterfly That Stamped” (1902), using Solomon’s ring as part of comic imperial power;
- Jonathan Stroud, *The Ring of Solomon* (2010), a *Bartimaeus* prequel that turns the ring into an overwhelmingly powerful magical weapon;
- modern fantasy, comics, manga, games, and role-playing systems, which often standardize the seal as a hexagram or a demon-summoning sigil.

### 9.5 Film, television, comics, and games

Modern media commonly combine four historically separate elements:

1. the *Testament*’s demon-binding ring;
2. the Talmudic theft by Ashmedai;
3. the Islamic fish recovery;
4. early-modern Goetic sigils.

This composite is a modern reception object rather than any one traditional version.

### 9.6 Tourism and commerce

Rings sold near Jerusalem, in Turkey, North Africa, online occult markets, and museum-style gift venues may be advertised as:

- Ring of Solomon;
- Seal of Solomon;
- ring of wisdom;
- jinn-control ring;
- protection against evil eye;
- “authentic Kabbalah” ring;
- Qurʾanic or Greatest-Name ring.

Frequent warning signs of modern manufacture include machine engraving, mixed pseudo-Hebrew and pseudo-Arabic, symbols copied from nineteenth-century printed grimoires, fabricated antiquity claims, and inscriptions that cannot produce a correct seal impression because they were not mirror-cut.

### 9.7 Modern hoaxes and pseudoarchaeology

Common unsupported assertions include:

- the ring has been found beneath Hagia Sophia;
- it is hidden in Topkapı Palace;
- it was recovered in Jerusalem;
- Freemasons, Templars, or intelligence services possess it;
- one online commercial design exactly reproduces Solomon’s bezel;
- a generic ancient hexagram proves Solomonic ownership.

No cited museum accession, excavation report, or scientific publication verifies these claims.

## 10. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### 10.1 From collection to textual criticism

F. C. Conybeare’s 1898 publication made a Greek witness and English translation of the *Testament* widely available. It remained influential but represented a limited textual base. McCown’s 1922 edition substantially enlarged the manuscript evidence and demonstrated that the work exists in recensions rather than as one uniform text. [Conybeare edition](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ATestament_of_Solomon.djvu) [Open Library record for McCown](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL6652835M/The_Testament_of_Solomon)

Duling’s 1983 translation and introduction stressed:

- Koine Greek composition;
- textual layering;
- Jewish, Christian, Egyptian, astrological, and medical-demonological elements;
- uncertainty about date and provenance.

Recent editions and studies continue to reassess individual recensions rather than treating Conybeare’s translation as “the original.”

### 10.2 Solomon’s transformation into a magician

Pablo Torijano’s *Solomon the Esoteric King* studies the transition from biblical wise king to astrologer, exorcist, alchemist, and magician. The ring is one instrument within a larger pseudepigraphic economy: attributing knowledge to Solomon authorizes practices involving plants, spirits, astrology, and hidden divine names. [Torijano](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004494497/front-1.pdf)

### 10.3 Christian innovation thesis

Boustan and Beshay argue that the fully articulated demon-sealing ring belongs especially to third- and fourth-century Christian contexts. They emphasize:

- controversies over whether Solomon truly neutralized demons;
- baptism as a superior Christian sealing;
- Jerusalem’s creation of tangible Solomonic relics;
- the ring’s role in aligning biblical kingship with Christian imperial and episcopal power.

This challenges older presentations of the received *Testament* as a straightforward survival of early Jewish magic.

### 10.4 The new ring corpus

Gradel and Pedersen’s publication of ten “Solomon says: Watch out!” rings proposes a material anchor for an early narrative resembling the *Testament*. Strengths include close study of formulae, gems, letter forms, and comparative material. Limitations include:

- several objects lack controlled archaeological contexts;
- market appearance complicates provenance;
- the formula does not reproduce the whole extant text;
- dating rings does not automatically date every literary layer;
- localization to Alexandria is an inference rather than a demonstrated workshop archive.

The corpus is nevertheless important because it supplies genuine physical objects associated with Solomon’s apotropaic authority in Roman antiquity.

### 10.5 The Seal of Solomon and Magen David

Scholem’s work remains foundational in separating:

- ancient geometric occurrences of six-pointed stars;
- medieval magical designation as Solomon’s seal;
- the later Jewish communal *Magen David*;
- modern national symbolism.

The discredited popular theory is that every ancient hexagram is Jewish or Solomonic. Context, inscription, findspot, and function must be established independently.

### 10.6 Grimoire transmission

The *Clavicula Salomonis* is not a fixed book copied unchanged from Hebrew antiquity. It is a multilingual and unstable manuscript family.

Evidence includes:

- a probable Latin tradition by the later thirteenth century;
- references around 1310;
- recorded but lost courtly copies;
- the 1446 Italian witness;
- later Latin, Italian, French, English, German, Dutch, Czech, Greek, and Hebrew witnesses;
- inquisitorial confiscation and destruction;
- early-modern scribal recombination;
- nineteenth-century printed normalization.

The British Library preserves relevant manuscripts, including **Sloane MS 3648**, catalogued as containing a French *Lesser Key of Solomon* dated 1572, though titles and internal datings in magical manuscripts require critical handling. [British Library catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002116038)

The Hebrew *Mafteaḥ Shelomoh*, British Library Or 6360, is digitally available in full images. Its existence does not prove that the Western *Clavicula* originated as a biblical Hebrew composition. [OPenn manuscript record](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0047/html/or_6360.html)

### 10.7 State of primary evidence

Surviving evidence consists of:

- Josephus manuscripts and critical editions;
- late-medieval and early-modern *Testament* manuscripts;
- rabbinic manuscripts and printed witnesses;
- Qurʾanic commentaries transmitting multiple reports;
- pilgrimage texts preserved in later manuscripts;
- archaeological and market-derived amulets;
- manuscript grimoires and later printed editions;
- museum catalogues;
- flags, decrees, orders, and other official modern emblems.

Lost or missing evidence includes:

- any contemporary Solomonic royal inventory;
- the late-antique Jerusalem relic;
- the autographs of all major legendary narratives;
- many condemned or destroyed magical manuscripts;
- secure excavation records for several inscribed rings.
