# Ahriman-associated legendary object(s): Object Dossier

**Suggested filename:** `ahrimans_instrument_object_dossier.md`

## Scope and finding

The closest thing in surviving Iranian religious literature to a discrete legendary object personally associated with Ahriman is an unnamed **instrument or weapon transmitted to him by Zurwān (Time)**. Older scholarship commonly reconstructed it as **Ahriman’s black, ash-colored garment or skin**, containing or embodying **Āz**, the demon of insatiable appetite or concupiscence. A detailed philological re-examination by Salman Aliyari Babolghani instead argues that the difficult Middle Persian passages describe **a toad-shaped primordial form of Ahriman’s counter-creation**, not an article of clothing.

This dossier therefore tracks one unstable textual object under a descriptive title:

> **Zurwān’s instrument for Ahriman / the black-and-ashen garment / the toad-form of Ahriman’s creation**

These are not demonstrably three separate objects. They are competing readings and theological interpretations of the same corruptly transmitted passages in the *Wizīdagīhā ī Zādspram* 1.29 and 34.35, compared with *Bundahišn* 1.46 and *Dēnkard* III.192.

No archaeological artifact, cult implement, museum object, relic, or historically documented weapon has a defensible claim to this identity. Its “life” consists entirely of composition, manuscript transmission, translation, reinterpretation, and modern occult appropriation.

## 1. Identification and Physical Description

### 1.1 Object class

**Mythic class:** cosmogonic instrument, weapon, embodied form, or primordial matter.

**Physical status:** purely legendary or theological. It has no established material existence outside texts.

**Principal identification:** an *abzār*, “instrument, equipment, means, implement,” carried or brought forth by Zurwān to Ahreman immediately before the assault on Ohrmazd’s material creation.

**Alternative identifications:**

1. A black and ash-colored garment or vestment.
2. A skin or bodily envelope covering Ahriman.
3. A treaty-like instrument or pledge imposed on Ahriman.
4. A weapon containing the substance or power of Āz.
5. A toad-shaped primordial form from which Ahriman’s counter-creation derives or receives motion.

The first four interpretations descend chiefly from twentieth-century readings associated with R. C. Zaehner. The fifth is Salman Aliyari Babolghani’s 2014 philological reconstruction.

### 1.2 Names and terminology

The object has no proper name in any surviving source. “Ahriman’s garment,” “weapon of Āz,” and similar expressions are modern descriptive labels.

Relevant Middle Persian terms include:

- **Ahreman**: Middle Persian form of Avestan **Aŋra Mainiiu**, conventionally “Destructive Spirit” or “Hostile Spirit.”
- **Zurwān**: “Time,” here the cosmogonic agent who carries the instrument forth.
- **abzār**: instrument, equipment, means, apparatus; context does not require a handheld weapon.
- **zay / zēn**: weapon or means of attack.
- **kirb**: form, shape, bodily form, appearance.
- **paymōg / patmōg**: garment, dress, covering; the reading in *Zādspram* 34.35 is disputed and involves emendation.
- **pōst**: skin, hide, outer covering.
- **pašt**: treaty or compact, proposed by Zaehner as an alternative reading.
- **tārīgīh**: darkness.
- **syā**: black.
- **ādurestarēn / āturastar-gōn**: ash-colored, ashy, or resembling fire-ash.
- **wazaγ**: toad or frog; in the proposed expression **wazaγ kirb**, “toad-form.”
- **xrafstar**: a harmful or noxious creature.
- **Āz**: greed, voracity, appetite, concupiscence; also a personified demon. Translating it simply as sexual “lust” is too narrow.

### 1.3 Description in *Wizīdagīhā ī Zādspram* 1.29

In the current critical transcription, the relevant sentence begins:

> *u-š frazām-nigerīhā abzār-ē ī az xwad gōhr ī tārīgīh … frāz ō Ahreman burd*

The secure elements say that Zurwān, looking toward the end, brought or carried forth to Ahreman an **instrument derived from Ahreman’s own substance, darkness**, to which the power of Zurwān was joined.

The remaining descriptors are textually difficult. Earlier editors read them as something like an envelope or skin, lowly or coal-like, black and ash-colored. Aliyari Babolghani proposes that they characterize the thing as noxious-creature-like, toad-like, gloomy or rough, black, and ashy.

The passage supplies:

- no dimensions;
- no weight;
- no separate handle, blade, haft, clasp, or woven structure;
- no inscription;
- no maker’s mark;
- no statement that Ahriman wears it;
- no surviving visual representation.

Consequently, visualizations of a black robe, cloak, armor, or staff go beyond the securely readable text.

### 1.4 Description in *Wizīdagīhā ī Zādspram* 34.35

The object reappears in the eschatological section. In the traditional translation, Zurwān brings Ahriman a “black and ashen garment” and identifies it with an implement that is fiery or blazing, afflicts creatures, and has the substance of Āz.

The older English rendering reads, in part, “that implement like unto fire,” followed by a statement that it contains Āz’s substance. Because the transmitted text is corrupt, this should not be treated as an uncontested literal description.

In narrative terms, the instrument has two inseparable aspects:

- it animates or empowers Ahriman’s creation;
- it contains the principle that will eventually consume that creation.

### 1.5 Description in *Bundahišn* 1.46

The parallel *Bundahišn* passage says that the Evil Spirit fashions his own creation from the essence of darkness. Older translations rendered its form generically as black, ashy, and appropriate to harmful creatures.

Aliyari Babolghani reconstructs:

> *Gannāg Mēnōg … pad wazaγ kirb*

“The Evil Spirit … [fashioned his creatures] in the form of a toad.”

In this reconstruction the “object” is not clothing at all. It is the first embodied configuration or primordial matter of Ahriman’s counter-creation, symmetrical with Ohrmazd’s creation being fashioned in the form of fire.

### 1.6 What survives

Nothing material survives. The only witnesses are manuscripts and editions of the works containing the story:

- *Wizīdagīhā ī Zādspram* manuscript TD, the only witness preserving both 1.29 and 34.35;
- K35, preserving 1.29;
- BK and related manuscript evidence used by editors;
- manuscripts of the Iranian and Indian recensions of the *Bundahišn*;
- printed editions, photographs, facsimiles, transcriptions, and translations.

The identifiable physical objects are therefore manuscripts containing descriptions of the instrument, not the legendary instrument itself.

## 2. Origin, Manufacture, and Earliest Attestation

### 2.1 Absence from the Avesta

No named garment, tool, or weapon belonging to Angra Mainyu is securely attested in the extant Avesta. Avestan passages describe Angra Mainyu’s evil creations, his assault on the good creation, and the weapons or formulas used **against** him. They do not supply the object studied here.

This negative finding matters. The instrument cannot currently be called an Avestan object, much less an artifact belonging to the earliest recoverable stratum of Zoroastrian tradition. The Avesta itself underwent a long oral transmission before its surviving manuscript history, and its canonized written form appears to have been established during the Sasanian period. Nevertheless, the particular object appears only in later Middle Persian exegesis and cosmogony. [“Avesta: Survey of the History and Contents,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/avesta-holy-book/)

### 2.2 Earliest securely datable textual witness

The earliest securely attributable work containing the object is the **ninth-century Middle Persian** *Wizīdagīhā ī Zādspram*, “Selections of Zādspram.”

Zādspram was a Zoroastrian priest and scholar active around the later ninth century, particularly in Fārs and at Šīrkān. The text’s first chapters concern creation and the cosmic assault; chapters 34–35 concern renovation and the end of evil. The instrument appears once near the beginning of cosmic history and again in its eschatological conclusion. [Philippe Gignoux, “Zādspram,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/zadspram/)

The story may preserve older Sasanian exegesis. That is plausible but not independently datable. “Ninth-century attestation of an older tradition” is therefore more accurate than “Sasanian object.”

### 2.3 Mythic manufacture: *Zādspram* version

The recoverable origin sequence is:

1. Ohrmazd’s good creation exists in an initially stationary condition.
2. Zurwān supplies the condition of temporal motion.
3. Because Time moves the cosmos, it cannot move only one of the opposed creations.
4. Looking toward the predetermined end, Zurwān carries forth to Ahriman an instrument.
5. Its substance is Ahriman’s own darkness, but Zurwān’s power is joined to it.
6. Through it, Ahriman’s counter-creation enters motion.
7. The instrument simultaneously contains the mechanism of that creation’s eventual destruction.

Thus Zurwān is not straightforwardly the material creator. The substance belongs to Ahriman; Zurwān mediates, equips, activates, or carries it forth.

### 2.4 Mythic manufacture: *Bundahišn* version

In the corresponding *Bundahišn* account, no transfer from Zurwān is stated. Ahriman fashions his creatures from his own essence, darkness, in the disputed form.

This is an important variant:

- **Zādspram:** Ahriman receives the form through Zurwān’s mediation.
- **Bundahišn:** Ahriman fashions it himself.

Aliyari Babolghani considers both passages versions of the same cosmogonic phenomenon. He argues that the source common to the two works may have made Time the mediator, while the extant *Bundahišn* omits or suppresses that role. This remains a scholarly reconstruction, not an explicit manuscript statement. [Salman Aliyari Babolghani, “What Was the Instrument That Zurwān Bestowed on Ahreman?”](https://www.edit.fis.uni-hamburg.de/ws/files/68384251/Aliyari.S_2018_Instrument_Zurwan_Bestowed_on_Ahreman_in_W-Zadspram.pdf)

### 2.5 Relation to Āz

In *Zādspram* 34, Āz is captain over other demonic commanders and the source of much of Ahriman’s strength. Her subordinate or associated forces include hunger, thirst, sexual appetite, acquisitive desire, avarice, wrath, winter, old age, and death or harm.

The instrument’s “substance of Āz” is therefore not a mineral or textile composition. It means that limitless appetite is the instrument’s operative principle. The object is made, metaphorically or ontologically, from darkness and insatiability.

## 3. Provenance and Custody History

Because this is a mythic object, two chains must be separated: the custody chain **inside the story**, and the documentary chain of the texts preserving it.

### 3.1 Traditional or mythic chain

#### Primordial state

The material or essence exists as Ahriman’s own darkness. It is not quarried, forged, woven, or purchased.

#### Formation or mediation by Zurwān

At the transition from stationary creation to cosmic motion, Zurwān carries the instrument forth. In the older garment interpretation, this resembles an investiture: Time gives the Evil Spirit a dark vestment or weapon. In the toad-form reconstruction, Zurwān mediates the embodiment and animation of Ahriman’s creation.

#### Transfer to Ahriman

The transfer is not a sale, gift of affection, inheritance, or theft. It is a conditional cosmic allocation. Zurwān attaches a warning or compact: if Ahriman fails to fulfill his boast within the allotted nine-thousand-year struggle, the same power will turn upon his creation.

#### Use by Ahriman

Ahriman uses the instrument to place his counter-creation in motion and to attack or corrupt the material world. The thing does not thereafter pass into the custody of a human ruler, priest, hero, or institution.

#### Diffusion into Ahriman’s creation

If identified with Āz, the instrument becomes immanent in appetite and deficiency throughout embodied existence. *Zādspram* divides Āz’s activity into:

- hunger and thirst necessary to bodily life;
- sexual desire;
- desire for visible or audible goods;
- hoarding, robbery, and refusal to give.

This is less a transfer between holders than an expansion of the instrument’s operation throughout creation.

#### Eschatological reversal

At the Renovation, Āz consumes Ahriman’s creation and then starves because no further nourishment remains. The instrument destroys its own side. It is not captured by Ohrmazd, deposited in a treasury, broken, or ritually purified.

Its mythic endpoint is self-exhaustion.

### 3.2 Documentary transmission

#### Ninth century: composition

Zādspram composed or compiled the *Selections* in Middle Persian Book Pahlavi. His work drew on Zoroastrian learned tradition, but the precise written source of the two instrument passages is unknown.

#### Medieval manuscript gap

No authorial manuscript survives. The chain between ninth-century composition and later manuscript copies is undocumented. This is the largest evidentiary gap in the dossier.

#### K35

Copenhagen manuscript K35 was copied in 1572. Its surviving *Zādspram* section occupies folios 233v–254v; the instrument passage at 1.29 occurs around fols. 235r–235v. K35 was purchased by N. L. Westergaard in Kerman in 1843 and ultimately entered the University of Copenhagen collection. [“Codices Hafnienses,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/codices-hafnienses/)

#### TD

TD is the most complete manuscript witness and the only one retaining both relevant passages: TD4a pp. 485.5–8 for 1.29 and pp. 580.13–581.11 for 34.35. Its full modern institutional custody is not clearly published in the accessible sources consulted here and should be marked **not established in this dossier**.

#### BK

BK is related to K35 and preserves material missing from it. Its exact copying circumstances and present shelf location require manuscript-catalogue research beyond the published summaries available here.

#### Modern editorial custody

B. T. Anklesaria prepared an edition from K35, BK, and TD. Printing was substantially completed in 1943, but most copies were destroyed in the Fort Press fire of 1945. A first volume containing the Pahlavi text and introduction appeared in 1964; the promised translation volume did not appear.

Philippe Gignoux and Ahmad Tafazzoli published the standard complete critical edition, transcription, French translation, commentary, and glossary in 1993. [Bibliographical record for *Anthologie de Zādspram*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Anthologie_de_Z%C4%81dspram.html?id=dVFoAAAAMAAJ)

The online English text hosted by Avesta.org reproduces the older translation tradition and therefore preserves the garment interpretation rather than the later toad-form reconstruction. [*Selections of Zadspram*](https://www.avesta.org/mp/zadspram.html)

## 4. Authenticity and Identity Disputes

### 4.1 Does the object physically exist?

There is no evidence that it does or did. The sources place it before ordinary human history and describe its substance in cosmological terms. No museum, shrine, treasury, excavation, or private collection has produced a candidate object.

Accordingly:

- no accession number exists;
- no physical dimensions are known;
- no materials analysis has been performed;
- no radiocarbon, metallurgical, textile, or residue testing is applicable;
- no archaeological provenance can be reconstructed;
- no current possessor can authenticate it.

### 4.2 Garment versus toad-form

This is the central identity dispute.

#### Garment/skin case

Zaehner and several subsequent translators connected the difficult readings in *Zādspram* with *Dēnkard* III.192. The *Dēnkard* describes four instruments or powers, among them the “costume of tyranny,” Ahriman’s weapon and also a cause of his final destruction. This “selfhood” of the Evil Spirit is called his garment and is manifested, among other things, as an ash-colored garment.

On this analogy, scholars emended the corrupt word in *Zādspram* 34.35 to **paymōg**, “garment,” and understood an ambiguous expression in 1.29 as “skin,” “treaty,” or “coal-like.” Zaehner’s influential formulation made the instrument a black-and-ashen vestment containing Āz. [R. C. Zaehner, “A Zervanite Apocalypse I”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-school-of-oriental-and-african-studies/article/abs/zervanite-apocalypse-i/AAAEFF3AE949301D725EA9634F366D90)

Strengths of this case:

- *Dēnkard* III.192 explicitly associates Ahriman’s power with a garment.
- Ash-colored clothing is part of the *Dēnkard* manifestation scheme.
- “Skin,” “covering,” and “garment” produce a coherent investiture image.
- The reading influenced nearly all twentieth-century discussion.

Weaknesses:

- the decisive garment reading is partly emended rather than plainly transmitted;
- the comparison with *Dēnkard* III.192 may conflate separate theological schemes;
- *abzār* does not inherently mean clothing;
- the parallel *Bundahišn* passage concerns the form of Ahriman’s creation, not clearly his apparel.

#### Toad-form case

Aliyari Babolghani rejects the controlling analogy with *Dēnkard* III.192 and gives priority to the close narrative and verbal parallels with *Bundahišn* 1.46.

His reconstruction identifies the disputed words as **wazaγ kirb**, “form of a toad.” The instrument is then:

- made from Ahriman’s own dark substance;
- black and ashy;
- like a harmful creature;
- the primordial form of the demonic creation;
- an instrument for setting that creation in motion.

Strengths:

- *Zādspram* and *Bundahišn* describe the same stage of cosmogony;
- both contrast the essences of light and darkness;
- both contrast Ohrmazd’s fire-form with Ahriman’s corrupted creature-form;
- the frog or toad comparison later in *Zādspram* 34 provides internal support;
- it avoids an emended garment reading.

Weaknesses:

- it too requires philological emendation;
- the manuscripts are corrupt and incomplete;
- the later comparison of Āz to a frog does not prove that the earlier form is literally the same frog or toad;
- the reading has not erased the older garment interpretation from translations or reference works.

Aliyari Babolghani’s conclusion is presently the most sustained object-specific philological study, but it remains an argued reconstruction rather than a laboratory-verifiable identification.

### 4.3 Weapon versus living power

Even under the garment reading, the “weapon” may not be a discrete portable object. Middle Persian religious prose calls winds, divine beings, truth, falsehood, creatures, mental powers, and religious practices “instruments.” The term can signify causal agency.

The safest classification is consequently:

> a personified or embodied cosmogonic instrument, represented in older scholarship as a garment-weapon and in newer scholarship as the toad-like form of Ahriman’s moving counter-creation.

### 4.4 Āz and the instrument: identity or contents?

The tradition allows several models:

1. the instrument **is** Āz;
2. the instrument contains Āz’s substance;
3. Āz is the living weapon placed at Ahriman’s disposal;
4. the toad-form is Ahriman’s creation as a whole, while Āz is its operative force;
5. *Zādspram* 1.29 and 34.35 have undergone exegetical harmonization and did not originally refer to precisely the same conception.

The sources do not settle the metaphysics.

### 4.5 Authority to authenticate

No religious or civil institution has authenticated a physical object. Interpretive authority has moved among:

- Zoroastrian priestly compilers;
- manuscript copyists;
- Pahlavi editors;
- historians of religion;
- philologists able to collate the surviving manuscripts.

Modern occult writers sometimes claim practical or initiatory knowledge of “the garment of Āz,” but that is reception history, not evidence for the ancient object’s identity.

## 5. Associated Persons, Networks, and Divine Beings

### Zurwān

**Connection:** mediator and conditional giver of the instrument in *Zādspram*.

**Date of association:** primordial mythic time; textually attested in the ninth century.

**Tier:** early surviving Middle Persian attestation.

Zurwān’s participation is absent from the corresponding extant *Bundahišn* formulation.

### Ahreman / Ahriman

**Connection:** recipient, possessor, substance-source, and user. In the *Bundahišn* version, he is also the direct fashioner.

**Tier:** early surviving Middle Persian attestation.

### Āz

**Connection:** the instrument’s substance, power, captain, or personification. She gives Ahriman exceptional strength but ultimately devours his creation and starves.

**Tier:** early surviving Middle Persian attestation; precise identity relation disputed.

### Ohrmazd

**Connection:** opponent whose material creation the instrument helps Ahriman attack. His creation is formed from light and, in the *Bundahišn*, initially assumes fire-form, providing the structural counterpart to Ahriman’s dark form.

### Ahriman’s counter-creation

**Connection:** beneficiary and eventual victim. The instrument moves or constitutes it, then enables Āz to consume it.

### Zādspram

**Connection:** ninth-century author/compiler of the earliest securely attributable surviving text describing the instrument. He is not an owner or witness to a physical object.

### Manuščihr

**Connection:** Zādspram’s brother, high priest, and critic in controversies over purification practice. His relevance is to the intellectual network in which Zādspram wrote, not to the object’s mythic custody.

### R. C. Zaehner

**Connection:** principal twentieth-century architect of the “black and ashen garment” interpretation. His 1930s–1960s work joined *Zādspram* to *Dēnkard* III.192 and popularized the idea that Zurwān armed Ahriman with Āz.

### Philippe Gignoux and Ahmad Tafazzoli

**Connection:** editors of the 1993 critical edition, which made the complete Middle Persian text, transcription, French translation, commentary, and glossary available in a modern scholarly form.

### Salman Aliyari Babolghani

**Connection:** author of the principal modern re-identification, arguing in 2014 that the instrument was the toad-form of Ahriman’s creation rather than clothing.

## 6. Ritual, Ceremonial, and Symbolic Function

### 6.1 No documented cult use

There is no reliable evidence that historical Zoroastrians manufactured, wore, displayed, consecrated, or processed a replica of Ahriman’s instrument. It does not belong to the standard documented equipment of Zoroastrian worship.

No traditional procedure is known for:

- touching it;
- averting it as a physical relic;
- installing it in a temple;
- carrying it in procession;
- destroying a material representation;
- transferring it between priests.

### 6.2 Cosmic investiture

Under Zaehner’s garment reading, Zurwān’s act resembles investiture. The garment gives Ahriman an authorized but temporary field of operation. It is simultaneously regalia, weapon, and bond.

That interpretation is analytically powerful but depends on the disputed garment reading.

### 6.3 Instrument of motion

Under the newer interpretation, its primary function is mechanical or cosmological: it sets Ahriman’s creation in motion because temporal movement must affect both opposed creations.

This does not mean it is a machine. “Instrument” expresses a causal theological function.

### 6.4 Instrument of corruption

Through Āz, the object operates in embodied beings as appetite. Necessary hunger becomes excessive consumption; sexual function becomes ungoverned desire; perception becomes covetousness; possession becomes hoarding or robbery.

This moral-psychological application is explicit in *Zādspram* 34.36–38.

### 6.5 The weapon that reverses direction

The object’s most important symbolic feature is reflexivity. The same principle that sustains Ahriman’s activity ensures his defeat. Insatiable appetite consumes its own supporting creation and, when no food remains, consumes or extinguishes itself.

### 6.6 Legitimation and sovereignty

Unlike a crown or sword, the instrument does not legitimate an earthly dynasty. It does, however, encode the bounded nature of Ahriman’s cosmic rule. Zurwān’s grant does not make evil eternally sovereign; it confines Ahriman’s operation within a fixed cosmic term.

## 7. Myths and Legends Attached to the Object

### 7.1 The conditional gift

**Tier:** ninth-century attested myth, possibly derived from older exegetical material.

Zurwān gives Ahriman an instrument made from Ahriman’s own darkness but joined to the power of Time. The transfer is accompanied by a condition connected with Ahriman’s boast that he will make the material world hate Ohrmazd and love him.

If the boast is not fulfilled by the end of the appointed period, the weapon’s own appetite will destroy Ahriman’s creation.

### 7.2 The black-and-ashen garment

**Tier:** philologically disputed twentieth-century reconstruction.

In Zaehner’s formulation, the object is a sinister vestment. Its black and ashen color expresses the substance of darkness and Ahriman’s selfhood. Its content is Āz, the power of greed or concupiscence.

This version became the standard English-language image because it is vivid, because Zaehner repeatedly published it, and because accessible English translations reproduce it. It should not be represented as an uncontested literal rendering.

### 7.3 The toad-form

**Tier:** modern scholarly reconstruction from medieval textual evidence.

The object is the noxious-creature or toad-shaped first form of Ahriman’s counter-creation. Its opposition to Ohrmazd’s fire-form creates a formal pair:

- light → fire-form → good creation;
- darkness → toad-form → demonic counter-creation.

The toad is not given a personal name, biography, or separate adventures.

### 7.4 The frog in drying water

**Tier:** attested eschatological simile.

Āz is compared to a frog living in water that it pollutes. Once the water is withdrawn, it dies parched. The analogy explains how evil can be powerful in mixture with creation yet incapable of independent eternal existence.

Whether this frog simile preserves the key to the disputed **wazaγ** reading is a scholarly question.

### 7.5 The garment of tyranny in the *Dēnkard*

**Tier:** related but not securely identical Middle Persian tradition.

*Dēnkard* III.192 calls the “costume of tyranny” an instrument and weapon of the Evil Spirit and a cause of his destruction. It associates this power with Akōman, Saturn, and ash-colored clothing.

Zaehner treated this as decisive evidence for the garment. Aliyari Babolghani argues that the *Dēnkard* is describing a broader symbolic classification and should not determine the identity of the specific instrument in *Zādspram*.

### 7.6 Modern occult elaboration

**Tier:** modern invention or reinterpretation.

Michael W. Ford’s Luciferian or “Ahrimanian” writings convert the scholarly phrase “weapon of Āz” into a ritual and initiatory symbol. In this system the practitioner may assume an Ahrimanic “deific mask” and the black-and-ashen garment of Āz as an emblem of individual will, rebellion, and mastery.

Ford further identifies Āz with Lilith-like demonology and treats her as a witch-queen or bride of Ahriman. Those equations are not contained in the ancient or medieval Iranian passages. They are modern syncretic constructions. [Modern commercial description of Ford’s ritual system](https://www.becomealivinggod.com/michaelford/completeworks/)

## 8. Current Status and Location

### 8.1 Legendary object

The object has no present physical location. It is not lost in the archaeological sense, because no evidence shows that it was ever a manufactured artifact.

Its current status is:

- **existence:** textual and mythological only;
- **identity:** disputed;
- **condition:** not applicable;
- **owner:** none in historical law;
- **ritual custodian:** none documented;
- **public display:** none;
- **scientific examination:** none possible without a candidate artifact.

### 8.2 Manuscript witnesses

The most precisely located major witness is K35 in the University of Copenhagen manuscript collection. It was copied in 1572, acquired in Kerman by Westergaard in 1843, and donated to the university before his death in 1878.

The relevant leaves and published manuscript photographs—not the legendary thing—are the nearest physical survivals.

### 8.3 Legal status

There is no known ownership or repatriation dispute concerning an artifact claimed as Ahriman’s instrument.

Questions concerning the acquisition and movement of Iranian Zoroastrian manuscripts are separate provenance issues. The accessible sources establish Westergaard’s purchase of K35 in Kerman but do not provide sufficient evidence to characterize that acquisition as theft, confiscation, or illicit export.

### 8.4 Present scholarly state

The older garment translation remains widely available online and in twentieth-century literature. The toad-form interpretation is available in a specialist article but has not displaced the garment in popular accounts. The object consequently exists today in two parallel forms:

1. the familiar black-and-ashen garment or weapon of Āz;
2. the philologically reconstructed toad-form of Ahriman’s counter-creation.

## 9. Modern Reception

### 9.1 Academic reception

Zaehner’s work made the object a striking example of Zurvanite theology: Time arms Ahriman with the force that will finally destroy him. The phrase “black and ashen garment” entered dictionaries of mythology, surveys of dualism, and popular religious history.

The image’s prevalence reflects the influence of one interpretive tradition, not manuscript unanimity.

### 9.2 Persian-language reception

Modern Persian summaries commonly render the object as a **jāme**, **tan-pūš**, **ḵerqa**, or dark, ash-colored robe bestowed by Zurwān. Some presentations say that Zurwān constructs Āz from his own power but from Ahriman’s essence and gives it to him as a robe or instrument.

These accounts generally reproduce the older reading and often smooth over the textual uncertainty.

### 9.3 Occult and Luciferian reception

Modern “Ahrimanian” occultism materializes the garment symbolically. Ford’s system treats donning or assuming the weapon of Āz as a ritualized act. This is the closest documented modern practice in which the legendary object is treated as something that can be “worn.”

It is nevertheless a symbolic visualization created in a contemporary esoteric movement. It is not evidence for ancient Ahriman worship or for a historical garment.

### 9.4 Visual and popular culture

No ancient image is securely identified as Zurwān handing Ahriman this instrument. Roman-period leontocephalic figures formerly labeled “Ahriman,” Sasanian reliefs showing defeated enemies under divine or royal feet, and later horned-devil images cannot be used as representations of the garment or toad-form without independent evidence.

Likewise, the “Black Staff of Ahriman” in *Warhammer 40,000* belongs to the fictional character Ahzek Ahriman. It has no historical connection to the Iranian object beyond use of the name Ahriman.

### 9.5 Replicas and forgeries

No documented replica or forgery claims to be the ancient original. Modern ritual robes, illustrations, gaming weapons, and commercial designs are imaginative representations, not rival authentic objects.

## 10. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### 10.1 Early publication history

European access to Middle Persian cosmogonic literature initially depended on manuscripts transported from Iran and India and on imperfect early editions. The Pahlavi script’s heterograms, ambiguous spellings, damaged witnesses, and copyists’ errors made the instrument passages especially unstable.

### 10.2 Zaehner’s reconstruction

R. C. Zaehner addressed the passages repeatedly:

- “Zurvanica I” in the 1930s;
- “A Zervanite Apocalypse I” in 1940;
- later books on Zurvanism and Zoroastrianism.

He compared *Zādspram* with *Dēnkard* III.192, proposed readings involving skin, treaty, coal, and garment, and interpreted the object as a dark investiture containing Āz.

Zaehner himself recognized that some readings were conjectural. Subsequent summaries often omitted those reservations.

### 10.3 The 1993 critical edition

Gignoux and Tafazzoli’s *Anthologie de Zādspram* is the central modern edition. It collated the manuscript tradition and supplied a critical apparatus, transcription, French translation, commentary, and glossary. Its publication placed the argument on a substantially firmer documentary basis, although it could not repair every corrupt passage.

### 10.4 Aliyari Babolghani’s revision

Aliyari Babolghani’s article is devoted specifically to the object. Its method is to compare *Zādspram* 1.29 and 34.35 with the structurally parallel *Bundahišn* 1.46 rather than allowing the more remote *Dēnkard* classification to govern the reading.

His conclusion is that:

- the object is **wazaγ kirb**, “the form of a toad”;
- it derives from Ahriman’s dark substance;
- Zurwān mediates its appearance in *Zādspram*;
- it is the primordial matter or form of the demonic creation;
- it gives that creation motion;
- the “garment” interpretation is an artifact of emendation and misplaced comparison.

### 10.5 Dating problems

The *Bundahišn* grew through several redactions. Its first compilation cannot be precisely dated, although much of its material is conventionally assigned to late Sasanian and early Islamic learned tradition. The Iranian and Indian recensions survive in much later manuscripts. [“Bundahišn,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bundahisn-primal-creation/)

The first thirty chapters of the Iranian recension are often associated with late Sasanian learned culture, but final compilation and copying are later. [“Middle Persian: Pahlavi Literature,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/middle-persian/pahlavi-literature/)

It is therefore unsafe to assign the object a precise Sasanian “creation date.”

### 10.6 Unresolved questions

- Did *Zādspram* 1.29 originally say “skin,” “treaty,” “coal-like,” “toad-like,” or something now unrecoverable?
- Is 34.35 a faithful repetition of 1.29 or a later theological reinterpretation?
- Are the instrument and Āz identical?
- Is the *Dēnkard* garment of tyranny the same entity?
- Did *Zādspram* and the *Bundahišn* draw on one lost common source?
- Was Zurwān removed from one recension for doctrinal reasons?
- Is “toad” literal mythic imagery or a classification of the demonic creation as noxious?
- How much of the episode should be called specifically Zurvanite rather than part of a broader Middle Persian Zoroastrian cosmology?

None is settled by existing physical evidence.

## 12. Sources

### Primary and primary-text editions

- Zādspram. *Wizīdagīhā ī Zādspram*, especially 1.29 and 34.35. The online English version reproduces an older translation and must be checked against modern critical work.
- *Bundahišn*, especially 1.43–46, for the opposed fire-form and dark or toad-form of the two creations.
- *Dēnkard* III.192, for the related “costume of tyranny,” the Evil Spirit’s weapon, selfhood, and ash-colored garment.

### Critical editions and major studies

- Philippe Gignoux and Ahmad Tafazzoli, eds. and trans., *Anthologie de Zādspram*, Cahiers de Studia Iranica 13, Paris, 1993.

### Source assessment

The decisive evidence consists of two corrupt Middle Persian passages preserved in late manuscripts, one closely parallel *Bundahišn* passage, and a related but potentially separate *Dēnkard* classification. The garment remains an important historical interpretation, not an established physical description. The toad-form is a stronger recent contextual reconstruction, not an indisputable reading. The object’s actual existence outside theology cannot be established.
