# "El Arquero Divino" (Troiani, 1924): Object Dossier

> **Suggested filename:** `el_arquero_divino_object_dossier.md`  
> **Research status:** compiled 6 September 2026.  
> **Object tracked:** the bronze archer by Troiano Troiani installed in Plaza Moreno, La Plata, Argentina—not Antoine Bourdelle’s separate *Héraklès archer* in Buenos Aires.  
> **Evidentiary labels:** **Documented** = supported by a contemporary record, official catalogue, legal instrument, or technical report; **reported** = later secondary account; **inference** = plausible interpretation not established by primary documentation; **tradition/legend** = a circulating story whose occurrence is documented but whose asserted historical content is not.

## 1. Identification and Physical Description

### Identity and names

The object is a monumental outdoor bronze sculpture of a nude male archer by the Italian-born, later naturalized Argentine sculptor **Troiano Troiani** (Buja, Udine, 1885–Buenos Aires, 1963). Troiani modeled it in 1924; the bronze was cast that year in Buenos Aires by the **Trivium** foundry. The Municipality of La Plata acquired and installed it in Plaza Moreno in 1970. A national heritage inventory now identifies it officially as **Hércules Arquero**, while other institutional and popular sources call it **El Arquero**, **El Arquero Divino**, or **Hércules Arquero**. The Italian-language title is **Arciere**, literally “Archer.” The federal object page uses *El arquero divino*, whereas the schedule underlying its heritage designation uses *Hércules Arquero*. [Argentina.gob.ar object record](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/escultura-el-arquero-divino); [official protected-sculpture schedule](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/esculturas_listado.pdf)

The following names must be distinguished:

- **El Arquero / “The Archer”:** the title used in the contemporary La Plata newspaper notice of 10 December 1970, as reproduced by a later local history compilation.
- **Arquero / Arciere:** the title under which the sculpture reportedly appeared in Troiani’s 1925 Buenos Aires solo exhibition and subsequently won a prize.
- **Hércules Arquero:** the iconographically explicit name used by conservation researchers and Argentina’s national heritage inventory.
- **El Arquero Divino:** the now-common popular and federal catalogue name. No located 1924 or 1925 primary record establishes that the adjective *Divino* belonged to Troiani’s original title.
- **Hércules de Bordel:** a corrupted popular name reported in 2021. “Bordel” is apparently a deformation of **Bourdelle**, the surname of the French sculptor whose *Héraklès archer* supplied Troiani’s immediate model; it is not a secure original title.
- **Heracles/Héraklès/Hércules:** Greek Ἡρακλῆς (*Hēraklês*), Latin *Hercules*, Spanish *Heracles* or *Hércules*. The word “divine” is iconographically defensible because Heracles was a demigod and, after death, a god in Greek tradition, but the title’s documentary origin remains unestablished.

### Form and iconography

The sculpture is a full-length, over-life-size—or at least monumentally conceived—male nude mounted on a separate pedestal. The archer leans into a dramatically extended, asymmetrical pose. His legs spread into a broad triangular support; one foot braces against a rocklike mass. His torso twists, his arms pull in opposite directions, and his head concentrates on an elevated target. The anatomy is strongly articulated: shoulders, abdomen, arms, thighs, and calves are treated as interlocking masses under maximum tension.

Eduardo Migo’s formal study describes the figure as governed by a stable triangular structure while simultaneously conveying arrested action. The small head relative to the body, emphatic musculature, open silhouette, and opposition of limbs make the figure legible from multiple viewpoints. Migo’s study was published by the Fundación Museo La Plata in 2015. [Migo bibliographic record](https://repositoriosdigitales.mincyt.gob.ar/vufind/Record/SEDICI_5b0e5b678ba92d29542ecbe2bc56bf9e)

The figure is normally identified as Heracles shooting at the **Stymphalian birds**, one of his canonical labors. A La Plata architectural and symbolic study calls it the fifth labor; conventional modern enumerations more often number the episode sixth. This numbering discrepancy concerns the sequence of the myth, not the sculpture’s identity. [La Plata study](https://idihcs.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/greenstone3/library/sites/localsite/collect/granlapl/index/assoc/L_1982_I.dir/doc.pdf%3Bjsessionid%3DA09A8AC0F5D0BB6FB064D84FBF534290)

### Material and manufacture

**Documented by technical study:**

- Material: cast bronze.
- Foundry: **Trivium**, Buenos Aires.
- Date of casting: 1924.
- Format: full-length figure on a pedestal.
- Present support: a later pedestal faced in marble.
- Installation: outdoors in Plaza Moreno.

The technical report does not publish the alloy composition, casting-wall thickness, internal armature, joining scheme, overall weight, or exact dimensions. No published metallographic, X-ray, ultrasonic, or elemental analysis was located. “Bronze” is therefore the documented material class, but its precise copper–tin–zinc–lead composition remains unreported. [UNLP conservation report](https://host170.sedici.unlp.edu.ar/server/api/core/bitstreams/bc74f216-a331-4770-80bb-5b290514e444/content)

Descriptions calling the work “modeled in the Rodin manner” refer to the plastic method and surface conception, not to a scientifically demonstrated manufacturing process. Troiani presumably produced a modeled original from which molds were taken for casting, but the material and present location of that original model are unestablished.

### Bow and arrow

The archer was originally displayed with a bow and apparently an arrow. These were separate, vulnerable projecting elements.

- A photograph accompanying the 10 December 1970 installation notice reportedly shows the complete work.
- A photograph dated 1979 shows that the bow had already disappeared.
- One 2025 newspaper retrospective says someone removed the “weapon and projectile” one year after installation, implying 1971.
- A 2021 report says the bow had been missing “since 1979,” which establishes only that it was absent by then, not necessarily that the removal occurred during that calendar year.
- In 2021 municipal conservators installed a reconstructed bow based on photographs. The replacement is therefore not Troiani’s surviving original.
- The 2021 reports describe restoration of the bow but do not establish that an arrow was replaced. Recent descriptions consequently present a figure with a reconstructed bow but no securely documented restored arrow.

The original bow and arrow have not been recovered in the located record. Accounts variously describe their fate as theft, deliberate cutting, destruction, or removal. No police report, arrest, named culprit, recovery record, or surviving fragment was found.

### Surface, damage, and alterations

A technical survey of the Plaza Moreno metal sculptures recorded the following condition for the archer:

- missing bow;
- oxidation stains and runoff streaks;
- graffiti in indelible white material;
- deficient maintenance;
- outdoor exposure to dust, soot, grease, moisture, carbon dioxide, sulfur compounds, chlorides, and industrial pollution associated partly with the Ensenada petrochemical area.

The report characterized the plaza sculptures collectively as broadly sound but suffering atmospheric corrosion, vandalism, lost elements, and inadequate maintenance. For the archer it recommended gentle abrasive cleaning, identification of the original patina, filling and finishing defects, possible reproduction of the bow, stabilization if the green patina were retained, and a transparent protective coating. It did not report laboratory confirmation of the original patination recipe. [UNLP conservation report](https://host170.sedici.unlp.edu.ar/server/api/core/bitstreams/bc74f216-a331-4770-80bb-5b290514e444/content)

The pedestal is not contemporary with the 1924 modeling. La Plata heritage director Roberto Ciafardo observed in 2021 that its marble-faced, comparatively modern design belongs to a different artistic language. [El Día, 16 October 2021](https://www.eldia.com/nota/2021-10-16-3-14-16-completan-con-el-arco-una-escultura-historica-de-plaza-moreno-la-ciudad)

No authoritative publication located for this dossier supplies:

- exact sculpture or pedestal measurements;
- weight;
- a transcription of Troiani’s signature or the Trivium foundry mark;
- an accession or municipal inventory number;
- the location of the original plaster;
- confirmation that the marble facing is original to the 1970 installation;
- a comprehensive post-2021 condition report.

Those data must remain **not established**.

## 2. Origin, Manufacture, and Earliest Attestation

### Artistic origin

Troiani made *Arquero* in Argentina in 1924. The securely published technical claim is that Trivium cast it in bronze in Buenos Aires that year. The work was not made for the 1882 foundation of La Plata and could not have been part of the city’s original plan.

Its immediate artistic precedent was Antoine Bourdelle’s *Héraklès archer*. Bourdelle developed that composition through studies made from 1906 to 1908, enlarged it in 1909, and had a monumental bronze cast by Rudier for the patron Gabriel Thomas. It entered the Salon de la Société Nationale des Beaux-Arts in 1910 to substantial acclaim. The subject is Heracles killing the birds of Lake Stymphalus. [Musée Bourdelle history](https://www.bourdelle.paris.fr/en/10-bourdelles-masterpieces/hercules-archer-herakles-archer)

Troiani did not make a mechanically exact replica. Both works use a heroic nude, extreme muscular tension, strong diagonals, an open silhouette, a braced leg, and the unseen aerial target. Troiani’s figure nevertheless has its own pose and volumetric construction. It should be described as **inspired by, adapted from, or responding to Bourdelle**, not automatically as an authorized Bourdelle edition.

Bourdelle’s own second-version bronze in the Musée d’Orsay measures 248 × 247 × 123 cm and weighs 580 kg, bears Bourdelle’s and Rudier’s inscriptions, and includes reliefs of the Hydra and Nemean lion. Those measurements and inscriptions belong to Bourdelle’s object, **not** to Troiani’s. [Musée d’Orsay catalogue](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/oeuvres/herakles-tue-les-oiseaux-du-lac-stymphale-5124)

Claims that Bourdelle drew directly from the pediments of the Temple of Aphaia at Aegina recur in La Plata popular literature. The general archaizing/classical genealogy is plausible, and Bourdelle’s work unquestionably invokes Greek heroic sculpture, but the located Bourdelle museum material describes a broader engagement with primitive Greek and Romanesque form rather than documenting a direct copy of a particular Aphaia archer. The narrower Aphaia derivation should therefore be treated as a **repeated secondary interpretation**, not a demonstrated commission fact.

### Commission and purpose

No contract commissioning *Arquero* in 1924 has been located. No evidence shows that La Plata ordered it at that time. The available chronology instead indicates an independently produced exhibition work that entered municipal ownership decades later.

The cost of modeling, casting, or the later municipal purchase is not published in the sources located. The source of its bronze and the circumstances of Trivium’s engagement are also unknown.

### Earliest attestation

The earliest reconstructable sequence is:

1. **1924:** Troiani creates and Trivium casts the bronze.
2. **1925:** Troiani holds his first solo exhibition in Buenos Aires; *Arquero/Arciere* is among the works exhibited.
3. **Later, date not securely identified:** the work wins a prize at a municipal exhibition of industrial arts.

The Italian *Dizionario Biografico dei Friulani* supplies the 1925 exhibition and prize history. Its wording separates the first solo exhibition from the subsequent municipal prize, so they should not be collapsed into one event. [Giuseppe Bergamini, “Troiani Troiano”](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/troiani-troiano)

Buenos Aires’s first municipal industrial-arts exhibition opened at the Pabellón de las Rosas on 20 December 1924 and included a sculpture section. That institutional chronology makes the exact prize date uncertain: if Troiani’s *Arquero* was shown in his 1925 solo exhibition and “subsequently” awarded, either a later annual exhibition is meant or the Italian biographical sequence is imprecise. A prize list or catalogue naming the work and year was not located. The prize is credible but incompletely documented.

A 1928 Bahía Blanca periodical report, preserved in later scholarship, identifies Troiani’s *Arquero* as first-prize winner in a sculpture exhibition at the Policlínico, ahead of Luis Perlotti’s *La oración*. It is unclear whether that was the same award described as the municipal industrial-arts prize or a different exhibition and prize. The two claims should remain separate pending examination of original catalogues.

## 3. Provenance and Custody History

### 1924: creation and casting

**Documented by later technical scholarship:** Troiani’s bronze was cast at Trivium in Buenos Aires in 1924.

The initial ownership arrangement is not known. Possibilities include ownership by Troiani, by the foundry pending payment, or by an unlocated patron, but none can be asserted.

### 1925: exhibition

**Documented by scholarly biography:** the sculpture appeared in Troiani’s first one-man exhibition in Buenos Aires. Custody presumably remained with the artist or an exhibition intermediary, but no lender or owner is named.

### After 1925: prize exhibition or exhibitions

*Arquero* received at least one reported first prize. The located record does not say whether a prize brought purchase or transfer of title. Exhibition and award do not by themselves establish ownership.

### 1925–1963: undocumented custody

This is the longest gap in the object’s chain. No located source states where the bronze stood, whether it remained in Troiani’s studio, whether it entered a private collection, or whether it was stored by a gallery or institution.

Troiani died in Buenos Aires in 1963. It is plausible that any work still owned by him passed into his estate, but no will, estate inventory, succession file, or family declaration identifying *Arquero* has been located. The bronze must not be assigned to his heirs without evidence.

### 1963–1970: undocumented custody continues

No transfer document bridging Troiani’s death and the municipal acquisition has been found. This gap matters: the Municipality acquired an already existing bronze six years or more after the artist’s death, but the seller, donor, intermediary, legal mechanism, and price are absent from published accounts.

### 1970: municipal acquisition and installation

A report in *El Día* dated **10 December 1970** stated that the Municipality had acquired a work by Troiani and placed it in Plaza Moreno at the angle of streets 14 and 54. It called the work *El Arquero* and described the acquisition as part of the municipality’s cultural-diffusion program and its purchases from salons of painting, sculpture, engraving, and drawing. This is the closest located contemporary notice to an acquisition record. [Reproduction and citation of the 1970 notice](https://misteriosdelaplata.blogspot.com/2011/03/el-arquero-divino.html)

A separate local monument history gives **10 October 1970** as the date on which the sculpture entered city patrimony. That may represent the legal acquisition date, two months before the newspaper notice, but the underlying municipal resolution has not been located. The safest reconstruction is:

- municipal incorporation reportedly dated 10 October 1970;
- installation and acquisition publicly announced by 10 December 1970.

The acquisition occurred under a military-appointed municipal administration during Argentina’s “Revolución Argentina.” Later accounts identify the municipal intervener as Colonel Franco A. Icazatti. This political context decisively contradicts stories that La Plata’s 1882 founders themselves installed the archer.

### Original placement

The bronze was mounted in the southern portion of Plaza Moreno, conventionally described as near 14 and 54. Later reports sometimes say 14 and 53; the federal coordinates are **34°55′20.87″ S, 57°57′14.60″ W**. The discrepancy appears to reflect descriptions of its position inside a very large square rather than evidence of a documented relocation. No source located records a post-1970 move of the statue itself.

The pedestal was created for the 1970 installation and is not part of Troiani’s documented 1924 casting.

### 1970s: loss of bow and arrow

The complete sculpture’s projecting weapon elements disappeared shortly after installation.

- **Strong terminus ante quem:** a photograph identified as dating from 1979 already shows the archer without its bow.
- **Later claim:** a 2025 article says the weapon and projectile were removed one year after inauguration, which would mean approximately 1971.
- **Alternative shorthand:** the 2021 restoration report says the bow had been absent “since 1979.”

Because the 1971 claim appears only in a much later retrospective and no contemporary police or municipal file was found, the most defensible statement is: **the original bow and arrow disappeared sometime after installation in 1970 and no later than the photographed state in 1979**.

The loss is often called theft, and some writers say the bow was cut away. No identified offender or motive is documented. The proposition that someone removed it because they believed the anti-Cathedral legend is speculation.

### Late twentieth century: exposure and vandalism

The statue remained outdoors under municipal control. By the date of the UNLP-associated conservation study it lacked its bow and showed corrosion streaks and graffiti. A 2000 report concerning La Plata’s proposed monument-restoration program described Troiani’s archer as one of the city’s artistically valuable sculptures. It does not establish that the planned restoration was then completed. [El Día, 30 October 2000](https://www.eldia.com/nota/2000-10-30-decidieron-restaurar-los-monumentos-de-la-ciudad)

### 2019: federal heritage protection

On 13 November 2019, Argentina’s executive issued **Decree 769/2019**, published the following day. It created national protective classifications for qualifying public sculptures and directed the National Commission of Monuments, Places and Historic Assets to make the necessary listings and registrations. Troiani’s *Hércules Arquero* appears as item 27 in the official schedule, with Plaza Moreno and geographic coordinates specified. It is categorized as a **Bien de Interés Histórico Nacional** and **Bien de Interés Artístico Nacional**. [Decree 769/2019](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/decreto-769-2019-331715/texto)

The decree did not transfer municipal title to the national government. It overlaid federal heritage protection and administrative supervision on the municipally held work.

### 2020: anonymous interventions and restoration planning

In August 2020 municipal public-space officials and representatives of the National Monuments Commission inspected the monument in connection with a proposed comprehensive restoration of it and Plaza Moreno’s heritage.

On the morning reported as 20 or 21 August 2020, an unidentified person wrapped the figure very neatly in red or pink gift paper decorated with yellow stars and placed a gold-colored bow on its head. Contemporary reporting called the act mysterious; no author or purpose was established. [El Día, 20 August 2020](https://www.eldia.com/nota/2020-8-20-9-43-0-lo-envolvieron-para-regalo-curiosa-intervencion-a-el-arquero-divino-de-plaza-moreno-la-ciudad)

A later academic analysis distinguishes multiple overlapping anonymous interventions visible in the event photograph:

1. the gift wrapping;
2. the inscription **“DESTRUYE LO QUE TE DESTRUYE, FUEGO A LA IGLESIA”**;
3. an anarchist symbol associated by placement, color, and typography with that inscription;
4. other accumulated markings or material traces.

The analyst properly treats the relation among these interventions as inferential; their makers and exact dates are unknown. The wrapping was temporary, but the episode entered the statue’s reception history because it visually concealed a monument already repeatedly altered by subtraction and graffiti. [UNLP conference proceedings](https://papelcosido.fba.unlp.edu.ar/pdf/libros/Actas%20ENCUENTRO%20DE%20ARTE%20Y%20POL%C3%8DTICA.pdf)

### 2021: reconstruction of the bow

A municipal restoration team based in the workshop popularly called the **“hospital de las estatuas”**, in the former Pedro Benoit workhouse in Parque Saavedra, reconstructed the bow. The team was directed by sculptor and Faculty of Arts teacher **Eduardo Migo**.

Because the original was lost, the conservators used historical photographs, enlarged them to one-to-one scale, and produced a simulacrum of the photographed bow. The reconstructed element was installed by October 2021. This was a restoration of visual completeness through a modern replacement, not the recovery or reattachment of Troiani’s original component. No located report gives the replacement’s alloy, internal fastening system, maker-by-maker credits, or treatment specifications. [El Día, 16 October 2021](https://www.eldia.com/nota/2021-10-16-3-14-16-completan-con-el-arco-una-escultura-historica-de-plaza-moreno-la-ciudad)

### 2021–present

The bronze remains in Plaza Moreno under municipal custody and national heritage protection. No credible report located through September 2026 records deaccession, relocation, major new damage, removal of the 2021 bow, or a title dispute.

## 4. Authenticity and Identity Disputes

### Is the La Plata bronze a genuine Troiani?

The attribution is strong:

- repeated municipal and national identification;
- the 1970 contemporary acquisition notice;
- a scholarly biography connecting *Arquero/Arciere* with Troiani’s 1925 exhibition;
- a technical report naming both Troiani and the Trivium foundry;
- consistent dating to 1924.

No competing attribution or forgery allegation was found. The absence of a published signature transcription, foundry-mark photograph, or 1924 casting ledger limits forensic proof but does not create a serious rival attribution.

### Is it materially intact?

No. Its present state combines components of different dates:

- **1924:** Troiani/Trivium bronze figure;
- **1970:** later pedestal and public installation;
- **post-1970, pre-1979:** loss of original bow and arrow;
- **2021:** photographically reconstructed bow;
- **arrow:** apparently still absent, unless an unreported later intervention replaced it.

The current monument is therefore authentically Troiani’s bronze figure but no longer preserves the whole original accessory ensemble.

### Is it a Bourdelle cast?

No evidence supports that classification. Troiani’s sculpture is a separate work inspired by Bourdelle. The confusion is strengthened by the existence of an authorized or editioned Bourdelle *Héraklès archer* in **Plaza Dante, Recoleta, Buenos Aires**. Buenos Aires’s official tourism site explicitly identifies the Recoleta figure as a bronze replica of Bourdelle’s 1909 work. [Buenos Aires official monument guide](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/node/49554)

Some La Plata sources say that “a copy of this archer” exists in Recoleta. That is misleading if read as meaning a second cast of Troiani’s sculpture. The Recoleta object is a Bourdelle composition and belongs to Bourdelle’s edition history; it is not documented as a replica cast from Troiani’s model.

### Original, edition, or unique cast?

The number of bronze casts Troiani authorized is unestablished. No catalogue raisonné, foundry ledger, edition number, or second securely identified Troiani cast was located. The La Plata bronze should not be called unique merely because no other example has been verified.

### Scientific authentication

No radiocarbon dating would be relevant to the bronze itself, and none has been reported. No thermoluminescence, metallography, alloy fingerprinting, lead-isotope study, internal imaging, or comparative tool-mark examination has been published. Authentication currently rests on documentary, institutional, stylistic, and foundry attribution rather than laboratory testing.

### Authority to authenticate

Authority has shifted among overlapping actors:

- Troiani and Trivium initially controlled authorship and casting identity.
- Exhibition juries authenticated the work institutionally by accepting and rewarding it.
- The Municipality of La Plata accepted it into public patrimony in 1970.
- Local conservators and UNLP-linked researchers documented its material state.
- Since 2019, the National Commission of Monuments has federal heritage standing.
- The 2021 municipal team exercised conservation authority when deciding how to reconstruct the missing bow.

## 5. Associated Persons, Networks, and Divine Beings

- **Troiano Troiani (1885–1963)** — maker; modeled the work in 1924. He exhibited *Arquero* in Buenos Aires in 1925. He died before its La Plata acquisition and never saw it in Plaza Moreno.

- **Trivium foundry, Buenos Aires** — documented caster of the bronze in 1924. No surviving contract or casting ledger was located.

- **Antoine Bourdelle (1861–1929)** — artistic precedent, not maker or owner of the La Plata bronze. His 1909 *Héraklès archer* established the modern composition to which Troiani responded.

- **Gabriel Thomas** — patron of Bourdelle’s first monumental version; associated with the prototype’s publication history, not with custody of Troiani’s object.

- **Heracles/Hercules** — divine or heroic subject represented. The pose is interpreted as the hero shooting the Stymphalian birds.

- **Stymphalian birds** — mythological antagonists. They are the implied but unseen target; they are not materially represented in Troiani’s surviving composition.

- **Municipality of La Plata** — purchaser and custodian from 1970 onward; commissioner of the pedestal, public installer, maintenance authority, and organizer of the 2021 bow reconstruction.

- **Franco A. Icazatti** — military-appointed municipal intervener reportedly governing when the monument was installed. No document proves that he personally chose its orientation.

- **Eduardo Migo** — sculptor, teacher, formal analyst of the work, and director of the municipal team that reconstructed the bow in 2021.

- **Roberto Ciafardo** — La Plata’s director general for heritage preservation in 2021; publicly distinguished the sculpture’s 1924 artistic language from its later pedestal and rejected the anti-clerical placement story as implausible.

- **Jorge Ferioli** — municipal heritage-restoration official quoted in advance of the restoration program that aimed to return the bow.

- **National Commission of Monuments, Places and Historic Assets** — federal heritage body involved in the 2019 classification and 2020 restoration planning.

- **Gualberto Reynal** — principal modern popularizer of an “occult” reading in *La historia oculta de la ciudad de La Plata* (1993). His role is interpretive, not custodial.

- **Eduardo Sebastianielli** — researcher of Freemasonry who publicly rejected the claim that the founders placed the statue as an anti-Church gesture, emphasizing its much later date.

- **Anonymous remover or removers** — responsible for the disappearance of the original bow and arrow between 1970 and 1979; identity, motive, and fate unknown.

- **Anonymous graffiti writer or writers** — added anti-Church and anarchist language to the pedestal or monument area; identity and precise dates unknown.

- **Anonymous wrapper** — wrapped the sculpture as a gift in August 2020; no verified identity or explanation is recorded.

- **La Plata Freemasons and the city’s founders** — objects of later attribution, not documented participants in the commission or placement. Some founders were Freemasons, but the sculpture postdates the city’s founding by 42 years and its installation by 88 years.

## 6. Ritual, Ceremonial, and Symbolic Function

The archer is a public artwork, not a cult image or relic. No documented worship, liturgy, sacrifice, oath, healing practice, procession, or controlled-access ritual attaches to it.

Its documented functions are:

- **Exhibition object:** shown in Troiani’s 1925 solo exhibition.
- **Prize object:** reportedly rewarded in later exhibition contexts.
- **Civic ornament:** acquired to enrich La Plata’s public sculpture collection.
- **Landscape focus:** placed asymmetrically among the trees of Plaza Moreno as a visual accent outside the square’s more regular sculptural scheme.
- **Heroic-classical symbol:** represents concentrated force, physical discipline, control, and the conquest of a destructive natural threat.
- **Heritage asset:** since 2019, protected for historical and artistic interest.
- **Urban interpretive screen:** repeatedly used to project narratives about Freemasonry, Catholicism, state power, and the hidden plan of La Plata.

The 1982-era symbolic reading interprets Heracles as a liberator from scourges that afflict humanity and suggests that the upward shot places success in divine will. That is a scholarly or civic interpretation, not evidence of Troiani’s stated program.

The statue’s direction toward the Cathedral has become its most powerful symbolic fact. Whether its actual line of aim terminates at the cathedral’s central rose window, roof, cross, or an elevated point near them varies with the observer’s position and with descriptions written before and after the cathedral towers were completed in 1999. No surviving siting memorandum establishes a deliberate target.

There are no documented taboos governing touch or sight. Its unrestricted outdoor accessibility facilitated vandalism and the removal of projecting components.

## 7. Myths and Legends Attached to the Object

### The anti-Cathedral Masonic shot

**Legend:** Freemasons who founded La Plata positioned the archer so that its arrow attacked the Cathedral, expressing hostility between Masonry and the Roman Catholic Church.

**Documentary problems:**

- La Plata was founded in 1882.
- The sculpture was made in 1924.
- It was installed only in 1970.
- The founding generation therefore did not install it as part of the original city plan.
- No commissioning, acquisition, council, lodge, or placement record located states an anti-clerical purpose.
- The installation took place under a military administration not ordinarily characterized as institutionally anti-Catholic.

The story’s best located publication history points to Gualberto Reynal’s **1993** *La historia oculta de la ciudad de La Plata* as a major source of its diffusion. A 2009 Masonic publication explicitly says the fantasy was “triggered” by that book and quotes Eduardo Sebastianielli’s chronological rebuttal. [Hiram Abif, no. 111](https://eruizf.com/masonico/rev_hiram_abif/111_jun_2009.pdf)

**Classification:** modern urban legend or late interpretive accretion. The legend’s circulation is real; its account of the monument’s origin is contradicted by the creation and installation chronology.

### The missing bow was removed to stop the attack

**Legend or unverified claim:** someone cut off or stole the original weapon because the sculpture was believed to be firing upon the Cathedral.

The physical loss is real. The motive is not documented. No contemporary confession, police file, eyewitness statement, or municipal finding connects the removal to religious or Masonic controversy.

**Classification:** factual loss plus speculative motive.

### The “hidden” or displaced archer

Popular accounts call it the **arquero escondido**, the “hidden archer,” because it sits among trees and outside Plaza Moreno’s strong axial and symmetrical arrangement.

This description has a material basis: the statue is asymmetrically located and vegetation can obstruct long views. Claims that concealment proves an occult intention go beyond the evidence.

**Classification:** observational nickname with later esoteric elaboration.

### The “true target” is the Stymphalian birds

A recent tourism account counters the anti-Cathedral story by arguing that the archer’s true target is mythological or cosmic: the fleeing Stymphalian birds. Iconographically, Heracles’ unseen target is indeed the birds. The further proposition that their imagined flight fixes a literal celestial trajectory over La Plata is interpretive rather than documentary.

**Classification:** mythologically grounded iconographic explanation, with modern rhetorical elaboration.

### The Recoleta “copy”

**Claim:** another copy of Troiani’s archer stands in Recoleta.

The real Recoleta sculpture is Bourdelle’s *Héraklès archer*. It is a close thematic relative and Troiani’s model, but no evidence found identifies it as a cast from Troiani’s sculpture.

**Classification:** modern conflation of prototype and derivative.

### “Became part of the founding symbolism”

The sculpture is regularly folded into stories of La Plata’s 1882 foundation, its diagonals, foundation stone, cathedral, and Masonic planners. Its position now makes it part of that symbolic landscape, but it entered that landscape in 1970. Its **reception** belongs to the city’s foundation mythology; its **manufacture and installation do not**.

## 8. Current Status and Location

As of the latest located documentation, the monument stands outdoors in:

**Plaza Moreno**  
La Plata, Province of Buenos Aires  
Argentina  
Near the southern sector conventionally described as streets 14 and 54  
Official coordinates: **34°55′20.87″ S, 57°57′14.60″ W**

It is publicly accessible without admission. The Municipality of La Plata retains immediate custody and maintenance responsibility. The object is also listed under the federal regime established through Law 12,665 and Decree 769/2019 as both a **Bien de Interés Histórico Nacional** and **Bien de Interés Artístico Nacional**.

The visible ensemble consists of:

- Troiani’s 1924 bronze figure;
- the later marble-faced pedestal installed for its La Plata display;
- a reconstructed bow made and attached in 2021 from photographic evidence;
- no securely documented replacement arrow.

Condition after the 2021 intervention has not been reported in a detailed conservation dossier accessible through the sources used here. Continued outdoor exposure means atmospheric soiling, chlorides, humidity, vandalism, and failure of protective coatings remain foreseeable risks, but no new damage should be asserted without inspection.

No current ownership, repatriation, restitution, or deaccession dispute was found. This is not a known case of wartime seizure, colonial removal, illicit excavation, or foreign export.

## 9. Modern Reception

### Public image

The monument’s public identity is now determined by three intertwined images:

1. Troiani’s dynamic heroic figure;
2. the conspicuous absence—and later reconstruction—of the bow;
3. the apparent aim toward La Plata Cathedral.

For decades, photographs of the bowless figure invited viewers to imagine the lost implement and its target. The missing component thus became part of the monument’s received form, even though it was not Troiani’s intended state.

### Scholarship and art criticism

Eduardo Migo’s 2015 analysis treats the work as a highly accomplished solution to sculptural movement: a stable visual triangle containing explosive muscular action. That formal analysis helped support the 2021 reconstruction carried out under his direction.

### Urban folklore and tourism

Local journalism, blogs, walking tours, and tourism sites have made the anti-Cathedral legend the principal narrative through which many visitors encounter the object. More recent tourism presentations attempt to redirect the story toward Heracles and the Stymphalian birds. Both approaches increase the statue’s visibility, but the former frequently blurs the 1882 foundation of La Plata with the 1970 installation.

### Political and anonymous reuse

The anti-Church graffiti documented by 2020 materially reinscribed the legend’s religious conflict onto the monument. Whatever the unidentified writer intended, the phrase “Fuego a la iglesia” made the alleged conflict explicit in a way the 1970 acquisition record does not.

The gift-wrapping action temporarily transformed the bronze into a concealed public package. Academic analysis treats it not as an isolated prank but as one layer among repeated anonymous interactions with the monument. No reliable source attributes the act to an artist, collective, political organization, or publicity campaign.

### Replicas and confusion with Bourdelle

There is no verified commercial or public replica of Troiani’s exact sculpture in the located record. The Bourdelle bronze in Plaza Dante is frequently described as its “copy,” reversing the actual relationship of influence. Bourdelle’s composition existed first and circulated internationally in several variants and casts; Troiani made his own related work in 1924.

### Digital circulation

Online photographs and articles routinely use *El Arquero Divino*, a title that may be later than *El Arquero*. The federal catalogue’s adoption of the popular name has effectively institutionalized it alongside *Hércules Arquero*.

No consequential use on currency, postage, official arms, flags, or political-party emblems was found.

## 10. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Principal evidentiary groups

**1. Contemporary press**

The strongest located evidence for municipal acquisition is *El Día*, 10 December 1970. The original issue should be consulted directly to confirm the complete wording, photograph, caption, and whether the acquisition date or price appeared elsewhere in that edition.

**2. Municipal inventories**

Later local histories cite an *Inventario y catalogación de Esculturas* and *La Plata. Paseo de esculturas*, both municipal publications. These appear to underlie the reported 10 October 1970 incorporation date, but accessible complete copies were not located.

**3. Conservation research**

The technical report *Restauración y puesta en valor de esculturas metálicas* is the most important material source. It establishes the Trivium casting, material, date, installation context, atmospheric risks, corrosion, graffiti, missing bow, and proposed treatment. Its publication metadata are incomplete in the web presentation, and it precedes the 2021 replacement.

**4. Formal analysis**

Eduardo Migo’s 2015 article is the principal object-centered art-historical reading.

**5. Italian biographical scholarship**

Giuseppe Bergamini’s entry in the *Dizionario Biografico dei Friulani* connects *Arciere* to Troiani’s 1925 solo exhibition and later prize. It also clarifies Troiani’s Italian origins and Argentine career.

**6. Federal legal and catalogue records**

Decree 769/2019 and its schedule establish the current national protective status, official name *Hércules Arquero*, attribution, location, and coordinates. The individual federal web record notably leaves technique, dimensions, and description blank; this silence should not be filled with Bourdelle’s measurements.

**7. Local legend literature**

Gualberto Reynal’s 1993 book and derivative journalism shaped the Masonic interpretation. These sources are evidence for the legend’s history, not for a Masonic commission.

### Major unresolved questions

- What document records Troiani’s 1924 commission or decision to create the work?
- Does the Trivium foundry archive survive, and does it contain a casting ledger, alloy specification, price, mold number, or edition count?
- Where was the bronze from 1925 until 1970?
- Was it still owned by Troiani when he died in 1963?
- From whom did La Plata acquire it?
- What was the purchase price and legal instrument?
- Does “10 October 1970” mark acquisition, accession, or installation?
- At which municipal industrial-arts exhibition did it win a prize?
- Is the 1928 Bahía Blanca first prize the same award or another one?
- When precisely were the original bow and arrow removed?
- Were they stolen together, deliberately cut away, removed for safety, or lost in separate events?
- Do fragments survive in municipal storage?
- What were the original bow and arrow made of?
- What metal and attachment method were used in the 2021 bow?
- Does a Troiani plaster, maquette, or second bronze survive?
- What signature and foundry marks appear on the La Plata bronze?
- What are its exact measurements and weight?

### Discredited or unsupported theories still circulating

- **Founders’ placement:** chronologically impossible; installation occurred 88 years after the city’s foundation.
- **Documented Masonic commission:** no supporting commission or placement record has been produced.
- **Recoleta duplicate by Troiani:** the known Recoleta work is by Bourdelle.
- **Original bow restored in 2021:** false; the installed bow was reconstructed from photographs.
- **Exact theft in 1979:** 1979 is firmly a date by which the bow was absent, but the removal may have occurred earlier.
- **Bourdelle dimensions applied to Troiani:** unsupported; the works are separate objects.

## 12. Sources

### Primary, official, and near-primary sources

- **Argentina, Poder Ejecutivo Nacional. Decree 769/2019**, signed 13 November and published 14 November 2019. Establishes the legal protective framework.
- **National protected-sculpture schedule.** Lists *Hércules Arquero* by Troiano Troiani as item 27 and supplies its official location and coordinates.
- **Argentina.gob.ar individual object record.** Uses *El arquero divino*, names Troiani, Plaza Moreno, and the two federal heritage categories; leaves physical-description fields blank.
- ***El Día*, 10 December 1970, “Obra de arte en Plaza Moreno.”** Contemporary acquisition and installation notice, accessed through a transcription and reproduced photograph in a later local-history source.
- ***El Día*, 20 August 2020.** Contemporary report on the anonymous wrapping and restoration planning.
- ***El Día*, 16 October 2021.** Contemporary account of the replacement bow, its photograph-based reconstruction, responsible municipal team, and pedestal chronology.
- **Buenos Aires official monument guide.** Identifies the Recoleta/Plaza Dante bronze as a replica of Bourdelle’s work, helping distinguish it from Troiani’s statue.
- **Musée d’Orsay and Musée Bourdelle catalogues.** Establish Bourdelle’s prototype history, versions, material, dimensions, inscriptions, and iconography.

### Technical and scholarly sources

- **“Restauración y puesta en valor de esculturas metálicas.”** UNLP-hosted technical report documenting the Trivium casting, material, deterioration, atmospheric environment, graffiti, missing bow, and proposed conservation.
- **Eduardo Migo, “‘El Arquero’: Análisis de la obra escultórica de la plaza Moreno,” 2015.** Formal analysis of the figure’s composition.
- **Giuseppe Bergamini, “Troiani Troiano,” Dizionario Biografico dei Friulani.** Italian scholarly biography documenting the 1925 solo exhibition and subsequent prize.
- **La Plata architectural and symbolic study.** Provides an early scholarly interpretation of Heracles, the birds, and the statue’s function in the urban landscape.
- **UNLP conference proceedings, “Un arquero envuelto para regalo.”** Analyzes the multiple anonymous interventions visible in the 2020 photograph.
- **Hiram Abif, no. 111, 2009.** Documents the diffusion and rebuttal of the Masonic-placement legend.

### Source limitations

The documentary base is substantially stronger after 1970 than before it. No accessible commission, foundry ledger, sale invoice, municipal purchase resolution, accession record, police complaint, estate inventory, or complete modern conservation report was located. The 45-year interval between the 1925 exhibition and municipal acquisition remains a genuine provenance gap. Exact dimensions, weight, inscriptions, alloy, original accessory construction, edition size, purchase price, and the present fate of the original bow and arrow remain unestablished.
