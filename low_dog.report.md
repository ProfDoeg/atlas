# Low Dog (Šúŋka Khúčiyela, Perro Bajo): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Oglala Lakota war chief; present at the Battle of the Little Bighorn, 25 June 1876; author of one of the earliest Native accounts of that battle to reach print.*

**Method.** Worked from a verified research pool (four lenses: chronology, geography, quotations, afterlife), adversarially checked against the underlying sources. Overturned first-pass findings are recorded here, not silently absorbed. Disagreeing sources are both printed and neither adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference from general pattern.

---

## 1. The name, and the problem of the name

**[A]** Lakota name recorded as *Šúŋka Khúčiyela* / *Sunka Khuciyela* / *Sunka kucigala*. The 1881 Standing Rock agency census writes "Sunka kucigala Low Dog." Corpus name: **Perro Bajo**.

**[A]** The 1900 and 1910 federal censuses at Standing Rock enter him as **"Joshua Low Dog"** (american-tribes.com, citing both censuses).

**[R], and a first-pass claim collapses.** The pool carried the alias **"Phil Cosgrove"** as [A], dated 1885. The name appears only in Wikipedia's uncited lead and infobox; none of Wikipedia's five references supports it; it is in none of the american-tribes.com census records; the 1885 date was invented by the first pass, self-admitted in its own note. A downstream pool entry claiming agency documents carry the name has no source at all. **Kept out of the journey file entirely.**

### Tribal affiliation: five labels, none adjudicated

| Label | Source |
|---|---|
| Oglala | Wikipedia, "Low Dog"; He Dog's own recorded account ("Low Dog was an Ogalalla") |
| Siha Sapa (Blackfeet Lakota) | Sprague, *Standing Rock Sioux* (2004), p. 56: "Low Dog is always listed as an Oglala but is a Siha Sapa" |
| Sans Arc | 1910 Standing Rock census. **Correction:** the pool said "1900 and 1910"; only the **1910** entry carries the label, the 1900 entry carries none. |
| Minneconjou | indigenouspeople.net, in an article titled "Low-Dog, **Oglala** Sioux Chief" |
| "northern chief... of the Lame Deer band" | William Garnett, Ricker interview, 1907 |

**Not resolved.** The journey file names him Oglala where the sources overwhelmingly do, and gives the contradiction its own stop (the 1910 census) rather than burying it.

**[R]: one pool claim dropped.** A pool note asserted the Leavenworth headline "misdescribes Low Dog as 'A Hunkpapa Sioux'." Unverifiable: every located reprint and transcript frames him as Oglala (Welker's transcript is headed "Low-Dog, Oglala Sioux Chief"). Dropped; absent from the journey file.

## 2. Birth and youth (c. 1846 to c. 1860)

**[A, year only]** Born among the Oglala. Wikipedia: "c. 1846". indigenouspeople.net: "born ca. 1847". The two do not agree and neither gives a month.

**[R]** **Birthplace unrecorded.** No source names a camp, river, or district. The journey pins the birth in the Powder River hunting country (45.8, -105.6) as a reconstruction from Oglala range in the period, flagged as such in `date_confidence`.

**[A]** First war path at about fourteen, against Assiniboine hunting buffalo, on a stream above the mouth of the Yellowstone, and made a war chief for it (his own 1881 account; Wikipedia; indigenouspeople.net).

**Contradiction found in this pass, flagged and not resolved: one man or two.** His own 1881 account says *"I killed one of their men."* Wikipedia and indigenouspeople.net credit him with **two** killed in the single fight, which is the count on which the chieftainship at fourteen is usually explained. The pool, and the first draft of this report, printed **two** as [A] and cited his own account for it; **his own account does not say two.** No source reconciles the counts. The journey stop now prints both and attributes each.

**Correction.** The pool's geography lens placed this fight on the **Milk River** (48.55, -106.75), **wrong by roughly 300 km** and self-contradicting the chronology lens in the same pool. The source places it on a stream above the Yellowstone's mouth: approximately **47.99, -104.00**. The Milk River appears in no source.

## 3. Summer 1876, before the battle

**[A]** The Sun Dance at Deer Medicine Rocks on the Rosebud, early June 1876: Sitting Bull's hundred cuts and the vision of soldiers falling into camp.

**[R]** **No source places Low Dog at the rocks.** The journey states the gathering as fact and states that no account names him there.

**Coordinate discrepancy, left standing.** Wikipedia's "Deer Medicine Rocks" gives **45.6950, -106.6917**; `sitting_bull.journey.json` pins the same Sun Dance at **45.35, -106.75**. The journey **inherits the canonical atlas pin**. Fixing this is an atlas-wide edit, not a Low Dog edit.

**[R]** Battle of the Rosebud, 17 June 1876. **No source places him on that field, and none places him elsewhere that day.** An [R] stop at the canonical pin, with the absence stated inside the campa.

**[A]** The combined village: roughly **1,800 lodges** on the **west** bank (Weir's estimate, per Wikipedia). **Correction:** the pool's coordinate, 45.565, **-107.428**, sits on the **east** bluffs while its own text says west bank. The journey uses the canonical village pin, **45.5646, -107.4319**.

## 4. The day: 25 June 1876

Everything [A] here rests, directly or through Graham, on **Low Dog's own interview**, *Leavenworth Weekly Times*, 18 August 1881, reprinted in W. A. Graham, *The Custer Myth* (Stackpole, 1953), pp. 74-76. **Graham's pagination was not independently verified**; it is carried from the pool.

**[A]** *"We were in camp near Little Big Horn river. We had lost some horses, and an Indian went back on the trail to look for them."* (Pool had "Little Bighorn" as one word; every reprint reads three. Corrected.)

**[A]** *"I was asleep in my lodge at the time. The sun was about noon (pointing with his finger). I heard the alarm, but I did not believe it. I thought it was a false alarm."* The stage direction is genuinely in the transcript, and Captain Howe is named in the article as interpreter. (Pool closed the parenthesis after the period; sources close it before. Corrected.)

**[A]** Reno met at the southern end of the village and driven back across the river; his own account is the primary source for his presence in the valley fight. *"I do not say that Reno was a coward. He fought well, but our men were fighting to save their women and children, and drive them back."*

**[A], but re-sourced.** Low Dog at the Medicine Tail Coulee ford. **The 1881 narrative contains no ford or crossing reference at all.** The pool credited the placement to Low Dog; it is **John Stands In Timber** who puts him there ("They had been back and forth between the two fights... They were just leaving the Reno fight to go down toward Custer"). **Correction:** the pool's ford coordinate, 45.5765, -107.4436, is **north of Last Stand Hill**, reversing the battle geography, since Custer descends into the coulee and then withdraws **north**. Corrected to about **45.560, -107.442**.

**[A], and a tag reversed.** *"This is a good day to die: follow me."* The pool tagged this **[R]** on the ground that "Wikipedia flags the attribution." A misreading: the line is **verbatim in the 1881 primary source**, and Wikipedia's `{{better source needed}}` tag (March 2026) attaches to **its own weak citation to indigenouspeople.net**. Corrected to **[A]**. The **colon** is in the 1881 text where the online form substitutes a period, and the cry belongs to **the charge against the second column** ("They came on us like a thunderbolt"), not the final encirclement. The journey places it at the charge, with the colon.

**[A]** On the soldiers: *"I never before nor since saw men so brave and fearless as those white warriors."* · *"No white man or Indian ever fought as bravely as Custer and his men,"* which follows his statement that he does not know who killed Custer and did not know Custer was on the field.

**[A]** Losses: *"Thirty-eight, who died then, and a great many—I can't tell the number—who were wounded and died afterwards."* (Pool corrupted the dashes to "the number-who"; corrected against Graham, Utley, Upton, Tillett. Em dashes inside a verbatim quotation are untouched by the corpus prose rule.)

**[A]** His full brother is killed. **Correction:** the pool placed this at **Deep Ravine**, tagged [A]. **No source places the brother at Deep Ravine or on the ridge.** In his own account the brother is one of three richly dressed dead laid out in tepees **in the village**: *"One was his own brother, born of the same mother and the same father, and he did not know who the other two were."*

**[A]** On Sitting Bull's part in the fight: *"If some one would lend him a heart he would fight."* The newspaper states plainly that Low Dog is not friendly to Sitting Bull.

## 5. Scattering, surrender, exile (1876-1881)

**[R]** Dispersal of the camp toward the Bighorn foothills and Powder River country. **Low Dog's movements in these weeks are not documented.**

**The winter of 1876-77 is the largest hole in the record. Three sources fill it three incompatible ways:**

1. **[R]** He stays out among the Powder River holdouts (the pool's first-pass reconstruction).
2. **[A]** He surrenders in May 1877 and leaves for Canada later (Hardorff, via american-tribes.com).
3. **[A]** He enrolls at Spotted Tail Agency on **4 September 1877** with the surrendering Lame Deer village (Spotted Tail ledger p. 64, via american-tribes.com).

The pool's first pass claimed "no source consulted names Low Dog specifically in this interval." **Refuted:** american-tribes.com names him specifically and repeatedly, which reduces version 1 to the weakest of the three. **Not resolved.** The journey gives the interval its own stop and prints all three.

**[A]** Enrollment at Spotted Tail Agency, Beaver Creek, by Camp Sheridan, 4 September 1877 (42.852, -102.7442; verified). **[A]** Autumn 1877 removal of the Spotted Tail and Red Cloud bands east to the Missouri; route not fixed by any source. **[A]** He leads a breakout from the column and turns north for the British line. Garnett, 1907: *"Low Dog, a northern chief an upstart who belonged to the Lame Deer band and was a brave man, he led these Indians off."* The point of the break is unnamed.

**[R]** Crossing into Canada, joining the Wood Mountain exile community. **Correction:** the pool labelled 49.3708, -106.3836 "international boundary near Wood Mountain." That coordinate is **Wood Mountain village**, some 30 km **north** of the 49th parallel. Coordinate kept, label corrected.

**[R]** Fort Walsh. **No source places him there by name.** **Correction:** pool gave 49.5322, -109.8371; Wikipedia gives **49.5727, -109.8815**. The post was NWMP headquarters 1878-1882, so the window holds.

**[A]** Finerty, 1879, at the exile camp: *"brave and sullen. He had little to say, but was a man of considerable action when it came to blows."* **The only contemporary physical description of Low Dog that survives**, written by a newspaperman.

**[A]** Surrender at about 34, through Fort Buford (47.9864, -104.0014) to Standing Rock. **Month contested within the pool:** the quotations lens says "his May 1881 surrender"; the geography lens gives only "1880-1881" and states no exact date is recorded. Sitting Bull surrenders his rifle at the same fort on 19 July 1881.

## 6. The account in print, and the agency years (1881-1889)

**[A]** Interview given to army officers through Captain Howe; published in the *Leavenworth Weekly Times*, **18 August 1881**. **Correction:** the pool pinned Leavenworth at 39.3111, -95.0031, about 7 km west of the city and off the river bank it sits on. Corrected to **39.3228, -94.9242**.

**[A]** Closing line: *"Now, I want to learn the white man's way, for I see that he is stronger than we are, and that his government is better than ours."* **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** indigenouspeople.net's version stops at *"stronger than we are"*, and **"his government is better than ours" does not appear in the retrieved text of that source**; it also carries a separate fragment, *"wise thinking and not so much fighting."* The quotations lens carries the full sentence, via Graham. The journey uses the quotations-lens text; the split between the two source chains is recorded here.

**[A]** December 1881 Standing Rock census: Low Dog, 34; wife **Medicine**, 35; two sons, **One Who Makes Disorder** and **Horse**; twenty-two families of his band listed.

**[A]** Transfer with the other Oglala to Pine Ridge, **May 1882**. **Correction:** pool gave 43.3533, -102.0892, the infobox centroid for the **reservation**, about 55 km from the agency at Pine Ridge village (**43.026, -102.556**), and called the agency "newly established"; it dated from **1878**, and the reservation was not created in law until **2 March 1889**. Both corrected.

**[A]** 1884: Red Dog's crayon ledger drawing of Low Dog's valor (Wikipedia), placed by the source at Standing Rock **two years after the rolls move him to Pine Ridge**. Tension noted, not resolved.

**[R]** He leaves Pine Ridge before 1886. The source registers the departure and nothing else: no destination, no reason, no date beyond the year. **Several years are blank.**

## 7. 1890, Fort Snelling, Wounded Knee

**[A]** 1890: arrested at Cherry Creek, Cheyenne River reservation, charged with attempting to take Big Foot's young son by force over the boy's refusal to attend school. The sources give the charge and nothing further: no hearing, no outcome, no statement from him.

**[A]** Imprisoned at Fort Snelling (44.8928, -93.1806; verified), a few months, released on the local Indian agent's letter. **Correction:** the pool called him "one of the Lakota men removed east from the reservations during the unrest of that winter." **Unsupported conflation** with the Ghost Dance prisoners sent to Fort Sheridan, Illinois; the source ties the imprisonment **solely to the kidnapping arrest**. Removed.

**[R]** Wounded Knee, 29 December 1890 (43.1462, -102.3555; canonical pin). **Low Dog is not there.** He is in the Minnesota cell. The journey includes the pin as an absence and sets beside it the fact that the boy he was arrested for taking was Big Foot's son and Big Foot dies on this creek. **No source draws that connection**; the two facts are printed next to each other, not fused.

**[A]** August 1891: escorted from Fort Snelling to Fort Bennett at the mouth of the Cheyenne River (44.6717, -100.6406) and returned to his people.

## 8. Death, and the records that run past it

| Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Died 14 November 1894 | Wikipedia infobox | **Uncited**; its `death_place` field is **empty** |
| Died 1894 at **Cheyenne River Agency** | Hardorff, *The Death of Crazy Horse*, p. 86, via american-tribes.com | Supplies **year and place, not the day** |
| Died 1896 | Hardorff, elsewhere | "frustratingly, gives two very different years" |
| Died 1910 | Hardorff, elsewhere again | as above |
| Alive at Standing Rock 1900, "Joshua Low Dog," 52 | 1900 U.S. Census | Six years after |
| Alive at Standing Rock 1910, "Joshua Low Dog," 61, widowed, Sans Arc | 1910 U.S. Census | Sixteen years after |
| Resident at Standing Rock through at least 1920 | indigenouspeople.net | Twenty-six years after |

**Corrections.** (a) The pool gave the death place as "Standing Rock Reservation, North Dakota," sourced to Wikipedia. **Wikipedia does not say this.** The only source naming a place is Hardorff: **Cheyenne River Agency**. The day, 14 November, comes only from the uncited infobox and is **not** attributable to Hardorff, as the geography lens wrongly did. (b) The pool presented the 1900 household as an **1890 Cherry Creek** record with children "Luke, Louise, Jessica, and Regina." There is **no Regina**, "Jessica" is **Jessie**, and the listing is the **1900 Standing Rock** census. (c) The pool located the 1910 entry at **Cheyenne River Agency**; it is the **1910 Standing Rock** census, and as written it manufactured a residence no source supports. Journey pins it at Fort Yates.

**[A]** 1900 household: wife **Louise** (Lakota name **Tiolutawin**); children **Louise 18, Luke 15, Jessie 13**.

**Gap, stated as a gap.** No grave, tomb, marked burial, or cemetery record located. **Find a Grave returned HTTP 403** and could not be checked; other burial-record sources returned HTTP and certificate errors. No burial site is named in Wikipedia, indigenouspeople.net, or american-tribes.com. This is an **unchecked absence, not a demonstrated one**.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A]** Photographs from the surrender period: D. F. Barry portraits, 1881; an 1880s albumen cabinet card; **Edward Augustus Bromley, 1891**, likely during the Fort Snelling imprisonment. One earlier portrait is taken to suggest he travelled to Canada before surrendering.

**[R]** **No monument, statue, or dedicated memorial to Low Dog was located.** The Little Bighorn Indian Memorial commemorates Native combatants **collectively**, and the red granite warrior markers are reserved for those documented as **killed**, which excludes survivors. Cross-checked against general knowledge of the marker program, not a claim from a named source.

**[A]** **Carideo Low Dog of Dupree, South Dakota**, Cheyenne River Sioux tribal member, recorded as the chief's great-grandson (Sprague, p. 56, via american-tribes.com). **Correction:** pool put Dupree at 44.9067, -101.5989; Wikipedia gives **45.0494, -101.6014**.

## 10. Atlas interlocks

Low Dog was **already named in the corpus before this research began**; all four items are **[A]**. `sitting_bull.journey.json`, "Custer's column destroyed": *"Low Dog, whom this atlas keeps under his own name of Perro Bajo, fights in the same hour"*, its `sources` field citing **QUEUE.md line 158**, so the atlas flagged this as a *planned* interlock. **QUEUE.md line 158**: "Oglala Lakota chief at Little Bighorn beside Sitting Bull." **`census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md`, lines 427-430**: Perro Bajo, Sitting Bull, General Custer, Coronel Reno listed consecutively as the four Little Bighorn figures tracked, and **Custer and Reno hold no journey file**. `sitting_bull.journey.json`, "Fort Randall": *"Low Dog and Gall have already given their versions to the press"*, so the Leavenworth account conditions how Sitting Bull's own version is received.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical** from `sitting_bull.journey.json`: Little Bighorn battlefield/village **45.5646, -107.4319** (5 stops); Deer Medicine Rocks **45.35, -106.75**; Rosebud battlefield **45.2231, -106.9978**; Wounded Knee Creek **43.1462, -102.3555**; Fort Buford **47.9864, -104.0014**; Fort Walsh **49.5727, -109.8815**.

**Offered by the interlock lens and deliberately NOT used.** **Kaaba** (21.4225, 39.8262) and **Temple Mount** (31.778, 35.2354), offered for house-style contrast writing: no documented link, absent. **Buenos Aires** (-34.6037, -58.3816), offered as a Roca parallel: not a waypoint, so Roca is named once in campa instead. **Paris** (48.8566, 2.3522), offered via the Buffalo Bill cluster: nothing places Low Dog on that tour.

**Travelers named in campa (mutual gaze).** `sitting_bull`, at nine stops. The density is earned: same camp June 1876, same Canadian exile, same surrender season, same press cycle, and Low Dog's low opinion of him is on the record. `julio_argentino_roca`, once, at Fort Buford 1881, as contemporaneity and not as crossing.

## 11. Contradictions carried forward, unresolved

1. **Birth year**: 1846 vs 1847.
2. **Tribal affiliation**: Oglala vs Siha Sapa vs Sans Arc vs Minneconjou vs Lame Deer band.
3. **Winter 1876-77**: Powder River holdout vs May 1877 surrender vs September 1877 enrollment.
4. **Death year**: 1894 vs 1896 vs 1910, against census entries in 1900 and 1910 and a residence claim to 1920. Whether two men have been conflated is a question no source addresses.
5. **Death place**: Cheyenne River Agency (Hardorff) vs unstated (Wikipedia) vs the pool's erroneous "Standing Rock."
6. **1884 residence**: the Red Dog drawing sits at Standing Rock two years after the transfer to Pine Ridge.
7. **The closing quotation**: whether "his government is better than ours" belongs to the sentence.
8. **Deer Medicine Rocks coordinate**: 45.35, -106.75 (atlas canon) vs 45.6950, -106.6917 (Wikipedia).
9. **Surrender month**: May 1881 (quotations lens) vs "1880-1881, no exact date" (geography lens).
10. **The first war path**: one Assiniboine killed (his own 1881 account) vs two (Wikipedia, indigenouspeople.net, and the usual explanation of the chieftainship at fourteen).

## 12. Gaps, stated as gaps

Exact birthplace and date · the council that made him a chief at fourteen · his whereabouts through the winter of 1876-77 · the point on the removal march where he broke the column · whether he was ever inside Fort Walsh · everything between leaving Pine Ridge before 1886 and appearing at Cherry Creek by 1890 · the outcome of the 1890 charge · cause of death, exact location, burial site · whether the "Joshua Low Dog" of 1900 and 1910 is the same man.
