# Pierre-François-Xavier Bouchard (1771-1822): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-09 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: the French engineer officer who found the Rosetta Stone at Fort Julien in July 1799 and had nothing further to do with it. This report is the evidence layer under the journey file and does not share its register.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or inference, asserted as such and no further. Contradictions are flagged and not adjudicated unless the adjudication rests on a document, which is then named. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**Standing caution.** No monograph on Bouchard was reachable. The biography rests on two tertiary encyclopedia articles, themselves compressions of nineteenth-century French military dictionaries not consulted directly. The one older text used, Budge (1913), is secondary and wrong about Bouchard in at least two particulars.

## 1. Jura origins and the Republic's schools (1771-1798)

Born **29 April 1771 at Orgelet, Jura**, youngest of seven children of Pierre Bouchard, master joiner, merchant and teacher, and Pierrette Janet de Cressia **[A: en/fr Wikipedia]**.

*Coordinate contradiction, resolved.* The pool carries two Orgelet pins a kilometre apart, 46.5169/5.6206 and 46.5217/5.6114; the fr.wikipedia gazetteer gives 46.5231, 5.6111. The atlas uses **46.5217, 5.6114 [A: fr.wikipedia, Orgelet (Jura)]**.

Attends the **collège of Orgelet through the rhetoric class**, then **two years at Besançon** on philosophy and mathematics **[A for both]**. No years are given for the collège, and the journey file's 1785 is a placeholder; the pool puts Besançon at about 1789-1791 **[R for both datings]**.

**1793: sergeant-major, Grenadiers de Paris**, then Champagne and Belgium **[A]**; month and day unrecorded. **1794: École Nationale d'Aérostatique at Meudon** under **Nicolas-Jacques Conté**, promoted lieutenant of aérostiers, **eyes damaged in a hydrogen explosion** during one of Conté's experiments **[A]**. The most consequential detail outside Rosetta survives only in encyclopedia summary; no contemporary account of the accident was located. **Gap.**

**21 November 1796: enters the École polytechnique** at the Hôtel de Lassay, studying descriptive geometry under **Gaspard Monge [A; the Lassay location from fr.wikipedia, École polytechnique]**.

*Contradiction, not resolved.* French Wikipedia has him **completing his examinations at the polytechnique in November 1798**, when he had been attached to the Egyptian expedition since 20 April 1798 and had sailed from Toulon on 19 May. Both statements cannot describe a man in Paris. Examinations in absentia, a cohort-dated record, or plain error are all speculation and none is documented. **Flagged, not adjudicated**; no stop carries this event.

**20 April 1798: attached as lieutenant** to the expeditionary force. **23 April 1798: marries Marie-Élisabeth Bergère**, five years his junior **[A, both to the day]**. The pool's "days before the expedition sails" understates the interval: the sailing was 19 May, four weeks later.

## 2. Egypt: fleet, Delta, Institut (1798)

**19 May 1798: embarks at Toulon aboard the *Franklin* [A]**. **10-12 June: Malta taken** from the Order of Saint John **[A: en.wikipedia, French campaign in Egypt and Syria]**; his personal presence there is not separately attested, but he is aboard the fleet. **1-2 July: the fleet raises Alexandria and the city falls**, all troops ashore by 3-4 July **[A]**.

*Date contradiction, resolved.* English Wikipedia's Bouchard article gives his landing as **4 July 1799**. That is a year-typo: the same article dates his attachment to 20 April 1798 and his embarkation to 19 May 1798, and he found the stone in July 1799. A 1799 landing is impossible. The atlas uses **1798** **[A: en.wikipedia, French campaign in Egypt and Syria]**.

**21 July 1798: the Battle of the Pyramids**, fought at **Imbaba on the Nile**, some 29 km north of the pyramids **[A]**. *Coordinate correction:* the pool's 29.9773, 31.1325 is the Great Pyramid of Giza and matches neither the label "Cairo" nor the battle; the atlas uses **30.077, 31.207**. Bouchard's unit moves with the column, but **his presence at the battle is not separately attested [R]**, and the campa says so in its own text.

**22 August 1798: Bonaparte founds the Institut d'Égypte** in a requisitioned Cairo palace, seating Monge, Berthollet, Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire and Denon **[A: napoleon.journey.json; en.wikipedia, Rosetta Stone]**. **28 November 1798: promoted engineer lieutenant [A]**, folded into that campa rather than given a stop.

## 3. Fort Julien and the stone (July to September 1799)

This is where the sources actively disagree, and the disagreement is the substance of the section.

**The find.** Supervising the rebuilding of the old fort at Rashid that the French had renamed **Fort Julien**, Bouchard, under **Colonel d'Hautpoul**, notices an inscribed dark slab in a wall being demolished, carrying three scripts including Greek **[A: en.wikipedia Rosetta Stone and Bouchard; fr.wikipedia Pierre de Rosette]**.

*Date contradiction, NOT resolved.* Three candidate datings stand:
1. **15 or 19 July 1799**, English Wikipedia's Bouchard article, which declines to choose **[A as a statement of uncertainty]**;
2. **19 July 1799** specifically, French sources;
3. **August 1799**, Budge 1913, verbatim: "He made his great discovery in August, 1799."

Budge's August is hard to square with Lancret's 19 July report to the Institut, which the same body of sources attests. The atlas takes **15 July** as the earliest candidate, records all three in `date_confidence`, and does not close the question.

**The report to Menou.** Bouchard and d'Hautpoul inform **General Jacques-François Menou**, then at Rosetta **[A]**. **No verbatim text of Bouchard's own report was located anywhere.** This is the central absence of the dossier and why the discoverer has no voice in his own journey. In its place stands Budge's paraphrase: "Finding that there were on one side of the Stone lines of strange characters, which it was thought might be writing, as well as long lines of Greek letters, Boussard reported his discovery to General Menou, who ordered him to bring the Stone to his house in Alexandria." **[A as Budge's wording, R as to the facts reported]**

**Budge is unreliable on Bouchard**, by his own admission and beyond it: "The actual finder of the Stone was a French Officer of Engineers, whose name is sometimes spelt Boussard, and sometimes Bouchard, who subsequently rose to the rank of General, and was alive in 1814." He is unsure of the name, and Bouchard never became a general; he died a chef de bataillon serving as engineer in chief. The journey file uses the quote as evidence of how badly the finder was recorded, not as evidence about him.

**19 July 1799: Michel-Ange Lancret announces the find to the Institut d'Égypte [A]**. **Mid-August 1799: Bouchard conducts the stone to Cairo [A: fr.wikipedia, Pierre de Rosette,** "Bouchard est missionné pour conduire la pierre au Caire, où il arrive vers le milieu du mois d'août"**]**. Budge has Menou sending it to **Alexandria**, and elsewhere has **Napoleon** ordering it to Cairo: three accounts of who sent it where. **Flagged, not resolved.**

**Conté prints from the stone**, inking the incised face so the text can travel to Europe as paper; copies circulate from 1800, two lithographers, "citizens Marcel and Galland," having been brought from Paris for the copying **[A: en.wikipedia, Rosetta Stone; the lithographers as Budge's wording]**. **September 1799: first public notice, unsigned, in the *Courrier de l'Égypte* [A]**.

*Quotation contradiction, corrected.* The pool attributed the English phrase "might one day be the key to deciphering hieroglyphs" to the *Courrier*. It is **English Wikipedia's own paraphrase**, and the footnote beside it (n. 10, "Cracking", p. 20) supports a different statement entirely. French Wikipedia gives the underlying line as "Cette pierre offre un grand intérêt pour l'étude des caractères hiéroglyphiques, peut-être même en donnera-t-elle enfin la clef." The journey file quotes that French sentence, attributed to the anonymous notice as given in French Wikipedia. The original issue could not be fetched: no accessible digitised copy was located, so the sentence reaches the atlas at one remove.

## 4. Captivities in the East (December 1799 to July 1801)

**Count of captures.** French Wikipedia distinguishes them precisely: twice by the Ottomans in Egypt, three times by the English **[A]**. The pool's "captured twice by Ottoman forces, exact dates not specified, 1799" under-specified and misdated this. Five captures in thirteen years, on three continents.

**El-Arish, late December 1799.** The fort's French garrison of about 250 mutinied and opened the town to the Ottomans; Bouchard is taken there **[A for the capture; December dating from en.wikipedia, Convention of El Arish]**.

*Sequencing correction.* The pool placed this at "the collapse of the El-Arish armistice" in January 1800, reversing cause and effect: the fort fell in late December 1799, and its fall helped prompt the Convention of 24 January.

*Attribution correction.* The pool credited **Sidney Smith** with refusing the El-Arish terms. It was **Admiral Lord Keith**, under orders forbidding separate Ottoman-French treaties; Smith negotiated the convention and supported it, though he did not sign it **[A: en.wikipedia, Convention of El Arish]**.

**Damascus: 42 days' imprisonment** after that capture **[A]**. The pool dates it 1799 in one entry and 1800 in another; since the fort fell in late December 1799 the 42 days run into 1800, which the atlas uses while recording the disagreement **[R on the exact window]**.

**1 May 1800: promoted captain of engineers [A to the day]**, place within Egypt unspecified and Cairo used as marker **[R on place]**. **1801: captured a second time at the surrender of Fort Julien [A for fact and year; R for the month]**. **30 July 1801: lands at Marseille [A to the day]**, so he is already home when the Capitulation of Alexandria transfers the stone later that year; the journey file therefore puts that surrender in its closing segment, not in his line of march.

## 5. Saint-Domingue and Jamaica (1801-1804)

*Correction, three years wide.* The pool bundled expedition and internment under a single 1801 date. They are two events three years apart: **14 December 1801, the fleet sails from Brest [A]**; **November to December 1803, the French position collapses after Vertières (18 November 1803) and Bouchard is captured on the colony's surrender [A: en.wikipedia, Saint-Domingue expedition]**; **August 1804, released on parole from Jamaica [A]**.

*Coordinate correction.* The pool pinned the 1803 capture at 19.076, -72.3074, empty mountain terrain near Marmelade, unconnected to anything in his life. The atlas uses **Cap-Français / Cap-Haïtien, 19.7574, -72.2014** for both posting and surrender.

**Yellow fever** contracted in the colony **[A for the fact; R for date and station]**: neither port nor posting is specified anywhere, and Cap-Français and Kingston both stand as markers, which the campas state in their own text.

**Family.** A **daughter born 1802 [A for the year only]** and a **son born "around 1807" [R, the sources' own hedge]**. Neither child is named anywhere. **Gap**, recorded as such in the journey file.

## 6. The Vendée and seven years in the Peninsula (1805-1812)

**1805-1807: construction oversight in the Vendée**, including at **Napoléon-Vendée**, later La Roche-sur-Yon **[R: the pool tags this a reconstruction and gives no detail]**.

**1807: enters Spain**, seven years under **Dupont, Soult, Masséna, Marmont [A for the service; R for the crossing point and date]**. The Bidasoa pass at 43.3183, -1.7813 is the army's route and not a documented position of his, which the campa states.

**Bailén, 22 July 1808.** *Apparent contradiction, dissolved.* The battle ran 16-19 July; Dupont's capitulation was signed 22 July; English Wikipedia says Bouchard was taken "after Dupont's surrender at Baylen on 22 July 1808." These are a battle and its consequence three days apart, not two sources in conflict **[A]**. Coordinates 38.0975, -3.7754 confirmed.

**Cádiz Bay hulks, 1808 [R, flagged in the campa as open].** Most Bailén prisoners were held on hulks there and many then deported to Cabrera. Whether Bouchard passed through them, or was exchanged early, is not established anywhere; he is fighting in Portugal within a year, which very few Cabrera prisoners ever did.

**Amarante, 1809: distinguishes himself at the crossing of the Tâmega** under Soult **[A for the action; R for month and day]**, the only action of his career for which any source records him personally distinguished. **24 November 1809: promoted chef de bataillon [A to the day]**, place unrecorded, folded into that campa rather than pinned to an invented garrison. **1810-1811: Masséna's invasion of Portugal; Knight of the Legion of Honour (1811) [A; R for any engagement, none named]**; the Coimbra coordinate marks a theatre, not a position.

**Astorga: correction of two years and one month.** The pool carried both "captured at Astorga, 1810" and "captured at Astorga, September 1812." The 1812 siege ran 29 June to 19 August, the garrison surrendering on **18 August 1812 [A: en.wikipedia, Siege of Astorga (1812)]**. The 1810 siege was the opposite event, the French taking the town from the Spanish. September 1812 is when he was shipped to England: English Wikipedia reads "captured on the city's surrender and sent to England in September 1812." Coordinates 42.4548, -6.0526 confirmed.

## 7. England, the Restoration, Givet (1812-1822)

**September 1812: sent to England as a prisoner of war [A]**. **The depot or hulk is not named in any source. Gap.** Portsmouth (50.8058, -1.0872) is an approximate marker, stated as such inside the campa.

**July 1814: returns to France [A]**, and is made **Officer of the Legion of Honour, knight of the Order of Saint Louis, and chief engineer of Orléans [A for the year; R for the month]**. **1815: rejoins Napoleon in the Hundred Days, defends Laon, placed on half-pay after Waterloo [A]**.

*Date contradiction, resolved by internal consistency.* The pool put the reinstatement to the northern fortifications at 1815 in one entry and 1816 in another, and half-pay and reinstatement cannot occupy one year. The atlas follows **1816** and says so in `date_confidence` **[A for the fact; R for the adjudication, labelled as one]**.

**5 August 1822: dies at Givet, Ardennes, aged 51**, as engineer in chief, "after a long and painful illness" **[A]**. *Coordinate correction:* the pool's 50.1319, 4.8283 sits about 800 m southeast of the town, across the Meuse from the Fort de Charlemont; Givet is at **50.1389, 4.8250** (fr.wikipedia, Givet), and the atlas uses that fix and relabels the stop.

**His daughter dies at thirteen shortly before him; his widow receives a royal pension of 450 francs, one account calling it a quarter of what he drew in life [A].** **His grave is a gap:** no source consulted describes the location, form, or survival of any tomb at Givet, and the journey file's last segment records that as unknown.

## 8. The stone without him

**1801: the antiquities pass to the British** under the Capitulation of Alexandria, Menou having first claimed the stone as personal property **[A for fact and year; no day in any source, and the stop's 1801-09-01 orders it after his 30 July repatriation]**. **February 1802: the stone reaches England aboard the captured frigate *Égyptienne* [A]**. **1802: enters the British Museum [A for the year; month reconstructed]**. **1803: first full translation of the Greek; 1822: Champollion's announcement; 1841: Letronne; 1858: first English translation, Philomathean Society [A: en.wikipedia, Rosetta Stone]**.

*Arithmetic correction.* The pool asserted Bouchard died "twelve years before Champollion's death." **Champollion died 4 March 1832**, Bouchard **5 August 1822**: **ten years [A: en.wikipedia, Jean-François Champollion]**.

**Bouchard never publishes on the stone [R: inference from the absence of any Bouchard-authored publication in the sources consulted]**. **No monument, statue, or plaque to him** was found at **Orgelet**, **Givet**, or **Fort Julien [R: absence of evidence, not proof no marker exists]**. Commemoration settles instead on the stone in Bloomsbury and on **Joseph Kosuth's giant replica at Figeac**, birthplace of **Champollion [A for the replica; no installation date in any source, which is why that stop carries the compilation date and says so]**.

## Interlocks used

Named in campa text, canonical pins inherited byte-identical. **napoleon** (Toulon, Alexandria, Imbaba, the Institut, Brumaire seen from Cairo, the Hundred Days); **ibn_battuta** 1326, **gustave_flaubert** 1849 and **rene_guenon** 1930, all three at the same Alexandria beachhead **31.2001, 29.9187**; **empress_josephine** at Cairo 1798, **30.0459, 31.2243** ("Joséphine's husband is building an academy inside an occupation"); **rene_hauy** at the same Cairo pin, as founding member of the Institut National that Bonaparte copied; **abdelkader** at Damascus **33.5131, 36.3053**, where Bouchard is held 42 days in 1800 and Abdelkader will die in 1883.

**Not used.** **Joseph Fourier**, on the same campaign and the man who introduced Champollion to Egyptology at Grenoble, is the tightest link available, but he is queued and not researched, so no journey file gazes back. **Gustave Eiffel** appears via Suez 1866; no Suez stop earned a place here.

**Not reached, with the reason**
- **Bouchard's own report to Menou (July 1799)**: no verbatim text located in anything reachable. The discoverer of the most-read object in the world leaves no first-person account here.
- **The *Courrier de l'Égypte* issue of September 1799**: no accessible digitised copy found; the notice arrives through one transcribed sentence.
- **The nineteenth-century French military biographical dictionaries** behind both encyclopedia articles: not named there and not searched directly. The obvious next lead, and it would probably settle the 15-vs-19 July question, the Fort Julien surrender month, and the son's birth year.
- **Any monograph on Bouchard, and burial records for Givet 1822**: none found and not searched in archives; the grave remains unlocated.

**Open contradictions carried into the journey file, not adjudicated** (each flagged in place above): the date of the find, 15 July, 19 July, or August 1799; where Menou sent the stone, Alexandria or Cairo and on whose order; the November 1798 polytechnique examinations against the May 1798 sailing; and the Damascus captivity's year, 1799 or 1800, inside the pool's own entries.

**Twelve corrections were made against the research pool**, each stated in place above with its source: five coordinates (Orgelet, Givet, Imbaba, Cap-Haïtien, Alexandria), the 1799 landing year, the El-Arish attribution and sequencing, the Jamaica internment, the Astorga capture, the 1816 reinstatement, the *Courrier* misattribution, and the Champollion arithmetic.
