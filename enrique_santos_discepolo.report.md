# Enrique Santos Discépolo (1901-1951): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-22 for `enrique_santos_discepolo.journey.json`, 45 stops in 9 segments.
Buenos Aires lyricist, composer, playwright, actor, film director, and in his last two
years a state radio propagandist.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a
single uncorroborated source. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Where
verification caught an error in the gathered material, the correction is carried here and
the original claim named, so the trail stays visible.

---

## 1. Balvanera and the brother, 1901-1925

**Birth** 27 March 1901, Balvanera **[A: es.wikipedia and en.wikipedia agreeing]**,
youngest of five children in a family of Neapolitan immigrant musicians **[A]**. Barrio
coordinate -34.6083, -58.4055.

**Orphaned.** Both parents die during his childhood; his brother **Armando Discépolo**,
thirteen years older, takes over his upbringing **[A]**. *Gap: no year anywhere consulted.*
The file dates this stop 1906-01-01 to order the segment and says so in `date_confidence`.

**Theatrical debut** 1917 alongside the capocómico **Roberto Casaux** **[A]**; no
playhouse, no month. **First plays staged** 1918, *El señor cura*, *El hombre solo*, *Día
feriado* **[A]**, no premiere dates. **Acts in Armando's *Mateo*** 1923 **[A]**;
**co-writes *El organito* with Armando** 1925 **[A]**. The grotesco criollo framing in the
campa is interpretation resting on those facts, not a sourced claim about his statements.

## 2. The tangos nobody wanted, 1925-1927

**Bizcochito** 1925, music **[A]**. **Qué vachaché** 1926 **[A:
es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qué_vachaché]**, with three corrections carried from verification.
**Date:** the gathered material bundled it with *Bizcochito* under 1925, but its own
article gives 1926 and only *Bizcochito* belongs to 1925. **Authorship:** it said he
"co-writes the lyrics and music"; he wrote **both, alone**. **Place of premiere:** not
Buenos Aires but **Montevideo**, sung by **Mecha Delgado**, whistled off the stage **[A]**;
the Buenos Aires failure is a separate event, **Tita Merello** at the **Teatro Apolo** in
the revue *Así da gusto vivir*, **22 March 1926** **[A]**. The file splits these into a
Buenos Aires writing stop and a Montevideo premiere stop at -34.9011, -56.1645.

**Tania.** He meets **Ana Luciano Divis**, working as **Tania**, at the **Folies Bergère**,
1927 **[R: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tania_(cantante), single source]**, introduced by **José
Razzano** after hearing her sing *Esta noche me emborracho*.

> **Contradiction, flagged, unresolved.** *Esta noche me emborracho* is dated **1928**
> everywhere consulted. If Razzano heard her sing it, the meeting cannot be 1927. Either
> the meeting is later or the song is earlier. Both dates stand as the sources give them
> and the stop's `date_confidence` states the clash.

**Cangallo street apartment** 1927 **[R]**: Tania's article only, no number, no second
source. The coordinate -34.606, -58.383 marks the street, not a building.

## 3. The sudden author, 1928-1933

**Azucena Maizani** performs one of his compositions, 1928, spreading it nationally and
bringing his first real notoriety; **Tita Merello** popularizes another the same year
**[A: en.wikipedia]**. *Gap: neither article names which songs*, so the campa says "one of
his tangos" rather than inventing a title. **Esta noche me emborracho** and **Chorra** are
both 1928 **[A]**.

**Malevaje.** Gathered as 1929 and as wholly his. Corrected: **1928**, music by **Juan de
Dios Filiberto**, lyrics by Discépolo, who did **not** write the melody **[A:
es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malevaje]**. **Soy un arlequín** stays at 1929 **[A]**.

**Yira, yira.** Three dates, unreconciled: the **film** in which he acts, **1930** **[A:
filmography]**; the **tango**, **1931** **[A: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yira,_yira]**; the
biography's grouping with 1929-1930 **[R]**. A film cannot easily precede the song it is
named for. Flagged, not resolved. The gathered placement of the tango in **1928** is
supported by nothing and was rejected.

**Ada Falcón.** She recorded his lyrics and the royalties helped pay for the Palermo Chico
house **[A: ada_falcon.journey.json]**. His remark about her is a **two-layer
attribution**: his words survive only inside her own 1995 recollection, with no
contemporary record. Flagged in `date_confidence`. The Spanish original was not recovered;
the English rendering from the pool is used and labelled as such.

**Wunder Bar** 1933, Tania directed by him **[A: Tania's article]**; no venue named.

## 4. Cambalache, 1934-1935

**Written 1934** **[A: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambalache and both biographies]**.
**Premiered at the Teatro Maipo**, sung at his own request by **Sofía "La Negra" Bozán**,
"a fines de dicho año" **[A]**. Two corrections: the **Maipo is not on Avenida
Corrientes**, it stands at **Esmeralda 449**; and its coordinates are **-34.6028,
-58.3780**, not the -34.6034/-58.3807 supplied, some 250 m off **[A:
es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Maipo]**. **Cambalache in film**, 1935, sung by **Ernesto
Famá** with **Francisco Lomuto's** orchestra in *El alma del bandoneón*; the tango **Alma
del bandoneón** is also 1935 **[A]**.

**Quote discipline on this song.** Three lines were offered; one is a misattribution.
"Que el mundo fue y será una porquería, ya lo sé" is **[A]** verbatim, cited as the song's
most famous phrase, and is used. "Siglo veinte cambalache" is **[A]** but mispunctuated,
the lyric block reading "Siglo veinte, cambalache, problemático y febril"; it is used with
the comma restored. **"La Biblia y el calefón" is NOT a lyric.** The song's line is "ves
llorar la Biblia contra un calefón"; the offered string exists only as the title of Jorge
Guinzburg's television programme and of the Joaquín Sabina song written for it. The file
quotes the real line and names the derived phrase in `quote_source` as derived, never as
his words.

## 5. The tour and the camera, 1935-1943

**Chile and Uruguay** 1935 **[A: Tania's article for Chile, the biography for Montevideo]**;
no cities beyond the countries, no months, no venues. Santiago (-33.4489, -70.6693) is a
reasonable but unproven placement and the stop says so.

**Europe** 1935 **[A as to the fact, nothing else]**. His biography records the journey
and the turn to cinema on his return, and names **no city at all**.

> **Correction of consequence.** The gathered geography gave this stop **Paris coordinates
> (48.8566, 2.3522)** while its own note admitted no city was corroborated, and framed the
> trip as one Tania shared. That framing is an inference: her article names only Chile and
> Uruguay for 1935 and puts her European tours in **1955-1959, after his death**. **The
> file pins this stop in Buenos Aires**, the one documented place in it, and says in
> `date_confidence` that no European coordinate exists and no evidence puts Tania on the
> trip. The canonical Paris pin was available and was deliberately not used.

**Cinema 1937-1943** **[A: filmography throughout]**: acting in *Mateo* and *Melodías
porteñas* (1937); *Cuatro corazones*, premiered **1 March 1939**, co-directed with **Carlos
Schlieper**, plus *...Y mañana serán hombres* (1939); *Un señor mucamo*, *Caprichosa y
millonaria*, the screenplay of *Confesión* (1940); *En la luz de una estrella* (1941);
*Fantasmas en Buenos Aires* (1942); *Cándida, la mujer del año* (1943), the last he
directs. The tango **Desencanto** is 1936 **[A]**.

## 6. Uno, and the censorship, 1943-1945

**Uno.** The gathered material said he "composes the music." **He did not.**
es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uno_(tango): **Mariano Mores** handed him the score of a tango
romanza called **Cigarrillos en la oscuridad**; Discépolo wrote **only the lyric**, taking
by Mores's own account **"casi tres años"**. It was titled **Si yo tuviera un corazón** and
renamed **Uno** because audiences asked for it that way **[A]**. **Premiered April 1943,
Teatro Astral**, sung by **Tania** in the revue *La revista loca* **[A]**. *Gap: the
Astral's coordinate was not established*; the Buenos Aires pin stands in and says so.

**Censorship from 1943** **[A]**: directives against lunfardo and against references to
drink and prostitution; *Cambalache* banned outright, *Uno* kept off Buenos Aires radio.
No month attested; the file uses June, alongside the June 1943 coup, and says so.
**El fin de la noche** is withdrawn from the **Ambassador** cinema by Interior Ministry
order after one successful week, **November 1944** **[A]**; that venue's coordinate was
not established either.

**Re-release**, two errors corrected: Argentina declared war on Germany and Japan on
**27 March 1945** (Decree 6945) **[A: en.wikipedia]**, not 25 March, the Spanish article's
"25 de marzo" probably contaminated by the unrelated 25 March 1949 SADAIC date; and **the
source gives no re-release date at all**, only "se la repondría recién en 1945". The file
dates the stop 1945-01-01 and states the month is unknown. **Canción desesperada** is
**1944**, not 1945 **[A]**.

## 7. Mexico, Havana, and the son, 1945-1947

**Mexico** 1945, a relationship with the actress and journalist **Raquel Díaz de León**
**[A: en.wikipedia]**. The Spanish article says only "México", never the capital. The
canonical Mexico City pin (19.4326, -99.1332) is inherited byte-identical and the stop
states the city is not itself attested. **El Patio cabaret**, Mexico City, with Tania **[A
as to the engagement, undated]**: her article records a gira through **Cuba and México**
with an appearance at "El Patio, el cabaret más relevante" and **gives no year**. The
gathered **1947 is unsupported**; the surrounding text points to the middle 1940s.

**Havana** **[R]**. Cuba is named; no city, year, month, or venue is. Havana (23.1136,
-82.3666) is a flagged placement. The pool tagged this [A]; undated and unaddressed, it is
treated as [R] here.

**The son. Enrique Luis Santos Discépolo Díaz de León**, born **21 April 1947**, died
**28 May 2017**; Discépolo never met him **[A: both articles]**.

> **Contradiction, flagged, unresolved.** The sources date the journey to **1945** and the
> birth to **April 1947**, which implies conception around July 1946, impossible for a stay
> confined to 1945. The further claim that he left when she was **six months pregnant** was
> **not found in any article text retrieved**; that departure would fall around January
> 1947. The campa states the gap rather than smoothing it into a sequence.

## 8. El Choclo and the Cafetín, 1947-1949

**El Choclo.** In 1947, twenty-eight years after **Ángel Villoldo's** death, Discépolo
writes a wholly new lyric for Villoldo's tango, beginning "Con este tango que es burlón y
compadrito." It becomes the version most commonly sung, eclipsing Villoldo's own words
while the melody carries on **[A: angel_villoldo.journey.json]**. **Libertad Lamarque**
premieres it in **Gran Casino**, Luis Buñuel's first Mexican film **[A: same file]**. That
premiere is another person's travelling in another country and gets **no stop of its own**;
it closes his El Choclo stop, with the film in `suggested_refs`.

**Cafetín de Buenos Aires** 1948 **[A: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cafetín_de_Buenos_Aires]**:
Mores gives him a melody written for a film scene and asks for lyrics within a week; he
delivers on deadline. **Tania records it 1 July 1948** with **Héctor Stamponi**; **Edmundo
Rivero** a week later with **Aníbal Troilo** **[A]**. The film *Corrientes, calle de
ensueños* is released **September 1949** **[A]**.

**Quote correction.** "los sabihondos y suicidas" was offered as a verbatim lyric line.
The string is verbatim on the page, but Wikipedia presents it, with "la ñata contra el
vidrio", as a **phrase extracted from the letra that entered popular speech**, not as a
quoted line; in the song the words sit inside a longer clause built on "de sabihondos y
suicidas", whose surrounding wording is unverified and was **not** reconstructed.

**Perón lifts the ban, 25 March 1949**: SADAIC directors obtain a meeting; Perón says he
was unaware of the directives and orders them lifted **[A: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambalache]**.

> **Contradiction inside the research pool, flagged.** The chronology and afterlife lenses
> describe this event as lifting the ban on **Cambalache**; the geography lens describes
> the same date as lifting the ban on **Uno**. Both bans existed. The date is carried by
> the *Cambalache* article, so Cambalache is followed and the divergent reading is
> recorded in `date_confidence`, not resolved.

He also writes and acts in **Yo no elegí mi vida**, 1949 **[A]**.

## 9. Mordisquito and the end, 1950-1951

**Mordisquito.** The gathered material dated the broadcasts **1947**, wrong by four years.
es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordisquito,_a_mí_no_me_la_vas_a_contar records that **Raúl Apold**
convened him for monologues defending state policy "de cara a las elecciones
presidenciales", i.e. the **November 1951** election; the biography places the Peronist
conflict, per **Pichon-Rivière**, "entre 1950 y 1951" **[A]**. Apold's script approval is
confirmed, as are his terms for opponents, "carneros de la oligarquía" and "cipayos" **[A]**.

**The empty houses.** Opponents buy out entire theater houses so his performances play to
empty seats; the people doing it include men he considered friends, and his circle records
profound sadness **[A]**. Undated beyond 1950-1951. **Pichon-Rivière** records a strong
conflict of ambivalence toward Peronism in his final years, which biographers link,
alongside declining health, to the acceleration of his death **[A]**.

**El hincha**, premiered **13 April 1951**, written by him, his last screen role **[A]**.

**Death.** Heart attack compounded by cancer, at fifty, in **Balvanera**, Tania beside him
**[A]**.

> **Three-way contradiction, flagged, unresolved.** The Spanish article on Discépolo and
> the Spanish article on Tania **independently agree on 23 December 1951**. The **English
> article contradicts itself internally**: infobox **27 December**, body text **24
> December**. Three candidate dates are in circulation, not two, and the English article
> disagrees with itself before it disagrees with the Spanish. The file follows 23 December
> on two independent Spanish sources and states all three in `date_confidence`.

**Burial** December 1951 in the **Panteón de SADAIC**, a private pantheon for musicians
inside the **Cementerio de la Chacarita** **[A]**. No day is given; the file uses
24 December to follow the death by one day.

> **Coordinate correction.** The pool supplied **two mutually inconsistent coordinates for
> the same grave**, -34.5852/-58.4569 (geography) and -34.5936/-58.4562 (afterlife), and
> both are wrong. The cemetery's own coordinate is **-34.5908, -58.4597**; the first sits
> some 700 m north of it, outside the cemetery bounds. The corrected value is used.

**The esquina de Discépolo.** Plaques mark the corner of **Avenida Corrientes al 900** and
the street later named for him **[A]**. The pool gave -34.6037/-58.3861 and
-34.6046/-58.3805, both wrong; the corner is approximately **-34.6032, -58.3793**, fixed by
the Teatro Gran Rex at Corrientes 857. Being **posthumous**, it is mentioned inside the
burial campa rather than given its own stop.

## Honest gaps

- No date for the parents' deaths or for Armando taking the household.
- No European city, port, or month for the 1935 journey.
- Which songs Maizani and Merello carried in 1928 is not stated in either article.
- No coordinates for the Teatro Astral, the Teatro Apolo, the Ambassador cinema, or the El
  Patio cabaret. All four stops fall back on a city pin and declare it.
- No date for the Cuba and Mexico gira, only the itinerary.
- The gap between the 1945 Mexican journey and the April 1947 birth.
- The Spanish original of his remark about Ada Falcón.
- No Gardel recordings of Discépolo lyrics attested, though the relation is likely real.
- **No primary source was consulted at all.** Everything descends from Wikipedia in
  Spanish and English plus three journey files: no SADAIC records, no Odeón or Victor
  discographies, no period press, no biography (Pichon-Rivière reaches this file only as
  quoted in Wikipedia). A Wikipedia-grade dossier; read it as one.
