# Luis Perlotti (1890-1969): research report
*2026-08-16. Sculptor. Born Buenos Aires 23 June 1890; died Montevideo 25 January 1969. House-studio at Pujol 642/644, Caballito, now the Museo de Esculturas Luis Perlotti.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · **GAP** = stated absence · **CONFLICT** = sources disagree, recorded and not adjudicated.

Note on the source base: this life rests almost entirely on two Spanish Wikipedia articles ("Luis Perlotti" and "Museo de Esculturas Luis Perlotti"), their shorter English counterpart, and the City of Buenos Aires museum page. No monograph, catalogue raisonné, exhibition catalogue, or archival holding was reached. Where those pages are silent, the record below is silent, and says so.

---

## 1. Formation: the Italian districts, 1890-1914

- Born in Buenos Aires on **23 June 1890** to a family of Italian immigrant workers **[A: es.wikipedia "Luis Perlotti"; en.wikipedia "Luis Perlotti"]**. The birth neighbourhood, street, and parish are **GAP**: no source reached names them. City-centre coordinates (-34.6037, -58.3816) are used as the canonical Buenos Aires pin, not as a claim about the address.
- His father, a shoe cobbler, **dies in 1899**, and the boy goes out to paid work **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- He works at the **Rigolleau glass factory** and at a **cabinetmaker's shop** before any formal art training **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **No year is given**; the period falls between the 1899 death and the Academia enrolment, and is dated by inference only.
- He trains as craftsman and sculptor at the Italian mutual aid society **Unione e Benevolenza** **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. Year **GAP**. The building actually used for his training is **not confirmed**; the coordinate (-34.6142, -58.3776) is approximate for the society in central Buenos Aires.
- Admitted to the **Academia Nacional de Bellas Artes**, studying under the painters **Pío Collivadino** and **Pablo Ripamonti** and the sculptor **Lucio Correa Morales** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Year of admission **GAP**; coordinate approximate for the historic academy.
- **1914**: first exhibition at the **Salón Nacional** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Which work he sent, and the jury's response, are **GAP**.

## 2. The turn inland, 1915-1929

- He frequents the gatherings at the **Café Tortoni** with the painter **Benito Quinquela Martín** and the poet **Alfonsina Storni** **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. No date range is given. Both friendships have separately attested consequences: Quinquela's murals in the Pujol 644 patio, and the 1942 Storni monument at Mar del Plata.
- **Influences, corrected.** The first pass recorded "regionalist writers Eduardo Holmberg, Juan B. Ambrosetti and Ricardo Rojas." The names are right, the label is wrong. es.wikipedia says only "Fue influenciado por Eduardo Holmberg, Juan B. Ambrosetti y Ricardo Rojas"; en.wikipedia distinguishes the *archaeological work* of Holmberg and Ambrosetti from the *regionalist fiction* of Rojas. Ambrosetti was an archaeologist and ethnographer, founder of the Museo Etnográfico; Holmberg a naturalist. Recorded here as archaeology and ethnography plus Rojas's americanista writing **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, corrected]**.
- **From 1925** he travels through the Americas, above all the **Andean Altiplano**, and the indigenista style dates from these journeys **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. **GAP**: no towns, no itinerary, no dates of arrival, no hosts, no duration are named by any source reached. The Altiplano coordinate (-16.5, -68.15) marks the Titicaca basin generically and is not a claim that he stood there.
- **1927**: First Prize for **La danza de la flecha** at the Buenos Aires Municipal Exhibition **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The venue within the city is **GAP**.

### CONFLICT resolved as a counting slip, not as a genuine dispute: the Seville medal

es.wikipedia's Perlotti article says the work won "al año siguiente una medalla de oro en Sevilla," which read against the 1927 prize yields **1928**, and names **no exposition**. en.wikipedia says "a gold medal in the Ibero-American Exposition of 1929, in Seville."

es.wikipedia's own article "Exposición Iberoamericana de Sevilla (1929)" fixes that event's run as **9 May 1929 to 21 June 1930**. No international exposition ran at Seville in 1928. The journey file uses **1929 [A: en.wikipedia, corroborated by es.wikipedia on the exposition]** and records 1928 as an artefact of the loose phrase "al año siguiente," not a rival attestation. Two further points: the identification of the award with the Ibero-American Exposition rests on **en.wikipedia alone**, since es.wikipedia names only "Sevilla"; and the **Plaza de España** is at **37.3771, -5.9869**, not the 37.3826, -5.9823 of the first pass, 700 m northeast in Santa Cruz.

### Corrections to the first pass, itemised

1. **Monumento a Mitre, Corrientes.** Tagged [R] in the first pass as unsourced. **This is wrong: it is [A].** es.wikipedia's body names "el Monumento a Mitre, en la ciudad de Corrientes" among his large public commissions, and en.wikipedia repeats it as "Monument to Bartolomé Mitre in Corrientes." What genuinely remains **GAP** is the inauguration date and the plaza. The gap is narrowed accordingly, not maintained as a blanket non-attestation.
2. **Coordinates corrected against es.wikipedia's own points**, all carried wrong in the first pass: Plaza Irlanda, **-34.61447, -58.45778** (was 1.7 km south-southeast); Colegio Militar de la Nación, **-34.60083, -58.60306**, used twice (the first-pass -34.599, -58.611 is neither the college nor El Palomar locality, centred -34.6263, -58.593); Pujol 644, **-34.615, -58.4482** (was a kilometre southeast, nearer Plaza Primera Junta), applied to every Pujol stop; Plaza Miserere, **-34.60972, -58.40744** (was 620 m east near Congreso); Caminito, **-34.6393, -58.3628** (was 530 m north).
3. **Plaza Irlanda, the object.** More precisely a "Monumento-mástil a la Confraternidad argentino-irlandesa (relieve en bronce)."
4. **Paraná.** The site *is* specified by the source, contrary to the first pass note: "en la rotonda del cruce de las calles Intendente Bertozzi y Avenida Rivadavia." And "five years after its gold medal in Seville" becomes **four** once the medal is correctly dated 1929.
5. **Mar del Plata.** Balneario La Perla is at **-37.99333, -57.54444** (es.wikipedia). The first pass carried two different and both-wrong points for the same Storni monument (-38.0023, -57.5288 in one lens, -38.0333, -57.5333 in another). The material is **carved stone**, "la figura tallada en la piedra." And "seven years after Storni walked into the sea" is arithmetic error: Storni died **25 October 1938**, the monument is **1942**, so four years. The Ameghino monument is on the same paseo Dávila and belongs on the same frontage.
6. **Fortín Olavarría.** -35.944, -63.648 lies 65-70 km outside the Partido de Rivadavia. Wikipedia's coordinate for the locality is **-35.7, -63** (partido seat América at -35.48333, -62.96667). Siting given by the source: the **plaza principal**.
7. **Punta Cuevas.** The work is confirmed ("Monumento al Indio Tehuelche, ubicado en Punta Cuevas, al sur de la ciudad"), but -42.785, -65.045 is roughly 2 km inland. Punta Cuevas is the headland at the southern end of the Puerto Madryn shore, near the Museo del Desembarco, around **-42.790, -65.018**. es.wikipedia has no coordinate-bearing article for it (the title is a disambiguation page), so this figure is **[R]**, to be checked against the municipality.
8. **El retorno a la patria.** es.wikipedia places it in the Valle de Uco, localidad de Tunuyán. The monument in question is the Monumento del Retorno at **El Manzano Histórico**, which es.wikipedia locates at **-33.6011, -69.3819**, some 35-40 km southwest of Tunuyán city (-33.5667, -69.0167). **Hedge retained:** the El Manzano Histórico article names the monument but does **not** name its sculptor, so the identification rests on the Perlotti article's "Valle de Uco / Tunuyán" wording alone.
9. **Luján, two institutions conflated.** The first pass merged them. es.wikipedia puts the plaster busts of **Ameghino** and **Sarmiento** and the bronze *Viejo* at the **Asociación Cultural y Biblioteca Ameghino**; what is at the **Complejo Museográfico Enrique Udaondo** is only the bronze plaque carrying a copy of the museum's 1917 founding decree, in the Cabildo de Luján. Also at Luján: a bust of Sarmiento at **Escuela No. 12**, *Las Dolorosas* and *Cristo en la Cruz* in the **municipal cemetery**, and, omitted by the first pass entirely, a bust of intendente **Juan Bautista Barnech** at the Escuela Normal Mixta Florentino Ameghino.

### CONFLICT, unresolved: which Concepción holds the third Tambor de Tacuarí

es.wikipedia "Luis Perlotti" says the Arsenal de Guerra cast three bronzes of *El Tambor de Tacuarí*: one at **Concepción del Uruguay, Entre Ríos**, one at the entrance portal of the Círculo Militar in Buenos Aires, one at the Colegio Militar in El Palomar.

es.wikipedia "Tambor de Tacuarí" says the drummer, **Pedro Ríos**, was born in September 1798 at Yaguareté-Corá, today **Concepción, Corrientes**, and places Perlotti's replica in the **Plaza 25 de Mayo of Concepción, Corrientes**.

Two articles on the same encyclopedia, two provinces, roughly 500 km apart. **No source reached reconciles them.** The first pass compounded the problem by calling Concepción del Uruguay the drummer's birthplace, false on the encyclopedia's own showing. The journey file pins the Corrientes town, states the split in the stop's confidence field, and does not assert the birthplace claim in its Entre Ríos form.

## 4. Pujol 644: the house that was always going to be a museum

- **1948**: he acquires the property at **Pujol 642/4** in Caballito and redesigns it, laying out **two exhibition halls and a library** **[A: es.wikipedia "Museo de Esculturas Luis Perlotti"]**. Two large enamelled-iron murals by **Benito Quinquela Martín** are placed at the patio entrance **[A: same]**.
- **1954**: he begins personally leading guided tours of the house-studio, to engage the neighbourhood with the work **[A: same]**.
- **1969**: on his death the house and its entire art collection pass to the Municipality of Buenos Aires by his own bequest **[A: es.wikipedia museum article; en.wikipedia]**.
- **23 May 1973**: the city formally accepts the donation by **municipal ordinance 27.726**; a **further ordinance in 1976** refines the terms **[A: es.wikipedia museum article]**. **CONFLICT flagged:** a shorter secondary account states the property was "converted into a public sculpture museum in 1969," immediately on donation. Legal acceptance (1973) and functioning museum (1990) both postdate that shorthand.
- **1987**: Professor **César A. Fioravanti** spearheads the project to open the house to the public **[A: same]**.
- **December 1990**: the **Museo de Esculturas Luis Perlotti** opens **[A: same]**.
- **2004**: design competition for the renovation, won by architect **Mario Roberto Álvarez [A: same]**. **22 December 2008**: reopening in a modernised building **[A: same; buenosaires.gob.ar gives the restoration span as 2003-2008]**.
- Collection: **over 1,600 pieces**, wood carvings, marble, bronze, ceramics, reconstituted stone, plus drawings, paintings and engravings; donations from **Lidia Battisti, Juan Carlos Ferraro, Alfredo Yacussi**; named works include *La Danza de la Flecha* and *Despertar de la Raza* **[A: es.wikipedia museum article]**.
- A secondary house-studio annex at **Nicasio Oroño 556**; three city Directrices distinctions (Environmental Management 2019, Accessibility 2019, Equality Seal 2022); and 2020s temporary exhibitions including *Geografía del encuentro* and *La persistencia de la figura* **[R: buenosaires.gob.ar official museum page; institutional self-description, exhibition years not given]**.

## 5. Death

**25 January 1969.** The two encyclopedias split.

- **es.wikipedia, prose, specific:** "murió en el hospital Británico de Montevideo víctima de un accidente de tránsito en Punta del Este, Uruguay, frente al casino." The accident is at Punta del Este, in front of the casino; the death is at the **Hospital Británico, Montevideo**.
- **en.wikipedia, infobox:** place of death **Punta del Este**.

**Recorded, not silently resolved.** The journey file gives both as two stops on the same day and states the split in the Montevideo stop's confidence field. Two further points: the first pass's chronology said he died *at* Punta del Este while its own geography lens said Montevideo; and "while vacationing" is **unsourced invention**, removed. No source reached states the purpose of the trip. The Hospital Británico is at **Avenida Italia 2420, Parque Batlle**, approximately **-34.8865, -56.1488**, derived from the street address and therefore **[R]**.

**GAP, burial.** No source reached names a cemetery, tomb, or grave for Perlotti. Not es.wikipedia, not the official museum pages, not the search attempts. This is a real hole in an otherwise well-documented civic life, and it is left open rather than guessed at. He is not, so far as anything reachable shows, buried at Pujol 644.

## 6. Quotations

Three sentences of Perlotti's own words were reached, all from a single statement to **Clarín, 9 November 1960**, describing his design for the Monumento a los Galeses, and all quoted **at one remove** through es.wikipedia's article. The Clarín page itself was not reached. Used verbatim in the journey file, untranslated:

1. "He concebido el monumento a los Galeses con una gran base que tiene la forma de un paralelepípedo ascendente."
2. "Sobre la parte superior he puesto una figura de mujer, de tres metros de altura, que simboliza el espíritu de la gesta galesa..."
3. "El otro relieve representa al Tehuelche, hijo de la tierra, tendiendo su mano de paz a los recién llegados."

Beyond these, **GAP**: no letters, no manifesto, no interview, no studio notes were reached. For a sculptor whose entire programme was an argument about what Argentine art should depict, the absence of his own prose is the single largest hole in this file.

## 7. Interlock discipline

The interlock pass returned seventeen candidate links. Almost all are **co-location and nothing more**, and are recorded here rather than written into the narrative: Christophersen (Caballito, Corrientes); Buschiazzo (Caballito); Troiani and Viladrich (the same Salón and monument circuit); Maradona, López Rega, the Difunta Correa shrine at Batán, the frigate Libertad and the 1879 Partido de General Pueyrredón (all Mar del Plata); Roca and Ceferino Namuncurá (indigenous dispossession as a theme). Sharing a city with Perlotti is not a relation to Perlotti.

Two relations survive the test, both one-directional and both real:

- **José de San Martín.** Perlotti modelled him repeatedly: the bust at Fortín Olavarría (1943), the monument in Plaza Balcarce at Suipacha, and *El retorno a la patria* at El Manzano Histórico, the site of the traditional resting place on the return from Chile. An artist working a historical figure as subject is a citable engagement even though the reverse could never be true.
- **Domingo Faustino Sarmiento.** Modelled at least twice at Luján alone: a plaster bust at the Biblioteca Ameghino and a bust at Escuela No. 12.

Direct relations to people **without** journey files, named in the narrative on their own merits: **Benito Quinquela Martín** and **Alfonsina Storni** (Tortoni circle; the murals in his patio, the 1942 monument), **Lucio Correa Morales**, **Pío Collivadino**, **Pablo Ripamonti** (teachers), **Eduardo Holmberg**, **Juan B. Ambrosetti**, **Ricardo Rojas** (attested influences), and **Juan de Dios Filiberto**, **Florentino Ameghino**, **Luis Piedrabuena**, **Bartolomé Mitre** (subjects he modelled).
