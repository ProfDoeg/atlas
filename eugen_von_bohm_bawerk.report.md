*2026-08-25, for `eugen_von_bohm_bawerk.journey.json`.*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim traceable

## 1. Brünn, and the house that was ennobled (1851-1868)

- **Birth: Eugen Böhm, 12 February 1851, Brünn (Brno), Moravia. [A: ÖBL, biographien.ac.at, entry Boehm-Bawerk_Eugen_1851_1914, fetched directly.]** The common English-reference error gives Vienna; ÖBL, the NDB and Brno's municipal record all say Brünn.
- **Parents. [A: NDB, deutsche-biographie.de/sfz4997.html.]** Johann Karl Ritter Böhm von Bawerk (1798-1857), Vizepräsident der Mährischen Statthalterei; Karoline (1820-1909), **of the Wieser family**. Two corrections: the gathering pass had the father's 1857 death as [R], but the NDB gives both parents' dates; and the NDB states the mother's Wieser descent, which the dossier missed entirely, so **the Böhm-Bawerk / Wieser tie is a blood relation, not merely a school friendship that later produced a marriage.**
- **Ennoblement 1854, predicate "von Bawerk." [R: encyklopedie.brna.cz via dossier, not refetched.]** The compound name form is confirmed by the NDB's rendering of the father's name; the 1854 date is not.
- **Relocation to Vienna, 1857. [R]** Nothing reached documents the move; it is reconstructed from his schooling from the early 1860s being Viennese. No address located.
- **Schottengymnasium. [A: de.wikipedia, 'Schottengymnasium' alumni roster; 'Friedrich von Wieser'.]** Both Böhm von Bawerk and Wieser are on the roster, born the same year and matriculating in the same one, 1868. The gathering pass tagged this whole item [R]; **only the "friendly rivals for top marks" anecdote stays [R]**. School records not consulted.

## 2. Vienna, the Grundsätze, the German tour (1868-1877)

- **Law and state sciences, University of Vienna, 1868-1872. [A: ÖBL; en.wikipedia.]** The Alte Universität, not the Ringstrasse palace. **Finanzprokuratur 1872; Dr. jur. 1875. [A: ÖBL and en.wikipedia.]** Building unconfirmed; the pin approximates the government quarter.
- **Menger's Grundsätze, 1871.** Publication **[A]**, with its own stop in Menger's file. That **he read it as a student and turned to economics because of it is [R]**: the standing account, not confirmed against a primary source. Menger never examined them; "student of Menger" is lineage, not classroom fact.
- **German tour: date corrected.** The dossier said "from 1875"; nothing reached supports it. de.wikipedia dates the tour **1876-1877**, the NDB calls it a two-year leave without years. The file uses 1876 / 1876 / 1877 for Heidelberg, Leipzig, Jena, with `date_confidence` calling these placements inside that window. The teacher-to-city mapping is correct. **[A]**
- **The 1876 interest manuscript. [A: bibliographical record of the 2023 first English translation.]** Drafted in Knies's seminar, unpublished in his lifetime.

## 3. Habilitation, marriage, Innsbruck (1880-1889)

- **Habilitation and Privatdozent, 1880. [A: en.wikipedia.]** **Marriage to Paula Freiin von Wieser, 1880. [A]** as to fact and year. "Close and childless" comes from **Menger's 1915 Academy memorial** and **Schumpeter's account**, through the secondary literature, not read directly. **Gap: no church, no register entry, no date within the year.** No evidence of any other spouse, partner or descendant.
- **Rechte und Verhältnisse vom Standpunkte der volkswirthschaftlichen Güterlehre** (Vienna: Wagner, 1881). **[R]** as to the imprint: catalogues agree, no copy inspected. **Innsbruck: extraordinary professor 1881, ordinary 1884. [A: ÖBL and en.wikipedia.]**
- **The books. [A]** *Kapital und Kapitalzins I: Geschichte und Kritik der Capitalzinstheorien* (1884), Smart's *Capital and Interest* (1890); 'Grundzüge der Theorie des wirtschaftlichen Güterwerts,' *Jahrbücher für Nationalökonomie und Statistik* 47 / n.s. 13 (1886); *Kapital und Kapitalzins II: Positive Theorie des Capitals* (1889), Smart's *The Positive Theory of Capital* (1891).
- **The three-volume architecture is posthumous. [A]** History and Critique / Positive Theory / Exkurse is a **post-1914 reorganisation by Friedrich von Wieser for the 1921 fourth German edition**; the *Exkurse* reached English only in 1959. Not staged as a stop: Wieser's editorial act, not his.

## 4. The ministry (1889-1904)

- **Return to the Finance Ministry, 1889**, Ministerialrat then Sektionschef, direct-tax reform with **Robert Meyer**. **[A: ÖBL, which credits him with preparing the personal- and earnings-tax bills.]** The reform reaches the statute book as the **1896 personal income tax**, general and progressive.
- **1892.** Journal founding and 'Unsere Aufgaben' in the first number of the *Zeitschrift für Volkswirtschaft, Sozialpolitik und Verwaltung*: **[A]**. The **currency-commission vice-presidency: [R]**, a chronology relayed via the dossier, no primary record.
- **1895, first ministry (Kielmansegg). Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The dossier gives 19 June to 30 September 1895; de.wikipedia gives **the cabinet** as 18 June to 2 October. Personal dates could differ by days; neither set is corroborated. The file uses 1895-06-18 and states the disagreement. **Order of Leopold Grand Cross and Wirklicher Geheimer Rat the same year: [R]**, Academy biography via dossier.
- **1896, 'Zum Abschluss des Marxschen Systems.' [A]** In Boenigk, ed., *Festgaben für Karl Knies*; English by Macdonald (1898), **full text fetched from archive.org, `karlmarxandclos00macdgoog`**, so the file's quotations are verbatim and page-cited (pp. 64, 80). **Reception legend flagged:** that the essay "definitively destroyed" Marxian economics is **[R]** and school-dependent. Hilferding answered in 1904 and Sweezy printed the two together in 1949. The file states argument and reply and does not adjudicate.
- **1897, the phantom embassy to Berlin. [R, negative.]** The ambassadorship claim appears in the **1920 Encyclopedia Americana** and **was still in the English Wikipedia text fetched during research**. **No Austrian career summary lists it: not ÖBL, not the NDB, not the University of Vienna, not the Academy.** The file gives it a stop **at Vienna, not Berlin**, written as a documented error rather than an event.
- **1897-1898, second ministry (Gautsch), 30 November to 5 March**, amid the Badeni crisis. **[A: ÖBL; en.wikipedia.]**
- **Administrative Court: the 1898 start year is not attested.** ÖBL gives only "Senatspräsident am Verwaltungsgerichtshof **bis 1900**," an end year; so does the Wikipedia line the dossier leaned on. 1898 orders the segment only. **Venue corrected: the court was never in the Justizpalast.** Per Wien Geschichte Wiki it sat at Weihburggasse 22 from 1876, then from 1 May 1882 in the **Palais Porcia, Herrengasse 23**, until 1902.
- **1899, life appointment to the Herrenhaus, September. [A: ÖBL; en.wikipedia.]**
- **1900-1904, third ministry. Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The dossier attributes an 18 January 1900 start to Koerber's cabinet, but de.wikipedia dates that ministry from **19 January 1900**, with **Wittek's caretaker cabinet running to 18 January**, so a strict 18 January appointment fell in the transition. The file keeps 1900-01-18 as an ordering date and states the conflict.
- **The Koerber conflict.** Balanced budgets and gold orthodoxy against the railway and canal programme; principled restraint to admirers, obstruction of development to **Gerschenkron**. Both readings appear in the campa, neither endorsed.
- **1902, Brussels Sugar Convention** (signed 5 March 1902), implemented for Austria. **[A]** **No evidence he travelled to Brussels**, so the stop sits at the ministry, per the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there. The telling that he single-handedly ended a two-hundred-year subsidy is written as the compression it is.
- **26 October 1904, resignation. [A]** as to date and professorship; the army-budget explanation is **[R]**, from later accounts. **No investigation naming him as an accuser of criminal conduct was found**; that version is not written.

## 5. The chair, the seminar, the Academy (1904-1914)

- **Chair of political economy, 1904-1914. [A: en.wikipedia; ÖBL.]** **The private seminar. [A]** Mises, Schumpeter, Otto Bauer, Hilferding. `ludwig_von_mises.journey.json` carries its own stop for it, October 1904 through 1914, naming the same four; the file **inherits that pin byte for byte** and quotes the Mises fragment through it. Enrolment evidence is uneven for other names popularly attached to the seminar (Bukharin, Grossmann), so only the four the Mises file vouches for are named.
- **Academy of Sciences.** Corresponding member 1900, full member 1902 **[R: Academy institutional biography via dossier, no roster refetched]**; vice-president 1907-1911 **[A: ÖBL and en.wikipedia]**; president 1911-1914 **[A: same]**; **100,000 crowns donated 1908 [R: same route]**. The **1807 typo** on an Academy display page for the vice-presidency is real, corrected by the career sequence. **Where the money came from is a gap, left as one.**
- **1911, Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei: correction, [A] not [R].** de.wikipedia states the foreign membership directly, and ÖBL lists the degrees as Dr. jur. (Wien) and **Dr. phil. h.c. (Heidelberg)**. **No travel to Italy is documented**, so the stop sits at his own Vienna pin.
- **1913, 'Eine dynamische Theorie des Kapitalzinses,'** *Zeitschrift* 22, against Schumpeter, and **1914, 'Macht oder ökonomisches Gesetz?',** *Zeitschrift* 23, 205-271. **[A]** The second argues that coercion operates within the constraints of scarcity, demand and supply, not that power is irrelevant to bargaining; the distinction is kept.
- **January 1914, 'Unsere passive Handelsbilanz,'** *Neue Freie Presse*, 6, 8 and 9 January; reprinted *Gesammelte Schriften* I, 497-515. **[A]** His last publication. Wien Geschichte Wiki confirms the address as 1., Fichtegasse 9-11 (the dossier had "11").

## 7. Interlocks used, and interlocks declined

QUEUE.md enters him as building "on carl_menger's just queued foundation": editorial
framing, not biography, but it confirms Menger as the intended primary interlock.

**Named in a campa:** **`carl_menger`** four times (the 1871 Grundsätze, book **[A]** and
his reading of it **[R]**; the 1888 *Zur Theorie des Kapitals* dispute over the
definition of capital, **[A]**, in Menger's own file; Menger's private verdict on the
interest theory; the two graves in one cemetery), and **`ludwig_von_mises`** once, at the
seminar stop, pin inherited byte-identical and Mises's own description quoted through
that file. Mises sat in his seminar in his lifetime: direct and documented.

**The Menger verdict quote: the biggest single fix in the pool.** The gathering pass had
a bare fragment, "One of the greatest errors ever committed," tagged **[R]** and sourced
to "Schumpeter's biographical essays." All three parts were wrong. Menger's sentence is
**"The time will come when people will realize that Böhm-Bawerk's theory is one of the
greatest errors ever committed,"** the source is **Schumpeter, *History of Economic
Analysis* (1954)**, Part IV ch. 6, footnote block [1393], and the transmission is
**first-hand to Schumpeter**, so the file carries it as attested.

**Cemetery contradiction resolved in Menger's favour.** `carl_menger.journey.json`
asserts in its burial campa that Böhm-Bawerk "lies in the same cemetery"; the interlock
lens flagged that unverified. **Verification confirms Menger's file is right**, so **no
change is needed there.**

**Named in prose but holding no journey file** (confirmed at the interlock pass), so real
relations rather than interlocks: Wieser, Marx, Schumpeter, Hilferding, Otto Bauer,
Knies, Roscher, Hildebrand, Robert Meyer, Gerschenkron. The first five are candidate
future subjects; each is named here only where Böhm-Bawerk himself was present.

**Declined: Sigmund Freud.** `freud.journey.json` puts him at Berggasse 19 through the
same Vienna decades, but **no documented contact of any kind was found**, in either file
or independently. Shared geography and era are not a relation. Not named.

## 9. Honest gaps

- **No birth house or address in Brünn**, and **no Viennese address after 1857** or record of the move itself.
- **No wedding date, church or register entry** for the 1880 marriage.
- **No source for the 100,000 crowns.** The record does not say; this file does not guess.
- **No start year for the Administrative Court presidency**, and **no record of a single judgment** he handed down there.
- **No personal appointment dates** for the 1895 and 1900 ministries independent of the cabinet dates, which is why both stops carry flagged contradictions.
- **No cause of death**, and **no burial date**: the Ehrengrab number is solid, the date is not.
- **Almost nothing of his private life** beyond the marriage. This is a file about a career, because a career is what the sources preserve.
