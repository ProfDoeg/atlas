# Alexandre Dumas père (1802-1870): research report

*2026-08-24. Prepared for `alexandre_dumas.journey.json`. Subject: Alexandre Dumas, called Dumas père, born Villers-Cotterêts (Aisne) 24 July 1802, died Puys near Dieppe 5 December 1870, interred in the Panthéon 30 November 2002. 44 stops, 9 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a source unreachable this pass. Contradictions are flagged, **not** adjudicated. Spine: the **Société des Amis d'Alexandre Dumas** chronology and biography at `dumaspere.com`, against French and English Wikipedia. Quotations are from Project Gutenberg #1257, #1184 and #49678, confirmed by direct fetch; quoted punctuation is normalized to straight quotes, and one em dash in the Gutenberg *Monte Cristo* closing became a comma, which the stop states.

---

## 1. Villers-Cotterêts, 1802-1822

- Born 24 July 1802 in the house on rue de Lormet, now rue Alexandre-Dumas, to General Thomas-Alexandre Dumas and Marie-Louise-Élisabeth Labouret. **[A: dumaspere.com; fr.wikipedia, which gives the number as 46]**
- **Hour contradicted, not resolved.** dumaspere.com gives 5.30 a.m.; Dumas in *Mes Mémoires* writes "I was born on the 5th Thermidor, year X (24th of July 1802), at 4.30 a.m." **[A on both sides]** Both are in the stop's `date_confidence`.
- Château des Fossés near Haramont, 1804-1806 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The general dies 26 February 1806, health destroyed by two years in the Neapolitan prisons at Taranto and Brindisi after Egypt **[A: dumaspere.com; en.wikipedia, Thomas-Alexandre Dumas]**.
- **Not resolved: who withheld the general's arrears.** The pool says he was "denied his back pay" without naming an agent, and nothing fetched fixes whether that was Bonaparte personally, the war ministry, or Consulate finance. Service under Bonaparte is **[A]**, the withholding **[R]**.
- **Interpretation stripped.** The pool tagged as **[A]** that the child's grief "became the central bereavement of his imagination per his own later memoir." *Mes Mémoires* does not say that; the file treats it as the biographer's reading.
- School of the abbé Grégoire from 1811; junior clerk to the notary Me Menneson from August 1816, hired for his handwriting; meets Adolphe Ribbing de Leuven 1819. **[A: dumaspere.com]**
- The name scene ("I will be called Alexandre Dumas, and nothing else") is **[A]** as text, Gutenberg #49678, **[R]** as to date: undated in the memoirs, placed at 1819 to order the segment.

## 2. Paris, 1822-1829

- Exploratory trip 1822; settles 1823 with a copying post in the household bureaux of the duc d'Orléans at the Palais-Royal, got through his handwriting and his father's old friends. **[A: dumaspere.com; fr.wikipedia]** Salary figures and named patrons were not verified and are not in the file.
- **Birth of Dumas fils: date contradicted, and the pool's source is the weaker one.** dumaspere.com gives 24 July 1824, duplicating Dumas's own birthday; fr.wikipedia gives **27 July 1824**, 1 place Boieldieu, mother **Catherine Laure Labay** (1793-1868), the child registered *père et mère inconnus* and recognised only on **17 March 1831**, twelve days after Marie-Alexandrine's birth. The file uses 27 July and states the conflict. **[A: fr.wikipedia, Alexandre Dumas fils]**
- *La Chasse et l'Amour*, Ambigu-Comique, 22 September 1825, signed "Rousseau, Adolphe and Davy" **[A]**. *Christine de Suède* accepted by the Comédie-Française "with corrections" 20 March 1828, not staged in that form **[R: dumaspere.com biographie]**.

## 3. 1829-1835

- *Henri III et sa cour*, Comédie-Française, **10 February 1829** **[A]**; **flagged**, some bibliographies give 11 February. The revised *Christine* opened at the Odéon 30 March 1830 **[A]**, folded into that stop. 25 rue de l'Université 1829-1831 **[R]**, not pinned.
- **July Revolution.** Armed in insurgent Paris, the ride to Soissons for gunpowder, then his own printed *Rapport au général La Fayette sur l'enlèvement des poudres de Soissons*: mission and pamphlet **[A]**; the day, 29 July, is fr.wikipedia alone **[R]**; what was said at the magazine rests on Dumas's own narrative.
- Marie-Alexandrine born to Belle Kreilssamner 5 March 1831 and acknowledged; Dumas fils recognised 17 March 1831. **[A]**
- *Antony*, Porte-Saint-Martin, 3 May 1831, Bocage and Marie Dorval **[A]**. The biographical reading against Dumas's own paternity is critical commonplace, **[R]** as to intention.
- **The Galois banquet, 9 May 1831.** Date and event **[A: en.wikipedia; galois.ihp.fr]**. The scene is **[R]**: one account only, *Mes Mémoires* ch. CCIV, decades later, no second witness.
- Switzerland 1832 and *Impressions de voyage: Suisse* **[A]**; the Chateaubriand meeting **[R]**. Geneva stands for a route, not a residence. Italy 1835, feeding *Le Speronare*, *Le Capitaine Aréna*, *Le Corricolo* **[A]**, carried in the Florence stop. Rhine travel 1838-1840 is **[R]**; the pool's Cologne pin was reconstruction from an itinerary and was dropped.

## 4. 1836-1847

- Drama critic on Girardin's *La Presse* from June 1836 **[A]**, folded into the 1844 stop. Marries **Ida Ferrier** 1 February 1840 **[A]**; Florence household 1840-1844 and *Une année à Florence* **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; they separate without divorcing.
- ***Georges***, 1843, Mauritius, slavery and colonial revolt **[A: dumaspere.com]**. Reading it as a workshop for *Monte-Cristo* is **[R]**.
- **1844.** *Les Trois Mousquetaires* in *Le Siècle* March to July, book the same year; *Le Comte de Monte-Cristo* in the *Journal des débats* 28 August 1844 to 15 January 1846, book 1846. **[A: en.wikipedia; dumaspere.com]** Principal collaborator **Auguste Maquet**, whose exact share is an open question this pass did not attempt to settle.
- **1845.** Mirecourt's *Fabrique de romans: Maison Alexandre Dumas et compagnie*, the defamation suit and Dumas's victory **[A: the pamphlet; Gustave Simon, *Histoire d'une collaboration*]**. Its racial abuse and its collaboration charge are separate things, kept separate. The figure of some three hundred volumes is **[R]**.
- *Joseph Balsamo* serialized 1846-1848 with Maquet **[A]**. Dumas published no book titled simply *Cagliostro*; English publishers later subdivided *Joseph Balsamo* under that title.
- **Théâtre Historique** opens 20 February 1847 with *La Reine Margot*, 72 boulevard du Temple **[A]**. **Château de Monte-Cristo**, architect Hippolyte Durand, with the Gothic pavilion called the Château d'If, inaugurated **25 July 1847**, 50 invitations sent and roughly 600 guests **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

## 5. 1848-1857

- Stands unsuccessfully for the legislature 1848 in the Seine, Seine-et-Oise, Yonne and Guadeloupe **[A]**. Not pinned.
- **The château sold.** fr.wikipedia gives one forced sale, **March 1849**, 31,000 francs in gold. The pool's "1848-1849" range and its ~500,000-franc construction cost are unsupported there; the cost is **[R]** and the file asserts no ratio. Bankruptcy judgments 20 December 1850 and 11 December 1851 **[R]**.
- **Jules Verne, *Les Pailles rompues*, 12 June 1850.** The attested version, and the one published: Verne came to Paris independently in 1848 to read law, showed the play to **Dumas fils**, the two revised it together, and Dumas fils arranged production with his father, the company then in the theatre's house being the Opéra-National. **[A: en.wikipedia, Jules Verne; fabula.org/revue/document12302.php]**
  - **Rejected:** the brief's "Dumas personally introduced Verne to Paris literary life" in 1849 via the chirologist **Chevalier d'Arpentigny**, unconfirmable, and d'Arpentigny appears nowhere in `jules_verne.journey.json`. en.wikipedia's Théâtre Historique article does not mention Verne either, so the venue rests on the Verne page alone. **[R]**
- **Brussels** from December 1851, 73 boulevard Waterloo **[A]**, the house open to the proscribed. That **Victor Hugo** lodged there specifically is **[R]**; only his presence in the Brussels exile circle that winter is asserted.
- **Return contradicted.** Pool chronology 1854; fr.wikipedia **April 1853**. Only 1853 is self-consistent, since he founded and edited *Le Mousquetaire* from Paris that November (12 November 1853; *Le Monte-Cristo* weekly 27 April 1857 to 10 May 1860, both **[R: dumaspere.com]**).
- **Guernsey: visit attested, year not.** dumaspere.com has Dumas crossing to embrace "son vieil ami Hugo." No fetched source dates it; the pool's 1857 is a dossier synthesis. Hugo arrived October 1856, so any year from 1857 fits, which is why the year needs a document. Dated 1857 **[R]** with the gap stated.

## 6. 1858-1859, Russia and the Caucasus

- June 1858 departure; Saint Petersburg, Moscow, the Volga through Nizhny Novgorod and Kazan to Astrakhan, then Baku and Tiflis, out by a Black Sea port in February 1859. Books: *De Paris à Astrakan*, *Le Caucase*. **[A: dumaspere.com; en.wikipedia]** **The exit port is contradicted**, Poti or Trebizond, and is not adjudicated.
- All five pins here are **city-centre points, not landmarks [R for precision, A for the itinerary]**. Nizhny Novgorod and Kazan are carried in the Astrakhan stop's prose. Month-level dates beyond the June departure are **[R]**. The naphtha fields at Baku are standard *Le Caucase* content, not verified against the text: **[R]**.

## 7. 1860-1864, the Thousand and Naples

- Sails from Marseille 9 May 1860 in the schooner **Emma** with **Gustave Le Gray** **[A]**; that he bought arms and mobilized funds there is **[R]**, no primary source fetched.
- Garibaldi enters Naples 7 September 1860; the directorship of museums and excavations follows, called **honorifique** by dumaspere.com. Pompeian archaeology, the founding and editing of **L'Indipendente** in 1860, and the eventual resignation under nationalist hostility are **[A]**; the exact title and resignation date are variably documented **[R]**.
- **Residence dates corrected.** The pool wrote 1860-1864 for the via Chiatamone house while citing fr.wikipedia, which gives **1861-1864**. The file uses 1861-1864 for the house, 1860 for the appointment and paper.
- **Vita e memorie di Giuseppe Garibaldi** (Milan, 1860), redacted from Garibaldi's manuscript and conversations **[A]**; the voice is Garibaldi's. It is one of two recensions cited in `anita_garibaldi.journey.json` for the Gualeguay imprisonment and the Laguna dos Patos meeting, so his editorial hand sits inside her record: **[R]** as inference, **[A]** that the citation exists. He never met her.
- **The paternal echo is interpretive.** The general's imprisonment at Naples and the son's 1860 posting there are both **[A]**; the causal link, including the standard derivation of *Monte-Cristo*'s plot from it, is **[R]** and is flagged in the file.

## 8. 1864-1870

- Summer 1864 at Enghien-les-Bains, then boulevard Malesherbes that autumn **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; Delacroix lecture December 1864 **[A]**. Both folded into one stop.
- **Adah Isaacs Menken: year corrected, framing downgraded.** en.wikipedia places the affair in her 1866 Paris run of *Les Pirates de la Savane* and captions the photograph "Menken with Alexandre Dumas, 1866," naming no photographer and describing no scandal. The file uses **1866** **[A]** and treats the press scandal as **[R]**.
- **Roscoff 1869**, the *Grand Dictionnaire de cuisine*, published posthumously **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Le Chevalier de Sainte-Hermine** ran unfinished in *Le Moniteur Universel* January to November 1869; Schopp found it on microfilm at the Bibliothèque Nationale in 1990, restored it and wrote three closing chapters from Dumas's notes; Phébus published it 3 June 2005, 60,000 copies against a 2,000 print run; *The Last Cavalier* followed from Pegasus in 2007. **[A: en.wikipedia]** Cited on the 1869 stop, not staged: see section 12.
- **Puys, 5 December 1870**, at his son's villa, aged 68 **[A: both Wikipedias]**. **Cause of death dropped:** the pool tagged "probable cause a vascular or neurological decline never clinically fixed" as **[A]** with no source; nothing supports it and it is not in the file.
- Funeral 8 December 1870 **[R: not independently verified]**, in the cemetery at **Neuville-lès-Dieppe**. The pool's "Neuville-lès-Pollet" fuses Neuville with Le Pollet, the adjacent Dieppe quarter.

## 9. After 1870

- **Reburial at Villers-Cotterêts 15 April 1872** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; the Hugo letter attached to the commemoration is **[R]**, not fetched.
- **Doré's monument**, place Malesherbes (place du Général-Catroux since 1977), inaugurated **4 November 1883**, d'Artagnan relief on the rear of the pedestal; Saint-Marceaux's 1906 monument to Dumas fils on the same square; the general's 1912 statue melted down in 1942, replaced 2009 by Driss Sans-Arcidet's *Fers*. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** The pedestal attribution to the engineer Joseph Bouvard is flagged in his own file as compilation tradition **[R]** and stays in `suggested_refs`.
- **Musée Alexandre Dumas: founding year contradicted**, 1902 (en.wikipedia) against 1905 (fr.wikipedia); the 1952 relocation to Maunoury's former WWI headquarters is confirmed by both. 24 rue des Moustiers, OSM 49.2530, 3.0896. Not pinned.
- **Métro station: year right, reason wrong.** Opened 1903 as Bagnolet, renamed **13 September 1970** to avoid confusion with Porte de Bagnolet on line 3, after the nearby rue Alexandre-Dumas. en.wikipedia's "upon the centenary of his death" imports a causation fr.wikipedia does not carry. **[A]** Not pinned.
- **Panthéon, 30 November 2002.** Decree signed 26 March 2002 and the five-day ceremonial through Villers-Cotterêts, the Château de Monte-Cristo and the Senate are **[R: dumaspere.com]**. Blue velvet drape, four mounted Republican Guards costumed as Musketeers, and Chirac's framing of the transfer as correcting a racial injustice are **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **The motto on the drape is not confirmed:** "Tous pour un, un pour tous" is widely reported, but en.wikipedia describes the cloth and the guards with no lettering, the pool's geography lens asserts it **[A]** and its afterlife lens flags it as unverified. The file follows the afterlife lens, **[R]**, and does not state it.

## 10. Coordinates: corrections and inheritances

The **Villers-Cotterêts grave is unresolved**: the pool's two pins disagree, 49.2513, 3.0838 and 49.2506, 3.0859, and Nominatim returns only Pisseleux and the Nécropole nationale, neither of them the communal cemetery. The commune point 49.2600, 3.0906 is used as a declared fallback.

Three pins are **inherited byte-identically** from other files so that two files never put one building in two places: Théâtre Historique **48.8656, 2.3654** from `jules_verne` (geography lens said 48.8673, 2.3648, 200 m off); place Malesherbes **48.8825, 2.3086** from `joseph_bouvard` (fr.wikipedia says 48.8828, 2.3103, 130 m); Panthéon **48.8462, 2.3464** from `victor_hugo`.

**One inheritance is knowingly wrong and needs an operator decision.** The Galois banquet is pinned at **48.8566, 2.3522** from `evariste_galois.journey.json`. Fixing it properly means editing the Galois file too, outside this run's scope, so the two files agree and the stop states the discrepancy.

Four pins are **approximations that say so**: the abbé Grégoire's school, Menneson's office, the 1825 Ambigu-Comique, boulevard Malesherbes. Seven stops use the generic Paris point for want of a street-level source.

## 13. Honest gaps

- Ungeocoded: Les Vendanges de Bourgogne, the house where General Dumas died, the communal cemetery of Villers-Cotterêts. Street numbers unlocated: boulevard Malesherbes, the 1825 Ambigu-Comique, Menneson's office, the abbé Grégoire's school. The **year of the Guernsey visit** rests on no fetched document; the **construction cost of the Château de Monte-Cristo** is unsourced, which removes the ratio against the 31,000-franc sale; the **wording on the 2002 coffin drape** is unverified.
- The **ancestry retort** ("my father was a mulatto, my grandfather a Negro, my great-grandfather a monkey; my family begins where yours ends") circulates in many versions with no contemporaneous text found for any. **[R, tradition]** Not in the file: an unsourced retort in a real man's mouth hardens into fact on republication faster than anything else in this pool.
- **Maquet's share** of the two 1844 novels is unsettled here; Gustave Simon is the reachable starting point and was not read in full.
- The **rescue of the Château de Monte-Cristo** could not be checked at source (see below). fr.wikipedia gives the Société founded by Alain Decaux in 1969, acquisition by Port-Marly, Marly-le-Roi and Le Pecq in 1970, protection 1975 and 1987, museum opening **1994**. The pool's "1971" is right for neither the campaign nor the opening.
