# Harriet Tubman: research report
*Slug `harriet_tubman`. Araminta "Minty" Ross, later Harriet Tubman, later Harriet Tubman Davis. Born Dorchester County, Maryland, c. 1822; died Auburn, New York, 10 March 1913.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named in the same line · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a lone unsupported secondary assertion · contradictions are printed as contradictions and **not adjudicated**.

The governing problem here is not scarcity. The earliest and most quoted source, Sarah Bradford's *Scenes in the Life of Harriet Tubman* (1869), was written fast and to sell, from interviews with a subject who could not read what was printed under her name. It is at once the best record of Tubman's voice and the origin of the two numbers everybody knows and that modern archival work does not support.

---

## 1. Dorchester County (c. 1822 to 1844)

- Born enslaved to Harriet "Rit" Green and Ben Ross; named Araminta, called Minty. **[A: NPS, nps.gov/people/harriet-tubman.htm]**
- **Birth year unresolved.** No birth record survives; 1815, 1820, 1822, and 1825 all appear in competing records. **[A as a dispute: Larson and NPS]** The file uses 1822-03-01 as a sorting key and says so.
- **Birthplace contested.** Popular accounts put it at the Brodess farm at Bucktown; Larson's genealogical reconstruction puts it on Anthony Thompson's land at Peter's Neck, where Rit was then working **[R: Larson]**. NPS and Wikipedia as fetched confirm only the Dorchester / Blackwater-Madison area.
- Hired out from about five or six as nursemaid and domestic, later muskrat trapping, field labor, and timber work for John Stewart alongside Ben Ross on the hand-dug canal running from Parsons Creek to the Blackwater. **[A: NPS hatu; corroborated in Bradford via docsouth]**
- c. 1835, an overseer throws a two-pound weight at a fleeing enslaved man, misses, and strikes Tubman in the head; lifelong seizures, hypersomnia, and visionary states follow. **[A: NPS; Balmuth and Scammell, PMC10713879 (2023)]** Age given as twelve or thirteen, year approximate.
- c. 1844, marries John Tubman, a free Black man; Maryland's maternal-line rule leaves her status untouched. **[A: NPS]** The change from Araminta to Harriet is conventionally dated here but **[R]**.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable dates Ben Ross's manumission, so the file states his legal status nowhere. Relevant, and unsourced in this pass.

## 2. 1849: the estate, the advertisement, the escape

- March 1849, Edward Brodess dies leaving an indebted estate; sale to settle debts becomes an immediate threat. **[A: NPS; docsouth]** Her account of praying first for his conversion and then for his death, and regretting it, is **[A as her own testimony via Bradford]**: testimony at one remove, twenty years later, in a fundraising book.
- 17 September 1849, escapes with brothers Ben and Henry; the brothers turn back and she returns with them. **[A: date fixed by the advertisement three weeks later]**
- 3 October 1849, Eliza Brodess advertises "Minty," "Ben," and "Harry" in the *Cambridge Democrat*, $100 each if taken outside Maryland, $300 total. **[A: Cambridge Democrat, 3 Oct. 1849, via Larson as reproduced in NPS materials]** **The $40,000 bounty is legend, contradicted by the only contemporary document. [A as correction: Larson]**
- Autumn 1849, escapes alone through Delaware into Pennsylvania and reaches Philadelphia. **[A: NPS]**
- **Her 1849 route is [R] in every intermediate detail.** The Quaker household near Preston, Sandtown, the Willow Grove-Smyrna-Blackbird stretch, and the C&D Canal crossing are modern reconstruction; no contemporary document names Tubman at any of them. The file therefore writes **no stop for Preston, Sandtown, or the canal**, giving the escape one Philadelphia arrival stop that says so.

## 3. The rescue decade (1850-1860)

- December 1850, first documented family rescue: niece Kessiah Bowley and her children out of a sale at Cambridge, with her free husband John Bowley; Tubman receives them in Baltimore at Tom Tubman's house. **[A: NPS; William Still (1872) via NPS]** The auction-day mechanics are **[R]**, from Still and later synthesis, not a court record.
- The Fugitive Slave Act of 1850 pushes her routes to Canada West, principally St. Catharines. **[A: NPS]**
- 1851, returns for John Tubman, finds he has married a woman named Caroline, and leads others north instead, roughly eleven later that year. **[A: NPS; Bradford]** The suit of clothes bought with her wages is **[A as Bradford, R as fact]**.
- Thomas Garrett's station, 227 Shipley Street, Wilmington: lodging, money, food, shoes, many times across the decade · Cape May hotel kitchens 1852-1854, wages banked to finance returns · the Camden, Delaware station of William and Nat Brinkley and Abraham Gibbs · Sam Green's house at East New Market, a node not used as a stop, on space grounds. **[A: Wikipedia, Harriet Tubman and Thomas Garrett]**
- December 1854, the coded letter dictated to Jacob Jackson, signed "William Henry Jackson"; the Christmas rescue of Henry, Robert, and Ben, with Ben's fiancee Jane and others. **[A: NPS, new-beginnings-harriet-tubman-jacob-jackson-eastern-shore]** The letter does not survive; its wording comes through Bradford and NPS. Ben Ross blindfolding himself so he could swear he had not seen his sons is **[A as family testimony via Bradford/NPS]**, not a document of the day.
- 1857, the betrayal of the Dover Eight exposes her father; she removes Ben and Rit Ross to Canada. **[A: NPS]** The horse-and-board cart is Bradford's: **[R]** as to detail.
- 1859, buys roughly seven acres at Auburn from Frances Seward for $1,200 on favorable terms **[A: NPS hart]**; 28 December 1860, the last known pre-war rescue reaches Auburn, Ennalls family among the party **[A: NPS]**.

**The numbers, flagged.** Bradford's "nineteen trips" and "three hundred people" are **[A as Bradford]**, **[R] as history**. Larson gives roughly thirteen missions and about seventy people personally conducted, separate from the 700+ freed at Combahee **[A: Larson (2004)]**. The file's final stop prints both and names which is which.

## 4. John Brown, and the raid she was not at

- April 1858, first documented meeting with Brown at St. Catharines; he calls her "General Tubman." **[A: NPS hafe]**
- **Chronology contradiction, not resolved.** NPS dates the St. Catharines meeting to April 1858; Wikipedia dates Brown's Chatham convention to 8-10 May 1858. Neither firmly places Tubman inside the Chatham hall. **No Chatham stop was written**; the convention is named only inside the St. Catharines campa, where she demonstrably was.
- 16 October 1859, Harpers Ferry happens without her; illness is the usual explanation and no source establishes it **[A as absence: NPS; R as to the reason]**. **Location contradiction, not resolved:** sources put her at New Bedford and at New York City in the same week, on the same secondary reconstruction **[R both]**; the file pins New Bedford and says this settles nothing. **No Harpers Ferry stop was written: she was not there.** John Brown's Fort appears in `suggested_refs` on the New Bedford stop instead.

## 5. The Civil War (1862-1865)

- 1862, travels to the occupied Port Royal-Beaufort district for relief, nursing, and intelligence work in the Department of the South. **[A: NPS]** Selling pies and root beer rather than drawing rations, and treating dysentery with marsh roots, are **[A as Bradford]**: her own account, no independent document reached.
- 1-2 June 1863, the Combahee River Raid: three gunboats, roughly 300 soldiers of Col. James Montgomery's 2nd South Carolina Volunteers, guided past Confederate torpedo placements by intelligence her scout network gathered. **[A: NPS, we-called-ourselves-combee]**
- **The count varies and both figures are printed in the file's `date_confidence`.** Contemporary press and older accounts: "more than 700." Edda L. Fields-Black, *Combee* (Oxford UP, 2024): 756. More than 150 of the men liberated enlisted. **[A: NPS; Fields-Black]** Her reframing matters for any later Lowcountry subject: the raid was a long collective intelligence operation, not a solo feat.
- **October 1865, the train assault.** Ordered into a smoking car, she resists and is forcibly removed by railroad employees, injuring arm, shoulder, or ribs. **[R]**: the dossier attributes it to correspondence in Martha Coffin Wright's circle and records that its own fetch did not confirm it. The place on the line is not recorded at all. **No stop was written**, since pinning it would mean staging her at a coordinate nobody can supply. It belongs in the file the day a primary source turns up.

## 6. Auburn, 1868-1899

- Bradford's *Scenes*, circulating from late 1868 with an 1869 title page, produced to pay her debts; rewritten as *Harriet, the Moses of Her People* (1886, rev. 1901). **[A: docsouth; standard cataloging]** Neither is a Tubman-authored memoir.
- Douglass's letter, datelined **Rochester, 29 August 1868**, printed in Bradford. **[A: verified against the docsouth text]** **Correction:** the circulating "in the darkness" is wrong; he wrote **"I have wrought in the day--you in the night."** **Placement:** he wrote it at his own house and never came to Auburn, so Rochester is his ground; the file makes an **Auburn** stop for the letter as a document of her book and stages no Rochester scene.
- 18 March 1869, marries Nelson Davis, 8th USCT veteran and bricklayer, substantially younger **[A: Auburn marriage records via NPS]**; c. 1874, adoption of an infant girl, Gertie Davis **[R: family photographs and household tradition]**; 1873, defrauded of about $2,000 by con men promising Southern gold, chloroformed and robbed **[A: Auburn press via auburnpub.com]**.
- **1874 bill: the circulating amount is wrong.** MacDougall (NY) with Hazelton (WI) sought **$2,000**, not $5,000; a committee recommended less, nothing was enacted. **[A as correction: Wikipedia, Later Life; the NARA claim page knows no 1874 $5,000 bill]**
- **1880 fire: the day is unsupported.** NPS gives only "In 1880, a careless boarder accidentally set Tubman's wood-frame house on fire"; Wikipedia narrows to February; nothing supports 10 February. Brick replacement 1881-1882. **[A: NPS hart; Wikipedia]**
- October 1888, Nelson Davis dies, tuberculosis commonly cited. **[A: NPS; pension records via archives.gov]**
- **Widow's pension: the pool's year is wrong.** $8/month plus a $500 lump sum, granted **1895**, not 1892, which is likely a confusion with the Dependent Pension Act application period. **[A as correction: Wikipedia]**
- 1896, participates in the founding-era NACW convention, Washington. **[A: NPS]** Sources vary between "founding member" and "participant"; the conservative wording is used. **[R as to status]**
- **The conductor's boast is dated but not placed.** "never ran my train off the track and never lost a passenger," dated 1896 by the Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth exhibit, which offers Rochester and Boston and fixes neither. **[R]** It rides on the NACW stop, flagged there as a compromise.
- **MGH skull surgery: the year is unsupported.** Wikipedia gives only "late 1890s"; the circulating 1898 is unsourced. The refused anesthesia and the bullet between her teeth survive only in her own retrospective telling. **[R]**
- December 1897, Payne introduces H.R. 4982 for $25/month; the House passes relief, the Senate reduces it and reroutes it through widow and nurse categories. 1898, she swears an affidavit describing her service as "a scout, nurse, cook and spy." **[A: NARA, claim-of-harriet-tubman]**
- **1899 enactment date: corrected.** NARA says only that "In 1899 Congress passed, and the President signed, H.R. 4982"; the 19 January 1899 it prints is **the date on the bill document**, not the enactment. Wikipedia dates approval of the $20 pension to February 1899. **[A as correction]**

## 7. The Home, the death, the afterlife

- Home for the Aged incorporated 3 December 1895; adjoining twenty-five acres bought 1896; deeded to the AME Zion Church 1903; opened 23 June 1908; she becomes a resident of it in 1911. **[A: NPS places/harriettubmanhome; Wikipedia]**
- 10 March 1913, dies of pneumonia there; funeral at Thompson Memorial AME Zion Church; buried with military honors at Fort Hill. **[A: NPS]**
- 1937 replacement grave marker, Empire State Federation of Women's Clubs **[R: dossier; Findagrave 403-blocked, NPS bio silent]**; 1914 Auburn memorial tablet repeating "more than 300" **[R: dossier citing NPS/Sernett; no primary description located]**, consistent with Sernett's thesis that Bradford's numbers were canonized at once.

Posthumous material kept here and **not made into stops**, a stop belonging to whoever was actually there: the Liberty ship SS *Harriet Tubman*, 1944 **[A: Smithsonian photo record]** · the 13-cent Black Heritage stamp, 1 February 1978 **[A: Smithsonian National Postal Museum]** · "Step on Board," Fern Cunningham, Boston South End, 1999, first memorial to a woman on Boston city-owned property, and "Swing Low," Alison Saar, Harlem, 13 November 2008, and the Combahee River Collective, Boston 1974-1980, named for the raid **[A: Wikipedia, all three fetched]** · 2021 Army Military Intelligence Hall of Fame, 2023 USNS *Harriet Tubman* (T-AO 213), 2024 Mint bicentennial coins, 2024 Maryland National Guard honorary brigadier-general commission **[R: dossier, not re-fetched]**.

Two park corrections. **Harriet Tubman National Historical Park (Auburn)**, established 10 January 2017, comprises the Home for the Aged, the Residence, and Thompson AME Zion Church; **the grave at Fort Hill is explicitly not part of it.** **Harriet Tubman Underground Railroad park (Church Creek, MD)** was proclaimed a national **monument** in 2013 by Antiquities Act proclamation, not legislation, and redesignated a **historical park** in 2014. **[A: NPS hart, NPS hatu, Wikipedia]**

Also flagged: Brown's 1858 "General Tubman" and the 2024 honorary commission are **not the same kind of title**, one being informal respect from a man raising an insurrection, the other a modern ceremonial rank.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Harriet Tubman is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. The relations below are real, were found by the interlock pass, and every one **reaches forward past her death in 1913**. Under the block rule they cannot go in her file; under the standing rule they are not her stops, because she was not there. **They are not deleted. They are here for a ruling. Direction caught: FORWARD, in all three.**

1. **The twenty-dollar bill.** `andrew_jackson.journey.json` carries "The Bureau of Engraving, the face on the paper," which names Tubman directly and pins the Treasury / Bureau of Engraving at **38.8976, -77.0343**; `alexander_hamilton.journey.json` carries "The Treasury, the face that stays on the note," dated **2016-04-20**, at the byte-identical pin, narrating the same sequence: a woman planned for the ten, the plan reversed in April 2016, Jackson moved off the twenty, Tubman put there. Both files hold the event; hers does not, a 2016 announcement being an artifact that did not exist when her clock stopped. To have it, add Tubman to `EXCEPTIONS.md` with a forward grant; one Auburn-pinned stop could then carry it in a sentence or two, inheriting the pin recorded here byte-exact.
2. **The 2024 Maryland National Guard commission** and the 2024 Mint program. Same direction, same reasoning.
3. **The Combahee River Collective (1974)**, named for her raid. One-directional, real, citable; it appears above as afterlife and nowhere else.

Ruled out, so a later pass does not re-derive them. **Fort Monroe / `edgar_allan_poe.journey.json`**: the Poe file pins it at 37.0021, -76.3055 for his 1829 service, and the dossier places Tubman there in her earliest war work while hedging its own date, but Poe was three decades earlier, so this is coincidence of place, not of persons; no stop was written. **Susan B. Anthony**: in `edgar_allan_poe.journey.json` and `nelson_mandela.journey.json`, neither in a suffrage context, and no Anthony or Seneca Falls file exists **[R]**. **Frederick Douglass**: no journey file, the hits being a namesake ship in `marcus_garvey.journey.json` and an unrelated John Brown in `robert_finley.journey.json` **[R]**. **John Brown**: queued right after her, no file yet, named in her campas because the relation is direct and documented. **Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354), Paris (48.8566, 2.3522), Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816)**: checked, her geography touches none.

**The one interlock written into a campa:** `abraham_lincoln`. The Beaufort waterfront stop names Lincoln's proclamation of 1 January 1863 as the reason the 2nd South Carolina Volunteers exists at all, that regiment being the one she guides up the Combahee five months later. Real and one-directional: she served under his act, his file does not mention her, and nothing was written into it. The causal link itself is **[R]**, from the dossier's Civil War section.
