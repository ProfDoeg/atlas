# Jacob Boehme (Jakob Böhme), 1575-1624: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. The Görlitz shoemaker whose two visions, one writing ban and thirty manuscript books founded the German theosophical line running to Saint-Martin, Novalis, Schelling and Hegel.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, legend, or an inference of this file. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Coordinates that could not be anchored to a documented site are named as unanchored rather than quietly rounded to something plausible.

**Calendar.** The journey carries `"calendar": "julian"`. Not only the pre-1582 birth rule: Lutheran Lusatia and Saxony kept the Julian reckoning until 1700, so every date the sources give is Old Style as recorded. No source consulted converts them; neither does this file.

---

## 1. Alt Seidenberg, 1575 to c. 1589

**[A en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia]** Born 24 April 1575 at Alt Seidenberg, now Stary Zawidów in Poland, near Görlitz in Upper Lusatia, then a Bohemian crown land. Father George Wissen, a Lutheran peasant holding his own land. **[A en.wikipedia]** Fourth of five children. Some accounts give only the year; both are carried in date_confidence. **[R traditional account, en.wikipedia]** A boy herdsman on his father's land, judged too frail for field labour, which is why he is put to a trade.

**[R Franckenberg 1651, via Hotham 1654 and Stoudt 1957]** The gold-cave legend: tending goats on a hill, he follows an unfamiliar path to a hollow containing a vessel of red gold, flees without touching it, and can never find the place again. Franckenberg's *Bericht* is the earliest carrier, twenty-seven years after the death.

**Coordinate refused, gap stated.** The pool offered 51.1211, 14.9636, identified with the Landeskrone. Two problems: de.wikipedia puts the Landeskrone at 51.1294, 14.9328, about 2.5 km off; and it stands roughly 20 km from Alt Seidenberg, so it cannot be where a village boy herded his father's animals at home. The de.wikipedia Landeskrone article carries no Böhme legend, and Franckenberg names no hill. The stop is placed at the village; the hill is recorded as unidentified.

**[A en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia 'Zawidów']** About age fourteen, c. 1589, apprenticed to a shoemaker at Seidenberg (Zawidów). **[A New World Encyclopedia]** Little schooling beyond the village; his later command of Scripture, alchemy, Paracelsian natural philosophy and Kabbalistic material is self-directed, with no degree and no ecclesiastical license.

**[R Franckenberg 1651, via Hotham and Stoudt]** The stranger in the shop: minding the shop alone, the apprentice sells shoes at an oddly generous price; the buyer leaves, calls him into the street by name, tells him he will be small before the world and great before God, and vanishes. Not independently attested.

## 2. The Wanderschaft and the return, c. 1592 to 1599

**[A en.wikipedia]** Leaves Seidenberg as a journeyman c. 1592.

**Gap.** No early life names a single town of the Wanderschaft; en.wikipedia says outright that it is unknown how far he went. Anything placing him at Prague, in the Silesian mining country or in a Schwenkfelder household in these years is invention, and none is supplied here.

**Contradiction, NOT resolved.** The settlement year. en.wikipedia's chronology implies c. 1592; de.wikipedia states "1594 ließ er sich in Görlitz nieder". The pool's chronology lens gave 1592 as fact tagged [A]; its geography lens tagged the same event [A] while conceding in its own text that the discrepancy is unresolved. Both taggings are wrong. Retagged **[R]**, with the split in date_confidence.

**[A de.wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia]** 24 April 1599: enrolled a citizen of Görlitz, the status required to hold a workshop and a stall. Same year: buys one of the forty-four licensed Schuhbänke on the Untermarkt for 240 Görlitz marks, is admitted a master, and marries Katharina, daughter of the Görlitz butcher Hans Kuntzschmann.

**Contradiction, flagged.** The children. de.wikipedia: "Seine Frau gebar zwischen 1600 und 1611 vier Söhne", four sons, no daughters. New World Encyclopedia: four children. en.wikipedia alone: four sons and two daughters. The six-children figure is attributable to en.wikipedia only.

**Venue downgraded.** The pool placed the wedding at St. Peter und Paul (51.158388, 14.992366) tagged [A]. The coordinates are exact per de.wikipedia and the parish membership is confirmed: "Gregor Richter bekämpfte die theologischen Anschauungen seines Pfarrangehörigen Jakob Böhme". But no source states the wedding happened there. Retagged **[R]**; the journey stop says parish, not venue.

## 3. The pewter dish, 1600; the second illumination, 1610

**[A en.wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia; the tradition from Franckenberg on]** 1600: contemplating sunlight in a pewter dish, Böhme experiences the vision he later describes as revealing the spiritual structure of the world, the relationship between God and man, and good and evil. The pool compressed this to "the spiritual structure of all things"; the fuller en.wikipedia phrasing is used.

**[A en.wikipedia]** 1610: a second inner vision. **Contradiction of content, flagged.** The pool described the 1610 vision as impelling him toward systematic writing. en.wikipedia does not: "In 1610 Böhme experienced another inner vision in which he further understood the unity of the cosmos and that he had received a special vocation from God." The impulse to write is dated separately to 1612. Kept apart here. **[R]** The vision is located "outside the walls" (New World Encyclopedia); no site is given, and 51.15, 14.98 is approximate open ground.

**[A en.wikipedia]** 1610: buys a house in Görlitz.

**Contradiction of address, flagged, partially resolved by a third source.** de.wikipedia's Böhme article names a Töpferberg purchase in 1599 and, in a photo caption, dates the Prager Straße 12 residence "from 1590 to 1610", which cannot stand against a settlement in 1594. Its *Zgorzelec* article gives the same house as 1599-1610, which fits the 1599 stall purchase and the 1610 move exactly. The 1590 appears to be a caption error. Recorded, not silently corrected.

**Coordinate correction, load-bearing.** The pool used 51.1524, 14.9865 for "Boehme's house" at seven stops and asserted the Jakob-Böhme-Haus stands on the Neißstraße. It does not. de.wikipedia 'Zgorzelec': "Das Jakob-Böhme-Haus in der Ul. Daszyńskiego 12 (ehem. Prager Straße)", on the Polish east bank, 1.4 km east and across the Neisse. **51.1524, 14.9865 is unanchored and is not used.** House stops use approximately 51.15, 15.005, declared as approximate in date_confidence.

## 4. Aurora, denunciation, ban, 1612 to 1618

**[A en.wikipedia]** 1612: begins *Aurora, oder Morgenröte im Aufgang*, left unfinished. **[R]** New World Encyclopedia dates the manuscript's *completion* to about 1612; the start and finish boundary is not fixed by any source consulted.

**[A de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** The manuscript circulates unauthorized, copied by **Karl Ender von Sercha** (de.wikipedia) / **Karl von Ender** (en.wikipedia). The pool's chronology lens omitted the copyist entirely; its geography lens named him but dated the copying to the "early 1620s", conflating it with the later patrons' circle. The copying belongs to **1612**.

**[A de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** Gregorius Richter, Oberpfarrer of St. Peter und Paul from 1606 until his death in 1624 and Böhme's own minister, denounces the manuscript from the pulpit.

**Contradiction of date, NOT resolved.** de.wikipedia: "Daraufhin wurde Böhme kurzzeitig arrestiert und im Jahr 1612 mit einem Schreibverbot belegt." en.wikipedia gives **no year at all** for the pulpit attack. The pool asserted 1613 in two lenses with nothing behind it. 1612 is used and the absence of an en.wikipedia year is recorded.

**Contradiction of agent, flagged.** The pool had the *town council* imposing the exile threat. en.wikipedia attributes it to **Richter personally**: he "threatened Böhme with exile if he continued working on it". The only Town Council summons in en.wikipedia is **26 March 1624**, twelve years later.

**[A en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]** 1613: sells the stall and the trade, turns to woollen gloves and yarn. The trade "caused him to regularly visit Prague to sell his wares", recurring for the rest of his life. **Gap:** no street, market, host or dated visit. The journey uses the corpus canonical Prague pin **50.0755, 14.4378**, inherited byte-identical, precisely because it is a convention and not a Böhme address.

**Contradiction on the silence, flagged.** Wikipedia's timeline implies renewed writing c. 1618-1619; New World Encyclopedia says approximately five years. Broadly consistent, endpoints not identical, not pinned.

## 5. The flood, 1618 to 1623

**[A en.wikipedia]** 1618 resumes writing. **1619** *De Tribus Principiis*. **1620** *Vom dreifachen Leben des Menschen*. **1621** *De Signatura Rerum*. **1623** *Mysterium Magnum*, the Genesis commentary.

**[A de.wikipedia]** A circle of Silesian and Lusatian noblemen, Karl Ender von Sercha among them, copies and preserves the manuscripts. Dated to the early 1620s; the Aurora copying itself is 1612.

**[A synchronism, kircher.journey.json]** Kircher's Paderborn accident falls in October 1618, the month of the Prague defenestration. Görlitz sits inside the war's Lusatian theatre thereafter. The synchronism is attested on both sides; the claim that the war shaped Böhme's writing is **not** made, because nothing supports it.

## 6. Der Weg zu Christo, Dresden, the last journey, 1624

**[A en.wikipedia]** *Der Weg zu Christo*, printed by a friend, the only work published in his lifetime. **Contradiction, NOT resolved:** en.wikipedia gives 1 January 1624, New World Encyclopedia 1623. Both carried. **26 March 1624:** summoned before the Görlitz town council and threatened with exile. **8 May 1624:** departs for Dresden, received favourably by nobility and clergy; lodges two months with a court physician; examined by Dresden professors and clergy, who decline to condemn him and advise him to go home.

**Two venue claims refused.** The pool placed both the lodging and the colloquium at the Residenzschloss (51.0527, 13.7369, correct for the palace per de.wikipedia). **No source places either event there.** The physician is unnamed and his house unlocated; the colloquium's room is unnamed. The journey uses the canonical Dresden pin 51.0504, 13.7373 and declares the venue unknown. **Date refused:** the pool dated the professors' counsel to "July 1624", which is arithmetic from the two-month stay, not a source date.

**Contradiction of framing, flagged.** New World Encyclopedia treats Dresden as a formal year-long banishment dated 1623-1624; en.wikipedia dates the departure to May 1624 and treats it as a journey. Not reconciled. **[A en.wikipedia]** He stays with **Herr von Schweinitz** at a Silesian country seat and begins the *177 Theosophic Questions*, left incomplete. **Internal contradiction in the pool, resolved against its own geography lens:** en.wikipedia places the stay in **summer 1624**; the chronology lens said "1624", the geography lens said "autumn 1624" and "October-November 1624". Summer is used. **Gap:** several Silesian families bore the name and no source identifies the seat. 51.2058, 16.1619 is a Lower Silesian placeholder near Legnica, not a located estate.

**[A en.wikipedia]** 7 November 1624: terminally ill with a bowel complaint, described elsewhere as dropsy, he travels home. No waystation is named; 51.18, 15.5 is a reconstruction.

**[A en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]** A formal Glaubensverhör is held at his bedside before the clergy will administer the sacraments.

**[A en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia]** 17 November 1624: dies at Görlitz, aged 49. **[R New World Encyclopedia, traditional death-bed account]** Last words: "Now I go hence, into Paradise."

## 7. The grave

**[A de.wikipedia]** Richter's successor at first refuses a Christian burial; the burial is delayed while the matter is settled.

**[A de.wikipedia 'Nikolaifriedhof (Görlitz)']** Buried in the Nikolaifriedhof outside the old walls near the Nikolaitor. A stone labelled "Grabmal Jakob Böhme" stands there today among roughly 850 preserved stones and epitaphs of the 17th to mid-19th centuries. **[R]** Whether the present stone is the 1624 marker or a later replacement is not established anywhere consulted.

**[R New World Encyclopedia, Stoudt, Weeks; stated as fact by de.wikipedia]** An incited crowd defiles the new grave shortly after burial. de.wikipedia asserts it flatly; no primary account was re-verified in this pass. The two sources' confidence levels differ and are not harmonised.

## 8. Afterlife and transmission

**[A en.wikipedia]** 1645-1662: nearly all treatises and most letters into English, two pamphlets into Welsh by Morgan Llwyd. **1682**: Richter's son edits a volume of extracts; the same year a fuller edition appears at Amsterdam with Coenraad van Beuningen's assistance. **1730**: first complete printed edition. **1764-1781**: William Law's four-volume English edition with Dionysius Andreas Freher's engravings; Blake on the plates, "Michelangelo could not have surpassed them."

**[A de.wikipedia]** Behmenists in England and the Netherlands; Quaker adherents carry his ideas to America. **Limb dropped:** the pool added "adherents in Scandinavia and Russia"; the fetched text gives no support for it. **[A de.wikipedia]** Saint-Martin learns German "fast fünfzigjährig", nearly fifty, to read Böhme in the original, and helps trigger a German revival of a writer by then largely forgotten at home; the pool's "at the age of fifty" overstates it. **[A en.wikipedia]** Hegel calls Böhme "the first German philosopher"; Schelling and Schopenhauer hold him in high regard; Baader builds directly on him.

**[A de.wikipedia]** In the GDR Böhme is **honoured** as a dialectician and humanist: "in der DDR wurde Böhme als Dialektiker und Humanist gewürdigt." The pool's stronger claim of a formal "official rehabilitation" overturning the clerical verdict has no source and is softened.

**[A de.wikipedia]** 31 October 1898: bronze seated statue by Johannes Pfuhl unveiled near the Görlitz Stadtbrücke. **[R de.wikipedia]** Relocated 1972 to the Friedenspark. **Coordinate correction:** the pool put the unveiling at 51.1526, 14.9922; de.wikipedia gives the Stadtbrücke at 51.1503, 15.0009, about 1.1 km southeast. Since the statue left the bridge in 1972, the journey pins the Friedenspark (51.1567, 14.9825) and puts the original site and the move in date_confidence.

**[A de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** EKD commemoration annually on 17 November; a 2017 Dresden exhibition presents his thought as a walkable thought-structure; in 2022 the Episcopal Church (USA) adds Jacob Boehme to its calendar, feast 11 May.

**[R en.wikipedia, citing a 1686 Joseph Mulder illustration]** The slipper anecdote: Richter throws a slipper at Böhme's head; Böhme returns it and wishes him every blessing. History or pious legend, not resolved.

## 9. Quotations

Thirteen quotations are placed in the journey; all were seen in a fetched text.

- **[A]** *Aurora*, "I am not come to this meaning...": Underhill, *Mysticism* (1911) Part 1 ch. IV on Wikisource, citing the 1764 English *Aurora* p. 237.
- **[A]** From Whyte (1895), Gutenberg #16306: *Aurora* ch. 19 "And then, when I had wholly hazarded my life..."; *Signatura Rerum* "the greatest understanding lies in the signature..."; *The Way to Christ* "If thou dost once every hour throw thyself by faith..."; *Of the Four Complexions* "O hear me! for I know well myself what melancholy is!..."
- **[A] with gaps:** two further lines in Whyte carry no treatise, "The gate of the Divine Mystery..." (likely *Aurora*, no chapter) and "Some men will mock me..." (origin unnamed).
- **[A]** *Dialogues on the Supersensual Life*, Dialogue I, trans. Law, Gutenberg #33742: "When thou standest still from the thinking of Self..."; "Love is higher than the Highest..."; "Thou must hate thy own Will..."
- **Citation corrected.** "Where Desire is there is also Essence or Being." Verbatim and genuine, but it does **not** appear in Dialogue I. It occurs once, quoted inside Bernard Holland's Introduction to #33742, where Holland attributes it to Böhme, names no treatise and gives no footnote.
- **[R]** "Now I go hence, into Paradise", traditional death-bed account, New World Encyclopedia. **[A]** "Michelangelo could not have surpassed them", Blake on the Freher plates, en.wikipedia.

## 10. Corpus interlocks

Named in campa where the paths genuinely cross:

- **novalis** [A] Tieck lends Böhme to Novalis at Jena, summer 1799; the blue flower and the *Ofterdingen* cosmology follow. The only journey file in the corpus that names Böhme outright.
- **schelling** [R] The *Freiheitsschrift* (1809) and its dark ground of God; the Ungrund vocabulary is Böhme's, mediated by Baader. schelling.journey.json quotes the text without naming Böhme.
- **hegel** [A] "the first German philosopher", en.wikipedia.
- **fichte** [A birthplace / R proximity] Born at Rammenau in Lusatian Saxony, 1762, some 60 km from Görlitz. The adjacency is this file's inference, stated only as geography.
- **giordano_bruno** [R] Prague 1588, thirteen years before *Aurora*. No contact, no transmission claimed.
- **kircher** [A synchronism] October 1618: Paderborn accident, Prague defenestration, the war that runs through Lusatia to Böhme's death.
- **steiner** [R] Claimed Böhme a forerunner of spiritual science; staged at Prague, 1911.
- **blavatsky** [R] Theosophical self-histories name Böhme an ancestor of *theosophia*.
- **mikhail_bakunin** [R] Dresden 1849. Regional proximity only, and the campa says nothing more.

Recorded, not used in campa: **jung** [R] and **theodor_reuss** [R].
