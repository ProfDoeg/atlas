# James Joyce (1882-1941): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-04 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `james_joyce`.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or attribution not run to ground in this pass. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Coordinate corrections carry both the corrected value and the value they replace, so a later pass can re-check rather than inherit silently.

Two strata sit under this file and they do not agree. The **chronology and afterlife strata** rest on Wikipedia; the **geography stratum** rests on Richard Ellmann, *James Joyce* (1959, rev. 1982). Where both pin the same place they often pin it differently. Ellmann was never read directly in this pass: every claim credited to him is inherited from the geography lens's digest, which is why several of that lens's specifics failed independent checking. **Treat Ellmann-only material as [R] in practice, whatever tag it carries.**

## 1. Dublin, the fall of a household (1882-1898)

Born **2 February 1882 at 41 Brighton Square, Rathgar**, into a Catholic family **[A: Wikipedia; Ellmann]**; baptized **James Augustine Joyce at St Joseph's Church, Terenure, 5 February 1882 [A]**. Terenure has no coordinate in either lens and is folded into the birth stop rather than pinned on a guess. The birthplace itself is the worst coordinate in the pool: chronology gives 53.3234,-6.2653 (Rathmines/Portobello, ~1.5 km off), geography gives 53.3183,-6.2664 (near Palmerston Park, ~1 km off), two different wrong pins for one house. **Corrected to 53.3155,-6.2800 (OSM).**

1887: John Stanislaus Joyce is made a rate collector and moves the family to **1 Martello Terrace, Bray**, where they stay to 1891; the *Portrait* Christmas dinner is set there **[A: Wikipedia; Ellmann]**. Corrected from 53.2028,-6.0983 (Bray town centre) to **53.2073,-6.1012 (OSM)**. September 1888: boarder, aged six, at the Jesuit **Clongowes Wood College, Clane [A: Wikipedia; Ellmann; Clongowes archives]**, corrected from 53.2839,-6.6934 to **53.3110,-6.6869 (OSM)**, ~3 km.

1891: leaves Clongowes when the fees stop; writes **"Et Tu, Healy"** on Parnell's death **[A]**. The poem survives in no reachable text: **gap**. The household slides into poverty as the father's drinking worsens. 1893: brief enrolment at the **Christian Brothers school, North Richmond Street**, which his father resents as a demotion **[A: Ellmann, geography lens only]**. Then 1893-1898 at **Belvedere College**, free places arranged by **Fr John Conmee [A: Ellmann; Belvedere records]**, corrected from 53.3546,-6.2606 to **53.3558,-6.2624 (OSM)**.

## 2. University, first print, first Paris (1898-1903)

1898-1902, English, French and Italian at **University College Dublin**, then in **Newman House, 85-86 St Stephen's Green [A: Wikipedia; Ellmann; UCD archives]**. 1900: first published work, a review of Ibsen's *When We Dead Awaken* in **The Fortnightly Review [A]**; Ellmann's claim that it drew Ibsen's attention is **[R]**, the correspondence was not fetched. Through these years a regular reader at the **National Library of Ireland**, later the setting of Scylla and Charybdis **[A: Ellmann; *Ulysses* ep. 9]**.

**October 1902**: graduates from the Royal University of Ireland; briefly at the Catholic University Medical School before leaving for Paris **[A]**. December 1902 to April 1903, the **Hotel Corneille, rue Corneille**, nominally at the École de Médecine, in practice reading at the Bibliothèque Sainte-Geneviève on money from home **[A: Ellmann]**. **January 1903**: abandons medicine. **April 1903**: recalled by telegram. **Mary Jane Joyce dies 13 August 1903 [A]**, buried at **Glasnevin 17 August [A: Ellmann]**.

**Contradiction flagged.** Chronology: James and Stanislaus refuse to kneel at the deathbed. Geography: Joyce refused "at his siblings' urging." The correction reverses the second: it was the uncle **John Murray** who gestured for the family to kneel, and the two brothers who refused. The journey file follows the correction; the disagreement stands recorded.

## 3. Nora, the tower, the departure (1904)

**10 June 1904**: meets **Nora Barnacle**, twenty, a Galway chambermaid at **Finn's Hotel**, on Nassau Street **[A: Wikipedia; Ellmann]**. Corrected from 53.3417,-6.2503 (Lincoln Place) to **53.3420,-6.2540 (OSM, Leinster Street South)**. **16 June 1904**: they walk out at Ringsend; the date becomes **Bloomsday [A]**.

**September 1904**: about a week as Oliver St John Gogarty's guest in the **Martello tower at Sandycove**, ended by a night-time revolver incident **[A: Ellmann]**. Chronology implies Gogarty fired; the correction names the fellow lodger **Samuel Chenevix Trench**. Afterlife says six nights, geography says about a week: **both approximate, not reconciled**. Corrected from 53.2903,-6.1131 to **53.2886,-6.1136**, the afterlife lens's own value, so the two lenses had contradicted each other by ~190 m. **October 1904**: Joyce and Nora leave Ireland together via London and Paris **[A]**.

**Sweny's Pharmacy**, Lincoln Place, is carried **[R]**: Bloom buys the lemon soap there, Joyce knew the shop, but **no record fixes a visit**. No stop given.

## 4. The Berlitz road: Zurich, Pola, Trieste (1904-1906)

**October 1904**: arrives in Zurich for a Berlitz post that does not exist, is redirected to Trieste where there is also no post, and is sent on to **Pola [A]**. Teaches Austro-Hungarian naval officers at the **Berlitz School, Via Giulia, Pola**, lodging with **Alessandro Francini Bruni [A: Ellmann; letters]**. **March 1905**: a spy scare expels foreigners from Pola; transfer to the **Berlitz branch, Via San Nicolò, Trieste [A: Ellmann]**. Corrected from 45.6494,13.7724 to **45.6508,13.7713 (OSM)**.

**June 1905**: the satirical poem **"The Holy Office"**, carried **[R]** by the chronology lens itself, no printer or place named. Not promoted. **27 July 1905**: **Giorgio** born; the room is placed on Via Giovanni Boccaccio with the geography lens's own hedge, "exact address disputed between biographers," preserved in the stop's `date_confidence`. **October 1905**: **Stanislaus** arrives and is drawn into paying for the household.

## 5. Rome, the return, the Trieste decade (1906-1915)

**Contradiction corrected.** Chronology dates the Rome move to 1906-05, which is when the Berlitz director absconded with the school's funds. Joyce left at the end of July and the family **arrived 31 July 1906, lodging at Via Frattina 52 [A]**. The employer was **Nast, Kolb & Schumacher**, Via S. Claudio 87; both lenses misspell it "Schumann." Duration **seven months**, not the geography lens's eight.

**Chamber Music**: chronology dates the Elkin Mathews edition 1906, Wikipedia **May 1907**. Corrected. **March 1907**: back in Trieste on part-time Berlitz work and private lessons. **May 1907**: rheumatic fever. **26 July 1907**: **Lucia** born. During the convalescence he finishes **"The Dead"**; in 1908 he reworks *Stephen Hero* into *A Portrait* **[A]**.

1907-1913: private lessons to **Italo Svevo (Ettore Schmitz)**, whose unpublished novels Joyce reads and praises. The geography lens draws a line from Svevo's Jewish protagonist to Leopold Bloom: **[A] for the friendship, [R] for the influence**, an inference stated as fact and hedged in the journey file.

**Dubliners submission, off by a year.** Chronology says 1908 to Maunsel and Company. The correction places it in **1909**, around that year's Dublin visit, roughly three years after the 1905 Grant Richards submission.

## 6. Dublin twice more, and the end of Ireland (1909-1915)

**July 1909**: Dublin, then Nora's family at **Bowling Green, Galway** (the Barnacle house is No. 8); returns with his sister **Eva [A]**. Corrected from 53.2724,-9.0508 to **53.2734,-9.0547 (OSM)**. **Volta Cinematograph, dates conflated**: chronology gives 1909-10, but Joyce arrived that October to set the venture up and the cinema, Ireland's first, **opened 20 December 1909 at 45 Mary Street**; he went back to Trieste with his sister **Eileen in January 1910 [A, corrected]**. It failed within months when the Triestine backers withdrew.

**1912**: George Roberts refuses *Dubliners* and has the printed sheets destroyed; Joyce answers with **"Gas from a Burner." Last visit to Ireland; he never returns [A]**. The 1912-1915 Trieste flat on Via Donato Bramante / Via Scussa is flagged **[R]** at source, the address sequence reconstructed from letters. **1913**: **Ezra Pound** opens the correspondence and becomes his principal champion. **15 June 1914**: **Grant Richards** publishes *Dubliners*, eight and a half years after the first submission; *A Portrait* begins serialization in **The Egoist** as Joyce starts drafting *Ulysses* **[A]**.

## 7. Zurich, the neutral city (1915-1919)

**June 1915**: as a British subject Joyce is permitted to leave wartime Trieste for neutral Switzerland. The geography lens puts the crossing at **Feldkirch, 27 June 1915**, on parole, **Stanislaus interned** behind them. Ellmann only, no document cited: kept **[R]**.

1915-1919: Zurich, on gifts and stipends from **Edith Rockefeller McCormick** and later **Harriet Shaw Weaver**, plus the Royal Literary Fund and the British civil list. Most of *Ulysses* is written here, at **Universitätstrasse 38 [A]**. October 1917 to January 1918: **Locarno**, chiefly at the **Pension Daheim** from 5 November. The geography lens calls this "the first of many eye crises." **Wrong**: the iritis dated from Trieste around 1907, and Locarno was convalescence after **Ernst Sidler's iridectomy on the right eye, Zurich, 24 August 1917 [A, corrected]**.

**English Players: venue and motive both fail checking.** The geography lens stages the troupe at the **Theater am Pfauen** and credits its founding partly to spite against "touring British Consulate players." Neither survives. The company was co-founded with the actor **Claud Sykes** in 1918, and the first production, *The Importance of Being Earnest*, **29 April 1918**, was at the **Theater zu den Kaufleuten**; no Pfauen link could be corroborated, and the lens coordinate is ~300 m north of the actual Pfauen. The quarrel was a **lawsuit against Henry Carr**, a consulate employee recruited into the cast, over ticket money and his costume. **Unresolved, so the stop is pinned to the canonical Zurich coordinate rather than pick a side.**

**August 1919**: *Exiles* premieres in **Munich**, organized by **Stefan Zweig [A]**. **No coordinate in any lens**: no stop given, recorded as a gap.

## 8. Paris (1919-1939)

October 1919 to June 1920: back in Trieste after the armistice, the city changed under Italian rule; Circe and Eumaeus finished there. Address **Via Sanità**, carried **[R]**. **July 1920**: Pound persuades him to Paris, a borrowed apartment at **9 rue de l'Université** for what Joyce plans as a week. He stays in France two decades **[A]**.

1918-1921: *Ulysses* serialized in **The Little Review**; the Nausicaa episode triggers the 1921 Comstock Act prosecution, the magazine is found obscene, and the novel is effectively banned in the United States **[A]**. **2 February 1922**: **Sylvia Beach** publishes *Ulysses* at **Shakespeare and Company, 12 rue de l'Odéon**, on Joyce's fortieth birthday **[A: Beach (1959); Ellmann]**. The afterlife coordinate 48.8534,2.3488 is ~800 m off; the geography value 48.8514,2.3389 agrees with OSM to within ~70 m and is retained.

October 1922: the **Egoist Press** first English edition; banned in the UK until the Bodley Head edition of **1936**, and Irish customs practice keeps it unavailable in Ireland into the **1960s**. 1923: begins *Work in Progress*. 1929: **Samuel Roth's** pirated American edition introduces numerous textual errors. June 1925 to April 1931: **2 Square Robiac**, off 192 rue de Grenelle, his longest Paris address **[A]**, corrected from 48.8556,2.3138 to **48.8575,2.3052 (OSM)**.

**4 July 1931**: Joyce and Nora marry at the **Kensington Register Office**, London **[A]**. Chronology says "more than 27 years" after they left Dublin; they left in October 1904, so the gap is **26 years 9 months**. Corrected. **1933-1934**: in *United States v. One Book Called Ulysses*, Judge **John M. Woolsey** rules the novel not obscene, the Second Circuit affirms, and **Random House** issues the first authorized American edition.

1935 to April 1939: **7 rue Edmond Valentin**, near the Champ de Mars, through the last of the *Wake* and the crisis of Lucia's illness. Corrected from 48.8608,2.3037 to **48.8597,2.3020 (OSM)**; the geography lens also ends the tenancy in 1938, where it ran to April 1939. **4 May 1939**: *Finnegans Wake* published. Chronology says seventeen years of composition; begun March 1923, that is **sixteen**. Corrected. Last Paris address: **34 rue des Vignes [A]**.

## 9. The last flight, and the grave (1939-1941)

December 1939 to December 1940: **Saint-Gérand-le-Puy, Allier**, where his grandson Stephen is at school, a year spent seeking an exit visa **[A]**. Corrected from 46.2308,3.6667 to **46.2581,3.5130 (OSM)**, ~12 km. **17 December 1940**: the family crosses into Switzerland, after visa difficulties complicated by Lucia's confinement in an occupied-zone clinic and Swiss doubts about Joyce's finances **[A]**.

**13 January 1941**: dies before dawn at the **Red Cross nursing home, Gloriastrasse**, two days after surgery for a **perforated duodenal ulcer**, Nora and Giorgio present. Fifty-eight **[A]**. Corrected from 47.3775,8.5570 (up the Zürichberg) to **47.3750,8.5527**. **15 January**: buried at **Fluntern Cemetery**, within earshot of the zoo's lions **[A]**. The lenses disagree by ~360 m; OSM and Wikipedia both give **47.3830,8.5709**, the afterlife value, which is taken.

**10 April 1951**: **Nora dies** and is buried at Fluntern in a **separate grave [A, corrected]**; both lenses hedged on this. **16 June 1966, Bloomsday**: Joyce is reburied in an **honorary grave granted by the city of Zurich** alongside Nora, and **Milton Hebald's** seated bronze is unveiled. The afterlife lens carried this **[R]**; the correction retires the hedge and it is promoted to **[A]**.

**16 June 1962**: the Sandycove tower, bought by the architect **Michael Scott** in 1954 and donated, opens as the **James Joyce Museum [A]**. **16 June 1990**: **Marjorie Fitzgibbon's** bronze, commissioned by the Dublin City Centre Business Association, is unveiled on **North Earl Street**, nicknamed locally "the Prick with the Stick" **[A, closing the afterlife lens's stated gap]**. Corrected to **53.3505,-6.2593**.

## Quotations: verbatim, inherited, corrected

**Verified verbatim by direct fetch [A]:** "History, Stephen said, is a nightmare from which I am trying to awake" (*Ulysses* ep. 2, Gutenberg 1922 e-text); the closing paragraph of "The Dead" (Wikisource *Dubliners*); "Heavenly God! cried Stephen's soul, in an outburst of profane joy" (*A Portrait* ch. 4, confirmed, though the surrounding sentence was lost to page-length truncation).

**Not run to ground [R], and hedged as such in the journey file:** "I will not serve" (*Portrait* ch. 5, fetch truncated before it); "portals of discovery" (*Ulysses* ep. 9, search budget exhausted); "All things are inconstant" (Lady Gregory, Nov 1902); "the most ingenious torture ever devised" (Fanny Guillermet, 5 Sept 1918); "I think I would know Nora's fart anywhere" (*Selected Letters*, 1909); "if I can get to the heart of Dublin" (Ellmann); "a picture of Dublin so complete" (Budgen, 1934); "wideawake language, cutanddry grammar and goahead plot" (Weaver, 24 Nov 1926); "the kingdom of heaven is like a woman" (*Exiles*, Act II).

**Two corrections, and the corrected text is what the journey file carries.** The **Lady Gregory letter** circulates as "no heresy or philosophy"; Joyce wrote **"no heresy or no philosophy,"** doubled negative, confirmed by 35 Open Library full-text hits for the exact string across Anderson, Bloom, Pindar, Brivic, Parr and Lyons. Some editions read "the church" rather than "my church": **flagged, not resolved**. The **Djuna Barnes interview** wording is genuine but the **date is wrong**: *Vanity Fair* vol. 18 no. 2, **April 1922**, not March, per Messerli, Herring, Goldstone, Scott, Warren and Wikipedia. The original punctuates with a dash: "The pity is," he said, "the public will demand and find a moral in my book—or worse they may take it in some more serious way..."

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

Birthplace, three coordinates and no two agreeing. Fluntern and the Sandycove tower, geography against afterlife, OSM backing the afterlife on both. Length of the Sandycove stay, six nights against about a week. English Players venue, Pfauen against Kaufleuten. "My church" against "the church" in the Lady Gregory letter. The Svevo-to-Bloom influence, asserted flatly where it is an inference. Figures are given in the phase sections above.

## Gaps stated as gaps

St Joseph's Church, Terenure: no coordinate in any lens. "Et Tu, Healy": text lost. Munich, *Exiles* premiere: attested, no coordinate, no stop. The Fitzgibbon bust in St Stephen's Green and the James Joyce Centre (opened June 1996): attested, no room for stops. The Trieste room where Giorgio was born: address disputed between biographers at source. The 1912-1915 Trieste address sequence: reconstructed from letters. The Feldkirch crossing: Ellmann only. **Joyce's own Zurich addresses are absent from the atlas corpus**, so the overlap with the Dada and Laban circles is **city-level only**; the journey file says this in the relevant campa rather than implying a nearer miss than the evidence supports.
