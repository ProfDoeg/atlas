# Christina Rossetti (1830-1894): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `christina_rossetti`. Companion: `christina_rossetti.journey.json`.*

**Method.** Written from a verified research pool assembled in five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife) and passed through adversarial verification. Corrections are additions, not rewrites: the original claim is stated, then what the source says, and real source conflicts appear again under **Contradictions**.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or consensus claim not reverified against a fetched source in this pass.

Two cautions govern the whole file. First, much of the geographical spine (the 1865 Continental journey, the Albany Street years, the seaside convalescences, the Cayley courtship) rests on **Jan Marsh, *Christina Rossetti: A Literary Biography* (1994)** and **W. M. Rossetti, *Some Reminiscences* (1906)**, neither reachable in full text this session; all of it is **[R]**, stop by stop, in the journey file's `date_confidence`. Second, **no completed journey file in `working/journeys` mentions Christina Rossetti by name**: every link to the atlas runs through her brother Dante Gabriel and the Pre-Raphaelite circle, not through her. **[R, absence across `working/journeys/*.journey.json`]**

## 1. Origins and childhood, 1830-1847

**Birth**, 5 December 1830, 38 Charlotte Street, Marylebone, youngest of four children born in four years. Father: Gabriele Rossetti, political exile from Vasto in the Abruzzo, poet, teacher of Italian. Mother: Frances Polidori, sister of John William Polidori, Byron's physician and author of *The Vampyre*. Siblings: Maria Francesca (b. 1827), Dante Gabriel (b. 1828), William Michael (b. 1829). **[A, Wikipedia 'Christina Rossetti']**

**Education.** At home, chiefly by her mother, while her brothers went to school. **[A, Wikipedia]** *Correction:* the pool also named Maria Francesca as her tutor; the cited source does not say so. Downgraded to **[R]**, dropped from the journey prose. *The birth house's modern number* is unresolved: Wikipedia's *Christina Rossetti* article renumbers 38 Charlotte Street as **110 Hallam Street**, its *Dante Gabriel Rossetti* article as **105**. The journey file asserts no number.

**Later addresses.** 50 Charlotte Street (c. 1836), then **166 Albany Street** on the eastern edge of Regent's Park, the longest-held childhood address, c. 1836-1853; childhood summers at Gaetano Polidori's cottage in **Holmer Green, Buckinghamshire**. **[R, W. M. Rossetti 1906; Marsh 1994]**

**Christ Church, Albany Street.** Consecrated 13 July 1837, firmly Tractarian, first vicar William Dodsworth, himself later a convert to Rome. Christina worshipped here. **[A for the church and her worship, Wikipedia; R for the depth of its formative effect, Marsh]**

**First poems, the home press, the breakdown.** *Correction:* the pool fused two dates. Poets.org: first poems **written 1842**. Wikipedia: Gaetano Polidori **printed the *Verses* in 1847**. **[A, both]** Around 1845, in her mid-teens, a breakdown ends formal lessons; 1845-1847 are marked by illness and a turn to Anglo-Catholic devotion. **[A, Wikipedia]**

*Gap:* Polidori's press has no established address. The journey file pins the *Verses* stop to the generic London coordinate rather than invent one.

## 2. The Brotherhood years, 1848-1852

**The Brotherhood is founded**, 1848, at the Millais family house on Gower Street; seven founders, including Dante Gabriel and William Michael Rossetti. **[A, inherited from `william_holman_hunt.journey.json`, coordinate reused byte-identical]**

**First public print.** Two poems in *The Athenaeum*, 1848. **Engagement to James Collinson**, founding PRB member, 1848. **[A, Wikipedia]**

**Sitting for her brother.** She poses as the Virgin for *The Girlhood of Mary Virgin*, 1849, among the first pictures shown under the PRB initials **[A, Wikipedia]**, and again for *Ecce Ancilla Domini!*. *Correction:* the pool dated the second sitting to 1849 alone. Wikipedia dates the picture 1850 and its first exhibition to **April 1850, Old Portland Gallery, Regent Street**. The journey file uses that exhibition, pinned to the street, since the gallery's position on it is unknown.

**The Germ**, founded and edited by William Michael Rossetti, subtitled *Thoughts towards Nature in Art and Literature, conducted principally by Artists*; four issues, January to April or May 1850, sold poorly. Christina publishes in it under a pseudonym her brother chose for her, Ellen Alleyne. **[A, Wikipedia 'The Germ (periodical)']** *Correction:* the pool made the number of poems an unresolved **[R]** crux because Poets.org says seven and Wikipedia gives no count. Silence in one source is not a contradiction of another. **Seven** is carried **[A, Poets.org; W. M. Rossetti's 1901 facsimile reprint]**; the crux is withdrawn.

**The engagement is broken, 1850.** *Correction, reversing the gathered chronology.* Wikipedia: "she ended the engagement in 1850 when Collinson reverted to Catholicism." **Christina broke it**, not Collinson. Wikipedia, 'James Collinson', adds that he trained for the priesthood at a Jesuit college only after resigning from the PRB, and never finished. The geography lens had this right and is followed. **[A]**

**North London Drawing School**, early 1850s. **[A for the attendance, Wikipedia]** The address is not established, so no stop is pinned to it.

## 3. Frome and the father's death, 1853-1855

**The day school at Fromefield.** In 1853 Christina helps her mother run a school at Fromefield, Frome, Somerset, to shore up the family's finances. It fails. **[A, Wikipedia]** Her words on the town, verbatim from that source: "an abundance of green slopes and gentle declivities: no boldness or grandeur but plenty of peaceful beauty."

**Gabriele Rossetti dies** in London, 24 April 1854, aged 71. **[A, Wikipedia 'Gabriele Rossetti']** The return from Frome and her nursing duties are **[R, W. M. Rossetti 1906]**. **Hastings:** recurring 1850s retreats for sea air, a pattern of English seaside convalescence that holds for life. **[R, Marsh 1994]**

## 4. Highgate Hill and *Goblin Market*, 1859-1862

**The penitentiary.** From 1859, roughly a decade volunteering at the St Mary Magdalene diocesan penitentiary on Highgate Hill, a refuge for "fallen women". **[A, Wikipedia 'Christina Rossetti' and 'Goblin Market']** That its rule of silence and penance feeds directly into the poem's fallen-sister plot is interpretation, carried **[R]**.

**Composition and publication.** *Goblin Market* written spring 1859, just before the volunteering begins; *Goblin Market and Other Poems*, Macmillan, 1862, illustrated by Dante Gabriel Rossetti, her first commercial collection and the book that establishes her. **[A]**

**16 Cheyne Walk.** Dante Gabriel takes the Chelsea house in 1862 after Elizabeth Siddal's death and fills it with a menagerie and a widening circle; Christina visits. **[R, Marsh; the 1862 move is attested]**

**Atlas interlock.** Morris, Marshall, Faulkner and Company is founded 1861 at Red Lion Square with Dante Gabriel Rossetti a partner alongside Morris, Burne-Jones, Brown, Webb and Marshall; Holman Hunt was not a partner. **[A, `william_holman_hunt.journey.json`, coordinate reused byte-identical]**

## 5. The single crossing into Italy, 1861 and 1865

The geographical heart of the life and the weakest-sourced stretch of the file. Every stop is **[R, Marsh 1994 and the W. M. Rossetti diaries as relayed in the secondary literature]**.

- **Paris, 1861.** A first, brief Channel crossing with her mother and William Michael.
- **Paris, May 1865.** The party sets out on her only extended Continental journey.
- **Lucerne, 1865.** A pause beneath the Rigi; her letters show a traveler more anxious than elated.
- **Splugen Pass, 1865.** The crossing into Italy. Route detail explicitly treated as reconstruction in the pool.
- **Lake Como, Milan, Verona, 1865**, then **Bologna**, the furthest point, where the party turns north. *Sources disagree on why:* ill health, homesickness, or simply the itinerary. **Left open.**
- **Return through Geneva and Paris, summer or autumn 1865.**

**Vasto.** Gabriele Rossetti's birthplace. **Christina never goes there and no source claims she did.** It appears in the journey file only as the endpoint she does not reach; its `date_confidence` says so in capitals, so the pin is never misread as travel. **[R]**

**Interlock contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** The interlock lens found *no direct evidence that Christina Rossetti visited Paris*, and notes that the atlas's Paris pin belongs to **William Holman Hunt**; the geography lens carries two Paris visits from Marsh. The journey file reuses Hunt's coordinate for the 1865 departure and marks the visit **[R]** with the objection named. **Neither lens is overruled.**

## 6. Illness, refusal, retreat, 1866-1876

**Charles Bagot Cayley.** Involvement from about 1864; he proposes; she declines, reportedly over his agnosticism. **[A, Wikipedia]** That she stayed close to him and nursed him at the end is **[R, Marsh]**. **The Prince's Progress and Other Poems**, Macmillan, 1866 **[A]**. **56 Euston Square**, late 1860s **[R, Marsh]**.

**Siddal's coffin.** October 1869: Elizabeth Siddal's coffin is exhumed from the Rossetti plot at Highgate at Dante Gabriel's request, to recover the only manuscript of poems buried with her. *Correction, tightening rather than loosening:* the pool carried this **[R]** as legend. It is stated as fact in Wikipedia's *Christina Rossetti* article, so **[A]**. Only the graveside embellishments (hair reported still growing, Howell's exact words) stay **[R]** and are not repeated in the journey file.

**Sing-Song: A Nursery Rhyme Book**, illustrated by Arthur Hughes, Routledge, 1872 **[A, Poets.org]**; enlarged edition 1893 **[R]**. **Graves' disease** diagnosed **1872**, with a near-fatal attack; it permanently alters her appearance and health. **[A, Wikipedia]** *Correction:* the geography lens dated it 1871, contradicting the chronology lens; Wikipedia says 1872.

**Maria Francesca.** Enters the Anglican Society of All Saints (All Saints Sisters of the Poor) in 1874 at 46; dies of ovarian cancer 24 November 1876 at 49, buried in the convent plot at Brompton Cemetery. **[A, Wikipedia 'Maria Francesca Rossetti']**

**30 Torrington Square, Bloomsbury.** Her final home, shared for a time with her mother and aunts; a stone tablet marks the facade; she dies here. **[A, Wikipedia]** The span 1876-1894 is **not** in the cited source and is **[R]**.

## 7. Torrington Square and the devotional prose, 1876-1894

- **Seek and Find**, 1879; **A Pageant and Other Poems** and **Called to Be Saints**, both 1881; **The Face of the Deep**, a devotional commentary on Revelation, 1892. **[A, Poets.org and Wikipedia]**
- **Dante Gabriel dies** of Bright's disease at Westcliff Bungalow, Birchington-on-Sea, Kent. *Correction:* the gathered 1882-04-10 is off by one day. Wikipedia: **9 April 1882**, Easter Sunday, aged 53. Place and cause confirmed. **[A]**
- **Declining health through the 1880s** restricts her teaching work and social life while she keeps writing. **[R, Poets.org]**
- **Breast cancer** diagnosed 1893, tumor removed, recurrence by September; **death** in London, 29 December 1894, of recurrent cancer. **[A, Wikipedia; Poets.org]**

## 8. Grave and afterlife

**The grave.** Buried 2 January 1895 in the family grave on the west side of Highgate Cemetery with her father, her mother and Elizabeth Siddal; William Michael added 1919, then the ashes of four further family members. **[A, Wikipedia 'Christina Rossetti' and 'Highgate Cemetery']**

**The Church of England** commemorates her on **27 April**. **[A, Wikipedia]** *Gap:* the year of her addition to the calendar is in no reachable source; the journey file's final stop carries a placeholder year, stated as such in `date_confidence`.

**The collected edition.** William Michael Rossetti edits the collected poetical works with his own memoir, 1904, shaping her posthumous reputation for decades. **[A, Poets.org]** The full description (*The Poetical Works of Christina Georgina Rossetti*, Macmillan) is bibliographic record, not a source fetched this pass.

**The scholarly edition.** *Correction:* the pool dated *The Complete Poems of Christina Rossetti* to 1979 and named Rebecca W. Crump as editor. Poets.org says the three volumes "were published by Louisiana State University Press between 1979 and 1990." The span is **1979-1990**. **Poets.org does not name Crump**; that attribution is bibliographic record only, **[R]**.

**In the Bleak Midwinter.** 'A Christmas Carol' first appears in *Scribner's Monthly*, January 1872; set by Gustav Holst to the tune Cranham for *The English Hymnal* (1906) and by Harold Darke (1909). Darke's is the version broadcast from King's College, Cambridge, and in 2008 British choirmasters and choral experts voted the carol the best there is. **[A, Wikipedia 'In the Bleak Midwinter']**

**The Frome poetry stone.** A millennium project in 2000 sets an inscribed stone in the former grounds of North Hill House, Frome, with lines from 'What Good Shall My Life Do Me'. **[A, Wikipedia]** Town-centre coordinate used; the stone's exact position is unconfirmed.

**Illustrated and staged afterlife.** Illustrators after Dante Gabriel Rossetti (1862): Housman 1893, Rackham 1933, Raskin 1970, Craft 1973 (in *Playboy*), Ware 1980. Stage and broadcast: New York musical 1985, Edinburgh Fringe opera 2005, Dust Palace circus 2016-2019, BBC Radio 4 dramatization 2020, a 2011 *In Our Time*. **[A, Wikipedia]**

**Translations.** *Correction, and this one matters.* The gathered claim of eight languages including Chinese is wrong twice. Wikipedia 'Goblin Market' lists **seven**: Arabic, French, Italian, Russian, Toki Pona, Ukrainian, Urdu. Worse, these are the **article's own language editions**, not attested translations. The claim over-reads its source and is carried only with that warning. **[R]**

**The Cuckoo's Calling.** *Correction:* Wikipedia supports only that J. K. Rowling's 2013 title comes from a line in 'A Dirge'. That the novel is epigraphed with the poem is not in the source and is dropped. **[A for the title derivation alone]**

**Critical reception.** From the late 1970s *Goblin Market* is reread as coded feminist and homoerotic text, as an addiction narrative, and, in Cynthia Scheinberg's reading, as antisemitic "Hebraic" stereotyping the Christian sisters must resist. These readings are built onto the poem after her death and sometimes contradict each other; the sources do not resolve which, if any, was intended. **[R, Wikipedia 'Goblin Market']**

## Contradictions, flagged and not adjudicated

1. **Who broke the Collinson engagement.** Chronology lens: Collinson. Wikipedia and the geography lens: Christina, in 1850. The journey file follows Wikipedia; the chronology lens's version is recorded, not deleted.
2. **The birth house's modern number.** 110 Hallam Street against 105, across two Wikipedia articles. Unresolved; no number asserted.
3. **Year of the Graves' disease diagnosis.** 1871 (geography lens) against 1872 (chronology lens and Wikipedia). 1872 used.
4. **Date of Dante Gabriel's death.** 10 April 1882 (gathered) against 9 April 1882 (Wikipedia). 9 April used.
5. **Why the party turned back at Bologna.** Ill health, homesickness, or itinerary. Left open.
6. **Whether Christina visited Paris at all.** Two visits (Marsh) against no direct evidence found (interlock lens). Both carried; the visits tagged **[R]**.
7. **Number of poems in The Germ.** Withdrawn: seven, per Poets.org and W. M. Rossetti's reprint. Wikipedia's silence is not a competing figure.
8. **The date of Ecce Ancilla Domini.** 1849, 1850, or the convention 1849-50. The April 1850 exhibition is the firm point and is used.
