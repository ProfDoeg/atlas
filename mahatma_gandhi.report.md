# Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869-1948): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-09 for the Atlas of Journeys. Register of the finished journey: national mythology, the canon is true. This report is the opposite register. It records where the canon is documented, where it is a smoothed retelling, and where the sources disagree.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference from coincidence. Contradictions are printed and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

Gandhi is one of the most heavily documented lives of the twentieth century, which is a different problem from an undocumented one. The autobiography is a devotional text the subject wrote about himself at fifty-six, and the mythology is state-sponsored. Almost every date below can be sourced; a large share of the interpretations attached to those dates cannot be. The atlas's own chronology and geography passes contradicted each other in nine places, all printed here rather than quietly averaged.

---

## 1. Kathiawar, 1869-1888

- Born 2 October 1869 at Porbandar, Bombay Presidency, into a Modh Bania merchant family; his father Karamchand is diwan to the princely state. **[A: Wikipedia, "Mahatma Gandhi"; Britannica]** The birth house adjoins the Kirti Mandir complex, 21.6411, 69.6006. **[A: Wikipedia, "Kirti Mandir, Porbandar"]**
- Family moves to Rajkot in 1876 on Karamchand's appointment as diwan there; Alfred High School from about age eleven. **[A: Wikipedia; *An Autobiography*, Pt I]**
- The theft, the secret meat-eating undertaken to grow strong enough to expel the British, and the written confession come from the autobiography alone. Tagged **[R]** as to detail and motive, because the text is a confession composed for moral instruction.
- Married at thirteen to Kasturbai Makhanji, May 1883. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Karamchand dies late 1885; some accounts give November, and Wikipedia fixes no month. **[A as to year, R as to month]** The son's absence at a conjugal visit on the night of the death is the autobiography's own account. **[A: *An Autobiography*, Pt I]**
- Matriculation at Ahmedabad, November 1887. Enrolls at Samaldas College, Bhavnagar, January 1888, and withdraws within a term. **[A: Wikipedia; *An Autobiography*, Pt I]**

**Contradiction, resolved against the atlas.** The geography pass placed the marriage at Kaba Gandhi No Delo in Rajkot; the chronology pass and the autobiography ("Child Marriage") place it at Porbandar, the families travelling there for a joint ceremony. Porbandar adopted. The same pass dated the family's settling into that house to 1881; nothing reachable supports 1881, and the move to Rajkot was 1876. Dropped.

## 2. London, 1888-1891

- Leaves Porbandar 10 August 1888; sails from Bombay 4 September aboard the S.S. Clyde. **[A: Wikipedia; *An Autobiography*, Pt II]**
- The Modh Bania caste council excommunicates him for crossing the ocean; before departure he vows before a Jain monk to touch no wine, woman or meat. **[A: *An Autobiography*, Pt II]**
- Lodges at 20 Baron's Court Road, West Kensington. **[A: English Heritage plaque records; *An Autobiography*, Pt II]**
- University College London attendance is repeated in secondary literature without a primary citation reachable here: **[R]**. The Inner Temple admission is **[A: Inner Temple archives; Wikipedia]**.
- Joins the London Vegetarian Society and is elected to its executive committee, 1890. **[A: Wikipedia; LVS records]**
- Called to the bar 10 June 1891, enrolled in the High Court 11 June, sails 12 June. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Learns on landing that his mother Putlibai died during his absence and that the family withheld it. **[A: Wikipedia, "Mahatma Gandhi", section "Called to the bar"]**

**Flag withdrawn.** The research pass logged a contradiction here, Biography.com asserting the mother's death and Wikipedia supposedly silent. Wikipedia states it explicitly in the section named above. The withdrawal is recorded so it is on the file.

## 3. South Africa, 1893-1914

- Sails for South Africa April 1893, aged 23, on a one-year contract to Dada Abdulla and Co.; reaches Durban in May. **[A: Wikipedia; *An Autobiography*, Pt III]** The chronology pass had April as the arrival. Corrected.
- Thrown from a first-class compartment at Pietermaritzburg and left overnight in the waiting room. **[A: *An Autobiography*, Pt III]** **Date contradiction, unresolved:** Biography.com gives 7 June 1893, the station literature 31 May 1893. The journey carries 7 June with the disagreement stated in `date_confidence`.
- Abdulla case concludes May 1894; he stays on and founds the Natal Indian Congress 22 August 1894 against a bill stripping Indians of the franchise. **[A: Wikipedia, "Natal Indian Congress"]**
- Attacked by a white mob at Durban 13 January 1897, returning aboard the *Courland*, over pamphlets he had circulated in India. **[A: Wikipedia; *An Autobiography*, Pt IV]** Raises an Indian Ambulance Corps for the British in the Boer War, 1900. Begins *Indian Opinion*, 1903. Founds the Phoenix Settlement, 1904, after reading Ruskin's *Unto This Last* on a train. **[A: Wikipedia, "Phoenix Settlement"]**
- 11 September 1906, mass meeting of Transvaal Indians at Johannesburg: the oath against the Asiatic Registration Act. **[A as to event and date: Wikipedia; Biography.com]** The **Empire Theatre** as venue is **[R]** in the pool and stays **[R]**. *Sadagraha* was coined by Maganlal Gandhi in a prize competition in *Indian Opinion* and altered by Gandhi to *Satyagraha*. **[A: *An Autobiography*, "The Birth of Satyagraha"]**
- *Hind Swaraj* written 1909 aboard the *Kildonan Castle*. **[A: text; mkgandhi.org edition]** **Gap:** the coordinate the pool carries for it (51.5, -0.1) is the London departure point, not a position at sea; stated as such in `date_confidence`.
- Tolstoy Farm established 1910 near Lawley, roughly 30 km southwest of Johannesburg, 26.364014 S, 27.8159265 E. **[A: Wikipedia, "Tolstoy Farm"]** The pool's coordinate was downtown Johannesburg. Corrected. Kallenbach **bought** the property and made it available; "donated" is the loose retelling, **[R]**.
- Newcastle strike begins mid-October 1913; the march leaves Newcastle 28 October and crosses at Volksrust in early November, where Gandhi is arrested. **[A: Wikipedia; South African History Online]** The pool's "1913-11" caught the arrest and missed the departure. Widened.
- Settlement with Smuts 1913-1914; leaves South Africa 1914 after roughly 21 years. **[A: Biography.com]**

**Correction.** The geography pass gave "Johannesburg, 1893-1903" for the law practice. He was based in Natal from 1893 to about 1901-02; the Johannesburg office opens in 1903 and runs to about 1913. The 1893 Transvaal link is the Pretoria residence for the Abdulla case.

## 4. India, 1915-1922

- Returns permanently 9 January 1915 at Gokhale's urging; tours by rail for a year before entering politics. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Kochrab Ashram founded at Ahmedabad 25 May 1915, coordinates 23.0158, 72.5667; the community moves to the Sabarmati riverbank 17 June 1917 after plague. **[A: Wikipedia, "Kochrab Ashram"]** The pool's 23.03, 72.58 was about 2 km off. Corrected.
- Champaran, April 1917: goes to Motihari at Raj Kumar Shukla's invitation, is ordered out of the district, refuses, is prosecuted, and forces a commission that abolishes the indigo system. **[A: Wikipedia, "Champaran Satyagraha"]**
- Kheda, 1918, headquarters at Nadiad. **[A: Wikipedia, "Kheda Satyagraha"]** In the same year he campaigns to recruit Indians for the British war effort. **[A: Wikipedia]** The two facts sit in the same twelve months and the atlas does not reconcile them, because they were not reconciled.
- 13 April 1919, Jallianwala Bagh: Dyer's troops fire on an unarmed crowd, hundreds dead; Gandhi is not present. **[A: Wikipedia]** Nagpur Congress, December 1920: noncooperation adopted. **[A: Wikipedia, "Non-cooperation movement"]**
- Madurai loincloth. **Date contradiction, unresolved:** Wikipedia gives 21 September 1921, several Gandhian chronologies 22 September. Journey carries 21 with the hedge.
- Chauri Chaura, 4 February 1922: 22 policemen die; Gandhi suspends noncooperation nationally on 12 February and fasts five days. **[A: Wikipedia]** The pool's coordinates sat roughly 50 km south, outside Gorakhpur district; corrected to 26.6428, 83.5897.
- Arrested at Sabarmati 10 March 1922, tried at Ahmedabad 18 March before Judge Broomfield, sentenced to six years, serves at Yerwada. **[A: mkgandhi.org, "The Great Trial"; Wikipedia]** The pool placed the trial at Yerwada; it did not happen there. Yerwada's own coordinates are 18.5646, 73.8897, about 2 km from the pool's value. Both corrected.
- Released 1924 after an appendicitis operation; fasts three weeks for Hindu-Muslim unity. **[A: Wikipedia; Biography.com]**

## 5. Salt and withdrawal, 1924-1934

- Vaikom temple-road satyagraha 1924-1925, Gandhi visiting in support. **[A: Wikipedia, "Vaikom Satyagraha"]** Coordinates corrected from 9.75, 76.3 to 9.75, 76.39278.
- *An Autobiography* completed 1929 at Sabarmati; the journey's quotations are from the final chapter, "Farewell". **[A: text]**
- Salt March departs Sabarmati 12 March 1930 with 78 followers and reaches Dandi 6 April at 8:30 a.m. **[A: Wikipedia, "Salt March"]** **Distance discrepancy:** chronology pass 388 km, geography pass "roughly 385", Wikipedia 387 km (240 miles). The journey uses 387 throughout. Dandi's coordinates are 20.886472, 72.800944, about 4 km from the pool's value; corrected.
- Dharasana raid 21 May 1930 under Sarojini Naidu, reported by Webb Miller. **[A: Wikipedia, "Dharasana Satyagraha"]** Roughly 60,000 arrests follow the campaign; Gandhi interned in May. **[A: Wikipedia; History.com]**
- Second Round Table Conference, London, 1931; lodges at Kingsley Hall in Bow, receives Charlie Chaplin. **[A: Wikipedia, "Round Table Conferences"; "Kingsley Hall"]** The conference sat September to December 1931; the pool fixes only the year.
- September 1932, Yerwada: fast against separate electorates, ending in the Poona Pact with Ambedkar. **[A: Wikipedia, "Poona Pact"]** Resigns from Congress, 1934. **[A: History.com; Biography.com]**

## 6. Sevagram and the war, 1934-1944

- Settles near Wardha 1934. Establishes residence at Segaon, renamed Sevagram in 1940, from April 1936, first visit 30 April 1936; coordinates 20.73611, 78.66250. **[A: Wikipedia, "Sevagram"]** The pool's longitude was about 5 km off and its end date of 1946 understates a tenancy that ran to 1948. Corrected.
- Quit India launched 8 August 1942 at Gowalia Tank Maidan; arrested 9 August with Kasturba and interned at the Aga Khan Palace. **[A: Wikipedia; Biography.com]**
- Mahadev Desai dies in custody within days; Kasturba dies at the palace 22 February 1944. **[A: Wikipedia, "Aga Khan Palace"; Gandhi Heritage Portal]**
- Released 6 May 1944, after 21 months and not the 19 the pool carried. **[A: Wikipedia, "Aga Khan Palace"]** Corrected.

## 7. Partition and the assassination, 1946-1948

- Noakhali, 1946-1947: walks village to village after the communal massacres, at seventy-seven. **[A: Wikipedia; "Noakhali riots"]**
- 15 August 1947: fasting in Calcutta at Hydari Mansion, Beliaghata, with Suhrawardy, not at the Delhi celebrations. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- 9 September 1947 to 30 January 1948: the last 144 days at Birla House. **[A: Wikipedia, "Gandhi Smriti"]** Coordinates 28.6019, 77.2144; the geography pass carried 77.2028, about 1.1 km west, while the atlas's own afterlife entries used the correct value. Corrected. In January he fasts for communal peace and for the payment owed to Pakistan. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Shot at 5:17 p.m. on 30 January 1948 by Nathuram Godse with a Beretta M1934, at point-blank range, on the raised lawn behind the house. He did **not** die at 5:17; he died roughly thirty minutes later in the bedroom of the left wing. **[A: Wikipedia, "Assassination of Mahatma Gandhi"]** The pool conflated shooting with death. Corrected. That article notes most accounts give three shots and some four; the journey says three and does not adjudicate.
- "Hey Ram" as his last words: **[R]**. The account descends from Manuben Gandhi's memoir and eyewitnesses differ on whether he spoke at all; the phrase is inscribed at Raj Ghat. The pool's phrasing inverted the subject ("Godse's dying words attributed to Gandhi"); corrected.

## 8. Afterlife

- Funeral procession of about five miles over more than five hours, an estimated two million people; cremation at Raj Ghat 31 January 1948. Most ashes immersed at the Sangam, Allahabad, 12 February; portions sent to other rivers. **[A: Wikipedia, "Assassination of Mahatma Gandhi"; "Raj Ghat"; Government of India funeral records]**
- 30 January 1997: Tushar Gandhi immerses an urn recovered through the courts **at the Allahabad Sangam**; the urn had been held in a bank vault at Cuttack. The pool put the ceremony at Cuttack, conflating vault with rite. **[A: Wikipedia]** Corrected. A separate urn went into the sea at Girgaum Chowpatty, Mumbai, 30 January 2008; some ashes were scattered near the Nile's source at Jinja, Uganda.
- 2007 attempted Los Angeles auction of claimed ashes and hair, withdrawn after Indian protest. **[R: contemporary press]** Not independently confirmed.
- Birla House opened as Gandhi Smriti, 15 August 1973. **[A: Wikipedia, "Gandhi Smriti"]**
- *Collected Works* in roughly 100 volumes; the late-1990s CD-ROM re-edition withdrawn after scholars alleged thousands of silent edits. **[R: Indian press reporting]** No volume comparison performed; the allegation is reported, not adopted.
- Philip Jackson's bronze unveiled in Parliament Square 14 March 2015, on a plinth deliberately lower than the British statesmen around it; sprayed with "racist" in June 2020, in an argument drawing on his South African writings about black Africans. **[A: Wikipedia, "Statue of Mahatma Gandhi, Parliament Square"]**
- "Be the change you wish to see in the world": **[R], and probably not his.** No such sentence appears in the collected writings; quote-tracers derive it from a paraphrase of a 1913 remark. Not used anywhere in the journey.
- Attenborough's *Gandhi* premiered in New Delhi 30 November 1982, opened in the United States 8 December, and won eight Academy Awards from eleven nominations at the April 1983 ceremony. **[A: Academy Awards records]** The pool's 3 December 1982 matches no milestone. Corrected.
- Mahatma Gandhi Series banknotes introduced 1996. **[A: RBI currency history]** UN resolution A/RES/61/271, 15 June 2007, makes 2 October the International Day of Non-Violence. **[A: UN records]**

## 10. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **Pretoria, 1893-1894.** The Abdulla case was largely conducted there, and no reachable source gave a usable coordinate or dated episode beyond the Pietermaritzburg night. Not carried as a stop.
2. **The 1918 recruitment campaign** is attested and unexplained by anything in the pool.
3. **The South African writings on black Africans** were not read in the original; the 2020 reassessment is reported at second hand.
4. **Kasturba's chronology** appears only where it touches his: the marriage, the 1897 return, the 1942 arrest, the 1944 death. A limit of the pool.
5. **The Poona Pact** is summarized in one clause; Ambedkar's side is absent and the atlas has no Ambedkar journey to cross with.
6. The pool contains **no Gujarati or Hindi source** and no archival document. Everything is English-language encyclopedic, biographical-portal, or Gandhi's own prose.

---

## 11. Contradictions left standing

1. Pietermaritzburg ejection, 31 May vs 7 June 1893: both attested, journey carries 7 June with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
2. Madurai loincloth, 21 vs 22 September 1921: Wikipedia 21, Gandhian chronologies 22, journey carries 21 with the hedge.
3. Salt March distance, 385 vs 387 vs 388 km: journey uses 387, the others recorded.
4. Shots at Birla House, three vs four: journey says three, the source itself notes accounts differ.
5. Marriage venue, Porbandar vs Rajkot: Porbandar adopted, the Rajkot reading recorded as an atlas error.
6. Karamchand's death, "late 1885" vs November 1885: year attested, month traditional.
7. Tolstoy Farm, bought and made available vs donated: journey says bought and given over.

## 12. Sources

**Reached and used**
- Wikipedia: the article "Mahatma Gandhi", plus the twenty-odd site- and event-specific articles named individually at each point above, from "Kirti Mandir, Porbandar" through "Statue of Mahatma Gandhi, Parliament Square". Every coordinate correction in this file is sourced to one of them.

- Britannica, Biography.com, History.com, all "Mahatma Gandhi"; Gandhi Heritage Portal life-events page; South African History Online; UN resolution A/RES/61/271; RBI currency history; Academy Awards records

**Not reached, named with the reason**
- *The Collected Works of Mahatma Gandhi*: not consulted. Every quotation in the journey comes instead from a published edition of a single work or from mkgandhi.org's transcription of a speech. Given the CD-ROM controversy, the distinction is worth stating.
- Louis Fischer's and Ramachandra Guha's biographies: cited in the pool at second hand, not read.
- Manuben Gandhi's memoir, source of "Hey Ram": not read; the phrase is carried at **[R]** on that account.
- Inner Temple admission register: cited through Wikipedia, not consulted directly. Gujarati-language sources of any kind: none attempted.

**Quotation discipline**
Four of the fourteen quotations in the pool arrived silently truncated and were restored to the source wording before use: the Great Trial passage, *Hind Swaraj* on slavery ("...and of the luxuries that money can buy"), and the Dandi eve speech ("...and turns a deaf ear to the inner voice"). Quotations in the journey file are verbatim and stand outside the house style rules governing the prose around them.
