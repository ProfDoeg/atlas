# Carmen Miranda: research report
*Maria do Carmo Miranda da Cunha, 9 February 1909 (Várzea da Ovelha e Aliviada, Portugal) to 5 August 1955 (Beverly Hills, California). Compiled 2026-08-13. Journey: `carmen_miranda.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** tradition, reconstruction, or claim traceable only to secondary aggregation · **[gap]** the record is silent and is left silent.

**A defect in the pool that governs the whole file.** The geography lens carried a latitude sign error across essentially every Brazilian coordinate (Praça Mauá as 22.8952 instead of -22.8952, landing in the Red Sea; Salvador as 12.9718, landing in Yemen). The chronology and afterlife lenses used the correct sign, so every Brazilian coordinate here comes from those or was re-derived. Where the lenses disagree about the *place* and not the sign, that is flagged below and not averaged.

## 1. Portugal and the crossing, 1909-1910

**Birth. [A, English Wikipedia 'Carmen Miranda'; Observador (2015)]** 9 February 1909, parish of Várzea da Ovelha e Aliviada, concelho of Marco de Canaveses; second daughter of José Maria Pinto da Cunha, barber and amateur musician, and Maria Emília Miranda. The geography lens narrates the birth against "a republic barely a year old", wrong by twenty months: the First Republic was proclaimed 5 October 1910.

**Contradiction unresolved: three coordinates, one parish.** Chronology lens 41.1833 / -8.1667; geography lens 41.1919 / -8.1424; Wikidata Q1024873 **41.2086 / -8.1042**, spread over roughly 6 km. The journey uses the Wikidata value as the only one attached to an identifier rather than a lens; that does not make it right, and no parish source was reached. **[gap]** The house is identified nowhere. The parish was extinguished in 2013 and merged into Várzea, Aliviada e Folhada **[A]**, so the birthplace no longer exists under the name the register gives it.

**The name. [R]** English Wikipedia relays a family tradition that her father named her Carmen out of fondness for Bizet's opera; no primary document is cited. Load-bearing again in 1999 (§9).

**Emigration. [A, English Wikipedia]** The father went ahead to Rio in 1909; the mother followed in 1910 with Olinda and the infant Carmen. **[gap]** No ship, manifest, or pier is named; Praça Mauá is used as the period's dominant passenger port, an inference rather than a documented landing.

## 2. Rio childhood and the trades, 1910-1928

- **Siblings. [A]** Amaro (1912), Cecília (1913), Aurora (1915), Óscar (1916), all born in Brazil. Aurora became a film actress and appeared with Carmen in *Alô, Alô, Carnaval!* (1936).
- **Travessa do Comércio. [R, Cinema Clássico and the biographical literature]** Several secondary biographies place the family home on this lane; no document fixes the house. The geography lens gave -43.1729, the corpus central-Rio pin rather than the street, which sits nearer -43.177.
- **Convent of Saint Therese of Lisieux. [A as to the school, English Wikipedia]** The pool tagged this **[R]**, under-reading the source: Wikipedia names the convent flatly as her school. Only the address is unverified, a caveat carried in `date_confidence`. **[gap]** The years of attendance are nowhere given.
- **The trades, c. 1923 to the later 1920s. [A, English Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Work at about fourteen in a necktie shop to help pay for Olinda's tuberculosis treatment, then a boutique, hat-making, and her own millinery business. **[gap]** Neither shop is named and no address is given. This is the material link between the Rio shopgirl and the Fox turban: she designed her own costumes for the rest of her life.

## 3. Records and radio, 1929-1938

All from English Wikipedia, 'Carmen Miranda', unless noted.

- **First single, 1929. [A]** 'Não Vá Simbora', with Josué de Barros. **Correction:** the chronology lens attached this to RCA Victor. The label was **Brunswick**; the RCA Victor deal is a separate event of 1930.
- **'Taí' / 'Pra Você Gostar de Mim', 1930. [A]** Joubert de Carvalho; 35,000 copies; two-year RCA Victor contract follows. The sales figure is repeated with no label statement cited anywhere: received, not audited. The sound documentary *O Carnaval Cantado no Rio* follows in 1932 **[A]**.
- **Rádio Mayrink Veiga, 1933. [A]** First contract singer in Brazilian radio history, two-year term, highest-paid voice on the air; nicknamed **A Pequena Notável** by the announcer César Ladeira. **[gap]** No station address in the sourcing; Cinelândia is an approximation. The geography lens dated the stint 1933-1936, an endpoint requiring an unstated renewal, so it is not asserted.
- **Rádio Tupi, 1937. [A]** One-year engagement.
- **Films, 1935-1939. [A, with the filmography article]** *Alô, Alô, Brasil!* and *Estudantes* (1935, first speaking role), *Alô, Alô, Carnaval!* (1936, with Aurora), and *Banana da Terra* (1939), which introduced the baiana costume with 'O Que É Que A Baiana Tem?'. The fruit was never real.

**The Bahia gap, stated as a gap.** No source places her performing in Salvador or anywhere in Bahia, the region whose street-vendor dress she wore in nearly every American film; the geography lens flagged this correctly as **[R]**. The most important negative finding in the file: the costume travels where she does not. An absence in the reachable record, not a proof of absence.

## 4. Broadway and Washington, 1939-1940

- **Shubert at the Cassino da Urca, 1939 [A, English Wikipedia; Cinema Clássico]**, offering eight weeks on Broadway; **departure 4 May 1939 aboard the SS Uruguay, arrival New York 18 May 1939 [A]**, with the Brazilian government funding the Bando da Lua's passage.
- **Broadway debut, 19 June 1939, *The Streets of Paris*, Broadhurst Theatre. [A]** Four words of English onstage; excellent reviews; "the girl who saved Broadway from the World's Fair."
- **Down Argentine Way, 1940. [A]** Hollywood debut with Don Ameche and Betty Grable; about $2 million gross; shot partly in New York because the Broadway contract would not release her. **[R]** The claim that it was *banned in Argentina* appears only in the interlock lens's note on the Eva Perón file; the journey says only that Argentine critics objected.

**Quote problem, unresolved. [R]** The pool gave an Atkinson line as *"South American contributes the revue's most magnetic personality."* The only traceable text is Wikipedia citing his June 1939 *NYT* review as **"South American contributes the [revue's] most magnetic personality"**, the brackets marking *revue's* as an editorial insertion, not Atkinson's word. The pool stripped them; the journey restores them and keeps the **[R]** tag.

**Roosevelt and the Good Neighbor Policy. [A, themovierat.com; English Wikipedia for the poll]** Presented to FDR, performed with the Bando da Lua, polled third most popular personality in the United States. **Contradiction flagged:** the chronology lens dates this 1940, the geography lens "c. 1939-1941". The journey uses 1940-01-01 and records the spread.

## 5. The homecoming and the booing, July to September 1940

The hinge of the life, and the part of the record in worst repair. **Return, 10 July 1940. [A]** A cheering welcome that soured within days into press and upper-class attack: pandering to American commercialism, and an image held "too black."

**The booing: two incompatible placements, both in the pool, unresolved.**
- Chronology and afterlife lenses: the charity concert organized by First Lady **Darcy Vargas, 15 July 1940**; she wept in her dressing room afterward.
- Geography lens: a benefit at the **Cassino da Urca**, where she greeted the crowd in English, opened with 'South American Way', was booed off, and cancelled the engagement.

One event told twice, or two in one week. Nothing reached adjudicates. The journey carries **both stops**; the Urca one is tagged `date_confidence: "contested"` and says outright that accounts differ and that only the fact of the booing is common ground.

**Correction to the pool.** The afterlife lens asserted that after 1940 "she does not perform live in Brazil again." False: she returned to Rio **3 December 1954**, stayed four months, left **4 April 1955** **[A]**. The correct formulation, used throughout, is that she did not return for **fourteen years**. Her lodging at the Copacabana Palace in 1940 is **[R]**, tradition with no primary confirmation.

**Lyric correction, important.** 'Disseram que Voltei Americanizada' was recorded for Odeon on 2 September 1940, by Luiz Peixoto and Vicente Paiva **[A]**. The pool gave the opening as *"Disseram que eu voltei americanizada / Com o dinheiro que tanto ambicionei."* **The second line does not exist in the song.** The authentic opening, per cancioneros.com (entry 23153) and Brazilian lyrics archives:

> Disseram que eu voltei americanizada / Com o burro do dinheiro / Que eu estou muito rica

The journey uses the corrected text; the spurious line is deleted rather than footnoted, because a wrong lyric propagates.

**Two further quote problems. [R]** The pool lists 'Bananas Is My Business' as a later song about her stereotyped image; the same phrase titles Solberg's 1995 documentary, and the material does not separate the two. And "singing bad-taste black sambas" recurs in secondary summaries attributed to an unnamed Brazilian newspaper of 1940, with no original text, title, or date located.

## 6. The Fox years, 1941-1946

Sourced as in §3 unless noted.

- **Grauman's Chinese Theatre, 24 March 1941. [A]** First Latin American to leave hand- and footprints in the forecourt. **[R]** The pool also cut the Variety notice of *That Night in Rio* mid-clause with no ellipsis; the journey restores the sentence, ending "from the first sequence," as quoted in Wikipedia.
- **Week-End in Havana, 1941. [A]** Topped the box office; reportedly outgrossed *Citizen Kane*, a repeated claim rather than a reconciliation of takings. **Shubert bought out, 1942 [A]**: Fox paid him $60,000 to end his claim. **The Gang's All Here, 1943 [A, with 'The Lady in the Tutti Frutti Hat']**: Busby Berkeley's number, top-ten grossing release of the year.
- **Highest-paid woman in the United States, 1945, over $200,000 a year. [A as a report]** No tax record or studio ledger is cited anywhere. The Fox contract expired in **1946 [A]**, postwar films increasingly shot in black and white.

**Correction, and a split event.** The chronology lens bundled the Walk of Fame star into the 1941 Grauman's entry. Impossible: the Walk was not created until 1958 and its first stars were laid in 1960. Her star came **posthumously in 1960, television category, 6262 Hollywood Boulevard**, so the journey splits these into two stops. The date usually printed, 8 February 1960, is the Walk's **groundbreaking** applied to the whole first batch, not a dedication of her own.

## 7. After the studio, 1947-1953

All from English Wikipedia, 'Carmen Miranda' **[A]**: *Copacabana* (1947) with Groucho Marx, on whose set she met David Sebastian; marriage to Sebastian at the Church of the Good Shepherd, Beverly Hills, 17 March 1947; pregnancy and miscarriage after a show, 1948; *A Date with Judy* (MGM, 1948), for which the studio removed the turban and put her in gowns; three Decca records with the Andrews Sisters, 1948-1950, 'Cuanto Le Gusta' reaching No. 12 on Billboard; separation announced September 1949, reconciled within months; *Nancy Goes to Rio* (1950); *Scared Stiff* (1953), her last film. **[gap]** The Decca recording city is undocumented, so that stop sits on the corpus Hollywood pin.

**MGM coordinate correction.** The geography lens put MGM Culver City at 34.0211 / -118.4165, about 1.4 km west. The lot (now Sony Pictures Studios, 10202 W Washington Blvd) is at **34.0172 / -118.4017**.

**Two undated club stops. [R]** Ciro's on the Sunset Strip came from the geography lens with no dated booking located, and is **dropped** rather than invented. The New York Copacabana is **kept and flagged**, its year defaulted on tradition.

**Cincinnati collapse, October 1953, and the LeRoy Sanitarium. [A, corrected]** The pool placed the sanitarium **in Cincinnati**. It was not: it stood at **40 East 61st Street, Lenox Hill, New York**, founded 1928 by Alice Fuller LeRoy, closed 1980. She collapsed in Cincinnati and was hospitalized in New York, so the journey carries two stops. **[gap]** Its coordinate (40.7644 / -73.9695) was derived from the street address by this compiler. Smoking, alcohol, amphetamines and barbiturates in these years are **[A] as reported**, with no medical record quoted anywhere reached.

## 8. Death and funeral, August 1955

**The Jimmy Durante Show, 4 August 1955. [A, with a correction]** She **filmed**, did not broadcast live, a musical segment; during 'Jackson, Miranda and Gomez' she fell to one knee, recovered, finished the number, then performed again informally for cast and technicians. **The pool overstated this twice:** the chronology lens said she "collapsed on set after completing a number", the afterlife lens that she collapsed on air "the night before" her death. Neither matches the source, and "the night before" wrongly implies a day's gap: taping and death fall inside the same twenty-four hours. The segment aired posthumously.

**Death, 5 August 1955, Beverly Hills, heart attack, age 46. [A]** Found in a hallway about 10:30 a.m. **[gap]** The address is unverified; the coordinate marks the city.

**The funeral. [A, date corrected]** The pool dated the whole sequence 12 August. The body lay in state at Rio's câmara municipal from the **evening of 12 August to the morning of 13 August**; cortège and burial followed on **13 August 1955**. Roughly 60,000 filed past, an estimated half a million or more lined the route, and national mourning was declared. Crowd figures vary by source and none is independently verifiable.

**Coordinate corrections.** The geography lens put the "municipal hall" at 22.9083 / -43.1964: sign error plus 2 km too far west. The **Palácio Pedro Ernesto** on Cinelândia is about **-22.9105 / -43.1762**, and the coffin was driven there from Praça Mauá. The cemetery is given three ways in the pool; **Cemitério de São João Batista**, Botafogo, is at **-22.9590 / -43.1880**.

## 9. Afterlife, 1960-2023

- **Museu Carmen Miranda [A, English Wikipedia 'Carmen Miranda Museum']:** decreed 1956, opened **5 August 1976** on the twenty-first anniversary of her death, circular pavilion by **Affonso Eduardo Reidy** adapted by **Ulisses Burlemaqui**, Flamengo Park; closed 2013, reopened **4 August 2023**. **[gap]** The claim that flooding caused the closure could not be confirmed.
- **Collection and statue. [A, pool figures corrected]** Wikipedia gives **3,560 items including 461 pieces of clothing**; the pool's upper figures (3,862 items, 500 garments) are unsupported and dropped, and the claimed 1941 Academy Awards gown could not be verified **[gap]**. The life-size resin figure by **Ulysses Rabelo**, cast from her death mask and dental arch in her costume from *That Night in Rio*, dates from **February 2009**, not 2023 as the pool had it.
- **Carmen Miranda Square, 25 September 1998. [A]** Ceremony led by honorary Hollywood mayor Johnny Grant. **Coordinate correction:** the afterlife lens gave 34.1016 / -118.3267, the Walk of Fame star, 1.2 km east. The Square is at Hollywood Boulevard and Orange Drive, approximately **34.1020 / -118.3403**.
- **Bananas Is My Business (Helena Solberg, 1995) [A]**, pivot of the modern reappraisal; the camp and drag iconography **[A as commentary]**, which has no fixed site and gets no stop; the **Google Doodle of 9 February 2017 by Sophie Diao [A]**, crediting the fruit hat to the baianas rather than to Hollywood, as Miranda herself did in the 1948 London interview quoted in the journey.

**Veloso quote, contradiction flagged.** The pool carried *"later, a symbol of the intellectual violence with which we wanted to face our reality"* as Veloso's *NYT* wording. That matches no located source. Remezcla, reproducing his English text, gives **"And later, a symbol that inspired the merciless gaze we began to cast upon ourselves."** A translation of the Portuguese (Revista Pesquisa FAPESP) gives "afterwards a symbol of intellectual violence on how we wanted to confront our reality." The journey uses the Remezcla wording; the *NYT* original is paywalled.

## Interlocks with the existing atlas

Named in campa where the paths cross. In each the overlap of place and date is attested; the juxtaposition is the compiler's.

**arthur_bispo_do_rosario**, committed to the Colônia Juliano Moreira, Rio, 24 December 1938, five months before Miranda sailed for Broadway from the same city under the same Estado Novo. **george_balanchine**, whose Ballet Caravan toured South America in 1941 through Nelson Rockefeller's Office of Inter-American Affairs, the apparatus behind Miranda's Good Neighbor deployment, and **diego_rivera**, whose Rockefeller Center mural the same Rockefeller destroyed in 1934. **eva_peron**, a Buenos Aires radio actress 1935-1945, exactly Miranda's rise, pinned at *Down Argentine Way*: an Argentina played by a Brazilian. **madame_chiang_kai_shek**, before Congress in February 1943 as a foreign woman American media had already decided it knew. **meyer_lansky** and **charles_lucky_luciano**, the supper-club economy her post-Fox career lived inside; weakest of the set, used once. **caterina_valente**, queued directly below Miranda, American television debut in Hollywood in 1955, the year Miranda died. **augusto_boal**, who staged a samba version of Bizet's *Carmen* in Rio in 1999, Bizet's and not Miranda's though she was named for it **[R]**; framed as coincidence.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identically:** central Rio **-22.9068 / -43.1729** (`augusto_boal`, `arthur_bispo_do_rosario`); Hollywood **34.098 / -118.3267** (`l_frank_baum`, `fritz_lang`, `caterina_valente`). Paris and Buenos Aires were **not** inherited: no documented Miranda stop exists in either city.
