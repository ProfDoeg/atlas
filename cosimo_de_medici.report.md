# Cosimo di Giovanni de' Medici (Cosimo il Vecchio)
*Research report, 2026-08-28. Florence, 1389-1464. Banker; three times gonfaloniere di giustizia; holder of no permanent office; decreed Pater Patriae seven months after his death.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried only by secondary literature not verified at the primary level. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated. Where the incoming research pool was wrong, the wrong version stays visible beside the correction.

---

## 1. Birth and formation, 1389-1420

**Born 10 April 1389, parish of San Lorenzo [A: Treccani DBI, Dale Kent; en.wikipedia].** Son of Giovanni di Bicci de' Medici and Piccarda Bueri; a twin, Damiano, probably died in infancy.

**Two birth dates coexist and neither cancels the other [A].** Treccani gives 10 April as the documentary date; 27 September, feast of Saints Cosmas and Damian, was the observed birthday and name-day. One is archival, the other liturgical, and the family kept the second.

**Tutored by the humanist Roberto de' Rossi [A: Treccani].** No year is fixed; 1400 in the journey file is an ordering date.

**No Greek; about a hundred non-literary vernacular letters certainly autograph [R].** Attributed to James Hankins in the crawled dossier. **The Hankins article was not fetched at any stage.**

**Council of Constance, 1414-15, in Baldassarre Cossa's entourage [R].** *A contradiction inside the research pool itself, unresolved:* the chronology lens tagged the trip **R**, the geography lens tagged the same event **A**. Both source families hedge, Treccani with *si ritiene che il M. abbia partecipato*, en.wikipedia with *allegedly accompanied*. The weaker reading is used; the return-in-disguise story is [R] and unused.

**Prior of the Arte del Cambio, 1415 [A: Treccani].**

**Marriage to Contessina de' Bardi, c. 1415 [A as fact, gap as date].** Janet Ross states outright that the date **is not recorded**; 1415 is the reference works' convention, corroborated by Treccani. Contessina's parentage is given inconsistently across sources; the Bardi affiliation is not. **Piero born 1416 [A: Ross].**

**Old Sacristy of San Lorenzo commissioned by Giovanni di Bicci, 1419 [A: Treccani].** Brunelleschi builds, Donatello later decorates, the parents' tomb sits in the floor. The division of work between father and sons is undocumented year by year.

## 2. Embassies and succession, 1420-1429

**Missions, all [A: Treccani]:** Milan 1420, Lucca 1423, Bologna 1424, papal court at Rome 1426. The venue within each city is inferred and no month is recorded. A **Ferrara** mission "in the later 1420s" appeared in the geography lens with no year at all; dropped rather than kept with an invented date.

**Bank, 1420 [A: Treccani].** Cosimo and Lorenzo take operational control from their father; the Roman manager becomes *depositario* to Martin V, an office held for roughly twenty-two years.

**Tomb of Cossa in the Baptistery: the pool was wrong twice.** **Cosimo was not an executor of the will [A: en.wikipedia, 'Tomb of Antipope John XXIII'].** The four executors were *Bartolommeo di Taldo Valori, Niccolò da Uzzano, Giovanni di Bicci de' Medici, and Vieri Guadagni*: the father only, though all three lenses asserted Cosimo's executorship. And the dates were given as 1419, 1421-1427 and 1424-1427 in different places: Cossa was freed 1418 on a Medici ransom and died 22 December 1419, and the tomb was completed during the 1420s, documented from at least 1424.

**Carlo born to Maddalena, an enslaved Circassian woman of the household [A as fact, contested as date].** The pool gave 1429; **1429 is not an attested year.** The literature gives **1428 or 1430**, Maddalena bought in Venice 1427. Carlo was acknowledged, raised with the legitimate children, and died provost of Prato.

**Giovanni di Bicci dies 20 February 1429 [A: Ross; Treccani].** The deathbed counsel (stay out of the public eye, never appear to give orders) is **[R]**, Machiavelli's and the later biographers', not an instrument; Ross preserves instructions to the sons without those words.

## 3. The Lucca war, the tower, the exile, 1429-1434

**War with Lucca, 1429-1433 [A: Treccani].** Cosimo, his cousin Averardo and their associates financed a costly failure that sharpened the conflict with Rinaldo degli Albizzi's circle.

**Arrest at the Palazzo dei Priori, 5 September 1433 [A].** *A claimed source conflict is withdrawn.* The chronology lens reported Ross giving 7 September against Treccani and it.wikipedia giving 5 September; on direct inspection **the Ross text supplies no 7 September in the exile narrative.** Inventing a standoff would be worse than the original error.

**Confinement in the Alberghettino, 5 September to 3 October 1433 [A as to the confinement].** Venetian intervention: **[R]**. Money paid to convert a death sentence into banishment: **[A as Cosimo's own later account]**, which is not independent attestation. The loyal jailer tasting his food: **[R]**, later embellishment on no trial record.

**The exile itinerary is the strongest primary material in the subject**, Cosimo's own dated *ricordo* in Ross, pp. 24-25: leaves Florence 3 October; **4 Oct Cutigliano** in the mountains of Pistoia; **5 Oct "Fassano"** in the territory of the Marquess of Ferrara; **6 Oct Modena**; **7 Oct Bondeno**; **8 Oct Francolino** by water; **11 Oct Venice**; **13 Oct Padua**, the assigned place of exile. Every leg is [A] and quoted verbatim in the journey file. Three corrections:
- **Bondeno's hedge is withdrawn.** The pool called it "less independently verified than its neighbours"; it is in Ross verbatim, *On the 7th I was at Bondeno and next day went by water to Francolino*.
- **Francolino retagged R to A** on that same sentence, which also fixes its date at 8 October.
- **"Fassano" is an honest gap:** quoted verbatim, unmatched to any present-day settlement, and its pin is an approximation inside Este territory, the weakest in the file.

**Venice [A for the words, R for the politics].** *Like an ambassador, not like an exile*, and the offer of *the Signoria, the city, and their treasure for my needs, and a house*, are Cosimo's own in Ross. Foscari's personal role and the Venetian elite's support are **[R]**, dossier synthesis.

## 4. Return and regime, 1434-1441

**The balìa revoking the exile [R], September 1434.** Dated **28 September** by storiadifirenze.org and **29 September** elsewhere; unresolved. And **storiadifirenze.org could not be retrieved on re-check**, WebFetch being served the site portal rather than the article, so its Petriboni quotations are second-hand here.

**The return, 5-6 October 1434. Sources agree on the window and differ on the sequence; the conflict is preserved.** **Ross (Cosimo's own)** has him re-enter the Commune on the 5th at the same place he left, then *On the 6th we arrived at our own house, Careggi, to dinner, and found much people.* **storiadifirenze**, citing Petriboni's diary, has him reach the city on the 5th and sleep at the palace welcomed by the priors, with Eugenius IV receiving him on the 6th. **it.wikipedia** dates the return, and the start of his de facto rule, to 6 October. The file dates both stops 6 October, orders them Careggi then palace, and prints the disagreement.

**Albizzi and Peruzzi condemned to exile, 2 October 1434 [A: storiadifirenze, second-hand].**

**The accoppiatori [A: storiadifirenze, second-hand].** Officials controlling which eligible names entered the electoral bags: preemptive control of office-holding, with the drawing by lot left intact. This is the mechanism of the regime, and it is stated at the 1435 gonfalonierate, where Cosimo's diary claim to have banished nobody is set against the mass exile the balìa had already voted. **Gonfaloniere di giustizia January 1435 and January 1439 [A: Treccani].**

**Consecration of Florence Cathedral by Eugenius IV, 25 March 1436 [A: en.wikipedia].** Pool tagged **R**; verifiable verbatim, including that 25 March opened the Florentine year, so **retagged A**. That Cosimo funded Brunelleschi's dome is unsupported by anything consulted: the dome (1420-1436) was a cathedral-works and guild project predating his supremacy.

**Council of Ferrara-Florence, 1439 [A].** As gonfalonier he received John VIII Palaiologos, Patriarch Joseph II, Bessarion and Plethon; Medici money helped fund the transfer. The pool's hedge on the venue is weaker than the evidence: en.wikipedia states all sessions were held at Santa Maria Novella, and only the room used for the reception is unconfirmed.

**Bruges branch founded 24 March 1439 [A: en.wikipedia, 'Medici Bank'].** Pool tagged **R**; verbatim, **retagged A**. Cosimo never went to Bruges, so it sits inside the 1464 Careggi stop, not as a pin in Flanders.

**Anghiari, 29 June 1440 [A].** Cosimo was neither present nor in command; the stop stands in Florence, where he was.

**Lorenzo the Elder dies at Careggi [A, date corrected].** Pool gave **20 September 1440**; his dates are 12 October 1394 to **23 September 1440**.

**Baldaccio d'Anghiari killed 6 September 1441 [A as to the killing, R as to Cosimo].** Summoned by Gonfalonier Bartolomeo Orlandini, set on by his men, thrown from a height, beheaded. Cosimo's authorization is **alleged by contemporaries (Filelfo), repeated by Machiavelli, proven by no surviving order**, and the pool's lenses disagreed on the tag (chronology R, geography A). The file gives him the benefit of neither: he gained from the weakening of Neri Capponi's client, and nothing surviving connects him to the order.

## 5. Building, 1420-1456

**Bosco ai Frati [A, retagged and redated].** Pool tagged **R** and dated it to the 1440s from the general Mugello pattern. it.wikipedia is explicit that Cosimo bought the convent in 1420 and financed Michelozzo's rebuilding of the church of San Bonaventura from that year. **Retagged A, redated to 1420.**

**Orsanmichele, Ghiberti's bronze Saint Matthew, 1419-1422 [A as a guild act].** Cosimo's role beyond guild membership is not separately documented; the commission is the guild's and not his.

**Convent of San Marco, from 1437 [A].** Michelozzo 1437/38-1444; Fra Angelico resident 1438-1445; some 40,000 florins; the library built for Niccolò Niccoli's collection, often called the first public library of Renaissance Europe, where "public" means controlled scholarly access. **One overstatement removed:** the source gives Cosimo *a cell*, not the *double* cell the pool claimed.

**Villa di Cafaggiolo [A as fact, contradiction as date].** villegiardinimedicei.it gives **1443-1451**, en.wikipedia gives **1452**. The pool carried the first without noticing the second. Both are printed and neither is chosen.

**Villa di Trebbio [R].** Michelozzo's earlier Mugello remodelling. Kept here; **dropped from the journey file** for weak attestation and to hold the stop count, not because it is doubted.

**Palazzo Medici, commissioned 1444, built to 1484 [A].** Michelozzo architect. Vasari's story of a grander Brunelleschi design refused for fear of envy is **[R]**, labelled as Vasari's in the campa.

**Badia Fiesolana [A, retagged].** Pool tagged **R** for want of a site-specific source. en.wikipedia: *The present appearance dates from between 1456 and 1467, after the architect Michelozzo was commissioned by Cosimo de' Medici.* That span independently supports the "unfinished at his death" reading.

**Jerusalem hospices [R, dropped].** Medici-sponsored houses at Jerusalem, reported in commemorative accounts, verified by nobody. The canonical Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris and Buenos Aires pins offered by the interlock lens are therefore **not inherited**.

**Ten pins were wrong in the pool and are corrected in the stops**: Francolino by 6 km, Bosco ai Frati 5.5, Cafaggiolo 4.5, Cutigliano 1.3, **Careggi at six separate stops**, and five more by 120-310 m. Palazzo Medici is the instructive case, the geography lens putting it a block west of the Via Larga while the afterlife lens had it right.

## 6. Foreign policy without an office, 1450-1458

**Backing Francesco Sforza's seizure of Milan, 1450 [A: Treccani]**, reversing the traditional Florentine alliance with Venice. **Peace of Lodi, 9 April 1454 [A: Treccani]**; Cosimo did not go to Lodi, so there is no Lodi pin.

**Letter to Sforza, 25 February 1454 n.s. [R].** Autograph or probably autograph, on papal intentions and secret Milan-Venice negotiations. **The Christie's lot description is behind an HTTP 403**; only the surrounding diplomacy is attested, in Treccani and en.wikipedia.

**Refusal of Sforza's offer of troops, 1455 [R].** Dossier synthesis; folded into the 1458 stop.

**Catasto restored and the Council of One Hundred created, 1458 [R].** Reported in secondary literature (the dossier cites George Mason University's Catasto project); not verified against any primary source. Sforza's troops under Astorgio Manfredi nearby during the scrutiny: same status.

## 7. Last years, death, afterlife

**Cosimino dies young, 1459 [A: Treccani]**, and **Giovanni di Cosimo on 1 November 1463 [A: Treccani]**, removing the healthier son and leaving the chronically ill Piero as heir. **"Too large a house for so small a family" [R]:** Vespasiano da Bisticci's, not confirmed in anything retrieved, and carried as "is remembered saying."

**Still directing Tommaso Portinari at Bruges, July 1464 [R].** Dossier synthesis of bank-correspondence scholarship, not retrieved. Kept and labelled, because it cuts against the humanist image of a contemplative retirement.

**Death at Careggi, 1 August 1464 [A: Treccani].** Buried in San Lorenzo before the high altar. **Verrocchio's floor monument, 1465-1467 [A].**

**The gout question: the pool debunked itself wrongly.** The chronology lens claimed PMC3279076 concerns only Cosimo I (1519-1574). Not so: the paper (Tonelli, *Was it true gout? New interpretations of the skeletal disease(s) of the Medici family*, not *A confocal microscopy study* as the afterlife lens titled it) does treat Cosimo the Elder, through the hands in Pontormo's portrait. **The real caveat is different and stronger:** the skeletons yielding the diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis reading are the **grand-ducal** Medici, from the 1924, 1955 and 2009 campaigns, and **Cosimo il Vecchio's remains lie in the San Lorenzo crypt and were not in that series.** Any attribution to him rests on **iconography, not bone**.

**Pater Patriae decreed March 1465 [A: Treccani].** Never a lifetime title. **Inherited error corrected:** the afterlife lens called it "an honour last given in antiquity to Cicero." False. It became an imperial honour from Augustus in 2 BC and twenty-three emperors held it, down to Julian in 361; en.wikipedia says *once awarded to Cicero*, and "once" had been silently upgraded to "last."

**Gibbon [A as quotation, misdated in the pool].** *His credit was ennobled into fame; his riches were dedicated to the service of mankind; he corresponded at once with Cairo and London.* Dated 1880 by the afterlife lens; Gibbon died 1794, so 1880 is only the edition cited.

## 8. Quotations: what is real, what is not

**Machiavelli, *History of Florence*, Book VII [A]**, archive.org item `historyofflorenc00machuoft`. Four passages verified by search-inside and all four used: the character portrait, the debts discovered after his death, the complaint about never finding the balance in his own favour, and the funeral with the decree inscribing FATHER OF HIS COUNTRY. All are **about** Cosimo, and the third is **indirect speech**.

**Lorenzo de' Medici, Book VIII [A].** *Cosmo, my grandfather, did not return from exile with arms and violence, but by your unanimous desire and approbation*, spoken 1478, used as `quote_source` on the return stop, whose campa notes this is true in form and that the parliament which voted it met in a piazza full of Cosimo's armed men.

**Francis Bacon, *Apophthegms New and Old* no. 206 [R].** The saying about forgiving enemies but not friends, verbatim on Wikisource. **The attribution is not safe**: "Duke of Florence" belonged only to Cosimo I from 1532. Not used as a quote; a `suggested_ref` with the conflation stated.

**Lucas-Dubreton (1961), p. 58, the envy saying [R].** Located through Open Library's index after every archive.org copy returned 401. It is **indirect speech** (*He also observed that there is in gardens a plant…*), so the file quotes only the clause and labels it reported, and it remains a **1961 popular history with no traceable primary source**.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Cosimo de' Medici is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** Relations found in the research that are **not rooted in his own life** are listed rather than silently deleted. **Every one is FORWARD**, later people engaging with places and institutions he made, which is the direction the exception never licenses. None was staged as a scene.

- **Michelangelo as a boy in the Medici sculpture garden at San Marco**, Cosimo's foundation a generation earlier. **Forward.** The garden is a `suggested_ref` on the San Marco stop; the boy is absent, and that scene is already in `michelangelo.journey.json`.
- **Rodin in the New Sacristy at San Lorenzo, 1875. Forward, doubly.** The Old Sacristy stop notes the New Sacristy in a `suggested_ref`.
- **Leonardo:** boyhood Florence "with the Medici bank running the credit of half of Europe"; Ser Piero notarising in Cosimo's last decade; the 1476 denunciation; later Medici papal service. **Forward.** Not named; the lens itself tagged the Ser Piero overlap R, "weak, purely chronological." The Anghiari stop cites Leonardo's lost mural of that battle in a `suggested_ref`, the licensed form: a later engagement cited on the object.
- **Pico at the Palazzo Medici in 1484; Lorenzo dying at Careggi in 1492. Forward.** Not named.
- **Constantine XI and the Union of Florence, 6 July 1439.** One-directional: his brother attended, he did not. The lens itself ruled this a source and not a scene, and *Laetentur Caeli* is a `suggested_ref`.
- **Nicholas of Cusa fetching John VIII and Joseph II for the council Cosimo hosted and financed.** Contemporary, not forward. **Considered and left out**: Cosimo received the participants as gonfalonier and Cusa was in the Latin party, but no source attests a meeting, and co-presence is not a relation here. **If the operator counts host-and-delegate, this is the one live candidate for an added interlock.**
- **Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris, Buenos Aires pins.** Structural, not biographical. Not used.

**The one interlock named in a campa is `lorenzo_de_medici_il_magnifico`**, at the Palazzo Medici stop: Cosimo's grandson, born 1449 in the casa vecchia fifty metres from the palace then rising. A direct family relation, not shared geography. Lorenzo also supplies the `quote_source` on the return-from-exile stop.

## 10. Honest gaps

"Fassano," 5 October 1433, quoted verbatim and unlocatable on any modern map · the day of the marriage to Contessina, which Ross says outright is not recorded · Cosimo's schooling dates · Carlo's birth year, 1428 or 1430 and no better · the day of the funeral in August 1464 · the room within Santa Maria Novella used for the 1439 reception · whether Cosimo ordered Baldaccio's killing, no surviving order either way · whether he went to Constance at all, both source families hedging · the contents of the 1454 Sforza letter, whose only description is behind a 403.
