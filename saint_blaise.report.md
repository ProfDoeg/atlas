# Saint Blaise of Sebastea: research report

*2026-08-08. Blasius of Sebastea; Sveti Vlaho of Dubrovnik; San Biagio of Maratea. Bishop, physician, one of the Fourteen Holy Helpers, patron of the throat and of the wool combers.*

**[A] = attested, source named. [R] = reconstruction, legend, local tradition, hagiographic accretion.**

## Method

The cult is enormous, continuously observed, pinned to real buildings in five countries; the life rests on one document written roughly four centuries after the death by an unknown author. The controlling verdict is the church's own: "All the particulars concerning his life and martyrdom which are found in the Acts are purely legendary and have no claim to historical worth" **[A, Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), s.v. "St Blaise"]**. Everything in sections 1 to 5 is therefore **[R]** unless tagged otherwise; an **[A]** there attaches to *what a text says*, never to the event narrated.

What survives: the name; the see of **Sebastea in Lesser Armenia**, modern Sivas **[A, Wikipedia; OrthodoxWiki]**; a martyrdom **under Licinius, about 316** **[A as the near-unanimous traditional reckoning, Catholic Encyclopedia]**; and one sixth-century medical sentence invoking him for the throat **[A]**, treated in section 6.

## 1. Birth and origins

Born at **Sebastea (Sivas; 39.7477, 37.0179)**, capital of Lesser Armenia, to a wealthy noble family **[R, legendary Acts via theeasternchurch.com, maryourhelp.org]**.

**No source gives a birth year.** Wikipedia's infobox carries "? AD"; the Acts date nothing. The **280** in the research pool has no support in any consulted source **[gap]**. The journey carries `0280-01-01` because the schema needs a sortable date, with the hedge in `date_confidence`; the honest answer is *unrecorded*.

City and see are the solid part **[A, Wikipedia; OrthodoxWiki; Butler's]**. He studies philosophy, then medicine, practises without charging, treats animals as well as people **[R]**; Orthodox usage classes him among the **anargyroi**, the unmercenary physician saints **[A as classification, OrthodoxWiki]**. Elected **bishop by acclamation** **[R]**.

**A cross-reference no source makes.** Armenia's conversion under **Tiridates III via Gregory the Illuminator, traditionally 301, at Vagharshapat (40.1596, 44.2907)** falls inside his supposed adult lifetime, a fortnight's ride east. Nothing ancient or modern connects the two, and the atlas holds no traveler for either. A **gap**, not a crossing **[absence check across working/journeys/]**.

## 2. The imperial frame, and its first contradiction

The **Licinian rescript** of toleration is published at **Nicomedia (40.7654, 29.9408), June 313** **[A; pin and date inherited byte-identical from constantino_el_grande.journey.json]**. The Acts have persecution **continue** in Lesser Armenia afterwards, and place the arrest and death in **316, under the governor Agricolaus (Agricola), by order of Licinius** **[R, Acta Sanctorum tradition per Wikipedia]**.

> **CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED.** Licinius is not documented persecuting Christians in 316. In 316 he is fighting Constantine in Pannonia: **Cibalae, 8 October 316 (45.2019, 18.6939)** **[A, constantino_el_grande.journey.json]**. His anti-Christian measures are dated c. 320 to 324 and reported chiefly by Eusebius under Constantine **[A, Wikipedia "Licinius"]**. Either the Acts have the emperor wrong, or the year wrong, or they describe a governor's private initiative the imperial record never noticed. None of the three is adopted; the strain is stated in `date_confidence` on the martyrdom and Cibalae stops.

**Licinius is broken at Chrysopolis, 18 September 324 (41.0186, 29.0281)**, flees to Nicomedia, is later killed **[A, constantino_el_grande.journey.json]**.

## 3. The cave

He withdraws to a cave during the persecution; wild animals come to him, are healed, do him no harm **[R, Golden Legend; Wikipedia's summary of the Acts; franciscanmedia.org]**.

> **BAD PIN, CORRECTED, RIVAL CLAIM RETAINED.** The pool put this cave on **Mount Argaeus (Erciyes, 38.531, 35.447)**. That identification is not in the cited sources. Wikipedia says only "he retired for a time to a cavern where he remained in prayer." The Golden Legend says he hid "in an hermitage in a ditch, in which place the birds of heaven brought to him meat for to eat": unnamed, unlocated **[A as to what the texts say]**. Erciyes stands beside **Caesarea / Kayseri, about 165 km straight-line from Sebastea**, in another province with its own metropolitan see **[A, Wikipedia "Mount Erciyes"]**. The journey carries **two stops**: the hermitage at Sebastea's coordinates, the sources' actual position (unlocated), and Argaeus pinned separately and labelled a late, geographically implausible gloss. Both mapped, neither preferred.

## 4. Arrest, the road, the prison

All **[R]**, all from the Acts and their Latin descendant the Golden Legend.

**Hunters sent by Agricolaus** find him through the tame animals **[R, theeasternchurch.com; Acta Sanctorum cited in Wikipedia]**. *Tag corrected from the pool's [A]: citing the Acta Sanctorum for a hunters-and-tame-beasts arrest does not make it documentary.*

**The widow's pig.** A wolf takes a poor widow's pig; at Blaise's word it returns alive **[R, Golden Legend]**. Verbatim: "Good woman anger thee not, for thou shalt have again thy swine" **[A as text, Caxton, Fordham]**.

**The fishbone.** A mother brings her choking son; he prays and the bone comes free **[R, Golden Legend; maryourhelp.org]**. Source of the throat patronage; the Caxton wording is quoted in full at the corresponding journey stop.

- *Two flags, neither adjudicated:* that wording is nearly identical to the dying prayer at the beheading, so the two petitions may be one episode split in transmission; and some tellings put the healing on the road, others in the prison.

**The widow returns to the cell** with the pig's head and feet, bread, and **two wax candles** **[R, Wikipedia "Feast of Saint Blaise"]**: the visible ancestor of the crossed candles. The further link to **Candlemas**, 2 February, is a later reading, not in the Acts **[R]**.

## 5. Trial, torture, death

**Before Agricolaus** he refuses to sacrifice, is beaten, imprisoned **[R, Passio; tag corrected from A]**. Verbatim: "Poor caitiff, know thou that I am servant of God, and I adore not the fiends as ye do" **[A as text, Caxton]**.

**Second hearing: pillar, whip, iron combs or carding rakes** **[R, Passio; tag corrected from A]**. Only the **derived** facts are attested: he is patron of the wool combers and the comb is his fixed attribute **[A, Wikipedia; Reau]**, Wikipedia framing the causation as conjecture, "likely due to the similarity" of the instruments.

**The lake.** Thrown in to drown, he signs the cross, walks on the water, challenges the pagans to follow **[R, Golden Legend]**. **NUMBER CORRECTED:** the pool said "seven soldiers" drowned; the cited source says **sixty-five**, seven belonging to the separate episode of the women.

**Seven women** gather the drops of his blood, are arrested, refuse to sacrifice, are martyred **[R, Golden Legend, quoted verbatim at the corresponding journey stop]**.

**Beheaded by order of Agricolaus, with two children** **[R, Golden Legend: "thus with the two little children he was beheaded"; OrthodoxWiki]**. The East remembers ten martyrs at Sebastea where the West remembers one.

- **FALSE PRECISION REMOVED.** The pool carried `316-02-03`. **3 February is the Western feast, not a date of death; no source gives a day.** The journey carries `0316-01-01` **[gap]**.
- **VARIANT DROPPED FOR LACK OF A CARRIER.** The pool's geography lens mentioned "some calendars give 283 CE". Nothing reachable corroborates it; Wikipedia gives 316, the Catholic Encyclopedia "about 316" **[gap]**.

**A woman named Elisa** buries the body **[R, theeasternchurch.com]**.

> **THE CENTRAL GAP: there is no tomb.** No grave, shrine or martyrium at Sebastea is documented, described by a pilgrim, or excavated at Sivas **[A as a stated absence across standard reference works; no positive archaeological record found]**. Every relic claim begins with a translation or a shipwreck; none with an opened grave.

## 6. The earliest trace, and the writing of the Acts

**Aetius of Amida**, Byzantine court physician of the earlier sixth century, instructs the practitioner in his medical compendium to invoke Blaise for objects lodged in the throat: **the earliest datable reference to Blaise anywhere** **[A, Wikipedia; theeasternchurch.com]**. It stands two centuries before the Acts and already contains the throat, so **the patronage is older than the story that explains it**. Which generated which is not determinable **[gap]**. Aetius is *of Amida* but worked at court; the journey pins **Constantinople (41.0086, 28.9802)** and says so in `date_confidence`.

Veneration **established in the Byzantine church by the sixth century**, feast **11 February** **[A, theeasternchurch.com; OrthodoxWiki]**. The **Acts composed roughly 400 years after the death**, so around 700 **[A as to the interval, Wikipedia: "The legendary Acts of St Blaise were written 400 years after his death"]**, place and author unknown **[gap]**. The **cult reaches the Latin West in the 8th century, no group honouring him there before** **[A]**, arriving with the legend attached, and is widespread in Europe by the 12th century **[A]**.

## 7. Maratea: the Italian relic tradition

**732: a ship carrying the relics west, fleeing Leo III's iconoclast persecutions, comes ashore at Maratea in Lucania** **[R, local tradition reported in Wikipedia "Maratea"; tag downgraded from A, since Wikipedia reporting a town's tradition does not make it documentary]**. The pool's mechanism was wrong: it had "part of an arm bone" brought by a ship that "refused to move", where Wikipedia says the ship "brought the sacred remains of Saint Blaise to Maratea", the whole remains, "still kept in the Maratea's Basilica". The **temple of Minerva** beneath the basilica is unproven, it.wikipedia noting "finora non siano state ufficialmente riscontrate prove".

**COORDINATES CORRECTED.** The pool carried three Maratea pins (39.9968/15.7136, 39.9928/15.689, 39.9944/15.7139), all wrong by 1 to 3 km. The basilica on Monte San Biagio is at **39.9880, 15.7239** **[A, it.wikipedia "Basilica di San Biagio (Maratea)"]**. All five Maratea stops use it.

- **Procession** of the silver statue to the old town, **second Sunday of May** **[A, Wikipedia]**. The pool's "a tradition kept for nearly thirteen centuries" is unsourced embellishment, dropped **[gap]**.
- **4 March 1562: Pius IV grants indulgences and recognizes the "Santa Manna"** **[A, it.wikipedia]**; the pool's 1563 is a year late. **23 December 1629: Philip IV, as King of Naples, makes the basilica a regia cappella under the royal curia** **[A, it.wikipedia]**; the pool placed this Royal Chapel in **Madrid (40.4168, -3.7038)**, badly wrong, place and date corrected.
- **December 1806: an unexploded French cannonball strikes the basilica and is preserved** **[R, local tradition]**. The siege is historical; the shot is the town's account.

## 8. Ragusa and Dubrovnik

**The vision of the priest Stojko.** Blaise warns by night that a Venetian fleet watering off the city means to seize it; the city is alerted, the fleet leaves, Ragusa takes him as patron **[R, civic legend; Stojko's name confirmed, A, Wikipedia]**.

- **DATE CONTRADICTION.** The pool and `catorce_santos_ayudantes.journey.json` give **971**. Wikipedia "Festivity of Saint Blaise, the patron of Dubrovnik" gives **972**, with observance "continuously since the year 972 AD" **[A]**. The journey carries 972 with 971 named in `date_confidence`, not silently harmonised with the company file.

**Coordinates.** The canonical company-file pin **42.6403, 18.1096** is inherited byte-identical for the vision; treasury 42.6405 / 18.1105 and feast 42.6414 / 18.1096 take the geography lens's pins. The afterlife lens's 42.6507 / 18.0944 is a coarse centroid 1.5 km off and is not used.

- **Treasury**: gold-plated **skull cased as a Byzantine imperial crown**, plus arm and leg reliquaries **[A as to present holdings, Wikipedia "Dubrovnik Cathedral"]**. Route and authenticity of transfer are tradition **[R]**; arrival date unrecorded **[gap]**.
- **By 1190 the Festa Sv. Vlaha is documented as an established civic rite** **[A, theeasternchurch.com]**; **2009, UNESCO inscribes the Festivity on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** **[A]**.

## 9. The Fourteen Holy Helpers

Blaise is one of the **Vierzehn Nothelfer**, his portfolio the throat and suffocation **[A, Wikipedia]**.

> **INTERNAL CONTRADICTION IN THE POOL.** Its chronology lens dated the grouping to **1150 in Germany**: wrong by two centuries and wrong in region. Wikipedia: the group "originated in the 14th century at first in the Rhineland, largely as a result of the epidemic (probably of bubonic plague) that became known as the Black Death" **[A]**. The pool's own afterlife lens said 14th century and was right. The journey carries **c. 1350 in the Rhineland**, pinned at Cologne (50.9375, 6.9603) for the region since no founding church is named, and **c. 1400 in Franconia**.

- **The company fixed as a set of fourteen** across **Regensburg, Bamberg, Wurzburg and Nuremberg by about 1400** **[A, catorce_santos_ayudantes and saint_acacius journey files; Franconia pin 49.4534, 11.0775 inherited byte-identical]**. The roster is listed in the corresponding journey campa.
- **Vierzehnheiligen is not the devotion's 14th-century home**: the basilica is Balthasar Neumann's, 1743 to 1772. The pool's coordinates (50.1219, 11.1725) are 8 km off; correct is **50.1156, 11.0544** **[A, Wikipedia]**. The stop was dropped rather than carried with a misleading date.
- **Goreme (38.6431, 34.8286)** preserves some of the earliest images of **Christopher** as an armoured soldier martyr **[A, san_cristobal.journey.json]**. No figure there is identified as Blaise in the reviewed material **[gap]**.

## 10. Cult geography, iconography, wool

- **San Biagio della Pagnotta, Via Giulia, Rome (41.8983, 12.4661)**: first recorded 1072, rebuilt under Alexander II, named for the loaves given to the poor on his feast; in 1836 Gregory XVI grants it to Rome's Armenian community as their national church **[A, Wikipedia]**.
- **Saint-Blaise, Neuchatel**: *Arins* in 1011, "Sanctus Blasus" in 1209 **[A, Wikipedia]**. Coordinates corrected to **47.017, 6.983**; the pool's 46.9819 / 6.9906 falls 4 km south of the village.
- **St Blazey, Cornwall**: church built 1440 to 1445, replacing one mentioned in 1294; procession and service on 3 February; patronage of toothache, sore throats and cattle diseases **[A, Wikipedia]**. Coordinates corrected to **50.361, -4.716**; the pool's pin lands 2.5 km north, near Luxulyan.
- **St Blaise's Well, Bromley** (not Lewisham): "Pilgrims came to the town to visit St. Blaise's Well" **[A, Wikipedia "Bromley"]**, in the former Bishop's Palace grounds, about **51.405, 0.018**. The pool's 51.4636 / -0.0164 is 7 km off and on the wrong side of the meridian; its wool-comber rationale is unsupported, this being a chalybeate spring drawing pilgrims for indulgences **[R, rationale rejected]**.
- **Bishop Blaize processions, Bradford and Norwich** **[A as early modern to 19th century]**. The pool's 1200 England anchor for a medieval comber cult is unsupported and dropped **[gap]**; the journey uses 1750 as a stated placeholder.
- **Via San Biagio dei Librai, Naples** **[R, uncorroborated]**, not carried; the February folk custom of "Blaise's pig" **[R, folk collections]**; **iconography**, bishop in vestments with an iron wool comb, often two crossed candles **[A, e.g. Reau]**.
- **The Blessing of Throats**, crossed candles under the chin on or near 3 February, derived from the fishbone **[A as to rite and stated derivation]**, documented by the later Middle Ages with first appearance unrecorded **[gap]**. Latin **[A, Roman Ritual via Catholic Encyclopedia]**: "Per intercessionem S. Blasii liberet te Deus a malo gutteris et a quovis alio malo."
- **Feasts**: 3 February (Roman Catholic, Anglican), 11 February (Byzantine) **[A]**. Neither is a recorded date of death.

## 12. Open contradictions and stated gaps

**Contradictions, left open.** Licinius persecuting in Armenia in 316 against Licinius at war in Pannonia (s. 2). The unnamed cavern against Mount Argaeus, both pinned (s. 3). Dubrovnik 971 against 972 (s. 8). Death year 316 against a rumoured 283 with no carrier (s. 5). The fishbone on the road against in the prison, and the fishbone prayer worded almost identically to the dying prayer (s. 4). Sixty-five drowned against seven (s. 5). Two children and seven women against accounts where he dies alone (s. 5). Maratea against Ragusa on the same relics (s. 7, 8). The pool's two lenses on the Fourteen Holy Helpers (s. 9).

**Gaps.** Birth year unrecorded. Day of death unrecorded. Author, place and year of the Acts unknown. Tomb at Sebastea none documented, none excavated. Route, port, captain and year of the relic voyage none named. Arrival date of the Dubrovnik skull not recorded. First appearance of the throat-blessing rite not recorded. Priority between the fishbone story and the throat patronage undeterminable. Gregory the Illuminator and Tiridates III: no atlas traveler, no source linkage.
