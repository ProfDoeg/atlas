# Larry Fink: research report
*Laurence Douglas Fink, b. 2 November 1952, Los Angeles. Living. Chairman and CEO of BlackRock, Inc.; interim co-chair of the World Economic Forum board since August 2025. Written 2026-08-23 for `larry_fink.journey.json`.*

**[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, inference, or a claim whose source could not be reached. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved; unreachable sources are named with the reason. The commonest defect in the pool was **announcement dates read as completion dates**: six transactions were misdated that way, and where the two differ both are given.

## 1. Los Angeles, 1952–1976

**[A: Wikipedia, 'Larry Fink']** Born 2 Nov 1952, middle of three children. Father **Frederick Fink** (1925–2013), shoe-store owner; mother **Lila Fink** (1930–2012), **professor of English** at what is now Cal State Northridge (the pool called her an English teacher). Grew up in **Van Nuys**, 34.1867 / −118.4487, confirmed. BA political science, UCLA, **1974**; MBA, real-estate concentration, UCLA Graduate School of Management, **1976**. Married **Lori Weider**, his high-school girlfriend, **1974**, with no place or date beyond the year anywhere reachable, so it is carried in prose rather than pinned.

**Gaps.** No school, address or synagogue for the family in Van Nuys. The much-repeated stories that he worked in his father's store from age ten and was the less-promising of two brothers appear only in profile journalism, are **not** in the pool, and are absent from the journey file. No hospital of birth.

## 2. First Boston, 1976–1988

**[A: Wikipedia; Euromoney]** Joined **The First Boston Corporation** in 1976 at twenty-three; bond desk; early mortgage-backed-securities specialist. Euromoney credits him with pitching **the first CMO Freddie Mac ever did**, but the CMO is **undated in every source read [R]**, and 1983 orders the segment only.

**[A]** **1986**: the desk lost ~**$100m in one quarter** after earning ~$130m the quarter before, on a mistaken interest-rate position. Euromoney: *"his desk lost $100 million in a single quarter when the markets moved in ways he didn't understand."* Q2 per the pool's geography lens.

**[A]** Left **spring 1988**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** resignation vs. dismissal is disputed, the pool's own dossier detail being "not independently re-verified." No contemporaneous account was read, so the file states the dispute rather than picking a side.

## 3. BlackRock, 1988–1999

**[A: Wikipedia; Forbes; MoAF]** **1988**: Fink and seven partners (eight founders, incl. **Robert S. Kapito**, **Susan Wagner**) found **Blackstone Financial Management** inside the Blackstone Group. MoAF: *"He and seven partners founded BlackRock in 1988."*
**[R], lore excluded.** "One phone, one couch" comes from BlackRock's own anniversary film and is not used; the eight-founder count it also carries is corroborated by MoAF and is used.

**[A]** Name change to **BlackRock**, ~**$17bn** AUM at year-end **1992**. Separation from Blackstone **1994** (Forbes); year-end 1994 AUM confirmed at **$52,691m** in the S-1 table. **[R]** The *cause* of the split, Fink wanting to widen the equity and Schwarzman refusing to dilute, and the estrangement that followed, are secondary claims, not verified against a document.

**[A: BlackRock Form S-1/A, 24 Sep 1999]** PNC's purchase, stated twice in the prospectus: *"In 1995, PNC acquired BlackRock Financial Management L.P., then a $25 billion fixed income manager."* PNC sold out **May 2020**. IPO on the NYSE **1 Oct 1999** at **$14/share**; ~**$165bn** AUM at year-end.

## 4. 2003–2010

**[R: Wikipedia]** Helped negotiate **Richard Grasso's** NYSE resignation, **2003**. No primary account of Fink's role read; the day used (17 Sep 2003) is Grasso's resignation date, not one sourced to Fink, and the ~$140m package figure is general reporting.

**[A]** **2004**: Finch Farm, North Salem NY, from **Stanley Tucci** for **$3.7m**, later >250 acres. Ongoing residences: **Upper East Side**; **Aspen**, with Aspen Institute ties.

**[A: BlackRock Form 10-K 2009]** **SSR** bought from MetLife, closed **31 Jan 2005**, *"an adjusted purchase price of $265 million in cash and 550,000 shares"* · **MLIM** contributed by Merrill Lynch **29 Sep 2006**, leaving Merrill with ~45% of the common · **BGI** completed **1 Dec 2009**.

**[A]** **Oct 2006**: a Tishman Speyer-led partnership with BlackRock client capital buys **Stuyvesant Town–Peter Cooper Village** for **$5.4bn**, mostly debt. **Jan 2010**: default and surrender to creditors, the complex then worth *"around $1.9 billion or less than 40 percent"* of the price. "Largest commercial mortgage default in US history" is secondary and reported as such.

**[R], Maiden Lane, and it stays [R].** Wikipedia's *BlackRock Solutions* article has the firm retained (May 2009) by Treasury to analyse, unwind and price toxic assets from Bear Stearns, AIG, Freddie Mac and others. **No primary Fed source names BlackRock:** newyorkfed.org 403s, federalreserve.gov and the *Maiden Lane Transactions* article do not name it, and the contract PDF returned unreadable binary.

## 5. The letters, the politics, the recirculated clip

**[A: HLS Forum on Corporate Governance]** *A Sense of Purpose*, **17 Jan 2018**; *A Fundamental Reshaping of Finance*, **14 Jan 2020**. Every sentence quoted in the journey file was verified verbatim against the HLS reproductions.

**[A, upgraded from [R]] "You have to force behaviors."** Forbes, on the video: *"Behaviors are going to have to change… You have to force behaviors. At BlackRock we are forcing behaviors."* (Jon McGowan, 5 Jun 2023), at the **2017 NYT DealBook conference**, panel with Andrew Ross Sorkin and Kenneth Chenault. **The load-bearing point: the remark was about DIVERSITY IN CORPORATE LEADERSHIP, not ESG or climate.** The NYT video is still not loadable, techstartups.com renders the first sentence singular, and **no day-level date is assigned**.

**[A: Wikipedia, 'Strategic and Policy Forum']** Announced **Dec 2016**, chaired by **Stephen A. Schwarzman**, **sixteen members** incl. Fink, **first meeting Feb 2017**, dissolved **16 Aug 2017** on a conference call.

Three further **[R]** items. Fink backed **Obama** (2012) and **Hillary Clinton** (2016), with pre-election reporting that associates thought he wanted Treasury: documented outside `hillary_clinton.journey.json`, carried with the hedge in `date_confidence`. **Riyadh**: the Oct 2018 withdrawal from the Future Investment Initiative after Khashoggi's murder, and the office opened ~a year later, come from the dossier, whose Reuters source was unreachable in both passes. **Fed, March 2020**: the SMCCF and BlackRock's retention to run it come from the pool's interlock lens, which itself noted the mandate is documented outside the files it read; no primary document was reached, and the reported ETF purchases under disclosed conflict arrangements are unverified.

## 6. 2019–2025: the private-markets turn

Four acquisitions, all **[A]**, all misdated in the pool as announcement = completion (rows 16 to 19 below). **eFront**: announced 22 Mar 2019, acquired **10 May 2019**, ~$1.3bn. **Global Infrastructure Partners**: announced 12 Jan 2024 at $12.5bn, completed **1 Oct 2024**. **Preqin**: announced 1 Jul 2024 at £2.55bn, completed **Mar 2025**. **HPS Investment Partners**: announced 3 Dec 2024, completed **1 Jul 2025**, ~$157bn of client assets. HPS was an internal inconsistency inside the pool, its chronology contradicting its own geography lens.

**[A: Wikipedia, 'Saudi Aramco']** **Aug 2025**: a BlackRock-led consortium buys **49% of Aramco's Jafurah gas-processing facilities for $11bn**, lease-and-leaseback, Aramco keeping operational control. *Do not conflate* the dossier's separate **2021 $15.5bn Aramco Gas Pipelines** deal led by BlackRock, which is real, distinct, unverified and not carried; the EIG-led consortium on the Aramco page is the *other* 2021 pipeline deal.

**[A: Form 10-K 2016]** *"approximately 13,000 employees"* and *"$5.1 trillion of assets under management"* at 31 Dec 2016, against the pool's ~$5tn and ~12,000.

## 8. Afterlife: a living subject

No tomb, grave or funeral; "afterlife" here means institutional monuments, editions, and legend.

**[A: UCLA Newsroom]** **UCLA Medal**, Chancellor **Gene Block**, **7 Mar 2016**: *"a great role model for engaged citizenship and philanthropic leadership."* Ceremony venue not named. **UCLA College commencement address**, **Pauley Pavilion**, **10 Jun 2016**, twice in one day. **[A, upgraded]** The **Laurence D. and Lori W. Fink Center for Finance and Investments** was *"founded in 2006 under faculty leadership of Professor Richard Roll"* and *"endowed in 2008"* with the Finks' $10m.

**[A: MoAF]** **2019 Schwab Financial Innovation Award**, plus Fortune's "World's Greatest Leaders" and Barron's "World's Best CEOs" for twelve consecutive years. The museum had itself left **48 Wall Street** after a 2018 flood and had no permanent space until Boston, July 2026.

**[R]** Fink co-chairs the NYU Langone board and **Lori Fink** is a listed Trustee; the **Fink Children's Ambulatory Care Center** could not be confirmed (newswise 403, facility page 404). **[R], softened from the pool's [A]:** the **Bethel Park / Thomas Matthew Crooks** advertisement story (2022 ad filmed at the school; Crooks in the background; viral "foreknowledge" theory after 13 Jul 2024; ad withdrawn, footage given to investigators), Reuters being unreachable in both passes.

**[A]** BlackRock's rebuttal page *"Setting the Record Straight: Buying Houses, Facts"* answers the viral claim (which frequently confuses BlackRock with **Blackstone**) that it is buying up American single-family houses. **[R]** Wider conspiracy material, "BlackRock owns everything," "shadow government" via Brian Deese and Wally Adeyemo, and antisemitic variants of the WEF role, sits in the dossier's myth catalogue, unverified and unused, as does *We Are BlackRock* (50 Eggs Films, 2008), which rests on one unreachable promotional page.

**[A, with fixes]** **WEF:** Fink and **André Hoffmann** (Roche) named **interim CO-chairs of the board**, **Aug 2025**, after the review cleared Klaus Schwab, who had resigned on 21 Apr 2025 with **Peter Brabeck-Letmathe** acting in between. **[R] Davos:** Wikipedia confirms the Congress Centre as the venue from Feb 1971 and Fink's Forum role generally, but documents **no dated appearance by him**, so this stays a pattern inference.
