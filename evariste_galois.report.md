# Évariste Galois (1811-1832): research report

*2026-08-23. For `evariste_galois.journey.json`: 9 segments, 42 stops, 11 quotes.*

**[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the research pass could not corroborate. Conflicts are flagged, not adjudicated. Shorthand: **MT** = MacTutor (St Andrews), biography of Galois · **EC** = Encyclopedia.com, same · **FRW** / **ENW** = the French and English Wikipedia articles · **DUP** = Dupuy, *La vie d'Évariste Galois* (1896).

The difficulty here is not scarcity of sources but density of legend. Two of the three principal narrative sources are literary compositions: Dumas's *Mes Mémoires* and Raspail's prison memoir. DUP underlies everything after it and is used at second hand. Bell's *Men of Mathematics* (1937) fixed the popular image [A for the book's influence, ENW; R for the image, which is the legend]. Every hedge lives in `date_confidence`, never in campa prose.

## 1. Bourg-la-Reine, 1811-1823

- Born **25 October 1811** in the family pension on the Grand'Rue **[A, EC; MT]**. DUP has the birth act declared next day, fixing the town against a tradition placing him vaguely "in Paris".
- Father **Nicolas-Gabriel Galois**, director of the pension and later **mayor**; mother **Adélaïde-Marie Demante**, a magistrate's daughter; married 1808. Évariste is second of three: Nathalie-Théodore (1808), Alfred (1814) **[A, EC]**.
- Educated **at home by his mother** in classics and religion until secondary school, with **no evidence of childhood mathematical precocity** **[A, EC; MT]**. The record is thin rather than contradictory: nobody claims a prodigy, nobody records the ordinary boy.
- **Gap:** no year for the father's election as mayor, none for the start of the campaign that later killed him. The mairie stop is dated 1815-01-01 as an ordering device and says so.

## 2. Louis-le-Grand, 1823/24-1829

- Enters as a boarder in *quatrième* **[A, date contested: correction 2]**.
- **1824:** a wave of expulsions over resistance to school authority; Galois neither leader nor expelled **[A, MT]**. The occasion and the number expelled are given nowhere reached.
- **1824-1826:** prizes and *accessits* in Latin verse and Greek translation, recognition at the **Concours général** **[A, MT]**. Direct evidence against the "boy who could not do schoolwork".
- **1826:** results collapse in *seconde*; the headmaster wants him held back, the family resists, he enters rhetoric and is returned to *seconde* after a term; a teacher's note, "**bon, mais singulier**" **[A, MT]**.
- **February 1827:** the *mathématiques* class under **Hippolyte Vernier**; reads **Legendre's *Éléments de géométrie*** straight through, then **Lagrange**; classmate **Ludovic Lalanne** remembers the intensity **[A, MT, drawing on DUP]**. Vernier: "Intelligence, marked progress but not enough method." The director of studies: "It is the passion for mathematics which dominates him...", used as the stop quote.
- **1828:** first **École polytechnique** failure **[A, MT; EC]**. Month not fixed. The **chalk-rag anecdote** attaches to this failure or the next, was written down decades later, and names nobody who was in the room **[R]**.
- **Autumn 1828:** *mathématiques spéciales* under **Louis-Paul-Émile Richard**, who reports "supériorité marquée sur tous ses condisciples" and keeps Galois's exercises, which survive legible and orderly **[A, MT; dossier citing Richard's reports]**. Physics report the same year: "travail nul."

## 3. 1829: the year everything is taken

- **April 1829:** first paper, on periodic continued fractions, in **Gergonne's *Annales*** **[A, MT]**. Pinned at Louis-le-Grand, where Galois was, not at the place of printing.
- **25 May and 1 June 1829:** two memoirs on the algebraic solution of equations to the **Académie**, referred to **Cauchy** and **Poinsot**; the manuscripts are **lost** **[A, MT]**. **Taton (1971)** suggests instead that Cauchy engaged seriously and that Galois withdrew them himself for the Grand Prix **[R: Taton not opened]**. Both readings are in the campa, with the note that neither can be tested, because there are no papers.
- **2 July 1829:** the father **asphyxiates himself** on the **rue Jean-de-Beauvais**, "à deux pas du collège de Louis-le-Grand," after a campaign built on **forged obscene verses** attributed to him **[A: correction 4]**.
- **July 1829:** the funeral returns to Bourg-la-Reine; a confrontation near the church, the priest reportedly struck by a stone **[A for the disturbance, DUP; R for the stone]**. **The day is given nowhere reached**; 5 July is an ordering device. Reading the scene as the origin of his republicanism is later biographers' interpretation, and the campa does not assert it.
- **Summer 1829:** second and final Polytechnique failure **[A, EC; MT]**. DUP could not establish whether the examiner was **Binet or Lefébure de Fourcy**: no transcript, no name **[A as a gap]**.
- **25 October or November 1829:** admitted to the **École préparatoire** (Collège du Plessis, soon renamed École normale) near the top of the science intake; **Leroy** gives 8/10 in mathematics, noting originality and obscurity; the physics oral goes badly **[A, FRW; MT]**. Dates conflict: correction 5.
- **29 December 1829:** the **baccalauréat**, with two examiners' reports, mathematics ("sometimes obscure... but he is intelligent") and literature ("he knows absolutely nothing") **[A, MT; the first also on ENW]**. **Open oddity, left standing:** the degree falls *after* the admission, and no source reached explains the sequence.

## 4. 1830: the prize, the revolution, the expulsion

- **February 1830, probably late:** the reworked memoir on solvability by radicals reaches **Joseph Fourier**, perpetual secretary of the Académie, as a **Grand Prix** candidate. Fourier **dies 16 May 1830** before judging concludes and the manuscript is **never found** **[A, MT; death date fixed independently in `joseph_fourier.journey.json`]**. Best documented of the three disappearances.
- **April 1830:** three short papers in **Férussac's *Bulletin***, including the work later understood as introducing **finite fields** **[A, MT]**. That vocabulary is modern and no source reached quotes Galois using it, so the campa says only that the name the object later takes is his.
- **June 1830:** the Grand Prix goes to **Abel**, posthumously, and to **Jacobi** **[A, MT]**. Abel's death of tuberculosis at twenty-six is standard biography, not re-verified **[R]**.
- **27-29 July 1830:** confined inside the École préparatoire by **Guigniault** during the Trois Glorieuses; reportedly tries the courtyard wall at night; learns Polytechnique students fought openly and that a former Normalien, **Farcy**, was killed **[A, MT; the wall attempt reported, not documented]**.
- **9 December 1830:** the anonymous letter signed "Un élève de l'École Normale" in the *Gazette des Écoles*; Guigniault identifies him and **dismisses him the same day**; ratified **4 January 1831** **[A, FRW; MT: correction 6]**.
- **December 1830:** an abstract in Gergonne's *Annales* **[A, MT]**. Not given its own stop: a fourth publication stop would be a line item without a scene.

## 5. 1831: the republican year

- **13 January 1831:** advertises a **public course in higher algebra** at the bookseller **Caillot, 5 rue de la Sorbonne**; roughly forty attend at first, and it does not become a living **[A, MT]**.
- **17 January 1831:** submits "**Mémoire sur les conditions de résolubilité des équations par radicaux**" at Poisson's encouragement; **Lacroix and Poisson** named referees **[A, MT]**.
- **18 April [1831]: Sophie Germain to Guillaume Libri**, the only surviving contemporary description of Galois by a mathematician of standing **[A for the text, MT: correction 7]**.
- **9 May 1831:** the banquet at **Les Vendanges de Bourgogne**, for the acquittal of **nineteen** republicans, former National Guard artillerymen; Galois toasts Louis-Philippe with an open knife in the same hand **[A, FRW; MT; Dumas, whose escape is verbatim his own: "nous sautâmes, de l'appui de la fenêtre, dans le jardin"; correction 8]**.
- **10 May 1831:** arrested **at his mother's**, preventive detention in Sainte-Pélagie **[A, DUP: "Galois fut arrêté chez sa mère et mis à Sainte-Pélagie en prison préventive"; correction 9]**.
- **15 June 1831:** tried before the **Seine assize court** and **acquitted** **[A, MT; EC]**. Claims that he was convicted here are wrong. The toast's wording, "To Louis-Philippe, if he betrays," is **[R]**: both sources say the crowd drowned the end of it, so the conditional is the defence's courtroom reconstruction.
- **4 July 1831:** the **Lacroix and Poisson report** returns the memoir, "neither sufficiently clear nor sufficiently developed to allow us to judge its rigour" **[A for the language, MT; R for the date, from the dossier and unconfirmed against any accessible primary source]**.
- **14 July 1831:** police miss him at **16 rue des Bernardins** (the prison register's address, occupation "**répétiteur**", a plaque there today) and take him near the **Pont-Neuf** with **Ernest Duchâtelet**, both in the dissolved artillery's uniform and armed **[A, MT; FRW for register and plaque]**.
- **25 July 1831:** the drunken speech in Sainte-Pélagie, and the most consequential fix in the pool: correction 10. MT also states, for the same date, an **attempted suicide with a dagger [R]**, uncorroborated in the research pass, while the dossier's prison-life section describes a different incident, a stray shot from next door wounding a prisoner. Both are carried: the shot in the October campa, the dagger only in `date_confidence`.
- **23 October 1831:** sentenced to **six months** for the uniform and weapons, served at Sainte-Pélagie among the political prisoners; his sister **Nathalie** alarmed at his appearance **[A, MT; EC; FRW]**.
- **22-31 January 1832:** transferred to **La Force** and returned nine days later, **no reason entered** **[R: prison-register documentation reported in the dossier, not re-verified]**. Nothing else survives. Kept as a stop because it is a documented blank.

## 6. 1832: the last ten weeks

- **16 March 1832:** moved to the **maison de santé Faultrier**, rue de Lourcine, because of the **cholera epidemic** **[A, EC; FRW]**. The entanglement with "**Stéphanie**" begins in the weeks after; the 1968 identification with **Stéphanie-Félicie Poterin du Motel** has been challenged and is not adopted **[R]**.
- **29 April 1832:** formal end of sentence, released **[A, MT; EC]**. Whether he left the house at once is stated nowhere reached.
- **25 May 1832:** letter to **Auguste Chevalier** about the romantic collapse, which **also** proposes meeting around 1 June and imagines a trip to the **Dauphiné** in mid-June **[A at second hand: dossier summary corroborated by MT; the letter not opened, its date flagged unverified]**. The plans are the strongest argument against reading the last week as an orchestrated suicide.
- **29 May 1832:** the letters. "À tous les républicains," naming an "infâme coquette" and two dupes; letters to friends, "provoqué par deux patriotes" acting "de bonne foi"; and the **scientific testament** to Chevalier, asking that **Jacobi or Gauss** judge publicly not the truth of the theorems but their importance **[A, EC; MT; French text on ENW]**.
- The marginal note "**Je n'ai pas le temps**" **[A, MT]** belongs to manuscripts annotated in this period and is routinely miscited as proof he wrote the whole theory in one night; FRW treats that as legend construction **[R]**. The file carries it as the quote on the final stop.
- **30 May 1832:** the duel "près de l'étang de la Glacière," at dawn; ball into the abdomen at about twenty-five paces, lodging near the left buttock **[A for date and general location, FRW; correction 12]**. The **passerby** who carts him toward help is **[R]**, DUP through the dossier; the story that his opponent *and his own seconds* left him outruns the evidence, and what is documented is that a stranger moved him.
- **31 May 1832, about 10 a.m.:** dies at **Hôpital Cochin** of peritonitis, aged twenty years and seven months; Alfred at the bedside, a priest refused, the death certificate wrong by a year **[A, EC; MT; FRW]**. Last words to Alfred **[R]**: universally repeated, primary citation not opened.
- **2 June 1832:** buried in the **fosse commune** of **Montparnasse**; two to three thousand mourners, heavy police watch, **calm funeral** **[A, FRW; EC]**. No individual grave survives. **Conflation flagged and not carried:** MT's "riots lasted several days" appears to compress the funeral with the **June Insurrection**, which gathered around **Lamarque's funeral on 5 June**.

## 7. The papers

- **4 September 1843: Joseph Liouville** tells the Académie he has found in Galois's papers "une solution aussi exacte que profonde de ce beau problème" **[A, FRW citing Verdier 2003; EC gives September 1843; correction 13]**.
- **1846:** "Œuvres mathématiques d'Évariste Galois," *Journal de mathématiques pures et appliquées*, ser. 1, vol. 11, **pp. 381-444** **[A, Numdam record, fetched directly]**. Later diffusion through **Betti, Cayley, Jordan, Serret, Dedekind, Kronecker, Cockle, Bachmann, Weber** **[A, FRW citing Ehrhardt 2011]**.

**A placement decision, stated openly.** In 1843 the person in the room is **Liouville**, not Galois. The stop stays here because its subject is **the manuscripts**, which are Galois's, in the building that lost two of them and returned the third, and Galois has three attested stops of his own at that pin. Liouville is named once as the reader, never staged as a traveller, and `date_confidence` says so. A stricter operator can delete the stop and fold it into `sources` on the 1831 Institut stop.

6. Expulsion: the pool's "8 December 1830, per EC" is supported by nothing reached. Attested: dismissal 9 December 1830, ratification 4 January 1831.
7. The Germain letter: recipient restored (**Guillaume Libri**); MT gives **no year**, so 1831 is contextual; MT's ellipsis and typo noted, normalised in the quotation.
8. The banquet: rue du Faubourg-du-Temple, not Belleville. File **48.8688, 2.3706**; street number unestablished.
9. A fabricated coordinate removed: the mother's address, plotted at 48.85, 2.35, is given by no source. The arrest is folded into a Sainte-Pélagie stop.
10. Raspail: the two most quoted passages in the life are **not Galois's letters** and **not addressed to Chevalier** but Raspail's report of his drunken speech, and they are **one block quote**, not two occasions. Credited to Raspail; DUP warned the scene reads as arranged after the fact **[R]**.
11. Sainte-Pélagie coordinates: pool about 160 m too far east. File **48.8425, 2.3528**.
12. The duel: Dumas's own spelling is **Pescheux d'Herbinville**, the cartridge ribbons pink, and he was **no eyewitness [R]**. File **48.8312, 2.3440**; pond site undocumented.
13. Liouville: the pool's "1842" is unsupported by its own cited source; the attested date is 4 September 1843.
14. Sophus Lie: elected to the Académie in 1892, so not "newly elected" in 1895, and Cauchy, whose "old seat" the pool gives him, died in 1857.

## Interlocks used

Two atlas travellers are named in campa prose, both on a **direct relation of Galois's own**, not shared geography.

- **`joseph_fourier`.** Fourier received the Grand Prix memoir as perpetual secretary in February 1830 and died on 16 May before judging; the manuscript was never found. Named in two campas.
- **`gauss`.** Named by Galois himself in the testament of 29 May 1832: "Tu prieras publiquement Jacobi ou Gauss de donner leur avis, non sur la vérité, mais sur l'importance des théorèmes." A one-directional citation running from Galois outward, which is a real relation.

**Rejected as name-drops:** Tocqueville, Lafayette, Balzac, Jacques de Molay, Dreyfus, Bartholdi, Beauvoir, Cortázar, René Hauy, and Sophie Germain via `gauss.journey.json`. Each is co-location or institutional echo, which the map already shows a viewer. Germain herself **is** named, because she wrote about Galois while he was alive; her Gauss correspondence is her relation and stays out of that campa.

**Pins inherited byte-identical:** Institut de France 48.8566, 2.3376 (`joseph_fourier`, on all five Institut stops); Hôpital Cochin 48.8370, 2.3402 (`simone_de_beauvoir`); Cimetière du Montparnasse 48.8381, 2.3269 (`julio_cortazar`); canonical Paris 48.8566, 2.3522 (`gauss`). The atlas holds a **second** Montparnasse pin, 48.8409, 2.3265, in `captain_alfred_dreyfus.journey.json`; since Galois lies in the common ground, neither marks a plot of his, and the file says so.

## Honest gaps

No address for his mother's residence, where he was arrested on 10 May 1831. No street number for Les Vendanges de Bourgogne and no documented site for the étang de la Glacière, both pins being placements on the attested street. No date for the father's election as mayor, none for the start of the campaign against him, no day for his funeral. No examiner's name or transcript for either Polytechnique failure. No explanation for the nine days at La Force. No identification of "Stéphanie" that current scholarship accepts. No provable duellist. No grave. The occupation-and-address line in the Sainte-Pélagie register is one of very few documents in which Galois states anything about his own daily life.
