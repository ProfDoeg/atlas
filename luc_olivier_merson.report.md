# Luc-Olivier Merson (1846-1920) — research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: French academic painter, illustrator, designer of stained glass, postage stamps, banknotes, and the apse mosaic of the Basilique du Sacré-Coeur.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Where two sources disagree, both readings are printed and neither is adjudicated. Where a source could not be reached, it is named along with the reason.

---

## Method and warning

The reachable evidence for Merson is thin and lopsided. Two Wikipedia articles (French and English) carry almost the whole biographical spine; they disagree with each other on several points and the French one carries at least one anachronism of its own. Two period books were read directly in scanned full text and are the only sources here that reproduce anyone's actual words: the 1921 memorial exhibition catalogue of the École Nationale des Beaux-Arts, and Adolphe Giraldon's 1929 memoir. Everything about Merson's *movements* outside Paris is weak. He is a painter whose life happened in a two-kilometre radius of the Left Bank, and the Atlas has to be honest that most of the pins outside that radius mark where his *work* went, not where his body did.

A specific hazard was found and is flagged throughout: an earlier draft of this dossier contained several wrong coordinates and one confidently wrong attribution (a 50-franc banknote dated to 1908). Both are corrected below with the correction shown, not silently.

## 1. Origins and training (1846-1869)

**Birth.** Born in Paris on 21 May 1846 **[A: fr.wikipedia, "né à Paris le 21 mai 1846"]**, in the 12th arrondissement **[A: fr.wikipedia category "Naissance dans le 12e arrondissement de Paris"]**.

*Coordinate correction, flagged.* The draft placed the birth at 48.8399 / 2.3773, which is the **modern** 12th arrondissement (Bercy, rue de Charenton). In 1846 Paris still used the pre-1860 numbering, under which the old 12e covered the Panthéon and Jardin des Plantes quarter, that is, today's 5th. The journey file uses approximately **48.8425 / 2.3505** **[R]**, a Left Bank point consistent with his dying in the 5th and keeping a studio at 115 boulevard Saint-Michel. This is a reconstruction from the arrondissement numbering, not an attested address; no birth address was located.

**Drawing school.** He studies drawing under Gustave Adolphe Chassevent-Bacque before entering the Beaux-Arts **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Downgrade flagged:* the draft named this "École gratuite de dessin" and dated it c.1858-1862 **[both R, not A]**. French Wikipedia writes only "à l'École de dessin" and then appends a `{{laquelle}}` maintenance tag, which is Wikipedia explicitly declaring that it does not know which school. The name and the dates are both guesses. The journey file carries the stop with the ignorance stated.

**École des Beaux-Arts.** He studies painting under Isidore Pils **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**. No entry year is attested; the journey file's c.1862 is **[R]**.

**First Salon, 1866** **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**. The venue is the Palais de l'Industrie on the Champs-Élysées, which housed the annual Salon until the 1890s.

**Premier grand prix de Rome, 1869**, with *Le Soldat de Marathon*, awarded under the superintendent Émilien de Nieuwerkerke **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

## 2. Italy (1869/1870-1874/1875)

This is the weakest section in the dossier and it needs to be said plainly.

**What is attested:** English Wikipedia has one vague sentence, "During the five years spent working in Italy…" **[A, but thin]**.

**What is not attested:** French Wikipedia's Merson article says **nothing** about Rome, nothing about the Villa Medici, and gives no residency dates. The draft claimed "French sources give 1870-1874"; that sourcing statement is **false** and is withdrawn here. The Villa Medici residency is an *inference from the Prix de Rome*, which is a reasonable inference (the prize carried a pensionnaire's place) but is not a sourced fact about Merson. Tagged **[R]** in the journey file.

*Coordinate correction:* the draft used 41.9028 / 12.4964, which is generic Rome city centre. The Villa Medici is at approximately **41.9077 / 12.4823**.

**The southern and northern tours.** Naples (Museo Archeologico), Pompeii, Assisi, Florence, Venice were all proposed on the strength of the standard pensionnaire itinerary of the 1870s. **No document places Merson personally at any of them.** All are **[R]**, and the journey file says so inside the campa, not only in the metadata. *Coordinate correction:* the draft put the Basilica of San Francesco at 43.0705 / 12.5985, roughly 800 m southwest of the church complex; the basilica is at approximately **43.0748 / 12.6053**.

**The last envoi.** Giraldon records that Merson's final-year Rome envoi was *Bella matribus detestata*, a large composition prompted by the disaster of 1870 and much noticed at the Salon of 1875 **[A: Giraldon 1929, p. 12]**. The title is Horace, *Odes* I.1. This is the one hard anchor tying the Italian years to a dated Paris event.

*Transcription flag.* The scan of Giraldon reads "8on envoi de dernière année…". The leading `8` is an OCR misread of `S`; the same substitution recurs elsewhere in the same scan ("8a bonne grâce", "8on enseignement", p. 43). The defect is a digitization artifact and is **not** reproduced in the journey file's quote, which prints "Son".

## 3. The Salon painter (1875-1881)

**First-class medal at the Salon of 1875** **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**.

*Anachronism flagged, not resolved.* French Wikipedia calls this a medal of the **Société des artistes français**. The Société was founded in 1881, following Jules Ferry's request of 27 December 1880, and only then took over running the Salon from the State **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Société des artistes français"]**. The 1875 Salon was still State-run. French Wikipedia's Merson article therefore contradicts French Wikipedia's own article on the Société. The journey file reads "the Salon of 1875" and states the conflict.

**Saint Michel terrassant le dragon.** French Wikipedia dates the canvas 1875 and places it at the musée Mandet, Riom **[A]**. It **never** says this was the Salon exhibit or the medal-winning work; the draft's linkage of the two is unsourced and is withdrawn.

**Catalogue works of the period** **[all A: fr.wikipedia catalogue listing]**: *Le Sacrifice des poupées* (1871), *La Vision* (1872), *Le Loup d'Agubbio* (1877, the wolf of Gubbio), *Saint Isidore, le laboureur* (1878).

**Le Repos en Égypte / Le Repos pendant la fuite en Égypte.** The draft recorded an unresolved "1879 vs 1880" disagreement between the two Wikipedias. **This is resolved, and it was never a disagreement: they are two different paintings** **[A: fr.wikipedia's dedicated article on the painting]**.

- **1879 original.** Exhibited at the Salon of May 1879 under the catalogue title *Le Repos en Égypte*. 71.8 x 128.3 cm, inv. 18.652, **Museum of Fine Arts, Boston**, gift of George Golding Kennedy.
- **1880 variant.** 77 x 133 cm, **musée des Beaux-Arts de Nice**, bequest of M. de Saint-Aignan, in which Joseph gazes at the Virgin rather than sleeping turned away.
- Further studies and an **inverted-composition version** are at the **Cleveland Museum of Art**.

*Gap.* The Boston accession year is not stated in any source read. The inventory number 18.652 is consistent with a 1918 accession, but that is inference and is tagged **[R]** in the journey file.

*Studio.* The draft used a placeholder "Merson's Paris studio (Left Bank)" at 48.85 / 2.34. The address is documented: French Wikipedia captions a photograph "Luc-Olivier Merson dans son atelier du 115, boulevard Saint-Michel à Paris" **[A]**, approximately **48.8437 / 2.3407**, about 700 m south of the guess.

*Egypt.* No record places Merson in Egypt at any point in his life. This is an **argument from silence** and is written as a gap, not as a finding. The sphinx of the painting is built from prints and imagination.

**Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur, 1881** **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**.

*Coordinate correction.* The draft placed all three Legion of Honour grades at the Institut de France (48.8577 / 2.3376). The Légion d'honneur has nothing to do with the Institut. Its Grande Chancellerie is the **Palais de Salm**, 64 rue de Lille / 1 rue de Solférino, approximately **48.8600 / 2.3250**. The error recurred three times in the draft and is corrected at all three.

## 4. Glass carried across the Atlantic (1882-1890)

**The Vanderbilt window, 1882** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Designed by Merson, executed in 1882 by the atelier of **Eugène-Stanislas Oudinot** for the "Petit Château", William K. Vanderbilt's residence at **660 Fifth Avenue**, New York. The house was demolished in 1926 (en.wikipedia says 1927; the discrepancy is noted, not resolved).

*Coordinate correction.* The draft used 40.7128 / -74.0060, which is City Hall Park in Lower Manhattan, about 5.6 km wrong. Fifth Avenue at 52nd Street is approximately **40.7600 / -73.9764**.

**La Danse des Fiançailles**, stained-glass triptych, 1885 **[A: fr.wikipedia catalogue listing]**. No location given for the making; the journey file pins it to the studio and says so.

**The Belmont Chapel, Newport, 1890** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The chapel stands in the **Island Cemetery**, Newport, Rhode Island. Merson designed the chancel windows (executed by Oudinot's atelier) plus three nave windows executed by the atelier of **Félix Gaudin**, Oudinot's successor from 1890.

*Coordinate correction.* The draft used 41.4901 / -71.3128, downtown Newport. The Island Cemetery is at approximately **41.4939 / -71.3095**.

**Église Sainte-Eugénie, Biarritz** **[A for the window's presence: fr.wikipedia]**. Whether Merson travelled to the Basque coast or the design was shipped from Oudinot's Paris workshop is **not established**; a gap. No date is given by any source; the journey file's date is a placement, not a finding, and the date_confidence field says so.

## 5. The decorator of the Republic (1889-1908)

**Gold medal, Exposition Universelle, Paris, 1889** **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**, on the Champ de Mars under the new Eiffel Tower.

**Elected to the Académie des beaux-arts, 1892, fauteuil 1** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Illustration.** He illustrates Victor Hugo's *Notre-Dame de Paris* and José-Maria de Heredia's *Les Trophées*, and contributes to *La Mosaïque* and the *Revue illustrée* **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Gap:* no edition years, print runs, or later editions were located. Whether he studied the cathedral fabric on site or worked from prints and photographs is **[R]**.

**Hôtel de Ville de Paris, decorative murals** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Gap:* no commission date found.

**Palais de Justice, Paris, decorative commission** **[R: en.wikipedia only]**. *Gap:* no date found.

**Opéra-Comique (Salle Favart), mural paintings** **[A: fr.wikipedia, which lists "Opéra-Comique : peintures murales"]**. *Mis-sourcing corrected:* the draft cited en.wikipedia too, but en.wikipedia does not mention the Opéra-Comique at all. *Date withdrawn:* the draft's "c.1898" appears in **no source**; it was reasoned backwards from the 1887 fire. The coordinate 48.8712 / 2.3376 is correct for the Salle Favart.

**Musée Pasteur, decorative work** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Undated.

**Entrée de Jeanne d'Arc à Orléans, 1895.** Pen and black ink, grey wash, white gouache heightening on white paper; sheet 44 x 31 cm, framing line 33.8 x 22.5 cm; **musée des Beaux-Arts d'Orléans** **[A: fr.wikipedia, citing Brême & Korchane, *Dessins français du musée des Beaux-Arts d'Orléans. Le Trait et l'Ombre*, 2022, no. 160]**. Better sourced than the draft claimed, which had it from en.wikipedia alone.

*Coordinate correction.* The museum is at 1 rue Fernand Rabier, 47°54'09"N 1°54'34"E, approximately **47.9025 / 1.9094**. The draft's 47.9029 / 1.9046 is about 360 m west.

**The Panthéon: a contradiction inside this dossier, left open.** The interlock lens asserted that Merson painted a Joan of Arc fresco cycle in the Panthéon, installed 1889-1896 alongside cycles by Puvis de Chavannes and Jean-Paul Laurens. The afterlife lens searched for it and **found nothing**: French Wikipedia's article on the Panthéon names other painters and does not mention Merson; the Sainte-Geneviève dome imagery is attributed to Antoine-Jean Gros **[A for the absence of mention; R as an argument from silence]**. The two lenses of this same research pass therefore contradict each other. **Not resolved.** The journey file keeps the Panthéon pin (inherited byte-identical from `braille.journey.json`) and states the negative finding in the campa rather than asserting a mural that could not be confirmed.

## 6. Small images, many hands (1900-1909)

**Type Merson postage stamps, issued 4 December 1900** **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Type Merson"]**. France's first bicolour stamps, printed in typography, perforation 13.5 x 14, sheets of 75; most values engraved by **Auguste Thévenin**. Issues run 1900-1927, withdrawn from sale during the 1920s.

*Corrections.* The draft's "by 1900" is sharpened to the issue date. The draft's "in circulation into the 1920s and 30s" is **overstated**; French Wikipedia gives withdrawal during the 1920s.

**Monaco.** The same type is adopted by the Monégasque post **[A: en.wikipedia]**. No evidence places Merson in Monaco; the pin marks where the design circulated, and the journey file says so **[R]**.

**Officier of the Légion d'honneur, 1900** **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**.

**The banknote. This is the draft's largest single error and it is corrected in full.**

The draft claimed Merson was contracted "circa 1908" to design a **50-franc** note "put into circulation from 1907". That conflates two different banknotes and fabricates a circulation date. Per French Wikipedia's dedicated articles:

- **100 francs Luc Olivier Merson.** Created **2 January 1908**, put into circulation **3 January 1910**, printed 1908-1939, **1,689,725,000** notes, 182 x 112 mm. France's first polychrome note actually to circulate. Engraving by **Romagnol**, watermark by **Frédéric Florian**. Recto: allegories of Agriculture and Commerce. Verso: a seated blacksmith for Labour and Fortune with a cornucopia. **[A]**
- **Merson's design work for the 100 F is datable to 1902.** The first printings (2 January 1908 to 10 May 1909) carry the mark **L.O.M 02**, for Luc Olivier Merson 1902. The mark was **removed after lawsuits by the Merson family**. **[A]**
- **50 francs Luc Olivier Merson.** A separate and much later note: created **11 February 1927**, circulated from **3 January 1928**, some 393.3 million printed through 1934, withdrawal begins 26 January 1942, legal tender ends 4 June 1945. Executed from Merson's design by the engravers **Romagnol** and **Ernest Deloche**, seven years after his death. Obverse: two putti and a young pensive Mercury in white filigree cartouches. Reverse: a young blacksmith with a hand on an inverted hammer beside a young woman personifying Minerva and Labour; dominant blue and green; 170 x 123 mm. **[A]**

*Coordinate correction.* The draft used 48.8657 / 2.3417 for the Banque de France, about 150 m off; 1 rue La Vrillière is approximately **48.8646 / 2.3403**.

*Contradiction between sources, flagged.* English Wikipedia says Merson designed banknotes for the Bank of France "by 1908" and mentions a 100-franc note; French Wikipedia's 50-franc article gives 1927 and is silent on any 100-franc design of his. The two are reconciled above only because French Wikipedia carries **separate** articles for the two notes; the two encyclopedias themselves are not reconciled.

## 7. Teaching, and the summers in Brittany

**Académie Vitti.** English Wikipedia gives 1903 **[A]**, but this is not a start date and is probably late. French Wikipedia's own Merson article records the painter **Marguerite Delorme** (1876-1946) studying under him "à l'Académie Vitti de 1890 à 1895" **[A]**. So Merson was teaching there in the early 1890s, within a few years of the academy's 1889 founding; it closed in 1914. The journey file reframes this as "from the early 1890s; still teaching there in 1903" and flags the two datings. Address (49 boulevard du Montparnasse, approximately 48.8422 / 2.3283) is itself approximate.

**Chef d'atelier at the École des Beaux-Arts.** French Wikipedia: 1905-1911. English Wikipedia: 1906-1911. **Contradiction of one year, flagged, not resolved.** **[A both]**

**Carantec, Baie de Morlaix.** He summers with his family at the **Fransic** estate in Carantec, in the company of the composer **Gabriel Pierné**'s household **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Gap:* the estate's coordinates are unknown; the town centre is used as a placeholder and the file says so. No year range is given beyond "1890s onward, recurring".

## 8. The apse of the Sacré-Coeur

Merson designs the great chancel-vault mosaic, *Le Christ en gloire*, for the Basilique du Sacré-Coeur de Montmartre **[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]**. It is one of the largest mosaics in France, 473.78 m².

**Two French Wikipedia articles contradict each other and the draft followed the weaker one.**

- fr.wikipedia's **Merson gallery caption**: "Christ en gloire (1923)… en collaboration avec Henri-Marcel Magne", from which the draft derived "finished three years after Merson's death".
- fr.wikipedia's **Basilique du Sacré-Coeur** article: the apse mosaic was executed **from 1918 to 1922** by the Parisian atelier **Guilbert-Martin**.

If the basilica's own article is right, execution began roughly **two years before Merson's death**, not after it, and completion is **1922**, not 1923. The "finished three years after his death" framing is dropped. The draft's design range "c. early 1900s-1918" is unsourced guesswork and is withdrawn. Coordinate 48.8867 / 2.3431 is correct.

## 9. The last year, the death, the grave

**The banquet of 23 June 1920.** Merson is honoured at a banquet marking the Salon's Médaille d'honneur for lifetime achievement. The toast, by **Paul-Albert-Jean Dagnan-Bouveret**, is printed the following year in *Exposition de l'oeuvre de Luc Olivier Merson* (École Nationale des Beaux-Arts, Paris, May 1921). Read directly in the archive.org OCR text. Three passages are used verbatim in the journey file **[all A]**:

- p. 11: "Car elle est belle et captivante et personnelle, votre oeuvre, et son charme est bien pénétrant !"
- p. 12: the coinage of the adjective **"mersonien"** for the painter's imaginative world.
- pp. 13-14: the comparison to the seventeenth century's ideal of **l'honnête homme**.
- pp. 12-13: nested inside the toast, **Ernest Hébert**'s parting words to his Villa Medici pensionnaires, which Merson heard as a young laureate: "Allez, Messieurs, et ne faites que de l'exquis". This is a quotation inside the primary source, about Merson's formation rather than by him.

**Médaille d'honneur, Salon, 1920** **[R]**: French Wikipedia carries it; the banquet catalogue corroborates a Salon 1920 Médaille d'honneur. Kept as [R] because the two are not fully independent.

**Commandeur of the Légion d'honneur, 1920** **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**. His third grade.

**Death:** 13 November 1920, 5th arrondissement of Paris, aged 74 **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia; the French edition gives the precise date and arrondissement]**.

**Burial:** Père-Lachaise, **division 41** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. English Wikipedia confirms Père-Lachaise only through a category tag, without the division. *Gap:* no tomb inscription, epitaph, or monument photograph was located; the tomb's present physical state is unknown. The burial **date** is not recorded in any reachable source.

## 10. Afterlife

- **The Legion of Honour three times over a career** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**: Chevalier 1881, Officier 1900, Commandeur 1920.
- **The 50-franc note (1927-1945)** and the **posthumous legal dispute** between Merson's heirs and the Banque de France over rights to the design. In its wake **the Banque de France stopped naming painters and engravers on all subsequently issued banknotes** **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Billet de 50 francs Luc Olivier Merson"]**. This is the one durable legend element found in the whole pass: a copyright quarrel that changed a national institution's crediting practice. Note that a *first* quarrel by the same family had already forced the removal of the L.O.M 02 mark from the 100 F in May 1909.
- **The eclipse.** English Wikipedia states Merson's work became "largely forgotten as a result of the overwhelming popularity of the avant-garde art forms" **[A for the statement]**. *Gap:* no corroborating detail, no date range, and **no account of any modern rediscovery, retrospective, or exhibition** was located. The eclipse is attested; a rediscovery story is missing.
- **No dedicated monument, statue, or memorial to Merson himself** (as distinct from his works) was located **[R, stated as a gap rather than as a finding of absence]**.

- **Louis Braille** (`braille.journey.json`). The Panthéon pin is his; Merson's presence in that building could not be confirmed (section 5).

- **Isadora Duncan** and **Rudolf Laban** (`isadora_duncan.journey.json`, `laban.journey.json`). Both arrive in Paris in 1900, the year of the Exposition Universelle and the year the Type Merson stamp is issued.

## 12. Contradictions carried forward unresolved

1. **Société des artistes français in 1875.** fr.wikipedia's Merson article versus fr.wikipedia's own Société article (founded 1881).
2. **Académie Vitti start.** en.wikipedia's 1903 versus fr.wikipedia's own evidence of teaching there from 1890.
3. **Beaux-Arts professorship start.** fr.wikipedia 1905 versus en.wikipedia 1906.
4. **Sacré-Coeur mosaic.** fr.wikipedia's Merson caption (1923, posthumous) versus fr.wikipedia's Basilique article (executed 1918-1922).
5. **Banknotes.** en.wikipedia's "by 1908" and a 100-franc note versus fr.wikipedia's 50-franc article dating everything to 1927 and never mentioning a 100-franc Merson.
6. **Panthéon murals.** Asserted by this dossier's interlock lens; not found by its afterlife lens.
7. **Petit Château demolition.** 1926 (fr.wikipedia) versus 1927 (en.wikipedia).

## 13. Gaps, stated as gaps

- No birth address; the pre-1860 arrondissement is all there is.
- The name of the drawing school is unknown, and Wikipedia says so explicitly.
- No document places Merson anywhere in Italy outside the inference from the Prix de Rome, and no document places him in Naples, Pompeii, Assisi, Florence, or Venice at all.
- No document places him in Egypt, in Monaco, in New York, in Newport, or in Biarritz. His glass went; there is no evidence he did.
- No dates for the Hôtel de Ville, Palais de Justice, Opéra-Comique, Pasteur, or Biarritz commissions.
- No accession dates for the Boston, Nice, or Cleveland pictures.
- No burial date, no epitaph, no tomb photograph.
- No rediscovery story after the eclipse.
- No first-person utterance by Merson survives in the reachable sources. Giraldon's roughly 2,100-line memoir records **not one** verbatim statement in Merson's own voice, which is itself a notable gap for a man who taught for two decades. Everything quoted in the journey file is somebody speaking *about* him or *near* him.
