# Alejandro Christophersen (1866-1946): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: architect, born Cádiz, died Buenos Aires; Norwegian father, Spanish birth, Argentine career.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, with the source named in the bracket · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, proxy, or inference · Contradictions between sources are printed side by side and left open. Gaps are printed as gaps.

**Method and its limits.** This subject has no reachable scholarly biography. Everything below rests on encyclopedic articles (Spanish, English and Norwegian Wikipedia), on the individual building articles those link to, on two Wikimedia Commons file descriptions, and on two web pages of a different order: a hospitality site (Casa Cavia) and an architecture magazine (Arquine). The standard modern reference is named in the Spanish article's bibliography but was **not** consulted: Horacio Caride Bartrons and Rita Molinos, *Alejandro Christophersen* (2014) **[A: es.wiki bibliography]**. No archive, no society minute book, no family paper was reachable. The works list carrying most of this report is a table without per-line citations; where a building has its own article, that article was preferred and the disagreement recorded.

**A note on coordinates.** The geographic pass feeding this file dropped the minus sign from nearly every southern latitude, putting some thirty Buenos Aires points in Syria, and placed a dozen addresses hundreds of metres to kilometres off. Every coordinate here is re-derived from the building's own infobox where one exists, from the street address otherwise, and from the town centre where only a town is known (Serrano, Córdoba).

## 1. Origins and training, 1866-1885

Alejandro Christophersen is born in Cádiz on 30 August 1866 **[A: es.wiki]**, son of Otto Thorvald Alexander ("Thorvald") Christophersen, at that date Norwegian consul in Spain **[A: en.wiki; es.wiki]**, and of Fanny Younger **[A: es.wiki]**.

- **Gap, and a contradiction inside it.** The Spanish article names the mother only as Fanny Younger and gives her no nationality. The claim that his mother was Spanish appears **only** in the Norwegian article **[R: no.wiki]**. The surname is not Spanish. Recorded as unresolved; the journey file says "Norwegian father, Spanish birth" and does not assert the mother's nationality.
- In 1877, aged eleven, he is sent to Norway for secondary studies **[A: es.wiki]**. **Neither the city nor the school is named in any reachable source.** Kristiania (Oslo from 1925) is used in the journey file as a stated proxy for the capital, flagged **[R]** in the stop's date_confidence and campa. This is a real gap: five to seven years of a boy's life are documented by a single clause.
- He graduates in 1885 from the Escuela de Arquitectura y Escenografía of the Royal Academy of Fine Arts, Brussels **[A: es.wiki]**. The pairing of *architecture and scenography* in the school's own name is worth keeping; it is the training of a man taught to build a facade as a stage flat as well as a wall.
- He then studies at the École des Beaux-Arts, Paris **[A: es.wiki / en.wiki]**. **The dates of the Paris period are given nowhere.** The atelier of Jean-Louis Pascal is named in the research pool; it is not in the Spanish article's text and is carried here as **[R]**, unconfirmed.

## 2. The crossing, and the Kihlberg partnership, 1887-1892

**Contradiction, not resolved.** The Spanish article has him cross in **1887**, landing first in Uruguay and moving on to Buenos Aires. The English article gives **1888** as the year he arrives and settles in Buenos Aires **[A: es.wiki vs en.wiki]**. The journey file keeps both as two stops, Montevideo 1887 and Buenos Aires 1888, and says in the date_confidence field that the two years may be two accounts of one crossing.

- The port stop is a reconstruction of place. 1880s transatlantic passengers landed at the Dársena Norte, by the Hotel de Inmigrantes; the research pool's "Dársena Sur" point fell in what is now Puerto Madero, which did not exist. **[R]**
- He practises in partnership with the Swedish architect **Carl Kihlberg** until 1892 **[A: es.wiki]**. **The start year is not attested**; the widely repeated "1887-1892" has no source behind its first number. Joint works named: the Estación Rosario for the Ferrocarril Bahía Blanca al Noroeste, and the Nicholas Bouwer residence at Juramento 1938, Belgrano, c. 1892 **[A: es.wiki works list]**. The further claim that the Bouwer house passed to the Mihanovich shipping family is **not** in the cited source and is dropped.
- From 1892 he practises alone, for nearly five decades **[A: es.wiki]**.

## 3. Avenida de Mayo and the first decade alone, 1893-1898

- **Hospital de Niños Dr. Ricardo Gutiérrez**, Gallo 1330, built 1893-1896 **[A: es.wiki works list; hospital article confirms the 1896 opening]**. The characterization of this as "his earliest major commission" **overstates the record** and is dropped: the Kihlberg works precede it and the Unzué job runs alongside it.
- **Residencia Unzué / Café Tortoni**, Av. de Mayo 825. The works list dates the job 1893; the Café Tortoni article dates the new French-academicist facade to **1898**, cut when the opening of Avenida de Mayo destroyed the building's original front **[A: es.wiki works list 1893 vs es.wiki, 'Café Tortoni', 1898]**. Contradiction left standing; the journey file places the stop at 1898 and says so.
- **Correction of a conflation.** Two buildings had been merged into an "Adela Unzué de Leloir residence" attached to the Tortoni. No such name is in the Spanish article: the Tortoni house was Saturnino Unzué's, while the **Adela Unzué de Leloir** residence is a separate English-article entry, Libertad 1264, dated 1903 there and 1908 in the Spanish works list as Palacio Leloir / Círculo Italiano **[A: en.wiki 1903 vs es.wiki 1908]**. Date contradiction flagged.
- **Edificio María Carranza de Lawson**, Av. de Mayo 902, 1893-1895, later the Hotel Astoria **[A: es.wiki works list; en.wiki gives 1895]**.
- **Escuela No. 19 Leandro N. Alem**, Fray Cayetano Rodríguez 95, 1894 **[A: es.wiki works list]**. The address is in Flores, not, as the research pool glossed it, near the Congreso; the gloss was false by some six kilometres and is deleted.
- **Chalet for Antonio Leloir**, Mar del Plata, 1895, demolished **[A: es.wiki, demolished works]**.
- **Panteón de la Sociedad Española de Socorros Mutuos**, Cementerio de la Chacarita, 1898 **[A: es.wiki works list]**. The name "de Beneficencia" is wrong. A Wikimedia Commons file caption dates it 1896-1898 **[R: Commons file description]**; the works list gives 1898 flat. Flagged, unresolved.
- **Iglesia Ortodoxa Rusa de la Santísima Trinidad**, Av. Brasil 315, built 1898-1901 **[A: es.wiki, 'Catedral de la Santísima Trinidad']**, inaugurated with President Roca present. Christophersen **adapts a design by Mihail Preobrazhensky** of the Russian Holy Synod, working with the engineer Pedro Coni **[A: same article]**. The English article's date of 1905 is contradicted by the building's own article and is recorded as wrong rather than as an open question. The English article gives the address as Brasil 323 **[A: en.wiki]**; the Spanish infobox gives 315.

## 4. The profession, and the provincial banks, c. 1900-1908

- He co-founds Argentina's school of architecture with **Paul Hary** and **Joaquín Mariano Belgrano**, and later holds a titular chair at the School of Architecture in the Faculty of Exact and Natural Sciences of the University of Buenos Aires **[A: es.wiki]**. **No source gives a year for the founding.** The often-quoted 1900 is unsupported; the journey file dates the stop 1901 only to hold its place in sequence and says in the date_confidence field that no year is attested.
- He is **one of the refounders** of the Sociedad Central de Arquitectos **[A: es.wiki]**. The claim that he **led** it, or served multiple terms as its president, rests on the **Norwegian** article alone **[R: no.wiki]** and is contradicted by silence in the Spanish one. No year anywhere. Flagged; the journey file uses "one of the refounders" and mentions the presidency claim as a claim.
- **Residencia Braceras**, Av. Belgrano 2852, 1902, later the AFCP headquarters **[A: es.wiki works list]**. The source expands the acronym nowhere; a guessed expansion circulating in the research pool ("Asociación de Fomento y Cultura Popular") is unsupported and has been deleted.
- **Banco de la Nación Argentina, Bahía Blanca**, Estomba 2, 1903, later the customs house **[A: es.wiki works list]**. The claim that this is his first work outside greater Buenos Aires is **false**: the Mar del Plata chalet of 1895 and the Kihlberg-era Estación Rosario both precede it.
- **Compañía Nueva de Gas**, Adolfo Alsina 1169, by 1904 **[A: es.wiki works list]**.
- **Sociedad Hipotecaria Belga América**, Bartolomé Mitre 226, 1905 **[A: es.wiki works list]**. That the commission followed from his Brussels years is an inference, not a source statement **[R]**.
- Branches of the **Banco de la Nación Argentina** at **Chacabuco** (Belgrano y Sarmiento, 1905), **Dolores** (Belgrano y Gral. Rico, 1905) and **Corrientes** (Carlos Pellegrini y La Rioja, 1905, later the federal courts) **[A: es.wiki works list]**.

## 5. The palaces, 1906-1916

- **Residencia Pedro Méndez**, Cerrito 1250, 1906; **Parroquia Nuestra Señora de Caacupé**, Av. Rivadavia 4879, 1906-1909; **Residencia de Ignacio de Urtubey**, Montevideo, 1907, demolished. All **[A: es.wiki works list]**.
- **Círculo Italiano, former Palacio Leloir**, Libertad 1264: 1908 in the Spanish works list, 1903 in the English article, where it is called the residence of Adela Unzué de Leloir and dated to the Círculo Italiano's occupancy from 1944 **[A: es.wiki vs en.wiki]**. Contradiction left open.
- **Palacio Anchorena**, Arenales 761, Retiro, for **Mercedes Castellanos de Anchorena**. Design 1905, completed and inaugurated **1909** **[A: es.wiki, 'Palacio San Martín']**; the English article says 1910 **[A: en.wiki]**. The repeated "1909-1916" has **no source at all** and looks like contamination from the Bolsa's 1916; deleted. The gloss that the inauguration marked "the centenary of Argentine independence" is also wrong: 1910 was the centenary of the May Revolution, independence having been declared in 1816. The state buys the palace in **1936**, renames it **Palacio San Martín** and seats the Ministry of Foreign Relations and Worship in it **[A: en.wiki / es.wiki]**; **Monumento Histórico Nacional in 1994** **[A: en.wiki]**. The family held it about twenty-seven years, not "roughly twenty".
- **Rosario**: residence for **Luis Pinasco**, Córdoba 1814, now the rectorate of the Universidad Nacional de Rosario; residence for **Casiano Casas and Adela Echesortu**, Córdoba 1852, now the Colegio de Escribanos; and in the **Cementerio El Salvador**, the pantheons of Casiano Casas and of Melitón Ibarlucea **[A: es.wiki works list, Rosario]**. **No dates are given for any of the four.** The journey file places them by street logic and says the years are unattested.
- **A house for the Williams family**, c. 1910, called by one critic possibly his best work: see quotes, section 8.
- **Bolsa de Comercio de Buenos Aires**, Sarmiento 299 / Av. L. N. Alem 356 / 25 de Mayo 367, designed 1913 in Louis XVI Beaux-Arts style with simil-piedra-París facades, inaugurated **1916**; a modernist tower by Mario Roberto Álvarez is appended 1971-1977 **[A: es.wiki, 'Bolsa de Comercio de Buenos Aires']**. A sentence about "compressing the office plan around minimal light courts" is **not in the source** and is struck as editorializing.
- **Asociación Argentina de Actores**, Adolfo Alsina 1762, and **Palacio Dassen**, also on Adolfo Alsina, both 1914; the Dassen work is done **with Claro Cornelio Dassen** **[A: es.wiki works list]**. **Instituto Nuestra Señora del Buen Consejo**, Santa María del Buen Aire 953, Barracas, 1914-1916 **[A]**.

## 6. The Nordic commissions and the exposition, 1918-1922

- **Iglesia de los Marineros Noruegos**, Av. Ing. Huergo 1267, 1918, demolished for the Autopista 25 de Mayo **[A: es.wiki, demolished works]**.
- **Banco Escandinavo Argentino**, Sarmiento 354, projected 1919, inaugurated 1920, since demolished **[A: es.wiki, demolished works]**.
- **Iglesia Asunción de la Santísima Virgen and Colegio San Alberto y San Enrique**, Serrano, Córdoba province, by 1922 **[A: es.wiki works list]**. Serrano is in the Departamento Presidente Roque Sáenz Peña at -34.4711, -63.5401; the building footprint is not confirmed **[R]**.
- **Pabellón Argentino**, Exposição Internacional do Centenário, Rio de Janeiro, 1922, not surviving **[A: es.wiki, demolished works]**. The fair occupied the Esplanada do Castelo and the downtown waterfront **[A: general reference]**.

## 7. The quarrel with the new century, and the late churches, 1920s-1941

- **In the 1920s he carries on a public polemic with the avant-garde architect Alejandro Virasoro**, academic ornament against rationalism and art déco **[A: es.wiki]**. **Source and date both had to be corrected**: the English article does not mention Virasoro at all, and no year is given anywhere. The often-cited 1925 is invented. The journey file dates the stop mid-decade and says the decade is all that is attested.
- **Casa Cavia**, Palermo Chico, c. 1927 **[R: Casa Cavia website]**: see quotes below.
- **Residencia Ovidio Morató**, Av. Dr. Juan B. Alberdi 6189, Montevideo (Carrasco Norte), 1924 **[A: es.wiki works list, which misspells the street "Alberbi"]**.
- **Basílica Santuario de Santa Rosa de Lima**, Av. Belgrano and Pasco. The works list says 1926-1934; the basilica's own article says **1928-1934**, inaugurated 12 October 1934 and blessed by Cardinal Pacelli, consecrated 30 August 1941 **[A: es.wiki, 'Basílica Santuario de Santa Rosa de Lima'; en.wiki also 1928-34]**. Preferred: 1928-1934, with the works-list year flagged. A derived claim that this was "completed eight years before his death" is arithmetically wrong; 1934 is twelve years before 1946.
- **Iglesia Regina Apostolorum**, Av. Avellaneda 2679, Flores, by 1931 **[A]**. **Edificio Transradio**, Av. Corrientes 480, later HSBC, by 1940 **[A]**. **Congregación / Colegio Santa Magdalena Sofía Barat**, Salvador María del Carril 2458, by 1941 **[A]**, among the last documented commissions.

## 8. Quotes: what survives in his own voice, and what does not

**Nothing verifiably in his own voice was found.** This is the single largest gap in the file.

- "El arte arquitectónico moderno no se deja atrapar por los detalles, sino que le da un giro nuevo a una idea antigua." Attributed to Christophersen ("escribió el noruego") on the Casa Cavia website, fetched and confirmed word for word on the page. **[R]**: a hospitality and restaurant site, citing no letter, article, memoir or date. Carried in the journey file as a quote with its doubtful provenance stated in the quote_source field.
- Two phrases from the City of Buenos Aires heritage bill for the Cavia house, quoted on the same site and confirmed there: "la esencia misma del grand siècle" and "un equilibrado juego de masas y luz, sintético y refinado" **[A: as quoted; the bill text was not independently located]**. About his building, not by him.
- Arquine, Alejandro Hernández Gálvez, "La casa: el puente y el pasillo", 19 February 2016: "Esta casa perteneció a su padre, el compositor Alberto Williams, y fue realizada por Alejandro Christophersen -posiblemente su mejor obra de arquitectura- alrededor de 1910." **[A: fetched and confirmed]**. Context that must travel with it: the subject of the passage is **Amancio Williams**, and the house is his father's, the composer **Alberto Williams**. The judgement is a critic's, made ninety years after the fact, not the architect's.

## 9. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **No marriage, spouse, or children are recorded** in any article consulted, Spanish, English or Norwegian. This is a silence in the record, not evidence of a single life.
2. **No cause of death, and no address of death.** He dies in Buenos Aires on 4 February 1946, aged 79, and is buried in the **Cementerio de la Recoleta** **[A: es.wiki]**.
3. **His own grave is unlocated within Recoleta**: no section, no bóveda, no marker is named anywhere reachable. He is recorded as the architect of numerous funerary monuments inside that same cemetery **[A: no.wiki]**, and none of them is named either.
4. **No plaque, street, monument, state honour or posthumous tribute** in his name is documented in any source consulted **[R: absence across es/en/no.wiki and Commons categories]**. Absence of evidence, recorded as such.
5. **No total work count is attested.** The figure "more than 200 works" circulates widely and appears in **no** source consulted; the 2014 Caride Bartrons and Molinos monograph is real, its content unread.
6. **The Norwegian and Spanish years, 1877-1887,** are covered by three sentences in total.
