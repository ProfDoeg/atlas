# Pedro Subercaseaux — report

**Sources.** Biography triangulated across Memoria Chilena (BNC, the three linked articles on his life, the 1920 dispensation, and Von Pilsener), the Spanish and English Wikipedia entries, Artistas Visuales Chilenos/MNBA, two Humanitas.cl pieces on his Benedictine years and the Las Condes monastery, Tebeosfera's Montealegre essay on the comic strip, La Tercera/Cooperativa on the restored *Descubrimiento de Chile*, Senado.cl on the Congress controversy, museohistoriconacional.cultura.gob.ar and canal26 on the *Cabildo Abierto*, and iglesiaspatrimoniales.cl's dedicated Las Condes founding history (the most careful of the lot). Direct quotes are drawn from his own *Memorias* (1962) as excerpted by Memoria Chilena and Senado.cl. All 31 stops are sourced; none rely on a single outlet alone.

**Judgment calls / corrections to the brief.** The brief names Montecassino as the site of his 1920 conversion. Every biographical source consulted — Memoria Chilena, both Wikipedias, Humanitas, artistasvisualeschilenos — places the actual entry at **Quarr Abbey**, Isle of Wight, with solemn profession there and priestly ordination at **Solesmes** (1927). I could not find any documented Subercaseaux visit to the Italian Montecassino. I did find the likely source of the confusion: the Las Condes monastery's own street address, discovered mid-research, is **Montecassino 960** — a Chilean street literally named for the Benedictine motherhouse, decades after Subercaseaux's real conversion at Quarr. I kept Quarr/Solesmes as the true sites and folded the Montecassino echo into the death stop's campa as an honest, sourced coincidence rather than inventing an Italian retreat. Second call: the brief credits him with founding Las Condes outright; the fuller iglesiaspatrimoniales.cl history shows the *idea* originated with his brother Juan (future Archbishop of La Serena) in Rome in 1916, and Pedro joined the 1938 Solesmes founding party as one of its monks, never holding office — the campa for the arrival stop keeps both facts. Third: several AI-summarized web sources gave a fabricated-sounding "4 December 1938 cornerstone, land donated by Loreto Cousiño" detail that does not appear in the more careful primary history; I dropped it rather than launder an unverifiable claim. Fourth: birth date is given as both 10 December and 10 January 1880 across sources; the weight of independent citations (both Wikipedias, Memoria Chilena's main article) favors 10 December, which I used.

**Time-folds.** The Jura de la Independencia canvas (Talca/Santiago stop) was actually painted in 1945, twenty-seven years after the events and seven years after Subercaseaux entered religious life in Chile — I kept it in true chronological position (segment 7, not segment 4) rather than clustering it with the other centenary canvases, since that gap is itself the point: the nation kept commissioning its own myth from the monk. Segment 4's Assisi-to-Vatican stretch compresses several years of parallel activity (battle canvases, the Pius X sitting, the Congress mural) into a tight run; the actual research and gestation of the Franciscan interest likely began earlier and finished only at Quarr (1925), so the "Assisi" stop (1914) is marked *inferred* rather than *attested*.

**Five richest episodes.** (1) The 1911 Pius X portrait, sat for in tails and hung — uniquely for a South American — in the Vatican's Gallery of the Popes, then revisited in 1954 by the same hand, now tonsured, to paint a halo over the same face after its canonization: one object, two signatures, one life. (2) The maqueta Subercaseaux built of the Buenos Aires Cabildo's upper floor from Carranza's mailed photographs, to get 22 May 1810 right from a Santiago studio he'd never left. (3) His own *Memorias*' account of the 1920 parting from Elvira — "no tears, no sobs" — carried in his pocket as a holy card with her photograph glued beneath it. (4) The confession, at Quarr, that the vow of stability did not stop him dreaming nightly of trains, steamers, hotels, passports — the wanderer's vocabulary outliving the wanderer's life. (5) Learning, only well into this research, that the monastery's own address is Montecassino 960 — a coincidence the brief anticipated by name, even if the biography itself resolves differently.

**Connections to the atlas.** This is the first *meta*-journey: four of its centenary-decade stops are deliberately pinned to the exact coordinates of stops already claimed by belgrano (*Cabildo Abierto*, Buenos Aires), san_martin and ohiggins (*Batalla de Chacabuco* and *El Abrazo de Maipú*, both cited by both files), and ohiggins again (*Jura de la Independencia*, Talca) — the painter's itinerary literally overlays theirs, a journey made of other journeys' stations. Lautaro's file already cites Subercaseaux's Conquest canvases in general terms; this file gives that citation a face and a biography. The Douai schoolboy stop and the Quarr/Solesmes arc extend the atlas's small cluster of Benedictine lives (bernard_clairvaux) into the twentieth century and into Chile specifically, while the Von Pilsener stop is the atlas's own "one light stop" instruction taken literally — Chile's first comic, folded into a life otherwise spent on generals, popes, and saints.
