# Juan Martín de Pueyrredón (1777-1850): research report

*Atlas of Journeys working file, 2026-08-06, from the verified research pool for slug `juan_martin_de_pueyrredon`. **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or uncorroborated single-source claim. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.*

The merchant who put the first criollo militia in the field against the British in 1806, and the head of state who paid for the crossing of the Andes in 1817. The pool is strong on chronology (both Wikipedias cross-checked, six datings corrected in verification) and on his own voice (three bodies of first-person text via Wikisource), and weak on the material afterlife: every fetch of a museum or municipal page returned 404.

---

## 1. Dates at the outset, and one internal contradiction

Born **18 December 1777**, Buenos Aires, to **Juan Martín de Pueyrredón y Labroucherie**, French Basque merchant, and **María Rita Damasia O'Doggan y Soria**, criolla **[A: en. and es.wikipedia, "Juan Martín de Pueyrredón"]**. Died **13 March 1850** at the quinta of San Isidro, aged 72 **[A: both]**.

**Flagged:** the pool's geography index gave the birth as **1776**-12-18 while its chronology gave **1777**-12-18. Both Wikipedias give 1777 and the age at death is consistent only with 1777. Treated as a transcription slip, recorded because it propagated into the coordinate block.

## 2. The merchant's apprenticeship, 1777-1805

- **Real Colegio de San Carlos**, withdrawn at about fourteen; father dies **1791** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The withdrawal is dated by his age, not a document.
- Sent abroad **1795** to **Cádiz** for the family business **[A: es.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the pool dated Cádiz "c.1791-1795", putting the arrival where the departure belongs; es.wikipedia has him four years in Buenos Aires after the 1791 death. Cádiz years run c.1795-1803.
- Business travel to **Madrid** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; travel **into France**, no city named **[R]**. The pool geocoded this to Bordeaux, a placement; Bordeaux is used in the journey only for the 1835 exile, where it *is* named **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- Marries his cousin **Dolores Pueyrredón**, 1803, in Spain **[A: en.wikipedia]**; no city given, so the pool's "Cádiz (marriage)" pin is **[R]**.
- Dolores dies **May 1805** after complications from a miscarriage **[R: en.wikipedia only; the es.wikipedia infobox ends the marriage in 1804]**.
- Returns to Buenos Aires **1805** with a considerable fortune **[A: es.wikipedia: "Regresó a su ciudad natal en 1805, habiendo ya reunido una fortuna considerable como comerciante"]**. *Correction:* the pool had the wife dying in Buenos Aires "soon after" the return. Both datings put her death in Spain; he returns a widower.

## 3. The English invasions, 1806-1809

- **1 August 1806, Combate de Perdriel:** commands criollo militia, overrun **[A: es.wikipedia, both articles]**. *Coordinates corrected:* the pool gave -34.547 / -58.713; es.wikipedia gives **-34.5633 / -58.5692**, in Villa Ballester Oeste, ~13 km east.
- Escapes to **Colonia del Sacramento**, joins Liniers **[A: en.wikipedia]**; no day.
- **12 August 1806, the Reconquista** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. **14 August 1806**, given the **Húsares de Buenos Aires** **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **November 1806**, named by the **Cabildo** its representative to the **Madrid government** **[A: es.wikipedia: "En el mes de noviembre, el cabildo de Buenos Aires lo nombró su representante ante el gobierno de Madrid para pedir ayuda"]**. *Two corrections:* the pool dated this **1807** after en.wikipedia, and said he was sent to the **Junta Central**, which did not exist until 25 September 1808 (es.wikipedia, "Junta Suprema Central"). He went to the Madrid government of Carlos IV and dealt with the Junta Central only in 1808-09. **Contradiction left standing:** en.wikipedia's 1807 dating is not withdrawn by its own text; the journey uses November 1806 and carries the divergence in `date_confidence`.
- **1809, Montevideo:** arrested by governor **Elío** for suspected Carlotism; escapes **[A: es.wikipedia]**. No month.
- **1809, Rio de Janeiro:** unofficial envoy of the Carlotist faction, which sought to seat the Infanta **Carlota Joaquina** over the Río de la Plata **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Returns via Brazil the same year **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Claim rejected for want of a subject-specific source.** The lens asserted, tagged [A], that Pueyrredón "was among the voting delegates" at the **Cabildo Abierto of 22 May 1810**, citing `belgrano.journey.json`, which is evidence for Belgrano and not for him. Neither Wikipedia places him there. **No Cabildo Abierto stop was written.** If a padrón of the voters is later consulted and he is on it, add one.

## 4. Córdoba, Chuquisaca, and the silver, 1810-1812

- **3 August 1810**, governor intendente of **Córdoba** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. (Liniers's execution at Cabeza de Tigre, 26 August 1810, is standard context not in the pool; Pueyrredón's relation to it is **[R]**.)
- **26 January 1811**, governor intendente of **Chuquisaca** **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **1811:** retreats through **Potosí** saving the royal silver, and brings it and the treasury funds south to **Salta** **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **September 1811**, commander of the **Ejército del Norte** after Huaqui **[A: es.wikipedia for the appointment; **[R]** for Jujuy as the place, so tagged in the pool]**.
- **4 October 1811, Campo Santo:** writes the *Informe... sobre la retirada de los caudales de Potosí* **[A: es.wikisource, date and place verified in the document heading]**.
- **26 March 1812, Posta de Yatasto:** hands the Army of the North to **Belgrano** by order of the Triumvirate **[A: es.wikipedia, "Posta de Yatasto"]**. *Coordinates corrected:* the pool gave -25.15 / -64.9333; es.wikipedia gives 25°35′24″S 64°56′59″O near San José de Metán, ~49 km away, and the atlas already pins Yatasto at **-25.5901 / -64.9498**, which is inherited. *Caveat:* the "disputed Yatasto meeting" of the literature is the 1814 Belgrano-San Martín handover, not this one.

**Quotations from the *Informe*, all [A] via es.wikisource** (English as used in the journey file):

- "Apenas se supo la derrota de nuestro ejército en Guaquí, ó mas bien su increible disolución..."
- "Así es que vimos al momento á todo el pueblo de Oruro convertido en nuestro daño."
- "El populacho dormía descuidado, ó preparaba tal vez en el silencio de la noche los cordeles con que intentaba atarme al yugo de su infelicidad."
- "Los oficiales han hecho las veces de soldados... Los soldados han hecho prodigiosamente el ministerio de tales."

Two translation corrections. The opening line is impersonal (`se supo`); the pool's "we learned" inserted an actor the text lacks. The "cords" line was clipped without an ellipsis, dropping `de su infelicidad` and with it the sense: the yoke is *their* wretchedness.

## 5. The Triumvirate, the fall, San Luis, 1812-1815

- **1812**, member of the **Primer Triunvirato** **[A: es.wikipedia]**; no day of taking his seat given.
- **8 October 1812**, overthrown in the coup led by the **Logia Lautaro** with Alvear and San Martín; sent to San Luis **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **1812-1815, San Luis.** **The sources differ on whether this was exile or reassignment** and it is **not resolved**: en.wikipedia says exile, es.wikipedia is less categorical. Illegitimate daughter **Virginia** by **Juana Sánchez** there **[R: en.wikipedia only]**.
- **1814, Mendoza:** San Martín meets him about the Andes plan **[R: es.wikipedia, tagged reconstruction in the pool]**.
- **May 1815**, marries **María Calixta Tellechea y Caviedes**, aged fourteen **[A: both]**. *Corrections:* en.wikipedia gives his age as 39; born 18 December 1777 he was **37**. The pool's "back in Buenos Aires early in the year" and "settles at a country house in San Isidro" are unsupported: es.wikipedia places the marriage in the San Luis context and says Calixta **already owned** the quinta ("Ésta era dueña de una quinta en San Isidro, que sería su casa durante el resto de su vida"). **Contradiction inside the pool:** its afterlife index dated the marriage to **1821**, six years late, against its own chronology and geography. 1821 is the return; 1815 the marriage.

## 6. Director Supremo, 1816-1819

- **3 May 1816**, elected **Director Supremo** by the Congress of Tucumán **[A: es.wikipedia, "Juan Martín de Pueyrredón" and "Congreso de Tucumán"]**.
- **1816, Trancas:** meets **José Rondeau** on the road north **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Folded in as context, not written as a stop.
- **15 June 1816, Fuerte de Cobos:** meets **Güemes** **[A: es.wikipedia "Cobos (Salta)", and independently in the Pueyrredón article]**. *Tag upgraded* from the pool's [R]. *Coordinates corrected:* the pool gave -24.9 / -65; Cobos is at 24°43′00″S 65°06′00″O, i.e. **-24.7167 / -65.10**.
- **9 July 1816**, the Congress declares the **Provincias Unidas en Sud América** independent **[A]**. **Major correction:** the pool said Pueyrredón "as Director Supremo presided in influence" and flagged an unresolved en/es discrepancy. Both parts fail. He was elected 3 May, had left Tucumán, was at Cobos on 15 June, and reached Buenos Aires on 29 July; **Laprida** presided on 9 July. The "discrepancy" is not one: 3 May is the election, 9 July the declaration. What survives is that **en.wikipedia misdates his term start to 9 July 1816**, carried in `date_confidence`. **A third date, from the lens, rejected:** two lens items give the election as **"3 July 1816"** and argue from it. No source supports 3 July; the lens's geographical point survives, its chronology does not.
- **29 July 1816**, arrives in Buenos Aires and takes the Fuerte **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **1816-1817**, reorganizes the **Logia Lautaro** as the **Gran Logia** or **Logia Ministerial**, with minister **Gregorio García de Tagle** and general **Tomás Guido** **[A: es.wikipedia, "Logia Lautaro"]**.
- **1816-1817**, backs and finances the **Army of the Andes** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **9 June 1819**, resigns amid provincial revolts against the centralist 1819 constitution **[A: both]**.

**Correspondence with San Martín, all [A] via Mitre/Pilling, *The Emancipation of South America* (Chapman and Hall, 1893), Wikisource:**

- Ch. 13, c. January 1817: "Don't ask me for anything more unless you wish to hear that I have hung myself to a beam in the fort."
- Ch. 13, second quoted passage: "You may well say that among us there has never been seen an army so well fitted out; but neither has there been seen a Director who had equal confidence in a general, and, it must be added, never a general who so well merited that confidence as yourself."
- Ch. 13, closing line of that second passage: "After all, my mind would be easier if you had another thousand good soldiers with you."
- Ch. 19, 1818: "If you do that again, I am bankrupt, and we are lost."

Two corrections to the pool's framing. (1) It called the second and third passages a **continuation of the beam letter**; Mitre separates them with "And also:", marking a distinct quotation. **Whether the three lines come from one letter or two is not settled by Mitre and is not settled here**; "sent shortly before the crossing" is inference. (2) The pool said San Martín seized "specie belonging to Chilean merchants" and that Pueyrredón could not honour the drafts. Mitre reverses both: coin travelling **from** Chile **to** merchants **in Buenos Aires** was intercepted because transit was unsafe, and "Pueyrredon, with great difficulty managed to pay these drafts on presentation, but he wrote to San Martin:—".

## 7. The Year Twenty and the retirement, 1820-1834

- **1820**, after **Cepeda** and during the **Anarquía del Año XX**, governor **Sarratea** has him arrested at the caudillos' insistence, then helps him escape to **Montevideo** **[A: es.wikipedia, "terminando exiliado en Montevideo"]**. **Contradiction inside the pool, corrected:** its chronology dated the exile **1819**; its geography and afterlife indexes said **1820**, which the causal chain requires.
- **March 1821**, returns to Buenos Aires **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **24 January 1823**, birth of **Prilidiano Pueyrredón** **[A: es.wikipedia on the son; the father's article gives only 1823 and no place]**.
- **1829**, publishes ***Refutación a una atroz calumnia*** (Imprenta de la Independencia), answering **Alexander H. Everett**, U.S. minister, who had printed the claim that he took Spanish bribes as Director **[A via es.wikisource]**. The pool called it "his one documented piece of writing", ignoring the 1811 *Informe*.
- **1829**, attempts to mediate between **Lavalle** and **Rosas**; it fails **[A: both]**. No month given; the pool's coordinate was a generic provincial point, so the journey pins it in the city.

**Quotations from the *Refutación*, all [A] via es.wikisource** (two are carried into the journey file with English):

- "el señor ministro me ha ofendido groseramente, imputando á mi nombre el crimen mas infame."
- "siempre me ha sobrado fuego y decision por la libertad y la independencia de mi pais."
- "yo devolví un estado con importancia interior, y con un crédito exterior, superior á todo concepto." (On the June 1819 resignation.)
- "No es, señor, de la mansion de los muertos en que V. me coloca mas ha de tres años... es del seno de mi patria, en que disfruto la tranquilidad que dá una conciencia sin remordimientos."
- "yo protesto que me valdré de todos los arbitrios que dan las leyes del país a que V. pertenece." (A threat of suit if Everett did not retract.)

## 8. The second exile, 1835-1849

Leaves the country **1835** with Rosas's return to power **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia gives 1835-1849 without a departure city]**. Residences named, all es.wikipedia: **Bordeaux [A]**, **Rio de Janeiro [A]**, **Paris [R: city only, no address, duration, or year]**. **No internal chronology of the fourteen years is recoverable**; the journey places Rio at 1840 and Paris at 1845 as placements, not datings, and says so in `date_confidence`. Returns to **San Isidro, October 1849 [A]**.

## 9. Death and afterlife

- **13 March 1850**, dies at San Isidro, aged 72 **[A]**. **False contradiction removed:** the pool claimed en.wikipedia put the death at the ranch and es.wikipedia in Buenos Aires, "a discrepancy this record does not resolve". es.wikipedia says he returned to San Isidro in October 1849 and "allí murió en marzo del año siguiente". Both put it at San Isidro. **Caveat kept:** the es.wikipedia prose gives only "marzo"; the day **13** comes from the infobox and en.wikipedia, not a register.
- Buried at the **Cementerio de la Recoleta** **[A: both]**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the es.wikipedia article **on the cemetery does not list him** among its notable interments. No tomb photograph, vault, or section confirmed. An open question, not a refutation.
- **3 November 1870**, Prilidiano dies at the same quinta **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The house survives as the **Museo Juan Martín de Pueyrredón** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; no founding date, address, or institutional history obtainable (§11).
- A **monument** in Buenos Aires by **Wilfredo Villadrich** **[A: en.wikipedia image caption only]**. Site, date, materials unconfirmed. No coordinates asserted, no stop written.
- **15 October 1879**, the **Partido de General Pueyrredón**, seated at Mar del Plata, is created and named for him for the invasions and the war of independence **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- Prilidiano painted a portrait of his father called "particularly appreciated" **[A for existence and reputation; the canvas not located or dated]**.
