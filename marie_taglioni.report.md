# Marie Taglioni (1804-1884): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14. Subject: Marie Sophie Taglioni, the dancer to whom expressive pointe work and the white Romantic tutu are conventionally credited.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, lore, or a flagged inference. Contradictions are printed and left open unless the adjudication is shown.

**Coordinates.** Approximate pins, flagged in the journey file: the Moscow-Petersburg road (57.7 / 34.5, illustrative, attesting nothing), the cimetière Saint-Charles (cemetery not plot), and city-centre pins where no building is identified. The Salle Le Peletier pin (48.8722 / 2.3378) is inherited byte-identical from `richard_wagner.journey.json`.

## 1. Birth and family, 1804

- Born **Stockholm, 23 April 1804**, to the Italian ballet master **Filippo Taglioni** and the Swedish dancer and singer **Sophie Karsten**, daughter of the actor **Christoffer Christian Karsten** of the Royal Swedish stage. **[A: Wikipedia "Marie Taglioni"; New World Encyclopedia; fr.wikipedia]**
- Brother **Paul Taglioni**, dancer and choreographer **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Filippo held posts at Stockholm (1803-04, 1817-18) and the Vienna Kärntnertortheater **[A: Wikipedia "Filippo Taglioni"]**.
- **Gap:** the birth house is not identified anywhere reachable; the pin is city-centre. Her birth date is the origin of the spurious "23 April 1884" death date in circulation (see §9).

## 2. Training: Paris, then Vienna, c.1815-1822

- Child study in Paris under **Jean-François Coulon**, before her father takes the training over. **[A: fr.wikipedia; Wikipedia]**
- A Paris instructor reportedly asked whether "that little hunchback" would ever learn to dance. **[R: New World Encyclopedia; untraced to any primary document, wording varies by teller]** Contemporaries judged her build wrong for the stage: curved back, long arms, weak ankles, against the compact and forceful dancers then in fashion. **[A: Wikipedia "Filippo Taglioni" and "Marie Taglioni"]**
- **Sequence corrected:** Filippo was discouraged by her progress *in Paris*, then took her to *Vienna* for the regimen; many summaries blur the two cities. **[A: Wikipedia "Filippo Taglioni"]**
- The regimen: **six hours a day for six months**, Vienna, c.1822, drilling leg exercises and adagio positions by "a level method of technique training," with no sympathy shown for her bleeding feet. **[A: Wikipedia "Filippo Taglioni"; it.wikipedia]** **Corrections logged:** the pool's "four to six hours" is wrong, sources being unanimous on six; and its "circa 1822-1827" conflates the six-month training with the later German engagements.

## 3. Debut and the German circuit, 1822-1827

- Debut aged 18 in **Vienna** in a divertissement choreographed by her father. **[A: Wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia; Vuillier 1898]**
- Exact date and venue: **10 June 1822, Kärntnertortheater**. **[A: de.wikipedia]**
- **Title contradiction, open:** de.wikipedia names the piece *Anacreontisches Divertissement*, role the nymph Delia; fr./en. accounts render it *La Réception d'une jeune nymphe à la cour de Terpsichore*. Almost certainly one evening under two titles, but no source states that identification. **[A for each rendering / R for the identification]**
- Between the debut and Paris she dances in **Munich, Stuttgart, Kassel (Cassel)**. **[A: fr.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]**
- **Date contradiction, open:** de.wikipedia gives Munich and Stuttgart **1825-1827**, fr.wikipedia gives Cassel, Stuttgart, Munich **1822-1826**. Cities agreed, span not; the journey file carries the disagreement in `date_confidence` at all three stops.
- **Claim dropped:** the pool asserted Filippo was *ballet master at Stuttgart*. Wikipedia's "Filippo Taglioni" lists only Stockholm and the Vienna Kärntnertor, so the journey file says some accounts make him ballet master there and the standard list does not. **[R, unsupported]** Kassel rests on **one source** (fr.wikipedia): recorded as thin.

## 4. Paris, 1827-1837

- **Paris Opéra debut, 23 July 1827**, in *Le Sicilien, ou L'Amour peintre*, Salle Le Peletier **[A: de.wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia]**; engaged as a principal **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Correction logged:** the ballet was **not** her father's. It was by **Anatole (Petit)**; Filippo made only the pas de deux she danced within it. **[A: de.wikipedia]**
- Filippo negotiates a **six-year contract** for the pair on the strength of the debut. **[R: Wikipedia "Filippo Taglioni"; no date, no terms, no register reachable]**
- **First London appearance, 1829**, King's Theatre, Haymarket (renamed Her Majesty's on Victoria's 1837 accession). **[A: Justin McCarthy, *A History of Our Own Times*, in *Dance Index* 3.7-8 (1944), p. 119]**
- **21 November 1831:** the "Ballet of the Nuns," Act III of Meyerbeer's *Robert le Diable*, choreographed by Filippo. Marie dances the **Abbess Helena six times**; **Louise Fitzjames** succeeds her and dances it **232 times**. **[A: Wikipedia "Robert le diable"]** **Claim dropped:** the pool credited the scene to "new gas lighting"; the article credits **Duponchel** and **Ciceri**'s set design and says nothing about gas.
- **12 March 1832: *La Sylphide***, Salle Le Peletier. Choreography Filippo Taglioni, libretto **Adolphe Nourrit** after Nodier's *Trilby*, music **Jean-Madeleine Schneitzhoeffer**, Marie in the title role, **Joseph Mazilier** as James. **[A: Wikipedia "La Sylphide"]**
- The costume: a calf-length white bell of gauze by **Eugène Lami**, shortened to display pointe work, thought scandalous, and the template for the Romantic tutu. **[A: Wikipedia "La Sylphide"; "Tutu (clothing)"]** **Correction logged:** the basic silhouette was drawn by **Hippolyte Lecomte** for **Pauline Montessu** in *La Somnambule* (**1827**), **five** years before 1832, not the pool's "two."
- Historiographic credit: *La Sylphide* is where pointe work acquires an expressive rather than acrobatic rationale, and where the ballerina in white becomes the century's dominant image. **[A: Wikipedia "La Sylphide", "Marie Taglioni"]**
- **N. P. Willis** saw her in *Le Dieu et la Bayadère* at the Opéra; published in *Pencillings by the Way*. **[A: *Dance Index* 3.7-8 (1944), p. 118]** **Gap:** he does not date the evening, and the journey file's 1833 is flagged as a placeholder.
- **Rivalry with Fanny Elssler** (at the Opéra from 1834) is the reason usually given for the 1837 departure. **[R: general historiography; Vuillier pairs the names but does not narrate the departure]**

## 5. Marriage, 1832-1844

- Married **Jean-Pierre-Victor-Alfred Gilbert de Voisins (1800-1863)**, comte. **[A]** **Contradiction, adjudicated in the open:** de.wikipedia gives **18 September 1832, in London**; English summaries give **1835**, and the pool put the wedding in **France**. de.wikipedia is the only source with a full date and a city, so the journey file follows it and records the disagreement in `date_confidence`.
- Breaks down after roughly three years amid infidelity; the couple separate. **[A: it.wikipedia, no month]**
- **Divorce 1844.** Two children, "of disputed paternity in some accounts"; the son marries into the **Ralli** family, the daughter marries Prince **Alexander Trubetskoy**. **[A for the marriages, it.wikipedia, Wikipedia; R for the paternity doubt, which nothing consulted substantiates]**
- **Gap, and a dropped stop:** the son **Georges-Philippe Gilbert de Voisins** was born **1843**, not 1842 (de. and it.wikipedia agree). The pool inferred a Saint Petersburg birthplace from the 1842 date; with 1843 her Russian seasons had ended and the inference collapses. The stop was **dropped from the journey file**.

## 6. Russia, 1837-1842

- She leaves the **Paris Opéra Ballet** in 1837 for Saint Petersburg. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Corrections logged:** the pool said she left *Her Majesty's Theatre, London*; and the contract was for **three years**, not five, with the winter seasons then running through **1842**. **[A: Wikipedia; de.wikipedia]**
- Dances at the **Bolshoi Kamenny Theatre**, since demolished, the Conservatory now on the site, and becomes the object of an unprecedented cult among Russian **balletomanes**. **[A: Wikipedia, in general terms]**
- **Moscow appearances: [R].** In general accounts only; no date, venue or programme confirmed. Carried as a reconstruction stop and labelled one.
- **20 May 1841: debut at the Teatro alla Scala, Milan.** **[A: it.wikipedia, to the day]** It falls inside the Russian span: the engagement was seasonal, not continuous residence. **1842:** last Russian season. **[A]**
- **The auctioned shoes: [R].** A pair of her worn shoes said to have sold for **200 roubles**; admirers said to have cooked, sauced and eaten them. **Correction logged:** the pool called the auction "well attested" and only the meal embellishment. English Wikipedia hedges **the sale itself** with "reportedly" and cites nothing for either half. **Both halves are lore.**
- **The bandits on the road: [R], the largest unresolved gap here.** Tradition holds her carriage was stopped on a Russian road (usually the Moscow-Petersburg road; sometimes Cossacks rather than bandits) by men who made her dance on a table by the roadside and then paid her in gold. **No primary citation could be located** in EN/FR/IT Wikipedia, New World Encyclopedia, or general search. QUEUE.md's brief flagged this story, so it is what put her in the queue; the honest finding is that it has no traceable source.

## 7. *Pas de Quatre* and retirement, 1845-1847

- **12 July 1845:** Jules Perrot's *Pas de Quatre*, **Her Majesty's Theatre**, London, uniting **Taglioni, Carlotta Grisi, Lucile Grahn, Fanny Cerrito**, ordered onstage by seniority (Taglioni last) to manage the rivalries. **[A: Wikipedia "Pas de Quatre"]** **Correction logged:** the pool said "staged by Jules Perrot for Queen Victoria." It was staged for **Benjamin Lumley's** season; Victoria and Albert merely **attended the third performance, 17 July 1845**.
- **Retirement, 21 August 1847**, Her Majesty's Theatre, in ***Le Jugement de Paris*** (Perrot / Pugni), aged 43. **[A: de.wikipedia]** Several summaries place the farewell in Paris and give the year only. **Coordinate correction:** the Haymarket house is at **51.5081 / -0.1319**, not the pool's 51.5091 / -0.1347 (~250 m off); the corrected pin is used at all three Haymarket stops.

## 8. Venice, Como, and the Opéra again, 1846-1863

- **Ca' d'Oro, Venice: Alessandro Trubetzkoi purchased it in 1846 and gifted it to her.** **[A: Wikipedia "Ca d'Oro"]**
  - **Correction logged, and it inverts the story:** the pool had her *purchasing and restoring* the palace. She did neither. She **sold architectural features out of it**, including the **Bartolomeo Bon red Verona marble wellhead**; **Baron Giorgio Franchetti** later restored the building "after the reckless renovation." Coordinate corrected to 45.4406 / 12.3339 (pool ~185 m off). **Gap:** the dates of the individual sales are unrecorded.
- **Villa Maria, Blevio, Lake Como.** it.wikipedia "Blevio" names *Villa Maria*, also called *la Florida*, raised out of an older *Ca' dell'Imperatore*, for the ballerina who lived there: this upgrades the pool's **[R]** unidentified villa to **[A]**. **Claim dropped:** "shared with her son's family" is unsupported; the Blevio link runs to **Prince Alexander Troubetzkoy**, who kept his own villa on that shore.
- **Inspectrice de la danse, Paris Opéra.** **[A: Wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]** **Contradiction, open:** the tenure is given as **1858-1870** and as **1859-1870**; both carried in `date_confidence`, neither picked.
- **13 April 1860:** juror at the first annual corps de ballet competition. **[R: one uncorroborated summary, whose day-level precision exceeds what it can support; the journey file says so]**
- **26 November 1860: *Le Papillon***, her only original choreographic work, for her pupil **Emma Livry**, music by **Jacques Offenbach**. **[A]**
- Livry had danced the title role in an **1858 revival of *La Sylphide***, which brought Taglioni out of retirement to coach her. **[A: Wikipedia "La Sylphide"]**
- **Correction logged, three errors in the pool's Livry material.** Per Wikipedia "Emma Livry": the fire was **15 November 1862**, at a **rehearsal**, of **Auber's opera *La Muette de Portici*** (Livry dancing the mime role of Fenella), **not "another ballet"**; she died **26 July 1863** of **septicaemia**, two years eight months after *Le Papillon*, not "three years."
- **Gap:** nothing records what Taglioni said or did about Livry's death. The journey file states the fact and the twenty-one following years without choreography, and invents no reaction.

## 9. Poverty, death, and the graves, 1871-1884

- Moves to **London**, teaching social dance and a few private ballet pupils; residence **14 Connaught Square**. **[A]** **Contradiction, open:** Wikipedia dates the residence **1875-1876**, it.wikipedia the move to London **1871**. **Henri Bauer** left an account of her teaching English schoolgirls in Hyde Park, quoted by Vuillier just before the "very old and very poor" line. **[A: Vuillier 1898, p. 206]**
- **1880:** moves to **Marseille** to live with her son, having lost most of her fortune. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **Cause of the collapse: [R]**, bad investments, family mismanagement and general upheaval are all named in general accounts; nothing pins a cause to a source.
- **Death: 22 April 1884, Marseille**, one day before her eightieth birthday. **[A]** **The apparent contradiction dissolves and is recorded as dissolved:** EN, FR, DE and IT Wikipedia all give 22 April 1884, the "23 April" variant being her **birth** date migrating across the page, and "24 April" appearing in one English summary only. **Claim dropped:** the pool's "at her residence in the Saint-Giniez quarter" appears in neither the EN nor the FR article text.
- **First burial: cimetière Saint-Charles, Marseille** (plot unidentified; the pin marks the cemetery), then **transfer to Père-Lachaise, 94th division, tomb of her grandson Auguste Gilbert de Voisins**, pin 48.8619 / 2.3936. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** **Gap:** the transfer is **undated** everywhere consulted.
- **Contradiction, open:** English Wikipedia calls the burial site debated between **Montmartre** and **Père-Lachaise**, and raises the possibility that the Montmartre grave is **her mother's**; fr. and it.wikipedia give Père-Lachaise flatly. The journey file carries both stops.
- **The pointe-shoe tribute: [R], and it attaches to the *Montmartre* grave**, which is the whole interest of it: dancers leave worn pointe shoes there "as a tribute and thanks to the first pointe dancer" (Wikipedia). The pool placed this vaguely in "Paris," losing the point that the tribute is paid at a stone she is probably not under. **No origin date is reported;** the journey file's 1900 is a placeholder.

## 10. Afterlife and material traces

- **1892: Marius Petipa** revives *La Sylphide* for the Imperial Ballet, additional music by **Riccardo Drigo**. **1972: Pierre Lacotte**'s archival reconstruction of the 1832 choreography. **[A: Wikipedia "La Sylphide"]**
- Not made stops: the **1828 Musée Carnavalet portrait** and the **Kriehuber lithograph of 1839** **[A: fr.wikipedia]** would pin an object's present home or assert an unsupported Vienna journey; the **Venus crater "Taglioni"** **[R]** is not mappable.

## 11. Interlocks with existing atlas travelers

Two are named in campa text, both relations to Taglioni herself rather than co-location:

- **sergei_diaghilev.** Fokine's *Les Sylphides*, on the Ballets Russes' second Paris programme (2 June 1909, Châtelet), is named directly after *La Sylphide* and restages its image of women in tulle en pointe: a one-directional citation of her ballet. **[A: sergei_diaghilev.journey.json]** Named in the *La Sylphide* campa.
- **isadora_duncan.** Duncan's rejection of pointe shoes, corsetry and the tutu is a direct repudiation of the convention Taglioni founded in 1832. **[R: george_balanchine.journey.json]** Named in the "skirt cut short" campa, where the refused garment is the subject.

**Deliberately not named**, the relation being institutional or geographic only: **vaslav_nijinsky**, **george_balanchine**, **tchaikovsky** (Imperial lineage and repertory, no personal connection), **richard_wagner** (the 1861 *Tannhäuser* riot on the same stage, twenty-nine years later), **coco_chanel** (Ballets Russes costume work), **hans_christian_andersen** (whose Copenhagen link runs through Bournonville).

**Missing interlock worth queueing: August Bournonville**, who saw the Paris *Sylphide* in 1834 and restaged it in Copenhagen in 1836 with Lucile Grahn. His is the version through which the ballet survived. No journey file.

## 12. Open contradictions and honest gaps

**Contradictions carried forward, unresolved**, each flagged in its section above: German circuit dates · debut title · marriage year and city (adjudicated for de.wikipedia, disagreement printed) · inspectrice tenure · move to London · burial site.

**Gaps, stated as gaps.** The bandit legend has no traceable primary source, and neither half of the auctioned-and-eaten shoes is documented · Georges-Philippe's birthplace is unknown once 1842 is corrected to 1843, so the stop was dropped rather than guessed · the Saint-Charles plot and the date of the Père-Lachaise transfer are unrecorded · no dated Moscow appearance, no Berlin date, no programme for either · nothing survives on what she said about Emma Livry's death, or in her own words about her father's training, the bleeding-feet detail coming from biographers · the six-year Opéra contract's terms are in no reachable register.
