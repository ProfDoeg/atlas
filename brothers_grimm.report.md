# Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm: research report
*2026-08-08. Atlas of Journeys, slug `brothers_grimm`. Two travelers treated as one itinerary: from 1798 until Wilhelm's death in 1859 they were almost never separately housed.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, tourism folklore, or an inference the sources do not make themselves. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Where a coordinate or date came from outside the verified pool, it is said so.

**What this subject is.** Two brothers walking Hessian lanes to take down tales from illiterate peasants at the hearth is a nineteenth-century construction, encouraged in part by the brothers' own prefaces, and source criticism (Heinz Rölleke above all) has dismantled it: many of the best informants were literate, middle-class, and of Huguenot descent, with Perrault in their ears. The report keeps both layers visible. The journey file, whose register is "national mythology: the canon is true", narrates the canon and lets the correction sit in `date_confidence` and in this document.

## 1. Hanau and Steinau, 1785-1798

**Births [A].** Jacob Ludwig Carl Grimm, Hanau, 4 January 1785; Wilhelm Carl Grimm, Hanau, 24 February 1786 (Britannica, "Brothers Grimm"). The birth house stood on the Paradeplatz, today Freiheitsplatz, destroyed in the air raid of 19 March 1945; a municipal marker names the spot (Hanau Gedenktafeln PDF). Coordinate 50.1344, 8.9188.

**Neustadt residence [R].** The family is said to have lived in the Hanau Neustadt before 1791 (Brüder Grimm-Gesellschaft Kassel). Unverified; no stop.

**Steinau [A].** 1791: Philipp Wilhelm Grimm appointed Amtmann at Steinau an der Straße; the family moves into the official residence, a Renaissance Amtshaus of 1562, children below and the father's court above. Now the Brüder Grimm-Haus museum.

> **Contradiction.** Geography lens: Brüder-Grimm-Straße **70**; afterlife lens: **80**. Checking (brueder-grimm-haus.de, steinau.eu, de.wikipedia) supports **80**. The journey names the house, not the number.

**The father's death [A].** Pneumonia, Steinau, 10 January 1796. Widow and six children lost income and residence together. Jacob was eleven, Wilhelm nine.

**Kassel [A].** 1798: the two eldest sent to their mother's sister Henriette Zimmer, lady-in-waiting at the Hessian court, to attend the Lyceum Fridericianum. They entered below their proper class rank and worked up.

**Health [R].** An unattributed claim (Wikipedia, "Wilhelm Grimm") holds that both suffered severe illness as adolescents and that Wilhelm was left with chronic heart and respiratory trouble. No primary source in the pool.

## 2. Marburg, 1802-1806

**Enrolment [A].** Jacob 1802, Wilhelm 1803, both to read law. As sons of a lower civil servant they were excluded from stipends reserved for noble students, a slight Jacob records with bitterness.

**Savigny [A].** Friedrich Carl von Savigny's private library and historical method turned them from law toward philology and the German past. The hinge of the whole life, well attested, but only through scholarship: the Grimm-Savigny correspondence itself was not reached.

**Paris 1805 [A].** Savigny took Jacob to work on medieval legal manuscripts for the *Geschichte des römischen Rechts im Mittelalter*. First journey outside the German lands. Bibliothèque impériale, rue de Richelieu: 48.8675, 2.3389.

**Kassel 1805 [A].** Dorothea Grimm and the younger children moved to Kassel; Jacob joined them on his return, the household gathered for the first time since 1796.

## 3. The collecting years, 1806-1815

**The impulse [A].** From about 1806, moved by Brentano and Achim von Arnim's *Des Knaben Wunderhorn*, the brothers began systematically collecting Märchen (uc.edu). The deeper cause, Savigny's method applied to language instead of law, is inference, [R] where used.

**The Wild family [A].** A pharmacist's household in Kassel; the daughters, Dortchen among them, told tales that entered the collection. Wilhelm married Dortchen in 1825 (Rölleke).

**The Hassenpflugs [A].** A Kassel family of Huguenot descent whose daughters supplied tales later recognized as closer to French literary tradition than to German oral folklore (Rölleke). The sharpest evidence against the peasant-hearth story.

**Jérôme Bonaparte's library [A, location corrected].** 1808: Jacob appointed librarian to the King of Westphalia at Kassel, a paid post that carried the family through the occupation.

> **Correction.** The geography lens gave "Palais Bellevue area", 51.3237, 9.4996, matching neither candidate site. Jérôme's library was at Schloss Napoleonshöhe, the renamed Wilhelmshöhe, about **51.3150, 9.4161**. Palais Bellevue (51.3098, 9.4939) later housed the Brüder Grimm-Museum, the likely source of the confusion.

> **Second correction.** The chronology lens dates "auditor to the state council" to 1813. Jérôme appointed Jacob auditor in **1809**, concurrent with the librarianship; the Secretary of Legation post belongs to **1813**. Two events fused; neither gets a stop.

**The mother's death [A].** Dorothea Grimm died at Kassel, 27 May 1808, leaving Jacob at twenty-three responsible for four younger brothers and a sister.

**Dorothea Viehmann [A].** A tailor's widow of Huguenot descent at Niederzwehren told the brothers a large part of the second volume's material from about 1813. Wilhelm named her in the 1819 preface and called her narration exemplary. The most famous informant, and the clearest case of the peasant-storyteller image being applied to a person who did not quite fit it.

**Bökendorf [A, coordinate corrected].** The Haxthausens hosted the brothers at Schloss Bökerhof; the Bökendorfer Romantikerkreis ran 1810-1834, drawing the Haxthausen brothers, Annette and Jenny von Droste-Hülshoff, Brentano, and both Grimms. The pool gave 51.6883, 9.1136; Bökendorf is at **51.7715, 9.2184** (de.wikipedia).

**Volume one [A].** *Kinder- und Hausmärchen* vol. 1, 86 tales, Berlin, Realschulbuchhandlung, 20 December 1812, dedicated to Bettina von Arnim.

**Vienna and Paris [A, sequence corrected].** Jacob attended the Congress of Vienna as secretary of the Hessian legation, September 1814 to June 1815, then went to Paris on the restitution mission for material removed under Napoleon.

> **Contradiction.** The chronology lens compresses both into 1814; the geography lens correctly gives Vienna 1814-1815 and Paris 1815. The journey splits them.

**Volume two [A, date corrected].** 70 further tales, Berlin, **1815**.

> **Correction.** The chronology lens claimed publication in 1814 with an 1815 title page. Nothing supports this; Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin and de.wikisource give 1815, as does the geography lens of the same dossier. The inverted detail appears to be invented and is discarded.

## 4. The Kassel library years, 1816-1829

**Appointment [A].** From 1816 both held posts at the Kassel Electoral Library, Jacob as second librarian under Völkel. Modest pay, excellent access. Coordinate 51.3151, 9.4956.

**Deutsche Grammatik [A].** First part 1819, further volumes to 1837; ground of what is now called Grimm's Law (Britannica). Whether Rasmus Rask (1818) or Grimm should be credited with the consonant shift is untouched by every source in the pool. Stated as a gap, not narrated. Honorary doctorates from Marburg, both brothers, the same year (uc.edu); no stop.

**Marriage [A on date, R on venue].** Wilhelm married Dortchen Wild at Kassel, 15 May 1825, aged 39; the pool tags the Martinskirche [R]. Jacob went on living with the couple for the rest of his life.

**Children [A].** Jacob b. 3 April 1826, d. 15 December 1826; Herman Friedrich b. 6 January 1828; Rudolf Georg b. 31 March 1830; Auguste b. 21 August 1832, the only one to marry and have children. The pool places Rudolf's birth at Göttingen, where the family had not yet moved; de.wikipedia puts all the births at Kassel. Corrected, and no stop given to individual births.

**Passed over [A].** Völkel died 1828 and Jacob was not given the head librarianship he expected. Immediate cause of the Göttingen move.

## 5. Göttingen and the Seven, 1829-1837

**Die deutsche Heldensage [A, place corrected].** Wilhelm's chief solo work, **Göttingen**, Dieterichsche Buchhandlung, 1829. The pool said "Göttingen/Leipzig"; Leipzig has no part in it (title-page copies in ETH-Bibliothek e-rara, Internet Archive, ZVAB). The expanded second edition of 1867 was Berlin, Dümmler.

**The move. Contradiction, not resolved.** Chronology lens **1829**; geography lens **1830-1837**; Britannica **1830**; de.wikipedia gives Wilhelm's start as **1831**, recording him as Sekretär at Kassel through 1829. Best reading: resignation late 1829, Jacob installed 1830, Wilhelm 1830 or 1831. The journey pins **1830** and carries the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

**Deutsche Mythologie [A].** Göttingen, 1835. The quotes attached to it in the journey come from the preface to the **second** edition (1844) in Stallybrass's translation of 1880-88, and `quote_source` says so. No first-edition preface text was reachable.

**The Göttingen Seven [A].** 1837: both brothers joined five colleagues protesting Ernst August of Hanover's abrogation of the 1833 constitution. All seven dismissed; Jacob, with two others, ordered out of the kingdom within days. The pool gives November 1837 in one lens and bare 1837 elsewhere; the corpus's Gauss file dates the dismissal December 1837. Days are not attested and are defaulted.

## 6. Kassel again, 1837-1840

**The exile [A].** Back to Kassel without posts, on savings and on money raised by admirers. **The dictionary begun [A, place contested].** 1838: the *Deutsches Wörterbuch*.

> **Contradiction.** Chronology lens says Kassel; geography lens gives a **Frankfurt am Main** stop (50.1155, 8.6842) for the same 1838 event. The brothers were living in Kassel and the contract was with Weidmann/Hirzel in Leipzig; Frankfurt has no role. The journey pins Kassel and drops the Frankfurt entry, which returns only for the Paulskirche in 1848.

## 7. Berlin, 1840-1863

**The invitation [A].** Friedrich Wilhelm IV called both to Berlin in 1840: professorships, the right to lecture without holding chairs, election to the Prussian Academy of Sciences, residence and posts from 1841.

**Andersen [A, via the corpus].** Hans Christian Andersen called on Jacob Grimm at Berlin on 26 July 1844 and Grimm had never heard of him. Taken from `hans_christian_andersen.journey.json`, which itself flags that secondary sources put the meeting in 1845-46. Carried into `date_confidence`, not resolved. Coordinate inherited byte-identical: 52.517, 13.3888.

**The Paulskirche [A].** Jacob sat as an elected member of the Frankfurt National Assembly in 1848 and withdrew as its prospects collapsed. Month and day not in the pool; defaulted.

**The dictionary in print [A].** Volume one, Leipzig, 1854. **Coordinate note:** no Leipzig coordinate exists anywhere in the pool. The journey uses the standard centroid 51.3397, 12.3731, supplied from outside the dossier.

**The seventh edition [A].** 1857, the last in the brothers' lifetime: 200 numbered tales plus 10 children's legends. The pool gives the place as "Göttingen/Berlin", which is not a place; the brothers were in Berlin and the journey pins Berlin. The same year Jacob was elected Foreign Honorary Member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences; no stop.

**Deaths [A].** Wilhelm, Berlin, of an infection, 16 December 1859, aged 73. Jacob, Berlin, 20 September 1863, aged 78, the dictionary only partway through F. The account that he was at work on *Frucht* is [R]: the pool itself says "reportedly".

**The graves [A, coordinate corrected, one detail downgraded].** Both buried in the Alter St.-Matthäus-Kirchhof, Berlin-Schöneberg, as honorary gravesites.

> **Two problems.** (1) Afterlife lens: 52.4896, 13.3617. Geography lens: 52.4906, 13.3653. de.wikipedia: **52.4901, 13.3678**, which the journey uses. (2) "Four columns in a row" including sons Rudolf and Herman is unsupported: de.wikipedia's burial list records Herman under "weitere Gräber von Interesse" and does not list Rudolf at all. Downgraded to [R], not asserted.

## 8. Afterlife

- **Edgar Taylor, *German Popular Stories*, London 1823-1826, illustrated by George Cruikshank [A].** First English translation, first fully illustrated edition anywhere; Taylor Anglicized names, softened violence, cut religious references. The tales reach English already edited a second time.
- **The Hanau monument [A].** Bronze double statue by Syrius Eberle, unveiled 1896 on the Neustädter Marktplatz.
- **The Märchenstraße sites [R throughout].** Sababurg as Dornröschen's castle, Trendelburg's tower as Rapunzel's, Alsfeld's red hood as Rotkäppchen's: tourism tradition, none attested in Grimm source scholarship. The pool's coordinates were wrong for both Reinhardswald sites by 11 to 14 km and in the same direction, pulled roughly 0.1 degrees south toward Kassel, which suggests one systematic fault rather than two slips. Corrected: Sababurg **51.5436, 9.5375**; Trendelburg **51.574, 9.419**. Only Sababurg gets a stop.
- **The Deutsche Märchenstraße [R on the date].** Roughly 600 km, Hanau to Bremen. The pool gives 1975 "per tourism-board convention", explicitly unconfirmed. Used only as a peg for the undated tradition stop.
- **The Third Reich and the 1946 ban [R].** Nazi propaganda read several tales, Rotkäppchen among them, as racial allegory, and "The Jew Among the Thorns" figured in the appropriation. British occupation authorities are said to have banned publication in their zone in 1946; the ban did not hold. Both claims rest in the pool on blogs, a TES article, and general academic discussion. No primary occupation document was reachable. Carried as contested.
- **Brüder Grimm-Haus, Steinau, opened 1998 [A].** In the 1562 Amtshaus; one room devoted to Ludwig Emil Grimm, the brother who painted the family.
- **GRIMMWELT Kassel, opened September 2015 [A, coordinate corrected].** Organized around 25 headwords from the *Wörterbuch*; supersedes the Brüder Grimm-Museum at the Palais Bellevue. The pool holds two values (51.3016, 9.4794 and 51.3086, 9.4864), neither matching Weinbergstraße 21; corrected to **51.3094, 9.4893**.
- **UNESCO Memory of the World, 2005 [A].** Manuscripts and the 1812/1815 first edition inscribed; UNESCO calls it, next to the Luther Bible, the best-known work of German cultural history. The Ölenberg manuscript is at the Fondation Bodmer. Tales now in 160-plus languages.
- **The dictionary finished, 1961 [A].** 33 volumes, roughly 320,000 entries, 123 years after it began.

## Contradictions carried, not resolved

1. Göttingen move: 1829 vs 1830 vs 1831; two lenses of this dossier disagree with each other, and Britannica disagrees with de.wikipedia on Wilhelm. Journey pins 1830 and flags it.
2. Vol. 2 of the tales: 1814 vs 1815. The 1814-with-1815-title-page claim is unsupported and looks fabricated. 1815 used.
3. Vienna and Paris: 1814 vs 1814-1815 plus 1815. Split into two stops.
4. The dictionary's founding place: Kassel vs Frankfurt. Kassel used, Frankfurt pin dropped.
5. Grimmwelt coordinate: two pool values, neither matching Weinbergstraße 21.
6. St.-Matthäus-Kirchhof coordinate: two pool values, neither matching de.wikipedia.
7. The Andersen meeting: 1844 vs 1845-46. The corpus's Andersen file already flags this. Carried.

The corrections at Steinau, Napoleonshöhe, Bökendorf, Sababurg, and the 1809/1813 auditor confusion are settled above against external sources rather than carried.

## Honest gaps

- **No German primary correspondence was reachable.** The Grimm-Savigny and Grimm-Arnim letters and the brothers' diaries are cited in the pool only at second hand. Every claim resting on them is [A] on the secondary source, not on the document.
- **The Rask question** (priority for the Germanic consonant shift) is untouched by every source in the pool. Not narrated.
- **Wilhelm's editorial hand.** The pool records that he drove the revisions and that the tales grew more literary, more Christian, and less sexually frank, but gives no per-edition detail. The journey states the direction of travel and no more.
- **Ludwig Emil Grimm**, the painter brother, appears only through the Steinau museum entry. He merits his own research and does not get it here.
- **Exact days** are absent for most events after 1830; the journey defaults unknown month or day to 01 and says so in `date_confidence`.
