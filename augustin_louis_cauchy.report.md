# Augustin-Louis Cauchy (1789-1857), research report

*Slug: `augustin_louis_cauchy`. Compiled 2026-08-23 from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife). Journey register: "national mythology: the canon is true". Calendar: gregorian.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the verifying pass could not raise above second hand. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and are **not** adjudicated.

## 1. Paris and Arcueil, 1789-1804

- Born **21 August 1789**, rue Saint-Honoré, Paris, five weeks after the Bastille; baptized the same day at **Saint-Roch**. **[A: fr.wikipedia; MacTutor; en.wikipedia]** The house number is not recorded anywhere reached; the pin is the church.
- Father **Louis-François Cauchy**, police official of the old regime, moved the household to **Arcueil** to escape the Terror and the bread shortage. Move **[A: MacTutor]**; **the year is contested.** MacTutor and en.wikipedia render it loosely as "at age four", i.e. c. 1793; fr.wikipedia ties it to the aftermath of Thiroux de Crosne's execution (28 April 1794). Safest: **c. 1793-94**. Not resolved.
- The bread quote **[A: MacTutor, citing Belhoste (1991)]**. **Attribution correction:** MacTutor introduces it as Cauchy's own letter, not one "from the Cauchy family" as an earlier draft of the pool had it. He was about five. Whether a five-year-old wrote it or MacTutor compresses a family letter is **left open**; the file quotes it as MacTutor gives it and says so.
- **École Centrale du Panthéon**, 1802-1804: classics, Latin verse, prizes. **[A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia]**
- **Lagrange** advising the father to withhold mathematics until the boy had languages, and **Laplace** as a household visitor: **[R]**, Belhoste-derived tradition via the dossier, not independently sourced. Both men are queued in the atlas with no file to check against.

## 2. Polytechnique and Ponts et Chaussées, 1805-1810

- **1805**: examined by **Biot**, placed **second of roughly 293**, admitted. **[A: en.wikipedia; MacTutor]** Teachers **Lacroix, de Prony, Hachette**, with **Ampère** as analysis tutor **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **1807-1810**: **École des Ponts et Chaussées**, with trainee work on the **Ourcq Canal** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Girard** as named supervisor is the dossier's **[R]**. Completion "with highest honors" rests **only on en.wikipedia's wording**, which the pool itself flags as reported; no examination record reached, so **[R]** in the file.

## 3. Cherbourg, 1810-1812

- Posted **aspirant-ingénieur** to Napoleon's harbour works, **March 1810** **[A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia]**. The four-book luggage list (Laplace, Lagrange, Virgil, the *Imitation of Christ*) is Belhoste via the dossier **[R]**, though MacTutor carries the two mathematical volumes.
- Two 1810 letters **[A: MacTutor, citing Belhoste]**: the four o'clock risings, and the charge of pride over his devotion.
- **1811** rigidity theorem for convex polyhedra; **1812** further papers, encouraged by **Legendre** and **Malus** **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **September 1812**: left Cherbourg for Paris **ill** **[A]**. Physical illness or depression is **explicitly unresolved**; the file says so rather than choosing. **November 1812**: memoir on symmetric functions submitted, printed 1815 **[A]**.

## 4. The years of rejection, 1813-1817

- Unpaid sick leave c. 1813-1815 and repeated failure to get a post **[R: en.wikipedia, MacTutor corroborating the pattern only]**.
- **Zero votes out of fifty-three** in an 1814 Institute ballot: **[R]**, reaching the pool through Belhoste as digested in the dossier, never checked against a register. The Bureau des Longitudes place going to **Legendre** and the geometry seat to **Poinsot** is attested.
- **1814** memoir on definite integrals **[A]**. **1815**: mechanics chair lost to **Binet**, assistant professorship of analysis and the second-year course gained, full professor after a one-year contract **[A]**.
- **1816**: **Grand Prix** for the memoir on wave propagation **[A]**; **March 1816** appointed to the Académie into a seat opened by the Restoration purge of **Carnot** and **Monge** **[A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia]**. That contemporaries held the entry against him is biographical tradition; that his mathematics already met Academy standard is the pool's own judgement.
- **1817**: substituted for **Biot** at the **Collège de France** during the Shetland expedition **[A: MacTutor]**.

## 5. Marriage, the Cours d'analyse, the referee's table, 1818-1829

- **4 April 1818**: married **Aloïse de Bure** at **Saint-Sulpice** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; daughters **1819** and **1823** **[A]**.
- Residence at **7 rue Serpente** is **[R]** (Belhoste via the dossier); that **De Bure frères** printed the 1829 *Leçons* is **[A: MacTutor]**. The file uses that pin only for the publishing stop, never as a residence scene.
- **Sceaux summers from c. 1822** are **[R]** (Belhoste), corroborated only indirectly by fr.wikipedia's account of the death "dans la maison familiale de sa femme à Sceaux".
- **1821** *Cours d'analyse*; **1823** *Résumé des leçons sur le calcul infinitésimal* **[A]**.
- **1825** integral theorem in developed form; **1826** calculus of residues **[A: en.wikipedia; MacTutor]**. The **1826 start of the self-published *Exercices de mathématiques*** is **[R]**, resting on the Belhoste-derived standard account, not re-checked bibliographically.
- **1826**: **Abel's** memoir referred to Cauchy and Legendre; no report; Abel dead 1829 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The "buried out of jealousy" reading is **later legend, not fact**, labelled as such in the campa. Abel's remark **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **Poncelet**: the long quotation is **[A: MacTutor]**; the framing (harsh refereeing after Cauchy's 1820 criticism) is the dossier's **[R]**. **Poncelet has no atlas file and is not in QUEUE.md.**
- The **1824 "missionary in the Academy"** scene is **[R]** and **is not staged as a stop**: MacTutor attributes the jibe only to "a journalist", with no name, publication or date. Recorded here instead: "...it is certain a curious thing to see an academician who seemed to fulfil the respectable functions of a missionary preaching to the heathens."
- **1829** *Leçons sur le calcul différentiel*, with one of the first formal definitions of a complex function of a complex variable **[A]**. **Dirichlet's 1829 correction** of an earlier Cauchy attempt on Fourier-series convergence is **[R]**, summarized through Belhoste with no primary paper re-fetched.
- **25 May and 1 June 1829**: **Galois's** two memoirs, **Cauchy and Poinsot** as referees, manuscripts lost; Taton's 1971 reconstruction **[R, second hand]**. Inherited from `evariste_galois.journey.json`.

## 6. Exile, 1830-1838

- **September 1830**: left rather than swear to Louis-Philippe; **Fribourg**, and a Catholic *Académie Helvétique* that never opened **[A: fr.wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- **1831-1833 Turin**: chair of theoretical physics created for him, teaching from 1832, papers to the Turin Academy **[A]**. **Menabrea's** recollection of the lectures **[A: MacTutor]**. **1832**: Foreign Honorary Member, American Academy of Arts and Sciences **[A]**.
- **July/August 1833**: to **Prague** as tutor to **Henri d'Artois, Duke of Bordeaux** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The **Damas letter of 22 June 1833** is **[R]**.
- **The barony.** The pool's chronology mis-tagged and mis-dated this. It is **[A]**: en.wikipedia states flatly that Cauchy was named a baron and set great store by it; fr.wikipedia names the grantor, "Charles X lui accorde le titre de baron". **Charles X died 6 November 1836**, so the grant predates that and fr.wikipedia's chaining of it to the 1838 return cannot stand. Place: the exiled court generally, not Prague. **Flagged, not resolved.**
- **1834**: **Bolzano** meeting at Bolzano's request **[A, via the dossier's reading of the modern literature]**. The **plagiarism controversy** is **[R]**, a disputed later interpretation; the campa gives the publication dates (1817, 1821) and stops.
- **1834**: wife and daughters join him **[A]**. Court itinerary **Teplice 1835**, **Budweis, Kirchberg, Görlitz 1836** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The geography lens **omits Kirchberg entirely**; it gets no stop for want of a coordinate and is named in the Görlitz campa.
- The **"sewers of Paris"** line is **[R]**, a later comic anecdote garbling a career that began in elite state engineering. Kept as legend, labelled as legend.
- **September 1838**: tutorship ends at the Duke's eighteenth birthday **[A]**.

## 7. Return without a chair, 1838-1848

- Académie seat recovered, every salaried and teaching post shut for refusing the oath **[A]**.
- **November 1839**: elected to the **Bureau des Longitudes**, backed by Biot and Arago, opposed by Poisson; **election not validated**, no salary; replaced by **Poinsot** end of 1843 **[A: en.wikipedia; MacTutor]**. The named backers reach the pool through a Bureau des Longitudes historical project **not opened directly**.
- **1840 onward**: four volumes of the *Exercices d'analyse et de physique mathématique* **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **1 February 1843**: "La Chandeleur", *Bulletin de l'Institut Catholique* **[A: Belhoste ch. 13 via Wikiquote]**.
- **1843**: **Lacroix's** Collège de France chair contested against **Liouville** and **Libri**, and won by Libri **[A]**; Liouville's letter the next day **[A: MacTutor]**.

## 8. After the oath, 1848-1857

- **1848** oath abolished; **1 March 1849** reinstated professor of mathematical astronomy, Faculté des sciences de Paris **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **1850**: chair lost to **Liouville** **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **1850**: *Considérations sur les ordres religieux*, p. 26 **[A: Wikiquote, from the Gonzalo translation, 2008, p. 301]**. The companion sentence on the same page (Corneille, Racine, Bossuet, Fénelon) survives on Wikiquote only in a rendering with **visible OCR damage** ("out science", "ustre") and is **not used**.
- **1852 exemption.** **Correction:** the pool overstated this as a personal decree. en.wikipedia verbatim: "This time a cabinet minister was able to convince the Emperor to exempt Cauchy from the oath." Intercession, not initiative. The file follows the corrected version. **1853**: American Philosophical Society **[A]**.
- **4 April 1856**: co-founder of the **Œuvre des Écoles d'Orient** with Lenormant, Falloux, Montalembert, Ravignan **[A, via the modern Œuvre d'Orient's own history and a BnF listing, both reached through the dossier]**. The founding venue is not recorded; a generic central Paris pin is used.
- Sceaux charities and the mayor **Edmond Guyon's** tribute **[A: sceaux-lagazette.fr]**.
- **23 May 1857, about 4 a.m.**, death at Sceaux of a bronchial condition. **Source pairing flagged**: sceaux-lagazette.fr gives the death and the house but **neither the hour nor the cause**, both of which come from en.wikipedia and MacTutor alone. The deathbed account is **his daughter's letter**, printed in **Valson (1868)** and reproduced by MacTutor: later family testimony, not an independent record.
- Buried in the **cimetière de Sceaux** **[A]**. **The plot is unknown** to everything reached.

## 9. Contradictions flagged and not resolved

1. **Arcueil**: 1793 (MacTutor, en.wikipedia) vs 1794 (fr.wikipedia).

3. **The *Œuvres complètes***: MacTutor **27 vols, 1882-1970**; en.wikipedia **27 vols, 1882-1974**; fr.wikipedia **28 vols, 1882-1974**. The file takes 1974 (two sources against one) and carries "twenty-seven by one count and twenty-eight by another" in the prose.
4. **Paper count**: MacTutor's **789** vs fr.wikipedia's "près de 800".
5. **The 1812 illness**: no diagnosis is possible.
6. **Cauchy and Bolzano**: independent discovery or borrowing, unresolved, with the dates running against the charge.
7. **The bread quote's speaker**: MacTutor's phrasing makes it Cauchy's own, at about age five.
8. **The two lenses disagreed with each other on the grave**: geography gave 48.7799, 2.2969; afterlife gave 48.7793, 2.2893. **Both are wrong.** OSM verifies the cemetery at **48.7798, 2.2838**.

## 11. Interlocks used, and interlocks refused

**Named in a campa** (a real relation of Cauchy's own, including one-directional ones):

- **`evariste_galois`**, Cauchy was named referee on the two 1829 memoirs, and Galois was extending the theory of substitutions Cauchy began in the 1812 memoir on symmetric functions. Named at two stops.
- **`joseph_fourier`**, Cauchy attempted the convergence of Fourier's trigonometric series and was corrected by Dirichlet in 1829. Cauchy working on Fourier's series is a genuine one-directional engagement. Named once; the Dirichlet correction is **[R]** and the confidence field says so.

**Found and deliberately not named** (shared geography or shared acquaintance only, which the map already shows the viewer):

- `charles_babbage`: Arcueil (the Laplace/Berthollet circle, years after the Cauchys lived there) and Turin (Babbage's visit usually dated 1840, seven years after Cauchy left), plus **Menabrea** as a shared acquaintance. No Cauchy-Babbage contact of any kind. Refused; Turin pin inherited.
- `erik_satie`: twenty-seven years at 22 rue Cauchy, Arcueil. The Satie file does not say the street is named for this Cauchy and the verifying pass could not confirm it. Toponymic echo. Refused.
- `victor_hugo`: married at Saint-Sulpice in 1822, four and a half years after Cauchy. Same church, no relation. Refused; pin inherited.
- `alexis_de_tocqueville`: swore the July Monarchy oath and was ashamed; Cauchy refused it and left the country. A real **contrast**, not a relation. The dossier's claim that Cauchy sent Tocqueville an **1843 opuscule on crime prevention** could not be corroborated anywhere, including in Tocqueville's own file. **If the operator can source that opuscule this becomes a true interlock**; the Tocqueville pin is already inherited.
- `louis_xvi`: inspected the Cherbourg breakwater in June 1786; Cauchy worked on Napoleon's continuation from 1810. Shared project across two regimes, and Cauchy's legitimism attaches to the house, not to Louis XVI personally. Refused in prose, **cited in `sources` and `suggested_refs` on the Cherbourg stop**, which is where a one-directional citation belongs.
- `georges_sorel`: born at Cherbourg 1848, twenty-three years in the same corps. No overlap. Refused; pin inherited.
- `rene_hauy`: shared patron in Laplace. Refused.
- `eugene_grasset`, `elisabeth_sonrel`, `paul_berthon`, `simone_de_beauvoir`: all have Sceaux stops, none has any connection to Cauchy. Refused; pin inherited.

**Searched and empty:** **Poncelet** has no file and is not in QUEUE.md, though the referee scene is among the best-documented human episodes in the record. **Montalembert, Ravignan, Falloux**: the only atlas hit is a "Falloux" in `alfred_jarry.journey.json`, unrelated, sharing neither place nor date; checked and dropped. **Lagrange, Laplace, Poisson**: queued, no files, so the Lagrange-advises-the-father story has nothing to check it against.

## 12. Apparatus relations for the operator

Cauchy is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework may travel backward or forward on his file. One relation in the pool runs **forward** and is **kept out of the campas and recorded here rather than silently deleted**:

- **The Cauchy invariants.** The 1815 prize memoir contains a Lagrangian formulation of the 3D incompressible Euler equations with three conserved invariants. Cited occasionally in the nineteenth century, effectively lost for most of the twentieth, resurfaced through **Noether's theorem in the late 1960s**, reattributed to Cauchy by Russian researchers, developed further in **Frisch and Villone (2014), arXiv:1402.4957**, which the verifying pass confirmed directly. **[A]** Writing it into a campa would put a 2014 artifact inside an 1857 file. It sits in `suggested_refs` on the 1816 Grand Prix stop instead, where the memoir itself belongs. **Direction caught: forward.**

Handled the same way, as posthumous commemorations at places Cauchy never went (named inside the burial campa or held in refs, never staged as scenes): the **seventy-two names on the Eiffel Tower** (1889); **Rue Cauchy**, Paris 15e; the **avenue Augustin-Louis-Cauchy** at Nantes; the lunar **crater, rupes and rima Cauchy**; **Bradley and Sandifer's** annotated translation of the *Cours d'analyse* (2009); and the **Catholic Encyclopedia** (1908) calling Cauchy "an admirable type of the true Catholic savant" **[A: newadvent.org/cathen/03457a.htm]**, which the dossier identifies as the seed of the later hagiographic image. There is **no canonization process**: this is reputational, not formal.
