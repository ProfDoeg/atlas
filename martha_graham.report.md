# Martha Graham (1894-1991): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-09 for the Atlas of Journeys. Choreographer; founder of the Martha Graham Dance Company (1926) and of the first codified American modern dance technique.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or unitemized aggregate. Contradictions are flagged and left open; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method.** The pool came in five lenses (chronology, geography, quotations, corpus interlock, afterlife), then ran against an adversarial verification pass producing eighteen corrections. Those corrections rule: where a lens claim and its correction disagree, the correction is what appears below and in the journey.

---

## 1. Allegheny City and the move west, 1894-1913

**Birth, 11 May 1894, Allegheny City, Pennsylvania [A: Wikipedia, "Martha Graham"].** Allegheny was an independent municipality until Pittsburgh annexed it on 9 December 1907 [A: Wikipedia, "Allegheny, Pennsylvania"]; the district is now the North Side.

> *Correction.* Chronology gave 40.4406, -79.9959, which falls in downtown Pittsburgh, south of the rivers, outside the former Allegheny City entirely. The journey uses 40.4530, -80.0084 (Allegheny Center), which the geography lens had already used for the same event.

**The father.** George Graham was "an alienist, a practitioner of an early form of psychiatry" [A: Wikipedia]. Chronology called him "a physician of Irish descent"; the cited source says nothing of Irish descent, the Graham line is Scottish, the household strict Presbyterian. **The descent element is dropped as unsupported and was wrongly tagged [A].**

**The move to California, c. 1908. [R: contradiction, not adjudicated.]** Wikipedia, "Martha Graham": "The Graham family moved to Santa Maria, California, when Martha was fourteen years old." The geography lens asserted Santa Barbara while citing that same article and tagged it [A], which is a self-contradiction. Santa Barbara is the reading of de Mille's *Martha* and of *Blood Memory* and is what most biographies print. The towns are ninety miles apart on one coast. The journey pins Santa Maria (34.953, -120.4357), the town the cited source names, and states the disagreement in the stop.

**Cumnock School of Expression, c. 1913-1916. [R: gap.]** The Cumnock Wikipedia page returned 404; no primary source was reached for address or dates. It appears in the journey as one clause, not as a stop.

## 2. Denishawn, 1911-1923

**Ruth St. Denis at the Mason Opera House, 1911 [A].** The decisive performance, cited by Graham for the rest of her life. *Correction:* geography gave 34.0505, -118.2472; Wikipedia places 127 South Broadway at 34.0535, -118.2464, so the supplied point sat about 340 m south.

**Denishawn, 600 St. Paul Avenue [A: Wikipedia, "Denishawn"], with two corrections.**

> *Corrections.* (a) The school opened in the summer of **1915**, as the Ruth St. Denis School of Dancing and Its Related Arts; **1916 is Graham's arrival**, "during its second summer," not the founding. Both lenses had 1916 as the founding year. (b) 600 St. Paul Avenue sat in the old Crown Hill district near 6th Street, about 34.0535, -118.2665; the supplied 34.0614, -118.2795 lies 1.3 km northwest.

**Westlake Park quarters, c. 1918-1923 [A].** Folded into the St. Paul Avenue stop. **Silent film with Lillian Powell, directed by Hugo Riesenfeld, 1922 [A].** No location reached, so it rides inside the Flute of Krishna stop rather than taking a pin.

## 3. New York and Rochester, 1923-1926

**Greenwich Village Follies, two seasons from 1923 [A]** for the engagement.

> *Correction.* Geography pinned the Greenwich Village Theatre. Wikipedia, "Greenwich Village Follies": by the third year "the revue simply skipped its native venue and opened at the Shubert Theatre." The downtown house held only the 1919 edition. The journey retitles the stop to the Shubert (40.7586, -73.9877) and says in the campa that the name outlived the address.

**Eastman School of Music, 1925 [A]; The Flute of Krishna, 1926 [A].**

> *Corrections.* (a) The two are separated: the appointment under Rouben Mamoulian is 1925, the film was shot in **1926**; chronology had merged them. (b) The Eastman Theatre, 26 Gibbs Street, is at 43.1572, -77.5986; the supplied 43.158, -77.607 sat 700 m west. (c) The theatre opened 4 September 1922, so "new theatre" in 1925 is loose; the campa says it has been open three years.

## 4. The break and the company, 1926-1932

- **First independent concert, 48th Street Theatre, 18 April 1926**, eighteen solos and trios [A]; **recital, Klaw Theatre, 28 November 1926** [A]; **company founded 1926**, later the oldest in America [A].
- **Carnegie Hall Studio 61** as first school and company home, 1926 [A: marthagraham.org history page]. Self-published; recorded as such.
- **Neighborhood Playhouse School of the Theatre, 1928 [A]**, with a correction.

> *Correction.* Geography gave 40.7168, -73.9843, Lower East Side. Wikipedia: founded 1928 by Alice and Irene Lewisohn at **340 East 54th Street**, about 40.7570, -73.9648, Midtown East. The supplied point is the original Neighborhood Playhouse *theatre* on Grand Street, closed by 1927, a year before the school existed.

- **Heretic, 1929 [A]**, year attested, no premiere day reached; **Guggenheim Fellowship in choreography, 1932** [A], reportedly the first to a dancer, with further fellowships **1943** and **1944** [A].

## 5. Bennington, the American subject, Berlin, 1934-1938

**Bennington School of the Dance from 1934 [A: Wikipedia, "Bennington College"]:** founded by Martha Hill, with Graham, Doris Humphrey, Hanya Holm and Charles Weidman teaching. *Correction:* Wikipedia gives the campus at 42.9247, -73.2367 (North Bennington); the supplied 42.8975, -73.202 sat about 4 km off, toward Bennington town.

**Frontier, 1935, set by Isamu Noguchi [A]**, the start of the design collaboration.

**Refusal of the 1936 Berlin Olympics invitation [A]**, full public statement quoted in the journey, attributed via the Wikipedia article citing Hanley (2004), "The Role of Dance in the 1936 Berlin Olympic Games," p. 136.

**The Goebbels letter [R].** Only the fragment "receive complete immunity" appeared in quotation marks in reachable material; the primary letter was not accessible.

**American Document, 1938 [A].** No venue or day reached, so the journey pins the company's New York base and says so in `date_confidence`.

**The White House, 1938 [A].** *Correction:* geography wrote "the first dance company ever to do so." Wikipedia says the Roosevelt family invited Graham to dance there, "making her the first dancer to perform there." Singular. The journey says first dancer and notes that the claim is often enlarged.

**Erick Hawkins joins. [R: contradiction, not adjudicated.]** Wikipedia's "Martha Graham" puts it in 1938 (he appears in American Document); Wikipedia's "Erick Hawkins" says "In 1939, he officially joined her troupe." 1938 is the first appearance, 1939 the formal joining. Chronology tagged this [A]; **that tag is unwarranted.** The journey anchors 1938 and carries the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

## 6. The war years and Appalachian Spring, 1939-1948

- **Mills College, Oakland, summer 1939:** Graham teaches at the relocated Bennington summer school and recruits the twenty-year-old Merce Cunningham [A: merce_cunningham.journey.json, 1939-07-01]. He had already been taught the Graham technique at the Cornish School, Seattle, 1937-1939 [A: same file], so the contraction reached him two years before she did. He joins in New York that October and dances for her six years [A].
- **Appalachian Spring, Coolidge Auditorium, Library of Congress, 30 October 1944 [A]:** score by Aaron Copland, set by Isamu Noguchi, closing a four-day chamber festival for Elizabeth Sprague Coolidge's eightieth birthday. Graham the Bride, Hawkins the Husbandman, May O'Donnell the Pioneer Woman, Cunningham the Revivalist, his last created role in her company [A: merce_cunningham.journey.json, 1944-10-30].
- **Cunningham leaves, January 1945 [A]**, over aesthetic divergence: Graham builds from myth and psychology, Cunningham has stopped believing a dance needs to be about anything.
- **Marriage to Erick Hawkins, July 1948**, after the premiere of Night Journey; her only marriage [A]. **Gap:** no ceremony venue named within New York City.

**Coordinate note.** Geography and afterlife gave the Library of Congress as 38.8887, -77.0047; the interlock lens, from merce_cunningham.journey.json, gave 38.8887, -77.0046. The journey inherits the corpus pin byte-identically.

## 9. Last performance, collapse, return, 1970-1990

**Final performance as a dancer. [R: year disputed, not adjudicated.]** 1968, 1969 and 1970 all appear in the literature.

> *Correction.* Chronology anchored 1969. Wikipedia, verbatim: "Graham herself lists her final performance as her 1970 appearance in *Cortege of Eagles* when she was 76 years old." **The subject's own account is the anchor (1970)**, with 1968 and 1969 as variants in `date_confidence`. The [R] tag stands.

**The collapse, c. 1970-1972 [A]** in substance, via her own account in *Blood Memory* p. 237 (two quotations in the journey). The 1971 date on that stop is an anchor inside the stretch, not an attested date, and says so. **Recovery and reorganization, 1972**; ten new ballets follow [A].

**Honors, all [A].** Presidential Medal of Freedom, Gerald Ford, **1976**; Kennedy Center Honor **1979**; Samuel H. Scripps / American Dance Festival Award, presented by Betty Ford, **1981**; Legion of Honour **1984**; National Medal of Arts **1985**; National Museum of Dance Hall of Fame **1987**; TIME "Dancer of the Century" **1998**; National Women's Hall of Fame **2015**.

> *Correction.* Geography called the 1979 Kennedy Center Honor "one of the inaugural generation." The first class was **1978** (Anderson, Astaire, Balanchine, Rodgers, Rubinstein); Graham was in the **second**, 1979, with Copland, Ella Fitzgerald, Henry Fonda and Tennessee Williams. Year right, framing wrong.

**Gap:** no ceremony venue reached for the 1984 Legion of Honour, only the city of Paris.

**Maple Leaf Rag, 1990**, her last completed ballet [A]. **The lifetime total of 181 works [R]:** an aggregate as reported, not itemized work by work anywhere reached.

## 10. Death and afterlife, 1991-2025

- **Death, New York, 1 April 1991**, pneumonia, age 96 [A]. **Gap:** no hospital, residence or neighbourhood named in reachable sources.
- **Ashes scattered over the Sangre de Cristo Mountains, northern New Mexico [A]** for the scattering itself. No coordinates and no date are published; the range runs roughly Santa Fe to Taos.

> *Correction.* Afterlife attached an interpretive clause calling the range "a landscape she had used as source material." Nothing reached supports it, and the geography lens says the opposite of the same event, "a landscape she never choreographed directly." **The causal clause is dropped**, and the journey names the contradiction rather than picking a side.

- ***Blood Memory: An Autobiography***, finished shortly before her death, published autumn 1991 [A].
- **The Protas litigation.** Her will named Ron Protas heir to the choreography rights; the board dismissed him in 2000; he sued [A: Wikipedia, "Ron Protas"]. Judge Miriam Goldman Cedarbaum (S.D.N.Y., 2002) held that Graham did not personally own most of the work, having made it as an employee of the company bearing her name [A].

> *Two corrections, both to errors originating in Wikipedia itself.*
> **(a) Counts conflated.** The holding concerned roughly seventy dances in suit, not the 181 lifetime compositions; many of the 181 are lost, unfixed, or already public domain and were never adjudicated. Wikipedia's "all but two of the choreographic works" means two **of the works in suit**; the journey says "all but two of the dances at issue."
> **(b) "Two Supreme Court cases" is false.** The company article claims the company won "two supreme court cases with Ron Protas." The CourtListener docket shows S.D.N.Y. rulings of 7 Aug 2001, 23 Aug 2002 and 23 Jun 2005, and Second Circuit rulings of 2 Jul 2002, 18 Aug 2004 (380 F.3d 624) and 11 Jul 2006. **The Supreme Court never decided the merits.** The journey says district court and Second Circuit.

- **Janet Eilber appointed artistic director, 2005 [A].** Not pinned; the litigation stop carries the recovery.
- **National Film Registry, 2013**: Heretic, Lamentation, Frontier, Appalachian Spring [R], carried at the pool's own tag, not independently confirmed. Not in the journey.
- **Google doodle, 11 May 2011 [A]; NYPL Jerome Robbins Dance Division acquires the archive, announced 11 May 2020 [A].** Both fall on her birthday; the journey pairs them in one stop.
- **Headquarters move to the Paramount Building announced 2025; PBS centennial documentary 2026 [A].** Not in the journey: a forward-looking corporate announcement, and the archive is the better closing pin.

## Quotations: what was fixed

Eleven quotations are carried into the journey. Five of the pool's thirteen had wrong attributions, all corrected:

1. **"...gave me a landscape to move in"** (on Louis Horst). Wording verified; the pool guessed *Blood Memory*. Raw wikitext gives `{{sfn|Freedman|1998|p.134}}`: **Russell Freedman, *Martha Graham: A Dancer's Life* (1998), p. 134**.
2. **"...they strike like a blow"** (John Martin). Wording verified; the pool called it a New York Times review. Footnote 61 reads "Armitage, p. 9": **Merle Armitage, ed., *Martha Graham* (1937)**. Martin was the Times critic, but no Times piece was located.
3. **"...art is eternal, for it reveals the inner landscape."** Verified on Wikiquote, which files it as a *This I Believe* essay; that archive publishes Graham's essay as **"An Athlete of God,"** which does not contain the sentence. Cited as **"I Am a Dancer" (1952)**, reprinted in *The Routledge Dance Studies Reader* (1998).
4. **"To me, the only sin is mediocrity."** Real wording, wrong source: not a 1952 essay but the **New York Times, 31 March 1985**.
5. **"To me, the body says what words cannot. ... Dance is the hidden language of the soul, of the body."** 1985 Times attribution right, but **the sentences are not contiguous**; a passage about dance as the first art sits between them. The journey inserts an ellipsis and says so.

Also corrected in framing: the "vitality, a life force" passage is **Graham's counsel to Agnes de Mille**, printed in de Mille's *Martha* (1991) p. 264, not a description of Graham, which is how it circulates.
