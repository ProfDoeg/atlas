# Guy Debord (1931-1994): research report

*Compiled for `guy_debord.journey.json`. **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or secondary report not run down to a primary source. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Coordinate corrections are recorded in full: several move a pin by kilometres.*

## 1. Childhood and the wartime displacements (1931-1945)

**Birth.** Guy-Ernest Debord born in Paris, 28 December 1931, to Martial Debord, pharmacist, and Paulette Rossi **[A: Wikipedia FR/EN]**, in the 20th arrondissement, where his maternal grandfather kept a luxury shoe business **[A: Wikipedia FR]**. No street address anywhere reachable; the pin is Place Gambetta, a placeholder **[gap]**. After the father's early death the mother sends the boy for periods to a grandmother in Italy **[A]**, with no dates, durations or place name **[gap]**, so that episode is narrated in the birth campa rather than pinned.

**Nice, autumn 1939** **[A: Wikipedia FR]**; residence not documented. *Coordinate correction:* Place Massena is 43.6973 / 7.2701; the working figure 43.697 / 7.2761 fell about 480 m east, in the Paillon gardens. Then **Fontainebleau, College Carnot, summer 1941** and **Pau, Lycee Louis-Barthou, summer 1942** **[A: Wikipedia EN and FR]**.

> **Contradiction retracted.** An earlier pass recorded a 1941/1942 conflict over the Pau move. The French gives Fontainebleau summer 1941 *and* Pau summer 1942, the same sequence as the English. No conflict exists; the caveat is removed from both stops, and the removal is recorded rather than made silently.

**Cannes, March 1945 to June 1951** **[A: Wikipedia EN and FR]**: Lycee Carnot to the baccalaureat, and the years in which the interest in cinema forms **[A]**. One summary also attributes "vandalism as provocation" to these years; no source consulted supports it, so the claim is **[R]** at best and is not carried in the journey.

## 2. Lettrism and the Lettrist International (1951-1955)

**Contact with Isou, April 1951** **[A: Wikipedia FR]**, at the Cannes festival, where Isou's *Traite de bave et d'eternite* is shown.

> **Britannica set aside for this date only.** It dates the joining to 1950 and gives his age as "about eighteen," which cannot stand with the baccalaureat of June 1951 and the move to Paris after it **[A: Wikipedia FR]**. He was nineteen; the conflict is stated in the stop's `date_confidence`.

**Law studies abandoned, Paris, 1951** **[A: Wikipedia]**. No month; October is the ordinary start of the academic year, flagged **[R for the month]**. **Chez Moineau, rue du Four, 1952-1957** **[A: Wikipedia FR]**, headquarters of the Lettrist International.

**Hurlements en faveur de Sade, 1952.** Film and year **[A]**. Screening history upgraded from **[R]** to **[A]**: premiere 30 June 1952, cine-club d'avant-garde of the Musee de l'Homme, cut off by the organisers in the first minutes; first complete screening 13 October 1952, Salle des Societes savantes **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Hurlements en faveur de Sade"]**.

> **Two corrections.** (a) An earlier draft had Michele Bernstein contributing; the French article names the five voices as Wolman, Debord, Serge Berna, Barbara Rosenthal and Isou, and the clause is removed. (b) The description was inverted: the **white** screens carry the voices, the **black** are silent, and the film ends in 24 minutes of silent black.

**The Lettrist International, 1952** **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia EN]**, led by Debord at Gil Wolman's urging. Month not established **[gap]**; dated 1952-12-01, placeholder flagged. The campa's summary of the group's programme comes from its own later texts and is **[R]** for the winter of 1952. **Marriage to Michele Bernstein, 1954** **[R: general consensus, no date confirmed]**, not pinned; she is named only inside the 1972 marriage campa. **"Ne travaillez jamais," rue de Seine, 1953** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**, no month or day **[gap]**.

**"Introduction a une critique de la geographie urbaine," Les Levres nues, 1955** **[A: Wikipedia, "Derive"]**. Month not established; September is a declared placeholder. The link to Ivan Chtcheglov's 1953 "Formulaire pour un urbanisme nouveau" and its attack on **Le Corbusier** and the Plan Voisin is **[R: interlock lens]**, though Chtcheglov's naming of Le Corbusier is standard in the literature.

## 3. The two methods (1956)

**"Mode d'emploi du detournement," Les Levres nues no. 8, May 1956** **[A: Wikipedia, "Detournement"]**, with Wolman. Quotation used: "Detournement by simple reversal is always the most direct and the least effective" **[A: Knabb, bopsecrets.org]**.

> **Misattribution caught.** "Plagiarism is necessary, progress depends on it" appears verbatim in Knabb's translation but **not as Debord and Wolman's own formulation**: they introduce it as a maxim already in circulation and poorly understood. The words are Lautreamont's, *Poesies* II, and bopsecrets' endnote flags it as the passage Debord plagiarised again in thesis 207. It is used in no `quote` field.

**"Theorie de la derive," Les Levres nues no. 9, November 1956** **[A: Wikipedia, "Derive," quoting Debord]**. An earlier draft called this the first fully public statement of the derive; the 1955 "Introduction" preceded it in the same journal, and `date_confidence` says so, calling the 1956 text the fullest early statement instead **[overclaim corrected]**.

**Walkie-talkie derives, Amsterdam and Strasbourg** **[R: Wikipedia, "Derive," attributing the account to Henri Lefebvre; dates uncertain]**. The literature gives only "the 1950s," so Amsterdam is pinned at 1956-01-01 as a declared placeholder: the weakest stop in the file by evidence.

## 4. The Situationist International (1957-1966)

**Founding, Cosio d'Arroscia, 27-28 July 1957** **[A: Wikipedia EN, "Situationist International"; FR, "Guy Debord"]**. Merger of the Lettrist International, the Imaginist Bauhaus and the London Psychogeographical Association; Debord and Asger Jorn lead **[A]**. *Coordinate contradiction, left standing:* geography gives 44.0937 / 7.8207, the afterlife lens 44.0964 / 7.8181, 400 m apart and both inside the village. The first is used; no source names a house.

**Alba, First World Congress of Free Artists, 2-8 September 1956: Debord did not attend.** The Lettrist International was represented by Gil J Wolman **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Bauhaus imaginiste"]**, so the stop an earlier draft pinned here is removed.

**Conferences** **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Internationale situationniste," unless noted]**. 1st Paris 25-26 Jan 1958 · 3rd Munich 17-20 Apr 1959 · 4th London 24-28 Sep 1960, where the disagreement over the revolutionary proletariat surfaces **[A: also Wikipedia EN]** · 5th Gothenburg 28-30 Aug 1961 · 6th Antwerp 12-16 Nov 1962 · 7th Paris 5-11 Jul 1966, adopting the Minimum Definition of Revolutionary Organizations · 8th and last, Venice 16 Sep to 1 Oct 1969 **[A: Wikipedia EN]**.

Personal attendance is not stated in every case; Debord is de facto leader from 1960 **[A: Wikipedia EN]**, so it is inference, and two stops say so. The **second conference** is missing from the material and is not reconstructed. **Venue addresses** for both Paris conferences are absent, so 48.8534 / 2.3488 is a flagged Latin Quarter reference. **Brussels, 1958** **[R: city inferred, not named]** was dropped rather than pinned.

**Manifesto of the 121, September 1960** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**. **Andre Breton** is a fellow signatory **[A]**; the campa's characterisation of the signatory milieu is **[R]**.

**Strasbourg, November 1966** **[A: Wikipedia EN, "On the Poverty of Student Life"]**: five situationist-influenced students elected to the union, ten thousand copies of Khayati's pamphlet printed at its expense, outcry, the union closed by court order. *Coordinate correction:* the Palais universitaire is 48.5848 / 7.7624 **[A: Wikipedia FR]**; the working figure 48.582 / 7.7677 lay 500 m southeast, off the building. The campa's line on the pamphlet's contents is paraphrase **[R]**; nothing from it is quoted, no line having been verified verbatim.

## 5. La Societe du Spectacle and May 1968

**Publication, 1967, Buchet-Chastel, Paris** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**, 221 numbered theses. Theses 1, 4 and 34 were verified against the Black and Red translation on marxists.org **[A]**; thesis 4 is used in the journey: "The spectacle is not a collection of images, but a social relation among people, mediated by images." The November dating appears in one summary only **[A year, R month]**.

**Marx and Bakunin.** That thesis 1 detourns the opening of **Karl Marx**'s *Capital*, and that the book is a Marxist analysis of commodity society, are **[A]** on the face of the texts. The First International's Marx/Bakunin quarrel inside the argument, and the descent of the SI's council communism from **Mikhail Bakunin**, are **[R: interlock lens]**.

**First English translation, 1970** **[A]**: **Fredy Perlman** (with John Supak) for **Black & Red, Detroit**, revised 1977, unauthorised. Two corrections: the place was given as "United Kingdom/United States" and is Detroit only, the UK edition (Rebel Press, Knabb) being 2004; and the edition was called anonymous, though Perlman is named. The standard academic English text is Nicholson-Smith, Zone Books, **1994**; the English Wikipedia article's 1995 is erroneous **[flagged; 1994 followed]**. Detroit is not pinned, Debord never having gone there.

**Nanterre, early February 1968** **[A: Wikipedia FR]**, formation of the Enrages. *Coordinate correction:* the university is 48.9039 / 2.2138; the working figure 48.8918 fell about 1.35 km south, near Nanterre-Prefecture. The campa's remark that the campus was new, on cleared ground and disliked by its students is **[R]**, standard in accounts of 1968.

**The Sorbonne, 14-17 May 1968** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**: occupation, situationists on the Occupation Committee, their slogans and detourned phrases across Paris **[A]**. *Coordinate contradiction, left standing:* geography gives 48.848 / 2.3437, the afterlife lens 48.8489 / 2.3436, 100 m apart and both inside the building. The first is used.

**CMDO, Centre Censier, May to 15 June 1968** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**: tracts for workers' councils, dissolution 15 June; founding day not given **[gap]**, so 17 May is a declared placeholder. **Internationale Situationniste no. 12, 1969** **[A]**, the last number, is folded into the Venice campa.

## 6. Dissolution, retreat, nomad years (1969-1983)

**Dissolution, 1972** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**, with only Debord and Gianfranco Sanguinetti left; month not established **[gap]**. That *La Veritable Scission dans l'Internationale* (1972) carries the reasoning is **[R]**, no source in the pool naming the book. **Marriage to Alice Becker-Ho, 2 August 1972** **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Alice Becker-Ho"]**, upgraded from **[R]** with an exact date; they met in 1963 through the Socialisme ou Barbarie milieu **[A]**.

**Champot, from 1972** **[A: Wikipedia EN and FR]**: the hamlet Champot Bas, the property the "mas le Minour," commune of Bellevue-la-Montagne, Haute-Loire.

> **The largest error in the pool, resolved.** Geography gave 45.1728 / 3.7797 and the afterlife lens 45.1975 / 3.7567 for the *same house*, about 3 km apart and both southwest of the commune. Bellevue-la-Montagne is 45.2217 / 3.8208 **[A: Wikipedia FR]**, which all four Champot stops now carry, as a commune-level pin **[gap: le Minour itself is not located]**.

The Clausewitz and seventeenth-century reading in that campa is **[R]**; the war game Debord designed is not mentioned, being absent from the pool. **The film La Societe du Spectacle, 1973** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**; its continuous run at a rue Cujas cinema funded by Lebovici is **[R: unconfirmed]**, dropped as a stop and left as a clause.

**Editions Champ Libre** founded 10 October 1969 by Gerard Lebovici with Gerard Guegan, Alain Le Saux and Floriana Valentin **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Gerard Lebovici"]**, upgraded from **[R]**; Lebovici met Debord in 1971 **[A]**. Champ Libre published individual Debord titles, not his collected works: the *Oeuvres completes* came from Gallimard in 2006.

**Tuscany from the 1970s** and **Seville in the 1980s** **[R: Wikipedia FR]**: patterns only, no arrival dates, no addresses **[gap]**, pinned at 1975-01-01 and 1980-01-01 as declared placeholders. The Sanguinetti hoax pamphlet in the Tuscany campa is **[R]**, not carried by the pool. **In girum imus nocte et consumimur igni, 1978** **[A]**, no month **[gap]**.

## 7. The Lebovici murder and the last decade (1984-1994)

**5 March 1984, 41 avenue Foch** **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Gerard Lebovici"]**: four shots to the back of the neck in the underground garage; three .22 Long Rifle casings on the car floor, a fourth upright on the rear window ledge; papers missing; body found 7 March; never solved. *Coordinate contradiction, left standing:* geography gives 48.8725 / 2.284, the afterlife lens 48.8712 / 2.2823, about 200 m apart on the same avenue. The first is used.

**The press campaign** **[A: Wikipedia FR]**: coverage implicated Debord by insinuation, the friendship having provoked jealousy in Lebovici's circle. No charges, no assailant identified. **No source substantiates any concrete theory implicating Debord**: the conspiracy talk is itself the attested phenomenon, and both files hold that distinction.

**Withdrawal of the films, 1984** **[R: widely reported in secondary accounts; no primary source checked]**, pinned at 1984-06-01, the month a declared placeholder set after the March murder.

**Letters, 1984-1985** **[A: NOT BORED! translations, notbored.org]**: to Thierry Levy, 23 March 1984, denying any "power" over Lebovici and denying he was employee or owner of Champ Libre; to Floriana Lebovici, 12 February 1985, "Le Monde must disappear and for us it already has." Both are used as `quote` fields. NOT BORED! is unofficial and the originals were not consulted **[flagged]**.

**Considerations sur l'assassinat de Gerard Lebovici, February 1985** **[A: Wikipedia FR]**, pointing toward the anti-terrorism unit run under Paul Barril **[A: same]**. The journey states this as the book's claim, not as fact.

**Commentaires sur la societe du spectacle, 1988** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**: the integrated spectacle fusing the concentrated and diffuse forms. The opening line is quoted from the unattributed English PDF on libcom.org **[A as to that edition; Verso's not consulted]**. **Panegyrique, 1989** **[A]**, first volume of the autobiography, addressing the drinking directly. **Polyneuritis from years of heavy drinking, 1990s** **[A]**. **Guy Debord, son art et son temps, 1994** **[A]**, with Brigitte Cornand for French television, described as darker and possibly autobiographically final; broadcast month not established **[gap]**, so the stop defaults to 1994-01-01.

**Death, 30 November 1994** **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Guy Debord"; "Bellevue-la-Montagne"]**: suicide by gunshot through the heart, at le Minour, late afternoon.

> **Genuine interpretive contradiction, left open.** Sources disagree not on the facts but on the meaning: some read the suicide as politically motivated, consistent with his refusal of spectacle, others point to the alcoholism and the polyneuritis. No consensus resolves it, and the campa states the division as a division.

**Ashes, December 1994** **[A: Wikipedia FR]**, into the Seine from the Square du Vert-Galant. No day **[gap]**. The absence of ceremony is **[R]**.

**29 January 2009** **[A: Wikipedia, quoting Christine Albanel]**: the Minister of Culture classifies the archives a "tresor national," calling him one of the most important contemporary thinkers; the classification blocked the sale to Yale's Beinecke Library, and the Bibliotheque nationale de France acquired the papers **[A]**.

## 9. Honest gaps

1. **No street addresses** for the Paris birth, the Nice residence, the Tuscan stays, the Seville apartment, or le Minour; five pins are commune- or quarter-level and say so.
2. **The second SI conference** is absent from the material; **attendance** at Gothenburg and Antwerp is nowhere stated.
3. **The 1954 Bernstein marriage** has no confirmed date; **the Italian childhood intervals** have no dates, durations or place name; the Pau and Fontainebleau years yield nothing about what he read.
4. **The withdrawal of the films** rests entirely on secondary report; **the walkie-talkie derives** have no date in any source that passes the story on; **the French letters** were read only in NOT BORED! translation.
