# Motecuhzoma Xocoyotzin (Moctezuma II), ninth tlatoani of Tenochtitlan

*Research report, 2026-08-29. Companion file: `moctezuma_ii.journey.json`, 9 segments, 42 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference.
Contradictions are printed as contradictions and left standing.

One condition governs the whole file. No Nahua text about him composed while he was alive and
free survives. Every quotation in the journey file comes from two Spaniards: Cortés reporting to
an emperor he needed to impress, and Bernal Díaz writing as an old man forty years on. The Nahua
side survives mainly in Book XII of the Florentine Codex, compiled decades later from Tlatelolca
survivors, that is, from the *other* Mexica city.

---

## 1. Birth and schooling (c. 1466-1487)

**Birth year unknown; the source contradicts itself.** [R] Wikipedia's *Moctezuma II* gives
"c. 1471" in the infobox and "c. 1466" in the body, and states he died 29 June 1520 "aged 48-49",
consistent only with c. 1471. Indigenous chronicles give his age at death as 52, which some
scholars read as the calendar cycle rather than a count of years [R]. **Not resolved.** The stop
carries 1466-01-01 purely to order the segment. The "1468" circulating in secondary summaries is
an invented midpoint and appears nowhere here.

**Father: Axayácatl, sixth tlatoani** [A]. It is his father's palace he later gives the
Spaniards, and in which he dies.

**Calmecac at about five** [R]. The institutional pattern is applied to him, not documented for
him.

**The 1487 rededication of the Templo Mayor under Ahuitzotl** [A for the event, archaeologically
dated; **R** for his presence, inferred from age and rank].

**Gap:** his mother is unnamed in everything reachable, and nothing of his first two decades
survives that is not an application of custom.

## 2. The warrior years under Ahuitzotl (c. 1490-1502)

- **Titles tlacatecuhtli and tlacochcalcatl, and service as a principal priest of

## 3. Accession and coronation (1502-1503)

**Ahuitzotl dies; election in the year 10 Tochtli** [A, Noticonquista (UNAM),
amoxtli/2103/2098]. 10 Rabbit corresponds to 1502.

**Coronation 1502 or 1503, unresolved.** Wikipedia reports that most historians say 1502 and some
1503, and that some scholars read the Stone of the Five Suns as carrying a date corresponding to
15 July 1503. The file uses 1503-07-15 as an ordering value and prints the disagreement in
`date_confidence` rather than picking a winner in prose.

**The electors find him sweeping in the temple** [R, Durán and Tezozómoc]. A stock accession
topos, kept because it may preserve fact and certainly preserves what the tradition wanted said
about him; the stop states its status.

**The Coronation Stone, 11 Reed / 1 Crocodile, Art Institute of Chicago** [A for object and 1503
date; **R** for the museum's wording, its page returning **HTTP 403**]. **Open question:** whether
the "Coronation Stone" and the "Stone of the Five Suns" are one object under two names is not
settled in anything reaching me. One stop, which says so, standing in Tenochtitlan where the stone
was carved, with Chicago in `suggested_refs`.

## 4. The reign (1503-1518)

Conquest entries, all [R] from Wikipedia's campaign list, itself drawn from pictorial and
annalistic sources. Given their own stops: Nopallan (1503), Icpatepec near Silacayoápam (1503),
Tototepec (1507), and the undated Tlachquiauhco (Tlaxiaco) and Quetzaltepec, the latter listed
twice. Named inside the Tlachquiauhco campa because no coordinate could be fixed: Achiotlan
(1504) and Zozollan (1506, "28 May"). **Dropped for the same reason:** Jaltepec and Cuatzontlan
(1511), Quetzalapan (1514). **Tecuhtepec at 1507 dropped for a different reason:** verification
could not confirm it at that date in the fetched article, only Tototepec, and it is asserted
nowhere in the file.

**Battle of Atlixco, a Mexica defeat** [A for the defeat; **date contradictory**, c. 1503 or
1507]. The file uses 1507 to order the segment and prints the variance. Coordinates (the modern
town) were **not independently verified**; the stop says so.

**Drought and famine, 1505** [A for 1505]. **The "three-year" duration and the "redistribution of
stores" relief mechanism are in no source reaching this file and were dropped**: the four-year
famine of 1450-1454 belongs to Moctezuma I. The campa states outright that the mechanism of relief
cannot be given here.

**The New Houses of Moctezuma** [A, Cortés's Second Letter and Bernal Díaz; site corroborated by
Wikipedia 'National Palace (Mexico)', which states that the site and much of the material of the
current building is of what were called Moctezuma II's New Houses].

**The Teocalli of the Sacred War** [A]. **The dossier's provenance note was wrong and is
corrected:** discovered in 1831 in the **foundations of the National Palace**, not in the Templo
Mayor precinct excavation, and now in the Museo Nacional de Antropología. Scholars hold it served
as his throne and that its Headdress Glyph carries his name, a stronger link than the dossier
claimed. The coordinate moved accordingly.

**Marriage to Tlapalizquixochtzin, queen of Ecatepec** [R, genealogical tradition, undated].
Coordinates are the modern municipality, **not independently verified**.

**Texcoco succession, date contradiction.** Wikipedia's *Moctezuma II* dates Nezahualpilli's
death to **1516**; its *Nezahualpilli* article gives 1464-1515 and its *Cacamatzin* article has
Cacamatzin ascending in **1515** "under considerable pressure from Moctezuma II". **Two of three
articles and the mainstream literature say 1515; the 1516 is the outlier.** The file uses 1515 and
prints the contradiction; the research lenses also disagreed with each other here. **Whether he
travelled to Texcoco himself or acted through envoys is not specified in any source consulted**,
which the stop states.

**Structural gap:** for eighteen years of rule the reachable sources give almost nothing on
administration, law, tribute reform, or court protocol. The campas here are built from tribute
geography and campaign lists because that is what survives.

## 5. Contact (1517-1519)

**Hernández de Córdoba reaches Yucatán 1517; Grijalva coasts the Gulf 1518** [A for the voyages;
**R** for the reporting chain to court, standard narrative rather than a dated document]. The
coastal system of observers, painters and relay runners is described in Cortés's Second Letter
and the Florentine Codex [A].

**What he made of the reports is the central gap of his life.** Everything that fills it,
including the whole "Cortés taken for the returning Quetzalcóatl" apparatus, postdates his death.
Townsend, "Burying the White Gods", *AHR* 108:3 (2003), argues it is post-defeat mythmaking with
no secure preconquest attestation; Restall extends the argument. **The journey file asserts no
omens and no god-identification anywhere.**

**Cholula massacre, 18 October 1519** [A, Wikipedia 'Cholula massacre']. **His implication is
allegation against counter-allegation and is left contested:** Spanish sources claim his orders,
the Cholulan lords said they had orders and did not follow them. **He was not there**, so Cholula
is not staged here; the stop stands at his own palace, named "Tenochtitlan, word from Cholula".
Cholula proper is already a stop in `cortes.journey.json` on the same day.

## 6. The Spaniards in the city (November 1519 - May 1520)

**Causeway meeting, 8 November 1519** [A as an event, not seriously disputed]. Its framing as a
surrender is disputed, and the `quote_source` on the welcome speech says in the same breath that
no Nahua source corroborates it in that form. **The speech exists in two independent
nineteenth-century English renderings**, Folsom's 1843 Cortés and a second in Lockhart's
**endnote** 50 to Bernal Díaz vol. 1 ch. LXXXIX (an endnote in the back matter, not a footnote;
the "(p. 224)" is its back-reference). The second is a `suggested_ref`, not a second quotation.

**Lodging in the palace of Axayácatl** [A, Wikipedia 'Palace of Axayacatl'].

**The seizure: timing and nature disputed.** The traditional chronology, followed by
`cortes.journey.json`, puts it on 14 November 1519, linked to the Qualpopoca affair. **Restall
(2018) argues hard imprisonment may date only from May or June 1520.** Both readings are printed
in the stop; neither is adopted.

**Arrest of Cacamatzin, early 1520: the agency in the circulating version is backwards and is
corrected.** Wikipedia states that **Cortés ordered Moctezuma** to send someone to arrest
Cacamatzin, and the *Cacamatzin* article agrees the arrest was carried out under Cortés's orders.
The event is [A]; **the motive is not**, and the file supplies none. The stop stands in
Tenochtitlan, where Cacamatzin was brought, not Texcoco, where he was seized.

**Narváez lands, Cortés leaves, Alvarado holds the city, April-May 1520** [A in outline].
Narváez's defeat at Cempoala on 27 May 1520 is already a stop in `cortes.journey.json` and is only
a `suggested_ref` here.

**Toxcatl massacre: date contradiction between atlas files.** worldhistory.org gives **22 May
1520**, `cortes.journey.json` **20 May 1520**. Both are printed in the stop, unreconciled. He was
confined nearby and not present; the stop marks the political effect on him, the massacre itself
being Alvarado's scene.

## 7. Death (late June 1520)

**Date conventionally 29 June 1520, not proven** [A that he died in Spanish custody in
Tenochtitlan in the last days of June].

**Cause: two irreconcilable traditions, both kept** [both A as traditions, PBS *Conquistadors*
companion page]. The **Spanish** version has him brought to a terrace to appeal to his people,
struck by stones and darts past the Spanish shields, dying later, in some accounts after tearing
off his dressings and refusing food. Nearly all surviving **indigenous-derived** sources instead
say the Spaniards themselves stabbed or strangled him, in some versions with the other lords held
beside him, on the eve of the escape. **Age at death is contradictory too**: 48-49 per Wikipedia
against 52 in the indigenous chronicles, the latter possibly a calendar figure [R].

**Quotation corrections, all applied in the file:**

"Why does Malinche now turn to me?..." (ch. CXXVI, p. 344) is **two separate utterances spliced**,
a full sentence of narration standing between them in the source; the file joins them with an
explicit ellipsis and says so, and the campa states that Díaz reports it at second hand ("is said
to have exclaimed"). "Alas! for all this, it is now too late..." is the same chapter and page,
spoken to father Olmedo and Olid, its ellipsis marking an omitted middle clause. "Alas! great
king, your own misfortune..." (p. 345) is **not his**, but four Mexica captains addressing him,
and Díaz names the cousin they had raised as **Cuitlahuatzin, lord of Iztapalapa**, not Cuauhtémoc,
who succeeds two months later. "Now, we will make you pay dearly..." (ch. CXXVII, p. 347) is
**not his either**: warriors on seeing the body.

**Citation corrections to every Bernal Díaz item.** The 1844 Lockhart translation is **not**
titled *The True History of the Conquest of New Spain* (the Maudslay/Cohen title); its title page
reads *The Memoirs of the Conquistador Bernal Diaz del Castillo* (London: J. Hatchard and Son,
1844). Pages fixed against the scan by running head: ch. XC at **pp. 226-227**, not 231-232;
ch. XCV at **pp. 251-252**, straddling a page break, not 254. Cortés's "flesh and bone" passage
sits entirely on **p. 89** of Folsom 1843. Díaz's "Motecusuma answered, that gold was found in
three different parts..." (ch. CII) was **excluded**: indirect speech, not a quotation.

**The Noche Triste is not staged here.** The Tacuba causeway flight and the tree at Popotla are
already stops in `cortes.journey.json`, and he was dead. His file stops at his own death, his
body, and his fire.

**Cremation at Copolco** [R, Florentine Codex Book XII]. **Coordinate conflict:** the research
material carried **two coordinates for the identical event**, 19.4464, -99.1372 and 19.4361,
-99.14, 1.2 km apart, neither sourced, and Copolco's footprint is not fixed archaeologically. The
file uses the first, says a second circulates, and marks the location a textual inference rather
than a mapped site. **No remains, no tomb, no ashes.** Book XII itself was not fetched.

**Succession, corrected:** Cuitláhuac ruled **80 days** in the year 2 Flint and died in
**December 1520**, "perhaps dying from smallpox" [A, Wikipedia 'Cuitláhuac', which states that
early sources do not say from what he succumbed]. The confident "died of smallpox" is **not
earned** and is hedged. Tenochtitlán fell 13 August 1521 under Cuauhtémoc [A]; that is
Cuauhtémoc's and Cortés's event and is not staged here.

## 8. Coordinates: inherited, corrected, guessed

**Inherited byte-identical from `cortes.journey.json`:** causeway meeting **19.4283, -99.1332**;
palace of Axayácatl stops **19.434, -99.1335**; sacred precinct and death stops **19.4345,
-99.1332**. **From `diego_rivera.journey.json`:** the Palacio Nacional pin **19.4325, -99.13139**,
used for the New Houses and the Teocalli, the same site.

**Three discrepancies printed rather than smoothed:** the atlas pins the sacred precinct at
19.4345, -99.1332 while Wikipedia's *Templo Mayor* gives **19.4333, -99.1328**, ~130 m off;
Wikipedia's *Palace of Axayacatl* gives **19.4344, -99.1341** against the atlas 19.434, -99.1335;
and he died inside the palace although the death stop carries the sacred-precinct pin, since that
is where `cortes.journey.json` puts the same event. Atlas continuity was preferred; the source
values are recorded in the stops.

**Dossier coordinates corrected** against Wikipedia: Santos Reyes Nopala **16.100, -97.150**
(~14 km off); Silacayoápam **17.500, -98.133** (~15 km); San Miguel Quetzaltepec **16.967,
-95.817** (~27 km, wrong municipality); the New Houses moved off the Zócalo onto the palace
footprint. **Unverified and flagged in their own stops:** Ayotlan, the Xoconochco zone, Atlixco,
Ecatepec, Copolco.

**Canonical pins checked and not used:** the Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262) and the Temple Mount
(31.778, 35.2354), supplied by the interlock pass for format consistency with **no factual link of
any kind** to Moctezuma, so absent from the file.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

Moctezuma II is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Relations not rooted in his own life were kept
rather than silently deleted, with the direction named.

**Forward-direction** (later people engaging with him or his world; not licensable without the
author's say-so, and **none staged as a stop here**):

- **Vivaldi's *Motezuma* (RV 723)**, premiered 14 November 1733 in Venice on a wholly fictionalized
  Cortés-Moctezuma plot, the score lost until **2002** and staged again at Düsseldorf in **2005**
  [A]. A real one-directional engagement, and it is Vivaldi's stop, on a file the corpus does not
  have. Neither staged nor cited here.
- **British Museum, *Moctezuma: Aztec Ruler* (2009)** and its BBC documentary with Dan Snow
  [**R, unconfirmed**, see section 10]; **Restall (2018)** and **Townsend (2003)**; and **the
  1978-1982 Templo Mayor excavation** [A]: all cited as sources or suggested_refs on his own
  stops, the licensed handling, never staged.
- **The Weltmuseum Wien featherwork headdress** and the repatriation campaign of **Xokonoschtletl
  Gómora** [A for the object's history and the dispute; **R** for the attribution to him
  personally, which the museum frames as tradition]. Named in the cremation campa; Vienna is not
  a stop.
- **Diego Rivera's Palacio Nacional murals** (1929-1935) on ground adjoining the New Houses [A
  that they treat pre-conquest Tenochtitlan; **unconfirmed** whether any figure is an explicit
  Moctezuma likeness]. A `suggested_ref` there. **Rivera is deliberately not named in any campa**:
  the confirmed relation is to the site and the subject matter, not to the man.

**Examined and dropped as not relations:** the **obsidian mirror** in the British Museum, a stop
in `john_dee` and `edward_kelley`, whose maker and owner are unattributed [R]; **Martin Luther at
Worms**, a synchronism ten months after this death; **Atahualpa**, whose file does not mention him
and whom he never met, however illuminating the comparison of two sacral rulers seized alive;
**Pizarro**, kin to Cortés and not to him; **Sor Juana** and **Lewis Spence**, engagements with
Aztec religion generally; **the Estadio Azteca** and Maradona [R], toponymic.

**Only one atlas traveler is named in a campa: Hernán Cortés** (`cortes`), whom he met, housed,
argued with, was seized by, and died in the custody of.

His descendants are family and so backward-rooted: the Isabel Moctezuma line and the dukedom of
Moctezuma de Tultengo are `suggested_refs` on his Ecatepec stop. **The dossier tangled these:**
Wikipedia gives the roughly 2,000 living Spanish descendants and the Alba descent to **Isabel's**
line and the dukedom to her **brother** Pedro. Split accordingly; the 2,000 is an estimate [R].
