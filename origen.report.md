# Origen of Alexandria (Origenes Adamantius), c. 185 to c. 253

*Research report for `origen.journey.json`. 2026-08-30.*

**[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on one
interested witness. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated; unreachable sources are named
at the end with the reason. Unqualified chapter numbers are Eusebius, *EH*, book VI.

**The structural fact governing the file.** Nearly the whole biography comes from Eusebius, writing
seventy years after Origen's death in the city that held his library and where his own teacher
Pamphilus had written an *Apology for Origen*. He is a partisan: where he is sole witness that is
said, and his silence about the Alexandrian synods is read as a position. Jerome, the next most cited
source, changed sides mid-career.

---

## 1. Alexandria: the martyr's son (c. 185 to 202)

Born at Alexandria into a Christian household, c. 185 **[R]**: no source gives a date, and the year is
derived backward from VI.2.12, "not quite seventeen" at his father's death in 202. Leonides trained
him in scripture by daily memorisation alongside the Greek liberal curriculum **[A, VI.2]** and was
arrested in the Severan persecution of 202 **[A, VI.1]**; charge, informer and length of imprisonment
are not given. Origen wanted to give himself up with him, his mother hid all his clothes, and he wrote
instead: one line survives, "Take heed not to change your mind on our account" **[R, VI.2, sole
witness]**. Leonides was beheaded and the estate confiscated **[A, VI.1-2; Jerome, *DVI* 54]**. A
wealthy Alexandrian woman then took him in as an adopted son, alongside a teacher named Paul of
Antioch whose prayers he refused to join **[A, VI.2]**. **Gap:** she is unnamed in every source.

## 2. The catechetical school (203 to c. 213)

He began instructing converts in his eighteenth year **[A, VI.3]**.

- **Contradiction inside Eusebius, unresolved.** VI.3.1 has him start because persecution drove every
  teacher out; VI.3.8 says the school "had been entrusted to him alone by Demetrius." The pool's "on
  his own initiative" is unsupportable and was dropped. **Second contradiction:** VI.3.3 sets the
  persecution "under Aquila, the governor of Alexandria," but Subatianus Aquila's prefecture runs
  c. 206-211 and will not sit inside a 203 date. The file moves neither, and says so.
- Seven students martyred, all named at VI.4-5: Plutarch, Serenus (burned), Heraclides, Hero, a second
  Serenus (beheaded), Herais, and Basilides "may be counted the seventh" **[A]**. The pool omitted
  Basilides and the second Serenus in **both** arrays. Origen attended them and "would boldly salute
  them with a kiss" (VI.3.4).
- He closed the grammar school, **then** sold his library for four obols a day **[A, VI.3.8-9]**.
  **The pool had this backward and misattributed:** Eusebius has that order and frames the obols as
  self-sufficiency, not as support for his mother and brothers. **Gap:** how the family ate.
- **Self-castration [R], and the tag is the finding.** VI.8 reports that he took Matthew 19:12 "in
  too literal and extreme a sense" and acted on it in secret, and that Demetrius "admired greatly
  the daring nature of the act." Eusebius is the sole ancient witness; scholarship since 1900 widely
  doubts it (Trigg accepts, Chadwick and McGuckin doubt), and Origen's own *Commentary on Matthew*
  rejects the literal reading. **The pool tagged this [A] in one array and [R] in another;**
  carried as **[R]**. **A false quotation was caught:** the sentence circulated as Eusebius's ("Origen
  made himself a eunuch, in order to obey the command of the Saviour...") appears **nowhere in the New
  Advent NPNF2 text**; the file quotes the real one, and Eusebius never says "command."
- Elementary teaching delegated to Heraclas **[A, VI.15]**; study under a teacher named Ammonius and
  the philosopher's cloak **[R, re-tagged]**, since both reach Eusebius only inside a quotation from
  the hostile pagan Porphyry at VI.19.5-8 which he argues against. Identification of that Ammonius
  with **Ammonius Saccas** is disputed and left open.

## 3. The first journeys out (c. 212 to 217)

Rome under Zephyrinus, "to see the most ancient church of the Romans" **[A, VI.14.10]**; **gap:** no
building, meeting or interlocutor is named. Summoned to Roman Arabia by the governor, who sent a
soldier with letters to Demetrius **and** to the prefect of Egypt **[A for the summons, VI.19]**,
**[R] for the location**, since Eusebius names no town, Bostra is inferred as the governor's seat, and
the purpose is unrecorded. Fled Alexandria for Caesarea amid disturbances **[R]**: Eusebius reports
the trouble without naming Caracalla. Theoctistus of Caesarea and Alexander of Jerusalem invited him
to expound scripture publicly while still a layman; Demetrius objected that this was unprecedented and
recalled him **[A, VI.19.16-17]**.

## 4. Ambrose and the Alexandrian works (c. 218 to 230)

- Ambrose converted from Valentinianism, then became patron **[A, VI.18.1]**. **Citation corrected:**
  the pool cited VI.21, the Mamaea chapter; the stenographers are VI.23.2, "more than seven
  amanuenses, who relieved each other at appointed times."
- The **Hexapla** **[A, VI.16]**: parallel columns, Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion, the Septuagint, and
  the fifth to seventh Psalms versions. **One column removed:** Eusebius does **not** mention the
  second column of Greek transliteration, which rests on later witnesses. Commentary on John against
  Heracleon, the first eight books on Genesis, Psalms 1-25, Lamentations, two books *On the
  Resurrection*, the *Stromateis* and *De Principiis* are all Alexandrian **[A, VI.24]**; the pool
  tagged that **[R]** as "not independently re-quoted here" and the hedge was removed.
- **Mamaea summons him to Antioch under escort [A for the event, VI.21], date contested, unresolved.**
  Eusebius calls her "the mother of the emperor" (Severus Alexander), so nothing before **March 222**
  is possible and the pool's c. 218-222 falls in Elagabalus's reign. Reconstructions favouring
  **c. 231-232**, when Alexander was at Antioch for the Persian campaign, put it *after* the break with
  Demetrius, whereas Eusebius has Origen return to Alexandria. The file dates the stop by Eusebius's
  narrative position, calls that an ordering device, and gives both. **No conversion is claimed**, and
  claims of Origen as court adviser go beyond the text.
- **Transmission problem carried into the *De Principiis* stop.** The Greek is substantially lost;
  what survives is **Rufinus of Aquileia's** Latin of c. 397-398, whose translator said openly that he
  cut passages he took for heretical interpolations, while **Jerome's** rival literal version, made to
  expose those cuts, **is itself lost**. No modern edition can adjudicate.

## 5. The break with Demetrius (c. 230 to 232)

- Sent to Greece "on account of a pressing necessity in church matters" **[A, VI.23.4]**.
  **Mis-sourcing corrected:** Eusebius names **neither Achaea, nor Corinth, nor any doctrinal
  dispute**; the Achaean heresy detail comes from **Photius, *Bibliotheca* cod. 118**, reporting
  Pamphilus's lost *Apology*. The file uses the Ancient Corinth pin and states that no arrival there
  is narrated anywhere. Ordained presbyter at Caesarea by Theoctistus and Alexander without
  Demetrius's knowledge **[A, VI.23.4; Jerome, *DVI* 54; Catholic Encyclopedia]**.
- **The Alexandrian synods: re-tagged [R] and re-sourced.** The pool tagged banishment and deposition
  **[A]** citing VI.26. **That chapter contains no synod, no expulsion and no deposition**: its
  content is the move to Caesarea, Demetrius's death after forty-three years, and Heraclas's
  succession. The synods come from **Photius cod. 118**, echoed by Jerome, and **the decrees do not
  survive**. Eusebius's attribution of jealousy to Demetrius is partisan, and his silence about the
  synods is itself a datum.
- Palestine, Arabia, Phoenicia and Achaea declined to recognise the sentence **[A]**. Heraclas,
  Origen's own former assistant, succeeded Demetrius and did not restore him, and Origen settled
  permanently at Caesarea, 231/232 **[A, VI.26; Catholic Encyclopedia]**.

## 6. The school at Caesarea (232 to 249)

- **Gregory Thaumaturgus and Athenodorus [A for the pupils, VI.30; the details are Gregory's].**
  VI.30 names the brothers but supplies **neither** the five-year figure **nor** the abandoned law
  studies at Berytus; both come from Gregory's own *Address of Thanksgiving*. **Date contradiction
  inside the pool:** chronology gave c. 233-238, geography c. 232-235; a five-year course beginning in
  the year Origen arrived is the weaker reconstruction, so the file uses 233.
- Firmilian of Caesarea in Cappadocia: friendship and mutual visits **[A in general terms, VI.27]**,
  **[R]** for any specific stay and for the claim that Origen sheltered there through the Maximinus
  persecution. **Gap:** his whereabouts in 235-238 are not recoverable. *Exhortation to
  Martyrdom* for Ambrose and Protoctetus, who suffered "unusual hardships" **[A, VI.28]**. Athens
  (Ezekiel finished, Song of Songs begun) is **[R]**, synthesised by the pool from Eusebius and
  Jerome without re-fetching. **Gap:** who invited him, and for how long.
- **Beryllus of Bostra [A, VI.33]. Citation corrected:** the pool's chronology array cited VI.27, the
  Firmilian chapter, while its geography array cited VI.33 correctly. The record of the discussion
  existed in Eusebius's day and does not survive. A second Arabian synod, on whether the soul dies
  with the body **[A, VI.37]**, is unlocated, Bostra being a guess by precedent. Correspondence with
  **Julius Africanus** on Susanna **[A, VI.31; both letters survive]**.

## 7. Past sixty, and the Decian persecution (245 to 253)

- Stenographers admitted to his public discourses for the first time; the twenty-five books on Matthew
  and the eight *Against Celsus* belong here, as do letters to **Philip the Arab** and his wife
  **Severa** **[A, VI.36]**. **Gap:** those letters' contents are lost and quoted by nobody, and
  Eusebius does not say Philip was a Christian.
- **The *Dialogue with Heraclides*: anachronistic sourcing caught and replaced.** The pool cited
  "Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* context" and tagged it **[A]**. Eusebius nowhere mentions the
  *Dialogue*: it was **unknown until the Tura find of August 1941**, so no chapter of his can support
  it at any tag. Re-sourced to Scherer and Nongbri, tagged **[R]**.
- Arrested under Decius: "bonds and bodily tortures and torments under the iron collar," feet stretched
  in the stocks for many days **[A, VI.39, from Origen's own letters]**. **Gap:** the city is unnamed,
  Caesarea is presumed as residence not confirmed as the arrest site, and no transcript survives. He
  survived without recorded recantation **[R]**, and **no ancient source gives a cause of death or a
  causal link from the torture to it**.
- **Death and burial [R], date corrected.** Jerome, *DVI* 54, verified verbatim, anchors the death to
  the reign of Gallus and Volusianus, "his sixty-ninth year," at Tyre, where he was also buried.
  **Contradiction, unresolved:** Gallus and Volusianus were killed in August 253, so Jerome's anchor
  will not yield 254 and the pool's "253/254" overshoots, while Wikipedia has him dying "less than a
  year" after his 251 release; the file gives c. 251-253. **The pool tagged the burial [A] while
  citing a source that undercuts it:** Wikipedia calls it "a later legend... but little value can be
  attached to this." Re-tagged **[R]**; no grave has been identified.

## 8. After (543 to 2012)

- Justinian's edict to Patriarch Mennas condemning Origen by name and ordering his writings burned,
  543 (sometimes 542/544), endorsed by a home synod in the city that year **[A]**.
- **Second Council of Constantinople, 553: contested, and the contest is the finding.** The fifteen
  anathemas popularly assigned to it **do not appear in the surviving acts of its eight sessions**,
  and no debate on them is recorded there; critical scholarship assigns them to a separate
  pre-conciliar synod. Origen's name **does** appear in the eleventh general anathema in most of the
  manuscript tradition. Not adjudicated.
- **Tura, August 1941 [A, Nongbri]:** papyrus codices in the quarry tunnels south of Cairo, holding
  the *Dialogue with Heraclides*, *On the Passover*, and fragments of *Against Celsus* and of the
  Romans commentary. **Munich, 2012 [A]:** Marina Molin Pradel identified twenty-nine unknown Greek
  homilies on the Psalms in Codex Monacensis Graecus 314; Perrone's edition 2015, Trigg's
  translation 2021.
- **[R] and unsourced, so no stop was built on it:** the modern rehabilitation through Daniélou,
  Crouzel and the Origeniana colloquia. See Sources for what is wrong with the pool's citation.
- Never canonised; revered in antiquity as scholar and confessor rather than martyr, having survived
  the Decian imprisonment **[A]**.

## Coordinate corrections against the pool

1. **Alexandria.** The pool used 31.2126, 29.8951 for **fifteen** stops. That point lies about 1.7 km
   north of Alexandria's civic point, near Qaitbay, i.e. **in the Eastern Harbour, not on the
   mainland**, and not in the Brucheion southeast of it.
2. **Tura.** 29.9394, 31.2864 lies about 3.2 km west of the town, at the riverside rather than the
   quarries. Corrected to 29.9458, 31.3181.
3. **Tarsus stop dropped entirely.** Its only source was Jerome's *Letter 33*, a catalogue of writings
   and not an itinerary, and its coordinate was about 1.1 km off the town.
4. **Tyre / Al-Bass.** The pool's second Tyre entry labelled itself "Al-Bass necropolis area" while
   pinning a point 1.5 km away on the peninsula, and no cited source ties the shown tomb to that
   necropolis; the label is dropped. **Caesarea Maritima:** the pool's two lenses disagreed by 1.1 km
   (32.5, 34.9036 against 32.5, 34.8913).

**Inherited byte-identical:** Alexandria 31.2001, 29.9055 (`hypatia`); Caesarea Maritima 32.5,
34.8913, Tyre 33.2704, 35.1937, Antioch on the Orontes 36.2047, 36.1817 and Rome 41.9028, 12.4964
(`apostle_paul`); Constantinople 41.0034, 28.9776 (`justinian_i`). **Non-inherited:** Bostra 32.5172,
36.4811 (also used for the unnamed second Arabian synod, by precedent, not attestation); Ancient
Corinth 37.9058, 22.8781; Athens 37.9727, 23.7228; Caesarea in Cappadocia 38.7225, 35.4875; Tura
29.9458, 31.3181; Bayerische Staatsbibliothek 48.1447, 11.5836.

## Interlocks used, and interlocks refused

**Named in a campa**, each on a direct one-directional relation, Origen engaging with the figure in
writing: `apostle_peter` (he identified the unnamed "high mountain" of Matthew 17 as Mount Tabor, and
*EH* 3.1 cites him for Peter's inverted crucifixion; both are already on
`apostle_peter.journey.json`, which credits Origen by name); `apostle_paul` (Origen on the authorship
of Hebrews, "But who wrote the epistle, in truth, God knows"); `simon_magus` (the near-extinction of
the Simonians, **hedged in `date_confidence`** because `simon_magus.journey.json` itself flags its
Origen citation as not independently re-fetched); `justinian_i` (the 543 edict, in the afterlife
segment per the `alan_turing.journey.json` convention; the existing `justinian_i` stop for the 553
council covers the Three Chapters only, so this is new).

**Refused**, because shared geography or a shared milieu is not a relation: `hypatia` (Alexandrian
Platonism two centuries later; the Ammonius Saccas thread is disputed inside Origen's own dossier, and
the other Ammonius in that file, the monk who attacks Orestes, is an unrelated same-name figure);
`flavius_josephus` (shared philological technique, no documentary link in the pool); `egeria` (passes
near Alexandria 150 years later and mentions none of it). **Tertullian** has no `journey.json` yet, so
`QUEUE.md` rows 84-85 are a queue relationship, not an interlock. **Gap confirmed:** no journey file
exists for Clement of Alexandria, Ammonius Saccas, Plotinus, Philo, Jerome, Rufinus, Pamphilus,
Epiphanius, Eusebius of Caesarea, or Gregory Thaumaturgus.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

One relation in the pool is **not rooted in Origen's own life** and, per `EXCEPTIONS.md`, is reported
rather than silently deleted. **`saint_augustine_of_hippo`, direction caught: FORWARD.** The pool links
the two through a shared Middle Platonist current and through Rufinus's translations, by which
"Augustine's circle later inherited Origen's texts directly." Augustine was born in 354: a new hash in
an old block, and **Origen is not on the `EXCEPTIONS.md` list**, so it is not in the journey file. The
licensable form is the reverse, a stop on **Augustine's own file**. No **backward** relation was found.

## Honest gaps

Marked **Gap:** inline above. In sum: no birth date or quarter, no death date narrower than a reign
ending August 253, no grave, no name for the patroness, no purpose for the Arabian interview, no trace
of the Rome visit, no whereabouts for 235-238, no city for the Decian arrest, nothing on what the
imprisonment did to his body. Lost entirely: the letters to Philip and Severa, the Alexandrian synod
decrees, and the acts of the Bostra discussion, which Eusebius still had.
