# Ratramnus of Corbie: research report

*Slug: `ratramnus_of_corbie`. Benedictine monk of Corbie in Picardy, c. 800/805 to c. 868. Teacher, controversialist, and the man Rimbert wrote to when he needed to know whether dog-headed people could be baptized.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged where found and **not** adjudicated. Where the research pass produced a date or coordinate the sources do not support, the correction is recorded in place rather than quietly applied.

One structural fact governs the file: **Ratramnus does not travel.** No source places him outside Corbie at any point in his life. The councils that decided the controversies he wrote into (Mainz 848, Quierzy 849, Valence 855, Savonnières 859) met without him. Everything here happens inside one Picard cloister or to books that left it, so the journey file is Corbie plus a short tail of places where his own texts, and his abbey's library, ended up.

---

## 1. Origins (c. 800 to c. 840)

- Birth date and birthplace are **unknown**. Nothing names his parents, his year, or his village; historians infer a birth in the first years of the ninth century by counting backward from a documented teaching career. **[R: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'; no vita survives.]**
- He is a monk of **Corbie**, founded 661 under Balthild and Clotaire III, colonized from Luxeuil, housing some 300 monks in the ninth century and one of the great scriptoria of the Carolingian renaissance, credited with helping fix Carolingian minuscule. Its three celebrated ninth-century scholars are Ratramnus, Paschasius Radbertus, and Hadoard. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Corbie Abbey'.]**
- Abbots in his lifetime: Adalard (a cousin of Charlemagne), Wala, Isaac, Paschasius Radbertus (843-851 per the abbey list), Odo (851 to c. 860). **[A: Wikipedia, 'Corbie Abbey'; 'Paschasius Radbertus'.]**
- In 822 Corbie founded the Saxon daughter house of **Corvey**, putting a Corbie institution on the missionary frontier a generation before Rimbert's letter. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Corbie Abbey'.]** That Corvey was a channel for northern report is **[R]**, an inference, not a documented route.
- He is said to have become teaching master around **844**, with Paschasius's elevation to abbot. **[R: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'; other accounts leave the appointment undated.]** The elevation itself is 843, so 844 and "with the elevation" cannot both be exact; the journey file shows the stop at 843 and says in `date_confidence` that the dating sources disagree.

## 2. The Eucharist (833 to c. 850)

- **Paschasius Radbertus** writes *De Corpore et Sanguine Domini* c. 831-833, revising later, arguing that the consecrated bread and wine are really and substantially the historical flesh born of Mary and crucified. First sustained Latin treatise on the subject. **[A: Wikipedia, 'De Corpore et Sanguine Domini'; 'Paschasius Radbertus'.]**
- **Charles the Bald visits Corbie in 843**; his unease with Paschasius's teaching is the conventional occasion for the commission to Ratramnus. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'.]**
- Ratramnus answers with his own *De Corpore et Sanguine Domini*, using the same vocabulary (*figura*, *veritas*) to the opposite conclusion: the sacrament holds Christ's body and blood spiritually and figuratively, not as the identical flesh that walked in Judea. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'.]**
- **CORRECTION.** One pool entry called the treatise "addressed to the emperor." Charles the Bald was **king** of West Francia from 843 and was crowned emperor only on 25 December 875, seven years after Ratramnus's death. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Charles the Bald'.]** The journey file says king.
- **Willemien Otten challenges the framing**: neither monk names or quotes the other, and the two use *figura* and *veritas* differently, so the "Carolingian eucharistic controversy" as live combat between them may be a later construction. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus', citing Otten.]** Left open.
- **CONTRADICTION, unresolved.** One pool entry put **Rabanus Maurus** into the debate in **845** as abbot of Fulda and later archbishop of Mainz. In 845 he held neither office: Fulda 822-842, Mainz only 847-856 **[A: Wikipedia, 'Rabanus Maurus']**. His eucharistic intervention (the letter to Egil of Prüm) is conventionally dated to the early 850s, and the Rabanus article does not document his part in this debate at all. Downgraded to **[R]** and kept out of the journey file.

### Quoted text (verified against the English translation)

All fetched and grep-verified in *The Book of Ratramn... on the Body and Blood of the Lord* (English translation, American edition), archive.org id `bookofratramnpri00ratr`. **[A]**

- sec. V, the king's question restated: "Your excellent Majesty inquireth, whether the Body and Blood of Christ... be made so in a mystery or in truth"
- sec. VII: "Figure is a certain outshadowing, which exhibiteth what it meaneth under some sort of veil"
- sec. XVI (p. 21): "under the veil of corporeal bread and corporeal wine, the spiritual Body and the spiritual Blood of Christ do exist"
  - **CORRECTION.** Cited in the pool as sec. LXXVII "near p.91". It is sec. XVI; the "91" is an OCR artifact of the running head, and the true page is 21.
- sec. LVII: "For in its nature it is bread, but sacramentally it is the true Body of Christ"
  - **CORRECTION.** Cited in the pool as sec. LVIII. The passage closes sec. LVII (djvu lines 2310-2314); sec. LVIII begins at line 2317.
- sec. LIV: "a change hath inwardly been wrought by the mighty power of the Holy Spirit"
- sec. LX: "Of a truth it is the Body of Christ, yet not His corporeal, but His spiritual Body"
- sec. LXXX-LXXXI, quoting John 6:63: "It is the Spirit that quickeneth, the flesh profiteth nothing"

## 3. Predestination (848 to 859)

- **Gottschalk of Orbais**, teaching double predestination, is condemned at Mainz in **848**, beaten, and made to swear off East Francia; condemned again at **Quierzy in 849** under Hincmar of Reims and Charles the Bald, unfrocked, flogged, and imprisoned at **Hautvillers**, where he is forced to burn his own writings. He dies there unreconciled on 30 October 868. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Gottschalk of Orbais'.]**
- **Ratramnus is at neither council.** He develops arguments substantially aligned with Gottschalk's position, apparently without direct contact with the imprisoned man, and becomes one of his few allies among established scholars. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Gottschalk of Orbais'; 'Ratramnus'.]**
- **John Scotus Eriugena** is commissioned by Hincmar to write against Gottschalk; his *De Divina Praedestinatione* draws fire even from bishops who opposed Gottschalk, and provokes Ratramnus's *De Praedestinatione Dei* in two books. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'.]**
- Ratramnus also compiles a patristic dossier defending Gottschalk's *trina deitas* against Hincmar's *summa deitas*. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'.]**
- **CORRECTION.** A pool entry put the **Third Council of Valence (855)** in "West Francia." After Verdun (843) the provinces of Vienne, Lyon, and Arles, Valence among them, lay in Lothar I's Middle Kingdom, passing to Provence on his death in September 855. The council, condemning Eriugena's treatise and repudiating the Quierzy chapters, is real.
- **CONTESTED, not resolved.** The **Council of Savonnières (859)** appears in conciliar chronologies, but Wikipedia has no article on it and the Savonnières-devant-Bar article mentions neither a Carolingian villa nor an 859 assembly. The site identification is uncertain, so the pool's **[A]** tag is too strong.

## 4. The other quarrels of the house

**On the birth of Christ.** Ratramnus writes *The Birth of Christ*, generally read as a rebuttal of Paschasius's *De Partu Virginis* and its claim of Mary's unbroken womb. **[R]**: Wikipedia hedges ("possibly responding to Paschasius"), and **no source dates it**. The pool's date of 850 is unsupported; the one firm anchor is the critical edition by José María Canal Sánchez-Pagín (1968).

**On the soul.** Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus', distinguishes **two** treatises:
1. *On the Soul*, written **against Macarius Scotus**, an Irish master;
2. *The Book on the Soul* (*Liber de anima ad Odonem Bellovacensem*), **addressed to Bishop Odo I of Beauvais**, challenging the notion of a universal soul.

**CORRECTIONS.** (a) Macarius is the **opponent**, not the addressee. (b) The doctrine refuted is a **universal or common soul**, not the "tricorporal soul theory" of the pool, a phrase in none of the sources. (c) A work addressed to Odo cannot date to 860: **Odo I held Beauvais 861-881**, so the range is c. 861-868. (d) Another pool entry made the Odo correspondence a treatise on **Christ's birth**; it is not, and the two must not be merged. (e) This **Odo had been abbot of Corbie 851-860**, so the letter goes from a monk to his own former abbot.

**Paschasius resigns.** He gives up the abbacy after roughly a decade, withdraws to Saint-Riquier, returns to Corbie late in life, and dies there in 865. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Paschasius Radbertus'.]**
**CONTRADICTION, unresolved.** That article gives the resignation as "after ten years (around 853)"; the Corbie abbot list gives Paschasius 843-851 with Odo from 851, leaving no room for 853. The journey file says the sources disagree. No late-life meeting or reconciliation between the two monks is recorded anywhere.

## 5. The letter on the cynocephali (c. 863-865)

- **Rimbert**, missionary in Scandinavia under Ansgar and later archbishop of Hamburg-Bremen (865-888), asks a practical question: the **cynocephali** reported at the edge of the mission field, do they possess reason and descend from Adam, so that preaching and baptism apply, or are they beasts? **[A: Wikipedia, 'Rimbert'; 'Cynocephaly'.]**
- Ratramnus answers with the ***Epistola de Cynocephalis***, weighing reported behaviour: villages, clothing, cultivated land, observed law, and a sense of shame. Beings showing reason, law, and shame may have rational souls, are therefore human, and are to be evangelized. He has never seen one. **[A: as above; the triad of reason, law, and shame follows the treatise as characterized in secondary scholarship.]**
- He draws on the **hagiography of Saint Christopher**, whose legend, rooted in the Coptic *Acts of Bartholomew* tale of a dog-headed cannibal converted and given human speech, already supplied a precedent for a cynocephalus baptized and named. **[A: `san_cristobal.journey.json`; Wikipedia, 'Cynocephaly'.]**
- The underlying rule is **Augustine, *City of God* XVI.8** (c. 426): whatever is rational and mortal descends from Adam, whatever its bodily shape. **[A]** **Isidore, *Etymologiae* XI.3.15**, supplies the term: *Hi in India nascuntur*, and their barking confesses them beasts rather than men. **[A]** **CORRECTION.** A pool entry had Isidore placing them "in India or in Libya"; Libya is where he locates other monstrous races.
- **CONTRADICTION inside the pool.** Two entries give the letter's origin as **Bremen**; two others date it **c. 863-865, before Rimbert's elevation**, when he was still "working as a missionary in Scandinavia." Rimbert reached Bremen only in 865, after Ansgar died on 3 February. Both cannot hold. The journey file dates the exchange 863-865 and puts Rimbert in the mission field, not the see.
- **CONTRADICTION on Rimbert's origin.** English Wikipedia calls him "Danish by birth"; German Wikipedia has him *aus dem Adel Flanderns*, born near Torhout in Flanders, following Adam of Bremen. Not adjudicated; the journey file asserts no birthplace for him.
- **Text.** The letter survives in **Patrologia Latina 121, cols. 1153-1156**, with a critical edition by **Ernst Ludwig Dümmler, 1902**. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Cynocephaly'; 'Ratramnus', Published Editions.]**

## 6. Last work and death

- After the **Photian schism**, Ratramnus writes *Contra Graecorum Opposita Romanam Ecclesiam Infamantium*, defending the Filioque and Latin usage. Photius's Encyclical dates to 867; the treatise summary gives a range of 863-867, which sits awkwardly against an 867 provocation. Flagged, not resolved. **[A on the work; the date range is [R].]**
- **Death: c. 868, at Corbie. [A: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'.]** Neither day nor month is recorded, and **no tomb, grave marker, or burial record** appears in the Ratramnus or Corbie material. Stated as a gap, not filled. **[R]**

## 7. Afterlife of the texts

- **1050, Vercelli.** The eucharistic treatise circulates **misattributed to John Scotus Eriugena**; Berengar of Tours uses it under that name and is condemned at the Council of Vercelli. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'.]**
- **c. 1100.** A scribal error renders his name **"Bertramus,"** and the corruption persists into the nineteenth century. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'.]** The Maurist *Histoire Littéraire de la France* (tome v, p. 333) opens on this point: "Ratramn, whom many, by disfiguring his true name, have called Bertram, rendered himself very celebrated among the writers of his age." **[A]**
- **1531 or 1532, first printing.** English Wikipedia gives 1531; German Wikipedia gives 1532. **Neither names a city or a printer**, so the pool's **Cologne is unconfirmed** and is hedged inside `date_confidence`. The text had not circulated under his own name before this: it went as Eriugena's, and as "Bertramus."
- **Reformation reception. [A]** Protestant readers seize on the treatise against transubstantiation. **Nicholas Ridley** reads it at Cambridge in the mid-1540s and credits it with turning him; **Cranmer** credits Ratramnus with deciding him. (MacCulloch, *Thomas Cranmer*, 1996; Wikipedia, 'Ratramnus'.) **That is Ridley's and Cranmer's traveling**, so it sits on the book's own stop, never as a scene in England.
- **1790.** Corbie is suppressed. Its church survives, portal and western towers, and is shelled in April-May 1918. Over 200 manuscripts survive, scattered among **Amiens, Paris, and St Petersburg**, the last carried east by **Petr Dubrovsky**. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Corbie Abbey'.]**
- **Modern reception. CORRECTION.** The pool dated Scott G. Bruce's widely cited English translation to **2015**. Bruce's "Hagiography as Monstrous Ethnography: A Note on Ratramnus of Corbie's Letter Concerning the Conversion of the Cynocephali" appeared in **2006**, in the Herren festschrift *Insignis Sophiae Arcator* (Brepols). The imprint could not be re-verified in this pass, so check a catalogue, but 2015 should not stand.

## 9. Honest gaps

1. **No vita.** Nothing about his family, birth, entry, face, or voice. Everything personal here is inference from the books.
2. **No burial.** No tomb, epitaph, or record of where in Corbie he lies.
3. **No English translation of the *Epistola de Cynocephalis* was reachable.** Searched: archive.org, Fordham's medieval sourcebook, academic aggregators, and medievalists' blogs including Jonathan Jarrett's tenthmedieval.wordpress.com (which discusses Ian Wood on the letter without reproducing translated text). The one archive.org item purporting to be a translation (`letter-on-the-cynocephali`) is **explicitly AI-generated** and was excluded rather than tagged **[A]**. So **the journey file quotes the cynocephali letter nowhere**, only the eucharistic treatise and the Maurist biography. A real hole, left as one.
4. **Rimbert's side of the correspondence** is not reachable here; his question is known through Ratramnus's restatement and the Rimbert literature.
5. **The dating of *Contra Graecorum Opposita*** (863-867 against an 867 provocation) and **Paschasius's abbacy end date** are both unresolved between sources.

## 10. Atlas interlocks

**Used in a campa (one only).** `san_cristobal.journey.json` carries "Corbie, Ratramnus answers the missionary" at 49.9086, 2.5083, dated `0865-01-01`; that pin is inherited byte-identical for the five cynocephali stops. Ratramnus **cites Christopher's Life inside the Epistola**, a real one-directional relation, so Saint Christopher is named. The episode is expanded, not duplicated.

**Pointing the other way, and NOT named in any campa here.** These files cite him; he could not have written about any of them, so naming them would be back-projection: `ibn_battuta.journey.json` (Barahnakar, 1345, "Ratramnus of Corbie had ruled five centuries earlier that such men could be baptized"), `fyodor_yevtishchev.journey.json` (1884), `gustave_flaubert.journey.json` (1874), `laura_renee_maier.journey.json` (2022), `matias_carbone.journey.json` (2023).

**In the corpus as a source, not a traveler:** Hincmar of Reims, cited in `charles_martel.journey.json` and `clovis.journey.json`. He is named in campas here as Ratramnus's actual adversary in the predestination dispute, not for shared geography.

**Gaps this subject exposes:** **Paschasius Radbertus** is in no journey file and not in QUEUE.md, despite being the other half of the controversy and a monk of the same house. **Ansgar** appears nowhere under his own name. **Rimbert** is queued and unresearched, paired with Ratramnus in QUEUE.md, each entry naming the other. The queue also holds an untouched cynocephalus cluster (Jacobus de Voragine, Ctesias, Megasthenes, Aelianus, Paul the Deacon, Solinus, Odoric, Herodotus, Saint Ahrakas, Saint Augani, and **Adam of Bremen**, whose *Gesta Hammaburgensis* describes northern cynocephali in Rimbert's own territory). The **Kaaba** and **Temple Mount** pins have no documented connection to any of this, confirmed by grep, and are deliberately absent.
