# Carl Friedrich Gauss (1777-1855): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `gauss`. Companion: `gauss.journey.json`.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition. **[B]** cited, but second-hand or unopened. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Corrected coordinates are given with their source, the wrong value preserved so the error stays traceable.

**Method.** The pool arrived already adversarially checked, and its corrections matter more than the claims they correct. Much of the standard Gauss furniture is wrong in ways that propagate: MacTutor compresses two events into a sentence and the compression becomes a date; one bad Göttingen coordinate is reused for five places; the German "original" of his most famous line is a back-translation.

## 1. Brunswick, 1777-1795

**Birth**, 30 April 1777, to a bricklayer's family. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia; Dunnington 1955]**

**Birth house, corrected.** Pool gave 52.2625, 10.5211 and said the house was demolished in the 1890s. Both wrong. Wilhelmstrasse 30, formerly the Wendengraben, is at **52.2681, 10.5266** (de.wikipedia); the pool point lies ~720 m southwest, and the half-timbered house survived, opened as a Gauss museum in 1929, and was destroyed in the Second World War. **[A]** The same wrong pair was reused for "Gauss's residence, Brunswick" in 1801.

**The summation anecdote.** At about seven, in J. G. Büttner's class at the St. Catherine's parish school, Gauss reportedly sums 1 to 100 by pairing terms. **[R]** Earliest source: Sartorius von Waltershausen (1856), writing a year after Gauss died; nothing from Gauss's hand survives, and the details vary across retellings. **Corrected**: St. Katharinen is at **52.2675, 10.5253** (de.wikipedia); the pool's 52.2635, 10.5289 is ~550 m southeast.

**1788** Gymnasium, with Büttner and the assistant Martin Bartels. **1792** Collegium Carolinum on the Duke's stipend, where Gauss independently gets Bode's law, the binomial theorem, the arithmetic-geometric mean, quadratic reciprocity and the prime number theorem. **[A: MacTutor; Dunnington]** **Ducal audience, 1791, anachronism corrected**: Duke Carl Wilhelm Ferdinand grants the stipend **[A: Dunnington]**, but the pool calls the site the *Residenzschloss*, when the building standing in 1791 was Hermann Korb's palace of 1718, burned 1830, Ottmer's Residenzschloss dating from 1841. Corrected to **52.2634, 10.5275** (en.wikipedia); pool's 52.2622, 10.5231 is ~330 m southwest.

## 2. Göttingen and Helmstedt, 1795-1801

**Oct 1795**, matriculation under Abraham Gotthelf Kästner; the Farkas Bolyai correspondence begins. **[A: MacTutor]** **30 March 1796**, the regular seventeen-gon, first entry of the mathematical diary. **[A: Gauss, *Mathematisches Tagebuch*]**

**Conflation corrected.** The pool dated both the 17-gon and the departure from Göttingen to 1796. MacTutor verbatim: "Gauss left Göttingen in 1798 without a diploma, but by this time he had made one of his most important discoveries - the construction of a regular 17-gon by ruler and compasses." Discovery 1796, departure **1798**; separate stops in the journey file. **Gap**: his student lodging address is not established anywhere consulted, so the journey file uses an approximate point and says so.

**1799**, doctorate from Helmstedt, dissertation on the fundamental theorem of algebra, assessed by Johann Friedrich Pfaff. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia; Dunnington]** Dunnington has it taken *in absentia* with Pfaff as nominal supervisor; MacTutor neither confirms nor contradicts. Not adjudicated. **1801**, *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*. **[A]**

## 3. Ceres, 1801

The most-corrected item in the pool.

**Attested.** From Piazzi's few observations of the lost planetoid, Gauss computes an orbit and predicts a recovery position; astronomers refind Ceres almost exactly there. **[A: MacTutor; Zach]** **1809**, Lalande Prize. **[A: Wikipedia]**

**Correction 1, method.** The pool credits "the method of least squares." Anachronistic: Gauss used a new method of **orbit determination from three observations**, and did not publish least squares until *Theoria motus* (1809), Legendre having published first in 1805.

**Correction 2, date, contradiction flagged.** MacTutor: 7 December 1801. en.wikipedia: "On 31 December 1801, von Zach and fellow celestial policeman Heinrich W. M. Olbers found Ceres near the predicted position," Olbers confirming 1-2 January 1802. The 7 December sighting was a single inconclusive observation frustrated by weather. The journey file uses 31 December and keeps MacTutor's date in `date_confidence`.

**Correction 3, vocabulary.** "Dwarf planet" is a 2006 IAU category; the journey file says "planetoid."

## 4. Bremen, Paris, and the correspondents

**1802**, Bremen: Gauss visits Wilhelm Olbers and takes up the orbit of Pallas. **[A: MacTutor]** No coordinate for Olbers's house was established; the journey file pins central Bremen and says so.

**Two-year error corrected.** MacTutor bundles the Bessel and Germain correspondences into June 1802. Both begin in **1804**: Germain's first letter, as M. Le Blanc, is dated **21 November 1804** (en.wikipedia); Gauss and Bessel did not meet until 1825. **[A]** The Olbers visit and the Pallas work in 1802 stand.

**1807 Germain intervention, causal link corrected.** The pool says Germain intervened with General Joseph-Marie Pernety over a French war tax and that Gauss was spared it. Unsupported: she wrote to Pernety, a family friend, **fearing for Gauss's safety**, and Pernety sent a battalion chief to confirm he was unharmed. The 2,000-franc contribution was a separate matter, and Olbers and Laplace offered to pay it; Gauss declined. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

**Paris.** The Laplace/Legendre/Académie relation is correspondence, not travel: no source places Gauss in Paris. The journey file uses the canonical pin **48.8566, 2.3522** and says in that stop's `date_confidence` that Gauss is not there. **[R]** for the framing.

## 5. Marriage, loss, observatory, 1805-1816

**9 Oct 1805**, marriage to Johanna Elisabeth Rosina Osthoff. **[A]** **Venue is a gap**: the pool named St. Catherine's Church, but de.wikipedia's Gauss article gives only the date and the St. Katharinen article never mentions Gauss, so the journey file drops the church. **1806** Joseph born; **10 Nov 1806** the Duke, mortally wounded at Jena-Auerstedt, dies at Ottensen in Altona in flight from the French, the duchy abolished and the patronage ended **[A: Dunnington]** (53.5522, 9.9247). **Nov 1807**, full professor and director of the Göttingen observatory until death **[A: Wikipedia]**, the building unfinished until 1816 so that he works from provisional quarters.

**1808** Wilhelmina born, Gauss's father dies. **1809** Louis born, *Theoria motus* published; **11 Oct 1809** Johanna dies, Louis soon after. **[A]** **Place corrected**: the pool labels the death site "Göttingen Observatory residence," anachronistic since the observatory was unfinished until 1816 and Gauss not resident until 1815. **4 Aug 1810**, marriage to Wilhelmine (Minna) Waldeck **[A]**, church unknown; children Eugen 1811, Wilhelm 1813, Therese 1816. **1816** observatory completed, **month dropped**: the pool's "1816-09" is unsupported, en.wikipedia having construction "extended from 1803 until 1816" and Gauss resident "between 1815 and 1855."

**Observatory coordinate, a five-fold error.** The pool gave 51.5323, 9.9367 and reused it for four more stops. The Alte Sternwarte on the Geismar Landstrasse is at **51.5285, 9.9433** (en.wikipedia); the pool point sits ~600 m northwest inside the old town. Corrected throughout.

## 6. The survey of Hanover, 1818-1832

Commissioned to triangulate Hanover and tie it to the Danish grid; Gauss accepts and directs the fieldwork personally. **[A: MacTutor; Dunnington]** **1821**, the heliotrope. **[A]**

**Three stations, three coordinate corrections.** Hoher Hagen **51.4739, 9.7658**, against the pool's 51.4256, 9.7864 (twice) and 51.4886, 9.7614, wrong by 5.5 km and 1.7 km: **three different coordinates for one hill, all three wrong.** Grosser Inselsberg **50.8511, 10.4658**, against 50.8508, 10.4525, 0.9 km off the summit. St. Michaelis, Lüneburg **53.2481, 10.4053**, against 53.2483, 10.4139, 570 m too far east. The Brocken (51.7997, 10.6151) was the one summit the pool got right.

**St. Michaelis, tag upgraded to [A], date fixed to 1823.** The pool tagged it [R] as survey-history literature; it is documented, de.wikipedia having Gauss "nutzte er 1823 den Kirchturm von St. Michaelis als Vermessungspunkt," with his son still using the station in 1830. **The great triangle** (Hoher Hagen, Brocken, Inselsberg): the geodetic purpose is documented, the 180-degree test inferred from it and carried as **[R]**. **Deister ridge**, 52.2833, 9.4167: a station is confirmed in general accounts, precise coordinates unverified **[R]**, so no stop was placed. **Altona** (53.5461, 9.9354): Schumacher reconciles the Hanoverian triangulation with the Danish arc. **[A: *Briefwechsel*, not opened]**

**Copenhagen prize, title corrected.** MacTutor: "In 1822 Gauss won the Copenhagen University Prize with *Theoria attractionis*." Wrong and not to be propagated: the 1822 prize essay is the conformal-mapping memoir *Allgemeine Auflösung der Aufgabe die Theile einer gegebnen Fläche...* (1825), while *Theoria attractionis corporum sphaeroidicorum* (1813) is a separate work on ellipsoids. **1823** *Theoria combinationis observationum*; **1828** *Disquisitiones generales circa superficies curvas*, Gaussian curvature and the theorema egregium. **[A: MacTutor]**

## 7. Berlin, 1828

At Humboldt's urging Gauss attends the Naturforscherversammlung and lodges with him; he dislikes the travel and the crowds and does not repeat it. **[A: Dunnington]** (52.517, 13.3888; Humboldt's exact address is a gap, so the point marks central Berlin near Unter den Linden.) That he appeared at academy-linked sessions is **[R]**, inferred from the programme and Dunnington, no building-level record verified.

## 8. Weber, magnetism, telegraph, 1831-1841

**1831** Weber arrives as professor of physics, Gauss having known him since 1828; **12 Sept 1831** Minna dies. **1832** magnetic work begins at Humboldt's prompting: *Intensitas vis magneticae terrestris ad mensuram absolutam revocata*, the first absolute measurement of the Earth's field. **1833** the telegraph line, roughly a kilometre of wire between observatory and physics institute, the magnetometer, and the iron-free magnetic observatory; the rooftop routing is not preserved, a gap, so the journey file uses an approximate midpoint. **1836** *Erdmagnetismus und Magnetometer*, **1839** *Allgemeine Theorie des Erdmagnetismus*. **[A]**

**Magnetischer Verein, dates corrected.** The pool gives 1832-1841; de.wikipedia puts the coordinated programme at **1836-1841**, six years on 28 designated days a year across up to 50 observatories. The Verein did not exist in 1832, the year Gauss and Weber began their own work. Two facts, split in the journey file.

**Göttingen Seven, overstatement corrected.** The pool says Weber "is forced to leave Göttingen" after his December 1837 dismissal. Unlike the three banished from the kingdom, Weber was allowed to **remain as a private scholar**, leaving only in 1843 for Leipzig. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

**Bolyai reply, year corrected.** Pool and MacTutor give 1831, but that 1831 dispatch went astray in the post; Gauss got the resent copy in early 1832 and replied **6 March 1832**. **[A: en.wikipedia]** MacTutor's date is the sending, not the receipt.

**Mother's age corrected.** Dorothea Gauss, resident with her son since 1817, born 1743, dies 1839, called by de.wikipedia "die 96-Jährige": 95 or 96, not the pool's "nearly ninety-seven." The pool's **18 April 1839 could not be confirmed** and is not asserted. **1838** Copley Medal **[A: Wikipedia]**; Gauss is not recorded as travelling to receive it.

## 9. Death, 1855

**1849** golden jubilee lecture. **1854** last scientific exchange, with Gerling, on a modified Foucault pendulum. **23 Feb 1855** dies in his sleep at Göttingen, recorded as a heart attack, aged 77. **24 Feb 1855** brain removed and preserved by Rudolf Wagner, mass 1,492 g. **[A]**

**Unit error of four orders of magnitude.** The pool gives the cortical surface as 219,588 **square centimetres**, which would be 21.96 m², impossible. en.wikipedia: "Wagner's son Hermann, a geographer, estimated the cerebral area to be 219,588 square **millimetres**," about 2,196 cm². The measurement is **Hermann** Wagner's; Rudolf is responsible for removal, preservation and mass.

**Burial, Albanifriedhof, corrected to 51.5319, 9.9419** (de.wikipedia). The pool held **two different wrong pairs**, 51.5286, 9.9515 and 51.5325, 9.9351. The **burial date of 26 Feb 1855 was not confirmed** anywhere consulted. The cemetery was consecrated 1783 and closed to new burials in 1881; 30 monuments survive, oldest from 1816, so the pool's "only a handful survive" is too weak.

**Gravestone not contemporaneous.** The pool dates it 1855; it was erected **January 1859**, neo-Gothic granite by Heinrich Köhler with a bronze medallion by Heinrich Hesemann. **[A: de.wikipedia]**

## 10. Afterlife

**Heptadecagon on the tombstone.** Gauss asked for it and the mason is said to have refused, judging it would read as a circle. **[R]** Widely repeated, the documentary trail thin. **The wish honoured elsewhere**: the Braunschweig monument, unveiled **27 June 1880** by Fritz Schaper, cast by Hermann Heinrich Howaldt, carries a golden regular heptadecagon on the western face of its red granite pedestal, on the southwestern slope of the Gaussberg at **52.2720, 10.5226** (de.wikipedia); the pool's 52.2689, 10.5268 is ~500 m off.

**The brain that was not his.** 2013: Renate Schweizer at the Max Planck Institute, using Jens Frahm's 1998 MRI scans, shows the specimen studied as Gauss's belonged to the physician Conrad Heinrich Fuchs, the two mislabelled probably soon after Wagner's 1860-62 examinations. **[A: MPI; *Brain*]** Exact date of the mix-up: a gap.

**Also [A]**: the *Werke*, Göttingen academy, from 1863, twelve volumes; the mathematical diary, 1796-1814, unknown outside the family until brought to Felix Klein around 1898 (**[R]** as to who recovered it and from where); the lunar crater Gauss, selenographic only and so not a stop.

**Two contradictions, unresolved** (see the gaps list below): 1965 against 1966 for Clarke's English *Disquisitiones*, and 1936 naming against the pool's 1930 IEC adoption for the unit.

**Gaussturm, Hoher Hagen: construction date unknown.** No consulted source gives one; the pool's 1909 is uncorroborated. Not asserted, and not used as a stop.

## 11. Quotations: what survives verification

**Clean [A]** (MacTutor, Wikiquote, Wikipedia; underlying letters not fetched): Farkas Bolyai 1808, "the act of learning"; Gerling 1832, "a genius of the first rank"; the 1816 review; Olbers c. 1809, "the arms of your friendship"; Dedekind on Gauss lecturing and Klein on rigour, about him rather than by him.

**Repaired before use:**

1. **Queen of the sciences.** The circulating German "original" is a back-translation: Sartorius 1856 (p. 79) writes *Arithmetik*, not *Zahlentheorie*, and in **indirect speech**.
2. **Lawyers and half proofs**, to Olbers 14 May 1826: the submitted text was truncated, dropping "and it is demanded for proof that every doubt becomes impossible."
3. **"...space has a reality outside our minds"**, to Bessel 1830: Wikiquote has "**properties** a priori," the submitted text "laws," defensible for *Gesetze* but in no fetched source.
4. **"to praise it would mean to praise myself."** MacTutor dates 1831; the reply is **6 March 1832**.
5. **Laplace**, clipped and with the wrong preposition: "...the greatest mathematician of Germany, and Gauss is the greatest mathematician **of Europe**." The point is the contrast with Pfaff. Second-hand in *Nouvelle Biographie Générale*, vol. 19, p. 686. **[B].**
6. **The fox effacing his tracks.** The **Abel attribution is unsupported**: reported by Christopher Hansteen, *Illustreret Nyhedsblad*, 2 March 1862, crediting an unnamed German student. **[A for Hansteen; R for Abel]**
7. **"by the grace of the Lord."** Pool tagged [R] for want of a citation; wrong, Wikipedia cites Göttingen Academy record 731, Olbers letter of 3 September 1805. **[B].**
8. **"God arithmetizes."** Also wrongly [R], the Jacobi rival attribution unfounded: Sartorius 1856, p. 97, has it in **Greek**, ὁ θεὸς ἀριθμητίζει. **Upgraded to [A/B].**
9. **"I have had my results for a long time..."** Real wording, wrong provenance: not an 1808 Bolyai letter but Agnes Arber, *The Mind and the Eye* (1954). **Stays [R]**, unused.

## 12. Corpus interlocks

- **Göttingen**, canonical pin **51.534, 9.935**, inherited byte-identical. `heisenberg.journey.json`, `niels_bohr.journey.json` and `robert_oppenheimer.journey.json` stand where the Gauss-Weber telegraph ran; `froebel.journey.json` and `johann_jahn.journey.json` put both men on the same campus during Gauss's residency.
- **`humboldt.journey.json`** crosses twice and genuinely: Humboldt read at Göttingen in the Forster circle, hosted Gauss in Berlin in 1828, and prompted the magnetism programme.
- **Hanover**, canonical pin **52.3745, 9.7386**, inherited from `mary_wigman.journey.json`, shared with `karl_marx.journey.json` (Kugelmann's, *Capital* proofs, 1867) and `savitri_devi.journey.json`: the kingdom Gauss triangulated.
- **`lord_kelvin.journey.json`** sources Tait's knot theory to "History of Knot Theory from Gauss to Jones." **`kyot-willehalm.report.md`** computes 13 May 848 by "the standard Gauss/Meeus algorithm."

**False positives, flagged.** `freud.journey.json` names a "Rabbi Hanover" at the Wandsbek synagogue, a surname; `bernard_clairvaux.journey.json` cites `history.hanover.edu`, Hanover College, Indiana. **Negative finding**: no journey file places Gauss, or anyone documented as having met him, at the Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), the Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354) or Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816), and those pins were not forced. **Census**: `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md`, row 40, Gauss at 6 mentions, tied with Virgil.
