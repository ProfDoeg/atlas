# Arthur Bispo do Rosário: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Brazilian artist, sailor, boxer, domestic servant, psychiatric inmate. Born Japaratuba, Sergipe, 1909 or 1911; died Jacarepaguá, Rio de Janeiro, 5 July 1989. Fifty years inside one institution, the Colônia Juliano Moreira, where almost the whole surviving body of work was made.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including claims that circulate in secondary retellings without a locatable document. Contradictions are printed and left open. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**The sourcing floor.** The base is thin. The two encyclopedia articles carry the chronology and disagree on the birth date, the employer's name, the end of the Light employment, the cause of death, and the year the work reached Venice. The one piece of scholarly apparatus reachable here is Marlon Miguel's chapter in *Cultural Inquiry* 17 (2020), cited below. The museum site is live and supplies the institutional history. The rest is press repetition.

## 1. Sergipe, to 1925

**Birth, Japaratuba.** English Wikipedia: 14 May 1909, with 16 March 1911 also cited as an alternate. Portuguese Wikipedia: 16 March 1911. Museum biography: 1909. **[A on the place; contradictory on the date, not adjudicated.]** An earlier pass carried a third date, "10 May 1911," attributed to Portuguese Wikipedia. It appears in no source and is struck. There is no third date.

**Coordinate correction.** Japaratuba is at **-10.5928, -36.9400** (pt.wikipedia: 10°35'34"S, 36°56'24"W). An earlier pass carried -36.8508, about 10 km east.

**Parents and fostering.** Adriano Bispo do Rosário and Blandina Francisca de Jesus **[A, pt.wikipedia]**. Raised and schooled by a cacau-farming couple of the region **[R: stated without naming the couple or the farm]**. No source dates the departure from the farm. Gap.

**February 1925, Escola de Aprendizes de Marinheiros, Aracaju** **[A, pt.wikipedia]**. The school's building was not established; the coordinate is the city center, flagged as approximate.

## 2. The Marinha de Guerra, 1925-1933

**December 1925, enlistment, nine-year term, Rio de Janeiro.** Portuguese Wikipedia splits the year (February school, December enlistment); English Wikipedia gives 1925 undivided **[A, both]**.

**The ships are not documented.** No vessel, posting, or cruise itinerary appears in anything reachable. The span and its ending are attested; everything inside it is **[R]**.

**Montevideo and Buenos Aires.** Secondary retellings place him at River Plate ports. No log or muster roll was located **[R, unverified]**. Both are entered in the journey file as stops with the hedge in `date_confidence`, because omitting them would tidy the life and including them unmarked would forge one. The atlas's canonical Buenos Aires pin (-34.6037, -58.3816) is **not** used: the interlock lens returned an explicit negative finding that Bispo's itinerary touches none of the canonical Paris, Buenos Aires, Kaaba, or Temple Mount anchors. The coordinates used are dockside values.

**1928 onward, boxing**, lightweight and middleweight, by some accounts nationally **[A on the career and divisions, pt.wikipedia; R on national competition; venues unspecified]**.

**1933, discharge for indiscipline** **[A, pt.wikipedia]**. The infractions are not itemized anywhere reachable.

## 3. Rio de Janeiro, 1933-1938

**Light and Power tram department**, washing streetcars and mending tires: 1933-1937 (en) or 1933-1936 (pt) **[A on both statements; the end date is a contradiction, left open]**. Depot address unconfirmed; the coordinate is Botafogo, associated with the company's works, an approximation.

**1936, work accident**: fractured foot, two surgeries, permanent limp **[A, pt.wikipedia]**. No treating hospital, medical file, or compensation record located.

**1937, the household in Botafogo** **[A on year, district, and employment; en.wikipedia and the museum biography]**.

*Correction.* The employer is **Humberto Leone** (museum) / **Humberto Magalhães Leoni** (en). An earlier pass gave "José Maria Leone," supported by nothing; struck. The museum supplies the causal link the encyclopedias omit: Leone was the lawyer in the litigation over the 1936 accident, which is how the injured man came into the lawyer's house.

## 4. December 1938

**22 December, Mosteiro de São Bento.** Hallucinations lead him to his employer and then to the Benedictine monastery, where he declares himself Jesus Christ, sent by God to judge the living and the dead **[A on episode, date, and place; both encyclopedias]**. Accounts of the vision's content vary (seven blue angels in some, a bare commission in others): recorded, not adjudicated.

**24 December, arrest and committal** to the Hospício Pedro II at Praia Vermelha. English Wikipedia gives the day; Portuguese Wikipedia gives late December without one **[A, both]**.

The 1938 police record describes him as a **"wandering black beggar [indigent], bearing no documents"** **[A; Miguel note 529, via Hidalgo, *O senhor do labirinto*, p. 18]**. Institutional record about him, not his speech.

**Coordinate correction.** The Hospício Pedro II / Hospital Nacional de Alienados building is the present UFRJ Praia Vermelha campus, approximately **-22.9553, -43.1668**. An earlier pass used -22.9508, -43.1665, about 500 m north.

**The theological reading.** A circulated tradition frames the crisis as a vision ordering him to reconstruct the world in miniature and in textile **[R]**: interpretation built on the documented incident, not a separate event, and not presented as one in the journey file.

## 5. Colônia Juliano Moreira, 1939-1989

**January 1939**, transfer from Praia Vermelha, diagnosis of paranoid schizophrenia, registration as patient **01662** **[A, both encyclopedias]**.

**Coordinate correction, propagated.** Portuguese Wikipedia locates the Colônia at 22°56'12"S, 43°23'56"W, that is **-22.9367, -43.3989**, in Curicica. Earlier passes used -22.9447, -43.3697 across seven entries (about 3 km east) and -22.9515, -43.3716 elsewhere. All Colônia stops now carry the corrected value. The museum's postal address (Estr. Rodrigues Caldas 3400, Taquara) is a different point in the same complex, pinned separately at -22.9497, -43.3776; the divergence is noted, not averaged.

**The institution.** Colonial Engenho da Taquara, later Fazenda Nossa Senhora dos Remédios (mill and chapel from c.1660, separated 1664, "Engenho Novo da Taquara" by 1778); land expropriated 1912-1918; opened 1924 as the Colônia de Psicopatas Homens de Jacarepaguá; renamed for Juliano Moreira in 1935 after his death **[A, museum "Colônia Juliano Moreira" page and pt.wikipedia, same title]**. *Contradiction:* construction begins 1919 (museum) or 1920 (pt.wikipedia). One year, open.

**The making.** He unravels the blue thread of discarded Navy uniforms and embroiders words, maps, saints, cities, and inventories onto found cloth, and assembles objects from institutional waste **[A on the practice; R on when it begins]**. No document fixes a start year. An earlier pass anchored it to 1939, which nothing supports; the museum places the decisive production after a 1964 punishment and vision, and pt.wikipedia dates the first showing to 1982 and the surviving corpus at roughly **802 works**. The journey file carries the practice as 1960s-onward, with the uncertainty in `date_confidence`.

**27 November 1943, O Cruzeiro.** David Nasser and Jean Manzon publish "Os loucos serão felizes?", *O Cruzeiro* n. 5, p. 38, shot inside the Colônia, digitized in the Hemeroteca Digital Brasileira **[A]**.

*This is the most-corrected item in the pool.* The famous line **"Eu sou o enviado da providência. Nasci numa estrela e venho salvar a humanidade"** is real and traceable, but it is **a caption written by Nasser**, printed as: "Temores celestiais assustam alguns. Manias do poder divino. 'Eu sou o enviado da providência. Nasci numa estrela e venho salvar a humanidade'. Este teceu o seu manto divino e prepara-se para a viagem às paragens da eternidade." The article names nobody; pt.wikipedia says the caption "possivelmente" refers to him; English Wikipedia does not carry it at all. It must never be presented as his own declared mission, and the journey file uses it only in that frame.

**1940s-1960s, the alternation** between confinement and domestic work in private houses **[A, pt.wikipedia]**. Secondary retellings add escapes and recaptures; no departure or return is dated in anything reached **[R]**.

**1961-1964, the AMIU.** Attic room at the Assistência Municipal de Infância e Adolescência pediatric clinic **[A, pt.wikipedia]**. Address not located; the coordinate is central Rio, flagged.

**1964, the permanent return** to the Colônia for the rest of his life **[A, pt.wikipedia and the museum biography, which states that by 1964 he permanently remained there]**. An earlier pass tagged this **[R]**, an inference from the end of the AMIU residence; the downgrade was wrong and is reversed.

## 6. The works, undated

**Manto da Apresentação.** A cape hung with applied insignia and stitched cloth, to be worn when he presents himself before God on Judgment Day **[A that it exists, is his best-known work, and has this declared purpose: both encyclopedias and the museum. R on any completion date: none established]**.

**Jangada (Raft)**, Museu Bispo do Rosário Collection, inscribed **"[It] represents the maritime departments of all states. Universe. Raft."** **[A, Miguel p. 266, Figure 7]**. *Correction:* an earlier pass called the work "Balsa," treated the words as spoken description, and cited p. 28. The work is **Jangada**; the words are **written on the object**, part of the piece rather than commentary on it; the page is **266** ("28" was an internal PDF index). No footnote attaches to the quotation.

**Eu preciso destas palavras. Escrita** ("I need these words. Writing"), Figure 2, same collection. Embroidered: **"No Peito Traz Água É Nome"**, which Miguel renders **"In The Chest Carries Water Is the Word"** and glosses, hesitating in print over whether it should be ordered at all, as "In the chest, water is carried, that is the word" **[A, Miguel pp. 271-272]**. *Correction:* an earlier pass rendered the ending "is name," called the source a figure caption, and cited p. 276. Paulo Herkenhoff, cited on the same pages, notes the banner also carries "Africa," "afazeres," "aflito," and "fogamento," probably "afogamento," drowning, misspelled.

**The mission, in his own words:** **"My mission is that, it is to achieve that, what I have to the following day is to represent the existence of Earth that is there, everything I did. [...] I will be preparing and packing [encaixotando, boxing] the things. Because the order is to pack."** **[A, Miguel note 532.]** The broken syntax is close translation of transcribed speech and is left untouched.

**"slave" / "slave of the Lord,"** his own term for the compulsion to work at the voices' command **[A, Miguel note 528; identical across two independent fetches]**.

**"The voices tell me to do this way."** **[R; Bruno Hoffman, Almanaque Brasil, via English Wikipedia. Popular secondary reference; original Portuguese not located.]**

**"One day I simply appeared."** **[R, downgraded and not used as a quotation: repeated fetches of Miguel's chapter were inconsistent on whether this is Bispo's speech or the author's paraphrase.]**

## 7. Recognition and death, 1982-1989

**1982, Margem da Vida, MAM Rio.** First public showing, a group exhibition; the museum describes it as the only exhibition of his work held in his lifetime **[A, museum biography]**.

**1982, Frederico Morais** is credited with carrying the work into Rio's art conversation **[R: widely repeated; the site and circumstances of a first viewing inside the Colônia are unspecified in anything reached]**. His characterization, **"Total inventory of a certain stage of Brazilian society,"** is attested **[A, Miguel note 539]**.

**1982, O Prisioneiro da Passagem**, documentary **[A, pt.wikipedia]**. No credits or first screening located.

**5 July 1989, death. Contradiction, unresolved.** Bronchopneumonia at Hospital Manfredini (pt) vs heart attack at the Colônia (en) **[A on the date from both]**. The conflict of place dissolves: pt.wikipedia's Colônia article documents Manfredini as a 60-bed psychiatric hospital **inside the grounds**, converted in 2022 into the Clínica da Família Arthur Bispo do Rosário. Same complex; the cause alone stays open.

**The grave is a gap.** No source gives the location or fate of his tomb. Both Wikipedias, museubispodorosario.com, and several Brazilian press domains (blocked or 404) were searched. No cemetery is named here, and none should be invented.

**1989, Registros de Minha Passagem na Terra**, Escola de Artes Visuais do Parque Lage: first solo exhibition, posthumous, in the year of his death **[A, pt.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the title is "na Terra," not "pela Terra," and the show is posthumous.

## 8. Afterlife

- **26 April 1994**, INEPAC registers the entire body of work as cultural patrimony **[A, pt.wikipedia]**.
- **Venice. Contradiction, unresolved.** 1995 and first critical recognition (en) vs the Biennale of 2013 (pt). The page for the 46th Biennale does not mention him. Neither date confirmed **[A that the claims exist; R as to which is true]**.
- **The museum.** Founded 1982 on the Colônia grounds as the **Museu Nise da Silveira**; renamed Museu Bispo do Rosário in **2000**; "Arte Contemporânea" added **2002** **[A, museum "Colônia Juliano Moreira" page]**. *Correction:* an earlier pass hedged these dates and implied the founding followed a Morais rediscovery of Bispo. It did not; the institution was founded for Nise da Silveira and took his name eighteen years later. Collection pages name Manto da Apresentação, Grande Veleiro, Moedas I, Eu Vim.
- **1997, 1999, 2018**, Rio samba school tributes; in 2018 Acadêmicos do Cubango parades **"O Rei que Bordou o Mundo"** **[A, pt.wikipedia]**.
- **2009**, posthumous Order of Cultural Merit **[A, pt.wikipedia]**.
- **A statue at Japaratuba** **[A, both encyclopedias]**. No dedication date in either, and none found. Gap.
- **2020, Daniel Mason**, *A Registry of My Passage Upon the Earth* (Little, Brown): a **short-story collection**, not a short story, whose title translates the phrase Bispo embroidered **[A on book, publisher, year, title provenance; R on "inspired by his life"]**. Neither Mason's page nor the listing confirms an individual story about him. Mason is a psychiatrist as well as a novelist, likely why English Wikipedia links them.
- **Louise Bourgeois on Bispo:** **"He was looking for order in chaos, for the structure and rhythm in time and thought."** **[A, Miguel note 544; also cited to *Arthur Bispo do Rosário*, ed. Araújo, p. 27.]** About him, not by him.
- ***Um Canto, Dois Sertões*** reportedly exists in an English edition **[R, from a filename on the museum's Publicações page; not opened]**.

## Contradictions, left open

1. **Birth date.** 1909-05-14 (en, museum) vs 1911-03-16 (pt, and an alternate in en). The journey file uses 1909-05-14 with the conflict in `date_confidence`.
2. **End of the Light employment.** 1937 (en) vs 1936 (pt).
3. **Employer's name.** Leoni (en) vs Leone (museum): one man, two spellings. "José Maria Leone" was an error of an earlier pass, not a source conflict.
4. **Arrest date.** 24 December 1938 (en) vs late December, no day (pt).
5. **Cause of death.** Heart attack (en) vs bronchopneumonia (pt). Place resolved, cause open.
6. **Venice.** 1995 (en) vs 2013 (pt). 7. **Colônia construction start.** 1919 (museum) vs 1920 (pt).

## Gaps, stated as gaps

The year he left the farm for Aracaju. The ships and postings of nine years of service (not one vessel named). Whether he ever called at Montevideo or Buenos Aires. The start year of the textile practice. Any dated escape or return before 1961. The addresses of the Light depot, the Leone household, and the AMIU clinic. The completion date of the Manto. His grave. The dedication date of the Japaratuba statue.

## Interlock with the corpus

The census classes him "Brazilian outsider textile artist," the only descriptor of its kind, with Chiachio & Giannone and Duwenavue Sante' Johnson queued on the same embroidery thread **[A, census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md line 164; QUEUE.md lines 259, 261, 290]**. That is a craft lineage. The stronger crossings are named in campa:

- **claude_levi_strauss**, in Brazil 1935-1939 (São Paulo chair, Mato Grosso and Nambikwara), exactly the years Bispo washes trams, breaks his foot, and is committed. No meeting; overlap only **[R]**. Botafogo stop.
- **augusto_boal**, born Penha 1931, Rio-based for his final decades, seized off a São Paulo street in 1971 and tortured while Bispo is held by the medical arm of the same state **[A, augusto_boal.journey.json lines 12, 212, 224, 236, 366-426]**. Carnival stop.
- **artaud**, drawing at Rodez in the 1940s in which O Cruzeiro photographs the Colônia. Structural parallel, no contact **[R]**. 1943 stop.
- **michel_foucault**, *History of Madness* defended 1961, the year Bispo moves into the AMIU attic **[A, michel_foucault.journey.json lines 3, 135, 294]**. AMIU stop.
- **gilles_deleuze** and Guattari, *Anti-Oedipus* 1972, out of La Borde **[A on the book; R on the resonance with Bispo's map-flags; Deleuze is not documented as having visited La Borde, a gap flagged in that file]**. "Order to pack" stop.
- **jung** and **reich** were considered and left unnamed: real parallels, but neither touches Brazil or Bispo.

**Negative finding, confirmed.** No existing journey file places a traveler in Jacarepaguá or names the Colônia; the nearest Rio pins are all Boal's (Penha, Lapa, Botafogo, Cinelândia) **[R, augusto_boal.journey.json lines 12, 390, 414, 544]**. The Colônia is a new pin. The canonical Paris, Buenos Aires, Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), and Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354) anchors are not inheritable here and none is used.
