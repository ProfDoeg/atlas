# Wences Casares: research report

*Compiled 2026-09-09 for slug `wences_casares`. Subject living (b. 1974): the record is press, corporate filings, and his own retellings.*

**[A]** = attested, source reachable. **[R]** = reconstruction, uncorroborated self-report, or a claim surviving on repetition. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. Per-stop sourcing lives in the journey file's `sources` and `date_confidence` fields; this report carries the argument.

One fact governs the rest: **the strongest documents in this life were filed by other people's compliance departments.** Santander's RNS announcements, LifeLock's 8-K, Coinbase's S-1/A, PayPal's proxies and the Gibraltar FSC register are the only places where dates, sums and names are pinned. The childhood, the voyage, and the origin of his bitcoin conviction rest on his own account: repeated, consistent, corroborated by no second witness.

## 1. Patagonia, 1974-1991

**Birth date [A], three ways.** En.wikipedia's infobox reads verbatim `26 February 1974`; PayPal's 2016 DEF 14A gives "Age 42"; Pagina/12 of 1 Aug 1999 calls him 25. Mutually consistent, and as firm as anything here.

**Birthplace [R], irreconcilable.** Two Chubut towns 130 km apart: **Gobernador Costa** (en.wikipedia infobox) and **Esquel** (Infobae, 22 Apr 2018, and Diario Jornada, both fetched, same wording). *Not adjudicated.* No civil registry entry, certificate, baptismal or school record was located for either; some profiles hedge further ("in a field near Esquel"). Both are stops in the journey file, because press repetition is not evidence. *On the task brief:* it named **Rio Gallegos, Santa Cruz**, which nothing supports and which is the wrong province. The family ranch is likewise unnamed and unlocated in every source, so its stop carries a disclosed placeholder coordinate.

**Family losses [R, self-reported].** "Growing up I saw my family lose everything three times," each time because of "something that the government and or the bank did" (Unchained, Laura Shin, Feb 2018, two transcripts fetched). Infobae's version is childhood feeling, not quantified loss. **[A as an utterance; R as a description of financial events.]** *Correction:* a chronology item dated that quote to **1980**. Invented: it was given on 22 April 2018.

**The supermarket, 1989 [R, single witness].** The hyperinflation is public record. His mother arriving at school with two plastic bags of salary in cash, assigning each child an aisle, sending them back to spend the remainder, is his own account, told once. *Discrepancy left standing:* it says "me and my two sisters," against the eldest-of-four figure carried elsewhere.

**Two quotes corrected against the transcripts.** Both print "go in front of that person," not "be ahead of." And "and that everything else will follow" is in no transcript; the official text ends "the rest will follow."

**Rotary exchange year, Washington, Pennsylvania, c. 1991 [R].** In en.wikipedia and speaker-bureau biographies, all traceable to his own retelling. **Gap:** no Rotary district record, host family, or school name found.

## 2. San Andres, Internet Argentina, Patagon, 1992-1999

**University [R], contradictory.** El Cronista's "Lo que dejo Patagon" calls Casares and Constancio Larguia **graduates** of the Universidad de San Andres. The more widely repeated account, consistent with founding an ISP in 1994, has him leaving after about three years without a degree. *Not adjudicated; no registrar record reached.* *Name correction, applied throughout:* the co-founder is **Larguia**, not "Largia," per Pagina/12, El Cronista and es.wikipedia, all fetched.

**Internet Argentina S.A., 1994 [A as a company; R as "the first"].** Founding is attested in business press and in *Historia de la industria informatica argentina*. That it was literally Argentina's **first** commercial ISP is supported by no regulator's chronology reached here.

**Patagon, 1997 [A], two open questions.** *Capital:* reported variously as under US$10,000 and about US$30,000. **Contradiction left standing.** Larguia's line about selling a bicycle to fund his share is in Expansion (Mexico), 14 Sept 2011. *Third founder:* es.wikipedia names **Federico Agardy** alongside Casares and Larguia; Pagina/12 (1 Aug 1999, contemporaneous, fetched) instead names **Anibal Borra**, co-founder and programmer, by then in Sao Paulo. **Not adjudicated.** The clean two-man origin story is wrong in both tellings.

**Zsolt Agardy's investment, 1998: material correction.** The figure reaching this pass was "approximately US$1 million, bringing it to fully operating status." Pagina/12 (fetched) says Agardy, who owned Editorial Estrada and the brokerage InvestCapital, put in **200 mil dolares for 51 per cent**: one fifth of the money, and it bought **majority control**. The contemporaneous account is preferred. The surname recurs, since es.wikipedia's third co-founder is a *Federico* Agardy, and no source says whether they are related. **Open.** A US$53 million round in Dec 1999 appears in roadshow retrospectives only **[R]**.

## 3. Madrid: the Santander transaction, 2000-2002

**9 March 2000 [A, primary].** Santander's RNS (fetched): 75% of Patagon.com, buying out Chase Capital Partners, JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs, at a **US$705 million valuation**, the founding group retaining the balance. On 22 March Patagon is folded into Open Bank, Angel Corcostegui chairman, Casares managing director. *Money contradiction, flagged:* El Pais of 10 March gives the consideration as **91.5 billion pesetas**, roughly US$528 million for the 75%, which is not the headline valuation. Left standing.

**"He sold Patagon for $750 million" [R, and wrong].** La Nacion's contemporaneous interview (quotes fetched) has him saying he sold **not a single share**, remained a paper millionaire, lived on a **US$6,000 monthly salary**, and had not yet been able to buy a car. The popular figure conflates valuation, press rounding, and the 2002 buyback into a personal payout.

**Harvard Business School OPM, c. 2000-2002 [R].** Executive education, not a degree; attendance and the meeting with Pablo Bosch come from profiles of the Las Majadas partnership, with no HBS record reached and no program dates established.

**24 May 2002 [A, primary].** Santander's RNS disposal (fetched) and the CNMV filing: the bank buys the outstanding **11.41%** of Patagon Euro S.L. from Casares, Guillermo Kirchner and other minorities for **US$22.02m**, sells **Patagon America** to the two of them for **US$9.84m**, and charges **EUR 700m** against Q2, **EUR 616m** of it unamortised goodwill. A severe commercial failure for Santander, and no finding reached here establishes that Casares defrauded the bank.

## 4. Wanako, Banco Lemon, the Simpatica voyage, 2002-2007

**Marriage, Miami, 2002 [A].** Belle Wylie Holahan, met through **Endeavor**, where she worked (simpaticasail.com, fetched).

**Wanako Games, Santiago, 2002 [A, tag raised from R].** Wikipedia (Behaviour Santiago) confirms founders Esteban Sosnik, Tiburcio de la Carcova, Santiago Bilinkis and Casares, corroborated by GamesBeat. **Gap:** Casares's day-to-day role is documented nowhere.

**Banco Lemon, Brazil [R throughout].** Founded with Meyer "Micky" Malka, serving underbanked consumers through a correspondent network rather than branches; network acquired by **Banco do Brasil in 2009**. No incorporation record, no transaction announcement, **no public price**, and the perimeter of that sale unresolved.

**Diogenes's birth year: correction with downstream effect.** A birth year of **2004** reached this pass and is arithmetically impossible. Simpaticasail.com (fetched) has the marriage in 2002, Diogenes born "a year and a half later," the boat leaving "days after Dio's first birthday," and the voyage ending in February 2007 "almost three years after departing." Working back gives departure mid-2004 and **birth mid-2003**, which also fits the age of about 22 reported for him in the 2025 $LIBRA coverage.

**The voyage [A as route, R as dates].** Miami mid-2004; the Marquesas, Tuamotus and Society Islands; **Theodore born in Auckland** about a year in; Indonesia, Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand; Sri Lanka, Oman, India, Egypt, Israel; Suez; Miami **February 2007**, family then in Woodside, California. **Gap:** the site itemises the route and dates not one call, so every intermediate date in the journey file is an ordering device and says so.

**Las Majadas de Pirque, 2006 [A]: date-field error corrected.** The item reached this pass with a date field of **1906-01-01**, flagged in its own note but still in the field. Lasmajadas.cl (fetched) puts the purchase from the Nieto family in 2006; house by **Alberto Cruz Montt** (1905), park by **Guillermo Renner**. **The page states no price**, so the repeated figure of about US$2 million is uncorroborated, and the satellite-phone assessment from the boat is reported, not documented **[R]**.

**Vivendi buys Wanako, Feb 2007 [A]: aftermath corrected.** Vivendi Games paid **US$10 million** and placed the studio under Sierra Online. It did **not** remain inside Activision Blizzard: after the July 2008 merger it was sold to **Artificial Mind and Movement on 15 December 2008**, renamed **Behaviour Santiago** in November 2010, closed **20 November 2017**.

## 5. Palo Alto: Bling Nation, Lemon, the cafe, 2007-2013

**Bling Nation, March 2007 to October 2011 [A].** Tenure dates from PayPal's 2016 DEF 14A; co-founded with Malka; **US$20m Series B led by Balderton, 30 Oct 2009** (TechCrunch), part of roughly US$33m total. It never reached durable scale: the clearest commercial failure on the record.

**Las Majadas, 27 Feb 2010 [R].** The earthquake is public record; that it damaged the palace comes from Chilean press profiles. **The direct fetch of lasmajadas.cl did not mention the earthquake at all**, which matters, since that page is the primary source for the rebuild. The Bosch persuasion in Palo Alto **is** on it, with Bosch quoted directly. Combined investment about US$30m; opening 2015; hotel 2016.

**The first bitcoin purchase, 2011 [A in outline, R in one famous detail].** Three sources fetched, carrying different details. The **US$2,000 in cash** and the QR code appear verbatim only in the Unchained transcript. The Craigslist seller, the Palo Alto cafe and "I felt like I was witnessing the beginning of the Internet" appear only in Bitcoin Magazine. EconTalk has the cafe and the transfer and neither the amount nor the reason. **The bus is in none of them.** It comes from **Reid Hoffman's** Masters of Scale retelling. Carried as [R], attributed to Hoffman.

**Buenos Aires meetups, 2012 [R].** The whiskey bar, the friend "Fede," and the handing of the group to "Diego," understood to be **Diego Gutierrez Zaldivar**, are attributed to Nathaniel Popper's *Digital Gold* (2015). **No fetchable excerpt of Popper's text was reached**, so the narrative rests on a secondary summary of a book nobody in this chain has opened. Bitcoin Argentina's own founder list credits Gutierrez Zaldivar, Rodolfo Andragnes and Franco Amati, not Casares.

**LaBitConf, 7-8 Dec 2013 [A as an event; R as to his absence].** Buenos Aires, about 400 attendees, organised by Gutierrez Zaldivar. Popper has Casares unable to attend the inaugural edition. A **negative constraint**, honoured: the journey file does not stage him in the room, and uses a city-centroid pin rather than the Melia.

**LifeLock acquires Lemon, 11 Dec 2013 [A, primary].** Form 8-K (fetched): vehicle **Lavender Acquisition Corporation**, about **US$42.6m** cash subject to adjustment, plus 44,774 shares to three former Lemon stockholders. **May 2014:** LifeLock discloses that parts of the Lemon-derived Wallet app were not fully PCI-compliant and suspends functions.

## 6. Xapo, the litigation, PayPal, 2014-2020

**Founding and launch [A], two corrections.** En.wikipedia (Xapo): founded "in late 2013 by Casares and COO **Federico Murrone**," products opened to the public in March 2014, so March 2014 is the **public launch and CEO-tenure start**, not the founding. The US$20m Series A was led by Benchmark alongside Fortress, **Pantera Capital** and Ribbit; Pantera was missing from the list reaching this pass. Murrone matters downstream: he is among the defendants in the 2014 Santa Clara suit, an interlock the incoming material did not draw and the journey file does.

**Second round, July 2014 [A].** US$20m led by **Greylock** and **Index Ventures**, with individual participation reported from **Max Levchin**, Yuri Milner and Jerry Yang; **Reid Hoffman** and Index's Mike Volpi as board observers. This is the concrete Hoffman-Casares financial tie, distinct from the friendship and the retellings.

**Bill Gates and the first bitcoin [R, thin].** Self-reported across interviews: a demonstration to Gates and a gift of his first bitcoin, in the orbit of the Allen and Company conference at Sun Valley. **Quantity unknown, year unpinned, Gates's disposition unknown**, and the Forbes Argentina piece usually cited **returned 404 on fetch**. `bill_gates.journey.json` carries no Sun Valley stop, so this is a fresh anchor. The **"Patient Zero"** label is Hoffman's, and the journey file names it as network-erasing rather than repeating it flat.

**Santa Clara County litigation, Aug 2014 onward [A as allegations only].** Lemon and Lemon Argentina sued Casares, Cynthia McAdam, Federico Murrone, Martin Apesteguia and Fabian Cuesta, alleging Xapo was developed using Lemon and LifeLock facilities and resources while its founders were still employed there. Casares cross-claimed and produced a **February 2014 letter from LifeLock president Hilary Schneider** recognising his bitcoin work as unrelated to LifeLock's business (Fortune, 28 July 2015). The action was stayed on a forum motion, related litigation went to Delaware, and a further suit followed on 22 Sept 2016. **Gap, and it is the important one:** no source discloses a **merits verdict or a public settlement**. It is inaccurate to say he was convicted of anything.

**The Swiss vault [A as facility, R as contents-mythology].** A decommissioned 1947 military installation dug more than 320 m into granite near **Attinghausen, Uri**: offline computers, multisignature authorisation, biometric access, mantraps, a delay between request and signing. Clients told reporters it held about US$10bn in bitcoin, an **informed estimate, not an audited disclosure**. *Mythology rendered with its label, per house rule:* nothing is buried in the mountain. The granite protects key material and procedure; the coins sit on a public ledger.

**PayPal board, Jan 2016 to May 2020 [A, primary].** Independent director and Compensation Committee member (2016 proxy); the 2020 proxy (fetched) records that he would not stand for re-election. *Place correction:* that annual meeting was **online only**, with **no physical location**, so the journey file records the tenure at PayPal's North First Street address (pin inherited byte-identical from `max_levchin.journey.json`). A later derivative complaint named many former PayPal directors including Casares; that is not a finding of personal liability.

## 7. Gibraltar, Libra/Diem, the bank, 2018-2024

**Gibraltar meeting, 2018 [A, primary unreadable].** Government press release **308/2018** names Chief Minister Fabian Picardo, Commerce Minister Albert Isola, Casares, Xapo's Seamus Rocca and lawyer Joey Garcia. **The PDF returned corrupted binary text on fetch and could not be quoted.** Corroborated by the FSC record of the entity incorporated **19 November 2018**, later renamed **Xapo VASP Limited**.

**Coinbase Custody acquires Xapo's institutional business, 15 Aug 2019 [A].** **Coinbase's own blog post returned HTTP 403**; the numbers come from its Form S-1/A: cash portion **US$55.4m**, combined custody above **US$7bn** across more than 120 clients in 14 countries. Not a sale of the whole company or brand.

**Libra Association, 14 Oct 2019 [date A, place R].** The council meeting of that date is confirmed, as is the withdrawal sequence: PayPal 4 Oct; eBay, Mastercard, Stripe, Visa and Mercado Pago 11 Oct; Booking Holdings 14 Oct. Casares elected one of five directors, representing Xapo. *Correction:* **Geneva is not attested.** It is the Association's registered seat, which makes it plausible, not documented; the **Xapo Medium post announcing the election returned HTTP 403**, and no reachable source states where the council sat. Downgraded to [R], pin retained and labelled. Renamed Diem in **December 2020**, not merely "in 2020"; assets sold to **Silvergate Capital for a reported US$200 million** in January 2022.

**Xapo Bank, 2021 [A, company's own history].** Licensed credit institution in Gibraltar, separate from the VASP custody entity: fiat under statutory deposit protection, bitcoin under segregation and custody law rather than deposit insurance. Seamus Rocca CEO since 2017; Casares founder and executive chairman. **February 2024 [A]:** Fortune (fetched) puts the bank at roughly **25,000 bitcoins**, about **US$1.3bn**, customers concentrated in Argentina and Latin America.
