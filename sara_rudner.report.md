# Sara Rudner: research report

*2026-08-31. Dancer and choreographer. Born Brooklyn, 16 February 1944; living. Founding member and principal dancer of Twyla Tharp's company; founder of the Sara Rudner Performance Ensemble; first dancer of Christopher Janney's HeartBeat; Director of Dance at Sarah Lawrence College until 2019.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named and fetched. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, dossier lead, or a claim whose only carrier could not be retrieved. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

## Method, and the shape of the evidence

The trace is lopsided. The **Twyla Tharp Foundation's work pages** (twylatharp.org) are the best primary-grade material available: premiere dates, venues, casts, durations, design credits for the works Rudner danced in the company's first decade. Every dossier claim checked against them held, and three items were upgraded from [R] to [A]. Against that, the **Sarah Lawrence College retirement announcement of 19 June 2019** is cited throughout the gathered material and **returned HTTP 404 on every attempt**, though it alone carries the 1999 directorship start, the 1976-1982 ensemble span, the "twenty years," Peggy Gould, and the Salzburg credit. Everything resting on it is [R] below.

## 1. Brooklyn and Barnard, 1944-1964

- **Born 16 February 1944, Brooklyn.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Sara Rudner"]**, corroborated in her own words: "I was born in February 1944, and lived until the age of 17 in Brooklyn, New York..." **[A: Rudner, life-as-a-modern-dancer.com, 2015]**. Russian Jewish immigrant grandparents **[R]**; no hospital named anywhere reachable.
- **Full name Sara Mae Rudner**, and **childhood address 960 East 27th Street** **[R]**: both from a Barnard 1964 yearbook (the name also from Perron, *PAJ* 2022); neither page reachable. Money for dance or music lessons but not both **[R]**.
- **Early training at a neighborhood music school, ballet with Bella Malinka.** **[A: Wikipedia]** for the fact and the teacher; the Avenue I address, the age of nine, the Chopin waltzes: **[R]**. Her own line is verbatim: "Black cotton leotards, naked legs, soft slippers and then pointe shoes at the barre." **[A: Rudner 2015]**.
- **Midwood High School, 1956-1960, out at sixteen on an accelerated track** **[R]**: her own recollection, no fetched source names the school. Declining the High School of Performing Arts, the typing, and a **congenital amblyopia**: all **[R]**.
- **B.A., Barnard College.** **[A]** twice: Wikipedia, and her bio in Janney's PDF. **The year 1964 appears in neither**, and it and the Russian-studies major are **[R]**.

## 2. The apprentice years, 1964-1965

- **American Dance Festival, Connecticut College, summer 1964**, in a revival of Pearl Lang's *And Joy Is My Witness*. **[R]**, dossier only.
- **Paul Sanasardo's company, before Tharp.** **[A: Wikipedia]** for the sequence ("performed with Paul Sanasardo before joining Twyla Tharp's dance company in 1965"). **The 1964 date, the West 21st Street studio, and the roles in *Excursions*, *The Animal's Eye* and *Fatal Birds* are [R]**.
- **Broome Street, SoHo**, in the same blocks as Margaret Jenkins and, through her, Tharp. **[A: Perron 2015]**.
- **Judson Hall, 165 West 57th Street: "She'll do."** Substituting for an injured dancer in a Barbara Gardner work, Rudner is watched a few minutes by Tharp. The line is **confirmed verbatim in Perron's fetched text**, but the circulating attribution is wrong: **not two independent recollections**. Rudner relays Jenkins's account inside her own telling. **One chain of testimony.** The variant "OK, she'll do" is in no fetched source. Tagged **[R]**; the source is **[A]**.
- **Founding member, 1965; principal dancer from 1966, about twenty years.** **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 3. Twyla Tharp and Dancers, 1965-1974

The best-attested stretch of her life, because the Tharp Foundation publishes its own records. All five work entries below are **[A: twylatharp.org]**.

- ***Re-Moves*, 29 October 1966, Judson Memorial Church.** Cast Tharp, Rudner, Jenkins; lighting Tipton; design Huot; c. 45 minutes; audience on three sides. Rudner confirms it: "The first thing I ever did with Twyla was with a stopwatch; it was at Judson Church." **[A: Perron]**. Rope ladder and relevé zigzag: **[R]**.
- **Clerical work for the Merce Cunningham Dance Foundation at BAM.** **[A: Perron]**: typing, phones, barre in a dressing room, back to the desk. The Foundation was incorporated in 1964 **[A: merce_cunningham.journey.json]**. The one hard crossing with another corpus traveler, and the only reason Cunningham is named in a campa.
- **First European tour, 1967**, a bad floor and Tharp dancing on a cut foot rather than stopping. **[A: Perron]**, quoting her **naming Germany**. **The dossier names the Netherlands, Paris and London.** A country centroid is used.
- ***Sowing of Seeds*, 7 June 1970, UMass Amherst**, on a field by the Agricultural Department; cast Dunn, Garcia-Lorca, Rudner, Tharp, Wright, plus students scattering seed from tin cans. Every dossier cast name checks out. *Upgraded from [R].*
- ***The Fugue*, 1 August 1970, same campus**, cast Tharp, Rudner, Wright, called there "the first work intended to become a piece of active repertory." *The gathered chronology collapsed* Re-Moves *and* The Fugue *into one "1970" item; they are six years and two venues apart.*
- ***Sunrise/Noon/Sundown*, 28 March 1971**, once only, entire: Fort Tryon Park (from 5 a.m.), Battery Park (midday), City Hall (evening), all three dancers throughout. *Upgraded from [R].* The page glosses Fort Tryon as "Harlem"; it is in Washington Heights.
- ***Eight Jelly Rolls*, 16 September 1971, Delacorte Theater**, cast Cohn, Curry, Garcia-Lorca, Rudner, Tharp, Wright. **Two dossier claims fail and are not carried over:** (a) "Tharp's first proscenium work," doubtful, the Delacorte being open-air; (b) **the solos "Mournful Serenade" and "Shreveport Stomp" are not attributed to Rudner** there. Her dancing in the Tharp film sequences of *Hair* (1979) and *Amadeus* (1984) is **[R]**, databases unchecked, and is not staged.

## 4. The Sara Rudner Performance Ensemble, 1975-1982

- ***Dancing-on-View*, five hours at St. Mark's Church in-the-Bowery, 10 June 1975**, with Wendy Perron, Wendy Rogers, Risa Jaroslow, against a Robert Kushner pomegranate backdrop. **[A]** for the St. Mark's marathons beginning 1975 (Perron) and the address (Wikipedia). **Exact date, cast, backdrop, red linoleum: [R]**.
- **Goddard College, Vermont, summer 1975**: a semi-improvised barn solo, "Yes," made while Meredith Monk developed *Quarry* nearby. **[R]**, unsupported.
- ***Some "Yes" and More*, 16 February 1976, Lucinda Childs's loft, 541 Broadway**: thirty-two dances for a thirty-second birthday, Davies and Alston ushering. **[R]**, but **internally consistent**: the arithmetic matches the confirmed 1944 birth.
- **The ensemble, active 1976-1982** **[R]**: span from the 404'd announcement, as is **Peggy Gould beginning to dance with her in 1987**. Additive duets with Wendy Rogers at the Roundabout Theatre, late 1970s **[R]**.
- **Guggenheim Fellowship in choreography, 1981** **[A: gf.org]**. **Detached retina, c. 1981**, weeks of bed rest, later linked by her to closing the ensemble: **[R]**, her account only.

## 5. HeartBeat, 1983-1990

- **HeartBeat premiered by Rudner at Boston's Institute of Contemporary Art, 1983**, telemetry hardware by Transkinetics, Inc. reading her live cardiac activity and driving a percussive score. **[A: MIT News, "ArtHeart," 13 May 1998]**, which carries the date verbatim. Wikipedia lists "Heartbeat" **without one**.
  - **Authorship correction.** MIT News, the Small Press Traffic conversation and Janney's own document all present HeartBeat as **Janney's piece with Rudner as its first dancer**. The gathered phrasing implied joint authorship of the score; it is Janney's.
- **First encounter in Janney's MIT studio, Cambridge**: "My intuitive preference for 'HeartBeat' defined the direction my dancing would take. It was 1983." **[A: Rudner, "HEARTBEAT MEMORIES," in Janney's PDF]**. *Correction:* she does not say meeting Janney defined her direction; she says her choice between two proposed projects did. Same source: it coincided with Tharp's *Fait Accompli*, "which would become my final participation and contribution to her work," and Janney performed it with her "on and off until 1990."
- **On leaving Tharp:** "You know, I left twice." / "The reason I left the first time was sheer exhaustion, and curiosity." / "I learned how to work from Twyla Tharp." **[A: Small Press Traffic, 2025]**.

## 7. Bennington and Sarah Lawrence, 1997-2019

- **M.F.A., Bennington College.** **[A]** twice: Wikipedia, and her Janney-PDF bio. **Neither gives a year; neither names a mentor.** The 1999 date and the Creach / Reitz / Sgorbati mentors are **[R]**, and 1999 is also the unverified Sarah Lawrence start year.
- **Director of the Dance Program, Sarah Lawrence, until retiring in 2019.** **[A: Wikipedia]**. **The 1999 start, the "twenty years," the 19 June date, and all programmatic detail are [R]**, on the 404'd announcement.
  - **Succession, flagged as inference.** Viola Farber joined the faculty in 1987, chaired from 1988, and died in office in December 1998 **[A: viola_farber.journey.json]**. Rudner's appointment falls the year after. **No source states that Rudner succeeded Farber.** The journey file names Farber once and says in its confidence field that this is inferred from adjacency alone.
- ***Dancing-on-View (Preview/Hindsight)*, Baryshnikov Arts Center, 2007**, sixteen experienced women, made on sabbatical. **[A]**: Macaulay's review ran in the *NYT* on **15 May 2007**, and his 13 April 2008 essay recalls her dancing "in 2007 at the Baryshnikov Arts Center." **Date contradiction resolved: 2007 is right**; the gathered geography said 2008, which is the second essay's date.
- **"The Divine Sara,"** a 1970s audience nickname echoing Sarah Bernhardt, in print via Macaulay 2007: **[R]**, legend, not a title. ***This Dancing Life*, Kilkenny, 2007**, and the **ICA Boston tour, c. 2008-2009**: both **[R]**, dossier only.
- ***Necessary Weather* revived, Baryshnikov Arts Center, late October 2011.** **[A: Jowitt, DanceBeat, 6 November 2011]**: "Movements settle on them, tickle them, emanate from them, are born in them." **Correction:** the dossier's second quotation is not verbatim. Jowitt wrote "The dancing in *Necessary Weather* doesn't involve high kicks or big jumps," which must not appear as "no high kicks or big jumps." **The dossier's 2012 American Dance Institute revival is unsupported and is replaced by this 2011 staging.**

## 10. Contradictions flagged, not adjudicated

1. ***Re-Moves* premiere: 29 October 1966 (twylatharp.org) or 18 October 1966 (Wikipedia).** The Foundation date is used; the conflict stands.
2. **The 1967 tour: Germany (Perron, quoting Rudner) or the Netherlands, Paris and London (dossier).** Neither gives a city.
3. ***Dancing-on-View (Preview/Hindsight)*: 2007 or 2008.** Macaulay settles it at 2007; the doubled record is noted because it is the kind of error that propagates.
4. ***Necessary Weather* revival: BAC 2011 (sourced) or American Dance Institute 2012 (unsupported).** The 2012 claim is not disproven, merely uncarried.
5. **The 1999 pair.** Bennington M.F.A. and Sarah Lawrence appointment are both dated 1999 by the dossier and nothing else. They cannot corroborate each other.
6. **"She'll do" versus "OK, she'll do."** Only the first is in the fetched text, and only as Jenkins's report relayed by Rudner.
7. **Bessie / NEA / NYSCA as fellowships or grants.** Wikipedia says fellowships; her bio says grants and omits the Bessie.
