# Flavius Josephus: research report

*Yosef ben Mattityahu, c. 37/38 to c. 100 CE. Priest of Jehoiarib, commander in Galilee, prisoner of Vespasian, client of three Flavian emperors, author of nearly everything anyone knows about first-century Judaea, himself included.*

## Method and evidence key

Josephus has a structural problem no research removes: **he is very nearly his own only source.** Four works survive (*Jewish War*, *Jewish Antiquities*, *Life*, *Against Apion*), and the external witnesses to his person amount to a sentence in Suetonius, a paragraph in Cassius Dio, and the Christian transmission chain from Origen and Eusebius on. His chief contemporary critic, Justus of Tiberias, wrote a rival history that is lost, reaching us only through a hostile ninth-century summary by Photius.

**[A]** means attested with a source named. **[R]** means reconstruction, tradition, or a self-report nothing outside Josephus corroborates. Many claims are formally [A] on his own text while remaining uncheckable in substance; where that gap matters it is said so rather than hidden behind the tag.

The archaeology is the one genuine external control: Yodfat and Gamla have been excavated, and their fortifications, ballista stones and destruction layers corroborate parts of the siege narrative. Nothing comparable exists for the Galilean politics, the cave, or the Rome years.

## Phase 1: Jerusalem, the priestly house (c. 37 to 63 CE)

- Born in Jerusalem in the first year of Gaius Caligula, to a priest named Matthias, of the course of Jehoiarib, first of the twenty-four. **[A** *Life* 1-6, Whiston**]** Month and day unrecorded. Hasmonaean royal descent through his mother is **[R]**: self-report at *Life* 1, no registry, and the genealogy at *Life* 2-5 has known internal difficulties.
- At about fourteen, high priests and principal men consult him on points of the law. **[R** *Life* 9**]** A topos of the ancient *Life* genre; a rhetorical credential, not a datum.
- At about sixteen, personal investigation of Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes **[A** *Life* 10-11**]**, then about three years in the wilderness with the ascetic Bannus **[A** *Life* 11-12**]**. The desert site is never named; the Dead Sea placement in the journey file is convention, flagged in the stop's `date_confidence`.
- Returns at nineteen and conducts himself by the rules of the Pharisees. **[A** *Life* 12**]** Whether that is lived practice or a claim made in Rome once the Pharisaic party had become the surviving one is a standing dispute. Not adjudicated.

**Chronological note.** *Life* 10-12 fits a survey of three sects and three years with Bannus into a span running from sixteen to nineteen. It does not fit. The compression is his own and is left visible rather than smoothed.

## Phase 2: The embassy to Nero's house (c. 63 to 66 CE)

- At twenty-six, sails for Rome to plead for priests whom the procurator Felix had sent to Nero in chains; shipwrecked in the Adriatic, where six hundred swim through the night and about eighty are picked up by a Cyrenian vessel at dawn. **[A** *Life* 13-15**]** Wreck site unrecorded; the journey file's pin (40.0, 19.0) is a mid-sea approximation and says so.
- Ashore at Puteoli; friendship with Aliturus, a Jewish actor favoured by Nero; introduced through him to Poppaea Sabina; the priests released, gifts received. **[A** *Life* 16**]** No building in Rome is named; the Palatine pin is inference.
- Returns to a Judaea sliding into revolt and says he counselled restraint. **[A** *Life* 17-19, self-report of motive**]** Motive-claims written after 71 on a Flavian pension are the least testable thing in the corpus.

## Phase 3: The Galilean command (66 to 67 CE)

- After Cestius Gallus's column is destroyed at Beth Horon in autumn 66, the Jerusalem authorities send Josephus north. **[A** *War* 2.562-568; *Life* 28-29**]**
- **Contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** In *War* he is a general with plenary charge of Galilee; in *Life*, written thirty years later against Justus, he is one of three priests sent to calm the province. Both accounts are his. Scholarship reads the discrepancy as apologetic revision; nobody can test it.
- Council of seventy, local courts of seven, forces drilled on Roman lines, towns fortified. **[A** *War* 2.569-584; *Life* 187-188**]** Yodefat and Gamla are archaeologically corroborated; the administrative apparatus is not. The fortification list runs Jotapata, Tarichaeae, Tiberias, Gamla, Gischala, Japha, Tabor, Arbela, Meroth, Achabare; several identifications are conventional rather than certain, Meroth especially.
- Rivalries with John of Gischala and Justus of Tiberias. **[A** *Life* 34-45, 70-76, 336-367**]** Every surviving word about either man was written by their opponent.
- Tiberias: threatens to cut off a captive's hand to disperse a crowd. Tarichaeae: a staged mock execution to frighten mutineers. **[A** *Life* 96-100, 138-144, 168-173, all self-report**]**

## Phase 4: Jotapata (spring to July 67 CE)

- Siege of about forty-seven days by Vespasian and Titus; the town falls before dawn after a deserter reveals the guards asleep. **[A** *War* 3.141-339; corroborated in outline by Aviam's Yodefat excavations**]**
- **The cave and the lot. [R]** Hidden with about forty companions; the place betrayed; the group agrees to kill one another by lot; Josephus and one other are left alive by "fortune or the providence of God" and surrender. *War* 3.340-391 is the sole witness and neither the mechanism nor its fairness is testable. The pool's geography lens tagged this **[A]** and its chronology lens **[R]**; **[R] is adopted**, the two lenses having contradicted each other on the most contested scene of the life.
- The prophecy before Vespasian. **[A** *War* 3.399-408, with genuine external corroboration in Suetonius, *Vespasian* 5, Cassius Dio 66.1.4, and the wider oracle tradition at Tacitus, *Histories* 5.13**]**
- **Discrepancy, flagged.** Dio has Josephus name a deadline, "a year from now, when you have become emperor, you will release me." Josephus gives no timeframe; his line is "Bind me now still faster, and keep me for thyself." Conversely Josephus includes Titus ("thou, and this thy son") and Dio does not. The deadline should never be quoted as Josephus's own.

## Phase 5: Captivity, freedom, and the turn (67 to 70 CE)

- Held about two years rather than sent to Nero, with an unnamed captive woman given him in marriage at Vespasian's direction; she left him at his release. **[A** *War* 3.408; *Life* 414**]** Whether the confinement was continuously at Caesarea is not itemised; the journey file's placement there is inference and says so.
- Freed on Vespasian's proclamation, July 69; Titus has the chains cut with an axe rather than unlocked, to annul the stigma of bondage. **[A** *War* 4.622-629**]**
- Second marriage, at Alexandria, to an Alexandrian Jewish woman; three sons, of whom only Hyrcanus survives. **[A** *Life* 414-415**] Correction applied**: the pool's chronology lens put this at Rome in 71; the text puts it at Alexandria, before the siege, c. 69/70. The geography lens had it right.

## Phase 6: The siege of Jerusalem (70 CE)

- On Titus's staff as interpreter, intelligence source and negotiator; circles the walls calling on the defenders to surrender; wounded by a stone. **[A** *War* 5.67-70, 5.261, 5.361-420, 5.541-547, self-report throughout**]**
- **Parents held inside the city. [A** *War* 5.544-545**] Correction applied.** The pool attributed the detention to Simon bar Giora, citing *War* 5.533; verified against lexundria.com/j_bj/5.527-548/wst, the passage names no faction, only "the seditious." *War* 5.527-533 is a different episode, Simon's murder of **Matthias son of Boethus, the high priest**. Two men named Matthias sit twenty paragraphs apart and exactly one is Josephus's father; the journey file says so.
- The burning of the sanctuary, late summer 70. **[A** *War* 6.220-270**]**
- **Contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** Josephus has Titus convene a council, wish the building spared, and be pre-empted by a soldier acting on a divine impulse. Sulpicius Severus, *Chronica* 2.30.6-7, probably from lost Tacitus, has Titus argue for destruction to extirpate the religion. Josephus wrote as the client of the man whose conduct is at issue; Sulpicius wrote three centuries later at second hand.
- After the fall: his brother Matthias and named friends released, sacred books recovered from the plunder, an estate in Judaea whose first parcel proved unusable. **[A** *Life* 418-419, 425; *War* 6.428**] Gap**: his mother's fate is never stated, and his father's release is reported nowhere.

## Phase 7: Rome under the Flavians (71 to c. 93 CE)

- Travels to Rome with Titus; citizenship, a pension, lodging in a house Vespasian had occupied before his accession, and the patrons' *nomen*, Flavius. **[A** *Life* 422-423; *Against Apion* 1.51-52**] Tag corrected**: the pool put the house on the Quirinal at **[A]**, but Josephus names no district, and the Quirinal rests only on Suetonius's note that the Flavian family house stood in Region VI *ad Malum Punicum*. Demoted to **[R]**.
- Witnesses the Flavian triumph of 71 and writes the fullest surviving description of it **[A** *War* 7.116-157**]**, and describes the Temple of Peace and the deposit of the Temple vessels there **[A** *War* 7.158-162**]**. Whether he went in to look is not stated. The Arch of Titus, dedicated after 81, carves the same procession and postdates his presence at it.
- Third marriage, to a Jewish woman of a Cretan family; sons Justus (Vespasian's seventh year) and Simonides called Agrippa (ninth). **[A** *Life* 5, 427**] Date corrected**: Hyrcanus is born in Vespasian's fourth regnal year, 1 July 72 to 30 June 73, which Whiston glosses "[A.D. 72]", not the pool's 73.
- *Jewish War*, seven books in Greek, presented to Vespasian and Titus. **[A** *War* 1.3-6; *Life* 361-363; *Against Apion* 1.50-51**]** The earlier version in his "ancestral language," for the Jews beyond the Euphrates, is lost entirely, as are Agrippa II's sixty-two letters attesting its accuracy, two of them quoted **[A** *Life* 364-367**]**. Nothing of Agrippa II survives outside Josephus.
- Under Titus (79-81) and Domitian (81-96): privileges continued, tax exemption on the Judaean estate, accusers punished, kindness from Domitia Longina. **[A** *Life* 428-429, sole witness**]** The accusations are never specified.

## Phase 8: The last works, and the silence (93/94 onward)

- *Jewish Antiquities*, twenty books, completed in the thirteenth year of Domitian, when he was fifty-six. **[A** *Antiquities* 20.267**]** That is 93/94 CE and his **last securely datable statement of any kind**. Dedicated to a patron named Epaphroditus **[A** *Antiquities* 1.8; *Life* 430**]**, unidentified: Nero's freedman-secretary, killed by Domitian around 95, or the grammarian Mettius Epaphroditus.
- **The Testimonium Flavianum**, *Antiquities* 18.63-64. **[R]** The received Greek carries confessional phrasing ("He was the Christ") most scholars regard as at least partly Christian editing; Eusebius is the earliest secure witness to the wording. Disputed, resolved in neither direction. **The James passage**, *Antiquities* 20.200, is more widely accepted as authentic, though not without dispute. **[A]**
- *Life*, a one-book self-defence against Justus, transmitted appended to *Antiquities*. **[A** on the text; date disputed, mid-90s to c. 100, on Agrippa II's death**]** *Against Apion*, two books, after *Antiquities* **[A** 1.1**]**, with the canon-count statement and the extracts from Manetho, Berossus and Menander. **Quotation corrected**: the pool gave 1.8 with Whiston's bracketed clause silently dropped; the journey file restores "[as the Greeks have:]".
- **Death: no record. [R]** No ancient source states where, when or how he died, or where he was buried, and no grave was ever shown. The conventional c. 100 CE is a modern estimate resting on the absence of later work. No authentic portrait exists either; images bearing his name, the 1880 Sartain engraving included, are inventions.

## Contradictions flagged, not resolved

1. **His Galilean authority**: general with plenary command (*War*) versus one of three priests sent to pacify (*Life*). Both accounts are his.
2. **Who burned the Temple**: a soldier against Titus's wishes (Josephus) versus Titus ordering it (Sulpicius Severus, probably from lost Tacitus). Josephus was Titus's client.
3. **The prophecy's wording**: Dio's one-year deadline is absent from Josephus, and Josephus's inclusion of Titus is absent from Dio.
4. Also unresolved and left so: the date of *Life*, the identity of Epaphroditus, the Testimonium, and whether he was in fact a Pharisee.

## Honest gaps

- **No independent witness to the Galilean command at all**, and **none to the cave and the lot**: the most famous scene of his life rests entirely on the survivor's own account.
- **His mother**, imprisoned in 70 and never mentioned again; **his first two wives**, unnamed; **his death, burial and appearance**, on which there is nothing. **The Aramaic *War*** and **Agrippa II's letters**: no line, no original.
- **Rabbinic silence.** The Greek Josephus is not clearly engaged by name in rabbinic literature; his survival is owed to Christian monastic copying. For roughly nine centuries Jewish readers meant by "Josephus" the tenth-century Hebrew *Sefer Yosippon*, compiled in Byzantine southern Italy from the Latin Pseudo-Hegesippus and printed at Mantua in 1476. **[A** on Yosippon, **[R]** on the framing**]**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Josephus is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing has been silently deleted. The interlock lens surfaced five relations not rooted in his own life, all caught in the **forward** direction, each recorded here rather than written into the journey file:

1. **Hadrian and Aelia Capitolina (130 CE), `adriano.journey.json`.** The refoundation stands on the Temple Mount pin Josephus's own stops use. Hadrian's stop, on Hadrian's file.
2. **Pliny the Elder in Flavian Rome, `plinio_el_viejo.journey.json`.** Both in Vespasian's circle in the mid-70s; Pliny's Temple of Peace stop notes some displayed loot is Jerusalem's. **No meeting is documented anywhere**, and contemporaneity plus shared geography is not a relation. Josephus's own Temple of Peace stop stands on *War* 7.158-162 and does not mention Pliny.
3. **Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's cave at Peki'in (c. 145 CE), `rabbi_shimon_bar_yochai_rashbi.journey.json`.** A motif parallel to Jotapata, different site, eighty years later.
4. **Paul at Puteoli (60 CE), `apostle_paul.journey.json`.** Two Jewish travellers ashore at the same port a few years apart; pure co-location, and Josephus never mentions Paul. The reverse relation is real and already sits on Paul's file, which cites *War* 5.243 and Josephus on Helena of Adiabene.
5. **The whole transmission history**: Eusebius, the Latin versions, *Sefer Yosippon*, codex Ambrosianus 370, Arlenius and Froben (1544), Lodge, Whiston, Niese, the Loeb, Schultz identifying Yodfat from Josephus's own topography in 1847, Aviam's excavations, and the Yodfat memorial stone whose commemorated date of 20 July 67 CE comes from *War* alone. None staged as a scene; all of it sits in `sources` and `suggested_refs` on his own stops.

One relation runs the other way and is already in the corpus: `hiram_abiff.journey.json` carries a Rome stop of 93 CE built on Josephus finishing the *Antiquities* and saying nothing of a murdered master-builder, a legitimate use of Josephus as a dated silence on another file. The corresponding fact here is stated positively: he names Hiram the king who sent the cedars and the Tyrian craftsman of the same name who cast the bronze, and reports no murder of any workman.
