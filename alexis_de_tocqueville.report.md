# Alexis de Tocqueville (1805-1859): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject 0 of the queue, framed as the anchor of the completed American cluster (Washington, Jefferson, Franklin, Jackson, Lafayette) and the hinge between that cluster and the Argentine one (Sarmiento).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, with the source named in place · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or route inference · corrections carried out of the verification pass are marked **[corrected]** with the erroneous version kept visible, never silently overwritten.

The journey file built from this report has 9 segments and 45 stops. Where the verification pass overturned a claim in the gathering pass, this report keeps both readings.

## I. Norman blood and the Terror, 1805-1826

**Birth.** Alexis-Charles-Henri Clérel de Tocqueville is born in Paris on **29 July 1805** to an old Norman noble house **[A, en.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia]**.

**The family's dead.** His great-grandfather Guillaume-Chrétien de Lamoignon de Malesherbes, who had defended Louis XVI before the Convention, was guillotined together with his daughter Antoinette, his granddaughter Aline, and her husband Jean-Baptiste de Chateaubriand. Alexis's parents were imprisoned and released after Thermidor **[A, en.wikipedia, Malesherbes article]**.

> **Contradiction, flagged and corrected.** The English Wikipedia article on Tocqueville dates the Malesherbes execution to **1793**. The Malesherbes article and every standard source give **22 April 1794**. The 1794 date is used in the journey file; the 1793 reading is recorded here as an error in one of the two most-consulted sources, not adjudicated away. **[corrected]**

**Metz.** Hervé de Tocqueville is appointed prefect of the Moselle in **1817**; the family moves and Alexis enters the Collège Royal at Metz, later the Lycée Fabert, taking his baccalauréat there in **1823** **[A, fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia for the baccalauréat]**.

**Law.** Licencié en droit after study in Paris, **1826** **[A, fr.wikipedia]**. Month not given by any consulted source; the journey file defaults to 1826-01-01 and says so in `date_confidence`.

**Gap.** The loss of religious faith at Metz, from reading in his father's library, is standard in the biographical literature and is *not* in the verified pool. It is therefore stated in the journey file's campa only in the weak form the pool supports (a prefect's son reading without supervision in a garrison town) and is not asserted as a dated event.

## II. Versailles, Guizot, and the oath, 1827-1830

- **1827**: appointed *juge auditeur*, an unpaid apprentice magistrate, at the tribunal of Versailles; meets **Gustave de Beaumont** there, beginning the friendship that runs to his deathbed **[A, fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, Gustave de Beaumont]**.
- **1828-1830**: attends François Guizot's public lectures on the history of European civilization in Paris **[A, fr.wikipedia]**.
- **1830**: after the July Revolution deposes Charles X, he swears the oath of loyalty to Louis-Philippe to keep his post, reluctantly **[A, fr.wikipedia]**. The exact date of the oath is not established in the sources consulted; the journey file dates the stop 1830-08-01 and hedges it.

**Interlock.** The July Revolution is already in the atlas through **marqués de Lafayette** (Paris, 1830-07-31, pin 48.8566 / 2.3522). Lafayette presenting the Duke of Orléans to the crowd and Tocqueville swearing to the resulting regime are the same event seen from two ends. Named in campa.

### Corrections made in this phase, with the superseded readings kept

1. **The Canada leg was August, not July.** The gathering pass filed the Michigan frontier and the Canadian excursion together under 1831-07 at Quebec City coordinates. The Michigan excursion is July; Niagara is 19 August, Montreal 23 August, and the Lower Canada stay runs roughly 20 August to 2 September, about two weeks and not "a few days" as en.wikipedia loosely has it. **[corrected]**
2. **The river descent began in November, not December.** Pittsburgh in the second half of November, the *Fourth of July* wrecked on the Burlington Bar near Wheeling in late November, four days at Cincinnati in early December, Memphis mid-December, New Orleans on or about 1 January 1832. The gathering pass had the whole descent at 1831-12 and tagged it **[R]**; it is **[A]** on the standard chronology (Pierson; Mises Institute, *Travels in America*). **[corrected]**
3. **Sam Houston was met on the water, not in a Memphis hotel.** The traditional version places the meeting in a hotel while they were stranded. HistoryNet ("The Day Sam Houston Met Alexis de Tocqueville") and the Mises Institute account both put it aboard a New Orleans-bound steamboat that Houston joined with a Cherokee delegation two days after Christmas 1831. The conversation is in Tocqueville's own notebooks, so the stop is **[A]**. The Memphis-hotel version is recorded here as tradition, not carried into the journey file. **[corrected]**
4. **There was one Washington visit, not two, and Jackson received them.** The gathering pass produced an October 1831 Washington stop tagged **[R]** ("a personal audience with Jackson is traditionally asserted") and a January 1832 "second visit" also tagged **[R]**. The Journal of the Early Republic article "Tocqueville and the American Presidency" fixes the audience to the **afternoon of 19 January 1832**. The October stop is deleted; the January stop is **[A]**. **[corrected]**
5. **Charles Carroll's age.** 94 at the meeting, not 95: born 19 September 1737, visit late October 1831. **[corrected]**

### Contradiction left open

**The 3 February 1832 stage.** The underlying source reads: "On February 3, 1832, before dawn, they took a stagecoach to Philadelphia, then to New York City." That is a **departure from Washington and arrival at Philadelphia**, which contradicts the gathering pass's phrasing ("before dawn they take a stagecoach *from* Philadelphia"). The journey file reads it as arrival at Philadelphia and says so in `date_confidence`. The opposite reading is not disproved; it is simply less consistent with the mid-January Washington stay.

### Gap

No consulted source gives the return crossing's arrival date at Le Havre in 1832. The five-to-six week estimate is **[R]** and the stop was cut rather than invented.

## IV. The books and the Chamber, 1833-1847

- **1833**: *Du système pénitentiaire aux États-Unis, et de son application en France*, jointly with Beaumont **[A, en.wikipedia, Gustave de Beaumont]**.
- **1833**: five weeks in England **[A, en.wikipedia]**.
- **1833**: meets **Mary Mottley** at Versailles **[A, en.wikipedia]**. *Contradiction, corrected*: the gathering pass dated the meeting **1835**, which cannot be reconciled with the same article's "met at Versailles in 1833 ... married in 1836". The 1833 reading is used. **[corrected]**
- **1835**: *De la démocratie en Amérique*, volume I, Paris; immediate success **[A]**. Beaumont publishes *Marie, ou l'esclavage aux États-Unis* the same year **[A]**.
- **1835**: England again, then Ireland with Beaumont **[A]**.
- **1836**: marriage to Mary Mottley; 23 years, no children **[A]**. Month and church not established: a **gap**.
- **1836-09**: Metz, Strasbourg, Basel, Bern **[A, fr.wikipedia]**. Folded into the marriage stop's campa rather than given its own pin.
- **1837**: letters hoping for French-Arab intermarriage and fusion in Algeria **[A, en.wikipedia]**.
- **1839**: elected deputy for **Valognes** (Manche); sits until 1848 **[A]**. Polling day not established.
- **1840**: *Democracy in America*, volume II **[A]**.
- **1841**: elected to the **Académie française** at 36 **[A, fr.wikipedia]**; **first visit to Algeria** **[A]**.
- **1842**: elected to the **American Philosophical Society** **[A]**; elected *conseiller général* of the Manche **[A, fr.wikipedia]**. The APS election is carried in the report but not given a stop, since he did not travel to Philadelphia for it.
- **1846**: second visit to Algeria **[A]**. **1847**: *Travail sur l'Algérie*; the attempt at a Young Left party around labour reform **[A]**.

**Interlock.** The Algerian stops sit against **abdelkader** (the Mitidja, 1839-11-20, pin 36.55 / 2.9). Tocqueville's Algeria pin is the Algiers pin from the pool, 36.7538 / 3.0588, not Abdelkader's Mitidja pin; the two are different places and are not merged.

**Not resolved.** The pool contains both the 1837 letters hoping for a fused French-Arab people and the 1841/1847 reports recommending permanent domination and accepting the razzia. No consulted source reconciles them and this report does not either; the journey file states both and lets the juxtaposition do the work.

## V. 1848 and the Second Republic

- **1848-02-24**: the February Revolution; his own account in the *Souvenirs* (the cook, the shut shops, the empty road) **[A, archive.org, recollectionsofa00tocquoft]**.
- **1848-04-23**: elected to the Constituent Assembly by the Manche; sits on the constitution commission **[A]**. *Corrected* from the gathering pass's **25 April**, on the French text: "M. de Tocqueville fut envoyé, le 23 avril 1848, à l'Assemblée constituante par le département de la Manche." **[corrected]**
- **1848-06**: supports Cavaignac's suppression of the June Days **[A]**.
- **1849-06**: Minister of Foreign Affairs in Odilon Barrot's government under President Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte **[A]**. Exact day of taking office not established: a **gap**.
- **1849-08-06**: elected president of the general council of the Manche, second ballot, 24 votes of 44; holds it to 1852 **[A]**. *Corrected* from the gathering pass's **27 August**. **[corrected]**
- **1851-12-02**: opposes the coup, is briefly detained at Vincennes, is released, and leaves active politics **[A]**.

## VI. Tocqueville, Manche, and the end, 1852-1859

- **1852**: withdraws to the Château de Tocqueville **[A]**.
- **1856**: *L'Ancien Régime et la Révolution*, volume I; the planned second volume never written **[A]**.
- **1859-04-16**: dies of tuberculosis at Cannes, aged 53, Beaumont at the bedside **[A]**.
- **1859**: buried in the cemetery of the village of Tocqueville, Manche **[A]**. Exact burial day not established: a **gap**. Tomb inscription and plot condition could not be confirmed from any accessible source: a **gap**.

> **Coordinate contradiction inside the pool, corrected.** The chronology lens placed the Château de Tocqueville at **49.6167 / -1.4**; the afterlife lens placed the same village at **49.6714 / -1.3356**. The village of Tocqueville (Manche) sits at 49°40'14" N, 1°20'12" W = **49.6706 / -1.3367** (fr.wikipedia). The chronology figure is roughly seven kilometres off, in open country southwest of the village. The journey file uses **49.6714 / -1.3356** for both the château and the grave, matching the afterlife lens. **[corrected]**

## VII. Afterlife

- Henry **Reeve**'s English translation begins appearing in London in **1835** and is the version through which most nineteenth-century Anglophone readers meet the book **[A, general bibliographic record]**. Later scholarly criticism of Reeve for softening Tocqueville's phrasing is reported but could not be verified in detail from accessible sources: a **gap**.
- **Mansfield and Winthrop**, University of Chicago Press, **2000** **[A]**; **Goldhammer**, **2004** **[A]**.
- Beaumont edits the collected works and correspondence after 1859, leaves the project unfinished at his own death, and his widow completes it **[A]**. Criticism of Beaumont's selective omissions from the letters is reported but unverified: a **gap**.
- French eclipse and post-1945 revival through **Raymond Aron** and **François Furet** **[A]**.
- The Volume I closing passage on the United States and Russia as the two powers marked to sway the destinies of half the globe is textually **[A]**; reading it as a prophecy of the Cold War is a twentieth-century framing, not his own claim, and is labelled as such.
- **Misattribution, carried as legend and labelled.** "America is great because she is good, and if America ever ceases to be good, she will cease to be great" appears nowhere in Tocqueville. Earliest traceable citations are mid-twentieth-century American religious and political oratory **[R]**. The journey file's final campa names it as a sentence America put in his mouth, not as a quotation.
- **Struck.** The claim that *L'Ancien Régime* has been translated into "at least nine languages, including Arabic, Persian, Hebrew, Korean, and Chinese" is an artifact: that list is exactly the interlanguage sidebar of the English Wikipedia article, which records which other Wikipedias have an article, not which languages the book exists in. No sourced translation bibliography was reachable. Removed rather than restated. **[corrected]**
- Manuscript pages and working drafts survive in research libraries; the specific holding institution for the pages cited by en.wikipedia could not be confirmed, though the Beinecke at Yale holds related Tocqueville materials per general bibliographic record: a **gap**.
