# Saint George: research report

*2026-08-08. Georgios of Lydda; Sant Jordi; San Giorgio; Giyorgis; Jirjis. Soldier martyr, dragon-slayer, one of the Fourteen Holy Helpers, patron of England, Georgia, Ethiopia, Aragon, Catalonia, Portugal, Moscow and Gozo.*

**[A] = attested, source named. [R] = reconstruction, hagiographic tradition, local devotion, later accretion.**

## Method, and the hole at the centre

George is the best-travelled saint in the atlas and the worst-documented. The cult is continuous, physical and datable from the early sixth century; the life is not documented at all. **No contemporary source names George.** The only near-contemporary text anyone cites, Eusebius *HE* VIII.5, describes an unnamed man of rank who tore down the persecution edict at Nicomedia and died for it **[A as text]**. No name, no province, no date beyond the edict's. Everything in sections 1 to 3 is therefore **[R]**, and an **[A]** there attaches to *what a text says*, never to the event narrated. Corrections to the pool are stated in place, with the wrong version named, because some of the same errors sit in neighbouring atlas files.

What is reasonably hard: the **shrine at Diospolis/Lydda, active by c. 518-530** **[A, Theodosius]**; the **site's building history from 614 to the church of 1872** **[A]**; the **Gelasian Decree's** refusal to vouch for the acts **[A]**; and the whole afterlife from the eleventh century on.

## 1. Birth, parents, the Nubian father

**Cappadocia**, town unnamed; father **Gerontius**, a Cappadocian Greek; mother **Polychronia**, a Christian of Lydda **[R, Coptic tradition from Qasr Ibrim, via Wikipedia "Saint George"]**. Birth placed under **Aurelian, 270-275** **[R]**. Gerontius is said to have served in **Nobatia**, Lower Nubia **[R]**; no fort, town or unit named, so the journey pins **24.0889, 32.8998** (Aswan area) as a declared proxy. After his death Polychronia returns with the boy to **Lydda** **[R]**, the hinge explaining why the cult sits in Palestine and not Cappadocia. No independent record of that journey exists and the Passio families do not all contain it.

- **Gaps.** No birth year in any source: the chronology lens said 270 under Aurelian, the geography lens 280. `0270-01-01` is a declared placeholder. No Cappadocian town named; pinned at **38.6431, 34.8286**, the atlas's Cappadocia/Goreme pin.

## 2. Nicomedia: soldier, edict, torments

**Service under Diocletian, in or near the guard** **[R, Wikipedia; Passio families]**; rank and posting differ between recensions.

The imperial frame is attested independently of George: the church at Nicomedia is pulled down on **23 February 303**, the Terminalia, and the first edict published **the next day, 24 February** **[A, Wikipedia "Diocletianic Persecution"]**.

> **CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED, AND PROPAGATING.** The pool dated the edict `0303-02-23`, citing `saint_blaise.journey.json`. That is the demolition date, not the publication date. **`saint_acacius` and `constantino_el_grande` both attach 0303-02-23 to the edict itself.** This journey splits the two into separate stops, inherits the **Nicomedia pin 40.7654, 29.9408 byte-identical**, and states the disagreement in `date_confidence`. Correcting the neighbours is outside this pass.

**Eusebius on the man who tore it down [A as text]:** "a certain man, not obscure but very highly honored with distinguished temporal dignities ... seized the edict as it was posted openly and publicly, and tore it to pieces as a profane and impious thing" (*HE* VIII.5, NPNF translation at newadvent.org; quoted in full at the corresponding journey stop). **He names nobody.** The identification with George is later tradition **[R]**, and the tearing episode is absent from the earliest Greek Acts **[R]**.

**The persecutor's name will not hold still.** Most Latin and later Greek texts say **Diocletian**; some Eastern recensions say **Dadianus**; Caxton runs the trial under a provost named **Dacian**. Three names, three manuscript families, one scene. Not adjudicated.

**The torments [R, Passio, via Catholic Encyclopedia (1913)]:** wheel set with swords, red-hot iron shoes studded with nails, burial alive in quicklime, poison from an enchanter. The sixth-century **Latin Passio** runs to **twenty tortures over seven years, with resurrections between, and 40,900 converted** **[R]**; recensions disagree on order and count. **Alexandra**, empress in some texts and the provost's wife in others, converts and is martyred **[R]**; her historicity is no better than George's.

**Five Caxton passages are carried verbatim at the corresponding journey stops [A as text].** **CORRECTION:** the pool expanded "S. George" to "Saint George" in the line on the enchanter Athanasius; the Fordham text reads "S. George" and is quoted unexpanded.

## 3. Lydda: the head, the grave, the shrine

**Beheaded at Diospolis on 23 April, traditionally 303** **[R]**.

> **TAG CORRECTED, AND THE POOL CONTRADICTED ITSELF.** Chronology lens: [A]. Geography lens, identical event: [R]. Afterlife lens: [A], with a note explaining why it could not be. It is **[R]**. Some manuscript families put the death at **Nicomedia**; that variant is carried unresolved, the Lydda strand followed because it is the strand that grew a shrine.

> **COORDINATES UNIFIED.** The geography lens put every Lydda stop at **31.9515, 34.888** (1.1 km west) and the cathedral at **31.9506, 34.8892**. The afterlife lens was right: **31.953, 34.8995**, used by all six Lydda stops.

> **THE CONSTANTINIAN CHURCH IS REJECTED, AND THE PIACENZA PILGRIM DROPPED.** Chronology lens: a church here in 330. Afterlife lens: 306-337. **No fourth-century church at Diospolis is attested anywhere reachable.** The earliest documented phase is Byzantine, three aisles and three apses plus a smaller church southwest, destroyed **614**. The pool also had the Piacenza Pilgrim recording the shrine c. 570; his travel dates are confirmed, but his itinerary summary lists **no Diospolis and no George**. **Theodosius, c. 518-530**, stands alone as the earliest attestation. The journey carries an undated [R] stop for the grave, an [A] stop for Theodosius, no Constantinian church.

**Modern verdict, restored in full [A as text, R as judgment]:** "No historical particulars of his life have survived, ... The widespread veneration for St George as a soldier saint from early times had its centre in Palestine at Diospolis, now Lydda. St George was apparently martyred there, at the end of the third or the beginning of the fourth century; that is all that can be reasonably surmised about him." Donald Attwater, *Dictionary of Saints*, 3rd ed. (1995), p. 152. The pool spliced this from "survived" straight to "St George was apparently martyred there", unmarked, stranding "there" with no referent. Not carried as a journey quote, being modern secondary commentary.

**Site history [A, Wikipedia "Church of Saint George, Lod"]:** basilica destroyed **614**; **Crusader cathedral on the identical footprint**, c. 47 by 24 m, reusing earlier masonry; **Saladin demolishes it, 1191**, the eastern end with the tomb surviving; the ruined western half made a mosque under the **Mamluks, fifteenth century**; present Greek Orthodox church **1872**, crypt sarcophagus venerated as the tomb, translation kept **3 November**. Continuous custody of a body across those destructions is tradition **[R]**.

## 4. The texts

- **Fifth-century Greek palimpsest**, oldest preserved narrative fragments, identified by **Hippolyte Delehaye** **[A as to the identification]**. Place of writing and of finding unrecorded **[gap]**; pinned at Constantinople **41.0086, 28.9802** as a declared proxy.
- **The so-called Gelasian Decree**, *De Libris Recipiendis*: George among saints "whose names are justly reverenced among men, but whose actions are only known to God" **[A as text; R as to authorship]**. **CORRECTION:** the pool gave "Pope Gelasius I, 494" and "known only to God". The *Decretum* is generally held pseudonymous, a sixth-century Italian compilation; the wording carried is the **Catholic Encyclopedia (1909)** rendering, cited as such, and tertullian.org renders the clause quite differently, as passions "which are better known to God than to men".
- **Sixth century:** a Latin *Passio* in the West, a centre of veneration in Cappadocia **[A as summary]**. **c. 600:** a Syriac version, fragments in the **British Library**, Englished **1925** **[A]**; translator, series, shelfmark unestablished **[gap]**. **Qasr Ibrim, 1964:** a Coptic manuscript of c. 350-500, oldest surviving strand of the parentage **[A as to find and content; R as to it being biography]**. **Bede** names George among the martyrs, the first English trace **[A]**.
- **Georgslied.** **CORRECTION:** the pool's **896** is unattested; Wikipedia gives "towards the end of the 9th century", partially transcribed by c. 1000 in the Heidelberg Otfrid codex by the scribe **Wisolf**, who broke off with "nequeo Vuisolf". **Not carried as a stop**: no place of composition recorded.

## 5. The dragon, which arrives late and from the east

Absent from the earliest hagiographies **[A]**. **Earliest written record: an eleventh-century Georgian text** **[A, Wikipedia "Saint George and the Dragon"]**, pinned Tbilisi **41.6938, 44.8015** as a proxy, place of composition unspecified **[gap]**. **Earliest images: Goreme** cave churches, tenth and eleventh centuries **[A, art-historical]**, pin **38.6431, 34.8286** inherited from `san_cristobal`. **Voragine, c. 1260**, fixes the canonical form; **Caxton, 1483**, fixes it in English **[A]**.

> **SETTING CONTRADICTION, LEFT OPEN.** Earliest versions set the fight **in Cappadocia**; the Golden Legend moves it to **Silene in Libya** **[A as to both, same article]**. Both carried, neither preferred; Silene pinned at Cyrene **32.8244, 21.8578**, the stop stating that Silene is unlocatable and that medieval "Libya" can mean anywhere west of the Nile.

**BEIRUT DROPPED.** The pool's Lebanese coastal tradition carried its own caveat that the dragon-legend article contains no Beirut reference **[gap]**. **The lance named Ascalon** is chivalric romance, not Passio material **[R]**, also not carried.

## 6. The crusaders' captain

- **Alcoraz, 15 November 1096:** George appears to **Peter I of Aragon** **[battle A; apparition R]**. **COORDINATES CORRECTED:** the pool's **42.1263, -0.4478** falls southwest of both battlefield and city; Wikipedia "Battle of Alcoraz" gives **42.1414, -0.3825**. Bare year corrected to the day.
- **Antioch, 28 June 1098:** **George, Demetrius and Mercurius** reported in the Christian line **[battle A; vision R]**. **TAG CORRECTED:** the chronology lens said [A] while the geography lens's note said the opposite. The *Gesta Francorum* report is attested; the vision is not. **Jerusalem, 1099:** apparitions before the city **[R]**, on the **Temple Mount pin 31.778, 35.2354 byte-identical** from `richard_lionheart`, a declared regional anchor.
- **Al-Khader near Bethlehem**, Crusader "Casale S. Georgii", recorded by **John Poloner c. 1421-1422**; imprisonment tradition and healing chains **[place-name and Poloner A; the rest R]**. **Genoa:** the red cross ensign is attested civic history; the arm relic and the derivation of England's cross from it, with an annual fee, are **[R]** and disputed. Voragine compiled the *Legenda Aurea* here **[A]**.

## 7. England

- **Fordington, Dorset**, called the earliest English dedication on the strength of **Alfred's will** **[R]**. **TAG CORRECTED from [A]:** the fabric has "late 11th century origins", most of it twelfth to fifteenth century, three centuries after Alfred's death in 899; his will (S 1507) concerns the estate, not a church of George. Carried as [R] at **50.7138, -2.4283** **[A, from that article, fetched this pass]**.
- **Order of the Garter, Windsor.** **DATE HEDGED:** traditional 1348 is the year of proclamation; *The Complete Peerage* puts the first institution at **23 April 1344**, and 1344-1351 have all been proposed **[A]**. Carried as 1348 with the contest in `date_confidence`. Pin **51.4839, -0.6046** (the two lenses differ by about 15 m).
- **Patron of England.** **OVERSTATEMENT CORRECTED:** the pool had him supplanting Edward the Confessor by the fourteenth century. Wikipedia "Flag of England" makes him primary patron only at the **Reformation, c. 1552**, when the revised prayer book abolished all religious banners except his. Both stages carried as separate stops. **1415:** the feast raised to a **festum duplex** **[A]**. **1493:** George in the **Nuremberg Chronicle** **[A]**, pinned **49.4534, 11.0775**, the Franconia pin inherited from `saint_blaise`.

- **Kyiv, 1054:** a church of George, dedication kept **26 November** **[R]**; pinned at the city centre **50.4501, 30.5234**, no precise coordinate verified **[gap]**. **Moscow:** the mounted George on city arms and state emblem **[A]**, no founding date **[gap]**.
- **Georgia:** patronage traditionally fourth century, **365 churches** one per day **[R, folkloric tally]**, folded into the Tbilisi stop. **Ethiopia:** **Bete Giyorgis** at **Lalibela**, thirteenth century **[church A; the king's vision R]**. **Constantinople:** **DATE CORRECTED** from 1601: **Matthew II moved the Patriarchate to the Phanar convent of St George in 1597**, pin **41.0291, 28.9519**.

## 9. Interlocks with the existing atlas

**Pins inherited byte-identical.** Nicomedia **40.7654, 29.9408** (`constantino_el_grande`, `saint_acacius`, `saint_cyriacus`); Jerusalem / Temple Mount **31.778, 35.2354** (`richard_lionheart`); Goreme / Cappadocia **38.6431, 34.8286** (`san_cristobal`); Constantinople **41.0086, 28.9802** (`saint_blaise`); Franconia / Nuremberg **49.4534, 11.0775** (`saint_blaise`, `saint_acacius`).

**Travelers named in campa where the paths genuinely cross.** `catorce_santos_ayudantes`: George is one of the Fourteen, in that file's roster, "Fourteen and no more" **[A]**. `saint_blaise`: a 1908 source there names George with Pantaleon and Vitus for animals struck by plague **[A]**; Blaise dies a decade later under Licinius in another province, and that file's refusal to merge the dossiers is respected. `constantino_el_grande`: Constantine at Diocletian's court from **293**, same pin **[A]**; the link to George's cult surviving is **[R]**. `saint_acacius`: the edict and the **Myriad Martyrs, 18 March 303** **[A]**, that file itself flagging calendar confusion. `saint_cyriacus`: martyred at Nicomedia, the household of Artemia and Serena **[A to that file; the proximity R]**. `saint_erasmus`: persecution ordered by **Maximian Herculius** in the same wave but at Formiae **[A to that file]**. `san_cristobal`: records Christopher as "hard to tell from George or Theodore" **[A]**, and holds the Goreme pin. `richard_lionheart`: Jaffa and Beit Nuba, 1191-92, the same landscape **[those stops A; the George link R]**.

**Negative interlocks.** No atlas file states a textual link between George and `genghis_khan`, `columbus` or `mary_magdalene`; grep hits are false positives. The canonical **Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262)**, **Paris (48.8566, 2.3522)** and **Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816)** pins have no attested crossing with this dossier and none is force-inherited.

## 10. Open contradictions, left open

Twenty corrections to the pool are stated in place above, each with the pool's version named. What follows is what could not be corrected because the sources themselves disagree.

1. The martyrdom's tag: [A] in two pool lenses, [R] in a third. Resolved to [R], the disagreement recorded.
2. **Nicomedia against Lydda** as the place of death, both in the manuscript tradition.
3. **Diocletian against Dadianus against Dacian** as the persecutor.
4. **23 February against 24 February 303** for the edict; three atlas files carry the twenty-third.
5. **Cappadocia against Silene in Libya** as the dragon's setting; both pinned, neither preferred.
6. **1344 against 1348 against 1351** for the Order of the Garter.
7. **Fourteenth century against 1552** for the supplanting of Edward the Confessor.
8. **Genoa against England** on the origin of the red cross, and the alleged annual fee.
9. The two-century gap between the eleventh-century Georgian dragon and the Golden Legend, with no traced intermediate carrier into Latin beyond "reaches Latin Europe in the twelfth century".
