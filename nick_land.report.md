# Nick Land: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `nick_land`. Living subject; the record is open at the far end.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition carried by no located source · *gap* a stated absence, not filled.

---

## 0. Method

The evidence is lopsided. Land's institutional life (Essex, Warwick, the New Centre) is documented at the level of a Wikipedia biography and no deeper, while his *texts* are documented well, because `Fanged Noumena: Collected Writings 1987-2007` (Urbanomic/Sequence Press, 2011) exists as one edited volume with a datable contents page and a List of Sources. The quotations are therefore the strongest material here: all twelve were checked line by line against the full OCR of that book, downloaded from archive.org (item `fangednoumena`) and read directly, not summarized by a tool. Twelve came back verbatim; seven came back with the wrong page.

The biography rests on English Wikipedia, the articles listed in section 14. Where they hedge ("in the early 2000s", "late 1995"), an earlier draft of the research pool hardened the hedge into a year; those hardenings are reversed. Four pool dates were wrong and are corrected against Companies House, the Wayback Machine, the Library of Congress name authority file, and the published record of Shanghai's 2022 lockdown.

Snippets circulate a Kent village and a March date, uncorroborated by Wikipedia, an academic profile, or an authority record; both excluded. The journey places the birth stop at an England centroid and says in the campa that no record names the town. The coordinate is a placeholder, not a finding.

**Undergraduate institution: unknown.** *gap.* No source names one, and none is guessed at.

## 2. Essex, 1987

**PhD, University of Essex, under David Farrell Krell, on Heidegger's 1953 essay "Die Sprache im Gedicht", which reads Georg Trakl's poetry. [A: Wikipedia, `Nick Land`.]**

*Coordinate correction.* The pool carried two figures for one campus, 51.8721 / 0.9508 and 51.8809 / 0.941, both wrong. The Essex infobox gives 51.87633 N, 0.94487 E for Wivenhoe Park; the journey unifies on **51.8763, 0.9449**.

*Contradiction, not resolved.* The interlock lens claims [R] that the thesis was titled "The Death of Time" and read Kant's Transcendental Aesthetic. Wikipedia says Heidegger on Trakl. These are different theses; nothing located reconciles them or supports the Kant title at all. Recorded as unsupported corpus tradition, flagged in the campa, not adopted.

## 3. Warwick, 1987-1998

**Lecturer in continental philosophy, 1987 to the 1998 resignation. [A: Wikipedia, `Nick Land`.]**

*Place correction.* The pool labelled the post "Gibbet Hill campus", which houses Life Sciences and the pre-clinical medical school; Philosophy sits in Social Sciences on the main campus. The pool's coordinate (52.3793, -1.561) is in fact main campus, so the label was wrong and the number roughly right. The journey uses the infobox value **52.38000, -1.56167**, labelled main campus, which straddles the Coventry boundary into Warwickshire.

**`The Thirst for Annihilation: Georges Bataille and Virulent Nihilism`, Routledge, 1992. [A]** Full subtitle per the LC record: `...Virulent Nihilism: An Essay in Atheistic Religion`. Imprint London **and** New York.

*Claim withdrawn.* The pool called this "his only monograph published during his academic career". The cited source contradicts it: Wikipedia's own bibliography lists `Machinic Postmodernism: Complexity, Technics and Regulation` (SAGE, 1996), also indexed by Open Library as a Land book.

**"Machinic Desire", c. 1993. [R]** Reconstructed from secondary accounts; `Accelerationism` references it undated. The year is a placement.

**"Meltdown", 1995, not 1994. [A]** The pool dated it 1994; the `Fanged Noumena` contents, the authoritative dating for the CCRU-era texts, give 1995.

**"No Future", 1995. [A]** Per that volume's List of Sources: "a previously unpublished conference paper dating from 1995."

**"CyberGothic", 1998. [A: `Fanged Noumena` contents.]**

## 4. The Cybernetic Culture Research Unit, 1995-1998

**Land and Sadie Plant co-found the CCRU at Warwick. [A: Wikipedia, `Nick Land` and `Cybernetic Culture Research Unit`.]**

*Contradiction, not resolved.* Wikipedia gives "late 1995" for the formal establishment while also recording that Plant had run a cyberfeminist research group at Warwick since 1993-94. The article does not reconcile the two, and neither does this dossier. The journey dates the founding stop to December 1995 and puts the difficulty into `date_confidence`.

**Graham Harman's description** of what the unit welded together (futurism, technoscience, mysticism, numerology, complexity theory, science fiction) is quoted second hand via Wikipedia. **[A]**

**Hyperstition and the Numogram. [A: Wikipedia, `Cybernetic Culture Research Unit`.]** The first is the practice of engineering fictions that make themselves real, the second an esoteric numerological-cybernetic system. Later online communities treat both as quasi-occult lore well past the unit's activity: tradition, not CCRU-attested fact.

*Disambiguation.* The pool had output circulating in "zines such as Collapse and Abstract Culture", conflating two publications. Wikipedia names the lowercase ***collapse***, 1995-1996, edited by Robin Mackay with Robert O'Toole. Urbanomic's `Collapse: Philosophical Research and Development` is a separate journal, founded 2006, based in Oxford.

*Date withdrawn.* The pool gave the unit's span as "1995-2003"; Wikipedia dates the dissolution only to "the early 2000s", and the 2003 endpoint is unsourced.

**Mark Fisher on the unit: "was never formally disbanded but then again it was never formally constituted". [A: via Wikipedia.]**

**Virtual Futures 96, Warwick. [A]** Robin Mackay recalls Land lying on the ground croaking into a microphone while Mackay played jungle records. Wikipedia carries two distinct quoted phrases about the series, "anti-disciplinary event" and a conference "in the post-humanities"; the pool merged them and they are kept apart. *Coordinate correction:* the pool's 52.381, -1.5606 is the wrong part of campus; the Warwick Arts Centre infobox gives 52.3798 N, 1.5617 W.

**An earlier Virtual Futures, c. 1994. [R]** Traditionally placed at Warwick before the 1996 event; neither the date nor Land's participation is confirmed by anything fetched.

**Plant leaves Warwick to write full-time, 1997. [A: Wikipedia, `Sadie Plant`.]** She took her own doctorate at Manchester in 1989 and reached Warwick via Birmingham. That is her itinerary, not Land's; the journey includes it as context and labels it so.

**Land resigns, 1998. [A]** **Mark Fisher completes his Warwick PhD, `Flatline Constructs`, 1999, inside the milieu Land led. [A: Wikipedia, `Mark Fisher`.]**

## 5. Leamington Spa and the disappearance, 1998 to the early 2000s

**The CCRU continues after the resignation, unaffiliated, from a flat in Leamington Spa. [A as to the flat; R as to any date.]** Wikipedia gives no year: "The CCRU continued to operate from a flat in Leamington Spa up until Land suffered a breakdown and disappeared from public view in the early 2000s." The pool's 1998 is invented. The move is bounded only as after the resignation and before the breakdown, and the journey dates it inside that range.

**The breakdown, early 2000s, after a period of "fanatical" amphetamine abuse; the unit dissolves around the same time. [A as to the event; R as to the year.]** The pool tagged 2001 as attested while its own note conceded the year was an approximation, which is a reconstruction by definition.

**Where it happened: unknown.** *gap.* No accessible source names a place. The journey pins it at the last known address and says so in the campa.

## 6. Shanghai and Urbanatomy, c. 2010-2014

**Residence in Shanghai confirmed as of 2017. [A: Wikipedia; corroborated by the Guardian, 11 May 2017.]**

*Contradiction, not resolved.* The residency marker is 2017, but the bibliography contains two Shanghai guidebooks dated 2010, `The Shanghai World Expo Guide 2010` and `Shanghai Basics`, both China Intercontinental Press. Writing a city's Expo guide implies residence considerably earlier. No located source gives an arrival date; the arrival is recorded as undated and both readings left standing.

*Label and coordinate corrected.* The pool labelled the Expo pin "Expo Axis" but gave 31.1935, 121.4864, which is the Expo 2010 site centroid, not the Axis. The Expo Axis infobox gives 31.185733 N, 121.487986 E, used in the journey. Expo 2010 ran 1 May to 31 October 2010. Authorship is attested; on-site attendance is not.

**Urbanatomy Electronic titles. [A: Wikipedia bibliography.]** `Calendric Dominion`, `Suspended Animation` (2013); `Templexity: Disordered Loops through Shanghai Time`, `Xinjiang Horizons`, `Dragon Tales`, `Shanghai Times` (2014).

**Urumqi, Xinjiang. [R]** `Xinjiang Horizons` is attested; travel to Xinjiang is not, and Urbanatomy titles were sometimes multi-author or compiled.

**`Fanged Noumena`, ed. Robin Mackay and Ray Brassier, Urbanomic/Sequence Press, 2011. [A]** Editorship confirmed from the scan's title page. The Urbanomic product pages fetched returned no ISBN, print run, or translation data.

**Translation status.** *gap.* No confirmed foreign-language edition of `Fanged Noumena`, `The Thirst for Annihilation`, or `The Dark Enlightenment` was found; a WorldCat lookup for a French translation returned HTTP 403. Unchecked, not absent.

## 8. Xenosystems, the Dark Enlightenment, and the 2017 break

**"The Dark Enlightenment", 2012. [A as to the essay; R as to the exact date.]** It coins the term and elaborates Curtis Yarvin's neoreactionary theorizing, begun 2007-2008. Sources place the essay after Yarvin's writings and no closer; 2012 is the commonly cited year. Issued as a book in 2022.

**Xenosystems, from 2013. [A, firmed up from R.]** The earliest Wayback capture of `xenosystems.net` is **22 February 2013**, with nothing archived before roughly late 2012. *Attribution corrected:* the pool said the blog was later collected as `Templexity`. It was not. `Templexity` is a standalone 2014 Urbanatomy book; the blog was collected as `Xenosystems` (Passage Publishing, 2024).

**"A Quick-and-Dirty Introduction to Accelerationism", 2017. [A: Wikipedia, `Accelerationism`.]**

**The New Centre for Research & Practice ends its relationship with Land, March 2017, after tweets in which he "espoused intolerant opinions about Muslims and immigrants". [A: Wikipedia, direct quote.]** The teaching was online and no travel to the Centre's US base is documented. *Inference removed:* the pool had the tweets posted from Shanghai. Wikipedia attaches no location to them; the 2017 residence is confirmed, the tweets' origin is not.

**Vox, 2019, not 2017. [A]** The pool dated a Vox appearance to 2017 and called it an interview. The cited piece is Zack Beauchamp, "Accelerationism: the obscure idea inspiring white supremacist killers around the world", Vox, **18 November 2019**, a reported feature that quotes Land. Year and framing both corrected.

## 9. Lockdown, erasure, Xenocosmography, 2022-2026

**Xenosystems is removed from the internet, 2022; Land takes a hiatus and returns to social media later the same year. [A: Wikipedia.]** No month for either half, no reason given.

**Shanghai's strict COVID-19 lockdown, 2022, not 2020. [A]** Wikipedia says only "During the COVID-19 pandemic, he experienced Shanghai's strict lockdown measures firsthand." The pool read that as 2020 and called 2020 "the earliest plausible year". There was no Shanghai citywide lockdown in 2020; those were Hubei's. Shanghai's ran from 28 March 2022 (partial) and 5 April 2022 (citywide, some 25 million residents) to midnight on 1 June 2022. No district is named. *gap.*

**Xenocosmography, October 2023. [A]** A new account, marking a shift toward religious and numerological themes.

**Land meets Curtis Yarvin in person for the first time, San Francisco, February 2026. [A]** The venue is not named. *gap.*

**No tomb. [R, by absence]** Land is living, so the standing brief on the tomb and its fate has no object. His only sites of record are institutional (Essex, Warwick) or residential (Shanghai). No obituary was searched for because none exists in the material gathered.

# Deep-dive addition, 2026-08-17: the numogram, the Cthulhu Club, and hyperstition in practice

*Added to the existing file; nothing above this line was altered. Two new segments, twelve new stops, appended after `Xenocosmography`.*

## 15. Scope of this pass

Four lenses were gathered and adversarially verified: the numogram and CCRU apparatus; Lovecraft and the Babylonian material; hyperstition's performativity; and Land's podcast and interview appearances from 2015 onward. The primary text throughout is `Ccru: Writings 1997-2003` (Time Spiral Press, 2015), read as a PDF and grepped, which is why several widely circulated attributions collapse below. The two new segments are:

- **The Decimal Labyrinth** (6 stops): the ten zones and their currents; Pandemonium and the count of forty-five; the Cthulhu Club correspondence; Beaconsfield and Syzygy; the Ghost Lemurs and Captain Mission; the book nobody wrote.
- **Hyperstition in Practice** (6 stops): Catacomic and the four features; sixty, the number of Anu; the intelligence explosion; a critique of trusted third parties; the numogram spoken aloud; Christ dressed up as a computer.

## 16. The numogram and Pandemonium

**Zones, syzygies, currents, tractor zones, gates, channels, the Time-Circuit, Outer-time. [R]** All of it is the unit's own system, presented as discovered rather than invented, and undated in `Ccru: Writings 1997-2003`, p.174-175. Corroborated for the Time-Circuit reading by `ccru.fandom.com`, "Pandemonium Matrix". Tagged **[R]** throughout: it is lore the unit produced, not a fact about the world.

**Pandemonium: numogram plus Matrix, forty-five demons, Gt-45 Utterminus of Cthelll. [R]** Quotation verbatim from p.176 of the collected PDF.

*Contradiction, flagged and not resolved.* The Ccru sentence says the demons are proliferated "by a digital cumulation to the ninth power". Forty-five is the digital cumulation **of** nine, that is 1+2+...+9, not nine to any power. The research pool reproduced the error in a different form ("9 zones times digital cumulation to the ninth power"). The journey states both the arithmetic and the text's own gloss and adjudicates neither.

**Zone-by-zone System Notes (Zone-1 twinned with Zone-8, the demon Murmur, the 5-4 or Sink Current). [R]** Cited in the stop's `sources` as the schema, not narrated.

## 17. The Cthulhu Club

**Vysparov, Stillwell, the 1949 letters, Kingsport, Miskatonic Virtual University, the N. W. Peaslee Chair in Hydro-History, the Jung letter of 27 February 1929. [R, and fiction as to substance]** All from `Ccru: Writings 1997-2003`, and all of it invented. The 7 May 1949 quotation is verbatim, including the volume's own misspelling "Massachusettes"; the letters run p.43-48 of the PDF, not p.42-46 as the pool had it. Stillwell's reply of 28 May is on p.48, not p.47.

*Pool errors corrected before use.* (a) The pool placed the 19 March 1949 letter's Dibboma material in Massachusetts; the text sets it in **eastern Sumatra**, under psychological operations. (b) The pool truncated Stillwell's "electromagnetic cauldron of the inner earth" to "electromagnetic field", inventing a word; the phrase is not used in the journey at all. (c) The pool had Vysparov's Lovecraft interest **caused by** the 1958 Burroughs meeting; the text has the club convened "in the immediate postwar years" and Vysparov seeking Burroughs out afterwards. The causal direction is inverted in the pool and is not carried.

**Echidna Stillwell as "more an ethnographic legend than a social fact". [R]** Verbatim, but **not** from the volume's Foreword as the pool claimed. It sits inside a Ccru self-mythologising text that is itself part of the fiction, so it cannot be read as an editorial disclosure. Not quoted in the journey for that reason; the campa states the flatter fact, that neither correspondent existed.

**Syzygy, a five-week art show co-produced by Ccru and Orphan Drift, named for twinning. [A as to the description: PDF p.8-9, quoted verbatim.]**

## 19. Burroughs and the Lemurian Time War

**Ghost Lemurs of Madagascar is a real Burroughs text of 1987. [A]** **The unit's claim that it retro-influenced Captain Mission three centuries earlier, with a transcript in Mission's own hand in the Vysparov library, is the unit's fiction. [R]** Quotation verbatim.

*Pool errors corrected.* The pool said the text was "sent backward from the future" and implied Ccru invented the title. Neither holds: the book is Burroughs's own, and the Ccru mechanism is retro-influence, not future transmission. The pool also had Burroughs's `Ah Pook Is Here` virus formulation as a **precursor** to hyperstition; the Ccru text reverses this, reading the passage as evidence of Vysparov's influence **upon** Burroughs after 1958. Neither direction is asserted in the campa.

*Also corrected.* "Atlantean White Magic" is not verbatim in the volume; the phrases there are "White Magical orbit" and the **Architectonic Order of the Eschaton (AOE)**, which is what the campa uses.

**Interlock.** `william_s_burroughs.journey.json` exists and carries no reciprocal mention of Land, the CCRU, hyperstition or the numogram. The relation is one-directional from Land's file outward, which is the standing convention for a later figure's engagement with an earlier one. Burroughs died in 1997; the Ccru material about him is contemporary with his last years and after, so the stop makes no claim that he knew of it, and says the opposite.

## 20. Lovecraft: the backward apparatus stop

Lovecraft died in 1937, twenty-five years before Land was born, so this is the ordinary backward case and not a forward reference. The stop sits at Land's own Warwick pin, is dated only to order the segment, and its `date_confidence` states plainly that Land is nowhere on record making this argument in these words. `EXCEPTIONS.md` was updated accordingly: the Nick Land row's "applied backward to" column now names the Necronomicon and the Austin reading.

**Harman on Land as "the first philosopher in recent times to seriously engage with Lovecraft". [A]** *Attribution corrected:* this is **Graham Harman's** judgement as reported by Wikipedia, not Wikipedia's own description, and the pool's gloss about a critique of anthropocentrism is the researcher's addition. The quote field says so.

**"Unuttera", the polytendriled abomination at 9-Barkers. [A as a Ccru coinage: PDF p.6.]** Not used. The blog framing that has Land "envisioning history as presided over by" them is the blogger's, not Land's, and nothing verbatim survived the check in a usable form.

**Cthulhu as a metaphor for capital, the "Cthulhuocene". [R]** No single primary quote located. Not used.

**Land "inhabited" by entities named Cur, Vauung and Can Sah. [A per the Berkeley Institute for Islamic Studies piece, secondary]** Not used: single secondary source, no primary text located, and it makes a psychiatric claim about a living man that the sourcing does not carry.

## 21. Babylon, and what the primary text actually says

**Sumero-Babylonian sexagesimal numeracy as the origin of the second and the degree; the ideal 360-day year mapped onto 360 degrees of zodiac; "Sixty is the number of Anu, the Over-God"; Georges Ifrah on finger-counting; Zecharia Sitchin on the 3,600-year Annunaki astrocycle. [A as to what the Ccru volume says.]**

*The pool's central Babylonian claim is refuted by the primary text and the refutation is what the stop carries.* Commentary (a Scribd piece, and `socialecologies.wordpress.com`) describes the numogram as gathering "Babylonian astrology, Pythagorean tetractys, Kabbalistic gematria" into one chart. The Ccru volume positions the numogram **against** that material: "In contradistinction to the late-Babylonian (or Judeo-Christian) qabbala, the 'method of Mu' involves a rigorous collapse of transcendent symbolism into intrinsic or immanent features", and files "Atlanto-Babyonian [sic] State-societies" as preservers of degraded conclusions. Babylon is a foil, not an ingredient. The campa states both halves.

*Not carried.* "AI does not think; it hallucinates, executes, and loops, like any Pandemonium demon" is the Social Ecologies blogger's own line, not Land's and not the Ccru's. It is named in the stop's `sources` as a tertiary essay whose reading is not adopted, and it is not quoted.

**Babylonian mythology in relation to AI.** The verified route is not through the Babylonian material at all but through Land's own recent speech: the Compact remark that there are not two things going on, superintelligence on one side and demons on the other, and the Greer exchange. That is where the journey puts it. Any direct Land statement joining Mesopotamian numerology to machine intelligence stayed **unverified** and is not asserted.

## 22. Hyperstition: performativity and its real cases

**Coinage 1995, fanzine `Catacomic`. [A: Wikipedia, `Hyperstition`.]**

**The four features: an element of effective culture that makes itself real; a fictional quantity functional as a time-travelling device; a coincidence intensifier; a call to the Old Ones. [A, quoted through Delphi Carstens, who does not page his Ccru source.]**

*Major sourcing correction, and the point of the stop.* The definition everybody quotes, "a positive feedback circuit including culture as a component. It can be defined as the experimental (techno-)science of self-fulfilling prophecies", **does not appear anywhere in `Ccru: Writings 1997-2003`**. A grep of the full 342-page text returns zero hits. It originates in Land's 2009 email interview with Delphi Carstens, "Hyperstition: An Introduction", i.e. a post-CCRU statement by Land alone. The Ccru's own contemporaneous wordings are "fictions that make themselves real" (p.25) and "semiotic productions that make themselves real" (p.47). Two separate lenses in the pool both mis-sourced this to ccru.net or to the Ccru corpus; both are corrected.

**Real cases used.** Bitcoin, from Land's own argument, and the Necronomicon, which is the Ccru's own recurring paradigm case.

*Case rejected.* The pool called Gibson's "cyberspace" the example "most consistently offered by Land/Ccru". It is not. The Ccru volume mentions Gibson once, and names the **Cyberspace Trilogy**, not `Neuromancer` specifically, in a list beside Deleuze and Guattari, Braudel, Octavia Butler, Lynn Margulis and Lovecraft. The framing of it as the canonical example is Macon Holt's (Full Stop, 2020). Dropped rather than downgraded.

**`Crypto-Current: Bitcoin and Philosophy`, version 1.0, 31 October 2018, serialised on Land's Urban Future 2.1 blog. [A]** *Publisher claim withdrawn:* no Urbanomic or MIT Press edition was located. That imprint pairing belongs to `Fanged Noumena` and `#Accelerate`, and is the likely source of the confusion.

**Mackay and Avanessian, `#Accelerate: The Accelerationist Reader`, Urbanomic, 2014. [A as to editors, publisher, year.]** *Quotation dropped:* the framing "earlier hyperstitions coming to pass" could not be located in the editors' introduction; what circulates is a reviewer's line. Not used anywhere in the journey.
