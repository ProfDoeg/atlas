# René Guénon (1886-1951): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02/03 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `rene_guenon`. Calendar gregorian.*

Almost nothing in Guénon's life is a public act: no battles, no embassies, only addresses,
journals, lodges, and two cities. The two Wikipedias carrying most of the reachable chronology
disagree on several load-bearing dates, and the English one carries a date that is physically
impossible. This report keeps the disagreements visible.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or
placeholder coordinate · **[CORR]** a pool claim found wrong and corrected here. Several pool
pins were placeholders silently promoted to [A]; a coordinate invented to put a dot somewhere
in Paris is not an attested fact about a building, and is retagged [R].

---

## 1. Blois and the Loire (1886-1904)

**Birth. [A: English and French Wikipedia, "René Guénon", fetched 2026-08-02]**
René-Jean-Marie-Joseph Guénon, born Blois, Loir-et-Cher, 15 November 1886, devoutly
Catholic family, father an architect. Neither article gives a street, so the pin is the
town, 47.5861, 1.3358.

**The aunt. [A: French Wikipedia]** Fragile health leaves the boy largely in the care of
his aunt, Madame Duru, of Montlivault. The visits continue for decades and stop about 1928.

> **[CORR] Montlivault.** Pool gave 47.6494, 1.4419; French Wikipedia gives 47.6403, 1.4444,
> a kilometre south. The pool also treats Montlivault as merely a place visited; French
> Wikipedia has him *married* there, to Berthe Loury, in July 1912, which the pool's geography
> lens misfiled to Paris. Both corrected.

**Schooling. [A: French Wikipedia]** Baccalauréat at the Blois school now the cité
scolaire Augustin-Thierry, 13 avenue de Châteaudun, 1902-1903; then mathématiques
élémentaires, 1903-1904.

> **[CORR] School coordinates.** Pool gave 47.5875, 1.3305; French Wikipedia gives 47.5975,
> 1.3300, about 1.1 km north. The school was named for Augustin Thierry in 1872, not recently,
> and since January 2025 the lycée is renamed Robert-Badinter while the collège keeps the older
> name. A visitor sent to "the Lycée Augustin-Thierry" will not find it.

**Gap.** No source consulted describes the interior of these eighteen years: what he read,
who taught him, when the interest in metaphysics began.

## 2. Paris and the occultist apprenticeship (1904-1912)

> **[CORR] The year of the move.** The pool's chronology said 1905 while its own geography lens
> said 1904-1906. French Wikipedia's education infobox settles it: Collège Rollin, 1904-1906.
> What belongs to 1905 is the abandonment of the preparation and the move to the island.

**Collège Rollin. [A: French and English Wikipedia]** 1904-1906, mathematics and
philosophy, 48.8797, 2.3421.

**The abandoned competitions. [A: English Wikipedia]** In 1905 poor health ends his
preparation for the Polytechnique and the École normale supérieure.

**Military discharge. [R]** English Wikipedia records a discharge for poor health but does not
date it. The pool's chronology attaches it to 1905, its geography lens to the Sorbonne years.
**Contradiction, not resolved.** The journey carries it in the 1905 stop, undated.

**Rue Saint-Louis-en-l'Île. [A: French Wikipedia]** From 1905 he lives on that street, keeping
an address there for years, including after 1912. **Gap:** no building number for the
pre-marriage lodging.

**Papus. [A for the association, R for the pin]** He enters the milieu of Gérard Encausse
("Papus") and the Martinist order, and is struck off the Martinist rolls in 1909. No source
names the meeting hall; the pool's 48.85, 2.34 is a placeholder, retagged [R].

**Ordre du Temple Rénové, 17 rue des Canettes. [A: French Wikipedia]** First session
6 March 1908; automatic-writing seances at that address the same year; dissolved 1911.

**Second Spiritualist and Masonic Congress. [A: French Wikipedia]** June 1908, where
contact with Léonce Fabre des Essarts begins.

> **[CORR] Who founded the Gnostic Church.** The pool said the Église gnostique de France was
> "founded by Léonce Fabre des Essarts". Jules Doinel founded it in 1890; Fabre des Essarts
> succeeded him as patriarch in 1900. The pool's 48.848, 2.345 is another placeholder, [R].

He is received into it and writes as **Tau Palingenius**.

**La Gnose. [A: French and English Wikipedia]** He directs the review November 1909 to
February 1912, writing much of it himself. **[R]** for the editorial address: inferred from
his residence, not from a masthead.

**Aguéli and the Sufi initiation. [R, contradiction not resolved]** French Wikipedia and most
scholarship place the initiation, mediated by the Swedish painter and Shadhili convert Ivan
Aguéli, in Paris c. 1910-1912, the name Abd al-Wahid Yahya conferred then. English Wikipedia
places the same Aguéli-mediated initiation in Cairo in 1930. **That cannot be right: Aguéli
died in 1917.** The journey follows the French account and states the conflict in
date_confidence. The Al Akbariyya coordinate 48.8867, 2.3431 comes from the English article on
Aguéli; no meeting address appears anywhere consulted.

## 3. Marriage, lodge, war, Sétif (1912-1918)

**Marriage. [A: French Wikipedia; year corroborated by English Wikipedia]** July 1912,
Berthe Loury, religious ceremony at Montlivault.

> **[CORR] The Masonic dating.** The pool's geography lens said "circa 1908-1909", its
> chronology 1912. French Wikipedia gives 1910-1912 for admission to the Loge Thébah and calls
> the affiliation *brief*; the pool's "adding a Masonic thread to his affiliations" overstated a
> short membership. The corrected date settles the pool's hedge about the building: the Grande
> Loge took the rue Puteaux premises in 1911. Pin 48.8847, 2.3183.

**La France antimaçonnique. [A: French Wikipedia]** From 1913 he writes for the
anti-Masonic periodical as "Le Sphinx" while a lodge member. No source consulted explains
the arrangement; recorded as it stands.

> **[CORR] The Sorbonne years.** The pool's geography lens gave 1912-1917; French Wikipedia
> gives the baccalauréat ès arts at the Université de Paris as 1914-1917.

**Sétif. [A: English and French Wikipedia]** A one-year teaching post in French Algeria,
1917-1918. French Wikipedia notes that **no contact with Eastern spiritual masters is
documented for this stay**, against a popular tradition placing his decisive Sufi initiation
there: one of the few places where a source explicitly refuses a legend.

**Return to Paris. [A: French Wikipedia]** 1918.

## 4. The Paris books (1921-1929)

**First book. [A: both Wikipedias]** *Introduction générale à l'étude des doctrines hindoues*,
1921, Marcel Rivière, rue Jacob. Same year: *Le Théosophisme, histoire d'une pseudo-religion*,
and the first articles in the *Revue de Philosophie*. Malraux's verdict, "At its date, a book
capital", reaches this report only through Accart (2005), p. 76, via English Wikipedia.
**[A at one remove]**

**The rejected thesis. [A: English Wikipedia, "Traditionalist School"]** A thesis grounded in
Hindu metaphysics is refused and he leaves the university in 1923. **Caveat:** the source does
not name the rejecting body; the pool's geography lens named the Sorbonne anyway. The journey
keeps that pin and states the gap in date_confidence. *L'Erreur spirite* appears the same year.

> **[CORR] Orient et Occident.** Pool dated it 1925; English Wikipedia's article on the book
> states it was originally published in 1924 in French. The pool had clustered three
> separate events on 1925.

**Le Voile d'Isis. [A: English Wikipedia]** From 1925 he contributes to Paul Chacornac's review,
later renamed *Études Traditionnelles*. **Gap:** no editorial address in any source consulted;
the 48.85, 2.34 pin is a placeholder, [R].

**Vedanta book and Sorbonne lecture. [A: both Wikipedias]** *L'Homme et son devenir selon le
Vêdânta*, 1925; a lecture at the Sorbonne on 17 December 1925.

**La Crise du monde moderne, 1927.** Quotations verified against the Sophia Perennis text.

> **[CORR] The aristocracy quotation splices.** The pool printed "'Aristocracy', taken in its
> etymological sense, means precisely the power of the elite...". The original (ch. 6, p. 78)
> reads "...for this latter word, **at least when** taken in its etymological sense...". The
> pool dropped three words with no ellipsis; the journey marks the elision.

> **[CORR] The sacred/profane quotation is from the wrong book.** The pool attributed "The
> truth is that there is really no 'profane realm'..." to *Initiation and Spiritual
> Realization* (1952), place Cairo, following a Wikiquote heading error. A full-text search of
> all twenty-two English volumes of the Collected Works (archive.org item
> `the-collected-works-of-rene-guenon`) finds it **only** in *The Crisis of the Modern World*
> (1927), ch. 4, p. 53; the 1952 book's nearest parallel is worded differently and argues
> something else. A Paris sentence, not a Cairo one.

**The "période charnière". [R: French Wikipedia]** The later 1920s as a hinge, a growing
conviction that Western restoration is impossible. A summary, not a dated event.

**The deaths of 1928. [A each, contradiction not resolved]** English Wikipedia has his wife
Berthe Loury dying in 1928; French Wikipedia attaches a 1928 death to his aunt, Madame Duru.
Not the same woman. One journey stop states both and resolves neither.

**Autorité spirituelle et pouvoir temporel. [A: English Wikipedia]** 1929.

## 5. The crossing (1930)

**[A for the fact and month, R for both ports]** He leaves France for Egypt in September 1930
on a journey conceived as temporary research travel. No source consulted names the port of
embarkation, the ship, or the port of landfall. Marseille and Alexandria are the standard
Mediterranean route of the period, carried as reconstructions and labelled as inference in
date_confidence.

## 6. Cairo, the old city (1930-1937)

**Khan el-Khalili. [A: English Wikipedia]** He settles in the medieval Islamic quarters and
lives there seven years, among artisans and shopkeepers rather than in the European city.

> **[CORR] Chacornac is not being quoted.** The pool printed "When he arrived, his outward
> behaviour had changed and he had completely immersed himself in the popular Islamic milieu of
> the city" as a direct quotation from Paul Chacornac. It is English Wikipedia's own body text,
> footnoted to Chacornac 2005, and must not sit in quotation marks attributed to him.
> Paraphrased in the journey; the French original was not consulted.

**Al-Azhar. [A: English Wikipedia]** He attends lectures and frequents the circles of
al-Azhar. Undated beyond "the 1930s"; no course or year recorded.

**Sayyidna al-Hussein. [A: English Wikipedia]** He meets Sheikh Mohammad Ibrahim at the
mosque; the friendship leads to marriage into the sheikh's family.

**Hamidiyya Shadhiliyya. [R: English Wikipedia, Guénon and Aguéli articles]** He deepens his
standing in the Shadhili order in the orbit of Sheikh Salama Hassan al-Radi's Hamidiyya branch.
Coordinate approximate. Cairo consolidates an affiliation begun in Paris; see section 2.

**Marriage to Fatima Ibrahim. [A for the marriage, R for the site]** 1934, four children. The
ceremony's location is not given in any source consulted; the old-city pin is a placeholder.

**Mecca. [R, and not a journey]** Guénon is not recorded performing the hajj. The Kaaba pin is
inherited from the corpus (abdelkader, hallaj, ibn_battuta all carry 21.4225, 39.8262) as the
qibla of the Cairo years, labelled in the journey as orientation, not visit.

## 7. Dokki and the last years (1937-1951)

**Villa Fatima. [A: English Wikipedia]** In 1937 he and his wife acquire a house in Duqqi
(Dokki), west across the Nile, named Villa Fatima; the purchase is made possible by the
English disciple John Levy. He lives there until his death.

> **[CORR] "At the foot of the pyramids" must go.** English Wikipedia says the villa was "in the
> modern district of Duqqi, west of Cairo, at the foot of the pyramids". The trailing phrase is
> false: Dokki is an inner-city district on the west bank, roughly 10 km from Giza. The pool
> quoted the error, then flagged the mismatch as a "contradiction" with an Azhar-area house.
> There is none: he lived near al-Azhar 1930-1937 and in Dokki from 1937, in sequence.

**Schuon. [A: English Wikipedia, "Frithjof Schuon"]** Schuon visits Egypt in 1938 and meets
Guénon for the first time after seven years of correspondence from Mulhouse; he stops again in
1939 en route to India. Presumed to be at Villa Fatima, which the article does not state,
making the location [R] inside an [A] event.

> **[CORR] Maritain and the Index: the date.** The pool said 1940, admitting it was
> "approximately in the World War II era pending firmer dating". English Wikipedia is explicit
> that the request came after the war, when Maritain was French Ambassador to the Vatican, and
> failed on the refusal of Pius XII. Maritain held the post 1945-1948, so c. 1946.

**Egyptian citizenship. [A, minor discrepancy]** English Wikipedia gives 1949, French Wikipedia
a range 1948-1951. Both retained; 1949 pins the stop, the range sits in date_confidence.

**Death. [A: both Wikipedias]** 7 January 1951, at home in Dokki, aged 64.

> **[CORR] The last word, and the biographer's title.** English Wikipedia reads: "his final
> word was 'Allah'." Only that word sits inside quotation marks; the rest is Wikipedia's
> narration, footnoted to Chacornac 2005, p. 98. The pool also gave the book the wrong title: it
> is *La Vie simple de René Guénon* (1958; 2005 reprint), not *Le Simple et le Sens*.

> **[CORR] Burial: one marker, not two, and the coordinate was wrong.** The pool carried the
> burial twice and 4 km apart: 30.0074, 31.2528 (geography lens) and 30.0387, 31.2653
> (afterlife lens, "the Darassa cemetery"), the latter tagged [A] while its own source line read
> "exact primary citation not confirmed". Neither Wikipedia names a cemetery, and al-Darrasa is
> a *neighbourhood* beside Al-Azhar Park at roughly 30.050, 31.266, not a necropolis. The
> journey carries **one** burial stop, [R], at 30.0500, 31.2660.

**Gap.** No verified account or photograph of the grave marker was located; reports that
visitors find it by chance are web aggregations with no citable source. The post-1951 fate of
Villa Fatima is likewise unestablished.

## 8. Afterlife

**Output. [A: French Wikipedia]** Seventeen books in his lifetime; ten posthumous
collections of articles; twenty-seven regularly reprinted titles.

**Publishers. [R: aggregated publisher-catalogue web sources]** Éditions Traditionnelles held
the historic catalogue; Gallimard later revised eleven titles with critical apparatus while
reprinting thirteen from that list. **[R: Sophia Perennis via religioperennis.org]** The English
collected-works project was undertaken because prior translations had been "piecemeal".

**Spanish complete edition. [A: Spanish Wikipedia]** April 2023, twenty-three volumes plus an
introductory volume, edited by Javier Alvarado Planas of the UNED.

**Papers. [R]** Manuscripts and correspondence said to be held in part at the BnF; the claim
reaches this report only through aggregated web sources and no shelfmark was obtained. An
unpublished manuscript is said to have appeared in 2001, likewise only through web commentary.

**Influence. [A: English Wikipedia, "René Guénon" and "Traditionalist School"]** Translated into
more than twenty languages; a foundational chapter in the scholarly history of Western
esotericism; traced into Iran through Ali Shariati and Seyyed Hossein Nasr, into some Pakistani
madrassa curricula, into Moroccan Sufi circles, and among European writers including Eliade,
Daumal, and Queneau. The claim that King Charles III has read him is unverified here.

> **[CORR] Coomaraswamy was not a disciple.** The pool said Coomaraswamy, Schuon, and Nasr
> "each explicitly named him as their intellectual source". The Traditionalist School article
> distinguishes them: Nasr "credits Guénon from whom he derives his idea of Tradition"; Schuon
> followed "in Guénon's footsteps"; of Coomaraswamy it says only that his idea of Tradition is
> *similar to* Guénon's. Nine years the elder: a parallel, not a debt. His published praise,
> quoted in the journey, is genuine.

**Legend. [R: traditionalist commentary, uncorroborated]** A later legend frames the death as a
sacrificial compensation, and ties his birth to a supposed return of "Isiac" influence in the
Razès region. Recorded as legend, not pinned.

## Contradictions left open

The Sufi initiation, Paris c. 1910-1912 vs Cairo 1930, the latter impossible since Aguéli died
1917. The 1928 death, Berthe Loury the wife (English) vs Madame Duru the aunt (French). The
military discharge, 1905 vs the Sorbonne years, undated in both. Egyptian citizenship, 1949 vs
1948-1951. The rejecting body of the 1923 thesis, named as the Sorbonne by the pool but not by
the source. The burial place: no cemetery named by either Wikipedia, and two mutually exclusive
pool coordinates 4 km apart, now one hedged marker.

## Gaps stated as gaps

The birth house in Blois. The building numbers on the rue Saint-Louis-en-l'Île. The meeting
places of Papus's circle, of the Église gnostique, and of Aguéli's Al Akbariyya, all three
placeholders. The editorial address of *Le Voile d'Isis*. The port of embarkation, the port of
landfall, the ship, and the date of arrival in September 1930. The site of the 1934 marriage.
The zawiya frequented in Cairo. The office where citizenship was processed. The cemetery, and
the grave. The present state of Villa Fatima.

## Interlocks with the existing corpus

Named in the journey campa: **abdelkader** (Akbarian current; Freemasonry at Alexandria 1864,
mirror of the Loge Thébah), **ibn_arabi** (source of the silsila), **blavatsky** (target of
*Le Théosophisme*; her Cairo of 1871, her Paris of 1884), **molay** (Templars, 1314),
**napoleon** (1798, Egyptomania and Egyptian Rite Masonry), **ibn_battuta** (Cairo, 1326),
**hallaj** (Baghdad, 922), **emin_arslan** (Paris 1893-1909), **borges** (the posthumous
readership). **gurdjieff** is in this report but not in a campa: Paris and Fontainebleau-Avon
1922-1949, classed among pseudo-initiations. No corpus file mentions Guénon, so every pairing is
[R] beyond what each file attests of its own subject.

Canonical pins inherited byte-identical: Kaaba **21.4225, 39.8262**; Paris **48.8566,
2.3522**; Buenos Aires **-34.6037, -58.3816**. The Jerusalem pin (31.778, 35.2354) was offered
as a comparative-religion anchor but is **not used**: Guénon never went there.
