# Carl Theodor Dreyer (1889-1968) research report
*2026-08-24, for `carl_theodor_dreyer.journey.json`. 45 stops, 9 segments, gregorian.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or composite-filmography claim not traced to a reachable source. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved. Placeholder coordinates are named as such here and in the stop's `date_confidence`. Two independent institutional sources carry most of the weight, **Dansk Biografisk Leksikon** (**DBL**) and the **Danish Film Institute extended biography** (`carlthdreyer.dk/en/carlthdreyer/about-dreyer/biography/biography-extended`, **DFI-bio**); where they disagree, both readings are stated.

## 1. Copenhagen, 1889-1904

**[A DBL; DFI-bio]** Born 3 February 1889 in the Vor Frue parish, illegitimate son of Josefina Bernhardina Nielsson, a Swedish housekeeper, and Jens Christian Torp, the Danish landowner in whose house she worked. **[A DFI-bio; en-wiki]** First two years in foster or orphanage care; **no institution is named in any source reached**, and the coordinate 55.6761, 12.5683 is the central-Copenhagen placeholder the dossier reused for five unrelated addresses.

**[A DBL]** Adopted 1890 by the typographer Carl Theodor Dreyer Sr. (1864-1911) and Inger Marie Dreyer, née Olsen (1863-1937), and given the adoptive father's full name.

**[A DFI-bio]** The biological mother died 20 January 1891, aged 35; DBL independently gives her dates as 1855-91. **[R]** The tradition that she died attempting to end a later pregnancy with phosphorus scraped from match heads (Drouzy) is stated by **neither** DBL nor DFI-bio as fetched. Death [A], method [R]; the file carries the split.

**[A DFI-bio]** Municipal school on Nansensgade, then the private Frederiksberg Realskole; finals 1904; left home shortly after, aged fifteen. No university, no film school. The dossier had this at [R]; it upgrades to [A] and gains both school names. Quotation used: *"constantly let me know that I should be grateful for the food I was given and that I strictly had no claim on anything since my mother got out of paying by lying down to die."*

## 2. 1904-1912: ledgers, newsprint, the air

**[A DFI-bio]** The brief Copenhagen Utility Company post came **first**; the Great Northern Telegraph Company ran **September 1905 to early 1908**, left "horrified by the prospect of a lifetime filing ledgers." The dossier reversed the order and had both ranges wrong. The Kongens Nytorv pin is a landmark-level approximation.

**[A DBL]** Berlingske Tidende from 1909. **[A DFI-bio]** The aviation work belongs to **Berlingske, not Riget**: he covered aviation there, took flying lessons, scooped the first cross-Sound flight, and in **1911** was the first passenger on such a flight, with the French aviator Poulain. Riget came afterward. The dossier's "aviation and ballooning specialist at Riget" is wrong twice: wrong paper, and **no fetched source puts him in a balloon**. DBL lists Riget as an employer, so the paper is [A] while the aviation framing is not. *Gap:* no departure field for the flight; the stop uses a strait-level proxy.

**[A DBL]** Married Ebba Frederikke Larsen 19 November 1911; the marriage ran to his death. Adoptive father died 1911 (DBL: 1864-1911; exact date not given). **[A en-wiki]** Gunni born 1913, Erik 1923.

**[A DBL]** Ekstra Bladet from 1912, **[A DFI-bio]** including a profile of Asta Nielsen. **[R]** The sale of *Bryggerens Datter*: composite filmographies only. Quotation used, via Bordwell: *"Even when I was with Ekstrabladet, I was conservative...I don't believe in revolutions."*

## 3. 1913-1921: Nordisk, Valby

**[A DBL]** Hired 1913 as scenario reader, writer, and editor; roughly twenty-two scripts filmed by 1918. **[A DFI Filmdatabasen]** *Ned med Vaabnene!* (von Suttner, dir. Holger-Madsen, Nordisk) premiered **18 September 1915**; the dossier's flat "1914" is at best the writing year, and the file says written 1914, premiered September 1915.

**Contradiction, not resolved: *Præsidenten*.** DFI-bio: directed **1918**, shot on **Gotland**. DFI's own film page: production year **1919**, Swedish premiere **3 February 1919**, Danish premiere **9 February 1920** at Panoptikon, **no mention of Gotland**. DBL: **1920**, which on the above is the Danish premiere. The stop is dated 1918, narrates both premieres, and states all three readings. **Gotland downgrades to [R]**, and 57.6349, 18.2948 is Visby, an island-level proxy.

**[A DBL]** *Blade af Satans Bog*, 1921, four episodes, built on Griffith's *Intolerance*.

## 4. 1920-1926: stave church, rented studio, foreign money

**[A en-wiki, 'The Parson's Widow']** *Prästänkan*, Svensk Filmindustri, released **4 October 1920**; exteriors and interiors both at the Garmo Stave Church and the Maihaugen museum; source Kristofer Janson's *Prestekonen*, omitted by the dossier. Upgraded [R] to [A], with three corrections: the coordinate is **61.1111, 10.4761**; the church is **medieval**, c.1150 on the site of a 1021 predecessor, not "seventeenth-century"; and it was re-erected at Maihaugen only in **1920-1921**, so he filmed a building being reassembled around the production.

**[A DBL]** *Die Gezeichneten / Elsker hverandre*, Germany 1922, from Madelung, casting Jewish refugees; **[R DFI-bio]** Primus-Film of Berlin, city named, no address. **[A DBL]** *Der var engang*, 1922, after Drachmann, surviving only incompletely; **[R DFI-bio]** Hellerup studio space rented from Benjamin Christensen, no date or address, so the stop's month orders the segment.

**[A en-wiki, 'Michael (1924 film)']** *Michael*, from Herman Bang, premiered Berlin **26 September 1924**; producer Erich Pommer, cameras Karl Freund and Rudolph Maté, screenplay Thea von Harbou and Dreyer. Two corrections: the production was **Decla-Bioscop, not UFA** (en-wiki dates the merger to 1928, so "at UFA" is anachronistic for 1924), and Studio Babelsberg is at **52.3869, 13.1194**, 2 km from the dossier's value. The **Fritz Lang** link is real but indirect and the campa says so as it makes it: von Harbou was Lang's wife and collaborator, and **no source reached shows a personal association between the two directors**.

**[A DBL]** *Du skal ære din Hustru*, 1925, from Svend Rindom; its French success produced the Joan of Arc commission.

## 5. 1926-1931: the trial at Billancourt

**[A en-wiki, 'The Passion of Joan of Arc']** Over eighteen months in the 1431 transcripts; twenty-nine interrogations across eighteen months condensed into one day.

**[R]** The matches anecdote (Marie Antoinette, Catherine de' Medici, Joan of Arc) **is** in that article, framed as Dreyer's own **claim**, which is why it stays [R]. **[R, attribution corrected]** On Delteil: en-wiki, citing **Dreyer, *Four Screenplays* (Indiana UP, 1970), p. 21**, says the rights to Delteil's 1925 book were bought and he was credited but nothing of his used. The dossier credited Bordwell and misnamed his book. Rendered as narration, never in quotation marks: **no verbatim Dreyer sentence to this effect was found.**

**[A en-wiki, 'Renée Jeanne Falconetti']** Falconetti was found in an amateur production of **La Garçonne**, specific where the dossier was vague; screen tests without makeup the next day; Artaud as Massieu, confirmed twice. **[R en-wiki]** The concrete Rouen at Billancourt, walls ten centimetres thick, painted pink to read grey on panchromatic stock, by Hermann Warm and Jean Hugo.

**[A en-wiki; DFI film page]** Copenhagen premiere, **Palads Teatret, 21 April 1928**; Paris premiere, **Cinéma Marivaux, 25 October 1928**, after nationalist objections to a non-French, non-Catholic director and after the Archbishop of Paris and government censors demanded cuts. **[A en-wiki]** The original negative burned at UFA in **Berlin, 6 December 1928**.

**[A en-wiki]** The lawsuit upgrades [R] to [A]: he "sued them for breach of contract. The lawsuit went on until the fall of 1931, during which time Dreyer was unable to make another film." *Gap:* **no docket, judgment, or damages figure was located**, so the outcome is stated as unknown.

**The cruelty legend, left open. [R]** The claim that Dreyer tormented Falconetti into the performance, traced to Hélène Falconetti's family biography and postwar criticism, is not adjudicated. Documented: the shaving was contractual from the start, she knelt on stone through repeated takes, the close-ups are extreme, she never filmed again. Not documented: any contemporary investigative or court record of systematic cruelty. The stop states both sides.

## 6. 1930-1936: Courtempierre, and the silence

**[A en-wiki, 'Vampyr']** Financed by and starring Nicolas de Gunzburg as "Julian West"; loosely from Le Fanu; manor scenes April-May 1930, churchyard August 1930, shot around **Courtempierre, Loiret**, the church a barn with tombstones placed by Warm. Coordinate corrected to the commune's own **48.1036, 2.615**. Caution: DFI's *Vampyr* page calls Courtempierre the **fictional village name inside the film** and names no château, so the dossier's "château at Courtempierre" is softened to interiors shot in and around the commune. Berlin premiere 6 May 1932, booed; a Vienna showing produced a riot demanding refunds; Paris that September on the Boulevard Raspail.

**[A en-wiki]** Absent from the March 1933 Copenhagen premiere, having suffered a nervous breakdown and being "in a mental hospital in **France**." **The Paris siting is not attested**: no city is named anywhere reached, DFI-bio as fetched does not mention a clinic, and 48.8566, 2.3522 is the Paris centroid. The stop keeps the pin for map legibility and says only "France" is on record. Clinic, doctor, and diagnosis are gaps.

**[A DFI-bio]** 1934: "Dreyer contracted malaria while attempting to film **L'Homme ensablé** in Italian Somaliland and returned to Denmark unable to complete the project." The dossier left the travel open; he travelled. Mogadishu remains a **territory-level proxy**. **[R DFI-bio]** A brief return to criticism at *B.T.*, c.1936, recorded here but not given a stop, to hold the file inside the 45-stop cap.

## 7. 1942-1945: under occupation

**[R]** *Mødrehjælpen* (1942) comes from composite filmographies; **[A DFI-bio]** the run of commissioned public-information shorts through the state cultural-film apparatus from 1942 is attested. **[A en-wiki, 'Day of Wrath']** *Vredens Dag* "premiered at the **World Cinema** in Copenhagen on November 13, 1943." The dossier's own note claimed the cinema was unnamed in everything consulted; it is named, and this upgrades [R] to [A]. En-wiki also supports the reception: poor reviews, complaints of slow pacing, weak box office, postwar rehabilitation. *Gap:* the street address, so the pin is a placeholder.

**[A en-wiki]** Left occupied Denmark for Sweden shortly after release on the stated pretext of arranging foreign sales; stayed to the war's end; later denied a conscious Nazi allegory. **Contradiction, not resolved:** one fetched source gives the residency as **1940-1945**, the whole occupation, against the filmographies' **1943-45**; the stop is dated December 1943 and states the conflict. **[R]** *Två människor* (1945), from W. O. Somin, made under restrictions he disliked and afterward repudiated: composite filmographies only.

## 8. 1952-1963: Dagmar, and the Word

**[R for 1952; A DFI-bio for the award]** Management of the Dagmar Bio cinema, awarded by the state, called by DFI-bio his most consequential business appointment and a stable late-life income.

**[A en-wiki, 'Ordet']** Four months of filming, two in studio and two at **Vedersø**, West Jutland, where **Kaj Munk** was parish priest before his 1944 murder by the Gestapo; premiere at the **Dagmar Teatret, 10 January 1955**; Golden Lion at the **16th Venice festival**. The Vedersø coordinate is village-level.

**[A DFI-bio]** Jesus-film research at the **British Museum**; travel to **Israel**; taught himself Hebrew. *Gap:* **no year for the Israel journey**, so the stop's 1958 is an ordering device and says so. **[R]** *Om filmen* (1959), corroborated by Danish Wikipedia as a gathering of articles and interviews. **[A DBL]** Ingenio et Arti medal, 1963.

**Quotation-source correction.** *"there must be harmony between the genuineness of feelings and the genuineness of things"* is real and the page cite is right, but the essay carrying it is **not** Casper Tybjerg's; it is **Acquarello, 'Dreyer, Carl Theodor', Senses of Cinema (2002)**, the dossier's Tybjerg URL (`/2003/…`) returning 404. Chain as used: Dreyer, radio interview for *New Perspectives on the Arts and Science*, 23 October 1950, in Skoller, ed., *Dreyer in Double Reflection* (Da Capo, 1973), p. 143, quoted in Acquarello.

## 9. 1964-1968: Gertrud, and Dalgas Boulevard

**[A DFI-bio]** *Gertrud* shot at Nordisk Film, Valby, 1964, the complex where he was hired in 1913. **[A en-wiki, 'Gertrud (film)']** Premiere at **Le Studio Médicis, Paris, 18 December 1964**, wrecked by equipment failures, poor subtitles, and reels shown out of order. Booed at **Cannes, 1965**; at a **Venice** screening more than half the audience walked out and the rest gave a standing ovation that visibly moved him. The rue Champollion coordinate is an approximation, and Cannes is named inside the Venice stop's confidence field rather than staged as a scene, the stop count being at cap. **[R]** The unrealized *Medea* with Elsa Gress (1965): standard bibliographies only. The later Danish television production made from that script postdates his death and is **recorded here only**; the campa named it in an earlier pass and the forward reference has been removed, so the file embeds nothing after 20 March 1968 except the Gyldendal printing.

**[A DFI-bio]** In **1967** the Ministry of Culture allocated **three million kroner**, one tenth of the estimated budget, to the Jesus film. **Dropped for want of a source:** the claim that he corresponded with the American producer **Blevins Davis for sixteen years** appears in **no fetchable source**, is not in the journey file, and is recorded here in case a citation turns up.

**[A DBL; DFI-bio; en-wiki]** Died **20 March 1968**, aged 79, of **pneumonia**. The dossier's hedge that the cause was contested and Wikipedia-only is withdrawn: DFI-bio states "Dreyer died March 20, 1968, at age 79 from pneumonia," making two independent sources. DBL localises the death to **Frederiksberg (Lindevang)**, the parish containing Dalgas Boulevard, validating the 55.6821, 12.5145 pin.

**[A DBL; Danish Wikipedia; en-wiki]** Buried at **Frederiksberg Ældre Kirkegård**, established 1734 behind Frederiksberg Church. Upgraded from [R]; coordinate corrected to **55.67333, 12.53444**, the dossier's value being about 1.2 km west in a residential block. *Gap:* the plot. Quotation used: *"Carl Th. Dreyer was a modest man, as were his sitting room and, later, his grave."* **[A Danish Wikipedia]** *Jesus fra Nazaret: Et filmmanuskript*, Gyldendal, 1968.

## Coordinates, and interlocks used and refused

The dossier reused **55.6761, 12.5683** (Rådhuspladsen) and **55.6759, 12.575** for at least seven unrelated Copenhagen addresses; those pins are kept where nothing better exists and every stop using one declares it, as do Visby, Mogadishu, the Øresund midpoint, and Paris for the 1933 hospital. Four were **corrected**: Garmo, Babelsberg, Courtempierre, Frederiksberg Ældre Kirkegård. The 1928 fire, where the dossier's lenses contradicted each other (geography at Neubabelsberg, afterlife at 52.4732, 13.2624, which is Berlin-Dahlem, with no UFA laboratory), is settled **not by a better guess but by inheriting the corpus pin byte-identical**: **52.464, 13.409**, from `falconetti.journey.json` and `fritz_lang.journey.json`. Also inherited: 48.87, 2.308 · Billancourt 48.831, 2.256 and 48.8303, 2.2562 for the Artaud scene · Palads Teatret 55.676, 12.564.

Named in a campa, each on a relation of Dreyer's own: **`joan_of_arc`**, in whose trial minutes he worked for eighteen months and out of which he built the film, one-directional across five centuries and real · **`falconetti`**, whom he found, tested, directed, and had shaved on camera · **`artaud`**, whom he cast as Massieu and who wrote about the film afterward · **`fritz_lang`**, indirectly through Thea von Harbou, the campa saying in the same breath that no friendship is evidenced.

**Refused: `lionel_royer`.** That file compares Royer's 1887 *Jeanne d'Arc à Reims* with Dreyer's film as "two reconstructions of one document," correctly placed there. **Royer died in 1926, before production, and Dreyer never engaged with him in any direction.** Shared subject matter is not a relation Dreyer had, so he is not named here.

**Not staged here, by the same rule:** **Rouen**, the research attested but **a personal visit confirmed nowhere**, so the trial record is a `suggested_ref` on his Paris stop · **Falconetti's 1946 Buenos Aires death and 1960 reburial**, hers · **the 1981 Dikemark find and 1984 Oslo examination**, posthumous and already in `falconetti.journey.json`, though his own 1929 reconstruction is a `suggested_ref` on the fire stop · **the 2006 Kulturkanonen induction** and **Jensen's *My Metier* (1995)**, both [A] posthumous reception, recorded here only.
