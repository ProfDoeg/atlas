# Alfonso X el Sabio (1221-1284): research report

*Research pass for `alfonso_x_el_sabio.journey.json`. Castile and Leon, 1221-1284; the body's own fate carried to 1948.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-only claim not independently confirmed in this pass. Contradictions are printed as contradictions and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

The methodological fact about this subject: almost every date is softer than the secondary literature makes it look. Four candidate years for the conquest of Jerez, two each for the Fuero Real, the executions of 1277, the repopulation of Cádiz, the death of Fernando III, and the birth of an illegitimate son. The journey carries each as a contradiction inside `date_confidence` and picks none.

---

## 1. Birth and minority, 1221-1240

- Born **Toledo, 23 November 1221**, feast of San Clemente, son of **Fernando III** and **Beatriz de Suabia**. **[A, es.wiki Alfonso X]**. No contemporary source names the palace or room; the pin is the old-city landmark. Gap stated in the stop.
- Sworn to as heir, **Burgos, 21 March 1222. [A]**
- **1231, the lower-Andalusia cabalgada** (Salamanca, Toledo, Andújar, Córdoba, Palma del Río, a battle near Jerez). Campaign and year **[A]**; any *personal role* is **[R]** and is contradicted by the same source, which gives command to **Álvar Pérez de Castro**. Alfonso was nine. The journey states the campaign was fought in his name.
  - *Pool corrections:* the pool had Alfonso "participating"; he did not. Both long Castilian quotations arrived with every accent and cedilla stripped, which changes words rather than merely dropping diacritics, and are restored as the source prints them (*más, quebrantó, Andaluzía, después, esfuerço, avién, tamaño; Mandó, él, moço, esfforçado, Pérez*). The pool also filed the Álvar Pérez passage under "Murcia/Andalusia campaigns"; in the source it belongs to the 1231 cabalgada ordered by Fernando III. The companion *Estoria de España* passage on the "cabalgada de Xerez" is **retrospective chronicle written under Alfonso decades later, not eyewitness testimony**, and its voice is third person about the Muslims of Andalusia, not a royal first person plural.
- **Beatriz de Suabia dies at Toro, 5 November 1235. [A, es.wiki Beatriz de Suabia]** *Pool correction:* the pool placed the death at Toledo with Toledo's coordinates. Toro is 41.5214, -5.3933. Buried at Las Huelgas, Burgos; moved to Seville in 1279.
- **Alfonso Fernández "el Niño"**, illegitimate son by **Elvira Rodríguez de Villada**, born **c. 1240-1242. [A, es.wiki Alfonso Fernández el Niño]**, which also gives the marriage to Blanca Alfonso de Molina, the lordship of Molina and Mesa, and death in 1281. *Upgrade applied:* the pool carried this as [R] ("dossier assertion"); it is independently verifiable, and only the year is in dispute. Place is **[R]**, given only as "Castile" and pinned at Burgos as a declared placement.

## 2. The heir in the field, 1243-1249

- **Treaty of Alcaraz, 1243. [A, es.wiki Tratado de Alcaraz]**, signed at **Alcaraz, Albacete (38.66, -2.49)**, with the Hudi rulers of Murcia. Murcia is what the treaty delivered, not where it was signed. *Pool correction:* the pool sited it at Murcia. The journey splits treaty and consequence into two stops. Campaign runs 1243-1245 (Lorca and Mula spring 1244, Cartagena early 1245).
- **Treaty of Almizra, 26 March 1244. [A]**, **Jaime I signing in person**. Two pool corrections: (i) Almizra is modern **Campo de Mirra, 38.6867, -0.7803**; the pool's 38.6333, -0.8667 is Villena, about 8 km off, and the geography lens's 38.7167, -0.7667 about 3.5 km off. (ii) The pool said the treaty was concluded "by Fernando III and Jaime I through their heirs." The source names Jaime I and the Castilian heir.
- **Jaén, 1246. [R]** for Alfonso's role: presence in the army is established, independent command is not.
- **Seville, 1248. [A]** Alfonso present among the besieging princes.
- **Marriage to Violante de Aragón, Colegiata of Valladolid, 29 January 1249. [A]**

## 3. Accession and the southern frontier, 1252-1266

- **Proclaimed king, Seville, 1 June 1252. [A]**
  - *Contradiction flagged:* Cervantes Virtual gives "1252, 30 de mayo: Muere Fernando III"; es.wiki gives 31 May. The pool silently merged death and proclamation into one dated line. The journey states the proclamation and prints the 30/31 May split.
- **Algarve, 1253. [A, en.wiki Alfonso X]**, year only. Route, terms, duration of occupation are in no source reached here. Stated as a gap in the stop.
- **Jerez: four candidate dates, none preferred.** **1253**, an action in es.wiki Alfonso X; **late 1260 / early 1261**, a forced submission, same article; **1264**, es.wiki Jerez, definitive incorporation after the Mudéjar revolt; **1266**, same article, "algunas investigaciones recientes sitúan la reconquista en el año 1266," on a royal charter. *Pool correction:* the pool led with 1253, the weakest, and the geography lens named only 1253 and 1261, missing what the Jerez article says. All four printed in the stop.
- **Sack of Salé, 1260. [A]** Alfonso's presence with the fleet is not established and is not claimed.
- **Niebla, February 1262. [A]**, quoted.
- **Cádiz: contested year.** The reign chronology gives 1262 for the repopulation; **es.wiki Cádiz** gives incorporation into Castile in **1264**, from the Marinid sultanate, with Alfonso granting tax privileges, city status, an episcopal see and an African trade monopoly. *Pool correction:* the pool stated a flat 1262.
- **Mudéjar revolt, 1264. [A]** Jerez, Arcos, Lorca, Murcia, with Muhammad I of Granada behind it.
- **Jaime I recovers Murcia, January 1266. [A]** for the event, **[R]** for the month, which is dossier-sourced and not re-fetched.

## 4. The law codes

- **Fuero Real: contradiction flagged.** Cervantes Virtual under 1254, "Se termina de redactar el Fuero real"; es.wiki 1255. Both printed. *Pool correction:* the pool's claim also conflated this with Cervantes Virtual's separate 1254 entry, "Creación del Estudio general de Sevilla." Two entries, kept apart.
- **Espéculo, 1255. [A]** as a listed date; its relation to the Partidas is unsettled and not settled here.
- **Libro de las Leyes / Siete Partidas**, composition framed by its own prologue **1256-1265 [A]**, not promulgated in his lifetime.
- **Siting corrected.** The pool's geography lens sited the Fuero Real and Libro de las Leyes at a "Toledo royal scriptorium." Neither source attributes them to Toledo; the court was itinerant and the texts are **dated, not localised**. All three law stops are pinned at Seville as a declared placement, stated in each `date_confidence`. Toledo is kept only for the translation and astronomical work, where it is secure.
- **Quotations used**, Scott's translation as printed in en.wiki Siete Partidas: I.i.11 (the law-maker and God), II.i.10 (the tyrant). In `suggested_refs`: I.i.20, II.x.1, II.xxi.4. *Defect flagged, not repaired:* the pool's innkeeper quotation (V.viii.26) splices across a silent elision of roughly twenty words with no ellipsis, and the source text itself carries a garbled clause, "or in ships which knowledge of the owners," a defect in the printed source and not ours. **Not used**, because it cannot be given cleanly in one line.

## 5. The scriptorium

- **Ghayat al-Hakim into Castilian, 1256-1258. [A, en.wiki Picatrix]**, from the Latin prologue. Year inside the window, translator's name, and **city** are all unrecorded. Toledo is the canonical atlas placement and the gap is stated. *Pool correction:* the afterlife lens called the Castilian version "now lost." en.wiki Picatrix says the Latin was made "based on translation of the Spanish manuscripts," and that Spanish and Latin were the only versions known to Western scholars until Printz found an Arabic one around 1920. The Castilian **survives in part**; the journey does not call it lost. Editions in `suggested_refs`: Pingree (1986), Attrell and Porreca (2019), Ouroboros partial English 2002 and 2008.
- **Alfonsine Tables.** Observations from c. 1262 **[R]** (start year soft). **Epoch tied to the coronation of 1 June 1252 [A, en.wiki Alfonsine tables]**. Castilian lost; a Paris Latin revision is what circulates. **First printed 1483, second 1492, superseded by Kepler's Rudolphine Tables 1627 [A]**. *Pool correction:* the pool twice gave **Venice** for the 1483 printing. The cited article says neither Venice nor that the printed editions were Latin. Venice and Ratdolt are standard bibliography carried by no source reached here, so **Venice is dropped**.
- **Libro de las cruces** and the astrological corpus at Toledo, c. 1259-1272. **[R, Cervantes Virtual]**
- **Yehuda ben Moshe ha-Cohen** and **Isaac ben Sid ("Rabicag")**, the scriptorium's two most load-bearing collaborators. Almost nothing of either life survives in the sources reached, and **neither has an atlas file.** Queue candidates, recorded in `suggested_refs`.
- **Cantigas de Santa María**, first compilation **1274 [A]**; Escorial and Florence codices early 1280s **[A, en.wiki Cantigas]**. Four codices: Toledo (BNE, 129 songs, no miniatures), Escorial *Códice Rico* (c. 195 songs, narrative miniatures), Florence (111 songs, empty staves), Escorial "Musicians' Codex" (406 songs, 41 miniatures). **Authorship is open and the file says so:** the es.wiki "Autoría" section presents the prologue as something that *"apoyaría"* personal authorship, a hedged claim, and notes Mettmann's attribution of many cantigas to **Airas Nunes**. The prologue text used is a **modernized Castilian rendering**, not the Galician-Portuguese original and not Kulp-Hill, and the stop says so; one accent restored (*razón*).

## 6. The fecho del Imperio

- **Pisan embassy at Soria, 1256. [A]**, quoted. **Double election, April 1257 [A]**, in Germany, **Alfonso not present**, so it is carried inside the Soria campa and is not a stop.
- **Beaucaire, 1275. [A]** Interview with Gregory X, refusal, renunciation.
- **Fernando de la Cerda dies at Villa Real, 1275. [A, O'Callaghan 1993; es.wiki; en.wiki]** **Alfonso was not there**; he was in Provence. Under the standing rule the death scene is Fernando's. What sits on Alfonso's file is the stop where the news reaches him, pinned at Beaucaire, with `date_confidence` stating that the place is not recorded.

## 7. The last decade

- **Noble revolt, 1272. [A]** Nuño González de Lara, Lope Díaz III de Haro, the infante Felipe. Place given only as "Castile"; declared placement at Burgos.
- **Mesta, 1273: split tags.** **[A]** for the royal privilege (es.wiki Mesta, "creado en el Reino de Castilla en 1273"); **[R]** for the founding, since es.wiki Alfonso X says "Se desconoce el año exacto de la fundación, pero debió tener lugar entre 1230 y 1263." Both halves carried.
- **Executions of the infante Fadrique and Simón Ruiz de los Cameros: contradiction flagged.** es.wiki places the **conspiracy in 1277** and says only that the king, recovering from illness, "mandó ajusticiar a los principales confabulados," naming no year for the killings. Cervantes Virtual reads **"1278: Conjuración y muerte del infante don Fadrique."** *Pool correction:* the pool claimed Cervantes Virtual "converges on 1277." It says 1278; the false claim is removed. No formal charge survives.
- **Algeciras, 1278 or 1279: deliberately NOT a stop.** es.wiki dates the failed siege and naval defeat to 1278, other accounts to 1279, and **nothing in the verified pool establishes that Alfonso was personally there**; the operation was run by his commanders. The defeat is carried as a closing fact inside the **Salé 1260** campa. Flagged for the operator in case a source does place the king there.
- **Valladolid assembly, 21 April 1282. [A]** **Summoned by Sancho, with Alfonso in Seville.** Not a stop here for the same reason; narrated inside the Seville stop for the November sentence.
- **Testament and judicial sentence disinheriting Sancho, Seville, 9 November 1282. [R]** as to the exact day.
- **Marinid alliance, 1282-1283.** The alliance is a documented reversal **[A]**; the detail that Alfonso **pledged or deposited his crown** as security is **[R]** and could not be traced to a primary document. Carried as reported tradition and labelled as such in both campa and `date_confidence`.
- **Libro de los juegos, Seville, 1283. [A]** The colophon of Escorial MS T-I-6 states it was begun and completed in Seville (RBME catalogue item 13125).

## 8. Death and the body

The journey has **no afterlife segment**. The only thing that follows the death is Alfonso's own body, because he was there.

- **Death, Seville, 4 April 1284. [A]**, cause unrecorded.
- **Burial, Seville Cathedral, 1284. [A]** Beside Fernando III and Beatriz de Suabia. *Pool correction:* the Capilla Real now shown as his grave **did not exist**. es.wiki Capilla Real: construction began **1551** under Martín de Gainza, most work finished by 1575, and "los restos mortales de los monarcas sepultados en la catedral de Sevilla no fueron trasladados a la nueva Capilla Real hasta **1579**," under Felipe II. Burial and translation are two separate stops.
- **Heart and entrails to Murcia, 1284. [A]** Transferred into the cathedral's Capilla Mayor **in 1525**, at the request of the Murcian council and by order of Charles V (es.wiki Catedral de Murcia: "En el año 1525, a petición del concejo de Murcia, y por orden del emperador Carlos V"). *Source error flagged, not propagated:* the Murcia municipal press release prints **"1425,"** impossible, since Charles I of Spain was born in 1500. The loyalty-as-cause explanation is framed by the source as *"la tradición atribuye"* and carried as tradition. The forensic detail of two parallel surgical incisions on the left ribcage is **[R]**. Restoration chronology partly verified: the municipal article yields **1854** (after a fire), **1997** (capillary humidity), and the **11 August 2025** agreement with the Diocese of Cartagena, 43,000 euros over about two months. The **1677** and **1948** openings claimed in the same pool entry are **not in that article**; carried in `suggested_refs`, not asserted in prose.
- **1579, Seville: translation into the new Capilla Real. [A]**
- **1948, Seville: new sepulchres for Alfonso and Beatriz for the seventh centenary of the conquest; tomb opened. [A, es.wiki Capilla Real]** The *ajuar* inventory (gold-worked tunics with castles and lions, pearl-and-coral linen cap, gloves, silk purse, three pillows, three rings, a small dog's skeleton at the feet; crown, sword, sceptre and baton already missing) is **[R]**.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Alfonso X is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`**, and nothing in this pass needed the backward-apparatus licence: every stop is rooted in his own life or in the fate of his own body. But the interlock lens surfaced relations **not rooted in his life**, and per the adversary's duty they are named here rather than silently deleted. **All of them run FORWARD, the direction the exception never licenses.** None is staged as a stop; each is carried only as a `suggested_ref` or `sources` line on Alfonso's own stop for the work concerned.

1. **Nicolaus Copernicus** owned and had professionally bound a printed copy of the Alfonsine Tables while a student at Kraków. Copernicus engaged Alfonso's work; Alfonso could not engage Copernicus. `suggested_ref` on the Toledo observations stop, direction stated inside the ref; the scene belongs on `nicolaus_copernicus.journey.json`. **Johannes Kepler**, whose 1627 Rudolphine Tables displaced the Alfonsine, is the same case. Ref only.
2. **The Latin Picatrix at Florence, 1489**, and **Giovanni Pico della Mirandola**'s *Disputationes adversus astrologiam divinatricem*, which attacks exactly the judicial astrology Alfonso's commission transmitted; Pico's file never names Alfonso or the Picatrix. Likewise **Kraków c. 1450, Vatican Library c. 1450, London c. 1600**. All postdate him; not carried.
3. **Marsilio Ficino.** `QUEUE.md` pairs Alfonso with Ficino as "the Picatrix line," an editorial framing of two queue entries, not a journey-file intersection, and unwritable into Alfonso's file in the forward direction.
4. **The 14th-century Paris Latin revision of the Alfonsine Tables** is the one forward item in the journey's prose, because the Castilian does not survive and the revision is the only form in which the commissioned thing exists. Transmission history, named to no later person.

Not apparatus questions, flagged in the same spirit. **Moses de León**: his file cites an Oxford abstract situating an unsigned penitential work "in Alfonsine Castile's cultural context," and itself flags the contest, since another pool ties that work to **Sancho IV's** court one reign later, with 1284 on the boundary. Shared context, not a relation between two people; not named. **Roger Bacon**: his file has Ibn al-Haytham's *De Aspectibus* "carried west through Toledo's translators," a movement a century older than Alfonso's scriptorium. Institutional continuity, not contact; not named. **Jaime I of Aragon** is a genuine relation (father-in-law, Almizra, the 1266 recovery of Murcia) with **no journey file**; named in campas as a historical actor, with no slug to interlock. `yolande_aragon.journey.json` covers a later branch of the Aragonese house (14th-15th c., via John I) and must **not** be treated as covering this.

## Interlocks named in a campa

**One: `maslama_al_majriti`.** Alfonso ordered the translation of the book that travels under his name, a direct if one-directional engagement with that author's work, exactly the relation the house rule licenses. The two rival candidates for the *Ghaya*'s authorship, `maslama_al_qurtubi` and `ahmad_al_majriti`, are **not** named: the dispute is modern and Alfonso had no relation to either man as such. Canonical pins inherited byte-identical from the three Picatrix-line files: Toledo **39.8628, -4.0273**; Seville **37.3833, -5.9903**; Murcia **37.9922, -1.1307**.

## Honest gaps

**The Algarve campaign of 1253**: year only, no route, terms, or duration. **The place of the law codes**: dated, not localised; every legal stop carries a declared placement. **Places given only as "Castile"**: the illegitimate son of c. 1240, the births of Fernando de la Cerda and Sancho, the 1272 revolt, the 1277 executions. **Personal presence unestablished** at Jerez 1231, Jaén 1246 (presence in the host only), Salé 1260, Algeciras 1278-79, Valladolid 1282. **The Vitoria illness-and-Marian-cure tradition** could not be tied to any cantiga number, text, or scholarly citation; the corpus does contain autobiographical illness material, but **nothing locates it at Vitoria**, so it is dropped rather than staged on a guess. **Partidas quotations** are Scott as reprinted on Wikipedia.

## Attributed sayings

**"Quemad viejos leños…"** Unused. Attributed to Alfonso X only by Luis Señor, *Diccionario de citas* (1997), p. 17. The English provenance chain the pool carried is a **misattribution and has been cut**: English Wikiquote traces "Old wood to burn! Old wine to drink!…" through Melchior de Santa Cruz (1576) ii.1.20 and Francis Bacon, *Apothegms* (1624) 97, to **"Alonso of Aragon"**, that is **Alfonso V of Aragon**. **[R]**, for a stronger reason than the pool claimed; the pool also stripped the tilde from *leños*.

**"Those who knowingly allow the King to err…"** Unused. Vicente Vega (1952), no Partidas book, title or law number, not locatable in the Scott excerpts reachable here. **[R]**.

**The creation remark**, used on the Segovia stop as **[R]** posthumous legend: no document of his reign contains it, and Jerónimo de Zurita rejected that form in the sixteenth century. es.wiki *Alcázar de Segovia* records a **structural collapse** in 1258 with the king inside, no fire, and does not mention the legend at all. *Pool correction:* the pool carried the legend half under an [A] tag. It is [R].
