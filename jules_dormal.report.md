# Jules Dormal Godet (Julio Dormal), 1846-1924: research report

*2026-08-21. Belgian architect-engineer of Buenos Aires: third and last architect of the Teatro Colón, finisher of the Palacio del Congreso, author of the La Plata Government House facades, the Portones de Palermo, the Estación Mar del Plata Sud, and the house on Plaza San Martín in which he died.*

**[A]** attested · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim not independently confirmed · **[X]** source named and unreachable.

The spine of the record is one document, cited below as **PDF**: the heritage report attached to expte. 2680/09-10, HCD Provincia de Buenos Aires, a bill to protect the Estación Mar del Plata Sud, whose pp.4 and 8-9 carry the fullest Spanish-language biography of Dormal in print. Direct fetches returned corrupted binary; it was parsed locally with pypdf. It is also the origin of most of the errors in circulation about him: it misdates the Teatro de la Ópera, misspells Pestalardo, misnames Celedonio Pereda, gives him sole authorship of the Palermo park, and mis-transcribes its own key stylistic phrase. Each is flagged rather than quietly fixed.

---

## 1. Liège and Paris, 1846-1868

- Born at **Liège in 1846** **[A**, PDF p.8, "Nació en 1846 en Lieja (Bélgica)"; weboeba.com; hilariobooks.com**]**. No civil birth act located, **no day or month on record** (gap). Full name **Jules Dormal Godet** **[A**, weboeba.com, matching the FADU-UBA inventory**]**.
- **Engineering at the University of Liège** **[R**, Hodge 1979; PDF**]**. No matriculation record produced.
- **The Paris school is a genuine contradiction, left open.** École des Beaux-Arts under Trélat, Chabat (PDF: "Chabet"), Gosicher and Viollet-le-Duc **[R**, PDF**]**; École centrale des arts et manufactures **[R**, buenosaires.gob.ar; weboeba.com**]**, which trains engineers and not architects; a third strand names the Polytechnique. No roll, diploma or dated inscription found for any of the three. Not adjudicated.

## 2. Arrival in Argentina, 1868-1870

- **Contracted to build a meat-extract factory at Gualeguay, Entre Ríos** **[A**, PDF p.8**]**. It failed; no company name, site or closure record located (gap).
- **The arrival year is contested three ways**: 1868 (PDF; Hodge) · 1869 (buenosaires.gob.ar) · 1870 (Belgian minister-counsellor **Francis Sobry**, 2024, per fmradiocultura.com.ar). All three carried.
- The placement at **Puerto Ruiz** is the pool's own inference; **the PDF never mentions it**, saying only "una fábrica de extractos de carne en Gualeguay". Softened in the journey file.
- **Settled in Buenos Aires 1870, office opened** **[A**, PDF; Sobry**]**; address unknown (gap).
- **Friendship and patronage of Sarmiento and Avellaneda** **[R**, Hodge, "a close friend of Sarmiento and Avellaneda"**]**. No letter, minute or diary entry on either side, and `sarmiento.journey.json` does not mention Dormal.

## 3. Palermo, 1874-1876: a claim cut back

The pool's headline claim, that Dormal made **the first layout of the Parque Tres de Febrero**, is **rejected**. Its quote is accurate to the PDF p.8 ("Dormal realizó el primer diseño del trazado del Parque Tres de Febrero... El trabajo comenzó en 1874"), but es.wikipedia contradicts it: the design plan is **Nicolás Descalzi's**, the engineer in charge **Jordán Czeslaw Wysocki**, the competition **won by Methfessel and Börmel** (whose scheme "no se concretó por sus costos elevados"), and Dormal is assigned **the Portones de Palermo and the main avenue**.

Carried: second place in the 1874 competition **[R**, Schávelzon via the pool**]**; authorship of **the gates and the avenue [A]** inside a project led by Wysocki; park inaugurated **11 November 1875**, 25 ha **[A]**; the Portones down around **1917** **[R]**, no demolition order located.

## 4. The associations, 1876 and 1886

- **Sociedad Estímulo de Bellas Artes, 23 October 1876** **[A]**. The PDF confirms founding membership but **carries no year**; the date and roster (Sívori, Camaña as first president, Schiaffino, Vaca Guzmán, Alfred Paris, Gutiérrez, Agujari, "el arquitecto Julio Dormal") come from es.wikipedia, corroborated **from inside the atlas** by `angel_della_valle.journey.json`. Meetings in Sívori warehouse space near the port **[A**, hilariobooks.com**]**, no street address (gap).
- **Sociedad Central de Arquitectos, 18 March 1886: his founding membership is doubtful and is left doubtful.** The PDF calls him "uno de los diez miembros fundadores... junto a Ernesto Bunge y Juan Antonio Buschiazzo" **[R]**, but `juan_antonio_buschiazzo.journey.json` carries an independent roster of **nine** founders and **Dormal is not on it**, while the PDF's own enumeration also runs to nine names without him. A real contradiction between an Argentine state document and the atlas's own file, stated as such in the stop.
- **SCA reconstitution c.1901-02 jointly with Alejandro Christophersen** **[R]**: his own file names him among the refounders and **does not name Dormal as co-convener**, so the partnership rests on the pool's tradition alone.

## 5. La Plata, 1882-1892/1895

- **Cornerstone 27 November 1882** **[A**, es.wikipedia; same date in `pedro_benoit.journey.json`**]**. **General plan from the Architecture Section of the provincial Department of Engineers under Pedro Benoit; facades, interiors and direction of works by Dormal** **[A**, argentina.gob.ar; decree norma-304920**]**. The decree also puts an **Italian architect, Luis Baldi**, beside Benoit; the encyclopedias omit him in favour of Dormal. Flagged, not resolved.
- **Builders**: José Porret from 1882, **Santiago Bertelli y Cía 1883-1892** **[A]**. **Completion contested between two arms of the same state**: es.wikipedia has it inaugurated **1892**, argentina.gob.ar "concluded in **1895**". Formulation used: cornerstone 27 Nov 1882, inaugurated 1892, concluded 1895. No inauguration day or month located (gap).

## 6. Buenos Aires, 1880-1905

- **San Martín's cathedral mausoleum, 1880: division of labour contested.** Two government sources credit Dormal (PDF p.8, "Fue responsable del Monumento a San Martín"; buenosaires.gob.ar) and **neither says what "responsable" covers**; es.wikipedia credits the ensemble **solely to Carrier-Belleuse**. Carried: **[A]** for "credited with the architectural setting", **sole authorship rejected**. 1880 is the year of interment, not necessarily of his work.
- **Plaster bust of Eduardo Sívori, signed** **[A**, hilariobooks.com**]**, restored at TAREA/UNSAM, shown at the 2023 MNBA exhibition. **The year 1881 is not in the source**: the pool rendered it "signed and dated [1881]", the brackets a tell that the year was supplied rather than read. **Dropped as a claim**, used only to order the segment.
- **Teatro de la Ópera remodel, 1888** **[A**, argentina.gob.ar, confirming the date and the phrase "suntuosa imagen Beaux-Arts"**]**. Three corrections to the PDF: the original theatre is **1872**, not 1871; the entrepreneur is **Antonio Pestalardo**, not "Petalardo"; the original architect, omitted by the PDF, is **Émile Landois**. Demolished 1935, rebuilt by Bourdon, reopened 7 August 1936.
- **Hipódromo Argentino de Palermo** **[R]**, listed by buenosaires.gob.ar and the PDF ("procedimientos novedosos para su época"), both **undated and unsupported by any permit**; the city tourism page covering the course from 1883 does not mention him.
- **Palacio Ortiz Basualdo, completed 1904** **[A**, FADU-UBA inventory**]**, at **Arenales y Maipú** per the PDF, not "opposite Plaza San Martín". **Do not conflate** with the other Palacio Ortiz Basualdo, the French embassy at Cerrito 1399, which is Pablo Pater's. **First prize for facades, 1905** **[A**, PDF**]**, for "several private residences" per the PDF but for this townhouse per Hodge; citation not located (gap).
- **Professor of architecture at the UBA until his death** **[R**, Hodge**]**. No appointment record or course listing located.
- **Municipal art commission under intendente Alberto Casares, with Carlos Thays, Eduardo Schiaffino and Ernesto de la Cárcova** **[R]**, PDF only. **[X]** SEDICI/UNLP, "Fragmentos de admiración", exceeded fetchable size.

## 7. The Colón and the Congreso, 1904-1909

- **Meano murdered 1 June 1904, aged 44** **[A**, teatrocolon.org.ar**]**; **Dormal assumes honorary direction February 1905**, and all that follows is **[A**, es.wikipedia *Historia del Teatro Colón***]**: elimination of spaces; the **glazed marquee over Calle Libertad**; **demolition and reconstruction of the mobile orchestra platform**, charged to Pellizzari y Armellini; disagreements with Meano "especialmente en materia de seguridad"; **Armellini's withdrawal, 11 April 1907**, "con su sistema nervioso agotado", sharpening the pool's vague "April 1907"; **structure completed 28 February 1908, opened with *Aida* 25 May 1908**.
- **The safety charge predates him.** `francesco_tamburini.journey.json` carries the 1903 municipal inspection verdict (Susini, *Arquitectura* N.6, July 1904, via Shmidt), calling the half-built theatre "el producto de una empresa de lucro y de especulación". Corroboration from outside the pool that the charge was not contractor-family lore.
- **The stylistic verdict is a misquote, corrected.** The pool attributed "genialidad artística" to the PDF. PDF p.8 reads "toda su **GENEROSIDAD** artística y su **influencia estilística BORBÓNICA**". The claim is verbatim, the phrase is not, and "influencia estilística borbónica" is the source's real judgement.
- **Tamburini's death year is wrong in the Argentine tradition.** es.wikipedia has him dying in 1890 "con apenas 44 años"; en.wikipedia and it.wikipedia give **1891**, and his birth year is itself contested (1846 vs 1848), so **"aged 44" is not securely establishable**. That figure seeds the architects'-curse legend.
- **Palacio del Congreso**: Meano's 1895 competition win; construction begun **August 1897 (es.wikipedia) or 1898 (PDF)**, carried open; **inaugurated 12 May 1906** **[A]**; Dormal completes it keeping Meano's design and adding his own decoration including the dome **[A]**; fully finished only in **1946**.
- **The Lagae bust: upgraded from [R] to [A].** The claim failed a first pass (AGORHA/INHA 403; es.wikipedia and teatrocolon.org.ar silent). **weboeba.com supplies the whole chain**: bronze, 40 cm, modelled by **Jules Lagae in 1909**, donated by the sculptor's son, formalised by "Decreto – Ordenanza n.o 9966 del 12 de agosto de 1962", inaugurated **September 1962** in the foyer off Calle Libertad, with the plaque transcribed. Silence in two sources was not evidence of absence.

## 8. Mar del Plata and the last works, 1910-1922

- **Estación Mar del Plata Sud, "en forma de L", "proyectado y dirigido" by Dormal** **[A**, PDF Art. 1, p.4**]**, on the block of Alberti, Sarmiento, Garay and Las Heras, entrance "solo siete cuadras de la plaza Colón". **The clock tower on the Sarmiento axis was drawn and not built.** First train to the platforms **1 December 1910**, a wooden shed serving meanwhile; definitive building **1 December 1911**. Last season 1947-48, rails lifted 1951, buses until 2009. **[X]** es.wikipedia's *Estación Mar del Plata Sud* and a Ferrocarril Sud variant, both 404.
- **The old wooden rambla at Playa Bristol is NOT carried as his.** The attribution recurs in secondary catalogues **[R]**, but the PDF discusses the "rambla afrancesada" repeatedly and **never names Dormal as its author** while devoting a full section to him. Meaningful negative evidence.
- **Concejal of Buenos Aires 1910-1915** **[A]**; the specific proposals (widening Corrientes, taller buildings) are **[R]**, and the Concejo's minutes were not consulted (gap).
- **Edificio Peña, Florida 466, inaugurated 1913** **[A**, buenosaires.gob.ar**]**. **Confitería Richmond opened 17 November 1917 at Florida 468** **[A**, es.wikipedia, sharpening the city page's bare "1917"**]**; note the **two-number discrepancy**, building at 466, confitería at 468. The Grupo de Florida association (Güiraldes, Borges, Girondo) is confirmed by both. **Dormal's presence in the café is nowhere documented** and is not asserted. The neighbouring **Peña-Blaye residence, later the Sociedad Rural Argentina**, rests **[R]** on a Wikimedia Commons caption alone.
- **Palacio Pereda**: **Celedonio** Pereda (the PDF's "Celestino" is its own error) replaces **Louis Martin** with Dormal in **1919** over the interior and staircase **[A**, argentina.gob.ar; es.wikipedia for the date**]**; work runs 1920-1936, Sert ceilings 1932. It passed to Brazil **after Pereda's death in 1945**, **not "desde 1944"** as the PDF has it.
- **San Martín 1137, his own house** **[A** for authorship and description, arquitectos-franceses blogspot, confirming Corinthian shafts, cherub reliefs, Ionic pairs and triangular pediment verbatim**]**. Occupant: the **Instituto del Cemento Portland Argentino**; the PDF's "Asociación de Cemento Portland" and the pool's "Instituto/Cámara Argentina" are both loose. **Construction year unresolved, 1913 vs 1920** (infobae Nov 2024 gives 1920, the blogspot none). The figures (1,700 m², six floors, 30 rooms, 315 m² lot, APH 38) come from a **2024 sale listing**, advertised and not surveyed.
- **Holograph will, 1922**, "in perfect Spanish, entirely in his own handwriting", signed "Julio Dormal", in family hands and unpublished **[A**, Magdalena Paz Miguens, 2024**]**. **The text was not seen.**

## 9. Death and the unresolved remains

- **Died 22 March 1924 at San Martín 1137** **[A**, blogspot "falleció aquí el 22 de Marzo de 1924"; PDF p.9**]**, aged 77 or 78, no birth day being on record. **Never adopted Argentine citizenship despite fifty-plus years' residence** **[A**, blogspot**]**, **contradicted** by Sobry's 2024 remarks calling him a naturalised Argentine. Not resolved.
- **Buried at Recoleta in the mausoleum of his second wife, Elena/Elina Sosa Díaz** **[A**, recoletacemetery.com; PDF**]**. **Cremated 1989, remains "likely placed inside the Dolmas Arévalo vault"** **[R]**, the source itself calling the reason undocumented. **Neither tomb exists at Recoleta today** and the final resting place is unknown **[A]**. No official cemetery response obtained.
- **Family, two irreconcilable counts.** PDF p.9: **two marriages** (Amalia Peña, then, widowed, Elina Sosa Díaz) and **three children** (Carmen, Julia, Carlos). fmradiocultura.com.ar, from the descendants in 2024: **three marriages, ten children, six grandchildren, thirty great-grandchildren, over 250 great-great-grandchildren.** The pool's "over 300 living descendants" and its branch names **are not in the source**. Both carried, neither preferred.
- **No writings by Dormal exist to edit** (gap). No book, memoir or treatise by him was located; the nearest thing to an edition is the project catalogue reconstructed in secondary literature (Hodge 1979; De Groof et al. 1998). Iconography is thin: one c.1910 portrait via the Archivo General de la Nación, a scatter of building photographs, and the 1909 bronze.

## Coordinates and interlocks

Every working coordinate was re-checked against Nominatim and **eight were materially wrong**, from ~310 m (Hipódromo) to ~17 km (Puerto Ruiz, in open country); the Palacio Pereda had the wrong street entirely, and San Martín 1137 came in at two contradictory values, neither right. Each corrected value is stated in its stop's `date_confidence`. Canonical pins are inherited byte-identical for the Teatro Colón, the La Plata Casa de Gobierno, Paris, the Casa Rosada and Parque 3 de Febrero. The cathedral pin is taken from `san_martin.journey.json` rather than the value the pool proposed; **Recoleta** is -34.5875, -58.3936 from Buschiazzo's file, while Benoit's carries -34.5878, -58.3932 for the same cemetery, noted in the stop and not resolved.

Seven travellers are named in campas, each on a direct or one-directional relation, never on shared geography: `pedro_benoit` (supplied the La Plata plan Dormal built the facades and interiors over, **[A]**) · `francesco_tamburini` (first architect of the Colón, whose drawings Dormal inherits at two removes, **[A]**) · `juan_antonio_buschiazzo` (SCA roster contradiction; 1899 demolition campaign; named in the Pellizzari chronicle, **[A]**) · `san_martin` (Dormal credited with the setting of his cathedral mausoleum, **[R/A]**) · `sarmiento` (reported patron and friend, **[R]**) · `alejandro_christophersen` (claimed co-convener of the SCA reconstitution, **[R]**) · `carlos_thays` (claimed fellow member of the 1903 art commission, **[R]**). `angel_della_valle.journey.json` is cited as a **source** only: della Valle's link is eighteen years' later employment by the institution Dormal helped found, which is not a personal relation.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Jules Dormal is NOT listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** Nothing was silently deleted. Five relations in the pool are **not rooted in his own life**, and all five are **FORWARD**: events postdating him, or other people's travel. A forward reach is not licensable by an agent's judgement, and none of the five is a backward apparatus application, so none is a candidate for the list. They survive as `sources`, `suggested_refs`, or closing facts inside a stop otherwise entirely his.

1. **The 2024 centenary homage** (Salón Dorado, Teatro Colón; Magdalena Paz Miguens, Roberto Dormal, Roy Cortina, Francis Sobry, Grementieri, Bellucci). Their travel, not his. Carried as the *source* for his own 1922 will stop.
2. **The 1962 installation of the Lagae bust.** The stop is dated **1909**, the year Lagae modelled him alive; the 1962 decree and installation close that stop as facts about the object, never as a scene.
3. **The 1989 cremation and possible transfer to the Dolmas Arévalo vault.** Forward, but concerning his own remains, so folded into his burial campa, on the pattern of `abdelkader.journey.json` and its 1966 repatriation.
4. **Rodin's *The Thinker* in Plaza del Congreso, 1907** (`auguste_rodin.journey.json`). **Dropped**: shared geography and a shared year, nothing more, and the Rodin stop does not mention Dormal. Dropped for the same reason, the Rodin **Sarmiento** of 1900 and **Carrier-Belleuse's Paris atelier**, which is Rodin's relation and not Dormal's.
5. **Carlos Thays enlarging the Parque 3 de Febrero in 1893** (`carlos_thays.journey.json`). Thays is named **only** at the 1903 municipal art commission, a claimed direct professional relation **[R]**, and **not** at either Palermo park stop, where the link would be shared ground alone.

**One gap flagged the other way.** `san_martin.journey.json` has **no stop for the 1880 repatriation and interment**. Noted here; **nothing was written into that file**.
