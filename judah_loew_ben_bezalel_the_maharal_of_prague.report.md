# Judah Loew ben Bezalel, the Maharal of Prague: research report
*2026-08-28. For `judah_loew_ben_bezalel_the_maharal_of_prague.journey.json`: 8 segments, 43 stops, register "national mythology: the canon is true", calendar `gregorian`.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or legend. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

The hardest fact about this subject: the famous part of him is not attested and the attested part is not famous. In the documents he is a Moravian regional rabbi who spent most of his life failing to get the Prague chief rabbinate, printed nine or ten books, and died at about ninety. The golem, the emperor's magician, the miraculous birth: none of it appears before the 1830s. The file is built on the documented man and treats the legend as a separate, dated afterlife.

## 1. Name, dates, origins

**[A]** Judah Loew ben Bezalel, called by the acronym MaHaRaL. Loew, Löw, Liwa and Leib all render "lion," and the lion is on his tombstone.

**[R] Birth year: unresolvable.** Jewish Encyclopedia: "about the second decade of the sixteenth century," in Posen. Jewish Virtual Library: c. 1525. Encyclopedia.com: "approximately 1520" in its text while its own bibliography carries André Neher's 1512, so **the entry contradicts itself**. Jewish Museum in Prague: "ca. 1525-1609." No autobiographical statement, no register, no record. The file uses **1520-01-01** to order the segment and says so in `date_confidence`.

**[R] Birthplace: Posen, with Worms competing.** The Jewish Encyclopedia has the family migrating from Worms to Posen "toward the end of the fifteenth century, probably in consequence of persecution." Devotional biography turns that ancestral migration into a personal birth in Worms on the night of a blood libel. Loew's writings never claim Worms. Both are in the file, the second as legend. **Stop order corrected at the gate:** the Worms stop (placeholder 1490) now precedes the Poznań birth stop (1520) so that dates within the segment do not run backwards.

**[A, disputed]** Father Bezalel ben Ḥayyim; brothers Ḥayyim (scholar and author), Sinai, Samson. **Birth order among the brothers is disputed** between the Jewish Encyclopedia and the Jewish Virtual Library, and is not adjudicated.

**Contradiction flagged: the wife. [R]** The Jewish Encyclopedia puts her in the Altschuler family of Prague; popular tradition names her Perl, a poor baker's daughter. Both cannot hold, and the Jewish Encyclopedia is itself a nineteenth-century compilation. Perl is named only in the burial stop, as tradition.

## 2. Moravia, 1553-1573

**[A]** From 1553 Landesrabbiner of Moravia at Mikulov (Nikolsburg), heading the rabbinical court and academy for about twenty years, taking part in regulating communal ordinances, taxation and restraints on luxury across the Moravian communities (Jewish Virtual Library; Jewish Encyclopedia; both following David Gans, *Tsemah David*, 1592).

**[R]** The "311 Ordinances" (Shay takkanot) gather Moravian legislation imperfectly and are routinely credited to him wholesale. Sládek's caution is followed: the compilation is later and only part of it is his.

**[A]** A surviving Moravian text reads as a threat of excommunication against scholars seeking ordination outside his control (Sládek, "Admiration and Fear," *Judaica Bohemiae* 52.2, 2017).

**Gap, stated as a gap:** why he leaves Moravia in 1573 is documented nowhere reachable. Sládek reads the ordination document as evidence his authority made enemies, which is a reading and not a cause.

## 3. First Prague, 1573-1584

**[A]** He founds a *beit va'ad la-ḥakhamim*, a scholars' meeting house remembered as the Klaus, and organizes systematic Mishnah study (Gans, via Putík/Polakovič; Jewish Virtual Library, dating "Die Klaus" to 1573-1584). **[R]** He revises the statutes of the burial society, founded 1564; tagged R because the revision is reported without a document behind it.

**[A] 1578, Gur Aryeh**, the supercommentary on Rashi's Torah commentary, printed in Prague. The one unambiguous non-legendary link to a traveler already in the atlas: Loew wrote a book-length commentary *on* Rashi.

**[R] c. 1579, the rabbinate he does not get.** Putík and Polakovič reconstruct a contested succession after Isaac Melnik's death, Loew passed over for his brother-in-law Isaac Ḥayyot in what older accounts call a "rabbinical overthrow." **Contradiction flagged:** the Jewish Virtual Library narrates no 1579 defeat at all, recording only a *second* position taken in 1588 after Ḥayyot's resignation. The file keeps the 1579 placement and states the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

**[A] 1582**, *Gevurot ha-Shem* printed in Kraków, announcing a projected six-part series he never finished.

**[A] 1583-1584, the Nadler affair.** A Sabbath of Repentance sermon against a lineage slander, printed 1584 as *Derush na'eh le-Shabbat Teshuvah*, and a communal ban with ten other Prague scholars (Jewish Virtual Library; Reiner, "Lineage and Libel"). Reiner traces support outward through Poznań to Gniezno, Mordecai Jaffe among the adherents: **[R]**, a reconstruction rather than a dated act.

**Gap:** 1584 is when he stops being in Prague, and nothing says why. The Jewish Virtual Library's formula, "rabbi in Moravia or Posen," admits the sources do not know.

## 4. The interval and the return, 1584-1592

**[A, at one remove] 1587, Prostějov.** Archival evidence placing him there is cited in Putík and Polakovič (2009). **That paper could not be fetched: academia.edu returned 403 Forbidden.** The date rests on the dossier's citation plus the corroborating interval in the Jewish Virtual Library.

**[A] 1588**, a letter of greeting to Loew in Moravia survives in the Wrocław University Library, read through the dossier's archives list and not from the manuscript.

**[R] 1588, Bongars and Le Normant.** Jacques Bongars, French Protestant diplomat and humanist, and Guillaume Le Normant de Trougny enter Loew's academy; Judah Seligmann, a scholar of the house, assists their study of the Hebrew Bible; a friendship-album entry records it (Weinberg, *Tablet*). Kept with its friction, which the campa states: it sits badly against Loew's own written restriction of gentile access to Torah beyond the Noahide commandments.

**[A] 1588**, again active in Prague after Ḥayyot's departure. **[A] 1589**, *Derekh ḥayyim* printed in Kraków and a Sabbath ha-Gadol sermon in Prague. The sermon's reference to a revered "R. Sh." is tentatively identified by Sládek with Shalom Shakhna of Lublin: **[R]**, with chronological objections Sládek himself flags.

**[A, day disputed] February 1592, the audience with Rudolf II at Prague Castle**, with his brother Sinai and son-in-law Isaac Katz, a courtier remembered as Prince Bertier present. **The Jewish Virtual Library gives 16 February, the Jewish Encyclopedia 23 February**, both rendering 3 Adar 5352. The file uses 16 February and states the conflict. The content is recorded by nobody.

**Quotation not verified.** Gans's phrase for the audience's subject, "sealed, hidden, and concealed," is quoted everywhere in secondary literature and appears in the dossier, but could not be located in *Tsemah David* or any translation during this pass: every route tried carried no direct quote or returned 403/404. It is **paraphrased in the campa and kept out of the `quote` field.**

## 5. Poznań, 1592-1596

**[A]** He leaves Prague on 4 Iyyar 5352 (16 April 1592 by the dossier's conversion) to head the yeshiva and rabbinical courts of Poznań, in what scholars read as a swap of positions with Mordecai Jaffe. **Contradiction flagged:** secondary literature styles him "chief rabbi of all Poland," which the dossier notes is likely an inflation of Gans's wording; the territorial scope of the office is disputed.

**[A] 1593**, two sermons on Torah, the commandments and worship, delivered in Poznań and printed in Prague by Isaac Katz. **Surviving copies vary in the title**, a small fact with large consequences for the works list. **[A] 1594**, *Pesak 'al 'agunah*, a ruling for a wife whose husband has vanished.

## 6. Last Prague, 1596/97-1609

**Correction applied.** The pool had "chief rabbi de jure, probably after 1597," later than the evidence supports: the English Wikipedia article on Mordecai Jaffe records the two men exchanging positions in **1596**, Jaffe moving to Posen, so the 1597 Vienna document confirms an office already held. The file gives **1596/97**.

**[A] 1597**, a document in the Austrian State Archives, Vienna, styles him the highest-ranking rabbi of Prague's Jews and carries his signature (Putík and Polakovič). One of very few first-hand traces of his life. **The stop is in Prague, where he signed, not in Vienna, where the paper sits**: he is not recorded as ever having gone to Vienna, and the archive is cited in the stop's sources instead.

**Late works, all [A] from the Jewish Encyclopedia's works list, corroborated by the Jewish Virtual Library's 1598-1600 cluster:**

- *Netivot olam*. **Correction applied:** printed across **1595-1596**, not 1596 alone (English Wikipedia works list). The Jewish Encyclopedia's "Netibot Shalom" is a separate title in its own list, not an older transliteration of this one; the dossier's parenthetical equating them is dropped.
- *Be'er ha-golah*, 1598, defending rabbinic aggadah against Azariah de' Rossi's criticism, with a funeral sermon for Akiva Günzburg the same year. *Netzaḥ Yisra'el*, 1599. *Or ḥadash* and *Ner mitzvah*, 1600, bound together, his last lifetime imprints. *Pesakim bi-devar ḥalitzah*, ca. 1608.
- *Tif'eret Yisra'el*, Venice, 1599. **The book travels and the man does not.** No evidence he crossed the Alps, and the stop says so; the same caution applies to the Kraków printings.
- *Ḥiddushei aggadot*, never printed by him; manuscripts with his revisions survive in the Bodleian's Oppenheim collection. Sládek reads the deteriorating hand cautiously as failing eyesight and **refuses to call it a diagnosis**, which the file keeps. **The stop is in Prague, where he wrote**, with the Bodleian in `suggested_refs`.

**[A] 1599**, his only known son, Bezalel ben Judah Loew, rabbi in Kolín, dies; the surviving grape-vine tombstone dates it (Putík/Polakovič; Czech Wikipedia, also the corrected coordinates). Secondary sources sometimes round the year to 1600.

**Dropped for thinness, not for doubt:** a manuscript enactment enforcing silence during portions of worship, c. 1600-1609, attested via Putík/Polakovič. Recorded here so it is not lost.

## 7. Death: a three-way dispute that is mostly a misread

**Correction applied.** 18 Elul 5369 converts to **17 September 1609 Gregorian**, which is **7 September Julian**; English Wikipedia states exactly this, so the two September dates are one day in two calendars. The Jewish Museum in Prague page does not give 7 September as a 1609 death date: it says the 400th anniversary fell on "the 7th of September 2009 (18th of Elul 5769)." That leaves one real outlier, the Jewish Encyclopedia's "Aug. 22, 1609," which appears simply wrong. The file dates the death **1609-09-17**.

**[A]** Buried in the Old Jewish Cemetery, Prague, under a baroque *tumba*, a house-shaped monument rather than a slab, carved with grapes and a **two-tailed lion**: a pun on Loew/Leib and the two-tailed lion of the Bohemian arms. Site no. 8 on the cemetery plan. **[R]** That the monument is shared with his wife is in the dossier; the article fetched for this pass describes the iconography but no wife's cartouche, so it is carried as tradition, as is her death within months.

## 8. The afterlife, and where it actually starts

**[R] The load-bearing absence.** No contemporary source, not Gans, not Loew's writings, not his epitaph, not Prague Hebrew works of the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries, connects Loew to the creation of a golem.

- **[R] 1745, Meir Perels, *Megillat yuḥasin*, Żółkiew.** The family genealogy written more than a century after the death, which introduced systematic distortions into everything after it (Putík and Polakovič).
- **Correction applied: Weisel is not the founding text.** The pool made Leopold Weisel's "Der Golem" (1847) the first attribution. English Wikipedia lists **Auerbach (1837), Philippson (1841), Klutschak (1841), Tendlau (1842)**, then Weisel, with earlier instances in 1834 and 1836. Weisel is reframed as the most influential early version, a decade after the earliest.
- **[R] 1909, Yudl Rosenberg, *Nifla'ot Maharal mi-Prag 'im ha-Golem***, presented as a manuscript by Isaac Katz from a "Royal Library of Metz." A hoax by consensus (Scholem: it "contains not ancient legends, but modern fiction"); the villain cardinal never existed; the 1573 audience it alleges is impossible, Maximilian II having reigned 1564-1576; Rosenberg also lifted from Conan Doyle's "The Jew's Breastplate" (Leiman). **Contradiction flagged: the imprint**, usually **Piotrków 1909** but **Podgórze, Kraków** in English Wikipedia. Not secure, so **no stop is pinned at either press**; the episode sits at the Old-New Synagogue precinct, where the stories are set. **[A]** Wegener's *Der Golem* films (1915, largely lost; 1917; 1920) made it a cinematic icon.
- **[A] 1883 and August 2014**, the Old-New Synagogue attic was checked for a golem and nothing found; the lowest three metres of the external stairway have been removed and the attic is closed. The wartime legend of a Gestapo agent dying there is contradicted by the same source, which records that the Gestapo never accessed it: **[R]** legend, **[A]** searches.
- **[A] 1917, Šaloun's statue. Correction applied:** the pool's geography lens put it on the **Novoměstská radnice** on Charles Square, the wrong building, a kilometre away. It stands on the **Nová radnice**, Mariánské náměstí 2, Old Town, completed 1911 with Osvald Polívka. The pool's afterlife lens had this right, so its two lenses contradicted each other.
- **[A] 2009**, the exhibition and catalogue *Path of Life*, Prague Castle, 315 pp. **Corrections applied:** the museum's page credits **Alexandr Putík** and does not name Olga Sixtová as co-editor; and the philately was conflated into one anniversary programme, when there are three items, a Czech and Israeli joint stamp of **April 1997**, twelve years early and no part of it, a Czech Post stamp of **May 2009**, and a Czech Mint coin of **June 2009**.
- **[A] Kerem Maharal**, moshav founded 1949 by Czechoslovak survivors and named for him. **Coordinate correction:** 32.6492, 34.9919 near Atlit, 3.3 km off the pool's figure. In the 2009 campa; **no stop placed**, being a village named after him. **[R]** No authenticated life portrait exists: the familiar visual Maharal was assembled from postcards, paintings, Mikoláš Aleš's imagery and the statue, and the claim was not verified beyond the dossier's framing.

## 10. Interlocks: named, and refused

**Named in a campa**, each a real relation of Loew's own. **`rashi`**: *Gur Aryeh* (1578) is a book-length supercommentary on Rashi's Torah commentary; Rashi died in 1105 and the relation can only run one way, which the standing rule allows. **`moses_de_leon`**: named once, in the *Netzaḥ Yisra'el* stop, hedged in both campa and `date_confidence`. The relation is textual: the dossier reports Loew's writing "often parallel[s] kabbalistic patterns" while he "rarely cites the Zohar explicitly," so the campa says his symbolism runs close to the Zohar's patterns and that he seldom names the book. **[R]**, and the weaker of the two.

**Refused, with the reason.** Every one is shared geography, which the map already shows a viewer: `john_dee` and `edward_kelley` (same emperor, same castle, no recorded meeting), `giordano_bruno` (Prague 1588, same court culture, no contact), `san_vito` (the castle hill), `samuel_oppenheimer` and `joseph_oppenheimer` (the same chief rabbinate ninety years later, the same quarter in 1715), `martin_buber` (Prague 1909-1911). `johannes_kepler` is the tempting one: Imperial Mathematician in Prague 1601-1612, and Loew's pupil **David Gans** is the documented bridge to Brahe and Kepler. That is Gans's relation, not Loew's, and the rule does not stretch to a pupil's contacts. Gans is named throughout the file as himself; Kepler is not named at all.

**Gap flagged for the queue: David Gans**, Loew's pupil, author of *Tsemah David*, the source for the Klaus, the Moravian appointment, the audience and the Poznań departure, and the link between the Prague Jewish Town and the astronomy of Brahe and Kepler. No `journey.json` in this checkout.

## 11. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Judah Loew is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** No apparatus stops are licensed and none were written. Per the adversary's duty in that file, relations not rooted in his own life are reported here rather than silently deleted.

**Direction caught: backward, licensable if the author wants it.** **Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai (`rabbi_shimon_bar_yochai_rashbi`)**: the interlock lens raises him as the Zohar's purported author and the root of the kabbalistic material Loew's writing echoes, and Loew spent his last years on *Ḥiddushei aggadot*, in which Rashbi recurs. That would be an ordinary one-directional relation needing no exception, **but the pool attests no specific passage of Loew on Rashbi**, so asserting one would be inventing evidence, and he is in no campa. A citation from *Ḥiddushei aggadot* would make this a normal interlock; the Meron material stays on Rashbi's own file either way. Also **Sefer Yetzirah and the Temple Mount pin**: Loew is connected to that book only through the nineteenth-century golem legend, so pinning a Jerusalem stop on a legend's internal logic was refused.

**Direction caught: forward, none written, plus one boundary.** The last segment carries six stops dated after his death (1745, 1847, 1883, 1909, 1917, 2009). These are not forward references in the `EXCEPTIONS.md` sense: no framework of Loew's is applied to later events and no later traveler is staged as a scene. Each is an object or legend attached to Loew himself, at one of his own pins. Perels, Weisel, Rosenberg, Šaloun and Putík are named only as makers of those objects. If the operator holds that a file may carry nothing after the death date, the eighth segment is the thing to cut, as a unit.
