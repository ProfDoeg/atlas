# Honoré de Balzac (1799-1850): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-23 from the verified research pool, for `honore_de_balzac.journey.json`.*

**[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source institutional claim · **[C]** a correction the verify pass made to the gathering pass, carried into the file's `date_confidence` fields.

The file has **9 segments, 45 stops**, calendar `gregorian`, register `national mythology: the canon is true`. Every correction below is applied in the file and stated in the stop's own confidence field, never in campa prose.

## 1. Touraine, 1799-1814

- Born Tours, 20 May 1799, eleven in the morning, second child of Bernard-François Balzac and Anne-Charlotte-Laure Sallambier, married in Paris 30 January 1797 **[A: Encyclopedia.com; fr.wikipedia]**. Birth name **Honoré Balzac**; the particle is self-applied later **[A]**. **[C]** The father was **50** at the marriage, not about 51; the bride, born 1778, was 18 or 19, so the pool's "18" stands.
- Father born **Bernard-François Balssa**, 22 July 1746, Montirat (Tarn), peasant family, later deputy mayor of Tours 1803-1808 **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Bernard-François Balssa']**. **[C]** He remade the name in Paris **between 1773 and 1783**, a bracket, not "by the 1770s." No link to the aristocratic **Balzac d'Entragues** line: consistent with the sources, never positively verified **[R]**, which matters: the 1834 cane is cut with those arms.
- Elder brother **Louis-Daniel** (b. 1798) died at about 33 days; Honoré went to a **wet nurse at Saint-Cyr-sur-Loire** until about four **[A: Maison de Balzac chronology, per dossier]**.
- **Collège de Vendôme**, June 1807 to April 1813 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Contradiction flagged, not resolved:* sources differ on entry at **eight or ten**, and both readings sit in `date_confidence`. He left in what biographers call collapse, blamed on excessive reading; **the cause is not medically established [R]**.
- Family to Paris **November 1814** **[A]**: Pension Lepître, rue de Turenne, then the **Lycée Charlemagne**. **[C]** Charlemagne is **1814-1816**, not 1815-1816, and **Jules Michelet** followed the same courses **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Michelet has no atlas file and is named in campa as an ordinary historical person.

## 2. Law, garret, pseudonyms, 1816-1825

- Clerk to the avoué **Guillonnet-Merville** (Nov 1816 to Mar 1818), then the notary **Passez** (1818-1819) **[R: "Balzac juriste" scholarship per dossier; corroboration in Robb]**, the weakest-sourced material of the early life. One stop: the addresses lie about 100 m apart and neither is documented.
- **[C]** 4 January 1819 was a **first law examination only**, the degree never completed, not "his baccalauréat en droit" as the pool had it **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- August 1819: the trial allowance, the garret at **9 rue Lesdiguières**, family to **Villeparisis** **[A: Wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**. **Cromwell**, 1820, read aloud and rejected **[A]**; the friend-of-the-house verdict against literature is tradition **[R]**. Pseudonymous novels as **Lord R'Hoone** and **Horace de Saint-Aubin**, 1821-1827 **[A]**.
- **Laure de Berny** from 1822, some 22 years his senior: lover, lender, first reader **[A: Maison de Balzac, cross-referenced against Wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no address recoverable and no month given, so the stop defaults to May 1822 on the canonical Paris pin 48.8566 / 2.3522, declared nominal.

## 3. The presses, 1825-1830

- Cheap Molière and La Fontaine editions, 1825, failed; roughly **50,000 francs** borrowed by April 1828 **[A: Encyclopedia.com]**. Printer's brevet **June 1826**, type foundry as **Laurent, Balzac et Barbier**, both gone by 1828 **[A: BnF/Gallica and Maison de Balzac]**.
- **[C] Coordinate, about 420 m.** Number 17 rue des Marais-Saint-Germain (renamed **rue Visconti** 1864) is **48.8558 / 2.3356**, not the pool's 48.852 / 2.335; the same source confirms the dates to the day, "du 4 juin 1826 à 1828" **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Rue Visconti']**.
- **Le Dernier Chouan ou la Bretagne en 1800** (1829), first book under his own name, and **Physiologie du mariage**, December 1829 **[A]**, both at the rue Cassini stop, tenancy from 1828 **[A]**.
- **Fougères**, September 1828, with General de Pommereul **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Le Croisic and Guérande**, 1830, with Berny **[R: dossier only]**: single-source, no dated movement (folded into the Fougères campa).
- **Château de Saché.** **[C] Coordinate, about 6.7 km:** Saché is **47.2478 / 0.5442**, not the pool's 47.2306 / 0.6294, which fell in open country near Pont-de-Ruan **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Contradiction flagged:* the pool says "roughly a dozen stays, 1825-1848," fr.wikipedia gives yearly stays **1829-1838**. Not adjudicated; the file records both.
- **Bernard-François died 19 June 1829** **[A]**, in the former 7th arrondissement **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **[C]** The pool pinned a bare Paris centroid; the file keeps it and declares it nominal.

## 4. Fame, the letter, the provinces, 1831-1834

- **La Peau de chagrin**, 1831 **[A]**. **[C] Politics overstated in the pool**, which had him "attempting electoral political candidacies in the early 1830s." en.wikipedia has the opposite for 1832: after a fall in which he cracked his head he **decided against standing at Chinon**. The 1831 candidacies usually cited (Cambrai, Fougères, Angoulême) are the ones that happened, and **none is sourced in the dossier [R]**.
- **28 February 1832:** the letter signed **L'Étrangère** from Odessa; the writer is **Ewelina Hańska**, married to **Wacław Hański** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Pinned at rue Cassini where he was living; **the delivery address is not attested** and the confidence field says so.
- **Claire de Castries**, Aix-les-Bains and on toward Geneva, 1832 **[A]**. **[C]** Savoy *was* Sardinian territory until 1860, so the pool's "to Aix-les-Bains, then into the Kingdom of Sardinia" is wrong: he was already there.
- **Zulma Carraud at Angoulême.** **[C]** The window is 1831 to **February 1834**, when the Carrauds left, not the pool's open "1830s" **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Issoudun / Château de Frapesle** was **cut for space**; its correction is recorded so it is not lost. The Carrauds were at Frapesle outside Issoudun 1834-1848 and kept a room for him; stays **April 1834, August 1835, February-March 1838**, coordinate about **46.953 / 2.011** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Eugénie Grandet**, 1833 **[A]**. **[C] Marie du Fresnay: tag upgraded R to A, the paternity itself left probable.** The gathering pass filed the episode as tradition and wrote "not a documented legal paternity." There **is** a documentary act, the **1846 will** leaving Marie-Caroline a **Girardon statue**, alongside the letter to his sister announcing he would become a father, the dedication "à Maria," the 1946 family testimony that Maria was the household name for Marie du Fresnay, and the Pierrot and Chancerel reconstruction **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Marie du Fresnay', 'Marie-Caroline du Fresnay']**. Marie-Caroline born **4 June 1834**, registered to the du Fresnay household.
- **Neuchâtel, 25 September 1833**, first meeting **[A]**. **[C]** The pool's Geneva entry had absorbed that meeting; Geneva's role is the **second**, **December 1833 to February 1834** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Split, as the correction recommended.

## 5. The Comédie humaine takes shape, 1834-1837

- **Le Père Goriot** serialised 1834, book form 1835, introducing the recurring-character system **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Wikipedia]**.
- **Vienna, audience with Metternich**, 1835 **[R: Maison de Balzac, single source]**. Kept and tagged; the campa says plainly that nothing of what was said survives.
- **La Chronique de Paris**, bought 1835, collapsed 1836 **[A]**. **Balzac v. Buloz**, June 1836, over proofs of *Le Lys dans la vallée* sent abroad **[R: no judgment text reached]**. **La Vieille Fille** in *La Presse*, 1836 **[R]**, was cut for space.
- **Hôtel des Haricots**, about a week's detention for Guard non-service, 1836 **[A]**. **[C] Site error:** in 1836 the prison stood at the **Hôtel de Bessancourt, quai Saint-Bernard** (its site 1800-1837), about **48.8465 / 2.3595**, moving to the quai d'Austerlitz only in 1837; the pool's 48.8496 / 2.352 matches none of its three sites **[A: fr.wikipedia, which names Balzac among the inmates]**.
- **Laure de Berny died 27 July 1836 at Grez-sur-Loing, aged 59** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **[C]** Two pool errors: the death was placed on the Paris centroid, about **65 km** off, and the relationship called "nearly two decades" when it ran **fourteen years**.
- **Italy.** **[C]** He reached **Milan on 19 February 1837** and stayed over a year **[A: it.wikipedia]**, which corroborates **no other Italian city**. The pool's **Turin, Venice, Genoa and Florence** stops are thinner than their tags implied; folded into the Milan campa, which says so.

## 6. Jardies, Sardinia, the writers' cause, 1837-1840

- **Les Jardies, Sèvres.** **[C]** Settled **1837**, forced out **1840**, not 1841, consistent with the Passy move; coordinate moved about 300 m to **48.8272 / 2.1986** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The pineapple scheme is tradition **[R]**.
- **Sardinia**, 1838, silver from old slag, financed by pawned jewelry and family loans, via Marseille, Toulon, Corsica **[A: correspondence-based reconstruction]**; the mine-site pin is approximate **[R]**.
- **Nohant with George Sand**, about a week, 1838 **[A: Maison de Balzac]**. **[C]** Nohant-Vic is **46.6394 / 1.9606**, about **24 km** from the pool's pin, and that article, while confirming Balzac among the estate's guests, **does not date the visit to 1838** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Wrong pin plus single-sourced year, so the stop was cut to one sentence.
- **Société des gens de lettres, president.** **[C]** The presidency ran **1839-1840**, not to 1841 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **[C]** The pool's 48.8656 / 2.3376 matches no sourceable SGDL address, and the present seat, the **Hôtel de Massa**, has stood on the rue du Faubourg-Saint-Jacques only since 1930. The 1839 address is **an honest gap**; the stop uses the canonical Paris pin, declared nominal. The **Peytel letter** in *Le Siècle*, September 1839, ending in Peytel's execution **[A]**, is folded in here.
- **Académie française.** **[C] Two errors.** (1) The pool's label, "Théâtre-Français / candidacy withdrawal, Académie française" at 48.8532 / 2.3417, conflated two buildings and matched neither; the file names only the **Institut de France, quai de Conti**, about **48.8568 / 2.3372**. (2) **Joseph-François Michaud** died **30 September 1839** holding **fauteuil 29**, not 26; the seat went in **1840** to **Pierre Flourens**, in the election **Victor Hugo stood in and lost** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. So the withdrawal straddles 1839-1840, and Hugo did not win the chair Balzac stepped aside from.
- **Vautrin** at the **Porte-Saint-Martin, 14 March 1840**, banned after one performance **[A]**. The Lemaître wig read as a caricature of Louis-Philippe is theatre-history tradition **[R]**.
- **Traité des excitants modernes**, 1839. **[C] Quote upgraded from [R]:** the passage is traceable to **fr.wikisource, §III "Du café"** (Œuvres complètes, Michel Lévy, t. 20, 1870). The file **quotes the French**: the circulating English is a translator's wording (Balzac has lower-case *la grande armée*), and the standard version (Onopa, 1996) was unreachable, HTTP 403.
- **Fifty cups a day** remains **[R]**: no stable count in any source, the causal link to his death unproven in the sources that repeat it. The campa states it as legend, the legend being part of the record.

## 7. Passy, Russia, the Furne, 1840-1846

- **47 rue Raynouard, Passy**, 1840-1847, rented under the housekeeper's name, with the lower rue Berton exit **[A: Maison de Balzac; Wikipedia]**. *Minor contradiction flagged:* the pool's lenses give **48.8551** and **48.8558** for the same building, about 80 m apart; the file uses 48.8558 at both Passy stops. The **1841 contract** with **Hetzel, Paulin, Dubochet and Sanches**, Furne becoming central **[A]**, is folded in here.
- **[C] The 1841/1843 conflation.** The pool tagged the **1841** death of Wacław Hański with the place **Saint Petersburg**. Hański died in 1841 **[A]**; Balzac did not reach Saint Petersburg until **1843** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, which the pool's own geography lens had right. **Berlin and Dresden** on the return **[R: dossier only]**, folded in, not pinned.
- **The Furne corrigé**, his own copy of the definitive edition corrected in ink throughout, basis of modern critical editions: **soft [A]**, since WebFetch could not extract the explanatory text from the Maison de Balzac digital-edition pages, though the project's existence was confirmed. The **Légion d'honneur, 24 April 1845** **[A: BnF]**, is folded in here.
- **Rome with the "Saltimbanques,"** 1846 **[R: dossier only, year without month]**.
- **Rue Fortunée bought 28 September 1846**, an old dependency of the **folie Beaujon**, never fully paid for; street renamed **rue Balzac** in 1850 **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Rue Balzac']**. **[C] Coordinate, about 420 m:** rue Balzac is **48.8733 / 2.3003**; the geography lens gave 2.306 and the afterlife lens 48.8746 / 2.301. The lenses disagreed and geography's was the worse; corrected at all three rue Fortunée stops. The hoped-for child named **Victor-Honoré**, 1846, produced no surviving child **[A]**.

## 8. Ukraine, the marriage, the death, 1847-1850

- **Wierzchownia (Verkhivnia)**, Volhynia: first stay **September 1847 to January 1848**, second **September 1848 to spring 1850**, prolonged by illness; there he finished the second part of **L'Envers de l'histoire contemporaine**, his last completed work **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **[C] Coordinate, about 32 km:** Verkhivnia is **49.8044 / 29.3128**, not 49.9333 / 28.9333.
- **February 1848**: back just before the revolution, into a publishing and theatrical collapse **[A]**. **Saché, 1848**, heart disease and failing breath **[A: Musée Balzac]**, was folded into the second Verkhivnia campa.
- **Married Ewelina Hańska at Saint Barbara's, Berdychiv, 14 March 1850**, her daughter Anna and son-in-law Georges Mniszech attending **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Back in Paris 20 May 1850**, his fifty-first birthday **[A]**; the locksmith and the deranged servant are **[R] in detail, [A] in outline**.
- **Died 18 August 1850**, gangrene with congestive heart failure, aged 51 **[A]**. **[C] The dispute is smaller than the pool made it**, which framed 18 against 19 August as a standoff to be "kept in tension." **Three** sources give 18 August flat: fr.wikipedia 'Honoré de Balzac', en.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia 'Rue Balzac'. The 19 August reading is the Maison de Balzac chronology's, reflecting a death late at night.
- **Funeral and Hugo's oration, 20 August 1850**, Père-Lachaise **Division 48**; the tomb later received Hańska **[A: fr.wikipedia infobox; fr.wikisource, 'Funérailles de Balzac', headed "20 août 1850"]**. **[C] Date error:** the pool's afterlife lens gave **21 August**, contradicting its own chronology lens, which was right.

## 9. Afterward

- **1890**: the rue Fortunée house **demolished** by Baroness Adèle de Rothschild, a plaque on the Hôtel Salomon de Rothschild garden wall marking it **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **1836**: **Croker** in the *Quarterly Review* ruled only a handful of titles fit for English readers; **1895-1898**, **Ellen Marriage's** 40-volume English *Comédie humaine*, ed. **Saintsbury**, five volumes omitted as too shocking, with a rival complete version from Philadelphia **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **1949**: Paris acquires 47 rue Raynouard, the only surviving Paris residence, now the **Maison de Balzac** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. It holds **the cane** from the goldsmith **Le Cointe**, 1834, 700 francs on credit and still being paid in April 1835, turquoise pommel cut with the arms of the unrelated **Balzac d'Entraigues** family, inv. **BAL 186** **[A: Paris Musées]**. **Delphine de Girardin** published **La Canne de M. de Balzac** in **1836**, making it an invisibility talisman: a legend that grew up *during his lifetime* **[A]**.
- **Octave Mirbeau, La Mort de Balzac (1907)** **[A as a publication: fr.wikisource]**, from **Jean Gigoux's** stories: it disputes Hugo's version, claims the family stayed away, and has the dying man call for the fictional **Dr Bianchon**. Its value as eyewitness history is **[R]**, as is the Bianchon legend; it is a `suggested_ref` on the death stop.
- **Not in the file:** the **2021 Giannoli film** *Illusions perdues*, another maker's work about Balzac. **[C]** The pool conflated its premiere (**Venice, 5 September 2021**) with its French release (**20 October 2021**) **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Balzac is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Four relations in the pool are not rooted in his own life, and none was silently deleted. All four run **forward** from this file's clock, a later artifact embedded in an earlier subject's block, which the exceptions file does not license by default. Each is kept as a `source` or `suggested_ref` on a stop that is genuinely Balzac's own, none staged as a scene:

1. **Auguste Rodin (`auguste_rodin`), forward.** The Société des gens de lettres, which Balzac presided over in 1839-1840, commissioned Rodin in **1891**, rejected the plaster at the 1898 Salon, and the bronze went up at Boulevard Raspail / Montparnasse on **2 July 1939** **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Monument to Balzac']**. That is Rodin's stop and his file already carries it; held here only as a `suggested_ref` on the SGDL stop. **[C]** The pool's lenses give three different latitudes for the one monument (48.8434, 48.8425, Rodin's file 48.8422) and wrongly tagged it [R]; it is [A].
2. **Roland Barthes (`roland_barthes`), forward.** *S/Z* (1970), a book-length reading of *Sarrasine* **[A: his file]**. One-directional and real, but *Sarrasine* has no stop of its own here and the artifact postdates the block.
3. **W. B. Yeats (`w_b_yeats`), forward.** Yeats's father read Balzac aloud to him at Bedford Park about 1879 **[A: his file]**. That is Yeats's scene, in London, on Yeats's file.
4. **Hetzel via Jules Verne (`jules_verne`), forward.** Hetzel, in the 1841 consortium, published Verne from January 1863 and is called "publisher of Hugo and Balzac" in Verne's file **[A]**. Hetzel *is* named in the Passy campa as Balzac's own publisher, a lifetime fact; Verne is not.

**Rejected as non-relations** (shared geography only): Dickens's Paris winter of 1846-47; Marx's rue Vanneau arrival in 1843, the Lafargue-recalled admiration being uncorroborated in any atlas file; the Rothschild Vienna pin against the Metternich audience; the Italy pin-cluster overlap with Pavlova, Diaghilev, Roerich.

**Named in campa (interlocks): `victor_hugo` only**, at the Académie candidacy withdrawn in Hugo's favour and the oration at Père-Lachaise. Both contemporary, direct, documented from both sides. **[C]** `victor_hugo.journey.json` carries **no entry for the Balzac funeral oration**, though it is well attested: a gap on Hugo's file, left for the operator.

**One-directional relation used as a source, not a scene:** Engels to Margaret Harkness, April 1888, calling Balzac "a far greater master of realism than all the Zolas passés, présents et à venir" **[A: Marxists Internet Archive]**, in `suggested_refs` on the London translation stop.

## Honest gaps

- **No address for Berny (1822), the SGDL's 1839 seat, or the father's death in 1829.** Three stops sit on nominal Paris pins and say so.
- **The clerkships, Balzac v. Buloz, and the Metternich audience** all rest on summarised single sources: no engagement document, no judgment text, no record of the conversation. Thin, for claims carrying the legal apprenticeship and the authors'-rights theme.
- **Turin, Venice, Genoa, Florence**: only Milan corroborated for 1837. Four pool stops reduced to one clause.
- **The English translation of the coffee passage** is behind HTTP 403; the file quotes the French rather than an unattributed rendering.
- **Ewelina Hańska's own death and burial date** in the Père-Lachaise tomb is not in the pool; the 1890 stop says only "decades."
- **The funeral date** was guessed by the gathering pass as "two days after death." Wikisource settles it at 20 August. The guess was right, but it was a guess.
