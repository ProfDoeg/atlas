# Louis Vuitton (1821-1892): research report
*2026-08-08. Subject: Louis Vuitton, layetier-emballeur-malletier; born at the hamlet of Anchay, commune of Lavans-sur-Valouse (Jura), died at Asnières-sur-Seine.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested in a source that could be fetched and is named · **[R]** = reconstruction, house tradition, single-source chronology, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated, except where an external fact makes one branch impossible; there the impossibility is stated and both readings kept.

A structural warning first. This subject has no scholarly biography in the reachable material. Everything below rests on three tertiary layers: en.wikipedia and fr.wikipedia *Louis Vuitton*, and company heritage pages whose claims circulate through every secondary account without attribution. Encyclopedia.com's *Vuitton, Louis*, cited heavily by the first pass, now 301-redirects to a 404. No primary document was consulted. The house is its own principal historian, and the atlas should read it that way.

---

## 1. The mill at Anchay, 1821-1835

**Birth. [A]** 4 August 1821 at Anchay, a hamlet of Lavans-sur-Valouse in the Jura, into a family of millers, carpenters, and farmers. fr.wikipedia specifies the water mill of Charbouilla; en.wikipedia gives the commune; Encyclopedia.com said simply Anchay. The first pass flagged this as a contradiction. **It is not one, and it closes here:** fr.wikipedia *Lavans-sur-Valouse* states that Vuitton "est né au hameau d'Anchay," and Wikidata Q277616 gives place of birth as Lavans-sur-Valouse. The difference is granularity.

**Coordinates. [A]** fr.wikipedia gives the commune at 46° 20' 05" N, 5° 33' 20" E, that is 46.3347 / 5.5556. It was merged into Saint-Hymetière-sur-Valouse by decree of 11 December 2018, effective 1 January 2019.

**Population figure withdrawn. [corrected]** The first pass gave "some 148 inhabitants" at the date of birth. That is the 1999 census (146 in 2016); the nineteenth-century peak was 426 in 1851. No 1821 figure was recovered, so the number is dropped.

**The mother's death. [R]** The mother, a hat-maker, dies when Louis is about ten; the father remarries, and the accounts describe conflict with a stepmother. en.wikipedia only. No name, no date; the Jura civil registers were not opened.

## 2. The walk, 1835-1837

**Departure. [A on year, R on age]** 1835. en.wikipedia: spring 1835, aged 13. Encyclopedia.com: 14. **Flagged, not resolved.**

**The distance: the first pass misstated the disagreement. [corrected]** The pool's "275-292 miles ... or 400 km" conflates incompatible figures. Two numbers are live: fr.wikipedia has "les 400 km" on foot, en.wikipedia has "approximately 292 miles (470 km)." Those are seventy kilometres apart. The 275-mile figure appears in **neither** source and is struck. **Flagged, not resolved.**

**The route. [R, a real gap]** Two years on the road, taking odd jobs. **Not one source consulted names a single town or village on the route.** This is the founding scene of the house's telling of itself and the least documented episode of the life. The journey file's route stop carries a midpoint coordinate labelled a marker, not a stage.

## 3. Apprenticeship and the imperial household, 1837-1853

**Arrival and apprenticeship. [A]** Paris in 1837, apprenticed to a Monsieur Maréchal, an established layetier-emballeur-malletier. en.wikipedia puts his service at seventeen years, which places his departure in 1854, the year of both the marriage and the founding.

**The workshop address: retagged. [R, corrected]** The first pass placed Maréchal at 327 rue Saint-Honoré and tagged it [A] while admitting the "exact primary URL not captured." Neither Wikipedia edition gives any address for Maréchal; the number appears only in brand repetition. **Retagged [R].** The coordinate is an estimate on the correct street, moved east from the first pass's 48.8676 / 2.3220, since numbering rises westward.

**Empress Eugénie. [A on the fact; contradiction on the year, one branch impossible]** Vuitton becomes personal trunk-maker and packer to Empress Eugénie. fr.wikipedia gives 1852; Encyclopedia.com gave 1853; en.wikipedia dates it only as "after the reestablishment of the French Empire under Napoleon III" and gives **no year**. Napoleon III became Emperor 2 December 1852; Eugénie became Empress on marrying him 30 January 1853. **An appointment to "the Empress" cannot predate 1853.** The 1852 reading survives only as the year he may have begun working for her household before she was Empress. Both are carried; 1853 is the only internally coherent one.

**Geographic invention removed. [corrected]** The first pass pinned this to the Palais des Tuileries and tagged it [A]. **No source places Vuitton or the appointment at the Tuileries**; the palace is a defensible marker for the imperial household's seat, but the placement is inferred. Retagged [R]. The journey file inherits the atlas's existing Tuileries pin, 48.8635 / 2.3328, from `empress_josephine.journey.json`, and says so.

## 4. Marriage and founding, 1854-1857

**Marriage. [A on month and year]** April 1854, to Clémence-Émilie Parriaux, at Créteil; Vuitton is 32 or 33. en.wikipedia gives her age as 16; **no second source corroborates it, carried [R].** Her birth year is given loosely as 1836-1838; the church is not identified anywhere consulted.

**The house founded. [A]** 1854, workshop and shop at 4 rue Neuve-des-Capucines (today rue des Capucines), near the Place Vendôme, with a sign advertising the secure packing of fragile objects and of fashions.

**Georges Ferréol Vuitton. [R]** Given as 13 July 1857, Paris, from company materials whose page could not be fetched. **Flagged, not resolved:** one heritage source calls him the only son; a secondary account describes five children.

## 5. The flat lid and the river, 1858-1869

**Trianon canvas and the flat top. [A, but weaker than the first pass claimed]** Between 1854 and 1858 Vuitton introduces the flat-topped rectangular trunk in grey Trianon canvas, replacing the domed lid built to shed rain off a coach roof and making trunks stackable for ship and rail. The first pass presented this as a French claim corroborated by English. **It is the reverse:** fr.wikipedia names "toile Trianon grise" as a material and gives **no date at all**. The 1858 date is en.wikipedia's alone, and the French/English conflict the first pass asserted over an 1854 dating does not exist, because the French article makes no dating claim. **[A] stands, on one source.**

**H.J. Cave and Sons. [R]** en.wikipedia credits the London trunk-maker as an influence on the flat top. No source places Vuitton in London at this date, and en.wikipedia's own *H. J. Cave & Sons* makes no mention of him, so the claim is one article talking about a firm that does not talk back. Cave was founded 1839 and at 74/76/78 Wigmore Street from 1856; the first pass's 51.5142 / -0.1494 sits about 250 m south in Mayfair and is corrected north. Cave's 40 Wigmore Street was billed "the largest travel goods store in the world," the phrase the Vuitton building on the Champs-Élysées would take for itself in 1913.

**Asnières-sur-Seine. [R, retagged]** Vuitton buys land downstream of Paris for river transport, and builds an atelier with the family house adjoining. **Flagged, not resolved:** fr.wikipedia *Louis Vuitton* gives 1859; fr.wikipedia *Asnières-sur-Seine* gives "1860 - Louis Vuitton installe ses ateliers à Asnières"; en.wikipedia gives no year. **The "about twenty workers" figure appears in no reachable source and is dropped.** The coordinate 48.9136 / 2.2846 approximates the present rue Louis-Vuitton site.

**Exposition Universelle of 1867. [A, gap closed]** The first pass declared participation unknown. It is not: en.wikipedia states that "the company participated in the 1867 Universal Exhibition in Paris." **Retagged [A].** Only the medal, if any, remains unknown. The Champ de Mars coordinate 48.8556 / 2.2986 is byte-identical to the 1867 pin in `gustave_eiffel.journey.json`.

**Suez, 1869. [R, retagged down]** Encyclopedia.com reported Eugénie carrying Vuitton luggage to the canal's inauguration. Eugénie did attend: the canal opened 17 November 1869 and she arrived on the 16th aboard *L'Aigle*. But **no Wikipedia article on Eugénie or on the Canal mentions Vuitton luggage.** House tradition. Retagged [A] to [R], and given no stop in the journey file.

## 6. War, forgers, and the second and third canvases, 1870-1888

**The Franco-Prussian War. [R, inference reversed]** The pool speculated that the 1859 move "may have buffered the business from direct war damage." **Asnières lies immediately north-west of Paris, outside the fortifications, inside the zone the Prussian armies occupied and fought over.** The speculation is not merely unattested, it runs against the geography, and is dropped. What became of the workshop is recorded nowhere. Stated as a gap.

**1 rue Scribe. [R, retagged]** The first pass tagged an 1871 boutique here [A] while conceding "exact primary URL not captured." **The address appears in no reachable source**, and fr.wikipedia has no *Hôtel Scribe* article (404). Further, "beside the newly built Palais Garnier" is wrong for 1871: the opera was unfinished, work halted by war and Commune, and opened only 5 January 1875. Retagged [R]; the coordinate 48.8712 / 2.3297 is right for the street.

**Japan, 1871. [A]** en.wikipedia records a customer associated with Ōyama Iwao purchasing Vuitton luggage, an early instance of the house reaching Japan. Whether Ōyama or someone in his circle ordered it is not stated.

**Striped canvas, 1876. [A]** The grey Trianon canvas is replaced by beige-and-brown stripes, explicitly because imitators had proliferated.

**Death of Clémence-Émilie Parriaux, 1882. [R]** Year only, from a secondary genealogical account. **No place, no day, no cause.** Asnières is used as an approximation and labelled as one.

**London, 1885. [A, retagged up]** The first pass tagged this [R] for lack of corroboration. It is doubly sourced: en.wikipedia has the company "opened its first store in London on Oxford Street"; fr.wikipedia has "En 1885, une première boutique ouvre hors de France, avec succès, sur Oxford Street à Londres." **Retagged [A], with the correction that it was the first store outside France.**

**The Damier, 1888. [A]** The checkerboard canvas, printed with *marque L. Vuitton déposée*, against still-rampant counterfeiting. **No primary image or period filing could be fetched**, so the quoted string is carried [R] though the Damier is [A].

**The lock. [R]** en.wikipedia credits the pick-resistant lock to Louis; Encyclopedia.com credited the five-tumbler patent to Georges in 1890. **Not adjudicated.**

**Explorer trunks. [R]** Zinc-, copper-, and brass-clad trunks for tropical humidity, made at Asnières, Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza among the customers; the series runs through the 1890s. **The founder's personal involvement is not established.** Secondary accounts only.

## 7. Death and burial, 1892

**Death. [A]** 27 February 1892, Asnières-sur-Seine, aged 70. Management passes to Georges.

**Burial: gap closed. [A, corrected]** Both first-pass lenses declared the burial site unknown. **The gap does not survive checking.** Wikidata Q277616 gives the Cimetière ancien d'Asnières-sur-Seine; fr.wikipedia *Asnières-sur-Seine* states: "Louis Vuitton (1821-1892) meurt à Asnières. Il est enterré au cimetière de la rue du Ménil." **Retagged [A].** What remains unknown is narrower: whether the grave carries any monument or inscription. No source describes the stone. That is the gap now.

**A coordinate error corrected.** The first pass's afterlife lens placed the Anchay departure at 46.7228 / 5.5536, roughly 44 km north in the Bresse plain near Bletterans, contradicting its own geography lens. All Jura pins use 46.3347 / 5.5556.

## 8. The posthumous house, 1896-2014

**The Monogram, 1896. [A]** Georges creates and registers it: his father's interlocked initials with quatrefoil and flower motifs drawn from the Japonisme then fashionable, patented worldwide, made like the stripe and checkerboard before it against forgers. **All sources agree Louis did not design it.**

**Georges, 1892 to about 1936, and Gaston-Louis Vuitton, 1936-1970. [R]** The grandson is credited with some twenty-five new luggage models a year between 1959 and 1965. Both tenures rest on one secondary chronology, **because the Wikipedia articles for Georges Vuitton and Gaston-Louis Vuitton return 404 in both editions, re-confirmed in this pass.**

**The Champs-Élysées building, 1913. [A, coordinate corrected]** Described as the largest travel-goods store in the world. The first pass's 48.8698 / 2.3079 is Wikipedia's centroid for the whole avenue; the store stands at no. 101 near the Étoile end, around 48.8722 / 2.2998, which the journey file uses.

**A muddled attribution recast. [corrected]** The first pass listed the 1885 Oxford Street opening as an example of Georges's post-1892 expansion, noticing in its own parenthesis that it happened while Louis lived and keeping the causal frame anyway. **Louis himself opened London in 1885.** Georges's expansion is the New York and Philadelphia phase and the 1913 building.

**Racamier and LVMH. [A]** Gaston-Louis dies 1970; Henry Racamier is described as assuming leadership by 1977. In 1987 the firm merges with Moët Hennessy, itself formed 1971 from Moët & Chandon (1743) and Hennessy (1765).

**Arnault. [A on narrative, contradiction on date]** Racamier invites Arnault to invest after boardroom conflict; Arnault consolidates control. en.wikipedia: chairman and CEO "since January 1989." fr.wikipedia: he took some 40% with Guinness after the October 1987 crash and "devient officiellement patron de LVMH en avril 1990." **Both carried; not adjudicated.**

**Fondation Louis Vuitton, 2014. [A, date corrected]** The first pass gave 27 October from memory. **Both editions give 20 October 2014**, inaugurated with President François Hollande present; Gehry, Bois de Boulogne, 8 avenue du Mahatma-Gandhi confirmed; the coordinate 48.8766 / 2.2634 is exact. **Retagged [A].**

**Asnières today. [A]** The workshop and adjoining Musée Louis Vuitton survive as a working atelier of roughly 180 artisans, presented as an unbroken line back to the founder's bench.

**Grand Palais, 2015-2016. [R]** *Volez, Voguez, Voyagez*, recalled from general knowledge and not confirmed against a fetched source. **Given no stop in the journey file.**

1. **"Marque L. Vuitton déposée"**, **[R]**. Trademark text woven into the Damier of 1888, reproduced across company histories and museum notes; no primary image or period filing could be fetched.
2. **"chez Vuitton, le magasin de malles de la rue Scribe"**, **[R]**. Surfaced by a Wikisource search snippet attributed to a French text titled *Mademoiselle Carrière, de l'Odéon*. **The page 404'd on direct fetch; the passage was never seen in situ; author, edition, and date unconfirmed.**

Actual source: Catherine Baker, *Pourquoi faudrait-il punir ?*, Tahin Party, 2004, chapter 7 (pp. 129-150), an anti-prison polemic, seen in situ at fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Pourquoi_faudrait-il_punir/7. **Two cautions carried into the journey file:** the bag is a passing prop in a hypothetical robbery, a vessel for stolen banknotes, and must not be read as writing about the maison; and it is a 2004 text supporting no claim about the nineteenth-century house.

## 9. The occupation: Gaston-Louis Vuitton, Pétain, and the Hôtel du Parc, 1940-1944

*Follow-up pass on one angle, added 2026-08-08. Section 8 carried Gaston-Louis's tenure of 1936-1970 on a single unreachable chronology and said nothing whatever about the four years in the middle of it. Nothing above this line is altered.*

**The evidentiary footing, stated first.** Everything in this section descends from one book that was not read: Stéphanie Bonvicini, *Louis Vuitton, une saga française*, Fayard, 2004, ISBN 2213618798. **[A: fr.wikipedia, *Stéphanie Bonvicini*, fetched in situ]** for author, title, publisher, year, and ISBN. No copy, no excerpt, and no review text was obtained in this pass. What follows is press and encyclopedia summary *of* a book, not the book, and the tags below keep that line visible.

**The article of record could not be opened. [gap]** The entire English-language chain rests on Kim Willsher, "Louis Vuitton's links with Vichy regime exposed," *The Guardian*, 3 June 2004, at theguardian.com/world/2004/jun/03/france.secondworldwar. **theguardian.com is blocked to this session's fetch tool, and web.archive.org, which holds the 2 June 2021 snapshot, is blocked as well.** The piece was never read in situ. Both Wikipedia editions cite it by author, headline, date, and live URL, and a third page quotes it in whole sentences. That corroborates the citation; it does not corroborate the text.

**The loyalty and the objects. [A: fr.wikipedia, *Louis Vuitton Malletier*, read in situ, footnoted to Bonvicini 2004]** The French article's whole treatment of the occupation is two clauses and one sentence about the son: "pendant la période du régime de Vichy (de 1940 à 1944), la maison Louis Vuitton affirme sa fidélité au régime du maréchal Pétain, accepte de fabriquer des objets à sa gloire." **The French article names neither Gaston-Louis, nor the busts, nor the Hôtel du Parc.** That is worth stating plainly: the fullest French-language encyclopedia account of the episode contains none of the three specifics this angle was set to test.

**The busts, and the number. [A: en.wikipedia, raw wikitext read in situ, citing Willsher]** Under the heading "WWII Nazi collaboration": members of the family "actively aided the Vichy government led by Marshal Philippe Pétain and increased their wealth from their business affairs with the Germans," and the family manufactured "more than 2,500 busts" glorifying Pétain. **One element of the research pool is struck here.** The claim that the busts are absent from the company's own business archives appears in no text fetched in this pass; retagged **[R]**.

**The Hôtel du Parc boutique. [R, one relay only]** Dirk de Klein, "Fashion and Fascism," dirkdeklein.net, 24 September 2025, read in situ, crediting Vogue UK and *The Guardian*: "Louis Vuitton had a store on the ground floor of a fabulous property, the Hotel du Parc in Vichy, where Pétain set up his puppet government," and "while the other shopkeepers, including the jewellers Van Cleef & Arpels, were shut down, Vuitton was the only one allowed to stay." **Neither named original could be opened.** The Hôtel du Parc claim is the sharpest fact in the angle and it rests on one blog relaying two newspapers. It is tagged accordingly and must not be promoted.

**Gaston-Louis's instruction to his son. [R, the same single relay]** Same page: "Vuitton's grandson, Gaston, the wartime head of the company, had instructed his eldest son, Henry, to forge links with the Pétain regime." This is the only sentence found anywhere in this pass that names Gaston-Louis in connection with the regime. Every other source discusses the house in the abstract, or discusses Henry.

**Henry Vuitton's decoration: a contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** The two encyclopedias credit the decoration to two different states.

1. **French, read in situ:** Henry "aurait entretenu des relations avec des officiers de la Gestapo, obtenant même de devenir l'un des rares industriels décorés par **l'Allemagne nazie** en remerciement de sa loyauté." Note the conditional *aurait*: the French article alleges, it does not assert.
2. **English, as the Guardian sentence is relayed:** Henry, "a regular at the local cafe frequented by the Gestapo, was one of the first Frenchmen to be decorated by the **Nazi-backed government**," which is to say by Vichy.

**Not adjudicated.** A decoration by the German Reich and a decoration by the French State are different facts, and no source consulted holds both. A second and smaller discrepancy is corrected rather than carried: "a regular at the local cafe frequented by the Gestapo" is not the claim that he frequented cafés *with* Gestapo officers, and the stronger reading is dropped.

**The publisher's answer. [A: en.wikipedia raw wikitext, quoting Willsher]** Caroline Babulle, spokeswoman for Éditions Fayard: "They have not contested anything in the book, but they are trying to bury it by pretending it doesn't exist." The pool attributed this to "Bonvicini's publisher"; she is named, and is named here.

**LVMH's answer. [A: same source]** An LVMH spokesman: "This is ancient history."

**A quotation surfaced and then dropped. [corrected]** A first, summarising fetch of the English article returned two further lines: an LVMH statement that the period "was family-run and long before it became part of LVMH," and a statement to *Le Canard Enchaîné*, "We don't deny the facts, but regrettably the author has exaggerated the Vichy episode." **Neither appears in the raw wikitext of that article, fetched immediately afterwards.** Both are dropped rather than repeated. The incident is logged because it is precisely the failure this legend exists to catch.

**Le Journal du Dimanche. [gap]** The brief for this addendum names a 2004 *Le Journal du Dimanche* investigation alongside the book. **No such article was found.** Not one page reached in this pass cites the JDD on this subject, and the session's web-search budget was exhausted before the point could be pressed further. The JDD investigation is neither confirmed nor refuted here. It is simply absent.

**Sydney Morning Herald, 4 June 2004. [R]** "Vuitton's wartime deal out of the bag," seen as a search snippet only; smh.com.au could not be fetched. Headline and date are carried, content is not.

**What this section does not establish. [gaps]**

- **No archival document of any kind was consulted.** No German or Vichy administrative record, no trade register for the Hôtel du Parc premises, no *épuration* file for any member of the family.
- **No bust was seen.** No museum holding, catalogue entry, photograph, or auction record of a Vuitton-made Pétain bust was found anywhere. The figure of 2,500 travels only as a sentence.
- **The workshop is unlocated.** Nothing consulted says where the objects were made. Asnières is not named in this connection by any source, and the journey file does not pin them there.
- **Gaston-Louis's own conduct**, beyond the one relayed sentence about instructing his son, is unrecorded in everything reached. Section 8's finding stands: the Wikipedia articles for Georges and Gaston-Louis Vuitton return 404 in both editions.
- **The postwar reckoning is blank.** Whether any Vuitton faced proceedings after 1944, and what became of the Vichy premises, is described in no source consulted.
