# Joseph-Antoine Bouvard (1840-1920): research report
*2026-08-21. Slug: `joseph_bouvard`. Architect of the City of Paris; director of architecture for the 1900 Exposition Universelle; consulting urbanist to Buenos Aires, Rosario and Sao Paulo, and on paper to Istanbul and Brussels.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named and reachable · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim whose only carrier could not be reached. Where the pool's verification pass overturned a tag, the overturn is recorded, not quietly applied.

One structural fact governs everything below. Almost nothing about Bouvard's private life is attested from a primary document anyone in this chain opened: marriage, son, wound, Legion d'honneur file and death day all descend from the INHA/AGORHA authority record and from Roseli Maria Martins D'Elboux's reconstruction of his service statement, and AGORHA returned 403 and 429 throughout. What is well attested is the built work, because it left buildings and municipal print behind.

## 1. Isere: the plasterer's son (1840-1863)

- Born 19 February 1840 at Saint-Jean-de-Bournay, Isere, son of Joseph Bouvard, plasterer, aged twenty-two, and Madeleine Rostaing. [A fr.wikipedia, 'Joseph-Antoine Bouvard'; consistent with D'Elboux's thesis and the italyworldsfairs.org profile]. Coordinates corrected to 45.5031, 5.1386 per fr.wikipedia's own figures.
- Father dead by 1843; mother remarried the wheelwright Gaspard Laurent at Vienne in 1844 [A fr.wikipedia]; mother dead at Vienne 1853, orphaning him at thirteen [R tradition reconstructing the Legion d'honneur file; no act located].
- Apprenticed to Hugues Quenin, architect of the City of Vienne. The apprenticeship is [A fr.wikipedia: "eleve de Hugues Quenin, architecte a Vienne"]; the year 1855 is [R], in no cited source, and the journey dates it only to order the segment.
- Tradition holds that Simon-Claude Constant-Dufeux, working on the Roman Temple of Augustus and Livia at Vienne, noticed the young draftsman and took him to Paris. [R; no contemporary letter or diary in any fetched source]
- Reliability flag: italyworldsfairs.org confirms the Quenin training but places it in "Vienna, Austria," and by the end of the run that host returned DNS failure and could not be re-checked.

## 2. Paris: the municipal apprentice (1864-1878)

- 30 August 1864, attached to the City of Paris architecture service; 1 December 1864, admitted to Constant-Dufeux's atelier at the Ecole imperiale et speciale des Beaux-Arts. The year is [A fr.wikipedia]; the exact day, the "second class" admission and matriculation number 2260 are [R], resting on Archives nationales AJ/52/357, which nobody in this chain viewed.
- 1865: two school medals, timber construction and perspective [A fr.wikipedia]. 1866 sub-inspector, 1870 inspector [R career summary without document].
- The wound, at Buzenval during the Siege of Paris. [R via D'Elboux's reading of the Legion d'honneur file; fr.wikipedia's Bouvard article does not mention it]. Unresolved: the pool dated it November 1870, when there was no action at Buzenval; fr.wikipedia's *Bataille de Buzenval* gives exactly two engagements, 21 October 1870 and 19 January 1871. The stop takes the later date to order the segment and says no source chooses. Coordinates corrected to 48.8607, 2.1929.
- 1 February 1871, assigned under Gabriel Davioud on the post-siege adaptation of the Palais du Luxembourg. [R tradition; date from the unviewed Legion d'honneur file]. Coordinates corrected to 48.8485, 2.3378, the palace and not the garden.
- 12 November 1874, married Clarisse Agathe Helene Basselier; 1 October 1875, son Charles Louis Roger Bouvard, also an architect, born at Marly-le-Roi. Both [R INHA record, 403 on every attempt]. Marly-le-Roi standardised at 48.8682, 2.0966.
- 1878: Pavilion of the City of Paris at the Exposition Universelle, and Chevalier of the Legion d'honneur. [A fr.wikipedia for the decoration, italyworldsfairs.org for the pavilion]

## 3. The municipal architect (1879-1890)

- 20 October 1879: architect of the permanent City of Paris service [R fr.wikipedia, undocumented]. 1880: government architect on the ministry's school-buildings committee [R unconfirmed against any ministerial record].
- Minor Paris work, all [R] and none carried as its own stop: motifs for the Place de la Republique with Ulysse Gravigny, 1881; Caserne Louviers for the Republican Guard, boulevard Morland, 1882-1883; the setting of the Alexandre Dumas monument at Place Malesherbes with Gustave Dore, 1883; the setting of the Diderot monument on the boulevard Saint-Germain with Jean Gautherin, 1886; the Archives de la Seine, quai Henri-IV, with Felix Roguet, circa 1888 (Quantin 2005, persee link not retrieved).
- Ecole nationale professionnelle de Voiron. That he was its architect is [A fr.wikipedia], with three corrections. Not "Vers 1885" but creation by decree 26 July 1882 and opening 5 October 1886, per fr.wikipedia's *Ecole nationale professionnelle*. Not the first ENP created, since Vierzon was decreed 9 July 1881, but the first to open, Vierzon opening only in 1887; the journey uses that narrow form. Coordinates corrected to the school, 45.3609, 5.6013.
- Gare de Saint-Etienne-Chateaucreux. [A archives.saint-etienne.fr: a Bouvard as architect, the iron armature on verins hydrauliques, opening 27 October 1884]. Corrections: construction is 1884-1886; the 1.80 m subsidence figure belongs to the earlier station's settling over twenty years, the reason for the rebuild, not to an allowance designed into Bouvard's structure; the page names only "M. Bouvard."
- 1889 Exposition Universelle: architect of the palace and central dome (appointment dated 31 July 1886 in the pool, [R]), designing the Palais des Industries diverses and its dome facing the Eiffel Tower. Gold medal; Officier of the Legion d'honneur the same year. [A fr.wikipedia] Citation correction: the pool credited him with the dome of the Galerie des Machines, which was Contamin and Dutert's building; fr.wikipedia's *Exposition universelle de 1889* states "Le palais des Industries est amenage par Joseph Bouvard." Huysmans's attack on the dome's polychromy is press reception [R].

## 4. Alphand's succession, the Bourse, the 1900 Exposition (1891-1904)

- Succession to Jean-Charles-Adolphe Alphand. Tag downgraded. Alphand died 6 December 1891, not 1892, and the cited article says only that Bouvard "lui succeda," with no year. Dated 1891-1892, tagged [R].
- Bourse du Travail, 3 rue du Chateau-d'Eau, 1888-1896, inaugurated May 1892. [A fr.wikipedia: Bouvard as architect, three allegorical heads for Travail, Paix and Republique, the wood-and-iron hall now named for Ambroise Croizat]. Coordinates corrected to 48.8689, 2.3619.
- Gare de Marseille-Saint-Charles, 1893-1896. [A fr.wikipedia: "La gare actuelle est batie de 1893 a 1896 par l'architecte Joseph-Antoine Bouvard"]
- 1897: director of architecture and festivals for the 1900 Exposition; Commandeur, then Grand Officier and a Grand Prix in 1900. [A fr.wikipedia; parismuseescollections.paris.fr calls him "directeur des travaux d'architecture de l'Exposition universelle de 1900"]. He fixed the siting of the Grand Palais and Petit Palais without designing either.
- 28 November 1900: his half-brother Florentin Laurent receives the Legion d'honneur from Bouvard's own hands. [A fr.wikipedia: "des mains meme de son frere Joseph-Antoine Bouvard le 28 novembre 1900"]
- 1900: Francois Flameng's ink-wash and gouache portrait, dedicated "A MONSIEUR BOUVARD," inv. PPD5844, Petit Palais. [A parismuseescollections.paris.fr]. Correction: the record says the drawing was made "pour la decoration detruite en 1965 du Conservatoire des arts et metiers." The scheme was destroyed, not the Conservatoire, which still stands.
- 17 June 1904, *Le Temps*: Bouvard in his own words on the Invalides esplanade, the Champ de Mars and gardens along the Seine embankments. [A verbatim French, in the fetched PDF of Novick 2000], which reads it as the precedent for the riverside esplanade he later proposed at Buenos Aires.

## 5. Cities on paper: Istanbul and Brussels (1902-1907)

- Istanbul: an unexecuted avant-projet for Sultan Abdulhamid II, watercolours for the Hippodrome, Beyazit Square, Valide Square and the Galata Bridge, the last reportedly borrowing from the Pont Alexandre III. Held at Istanbul University Library. [R throughout]
- The live contradiction, kept open. Zeynep Celik, "Bouvard's Boulevards" (*JSAH* 43 no. 4, 1984), and everything downstream of her: the work is 1902 and Bouvard never visited the city; her text could not be retrieved, JSTOR and UC Press both 403. Against that, fr.wikipedia verbatim: "1907 : il fait des propositions pour l'amenagement de Constantinople qu'il decouvre avec son fils Roger Bouvard." All three Istanbul stops state the conflict and none adjudicates.
- Brussels, 1903-1904: at Leopold II's request, a study of the approaches to the Palais de Justice and the Porte de Namur; a signed 1904 watercolour survives, unexecuted. [R D'Elboux; no Belgian archive consulted]

## 6. Buenos Aires (1906-1910)

- 9 December 1906, *Le Temps*, "Une autre «entente municipale»": the Paris press announces that Bouvard has accepted and will sail. [A as a quotation from D'Elboux, the direct witness; the 1906 issue was not opened, and her footnote 20 fixes the day the pool left vague]. The mission was mediated by the journalist Henri Turot and sought by mayor Carlos Torcuato de Alvear. [R]
- The 1907 mission is [A fr.wikipedia and es.wikipedia: "1907-1909 : plan d'urbanisme de la ville Buenos Aires"]. The travelling architects Georges Delattre and Louis Faure-Dujarric, the month of July and the council's approval appear in no fetched source and are [R]. Date contradiction, left visible: the arrival is dated July 1907, while "La futura Plaza del Congreso" is dated 27 June 1907, before he supposedly landed.
- Plaza del Congreso: the article in *La Prensa*, 27 June 1907 [R issue not retrieved; observatorioamba.org lists the scheme among the plan's associated projects]. Plaza de Mayo: an esplanade opening the ceremonial centre toward the Rio de la Plata [A observatorioamba.org ficha].
- Quinta Hale / Plaza Mitre, Recoleta, the enclave called La Isla. [A arcondebuenosaires.com.ar: "Jose Bouvard, Director de Parques de Paris" traced the streets and Plaza Mitre and built the terraces and staircases]. No year is given there, so 1907 is inference, and that title is a loose rendering of his post. Coordinates corrected to -34.5840, -58.3960.
- 2 July 1907: the architect Victor Julio Jaeschke attacks the diagonals in *El Tiempo* [R Tartarini; issue not retrieved]. 1908: mayor Manuel J. Guiraldes interrupts, then renegotiates the commission [R]. A French voice warns, in a passage D'Elboux quotes, that cancelling would strain Franco-Argentine relations; the pool called this a French diplomatic or press source, but her footnote 32 names it as Novick 2003, p.118, so it is a quotation at two removes and is cited that way.
- 1909: *El nuevo plano de la ciudad de Buenos Aires*, produced with a municipal commission of Carlos Maria Morales, Carlos Thays, Anastacio Iturbe, Fernando Perez and Roman Bravo, published 1910. [A observatorioamba.org ficha for the membership, the 1907-1910 span and the "Plano de Embellecimiento y Extension" framing]. The figure of roughly thirty-two diagonals is not in that source and is dropped. The source renders his name "J. J. Bouvard."
- The five quoted fragments of the 1909 report (all via Novick 2000) belong to it as a joint document: Novick says it was drawn up by "el frances Bouvard conjuntamente con una Comision de Tecnicos." One needed repair, the pool having replaced Novick's bracketed editorial insertion (hace) with an invented connective; the verbatim reading is restored.

## 7. The second crossing: Rosario, Montevideo, Curitiba, Sao Paulo (1909-1916)

- Bayonne 1909, and Antwerp 1910, where he presides over an international competition until a ministerial nationality rule forces his replacement and his former student Henri Prost goes on to win it. Both [R D'Elboux, unfetched]. They break the tidy story that his international practice began at Buenos Aires.
- Rosario, June-October 1910: invited after repeated proposals by council member Jose Martinoli and mayor Isidro Quiroga, he spends only days in the city and produces a generic embellishment report. [R Garcia Ortuzar and Berjman, Tartarini; cdsa.aacademica.org not retrieved]
- Montevideo, 1911: attribution unresolved. Compilations credit him with an embellishment plan; the record does not establish whether that was a commission, advice, or a conflation with the city's separate international avenues competition. Carried as a stop, flagged provisional. [R]
- 10 March 1911: retirement from full City of Paris service by prefectural order, becoming honorary director [R no order text seen]. Curitiba, March 1911: he travels to Brazil with the Belgian banker Edouard Fontaine de Laveleye, who controls the city's tramway, paving and electric-distribution interests; they seek an improvement-plan commission and are received coolly [R].
- Sao Paulo, 1911: invited by the public-works director Victor da Silva Freire to arbitrate the dispute over the Vale do Anhangabau, he spends roughly two months and proposes keeping the valley as a connected park that receives roads rather than a traffic conduit; report approved by municipal resolution of 16 June 1911. [R] Mis-sourcing corrected: the pool tagged this [A] to pt.wikipedia's *Companhia City*, which says nothing about a report, a resolution, the Anhangabau or Freire.
- The Companhia City. [A pt.wikipedia: founded 1911 with initial headquarters in London and French, English and Brazilian investors; in 1912 established in Sao Paulo, acquiring more than 15 million square metres; Barry Parker and Raymond Unwin responsible for the layout of Jardim America, original plan of 1919, 672 lots]. Not in that source, therefore [R]: Boulton Brothers, Lord Balfour of Burleigh, Laveleye, Bouvard's organizing role, and his vice-chairmanship until death. The company history carrying two competing constitution days, 11 and 25 September 1911, was not fetched.
- Pacaembu / Rua Traipu, 1911-1916: the first Pacaembu subdivision scheme for the company; survey and earthwork begin; abandoned in 1916, leaving Rua Traipu as the trace of a wider road never built. [R D'Elboux's thesis, abstract fetched, body not]

## 8. Death and afterlife

- Death at his home in Marly-le-Roi, aged eighty. The day is contested and stays contested: fr.wikipedia gives 5 November 1920, the INHA civil-act tradition 4 November 1920. Both trace to documentary sources; nothing consulted resolves it. The stop is dated 4 November and says so.
- No tomb, cemetery or grave marker was found in any source consulted: fr.wikipedia, INHA-linked pages, D'Elboux, Tartarini. The Marly-le-Roi civil registers would probably settle it and were not reached.
- Obituary, *La Construction moderne*, 28 November 1920. [R issue not retrieved]
- Avenue Joseph-Bouvard, Champ-de-Mars, named 18 March 1921, 450 m by 50 m, from the Place du General-Gouraud to 35 avenue de Suffren, the record describing him as "directeur administratif des services d'architecture, des promenades et plantations de la ville de Paris." [A fr.wikipedia]. Coordinates 48.8569, 2.2993. In 1984 a roundabout carved out of the avenue was detached and renamed Place Jacques-Rueff. [A same article]
- The 1910 imprint. The pool credited the Talleres Graficos de la Penitenciaria Nacional; observatorioamba.org, its own cited source, gives Imprenta Litografia y Encuadernacion Kraft, Buenos Aires, 1910, and states no page count. What survives is the cataloguing garble: that source's bibliography reads "Bouvard, Andres," its text "J. J. Bouvard."

### Legends, listed as legends, all [R]

1. Disciple of Haussmann. His master was Constant-Dufeux and his predecessor Alphand, himself a former Haussmann collaborator: institutional, one generation removed.
2. Sole author of a "Plan Bouvard" that reshaped Buenos Aires, against a 1907 report, a 1908 interruption, a commission and a collective 1909 plan.
3. Creator of Avenida 9 de Julio and the diagonals, proposed before he arrived and legislated later; 9 de Julio opened only from 1937.
4. Designer of the Parc des Buttes-Chaumont, which opened in 1867 under Alphand, Darcel, Davioud and Barillet-Deschamps, when Bouvard was a junior municipal employee.
5. The other Bouvard: auction listings sell Venetian oils under "Joseph Antoine Bouvard (1840-1920)," the architect's exact name and dates, when the painter was Antoine Bouvard, called Marc Aldine (1870-1956).

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. Istanbul: 1902 and never visited (Celik) against 1907 and visited with his son Roger (fr.wikipedia). Celik unreachable. Both kept.
2. The wound at Buzenval: November 1870 against the only two engagements that exist, 21 October 1870 and 19 January 1871.
3. Death: 4 against 5 November 1920.
4. Buenos Aires arrival in July 1907 against a Buenos Aires newspaper article of 27 June 1907.
5. Voiron: "vers 1885" against creation 26 July 1882 and opening 5 October 1886.
6. Companhia City constitution: 11 or 25 September 1911.
7. fr.wikipedia contradicts itself on 1889, giving Bouvard a dome that belonged to Contamin and Dutert's building while its own Exposition article assigns him the Palais des Industries.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Joseph Bouvard is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so nothing here applies a framework backward or forward. Two relations surfaced that are not rooted in his own life. Per the standing instruction they are flagged, not deleted. Both are forward, and forward is never licensable.

- Le Corbusier at Buenos Aires, 1929: lectures proposing to extend the city over the water toward the river, echoing the river-facing ambition of the 1909-1910 plan. Bouvard was nine years dead and no document connects them. Direction caught: forward. Not written into any campa.
- Emin Arslan, the exiled Ottoman journalist in Buenos Aires, offered in the pool as sharing Bouvard's double Ottoman-Argentine geography. Coincidence of geography, not relation. Direction caught: forward, and independently geography-only. Not written.

Four further pool interlocks were dropped as shared geography alone, which the map already shows: Auguste Rodin (the Thinker in Plaza del Congreso, November 1907), Troiano Troiani (lamps for the same plaza), Julio Argentino Roca (a monument standing on a diagonal the plan produced) and Juan Antonio Buschiazzo (Recoleta, a generation earlier).

Disambiguation flag, kept loud: the atlas traveler `torcuato_de_alvear` is Torcuato Antonio de Alvear, first intendente 1880-1887, died 1890. The mayor who recruited Bouvard was Carlos Torcuato de Alvear, elected 1906, a different man. Nothing here names the atlas traveler.

## Interlocks written into campas

Two, both relations rather than co-location, both in the 1909 commission stop.

- `carlos_thays`: sat on the municipal technical commission with Bouvard that produced the 1909 *Nuevo Plano*; observatorioamba.org's ficha lists him among the five. The one documented working relationship between Bouvard and an existing atlas traveler.
- `jean_claude_nicolas_forestier`: the pool records that Bouvard's plan cited Forestier among its theoretical authorities, with Henard and Buls, a one-directional citation that counts as a relation. [R], not verified against the 1910 printed plan. Named once, hedged.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- No burial place. Nothing at all.
- No primary document from the Legion d'honneur file was opened by anyone in this chain; every personal-life date descends from a summary of it.
- Whether the 1907 hospital and school designs belonged to the municipal contract or to a separate commercial negotiation is explicitly unresolved by Novick and D'Elboux. The hospital scheme, reportedly of two thousand beds, is therefore not carried as a stop.
- Whether the 1907 report and the 1909 plan are one evolving process or two distinct plans divides the scholarship, and this file does not choose.
- Delattre and Faure-Dujarric, the architects named for the 1907 crossing, appear in no reachable source.
