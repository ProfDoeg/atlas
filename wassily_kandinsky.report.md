# Wassily Kandinsky (1866-1944): research report
*2026-08-23. Compiled for `wassily_kandinsky.journey.json`: 8 segments, 43 stops, 13 quotes.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or an
autobiographical claim with no independent witness. Contradictions are flagged and **not**
adjudicated. Corrected coordinates are given with the value they replaced.

The governing difficulty here is that the three episodes everyone tells about him, the
Lohengrin at the Bolshoi, the unrecognisable Monet haystack, and his own canvas seen
sideways at dusk, all come from one document: his memoir *Rückblicke* (1913), written
fifteen to twenty years after the events by a man who by then had a theory to justify. They
carry the standard narrative and none of them has a second witness. All three are in the
journey file, all three tagged **[R]** in `date_confidence`.

---

## 1. Moscow, Odessa, Vologda: the law years, 1866-1896

- Born Moscow, **16 December 1866**, to the tea merchant Vasily Silvestrovich Kandinsky
  and Lidia Ivanovna Ticheeva **[A, en.wikipedia]**. Birth address not identified; city
  centre pin.
- Parents separate; raised largely in Odessa by his aunt Elizaveta Ticheeva; classical
  gymnasium; **piano and cello lessons from 1871** **[A, en.wikipedia]**. Household and
  school addresses unconfirmed; city centre pin.
- **1889**: ethnographic expedition to the Vologda region, on Komi (Zyrian) customary law
  **[A, en.wikipedia; The Art Story]**. The "moving about inside a painting"
  formulation is his own, from *Rückblicke* **[R as to the feeling, A as to the
  expedition]**. The villages visited are named in no reachable source; pin on the city.
- **1892**: degree in law and political economy under Aleksandr Chuprov, lectureship in
  jurisprudence, marriage to his cousin Anna ("Anja") Chimiakina **[A, en.wikipedia;
  The Art Story]**.
- **c. 1895, Bolshoi Theatre, Lohengrin** **[R]**. *Rückblicke* alone: no performance date,
  no witness. Year supplied to order the segment.
- **1896**: Monet's *Haystacks* at a French exhibition **in Moscow** **[A for the
  exhibition, R for the failure of recognition]**. A research pass labelled this stop
  "Munich exhibition hall" while giving Moscow coordinates; corrected, error recorded.
- **1896, Munich.** *Correction made:* "at age 29 he abandoned law" is not usable. He was
  29 for most of 1896 and turned 30 that December, and en.wikipedia says he began art
  studies "at the age of 30". The file gives the facts, not a single number.

## 2. Munich: the late apprentice, 1896-1908

- **Anton Ažbe's private school**, with Jawlensky and Werefkin in the same rooms
  **[A, en.wikipedia; The Art Story]**. *Contradiction left open:* The Art Story gives
  "through 1898", en.wikipedia an unbroken 1896-1900, standard biographies 1897-1899. The
  stop is dated 1897 and states the disagreement instead of choosing an endpoint.
- **1900, Franz von Stuck's class at the Munich Academy**, after an earlier rejection
  **[A, en.wikipedia]**. Klee enters the same class at nearly the same moment
  **[A, `paul_klee.journey.json`, in its own words]**.
- **1901, Phalanx** founded, Kandinsky president **[A, The Art Story]**. Month unknown.
- **Kallmünz, summer 1903** **[A, de.wikipedia, 'Gabriele Münter']**. *Correction made:*
  the pool placed the Kallmünz course and the start of the affair in **1902**; German
  Wikipedia gives **Kochel am See for summer 1902 and Kallmünz for summer 1903** and dates
  the affair to Kallmünz. The coordinate was also about 5 km east (given 49.1667/12.0333;
  Kallmünz is roughly 49.16/11.96). Both corrected.
- **Travel years 1903/04-1908**: Netherlands, Tunisia, Italy, Dresden, Brussels, Sèvres
  **[A, The Art Story, which gives 1903-1909; the dossier gives 1904-1908]**. Span
  contradiction noted, not resolved. Two stops carry the period:
  - **Kairouan [R]**: Tunisia is attested, *Kairouan is not*. It is the town the standard
    biographies name and was not confirmed as an itinerary stop.
  - **Sèvres 1906-07 [A]**, with the Salon d'Automne and Salon des Indépendants exhibits.
    Coordinate corrected about 650 m east to the town centre (given 48.8236/2.2033; town
    centre roughly 48.8236/2.2119). "Villa Beau-Séjour" circulates widely but no fetched
    source names it; the file does not assert it.
  - Dresden, Brussels and the Netherlands sit inside the Sèvres campa: the pool supports
    the itinerary but no event at those places.

## 3. Murnau and Der Blaue Reiter, 1908-1914

- **January 1909, Neue Künstlervereinigung München** founded, Kandinsky first president
  **[A, The Art Story; en.wikipedia]**. Founding address unidentified, approximate pin.
- **1909, Münter buys the Murnau house**, later the Russenhaus **[A, en.wikipedia]**.
  Kottmüllerallee is general knowledge and is not asserted as verified.
- **c. 1910, the sideways canvas on Ainmillerstraße [R]**. *Rückblicke* only: no date, no
  witness, no identification of the canvas, and the address itself unconfirmed.
- **1910, manuscript of *Über das Geistige in der Kunst* completed; Piper prints it late in
  1911 with a 1912 imprint [A]**; sources date it both ways. Sadleir's English translation,
  1914 **[A by bibliographic consensus, not re-fetched from a catalogue]**.
- **January 1911, the first letter to Schoenberg [A as to the correspondence, R as to the
  month]**: the month rests on the dossier alone.
- **December 1911**: Composition V refused by the jury, resignation, and the rival Blaue
  Reiter show opens at Thannhauser's Moderne Galerie **18 December 1911** **[A,
  en.wikipedia]**. Gallery address from general knowledge only.
- **1912, *Der Blaue Reiter Almanach*** with *Der gelbe Klang* bound into it
  **[A, en.wikipedia, 'The Yellow Sound']**.
- ***Klänge*: 1913, not 1912.** *Correction made:* de.wikipedia gives the imprint as
  "R. Piper, München 1913"; the 38 prose poems and 56 woodcuts are standard. The stop was
  cut in the trim to 43, not for the date; the correction is recorded so it is not lost.

## 4. War, Russia, the Soviet institutions, 1914-1921

- **August 1914**: enemy alien, flight to Switzerland with Münter **[A]**. *Goldach* is
  general knowledge; latitude corrected about 1 km south (47.4864/9.4667 falls out over
  Lake Constance; Goldach SG is roughly 47.477/9.468).
- **Late 1914**, Moscow via the Balkans **[A]**.
- **1916, Stockholm, last meeting with Münter [A, en.wikipedia, 'Gabriele Münter']**.
- **11 February 1917**, marriage to Nina Nikolaevna Andreevskaya, then about 17 or 18
  **[A]**.
- **Vsevolod ("Lodya")**: *contradiction left open.* The dossier gives **September 1917**,
  Wikipedia "end of 1917"; death **June 1920** at about two and a half is attested. The file
  uses September and says so.
- **1918-1921, Narkompros, SVOMAS/VKhUTEMAS, the provincial museum network [A in general
  chronology, dossier-sourced in its specifics]**, flagged as such in the stop.
- **1920, first director of INKhUK**; the psychophysical programme rejected by Rodchenko,
  Stepanova and Popova; he withdraws **[A, en.wikipedia]**.
- **December 1921**, departure for Berlin, legal, on a nominal cultural mission **[A]**.

## 5. The Bauhaus, 1922-1933

- **June 1922**, Weimar, at Gropius's invitation **[A]**. The Art Story dates the invitation
  to 1921 and the teaching start to 1922; the file keeps the teaching date.
- **Die Blaue Vier: March 1924, not 1923.** *Correction, and a live contradiction with an
  existing atlas file.* de.wikipedia gives the founding as "im März 1924 in Weimar",
  assembled and named by Galka Scheyer, first US show at the Charles Daniel Gallery, New
  York, **1925**. **`paul_klee.journey.json` says 1923.** This file uses 1924 and names the
  conflict; **Klee's file has not been touched** and needs the operator's decision.
- **1925**, forced move to Dessau **[A]**. Coordinate corrected 340 m west (51.8395/12.2323
  given; the Bauhausgebäude is 51.83944/12.22737 per de.wikipedia).
- **July 1926**, the Meisterhaus half shared with Klee **[A, `paul_klee.journey.json`;
  de.wikipedia]**. The pool's pin was 700 m south-east (51.8371/12.228); the correct
  51.8432/12.2205 is what Klee's and Albers's files already hold, inherited byte-identical.
- **1926, *Punkt und Linie zu Fläche*, Bauhausbuch 9 [A]**.
- **April 1928**, Mussorgsky's *Pictures at an Exhibition* staged at the Friedrich-Theater,
  Dessau **[A in the pool, exact day unknown]**. The theatre is gone; approximate pin.
- **1932** Dessau closure; **1933** the Berlin-Steglitz factory Bauhaus, sealed by police
  in April, dissolved by its own faculty in July **[A]**. The 20 July date is as usually
  reported, unconfirmed; the Birkbuschstraße address is general knowledge.
- **The "Wassily chair" is not a Kandinsky object [A, en.wikipedia, 'Wassily chair'].**
  Breuer designed it at Dessau in 1925-26, Thonet sold it as Model B3, Gavina attached the
  name decades later on reissue after learning Breuer had made a duplicate for Kandinsky's
  quarters. The file states only the duplicate. The pool's pin (51.8402/12.2496) is 1.5 km
  east of the building and was not used.

## 6. Neuilly, 1933-1944

- **End of 1933**, flat on the boulevard de la Seine, Neuilly, living room as studio
  **[A in general, place of death matches; exact building unidentified]**.
- **1937, Entartete Kunst.** *Correction made:* the pool first hedged the figure of 57,
  then corrected itself. de.wikipedia: **57 works confiscated from German museums, 14 shown
  in the Munich exhibition**, open 19 July to 30 November 1937. So "works", not "paintings",
  and no hedge. **The stop sits at his Neuilly pin, not in Munich**: he did not travel to it,
  and staging him there would put him at a place he never went.
- **March 1939, Galerie Renou et Colle [A, `frida_kahlo` and `diego_rivera` files]**.
- **1939**, French naturalisation **[A]**. *Contradiction left open:* The Art Story says
  he "gained German citizenship" in **1928**, against the consensus that he never naturalised
  German. The file records the conflict and asserts only the 1939 French naturalisation.
- **13 December 1944**, death at Neuilly **[A]**.

## 7. The estate

- **Burial: Cimetière nouveau de Neuilly-sur-Seine, Division 16 rang 35 [A, fr.wikipedia]**.
  *Correction made:* the cemetery is **not in Neuilly**. Its gate is at 40 rue de Vimy,
  **Nanterre**, grounds running into Puteaux, at 48.8958/2.2328. The pool held two wrong
  pins (48.885/2.199, 2.7 km south-west; 48.8836/2.2432, inside Neuilly proper and pointing
  at a different cemetery). The corrected value is used.
- **19 February 1957, the Münter gift to the Lenbachhaus.** *Correction made:* the count is
  **more than 90 oils**, not "more than 80", with over 330 watercolours and drawings,
  sketchbooks, reverse-glass paintings, most of his prints, 25 of her own canvases, and works
  by Jawlensky, Marc, Macke and Werefkin. Coordinate corrected to 48.1469/11.5636
  (Luisenstraße 33) from 48.1477/11.5678.
- **The 1926 settlement [A, en.wikipedia]**: Münter returned 26 boxes and about a dozen
  paintings and kept title to the rest, which is why the 1957 gift was hers to make.
- **12 May 1972, Der gelbe Klang premiered at the Guggenheim [A, en.wikipedia]**, 63 years
  after composition. Pin inherited byte-identical (40.783/-73.959).
- **Nina Kandinsky murdered at Gstaad, 2 September 1980 [A, en.wikipedia]**, case unsolved.
  *The apparent 1980/1981 contradiction is not a real one*: the Pompidou page's 1981 is the
  execution of the bequest under her January 1980 will, not her death. A conflation, and the
  file says so.
- **Bild mit Häusern [A, Restitutiecommissie; Stedelijk]**: sold at Frederik Muller,
  Amsterdam, **9 October 1940** (*correction*: not "8-9 October"), 160 guilders hammer, 176
  with premium; claim rejected 22 October 2018, handed over 27 February 2022.
- **Murnau mit Kirche II [R]**: restituted by the Van Abbemuseum to the Stern heirs, sold
  2023 for a reported GBP 37.2 million, **not** verified against an auction house this pass.
  The pool gave the museum two longitudes (5.4781 and 5.4859); nl.wikipedia gives
  51.4347/5.4819. The stop is folded into the Amsterdam campa, so no Eindhoven pin is used.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Kandinsky is **not** on `EXCEPTIONS.md`. The following relations came out of the interlock
pass, are **not rooted in Kandinsky's own life**, and are therefore **not** written into the
journey file. They are recorded here rather than deleted, per the adversary's duty in
`EXCEPTIONS.md`. **All are forward, not backward**: they are relations constructed
after his death, or by other people about him, so none of them is licensable by the
existing exception even if he were added to the list.

1. **`hilma_af_klint`, forward.** Her De Fem notebooks carry non-figurative marks from
   1896, fourteen years before his claim of priority; her landscape hung at the 1914 Baltic
   Exhibition in Malmö in the same show as early abstract work of his; LACMA 1986 and Venice
   2013 made the two names terms in one argument. **Kandinsky never heard of her.** Every
   link is a posthumous construction, so naming her in his file would write a later artifact
   into an earlier block. Her own file carries the relation one-directionally.
2. **`laban`, none.** A movement atelier in Schwabing from around 1910, same square
   kilometre. Pure co-location; his file carries it from his side.
3. **`anni_albers`, co-location.** The Alberses took the Moholy-Nagy half of the next
   Meisterhaus from 1928; her file says only that they "share the double house next door".
   Josef Albers was a fellow master and entering students sat Kandinsky's form-and-colour
   course, so a teacher-student relation is plausible, but **no source reached attests that
   Anni Albers sat it**, so she is not named. If the operator can attest it, the Dessau campa
   is where it goes.
4. **`frank_lloyd_wright` and `cecilia_vicuna`, forward.** His Guggenheim relation is to
   **Hilla Rebay and Solomon Guggenheim**, who bought from him from 1929 and are named in
   that campa. The building opened in 1959. Neither Wright nor Vicuña is named.
5. **`jacques_lacan`, one step too far.** The real relation is **Alexandre Kojève**, born
   Aleksandr Kozhevnikov, given in the dossier and in standard Kojève biography as
   Kandinsky's **nephew** and author of an essay arguing that these paintings are not
   abstract but *concrete*. Lacan attended Kojève's seminar; Kandinsky did not. Kojève is
   named in the file, **Lacan is not**, and the family tie is tagged **[R]**: it was not
   re-confirmed from a primary source this pass, and it should be.
6. **`richard_wagner`, legitimate.** Kandinsky heard *Lohengrin* and wrote about it, a real
   one-directional relation from his side, so Wagner is named. The pool also offered a Weimar
   bridge (Liszt's 1850 *Lohengrin* premiere in the town Kandinsky later taught in): shared
   geography seventy years apart, **not** used.

## Honest gaps

- **No primary source was consulted this pass.** Everything rests on Wikipedia (English,
  German, French), The Art Story, the Lenbachhaus and Stedelijk pages, the Dutch Restitutions
  Committee, and a Project Gutenberg text. *Rückblicke* was not read: it is quoted at second
  hand and every claim depending on it is tagged **[R]**.
- **No exact address is verified anywhere in Munich.** Ažbe's school, the Phalanx school,
  Ainmillerstraße, Thannhauser's gallery and Piper's offices are approximate or
  general-knowledge pins, and each stop says which.
- **The Vologda villages, the Tunisian itinerary and the Swiss town of 1914** come from
  general knowledge, not a fetched source. Kairouan and Goldach are the weakest.
- **The Kojève family tie** needs a primary source; see above.
- **The birth month of Vsevolod** is unresolved between two sources.
- **The Ažbe study endpoint** (1898? 1899? 1900?) is unresolved between three.
- **Nina Kandinsky's memoir, *Kandinsky und ich* (1976)**, was **not** consulted, and most
  twentieth-century Kandinsky biography passes through it. It is also the most interested
  document in the corpus: the widow's account, by the sole legatee, thirty-two years after
  his death. The Moscow marriage, the son and the departure from Russia are ultimately hers.
- **Münter's side** survives because she kept the paintings and outlived him by eighteen
  years; what she thought between 1916 and 1926 is known from settlement papers.
