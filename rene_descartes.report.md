# René Descartes (1596-1650), research report
*Compiled 2026-08-15 for the journey file `rene_descartes.journey.json`. Calendar: Gregorian throughout (France from 1582; the Dutch Republic mixed by province; Sweden still Julian in 1650).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a single late biographer. The earliest full life is **Adrien Baillet, *La Vie de Monsieur Des-Cartes* (1691)**, written forty-one years after the death from papers (including the lost *Olympica* notebook) that no longer exist. Baillet is indispensable and unverifiable at once; anything resting on him alone is **[R]** here.

The verification pass turned up **fifteen substantive corrections** to the gathered material, most inherited from en.wikipedia. They are flagged in place and tabulated in section 9.

---

## 1. Touraine and the Jesuits, 1596-1616

**Born 31 March 1596 at La Haye en Touraine [A]**, in his maternal grandmother's house, son of **Joachim Descartes**, councillor in the parlement of Brittany, and **Jeanne Brochard** (Britannica; en.wikipedia; Rodis-Lewis; Grayling). **Baptised 3 April [A]** at the parish church of Saint-Georges, the register naming the father's office (cited in Rodis-Lewis). Minor robe nobility: lawyers, physicians, land in Poitou, no fortune.

> **COORDINATE CORRECTION.** The dossier pinned this one town in **three different places** (47.0089/0.6864, 47.0093/0.6857, 46.9744/0.6875), the first two about **4 km north-northwest of it, in open country**. fr. and en.wikipedia agree on **46.9744, 0.6986**.

**Mother dead 13 May 1597 [A]** (fr.wikipedia), in childbed with another infant. Raised by his grandmother **Jeanne Sain** and a nurse at **Châtellerault** while Joachim remarried and kept to Brittany **[A** Rodis-Lewis**]**. The frail health, the exemption from ordinary discipline, and the lifelong late rising (defended to Constantijn Huygens) are attested **[A]**.

**Collège Royal Henri-Le-Grand, La Flèche**, founded 1604 when Henri IV gave a royal château to the Society of Jesus **[A** en.wikipedia, 'Prytanée National Militaire'**]**.

> **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** Entry **1606** or **Easter 1607** (SEP, IEP). Departure **1614** (IEP, and this dossier's chronology) or **1615** (SEP: "Left La Flèche: 1614 or 1615"; the Prytanée article: a pupil "from 1607 to 1615"). The dossier contradicted itself, chronology against geography. The stop is dated 1607 and carries the hedge in `date_confidence`.
> **COORDINATE CORRECTION.** The college was given twice with two different longitudes, 47.6975/-0.0784 and 47.6975/-0.0733, neither right. The Prytanée is at **47.70056, -0.07583**.
> **FACTUAL CORRECTION.** The Église Saint-Louis was said to be "completed in 1607". en.wikipedia puts its construction **after Henri IV's death in 1610**, as the shrine for his and Marie de Médicis's hearts, running into the 1630s. Dropped.

**Poitiers, 1615-1616 [A].** Baccalauréat then licence in canon and civil law, the degree dated 1616 by SEP. **CORRECTION:** the dossier said he "is admitted to the bar though he never practices". No bar admission followed from a licence, and the dossier inverted the order of the two qualifications.

## 2. Arms, dreams, and the roads, 1616-1628

**Paris, c. 1616-1618 [R].** Baillet alone for the gamblers and fencers, the Faubourg Saint-Germain retreat, and the geometry with **Claude Mydorge**.

**Breda, 1618 [A].** Unpaid gentleman volunteer under **Maurice of Nassau** (SEP; en.wikipedia). The friendship with **Isaac Beeckman** dates from the same year, documented in the two men's own notebooks and letters (Adam and Tannery) **[A]**.

> **TAG CORRECTION, MISCITED SOURCE.** The **placard anecdote** was tagged **[A]** and cited to the Descartes-Beeckman correspondence in Adam and Tannery. It is not there. en.wikipedia's Beeckman article hedges it ("It is said that they met when both were looking at a placard..."); the story comes from the Baillet tradition, so it is **[R]**. The placard was "posted on a wall"; the sources say **in the marketplace**.
> **DATE CORRECTION.** Frankfurt was given as **July 1619**. Ferdinand II was elected **28 August** and crowned **9 September 1619**, and Descartes's own sentence has him "returning to the army from the coronation of the emperor" when winter caught him.

**The stove-heated room, 10-11 November 1619.** The room itself is Descartes's own, *Discourse* Part II **[A]**: shut up alone all day, no company that interested him, undisturbed by cares or passions. The **three dreams** (whirlwind, thunderclap, a book opening at *quod vitae sectabor iter*) are **Baillet [R]**, summarizing the lost *Olympica*.

> **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** en.wikipedia says **Neuburg an der Donau**; the IEP says **Ulm**. Descartes names no town. The file pins Neuburg and says so in `date_confidence`.

**White Mountain, 8 November 1620 [R as to his presence].** en.wikipedia's battle article has him with the Catholic League "as an official observer"; its Descartes article is vaguer; Rodis-Lewis treats the claim as unverified. **COORDINATE CORRECTION:** the dossier's 50.0836/14.2953 sits ~1.5 km west of the battlefield, out toward Ruzyně. Used: **50.081, 14.317**.

**Poitou, 1622 [A].** Sells the inherited land, puts the proceeds into bonds. This is the financial fact the whole shape of the life rests on.

**Italy, 1623-1625 [R].** Loreto (a vow made after the dreams), Venice, Rome in the Holy Year, all Baillet; SEP itself calls the stretch poorly documented. The journey keeps Loreto and Rome.

**Paris, 1625-1628 [A].** The **Mersenne** circle, optics, and the abandoned *Rules for the Direction of the Mind*.

> **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED (La Rochelle).** The dossier's chronology says **1627** and tags **[A]**; its geography says **1628** and tags **[R]**. en.wikipedia says 1627 in both relevant articles. The siege ran 10 September 1627 to 28 October 1628, so both years fall inside it. Used: 1627, with the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

## 3. The Dutch Republic, 1628-1649

Relocates permanently in 1628 **[A** SEP**]** for solitude and freedom from ecclesiastical interference, changing address **more than twenty times [A** Gaukroger**]** in twenty-one years.

- **Franeker, April 1629 [A]**, matriculation confirmed by SEP. **CORRECTION:** the dossier attached "under a false name" here. It belongs to **Leiden, 1630**, where he enrolled as **Poitevin** (en.wikipedia); fr.wikipedia records Franeker with no assumed name. The dossier had it right in its chronology and misplaced it in its geography.
- **Amsterdam, from 1629 [A]**: lodgings, dissections at the butchers, printers and instrument makers.
- **Deventer, 1632-1633 [A]**: writing *Le Monde*, which he suppresses in **1633 [A]** on learning of Galileo's condemnation; printed only in 1664.
- **Francine born at Deventer, 1635 [A]** to **Helena Jans van der Strom**, a household servant. **1644 [R]**: on a visit to France he contributes toward a dowry for Helena's marriage (SEP chronology, no document reached).
- **Discourse on the Method**, Leiden, Jan Maire, **8 June 1637 [A]**: anonymous, in French, with the *Dioptrics*, *Meteors* and *Geometry*.
- **Francine dies of scarlet fever, September 1640, aged five [A]**, reportedly the greatest sorrow of his life. **GAP:** the reachable sources give the month but **not the town**; the stop is named "The Dutch Republic" and falls back on the Deventer coordinate.
- **Meditations on First Philosophy, 1641 [A]**, Latin, Paris, with six sets of Objections and Replies (Hobbes, Gassendi, Arnauld among them); second edition Amsterdam **1642** with a seventh set and the letter to Father Dinet **[A]**.
- **Elisabeth of Bohemia, from 6 May 1643 [A]**: six years of correspondence, the mind-body question pressed harder than by any other reader, the dedication of the *Principles*, and the material behind *The Passions of the Soul*. Two of her letters and two of his are quoted in the journey file from **Bennett's translation** (2017), which modernizes forms of address by its own editorial note.
- **Egmond-Binnen, c. 1643-1649 [A]**, the longest single Dutch residence.
- **Principles of Philosophy**, Amsterdam **1644 [A]**; French translation, Paris **1647 [A]**; *Comments on a Certain Broadsheet* against **Regius**, Amsterdam **1648 [A]**; *The Passions of the Soul*, Amsterdam **1649 [A]**.

> **CORRECTION (Endegeest), date and place both.** Given as **1637-1643** at **52.1611, 4.4649**. nl.wikipedia has 1641 to 1643; fr.wikipedia is precise, "Le 31 mars 1641, l'auteur reconnu s'installe dans le petit château d'Endegeest". The coordinate is **52.1725, 4.4631** (Oegstgeest), 1.3 km north. This changes the narrative: he moved in **months before** the *Meditations* appeared, so the writing and most of the Objections correspondence predate Endegeest. The campa says he answered objectors there, not that he wrote the book there.
> **CORRECTION (Utrecht).** Given as **1635-1645**. Nothing places the affair in 1635. **Voetius** attacked from **1639**, was rector 1641-42, the senate condemned the new philosophy in **March 1642**, and the appeal to the French ambassador follows in **1643**.

> **QUOTE CORRECTION.** The dossier carried "Staying as I am, one foot in one country and the other in another, I find my condition very happy, in that it is free," sourced only to **Wikiquote**. The passage is real, the wording untraceable. Bennett (descartes1643_4.pdf, "Descartes writes in vi or vii.1648", p. 64) reads: **"Meanwhile, having one foot in each country, I find my condition a very happy one in that it is free."** That wording and citation are used.

## 4. Sweden and the death, 1649-1650

Accepts Christina's invitation and travels north in the **autumn of 1649 [A]**. The queen requires philosophy at **five in the morning [A** IEP**]**, against a lifetime of lying in bed until nearly eleven. Falls ill at the start of February **[A]**, generally called pneumonia; Chanut's own word was *peripneumonia*, and Descartes refused treatment from the queen's physician **Johann van Wullen [A]**. **Dies 11 February 1650 [A]**, aged 53, in the house of the French ambassador **Pierre Chanut**. The **arsenic theory** is **[R]**, left unresolved by Rodis-Lewis.

**Burial [A as to the ground].** As a Catholic in Lutheran Sweden he could not lie in consecrated ground and was put in a plot used mainly for orphans and unbaptised children.

> **ANACHRONISM CORRECTED.** The place was named **Adolf Fredriks kyrkogård**. That church was **built 1768-1774**; in 1650 the ground belonged to **St Olof's chapel**. Its coordinate is within ~120 m and is kept as a pin, the anachronism spelled out in the campa.
> **DATE GAP.** The dossier's **14 February 1650** could not be corroborated anywhere; the funeral is usually placed later in February. Kept, with the failure stated.
> **IMPOSSIBLE DATE DROPPED.** A memorial was said to be raised at Adolf Fredriks kyrka **"in the 1720s"** (an en.wikipedia caption). The church did not exist, and en.wikipedia's own article on it credits **Gustav III** (reigned 1771-1792).

## 5. The bones, and the reception

**Exhumed 1 May 1666 [A** fr.wikipedia**]**, sealed in a copper box. The coffin reaches **Paris in January 1667**, waits at **Saint-Paul**, and on **24 June 1667** is placed in the church of the **abbey of Sainte-Geneviève**, monument added 1669.

> **THE CONFLATION.** en.wikipedia's Descartes article says **Saint-Étienne-du-Mont**, and the dossier reproduced this twice, once bracketing the two names as one building. They are **two churches**. Saint-Étienne-du-Mont is the surviving parish church next door; the **abbey church was demolished in 1804**, only its bell tower left inside the Lycée Henri-IV, near **48.846, 2.349**. A second dossier entry sent the 1667 remains to **Saint-Germain-des-Prés**, which they reached only in **1819**.
> **YEAR CORRECTION (Panthéon).** Given as **1791-1793** in one place and **1792** in another. fr.wikipedia: **2 October 1793**, Chénier's motion, decreed and never executed. en.wikipedia's 1792 is impossible, the Convention having convened only on 21 September 1792.

**Saint-Germain-des-Prés, 26 February 1819 [A]**: the present grave, a floor slab naming him, the skeleton short a finger and the skull.

> **DATE RANGE CORRECTED BY DECADES (the skull).** Given as "**1878-present**, Musée de l'Homme". fr.wikipedia: allegedly taken by **J. Fr. Planström** at the 1666 exhumation, bought at auction and brought to France by **Berzelius in 1821**, given to **Cuvier**, put in the anatomical collection at the **Jardin des Plantes**, and transferred to the **Musée de l'Homme only in 1931**. That museum was founded in 1937; its predecessor of 1878 is presumably the source of the error. fr.wikipedia adds that **five other skulls** have been attributed to him.

Two further **[R]** relic claims get no stop: a fragment reaching **Lund in 1691**, and **2020 research** doubting the Musée de l'Homme cranium.

**Suppression [A].** Alexander VII put the works on the **Index in 1663**; **Louis XIV forbade the teaching of Cartesian philosophy** in France in **1671**.

**The Frans Hals portrait, c. 1649 [A]**, template for most later images, gets no stop: no location beyond "Netherlands", and a contested attribution.

**The town [A].** La Haye was renamed during the Revolution, **La Haye-Descartes** from 1802, and simply **Descartes** from **1967** after the 1966 merger with Balesmes. Statue near the town hall, museum in the birth house. en.wikipedia's La Flèche article notes that **no Descartes monument survives on the college campus**.

## 6. Interlocks with travellers already in the corpus

Named inside campas, on the strength of a real relation between persons:

- **jean_jacques_rousseau**: at Chambéry, around nineteen, reading "Descartes, Locke, Voltaire, geometry, chemistry, botany, in no order and with no teacher". Used at the Leiden 1637 stop, because the *Discourse* was in French.
- **michel_foucault**: lecturing on Descartes at Tunis in 1966. Used at the *Meditations* stop.
- **leonard_euler**: the 1723 Basel dissertation setting "the natural philosophy of Descartes against that of Newton", and **kant**: the abandoned 1746 manuscript on "the quarrel between the Cartesians and the Leibnizians over how force is to be measured". Both at the *Principles* stop.
- **newton**: Trinity tradition that Barrow examined him in 1664 and "found him weak on Euclid and strong on Descartes". The Newton file flags this as a **Conduitt anecdote from half a century later**; the campa repeats the flag.
- **vico**: the oration of 18 October 1708 against "the reigning Cartesian fashion, which prizes the geometrical method and the critical faculty above all". Used at the 1671 prohibition stop, where the point is that the ban did not take.

Deliberately **not** named, though present in the interlock material, because shared geography is not a relation and the map already shows co-location: **johan_palmstruch** (Amsterdam 1634-1647, and freed from prison by Christina in 1646, but no contact in any source), **kircher** (shared Jesuit formation and war geography; his file never names Descartes), **luis_federico_leloir** (studied on the rue Descartes in 1925, which is commemoration, not relation), and the queued **John Dee and Edward Kelley** and **Jan Amos Comenius**. Comenius and Descartes are reported to have met in the Dutch Republic in the early 1640s, but that meeting appears **nowhere in the verified pool for this run**, so it is unused; worth checking when Comenius is researched.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** **Paris 48.8566, 2.3522** (from `luis_federico_leloir.journey.json`; four stops) and **Amsterdam 52.3676, 4.9041** (from `johan_palmstruch.journey.json`; four stops). The Amsterdam harbour stop keeps the dossier's distinct 52.3731, 4.8922; Stockholm uses site coordinates rather than a city pin.

## 7. Honest gaps

1. **The town of Francine's death is unknown.** The stop is placed on Deventer, where she was born, and says so.
2. **The Stockholm burial date (14 February 1650)** could not be corroborated anywhere.
3. **The Italian years, 1623-1625**, rest entirely on Baillet, who was not read directly in this run; he arrives through SEP, IEP, and the standard biographies.
4. **No primary text was reached** for the two most-quoted Sweden details, the five-o'clock lessons and the death scene. Both come through the biographical tradition. Nothing was invented to fill the hole; the journey file quotes nobody at those stops.
5. **The 1644 dowry for Helena Jans** is **[R]**, from the SEP chronology, no document reached.
6. **The 2020 forensic doubt** about the Musée de l'Homme skull is **[R]**, secondary press only.
