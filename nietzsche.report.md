# Nietzsche — report

**Sources.** Standard biographical spine: Wikipedia/Britannica/Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entries; Curtius-era chronology cross-checked against fnietzsche.com, the Nietzsche Channel (letters archive), and Klassik Stiftung Weimar / Nietzsche-Gesellschaft memorial pages for each site (Röcken, Naumburg, Schulpforta, Basel, Tribschen/Richard-Wagner-Museum, Bayreuth Festspiele history, Sils-Maria Nietzsche-Haus, Genoa local history sites, Sorrento tourist-historical pages, Turin/Piemonte tourism pages). Quotes drawn from *Ecce Homo* (Gutenberg/Nietzsche Channel texts), the Burckhardt and Cosima Wagner "Wahnbriefe," letters to Gersdorff, Elisabeth, Peter Gast, and Lou von Salomé, and — for the Naumburg sickroom stop — Rudolf Steiner's *Mein Lebensgang*, matching the wording already used in `steiner.journey.json`.

**Judgment calls.** (1) Several exact days are inferred/traditional where sources give only a month or season: the 1850 Naumburg move (Easter), the 1861 Schulpforta confirmation, the household-of-women stop (1854), the Genoa 1882 *Gay Science* stop, Sils-Maria Part Two (1883 summer), Beyond Good and Evil at Nice (1886), the Tautenburg idyll (1882), and the 1897 Weimar move — all marked `traditional`, never `attested`, per the rule. (2) The horse incident and the "Wahnbriefe" (Jan 4–6) are folded into one stop dated to the horse (Jan 3) rather than given separate entries, to hold the stop count at 45. (3) "Piazza Carlo Alberto" is used for the collapse site per the curator's brief; some sources instead name Piazza Carignano/San Carlo — the ambiguity is real in the literature, so the canon-name choice follows direction rather than adjudicating it. (4) Erlangen (not a front-line town) stands in for "the war" because that is where Nietzsche's dysentery/diphtheria recovery is actually documented, rather than inventing a battlefield stop. (5) The Rome/Orta/Tautenburg triptych (Lou von Salomé, 1882) is not named in the curator's arc list but is kept — it is one of the best-attested and richest episodes of the wandering decade and does not conflict with the named anchors.

**Gaps / time-folds.** The ten Basel teaching years (1869–79) are compressed to war service, the Tribschen visits, the inaugural lecture, and *The Birth of Tragedy* — the ordinary term-time is passed over in silence, as Goethe's Weimar decade often is in this atlas. The Engadine summers (seven of them, 1881–88) are represented by three stops (the rock, Part Two, and by implication the returns); the five Nice winters (1883–87) by three. The Weimar-Naumburg overlap with Steiner is treated as coincidence-in-fact (same city, adjacent years) rather than any encounter Nietzsche himself could register — he is present in his own sickroom stop only as an object seen, never a subject who acts, which is the honest register for a mind already gone.

**Five richest episodes.** (1) The Rohn bookshop discovery of Schopenhauer, Leipzig 1865 — the first real philosophical lightning-strike. (2) Tribschen, 1869–72, twenty-three pilgrimages to Wagner's villa — the closest thing to a home he ever has. (3) The Surlej rock at Sils-Maria, August 1881 — the eternal-recurrence vision, the stop this file is built toward, mirroring Zoroaster's river-ford revelation as the second great prophet-vision in the atlas. (4) Rapallo, February 1883 — Zarathustra Part One written entire in ten days of grief-fevered walking. (5) Turin, January 1889 — the horse, the god-signed letters, and the twenty years of silence that follow, ending in the Weimar sickbed Steiner actually saw.

**Connections in the atlas.** Schulpforta stop cross-refs `fichte.journey.json` (same cloister, explicit line "the same cloister-walls that will one day form Nietzsche"). The Surlej rock is the stop the whole file is built toward and sits opposite `zoroaster.journey.json`'s river-ford vision as the atlas's second eternal-fire revelation. The Weimar/Naumburg closing arc is deliberately pinned to `steiner.journey.json`'s "Naumburg — Nietzsche's sickroom" stop (same coordinates, same 1896-01-25 date, matching quote) and to its Goethe-Schiller-Archiv stop, making the two files interlock rather than duplicate. Background mentions in `dannunzio`, `jung`, `laban`, `savitri_devi`, and `serrano` (all read for this brief) confirm Nietzsche as the modern esoteric wing's shared ancestor-text — none of those files needed correction, only cross-reference here.
