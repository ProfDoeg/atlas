# Adriano (Publius Aelius Hadrianus, Hadrian), 76-138 CE

*Atlas of Journeys research report, 2026-08-02. Journey artifact: `adriano.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops, calendar julian.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged in place and left unresolved. Where a pool date or coordinate was a modern guess dressed as a datum, the correction is recorded rather than applied silently.

## The evidence base

There is no contemporary biography of Hadrian. Three compromised pillars carry the life.

1. ***Historia Augusta*, *Vita Hadriani*,** attributed to a "Aelius Spartianus" who did not exist. Modern scholarship treats the collection as a single late-fourth-century composite, derivative of lost sources and salted with fabricated documents. **[A on the scholarly judgement: Livius.org and standard HA historiography]** It is still the most detailed ancient life, and much about Hadrian's childhood, character and death exists nowhere else.
2. **Cassius Dio, Book 69,** surviving only in Xiphilinus's *Epitome*: a summary of a summary, hostile in places, and the source of the strongest ancient claim that the deathbed adoption was a fraud.
3. **Inscriptions and coins,** the only contemporary class: the Lambaesis adlocutio, RIB 1340, Balbilla's epigrams, the Panhellenion dossier, the Palmyrene texts, the Pantheon brick stamps. These fix several dates the narrative sources fumble, and they are why a number of pool entries had to be corrected.

Hadrian also wrote an autobiography, published under his freedman Phlegon's name. **It is lost.** Every statement about his motives reaches us through writers who had a book we do not.

## 1. Birth and the disputed birthplace (76)

**[A, HA 1.1-1.3]** Born 24 January in the seventh consulship of Vespasian and the fifth of Titus, so 76. The consular dating is precise.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** HA says he was born **at Rome**; Wikipedia, Britannica and the handbook tradition say **Italica in Baetica**, resting on the family's undisputed Italican origin rather than on a competing ancient testimony. The journey pins Rome and states the dispute in `date_confidence`. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool pinned the HA birthplace at the **Colosseum**, a building not dedicated until 80 and unconnected to any Aelian house. No source locates the domus, so the journey uses a neutral Palatine and Forum point, **41.8893, 12.4853**, labelled unlocated.

**[A]** Father Publius Aelius Hadrianus Afer, senator of praetorian rank; mother Domitia Paulina of Gades; family in Baetica since the Punic wars, related to Trajan.

## 2. Ward, schooling, first arms (85 to 95)

**[A, HA 1.4]** "Patre orbatus... decimo aetatis anno": deprived of **his father** in his tenth year, so 85 or 86. **Overstatement corrected:** the claim that he was orphaned of both parents at ten is **not attested**. No ancient source dates the death of Domitia Paulina the elder, who may have outlived 86. The pool's two lenses also disagreed, 85 against 86; HA permits either and the journey pins 85.

**[A, HA 1]** Joint guardianship of Trajan and the eques Publius Acilius Attianus; Roman education and the nickname **Graeculus**. **Sequence corrected:** HA places the Rome schooling *before* the return to Italica, so it belongs to c. 85-90, not to 90. **[A, HA 2.1]** "Quintodecimo anno ad patriam rediit": in his fifteenth year, c. 90 rather than the pool's 91, he returns to Italica, takes up arms, and is recalled by Trajan for hunting too much.

## 3. The Trajanic cursus (95 to 117)

**[A, HA 2.2]** Military tribune, **Legio II Adiutrix**, c. 95.

**[R, anachronism corrected]** The pool pinned this at **Aquincum, "Pannonia Inferior."** Both halves are anachronistic: II Adiutrix does not garrison Aquincum until about 106, and **Pannonia Inferior does not exist until Trajan divides Pannonia in 103**. In 94/95 the legion was in the Singidunum and Sirmium sector, so the journey relocates to **44.9769, 19.6094** and reuses Aquincum only for the governorship of 107.

**[A, HA 2.2 and 2.5-2.6, corrected]** Tribune of **Legio XXII Primigenia at Mogontiacum, 97-98**, not "c. 95": this is the posting during which Nerva's adoption of Trajan was announced in Germania Superior, in the **autumn of 97**, when the congratulatory embassy belongs. The pool dated it 98, but 98 is a *different* ride, carrying news of **Nerva's death** in January. Both are in the Mogontiacum campa, separated.

**[A]** Marriage to **Vibia Sabina** c. 100, arranged with Plotina's backing and, per HA, without Trajan's enthusiasm. **[A, HA 3.1]** Quaestor 101, *ab actis senatus*.

**[A, corrected]** **Tribune of the plebs 105.** The pool assigned this to the First Dacian War, which is 101-102; 105 falls in the **Second**, 105-106. **[A]** Command of **Legio I Minervia**; governor of **Pannonia Inferior** 107, with the Iazyges beaten and then negotiated with; consul 108; Athenian citizenship and **eponymous archon, 112**.

**[A, corrected]** **Governor of Syria, 117 only.** The pool gave "114-117". Hadrian held no provincial command during the Parthian war until 117, when the sitting legate went to Dacia; before that he travelled in Trajan's *comites*.

## 4. The accession (117 to 118)

**[A]** Trajan dies at **Selinus in Cilicia, 8 August 117**. **[R, disputed in antiquity]** The adoption document is signed by **Plotina**. Dio 69.1 holds that she concealed the death and produced the adoption; HA 4.8-4.10 reports the doubt without settling it. **This is the central contradiction of the life and it is left standing.**

**[A]** Acclamation **11 August 117**, Senate ratification following. **[A, HA 5]** Immediate abandonment of the trans-Euphratean conquests: Mesopotamia and Armenia given up. The reign's defining act is a subtraction, and it is the first thing he does.

**[A, HA 7]** Execution of four consulars in 118, **Lusius Quietus, Celsus, Palma, Nigrinus**, on Attianus's authority. Hadrian denied ordering it; the denial is neither provable nor refutable, and the Senate's memory of these four is why it later refuses deification. **[A, HA 6.7, 7.6]** Entry into Rome **9 July 118**; donative; the debt arrears burned in the Forum of Trajan.

## 5. The first great tour: Gaul, Germany, Britain, Spain (121 to 123)

**[A]** 121: the frontier armies of Gaul and Germania; the **basilica for Plotina at Nemausus** (HA 12.2); the discipline reforms; the first timber-and-earth phase of the Upper German limes.

**[A, HA 11.2]** 122: Britannia and the order for a wall **"to separate the Romans from the barbarians,"** the one verbatim statement of frontier doctrine from a Roman emperor. **[A, RIB 1340]** **Pons Aelius** at the Tyne. **Legend corrected:** the pool said Legio VI Victrix replaced the "vanished" Legio IX Hispana. **The Ninth did not vanish.** Officers and stamped tiles place it at Nijmegen on the Lower Rhine after about 120: it was transferred off the island, and its ultimate fate is unknown.

**[A, HA 12.3-12.5]** Winter 122/123 at **Tarraco**; the Temple of Augustus restored; a provincial council that refuses him levies; the slave who attacks him and is judged mad and handed to the doctors. **[R]** Mauretania: a rising draws him across, but **no ancient source names a site**, so the pool's conjectural Volubilis pin is not carried.

## 6. The eastern circuit and the Greek years (123 to 128)

**[R, tag corrected]** The conference with **Osroes I**: HA 12.8 says only "on the Euphrates" and **names no city**, so the pool's [A] tag on Samosata is wrong, as is its coordinate, which mixes ancient latitude with the modern town's longitude and lands about 4 km off. The journey uses **37.526, 38.53** and notes the ancient site is now under the Ataturk reservoir. **Parthamaspates was given Osrhoene**, while what Osroes got back was his captured daughter and the promise of the golden throne.

**[A, Dio 69.14]** Rebuilding of **Nicomedia** after earthquake at the emperor's own cost. **[R throughout]** The **meeting with Antinous**: no ancient source records where, when, or how. Claudiopolis comes from the boy's home town, 123/124 from the itinerary, the age from the age at death.

**[R, tag corrected]** **Hadrianutherae** in Mysia, said to be founded after a boar hunt. The city is attested by coinage, but no ancient narrative source and no year exist for the story, so the pool's [A] tag is wrong and the site is not pinned.

**[A, Dio 69.11.1]** Initiation at **Eleusis**, lower grade 124, higher 128. **[A]** Athenian constitutional reform, the tribe **Hadrianis**, the Olympieion funding, the Dionysia of March 125, and the Peloponnesian restorations at Epidaurus, Mantinea, Megara and Argos.

**Return to Italy, two corrections.** The pool dated it 126 and called the villa complete. The date is **125**: recorded at Dyrrhachium on 20 May 125, then Etna, then Rome by late spring or summer. And the **villa was not complete**: Phase I is 118-125, Phase II 125-134, with the Canopus, the Piazza d'Oro and the Antinoeion all later.

**Pantheon corrected.** The pool gave "c. 118-128" and said Hadrian "rebuilds" it. The rotunda's **brick stamps cluster in 113-125**, so it began under **Trajan** and was finished under Hadrian by about 125-128, keeping Agrippa's name on the pediment (Dio 69.7). **[A, HA 13.3]** The night ascent of **Etna** for the sunrise: one line in the biographer, no consequences, kept because it explains nothing.

## 7. Africa, the Panhellenion, the Near East (127 to 130)

**[A]** 127: Italy divided into four regions under imperial legates, unpopular with the Senate; recorded here, not pinned. **[A, CIL VIII 2532, date corrected]** The **Lambaesis** inspection of Legio III Augusta: the pool said May 128, but the inspection ran through late June and the surviving adlocutio is dated **1 July 128**. **[R, HA 22.14]** The drought broken by his arrival is anecdote, placed on this tour by Birley.

**[A]** September 128 at Eleusis for the higher grade, and the organizing of the **Panhellenion**, constituted 131/132, Athens at its head, membership by proof of Hellenic descent, its full roster known only from scattered inscriptions. **[A]** **Palmyra** takes the title Hadriane; **Gerasa** builds the arch that still stands outside the walls, winter 129/130. **[R]** **Petra** takes the honorific Hadriane, which implies favour but not a documented visit, and is not pinned.

**[A, contradiction flagged]** **Jerusalem, 130:** the decision to refound the city as **Aelia Capitolina** with a temple of Jupiter on the Temple platform. **Dio 69.12 says the colony provoked the revolt; HA gives no such cause and the ban on circumcision is offered instead.** Not reconciled. The Temple Mount pin is the atlas's canonical **31.778, 35.2354**, inherited byte-identical from `solomon` and `nabucodonosor`.

## 8. Egypt and the death of Antinous (130)

**[A, HA 14.4]** Restoration of the **tomb of Pompey at Pelusium**, with verses by the emperor; Dio preserves a line. **[A, citation corrected]** The disputation with the professors of the **Museum at Alexandria** is **HA 20.2** and Dio 69.3.1, not HA 14.4-5 as the pool cited. **[A]** The **lion hunt in the Libyan desert** with Antinous present is the earliest firm evidence of the two travelling as a pair; no coordinate is available, so it rides in the Alexandria campa.

**[A as to the fact, [R] as to everything else]** **Antinous drowns near Hermopolis.**
- **Date corrected.** No ancient source dates the drowning. The party kept the Nile festival of Osiris on **22 October 130** and the body was found shortly after. The "24 October" in the pool and in popular writing is a **modern reconstruction**. The journey pins 0130-10-22.
- **Cause: a permanent gap.** Accident, suicide, sacrifice: three ancient explanations, no adjudication, and Hadrian's own account survives only as HA's paraphrase that the boy fell into the river.

**[A]** Foundation of **Antinoopolis**, 30 October 130; deification without Senate consultation; the Antinoeia games.

**[A]** **Colossi of Memnon, 20 November 130.** Balbilla's four epigrams, dated internally by regnal year and by the day of Hathyr. **Two corrections to the quotation.** (1) **Text:** the pool spliced two sentences with a comma; "In the fifteenth year of Hadrian's reign..." starts a new sentence, so only the first is quoted. (2) **Location:** the poem is headed *In Memnonis pede sinistro*, the left **foot**; the left leg carries a different poem. The translation is from sententiaeantiquae.com with **no translator credited on the page consulted**, stated in the quote source.

## 9. The Bar Kokhba war and the last years (132 to 138)

**[A]** Dedication of the **Olympieion**, winter 131/132, after some six centuries unfinished. **[A]** Revolt under **Simon bar Kokhba**, 132; **Sextus Julius Severus** recalled from Britain; Danube troops drawn south; heavy Roman losses on the order of a legion, though the identification of that legion as XXII Deiotariana is contested and is not asserted. **[A]** Fall of **Beitar**, 135; Judaea renamed **Syria Palaestina**; Jews barred from the city. Dio's figures (985 villages, 50 fortresses, 580,000 killed) are ancient numbers, not a census.

**[A, corrected]** **Return to Rome.** The pool had him in Italy in 131. He was not: he left Egypt in 131, went through Syria and Asia, **wintered in Athens 131/132**, and was pulled into the Judaean war in summer 132. The return is **epigraphically certified only on 5 May 134**. **[A]** Death of **Sabina**, 136; adoption of **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** as Lucius Aelius Caesar, 136, and his death **1 January 138**. **[A, year corrected]** Execution of **Servianus and Pedanius Fuscus**: **137**, not 138, so it precedes the adoption of Antoninus by months. **[A]** **25 February 138:** adoption of **Antoninus**, conditional on his adopting both the younger Lucius Ceionius Commodus and Marcus Annius Verus.

**[A, HA 25]** **Death at Baiae, 10 July 138**, aged 62, after 21 years, the requests for poison and for a sword refused by his household. **[A, HA 25.7, corrected]** **Burial at Puteoli, "in villa Ciceroniana."** The pool's chronology lens said vaguely "near Baiae"; HA is specific. The body then moved to the **Gardens of Domitia** to await the tomb.

**[R, HA *Ant. Pius* 5.1-5.2]** The Senate's refusal of deification, and Antoninus forcing it by threatening to decline the throne, thereby earning **Pius**. Not independently confirmed. **[A]** **Mausoleum completed 139**, receiving the ashes of Hadrian, Sabina and Lucius Aelius.

## 10. Afterlife

**[A]** **Caracalla, 217**, is the last emperor interred in the mausoleum, which is absorbed into the Aurelian Walls as a fortress about **401**. **[R]** **410:** Alaric's Goths break in and scatter the ashes. The sack is dated 24 August 410, but the breaking of the urns comes from later accounts, not an eyewitness; nothing placed inside in 139 is known to survive. **[R]** **590:** the legend of Michael sheathing his sword at the end of a plague, which gives **Castel Sant'Angelo** its name.

**[A]** The **cult of Antinous** reaches some 70 cities and an estimated 2,000 sculptures within eight years; suppressed under Theodosius I, **391**.

**[R on the attribution]** **Animula vagula blandula** survives only inside the *Historia Augusta*, with no independent manuscript, so the attribution cannot be checked against a contemporary witness. **Quotation corrections:** the English verse rendering is credited within Magie's Loeb by footnote to **A. O'Brien-Moore**, not to Magie; the Latin follows The Latin Library, lowercase *animula*, **no commas** inside *pallidula rigida nudula*, closing exclamation mark, edition named because punctuation varies.

**[A]** **Villa Adriana:** dug under Alexander VI about 1500 and surveyed in **1550** by **Pirro Ligorio**, producing the first measured plans; Francesco Piranesi publishes a second plan in **1781**; UNESCO inscription **1999**. **[A]** **Hadrian's Wall:** **William Camden** visits 1599 and writes the first coherent antiquarian account in *Britannia*; **John Clayton** begins buying land in **1834** to stop stone-robbing and by 1890 owns five forts and most of a twenty-mile stretch. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's Chesters pin sits about 6 km west of the fort; Cilurnum is **55.0275, -2.1376**.

## Contradictions carried into the journey, not adjudicated

1. **Birthplace.** HA says Rome; modern consensus says Italica, by inference from family origin rather than from a competing ancient source.
2. **The adoption of 117.** Dio says Plotina produced it after Trajan was dead; HA reports the doubt. Nothing decides it.
3. **The cause of the Bar Kokhba revolt.** Dio: the colony of Aelia Capitolina. Other tradition: the ban on circumcision.
4. **The death of Antinous.** Accident, suicide, sacrifice, with the emperor's own account lost.
5. **The four consulars of 118.** Hadrian blamed Attianus; the Senate did not believe him. Likewise the poison rumour at Sabina's death in 136, recorded by HA and not endorsed.
6. **The death-place villa.** "At Baiae" against "near Baiae"; only the burial site, Puteoli in villa Ciceroniana, is precise.
7. **The fate of Legio IX Hispana.** On the Lower Rhine by c. 120, then unrecorded.

## Gaps, stated as gaps

- **Domitia Paulina's death is undated.** Not "shortly after her husband's": undated.
- **Hadrian's autobiography is lost**, so everything about his interior life is at second hand.
- **The meeting with Antinous has no attested place, date, or circumstance.**
- **No site is named for the Mauretanian campaign of 122/123**, and none for the Euphrates conference. Volubilis is not pinned; Samosata is pinned and flagged as a guess.
- **Hadrian's own verses at the Colossus of Memnon are lost.** We know of them only because Balbilla mentions them.
