# Mansa Musa (Musa I of Mali): research report
*2026-08-30, for `mansa_musa.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition · **[C]** contradiction, not adjudicated · **[G]** gap.

**The governing fact.** Nothing survives that Mansa Musa wrote **[A: no text, decree, letter, coin legend or inscription is attributed to him in any source checked; the dossier's line reads "None securely known."]** Every sentence quoted as his words comes through Egyptian intermediaries: he spoke in Cairo in 1324 to Ibn Amir Hajib, whose recollections al-Umari wrote up a decade later. There is no Malian archive and no chronicle of the reign. The biography of the richest man of the Middle Ages is, evidentially, the visitor file kept by the country he passed through.

---

## 1. Origins and accession (c. 1280 to c. 1312)

**Birth and descent. [R/C]** Year and place both unestablished; the universal "c. 1280" is modern arithmetic run backward from a disputed death year and a reported reign length **[R: Wikipedia, 'Mansa Musa']**. The file pins it at **Kangaba (11.9333, -8.4167)**, declared a stand-in for the Keita heartland. Ibn Khaldun calls him **Musa ibn Abi Bakr** and stops there; the pedigree in the modern books (Abu Bakr, Faga Leye, Musa) is a reconstruction over that one patronymic. **[C: Fauvelle-Aymar argues instead for descent through Sunjata's sister Kolonkan. Not adjudicated.]**

**Kirina. [A]** The dynastic founding battle, c. 1235. The dossier coordinate was wrong by roughly 60 km and its administrative label wrong too; corrected against `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kirina` to **12.3063, -8.1516**, Koulikoro Region, not Kati Cercle. It gets **no stop**: Sunjata's battle, a generation before Musa's birth, it is context inside the birth campa only.

**The Atlantic fleets. [R]** The predecessor sent 200 vessels west; one returned, its captain reporting a current in the open sea that swallowed the rest. The king then led some 2,000 himself, appointing Musa deputy, and never came back **[R: al-Umari via Ibn Amir Hajib; three quotations verified in the translation at Wikipedia, 'Abu Bakr II']**. Cautions: "Abu Bakr II" is not a medieval name; no departure point is named anywhere, so the pin (16.0179, -16.4896) is declared geographic convention; **[G]** no archaeology corroborates a landfall. The "discovered America" extrapolation appears nowhere.

**Accession. [R/C]** Conventionally 1312, worked backward from a death year plus Ibn Khaldun's twenty-five-year reign figure; a death in 1332 would put it near 1307. Both stated, neither resolved.

**Kanku's death. [R]** The horse accident and the remorse motive come from the seventeenth-century *Ta'rikh* tradition, three centuries later, and from no near-contemporary source. Carried as a stop labelled legend.

## 2. The economic base

**Bure. [A]** Attested medieval goldfield of the upper Niger **[A: Levtzion, *Ancient Ghana and Mali*; Insoll, *The Archaeology of Islam in Sub-Saharan Africa*]**. The crown did not own the mines; revenue came as tax and tribute, not production.

**Bambuk. [R, downgraded from the dossier's [A]]** `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambouk`: by the eleventh and twelfth centuries "wasteful methods reduced the productivity of the goldfields", two centuries before Musa, so calling it a live pillar of his bullion overstates the case. Coordinate corrected from the dossier's fringe point (12.85, -11.23) to **13.3, -11.7**.

**Tadmekka. [R]** Glass-assisted gold refining is documented archaeologically for the period. The dossier coordinate (18.43, 1.33) is Kidal town, 40 km southeast of the site; corrected to **18.75, 1.175** per `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essouk`, which does not mention the technique, so **[G]** it needs Nixon's reports directly.

## 3. The hajj outbound (1324)

**Scale. [A for the year; [R] for every number.]** The figures are not counts: `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansa_Musa` gives "upwards of 12,000 slaves" and 80 camels bearing gold, calling such numbers **"rhetorical rather than statistical."** The dossier's composite of "roughly 60,000 attendants" and "dozens to a hundred camels" is **misattributed**, coming from other chronicle traditions; the file uses 12,000 and 80 only.

**Route. [R throughout.]** No member of the caravan left an itinerary; Mema, Walata, Taghaza, Tuwat, perhaps Ghadames, then Giza is trade-route reconstruction, and each stop says so. **Walata**: the route "began at Sijilmasa and passed through Taghaza with its salt mines and ended at Oualata" is verbatim from `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oualata`, as is Mauny's 2,000 to 3,000 inhabitants, but the dossier's "recently overtaken Aoudaghost" is **wrong**, the article dating that replacement to "the beginning of the thirteenth century". Pin from `ibn_battuta.journey.json`: **17.3167, -7.0333**. **Taghaza**: salt-block construction and the fourfold southbound price rise are attested from Ibn Battuta a generation later; pin from `leo_africanus.journey.json`, **23.6, -5.0**. **Ghadames**: the dossier's own "perhaps", kept with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

**Giza and the entry. [A]** "Camped for three days by the Pyramids of Giza before crossing the Nile into Cairo on 19 July" 1324, i.e. 26 Rajab 724 AH.

## 4. Cairo (July to October 1324)

**Coordinate convention.** The dossier mixed Citadel and city-centre pins for one city. The file uses the **canonical Cairo pin 30.0444, 31.2357** everywhere except the audience with the sultan, which uses the **Citadel, 30.0287, 31.2599**, and says so.

**The prostration. [A, on a broken citation.]** Ordered to kiss the ground before al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun, he refused, then resolved it by prostrating while declaring the obeisance was to God. **The dossier's source is dead**: the `bu.edu/africa` al-Umari PDF returns HTTP 404, and neither "Kiss the ground!" nor "I make obeisance to God who created me!" could be verified there. The substance is standard al-Umari and near-certainly right; the stop's `quote_source` says outright that the excerpt is a dead link needing a check against **Levtzion and Hopkins**. **[C/G: flagged, not laundered.]**

**The gold price. [A for the fact; [C] for the strong version; fabrication risk flagged.]** Al-Umari: a mithqal stood at 25 silver dirhams before his arrival and generally above, fell below 22 after, and did not recover for at least twelve years. **This must never be set in quotation marks.** Wikipedia renders it as its own running prose, not translated text, footnote 109 resolving to **Levtzion & Hopkins 2000, p. 271**; presenting these words as a sentence of al-Umari is fabricated attribution. In the file they appear twice as indirect statement, never as a quote. Schultz's work on Mamluk exchange rates accepts a short-term shock and rejects the twelve-year single-cause version; both are stated. The second bu.edu quotation, "This man flooded Cairo with his benefactions," carries the same dead-link caveat.

**Twenty-four cities. [A, a softening reversed.]** Wikipedia verbatim: "Musa said that he had **conquered** 24 cities and their surrounding districts." The dossier softened this to "ruled over twenty-four subordinate kings/cities", turning conquest into tributary administration. Restored.

**The purchases. [A]** Al-Maqrizi: "the members of his entourage proceeded to buy Turkish and Ethiopian slave girls, singing girls and garments, so that the rate of the gold dinar fell by six dirhams" **[A: Wikipedia, 'Mali Empire']**. Given its own stop and stated flat, softened nowhere.

**"Not even for kings?" [R, citation corrected.]** An Egyptian official told Musa it was unlawful to take an unmarried subject's daughter to his bed; he asked "Not even for kings?", was told no, and reportedly stopped. **The dossier's citation is wrong**: the episode is not in `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansa_Musa` and must be cited to al-Umari via Ibn Amir Hajib in **Levtzion & Hopkins**, which the stop now does. It is at once a record of institutionalised royal sexual coercion and Egyptian religious-didactic literature; the campa says both.

**Physical description. [A, near-duplicate flagged.]** Three notices: Ibn Kathir ("He was a handsome young man"), Ibn Habib al-Halabi, and al-Maqrizi. The last two are near-identical and **may reflect one garbled transmission rather than two chroniclers**; flagged, not resolved, with only Ibn Habib's wording used as a `quote`. The dossier's al-Maqrizi attribution ("Djibril Tamsir/Joseph Ki-Zerbo") **conflates two scholars**: the "Zerbo, 59" short-cite resolves to **Ki-Zerbo, J. (ed.), UNESCO *General History of Africa* vol. IV, Abridged Edition (1998), p. 59**, and with blackpast.org now 403 the apparent two witnesses reduce to one page of one book.

## 5. The Hijaz (late 1324 to 1325)

**Departure and the Sinai leg. [A for the date, R for the route.]** Out of Cairo on 28 Shawwal 724 AH, 18 October 1324. The dossier's 29.576, 34.5136 points at empty interior Sinai 45 km WNW of Aqaba, on no named site and no stage of the Darb al-Hajj al-Misri; no source places Musa on this leg, so the file uses **Aqaba, 29.527, 35.008**.

**Mecca and Medina. [A in outline, [R] in detail.]** He performed the rites and gave alms; Medina is named among the pilgrimage cities, but order and dates within the season are unfixed. **Hajj dates in the file are calculated, not attested**: 9 Dhu al-Hijja 724 falls around 28 November 1324, counted from the attested 28 Shawwal. **Arafat is explicitly presumed**; **Mina was dropped** as padding. The brawl between Malian and Turkic pilgrims in the sanctuary is attested **[A]**; the four sharifs said to have returned with him are later tradition **[R]**, kept inside the Mecca campa.

**Al-Sahili. [A]** Abu Ishaq Ibrahim al-Sahili of Granada, b. c. 1290, poet, jurist and secretary, met Musa on the pilgrimage and returned with him **[A: `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ishaq_al-Sahili`]**. He died at Timbuktu on 15 October 1346. **His death is not a stop here**: nine years after Musa's, and his own event, it appears only inside the recruitment campa.

## 6. The return and the debt (1325)

**Suez. [A/G]** A setback with pilgrim deaths and lost supplies; no cause, numbers or names.

**Cairo, second passage. [A, attribution corrected.]** Sales at a loss, borrowing at high interest, and about 50,000 dinars from the **Banu al-Kuwayk**. The dossier credited this to "al-Umari and Ibn Khaldun" jointly; Wikipedia verbatim: "**Ibn Khaldun** records that the Banu l-Kuwaykh, or his family, were among those who loaned money to Musa, in this case 50,000 dinars." Repayment is disputed and left so; Ibn al-Dawadari, *Kanz al-durar*, vol. 9, supplies the half-price sales.

**Gao. [C, the worst-supported episode in the file, kept as such.]** The dossier had Musa receiving Ali Kolon and Sulayman Nar as hostages after his commander Saghmanja took Gao. Three problems, all in the stop's `date_confidence`: the two names are **not in Ibn Khaldun** but in the seventeenth-century *Ta'rikh* lineage, three centuries later; `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao` calls Ali Kulun "the first ruler of the Sunni dynasty", who "rebelled against the Malian hegemony", a **rebel, not a hostage**; and it dates the loss of Gao's independence to "towards the end of the 13th century", making a conquest by Musa in 1325 impossible.

A fourteenth-century **Kankou Moussa Mosque** at Gao exists, distinct from the later Askia tomb-mosque **[A for the building, R for the attribution: no contemporary inscription ties it to Musa]**.

## 7. Building, and its myths

**Djinguereber. [R]** Attributed to Musa in 1325, built 1327, rebuilt many times **[A for attribution and dates at `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djinguereber_Mosque`; R for a personal commission]**. **The al-Sahili-as-architect story does not survive Hunwick**, who found him firmly attested only for a royal audience chamber: domed, plastered, coloured arabesques, windows possibly glazed, 12,000 mithqals (about 51 kg) of gold **[A: al-Umari]**. **An inherited contradiction, resolved explicitly**: the dossier pinned that chamber to Niani while calling the capital unresolved. The file keeps the pin and declares pin and date alike placeholders; the "1330" is unsourced.

**Niani as capital. [C]** The dossier credited Fauvelle-Aymar with a finding that Niani lacks a fourteenth-century occupation horizon. **That attribution does not hold as cited**: `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansa_Musa` cites him only on succession and never mentions Niani, and `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niani,_Guinea` reports that the 1965 and 1968 Guinean-Polish missions **did** find the Arab quarter and royal town, house foundations, a mihrab, royal walls. The argument against Niani exists in the scholarship (Fauvelle-Aymar, Conrad on Dakajalan) but must be cited there directly and never stated as "excavation found nothing."

**Sankore. [R, myth corrected.]** `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankore_Madrasah` **does not mention Musa at all**, crediting early funding to "a Tuareg woman of the Aghlal tribe" and the flourishing to the later Songhay Askias. Musa founded neither Timbuktu, which predates him, nor a university there. **[A]** He did recover the city from a Mossi occupation and order a rampart and stone fort; 1330 is conventional, not fixed.

## 8. Death and afterlife

**Death. [C/G]** Most commonly **1337** (Levtzion), with **c. 1332** seriously argued against it. Place, cause and month unknown, **and no tomb has ever been identified**, checked against National Geographic Education and Wikipedia. An attested gap, not a failure of searching. The death stop carries the Niani pin and declares it a placeholder for the royal seat.

**Tlemcen. [A]** Musa congratulated the Marinid Abu al-Hasan Ali on the capture of Tlemcen (May 1337); the reply arrived to find a successor on the throne. The strongest evidence for a death near 1337, inside an Ibn Khaldun passage that elsewhere confuses son with brother.

**Sulayman's reign. [A, figure corrected.]** The dossier gave "roughly c. 1341 to c. 1360". Wikipedia gives **"c. 1340 to c. 1359"**, noting only that some scholars argue 1341 to 1360; the dossier quoted the minority variant as the article's figure.

**The Catalan Atlas. [A, coordinate corrected.]** BnF **MS Espagnol 30, sheet 6**, conventionally 1375 (some scholars now late 1370s or early 1380s), attributed to Cresques Abraham; "Musse Melly" crowned with a gold orb, the caption used verbatim as the stop's quote. The dossier's 48.8322, 2.3763 is the **Tolbiac site**; the manuscript sits with the Département des Manuscrits at **Richelieu, approx. 48.8686, 2.3376**, which the file uses. Dulcert (1339) and Russo (1525) are named on Wikipedia's authority; **[G]** neither was inspected.

**Fajigi. [R, half-confirmed.]** Wikipedia verbatim: "Some aspects of Musa appear to have been incorporated into a figure in Mandé oral tradition known as Fajigi, which translates as father of hope." The merger with **Fakoli**, and the claim that Musa's name became near-synonymous with pilgrimage, **could not be confirmed** and are asserted nowhere.

**Timbuktu 2012. [A, two corrections.]** From about 30 June to 10 July 2012 Ansar Dine attacked ten monuments, destroying nine mausoleums and damaging the Sidi Yahya mosque. The dossier's note that Djinguereber was untouched **overshoots**: two tombs there were destroyed on 1 July with "hoes, pick-axes and chisels", damage to the fabric minimal. The danger-list history was also incomplete: Timbuktu was **listed 1990 to 2005**, removed, **re-listed 2012**. **The ICC trial of al-Faqi al-Mahdi is not a stop**: The Hague is his story, appearing only as a clause and a `suggested_ref`.

**"$400 billion." [R]** A Celebrity Net Worth estimate, confirmed at source, measured against Elon Musk's reported September 2021 net worth. No price basket, no inventory, no audited figure behind it.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Mansa Musa is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are licensed and none were written. One relation was kept rather than deleted, and is flagged here for a ruling.

**Direction caught: backward.** The final segment's stop *"The royal seat, the standard he set"* (1352) rests on a judgment made about Musa after his death by a man who never met him: Ibn Battuta, reaching Mali under Mansa Sulayman about fifteen years later, wrote that Sulayman was "a miserly king from whom no great donation is to be expected", a sentence that only signifies because Musa's generosity had become the standard of comparison. Under the block rule this is an earlier file embedding a later artifact, and the operator may read it either way.

It was kept because the relation is **one-directional and real**, the licensed case in the brief; because **no scene of Ibn Battuta's journey is staged here**, his arrival, reception and Niger travel all staying on `ibn_battuta.journey.json`; and because the corpus already treats posthumous reception this way (`leo_africanus.journey.json` carries stops for Ramusio, Pory and Maalouf).

**Relations found and deliberately dropped, so nothing is lost silently:** **`leo_africanus`**, three points of contact from the interlock lens (Timbuktu c. 1510, Taghaza, Gao), all **shared geography only**, since Leo does not write about Musa; he is named nowhere and only his pins are inherited. **`ahmad_al_qalqashandi`**, whose *Subh al-A'sha* compiling al-Umari is the researcher's inference and is not stated in that file, which never mentions Musa or Mali. **Askia Muhammad's 1497-98 hajj as a following of Musa's precedent**: plausible, standard, **unconfirmed in this pass**, asserted nowhere.

No forward reference of any kind was written.
