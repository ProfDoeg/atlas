# Max Glücksmann (1875-1946): research report

*Slug `max_glucksmann`. Compiled 2026-08-14 from the research pool, corrections carried through rather than smoothed over.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, single-source testimony, or inference. Contradictions are flagged in place and listed again below; none is adjudicated.

**Name.** Born Mordechai David Glücksmann; in Argentina Max Glücksmann, and in Spanish sources often *Glucksmann* or *Glucksman* without the umlaut, Wikipedia titles included. The house he traded under appears as *Casa Max Glucksmann* in trade press of 1915.

---

## 1. Czernowitz and the crossing (1875-1890)

- Born **8 March 1875 in Czernowitz**, capital of Austrian Bukovina, Austro-Hungarian Empire, today Chernivtsi, Ukraine. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES, 'Max Glücksmann']**
- City context (Jews near a third of the population; German, Yiddish, Romanian and Ruthenian in common use) is **[A: Wikipedia, 'Chernivtsi']**; nothing in the record connects the family to any trade he later entered. **Gap:** parents, siblings, schooling and religious formation are entirely absent from every source consulted.
- **Emigrates to Argentina in 1890, arriving at Buenos Aires** at fifteen. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**. **Gap / [R]:** no source names the ship, the port of embarkation, the route, or whether he travelled alone; the wharf coordinate (-34.6021, -58.3676) is a placement on the period port, not an attested berth.

## 2. Casa Lepage (c. 1891-1908)

- **Photographic assistant at Casa Lepage, Avenida Bolívar 375**, the shop of Enrique Lepage, the first house in Argentina importing filming and projection apparatus. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**
- **Contradiction, flagged:** ES dates the start of the job to 1890 while stating in the same sentence that Lepage "se había instalado en Buenos Aires en 1891"; EN says only "shortly after his arrival". The file uses 1891-01-01 with the contradiction in `date_confidence`; treat the year as **c. 1891 [R]**, the employment as **[A]**.
- **Eugène (Eugenio) Py works the same rooms**, for eighteen years by the corpus's account. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES; `eugene_py.journey.json`]**. The strongest single interlock.
- **First cinematograph screening in Argentina, Teatro Odeón, July 1896**, with Glücksmann present alongside Lepage and Py. **[A]** as to the event and his presence; **date disputed**: ES gives **18 July 1896**, EN 'Cinema of Argentina' and `eugene_py.journey.json` **28 July 1896**. Not resolved; the file uses 1896-07-28 and prints both.
- **The Lumière negotiation fails.** ES states plainly that Glücksmann, Py and Lepage approached the Lumière brothers to buy the apparatus "pero no prosperaron las conversaciones". **[A]**. The pool's claim that Lumière equipment reached Argentina through Lepage's store is therefore **struck**.
- **Import of a Cronofotógrafo Elgé (Gaumont-Demeny), 1897.** **[A: Wikipedia ES]**
- **Import of a Cinematógrafo Pathé**, distributed via the Compañía General de Fonógrafos, Cinematógrafos y Aparatos de Precisión. **[R: undated in the source]**. The file places it at 1898-01-01 as "after the 1897 Gaumont order" and says so.
- **Py shoots 'La bandera argentina' (1897)**, the flag over the Plaza de Mayo, roughly 17 m of film, with the Gaumont camera that arrived that year; generally counted the first Argentine film. **[A: Wikipedia EN 'Cinema of Argentina'; ES 'Cine de Argentina']**. Glücksmann's role is that of the man who ordered and handled the equipment, not the director.
- **Salón Nacional, Maipú 471, opens 1900**, Argentina's first dedicated film hall. **[A: EN 'Cinema of Argentina'; `eugene_py.journey.json`]**. It is the hall later commentary forgets when calling the Grand Splendid the first cinema in Argentina.
- **1902: Glücksmann sends Py and the journalist Enrique Casellas over the Andes** to film the Argentina-Chile boundary arbitration before Sir Thomas Holdich, commissioned by Edward VII. **[A: quoted in `eugene_py.journey.json`]**. The earliest attested instance of his commissioning production rather than supplying equipment, six years before he owns the firm.
- **Debate over Argentina's first narrative film** (Gallo's 'El fusilamiento de Dorrego', 1908 or 1910, vs 'La Revolución de Mayo', 1909) is **[R]** context only; no source ties Glücksmann to either. Not made into a stop.
- **Frederico Figner's kinetoscopes, 18 September 1894** **[A: EN 'Cinema of Argentina']**: pre-history of the trade, dropped from the journey file for lack of attested Glücksmann involvement.

## 3. Records, and the purchase of the firm (1903-1914)

- **Odeón founded in Berlin by Max Straus and Heinrich Zuntz** of the International Talking Machine Company. **Contradiction, flagged:** EN 'Odeon Records' dates the label **1903**, the Glücksmann articles **1904** (more plausibly his agency date). The file uses 1903-01-01 and prints both.
- **Glücksmann becomes Odeón's Argentine importing agent, 1904.** **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**
- **No source attests Glücksmann in Berlin**, or a meeting with Straus or Zuntz; the relationship is contractual. **Gap:** no evidence of *any* transatlantic travel after 1890.
- **Founds Discos Glücksmann**, with Odeón building him a pressing and processing plant in Buenos Aires. **[A: Wikipedia EN]**, but dated only to "the 1900s"; the journey file places it at 1905-01-01 and says so.
- **Pays royalties rather than flat session fees**, unprecedented in Argentina. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**
- **Buys the Casa Lepage firm outright in 1908** after Lepage's departure. **[A: Wikipedia ES; `eugene_py.journey.json`]**. The corpus file calls the seller "Henri Lepage", Wikipedia "Enrique Lepage": almost certainly the same man, variance recorded not harmonised.
- **Expansion into the Argentine interior, Uruguay, Paraguay and Chile.** **[A]** as to the four countries. **Contradiction, flagged and not resolved:** ES 'Max Glucksmann' says **100 halls**; ES 'Cine de Argentina' says **more than sixty**; EN gives **no figure**. The journey file never states a single number. **Gap:** not one hall is named anywhere; the interior stop is pinned at Argentina's geographic centre as a declared placeholder.
- **By 1914 he has effectively cornered the Argentine recording industry**, marginalising his only competitor, Victor, through exclusive long-term contracts. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**
- **Struck claim:** ES Wikipedia states "En 1914, la revista Fortune incluyó su nombre...". That cannot be true. *Fortune* was founded by Henry Luce in 1929 and first published February 1930 (EN 'Fortune (magazine)'). The campa states the impossibility rather than omitting it, because the error is in circulation.

## 4. The screen and the voice (1914-1920)

- **'Amalia' premieres at the Teatro Colón, 12 December 1914**, with the President of Argentina present; Glücksmann produced. **[A: EN 'Cinema of Argentina']**. **Gap:** the pool gives no Teatro Colón coordinate, so the stop inherits the canonical Buenos Aires pin.
- **'Nobleza gaucha' (1915)**, unprecedented commercial success, in theatres for over two decades. **[A: Wikipedia EN]** as a fact about Argentine cinema, but **the pool never ties Glücksmann to it**. Deliberately **not** made into a stop.
- **Casa Max Glucksmann shoots and develops a Chilean government film for the 1915 San Francisco exposition.** **[A: contemporary trade press]**, the best primary document in the pool: *Cine Gaceta*, Prospecto Gratuito, July 1915, 'Películas Nacionales' item 1, quoted at p. 145 n. 18 of Santa Cruz (2004; full citation below). **Correction carried:** the printed source reads "no podía **hacerse** representar"; the corrected text is in the journey file, which also notes that the house **shot** the film as well as developing it.
  - *Citation caveat:* the journal's OJS metadata lists the article under a different title ('Los orígenes de la cultura de masas en Chile: las revistas de cine (1910-1920)'); the printed title page reads as cited.
- **Gardel and Razzano signed to exclusive contract, 1917**, with Corsini, Firpo and Canaro across the same years. **[A: Wikipedia ES]**
- **'Mi noche triste' (1917), c. 10,000 copies.** **[A]** as to record and sales figure (EN 'Carlos Gardel'); **[R]** as to the label, which the article does not name. The Glücksmann link is inference from the same year's contracts, labelled as such.
- **Grand Splendid, Avenida Santa Fe 1860.** **Contradiction, flagged:** the pool's chronology dated the commission **1919**; EN/ES 'El Ateneo Grand Splendid' say construction was **initiated in 1917**, 1919 being the year of the dome and the opening. The file splits them into two stops.
  - Architects **Peró and Torres Armengol**; builders **Pizoney and Falcope**; site of the former Teatro Nacional Norte and a carriage works. **[A: Wikipedia ES]**
  - Ceiling frescoes by **Nazareno Orlandi**; caryatids and cornice work by **Troiano Troiani**. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES; corroborated by `nazareno_orlandi.journey.json` and `troiano_troiani.journey.json`]**
  - **Inaugurated 14 May 1919**, in the district then nicknamed "el Saint Germain porteño". **[A]**
  - **Capacity: contradiction, flagged.** "Initially seating 1,050 patrons" (ES 'Max Glucksmann') vs "four tiers of boxes and orchestra seating for 500" (ES 'Ateneo Grand Splendid'). Possibly compatible, stalls versus total, but no source reconciles them; the file prints both.
- **Gardel begins recording for Odeón in a studio Glücksmann installed inside the Grand Splendid, from 1920.** **[A: Wikipedia ES, 'Ateneo Grand Splendid']**

## 5. Radio, contests, factory, crash (1923-1931)

- **Radio Splendid begins broadcasting from inside the theatre, 23 May 1923.** **[A: Wikipedia ES]**
- **Concurso del Disco Nacional**: first edition **1924**, **seven** editions in Buenos Aires through **1930**, plus **two** in Montevideo (**1927**, **1928**). **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**. **Gap:** not one venue is named for any of the nine editions.
- **Factory for "The Arg. Talking Mach. Works", Montañeses 2130/50, completed November 1928**, architect **Paul Ploetz**, **1,690 m²** covered, in what is now the Barrio Chino of Belgrano; "the building does not exist anymore". **[A: Wikipedia EN]**. **Correction carried:** the pool's coordinate was ~800 m off; the file uses the OSM geocode (-34.5571, -58.4493). **Gap, narrowed:** the site is recorded, the demolition date and cause are not.
- **First sound films in Argentina shown at the Grand Splendid, 1929.** **[A: Wikipedia EN]**
- **Carlos Gardel's radio debut from Radio Splendid, 1 October 1929.** **[A: Wikipedia ES]**
- **The 1929 crash forces the sale of the cinema circuit**; he concentrates on records. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**. The file dates the sale 1929-11-01, a declared placement after the October collapse.
- **1931: EMI formed in Britain; the Argentine operation is reorganised as Disco Nacional-Odeón.** **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**

## 6. Community, death, burial

- **Synagogue on Calle Libertad, funded 1925-1942.** **Downgraded to [R]**. ES attributes the claim to family testimony ("tal como afirma uno de sus nietos"), cited to Clarín, 4 December 2000. ES 'Templo Libertad' (CIRA, Libertad 785) credits **Baron Hirsch** for the 1889 land purchase, dates the cornerstone to **27 September 1897** and the rebuild to **1932**, and **never mentions Glücksmann**. Which building on Libertad is meant is not settled. Both accounts stand in the campa. Coordinate corrected to -34.5994, -58.3836.
- **Dies 20 October 1946 in Buenos Aires, aged 71, of a heart attack**, having directed the record business to the end. **[A: Wikipedia EN/ES]**. **Gap:** the address of death, home or hospital, is unrecorded.
- **Buried at the Cementerio Israelita de Ciudadela**, partido de Tres de Febrero. **[A]** on a single source (Wikipedia EN), **[R]** in practice, unchecked against the cemetery's registers. Coordinate corrected to the OSM geocode (-34.6202, -58.5492; Av. Marcelo T. de Alvear 307, Villa Santamarina), ~2.5 km from the pool's figure. The burial *date* in the file is a placement, not an attestation. **Gap:** no source describes the tomb, whether it is marked, maintained or visited.

## 7. Afterlife

- **1944 (after the coup of 1943): the state expropriates his film archive**, 20,000 m of positive and 9,000 m of negative, for **2,600 pesos**; the Supreme Court later values it at **155,000 pesos**, by which time the material is dispersed or lost, some possibly surviving in the Archivo General de la Nación, itself burned around 1950. **[A: Claudio España, *Crónica de la destrucción del cine argentino* (1998), quoted in Izquierdo 2013]**. It appears in neither Wikipedia article.
- **1978: Couselo on the loss.** **[A]**, correction carried: the printed text reads "**contratiparse**", not "contratarse"; *contratipar* is the term for striking a duplicate negative, so the sentence says the material burned because **no dupes were struck**. Verified by text extraction and page image. Citation: J. M. Couselo, "¿Dónde están las viejas películas argentinas?", *Clarín: Espectáculos*, 5 February 1978, p. 1.
- **The building**: theatre and cinema until **1982**, cinema alone until **1999**, then closed. Leased by **Grupo Ilhsa from February 2000**, converted by **Fernando Manzone** for three million dollars with dome, balconies and stage curtain kept, opened as **El Ateneo Grand Splendid on 4 December 2000**, 120,000 titles over 4,000 m². *The Guardian*, second most beautiful bookshop in the world (2008); *National Geographic*, most beautiful (2019). **[A: Wikipedia ES/EN]**
- **Commemorative plaque on the Casa Lepage building, Avenida Bolívar**, placed by the city government. **[A: Wikipedia ES]**, **no installation date given**; the journey file uses 2000-01-01 as an openly declared placeholder.
- **No monument, statue or street named for Glücksmann** was found. **[R: argument from absence]**, and a weak one: AMIA and CIRA archives and the Spanish-language press could not be checked this session.

## Contradictions carried, not resolved

1. **First Odeón screening**: 18 July 1896 (ES 'Max Glucksmann') vs 28 July 1896 (EN 'Cinema of Argentina', `eugene_py.journey.json`).
2. **Start of the Casa Lepage job**: 1890 vs c. 1891, inside a single ES sentence.
3. **Odeón label founding**: 1903 (EN 'Odeon Records') vs 1904 (the Glücksmann articles).
4. **Hall count**: 100 vs "más de sesenta" vs no figure at all.
5. **Grand Splendid commission**: 1917 construction start vs 1919 (the pool's chronology).
6. **Grand Splendid capacity**: 1,050 seats vs four tiers of boxes plus 500 in the orchestra.
7. **Calle Libertad synagogue**: a grandson's testimony via Clarín (2000) vs Templo Libertad's own history.
8. **Casa Lepage coordinates**: -34.6117/-58.3731 (`eugene_py.journey.json`, inherited here) vs -34.6132/-58.3733 (geography lens) vs -34.61219/-58.37339 (OSM geocode for Bolívar 375) vs -34.6091/-58.3722 (afterlife lens, which lands near Plaza de Mayo).
9. **Teatro Odeón coordinates**: -34.5989/-58.3753 (`eugene_py.journey.json`, the same pin that file gives the Salón Nacional) vs -34.60361/-58.37750 (ES 'Teatro Odeón'). The file takes the ES figure for the Odeón and keeps the corpus pin for the Salón Nacional: a deliberate departure from byte-identical inheritance.
10. **"Henri Lepage" vs "Enrique Lepage"** for the seller of the firm in 1908.

## Claims struck outright

- **Lumière equipment reaching Argentina through Casa Lepage.** ES says the Lumière negotiations failed, which is why they went to Gaumont-Demeny and Pathé.
- **Fortune magazine listing Glücksmann's fortune in 1914.** The magazine did not exist until 1929/1930. The error is ES Wikipedia's, and is named as an error in the journey file rather than quietly dropped.

## Gaps (stated as gaps)

- Family of origin: parents, siblings, education, religious formation in Czernowitz. Nothing.
- The emigration itself: ship, route, port of embarkation, travelling companions. Nothing.
- Marriage and children: no source names a wife or a child, though a **grandson** is cited for the synagogue claim, which entails descendants the record never otherwise mentions.
- Not a single one of the sixty-to-a-hundred cinema halls is named, in any of the four countries.
- No venue is named for any of the nine record contests.
- Demolition date and circumstances of the Montañeses plant.
- Physical state of the grave.
- Any evidence of travel by Glücksmann outside Argentina after 1890. The Berlin, Santiago, Montevideo and Asunción stops are about business conducted at a distance, and say so.

## Interlocks used in the journey file

Named in a campa only where a direct relation to Glücksmann is attested:

- **`eugene_py`**: colleague at Casa Lepage for eighteen years, his employee after the 1908 purchase, and the operator he sent over the Andes in 1902; named in four campas.
- **`nazareno_orlandi`**: painted the dome of the theatre Glücksmann commissioned; named in two campas.
- **`troiano_troiani`**: supplied the sculptural ensemble for the same commission; named in one campa.

Considered and **rejected** as name-drops, since shared city and shared decade are not a relation: `mario_palanti`, `alberto_gerchunoff`, `victoria_ocampo`, `borges`. Federico Figner shares the Austro-Hungarian origin and the phonograph trade and is Py's rival in the corpus, but no source has the two meeting and he has no journey file; his 1894 kinetoscope stop was cut.
