# Paul the Deacon: research report

*Slug: `paul_the_deacon`. 2026-08-27, from the verified research pool. Life: c. 725 to 13 April, probably 799.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or argument from silence. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**; unreachable sources are named with the reason. Paul is his own principal source for his family and his nation, and a poem written after his death by somebody else is the principal source for him. The two standard modern accounts, Capo's DBI entry and the English Wikipedia article, disagree about the **order of his life**, not merely about dates.

---

## 1. Friuli and the family (c. 725 to c. 744)

- Born at **Cividale del Friuli**, ancient **Forum Iulii**, son of **Warnefrid** and **Theodelinda**. **[A: Capo; en.wikipedia]** Birth year: Capo "probably between 720 and 730", Wikipedia "about 720" **[R]**; the file uses 725 as a stated midpoint ordering device.
- **"Winfrid" is disputed.** Wikipedia: "born Winfrid, son of Warnefrid". Capo: *Warnefridi* is better read as a **patronymic**, with no personal birth name securely established. **Contradiction, flagged.**
- **Descent from Leupchis**, who entered Italy with Alboin in 568/569, is Paul's own report in **HL IV.37**, on his elders' authority. **[A as Paul's claim; R as event.]**
- **The sister at the Tempietto Longobardo.** The sister is **[A]**, from the petition poem: vowed to Christ from girlhood, nearly blind from weeping. Her identification with Santa Maria in Valle is **[R]** and weak: it.wikipedia dates the building mid-eighth century, credits **Astolfo and Giseltrude**, and never mentions Paul.
- **The Aquileia education is very probably false, not merely unproven.** Popular lives credit **Patriarch Calixtus** with directing Paul's first studies; Capo finds no proof, and en.wikipedia dates Calixtus **726 to 734**, so a child born c. 720-730 was at most nine when he died. **[R, chronologically near-impossible.]** Kept as a stop *about a false tradition*, stated as such.

## 2. The palace school at Pavia (c. 744 to c. 774)

- Moved into the **Lombard royal palace** milieu, probably under **King Ratchis** (r. 744-749) **[R: Capo, corroborated by Wikipedia]**; studied grammar under **Flavian** **[A as to fact, but the sole witness is Ilderic's epitaph, a generation after Paul's death]**.
- **The Greek joke belongs at Pavia, not boyhood Cividale.** The Latin, confirmed at thelatinlibrary.com: *"Tres aut quattuor in scolis quas didici syllabas"*, three or four syllables learned **in the schools**. **[A]** Place and wording both corrected.
- **Desiderius.** Later tradition makes Paul secretary or chancellor; **no appointment document survives** and Paul claims no title. **[R as to office, A as to milieu.]**
- **Lake Como.** Attested: the poem *Versus in laudem Larii* **[A]**. One Wikipedia sentence turns this into a residence; Capo knows of no such house. **[R, single lower-tier source.]**

**The ordination contradiction, flagged not resolved.** Capo puts the diaconate **before Monte Cassino, at Pavia, exact date unrecorded**, so deacon first and monk second; en.wikipedia says "ordained as deacon around 782", so monk first and deacon forty years later. Capo's version rests on the form of address (*Paulus levita*) plus argument from silence, so it is **[R], not [A]**, as the verify pass insisted; Wikipedia's flat 782 is **[R]** too. **No document exists either way.** The file dates a Pavia stop 0755 as an ordering device.

## 3. Benevento and Adelperga (c. 757 to c. 774)

- **Marriage date corrected.** The gather pass said "around 758-762"; en.wikipedia says "Around 757, Arechis married Adelperga", and he became duke in 758. **[A]** Paul is her tutor and adviser **[A: his Historia Romana preface via Capo]**; the year of his move south is nowhere recorded **[R]**.
- **Santa Sofia**, built by Arechis around 760 **[A: en.wikipedia, which supplies the coordinates]**. That page does not name Paul; his relation is to patron and household, not fabric, and the file says so.
- **Salerno.** Arechis "put resources into building projects in Salerno, including a new palace and castle" **[A]**; Paul wrote verse for the programme **[A: Capo]**. **The Castello di Arechi attribution is bad**: its own article says the prince's "intervention on the castle was practically nonexistent". **Flagged in the campa.** San Pietro a Corte is not in the Salerno article and is not asserted.

**A principio saeculorum: could not be verified, kept and flagged.** The dossier claimed an acrostic poem dated **763** spelling **"Adelperga pia"**, citing Duemmler and geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3884. The citation is misattributed: **werk/3884 is the general "Carmina" entry, dated broadly c. 750-799, with no acrostic and no year**, and the poem is absent from the Latin Library page. **Neither the acrostic nor the date could be confirmed.** The stop is kept, year as ordering device.

**Historia Romana: endpoint disputed.** Sixteen books expanding Eutropius at Adelperga's instigation, in **more than 160 manuscripts** **[A: geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3890]**. Composition "um 766-774" or 766-771, **both [R]**. **Endpoint corrected**: the dossier said 552/553, geschichtsquellen gives "um 565", so the file says "553 or c. 565, variously". The quotation at this stop is **editorial prose by Bethmann and Waitz, not Paul's words**, and `quote_source` says so; the dossier's form was corrupted. **Paul's dedicatory epistle to Adelperga was not located in any reachable source. Honest gap.**

**The Mercurius relics: a one-directional link, tagged [R].** The dossier states Paul praised in verse the translation of the relics of **St Mercurius** to Benevento under Arechis II. **[R, not verified against a primary source.]** `saint_mercurius.journey.json` says **no reached source dates the arrival of the relics** and dates them 0800 to order its segment; if Paul's verse is genuine the arrival falls **before 787**. *Operator: verify the verse, and if it holds, tighten that date.*

## 4. The conquest and Monte Cassino (774 to 781)

- **774, Charlemagne takes Pavia. [A] Paul's location during the siege is unknown. [Gap.]**
- **Entry into Monte Cassino: contradiction, flagged.** Capo reads Ilderic's epitaph as framing the conversion as the **culmination of the Lombard career**, hence **before 774**. Wikipedia says only "before 782", and further suggests Paul **met Charlemagne at Monte Cassino** rather than in Francia, which Capo does not support. **Neither discrepancy resolved.** The file dates the stop 0772 on Capo's reading, as an ordering device.
- **776, Hrodgaud of Friuli's revolt.** Suppressed; **Paul's brother Arechis carried captive into Francia**. **[A: Paul's petition poem, confirmed verbatim.] Paul's own participation is documented nowhere. [Gap.]**
- **Teaching under Abbot Theodemar**: Bishop **Stephen of Naples** sent clerics to study under "Paul the deacon" **[A as reported by Capo from the *Gesta episcoporum Neapolitanorum*, itself not fetched]**.

## 5. The Frankish years (781/782 to 786)

**Rome 781 is a hypothesis.** The dossier calls the Easter meeting with Charlemagne "possible but not certain", and nothing places Paul in the city. **[R.]** `charlemagne.journey.json`'s Saint Peter's stop is dated Easter **774**, so the two files do not share a scene.

**Verba tui famuli**, the strongest attested material in the dossier, confirmed line by line at thelatinlibrary.com (headed *ca. 782*) against Bibliotheca Augustana: the opening couplet, *"Septi[m]us annus adest"*, the wife begging with a trembling mouth for four children, the sister nearly blinded by weeping. **All [A].** Two corrections. **The "28 lines" claim fails**: the text reads as **27 lines**, impossible for continuous elegiac couplets, so the file drops the count. **The impoverishment couplet was a splice** of lines 17 and 21, which are not adjacent; the file quotes the real couplet, 21-22.

**The letter to Theodemar, 10 January 783**: grace not granted, confined at the palace, the place like a prison. **[A: Capo, including the substance.]** The only firmly dated document of these years.


**Peter of Pisa: the dossier's account of Peter's side is wrong.** Paul's reply is confirmed verbatim **[A]**, but **the Homer / Horace / Virgil comparison and the request to teach Greek are not attested**: en.wikipedia quotes Peter calling him "most learned of poets and bards" sent "to our back-water", and dates the exchanges 782-786. **Dropped from the file.**

**The Hildegard epitaph, upgraded [R] to [A]**: on the Latin Library page as *Epitaphium Hildegardis filiae*. Hildegard was Charlemagne's infant daughter, named for her mother the queen, and died in **783**, not "before 783".

**Metz.** *Liber de episcopis Mettensibus* for Bishop **Angilram**, linking the Carolingians to **St Arnulf**. **[A: Kempf, 2013.] Whether Paul travelled to Metz is not established. [Gap.]**

**Corbie.** Paul sends **Adalard** fifty-four letters of Gregory the Great. **[R: from Paul's letter collection via the dossier; en.wikipedia 'Corbie Abbey' does not name Paul.]** **Adalard's dates conflict inside Wikipedia itself**: ninth abbot 780-824 on one page, exiled 817 and dead 827 on the other. The "illness from September to Christmas" was **dropped** on the verify pass's instruction, a loose end since the reason it gave points at the *Vita Gregorii* entry.

**The Aachen problem.** The gather pass pinned four Frankish stops at **Aachen** and spoke of a "palace complex". en.wikipedia: the Palatine Chapel's core "was begun in 793 at the earliest", a decade after these scenes, and in 782-783 the court was **itinerant in the Rhine, Moselle and Saxon theatre**. The file renames all four "The itinerant Frankish court, …", drops the palace language, and uses the palace-school pin from `charlemagne` **as an explicitly conventional marker, with `date_confidence` stating that Paul is not thereby placed at Aachen and that the buildings did not yet exist.** No source fixes where the petition was handed over, and inventing Thionville would be a fresh assertion.

## 6. Monte Cassino again, and the late books (786 to c. 797)

- Return **no later than autumn 786** **[A: Capo]**. **The brother's fate is never recorded; the sources simply stop mentioning him. [Gap.]**
- **26 August 787, Arechis II dies** after retreating to Salerno before Charlemagne's advance; Paul composes *Lugentum lacrimis populorum*. **[A: Capo; the retreat confirmed at en.wikipedia.]** The Arechis article notes the exact place of death "isn't explicitly stated". **[Flagged.]**
- ***Vita Gregorii Magni*. Dating corrected**: geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3891 gives **c. 782-787**, so "shortly after his return from Francia" was unsupported narrowing. Sources: Gregory's own writings, Gregory of Tours, Bede; a ninth-century recension added legends. **[A]**
- ***Epitome Festi***, dedicated to Charlemagne **[A as to work and dedication; R as to place and date, c. 786-799]**. **The Homiliary**, two parts, authorized by Charlemagne's *Epistola generalis*, probably late 790s **[A: Guiliano (Brepols, 2021) via publisher description; the book itself not read]**.

## 7. The *Historia Langobardorum*

- Composed at Monte Cassino, "after 787 and at any rate no later than 796" **[A]**; Book I precedes 796 because it speaks of **Avar dominion as still current** **[A: Capo]**. The narrative **stops at the death of Liutprand in 744**, thirty years before the fall of the kingdom. **Whether by authorial choice or by death is an open dispute, flagged not resolved.**
- **I.1, the northern thesis**, confirmed verbatim on two fetches of Foulke at elfinspell.com. **[A]**
- **The Godan and Frea legend is I.8, not I.1, and Paul's verdict was misquoted.** Foulke has **"a silly story"**, closing **"These things are worthy of laughter and are to be held of no account"**; the dossier had "a ridiculous fable". **Corrected.**
- **I.11, the Cynocephali stratagem**, the passage that makes this subject worth having, confirmed verbatim: too few to force passage from Scoringa into Mauringa past the **Assipitti**, the Lombards spread the rumour that they have dog-headed men in camp who drink human blood, and widen the camp with tents and fires until the enemy withdraws. **[A] Paul frames it explicitly as a fabricated rumour and a stratagem**, not as a claim that such men exist, and the file preserves that framing exactly. Reversing it would erase the difference between this passage and the rest of the medieval cynocephalus corpus.

**The legendary pins.** Two stops (Scadinavia 58.0/13.0; the Scoringa to Mauringa route 53.6/12.0) sit at **notional coordinates for geography Paul narrates and never visited**, and both confidence fields say so in the first clause. **Scoringa, Mauringa and the Assipitti cannot be securely mapped**, and **Paul's written source is not securely identified either**. *If the operator prefers these two at Monte Cassino, that is a one-line change to each.*

## 8. Death and afterlife

- **Died 13 April** at Monte Cassino **[A as to the day: the necrology]**. **The necrology gives no year.** Reconstructions range **797 to 799**; Capo notes an alternative tradition of **21 July**. 799 is conventional because Paul seems to have died before the coronation of December 800. **[R, argument from silence.]**
- **The necrology Latin was corrupted in the dossier**: *"venerandus memoriae"* is ungrammatical (genitive required), and *monachus* classicizes the manuscript's *monacus*. **The MGH page carrying the notice could not be reached** (archive.org OCR truncates around p. 21; dMGH serves page images), so the file uses the it.wikipedia form and cites the *Necrologium Casinense* through it.
- **Burial: the dossier's two lenses contradicted each other.** The Latin is *"iuxta ecclesiam Sancti Benedicti, ante capitulum"*, **beside** the church and **before** the chapter house, not inside it. **[R, later tradition, downgraded from the afterlife lens's A.] No identifiable grave survives**; the abbey was levelled in 1944. **No cult, no canonization, no relic. [R, negative finding.]**
- **Ilderic's acrostic epitaph**, probably early ninth century, is the **principal near-contemporary narrative source** for the court upbringing, Flavian, and the conversion. **Authenticity questioned, not decisively overthrown. [R]**
- **Codex XXVIII**, Cividale **[A: shelfmark at en.wikipedia]**. **The museum's site refused connection on every attempt, both passes.** About **115 copies** per Laura Pani (2000); oldest the **Assisi palimpsest**, earliest complete the **Codex Sangallensis 635**.
- ***Ut queant laxis*.** Attribution to Paul is **[R] and disputed**: first securely traceable to **twelfth-century Cassinese writers (Alberic, Peter the Deacon)**, repeated by **Durandus c. 1286**; the lost-voice story is theirs. **Guido of Arezzo's eleventh-century use of the hemistich initials is [A]** and independent of the authorship question. The seventh syllable "si" is credited variously to **Zacconi, 1592** (it.wikipedia) or **Anselmo of Flanders, c. 1600**, and to nobody at all in en.wikipedia.
- **Piazza Paolo Diacono**, Cividale, located by Nominatim **[A as to existence; naming date not established]**. **Several searches failed to find any statue or bust in the town.** **Capo's edition** is **Vicenza 1992**, not Milan **[A: geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3889]**.
