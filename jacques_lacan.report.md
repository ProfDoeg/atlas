# Jacques Lacan (1901-1981): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for `jacques_lacan.journey.json`. **[A]** = attested, source named inline · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or synthesis. Contradictions are flagged and not adjudicated unless a cited source settles them, in which case the correction is stated and the discarded value kept visible. Lacan is written about constantly by people who care about the doctrine and not at all about the record. The corrections are most of the work.*

---

## 1. Origins and schooling, 1901-1918

Born 13 April 1901 in Paris to Alfred Lacan and Émilie Baudry **[A: en.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia]**, at 95 boulevard Beaumarchais **[A: fr.wikipedia; Roudinesco]**.

- **Coordinate corrected.** The circulating 48.8625 / 2.365 lands about 350 m off the boulevard near the rue Oberkampf; Nominatim gives number 95 as **48.8598 / 2.3673**.
- **Arrondissement unresolved.** fr.wikipedia puts number 95 in the **3rd**, OpenStreetMap in the **11th**. The boulevard is itself a boundary. Both recorded.
- **Father's trade corrected.** en.wikipedia calls Alfred a **soap and oils salesman**; the Dessaux vinegar and mustard business of Orléans, usually attributed to him, belongs to the **grandfather's** line.

**Collège Stanislas, 1907-1918** **[A: en.wikipedia, range explicit]**. Coordinate corrected from a value 230 m off to fr.wikipedia's **48.8453 / 2.3275**. Caveat: fr.wikipedia **does not list Lacan among the alumni**; the attendance is attested from the Lacan side, so that is a gap in the school's article, not counter-evidence.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable documents the household's practice beyond the adjective "Catholic."

## 2. Medicine and the case of Aimée, 1919-1932

Ruled unfit for military service, enters the Paris Faculty of Medicine **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**End date corrected.** The pool gave 1919-1926; fr.wikipedia gives **1919-1932**, which the internal evidence supports, since the Sainte-Anne internship (1927-1931) and the November 1932 doctorate both fall inside the enrolment. The faculty coordinate 48.8508 / 2.3405 is within about 110 m of number 12 and is accepted.

### The avant-garde claim: the largest correction here

The pool carried a chronology entry dated **1920**: "contact with Surrealist and Dadaist circles begins." Impossible as dated and unsourced. Surrealism did not exist in 1920, Breton's first manifesto being **1924**; neither en.wikipedia nor SEP asserts a 1920 date; and Lacan's surrealist connections (Breton, Dalí, Crevel) belong to the **early 1930s**, after the thesis. The one documented early contact is **Adrienne Monnier's circle, 1921**.

The file replaces it with a single stop tagged hard **[R]**: the séance de lecture of **7 December 1921** at Monnier's shop, Larbaud reading Ulysses in French with Joyce present. **No source consulted places Lacan in that room on that day**, and the stop says so twice. It is kept because it is the only reconstruction the corrected pool itself proposes and because it sets up the attested 1975 Joyce lecture. Pin **48.8514 / 2.3389**, inherited byte-identical from `james_joyce.journey.json`.

### The Sainte-Anne coordinate: three-way disagreement, unresolved

The most consequential pin here, since the hospital recurs (internship 1927, Artaud 1938, seminar 1953-1963). Four values were in play: the pool's 48.8262 / 2.3316, which reverse-geocodes to the rue de la Tombe Issoire about 600 m west and is **wrong**, discarded; fr.wikipedia's 48.8287 / 2.3385; the corpus's 48.8283 / 2.3391 in the Foucault and Breton files; and **48.829 / 2.3396** in `artaud.journey.json`, on the stop that *is* Lacan's appearance in the corpus. The surviving three agree to within about 90 m. The file uses the Artaud value and records the split in `date_confidence` on every Sainte-Anne stop, leaving the corpus's two pins unreconciled.

**Internship** under Henri Claude, 1927-1931 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Clérambault** at the Infirmerie spéciale, 1928-1929 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; the "only master in psychiatry" line is standard in the biographies with no primary citation retrieved, and the stop's coordinate 48.8555 / 2.3465 comes from the pool ungeocoded and is **not verified [R]**.

**Aimée, 18 April 1931.** Marguerite Pantaine stabs Huguette Duflos at a Paris theatre; committed to Sainte-Anne, she becomes the core of the thesis **[A: standard accounts, Roudinesco]**. **Unresolved:** the theatre is named differently across sources and no reachable source fixes the address. Only the city and the day are firm, and the campa says so.

**Doctorate, November 1932**, *De la psychose paranoïaque dans ses rapports avec la personnalité* **[A: en.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia]**; day of defence not recorded. **Surrealist reception [R]:** Dalí's letter and the Breton-Crevel reading are standard and consistent with the corrected pool, but no source consulted dates their reading of the thesis itself.

**Loewenstein analysis, 1932-1938** **[A: Roudinesco]**. No consulting-room address anywhere; pin is the Paris centroid, declared. That the analysis was unfinished is Lacan's own testimony.

## 3. Kojève, the mirror, the occupation, 1933-1949

**Kojève's Hegel seminar, 1933-1939**, alongside Bataille, Queneau, Merleau-Ponty **[A: en.wikipedia 'Alexandre Kojève'; SEP]**.

**Marriage to Marie-Louise Blondin, 29 January 1934**, mairie of the 17th **[A: fr.wikipedia, to the day]**. Children Caroline (Jan 1937), Thibaut (Aug 1939), Sibylle (Nov 1940), each to the month **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**SPP:** candidate 1934, **membre titulaire 1938** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, neither dated within its year. The file folds the 1938 elevation into the Artaud stop and states in `date_confidence` that only the coincidence of **year**, not season, is attested.

**Marienbad, August 1936.** First mirror-stage paper; Jones in the chair cuts it short; **no text survives, none was published** **[A: en.wikipedia 'Mirror stage']**. The cutting-off rests on Lacan's own later account.

**Artaud, 1 April 1938.** Date, pin and content of the verdict inherited whole from `artaud.journey.json` **[A]**: the corpus's own record of Lacan's clinical debut.

**Occupation, 1940.** SPP disbands; military medical service at the **Val-de-Grâce** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no coordinate for that service in any source consulted, so the file uses the Paris centroid rather than inventing a hospital pin.

**Judith, 1941**, born to Lacan and **Sylvia Bataille née Maklès**, registered under the Bataille name **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**5 rue de Lille.** The pool tags the move **[R]** and dates it **1942**; other summaries give 1941. Both recorded; stop dated 1941. **Coordinate corrected:** circulating values 48.8592 / 2.3288 and 48.8595 / 2.3268 are street centroids up to 425 m off, against Nominatim's house-number **48.8578 / 2.3320**. This also corrects the afterlife lens's plaque entry.

**England, 1945.** Five weeks; meets Jones, Bion, Rickman **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Gap, stated in the campa:** which hospitals he entered is reconstructed from secondary literature; the pin is central London for an unnamed itinerary.

**Zurich, 1949.** The reworked mirror-stage paper, the version printed in Écrits **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no venue named; pin **47.3769 / 8.5417** is the city centre, declared a placeholder, not inherited from `jung.journey.json`, whose Zurich pins are specific addresses and would be false precision here.

## 4. The Rome Discourse and the first break, 1951-1953

**Guitrancourt, 1951** purchase of La Prévôté **[A: es.wikipedia]**. **Contradiction recorded:** fr.wikipedia's village article dates his regular presence from **1955**; both stand. **Coordinate: three values, two wrong.** The geography lens gave 49.0667 / 1.7333 (about 7 km off) and the afterlife lens 49.0333 / 1.7333 (about 4 km off) for the *same village*; fr.wikipedia gives **49.0094 / 1.7778**, used for house and grave.

**Marriage to Sylvia Bataille, 1953** **[A: Roudinesco]**; venue and month unknown, Paris centroid declared.

**The 1953 rupture.** Elected SPP president in January, resigns in June after a motion aimed at his variable-length sessions, co-founds the **SFP** with Lagache, Dolto and others **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The critical formula quoted in the file ("everyone is well aware what is meant by the deceptive phrase 'variable length'") is **a critic's, not Lacan's**, and the `quote_source` says so. The pool's framing of the controversy is tagged **[R]** as a Wikipedia-plus-SEP synthesis.

**Rome Discourse, 26 September 1953 [R].** Date and venue are universal in Lacan scholarship and the pool itself tags them **[R]** for want of primary attestation. **Corrected: the structuralist turn is not an event.** The pool dated the engagement with Saussure, Jakobson and Lévi-Strauss to 1953; SEP has him conversant with them "at the end of the 1940s and beginning of the 1950s," the dictum emerging **across the 1950s**.

**Seminar opens, Sainte-Anne, 1953-1963** **[A: en.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia]**. **Date hedge:** only the year is attested; the month is set to November, where the first seminar of the public series is conventionally placed. A flat 1953-01-01 would have put the seminar before the June resignation that caused it.

## 5. The excommunication, 1963-1964

**Japan, April 1963** **[A: Roudinesco, standard chronology]**. **Corrected:** the pool attached *Lituraterre* to this trip; it was published in *Littérature* no. 3 in **1971** out of the **second** Japanese journey, so the pool collapsed two visits eight years apart. **Gap:** nothing beyond Tokyo is documented.

**Stockholm: month corrected.** The geography lens gave 1963-07; the 23rd IPA congress ran **28 July to 1 August 1963** and en.wikipedia dates the condition on the SFP to **August 1963**. The file uses 1963-08-01, matching the chronology lens.

**SFP complies, November 1963** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. "Excommunication" is Lacan's own word, presented as his own.

**ENS, 15 January 1964** **[A: en.wikipedia; Britannica]**, on Althusser's invitation with Lévi-Strauss's support.

- **Pin.** The corpus value for 45 rue d'Ulm is **48.8433 / 2.3447** (`michel_foucault.journey.json`). The pool's interlock lens reported 48.8425 / 2.3465 as coming from that same file, which is not what the file contains. The file on disk wins.
- **Attendee list corrected.** The pool put **Barthes** and **Lévi-Strauss** among seminar attendees and asserted that Lévi-Strauss's kinship structuralism "directly shaped Lacan's return to Freud." SEP supports **Hyppolite, Foucault, Deleuze, Irigaray, Kristeva** in the audience but neither of those two, and the causal claim is synthesis. Both are dropped; Lévi-Strauss is named separately for backing the move onto the premises.
- **The quotation here is tagged [R] in its own `quote_source`:** "In order to understand the Freudian concepts, one must set out on the basis that it is the subject who is called," attributed to the Sheridan translation but retrieved through an aggregator with OCR corruption in neighbouring entries, and unchecked against the Norton text.

**École Freudienne de Paris, 21 June 1964** **[A: en.wikipedia x2]**: three sections, half the SFP's assets.

## 6. Écrits and the Americans, 1964-1975

**Miller at La Prévôté: year corrected.** The pool dated the meeting with Judith to **1963**; en.wikipedia's Jacques-Alain Miller article places his first stay there after the **summer of 1964**. The discarded 1963 is named in the campa. Marriage 1966, index of concepts 1966, transcription from 1973 **[A]**.

**Écrits, 1966, Seuil** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Corrected:** the geography lens attached a two-volume edition to **1969** and the afterlife lens gave **1969-1971**; en.wikipedia has it reissued in two volumes in **1970/1**. The 1969 date belongs to the move to the Faculté de Droit, a different event of the same year.

**Johns Hopkins, 18-21 October 1966** **[A: en.wikipedia 'Structure, Sign, and Play'; corpus files]**. Pin **39.3299 / -76.6205**, inherited byte-identical from the Barthes and Deleuze files and identical to the pool's value; the quotation "the unconscious is structured as a language" is verbatim from lacan.com/hotel.htm. Artaud is named in the campa, reaching back to 1938; Barthes and Deleuze are not, for the reason given in section 8.

**The Pass, 1967** **[A: en.wikipedia 'École Freudienne de Paris']**. The quotation here is from the 1955 Purloined Letter seminar, and its `quote_source` says it is placed for its subject, not its provenance.

**Faculté de Droit, 1969-1980** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Venue sequence corrected:** the afterlife lens inserted a **Sorbonne** stage between the ENS and the law faculty. No such stage existed; the "Sorbonne" of the French summary *is* the Panthéon law faculty, then part of the Sorbonne. Real sequence: Sainte-Anne 1953-63, ENS 1964-69, Faculté de Droit 1969-80 **[A: fr.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]**.

**Vincennes, 3 December 1969** **[A: standard citation, published in Seminar XVII]**. **Pin corrected:** the pool's 48.8467 / 2.4372 reverse-geocodes to the town centre of the commune of Vincennes, a different municipality; the experimental centre stood in the Bois de Vincennes on City-of-Paris land in the 12th and was demolished in August 1980. The file uses the corpus campus value **48.8434 / 2.4415** from the Deleuze and Foucault files. The line about revolutionaries wanting a master is **indirect speech**, since no verbatim text was verified.

**Milan, May 1972** **[A: bibliography of the occasional lectures]**. **Attribution corrected:** "sinister priest-manipulators" is **not** Deleuze and Guattari's phrase. Frederick Crews wrote that they "pilloried analysts as the most sinister priest-manipulators of a psychotic society," and it is Crews who says the book devastated the shrinking Lacanian camp **[A: en.wikipedia 'Anti-Oedipus']**. The campa states the attribution correctly, unusual inside a campa but justified because the misattribution is what circulates. Guattari's analysis with Lacan is **[R]**.

**Joyce, 16 June 1975**, Sorbonne, with Jacques Aubert; Seminar XXIII *Le sinthome* the following winter **[A: en.wikipedia; standard bibliography]**.

**United States, November-December 1975:** Yale 24 Nov, Columbia 1 Dec, MIT 2 Dec **[A: en.wikipedia for the trip; lacan.com/itinerary.htm day by day]**.

**The most important quotation correction in this pass:** "It is impossible to tell the whole truth because the structure of language always introduces an element of fiction" is **NOT a Lacan quotation**. It appears on lacan.com/itinerary.htm inside the chronicler's own summary prose, unquoted, reporting the opening thesis of Television, while the adjacent 1 December material **is** in quotation marks. The file recasts it as description in the Columbia campa and flags it in `date_confidence`. The Television line usually wanted instead ("I always speak the truth. Not the whole truth, because there's no way to say it all") was not verified here and is not used. The three quotations that are used from the trip **are** in quotation marks on that page **[A]**.

## 7. Dissolution and death, 1980-1981

**Dissolution of the EFP, January 1980** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; French sources open the dissolution on **5 January 1980**.

**Corrected: la Cause freudienne is 1980, not 1981.** Lacan announced it **21 February 1980**, entrusting direction to Solange Faladé, Charles Melman and Jacques-Alain Miller; it is the successor **École de la Cause freudienne** that is constituted in **1981** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Two bodies a year apart, routinely collapsed. The file separates them.

**Caracas, 12 July 1980** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The Caracas Discourse is quoted across the Lacanian literature but **no agreed printed text was located**, so the famous line is given as secondary-literature consensus **[R as to wording, A as to substance]**.

**Death, 9 September 1981, 6th arrondissement** **[A: fr.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]**. **Corrected:** the afterlife lens pinned it at the generic Paris centroid 48.8566 / 2.3522, which is the Hôtel de Ville in the **4th** and contradicts the arrondissement its own source gives; the file uses **48.8496 / 2.3327**. **Unresolved:** whether he died at home or in a clinic differs across accounts.

**Burial, Guitrancourt, September 1981** **[A: fr.wikipedia, municipal cemetery]**. **Day not attested:** the file sets 1981-09-12 purely so the burial sorts after the death, and says so.

**Afterlife.** Miller's sole editorship after the death, Books I through at least XIX across the 1970s-2010s; head of the World Association of Psychoanalysis 1992-2002; Le Monde's hundred books of the century; Sheridan's partial translation 1977, Fink's complete edition **2006** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 8. Interlocks used, and interlocks refused

Named inside a campa, each on a documented Lacan-side relation: **`artaud`** (examined and pronounced on him, 1938); **`james_joyce`** (the 1975 Sorbonne lecture and Seminar XXIII, with the 1921 Monnier stop as the [R] end of the same thread); **`edgar_allan_poe`** (the 1955 Seminar on "The Purloined Letter"); **`hegel`** (six years in Kojève's Hegel seminar); **`freud`** (the return to Freud is the programme, and the Caracas line declares for Freud against Lacan's own followers); **`claude_levi_strauss`** (drew on the *Elementary Structures*; Lévi-Strauss backed his EPHE appointment and later called his structuralism abstract); **`michel_foucault`** and **`gilles_deleuze`** (both attested by SEP in the audience, Deleuze also through Anti-Oedipus and Guattari); **`andre_breton`** (surrealist reception of the 1932 thesis, [R]).

**Refused.** `roland_barthes`: co-present at Johns Hopkins, but no documented Lacan-Barthes relation surfaced, and the claim that Barthes attended the seminar is corrected above. `jung`: the pool's link is an analogy between the 1913 and 1963 IPA schisms. Buenos Aires as an inherited pin: no atlas file states the link and Lacan never went, so Caracas carries the Spanish-American reception.
