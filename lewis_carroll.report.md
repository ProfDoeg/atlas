## 1. The rectory childhoods (1832-1849)

- Born **27 January 1832** at the Old Parsonage, Newton-by-Daresbury, Cheshire, third of
  eleven children, eldest son, of Rev. Charles Dodgson and Frances Jane Lutwidge. **[A,
  Wikipedia 'Lewis Carroll'; Cohen, *Lewis Carroll: A Biography*, 1995]**. The parsonage
  burned down in 1883; only All Saints' church survives. **[A, Findmypast; MacTutor]**.
- **Coordinate correction (logged).** The pool first gave Daresbury as 53.3379, -2.6233,
  then corrected itself: All Saints' Church is at **53.3406, -2.6312** (Wikidata Q4729313).
  The journey uses the corrected value. A third value in the afterlife lens (53.3394,
  -2.6378) was also rejected.
- Family moves to the **Croft-on-Tees** rectory c. 1843, the family seat for a
  quarter-century, where the boy edits his family magazines. **[A, Wikibooks]**.
- **Richmond School** under James Tate, Aug 1844 to Nov 1845. **[A, LCSNA chronology]**.
- **Rugby School**, 1846 to end of 1849; years he and his biographers call unhappy, yet
  R. B. Mayor rates him the most promising boy of his age. **[A, Wikipedia]**.

## 2. Christ Church, student and lecturer (1850-1857)

- **Matriculates 23 May 1850**, into residence Jan 1851. **Date correction (logged):** the
  pool's geography prose said "Matriculates in 1851," wrong against its own chronology; the
  journey uses 1850-05-23. **[A]**.
- Mother dies **1851**, recorded cause "inflammation of the brain." **[A]**.
- Firsts in mathematical moderations (**1852**) and the Final Honours School (**1854**).
  **[A]**.
- **Mathematical Lectureship, 1855** (not 1856), held **26 years to 1881** (not 25); the
  journey follows the pool's own correction of the geography prose. **[A]**.
- First work as "Lewis Carroll": the poem **'Solitude'**, *The Train*, March **1856**.
  **[A]**.
- **Meets John Ruskin, 1857.** **[A]**. Ruskin later tutored Alice Liddell in drawing;
  Dodgson photographed him and kept his books. **interlock: `john_ruskin`.**

## 3. The camera and the Deanery (1856-1862)

- **1856:** Dean Henry Liddell arrives with daughters Lorina, Alice, Edith; Dodgson takes
  up photography under his uncle Skeffington Lutwidge and friend Reginald Southey. **[A]**.
  Photography and the Liddells enter his life the same year, the pivot of the biography.
- **Farringford, 1859:** calls unannounced on Tennyson. **[A]**. Tennyson named in campa as
  a historical acquaintance, not an atlas slug.
- **Dimbola Lodge:** Julia Margaret Cameron, fellow photographer, joint exhibition; he
  privately dismissed her soft-focus work. **[A]**. Named as a historical figure only.
- **Ordained deacon 22 December 1861**; never proceeds to priest; Liddell waives the
  college rule. **[A]**.

## 4. The golden afternoon and Alice (1862-1865)

- **4 July 1862:** rowing party from **Folly Bridge** to **Godstow** with Robinson
  Duckworth and the three Liddell girls. **[A]**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:**
  Oxford weather records for that day read cool and wet against the book's "golden
  afternoon," and scholars dispute whether the tale was told that single day or assembled
  over longer acquaintance (logged in the Folly Bridge `date_confidence`).
- **Rupture with the Liddells, June 1863. [R].** The diary page is physically missing,
  cut by a family hand; biographers disagree (gossip re a governess, a supposed attachment
  to Lorina, or the later marriage-proposal legend). The **'Cut Pages in Diary'** document,
  published 1996, points to gossip and contradicts the proposal legend. The gap and the
  competing theories are stated in the stop; none resolved.
- **Cheyne Walk, 7 Oct 1863:** photographs the Rossetti family at Dante Gabriel Rossetti's
  Chelsea house; shower spoils the group negative; begins a correspondence with Christina
  Rossetti running into the 1890s. **[A, NPG P56; Bonhams; victorianweb]**. Quote used is
  verbatim from the real two-way correspondence. **interlocks:
  `dante_gabriel_rossetti`, `christina_rossetti`.** Christina's *Goblin Market* (1862),
  cited as a likely influence on Wonderland's descent, sits in `suggested_refs`, not staged
  as a scene.
- **Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, 1865** (Macmillan; Tenniel; first printing recalled
  over plate quality, reissued 1866), expanded from the 1864 *Alice's Adventures Under
  Ground*. **[A]**.
- **The Dynamics of a Particle (1865)** and the quaternion reading of the Mad Tea-Party.
  **[R, contested]**, see section 8.

## 5. The determinant and the only journey abroad (1866-1868)

- **17 May 1866:** Royal Society paper, published as *Condensation of Determinants*, now
  **Dodgson condensation**, later tied to the Desnanot-Jacobi identity and alternating-sign
  matrices. **[A, Wikipedia 'Dodgson condensation'; Thales & Friends]**. **Coordinate note:**
  the pool gave no Royal Society coordinate; I placed it at Burlington House, 51.5085,
  -0.1393 (the Society's home 1857-1967). Logged as my addition.
- **Russia, 1867**, the **only trip of his life beyond western Europe**, with Henry Liddon
  on an Anglican-Orthodox goodwill errand. Travel order: **St Petersburg** (59.95,
  30.3167), **Moscow** Kremlin (55.752, 37.6175), **Nizhny Novgorod** fair (56.3287,
  44.002, the farthest point), **Trinity Lavra, Sergiev Posad** (56.31, 38.1333) on the
  way back. **[A, rusmirjournal; rbth; graceguts]**. His *Russian Journal* was published
  posthumously **1935**.
- **The Alphabet Cipher, 1868:** a Vigenere-type polyalphabetic cipher he called
  unbreakable; Kasiski had published a general attack in 1863, Babbage earlier still.
  **[A, Wikipedia 'The Alphabet Cipher']**. **interlock: `blaise_de_vigenere`**, since
  Carroll's cipher is, in mechanism, Vigenere's own, a real one-directional debt.

## 6. The Oxford workshop (1868-1884)

- **Max Muller**, Oxford's first Chair of Comparative Philology (**1868**), well known to
  Dodgson in the same college world; Dodgson read Muller's *Lectures on the Science of
  Language*, cited as an influence on the nonsense language of the Alice books. **[A for
  the acquaintance and the reading; R for the influence on the nonsense tongue]**.
  **interlock: `max_muller`.**
- **Through the Looking-Glass, 1871** (chess structure; 'Jabberwocky'). **[A]**.
- **The studio over his rooms, 1872;** photographic corpus about 3,000 images, see
  section 9. **[A]**.
- **A Method of Taking Votes on More than Two Issues**, Clarendon Press, **March 1876**,
  "Dodgson's method" (Condorcet winner, fewest preference reversals). **[A, Bartholdi/Tovey/
  Trick, *J. ACM* 1989; Wikipedia 'Dodgson's method']**. **Date correction (important):**
  the pool's chronology bundled this under **1884** and tagged its own mis-dating. 1884 is
  the *separate* proportional-representation work, *The Principles of Parliamentary
  Representation*. The journey dates the method 1876-03-01, names 1884 only for the PR work,
  and says so in `date_confidence`.
- **The Hunting of the Snark, 29 March 1876** (Henry Holiday). **[A]**.
- **Euclid and His Modern Rivals, 1879**, as a stage comedy; preface quote used verbatim.
  **[A]**.

## 7. The Society, the logic, Guildford, death (1880-1898)

- **Photography abandoned, 1880**, after about 24 years; about 60% of his original output
  deliberately destroyed; surviving corpus catalogued by **Taylor and Wakeling**. **[A]**.
- **Society for Psychical Research**, see section 10.
- **Eastbourne**, 7 Lushington Road, summers **1877-1896**. **[A, openplaques]**. The stop
  is dated to a representative summer (1884) to keep the final segment in order; the
  `date_confidence` says so and the campa gives the real span.
- **Sylvie and Bruno** (1889) and **Sylvie and Bruno Concluded** (1893); the second's
  preface names Sinnett's *Esoteric Buddhism*, the root of the theosophy rumour. **[A]**.
- **Hymn-number premonition, 6 Sept 1891**, Christ Church chapel; quote verbatim. **[A,
  Psi Encyclopedia; Wakeling, *Diaries*]**.
- **Dies 14 January 1898** of pneumonia following influenza at **The Chestnuts, Guildford**,
  13 days short of 66; buried at the **Mount Cemetery, Guildford**. **[A]**. **Coordinate
  note:** geography gave the Mount at 51.2379, -0.5833, afterlife at 51.2362, -0.5842; I
  used the geography value and did not adjudicate the ~120 m gap.

## 8. The quaternion / Hamilton question (contested, [R])

The claim (Melanie Bayley and others): the Mad Tea-Party parodies William Rowan Hamilton's
1843 quaternions, Dodgson the Euclid-loyal conservative needling the new symbolic algebra.
The counter (*'Alice without quaternions'*, BSHM Bulletin, 2022, and Bayley's own critics):
**no evidence Dodgson engaged Hamilton's actual mathematics**, only that Hamilton "was in
the air" at Oxford. The journey carries one stop naming Hamilton, stating in both campa and
`date_confidence` that the kinship of temper is attested but the specific satire is a
**reconstruction** with no proof he studied Hamilton. **interlock:
`william_rowan_hamilton`**, named because the relation, if real, is Dodgson writing
(satirically) about Hamilton's work, a licit one-directional engagement, flagged [R]
throughout.

**Arthur Cayley** (`arthur_cayley.journey.json` exists) was offered as "direct dialogue"
with Dodgson's determinant work. **Dropped from the campas:** this is shared mathematical
culture, not a documented personal relation or a Dodgson citation of Cayley, and naming it
would be co-location name-dropping, which the house rule forbids. Recorded here so the
operator can overrule if he has a specific citation I do not.

## 9. The child-photography question (attested facts, unresolved interpretation)

Handled as the pool and the house style require: attested facts and the unresolved split
side by side, not resolved.

- **[A]** About 3,000 surviving-record photographs; by the Taylor-Wakeling catalogue, just
  over half depict young girls; about 30 show nude or semi-nude children; about 60% of his
  output was deliberately destroyed.
- **[A] the split is real and open.** Cohen (1995): "we cannot know to what extent sexual
  urges lay behind" the preference. Lebailly: nude child images were a mainstream Victorian
  art genre. Leach (*In the Shadow of the Dreamchild*): the "Carroll Myth" overstates the
  fixation and suppresses evidence that many "child-friends" were teenagers or adults.
- **[A, disputed]** The 2015 BBC documentary presented a nude attributed in pencil to
  "L. Carroll" (of Lorina Liddell), Musee Cantini, Marseille, as new evidence; **Edward
  Wakeling disputed its authenticity and attribution the same year.**

The studio stop states the facts and calls the meaning of the pictures "the long unsettled
argument." The Marseille print and the named scholars live in `suggested_refs` and this
report, not as scenes. The 'I am fond of children (except boys)' line (letter to Kathleen
Eschwege, 24 Oct 1879) is the stop quote: verbatim and attested, its reading left open.

## 10. The SPR vs. the Theosophical Society (the verification task)

**This is the second half of Anthony's order, and the finding is clear.**

- **SPR membership: ATTESTED. [A]** Dodgson appears as "Dodgson, Rev. Charles Lutwidge,
  M.A." in the SPR's published Proceedings membership lists **from at least 1883 through at
  least 1889** (SPR founded 1882). Confirmed by the SPR's own **Psi Encyclopedia** and
  corroborated by the **Lewis Carroll Society of North America** 'Religion' page ("Carroll
  also joined the Society for Psychical Research, founded 1882"). No exact joining date or
  resignation is in the secondary literature consulted: stated as a gap. One source lists
  him among charter-era SPR names (Gladstone, Ruskin, Balfour, Conan Doyle); I could not
  verify the charter claim and did **not** assert it, writing "from at least 1883," the
  documentary floor.
- **The 1882 letter: [A]** to James Langton Clarke, 4 Dec 1882, arguing for a natural
  force "allied to electricity and nerve-force." Used verbatim as the SPR stop quote.
- **The 1891 premonition: [A]** notebook, 6 Sept 1891. Separate stop, verbatim quote. Shows
  him weighing such data as a working member, neither debunking as conjuring nor crediting
  the dead.
- **Theosophical Society membership: NOT SUBSTANTIATED. [R, judged a conflation].** No
  membership roll, minute, letter, or biography consulted lists Dodgson in **Helena
  Blavatsky's Theosophical Society** (founded 1875). Every *dated* membership record is the
  SPR. The one real thread is **intellectual, not institutional**: the preface to *Sylvie
  and Bruno Concluded* (1893) borrows from theosophy, naming Sinnett's *Esoteric Buddhism*;
  Sherry Ackerman's *Behind the Looking-Glass* describes his writing as absorbing
  theosophical currents **as ideas**, not as membership. The interlock lens cross-checked
  `blavatsky.journey.json` (1875 founding; the 1885 SPR *Hodgson Report* investigating
  Theosophy) and `dion_fortune.journey.json` (the TS's actual London lodge life): the SPR
  and the TS were in the period **institutionally opposed** (the SPR investigated and
  discredited Blavatsky), which makes a double membership the less likely still.
  **Conclusion: SPR membership is real and documented; the Theosophical Society membership
  is a likely confusion with it, compounded by the *Sylvie and Bruno* content.**

The SPR stop (at Christ Church, where he actually was, see section 11) states the SPR
membership as fact and names "Helena Blavatsky's Theosophical Society" as the thing no
record places him in; the `date_confidence` carries the full hedge. **interlock:
`blavatsky`**, named because the question Anthony asked turns on her Society and because
Dodgson did cite the movement's literature, a real one-directional intellectual relation.
