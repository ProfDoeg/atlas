# Jean-Paul Sartre (1905-1980): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-18 for `jean_paul_sartre.journey.json`. 45 stops, 9 segments; register
`national mythology: the canon is true`; calendar `gregorian`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a
claim not run down to a fetchable source. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated, unless a
third source settles them outright, in which case that source is named. Gaps are printed as gaps.

## 1. Meudon and La Rochelle, 1905-1920

- Born 21 June 1905 at **13 rue Mignard**, Paris 16e, only child of the naval officer
  Jean-Baptiste Sartre and Anne-Marie Schweitzer **[A, fr.wikipedia "Jean-Paul Sartre"; address
  confirmed independently by fr.wikipedia "Rue Mignard"]**. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool's pin
  sits about 230 m east of the street, so the file uses the street's own **48.8636 / 2.2744**.
- Father dies of an Indochina fever when the boy is about two; **Charles Schweitzer**, a language
  teacher, raises him at Meudon and opens his library to him **[A, en.wikipedia]**. Month and day
  unrecorded, default 01-01. **Cours Hattemer**, 1910s **[A, en.wikipedia]**: *no stop*, no address
  or coordinate for the school as it stood then could be established, so it is a `suggested_ref`.
- **La Rochelle, 26 April 1917**: mother remarries, family relocates, Sartre bullied at the lycée
  **[A, fr.wikipedia]**. **Lycée Henri-IV, 1920** **[A, fr.wikipedia]**, month inferred from the
  school calendar.
- *Gap.* Nothing reachable names the stepfather or his occupation, and the much-repeated claim
  that Sartre called La Rochelle the worst years of his life could not be sourced.

## 2. Rue d'Ulm, 1924-1931

- **ENS, 45 rue d'Ulm, 1924**; befriends **Raymond Aron** and **Paul Nizan**; MA thesis "L'Image
  dans la vie psychologique" under **Henri Delacroix**, 1928 **[A, en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**.
- **The Kojève claim: dropped.** The pool attached "weekly attendance at Alexandre Kojève's
  seminars" to the 1924 entry. Impossible as dated: the Hegel seminars ran **1933-1939** at the
  EPHE **[A, en.wikipedia "Alexandre Kojève"]**, when Sartre was at Le Havre, Berlin, then Laon,
  and he is not among the documented attendees. **Excluded from the file. [R, rejected]**
- **1929**: meets Beauvoir; places first in the agrégation at his second attempt, she second **[A,
  en.wikipedia, cross-checked against the crawled dossier table]**. The October pact on a bench
  outside the Louvre comes from `simone_de_beauvoir.journey.json` **[A; pin 48.8484 / 2.3437
  inherited byte-identical]**.
- **Military service, Essey-lès-Nancy, 1929-30** **[A, fr.wikipedia]** and the **1927 Lindbergh
  hoax** **[R, britannica.com]**: both cut for length, **no stops**. **Le Havre, Lycée
  François-Ier, March 1931 to 1936**, where the early drafts of *La Nausée* are written **[A,
  fr.wikipedia]**.

## 3. The turn to phenomenology, 1932-1939

- **Bec de Gaz, Montparnasse**: Aron describes phenomenology over an apricot cocktail **[A,
  plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/]**. SEP places it in the early 1930s without a month; the
  pool's 1932 is used and the approximate pin is flagged in `date_confidence`.
- **Berlin, 1933-34, Institut français d'Allemagne.** *Correction applied.* The pool had Sartre
  studying "under the direction later taken over from Raymond Aron," which inverts the relation.
  en.wikipedia: "In 1933-34, he **succeeded** Raymond Aron at the Institut français d'Allemagne
  in Berlin" **[A]**. Beauvoir joins him in 1934 **[A, `simone_de_beauvoir.journey.json`]**.
  *Coordinate gap, flagged in the file:* the pool's pin could not be verified against any source
  naming the institute's 1933 address, so the file inherits the canonical Berlin pin **52.52 /
  13.405** and says so in `date_confidence`.
- **Laon, 1936-37.** *Correction applied.* The pool named the École normale d'instituteurs;
  en.wikipedia names the **Lycée de Laon** **[A]**. Coordinates 49.5642 / 3.6217 are kept.
- *La Transcendance de l'ego* 1936; **Lycée Pasteur, Neuilly, 1937-39**; *La Nausée* 1938, titled
  *Melancholia* until the publisher urged the change; *Le Mur* and an NRF study of Husserlian
  intentionality 1939 **[A, en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia; plato.stanford.edu; en.wikipedia "La
  Nausée"]**.

## 4. Mobilisation and captivity, 1939-1941

- **2 September 1939**: leaves Paris by train the day before the declaration, Beauvoir seeing him
  off **[A, `simone_de_beauvoir.journey.json`; pin 48.8768 / 2.359 inherited]**; drafted as a
  meteorologist **[A, plato.stanford.edu]**.
- **The Padoux / Alsace question, resolved.** The pool flagged en.wikipedia ("Padoux") against SEP
  ("Alsace") and left it open; it is not a genuine contradiction. fr.wikipedia "Padoux": "Le 21
  juin 1940, Jean-Paul Sartre est arrêté par les Allemands à Padoux." Padoux is in the **Vosges
  (Lorraine)**; SEP's "Alsace" names his **posting sector**, a separate fact carried as its own
  stop **[A, fr.wikipedia "Padoux"; plato.stanford.edu for the posting]**. Capture dated **21 June
  1940**, his thirty-fifth birthday. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool's pin is about 8.5 km off, so
  the file uses the commune's own **48.2808 / 6.5706**.
- Held at **Nancy**, then **Stalag XII-D near Trier**, among some 25,000 detainees; writes and
  stages *Bariona* at Christmas **[A, fr.wikipedia; britannica.com]**, superseding the pool's
  unverified crawled-dossier version **[R]**. **Released April 1941**, reportedly on grounds of
  poor health; back at the Lycée Pasteur in May **[A, en.wikipedia; the medical ground is the
  source's own "reportedly"]**.

## 5. Occupation, 1941-1944

- **Socialisme et Liberté, 1941**, with Beauvoir, Merleau-Ponty and others; **Lycée Condorcet,
  October 1941**, the post having been held by a Jewish teacher removed under Vichy law **[A,
  en.wikipedia]**. The file states this flat and supplies no defence the sources do not supply.
- **Lodgings: a correction, and a stop dropped.** The pool put Sartre at the **Hôtel La Louisiane**
  from 1941, writing *L'Être et le néant* there; en.wikipedia says that on release he "settled at
  the **Hotel Mistral**," and La Louisiane belongs to **1943 onward** **[A,
  `simone_de_beauvoir.journey.json`]**, after most of the book was written. **No coordinate for the
  Mistral could be found**, so the lodging stop was cut rather than pinned by guess.
- **Café de Flore**: works there for want of coal at home; the Flore and the Deux Magots are the
  meeting point for him, Beauvoir and the *Temps modernes* circle **[A, en.wikipedia "Café de
  Flore"; `simone_de_beauvoir.journey.json`, pin 48.8539 / 2.3336 inherited]**.
- **Camus.** Sartre reviews *L'Étranger* at length and favourably in *Cahiers du Sud*, February
  1943, making Camus's name **[A, `albert_camus.journey.json`; pin 48.8558 / 2.3266 inherited]**;
  Camus comes to Paris November 1943, joins Combat, and meets Sartre and Beauvoir there **[A, same
  file]**. Sartre contributes to *Combat* **[A, en.wikipedia]**.
- *L'Être et le néant*, Gallimard 1943 **[A, plato.stanford.edu, corroborated by en.wikipedia]**;
  *Les Mouches* 1943, *Huis clos* 1944 **[A, plato.stanford.edu]**. **"Hell is other people."
  [R]:** no readable full text of *Huis clos* could be reached, archive.org lending copies
  returning 401/403 on every mirror tried, and the stop's `sources` field says so.

## 6. Les Temps Modernes and the postwar quarrel, 1945-1952

- **Founding date: the SEP contradiction, resolved.** The pool flagged en.wikipedia (October 1945)
  against SEP (1943). The review took shape at Gallimard in **October 1944**, was named in
  **December 1944**, and its **first issue appeared October 1945** **[A, en.wikipedia "Les Temps
  modernes"]**; SEP's 1943 is unsupported by anything else consulted and is not carried as a live
  contradiction. Beauvoir and Merleau-Ponty on the committee **[A, `simone_de_beauvoir.journey.json`;
  Gallimard pin 48.857 / 2.3286 inherited]**.
- **Lecture, 29 October 1945, 20h30, Salle des Centraux, rue Montgolfier**, for the Club
  Maintenant, overflow crowd **[A, fr.wikipedia; plato.stanford.edu]**, published 1946; **42 rue
  Bonaparte, 1945-1962** **[A, fr.wikipedia]**. *Not carried:* the traditional broken chairs and
  fainting women, and the corydrane regimen, neither in any source reached.
- The *Chemins de la liberté* volumes 1945 and 1949 **[A, plato.stanford.edu]**; *Réflexions sur la
  question juive* 1946, *Les mains sales* and *Qu'est-ce que la littérature?* 1948 **[A,
  en.wikipedia]**; *Saint Genet* 1952 **[A, crawled dossier table cross-checked against the SEP
  chronology]**, which has no stop and is named in the rue Bonaparte campa.
- **The Camus rupture: date sharpened, causal clause dropped.** *L'Homme révolté* published
  **1951**; **Francis Jeanson's** hostile review **May 1952**; the exchange of open letters in the
  **August 1952** issue **[A, en.wikipedia; `albert_camus.journey.json`, pin 48.8534 / 2.3488
  inherited]**. The pool's added clause, that the break was "also fed by their earlier disagreement
  over Camus signing an appeal to spare ... Robert Brasillach," is **not carried**: Camus did sign
  the 1945 clemency petition and Sartre and Beauvoir did not **[A as a fact]**, but no reachable
  source makes it a cause of 1952 **[R as to the causal link]**.

## 7. Fellow traveller, Algeria, tribunals, 1954-1971

- **Travel.** Soviet Union 1954, Beijing 1955 with Beauvoir, Havana 1960 with **Che Guevara** and
  Antonio Núñez Jiménez, Cairo February 1967 (Nasser), Venice 1967 **[A, en.wikipedia and
  en.wikipedia "Simone de Beauvoir"]**. *No stops* for the Soviet Union (only the country is given,
  and a Moscow pin would be an invention), Cairo or Venice (cut for length). **No meeting with Mao
  or Castro is claimed**, neither being attested.
- **The PCF: correction applied.** The pool said Sartre "breaks with the French Communist Party" in
  1956. He was **never a member**: a fellow traveller from 1952 who broke publicly over the
  invasion of Hungary in **November 1956**, in *Le fantôme de Staline* **[A, en.wikipedia]**.
- **Camus dies at Villeblevin 4 January 1960**, buried at Lourmarin 6 January; Sartre writes the
  obituary **[A, `albert_camus.journey.json`]**. *Stop ownership:* **Villeblevin and Lourmarin are
  Camus's stops** and Sartre travelled to neither, so the obituary is staged at the canonical Paris
  pin, citing the Camus file.
- **Les Séquestrés d'Altona: corrected and split off.** The pool paired the play with the 1960
  *Critique*; it **premiered 23 September 1959** at the Théâtre de la Renaissance **[A,
  fr.wikipedia]**, while *Critique de la raison dialectique* vol. 1 is 1960 **[A,
  plato.stanford.edu]** and carries the stop.
- **Manifeste des 121, September 1960**; letter to the Jeanson network trial, 1960; preface to
  Fanon's *Les Damnés de la terre*, 1961 **[A, en.wikipedia; marxists.org /1960/jeanson.htm,
  /1961/preface.htm]**. **The bomb: date corrected**, the pool's geography item said July 1961 and
  en.wikipedia gives **June 1961**, matching the pool's own chronology item **[A]**.
- **Nobel, 1964.** Letter to the Academy about a week before the vote of 22 October, already
  decided and the letter disregarded; statement in *Le Figaro* 23 October; the only literature
  laureate to decline voluntarily **[A, en.wikipedia "1964 Nobel Prize in Literature" and
  "Jean-Paul Sartre"]**. *Stop ownership again:* **Sartre never went to Stockholm for it**, so no
  ceremony stop is staged there; his one Stockholm stop is the 1967 tribunal. He goes to ground at
  Hélène de Beauvoir's house at **Goxwiller** instead **[A, en.wikipedia]**, *coordinate corrected*
  from a pin about 4.3 km off to the commune's own **48.4336 / 7.4853**. The Academy's permanent
  secretary later recorded that someone connected to Sartre asked in **1975** whether the prize
  money was still available **[R, en.wikipedia citing Gyllensten's memoirs, the memoir unread]**;
  the campa states that secondhand status in its own prose.
- **Arlette Elkaïm: date corrected**, met 1956, **adopted 1965**, not 1964 **[A, en.wikipedia
  "Arlette Elkaïm-Sartre"]**. **Russell Tribunal, 1967: place corrected**, sessions at **Stockholm**
  and **Roskilde, Denmark**, not Copenhagen, Sartre executive president **[A, en.wikipedia]**;
  Stockholm pin 59.3327 / 18.0649 inherited from `albert_camus.journey.json`, **Roskilde named in
  the campa but not pinned**, no coordinate for it existing in the corpus.
- **May 1968**: arrested for civil disobedience, released on de Gaulle's order **[A,
  en.wikipedia]**, reportedly with the remark that one does not arrest Voltaire **[R, the source
  gives it as reported]**.
- **Goutte d'Or, winter 1971**: Foucault takes to the streets with Genet, Claude Mauriac and Sartre
  over the killing of Djellali Ben Ali, forming a committee of inquiry **[A,
  `michel_foucault.journey.json`; pin 48.8534 / 2.3488 inherited]**. **Live contradiction, carried
  unresolved:** `gilles_deleuze.journey.json` makes Deleuze a co-signer of the **Groupe
  d'Information sur les Prisons** with Foucault, Sartre and Genet, while
  `michel_foucault.journey.json` names only Foucault, Vidal-Naquet and Domenach as founders. The
  stop's `date_confidence` states the disagreement and takes no side.

## 8. The blind years, 1971-1980

- *L'Idiot de la famille*, first volumes 1971, a third 1972, unfinished **[A,
  plato.stanford.edu]**; Flaubert is named in the campa on the strength of two thousand pages
  written about him. **Blindness, 1973** **[A, en.wikipedia]**.
- **Rome, late summer 1974**: Beauvoir tape-records the conversations later published as *La
  Cérémonie des adieux* **[R, fr.wikipedia "Simone de Beauvoir"; single source]**, as the stop's
  `date_confidence` says. **Benny Lévy conversations, 1980**, published as *L'Espoir maintenant*
  **[A, plato.stanford.edu]**; *not carried:* the *Nouvel Observateur* serialisation, named by no
  source reached. **Death 15 April 1980**, pulmonary edema, 14th arrondissement, aged 74 **[A,
  en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**; no hospital is named anywhere reachable, so the pin is the
  arrondissement.
- **Funeral, Saturday 19 April 1980.** *Figure corrected:* the pool offered "fifty to a hundred
  thousand"; en.wikipedia gives one number, **50,000**, walking the boulevard du Montparnasse
  behind the cortege, routed through the 14th past what the cited account calls "all Sartre's
  haunts" **[A]**. The upper bound is unsourced and is not in the file. Pin 48.8398 / 2.3281
  inherited from `simone_de_beauvoir.journey.json`.
- **Burial and reburial**: temporary grave left of the gate, then disinterment, cremation at
  Père-Lachaise, ashes reburied to the right of it; he had declined the family plot at
  Père-Lachaise **[A, en.wikipedia]**. Beauvoir buried beside him in 1986 **[A, en.wikipedia
  "Simone de Beauvoir"]**. Metro tickets on the stone **[R, one source, in passing; the campa says
  in its own prose that it is folk tradition]**.
- **Posthumous apparatus, none staged as a stop.** The Place inaugurated 11 April 2000 **[A,
  fr.wikipedia]**, a `suggested_ref` only; *Being and Nothingness* Gallimard 1943, Barnes 1956,
  Richmond 2018 **[A, en.wikipedia]**. The pool's clause that the 2018 retranslation "renewed
  attention" is the researcher's own inference, attributed to nobody by the source, and is dropped
  **[R, rejected]**.

## 9. Quotes: verified and not

All quote fields are verbatim from the sources named beside them, and all but one were **verified
by direct fetch [A]**: the marxists.org texts, the two *Nausea* passages (archive.org
`nausea-jean-paul-sartre`, Lloyd Alexander), the Barnes *Being and Nothingness* line, and Aron's
cocktail line (SEP).

Two corrections applied inside the pool's own notes. First, the *Nausea* diary dates were
**swapped**: "I am no longer free ..." sits under **"Tuesday, 30 January"**, "The Nausea is not
inside me ..." under **"Friday"**, the café scene with Cousin Adolphe's purple suspenders. Beware
a near neighbour in the Tuesday entry, "I felt I was no longer free," a different sentence.
Second, "In our view Philosophy does not exist." is **not the opening line** of the *Marxism and
Existentialism* excerpt but the **fourth sentence of its first paragraph**, and the `quote_source`
says "first paragraph."

**Not verified [R]:** "Hell is other people," the only quote in the file unchecked against a
primary text. **Verified but not placed:** "I am no longer free, I can no longer do what I will."
It belongs to Roquentin, not to Sartre's biography; hanging it on Padoux or the Stalag would be an
insinuation the record does not support.

## 10. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Sartre is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`**, and no stop applies his framework backward to material
he never treated: every stop is rooted in his own travel or his own writing. Two items are flagged
rather than silently deleted.

1. **Forward prose in the closing stops. Direction: forward.** Rome 1974 names *La Cérémonie des
   adieux* (1981); the grave stop names Beauvoir's 1986 burial; the 1943 *L'Être et le néant* stop
   carries the 2018 Richmond retranslation in `suggested_refs`. The prose cases follow
   `abdelkader.journey.json`, whose final stop names the 1966 repatriation inside an 1883 scene.
   The **`suggested_ref`** case is the one to rule on: a `suggested_ref` is a hash, and a 2018
   artifact on a 1943 stop may need striking. Left in place for the operator.
2. **The Brasillach clemency petition as a cause of the 1952 break**, and **the Kojève seminars**,
   both dropped for the reasons given above. **Direction: neither.**

**Stops deliberately not staged here because they belong to someone else:** Villeblevin and
Lourmarin (Camus's death and burial), and the Stockholm Nobel ceremony Sartre never attended.
