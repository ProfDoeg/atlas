# Carl Jung of Mainz: research report

*Slug: `carl_jung_of_mainz`. Compiled 2026-08-09. Subject: a physician said by family and
by three published biographers of C. G. Jung to have practised at Mainz in the seventeenth
century, and to stand at the root of the Jung paternal line.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, with the source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition,
or inference. Contradictions between sources are flagged and left standing. Sources that
could not be reached are named together with the reason.

---

## 0. What is being catalogued, and what is not

This journey does **not** cover Carl Gustav Jung the psychiatrist (1875-1961), who is already
in the atlas as `jung.journey.json` **[A]**, nor Carl (Karl) Gustav Jung the Elder
(1795-1864, Basel), who sits in QUEUE.md as a separate approved subject
`carl_gustav_jung_the_elder` **[A: QUEUE.md line 364]**. The subject here is the figure two
to four generations behind them: "Dr Carl Jung" of Mainz.

The honest headline: **no seventeenth-century document naming this man has been located, and
the reason that is so is itself documented.** The Mainz municipal archives burned in a siege
**[A: Barbara Hannah, *Jung: His Life and His Work*]**. What survives is a sentence, carried
in three published biographies and then copied outward across crowd genealogy. The sentence
does not agree with itself about the man's date, his offices, or his affiliations.

An earlier draft of this research treated the tradition as pure family memory relayed by the
author in conversation. That was wrong and is corrected here: **the tradition is in the
printed Jung biographical literature and has been since at least 1976** **[A: Hannah;
McLynn; Bair-derived chronologies, all below]**.

## 1. Mainz, the setting (context, not biography)

- Throughout the 1600s Mainz is the seat of the Archbishopric-Electorate of Mainz, a Catholic
  ecclesiastical principality and one of the empire's prince-electorates; its archbishop is
  arch-chancellor for Germany **[A: general historical record of the Electorate]**.
- The University of Mainz, founded 1477, remains a Catholic institution through the
  confessional century, and it grants degrees in theology, law and medicine
  **[A: general historical record]**. This matters: the tradition claims a *doctor of
  medicine and of law* and, in one version, a *rector*, and Mainz is a city that could
  produce both.
- Mainz suffers severely in the Thirty Years War: Swedish occupation from December 1631 to
  1635/36, repeated sieges, epidemic disease, heavy population loss **[A: general
  historical record; corroborated at the level of individual movement by
  `kircher.journey.json`, which pins Kircher's flight through Mainz to November 1631]**.
- No claim in this section is tied to any Jung individual. It is the weather of the life,
  not the life.

**Atlas crossings at Mainz already in the corpus [A]:**
Bernard of Clairvaux at Mainz Cathedral, 1146, turning back Radulf's mob against the
Rhineland Jews (`bernard_clairvaux.journey.json`); Luther's 1517 letter enclosing the
Ninety-Five Theses to Albert of Brandenburg, Archbishop-Elector of Mainz
(`martin_luther.journey.json`); Kircher's ordination at Mainz, 1628, and his flight through
the city in November 1631 (`kircher.journey.json`); Goethe at the 1793 siege
(`goethe_full.journey.json`). Mainz is one of the corpus's most densely pinned cities, and
the physician of this tradition is the only one of its inhabitants the atlas cannot date.

## 2. The claimed life: the doctor of medicine and of law

**The Hannah version [A as text, R as fact].** Barbara Hannah, in *Jung: His Life and His
Work*, writes that "an evidently learned doctor of medicine and of law called Carl Jung"
lived in Mainz in the early seventeenth century, and notes that he was contemporary with the
alchemists Michael Maier and Gerardus Dorneus, working nearby at Frankfurt. Hannah does
**not** assert a connection. She writes that "it would be noteworthy - in view of Jung's
later great interest in alchemy - if his direct ancestor had been connected with these two
famous alchemists." Text quoted at
`carljungdepthpsychologysite.blog/2021/04/23/early/`; the printed page was not independently
inspected in this pass.

**The Bair-derived version [A as text, R as fact].** The "Chronobiography of Carl Gustav
Jung" at `junguipediaeng.miraheze.org`, which draws on Deirdre Bair's *Jung: A Biography*
(2003), gives as the earliest known ancestor: "Dr. Carl Jung (c. 1650), a Catholic
physician, lawyer, and university rector."

**The McLynn version [A as text, R as fact].** Frank McLynn, *Carl Gustav Jung: A Biography*
(London: Bantam Press, 1996; St Martin's Press, 1997), opens his ancestry chapter: "The Jung
family came from Mainz, and it is known that there was a Dr Carl Jung who died there in 1645
and - interestingly in view of Jung's own later preoccupations - was affiliated to the
Rosicrucians, but the early part of the [family history]..." McLynn cites **no** archival
source for this.

**Provenance note, important.** This sentence was initially attributed in the research pool
to a Geni.com genealogy profile seen only through a search snippet. That attribution is
**wrong** and is corrected here. Three independent Internet Archive / Open Library
"search inside" hits return **McLynn alone** as the source of the wording, in the scanned
book (`archive.org` item `carlgustavjung00mcly`) **[A]**. The Geni profile is downstream of
McLynn, not a witness alongside him.

**A fourth, differing version [A as text].** Michael Owen, *Jung and the Native American
Moon Cycles*, also confirmed by full-text search, opens "The Jung family came from Mainz, and
a doctor of medicine and law, called...", i.e. Hannah's description rather than McLynn's,
in a book that borrows McLynn's opening clause verbatim. The sentence is migrating between
authors.

## 3. Contradictions, flagged and not resolved

1. **Date of the man.** McLynn: died 1645. Bair-derived chronologies: c. 1650. Hannah: only
   "the early seventeenth century." No burial register, no testament, no matriculation entry
   has been produced by any of them. **Recorded as a spread, not resolved.**
2. **What he was.** Hannah: doctor of medicine and of law. Junguipedia/Bair: physician,
   lawyer, *and university rector*. McLynn: "Dr Carl Jung," plus Rosicrucian affiliation.
   The rectorship appears in one line of transmission only. **Not resolved.**
3. **The Rosicrucian claim.** Present in McLynn, absent in Hannah, absent in the Junguipedia
   chronology. Hannah supplies instead a geographical proximity to Maier and Dorneus and
   explicitly declines to convert it into a connection. **Not resolved; McLynn's version is
   the stronger claim on the weaker evidence.**
4. **When the record ends.** Genealogical compilers state the documented tree "comes to an
   abrupt end in 1688." Junguipedia says records before 1688 were destroyed. Hannah gives no
   year and attributes the loss to a siege in the **War of the Spanish Succession**
   (1701-1714), which is a decade and a half too late for a 1688 cutoff. The event that fits
   is the French occupation of Mainz from October 1688 and the imperial bombardment ending
   8 September 1689, in the Nine Years War **[A: de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geschichte_der_Stadt_Mainz]**.
   **Hannah's attribution is flagged as probably mistaken; not silently corrected in the
   journey, where the date_confidence field carries it.**
5. **An arithmetic error in circulation.** Secondary summaries describe "roughly seventy
   years" between the claimed 1645 death and the 1688 cutoff. It is forty-three years.
6. **Birth year of the Basel grandfather.** 7 September **1795** in both
   `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Gustav_Jung` and `de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Gustav_Jung`;
   **1794** in Wikidata Q2739430, which also wrongly gives Basel as the birthplace.
   **1795 is taken as primary here; 1794 recorded as the variant.**
7. **Which Sophie.** English Wikipedia's *Carl Jung* article says the psychiatrist told the
   Goethe legend of "his paternal grandfather... and his German great-grandmother, Sophie
   Ziegler." Per the *Karl Gustav Jung* article, Sophie Maria Josepha née Ziegler was the
   Elder's **mother** (wife of Franz Ignaz), i.e. the psychiatrist's great-**great**-grandmother.
   The psychiatrist's actual paternal grandmother was Sophie Frey, the Elder's third wife
   (the wives being Virginie de Lassaulx, Elisabeth Reyenthaler, Sophie Frey).
   **Flagged; the journey uses the Karl-Gustav-Jung-article relationship.**

## 4. The corrections applied to the first research pass

Recorded openly, because several of them reversed the earlier finding:

- **"Family memory only, uncorroborated"**, wrong. The tradition is in Hannah (1976),
  McLynn (1996), and Bair (2003) by way of the Junguipedia chronobiography.
- **"No source traces the family further back than the 1795 grandfather"**, wrong. English
  Wikipedia names his father **Franz Ignaz Jung**, a Mainz medical practitioner, and his
  mother Sophie Maria Josepha née Ziegler; Wikidata Q108838675 gives Franz Ignaz 1759-1831
  **[A]**.
- **"Mainz/Mannheim may be a family conflation"**, unnecessary. The family *was* from
  Mainz; Franz Ignaz relocated to Mannheim to manage a field hospital before his son's birth
  **[A: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Gustav_Jung]**. Straight-line Mainz to Mannheim is about
  59 km, not 80.
- **"The name Carl recurs across three generations"**, the pattern does not hold as stated.
  Franz Ignaz (1759-1831), then Carl Gustav (1795-1864), then **Johann Paul Achilles**, then
  Carl Gustav (1875-1961). The name skips generations. The inference drawn from it in the
  first pass is withdrawn.
- **Coordinates.** The first pass placed the Mainz stops at 49.9929, 8.2473, which is about
  2 km southwest of the historic city, on the modern Johannes Gutenberg University campus,
  well outside the seventeenth-century walls. Also placed "University of Basel" at
  47.5596, 7.5886, which is the Grossbasel riverside old town near the Rathaus, roughly
  400 m from the university's own point. Both corrected in the journey file; the atlas's
  existing canonical Mainz pin (49.999, 8.267, as used by `kircher.journey.json` and
  `goethe_full.journey.json`) is inherited byte-identical for the city stops.

## 5. The documented line, resuming

- **Franz Ignaz Jung, 1759-1831 [A: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Gustav_Jung; Wikidata
  Q108838675]**: born Mainz, medical practitioner there, later manager of a field hospital at
  Mannheim during the Revolutionary wars. He is the first Jung the record actually holds.
  Between him and the claimed 1645 physician lie about 115 years and a burned archive, and
  **no source supplies a single link across that gap [R: gap statement]**.
- **Carl (Karl) Gustav Jung the Elder, 7 September 1795 - 12 June 1864 [A: de/en Wikipedia]**:
  born Mannheim, emigrated to Basel 1822, professor of medicine at the University of Basel
  holding chairs including anatomy, surgery and obstetrics, installed as rector 1828, died
  Basel 1864. Full treatment belongs to `carl_gustav_jung_the_elder`; he appears here for
  lineage only.
- **The Goethe legend [A as a legend]**: the family held that the Elder was Goethe's
  illegitimate son. English Wikipedia calls it "likely a legend"; the psychiatrist withdrew
  it in later life. Cited here to show the family carried **more than one** unprovable
  ancestry at once, not as support for the Mainz claim.
- **Johann Paul Achilles Jung [A]**: pastor, father of the psychiatrist. The medical line
  breaks for one generation and the name skips it.
- **Carl Gustav Jung, born 26 July 1875 at Kesswil, Thurgau [A]**: the psychiatrist, whose
  thirty years of work on Dorn and Maier is the reason the Mainz tradition is interesting to
  anyone at all. **No published work of his cites the Mainz ancestor among the alchemists
  [R: no such passage found in this pass; this is a non-finding, not a proof of absence]**.

## 6. Gaps, stated as gaps

- **No primary seventeenth-century document naming the man [gap, with cause].** Not a
  research failure and not merely a tooling limit: the municipal archives burned
  **[A: Hannah]**.
- **No admission to a physicians' guild, medical-faculty roll, or Collegium medicum entry**
  for a Carl Jung at Mainz has been located **[gap]**.
- **No tomb, marker, epitaph, or parish burial record** at Mainz for the 1640s **[gap]**.
- **No treatise, edition, translation or manuscript** attributed to a seventeenth-century
  Carl Jung of Mainz in any bibliography searched **[gap]**. Contrast Maier, whose works were
  reprinted for a century.
- **No monument, plaque, or street name** in modern Mainz for a Carl Jung of the 1600s
  **[gap]**.
- **The genealogical bridge from Mainz to Basel** across roughly six generations is asserted
  by tradition and by biographers and is **not documented at any link before Franz Ignaz**
  **[gap]**.
- **Two waypoints the first pass missed and this one adds as corroborable context, not as
  biography**: the Alte Universität at Mainz (the rectorship claim needs a university), and
  Frankfurt am Main, about 32 km away, where Hannah places Maier and Dorneus.
