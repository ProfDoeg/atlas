# Karl Max Engelmann (Liga Pitagórica) — research report
*2026-08-02. Life-reformer, co-founder of the Bund der Pythagoräer (1884), keeper of a vegetarian pension in Ticino, the man who pointed the founders of Monte Verità at their hill.*

Engelmann is a subject with no biography. He has no Wikipedia article in German or English (https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Max_Engelmann returns HTTP 404, verified this pass), no birth date, no death date, no grave, and no modern monograph. Everything reachable about him is embedded as a supporting clause inside somebody else's story: Guttzeit's, Springer's, Diefenbach's, the Gräser brothers', Monte Verità's. This report keeps that shape rather than smoothing it into a life.

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or the source's own conjecture · **GAP** = stated absence.

The single richest source is Hermann Müller's essay **"Bruder-Bund und Pythagoräer-Bund"**, a PDF on gusto-graeser.de (Reinhard Christeller's Gusto Gräser archive), fetched 2026-08-02. It quotes documents no other reachable source quotes: the League's motto, Otto Rabe's 1885 pamphlet, a letter from Karl Wilhelm Diefenbach addressed to Engelmann by name, Ida Hofmann's memoir, and a verse from Engelmann's own 1918 tract. It is a secondary essay, not an archive; where it is the only witness, this report says so.

---

## Phase 1. Berlin, 1884: four men found a league

**[A]** In 1884 four men found a life-reform league: the writer **Robert Springer**, the officer-turned-nature-preacher **Johannes Friedrich Guttzeit**, the agricultural economist **Otto Rabe**, and **Max Engelmann**. Source: gusto-graeser.de PDF, "Im Jahr 1884 stifteten Johannes Guttzeit, Robert Springer, Otto Rabe u. Max Engelmann den lebensreformerischen Bund der Pythagoräer." Engelmann is a founding member, not a later joiner.

**[A]** The same four names appear in German Wikipedia's article "Robert Springer (Schriftsteller)", but attached to a differently named body: the **Internationaler Bund für konsequente Humanität** (International League for Consistent Humanity), also 1884.

> **Contradiction 1, not resolved.** Two organizational names, one year, one identical founder list. They are plausibly the same body under two names, or a league and its umbrella. No reachable source states the relation. German Wikipedia's article on Guttzeit says he "gründete er 1884 den Pythagoräer-Bund" and does not name Engelmann; German Wikipedia's Springer article names Engelmann but calls the body the Internationaler Bund. Engelmann's membership in the Pythagoräer-Bund is nevertheless **[A]**, not reconstructed, since the gusto-graeser.de PDF names him outright and both the German and English "Monte Verità" articles say he "zum Pythagoräer-Bund ... gehört hatte" / "belonged to the Pythagorean League".

**[A]** Guttzeit's route to Pythagoras is dated: de.wikipedia, "Johannes Friedrich Guttzeit", says he "ging 1881 für zwei Jahre nach Italien", so he returned in 1883 and founded the League the following year. The often-repeated framing that he founded it immediately on returning from Italy compresses a year that the source leaves open.

**[A]** The League's motto, in its own reform orthography (missing double consonants are original, not transcription error):

> Unser Grundsaz: Ni Fleisch, ni Fisch! (Pythagoras). Unser Zil: Mässigkeit und Stärke, Weisheit und Libe! (Plato).

Source: gusto-graeser.de PDF. Untranslated in the source fetched.

**[A]** The League's foundational text is Springer's **"Enkarpa. Culturgeschichte der Menschheit im Lichte der pythagoräischen Lehre"** (Hanover, 1884), which derives vegetarianism from a Pythagorean doctrine of soul-purification and cites Porphyry on Pythagorean gentleness toward animals. Source: gusto-graeser.de PDF; the title is also listed in de.wikipedia's Springer bibliography as "Enkarpa" (Hannover 1884).

**[A]** Springer's own dates, from de.wikipedia: born 23 November 1816 in Berlin, died 21 October 1885 in Berlin. He is the League's main initiator and dies the year after founding it; the PDF states this early death contributed to the group's disintegration.

> **Correction to an earlier pass.** de.wikipedia's Springer article makes no claim about a "wider Lebensreform network" and never mentions Monte Verità. That framing ("Der Pythagoräer-Bund oder Bruder-Bund war ein bedeutsamer Knotenpunkt in der Geschichte der Lebensreform") belongs to the gusto-graeser.de PDF and should be credited there.

**[A]** Otto Rabe's pamphlet, the **Erste Flugschrift des Bundes der Pythagoräer** (Berlin, self-published, 1885), is titled in the source's Literatur list in reform orthography: **"Di hohe Kulturbedeutung der fleisch- und blutlosen Ernarung des Menschen. Ein Beitrag zur Losung der sozialen Frage"**. The source flags the spelling itself with "[sic, Reform-orthographie!]". Quoted from p.15:

> dringt nicht nur auf di Rückker zur Pflanzenkost; ir Streben richtet sich auf eine durchgreifende Reform in der Wonung, Kleidung, in allen Gewonheiten, kurz in der ganzen Lebensweise des Menschen. Si hat es sich zur Aufgabe gestellt, di Bedürfnisse des Menschen zu verringern und dadurch seine Unabhängigkeit zu erhöhen.

The grammatical subject in the source is **"Die pythagorische Lebensanschauung"**, the Pythagorean view of life, not the League as an organization. An earlier pass glossed this as Rabe describing the League's aims; the softer reading is the correct one.

## Phase 2. The letter from Thalkirchen, 22 June 1885

**[A]** On 22 June 1885 the painter-prophet **Karl Wilhelm Diefenbach** writes from Munich-Thalkirchen to Max Engelmann:

> Liebe ist die Formel zur Erlösung der Menschheit. Wahre Liebe überwindet alles, auch den Ekel. Mensch sein heisst lieben. Gott ist die Liebe, Gott ähnlich zu werden des Menschen Bestimmung. ... Ein ganzer Mythos umschwebt mich jetzt schon.

Source: gusto-graeser.de PDF, citing the Diefenbach Nachlass, Archiv der Deutschen Jugendbewegung, Burg Ludwigstein. This is the only document in the whole dossier **addressed to Engelmann personally**, and it establishes him as a correspondent of Diefenbach's cult of Love fifteen years before either man reached Ticino. The Nachlass itself was not consulted; the letter reaches this report only through Müller's essay.

## Phase 3. The renaming, and then nothing

**[A]** After Springer's death Guttzeit redirects the League, renaming it **"Bund für volle Menschlichkeit. Verbrüderung, Vernatürlichung"** and launching its journal **Bruder**, from which the alternate name Bruder-Bund derives. de.wikipedia's Guttzeit article gives the journal's full title as "Der Bruder: Zeitschrift des Bundes für volle Menschlichkeit" under Guttzeit's editorship.

**[R]** The PDF hedges the whole redirection: "Es scheint, dass er nach dem Tod von Springer dem Pythagoräer-Bund eine neue Richtung und einen neuen Namen gegeben hat". It gives **no year**. Since Springer died on 21 October 1885, dating the renaming to 1885 would compress it into the last ten weeks of that year; the 1885 in the PDF's filename is not a claim in its text. The date should read "after October 1885, undated in source".

**GAP.** No source says whether Engelmann remained affiliated through the renaming, or how long his membership lasted. Between the Diefenbach letter of June 1885 and his appearance in Ticino "vor 1900", **the record on Engelmann is empty for roughly fifteen years**.

## Phase 4. Ticino, before 1900: the pension

**[A]** Around 1889 **Alfredo Pioda**, with the theosophists **Franz Hartmann** and **Constance Wachtmeister**, plans a theosophical monastery called **Fraternitas** on Monte Monescia above Ascona. It is never built. Source: en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia, "Monte Verità".

**[R]** Engelmann is presumed to have been a candidate resident. de.wikipedia, "Monte Verità": "Vermutlich als «Kandidat» für dieses nie realisierte Kloster hatte sich in Monti sopra Locarno der deutsche Lebensreformer Karl Max Engelmann niedergelassen." The conjecture is the source's own, marked by its own quotation marks around Kandidat.

> **Correction to an earlier pass.** That sentence places Engelmann's settlement at **Monti sopra Locarno**, not at Monte Monescia. Monescia is the site of the planned monastery, roughly five kilometres away across the Maggia delta. Nor is 1889 the date of his arrival: 1889 dates Pioda's plan. The gusto-graeser.de PDF dates Engelmann's settlement only as "vor 1900".

**[A]** He and his wife run a **vegetarian pension** on **Monte Trinità** above Locarno. Ida Hofmann's memoir names it as "die Pension des Ehepaares Engelmann auf dem Monte Trinità oberhalb Locarno". The PDF's own imprint line for Engelmann's 1918 tract gives "Monte Trinità, Locarno" as the place of publication, so he is still there eighteen years later.

> **Contradiction 2, not resolved.** Hofmann's memoir describes an Engelmann **couple** keeping the house; the same PDF elsewhere speaks of Engelmann's **Anhänger**, long-haired followers. Household or small community, the sources do not reconcile.

> **Contradiction 3, not resolved: the two mountains.** en.wikipedia, "Monte Verità", says "the German life-reformer Karl Max Engelmann, who had settled in Monte Brè", wikilinking the Lugano mountain. de.wikipedia, same article, says Monti sopra Locarno. These are two different lakes, fifty-five kilometres apart. **The weight of evidence favours Locarno decisively**: Hofmann's own memoir (Monte Verità, p.15) and the gusto-graeser.de PDF ("er sich vor 1900 in Monti über Locarno angesiedelt hat") both put him above Locarno, and de.wikipedia's article never mentions Monte Brè or Lugano at all. en.wikipedia's clause is most likely a mistranslation. It is recorded here as a contradiction rather than deleted, because the corpus's own essay follows it.

> **The ivu.org citation is unverifiable.** An earlier pass attributed the Monte Brè siting jointly to en.wikipedia and to ivu.org's Monte Verità history page. Both ivu.org/history/europe19b/monteverita.html and ivu.org/history/monte_verita.html return **HTTP 404**, and no ivu.org citation appears anywhere in the en.wikipedia article's wikitext. The attribution should be dropped.

## Phase 5. The corpus's own account, set against the German sources

The corpus essay **191_goethe_italian_exit.md** (and its Instagram post 2023-12-19) tells the story this way: in the space of Goethe's undocumented passage along Lago di Lugano, Engelmann of the Pythagorean League establishes a **sanitarium on Monte Brè**, offering **helio-hydroscopic nude therapies developed by Arnold Rikli at Bled**; Oedenkoven, Hoffmann and the Gräser brothers first meet at Bled in 1899; in October 1900 they meet in Munich to plan their own sanitarium; **they are informed by Engelmann** of a failed Theosophist colony atop Monte Monesca in Ascona; the founders pool resources for the **4-acre** property from Pioda and name it Monte Verità in **1902**.

Point by point against what the German-language sources say:

| Corpus essay | German sources |
|---|---|
| Sanitarium on Monte Brè, Lugano **[R]** | Vegetarian pension on Monte Trinità / Monti sopra Locarno **[A: Hofmann, PDF, de-WP]** |
| Rikli-style helio-hydroscopic nude therapy **[R]** | "eine vegetarische Pension", no therapy named; the PDF never mentions Rikli **[A by absence]** |
| Oedenkoven, Hoffmann and both Gräser brothers meet at Bled 1899 | de-WP: Hofmann and Oedenkoven meet at the Naturheilanstalt Rikli at Veldes in 1899, with Karl Gräser taking a cure there at the same time **[A]** |
| October 1900 Munich meeting of Oedenkoven, Hoffmann and the Gräsers | de-WP: "Zwischen Oedenkoven und Hofmann entwickelte sich ein intensiver Briefwechsel, der im Oktober 1900 zu einem Treffen in München führte." Oedenkoven and Hofmann only; the Gräsers' presence is unattested **[A]** |
| Engelmann informs the Munich meeting | The Gräsers stumble on his pension while already scouting Ticino: "Auf diese Pension und ihren Inhaber stießen die Brüder Gräser auf ihrem Erkundungszug" **[A]**. Direction of contact reversed. |
| 4-acre property | **Four hectares** (ten acres). en-WP: "With purchases from other owners, they acquired four hectares (10 acres)"; de-WP: "erwarben sie vier Hektar" **[A]**. The corpus figure understates the site by about sixty percent. |
| Property acquired, named Monte Verità 1902 | Purchase **autumn 1900**: "Im Herbst 1900 wurden Henri Oedenkoven und Ida Hofmann gemeinsam mit Karl Gräser, Gusto Gräser und Lotte Hattemer nach einigen Wochen des Suchens in Ascona fündig und kauften das Grundstück von Alfredo Pioda." 1902 is only the year of the **naming**: "gaben dieser 1902 den Namen Monte Verità" **[A]** |

> **Correction to an earlier pass.** A previous version flagged an October-versus-November 1900 discrepancy between the essay and Wikipedia. There is none: an October Munich meeting followed by a November encounter with Engelmann is a consistent sequence. The real discrepancies are the two above, who attended Munich and which way the information travelled.

**[A]** The encounter itself: "Im November 1900 traf Engelmann die Brüder Gräser" (de-WP and en-WP, "Monte Verità"). Ida Hofmann's account of the arrival, from her memoir p.15 as quoted in the PDF, with the internal quotation marks and dash restored exactly as the source prints them:

> "Hier findet man Menschen", "auch langhaarige - vegetarische Pensionen u.s.w." schrieb Karl, "kommet zu uns" und nach vielem Suchen und Fragen fielen wir dann gegen die Abendstunde in die Pension des Ehepaares Engelmann auf dem Monte Trinita oberhalb Locarno. Wir wurden freundlichst empfangen.

The PDF places the Gräser brothers' Erkundungszug at "Spätherbst 1900" and credits the passage as "Ida Hofmann: Monte Verità, S. 15". Expanding the title to "Monte Verità: Wahrheit ohne Dichtung" (Hofmann's 1906 book) is this report's expansion, not the source's.

**[R]** What Engelmann then does is stated conjecturally by the source itself: "Er wird sie auf das von den Locarneser Theosophen angekaufte Grundstück auf dem Hügel Monescia hingewiesen haben." He *will have pointed them* at the hill. The whole of his significance to the atlas rests on a German future-perfect.

## Phase 6. The writings, 1905 and 1917/18

**[A]** The PDF says of Engelmann: "hatte über Goethe geschrieben und über *Das Germanentum und sein Verfall* (1905)". The 1905 attaches only to *Das Germanentum und sein Verfall*; the Goethe writing is **undated** and, from the pluperfect, probably earlier. No place of publication is given for either.

> **Correction to an earlier pass.** A previous version dated the Goethe writing to 1905 and placed both in Ticino. Neither is supported.

**[A]** **"Der Uranische Staat als Pflichterfüllung des Stärkeren"** (The Uranian State as the Duty-Fulfilment of the Stronger), a starkly elitist constitution for a future life-reform polity.

> **Contradiction 4, not resolved.** The PDF's body text dates the tract **1917**; its own bibliography gives the published imprint as **Monte Trinità, Locarno, 1918**. Both years stand uncorrected in the same document.

**[A]** From p.74, the tract's closing verse:

> Verderben wohnt in dumpfer Mauern Enge! / Fort aus der Städte drückendem Gewühl! / Für freier Allheit ew'ge Urgesänge / erblüh Euch ein empfängliches Gefühl!

**GAP.** No digitized or translated copy of the full 1918 pamphlet was located, only this excerpt as embedded in Müller's essay. The 1905 *Germanentum* tract and the Goethe writing were not located at all.

**[R]** The PDF speculates that persecuted homosexuality, self-styled **Uranism**, among some members including Guttzeit, may have supplied a motivating undercurrent for founding the League, given Engelmann's later use of *uranisch* in his title. The source's own language is "kann angenommen werden, dass...". No direct biographical statement about Engelmann's own orientation is offered anywhere.

## Phase 7. No death, no grave

**GAP.** No source consulted gives Engelmann's birth date, birthplace, death date, or death place. No monument, no legend-cycle, no dedicated biography. Modern attention is confined to Christeller's Gusto Gräser archive sites (gusto-graeser.de, gusto-graeser.info) and to the German Wikipedia article on Monte Verità, where he occupies the prehistory of somebody else's colony. Even his first name is unstable across sources: **Karl Max Engelmann** in de.wikipedia, plain **Max Engelmann** in the PDF, and **"Here Engelmann"** in the corpus's own census of real persons (line 158), an evident transcription variant.
