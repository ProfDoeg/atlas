# Ezekiel son of Buzi, research report

*Priest of Jerusalem, deported 597 BC, prophet by the Chebar canal c. 593-571 BC. Compiled 2026-08. Slug: `ezekiel`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition · **gap** = nothing reachable.

What "attested" can mean here: everything about Ezekiel's life comes from one document, and there is no external record of the man, no seal, no ration tablet, no notice in Kings or Chronicles. Where this report writes **[A]** it means *the Book of Ezekiel says so, at a cited verse*, not that an independent witness corroborates it. The book carries roughly thirteen date formulas, all reckoned from Jehoiachin's exile, which makes the chronology tight and the biography thin.

---

## 1. Origin: the priest's son of Jerusalem (before 597 BC)

- Son of **Buzi**, a **priest (kohen)** **[A, Ezekiel 1:3]**. That is the whole birth notice: no mother, no birthplace, no brothers.
- Priestly training and Jerusalem residence before the deportation are **[R]**, inferred from his office, the deportation list (2 Kings 24:14), and the book's saturation in priestly categories. Whether he ever officiated at the altar is undecidable from the text.
- **Birth year: no source attests one.** The quoted **c. 623 BC** is **[R]**, from reading "the thirtieth year" of Ezekiel 1:1 as his age at the call in 593. *Correction applied:* the pool tagged a birth in 622 BC as **[A]**, wrong on both the year and the evidence class. The journey file carries `-0623-01-01` with the reconstruction in `date_confidence`.
- **The "thirtieth year" problem, not resolved.** Ezekiel 1:2 dates the same event to "the fifth year of king Jehoiachin's captivity", unambiguously 593 BC. The thirtieth year is not: his age, a jubilee reckoning, and a Babylonian era count all circulate. No reading is adopted.

## 2. The deportation of 597 BC

- Jehoiachin surrenders after three months on the throne; the king, the queen mother, the court, the smiths and armourers, and thousands of leading citizens are deported **[A, 2 Kings 24:10-16]**. Ezekiel is in this column **[A, Ezekiel 1:1-3, "among the captives"]**.
- Date **[A]**: 2 Adar of Nebuchadnezzar's seventh year, **16 March 597 BC**, from the Babylonian Chronicle (ABC 5). This is the one point in his early life fixed by a non-biblical document, and it fixes the deportation, not the man.
- **The road is entirely [R].** No source describes the route of the 597 column or places Ezekiel between Jerusalem and Babylonia. The journey file carries six reconstructed waypoints (Riblah, Damascus, Tadmor, Circesium, Mari, Babylon), each flagged. Riblah is attested as Nebuchadnezzar's western headquarters, but for the *later* deportation (2 Kings 25:6-7).
- **Two errors in the supplied pool, corrected here:**
  1. **Circesium coordinates.** The pool gave 34.4667 / 40.9167, roughly 90 km off, landing in desert near Abu Kamal rather than at the Khabur mouth. Wikipedia, "Circesium" gives 35°09'21"N 40°25'48"E = **35.1558 / 40.4300** (modern al-Busayra, at the confluence). Corrected.
  2. **Route order.** Because of that coordinate, the pool ordered Mari (34.55N) *before* Circesium (35.16N) on a march "downstream, south into Babylonia", running the column back up the river. Corrected: Tadmor, Circesium, Mari, Babylon.
- **Did Ezekiel enter Babylon itself? Gap / [R].** The book never places him in the capital; his residence is consistently Tel Abib (Ezekiel 3:15). The Babylon stop stands as the deportation's attested destination (2 Kings 24:15), with that distinction on the stop.

## 3. The call by the Chebar (593 BC)

- **31 July 593 BC [A to the day]**: fourth month, fifth day, fifth year of Jehoiachin's exile (Ezekiel 1:1-2). Conversion by the standard Babylonian tables.
- **Location [A as named, R as identified]**: "by the river Chebar, in the land of the Chaldeans". Identifying the Chebar with the *naru kabari*, a great irrigation canal past **Nippur**, is a reconstruction from cuneiform records, not a claim in the text. Pin: 32.1264 / 45.2334 (Wikipedia, "Nippur").
  - *Correction applied:* the pool credited this to the **Al-Yahudu archive**. Wikipedia, "Chebar River" cites the **Murashu archive** from Nippur: "The ka-ba-ru waterway (Akkadian) is mentioned among the 5th century BCE Murashu archives from Nippur." Al-Yahudu documents exile settlements but does not supply the identification; that citation is dropped.
- **Tel Abib [A as named, unlocated]**: Ezekiel 3:15, "an unidentified tell on the Kebar Canal" known only from this book. Pinned at 32.13 / 45.23 as a Nippur-vicinity proxy, an **[R]** placement, stated as such.
  - *Correction applied:* the pool glossed the name as Akkadian for "mound of the flood" **or** "mound of ears of grain". The second is a Hebrew folk etymology from *aviv*, the route by which modern Tel Aviv got its name, not Akkadian. The gloss is dropped from the journey prose rather than half-stated.
- Vision **[A, 1:4-28]**: storm wind from the north, four living creatures with four faces and four wings, wheels with rims full of eyes, firmament like terrible crystal, throne of sapphire, rainbow. Commission **[A, 2:1-3:11]**: a scroll of "lamentations, and mourning, and woe", eaten, sweet as honey. Seven days sitting astonished among the exiles **[A, 3:15]**.
- **The watchman charge and the dumbness [A, 3:16-27]**, released at 24:27 and 33:21-22. Whether the muteness is total, selective, or a literary device is disputed and not adjudicated.

## 4. The sign-acts (c. 593-592 BC)

All **[A]** as to text, all **undated** and placed by chapter order: the brick with Jerusalem drawn on it and siege works against it **[4:1-3]**; 390 days on the left side for Israel and 40 on the right for Judah **[4:4-8]**, whether performed or figurative disputed and the Septuagint transmitting a different first count; mixed-grain bread by weight baked over dung, the fuel relaxed from human to cow dung after he objects on priestly grounds **[4:9-17]**, the only instruction in the book he argues with; head and beard shaved with a sword, the hair divided in three **[5:1-4]**.

## 5. Carried to Jerusalem in visions (592 BC)

- **17 September 592 BC [A to the day]**: sixth year, sixth month, fifth day (Ezekiel 8:1).
- The transport is **visionary, not bodily**, and the text says so: a hand takes him "by a lock of mine head" and brings him "in the visions of God to Jerusalem" **[A, 8:3]**. The three Jerusalem stops in the journey file carry that distinction in `date_confidence`.
- Four abominations **[A, 8:5-16]**: the image of jealousy, seventy elders with censers in a painted chamber, women weeping for Tammuz, twenty-five men worshipping the sun eastward. The inkhorn man marks foreheads and six others kill **[A, 9]**. The glory lifts from the cherub to the threshold, to the east gate, then out to "the mountain which is on the east side" **[A, 10:18-11:23]**; identifying that as the **Mount of Olives** is **[R]**, conventional, not in the text.

## 6. The years between (c. 591-589 BC)

- The exile's baggage carried out through a hand-dug hole in his own house wall, face covered **[A, 12:1-16]**, glossed as the prince going out through a breach and taken in a net to Babylon without seeing it though he dies there. **Josephus** reads this as Zedekiah's blinding, and reports that Ezekiel's word and Jeremiah's looked contradictory until both came true **[A, Antiquities X]**. Josephus also says Ezekiel left "two books"; nothing reachable explains the second.
- Prophets daubing the wall with untempered mortar **[A, 13]**; Noah, Daniel and Job delivering only themselves **[A, 14:1-23]**, that "Daniel" disputed (Ugaritic Danel, or the book's) and not adjudicated; Jerusalem as the exposed newborn raised to a queen **[A, 16]**; the sour-grapes proverb answered by individual responsibility **[A, 18]**, the same proverb at Jeremiah 31:29 with no way to tell which prophet answers which.
- The two eagles and the vine **[A, 17:1-24]**, read as Zedekiah's Egyptian overture in breach of his Babylonian oath. Ezekiel 17:13-19 is the clearest surviving contemporary description of that vassal oath, and `nabucodonosor.journey.json` already cites it.
- **Elders' consultation, 14 August 591 BC [A to the day]**: seventh year, fifth month, tenth day (Ezekiel 20:1).

## 7. The siege, the wife, the fall (589/588-585 BC)

**This is where the supplied research pool contradicted itself worst, and the contradictions are recorded rather than smoothed.**

### 7a. The start of the siege, unresolved, two values

- Ezekiel 24:1-2 **[A to the day as a text]**: "In the ninth year, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month ... this very day. The king of Babylon has laid siege to Jerusalem." 2 Kings 25:1 gives the same day by Zedekiah's regnal year.
- **Value 1: 15 January 588 BC**, Parker and Dubberstein's reckoning from Jehoiachin's exile, given by the pool's geography lens.
- **Value 2: January 589 BC**, from Wikipedia, "Siege of Jerusalem (587 BC)", given by the pool's chronology lens and carried by the existing `nabucodonosor.journey.json` (`-0589-01-10`).
- The pool carried **both**, in a single entry that contradicted its own note. **Not resolved here.** The journey file carries `-0588-01-15` with both values spelled out in `date_confidence`. For the reviewer: the Nebuchadnezzar file and this one will disagree by a year on the same event until the reckoning is ruled on once, corpus-wide.

### 7b. The wife's death, a synchronism that is not in the text

- **[A]** Ezekiel 24:15-27: he is told the desire of his eyes will be taken at a stroke and forbidden the mourning rites; he speaks to the people in the morning, at evening his wife dies, and "I did in the morning as I was commanded" (24:18).
- **[R], and widely repeated as if [A]:** that she dies *on the day the siege begins*. Ezekiel 24:15 opens a fresh oracle formula with no date of its own; the same-day reading is an inference from the chapter's arrangement. *Correction applied:* the pool asserted the synchronism as attested and compressed the obedience into the same evening; the text puts it the next morning. **Gap:** her name, her age, and whether they had children are nowhere recorded.

### 7c. The oracles against the nations

- Ammon, Moab, Edom, Philistia **[A, 25]**, undated as a block. Tyre, three chapters plus the lament over its king **[A, 26-28]**: Ezekiel 26:1 gives "the eleventh year, the first day of the month" with **the month missing from the received Hebrew text**, a real textual gap, giving 587/586 BC and no better. Egypt, "the great dragon that lieth in the midst of his rivers" **[A, 29:1, tenth year, tenth month, twelfth day = 7 January 587 BC]**.

### 7d. The fall of Jerusalem, year unresolved

- **[A]** 2 Kings 25:8-9: Nebuzaradan burns the house of the Lord in the fifth month of Nebuchadnezzar's nineteenth year. Scholarship divides between **587 and 586 BC**, genuinely, not by slip.
- The pool used both years in different lenses and derived *different delay intervals* for the news reaching Babylonia (about six months on a 586 fall, about eighteen on a 587 fall). Neither is adopted. The journey file carries `-0587-07-07` to match `nabucodonosor.journey.json`, with the dispute stated in `date_confidence`. **Consistency with the corpus, not adjudication, is the reason.**
- **Ezekiel is not present.** He is in Babylonia and does not learn of it for months. The stop exists because the burning is the hinge of his life's work, and its pin and date are inherited rather than invented.

### 7e. The fugitive and the opened mouth

- **8 January 585 BC [A to the day]**: twelfth year, tenth month, fifth day (Ezekiel 33:21). **Some manuscripts and some scholars read "eleventh year"**, which would shorten the interval; flagged, not resolved. "The evening before he that was escaped came" the hand of the Lord opens his mouth **[A, 33:22]**, ending the dumbness imposed at 3:26.

## 8. Restoration (after 585 BC)

- The valley of very dry bones **[A, 37:1-14]**, **undated and unplaced**: the text names no valley, and the journey file pins it at Tel Abib because that is where his body is. The **plain of Dura** siting is **[R]**, later folk geography borrowing the Daniel 3 location, coordinates approximate.
- The two sticks joined for Judah and Joseph **[A, Ezekiel 37:15-28]** and Gog of the land of Magog **[A, Ezekiel 38-39]**, both undated.
- **28 April 573 BC [A to the day]**: twenty-fifth year, beginning of the year, tenth day, fourteenth year after the city was smitten (Ezekiel 40:1). The new-Temple vision: the man with the line of flax and the measuring reed, the plan of the sanctuary, the glory returning by the east gate (43:1-5), the river from the threshold (47:1-12), the tribal allotments, the city renamed *YHWH Shammah* (48:35).
  - Internal arithmetic: 573 + 14 = 587 for the fall, one datum in favour of the 587 reading in 7d, though it rests on the same disputed reckoning. Recorded, not decisive.
- **26 April 571 BC [A to the day]**: twenty-seventh year, first month, first day (Ezekiel 29:17), the latest date in the book, an oracle that openly revises the earlier Tyre prophecy by awarding Nebuchadnezzar Egypt as unpaid wages.

## 9. Death, tomb, and afterlife

- **The Hebrew Bible records no death, no burial, no successor.** The book stops. That gap is where the whole tomb tradition grows.
- **[R]** *Lives of the Prophets*: killed by an Israelite prince among the exiles whom he had rebuked for idolatry, buried in Chaldea in a double cave, the cave of Shem and Arpachshad. **[R]** Benjamin of Tudela, relaying local tradition: the exiled Jeconiah built the structure and its dome. **[R]** **Sherira Gaon** (10th century) gives the first known documentary mention of the tomb; the year within that century is not specified anywhere reachable.
- **[A]** Benjamin of Tudela, *Itinerary* (c. 1169-1170): the synagogue of Ezekiel a day from Baghdad, sixty towers with a synagogue between each pair, the ark in the court, the grave behind it, a lamp said never to have gone out, a library said to hold books of the first and second Temple, and **60,000 to 80,000** Jews in the festival weeks.
  - *Correction applied:* the pool gave the 60,000-80,000 figure to **Petachiah of Regensburg**. It is **Benjamin's** (Wikipedia, "Al-Kifl"). Petachiah supplies something else: the shrine's importance to trade and hajj caravans, and the belief that votive deposits there granted children and livestock.
- **[A]** The standing shrine: conical dome on an octagonal drum with four windows, and the adjacent **Al-Nukhailah mosque built under the Ilkhanid ruler Oljeitu in 1316 CE**, the earliest agreed date for elements of the current structure. **Baruch ben Neriah** is among the others venerated there. In Islam the prophet is **Dhu al-Kifl**, and the town is named for him.
- **[A]** Shared Jewish and Muslim custodianship for centuries; over 5,000 Iraqi Jews on annual Passover pilgrimage through the early-to-mid 20th century **[R as to figure and year]**, ending with **Operation Ezra and Nehemiah, 1950-1952**. Restoration **2010-2014** by Iranian companies and the Shi'i Endowment Office at roughly 800 million dinars, after earlier work under UNESCO supervision to 2007.
  - *Correction applied:* the pool dated that redevelopment to **2020** and cited a "Wikipedia, 'Tomb of Ezekiel'" passage for the conversion of the synagogue space to a prayer hall and the replacement of Hebrew inscriptions with Quranic verses. The date is wrong and those assertions are **not** in the Al-Kifl article. Unverified here, and **left unasserted** in the journey file.
- **Merkabah reception [A]**: Ben Sira already singles Ezekiel out for the chariot (Sirach 49:8); **Mishnah Chagigah 2:1** restricts exposition of the Account of the Chariot to one qualified student at a time; the **Hekhalot** corpus (c. 200-700 CE) descends from Ezekiel 1; **Maimonides** reads the merkabah philosophically rather than as ascent, Kabbalists map it onto the Four Worlds, Christian art turns the four creatures into the tetramorph. Feast days: 23 July (Orthodox and Catholic), 28 August (Armenian).
- **Textual note [A]**: the Septuagint Ezekiel is about 8 verses shorter than the Masoretic Text across 1,272 verses, and Qumran fragments show real variants; which recension is prior is unsettled.

## Interlocks with existing atlas travelers

Named in the journey prose, on the strength of a real relation rather than shared ground:

- **nabucodonosor**: Ezekiel names Nebuchadnezzar in his oracles (26:7, 29:17-19), dates his own life by the exile Nebuchadnezzar imposed, and describes the vassal oath Nebuchadnezzar exacted (17:13-19) in terms that file already cites as a source.
- **benjamin_de_tudela**: visited and described the tomb in 1169, and is the source of most of what medieval Europe knew of it. One-directional and centuries late, which the brief admits.
- **newton**: reconstructed the Temple from the cubits of Ezekiel 40-42 in his unpublished chronological manuscripts, treating these measurements as the key to his scheme.

**Rejected** as name-drops (shared ground or era only): `cyrus` (postdates, no attested link), `solomon` and `hiram_abiff` (Ezekiel prophesies about the house Solomon built but never mentions either man), `abraham` (Chaldea in common, nothing else), `adriano`, `saddam_hussein`, `benjamin_netanyahu`, `gustave_flaubert` (Nebuchadnezzar or Babylon echoes, not Ezekiel links).

## Honest gaps

1. No external attestation of the man exists. Every biographical fact is single-source.
2. Birth year, death year, death circumstances and burial are all unattested; the tomb tradition is first documented some fifteen centuries after the life.
3. His wife is unnamed, no children are mentioned, the fate of his household is unknown.
4. The 597 route is entirely reconstructed, and generic to Babylonian practice rather than specific to this column. Tel Abib has never been located on the ground.
5. Josephus says Ezekiel left "two books". Nothing reachable explains the second.
6. The claim that Hebrew inscriptions at al-Kifl were replaced with Quranic verses after 2003 could not be verified and is not asserted. Sherira Gaon's exact date was not recoverable.
