# Pylades of Cilicia: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-29 for `pylades_of_cilicia.journey.json`. Pantomime dancer at Rome; with Bathyllus of Alexandria the maker of the "Italian" dance; teacher and then rival of Hylas. The floruit is fixed at both ends by dated notices: Jerome at 22 BCE, Cassius Dio at 2 BCE. Birth year, death, tomb and legal status are all unrecorded.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

---

## 1. Cilicia: origin, name, and the road to Rome

- **[A, Suda pi 3159]** A Cilician, from a village called Mestarne: `Πυλάδης, Κίλιξ ἀπὸ κώμης Μεσταρνῶν.` Read in full at Suda On Line. *Correction:* earlier notes transliterated the village **Mastarnai**; the manuscript form is `Μεσταρνῶν`, genitive plural, i.e. **Mestarne**.
- **[A, CIL VI 10115 = ILS 5197]** A second, non-literary attestation of the ethnic: the pantomime Theoros lists "Pylades of Cilicia" first among four dancers he claims to have beaten. **[R]** that this is the same man; Pylades was a reusable stage name.
- **Gap.** The village **cannot be located**: it occurs in no other Greek or Latin text, no itinerary, no survey. *Correction to the pool:* "nothing establishes his ethnicity more precisely than Cilician" is too strong. The Suda names a village; what no source does is place it.
- **[R]** Birth date. None given anywhere; "first century BCE" is a modern inference from the Augustan floruit.
- **Gap.** No parents, siblings, spouse, partner or children in any witness; no teacher, school, itinerary, arrival date or first engagement. The record's first datable statement about him is a technical reform, not a debut.
- **[R]** The name. Pylades is Orestes's companion in the tragedians, and imperial pantomimes repeatedly bear mythological names. No source says whether this was a birth name or a professional one; the pattern is a modern observation (Hall, at second hand).
- **[R], unattested rather than denied:** "freedman of Augustus," which reference works say routinely. No manumission record, no tria nomina, no inscription, no ancient text mentioning his legal condition in any direction. A likely mechanism is back-formation from the later imperial freedmen of the name (section 8).

## 2. The making of the Italian dance (c. 22 BCE)

- **[A, Athenaeus 1.20e, citing Aristonicus]** Bathyllus, "together with Pylades, who wrote a treatise on dancing," developed the Italian style out of the comic *kordax*, the tragic *emmeleia* and the satyric *sikinnis*. Fetched directly. **Athenaeus gives no year.**
- **[A, Jerome, Chronicle, Ol. 189.3 = 22 BCE]** "Pylades of Cilicia, a performer of mimes, was the first to have a chorus and piper to perform for him, when his predecessor had sung and danced for themselves." **The one firm date in his record.** The entry names Pylades alone. *Provenance flagged:* not in the supplied pool; it comes from `bathyllus_of_alexandria.journey.json`, which verified it at attalus.org, and was **not refetched**. *What it does not say:* the chorus and piper performed **for him**, not that the dancer was silent.
- **[A, Athenaeus 1.20e]** He wrote a treatise on dancing. **Gap:** no title, fragment, quotation, manuscript or named reader; language, place and date of composition unknown. *Modern error flagged:* the treatise is regularly transferred to Bathyllus.
- **[A, Athenaeus 1.20e]** "Pylades' dancing was solemn, expressing passion and variety of character, whereas Bathyllus' was more jolly." The Greek of the Pylades half is `πολυπρόσωπος`, many-charactered, describing a solo dancer's **range of roles**. *Two corrections to the pool:* the dossier attributed this contrast to **Plutarch, Table Talk 7.8** (it is Athenaeus; the Plutarch citation is dropped), and glossed the style "personnel-heavy" (that belongs to Macrobius 2.7.18).

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Athenaeus contradicts himself on the same page: at 1.20d, on Seleucus's authority, it is **Bathyllus** who first introduced tragic dancing in the Memphite style; at 1.20e Bathyllus is the jollier and Pylades the solemn one. The tidy modern split is contradicted by the text cited for it.

**[R] Debut and venue.** No source records a debut; the modern chronology (Jory, 1981) is an unverified reconstruction, and the journey file borrows 22 BCE from Jerome as a *placement*. No source names a building either; the Theatre of Pompey is used as Rome's only permanent stone theatre from 55 BCE, with the caveat that Marcellus's was already in partial use by 17 BCE.

## 3. Bathyllus, patronage, and an asymmetry in the record

- **[A, Dio 54.17.5]** Bathyllus is "a fellow-artist, and a favourite of Maecenas." **[A, Tacitus, Annals 1.54]** independently: Augustus indulged the entertainment "by way of humouring Maecenas's extravagant passion for Bathyllus." *(Tacitus not refetched.)*
- **Gap, and a correction.** **No source names any patron for Pylades.** The symmetrical modern picture, Pylades to Augustus as Bathyllus to Maecenas, is in neither text; Dio calls him simply "a dancer," and the first thing any source shows Augustus doing to him is expelling him from Italy. The pool carried the symmetry, it is dropped, and the asymmetry is made the point of the stop instead.
- **[A, Seneca the Elder, Controversiae 3 praef. 10]** "Pylades in comoedia, Bathyllus in tragoedia multum a se aberrant." A rhetorician's analogy, presupposing that Romans had watched both men work outside their register and come off badly. No performance, venue or year. *Provenance flagged:* not in the pool, verified by the sibling file, not refetched.
- **[A, Dio 54.17.4]** The rivalry hardened into faction: Dio's word for the ground of the exile is *stasis*. **Gap:** no account of what the partisans did. **[A, Tacitus, Annals 1.54, 1.77]** the same factionalism kills members of the public, several soldiers and a centurion in 15 CE, which is the afterlife of the system and **not biographical testimony about Pylades**.

## 4. Sedition, exile and recall (18 BCE)

- **[A, Dio 54.17.4]** Augustus "restored one Pylades, a dancer, who had been exiled on account of sedition," filed among the concessions that reconciled the public to the strictness of his other regulations.
- **[A] The year is 18 BCE.** Dio 54.18.1 opens the next year with the consulship of Gaius Furnius and Gaius Silanus, consuls of **17 BCE**, so chapter 17 belongs to 18 BCE. *Correction to the pool, resolved rather than left open:* the dossier flagged an unresolved 18-vs-13 BCE conflict. **13 BCE is wrong.** The "Crassus and Lentulus" pairing behind it is doubly wrong: those two were consuls of **14** BCE and belong to Dio **54.24**. The hedge is deleted.
- **[A, Dio 54.17.5]** Rebuked for quarrelling with Bathyllus, he replies: "It is to your advantage, Caesar, that the people should devote their spare time to us." Dio writes in the early third century CE, so **no verbatim value attaches to the reported speech**, and the journey file says so.
- **[A, Suetonius, Divus Augustus 45.4]** "Pylades was expelled from the city and from Italy as well, because by pointing at him with his finger he turned all eyes upon a spectator who was hissing him." Fetched directly. The clause sits in a list of three punishments in one breath, and **Suetonius does not say the three cases are connected.**
- **Gap.** **No source names where he went.** No destination, duration, companions or means of living; the journey file **invents no coordinates for the exile**.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: two exiles, or one?** Dio gives the ground as *stasis* and does not mention the finger. Suetonius gives the finger, does not mention Bathyllus, and supplies no date. The two may be different episodes entirely; neither is used to date or explain the other, and the journey file separates them into two segments.

## 5. The school: Hylas (undated, inside the Augustan period)

All from **Macrobius, Saturnalia 2.7.12-19**, early fifth century CE, four hundred years after the events.

**Retrieval correction.** The pool flagged this whole strand unverifiable ("404s throughout"). **The text is fully retrievable** under `/Thayer/L/` at penelope.uchicago.edu; the failures came from guessing English-directory URLs. Every item below was read in the Latin, so the **[R]** tags applied for inaccessibility are lifted; what remains is the source's date.

- **[A, 2.7.12]** "Hylam discipulum usque ad aequalitatis contentionem eruditione provexit."
- **[A, 2.7.13]** "Populus deinde inter utriusque suffragia divisus est."
- **[A, 2.7.13]** The Agamemnon: Hylas measures out something lofty and huge; Pylades calls from the benches, `Σὺ μακρὸν οὐ μέγαν ποιεῖς`.
- **[A, 2.7.14]** The crowd forces the master onto the floor to dance the same passage; he shows Agamemnon **thinking**, holding that nothing suits a great commander better than to think on behalf of everyone. The only surviving description of Pylades's own body in performance.
- **[A, 2.7.15]** "Saltabat Hylas Oedipodem, et Pylades hac voce securitatem saltantis castigavit: `Σὺ βλέπεις`." *Correction:* the traditional English "You are using your eyes" is **not attested**; the Greek is two words. *Flagged, not resolved:* the target was the dancer's *securitas*, his unconcern. Macrobius **never mentions blindness** and never says which episode was danced; the blind-Oedipus reading is a modern completion.

**Ownership.** These scenes belong to Pylades: he shouts from the benches, he is dragged onto the floor, he dances the passage himself. `hylas.journey.json` stages them from the pupil's side and the two files cross-cite each other. **Date placement discrepancy, declared:** that file puts the taking of the pupil at 20 BCE, this one at 17 BCE, after the securely dated recall. Both are arbitrary, Macrobius gives no year, and both files say so.

## 6. The madman, the bow, and the answer in Homer

- **[A, 2.7.16]** Dancing the mad Hercules he shot arrows at the spectators. Several people objected that he was **not keeping the bearing proper to a performer**, "incessum histrioni convenientem non servare videretur." He pulled off his mask and rebuked the ones who were **laughing**: `Μωροί, μαινόμενον ὀρχοῦμαι`. *Corrections to the pool.* The complaint was not "excess" and came from *nonnulli*, not one spectator; **Augustus did not reprove him**, the rebuke goes to the laughing crowd; and the pool's `quote` field reading "the emperor should not forget he was mad" corresponds to **no utterance in Macrobius** and is deleted. *Duplicate merged:* the pool staged this as two stops; both are 2.7.16-17.
- **[A, 2.7.17]** "Eandem personam cum iussu Augusti in triclinio ageret, et intendit arcum et spicula inmisit. Nec indignatus est Caesar eodem se loco Pyladi quo populum Romanum fuisse." *Correction:* the repeat was at Augustus's **order**, not a private request after a reproof, and Caesar **was not annoyed**.
- **[A, 2.7.18]** Asked what he had contributed to dancing, he answered with Iliad 10.13: `Αὐλῶν συρίγγων τ' ἐνοπὴν ὁμαδόν τ' ἀνθρώπων`. *Correction:* Macrobius reports no question about "the older, simpler manner" and no explanation about Roman spectacle; the answer is a bare Homeric quotation. *Attribution corrected:* the chorus-and-pipes reform is **not in the Suda**, contrary to the dossier; its anchors are Jerome (dated) and, at a stretch, this passage (undated).
- **[A, 2.7.19]** The rivalry with Hylas produced a *populi seditio*; Augustus's anger fell on Pylades, who answered `Καὶ ἀχαριστεῖς, βασιλεῦ· ἔασον αὐτοὺς περὶ ἡμᾶς ἀσχολεῖσθαι`.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: the same scene with two rivals.** Dio 54.17.4-5 has the quarrel with **Bathyllus** and the spare-time reply, in 18 BCE; Macrobius 2.7.19 has the quarrel with **Hylas**, a differently worded reply, and no year. Modern retellings weld the two and manufacture an "exile of Hylas" that **no ancient writer reports**. The journey file keeps them apart and gives the conflict its own stop.

## 7. Old age: the festival of 2 BCE, and the end of the record

- **[A, Dio 55.10.11]** "Pylades, the dancer, gave a festival, though he did not perform any of the work himself, since he was very old, but merely wore the insignia of office and provided the cost of the entertainment." Dio adds that on this occasion **knights and women of rank were brought onto the stage**, and pairs the entry with a festival by the praetor Quintus Crispinus. *Retag, [R] to [A]:* the pool carried this as a "secondary/late tradition" with no primary basis. It is Cassius Dio. *Year fixed at 2 BCE by position:* it stands after the appointment of the first praetorian prefects and before Julia's banishment. **[R] Venue:** Dio names none; the Forum of Augustus was dedicated the same year, so the pairing is apt and otherwise unattested. He was alive, wealthy and conspicuously aged in 2 BCE, so the pool's "if his career continued past 13 BCE" hedge is removed.
- **[A] Theatre chronology, corrected.** Balbus's theatre was **dedicated in 13 BCE**; Marcellus's was **completed in 13 and inaugurated in 12** (notices vary between 13 and 11) and had already housed part of the ludi saeculares in 17. The pool's "both dedicated by Augustus in 13 BCE" is wrong. **No source places Pylades in either building.**
- **Gap, confirmed across all five witnesses.** **No death.** No date, place, cause, retirement, last performance, illness, testament, heir, funeral or tomb. The pool could confirm the absence only in Suetonius and Dio; Macrobius and the Suda were read here and contain nothing either.

## 8. Afterlife of the record

- **[A, Seneca the Younger, Naturales Quaestiones 7.32.3]** "Stat per successores Pyladis et Bathylli domus, harum artium multi discipuli sunt multique doctores; priuatum urbe tota sonat pulpitum." Eighty years on, the two rivals are one noun. *Domus* may mean building, lineage or school; Seneca does not disambiguate. *(Verified by the sibling file, not refetched.)*
- **[A, Suda pi 3159]** The entry preserves the village and garbles the rest: it gives the invention of the Italic dance to **Pylades alone**, dropping Bathyllus, and **reverses** *sikinnis* (given as tragic) and *emmeleia* (given as satyric) against Athenaeus. The Suda On Line editors call it "a confused version of Athenaeus, Deipnosophists 1.20E." **[A, Suda omicron 671]**, from Zosimus, credits Augustus with inventing pantomime and names "Pylades and Bacchylides" as its first practitioners, where the editors note **"Bacchylides" is a corruption of Bathyllos**. Both garbles are flagged in `date_confidence`, not silently corrected.
- **[R] Later bearers of the name.** A performer under Trajan; the imperial freedman **L. Aurelius Pylades** honoured at Puteoli in the later second century CE. **Neither was independently verified**: 404s and connection failures, no epigraphic database reached. Carried as leads and named as unchecked inside the journey file.
- **[R] The Lyon medallion.** A Roman terracotta medallion shown at the **Musee Lugdunum, Lyon**, in "SPECTACULAIRE! Le divertissement chez les Romains," photographed 4 December 2022 and catalogued on Wikimedia Commons as bearing "l'effigie du pantomime Pylade." *Correction:* the pool's attribution to the **Musee Ziem, Martigues**, could not be confirmed and is dropped. Nothing establishes that it depicts the Augustan founder rather than a later performer of the name.
- **Gap.** **No securely identified ancient portrait, statue or relief exists.** Images circulating under the name mostly show the mythological Pylades. **[R]** His modern rediscovery has been philological, not archaeological: Jory and the Hall and Wyles collection make him reformer and systematiser rather than sole inventor, both cited at second hand.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Pylades of Cilicia is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** No apparatus relation was found in either direction and none was deleted. Two things are recorded so the operator can judge them rather than discover them.

1. **The closing segment carries five stops later than his life:** the Theoros inscription, Seneca the Younger, the later name-bearers at Puteoli, the Suda, and the 2022 Lyon exhibition. None stages another person's travel as a scene; each is a stop for a **record of Pylades**, placed where the artifact is, following house practice set by `hylas.journey.json` and `bathyllus_of_alexandria.journey.json`. Under the block rule this is legal, the file's own clock stopping in 2026. If the operator reads the practice differently, these are the five stops to cut.
2. **Nothing was written into any other file.** No forward reference was created in an earlier subject's file.

**Rejected as a non-relation.** The interlock lens suggested checking `virgil.journey.json` for Maecenas material, Virgil having been Maecenas's client in the same city in the same decade. **Maecenas was Bathyllus's patron, not Pylades's**, and no source connects Pylades to that household. Shared city and shared decade are not a relation; Virgil is named nowhere in the journey file's prose, and his file is cited only for the canonical Rome and Palatine pins.

**Named in a campa, and why each is a real relation.** **Hylas**, his pupil and then his public rival, in four scenes where both men are present. **Bathyllus of Alexandria**, co-maker of the Italian dance in Athenaeus, named opposite him in Dio's rebuke, paired with him in both Senecas.
