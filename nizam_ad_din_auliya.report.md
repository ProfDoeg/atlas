# Nizam ad-Din Auliya (c. 1238-1325): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-09 for the Atlas of Journeys. Queued in QUEUE.md line 190 as the middle term of a Chishti cluster: Moinuddin Chishti (researched), Nizam ad-Din Auliya (this file), Amir Khusrow (queued, no file yet).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or hagiography.

The evidence is unusually good and unusually bad at once. Good, because a disciple sat in the room and wrote it down: the *Fawa'id al-Fu'ad* of Amir Hasan Sijzi is a dated, contemporaneous record of his assemblies, and the Bruce B. Lawrence translation (Paulist Press, 1992) was reachable in full through an archive.org djvu-text scan, so every quotation in the journey file is verified to the line. Bad, because almost nothing else is contemporaneous, and the single most famous sentence attributed to him is rejected as improbable by the modern editor of his own discourses. Nothing below has been silently reconciled.

---

## 1. Badaun, 1238-1245

Born at Badaun (Budaun), Uttar Pradesh, into a Sayyid family that fled Bukhara ahead of the Mongols; father Syed Ahmad (variously Abdullah) Bukhari Badayuni, mother Bibi Zulekha. **[A: Wikipedia "Nizamuddin Auliya"; thefamouspeople.com].** Pin 28.0339 / 79.1198.

Birth year: **contradiction, not resolved.** 1238 is commonest; 1237 (635 AH) is also given, with the Wikipedia article noting the discrepancy; a minority tradition gives 1244. The two lenses that gathered this pool frame the live alternatives differently, one as 634 vs 636 AH, the other as 1238 vs 1244. Both recorded; neither adopted.

Father dies when the boy is about five **[A]**. The tomb compound at Iltutmish's Shamsi Jama Masjid (28.0386 / 79.1219) is said to hold father and grandfather **[R: thefamouspeople.com; livehistoryindia.com]**.

Early schooling under Maulana Alauddin Usuli: seven modes of Quranic recitation, Arabic grammar, hadith, tafsir, mathematics, astronomy **[R]**. *Correction:* one pool item placed this in Delhi and tagged it [A]. Usuli is consistently "of Badayun," thefamouspeople.com puts the schooling at Badaun before the move, and the Wikipedia article cited alongside names no teacher at all. Placed at Badaun, tagged [R].

## 2. Delhi, the Qutb quarter

Bibi Zulekha takes her son to Delhi **[A that it happens; the date is a live contradiction]**. Wikipedia has it immediately after the father's death, c. 1243, at age five; iloveindia.com dates it to 1260, when he is in his twenties finishing hadith. Both cannot be true and the record consulted does not choose. The stop is dated 1243 with the contradiction in `date_confidence`.

*Coordinate correction:* the pool twice pinned "Delhi" at 28.6139 / 77.209, which is Connaught Place, laid out in the 1930s. Every generic-Delhi stop uses **28.5244 / 77.1855**, the pin the atlas assigns to the Qutb complex in `moinuddin_chishti.journey.json` and `ibn_battuta.journey.json`.

He trains in the teaching circles near the Qutb quarter **[A: Wikipedia]**. A traditional visit to the tomb of Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki at Mehrauli is **[R]**: Kaki died 1 December 1235, three years before Nizam ad-Din's birth, so only a tomb visit is possible. The pool gave 28.5165 / 77.1853, about 600 m off; the file uses **28.5193 / 77.1799**, byte-identical to the `moinuddin_chishti` pin for the same tomb.

## 3. Ajodhan

At twenty he goes to Ajodhan, now Pakpattan Sharif, Pakistan, and takes initiation from Fariduddin Ganjshakar **[A: Wikipedia; auqaf.punjab.gov.pk]**. Pin 30.3411 / 73.3875 (Wikipedia gives 30.34417 / 73.38389; the ~450 m difference is accepted).

**Year contradiction:** iloveindia.com says 1257; Wikipedia and auqaf.punjab.gov.pk say "age twenty," which from a 1238 birth gives 1258. The file uses 1258 and names 1257 in `date_confidence`.

**Silsila rank corrected.** One pool item called Baba Farid "fourth in the Chishti silsila from Moinuddin Chishti." He is third: Moinuddin Chishti > Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki > Fariduddin Ganjshakar > Nizam ad-Din Auliya. Confirmed via Wikipedia "Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki," consistent with `moinuddin_chishti.journey.json`.

**"Seven months and some days"** in Farid's company on the first visit: **source misattributed.** auqaf.punjab.gov.pk was fetched directly and contains only "while traveling to Ajodhan, Farid met the 20-year-old Nizamuddin Auliya." The detail is not on that page; no replacement found.

Annual Ramadan returns, three within Farid's lifetime **[A: Wikipedia]**; auqaf.punjab.gov.pk raises the lifetime total to ten, seven of them to the grave **[R]**. Khilafat on the third visit, with the turban said to have come down from Chisht, and the saying "You will be a tree under whose soothing shade people will find comfort" **[A: Wikipedia; sufinama.org]**.

**Investiture date: one leg is impossible.** The pool carried "2 Rabi al-Awwal 656 AH (1258)" credited to auqaf.punjab.gov.pk, a page containing no AH dates at all. It converts to c. 9 March 1258, which falls on the **first** visit, while every account puts khilafat on the **third**. Dropped; c. 1265 used.

**Baba Farid's death: contradiction, not resolved.** Wikipedia "Baba Farid" and auqaf.punjab.gov.pk give c. 7 May 1266, aged 78; a minority chronology gives 5 Muharram 664 AH = 17 October 1265, a sound conversion whose only carrier here is grokipedia.com. Stop dated 1266-05-07, with 1265 in `date_confidence`.

## 4. Ghiyaspur

He prays to be shown where to live and is answered. One of the very few episodes with a surviving first-person account, in Fascicle IV, Assembly 19, Wednesday 27 Sha'ban 715 AH (26 November 1315), p. 244: the prayer is made at the Rani Reservoir in the Jasrat Garden, a voice answers "GHIYASPUR," and he adds that he had never seen the place and did not know where it was. **[A, verified line by line].**

He settles at Ghiyaspur c. 1266 and raises a hut **[A for the settlement: Wikipedia; R for the straw roof: travelogyindia.com]**. Pin 28.5905 / 77.2431. He lives there close to sixty years through the reigns of seven sultans, running a khanqah whose open kitchen draws Hindus and Muslims, nobles and laborers **[A/R mixed: Wikipedia for the reach across classes, tourmyindia.com for the sixty-year framing]**.

Muizuddin Qaiqabad founds a palace-city, Kilokhri or Kilugarhi, immediately adjacent, in 1287 **[R]**. *Source correction:* the pool credited Wikipedia "Ghiyaspur," which on direct fetch mentions neither Kilokhri nor Qaiqabad and attributes the village's name to Ghiyasuddin Balban. Historically sound but presently uncited.

Amir Khusrow, born Patiyali 1253, enters the circle **[A for the relationship; the year is unfixed and the pool's 1272 is unsupported]**. Wikipedia "Amir Khusrau" says 1310, at age fifty-seven; hagiography says age eight, c. 1261, impossible since Ghiyaspur is only settled c. 1266; 1272 is the year of his court appointment, not an initiation. The file uses 1272 as a placeholder and carries all three.

## 5. The assemblies, 1307-1322

Amir Hasan Sijzi records the evening discourses; the compilation becomes the *Fawa'id al-Fu'ad*, the first malfuzat of its kind in India **[A: Wikipedia; the text itself]**. Fascicle I assemblies are dated in the text: Assembly 1 on 3 Sha'ban 707 AH / 27 January 1308, Assembly 2 on 8 Sha'ban / 4 February, Assembly 3 on 15 Sha'ban / 11 February, Assembly 5 on Friday 5 Ramadan / 2 March, Assembly 6 on Friday 5 Shawwal / 30 March. **Page corrections were required on five of the twelve pool quotations**, all in the same direction: the folio reported one page early.

| Quotation | Pool page | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| "He who strives to become famous during his lifetime..." | 81 | **82** |
| "Fie! ... he has been disrespectful" | 81 | **82** |
| "To perfect his soul, man must do four things..." | 82 | **83** |
| "Whoever achieves his objective through virtuous action..." | 86 | **87** |
| "Whatever you do not find agreeable for yourself..." | 87 | **88** |
| "I must leave this city..." | 243, "later fascicle" | **244**, Fascicle IV Assembly 19 |
| "Our Khusrau is like a saltish river..." | Lawrence intro, 41 | **Nizami intro, 44** |
| "Amir Khusrau ... offered to exchange" | Lawrence intro, 24 | **Nizami intro, 4** |

Four pool citations checked out unchanged: the penitent passage (Assembly 1, p. 81), the slavery passage (Assembly 2, p. 83), the elect passage (Assembly 3, p. 84), the renunciation passage (Assembly 6, p. 88). The last two table rows are attribution errors, not page slips.

**The introduction is not Lawrence's.** The title page reads: translated and annotated by Bruce B. Lawrence, **introduction by Khaliq Ahmad Nizami**, preface by Simon Digby. Two pool quotations credited to "Lawrence's introduction" are Nizami's. The onward citation "Siyar al-Awliya, p. 107" attached to one of them is unverified, the superscript markers being illegible in the OCR. The file cites Nizami, Introduction, p. 44, and stops there.

**The Khusrau-exchange line was mis-completed** with an invented bracket, "[places with him]," reversing the sense. Actual sentence, Nizami Introduction p. 4: "While mystics like Maulana 'Ala ad-din Nili kept themselves busy in reading it, Amir Khusrau, the famous poet, offered to exchange all his works for it." He offered to trade his oeuvre for the book, not places with anyone. It is also the modern editor's prose, not a saying of either man, and the file labels it so.

Two verified quotations did not fit the stop budget: the four littles (Assembly 1, p. 83) and the rule of Khwaja Ajall Shirazi (Assembly 6, p. 88, where Lawrence's parentheses mark a supplied word the pool dropped).

## 6. The sultans

Through Alauddin Khalji's reign (1296-1316) he never appears at court and never receives a sultan in person **[R: secondary synthesis; no court record read]**. No source places him at a Khalji court site **[A as an absence]**.

The Jamat Khana Masjid, red sandstone, three bays, is built by Khizr Khan under Alauddin Khalji, completed 1312-1313 **[A: Wikipedia "Nizamuddin Dargah"]**. *Correction:* the pool's geography lens dated it 1315-1325, impossible, since Khizr Khan was imprisoned and blinded in 1316. Muhammad bin Tughlaq added the flanking domed halls c. 1325, likely the source of the confusion. More than six hundred khalifas are authorized across his life **[A]**.

Under Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah (r. 14 April 1316 to July 1320), attested verbatim: "Mubarak Shah also constructed a mosque, the Masjid-i Miri, where all the Sufis and ulama were ordered to perform their prayers. The Shaikh refused to comply." Two pool details do **not** survive checking: that the sultan "prohibited nobles from visiting Ghiyaspur" (in neither relevant article) and that the shaikh "sent a delegate instead" (absent; the article says only that he refused, "remonstrating that the mosque in his neighborhood had a greater claim on him"). What the Mubarak Shah article attests instead is a personal summons under threat of executive order at the start of the next lunar month.

"Refusing to heed the threat, the Shaikh quietly prayed at his mother's tomb and returned to his jama'at-khana" **[A, verbatim]**. *Date corrected from 1319 to 1320:* the ultimatum named the next lunar month and the sultan died on 1 Jumada II, so the confrontation falls in the weeks before the July 1320 assassination. Mubarak Shah is murdered and beheaded by Khusrau Khan in a palace revolt; **date contradiction**, 8 July 1320 in Wikipedia "Nizamuddin Auliya" against 9 July 1320 in Wikipedia "Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah."

## 7. Tughlaqabad

Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq founds the dynasty in 1320; the relationship, initially cordial, deteriorates. Wikipedia's phrase is "opinion disharmony," offered without documentary specifics **[A that it soured; R as to why]**.

The baoli is completed in 1321 **[A: Wikipedia "Nizamuddin Dargah"]**. Everything attached to it is legend: the ban on working after hours, the cut-off oil supply, the masons lighting their lamps with blessed water **[R: millenniumpost.in, homegrown.co.in, enrouteindianhistory.com]**, and the pool tagged this [A] in one lens and [R] in two others. It is [R], as is the curse, "ya rahe ujar, ya base gujar." Tughlaqabad Fort is at **28.51194 / 77.26083**; the pool's 77.2818 falls about 2 km east of the walls. The fort was abandoned in 1327, six years after founding.

**"Hunuz Dilli dur ast": [R], and the modern authority rejects the episode.** The pool guessed Siyar al-Awliya or Barani's Tarikh-i Firuz Shahi; both guesses are wrong. Khaliq Ahmad Nizami, Introduction to *Morals for the Heart*, p. 37, says the Bengal message and "the Shaykh's alleged remark 'Delhi is still far off,' both seem highly improbable," and his endnote 145 names the earliest carrier as Yahya Sirhindi's *Tarikh-i Mubarak Shahi*, p. 96. Kept in the journey file as the later legend it is, with the rejection on the face of the stop.

Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq dies **1 February 1325** when a wooden reception pavilion collapses on him and on his son Prince Mahmud Khan **[A: Wikipedia "Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq"; corroborated by "Muhammad bin Tughluq," recording Muhammad's accession on 4 February, "three days after the death of his father"]**. **Place contradiction:** the Wikipedia infobox says Kara-Manikpur; the Ibn Battuta-derived account says Afghanpur near Delhi, and Ibn Battuta alleged the collapse was engineered by Jauna Khan, the future Muhammad bin Tughlaq. *Correction applied twice:* the pool claimed Nizam ad-Din outlived the sultan "by only days." The interval is roughly two months.

## 8. Death and shrine

He dies on the morning of **3 April 1325 CE = 18 Rabi al-Thani 725 AH**, aged eighty-six or eighty-seven **[A; the Hijri conversion checks]**. *Correction:* the afterlife lens gave "12 Rabi al-Awwal 725 AH," converting to c. 27 February, six weeks early and a different month. He dies at the khanqah at Ghiyaspur, **28.5911 / 77.2422**, not at the Connaught Place coordinate the pool twice supplied.

Buried at the khanqah; the village is renamed after him **[A]**. **Who first built over the grave is a contradiction:** tourmyindia.com and Wikipedia "Nizamuddin Dargah" credit Muhammad bin Tughlaq with the main structure in 1325; Wikipedia "Nizamuddin Auliya" dates the standing shrine to 1562. Firuz Shah Tughlaq repairs it and suspends four golden cups from the recesses of the dome **[A, undated]**. The present marble dome, roughly 6 m across, is built by Faridun Khan in 1562 under Akbar **[A]**.

Amir Khusrow tears his clothes and covers himself in dust on hearing of the death **[R: sufinama.org, newageislam.com]**. He dies in October 1325 and is buried at his master's feet **[A]**, Nizam ad-Din having said that were it permitted by sharia he would have wished him in the same grave **[A]**. **Khusrow as father of qawwali is [R], not [A]:** Wikipedia "Amir Khusrau" calls him "regarded as the father of qawwali," a traditional attribution with no contemporary primary attestation, and the pool tagged the identical claim [A] in one lens and [R] in another.

Nasiruddin Mahmud, Chiragh-e-Dehli, is the designated successor and the last of the great early Delhi Chishtis **[A]**. *Siyar al-Awliya* is written by Amir Khwurd (Sayyid Muhammad Mubarak Kirmani), a disciple distinct from Khusrow, around the 1360s **[A: Academia.edu; Internet Archive Urdu editions]**.

More than seventy people are buried in the complex, including Jahanara Begum and the emperor Muhammad Shah **[A]**; Mirza Ghalib lies nearby in the Loharu family plot, his tomb restored by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture with the ASI in 2009 **[R]**. The Nizamuddin Urban Renewal Initiative, from a partnership signed 11 July 2007, conserved sixty-plus monuments and over two hundred acres, and won UNESCO Asia-Pacific Awards on 1 December 2021 **[R]**. The urs is kept on 17-18 Rabi al-Thani, Khusrow's separately on 18 Shawwal **[A]**.

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. **Birth year:** 1238 / 1237 (635 AH) / 1244, the two lenses framing the alternatives differently.
2. **Move to Delhi:** age five, c. 1243, vs 1260 in his twenties. Mutually exclusive.
3. **Initiation at Ajodhan:** 1257 vs 1258.
4. **Baba Farid's death:** c. 7 May 1266 vs 17 October 1265.
5. **Khusrow's initiation:** c. 1261 (impossible), 1272 (his court appointment), 1310 at fifty-seven.
6. **Mubarak Shah's death:** 8 vs 9 July 1320, between two Wikipedia articles.
7. **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq's death place:** Afghanpur near Delhi vs Kara-Manikpur.
8. **Who built the shrine:** Muhammad bin Tughlaq in 1325 vs the standing structure of 1562.

## Gaps, stated as gaps

- **No hajj.** No source consulted records him leaving the Badaun-Delhi-Ajodhan triangle, and none records a journey to Mecca. The absence is the finding. The canonical Kaaba pin (21.4225, 39.8262), reused across `moinuddin_chishti`, `ibn_battuta`, `attar`, and `rumi`, is **deliberately not inherited** here.
- **No westward travel.** The Temple Mount pin (31.778, 35.2354) from `ibn_battuta.journey.json` is checked and excluded; neither Nizam ad-Din nor Khusrow is attested west of Delhi. Paris and Buenos Aires were also checked for any Chishti, Khusrow, or qawwali connection: zero hits.
- **The Balban and Alauddin Khalji court relationships** rest entirely on secondary synthesis. No court record has been read.
- ***Siyar al-Awliya* was not fetched**, so every claim routed through it comes through a modern citation.
- **The "seven months and some days"** of the first Ajodhan stay is not on the page it was credited to, and no replacement source was found.

## Interlocks with existing atlas files

- **`moinuddin_chishti.journey.json`** is load-bearing. Its stop "Delhi, the khirqa to Baba Farid" (28.5193, 77.1799, 1214) names both Nizam ad-Din Auliya and Amir Khusrow and frames the transmission as one bodily act running Moinuddin to Farid to Nizam ad-Din to Khusrow. Its stop on Kaki's death calls Mehrauli "the anchor of the Delhi line"; Nizam ad-Din inherits that line two links on, and his grave four miles east replaces it as the anchor. Both pins inherited byte-identical.
- **`ibn_battuta.journey.json`.** Its "Delhi, qadi of the capital" stop (28.5244, 77.1855, 1334) begins nine years after Nizam ad-Din's death, inside the reign of the sultan whose accession year is the year the saint died. The Moinuddin file already notes that "a hundred and twenty years later Ibn Battuta will sit as qadi of this city" in a Delhi where the Chishti shrines are the ones the sultans go to. **[R]:** no atlas file states Ibn Battuta visited the dargah, and the Rihla was not checked here.
- **`ibrahim_ibn_adham.journey.json`.** Its sole support for a contested Fudayl ibn Iyad discipleship claim is "Siyar ul Auliya i Chisht, 1884 Delhi reprint," which is Amir Khwurd's *Siyar al-Awliya*, compiled inside Nizam ad-Din's own khanqah circle. The weight the atlas leans on for an eighth-century Sufi traces back to a text produced in this man's house six centuries later, a provenance the existing file does not explain.
- **Two homonym false leads, flagged so nobody chases them.** The "Nizam Gate" at Ajmer in `moinuddin_chishti.journey.json` is the Hyderabad Nizam's title. `nizami.journey.json` covers Nizami of Ganja, d. c. 1209, sharing only a near-homonym.
- **`amir_khusrow`** does not exist yet. QUEUE.md line 191 has him. Roughly a quarter of this file's stops are also his.
