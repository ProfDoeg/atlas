# Hans Christian Andersen (1805-1875): research report

*2026-08-04. Odense to Copenhagen; thirty journeys abroad; buried at Assistens Kirkegård, Nørrebro.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or biographers' framing. Contradictions are flagged where found and **not adjudicated silently**: where this report prefers a side it says which and why, and leaves the other visible.

The instrument deciding most of what follows is the **H.C. Andersen Centre year-by-year timetable** (andersen.sdu.dk/liv/tidstavle), a day-level chronology built on the diaries, letters, and almanacs. Where it disagrees with English Wikipedia it wins here, and the disagreement is recorded rather than erased: the English article carries at least three widely propagated date errors.

## 1. Odense, 1805-1819

**Birth.** 2 April 1805, Odense, to the shoemaker Hans Andersen and the washerwoman Anne Marie Andersdatter **[A: Wikipedia; hcandersen-homepage.dk]**.

**The baptism, corrected.** The English formula "baptized at Saint Hans Church on 15 April 1805" is wrong in a way worth keeping. Danish Wikipedia has him baptised **at home on 2 April**, his birthday, because he was not expected to live; the 15 April ceremony at Sankt Hans Kirke was the **publicering**, the public confirmation of that home baptism **[A: da.wiki]**. Both dates are real and name different acts.

**The birthplace, and a museum-level contradiction.** Two Odense addresses are constantly conflated. **Hans Jensens Stræde 45 / Bangs Boder** is the corner house held to be the birthplace, museum since 1908, anchor of the H.C. Andersens Hus complex, roughly **55.3986, 10.3907** **[A: Odense City Museums; en.wiki 55°23′58″N 10°23′31″E]**. **Munkemøllestræde 3-5** is a *different* site, the **childhood home** from age two to fourteen, museum since 1930, roughly **55.3947, 10.3849** **[A]**. The pool erred both ways, labelling Munkemøllestræde the birthplace and elsewhere giving Hans Jensens Stræde a coordinate (55.3959, 10.3833) that lands on Flakhaven, 600 m off. Both corrected; the journey file pins both houses, labelled. Separately, biographers note Andersen gave varying accounts, so even the Hans Jensens Stræde attribution is town tradition **[R: Wullschlager 2000]**.

**The father.** Hans Andersen introduced the boy to the puppet theatre, to Holberg and *The Arabian Nights*, and died in 1816 **[A: hcandersen-homepage.dk]**. Anne Marie remarried in 1818 **[A: Wikipedia]**. Charity school; brief apprenticeships to a weaver and a tailor **[A: Wikipedia]**, undateable from reachable sources and therefore not pinned.

**Odense Castle, reversed.** The pool had Andersen singing for the royal family in 1819 and "sent off with encouragement." The Centre's 1819 timetable **records no such audience at all** **[A: negative finding]**. The tradition, where one exists, runs the other way: an audience with **Prince Christian Frederik** (later Christian VIII), then governor of Funen, who advised the boy to learn a respectable trade **[R]**. Tag kept, sense reversed: the framing was wrong-signed, not only the date.

**The departure.** Left Odense **4 September 1819**, reached Copenhagen **6 September** with 13 rigsdaler **[A: Centre, 1819]**. His mother's prediction that he would turn back at Nyborg and his prayer behind a shed on the Zealand shore are both verbatim in *The True Story of My Life* **[A: Gutenberg #7007]**, and are used as quote fields.

## 2. Copenhagen, 1819-1822: the inverted sequence

The claim that Andersen moved to Copenhagen "having been accepted at the Royal Danish Theatre for his soprano voice" is **false and inverts cause and effect** **[A: Centre, 1819]**. The order: he arrived 6 September with no position; he improvised a grotesque ballet for the dancer Madame Schall and, in the Centre's phrase, "was then thrown out"; director **Holstein** turned him down; only in **mid-September** did **Giuseppe Siboni**, new head of the theatre's singing school, offer him lessons and meals. Acceptance followed the move. The false version turns a leap into an appointment.

**Voice breaks, 1822** **[A: hcandersen-homepage.dk]**; he turns to submitting plays. First printed story, *The Ghost at Palnatoke's Grave*, 1822 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 3. School, 1822-1828

**Slagelse: date corrected by a year.** English chronologies give 1823. Andersen accepted the board's offer **13 September 1822** and arrived **26 October 1822** **[A: Centre, 1822; da.wiki]**, on **350 rigsdaler a year for three years from the royal trust fund *ad usus publicos***, arranged by **Jonas Collin**, approved by **Frederik VI** **[A]**.

**Helsingør, 1826-1827** under the same rector, **Simon Meisling** **[A: Wikipedia; hcandersen-homepage.dk]**. Andersen later called these the darkest years of his life. Meisling's verdict on the poem *The Dying Child* ("sentimentality and idle trash") is verbatim in the autobiography **[A: Gutenberg #7007]**.

**Kronborg** is geographic inference only: the castle stands within a mile of the school, and **no primary account of Andersen entering it in this period was located** **[R, stated as a gap]**. **Student examination, 1828** **[A: hcandersen-homepage.dk]**, entrance to the University of Copenhagen.

## 4. First success and first journeys, 1829-1831

*A Journey on Foot from Holmen's Canal to the East Point of Amager*, 1829, his first substantial success **[A: Wikipedia; hcandersen-homepage.dk]**. Month and day not established; the journey file defaults to 01-01 and says so in `date_confidence`.

**Riborg Voigt, 1830, Faaborg** **[R]**. The unrequited attachment is attested; the pouch-around-the-neck tradition is repeated by Danish biography and unverifiable in its detail. Kept as tradition, not asserted.

**The Harz journey: month corrected.** The pool gave June 1831; the Centre gives departure **16 May 1831**, Hamburg 19 May, Dresden 3 June, home 24 June **[A]**. A May-to-June journey, made partly to get over Riborg Voigt, producing *Skyggebilleder fra en Reise til Harzen* (1831). Brocken and Dresden rest on that book; **Lüneburg and Braunschweig are route reconstructions from it [R]**, dropped for space.

## 5. The grand tour, 1833-1834

Royal travelling stipend. Confirmed leg dates from the Centre's 1833 timetable **[A]**:

**Paris**, 10 May to 15 August 1833, Heine met 25 June, Hugo visited 14 August. **Le Locle**, Jura, 25 August to 14 September, part two of *Agnete og Havmanden* posted home 12 September. **Rome**, **18 October 1833** to 12 February 1834, Thorvaldsen met, *The Improvisatore* begun 27 December.

**Two coordinate errors corrected.** Paris was given as 48.853, **12.3499**, in Bavaria near Straubing, 800 km off, a digit slip for 2.3499; the journey file uses the canonical Paris pin **48.8566, 2.3522**. Le Locle's coordinate was right, but the Houriets were **Swiss** watchmakers, not Danish, the Copenhagen connection running through Jules Houriet **[A]**.

**The Rome contradiction, resolved with the losing side left visible.** English Wikipedia gives **October 1834** and the pool propagated it twice; the Centre gives **18 October 1833**, with the whole subsequent chronology hanging off it consistently. The Centre is followed and Wikipedia's date called an error, not an alternative. **Andersen's mother died 7 October 1833**, during this leg.

**Milan and Florence** are route reconstructions **[R]**; both dropped from the journey file for length, not doubt. **Vesuvius and Pompeii** (1834) **[A]** are merged into one stop.

**Sestri Levante.** An evening's lodging in 1833 **[A]**. The **Baia delle Favole** naming is a **posthumous civic honour**, not a contemporary event, and the journey file says so rather than implying he named it.

## 6. The tales, 1835-1846

- *The Improvisatore*, 1835 **[A]**; month not fixed, defaulted.
- *Eventyr, fortalte for Børn*, first booklet **8 May 1835**, **C. A. Reitzel, Copenhagen**: The Tinderbox, Little Claus and Big Claus, The Princess and the Pea, Little Ida's Flowers; reviews initially negative **[A: Wikipedia]**. The pool gave the place as "Odense/Copenhagen"; **Odense has no part in it** and is struck.
- Second booklet **16 December 1835** (Thumbelina, The Traveling Companion, The Naughty Boy) **[A]**.
- Third booklet **7 April 1837**: **The Little Mermaid**, **The Emperor's New Clothes** **[A]**. The pool put these in the 1835 installments; they were not.
- *Billedbog uden Billeder*: pool gave 1840, en.wiki's works list **December 1839** **[A]**. Corrected; dropped from the journey file for length.

**Nysø Manor: coordinate corrected by 13 km.** The pool gave 55.2306, 12.1236, NNE of the estate near the coast; Danish Wikipedia (*Nysø*) gives **55.12667, 12.03056** **[A]**. Hosts **Baroness Christine Stampe** and **Baron Henrik Stampe**, owners 1826-1876, with **Thorvaldsen** in a garden studio built for him **[A]**. **Glorup** on Funen **[A]** is dropped for length.

## 7. *A Poet's Bazaar*, 1840-1841

Trieste (1840), Valletta (1841), Athens, Constantinople, the Danube and the Iron Gates, Vienna: all rest on *En Digters Bazar* (1842) **[A]**. Athens arrival **24 March 1841** confirmed **[A: H.C. Andersen Centre, 1841]**. Valletta is dropped from the journey file for length.

**One superlative cut.** The pool called Andersen "the first Dane of his literary stature to describe classical Athens firsthand for a Danish readership." **No reachable source supports this**, and the claim is either trivially true by narrowing "of his literary stature" or false. Cut, not softened.

## 8. Fame, 1844-1857

**Berlin and Jacob Grimm: date corrected, framing reversed.** The pool placed the meeting in 1845-46, with Andersen preceded by German celebrity. The Centre has him in Berlin **23-31 July 1844**, calling on Grimm **26 July**, and records that **Grimm "had never heard of HCA"** **[A]**. The celebrity Berlin season comes *after*, in 1845-46. Second wrong-signed pool item, after Odense Castle.

**Weimar, 1844**: friendship with the hereditary Grand Duke **Carl Alexander**, later Knight of the White Falcon **[A: Wikipedia; the Carl Alexander letters]**.

**London, June 1847**: first meeting with **Charles Dickens** at **Gore House**, Kensington, at the **Countess of Blessington's** party **[A]**. English translations by **Mary Howitt** from 1846, *Wonderful Stories for Children*, with the standing criticism that early English versions were loose, moralising, and sometimes re-ended **[A: Zipes 2005]**.

**Trollhättan, 1849**, in *I Sverrig* (1851) **[A]**. **Nyhavn 67, 1845-1864** **[A]**, memorial plaque on the canal front.

**Gads Hill Place, 1857: coordinate corrected, closing clause weakened.** The pool gave 51.4 / 0.45 in one lens and 51.3866 / 0.455 in another, the latter 2.7 km south of the house; Wikipedia gives **51.4112, 0.4579** **[A]**, and confirms he was invited for two weeks and stayed five. The pool said the two never corresponded again. What is supportable is weaker and stranger: **Dickens simply stopped answering his letters** **[A]**.

## 9. Late years, 1861-1875

**Harald Scharff: year corrected.** The pool gave 1861. Per the Centre's **1862** timetable the friendship, with a man Andersen had known since 1857, began in **February 1862**; **Theodor Collin** reproached him **17 February**; he spoke of "my erotic time" to **Einar Drewsen** **5 March** **[A]**; it ended **13 November 1863** **[A]**. Kept **[R]** as to what it *was*: "erotic period" is biographers quoting Andersen, and the diaries settle nothing further.

**Spain, 1862** (*I Spanien*, 1863): Madrid and Granada **[A]**, Toledo **[R]**.

**Portugal, 1866: the wrong O'Neill.** The pool put Jorge and José O'Neill at Setúbal. Per the Centre's 1866 timetable **[A]**: **Carlos O'Neill** at Setúbal, **Quinta dos Bonecos**, 8 June to 9 July; **George (Jorge) O'Neill**, Danish consul-general, in Lisbon at **Quinta do Pinheiro**, Palhava, 6 May to 8 June and 9-26 July; **José O'Neill** at Cintra, 26 July to 8 August. The "known about forty years" detail holds. *Et Besøg i Portugal 1866* appeared in **1868**.

**Odense honorary citizenship, 6 December 1867**, with torchlit celebration, and the **Paris Exposition Universelle, 1867** **[A]**.

**1872, split.** The pool ran two claims together. Holds: the last new tales appeared in 1872 (*Eventyr og Historier. Ny Samling*, 30 March; *Nye Eventyr og Historier*, 23 November) **[A: Centre, 1872]**; he fell from his bed and never fully recovered **[A]**; he was gravely ill that November and December **[A]**. Does not hold: **liver cancer was not apparent in 1872**, but emerged in **1874-75** **[A]**, where the journey file keeps it.

**Death, 4 August 1875**, at **Rolighed**, the Melchiors' country house near Copenhagen **[A]**. The villa is demolished and the coordinate (55.708, 12.585) is a reconstruction, tagged as such in `date_confidence`.

**Burial: coordinate corrected.** The pool offered three Assistens coordinates (55.6889/12.5522, 55.6893/12.5474, and a "55.6942" buried in a note about Rolighed). Danish Wikipedia puts the cemetery at **55.6908, 12.5494**, plot **section P, no. 513**; the journey file uses **55.6906, 12.5479**. The stone originally carried **three names**, Andersen's and **Edvard and Henriette Collin's** **[A: Wullschlager 2000]**, read by biographers as a last statement of his unreciprocated attachment to Edvard **[R]**.

**Headstone moved to Frederiksberg's older cemetery, 1914** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Not used: the claim is thin and its relation to the section P plot goes unexplained in the source, which is itself a gap.

## 10. Afterlife

**The Little Mermaid statue**, Edvard Eriksen, commissioned by **Carl Jacobsen** of Carlsberg after a ballet on the tale, unveiled **23 August 1913** at Langelinie **[A]**. Vandalism record, corrected in one detail **[A: Wikipedia, *The Little Mermaid (statue)*]**: head sawn off **24 April 1964** and **never recovered**, a new one cast from the original mould (the pool wrongly had it recovered each time); attempted beheading **1990**, an 18 cm neck wound; right arm severed **22 July 1984**, back in two days; head taken **6 January 1998**, returned anonymously and reattached **4 February**; blown off its base **10 September 2003**.

Also **[A]**: **August Saabye's seated bronze**, Kongens Have, **1880**; the **Hans Christian Andersen Award (IBBY) from 1956**; **H.C. Andersens Hus** rebuilt by **Kengo Kuma**, reopened **June 2021**; the **2005 bicentenary** and the Jeffrey Frank / Diane Crone Frank translation.

**The nobleman legend**, that Andersen was the illegitimate son of a nobleman or of royal blood, is **[R]**: no documentary evidence, treated by Wullschlager and Bredsdorff as self-mythologising.

## Honest gaps

- **No day or month** for: the apprenticeships; *A Journey on Foot* (1829); *The Improvisatore* (1835); Riborg Voigt (1830); the Jenny Lind attachment, which has no single dateable event and gets no stop.
- **Kronborg**: proximity only, no primary account of entry.
- **Rolighed**: villa demolished, coordinate reconstructed, not surveyed.
- ***Mit Livs Eventyr* (1855) was not read** (see Sources). Everything quoted verbatim here from "the autobiography" comes from the **1847** *True Story of My Life* (Gutenberg #7007), a **different and much shorter book**.
- **"At reise er at leve."** **[R]**. The pool called it possibly a misattribution because it was absent from the fetched autobiography; **that reasoning is wrong**, since the fetched text is the *other* autobiography. hcandersen-homepage.dk (page_id=23932) locates the line in ***Mit Livs Eventyr* (1855)**, under 1844, right after the Jenny Lind passage. Cite that book, and note Andersen's own orthography, **"At reise er at leve"**, not the modernised "At rejse". The book being unreachable, this rests on a secondary locator and stays **[R]**.
- **Edvard Collin letters.** The much-quoted line ("I languish for you as for a pretty Calabrian wench... my sentiments for you are those of a woman") was seen only at Wikipedia footnote 32, no translator or edition examined. **[R]**, not used as a `quote` field.
- **Jenny Lind letter** and the **deathbed funeral-music instruction**: Wikipedia secondary quotation only, **[R]**. The funeral-music line is used as a quote field with its R status stated in the quote source, being the only surviving utterance attached to the death.

## Corpus interlocks used in the journey file

Named in campa where paths genuinely cross, with the corpus pin inherited byte-identical where the interlock lens found one. **fichte** and **gauss** at the student-examination stop, canonical Copenhagen pin **55.6761, 12.5683**: Fichte fled to Copenhagen c. 1806-07 while Andersen was an infant in Odense, and the university gave Gauss the conformal-mapping prize in 1822, while Andersen was at Slagelse. **niels_bohr**, **heisenberg**, **dirac** at Nyhavn 67: Bohr born at Ved Stranden 14 (**55.6774, 12.5785**) forty years after Andersen took the rooms, and Blegdamsvej (**55.6989, 12.5675**) later brings the other two through the same streets. **karl_marx** at Paris, canonical pin **48.8566, 2.3522**: Andersen met Heine there in June 1833, Marx is expelled in 1845 and leaves Heine behind. **goethe_full** at Weimar 1844, the court Goethe made, twelve years after his death. **luc_olivier_merson** at Paris 1867, twenty-one and at the Beaux-Arts the year Andersen walks the Exposition. **maurice_sendak** at Langelinie, Andersen Medal 1970 at Bologna, the corpus's only file naming Andersen directly.

The lens also flagged a **negative**: Andersen never reaches the Kaaba, the Temple Mount, or Buenos Aires, so none of those canonical pins is inherited here.
