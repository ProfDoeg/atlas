# John Chapman, called Johnny Appleseed: research report

*2026-08-10. For the Atlas of Journeys. John Chapman (1774-1845), nurseryman, Swedenborgian colporteur, folk hero.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or claim the sources themselves hedge · *contradictions are flagged where found and are not adjudicated here.*

A note on the evidence base first. Chapman left no papers: no diary, no letter in his hand, no ledger. Everything below rests on four kinds of material: two death notices printed within nine days of the event; land and probate records; neighbours' reminiscences written down fifteen to fifty-five years later; and a folklore literature beginning in 1871 and still running. The first three are thin, the fourth is enormous, and almost every popular fact comes from the fourth.

---

## 1. Massachusetts, 1774-1792

**Birth.** John Chapman is born 26 September 1774 at Leominster, Massachusetts, second child of Nathaniel Chapman, farmer, and Elizabeth Simonds Chapman, who married 8 February 1770. **[A: Wikipedia; Leominster records via Robert Price, *Johnny Appleseed: Man and Myth* (1954)]**. Older popular accounts sometimes give 1775.

**The mother's death.** Elizabeth dies in 1776, shortly after bearing a second son, Nathaniel Jr., who dies within days. **[A: Wikipedia]**. Two corrections, both load-bearing: the pool placed the death at **Longmeadow**, where the family does not arrive until 1780 (she dies at Leominster); and the cause it gave, tuberculosis, is **not** in the cited article but descends from Price 1954, so it is retagged **[R]** and kept out of the journey prose.

**The father's war service.** The pool carried, at tag [A] on a Wikipedia citation, that Nathaniel Chapman served in the Continental Army and fought at Bunker Hill. A targeted re-fetch of the article wikitext returns nothing on either point; the substance (minuteman in 1775, later Bunker Hill) traces to Price 1954 and town records, not to the page cited. **Retagged [R], source corrected.** It appears in the journey as context inside a campa and never as its own stop, because the atlas should not plant a pin on a claim whose citation failed.

**Longmeadow.** In 1780 Nathaniel returns, marries Lucy Cooley, and moves the household to Longmeadow; the couple have ten more children. **[A: Wikipedia; Price 1954]**

**Gap.** Nothing is documented of John Chapman between 1780 and 1792: no schooling, no trade, no first orchard. The stories that fill those twelve years are all retrospective.

## 2. Pennsylvania, 1792-1801

**Westward.** At about eighteen he sets out west with his younger half-brother Nathaniel Cooley Chapman. **[R: Wikipedia, which does not document the departure with a record]**

**The Crawford apprenticeship.** Wikipedia: "Shortly after the brothers parted ways, John began his apprenticeship as an orchardist under a Mr. Crawford who grew apples." **[R]**. The pool placed this in **Massachusetts in 1792**; the source gives **no location at all** and sequences it *after* the departure west, not before. Tradition (Price 1954) puts Crawford near Wilkes-Barre, where the journey pins it as tradition.

**Grant's Hill, Pittsburgh, 1794.** The pool tagged this **[A]**. The source introduces it as "Another story has Chapman living in Pittsburgh on Grant's Hill in 1794 at the time of the Whiskey Rebellion." A claim introduced as another story is **[R]**. Retagged; the coordinate (40.4394, -79.9958) is correct.

**The Potomac cider mills.** Wikipedia: "picking seeds from the pomace at Potomac River cider mills in the late 1790s." The pool added "in present-day western Pennsylvania," a **geographic impossibility**: the Potomac touches Pennsylvania only in a thin south-central sliver some 250 km from the frontier described. Dropped; Harpers Ferry is used as a **regional proxy pin**, labelled as such. **[R]**

**Brokenstraw Creek.** "He planted his first nursery on the bank of Brokenstraw Creek, south of Warren, Pennsylvania." **[A as event, R as to date]**. The pool asserted **1798**; the source attaches **no date**, and the pool's geography lens said c.1797-1800, contradicting its own chronology lens. 1798 is kept as an ordering value with the hedge in `date_confidence`. The pool's coordinate, 41.8434 / -79.1436, is **the city of Warren, not the creek**, which meets the Allegheny at Irvine some 9 km west. Pin corrected to 41.8356 / -79.2594.

**French Creek, Venango County.** "moved to Venango County, along the shore of French Creek." **[A as event, R as to date]**. The pool asserted 1798 here too, which cannot hold if Brokenstraw is 1798 and precedes it; its geography lens said early 1800s. Unresolved, dated 1800 for ordering.

## 3. The Ohio Country, 1800-1809

**First Ohio orchard.** At about twenty-six Chapman is documented in Ohio, planting on Isaac Stadden's farm at the forks of the Licking. **[A: Wikipedia; Henry Howe, *Historical Collections of Ohio*]**

**The canoes.** He travels the Ohio, Muskingum, and Walhonding with two canoes lashed together and loaded with seed. **[A: Wikipedia; Price 1954]**. Dated 1806, best-known telling.

**Beaver, Steubenville, Zanesville, Coshocton, New Philadelphia.** All **[R]**, from county histories and river tradition relayed through Price 1954. None rests on a document naming Chapman on a date; they are a route, not events.

**Duncan Falls and Dresden.** The pool gave these two places **one shared pin** (40.1223, -82.0132), which is Dresden. Duncan Falls is at 39.8945 / -81.9268, 40 km south, *downstream* and on the far side of Zanesville, so it cannot be the upper Muskingum alongside Dresden. Split into two stops, and the river order (Duncan Falls, Zanesville, Dresden) now runs upstream as a real journey would. **[R]**

## 4. The Mohican circuit, 1809-1820

**The circuit itself.** Mansfield, Mount Vernon, Perrysville, Loudonville, and Lucas form a working nursery round in north-central Ohio. **[A for the cluster: Wikipedia; Richland, Knox, and Ashland county histories]**. Mount Vernon is the strongest of them: two town lots he owns there are in the Knox County deed records cited by Price. **[A]**

**Mohican Township, 160 acres.** He buys the southwest quarter of section 26, Mohican Township, Ashland County, and loses it. **[A: Wikipedia; Ashland County deeds]**. Two corrections: the pool said he lost it "to debt or tax default," where the source says he **did not record the deed**, a different failure; and the pool's pin, 40.8687 / -82.3182, is the **city of Ashland**, not the township, which lies in the county's southeastern corner near 40.7922 / -82.1842. Pin corrected. (The Ashland city coordinate is kept, correctly labelled, for the Rosella Rice stops.)

**Nova, the Algeo farm tree.** Tradition holds that a tree here is the last surviving tree he planted. **[R]**. Pool coordinate 40.9678 / -82.3477 is 8.5 km SSW of Nova, at 41.0289 / -82.3047. Corrected.

**The alarm run of September 1812.** After the Copus killings, Chapman is said to have run through the night from Mansfield to warn Owl Creek settlers into Captain Douglas's blockhouse. **[R]**. **Source misattribution:** the pool cited Wikipedia, whose article contains no War of 1812 passage at all, no run, no Copus, no warning; the story descends from **Henry Howe, *Historical Collections of Ohio***, which Price 1954 treats as legend. The pool's geography lens cited Howe correctly at the same event, contradicting its own chronology lens. **Duplicate coordinate:** the blockhouse endpoint carried the byte-identical coordinate of the Mount Vernon stop, leaving the run no destination; Clinton lay about two miles north, pinned near 40.42 / -82.49. The date, 15 September 1812, is right. The most repeated Chapman story after the seed-scattering has no contemporary documentation whatsoever, and stays labelled as legend.

## 5. The New Church, 1817-1830

**The first written trace.** The earliest known written report of Chapman appears in 1817 in a bulletin of the Church of the New Jerusalem at **Manchester, England**. **[A: Wikipedia, citing the New Jerusalem Church periodical]**. He is forty-three the first time anyone writes him down, and it happens across an ocean he never crosses, in the paperwork of his own church. The first known written use of "John Appleseed" follows in 1822, in a letter by a member of the same church. **[A]**

**The tracts.** He carries and distributes Swedenborg's writings, reportedly tearing books into sections so several homesteads can read at once. **[A: Wikipedia]**. In 1819 he falls from a tree, his neck catches in the branches, and an eight-year-old named John White saves him from strangulation. **[R]**. No place is given in the source; pinned to the Mohican hub with the gap declared.

**The appearance.** The famous description (small, chunked, quick, unshaven, long dark hair, black sparkling eye) is the best-known prose about him. The pool gave it to **Rosella Rice as an eyewitness**, in *History of Ashland County, Ohio* (1863). The wording checks out verbatim; the attribution is **wrong twice over**. It is **not Rice**: the book introduces the passage with "In 'Ohio Historical Collections,' by Henry Howe, p. 432, occurs the following notice of Johnny Appleseed…", and Rice's own sketch is a separate block further down. And the 1863 book is **H. S. Knapp, *A History of the Pioneer and Modern Times of Ashland County***; the title the pool used is George W. Hill's 1880 book. The eyewitness framing goes with the attribution, Howe being a compiler who never knew Chapman.

**The reading in the doorway.** The quilting passage **is** Rice. But the pool altered it three ways, one of which inverts the original: "the roar of wind and waves" **reverses** her "the roar of waves and winds"; "quilting upstairs" modernizes her "quilting up stairs" and adds a comma she did not write; and the quote is **cut off mid-clause** at "as the balmy airs." dropping "that stirred and quivered the morning-glory leaves about his gray head". The pool's note claimed the only change was an em dash swapped for a comma. That was false. Her wording is restored in full, em dash included, quote fields being verbatim.

## 6. Indiana and the last years, 1830-1845

**The move west.** As Ohio fills, Chapman shifts into the Maumee valley and then Indiana, still planting ahead of the line and returning to tend and sell. **[R for the Defiance and Van Wert sites, county tradition via Price 1954; A for the westward shift]**

**Milan Township, Allen County, Indiana.** A nursery reported to hold 15,000 trees. **[A: Wikipedia; Allen County records]**. The pool's pin, 41.0334 / -85.2453, sits on the **west side of Fort Wayne**, 28 km from the real Milan Township at 41.13806 / -84.94833, on the wrong side of the city entirely. Corrected.

**The Panic of 1837** devalues his holdings across Ohio and Indiana. **[A]**. **No marriage:** he never marries, reportedly believing a spiritual soulmate would be revealed to him in Heaven if not on Earth. **[R]**

**Death.** He dies 18 March 1845 at Fort Wayne, about seventy, in the Worth cabin, reportedly of the winter plague after walking in cold weather to tend a nursery. **[A for date, place, age, cabin]**. The pool named the host **William Worth**; that forename is **not in the source**, which says only "the Worth cabin sat in which he died." Dropped.

**Coordinate chaos at the death site.** Three coordinates were offered for one cabin: geography lens 41.105 / -85.125, afterlife lens 41.1306 / -85.1289, and Wikipedia's Canterbury Green site at 41.1275 / -85.1050 per OSM. The geography value sits 3 km southwest and lands on the **rival grave site**, collapsing the distinction the entry depends on. The journey uses 41.1275 / -85.1050.

**The estate.** Over 1,200 acres across several states, chiefly to Persis Broom, including the Milan Township nursery of 15,000 trees and two plots near Mount Vernon, Ohio. **[A]**. The pool's "four plots in Allen County, Indiana" is **not in the source** and is dropped, and Persis was a **half-sister**, a child of Nathaniel and Lucy Cooley; the pool hardened Wikipedia's loose "Johnny had one sister, Persis Broom, of Indiana" into a full sister named "(Chapman) Broom".

## 7. The grave, which is not one place

Two Fort Wayne sites are claimed and neither is settled. **[R for both]**

- **Canterbury Green**, near the former Worth cabin, where developers identified a rock as the burial site. Actual location 41.1275 / -85.1050; the pool's 41.147 / -85.118 is 2.4 km NNW and is corrected.
- **Archer cemetery**, now within Johnny Appleseed Park, argued for by Steven Fortriede of the Allen County Public Library from an 1858 interview with Richard Worth Jr. saying Chapman was buried respectably on hallowed ground rather than by the cabin. The pool pinned it at 41.094 / -85.1173 in one lens and 41.1118 / -85.1232 in the other: **one cemetery, 2 km apart, in one report**. The journey uses Wikipedia's 41.1118 / -85.1232.

**The 1916 fence.** An iron fence goes up in the Archer graveyard in 1916. The pool credited "the Horticulture Society of Indiana"; the source ties it to **Indiana's centennial** and names no society. The pool also paraphrased a 1934 committee report as saying men who attended the funeral were still living; the line the source actually quotes is "There was little or no reason for them to make a mistake about the location of this grave." Paraphrase dropped.

**John H. Archer, 4 October 1900**, on the site: "The grave, more especially the common head-boards used in those days, have long since decayed and become entirely obliterated." **[A]**. This is the honest terminus. The physical evidence was gone within two generations, and everything since is argument over ground.

## 8. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Death year.** Rice, who knew him, writes "in 1846 or 1848". The two obituaries and the probate say 1845. *Harper's*, November 1871, prints 1847, and since that article became the founding popular source the wrong year propagated for decades. Three dates, one death; the journey states all three and settles on 1845 because the newspapers are contemporary, not because Rice has been refuted.
2. **The grave.** Canterbury Green versus Archer cemetery. Unresolved and probably unresolvable.
3. **The Lisbon festival year.** The pool's geography lens said **1966**, its afterlife lens **1968**. Wikipedia says 1968; **1966 is the year of the commemorative stamp**, and the two facts had been crossed. Corrected both ways.
4. **Scattering versus business.** The folk image is a man flinging seed at random; the record is fenced nurseries, agents selling on shares, periodic returns. **[A]**. Paul Aron argues further that he was a successful speculator who wore rags by choice. **[R]**. The journey holds both rather than picking.
5. **What the apples were for.** Seed-grown trees do not breed true; the fruit was mostly too tart to eat and went to hard cider. **[A]**. Not a contradiction in the sources, a contradiction with the schoolroom.

## 9. Gaps stated as gaps

- **1780-1792.** Twelve childhood years with nothing in them.
- **No date attaches** to the Brokenstraw or French Creek nurseries in the cited source.
- **The Crawford apprenticeship has no location** in the source. Wilkes-Barre is tradition.
- **The 1819 fall from the tree has no location.**
- **The alarm run has no contemporary document**, only Howe writing decades later.
- **Cause of death.** "Winter plague," usually glossed as pneumonia, is reported, not certified.
- **Chapman's own voice is entirely absent.** Not one sentence in this journey is quoted from him. Every quote is somebody describing him, or a paper announcing that he has stopped.

## 10. Interlocks with existing atlas travelers

No journey file and no line of the census names Chapman, Mansfield, Fort Wayne, or Swedenborgianism. The corpus grep returns only the QUEUE.md listing, which puts him in "the same American cluster" as Washington, Jefferson, and Jackson. **[A: QUEUE.md line 210]**. Every crossing below is geographic and temporal, not a link any file states.

- **george_washington** (Grant's Hill): claims Ohio and Great Kanawha bounty land through William Crawford, and in 1794 marches militia west against the Whiskey Rebellion, the year Chapman is said to be on Grant's Hill. **[R for Chapman; A for Washington]**
- **thomas_jefferson** (Steubenville): the Louisiana Purchase of April 1803 falls in the season Chapman is first recorded planting in Ohio, and releases the wave his nurseries are placed to meet. **Kept in campa**, a genuine causal link (the Purchase releases the settler demand Chapman's business serves), not a coincidental crossing.
- **symmes** (Marietta): Symmes the elder opens the Miami Purchase in 1788 and builds Cincinnati out of it, selling settlers the land they would want orchards on; his nephew lectures the hollow earth through the same Ohio courthouse towns in the 1820s. **Kept in campa**, same causal logic as Jefferson.

**Cut from campa on house-style review (2026-08-15):** alexis_de_tocqueville (Forks of the Ohio), andrew_jackson (Clinton alarm run), abraham_lincoln (Defiance), and thomas_edison (Milan, stop retitled "the ground two years on" and rewritten around the Milan Canal's own real history) were all pure geography-and-calendar coincidences with explicit in-campa admissions of no documented link ("nothing written connects the two passages," "no atlas file joins the two men"). Per Anthony's ruling, an honest disclaimer of non-connection does not exempt a coincidental crossing from the house rule; all four are removed by name, with each campa rewritten around Chapman's own attested facts.

**Method note.** The pool arrived with per-claim correction notes from an adversarial verification pass. Fourteen were applied: three source misattributions, six wrong or duplicated coordinates, three date errors, one relationship error, one invented forename, one invented plot count, and one misquotation that reversed a phrase. Each is recorded above at the claim it touches.
