# Cagliostro: research report

*Giuseppe Balsamo, self-styled Count Alessandro di Cagliostro, Grand Cophta of the Egyptian Rite. Palermo 1743 - San Leo 1795.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or self-report. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and left standing; the journey file carries the hedge in `date_confidence`, never in the prose.

A warning that governs the dossier: the two fullest narrative sources are both interested witnesses. The *Compendio della vita e delle gesta di Giuseppe Balsamo* (Rome, 1791) was written by his prosecutors while he was alive in a cell; his own memorials were written by a defendant selling a persona. Where neither can be checked against a third source, the entry is **[R]**.

---

## 1. Palermo: the Albergheria, 1743-1760

- Born in the Albergheria, Palermo's oldest quarter, to the cloth merchant Pietro Balsamo and Felicita Bracconieri **[A: it.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]**.
- **Date, flagged and resolved as a non-conflict**: Italian Wikipedia gives birth **2 June 1743** and baptism **8 June 1743**; English Wikipedia reports the baptismal date as the birth date. These are not two rival birth dates but one date and one sacrament. The journey uses 2 June with the baptism noted in `date_confidence`.
- The father declares bankruptcy and dies while the boy is small **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The geography lens says "within a year," the chronology lens "while Giuseppe is still a child." **Contradiction flagged, not resolved**; the journey says only that the father dies early.
- Seminary of St Roch, then a religious house near **Caltagirone**, 1756 **[A for the date: it.wikipedia]**. **Order contested**: en.wikipedia names the Benedictine convent of Santissimo Salvatore, it.wikipedia the Fatebenefratelli. Both are carried, neither asserted. He learns herbalism and pharmacy from the convent apothecary and is expelled, or flees, depending on the telling **[R]**.
- Before fifteen he is known in Palermo as **Beppo Maldetto**, Beppo the Cursed, for drunkenness, gambling, brawling, forged theatre tickets, theft, extortion, and a forged will **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Not "dueling," which no consulted source supports.
- The **Marano fraud**, c. 1760: he persuades a goldsmith that a buried treasure lies guarded by demons, takes sixty ounces of gold for the evocation, and leads the man to an **olive field outside the city** where he is beaten instead **[A: en.wikipedia, Compendio]**. The pool's c. 1764 date and Piazza Marina setting are both unsupported: English Wikipedia puts the fraud at age seventeen, c. 1760, and outside the walls.
- He flees to **Messina** and there, according to himself alone, meets **Althotas** **[R: Cagliostro's own testimony, relayed by Trowbridge; the existence of Althotas rests on no other witness]**.
- **The surname, contradiction flagged.** A deceased wealthy **aunt** in one lens, a **maternal great-uncle** in the other; the usual biographical account names his godmother or great-aunt **Vincenza Cagliostro**. The journey says only "a dead kinswoman of his mother's line." He takes the title "Count Alessandro Cagliostro" on reaching Messina, c. 1760-61, not 1763.

## 2. The claimed East, c. 1766

Everything in this phase is **[R]** and self-reported.

- Initiation by Althotas, including a claim of the **Knights of Malta** under Grand Master Pinto **[R: Cagliostro's memorial]**.
- Travel through **Egypt** and parts of Asia; instruction from Egyptian priests at **Alexandria** and in chambers beneath the **pyramids of Giza** **[R: wholly self-reported, no independent record]**.
- **Gap, stated as a gap**: no document, port record, or third-party sighting places Balsamo east of Sicily in these years. The Egyptian journey carries the whole Egyptian Rite and rests on his word alone.

## 3. Rome and the roads of the fugitive, 1768-1776

- Marriage to **Lorenza Serafina Feliciani**, **21 April 1768**, church of **San Salvatore in Campo**, Rome **[A: it.wikipedia, parish record cited by Trowbridge and the Compendio]**.
- **Her age, corrected.** English Wikipedia's "fourteen" is an arithmetic error propagated from her birth year. Lorenza was born **8 April 1751**; at the marriage she was **seventeen** **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **Coordinate corrected**: the pool pinned the marriage at Piazza di Spagna (41.9057, 12.4823); San Salvatore in Campo is in the rione Regola at **41.8937, 12.4740**, 1.5 km southwest.
- Forgery of papal seals and lottery tickets with the confidence man calling himself the **Marquis Agliata**; flight from Rome **[A: Compendio]**.
- **Aix-en-Provence, 1770**: Casanova stops at the same inn as the Balsamos, travelling as pilgrims to Compostela, and describes Lorenza **[A: Casanova's Mémoires, quoted in Trowbridge 1910]**. The pool placed this in Rome; Trowbridge is explicit that it was Aix.
- Barcelona, Madrid, Lisbon, and London, 1769-1772, living by scams and, per hostile sources, by Lorenza's prostitution **[A for the itinerary: both wikipedias; the prostitution charge is Compendio-derived and adversarial]**.

## 4. The Mason and the northern courts, 1776-1780

- Second arrival in **London**, July 1776 **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **Initiation into Freemasonry, 12 April 1777**, Espérance Lodge No. 289, meeting at the King's Head tavern, **Gerrard Street, Soho** **[A: both wikipedias; lodge minutes cited in Trowbridge]**. The pool's "Soho, Whitcomb Street" is wrong twice over: Whitcomb Street is in St James's, and the Soho address that matters is Gerrard Street (51.5117, -0.1311).
- Arrest and imprisonment in **King's Bench Prison** over debt and fraud allegations, 1777 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- Travels through Switzerland, Germany, and Prussia. The Hague, Nuremberg, Leipzig, and Königsberg rest on Trowbridge alone **[R]** and are dropped from the journey.
- **Mitau (Jelgava), Courland, 1779**: an Egyptian Rite lodge among the Baltic German nobility, the von Medem family devoted followers **[A: Trowbridge; McCalman]**. Elisa von der Recke's exposé fixes the stay to 1779.
- **Saint Petersburg, 1779-1780**, under the alias **Count Phoenix**, roughly nine months, medical practice, collision with the court physicians, departure under pressure **[A: ru.wikipedia; Trowbridge]**. English Wikipedia's "Spring 1778" would place St Petersburg before Mitau and reverse the documented route; corrected to 1779-1780.
- **Warsaw, May-June 1780**: guest of Prince Adam Poniński, alchemical demonstrations publicly exposed, hasty departure 26 June **[A: it.wikipedia]**.

## 5. The Grand Cophta: Strasbourg, Bordeaux, Lyon, 1780-1784

- **Strasbourg, arrival September 1780**, the period running to 1783 **[A: it.wikipedia]**. The pool's 1781 matches neither source; the standard date is 19 September 1780. He treats the poor without charge, stages cures, and wins **Cardinal Louis de Rohan**, Bishop of Strasbourg. **Caution recorded**: Cagliostro kept his own house, so the Palais Rohan (**48.58083, 7.75222**, not the pool's 48.5779, 7.7519) is Rohan's seat, not his address.
- **Bordeaux, arrival 8 November 1783**; during a winter illness he reports a vision that commits him to founding the Egyptian Rite, February-May 1784 **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **Lyon, 20 October 1784**: the mother lodge **La Sagesse Triomphante** **[A: it.wikipedia; Masonic archival record cited in Trowbridge and McCalman]**. The alias "Comte Phoenix" belongs to Russia in 1779-80, not to Lyon; in France he operated as **Comte de Cagliostro**, the name under which he was tried.
- The Rite promises "physical and moral regeneration" **[A: en.wikipedia]** and admits women in a parallel Isis working **[R: widely reported in the Cagliostro literature, not confirmed in the sources reachable here]**.
- **Rite of Misraim**: English Wikipedia has the rite originating in the **Republic of Venice**, possibly from a patent issued by Cagliostro, spreading through the Franco-Italian lodges of the **Kingdom of Naples**, and reaching Paris with the Bédarride brothers in 1814-15. Venice origin, Naples transmission, **[R]** throughout.

## 6. Paris: the necklace and the Bastille, 1785-1786

- **Paris, 30 January 1785**: lodging at the Palais Royal, then the house on the **Rue Saint-Claude** in the Marais; two Egyptian Rite lodges founded **[A: it.wikipedia; Trowbridge]**.
- **Affair of the Diamond Necklace.** Cardinal de Rohan is duped by **Jeanne de Valois-Saint-Rémy** into believing Marie Antoinette wants the necklace bought in her name **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Affair of the Diamond Necklace'; trial records]**.
- **Arrest sequence, corrected.** Rohan is arrested **15 August 1785** in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, Jeanne three days later, Cagliostro separately around **22-23 August**: a week apart, in different places, not one scene.
- Nine months in the **Bastille**; historians regard his actual part in the fraud as doubtful **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Acquittal by the Parlement of Paris, 31 May 1786**, with Rohan; Jeanne convicted, whipped, and branded **[A: both wikipedias, trial verdict]**.
- **Expulsion despite acquittal**, by royal order; departure from **Boulogne for Dover, 19 June 1786** **[A: it.wikipedia]**.

## 7. The descent, 1786-1789

- **Two London letters, not one.** The *Lettre au peuple français*, London, **20 June 1786**, carries the passage later read as a prophecy of the Bastille's fall **[A: fr.wikipedia; the 1786 memorial]**. The *Letter to the English People*, denying that he is Giuseppe Balsamo, is dated **1787** by en.wikipedia. The pool fused them; **kept distinct.**
- **Théveneau de Morande** publishes exposés in the *Courier de l'Europe* reconstructing the Balsamo past; Cagliostro forces a retraction **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Goethe in Palermo, April 1787**: during the *Italienische Reise* he seeks out the impoverished Balsamo family and gives them money **[A: Goethe, Italienische Reise]**. This is the first serious outside verification of the humble origin.
- Bienne (April 1787), then expulsion from Switzerland, Savoy, and Turin, with departure toward Aix-les-Bains and Turin **23 July 1788** and possessions pawned **[A: it.wikipedia; Trowbridge]**.
- **Trento, 25 March 1789**: the bishop writes to the Roman Curia in his favour, declaring him repentant **[R: it.wikipedia]**.

## 8. The Holy Office, 1789-1791

- Departure from Trento **17 May 1789**, Rome about ten days later **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **Where they lodged, contradiction flagged.** One lens places the final lodging at Piazza di Spagna; the other has Serafina denouncing him to the parish priest of **Santa Caterina della Rota**, in the rione Regola, 1.5 km away and in a different parish. The journey uses the Regola coordinate; the disagreement stands.
- **September 1789**: Serafina denounces him, alleging mistreatment and that he forbade her Catholic practice **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **Arrest by the Inquisition, imprisonment in Castel Sant'Angelo.** **Date contested**: it.wikipedia **27 December 1789**, en.wikipedia **29 December**. The journey uses 27 December with the disagreement in `date_confidence`.
- **Three separate events, often compressed into one**: the Holy Office sentences him to death by burning **21 March 1791**; Pius VI commutes it to perpetual imprisonment **7 April 1791**; the sentence is proclaimed publicly and he abjures **13 April 1791**, with the Inquisition condemning Egyptian Masonry as heretical. The public abjuration and book-burning took place at **Santa Maria sopra Minerva**, not Campo de' Fiori. The Campo de' Fiori association appears borrowed from Giordano Bruno.

## 9. San Leo, 1791-1795

- **Transfer to the fortress of San Leo, 20 April 1791** **[A: it.wikipedia]**. The Rocca was rebuilt for Federico da Montefeltro by the Sienese engineer **Francesco di Giorgio Martini** **[A: it.wikipedia, Forte di San Leo]**.
- **Geography corrected twice over.** San Leo stands in the **Montefeltro**, province of Rimini, not in the Tuscan-Romagnol Apennines; and every San Leo coordinate in the pool (43.9081/12.3421, 43.9089/12.3439) lands 1.4 km north of the site in open country. The fortress is at **43.8962, 12.3460**, the town at 43.8964, 12.3433.
- **11 September 1791**: moved from the Stanza del Tesoro to **il Pozzetto**, roughly 3 x 3 metres, entered only through a ceiling trapdoor **[A: it.wikipedia]**. **Contradiction flagged**: the geography lens calls it windowless; the chronology lens describes a window reduced to a triple-barred slit. The slit is the better-attested reading and the journey follows it.
- December 1793: permission to write to the Pope, describing visions **[R: it.wikipedia]**.
- **Serafina dies in 1794**, aged forty-three, in the convent of **Sant'Apollonia**, Rome **[A: en.wikipedia for the death and age; fr.wikipedia names the house]**.
- **23 August 1795**: found semi-paralyzed after an apparent stroke. **26 August 1795**, about 22:30: death, refusing confession, per the chaplain **fra' Cristoforo da Cicerchia**; buried without coffin or headstone, "like an infidel, unworthy of the suffrages of the Holy Church" **[A: it.wikipedia, on the chaplain's record; en.wikipedia gives the same date and the age of 52]**.
- **The walling-up legend.** Popular tradition, repeated even in some scholarly retellings, holds that he was walled up alive; Italian Wikipedia, on the chaplain's record, states flatly that he was not ("Non fu murato"). **Unresolved contradiction between record and legend**; the journey follows the chaplain.

## 10. Afterlife

- The **Compendio** (Rome, 1791), the Inquisition's authorized account of the trial, translated at once into French, English, and German, is the dominant printed source for a century **[A]**. Several later writers argue it fed the mystique it meant to kill.
- **Der Gross-Cophta**, Goethe's comedy on the necklace affair, premieres at the Weimar court theatre **17 December 1791** and fails **[A]**.
- **December 1797**: Polish troops allied with the French occupy the fortress, free the convicts, and the remains are dug up **[A: it.wikipedia]**. The skull is another matter: the story that it was kept as a drinking cup (one variant makes it Napoleon's) is **[R]**, local tradition, undocumented, and no verified relic survives.
- **Alexandre Dumas** serializes *Joseph Balsamo* (1846-48), recasting him as an occult mastermind steering France toward revolution **[A]**. Most readers inherit this version.
- **Theodor Reuss** receives the Rite of Memphis and Misraim from John Yarker in 1902, called in that file "the Egyptian Rite line" **[A: theodor_reuss.journey.json]**. Lineal descent from Cagliostro's own rite is **[R]**, claimed by the tradition, not documented.
- The **Rocca di San Leo** survives as a museum under the Musei Nazionali dell'Emilia-Romagna, with the Pozzetto preserved and shown **[A: fortezzadisanleo.cultura.gov.it]**.

### Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **No tomb, monument, or grave marker exists anywhere.** Searches across Italian, English, and San Leo museum sources turn up none: the pilgrimage site is a cell, not a grave.
2. **No complete original Egyptian Rite ritual corpus survives.** Texts and lodge records were largely suppressed after the Roman trial, and every modern lodge claiming to practise "the" Egyptian Rite works from reconstruction out of fragmentary secondhand description.
3. **The eastern journey is unverifiable in principle** on the sources reachable here: Malta, Alexandria, Giza, and Medina rest on his own narration, most of it transmitted by his prosecutors.
4. **A quote could not be cleanly recovered.** "I am innocent, and slandered like him" circulates as his words but is a paraphrase, and the 1786 memorial's OCR is damaged exactly there (long s: "ſlandered"). The journey quotes only the intact clause: "I, who, like him, have been honourably acquitted by the justice of the magistrates."
5. **A quote was misattributed and is corrected here.** "Adieu, nature's unfortunate child, adieu!" is verbatim in Evans 1919, but it is the **sherif of Mecca's** farewell to Cagliostro, not his own line, and is not quoted as his in the journey. Trowbridge's 1910 chapter VIII does carry the phrase as its title.
6. **A quote was mislocated and is corrected here.** "He denied every thing to his judges with the utmost intrepidity" is verbatim on p. 64 of the 1791 London translation, but the surrounding text is the **Paris tribunal of 1785-86**, not the Roman Inquisition: he has just been "arrefted, and imprifoned in the Baftile," and Madame de la Motte throws a candlestick at his head two sentences later. Re-anchored to Paris, 1786.

### Mutual gaze: where this life crosses others already in the atlas

- **mesmer**. Animal magnetism runs in Paris 1778-1785, the same salon market Cagliostro enters in January 1785; Mesmer leaves the year Cagliostro is arrested there.
- **etteilla**. From 1781 he manufactures an Egyptian pedigree for the tarot in the same city and years Cagliostro manufactures one for a Masonic rite; in a 1786 preface he denies being a Freemason, granting "true Masonry" respect while dismissing "little denominations."
- **louis_xvi**. The king who named the 1784 commission against Mesmer orders Cagliostro into the Bastille in August 1785.
- **empress_josephine**. Rose de Beauharnais, at Penthemont in 1781, is told by a fortune-teller she will be "more than a queen," in the same Paris market for prophecy.
- **giordano_bruno** and **tommaso_campanella**. The same Roman Holy Office and the same prison beside the Vatican (canonical pin 41.901, 12.4561), two centuries earlier.
- **theodor_reuss**. The Memphis-Misraim line, that file's "Egyptian Rite line," passing from Yarker to Reuss in 1902.
- **abdelkader**. Initiated in 1864 by the Alexandria lodge Les Pyramides d'Égypte, eighty years after Cagliostro sold Europe an Egypt he had probably never seen.
- **napoleon**. The 1798 expedition and the Rosetta Stone turn the invented Egypt of 1780s Paris into a country the French army measures.
