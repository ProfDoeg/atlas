# W.G. Sebald (1944-2001): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the atlas journey file `w_g_sebald.journey.json`. Slug: `w_g_sebald`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, with the source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, novel-derived setting, or an attribution circulating without a traceable primary. Every date given as `YYYY-01-01` in the journey file is a default for an unrecorded month or day, not a claim about January. Contradictions below are flagged and left standing.

Method. Sebald's books are not travel records. Where a stop derives from *Vertigo* or *Austerlitz*, the campa narrates the book's itinerary and `date_confidence` says so. The exception is the Suffolk walk, which the book dates itself in its first sentence ("In August 1992, when the dog days were drawing to an end") and which is treated as a real walk on a real date, because it is one.

---

## 1. Wertach and Sonthofen, 1944-1963

**Birth.** Winfried Georg Sebald is born 18 May 1944 in Wertach im Allgäu, Bavaria, second of three children of Rosa and Georg Sebald **[A: en.wikipedia, "W. G. Sebald"; de.wikipedia describes him as "aus Wertach gebürtig"]**. The research pool carried a hedge from an earlier pass that Wertach was "childhood home, not confirmed birthplace"; that hedge is **wrong and is dropped**: the Sebaldweg terminates at the birth house at Grüntenseestrasse 3 **[A: de.wikipedia]**.

**The father.** Georg Sebald joined the Reichswehr in 1929, served in the Wehrmacht including the 1939 invasion of Poland, and was held as a prisoner of war in France until 1947 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The maternal grandfather **Josef Egelhofer**, a police officer, is the male figure of the first years **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Whether the father's return is to be dated early or late in 1947 is not recorded anywhere reachable; the journey file defaults to 1 January and says so.

**Sonthofen.** From roughly age four to nineteen (1948-1963) the family lives at Sonthofen **[A: en.wikipedia; Angier, *Speak, Silence*, 2021]**, a town with an **Ordensburg**, an elite Nazi training academy, on its edge **[A: Angier, as relayed in the research pool]**. Angier's biography was **not read directly**; it reaches this report only through the pool's citations, which limits everything sourced to her.

**Schooling: a correction.** The pool's original chronology said Sebald "attends school in Oberstdorf" from 1948. This is **incorrect**. German Wikipedia records the **Realgymnasium Maria Stern at Immenstadt from 1954**, and only later the **Oberrealschule at Oberstdorf**, where he took his Abitur in **1963** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. The journey file carries Immenstadt as its own stop. Coordinates for the Immenstadt school building were not found; the stop uses the town centre and flags it.

**The camp footage: the largest date problem in the file.** The screening of concentration-camp film to Sebald's class, with no word of explanation, is one of the most repeated facts about him and it is **undated**. English Wikipedia says only that "while at school in Oberstdorf, Sebald was shown images of the Holocaust" **[A for the event, no year]**. The circulating date **1957** is **impossible**: in 1957 he was at Immenstadt **[A: de.wikipedia]**. A second pool entry gave "c. 1958" and mis-named the school a *Gymnasium*. His own interview accounts place it in his upper-school years at about seventeen, **c. 1961-1963 [R]**, and the file uses 1961-01-01 with the range stated in `date_confidence`. **No year for this event is attested.**

**The silence.** That his parents' generation did not discuss the Nazi period, the war, or the Holocaust, and that this shaped his subject, is his own repeated account in interviews (Maya Jaggi, Michael Silverblatt and others) **[R: reconstruction from interviews, no single dated primary document]**.

- German and English literature at **Freiburg im Breisgau**, 1963-1965 **[A]**, degree completed at **Fribourg**, Switzerland **[R: sources divide the two degrees differently and this file does not adjudicate]**.

## 4. *Vertigo* / *Schwindel. Gefühle*, published 1990

Four stops (Vienna, Venice, Verona, Riva del Garda) plus the return to W. are **book itineraries [R for Sebald's own travel]**. Three corrections were needed to the pool's account of the book itself.

1. **The Kafka ghost is not in Vienna.** The pool had the narrator half-glimpsing Kafka's ghost there. The Vienna material of "All'estero" is the compulsive walking and the visit to the poet **Ernst Herbeck**; the figure glimpsed in the crowd is **Dante**, and the apparition of a double comes later, in Italy. Kafka's retraced itinerary belongs to "Dr K Takes the Waters at Riva". Corrected.
2. **"The Hunter Gracchus" is not set in Verona.** Kafka's story is set at **Riva**, where its mayor receives the dead huntsman. Corrected.
3. **The Verona episode.** The pool's "twice refused service in a restaurant" is unsupported: the episode turns on the narrator fleeing the **Pizzeria Verona** after reading the proprietor's name, **Carlo Cadavero**. Corrected.

The date "c. 1980" for the Vienna and Italian passages, and "c. 1987" for the return to Wertach, are the **narrative's own approximations [R]**; no travel record for Sebald himself was located.

## 5. The Suffolk walk, August 1992, and *The Rings of Saturn*, 1995

The walk is dated **August 1992** by the book's first sentence **[A, from the text]**. The pool's chronology lens attached the Michael Hamburger visit to **1995**, which is the **publication year, not the walk**; corrected to August 1992 throughout.

**Carried in the file:** Somerleyton Hall, Lowestoft, Benacre Broad, Covehithe, Southwold, Dunwich, Middleton (Hamburger), Orford Ness, Boulge, Ditchingham, Bungay. Eleven stops.

**Dropped:** **Walberswick** and **Orford village** (stop budget; Orford is folded into the Ness stop) and **Sutton Hoo** (tagged **[R]** in the pool, approached only obliquely in the book).

**Coordinate corrections applied.**
- **Benacre Broad**: the pool's 52.3833, **1.6333** falls 4-5 km inland near Wrentham, nowhere near the eroding cliffs described. Corrected to **52.383, 1.700**.
- **Covehithe**: the pool's 52.3667, 1.6833 sits 2 km south-west of the village. Corrected to **52.376, 1.705**.

**Content corrections applied.**
- **Boulge**: the plant on Edward FitzGerald's grave is a **rose, not a mulberry**: hips gathered by William Simpson at Khayyam's tomb at Nishapur in 1886, raised at Kew, a cutting planted here **[A: en.wikipedia, "Edward FitzGerald (poet)"]**. The mulberry bled in from the book's sericulture material. The church is **St Michael's, Boulge**.
- **Covehithe church**: the large medieval St Andrew's was largely pulled down in the seventeenth century, the tall tower left standing, and a smaller thatched church built among the ruins in **1672 [A]**.

**Contradiction left standing: Ditchingham.** The pool placed H. Rider Haggard at **Ditchingham Hall**, the Earls Ferrers' eighteenth-century seat. Haggard lived and died at **Ditchingham House**, his wife's family home **[A: en.wikipedia, "Ditchingham"]**, and the supplied coordinates (52.4478, 1.4356) fit **neither**. The stop is kept, named as the estate rather than either house, with the problem recorded in `date_confidence`. **Not resolved.**

## 6. The air-war lectures

**Corrected publication chain [A: de.wikipedia, "Luftkrieg und Literatur"]:** lectures held **im Spätherbst 1997** at the Zurich puppet theatre; book published by **Carl Hanser in 1999**, two years later, not "the following year" as the pool's chronology had it; English translation *On the Natural History of Destruction* in **2003**, not 1999. The journey file dates the Zurich stop 1997-11-01 with the day defaulted.

The campa keeps the casualty and city figures general on purpose: the totals in circulation (roughly 600,000 civilian dead, roughly 131 cities) come from Sebald's own polemic and its reception, and are not independently verified here.

## 7. *Austerlitz*, 2001

Six stops (Antwerp Centraal, Liverpool Street, Prague, Terezín, Paris, Munich). All but Munich are **novel settings [R]**. The pool notes, on Angier's authority, that the narrator's journeys mirror Sebald's own research trips, but no individual trip is dated in anything reachable, so the file places them in the later 1990s and says so in each `date_confidence`.

- **Bala, Gwynedd** appears in the pool and was **dropped** for the stop budget; the Welsh fostering is described inside the Liverpool Street campa instead.
- **Paris**: the stop uses the **canonical pin, 48.8566, 2.3522**, byte-identical, as the interlock lens proposed for Austerlitz's Paris scenes. The Bibliothèque nationale at Tolbiac actually lies at about **48.8355, 2.3765**; the two are **deliberately not merged**, on the Mount-of-Olives precedent from `robert_maxwell.journey.json`, and the gap is noted in `date_confidence`.
- German publication date **2001-11-06** is carried from the pool; Anthea Bell's English translation appeared the same year **[A]**, and won the **2002 Independent Foreign Fiction Prize [A]**.

## 8. Death, burial, afterlife

**The death.** 14 December 2001; the car Sebald was driving collided with an oncoming lorry; his daughter Anna, a passenger, survived; a coroner's report roughly six months later concluded that a heart attack preceded and caused the collision **[A: en.wikipedia; contemporary obituaries]**.

**Contradiction, flagged and only partly resolved: where.** Three different locations were supplied by the research lenses for one event.

| Lens | Coordinates | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| geography | 52.59, 1.26 | Trowse / southern Norwich fringe, c. 8 km from home |
| afterlife | 52.6309, 1.2974 | Thorpe Hamlet, inside Norwich city |
| de.wikipedia | (none given) | "bei einem Autounfall in der Nähe seines Wohnorts", i.e. near Poringland |

The file uses **52.57, 1.35**, the Poringland area, flagged in `date_confidence` as an area and not a crash site. **No consulted source names the road or the milepost**, and press summaries do not agree on whether the lorry driver was publicly cleared. Both gaps stand.

**The grave.** St Andrew's churchyard, **Framingham Earl [A]**. The lenses disagreed here too: 52.5799, 1.3389 (geography, outside the village) against 52.5766, 1.3617 (afterlife). The pool's correction note directs reconciliation to the afterlife figure and the file uses it. The village proper is at 52.57488, 1.35852 and the church stands on Yelverton Road, so **even the adopted figure is approximate**.

**Sebald Copse, UEA, 2003 [A].** A copper beech planted by his family, with a circular bench whose form echoes *The Rings of Saturn*.

**Correction to the bench inscription.** The pool called "Unerzählt bleibt die Geschichte der abgewandten Gesichter" an epigraph from the Tripp collaboration. German and English are both verbatim correct, the framing is not: it is the **inscription on the UEA bench**, from the **penultimate poem of *Unerzählt***, Englished by **Michael Hamburger**, cited to Catling and Hibbitt, eds., *Saturn's Moons* (Legenda, 2011), p. 659 **[A]**. Hamburger, visited at Middleton in 1992, is therefore a documented relation and is named in that campa.

**Sebaldweg, 2005 [A].** Opened **13 November 2005**, about 11.8 km from Oberjoch to Wertach, six steles of excerpts from *Schwindel. Gefühle*, ending at the birth house. The pool's 47.6, 10.4 sits 1.4 km north of the village; the file uses the Wertach pin 47.5872, 10.4006.

**Grant Gee, *Patience (After Sebald)*, 2011 [A]** retraces the Suffolk walk. Recorded here; **not given a stop**, since it is a film about him rather than a place he went.

## 9. Quotations: what is solid and what is not

**Traceable to a named source [A]:**
- "As I sat there in Southwold overlooking the German Ocean, I sensed quite clearly the earth's slow turning into the dark.", *The Rings of Saturn*, trans. Hulse, p. 78.
- "Condemned to unrest I am, I am afraid", letter to Andreas Dorschel, June 2001.
- "someone who knows he has to leave before too long", reported statement to a visitor, August 2001.
- "Unerzählt bleibt die Geschichte der abgewandten Gesichter", the UEA bench, from *Unerzählt*.
- "A hybrid of a book – fiction, travel, biography, myth, and memoir", Roberta Silman, *NYT*, 1998 (a reviewer, not Sebald).
- "fictional twice over", James Wood on the *Austerlitz* photographs (a critic, not Sebald).
- Two interview lines ("I hate [...] the German postwar novel like pestilence"; "I see the catastrophe caused by the Germans...") are quoted in English Wikipedia, but **the interview and its date were not traced**. Used with that hedge in `quote_source`.

**Rejected.** "The shadow of the night is drawn like a black veil across the earth" is verbatim in the same blockquote (pp. 78-79), but there it is Sebald's narrator relaying **Thomas Browne's** *Garden of Cyrus*, not his own observation, and it is a mid-sentence fragment of the passage already used. **Dropped**; Browne appears in the Southwold campa instead.

**Used but tagged [R].** Six lines circulate on quotation-aggregator sites with no traceable transcript: on the dead needing attending to, on his parents' backgrounds, on hearing nothing of pre-1945 history until sixteen or seventeen, on going home, on the moral backbone of literature, on places having memory. All six are used, each carrying the untraced-attribution warning in `quote_source`. If a primary transcript turns up, rewrite those fields, not the campas.

## 10. Interlocks: none named

A traveler may be named in a campa only where Sebald stands in a real relation to them. **None qualified, and none is named.** The candidates raised by the interlock lens, and why each fails:

- **Margery Kempe**: same East Anglian coast, both pilgrim-walkers. **Shared geography and a critical analogy**, not a relation. Excluded.
- **Robert Maxwell**: continental refugee who rebuilt an identity in England, plus an ambiguous solitary death put down to a heart attack. Parallel biography, no contact. Excluded.
- **Sigmund Freud**: the paradigm of a German-speaking Central European driven to England, but no documented reading, citation or influence appears in the pool. Excluded, and the one worth revisiting: if a Sebald-Freud citation is ever attested, the Hampstead pin already exists.
- **Alan Turing**: provincial English university, official silence, contested death. Structural rhyme only. Excluded.

The people Sebald actually stands in relation to (Michael Hamburger, Kafka, Stendhal, Thomas Browne, Alfred Döblin, Robert Walser, Jan Peter Tripp, Michael Hulse, Anthea Bell) have no journey files: a directory listing on 2026-08-14 found only `freud.journey.json` among them. Hamburger is the obvious future addition, and the Middleton and Sebald Copse campas are where he would link.

**Canonical pins.** Four were supplied: **Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262)**, **Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354)**, **Paris (48.8566, 2.3522)**, **Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816)**. Sebald has no relation to Mecca, Jerusalem or Buenos Aires, so those three are **not used**. Paris **is**, byte-identical, at the *Austerlitz* library stop.

## 11. Honest gaps

1. **No year for the camp-footage screening**, the most-cited formative episode of his life. The circulating 1957 is impossible.
2. **No located crash site.** Road, milepost and village unfixed; three sources give three areas.
3. **Angier's biography was not read**, only relayed. The interior life of the Sonthofen years would be better sourced from it.
4. **No inquest transcript**; whether the lorry driver was publicly cleared is unresolved.
5. **Ute Sebald** is barely documented: maiden name and marriage details beyond the year 1967 are absent.
6. **Wymondham and Poringland dates** are positions within attested spans, not attested moves.
7. **The Immenstadt school building** has no located coordinates; the town centre stands in.
8. **Ditchingham Hall versus House** unresolved, coordinates matching neither.
9. **Sebald's own travel dates** behind *Vertigo* and *Austerlitz* are undocumented; the book itineraries are carried as book itineraries.
10. **Interview primaries** (Jaggi in *The Guardian*, Silverblatt's *Bookworm*, others) were **not consulted directly**, and several quotations depend on them.
