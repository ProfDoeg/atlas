# Johann Friedrich Overbeck (1789-1869): research report

*Atlas of Journeys. Slug `johann_friedrich_overbeck`, compiled 2026-08-11 from the verified pool after adversarial coordinate and citation audit.*

Legend: **[A: source]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or argument from silence. Contradictions are flagged and left standing, never adjudicated. **EN**/**DE**/**IT** = the respective Wikipedias; **ADB** = Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie, 'Overbeck, Johann Friedrich'; **Atkinson** = J. Beavington Atkinson, *Overbeck* (Sampson Low, 1882).

The life is easy to place and hard to date. He lived fifty-nine of his eighty years inside one city and left it, so far as anything reachable records, once. Almost every claim below is about a year rather than a day, and EN and DE disagree about many of the years. Where they disagree, both stand.

## 0. Coordinate audit

The pool's prose proved largely sound and its **coordinates systematically bad**: thirteen pins wrong by 160 m to 5.5 km, and the Vienna pin an anachronism naming a building erected three years after Overbeck's death. All are corrected in the journey file.

Corrected pin, then the pool's error: Vienna Academy, all four entries, **48.2046, 16.3716** (540 m, anachronistic); Katharineum **53.8688, 10.6892** (250 m); Holstentor **53.8663, 10.6796** (735 m); Marienkirche **53.8677, 10.6850** (160 m); Sant'Isidoro **41.9061, 12.4867** (270 m); Palazzo Zuccari **41.9056, 12.4839** (165 m); Casino Massimo **41.8885, 12.5054** (**2.5 km**); Porziuncola **43.0578, 12.5811** (1.45 km); Stadel **50.1032, 8.6739** (370 m); San Bernardo alle Terme **41.9036, 12.4944** (235 m); Overbeck-Gesellschaft **53.8702, 10.6901** (570 m); Behnhaus **53.8701, 10.6896** (490 m); Penymynydd **53.155, -3.040** (**5.5 km**).

The **Casino Massimo** error is the instructive one: the pool had pinned the modern German Academy at Largo di Villa Massimo, founded 1910, unrelated to Overbeck.

## 1. Lubeck, 1789-1806

**Birth**, 3 July 1789; third generation of Lutheran pastors and civic officials; father Christian Adolph Overbeck, doctor of law, poet, burgomaster of the free city. **[A: EN, DE, Encyclopedia.com]**

**Baptism.** Not documented. No reachable source cites a baptismal register. The Marienkirche is entered as the near-certain parish of a Konigstrasse patrician family, on custom. **[R]**

**First drawing instruction** from a man named Mau. **[A: DE]** *Corrected:* the pool called Mau an artillery **officer**; DE says *Artillerie-Unteroffizier*, a non-commissioned officer.

**Katharineum**, Prima class, September 1803. **[A: DE]** **Peroux:** formal study under the Lubeck painter Joseph Nicolaus Peroux, 1804. **[A: DE]** No street address for his workshop is recorded; the file says so and reuses the corrected Katharineum pin as a quarter-level stand-in.

**Departure**, 6 March 1806, for Vienna. **[A: DE gives the day; EN only "March 1806"]** Precision, not fact. The gate is **[R]**: the Holstentor is the traditional western exit and no source names the gate he used.

**Gap:** no source reached records him returning to Lubeck in the remaining sixty-three years. Stated in the file as absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

## 2. Vienna, 1806-1810

**The Academy** under Heinrich Friedrich Fuger from 1806. **[A: EN, DE]** In 1806-1810 it sat in the St.-Anna-Hof, Annagasse 3, not the Schillerplatz palace the pool had pinned, which was occupied only on 1 April 1877.

**The complaint**, 1808: he writes to his father that the teaching lacks heart, soul, and sensation. **[A: EN]** Month and day unrecorded.

**The Lukasbund**, founded 1809. **[A: DE; corroborated by EN]** *Corrected:* the pool named three founders (Overbeck, Pforr, Vogel). There were **six**; add Hottinger, Wintergerst, Sutter. All six are named in the file. No month is given in any source reached; the file defaults to January and says so.

**Expulsion**, 1810, after roughly four years of conflict. **[A: EN; ADB confirms, "nicht wieder aufgenommen, sondern ausgeschlossen"]** The ADB supplies the circumstance the pool had logged as a gap: it fell during the Academy's post-1809 reorganisation.

---

## 3. The road south, 1810: the largest gap in the file

He leaves Vienna with Pforr and Vogel carrying a half-finished canvas of *Christ's Entry into Jerusalem*, and **arrives in Rome on 20 June 1810**. **[A: ADB, which also gives travel south in spring 1810; EN and DE give only the year]**

**Everything between those points is [R].** No letter, diary, or itinerary was located. Three waypoints are entered, all labelled reconstructions in `date_confidence` rather than smuggled in as facts: **Brenner Pass** (47.0022, 11.5058), the standard Alpine crossing; **Venice** (45.4341, 12.3388), first Italian city on that road, argued from character not documents; **Florence** (43.7696, 11.2558), the Grand Tour route, strong logic and zero evidence. They are recorded rather than omitted so a later researcher need not re-run the dead end. If a letter surfaces, all three fall.

## 4. Rome, 1810-1815: the convent and the conversion

**Sant'Isidoro.** The Lukasbund occupies the disused Franciscan convent of Sant'Isidoro a Capo le Case on the Pincian slope in near-monastic common life; Rome nicknames them *i Nazareni*. **[A: EN, DE]**

*Roster and dating corrected.* The pool dated a community of Overbeck, Pforr, Vogel, Cornelius, Schadow and Veit to 1810. Impossible: **Cornelius and Schadow reached Rome in 1811, Philipp Veit not until 1815**, and the common life had broken up by 1812. Only Overbeck, Pforr, Vogel and Hottinger were there in 1810. The file names the four, dates the others' arrivals, and calls the famous roster a composite of years the convent did not survive. *Also:* "long hair and biblical dress" is embellishment; the nickname derives from the hairstyle worn *alla nazarena*, and dress is dropped.

**The first commission: the pool's most consequential citation error.** The pool attributed the December 1811 letters to the **Casa Bartholdy commission**, which comes only around 1816. Atkinson p. 28 states plainly that the painter's first commission came from **Queen Caroline of Bavaria, for an Adoration of the Kings in oils**. Both letters, 16 and 20 December 1811 (p. 29), belong to that order, and the "Henceforth and for ever" sentence sits **on p. 29 alone**, not pp. 28-29 as the pool stated. **[A: Atkinson pp. 28-29, verified word for word in two archive.org copies]**

**The conversion: a live contradiction, not resolved.** DE gives **April 1813**, under Professor Pietro Ostini, afterward cardinal. Atkinson pp. 33-34 gives Whitsuntide, which fell on 6 June 1813, and adds that Ostini was confessor and the poet Zacharias Werner stood godfather. Both **[A]**. The year is firm, the month is not; the file dates the stop 1813-04-01 for ordering and carries both readings in `date_confidence`.

*Two corrections to that quote.* (1) The pool called it "the book's own narrative sentence rather than a quoted third party's speech." The opposite is true: Atkinson frames it as a record, *"The decisive step ultimately taken is recorded as follows..."*. (2) The pool truncated it; the clause ends "...and with joy entered into the family of the world's Church." The full clause is used.

**Place of conversion is a gap.** The pool placed it at Sant'Isidoro and tagged it **[A]**. No source locates it there, and the convent life had already ended. The file uses the generic Rome pin and claims no building.

## 5. The Roman commissions, 1816-1829

**Casa Bartholdy, Palazzo Zuccari.** Joseph cycle (Overbeck: *Joseph Sold by His Brethren*, *The Seven Years of Famine*) for the Prussian consul-general Jakob Salomon Bartholdy, with Cornelius, Veit and Schadow. **[A: EN, DE]** **Contradiction:** DE dates the cycle 1816/17, EN gives completion 1818. The file uses 1816 for ordering and states both.

**Casino Massimo Lancellotti**, Via Matteo Boiardo 16, near the Lateran **[A: IT, 'Villa Giustiniani Massimo']**. Decoration of Prince Massimo's garden pavilion. **[A: DE gives the campaign 1817-1828; EN the commission 1818]** *Attribution corrected:* the pool credited Overbeck with the Tasso room outright. IT records the Stanza del Tasso as *Overbeck with interventions by Fuhrich (1819-27)*: **Joseph von Fuhrich finished what Overbeck left**. Other assignments check out, Schnorr von Carolsfeld the Ariosto room (1822-27), Veit and Koch the Dante room (1818-28).

**Marriage**, autumn 1818, to **Nina Hartl**, "die schone Wienerin". **[A: ADB]** DE gives "Ehefrau Anna (um 1790-1853)". *Corrected:* the pool tagged the wife **[R]** and called her undocumented. The ADB names her and dates the marriage; the file gives both name forms and keeps only the birth year approximate. Family: son **Alfons Maria** born about 1819 **[R: DE gives the son, not the date]**; two daughters dead in infancy **[A: DE, no names, no dates]**. **Vittoria Caldoni** portrait, 1821 **[A: EN]**.

**Christ's Entry into Jerusalem** completed 1824, installed in the Marienkirche, Lubeck. **[A: DE; the 1942 loss independently attested by EN, 'St. Mary's Church, Lubeck']** *Corrected:* the pool called this loss "a gap in the surviving corpus." Overstated for that church: **Overbeck's *Die Beweinung Christi* survives in the Marienkirche's prayer chapel, north ambulatory.**

**Three chairs refused:** Munich professorship 1826, Dusseldorf 1826, Stadel directorship 1829. **[A: DE]** All year-only, all with Overbeck resident in Rome, so the file pins them to Rome rather than the offering city. **Italia und Germania**, 1828. **[A: EN, DE]**

**Porziuncola, Assisi.** Vision of St Francis over the chapel's outer entrance, under the legend *Haec est porta vitae aeternae*, Francis receiving the indulgence from Christ and the Virgin. **[A: EN, 'Portiuncula']** **Contradiction, not resolved:** DE and EN 'Portiuncula' give **1829**, EN 'Overbeck' gives **1830**; two of three favour 1829, which the file uses for ordering while stating the split. *Corrected:* the "about 6.5 metres long" figure is unsupported. Neither Wikipedia nor the ADB gives dimensions; the ADB says only *grosstes Freskobild*, so the file says "called his largest fresco" and gives no number.

**Gap:** no source describes how, or whether, Overbeck travelled to Assisi, or whether cartoons were sent to be executed on site. The Spoleto waypoint is **[R]**, geography only.

## 6. Honours, losses, papal years, 1839-1864

All **[A]**, source and hedge in parentheses. 1839 Knight, Bavarian Order of St Michael (DE). 1840 son dies in Rome (DE; year only, no age, cause or rite). 1840 *The Triumph of Religion in the Arts*, Stadel (EN, DE). 1848-1857 *Christ Fleeing Persecution*, papal commission (DE). 1850 Papal Gold Medal of Merit (DE). 1853 wife dies (DE). 1854 Knight, Bavarian Maximilian Order (DE). 1855 Knight, Prussian Pour le Merite for arts and sciences (DE). *Assumption of the Madonna*, Cologne Cathedral (**contradiction: EN 1855, DE 1857**). 1858 Pius IX commissions *Christ Delivered to the Jews* (EN). Pius IX visits the house in person (DE, **undated**). *Die Taufe*, Neue Pinakothek Munich (EN, **range 1862-1864**). 1864 Foreign Honorary Member, American Academy of Arts and Sciences (EN).

**Overbeck's own text on antiquity: page corrected, a silently narrowed quote restored.** The pool cited Atkinson p. 13 with two ellipses. The passage is on **p. 14** (the running head "14 OVEKBECK." precedes it), and the unabridged wording, now confirmed word for word, is:

> "Although heathenism, as such, should be looked upon by the Christian painter with decided disdain, yet the arts as well as the literature of the ancients may be turned to advantage, as the children of Israel employed the gold and silver vessels which they brought with them out of Egypt in the service of the true God in His Temple, after melting them down and consecrating them anew."

The pool's second ellipsis had dropped **"as well as the literature"**, narrowing the claim from arts-and-letters to arts alone. The full text is used in the journey file. Atkinson presents it as Overbeck's own words from his explanatory text to *The Triumph of Religion in the Arts*, so the file attaches it to the Stadel stop. Niebuhr's dissent (Atkinson p. 32) is used verbatim on the papal-commission stop as a contemporary voice against the hagiography. **[A]**

**Family burials.** Wife, son and the two infant daughters lie in the **Campo Santo Teutonico** inside the Vatican walls, 41.9016, 12.4535. **[A: DE]** *Corrected:* the pool wrote "no burial location is recorded for her", which the same DE article contradicts. He is not buried with them.

**American Academy pin.** None existed in the pool. Boston (42.3601, -71.0589) is used; the file states it marks the institution's city, not a place Overbeck stood. **[R for the pin, A for the election]**

## 7. Death, burial, afterlife

**Death**, Rome, 12 November 1869, aged 80, after 59 years' residence. **[A: EN, DE]** The street address is not recorded; the file pins it to the painters' quarter below the Pincio and says so.

**Burial**, chapel of St Francis, **San Bernardo alle Terme**, a rotunda of the Baths of Diocletian. **[A: EN, 'San Bernardo alle Terme', which states the burial outright]** *Corrected:* "where he had regularly worshipped" appears in no source consulted; the file names the claim and marks it unsupported.

**The epitaph**, carved by **Karl Hoffmann** (1816-1872). **[A: DE]** *Two pool errors corrected.* (1) The pool called Hoffmann "the husband of his **adopted** daughter". DE says *Firm-Patenkind und gleichzeitiger Schwiegersohn*: confirmation godchild and son-in-law at once. "Adopted" is in no source and reads as an attempt to reconcile that with the infant deaths of two daughters. Dropped. (2) **The date 1871 is unsourced;** DE gives the epitaph no date. Dropped with its gloss "two years after the burial".

**Marienkirche, 29 March 1942.** *Christ's Entry into Jerusalem* burns in the Palm Sunday raid. **[A: DE dates the raid precisely; EN gives "Palm Sunday 1942"]** No replacement or copy recorded. **[R: argument from silence, tagged as one]**

**Overbeck-Gesellschaft, Lubeck, 1918**, named for the Nazarene born in the city and for his family; a venue for contemporary exhibition, not a memorial. **Behnhaus:** standing Nazarene collection centred on Overbeck and his circle, arising from **Charlotte Overbeck's 1914 donation of the estate to the city**, a fact absent from the pool and added here. **Retrospectives:** Frankfurt 1977, Rome 1981, Munich 1984, Lubeck 1989. **Street names** in Cologne-Ehrenfeld, Lubeck, Dresden-Mickten (1904), Berlin-Schoneberg (1939), Nuremberg, Vienna-Hietzing (1938); not given stops. **[A: DE throughout]**

**Penymynydd, North Wales.** Church of St John the Baptist, built 1843 by the Glynne family of Hawarden; its first vicar, the Rev. John Ellis Troughton, spent the church's first twenty years painting its interior with Overbeck murals. **[A: EN, almost verbatim]** 1843 is the build date; the murals span roughly 1843-1863.

**Margaret Howitt**, *Friedrich Overbeck: sein Leben und sein Schaffen*, 2 vols, Herder, Freiburg im Breisgau, 1886. **[A: EN bibliography]** *Split verdict.* The **book** is better attested than the pool's [R] implied; date and imprint hold. The **role** does not: "Overbeck's late-life amanuensis in Rome" is supported by no reachable source. Howitt, daughter of William and Mary Howitt, was an English writer and translator working from his papers after his death. The file keeps the book and separates the amanuensis story off as tradition. **[R]**

## 8. Interlocks with the existing corpus

- **`johann_tischbein`.** Tischbein lodged from 1779 "in the quarter below the Trinita dei Monti where every foreign painter keeps a room and a model", pin 41.9057, 12.4823, marked there as a quarter-level representative with no address. Overbeck enters the same quarter in 1810. **Pin inherited byte-identical**, used for the quarter stop and, where sources give no address, for his house and his death. **[A for the district, R for both men's doors]**
- **`goethe_full`.** Goethe lodged with Tischbein at Via del Corso 18 from 29 October 1786, pin **41.903, 12.483**. **Cut from campa on house-style review (2026-08-15):** no source places Overbeck at that door, and the stop named Goethe only via a coincidental same-street, different-era crossing ("no source puts Overbeck at this door... the pin is inherited from the corpus"). The stop is retitled "cured of antiquity" and rewritten around the Corso as the German artists' quarter generally, with no named-person claim; the pin coordinate is unchanged.
- **`edward_burne_jones`** and **`william_morris`.** Lineage, not contact. Burne-Jones goes to Florence for Botticelli and Fra Angelico, and Morris turns medievalist in England, for the reaction the Lukasbund staged from 1809. Named in the Florence campa, itself a flagged **[R]** waypoint. **[R]**
- **Queued, not researched:** Franz Pforr (QUEUE.md:274), Peter von Cornelius (QUEUE.md:275). Both appear in campa text; neither has a journey file, so neither is an interlock.
- **Checked and ruled out, so nobody re-runs it.** A grep for `nazaren|overbeck|lukasbund|pforr|cornelius|san isidoro` across `working/journeys/*.journey.json` returns only false positives (Lang, Paracelsus, Hannibal, Jesus, Trithemius, Virgil, Cunningham, Nietzsche all contain "Rome" and nothing relevant). `bartholdy|villa massimo|palazzo zuccari` returns nothing, which is why both fresco coordinates had to be sourced fresh. The canonical landmark registry (Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris, Buenos Aires) intersects nothing in his geography, which never left German-speaking lands and papal Rome.

## 9. Standing contradictions, none resolved

Each is flagged in place above and in the relevant stop's `date_confidence`: conversion month 1813; Casa Bartholdy completion; Casino Massimo start; Porziuncola fresco year; Cologne Assumption year; the wife's name; the precision of the 1806 departure.

## 10. Honest gaps

The 1810 itinerary between Vienna and Rome, entirely. The address of Peroux's workshop and of the Roman house where Overbeck lived and died. The gates by which he left Lubeck and entered Rome. The dates of Pius IX's visit and of Hoffmann's epitaph. Age, cause and rite of the son who died in 1840, and the names and dates of the two daughters dead in infancy. Whether he travelled to Assisi at all. The dimensions of the Porziuncola fresco.
