# Erik Satie (1866-1925): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `erik_satie`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition whose
documentary base is thin or absent.

The pool arrived annotated with `CORRECTED:` notes from an adversarial pass over four
lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, afterlife). Those corrections are applied below and
in the journey file. Where two lenses disagree and no third source settles it, the
disagreement is printed and left open.

## 1. Honfleur and the two baptisms, 1866-1878

Eric Alfred Leslie Satie is born 17 May 1866 at Honfleur, Normandy, to Alfred Satie, a
shipping broker, and Jane Leslie (Anton) Satie, of Scottish Protestant descent. Siblings
Olga (1867) and Conrad (1869). **[A: en.wikipedia, "Erik Satie"]**

**Coordinate correction.** The geography lens placed the birth house at 49.4197, 0.2338,
wrong by roughly 400 m. Les Maisons Satie, 67 boulevard Charles V, is at **49.4233,
0.2303** **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Maisons Satie"]**, the pair the afterlife lens already
carried. The two lenses contradicted each other.

**The move to Paris.** The geography lens dated it 1870 and said it "coincides with the
outbreak of the Franco-Prussian War." Both halves are wrong: en.wikipedia reads "After
the Franco-Prussian War Alfred Satie sold his business and the family moved to Paris,"
and the war ran to May 1871, so the move follows it. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

Jane Satie dies in 1872; Erik and Conrad go to their paternal grandparents at Honfleur
and are rebaptized Roman Catholic, having been baptized Anglican; no reachable source
gives the day or the parish. From 1874 Satie studies with Gustave Vinot, the local
organist, whose church the pool does not name. The grandmother dies in 1878 and Erik is
sent back to his father. **[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]** The geography lens says he returned
to a father "who has remarried the composer and pianist Eugenie Barnetche"; sequencing
error, since en.wikipedia dates that marriage to **January 1879**. Erik returned first.
**[A: en.wikipedia]**

**Gap.** The family's Paris addresses, 1871-1872 and after 1878, are recorded nowhere
reachable. Four journey stops carry the Paris centroid 48.8566, 2.3522, marked as such.

## 2. The Conservatoire, twice, 1879-1886

Enrolls in the preparatory piano class November 1879; coordinates 48.8724, 2.3486
unchallenged. Expelled 1882 for unsatisfactory performance; readmitted to the intermediate
class 1885; leaves again without a diploma **[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]**.

**Quote correction.** The quote lens attributed "the laziest student in the
Conservatoire" to Émile Decombes and dated it 1880. Wikipedia reads "The following year
Decombes called him 'the laziest student in the Conservatoire'," placing it in **1881**,
which the chronology lens independently gives. Year corrected, accent restored.
**[A: en.wikipedia]** Satie on the building, verbatim and unchallenged: "a vast, very
uncomfortable, and rather ugly building; a sort of district prison with no beauty on the
inside, nor on the outside, for that matter" **[A: quoted in en.wikipedia]**.

**Military service.** Volunteers for the 33rd Infantry Regiment at Arras, November 1886
**[A: en.wikipedia]**; invalided out 1887 after deliberately inducing bronchitis
**[A for the discharge; R for the mechanism, which the geography lens calls "standard
biographical tradition"]**. Arras pin 50.2911, 2.7783 unchallenged.

**The first signature.** The **Allegro** for piano of 1884 is the first known composition
and the first work signed "Erik" rather than "Eric" **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The chronology
lens placed it in Paris; en.wikipedia states it was written at Honfleur. Corrected.

## 3. Montmartre, 1887-1898

Discharged, Satie settles in the 9th arrondissement in 1887 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**, pin
48.8822, 2.3407. The geography lens says he composed the **Ogives** "during this first
year there"; they are conventionally dated **1886**, before the move **[R: conventional
dating, no document offered]**.

**Le Chat Noir.** Fixture then second pianist at Rodolphe Salis's cabaret, 1887-1888;
friendship with the poet Contamine de Latour **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Coordinate
correction: the lens gave 48.8822, 2.3372; the second Chat Noir stood at 12 rue
Victor-Massé, **48.8807, 2.3393** (OpenStreetMap), 230 m away.

**Gymnopédies 1888, Gnossiennes 1889** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Lodging pin 48.8867, 2.3431,
byte-identical to the Cimetière de Montmartre pin already used in the Delsarte,
Falconetti, Laban and Fritz Lang files. The geography lens supplied it and it is kept.

**Debussy: contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** The chronology lens dates the
meeting at the Auberge du Clou to **1890**; the geography lens dates it to **1891**.
English Wikipedia names the venue and gives no year, and nothing reachable adjudicates.
The journey file follows **1891**, the conventional date, and marks the stop contested.
Pin 48.8801, 2.3406.

**Rose+Croix: a second date contradiction, flagged.** The chronology lens says Satie
became composer to Joséphin Péladan's order in **1890**; fr.wikipedia gives **1891**. The
first Salon de la Rose+Croix opened at the Galerie Durand-Ruel in **March 1892**, and
Satie broke with Péladan by mid-1892 over *Uspud* **[A: en.wikipedia, "Salon de la
Rose + Croix"]**. The journey file pins the episode at the Salon, 1892, 48.8735, 2.3384.

**Suzanne Valadon.** A five-month liaison in 1893, during which Satie composes the
*Danses gothiques*; he proposes marriage and is refused **[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]**.
The geography lens dated it "1893-1896," misstating it, and attached to Satie a sentiment
about "cold loneliness that fills the head with fire," conceding in its source field that
this paraphrases things "attributed to Satie's letters." Not a quotation: downgraded to
**[R]**, unused. Rue Cortot pin 48.8868, 2.3406.

**The Église Métropolitaine d'Art de Jésus Conducteur**, founded 1893, Satie its sole
member and "Parcier et Maître de Chapelle," issuing polemics from a self-styled Abbatiale
**[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]**. Pin 48.8867, 2.3406.

**Vexations: tag corrected to [R].** The chronology lens tagged it **[A]**, dated 1893.
The manuscript is **undated**; scholars assign c. 1893-1894 on musical and biographical
grounds **[R: en.wikipedia, "Vexations"]**. The 840 repetitions and the preface telling
the performer to "prepare himself, in the deepest silence, by serious immobilities" are
genuine; the year is conjecture.

**The velvet suits.** A small inheritance in 1895 buys **seven** identical suits, one per
day, and the nickname "the Velvet Gentleman" **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Internal contradiction
inside the afterlife lens: item 3 says the wardrobe found after his death held "a dozen,"
item 4 says seven were bought, and fr.wikipedia gives no number. Seven is sourced; the
dozen is not.

## 4. Arcueil, 1898-1912

In 1898 Satie moves to a single room at **22 rue Cauchy, Arcueil**, in the Maison des
Quatre Cheminées, without running water or electricity, and lives there until his death
**[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Coordinate correction: the lens gave 48.8087, 2.3344, about 490 m
north of the street and outside it; rue Cauchy runs 48.8043, 2.3343 to 48.8018, 2.3320
(OpenStreetMap), and the journey file uses **48.8043, 2.3343**. He walks the roughly ten
kilometres between Arcueil and central Paris almost daily for twenty-seven years, stopping
at cafés **[R: biographical tradition (Orledge, Volta); route undocumented]**. Midpoint
48.8332, 2.3325, kept as a route marker.

**Cabaret songs: attributions corrected.** The chronology lens said Satie composed
"roughly thirty cabaret songs to texts by Vincent Hyspa, including 'Je te veux' and 'La
Diva de l'Empire'." The count and the Hyspa collaboration (c. 1898-1908) are sound
**[A]**, but both examples are wrong: "Je te veux" is to a text by **Henry Pacory** and
"La Diva de l'Empire" to one by **Dominique Bonnaud and Numa Blès**. The journey file
keeps the collaboration and the two songs separate.

Satie hears the premiere of Debussy's *Pelléas et Mélisande* in 1902 and re-evaluates his
own direction **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Schola Cantorum: dates reconciled, not smoothed.** Enrolls October 1905 to study
counterpoint with Albert Roussel and composition with Vincent d'Indy **[A: en. and
fr.wikipedia]**. The geography lens says he "completes his studies" in 1908; the
chronology lens gives **1912**; en.wikipedia says he continued "until 1912." Adopted:
**counterpoint diploma 1908, attendance to 1912**, both dates kept. Pin 48.841, 2.3418.

**Ravel, January 1911.** Ravel performs early Satie at a Société musicale indépendante
concert and Satie's belated fame begins **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Two corrections: Ravel
played the **second Sarabande** among other early pieces, not "the Sarabandes" as a set;
and the **Salle Gaveau attribution is unverified**, as the lens's own note admits
**[R: venue]**. Salle Gaveau is at 48°52'23"N 2°18'50"E = **48.8731, 2.3139**
**[A: fr.wikipedia]**, not the lens's 48.8725, 2.3117.

*Véritables Préludes flasques (pour un chien)*, 1912 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 5. Parade and the trial, 1914-1918

*Sports et divertissements*, 1914 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Quote correction, the chorale preface.** The quote lens gave "For the SHRIVELLED and
the DIMWITS, I have written a suitably ponderous chorale ... a kind of austere,
unfrivolous introduction ... I dedicate this chorale to those who do not like me,"
sourced to a generic "annotation in a score." The middle clause is **not** in the Atlas
Press blurb for *A Mammal's Notebook* that carries the rest; it comes from a different
rendering (en.wikipedia, "Sports et divertissements": "a kind of austere & unfrivolous
introduction"). The source is identifiable as Satie's **1914 preface to Sports et
divertissements** ("Pour les racornis et les abrutis j'ai écrit un choral grave et
convenable"). The journey file quotes only the Atlas Press text, interpolation removed.

Cocteau, 1915 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Polignac commission, October 1916, leading to
*Socrate* **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Parade premieres 18 May 1917 at the Théâtre du Châtelet**, Ballets Russes, scenario
Cocteau, décor Picasso, choreography Massine **[A: en.wikipedia; the exact day comes from
general Parade records, not from the fetched Satie chronology, which gives
only the year]**. Châtelet pin **48.8583, 2.347**, inherited byte-identical from the
d'Annunzio file.

**The postcard: wording verified, date wrong.** English Wikipedia's *Parade (ballet)*,
citing Mary Davis, *Erik Satie*, p. 120, gives "Monsieur et cher ami – vous êtes un cul,
un cul sans musique! Signé Erik Satie" ("Sir and dear friend – you are an arse, an arse
without music! Signed, Erik Satie") **[A]**. The pool stripped the diacritics from *êtes*
and *Signé* and replaced the dash with a comma; both restored. The pool dated the postcard
1917-05-18, which is the **premiere**. The postcard postdates Poueigh's review, itself
after the premiere (fr.wikipedia places the review in May 1917), and is commonly dated
**30 May 1917**, though neither Wikipedia article assigns a day **[R: 30 May]**. A variant
circulates in French sources: "vous n'êtes qu'un cul, mais un cul sans musique."

**The trial.** Poueigh sues for libel; Satie receives an eight-day sentence; Cocteau is
arrested and beaten by police for shouting "arse" in the courtroom **[A: en.wikipedia,
citing Austin and Davis]**. The Comtesse Étienne de Beaumont paying the fine, and the
courtroom's identity, are **[R]**: the geography lens calls the venue "inferred as the
Paris courts of the period." Palais de Justice pin 48.8558, 2.3452, tagged inferred.

Satie quarrels with Debussy in 1917, resentful of the greater appreciation Debussy
received; Debussy dies the following year without full reconciliation
**[A: en.wikipedia]**. *Socrate* completed 1918 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

## 6. The last years, 1920-1925

Salle Érard festival, 1920, and around that time the *Elégie* of the *Quatre petites
mélodies* in memory of Debussy **[A: en.wikipedia for festival and Elégie; R for Salle
Érard as venue, from "standard concert-history references, not re-fetched"]**. Pin
48.8672, 2.3421. Satie and Cocteau are regarded as spiritual fathers of **Les Six**,
formed around 1920 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**École d'Arcueil**, 1923: Henri Cliquet-Pleyel, Roger Désormière, Maxime Jacob, Henri
Sauguet **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

*Mercure*, 1924, Massine and Picasso **[A: en.wikipedia]**. *Relâche*, 1924, with Francis
Picabia and René Clair for the Ballets Suédois, incorporating Clair's film *Entr'acte*
**[A: en.wikipedia]**; the premiere date **4 December 1924** and the Théâtre des
Champs-Élysées as hall come from Orledge via the geography lens, not re-fetched **[R for
day and venue]**. Pin 48.8656, 2.3033. Satie's gloss, unchallenged: "The music for
Relache? I was portraying people 'out on a spree'. Using popular themes for the purpose."
**[A: quoted in en.wikipedia]**

**Picabia quote restored.** The lens truncated "He's a very susceptible man, arrogant, a
real sad child" mid-sentence without an ellipsis; the sentence ends ", but one who is
sometimes made optimistic by alcohol," which is what softens it. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

**Death.** Health collapses in 1925 after lifelong heavy drinking; admitted to the
Hôpital Saint-Joseph in **February 1925**, dies there of cirrhosis on **1 July 1925**,
aged 59 **[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]**. Friends called the cirrhosis "soigneusement
cultivée" **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Coordinate correction: the geography lens gave 48.8305,
2.3159, the afterlife lens the Paris centroid 4 km off; the hospital, 185-189 rue
Raymond-Losserand, is at **48.8294, 2.3122** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Burial: a three-way coordinate contradiction.** Geography lens 48.8065, 2.3312;
afterlife lens 48.8025, 2.3339; OpenStreetMap **48.8040, 2.3402**, roughly 700 m from the
first and 500 m from the second. All three are printed; the journey file takes
OpenStreetMap. The grave bears a white cross **[A: en.wikipedia captions a photograph of
"Satie's grave bearing a white cross in Arcueil"]**.

**The sealed room: tag corrected to [R].** Friends enter the Arcueil room for the first
time in twenty-seven years and find, in the various tellings, two decrepit pianos bound or
stacked together, unopened correspondence, unpublished manuscripts including a lost opera,
umbrellas, false collars, and identical grey velvet suits. Chronology and afterlife lenses
tagged this **[A]**; the geography lens tagged the same anecdote **[R]**, which is right:
fr.wikipedia carries the substance, but details vary, as the pool's cross-source note
concedes. Two flourishes are dropped from the journey file: that Satie had "nonetheless
answered" the letters, and that the manuscripts included "works Satie had told friends did
not survive."

## 7. Afterlife

Templier's first biography appears 1932, its English translation only 1969
**[A: en.wikipedia]**. The **first complete Vexations**, 9 September 1963, Pocket Theatre,
100 Third Avenue, Manhattan (40.7328, -73.986), organized by John Cage: 840 repetitions in
a relay over eighteen hours, the team including Cage, David Tudor, Christian Wolff, John
Cale, James Tenney and Joshua Rifkin, with $5 admission and a 5-cent refund per 20 minutes
attended **[A: en.wikipedia, "Vexations"]**. That the actor Karl Schenzer was the only
person present throughout is **[R: press report, possibly embellished]**. Cage's advocacy
recasts Satie as a forerunner of minimalism **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Les Maisons Satie**, 67 boulevard Charles V, Honfleur, three linked 15th-century
half-timbered houses, opens 21 June 1998 on the Fête de la Musique; façades listed since
1992; a mechanical winged pear and a self-playing grand piano among the displays. Streets
in Paris, Arcueil, Créteil and Bobigny; asteroid (9438) Satie; a 1992 postage stamp
**[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The 2025 centenary brought BBC coverage and a digital album of 27
previously unpublished compositions **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 8. Atlas interlock

Satie sits at **row 140 of QUEUE.md** with a one-line brief, "French avant-garde
composer," while Diaghilev, Stravinsky, Picasso and Debussy are queued with fuller briefs
naming the Ballets Russes and Parade cluster he belongs to **[A: QUEUE.md 227-296]**. His
brief should be rewritten to that framing. Only two journey files name him, both outside
that cluster:

`buckminster_fuller.journey.json` line 360, Fuller acting in John Cage's staging of a
Satie work at Black Mountain College, summer 1948, alongside Cage, Cunningham and de

Medusa **[R: identification]**; and `suzanne_perrottet.journey.json` line 449, Perrottet
playing whole soirées of Schoenberg and Satie for the Dadaists at Hugo Ball's Cabaret
Voltaire **[A]**.

Adjacencies sharing a pin or a room but not a name, all **[R]**:
`george_balanchine.journey.json` line 265 (Monte Carlo 1925, Balanchine with Stravinsky,

(Man Ray photographs Proust's deathbed, 20 November 1922, at Cocteau's request);
`gurdjieff.journey.json` line 817 (Institute demonstrations from 13 December 1923 at the

## 9. Honest gaps

Paris addresses 1871-1898 apart from rue Cortot: unrecorded, four stops on the centroid.
Gustave Vinot's church at Honfleur: unnamed in the pool. *Vexations*: undated manuscript,
c. 1893-1894 an assignment. The Poueigh postcard's date: convention only. Salle Gaveau
(1911) and Salle Érard (1920) as venues, the libel trial's courtroom, and *Relâche*'s
exact day and hall: all from Orledge via the pool or inferred, none re-fetched. The
near-drowning under the uncle Adrien: family anecdote, no document. The sealed-room
inventory: every version differs.
