# Eduardo S. Rodríguez Ortega: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: the engineer of the two Gaudí-referencing buildings on Avenida Rivadavia in Balvanera, Buenos Aires: the Casa (Palacio) de los Lirios at Rivadavia 2027/2031, and the domed corner building at Rivadavia and Ayacucho.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, with the source named in the same sentence. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, family testimony, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**The governing fact of this file.** There is no biography of Eduardo S. Rodríguez Ortega. Spanish Wikipedia has no article on him: the API returns `missing:true` for both "Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega" and "Eduardo S. Rodríguez Ortega", and the link inside the "Casa de los Lirios" article is a red link **[A: es.wikipedia API, fetched 2026-08-15]**. Everything below is assembled from four articles about *buildings*, plus one newspaper profile from 2021 that interviewed his grandson. The documented life runs from 1903 to 1938 and consists of two street addresses.

---

## 1. Origin: a birth year that cannot be right

The only birth year in circulation is **1831**, appearing in the body prose of the Spanish Wikipedia article "Casa de los Lirios": *"fue construido entre 1903 y 1905 por el ingeniero [[Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega]] (1831-1938)"* **[A: es.wikipedia, raw wikitext, Descripción section, fetched 2026-08-15]**.

Three cautions, all material:

1. This is **not** an infobox figure. An earlier draft of this research placed it in an infobox; the article has no infobox at all. The parenthesis sits in running text and is **unsourced within the article**, falling outside the span covered by the article's single La Nación reference **[A: direct inspection of the wikitext]**.
2. **1831 is almost certainly a transcription error.** It would make him seventy-two years old at his first known commission, in his eighties when he is said to have introduced reinforced concrete to Buenos Aires, and one hundred and seven at death. No corroborating source of any kind was located **[R: internal implausibility judgement, not a documentary refutation]**.
3. Because 1831 is unreliable, the paired **1938** in the same parenthesis inherits the doubt. See §6.

**Birthplace: no source gives one.** Not a city, not a province, not a country of origin beyond the contested nationality below **[A: gap, after searching es.wikipedia, La Nación, Barriada]**.

### Nationality: an unresolved contradiction

- **Argentine.** es.wikipedia, "Casa de los Lirios", lead: *"El proyectista fue el ingeniero argentino Eduardo S. Rodríguez Ortega"* **[A]**.
- **Spanish.** Barriada.com.ar, "La Casa de los Lirios": *"el ingeniero español Eduardo S. Rodríguez Ortega"* **[A]**.
- La Nación (2021) presents him as an Argentine who studied abroad, without asserting nationality in those words **[A]**.

The two encyclopedic assertions are flatly opposed. **Not adjudicated here.** Note that Barriada elsewhere reproduces es.wikipedia's prose verbatim (see §3), so it is not an independent witness on most points; on nationality it diverges, which is itself worth recording.

## 2. Berlin and the European years

All of this rests on **one** interview: the architect's grandson and namesake, **Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega**, speaking to Germán Wille for La Nación, 10 February 2021 **[A that he said it; [R] as to the content, which is family memory at one remove and a century's distance]**.

- He trained and graduated as an **ingeniero civil** in **Berlin, Germany** **[R: grandson, via La Nación]**.
- In Berlin he *"hizo muchas cosas"*, did many things. **No German commission, employer, drawing, diploma, or school has been traced.** The grandson names none and no other source supplies one **[A: gap]**.
- He *"estuvo mucho tiempo en Europa"* before bringing his practice to Buenos Aires **[R: grandson]**.
- **No date, no duration, no itinerary.** The European period is undated at both ends **[A: gap]**.

The German title has stuck. Sources that reach for a professional word call him *ingeniero* far more often than *arquitecto*; the "Balvanera" article is the outlier, writing *"el Arq. Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega"* **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Balvanera']**.

### Did he meet Gaudí?

The grandson, asked directly, **does not know**, and offers only that his grandfather may have absorbed Catalan modernist influence while in Europe **[R: grandson, via La Nación]**.

What *is* on the record is a reputation, not a meeting: *"ha sido catalogado como un admirador del arquitecto Antonio Gaudí (máximo exponente del modernismo catalán en Barcelona)"* **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Casa de los Lirios']**.

**Source-independence flag.** Barriada.com.ar carries that sentence **word for word**, so the "admirer" attribution is *one* source mirrored downstream, not two independent ones. La Nación does not use the formulation at all **[A: textual comparison, fetched 2026-08-15]**. An earlier framing of this material as "catalogued by multiple sources" overstated the evidence and is corrected here.

**No source places him in Barcelona, or in Spain at all.** A Spanish visit is a reasonable inference from the buildings and from "mucho tiempo en Europa", and it is nothing more than that **[R]**.

## 3. The commission: Casa de los Lirios, 1903-1905

- **Client: Miguel Capurro**, a businessman in textiles and wine, characterised by architecture researcher **Rodolfo Liechtenstein** as *"un millonario que no invertía en educación, pero sí en ladrillos"* **[A: La Nación, quoting Liechtenstein]**.
- **Site: Av. Rivadavia 2027/2031, Balvanera** **[A: La Nación; es.wikipedia]**.
- **Dates: built 1903 to 1905** **[A: La Nación and es.wikipedia agree]**.
- **Setting.** Avenida Rivadavia at the time was still *"el Camino real del Oeste"*, and the city's growth was running toward Balvanera; this was outside the fashionable palace circuit **[A: La Nación, quoting Liechtenstein]**.

### Program: a mild disagreement

- **La Nación**: ground-floor commercial premises, two rental floors of two apartments each, and a **third floor held for the owning family** **[A]**.
- **es.wikipedia**: ground floor with two commercial spaces and a central residential entrance, **three upper floors with paired apartments**, no family floor mentioned **[A]**.

Both describe the same income-producing machine; they differ on whether the top floor was let or reserved. **Left open.** No plan or municipal permit was located.

### Fabric and ornament

- **Facade cladding: "piedra París"** (Spanish in the source, *not* French "pierre de Paris"), described by **Juan Carlos Willy Pastrana**, Presidente de la Asociación Art Nouveau de Buenos Aires, as *"una copia de la piedra sólida que se usaba en Europa"* **[A: La Nación]**.
- **Ironwork by Italian immigrant blacksmiths**: *"las barandas de los balcones y la decoración de la puerta - todas de hierro - fueron hechas por herreros que venían de Italia"*, the entrance door standing roughly **four metres** high **[A: La Nación]**.
- **Carved flora**: *lirios* (irises/lilies) and *jacintos* (hyacinths). es.wikipedia: *"El capitel y los manojos de jacinto que rodean todo el frente"* **[A]**. **Correction to an earlier draft: there are no orchids.** No source mentions orchids; that detail was introduced in error and is withdrawn.
- **Organic facade.** Liechtenstein: *"la fachada es orgánica, lo que significa que tiene su inspiración en la naturaleza, el movimiento y las ondulaciones"* **[A: La Nación / es.wikipedia]**. An earlier draft quoted this as *"inspirada en la naturaleza, con flores, movimiento y ondulaciones"*; that string is a paraphrase and does not appear in the source. Withdrawn in favour of the verbatim text.
- **Bow windows** are noted in the es.wikipedia description **[A]**.

### The head at the cornice: an unresolved naming dispute

A plaster head of a bearded old man crowns the central cornice, his hair continuing outward to form the rooftop railing **[A: es.wikipedia; La Nación]**. Liechtenstein reads it as *"representa el viento, la libertad, la búsqueda de algo nuevo"* **[A: quoted in es.wikipedia; the phrase is unattributed within the article itself]**. Some accounts call the figure **Aeolus**, others **Neptune** **[A: es.wikipedia records both]**. **No source resolves it, and none cites a contract, drawing, or sculptor's name.**

### Class context

Pastrana and Liechtenstein both observe that Buenos Aires's Art Nouveau clients were the commercial bourgeoisie rather than the educated upper class, who regarded the style as *"algo superfluo"*; the ornate commissions were partly *"a modo contestatario"* **[A: La Nación]**.

**Reinforced concrete, claim one.** Barriada.com.ar cites Casa de los Lirios as among the first buildings in Argentina to use reinforced concrete **[A that Barriada says it; [R] as to the fact; no engineering-history source corroborates]**. Compare claim two, §4.

## 4. The corner at Rivadavia and Ayacucho

A second building, a few metres from the first, at the corner of Av. Rivadavia and Ayacucho.

**Construction date: a direct, unresolved contradiction.**

| Source | Date |
|---|---|
| es.wikipedia, "Balvanera": *"Tiene cinco pisos y fue construido en 1907 por el Arq. Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega"* | **1907** **[A]** |
| es.wikipedia, "Avenida Rivadavia": *"(Ing. Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega, año 1907)"* | **1907** **[A]** |
| La Nación 2021, quoting the grandson | **1914** **[A that he says it]** |

The two Wikipedia articles are not independent of each other. The grandson is family memory. **Not adjudicated.** A further possibility the sources leave open, and which no source states, is that these are two different structures at the same corner **[R: speculation, flagged as such]**.

**Attested features:**

- **Five storeys** **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Balvanera']**.
- A **glazed dome** (*cúpula vidriada*) **[A]**.
- Bearing the Catalan legend **"No hi ha somnis impossibles"**, "there are no impossible dreams" **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Balvanera', citing barriada.com.ar and a TripAdvisor listing]**.
- *"La ornamentación del edificio que la sostiene lleva el sello inequívoco del estilo catalán"* **[A: same]**.

### The dragon: which Gaudí work?

es.wikipedia, "Avenida Rivadavia", verbatim: *"En la esquina de la calle Ayacucho resalta la cúpula vidriada de un edificio de estilo modernista catalán que homenajea al catalán Antonio Gaudí con un dragón de hierro similar al del **Parc Güell** (Ing. Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega, año 1907)"* **[A]**.

The queue brief and a good deal of tourist material instead identify the ornament with the **dragon door of the Casa Batlló** **[A that the attribution circulates; no sourced statement of it was located]**. Note also that Wikipedia describes iron ornament *on the building*, not specifically door hardware.

**Both attributions circulate. Not resolved here.** An earlier draft of this research reported the dragon as unconfirmable; that hedge is withdrawn, the dragon is sourced, and the open question is which Barcelona work it copies.

**Reinforced concrete, claim two.** The grandson: this corner building was *"la primera edificación de Buenos Aires hecha con hormigón armado"*, built with technology his grandfather brought back from Europe **[R: single source, family testimony, uncorroborated by any engineering-history source]**. Set against Barriada's competing claim for the lily house (§3), the family has two "firsts" and the record has neither.

## 6. Death, 1938: a single-source date

**Downgraded from [A] to [R] in this report.** An earlier draft called 1938 "the one biographical date corroborated by two separate sources". That is wrong on both counts:

1. The Wikipedia "corroboration" is the parenthesis *(1831-1938)* in the body of "Casa de los Lirios", unsourced, and the same string whose first half this report judges an error. It is not independent evidence.
2. The La Nación anecdote does **not** state a death year. It states that in 1938 he intended to move into a second-floor apartment of the Palacio de los Lirios and *"mi abuelo murió antes de que a los inquilinos de entonces se les venciera el contrato"* **[A that this is what the grandson said]**. A death *before a lease expired* is compatible with a date later than 1938.

**No obituary, burial register, cemetery plot, or civil record was located.** No tomb is known **[A: gap]**. 1938 stands as a single-source, family-adjacent figure and is used in the journey file only with that hedge carried in `date_confidence`.

## 7. Afterlife

- **2006.** Senator **Luis A. Falcó** files **expediente PL 2993/06** in the Argentine Senate proposing that the Palacio de los Lirios be declared **Monumento Histórico Nacional** **[A: Senate expediente listing, linked from es.wikipedia]**. **The outcome of the bill is not recorded in any source reached**, a gap. The building's current status is municipal, not national, which is suggestive but not evidence.
- **Heritage status.** es.wikipedia: the Casa de los Lirios *"es considerado Representativo por el Gobierno de la Ciudad de Buenos Aires, y cuenta con un nivel integral de protección"* **[A]**. **The year of listing is given by no source**, a gap. **Correction:** an earlier draft extended this protection to *both* buildings; the sentence covers only the Casa de los Lirios, and neither the "Balvanera" nor the "Avenida Rivadavia" article assigns the corner building any tier **[A: direct check]**.
- **2021.** Germán Wille's La Nación profile, built on the interview with the grandson, is **the single fullest surviving account of his life** and the sole source for Berlin, the European years, the Gaudí uncertainty, and the unoccupied apartment **[A]**.
- **Milieu.** Pastrana counts roughly **300** buildings with Art Nouveau characteristics in Buenos Aires, **75** of high architectural quality, and calls the city *"la capital americana del Art Nouveau"* **[A: La Nación]**. Liechtenstein puts the wider mansion boom at some **220** ornate houses between the late nineteenth century and the First World War, *"una época récord para la construcción, en cantidad y calidad"* **[A]**.
- Pastrana on the lily house: *"Este edificio es realmente una joya de la elegancia, del refinamiento y de la expresión de la naturaleza."* **[A: La Nación]**. An earlier draft quoted this with three definite articles dropped; the verbatim form is restored.

## 9. Interlock

**One relation is real and one-directional: Antoni Gaudí.** Rodríguez Ortega is catalogued as an admirer of Gaudí **[A: es.wikipedia]**; his corner building *"homenajea al catalán Antonio Gaudí"* **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Avenida Rivadavia']**. This is influence and homage, running one way, and it is citable even though Gaudí certainly never knew the man existed. The QUEUE.md row for Gaudí (line 882) states the point from the other side: no Gaudí building exists in Argentina, but his influence appears in these two buildings, which are **not** to be attributed to Gaudí himself.

**Everything else found in the corpus is shared geography and is deliberately excluded from the journey prose**: Mario Palanti's Edificio de los Atlantes at Rivadavia 1916 and Palacio Barolo; Alejandro Christophersen's 1907 church further west on Rivadavia; Juan Antonio Buschiazzo and Torcuato de Alvear on the Avenida de Mayo axis two decades earlier; Alberto Gerchunoff's 1930s Once. Same street, no documented contact, no influence in either direction. Virginio Colombo, working the Liberty line in the same barrio, is likewise a neighbour and not a link; the further inference that this makes Rodríguez Ortega a "stylistic outlier" is the researcher's, **[R]**, and appears nowhere in the Balvanera article.

A direct grep across all `*.journey.json` files returns **no existing mention** of Rodríguez Ortega, the Casa de los Lirios, the "No hi ha somnis impossibles" building, or the Casa Batlló dragon door **[A]**.
