# Antoni Gaudí i Cornet (1852-1926) - research report
*Compiled 2026-08-16 for the Atlas of Journeys. Catalan architect, born Reus (or Riudoms), died Barcelona. Pool: five lenses, each adversarially re-checked.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or contested attribution. Disagreements between sources are printed, not adjudicated. Coordinate corrections are recorded because several were large enough (up to 14 km) to put pins in the wrong municipality.

---

## 1. Reus, Riudoms, and the birth two towns claim

- Born 25 June 1852, youngest of five children of Francesc Gaudí i Serra, coppersmith and boilermaker, and Antònia Cornet i Bertran. **[A - Britannica; en.wikipedia "Antoni Gaudí"; Reus civil and parish records as cited in Bergós and Van Hensbergen]**
- Baptized 26 June 1852 at the Església Prioral de Sant Pere, Reus, as Antoni Plàcid Guillem Gaudí i Cornet. That register is the document naming Reus. **[A - parish baptismal register]**
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** Riudoms tradition holds he was born at the maternal farmhouse, the Mas de la Calderera. English Wikipedia flags the birthplace as disputed, and both towns still claim him. **[R]**
  - *Coordinates.* The chronology lens gave Riudoms as 41.0864, 1.0508, open country ~5.9 km south of the village. Riudoms is at **41.1391, 1.0520** (Nominatim). The geography lens separately put the Mas de la Calderera at 41.1631, 1.0739. The two do not agree on the farmhouse; the journey pins the checkable village coordinate and says so.
- Childhood rheumatism; the tradition that convalescence sharpened his observation of natural form rests on no contemporary document. **[R]**
- Piarist school in Reus, 1863-1868; the schoolboy magazine *El Arlequín* with Eduard Toda and Josep Ribera, and the boys' scheme to restore the ruined monastery of Poblet. **[A - school records and Toda/Ribera recollections, in Bergós]** *Correction:* the geography lens named "Col.legi de les Escolapies," the **female** Piarist congregation; he attended the male house, the **Escoles Pies de Reus**.

## 2. Barcelona: the student, 1868-1878

- **Llotja** school of design, c.1868/1870-1873, working as a draughtsman to pay his way. **[A - en.wikipedia; Bergós]**
- **Escola Tècnica Superior d'Arquitectura**, 1873-1878. **[A]** *Correction:* the chronology lens gave "1876-1878," his final two years only; the geography lens has the split right.
- Military service as Military Administrator, 1875-1878. **[A]**
- Student work under **Josep Fontserè** on the Cascada, Parc de la Ciutadella. **[A - Fontserè project records, in Van Hensbergen]** *Correction:* built **1875-1881**, not 1876-1882, and his documented share is the **hydraulic calculation of the elevated reservoir** plus rockwork, not "its ironwork." Coordinates confirmed.
- 1876: mother dies at 57; brother Francesc, newly qualified physician, dies at 25. **[A]**
- Graduates 1878. **[A]** The remark attributed to director **Elies Rogent** ("either a fool or a genius") is an undocumented anecdote. **[R]** *Correction:* the chronology lens dated graduation 15 March 1878 and tagged the quote [A]; Wikipedia gives only the year, and the quote must carry [R].
- The **Manuscrito de Reus** (1873-1878) is the only substantial text in his own hand. Everything else circulating as his doctrine was written down by disciples: Ràfols, Bergós, Martinell, Puig i Boada. **[A - es.wikipedia]**

## 3. First commissions and Güell, 1878-1884

- Cast-iron lampposts, **Plaça Reial**, 1878. **[A]**
- A display case for the glove maker **Comella** at the Paris Universal Exposition, 1878, is seen by **Eusebi Güell**, who asks who made it; four decades of patronage follow. **[A for the meeting's origin; R for the detail that Gaudí did not travel to Paris - Van Hensbergen]** The journey keeps the Champ de Mars pin because the object is his, and says in `date_confidence` that he was not beside it.
- **Cooperativa Obrera Mataronense**, 1878-1882: factory, worker housing, a parabolic-arch hall. Mostly unbuilt. **[A - project archives]**
- c.1884: **Josefa Moreu**, teacher at the cooperative; unreciprocated. **[R - traditional, no correspondence survives]** *Correction:* the geography lens placed her in **Reus**; es.wikipedia has her as *maestra de la Cooperativa Mataronense*, i.e. **Mataró**. The marriage proposal is the embellishment.
- 1879: joins the **Centre Excursionista de Catalunya**. 1899: the **Cercle Artístic de Sant Lluc**. **[A both]**

## 4. The 1880s

- **Casa Vicens**, Gràcia. Plans signed 15 January 1883, presented to the Gràcia council 20 January, approved 1 March; built **1883-1885**. The garden gate's palmetto ironwork is cast from a palm growing on the site, moulded by Llorenç Matamala, cast by Joan Oñós. **[A - municipal permits; en.wikipedia "Casa Vicens"]**
  - *Corrections.* (a) The client **Manel Vicens i Montaner was a stock and currency broker**, not a tile manufacturer; en.wikipedia notes that "numerous bibliographies erroneously state that Vicens was a tile manufacturer." (b) The geography lens gave the span as 1883-1888. Coordinates **41.4036, 2.1508**.
- **Sagrada Família**: Villar resigns having laid the crypt foundations; Gaudí takes over **November 1883**, formally appointed Architect Director **28 March 1884**, and replaces the neo-Gothic scheme entirely. **[A]**
- **El Capricho**, Comillas, 1883-1885, for Máximo Díaz de Quijano; sunflower tile bands doubling as a sundial. **[A - Comillas municipal records]**
- **Finca Güell** pavilions and dragon gate, 1884-1887. **[A]** *Corrections:* coordinates **41.38944, 2.11917** (~400 m off in the lens); and the Ladon/Hesperides reading is **not** merely a later gloss. Wikipedia documents the programme Gaudí built in: an antimony orange tree above the dragon, the body laid to the star positions of Serpens, the source in Verdaguer's *L'Atlàntida*.
- **Palau Güell**, **1886-1888** (not 1886-1890), coordinates **41.37889, 2.17417**. The central dome is pierced with small holes with lanterns hung outside at night. **[A - building records; UNESCO file]**
- **Compañía Transatlántica pavilion**, Barcelona Universal Exposition of 1888; demolished with the fair, known from drawings. **[A - exposition catalogue]**

## 5. Beyond Catalonia, 1888-1900

- **Col·legi de les Teresianes**, 1888-1890, taken over from **Joan Baptista Pons i Trabal**, of whose Byzantine scheme only foundations existed. **[A - order records]** *Correction:* coordinates **41.3998, 2.1332** (Ganduxer 85); the lens was ~1.1 km off.
- **Palacio Episcopal de Astorga**, 1889-1893, for Bishop **Joan Baptista Grau i Vallespinós**, a fellow Catalan, after a fire. Design approved February 1889; Grau dies 1893 and Gaudí resigns that year; completed 1907-1915 by Ricardo García Guereta. **[A - diocesan archive]** Coordinates **42.4578, -6.0560**.
- **Casa Botines**, León, 1891-1892, for **Simón Fernández** and **Mariano Andrés**. **[A]** *Correction:* the Saint George crowns the **principal entrance**, not a corner niche, and belongs to the **original 1891-92 design** by Llorenç Matamala i Piñol, not "added later."
- 1891: **Málaga and Tangier**, surveying ground for a Franciscan mission. Never built. **[A]** Tangier pin 35.7595, -5.834, inherited byte-identical from the atlas's existing Tangier coordinate.
- **Celler Güell**, Garraf, 1895-1897, under **Francesc Berenguer**'s site direction. **[A]** *Correction, the largest in the pool:* the lens gave 41.2151, 1.7719, roughly **14 km** inland. The Celler is at **41.2559, 1.9054**.
- **Casa Calvet**, 1898-1900; Barcelona City Council prize for best building of 1900, the only such award of his life. **[A - prize records]** *Corrections:* coordinates **41.39083, 2.17306** (two lenses put it several blocks west). **Client contested:** en.wikipedia names **Andreu Calvet, textile manufacturer**; other sources the heirs of Pere Màrtir Calvet. "Pere Calvet" is unsupported; the journey says "a family of textile manufacturers" and flags the split.

## 6. The turn of the century, 1898-1914

- **Crypt of the Colònia Güell**, commissioned 1898; the hanging-chain models here are the laboratory for the Sagrada Família. **[A - Colònia Güell archive; UNESCO file]** *Corrections:* coordinates **41.36358, 2.02792** (~1 km off); and construction ran **1908-1914**, halted by the **Güell family in 1914**, not by Eusebi Güell's decline, since he lived until 1918. The lower nave was in use 1915-1917; the upper church never begun.
- **Park Güell**, 1900-1914: a garden-city scheme that sells two of sixty lots and is ceded to the city as a park. **[A]**
- **Bellesguard**, 1900-1909, on the ruins of the residence of Martí l'Humà, for Jaume Figueras and María Sagués. **[A]** Coordinates **41.4094, 2.1267** (~370 m correction).
- **Casa Batlló**, 1904-1906, remodelling for the Batlló family. The dragon-back reading is popular tradition. **[A for the commission; R for the iconography]**
- **Palma de Mallorca cathedral**, 1904-1914, for Bishop **Pere Campins**: choir screen removed, windows unwalled, altar canopy. Disputes with the chapter and with **Joan Rubió** end it unfinished. **[A - chapter records]**
- **Casa Milà**, presented to the council **2 February 1906**, built 1906-1912. **[A]** *Correction:* the lens anchored occupancy to December 1910; the Milà couple moved in after the council authorised it in **October 1911**, and the completion certificate is dated **31 October 1912**.
- **Park Güell house** (built 1903-1905 by Berenguer as an unsold show house): Gaudí buys it and moves in **1906** with his father, who dies there that year, and his niece **Rosa Egea**, who dies in **1912**. He lives there until late 1925. **[A - Gaudí House Museum]**
- **Montserrat**: the First Glorious Mystery on the Rosary path, with sculptor Josep Llimona, c.1900-1916. 1902: the painter **Joan Llimona** uses Gaudí as model for Saint Philip Neri. **[A both]**
- 1914: **Francesc Berenguer** dies. 1916: **Josep Torras i Bages**, Bishop of Vic, dies. 1918: **Eusebi Güell** dies. **[A all three]**

## 7. The last decade, 1915-1926

- From 1915 he works on nothing but the Sagrada Família. **[A]**
- 1917: the design that guides all later work on the **Passion façade**. **[A]**
- **1920**: beaten by police at the Jocs Florals for speaking Catalan. **[A]** *Correction:* the lens tied this to "Primo de Rivera-era repression"; the coup was September 1923.
- **11 September 1924**: arrested by the Guardia Civil on his way to the Basilica of **Sant Just i Sant Pastor** for a mass for the Catalan dead of 1714; released on 50 pesetas bail. **[A - en. and es.wikipedia]** *Correction:* the geography lens has this wrong on every particular, placing it at a Sant Josep Oriol procession near Sant Felip Neri with an overnight detention. Neither is supported. (The daily-mass habit at **Sant Felip Neri**, 41.3834, 2.1751, is correct.) *Gap:* no lens supplied a coordinate for Sant Just i Sant Pastor; the stop is pinned approximately and says so.
- Late 1925: sells the Park Güell house and moves into a room by the temple workshop, clothes held with safety pins. **[A - contemporary accounts]**
- **7 June 1926**: struck by tram 30 on the **Gran Via de les Corts Catalanes** between Girona and Bailèn; mistaken for a beggar, refused by taxi drivers, taken to the pauper's ward. **[A - Barcelona press, June 1926]** *Correction:* both lenses misplaced the site (41.3865, 2.1665, near Plaça Universitat; and 41.403, 2.1743, which is the Sagrada Família block). The stretch named by the sources is approximately **41.3934, 2.1742** (Nominatim cross-check: Gran Via 700 and Bailèn 29).
- **10 June 1926**: dies at the Hospital de la Santa Creu, aged 73, having refused a private clinic. **[A]** *Correction:* three lenses gave three different coordinates, none matching the building. It is now the Biblioteca de Catalunya, Carrer de l'Hospital 56, at **41.3810, 2.1695**.
- **12 June 1926**: funeral procession across the city; buried in the crypt, chapel of Our Lady of Mount Carmel, the one part of the building he did not design. **[A - crypt burial records; press accounts]**
- At his death the church stands roughly 15-25 percent built. **[A]**

## 8. Afterlife

- **1950**: **Juan Eduardo Cirlot** publishes *El arte de Gaudí* (Ediciones Omega), ahead of the general turn. **[A - juan_eduardo_cirlot.journey.json]**
- **1956-1957**: **Salvador Dalí** leads the reassessment; a retrospective at the Saló del Tinell (1956), then the Museum of Modern Art, New York (1957). **[A - es.wikipedia]**
- **1963**: the Park Güell house opens as the Gaudí House Museum, 28 September. **1969**: seventeen works hold Spanish cultural-monument status. **2007**: the Gaudí Centre opens in Reus, 27 April. **[A all three]**
- **1984-2005**: seven works inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. **[A - en.wikipedia]** *Gap:* the list of seven could not be independently confirmed; whc.unesco.org/en/list/320 returned an access error.
- **Canonization cause. Contradiction, unresolved.** es.wikipedia dates the cause's opening to 1998 under Archbishop Ricard Maria Carles, with a Vatican *nihil obstat* naming him Servant of God in 2000; en.wikipedia dates the formal opening in the Archdiocese of Barcelona to 2003.
- **14 April 2025**: Pope Francis declares him **Venerable**, one of the last acts of his pontificate. **[A - es.wikipedia]**
- **20 February 2026**: the Tower of Jesus Christ reaches structural completion at 172.5 m, the tallest church in the world; interior work projected to 2028; funding still tickets and donations, ~25 million euros a year. **10 June 2026**: on the centenary of his death, Pope Leo XIV blesses the tower. **[A - en.wikipedia]**

## 9. Quotations

Gaudí wrote almost nothing. "Gaudí's thought" is a compilation by disciples who wrote down what he said, which is why attribution here is unusually sharp.

- **"Men may be divided into two types: men of words and men of action..."** Interview in *La Razón*, 1913, cited in Gijs van Hensbergen, *Gaudí* (HarperCollins), intro p. xxxii. **[A]** His own rare recorded self-description.
- **"The straight line belongs to Man. The curved line belongs to God."** *Retagged in verification from [R] to [A].* Catalan Wikiquote carries it in its **main** section, not an attributed/disputed one, as "La línia recta és dels homes; la corba és de Déu," sourced to **Joan Bergós, *Gaudí, l'home i l'obra* (Aymà, 1954), p. 91**, a conversation collected c.1920-1926 by Gaudí's own disciple. The competing claim that the line is Pierre Albert-Birot's rests solely on English Wikiquote's "Attributed" note, whose only evidence is a Google Books link to Debra Kelly, *Pierre Albert-Birot: A Poetics in Movement*, p. 290, which would not load. **Tagged [A], with the dispute carried in `quote_source` rather than resolved.**
- **"Originality is returning to the origin."** *Retagged from [R] to [A].* Catalan Wikiquote, main section, "L'originalitat consisteix a tornar a l'origen," sourced to **Isidre Puig Boada, *El pensament de Gaudí* (COAC, 1981), p. 13**. The earlier pass searched only English and Spanish Wikiquote, where it is absent.
- **"My client is not in a hurry."** **[R]** The source itself hedges: en.wikipedia "Sagrada Família" says "Gaudí is said to have remarked."
- **George Orwell**, *Homage to Catalonia* (1938); **Louis Sullivan**, in Thomas E. Tallmadge, *Western Architect* XXXI (March 1922); **Le Corbusier**, *Gaudí* (Editorial RM), two passages; **Anthony Burgess**, *Homage to QWERT YUIOP* (1986), p. 71; **David Gascoyne**, *Journal 1936-1937* (Enitharmon, 1980), in Cunningham, ed., *The Spanish Front* (1986). **[A all]**

*House-style note:* several originals contain em dashes, rendered as commas in the journey file per the atlas rule, which is a departure from verbatim transcription.

## 10. Relations to other atlas travelers

Two survive the test that a relation must be more than shared geography.

- **Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega**: his cast-iron dragon on Avenida Rivadavia, Buenos Aires, is a direct quotation of Gaudí; sources split between the Park Güell dragon and the Casa Batlló dragon door as the model, and his file leaves the split open. **The Buenos Aires buildings are his stops, not Gaudí's**; they appear here only as a citation on Gaudí's own Casa Batlló stop. **[A - eduardo_rodriguez_ortega.journey.json]**
- **Juan Eduardo Cirlot**: *El arte de Gaudí* (1950), and Gaudí's ceramic serpents and spirals feeding his symbol dictionary. His Park Güell visit is **Cirlot's stop, not Gaudí's**, and is cited rather than staged. **[A - juan_eduardo_cirlot.journey.json]**

Rejected as shared geography only: Querol Subirats, Balenciaga, Eiffel, Claudel, Loïe Fuller, Grasset, Berthon, Colombo. Co-location is already on the map and needs no prose.

**Negative finding, deliberately recorded.** No source places Gaudí outside Catalonia except at Comillas, Astorga, León, Palma, Málaga/Tangier and possibly Paris. The Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262) and Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354) pins used elsewhere in the atlas carry **no** attested Gaudí connection, and no sacred-geography link should be invented for him.

## 11. Honest gaps

1. **The birthplace is not settled** and this report does not settle it.
2. **Paris 1878**: whether he traveled at all is unresolved; Van Hensbergen has the vitrine going without him. His 1910 Grand Palais showing is attested as an exhibition of his work, but **no reached source says he attended**, so it is not staged as a stop.
3. **Torras i Bages dies at Vic in 1916.** The friendship is attested; Gaudí's presence at Vic is not. No Vic stop.
4. **The day of graduation** is unknown; only the year 1878 is supported.
5. **UNESCO's list of seven** unverified: site error.
6. **The canonization chronology** conflicts between the two Wikipedias.
7. **No coordinate** for Sant Just i Sant Pastor was supplied; the 1924 arrest is pinned by approximation.
8. **Casa Calvet's client**: Andreu Calvet against the heirs of Pere Màrtir Calvet, unresolved.
9. **The Mas de la Calderera's position** differs between two lenses, and no reached authority fixes the farmhouse.
