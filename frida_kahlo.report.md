# Frida Kahlo (1907-1954): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-12 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `frida_kahlo`. Register: national mythology, the canon is true.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on a compilation rather than a document. Contradictions are flagged where they arise and **not adjudicated**.

---

## 0. A source-level correction governing the whole file

A large block of the gathered pool was attributed to "Frida Kahlo Museum (fridakahlo.org)". **The attribution is wrong.** `fridakahlo.org` is a commercial art-reproduction site; the museum publishes at `museofridakahlo.org.mx`. Every claim carrying that label has been demoted from institutional source to uncited secondary web text, and where it conflicts with Herrera or Wikipedia the conflict is recorded rather than resolved. This also explains the pool's worst outlier, the 1922 accident date below, which was never a museum position. **[A: page text inspected during verification]**

## 1. Coyoacán and the broken body (1907-1926)

- Born **6 July 1907** at Londres 247, Coyoacán, in the house Guillermo Kahlo built. **[A: English Wikipedia; Hayden Herrera, *Frida: A Biography of Frida Kahlo*, 1983]** She gave her birth year as **1910**, aligning her life with the Revolution; the false year is itself a documented fact and is kept in the journey as such, not silently corrected away. **[A]**
- **Poliomyelitis c. 1913**, about age six: right leg thinner and shorter, permanent limp, swimming and boxing prescribed by her father, the street name *pata de palo* **[A: Herrera, Wikipedia]**. The "nine months bedridden" figure comes from the misattributed site **[R]**.
- **1922**: enters the Escuela Nacional Preparatoria (San Ildefonso), one of some thirty-five girls among two thousand students; joins the *Cachuchas*; first sees Rivera painting *La Creación*. With **Alejandro Gómez Arias** 1922-1925, ending when he leaves for Europe in 1927. **[A: Herrera, Wikipedia]**
- **17 September 1925**: the bus carrying Kahlo and Gómez Arias is struck by a streetcar; a steel handrail passes through her pelvis; spine, pelvis, collarbone, ribs and right leg fractured. **[A: Wikipedia; Herrera, printing Gómez Arias's letters]**
  - **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** The `fridakahlo.org` biography dates the crash to **September 1922** ("On a September afternoon when she traveled with Gomez Arias on a bus the tragic accident happened", filed under 1922). Verified as genuinely on that page, against Wikipedia and Herrera. **[R]** on 1922, **[A]** on 1925.
- Taken to the **Hospital de la Cruz Roja**; the first of roughly thirty operations **[R: Herrera; the 1925 building's location unconfirmed]**. Then roughly **three months in a plaster corset** **[A: English Wikipedia]**; the pool's "full-body plaster cast" overstates that source. She begins painting in bed on an easel built for a recumbent body, with a mirror in the canopy of the four-poster **[A: Herrera, Wikipedia]**.

## 2. The elephant and the dove (1927-1930)

- **1927**: joins the **Partido Comunista Mexicano**. **1928**: introduced to Rivera at a party of the photographer **Tina Modotti** **[A: Wikipedia]**, and shows him her paintings while he works on the **Secretaría de Educación Pública** murals; he later records an "extraordinary force of expression" **[A: Diego Rivera, *My Art, My Life*, 1960, cited in Herrera]**.
- **21 August 1929**: civil marriage at the Coyoacán town hall. **[A: Wikipedia, Herrera]**
  - **Attribution correction.** The "elephant and a dove" remark is standardly given to her **mother**, or to her parents jointly, not to Guillermo Kahlo as the pool had it. **[R: contested]** The journey carries both readings and withdraws neither.
- **1929**: resigns from the PCM; the couple moves to **Cuernavaca** for Rivera's Palacio de Cortés commission, funded by ambassador Dwight Morrow; Kahlo begins to dress **Tehuana** **[A: Wikipedia, Herrera]**. The dress is traditionally linked to the matriarchal Zapotec culture of the **Isthmus of Tehuantepec**. **[R: Herrera notes the attribution is contested; no primary document places her on the isthmus before the adoption. Retained as an explicitly reconstructed stop, the tradition being load-bearing for the iconography.]**

## 3. Gringolandia (1930-1934)

- **November 1930**: San Francisco, for Rivera's Stock Exchange mural; meets the surgeon **Leo Eloesser**, who in 1930-31 advises against spinal surgery, prescribing rest, a proper corset and posture. The correspondence runs to her death. **[A: Herrera; the Eloesser-Kahlo letters]** **1931**: paints *Frieda and Diego Rivera*, exhibited at the **Palace of the Legion of Honor** **[A: Wikipedia]**; no coordinate for it in the pool, so it is folded into the San Francisco stop.
- **April 1932**: Detroit, for the Detroit Industry murals **[A: Herrera]**. **4 July 1932**: miscarriage at thirteen weeks, hospitalized until 17 July at **Henry Ford Hospital**; produces *Henry Ford Hospital* and *Self-Portrait on the Border Line* **[A: Herrera, Wikipedia]**. **September 1932**: returns briefly for the death of her mother, **Matilde Calderón y González**; *My Birth* dates from around then **[A: Herrera]**. **1933**: in New York while Rivera paints *Man at the Crossroads*, destroyed after he refuses to remove Lenin **[A: Herrera]**.
- **CHRONOLOGY CORRECTION.** The pool conflated two things under "1934". The return from the United States was **December 1933**; **1934** is the year she painted nothing, owing to health and to Rivera's affair with her sister **Cristina**. The journey splits them. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- The couple moves into the twin O'Gorman houses at **San Ángel**, joined by a rooftop bridge **[R: Herrera; tagged R in the pool and left there]**. The cutting-off of her hair after the Cristina affair rests only on the misattributed site **[R]**. *(The canvas `Self-Portrait with Cropped Hair` is a 1940 painting; the pool does not establish that the 1934 act and it are one episode, and this report does not assert it.)*

## 4. Trotsky and Breton (1937-1938)

- **1937-1939**: **Leon Trotsky** and **Natalia Sedova** live at the Casa Azul as guests of Rivera and Kahlo; a brief affair with Trotsky is recorded. **[A: Herrera; Wolfe, *The Fabulous Life of Diego Rivera*]**
- **April 1938**: **André Breton** visits Mexico **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Summer 1938**: first significant sale, four paintings at $200 each, to the actor **Edward G. Robinson** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
  - **ATTRIBUTION CORRECTION.** "A ribbon around a bomb" does **not** come from the Mexico visit. It is from Breton's **catalogue essay for the November 1938 Julien Levy exhibition in New York**, and the journey re-anchors it there. **[A: Wikipedia, Herrera]**

## 5. New York and Paris (1938-1939)

- **November 1938**: first solo exhibition, **Julien Levy Gallery**, East 57th Street; roughly half of twenty-five paintings sell **[A: Herrera, Wikipedia]**. **January 1939**: sails for Paris **[A: Wikipedia]**; the journey pins the arrival on the canonical Paris pin, **48.8566, 2.3522**, inherited byte-identical.
- **10 March 1939**: the exhibition **"Mexique"** opens at **Galerie Renou et Colle**; she meets Duchamp, Picasso and Kandinsky **[A: Wikipedia, Herrera]**. Four corrections: the Second World War had **not begun** (September 1939), and Wikipedia attributes the muted reception to the **approaching** war; **Marcel Duchamp**, not Breton, mounted the show after Breton failed to arrange it; it was a mixed Mexican display (pre-Columbian objects, folk art, Álvarez Bravo photographs) with roughly **seventeen** Kahlo paintings, **not a solo show**; and **[R]** on the coordinate, the Faubourg Saint-Honoré area right but the building unconfirmed.
- **1939**: the **Louvre** buys *The Frame*, reportedly making her the first twentieth-century Mexican artist in the collection **[A: Wikipedia, Herrera]**. Month not established; the hedge sits in `date_confidence`.

## 6. Divorce, remarriage, the father's death (1939-1942)

- **CHRONOLOGY CORRECTION.** The pool gave the divorce flatly as 1939. The **petition was filed 6 November 1939**, the **decree finalized in January 1940** (Spanish Wikipedia). "1939" is defensible for the separation; the legal divorce runs into 1940. Both kept.
- *The Two Fridas* is painted around the divorce **[A: Herrera]**; *The Dream (The Bed)* and *Self-Portrait with Thorn Necklace and Hummingbird* in 1940 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **8 December 1940**: remarriage in San Francisco, on terms she later described as financial independence and no sexual relations **[A: Herrera]**. **1941**: the ICA Boston shows her work **[A: Wikipedia]**, with no coordinate in the pool, so Boston is not a stop. **1942**: founding member of the **Seminario de Cultura Mexicana** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **April 1941**: death of **Guillermo Kahlo** at the Casa Azul; the couple settles permanently into the house **[A: Herrera]**. The same year Rivera supervises the **stepped pyramid** in the garden for their pre-Hispanic collection **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, *Museo Frida Kahlo*: "En 1941 Rivera supervisó la construcción de una pirámide escalonada"]**; the pool's 1946 is wrong by five years.

## 7. La Esmeralda, the corsets, the prizes (1943-1951)

- **1943**: teaching post at **"La Esmeralda"**; when her health stops the journey, the students later called **Los Fridos** come to the Casa Azul instead **[A: Wikipedia, Herrera]**. **1944**: spinal surgery in Mexico City and a steel corset; ***The Broken Column*** is painted this year **[A: Wikipedia; the pool's own chronology]**, the hospital's identity **[R]**.
  - **CORRECTION.** The pool's geography lens attached *The Broken Column* to the **June 1946 New York** surgery, against its own chronology: the painting is 1944 and follows a **Mexico City** operation.
- **17 March 1945**: the *Así* interview with **Antonio Rodríguez** **[A]**. **1946**: the 5,000-peso prize for ***Moses*** **[A: English Wikipedia]**, dated 1945 in the pool and wrong by a year; and in **June 1946** the New York spinal fusion by **Philip Wilson**, bone graft and metal rod, a failure **[A: Herrera, Wikipedia]**.
- **1947**: ***The Two Fridas*** acquired for the nation by **INBA** **[A]**.
  - **ANACHRONISM CORRECTED.** The pool, following English Wikipedia's Frida Kahlo article, said the **Museo de Arte Moderno** bought it in 1947. The MAM did not exist until 1964; INBA transferred it there on **28 December 1966**.
- **1950-51**: about a year in Mexico City hospitals after repeated operations and a bone-graft infection, then largely bedridden at the Casa Azul **[A: Herrera]**. The pool's "gangrene 1950, nine months bedridden" entry rests on the misattributed site **[R]**. **10 June 1951**: **Gisèle Freund** in *Novedades* **[A]**.

## 8. The bed carried into the gallery (1953-1954)

- **April 1953**: her only solo exhibition in Mexico in her lifetime, at **Lola Álvarez Bravo's Galería de Arte Contemporáneo**; too ill to stand, she arrives by ambulance and receives guests from her four-poster bed installed in the gallery **[A: Wikipedia, Herrera]**. **[R]** on the address: Amberes street, Zona Rosa, number unverified. The same year five paintings are shown at the **Tate Gallery**, London **[A: Wikipedia]**, with no coordinate in the pool, so not a stop.
- **August 1953**: the right leg amputated below the knee for gangrene; a prosthesis fitted **[A: Herrera]**. Deepening depression and increased drug and alcohol use are reported by "some accounts" **[R]**, as is the hospital **[R]**. **2 July 1954**: carried to a demonstration against the CIA-backed coup against **Jacobo Árbenz**, her last public appearance **[R: Herrera; the pool tags the Zócalo location R]**.
- **13 July 1954**: dies at the Casa Azul, a week after her forty-seventh birthday. Official cause **pulmonary embolism**; suspicion of overdose, recorded by her nurse and by later biographers, was never confirmed and no autopsy was performed. **[A on the official cause; R on the suicide]**

## 9. Afterlife (1954-2004)

- **14 July 1954**: the body lies in state at the **Palacio de Bellas Artes** under a Communist flag draped on the coffin, a scandal in a state institution. **[A: Herrera]**
  - **CORRECTION.** The pool's clause that Rivera was expelled from the Communist Party over this is **wrong**: he had been **readmitted** in 1954. The man punished was **Andrés Iduarte**, director of INBA, dismissed in 1955 "por haber permitido que la bandera soviética fuera puesta sobre el féretro de Frida Kahlo" (Spanish Wikipedia, *Andrés Iduarte*).
  - **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** One source calls the flag the **Mexican Communist Party** flag, another the **Soviet** flag.
- Cremation at the **Panteón Civil de Dolores**; witnesses including the poet **Andrés Henestrosa** describe the body sitting upright in the furnace heat **[R: transmitted through Herrera and later press retellings]**. The ashes are kept at the Casa Azul in a pre-Columbian urn, in the bedroom. There is no grave. **[R: museum description]**
- **July 1958**: the Casa Azul opens as the **Museo Frida Kahlo** **[A]**.
  - **CORRECTIONS.** Rivera died **24 November 1957**, so the museum opened about **eight months** later, not "one year after". And the house was not conveyed by will: Rivera created the **Fideicomiso de los Museos Diego Rivera y Frida Kahlo** in 1957, administered by the **Banco de México**, and that trust is the instrument.
- Reception: **1983** Herrera's biography **[A]**; **1984** national patrimony status **[R: primary text not reached]**; **1995** the Abrams diary facsimile **[A]**; **2002** Taymor's *Frida* **[A]**; **2010** the 500-peso note **[A]**; **2004** the opening of the cache sealed in Casa Azul bathrooms at Rivera's instruction **[A]**; **2018**, not 2016, Mattel's doll, criticized by grand-niece **Mara de Anda Romeo** for thinned eyebrows and lightened skin **[A]**, the pool wrong by two years on both.
- The **Frida Kahlo Corporation** licenses her name and is in litigation with other family members **[A]**. **CORRECTION:** the pool tied Banco de México to the 500-peso note. Its standing is as **trustee of the 1957 fideicomiso**.

## Coordinate corrections applied in the journey file

Seven pins in the pool were wrong. **San Ildefonso 1922**, pool point ~330 m west of the building, replaced by the canonical `artaud` pin **19.4361/-99.1305** rather than a third value. **Crash site 1925**, pool point ~2.5 km southwest in Narvarte, not on the road: **19.4222/-99.1345** (Herrera: Tlalpan and Cuauhtemotzín). **SEP 1928**, pool point is the Zócalo: **19.4372/-99.1315**. **Coyoacán town hall 1929**, pool point is essentially the Casa Azul: **19.3499/-99.1626**. **Henry Ford Hospital 1932**, pool point near the DIA: **42.3691/-83.0854**. **Spinal fusion 1946**, pool point ~800 m north of even the modern HSS campus: the 1946 address at 321 East 42nd Street, **40.7495/-73.9707** **[R]**. **Cremation 1954**, three coordinates for one event across two lenses: **19.4084/-99.2066**.

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Date of the bus accident.** 17 September 1925 (Wikipedia, Herrera, Gómez Arias's letters) against September 1922 (`fridakahlo.org`).
2. **The flag on the coffin.** Mexican Communist Party flag or Soviet flag.
3. **Date of the divorce.** Petition November 1939, decree January 1940; sources give a bare "1939".
4. **Who called it a marriage of an elephant and a dove.** Mother, parents jointly, or father.
5. **English Wikipedia against itself** on *The Two Fridas*: MAM 1947 in one article, INBA 1947 with a 1966 transfer in the other.

## Honest gaps

- **No coordinates** in the pool for the Legion of Honor (1931), the ICA Boston (1941), or the Tate (1953). None is a stop.
- **The 1925 Red Cross Hospital**, **the 1944 and 1953 hospitals**, **the 1940s La Esmeralda campus**, and **Galerie Renou et Colle**: locations approximate or identities unconfirmed, each flagged in its own stop.
- **Tehuantepec**: no primary travel document places Kahlo there before she adopted Tehuana dress.
- **The diary** (Abrams, 1995) could not be fetched in full text; the final entry is quoted through Wikiquote's citation of the last page.
- **Archives**: no Mexican holdings (INBA, the fideicomiso records) consulted directly. Everything institutional here arrives through Herrera, Wikipedia, or museum web text.

## Quotes: what survived verification

**Kept, verbatim and cited** (full text in the journey's `quote` fields): the self-portraits line, *Así*, 17 March 1945; the drowned-sorrows line, a letter to Ella Wolfe; two lines from "Mexican Autobiography", *Time*, 27 April 1953; the two-accidents line, Gisèle Freund, *Novedades*, 10 June 1951; the diary's final entry, July 1954.

One citation is hedged. The Wolfe letter is **dated only "Wednesday 13," 1938** in Herrera (1983), p. 197. **The pool's "13 July 1938" is an inference**: that date was a Wednesday, so the guess is plausible but undocumented, and the journey carries the hedged citation instead.

**Dropped as aggregator drift:** "I never paint dreams or nightmares..." (a corrupted variant of the *Time* line, tense shifted, no primary citation); "...**Diego was by far the worst.**" (that clause is in no primary text, and Goodreads swaps "trolley" for "train"); "Pies, para qué los quiero si tengo alas para volar." (carved on her monument, but traceable only to the secondary site *Cultura Genial*, so **[R]**, not used as a quote field, though the sense appears unquoted in the amputation stop's campa).

## Atlas interlocks used

- **`cortes`**: Cortés kept his headquarters in Coyoacán in 1521 (canonical pin 19.3467, -99.1618), the barrio where Kahlo was born, lived and died. Named at the birth stop and at the 1945 interview stop, which sits on that pin.
- **`artaud`**: Artaud lectured at San Ildefonso on 26 February 1936 "under the murals of Orozco" (canonical pin **19.4361, -99.1305**, inherited byte-identical), the building where Kahlo watched Rivera paint *La Creación* in 1922. Named again at the Breton stop: his search for indigenous ritual runs parallel to her indigenismo, no meeting claimed.
- **`che_guevara`**: Guevara reaches Mexico City in September 1954, ten weeks after Kahlo's last appearance against the same Guatemalan coup that drove him there. Named at the Zócalo stop, pin **19.4326, -99.1332**.
- **`mussolini`**: Mussolini and Trotsky crossed in Swiss exile cafés before the First World War; Trotsky then lived at the Casa Azul, 1937-1939. **That encounter is [A] in `mussolini.journey.json`; the link forward to Coyoacán is [R], made here.**

**Rejected:** Fructuoso Rivera, Juan de Dios Rivera de Potosí, and the Vijande Rivera of Maradona's 1994 shooting (surname collisions); Cecilia Vicuña (shared lineage, no contact); Julio Cortázar (unconfirmed); the Kaaba and Buenos Aires pins, canonical but with **no attested Kahlo connection**, so not inherited, since a pin without an event would be an invented stop.
