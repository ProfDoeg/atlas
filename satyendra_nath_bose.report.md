# Satyendra Nath Bose (1894-1974): research report
*2026-08-02. Companion file: `satyendra_nath_bose.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or inference. Contradictions are flagged in place and **not adjudicated**, except where one source demonstrably corrects another, in which case correction and warrant are both shown.

**Method.** Five lenses (chronology, geography, quotations, corpus interlock, afterlife) were verified against the **Biographical Memoir of the Indian National Science Academy (INSA)**, which proved decisive: it corrected about a dozen circulating claims, among them two dates, one chair, one de Broglie brother and the framing of the Curie episode. Five coordinates were off by 0.4 to 12.5 km and were regeocoded.

---

## 1. Calcutta childhood (1894-1913)

- Born **1 January 1894 in Calcutta**, eldest of seven and only son of **Surendranath Bose**, an East India Railways accountant later in business, and **Amodini Devi**. **[A, Wikipedia; MacTutor; INSA]** The **house of birth is undocumented**; only the city is fixed. **[gap]**
- Ancestral home **Bara Jagulia**, Haringhata block, Nadia district, ~45 km north. **[A, Wikipedia]** No adult visit is recorded. **[gap]** *Coordinate corrected:* the lens gave 23.05, 88.5, some 12.5 km north of the village; OSM gives **22.9456, 88.5459**.
- **Schooling begins at five near the house.** Only *after* the family moves to **Goabagan** is he admitted to the **New Indian School** **[A, Wikipedia: "His schooling began at the age of five, near his home. When his family moved to Goabagan, he was admitted into the New Indian School"]**. Two lens errors corrected: Goabagan was treated as the birth locality, and the two schools were collapsed into one. Goabagan Street geocodes to **22.5890, 88.3711**, 1.1 km off the lens pin.
- **Hindu School from 1907**, where a teacher turns him toward mathematics **[A, MacTutor]**. Sits the entrance examination in **1908**, is felled by **chicken pox**, and "had to remain in Hindu School for one more year" **[A, INSA]**; matriculates **1909, fifth in merit** **[A, Wikipedia]**.
- **Presidency College**: taught by **Jagadish Chandra Bose, Prafulla Chandra Ray, Sarada Prasanna Das**; first in the intermediate science examination (**1911**), first in the **BSc in mixed mathematics (1913)**. **[A, Wikipedia]**

## 2. Marriage, MSc, the relativity translation (1914-1920)

- **Married 1914, aged twenty, to Ushabati Ghosh**, aged eleven, only daughter of the physician **Jogindranath Ghosh** **[A, INSA, which names the year, the bride, her father and her age]**.
  - *Contradiction resolved, not left open.* MacTutor gives **1915** and the name **Ushabala**; the lens kept this as an open conflict. It is not one: INSA is the closer witness. The hedge is carried in `date_confidence`.
- **MSc in mixed mathematics, 1915**, a record result in the university's annals; **research scholar from 1916, lecturer at Rajabazar Science College 1916-1921**. **[A, Wikipedia]**
  - *Duplicate removed:* MacTutor's "appointed to University College of Science in 1917" is the same post Wikipedia dates 1916. One appointment, not two; 1916 preferred.
- **The relativity translation**, with **Meghnad Saha**, from the German and French originals, published by Calcutta University as *The Principle of Relativity*, introduced by **P. C. Mahalanobis**. Bose did the generalised relativity paper himself, as he told Einstein in 1924. **[A, INSA bibliography; Bose's letter]**
  - *Flagged, unresolved:* **INSA dates the volume 1920, Wikipedia 1919.** The INSA bibliography is followed, but the conflict stands.

## 3. Dacca and the four pages (1921-1924)

- **1921: Reader in physics, University of Dhaka (Dacca)**, then two years old; he builds the laboratories and writes the courses. **[A, Wikipedia; Banglapedia]**
- **c. March 1924: Saha visits Dacca** and puts the current literature in his hands (Einstein and Ehrenfest 1923, Pauli 1923, Compton, Debye). Bose then "set about thinking deeply and spending sleepless nights about the radiation problem" before identifying blackbody radiation as a photon gas. **[A, INSA]**
- **1924: "Planck's Law and the Hypothesis of Light Quanta"**, four pages, on two assumptions: the elementary phase-space cell has content h³, and light quanta are indistinguishable. **[A]**
  - *The "happy mistake" is a tradition.* **[R]** Wikipedia's *Bose-Einstein statistics* article carries the story of an accidental miscount in a lecture that came out right. INSA describes nothing of the kind, only months of deliberate work. A. D. Stone (*Einstein and the Quantum*, ch. 24) treats the anecdote cautiously. The journey states the paper as fact and the miscount as tradition.
- **Submitted to the Philosophical Magazine [A]**, after which the record forks three ways:
  - Wikipedia's two articles disagree with each other: the *Bose* article says it was rejected before he wrote to Einstein and "**It is not known why it was rejected**"; the *Bose-Einstein statistics* article says it was rejected **on a referee's negative report**.
  - INSA: "no acceptance letter was received by him for a long time. And so, he sent a duplicate copy of the manuscript... to Einstein."
  - *Handling:* the rejection is attested; **the referee's report is not**. The journey says he submitted, heard nothing, and posted a duplicate. **[flagged, not resolved]**
- **4 June 1924: the letter to Einstein**, in English, from Dacca. **[A]**

## 4. Berlin (1924-1925)

- **Einstein translates the paper into German himself** and submits it with an endorsing note. **[A, Wikipedia; de.wikipedia carries the German text]**
- **Received by *Zeitschrift für Physik* 2 July 1924**; published as *Plancks Gesetz und Lichtquantenhypothese*, vol. 26, pp. 178-181. **[A, INSA for the receipt date]** The translation therefore falls inside the four weeks after 4 June.
- **Einstein extends the method to a gas of massive particles**, asks the journal to print both papers together, and so predicts the **Bose-Einstein condensate**, realised only in **1995**. **[A]**
- *Venue:* the translation is pinned at **Haberlandstrasse 5, Schöneberg** (**52.4907, 13.3381**, OSM; the lens pin was 0.45 km off). **Home versus office is inferred.** **[R]**

## 5. The European leave (1924-1926)

The section INSA rewrites most heavily. Four corrections:

1. **The wrong de Broglie.** Sylvain Levi gave Bose the introduction to **Langevin**, who sent him to **Maurice** de Broglie, in whose private laboratory Bose worked under the chief assistant **Alexandre Dauvillier**. **[A, INSA]** The lens said **Louis**; no source puts Bose in working contact with Louis. **[corrected]**
2. **Langevin was not at the Sorbonne**, but "Professor titulaire at the **Collège de France**", his chair since 1909 **[A, INSA]**. The Sorbonne pin is dropped for **48.8489, 2.3459**. **[corrected]**
3. **Curie: one interview, no laboratory.** Langevin gave a note of introduction and "Bose accordingly sought an interview"; Curie told him to spend longer on his French, and "Thus Bose's desire for learning experimental techniques in Radioactivity under Mme Curie's guidance **remained unfulfilled**." **[A, INSA]** Every account of Bose "working in Curie's laboratory" is wrong. **[corrected]**
4. **The Einstein meeting is not October 1925.** After about a year in Paris he reached Berlin and **wrote to Einstein on 8 October 1925**; Einstein was at Leyden, and "the long-cherished meeting with him materialised **several weeks later**, when Einstein returned to Berlin." **[A, INSA]** The journey dates the letter exactly and the meeting to late 1925, hedged in `date_confidence`.
   - Of the meeting INSA records only that Einstein asked **how Bose had hit on the derivation**, then gave him **a letter of introduction to the Berlin physics colloquium**. **[A]** The lens placed the meeting at the **Prussian Academy**; no source supports that venue **[R, dropped]**, and the journey uses the canonical Berlin pin without asserting a room.

- **Arrival:** sails early September 1924, reaches Paris **mid-October**, lodging at **17 Rue du Sommerard, Paris 5e**. **[A, INSA]**
- **The return voyage is undocumented**: no ship, port or date. Marseille-Bombay is the standard interwar route and the journey says so as reconstruction. **[R, a stated gap]**

## 6. Dacca again, Calcutta, the honours (1926-1974)

- **Returns 1926.** Einstein's letter of recommendation is confirmed; he "was surprised at the request because he thought that Bose's scientific work should entitle him to the position". The chair was then **offered to D. M. Bose, who declined**, and **S. N. Bose was appointed Professor and Head of Department in 1927**. **[A, INSA]** *Corrected:* Wikipedia compresses return and appointment into 1926 and implies Einstein's word carried it. Two stages, and a second choice; MacTutor's 1927 preferred.
- **1929: Sectional President, Physics and Mathematics, Indian Science Congress, Madras.** **[A, INSA; Banglapedia independently]** *Corrected:* MacTutor's **1939** is wrong by a decade, apparently a typographical slip.
- **1937: Tagore dedicates *Visva-Parichay*, his only science book, to Bose** **[A, Wikipedia]**. **1944: General President, Indian Science Congress, Delhi** **[A, MacTutor]**.
- **Dean of the Faculty of Science at Dacca until 1945** **[A, Wikipedia]**. The **start of the deanship is nowhere dated**; the lens's "1926-1945" span is unsupported and has been reduced to an undated tenure. **[gap]**
- **1945: leaves Dacca for the Khaira Professorship at Calcutta**, vacant on the sudden death of **Professor B. B. Roy**. INSA heads the period "Back to Calcutta (1945-56)"; Banglapedia agrees ("Khoyra Professor", 1945); MacTutor also says 1945. **[A]**
  - *Corrected:* Wikipedia's **1947** and its "**Ghosh Professorship**" are both unsupported; three sources give 1945 and none names a Ghosh chair. Per INSA the trigger was **communal tension in Dacca plus a vacancy**, not Partition, still two years away, so the Dacca career ran about **twenty-four years (1921-1945)**, not twenty-six.
  - *Residual conflict, flagged:* MacTutor calls the Calcutta chair "Guprasad Sing", against Khaira/Khoyra in INSA and Banglapedia. Not resolved.
- **1949: President, National Institute of Sciences of India** **[A, MacTutor]**. **1952: nominated to the Rajya Sabha** **[A]**; *end of term disputed*, Wikipedia's infobox giving 3 April 1952 to 2 April 1960 and INSA **1952-58**, the standard six-year term **[flagged; the journey asserts only the start]**. **1954: Padma Vibhushan** **[A, official Padma list]**, though attendance at a Delhi ceremony is **undocumented** and the Delhi pin is marked inferred **[R]**.
- **1953-1955: the unified field exchange and the meeting that never happened.** Einstein replied to Bose's papers that he "did not quite follow how precisely Bose's solution was to be used in physics". Bose wrote a long answer and meant to hand it over at **Bern**, at the 50th anniversary of relativity. Einstein died first, and "Bose was so overwhelmed with grief that **he tore and threw away the only copy of his important paper**." **[A, INSA]** *Corrected:* the lens had built a three-decade Calcutta-to-Princeton correspondence at 112 Mercer Street, which nothing attests; the Princeton stop was dropped and a Bern stop built.
- **1956: Professor Emeritus at Calcutta; Vice-Chancellor of Visva-Bharati** to 1958 **[A]**. **1958: elected FRS** **[A, INSA honours list; Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- **1958: National Professor**, "three five-year terms... extending from 1958 till his death", INSA and MacTutor agreeing. **[A]** *Corrected:* Wikipedia's **1959** loses. Three five-year terms is where the repeated "fifteen years" comes from, while 1958-1974 is sixteen.
- **Late work:** nuclear physics, unfinished organic chemistry, **helium from the Bakreshwar hot springs** **[A, Wikipedia]**; the years are **undated** **[gap]**.
- **Nobel: four nominations (1956, 1959, twice 1962), never awarded**; evaluator **Oskar Klein** judged the work insufficient on its own. **[A, Wikipedia, citing the nomination archive]**
- **Dies 4 February 1974 in Calcutta at 80**, survived by Ushabati, two sons, five daughters **[A]**. The **house or hospital is unnamed** **[gap]**, and the **cremation ground is nowhere located**: the journey pins Nimtala as representative and says so in the campa. **[R, gap stated, not filled]**

## 7. Quotations

Eight verbatim passages verified, five carried in the journey.

- **Four passages from the letter of 4 June 1924** (Bose's own English), as reproduced in Wikipedia's *Bose* article: the opening ("Respected Sir, I have ventured to send you the accompanying article..."); the technical statement about the coefficient; the appeal ("Though a complete stranger to you... we are all your pupils"); the reminder about the relativity translation. **[A]** *Chain of custody flagged:* seen only via Wikipedia, not against a facsimile.
- **Einstein's translator's note**: "Boses Ableitung der Planckschen Formel bedeutet meiner Meinung nach einen wichtigen Fortschritt." **[A, *Zeitschrift für Physik* 26 (1924), German text via de.wikipedia]**
- **Banerjee's Nobel nomination.** *Attribution corrected:* not "K. Banerjee, 1956 (via Wikipedia)" but **Kedareswar Banerjee, head of physics, Allahabad, letter to the Nobel Committee of 12 January 1956**, the scholarly source being **Rajinder Singh, *India's Nobel Prize Nominators and Nominees* (Shaker, 2016), pp. 26-27**. Text matches exactly. **[A]**
- **"I have got all the recognition I deserve."** **[A as text, broken chain of custody]** Wikipedia cites Outlook India; **the Outlook page is unreachable**, so the remark is secondhand and the journey's `quote_source` says so. It is also **undated**, and is placed in the late Calcutta years by inference.
- **"You don't know who he was? Half the particles in the universe obey him!"** An unnamed professor to a student, The Telegraph (Calcutta), 3 January 2012. **[A as a 2012 anecdote; not Bose's words]**

## 8. Afterlife

- **6 December 1945: Dirac coins "boson"** in the lecture *Developments in Atomic Theory*, **Palais de la Découverte, Paris**. **[A, Wikipedia "Boson", citing lecture notes, Dirac Papers BW83/2/257889, via Farmelo, *The Strangest Man*, p. 331 n. 64]** *Tag corrected:* the chronology lens tagged this [R], date and venue unconfirmed, while the afterlife lens carried the citation above. The lenses contradicted each other; the afterlife lens is right. **Retagged [A].**
- **1986: S. N. Bose National Centre for Basic Sciences**, Salt Lake, by act of Parliament **[A]**. **1995: the condensate realised**, the 2001 physics Nobel following **[A]**. **4 June 2022: Google Doodle** for the 98th anniversary of the letter **[A]**.
- **Bust in the garden of the Birla Industrial and Technological Museum; 1994 stamp.** **[A]** *Coordinate corrected:* BITM (19A Gurusaday Road) is **22.5347, 88.3638**, over a kilometre east of the lens longitude.

## 9. Corpus interlock

- **Einstein.** `einstein.journey.json` has a five-year Berlin hole: last dated stop Madrid, 24 February 1923; next Brussels, 24 October 1927. **June 1924 sits inside it.** **[A as to the file; R as to the inference]** This journey fills the window from the other side, inheriting the canonical Berlin pin **52.5169, 13.3903** byte-identical at four stops. The canonical Paris pin **48.8566, 2.3522** (Blavatsky and the Paris cluster) is likewise inherited at two stops.
- **Heisenberg.** His file puts him at the **Berlin physics colloquium, 28 April 1926**, meeting Einstein and being walked home. Bose entered that same colloquium on Einstein's introduction a few months earlier. **[A as to both files; no meeting documented]** Named in the Berlin campa as a milieu crossing.
- **Keyserling and Annie Besant.** Keyserling in Calcutta, 10 March 1912, "the two Indias", during Bose's Presidency College years; Besant in Calcutta, 26 December 1917, presiding over the Congress while Bose lectured in the same city. **[A, overlap only, no documented contact]** Both named in campa.
- **Savitri Devi: a disambiguation trap.** Her file names "Bose" twice (1937, 1940), but that is **Subhas Chandra Bose**. **[A]** The census count of three corpus mentions is probably inflated by the confusion. Deliberately not used.
- **Rejected leads.** **Jung** in Calcutta, January 1938: the lens's milieu overlap rests on a false premise, since **in 1938 Bose was at Dacca** and had been for seventeen years **[R, rejected]**. **Niels Bohr**: the lens proposed a visit to his Institute, tagged [R] and sourced to "external biography"; nothing in the verified pool attests it, so no Copenhagen stop was built **[R, not carried]**. **Max Müller**: checked and negative, no Calcutta, Dhaka or Bengal stop in his file **[A, negative finding]**.
- **Dhaka is new ground.** No journey file contained "Dhaka" or "Dacca" before this one **[A, confirmed by grep]**; six stops here are the atlas's first pins in Bangladesh.

## 10. Standing gaps and unresolved contradictions

Unresolved: (1) **Rajya Sabha term end**, 1958 (INSA) vs 2 April 1960 (Wikipedia); (2) **the relativity volume**, 1920 (INSA) vs 1919 (Wikipedia); (3) **the Calcutta chair's name**, Khaira/Khoyra vs "Guprasad Sing" vs "Ghosh"; (4) **why the Philosophical Magazine rejected the paper**, and whether the rejection preceded the letter to Einstein; (5) **the "happy mistake"**, a tradition with no support in the memoir.

Gaps stated as gaps: birth house; wedding venue; residence or hospital of death; **cremation ground**; the 1926 return voyage; the start year of the Dacca deanship; the years of the Bakreshwar work; attendance at any Delhi honours ceremony; the date of the "recognition" remark.
