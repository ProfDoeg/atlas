# Gabriel García Márquez (1927-2014): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-29 from the verified research pool for slug `gabriel_garcia_marquez`.
Companion file: `gabriel_garcia_marquez.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim
the pool could not run to ground. Disagreements are printed, not harmonised. Short forms:

## 1. Aracataca and the grandparents, 1927-1937

- Born at Aracataca, Magdalena, **6 March 1927**, in the house of his maternal grandparents
  Colonel Nicolás Márquez and Tranquilina Iguarán. Parents Gabriel Eligio García, telegraph
  operator, and Luisa Santiaga Márquez Iguarán, married against her family's wishes.
  **[A: Nobel Foundation biographical note; Cervantes; enWP]**
- A **1928** birth year circulates in family testimony and in Gerald Martin's biography.
  **[R]** Not checked against Martin directly; the file uses 1927.
- **Masacre de las bananeras**, Ciénaga railway station, night of **5-6 December 1928**,
  toll disputed between roughly 47 and 2,000. **[A: esWP; Cervantes]** He was twenty-one
  months old and in the region, not a witness; staged as family memory from the colonel.
- **Colegio Montessori**, Aracataca, 1933. **[A: Cervantes]** Building coordinates
  undocumented, reconstructed to the historic core. **[R on the coordinate]**
- Move to **Sincé** (Sucre), 1936; the colonel dies at Aracataca, aged 73, **March 1937**.
  **[A: Cervantes]** Banrep adds a later move to the **river port of Sucre**. **[A]** The
  file stages Sincé at 1936, Sucre at 1943.

## 2. The schools of the interior, 1940-1946

- Boarder at the Jesuit **Colegio San José**, Barranquilla, from **January 1940**; then
  scholarship boarder at the **Liceo Nacional de Varones, Zipaquirá**, 1943-1946.
  **[A: Cervantes]**
- **Bachillerato in 1946**, not 1947. **[A: Banrep, "Obtuvo el grado de bachiller en
  1946"]** The February 1947 matriculation requires it.
- "Excelled in sports and served as team captain" is **unsupported by any source reached**
  and is dropped; the documented Zipaquirá record is poems, reading, the school paper.
  **[gap]**
- Meets **Mercedes Raquel Barcha Pardo** at Sucre on a school holiday, 1943.
  **[A: Cervantes]** Her age and the pharmacist father are commonplaces of the literature.
  **[R on the detail]**
- The **1951 honour killing at Sucre**, reconstructed thirty years later as *Crónica de una
  muerte anunciada*, is attested through the family's account. **[A: enWP]** Mentioned in
  the 1943 campa, not staged as its own scene.

## 3. Bogotá, the Bogotazo, and the coastal newsrooms, 1947-1955

- Law at the **Universidad Nacional de Colombia**, February 1947, under family pressure;
  **13 September 1947**, *La tercera resignación* in *El Espectador*, his first published
  story. **[A: Cervantes; enWP]**
- **9 April 1948**: Gaitán assassinated at Carrera 7 and Avenida Jiménez; the Bogotazo
  closes the university and burns out his boarding house. **[A: Cervantes; enWP]** The
  chronology puts him nearby, **not** at the corner the coordinate marks.
- **Universidad de Cartagena** and **El Universal** under Clemente Manuel Zabala,
  1948-1949. **[A: CVC Instituto Cervantes, Cartagena personalidades page]** The newsroom's
  street address is **not confirmed**; the coordinate marks the walled town. **[R]**
- **El Heraldo**, Barranquilla, 1950-1952: *La Jirafa*, at times signed Septimus; the Grupo
  de Barranquilla around Ramón Vinyes, Álvaro Cepeda Samudio, Germán Vargas, Alfonso
  Fuenmayor. Abandons law the same year. **[A: Cervantes; enWP]**
- **Anachronism caught.** *La Cueva* **opened in 1954**, after he left; the 1950-1952 circle
  met at the Café Colombia, the Café Roma and Vinyes's bookshop. Struck from the stop name.
- **1952**: back to Aracataca with his mother to sell the house. **[A: Cervantes]** Month
  not fixed.
- **February 1954**: staff reporter and film critic, *El Espectador* **[A: Banrep]**;
  **1955**, *La hojarasca*, first novel and first Macondo **[A: enWP]**; **April 1955**, the
  fourteen-part Luis Alejandro Velasco series, the ARC Caldas having lost eight men that
  28 February **[A: enWP, 'The Story of a Shipwrecked Sailor']**.

## 4. Europe and the closed newspaper, 1955-1958

- **Geneva**, Four Power summit at the Palais des Nations, 18-23 July 1955.
  **[A: Cervantes]**
- **Rome**, Centro Sperimentale di Cinematografia, 1955, no qualification taken.
  **[A: HRC exhibition pages]** One interlock note calls this dossier-only; the HRC page
  carries it.
- **Paris**, rue Cujas, 1956-1957, unpaid rent after the Rojas Pinilla government shut
  *El Espectador*; writes *El coronel no tiene quien le escriba* **[A: Cervantes]**, which
  appears as a fragment in *Revista Mito* in 1958 and as a book in **1961**, Aguirre Editor,
  Medellín **[A]**.
- **Caracas**, magazine *Momento*, from late 1957; present for the fall of Marcos Pérez
  Jiménez, 23 January 1958. **[A: Cervantes]**
- **21 March 1958**: marries Mercedes Barcha at Barranquilla. **[A: enWP, 'Mercedes
  Barcha']** esWP names the **Iglesia de Nuestra Señora del Perpetuo Socorro**; Cervantes
  names no parish and the church could not be fixed to street precision. **[R on the
  venue]**

## 5. Prensa Latina and the road to Mexico, 1959-1961

- **24 August 1959, Bogotá**: birth of Rodrigo García Barcha. **[A: Cervantes under 1959;
  esWP; Banrep]** See contradiction 2 below.
- The tradition that **Camilo Torres** baptised the child was **not confirmed**; the campa
  carries it as "said to have". **[R]**
- **Havana**, Prensa Latina under Jorge Ricardo Masetti, 1959-1960; then the **New York**
  bureau, early 1961, and resignation with Masetti's circle when pro-Soviet cadres displace
  it. **[A: Cervantes; enWP]** FBI surveillance of the period is documented in FOIA files
  reported by the *Washington Post*, 3 September 2015. **[A]**
- **Greyhound through the segregated South**, 1961. **[R]** Route and halts **not
  documented**; source is family accounts and Gerald Martin via secondary coverage.
- Settles permanently in **Mexico City**, mid-1961; screenplay work including *El gallo de
  oro* with Carlos Fuentes from Juan Rulfo. **[A: HRC]**

## 6. The eighteen months, 1962-1967

- **16 April 1962**, Mexico City: birth of Gonzalo García Barcha. **[A: Cervantes]**
- ***La mala hora* is a 1962 Madrid book**, whose editors corrected his Caribbean Spanish
  into peninsular usage and which he repudiated. **[A: esWP, 'La mala hora']**
- **1965, the Acapulco road.** **[R]** His own account, retold with unstable comic detail;
  the HRC magazine flags it as partly mythologised and the turnaround point is undocumented.
- **1965-1966**, some eighteen months of secluded writing; car sold, appliances pawned,
  credit run. **[A: Ransom Center Magazine, 'Ascent to Glory']** The street, Calle La Loma,
  comes from the biographical literature. **[R on the coordinate]**
- **Mailing the typescript in instalments** for want of postage: the poverty is attested;
  the "wrong half first" detail **accreted years later** per Álvaro Santana-Acuña's archival
  work and is **not asserted**.
- **End of May 1967**: *Cien años de soledad*, Editorial Sudamericana, Buenos Aires, run of
  about 8,000, at Humberto Primo 555. **[A: enWP; Cervantes; esWP]** **1970**, Rabassa's
  English translation, which he said he preferred to his own original **[A: enWP]**.
  Cumulative reach **46 languages, over 50 million copies** **[A]**, superseding the
  dossier's older 37-language figure.

## 7. Barcelona, the Boom, and the political years, 1967-1976

- **Autumn 1967 to 1975**, Barcelona, Sarrià-Sant Gervasi, in the network around Carmen
  Balcells and Carlos Barral; *El otoño del patriarca* follows in 1975. **[A: Cervantes;
  HRC; enWP]**
- **The 1972 Rómulo Gallegos Prize was for *Cien años de soledad***, not for the *Eréndira*
  collection of the same year. **[A: esWP, winners table]** It was quinquennial then:
  Vargas Llosa 1967, García Márquez 1972, Fuentes 1977. The donation of the money to
  Venezuela's Movimiento al Socialismo is **not independently confirmed**. **[R]**
- **1973, the Chilean coup.** He condemned the overthrow of Allende and is widely reported
  to have pledged no more fiction until Chile was free. The pledge **could not be traced to
  a primary text**. **[R, flagged]** Staged at his own Barcelona pin with the hedge in
  `date_confidence`.
- **12 February 1976**, Mexico City: Mario Vargas Llosa punches him at the **Palacio de
  Bellas Artes**. **[A: enWP, 'Mario Vargas Llosa']** Neither principal ever gave a public
  account of the motive **[A]**, but a second-hand account **does** exist, via a mutual
  friend, so "no full account" must not be read as "no account exists".

## 8. Nobel, Cuba, Cartagena, the late books, 1982-2004

- **21 October 1982**: Swedish Academy announcement, at the Börshuset on Stortorget.
  **[A: Nobel Foundation; enWP]** **10 December 1982**, ceremony at the Konserthuset,
  Hötorget 8, in a white liqui-liqui; the lecture *La soledad de América Latina* was read
  8 December. **[A: Nobel Foundation]**
- **Cubanacán protocol house** and the domestic scenes of the Castro friendship. **[R]**
  The Vanity Fair piece of March 1988 (Pete Hamill) was **blocked on refetch**.
- **EICTV, San Antonio de los Baños**: created 1985, inaugurated **15 December 1986**, with
  Fernando Birri and Julio García Espinosa, on his initiative with Castro's backing.
  **[A: esWP]**
- **1989**: *El general en su laberinto*, and the objections it drew from historians and
  Bolivarian nationalists. **[A: enWP]**
- **1994 or 1995**: Fundación para un Nuevo Periodismo Iberoamericano, Cartagena, now the
  Fundación Gabo. **[A: Fundación Gabo]** The year is given both ways and **is not
  adjudicated**; the stop is dated 1995 and says so.
- **1996**: *Noticia de un secuestro*, on the 1990-91 kidnappings by Los Extraditables under
  Pablo Escobar. **[A]** The 2015 "Popeye" allegation about letters to Castro concerning
  narcotics is **unverified** and appears nowhere in the file. **[R, excluded]**
- **1999**: lymphatic cancer treated in Los Angeles, reported successful. **[A: enWP; New
  Yorker, 27 September 1999]** **The hospital is not established** (enWP says only "a
  hospital in Los Angeles"; the pool's "Cedars-Sinai" is a guess), so **no stop was made**
  and the illness sits inside the 2002 campa. **[gap]**
- **2002**: *Vivir para contarla*, the only completed volume of a projected three; **October
  2004**, *Memoria de mis putas tristes*, pirated in Colombia before authorised release,
  final chapter rewritten to obsolete the bootleg. **[A]**

## 9. Death and afterlife, 2012-2024

- **2012**: brother Jaime discloses the dementia. **[A: enWP]**
- **April 2014**: hospitalised in Mexico City for lung and urinary infections **[A]**;
  **17 April 2014**, dies at home in Pedregal de San Ángel, aged 87 **[A]**. The immediate
  cause (heart attack against a background of infection, prior lymphoma and dementia) is
  *El País*'s report; **that page was blocked on direct refetch**. **[R on the cause]**
- **21 April 2014**: state homage at the Palacio de Bellas Artes with Peña Nieto and Santos
  present. **[A]** The urn was *present at a ceremony*, which is not a burial.
- **2014**: archive sold to the **Harry Ransom Center**, Austin; some 27,500 items later
  online. **[A: HRC]** The quoted **2.2 million dollar** price is in press coverage but
  **not on the Center's own pages**, and is not asserted. **[R]** **February 2015**, legacy
  materials into the **Caja de las Letras**, Madrid **[A: enWP]**, folded into that campa.
- **22 May 2016**: ashes interred in the **Claustro de la Merced**, Universidad de
  Cartagena. **[A: esWP]**
- **6 March 2024**: *En agosto nos vemos*, published by Rodrigo and Gonzalo on what would
  have been his 97th birthday, against his instruction to destroy the manuscript; the sons
  call it a betrayal. **[A: enWP, 'Until August']**
- **15 August 2020**, death of Mercedes Barcha. **[R]** The date could not be verified
  against an accessible source; the file says only that she survived him by six years.
- Not staged: the **Netflix** licence of March 2019 **[A]**, which belongs to the estate,
  not to his travel; and the **2000** *La República* false death report with the "La
  Marioneta" poem misattributed to him **[R]**.

## Honest gaps

- **Zipaquirá sports.** Nothing supports the "team captain" claim. Dropped.
- **Barranquilla wedding church.** Named in one source, unconfirmed to street precision.
- **The Greyhound route.** No documented halts; one approximate corridor pin.
- **The Los Angeles hospital, 1999.** Unestablished. No stop made.
- **The Cubanacán protocol house.** Vanity Fair blocked; carried as reported.
- **Indira Cato**, a daughter from an early-1990s relationship with Susana Cato, reported
  posthumously. **[R]** The *El País* January 2022 reporting could not be confirmed.
  **Not staged**: no place, no date, and a living private individual. Recorded so the
  operator can decide.
- **The Jardín de los Poetas bust**, Palermo, Buenos Aires. Entered from the working brief,
  not from a source; esWP names only 9 of the garden's 26 honorees and tourism pages 404'd.
  **[R]** **Not staged**: unverified, and a bust is not a place he travelled to. Buenos
  Aires is in the file for the 1967 first edition instead.
- **"Fiction was invented the day Jonah arrived home..."** appears verbatim in *The
  Guardian*, 18 April 2014, **with no source line**; every other hit is an uncited
  aggregator. **[R]** **Not used** as a quote.
- **"What matters in life is not what happens to you..."** is a **paraphrase**. The Grossman
  epigraph reads "Life is not what one lived, but what one remembers and how one remembers
  it in order to recount it." The file uses that.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found, in either direction.** He is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md` and nothing in the
pool required him to be: every relation staged is rooted in his own life, reading, or
writing. Two items were considered and are flagged rather than silently dropped:

1. **Kaaba and Temple Mount, a negative result.** No attested visit or engagement beyond
   *Las mil y una noches* among his early literary shocks. Nothing was invented; a **gap**,
   not an apparatus relation.
2. **Simón Bolívar.** *El general en su laberinto* (1989) is an ordinary one-directional
   relation, a later writer treating an earlier subject. Staged on **his own file, at
   Cartagena**; nothing written into `bolivar.journey.json`.
