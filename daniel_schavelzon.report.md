# Daniel Schávelzon: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-13. Subject living at time of writing; no death date, tomb, or posthumous cult exists to research, which changes the shape of the usual last section.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or a single-source claim verification could not corroborate. Contradictions are **flagged, not adjudicated**.

---

## 0. What this subject is, and why the evidence behaves oddly

Daniel Gastón Schávelzon is an architect who became an archaeologist of the city he was born in, founding urban archaeology as a working discipline in Argentina: the Programa de Arqueología Urbana (1984), the Centro de Arqueología Urbana (1991), a national congress, a CONICET career, and something over eighty books.

The evidentiary problem is peculiar to living scholars. The **primary source for nearly the whole biography is the Spanish-language Wikipedia article "Daniel Schávelzon"** and its companion article on the Centro de Arqueología Urbana: unusually detailed (dated excavation lists, a bibliography running 1980 to 2022, a *Premios* section quoted verbatim below) and effectively unaudited, since the CAU's own site returned a **DNS failure** and search access was blocked for part of the session. Two things were reachable and read in full: **two PDFs from Schávelzon's own website**, which supply every quotation in the journey file. What follows separates what the article says, what a second source confirmed, and what fell apart on inspection.

## 2. Exile: Ecuador and Mexico (1976 – 1984)

- **1976:** with the dictatorship installed, he lives in **self-imposed exile on the coast of Ecuador**, excavating sites of the **Manteña** culture **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The period is given as 1976-1977 on the **Manabí coast**; the *specific* site is **not named in any accessed source**, so the coordinates are a regional approximation **[R for the point]**.
- **Master's degree and doctorate in architecture at the UNAM**, Mexico City, among the first generation of architecture PhDs in Latin America and, per the article, the first Argentine **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
  - **Contradiction in the pool, flagged:** one lens states "exact graduation year not given in the source consulted," another dates the stop **1978-1983**. That range has no source. The journey file carries a **reconstructed** date and says so.
- **Prehispanic restoration work in Mexico** is attested as a field of activity **[A]**. The pool proposed the **Great Pyramid of Cholula** as an emblematic site but could not confirm he worked it, tagging it **[R]**, "included as illustrative." *It is excluded from the journey file:* an illustrative site nobody verified does not become a place a traveler stood.
- Publications of the Mexican years **[A: es.wikipedia bibliography]**: *Corpus bibliográfico de la cultura olmeca* (1980), *El complejo arqueológico Mixteca-Puebla* (1980), *Arqueología y arquitectura del Ecuador prehispánico* (1981), *La polémica del arte nacional en México* (1984).
- **1981, UNESCO subsidy.** The pool logged this **[R]**, an uncorroborated CAU-derived mention placed vaguely at "Mexico/Ecuador." Verification **overturned it upward**: the award is verbatim in the es.wikipedia *Premios* section as "Subsidio de la UNESCO (PNUD, Lima), 1981," making it **[A]** and locating it at the **UNDP regional office in Lima**.
- He remains a **visiting scholar and consultant at Mexico's INAH** long after the student years **[A: es.wikipedia]**; the address is a proxy for the Mexico City headquarters, **not** confirmed **[R for the point]**.

## 3. The return, and the founding of a discipline (1984 – 1990)

- **February 1984:** returns to Buenos Aires **at the invitation of President Alfonsín**, democracy roughly two months old, and founds the **Programa de Arqueología Urbana** at the UBA **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The stop is pinned at Plaza de Mayo as the symbolic seat of the government that called him back, **not** a confirmed arrival point **[R for the point]**.
- **1985:** enters **CONICET** as a researcher **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
  - **Coordinate error corrected.** The pool gave -34.5875, -58.418, which matches no CONICET seat. Headquarters is the Polo Científico Tecnológico, Godoy Cruz 2290, approximately **-34.5806, -58.4239** (the historic seat at Rivadavia 1917 is -34.6094, -58.3927). The journey file uses the Polo Científico point.
- **Winter 1985:** excavates the **Caserón de Rosas** in the Parque Tres de Febrero, Palermo, the country house of **Juan Manuel de Rosas** **[A: es.wikipedia, "Centro de Arqueología Urbana"]**. A *direct* relation to an existing traveler: he digs Rosas's own house.
- **From 1985:** extends the method to **Mendoza**, excavating the colonial **Cabildo** **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
  - **Coordinate error corrected.** The pool's -32.8895, -68.8458 is the modern city centre. The colonial Cabildo stood on the founding site now occupied by the **Museo del Área Fundacional** (Plaza Pedro del Castillo), approximately **-32.8822, -68.8375**, 1.1 km northeast; es.wikipedia confirms the site "donde luego funcionó el Cabildo colonial antes de su destrucción en el terremoto de Mendoza de 1861."
- **1988:** publishes **"Los túneles de Buenos Aires: 140 años entre la memoria y el olvido,"** *Todo es Historia* no. 251, May 1988, pp. 8-35 **[A: cited verbatim in es.wikipedia, "Manzana de las Luces"]**. The best-corroborated publication in the file: journal, number, month and pages, cited by a *different* article than his biography.
  - **Coordinate note:** the pool's Manzana point (-34.6087, -58.3739) drifts onto the Cabildo block; the Manzana is at roughly -34.61056, -58.37444. The journey file instead **inherits the canonical Manzana pin (-34.6099, -58.3737)** from belgrano.journey.json, per the convention of shared coordinates for shared geography. A deliberate choice, not an oversight.
- **1990:** *Las ciudades mayas: historia de las teorías sobre su estructura urbana* **[A]**.

## 4. The Centro de Arqueología Urbana (1991 – 2000s)

- **7 May 1991:** founds and directs the **Centro de Arqueología Urbana** within the Instituto de Arte Americano, FADU-UBA, the first of its kind in Argentina **[A: es.wikipedia, "Centro de Arqueología Urbana," which supplies the date]**.
- **1991-1992:** excavates the **Cabildo de Buenos Aires** **[A: es.wikipedia, "Daniel Schávelzon," excavation list]**.
  - **Two false corroborations flagged.** (a) The pool cited the *Centro de Arqueología Urbana* article for this dig; that article **does not mention the Cabildo de Buenos Aires at all**, so the citation is re-pointed to the biography. (b) The pool claimed a 1991 book *El Cabildo de Buenos Aires* corroborating the dig. **No such 1991 title exists**: the 1991 entries are *Arqueología histórica de Buenos Aires* and *El Cabildo de Mendoza*, and the Buenos Aires Cabildo volume is **1995**. Clause dropped.
  - **Residual gap:** es.wikipedia's own *Cabildo* article does not corroborate the excavation. Single-source, flagged, not a contradiction.
- Books, all **[A: es.wikipedia bibliography]**: **1991** *Arqueología histórica de Buenos Aires* (Corregidor), the field's foundational text, and *El Cabildo de Mendoza*; **1992** *La arqueología urbana en la Argentina* and *Arqueología histórica de Buenos Aires: túneles y construcciones subterráneas*.
- **1994:** excavates and publishes on the **Imprenta Coni** print-works site, San Telmo **[A]**.
- Undated CAU-era excavations, **[A: es.wikipedia unless noted]**: **Museo Histórico Nacional / Parque Lezama**; **Museo Etnográfico Juan B. Ambrosetti**, where he finds its own 1905-1927 building phases **[R: the phase dating rests on the biography alone]**; the **Michelangelo** cellars on Balcarce; the **Altos de Ezcurra** on Defensa **[R]**; **Casa del Virrey Liniers**, Alta Gracia; the **barrancas de San Isidro** with **Mario Silveira**.
- Dated CAU chronology entries **[A: CAU article]**: **March 2000**, bone remains at **Plaza Roberto Arlt**; **August 2001**, restoration of a **convent structure of 1745** in the historic core, the convent **not named** in any accessed source, so the point is provisional **[R for the point]**; **September 2012**, seventeenth-century remains near **San Ignacio**.
- **1998:** *Las ruinas de San Francisco, arqueología e historia*, tied to the **Mendoza** project he directs for roughly two decades **[A]**.
  - **Coordinate corrected**, same displacement as the Mendoza Cabildo: the ruins stand in the Área Fundacional (Ituzaingó y Beltrán), approximately **-32.8830, -68.8368**, 1.2 km from the pool's point. No dedicated article verifies this; best available **[R for the point]**.
- **1999** *Arqueología de Buenos Aires*; **2000** *The Historical Archaeology of Buenos Aires*; **2001** *Excavaciones arqueológicas en San Isidro*; **2003** *Buenos Aires Negra*; **2005** *Túneles de Buenos Aires*; **2009** *La casa de Perón* **[A]**.
- **2000:** founds the **Congreso Nacional de Arqueología Histórica** **[R: in es.wikipedia but tagged R by the pool; the venue is an unconfirmed proxy]**.

## 6. The country beyond the capital

All **[A: es.wikipedia, "Daniel Schávelzon"]** unless noted; several are site-list entries with no dates, which the journey file marks in `date_confidence`.

- **Paraná, Entre Ríos:** the **Barrio del Tambor** and the ground around the **Capilla San Miguel Arcángel**, footprint of an Afro-Argentine community.
- **Puerto Deseado, Santa Cruz:** the town's Área Fundacional. **Ushuaia:** archaeology, history, and heritage of a town partly built by the prisoners of its own penal colony.
- **Roque Pérez:** the **Casa de Juan Domingo Perón**. Coordinates corrected to **-35.4167, -59.3167** (35°25′S, 59°19′W), roughly 7 km from the pool's point.
  - **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** Perón's **official birthplace is Lobos**; Roque Pérez is the revisionist claim. The pool called it "the modest rural home where he grew up," quietly taking a side. The journey file states the dispute instead.
- **Aduana Taylor, Plaza Colón.** The pool credited Schávelzon's team with a 2018 excavation here. **Both the date and the attribution are wrong.** The systematic dig ran **2009-2011** under archaeologist **Sandra Guillermo**, supervised by the Instituto Nacional de Antropología: 54 grid squares, 4 trenches, 7 test pits, the remains roofed in glass in 2011 and displayed in the Museo Casa Rosada. His connection is that **"Aduana Taylor" appears in his site list**, not that he led the dig **[A for the site on his list; the leadership claim is withdrawn]**. Coordinates corrected to **-34.60828, -58.36953**. Edward Taylor and the dates are right: built 1855, inaugurated 1857, demolished 1894.

## 7. Teyú Cuaré, the archive, and after (2015 – 2022)

- **2015:** with **Ana Igareta**, identifies structures at **Teyú Cuaré**, San Ignacio, Misiones, as a probable refuge built in anticipation of a German defeat in the Second World War **[A: es.wikipedia, "Daniel Schávelzon"]**.
  - **Coordinates corrected:** the pool's -27.2564, -55.5347 lands on San Ignacio town. The park is at 27°17′05″S, 55°35′19″W = **-27.28465, -55.58868**, about 6 km away.
  - **Gap, explicitly unclosed:** the es.wikipedia article on **Parque provincial Teyú Cuaré makes no mention** of the ruins, the 2015 study, or the Nazi hypothesis; the claim rests solely on the biography. The session's search budget was exhausted before period press (Guardian, BBC, Clarín, La Nación, Infobae) could be fetched, so **what he claimed versus what the international media inflated it into was never checked.** The largest unverified item in the file is the one the wider world knows him for.
- **2016:** *Easter Island Archaeology: A Tribute to Daniel Schavelzon on the 30th Anniversary of the Center for Urban Archaeology at the University of Buenos Aires*, ed. M. Silveira, Archaeopress, Oxford **[A]**.
  - **Arithmetic contradiction flagged.** The CAU was founded **7 May 1991**, making 2016 its **25th** anniversary; a 30th can only count from the 1984-86 Programa. Both files **quote the title as published** rather than assert the anniversary. The volume's subject is **Easter Island archaeology**, which no source explains.
- **2017:** donates his archive (correspondence, manuscripts, photographs, family documents including images of **Russia and Poland from the 1890s**) to the **Latin American Collection, University of New Mexico**, and part of his library to the **Biblioteca Nacional** **[A]**. Same year, *Teyú Cuaré: Arqueología de un refugio nazi en la Argentina* (Paidós) **[A]**.
- **2018:** retires from CONICET after more than three decades **[A]**. **2019:** *Arqueología de rescate en Casa Rosada*. **2022:** with Ana Igareta, *Arqueología de la Segunda Guerra Mundial en Sudamérica* **[A]**.
- **Corpus scale:** "more than 80 books, 1980-2022" **[R: es.wikipedia, uncounted assertion]**.

## 8. The absent afterlife

The closest analogue is **his own archive at Albuquerque**: the excavator's papers become a deposit someone else will one day read stratigraphically. Stated as a gap, not resolved: Argentine press retrospectives, or the CAU site (DNS failure), may document iconography not found here.
