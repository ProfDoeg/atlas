# Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (1840-1893): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-02. The journey file's register is "national mythology: the canon is true." This report is the audit the campa is allowed to forget.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in place; **TR** = Tchaikovsky Research (en.tchaikovsky-research.net), **W** = Wikipedia. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or wide circulation whose primary basis was not reached. Contradictions are printed side by side and left open; where the pool itself carried an error, the error is printed with its correction.

**One structural fact governs half the corrections.** He composed in one year and was premiered in the next, sometimes three years later, and timeline compilations print the composition year under the word "premiere." Six of the pool's date errors are that substitution.

---

## 1. Votkinsk and the Urals, 1840-1850

**[A W, 'Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky'; Tchaikovsky Research, Chronology]** Born 7 May 1840 at Votkinsk, Vyatka Governorate, second son of Ilya Petrovich Tchaikovsky, manager of the Kamsko-Votkinsk ironworks, and Aleksandra Andreyevna. The manager's house survives as a museum (57.0533, 53.9675). Governess Fanny Dürbach engaged 1844; first piano lessons from Maria Palchikova from about 1845, age five.

**[A TR, Chronology / Votkinsk] against [R Encyclopedia.com].** The pool carried an Encyclopedia.com claim that the family moved to St Petersburg in 1850 on Ilya's retirement. TR gives a granular and different sequence: left Votkinsk 26 September / 8 October 1848; briefly Moscow; St Petersburg November 1848; the hoped-for appointment failed; May 1849 back east to Alapayevsk; Pyotr alone returned to the capital in 1850, for the School of Jurisprudence. **Both recorded; the journey file follows TR and flags the divergence in date_confidence.** The Encyclopedia.com date is compressed rather than flatly wrong, collapsing a two-year zigzag into its endpoint.

**Gap.** No reachable source gives coordinates for the Alapayevsk residence; that stop is pinned on the town (57.85, 61.6833).

## 2. The School of Jurisprudence, 1850-1859

**[A W; TR]** Enrolled September 1850 at the Imperial School of Jurisprudence on the Fontanka, boarding nine years, some 1,300 km from his family; sang in the school choir. Mother died of cholera June 1854 (TR: 25 June); he was fourteen, inside the school, and did not see her before the end. Graduated 10 June 1859 as titular counselor, clerkship in the Ministry of Justice; from 1861, theory classes of the Russian Musical Society, his first adult formal training. The classmate cohort is also the body the 1979 suicide theory names as his tribunal (section 8).

---

## 3. The first circuit abroad, 1861

**[A TR]** At twenty-one, across Europe as secretary and interpreter to a businessman: Berlin, Hamburg, Antwerp, Brussels, London, Paris, in weeks. First journey outside Russia.

**Gap.** The chronology gives cities, no day dates, no order. The journey file carries one stop, pinned at Paris, the other cities named in campa rather than pinned on invented dates.

---

## 4. Conservatory, Moscow, first works, 1862-1875

**[A W; TR]** Enrolled 1862 at the new St Petersburg Conservatory under Anton Rubinstein; resigned the civil service 1863; graduated 1865 with a silver medal.

**[A St Petersburg State Conservatory official history] correcting the pool's coordinates.** The pool pinned the Conservatory at 59.9256, 30.2967, the Theatre Square building, occupied only from 1896. In 1862-66 it was in the Demidov house at Demidov Lane (now Grivtsova) and the Moika, roughly 59.929, 30.314. Corrected in the journey file.

**[A Moscow Conservatory (Wikipedia); TR] correcting the pool.** "Professorship at the new Moscow Conservatory, 1865" is anachronistic: it did not yet exist. Nikolai Rubinstein made the offer in autumn 1865 for the Moscow branch classes of the Musical Society; Tchaikovsky moved January 1866; the Conservatory opened 1 September 1866 (O.S.). Silver medal and 1865 graduation stand.

**[A W]** 11 September 1865: Characteristic Dances at Pavlovsk under Johann Strauss II, his first public hearing of his own orchestral writing. **[A TR]** From 1865, near-annual stays at Kamenka, the Ukrainian estate of his sister Aleksandra Davydova (49.0167, 32.15). The Voyevoda premiered at the Bolshoi, 11 February 1869.

**[A W, 'Désirée Artôt'] correcting the pool.** Meeting and engagement 1868 stand; "within the year" does not. Artôt married the baritone Mariano Padilla y Ramos in 1869, in Warsaw.

**[A W] correcting [R World History Encyclopedia timeline], twice.** Romeo and Juliet: composed 1869, first performed 16 March 1870, Moscow, under Nikolai Rubinstein (second version 1872, final version 1886 in Tbilisi). First Piano Concerto: premiered 25 October 1875 in Boston, Hans von Bülow soloist, Benjamin Johnson Lang conducting; 1874 is composition and Rubinstein's denunciation. In both the pool printed composition year as premiere.

## 5. Marriage, von Meck, the wandering years, 1876-1880

**[A TR]** August 1876: Bayreuth, inaugural festival and first complete Ring, attended partly as newspaper correspondent; he published reservations about Wagner's results as against his theories. **[R]** No meeting of the two composers appears in any source reached here, nor in the atlas's Wagner file.

**[A W, 'Francesca da Rimini'] correcting [R World History Encyclopedia].** Composed Moscow, October-November 1876; premiered 9 March 1877, Moscow, under Nikolai Rubinstein.

**[A W, 'Nadezhda von Meck'; TR]** From 1877, a fourteen-year correspondence and an annual allowance, on condition the two never meet; the Fourth is dedicated to her, inscribed only "to my best friend" so her name should not appear [A W, 'Symphony No. 4']. **[A W]** July 1877: marriage to Antonina Milyukova in Moscow, collapse within weeks, about two and a half months of cohabitation. **[A TR]** November 1877: Vienna, joined by the violinist Iosif Kotek; a year of wandering through Switzerland, Italy, France follows.

**[A TR, Clarens; Wikipedia, 'Violin Concerto'] correcting the pool's own geography entry.** The pool gave the Clarens output as The Maid of Orleans. The principal output there, March-April 1878, was the Violin Concerto, written with Kotek at hand; The Maid of Orleans was begun at Clarens in December 1878. The concerto premiered 4 December 1881, Vienna, Adolph Brodsky under Hans Richter, after Leopold Auer declined it: the pool's 1878 premiere is three years out and is again the composition year.

**[A TR, Florence] correcting the pool's own geography entry.** The villa-nearby-but-never-meeting arrangement belongs to November-December 1878, not February 1878. Eugene Onegin was completed at San Remo in early 1878, not in Florence.

**[A W, 'Brailiv'; von Meck estate, Wikidata] correcting the pool's coordinates.** Brailov, lent to him May 1878, is at approximately 49.103, 28.171; the pool's 49.3167, 27.6667 points at open country some 40 km northwest. Date and arrangement stand. **The Brailov stop was cut from the journey file for length, not doubt; the corrected coordinate is here for whoever restores it.**

**[A Metropolitan Opera educator guide; Wikipedia]** Eugene Onegin premiered 29 March 1879, Maly Theatre, Moscow, sung by Conservatory students under Nikolai Rubinstein. **[A TR]** December 1879 into early 1880: Rome, carnival street music feeding the Capriccio Italien. **[A W]** 1880: Dostoevsky's Pushkin speech credited with helping rehabilitate his critical standing at home; reputation, not travel, so no stop.

## 6. The court composer, 1881-1887

**[A W]** 23 March 1881: Nikolai Rubinstein dies in Paris. 1884: Order of St Vladimir, fourth class, conferring hereditary nobility; 1885: new Eugene Onegin production commissioned by Alexander III, plus a lifetime pension of 3,000 rubles. **[A Tchaikovsky Research; Rusmania]** 26 February 1885: rents the house on the Sestra at Maydanovo near Klin, his first fixed home.

**[A W] correcting [R World History Encyclopedia], twice.** The 1812 Overture was composed 1880 and premiered 20 August 1882 in Moscow under Ippolit Al'tani, in a tent beside the still-unfinished Cathedral of Christ the Saviour, during the All-Russia Arts and Industry Exhibition. Manfred was written May-September 1885 and premiered 23 March 1886, Moscow, under Max Erdmannsdörfer.

**[A TR]** April 1886: Tiflis, a month with his brother Anatoly; back in 1887 and 1889. **[A W]** January 1887: conducts Cherevichki at the Bolshoi. **[A TR]** 1887: the Volga route south from Nizhny Novgorod by steamer toward the Caspian, then Baku, Tiflis, Borzhom; **[R]** the Astrakhan leg is tagged reconstruction in the pool, gets no stop, and is named in the Baku campa. 15-27 July 1887: Aachen, at Nikolai Kondratyev's deathbed.

## 7. Conducting tours and America, 1888-1891

**[A Tchaikovsky Research; Berliner Philharmoniker] with correction.** First European tour opens at the Leipzig Gewandhaus, 5 January 1888; he meets Brahms and Grieg on that visit. **The pool's "at fifty-seven" is ten years wrong: born 7 May 1840, he was 47.**

**[A TR]** 20 January 1888 Hamburg, Conventgarten; February Berlin Philharmonic; Prague, National Theatre, the tour's warmest reception; February-March, three concerts in Paris. *None of the four got a stop: the tour is carried by Leipzig and London, the rest named in campa. Largest compression in the journey file, and a length decision.*

**[A TR, London / Serenade for String Orchestra] correcting the pool.** St James's Hall, Piccadilly, Philharmonic Society second concert, 22 March 1888 (10 March O.S.): Serenade for Strings and the Theme and Variations from Suite No. 3. The pool's 20 March is two days out.

**[A TR; W]** April 1888: leases Frolovskoye near Klin; Fifth Symphony and The Sleeping Beauty written there; plants trees and grieves when the owner has the wood felled. 1888: conducts the premiere of the Fifth in St Petersburg. **Gap:** the pool gives only the year. The standard date is 5/17 November 1888; the journey file dates the stop 1888-11-01 and says so in date_confidence rather than assert an unverified day. Mariinsky: Oprichnik 1874, Sleeping Beauty January 1890, Iolanta with Nutcracker December 1892.

**[A Carnegie Hall; TR]** 5 May 1891: conducts opening night of Carnegie's Music Hall, leading his Marche solennelle; the American visit runs 26 April to 21 May. 18 May: Academy of Music, Philadelphia, his last American concert; sails three days later on the Prince Bismarck [A Broad Street Review]. **[R Classical Music Guide Forums]** Baltimore 15-16 May and a Russian Embassy reception in Washington rest on a single discussion-forum post; neither got a stop, and both are named inside the Philadelphia campa instead.

## 8. Klin, the Pathétique, the death, 1892-1893

**[A W, Klin house-museum; TR]** May 1892: the house on the edge of Klin, his last address, where the Sixth is written. **Coordinate contradiction, three values, unresolved:** the pool's geography entry gives 56.3339, 36.7317; its afterlife entry 56.3339, 36.7278 for the same house; Wikipedia 56°19'44"N 36°44'49"E, that is 56.3289, 36.7469. All within about 1.4 km. The journey file uses the Wikipedia value; this line records that the others exist.

**[A W; TR]** 1892: corresponding member of the Académie des Beaux-Arts, Paris. June 1893: honorary Doctor of Music, Cambridge, with Saint-Saëns, Bruch, Boito, and Grieg in absentia. 28 October 1893: conducts the premiere of the Sixth, nine days before his death. 1 November: the glass of unboiled water at Leiner's on Nevsky Prospekt, drunk despite warnings. 6 November: dies at Malaya Morskaya at three in the morning, aged fifty-three; cholera given as the cause.

**[R calendar, not contradiction]** Russian sources of the period date the death 25 October 1893 (Julian), Western sources 6 November (Gregorian). Dual dating, not disagreement; the journey file declares `"calendar": "gregorian"`.

**[R Aleksandra Orlova, from 1979; Washington Post, 1981]** The court-of-honour theory: School of Jurisprudence classmates convening secretly and condemning him to suicide by arsenic, to suppress scandal over his homosexuality. Medical historians dispute both cholera and arsenic; the contemporaneous records are contradictory and incomplete. **Unresolved; the journey file carries both accounts and adjudicates neither.**

**[A W; Hartford Stage]** 9 November 1893: funeral at Kazan Cathedral, granted and paid for by Alexander III; capacity 6,000, some 60,000 applications, 8,000 pressed inside. Buried at Tikhvin Cemetery, Alexander Nevsky Lavra, among Glinka, Mussorgsky, Borodin, Dargomyzhsky, later Rimsky-Korsakov. **Minor contradiction inside the pool:** geography gives 59.9219, 30.3897, afterlife 59.9219, 30.3886. The journey file uses 30.3897; sixty metres, noted, not argued.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A W; Rusmania]** 9 December 1894: Modest opens the Klin house as a museum, archive and interiors intact, Russia's oldest memorial music museum, and directs it until his death in 1916.

**[A W, Klin house-museum] correcting the pool's tag from [R] to [A].** The wartime episode is documented, not tourist legend: collection evacuated to Votkinsk 1941; German forces in the Battle of Moscow used the ground floor as a motorcycle garage and the upper floor as a barracks; exhibits back late 1944; reopened 6 May 1945.

**[A Voices of British Ballet; Library of Congress, Nijinska Collection] with correction.** Diaghilev's The Sleeping Princess, Alhambra Theatre, London, from 2 November 1921, Bakst designs, Stravinsky re-orchestration, a disaster that broke the company. **105 consecutive performances to 4 February 1922, not the pool's 115.**

**[A TR]** Soviet Complete Collected Works, 107 volumes, 1940-1990: tsarist anthem fragments excised from the 1812 Overture and the Slavonic March; liturgical music omitted until a 1990 supplement; some works, such as the full score of Vakula the Smith, left out.

**[A cliburn.org; Juilliard Journal; WFIMC] with two corrections.** First International Tchaikovsky Competition, Moscow, April 1958; Van Cliburn, 23, wins and returns to a ticker-tape parade. **Shostakovich chaired the organizing committee, not the piano jury, over which Emil Gilels presided.** The recording went gold in 1961; the documented superlative is **first classical album to go platinum** (1989), not "first certified gold."

**[A Classical California]** Soviet editions bowdlerize letters to Modest and other men; uncensored originals surface after 1991. **[R PinkNews 2017; LGBTQ Nation 2022]** 2013: screenwriter Yuri Arabov, on a state-funded biopic, denies the composer's homosexuality; ministry officials echo it; references stripped from the script amid the anti-gay propaganda law.

## 10. Quotations: verbatim, misattributed, unverified

Used in the journey file, verbatim, attribution corrected where needed. Life & Letters means Modeste Tchaikovsky's edition, trans. Rosa Newmarch (1906).

- **"Oh, this music, this music! Save me from it!..."** [A Life & Letters, Part I, Ch. II] Dürbach's recollection of the child; reported speech in a biography, not a document in his hand.
- **"First I must give you some very sad news..."** [A letter to Fanny Dürbach, 1856] on his mother's death. **"I only feel I must do the work for which I have a vocation..."** [A letter to his sister, September 1862].
- **"Perhaps I shall not turn out a Glinka..."** Words verbatim; **the pool's attribution was wrong on three counts.** Newmarch places it c. late 1862, as reported speech to his **brother Nikolai**, not in a letter to his sister of October 1861. "Own me as a brother" only parses addressed to a brother.
- **"In my life, too, there are days, hours, weeks, aye, and months, in which everything looks black."** Verbatim, found by grepping Gutenberg 45259. **The circulating recipient, "his nephew," is an error inherited from The Marginalian.** Newmarch's footnote glosses the address as "goloubouska," a feminine diminutive, and TR identifies Letter 513, 8/20 November 1876, to his sister **Aleksandra Davydova**. The content, contrasting his bachelor life with hers as indispensable to a family, fits a mother of seven, not a five-year-old nephew.
- **"The weather was glorious in the morning..."** [A Diaries, 1/13 April 1886] and **two passages from the Autobiographical Account of a Tour Abroad in the Year 1888** [A trans. Sundkvist], on the Cherevichki conducting proposal and on Grieg.

**Materially truncated in the pool, restored, not used in cut form.** The diary of 2 April 1886 was carried as "A grey and rainy day. After tea read Sarcey's article on Tolstoy and could scarcely contain myself." It continues: "from **sobbing... with joy** that the French understand our Tolstoy so well." The cut inverts weeping for joy into unexplained distress. Sarcey is the drama critic Francisque Sarcey (1827-1899).

**Not used, flagged [R], primary text not reached:** two von Meck lines ("I don't need to tell you that you are the person I love with all my heart"; "Before I met you, I did not know that there were people with such an affectionate and deep disposition"), traced to *Beloved Friend* (1937) and unconfirmed, and "I assert that life is beautiful in spite of everything!", cited to spring 1870 via The Marginalian and not found in the full texts.

## 11. Honest gaps

1. **No Russian-language primary archive was consulted.** Everything passes through English translation or English reference works, mostly TR and W.
2. **Modeste Tchaikovsky is load-bearing and not disinterested:** brother, editor, museum director, the party with most motive to shape the record on sexuality and on the death. Every childhood anecdote reaches us through him.
3. **Antonina Milyukova has no voice in any source reached.** The marriage is described from his side and his brothers'; her own account was not consulted.
4. **Day dates missing** for the 1861 circuit, the 1887 Volga journey, the Prague and Berlin 1888 concerts, and the Fifth Symphony premiere. Where the journey file gives an unverified day, date_confidence says so.
5. **Bowen and von Meck, *Beloved Friend* (1937)** not read, though three circulating quotations depend on it; the Soviet Complete Collected Works are described from catalogue pages, not the volumes; The Nutcracker's ubiquity is an [R] synthesis of a trend with no single sourced event behind it.
6. **Klin and Tikhvin coordinates disagree between sources** (section 8). Printed, not argued.
