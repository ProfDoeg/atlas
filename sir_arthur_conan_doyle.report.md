# Sir Arthur Ignatius Conan Doyle (1859-1930) - research report

*Atlas of Journeys, written 2026-09-01 from the verified research pool and its correction
notes. Slug: `sir_arthur_conan_doyle`. Output: 43 stops in 8 segments.*

**Legend.** `[A]` attested, source named. `[R]` reconstruction, tradition, or a claim that
could not be carried back to a fetchable source. Contradictions are flagged in bold where they
arise and are not adjudicated; gaps are stated as gaps. The pool's five lenses disagreed with

## 1. Edinburgh and the Jesuits, 1859-1876

- Born 22 May 1859 at 11 Picardy Place to Charles Altamont Doyle, Scottish Office clerk and
  unsuccessful artist, and Mary Foley `[A: Wikipedia, 'Arthur Conan Doyle', date and address
  verbatim]`. House demolished "around 1970", a Sherlock Holmes statue now on the site `[A:
  same source, which names no sculptor and no unveiling date]`.
- **Coordinate defect, unresolved.** Three lenses gave three points for that one address:
  55.9581/-3.1875, 55.9578/-3.1882, 55.9578/-3.1873. No coordinate source was fetchable
  (Wikipedia has no "Picardy Place" article, 404). The file uses 55.9578/-3.1882 and says so.
- Baptised at St Mary's, Broughton Street `[A as to the church]`; the date 24 May 1859 is
  `[R]`, dossier-only. **The building must not be called a cathedral in 1859**: it was St
  Mary's Roman Catholic Church until 1878. The pool carried that anachronism; corrected. Pin
  about 100 m south of the church.
- Charles Altamont Doyle institutionalised for alcoholism and psychiatric decline, died
  10 October 1893 at the Crichton Royal `[R: dossier synthesis of a modern psychiatric-history
  article]`. Arthur boarded with Mary Burton at Liberton Bank House c. 1866-1868 `[R]`.
- **Family address 1868-1875, contradiction flagged.** Wikipedia gives **3 Sciennes Place**,
  the dossier **3 Sciennes Hill Place**. The pool called these "the same address, minor naming
  variance"; they are two distinct adjacent streets and nothing fetched establishes them as
  one. Both forms stand.
- Hodder Place from 1868, Stonyhurst from 1870, Stella Matutina at Feldkirch 1875-76 `[A:
  Wikipedia chronology; 'Stonyhurst College' for attendance and the Moriarty and Baskerville
  Hall connections, though it gives no dates]`. **Gap:** no page on Stella Matutina was
  fetchable.

## 2. Medicine and Joseph Bell, 1876-1881

- Matriculated in medicine 1876 `[A]`. 'The Mystery of Sasassa Valley', anonymously in
  Chambers's Edinburgh Journal, 6 September 1879 `[A, exact date]`.
- Outpatient clerk to Joseph Bell at the Edinburgh Royal Infirmary from 1877; Bell's clinical
  deduction the acknowledged Holmes model; Doyle wrote to Bell in 1892 saying so `[A:
  Wikipedia, 'Joseph Bell']`. **Gap:** the letter was not fetched, so nothing from it is
  quoted, and the famous "It is most certainly to you that I owe Sherlock Holmes" sentence is
  not in the pool and is not used.
- Ruyton-XI-Towns 1878: **four months, unpaid, for a Dr Eliot, lodging at Cliffe House** `[A:
  Wikipedia, 'Ruyton-XI-Towns', which also supplies the quotable line about the village name
  from the 1923 memoir]`. This upgrades the pool's dossier-only entry and **corrects its
  coordinate by about 4 km** (pool 52.8156/-2.9432, article 52.793/-2.899). Sheffield and
  Aston under Dr Reginald Hoare stay `[R]`.
- M.B., C.M. 1881 `[A]`; M.D. 1885, thesis *An Essay upon the Vasomotor Changes in Tabes
  Dorsalis* `[A]`.

## 3. The sea, 1880-1882

- Unqualified ship's surgeon on the Peterhead whaler Hope under Captain John Gray, Greenland
  waters, 1880 `[A]`. February sailing and Peterhead as home port are `[R]`. Doyle's own
  illustrated diary is primary, published 2012 as *Dangerous Work*. **Gap:** the cruise's
  tracklines are established nowhere fetched, so the pin (70.5/-20.0) is a declared
  representative point.
- Ship's surgeon on the SS Mayumba to West Africa, 1881; severe fever at Lagos `[A]`. The
  retrospective malaria diagnosis is unproven and the dossier flags it.
- Six-week Plymouth partnership with Dr George Turnavine Budd, early 1882 `[R]`, folded into
  the Southsea campa rather than given its own stop.

## 4. Southsea, 1882-1890

- Practice at 1 Bush Villas, Elm Grove, from June 1882 `[A]`. *A Study in Scarlet* sold
  outright to Ward, Lock for a flat £25, accepted 20 November 1886, published in the 1887
  *Beeton's Christmas Annual* `[A]`.
- Freemasonry at Phoenix Lodge No. 257, 26 January 1887 `[A]`. Goalkeeper for an amateur
  Portsmouth club as A. C. Smith `[R]`: the dossier itself flags the club's identity as hard
  to reconstruct and the ground could not be confirmed, so the coordinate is marked as not a
  confirmed venue. Psychical interest from the mid-1880s; joined the SPR 1893 `[A]`.
- **Marriage to Louisa Hawkins, 6 August 1885: the pool's `[R]` was too weak, corrected.**
  Wikipedia, 'Thornton in Lonsdale', states outright that Doyle married his first wife at that
  church in 1885, held the reception at The Marton Arms, left for a honeymoon in Ireland, and
  that his mother resided at nearby Masongill from 1882 to 1917 `[A]`. **Coordinate corrected
  by about 7.5 km**: pool 54.167/-2.381 lands near Clapham, the article gives 54.2232/-2.4364.
- **Lippincott's dinner, August 1889.** `[A]` that *The Sign of the Four* was commissioned at
  a London dinner also attended by Oscar Wilde. `[R]` for everything else: J. M. Stoddart as
  host, the August date, the pairing with *The Picture of Dorian Gray*. Wilde's own file stages
  the Dorian Gray outcome at Tite Street and does not name Doyle. **Gap:** no venue is
  attested, so the pin is a declared central-London placeholder.

## 5. The Strand years, 1890-1900

- **Address conflict, unresolved.** For the 1891 eye consulting room Wikipedia gives both
  **2 Upper Wimpole Street** and **2 Devonshire Place**, the dossier flagging the same
  uncertainty. Both stand; the file pins Upper Wimpole Street and says so. 'A Scandal in
  Bohemia', Strand, July 1891, illustrated by Sidney Paget `[A]`.
- Davos, winter 1892-93: Louisa's tuberculosis, Doyle on Norwegian skis, written up as 'An
  Alpine Pass on Ski' `[A: dossier, citing his own article]`. **The dossier itself flags as
  overstatement** the claim that he introduced skiing to the Alps; the campa rejects it.
- **Reichenbach Falls: coordinate confirmed exactly (46.71361/8.18306), tag demoted.** The
  pool's `[A]` on the *visit* overreaches: the article gives no date for it and rests the Sir
  Henry Lunn story on a grandson's recollection, and the pool's own date field conceded "exact
  date of visit unrecorded". The visit is `[R]`. Still `[A]`: 'The Final Problem', December
  1893, and the plaque dating the fictional confrontation to 4 May 1891.
- Egypt and the Sudan frontier 1895-96, Westminster Gazette reporting, *The Tragedy of the
  Korosko* `[A: dossier, citing the published work]`. **Gap:** no itinerary point, so Cairo
  stands for the theatre.
- Undershaw, Hindhead, 1897-1907 `[A: Wikipedia, 'Undershaw', for coordinate, the 1897 build,
  the architect Joseph Henry Ball, and the residence dates]`. Met Jean Leckie 1897 `[A]`; the
  specific 15 March is dossier-only `[R]`.

## 6. The public man, 1900-1912

- Langman Field Hospital, Bloemfontein, March to June 1900, volunteer senior physician,
  enteric fever killing more men than the fighting `[A]`. **Gap:** hospital site not located,
  pin is the city centre.
- **Edinburgh Central, October 1900: name error corrected.** The winner was **George Mackenzie
  Brown** (Liberal), not "C. M. Brown": 3,028 (55.2%) to Doyle's 2,459 (44.8%), majority 569,
  turnout 5,487 of 7,630 electors `[A: Wikipedia, 'Edinburgh Central (UK Parliament
  constituency)']`. The pool's totals were right, its dossier-only sourcing now replaceable.
- Knight Bachelor, Buckingham Palace, 24 October 1902; Knight of Grace of the Order of St John
  1903; 'The Adventure of the Empty House' 1903, driven partly by large American publisher
  offers. All `[A]`.
- **George Edalji: the pool contradicted itself on the date.** Its chronology lens put Doyle's
  involvement, the meeting, and the approach to Chief Constable Anson in **January 1907**; its
  geography lens staged the hotel meeting in **1906**. The file uses January 1907. He first
  read Edalji's statement in *The Umpire* in late 1906; that he went to the crime scenes and
  interviewed participants is `[A]`; the May 1907 inquiry pardoned the maiming conviction but
  **refused compensation**, holding that he had written certain anonymous letters `[A]`.
  **Gap:** Wikipedia names only "a hotel", so "Grand Hotel lobby" and 51.5074/-0.1246 are a
  declared placeholder.
- **Oscar Slater.** Marion Gilchrist, 83, beaten to death in West Princes Street, Glasgow,
  December 1908; *The Case of Oscar Slater* 1912 `[A]`. The article gives **no house number**,
  so the pool's "49" is `[R]`, case literature saying 15 Queen's Terrace; and 55.8687/-4.2669
  sits about 350 m east of the street, unresolved. The 1928 quashing and the compensation are
  `[R]`, dossier-only.
- Louisa died 4 July 1906; marriage to Jean Leckie September 1907; Windlesham from 1907. All
  `[A]`. That the ten-year courtship was scrupulously unconsummated is `[R]`, standard
  biography, undocumented in anything fetched, and marked so in the file.
- **White City Stadium, 24 July 1908: two pool errors corrected.** (1) The subscription Doyle
  proposed to the Daily Mail was for **Pietri's bakery**, reaching £300; the gilt silver cup
  was Queen Alexandra's separate gift. (2) The pool called the £5 "weakly sourced", which is
  backwards: Wikipedia, 'Dorando Pietri', states Doyle "provided the initial 5 pounds"
  himself. Date, commission, the four further falls, the disqualification on the American
  protest, and Doyle's own sentence about Pietri are all `[A]`.

## 7. Spiritualism and the war, 1914-1930

- Wellington House, 2 September 1914: twenty-five leading authors summoned in secret by
  Charles Masterman, Hardy, Bennett, Wells and Chesterton among them, Kipling invited but
  unable to come, the pledge holding until 1935 `[A]`. **Coordinate corrected:** the article
  puts the building at the corner of Petty France and Buckingham Gate, nearer 51.4996/-0.1370
  than the pool's 51.4989/-0.1417.
- Western Front tour 1916, *A Visit to Three Fronts*, the six-volume *British Campaign in
  France and Flanders* `[A: dossier, citing the published works]`. **Gap:** no fixed
  itinerary, so a Somme-sector point stands for the tour.
- *The Crime of the Congo* 1909 and the Congo Reform Association `[A]`; clemency sought for
  Roger Casement, refused, hanged 3 August 1916 `[A]` for the appeal, the execution date
  dossier-corroborated only. That Doyle knew Casement personally is `[R]`.
- Public commitment to Spiritualism 1916, *The New Revelation* 1918, *The History of
  Spiritualism* 1926, all `[A]`. Kingsley born 15 November 1892, died 1918 `[A]`; the Somme
  wound is dossier detail `[R]` and no cause of death is stated, the pool giving none.
- The Evan Powell sitting in Devonshire, 1920, attested from Doyle's own words in *The
  Wanderings of a Spiritualist* ch. I. **Gap:** no venue is named there, so the pin
  (50.72/-3.53) is a declared county placeholder.
- **Cottingley.** Photographs 1917; 'Fairies Photographed' in the Strand, December 1920; *The
  Coming of the Fairies* 1922; the 1983 confession in *The Unexplained*, cut-outs copied from
  Claude A. Shepperson's illustrations in *Princess Mary's Gift Book*, Frances alone
  maintaining the fifth photograph genuine. All `[A]`. **Coordinate corrected by about
  3.4 km:** the pool gave 53.8347/-1.7803 and 53.8317/-1.7897, both east of the village; the
  article gives 53.8295/-1.8308. **Doyle never went there**, see §8.
- **Atlantic City, 18 June 1922: the pool's `[R]` was too weak, the core is `[A]`.**
  Wikipedia, 'Ambassador Hotel (Atlantic City)', dates the meeting and Lady Doyle's automatic
  writing to 18 June 1922; PBS American Experience confirms the fifteen sheets and Houdini's
  objection that his mother "had barely uttered a word in English while alive". **Two
  sub-claims were dropped:** the Christian cross appears in neither fetched source, and Cecilia
  Weiss's birthday is usually given as 16 June, so it did not fall on the day. **Date
  contradiction, inherited:** Houdini's own *A Magician Among the Spirits* gives 17 June, as
  does `harry_houdini.journey.json`; the file follows the 18th and flags it. Coordinate
  39.3523/-74.4456 inherited byte-identical.
- All `[R]`, dossier-only: the Australasian mission 1920-21 (no venue, Sydney a representative
  point); the 1919 Royal Albert Hall lectures and the 1930 memorial with the empty chair; the
  1925 Paris congress and honorary presidency, no hall named; the Psychic Bookshop in Victoria
  Street, street number unconfirmed; the Bignell Wood fire of 15 August 1929, house site not
  pinpointed; the Home Office deputation to J. R. Clynes, 1 July 1930.

## 8. Death, burial, afterlife, and one ownership ruling

- Death at Windlesham, 7 July 1930, aged 71, heart failure after recurrent angina `[A]`. Last
  words to Jean, "You are wonderful": `[R]`, family testimony only, no independent transcript,
  the dossier flagging them as intimate rather than verifiable. Carried as a quote with that
  provenance stated in `quote_source`.
- Burial 11 July 1930, open-air, in the Windlesham rose garden `[A]`. **The "standing upright"
  detail is `[R]` and the pool's `[A]` was wrong:** it is unsourced in Wikipedia, 'Minstead'
  and 'Crowborough', two pages repeating each other, which is not independent attestation.
  **Coordinate conflict:** the afterlife lens gave 51.0537/0.1608 for the house its geography
  lens twice gave as 51.0553/0.1547; the file uses the latter.
- Reinterment 1955, All Saints churchyard, Minstead, under a tree on the south side `[A as to
  year, place and position]`. **Coordinate corrected by about 4.5 km:** afterlife lens
  50.8847/-1.6591, out in the forest; article 50.9027/-1.6097. **No month or day is recorded
  for 1955. Gap.**
- **Who lies in the grave: three readings, not reconciled.** (1) The pool: his second wife
  Jean Leckie, d. 1940. (2) Wikipedia, 'Crowborough': "later interred with his first wife at
  Minstead". (3) Wikipedia, 'Minstead': re-interred "by the family of his deceased first
  wife", naming the other occupant as "his daughter Jean Conan Doyle". The file states all
  three. The inscription is `[A]` from 'Arthur Conan Doyle', **not** from the Minstead
  article, which carries no inscription text.
- Posthumous items carried as `suggested_refs` on the Minstead stop, not as stops, all `[A]`
  except as noted: Sherlock Holmes Museum 1990; Undershaw's rescue (High Court ruling 30 May
  2012, DFN purchase 2014, Stepping Stones School September 2016); the Richard Lancelyn Green
  collection bequeathed to Portsmouth August 2004, its item counts `[R]`; *Klinger*; the canon
  in the US public domain from 1 January 2023. Recorded but not asserted in prose: the absence
  of "Elementary, my dear Watson" from the canon `[R]`, not re-verified against all sixty
  texts, and Doyle as the "St Paul of Spiritualism" `[R]`.
- **Stop ownership, one case, decided.** Doyle's only physical relation to Cottingley is that
  he never went there: Wikipedia states he "did not visit Cottingley himself", and Edward
  Gardner handled every contact with the Wright family. The beck is therefore not a Doyle
  scene. The file stages the episode where he actually acted, in London, examining prints and
  publishing, and carries the beck and the Bradford holdings as `suggested_refs`. No other
  candidate arose; every remaining stop is a place Doyle stood, or a declared placeholder
  whose `date_confidence` says so.
- **Cut for want of sourcing**, popular tradition absent from the pool: the polished front
  step, the "great northern diver", the Branger brothers, the armbands, Edalji's eight
  dioptres, the Stonyhurst pastry.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found, in either direction.** Doyle is not in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no relation in this
pool required a framework applied backward to material he never discussed, nor forward past
1930. Nothing was deleted on those grounds. Two things came close, and are named here so the
operator can rule if he disagrees. The **posthumous artifacts** (museum, *Klinger*, the 2023
expiry, Stepping Stones School, the 1983 confession) are not apparatus relations but later
events embedded in a file whose own clock is 2026, which the block rule permits; they sit in
`suggested_refs` and one closing campa clause, as `abdelkader.journey.json` carries the 1966
repatriation. The **Royal Albert Hall memorial of 13 July 1930** postdates his death, so it
sits inside the campa of a stop dated to his own 1919 lectures in that hall, not staged as a
scene he attended, with the `date_confidence` saying so.
