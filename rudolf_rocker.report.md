# Rudolf Rocker (1873-1958) research report

*2026-08-22, for `rudolf_rocker.journey.json`. 45 stops, 9 segments.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or inference · **[C]** correction applied against a research lens, disagreement stated not adjudicated away · **[G]** gap, recorded as a gap.

Rocker's whole public life happened in languages and countries not his own, and his principal biographical source is his own memoir. Nearly everything below traces to two families of source: the encyclopaedic entries, which themselves follow those memoirs, and *The London Years* in full text.

## 1. Mainz, 1873-1892

- Born **25 March 1873, Mainz**, to **Georg Philipp Rocker**, lithographer, and **Anna Margaretha Naumann**; Catholic working-class household. Father dies **1877**, mother **February 1887**. **[A, both entries agree]**
- Placed in a **Catholic institution in Mainz**, flees it repeatedly, once sleeping in the woods. **[A as fact; the sole authority is Rocker's memoirs]** The building is not located anywhere reachable; the file's pin is a Mainz interior coordinate and says so. **[G]**
- **c.1887** cabin boy on the **Köln-Düsseldorfer** line down to **Rotterdam**. **[R: reported for roughly his fourteenth year, no month, no day, no length of service]** **c.1888-1890** apprenticed **bookbinder and typographer**. **May 1890** joins the **SPD**, founds the reading circle **Freiheit**, is expelled after criticising an SPD candidate. **October 1891** founds a Mainz section of the **Union of Independent Socialists**. **[A]**
- **August 1891, Brussels**: converts to anarchism at the International Socialist Congress; a smuggler named **Karl Höfer** hands him Bakunin and Kropotkin. **[A for congress and conversion; R for Höfer and the pamphlets, reported without a document]**
- **18 December 1892** speaks at a meeting of the unemployed; another speaker calls for theft, police move on the hall, he escapes narrowly and leaves Germany within days, also evading conscription. **[A]**

**Cut.** The geography lens supplies a **Frankfurt** transit stop for the flight west, **[R]**, supported by nothing but plausibility. A place that exists only because a road runs through it is not a stop.

## 2. Paris and first London years, 1893-1898

- **1893, Paris**: Jewish anarchist émigré circles; shares lodging and bookbinding tools with **Solomon Rappaport**, later **S. Ansky**. **[A]** **1895**: the **German consulate** tells him return means prison. **[R, no document]** Folded into the London stop.
- **1895, London**: librarian of the **Communist Workers' Educational Union**; meets **Louise Michel** and **Errico Malatesta**; moves east, joins the **Arbeter Fraint** group as a gentile and begins learning Yiddish. **[A]**
- **Carburton Street**, first lodging, "near the Werners." **[A, memoir verbatim]** **[C]** Two lens errors: 51.5219, -0.1447 is about 350 m north of the street, which runs at roughly 51.5197, -0.1428; and the forename **Wilhelm** Werner is asserted but is not in the memoir. The forename is not carried forward.
- **Meeting with Milly Witkop.** **[C, contradiction flagged and stated in the stop's `date_confidence`]** The chronology lens says **1897**. Witkop's own entry says **1895** (she reached London 1894, he 1895), and 1897 is impossible against the same lens's next item, their joint emigration attempt of 29 May 1897. Dated **1895**.
- **29 May 1897, New York**: asked whether they are married, they answer that they are not and will not be, and are **denied entry and deported**. **[A]** **[C]** The geography lens staged this at **Southampton** as port of denied departure, tagged **[A]**. Wrong twice: the refusal happened at **New York**, and no reachable source, memoir ch. 6 included, names the outbound port at all. Stop moved to New York; the port stated as unknown. **[G]**

## 3. The Yiddish press, 1898-1912

- **1898, Liverpool**: edits **Dos Fraye Vort**, eight issues. **[A]**
- **October 1898**: takes the **Arbeter Fraint** editorship. **[C]** The geography lens gives **1899-1914**; both ends are wrong. He took it **October 1898**; the paper was suppressed **1915** when police closed press and offices, though he was interned from 2 December 1914. The range also elides the **1900-1902 lapse**.
- **March 1900 to March 1903**: founds and edits **Germinal**; its claimed circulation of 2,500 **could not be corroborated** and is omitted **[G]**. **1901-1902**: a year among the **Leeds** Jewish anarchists, folded into the 1898 stop. **26 December 1902** relaunch conference; **first new issue 20 March 1903**. **[A]** **[C]** The **5,000 weekly** circulation is confirmed but belongs to **early 1905**, its peak, not to 1906 where the chronology lens put it.
- **April 1903, Great Assembly Hall, Mile End Road**: speaks after the **Kishinev pogrom** to the largest Jewish gathering London had seen; then Leeds, Glasgow, Edinburgh. **[A, memoir ch. 15 and the entry agree]** **[C]** Coordinate corrected to **51.5218, -0.0490** (181-183 Mile End Road); the lens's pin sat 500 m west-southwest.
- **3 February 1906**: the **Workers' Friend Club** opens at **165 Jubilee Street**, a former Methodist chapel and Salvation Army depot; closes **1910**. **[A]** **[C]** The chronology lens placed it in **Whitechapel** at 51.5153, -0.0685, about 1.3 km west of the real Stepney site.
- **8 June to 1 July 1906**: garment strike, Rocker on the committee; funds exhausted, workers return with nothing won. **1907**: son **Fermin** born, folded into the Jubilee Street campa. **[A]**
- **24-31 August 1907, Amsterdam**. **[A]** **[C, the same overstatement in both lenses]** Both say he "becomes secretary of the Anarchist International." The congress elected a **five-member International Anarchist Bureau**: **Malatesta, Rocker, Schapiro, John Turner, Jean Wilquet**. He was one of five; Schapiro is conventionally named as secretary.
- **13 October 1909**: **Ferrer** shot at **Montjuïc**; Rocker, in France, denounces it and is **deported**. **[A]** **[C, stop-ownership decision]** The geography lens staged this at Barcelona, at a coordinate 5.3 km from the execution site anyway. **Rocker was never at Montjuïc**: the execution is Ferrer's event. The stop sits in **Paris**, with Montjuïc in `suggested_refs`.
- **May 1912**: general tailors' strike, roughly **13,000** out; he chairs finance, publishes daily, speaks at the mass meeting of the **24th**; all demands met by the **25th**. **[A]**

## 4. War and internment, 1914-1918

- **7 August 1914**: writes that Europe faces mass murder on an unprecedented scale, attacks the Second International's parties, takes neither side. **2 December 1914**: arrested as an enemy alien, interned at **Olympia**, Kensington. **1915**: the *Arbeter Fraint* is suppressed. **[A; Olympia from memoir ch. 27]**
- **c.1915, the *Royal Edward***. **[R as to date: memoir ch. 28 gives none]** **[C]** The geography lens said "Southampton or Tilbury," 50.8981, -1.3958. The ship lay **off Southend-on-Sea**, roughly 51.52, 0.75, an error of about **190 km**. It was **sunk 13 August 1915**, which caps his time aboard.
- **1915 to March 1918, Alexandra Palace**; elected prisoners' spokesman. **[A]** **[C]** The lens gives 1915-1919; he was exchanged out **March 1918**. His own "four years" rounds December 1914 to March 1918 and is reported as his own account.
- **March 1918**: Red Cross exchange to the **Netherlands**, sheltered by **Ferdinand Domela Nieuwenhuis**, reunited with Witkop and Fermin. **[R, reported without document; the house is not located, so the pin is a general Netherlands coordinate]** **[G]**

## 5. Berlin, 1918-1933

- **November 1918**: returns at **Fritz Kater's** invitation to rebuild the **FVdG**. **March 1919, Erfurt**: addresses a munitions workers' congress (cut for stop count; pin 50.9787, 11.0328). **27-30 December 1919**: the FVdG refounds itself as the **FAUD**, adopting Rocker's **Declaration of Syndicalist Principles**. **1920**: imprisoned under **Noske's** crackdown, converting fellow prisoner Kater inside; folded into the FAUD stop. **1924**: *Johann Most: The Life of a Rebel*, cut for stop count. **[A]**
- **1919**: writes **'Das Ende Gustav Landauers'** for *Gustav Landauer: Worte der Würdigung*, carrying the eyewitness report of Landauer's last words. **[A]**
- **December 1922**: founding of the **IWA-AIT**; Rocker its secretary with **Souchy** and **Schapiro**, and author of its platform. **[A]** Note the asymmetry with 1907: he really was secretary here and really was not in Amsterdam. The lenses conflated the two offices.
- **Makhno in Berlin.** **[C, the hardest error in the pool, present in both lenses]** Chronology dates it **1923** hedged **[R]**; geography dates it **1923** tagged **[A]**, so that tag is wrong twice over. Makhno was interned at Strzałkowo from April 1922, tried in Warsaw **November 1923**, moved to Toruń that December, released to Danzig only **July 1924**, and left for Paris **April 1925**. The meeting, Volin interpreting and Ugo Fedeli present, falls **c. late 1924 to April 1925**. Dated 1924-12-01, the earliest possible, tagged reconstruction.
- **1925**: North American tour, **162 appearances**; begins *Nationalism and Culture*. **[A]** No single coordinate is possible; the file pins arrival at New York and says so.
- **1928-1929**: with **Souchy**, shelters **Durruti** in Berlin after his expulsion from France, running the IWA secretariat until the Centre Party government expels Rocker in **1929**. **[A, `buenaventura_durruti.journey.json` names both hosts]** **1931**: IWA congress at **Madrid**, folded into that campa; Domela Nieuwenhuis memorial at **Amsterdam**, cut. **[A both]**

## 6. Flight and America, 1933-1958

- **27 February 1933**: after the **Reichstag fire** and word of **Mühsam's** arrest, he and Witkop decide to go. **8 March**: cross at **Basel** on the last unsealed train. **[A both]**
- **March 1933, Saint-Tropez**: joins **Emma Goldman**; writes *Der Weg ins Dritte Reich*, published only in Spanish. **[A]** No `emma_goldman` file exists in the corpus, so this is a fact, not an interlock.
- **May 1933**: back in London, the East End turns out. **July 1933, Paris**: extraordinary IWA meeting resolves to smuggle *Die Internationale* into Germany (cut for stop count). **26 August 1933**: lands at **New York** and is admitted, Fermin waiting. **1933-1937**: **Towanda, Pennsylvania**, with Milly's sister. **[A]**
- **July 1934**: **Mühsam murdered at Oranienburg**. Rocker breaks off the autobiography begun that summer for *The Life and Suffering of Erich Mühsam*; in 1949, *Der Leidensweg von Zensl Mühsam*, from Zensl's testimony of Sonnenburg, Brandenburg and Oranienburg. **[A, cited across `erich_muhsam.journey.json`]**
- **July 1936**: *The Truth About Spain*; writes for *The Spanish Revolution*. **[C, location contradiction, not resolved]** Chronology places this at **New York**, geography has the family at **Towanda through 1937**. Rather than pin a disputed place, the fact is folded into the 1938 Mohegan stop.
- **September 1937**: settles at **Mohegan Colony**. **[A]** **[C]** Three lenses carried **three different Mohegan pins**: 41.3223/-73.8318 and 41.2634/-73.8368 are off by 2 km and 5.5 km from the entry's **41.309, -73.851**. Only the geography lens is right; the file uses 41.3089, -73.8511 throughout, death included.
- **1937**: *Nationalism and Culture*. **[A]** **[C]** Chronology adds "a Spanish edition released the same year in Barcelona." The source says only **three volumes in Barcelona, no year**. "The same year" is dropped and the missing year stated.
- **1938**: *Anarcho-Syndicalism*, first published in Britain; Bombay/Indore 1947, Pluto 1989, German 2021. **1939**: an operation ends his lecturing, and the **Rocker Publications Committee** forms in **Los Angeles** (both cut; LA pin 34.0522, -118.2437). **February 1946**: parcel operation from Mohegan for surviving German anarchists after an appeal in the *Freie Arbeiter Stimme*. **1949**: *Pioneers of American Freedom*. **[A]**
- **1953**: eightieth-birthday dinner **in London**, tributes from **Mann, Einstein, Herbert Read, Russell**. **[A]** **Stop-ownership decision:** nothing says Rocker attended, and he gave up travelling in 1939 after an operation. The stop sits **at Mohegan** and says in campa and `date_confidence` that the dinner was in London and no account places him there. **[G]**
- **19 September 1958**: dies at **Mohegan Colony near Crompond**, aged **85**. **[A, both entries agree]** **Burial unresolved [G]:** no cemetery, marker, or disposition of the remains confirmed, and Find a Grave blocks automated access; the file says so rather than inventing a grave. **Milly Witkop** predeceased him and her memorial essay is dated **June 1956**, but **no death date for her was established [G]**, so the file says only "dies before him."

## 7. Afterlife

- **Rudolf Rocker Papers**, IISH **Amsterdam**, with a **microfilm copy** at the Deutsche Nationalbibliothek, **Leipzig**. **[A]** **[C]** Both coordinates corrected: the IISH has been at **Cruquiusweg 31** since 1989, roughly **52.3690, 4.9394** (the lens's pin lands 2.2 km away in the Rivierenbuurt); Leipzig is **51.32228, 12.39663** (the lens's was 1.2 km northwest). The **deposit date is not attested [G]**; the stop is dated 1989, the year of the move.
- **June 2024**: **Rudolf-Rocker-Institut** founded in Mainz. **July 2024**: **Stolpersteine** for Rocker and Witkop at **Buschkrugallee 246, Berlin-Britz**. **[A on the German entry alone in both cases; the institute's own site was unreachable, and the Britz address as a Rocker residence is uncorroborated elsewhere]** **[G]**
- **Rudolf Rocker Cultural Centre, Winnipeg.** **[C, downgraded and dropped]** The lens says it "operates." The only citation is a **Wayback capture of 23 August 2007**, so the live site was already gone; a defunct centre with unknown dates cannot be dated as a stop.
- Memoirs into **Spanish** 1947/1949/1952, **Yiddish** 1952/1963/1965, **English** as *The London Years* 1956 and 2005, **German** 1974, 2023, 2025. **[A]** The Kate Sharpley Library's judgement that Rocker drew more affection than any veteran since Kropotkin or Malatesta is an **assessment, not an event**, and is nowhere in the file.

## 8. Quotations

Twelve of forty-five stops carry a quote. Four of fifteen candidates needed correction; one needed reattribution.

- **[C]** *Anarcho-Syndicalism* on concrete freedom dropped "**the powers,**" and the closing clause "**and turn them to social account.**" Corrected here; **not used**.
- **[C]** "I am an anarchist not because I believe anarchism is the final goal..." dropped a second "**I believe**". Used corrected, with its context: Rocker replying to the Yiddish writer **Baruch Rifkin** (memoir ch. 20).
- **[C]** "For twenty years I gave the best years of my life..." misquotes the Foreword, which reads "**For two whole decades**", conflating it with the next sentence, which does begin "For twenty years I lived with them." Used corrected.
- **[C]** "It was clear to me that socialism was not a simple question of a full belly..." is verbatim but **wrongly attributed**: Rocker quoted by **Colin Ward in his Introduction**, not Rocker's own narrative. Cited that way.
- **[R, not used]** "This mildest of anarchists was indeed a man without a country" is **Ward describing Rocker**; a critic's framing line in a `quote` field would read as the subject's own voice. Also unused **[G]**: "No tyranny is more unendurable than that of an all-powerful bureaucracy," which arrived flagged as a summarised excerpt of unconfirmed wording.

## 10. Apparatus relations for the operator

Rocker is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing was silently deleted; here is what was caught, and which direction it runs.

1. **Chomsky: forward.** Chomsky reading Rocker at the newsstand in 1941 and naming him in a 1976 London interview are **Chomsky's stops on Chomsky's file**. Rocker's file carries only the **1970 introduction to Rocker's own book**, in `sources` and `suggested_refs` on the *Anarcho-Syndicalism* stop plus one closing sentence of that campa: a fact about the object Rocker made, not a scene of Chomsky travelling. Reception, not framework-applied-forward, but still **forward**, so it is flagged and that sentence can be struck if the block rule is read strictly.
2. **Bakunin's forecast and the Spanish collectives: neither direction.** No grant needed. Rocker wrote about the 1936-37 collectives himself, in his own lifetime, while they were happening.
3. **Pouget: backward, not taken.** Rocker's synthesis absorbs the direct-action and general-strike doctrine Pouget built in the CGT. That is influence, writable the day anyone finds Rocker citing Pouget by name. Nobody has. Recorded as the one relation that is probably real and simply unevidenced. **[G]**

No stop stages anyone but Rocker travelling. The two places where the pool tried to, Ferrer's execution and the 1953 London dinner, are moved to Rocker's own position (Paris; Mohegan), the other event named in `suggested_refs`.
