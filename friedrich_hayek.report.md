*Compiled 2026-08-25 for `friedrich_hayek.journey.json`: 45 stops, 7 segments, gregorian.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a
claim carried at second hand and not re-verified this pass. Contradictions are flagged and
**not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. The institutional
skeleton (degrees, chairs, prizes) is fixed by two archival finding aids fetched directly;

## 1. Vienna, 1899-1921

**[A - OAC finding aid, Hayek Papers, Hoover Institution, fetched directly]** Born 8 May
1899, Vienna. **[A - en.wikipedia.org, 'Friedrich Hayek']** Family of physicians, botanists
and statisticians; father August a municipal physician lecturing in botany, maternal
grandfather Franz von Juraschek an economist.

**[R]** Army from March 1917, artillery officer-cadet on the Italian front, malaria-like
illness, lasting deafness in the left ear. Tagged [R] because the same accounts carry a
decoration for bravery that is **not securely documented**, which the file does not assert.

**[A - OAC finding aid]** University of Vienna from 1918; Dr. jur. 1921; Dr. rer. pol.
1923; formation under Friedrich von Wieser. **CONTRADICTION, unresolved:** the Dr. rer. pol.
is dated 1923, but Hayek was resident in New York from March 1923 **[R]**. Nothing consulted
reconciles them. The Zurich laboratory (see corrections) is the origin of *The Sensory
Order* (1952); without that fix the atlas would show a Vienna stop for a Swiss episode.

## 2. Vienna, 1921-1931

**[R]** The **Abrechnungsamt**, the Treaty of Saint-Germain clearing office, c. 1921-1926,
hired on Wieser's recommendation by **Ludwig von Mises**. Not re-verified against a personnel
file; no street address located.

**[A - `ludwig_von_mises.journey.json`]** Mises's **Privatseminar**, every other Friday in
his Chamber of Commerce office, then an Italian restaurant and the Cafe Kunstler; Hayek
named among the regulars with Machlup, Morgenstern, Schutz, Voegelin. **Pin inherited
byte-identical: 48.2058, 16.3707.** That file notes the 1923 date is an ordering device, not
a documented first meeting; the caveat is carried here. **[A - en.wikipedia.org]** Founded
the **Geistkreis** with Herbert Furth, the same men plus Haberler, dated only "early 1920s".

**[R]** New York, March 1923 to June 1924, on Rockefeller-supported funds, assistant to
**Jeremiah W. Jenks** at NYU, classes at NYU and Columbia, hearing Wesley Clair Mitchell.
The Rockefeller Archive Center page was named in the dossier, **not refetched**.

**CONTRADICTION, unresolved.** The **Osterreichisches Institut fur Konjunkturforschung** is
founded "end of 1926" in some sources and 1927 in others. `ludwig_von_mises.journey.json`
records the same conflict and leaves it open; so does this file. **Pin inherited
byte-identical: 48.2, 16.3689.** **[A - WIFO history, URL named in dossier, not refetched]**
First director from 1927, at 28; succeeded by Oskar Morgenstern. The reported $4,000
Rockefeller subsidy is second-hand and is not repeated in the journey file.
**[A - OAC finding aid]** Habilitation and Privatdozent, 1929. **[R]** *Geldtheorie und
Konjunkturtheorie* 1929, not refetched.

## 3. London, 1931-1938

**[R]** Four LSE lectures at **Lionel Robbins's** invitation, early 1931; *Prices and
Production* published from them that year. **[A - OAC finding aid]** Visiting professor,
University of London, 1931; **Tooke Professor** 1932-1950; D.Sc. (Economics) 1940, attested
but given no stop, a paper award with no scene attached. **[R]** Keynes's "frightful muddle"
remark was **not** re-verified against a primary text; it appears inside a campa as reported
speech, never as a `quote` field.

**[A - `carl_menger.journey.json`]** Hayek edited Menger's four-volume *Gesammelte Werke*,
printed 1933-1936, restoring the unrevised 1871 *Grundsatze*. **That atlas file itself flags
the detail as reconstruction-level** after a Mises Institute page 404'd; same treatment
here. **Pin decision, declared:** the stop sits at that file's canonical Vienna pin (48.2082,
16.3738), but Hayek held the Tooke chair in London throughout and edited from there; moving
it to the LSE pin is a one-field change. **[R]** *Collectivist Economic Planning* 1935;
*Economics and Knowledge* 1937.

**[A - `ludwig_von_mises.journey.json`]** Present at the **Colloque Walter Lippmann**,
Paris, August 1938, with Mises, Ropke, Rustow, Rougier, Lippmann; "neoliberalism" coined
there. **Pin inherited byte-identical: 48.8566, 2.3522.**

## 4. War and *The Road to Serfdom*, 1939-1945

**[A - en.wikipedia.org, 'London School of Economics']** LSE evacuated to Cambridge, housed
in Peterhouse buildings; Hayek took rooms there. **[R]** The fire-watching nights with Keynes
and his help with the son's schooling are repeated recollections, not documented here, and
**[gap]** Hayek's Cambridge address was not located.

**[A - press.uchicago.edu/Misc/Chicago/320553.html, fetched directly]** Routledge published
in Britain **10 March 1944**; Chicago accepted the manuscript **28 December 1943** and
published **18 September 1944**, after **three** American rejections, on **Aaron Director's**
advocacy, resetting the text completely because the British "this country" meant England.
Book-of-the-Month Club: **over 1,000,000 reprints**. **[R]** *The Use of Knowledge in
Society*, AER 35:4 (1945); its opening sentence **was** confirmed by direct fetch of the
Econlib full text.

## 5. Mont Pelerin, Arkansas, Chicago, 1947-1962

**[A - en.wikipedia.org, 'Mont Pelerin Society'; `ludwig_von_mises.journey.json`]** Founding
meeting **10 April 1947**, Hotel du Parc above Vevey; **39** invited, including Mises,
Popper, Friedman, Stigler, Ropke; Hayek convened it and was founding president.
**CONTRADICTION, unresolved:** presidency given as **1947-1961** (English Wikipedia) and
**1947-1962** (Hoover/OAC). **[A - OAC finding aid]** 1950 visiting professorship,
University of Arkansas; 1950-1962 Committee on Social Thought, Chicago.

**[R]** The Arkansas post established residency for a divorce Hella would not grant and
English law would not give; decree final July 1950; married **Helene Bitterlich Warhanek**
within weeks. Its conduct (concealment, financial leverage, a graphological report on the
spouses) is in Caldwell and Klausinger and specialist scholarship **not refetched here**.
**[R]** The Chicago economics department declined to appoint him; the Volker Fund financed
the Committee salary for ten years, per Duke's Volker finding aid **as cited in the
dossier**, not refetched. **[R]** *The Sensory Order* (1952), first conceived in the 1920s.
**[A - hoover.org]** *The Constitution of Liberty* (1960). **[A - en.wikipedia.org]** The
IEA founded **1955** in Westminster by Antony Fisher with **Oliver Smedley**, after Hayek
told him to build a research institution rather than enter politics; and recurring summers
at **Obergurgl**, Tyrol, 1950s-1970s, no single visit dated, so declared as recurring.

**[A - OAC finding aid; de.wikipedia.org]** Professor of Economic Policy, Freiburg,
1962-1968.

**[R]** 8 July 1962: sent Salazar a copy of *The Constitution of Liberty* with a note

## 7. Chile, and the last Freiburg, 1977-1992

**[A - en.wikipedia.org]** Visited Chile 1977, met General Augusto Pinochet, accepted an
honorary link with the Centro de Estudios Publicos; returned 1981 for the *El Mercurio*
interview. **[R]** The character of the 1977 meeting (about twenty minutes, no interpreter,
little said) comes from Caldwell and Montes; **SSRN returned 403**, as did the interview
transcript, whose substance is corroborated through translated extracts. **Pin inherited
byte-identical from `augusto_pinochet.journey.json`: -33.437, -70.65**, a file that
**contains no Hayek mention at all**; the relation stays one-directional, correctly, since
the traveller here is Hayek. Per the house rule on hard facts, the file states flat that by
1977 the regime had killed, disappeared and tortured thousands of its own citizens.

**[A - hoover.org]** Honorary/senior fellow, Hoover Institution, from 1977; the Hoover
Archives hold the papers, parts still restricted. **[A - en.wikipedia.org]** *Remembering My
Cousin Ludwig Wittgenstein* (1977); second cousins via the Juraschek line, though **[R]** the
wartime train meeting is Hayek's own recollection, documented nowhere else. **[R]** Companion
of Honour, 1984, the "call me Frederick" anecdote reported rather than documented; cut for
length, not evidence.

**[A - hoover.org]** Presidential Medal of Freedom, 1991, from George H. W. Bush. **[R]**
Too frail to travel. Because he was never at the Washington ceremony, the stop sits at his
**Freiburg** pin and says so.

**[A]** Died at home, Freiburg im Breisgau, **23 March 1992**, aged 92 (English Wikipedia,
Hoover page, OAC finding aid). **[R]** Cause: contemporary press cites a heart ailment; the
**Washington Post obituary returned HTTP 403**, and **no certified cause is in the reachable
record**. **[A - de.wikipedia.org]** Buried **4 April 1992**, Catholic rite, **Gruppe 1,
Reihe 17, Nummer 11**, among the cemetery's 49 *Ehrengraber*. **[R]** The
deathbed-conversion story has nothing behind it, and the file says so.

## Quotations

The verified pool is small and three items were **correctly worded, wrongly sourced**. The
file carries 10 `quote` fields across 45 stops, deliberately below the usual proportion.

**[A], confirmed by direct fetch.** The AER 1945 opening sentence (Econlib); "To act on the
belief that we possess the knowledge and the power..." from *The Pretence of Knowledge*
(mises.org); two UCLA transcript lines, on Mises and on the fireman's horse.

**Corrected before use.**

- "Well these are the things I want to study". The UCLA transcript reads `When I heard this
  [my response was], "Well these are the things I want to study."` No comma after "Well";
  the bracket is editorial, as `quote_source` explains.
- "To the socialists of all parties" is the printed dedication; the pool's "dedicated to
  socialists of all parties" is a paraphrase. **Caveat: no page image inspected, so
  small-caps typography is unverified. The wording is not.**
- "the inherent logic of collectivism..." and "I think what is needed is a clear set of
  principles..." **Neither is a line from *The Road to Serfdom*.** The Chicago page is an
  excerpt from **Bruce Caldwell's introduction** to the 2007 Definitive Edition, which
  sources the first to Hayek's **spring 1933 'Nazi-Socialism' memorandum** and the second to
  his **talks before business groups on the April 1945 US tour**. The pool also cut the
  second short; the full sentence pair is restored.
- "The curious task of economics..." Traced to *The Fatal Conceit* (1988), **ch. 5, p. 76**
  (Econlib; Scheall's Routledge monograph subtitle), **confirmed in secondary scholarship,
  not in a page image**. The pool's claim that it is an epigraph to some editions of *The
  Road to Serfdom* is **unsupported and deleted**: those epigraphs are Hume, Tocqueville and
  Holderlin.

**[R], primary transcript unreachable.** Both Nobel banquet lines, **nobelprize.org having
returned 403 to every attempt**; the file uses "I must confess that if I had been
consulted..." as a `quote` with that caveat in `quote_source` and carries the other only as
reported speech.

## Honest gaps

No birth address; no 1917-18 battery or sector; no street address for the Abrechnungsamt,
the Vienna institute, or Routledge's 1944 offices, so those four pins are approximations and
say so. Unreconciled: the 1923 doctorate against the March 1923 New York departure; the
institute founding, 1926 or 1927; the Mont Pelerin presidency, to 1961 or 1962; the Rikkyo
doctorate, 1963 or 1964. No certified cause of death. And **after 1950 there is no settled
scholarly biography**, Caldwell and Klausinger's volume 2 being unpublished as of the
dossier's preparation and that status **not re-confirmed** this pass, so the Salzburg
depression, the Chilean meetings and the divorce all rest on secondary accounts, tagged [R].
