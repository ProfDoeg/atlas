# Arthur Ben: research report

*Research pass 2026-08-10, v3. Supersedes the v2 report in full. This pass keeps v2's spine and corrects nine things inside it, two of which the research pool tagged **[A]** and got wrong.*

**Naming convention.** The atlas carries him as **Arthur Ben**, the name the corpus uses. His legal name stands in the obituary cited throughout and every URL is given in full, so any claim here can be checked; the journey's authored prose does not reproduce the legal name.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, tradition, or family testimony. Corpus-internal material (the locked essay `200_arthur_ben.md`, the queue) is **not publicly citable** and is marked wherever used.

- Born **28 August 1947**, **Sewanee, Tennessee**, first of four children **[A, obituary]**.
- **Sewanee Public School**, then **Indian Springs School** near Birmingham **[A, obituary]**.
- **Skips his senior year of high school**; **enters the University of the South in 1964** **[A, obituary]**.

The journey keeps the schools attested and puts the placement entirely inside `date_confidence`, where the hedge belongs.

**Dropped from v2.** The Eagle Scout claim, which stood in v2 as **[A]** but appears nowhere in this pass's pool. Flagged rather than silently deleted.

## 2. Selma, March 1965

The obituary attests that he took part in the march from **Selma to Montgomery** led by **Dr. Martin Luther King**, shortly before he shipped out, and against his father's objection **[A]**.

**Coordinate corrected, three-way disagreement flagged.** The pool carried **three points** for one bridge: geography lens **32.4066 / -87.0201**, about 250 m northwest on the town-side approach; afterlife lens **32.4074 / -87.0211**, further northwest again; Nominatim **32.4047 / -87.0182** (Water Avenue Historic District, Selma, Dallas County AL). The journey uses Nominatim, and the other two are recorded so the disagreement stays visible rather than quietly averaged.

**Gap.** No day within the month, and no statement of which of the three attempted marches he joined. The completed march ran **21-25 March 1965**. His presence at the Montgomery endpoint is **[R]**, inferred from the fact of his marching. **No other traveler file carries a Selma pin.**

## 3. The Navy, 1965-1969

### The dating contradiction of v2 is now closed

v2 flagged a live contradiction and refused to resolve it: the pool asserted a **1965-1969** span that appeared in no source, and the ship's four WestPac deployments (**4 Jun-18 Dec 1965**, **15 Oct 1966-28 Mar 1967**, **Jun-Dec 1968**, **Jan-Aug 1970**) admitted two readings.

**His own words settle it [A].** In a message dated 26 January 2024, published in *Memories of Ben Chitty*, he gives his service as **"USN 1965-9 VN 1966-7, 1968"**. Navy 1965 to 1969; Vietnam 1966-67 and 1968, matching the ship's second and third deployments exactly. The 1970 reading is dead. The reconstruction v2 offered was right, and v3 states it as documented rather than probable.

**Enlistment wording corrected.** The chronology lens said "drafted into the US Navy". The obituary reads **"drafted and enlisted in the US Navy"**: a draft notice, then an enlistment, a different act. Corrected in the journey prose.

### Two corrections to attested-tagged material

1. **Search and Rescue and the eight aviators belong to 1965, not to his tour.** The geography lens attached the SAR Destroyer Unit assignment and the eight-aviator rescue credit to the **15 Oct 1966-28 Mar 1967** deployment and tagged the stop **[A]**. The ship history attaches both to the **first** cruise: "reassigned to the Search and Rescue Destroyer Unit in the Tonkin Gulf in September 1965. After participating in missions in which eight aviators were rescued..." The 1966-67 cruise is described only as "bound a second time for Southeast Asian waters." **The rescue credit is removed from his Yankee Station stop.**
2. **The Pearl Harbor call is on the wrong leg.** The pool put it on the **outbound** October 1966 transit as a refuelling stop. The source reads: "returning to her homeport on 28 March 1967 and making a midshipman training cruise to Pearl Harbor." **The call follows the homecoming**, and an outbound 1966 stop is supported by no source. The journey re-dates it to mid-1967 as the midshipman cruise. The two lenses also disagree on the coordinate, **21.3649 / -157.9506** against **21.3469 / -157.9769**; the journey uses the first.

**Three tours, or two.** The afterlife lens called the 1965 cruise "his first Vietnam-adjacent deployment" and numbered the later two his second and third, producing **three tours**. The obituary says **two**, and the 1965 cruise sailed 4 June, when a man drafted that year would still be in recruit training. **The journey carries two tours and pins no 1965 stop for him.**

### Three smaller points

- **Hull designation.** She was **DLG-20**, a guided missile frigate and destroyer leader, throughout his service; redesignation to **CG-20**, cruiser, came in **1975** (quotes lens says July, chronology lens June, a discrepancy immaterial here). The journey does not call her a cruiser in his lifetime.
- **Yankee Station's position moved 13 April 1966**, from 16°00'N 110°00'E to roughly **17°30'N 108°30'E**. Both his tours postdate the move, so the journey uses **17.5 / 108.5**.
- **Reconstructed stops.** **Great Lakes**, **Subic Bay** and the waters **off Da Nang** are **[R]**, from the 1965 enlisted pipeline and the Seventh Fleet logistics pattern. **No document places him at any of them.**

**Role contradiction, still open.** The obituary makes him an **electronics technician**; the locked essay calls him a **radio communications technician**. Related ratings, not identical descriptions. Neither is corrected against the other; the journey uses the obituary's wording and this report records the other.

## 5. Libraries and unions, 1980-2023

- **Hamden Public Library**, Connecticut, computer systems librarian, **1980** **[A]**. Miller Memorial, 2901 Dixwell Avenue: **41.3831 / -72.9045**.
- **Queens College, CUNY, 1984**, assistant professor, later teaching in the Graduate School of Library and Information Studies; **Benjamin S. Rosenthal Library** opens **1988** and he becomes its computer systems officer until retirement in **2023**, thirty-nine years in all **[A]**.
- Married **Priscilla Murolo**, labor historian **[A for the marriage; no date or place in any source]**. The journey's 1985 is a placement, stated as such.
- **Queens College Unions Joint Committee on Quality of Work Life**, founded **2006**, convening monthly **[A]**.

### The book: subtitle corrected

The pool and the v2 report gave the subtitle as *An Illustrated History of Labor in the United States*. That is the obituary's loose rendering, not the book. Both Open Library editions give **From the Folks Who Brought You the Weekend: A Short, Illustrated History of Labor in the United States**, New Press, **3 September 2001** (OL8666602M), revised **28 August 2018** (OL27352297M). Sarah Lawrence's faculty page for Murolo uses the same subtitle. **Corrected in the journey.**

### The award: giver corrected

The afterlife lens said the **PSC** gave him the Unsung Hero award, "presented by" NYSUT. That inverts it. The obituary says **NY State United Teachers**, the PSC's parent union, gave the award in **March 2011**; the PSC is his own chapter, where the organizing happened. The chronology lens has it right and the journey follows it. Venue is **[R]**, the coordinate inside the campus footprint.

### Residence

The obituary says only that he attended **Christ Church Bronxville** and nothing about where he and Murolo lived. **Residence in or near Bronxville is [R]**, inferred from the parish. Christ Church: **40.9429 / -73.8339** (OSM way 1291826578).

## 6. Vietnam Veterans Against the War

- **[A, obituary]**: a leader in the anti-war movement, member of both **VVAW** and **Veterans for Peace**, numerous articles in **The Veteran** over decades; very near the end of his life he signed up for a **lifetime membership** in VVAW.
- His chapter is the **Clarence Fitch chapter** of New York **[A, `vvaw.org/veteran/article/?id=4300`]**, named for the Black Marine combat veteran who was VVAW's East Coast coordinator until his death in 1990.

**Reconstructed stops.** The **Vietnam Veterans Memorial** and **Union Square** are **[R]**, standing for a documented pattern with no documented venue or date. No meeting hall, no single action, and no article of his by title was retrieved in any pass.

### Panama, 1989: the delegation is attested, his presence on it is not

A pre-invasion VVAW presence in Panama is **[A]**: **Pete Zastrow** of the national office writes of being in Panama at the end of November 1989 (*The Veteran*, Summer 1990, `vvaw.org/content/?id=2961`), and **Lamoin Werlein-Jaen** thanks VVAW, which "was at our side before the invasion" (`vvaw.org/veteran/article/?id=2960`). **His own participation is [R]**, on the author's family testimony; no published source names him there.

**Clarence Fitch stays out of this episode.** v2 established that no source links Fitch to Panama; his documented Central America mission is **Nicaragua, 1986**. The verified pool's interlock lens still names Fitch as his companion on the trip, on family testimony. The journey does not repeat that pairing, and the discrepancy is flagged rather than resolved.

**The invasion date, corrected.** The pool said the invasion "removes Manuel Noriega" on **20 December 1989**. The date is right, but **Noriega was not removed then**: he took refuge in the **Apostolic Nunciature of the Holy See** and did not surrender until **3 January 1990**. Chronology and geography lenses both carried the error; both are corrected. `manuel_noriega` sits in QUEUE.md with no journey file, so **Panama City, 20 December 1989** is a shared pin waiting to happen.

## 7. Hospice and death, January-February 2024

**[A, obituary]**: dies **11 February 2024** at **Calvary Hospital**, the Bronx, aged seventy-six; survived by his wife, two stepsons and siblings; parents and a younger brother predeceased him; **ashes to be interred at Sewanee** with theirs.

**Coordinate corrected for a third time.** Calvary Hospital, 1740 Eastchester Road, the Bronx: **40.8480 / -73.8438** (Nominatim; OSM way 527873169). The pool's afterlife lens gave **40.8468 / -73.8551** in three separate entries, about 1 km west-southwest and off the campus; the chronology and geography lenses both had it right. **The journey uses 40.848 / -73.8438 throughout.**

**Corpus-internal [R], family memory, not publicly citable**, from `200_arthur_ben.md`: the bedsheets folded in a fixed vertical-then-horizontal sequence, read by the family as the control panel of a **memory theatre**; the hand-stitched **funerary apron** with two pockets for his parents' ashes, tied with a **knot in the likeness of the Hebrew letter Shin**; **Hebrew and Arabic writing** emerging within his pancreas and liver to form a **phylactery**; **"I need help with my strings"** and the knots his stepson works loose between the fifth and sixth thoracic vertebrae; the **second life** spoken for him aloud in Buenos Aires; travels through **Bab al-Mandab** and the **Strait of Malacca**, the tribes of Yemen and of Gaul, the bloodlines of Abraham.

**The straits: delirium, and the atlas says so.** The author has confirmed directly that this was **deathbed delirium**. It needs no reconciliation and cannot be reconciled: USS Richmond K. Turner was a **Pacific Fleet** ship homeported at San Diego throughout his service, and her only documented **Suez transit was April 1991**, twenty years after his discharge. The journey pins both straits inside the hospice segment, names them as delirium in `date_confidence`, and offers no route, no ship, and no unresolved-puzzle framing.

## 10. Honest gaps

- **No service record.** No enlistment date, rating date, training station, discharge date or type. His own line gives the years; an NPRC request would give the rest.
- **No marriage date or place.** **No confirmation of a degree from the University of the South.** **No dissertation subject** for the UNC ABD, and no field named at Swarthmore or Keble.
- **No named venue or date for any single VVAW or Veterans for Peace action**, and not one of his articles in *The Veteran* retrieved by title in three passes.
- **No independent corroboration of his presence in Panama.** The mission is attested; the man is not attested on it.
- **No port-by-port itinerary** for either deployment; the six months of the 1968 cruise are dark between departure and return.
- **No explanation for Bab al-Mandab or Malacca**, and none is being sought: the author has named the material as delirium.
- **Names of his parents, siblings and stepsons** stand in the obituary and are not reproduced in the journey prose.
