# Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier (1861-1930): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-21. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim not corroborated. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.*

Forestier is a French forestry engineer turned municipal conservator turned urbanist, employed by the City of Paris for roughly forty years without ever heading the service, known chiefly for gardens and open-space plans made outside France. The evidentiary shape is worth naming: the Paris career is thinly documented, the foreign commissions richly so, and much of the popular literature consists of simplifications scholarship has already corrected but that keep circulating. Several of those corrections are recorded below.

---

## 1. Savoie, the Polytechnique, the forest school (1861-1885)

- Born **9 January 1861 at Aix-les-Bains, Savoie** **[A: en.wikipedia; Cité de l'architecture authority biography]**. Savoie had been annexed to France in 1860.
- **École polytechnique, Paris, 1880**, to about 1882 **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia gives 1880/1882]**.
- **École libre des sciences politiques** courses afterwards **[A: en.wikipedia and Cité de l'architecture both list it]**.
- **Ministry of Finance attachment [R], demoted from [A] and undated.** The Cité gives the formation as "l'École polytechnique... puis l'École libre des sciences politiques... et l'École forestière de Nancy jusqu'en 1885", with no Finance posting. The claim rests solely on the dossier's account of Guérin. Do not date it 1882.
- **École forestière de Nancy**, to 1885 **[A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia, Cité]**. Coordinate corrected: the school (today AgroParisTech, rue Lyautey) is at **48.6934, 6.1884**; the pool's 48.6874, 6.1878 falls about 670 m south.

## 2. The three forestry postings (1885-1887)

es.wikipedia, verbatim: "1885-1887: comienzo de carrera como guarda general en los servicios de Aguas y Bosques de Argelès, Annecy y Sallanches." **Three** postings, not the two the pool carried. **[A for the list; R for station-level detail, from the dossier's Guérin reconstruction]**.

- **Argelès [R]**, locality ambiguous. Coordinate corrected: the pool's 42.9, 0.1 has the wrong longitude sign and lands 16-18 km east of the valley. **Argelès-Gazost (Hautes-Pyrénées) is 42.9945, -0.0998**; Argelès-sur-Mer is 42.545, 3.023. The Pyrenean valley fits a Water-and-Forests district; that is a reading and stays one. **Annecy [R]**, 45.8992, 6.1294. **Sallanches [R]**, 45.9364, 6.6317.

## 3. The City of Paris (1887-1930)

- Enters the **Service des Promenades et Plantations, 1887**, at the Bois de Vincennes **[A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]**. Forty years, ended by his death.
- **First cycling track in the Bois de Vincennes, 1890 [R, single source].** The pool carried this as [A] citing "also English Wikipedia"; false. The full en.wikipedia wikitext mentions no cycle track, velodrome or cycling anywhere, its only Vincennes date being "the Bois de Vincennes (1889)". The claim is one line of the es.wikipedia timeline. No works order located.
- **1898, wording corrected.** en.wikipedia does not say he was promoted to conservator that year; it says he "worked on the Bois de Vincennes (1889), the Bois de Boulogne and the western sector of the Promenades de Paris (1898)." The Cité describes conservator posts at Vincennes and Boulogne without a year. So **project work in the western sector, 1898 [A]; promotion year unsourced [R]**.
- **Avenue de Breteuil, 1898 [A: en.wikipedia]**. The radical revision of Jean-Camille Formigé's planting scheme and its rejection on cost come from the dossier's Guérin **[R]**.
- **Champ-de-Mars, from 1904 [A: en.wikipedia]**; **Domaine de Bagatelle, 1904-1910 [A for the Bois de Boulogne in en.wikipedia's list; R for the acquisition campaign, dossier]**. The rose garden and its competition from 1907 are the firmest piece of his Paris work. **Reuse of the fortifications belt as open land, from 1905 [R: dossier]**, largely unbuilt in his lifetime.
- ***Grandes villes et systèmes de parcs*: live bibliographic contradiction, not resolved.** The dossier reports the text dated December 1905 with a **1906 Hachette** printing per digitized catalogue copies; es.wikipedia gives **1908**, as does Éditions Norma's own current page. Both circulate. The journey file dates the stop 1906 and states the conflict in `date_confidence`.
- **Musée social, Urban and Rural Hygiene section, founding participant, 1908 [A: dossier]**; **cofounder, Société française des architectes urbanistes, 1911 [A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **Giverny, illustrated report on Monet's garden, 1908 [A: dossier]**. Monet has no journey file, so no interlock arises; the relation is real and one-directional.
- **Cité internationale universitaire park with Lucien Bechmann, from 1921 [A: dossier]**. Negative finding: the CIUP's history page names an avenue for the patron David David-Weill and **no memorial to Forestier** **[A: ciup.fr]**.
- **Domaine de Sceaux preservation programme, 1924 [R: dossier]**. Cut from the journey file. **Inspector of the garden component, Exposition internationale des arts décoratifs, 1925 [A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 4. Spain and Morocco (1911-1922)

- **Parque de María Luisa, Seville, engaged from 1911 [A: en.wikipedia, "Parque de María Luisa"]**, redesigning the former San Telmo palace gardens for the planned Ibero-American Exposition. **Fountain of the Lions conceived 1913**, sculpted later by Manuel Delgado Brackembury **[A: same]**. **Park inaugurated 1914 [A: es.wikipedia]**, but the exposition itself did not open until **1929**: two real dates for two different events, not to be collapsed.
- **Casa del Rey Moro, Ronda, 1912**, for the Duchess of Parcent **[A]**.
- **Moratalla, Hornachuelos, c. 1915 [R: dating and authorship unsettled]**. Stop cut for length; coordinate corrected for the record from 38.0333, -5.25 (28 km NNW, in the empty Sierra) to Nominatim's **Jardines de Moratalla, 37.7795, -5.2052**.
- **Morocco, commissioned by Resident-General Hubert Lyautey, 1913 [A: en.wikipedia]**. The roughly seventy-page *Rapport des réserves à constituer au dedans et aux abords des villes capitales du Maroc* (December 1913) and his urging Lyautey to engage **Henri Prost** come from the dossier's citation of the report; es.wikipedia confirms a 1913 Marrakech anteproyecto.
- **Jardin d'Essais, Rabat, c. 1914 [R: dossier]**, folded into the Rabat stop. **Menara and Agdal, Marrakesh, 1913-1914 [R]**: proposal cannot be separated from decree in reachable sources, and the stop says so.
- **Montjuïc: start year corrected from 1916 to 1915.** en.wikipedia dates only "Pedralbes Park (1916)" and gives **no** date for Montjuïc. Its own cited scholarly source, **Vincent Casals Costa, *Scripta Nova* 296(2)**, has him arriving in Barcelona in 1915, called by **Francesc Cambó** of the Lliga Regionalista **[A]**.
- **Pedralbes, c. 1916-1922 [A for the 1916 start, en.wikipedia; R for scale and collapse]**. Collapse mechanism corrected: **Eusebi Güell's** refusal to sell halted acquisition, and **Cambó left Barcelona city politics in 1918 for the Ministry of Public Works in Madrid**, which stalled it permanently. Not a simple loss of office.
- **Jardins de Laribal (by 1922) and Jardins de Miramar (1915-1923) [A: en.wikipedia]**; **Teatre Grec and Umbracle setting [R: dossier]**. Miramar and the Teatre Grec are folded into the Laribal stop. Collaborator throughout: **Nicolau Maria Rubió i Tudurí [A]**. **Palacio de Liria, Madrid, 1916 [A: en.wikipedia]**, later altered and reconstructed. **Joseph Guy garden, Béziers, 1918 [R: dossier]**.

## 5. Buenos Aires (1923-1929)

- **Arrival 12 November 1923, stay of about a month [A: dossier]**, working with **Carlos León Thays**, son of Carlos Thays, on a citywide park hierarchy and a coastal system from Constitución to the Avenida del Libertador.
- **Costanera, 1924**: some ten kilometres of reclaimed coast, 200 to 300 metres wide, partly from dredged fill **[A: en.wikipedia lists "Avenida Costera de Buenos Aires, 1924"; dimensions from the dossier]**.
- **Parque Saavedra**, signed plan in the 1925 Plan Noel volume, not fully executed **[A: dossier]**. Stop cut; coordinate corrected from -34.5583, -58.4869 (1 km southwest) to **-34.5507, -58.4800**. Disambiguation risk: a distinct Parque Saavedra in La Plata at -34.9316, -57.9399.
- **"Ostend" was a mistranslation putting him in the wrong country.** en.wikipedia's wikitext: "the seaside town of [[Ostende, Buenos Aires]]", that is **Ostende, Buenos Aires Province, Argentina** (near Pinamar) **[A]**. No verified coordinate was in the pool, so it appears in a campa as a named place with no stop.
- **Plaza San Martín, misattribution corrected.** He advised on the corridor toward Retiro; the executed plaza design is credited to **Carlos Thays**, the Retiro extension to Carlos León Thays and 1930s works, and **no signed Forestier drawing for the plaza has surfaced** **[R for the tradition; A for the correction, per the dossier's reading of the Observatorio Metropolitano breakdown of the Plan Noel]**.
- **Benito Carrasco**, Thays's former student and successor at Parks and Gardens, publicly opposed hiring a foreign adviser in 1923 and had a rival 409-hectare green-belt scheme **[R: interlock lens]**.
- **Observatorio AMBA claim trimmed.** The page confirms the advisory role ("actuó como asesor de la Comisión de Estética Edilicia") and the axis ("Desde la Estación Constitución hasta la Avenida del Libertador") **[A]**. It does **not** credit him as first to propose treating Buenos Aires at metropolitan scale; that priority claim is dropped. It does not mention Noel, Carlos León Thays, the Costanera dimensions, or Parque Saavedra.
- **Patio / Glorieta Andaluza, Parque 3 de Febrero**: municipal decree of **22 February 1929** ordering the work according to Forestier's project, opened **13 October 1929**, reported cost 35,457 pesos moneda nacional **[A: dossier]**. He was in France for both dates. **Tradition rejected**: that he designed the whole of Parque 3 de Febrero **[R: tourism copy conflating the advisory plan with the park's earlier multi-author history]**.
- **Unverified lead, kept out of the campas**: that **Marcelo T. de Alvear**, as ambassador to France, urged mayor Carlos Noel to invite Forestier in October 1923. Dossier lead, not a fetched source. Logged for a future pass.

## 6. Havana (1918, 1925-1929) and Mexico (1928)

- **Castillo de San Salvador de la Punta, 1918**, invited by President **Mario García Menocal** **[R: dossier only; no Cuban archive reachable]**. His first and least-cited Cuban work. **1925: Carlos Miguel de Céspedes, Secretary of Public Works under President Gerardo Machado, brings him into Havana planning [A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **The five-year residency is reported, not adopted.** en.wikipedia says he "moved to Havana for a five-year collaboration"; the dossier, citing **Joseph R. Hartman** and Guérin, describes **three distinct visits**, much design work done in Paris and sent by diplomatic pouch **[R on both sides; not re-verified against a Cuban archive]**. The journey file states the conflict rather than choosing.
- **El Capitolio gardens and master plan, 1925-1929 [A: en.wikipedia]**; **Paseo del Prado redesign, 1928 [A: dossier]**. **Parque de la Fraternidad, 1928, attribution partly open.** The park's own en.wikipedia article confirms the renaming for the Sixth Pan-American Conference and the ceiba planted in soil from twenty-eight American countries, but **does not name Forestier** **[A for renaming and ceiba; R for his authorship, from es.wikipedia and the dossier]**. **Parque René Fraga, Matanzas, dated 1920 [R: single source; the date sits awkwardly between his 1918 and 1925-29 Cuban work]**. No stop.
- **Loma de los Catalanes: outcome corrected.** The pool's "largely not realized" inverts the source. OnCuba News, 12 April 2021: his proposals "were revisited in the 1950s when Havana... ultimately shifted its civic center to the Loma de los Catalanes." The civic centre **was** built there, as the **Plaza Cívica José Martí**, today the **Plaza de la Revolución** **[A]**. Coordinate tightened from 23.1281, -82.3833 to approx. **23.1222, -82.3834**.
- **Mexico City, 23 September to 9 October 1928**, invited by **Miguel Ángel de Quevedo**; a strong earthquake on his final night **[A: dossier]**.

## 7. Lisbon, the last commissions, death (1926-1930)

- **Officer of the Legion of Honour** and **Commander of Spain's Order of Civil Merit**, both 1926 **[A: es.wikipedia]**. **Base Léonore** holds a file under his name, but only the search-results title was retrievable; contents unread **[R]**.
- **Lisbon, February 1927**, invited by the Câmara Municipal for about six days; proposes extending the Avenida da Liberdade as a planted avenue 150 to 200 metres wide reaching a terraced park over the Tagus **[A: dossier account of the *Ville de Lisbonne* study; en.wikipedia dates the consultation 1927]**.
- **Bastide du Roy: three corrections.** fr.wikipedia gives the patrons as **the count and countess Jean de Polignac**, not the Princess of Polignac as en.wikipedia states; the years as **1927-1929**, not 1926-1927; and the commune as **Antibes**, not Biot **[A: fr.wikipedia; the contradiction is flagged, not resolved]**. Coordinate corrected from 43.5892, 7.1006 to **43.6140, 7.1069** per OpenStreetMap. **Louis Süe** collaboration confirmed.
- **Consequence**: "his final project, underway when he dies" is **not** the Bastide du Roy, finished in 1929. It belongs to **Villa Berriots, Ustaritz**, for **Jean Patou**, dated **1929-1932**, continuing after his death **[A: dossier]**.
- **Villa Barbarénia, Biarritz, 1927**, with Walter-André Destailleur **[R: dossier]**. Cut. **Grand Prize, Barcelona International Exhibition, 1929 [A: es.wikipedia]**.
- **Dies in Paris, 26 October 1930, aged 69 [A: en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia]**. Several accounts report a death after an operation; **none names the illness or hospital [R]**.

## 8. Afterlife

- **The grave is an open gap.** The Cité's biography gives no burial information, and no record of a grave or tomb was found anywhere.
- **Seville: the pool's central negative finding was false and is reversed.** The claim that the tourism page "names no monument, bust, or plaque" read silence as absence. A monument exists: sevillacitycentre.com describes "una estatua levantada en su honor sobre el tronco de un ciprés centenario", carved into a centuries-old cypress "tras el Pabellón Mudéjar y junto al Monte Gurugú"; en.wikipedia's Forestier article independently states "The park also includes an honorary statue of Forestier" **[A, two sources]**. **No date for the carving was established [gap]**.
- **Jardin Jean-Claude-Nicolas-Forestier, 12 rue Thomire, Paris 13e**, by **Louis Benech**, **1994** **[A: paris.fr]**. Coordinate corrected to paris.fr's own **48.8205, 2.3513**; the pool's 48.8195, 2.3444 lands near the Stade Charléty, 500 m off.
- **"Sort de l'oubli à la fin du XXe siècle"**, the Cité's phrase for his rediscovery. A signed **1920 first edition of *Jardins*** (Émile-Paul), inscribed to one Gordon Knox, survives in the trade **[A: gardenhistory.com]**.
- **Living use in Havana**: taxi drivers in the Fraternidad, children on the Prado walks, a century on **[A: OnCuba News, 2021]**. The article names no monument to him in Cuba and no legends.
- **Persistent legends [all R]**: discipleship under Haussmann (no record located); five continuous Havana years; authorship of Plaza San Martín; authorship of all of Parque 3 de Febrero; the lone-genius framing in tourism video, which could not be traced here and is not confirmed bibliography.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Forestier is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no apparatus relation was needed: nothing here required applying a framework outside his own life. Nothing was silently deleted. The relations resting on **shared geography or a shared third party only**, kept out of the campas but recorded, all **backward**:

1. **antoni_gaudi.journey.json**: the shared figure is **Eusebi Güell**, Gaudí's patron and the landowner whose refusal to sell helped kill Pedralbes. Forestier has a documented relation to Güell, none to Gaudí. Güell is named in the Pedralbes campa as a fact of Forestier's own life; Gaudí is not. If the operator judges patron-in-common a real interlock, Pedralbes is where it goes.
2. **juan_manuel_de_rosas.journey.json** (the Palermo ground was Rosas's confiscated caserón, passed to the state after 1852) and **agusti_querol_subirats.journey.json** (the Monumento de los Españoles stands in the Libertador corridor his plan addressed): geography only, neither named.
3. **fidel_castro.journey.json / celia_sanchez.journey.json**: shared Havana pin three decades apart. The canonical pin 23.1136, -82.3666 is inherited byte-identical for the 1925 arrival stop; nobody from those files is named.
4. **carlos_thays.journey.json, Paris 1913**: Thays spoke at a Paris forestry congress about the year Forestier delivered the Morocco report. **No evidence the two met.** Flagged for checking against attendee lists; unused.
5. **sarmiento.journey.json** has no Parque 3 de Febrero stop at all, though carlos_thays.journey.json credits Sarmiento with opening the park in 1875. A corpus gap, stated, not filled from here.
6. **QUEUE.md**: Joseph Bouvard and Carlos María della Paolera are queued and unresearched. Della Paolera is named in a campa as a fact of the 1925 planning year, not as an interlock.

**The one interlock used in a campa: `carlos_thays`.** Not shared geography. Forestier's Buenos Aires collaborator was **Carlos León Thays, Thays's own son**; the plan extended the park system Thays spent two decades building; the Plaza San Martín design legend gives Forestier is Thays's; and Thays's student and successor Benito Carrasco opposed Forestier's hiring. Succession and rivalry, not co-location.
