# Vito Dumas (1900-1965), research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Argentine solo sailor, first man to circumnavigate
alone by the three great southern capes, 1942-1943, in the 9.55 m ketch Lehg II, in wartime,
without radio.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, tradition or
single-witness recollection. Contradictions are printed, not silently resolved; where arithmetic
decides a date, it is shown and the losing date stays on the page. Note throughout that **the
Spanish Wikipedia article is the richest source and is internally self-contradictory in at least
four places**: two 1932 arrival dates in adjacent sentences, two 1943 return dates, a 1955
departure postdating its own 1955 arrival, and leg-day counts no pair of its dates satisfies.

---

## 1. Palermo, and the water (1900-1931)

- Born **26 September 1900** in Palermo, Buenos Aires, to Victorio Dumas and Vicenta Grillo.
  **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- The family had been wealthy and had lost the fortune; the boy took odd jobs **[A,
  es.wikipedia]**: *"Tuve que limpiar pisos, hacer mandados, lustrar chapas de bronce de algun
  negocio."* **[A, footnote 3]**
- Athlete, swimmer, boxer and aviator, self-taught. **[A]** The earlier pass listed three sports;
  the source lists **four** (natacion, boxeo, atletismo, aviacion). Corrected.
- **1925, "world champion of endurance in the water." [R, not A.]** The most over-tagged claim in
  the pool. The source does not assert it; it reports his widow's recollection: *"Segun conto su
  esposa, ocho anos menor que el, en 1925 fue campeon mundial de permanencia en el agua."* No
  competition, no sanctioning body, no distance, no time. One witness, decades later.
- The widow's character portrait is attested verbatim and is the best interior evidence here:
  painter and sculptor, student at Bellas Artes, built a radio without having studied radio, and
  *"Lo que le faltaba era constancia, disciplina."* **[A, footnote 2]**

**Gap.** Nothing reachable dates his entry into sailing, names a boat before Lehg, or explains how
a man with no sea experience came to own an 8 Metre class sloop lying in France.

## 2. The Lehg, Arcachon to the Plate (1931-1932)

- **13 December 1931**, departs **Arcachon**, alone, with no prior ocean experience. **[A,
  en.wikipedia and es.wikipedia]**
- **Vessel description corrected.** The pool's "eight-meter sloop about fifteen meters overall"
  misreads *"un 8 metros de formula, de unos 15m de eslora y 2,15 de manga."* **"8 metros de
  formula" is the International 8 Metre class rating rule, not a length.** Lehg was an
  8-Metre-class sloop, about 15 m LOA, 2.15 m beam, built 1918. **[A]** The afterlife pass called
  her a ketch, contradicting the same file's chronology: Lehg was single-masted, and **Lehg II**
  was the ketch, *"arbolado a ketch, o sea, con dos mastiles."*
- **Runs aground asleep near Rio Grande do Sul, rescued by the Brazilian Navy. [A,** es.wikipedia:
  *"Dormido, vara cerca de Rio Grande do Sul donde lo rescata la Marina brasilena."***]
  Coordinate error corrected:** the pool's -30.03 / -51.23 is **Porto Alegre**, 250 km inland at
  the head of the Lagoa dos Patos, where an Atlantic crossing cannot strike. Use the coastal bar
  at Rio Grande, **-32.035 / -52.0986**, the value the geography pass itself used.
- **The Buenos Aires arrival dates do not agree. [Contradiction, flagged; resolved by arithmetic
  only.]** es.wikipedia, one sentence: *"arriba a Buenos Aires el 13 de abril de 1932."* The next:
  *"Despues de 121 dias de navegacion recalo el 12 de marzo de 1932."* But 13 December 1931 + 121
  days = **12 April 1932**, and 13 December to 12 March is **90** days, so the 12 March date
  cannot coexist with the 121-day count. en.wikipedia gives 13 April. **The journey file heads the
  stop 13 April 1932 [A]** and keeps 12 March on the page as an internal corruption.
**Correction to the geography pass.** It made the Yacht Club Argentino his career base and the
terminus of *every* voyage. **Overstated:** es.wikipedia names the YCA once, as the 1932 landfall,
and credits the **Club Nautico Buchardo**, the **YMCA of Buenos Aires** and **El Grafico** with
helping him prepare Lehg II. **[A]**

## 3. Lehg II (1934-1941)

- **1934**, the **Astilleros Parodi** yard at **Tigre** builds Lehg II: double-ended ketch,
  9.55 m LOA, 3.30 m beam, 3,500 kg iron keel. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- **1937**, shakedown toward Rio de Janeiro: wind near 140 km/h, sails destroyed, and *"le hizo
  dar una vuelta en campana (giro de 360 grados sobre la linea de crujia)."* **[A]**
  **Terminology corrected:** that is a full **roll about the longitudinal axis**, not a
  **pitchpole**, which is end over end about the transverse axis. Wrong word, right event.
- **After that voyage Dumas sold Lehg II** to Dr. Rafael Gamba and had to **buy her back years
  later** to make the circumnavigation. **[A, es.wikipedia]** The earlier pass omitted this: the
  boat of the great voyage was for some years not his.

**Gap.** No reachable source gives the sale price or the years of sale and repurchase.

## 4. The circumnavigation (1942-1943)

### Preparation

- About a year of provisions aboard, because wartime restrictions made foreign resupply
  unreliable; **no radio**, for fear of being taken for a spy by a belligerent warship. **[A,
  en.wikipedia and es.wikipedia]** Argentina was officially neutral.
- He had **ten pounds sterling, lent by a friend**, and answered the question of money with
  *"Total para que quiero dinero, si en navegacion no voy a gastar."* **[A, es.wikipedia, cited to
  Revista Todo es Historia no. 10, February 1968]** The pool called the context ambiguous between
  1931 and 1942; it is not, the passage sitting in the 1942 preparation section right after the
  ten-pounds sentence. **1942.**
- Shortly before sailing: *"Voy en esta epoca materialista, a realizar una empresa romantica, para
  ejemplo de la juventud."* **[A,** es.wikipedia wikitext, *"Poco antes de partir justifico su
  viaje con esta frase"***]** **Attribution corrected:** the pool credited this to the Cresto
  biography, but the wikitext tag is `ref name=TODO`, resolving to **Revista Todo es Historia**,
  dir. Felix Luna, no. 10, February 1968.

### The legs

| Leg | Departure | Arrival | Days | Miles | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BA to Montevideo | 27 Jun 1942 | 1 Jul 1942 | hop | n/a | [A] |
| Montevideo to Cape Town | 1 Jul 1942 | 24 Aug 1942 | 55 | 4,560 | [A] |
| Cape Town to Wellington | 14 Sep 1942 | c. 27 Dec 1942 | 104 | 7,400 | [R] arrival |
| Wellington to Valparaiso | 30 Jan 1943 | c. 12 Apr 1943 | 72 | n/a | [R] arrival |
| Valparaiso to Mar del Plata | n/a | c. 8 Aug 1943 | 37 | 3,200 | [A] days, miles |
| Mar del Plata to BA | n/a | 7 Sep 1943 | hop | n/a | [A] |

- **The Wellington arrival date in the pool was fabricated**, the worst error in the set. The pool
  gave **30 November 1942**; es.wikipedia gives **no arrival date at all**. Two computations from
  its own numbers converge elsewhere: 14 September 1942 + the 104-day leg = **27 December 1942**;
  and 30 January 1943 minus its own 33-day stay = **28 December 1942**. The 30 November date would
  make the leg 77 days rather than 104 and the stay 61 rather than 33. **Corrected to circa
  27 December 1942, tagged [R].**
- **Cape Horn was rounded after Valparaiso, not before.** The pool placed the rounding in March
  1943, between Wellington and Valparaiso. Impossible: that leg ran 72 days from 30 January.
  es.wikipedia heads the section *"Valparaiso - Buenos Aires"*, and fr.wikipedia's table gives
  Valparaiso to Mar del Plata rounding the Horn at the first attempt, 3,200 miles in 37 days. The
  rounding falls **roughly June to August 1943 [A on sequence, [R] on the month]**.
- **The broken nose** is verbatim attested from the Horn passage (*"un fuerte golpe me arroja
  contra un mamparo... y le rompio el tabique de la nariz"*), as is **the infected right arm** on
  the South Atlantic leg, which left him barely able to work the ketch. **[A, es.wikipedia]**

### The return, and a second contradiction

- es.wikipedia, one paragraph: *"regreso durante la manana del domingo 7 de septiembre de 1943
  (437 dias de los cuales 274 fueron navegando)."* Elsewhere: *"arribo el 8 de agosto de 1943 a
  Buenos Aires."* it.wikipedia: *"settembre del 1943."* **27 June 1942 + 437 days = 7 September
  1943, exactly**, while 27 June 1942 to 8 August 1943 is 407 days, so the pool's pairing of the
  8 August date with the 437-day count cannot stand.
- **Reconstruction offered, not imposed:** 7 September 1943 is the Buenos Aires return, and
  **8 August 1943 is most plausibly the Mar del Plata landfall**, a port the same source names as
  touched. Labeled **[R]** in the journey file.
- Totals: **20,420 nautical miles, 437 elapsed days, 274 of them sailing [A]**, the 274
  reconciling with the leg breakdown (55 + 104 + 72 + 37 = 268, plus short hops). **Ports touched,
  corrected:** the pool said "only Montevideo, Cape Town, Wellington and Valparaiso," but the
  source adds Mar del Plata (*"de tocar los puertos de Ciudad del Cabo, Wellington, Valparaiso y
  Mar del Plata"*). **[A]**
- **Unsupported superlative removed.** The pool called the Pacific leg "the calmest" of the four.
  The source supports only *"logro navegar por el Oceano Pacifico sin mayores sobresaltos,"* and
  uses near-identical language for the South Atlantic leg. No ranking is attested.
- Jean Merrien, *Aux Limites du Possible*: *"la hazana mas inaudita que hombre solo jamas haya
  cumplido en el mar."* **[A as quoted in es.wikipedia; the French original was not obtained.]**

## 5. The crossing of the unforeseen (1945)

- Buenos Aires to New York and back aboard Lehg II, **17,045 miles in 234 days**, chronicled as
  *El crucero de lo imprevisto*. Ports and incidents, all **[A, es.wikipedia]** and none dated
  beyond the year: **Rio de Janeiro**; **Havana**; a near-death **off Coney Island** approaching
  New York; rescue by the ship **Serantes** in open Atlantic south of the **Canary Islands** after
  106 days of solitude; **Ceara** (Fortaleza); **Montevideo**; home.
- **Gap.** No month-level 1945 dates exist anywhere reachable, so every 1945 stop in the journey
  file defaults to 01-01 and is hedged in `date_confidence`.

## 6. Sirio, Peron, and the mufa (1955-1964)

- **1955, Buenos Aires to New York aboard Sirio: 7,100 miles in 117 days [A]**, but **the
  departure date is unusable.** The pool gave 16 September 1955, while the same source
  gives arrival at New York on **23 September 1955 after 117 days**, seven days later; counting
  back puts departure around **29 May 1955**. The 16 September date is very probably contamination
  from the **Revolucion Libertadora coup of that date**, discussed two sentences later in the same
  article. **Departure tagged [R], circa late May 1955.**
- **Bermuda**: dehydration in the equatorial calms, hospitalized, scurvy symptoms, blood pressure
  recorded at 28. **[R, es.wikipedia;** the 28 is reproduced as given and is not medically
  interpretable as written.**]**
- **New York, 23 September 1955 [A]**, after a hurricane encounter near the US coast; ill in the
  city nearly a year, sells Sirio, returns to Argentina by freighter **[A]**.
  - **Storm name corrected.** The source writes *"el huracan Ionne."* The 1955 storm is
    **Hurricane Ione**, which struck the US East Coast on 19 September 1955, independently
    corroborating the 23 September arrival.
- **The Slocum Award.** The first Joshua Slocum Award was presented **for 1956 to Vito Dumas**,
  for *"the most notable single-handed passage made during the past year."* **[A, es.wikipedia
  footnote 1]** **Framing error corrected:** the pool's quotation began *"the first man to sail
  alone around the world, is awarded for..."*, as though it described Dumas; that appositive
  belongs to **Joshua Slocum**, the namesake, and the wikitext quote opens with an ellipsis.
- **The Blue Water Medal. [Contradiction, flagged.]** The CCA's own roster lists Dumas *"Without
  Date"* (en.wikipedia); secondary sources commonly give **1957**; es.wikipedia places the
  **presentation in 1962**, at the skippers' briefing for the Buenos Aires to Rio regatta.
  **Nothing supports the pool's association of the medal with the 1943 completion.**
- **1961**, launches **Sirio II [A]**. **1962**, abandons the Buenos Aires to Rio ocean race off
  **Cabo Polonio**, ailing **[A]**. **1964**, last voyage, Buenos Aires to Mar del Plata **[A]**.

### The controversies (material the earlier pass logged as a gap, wrongly)

es.wikipedia carries a whole **"Controversias"** section, and the pool called it "unelaborated."
It is elaborated, and it is the richest omitted material in the file. **[A, es.wikipedia]** Dumas
was **disdained by part of Argentine society for his closeness to Peronism**. **Peron named him
Teniente de Navio** as an honorary title and offered him direction of the **Escuela Nautica
Deportiva**, which he founded and ran **so that people of limited means could sail**. Later
**anti-Peronist governments attacked the idea**, and a popular myth grew that Dumas was
**"mufa"**, that naming him brought bad luck, pushing his figure into **relative oblivion outside
sailing circles**.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable dates the naval commission, the school's founding, or its closure.

## 7. Death and afterlife (1965 onward)

- **28 March 1965**, dies of a cerebrovascular accident at **Vicente Lopez**, aged 64. **[A]**
  Buried first at the **Cementerio de Olivos [A]**; the two passes approximate the grounds
  differently (-34.5145 / -58.4864 and -34.5089 / -58.4931), which is approximation noise, not a
  source disagreement, and the journey file uses the geography value.
- **2000**, remains transferred to the **Panteon Naval, Cementerio de la Chacarita [A]**. The
  source dates the move to 2000 or later; the year is taken as 2000 and hedged.
- **1980**, Konex Diploma al Merito, Yachting **[A, en.wikipedia]**. **1982**, **Sirio II**
  restored at the **Club Nautico Mar del Plata [A]**. **November 1986**, bust by **Hidelberg
  Ferrino** at Boulevard Maritimo and Carlos Pellegrini, Mar del Plata **[A]**.
- **Lehg** preserved at the **Complejo Museografico Enrique Udaondo**, Lujan, with compass,
  sextant, boom iron and a map of the voyage **[A]**, undated. **Lehg II** donated to the Navy for
  training, **wrecked at the mouth of the port of La Plata in 1966**, restored, displayed at the
  **Museo Naval de la Nacion**, Tigre **[A]**, donation and restoration undated.
- Documentary ***El navegante solitario*, dir. Roberto Petriz [A]**, no release year found. The
  tango **"Navegante" [A]**, dated **5 August 1943** in one pass and **1943** in another;
  composer and lyricist unconfirmed.
- **Los cuarenta bramadores: la vuelta al mundo por la "ruta imposible."** First publication
  **commonly cited as 1944 [R, not verified against a library catalogue]**.
- **English editions corrected.** The pool gave McGraw-Hill 2001, ISBN 0-07-137611-9, as though it
  were the first. Publisher, ISBN and year are individually right (Open Library: International
  Marine / Ragged Mountain Press, 19 July 2001, Sailor's Classics no. 5), **but it is a reissue.**
  Open Library and the Internet Archive both record a **first English edition in 1960, Adlard
  Coles** (with Harrap and De Graff). **[A]**
- "Translated into numerous languages" (en.wikipedia) and "multiple streets and nautical
  organizations" named for him (es.wikipedia) are both **[R]**: neither source names one.

## 8. Interlocks with the existing atlas

A corpus-wide grep on 2026-08-14 frames everything below: **no atlas file mentions Vito Dumas,
Lehg II, Los cuarenta bramadores, or the Roaring Forties voyage by name**, outside one QUEUE.md
row (line 348, docs count 0). Every intersection is therefore **[R] inherited geography and
theme**, except the pin coordinates, which are **[A]** as facts about the atlas.

- **The canonical Buenos Aires pins [A].** -34.6037 / -58.3816 is the same coordinate used for
  San Martin's landing in 1812, for Victoria Ocampo born in 1890, and for Borges born in 1899.
  Alvear's fixes -34.608 / -58.3816 for the 1812 port landing; Ocampo's fixes -34.608 / -58.3646
  for the port; Tschiffely's fixes **Darsena Norte** at -34.6035 / -58.36, where his horses Mancha
  and Gato came home by sea on **20 December 1928**, fourteen years before Dumas sailed from that
  basin. All inherited byte-identical. **House-style pass, 2026-08-14:** the birth-stop campa
  named San Martin, Ocampo, Borges and Houssay purely as pin-sharers with zero documented contact
  with Dumas, alongside a bare "the atlas" self-reference; both cut, the stop now stands on the
  family's own facts.
- **Victoria Ocampo [R]** edits *Les Lettres Francaises* from "neutral Buenos Aires" under a
  government "whose government leans toward the Axis": the neutrality that made the 1942 departure
  legally possible and physically dangerous. **Bernardo Houssay [R]** frames 1940-1941 as the
  moment "Europe is closing, and North America has begun looking south."
- **San Brendan [A on the lineage, [R] on Dumas's place in it]** builds an explicit chain of
  Atlantic small-boat crossings: Brendan, Leif Erikson's L'Anse aux Meadows dated to 1021,
  Columbus on Toscanelli's chart, the 1977 replica landfall of 26 June 1977. **Cut from campa,
  2026-08-14**: the Arcachon stop named Brendan and Erikson only as a thematic echo, worse boats
  and thinner evidence, with zero documented citation either way and a bare "the atlas"
  self-reference; both removed.
- **Mikhail Bakunin [A]** takes the Panama Railroad in 1861 "rather than round Cape Horn."
  **Julio Argentino Roca [A]**: Ley 1532 of 1884 organized Tierra del Fuego as a National Territory
  governed from Buenos Aires. **Symmes [A]**: Reynolds's agitation of 1836-38 produced the
  expedition that sailed in 1838 "to chart the Antarctic verge." **Pedro de Mendoza [R]**: his
  1536 fort "Nuestra Senora de Buena Esperanza" shares only two words with the cape Dumas rounded.
