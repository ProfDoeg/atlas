# Marius Sophus Lie (1842-1899): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `sophus_lie.journey.json`. Nordfjordeid, Moss, Christiania, Berlin, Paris, Fontainebleau, Leipzig, Kristiania. 43 stops in 8 segments.* Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a single-source detail no pass confirmed against anything it fetched itself.

The research pool arrived carrying its own corrections, a second pass having caught source misattributions, coordinate errors and tags set too strongly; those are honoured rather than argued with. Much of what circulates about Lie rests on one German biography (Fritzsche) that no pass read directly, and those details are kept and marked, not dropped.

## 1. Nordfjordeid and Moss, 1842-1857

**[A]** Born 17 December 1842 at Nordfjordeid (then Eid, Nordfjord), Sweden-Norway; MacTutor, snl.no and both Wikipedias agree. Father **Johan Herman Lie**, Lutheran parish priest; mother **Mette Maren Stabell**, of a well-known Trondheim family; youngest of six. **[R]** The parents' life-dates usually appended (1803-1873, 1807-1852) appear in none of snl.no, no.wikipedia, en.wikipedia or MacTutor.

**[R]** The family moved to **Moss** on the Oslofjord in 1851 on a new clerical post (snl.no); no month.

**[A]** Lie first went to school at Moss; MacTutor says only "the town of Moss, which is a port in south-eastern Norway." **[R]** The name **Moss borgerlige Realskole** is attested by nothing fetched, so the campa says it "attaches to him only in later retellings."

**[R]** The mother died when Sophus was about nine, so 1852. No exact date, and her birth and death years are absent everywhere: a real gap in a life otherwise well covered from fifteen on. **[R]** A baptismal record of 3 May 1843 and a vaccination record of 14 August 1843 are cited by the dossier from the Historical Population Register; no pass pulled it. The municipality is now Stad, Vestland.

## 2. Christiania, 1857-1869

**[A]** Sent at fifteen, in 1857, to **Nissen's Latin- og Realskole** (MacTutor; snl.no), at **Rosenkrantz' gate 7** from 1847; no.wikipedia's article on the school lists Lie among its alumni. **[A]** Examen artium and entry to the **Royal Frederick University** in 1859 (snl.no), in Domus Academica, in use from 1851. **[A]** Qualifying examination 1865.

**[A]** In 1862 Lie attended **Ludvig Sylow's** lectures on Abel and Galois. This arrived tagged [R] with a note saying it could not be re-sourced; MacTutor states it verbatim, "Lie attended lectures by Ludwig Sylow in 1862." Upgraded to [A].

**[A]** MacTutor dates Lie's **first original idea to 1867**, not 1869: it came in the middle of the night, he rushed to Ernst Motzfeldt, woke him, and shouted "I have found it, it is quite simple!" An earlier note framed this as a night walk; MacTutor describes no walk, does not say which discovery it was, and is rendering an oral tradition rather than a transcript.

**[A]** First publication 1869, on imaginary elements in plane geometry, part privately printed at Christiania and part in Crelle's Journal. The self-financed printing is not Fritzsche-only, as an earlier note claimed: MacTutor says "he published at his own expense." **[R]** The figure of roughly **245 lifetime publications** is in no reference source and survives only hedged as "something in the order of."

## 3. Berlin, Paris, and the arrest, 1869-1870

**[A]** Scholarship travel via **Göttingen**, then **Berlin**, September 1869 to February 1870, meeting Kronecker, Weierstrass, Kummer, and decisively **Felix Klein** (en.wikipedia; MacTutor). **[A]** Spring 1870 in **Paris** with Klein, meeting **Darboux**, **Camille Jordan** and **Michel Chasles**. **[A]** France declared war on Prussia 19 July 1870; Klein, a Prussian subject, left, while Lie, a neutral Norwegian, stayed and then set out on foot for Italy. **[A]** Arrested at **Fontainebleau**, about sixty kilometres out, in roughly August 1870, as a suspected German spy; held about a month; released after **Darboux** intervened.

**Correction carried, and it matters.** An earlier note called the "equations mistaken for cipher" detail a later narrative condensation, implying folklore. MacTutor asserts it outright, "his mathematics notes being assumed to be top secret coded messages," so it is **[A] on MacTutor's authority**, while it stays true that **no interrogation record, arrest date, dossier number or named place of detention was located**.

**[R] The post-release itinerary is the weakest stretch of the life.** Switzerland, Italy, **Düsseldorf** by November 1870 and a December return arrived tagged [A] and cited to MacTutor. The verification pass searched MacTutor: **"Switzerland" and "Düsseldorf" do not appear**, and it gives no post-arrest itinerary at all. The citation is dropped, the stop stands as [R] on Fritzsche, and it is kept rather than deleted, a poorly sourced stop and a false one being different things. The return itself is [A] for the year, [R] for the month.

## 4. The chair the parliament made, 1871-1876

**[A]** Dissertation "Over en Classe geometriske Transformationer," **dated 1871**. **CONTRADICTION, not resolved:** snl.no compresses the doctorate to **1871**, MacTutor and en.wikipedia give **July 1872** for the award, and the title page reads 1871.

**[A]** In **1872** the Storting created an extraordinary personal professorship for Lie. **[R]** The circulating day-dates, **26 March** for the act and **1 July** for the appointment, are in no source consulted; the pool item was tagged [A] and cited to en.wikipedia, which gives only "1872: Norwegian Parliament established extraordinary professorship." The stop's `date` field is therefore the bare year. **[A]** de.wikipedia adds a **second** personal professorship in 1894.

**[R]** The autumn 1872 journey to Göttingen and Erlangen with Klein rests on Fritzsche and Rowe; MacTutor and both Wikipedias do not mention it.

**[A]** Engaged to **Anna Sophie Birch** at the end of 1872; married **11 August 1874 at Risør** (snl.no; year corroborated by en.wikipedia). **CONTRADICTION, not resolved:** Anna's birth year is given as **1852** and as **1854**, and en.wikipedia's "then eighteen years old" at end-1872 points to 1854. The campa carries both. **[R]** The church as venue is an assumption.

**[A]** "Ueber Gruppen von Transformationen," 1874, the landmark early publication of the theory of continuous transformation groups. **[A]** 1876: co-founded **Archiv for Mathematik og Naturvidenskab** with Jacob Worm-Müller and Georg Ossian Sars; **[R]** its characterisation as partly a reaction against conservative forces in Norwegian science comes from snl.no via the dossier.

## 5. Abel, the children, and Engel, 1873-1885

**[A]** From 1873, a state-supported new edition of **Abel's** complete works with **Sylow**, two volumes from Grøndahl & Søn, 1881, the imprint not re-fetched as a bibliographic record. **CONTRADICTION, not resolved:** en.wikipedia elsewhere dates the start to **1872**.

**[A]** Children: Marie Sofie (1877), Anna Dagny (1880), Johan Herman (1884). **[R]** The day **22 April 1884** is in no source consulted; en.wikipedia gives only "Herman (b. 1884)."

**[A]** **Friedrich Engel** arrived in Christiania in **1884** and left in 1885, the collaboration producing *Theorie der Transformationsgruppen*; **[R]** the month **September** is in neither MacTutor nor Wikipedia.

**[R]** **Drammensveien 84** for 1885 rests on the dossier's citation of histreg.no; the pin is approximate and the building was not identified.

## 6. Leipzig, 1886-1898

**[A]** Appointed at **Leipzig** in 1886, succeeding Klein, who went to Göttingen. **[R]** The **Seeburgstrasse** apartment and the **February** arrival come from Fritzsche, with no house number and no confirmation. **[A]** The **inaugural lecture title** "Über den Einfluß der Geometrie auf die Entwicklung der Mathematik" is confirmed by de.wikipedia for 1886, so it is upgraded from dossier-only. **[R]** The date **29 May** is in no source fetched, and the tradition that **King Albert of Saxony** attended is not merely unattested: de.wikipedia explicitly records no royal attendance. The Augusteum, where he taught, stood on Augustusplatz until 1968.

**[R]** **Czermak's Spectatorium, Bruderstrasse 32**, as Lie's lecture hall, with its student roll (Engel, Scheffers, Study, Hausdorff, Kowalewski, Żorawski), rests on the dossier alone: plausible, unconfirmed, kept, and the campa says so.

**[A]** *Theorie der Transformationsgruppen*: vol. I 1888 (Teubner, Leipzig), vol. II 1890, vol. III 1893. **[A] The Killing quarrel.** Rowe's DSB supplement prints Lie's sentence to Klein and dates the letter to **early 1888**, on Lie's seeing the **first instalment of Killing's four-part study in *Mathematische Annalen***, which dates the outburst to a publication rather than to the dispute in general. **Attribution corrected:** the quotation is **not** in Freudenthal's original DSB article; encyclopedia.com carries two articles on Lie and it belongs to Rowe's supplement. **[R] and not used:** the truncated form dropping the ellipsis and the clause "and I hold Engel responsible" is printed as Lie's sentence by no source, and dropping that clause hides that his complaint was aimed as much at his own collaborator as at Killing.

**[A]** **November 1889 to June 1890**: insomnia, neurological and psychiatric symptoms, hospitalisation (en.wikipedia). de.wikipedia independently supports the pernicious-anaemia reading, giving *perniziöse Anämie* combined with *Schwierigkeiten im wissenschaftlichen Umfeld*. **[R]** Neither the clinic at **Ilten** nor the day **16 November** is named by any source fetched. Supporting circumstance only: de.wikipedia confirms Ilten hosts Klinikum Wahrendorff, so a private asylum did exist there.

**[A]** Vol. III's preface **publicly rejected the claim that Lie had been Klein's pupil**, badly damaging his standing among German colleagues. **Two renderings, not harmonised.** Rowe: "I am no pupil of Klein's. Nor is the reverse the case, even though it perhaps comes closer to the truth." MacTutor's wording of the same passage differs throughout. Two translators, one German original, and **neither supplies the German**. The file quotes Rowe.

**[A] Klein's private verdict** on the Lie-Scheffers volumes is quoted verbatim in Rowe, who cites **Klein's papers, Göttingen, Cod. Ms. F. Klein, 22f** (an earlier note wrongly said no citation was given), with neither date nor recipient, so the circulating "circa 1890s" is inference and is flagged. This is Klein writing **about** Lie, so the stop sits at Lie's own Leipzig pin; staging Klein at Göttingen would be Klein's stop.

**Honours [A], separately sourced.** London Mathematical Society honorary member 1878; Académie des Sciences corresponding member 1892; Royal Society Foreign Member 1895; U.S. National Academy of Sciences Foreign Associate 1895; first **Lobachevsky Prize**, Kazan, 1897. Lie travelled to none of these places, so they are gathered into one Leipzig stop rather than pinned abroad.

## 7. The return, the death, the afterlife

**[A]** Resigned the Leipzig chair **May 1898**, citing the climate; **[R]** the day **22 May** is in no source fetched.

**[A]** The **1894** Storting act creating a second personal professorship is confirmed by de.wikipedia. **Source misattribution corrected:** en.wikipedia does **not** contain the 1894 offer, the **10,000-kroner** figure, or **Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson**, its honours and career sections having been checked directly. Both are **[R]**, and the campa says so.

**[A]** Returned to Christiania **September 1898**; died in Kristiania **18 February 1899**, aged 56. **[R]** Pernicious anaemia identified around **Christmas 1898**: the disease as cause of death is [A], the timing dossier-only. No deathbed address, so the pin is the city centre.

**[A]** Buried at **Vår Frelsers gravlund**, Oslo, in the **Vestre del**. This arrived tagged [R] and is **upgraded**: no.wikipedia states the burial and lists "Marius Sophus Lie, matematiker" in the Vestre del of the cemetery article, though en.wikipedia's cemetery article carries the entry with a citation-needed tag. **REJECTED CLAIM:** the afterlife pass's "listed among its formally recognized honorary graves" is **wrong**; he is in the Vestre del, not the æreslunden. **Same-day burial rejected:** the geography lens dated the burial 18 February, the death date; the file uses **24 February**. **[R]** Sylow's memorial address of that day appears in no source fetched.

**[A]** *Gesammelte Abhandlungen*, seven volumes, Teubner and Aschehoug, **1922-1960**, edited chiefly by Engel and Poul Heegaard. **[A] The Abel Prize effort.** Wikipedia confirms Lie began it in 1899 on learning Nobel's prizes would exclude mathematics, that **Sylow and Carl Størmer "drew up statutes and rules,"** and that the 1905 dissolution of the union with Sweden "ended the first attempt." Two corrections: the prize was announced August 2001, but Selberg received only an honorary award in 2002 and "the first actual Abel Prize was awarded in **2003**"; and the drafting window is **the last weeks of Lie's life**.

**[A]** **Sophus Lies auditorium**, Blindern (snl.no); **[R]** no dedication date exists, so `date_confidence` calls the 1960 date an ordering device with no evidential weight. **[A]** Wikimedia Commons holds a gravestone photograph and an image of a **monument at Nordfjordeid**; **[R]** the file pages returned 404, so the monument's sculptor, date and inscription are unknown, and **Erik Werenskiold's 1902 portrait** (snl.no) is necessarily posthumous.

## Contradictions and gaps

Six contradictions are flagged where they arise above and none is silently adjudicated: the doctorate, Anna Birch's birth year, the Abel edition's start, the reading of the 1889 crisis, the two renderings of the 1893 preface, and the meaning of the Klein rupture (contemporaries read instability; later historiography, per the dossier citing Rowe, Purkert, Straume and Fritzsche, reads documented conflict, a reframing itself never verified). The gaps:

- **No primary record of the Fontainebleau arrest**: no date, no dossier number, no place of detention, no transcript. Everything known comes from what Lie and his friends said afterwards. **The autumn 1870 route home** likewise exists only in the German literature. **Mette Maren Stabell** has no birth year, no death date, and almost no trace in any reference source.
- **Fritzsche's text was never read.** The Journal of Lie Theory PDF is a scanned image file, not extractable as text, yet it is the load-bearing authority for the Düsseldorf stop, the Seeburgstrasse address, the 29 May inaugural date, King Albert's attendance, the Ilten clinic, the 22 May resignation, the December 1898 diagnosis, and the 1872 day-dates: eight open questions, one unread source, and the highest-value target for a future pass. Stubhaug's full-length biography was likewise never consulted, and Merker's modern translations could not be verified for title, publisher or scope.
- **No film, documentary or television biography of Lie** could be located. A negative finding inherited from the dossier, not re-searched, because WebSearch quota was exhausted. It should not be quoted as established.
- **No Abel journey file exists in the corpus**, confirmed by listing `working/journeys`. Abel is named in Lie's campas as a historical person, Lie having edited his complete works and drafted the first statutes for a prize in his name, but there is no slug to point at.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Lie is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops were written. Per the standing instruction, relations found in the pool that are not rooted in Lie's own life were **not silently deleted**.

**Forward, therefore not licensable.** **Captain Alfred Dreyfus** trained at the school quartered in the château at Fontainebleau in **1880**, ten years after Lie's arrest in the same town, and was himself wrongly convicted of German-directed espionage in 1894-95. The parallel is exact in kind and runs the wrong way in time, so no Dreyfus material was written into any campa or `suggested_ref`. If the operator wants it recorded it belongs on `captain_alfred_dreyfus.journey.json`, pointing back at 1870, a legal direction.

**Co-location only, dropped from the prose, recorded here.** **Victor Hugo** in wartime Paris in 1870; **Tchaikovsky** at the Leipzig Gewandhaus on 5 January 1888, inside Lie's Leipzig years; **Nietzsche** and the Leipzig node two decades earlier. No source connects any of them to Lie personally, and the map already shows co-location, so none is named in a campa.

**Backward and indirect, and kept.** **Arthur Cayley** is named once, in the Erlangen 1872 stop: his 1859 memoir on quantics defined distance projectively via an absolute conic, Klein read it around 1871 as a model of non-Euclidean geometry, and that reading fed the 1872 Erlangen Program, which Lie was present for and later disputed credit over. A lineage he stood inside rather than a co-location, so `arthur_cayley.journey.json` appears in the stop's `sources`. Flagged rather than assumed.

**Directly related and named.** **Évariste Galois**, via Sylow's 1862 lectures, attested by MacTutor: Lie took the group idea from him before he had a subject of his own. No journey file in the corpus previously named Sophus Lie anywhere.
