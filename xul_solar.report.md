# Xul Solar (Oscar Agustin Alejandro Schulz Solari), 1887-1963: research report

*Compiled 2026-08. Argentine painter, astrologer, inventor of languages, games and notations; closest friend of Jorge Luis Borges. Slug: `xul_solar`. Companion file: `xul_solar.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend: [A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source report without documentary support · contradictions are flagged where they occur and **not** adjudicated.

A note on the evidence base first. Almost the whole spine of this life comes from two Wikipedia articles, English and Spanish, which disagree on several points and cite few primary sources. The Spanish one is richer in dates and carries most of the chronology below; where it stands alone and cites nothing, the claim is marked [R] however confidently it is phrased. The independent material reached is Gradowczyk (1994, extracts only), Bach (1994), Vicuña (2004), Demaria (1971) and the museum's site. The Fundación Pan Klub archive, which holds the manuscripts, games and library, was not consulted.

---

## 1. San Fernando and Buenos Aires, 1887-1912

Born **14 December 1887** in San Fernando, Buenos Aires province, as **Oscar Agustín Alejandro Schulz Solari**, to a Baltic German father, Emilio (Elmo) Schulz Riga, and an Italian mother, Agustina Solari, of Zoagli in Liguria **[A: Wikipedia EN and ES]**.

Schooling at the **Colegio Francés Fermy**, then the **Colegio Inglés**, then the **Colegio Nacional, Sección Norte**, in Palermo **[A: Wikipedia ES]**. The years of attendance are given nowhere reachable; the file dates the stop 1900, inferred. The building could not be confirmed and a Palermo coordinate stands in, flagged.

**Architecture studies begun c. 1905, abandoned after roughly two years [A: Wikipedia ES].** No institution is named. What he did between then and 1912 is essentially undocumented; the only interior view is retrospective, his own writings as quoted by Gradowczyk ("Dazzling light, colors never seen... I am pregnant with an immense, new world!"), which Gradowczyk ties to a crisis of his early twenties **[A as a quotation; R as to its date]**.

**Gap:** no work from before the European years survives, or none is catalogued in anything reachable. The formation of the visual language is invisible at its start.

---

## 2. The departure, 5 April 1912

He sails from Buenos Aires on the **England Carrier**, working his passage, **on 5 April 1912**, ostensibly bound for Hong Kong **[A: Wikipedia EN, which names ship and date]**. The Spanish article gives the same intention without the vessel: "se embarca rumbo a Hong Kong en abril de 1912, pero al llegar a Londres decide permanecer" **[A: Wikipedia ES]**.

**A contradiction alleged in the research pool does not survive checking.** The pool held that the two Wikipedias disagree on his destination, one giving London and Turin, the other Hong Kong. They do not: both give Hong Kong, both have him stop in London, and no source names Turin. The framing is dropped, the date fixed at 1912-04-05.

The motive is softer than the date. The destination is attested; the claim that he meant to become a **Tibetan monk** is tradition without a document **[R]**. The file keeps it as what he told people, not as what he did.

**Where he landed is a gap.** The sources say London and none names a dock. The file uses **Tilbury (51.46, 0.36)**, the era's usual long-haul terminus, and says so in date_confidence: a placeholder, not a finding.

## 3. Europe, 1912-1924

**The itinerary [A: Wikipedia EN and ES, combined]:** London, Paris, Turin, Genoa, Zoagli, Tours, Marseille, Florence, Milan, Munich, Hamburg, with Zoagli near Genoa serving as a family base. Only a few carry dates. The file assigns inferred dates in travel order and marks them so: the 1912-1918 sequence is a plausible ordering of undated residences, not a calendar.

- **Paris, c. 1912-1914 [A: Wikipedia ES].** The pool attached a contact with **Der Blaue Reiter** here, following a sentence in the Spanish article that itself misplaces the group in Berlin; it was founded in **Munich in 1911**. Wrong city, unsupported placement, no source putting him in contact with it anywhere: **dropped**.
- **Zoagli and Genoa, c. 1912-1918 [A: Wikipedia EN].** Zoagli is his mother's town. The pool's coordinate was 1.4 km off; the file uses **44.3368, 9.2670**.
- **Tours and Marseille, wartime [A: Wikipedia EN].** Undated, compressed into one stop with Marseille named in the campa.
- **Florence, 1916 [A: Wikipedia ES].** Meets **Emilio Pettoruti**; the friendship runs to the end of his life. Adopts the name **Xul Solar**, lux reversed, beside his mother's surname minus a letter **[A: both Wikipedias; Caleb Bach 1994 in the same terms]**. *Flag:* the English article dates the change to 1916 without a city, the Spanish puts it in Florence. Not a contradiction, but the Florentine location rests on one source.
- **Milan, July 1918 to c. end of 1919 [A: Wikipedia ES, "Viaja a Milán en julio de 1918. Allí permanecería aproximadamente un año y medio"], serving at the Argentine consulate [A: Wikipedia EN].** **Correction applied:** the pool's chronology lens dated the consulate to **1915**, three years early, contradicting its own geography lens. The file follows 1918-1919.
- **London, November 1919 to May 1920 [A: Wikipedia ES]**. A separate claim of a **nine-month Paris stay in 1915 [R: Wikipedia ES]** fits nowhere in the Italian sequence and is left out of the file rather than forced into it.
- **Milan, 27 November to 16 December 1920: first solo exhibition, shared with the sculptor Arturo Martini [A: Wikipedia EN and ES].** The gallery is given as Galleria Arte; the street address could not be confirmed and a city-centre coordinate is used, flagged.
- **Munich, 1921 to December 1923 [A: Wikipedia ES].** **Correction applied.** The pool asserted "Bauhaus-related workshops" and engagement with "Paul Klee's circle." The Spanish article says only "tomaría contacto con las obras de este artista", the works and not the man; the English says nothing of Klee or the Bauhaus, which was in Weimar when Klee joined it in 1921. The file has him attending workshops and encountering the work.
- **Stuttgart, 1923: attends lectures by Rudolf Steiner [A: Wikipedia ES].** The cycle is not specified.
- **Paris, 15 March to 15 April 1924: Exposition d'Art Américain-Latin, Musée Galliera [A: Wikipedia EN].** The pool gave a generic Paris coordinate although the venue is named; the file uses the **Palais Galliera at 48.8657, 2.2965**, 4.2 km from the placeholder.
- **Hamburg, June 1924: sails for home with Pettoruti [A: Wikipedia ES].**

### The Crowley problem (flagged, not resolved)

The English article places a meeting with **Aleister Crowley** in **Paris in 1924**, with Crowley's partner **Leah Hirsig**, just before the return to Buenos Aires. The Spanish places it in **1914** and adds that Crowley taught him to take visions through the **I Ching**. Neither cites a primary source; Crowley's diaries were not consulted. The 1924 dating is better supported, sitting beside the documented June departure from Hamburg, while 1914 has no anchor. The file uses **1924, tagged as reconstruction, both accounts named in the campa**.

## 4. Return and the Florida group, 1924-1927

**Sails from Hamburg in June 1924, arrives Buenos Aires in July [A: Wikipedia ES, "Zarpa en junio de Hamburgo con Emilio Pettoruti, llegando a Buenos Aires en julio"].** **Correction applied:** the pool's geography lens dated the *arrival* to June, contradicting its own chronology lens.

Joins the **vanguardista Florida group** around *Martín Fierro* **[A: both Wikipedias]** and begins the friendship with **Jorge Luis Borges** **[A]**, which both articles treat as the central relationship of his adult life. Their shared reading (Blake, Swedenborg) is the English article's characterization **[A: Wikipedia EN]**.

- **9 October 1924:** publishes in *Martín Fierro* on Pettoruti's first Buenos Aires exhibition **[A: the dated article, quoted in the literature]**. Quoted in full in the journey file.
- **December 1924:** first Argentine showing, **Primer Salón Libre, Galería Witcomb, Calle Florida [A: Wikipedia ES]**. The precise Witcomb address was not verified; a Florida-street coordinate is used and flagged.
- **November 1925:** Primer Salón de Artistas Independientes **[A: Wikipedia ES]**. No stop in the file, for length.
- **1926:** *Exposición de Pintores Modernos* at **Amigos del Arte**, with **Norah Borges** and Pettoruti; **F. T. Marinetti** visits Buenos Aires the same season **[A: both Wikipedias]**. **Correction applied:** the pool placed Amigos del Arte in Recoleta; it was at **Florida 659** in the Microcentro, and the file uses **-34.6004, -58.3753**.
- **1927:** paints *Drago* **[A: Wikipedia ES]**, folded into the 1929 stop.

## 5. Laprida 1212, 1928-1946

**November 1928: the family acquires the house on Laprida [A: Wikipedia ES].** Three problems in the pool's version:

1. **Street number.** The Spanish article says **1214**, the museum's own address is **1212**. The file uses 1212 and notes the discrepancy in date_confidence.
2. **"Remained his residence until 1957"** is **unsupported [R]**: the Spanish article's 1957 entry concerns his writing for *Lyra*, not a change of residence. Dropped; the 1957 move to the delta appears separately, tagged as reconstruction.
3. **The San Isidro question.** The brief that opened this subject put the museum in **San Isidro**. It is not there: both Wikipedias and the museum's own site give **Laprida 1212, Barrio Norte / Recoleta, city of Buenos Aires**. The error is probably confusion with the birthplace region (San Fernando adjoins San Isidro) or with Villa Ocampo. **Flagged, not silently absorbed.** The pool's pin was also 1.1 km out; the file uses **-34.5945, -58.4079**.

- **May 1929:** **62 works** at Amigos del Arte **[A: Wikipedia ES]**.
- **mid-1930:** finishes the **San Signos**, visions worked through the I Ching, largely in **neocriollo [A: Wikipedia ES]**. **Correction applied:** the pool dated it 1930 flat and glossed it "Book of Heaven"; the Spanish says "a mediados de 1930" and the gloss is in no source consulted. Removed.
- **1931:** publishes neocriollo texts in Argentine magazines **[A: Wikipedia ES]**. Neocriollo fuses Spanish and Portuguese and he is said to have spoken it; **panlengua / panlingua** is the more ambitious universal language grounded in number, astrology and music **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **1938:** develops **pensiformes**, a plastic writing derived from shorthand **[A: Wikipedia ES]**. No stop of its own.
- **1939:** begins drawing astrological charts and opens the **Pan Klub** at the house **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **1940:** **named in** Borges's *Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius* as the translator of Tlönian sentences ("Xul Solar traduce con brevedad") **[A: the story itself]**. **Correction applied:** the pool said he "appeared as a character (a version of himself)". He is cited by name as a real person. The fictionalized Xul Solar is instead the astrologer **Schultze** in Marechal's *Adán Buenosayres* (1948) **[R: literary tradition]**, left out as reception.
- **July 1940:** 25 works at Amigos del Arte **[A: Wikipedia ES]**, folded into the monochrome stop.
- **1941:** monochromatic phase begins **[A: Wikipedia ES]**; Gradowczyk ties the ascetic paintings of **1943-1944**, such as *Fiordo*, to the war **[A as his judgement]**.
- **1943:** lectures on astrology in **Rosario [R: Wikipedia ES]**.
- **1944:** illustrates a **300-copy** edition of *Un modelo para la muerte*, by Borges and **Adolfo Bioy Casares** writing jointly as **B. Suárez Lynch [A: Wikipedia EN]**.
- **c. 1945:** **panajedrez**, the **marionette I Ching**, the tarot, the piano notation, the duodecimal system **[R: both Wikipedias; board dimensions given differently in different places, one saying 13x13]**. A copy of the Pan Game and Marionette I Ching is reported at **MoMA, New York [R: not verified against MoMA's catalogue]**.
- **13 August 1946:** marries **Micaela "Lita" Cadenas [A: Wikipedia ES]**. **Correction applied:** the Spanish article says 59 and 44. Born 14 December 1887, he was **58** that August; her 44 is left as given. The claim that they met about 1936 at astrological gatherings **could not be confirmed [R]**.
- **1947:** manifesto describing his own inventions, for the magazine ***Él*** **[A: Wikipedia ES]**.

## 6. Kabbalah, the delta, and the end, 1951-1963

- **From 1951 into the mid-decade:** paintings on the **kabbalah [A: Wikipedia ES]**. **Correction applied:** the pool paired this with a conceptual **"Pan-árbol"**, which appears nowhere in the retrieved text; dropped.
- **October 1953:** solo show *Pinturas y Dibujos* at **Galería Van Riel [A: Wikipedia ES]**. No coordinate could be established, so the stop was dropped rather than pinned to a guess: a real event without a place.
- **1954:** buys a house on the **Río Luján at Villa La Ñata**, Tigre, and names it **Li-Tao [A: Wikipedia ES]**. **Correction applied, the largest geographic error in the pool:** the given pin (-34.4083, -58.5792) falls about 11 km away, near Victoria/San Fernando. The file uses **-34.3669, -58.6842** for every Li-Tao stop, reconciling three inconsistent pins for one house.
- **1957:** moves to the delta house permanently **[R: Wikipedia ES, no documentary support]**.
- **15 May 1960:** receives a **Certificado de Bautizo Espiritual [A: Wikipedia ES]**. **Correction applied:** the pool called it a "Kriya Yoga initiation certificate"; the Spanish term is the Self-Realization Fellowship's own and the file follows it. Whether a kriya initiation accompanied it is nowhere stated.
- **July 1961:** exhibits in **Rio de Janeiro [A: Wikipedia ES]**.
- **1962:** included in ***Art argentin actuel*** at the **Musée National d'Art Moderne**, then in the **Palais de Tokyo [A: Wikipedia ES]**. **Two corrections:** the pool called it "a major retrospective... his only significant European showing", unsupported (it is a group show of current Argentine art), and its pin sat 1.8 km from the 1962 museum site. The file uses **48.8640, 2.2965**.
- **9 April 1963:** dies at the Tigre house, aged 75, his wife beside him, **holding a wooden rosary of seventy-one carved beads** ("un rosario de setenta y una piezas talladas en madera") **[A: Wikipedia ES]**. **Borges delivers the eulogy [A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **October 1963:** *Homenaje a Xul Solar*, **93 works** at the **Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes**, prologue by Borges **[A: Wikipedia ES]**.
- **13 May 1993:** the **Museo Xul Solar** opens in the Laprida house, founded by Lita Cadenas through the **Fundación Pan Klub [A: Wikipedia ES, 'Museo Xul Solar'; museum site]**. The foundation holds exclusive rights over his work **[A: xulsolar.org.ar]**; the library of roughly **3,500 volumes** stays on site and the Pan Klub goes on operating inside the museum **[A: Wikipedia ES]**.

**Withdrawn claim.** The pool carried, as **[A]**, a **biography of Xul Solar published by Pettoruti in 1968**. This is almost certainly a misreading of the English article's "Pettoruti published his biography five years later," where the antecedent of "his" is ambiguous: Pettoruti's actual 1968 book is ***Un pintor ante el espejo***, his own memoir, which discusses Xul Solar. No such biography is documented anywhere checked. Withdrawn, and given no stop.

## 7. Interlocks with existing atlas files

Named in a campa only where the relation is his own, not merely shared ground:

- **`borges`**: the central friendship, attested from 1924 to the eulogy of 1963; named in five campas (the meeting, neocriollo, *Tlön*, the Suárez Lynch edition, the 1963 homage). **[A]**
- **`paul_klee`**: encountered Klee's work in Munich, 1921-1923 **[A, corrected: the works, not the man]**; Demaria's 1971 essay in *Lyra* pairs the two painters.
- **`steiner`**: whose Stuttgart lectures he traveled to hear in 1923 **[A]**.
- **`william_blake`**: the shared Blake-and-Swedenborg reading is the English article's own account of what drew Xul Solar and Borges together **[A: Wikipedia EN]**.
- **`etteilla`**: named once, in the Pan Klub stop, as originator of the reading deck whose form Xul Solar's tarot inherits. A form-lineage relation, not co-location; the `etteilla` file already names him as the Buenos Aires instance of it. **[R as to any direct knowledge of Etteilla on his part.]**

Declined for the campas, the relation being shared milieu or shared geography and nothing more: `victoria_ocampo`, `le_corbusier`, `keyserling`, `mario_palanti`, `rene_guenon`, `blavatsky`. Two real relations run the other way in time and are recorded here instead of in prose: **`leo_chiachio`**, whose file names Xul Solar as the nearest Argentine precedent for his work, and **`cecilia_vicuna`**, who translated the neocriollo and wrote the 2004 essay four quotations come from (she appears in quote_source fields, the honest place for her). `ral_veroni` is queued, not researched.

## 8. Gaps stated as gaps

1. **The grave.** No source consulted, in either language, names the cemetery or tomb; searches for "Xul Solar cementerio / sepultura" returned nothing usable.
2. **The pre-1912 work.** Nothing survives or is catalogued in reachable sources.
3. **Publication of the inventions.** No standalone edition or translation of the tarot, the panajedrez rules or the *San Signos* could be found; their existence outside the Fundación Pan Klub's holdings is unestablished.
4. **The 1912-1918 chronology** is a set of city names, not a calendar; every date the file assigns inside it is inferred and marked so.
5. **Three venue coordinates are placeholders**, each flagged: the Colegio Nacional Sección Norte, the Galleria Arte in Milan, the Galería Witcomb.
6. **The 2012-2013 Guggenheim / MALBA retrospective**, *Xul Solar: Visions and Revelations*, is real in art-press memory but could not be confirmed against a live source, those pages returning 404 or unreadable content **[R]**. It is absent from the file, which ends in 1993.
7. **The Fundación Pan Klub archive** was not consulted. It holds the *San Signos* manuscript, the games, the tarot, the library and the 1960 certificate, and is where the next advance lies.
