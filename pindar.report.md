# Pindar of Thebes: research report
*Slug `pindar`. Compiled 2026-08-23. Companion file: `pindar.journey.json`, 7 segments, 37 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, legend, or modern inference. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

The hardest fact about Pindar is that the poems are secure and the man is not. Forty-five complete victory odes survive with datable occasions, patrons, victors, and towns attached; the biography survives in late Lives that disagree about his father, his birthplace, his teachers, and how he died. Everything below keeps the two apart, and in the journey file the hedging lives in `date_confidence`, never in the prose.

---

## 1. Birth and Boeotian formation (c. 518 to c. 500 BCE)

- Birth dated to the 65th Olympiad, c. 518 BCE, at Thebes in Boeotia. **[R: Wikipedia, 'Pindar', on the chronographic tradition and the Vitae.]** No contemporary document fixes the year; no house is identified.
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** Some Lives give the birthplace as **Cynoscephalae**, "Dog's Heads," a village near Thebes. **[R]** Nothing adjudicates between the traditions, and the village has never been located, so its pin (38.34, 23.29) is an approximate placement, declared as such in `date_confidence`. The Cynoscephalae / Cynocephali confusion (Boeotian paired hills, not Ctesias's dog-headed men) is standard, but the ancient citation, probably the *Vita Ambrosiana* or the Suda, could not be located.
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** The father is named variously **Daiphantus, Pagondas, or Scopelinus**; the mother is usually **Cleodice**. **[R: ancient Lives.]** The Lives do not argue with each other, they simply differ, and the disagreement is itself evidence of a late assembled tradition rather than a preserved family record.
- The bee legend (bees on the infant's lips, honey left there, the "honeyed" style prefigured) is a standard inspired-child topos attached to other poets and philosophers. **[R]** Treated in the file as literary convention, not event; Pindar's own honey-bee image at Pythian 10.51-54 is separately **[A]**.
- Study at Athens under **Lasus of Hermione**, with a variant naming **Agathocles**. **[R: late testimony only.]** No contemporary confirmation. What the odes independently show is technical training in choral composition, an inference of the file's own, flagged as reconstruction.
- **Corinna** as teacher, critic, or rival, with the "sow with the hand, not the whole sack" rebuke and the "Boeotian sow" retort. **[R: ancient anecdote.]** **Contradiction flagged:** Corinna's dates are disputed and some put her well after Pindar, which would make the exchange impossible. Both anecdote and objection are carried in the file.
- **Pan heard singing a paean of Pindar's** in the Boeotian hill country. **[R]** Normally cited to **Pausanias 9.23**, stronger than the dossier's "later Lives and commentary," but Book 9 could not be fetched, so the upgrade is unconfirmed and the file says so. **Coordinate correction applied:** the pool's "Helicon / Cithaeron border" at 38.3167, 22.8333 is not a border, since Cithaeron lies 40 km away at 38.1842, 23.2492 and the point sits on Helicon itself. The stop is relabelled "Mount Helicon" at the corpus pin **38.3528, 22.8225**.

## 2. Pythian 10, 498 BCE: the earliest surviving poem

The best-documented episode in the file, and the only one where text, date, patron, victor, and occasion all hold together.

- **Hippocleas of Pelinna** wins the boys' *diaulos* at the Pythian games; the scholia date the ode to **498 BCE**, the earliest securely dated surviving poem, composed at about twenty. Commissioned by **Thorax of Larissa** of the ruling Aleuadae. **[A: Pythian 10, with scholia vetera; Race, Loeb 1997.]**
- The ode names **Pytho, Pelinna, and the Aleuadae** at 10.4-5 and credits the victor's father **Phricias** with two Olympic victories in the race in armour. **[A: Perseus text confirmed.]** **Coordinate correction applied:** the pool's Pelinna, 39.5236/21.9484, is about 6 km off; the ancient town (Palaiogardiki, Trikala) is at **39.5757, 21.9265**, which the file uses.
- **Correction applied, source-level.** The pool twice asserted that the close praises Thorax's brothers **Eurypylus and Thrasydaeus by name**. It does not: Perseus/Svarlien reads only "We shall further praise his noble brothers, because they exalt and strengthen the traditional laws of the Thessalians." The names come from the **scholia**. The journey file says so and does not present them as Pindar's text.
- **The Hyperborea passage, all [A] as text** (Pythian 10.27-54, Myers fetched from Wikisource): no road by ship or on foot; Perseus alone entering the halls and feasting while they sacrifice hecatombs of asses to a laughing Apollo; the Muse never absent, maiden choruses, lyres, shrill pipes, golden bay in the hair; no sickness, no wasting age, no toil, no war, no Nemesis; then Athena leading Perseus on to the Gorgon, and the poet "anchoring" the excursus.
- **Geographic decision.** Hyperborea gets **no map pin** anywhere in the file: the poem's own claim is that no road reaches it, and pinning it would contradict the text. All four Hyperborea stops sit at the **Delphi** pin, where the ode was performed, and each `date_confidence` says so.
- Modern reading: **Maria Pavlou, 'Localizing Pindar's Pythian 10', Mnemosyne 76 (2023): 375-393, DOI 10.1163/1568525x-bja10107.** **[A for the citation via Crossref; R for the argument, full text not retrievable.]** **Correction applied:** the pool gave two contradictory subtitles; Crossref carries **none**, so at least one was invented. The file cites the bare title.

## 3. The Persian invasion and the Athens fragment (480-478 BCE)

- Theban medism and Theban troops in the Persian line at Plataea. **[A: standard, Herodotus 9.31-32.]**
- **Correction applied.** The pool claimed Pindar's conduct in the invasion "is not documented by any surviving text." False: **Polybius 4.31** quotes a Pindaric verse counselling the citizens to seek "the light of lordly Peace" and attacks the poet by name for urging Thebes into the inaction that brought it disgrace. **[A: penelope.uchicago.edu, fetched.]** The file's formulation: no *contemporary* record, but one hostile later text reads a Pindaric fragment as advocacy of quietism.
- **Fragment 76**, hailing Athens as "bulwark of Hellas." **[A for the fragment's existence and wording in the quotation tradition; R for venue, occasion, and date.]** The anecdote that **Athens rewarded him and Thebes fined him** for it is **[R]**: no judicial or civic record survives, and the file says so in prose and in `date_confidence`.
- **The Athens statue.** **Pausanias 1.8.4** (fetched, Frazer/Jones, Gutenberg 68946) reports a statue of Pindar among the honorific monuments of the agora area, "because he praised them in an Ode," beside an image of the lawgiver Calades. **[A]** Not in the original dossier; one of the two new primary-text confirmations in the pool. No erection date is given, so the file's date is declared an ordering device and the monument may be posthumous.

- **Correction applied.** Pythian 4/5 are **462/461**, not a flat 462; the file's `date_confidence` uses the range.
- **Sicilian and Acragantine travel is [R], not [A].** The commissions are textual and certain; his presence at Syracuse, Acragas, or Aetna is inferred and asserted only by late Lives, and the court-rivalry stories with Simonides and Bacchylides are scholiastic inference. The file's prose says exactly that.
- **Aegina.** Roughly a quarter of the surviving epinikia honour Aeginetan victors, tied to the Aeacid hero cult. **[A: a count from the corpus.]** Residence on the island is not established by the count and is not claimed.
- **Olympian 7 inscribed in gold letters at the temple of Athena Lindia.** **[R]** No stone, no ancient inventory; kept as a tradition about the ode's prestige.
- **Pythian 4's plea for Damophilus** (Demophilus) is textual **[A]**. Whether Pindar knew the exile or held any assigned diplomatic role is unresolved and left so.
- **Pythian 8's "dream of a shadow."** **[R as a quotable line.]** Universally cited, but no verbatim translation could be verified this pass, so the file gives **no `quote` field** for it and paraphrases instead.

## 5. Corpus and transmission

- Seventeen books by genre in the Alexandrian edition, associated with **Aristophanes of Byzantium**; Pindar canonized among the nine lyric poets. Only the four epinician books survive complete: **14 Olympians, 12 Pythians, 11 Nemeans, 8 Isthmians = 45**. **[A: Wikipedia, Race's Loeb introduction, with P.Oxy. 2438 for the ordering.]** Some odes filed as "Nemean" were not won at Nemea; that heading records a filing decision as much as a festival. **[A]**
- **Correction applied, twice.** The pool twice stated that "the opening of the Isthmian book is lost." Backwards: the manuscripts break off **after Isthmian 8** and Race prints a fragmentary **Isthmian 9**. The loss is at the **end**, and both places are written correctly in the journey file.
- **Coordinate corrections applied**, both matching pins already in corpus use: **Nemea** from 37.8817, 22.7042 (about 8 km north, in open hills) to **37.8089, 22.7103**; **Isthmia** from 37.9219, 23.0034 to **37.9159, 22.9932**.
- **P.Oxy. XXVI 2438**, a damaged papyrus Life of Pindar, palaeographically late second or early third century CE, published 1961. **[A]** Database not re-queried. **Bundy's *Studia Pindarica* is 1962**, not 1961; the dates are kept apart and Bundy is a `suggested_ref`, not a stop.
- **Correction applied.** The pool credited **Grenfell and Hunt** with the Bacchylides papyrus. Per Wikipedia 'Bacchylides' (fetched), it was acquired in Egypt by **E. A. Wallis Budge** in 1896, and **Frederic Kenyon** reassembled 1,382 lines and published twenty poems in 1897. The dates and the effect on Pindaric scholarship (much of Pindar's supposed idiosyncrasy turning out to be genre convention) hold. Kenyon appears in the journey file only as a source line.

## 6. Death and posthumous cult

- Death at **Argos**, at about eighty, conventionally c. 438 BCE. **[R: late Lives.]** The only firm bracket is Pythian 8 of 446. **Contradictions, unresolved and left side by side in the file:** death in the theatre; death on the shoulder of **Theoxenus of Tenedos**; and the **Persephone** legend, in which the goddess complains she is the only deity he never hymned and he dictates her poem in a dream after his death. Competing variants, not one account.
- **Fragment 123**, addressed to Theoxenus, an explicit statement of erotic desire for a young man, inside the conventions of elite Greek lyric. **[A as a standard citation; not re-fetched from a critical edition.]** The file states its character without inventing its wording and declares place and date of composition unknown.
- Daughters **Eumetis and Protomache** carrying the ashes from Argos to Thebes. **[R: late Lives.]**
- **The iron chair at Delphi** and the herald's nightly summons to the god's supper at the Theoxenia **[R]**, attributed to **Pausanias 10.24.5**, unfetchable this session and reported at second hand. Likewise **the shrine of the Mother of the Gods** by Pindar's house at Thebes with its vision of the goddess **[R]**, resting on Pausanias Book 9; the file says plainly that the shrine is easier to credit than the apparition.
- **Alexander spares Pindar's house, 335 BCE.** **[A]** for the bare fact, Arrian, *Anabasis* 1.9, fetched: "It is said that Alexander preserved the house and the descendants of Pindar the poet, out of respect for his memory." **[R]** for the motive: the ancestor-verses explanation is **not in Arrian**, and the pool's attribution of it to him was wrong. The file splits the two and quotes Arrian verbatim.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Pindar is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. The relations below are **not rooted in his own life** and are **kept out of the journey file but recorded rather than deleted**, for the operator to rule on. Items 1 and 2 are **FORWARD**: he died c. 438 BCE, and each would make his file embed an artifact that did not exist when his clock stopped. Nothing in the exceptions table licenses that; it would need a **forward grant** of the kind recorded for Burroughs and Derrida.

1. **The Hyperborea thread (FORWARD, not used).** `pytheas_de_massalia.journey.json`, `serrano.journey.json`, and `maria_orsic.journey.json` all run Hyperborea material. Pythian 10 (498 BCE) is the earliest surviving Greek text carrying the full later inventory: a blessed, inaccessible people, ageless, sickness-free, warless, workless, favoured by Apollo, feasting without end. Blavatsky's root-races, the Thule Society, Savitri Devi's "Hyperborean descent," and Serrano's Antarctica-as-Hyperborea inherit that content wholesale, and the Pytheas file's own section "Hyperborea: the Name Is Taken" says "Hyperborea is older than Pytheas and he never used the word", acknowledging a pre-Pytheas source without naming it. **[A] for Pythian 10's content, [R] for the genealogy.** Two routes: a forward grant for Pindar, or, needing no grant, a **source line on those three files** pointing back at Pythian 10, a legitimate backward embed on each.
2. **Simonides of Ceos (FORWARD, and a wrong-owner case; not used).** `cicero.journey.json` carries the Crannon banquet-collapse scene (*De Oratore*, 55 BCE) in which Simonides founds the art of memory. Simonides was Pindar's rival in the same patronage market, Thessalian houses and Sicilian courts, in the same decades, and that rivalry is a real Pindaric relation. But Crannon is Simonides's scene, told by Cicero, and Simonides has no atlas file. Nothing is staged there; Simonides is named only in one `date_confidence` note, Cicero not at all.
3. **Homer and Hesiod (BACKWARD, used).** Pindar draws his genealogies and heroic matter from Hesiodic and Homeric epic: a one-directional influence reaching back, needing no exception. Both are named once each, in the Mount Helicon stop and the "matter the poems are made of" stop. Nothing is written into their files.
4. **Alexander (used).** `alexander.journey.json` holds the Theban stop at **38.317, 23.317, -0335-09-01** and Pindar's own house is its object. The reciprocal stop inherits coordinates and date byte-identically and is framed on the house and descendants, not the razing, which stays Alexander's scene. If the operator reads that as a forward embed too far, the stop lifts out cleanly.
5. **Delos (not used).** Flagged in the pool as a pin for the Hyperborean-offerings relay, which is Herodotus's material; no attested Pindaric link to Delos was found.

**Two transmission stops sit at places Pindar never went**, both declaring it in `date_confidence`: Alexandria (the seventeen books) and Oxyrhynchus (the papyrus Life). Their subject is his own corpus and his own biography, not another traveller's visit, and no atlas traveller is named in either. Flagged so the operator can strike them if the rule is read more strictly.

## Corrections carried out of the pool

Nine of the pool's ten flagged corrections are documented in place above: the scholiastic brothers, Polybius 4.31, the Isthmian book's defective end (twice), Pythian 4/5 as 462/461, Bundy 1962 against P.Oxy. XXVI 1961, Pavlou's absent subtitle, Arrian's missing ancestor motive, Budge and Kenyon for Grenfell and Hunt, and four coordinates. The tenth belongs nowhere else: Herodotus's Hyperborean offering-relay runs Hyperboreans, Scythians, then hand to hand west to the Adriatic, Dodona, the Maliac Gulf, Euboea, Carystus, Tenos, Delos. **Sinope is not in it** (Herodotus names Sinope at 4.12 for an unrelated Cimmerian settlement), and the Arimaspians and Issedones are not links in the chain; 4.32 says the opposite. That material is Herodotus's and appears nowhere in the journey file.

## Honest gaps

- **No primary Life of Pindar was read.** Every biographical claim passes through Wikipedia's summary of the ancient Vitae or through the dossier; the Vitae, the Suda entry, and P.Oxy. 2438's text were not opened.
- **Pausanias is unreachable and matters four times.** Book 9 (the Mother of the Gods shrine, the Pan story) and Book 10 (the iron chair, the Theoxenia summons) failed against Perseus, Theoi, Wikisource, Attalus, ToposText, and Gutenberg. Only 1.8.4, the Athens statue, was fetched. Every claim resting on Books 9 or 10 is marked secondhand.
- **No Loeb or Perseus English text of Pythian 10 loaded.** All eight quotes are **Ernest Myers, 1874**, public domain, fetched from Wikisource. Race's Loeb is cited as a reference, never quoted.
- **Three modern works are cited but unread**: Brown (1992), Pavlou (2023), Burnett (2005). All real and checkable; none opened.
- **Cynoscephalae is unlocated.** Its pin is a placement, not a site. **Dating outside the securely dated odes is conventional**, resting on scholia and modern consensus rather than documents.
- **Nothing is known about how Pindar travelled.** No itinerary, no dated arrival, no letter, no host named as a host. Every place in the file except Delphi, Thebes, and Argos is a place where a commission landed, and the file says so rather than staging arrivals.
