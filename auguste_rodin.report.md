# Auguste Rodin (1840-1917): research report
*Subject slug: `auguste_rodin`. Companion file: `auguste_rodin.journey.json` (45 stops, 8 segments).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in brackets. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on a single compiled biography rather than a document. Contradictions are printed as contradictions and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method.** Four research lenses were run over the subject (chronology, geography, quotations, interlock with travelers already in the atlas), then verified adversarially against Wikipedia in three languages, the Musée Rodin's official biography, and three full public-domain texts on Project Gutenberg. The pass overturned nine items outright; those reversals are carried into the journey file and listed under their own heading below, because each is an error in wide circulation that will reappear in any future pass.

---

## I. Paris origins, 1840-1863

- Born **12 November 1840** at 3 rue de l'Arbalète, Mouffetard quarter, Paris. Father a clerk in the prefecture of police, mother a seamstress. **[A: Musée Rodin, official biography; en.wikipedia, "Auguste Rodin"]**
- Boarded **1851-1853** at a pension in Beauvais run by his uncle Jean-Hyppolite Rodin. **[R: Grunfeld (1987), relayed; the primary text was not consulted]**
- Entered the **Petite École** in **1854**, under Horace Lecoq de Boisbaudran, who let students draw from memory and from the live model rather than from casts. The only formal training Rodin ever received. **[A: Musée Rodin biography; fr.wikipedia]**
- **Three consecutive rejections** from the École des Beaux-Arts, **1857-1859**. **[A: Musée Rodin biography; en.wikipedia]**
- **1862**: his sister Maria dies; Rodin enters the **Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament** as a laybrother. **[A: en.wikipedia]** Father Pierre-Julien Eymard tells him his vocation is sculpture **[R: Musée Rodin biography, reporting the order's own tradition]**. **1863**: he leaves and models a **bust of Eymard**, his first datable work. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

## II. The craftsman's two decades, 1864-1876

- **1864**: enters the studio of **Albert-Ernest Carrier-Belleuse** as an ornamenter, and meets **Rose Beuret**. Their son **Auguste-Eugène Beuret** is born in **1866**. **[A: en.wikipedia]** **1869**: unsigned bas-reliefs for the façade of the **Théâtre des Gobelins**. **[A: Musée Rodin biography]**
- **1870**: mobilized briefly into the **National Guard** during the siege of Paris; discharged for myopia. **1871**: leaves for **Brussels** with Carrier-Belleuse for the sculpture of the new **Bourse**, staying six years in partnership with Antoine van Rasbourgh. **[A: Musée Rodin biography; Grunfeld for the partnership]** *Correction carried:* the pool filed both the Guard service and the Brussels move under 1871. They are a year apart; the journey file splits them.
- **1875**: a **two-month Italian journey**, self-funded: Turin, Genoa, Pisa, Florence, Rome, Naples, Venice. Michelangelo's Medici Chapel figures are the decisive encounter. **[A for the journey and its effect: Musée Rodin biography; en.wikipedia. R for the itinerary: Grunfeld.]**
- **1876**: a monument to the Antwerp politician Jean-François Loos **[R: Musée Rodin biography]**; a cast of his work reaches the **Philadelphia Centennial Exposition**, the first of his sculptures to cross the Atlantic without him. **[A: Musée Rodin biography]**

## III. The Age of Bronze and the scandal, 1877-1880

- **January 1877**, Cercle Artistique et Littéraire, **Brussels**: *L'Âge d'airain* is exhibited, then shown the same year at the **Paris Salon**. Critics accuse Rodin of **surmoulage**, casting from a living model. He rebuts the charge with photographs of the Belgian soldier Auguste Neyt, held today by the Musée Rodin. The source notes the suspicion increased public interest rather than destroying him. **[A: en.wikipedia, "The Age of Bronze"]**
- **1880**: the state buys the work; Undersecretary **Edmond Turquet** commissions a bronze portal, **The Gates of Hell**, for a Museum of Decorative Arts that is never built, and Rodin receives a studio at the **Dépôt des marbres**. The door occupies him thirty-seven years and is never delivered; most of his famous single figures are extracted from it. **[A: en. and fr.wikipedia; Musée Rodin biography]**

## IV. The Gates, Claudel, and the monuments, 1880-1898

- ***Le Penseur*** is modeled as a figure of the poet Dante for the lintel of the Gates. The date is **not** settled here: fr.wikipedia "Auguste Rodin" gives **1882**; fr.wikipedia "Le Penseur" dates the first plaster (71 x 37 x 57.5 cm) to **1880**; en.wikipedia "The Thinker" says "around 1881". The journey file writes **c. 1880-1882**. **[Contradiction flagged.]**
- **1883**: **Camille Claudel**, eighteen, enters Rodin's circle while he supervises a course for young sculptors. Student, praticienne, collaborator on the Gates, lover. **[A: Musée Rodin biography; en.wikipedia; `camille_claudel.journey.json`]**
- **1884**: first bronze cast of *The Thinker* (now National Gallery of Victoria, Melbourne); the **Burghers of Calais** commission begins. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1887**: Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur. **[A: fr.wikipedia]** See section IX on the rank sequence.
- **1889**: the Burghers are shown publicly for the first time in **Paris**, at the Monet-Rodin exhibition, Galerie Georges Petit; the **French State** commissions a Monument to Victor Hugo. **1891**: the **Société des Gens de Lettres** commissions the **Monument to Balzac**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1893**: shown at the **World's Columbian Exposition**, Chicago; some pieces are screened from the public for their nudity. **[A: Musée Rodin biography]** **1894**: visits **Claude Monet** at Giverny. **[A: fr.wikipedia: "Claude Monet l'invite chez lui, en 1894, à Giverny"]**
- **3 June 1895**: the **Burghers of Calais** is inaugurated at Calais before the **Parc Richelieu**, on a large pedestal *contrary to Rodin's wishes*. The move to the Hôtel de Ville, and the low base matching his intention, come later. **[A: en.wikipedia, "The Burghers of Calais"; fr.wikipedia]** The same year he buys the **Villa des Brillants** at Meudon.
- **1898**: the Balzac plaster is exhibited to a hostile press; the Société rejects it as unfinished. Rodin refuses a subscription of 30,000 francs, repays the commission, and keeps the plaster at Meudon. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Monument to Balzac"]**

## V. 1900 and the international placement, 1900-1910

- **25 May 1900**, Buenos Aires: the **Monument to Sarmiento** is inaugurated in the **Parque Tres de Febrero**, Palermo, on the site of the **Caserón de Rosas**, demolished on the night of 2-3 February 1899, a date chosen for the anniversary of Rosas's fall in 1852. It reached Argentina through the museum director **Eduardo Schiaffino**. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **1900**, Paris: the **Pavillon de l'Alma**, a private retrospective of over 150 works built at Rodin's expense outside the official grounds of the Exposition Universelle. He meets **Isadora Duncan** there. Portrait commissions reach roughly 200,000 francs a year. **[A: Musée Rodin biography; en.wikipedia; `isadora_duncan.journey.json`]**
- **1902**: *The Thinker* is enlarged to monumental scale. **The height is not agreed**: 189 cm (fr.wikipedia, "Le Penseur"), 181 cm (en.wikipedia), 185 cm (the pool). The journey file writes "about 180 to 190 cm". **[Flagged.]** **1904**: the full-scale figure is shown at the Salon des Beaux-Arts; a subscription cast, city property of Paris, is sited before the **Panthéon** in 1906. **[A: en.wikipedia, "The Thinker"]**
- **1905**: **Rainer Maria Rilke** works as Rodin's secretary at Meudon and later writes a monograph on him. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1906**: **Marseille**, colonial exposition: Rodin follows the Cambodian Royal Ballet for days, filling notebooks with watercolour studies. **[A: Musée Rodin biography]**
- **November 1907**, Buenos Aires: a bronze *El Pensador*, **one of two casts taken from Rodin's original mould and signed by him**, is inaugurated in the eastern section of the **Plaza del Congreso**. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Plaza del Congreso"]**
- **1907**: honorary doctorate, **University of Oxford**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **January 1910**: the Plaza del Congreso, redesigned by **Carlos Thays** under National Law 6,286 of 30 September 1908, is inaugurated for the Argentine centenary. The Rodin bronze was already standing; Thays laid the plaza out around it. **[A: es.wikipedia]**

## VI. Hôtel Biron to Meudon, 1908-1917

- **19 January 1908**: Rodin writes from Meudon to **Loïe Fuller**, calling her a woman of genius who has "reawakened the spirit of antiquity". She prints the letter in her memoir. **[A: `loie_fuller.journey.json`]**
- **1908**: rents the ground floor of the **Hôtel Biron**, 77 rue de Varenne, let cheaply to artists awaiting the building's demolition (Rilke, Matisse, Cocteau). Rose Beuret stays at Meudon; Rodin begins an affair with the **Duchesse de Choiseul**. **[A: en.wikipedia; Musée Rodin biography]**
- **1911**: a cast of *The Walking Man* is installed at the **Palazzo Farnese**, Rome, then the French embassy to Italy. **[A: Musée Rodin biography]**
- **29 May 1912**: *L'Après-midi d'un Faune*, danced by **Vaslav Nijinsky**, is denounced by *Le Figaro*; Rodin defends it in print and is attacked for doing so. **[A: `vaslav_nijinsky.journey.json`]**
- **1914**: donates works to the **British nation**. The **Burghers** cast bought in 1911 is positioned in **Victoria Tower Gardens** in 1914, unveiled **19 July 1915**. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Victoria Tower Gardens", "The Burghers of Calais"]**
- **1915**: to Rome, to model a bust of **Pope Benedict XV**; the sittings are short and the bust is left unfinished. **[A: Musée Rodin biography]** **1916**: the **Musée Rodin** is founded by law on his donation of works, collections and casting rights to the state. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **29 January 1917**: marries **Rose Beuret** at Meudon after fifty-three years. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **Mid-February 1917**: Rose Beuret dies. **The date is contradicted**: en.wikipedia gives 16 February; fr.wikipedia "Rose Beuret" and "Villa des Brillants" both give 14 February. **[Flagged. The journey file dates the stop 1917-02-14 and carries the conflict in `date_confidence`.]**
- **17 November 1917**: Rodin dies at the Villa des Brillants, aged 77, of **congestion of the lungs** (en.wikipedia: "congestion of the lungs has caused great weakness"). The pool's afterlife lens wrote "severe influenza", which no consulted source supports.
- **24 November 1917**: buried in the grounds beside Rose Beuret, with a bronze *Thinker* set over the grave as headstone. **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Villa des Brillants"]**

## VII. The Buenos Aires dossier

Three Rodin sites in one city, two of them firm.

1. **Sarmiento**, Parque Tres de Febrero, 25 May 1900. **[A: es.wikipedia]** The framing that it is "the only work Rodin created expressly for a Buenos Aires site" comes from the brief and **could not be corroborated**; recorded as a gap.
2. ***El Pensador***, Plaza del Congreso, **November 1907**. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Plaza del Congreso"]** The 1909 date circulating in the pool has **no source support in any page reached**. *Attribution correction:* the pool sourced the mould-and-signature detail to es.wikipedia "El pensador", which contains none of it.
3. **Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes**. The figure of **19 sculptures and 3 drawings** comes from the brief and **remains unverified**: bellasartes.gob.ar returned **HTTP 403**. Only a marble ***El beso*** is confirmed present, and its provenance is contradicted: es.wikipedia "El beso (Rodin)" says Rodin donated it, es.wikipedia "Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes (Argentina)" says it was bought in the 1940s. **[Flagged.]**

## VIII. Posthumous

- **4 August 1919**: the **Musée Rodin** opens at the Hôtel Biron. **[A: en.wikipedia]** The street number is **77 rue de Varenne**, not 79; the pool carried both, inconsistently, in three places.
- **2 July 1939**: the Balzac is cast in bronze at last and installed at boulevard du Montparnasse and boulevard Raspail, forty-one years after its rejection. Kenneth Clark called it, in 1969, "the greatest piece of sculpture of the nineteenth century". **[A: en.wikipedia, "Monument to Balzac"]**
- **Casting limit**: French law restricts authorized posthumous casts to **twelve per edition**; the same source says the Burghers of Calais exists in **fourteen cities**. **Neither the source nor this report reconciles them.** **[Contradiction flagged.]**
- **Forgery**: a scheme trafficking fraudulent Rodin bronzes was uncovered in the **1990s**; the dealer **Guy Hain** was **convicted in 2001**. The pool implied a 1990s conviction. Rodin remains among the most-forged sculptors on the market. **[A: en.wikipedia; dating corrected]**
- **Monumental casts of *The Thinker***: about **29** worldwide, not all cast under Rodin's supervision. A 1928 Rudier cast was offered at Christie's Paris in **June 2022**, estimate reported up to 14 million euros. **[A: en.wikipedia, "The Thinker"]**

## IX. Corrections carried from the verification pass

Nine items in the research pool were wrong and are corrected in the journey file. Each is an error in wide circulation.

1. **Burghers of Calais, 1889.** Not unveiled at Calais in 1889; 1889 was the first public showing, in Paris. The Calais inauguration is 3 June 1895, before the Parc Richelieu, not the Hôtel de Ville.
2. **The pedestal at Calais.** Rodin lost that argument: the 1895 installation used a large pedestal against his wishes. Any account in which "Rodin insists and wins" is false.
3. **Monument to Victor Hugo, 1889.** Commissioned by the **French State**, not by the Société des Gens de Lettres, which commissioned the Balzac two years later.
4. **Légion d'honneur.** fr.wikipedia gives chevalier 1887, officier 1892, **commandeur 1903**, grand officier 1910. The pool paired the Commander rank with the 1914 British donation, an artefact of en.wikipedia naming it with no year.
5. **Buenos Aires *Pensador*: November 1907**, not 1909.
6. **Rose Beuret's death.** Not "two weeks" after the wedding on any reading: 29 January to 16 February is eighteen days, to 14 February sixteen. The pool mixed the two records.
7. **Monet and Rodin were born two days apart** (12 and 14 November 1840), not a month. "The greatest sculptor since Michelangelo", attributed to Monet, **could not be sourced** and is dropped.
8. **London Burghers.** Purchased 1911, positioned 1914, unveiled 19 July 1915. The claim that the London group stands without a pedestal "as Rodin always insisted" is unsupported.
9. **Coordinates.** Repaired: the Villa des Brillants (given 48.8117, 2.2418 and 48.8103, 2.235; correct 48.8147, 2.2518, per Nominatim for 19 avenue Auguste Rodin and fr.wikipedia's 48°48'51"N 2°15'10"E); the École des Beaux-Arts (given 48.8567, 2.335, which lands at Saint-Germain-des-Prés; correct about 48.8601, 2.3336); Calais (given 50.958, 1.8508, a kilometre north of both candidate sites); and **three Buenos Aires latitudes given as positive numbers**, which put the Sarmiento, the *Pensador* and the MNBA in the North Atlantic.

## X. Quotation attributions corrected

Three of the thirteen verified quotations were misattributed in the pool and are corrected in the journey file:

- **"Slowness is beauty."** Verbatim, but printed inside **James Huneker's** introduction to Judith Cladel's *Rodin: The Man and His Art* (1917), not in Cladel's own text. Huneker reports it in the context of Rodin praising the pace of Dutch life.
- **"No one may benefit mankind with impunity."** Same case: Huneker's introduction, not Cladel. Huneker does credit the remark to Rodin.
- **"One must not hurry."** Verbatim in Rilke's *Auguste Rodin*, but the English translation is **1919** (New York: Sunwise Turn, trans. Lemont and Trausil), not 1918.

The remaining ten are from Rodin's *Art: Conversations with Paul Gsell*, trans. Mrs. Romilly Fedden (1912), verified verbatim against Project Gutenberg #59799 and cited by chapter where the chapter is known.

## XI. Interlocks with travelers already in the atlas

Named in the journey file's prose, on the basis of a documented relation to Rodin himself:

- **camille_claudel**: student, praticienne on the Burghers, model and collaborator on the Gates, lover from 1883.
- **isadora_duncan**: meets him at the Pavillon de l'Alma in 1900, drawn repeatedly afterwards.
- **loie_fuller**: he wrote her the letter of 19 January 1908 that she later printed.
- **vaslav_nijinsky**: Rodin defended *L'Après-midi d'un Faune* in print in 1912 and was attacked for it.
- **dante**: the Gates of Hell take their whole program from the *Inferno*, and *The Thinker* was first conceived as Dante at the lintel. One-directional, and real.
- **sarmiento**: Rodin sculpted him. Sarmiento died in 1888 and never saw the monument.
- **troiano_troiani**: his 1924 *El Arquero*, La Plata, is worked in a surface manner read as Rodin's. Influence outward.

Deliberately **not** named in prose, because the relation is co-location only and the map already shows co-location: `erik_satie`, `anna_pavlova`, `agusti_querol_subirats`, `pio_collivadino`, `julio_argentino_roca`, `juan_manuel_de_rosas`, `frederic_auguste_bartholdi`, `john_everett_millais`.

**Coordinate divergence, not resolved.** The Dépôt des marbres appears at two pins: 48.8578, 2.3067 (this subject's geography lens) and 48.8566, 2.3235 (`camille_claudel.journey.json`, "182 rue de l'Université"). The Claudel stop here inherits her pin byte-identical; the 1880 studio grant uses the geography value. A future pass should reconcile them.

## XII. Gaps stated as gaps

Unverified: the MNBA count of 19 sculptures and 3 drawings (museum page HTTP 403); the *El beso* provenance (gift from Rodin, or a 1940s purchase: two Spanish Wikipedia articles, two answers); the claim that the Sarmiento is his only work made expressly for Buenos Aires (brief-sourced). Unsettled: the date of *Le Penseur* (three sources within 1880-1882); the height of the monumental *Thinker* (181, 185 or 189 cm); Rose Beuret's death date (14 or 16 February 1917); the twelve-cast rule against fourteen extant Burghers. Not consulted directly: Grunfeld, on which the Beauvais stop alone rests, tagged [R]. Verified but not carried as stops: the Antwerp Loos monument (1876) and the Musée Rath photography exhibition, Geneva (1896).
