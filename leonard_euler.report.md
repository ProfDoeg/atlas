# Leonhard Euler (1707-1783): research report

*2026-08-02. Atlas of Journeys, slug `leonard_euler`. Sources verified against each other and left unreconciled where they disagree.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim whose only source is a compilation.

**Calendar warning, systematic.** Russia kept the Julian calendar all Euler's life; Prussia and Switzerland did not. Every Petersburg date in the reachable literature is given without a stated style, and no source consulted says which it uses. The journey is tagged `gregorian` per house convention, but 17 May 1727, 7 January 1734, 19 June 1741 and 18 September 1783 may all be Old Style. A gap, not a resolved question.

## 1. Basel and Riehen, 1707-1727

- **Birth, 15 April 1707**, to Paul Euler, Reformed pastor, and Marguerite Brucker **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**. **Riehen, 1708**, the father's village pastorate, most of the childhood there **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**. **The grandmother's house and the Latin school, c. 1715** **[R: carried by Wikipedia and MacTutor; house unidentified]**, folded into the matriculation stop.
- **St Martin's Church dropped.** The pass pinned the birth there on the premise that this was the father's early pastorate and the baptism site. **The premise is unverified**: no source consulted places either at St Martin's, so the site is a weak **[R]**, not the **[A]** it was tagged, and the coordinate was ~250 m off besides. The journey uses the canonical Basel old-town point and says in the campa that the address is lost.
- **Matriculation, 1720**, at thirteen or fourteen **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**, pin inherited byte-identical from `jung.journey.json`. **Bernoulli's Saturdays, 1720-1723**, at Bernoulli's own house **[R: MacTutor; address unconfirmed, the pin is a placement]**.
- **Master's degree, 1723**, Descartes against Newton; theology, Hebrew and Greek begin the same year at the father's wish **[A: MacTutor]**. **Dissertatio physica de sono, 1726**, and the failed application for the physics chair **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. Two campa embellishments in these stops are **[R]** and flagged as such: that Bernoulli eventually talked Paul Euler out of the ministry plan, and that word of Petersburg vacancies came back through the Bernoullis.
- **Paris masts prize and departure, 5 April 1727** **[A: MacTutor]**; that he never re-entered Switzerland is verbatim in ru.wikipedia **[A]**. The pass's 47.5667, 7.5833 is the generic city gazetteer point, ~1.2 km from the old-town landing; **corrected** to the Schifflände, ~47.5601, 7.5885 **[R for the landing]**.

## 3. First Petersburg period, 1730/31-1741

- **Professor of physics: contradiction flagged.** MacTutor gives 1730, en-Wikipedia 1731, ru.wikipedia specifically 22 January 1731. 1731 is preferred on weight of evidence only; MacTutor's 1730 is named in `date_confidence`, where the hedge lives. **Head of the mathematics division, 1733**, on Daniel Bernoulli's return to Basel **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **Marriage, 7 January 1734**, to Katharina Gsell, daughter of the Academy painter Georg Gsell; thirteen children, five surviving infancy **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**. Two errors corrected. (i) **The venue was invented**: the pass pinned it at the Petrikirche and tagged it **[A]** while its own note said "tag would be R for the church itself." No source names the venue, and the coordinate was ~300 m off. **Removed.** (ii) **The house is dated wrong**: the pass put the purchase after the marriage, ru.wikipedia has it in 1733. **Corrected**, house and marriage on one pin, the coordinate still contested between 59.9421, 30.2996 on north-central Vasilievsky Island and the southern shore, roughly 59.934, 30.284, where the house traditionally called Euler's stands. Neither confirmed, and the campa says so.
- **Johann Albrecht born 1734**, Goldbach godfather **[A: Wikipedia]**. Month and day unestablished, folded into the marriage stop rather than given a fabricated date.
- **Geography department and near-fatal fever, 1735** **[A: MacTutor]**. **The Basel problem, 1735** **[A: MacTutor]**: the campa deliberately does **not** state the value of the sum, which the pool never supplies, nor claim Jacob Bernoulli had already proved it finite, which is textbook history but **[R]** here.
- **The seven bridges of Königsberg.** The pool's bare "1736" is a volume year, not a work year. Euler presented the solution **26 August 1735**; the paper appeared in 1741, in the *Commentarii* volume nominally covering 1736. **[A: Wikipedia]** All three years are in `date_confidence`.
- **Mechanica, 1736** **[A: MacTutor]**. **The right eye: three incompatible accounts, not reconciled.** 1735, from an astronomical calculation **[A: Hunter's 1795 Elogium, quoted in the journey]**; 1738 **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**; around 1740, from cartographic strain **[A: Wikipedia]**. The journey pins 1738 and lists all three.
- **Paris Grand Prize shared 1738 (fire) and 1740 (tides)** **[A: MacTutor]**, folded into the departure stop. **Departure 19 June 1741** **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**, post-Anna regency instability a contributing cause.

## 4. Berlin, 1741-1766

- **Arrival, 25 July 1741** **[A: MacTutor]**. Pin inherited byte-identical from `gauss.journey.json` and `fichte.journey.json`. **The residence before Charlottenburg** is **[R]**, "traditional attribution repeated in popular Euler biographies; not verified against a primary source": a placement, not an address, and the campa says so.
- **The remark to the Queen Mother: source upgrade, re-tagged [R] to [A].** The pass sourced it to Vucinich (1963); it is dialogue in the Hunter 1795 Elogium, 168 years earlier, ending "I have just come from a country, where people are hanged, if they talk." The word "just" had been dropped in circulation; the journey quotes the 1795 wording.
- **Director of the mathematics class, 1744** **[A: MacTutor]**. The pass's framing was wrong twice and the campa **corrects it on the page rather than dropping it silently**: the Academy was not founded in 1744 (the Society of Sciences dates from 11 July 1700, Leibniz founding president; 1744 was the merger of the Nouvelle Societe Litteraire into it, renamed 1746), and Maupertuis was not president in 1744 (1746 to his death in 1759). **[A: Wikipedia, "Prussian Academy of Sciences" and "Pierre Louis Maupertuis"]**
- **Introductio, 1748**; **Sanssouci hydraulics, 1749** **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**; **Institutiones calculi differentialis, 1755** with foreign membership of the Royal Swedish and French academies **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**; **Maupertuis dies 1759**, de facto direction without the title **[A: MacTutor]**. **Finow Canal, 1749** is **[R: MacTutor only]**, folded into the Sanssouci campa with the hedge stated.
- **Charlottenburg house, 1753**, with the widowed mother brought from Switzerland, and the **farm plundered, 1760**, Saltykov's compensation and Elizabeth's further 4,000 rubles **[A: Wikipedia]**. The plot is unestablished and the farm's location distinct from the house is not pinpointed, so one district pin serves both, a known imprecision. The corpus's Charlottenburg pins are an opera house and a Gymnasium, so nothing is inherited.
- **Letters to a German Princess: corrected on four points** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Written 1760-1762, not merely begun in 1760; 234 letters; addressed to Friederike Charlotte of Brandenburg-Schwedt **and her younger sister Louise**; vols 1-2 published at St Petersburg 1768 and vol 3 at Frankfurt 1774, not "through the early 1770s". **Discrepancy left standing:** Hunter's 1795 preface calls the addressee the Princess of Anhalt-Dessau, niece of Frederick II.
- **D'Alembert declines the presidency, 1763** **[A: MacTutor]**; the addition that he named Euler to Frederick is **[R]** and is **cut**. **Departure, 1766** **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**: Catherine's terms **[A]**, but the often-quoted 3,000 rubles is not in the pool and is **cut**.

- **Return under Catherine, 1766**; **Johann Albrecht to a chair of physics, 1766** **[A: MacTutor]**. **Fire and rescue, 1771**; **Katharina dies, 1773**; **one paper a week, 1775**; **Salome Abigail Gsell, 1776** **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- **Blindness: a direct self-contradiction in the pool, flagged and not resolved.** One geography item dates the couching and near-total blindness to 1766 (en-Wikipedia: surgery weeks after the cataract's discovery); another geography item and the chronology date it to 1771 (MacTutor). The journey carries **both**, as a 1766 stop and a 1771 stop, `date_confidence` on each naming the conflict.
- **Nicolas Fuss:** pool said 1772, **corrected to 1773** **[A: Wikipedia, "Nicolas Fuss": moved to Saint Petersburg 1773, assistant 1773-1783]**. He married Albertine Euler (1766-1822), daughter of Johann Albrecht, so grandson-in-law, which the pool had right.
- **Death, 18 September 1783**, brain haemorrhage, the Uranus conversation with Lexell, last words "I am dying" **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor gives the haemorrhage c. 5 p.m., death c. 11 p.m.]**. The grandchild's lesson, the balloon calculations and the two chalkboards **do not appear** in the MacTutor or Wikipedia text fetched; the detail descends from Condorcet's 1783 eloge and is **attributed to Condorcet in the campa, not to MacTutor**. The afterlife lens's death coordinate, 59.9343, 30.3351, is **rejected**: it is central mainland Petersburg, ~1.9 km from Vasilievsky Island, and contradicts the geography lens's own 59.9421, 30.2996 for the same event.

## 7. The hollow Earth: a refuted attribution, not an open question

The brief's premise, that Euler "proposed a version of the hollow-earth theory with an inner sun", is what queued him beside Symmes, Saint-Yves, Bradshaw, John Uri Lloyd and Halley. It does not survive contact with the sources.

- Primary check, negative: no passage on a hollow Earth or interior sun in the accessible volume of Hunter's 1795 *Letters*. **[R: proves only that the volume reached lacks it]**
- Positive refutation: Wikipedia's "Hollow Earth" states outright that the claim Euler "proposed a single-shell hollow Earth with a small sun (1,000 kilometres across) at the center" **is not true**, citing Edward Sandifer, "Euler and the Hollow Earth: Fact or Fiction?", MAA, April 2007. What Euler wrote was an unrelated thought experiment about an object dropped through a hole drilled to the Earth's centre. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- The real hollow-earth model of the period is **Edmond Halley's** 1692 proposal, a shell roughly 500 miles thick over two inner concentric shells and a core, luminous and possibly inhabited **[A: Wikipedia]**. Halley is separately queued.
- **Two loose ends left as gaps.** Sandifer could not be read firsthand (MAA 403, eulerarchive 404, web.archive.org blocked), so the refutation rests on Wikipedia's summary. The *Popular Science Monthly* (June 1882) lead about a seventy-fourth letter of 11 November 1760 was never obtained. No source traces when or by whom the misattribution began.

The journey gives Euler **no** hollow-earth stop in the Berlin segment, only a refutation stop in the afterlife dated to Sandifer's column.

## 8. Quotations

All from the Elogium and translator's preface of *Letters of Euler ... Addressed to a German Princess*, trans. Henry Hunter (London, 1795), vol. 1, archive.org `lettersofeuleron01eule`. The OCR renders the long-s as "f"; spelling is normalised and every `quote_source` says so.

**Used, eight in all [A]:** the 1735 astronomical-calculation line on the loss of the eye; the "hanged, if they talk" reply (word "just" restored, §4); "For the instruction of a young and sensible female."; the evening-devotion, chalk-on-slate, mental-calculation, pleasure-of-exertion and thirteen-children sentences.

Three were **damaged in the gathering pass and restored here.** (i) The devotion sentence had been reworded to "Every evening he collected", deleting "As long as his sight remained", which bounds the practice to his sighted years and is why that stop sits before 1771. (ii) The mental-calculation sentence had been truncated at "all belief", dropping "had not the history of his labours accustomed us to prodigies"; its note was wrong too, the source having two pupils sum a series "as far as to the seventeenth term" and differ "one unit at the fiftieth figure", not a fiftieth decimal place. (iii) "The pleasure of exertion..." is a subordinate clause of a general maxim, not a statement about Euler, and the pass's gloss (that he reworked others' problems) is contradicted by the next sentence, about his freedom from vanity in dispute. Full sentence used, gloss dropped.

Attested but unused: the "religious turn of mind" and "rigid Calvinism" sentences, the latter paraphrased into a campa with attribution.

**Rejected or downgraded, not used.** "Now I will have less distraction." **[R: Eves (1969); untraced further back, and the Hunter Elogium lacks it]**. "Read Euler: he is our master in everything." **[R: Laplace via Simmons (1992); original undocumented]**. "The study of Euler's works will remain the best school..." **[R: attributed to Gauss; Wikiquote's actual citation is Grinstein and Lipsey (2001), p. 235, and the pass inserted a comma the text lacks]**. "Mathematicians have tried in vain to this day..." **[unusable verbatim, though contrary to the pass's note it IS traceable: it opens E175 (1747, published 1751), Opera Omnia ser. 1 vol. 2 pp. 241-253. The French could not be opened (403), so the English is Pólya's 1954 translation, not a reading of Euler]**.
