# Theodor W. Adorno (1903-1969): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-25 for `theodor_adorno.journey.json`. QUEUE.md row 113, entered to join the Frankfurt School cluster: Benjamin (row 112) and Habermas (row 114) are queued and unresearched; Horkheimer and Marcuse already have files.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-only claim no consulted source confirms. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated.

**Coordinates.** The pool arrived with a systematic geocoding fault: eight geography entries reused one Frankfurt pair sitting ~350 m off the Bockenheim campus, and seven other pins were off by 700 m to 4 km. Corrections are listed in section 10 and repeated in each affected stop's `date_confidence`.

---

## 1. Frankfurt formation, 1903-1924

- **Birth [A].** 11 September 1903, Theodor Ludwig Wiesengrund, Schöne Aussicht 9 (German Wikipedia; date corroborated by SEP). Father Oscar Wiesengrund, wine exporter; mother Maria Calvelli-Adorno, professional singer; her sister Agathe in the household. The 1914 move to Seeheimer Strasse 19, Oberrad [A], is folded into this stop to hold the count down; the address survives in `suggested_refs`.
- **Schooling [A, name problem].** Sachsenhausen Gymnasium 1913-1921, Abitur at seventeen (English Wikipedia). **Correction:** the pool called it "the Kaiser-Wilhelm Gymnasium" as of 1913, but per German Wikipedia ('Freiherr-vom-Stein-Schule') the school bore that name only from **1917**, becoming Freiherr-vom-Stein-Schule in 1945.
- **Kracauer readings [R].** The Saturday readings of Kant's first *Critique* with Siegfried Kracauer, from about fifteen, are tradition and are **not** in the SEP or Wikipedia text fetched. Kept, tagged, given no room and no witness, because none exists.
- **University and doctorate [A].** Matriculated 1921 in philosophy, sociology, psychology, music; Hans Cornelius his principal teacher. Doctorate 1924 under Cornelius, on transcendence of the thingly and noematic in Husserl, unpublished in that form (English Wikipedia; SEP).
- **Benjamin [R on date, corrected on place].** The pool dated the meeting 1923 and placed it in "Germany." German Wikipedia says he met Benjamin *durch Vermittlung Kracauers als Student*, i.e. in **Frankfurt**; English Wikipedia's Benjamin article attaches the acquaintance to 1923. Place set to Frankfurt, year left soft at 1922-1923. Berlin, implied by the pool, is unsupported.

## 2. Vienna, 1925-1930

- **Arrival [contradiction, unresolved].** German Wikipedia: *"Im März 1925 zog Adorno nach Wien … wo er sich ein Zimmer in der Pension Luisenheim im 9. Bezirk nahm."* English Wikipedia: February 1925. The pool cited German Wikipedia while printing February, which is backwards. The file uses **March**, from the source that also names the lodging, and prints the conflict in the campa.
- **Study [A].** Composition with Alban Berg, piano with Eduard Steuermann, Schoenberg's circle (SEP; IEP). **[R]** Berg's Trauttmansdorffgasse address in Hietzing is musicological record, not re-verified here.
- **Criticism [R].** Editorial circle of *Musikblätter des Anbruch*, c. 1928-1930 (SEP plus standard bibliography; no masthead consulted).
- **Gretel Karplus [R].** The pool dates a Berlin meeting to 1923 and could not confirm it; the interlock lens separately flagged the "Benjamin/Bloch circle" framing as unverifiable, no Bloch or Karplus file existing. Retained as an [R] Berlin stop dated 1928 for ordering. The marriage itself is [A].

## 3. Habilitation and expulsion, 1927-1934

- **First habilitation withdrawn [A].** 1927, *Der Begriff des Unbewussten in der transzendentalen Seelenlehre*, withdrawn after Cornelius and Tillich signalled refusal (SEP).
- **Venia legendi [A].** February 1931, *Kierkegaard: Konstruktion des Ästhetischen*, sponsor Paul Tillich (English Wikipedia). Published as a book 1933.
- **Inaugural lecture [R on the day].** *Die Aktualität der Philosophie*, catalogued by SEP as the address of 7 May 1931; no primary programme consulted.
- **Zeitschrift [A].** *Zur gesellschaftlichen Lage der Musik*, 1932 (SEP). **Correction:** German Wikipedia ('Institut für Sozialforschung') gives the pre-1933 address as **Viktoria**-Allee, today Senckenberganlage, not Victoria-Allee as the pool spelled it.
- **1933 [A on year, R on month].** Licence revoked under racial legislation; Institute and family home searched; Reich Chamber of Literature later rejects him as non-Aryan (SEP). September is the pool's month, not separately confirmed.
- **Departure [contradiction, unresolved].** SEP: leaves Germany **spring** 1934. English Wikipedia: emigrates **autumn** 1934, while dating the Merton registration to June 1934, which autumn cannot accommodate. The pool asserted spring while citing the page that says autumn. The file asserts neither and prints both.

## 4. Oxford, 1934-1938

- **Merton [A].** Advanced student June 1934 to February 1938, under Gilbert Ryle on a critique of Husserl (English Wikipedia). That critique surfaces only in 1956 as *Zur Metakritik der Erkenntnistheorie*.
- **Über Jazz [A on the core, R on the reason].** *Upgraded from the pool's [R].* German Wikipedia states verbatim: *"den Aufsatz Über Jazz, der 1936 in der Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung unter dem Pseudonym Hektor Rottweiler erschien."* Essay, year, journal, pseudonym are [A]. The dossier's *rationale*, that the pseudonym shielded a man still travelling into Germany, is not in that source and stays [R].
- **Paris [R].** The dossier records repeated trips to see Benjamin and Horkheimer; no consulted source fixes a date. **Boundary observed:** Horkheimer's file carries a Paris stop of 1937-10-01, "the last meeting with Benjamin." That is Horkheimer's event, not claimed for Adorno. Adorno's Paris stop is his own repeated visiting, dated 1936 to order the segment.
- **Marriage [A, venue newly pinned].** 8 September 1937 (English Wikipedia). The pool's note that the London venue could not be confirmed is **superseded**: German Wikipedia specifies *"im Standesamt des Districts Paddington."*

## 5. New York and Newark, 1938-1941

- **Arrival [A].** Sails 16 February 1938; New York period to November 1941 (English Wikipedia). Institute beside Columbia in Morningside Heights.
- **Radio project [A].** Directs the music division of Lazarsfeld's Rockefeller-funded Princeton Radio Research Project, administered from Newark (English Wikipedia; SEP). Office address not established; city level only.
- **The Baudelaire letter [R].** The 1938 Institute rejection of Benjamin's Baudelaire essay is dossier-only, uncheckable against any atlas file because **no Benjamin journey exists yet**. Kept as an [R] stop with the gap stated; re-read it when Benjamin is researched.

## 6. Los Angeles, 1941-1949

- **Move west [A].** Pacific Palisades, November 1941, near the Horkheimers; the colony of Thomas and Heinrich Mann, Brecht, Eisler, Feuchtwanger, Fritz Lang, Schoenberg (English Wikipedia). Enemy-alien curfew and travel restriction after US entry. The rented house is unidentified. The five-mile figure often quoted for the restriction is **not** in the pool and is not used.
- **Dialectic of Enlightenment [A, placement corrected].** Composed in Los Angeles 1942-1944. **Correction:** the pool placed the 1944 hectograph *Philosophische Fragmente* in Los Angeles; English Wikipedia gives it as issued in **New York** by the Institute in May 1944. Written in Los Angeles, circulated from New York; the file splits it across two stops. Revised Querido edition, Amsterdam, 1947; Horkheimer's file already carries that stop (1947-05-01), cited rather than duplicated.
- **Quotation correction.** The pool carried *"Myth is already enlightenment, and enlightenment reverts to mythology"* as [R], at the "Concept of Enlightenment" chapter opening, hedged between Cumming and Jephcott. Both halves wrong: SEP and English Wikipedia verify the wording and cite **Jephcott, Stanford UP 2002, p. xviii**, the Introduction.
- **Naturalization [A on month, R on building].** November 1943 (English Wikipedia). Courthouse unidentified; the downtown coordinate is generic and says so.
- **Eisler [A].** *Composing for the Films*, from Rockefeller-supported research, first Oxford University Press edition crediting Eisler alone (English Wikipedia). The pool tagged this [R]; the attestation supports the core. The *Lang and Chaplin friendships* stay [R], dossier-only.
- **Minima Moralia [A, span corrected].** Written for Horkheimer's fiftieth birthday, published 1951. **Correction:** the pool said "written chiefly 1944-47"; English Wikipedia's *Minima Moralia* article says he began 1944 and **completed it in 1949**. Horkheimer's birth date of 14 February 1895 fixes the fiftieth to 14 February 1945, which orders the stop; that inference sits in the confidence field only.
- **The Authoritarian Personality [A on the work, R on the place].** With Else Frenkel-Brunswik, Daniel Levinson, R. Nevitt Sanford; American Jewish Committee financing under *Studies in Prejudice*; published 1950 (English Wikipedia). **Gap:** the pool attests only "United States." Berkeley is the standard account of where the Public Opinion Study team worked, used here and tagged [R]. If any pin here wants defending or moving, it is this one.

## 7. The rebuilt Institute, 1949-1966

- **Return [A].** October 1949 to substitute for Horkheimer (English Wikipedia: "Left America on October 31, 1949").
- **Darmstadt [contradiction, printed].** The dossier gave 1951-1958. German Wikipedia says he took part *"zwischen 1950 und 1966 als Referent und Kursleiter nahezu regelmässig"*: sixteen years, not eight, off by eight at both ends. The file uses **1950-1966** and prints both figures. Boulez, Stockhausen, Nono at Darmstadt is standard record, not re-verified.
- **Institute reopening [A].** 14 November 1951, Senckenberganlage (German Wikipedia). Adorno deputy director, later co-director; the Adorno-Ampel nickname is from the same source.
- **Hacker Foundation [A].** 1952-53, research director, Beverly Hills, explicitly to retain US citizenship (SEP, quoted). **[R]** The August 1953 permanent settling is dossier-cited and not separately re-verified.
- **Chairs [A, year soft].** Ausserplanmässiger Professor 1950, a further step 1953, full professor of philosophy and sociology 1956, Institute director 1958. The pool notes the exact year of the full chair was not isolated in the fetched text.
- **Positivismusstreit [A].** Tübingen 1961, opening papers by Adorno and Popper, collected with Habermas and Albert in 1969. Month and day are ordering defaults.
- **Negative Dialektik [A].** 1966; identity thinking; the new categorical imperative that Auschwitz not repeat (SEP; English Wikipedia).
- **Erziehung nach Auschwitz [R on the date].** Dossier-cited to 1966, broadcast date unconfirmed against any primary archive. The file dates it 1966-01-01 as an ordering default and **does not invent a broadcast day**, which is why this stop and Negative Dialektik share a date and are separated by array order alone.
- **Quotation left at [R] and unused.** *"To write poetry after Auschwitz is barbaric"* (*Kulturkritik und Gesellschaft*, 1951) appears in no `quote` field: the primary English translation could not be opened and variants circulate. It survives once as a `suggested_ref`, with its unverified status named.

## 8. 1969

- **The occupation [contradiction unresolved; one figure dropped].** The dossier gives 31 January 1969, Krahl-led, seventy-six arrests. German Wikipedia's Adorno article supports 31 January; **its Hans-Jürgen Krahl article dates the same occupation to 7 January 1969**, and *neither* gives the seventy-six figure. The file keeps 31 January, prints the conflict, and **drops the arrest count**. Krahl's prosecution is [A], Adorno summoned as witness 18 July 1969. The breach with Marcuse is corroborated in general terms by SEP.
- **Busenattentat [A, upgraded, title corrected].** German Wikipedia ('Busenattentat') gives 22 April 1969, Hörsaal VI, three women students, rose and tulip petals, Adorno leaving with his briefcase raised, and this as his last public appearance. The pool's lecture title was wrong: *Einführung* in dialektisches Denken, not *Einleitung*. The legend that this caused his death is named as legend.
- **Zermatt [A].** Ascent toward the Gandegghütte at nearly 3,000 m against medical advice, with preceding heart trouble (German Wikipedia).
- **Death [A, conflict printed, arithmetic fixed].** 6 August 1969, Spital Visp / Krankenhaus St. Maria, Valais, heart attack (German and English Wikipedia). **Conflict:** SEP's fetched summary gives Frankfurt am Main as the place of death; the file uses Visp and names the discrepancy without resolving it. **Correction:** the pool wrote "four days short of his sixty-sixth birthday." Born 11 September 1903, he died about **five weeks** short; SEP says "one month before." Gretel outlived him by twenty-four years (d. 16 July 1993), which the pool had right.

## 9. Afterward

- **Burial [A, no plot].** Frankfurter Hauptfriedhof (German Wikipedia: *"Adornos Grab befindet sich auf dem Frankfurter Hauptfriedhof"*), **with no section, row, or plot number in any consulted source.** Stated as a gap in the stop.
- **Ästhetische Theorie [A].** Unfinished at death, published 1970, edited by Gretel Adorno and Rolf Tiedemann. Hullot-Kentor's English version called a second magnum opus by SEP; the pool could not pin its year past 1997. *Nachgelassene Schriften* from 1993 (SEP).
- **Minima Moralia in English [correction, not staged].** The pool called Jephcott's Verso translation "new" in 2005. English Wikipedia cites it in a **Verso 1978** edition, first published NLB 1974; SEP merely uses the 2005 printing as reference. The supersession claim is dropped.
- **Adorno-Denkmal [A].** Vadim Zakharov, inaugurated 10 October 2003 for the centenary at what was then Theodor-W.-Adorno-Platz, Bockenheim (today Tilly-Edinger-Platz), relocated to Campus Westend April 2016 (German Wikipedia, 'Adorno-Denkmal'). Its repeated vandalism and the criticism that it fails to convey its subject are in the same source and carried in the stop's `sources`. **Pin note:** the coordinates are the present Westend location, *not* where the object stood in 2003.
- **Adorno Prize [A].** City of Frankfurt, every three years since 1977; Elias (1977), Habermas (1980), Godard (1995). Created after his death. A `suggested_ref`, not a stop.
- **Suhrkamp conflict [recorded, unresolved].** The rights catalogue dates a 20-volume *Gesammelte Schriften* to 1997, against the usual 1970-1986 dating of the originals. Probably a repackaged set; neither reading is preferred, and neither is used.

## 12. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Adorno is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** Nothing was silently deleted. One direction was caught and is reported rather than acted on.

- **Direction: FORWARD.** Hito Steyerl's 2012 installation *Adorno's Grey* investigates the legend that Adorno had his lecture hall painted a particular grey to keep students awake, and uses the hunt for that paint layer to ask why his critical theory did not translate into solidarity with the students he called police on in 1969. A 2012 artefact, and Adorno died in 1969: a new hash in an old block. It is therefore **not staged as a scene**; Steyerl's visit belongs to Steyerl. It appears only as a `suggested_ref` and `source` on Adorno's **own** stop for **his own** Hörsaal VI, the form the interlock rule licenses for a later one-directional engagement. Steyerl's project itself treats the grey-paint story as legend, so it is [R].
- **A milder forward question, for the operator's ruling.** The closing segment "The Posthumous Adorno" is dated after his death: burial (1969), *Ästhetische Theorie* (1970), Denkmal (2003). It follows atlas precedent, `max_horkheimer.journey.json`'s closing segment "The Name Afterward" (1985, 1992, 2015). If that precedent is not meant to generalize, the Denkmal stop is the one to cut first.
- **No backward application was attempted.** Adorno's framework is applied to nothing he did not treat.
