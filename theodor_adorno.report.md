## 1. Frankfurt childhood, 1903-1921

**[A]** (en/de.wikipedia) Born Theodor Ludwig Wiesengrund, Frankfurt, 11 September 1903. Father Oscar Wiesengrund, wine merchant, Jewish by descent, Protestant by conversion; mother Maria Calvelli-Adorno della Piana, singer, Corsican-Italian descent, Catholic; only child, her sister Agathe a pianist in the household. Abitur 1921. Saturday readings of the first Critique with Siegfried Kracauer through the school years, the file's licensed naming of **kant**.

**Gaps.** **[R]** The dossier's claim that he was baptized and raised in his mother's Catholic milieu is unconfirmable on SEP, Wikipedia EN/DE, Britannica or IEP beyond the bare parental identities; the file states only the parents' religions. It matters, because the 1933 revocation turned on descent, not confession. **[R]** Schöne Aussicht 9 as a house number is dossier-only: street and coordinate right, number unchecked. **[A / R]** Amorbach, the recurring Odenwald summers and a later Adorno essay, comes from de.wikipedia and was found independently, the originating dossier having omitted it; no summer is dated and no house located, so the stop's 1910-07-01 declares itself an ordering date.

## 2. University and Vienna, 1921-1928

**[A]** Matriculated 1921 in philosophy, psychology, sociology, music; doctorate 1924 on Husserl under Hans Cornelius (en.wikipedia; SEP). Vienna from February 1925: composition with Alban Berg, piano with Eduard Steuermann, entry to the Schoenberg circle. **[A, no stop]** Editorial role at Musikblätter des Anbruch, 1928, placed only as "Vienna/Frankfurt"; cut for budget and unresolved place, recorded so it is not lost.

**[C] Walter Benjamin, 1923: the most consequential geographic correction, left unresolved in the file.** The lens staged the meeting in **Berlin 1923, through a circle around Ernst Bloch**. Against it: en.wikipedia 'Walter Benjamin' places the acquaintance in 1923 in the **Frankfurt** context, where Benjamin was habilitating and the Institute was founded; de.wikipedia's Adorno article puts no Berlin 1923 meeting on record and dates the friendship's deepening to a September 1925 stay with Kracauer on the Gulf of Naples; and **the Bloch attribution is unsupported everywhere checked**. The file pins Frankfurt, states the contradiction in `date_confidence`, drops Bloch. Naples 1925 is named there but got no stop, a cut for budget, not for evidence.

**[R as to date] Gretel Karplus, Berlin.** The lens directed the Berlin thread to Karplus, on de.wikipedia's note of the Wiesengrund firm's tie to "die Fabrik von Karplus & Herzberger in Berlin". That tie is [A], but **no source dates a first meeting**; 1923 is the dossier's, kept only to order the segment, and the "fourteen-year courtship" is arithmetic off the 1937 marriage. **Gap:** the Alsergrund lodging is "a pension" with no address, so that coordinate is district-level and says so.

## 3. Habilitation and expulsion, 1929-1934

**[A]** Kierkegaard: Construction of the Aesthetic published 23 March 1933, the day of the Enabling Act; teaching licence revoked September 1933 (both en.wikipedia); departure April 1934 (SEP), folded into the same stop. **[A, corpus]** For 1931, `felix_weil.journey.json` has Horkheimer taking the chair and "bringing in the people the school will be named for: Theodor Adorno, Erich Fromm, Herbert Marcuse", a direct naming inside an existing file and the basis for the Institute stop.

**[C on the day]** The inaugural lecture "Die Aktualität der Philosophie" is dated 7 May 1931 by the lens, only to the year by SEP; the file says May without asserting a day. **[A as to fact, R as to place]** "Über Jazz" as **Hektor Rottweiler**, 1936, Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung, pseudonymous because he was still travelling in Nazi Germany. The place is genuinely unresolved: writing from Oxford, travelling into Germany, journal produced in exile. The file pins Oxford, where the author sat, and says so.

## 4. Exile, 1934-1949

**[A]** The attested spine of these years (Merton 1934 under Ryle, the Paris trips and the 2 August 1935 Benjamin letter, the 1937 marriage, New York 1938, the radio project, citizenship 1943, *Doktor Faustus*, Eisler, *Philosophische Fragmente* 1944, Querido 1947) rests on en.wikipedia, de.wikipedia, SEP and New Left Review I/81, cited per stop in the journey file.

**[A, corpus]** `max_horkheimer.journey.json` supplies October 1937 (the last meeting with Benjamin, the two sending manuscripts back "with demands for revision that Benjamin accepts and resents"), the California dictation "to each other and to Gretel Adorno, who types and keeps the notes", and the American Jewish Committee study putting "Adorno and a team of Berkeley psychologists to work building a scale". The Baudelaire dispute proper runs into 1938, so the file uses the corpus date and does not merge the years silently. That file's 1939 Fromm stop is **truncated mid-word** at "Horkheim"; flagged at the end as a corpus defect.

**Gaps and hedges.** No venue is established for any Paris meeting, the Paddington registry building, or the Newark office; those coordinates are city or district level and say so. **[R]** Personal travel to Berkeley is not established anywhere consulted, so that stop stays reconstruction on the point, though en.wikipedia's infobox lists Berkeley among his institutions. The file **does not** assert that Adorno withdrew his name from the 1947 Eisler publication, which was not re-verified.

## 5. Return and public life, 1949-1968

**[A, en.wikipedia]** Darmstadt 1951-1958 with the criticism of Boulez, Stockhausen and Nono, *Mahler* (1960) folded in for budget; chair of the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Soziologie from 1963, folded into the 1968 congress stop; *Jargon der Eigentlichkeit* 1964; *Negative Dialektik* and "Erziehung nach Auschwitz" 1966; Vienna 6 April 1967, published 2019. **[A, corpus]** Institute reopening 14 November 1951 with McCloy High Commission and Weil funding; `felix_weil.journey.json` for the same date: "Adorno and Pollock have come back to West Germany with him; Marcuse, Löwenthal and Kirchheimer have stayed in America."

**[C] The return.** SEP: "returned to Frankfurt in 1949" to posts in philosophy and sociology. en.wikipedia: left America end of October 1949, "Returned to Frankfurt: 1950". The file uses departure October 1949 and teaching resumed in the 1949/50 winter semester, with the disagreement in both `date_confidence` and the campa. **A bare "1949" must not be presented as undisputed.** Permanent resettlement August 1953 per the lens; unchecked.

**[C] The directorship.** en.wikipedia dates it 1958; `max_horkheimer.journey.json` says the handover ran over preceding years and that "whether that transfer happened in 1958 or in stages, the sources do not agree". The file carries the caveat forward rather than picking a year, per the pool's explicit instruction.

**Gaps and hedges.** **[R]** *The Stars Down to Earth* and the citizenship-preservation motive for the 1952 Beverly Hills directorship (itself attested by SEP) are widely reported but **not re-verified**; the astrology study is in the campa with a flag, the motive was cut. That *Jargon* began as a chapter of *Negative Dialectics* is standard in the literature and appears as narrative, not as a fetched citation. The 1967 lecture hall was not confirmed, and the English edition's translator and publisher **could not be verified**, so neither is named. The 1968 congress days were not re-verified; a draft campa tied the congress to the shooting of Rudi Dutschke (11 April 1968) and **that sentence was removed as unverified**, the two events falling close enough that the relation could be trivially true or flatly wrong.

## 6. The last year, 1969

**[A]** 31 January: de.wikipedia records the forced entry, the demand for a discussion of the political situation, and that Adorno and Ludwig von Friedeburg as directors **called the police and reported the occupiers**, at deep inner cost to a man who had spent a life opposing policing and surveillance. Seventy-six arrested, and Marcuse's public objection, from en.wikipedia and the corpus. The Busenattentat of 22 April, likewise de.wikipedia: chalked demands for self-criticism over the police eviction, his request for five minutes, three women of whom **only Hannah Weitemeier is publicly named**, rose and tulip petals, and Adorno leaving with his briefcase over his head. Letters to Marcuse of 5 May and 19 June from the field-journal.com edition, supplying two quotes. *Stichworte* completed June. Death: "wurde er ins Visper Krankenhaus **St. Maria** gebracht. Dort erlag er am Morgen des 6. Augusts 1969 einem Herzinfarkt".

**[A/R] The Busenattentat quote is a double re-quotation and is labelled so.** "I proposed a theoretical model for thought. How could I suspect that people would want to realize it with Molotov-cocktails?" reaches this file through Esther Leslie's introduction in New Left Review I/233, which cites a caption Adorno wrote for *Stern*. **No facsimile of Stern was seen.** [A] for the NLR text, [R] for the Stern original.

**[R]** Gretel's notice wording ("quietly in his sleep") comes from the lens and was not checked against a printed notice; Marcuse's California location in May and June is standard and not re-verified. **[R, marked as legend]** Later retellings make the April humiliation the cause of the August heart attack; four months separate them and **no medical evidence supports the chain**. The file states the legend as a legend, inside the Visp campa.

## 7. Afterlife

**[A, all de.wikipedia]** Burial in the Frankfurter Hauptfriedhof, the ground that also holds Schopenhauer. *Ästhetische Theorie* 1970, posthumous and unfinished. Adorno Prize from 1977: every three years, philosophy/music/theatre/film, presented by the mayor, 50,000 euros, given after only two years in 2003 for the centenary; winners cited include Habermas 1980, Boulez 1992, Godard 1995, Derrida 2001, von Trotta 2018. The Adorno-Ampel: campaigning since 1962 for a signal between Institute and Bockenheim campus, "allerdings wurde die Ampel erst 1987 installiert". The 2003 Denkmal, attested in unusual detail: Vadim Zakharov; five glass panes at 350 kg each; desk, chair, dusk-triggered lamp; a Mälzel metronome on a copy of *Negative Dialektik*; quotations in marble and granite paving laid as a labyrinth; ~220,000 euros; relocated to the Westend campus April 2016 after repeated glass breakage.

**The burial date is a gap.** Grave field and section number were not found, and the day is nowhere in the consulted sources, only "August 1969". *Convention deviation, flagged:* defaulting an unknown day to 01 would date the burial 1969-08-01, before the 6 August death, so the file uses **1969-08-07** and says in `date_confidence` that this orders the segment and only the month is attested. **[R]** Gretel Adorno and Rolf Tiedemann as constructing editors of the *Ästhetische Theorie* comes through the dossier, the editorial history not re-verified. **[R]** The Stolperstein at Seeheimer Straße 19 has **no installation year** in de.wikipedia; it is named in the Oberrad campa without a date.

**[R, held out of the file]** The Beatles/Tavistock legend, and the LaRouche "cultural Marxism" tradition. `max_horkheimer.journey.json` carries the latter for Horkheimer by name (1992 stop), but **that stop's text does not mention Adorno**, which is why the lens tagged it [R]. Neither is in the journey file: they are reception, not events, and there is no place to stand them. Recorded so the operator can decide whether a reception stop is wanted. **[R]** "Wrong life cannot be lived rightly" is a simplification of *Minima Moralia* §18; the file quotes the standard rendering, "There is no right life in the wrong one", on the stop for the book itself.

**[C] The archive, the largest unresolved item.** Besides the bad coordinate, the custody claim is doubtful: de.wikipedia cites "Michael Schwarz, Mitarbeiter des Walter-Benjamin- und des Theodor-W.-Adorno-Archivs", a joint post at the **Akademie der Künste in Berlin**, the Nachlass having moved from Frankfurt in the mid-2000s. adk.de could not be fetched (502/empty), so this is a strong flag, not a settled correction. **The journey file makes no archive claim at all.**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Adorno is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** No backward-apparatus stops were written and none are needed: every stop sits where Adorno himself was, or where his own body and papers were memorialized. Three things are reported rather than silently deleted.

1. **A forward reach was available and was not taken. Direction: forward.** The culture-industry thesis, and "Films, radio and magazines make up a system which is uniform as a whole and in every part," read as an invitation to close a stop by naming algorithmic recommendation or platform media. Adorno died in 1969 and holds no forward grant, so nothing of the kind is in the file; the operator would have to add him to the forward-grants table. The temptation is unusually strong here and will recur on every future pass.
2. **A shared-place relation, dropped as a naming and kept as a note. Direction: neither; not an apparatus relation at all.** The lens flagged that **Merton College, Oxford** is already a corpus pin from `tolkien.journey.json` (1945) and that Adorno was resident there from 1934. **There is no evidence the two met, and they were not in Oxford simultaneously.** Tolkien is not named in any campa; the pin is inherited byte-identical and nothing further is claimed. Recorded so nobody "restores" the connection later.
3. **A one-directional relation belonging to another file.** Jacques Derrida received the Adorno Prize in Frankfurt in 2001 and gave the lecture "Fichus" there. That is **Derrida's travel and Derrida's stop**, for `jacques_derrida.journey.json` if wanted. Here it appears only as a `suggested_ref` on the prize stop; Derrida is not named in the campa. The relation is real and citable, but the traveling was his.
