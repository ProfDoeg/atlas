# Tommaso Campanella (1568-1639): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `tommaso_campanella`. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or secondary citation only. Contradictions are flagged where found and **not adjudicated**, except where a documentary authority (Firpo, working from the trial records) plainly outranks a tertiary one; where that happens it is said so in the open.*

The hardest thing about this life is that the man spent twenty-seven years in four fortresses of one city, and the popular accounts have shuffled which fortress held him when. Almost every correction below is of that kind: right event, wrong wall.

---

## 1. Calabria and the Dominican circuit (1568-1590)

- Born **5 September 1568**, baptized **Giovanni Domenico Campanella**, son of an illiterate shoemaker. **[A: it.wikipedia "Tommaso Campanella"; Britannica]** Baptized at **San Biagio al Borgo**, Stilo. **[A: it.wikipedia "Stilo (Italia)"]**
- **Birthplace contested, not resolved.** it.wikipedia and the scholarly tradition give **Stilo**; a **1968 Italian ministerial decree** designated a building in **Stignano**, which the fetched en.wikipedia text also lists. Both claims are carried in the journey file: Stilo as the pin, Stignano flagged in `date_confidence` and again at the 1599 flight and the 1923 monument. **[A for the contradiction; unadjudicated]**
- The comet at the birth and the caul are **tradition**, reported by biographers as signs read afterward. **[R: Firpo, Bibliografia; Bonansea 1969]**
- **1583**: novitiate at **Placanica**; vows and the name *fra' Tommaso* at **San Giorgio Morgeto**, age fifteen. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
  - **Contradiction:** en.wikipedia says he entered the order "before age 14," i.e. before 1582. Not resolved.
  - **Correction to an earlier draft:** he did **not** enter at Stilo, nor at thirteen in May 1582. The colour that travelled with that version (a father who wanted him in law, a preference for Franciscans, a library of forbidden books) is carried by **no source consulted** and is dropped.
- **1585**: studies at **Nicastro**. **[A: it.wikipedia]** The full sequence of Calabrian houses is a biographers' reconstruction; the province had no seminary. **[R: Amabile 1882; Ernst 2010]**
- **1588**: at **Cosenza**, reads Telesio's *De rerum natura*. **[A: it.wikipedia; SEP]**
  - **Correction:** Telesio did **not** die "the year before." He died at Cosenza **2 October 1588**, the year Campanella arrived; the standard account is that the two never met. **[A: SEP; it.wikipedia]**
- **Altomonte** appears in the assignment registers as a student station. **[R: Amabile 1882, registers cited but not verified]** An earlier draft named the house "San Francesco," which is wrong: Altomonte's San Francesco di Paola is a **Minim** foundation. Altomonte matters chiefly as the convent he was **ordered back to** in 1592 and refused.
- **August 1589**: completes *Philosophia sensibus demonstrata*. **[A: it.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**
- **1590**: at **San Domenico Maggiore**, Naples; enters the circle around **Giovanni Battista della Porta**. **[A: Ernst 2010; Headley 1997]** Giordano Bruno wore the habit in the same convent and fled a heresy charge there in 1576 **[A: giordano_bruno.journey.json]**; the parallel is structural, not a documented tie **[R]**.

## 2. First arrest and the flight north (1591-1594)

- **1591**: arrested in Naples after the book's publication, on a charge recorded as including **demonic practice**. **[A: it.wikipedia]** Released, but a surveillance file follows him. **[A: Firpo; Ernst 2010]**
- **28 August 1592**: a Dominican tribunal sentences him and orders him to **Altomonte**; he refuses. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **September 1592**: to **Florence**, audience with Grand Duke **Ferdinando I**. **[A for the journey: it.wikipedia]** The substance is **disputed**: some accounts inflate it into a courtship of Medici patronage, others treat it as a courtesy stop. **[R; unadjudicated]**
- **October 1592**: at **Bologna** the Inquisition seizes his manuscripts. **[A: it.wikipedia; Ernst 2010]** Some never resurface; what they contained is **a gap no source fills**.
- **1593**: at **Padua**, meets **Galileo Galilei** and **Andrea Chiocco**. **[A: it.wikipedia; SEP]**
  - **Correction:** an earlier draft called the Galileo contact undocumented and "only inferred from their later correspondence." Wrong, and self-contradicting: the Padua meeting is standard biography. Galileo also holds the chair Bruno had wanted in 1592 **[A: giordano_bruno.journey.json]**.
- **The two Paduan proceedings must be kept apart.** **[A: it.wikipedia; SEP]**
  1. **Early 1593**, convent of Sant'Agostino: an investigation over a **sodomy** incident, from which he is **cleared**.
  2. **Late 1593 / early 1594**: arrest for co-authoring the ***De tribus impostoribus***, Democritean atheism, opposition to ecclesiastical authority. Tortured at Padua with **Ottavio Longo** and **Giovan Battista Clario**. SEP: "arrested at the beginning of 1594."
  - Secondary accounts fold these into one arrest "on two charges." That conflation is flagged and refused.
- **11 October 1594**: transferred to the **Roman Inquisition prison**. **[A: it.wikipedia]**

## 3. The Roman trials (1595-1598)

- **April 1595**: tortured at Rome. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **16 May 1595**: abjures **de vehementi** at **Santa Maria sopra Minerva**; confined to **Santa Sabina**. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
  - **Correction of a common error:** the abjuration is **1595**, not 1597. There were **two** Roman proceedings, not one continuous 1594-1597 detention.
- **31 December 1596**: released from Santa Sabina. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **5 March 1597**: re-arrested on the denunciation of the condemned criminal **Scipione Prestinace**. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **17 December 1597**: acquitted, ordered to write nothing further, released to his superiors. **[A: it.wikipedia; SEP]**
- **July 1598**: through Naples, then to Calabria. **[A: it.wikipedia]**

## 4. The Calabrian conspiracy (1598-1599)

- **15 August 1598**: arrives at Stilo, residence at **Santa Maria di Gesù**. **[A: it.wikipedia; SEP]**
- **1599**: preaches imminent revolutionary transformation on astrological and prophetic grounds and organizes a **conspiracy against Spanish rule**. **[A: it.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** **Catanzaro** as an organizing stop and the negotiation for **Ottoman naval support** are known through the prosecution's own record **[A for the testimony; R for what was actually agreed]**.
- **Squillace** furnishes clerical recruits **[A: Amabile 1882]**; the depth of organization there is **disputed among historians** **[R]**.
- **The informers, corrected.** An earlier draft named **Dionisio Ponzio** and "Giovanni Battista Pisano." Treccani (Firpo) gives **Fabio di Lauro** and **Giambattista Biblia** (10 August 1599), then **fra' Cornelio da Nizza** (14 August), and **Antonio Mesuraca**, who gave up Campanella on **6 September**. **Ponzio was not an informer** but a leading conspirator who escaped; "Pisano" appears to be a garbling of Biblia. **[A: Treccani/Firpo]**
- **17 August 1599**: flees the convent, hides at **Stignano** and **Titi**. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **6 September 1599**: captured, transferred to **Castelvetere**. **[A: it.wikipedia]**

## 5. The twenty-seven years: which fortress, when

Here the reachable accounts diverge most, and Firpo's *Dizionario Biografico* entry, written from the trial records, is treated as outranking it.wikipedia and its English derivatives.

| Period | Prison | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 1599 - Jul 1604 | **Castel Nuovo** | Treccani: reaches Naples 8 Nov 1599; it.wikipedia: identified at Castel Nuovo 23 Nov **[A]** |
| Jul 1604 - 1608 | **Castel Sant'Elmo**, blind underground cell, irons at hands and feet | Treccani **[A]** |
| 1608 - 1614 | **Castel dell'Ovo**, lighter regime, visitors and books | Treccani **[A]** |
| 1614 - 1626 | **Castel Nuovo** again; but Treccani places the 1616 *Apologia* at Sant'Elmo | **[A / internally strained: see below]** |
| Released 23 May 1626 | from **Castel Nuovo** | Treccani; SEP **[A]** |

**Corrections this table forces:**
- **Sant'Elmo did not hold him for "the bulk" of the captivity.** Castel Nuovo was first, last, and longest.
- **The 1604 and 1608 transfers are commonly swapped.** July 1604 is the move **into** the Sant'Elmo pit; 1608 the move **out**, to Castel dell'Ovo.
- **The veglia was not at Sant'Elmo.** See below.
- **A residual strain, flagged not resolved:** Treccani puts the *Apologia pro Galileo* (1616) at **Castel Sant'Elmo**, which sits badly against the same source's Sant'Elmo dating of 1604-1608. Either he returned there or the place is loosely attributed. The journey file carries Treccani's placement and says so in `date_confidence`.

**Events within the captivity:**
- **2 April 1600**: begins **fourteen months** of feigned madness, staged on a burning straw mattress. **[A: Treccani/Firpo]** en.wikipedia adds a total of **seven** tortures across all trials, not itemized there **[R for the count]**.
- **1600**: ***La Monarchia di Spagna***, for a Spanish-led universal monarchy under papal spiritual guidance. **[A: it.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** An earlier draft dated it 1607-1608; wrong (1600, with a lost Padua-years redaction). The puzzle survives the correction: the book argues for the crown then torturing him.
- **1601**: *Aforismi politici*. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **4-5 June 1601, Castel Nuovo**: the **veglia**. **Duration contradicted**: Treccani (Firpo, from the records) gives **thirty-six consecutive hours**, it.wikipedia roughly **forty**. Firpo is the authority and forty is the outlier, but both are recorded and the hedge is carried in the file rather than silently resolved. He survives, escapes the capital sentence, and is infirm for six months. **[A for event and date]**
- **1602, Castel Nuovo**: ***La città del sole*** in Italian. **[A: Treccani, "trascorre il 1602 componendo … la Città del Sole"]** Latin reworking 1613-14; first printed **Frankfurt 1623** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. An earlier draft set the composition at Sant'Elmo, three years before he was taken there.
- **1605-1607**: ***Atheismus triumphatus***. **Range contradicted**: it.wikipedia 1605-1607, SEP 1606-1607; the single year 1605 is too narrow. **[A within the range]**
- **1609 onward** the *Metaphysica*; **1613** the *Theologia*. **[A: it.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**
- **1612**: **Tobias Adami** visits the cell and afterwards prints Campanella in Germany. **[A: SEP]**
- **1616**: ***Apologia pro Galileo***, published 1622. **[A: it.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]** The claim in circulation that "the two men never meet" is **false** against the attested Padua meeting of 1593.
- **Total imprisonment**: the repeated figure is **twenty-seven years**; Treccani, "quasi ventisette anni." Sources differ on counting from the 1599 arrest or the sentencing after the 1601 torture. **[A for the figure; start year unresolved]**

## 6. Rome under Urban VIII (1626-1634)

- **23 May 1626**: released from Castel Nuovo through the intercession of Cardinal **Maffeo Barberini**, by then Urban VIII. **[A: Treccani; SEP; it.wikipedia]**
- **June 1626**: held briefly at **Castel Sant'Angelo** in transfer to Roman jurisdiction. **[R: itinerary reconstruction, Amabile 1882 and Bonansea 1969; duration unconfirmed]**
- **1627-1629**: Roman supervision in far lighter conditions, moving toward absolution **[A: Bonansea 1969; Ernst 2010]**; full liberty **1629** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **1628**: performs the **astrological countermeasure** against a feared solar eclipse for Urban VIII in the sealed papal apartment: laurel and rosemary burned, two candles and five torches for the seven planets. Recorded by Campanella in *De siderali fato vitando*. **[A: cited in Ernst 2010 and Headley 1997]**
- **1628-1629**: opens a school in Rome; resides at **Santa Maria sopra Minerva**, church of his own 1595 abjuration. **[A: it.wikipedia; Bonansea 1969]**
- **1630-1634**: advisor and astrologer in Urban VIII's circle; revised editions; European correspondence. **[A: Ernst 2010; Headley 1997]**
  - **Correction:** an earlier draft closed this period with "thirty years and one torture session." He was tortured repeatedly: Padua 1594, Rome April 1595, the *corda* at Naples 18 July 1600, the *veglia* 4-5 June 1601.
- **Atlas overlap:** **Athanasius Kircher** reaches Rome November 1633 and takes the Collegio Romano chair January 1634 **[A: kircher.journey.json]**; Campanella is in Rome until October 1634. **No meeting is documented**: the overlap is **[R]**, and the journey file gives it as simultaneity, not acquaintance. Kircher's 1638 Calabrian earthquake journey crosses Campanella's home ground a generation late; also **[R]**.

## 7. France (1634-1639)

- **October 1634**: flees **from Rome**, not Naples, after a new Calabrian conspiracy implicates a former disciple. **[A: SEP; it.wikipedia]**
  - **Correction of a widespread error:** he had been out of Neapolitan custody eight years. it.wikipedia credits **Cardinal Barberini** and the ambassador **François de Noailles** with the escape; **Richelieu's protection came on arrival**, not as the means of it.
- Lands at **Marseille**, aged sixty-six **[A: Bonansea 1969; Amabile 1887]**; **Aix-en-Provence** en route **[R: route in Bonansea, the Aix reception not independently sourced]**.
- **December 1634**: received at the court of **Louis XIII**; **Richelieu** grants protection and a pension; resides at the Dominican convent of **Saint-Honoré** (the Jacobins). **[A: it.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**
- **1637**: collected edition published at Paris; goes on defending Galileo after the 1633 condemnation. **[A: Ernst 2010; Firpo, Bibliografia]**
- **September 1638**: casts a horoscope and performs ritual observances at the birth of the future **Louis XIV**; writes a poem for the birth. **[A: it.wikipedia; court memoirs cited in Headley 1997]**
- **21 May 1639**: dies at the convent on the rue Saint-Honoré, aged seventy; buried in the convent church. **[A: it.wikipedia; en.wikipedia; SEP]**

## 8. Afterlife

- **1623**: *Civitas Solis* printed at **Frankfurt** while its author is still imprisoned. **[A: en.wikipedia, "The City of the Sun"]**
- **1844**: *Oeuvres choisies*, Paris, trans. Jules Rosset, preface by Louise Colet. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**
- **1923**: bronze by **Ernesto Gazzeri**, Piazza Carnovale, Stilo. **[A: it.wikipedia, "Stilo (Italia)"]**
- **1943**: **Luigi Firpo** finds at the Biblioteca Civica of **Trento** a 1602 manuscript of *La città del sole*, the oldest surviving copy; restored 1980. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1973**: Gianni Amelio adapts *La città del sole* for the screen; **1998**: Firpo's *I processi di Tommaso Campanella*; an **asteroid, 4653 Tommaso**, said to be dedicated to him. **[R: it.wikipedia only, none verified beyond the article]**
- **The tomb is a gap.** No source consulted (English, Italian, French Wikipedia; SEP; Treccani) names a surviving grave marker, and none traces the convent of Saint-Honoré past 1639. Stated as a gap, not filled.

## 9. Quotations: verified and not

All *City of the Sun* quotations are from **Thomas W. Halliday's translation in *Ideal Commonwealths*, ed. Henry Morley (London: George Routledge, 1885/1890)**, pp. 225-263, retrieved by Internet Archive full-text search inside `idealcommonweal00morlgoog`. **[A]** Two transcription corrections: p. 233 "the physical sciences, astrology and mathematics" had lost the comma after "sciences" (restored); p. 233 "no one attains to the dignity of Hoh except him who knows the histories" had gained an ellipsis that elided only the word "the" (removed).

**The Roush poem quotation is flagged.** "If You return to earth, come armed—Lord—because enemies are preparing other crosses—not Turks, not Jews—but those of Your own kingdom" appears on en.wikiquote citing **Sherry Roush, *Selected Philosophical Poems of Tommaso Campanella* (University of Chicago Press, 2011), p. 18**. An earlier transcription replaced the em dashes with commas, turning the vocative into "come armed Lord" and changing the sense; the punctuation is restored verbatim in the journey file's `quote` field. **Roush p. 18 could not be inspected**: Google Books 429, HathiTrust 403, Internet Archive search-inside 403. **[A for the wording as Wikiquote prints it; R for the page]**

Two further quotations are **[R]**: "The world is the book where the eternal Wisdom wrote its own concepts" (*Modo di filosofare*), with no translator, edition, or page on the source page; and "I learn more from the anatomy of an ant or a blade of grass...than from all the books which have been written since the beginning of time," a letter of 1607 seen only in **Eisenstein, *The Printing Revolution in Early Modern Europe* (2012), p. 218**, via Wikiquote. The letter itself was not located.
