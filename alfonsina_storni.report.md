# Alfonsina Storni (1892-1938): research report

*2026-08-29. For `alfonsina_storni.journey.json`: 9 segments, 43 stops, gregorian, register "national mythology: the canon is true".*

**[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a dossier claim no independently fetched page confirmed. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Every hedge below also lives in the stop's `date_confidence`, never in campa prose.

The attested spine is the **Centro Virtual Cervantes chronology** (`cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/storni/cronologia/`), cited as CVC01/02/03. Almost everything marked [A] is [A] because CVC prints it. Where CVC and Spanish Wikipedia differ, both readings are given.

## 1. Ticino and the crossing, 1892-1896

Born at **Sala Capriasca**, Ticino, to Alfonso Storni and Paulina Martignoni; CVC01 gives **29 May 1892, seven in the morning** **[A]**.

> **Contradiction, unresolved.** English Wikipedia states **22 May 1892** flatly. Spanish Wikipedia gives 29 May in its main text but carries 22 May among *otras versiones*, along with a version placing the birth **aboard a ship at sea**. The two Wikipedias disagree with each other, not merely with CVC, and the usual explanation, that the 29th was a registration date, is **unsourced**. The file uses 29 May.

Parents married at **Origlio**, 16 October 1886 **[A]**; Alfonso had followed his brothers to San Juan in 1883, into a concern that built, made soda water and ice, and brewed **Cerveza Los Alpes de Storni y Cía** **[A sanjuanalmundo.com; CVC]**. Back to Ticino on medical advice, then out from **Genoa**, reaching Argentina **August 1896** **[A]**. **Origlio is not a stop**, being her parents' wedding six years before her birth: folded into the birth campa, as Genoa is folded into the San Juan stop.

## 2. San Juan and the two Rosario failures, 1896-1907

- **Feb 1897**, kindergarten at the **Escuela Normal de San Juan**, three years of recitation, song, dance, small plays **[A]**; brother **Hildo Alberto** born 28 Aug 1898 **[A]**.
- **Aug 1900**, family to **Rosario**; a café near the **Sunchales station** **[A]**. **Apr 1901**, she leaves regular schooling, her mother opening a private school in the house **[A]**. The dossier compressed this to "1901" and added an **auction** of the café, absent from CVC: **[R]**, excluded.
- **Jul 1902**, the **Almacén Café Suizo, Mendoza 3699 esq. Constitución**, about three months, where she waits tables and washes glasses **[A Spanish Wikipedia for the address, CVC for the venture]**. The dossier's "near Rosario's Estación Central" is wrong, Echesortu not being adjacent to it, and is dropped; CVC says only "calle Mendoza".
- **Aug 1906**, Alfonso dies; at fourteen she apprentices in a **cap/hat factory** **[A]**, unnamed and unlocated in anything reachable.
- **Easter 1907**, plays **San Juan Evangelista** in a Rosario production **[A CVC01]**. The dossier folded this into the 1908 tour; it is a separate CVC entry, restored.

## 3. The Tallaví company and the Coronda diploma, 1908-1911

- **March to August 1908**, tours with the company of the **Spanish actor José Tallaví** through Santa Fe, Córdoba, Mendoza, Santiago del Estero and Tucumán, in repertory linked to Ibsen, Pérez Galdós and Florencio Sánchez **[A CVC01]**. *Two dossier errors:* it dated the tour "1907-1908" and called Tallaví an actor-director. Tucumán stands for the whole circuit, venues per province being **undocumented**. **Excluded [R]:** the retold story that a stage scandal on this tour drove her to a suicide attempt, in no institutional chronology reached.
- **Aug 1908**, leaves for **Bustinza**, Santa Fe **[A]**.
- **1909**, enters the new **Escuela Normal Mixta de Maestros Rurales, Coronda**; **Mercedes Gervasoni** admits her without a complete primary record; **celadora** at about forty pesos a month; diploma end of **1910** **[A]**.
- **Feb 1911**, teaches at **Escuela Elemental No. 65**, Rosario; publishes locally; circle including **Carlos Tercero Arguimbau** **[A]**.

## 4. Buenos Aires: arrival, child, desk, first book, 1912-1916

> **Dossier error.** The supplied chronology conflated two events: CVC puts the move to Buenos Aires in **January 1912** and the birth of her son on **21 April 1912**. The journey splits them.

- **Jan 1912**, leaves Rosario pregnant, with little money, her poems and books by **Rubén Darío** **[A]**. **The arrival station is inferred**: Retiro was the ordinary Rosario terminus and no source names where she got off.
- **21 Apr 1912**, **Alejandro Alfonso Storni** born **[A CVC for the date]**. The hospital, San Roque, later Ramos Mejía, is named **only** by a 2002 SEPA Argentina interview with Alejandro. **The father is never named by her, or by any source reached**, that interview included.
- **1913**, **corresponsal psicológico** at the **olive oil** importers **Freixas Hermanos, Bartolomé Mitre 1411** **[A CVC, address and trade]**. **[R]** for what the campa gives as "later accounts say": ~100 applicants, 200 pesos against a predecessor's 400. Biography, not payroll.
- **Mar 1916**, **La inquietud del rosal**, prologue by **Juan Julián Lastra** dated *Otoño de 1915* **[A CVC02, verified against the digitized first edition]**. Imprint corrected to **Librería "La Facultad", Juan Roldán editor**. Quotation verified in `stornilainquietuddelrosal`, LA LOBA, p. 89; the quatrain **opens with a guillemet** closing only at p. 91.

## 5. 1916-1919: the prize, the table, the passport

- **6 May 1916**, **"Por los niños que han muerto"** in **La Acción**, the Socialist Party paper, published **once only** **[A CVC02, which quotes it]**. The dossier dropped the opening **¡**, and the exclamation does not close until *obrero!* two lines on. Restored; the original issue **not located**.
- **9 May 1916**, the **Nosotros** dinner at the **Restaurante Génova** for **Manuel Gálvez**, with **Giusti, Bianchi, Gerchunoff, Ingenieros** **[A]**. **Nicolás Coronado's** review in **Nosotros no. 83** is quoted by CVC **[A via CVC, original issue not located]** and is the quote on this stop.
- **30 Nov 1916**, first prize at the ninth **Fiesta del Libro**, **Teatro Coliseo**, for *Canto a los niños* as **Piéridas**, 655.55 pesos from the Jockey Club **[A]**. **12 Jun 1917**, homage at the **Teatro Minerva**, Flores **[A]**, folded in: no address found, so no honest pin exists.
- **Jun 1917**, begins teaching at the **Colegio Marcos Paz** **[A]**.
- **Apr 1918**, **El dulce daño** **[A]**. Quotation verified in `stornieldulcedano`, p. 103, with two corrections: the accented **"Tú me quieres alba,"** (the dossier's accentless *Tu* is an OCR artifact on that one line) and the imprint **Sociedad Cooperativa Editorial Limitada**.

## 6. The citizenship year and the years of exhaustion, 1919-1922

- **Jan 1919**, Montevideo: lectures on **Delmira Agustini**, reads her own Spanish versions of **Delfina Bunge de Gálvez's** French poems **[A]**. **Venue unnamed in any source reached**; the pin is the Universidad de la República, a declared approximation reused for 1938.
- **1919**, **Irremediablemente** **[A]**; both quoted stanzas verified against the digitized first edition. The uncredited English *Hombre pequeñito* on poemhunter matches line for line, but **no translator is credited anywhere reachable**, so no English is used.
- **28 Mar to 21 Nov 1919**, permanent contributor to **La Nota**, Emin Arslan's weekly founded 1915 at **25 de Mayo 294**: **thirty-five pieces**, *Convalecer* and *Golondrinas* among them **[A `emin_arslan.journey.json`, corroborated independently by the dossier]**. The corpus's **generic Buenos Aires pin (-34.6037, -58.3816)** is used, the address not being geocoded; that convention is itself attested in `federico_garcia_lorca.journey.json`.
- **9 Nov 1919**, Argentine citizenship **[A]**; **Languidez**, First Municipal and Second National prizes.
  > **Contradiction, unresolved.** CVC groups the book under a November entry beside the citizenship item; **Spanish Wikipedia gives 1920**, the more commonly cited year. The file puts both on one stop at the attested 9 November date and says that is an editorial placement, not a claim they fell together.
- **Apr 1921**, the **Escuela de Niños Débiles**, Parque Chacabuco, amid exhaustion, anxiety and fear of tuberculosis **[A]**; **1923**, a chair of **reading and declamation** at the **Escuela Normal de Lenguas Vivas** **[A]**, folded in, its own pin being -34.5992, -58.3892.
- **From 1921**, annual rest stays at **Los Cocos**, Punilla **[A]**. *Demoted to [R]:* "on **José Ingenieros's** advice", **not in CVC**, which records only the destination and calls him a close friend.

## 7. The cellar, the sea festival, the three performances, 1925-1928

- **29 Jan 1925**, organizes and holds the **first Fiesta de la Poesía at Mar del Plata** **[A]**. **CVC does not place it at La Perla**; the dossier did. The file uses the La Perla shoreline standing for the town.
- **May 1925**, **Ocre** **[A]**, quotation verified against the digitized 1925 second edition; **Sep 1926**, **Poemas de amor** **[A]**, folded in.
- **c. 1926**, joins **La Peña**, led by **Benito Quinquela Martín**, in the **Café Tortoni** cellar **[A CVC02]**, with **Luis Perlotti**, **Baldomero Fernández Moreno**, **Conrado Nalé Roxlo**, **Juana de Ibarbourou**. `luis_perlotti.journey.json` independently notes that **no source it reached dates the gatherings**.
- **10 Mar 1927**, **El amo del mundo** (written as *Dos mujeres*) premieres at the **Teatro Cervantes**, closes after **three performances** **[A CVC03]**.
- **From Oct 1925**, convenes meetings at her tenth-floor flat, **Córdoba 807** **[A CVC; address confirmed by a 1927 letter to Amelia Tuells, Museo de Coronda]**.
- **8 Nov 1928**, **SADE** inaugurated under **Leopoldo Lugones** **[A CVC03]**.
  > **[R] on the roster**: that **Borges, Quiroga, Gálvez, Giusti and Glusberg** sat in the founding leadership comes from the dossier, and **no corpus file documents the 1928 roster**, `borges.journey.json` included. **[A] but undated**: the **Nosotros** comment crediting her energy for SADE is verbatim on CVC, but **CVC neither dates it nor gives an issue number**. It sits inside the 1928 entry while being adduced for her role *from October 1925*, so it may predate 1928, and is cited as an undated comment.

## 8. Two crossings and the Castelar basement, 1929-1934

- **1930**, travels **Spain, France and Switzerland** with **Blanca de la Vega**; **"Diario de navegación"** in **La Nación**, 16 Feb 1930 **[A CVC03]**. **Madrid is not attested**: no Spanish city is named in any source reached. The pin is Madrid as the likeliest node of the Generation of 1927 milieu, and the campa refuses the claim: "Which of them she met, the record does not say."
- **1932**, second European journey **with her son Alejandro** **[A]**; no itinerary documented.
- **1932**, begins attending the **Signo** group at the **Hotel Castelar** **[A]**. *Dossier error:* it dated this 1933; CVC puts her attendance from **1932**, the **1933** entry being the García Lorca meeting.
- **1932**, the **Lugones plagiarism charge** over *Blanco... negro... blanco...* against *El pierrot negro* **[A CVC03]**. **Splice corrected**: the dossier gave two separate quotations, but on CVC they are **one continuous passage** that does not end where the dossier ended it. **Primary print source unlocated**; CVC names none.
- **1933**, meets **Federico García Lorca** at the Castelar **[A CVC03]**. `federico_garcia_lorca.journey.json` dates his Buenos Aires stay **Oct 1933 to Mar 1934** but **does not name Storni**; that file supplies only the month, to order the segment.
- **Nov 1934**, **Mundo de siete pozos**, dedicated to Alejandro **[A]**.

## 9. The knife, the notebook, the sea, 1935-1938

- **20 May 1935**, surgery for a malignant breast tumor **[A CVC and Spanish Wikipedia, which names the *sanatorio Arenales*]**.
  > **Contradiction, unresolved**: CVC records the **right** breast, most derivative biographies the **left**. The campa states both. CVC does not name the hospital; the stop uses the generic Buenos Aires pin.
- **Feb 1936**, **"Film marplatense"** in the daily **Crítica** **[A CVC03]**. The dossier dated the quotation "circa 1930s" and called its context missing; CVC supplies both, and the passage runs past the dossier's cut, whose trailing "..." stood in for a **semicolon**, making it a truncation rather than a source ellipsis.
- **Jan 1937**, south through Argentina and Chile; **"Carné de ventanilla"** in **La Nación** **[A]**. **Honest weakness**: the route is documented only as "Argentina and Chile", so **Santiago (-33.4489, -70.6693) is not an attested stop**; it stands for the Chilean leg, as CVC's own account lets Tucumán stand for the 1908 tour.
- **Feb 1937**, learns on returning of **Horacio Quiroga's** suicide **[A]**; no detail beyond the fact is asserted, the pool supplying none.
- **27 Jan 1938**, shares a Montevideo platform with **Gabriela Mistral** and **Juana de Ibarbourou** **[A]**. *Correction:* "Entre un par de maletas a medio abrir y la manecilla del reloj" is a **conferencia she pronounced**, not a poem she read. Venue still unverified.
- **Aug 1938**, **Antología poética** (Espasa-Calpe); **Sep 1938**, **Mascarilla y trébol** **[A]**.
- **18 Oct 1938**, train from **Constitución**, seen off by Alejandro and **Lidia Oriolo de Pizzigatti**; posts **"Voy a dormir"** to *La Nación*, printed **25 Oct 1938**, the day of her death **[A CVC; the platform party from the dossier, corroborated only in outline]**.
  > **Translation problem, unresolved.** The circulating English ("Teeth of flowers, hairnet of dew…") is verbatim on poemhunter and renders the Spanish faithfully line for line, checked against **es.wikisource**, but poemhunter carries **no translator credit** and it traces to no credited edition. The file quotes **only the Spanish**.
- **18-24 Oct 1938**, lodges in the **Oriolo** house on **calle Tres de Febrero**; letters to Alejandro on the 19th and 22nd, a last letter to **Manuel Gálvez** about her son **[A in outline, CVC and a SADE manuscripts record cited in the dossier]**. **Street number undocumented**.
- **25 Oct 1938**, death at **playa La Perla**, the most corrected item in the pool.
  > argentina.gob.ar records that she **threw herself from the espigón del Club Argentino de Mujeres** and that **one of her shoes, caught in the ironwork, fixed the exact spot**; CVC records the body "rescatado horas más tarde" and sent to Buenos Aires for the wake. The dossier's "**washed ashore that morning**" and "two workers find her body" are **in neither source** and are removed. The hour, about one in the morning, is **[R]**.
  > **Legend flagged, not converted to fact.** English Wikipedia: "Although her biographers hold that she jumped into the water from a breakwater, a popular legend is that she slowly walked into the sea until she drowned." No eyewitness account exists in any source reached, and the campa ends on that. English Wikipedia confirms **Ariel Ramírez and Félix Luna** wrote *Alfonsina y el mar* but **gives no 1969**, and neither Wikipedia mentions **Mercedes Sosa's** recording or a **Chabuca Granda** cover: **[R]**.

## 10. After

- **Late Oct 1938**, waked at the **Club Argentino de Mujeres in Buenos Aires** **[A CVC]**: a **Buenos Aires** event, distinct from the Mar del Plata espigón of the same club's name, a conflation the dossier's phrasing invited. Buried in the **Recoleta** vault of **Salvadora Medina Onrubia de Botana** **[A CVC, Spanish Wikipedia]**.
- **21 Nov 1938**, Senate homage with **Alfredo Palacios** speaking: **[R], weaker than the dossier suggested**, being **absent from CVC01-03** and found on **no page reached**.
- **22 Sep 1963**, transfer to the artists' sector of **Chacarita**. Interment there is confirmed **[A Spanish Wikipedia, independently cited by two existing atlas files]**; the **1963 date is [R]**, widely repeated and confirmed on no page fetched.

## Gaps stated as gaps

The father of Alejandro Storni · the Rosario cap factory, unnamed and unlocated · the Teatro Minerva, Flores, no address and therefore no pin · the Montevideo venues of 1919 and 1938 · the Spanish city of the 1930 trip, Madrid a declared guess · the 1937 Chilean route, Santiago a declared stand-in · the Oriolo lodging's street number · the original *La Acción* issue and *Nosotros* no. 83, quoted only through CVC · the print source of the 1932 Lugones defence · the translator of the circulating English *Voy a dormir* · **María Sofía "Fifí" Kusrow's house at Real de San Carlos, Colonia**, a recurring residence in the dossier that no fetched source confirms and whose pin was also wrong, so the stop was **dropped** rather than staged on two unverified facts · Storni's bust in the Jardín de los Poetas, unconfirmed, below.

**No corpus file exists** for Horacio Quiroga, José Ingenieros, Gabriela Mistral, Juana de Ibarbourou, Manuel Gálvez, Alfredo Palacios or Salvadora Medina Onrubia: the most load-bearing relationships in the life, with nothing to interlock against beyond CVC.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Storni is **not** on `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Relations not rooted in her own life are kept and flagged here rather than silently deleted, with the direction named.

1. **FORWARD, excluded: the peña Urania roster.** `raul_veroni_padre.journey.json` records that his father's peña listed Storni among the poets it claimed, and that file itself flags the claim as false, Urania not existing until 1943. A posthumous fabrication by someone else, so it stays on Veroni's file and appears nowhere here.
2. **FORWARD, excluded: the 1957 film *Alfonsina*.** Kurt Land's biopic starring **Amelia Bence**, who had studied under Storni at the **Teatro Infantil Labardén** **[R, dossier citing es.wikipedia's film article, not re-fetched]**. Bence's relation to Storni is direct and one-directional, but the film is Land's and Bence's work, nineteen years after Storni's death. Not in the journey file; if wanted it belongs on a Bence or Land file, or in Storni's `suggested_refs`.
3. **FORWARD, kept: posthumous editions of her own writing, and the zamba.** *Un libro quemado* (Editorial Excursiones, 2016, collecting her 1919-1921 *La Nota* and *La Nación* articles) closes the La Nota campa; Muschietti's *Obras. Prosa* (Losada, 2002) is cited on the Teatro Cervantes stop for the unstaged 1927 play *La debilidad de Mister Dougall*; the 1969 zamba is glossed at La Perla. All three concern **her own artifacts and her own death** rather than importing another person's framework, and `abdelkader.journey.json` closes on the 1966 repatriation the same way. Flagged anyway, since strictly read they are later artifacts inside an earlier file's clock. **Muschietti**, **Tania Diz** and **Claudia Edith Méndez** appear only in sources, never in a campa.
4. **BACKWARD: none.** Storni applied no theoretical framework to earlier material; nothing in the pool needs the exception.
