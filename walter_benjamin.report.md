# Walter Benjamin (1892-1940): research report

*2026-08-28. Written from the verified research pool for `walter_benjamin.journey.json`. Legend:
**[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried on general
knowledge this pass did not independently source. Contradictions are flagged and left standing,
never adjudicated. Subject: German-Jewish critic, essayist, translator, radio writer, collector,
born in Berlin under the Kaiser, dead by his own hand at a Spanish border post in 1940, never the
holder of an academic post.*

---

## 1. Berlin, 1892-1912

- Born **15 July 1892**, Berlin-Tiergarten, to Emil Benjamin and Pauline née Schönflies, a
  prosperous assimilated Jewish family **[A: Deutsche Biographie gnd118509039; de.wiki]**. Family
  villa, **Delbrückstraße 23, Grunewald [A: de.wiki]**, since demolished.
- **Kaiser-Friedrich-Schule, Charlottenburg**, from 1901 **[A: en.wiki]**. Recurrent illness and
  dislike of conventional schooling **[R: the pool's geography lens graded this [R]]**.
- **1905-1907**, Hermann Lietz's reform school at **Haubinda**, Thuringia; **Gustav Wyneken**
  becomes the decisive authority of his adolescence **[A: SEP; de.wiki]**. **Dropped claim:** that
  he signed himself "Ardor" here, unconfirmable and not carried. **The pool's Haubinda pin was
  ~45 km out**, the largest of thirteen coordinate errors corrected in the file.
- The Charlottenburg-childhood stop is graded **[R]**: the quarter is attested, no address is, and
  the date is a placement inside the 1890s.

## 2. Freiburg, Berlin, Munich, 1912-1917

- **1912**: Abitur, then **Freiburg**, philosophy / German literature / psychology; attends
  **Heinrich Rickert [A: en.wiki; Deutsche Biographie; SEP]**.
- Returns to Berlin the same year; **Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität**, Free Students movement,
  eventually **president of the Berlin Freie Studentenschaft**; hears **Georg Simmel [A: en.wiki;
  SEP]**. **Gap:** the presidency's term dates are nowhere fixed, so the file dates the stop 1914
  and says so in `date_confidence`.
- **8 August 1914**: **Fritz Heinle** and **Rika Seligson** die by gas in the student meeting rooms
  **[A: de.wiki]**. Friends read it as a protest against the war; **later biographers leave the
  reading unresolved [A]**, and so does the file. **Gap:** the building is unidentified in every
  reachable source, and the pin is flagged approximate. **[R]:** the break with Wyneken over his
  war enthusiasm is standard in the literature but not in the pool.
- **1915**: meets **Gershom Scholem**; **1915-1917**, **Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität, Munich
  [A: en.wiki; de.wiki; SEP]**. **[R]:** the 1916 language essay is general knowledge.

## 3. Switzerland, 1917-1920

- **17 April 1917**: marries **Dora Sophie Kellner**, previously married to Max Pollak
  **[A: de.wiki]**. **Correction:** the pool gave the place as "unspecified (likely Berlin or
  Switzerland)", but de.wiki "Dora Sophie Kellner" has them marrying and *then* going to
  Switzerland, so the wedding was in Germany. The file pins Berlin and marks the inference.
- **11 April 1918**: son **Stefan Rafael** born at Bern **[A: de.wiki "Dora Sophie Kellner"]**;
  the pool carried only the bare year.
- **1919**: doctorate under **Richard Herbertz**, Bern, on the concept of criticism in German
  Romanticism, **summa cum laude [A: SEP]**.
- **1920**: return to Berlin **[A: SEP]**, first at **Erich Gutkind's** house in Grunewald
  **[R: de.wiki; his address is unestablished, so the file reuses the Delbrückstraße pin]**.

## 4. Berlin and Munich, 1921-1925

- **1921**: **"Zur Kritik der Gewalt" [A: en.wiki]**.
- **1921**: buys Klee's **Angelus Novus** in Munich for **1,000 marks [A: de.wiki; Verso]**.
  - **Contradiction, unresolved.** de.wiki "Walter Benjamin" gives **April 1921**, de.wiki "Angelus
    Novus" **"Mai oder Anfang Juni 1921"**. German Wikipedia contradicts itself; the file dates
    1921-04-01 and prints both readings.
  - **Anachronistic pin.** The pool put the gallery on Briennerstraße; de.wiki "Hans Goltz" has
    the Galerie Neue Kunst at the **Odeonsplatz** from 1912, moving to Briennerstraße only in
    **1928**. The site keeps the pool's **[R]** grade. The work is a Klee monoprint of **1920**.
- **1924**, Capri: **Ernst Bloch**, **Lukács**, **Asja Lācis**, and the start of the *Trauerspiel*
  research **[A: SEP; de.wiki]**. **1925**: "Neapel" with Lācis **[A: SEP]**.
- **1925**: withdraws **Ursprung des deutschen Trauerspiels** from the **University of Frankfurt**
  after **Hans Cornelius** and **Franz Schultz** find it unsuitable, ending the academic career
  **[A: SEP; en.wiki; UChicago Press]**.
  - **Pin policy.** The truer coordinate is the Bockenheim campus (~50.1180, 8.6523), but the
    interlock lens found a **canonical corpus pin for Frankfurt, 50.1109, 8.6821**, already used by
    Horkheimer, Weil, and Marcuse. Canonical pins are inherited byte-identical, so the file uses it
    and records the campus coordinate in `date_confidence`.

## 5. 1926-1930: Moscow, the street, the microphone, the divorce

- **Dec 1926 - Feb 1927**, Moscow: officially on commission, personally for Lācis; the *Moskauer
  Tagebuch*; **he does not join the Party [A: en.wiki; de.wiki; SEP]**.
- **1927**: begins the arcades notes with **Franz Hessel** at the **Bibliothèque nationale, rue de
  Richelieu [A: en.wiki; de.wiki]**.
  - **Anachronism in the pool.** Its afterlife lens pinned the BnF at **Tolbiac**, opened **1996**;
    its geography lens had Richelieu right, and the file uses Richelieu for both 1927 and 1940.
  - **[R]:** the Benjamin/Hessel **Proust** translation is named in the campa on general
    knowledge; Hessel as arcades co-conceiver *is* in the pool, the same partnership.
- **1928**: **Einbahnstraße**, dedicated to Lācis, and the *Trauerspiel* book **[A: en.wiki]**.
- **1927 - Jan 1933**: radio for **Berlin** and **Frankfurt**, roughly **eighty scripts**, most
  broadcasts lost **[A: en.wiki; Verso]**.
  - **Both radio pins in the pool corresponded to no broadcasting site.** The file uses the **Haus
    des Rundfunks** for Berlin, noting it opened only in **1931** and that earlier broadcasts came
    from the **Vox-Haus**, and an **Eschersheimer Landstraße** point for Frankfurt **[R]**.
- **Divorce: a contradiction inside German Wikipedia, left standing.** "Dora Sophie Kellner" gives
  **27 March 1930**, decree pronounced with Benjamin at fault; "Walter Benjamin" gives **April 1930
  [A, both articles]**. File uses 27 March and prints the disagreement.

## 6. 1932-1938: Ibiza, exile, the Institute

- **1932**: **Ibiza**, near Felix Noeggerath at Sant Antoni **[A: de.wiki]**; at **Nice**, drafts
  a will and a chronicle and prepares to kill himself **[R: SEP; month not fixed]**.
- **Leaves Germany permanently in September 1933 [A: en.wiki; de.wiki]**.
  - **Contradiction inside the pool**, resolved against its own geography lens, which dated the
    arrival **March 1933** citing SEP. SEP supports no March date, and the pool's chronology lens
    says 1933-09. **[R]:** the post-1933 pseudonyms are general knowledge.
- **Sanremo**: Dora's pension, the **Villa Emily / Villa Verde**, once Edward Lear's residence
  **[A: de.wiki]**. **Gap:** the address was never established, so the stop is labelled Sanremo
  rather than pretending to a building pin.
- **1934-1938**: repeated stays with **Brecht** at **Skovsbostrand, Svendborg [A: de.wiki]**.
  **[R]:** the chess and Brecht's "Jewish fascism" verdict on the Kafka essay come from the
  *Gespräche mit Brecht* literature.
- **1936**: French publication of the **Work of Art** essay in the **Zeitschrift für
  Sozialforschung**, altered through editorial negotiation **[A: de.wiki]**.
  - **Institute location, corrected.** The pool's geography lens titled a stop "Institute for
    Social Research" and pinned it in central **Paris**; from 1934 it was in **New York**. The file
    has **no Institute stop**: stipend and editing are told from Benjamin's Paris end.
- **1937-1939**: the **Collège de Sociologie** and **Georges Bataille**; Benjamin objects to the
  group's aestheticization of sacrifice and myth **[A: de.wiki]**.
- **1938**: **Adorno** rejects the first Baudelaire essay for lacking dialectical mediation, the
  rewrite appearing in 1939 **[A: de.wiki; en.wiki]**.
- **1938-1940**: last settled address, **10 rue Dombasle**, 15e **[A: en.wiki]**.

## 7. 1939-1940: statelessness, the camp, the theses, the border

- **1939**: stripped of German citizenship; **Sept-Nov 1939**, interned as an enemy national near
  **Nevers**, released in November **[A: AdK passport record; de.wiki]**.
  - **Contradiction narrowed, not closed.** The pool set "Clos Saint-Joseph near Nevers" against
    de.wiki's "Château de Vernuche, Varennes-Vauzelles" as an even standoff, but **Clos
    Saint-Joseph could not be sourced in any form**. File pins Varennes-Vauzelles, prints both.
    **[R]:** the straw, the internee lecture course, the writers' petition.
- **1940**: completes **"Über den Begriff der Geschichte" [A: en.wiki]**. **[R]:** the letter to
  **Gretel Adorno** about not publishing. The copy given to **Hannah Arendt** is **[A]**.
- Entrusts the arcades manuscripts to **Bataille**, who hides them in the Bibliothèque nationale
  **[A: Verso; en.wiki]**.
- **13 June 1940**: flees Paris to **Lourdes**; **August 1940**, a US entry visa at **Marseille**
  and no French exit permit **[A: en.wiki]**.
- **25 September 1940**: crosses the Pyrenees from **Banyuls-sur-Mer** to **Portbou** on **Lisa
  Fittko's** route, carrying a heavy black briefcase **whose contents and fate remain unknown [A
  for the crossing: en.wiki; SEP on Fittko, whose memoir is the sole witness for the briefcase]**.
  The mountain-route stop is graded **[R]**: only the general course is confirmed.
- **26/27 September 1940**: refused onward passage, dies at the **Hotel de Francia** of a morphine
  overdose **[A: en.wiki; de.wiki]**.
  - **Date contradiction, left standing.** en.wiki and de.wiki give **26 September**; the
    **Portbou register, certificate no. 25**, is dated **27 September**, and **SEP agrees with the
    27**. The file dates the hotel stop 26 and the registry stop 27, and prints the dispute.
  - **Cause of death, left standing.** The certificate records **cerebral apoplexy**, not overdose;
    no autopsy, no toxicology. Same source: the name inverted as **"Benjamin Walter"**, age 48, of
    Berlin, religion **Roman Catholic**, and the hotel's four-day bill **[A: UChicago Press]**. The
    Hotel de Francia no longer operates and cannot be geocoded, so the stop reads as the town.
- **28 September 1940**: **Henny Gurland** pays for a **five-year niche rental**; the priest's
  receipt of 1 October is for **96 pesetas**, with a carpenter's receipt for a cloth-lined coffin
  and eight pesetas to a bricklayer **[A: UChicago Press]**. **Correction:** the **Catholic**
  cemetery, not the "municipal" one, a consequence of the register entry; the pool gave it two
  coordinates, ~500 m and ~1.1 km out. The rental **lapsed** and the burial place is unverifiable.
- **27 September 1940**: the rest of the party crosses into Spain **[R: Gurland's account in
  secondary literature; no primary Gurland document reached]**.

## 8. After

- **1942**: brother **Georg Benjamin** dies in the Mauthausen system **[A: Deutsche Biographie]**.
  Not a stop: Walter was already dead and it is Georg's death.
- Editions: **1955** *Schriften*, ed. T. W. and Gretel Adorno, later criticized by Arendt and
  Brecht-aligned readers as muting his Marxism **[A: Suhrkamp with SEP]**; **1972-1989**
  *Gesammelte Schriften*; **1968** Arendt's *Illuminations*, trans. Zohn **[A for edition and
  translator: SEP; publisher [R]]**; **1982** *Passagen-Werk*, ed. Tiedemann; **1999** *The Arcades
  Project*, Harvard **[A: en.wiki]**.
- **1987**: **Fania Scholem** donates the **Angelus Novus** to the **Israel Museum**, after it
  passed from the Paris flat through Adorno in New York to Gershom Scholem **[A: Verso; en.wiki]**.
  This is the file's last stop: the object was Benjamin's own and its destination was set by his
  will, on the precedent of `paul_klee.journey.json`.
- **15 May 1994**: **Karavan's** *Passages* beside the Portbou cemetery, glass etched with the
  "memory of the anonymous" passage **[A: Fundació Angelus Novus]**. **Correction:** the initiative
  came from the **AsKI association in Bonn** with German federal states and the **Generalitat de
  Catalunya**, not from the Spanish state as the pool had it. Not a stop; it is Karavan's work.
- **2005** Mauas, *Who Killed Walter Benjamin?* **[A; its assassination theories produce no
  demonstrated perpetrator]**; **2005/2006** Taussig, *Walter Benjamin's Grave*, UCP **[A via
  OpenLibrary; year disputed, unresolved]**; **2017** The Jewish Museum, *The Arcades* **[A]**;
  **Walter Benjamin Archiv**, AdK Berlin, ~5,350 units **[R: page not re-fetched]**.

## Quotations

Thirteen stops carry quotations, all verbatim from fetched sources. Two were misquoted in the pool
and are corrected in the file:

1. **The farewell note.** Pool: "…no other choice but to **make an end**." Published English
   (*Selected Writings* vol. 4, Harvard/Belknap): "…but to **make an end of it**." **The caveat is
   kept in `quote_source`:** de.wiki records that **Gurland destroyed the original** and wrote the
   surviving version from memory, so **no autograph exists**. **[R]** wording, **[A]** substance.
2. **"Unpacking My Library."** Pool: "Of all ways of acquiring books, writing them oneself is
   regarded as most praiseworthy." Zohn has "Of all **the** ways" and "**the** most praiseworthy
   **method**."
3. **"the Jews represent an elite in the ranks of the spiritually active."** Real, but the source
   must not stop at Wikipedia: traced to **Gesammelte Schriften II:839**, quoted in **Witte,
   *Walter Benjamin: An Intellectual Biography***, and presented as a mid-sentence fragment.
   **Gap:** addressee and year unconfirmed, left unstated rather than guessed.

The theses and *Work of Art* quotations come from marxists.org (Redmond, Zohn).

**Anachronistic quote placement.** Some stops carry 1940 or 1935 text at an earlier date, Thesis IX
at the 1921 purchase above all. Each `quote_source` gives the composition date, as
`abdelkader.journey.json` does.

## Interlocks

**Named in a campa**, Benjamin having a direct or one-directional relation:

- **`paul_klee`**: bought the *Angelus Novus* in 1921 and wrote Thesis IX about it; Klee's file
  carries the reciprocal stop. Named twice.
- **`max_horkheimer`**: his Institute paid Benjamin's stipend and published the Work of Art essay;
  his own file dates a Paris meeting to 1937. Named at the 1936 stop.
  - **Discrepancy between two corpus files, flagged not resolved.** `max_horkheimer.journey.json`
    says Benjamin died at Portbou "with an exit visa that arrived a day late." The evidence gives a
    **refused-transit** and morphine reconstruction with no visa-timing failure: he **had** the US
    entry visa and Spanish and Portuguese transit visas, and lacked the **French exit permit**,
    which never came at all. Worth the operator's review.
- **`marcel_proust`**: Benjamin and Hessel translated the *Recherche* into German. Named at the
  1927 Bibliothèque nationale stop, graded **[R]** above.

**Found by the lens and deliberately not named**, the relation being shared geography or someone
else's engagement rather than Benjamin's own act:

- **`erich_fromm`**: married Henny Gurland in 1944. Benjamin's relation is to **Gurland**, not a
  corpus traveler; she is named often in the file, Fromm never.
- **`felix_weil`**: funded the Institute that later paid Benjamin. No personal relation on record.
- **`herbert_marcuse`**: quoted Benjamin at Frankfurt in **1979**. Marcuse's stop, Marcuse's file.
- **`andre_breton`, `claude_levi_strauss`**: Marseille and the Fry apparatus, 1940-41. Shared city
  only, and the map already shows co-location.
- **`jacques_lacan`, `nick_land`, `gilles_de_rais`**: linked only through Bataille, not a corpus
  traveler, who reaches Benjamin independently.
- **`w_g_sebald`**: wrote directly about Benjamin's suitcase and death at Portbou, one-directionally
  and for real, but it belongs **on Sebald's file**.

**QUEUE.md framing, flagged.** The queue entry says Benjamin died at Portbou "fleeing the Gestapo."
No Gestapo pursuit order is established anywhere reachable: he was a stateless Jewish leftist whose
safety collapsed structurally, stopped by **Spanish** officials enforcing a French-exit-visa rule.
The file does not use the phrase; the operator may want to soften the queue line.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Walter Benjamin is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** Nothing is silently deleted; the relations
are named so the operator can rule. **Direction caught: forward, in every case.** No backward
relation appeared in the pool, and none was invented.

1. **Karavan's *Passages*, 1994**: a later artist's work at Benjamin's grave. *Handled:* not a
   stop, cited in `suggested_refs` on Benjamin's own cemetery stop, the permitted move.
2. **The posthumous editions**: *Schriften* 1955, *Illuminations* 1968, *Passagen-Werk* 1982, the
   Harvard *Arcades Project* 1999. *Handled:* no stops, `suggested_refs` on the works' own stops.
3. **The Jewish Museum 2017 show, Taussig, Mauas, the Chemin Walter Benjamin, the AdK archive.**
   *Handled:* `suggested_refs` only, except the Chemin, which gets one clause in the crossing campa
   because it retraces Benjamin's own route, the campa adding that it was laid over ground nobody
   surveyed at the time.
4. **The one stop that postdates his death: Jerusalem, 1987.** Benjamin owned the Klee and willed
   it to Scholem, so the destination follows his own act, on the precedent of Klee's file. If the
   operator reads that as a forward reference, delete the stop and move the Israel Museum line into
   `suggested_refs` on the Munich purchase.

**No case was found for adding Benjamin to the list.** His apparatus (the angel of history, the
aura, the dialectical image, the flâneur) is nowhere applied to material he did not treat; applying
it backward onto an earlier subject would need a new line in the exceptions table first.
