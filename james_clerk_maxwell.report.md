## 1. Glenlair and the Edinburgh schoolrooms, 1831-1850

**Birth**, 13 June 1831, 14 India Street, Edinburgh, to John Clerk Maxwell, advocate, and Frances
Cay **[A: MacTutor; Foundation chronology; Wikipedia]**. Older reference works print a November
date; modern consensus is June. The parish register was not consulted (gap). **Glenlair**, 1833:
the family moves to the Middlebie land in Kirkcudbrightshire **[A: Foundation chronology;
Wikipedia]**.

**Childhood sayings**, all Campbell and Garnett (1882), verified against the Internet Archive OCR
**[A]**: "What's the go o' that? What does it do?" with its reiteration "But what's the particular
go of it?", from his cousin Mrs (Jemima) Blackburn (*page correction: p.28, not p.29*, per the
running heads); "It is the sun, papa; I got it in with the tin plate" (Mrs Murdoch, p.29); "Master
James is in great go" (his father to Jane Cay, 25 April 1834, p.27, continued in the mother's hand
with "Show me how it doos"). The nurse's stone-colour exchange (p.31) is **not used**: no stop fits
it without invention.

**Frances Cay dies** of abdominal cancer, December 1839, Maxwell aged eight **[A: Wikipedia]**.
The geography lens says he later died at "the same age" as his mother; that arithmetic is not
established in anything reachable here, so the journey claims only the same disease in the same
part of the body.

**Edinburgh Academy**, November 1841, and the nickname "Dafty" **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**;
classmates Lewis Campbell, later his biographer, and Peter Guthrie Tait. The lodging at 31 Heriot
Row with his aunt Isabella Wedderburn is confirmed **[A: Wikipedia]** but has **no reliable
coordinate**, so it is folded into the Academy campa rather than given a stop at an invented pin.

**Oval curves**, 1846, at fourteen; paper communicated by James David Forbes to the Royal Society
of Edinburgh, **6 April 1846** **[A: MacTutor; Foundation chronology]**.

**University of Edinburgh**, November 1847, under Philip Kelland, Forbes and Sir William Hamilton
**[A: MacTutor; Foundation chronology]**. *Disambiguation written into the campa:* this is Sir
William Hamilton the metaphysician, not William Rowan Hamilton of Dublin whose quaternions Maxwell
later used. The conflation is common; the campa names both to block it.

## 2. Cambridge, 1850-1856

**Peterhouse**, October 1850, then Trinity before the term ends; **Second Wrangler**, January
1854, behind Edward Routh, bracketed equal for the Smith's Prize; **Fellow of Trinity**,
10 October 1855; **father's death** at Glenlair, 3 April 1856 **[A, all four: MacTutor;
Wikipedia]**.

**Otley, Suffolk**, summer 1853. The pool said "exact parish not established". The verification
pass **raised [R] to [A]**: Wikipedia's Charles Benjamin Tayler article has him rector of Otley
from 1846 and states "Maxwell visited Otley in 1853", brought by Tayler's nephew George Wood Henry
Tayler, a Trinity undergraduate. The evangelical commitment dating from the illness is **[A:
Wikipedia]**.

**'On Faraday's Lines of Force'.** *Contradiction inside the pool, flagged and left standing:* the
chronology lens dates it 1855, the geography lens 1855-1856. MacTutor: "Read to Cambridge
Philosophical Society in 1855 and 1856." Dating the whole paper to 1855 drops Part II. The stop is
dated to the first reading with the two-part structure stated in `date_confidence`. Both
methodological quotations were verified against raw Wikisource HTML **[A]**; the first is used,
the second ("By a physical analogy I mean...") is not placed and is recorded here.

## 3. Aberdeen, 1856-1860

**Marischal chair**, November 1856, at twenty-five; **chair lost**, 1860, when Marischal and
King's merge, one natural-philosophy chair survives and the senior David Thomson keeps it **[A:
MacTutor; Foundation chronology]**.

**Saturn's rings: a two-year date error, corrected.** The chronology lens dated the Adams Prize to
1859, following Wikipedia's Maxwell article. **Three sources give 1857**: MacTutor, the Foundation
chronology, and Wikipedia's own Adams Prize winners list; 1859 is the monograph's publication
year. The journey uses both dates in their right places and names the conflict in
`date_confidence`.

**Marriage to Katherine Mary Dewar.** *Contradiction, unresolved:* Wikipedia and the Foundation
chronology give **2 June 1858** at Old Machar; MacTutor gives **June 1859**. 1858 is used and the
discrepancy is named in the stop. Engagement February 1858 **[A]**.

**Colour work at Aberdeen** (the spinning tops, colour as three measurable quantities, colour
blindness measured rather than described) is attested in substance but not to a date; the stop is
dated 1859 only to order the segment and says so **[A substance, R date]**.

## 4. London, 1860-1865

**Two wrong venues, both corrected. The largest factual fix in the file.** The geography lens put
the 1861 FRS election *and* the 8 December 1864 reading of 'A Dynamical Theory of the
Electromagnetic Field' at **Somerset House**, citing Wikipedia's Royal Society article for the
claim that the Society stayed there until 1873. Wikipedia's Somerset House article says "In 1857,
the Royal Society moved out of Somerset House," and its Burlington House article has the Society
there from 1857: the Royal Society article conflates that move with the 1873 move into the East
Wing of the same courtyard. Both stops sit at Burlington House **[A]**.

**On the Theory of Compound Colours** and the Rumford Medal, 1860, month unknown; **smallpox at
Glenlair**, 1860, month estimated; **King's College London chair** 1860 and **FRS** 1861; **'A
Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field'** read 8 December 1864, printed 1865 **[A, all
four: MacTutor; Wikipedia; Foundation chronology]**.

**The tartan ribbon**, 17 May 1861 **[A with a caveat carried forward]**: the verifier could not
re-fetch a primary source for the date or Thomas Sutton's role, confirming only the Albemarle
Street address. The stop states this.

**'On Physical Lines of Force'**, 1861-62 **[A: Wikipedia; Wikisource]**; the quotation on light as
transverse undulations of the same medium was verified against raw Wikisource HTML **[A]**.
*Deliberately omitted:* the figures usually quoted for Maxwell's calculated wave speed and
Fizeau's measured light speed, and the names Weber and Kohlrausch, none verified in this pool. The
campa says "a ratio of electrical units" rather than invent a number.

**Resignation from King's**, spring 1865. *Age error corrected:* the pool said "aged thirty-four",
but MacTutor dates the departure to spring 1865 and he turned thirty-four only on 13 June, so he
was **thirty-three**.

## 5. Glenlair as independent scholar, 1865-1871

**The riding injury and erysipelas**, 1865 **[R]**: Campbell and Garnett via secondary summaries,
no primary medical record reached. Tagged as such in the stop.

**The letter to Tait and the "very observant and neat-fingered being"**, 1867 **[A: Wikipedia,
citing the 1867 Tait correspondence]**, the verifier not having re-fetched the letter. Thomson's
later naming of the creature a demon is **[A]** in the same source.

**'On Governors'**, 1868 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the campa avoids the phrase "control theory" and any
claim about when the field was named. **Keith Prize**, 1869 **[A: Foundation chronology]**, folded
into the Theory of Heat campa to keep inside the stop ceiling.

**Theory of Heat.** *Year contradiction, resolved with reasoning shown:* the pool gave 1870, from
the Foundation chronology's compressed line "1870 - Multiple honors and publications including
Theory of Heat", which bundles several years. Wikipedia states flatly "He wrote the textbook Theory
of Heat (1871)." **1871** is used, and the conflict is named.

## 6. The Cavendish, 1871-1879

**First Cavendish Professor** 1871, laboratory designed by Maxwell and **opened 16 June 1874**;
**A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism**, 1873; **the Cavendish manuscripts** edited 1874-1879
and published 1879 **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia; Foundation chronology]**. *Deliberately omitted:* the
widely repeated claim that Thomson and Helmholtz were offered the chair first. Not in this pool.

**The Eranus essay on free will**, 11 February 1873 **[A: Campbell and Garnett, p.441, which
prints it entire]**; the quotation is Maxwell's own prose. *House-style note:* the 1882 printing
carries an em dash inside it. Quote fields are verbatim and exempt from the no-em-dash rule, so it
stands as printed, the only em dash in the journey file.

"The lip of truth shall be established for ever" (Proverbs 12:19) is not Maxwell's composition but
a text he hung in his private room, noted by F. J. A. Hort (Campbell and Garnett, p.419) **[A]**,
and the `quote_source` says so.

**Rede Lecture on the telephone**, 1878. *Tag raised [R] to [A]:* the Foundation chronology lists
it directly, "1878 Rede Lecture on telephone; Volta Medal". **The venue stays unconfirmed**, so the
pin is central Cambridge.

**No stop, recorded so the operator sees they were found and left out on purpose:** the 1875
Victoria Institute refusal **[R]**, secondary treatment of Maxwell's religious views with no
primary letter reached; and the American Philosophical Society election of 1875 or 1876 **[R]**,
whose date the Foundation chronology itself flags as inconsistent, an honorary election in absentia
rather than a journey.

## 7. The last year and the burial

**Last lecture course**, May 1879 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the Clifford remark is Maxwell's own words
recorded by Hort, 4 February 1882 (p.420) **[A]**. **Lorraine's letter**, 5 October 1879, to Dr
Paget (p.412) **[A]**. **Return to Cambridge**, 8 October 1879 **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**;
Guillemard's report of Maxwell asking for the Burial Service prayer is his letter of 9 December
1879 to the Rev. Isaac Bowman (pp.415-416) **[A]**.

**Death**, 5 November 1879, aged 48 **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia; Foundation chronology]**. *Page
correction:* Colin Mackenzie's eyewitness account of "God help me! God help my wife!" and "Lay me
down lower, for I am very low myself, and it suits me to lie low" sits on **p.416, not p.415**,
per the running heads. *Gap:* no source reached names the house or street where he died, so the
stop uses a central-Cambridge pin and says so.

**Burial at Parton Kirk**, November 1879, within the ruins of the Old Kirk, his father in the same
yard and Katherine (d. 1886) later in the same plot **[A: glenlair.org.uk; Wikipedia]**. *The exact
burial day is not established*; 8 November only orders the segment after the death, and
`date_confidence` says so rather than pretend to a source.

## 8. Posthumous

**Editions** **[A]**: Treatise 2nd ed. by W. D. Niven 1881, 3rd ed. by J. J. Thomson 1892
(Wikipedia); Niven's *Scientific Papers* 1890 and Harman's *Scientific Letters and Papers*
1990/1995/2002 (MacTutor bibliography; volume dates not re-verified, **[R]**).

**Glenlair fire, 1929.** *Direct contradiction between the two lenses, resolved:* the afterlife
lens tagged it **[R]** as unconfirmable; the geography lens tagged it **[A]** citing Wikipedia,
'Glenlair House' ("A significant fire occurred in 1929 that left the house gutted"). The geography
lens is right. Written as **[A]**.

**Westminster Abbey memorial stone**, 30 September 1931, north of Newton's grave, beside one for
Faraday, replaced by a cast-iron version in 1976 **[A: Abbey commemorations page]**. **The maxwell
(Mx)**, recommended at Paris in 1900 and reaffirmed at Oslo in 1930 **[A: Wikipedia]**, is folded
into that campa: neither Paris nor Oslo is a place Maxwell went.

**14 India Street as museum** and the **George Street statue**, both *raised [R] to [A]*: Wikipedia
documents Sydney Ross founding the Foundation in 1977 and its purchase and refurbishment of the
house in 1993, and names Alexander Stoddart as the statue's sculptor at the eastern end of George
Street; the Foundation chronology gives "2008 - Edinburgh statue unveiled 25 November".

**Not given stops, on the stop-ownership rule:** **Maxwell Montes**, the only Venusian feature
named for a man, and the **James Clerk Maxwell Telescope**, Mauna Kea, 1987 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
Real honours; naming is not travel.

**Legends recorded, not written in:** Einstein at Cambridge replying "No, I stand on the shoulders
of Maxwell" **[R]**, no contemporary transcript located; and the "forgotten genius rediscovered"
framing **[R]**, which the statue campa answers in one sentence.

**Michael Faraday** is named repeatedly and is Maxwell's most important scientific relation: he
read the whole of *Experimental Researches* before any mathematics, turned Faraday's lines into
fluid streamlines, and sent him the paper. **Faraday has no `.journey.json` yet**, only a QUEUE.md
entry annotated "laid the ground james_clerk_maxwell just queued formalized", so he is not counted
as an interlock slug.

**Refused, shared geography only.** `charles_darwin` was at Edinburgh 1825-1827, four years before
Maxwell was born: no relation of any kind. `charles_babbage` lectured at the Royal Institution
while Faraday was Davy's assistant, and the only path to Maxwell runs through the shared Faraday
node: inferred, not documented. Both pins are inherited; neither name is mentioned.
