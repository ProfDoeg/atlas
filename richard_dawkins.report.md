# Richard Dawkins: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-15 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Clinton Richard Dawkins, b. 26 March 1941, Nairobi. Zoologist, ethologist, populariser; coiner of "meme" (The Selfish Gene, 1976, ch. 11), the anchor point the queue entry names.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, retrospect, or a claim resting only on secondary repetition · *contradictions are flagged where they occur and not adjudicated.*

## 1. Africa, 1941-1949

- Born **26 March 1941, Nairobi, British Kenya**, to Clinton John Dawkins and Jean Mary Vyvyan Dawkins **[A: Wikipedia, "Richard Dawkins"; EBSCO Research Starters]**.
- **Correction.** The dossier named "Nairobi General Hospital." **No reachable source names a hospital**; the geography lens conceded this itself. Dropped, and stated as a gap in the stop's `date_confidence`.
- **Correction.** The dossier had the father working as an agricultural civil servant in Kenya *at the time of the birth*. He had by then been **called up into the King's African Rifles [A: Wikipedia]**; the agricultural service is the career he returns to afterwards. Both facts are true; the dossier's ordering was not.
- **Contradiction inside the dossier, flagged.** The chronology lens says the family "remains in Kenya through the war years." Dawkins's own words: *"In 1943 my father was posted back to Nyasaland, where we lived until I was eight"* **[A: Edge.org, "Growing Up in Ethology"]**, corroborated by the geography lens (Cholo/Thyolo, 1943-1949) and the obituary of John Dawkins (Boing Boing, 2010). The chronology lens's "1942 call-up" is separately unsupported: the call-up **precedes** the March 1941 birth. The file follows Nyasaland; the Kenya claim is discarded, not reconciled.
- Nyasaland residence **1943-1949 [A]**, coordinates -16.0667 / 35.1333 as supplied.
- Childhood Anglican belief **[A: An Appetite for Wonder, 2013, via Wikipedia]**. The sensory detail in the campa follows the memoir's register and is **[R]** as to any specific incident.

## 2. Over Norton and school, 1949-1959

- **1949: return to England**, the father having inherited **Over Norton Park, ~210 acres near Chipping Norton**, in Dawkins hands since **James Dawkins bought it in 1726 [A: Wikipedia, "Over Norton Park"; Oxfordshire History Centre, Dawkins family papers]**.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The geography lens narrates a **telegram reaching Nyasaland in 1946** from a "distant cousin." Accounts of *An Appetite for Wonder* place the telegram in **1949**, prompted by the death of the estate's tenant; the Wikipedia article on the estate mentions no telegram, no date, no cousin. **[R]** for the telegram, **[A]** for the 1949 relocation and the 1726 title. The campa says outright that the accounts differ.
- **Chafyn Grove School, Salisbury, from 1949 [A: Wikipedia]**. **Coordinate corrected** from 51.0722 / -1.7859 (~300 m SW of the school) to **51.0744 / -1.7833** (OpenStreetMap).
- **Oundle School, 1954-1959, Laundimer House [A: Wikipedia]**. **Coordinate corrected** from 52.4816 / -0.4720 (~800 m SE, outside the grounds) to **52.4872 / -0.4798** (OpenStreetMap).
- **Contradiction inside the dossier, flagged.** The geography lens calls Oundle "a period of conventional adolescent religious belief that would not survive university." The chronology lens and the memoir date the loss of faith to **about sixteen, still at Oundle**. The file follows the memoir, gives the unbelief its own stop at 1957-01-01, and records in `date_confidence` that only "about sixteen" is attested. **[R]** for the year; **[A]** that it happened at school and turned on the argument from design.

- **Assistant professor, Berkeley, 1967-1969 [A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**, 37.8719 / -122.2585.

- **New College: two coordinates in the dossier.** Geography lens 51.7541 / -1.2494 (~220 m off); afterlife lens 51.7541 / -1.2515 (~90 m off). The file uses **-1.2515** throughout, per the afterlife lens's own reconciliation note.

## 5. The Selfish Gene, 1976-1987

- **Oxford University Press, 28 October 1976, 224 pp. [A: OUP catalogue; Wikipedia]**, pin 51.752 / -1.2577.
- **Chapter 11 closes the first edition and coins the word [A: primary text via publicism.info; Oxford Reference; OED]**. Four verbatim ch. 11 passages were fetched and read directly (the "leaping from brain to brain" sentence, "the new soup is the soup of human culture," the "mimeme … rhyme with cream" passage whose ellipsis is in the reproduced text, and the "tunes, ideas, catch-phrases" list). Two are used in the file.
- **Correction.** The "survival machines" sentence is **not from the opening chapter**: it is the second sentence of the **Preface to the 1976 first edition** (verified at publicism.info), and the original carries a **dash**, not a comma. The quote field reproduces the dash verbatim, per the rule that quote fields are untouched by house style.
- **The Extended Phenotype, 1982 [A]**; **The Blind Watchmaker, 1986 [A]**; RSL award and **Los Angeles Times Book Prize, 1987 [A: Wikipedia; Britannica]**. The Los Angeles venue is **not named in any reachable source**; the stop says so.
- **Oxford Union, 1986, against A. E. Wilder-Smith and Edgar Andrews [A: Wikipedia]**, with the correction that **he debated alongside John Maynard Smith**. **Coordinate corrected** from -1.2530 (~450 m east, at the Radcliffe Camera) to **51.7538 / -1.2594** (Frewin Court). The **exact date within 1986 is a gap**.

- *Blind Watchmaker* and *River Out of Eden* quotations are **[R]**: widely reproduced, not checked against a primary edition. Both quote_source fields say so.

## 6. The Simonyi years, 1989-2001

- **ZSL Silver Medal 1989**, **Michael Faraday Prize 1990**, **Reader in zoology 1990 [A: Britannica]**, folded into one Carlton House Terrace stop; no venue is named anywhere for the ZSL medal.
- **Royal Institution Christmas Lectures, "Growing Up in the Universe," 1991 [A]**. **Coordinate corrected** from 51.5073 / -0.1466 (in Green Park) to **51.5097 / -0.1423** (21 Albemarle Street).
- **Claim struck, not softened.** The dossier called him "the first scientist since Faraday's own century to draw a British television audience of children to this particular podium." **False**: the lectures have been televised since 1936 and given from that theatre by many scientists before him. Removed.
- **"Viruses of the Mind": drafted 1991, delivered as the Voltaire Lecture at Conway Hall 6 November 1992, published 1993 [A: Wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the volume is **Bo Dahlbom (ed.), *Dennett and His Critics: Demystifying Mind* (Blackwell, 1993)**, not "Dennett and Hofstadter eds., *Free Will*" (PhilPapers confirms Dahlbom).
- **Conway Hall coordinates (51.5205 / -0.1178) are mine, not the dossier's**, from the hall's position in Red Lion Square: venue **[A]**, geolocation **[R]**.
- **Simonyi Professorship, first holder, 1995-2008, succeeded by Marcus du Sautoy [A: simonyi.ox.ac.uk; Wikipedia]**.
- **Climbing Mount Improbable (1996)** and **Unweaving the Rainbow (1998)** are **[A]** but got no stop, for stop-count reasons.
- **Cosmos Prize and RSL fellowship, both 1997 [A]**. **Venue claim dropped:** the dossier put the RSL election at **Somerset House**, but in 1997 the Society was at **1 Hyde Park Gate**, making those coordinates anachronistic. The fellowship is carried instead as a fact of the same year inside the Osaka stop, which states that neither handover location is recorded.
- **Fellow of the Royal Society, 2001 [A]**. **Unresolved:** the afterlife lens lists a **Kistler Prize (2001)** absent from the source it cites; omitted for want of a second source.

## 7. The God Delusion and the debates, 2002-2010

- **TED, "Militant atheism," Monterey, 2002 [A: Wikipedia; TED archive]**.
- **Galápagos: the weakest stop in the file, and marked so.** The geography lens attached the visit to the **2008 Channel 4 series**, hedging on whether he landed. The adversarial pass found Wikipedia associating the visit with a **2005 pilgrimage** tied to *The Ancestor's Tale*. **The two cannot be reconciled from anything reachable.** The file dates it 2005, states the contradiction in `date_confidence` and in the campa, and asserts only what both accounts share: that he stood on the islands.
- **Shakespeare Prize, Hamburg, 2005 [A]**; **The God Delusion and the foundation, 2006 [A]**; **Lewis Thomas Prize, 2006 [A: Britannica]** (no venue; no stop); **Deschner Prize, Frankfurt, 2007 [A]**.

- **The Four Horsemen, Washington D.C., 30 September 2007 [A: Wikipedia]**. **Coordinates are a city pin (38.9072 / -77.0369)**: the room was private and is named nowhere.

## Interlocks

- **`charles_darwin.journey.json`** is the only existing file with a direct relation to the subject, and the only traveler named inside a campa. The relation is one-directional and citable: Dawkins loses his faith to Darwin's argument at sixteen, restates natural selection in a vocabulary Darwin lacked, presented a television series about him, and travelled to the Galápagos. Named at two stops (Oundle 1957, Galápagos 2005).
- **Pin inherited:** Balliol at **51.754 / -1.2565**, byte-identical from `adam_smith.journey.json`. Smith is **not named**: two matriculations two centuries apart is co-location, which the map already shows.
- **Not inherited, with reason.** The interlock lens offered Jung's **Athi Plains** pin (-1.456 / 36.978) for the birth. The Athi Plains lie some 30 km from the city; reusing that pin would misplace the birth to save a duplicate. The file uses **Nairobi (-1.2921 / 36.8219)**, identical in the geography and afterlife lenses. The canonical **Buenos Aires** pin was not needed: no Argentine appearance is attested anywhere in the pool.
- **Not named, deliberately:** Tolkien (Oxford residence overlap only), Adam Smith (Balliol only), Cecil Rhodes (colonial-Africa frame only), Aristotle, Turing, Trithemius, al-Kindi (thematic ancestry, no attested citation in either direction).
- **Gap: William S. Burroughs** is queue-only, no journey file. The Dawkins-Burroughs link the queue entry names ("language is a virus" to meme) is real but **retrospective and third-party**, drawn by Burroughs scholarship (Oliver Harris), not by either man about the other. It is carried only as a `suggested_ref` on the chapter-eleven stop, never staged as a scene. **Dating correction:** "the word is now a virus" is from *The Ticket That Exploded* (**1962**, revised 1967); *The Electronic Revolution* is 1970/71. The afterlife lens's "1971-1976" is wrong.
