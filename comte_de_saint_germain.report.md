# The Comte de Saint-Germain: research report

*2026-08-28. Slug `comte_de_saint_germain`. c.1710 to 27 February 1784. Journey file: 8 segments, 45 stops.* Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, doctrine, or press report · **[F]** probable fabrication, kept because the legend runs through it.

**The structural fact.** Almost the entire English-language documentary base is one book: Isabel Cooper-Oakley, *The Comte de St. Germain: The Secret of Kings* (1912, Theosophical, foreword by Annie Besant), which nevertheless prints in its appendices the actual despatches, parish register, and church accounts. Best archive and interested witness at once. Where its own pages contradict each other, that is flagged and left unresolved. Pagination differs between the 1912 and 1927 printings; both are cited where the difference was visible.

## 1. Origins: nothing, and three stories about nothing

No birth record, no baptism, no parish, no country. Everything before 1735 is claim.

- **Rákóczi hypothesis** [R]. Unacknowledged son of Francis II Rákóczi (Cooper-Oakley; Overton Fuller). Its whole evidentiary content is that *Tzarogy*, an alias he really used at Ansbach, is *Ragotzy* reversed. Stop dated 1690-01-01 only to order the segment.
- **Medici childhood in Florence** [R]. Told to Charles of Hesse-Kassel, printed in his *Mémoires de mon temps* (1861): his own account, at one remove, decades late.
- **Born in Italy, 1712** [R]. Brussels Gazette notice reprinted in *Read's Weekly Journal*, 17 May 1760: a press report, not a civil record.
- **University of Siena** [R]. No matriculation register ever produced; folded into the Florence campa, not given a stop.

**Gap:** the first thirty-odd years are not thin, they are empty.

## 2. The first document, and a date that will not settle

**The Hague letter to Sir Hans Sloane** [A for existence, contested for date].

- **Contradiction, unresolved.** Cooper-Oakley's list of illustrations captions it a letter of **1735**; her Chapter II contents line reads **1733**. Same book.
- **Anachronism corrected.** It cannot have been "addressed to the British Museum," founded 1753; the addressee was Sloane personally, whose collection became the Museum's founding core.
- **Citation failure corrected.** The pool attributed "22 November 1735" and shelfmark Sloane MS 4026 to English Wikipedia, which has no Sloane letter, no such date, no shelfmark. Both dropped; the shelfmark survives only as an unverified `suggested_ref`.
- **Consequence.** German Wikipedia calls Walpole's 1745 letter the first secure report of a Count Saint-Germain, which makes this the earliest *claimed* trace rather than the earliest *secure* one. Both readings stand in `date_confidence`.

## 3. England 1743-1746

- **L'Incostanza delusa, King's Theatre, Haymarket** [A]. Arias credited to him "on all but one of the Saturdays from 9 February to 20 April 1745," a Saturdays-only run that a date range misrepresents. **Coordinates corrected** to 51.5081, -0.1320; the pool's -0.1348 falls on Lower Regent Street.
- **The December 1745 arrest** [A]. Walpole to Mann, 9 December 1745, quoted verbatim at Cooper-Oakley p.22: the name is not his own, he refuses another, plays the violin "wonderfully," has been in Britain "these two years."
- **Music as a trade** [A]. Six trio sonatas printed by John Walsh. **Two pool overstatements corrected**: IMSLP gives the print as "n.d.", not the 1750s, and does not call the British Library exemplar the sole survivor. It lists **no recordings and no modern editions**.

## 4. Vienna 1745-46: a residence that fights the London evidence

- **Palais Lobkowitz** [R]. Van Sypesteyn, *Historische Herinneringen* (1869), via Cooper-Oakley p.23 n.18: living in Vienna "as a prince," the *Musique raisonnée* arias dedicated to Prince Ferdinand von Lobkowitz. **Correction**: van Sypesteyn calls the patron "first minister to Emperor Francis I," but Lobkowicz (b. 1724) was **21 in 1745 and held no ministry**; he was an amateur violinist who studied under Gluck, which is what links him to the arias.
- **Contradiction, unresolved, and serious.** Walpole has the count in Britain through December 1745; Lobkowicz spent 1745 travelling to Italy and England with Gluck. Both men are documented elsewhere in the one year of the supposed patronage, on a single source written in 1869.
- **Belle-Isle** [R]. Same single source: the hinge of the entire life rests on a Dutch memoir published 123 years later.

## 5. France c.1748-1760

- **Arrival at court** [A in outline]. Appeared around 1748, employed by Louis XV in 1749; **the pool's 1757 is a decade late and is rejected.**
- **The flawed diamond** [A as memoir]. Du Hausset, *Mémoires* (1824) p.148ff, via Cooper-Oakley pp.23-24: eyewitness, published long after her death and **undated in the source**, so the journey's 1757 is an ordering device.
- **The Trianon laboratory** [R, downgraded from the pool's A]. **Hard anachronism corrected**: the pool put this at the *Petit* Trianon for 1756-1760, a building approved in 1762 and finished in 1768, so the German source's "Trianon-Schlösschen" can only be the **Grand Trianon** (1687). Pin moved to 48.8149, 2.1050.
- **Chambord** [A]. The strongest French documentation and the least romantic: quartermaster's correspondence about an apartment and workshop, letters of 10 and 19 May, 12 August, 2 September 1758 (Appendix I). **The pool fused court arrival and Chambord into one 1757 item; the journey splits them.**
- **"Milord Gower"** [A]. A Parisian mimic impersonating the count, inventing the Council of Trent and Pontius Pilate stories; four memoirists report it, all undated. This is the origin of the immortality legend, and Voltaire's line to Frederick of 15 April 1758 is downstream of it. **Citation corrected**: "Lettre cxviii, ed. Beuchot, lviii., p.360," not the pool's cxv, verified at p.96 (1927).

## 6. The Hague, 1760: the unauthorized peace

The one episode where he touches European history rather than European gossip. Wholly [A], from state papers: d'Affry to Choiseul, 22 February and 10 and 14 March 1760 (Appendix II); Holderness to Yorke, 21 and 28 March, demanding authenticated proof of royal authorization (Appendix III); Kauderbach to Galitzin, 14 March (Appendix VII); Cornet to Haslang, 29 April (pp.114-115); the Gazette of the Netherlands, 12 January 1761, on the returned red ribbon. Kauderbach also preserves his speech about the king's "want of firmness" and the Brothers Paris, **the closest thing to his own voice in any archive**, and a minister's report rather than an autograph. On the arrival month the sources split, February against March, and the journey opens at d'Affry's first dated report and says so.

- **Departure corrected.** Not "escapes across the Netherlands in April 1760" but **May 1760, from Hellevoetsluis**, on a British passport **made out in blank**, got from Ambassador Joseph Yorke by Willem Bentinck van Rhoon. The pool's geography lens had this right; its chronology lens contradicted it.
- **The Choiseul quotation.** Verified at p.99 (1927), footnoted to Gleichen, *Mémoires* xi, pp.131-132; the pool's "p.2031-area" is garbled. **"Versailles" is an unsourced inference**: Cooper-Oakley places the scene only "in Council." The journey keeps the pin and declares the inference.
- **East Friesland transit** [R]. Uncorroborated outside the pool; dropped rather than asserted.

## 7. The Low Countries 1762-1764: real chemistry, no business

- **Ubbergen** [A]. Hardenbroeck's diary, 20 March 1762 (Cooper-Oakley appendix p.101): the laboratory, the whole days shut in, the colours on leather, property near Zutphen. **Coordinates corrected** to 51.833, 5.917. **Contradiction, unresolved:** German Wikipedia dates the *purchase* to **1763**, with an August departure leaving considerable debts, while Hardenbroeck describes the laboratory in **March 1762**. The debts, omitted by the pool, are restored: they are why the venture ended.
- **Cobenzl to Kaunitz, Brussels, 8 April 1763** [A]. Quoted in full at pp.24-25: iron into a gold-like metal, dyeing of skins and wool, improved ultramarine, and the alias **M. de Zurmont** at Tournai. The best evidence that the "alchemy" was industrial chemistry with a marketing problem.
- **Casanova at Tournai** [A, with caveat]. Cooper-Oakley p.24 renders him partly in *indirect discourse*; the journey's quote is her reporting Casanova, not the *Histoire de ma vie*, which could not be accessed.
- **The India claim** [R]. A letter attributed to him, to Count Maximilian von Lamberg: melting jewels learned on "my second journey to India, in the year 1755, with General Clive." Undated and uncorroborated by any ship's list. It is carried in the Brussels campa and given **no stop**, since no source names a place to pin.

## 8. Russia 1762: the load-bearing absence

- **The claim** [R]: Orlov told the Margrave of Brandenburg-Ansbach that Saint-Germain "played a great part" in the coup, reported in *Curiositäten der Literarisch-historischen Vor- und Mitwelt* pp.285-286 (Weimar, **1818**), second-hand and fifty-six years late. The stop is kept at the Winter Palace, [R] throughout, with Pushkin's *The Queen of Spades* (1834) in `suggested_refs`; **Pushkin gets no stop**, that being his engagement rather than the count's presence.
- **What does not exist:** any Russian state paper, payment record, or contemporary eyewitness placing him in Saint Petersburg; Brill's DGWE classifies coup participation as unverified.
- **Cooper-Oakley's counter-evidence makes it worse.** She offers a stay with Princess Golitsyna at Arkhangelskoye, 3 March 1762 (p.20); her source is unnamed and Dutch records put him in Gelderland that spring. The documented acquaintance runs the other way in time, Livorno 1770 and Nuremberg 1772, so the legend was assembled backwards.

## 9. Germany and Italy 1769-1779: the aliases

Livorno 1770, Nuremberg 1772, Mantua 1773, The Hague 1774, Triesdorf 1774-76, Leipzig October 1776, Dresden 1776-77, Berlin 1776, Hamburg 1776-78, Altona 1778-79. Names in use: **Tzarogy, v. Welldone, Weldon, Zurmont, Aymar, Surmont, Montferrat**.

- **Leipzig and Dresden** [A]. Cooper-Oakley pp.68-69: Dubosc 15 March 1777, von Wurmb from Dresden 19 May 1777, Leipzig 8 May 1777, Gimmern 3 June 1777.
- **Berlin** [A]. Frederick II and Princess Anna Amalia's circle probe origins and Masonic grades; nothing is verified, and "has never been solved" is curiosity, not endorsement. Hamburg and Brunswick reach the same result and are folded in here.
- **The Hague 1774: contradiction, unresolved.** The pool's chronology sent him to **Bad Schwalbach** after Louis XV's death (10 May 1774, correct), but Cooper-Oakley's own sentence reads "M. de St. Germain came to the Hague after the death of Louis XV," which the journey follows.
- **Venice 1765-1769** [R, narrowed]. Neither the span nor the alias *Aymar* could be corroborated; English Wikipedia gives Venice as 1769 alone. The Italian decade is a **gap** and is described as one.

## 10. Wilhelmsbad and Philalèthes: two assemblies fused, now separated

The pool's worst internal collision: its chronology lens put him at "the Convent of Wilhelmsbad, 15 February 1785," its geography lens at Wilhelmsbad "claimed 1782." Both are half right, about **two different meetings**.

- The **Convent of Wilhelmsbad** (Strict Observance) met at Hanau in the **summer of 1782**, roughly fifty days, about thirty-five delegates. Coordinates corrected from 50.1268, 8.8918 (central Hanau) to **50.1481, 8.8822** (the spa park).
- The assembly opening on **exactly 15 February 1785** and sitting to 26 May was the **Convent des Philalèthes in Paris**, 228 invitations, Saint-Martin among the named. Cooper-Oakley's own source title, "Freimaurer Bruderschaft in Frankreich" (*Latomia* ii, p.9), points to France.
- Both kept as separate [R] stops, since Cooper-Oakley herself flags the second as conflicting with the church record. **He was buried eleven and a half months before the Philalèthes opened.**

## 11. Schleswig 1778/79-1784: the one patron who stayed

- **Prince Charles of Hesse-Kassel** [A]. Governor of Schleswig, Freemason, the most stable relationship of his life. **Date disagreement, unresolved**: German Wikipedia dates the arrival in Schleswig 1778, English Wikipedia 1779.
- **Schloss Louisenlund** [A]. Tower laboratory; joint silk-dyeing venture at Eckernförde. **Coordinates corrected** from 54.546, 9.704 to **54.4931, 9.6850**, in Guby; the pool's pin fell six kilometres off, north of the Schlei.
- **Otte dye works, Kieler Straße** [A, Eckernförde city museum]. Vats, alloys, no product, damp lodging blamed afterwards for his rheumatic attacks.
- **The 1783 engraving** [A, provenance restated]. The pool said it "exists in the collection of the Marquise d'Urfé," who died in 1775; reworded to "after a portrait from the d'Urfé collection," engraver Nicolas Thomas. **No stop**: made in Paris by another hand while he was in Holstein, so it sits in `suggested_refs` at Louisenlund.
- **Cagliostro** [R/F]. Hostile 1785 pamphlets have the count initiate him into Egyptian Masonry in Holstein; Waite judged the evidence very thin, and Cagliostro's movements do not bring him there. Kept as a stop because the claim asserts **Saint-Germain's own** presence, tagged [R].

## 12. Death, and the paperwork that ends it

All [A], and unusually clean.

- **27 February 1784** death at Eckernförde; **2 March** private entombment in St. Nicolai. The parish register, via Louis Bobe (1898) in Cooper-Oakley p.63, enters him under the joined names Saint-Germain **and Weldon**, further personal information unknown.
- **1 March 1784**, Nicolai church accounts: thirty-year tomb at 10 Reichsthaler plus 2 for the opening, 12 in all. **3 April 1784**, mayor and council proclamation. **Divergence, unresolved**: Cooper-Oakley describes a creditors' call with a 14 October deadline and a four-years-in-the-country clause; English Wikipedia describes the same proclamation as the **auction of his effects**. Both rest on one source each, and her late pages were unretrievable in this pass.
- **June 1785**, Biester in the *Berlinische Monatschrift*: he "died two years ago in Danish Holstein," loose by a year against the register.

## 13. Afterlife

- **The d'Adhémar Souvenirs** [F]. *Souvenirs sur Marie-Antoinette* (1836) is attributed by bibliographic scholarship to **Étienne-Léon de Lamothe-Langon**, a documented professional fabricator, and everything about Saint-Germain and the French Revolution descends from it, the Venice 1788 sighting and the prophetic letters included. **Date corrected on "All is lost, Countess!"**: the pool said 1788, but Cooper-Oakley introduces the sequence as the proscription of the Royalists in **1789** and the preceding letter names the murders of de Launay and Flesselles (14 July 1789), so the journey dates it 1789-07-15. **Citation corrected on the verses**: the pool gave Adhémar iv, p.63, the footnote closing the *preceding* extract; they fall under "Adhémar, iv., pp. 74-97," dated 1788 by her own footnote, the quoted ellipsis eliding roughly fourteen lines.
- **The two manuscripts** [R]. *La Très Sainte Trinosophie*, Troyes MS 2400, is attributed to him by a pasted bookseller's note and tied to Cagliostro by a pencil note; Brill's DGWE calls it **pseudo-Saint-Germain**, and Manly P. Hall's Los Angeles edition of 1933 carried the attribution into modern circulation, which is the date the journey uses for a stop pinned at Troyes. The Triangular Book is catalogued by the BnF under "auteur prétendu"; **shelfmark corrected**, the two Getty copies are "Hogart Manuscript 209 and 210," and the Koss translation and Ouroboros Press 2015 edition **could not be confirmed**.
- **The lost grave** [A]. The gravestone was destroyed in the Baltic storm surge of **1872**; the city museum states the grave is not findable and nothing marks it. On **1 April 2009** a local paper ran a hoax that divers had recovered the stone from the harbour.
- **The occult succession** [R]. Blavatsky builds him into "Master Rákóczi" from *Isis Unveiled* (1877) and a *Theosophist* article of May 1881, elaborated by Besant, Leadbeater, and Alice Bailey (1922); Steiner makes him Christian Rosenkreuz returned, in GA 130, **place corrected with a caveat** to **Neuchâtel, 27-28 September 1911** rather than the pool's "Germany," though rsarchive.org and anthroposophy.eu both returned HTTP 403, leaving this high-probability rather than archive-verified; and Guy Ballard's Mount Shasta encounter of **August 1930**, published as *Unveiled Mysteries* (1934), founds the I AM Activity, with *United States v. Ballard*, 322 U.S. 78 (1944) and *Ballard v. United States*, 329 U.S. 187 (1946) downstream [A]. Leadbeater's Rome sighting of 1901 was not independently fetched and is given **no stop**, noted here so the operator can add it.

## 15. Apparatus relations for the operator

Per `EXCEPTIONS.md`, nothing is silently deleted. **Saint-Germain is not on the exceptions list**, and no apparatus-framework relation was found that would require adding him: he advanced no theoretical apparatus to apply in either direction.

**Forward, several instances caught.** Blavatsky (1877, 1881), Leadbeater (1901), Steiner (1911), Ballard (1930), Hall (1933), Pushkin (1834), and the Ballard litigation all postdate his 1784 death and all concern him. They are handled two ways, with **none staged as a scene of the other person travelling**: where the later tradition asserts *the count's own* presence at a place (Venice 1788, Mount Shasta 1930), that is his stop, tagged [R] as legend; where the relation is a book, lecture, edition, or lawsuit about him, it appears only in `sources` and `suggested_refs`. **Backward: none.** If the operator disagrees with treating posthumous sightings as his own stops, those two stops are the ones to revisit.

## 16. Honest gaps

1. **Birth and the years to 1735.** No record of any kind; the 1712/Italy year is a newspaper's.
2. **The Italian years, roughly 1764-1770.** Scattered notices, no continuous archive.
3. **Russia.** No Russian source at all, only German and English report.
4. **India 1755.** His own claim; no ship's list, no place named in it.
5. **What he did for Louis XV.** No warrant, no office, no accounted duty, in a court that recorded the price of candles.
6. **The Masonic grades.** Implied by him, verified by nobody, in Berlin, Hamburg, or Brunswick.
7. **The estate.** No inventory recovered, only the proclamation and the disagreement about what it proclaimed.
