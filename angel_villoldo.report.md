# Ángel Villoldo (1861-1919): research report

*Research pass 2026-08. Subject: Ángel Gregorio Villoldo, Buenos Aires songwriter, payador, comic versifier, and the man credited with El Choclo, El Porteñito and the lyric of La Morocha. Slug: `angel_villoldo`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or single-witness anecdote. Contradictions are flagged and *not* adjudicated, except where a source's own internal chronology makes one branch impossible; there the reasoning is shown.

The evidence base is thin and encyclopedic: no scholarly monograph was reachable, and the spine is the Spanish and English Wikipedia articles on Villoldo and on the individual songs plus lyric-aggregator pages for the verbatim texts. Nothing primary (civil register, label ledger, SACEM registration, newspaper notice) was reached. Every date below rests on tertiary compilation.

---

## 1. Barracas, and the trades before music (1861 - c. 1889)

**Birth. [A]** Born 16 February 1861 in Barracas, a riverside working-class barrio on the south side of Buenos Aires. Date and neighbourhood agree across es.wikipedia and en.wikipedia.

**Birth name. [A / contested spelling]** es.wikipedia gives the registered name as **Ángel Gregorio Ghigiola Roggio**, an Italian-surname family; parents named as **Juan Ghigiolla (or Ghigiola)** and **Victoria Roggio**. en.wikipedia agrees on date and barrio but does not repeat the full birth name. **[R]** The surname is printed inconsistently within the Spanish article itself (*Ghigiola* / *Ghigiolla*); no primary civil register was consulted, so the correct registry spelling is unresolved.

**A name error to kill. [A]** The form "Ángel Gregorio Villoldo **Arroyo**" circulates in derived summaries and is attested nowhere. *Arroyo* is one of his five pen names, listed alongside A. Gregorio, Fray Pimiento, Gregorio Giménez and Mario Reguero: a pseudonym mistaken for a maternal surname.

**Trades. [A]** Before music he works successively as typographer, tram driver, circus clown and journalist; self-taught as guitarist and singer (en.wikipedia). **[R]** No employer, address, or ordering of years is given anywhere; placing the trades in the central city is a default, not a finding.

**Childhood context. [R]** Barracas passes from a wealthy Rosas-era enclave to an immigrant working-class quarter after the yellow fever epidemic drove richer families north. That is documented neighbourhood history; nothing ties a street, house, school, or his parents' occupations to him. A gap.

**Cantos criollos. [A]** 1889, publishes a compilation of criollo folk songs (en.wikipedia). No publisher or extant copy located.

## 2. The cafe circuit and the guardia vieja (c. 1890 - 1902)

**The harmonica-and-guitar apparatus. [A]** He performs solo in Buenos Aires taverns and cafes using a device of his own invention that lets one player work harmonica and guitar at once (es.wikipedia). No date, workshop, or surviving example located.

**Café de Hansen. [A, with a correction]** The Palermo cafe (1877-1912, demolished by order of Joaquín S. de Anchorena) is a fixed point of guardia vieja historiography. An earlier pass said "no Villoldo-specific citation located"; that was too strong. es.wikipedia's *Café de Hansen* records that a Villoldo tango so excited the patrons that the owner forbade the musicians to play it. Coordinate corrected: the cafe stood at today's Av. Sarmiento and Av. Figueroa Alcorta, where the Planetario stands, geocoding to **-34.5696 / -58.4117**, not the -34.5744 / -58.4153 an earlier pass carried.

**La Boca and San Telmo. [R]** Both are standard in tango-origin historiography (dock bars, brothels, academias de baile). **No source places Villoldo at a named address in either.** Context stops, hedged as such in `date_confidence`.

**Pseudonyms. [A]** A. Gregorio, Fray Pimiento, Gregorio Giménez, Ángel Arroyo, Mario Reguero (es. and en.wikipedia).

**The 1898 melody and the Casimiro Alcorta question. [R, open]** es.wikipedia's article *El choclo* dates the melody to around 1898 and reports a competing attribution: some sources credit the tune to **Casimiro Alcorta**, a Black violinist of the Buenos Aires dance floors who died in poverty in 1913 and is "today practically forgotten." The Spanish article raises the claim and does not resolve it. en.wikipedia's *El Choclo* does not mention Alcorta at all and treats Villoldo as sole composer. A further wrinkle: **es.wikipedia's own article on Alcorta lists El Choclo among his works but narrates no dispute and does not mention Villoldo.** Flagged, not adjudicated; the journey file carries it as an open question.

## 3. Danza criolla: the 1903-1905 hinge

**El Choclo, premiere. [A]** 3 November 1903, at the **Restaurante El Americano, Cangallo 966** (today Teniente General Perón 966), Buenos Aires. Villoldo's friend **José Luis Roncallo**, who directed a classical orchestra there, agreed to play it on condition it was billed a **"danza criolla"** rather than a tango, tango being then barred from respectable rooms. Attested in es. and en.wikipedia. **Coordinate corrected:** OSM geocodes Tte. Gral. Perón 966 to **-34.6061 / -58.3802**; an earlier pass carried -34.6037 / -58.3822, about 300 m off.

**El Porteñito. [A, date corrected]** en.wikipedia's works list places it loosely "circa 1900"; **es.wikipedia's dedicated article *El porteñito* dates the premiere to 1903.** The journey file uses 1903 and says the earlier date is a works-list approximation. Same article: Villoldo recorded it himself around its debut; early versions by **Alfredo Eusebio Gobbi** (RCA Victor, 7 December 1907), **Andrée Vivianne** (Odeón, 1907) and **Flora Rodríguez de Gobbi** (Gath y Chaves label, 1909-1910).

**La Morocha. [A]** 1905. Lyrics Villoldo, music **Enrique Saborido** (Uruguayan). Per Saborido's 1928 interview account in es.wikipedia *La morocha (tango)*: composed in one sitting, roughly 5:00 to 6:30 a.m., at the **Bar Reconquista**, after a late-night wager among friends, inspired by the dancer **Lola Candales**. **[R]** No street number for the bar was located; it takes the canonical Buenos Aires pin, hedged in the stop.

**El Choclo in print. [A]** Sheet music published 1905 (es.wikipedia, *El choclo*).

**The lyric layers. [A / R]** es.wikipedia's *El choclo* distinguishes four: Villoldo's 1903 original, which it says "hacía referencia efectivamente al alimento" (actually referred to the foodstuff); Villoldo's own second version, *Cariño puro*; a malevo-perspective lyric by Marambio Catán; and Discépolo 1947. **[R]** The couplet quoted in the journey file ("De un grano nace la planta / que más tarde nos da el choclo," verbatim at Letras.com) is straight agricultural imagery and so matches the 1903 layer by description, but es.wikipedia does not print the 1903 incipit, so the assignment is inference, not a textual match. An earlier pass guessed a c.1930 layer and half-credited Discépolo; that runs backwards, and his 1947 lyric is a different poem, beginning "Con este tango que es burlón y compadrito."

## 4. The records cross the ocean (1906-1910)

**First recording of La Morocha. [A, place corrected]** 23 July 1906, Victor, **matrix B 3.555-1**, by **Flora Rodríguez de Gobbi** with the ensemble **Los Gobbi**. es.wikipedia *La morocha (tango)* states the session was made **in the United States**, not in Buenos Aires or Paris as an earlier pass hedged. Flora Rodríguez was **Chilean**. **[R]** No studio is named; Victor's Camden, New Jersey plant is the reasonable placement and is flagged as inference in the stop.

**Sheet-music sales: contested. [A vs A]** en.wikipedia gives **more than 280,000** copies of La Morocha's sheet music; **es.wikipedia's *Enrique Saborido* gives about 100,000** after the early-1906 publication. Reported as a range with both sources named.

**Scores to Europe. [A]** es.wikipedia *La morocha (tango)*: among the first tango scores to reach Europe, carried to Paris by young Argentines of wealthy families and by sailors who had learned the dance socially.

**The Paris trip: a real contradiction, one branch untenable.** es.wikipedia's Villoldo article says Gath y Chaves sponsored the trip **in 1903**; en.wikipedia dates it **1907**. Both cannot hold. **1903 is untenable and the reasoning is internal to es.wikipedia itself:** its article *Alfredo Eusebio Gobbi* states that Gobbi and Flora Rodríguez married in **1905**, so a 1903 Paris trip by the couple is impossible; and its *El porteñito* dates Flora's Gath y Chaves-label recordings to **1909-1910**. The 1903 line is an error carried in that one article. The journey file uses 1907 and states the conflict in `date_confidence`.

**In Paris. [A]** Records with several companies, performs tango, joins France's society of authors and composers to collect royalties (es.wikipedia). **[R]** No Paris studio, label partner, address, or date of affiliation is named by any reachable source. An earlier pass carried a separate "1907-1908 Paris sessions" stop tagged [A]; it rested on a prior research summary alone, duplicated the trip, and has been demoted and merged.

**The Paris pin.** Prior journeys (thomas_edison, johan_palmstruch) rejected the canonical Paris coordinate 48.8566 / 2.3522 as too generic without an attested site. No Paris address exists for Villoldo, so the journey file uses that pin and says in each Paris stop's `date_confidence` that it marks the city only. Dropping the trip would lose a documented event; inventing a studio address would be worse.

**Odeón / Rondalla Vázquez, Buenos Aires, 1907. [R]** From a single prior summary, uncorroborated against any discography. Kept as a weak thread and labelled one.

**Los mamertos. [A]** 1910, a comic parody recorded performing a drunk character (es.wikipedia, *La morocha (tango)*; en.wikipedia).

## 5. The comic repertoire

All texts below are **verbatim** at Letras.com and were checked line by line. Attribution caveats matter and are given per item.

- **El Choclo**, "De un grano nace la planta / que más tarde nos da el choclo": confirmed verbatim (letras.com/angel-villoldo/798568/). The page's dual credit to Discépolo is site metadata noise, not a rival attribution for this text.
- **El Porteñito**, "Soy hijo de Buenos Aires, por apodo 'El porteñito'": confirmed. Credit: Villoldo.
- **La Morocha**, "Yo soy la morocha,": confirmed. Music Saborido, words Villoldo.
- **El Cachafaz**, full opening line "El cachafaz es un tipo de vestir muy elegante" (letras.com/angel-villoldo/798565/). An earlier pass quoted only the first five words as the whole line; corrected. Page credit is **"Angel Villoldo - Manuel Aróztegui"**, so Villoldo is the lyricist, not sole author.
- **Cuidado con los 50**, "Una ordenanza sobre la moral": confirmed. Credit: Villoldo.
- **Testamento de un paisano**, "Señores, mucha atención,": confirmed. Credit: Villoldo.
- **El criollo más criollo**, "Aquí tienen el criollo más criollo,": confirmed. Credit: Villoldo.
- **Matufias (o el arte de vivir)**, "Es el siglo en que vivimos": confirmed. Credit: Villoldo.
- **Homenaje a Saravia**, "Con el alma acongojada": confirmed verbatim (letras.com/angel-villoldo/798601/), **but the page shows no songwriter credit at all**, so it is Villoldo-associated only by sitting in his artist index. **[R]** Aparicio Saravia was killed in 1904, so the piece cannot be earlier; that is inference, not a sourced date.
- **Doña Juana Rabanito**: the three quoted words are a fragment of the first line, carry no evidentiary weight, and are **dropped** from the journey file.
- **El Torito**, "Aquí tienen a El Torito,": verbatim, but the page credits **Angel Villoldo, A. Timarni (Antonio Polito) and Carlos Pesce**, and Pesce/Polito wrote a separate later tango of the same title, so the aggregator may be merging two works. **Dropped** as the weakest-sourced item; the circulating 1907 date was never verified.

**Stage work. [A]** Collaborations with the **Podestá** family (José María "Pepe", Pablo) and the actress **Angelina Pagano**, supplying comic songs and musical numbers (en.wikipedia). **[R / gap]** No theatre, production title, or date survives anywhere reachable.

## 6. Last years and death (1916-1919)

**Argentine Popular Songs. [A]** 1916, a collection published for the centenary of the Declaration of Independence (en.wikipedia).

**The German band anecdote. [R, single witness, tag corrected]** es.wikipedia attributes it to one named journalist, **Tito Livio Foppa**: at a 1916 banquet for the foreign press "en el frente alemán," a band lacking the score of the Argentine national anthem played El Choclo instead. An earlier pass tagged this **[A]** and pinned it in Buenos Aires, contradicting the claim it carries. Both errors corrected: tag **[R]**, and the stop sits in Germany with an explicit "front unspecified" note.

**Gardel and Razzano. [A]** 1917, they record Villoldo's **Cantar eterno** (en.wikipedia). **[R]** No meeting, correspondence, or photograph links Villoldo and Gardel personally; the relation documented is one-directional and musical.

**Death. [A]** 14 October 1919, Buenos Aires, age 58; buried at the **Cementerio de la Chacarita**. **[R / gap]** No source names the street, house, hospital, or cause; none names the sector, panteón, or plot number of the grave.

**Marriage and children: a gap, not a finding. [R]** No source consulted, Spanish or English, documents a marriage or any child. The only closely associated woman on record is **Flora Rodríguez de Gobbi**, wife of Alfredo Gobbi, a professional connection. This is absence of evidence, not evidence of bachelorhood.

**"El padre del tango canción." [R]** Epithet attributed to **Horacio Ferrer** via en.wikipedia. The original Ferrer text, its exact wording and its publication were not located. Quoted in the journey file, tagged as an epithet in circulation rather than a located citation.

## 7. Afterlife of the melody

- **1947 [A]** Enrique Santos Discépolo writes a new lyric for El Choclo, framing the tango as a way of life; it becomes the version most commonly sung (es. and en.wikipedia).
- **1947 [A, year corrected]** Libertad Lamarque premieres Discépolo's lyric in **Gran Casino**, the Mexican film directed by Luis Buñuel with Jorge Negrete. An earlier pass dated the film **1955**; es.wikipedia's *Gran Casino* gives **1947**, the only year that makes the internal logic work.
- **1949 [A]** The Argentine film *La Historia del Tango* dramatizes El Choclo's story, Tita Merello performing it on screen (en.wikipedia, *El Choclo*).
- **1952 [A, date corrected]** Lester Allen and Robert Hill adapt El Choclo into **Kiss of Fire**, altering two measures and dropping the third section, crediting Villoldo as co-composer. **Louis Armstrong's recording is from 1952, not 1955**: en.wikipedia calls his the first recording of the English version and charts it at **No. 20 in 1952**, in the same wave as Georgia Gibbs (No. 1), Tony Martin (No. 6), Toni Arden (No. 14), Billy Eckstine (No. 16) and Guy Lombardo (No. 30). es.wikipedia's 19 March 1955 Decca date is not the original.
- **1977 [A]** Rudolf Nureyev dances to El Choclo in Ken Russell's *Valentino*.
- **1996 [A, scope corrected]** Julio Iglesias records El Choclo on his album *Tango*; legal action over lyric rights follows, brought by the descendants of **Discépolo and of Roberto Goyeneche**, not Discépolo's alone (es.wikipedia).
- **2013 [A]** **Martín Ron**, with **Leandro Frizzero**, paints Villoldo alongside Tita Merello on the wall of **Hospitales station** (Línea H, not Línea A as some summaries state), Parque Patricios, for the city's Paseo Cultural del Tango. Villoldo is named on the wall as "the man who wrote El Choclo." This is the one direct, named appearance of Villoldo inside an existing journey file (`martin_ron.journey.json`), and the only interlock used in the campa prose besides Gardel.

## 8. Coordinate reconciliations made in this pass

Two research lenses disagreed with each other on the same two places; the journey file uses one value for each.

1. **Barracas.** Two lenses gave -34.648 / -58.383 and -34.6345 / -58.3853; the second is about 1.2 km off and lands in Constitución, outside the barrio. **Adopted: -34.6453 / -58.3876**, the OSM centroid.
2. **Chacarita.** -34.5975 / -58.4566 falls just south of the cemetery's OSM bounding box. **Adopted: -34.5908 / -58.4597**, the es.wikipedia infobox value.
3. **Riachuelo docks vs Puerto de Buenos Aires.** A "Puerto de Buenos Aires" stop carried -34.6371 / -58.3628, the La Boca waterfront and a near-duplicate of the La Boca stop; the actual immigrant port is in Retiro at roughly -34.593 / -58.366. **Merged into one La Boca / Riachuelo stop** and retitled.
4. **Canonical pins inherited byte-identical** where the interlock lens found them: Buenos Aires **-34.6037 / -58.3816**; Paris **48.8566 / 2.3522** (used only with the explicit generic-pin caveat above); Hospitales station **-34.6414 / -58.4125**.
