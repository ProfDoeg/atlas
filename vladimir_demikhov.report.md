# Vladimir Petrovich Demikhov (1916-1998): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-16 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Soviet experimental biologist, founder of experimental transplantology, creator of the two-headed dogs. Slug: `vladimir_demikhov`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim the wider literature disputes · *contested* two reachable sources disagree and this report does not adjudicate.

**Method note.** The research pool rested almost entirely on the two Wikipedia articles, with a log entry asserting that no independent corroboration was reachable. That assertion is false and is corrected here: Konstantinov 2009 (*Texas Heart Institute Journal* 36:5, open access at PMC2763473) supplies dated primary detail contradicting Wikipedia at several points, and three declassified CIA translations of the Soviet medical press supply the only verbatim words of Demikhov's own in this file.

---

## 1. Origins: the Don farmstead, 1916-1931

**Birth. [A]** Born at khutor Kulikovsky, under the stanitsa of Yaryzhenskaya, Don Host Oblast. en.wikipedia gives 31 July 1916; ru.wikipedia "31 июля (18 июля ст. ст.) 1916"; Konstantinov 2009 independently 18 July, i.e. the Old Style date for the same day. **Coordinate correction:** the pool carried 49.9/42.0 and 50.2/42.75, wrong by 110 km and 72 km, the second outside the district entirely. Khutor Kulikovsky, Novonikolaevsky District, Volgograd Oblast, is gazetteered at 50°50'15"N 42°32'40"E = **50.8375, 42.5444** (ru.wikipedia, "Куликовский (Волгоградская область)", which lists Demikhov as its notable native).

**Father. [A]** Pyotr Yakovlevich killed in the Russian Civil War c. 1919, when his son is about three; Domnika Aleksandrovna raises three children alone and puts all three through higher education (ru.wikipedia; Konstantinov 2009). **Gap:** neither source says where or on which side he was killed. The pool placed the death at the family farmstead, which nothing supports; the journey file pins Kulikovsky and flags the place as unrecorded.

**Stalingrad Tractor Plant. [A]** Leaves school at fifteen for work as a mechanic at the plant from 1931 (en.wikipedia). **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 48.85/44.5333 falls in the Volga floodplain northwest of the city; the plant in Traktorozavodsky District is at **48.7938, 44.6029** (OSM).

## 2. Education and the machine of 1937

**Where he studied. *Contested*.** en.wikipedia: "In 1934, Demikhov began studying at the Voronezh State University... He then transferred to the Faculty of Biology (Moscow State University)... and graduated with honors in August 1940", with **no transfer year given**. ru.wikipedia and Konstantinov 2009 both have him at MSU from 1934 with no Voronezh at all. The pool credited both Wikipedias for the Moscow claim, which is wrong. **Not adjudicated;** the journey file carries both stops, each stating the conflict, and discards the pool's "MSU, 1937-1940", an invented range.

**The 1937 device. [R], venue contested.** ru.wikipedia: as a third-year student he builds the world's first artificial heart by hand and implants it in a dog, which survives about two hours. en.wikipedia places the same device at **Voronezh**. Konstantinov 2009 contradicts the implantation outright: a pump **too large for dog implantation**, maintaining circulation roughly five hours after the animal's heart was excised. The story is **tradition, not attested fact**, and the pool's [A] is downgraded to [R].

**Newspaper, degree, and a standing fact. [A]** A notice of the work appears in a university newspaper in April 1938 (en.wikipedia), the earliest paper trace reachable; he graduates with honors from the MSU biology faculty in August 1940 (both Wikipedias). Uncontested by any source: he is a biologist, never a physician, holds no medical degree, never qualifies to operate on humans and never does. Every operation of his career is performed on an animal.

## 3. The war, 1941-1945

**Service. [A]** Conscripted after graduation; serves as a senior laboratory technician in a pathological-anatomy laboratory attached to the active army, and as a forensic expert (both Wikipedias). **Gap:** no source names a single posting, town, unit, or front, so the journey file pins the conscription at Moscow and flags that as the point of call-up, not a place of service. **Manchuria. [R]** en.wikipedia alone places him in China after the Soviet declaration of war on Japan in 1945, naming no town; Harbin (45.7538, 126.6470) is kept as a labeled placeholder.

**Return. [A], correcting the pool.** The pool had him joining the Institute of Experimental and Clinical Surgery in 1945. Both independent accounts contradict it: en.wikipedia has him resuming his MSU physiology post and moving to the Institute only in 1947; Konstantinov 2009 gives 1947-1955 under Vishnevsky. **Corrected to: demobilized end of 1945, MSU, Institute of Surgery from 1947.**

---

## 4. Moscow State University, 1946-1947: the operations everything dates from

**The 1946 firsts. [A] on the operations; venue corrected.** First intrathoracic heterotopic heart transplant and an early heart-lung complex replacement, in dogs, 1946 (both Wikipedias); Konstantinov 2009 dates a heart-lung dog to **30 June 1946**, surviving **9.5 hours**. The pool sited these at the Institute of Experimental and Clinical Surgery, where he was not yet working, so the journey file moves them to MSU. They are performed **before** extracorporeal circulation exists, without immunosuppression or reliable tissue typing, by a man in a physiology department with no surgical team.

**Family. [A]** Marries Liya Nikolayevna, August 1946 (en.wikipedia); daughter Olga born July 1947, later a physician and professor (both Wikipedias). **Gap:** no venue for either; both pinned at a declared city-level placeholder.

---

## 5. Bolshaya Serpukhovskaya: the Institute of Surgery, 1947-1955

**Arrival, lung, liver, heart. [A]** Moves to the Institute of (Experimental and Clinical) Surgery in 1947 and performs the first isolated experimental lung transplant in a dog; first experimental liver transplant 1948; first orthotopic heart transplant in a dog, without artificial circulation support, 1951 (both Wikipedias; Konstantinov 2009 for the 1947-1955 span under Vishnevsky). **Coordinate correction:** Bolshaya Serpukhovskaya 27 is at **55.7247, 37.6274** per OSM, ~550 m northeast of the pool's 55.7213/37.621.

**Coronary bypass. *Contested dates*.** en.wikipedia dates a first mammary-coronary anastomosis to 1952 and separately credits 1953 with the first successful experimental bypass; ru.wikipedia dates the bypass to 1952 and notes a **1988 USSR State Prize**; Konstantinov 2009 gives **29 July 1953**. Left disagreeing. **The seven-year dog. [R]** en.wikipedia alone reports a transplanted dog surviving a further seven years (1953); single-source, no clinical write-up reachable.

**The CIA reads the Soviet medical press. [A]** Two declassified translations bear on this period:

- CIA-RDP80-00809A000700180466-4, "Fifth Scientific Session of Institute of Surgery imeni A. V. Vishnevskiy," translating *Meditsinskiy Rabotnik*, Moscow, **8 December 1953**, byline S. Navashin: "V.F. Demikhov reported on the transplantation of the heart, lungs, and kidneys in higher animals. As the method of operation used in heart transplantation was perfected, the length of survival of dogs with a transplanted heart was increased from several hours to several days." **Two pool errors corrected:** the pool read "other organs" where the document says *kidneys*, and dated the item 9 June 1954, which is not the article's date; page 1 of the release is stamped "Page Denied" so the CIA report date cannot be checked. "V.F." is the translator's error for V.P.
- CIA-RDP80-00809A000700200220-3, "Soviet Experiments on Transplantation of Vital Organs," translating an article under Demikhov's name in a Stockholm communist newspaper, **25 September 1954**. Verbatim: "The extra heart took over half the work of circulating the blood"; "It has not been possible to keep the dogs with two hearts alive for more than 2 1/2 months after the operation"; "The entire operation takes 25-30 minutes, exclusive of the time required to open and close the chest cavity." These are the only words of Demikhov's own this pass could attest to a document.

## 6. The head transplants, 1954-1959

**First graft and series. [R] on the date, [A] on the count.** en.wikipedia dates the first head-and-forelimb graft to **February 1954**; ru.wikipedia gives 1954 without a month and **twenty** two-headed dogs in all. The pool asserted the series was "concentrated in 1954-1955," a span **not in the Russian article**, which gives no range at all; en.wikipedia says the transplantations ran five years before Western surgeons noticed them in 1959, and Konstantinov 2009 gives a maximum survival of **29 days**. The journey file uses 1954-1959 with the 29-day figure.

**Institutional split. [A]** Institute of Surgery until 1955, First Moscow Medical Institute (Sechenov) 1955-1960 (ru.wikipedia; Konstantinov 2009). The pool pinned the whole 1954-1959 series at the First MMI, wrong for the first and most famous graft; the journey file splits it, February 1954 on Bolshaya Serpukhovskaya, the programme from 1955 on Bolshaya Pirogovskaya. **Coordinate correction:** Sechenov, Bolshaya Pirogovskaya 2 s1, is at **55.7324, 37.5711** per OSM, not 55.7267/37.5697.

**The priority dispute. [A]** *Meditsinskiy Rabotnik*, 13 May 1955, Vol 18 No 40, byline "Prof N. Sinitsyn, Gor'kiy," in CIA-RDP80-00809A000700240023-8: "In 1954, a communication was published by V.P. Demikhov, who used our method of grafting a second head onto a dog." **Pool error corrected:** the pool rearranged these clauses into a sentence never written. Gorky is Sinitsyn's own place and is not staged as a Demikhov stop.

**Last head transplant. [A], contested year.** An en.wikipedia image caption: "The last dog head transplant performed by Vladimir Demikhov on January 13, 1959, in East Germany." Konstantinov 2009 places the German demonstrations in **Leipzig in 1958**. Both recorded.

**Western press. [R]** Correspondents photograph a composite dog and the images run in Western magazines around 1959. Outlets, survival durations, and behavioral claims (lapping milk, both tails wagging) vary between retellings and could not be attested to a primary article. Tradition.

## 7. Sklifosovsky, the book, and the visitor, 1960-1967

**Transfer. [A], mis-sourced in the pool.** Joins the N.V. Sklifosovsky Institute of Emergency Medicine in 1960, stays to 1986. The reason is **ru.wikipedia's alone**: a breakdown with the director Vladimir Kovanov, who would not admit his dissertation to defense. en.wikipedia gives the move with no reason and no Kovanov; the pool wrongly credited both. **Coordinate correction:** the institute's own geotag, 55°46'24"N 37°38'07"E = **55.7734, 37.6353**, replaces two divergent pool values (55.7706/37.6339 and 55.7761/37.6122).

**The monograph. [A]** *Пересадка жизненно важных органов в эксперименте*, Medgiz, Moscow, 1960: the first monograph in the field and long the only one, and the term *transplantology* is his. Translations, en.wikipedia and Konstantinov 2009 agreeing city by city: **New York 1962** (tr. Basil Haigh, Consultants Bureau), **Berlin 1963**, **Madrid 1967**. **Pool error corrected:** the pool dated all three 1962.

**The doctorate. *Contested*.** en.wikipedia: doctorate 1960, Doctor of Science 1963. ru.wikipedia: the 1963 defense before the **biology council of Moscow State University** first yields only a candidate degree, upgraded to Doctor of Biological Sciences after a second vote. Not adjudicated. **Pool note corrected:** the pool called the awarding body unidentifiable; ru.wikipedia names MSU, so the journey file pins the defense there.

**Foreign honors. [A], undated.** Honored Doctor of Medicine, Leipzig; honorary member at Uppsala's Royal Society of Sciences, Hannover, and the Mayo Clinic (ru.wikipedia, no years); Konstantinov 2009 supplies **16 September 1960 for Uppsala**. **Gap:** nothing says whether he traveled to receive any of them, so the journey file records an election, not an arrival.

**Barnard. *Contested date, unresolved venue*.** en.wikipedia's Barnard article and ru.wikipedia both put the Moscow visit in 1960 (a second visit, 1963, implied elsewhere); Konstantinov 2009 dates it to **1962**, with Barnard a tourist diverting from his itinerary. No source names the laboratory. The pool sited a 1960 visit at the First MMI, contradicting its own chronology, since Demikhov had left for Sklifosovsky by then.

**The quotation, corrected. [A]** The pool carried "[the] father of heart and lung transplantation" as untraced. The primary source exists: Konstantinov 2009 prints a **1997 letter from Barnard**, in which Demikhov "was certainly a remarkable man, having done all the research before extracorporeal circulation. I have always maintained that if there is **a** father of heart and lung transplantation, then Demikhov certainly deserves this title." Barnard wrote *a* father, not *the*; the bracketed article is a Wikipedia artifact that inflates the claim, and the date is 1997 in writing, not a 1960s remark. Barnard's own operation, Groote Schuur, Cape Town, 3 December 1967, is **his** stop and not Demikhov's, and appears in the journey file only as a suggested reference.

**The organ collection. [A], and a large gap.** en.wikipedia: "Between 1963 and 1965, he also assembled the world's first collection of living human organs for future surgical use." The pool carried a single 1963 date. Nothing else is reachable: no provenance, authorization, preservation method, or disposition. The most conspicuous unfilled hole in the record.

## 8. Last years and death, 1986-1998

**Retirement. [A]** Retires from Sklifosovsky in 1986 (ru.wikipedia dates the laboratory programme 1960-1986). **Order of Merit. [A]** Order of Merit for the Fatherland, 3rd class, shortly before his death, 1998 (en.wikipedia). No ceremony described anywhere.

**Death. [A]** Dies of an aneurysm in his Moscow apartment on 22 November 1998, aged 82 (both Wikipedias). en.wikipedia places the flat "on the outskirts of Moscow" with no address. **Pool error corrected:** the pool attached 55.7558/37.6173, which is Red Square, contradicting "outskirts" in the same sentence; the journey file uses a declared city-level placeholder.

**Burial. [A]** Vagankovskoye Cemetery, plot 24 (ru.wikipedia), monument set over the grave. **Coordinate correction:** the pool gave two wrong points (55.7625/37.5502 and 55.7708/37.5764, the latter 1.8 km off in Khoroshevsky); the cemetery is at 55°46'05"N 37°32'54"E = **55.7681, 37.5483**, ul. Sergeya Makeeva 15. The funeral day is unrecorded; the journey file sets a declared placeholder day after the 22nd so a timeline cannot put burial before death.

## 10. Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. **Voronezh or Moscow in 1934.** en.wikipedia vs. ru.wikipedia + Konstantinov.
2. **The 1937 device.** Implanted artificial heart, dog surviving two hours (ru.wikipedia) vs. an unimplantable assist pump holding circulation ~5 h after cardiectomy (Konstantinov).
3. **Coronary bypass year.** 1952 vs. 1953, en.wikipedia listing both for different procedures, Konstantinov giving 29 July 1953.
4. **The doctorate.** Two degrees, 1960 and 1963 (en.wikipedia) vs. one contested 1963 defense needing a second vote (ru.wikipedia).
5. **Last head transplant.** 13 January 1959, East Germany (en.wikipedia caption) vs. Leipzig 1958 (Konstantinov).
6. **Barnard's visit.** 1960 (both Wikipedias) vs. 1962 (Konstantinov); a second 1963 visit implied and nowhere confirmed.
7. **1945-1947.** Institute from 1945 vs. MSU until 1947. Resolved here in favor of MSU, but the disagreement is recorded.

## 11. Gaps stated as gaps

- No wartime posting, unit, or location anywhere for 1941-1945.
- The 1963-1965 collection of living human organs: provenance, authorization, method, fate, all unknown.
- The address of the flat he died in: unrecorded, and deliberately not invented.
- Whether he traveled to Uppsala, Hannover, Rochester, or Leipzig for his honors: unstated. Leipzig 1958/1959 is the one foreign trip actually attested.
- The church plaque: undated, known from a photograph caption alone.
- The two documentary films: no credits, no dates.
- Wedding venue, maternity hospital, Order of Merit ceremony: none recorded.
