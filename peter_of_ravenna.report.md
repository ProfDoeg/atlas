# Peter of Ravenna (Pietro Francesco Tomai, Petrus Ravennas), c. 1448-1510

*Research report for `peter_of_ravenna.journey.json`. 2026-08-29.*
Jurist, doctor iuris utriusque, eques auratus; author of the *Phoenix, sive artificiosa memoria* (Venice, 1491).

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-only material no consulted source carries. Contradictions flagged, not adjudicated. Gaps stated as gaps.

**Structural note.** Four traditions cover this life and have never been reconciled: Italian (Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*, "Pietro Francesco Tomai"), older German (Eisenhart, *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* 25, "Petrus Ravennas"), modern German (Girgensohn, *Neue Deutsche Biographie*, gnd100958141), and the tertiary layer (en/it/de Wikipedia). Where they disagree it is about years, not events. Abbreviated below as Treccani, ADB, NDB, en/it/de.wiki.

**Two audit results on the pool feeding this file.** Seven of its citations pointed at articles not containing the claim: en.wiki was credited for Bologna, Pavia, Pisa, the Greifswald arrival, Wittenberg, the 1506 plague and Ortwin Gratius, and mentions none of them. Eight items were upgraded [R] to [A] once ADB and NDB were actually fetched.

## 1. Ravenna and Padua, c. 1448-1472

- Born c. 1448 at Ravenna, son of Gioacchino Tomai. **[A]** Treccani. **[gap]** No baptismal record, no address, no mother's name; the year is back-calculated from the degree dates.
- Father dead by c. 1467. **[R], not carried.** The name is Treccani's; the date appears in no consulted source.
- Studied law at Padua under Alessandro Tartagni da Imola. **[A]** en.wiki, Treccani. Arrival year inferred from the 1468 event.
- 25 November 1468, feast of St Catherine: public recitation of Roman law with glosses and doctors' opinions before both universities; Tartagni crossing himself. **[A]** ADB carries the exact date and feast independently of Peter's own later account in the *Phoenix*. **[gap]** No university protocol located.
- 1471, licence in Roman law. **[A]** NDB (gnd100958141), stated flatly.
- 1472, doctorate in both civil and canon law. **[A]** Treccani, en.wiki, NDB.
- **Pin.** The journey uses the atlas's canonical Padua pin, **45.4064/11.8768**, byte-identical from `giulio_camillo.journey.json`. Palazzo Bo has been the university's seat only since 1493 (en.wiki), so it was not his lecture hall; stated in `date_confidence`.

## 2. The itinerant years, 1472-1481

- 1472, *Oratio pro patria ad Nicolaum Tronum*, Venice, Nicolas Jenson. **[A]** Treccani. Only the year is supported.
- Lectured at Bologna, Pavia and Ferrara. **[A]** Treccani, ADB. **[gap]** No years, subjects, or duration anywhere.
- 1477-1479, Pisa, the Florentine Studio in exile, stipend 355 florins. **[A]** ADB.
- 12 September 1480, citizenship of Pistoia for himself and his descendants, office eligibility, thirty years' tax exemption. Grant **[A]** (Treccani places him at Pistoia 1477-1481 and says he gained citizenship there); the date and the term **[R]**, from the letter printed with the *Phoenix* only.

## 3. The Padua chair, 1482-1497

- Returned to Padua to a chair in canon law at 80 ducats. **Contradiction, flagged and not resolved:** Treccani 1482-1483, NDB late 1483, de.wiki **from late 1479**. The salary is firm, the year is not; the journey dates 1482 as an ordering device and states all three readings in `date_confidence`.
- 1484, raised to 150 ducats; 1492, a further 50 "because of his many children," total 200. Second wife Lucrezia Azzoni, of Este. **[A]** Treccani.
- c. 1485, *Carmina*, Padua, Matthaeus Cerdonis. **[R]**: Treccani carries it, but no copy or catalogue record was examined.

**The four testimonials printed with the *Phoenix*.** Peter printed his own references at the front of his own book: Montferrat 1487 (safe passage, the marquis having "seen and wonderfully experienced" the memory), Decio 1488, Ferrara 1491 (Eleonora of Aragon receiving him among her retainers, with free passage for servants, horses, books and silver), Pistoia 1480. All were read in English translation (artofmemory.com PDF); **none was traced to its original document**, so all are **[R]** as to the archive and **[A]** as to what the printed book says. **Discrepancy flagged and left standing on Decio:** the dossier calls it a letter praising Peter's prodigious memory, while the translated text praises nothing ("if you are well, I rejoice; I am well"), and he printed it among the testimonials to his memory regardless. **[gap]** No atlas journey file documents an Este-court stop against which the 1491 Ferrara episode could be cross-checked.

- Met Cassandra Fedele at Venice, c. 1490. **[R].** Attributed to Treccani but not present in the fetched text; en.wiki's Fedele article does not mention Peter. The pool tagged the identical claim [A] in one place and [R] in another; reduced to [R].

## 4. The *Phoenix*, Venice 1491

- Privilege 3 January 1491, colophon 10 January 1491, printer Bernardinus de Choris of Cremona. **[A]** Treccani, en/it.wiki, Cambridge *Memory Arts*. Both dates Venetian style, sometimes normalized to 1491/92.
- "The first known example of copyright." **[R], a modern legend.** it.wiki calls it "il primo esempio conosciuto di copyright"; Venice had already granted an author's privilege to Marcantonio Sabellico in **1486**. Carried in the journey *as* legend with the correction in the same campa, because the circulation of the claim is itself a checkable fact about reception.
- Method and images. **[A] for the text**, quoted from the English translation, fetched and read directly. Five passages carry into the journey as quotes: the hundred thousand places and "I carry all my things with me" (pp. 5-6); "Nor did I have a teacher in this artful memory" (p. 5); the church walked three or four times (p. 5); the golden conclusion and Juniper of Pistoia (p. 6); Gherard of Verona and the flea (p. 7). **[gap]** The translator is not named on the fetched pages, and neither Juniper nor Gherard is identified anywhere.
- Descent from the classical ars memorativa: Simonides of Ceos at Crannon, transmitted by Cicero (*De Oratore* II.86.351-354) and Quintilian (*Institutio* XI.2.11-17) and codified in *Rhetorica ad Herennium* 3.16-24. This is the standing uncontested account of the art Peter systematizes, and `cicero.journey.json` states the atlas's own version of the chain. **[R] on one point:** the *Phoenix* pages read for this file **do not name Cicero or Simonides**. The journey names both once, with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

## 5. Over the Alps: Greifswald, 1497-1503

- 1497, recruited by Bogislaw X of Pomerania for the University of Greifswald. **[A]** Treccani, en.wiki, NDB. Only the year is supported; the pool's month "November" is dropped.
- Bogislaw hearing him praised at Venice on the way back from Jerusalem. **[R]**, dossier-only and hedged in the dossier itself; carried explicitly as dossier material.
- 1497, Innsbruck: Maximilian I sends for him at night, he performs poetry and legal allegations, and is dismissed with honours and the title *eques auratus*. **[A]** ADB. **The pool had the wrong emperor and the wrong decade**, placing the title at 1487 under Frederick III, who appears nowhere in this connection and died in 1493; its two items are one episode, merged into one stop.
- April 1498, arrival with his family; matriculation 24 April; rector 3 May 1498; rector again spring 1501. **[A]** ADB, corroborated by NDB on both rectorates; Treccani knows only 1501 and does not contradict 1498.
- 1499, *Repetitio C. inter alia de immunitate ecclesiae*, Lubeck, **Lucas Brandis**. **[A]** de.wiki works list.
- 25 October 1502, his daughter **Marieta** dies at Greifswald. **[A]** ADB for name and day. **The epidemic the dossier places around her is [R]**, not carried as fact.
- 1502, *Aurea opuscula*, Leipzig, Wolfgang Stoeckel. **[A]** Treccani; Stoeckel independently attested at Leipzig from 1495.
- 1502, son Giambattista matriculates at Greifswald. **[R]**, dossier-only.

## 6. Wittenberg and Cologne, 1503-1508

- 1503, law chair at the Leucorea, founded 1502 by Frederick the Wise. **[A]** Treccani, NDB. 3 May 1503, public lecture on the power of pope and emperor, plus *sermones extraordinarii* on religious and moral topics; son Vincentius rector in spring 1504. **[A]** ADB.
- Summer 1506, departure from Wittenberg on account of plague. **[A]** de.wiki for the summer, Treccani for the reason. No consulted source gives a month or says the university formally dispersed.
- 3 December 1506, matriculation at Cologne, warm initial reception **[A]** NDB. 1507, Peter and Lucrezia enter the Franciscan Third Order **[A]** ADB, from the colophon matter of Peter's own book; only the precise year remains soft.

**The Cologne controversy: three claims that must be kept apart.**

1. **The burial dispute. [A]** en/de.wiki, ADB. Peter argued publicly that leaving unburied the corpse of an executed criminal who had repented and been absolved is contrary to natural and divine law and a mortal sin. Jacob van Hoogstraten, prior of the Cologne Dominicans, answered; a faculty commission warned the propositions could be punishable. Per ADB the quarrel also ran on **whether tithes are iuris divini**.
2. **Ortwin Gratius defended him in the *Criticomastix*. [A]** ADB. That Gratius later switched sides, and that Gerardus de Zutphania was a co-antagonist, appear nowhere consulted; both **[R]**, not carried.
3. **The remark about Italian students. Substance [A], wording [R], framing corrected.** The item most likely to be mishandled downstream. **Do not print the Latin**: the string *scholares Itali non poterant vivere sine meretricibus* circulates in secondary scholarship and could not be seen in any source actually opened; it is not quoted in the journey. What was traced is Theodor Muther, "Ausgang des Petrus Ravennas" (Erlangen: Deichert, 1866), pp. 114-115, translating the close of Peter's own 1508 pamphlet *Valete cum perpetuo silentio ad ... Jacobum de Alta platea*: Peter says he had said, orally and in writing and in jest, that Italian students cannot live without prostitutes; that some who "always hung on my mouth, to catch me somewhere" began to cry "Crucify him, crucify him"; and that he then resolved to show what he had said for a joke could be defended in law. **Muther prints only German**, his footnote 28 being a bibliographic reference (Panzer VI. 365. 165) rather than a Latin text, so *pro facetia et ioco* is likewise unverified. Hoogstraten answered in May 1508 with a tract on the same question (Muther, ~p. 120). **Framing:** this was **not** the sentence that triggered the controversy, which per ADB was already running on tithes and burial; it was a later flashpoint Peter himself raised in his farewell pamphlet.
- 1508, *Alphabetum aureum utriusque iuris*, Cologne. Book **[A]** Treccani, de.wiki. **Printer: the heirs of Heinrich Quentell**, since Quentell himself died 10 August 1501 and a 1508 book from that shop is a "Quentell Erben" imprint.
- 16 April 1508 (Palm Sunday), farewell sermon; 27 April 1508, departure by ship. **[A]** en.wiki.

## 7. Mainz and Worms, 1508-1510

- Summer 1508, ordinary chair in canon law at Mainz. **[A]** ADB, NDB. **Pin corrected, the pool's error being a twentieth-century one:** it gave 49.9929/8.2473, the modern Johannes Gutenberg University campus, built on a 1938 anti-aircraft barracks site and opened as a university only in 1946. Peter's university was the old University of Mainz (1477) in the Altstadt beside the cathedral, **49.9989/8.2739**, 2.2 km northeast. A gazetteer produces this error silently and no reader catches it on a map.
- Mainz, address at the Carmelite convent answering the charge that he condoned fornication, before an audience said to include the Cardinal of Santa Croce. **[R]**, unattested in ADB, NDB, Treccani or any Wikipedia. Pin corrected to the Karmeliterkirche, **50.0027/8.2733**. *Contra Gherardum de Zutphania et fratrem Jacobum*, Mainz, Johann Schoeffer, 1508, is likewise **[R]**, dossier-only.
- A "Compendium breve" on feudal custom at Mainz 1508. **[R] and internally contradictory in the pool**, which placed a feudal compendium at Cologne 1508 in one item and Mainz 1508 in another. Both en. and de.wiki give *Compendium juris civilis et Canonici*, **Cologne: Hermann Bungart, 1507**. Not carried as fact.
- Autumn 1509, advocate at the Reichskammergericht at Worms. **[A]** NDB, with structural corroboration: de.wiki gives the court's seats as Regensburg 1503-1509, then Worms 1509-1513. **[gap]** The courtroom building is not identified; the journey uses the canonical Worms pin, **49.63073/8.359961**, byte-identical from `martin_luther.journey.json`.
- Death between 1509 and 1510, probably at Worms. **[A] as a range only** (Treccani, "Mori tra il 1509 e il 1510"; NDB concurs). The older shorthand "died 1508 at Mainz" is treated as superseded. **[gap]** No day, no month, no cause, no witness.
- **No burial place, anywhere. [A] as a confirmed negative.** en., it. and de.wiki all stop at the death year with no burial detail, and a targeted search returned nothing in Worms or Ravenna: no slab, no plaque, no bust, no street. A finding, not a research shortfall.

## 8. Afterlife and reception

- Reprints at Erfurt 1500 and Cologne 1508, and translation into numerous languages. **[A]** it.wiki. Robert Copland's English *An Art of Memory That Otherwise Is Called the Phoenix* **[A]** en.wiki, Cambridge chapter, though **bibliographers date it 1545 or c. 1548 and do not agree**; flagged, not resolved. The French Paris 1545 version is **[R]**, a bare listing on en.wiki.
- Venice 1533 reprint (Nicolini da Sabbio for Melchiorre Sessa). **[R]. Failed verification recorded explicitly:** a Wellcome Collection search and an archive.org fetch both failed to surface the record. Vicenza 1600 (Pietro Bertelli) and Cologne 1608 are likewise **[R]**, from the dossier's editions list only.
- **Giordano Bruno read Peter's memory works as a boy. [A]** Cambridge chapter, which states Bruno "studied the works of Peter on memory as a boy"; it.wiki says only that he read and appreciated them. The further claim of a continuity between Peter's system and Bruno's mnemonic "seals" **is not in the Cambridge chapter it was attributed to** and was deleted.
- 1 November 1518, Reuchlin to Cardinal Achille de Grassis, that "der goettliche Petrus Ravennas" perished from grief after Hoogstraten drove him out. **[A] for the letter, its date and its gist** (ADB). **[R] for the Latin *prae maerore***, not located in any primary text and not quoted in the journey. Against the letter: Peter did not die at Cologne, having taught two further years at Mainz and pleaded at Worms afterward, and Reuchlin was himself five years into prosecution by the same Dominican.
- **Omission from the modern general survey. [A].** en.wiki's "Art of memory" article was fetched and checked and does not mention Peter anywhere. Rafal Wojcik's "Masters, Pupils, Friends, and Thieves" does: **[A] for the citation**, resolved internally, `johannes_cusanus.journey.json` giving it as *Daphnis* 41 (2012): 399-418 with a roster of memory-teachers c. 1474-1530 including "Petrus de Ravenna."

## Interlocks: carried and refused

Rule applied: a traveler is named in a campa only where Peter himself has a direct or indirect relation to that person. Shared geography is not a relation.

**Carried, four slugs named in campa.** **`giordano_bruno`**, who studied Peter's memory works as a boy: a one-directional relation, named at Peter's own Venice reprint stop as reception of Peter's object, not staged as a scene of Bruno's. **`johannes_reuchlin`**, who wrote substantively about Peter by name in a dated letter: named at Peter's Cologne pin, with `date_confidence` stating that Reuchlin's own act of writing belongs on Reuchlin's file. That file independently attests Hoogstraten as prior of the Cologne Dominicans running an almost identical campaign from 1513, which is what makes this antagonist checkable at all. **`simonides_of_ceos`** and **`cicero`**: intellectual descent, since the *Phoenix* systematizes the loci-and-images method whose founding scene is Simonides at Crannon as Cicero transmits it.

**Refused.** **`giulio_camillo`** (the Padua near-miss of 1498 is shared geography), **`martin_luther`** (Wittenberg 1508 and Worms 1521, co-location only, though both canonical pins are inherited from his file), **`johannes_cusanus`** (a parallel memory-teacher at Cologne c. 1501, milieu not encounter; his file's Wojcik citation is used as evidence instead), **`nicholas_of_cusa`** (the same two towns decades earlier).

**`heinrich_cornelius_agrippa`: a contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** The dossier claims, via the Cambridge volume and unverified, that Agrippa heard Peter lecture at Cologne. `heinrich_cornelius_agrippa.journey.json` puts Agrippa in Paris c. 1503-1507 and Spain 1508-1509, **out of Cologne for the whole of Peter's teaching there**. Not carried into the journey and not deleted from the record; left open for whoever verifies the Cambridge volume directly.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Peter of Ravenna is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`**, and no apparatus stop was written: he has no theoretical framework being applied backward to material he never treated. Nothing was silently deleted. One thing is flagged for a ruling.

**Direction caught: forward, in the block-rule sense, in the reception segment.** "The Phoenix After Him" carries four stops postdating the death in 1509/10: Reuchlin's letter (1518), the Venice reprint (1533) naming Bruno, Copland's translation (c. 1548), and the Vicenza printing (1600). Under the strict reading of `EXCEPTIONS.md`, a journey file is a block and can embed only what exists when its own clock stops, so each is a later hash in an earlier block. They were carried on the precedent of the atlas's own exemplar, `abdelkader.journey.json`, which closes its last campa on the 1966 repatriation of the subject's remains, eighty-three years after his death. The working distinction taken here: **posthumous reception of the subject's own object** is not the forward reference the exception forbids, whereas importing a later person's *framework* or staging a later person's *travel* is. Neither happens here. **If the operator reads the rule more strictly**, those four stops are the ones to cut, and the file drops to 41 stops and 7 segments. **Nothing was written into any other traveler's file.**
