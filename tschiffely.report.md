# Aimé Félix Tschiffely (1895-1954): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: the Swiss schoolmaster who rode two aged criollo geldings, Mancha and Gato, from Buenos Aires to New York, 1925-1928.*

**Short forms.** LRG = The Long Riders' Guild; HC I / HC II = Horse Canada, 'Tschiffely and the 10,000-Mile, Three-Year Ride', Parts I and II; en.wiki / es.wiki = the Wikipedia articles.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition or interpolation, including every coordinate no source published. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**; gaps are stated as gaps. About a dozen pool items arrived carrying CORRECTED notes overturning the first-stage claim; the corrections go into the journey file, the superseded versions are recorded here because several are in wide circulation.

---

## 1. Switzerland and England, 1895 to the early 1920s

- Born **7 May 1895 in Bern**, into an old Swiss family **[A: English Wikipedia, 'Aimé Félix Tschiffely']**.
- Educated in Switzerland, trained as a teacher **[A: en.wiki]**.
- Before settling into schoolmastering, a spell as a **professional footballer** and a spell as a **boxer**, then teaching in England **[A: en.wiki]**.

**Gaps.** Nothing consulted dates the football, the boxing or the English teaching, and no club, opponent, school or town is named anywhere reachable; the journey pins this phase on London as an avowed placeholder. No source gives the date of his emigration to Argentina, or the reason for it.

## 2. Argentina, the schoolrooms, early 1920s to 1925

- Teaching post at **St. George's College, Quilmes**, an English foundation **[A: en.wiki]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's longitude sat about 760 m too far west; Guido 800, B1878 Quilmes = **-34.7253, -58.2436 [A: school address]**.
- **Temporary headmaster of the Buenos Aires English High School**, using school vacations to ride the pampas **[A: en.wiki; LRG author page]**.
  - **Coordinate correction, serious.** The pool's pin (-34.6389, -58.3894) points at the pre-1906 downtown site. The school moved to **Melián 1880, Belgrano** in 1906 and stayed: about **-34.564, -58.463**, some 10 km away **[A: school's site history; R: address, not survey]**. **Gap:** no source dates the headmastership, and the journey uses 1924, `inferred`.

**Interlock, [R].** The pool's proposed overlap between this school world and Victoria Ocampo's Sur circle, patron of visiting lecturers such as Keyserling, is a milieu argument, not a documented contact: **no source places Tschiffely and Ocampo in a room together**.

## 3. The horses

- **1911:** Emilio Solanet buys roughly **90 mares and stallions** from a **Tehuelche cacique** in Chubut, including the stock later identified as ancestral to Mancha and Gato **[A: La Nación, 'La épica de Mancha y Gato', 2025]**. **Gap:** no source gives the cacique's name in verifiable form or the place of purchase, so the Chubut pin (-43.9, -70.0) is an **[R]** interpolation, labelled as such.
- **1923:** the **Asociación de Criadores de Caballos Criollos** is founded **16 June 1923** at a general assembly of breeders, first president **Raúl Videla Dorna** **[A: caballoscriollos.com/web/historia.php]**. **CORRECTED:** the pool said Solanet *founded* it. He did not; he was a founding figure and later president, and the campa says "among the founders".
- **1925:** Solanet gives Tschiffely **Mancha**, overo rosado, about 15, and **Gato**, gateado, about 16, both Patagonian-raised **[A: es.wiki 'Gato y Mancha'; La Nación 2025]**. No month given.

## 4. Departure, April 1925

- Departure from the grounds of the **Sociedad Rural Argentina, Palermo**, bound for Washington, D.C. **[A: en.wiki; es.wiki; Stork 2011]**.
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** **23 or 24 April 1925** (en.wiki, es.wiki) **[A]** against **20 April 1925** (La Nación 2025) **[A]**. Journey uses 1925-04-24 and records the disagreement in `date_confidence`.
- **Coordinate correction, serious.** The pool's departure pin (-34.6037, -58.3816) lands near the Obelisco, about 4.5 km southeast of Palermo. The predio ferial at Av. Sarmiento 2704 is **-34.5786, -58.4208 [A: venue address]**, and this is the departure pin of the whole journey. **Design decision:** the discarded pin is the atlas's canonical Buenos Aires coordinate, shared by nine travelers, so the journey carries **both**, a "city hears the plan" stop on the canonical pin and the departure on the corrected coordinates.
- **Quote, [R].** "a lunatic proposing to ride overland to New York", attributed to an unnamed 1925 Buenos Aires paper and repeated across LRG material and retailer blurbs. **No source names the paper or gives a dated citation.**

## 5. Argentina to Bolivia, 1925

- **Rosario** reached past the distance at which a skeptical veterinarian had predicted breakdown **[A: Infobae, 'La osada travesía de Gato y Mancha']**; **Santiago del Estero**, locust swarm **[A: Literary Traveler]**; **San Miguel de Tucumán**, last major Argentine city before the climb **[A: route summaries]**. **La Quiaca, Jujuy** as the frontier crossing is **[R: reconstruction from the standard overland route, named in no consulted source]** and is dropped rather than carried as a soft pin.
- **"Tres Cruces Pass, Bolivia"**, eleven thousand feet, nosebleeds, hail the size of eggs **[A for the episode: HC I]**.
  - **CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** The pool's coordinates (-21.9, -65.8) point at empty altiplano with no documented pass. Two candidates, neither matching the label: **Tres Cruces, Jujuy, ARGENTINA**, RN9, about -22.92, -65.58, 3,693 m, fitting itinerary and altitude; and **Abra Tres Cruces, BOLIVIA**, Quimsa Cruz, 4,729 m, far north and too high. The journey takes the Jujuy pin and calls the country label probably wrong. **[R]** on location, **[A]** on episode.
- **Potosí** anchors the stretch toward Challapata **[A: Stork 2011]**. **Paso El Cóndor**, between the two, is reported at **5,900 m** and said to set a world altitude record for mounted travel **[A for the report: Stork 2011; es.wiki]**. **[R] on location:** no coordinates published, so the pin (-19.0, -66.5) is an avowed interpolation. The record rests on one line of veterinary reporting with no survey behind it.
- **La Paz** reached about three weeks out from the border, the mayor receiving them **[A: HC I]**. **Gap:** no calendar date.

## 6. Peru and Ecuador, 1925-1926

- **Cusco** as gateway to the Inca heartland **[A: route descriptions; HC I]**. Year given as 1925 or 1926 depending on source; no date. **Ayacucho region, Peru**: landslides and river crossings **[A: LRG; Literary Traveler]**, pin inherited from `sucre.journey.json`.
- **Lima**, then **Trujillo** on the coast road **[A: route descriptions]**, no source attaching an episode to Trujillo.
- **Huarmey and Casma**: guide and animals give out at Huarmey; thirty leagues to Casma at 52 degrees in the shade, no water, no forage **[A: La Nación dispatch quoted verbatim in Stork 2011, p. 2]**.
  - **CORRECTED.** The pool had hooves sinking "to fifteen inches". Stork reads "de 6 a 15 pulgadas", a range. Its transcription had also lost every diacritic and the opening inverted exclamation; restored in the journey file.
  - **Gap.** Stork gives no issue date for this dispatch, dating only a separate La Nación editorial, **23 September 1928**.
- **Quito** approached through repeated landslides **[A: HC II]**. Crossing into Colombia at the **Rumichaca** natural bridge **[A: Literary Traveler]**; the coordinates (0.8167, -77.6667) are **[R]**, published by no source.

## 7. Colombia, the Darién, Central America, 1926-1927

- **Medellín** and **Cartagena** on the Colombian leg **[A: en.wiki; HC II]**. At the Caribbean the rains have set in and the roadless country ahead makes the overland route to Panama impossible.
- **The Darién is bypassed by ship [A: HC II; LRG, 'Heroes'; en.wiki]**; the legend that he rode it does not survive the sources. He is received as a celebrity by the canal administration and waits **six weeks** for the rains to break **[A: HC II]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's pin (8.9824, -79.5199) sits in Panama City, 8 km east of the locks; Miraflores is about **8.9961, -79.5911**.
- **Cerro de la Muerte**, horses falling repeatedly; **San José reached 15 April 1927 [A: route accounts dated to the day]**. **Nicaragua skipped by boat**, civil war and both sides short of horses, so the party sails to **El Salvador** **[A: route accounts]**. **Guatemala City**: the giant relief map at Minerva Park, and a passage by **Lake Atitlán** and **Nahualá** **[A: Literary Traveler]**.
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved: how many countries.** The pool said thirteen; no consulted source says thirteen. **Eleven** (LRG) **[A]**; **twenty** (Argentine tourism material) **[A as a published figure]**; "el polvo de veinte naciones" (La Nación, 23 September 1928, in Stork 2011), rhetoric not a count **[A as a quotation]**.

## 8. Mexico and the United States, 1927-1928

- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved: where Gato is injured.** A mule kicks him badly enough to end his ride. Infobae puts it at **Oaxaca**, the horse then entrained to Mexico City **[A]**; Stork 2011 and shorter summaries put it in **Mexico City** **[A]**. Both agree Tschiffely rides the rest on Mancha alone **[A]**.
- **Mexico City, 2 November 1927 [A: Infobae]**. Charro association reception and a mounted escort for the first ten miles out of the capital **[A: Literary Traveler]**; press, filmmakers, a bullfight parade **[A: Infobae]**.
- **CORRECTED: San Luis Potosí.** The pool routed him through it. **Attested in no consulted source.** Infobae's route runs Tapachula, Mexico City, Jalisco, Oaxaca. Dropped.
- **Entry into the United States at Laredo, Texas [A: LRG; HC]**, after bandit harassment in northern Mexico and a military escort.
- **CORRECTED: the US route.** The pool gave "San Antonio, St. Louis, Columbus". The documented route is **Laredo as guests of the Texas Rangers, Oklahoma, the Ozarks, St. Louis, the Mississippi, Indianapolis, Columbus, the Blue Ridge, Washington [A: LRG]**. **San Antonio is attested nowhere**; Indianapolis was missing. Gato, hurt on asphalt, cannot make the St. Louis stretch; only Mancha crosses **[A: LRG; Infobae]**.
- **Washington, D.C.**: received by **President Calvin Coolidge**, honoured separately by the **National Geographic Society** **[A: en.wiki; Literary Traveler; LRG]**. **Gap: no exact date** in any accessible source. Pin inherited, **38.8951, -77.0364**. **Fifth Avenue, New York, 20 September 1928**: **Mayor James J. Walker**, gold medal, ticker-tape parade **[A: LRG; Stork 2011; Infobae; La Nación 2025]**.
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved: distance and stages.** 21,500 km in 504 stages (La Nación 2025, es.wiki) against about 17,000 km in 494 (other summaries); the subtitle says ten thousand miles. All **[A]** as published figures.
- **CORRECTED: the elapsed interval.** The reprinted "three years, four months and six days" is an inherited arithmetic error. From 24 April 1925 to 20 September 1928 is **three years and 149 days** (es.wiki); Stork has "más de tres años y cinco meses".
- **Madison Square Garden exhibition [A but weak]**: **Infobae alone**, saying "several days" at an equestrian exposition; the "ten days" figure has no second source, and the LRG describes the New York honours without mentioning it. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's pin (40.7505, -73.9934) is the **current** Garden, opened 1968; in 1928 it was the **third**, Eighth Avenue between 49th and 50th, about **40.7625, -73.9877**.

## 9. Return and retirement, 1928-1947

- **Arrival at Dársena Norte, Buenos Aires, 20 December 1928**, aboard the steamer **Pan America** **[A: Infobae 2022, 2023; La Nación 2025]**. **CORRECTED:** the pool's geography lens said 21 December, its chronology lens 20 December; both cite Infobae, which says the twentieth. A reported 1 December sailing from New York is uncorroborated and dropped.
- Horses retired to **Estancia El Cardal** near Ayacucho, in the care of **Juan Dindart** **[A: es.wiki; Stork 2011]**. Solanet's daughter **Emma (b. 1934)** grows up there and rides Mancha in retirement **[A: La Nación 2025]**.
- **CORRECTED, and it matters: the pool's geography lens inverted the two deaths.** Every source consulted agrees on **Gato: 17 February 1944, aged 36**, and **Mancha: 24 December 1947, aged 40 [A: Infobae; es.wiki; Stork 2011; Camino Pampa]**. The lens had "Mancha dies in 1944, Gato in 1947, ages thirty-six and forty", inverting both years and both ages.
- **Non-contradiction, recorded so it is not re-raised.** Two pool [R] items claimed the ages are disputed (32 vs 36, 37 vs 40). **They are not.** Every consulted source gives 36 and 40; 32 and 37 are attested nowhere. Both items deleted.
- **Taxidermy.** On Solanet's instruction the hides are preserved, mounted, and exhibited at the **Museo del Transporte**, Complejo Museográfico Enrique Udaondo, **Luján** **[A: La Nación 2025; es.wiki; Infobae; Agroverdad]**. **Gap:** no source dates the mounting; the journey uses 1948-01-01, marked reconstruction.
  - **Coordinate correction.** The pool's two lenses disagreed (-59.0524 vs -59.1152) and one fell in open country east of town. The complejo, Lezica y Torrezuri 917, is about **-34.5703, -59.1075**.
  - **Myth corrected.** That Mancha and Gato lie at **La Recoleta** is unsupported by any source found. Bones at El Cardal, hides at Luján; Recoleta belongs to the man **[A: es.wiki; Stork 2011]**. The interlock lens's Recoleta proposal rests on the same confusion and is rejected.
- At El Cardal the horses' bones lie in the estate's park, facing a **templete** holding the urn with Tschiffely's ashes **[A: La Verdad de Ayacucho]**.

## 10. The London years and the writing

- **1933: 'Tschiffely's Ride: Ten Thousand Miles in the Saddle from Southern Cross to Pole Star'**, London, best-selling, translated into German, French, Finnish and Polish **[A: en.wiki; LRG]**. Written by Tschiffely in English, no translator **[A: HC]**. **December 1933: marries the radio performer Violet Marquesita [A: en.wiki]**, place presumed London **[R]**.
- **CORRECTED: the second book.** The pool gave "A Tale of Two Horses", 1935. The title is **'The Tale of Two Horses'**, published **1934** **[A: en.wiki bibliography; LRG reissue]**, told from the horses' point of view.
- **1937: returns to South America** for an automobile journey to Tierra del Fuego, later written up **[A: en.wiki]**, visiting Mancha and Gato at El Cardal on the way. **CONTRADICTION, minor:** 'This Way Southward' (Norton & Co.) is dated **1940** in the pool's quote apparatus and **1945** in its afterlife section. Through the 1930s he is a household name in the United States, living from book sales and appearing in **National Geographic Magazine** **[A: en.wiki]**.
- **1953: Emma Solanet visits the aging Tschiffely in Chelsea, London [A: La Nación 2025]**. Chelsea coordinates are the district, not an address: **[R]**.

## 11. Death and afterlife

- **Died 5 January 1954** at **Mile End Hospital, London**, entered shortly before Christmas 1953 for a very minor and routine operation **[A: LRG; en.wiki]**. **CORRECTED: age.** The pool said 60; born 7 May 1895, he was **58**.
- **Remains repatriated, arriving 13 November 1954**; buried at **La Recoleta Cemetery** with a gaucho-style funeral tribute **[A: La Nación 2025; El Día 2025; Infobae]**. Pin inherited from `eva_peron.journey.json`, **-34.5883, -58.3937**; the pool's lens gave -34.5875, -58.3931, under 100 m away.
- **AMBIGUITY, [R], not resolved.** Whether the urn stayed in one place at Recoleta for the full forty-four years, or moved within the cemetery after the gaucho ceremony: El Día (2025) implies an unbroken stay, other accounts describe a later relocation without saying whether the site changed.
- **22 February 1998:** per his final wishes the ashes leave Recoleta and are reburied at **Estancia El Cardal near Ayacucho**, beside a monument to Mancha and Gato **[A: en.wiki; La Nación 2025; La Verdad de Ayacucho]**.
  - **CORRECTED, framing.** The pool called El Cardal "the land where the ride began". It is not: the ride began at the Sociedad Rural, about 300 km north. El Cardal is where the horses came to him.
  - **Gap, stated.** Coordinates for the monument and horse burial are published nowhere consulted; only the Ayacucho district association is documented, so the journey uses the town coordinates (-37.1509, -58.4894) as a labelled proxy.
- An undated monument stands in **Plaza Colón, Ayacucho**, with an annual tribute by creole breeders' associations **[A: Argentina.gob.ar; Municipalidad de Ayacucho]**. **1999:** Argentina designates **20 September** the **Día del Caballo** **[A: La Nación 2025]**. **April 2025:** the Solanet family marked the centenary **[A: La Nación, April 2025; Agroverdad]**.
- His widow **Violeta Tschiffely** took charge of his papers and those of **Robert Bontine Cunninghame Graham**; the LRG later reissued much of the work as 'The Tschiffely Collection' **[R: LRG alone, undated]**.
