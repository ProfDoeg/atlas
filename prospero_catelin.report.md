# Próspero Catelin (Thomas Prosper Catelin), 1764-1842

*Research report, 2026-08-15. French architect and engineer; jefe de obras públicas of the province of Buenos Aires and director of its Departamento de Ingenieros Arquitectos until 1828; author of the neoclassical portico of the Catedral Metropolitana de Buenos Aires, of the Sala de Representantes in the Manzana de las Luces, and of the ground plan of the Cementerio de la Recoleta.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, with the source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including everything the sources leave blank. Contradictions between sources are printed as contradictions and are not adjudicated here.

A warning that governs the whole file: **this is a thin subject.** Catelin's documented life occupies seven years out of seventy-eight. Everything before 1821 and after 1828 is either absent from the accessible record or inferred from the one attested span in the middle. Three buildings carry his name, two of them disputed in part, and the burial that supplies the story's usual last line rests on a Wikipedia category tag rather than on any sentence of text.

---

## 1. France, 1764 to c. 1820: the undocumented half

- Born 1764 **[A: Getty Research Institute ULAN record 500117981, giving "Catelin, Thomas Prosper," 1764-1842; es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"; Wikidata Q9063508]**. No source consulted gives a month, a day, or a birthplace, not even a province **[R: gap]**.
- Getty's authority file carries a **second, separate record**, "Catelin, Próspero," ID 500517518, marked *unidentified*, alongside the dated one **[A: Getty ULAN search results]**. The authority file has not decided whether these strings name one man or two. Recorded, not resolved.
- Training, family, teachers, corps, and any work executed in France: **nothing**. Absence confirmed by direct consultation of es.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia, pt.wikipedia, Wikidata and Getty ULAN **[R: stated gap]**.
- The emigration is undocumented: no year, no port, no ship, no passenger record, no motive **[R: gap]**. The 1821 appointment is the first fixed point in his biography, and the inference of a crossing in 1820 or 1821 hangs on it.
- **A correction to an earlier framing.** A draft grouped Catelin with "men like Pellegrini and Bertrès who later cross to Buenos Aires beside him." Both halves are false. Felipe Bertrés (1786-1856) emigrated in 1808, landing at Maldonado on 9 August aboard *Le Consolateur*, thirteen years before Catelin **[A: es.wikipedia "Felipe Bertrés"]**. Charles Henri Pellegrini (born 1800) arrived in November 1828, seven years after **[A: es.wikipedia "Carlos Enrique Pellegrini"]**. Neither crossed with him; Catelin is twenty-two years older than Bertrés and thirty-six older than Pellegrini.
- The Palais Bourbon question belongs to this phase only as an object, never as a visit. Poyet's twelve-column colonnade was completed in **1807** **[A: es.wikipedia "Catedral Metropolitana de Buenos Aires," citing Mario Buschiazzo; fr.wikipedia "Palais Bourbon"]**, thirteen or fourteen years before Catelin left France, not "a few years before" as an earlier draft had it. **No source places Catelin in Paris at any date.** The journey file carries a Palais Bourbon stop, and its date_confidence says exactly this: the stop marks the model his portico is credited to, not a documented presence.

## 2. Buenos Aires, 1821: the appointment

- Recruited among the European technicians brought in under Bernardino Rivadavia's program to modernize the city away from its colonial Spanish inheritance **[A: es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"]**.
- Appointed **jefe de obras públicas** of the province of Buenos Aires, 1821 **[A: es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"]**. No month, decree, or salary located **[R: gap]**.
- **Correction on the department.** es.wikipedia says only that he "dirigió hasta 1828 el equipo del Departamento de Ingenieros-Arquitectos": an *end* year, no start year, and no statement that the department was newly formed. The commonly repeated "placed at the head of the newly formed department in 1821" is an inference from the separate public works appointment, tagged **[R]** here and in the journey file's date_confidence.
- The department's membership as given by es.wikipedia: Pierre Benoît, James Bevans, Felipe Bertrés, Paolo Caccianiga, Charles Henri Pellegrini, Joseph Pons, Charles Rann, Carlo Zucchi **[A: es.wikipedia, pt.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"]**. **This roster is not a snapshot of any single year and must not be presented as his 1821 or mid-1820s colleagues.** Benoît was appointed to the department only in 1823 **[A: es.wikipedia "Pierre Benoît": "fue nombrado en 1823 por Bernardino Rivadavia arquitecto constructor de planos en el Departamento de Ingenieros Arquitectos"]**. Pellegrini did not reach Buenos Aires until November 1828 and joined the Departamento de Ingenieros **Hidráulicos**, not the Ingenieros-Arquitectos **[A: es.wikipedia "Carlos Enrique Pellegrini"]**.
- es.wikipedia's Catelin article calls Rivadavia "el entonces presidente" for 1821, which is wrong: in 1821 he was minister of government under governor Martín Rodríguez, and became president only on 8 February 1826 **[A: es.wikipedia "Bernardino Rivadavia"]**.

## 3. The Sala de Representantes, 1821

- Catelin directs the building of the hall for the provincial Junta de Representantes, on the site of the former *calabozo de Oruro* in the Manzana de las Luces **[A: es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"; es.wikipedia "Manzana de las Luces": "La obra fue dirigida por el arquitecto Próspero Catelin y se ubicaba en una de las casas redituantes"]**, at the corner of Moreno and Perú.
- The plan has been read as reflecting Jeremy Bentham's theories on the space of a legislative assembly **[A as a reported reading: es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"]**. No source consulted cites a Bentham text or a document showing Catelin working from one; the attribution of the idea is the encyclopedia's **[R as to mechanism]**.
- Legislative use: the provincial legislature sat there from 1822 to 1884, "salvo dos años y medio"; the Congreso General Constituyente met there 1824-1827 and the Congreso Nacional 1862-1864 **[A: es.wikipedia "Manzana de las Luces"]**.
- **Coordinate correction.** An earlier draft gave -34.6112, -58.3731 for the hall, roughly 125 m east of the block, past Perú street. es.wikipedia geotags the Manzana at -34.61056, -58.37444; the journey file uses **-34.6106, -58.3744**. (daniel_schavelzon.journey.json uses -34.6099, -58.3737 for the same block; under 100 m apart, left standing.)

## 4. The cathedral facade, 1821/1822/1826 to 1824

**The start date is contradicted three ways and is not resolved here:**

1. es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin": "El pórtico de la catedral, comenzado en 1821."
2. es.wikipedia "Catedral Metropolitana de Buenos Aires": facade works commence **January 1822**.
3. en.wikipedia "Buenos Aires Metropolitan Cathedral": "Starting in 1826, French architects Prosper Catelin and Pierre Benoit built a new Neoclassical façade."

Getty ULAN's activity note, "active 1822-1827 Argentina," favours the 1822 start **[A: Getty ULAN 500117981]**, and the journey file dates the scaffolding to January 1822 while printing all three dates in its date_confidence. The 1826 date is hard to reconcile with the columns standing in 1823 (below) and is recorded as a contradiction rather than dismissed.

- The state of the building before him: the nave collapsed on **23 May 1752**, leaving the facade and towers standing; those were deliberately **demolished in 1778** as too small for the new cathedral, which was consecrated in **1779** without a facade **[A: en.wikipedia "Buenos Aires Metropolitan Cathedral"]**. An earlier draft's "since a wall collapsed decades earlier" misstates this and is corrected in the journey file.
- The design: a **dodecastyle portico in the giant Corinthian order**, a Greek temple front **[A: es.wikipedia "Catedral Metropolitana de Buenos Aires"]**. The twelve columns as the twelve apostles is given in the same source as the reading the city took up **[A]**.
- The model: **the Palais Bourbon**, "cuya fachada tiene también doce columnas," argued by **Mario Buschiazzo** against the popular tradition naming the Église de la Madeleine **[A: es.wikipedia, citing Buschiazzo]**. The Madeleine attribution fails on two counts in the same article: eight columns, and completion only in 1842 **[A]**.
- **Do not conflate Mario Buschiazzo, the architectural historian, with Juan Antonio Buschiazzo (1845-1917), who remodeled the Recoleta.** Different men; the corpus holds a file for the second only.
- Construction: Italian workmen and imported materials **[A: es.wikipedia]**; a draft's "imported stone" overspecifies, the source saying only "operarios italianos y materiales importados."
- **Authorship is explicitly unsettled in the sources themselves and is tagged [R].** es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin": "no se sabe a ciencia cierta si se trató de una producción propia o si también intervinieron en ella Pierre Benoit y Bernardino Rivadavia." es.wikipedia "Pierre Benoît": "se cree que intervino." en.wikipedia credits Catelin and Benoit jointly and flatly. Benoît's appointment to the department in 1823 makes any role in a 1822 design later than the drawing.
- **José Santos Sartorio: not found.** The received account names him among the facade's builders. A direct search of the cathedral's own article for "Sartorio" returned nothing, and no other source consulted attaches him to the work **[R: unverified, flagged as a gap and not as a refutation]**. He should be separated from the Benoît question, which is attested-but-uncertain, rather than bracketed with it.
- By **1823** the twelve column shafts stand, without capitals and without the tympanum relief; those elements would not be finished for another four decades **[A: es.wikipedia "Catedral Metropolitana de Buenos Aires"]**.
- **Coordinate correction.** Earlier drafts used -34.6084/-58.3731 and -34.6086/-58.3731, both of which land inside the Plaza de Mayo, roughly 115-135 m south of the building. The cathedral is geotagged **-34.607408, -58.373277** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The journey file uses -34.6074, -58.3733 throughout.

## 5. The Cementerio de la Recoleta, 1822-1823

- Founded **17 November 1822** on the *huerta* of the convent of the Franciscan recoletos (convent established 1732), under governor **Martín Rodríguez** with **Rivadavia** as minister of government; the first public cemetery of the city **[A: es.wikipedia "Cementerio de la Recoleta"]**.
- **Name correction.** es.wikipedia gives "Cementerio del Norte" as a "nombre menos común," used in contrast with the Cementerio del Oeste (Chacarita). It is **not** "Cementerio General del Norte" and is not a formal earlier title. Earlier drafts carried the wrong form.
- Design broadly modeled on **Père-Lachaise**, "a grandes rasgos" **[A: es.wikipedia "Cementerio de la Recoleta"; es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"]**; orthogonal streets, avenues and diagonal passages **[A: es.wikipedia]**. A French-language source independently credits "l'architecte et ingénieur Français Prósper Catelin (1764-1842)" with the design **[A: fr.wikipedia, article on the Cimetière de la Recoleta, retrieved via search index]**.
- **Collaborators disputed.** One account names Bertrés and Pellegrini beside Catelin on the layout. **Pellegrini is impossible**: born 1800, arrived November 1828, six years after the cemetery opened **[A: es.wikipedia "Carlos Enrique Pellegrini"]**. **Bertrés is defensible**: in the Río de la Plata since 1808 **[A: es.wikipedia "Felipe Bertrés"]**. fr.wikipedia credits Catelin alone **[A]**. Tagged **[R]** in the journey file and printed as a dispute.
- **Decree of 3 September 1823**, previously mischaracterized in drafts as a formal approval of the parceled plan. It is a **tariff and precedence decree**: a scale of prices according to a plot's location, with preferred plots reserved for those "cuyas virtudes o servicios relevantes a la sociedad" merited distinction **[A: es.wikipedia "Cementerio de la Recoleta"]**.
- The phrase "city within a city," carried in an earlier draft, **could not be traced to any consulted source** and has been dropped **[R: unsourced]**.
- The **1880/1881 remodeling**: Torcuato de Alvear commissioned Juan Antonio Buschiazzo in **1880** ("En 1880, Torcuato de Alvear ... encomendó al arquitecto Juan Antonio Buschiazzo para la remodelación del cementerio"), the work following in 1881. The brick perimeter wall, the paved internal streets, and the entrance portico of four Doric columns inscribed "Requiescant in pace" outside and "Expectamus Dominum" inside all date from then, not from Catelin **[A: es.wikipedia "Cementerio de la Recoleta"; corroborated in juan_antonio_buschiazzo.journey.json and torcuato_de_alvear.journey.json]**. Drafts giving 1881 as the commission year are corrected to 1880. The gate plaque carries 1822 beside the renovation date, the only place Catelin's year of work is visible on the site, and it does not name him.
- **Coordinate.** es.wikipedia geotags the cemetery at -34.58806, -58.39306 and fr.wikipedia at -34.587467, -58.392903. The journey file inherits the corpus pin **-34.5875, -58.3936** byte-identical from juan_antonio_buschiazzo.journey.json and torcuato_de_alvear.journey.json.

## 6. 1824-1828: the department, and the end of it

- Directorship ends in **1828** **[A: es.wikipedia, pt.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"]**. No circumstance, no month, no successor recorded **[R: gap]**.
- **Arithmetic correction.** A draft claimed Rivadavia's government "had already fallen two years earlier" than 1828. Rivadavia resigned the presidency on **27 June 1827**, one year before; and the government that actually appointed Catelin, Martín Rodríguez's, ended in **1824**, four years before **[A: es.wikipedia "Bernardino Rivadavia"]**. Neither reading yields two years. The related claim that Catelin "outlives the government that hired him by sixteen years" fails the same way: fifteen years on the Rivadavia reading, eighteen on the Rodríguez reading.
- Public works beyond the three named buildings: **not documented**. The title implies streets, drainage and government buildings, but no source itemizes a single further project under his name **[R: inference from title; gap otherwise]**.

## 7. 1828-1842: silence, death, and a burial that rests on a category

- **Fourteen years with nothing in them.** No source consulted covers any year between 1828 and 1842 **[R: stated gap]**.
- **Death 1842** **[A: es.wikipedia "Próspero Catelin"; Getty ULAN 500117981; Wikidata Q9063508]**. **Place of death is unattested.** The es.wikipedia article body never says where he died; it gives only "(1764 - 1842)." Buenos Aires is an inference and is tagged **[R]** in the journey file's date_confidence. No month, day, or cause located.
- **Burial at the Recoleta: [R], traditional and unverified.** The rhetorical payoff of the whole biography depends on it, so it deserves the plainest statement. The es.wikipedia article's **body text contains no statement that Catelin was buried at Recoleta**; the claim rests solely on the page's category tag, "Categoría:Sepultados en el cementerio de la Recoleta," an unsourced editorial label. es.wikipedia's article on the cemetery **does not list Catelin** among the personalities buried there. The sentence "he is buried in the cemetery he laid out" may well be true. Nothing consulted shows it.
- No plot, vault, inscription, photograph, or state-of-upkeep information for any grave of his was located; the Recoleta cemetery's own visitor pages on notable interments **did not resolve** during this research **[R: inaccessible source, named below]**.
- **1862-1863**: the sculptor **José Dubourdieu** executes the Corinthian capitals and the tympanum bas-relief, and the columns are stuccoed; the relief shows Joseph's reunion with Jacob and his brothers, read as an allegory of national reunification after the 1861 battle of Pavón **[A: es.wikipedia "Catedral Metropolitana de Buenos Aires"]**. Twenty years after Catelin's death, four decades after his drawing.

## 8. Written work

No published edition, treatise, or written architectural work by Catelin was found in any source consulted **[R: gap, not a confirmed non-existence]**. No drawing, signature, portrait, or letter of his has been located either.

## Interlocks with the existing corpus

Only relations in which Catelin himself, or his built work, is one of the two terms are listed. Shared city and shared decade are not relations.

- **juan_antonio_buschiazzo** and **torcuato_de_alvear**: Alvear commissioned and Buschiazzo executed the 1880-1881 remaking of the Recoleta, walling and paving the ground Catelin laid out and setting the Doric gate whose plaque carries 1822. This is a direct one-directional engagement with Catelin's plan and is named in the campa of the final Recoleta stop, with both files cited as sources. Neither man's own scene is staged inside Catelin's journey; Buschiazzo's commission and burial stops live in his own file.
- Everything else the interlock lens surfaced (San Martín at Remedios de Escalada's Recoleta grave in 1823, Rosas in 1820s Buenos Aires, Christophersen's Paris training, Liliana Crociati de Szaszak's tomb in the Recoleta precinct, the Cabildo remodelings, the Avenida de Mayo, Pedro Benoit's La Plata plan) is **co-location or shared period only**, and is deliberately not name-dropped in any campa.
- One genealogical link recorded without being used: **Pedro Benoit**, who drew the 1882 founding plan of La Plata, was the son of **Pierre Benoît**, Catelin's disputed collaborator **[R: not stated in the corpus files that mention Pedro Benoit]**.
