# Jacobus de Voragine (Iacopo da Varazze), c. 1228 - 1298

*Research report for `jacobus_de_voragine.journey.json`. 45 stops, 8 segments, calendar julian.*

Dominican friar, twice prior provincial of Lombardy, acting head of the Order of Preachers in the
interregnum of 1283-1285, archbishop of Genoa from 1292, civic chronicler, and compiler of the
**Legenda sanctorum**, renamed **Legenda aurea** and printed by Caxton as *The Golden Legende*. His
bibliography dwarfs his biography: one book's manuscript tradition is counted in four figures, and the
documented facts of his life fit on two pages.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single late witness ·
contradictions **flagged, not adjudicated**. The spine is **Casagrande, "Iacopo da Varazze," *Dizionario
Biografico degli Italiani* vol. 62 (2004)**, below **DBI 62**, fetched from treccani.it. Hedges live in
each stop's `date_confidence`, not in the prose.

**Coordinate corrections applied against the research pool**, all from it/en.wikipedia building and town
articles, and each also recorded in the affected stop's `date_confidence`: Bologna San Domenico
**44.4895 / 11.3445** (4 stops, pool ~360 m off); Milan Sant'Eustorgio **45.4540 / 9.1815** (pool ~360 m
off, and the Stefanardo stop sat on the Duomo, ~1.3 km wrong); Ceriana **43.883 / 7.767** (pool ~6.5 km
up-valley); Santa Maria di Castello **44.4056 / 8.9292** (the pool's afterlife lens gave San Lorenzo, a
lens-crossing); San Domenico Varazze **44.3608 / 8.5845** (pool had three wrong longitudes for it).

## 1. Origin and formation, c. 1228 - c. 1260

**Birth, 1228 or 1229 [R].** DBI 62 gives the year as approximate and the birthplace as *disputed*:
Varazze (medieval Varagine) by tradition and by the surname, Genoa by Casagrande's argument, since a
Genoese family *de Varagine*, descended from the coast town, is documented in the city. **Nothing is
known of his parents or means.** The stop pins Varazze and carries the dispute.

**Entry into the Order of Preachers, c. 1244, aged about fifteen or sixteen [A]** (DBI 62; Wikipedia).
"Probably at Genoa" is Casagrande's hedge, carried as hers. This is the **first securely dated fact of
his life**; who brought him, and why, is not recorded.

**Formation, c. 1244 - 1260s [R].** No register names a teacher, a study convent, or a year. The course
is reconstructed from the Dominican *ratio studiorum*; Bologna's San Domenico is a *plausible* venue,
not an attested one. **The comet of 1264 [A]** (DBI 62): visible forty days, place of observation not
given, so pinned at Genoa.

## 2. The Legenda sanctorum, from c. 1260

**Begun c. 1260 [A]** (DBI 62; Maggioni's SISMEL 1998 introduction as characterized in secondary
literature). A liturgical-year repertory: etymology of the name, then the life, then the doctrine. He
revised it for the rest of his life; it escaped his control early, and Maggioni reconstructs successive
*authorial* recensions against a tradition already contaminated locally.

**Quotations.** Eight stops carry verbatim quotes; six are Legenda text in Caxton's 1483 English (ed.
Ellis, Temple Classics 1900) from the **Fordham Internet Medieval Sourcebook**, fetched directly, all
[A] as text and all **Caxton's English, not Jacobus's Latin**, as every `quote_source` states. **No
Legenda chapter can be dated** within the decades of revision, so segment two's nine stops carry
ordering dates only, declared as such.

**Saints written into the year [A as to the source relation].** The interlock lens verified by grep that
eleven journey files cite the Golden Legend or Legenda as narrative source: `san_cristobal`,
`saint_george`, `catherine_alexandria`, `saint_barbara`, `saint_denis`, `saint_blaise`, `saint_erasmus`,
`san_vito`, `saint_eustace`, `saint_cyriacus`, `mary_magdalene`. He **wrote substantively about each**,
a real one-directional relation, which licenses naming them in his own campas. Four stops do so.

**The Fourteen Holy Helpers [R].** QUEUE.md row 87 names `catorce_santos_ayudantes`; **no file of that
slug exists in the checkout**, though several individual members do. The grouping postdates him, so the
campa says he supplies the parts and does not name the company.

## 3. The Lombard province, 1266 - 1286

**Priorates at Como, Bologna, Asti before 1267 [R], weakly.** Sole witness: **Borselli's
fifteenth-century chronicle**, two centuries late. DBI 62 flags the series as unproven; only Asti (1266)
has even indirect support, and the Legenda's chapter on Secundus of Asti is not corroboration.

**Elected prior provincial, general chapter of Bologna, 1267 [A]** (DBI 62; Wikipedia). **Governance
1267-1277 [A as to the office, R as to everything else]:** no itinerary survives, no route, no
residence, no dated letter placing him in one house rather than another; Sant'Eustorgio is a *likely*
base only.

**Absolutus at the general chapter of Bordeaux, 1277 [A]** (DBI 62; Wikipedia). Dominican usage attaches
no blame to the formula and **no scandal, trial, or disciplinary finding is recorded**. The four years
out of office [R] are inferred from output. **Restored by the provincial chapter of Bologna, 1281 [A]**.

**Second term ends 1286 [A as to the fact, R as to the manner].** *Contradiction flagged:* summaries
diverge on whether he was released at **Paris** or completed the term at a **Lombard chapter**.
Casagrande notes the divergence and does not resolve it; neither does this file, which pins the stop at
Bologna as the usual chapter seat. **No substantiated criminal or financial charge** attaches.

## 4. Relics, and the interregnum of 1283-1285

**Cologne relic obtained before Easter 1283 [A]**, from his own *Historia reliquiarum* via DBI 62: the
head of one of Saint Ursula's companion virgins. **Gap:** the text records the transfer and does **not**
place him at Cologne. **Installed at Saints Philip and James, Genoa, Easter Day 1283 [A]**, same source.
The convent no longer stands, so the pin is approximate; the sources give the feast, not a date, so the
file defaults to 04-01. The rubric quoted there calls him *quondam priorem provincialem*, but he held
the office until 1286, so **the text is later than the event it records**, which the campa says rather
than letting the quote imply a date.

**Acting head of the Order, 1283-1285 [A], upgraded from the pool's [R].** The pool tagged this
reconstruction because only "later Order histories" carry it. That under-credits: **DBI 62 itself has
him acting as regent 1283-1285** after Giovanni da Vercelli's death. Upgraded, with the caveat that the
constitutional title (regent, vicar) and where he exercised it are **not** fixed. Anchors: John of
Vercelli died **30 November 1283 at Montpellier**; Munio de Zamora was elected at the **general chapter
of Bologna in 1285** (en.wikipedia), correcting the pool's vague "Italy". **No stop is staged for
Munio's election**: that is Munio's event and nothing places Jacobus at it.

## 5. Curia and mitre, 1288 - 1292

**Diffinitor at the general chapter of Lucca, 1288 [A]** (DBI 62). The pool called this an unspecified
"chapter" and elsewhere a **provincial** chapter. Both wrong: **general** chapter, role **diffinitor**. **Papal faculty to absolve the Genoese,
1288 [A]** (DBI 62; Wikipedia), for supporting the Sicilians against Charles II of Anjou after the
Vespers, and a **failed candidacy for the archbishopric the same year [A]** (DBI 62), with no majority.
Those two are folded into one stop, because **their order within the year is not established**.

**General chapter of Ferrara, 1290 [A] for the chapter, [R] for the delegation.** DBI 62: *"capitolo
generale di Ferrara"*, so **general**, not provincial, correcting the pool. That he was **one of four
papally aligned delegates** conveying Nicholas IV's demand that Munio resign **rests on English
Wikipedia alone**. *Contradiction flagged:* en.wikipedia's own "Munio of Zamora" has Munio
**re-elected** at that chapter and mentions neither Ferrara nor a delegation.

**Munio deprived by bull, 12 April 1291 [A] as to date, place corrected.** The pool gave "Rome". The
bull went to the general chapter then meeting at **Palencia**, where King Sancho's men seized the convoy
and the bull, so it was not implemented until 1292. **No stop is staged**: nothing places Jacobus there.

**Two attempts on his life [R], one late source for both.** Borselli alleges confreres tried to throw
him down a well at **Ferrara** (1290) and attacked him again at **Milan** (1291) after he excluded prior
Stefanardo from a provincial chapter. **DBI 62 doubts both**, nothing contemporary corroborates either,
and both are kept as stops tagged tradition.

**Summoned to Rome; Nicholas IV dies, 1292 [A].** Arrival around **Palm Sunday, 30 March 1292**
(Wikipedia); the pope died **4 April** (DBI 62; Wikipedia) before consecrating him. **Gap:** where he
lodged is not recorded. **Consecrated 13 April 1292 [A]** (DBI 62; Wikipedia), during the vacancy, by
**Cardinal Latino Malabranca Orsini, bishop of Ostia**, a Dominican, "for the honor of the commune of
Genoa." **Gap:** the church is not identified; exceptional in timing, but no source calls it
clandestine, and the file does not. The Rome pin **41.9028 / 12.4964** is inherited byte-identical from
the corpus standing pin (`bernard_clairvaux`, `ramon_llull`, `rabban_bar_sauma`, `saint_eustace`).

## 6. Archbishop of Genoa, 1292 - 1298

**Temporal lordship of Sanremo and Ceriana [A]** (DBI 62); no visit to either is documented.

**Provincial council in San Lorenzo, June 1293 [A]** (DBI 62). **The recognition of the bones of Saint
Syrus is [R]**, resting on Promis's 1874 *Vita sancti Syri* as characterized in DBI 62, not read. **The
Mariale [A] as to the quoted phrase**: *"in episcopali speculo constitutus"* comes from the *Liber
Marialis* prologue **as quoted in DBI 62**, the edition itself not consulted, and the composition is not
precisely datable within the episcopate.

**The peace of early 1295 [A]** (DBI 62; Wikipedia): open-air preaching and a solemn procession
reconciling the Ghibelline *mascherati* and the Guelf *rampini*. **Collapse and the burning of San
Lorenzo, late 1295 [A]** (DBI 62, quoting his *Chronica*): the lament *"cithara nostra cito versa est in
luctum..."* is carried verbatim with a literal gloss, **through DBI 62**, since neither the manuscript
nor Monleone's edition was consulted. **Papal compensation, 12 June 1296 [A per the pool, unverified
here]:** the pool's gatherer records it as unconfirmed beyond the dossier, so it is a sentence in the
fire campa and gets no stop.

**Chronica civitatis Ianuensis, c. 1295-1298 [A]** (Wikipedia; Monleone, Rome 1941). Twelve parts, from
Genoa's founding by two figures named Janus to 1297, closing with a catalogue of his own works.
*"cum immenso gaudio et triumpho,"* of Oberto Doria's return, again through DBI 62.

**Sale of Sanremo to Oberto Doria and Ceriana to Giorgio De Mari, 1297 [A]** (DBI 62 plus the pool's
synthesis of Genoese notarial records). **The underlying acts were not read.** The devotional claim that
the proceeds went to the poor is **unverified**, and no evidence of profit or fraud has been produced
either; the campa states both absences.

## 7. Death and grave

**Final notarial acts, 1298 [R]:** from the pool's synthesis of the archiepiscopal record, and
"independently unconfirmed beyond the dossier."

**Death, night of 13-14 July 1298 [A]** (Wikipedia; corroborated by DBI 62). *Contradiction flagged:*
some older works give **1299**, some **16 July**; 1298 and the night of 13-14 July are better supported,
and the divergence is recorded, not resolved. **No cause of death is preserved.**

**Buried in San Domenico, Genoa [A]** (DBI 62; Wikipedia); the church was later suppressed and
demolished, its ground now under Piazza De Ferrari. **The effigy [A]**, Musei di Genova 198057: white
marble, carved after 1298 by a sculptor of the *Maestro degli angeli del Duomo* circle, **nose tip
missing**, sarcophagus lost, now in the Museo di Sant'Agostino.

## 8. Afterlife: body, cult, press

**Where the body is: the central unresolved contradiction of this dossier.**

| Source | Claim |
|---|---|
| DBI 62 (2004) | to **Santa Maria di Castello**, Genoa, end of the 18th c., *"dove tuttora si trova"* |
| santiebeati.it 62400 | to San Domenico's **high altar, 1582**, then Santa Maria di Castello, **1798** |
| it.wikipedia, "Santa Maria di Castello" and "Iacopo da Varazze" | stayed *"per quasi un secolo"*, then **definitively transferred 1974** to San Domenico, Varazze, where the remains are today |

**Not adjudicated.** The journey carries both stops (Genoa 1798, Varazze 1974), each confidence field
naming the other account; the 1582 high-altar move is in santiebeati.it and not in Treccani, also
flagged. **Relics at Varazze [A]:** it.wikipedia describes an urn in the **fifth chapel on the left**
holding bones and ashes; whether that is a *portion* or the *body* is the point above.

**Confirmatio cultus by Pius VII, 1816 [A] as to year, [R] as to day, place corrected.** Treccani,
santiebeati.it and the Catholic Encyclopedia give **only the year**; the day, **11 May**, rests on
**en.wikipedia alone**. The pool's geography lens placed the act at **Genoa**, wrong for a papal act,
while its other lens placed it at Rome, which the file uses. *Contradiction flagged:* **Fordham gives
1896** against three sources giving 1816, treated as a Fordham error, recorded not dropped.

**Feast on 13 July [A], source-attribution corrected.** New Advent permits the feast to "the clergy of
Genoa and Savona, and the whole Order of St. Dominic" only; the pool added **Varazze** without support
from the cited source, so Varazze is dropped. The date is confirmed by santiebeati.it and it.wikipedia.

**Print history [A]** (Catholic Encyclopedia): by 1500, **74 Latin editions**, 3 English, 5 French, 8
Italian, 14 Low German, 3 Bohemian; **Caxton, Westminster, 1483** for the first English; per Fordham,
Europe's best-selling printed book **1470-1530**. *Contradictions flagged:* the **1470 editio princeps**
is at **Basle** per New Advent and **Cologne** per Treccani, neither verified; and the manuscript count
runs **~900** (Fordham), **1,000+**, **1,200+** (Treccani), and "over 1,400", so all the ranges are named
in the Caxton campa rather than one being picked.

**Modern rehabilitation [A] for the titles, [R] for the framing.** Open Library verifies **Reames, 1985**
and **Epstein, Cornell UP** (catalogued at both 2015 and 2016; the 2016 imprint cited). Le Goff, Maggioni
and Benes are 403-blocked, so Benes's "first complete English translation" claim stays [R].

## 9. Rejected tradition: Boniface VIII and the ashes

Boniface VIII throws the Ash Wednesday ashes into the archbishop's eyes, calling him a Ghibelline. Not
a fabrication: the wording traces to *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, 11th ed. (1911), vol. 15, which prints
it as "a story, mentioned by the chronicler Echard as unworthy of credit." Kept out of the stops
because **the pool's date, 1300, is impossible** (he died 13-14 July 1298, so any Ash Wednesday scene
with Boniface, pope from December 1294, falls in 1295-1298); **DBI 62 does not mention it at all**; **no
Latin original and no contemporary witness was located**, the verifier's search budget running out
before the pre-1911 transmission could be traced; and **the anecdote is more commonly attached to
Porchetto Spinola**, archbishop of Genoa from 1299, Jacobus's *successor*. If ever wanted, the
defensible form is EB1911's sentence with Echard's rejection attached, tagged [R], not dated 1300.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Jacobus is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing has been silently deleted.

**Kept in the file, all FORWARD, all ordinary posthumous coda** (same shape as the 1966 repatriation in
`abdelkader.journey.json` and the cult coda in `saint_barbara.journey.json`, so no new licence is
assumed): Caxton 1483 and the print statistics; the 1798 and disputed 1974 transfers of the body; the
1816 *confirmatio cultus*; the effigy's passage into the museum. These are the fate of his own body,
cult, and book, not another person's apparatus applied to him. **Flagged rather than assumed:** if a
file whose clock stops in 1298 may not embed an 1816 or 1974 artifact, segment 8 is separable in one
edit.

**Dropped from the campas, kept here, all mere co-location**, since shared geography is not a relation:
`marco_polo` (imprisoned at Genoa in 1298, same city and year as the death, no contact documented);
`ramon_llull` (Genoa quay, June 1292 and August 1293, inside the archiepiscopate, no meeting in either
file, no mention in DBI 62); `rabban_bar_sauma` (Genoa 1288, and the Roman cardinalate 1287-1288);
`columbus` (born at Genoa 1451 into the city whose Janus origin myth the *Chronica* supplied,
**direction forward by a century and a half**, not licensable, not written).

**Backward relation NOT taken, for want of attestation.** The corpus holds `saint_francis`, `san_martin`,
`san_antonio_abad`, `bernard_clairvaux`, `saint_acacius`, `saint_pantaleon`, `saint_mercurius`,
`saint_thecla`. Several almost certainly have Legenda chapters, which would make them one-directional
relations of the same kind as the eleven verified, but **the lens verified only eleven by grep**, so
only eleven are named. The check for the rest is a grep of each file's `sources` for "Legenda".

**A stop belonging to someone else, correctly relocated.** it.wikipedia reports **St Catherine of Siena
at San Domenico, Varazze, 3 October 1376**, honoring his remains. That is **her** journey; it is cited in
`suggested_refs` on his Varazze stop and staged as a scene nowhere.

**Gaps the corpus has.** No journey file exists for **Munio de Zamora**, **Nicholas IV**, or **Latino
Malabranca Orsini**, the curial figures most entangled with his later career. And **Cologne is a pin
newly set, not inherited**: no existing file uses one, the lens proposed 50.9375 / 6.9603 as a generic
city pin, and this file uses **50.9458 / 6.9544**, the Basilica of St. Ursula, where the relic came
from. If a generic canonical Cologne is wanted, change it before another file is written against this.
