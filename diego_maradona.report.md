# Diego Armando Maradona (1960-2020), research report
*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Register: national mythology, the canon is true.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named in the same line · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or press claim with no primary record. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Where verification overturned an item in the pool, the overturned form is printed too, because the wrong form is what circulates.

---

## 1. Fiorito, 1960-1976

- Born 30 October 1960 at the Policlínico Evita (Hospital Presidente Perón), Lanús, Buenos Aires Province, fifth child of Diego "Chitoro" Maradona and Dalma Salvadora Franco. **[A: Wikipedia; the Argentine press; Asif Kapadia's *Diego Maradona* (2019)]**
- The family lives in Villa Fiorito, Lomas de Zamora, an unpaved shantytown south of the capital. **[A: Maradona, *Yo soy el Diego*; Vice, "The Nativity: Where Diego Maradona Comes From"]**
  - **Coordinate correction.** The pool placed Villa Fiorito at (-34.8071, -58.4227), roughly 12 km too far south, outside the barrio. It sits at approximately **-34.700, -58.450**. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Villa Fiorito"]**
- Given his first football at about age three and reportedly never afterward seen without one. **[R: Wikipedia biographical narrative; footballhistory.org. This is the founding anecdote of the myth and has no documentary anchor.]**
- Spotted at about eight playing for Estrella Roja; joins Los Cebollitas, the Argentinos Juniors youth side, March 1969; two Evita Tournament titles and a widely cited 141-game unbeaten run. **[A for the sequence, Wikipedia; the 141 figure traces to no match register: **[R]**]**
- Professional debut for Argentinos Juniors, 20 October 1976, ten days before his sixteenth birthday, wearing number 16. **[A: Argentinos Juniors club records; AFA historical archive]** The pool's "fifteen years and 258 days" does not survive arithmetic: 20 October 1976 is 356 days past his fifteenth birthday. The journey file omits the figure.
- First professional goals, 14 November 1976, two of them, two minutes apart, both past goalkeeper Rubén Lucangioli, in a 5-2 win. **[A: La Capital de Mar del Plata, "A 48 años de los primeros goles de Maradona"; El Gráfico, "A 40 años de los primeros goles"]**
  - **Correction, twice over.** The pool placed this match in Buenos Aires against San Lorenzo. The opponent was **San Lorenzo de Mar del Plata**, not San Lorenzo de Almagro, and the ground the **Estadio General San Martín in Mar del Plata**. Date and goals were right; the Buenos Aires and Almagro form is the one that circulates. No source reached gives coordinates for that ground, so the journey pin is approximate and says so.

## 2. The two capitals of Buenos Aires, 1977-1982

- Full international debut against Hungary, 27 February 1977, at sixteen. **[A: Wikipedia, "Diego Maradona"]** The pool gives the place only as "Buenos Aires"; La Bombonera as the ground is **[R]** here. Left off the 1978 World Cup squad by César Luis Menotti at seventeen; Argentina wins without him. **[A]** First senior international goal against Scotland at Hampden Park, Glasgow, 2 June 1979. **[A]**
- Captains Argentina to the FIFA World Youth Championship title, 3-1 over the Soviet Union, six goals in the tournament. **[A: FIFA tournament archive]**
  - **Date correction.** The pool's geography entry dated the Tokyo final 1 June 1979. The final was **7 September 1979**, National Stadium, Tokyo, attendance 52,000, referee José Roberto Wright. The tournament ran from late August into September. **[A: en.wikipedia, "1979 FIFA World Youth Championship final"]** The Tokyo coordinates in the pool (35.6775, 139.7146) are correct for the old National Stadium.
- 43 goals in 45 appearances in his final full Argentinos Juniors season. **[A: season-by-season statistics, Wikipedia]**
- Signs for Boca Juniors 20 February 1981; debuts **two** days later, 22 February, against Talleres de Córdoba, scoring twice in a 4-1 win; Boca takes the 1981 **Metropolitano**. **[A: en.wikipedia]** The pool said "four days later" and called the title simply the Primera División.
- 1982 World Cup: plays every minute of all five Argentina matches, is man-marked by Claudio Gentile in the loss to Italy, and is sent off late against Brazil at the Estadi de Sarrià on 2 July for a retaliatory kick on João Batista; Argentina lost 1-3 before 44,000. **[A: FIFA 1982 match records; contemporary press]** Sarrià stood at **41.3931, 2.1333**, not the pool's (41.3921, 2.1249), an error of about 700 m.

## 3. Barcelona, 1982-1984

- Bought by FC Barcelona for a world record fee of roughly £5 million, June 1982. **[A]** Hepatitis, then a broken ankle from Andoni Goikoetxea's tackle, September 1983, months out. **[A: FC Barcelona club history; El Mundo Deportivo, La Vanguardia]**
- The Copa del Rey final brawl. **[A: en.wikipedia, "1984 Copa del Rey final"]**
  - **Two corrections in one entry.** The pool's chronology section dated it June 1984 in Madrid; its geography section dated it **5 September 1984 at the Camp Nou**. Both are wrong, in different directions. The final was played **5 May 1984 at the Santiago Bernabéu, Madrid** (40.4531, -3.6883), Athletic Bilbao 1-0 Barcelona, King Juan Carlos present, three players a side suspended after the post-whistle melee.

## 4. Naples, 1984-1991

- Bought by SSC Napoli for a new world record of about £6.9 million; presented to roughly 75,000 people at the Stadio San Paolo, 5 July 1984, before kicking a ball for the club. **[A: Corriere dello Sport; SSC Napoli club history]** Begins using cocaine regularly in these years, and is fined by the club for missed games and training. **[A: Wikipedia]** The year 1985 is the pool's, **[R]** as a precise date.
- Son Diego Sinagra born in Naples to Cristiana Sinagra, 20 September 1986; paternity denied for years, an Italian court ruling against him in 1993 after he refuses DNA testing; public reconciliation in 2016. **[A: Wikipedia, "Diego Maradona Jr."]**
- First Serie A title, May 1987, with the Coppa Italia the same season, 4-0 on aggregate over Atalanta. **[A: Serie A records; SSC Napoli club history]** Second Serie A title, May 1990. **[A]** The pool's day for it (20 May) is uncorroborated in anything reached: **[R]** as a day.
- UEFA Cup, May 1989, the club's only European trophy. **[A]**
  - **Gap flagged.** The pool gives Naples as the place. The 1989 final was two-legged against Stuttgart and the tie was completed away. The journey file pins the first leg and hedges the date.
- World Cup semi-final at the San Paolo, 3 July 1990: he asks Neapolitans to back Argentina against Italy, appealing to the south's resentment of the north; Argentina wins on penalties; he is vilified across Italy afterward. Final lost 1-0 to West Germany at the Stadio Olimpico, Rome, 8 July 1990, on a disputed late penalty; he is booed through the ceremony. **[A]**
- Tests positive for cocaine after a Napoli match, 17 March 1991; fifteen-month ban; warrants tied to the city's cocaine and prostitution scene end his Naples career. **[A: FIGC record; contemporary press]**

## 5. The fall, 1991-1994

- Arrested in Buenos Aires on cocaine possession charges, 26 April 1991. **[A: Washington Post, 27 April 1991; Gulf News, "April 26, 1991: Maradona held by drug squad"]**
  - **Correction.** The pool put the arrest in Villa Devoto at (-34.6037, -58.5296). He was taken in a flat in **Caballito**, about 8 km east, near (-34.618, -58.441).
- Sevilla FC, September 1992, one season under Carlos Bilardo, his 1986 manager. **[A: Sevilla FC club history]** Newell's Old Boys, Rosario, a short and unhappy 1993-94 spell. **[A: Newell's club history]**
- Fires an air rifle at journalists outside his weekend house in Moreno; several wounded; suspended sentence of over two years handed down in 1998. **[A: IOL/Reuters, 23 February 2001; Goal.com]**
  - **Date correction.** 2 February 1994, not the pool's 25 February.
- 1994 World Cup, Foxboro Stadium, Foxborough, Massachusetts. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Foxboro Stadium"; New York Times, June-July 1994]**
  - **Correction of a conflation.** The pool merged two matches. The goal against Greece and the scream into the pitchside camera were **21 June 1994** (Argentina 4-0). The sample that failed for ephedrine was taken after the **Nigeria** match, **25 June 1994** (Argentina 2-1), his last World Cup appearance; expulsion followed on **30 June**. Career totals of 34 goals in 91 caps stand.
  - **Coordinate correction.** Foxboro Stadium stood at **42.0928, -71.2675**, not the pool's (42.0654, -71.2506).

## 6. The quotes: what survived verification

The pool's four 1994 quotations were confirmed word for word and **their place field was wrong in all four**: spoken in **Dallas, Texas**, 30 June 1994, in Juan Marcos Franchi's room at the team hotel, on camera, to Adrián Paenza, not in Buenos Aires. **[A: Infobae; ESPN, "Maradona en Estados Unidos 1994: 'Me cortaron las piernas'"]**

- The line as it circulates begins mid-sentence: the full run opens *"No quiero dramatizar, pero creeme que me cortaron las piernas..."* and closes *"...Tengo los brazos caídos, el alma destrozada."* The journey file carries it whole. **[A]**
- *"No corrí por la droga, corrí por la camiseta"* is a fragment; ESPN's transcript opens it *"Quiero que les quede claro a todos los argentinos que..."*. A variant, *"corrí por el corazón y la camiseta"* (CiberCuba), circulates elsewhere. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved**; the journey file quotes the ESPN form.
- The Bombonera farewell of 10 November 2001 is verified in full transcript, including *"Yo me equivoqué y pagué, pero la pelota no se mancha."* **[A: Los Andes]**
- The Hand of God line remains **[R]**: reported everywhere, in at least two wordings, confirmed here against no primary transcript or audio.
- The Castro letter is real, published by Castro himself in January 2015, and the pool's English rendering translates a Spanish original that runs past the pool's cut, on to *"...que un amigo vale más que todo el oro del mundo, y que las ideas no se negocian."* **[A: Diario Jornada, "Fidel Castro publicó cartas que intercambió con Maradona"]** The pool's date field was empty; it is January 2015.
- *"Tengo total confianza en los magistrados italianos..."* was dated 2020 in the pool. It is from **28 November 1998**, on arriving in Italy to testify before judge Raffaele Guariniello. **[A: La Nación, "Maradona declaró por su caso de doping de 1991", 28 November 1998]** The title the pool cited could not be verified as the origin and is replaced.
- The 2010 jab at Pelé is printed in the Spanish sources as two fragments, and as *"que vuelva al museo"*, not *"que se vuelva al museo"*. **[A: El Informador and Perfil/442, 16 June 2010]** The journey file quotes only the Platini-and-Pelé sentence, which is clean.

## 7. Retirement, coaching, 1995-2020

- Returns to Boca Juniors for two final seasons from 1995. **[A]** Last professional match **25 October 1997**, a Superclásico at River Plate's Estadio Monumental, River 1-2 Boca, substituted at half-time for Juan Román Riquelme; retirement announced on his 37th birthday, 30 October 1997. **[A: footballdatabase.eu; beIN Sports, "Maradona's Last Game For Boca Juniors"]**
  - **Correction.** The pool had him playing a farewell match at La Bombonera on his birthday. He did neither: wrong ground, wrong day.
- In and out of the Clínica La Pradera, Havana, for drug rehabilitation, roughly 2000-2004, and the friendship with Fidel Castro, whose face he had tattooed on his leg. **[A: Cuban and international press; his own statements]**
- Gastric bypass, 6 March 2005, at the **Medihelp** clinic in Cartagena de Indias, Bocagrande, director Francisco Holguín; admitted at 121 kg; about two hours. **[A: CBC Sports; Reuters archive, March 2005]** The pool named "Clínica Los Toros" at (10.391, -75.4794), about 8 km inland toward Turbaco.
- *La Noche del 10* ran a **single season**, 13 weekly episodes on Canal 13, 15 August to 7 November 2005. Pelé was the guest on the **debut** episode, in studio; the one large-venue special was the **finale**, at the Luna Park before some 5,000 people. **[A: en.wikipedia, "La Noche del 10"; Medio Tiempo; MDZ]** The pool gave a 2005-2007 run and staged the Pelé show at the Estadio Monumental.
- Head coach of Argentina from November 2008; eliminated 0-4 by Germany in the 2010 quarter-final. **[A: ESPN match 264119; Al Jazeera, 3 July 2010]** The match was at **Green Point Stadium, Cape Town** (-33.9036, 18.4109), attendance 64,100, not Soccer City in Johannesburg.
- Al Wasl, Dubai, 2011-2012, dismissed after one season; Dorados de Sinaloa, Culiacán, 2018-2019, close to promotion; Gimnasia y Esgrima La Plata from September 2019 to his death. **[A]** Estadio Juan Carmelo Zerillo is at **-34.9111, -57.9325**, about 2.5 km from the pool's pin.
- 60th birthday at the Gimnasia ground, 30 October 2020, visibly frail. **[A as event; "one of his last public appearances" is [R]]**

## 8. Death, and what is still in court

- Admitted 2 November 2020 to a clinic in La Plata with anaemia and dehydration, transferred to the **Clínica Olivos**, Vicente López, where a subdural haematoma was operated on 3 November by Leopoldo Luque; discharged from Olivos on 11 November. **[A: Washington Post, 11 November 2020; CNN, 12 November 2020; Infobae]** The pool had him discharged in La Plata; Olivos is where both surgery and discharge happened.
- Dies 25 November 2020 in the house at **lot 45 of the San Andrés gated community, Avenida Italia around 5200, Benavídez, Tigre Partido**, twenty-two days after the operation. **[A: Infobae, "Cómo era la casa donde murió Diego Maradona en Tigre", 30 April 2026; La Nación, 25 November 2020; Buenos Aires Times]**
  - **Two corrections.** English Wikipedia still carries the early-report locality **Dique Luján**, and the pool repeated it. The pool also said the address was never made public; it has been published repeatedly and entered into the trial record. Coordinates approximately **-34.41, -58.68**, about 9 km west of the pool's pin.
  - **Cause of death.** The autopsy recorded acute pulmonary oedema secondary to exacerbated chronic heart failure, with agony over roughly twelve hours. The widely repeated "died in his sleep of cardiac arrest" is not what it says. **Contradiction flagged.**
- Tens of thousands file past the coffin at the Casa Rosada, 26 November 2020; private burial the same day at the Jardín Bella Vista cemetery, San Miguel, beside his parents, about thirty people present. **[A: The Globe and Mail; NBC4; Infobae, "Por qué la familia eligió el Jardín Bella Vista"; Diario de Cuyo, 26 November 2020]** Two plaques on the tomb, "Gracias a la pelota" and "Te extrañamos"; a Puerto Madero mausoleum reportedly considered first. **[R: Diario de Cuyo, 13 December 2020, and unnamed family sources]**
- Seven medical and health staff charged with homicide by *dolo eventual*: Agustina Cosachov, Leopoldo Luque, Carlos Ángel Díaz, Nancy Edith Forlini, Ricardo Almirón, Mariano Ariel Perroni, Pedro Pablo Di Spagna. **[A]**
  - **Timeline correction, the pool was a year stale.** The first oral trial opened March 2025 in San Isidro and was **annulled in May 2025** after judge Julieta Makintach was recused over an unauthorised documentary filmed in the courtroom; she was removed from the bench on 18 November 2025 and faces criminal charges. The **retrial opened 17 March 2026** before Tribunal Oral en lo Criminal N°7, same seven accused, and is running as this is compiled. **[A: CNN en Español, 14 April 2026; Infobae, 10 May 2026; Diario Huarpe, 25 November 2025]**
- Five recognised heirs confirmed by Argentine justice in March 2021: Dalma, Gianinna, Diego Junior, Jana, Diego Fernando. Jana was recognised only after he refused DNA testing five times. **[A: COPE, 5 March 2021; ESPN Deportes]** Further paternity claims, including March 2019 reports of three unrecognised children in Cuba, remain contested. **[R: Infobae, 25 November 2020]**

## 9. Afterlife and cult

- **Stadio Diego Armando Maradona**, Naples: San Paolo renamed by unanimous council vote, 4 December 2020, nine days after his death. **[A: France 24; Infobae; Ámbito]** Argentinos Juniors had already renamed its own ground in La Paternal in 2004, for the club centenary. **[A: La Nación; es.wikipedia]**
  - **Coordinate contradiction, not resolved.** Verification produced two pins for that same La Paternal ground from the same pool: **-34.6061, -58.4725** (geography entry) and **-34.6055, -58.4776** (afterlife entry), about 470 m apart. The journey file uses the first; this line records the second.
- **Largo Maradona mural**, Via Emanuele De Deo 60, Quartieri Spagnoli: painted 1990 by Mario Filardi, restored 2016 by Salvatore Iodice, retouched 2017 by Francisco Bosoletti; a shrine, with offerings left beneath it. **[A: Napoli da Vivere; Analisi dell'Opera; Il Post]** Pool coordinates (40.8434, 14.2438) are approximate, nearer 40.842, 14.247. **[R]**
- **Bar Nilo**, Naples: a lock of Maradona's hair in a rotating glass urn among images of San Gennaro, San Giuseppe Moscati, the Pope and Pino Daniele; the owner says he took it from an airplane headrest. **[A: Palabras de Fútbol, 13 October 2025; La Tercera]** The capsule of blue liquid labelled "lacrime napolitane 1991" is **[R]**, one source only.
- **Iglesia Maradoniana**, founded 30 October 1998 in Rosario by Héctor Campomar, Alejandro Verón and Hernán Amez; its own Ten Commandments; 30 October kept as Navidad. **[A: La Razón de México; Independent Español; El Siglo de Torreón]** Membership figures are the church's own: **[R]**. The family's late-1970s house in La Paternal has been a museum, La Casa de D10S, since 2016. **[R: ESPN; Mundiario]**
