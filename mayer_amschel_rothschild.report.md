# Mayer Amschel Rothschild (1744-1812): research report

*Court factor to Hesse-Kassel, founder of M. A. Rothschild und Soehne, father of the five sons sent to five capitals.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named on the line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the literature carries without a document. Where sources disagree, both readings are printed and the disagreement left open.

**Method.** This report stands on the verification pool supplied with the assignment (English and German Wikipedia; the Juedisches Museum Frankfurt pages; Egon Caesar Corti, *The Rise of the House of Rothschild*, trans. Lunn, 1928). No independent web retrieval in this pass. Every correction note in the pool is applied; where a correction reverses an original claim, both readings are recorded.

## 1. The Judengasse and the name (1567-1756)

The name is older than the family's fame. A house built on the Judengasse in 1567 by Isaak Elchanan Bacharach carried the sign *zum roten Schild*, which became the hereditary name of those who lived under it **[A: en.wikipedia, "Rothschild family"]**. Mayer Amschel's household later occupied a different house on the same street; the sources differ on which building the family held when, and the article says so itself **[A]**.

He is born on the Judengasse in 1744, son of Amschel Moses Rothschild, currency trader and coin supplier to the Prince of Hesse, and of Schoenche nee Lechnich **[A: Wikipedia, "Mayer Amschel Rothschild"]**. The date 23 February appears in the pool's geography lens but not in its chronology lens, which gives only the year: attested by one lens. The street was walled and gated, locked at night and on Christian holidays, among the most crowded urban strips in the Empire **[A: Wikipedia, "Frankfurter Judengasse"]**. Amschel Moses dies 1755, Schoenche 1756 **[A]**. The geography lens gives smallpox as the father's cause of death; that is stated in **neither** Wikipedia article and is **[R]** only.

**Coordinate correction, load-bearing.** The pool first placed the Judengasse at 50.1095, 8.6862, wrong by roughly 500 m and outside the ghetto footprint. English Wikipedia gives 50°6'49"N 8°41'13"E = 50.1136, 8.6869 **[A: correction note]**. The journey file uses 50.114, 8.688 for the street (the pin this corpus already carries) and 50.1136, 8.687 for the family houses.

## 2. Fuerth, Hanover, the return (1755-1763)

The pool contradicts itself here and the contradiction is not resolved:

- **Reading 1** (pool, geography lens as first written): Mayer attends a Talmudic academy c. 1753-1755, and his father's 1755 death cuts the religious career short **[R]**.
- **Reading 2** (correction note, citing de.wikipedia): he attends the yeshiva of David Strauss in Fuerth *from* 1755, i.e. **after** the father's death, which is what sends him there rather than what ends it **[A]**.

The two invert the causal order. Reading 2 rests on a directly quoted German formulation and is what the journey file follows; reading 1 is recorded so the inversion stays visible. Fuerth at 49.4762, 10.9886 is sound either way.

1757: apprenticed under Jacob Wolf Oppenheimer at the banking firm of Simon Wolf Oppenheimer, Hanover, learning foreign trade and currency exchange **[A: Wikipedia]**. 1763: returns to Frankfurt **[A]**. The chronology lens says he joins "his brothers' business"; the geography lens says he takes up the late father's coin and antiquities trade. Compatible, not identical, neither documented further.

**Not established:** any kinship between the Hanover Oppenheimers and the Vienna Oppenheimer house of imperial war factors. A shared surname does not establish it and nothing in the pool does. None is asserted in the journey file.

## 3. Court agent to Hesse (1769-1785)

Through coins and medals he gains the patronage of Crown Prince Wilhelm of Hesse and receives the title of court factor, Hoffaktor, in 1769 **[A: Wikipedia, "Mayer Amschel Rothschild"]**.

**Place contradiction, flagged.** The geography lens sites the 1769 grant at Kassel (51.3127, 9.4797); the quote lens sites the surviving petition at **Hanau**, addressed to "Crown Prince William of Hanau" **[A: Corti p. 8]**. Wilhelm ruled as Count of Hanau until succeeding to Hesse-Kassel in 1785, so Hanau is the likelier seat; the journey file pins it there and keeps Kassel for the post-1785 relationship. A judgment on the pool's internal disagreement, not a documented resolution.

The petition asks to "most graciously be granted the advantage of being appointed court agent" **[A: Corti p. 8]**, in a style Corti calls one of extreme humility, and the first of an almost endless series of petitions the family sent to rulers over the following century.

Marriage to Guttle (Gutle) Schnapper, 29 August 1770 **[A]**; the geography lens adds that she was a Judengasse moneylender's daughter. Ten children follow, among them Amschel, Salomon, Nathan, Carl (Kalman) and Jakob (James) **[A]**.

Britain pays Hesse-Kassel for Hessian troops during the American war and Mayer's firm handles portions of those flows **[R: geography lens, no document cited]**; the chronology lens instead places the same kind of subsidy work after 1789 **[A: Wikipedia]**. Both are probably true of different decades; neither is documented at the level of a transaction. 1785: Wilhelm becomes Landgrave Wilhelm IX and the services expand past coin dealing into court finance **[A]**. 1801: the Hoffaktor status is formally confirmed **[A: Wikipedia, "William I, Elector of Hesse"]**.

## 4. The house on the Judengasse (1785-1796)

**House-identity contradiction, corrected in the pool.** The geography lens originally described one house, "Judengasse No. 148, known as Hinterpfann", occupied 1744-1812. That conflates two buildings **[A: de.wikipedia]**: the family first lived in the **Hinterhaus zur Pfanne, Judengasse 188**, and acquired the **Haus zum Gruenen Schild, No. 148**, between 1783 and 1787, moving in "entweder 1786 oder 1787". The German source will not choose between the two years; neither will this report.

Mayer dies in the Green Shield house in 1812; Gutle stays until her death in 1849 **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, "Frankfurter Judengasse"]**. The pool tagged the Gutle claim **[R]**; the correction retags it **[A]**, since German Wikipedia has her in the house "bis zu ihrem Tod 1849" and the English article adds that she stayed after her five sons were raised to the nobility. The retag is accepted.

1796: French artillery destroys roughly a third of the Judengasse houses; the business survives and goes on trading among the ruins **[A: Wikipedia, "Frankfurter Judengasse"]**. Whether the Green Shield house was among the damaged, the pool does not say.

## 5. The sons sent out (1798-1805)

1798: Nathan, the third son, is sent to England, settling first in Manchester, with roughly £20,000 in capital, to trade textiles **[A: Wikipedia, "Nathan Mayer Rothschild"]**. The £20,000 is repeated across the literature with no primary attestation in the pool, so **[R]** at the level of the exact sum.

The London sequence is the pool's most tangled dating problem: **1804** Stock Exchange dealing begins **[A]**; **1804** naturalization plus "establishes his banking house in London" **[as originally pooled]**, where the correction states the naturalization "is not stated in the cited Wikipedia article at all" and that the banking-house half misdates the New Court story, so **[R]**, used as no anchor; **1805** banking enterprise proper, in foreign bills and government securities **[A]**; **1809** New Court lease acquired and bullion becomes central **[A: en.wikipedia, "N M Rothschild & Sons"]**; **1811** N M Rothschild & Sons founded **[A: same]**.

The journey file pins New Court at 1809 and treats the 1811 founding as a separate fact in the same campa. The pool's "1805 at New Court" reading is rejected on the correction's authority, not silently smoothed. On coordinates: the pool gives New Court as 51.5122, -0.0894, but this corpus already carries it at **51.5133, -0.0888** from the Cecil Rhodes file, and the journey file inherits the corpus pin byte-identical; the ~120 m difference is noted, not adjudicated.

## 6. Napoleon, the Elector's fortune, the Hamburg loans (1806-1807)

October 1806: Napoleon orders Hesse-Cassel occupied, its treasuries sealed, General Lagrange installed as governor, and the House of Hesse-Cassel struck out of the list of powers **[A: verbatim order, Corti p. 44]**. Wilhelm flees; the Rothschilds are entrusted with safeguarding his fortune from French seizure **[A: Wikipedia, "William I, Elector of Hesse"]**. 1807: Hesse-Kassel is annexed into the Kingdom of Westphalia and Wilhelm goes into exile in Danish-held Holstein until 1813 **[A: same]**. That his fortune, routed through the Rothschilds, helped fund British operations rather than sitting idle is **[R]** and stays so.

Four Corti letters carry this stretch, quoted in full in the journey file where used:

- A Hamburg banker to Buderus, on Rothschild's own trip north to win Danish-loan business against rival Frankfurt firms **[A: Corti p. 35]**.
- Lawaetz to Buderus, 2 July 1806, defending him against those rivals' intrigues at the Hessian court: "the envious may say what they like against him" **[A: Corti p. 44]**.
- Buderus to the Elector, 8 March 1807, on the Wurzach debt **[A: Corti p. 54]**. Corti records that the French at Cassel had separately offered Rothschild 20 to 25 per cent of the sum to divert it to them.
- Buderus on why the operation runs through a city inside French-controlled Germany: "Frankfort is the center point of all my business" **[A: Corti p. 55]**. The correspondence used code names, the Elector as "Herr von Goldstein", Rothschild as "Arnoldi".

A fifth Lawaetz line, "Herr Rothschild always to be exceedingly prompt and businesslike and worthy of the most complete confidence", is verbatim but was pooled one page off: **Corti p. 39, not p. 40**, and Corti presents it as something Lawaetz "often declared" in conversation, not as a written assessment **[A: correction note]**. Conversational rather than epistolary, so not used as a stop quote.

## 7. The firm, the civil rights, the death (1810-1812)

1810: Mayer Amschel takes his three eldest sons into partnership, founding **M. A. Rothschild und Soehne**. 1811: he finances the purchase of civil rights for Frankfurt's Jews from the Grand Duke for 440,000 florins, with no breakdown and no document in the pool. 1811: Nathan undertakes to transfer funds to pay Wellington's troops in Portugal and Spain and to make subsidy payments to Britain's allies. 1812: James is sent to Paris to coordinate specie and bullion purchases for Nathan. **[All A: the respective Wikipedia articles.]**

**Date/event contradiction, flagged.** The chronology lens carries an entry dated **1814** describing James as the linchpin of Nathan's plan to fund Wellington's armies "during the Waterloo campaign". Waterloo was 1815. The correction offers two repairs, retag to 1815 or restate as Peninsular and Sixth Coalition subsidy work, and declines to choose; so does this report. The journey file keeps the 1811-1812 specie work and the June 1815 battle as separate stops, asserting no continuity beyond what each source says alone.

Death 19 September 1812 in the Haus zum Gruenen Schild **[A]**. He had refused offers to move his household out of the ghetto street even after wealth and the 1811 emancipation made it possible **[A: de.wikipedia]**. Buried in the Alter Juedischer Friedhof on what is now Battonnstrasse **[A]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pooled 50.1101, 8.6863 and 50.1111, 8.6894 both miss; the cemetery is at approximately **50.1125, 8.6903**. **Date correction:** the pool dated the cemetery's use from the 1270s, where German Wikipedia gives a first documentary mention of **1300** and closure in 1828. Amschel Mayer remains in Frankfurt to head the founding house **[A]**.

## 8. The five arrows: ennoblement and the five houses (1815-1825)

**The pool's worst error.** The chronology lens dated the five-arrows arms to the 1817 ennoblement; the afterlife lens dated them to 1822. The two contradict each other inside one pool. The correction resolves toward 1822: ennoblement (Edler von) in 1817, then the **hereditary Freiherr grant of 29 September 1822** from Francis I, which carried the arms **[A: en.wikipedia, "Rothschild family"; "Amschel Mayer Rothschild", "became a Freiherr (baron) in 1822"]**. A further wrinkle: the Rothschild family article has four sons ennobled in 1816 and Nathan in 1818, against a flat 1817, and the Heralds' College paperwork corroborates it from inside, having prepared **four** patents, not five.

Three Corti documents, the best material in the pool:

- Salomon Mayer Rothschild submitting the design, 1816: "fourth quarter, azure, an arm bearing five arrows (a symbol of the unity of the five brothers)" **[A: Corti p. 179]**. The approved version reduced them to four, Nathan not yet being ennobled.
- The Austrian Heralds' College, that it was "necessary to proceed with the greatest caution, particularly in the case of members of the Jewish nation" who "are not familiar with the prerogatives of nobility" **[A: Corti pp. 179-180]**. **Citation correction:** on p. 180, not p. 179, which ends mid-sentence at "The college replied that".
- On the patent's wording, "since the two brothers Meyer Amschel [sic] Rothschild and Solomon Meyer Rothschild have carried through a loan most meticulously and punctually..." **[A: Corti p. 179]**. **Attribution correction:** not Stadion reporting to Emperor Francis I, as the pool first had it, but **Stadion writing to Chancellor Ugarte**, arguing the patent should not detail the services. The ellipsis elides "the English subsidy transactions during the year 1815".

The motto is *Concordia, Integritas, Industria* **[A]**. Reading the arrows out of Psalm 127:4-5 as a blessing Mayer Amschel spoke over his sons is tradition, not a documented statement: **[R]**.

The five houses: Frankfurt under Amschel from 1812; **de Rothschild Freres**, Paris, 1817, banker to the French government by 1823; **S M von Rothschild**, Vienna, 1820; **C. M. de Rothschild & Figli**, Naples, 1821, closed 1861 with assets returned to Frankfurt; London under Nathan, whose £5 million Prussian loan of 1818 and 1825 bullion supply to the Bank of England belong here **[all A: the respective Wikipedia articles]**.

**Correction on Carl.** The pool called him the "youngest surviving branch founder". He was fourth of five, born 24 April 1788; James, born 15 May 1792, was younger and outlived him by thirteen years **[A: en.wikipedia, "Carl Mayer von Rothschild"]**. The phrase is deleted, not softened.

## 9. Afterlife: the legend, the grave, the street

**Waterloo.** Tradition holds Nathan had advance word through the family courier network and traded on it; historians dispute whether he was first to know **[R: Wikipedia]**. That he bought British bonds cheap after the battle and profited over the following two years is also **[R]**.

The legend's origin is datable, and that matters more than the legend. It was not contemporary reportage. It begins in an 1846 French pamphlet, *Histoire edifiante et curieuse de Rothschild Ier, roi des Juifs*, published under the pseudonym "Satan" by Georges Marie Mathieu-Dairnvaell **[A: en.wikipedia, citing the pamphlet]**, carried on by John Reeves's 1887 house history, Frederic Morton's mid-century popular history (**not** Frederick), and the 1934 film *The House of Rothschild* **[A]**. Brian Cathcart traced the earliest reliable news to Wellington's despatch; **date correction:** Wellington wrote it **19 June 1815** and it reached Lord Bathurst on the evening of **21 June**, the arrival Cathcart identifies **[A: correction note]**. Ferguson, in the Rothschild Archive, found the couriers likely did arrive slightly ahead of the despatch but that the short-term gain was offset by losses on wartime contracts disrupted by the peace **[A: via en.wikipedia]**.

**The apocryphal line.** "Let me issue and control a nation's money and I care not who makes its laws", attributed to Mayer Amschel since at least the early twentieth century, has no primary document behind it **[R]**. Variants circulate, sometimes attributed to Nathan or to an unnamed "international banker". Neither Corti nor Ferguson traces it to any Rothschild source. The journey file quotes it only in the frame of its 1846 origin.

**The grave.** The original stone survived Nazi-era destruction of the cemetery, was rediscovered in 1968 and re-erected in the Ehrenhain; the inscription is catalogued in the Steinheim Institute's epigraphy database **[A: de.wikipedia]**. What became of it between 1812 and 1968 is **not resolved in any source reviewed**. A gap, not a silence to fill.

**Further corrections, all from the pool's own correction notes [A].** Museum Judengasse, Battonnstrasse 47, stands over foundations uncovered in 1987 and was redesigned in 2016; its opening year, usually given as 1992, was not clearly stated in the fetched material. Rothschildpark is at 50.1179, 8.6699 in Westend, not 50.1246, 8.6584, and Rothschildallee is in Nordend; the park grew from an estate Amschel Mayer bought c. 1810-1816, sold under duress in September 1938 and renamed Woehlerpark in 1943. Nathan's funeral, **8 August 1836**, ended not in the City but at **Brady Street Cemetery, Whitechapel** (approx. 51.5232, -0.0606). Ferrieres is at 48.8197, 2.7119, not 2.7864, inaugurated 16 December 1862. The Rothschild Archive is at New Court, not 51.5136, -0.0983.

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Fuerth.** Yeshiva c. 1753-1755 cut short by the father's death, versus from 1755 entered because of it. Causal order inverted.
2. **1769 court-factor grant.** Kassel versus Hanau. Hanau chosen on Wilhelm's pre-1785 seat, not on a document.
3. **The move to the Green Shield house.** 1786 or 1787.
4. **Nathan's London house.** 1804 naturalization (unsourced), 1805 enterprise, 1809 lease, 1811 firm.
5. **The five arrows.** 1817 versus 1822, internal to the pool. Resolved toward 1822, not erased.
6. **The ennoblement year.** 1816 for four sons, 1818 for Nathan, against a flat 1817.
7. **The 1814 Waterloo entry.** Date and event cannot both be right; neither repair is adopted.
8. **New Court's coordinates.** 51.5122, -0.0894 (pool) versus 51.5133, -0.0888 (corpus pin).
9. **The Hessian subsidies.** 1776-1783 by one lens, after 1789 by the other.

## Gaps

- The transition from coin dealing to the Hofjude role, never checked against a primary source. The pool says so itself.
- The gravestone's fate, 1812-1968.
- Whether the Green Shield house was among the third of the Judengasse burnt in 1796, and Museum Judengasse's exact opening year.
- The £20,000 sent with Nathan to Manchester: repeated everywhere, attested nowhere in the pool.
- The 440,000-florin purchase of 1811: no breakdown, no document, no counterparty beyond "the Grand Duke".
- Gutle Schnapper's own life beyond the house she would not leave. The sources treat her as an address.
- Any Rothschild-side document on the Waterloo trades. Ferguson saw the archive; this pass did not.
