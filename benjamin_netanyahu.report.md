# Benjamin Netanyahu: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject living (b. 21 October 1949). Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, contested, or single-source. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.*

A note on the shape of this file. The subject is alive, in office, indicted at home and under an international arrest warrant abroad. The verification pass returned an unusual density of corrections: wrong coordinates, wrong dates, wrong signatories, and in three cases two lenses of one pool disagreeing about one event. Those disagreements are reported as disagreements.

---

## 1. The historian's house (1949-1963)

Born in Tel Aviv, 21 October 1949, to **Benzion Netanyahu** and **Tzila Segal** **[A: Wikipedia, "Benjamin Netanyahu"]**. Second of three sons: Yonatan (b. 1946), Benjamin, Iddo (b. 1952).

Benzion Netanyahu was a historian of the Spanish Inquisition and had been secretary to Vladimir Jabotinsky; his life's argument was that the expulsion of the Jews from Spain was racial in motive before it was religious. **[A: Pfeffer, *Bibi: The Turbulent Life and Times of Benjamin Netanyahu* (2018)]**

Early childhood in **Jerusalem**, attending the **Henrietta Szold Elementary School** **[A: Wikipedia, "Benjamin Netanyahu"]**.

**Contradiction / correction flagged.** The geography lens placed the childhood home in **Talpiot** at 31.758 / 35.227 and had the father's career pulling the family abroad in the early 1950s. Neither holds. Wikipedia names the city and the school and no neighbourhood; Benzion taught at **Dropsie College, Philadelphia, 1957 to 1968**, and the family move dates to **1963**. Talpiot is **[R]** at best and is dropped; the journey uses the canonical Jerusalem coordinate.

**Gymnasia Leyada (Hebrew University Secondary School), Jerusalem**. He studied there but **did not graduate**, because of the family's relocation to the United States **[A: Wikipedia, "Hebrew University Secondary School"]**.

**Contradiction flagged.** The geography lens dated Leyada to **1967-1968**, i.e. *after* Cheltenham, with him completing secondary studies there before enlistment. That inverts the chronology and the school's own article contradicts it. The journey places Leyada before 1963. The pool's coordinate was also ~1.4 km off: the school is **31.78056 / 35.19722**, beside the Givat Ram campus.

## 2. Cheltenham (1963-1967)

Family relocates to the Philadelphia area; he attends **Cheltenham High School**, graduating with the class of 1967, and is later named to the school's hall of fame **[A: Wikipedia, "Cheltenham High School"; Pfeffer (2018)]**.

**Correction.** The pool's 40.0768 / -75.1141 lands in eastern Cheltenham Township, ~4 km from the campus. The school is at 500 Rices Mill Road, **Wyncote**, PA: **40.087 / -75.160** **[A: Wikipedia, "Cheltenham High School"]**.

## 3. Sayeret Matkal (1967-1972)

Returns to Israel and enlists; selected for **Sayeret Matkal**, the General Staff Reconnaissance Unit **[A: Wikipedia, "Benjamin Netanyahu"]**. No reachable source names his unit's base, so the journey pins enlistment to the Kirya compound in Tel Aviv and says so in the confidence field. **Gap stated as a gap.**

**Battle of Karameh**, March 1968, against Jordanian and PLO forces at Karameh in the Jordan Valley **[A: Wikipedia, "Benjamin Netanyahu"]**.

**Sabena Flight 571**, hijacked 8 May 1972, stormed at **Lod Airport** on 9 May 1972 in Operation Isotope; Netanyahu is wounded **[A]**.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Two accounts of the wound stand in the same encyclopedia. "Benjamin Netanyahu": **shot in the shoulder**, implying hostile fire. "Sabena Flight 571": commando **Marko Ashkenazi** fired accidentally while striking the hijacker **Theresa Halsa**, the round passing through her into **Netanyahu's biceps**. The verification pass judged the friendly-fire arm wound better sourced. The journey carries both.

**Discharge**, 1972, with the rank of *seren* (captain) **[A]**. Marries **Miriam Weizmann**, 1972 **[A]**; divorced 1978 **[A]**.

**Correction.** The chronology lens dated the discharge to **October 1973**, attached to the Yom Kippur War entry. He was discharged in 1972, returned to the United States in late 1972 for MIT, and was **recalled** in October 1973. The journey follows that split.

## 4. Ben Nitay (1972-1979)

**MIT**, Cambridge, under the name **Ben Nitay**: bachelor's in architecture **February 1975**, master's from the **Sloan School of Management, 1976** **[A: Wikipedia, "Benjamin Netanyahu"; MIT alumni records]**.

**Yom Kippur War**, October 1973: recalled from the United States, fights on the Egyptian front **[A: Netanyahu, *Bibi: My Story* (2022); Pfeffer (2018)]**, located by the pool at the **Chinese Farm**. **Correction:** the pool's 30.6 / 32.35 is ~17 km north of the fighting; the battlefield is **30.45 / 32.4**, east of the Canal, ~6 km south of the Deversoir crossing **[A: Wikipedia, "Battle of the Chinese Farm"]**.

**Operation Spring of Youth, Beirut, 9-10 April 1973, DOWNGRADED TO [R] AND OMITTED.** The pool asserted his participation at **[A]**. Against it: Wikipedia's article on the operation lists the Israeli commanders (Barak, Lipkin-Shahak, others) without him; his own biography lists Karameh, the 1968 Lebanon raid and Sabena but not this, and has him discharged in 1972 and in the United States from late 1972. The only support is his memoir, and the date falls inside his MIT residency.

**Entebbe**, 4 July 1976: brother **Yonatan Netanyahu** killed leading the ground force of the hostage-rescue raid **[A]**. Benjamin was not present **[A: the pool's own note]**.

**Boston Consulting Group**, Boston, 1976-1978, economic consultant, colleague of **Mitt Romney** **[A: Wikipedia; Pfeffer (2018)]**.

**Jonathan Institute**, founded **1979**, first conference Jerusalem July 1979, second Washington June 1984 **[A: Wikipedia, "Yonatan Netanyahu"]**.

**Contradiction flagged.** The chronology lens dates the founding to **1978**, the geography lens to **1979**: two lenses of one pool disagreeing. The journey carries 1979, supported by the Yonatan Netanyahu article, and names the disagreement.

## 5. Embassy and mission (1981-1988)

Marries **Fleur Cates**, 1981 **[A]**; divorced 1988 **[A]**.

**Deputy Chief of Mission, Israeli Embassy, Washington, D.C., 1982-1984**, under Ambassador **Moshe Arens** **[A]**. **Correction:** the embassy is at 3514 International Drive NW, **38.9427 / -77.0684**, occupied since December 1980 and so the right building for this posting; the pool's 38.926 / -77.047 points at Woodley Park **[A: Wikipedia, "Embassy of Israel, Washington, D.C."]**.

**Permanent Representative to the United Nations, 1984-1988**, seventh holder of the post **[A: Wikipedia, "Permanent Mission of Israel to the United Nations"]**. **Correction:** the mission is at 800 Second Avenue, ~**40.7495 / -73.9718**; the pool's coordinate was ~0.5 km northeast.

## 6. Likud, and the first premiership (1988-1999)

- Returns to Israel, joins **Likud**, elected to the **12th Knesset**, 1988 **[A]**; Deputy Foreign Minister, and in the delegation to the **Madrid Conference**, 1991 **[A]**. Marries **Sara Ben-Artzi**, 1991 **[A]**.
- **Chairman of Likud**, 24 March 1993, defeating Benny Begin and David Levy **[A]**.
- Wins the **first direct popular-vote election for prime minister**, 29 May 1996, over Shimon Peres; sworn in 18 June 1996 **[A]**.
- First meeting with **Yasser Arafat**, **Erez crossing**, 4 September 1996 **[A]**.
- **Western Wall Tunnel** exit opened, 24 September 1996; days of deadly riots follow **[A: contemporary AP and Reuters reporting]**.

### The Hebron Protocol: a three-way date contradiction

| Source in pool | Date given |
|---|---|
| chronology lens | 14 January 1997 |
| geography lens | 15 January 1997 |
| verification note, citing Wikipedia "Hebron Protocol" | **17 January 1997** |

All three cannot stand. The sequence behind the confusion: Netanyahu and Arafat concluded the deal in the early hours of **15 January**; the Knesset approved it **16 January**, 87 to 17, and redeployment began that day; the protocol was **signed 17 January** by **General Dan Shomron** and **Saeb Erekat**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Hebron Protocol"]**

**Second correction, larger than the date.** Both lenses say **Netanyahu signed it**. He did not. The journey states this.

**Khaled Mashal affair**, Amman, September 1997: a failed Mossad poisoning; Israel supplies the antidote and releases **Sheikh Ahmed Yassin** **[A]**. **Correction:** the geography lens has Netanyahu meeting **King Hussein at the royal palace** over this. Not supported. Mossad chief **Danny Yatom** flew to Jordan with the antidote, with Netanyahu's consent; "Khaled Mashal" records no Netanyahu trip. The journey recasts the stop around Yatom, palace meeting **[R]**.

**Wye River Memorandum**, 1998. Talks ran **15-23 October 1998** at the Aspen Institute Wye River Conference Centers near **Wye Mills, Maryland**; the memorandum was **signed at the White House** by Netanyahu and Arafat on 23 October, Clinton witnessing **[A: Wikipedia, "Wye River Memorandum"]**. The pool located the signing at Wye River, with coordinates kilometres off the campus besides. The journey moves it to the White House.

Knesset approval, **17 November 1998** **[A]**.

**Loses office, 1999.** **Correction, roughly seven weeks:** the election was **17 May 1999** (Barak 1,791,020, 56.08%; Netanyahu 1,402,474, 43.92%). The chronology lens gives **6 July 1999**, the date Barak formed the 28th government and Netanyahu's term formally ended. Two facts, conflated. **[A: Wikipedia, "1999 Israeli general election"]**

**Ofra recording, 2001 [R].** A tape of Netanyahu speaking to a settler family, surfacing in Israeli media in 2010, carries the line quoted in the journey. Hebrew original; the circulating English wording could **not** be confirmed against a fetched primary transcript (Guardian and Haaretz pages unreachable).

- **Testimony to the U.S. House Government Reform Committee for the invasion of Iraq, 12 September 2002 [R in the pool's own tagging]**.
- **Foreign Minister** under Ariel Sharon, 2002; unsuccessful challenge for the Likud leadership **[A]**.
- **Finance Minister**, 2003: tax cuts, privatizations, welfare reductions **[A]**.
- **Resigns, 7 August 2005**, over the Gaza disengagement **[A]**; **retakes the Likud leadership, 20 December 2005** **[A]**.
- Designated by President **Shimon Peres** to form a government, 20 February 2009; **second government approved 31 March 2009** **[A]**.
- **Bar-Ilan speech**, 14 June 2009: conditional endorsement of a demilitarized Palestinian state **[A: PMO official transcript]**.

## 8. The long premiership (2009-2021)

- **Sharm el-Sheikh direct talks, September 2010 [A]**. **Correction:** the pool also listed **September 1999** at Sharm. That was the memorandum of 4 September 1999, **signed by Ehud Barak**, not Netanyahu, who had left office on 6 July. Dropped.
- **UN General Assembly, 27 September 2012 [A]**: the drawn bomb and the red line. Verbatim quotes confirmed from the Wikisource-hosted transcript.
- **Beijing, Great Hall of the People, May 2013 [A]**. **Correction:** the pool's coordinate landed in Tiananmen Square; the building is **39.90333 / 116.3875**.
- **Auschwitz-Birkenau [R], omitted.** "Israel-Poland relations" lists a 2013 prime-ministerial visit to Poland, consistent with the reported June 2013 Auschwitz visit, but no primary record was retrieved (search budget exhausted). The pool's 50.0359 / 19.1783 is in fact exact.
- **Joint session of Congress, 3 March 2015 [A]**: four verbatim quotes confirmed from the PMO transcript hosted on Wikisource.
- **Moscow, Kremlin, repeated visits 2015-2020 [A]**, coordinating with Putin over Syria. No single visit is dated in the pool; the journey dates one and says so. **New Delhi, January 2018 [A]** and **Davos [A]** are attested and omitted for stop budget, not for doubt.
- **US recognition of Jerusalem as Israel's capital, December 2017 [A]**.
- **Longest-serving prime minister**, surpassing Ben-Gurion's cumulative tenure, **July 2019 [A]**.
- **Indicted 21 November 2019**; trial opens **24 May 2020** **[A: Wikipedia, "Trial of Benjamin Netanyahu"]**. Cases 1000 and 2000: fraud and breach of trust. Case 4000 adds **bribery**.
- **Abraham Accords signed at the White House, 15 September 2020 [A]**. No confirmed personal visit to **Manama** was found; the pool tags that **[R]** and the journey does not pin it.
- **Removed from office 13 June 2021** as Naftali Bennett forms a coalition **[A]**.

### Two addresses the pool gets wrong twice over

**Jerusalem District Court.** Geography lens: 31.7857 / 35.2007, west Jerusalem. Afterlife lens: 31.7745 / 35.2249. The two lenses disagree with each other and neither matches the court, which is on Salah ad-Din Street in East Jerusalem, ~**31.7838 / 35.2318** **[A: Wikipedia, "Jerusalem District Court"]**.

**Beit Aghion.** The official residence is at **9 Smolenskin Street** on the corner of Balfour Street, in **Rehavia**, **31.7734505 / 35.2177691**. The afterlife lens says **Talbiya**; the geography lens has the coordinate ~0.35 km off. The 2020-2023 Balfour protest history is correct in both **[A: Wikipedia, "Beit Aghion"]**.

## 9. Return, overhaul, war (2022-present)

- **Returns to power 29 December 2022** after the 2022 Knesset election **[A]**.
- **Judicial overhaul advanced, 2023**, triggering sustained mass protests **[A]**, whose iconography (effigies, masks, the "crime minister" label) is attested in photojournalism of the cycle **[A]**.
- **7 October 2023**: Hamas attacks from Gaza; the government declares war **[A]**. The PMO statement of that morning is quoted verbatim in the journey.
- **ICC arrest warrant, 21 November 2024**, Pre-Trial Chamber I: war crimes of starvation as a method of warfare and of intentionally directing attacks against civilians, plus crimes against humanity of murder, persecution and other inhumane acts, for conduct from at least 8 October 2023 to at least 20 May 2024 **[A: ICC press release]**. Israel and the United States reject the court's jurisdiction; the warrant is contested and largely unenforced, and that contest is stated, not resolved. **Correction:** two wrong ICC coordinates appear across the pool (52.0345/4.3568, 52.0345/4.3559); the headquarters is at **52.10556 / 4.31778**.

## 10. The afterlife section, which does not exist

The brief's afterlife lens presupposes a dead subject: tomb, posthumous cult, later legend. **Benjamin Netanyahu is alive.** There is no tomb and no posthumous legend to document. That is a structural gap in the request, reported as a gap rather than filled. What exists in its place:
- **Beit Aghion and Balfour Street** as a protest address, 2020-2023 **[A]**, and the nickname **Bibi** as personal iconography across Israeli press and Likud campaign material for decades **[A: general pattern, no single dated event]**.
- **The memoir *Bibi: My Story***. **Correction:** the afterlife lens gives November 2022 and a Hebrew edition that "followed". Both wrong. Marketing of both began **August 2022**, official release moved up to **October 2022**; Hebrew (Sella Meir, 592 pp) and English (Threshold Editions, 736 pp) ran in parallel **[A: Wikipedia, "Bibi: My Story"]**.
- **His father's grave.** Benzion Netanyahu died 30 April 2012 at his Jerusalem home, aged 102, and "was laid to rest in Jerusalem" **[A]**; the attribution to **Har HaMenuchot** is **[R]**, unsourced in the reachable article, and the pool's coordinate for it was ~1.1 km off (correct: 31.79806 / 35.17778). Listed only to preempt conflation with the subject.
- **Street murals and graffiti [R]**: the phenomenon is attested piecemeal in photo features; no comprehensive catalogue was located.
