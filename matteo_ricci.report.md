# Matteo Ricci — Macerata to Peking (1552-1610): journey report

**Shape:** 41 stops in 8 named segments, gregorian calendar, register "the canon is true." Canon here = Ricci's own letters and journals (as preserved in Trigault's *De Christiana expeditione apud Sinas* and Gallagher's translation *China in the Sixteenth Century*), plus the well-attested Jesuit and Chinese record of the mission. Nothing in this life is legendary in the way Joan's voices or Aeneas's shades are; the "canon is true" register here means the strategy is narrated as Ricci himself understood and defended it — the disguise, the gifts, the map's shifted meridian — rather than debunked as mere expedience.

## Sources
- **Wikipedia, MacTutor, Britannica, BDCC (Biographical Dictionary of Chinese Christianity), Catholic Encyclopedia (New Advent), New World Encyclopedia, Grokipedia** — the spine chronology: Macerata, Rome, Collegio Romano, the 1578 voyage, Zhaoqing through Peking, death 1610.
- **Oxford Academic, *A Jesuit in the Forbidden City: Matteo Ricci 1552-1610*** (Hsia) — Shaozhou chapter: the Qu Taisu friendship, the 1592 robbery, the dress change.
- **Jonathan D. Spence, *The Memory Palace of Matteo Ricci*; ResearchGate, "Matteo Ricci's Occidental Method of Memory (Xiguo Jifa)"** — the 1596 memory treatise and its 1595 letter, quoted directly.
- **Billings (trans.), *On Friendship: One Hundred Maxims for a Chinese Prince*; Macalester Digital Commons** — the 1595 Jiaoyou Lun for Prince Jian'an, including the "second self" maxim.
- **Wikipedia, *Ricci's world map* (Kunyu Wanguo Quantu)** — the 1584 and 1602 maps, the shifted meridian, the annotation quoted at the 1602 stop.
- **Korea Science journal; Cambridge Core, *Journal of Chinese History*** — the Euclid translation (Jihe Yuanben, 1607) with Xu Guangqi.
- **silkqin.com, "Matteo Ricci: Xiqin Quyi"; Slate, "History of the piano in China"** — the 1601 clavichord gift and the 1608 songbook for the eunuchs.
- **riccimac.org, "Matteo Ricci's demise"; Beijing Administration of Cultural Heritage (BAI), "Cemetery of Matteo Ricci"; Wikipedia, *Zhalan Cemetery*** — the death, the burial petition, and the imperial grant of earth.

## Judgment calls
- **Many precise days are inferred or traditional**, not attested: the Macerata school entry (1561), the Sapienza law study (1568), the Collegio Romano mathematics years, the Meiling Pass crossing, and the Cape of Good Hope stop are all placed on plausible dates within well-attested years, since the sources give years or seasons but not calendar days.
- **The Cape of Good Hope stop** has no fixed "site" in the ordinary sense — I used the actual geographic point the fleet rounds (34.36°S), the one moment in the Italy-to-Goa passage where the canon's own sources (scurvy, storms, the "graveyard route") are richest, rather than skip five months of the crossing in silence.
- **The 1591 Cochin ordination date** (25 July) is given as "traditional" since sources agree on the year 1580 and the season but differ slightly on the exact day.
- **Beijing recurs as five different coordinate-pairs** across the atlas (Forbidden City for court events, the Xuanwumen residence/Nantang site for the mission's daily life, Zhalan for the grave) rather than one generic pin, since the canon itself distinguishes the throne room Ricci was never personally received in from the house he actually lived and died in.
- **Two quotes were deliberately dropped** during drafting once I could not verify them as genuinely verbatim rather than a secondary source's paraphrase (Qu Taisu's advice on dress; a line on geometric rigor at the Euclid stop): both are honored only in the campa prose, with `quote: null`, per the rule that null is more honest than an invented citation.
- **No "living traveler ends at the present" issue applies** — Ricci died in 1610, so the journey properly closes at the burial, not with an epilogue into later centuries.

## Gaps and time-folds
- The **1598 Beijing attempt** and the **1600 Tianjin detention by the eunuch Ma Tang** are compressed relative to their real length (six months at Tianjin) to keep the segment's pace; the canon itself treats this as one long chokepoint before the 1601 entry, so the fold is faithful to how the mission's own letters narrate it — one continuous frustration, not three separate crises.
- **Xu Guangqi's baptism location** is disputed between Nanjing and Peking in the sources I could reach; I placed it at Beijing (1603) to match the run of works that follow it there, flagging the uncertainty in the campa text itself ("sources place it either").
- The **Kunyu Wanguo Quantu's decision to shift the prime meridian** so China reads nearer the map's center is sometimes told as pure cartographic tact and sometimes as a half-legend of Chinese vanity Ricci had to flatter; I kept it descriptive and undebunked, as the prompt's register requires.

## The five richest episodes
1. **The robe of the literati (Shaozhou, 1595)** — the whole mission's method turns on this one wardrobe change: bonze to Confucian scholar, on Qu Taisu's advice, the hinge on which everything from Nanchang to the Dragon Throne pivots.
2. **The memory palace for Governor Lu (Nanchang, 1596)** — Ricci's own verbatim letter, "Chinese characters were, as it were, invented for this art of memory," and the origin of the treatise Spence's book is named for.
3. **The clocks laid before the Dragon Throne (Beijing, 1601)** — an emperor who never once grants a personal audience in nine years, won over entirely by a striking clock and a keyboard four eunuchs cannot learn to play.
4. **The Euclid dictated through a Peking winter (1607)** — Clavius's Roman classroom of the 1570s, delivered whole into Chinese forty years later, with Xu Guangqi coining the very words for point and line that Chinese mathematics still uses.
5. **The petition for a grave inside the Middle Kingdom (1610)** — an unprecedented request granted on a disgraced eunuch's confiscated garden, the one honor the emperor extends only after the man himself is already dead.

## Connections to the atlas
- **kircher.journey.json** — the letters and maps Jesuits like Ricci sent home from China are the raw ore Athanasius Kircher smelts, a half-century later and without ever leaving Rome, into *China Illustrata* (1667); the shared thread is noted explicitly at the 1602 Kunyu Wanguo Quantu stop.
- **marco_polo.journey.json** — the other Italian who crossed into China, three centuries earlier and by a wholly different road (Mongol court favor rather than Jesuit disguise); flagged at the Macau stop as the atlas's other pin on this same coastline.
- **giulio_camillo.journey.json / giordano_bruno.journey.json** (named by the curator, not yet inscribed in this directory) — the Western ars memoriae, the theater of images and the wheel of loci, is exactly the discipline Ricci carries to Nanchang and repackages as the Xiguo Jifa; the memory-palace stop names both as the tradition's other end, ready to link once those journeys exist.
- More generally, Ricci belongs beside the atlas's other **disguise-and-translation missionaries and go-betweens** (the Jesuit thread running through Kircher) and its **cartographers of the whole earth** (Marco Polo's Book of Marvels, Kircher's *Mundus Subterraneus*) — a life spent making one civilization legible to another without ever pretending the distance wasn't real.

### Errors found and fixed

1. **Burial year wrong by more than a year.** The dataset closed with "laid in the earth at Zhalan"
   on 1610-10-01. Ricci died 11 May 1610, but the ground was not granted until later that year and
   the body was not interred until **November 1611**; the funeral procession was **22 April 1611**,
   after which the coffin waited months in the cemetery chapel while the vault was finished
   (Wikipedia, *Zhalan Cemetery*; corroborated by the Jesuit-sites listing dating the cemetery
   1611-1773). The closing sequence was rebuilt as four honest stops: the petition (1610-05-15,
   downgraded to `inferred`), **the grant of the ground beyond Fuchengmen** (1610-12-01, `inferred`,
   NEW), **the funeral procession from Xuanwumen** (1611-04-22, `attested`, NEW), and the interment
   (1611-11-01, `inferred`). `years` left at "1552-1610": the sibling convention is that the field
   carries the traveler's lifespan and posthumous stops run past it (cf. bernard_clairvaux 1090-1153
   with a 1174 stop, braille 1809-1852 with a 1952 stop).

2. **Xu Guangqi baptized in the wrong city.** The stop placed the 1603 baptism in Beijing at Ricci's
   own residence. Xu was baptized **Paul at the Nanjing residence, by Fr. Joao da Rocha, with Ricci
   absent in Peking** (BDCC, *Xu Guangqi*; Wikipedia, *Xu Guangqi*). Stop renamed "Nanjing, Xu
   Guangqi is baptized Paul", moved to Nanjing coordinates, campa rewritten so Ricci learns of it by
   letter, confidence dropped to `inferred` (the year is firm, the month is not). It stays inside the
   Beijing segment, which the file already does elsewhere ("Beijing, the shut gate" sits in the
   Nanchang/Nanjing segment).

3. **Nanjing debate dated before Ricci was in Nanjing.** The Xuelang Hong'en debate was set at
   1599-01-15 and the campa said "settled now in Nanjing itself"; the Catholic Encyclopedia gives his
   return to Nanjing as **6 February 1599**. Moved to 1599-05-01 / `inferred` (the year is canon, the
   day is not) and the campa reopened accordingly. The tail sentence claiming he first met Xu Guangqi
   "this same winter" was cut: Xu sought Ricci out in Nanjing in **March or April 1600**, which the
   next stop already carries, so the sentence was both duplicated and a year early.

4. **Quote nulled as uncarried.** "It has seemed to me that the Chinese characters were, as it were,
   invented for this art of memory," sourced to a letter of 29 August 1595, could not be confirmed as
   verbatim anywhere; its own stated date also contradicted the stop's year (1596). Quote and
   quote_source set to null; the sense survives in the campa as plain paraphrase. The stop date moved
   from the borrowed letter-date 1596-08-29 to 1596-08-01 / `inferred`.

5. **Quote restored to the canon's wording.** The Jiaoyou Lun maxim was truncated and altered
   ("not another"). Restored to Billings' full rendering: "My friend is not an other, but half of
   myself, and thus a second me — I must therefore regard my friend as myself."

6. **Quote confirmed as carried.** The Kunyu Wanguo Quantu annotation ("testimony to the supreme
   goodness, greatness and unity of Him who controls heaven and earth") is quoted in exactly that
   wording on the Wikipedia article for the map. Kept unchanged.

### Smaller canon corrections inside the prose

- **Sant'Andrea anachronism.** Ricci entered the novitiate on the Quirinal on 15 August 1571; Bernini's
  Sant'Andrea was not begun until 1658. The campa now says he enters "the Society's novitiate on the
  Quirinal hill, the ground where Bernini's Sant'Andrea will rise a century after him."
- **The Peking house.** The 1601 stop had Ricci establishing the Xuanwumen residence on arrival; the
  Nantang foundation dates to **1605**. Campa now separates the 1601 stipend and leave-to-remain from
  the house bought four years later. (The 1602-1610 Beijing stops still carry the Xuanwumen coordinate
  as the mission's settled address; that is a deliberate simplification, not a claim about 1602.)
- **Residence count.** "six residences across the empire" at 1608 corrected to **four** (BDCC: Ricci
  "founded four Jesuit houses in China" — Shaozhou, Nanchang, Nanjing, Peking).
- **Memoir start.** 1609-01-01 dropped from `attested` to `inferred`; the year Ricci began the history
  at Acquaviva's request is not firmly fixed (1608 is as commonly given).

### Not changed

- `register` kept as "national mythology, the canon is true" — the comma form is the plurality across
  the directory (62 files vs 59 colon, 19 em-dash), so this matches convention.
- The mythic material stands untouched: the father's providential fever on the road to Rome, the
  robbers at Shaozhou, Ma Tang's greed broken by a clock only Ricci can wind, the emperor who keeps
  the man nine years and never once looks at him. None of these were debunked; where the canon is
  uncertain the confidence field carries the doubt, not the prose.
- 43 stops sits inside the 30-45 target with the two additions; no further padding.

### Residual uncertainties, flagged not fixed

- The Xuelang Hong'en debate is firmly 1599 but I could not pin the month; 1599-05-01 is a placement
  after his 6 February return, marked `inferred`.
- The exact date of the Wanli edict granting the Fuchengmen ground is not preserved; 1610-12-01 is a
  reasoned placement between the May petition and the April 1611 funeral, marked `inferred` and said
  aloud in the campa.
- Only 2 quotes survive verification. Search engines (WebSearch budget exhausted, DuckDuckGo
  CAPTCHA-walled) blocked further hunting for a third carried quote; a later pass with access to
