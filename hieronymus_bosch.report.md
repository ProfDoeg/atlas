# Hieronymus Bosch (Jheronimus van Aken), c. 1450 to 1516

*Research report for the atlas journey `hieronymus_bosch`. 44 stops in 8 segments; calendar julian (the life ends 66 years before the Gregorian reform, and every posthumous date after 1582 is given as the sources give it).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in brackets. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including anything resting on an argument from silence. Contradictions between the three research lenses (chronology, geography, afterlife) are flagged where they occur and **not** adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named at the end with the reason.

A warning governing the whole file: **Bosch dated no painting and signed only some of them.** Every date attached to a work here is an estimate from dendrochronology, costume, donor heraldry, or style. Where a research lens produced a bare year for a painting, that year was converted into a range or moved into the journey's `date_confidence` field, never into the prose.

---

## 1. Origins: a family of painters, and a name from a city nobody visited

- The surname **van Aken** means "from Aachen" **[R, genealogical tradition relayed in Koldeweij, Vandenbroeck and Vermet, *Hieronymus Bosch: The Complete Paintings and Drawings* (2001)]**. No document places any member of the family inside Aachen and no date attaches to a move west. The painter never signs with it; his panels read *Jheronimus Bosch*, after the town.
- **Nijmegen**: forefathers are placed on the Waal by genealogical reconstruction **[R]**. *Correction:* the geography lens gave the Nijmegen working life to the grandfather **Jan van Aken**; the sources give Jan only as a painter first mentioned in 1430, and the Nijmegen link belongs to earlier ancestors. Its coordinates also drifted about a kilometre (51.8425, 5.8528 used here).
- **Jan van Aken first mentioned in the 's-Hertogenbosch records in 1430 [A]**, **dies 1454 [A]**; no work survives under his name. The father, **Anthonius van Aken**, was **artistic adviser to the Illustrious Brotherhood of Our Lady [A]** and **died c. 1478 [R: approximate year only]**.
- **Birth: c. 1450, in his grandfather's house [R]**. The sources say outright that the birth date "has not been determined with certainty"; the year is worked backward from a portrait drawing made shortly before his death. No baptismal record.
  - *Correction:* the geography lens asserted that the Markt house "remains the center of his life for the next six decades." He was born in his **grandfather's** house; the Markt house **Inden Salvatoer** came to him only through marriage around 1480; and the couple then moved to Oirschot. Two houses, not one, and not six continuous decades.
- **Baptism at Sint-Jan [R]**: very likely, given the family's documented work on the church fabric, but no parish register of the 1450s proves it **[Gibson, *Hieronymus Bosch* (1973)]**.
  - *Coordinate correction:* the geography lens placed Sint-Janskathedraal at 51.6841, 5.3057, roughly 450 m south of the building. It is at **51.68806, 5.30750**; the journey uses 51.6881, 5.3075 for all three Sint-Jan stops, each of which carried the same error.
- **The fire of 1463 [A for the fire, R for the witnessing]**. Roughly four thousand houses burned **[municipal chronicle of 's-Hertogenbosch]**. That Bosch, then about thirteen, saw it is hedged in Dutch sources as *mogelijk*, possibly; the chronology lens's **[A]** is demoted to **[R]**. The further step, from the fire to the burning cities in his hells, is scholarly conjecture **[R, per Larry Silver, *Hieronymus Bosch* (2006)]**, and the journey says so.

## 2. The documented adult: 1474 to 1486

- **5 April 1474: Jheronimus van Aken is named for the first time in the municipal records, alongside two brothers and a sister [A, municipal records of 's-Hertogenbosch]**. This is the earliest document that is certainly about him.
- **Death of the father and inheritance of the workshop, c. 1478 [R]**. *Correction:* the geography lens tagged this **[A]** and specified an inheritance of "tools, patterns, and reputation." The date is approximate everywhere and no probate document was reachable; the journey marks the inheritance as inference from ordinary practice.
- **Marriage to Aleid Goyaerts van den Meervenne [A for the marriage, R for the year]**. *Contradiction flagged:* the chronology lens gave a flat **1480** tagged **[A]**; the sources say "sometime between 1479 and 1481." The journey pins 1480 with the range in `date_confidence`. The marriage brought the stone house **Inden Salvatoer** on the Markt and property at Oirschot **[A, notarial records cited in Gibson (1973)]**.
- **Oirschot [A that they moved, R for the detail]**. *Correction:* the geography lens called this biographers' speculation, the chronology lens said the couple lived there "partly." The sources state flatly that they moved to Oirschot, where Aleid had inherited a house and land. The name **Sint-Anna estate** is **unverified** and dropped.
- **Erasmus of Rotterdam at the Brethren of the Common Life school here, c. 1484 to 1487 [A that he attended, R for any Bosch connection]**. The chronology lens dated it 1483; the sources give around 1484, ranging to 1487. **No contact between Erasmus and Bosch is documented**, so this was **cut from the journey** rather than pinned, and is kept only as context for the town's religious climate.

## 3. The Brotherhood of Our Lady

- **Membership: 1486/87 [A for the fact, contested for the year]**. The sharpest internal contradiction the research produced: the chronology lens gave **1487**, the afterlife lens **1486**, the geography lens **1486/88**. The sources give **1486/7**, and the **"/88" variant has no source at all**. The journey pins 1486 and names the three-way disagreement in `date_confidence`. Not silently resolved.
- The **Illustrious Brotherhood of Our Blessed Lady** was founded in **1318 [A, Wikipedia 'Brotherhood of Our Lady']**; roughly forty sworn members, thousands of outer members across Europe, centred on a wooden Marian image in Saint John's. Sworn members were called **swan brethren**, for the swans donated to the annual banquet **[A, same]**.
- **The Brotherhood's account books are the single richest documentary source on Bosch's life [A]**. **They were not directly consulted for this file**, only through Gibson (1973) and Silver (2006). That is an honest limit on everything in sections 3 and 5.
- **The retable commission, c. 1488 to 1493 [R]**. The geography lens tagged this **[A]** on the authority of those same books; since they were not reachable, **demoted to [R]**. The object was later replaced and its fate is untraced.

## 4. The paintings

None of these dates is documented. All are estimates, and the journey says so at every stop.

- **Adoration of the Magi triptych, c. 1494 [R]**. *Correction:* the geography lens put the donors Peeter Scheyfve and Agneese de Gramme on the **outer** wings "when closed." They kneel on the **inner** wings; the closed exterior shows a grisaille **Mass of Saint Gregory**. The heraldic identification is itself contested **[Koldeweij et al. (2001)]**.
- **The Garden of Earthly Delights, c. 1500 [R]**, placed between the middle and late periods. **No contract, payment record, or letter naming a patron survives.** The closed exterior carries the painting's own inscription, Vulgate Psalm 33:9, used as the quote at that stop: the only text in this file literally by Bosch's hand.
- **The Temptation of Saint Anthony (Lisbon), c. 1500 to 1510 [R]**. *Contradiction flagged:* the chronology lens gave **1505**, the afterlife lens **"roughly 1495 to 1500,"** and Wikipedia places the related Prado Temptation in the late production, 1500 to 1516. The journey pins 1505 and states the range and the disagreement in `date_confidence`; the prose asserts no single year.
- **Philip the Handsome's Last Judgement, 1504 [A, Habsburg-Burgundian court accounts]**. The commission is solid. *Corrections:* the day **9 September 1504** given by the geography lens **could not be verified anywhere reachable** and is dropped; and the event is a **Brussels court document**, not something sited at a Markt workshop, so the journey pins it in Brussels.
- **The Haywain Triptych, c. 1516 [A, dendrochronological]**. The strongest date attached to any Bosch painting here.
- **No record of travel, c. 1480 to 1516 [R]**. The geography lens tagged this **[A]** and wrote that "tax rolls, property records, and guild entries all locate him in 's-Hertogenbosch." That is an **argument from silence**, and it is strained by the Oirschot move. Demoted to **[R]** and narrowed in the journey to: the registers that name him name him in Brabant, and no document places him outside the duchy.

## 5. Death, burial, and the first witnesses

- **9 August 1516: the requiem [A, Brotherhood account books]**. *Correction applied twice.* Both the chronology and the afterlife lens opened with "Bosch dies ... on 9 August 1516." The sources say **buried on** that date. It is the **burial and funeral-mass date**; no death date is documented. The afterlife lens also used **51.6978, 5.3037**, the generic city point a kilometre north of the church; corrected to the cathedral.
- **Burial in or near Sint-Jan, no marked grave [R]**. Standard consensus; no primary burial record has been published and the spot inside the building is lost.
- **Matthias Grunewald, Isenheim Altarpiece, 1512 to 1516 [A, Wikipedia 'Isenheim Altarpiece']**. Painted for the Antonine hospital at Issenheim, whose brothers treated ergotism. Same subject as Bosch's Lisbon triptych; **no contact between the painters is documented and none is asserted.** *Correction:* the afterlife lens put Issenheim at 48.0508, 7.3372, about 17 km off and near Colmar; Issenheim is 47.9031, 7.2547. The journey pins **Colmar (48.0777, 7.3579), inherited byte-identical from the interlock lens**, and says in prose that the altarpiece hangs at Colmar today, rather than mislabelling Colmar as Issenheim.
- **Michelangelo's Temptation of Saint Anthony [A, wrongly described in the pool]**. Not an engraving but an **oil and tempera panel**, *The Torment of Saint Anthony* (c. 1487 to 1488, Kimbell Art Museum), copied after **Schongauer's engraving** of c. 1470 to 1475; the chronology lens's fixed **1470** for the print has no documented basis, so **[R]**. Since Michelangelo's is a direct copy, the pool's claim that all three treatments "independently arrive at monstrosity" is false for his. Neither man is a stop.
- **Margaret of Austria's inventory, Mechelen, 1516 [R]**. Cited but not directly consulted.
- **Antonio de Beatis, 1517 [A, his travel diary]**: the earliest eyewitness description of the Garden. *Correction:* both geography-lens entries naming **Coudenberg Palace** are wrong. The collection hung in the **Nassau Palace**, a separate mansion on the same hill; the court only moved to Nassau after Coudenberg burned in 1731. Corrected to **50.84306, 4.35722**.
- **Marcantonio Michiel at the Palazzo Grimani, Venice [A, two corrections]**. He recorded the Grimani works as **tele**, canvases, not "on wood panel." The *Notizie d'opere di disegno* were written sporadically **1521 to 1543**, so 1521 is the start of a compilation, not a dated sighting. Coordinates corrected to 45.4372, 12.3410.

## 6. The afterlife: prints, Spain, dispersal, argument

- **Hieronymus Cock, Antwerp [A]**. *Correction:* the chronology lens dated the Big Fish Eat Little Fish print to **1556**, against the afterlife lens's **1557**. The **drawing** is 1556; Cock published the **engraving** in **1557**, naming Bosch as inventor rather than Bruegel. The journey uses 1557.
- **1568: Alba confiscates the Garden [A]**. *Correction:* the afterlife lens filed this with the place and coordinates of **El Escorial**, where the painting only arrived a quarter century later. The confiscation happened in **Brussels**. Split and repinned.
- **Philip II [A]**: Haywain acquired 1570, shipped to El Escorial 1574; a Temptation probably sent the same year **[R: not clearly itemized in the inventories]**; the Garden bought at auction **1591**, donated **8 July 1593**. The Haywain was later dispersed by the Marquis of Salamanca, its central panel bought by **Isabella II in 1848** for Aranjuez and the whole recomposed at the **Prado in 1914**; the Garden reached the Prado in **1939**.
- **Felipe de Guevara, "the inventor of monsters and chimeras" [A for the wording, corrected for the citation]**. The pool attributed the phrase to **Ilsink et al.**; the reference is **Gibson, p. 9**. Guevara's *Comentarios de la Pintura* was written c. 1560 and **not printed until 1788**, so the journey calls it written, not published. **Siguenza's 1605 defence [A]** is quoted in English only; the wording is translator-dependent and flagged as such.
- **The sawn triptych [A, one substantial correction]**. The dismembered wings run: **Ship of Fools** (upper left, Louvre) with **Allegory of Gluttony and Lust** (lower left, Yale) as the two halves of one wing; **Death and the Miser** (right, Washington); **The Wayfarer** on that wing's exterior (Rotterdam). The geography lens called Yale's panel the sister of Rotterdam's Wayfarer; it is the sister of the **Louvre's Ship of Fools**. Boijmans coordinates corrected to **51.9142, 4.4733**.
- **Ghent, Christ Carrying the Cross, acquired 1902 [A]; attribution rejected [A, contested]**. In **October 2015** the BRCP rejected the attribution on the underdrawing and the post-completion framing, gave the panel to a follower and proposed a date after Bosch's death. **Griet Steyaert contests this.** Both positions stated; neither adopted.
- **Vienna Last Judgement [R]**. *Correction:* the geography lens's "c. 1504 to 08" silently assumes the very connection to Philip's commission the same entry elsewhere calls unresolved. Dendrochronology shows the oak was **not used before 1482**; the usual range is c. 1482 to 1505. The journey gives the range and leaves the identification open.
- **Lisbon Temptation [R for the provenance]**. *Contradiction flagged:* the geography lens claimed arrival in Portugal by the **mid-sixteenth century** through **Damiao de Gois**, tagged **[A]**; the afterlife lens says only "since at least the seventeenth century" and calls the route in a genuine gap. The afterlife lens is adopted.
- **The interpretive fight**: **Fraenger 1947 [A as a publication]**, the Adamite or Free Spirit thesis, rejected by most scholars; **Bax 1949 [A]**, the folk-proverb decoding; **Tolnay 1937** and **von Baldass 1960 [A]**, the moralizing reading; Panofsky against Fraenger. The pool mis-dated Bax: the dissertation is *Ontcijfering van Jeroen Bosch* (**1949**), and *His Picture-Writing Deciphered* (1979) is its English translation.
  - *Two quotation corrections, both serious.* "Strove for a form of spirituality immune from sin even in the flesh..." was offered as a quotation **from Fraenger**. It is not: the reference is **Grange Books, p. 37**, and the sentence is Wikipedia's own prose about the sect. **Dropped.** "Hypotheses only and built on an unstable foundation" was offered as a modern scholarly rebuttal; it is unattributed encyclopedia voice with no citation and no named scholar. **Dropped entirely.**
- **BRCP, published 2016 [A, Ilsink et al., catalogue raisonne]**: roughly 2010 to 2016 of technical examination, the autograph corpus narrowed to around twenty paintings, several works reassigned. The **Prado disputed some downgrades publicly**.
- **Noordbrabants Museum, 2016 [A for the year, R for the day]**. *Corrections:* the title is *Visioenen van een genie* / *Visions of Genius*, not "Visions of a Genius"; the museum is at **51.6864, 5.3047**, about 430 m off the pool's coordinates; the **13 February opening date could not be confirmed** anywhere reachable.
- **The Markt statue [A, gap closed]**. The pool flagged sculptor and date as unverified. Dutch Wikipedia closes it: the *Standbeeld Jeroen Bosch* is by **August Falise**, made **1929**, unveiled **1930**, after the **Recueil d'Arras** portrait. The **Jheronimus Bosch Art Center** occupies the former New St James Church, opened **26 March 2007**, showing about sixty full-scale replicas.

## Interlock decisions

The atlas rule is that another traveler may be named in a campa only where Bosch has a real relation to that person, and never for shared geography or shared century.

**Named: `san_antonio_abad`.** Bosch painted the Temptation of Saint Anthony as a major triptych, and painting a hagiographic subject is a substantive one-directional relation to it. The existing `san_antonio_abad.journey.json` supplies the pig and the tau cross, the Hospital Brothers whose foraging pigs wore bell collars, and the ergotism wards behind the iconography. Three stops draw on it: the triptych's making, the Colmar comparison, and the Lisbon museum.

**Rejected, with reasons.** `michelangelo`: same subject, in a copy after Schongauer, and no link between the men. `leonardo_da_vinci`: the confraternity-commission parallel is structural, not a relation. `cortes`, `dante_gabriel_rossetti`, `giordano_bruno`, `giulio_camillo`, `arnold_rikli`: shared geography, era, or atmosphere only; the interlock lens itself called the last "very weak, geographic-name-only." `saint_erasmus` is Erasmus of Formia, a different man from Erasmus of Rotterdam.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical**: Colmar **48.0777, 7.3579**; Brussels **50.8417, 4.3582** (the 1504 court document); Louvre **48.8606, 2.3376**.

## Honest gaps

1. **No death date**, only a burial and requiem date. **No birth record**; the year is inferred from a portrait drawing.
2. **No patron for the Garden of Earthly Delights.** Engelbrecht II of Nassau and Henry III of Nassau-Breda are proposed **[R]**; no contract survives.
3. **The Brotherhood's retable is lost**, known only as an expenditure line; **Philip the Handsome's 1504 Last Judgement is lost or unidentified**.
4. **How the Lisbon Temptation reached Portugal is unknown**, and **when and by whom the Ship of Fools triptych was sawn apart is unrecorded**.
5. **The exact burial spot inside Sint-Jan is lost.**
6. **What Bosch's grandfather and father painted is unknown**; nothing survives under either name.
