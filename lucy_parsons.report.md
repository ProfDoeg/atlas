# Lucy Parsons (1851?-1942): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-21 for `lucy_parsons.journey.json`. 44 stops, 9 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or inference.
Contradictions are flagged and left standing, never adjudicated. The verified pool
arrived with adversarial corrections attached to individual entries; they are
carried into the phase they belong to, marked **CORR**, so the record of what was
wrong survives beside what replaced it.

---

## 0. The identity problem, which is not solvable

The contest here is not between careless sources. It is between the documentary
record and Parsons's own sixty-year testimony about herself.

- **The scholarship**: born 1851 in **Virginia** to an enslaved woman named
  Charlotte, on land held by **Thomas J. Taliaferro**, probably also her father.
  **[A: Jacqueline Jones, *Goddess of Anarchy* (2017), via Princeton AAS / WBEZ;
  Texas State Historical Association, *Handbook of Texas*, "Parsons, Lucy"]**
- **Her own account**: born in **Texas**, of **Mexican and Native American**
  parentage, never enslaved, no African ancestry. Held in print and on platforms her
  whole adult life. **[A as her testimony]**
- **Her death certificate**: birth year **1859** **[A: Wikipedia, citing it]**.
  libcom.org heads its entry "1853-1942"; that 1853 is a biographical estimate.

**CORR (geography 1).** The lens inverted the sources, saying most place the birth
in Texas with a minority giving Virginia. The reverse is true: TSHA and Jones give
Virginia 1851 and the removal to Texas in 1863, and it is Parsons's own account that
claims Texas. The same entry credited 1853 to the death certificate, which gives
1859. And 31.5493, -97.1467 is Waco, not a birthplace: the file uses it as childhood
locus only, and the birth stop carries a **placeholder pin at the geographic centre
of Virginia (37.5215, -78.8537)** because **no county is named in any reachable
source**.

The journey file gives both accounts and resolves neither. Her denial is itself one
of the largest facts of her life; a file that quietly picked Virginia and moved on
would have deleted it.

## 1. Texas, 1863-1873

- **1863**: Taliaferro marches the people he enslaves from Virginia into **McLennan
  County, Texas**, ahead of the Union armies. **1865**: Charlotte relocates the
  family to **Waco**, where Lucia grows up a **seamstress and domestic servant** and
  attends **a school for freed children**. **[A: TSHA]**
- **1868**: she begins living with **Oliver Benton**, a freedman **[A: TSHA]** who
  had been held on **James J. Gathings's place, Hill County**. Benton told an
  interviewer decades later that Lucia had been his common-law wife and left him for
  a white man **[R: AAIHS, "Searching for Lucy Parsons: A Racial Riddle"; Illinois
  RBML]**; historians disagree on its weight, and the journey file says so.
- **1870 census**, Waco: Lucia with her mother, brothers, and an **infant son
  reported as Champ**, later fate unknown. **[A: 1870 U.S. census, via TSHA]**
- **1871 or 1872**: she meets **Albert Parsons**, ex-Confederate soldier turned
  Radical Republican organizer among Black voters. **[A/R: TSHA; Wikipedia]**

### Contradiction, unresolved: the marriage

| source | date | place |
|---|---|---|
| TSHA, *Handbook of Texas* | September 1872 | **Waco** |
| Dictionary of American Biography (via Wikipedia) | 10 June 1871 | not given |
| popular accounts | 1872 | **Austin** |

**No marriage record has ever been found**, consistent with the difficulty or
illegality of interracial marriage in Texas.

**CORR (chronology 8; geography 3, 4).** (1) Both lens entries defaulted to
**Austin**, one committing a coordinate to it; TSHA names **Waco**, which the file
pins, with Austin recorded as the alternate. (2) "Legally impossible within weeks of
the marriage" is unsupported: **Texas's anti-miscegenation statute of 1858 was
already in force**, so the union was illegal at the ceremony, not weeks after. (3)
The lens dated Albert's meeting with Lucia to 1869, contradicting its own
chronology; TSHA dates 1869 to her pregnancy by Benton. Dropped.

## 2. Chicago, 1873-1883

- **December 1873**: arrival, driven out of Texas by Democratic restoration and
  hostility to the marriage and Albert's organizing **[A: TSHA]**; North Side
  tenements around **Larrabee Street and North Avenue** **[A: Wikipedia; IWW
  archive]**. **1874**: Lucy joins the **Social Democratic Workingmen's Party**;
  Albert works as printer and compositor **[R: Wikipedia]**.
- **July 1877**, the **Great Railroad Strike**: Albert addresses roughly **25,000**,
  is fired the next day and **blacklisted out of the printing trade** **[A: TSHA]**.
  Lucy dates her politics from this strike in her own words **[A]**.
- **1877**: she opens **Parsons & Co.**, a dress and clothing shop, at **306 Mohawk
  Street** **[A: Wikipedia]**. **1879**: son **Albert Parsons Jr.** born; **20 April
  1881**, daughter **Lulu Eda Parsons**.

**CORR (chronology 19).** The lens dated the **Chicago Working Women's Union** to
1885 and made Parsons a co-founder. The Working Women's Union No. 1 was organized in
**1877** under **Alzina Stevens**, and Wikipedia places Parsons's involvement,
alongside Stevens, Elizabeth Chambers Morgan, Elizabeth Flynn Rodgers and Lizzie
Swank, **well before 1881**: off by six to eight years. The file uses **c. 1877-79**
and does not call her a founder. The same entry bundled in the **Sunday lakefront
crowds of 1,000 to 5,000**, separately attested and belonging to the late 1870s and
1880s generally; separated.

**CORR (geography 6).** Two entries gave two coordinates for the *same* building,
41.911, -87.642 and 41.9147, -87.6423, both for 306 Mohawk Street, the pre-1909
number for **1908 North Mohawk Street** and the address of the 1997 city marker.
Both sit south of the real address, approx. **41.9163, -87.6427**, used in the file
for shop and marker alike.

## 3. The Alarm, 1883-1886

- **1883**: Lucy meets **Johann Most**; in **November** Albert helps found the
  **American Group of Chicago**, English-speaking wing of the **International
  Working People's Association**. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **4 October 1884**: **"To tramps, the unemployed, the disinherited and miserable"**
  runs in *The Alarm*, selling **over 10,000 copies** as a pamphlet between May and
  November 1885. **[A: The Anarchist Library; libcom]**
- **28 April 1885**: Parsons and **Lizzie Holmes** lead an IWPA march on the **Board
  of Trade** banquet. **1 May 1886**: she leads roughly **80,000** down **Michigan
  Avenue**. **[A: Wikipedia]**

**CORR (chronology 17).** **"Dynamite! The only voice the oppressors of the people
can understand"** ran in the **Denver *Labor Enquirer***, not *The Alarm*, and is
dated **1885**, not 1884. The lens had merged it with "To Tramps."

**CORR (quotes 15).** The opening of "To Tramps" reads **"A word to the 35,000"**,
not 30,000, and **"evidence"** singular. The 35,000 is Chicago's unemployed in 1884.
The file carries the corrected wording.

**CORR (quotes 14).** The anthologised passage *"Governments never lead; they follow
progress. When the prison, stake or scaffold can no longer silence the voice of the
protesting minority, progress moves on a step, but not until then"* is wrong twice as
usually cited. **(1)** It is not in *The Alarm* of 4 October 1884, which carried "To
Tramps"; it appears in **"The Principles of Anarchism,"** a lecture of roughly
1905-1910. **(2)** Parsons sets the sentences **inside quotation marks in her own
text**, so she is quoting an earlier writer, not coining the line. **It is carried
nowhere in the journey file**, and is recorded here so the next person reaching for it
knows what it is.

## 4. Haymarket, May 1886

- **3 May**: police fire on strikers at the **McCormick Harvesting Machine Works**.
  **4 May**: the meeting at **Haymarket Square**, Desplaines Street.
- **5 May**: warrantless raid on the ***Arbeiter-Zeitung*** offices; Lucy arrested,
  called "a black bitch" by an officer, released without charge. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **27 May**: a grand jury indicts **31 men**, Albert among them. **Lucy and Lizzie
  Holmes are not charged**, despite being prominent advocates of militant rhetoric in
  print. **20 August**: seven death sentences, Lucy present **every day** of the
  trial. **[A: Wikipedia]**

**CORR (chronology 21).** Sequence and casualties both wrong. The bomb was thrown
**as police advanced to disperse** the meeting, and the police **then** opened fire;
the lens had it "after police open fire." Casualties: **seven police officers and at
least four workers** killed, only **Mathias Degan** dying directly from the bomb, and
**Avrich** holds most police deaths came from **police gunfire**. **[A: Wikipedia,
"Haymarket affair"; Illinois Labor History Society]**

**CORR, three coordinates.** **McCormick works (geography 10)**: the plant stood at
**Western and Blue Island**, approx. **41.855, -87.684**, not 41.8524, -87.6763,
~800 m ESE; and "killing several" overstates **two** workers killed, six in some
newspapers. ***Arbeiter-Zeitung* (13)**: **107 Fifth Avenue**, later North Wells,
approx. **41.8838, -87.6337**, not 41.8853, -87.632. **Moderate confidence only**:
standard in the Haymarket literature, but neither Wikipedia entry consulted prints
the address, and the stop says so. **Criminal Court (15)**: **Hubbard at Dearborn**,
approx. **41.8898, -87.6294**, on the jail's own block; the lens gave 41.8869,
-87.6313, contradicting its own jail coordinate.

**Zepf's Hall** is tagged **[R]**: that the family left the wagon for the hall before
the bomb is standard in the literature and in the IWW archive biography, but **no
primary deposition was reachable this session**.

## 5. The tour, the gallows, the funeral, 1886-1887

- A **seven-week lecture tour** through **Cincinnati, New York, Philadelphia, New
  Haven, Columbus**, addressing an estimated **200,000**; prevented from speaking in
  Columbus and **briefly jailed in Franklin County**. **[A: Wikipedia; IWW archive]**
- **10 November 1887**: Lingg kills himself; Oglesby commutes Fielden and Schwab.
  **11 November**: execution of **Albert Parsons, August Spies, George Engel, Adolph
  Fischer** at the **Cook County Jail**. **13 November**: funeral procession of
  **10,000 to 15,000**, Lucy behind the casket. **[A]**

**CORR (chronology 26; geography 14).** The lens placed the attempted final visit,
the strip search for explosives and the detention until 15:00 on **10 November**, the
eve. Wikipedia places all three on **11 November 1887, the execution day itself**,
with **Lizzie Holmes** accompanying her; the 10th was Lingg's death and the
commutations. A second entry dated the strip search to 1886, where her detentions are
described as brief and repeated with no strip search recorded. The **East Chicago
Avenue station** stood at **113 W Chicago Ave**, approx. **41.8967, -87.6306**, not
41.8963, -87.6371, ~550 m west.

**Contradiction, unresolved: the execution site.** AAIHS names **Joliet State
Prison**; every other source names the **Cook County Jail**, which the file uses
while recording the dissent.

**Contradiction, unresolved: the tour dates.** The chronology lens places the seven
weeks in **1886**, between conviction and execution; the geography lens dates the same
stops **1887-1888**. The file uses 1886-1887 because the stated purpose, raising funds
for the condemned, requires it, and flags the disagreement.

**Two attested tour stops dropped for length, not doubt**: **Philadelphia** **[A: IWW
archive]** and a meeting at **Sheffield, Indiana**, held just outside Chicago police
jurisdiction alongside Thomas J. Morgan **[R: IWW archive]**.

## 6. Britain, October to December 1888

- Six-week propaganda tour: **Socialist League** platforms across London shared with
  **William Morris** and **Peter Kropotkin**; public disagreement with **Annie
  Besant** over the use of violence. **[A: Wikipedia; Lichtenstein, "Lucy Parsons's
  Propaganda Tour of Britain," *History* (2025)]**
- She speaks at **Kelmscott House**, Morris's Hammersmith home **[A: Lichtenstein;
  Black Outdoors]**, visits the **Tower of London** and **Westminster Abbey** **with
  Jane Morris**, and returns by ship to New York in **December** **[A: Wikipedia; IWW
  archive]**.

**CORR (geography 24, 25).** Tower and Abbey were tagged **[R]** as generic tourism.
Wikipedia attests both **with Jane Morris as her companion**, the detail that turns a
tourist note into a scene. Upgraded to **[A]**.

**CORR (geography 23).** The Kelmscott House coordinate was off by roughly 240 m
(lens: 51.4915, -0.2323). The file **inherits the canonical pin already in
`william_morris.journey.json`, 51.4905, -0.235, byte-identical**.

**Unverified, retained with a warning label.** The date **15 November 1888** for the
Kelmscott House address could not be confirmed in any reachable source; Wikipedia
dates only the **October 1888** arrival. It is retained solely on the Lichtenstein
citation supplied by the pool, and the stop's `date_confidence` says so.

## 7. The widow's decades, 1889-1901

- **1889**: she writes and self-publishes ***Life of Albert R. Parsons***; **Martin
  Lacher** is alleged by some accounts to have written much of it **[A for the book;
  R for the allegation]**. Second edition 1903. The same year Chief **George W.
  Hubbard** forbids her lecture "Review of the Labor Movement in Europe" and Lacher
  is arrested protesting the ban **[A]**.
- **November 1890**: prevented from speaking in **Newark**, arrested in the street
  for incitement to riot **[A]**. She edits ***Freedom***: Wikipedia dates it **from
  1891**, TSHA **1890-1892**. **Contradiction left standing.**
- **August 1896**: fire at the **999 Hammond Avenue** house (later North Troy
  Street), Avondale; she sells the fire-damaged books cheap. **1897**: meets **Emma
  Goldman**. **1899**: **Albert Parsons Jr.** committed to the Northern Illinois
  State (Elgin) hospital, dying there of tuberculosis in **1919**. **[A]**

**CORR, four entries.** **Lulu Eda (chronology 34)**: born **20 April 1881**, dead of
lymphedema **at eight**, so between April 1889 and April 1890, **not 1888**; the
"seven or eight" hedge is dropped and the file dates it 1889. **The Goldman clash
(36)**: Goldman advocated **free love against Parsons's defence of monogamy**, where
the lens attributed **both** positions to Goldman. **The committal (37)**: **she** had
Albert Jr. committed, after his attempt to enlist for the Spanish-American War; the
lens left it agentless, as though it happened to him. **The *Free Society* press
(38)**: only the correspondent role is "by 1900", and **McKinley was shot 6 September
and died 14 September 1901**, so the destruction of the press is **1901**.

## 8. The IWW and the long old age, 1905-1941

- **27 June 1905**, **Brand's Hall**: founding convention of the **Industrial Workers
  of the World**, 200 delegates, Parsons addressing it **on two occasions** **[A:
  libcom; Wikipedia, "Big Bill Haywood"; 1905 convention minutes, Tamiment]**. That
  year she also edits ***The Liberator*** **[A: TSHA]**.
- **1912**: she **hosts** the meeting founding the **Syndicalist League of North
  America**, **William Z. Foster** staying at her home. **January 1915**: arrested
  with **Father Irwin St John Tucker** and nineteen others at a hunger march of
  ~1,500 unemployed near **Hull House**. **[A]**
- **1 May 1930**: **Ashland Auditorium** May Day address, later reprinted in U.S.
  Congressional hearings on communist activity. **1933**: visits the **Century of
  Progress** exposition **[A: IWW archive]**, attested but dropped from the file for
  length. **1939**: joins the **Communist Party USA**, blind, in poverty, with a
  library of roughly 3,000 books. **1 May 1941**: last public appearance, guest of
  honour with the **Farm Equipment Workers' Organizing Committee**. **[A]**

**CORR (chronology 39; afterlife 8), a self-contradicting claim.** Two entries called
her the **only woman** among the IWW's founding leaders, or the only woman to address
the convention. Both are **false**, and one names **Mother Jones** in the same
sentence. Wikipedia's Big Bill Haywood entry names Debs, **Mother Jones** and Lucy
Parsons among the Brand's Hall speakers. Corrected throughout to **one of only two
women**.

**CORR, two coordinates.** Brand's Hall stood on the **near North Side, Clark at
Erie**, approx. **41.894, -87.631**, not 41.8977, -87.6533, roughly 1.8 km west. And
the Syndicalist League coordinate contradicted its own claim text: Foster "was staying
with Parsons at the time" and she hosted the founding meeting, i.e. at her **Avondale
house**, while the lens pinned a generic Loop point it also reused for two unrelated
stops.

## 9. Death, 7 March 1942, and the library

- Fire in the **Avondale house**; **George Markstall** cannot get her out and dies the
  next day of his own injuries. She is about **91**. **Cause disputed**: Ashbaugh
  gives a **wood stove**, an alternative account a **basement water heater**. **Not
  resolved.**
- **12 March 1942**: memorial service, ~300 attending; buried at **German Waldheim
  (Forest Home) Cemetery**, Forest Park, near the **Haymarket Martyrs' Monument**
  (Albert Weinert, dedicated 25 June 1893). The ashes of **Albert Jr.** and **George
  Markstall** are later interred in her grave. **[A]**

**CORR (chronology 45; afterlife 2), the FBI claim downgraded.** The pool asserted at
**[A]** that police confiscated the library and **turned it over to the FBI**. What
the sources actually support: part of the library was destroyed **by the fire itself**
[A: Wikipedia]; **police seized** books and papers from the gutted house [A: libcom];
the material was **never recovered** [A: both]; **FBI custody is [R]**, reaching us
secondhand through the head of the Chicago Red Squad telling **Ben Reitman** so, while
the **FBI denied any knowledge** of the books [A: Wikipedia]. The file states the
seizure as fact and FBI custody as contested and denied.

**CORR (afterlife 1, 5, 8, 9).** The death coordinate pointed at **41.8781, -87.6298**,
the Chicago **Loop**. She died in **Avondale**, approx. **41.9386, -87.7062**, the
coordinate the geography lens itself used for the same event; the same generic Loop pin
was reused across four unrelated afterlife items.

**Contradiction, unresolved: the cemetery coordinate.** Geography gives **41.8703,
-87.8241**, afterlife **41.86972, -87.81972**, for the same cemetery. The file uses the
first **consistently across both cemetery stops** and flags the disagreement in each
`date_confidence`. Neither was verified against a survey.

**CORR (afterlife 8).** The **Lucy Parsons Center** was founded in **1969 in Central
Square, Cambridge**, as the Red Book Store, not in Jamaica Plain; it moved there 1983,
back to Central Square 1994, Davis Square 1998, the South End 1999, and to Jamaica
Plain again 2011. *Not staged as a stop: Parsons was never there.*

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Lucy Parsons is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stop is licensed on
her file. Per the adversary's duty in that document, the relations below were found in
the pool, are **not rooted in her own life**, and are **recorded rather than silently
deleted**, with the direction named.

**FORWARD (not licensable without the author's say-so).** **Noam Chomsky** (b. 1928).
The pool proposes that Chomsky's self-described anarcho-syndicalism, traced to Rocker
and Bakunin, descends from the current the Chicago IWPA and IWW milieu fed into.
Parsons died in **1942**; Chomsky is a later hash and cannot be written into her block.
**Not carried.** If the operator wants the link it belongs on
`noam_chomsky.journey.json` pointing backward at the IWW founding, legal in that
direction.

**Shared geography only, dropped by the standing rule, listed so the operator can see
what was on offer.** `frank_lloyd_wright`, `thomas_edison`, `nikola_tesla`,
`sitting_bull`, `low_dog`, `marcus_garvey`: every one rests on **both parties being in
Chicago**, several on the 1893 Columbian Exposition, and the map already shows
co-location. `gustav_landauer`: the 1893 Zurich and 1896 London expulsions of anarchists
from the Second International parallel the marginalisation Parsons faced from AFL-aligned
unionism, but a parallel is not a relation and the two had no contact on the record.
`joaquin_trincado_mateo`: Buenos Aires's FORA took the Haymarket martyrs, **Albert
Parsons among the executed**, as a founding reference for May Day, a real one-directional
relation from Argentine syndicalism to Haymarket but **not a Trincado relation**; it
belongs on the Argentine files if anywhere, and it is the most interesting of the
discarded set. **None named.**

**Relations kept, and why.** **`karl_marx` and `mikhail_bakunin`**, named once together
in the November 1883 IWPA stop: not co-location, but the fact that the **IWPA is the
organisation Parsons joined**, and is the American section of the body the anarchists
built after the Hague split of 1872 put Bakunin's followers outside Marx's
International. Tagged **[R]** in the stop, being attested in the historiography of the
IWPA and not in any statement of hers. **`william_morris`**, at Kelmscott House: she
**spoke in his house and shared platforms with him**. **`jane_morris`**, at the Tower:
**Jane Morris took her sightseeing**. Both direct and attested.
