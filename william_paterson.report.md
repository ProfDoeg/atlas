# William Paterson (1658-1719), research report

*Slug: `william_paterson`. Prepared 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Founder-projector of the Bank of England (chartered 27 July 1694) and author of the Darien scheme, the Company of Scotland's colony at Caledonia Bay on the Isthmus of Panama (1698-1700).*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in the same line. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including anything a source hedges ("it is said", "tradition holds") and anything inferred beyond what a source states. Contradictions are flagged and left standing, not adjudicated. Corrections made to the verified research pool are gathered in their own section below rather than buried in the narrative.

**Calendar.** Britain was on the Julian calendar (Old Style) all Paterson's life and did not adopt the Gregorian reckoning until 1752. Dates below are as the sources print them, which is Old Style. The journey file's `calendar` field is `gregorian` per the atlas convention for post-1582 lives; where the distinction matters (the probate of the will) it is named.

## 1. Origins: Skipmyre, Bristol, an unresolved childhood

**[A: DNB 1885-1900; Encyclopedia.com]** Born 1658 in or near the parish of Tinwald, Dumfriesshire, most likely at the farm of **Skipmyre**. **[R]** Wikipedia gives the month as April; no parish register is reproduced in any source reached, so the day is unfixed and defaults in the journey file.

**Contradiction, unresolved.** The two principal sources disagree about the whole first seventeen years. **Wikipedia, "William Paterson (banker)":** he stayed at Skipmyre until about seventeen, then emigrated to Bristol and on to the Bahamas. **DNB:** he was "bred in England from his infancy", living at Bristol with a maternal relative, so there was no Scottish childhood to leave. Neither cites a document.

## 2. The West Indies and the origin of the Darien idea

**[R: Wikipedia, hedged with "it is said"]** Around 1680 he travelled to the Bahamas and worked as a merchant in the West Indies among buccaneers. The town is inferred; Wikipedia names only "the Bahamas".

**[R: Wikipedia; DNB dates the conception of the scheme to 1684]** Tradition holds he either walked the Isthmus of Darien himself or learned its geography from the privateer **William Dampier**. No primary account places him on the isthmus before the 1698 landfall.

**[A: DNB]** 16 November 1681, admitted to the Merchant Taylors' Company of London. **[A: Wikipedia]** His later trade under that company included the West Indies slave trade.

## 3. The projector at four courts, mid-1680s

**[A: Wikipedia]** He returned to Europe by the middle 1680s and took the Darien plan to the government of **James II**, which refused it, then to the **Holy Roman Empire**, the **Dutch Republic** and **Brandenburg**, all of which refused it too. **[R]** None of the three Continental cities is named by any source; Amsterdam, Vienna and Berlin are inferences of equal weight, and are tagged alike.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1687, settles in London and makes a fortune in foreign trade. **[A: DNB]** 21 October 1689, admitted to the livery of the Merchant Taylors' Company.

**[A as fact, gap as to date: Wikipedia]** He helped found the **Hampstead Water Company**, supplying north London from the Hampstead hills and surviving until the late nineteenth century. No source reached gives a founding date, so the journey file leaves it undated.

## 4. The Bank of England, 1691-1695

**[A: DNB; Wikipedia]** July-August 1691, Paterson with **Michael Godfrey** and others proposes a national bank, acting as chief projector. **[A: DNB]** January 1692, he is principal witness before a parliamentary committee on raising supplies for the French war.

**[A: Bank of England]** 1694, publication of **"A Brief Account of the Intended Bank of England"**, the founding prospectus, three years after the proposal it argues for.

**[A: Bank of England]** The subscription of 1,200,000 pounds was raised in twelve days from **1,268 subscribers**, women among them, at **Mercers' Hall**, Cheapside.

**[A: Wikipedia; DNB]** 27 July 1694, the Bank receives its **Royal Charter**; Paterson is a founding director holding the required 2,000 pounds qualification. **[A: Bank of England]** It opens at Mercers' Hall on 1 August 1694 and moves to **Grocers' Hall, Poultry** on 31 December 1694, where it stays nearly forty years.

**[A: Wikipedia; DNB]** 1695, Paterson withdraws from the court of directors after a disagreement with his colleagues. Neither source gives the substance or the month. **Gap.**

## 5. The Company of Scotland, 1695-1697

**[A: DNB]** 26 June 1695, he travels to Scotland and drafts the Act of the Parliament of Scotland establishing the **Company of Scotland Trading to Africa and the Indies**.

**[A: Bannister, *The Writings of William Paterson*, vol. I, Biographical Introduction, pp. xxxviii-xxxix]** Letters from Paterson in London to the Lord Provost of Edinburgh, 4 July, 9 July and 3 September 1695, printed in the Darien Papers and quoted by Bannister: secrecy, subscription tactics (the Bank of England's books "finished in nine days"), unity, and his misplaced September confidence that London would imitate rather than crush the venture. **Note:** Bannister runs the "act apart" passage on from the 9 July letter without a new date heading, so it may belong to that letter or to an undated continuation; treated as the same July 1695 sequence and flagged in the quote source.

**[A: Wikipedia, "Company of Scotland"]** Subscription book opened in **Edinburgh 26 February 1696**; a second book open in **Glasgow 5 March to 22 April 1696**; the full **400,000 pounds sterling** reached by the beginning of **August 1696**; the court of directors met in the company's offices in **Mylne Square** on Edinburgh's High Street **from July 1696**.

**[A: Wikipedia]** The foreign subscription effort in London, Amsterdam and Hamburg **failed**: English, Dutch and Hamburg capital was withdrawn under English government pressure, and the whole 400,000 pounds was raised inside Scotland. The Company of Scotland article estimates that sum at "a quarter of its liquid assets", which is not the same quantity as a fraction of national wealth.

**[A: DNB]** 1697, serves as a director of the Company working abroad, commissioned to purchase stores for the expedition. **[R]** DNB says "continental Europe" and names no city; the journey file pins Hamburg and labels the pin an inference.

## 6. Darien, 1698-1699

**[A: Wikipedia, "Darien scheme"; Encyclopedia.com]** 14 July 1698, five ships (Saint Andrew, Caledonia, Unicorn, Dolphin, Endeavour) sail from **Leith** with about 1,200 settlers; Paterson sails with them. **[A: Wikipedia]** The fleet calls at **Madeira** and claims **Crab Isle (Vieques, Puerto Rico)**; the claim is dated only to 1698 and must precede the November landfall.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 2 November 1698, landfall at **Caledonia Bay**; the settlers raise **Fort St Andrew** and lay out **New Edinburgh**. The fort mounted fifty cannon and the site had no reliable freshwater source.

**[A: Wikipedia; DNB]** Paterson falls seriously ill; his wife **Hannah (Kemp) Paterson** and their child die in the colony over the winter of 1698-1699. **Marriage note:** DNB records a **first wife, Elizabeth Turner**, who died earlier; Hannah Kemp was the second, and the journey file names both.

**[A: Wikipedia]** July 1699, the first colony is abandoned after roughly eight months, having lost about **900 of the original 1,200**. 10 August 1699, the **Caledonia** reaches **New York** with about 250 survivors including Paterson and the Drummond brothers; the **Unicorn** four days later.

**[R: Wikipedia]** Fleeing colonists sought help at **Port Royal, Jamaica** and were refused by English colonial authorities under orders not to provoke Spain. It is not confirmed that Paterson's own ship called there, so this gets no stop.

**[A: Wikipedia]** From 30 November 1699 a second wave of over 1,000 settlers reaches the abandoned colony, captures the Spanish stockade at **Toubacanti**, and gives the colony up under Spanish siege. Paterson was not present; the episode is carried inside a campa rather than pinned.

## 7. Return, the Union, the Equivalent

**[A: DNB gives 5 December 1699; Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com give December without a day]** Paterson returns to Edinburgh, having lost favour with the Court of Directors after a subordinate's embezzlement.

**[A: Bannister, vol. I, title page and Editor's Preface]** 1700, the sentence "Paterson has a prodigious genius", written from Edinburgh into the Carstares correspondence. The attributor is **T. Stewart**, not Carstares; see the corrections section.

**[A: Bannister, vol. I, pp. 158-159]** 1701, **"A Proposal to Plant a Colony in Darien, to Protect the Indians against Spain, and to Open the Trade of South America to All Nations"**, source of his two most quoted lines. **[A: DNB]** April 1701, received by **King William III** and consulted on public credit.

**[R: Wikipedia]** 1705, publishes writings advocating union and addressing Scottish trade and public credit. Place uncertain (London or Edinburgh); folded into the 1706 material rather than given a stop with an invented pin.

**[A: DNB]** 1706, employed in calculating the **Equivalent**, the compensation due to Scotland for assuming a share of England's national debt, and helps frame the union's trade and finance articles.

**[R: general historical record, not confirmed by any source fetched]** The Union commissioners met at **the Cockpit, Whitehall**. Paterson's presence in that room is **not** confirmed; this is the weakest [R] in the file and should be checked before promotion to canon.

**[A: DNB]** 25 March 1707, the Parliament of Scotland formally recommends Paterson's services and losses to Queen Anne shortly before its final dissolution. **[A]** 1 May 1707, the Acts of Union take effect.

## 8. Indemnity, death, burial

**[A: DNB]** 1713, the **Commons passed a bill awarding him 18,000 pounds**; the **Lords rejected it**, for a reason DNB does not record.

**[A: DNB]** 1715, Parliament grants the indemnity, reported as roughly 18,000 pounds. **Gap:** no source reached says whether it was paid whole, in instalments, or against deductions.

**[A: Bannister, vol. II, pp. 63-64]** 1717, *Wednesday's Club Dialogues upon the Union of Great Britain*, in which the interlocutor "Mr. May" narrates the 1691-1694 campaign for the Bank. **[R]** Bannister's editorial argument identifies Mr. May as Paterson's own mouthpiece; **no independent corroboration of the club's existence survives**, so the framing device, not the historical content of the speech, is the reconstruction.

**[A: DNB]** Dies **January 1719** at his home in **Queen Square, Westminster**, where DNB places him from 1703. The will is proved at **Doctors' Commons**, London, **22 January 1719 Old Style**.

**Contradiction, unresolved: the death date.** Wikipedia gives only "January 1719 in Westminster"; DNB gives 22 January as the probate date; undiscoveredscotland.co.uk asserts "died 22 January 1719". The journey file dates the death to January 1719 with the day defaulted and explains the conflict in the confidence field.

**Contradiction, unresolved: the burial.** **[R: Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]** The location is a mystery; Bank of England officials and others believe **Sweetheart Abbey**, New Abbey, Dumfries and Galloway, but this is explicitly called unconfirmed. **[R: undiscoveredscotland.co.uk]** States flatly that he "was buried in the graveyard at Sweetheart Abbey". That source is unreliable here: it titles him "Sir William Paterson", and **neither DNB nor Wikipedia records any knighthood**. The "Sir" is carried nowhere in either artifact.

**[A: undiscoveredscotland.co.uk]** A commemorative plaque to Paterson was unveiled at Sweetheart Abbey in **1974**.

## 9. Memorials, afterlives, absences

**[R: NLS, digitized "Histories of Scottish Families", p. 73]** A nineteenth-century antiquarian text proposes a memorial at Skipmyre farmhouse or on the ridge of **Tinwald Top**, calling it potentially "a thing of beauty, an object of attraction". **Gap:** nothing found shows whether it was built, abandoned, or purely rhetorical.

**[R: synthesis, a research gap and not a confirmed absence]** No public statue or civic monument to Paterson himself was located; the only commemorations found are the 1974 plaque and the unbuilt cairn proposal.

**[A: Wikipedia]** Afterlives: Eliot Warburton, *Darien, or, The Merchant Prince* (novel, 1852), in which Paterson is the central character; Douglas Galbraith, *The Rising Sun* (novel, 2000); Alistair Beaton, *Caledonia* (play, 2010); the Paul McKenna Band's song "Dreams of Darien" (2011); the boardgame *Darien Apocalypse* by the Ragnar Brothers (2018); the audiovisual installation *Astro-Darien* by Kode9 and Lawrence Lek (2021), Corsica Studios, London.

**[A: Wikipedia]** The colony's port, **Puerto Escocés**, was renamed **Puerto Inabaginya** in 2011. A ditch is described as the only identifiable physical remnant of the settlement today.

## Corrections applied to the research pool

Substantive, in descending order of consequence.

1. **The "prodigious genius" attribution.** The pool credited the epigraph to "T. Carstares". The words are verbatim, but the attributor is **T. Stewart**. Bannister's vol. I title page reads: `"Paterson has a prodigious genius." T. Stewart, Edinb. 1700. The Carstares Correspondence (p. 436).` **William Carstares is the recipient and namesake of the letter collection, not its author**, and Bannister's Editor's Preface confirms it by printing the underlying letter of 14 September 1700 with its writer named. Residual gap: his footnotes cite Carstares pp. 633 and 646 while the title page cites p. 436, and the original volume was not reached.
2. **The subscriptions inverted.** The pool had Paterson raising the 400,000 pounds "in Amsterdam, Hamburg, and London". The foreign effort failed; the money came from Scotland. Its "one-fifth to one-half of Scotland's national wealth" is replaced by the source's own "a quarter of its liquid assets".
3. **The Bank of Scotland claim dropped, not preserved.** The pool framed a 1695-vs-1696 conflict over Paterson "championing" the Bank of Scotland as unresolvable. It is not: the bank was founded 17 July 1695, John Holland founder and first governor. **No source consulted assigns Paterson any role in it**, so it appears nowhere in the journey file.

## Interlocks with the existing corpus

**[A: `working/journeys/QUEUE.md`, line 213]** The queue names the connection: Paterson is queued as the point where "the corpus's central-banking thread (`alexander_hamilton`, `adam_smith` just queued) reaches its English root." Everything below is **[R]**, an inferred connection to an existing traveler file.

- **`newton`.** Warden of the Royal Mint from April 1696, running the **Great Recoinage** two years after the Bank's charter under the same government; he later hangs **William Chaloner**, a forger of **Bank of England notes**, at Tyburn in 1699, and as Master fixes the gold-silver ratio behind two centuries of the gold standard. The clipped-silver crisis he was hired to end is the crisis that nearly sank the two-year-old Bank. Named in the Grocers' Hall withdrawal campa.
- **`edmond_halley`.** Deputy comptroller of the **Chester** branch Mint, 1696-1698, same recoinage, same campa.
- **`john_locke`.** Commissioner of the Board of Trade at **Whitehall**, 1696-1700, ruling on "the coinage, the colonies and their governors, the terms of trade", the same government and policy areas in which the Bank was defended and the Company of Scotland killed. His Whitehall pin (**51.5033, -0.1276**) is inherited byte-identical for the Cockpit stop.
- **`samuel_oppenheimer`.** His 1692-1694 imprisonment falls in exactly the years the Bank is chartered: court-Jewish private finance and the joint-stock public bank, two answers to one question in one decade. Named in the Mercers' Hall charter campa.
- **`tschiffely`.** The 1926-27 ride refuses the Darien on horseback ("the Darien... defeats horses absolutely"), over the terrain that killed the colonists. Named in the landfall campa.
- **`bolivar`.** The 1815 Kingston letter, quoted inside `tschiffely.journey.json`, imagines the isthmus gathering the American nations into trade, Paterson's "door of the seas" vision again. Named in the 1701 Proposal campa.
- **`alexis_de_tocqueville`, `andrew_jackson`, `abraham_lincoln`.** Jackson's 1832 veto of the Second Bank of the United States (in the Tocqueville file) and Lincoln's 1863 National Currency Act, which "fills the hole Jackson made", both bear on the model Paterson pioneered. Named in the Wednesday's Club campa.
