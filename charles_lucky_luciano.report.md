# Charles "Lucky" Luciano (Salvatore Lucania), 1897-1962: research report

*Atlas of Journeys working file, 2026-08-10. Slug: `charles_lucky_luciano`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the reachable sources do not carry at its usual strength. Contradictions are flagged in place and left open; unopenable sources are named with the reason.

---

## 1. Lercara Friddi and the crossing (1897-1906)

**Birth.** Salvatore Lucania is born at Lercara Friddi, a sulfur-mining town in the Sicilian interior, in 1897 **[A: en.wikipedia "Lucky Luciano"; Mob Museum; Britannica-derived chronology]**. The year is stable across every source consulted. Parents: Antonio Lucania and **Rosalia Caffarella [A]**. An earlier draft gave "Rosalia Cafarelli"; that form is not what the article carries.

**Birth order corrected.** He is the **third** of five, not the fourth. En.wikipedia lists Giuseppe (1885), Bartolomeo (1890), Salvatore (1897), Filippa (1901), Concetta (1903) **[A]**. The usual "fourth of five" does not survive the sibling list in the same article.

**Emigration.** The family emigrates in **1906** and settles on the Lower East Side **[A]**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** History.com puts the immigration at age 10, i.e. roughly **1907 [R]**. Two sources, two years, no document to adjudicate. His age at arrival is **eight** ("In 1906, when Luciano was eight years old"), which an earlier draft rendered as nine **[A]**. Two gaps: no manifest naming a port of embarkation has surfaced, so **Palermo [R]** is the biographers' assumption; and the specific tenement is not fixed in accessible sources **[R]**.

## 2. The street years (1911-1920)

**School and the dice game, causal order inverted in the popular version.** "At age 14, Luciano dropped out of school and started a job delivering hats... After winning $244 in a dice game, Luciano quit his job." He left school **first** (age 14, so 1911-12); the $244 ended his **legitimate job**, not his schooling **[A]**.

**Lansky.** The meeting belongs to the **teenage years, c. 1911-1915**: "Luciano met Meyer Lansky as a teenager when Luciano attempted to extort Lansky for protection money on his walk home from school" **[A]**. An earlier draft placed it in 1929, off by fifteen years and contradicting its own Five Points entry. **`meyer_lansky.journey.json`** pins the encounter at 40.716 / -73.989, dated 1918; that pin is inherited byte-identical and the 1911-1918 spread is carried in `date_confidence`.

**Five Points, and the charge list corrected.** History.com dates his standing in the Five Points milieu to **1916 [A]**. "From 1916 to 1936, Luciano was arrested twenty-five times on charges including assault, illegal gambling, blackmail and robbery but spent no time in prison" **[A]**. **Narcotics is not in that list.** An earlier draft added it; the 1923 heroin sting is a separate incident and must not be folded back into the twenty-five.

**The 1916 conviction, venue disputed, tag downgraded.** An earlier draft placed a 1916 narcotics conviction and prison term at the **Blackwell's Island Penitentiary**, tagged **[A]**. En.wikipedia contains no such conviction and no mention of Blackwell's Island. The standard biographies (Sifakis, Newark) give a June 1916 heroin conviction and six months at the **New Hampton Farms reformatory**, Orange County **[R]**. The pin is relocated and retagged; whether he served any custodial time before 1936 is left open.

## 3. Bootlegging, Rothstein, Masseria (1920-1929)

**Rank corrected.** "Lower Manhattan crime boss Joe Masseria recruited Luciano as one of his gunmen" **[A]**; "Luciano and his close associates started working for gambler Arnold Rothstein" **[A]**. He worked **for** Rothstein; the frequently asserted partnership overstates it and is not carried. Rothstein is murdered in Midtown in **1928 [A: `meyer_lansky.journey.json`, pin 40.7648 / -73.9814]**, framed there as the mentor who taught the milieu to run the underworld as a business with books.

**1923, income, Barbizon-Plaza.** Caught selling heroin; the arrest damages his standing and he repairs it through high-profile socializing **[A]**. "By 1925, Luciano was grossing over $12 million per year, and made a personal income of about $4 million per year" **[A]**: the quoted "$4 million a year" is the **personal net after payoffs**, not the gross, and both are carried. From 1927 he keeps rooms under the alias **Charles Lane [A]**, and History.com dates his appointment as Masseria's top lieutenant to the same year **[A]**.

**Atlantic City, 13-16 May 1929.** Lansky, Luciano, Torrio and Costello divide territory and set up arbitration in place of open war **[A: `meyer_lansky.journey.json`, pin 39.3512 / -74.4324]**. Inherited byte-identical.

**October 1929, tag downgraded, setting corrected.** En.wikipedia gives only: "In October 1929, Luciano was forced into a limousine at gunpoint by three men, beaten and stabbed, and strung up by his hands from a beam in a warehouse in Staten Island." No day of the month, no Huguenot, a **warehouse** rather than a shoreline. An earlier draft supplied "1929-10-16", the Huguenot district and a beach; all three are **[R]** and the beach is dropped. On the nickname the article is open: "It is not clear how Luciano earned the nickname," offering the beating, gambling luck, and a mispronunciation of Lucania **[R]**.

## 4. The Castellammarese War (1930-1931)

**War dates and the Reina actor corrected.** En.wikipedia "Castellammarese War" gives **26 February 1930 to 15 April 1931 [A]**. History.com's "in 1928 a feud broke out" is an outlier and is not followed **[R]**; an earlier draft carried 1928 while dating the trigger correctly to February 1930. On the trigger: "On February 26, 1930, Masseria ordered the murder of an ally, Gaetano Reina" **[A]**. **Masseria** ordered it; attributing it to Luciano is supported by no source checked and is dropped.

**The two killings.** Masseria is shot dead at the Nuova Villa Tammaro, Coney Island, on 15 April 1931, moments after Luciano excuses himself from a long lunch **[A]**; who fired is attributed differently across sources, and the restaurant's street address is **not** confirmed anywhere reachable **[gap]**. Maranzano is stabbed and shot in his office in the New York Central Building (now the Helmsley Building), 230 Park Avenue, by men posing as tax agents on 10 September, on Luciano's order **[A]**.

**The Commission and the Waldorf.** The 1931 founding is reported by en.wikipedia and History.com, with an organizing meeting placed in Chicago, but venue, date and roster vary too much across reachable sources to carry as **[A]**. The Waldorf suite under the name **Charles Ross** rests on the biographies (Raab, Newark), not on a document seen here **[R]**.

## 5. Dewey (1935-1936)

**Title and appointing authority corrected.** "In June 1935, New York Governor Herbert H. Lehman appointed Dewey, a United States Attorney, as a special prosecutor to combat organized crime" **[A]**. A **US Attorney**, not a District Attorney, and **Lehman** appointed him; an earlier draft got both wrong.

**Carter, the raids, Hot Springs.** "Dewey's assistant district attorney, Eunice Carter, led an investigation that connected Luciano to this prostitution network" **[A]**. On 2 February 1936 roughly 200 brothels are raided, 16 men and 87 women arrested **[A]**. He flees to Hot Springs after a warning **[R]** and is arrested there 3 April on 90 counts of compulsory prostitution **[A]**; the lodging is not fixed anywhere reachable **[gap]**.

**Trial venue corrected.** An earlier draft used **60 Centre Street**, which housed the **civil** and appellate terms of the state Supreme Court. Luciano was tried in the New York County **Court of General Sessions** before Judge Philip J. McCook, sitting in the **Criminal Courts Building on Centre Street**; the pin is moved and its coordinates are approximate **[R for the coordinates, A for the court]**. Trial opens 11 May 1936 with Dewey prosecuting in person; conviction on **62 counts** on 7 June; **30 to 50 years** on 18 June **[A]**.

**Trial quote, source corrected.** The "A&P stores ... chain stores ... crack the joint" fragments appear verbatim on the English Wikipedia article and are **witness testimony reporting Luciano's speech secondhand**, not his own courtroom statement. An earlier draft cited the raw trial record. The article's citation is **Robert Weldon Whalen, *Murder, Inc., and the Moral Life* (Fordham University Press, 2016), p. 114**. The transcript has **not** been seen and must not be cited. **Verification limit:** confirmed via raw wikitext; Whalen p. 114 could not be opened (Google Books HTTP 429).

**Intake corrected.** He goes to **Sing Sing** first and is moved to **Clinton** "later in 1936" **[A]**. An earlier draft skipped Sing Sing entirely.

## 6. Operation Underworld and the commutation (1942-1946)

**The arrangement, contested at its own source.** In 1942 the Navy, through **Meyer Lansky** and the lawyer Moses Polakoff, negotiates a wartime arrangement with the imprisoned Luciano covering waterfront security and, reportedly, aid for the invasion of Sicily **[A]**. En.wikipedia "Operation Underworld" then splits the claim: labour-peace cooperation with **Commander Charles Haffenden** is documented, but **no evidence ever tied Axis agents to the loss of the Normandie**, and postwar Axis records claimed no such sabotage operation existed **[R for the sabotage narrative; A for the cooperation and the commutation]**.

**Transfers and two coordinate corrections.** Moved to **Great Meadow, Comstock**, 1942 **[A]**. Clinton is **44.7236 / -73.7208**, not -73.7476; Great Meadow is **43.4575 / -73.4331**, not 43.3459 / -73.3907, an error of roughly 13 km. The ONI base is **[R]**: the Third Naval District ran the liaison from lower Manhattan, conventionally 90 Church Street, and the meeting rooms are not detailed anywhere reachable.

**Commutation and departure.** Governor **Thomas Dewey**, the man who convicted him, commutes on condition of deportation, 3 January 1946; Ellis Island 2 February; ship sails from Brooklyn 10 February **[A]**. The pier is **[R]**: en.wikipedia says only "On February 10, Luciano's ship sailed from Brooklyn for Italy", naming **neither vessel nor pier**, so Bush Terminal and the *Laura Keene* are reconstruction. An earlier draft tagged this **[A]** while conceding both were unconfirmed.

**Arrival, and a manufactured contradiction removed.** 28 February 1946, Naples, after a 17-day voyage **[A]**. An earlier draft claimed secondary accounts place the 1946 landfall at **Genoa** and left the discrepancy "live". No source checked does this: Genoa is the **April 1947** arrival from Cuba, and two voyages were being collided.

## 7. Havana (1946-1947)

**Route.** Cuba on **29 October 1946**, via Venezuela and Brazil, landing at **Camagüey [A]**; the Miramar residence is **[R]**.

**The Conference, opening date corrected.** "The Havana Conference convened on **December 20, 1946**"; held at the Hotel Nacional "during the week of December 22" and running "a little more than a week" **[A]**. An earlier draft used 22 December as the opening. The attendee list, the **Frank Sinatra** engagement as cover, and the vote to kill **Benjamin "Bugsy" Siegel** over the Flamingo overruns are confirmed; the killing was postponed until the Flamingo opened, and Siegel died 20 June 1947 **[A]**.

**Lansky's role, and two sums that must not merge.** Lansky organizes the conference at Luciano's direction and had arranged Luciano's roughly **$150,000** stake in the Hotel Nacional, in which Batista held a silent share **[A: "Havana Conference"]**. Separately, Lansky delivered **half a million dollars** to Batista, who "was guaranteed US$3- or US$5 million annually" **[A: "Lucky Luciano"]**. An earlier draft ran the two together. Luciano's *personal* ownership stake in any specific casino, as against Lansky's documented management role, is asserted more firmly in popular accounts than in any record reachable here **[R]**; **Cuban casino licensing records were not accessible [gap]**.

**Hotel Nacional coordinates, three wrong values found.** Earlier drafts carried **23.1431 / -82.3625** (Centro Habana), **23.1441 / -82.4207** (Miramar), and an interlock value with a dropped minus sign. En.wikipedia gives **23.14306 / -82.38056** (Calle 21 y O, Vedado). The canonical corpus pin in `meyer_lansky.journey.json` is **23.1431 / -82.3806**, agreeing to four decimals, and is inherited byte-identical.

**The Genovese quote, source corrected.** The "There is no Boss of Bosses" reply is **confirmed verbatim** on the English Wikipedia article, in the Havana passage on the private meeting with Vito Genovese. The footnote is **not** Gosch and Hammer: the wikitext reads `<ref>English, p. 28</ref>`, i.e. **T. J. English, *Havana Nocturne* (Harper, 2008), p. 28**. An earlier draft attributed it to *The Last Testament of Lucky Luciano* and built a caveat about that book's authenticity around a source not cited here at all. **Verification limit:** English p. 28 could not be opened (HTTP 429), and whether English in turn draws on Gosch and Hammer is **unverified and not asserted**.

**Expulsion, Genoa, Palermo.** Anslinger pressures Cuba by halting pharmaceutical shipments 21 February 1947; Cuba announces deportation within 48 hours on 23 February **[A]**. The vessel is **inconsistently reported [gap]**. He is arrested on arrival at Genoa, 11 April **[A]**, and later detained at Palermo; an earlier draft asserted a documented Genoa-to-Sicily transfer, which en.wikipedia does not describe, so the two are kept separate. Ucciardone coordinates corrected to **38.1328 / 13.3578** (it.wikipedia), about 4 km from the earlier pin. "The last time Luciano is jailed anywhere" is **false** and dropped: Rome, July 1949.

## 8. The Italian exile (1948-1962)

**Residence, conflict resolved toward the sourced pin.** Two drafts placed his Naples home in **Posillipo** (40.8144 / 14.1937) and on **Via Tasso** (40.8422 / 14.2472), roughly 4 km apart. It.wikipedia states he "si trasferì definitivamente in via Tasso" and opened "un negozio di elettrodomestici ed articoli sanitari" **[A]**, so Via Tasso wins. Both drafts' coordinates also miss the street itself, which climbs west of Corso Vittorio Emanuele around **40.834 / 14.228**; that value is used. The house number is not in the source **[gap]**. Igea Lissoni, the Milanese ballerina, lives with him from 1948, is never married to him, and dies of breast cancer in **1959 [A]**.

**Rome and the police measures.** Photographed on the Via Veneto **[A]**; arrested July 1949 on suspicion of narcotics trafficking, released without charges **[A]**; passport revoked 1952 **[A]**. Questioned at the Naples Questura 9 June 1951 **[R]** and restricted to Naples under **soggiorno obbligato** 1 November 1954 **[R]**; both rest on summary, not on an Italian police document seen here.

**Palermo 1957, tag corrected to [R].** History.com asserts Luciano "convened a meeting in Palermo between Italian and American mafiosi". The October 1957 gathering at the **Grand Hotel et des Palmes** is well attested as an event; its status as a **single formal summit with an agreed agenda**, and Luciano's role in convening it, are **disputed among historians**. An earlier draft tagged it **[A]** in one lens and **[R]** in another; it is **[R]** in both here.

**Death.** Heart attack at **Naples-Capodichino Airport**, 26 January 1962, aged 64, going to meet the producer **Martin Gosch [A]**. Accounts differ on whether he was still alive when reached **[R]**.

**Funeral and burial.** Three days later, 300 people attend a service at **Santissima Trinità** in Naples, the body drawn through the streets by eight black plumed horses **[A: it.wikipedia]**. With US permission the body flies to New York and is buried at **St. John Cemetery**, Middle Village, Queens; **more than 2,000 mourners** attend and **Carlo Gambino** gives the eulogy **[A]**. Coordinates corrected to **40.7150 / -73.8669**; an earlier draft carried **73.8604 without a minus sign**, putting the grave in the Bay of Bengal. Mausoleum, section and inscription are not in the source; a Find a Grave check was **blocked by HTTP 403 [gap]**.

## 9. Afterlife

**1975:** *The Last Testament of Lucky Luciano*, the as-told-to memoir by Gosch and Hammer, is published; the New York Times reports **before publication** that it quotes Luciano describing events occurring **after his death [A]**. **Contradiction flagged:** en.wikipedia dates first publication 1975, it.wikipedia dates the Italian edition **1974**, a year before the English original it translates. Not resolved.

Also **[A]**: Rosi's film *Lucky Luciano* with Gian Maria Volonté (1973); Jackie Collins's Lucky Santangelo (1981-2015); *Time*'s 1998 list of the century's 20 most influential "builders and titans"; *Boardwalk Empire* (2010-2014) and *Lansky* (2021), in a screen cycle beginning with *Marked Woman* (1937).
