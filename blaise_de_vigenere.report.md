# Blaise de Vigenère (1523-1596): research report

*Slug: `blaise_de_vigenere`. Compiled 2026-08-24 from the verified research pool. Companion: `blaise_de_vigenere.journey.json`, 8 segments, 43 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition, carried by the sources without a document behind it. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated; gaps are printed as gaps.

**The problem this subject presents.** Vigenère is famous for a cipher he did not invent, under a name attached to it two and a half centuries after his death, while the cipher he did invent, the 1586 plaintext autokey, is barely known outside specialist historiography. Nearly every popular source is wrong in the same direction, and the biography underneath is thin, encyclopedia-derived, and full of dates everyone repeats and nobody documents.

---

## 1. Birth and the Bourbonnais (1523)

- Born at **Saint-Pourçain-sur-Sioule**, Bourbonnais, **5 April 1523** **[A: en.wikipedia; corroborated by fr.wikipedia and BnF/INHA authority records]**. **Gap:** no baptismal record located. The accepted scholarly date, agreed across sources and cited to nothing. `date_confidence: traditional`.
- The landmark is the **église Sainte-Croix**, former priory, now parish church **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. No claim that the baptism happened there. **Coordinate corrected:** dossier 46.3159/3.2871 was ~900 m off; the church geo tag is **46.3079, 3.2902**.

## 2. Paris, schools, first employment (c. 1535-1545)

- Sent to Paris at about twelve, c. **1535**; at the **University of Paris** at about fourteen, c. **1537**, leaving after roughly three years with **no degree** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Ages, not dates; months and days defaulted.
- **CONTRADICTION, flagged, unresolved.** fr.wikipedia's infobox names the **Collège de Coqueret**, with Turnèbe and Dorat. Anachronistic: Dorat became principal there only "vers 1547", and Turnèbe became royal reader in Greek in 1547. The Coqueret article never mentions Vigenère, and on fr.wikipedia the college is only an unsourced infobox field. **Downgraded [A] to [R]** and moved: any Turnèbe/Dorat contact belongs to the post-1562 period.
- Household of **Gilbert Bayard**, first secretary to Francis I, **1539** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** address unknown; canonical Paris pin used and declared.
- **1543**, a campaign against Imperial forces **[R: fr.wikipedia tradition, uncorroborated]**. **Not staged as a stop:** no place, unit, or engagement is recoverable, so a coordinate would be invention.
- **1545**, junior secretary to **Louis Adhémar de Monteil, comte de Grignan**, at the **Diet of Worms** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Coordinate corrected:** dossier 49.6329/8.3667 was ~580 m off; Worms Cathedral is **49.6303, 8.3597**. **Framing corrected:** the 1545 Reichstag sat in the city, not in the Dom, and the campa says so.

## 3. The unrecorded years (1545-1547)

fr.wikipedia: "Allemagne et Hollande jusqu'en 1547" **[R]**. No town, patron, itinerary, or correspondent. **Placeholder decision:** the dossier used Brussels (50.8466/4.3528), which is in Brabant, Habsburg Netherlands but not Holland, misstating the source's own word. The stop is relabelled **"Holland, the two years with no record"** at **52.0705, 4.3007**, with `date_confidence` saying plainly that this is a placeholder for a gap and not a verified stop.

## 4. Nevers and the first Italian sojourn (1547-1562)

- **1547**, on Francis I's death, secretary to **François Ier de Clèves, duke of Nevers**, at about twenty-four **[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia same year]**. **Coordinate corrected:** Palais ducal de Nevers is **46.9883, 3.1582** (dossier 46.9886/3.1602). **Caveat kept:** the palace article does not name François Ier as resident, and residence versus travel with the household is unestablished.
- **c. 1549**, first Italian trip, chiefly **Rome** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **CONTRADICTION on duration:** en.wikipedia three to four years, fr.wikipedia two.
- **Tournon patronage hypothesis [R].** Maurice Sarazin's suggestion that Cardinal François de Tournon protected this sojourn is corroborated by neither Wikipedia nor cryptomuseum, and no appointment record exists. **Not used.**
- **CONTRADICTION on Venice and Florence, left standing.** Two independently fetched summaries of the *same* en.wikipedia article disagreed on whether those visits belong to 1549-53 or 1566-69 **[R]**. Both cities get a stop, placed in the first sojourn only to order the segment, each `date_confidence` stating the open question. Both are weakly attested: no business, patron, or correspondent attached.
- **1562**, François II de Clèves dies, ending the direct male line; Vigenère resumes intensive study **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 5. Greek, Hebrew, Christian Kabbalah (1562-c. 1566)

- After 1562 he deepens Greek and Hebrew with **Adrien Turnèbe** and **Jean Dorat** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
  - **Window bounded, correcting an open-ended dossier claim.** Turnèbe left the Greek-language chair in 1562 and **died 12 June 1565**, so any post-1562 association spans **1562-1565 at most**. Dorat's is not so bounded, and sources vary between calling him teacher and recording only an association.
  - **Anachronism corrected.** The dossier placed these at "Collège Royal" 48.8478/2.3447. The Collège de France site is **48.8489, 2.3456**, and **the Collège Royal had no building of its own in Vigenère's lifetime**: the royal readers lectured in borrowed halls, and the college dates from 1610. The campa states this rather than pretending a building.
- Hebrew with **Gilbert Génébrard**, Benedictine Hebraist, later archbishop of Aix, and association with **Guy Le Fèvre de La Boderie** of the Antwerp Polyglot milieu **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no Paris address located for either.

## 6. The Roman embassy (1566-c. 1569)

- Sent by **Catherine de Médicis and Charles IX** as embassy secretary under ambassador de Tournon, about three years **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia confirms 1566-1569]**.
  - **Name and dates corrected.** fr.wikipedia's *Liste des ambassadeurs* gives **Just II de Tournon**, ambassador **1566-1568**, succeeded by Charles d'Angennes. So the form is *Just II*, and Vigenère's stay **outlasts the embassy by about a year**. The file uses the corrected name, ends the Tournon material in 1568, and declares the overrun.
  - **Coordinate corrected.** The dossier put the embassy at 41.9029/12.4534, which is St Peter's. It was never at the Vatican, and **no sixteenth-century French ambassadorial residence in Rome is documented** (Palazzo Farnese only from 1635). The canonical Rome pin **41.8933, 12.4828** is used and labelled a placeholder.
- **December 1566**, the covert Polish overture: Vigenère to test verbally an approach from a secretary of **Sigismund II Augustus** about a succession favouring **Henri, duke of Anjou**, without disclosing his authorization **[R: dossier synthesis of Catherine's December 1566 correspondence, cross-checked only against the confirmed embassy dates]**. The instruction was not independently located.
- **Italian cipher exposure [R, narrowed].** The dossier left this floating between both residences. musicologie.org places his discovery of **Della Porta's** cryptography at the papal court during the post-Nevers mission, and *De furtivis literarum notis* was printed in **1563**, after the first sojourn ended, so it cannot belong to 1549-53. Dated 1567, with the reasoning in `date_confidence`.
- **Autokey formation, c. 1567-1568 [R].** Conventional dating from the embassy dates and the treatise's composition history. **No dated document fixes it.**

## 7. Paris and the working decades (1570-1588)

- **24 July 1570**, marries **Marie Varé** at forty-seven; ends the itinerant career; later titled **secretary of the King's Chamber** under Henri III **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia confirms date and title]**. **Gap:** no parish church located, and Marie Varé enters the record on her wedding day and never reappears in anything consulted.
- **The thousand-livre pension to the poor of Paris.** Tagged [A] in the dossier; **downgraded here to [A/R]**, since neither wiki cites a register, act, or witness. **Correction:** the dossier's "of which he gives the first year's income" is **in neither source** and is dropped; fr.wikipedia has him offering the annual pension, en.wikipedia the income, and neither limits it to a first year.
- **1573**, the two Polish books, **Paris, Jean Richer** **[A: en.wikipedia works list]**. He never travelled to Poland; the works are compiled. **Coordinate corrected:** dossier 48.8566/2.3466 sits by the Île de la Cité; Richer's shop was in the rue Saint-Jean-de-Latran, ≈ **48.849, 2.346**.
- **1576**, *La somptueuse et magnifique entrée...*, on **Henri III's 1574 entry into Mantua** **[A for the publication; R for his presence]**. **Stop-ownership ruling applied:** his presence is not established, so **no Mantua stop is made**. The book sits at its Paris imprint, and the campa says the book exists and the eyewitness does not.
- **1577**, Chalkokondyles, *L'Histoire de la décadence de l'Empire grec...*, **Paris, Nicolas Chesneau** **[A: BnF catalogue and musicologie.org independently]**. **Coordinate corrected:** Chesneau's shop, rue Saint-Jacques, "au Chesne verd", ≈ **48.848, 2.344**.
- **1578**, Philostratus, *Les images ou tableaux de platte peinture*, **Paris, Nicolas Chesneau** **[A: musicologie.org]**. **Correction:** the dossier gave the first edition to L'Angelier; it is Chesneau's, and only the **enlarged 1597** is L'Angelier's. en.wikipedia **omits 1578 entirely**, so musicologie.org carries this alone.
- **1578**, *Traicté des comètes* **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **1582** (expanded 1589), annotated **Caesar**; **1583**, **Livy**; **1585**, **Villehardouin** in parallel old and modern French **[A: dossier bibliography, consistent with the 1580s chronology]**.
- **1586**, **Abel L'Angelier** prints the ***Traicté des chiffres, ou Secretes manieres d'escrire***, and within it the **plaintext autokey**, presented before the court of Henri III, **distinct from the repeating-key cipher later misattributed to him** **[A: en.wikipedia, biography and cipher article]**. The single most important corrective fact in the file.
- **1588**, *Le Psaultier de David torné en prose mesurée, ou vers libres* **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 8. Late life and death (1593-1596)

- **July 1593**, a letter documents continued service to **Louis de Gonzague, duke of Nevers**, and the tutoring of the duke's son **[A: en.wikipedia biography]**. **INTERNAL CONTRADICTION IN THE POOL, resolved for the chronology lens:** the geography lens tagged it **[R]** as a dossier-only lead, the chronology lens **[A]**, and the verification note says the downgrade is wrong because the biography does document the letter. **The file uses [A]**. **Coordinate ruling:** the letter does not place him at Nevers, so the stop uses his Paris pin, not the dossier's ducal-palace guess.
- **1595**, *Prières et oraisons*, sources named by Vigenère himself as the Zohar, the Sefer ha-Bahir, the Midrash Tehillim, and other little-known works **[A: Encyclopaedia Judaica via encyclopedia.com, quoting him]**, likely via **Guillaume Postel's** Zohar.
- **19 February 1596**, dies in Paris, traditionally of throat cancer, buried at **Saint-Étienne-du-Mont** **[A for date, place, church: en.wikipedia and fr.wikipedia agree]**. The cause is retrospective; no medical record located. **COORDINATE CONTRADICTION IN THE POOL:** the geography lens gave the church as 48.8462/2.3477, the afterlife lens 48.8462/2.3488, three times. The file uses 2.3488 and states the disagreement; neither was re-verified against a geo tag.

## 9. Afterlife

- **1597**, revised and enlarged **Philostratus**, Paris, Abel Langelier, posthumous **[A: en.wikipedia works list]**.
- ***Traicté du feu et du sel*, DATE CONTESTED AND LEFT CONTESTED.** en.wikipedia says "First ed., 1608" but gives the catalogued imprint as **Paris: Abel Langelier, 1618** (then Rouen 1642); musicologie.org lists **only** "Paris, Vve A. L'Angelier, 1618". **L'Angelier died in 1610**, so an 1618 Paris printing is his widow's: two imprints, not a typo. **Only 1618 is bibliographically attested**, so the stop is dated 1618 with the disagreement in `date_confidence`. The "trouvé parmy ses papiers après son decés" framing is authentic, from the title page.
- **1649**, English translation, London: Richard Cotes, trans. Edward Stephens **[A: full text via Gutenberg 47365]**. **CONTRADICTION:** some listings render the title *A Discovery of Fire and Salt*; the Gutenberg text reads **A Discovrse**, which the file uses.
- **1613**, his cousin **Claude Duret** cites his cryptographic work **[A: encyclopedia.com]**: the reputation predates the nineteenth-century eponym. Not given a stop, being Duret's engagement, not Vigenère's travel. **1976**, Garland's English *Images* **[A: musicologie.org]**, folded into the 1578 campa.
- **1989, David Rouach. CORRECTED; the dossier had this wrong twice.** fr.wikipedia: "C'est l'ethnologue David Rouach qui va, en 1989, percer la clé de fonctionnement de cette table." Rouach is an **ethnologist**, he **worked out how the tserouf table functions**, and he did it in **his own book *Les Talismans***. He did **not** edit Vigenère and did **not** publish the complete tables. Cited in `suggested_refs` on the 1586 tserouf stop; not staged as a scene.
- **The eponym.** The cipher now called Vigenère's was **misattributed in the nineteenth century**; specialists following **Kahn's *The Codebreakers*** credit the repeating-key system to **Bellaso (1553)** and recognize the **1586 autokey** as his own advance **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Vigenère cipher']**. A correction spread over decades, not an event.
- **Namesake.** The **cité scolaire** of Saint-Pourçain bears his name **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Coordinate corrected:** dossier 46.3167/3.3333 is ~3.4 km outside the town, apparently a rounded 46°19′N 3°20′E; the commune is **46.3083, 3.29**. **Gap:** the naming is **undated**, so the stop's date is an ordering device and says so.
- **Two gaps.** Whether the tomb survives, and whether any marker inside Saint-Étienne-du-Mont names him, is documented by no consulted source: do not assert lost, do not assert intact. And no film, biopic, or documentary could be confirmed; his media presence is cipher-explainer content, most of it repeating the misattribution.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Vigenère is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing was silently deleted. Two relations in the pool are not rooted in his own life; both are kept and their direction named.

1. **FORWARD. Charles Babbage, London, 1854.** `charles_babbage.journey.json` already carries a stop titled "London, the Vigenere cipher broken", covering the Thwaites challenge over a passage from *The Tempest*. The relation is real but runs Babbage to Vigenère: a new hash in an old block. **Babbage is named in no campa here**, appearing only in `suggested_refs` and `sources` on the 1586 and 1863 stops. Naming him in prose would need a listing in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, not an agent's judgement.
2. **FORWARD. The nineteenth- and twentieth-century reception** (Kasiski 1863, Lewis Carroll's 1868 "The Alphabet Cipher", *Scientific American* 1917, Friedman's kappa test). **One stop was kept**, "Paris, the cipher that was never his", dated 1863 at the imprint pin of Vigenère's own book, because its subject is the name on that book rather than a place he went; it names only **Kasiski** and **Bellaso**, neither of whom has an atlas file. Carroll and *Scientific American* were **not** staged, being other people's writing. **Friedman is dropped on a date error as well as on direction:** the dossier said 1920, but the index of coincidence appeared in **1922** (Riverbank Publication No. 22), and the cipher article says only "during the 1920s". A fuller reception segment would be a forward grant to record, not assume.

**Backward relations used, and legal.** Two travellers are named in campas because Vigenère engaged with them directly, one-directionally, in his lifetime. **`julius_caesar`:** he translated and annotated the *Commentaires* (1582, expanded 1589); Caesar's shifted alphabet already has its Bibracte stop (46.92306, 4.0375), **not restaged**, only cited. **`johannes_trithemius`:** the *tabula recta* of the *Polygraphia* (Oppenheim, 1518) is explicitly drawn on by the 1586 treatise; named in that campa, Oppenheim stop cited, not restaged.

**Relations deliberately not used.** **`al_kindi`:** the lineage is real, polyalphabetic systems exist to defeat the frequency analysis he formalized c. 843, but the pool calls it "conceptual, not personal" and there is **no evidence Vigenère read him**. **`adi_shamir`, `alan_turing`:** QUEUE.md's cipher wing is corpus structure, not biography. **Trithemius's Rome 1609 stop:** shared geography thirteen years after Vigenère's death.

**Absent from the atlas, recorded for the queue.** No file exists for **Catherine de Médicis** (who directed the 1566 mission), **Henri III**, **Della Porta**, **Bellaso**, **Cardano**, or **Nostradamus**. QUEUE.md carries **Alberti**, **Bazeries**, **Scherbius**, **Rejewski**, **Agrippa**, unresearched. Nothing was written into another traveller's file.
