# Francisco Gianotti (1881-1967), research report
*2026-08-15. Slug: `francisco_gianotti`. Francesco Terenzio Gianotti, born Lanzo Torinese (Piedmont), died Buenos Aires; architect of the Galería Güemes (Florida 165) and the Confitería del Molino (Av. Rivadavia 1801), the two buildings by which Art Nouveau entered the Argentine capital at commercial scale.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, single-source claim, or contradicted claim carried forward unresolved. Contradictions are flagged and *not* adjudicated. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**Evidence base:** three Wikipedia biographies (ES, EN, IT) and the dedicated Spanish articles on the two buildings, cross-checked against four further articles and re-geocoded against Nominatim. The one monograph (CEDODAL 2000) was **not** consulted directly; see Sources.

---

## 1. Piedmont and the schools, 1881-1906

- Born **4 April 1881, Lanzo Torinese**, province of Turin, to **Luiggi Gianotti** and **Bernardina Monaco**. **[A: es/en.wikipedia, *Francisco Gianotti*]**
- **Graduated architect from the Accademia Albertina di Belle Arti, Turin, 1904**, alongside his brother **Giovanni Battista (Juan Bautista) Gianotti**. **[A: en.wikipedia; it.wikipedia]** Month and day are not given. Coordinate corrected: Nominatim puts the academy (Via Accademia Albertina 6) at **45.06758, 7.68973**; the gathered draft's 45.0654, 7.6919 was ~300 m off.
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved, the Melani problem.** ES and IT say Gianotti studied under **Alfredo Melani (1859-1928)**, and it.wikipedia's Melani article lists Gianotti among his students. That same article has Melani appointed professor at the **Scuola superiore d'arte applicata all'industria in Milan** in 1883 and later its director, with **no post in Turin**. The Turin degree and the Melani tutelage cannot both have happened at the Accademia Albertina. Either Gianotti studied with Melani in Milan at some unrecorded point, or the tutelage claim is an error propagated between language editions. **The journey file states only the attested Turin degree and carries Melani in `suggested_refs` with the conflict named. [R]**
- **Postgraduate year in Brussels, 1905**, with his brother. **[A: es.wikipedia]** No address, atelier, institution or month recorded. **GAP.**
  - **CORRECTION applied.** es.wikipedia frames the year as an encounter with "Victor Horta and Henri van de Velde". Horta was in Brussels with his major works standing. **Van de Velde was not**: per en.wikipedia he settled in **Weimar in 1899**. Personal contact in Brussels in 1905 is not possible as stated. The campa was written as exposure to built work, not to persons; van de Velde appears only in `suggested_refs`. **[R for van de Velde; A for the year]**
- **Pavilions for the Esposizione Internazionale del Sempione, Milan, 1906**, his first commission of size. **[A: es/en.wikipedia]** Which pavilions, and whether his brother collaborated, is not itemised. **GAP.**

## 2. The crossing and the Centennial, 1909-1911

- **Arrives Buenos Aires 1909**; works as **draftsman for Arturo Prins and Oscar Ranzenhofer**. **[A: es/en.wikipedia]**
- **The crossing is unattested in every particular.** No source names a ship, a port of embarkation, a date, or a class of passage. The journey file carries a **Genoa** stop at the corpus's canonical pin (**44.4056, 8.9463**), tagged in `date_confidence` as reconstructed. Justification: Genoa is the standard northern-Italian emigrant port for the River Plate and recurs across the corpus for this crossing (Collivadino's family, Palanti's 1909 departure, Troiani's 1910). **[R]**
- **Arrival point corrected and demoted.** The draft placed the landing at -34.5854, -58.365 tagged [A]. Both were wrong: Nominatim puts the **Hotel de Inmigrantes** at **-34.59246, -58.36818**, ~830 m away, and no source names any dock or ship. The file uses **-34.5925, -58.3682** and declares the landing reconstructed. **[R for the point; A for the year]**
- **1910, Exposición Internacional del Centenario: assembly of the Italian pavilion, with Mario Palanti.** **[A: es/en.wikipedia]**
  - **CORRECTION.** The pavilion was **designed by Gaetano Moretti**; the role of Gianotti and Palanti was its *armado*, its erection. The draft omitted Moretti and so implied authorship. The campa credits Moretti.
  - **GAP, site unpinned.** es.wikipedia, *Exposición Internacional del Centenario*, distributes the sections across Plaza San Martín, El Rosedal, La Rural, Palacio Unzué and Regimiento 1 de Patricios, and does **not** place an Italian pavilion in Parque 3 de Febrero as the draft did; the only Italian pavilion it describes sits in the railways section. Rather than invent a site, the stop takes the **canonical Buenos Aires pin (-34.6037, -58.3816)** with the unresolved location declared. **[A event / R location]**
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved, the return to Italy.** es.wikipedia records a brief return after the Centennial; en.wikipedia has him on the pavilion and says nothing of a departure. Neither gives destination, purpose or duration. The stop is kept, pinned at Lanzo Torinese, with the single-source status declared. **[R]**
- **1911: opens his own studio at Calle Paraná 972.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** Coordinate corrected: the draft's -34.6032, -58.3908 landed near Congreso, ~700 m off; Nominatim gives **-34.59755, -58.38834**.
- **1911: begins collaborating with Mario Palanti and Virginio Colombo** on Art Nouveau work for the Argentine bourgeoisie. **[A: it.wikipedia]** Colombo has no journey file; Palanti does, and the collaboration is a real professional relation, which is why he is named in campa.

## 3. The two buildings, 1912-1916

### Galería Güemes, Florida 165
- **Design competition won September 1912.** **[A: es.wikipedia, *Galería Güemes*]**
- **Construction begins 10 March 1913.** **[A]**
- **Opens 15 December 1915**, after 32 months. **[A]** Cost **15 million pesos fuertes**; **87 m** including tower; **14 floors, 14 elevators**; described as the first Argentine building constructed entirely in reinforced concrete and by many as the country's first skyscraper. Verbatim: *"el primer edificio de dicho país construido enteramente en hormigón armado."*
- **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED at the level of the gathered brief.** The brief that opened this research carried **1912** for the Güemes, which no source corroborates as a construction or completion date: 1912 is the **competition** year. The dedicated article's precise dates are what the journey file uses, and the 1912 figure is recorded here rather than adjudicated away.
- **Coordinate corrected, four times over.** The afterlife draft carried -34.6015, -58.3752 on four separate stops, ~530 m north of the building. Nominatim gives **-34.60630, -58.37438**, matching the es.wikipedia infobox (-34.606128, -58.374953). All Güemes stops now use **-34.6063, -58.3744**.

### Confitería del Molino, Av. Rivadavia 1801
- **Commissioned by Cayetano Brenna; construction begins 1912**, in reinforced concrete, by the **Compañía General de Obras Públicas**, with an order of more than 150 m² of stained glass. **[A: es.wikipedia, *Confitería del Molino*]**
- **Inaugurated 9 July 1916**, some sections still incomplete. **[A]** Verbatim: *"El edificio actual fue inaugurado el 9 de julio de 1916."* Biographical pages list 1916 without a day; the dedicated article gives the date. A difference of granularity, not a contradiction.
- **Recorded visitors across its active decades [A]:** presidents **Agustín P. Justo, José Félix Uriburu, Marcelo T. de Alvear**; **Prince Umberto II of Italy**; the tenors **Tito Schipa** and **Beniamino Gigli**; **Carlos Gardel**; and, omitted from the gathered draft and restored here, the writers **Leopoldo Lugones** and **Roberto Arlt**, and **Eva Perón**.
  - **Method note.** These visits are *their* movements. They appear only as a list inside the campa for the building Gianotti built, never staged as scenes of their own.

### Attributed in the same window, weakly
- **Mercado Central, Salta, 1914.** **[R: en.wikipedia only]** No drawing, contract or photograph; not corroborated by ES or IT. The stop is kept with the weakness declared in `date_confidence`. Apart from Mendoza (1933) it is the only work in the catalogue far from Buenos Aires.

## 4. Eclecticism, family, middle decades, 1917-1933

- **Pabellón Circus, Av. Corrientes 480/496, 1917, demolished.** **[A: es.wikipedia works list]** Verbatim: *"Pabellón Circus, Avenida Corrientes 480/496 (1917, DEMOLIDO)."* Demolition date unknown. **GAP.** Coordinate corrected: the draft sat ~530 m south near Av. de Mayo; Nominatim gives **-34.60334, -58.37352**.
- **c. 1918: stylistic turn** from Art Nouveau to *"un eclettismo tra neoclassicismo e revival moreschi."* **[A: it.wikipedia]** A periodisation, not a dated event, and flagged as such.
- **1919: marries Amelia Vercesi; three sons.** **[A: es.wikipedia]**
  - **CORRECTION to the draft's gap statement.** The sons are named: **Aníbal, Pedro and Juan Francisco**. What is genuinely absent is their **birth dates**. **GAP, narrowed.**
  - **CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** The 1959 commission is credited to a son called **Luis Pedro**, a name not matching the trio. Either "Pedro" and "Luis Pedro" are one man under two forms, or the sources disagree about the household. Nothing consulted reconciles them; both are carried without adjudication.
- **Residencia Brach, Posadas 1064, 1923.** **[A: es.wikipedia works list]** Coordinate corrected to **-34.59021, -58.38246** (draft ~250 m off).
- **Palacio Italia América, Av. Roque Sáenz Peña 660.** **[A for the building; R for the year]**
  - **DUPLICATE MERGED.** The gathered geography carried two stops 15 m apart for one building: **Diagonal Norte *is* Avenida Presidente Roque Sáenz Peña**, the same street under its popular name. The split was an artefact of the ES/EN naming divergence.
  - **CONTRADICTION, not resolved:** es.wikipedia gives *"Palacio Italia América, propiedad de la Compañía de Navegación Italia-América, Av. Roque Sáenz Peña 660"* **with no year**; en.wikipedia gives number **622**, year **1927**, and the firm as the "Italian-American Navigation Company". One building, two street numbers, one undated. The file uses 660 with 1927 flagged single-source.
- **Monumento a la Bandera, Rosario, 1928, first prize claimed.** **[R, CONTRADICTED]** es.wikipedia's Gianotti article credits him with a competition win; the monument built was won by **Ángel Guido and Alejandro Bustillo** in a competition of **1939-1940** and inaugurated in **1957**. Nothing by Gianotti was built there. Either an earlier round came to nothing, or the credit is an error carried forward. **Not resolved; carried as an explicitly contradicted stop.**
- **1930: the Molino damaged in the coup d'état.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** The source gives no month or day for the damage. **Reopened 12 October 1931.** **[A]**
- **Edificio Shaffhausen, Reconquista 336/356, 1932.** **[A: es/en.wikipedia]** Coordinate corrected to **-34.60398, -58.37248** (draft ~310 m off).
- **Gran Hotel Plaza, Mendoza, remodelled 1933.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** The last commission recorded outside Buenos Aires.

## 5. Late life, 1942-1967

- **1942: begins operating a marble quarry in Córdoba province.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** The site within the province is unspecified; the file uses the provincial-capital placeholder and says so. **GAP.**
- **1959: final project, Arenales 2080, with his son Luis Pedro.** **[A: es.wikipedia]**
  - **CORRECTION.** en.wikipedia calls the last project unnamed and the draft repeated that gap. It is not a gap. Coordinate corrected to **-34.59456, -58.39686** (draft ~460 m off).
- **Dies Buenos Aires, 13 February 1967**, aged 85, a naturalised Argentine. **[A: es/it.wikipedia]**
- **GAP, material:** **no funeral notice, cemetery or grave** was found in any source consulted. Nothing was assumed.
- **GAP:** no legend or folkloric tradition attaches to **Gianotti the man**, as distinct from the abundant folklore of his two buildings.
- **GAP:** the brother **Giovanni Battista Gianotti** has no biography beyond the 1904 degree and the 1905 Brussels year. Whether he emigrated, practised, or stayed in Italy is unrecorded here.

## 6. Afterlife of the buildings, 1967 to the present

Everything here belongs to the buildings rather than the man, and the journey file keeps it in two closing segments for that reason.

**Galería Güemes**
- **1967: fire destroys the entrance to the first dome.** **[A: es.wikipedia, *Galería General Güemes*]** The year coincides with Gianotti's death. **No source states whether the fire fell before or after 13 February, nor whether he knew of it.** Nothing is claimed.
- **10 September 1971: major fire damages the Florida façade.** **[A]** The draft dated both fires only as "1967/1971"; the second is precisely datable.
- **1987: the Museo de la Ciudad cites the gallery as *"Testimonio vivo de la memoria ciudadana."*** **[A]**
  - **CORRECTION.** The draft called this a "Cultural Heritage" designation. It is a **museum's honorific citation**, not a heritage listing. A separate *Patrimonio Cultural de la Ciudad* designation dated 2000 appears in a fetched summary of that article but **not in its current wikitext**, and is therefore **not asserted**.
- **2004-2008: restoration under architect Reinaldo Lemos; mirador reopens April 2010.** **[A]**
- **Literary afterlife [A: es.wikipedia]:** **Antoine de Saint-Exupéry** wrote *Vuelo nocturno* while living in a sixth-floor apartment of the gallery (no precise dates given); **Julio Cortázar's** story *El otro cielo* draws on the gallery; **Carlos Gardel** performed in its basement theatre on **27 February 1917**.
  - **Method note, applied strictly.** These are *their* stops, not Gianotti's. All three are carried only as `suggested_refs` on Gianotti's own stop for the building he designed. Cortázar's story is a genuine one-directional relation to the object, real and citable, but no campa in this file dramatises another traveler's visit.

**Confitería del Molino**
- **4 March 1996:** Madonna films a music video in the building during the *Evita* production. **[A]** The draft gave only "1996".
- **24 January 1997:** closes. **[A]**
- **24 October 1997:** declared **Monumento Histórico Nacional**. **[A]**
- **12 November 2014:** the Chamber of Deputies approves the expropriation law. **[A]** The draft gave only "November 2014".
- **Present:** closed to the general public, accessible by reserved guided tour, restoration aiming at a working confectionery, museum and cultural centre, with recovery of historical recipes. **[R: undated present-tense claim in the source]** The file's last stop declares that its date is placed provisionally, since the source gives no year.

## 7. Interlocks with the existing corpus

Only one relation is direct enough to name a corpus traveler inside a campa:

- **mario_palanti** **[A]**. Palanti and Gianotti **assembled the Italian pavilion of the 1910 Centennial together** (es/en.wikipedia), and it.wikipedia has Gianotti collaborating with Palanti and Virginio Colombo from **1911**. This is contact between two named men on the same job, not co-location. Named in two campas: the Centennial pavilion and the Paraná studio.

Everything else the interlock lens surfaced is **shared geography or shared decade** and is deliberately not name-dropped, per the standing rule that the map already shows co-location: Troiani's lamps and Querol's monument on the Plaza del Congreso opposite the Molino, Rodin's *Thinker* in that plaza since 1907, Christophersen's Bolsa de Comercio of 1913-1916, Collivadino at the academy in the same years, Buschiazzo of the previous immigrant generation, Torcuato de Alvear's Avenida de Mayo, Orlandi and Rodríguez Ortega in the same queue cluster, Grasset with an Argentina connection flagged unconfirmed. None of them met Gianotti, employed him, taught him, or wrote about his work in anything reachable.

**Virginio Colombo** is a genuine attested collaborator (it.wikipedia, 1911) and *is* named in a campa, but has **no journey file**, so the connection is queue-level and he is not counted as an interlock.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Buenos Aires **-34.6037, -58.3816** (as used by pio_collivadino, mario_palanti, alejandro_christophersen, troiano_troiani, torcuato_de_alvear, juan_antonio_buschiazzo) for the Centennial, the marriage, the monograph and the death; Genoa **44.4056, 8.9463** for the crossing.
