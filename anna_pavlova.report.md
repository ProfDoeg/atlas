# Anna Pavlova (1881-1931): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 from a verified research pool: Wikipedia ("Anna Pavlova", "The Dying Swan", "Pavlova (dessert)", "Palace Theatre, London"), the Encyclopedia.com biography, Dandré 1932 / Money 1982 / de Mille 1963 all at second hand, OpenStreetMap geocodes, and the atlas's own journey files for the interlock layer.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated.

---

## 1. Origin and the imperial school (1881-1899)

**Birth. [A]** Born 31 January 1881 Julian, 12 February Gregorian (Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com), to the laundress Lyubov Fyodorovna Pavlova; the surname comes from a soldier, Matvey Pavlov, briefly married to her mother.

**Birthplace. [R], contradiction flagged.** Wikipedia places the birth in Saint Petersburg proper (the Preobrazhensky Regiment hospital). Keith Money and most reference works give the village of **Ligovo** outside the city. The journey uses a Saint Petersburg city coordinate and carries the dispute in `date_confidence`.

**Paternity. [R]** Disputed by biographers. Tradition names a Jewish banker, **Lazar Polyakov**, as her biological father; contested, and resting on no primary documentation reachable here.

**The Sleeping Beauty. [A, with an age discrepancy]** Taken by her mother to Petipa's *The Sleeping Beauty* at the Mariinsky in 1890, which decides her on dancing (Wikipedia). The pool gave her age as "about eight"; Wikipedia gives nine. Born February 1881, she turned nine in February 1890, and the production premiered January 1890. Corrected to nine.

**Admission. [A]** Rejected at a first audition as too young and frail; admitted to the Imperial Theatrical School at ten, in 1891, first on stage in Petipa's *Un conte de fées* (Wikipedia). One pool entry said "eight-year-old Pavlova is admitted", against the pool's own chronology; corrected.

**School years. [R]** The nicknames ("the broom", "la petite sauvage"), the mockery of her arched feet, and the teachers Johansson, Gerdt, Legat and Cecchetti are biographical tradition relayed by Wikipedia with no documentary anchor. **[A]** Entry into **Ekaterina Vazem's classe de perfection** in 1898.

**The Gerdt speech. [R, and a source correction.]** The rebuke beginning "... leave acrobatics to others" is blockquoted in Wikipedia after her attempt at Legnani's fouettés and attributed to Pavel Gerdt. **It carries no reference tag there at all.** The pool credited it to Yona Zeldis McDonough's 2016 *Lilith* article; that footnote attaches only to the separate "God gives talent" line, and the attribution is deleted. The original begins mid-sentence with an ellipsis, so the pool's capitalised "Leave" misrepresents it; the ellipsis is restored. Chronicling America returns zero hits for the phrases. **A famous quotation with no located source.**

## 2. The Mariinsky years (1899-1908)

**Debut. [A]** Graduates at eighteen, enters the Imperial Ballet directly as **coryphée**, debuting in Gerdt's *Les Dryades prétendues*. Encyclopedia.com dates it **19 September 1899**. **Contradiction flagged:** one pool entry dated the debut to April 1899, conflating the spring graduation with the September debut.

**Ranks. [A]** *Danseuse* 1902; *première danseuse* 1905; **prima ballerina** in 1906 after a *Giselle* (Wikipedia). No date for that performance in any consulted source.

**The Duncan evening. [A, from the atlas's own files]** On **28 January 1905** Isadora Duncan dances barefoot in Saint Petersburg before a house including Diaghilev, Fokine, Kschessinska and Pavlova (`isadora_duncan.journey.json`, which names Pavlova directly). `sergei_diaghilev.journey.json` carries the same evening but places it only in the weeks after Bloody Sunday. **The discrepancy is noted in `date_confidence`.**

**The Dying Swan. [R]; the pool's [A] tag was not supportable.** Wikipedia contradicts *itself*: the body text gives a first presentation in **1905**, the infobox premiere field **1907**. The standard reference date for the first documented performance is **22 December 1907**, at a charity gala in the Hall of the Noble Assembly; 1905 is Fokine's recollected creation date. The journey dates the stop 1907-12-22, re-tags it **[R]** and states both years. The "single rehearsal" detail is from **Fokine's own memoir**, self-reported.

**Moscow. [A]** Encyclopedia.com: she took the Imperial Ballet on its first tour to Moscow in 1907. **[R]** Guest appearances at the **Bolshoi** are inferred from the exchange of principals between the two imperial stages; no dated appearance confirmed.

## 3. Paris, London, New York (1909-1912)

**Châtelet. [A]** She dances Diaghilev's inaugural Saison Russe at the Théâtre du Châtelet, opening **19 May 1909**; `sergei_diaghilev.journey.json` names Karsavina, Pavlova and Nijinsky as the three principals of the season. Coordinates 48.8583, 2.347, inherited byte-identical from the Diaghilev, Roerich, Nijinsky and Satie files.

**The Firebird refusal. [A in substance, misdated in the pool.]** Wikipedia confirms she was cast to lead Fokine's *The Firebird*, refused over Stravinsky's score, and that the role went to Karsavina. The pool anchored this to 1909; the ballet premiered **25 June 1910**, so the refusal belongs to the 1909-1910 preparation period. Dated 1910-01-01 with the range in `date_confidence`.

**American debut. [A]** Metropolitan Opera House, New York, **February 1910**, partnered by Mikhail Mordkin (Encyclopedia.com). Exact night not given.

**Palace Theatre, London. [R], two problems in the pool.** (1) The pool had her opening "with her own company" in March 1910, anachronistic against its own dating of the company's formation. (2) Wikipedia confirms her as one of **Alfred Butt's** attractions but gives **no year**; the March 1910 date is unsourced and the engagement is usually placed in April 1910.

**The break with Diaghilev. [R], contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The pool gave **three** inconsistent accounts: 1911, 1910, and a March 1910 Palace opening already under her own management. Wikipedia gives no clean year. The stop is dated 1911-01-01 with the c.1910-1911 range in `date_confidence`.

**Company name. [Gap.]** The pool called the troupe "Ballets Russes de Anna Pavlova". **Not confirmable in any consulted source.** Wikipedia says only "her own company"; her United States billing was generally *Anna Pavlova and her Ballet Russe*. The formal name has been dropped.

**Ivy House. [A]** Resident at Ivy House, North End Road, Golders Green, from 1912 until her death, with an ornamental lake and pet swans (Wikipedia; English Heritage blue plaque). The pool carried **three Ivy House pins, all wrong** (section 11). OpenStreetMap lists the building as Ivy House School, closing the pool's question about the occupant.

## 4. The American circuit and the war (1912-1916)

**Tours. [A]** Almost annual coast-to-coast United States tours, 1912 through 1926 (Wikipedia). **[R]** Chicago and San Francisco are itinerary, not dated engagements. **The San Francisco pin was dropped**: the pool dated it "1910s-1920s" at the **Curran Theatre**, which opened in **1922**.

**St Louis. [A]** A last-minute 1914 engagement arranged by the impresario **Hattie B. Gooding** at a reported expense of **5,500 dollars** (Wikipedia, citing St Louis engagement records).

**Marriage. [A for the fact, [R] for the place.]** She married her manager **Victor Dandré** in 1914 and the marriage was kept private for years (Wikipedia). The place is given only as "probably London".

**The war. [A]** Her last visit to Russia falls in 1914; she is stranded in Germany, and abroad thereafter, at the outbreak (Encyclopedia.com). The journey pins this to Berlin as a stand-in; **the specific city is not documented**, and is flagged.

**The film. [A, date corrected.]** *The Dumb Girl of Portici*, Universal, directed by **Lois Weber and Phillips Smalley**, was shot in 1915 and **released 3 April 1916**, not 1915 as the pool had it. Her only feature.

**Abridged Sleeping Beauty. [A]** Mounted in New York in 1916 at roughly fifty minutes (Wikipedia). Venue not named in the sources.

## 5. South America (1917-1919)

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Wikipedia: "In 1918-1919, her company toured throughout South America." Encyclopedia.com gives **1917**, with Brazilian dates (**Bahia, Salvador**) in **1919**. The pool conceded the disagreement in one entry while tagging the tour [A] in another. **Re-tagged [R], given as a 1917-1919 span.**

**Buenos Aires. [R]** Teatro Colón. Coordinates **-34.601, -58.3831**, inherited byte-identical from `vaslav_nijinsky`, `george_balanchine` and `victoria_ocampo` (the pool's geography entry gave -58.3832; the canonical pin wins).

**Lima. [A, upgraded; the pool's flagged gap can be closed.]** Wikipedia records that **Frederick Ashton** resolved to become a choreographer after seeing Pavlova dance **in Lima, Peru, at the age of thirteen**. Born 1904, so the performance falls around 1917: independent corroboration of both the city and the early dating, sitting unused in the pool.

**Rio de Janeiro, Montevideo, Santiago, Panama, Havana, Mexico City. [R, several dropped.]** All inference from "general accounts", no confirmed date or venue. Rio is kept as [R]; **the other five were dropped** rather than pinned on inference alone. Panama was described in the pool as "inferred from the logistics of interwar transcontinental touring", which is a route reconstruction, not a stop.

**Saint-Cloud. [A]** She supported Russian orphan girls displaced by the Revolution and Civil War, with a home for about fifteen of them near Saint-Cloud (Wikipedia). Exact date not given.

## 6. Asia and the Near East (1922-1923)

**Countries. [A]** Encyclopedia.com lists **Japan, China, India, Burma and Egypt**. **[R]** The venues within them are almost all inference. The journey keeps **Bombay, Rangoon, Tokyo, Giza** and drops **Colombo, Singapore, Shanghai, Manila**, which the pool itself called "inferred from standard Asian touring routes".

**Dating. [Contradiction flagged.]** The pool dates the Indian leg 1922-1923 in geography and the whole Asian tour to 1923 in chronology. Not reconciled; the journey states the ambiguity.

**Taj Mahal. [R, dropped.]** Repeated in popular accounts, confirmed against no itinerary, and unclear whether she performed or merely visited. Left off the map.

**Giza. [A]** The 1923 photograph at the pyramids is held in multiple archives (Getty, V&A Theatre Collections). The photograph is the attestation; the Egyptian dates around it are not documented here.

**Uday Shankar. [A]** She partnered **Uday Shankar** in *Krishna and Radha* (also *Radha and Krishna*), 1923, London (Wikipedia). Venue and dates not given.

## 7. Australasia, South Africa, and the dessert (1926-1929)

**Tour. [A]** South Africa, Australia and New Zealand (Encyclopedia.com). **Date correction:** the pool's geography entry dated Cape Town to **1928-1929**; Encyclopedia.com groups South Africa with the other two in **1926**, matching the pool's own chronology. Changed to 1926.

**Sydney and Perth. [R]** Venue and month are reconstruction. Cape Town's pin stays a city centroid because no venue is confirmed.

**The dessert. [A per recipe, [R] for the origin, unresolved.]** All from Wikipedia "Pavlova (dessert)":
- **11 November 1911**, *New Zealand Herald*: "Strawberries Pavlova", an ice or sorbet, not a meringue.
- **1922**, Australia: Emily Futter's "Meringue with Fruit Filling", a possible precursor.
- **1926**, Sydney: the Davis Gelatine Company publishes a "pavlova" that is a layered jelly.
- **1927**, New Zealand: the OED's earliest recipe explicitly under the name, still jelly-based.
- **1929**, New Zealand: Helen Leach's first recognisable pavlova *cake*.
- **1935**, Perth: the claim that Bert Sachse invented the dish at the Esplanade Hotel. The *claim* is attested (Wikipedia, citing *The Beverley Times*); the invention is contested tradition, no 1935 recipe or menu surviving.

**The dispute is live and is not adjudicated here.** Symons argues against a singular birthplace at all; Leach counts at least 21 New Zealand recipes by 1940 and rebuts Sachse on the ground that the dish reaches Australian cookbooks only in the 1940s; the 2010 OED lists the origin as "Austral. and N.Z."

## 8. Death (1931)

**The journey north. [A, minimally.]** Wikipedia says only: "While traveling from Paris to The Hague, Pavlova became ill."

**The rail accident. [R], and a fabricated coordinate removed.** The derailment, the unheated carriage and the wet clothes are popular tradition, in **no** consulted source as documented fact. The pool pinned the incident at **50.4, 3.3** while conceding this was "an approximate placement only" for an event whose location is "a genuine gap in the record". **The pin is dropped.** The illness in transit is kept; the map point is not.

**Pleurisy and the refused operation. [A]** She fell seriously ill with pleurisy at The Hague; doctors advised an operation that would have ended her dancing, and she is said to have refused (Wikipedia).

**Death. [A, with a date contradiction flagged.]** At the **Hotel des Indes**, The Hague, twenty days short of her fiftieth birthday. Wikipedia gives **23 January 1931**, Encyclopedia.com **22 January**; unresolved, and carried in `date_confidence`.

**Last words. [A as text, [R] as testimony.]** "Get my 'Swan' costume ready." Confirmed **verbatim** against the Wikipedia wikitext, which cites Dandré 1932, p. 360 (footnote 33). **Source correction:** the pool cited this to Dandré, *Anna Pavlova: Her Life and Art*, and **no such book by Dandré exists**; that is Money's 1982 title. Dandré's is ***Anna Pavlova in Art & Life*** (1932), per OpenLibrary. **Page 360 was not read directly; the book is not in accessible full text.** Everything intimate in the record passes through Dandré, with no second witness behind him.

## 9. Afterlife

**Cremation. [A]** Golders Green Crematorium, ashes in the columbarium (crematorium records).

**Ballet shoes and the theft. [R], split from the [A] item.** The crematorium's record mentions **neither** the ballet shoes said to stand with the urn **nor** the reported 1980s theft and recovery. The pool conceded the shoe detail was unverifiable while still tagging the item [A]. Split and re-tagged.

**Dandré. [A]** Died 5 February 1944, cremated at Golders Green, ashes below Pavlova's.

**Repatriation. [A that it happened, unresolved in detail.]** In 2001 Moscow's mayor **Yury Luzhkov** and Russian cultural figures pushed to move the ashes to **Novodevichy**, flight reportedly booked for **14 March 2001**. Abandoned after permission was withdrawn on the Russian side and no evidence surfaced that repatriation had been her wish. Who withdrew it, and why, is reported inconsistently.

**Victoria Palace Theatre. [A]** A gilded statue was set on the cupola at the theatre's 1911 opening, before any engagement of hers there, removed for safety in the Second World War and lost, replica restored 2006.

**The algal genus *Pavlova*. [A]** Named in her honour, its motion in water "positively balletic" (Wikipedia). The pool dates the naming to 1952; the journey says twentieth century.

## 10. The interlock layer

Named in the journey prose for a **direct relation**, never for shared geography:

- **`isadora_duncan`**: Pavlova is in the house on 28 January 1905 when Duncan dances barefoot in Saint Petersburg; the Duncan file names her among the watchers.
- **`sergei_diaghilev`**: she dances his inaugural 1909 Paris season, then breaks with the company. Named at the Duncan evening, at the Châtelet and at the break.
- **`vaslav_nijinsky`**: one of the three principals of the 1909 Châtelet season with her and Karsavina. Named only there.
- **`tchaikovsky`**: the 1890 *Sleeping Beauty* she watched as a child is his score and is the reason she became a dancer; she later danced Aurora in her own abridged staging. One-directional, and real.

**Deliberately not named**, though the lens surfaced them, because the only link is a shared building or institution at another date: `george_balanchine` (Imperial Theatre School, 1913), `nicholas_roerich` (same 1909 premiere, different production), `coco_chanel` and `erik_satie` (Ballets Russes after she left), `victoria_ocampo` (Teatro Colón, 1934), `joseph_pilates` (a generation later). The map already shows co-location.
