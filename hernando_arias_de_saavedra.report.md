# Hernando Arias de Saavedra (Hernandarias), c. 1561/1564 - 1634
*Research report, 2026-08-11. Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `hernando_arias_de_saavedra`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference no cited document carries. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and are **not** adjudicated.

The subject in one line: the first man born in the Americas to govern a Spanish colonial province, who held the joint governorship of Río de la Plata and Paraguay three times, released the cattle that seeded the pastoral economy of the Banda Oriental, and wrote the letter in which the ancestors of the gaucho are first named.

---

## 1. Birth and lineage (1561 or 1564)

- Born at Asunción, son of **Martín Suárez de Toledo** and **María de Sanabria**, of the Paraguayan founding generation. **[A: es.wikipedia; ecured.cu; Real Academia de la Historia, *Diccionario Biográfico Español*]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** English Wikipedia: **10 September 1561**. Spanish Wikipedia infobox and the Real Academia: **10 September 1564**. The body text of the Spanish article and EcuRed: **1561**. The discrepancy propagates to his age at death (about 70 per the Real Academia, 72 or 73 per English Wikipedia). The journey file carries this in `date_confidence`, never in prose.
- The second son, per English Wikipedia. **[A]** Uncorroborated elsewhere. **[gap]**

## 2. The Garay connection (1580-1582)

- 1582: marries **Jerónima de Garay y Becerra Contreras Mendoza**, daughter of **Juan de Garay**. **[A: es.wikipedia; ecured.cu]**
- **CORRECTION.** An intermediate note in the research pool called Garay an "uncle-by-marriage." He was the **father-in-law**. Recorded so the error is not repeated downstream.
- Traditional accounts place the young Hernandarias among the settlers of Garay's permanent founding of Buenos Aires, 11 June 1580. **[R]** No muster roll naming him was located; the Spanish narrative does not put him there at all, and his first documented post is Cuyo, 1583. The stop is carried explicitly as tradition.
- The sources give the marriage place only as "the Río de la Plata region." **[gap]** The journey file pins the canonical provincial seat and says so in `date_confidence`.
- Three daughters: **Jerónima, Isabel, María**. **[A: ecured.cu]** Birth years not given. **[gap]**
- The wife appears as **Jerónima de Contreras** (English Wikipedia) and as **Jerónima de Garay y Becerra Contreras Mendoza** (Spanish sources). One woman under two renderings; glossed, not silently merged.

## 3. The frontier offices (1583-1596)

- **1583.** Corregidor of Cuyo, succeeding **Alonso de Miranda**, in the trans-Andean district then attached to Chile. First independent command. **[A: es.wikipedia; Real Academia]** Replaced by **Juan Martínez Palomeque** in 1586. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **14 April 1585.** Founding of **Concepción del Bermejo** (Concepción de Buena Esperanza); first *alcalde ordinario*. **[A: es.wikipedia; ecured.cu; Real Academia]**
  - **CORRECTION.** An intermediate note put the site at -27.5 / -60.9 and called it never located. Spanish Wikipedia, "Concepción de Buena Esperanza," gives 26°08′33.43″S 60°18′26.28″O (**-26.1426 / -60.3073**), El Destierro, Chaco, about 60 km west of the Bermejo. The site **was** found: identified 1943 by Alfredo Martinet (the "Ruinas del Kilómetro 75" on old Route 95), confirmed by excavation in 1970 by Eldo Morresi. Destruction 1631-1632 is correct. **[A]**
- **March to 7 April 1588.** First interim *teniente de gobernador* of Corrientes. **[A: es.wikipedia; Real Academia]**
  - **CORRECTION.** Not a town "founded a decade earlier": Corrientes was founded **3 April 1588** by Juan de Torres de Vera y Aragón, the same month, and Hernandarias is named among the participants in the founding. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Corrientes (ciudad)"]**
- **13 July 1592.** *Teniente de gobernador* of Asunción. **[A: es.wikipedia; ecured.cu]**
  - **CORRECTION.** Not "under Juan Ramírez de Velasco." The succession list runs Juan Torres de Vera y Aragón to 1592, **Hernandarias 1592-1594**, Fernando de Zárate 1594-1595; Ramírez de Velasco governs only 1595-1596. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Gobernación del Río de la Plata y del Paraguay"]**
- **1593.** First interim governorship of the joint province. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **1594.** *Teniente de gobernador* of Santa Fe, then on its original site. **[A: Real Academia]**
  - **CORRECTION.** Santa Fe la Vieja is **not** at -31.7139 / -60.6167 (the modern Santa Fe / Paraná area). Cayastá is 31°12′00″S 60°10′00″O; the archaeological park sits about 1.5 km south of the town centre, **-31.19 / -60.15**, some 82 km north of the modern city. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Cayastá"]** The 1660-61 relocation south is correct.

## 4. The three governorships (1596-1618)

| Term | Begins | Ends |
|---|---|---|
| First | 7 December 1596 | 8 July 1599 |
| Second | 1 August 1602 | 8 July 1609 |
| Third | 1615 (day disputed) | 17 May 1618 |

- **First titular governor of Río de la Plata and Paraguay born in Spanish America**, that is, the first criollo governor in the Spanish colonies. **[A: es.wikipedia and ecured.cu both phrase it so]** No source consulted tests the claim against other provinces; this report does not either. **[gap]**
- **CORRECTION, chronology.** An intermediate note had him elected after the death of **Diego Rodríguez Valdés y de la Banda**. Impossible: Valdés governs **after** the first term (1599-1600) and dies at Santa Fe **20 December 1600**; **Francés de Beaumont** holds the interim 1600-1602; that chain leads to the **second** term, from 1 August 1602. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Gobernación del Río de la Plata y del Paraguay"]**
- **CORRECTION, confirmation.** "Confirmed by Philip II" is dropped, not repaired: Philip II died **13 September 1598**, mid-term, and no source consulted documents a confirmation. **[gap]**
- **CORRECTION, end of second term.** 8 July 1609, not 22 July. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **CORRECTION, third term.** English Wikipedia: appointed 7 September 1614, assumes 29 May 1615. Spanish Wikipedia, verbatim: "3 de mayo de 1615 recibió nuevamente título de gobernador." **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.**
- **CORRECTION, end of third term.** 17 May 1618, the day **Diego de Góngora** took office, not 1617 (which is the date of the appointment cédula, 16 December). **[A: es.wikipedia, "Diego de Góngora"]**

### Government of the second term

- **1603.** Reforms indigenous labour law against *mita* and *encomienda* abuses. **[A: es.wikipedia; ecured.cu]** English Wikipedia gives royal approval by Philip III in **1603**, EcuRed in **1608**. Compatible if read as successive approvals, but no source says so. **[flagged]**
- **1611.** The ordinances of **Francisco de Alfaro** regulate the reductions. **[A: en.wikipedia]** The 1608 Guairá reductions are **[A]**; framing them as their groundwork is **[R]**.
- **1603.** Orders the Buenos Aires chapel demolished, calling it "muy vieja e indecente." **[A: es.wikipedia, "Catedral de Buenos Aires"]**
  - **CORRECTION.** He did not "relocate the cathedral," as an intermediate note had it. He ordered a demolition.
- Founds the first primary schools of Buenos Aires; builds kilns to replace adobe with brick and tile; works against contraband through the port. **[A: en.wikipedia; ecured.cu, which dates the building program to 1610]**
- **1610.** Granted the title **Protector de los Naturales** with two islands. **[A: es.wikipedia]**

### The cathedral of 1616-1618

- **CORRECTION, claim narrowed.** An intermediate note had him "personally leading the reconstruction" with the carpenter Pascual Ramírez. Spanish Wikipedia, "Catedral de Buenos Aires": in **1616** the Paraguayan-timber roof neared collapse and the parish moved to San Francisco; the new building went up **January to July 1618**, Ramírez costing the masonry at 1,100 pesos; and the article states explicitly that Hernandarias did **not** carry out that work, his term having ended 17 May 1618. **[A]**
- The quotation "I have built them not just with my work and perseverance, but at the expense of my estate" is carried **only** with its English Wikipedia attribution, not as documented of this building. **[A on the attribution; R on the referent]**

## 5. The expeditions

- **1604. Patagonia, the Ciudad de los Césares.** A ride roughly a thousand kilometres south, a search for the legendary city and a survey of river navigability, a capture and an escape. **[R]** English Wikipedia flags it as thinly documented; the Real Academia says the captors were **Tehuelches**, English Wikipedia says **Mapuche**. **Contradiction flagged.**
  - **CORRECTION, tagging.** The geography lens tagged this **[A]** while the chronology lens tagged it **[R]**. It is **[R]**. The coordinate -40.8 / -63 is an unsourced approximation of the Río Negro area and is presented as approximate.
- **18 March 1607. The corsairs.** After English privateers seize vessels off Buenos Aires he has a defensive tower, later a larger fort, built at the Matanza-Riachuelo mouth, the district later called Vuelta de Rocha. **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**
- **1607. Banda Oriental, about six months.** He crosses the Santa Lucía country seeking timber, coal and cane and reaches the Queguay. Spanish Wikipedia, verbatim: "a su regreso, cruzó a su margen derecha, hacia el este del río Queguay." His report to Philip III praises the land for cattle. **[A: Real Academia; es.wikipedia, "Río Queguay Grande"]**
  - **CORRECTION.** The Queguay mouth is 32°08′21″S 58°08′30″O (**-32.1392 / -58.1417**), not -32.15 / -57.75, about 37 km east of the truth.
  - **CORRECTION.** The "six months surveying" belongs to **1607**, not to the 1611 cattle release where an intermediate note attached it.
- **1608. The Guairá.** Jesuit and Franciscan reductions established in the northern frontier province. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
  - **CORRECTION.** Villa Rica del Espíritu Santo stood from late 1593 until its destruction in 1632 at 23°54′57″S 51°58′42″O (**-23.9158 / -51.9783**), present-day Fênix, Paraná, Brazil, not at -24.15 / -54.68. An error of about 280 km. **[A: es.wikipedia]**

## 6. The cattle (1611, 1617)

- **1611.** Spanish Wikipedia, "Isla del Vizcaíno," verbatim: "En 1611 Hernandarias trasladó a las islas de Lobos y del Vizcaíno 50 vacas y algunos toros desde su estancia en Santa Fe." **[A]**
  - **CORRECTION, twice.** The animals went to **islands**, not to the mainland Banda Oriental; and to **both** Lobos and Vizcaíno, not to Lobos alone.
  - **CORRECTION, coordinate.** Isla del Vizcaíno lies at the mouth of the Río Negro in the Río Uruguay, 33°21′36″S 58°22′55″O (**-33.36 / -58.382**), Río Negro department, Uruguay. This Isla de Lobos is **not** the seal island off Punta del Este (-35.02 / -54.88).
- **1617.** The operation repeated: fifty more on Isla de Lobos, fifty on **Isla San Gabriel** off Colonia. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- Credited in Uruguayan and Argentine national mythology as the founding moment of River Plate ranching. **[A as a claim widely made; R as a causal account]** No source consulted separates these herds from earlier escaped stock. **[gap]**

## 7. The mozos perdidos (1617)

- In a letter of **1617** he names **"mozos perdidos,"** vagrant criollos and mestizos living free off wild cattle. **Emilio Coni** identifies this as the first documented reference to the population that becomes the gaucho. **[A: the attribution to Coni is carried at second hand through search summaries; the letter itself was not reached]**
  - **CORRECTION.** The geography lens dated the phrase "c. 1607-1611"; the afterlife lens in the same pool had **1617**, which multiple Argentine historiographical sources carry. The two lenses contradicted each other; 1617 is used.
  - The **exact wording and archival reference remain unverified**. **[gap]** Only the short form "mozos perdidos" is quoted.
- A summary on buenosaires.gob.ar describes the proto-gaucho population as already present in his era, managing *ganado cimarrón* in the vaquerías and known as *mozos perdidos*, *mozos vagabundos*, *mancebos de la tierra*. **[R: the page quotes an unattributed period description]**
- The word **"gaucho"** first appears in an official document only in **1771**, at Maldonado. **[A: buenosaires.gob.ar]** The 154-year interval is the ethnogenesis, and is presented as an interval rather than collapsed.

## 8. The division of the province (1617-1620)

- Royal cédula of **16 December 1617** authorises the split; formalised **1618**, implemented by **1620**: Buenos Aires at the river mouth, Paraguay seated at Asunción with Santiago de Jerez, Villa Rica and Ciudad Real. **[A: es.wikipedia; ecured.cu; en.wikipedia]** That the border outlives the colonial period is **[A]** as stated by English Wikipedia, **[R]** as a claim about the modern frontier.

## 9. Last years, death, burial (1618-1634)

- Retires largely to Santa Fe. **[R]** The chronology of these sixteen years is undocumented in anything consulted. **[gap]**
- Dies **21 December 1634** at Santa Fe la Vieja, aged about 70 (Real Academia) or 72 to 73 (English Wikipedia, year only), the difference following from the unresolved birth year. **[A]**
- Buried with his wife in the church of the **Convento de San Francisco**. **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]**
- **5 October 1642.** The widow donates a Virgin image still kept by the Franciscans at Santa Fe. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **1660.** Santa Fe la Vieja is abandoned and rebuilt some 80 km south; the old site, church and tomb go under the ground. **[A: histories of Santa Fe la Vieja / Cayastá, via search summary]**

## 10. Afterlife

- **From 1949.** **Agustín Zapata Gollán** rediscovers Santa Fe la Vieja, uncovering the church of San Francisco and the sepulchre holding Hernandarias's remains. The site is now the **Parque Arqueológico Santa Fe la Vieja**. **[A: search summary]**
- **Whether the remains are still in that tomb or were later moved is not established by any source consulted.** **[gap]**
- **8 September 1976.** The bronze by **Antonio Pena** on the Montevideo Rambla is decreed a national Historic Monument. **[A: Uruguayan heritage listing, via search summary]**
- **Villa Hernandarias, Entre Ríos**, carries his name and a bust. **[A]** Its coordinate is **-31.2167 / -59.9833**, not -31.9 (an error of about 76 km). **[A: es.wikipedia]** The claim that its coat of arms derives from a seal of the governor is **not** carried by the cited article and is softened to **[R]**.
- **Hernandarias, Alto Paraná, Paraguay**, about 335 km from Asunción, is named for him. **[A]** A monument and small museum in Asunción are reported on an unverified social-media reference alone. **[R]**
- The **subfluvial tunnel** between Santa Fe and Paraná was originally christened **Túnel Subfluvial Hernandarias**. **[A]** **CORRECTION:** renamed in **2001** to Raúl Uranga - Carlos Sylvestre Begnis; English Wikipedia's present tense is out of date. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **2001.** Walter Rela compiles, and the Paraguayan Embassy in Montevideo publishes, *Hernandarias criollo asunceno: estudio preliminar, cronología anotada y ordenamiento de cartas y memoriales al rey Felipe III y al Consejo de Indias, 1600-1625*. **[A: bibliographic record via search summary]** Not consulted here; the single most valuable unreached source. **[gap]**
- Some **13 handwritten pages** of Cabildo records dated **12 December 1598** in his hand, stolen from Paraguay's National Archive in the 1990s and offered at auction in New York, are repatriated with the help of the Manhattan District Attorney's office. **[A: news reports via search summary]** The year of return is not given. **[gap]**

The corpus already holds him: the census of real persons lists him as person 194, and `pedro_de_mendoza.journey.json` carries a stop "Hernandarias and the mozos perdidos" at **-32.5 / -58.2**, dated 1617-01-01. That pin is inherited byte-identical. So are the canonical Buenos Aires pin (**-34.6037 / -58.3816**), the canonical Asunción pin (**-25.2637 / -57.5759**), and the Garay refoundation pin (**-34.6083 / -58.3712**), which the geography lens independently produced for the fort and Plaza de Mayo.

Travelers already in the atlas whose paths cross this one do so through the gaucho lineage, never through personal contact, and none of their files names Hernandarias: `pedro_de_mendoza` (Garay, the refoundation, the loosed horses), `juan_manuel_de_rosas` (branding on the Salado frontier), `sarmiento` (*Facundo* and the rastreador), `martin_miguel_de_guemes` and `san_martin` (the División Infernal de Gauchos de Línea, the northern frontier handed over to hold), `alberto_gerchunoff` (*Los gauchos judíos*, the river-country colonies), `tschiffely` (gaucho dress in New York and at Recoleta), `borges` (the 1951 lecture against demanding "gauchos and local color").
