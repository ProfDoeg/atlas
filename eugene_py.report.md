# Eugène Py (Eugenio Py), 1859-1924, research report

*Compiled 2026-08-13. Carcassonne-born cameraman of Casa Lepage, Buenos Aires; the man traditionally credited with the first film shot in Argentina.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, with the source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim that survives only as one encyclopedia's summary of a list. Contradictions between sources are flagged and left open. Where a source could not be reached, it is named together with the reason.

**Standing warning about the source base.** Everything documented about Py descends from three Wikipedia articles (es. *Eugenio Py*, en. *Eugène Py*, fr. *Eugène Py*) plus related Spanish articles listed in section 10. The English article is a **stub**; its full wikitext was retrieved and it contains **no mention** of Casa Lepage, Lepage, Glücksmann, Bolívar street, the laboratory, the Salón Nacional, the Chile trip, or the 1907 sound experiments. Several of those facts had been cited to it during gathering. **Every such citation has been re-pointed to Spanish Wikipedia, which does carry them.** This is the single largest correction in this dossier: the English stub is not a witness.

---

## 1. Carcassonne and the crossing (1859 - late 1880s)

- **Born 19 May 1859 at Carcassonne, France.** **[A: es./en./fr. Wikipedia, all three in agreement on day, month, year and place.]** This is the most solidly attested single fact in his biography, precisely because three independent-looking encyclopedias converge on it.
- **Childhood, schooling, trade, family.** **Gap.** Nothing whatever was found. No source consulted names his parents, his education, or how he came by any knowledge of optics or chemistry. The blank runs from 1859 to his appearance behind a counter in Buenos Aires.
- **Emigration to Buenos Aires "in the late 1880s".** **[A: en./fr. Wikipedia]**, but with **no year, no ship, no port of embarkation, no arrival date**. The sources give a decade and stop.
- **Bordeaux, Lisbon, Rio de Janeiro as points on the crossing.** **[R]**, pure route reconstruction from the ordinary pattern of French steamer service to the Río de la Plata in the 1880s. **No Py-specific passenger record was located in any source.** These stops keep the ocean from being crossed in a single unexplained jump; each is tagged as reconstruction in `date_confidence` and must not be read back as evidence.

## 2. Casa Lepage, Bolívar 375 (before 1896 - 1908)

- **The shop.** Casa Lepage, a photographic-supply business owned by the Belgian **Henri Lepage**, at **"calle Bolívar N° 375, casi esquina Belgrano"**. **[A: es. Wikipedia, *Eugenio Py*, quoted verbatim.]**
  - **Coordinate correction.** Three different pins were carried for this address during gathering (-34.6082/-58.3714, -34.6112/-58.3722, -34.6088/-58.3713), all wrong by 155 m to 450 m. Nominatim resolves **Bolívar 375, Monserrat, CABA to -34.61219, -58.37339**. The journey file uses **-34.6122, -58.3734** at every Casa Lepage stop.
  - **Name variant flagged, not resolved.** es. *Eugenio Py* calls the owner **Henri Lepage**; es. *Max Glücksmann* calls him **Enrique Lepage**. Almost certainly the same man under a hispanized forename, but the two spellings are left standing.
- **When Py joined the firm.** **Gap, and a correction.** **No source gives any year.** The "c. 1888" and "c. 1890" readings proposed during gathering and tagged **[A]** were **inferred from Glücksmann's arrival date**, not from Py's, and are demoted to **[R]**. All the record supports is **"before 1896"**. The journey file dates the stop 1890-01-01 purely as an ordering device and says so.
- **Max Glücksmann.** Arrives in Buenos Aires around 1890 and starts at Casa Lepage as a photography assistant at roughly fifteen. **[A: es. Wikipedia, *Max Glücksmann*.]** He and Py are **co-workers, not partners and not rivals** **[A]**; their overlap runs to at least **1908**, not to 1897 as gathering supposed.
- **The 1896 Lumière screening.** Py attends the **first cinematograph projection in Argentina**, at the **Teatro Odeón**, with Lepage and Glücksmann. **[A: es./fr. Wikipedia.]** Date: **28 July 1896**. The specific day rested only on fr. Wikipedia while es. said "July 1896"; **es. *Cine de Argentina* independently gives "se hicieron una serie de presentaciones el 28 de julio de 1896 en el Teatro Odeón"**, so it can be stated firmly **[A]**, along with the organizers **Francisco Pastor and Eustaquio Pellicer** **[A]**.
  - **Coordinate correction.** The Odeón stood at Esmeralda 367, near Corrientes: **-34.60399, -58.37800**, not the -34.6031/-58.3743 carried during gathering (~355 m east). Journey file uses **-34.6040, -58.3780**.
- **The Paris debut of the Cinématographe, December 1895.** **[R]**, general film history, carried in the pool only as an inference. Included as one stop at the corpus's canonical Paris pin because it is the origin of the apparatus Py sees seven months later; **no source places Py in Paris at any time**, and the journey file says so.

## 3. "La bandera argentina" (1897) and the priority dispute

- **The film.** Py shoots **17 metres**, using a **Gaumont camera** and **virgin film stock newly imported that year**. **[A: es./en./fr. Wikipedia.]** es. Wikipedia calls it **"posiblemente la primera película filmada en Argentina"**, i.e. *possibly*; en. and fr. drop the hedge.
- **What is in the film, and where it was shot.** **Correction to a false gap.** Gathering declared the filming site undocumented and pinned it to Casa Lepage. That is wrong: **es. *Cine de Argentina* records the subject as the Argentine flag on its pole at the Plaza de Mayo, before the Casa Rosada** **[A]**. The journey file pins **-34.6083, -58.3717**.
- **Priority dispute, flagged, not resolved.** es. and en. Wikipedia call *La bandera argentina* the first film shot in Argentina without qualification. **fr. Wikipedia notes that later research credits Federico Figner with three earlier Buenos Aires shorts.** **[R: the dispute itself is real; no dates for Figner's views were found in any source.]** Both versions are left standing in the journey file, in a stop devoted to the disagreement.
  - **Nationality correction.** fr. Wikipedia calls Figner "l'Allemand", and the gathering pass copied this into two lenses. **pt. Wikipedia, *Frederico Figner*: born Milevsko, Bohemia (now Czech Republic), 2 December 1866, an Austro-Hungarian subject of a Jewish family.** **[A]** He is **Bohemian / Austro-Hungarian, not German**; fr. Wikipedia's word is itself an error and is recorded here as one.
- **The laboratory.** Casa Lepage installs **the first complete film-processing laboratory in the country** to develop the footage. **[A: es. Wikipedia, "la Casa Lepage instaló el primer laboratorio completo para procesar el material".]** Previously cited to en. Wikipedia, which says nothing of the kind; **re-cited**.

## 4. The camera turned on the state (1900-1902)

- **Salón Nacional, 1900.** The first purpose-built cinema in Buenos Aires opens at **Maipú 471/479**, capacity **250**. **[A: es. Wikipedia.]** Previously cited to en. Wikipedia with a quoted sentence that **does not exist there**; re-cited. The gathering claim that the exact address "is not given in the sources consulted" is **false**. **Py's personal involvement in the hall is nowhere stated** and remains **unconfirmed [R]**. Coordinates corrected to **-34.6026, -58.3767**.
- **"Visita del Dr Campos Salles a Buenos Aires", 1900.** Py films the visit of Brazilian president **Manuel Ferraz de Campos Salles**; called the **first Argentine documentary**. **[A: en./fr. Wikipedia.]** The **camera position is not documented**; the journey file pins the Casa Rosada / Plaza de Mayo as the obvious site of a state reception and marks the inference. **Whether President Julio Argentino Roca appears in the footage is nowhere stated and is asserted nowhere here.**
- **"La Revista de la Escuadra Argentina", 1901.** **[A: en. Wikipedia, filmography list.]** Title and year only. Quay, vantage, survival of the print: all **gaps**. Journey pins Dársena Norte (-34.601, -58.366) as the plausible naval-review site, tagged as such.
- **"Visita del general Mitre al Museo Histórico", 1901.** **[A: en./fr. Wikipedia.]** The Museo Histórico Nacional had been at **Defensa 1600, San Telmo** since 1897 **[A: es. Wikipedia, *Museo Histórico Nacional*]**; coordinates -34.6284, -58.3691.
- **The 1902 Chile assignment.** **[A as to the trip, es. Wikipedia: "En 1902 Max Glücksmann decidió que Eugenio Py y un periodista español, Enrique Casellas, viajaran al país trasandino".]** Three corrections, since gathering cited this to en. Wikipedia, **which contains no Chile sentence at all**: the source is **es.**; Casellas is **Spanish**, not Argentine; and Py **did not decide to go, Glücksmann sent him**, so the framing is an assignment, not a journalistic initiative.
  - es. adds the diplomatic hook: the filming covered the intervention of **Sir Thomas Holdich**, commissioned by **Edward VII** to mediate the boundary arbitration **[A]**.
  - **Santiago itself is [R], not [A].** No source places Py in Santiago specifically; es. says only that the pair travelled "al país trasandino". The geography lens tagged this **[A]** while its own prose conceded the gap; **demoted**.
  - **Uspallata pass: [R]**, standard 1902 Mendoza-Santiago route, no Py itinerary located.

## 5. Sound, the sale, and the distribution empire (1907-1919)

- **Synchronized-sound experiments from 1907.** **[A: es. Wikipedia: "desde 1907 venía realizando las primeras experiencias de películas sonorizadas a partir de la sincronización fonográfica".]** Re-cited from en., which lacks the line. **No title, no surviving print, no venue.** Siting them at the Bolívar street workshop is **[R] inference**.
- **1908: Lepage sells to Glücksmann.** **[A: es. Wikipedia, *Eugenio Py* and *Max Glücksmann*.]** The quoted en. sentence used during gathering does not exist. **Py's employment status after the sale is entirely undocumented**; the assertion that "Py stays on as the technical man" is unsourced inference and has been **removed**, not softened.
- **Glücksmann's network.** Exhibition across **Buenos Aires, the interior, and Montevideo**, later **100 halls in Argentina, Chile, Uruguay and Paraguay**. **[A: es. Wikipedia, *Max Glücksmann*.]** **Py is never placed in Montevideo**; the stop is **[R]**, marking the reach of the business rather than a visit. **Coordinate correction:** Montevideo was carried as -34.9011/-56.212, about 4.3 km west of the centre (a longitude digit slip); corrected to **-34.9011, -56.1645**.
- **Grand Splendid, Avenida Santa Fe 1860, opened 14 May 1919.** **[A: es. Wikipedia, *Max Glücksmann*]**; the site had held the Teatro Nacional Norte and a carriage works.
  - **Internal contradiction flagged, not resolved.** es. calls the Grand Splendid **"la primera sala de cine de Argentina"** while the same encyclopedia calls the **Salón Nacional of 1900** "la primera sala cinematográfica de Buenos Aires", and also notes the Grand Splendid opened as a **theatre**. The journey campa states the collision instead of choosing. **Py is not recorded at the opening**; the stop is **[R]** as to his presence.

## 6. Fiction films: two disputed credits

- **"Amalia".** **Year correction: 1914, not 1912.** es. *Eugenio Py* says 1912; the dedicated article **es. *Amalia (película de 1914)*** documents a **premiere on 12 December 1914 at the Teatro Colón**, before **President Victorino de la Plaza** and his ministers **[A]**. The journey follows the dated premiere and flags the 1912 reading rather than deleting it.
  - **Role correction.** Py is **not** the producer and **not** the director. **Enrique García Velloso** directed and wrote; **"Eugenio Py" is credited under Fotografía** (probably alongside Enrique Lucchetti) **[A: es.]**.
  - **Cast correction.** The claim of "professional actors" is **wrong**: the cast was **over 80 members of Buenos Aires high society**, **Susana Larreta y Quintana** as Amalia, **José Miguens** as Rosas; produced by the **Asociación del Divino Rostro** to fund a chapel and a girls' school **[A: es.]**.
- **"Mariano Moreno y la Revolución de Mayo", 1915.** **[R as to Py.]** Year and García Velloso confirmed, but the film's own es. Wikipedia article credits **García Velloso as director and screenwriter**, heads the cast with **Pablo Podestá** (Moreno) and **José Podestá** (Saavedra), **names no cinematographer, and does not mention Py anywhere**. "Py creates" cannot stand. The journey file leaves the attribution open.
- **Career span.** fr. Wikipedia's implied **1897-1911** span is **not a sourced statement**: it is merely the range of the filmography list on that page, and it is contradicted by the verified 1914 *Amalia* credit. **Active career is stated here as c. 1897 - 1915 [R]**; the "contradiction between sources" framing is withdrawn, since the tension resolves in favour of the later end date.

## 7. The Santos-Dumont footage: date withdrawn

- **The claim.** Py films the aviator **Alberto Santos-Dumont**, in footage containing what is described as probably the **first "gag" in Argentine film history**. es. supplies the content: **a man stepped between Py and Santos Dumont, interrupting the filming, until he received a spit in the face**. **[A as to the anecdote; source: es./en./fr. Wikipedia.]**
- **The 1901 date is withdrawn.** **[R, and previously mis-tagged A.]** The en. wikitext places this sentence in a general paragraph about Py "recording history in the making", **not** in the 1901 filmography line; the year was inferred from adjacency. 1901 is also implausible: **Santos-Dumont was in Paris that year** (Deutsch prize, 19 October 1901). His es. biography **records no Argentine visit at all**, mentioning Argentina only via a **1916 photograph with Eduardo Bradley**, which, if the footage exists, points to **c. 1916**.
- **Open contradiction, left open.** Either Py filmed a man whose own biography does not record him being in the country, or the aviator's biography is incomplete. The journey file places the stop at **1916-01-01** with the uncertainty stated in `date_confidence`, and the campa says the year, the field and the occasion are all unrecorded.

## 8. Death and afterlife

- **Died 26 August 1924 at San Martín, Buenos Aires Province, aged 65.** **[A: es./en./fr. Wikipedia, all three in agreement.]** **Coordinate correction:** San Martín (Ciudad del Libertador General San Martín) is at **-34.57716, -58.53772**; the pins carried during gathering were 750 m to 1.0 km off. Journey file uses **-34.5772, -58.5377**.
- **Street, house, cause of death:** **gaps**, none given anywhere.
- **Burial: total gap.** **No source consulted names a cemetery, plot, tomb or marker.** Web search was exhausted in this session and the plausible guesses (Chacarita, Recoleta, a parish ground in San Martín) are **explicitly refused** rather than inserted.
- **The one monument.** The former **Casa Lepage building at Bolívar 375 still stands and carries a commemorative plaque** **[A: es. Wikipedia]**. **No date for the plaque is given anywhere**; the journey file dates that stop 1997-01-01 as a pure ordering device and says so.
- **No street named for him.** **[R, corrected.]** Do not conflate him with **Avenida Comodoro Py**, which honours **Comodoro Luis Py (Lluís Pi i Palà)**, born Barcelona 1819, died Buenos Aires 1884, who raised the flag at Cañadón Misioneros on 1 December 1878 asserting sovereignty over Patagonia **[A: es. *Luis Py*]**. The gathering pass called him "Juan Bautista Py"; **no such person exists** and the name is deleted. No journey stop, since no verified coordinate for the avenue was obtained.
- **Standard reference works.** **José Agustín Mahieu, *Breve historia del cine argentino* (1966)** and **Domingo Di Núbila, *Historia del cine argentino*, vol. I, *La época de oro* (1998)** **[A: es. Wikipedia bibliography]**. **No translation into any other language is indicated.**
- **Print survival, restoration, centenaries, retrospectives:** **all gaps.** Nothing consulted describes whether *La bandera argentina*, *Amalia* or any other Py film survives, is held anywhere, or has ever been restored or re-screened.
- **Carcassonne:** **gap.** No memorial, plaque, repatriation of remains or French-side commemoration was located.

## 9. Contradictions carried forward, unresolved

1. **First film in Argentina:** Py's *La bandera argentina* (es./en.) vs. Figner's earlier undated Buenos Aires shorts (fr.). es. itself hedges with "posiblemente".
2. **Figner's nationality:** fr. says German; pt. documents Bohemia / Austria-Hungary. **This dossier treats fr. as erroneous** and records the error rather than silently correcting it.
3. **"Amalia" 1912 (es. *Eugenio Py*) vs. 1914 (es. *Amalia (película de 1914)*, with a dated premiere).** Journey follows 1914.
4. **First cinema in Argentina:** Salón Nacional 1900 vs. Grand Splendid 1919, both called "first" by Spanish Wikipedia in different articles.
5. **Owner's name:** Henri Lepage (es. *Eugenio Py*) vs. Enrique Lepage (es. *Max Glücksmann*).
6. **Py's credit on "Mariano Moreno y la Revolución de Mayo":** asserted in career summaries, absent from the film's own record.
7. **Santos-Dumont in Argentina:** asserted by Py's biographies, absent from Santos Dumont's.
8. **Career end date:** fr.'s list stops at 1911; verified credits run to 1915.
