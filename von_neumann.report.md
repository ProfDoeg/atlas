# John von Neumann (1903-1957): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-04. Slug `von_neumann`; companion file `von_neumann.journey.json`, 7 segments, 44 stops, 13 quotations.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or attribution whose primary document was not reached. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Where a pool coordinate proved wrong, the correction and its authority are recorded rather than silently swapped in.

---

## 1. Budapest, 1903-1921

**Birth. [A: Wikipedia, "John von Neumann"; MacTutor]** 28 December 1903 in Budapest, eldest of three sons of Miksa (Max) Neumann, a banker, and Margit Kann. Wealthy assimilated Jewish family of the Lipótváros, living above the Kann-Heller offices in an apartment shared with the wider Kann clan.

*Coordinate correction.* The pool's birth pin, 47.4979 / 19.0402, is the Budapest centroid and falls on the **Buda** bank, not in Lipótváros (Pest, District V). The birthplace plaque is at Bajcsy-Zsilinszky út 62, formerly Váci körút 62: approximately **47.5100 / 19.0540**, used for the three childhood stops. The centroid is kept only for the two marriage stops, where no address is documented.

**The prodigy anecdotes. [R: popular biographical tradition]** Division of two eight-digit numbers in the head, Ancient Greek with his father, total recall of volumes read once. These come through admirers, not documents; the pool's c. 1908 is a convention. Marked as tradition in `date_confidence`.

**Ennoblement. [A: Wikipedia]** 20 February 1913. Franz Joseph elevates Max Neumann to the Hungarian nobility; the family takes the predicate *margittai*, which the son later Germanizes to *von*.

**The Fasori gymnasium. [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** Enters the Lutheran (Fasori Evangélikus) Gimnázium in **1914**. László Rátz arranges university-level tutoring outside the school.

*Three corrections.* (i) The pool's range "1911-1921" is wrong at the front: Wikipedia states 1914. (ii) The pool's pin 47.5147 / 19.0781 is roughly 700 m too far north; Wikipedia's "Fasori Gimnázium" gives 47°30′31″N 19°4′34″E = **47.5086 / 19.0761**. (iii) The pool calls Eugene Wigner a classmate; he was **a year ahead**.

**Tutoring under Gábor Szegő. [A: Wikipedia]** From about age fifteen (1918), because the school could not otherwise challenge him. Folded into the gymnasium stop.

**Flight to Vienna. [A: Wikipedia]** 1919, during Béla Kun's Hungarian Soviet Republic. Duration undocumented; the journey says "some months" and invents no return date. **First paper. [A: MacTutor, with a date correction]** With Michael Fekete: the pool dates it 1921, MacTutor says it "was published in **1922**." The work is of 1921, the publication of 1922; the journey's Berlin stop says it goes to press "the following year," which fits both.

## 2. The double enrollment: Berlin, Zurich, Göttingen, 1921-1929

**Two universities at once. [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** Mathematics at the Pázmány Péter University in Budapest, attended only for examinations; chemistry at the University of Berlin, attended, to satisfy his father's demand for a practical profession. **ETH Zurich. [A: Wikipedia]** Entrance examination passed September 1923; chemistry transferred there. Weyl and Pólya are in Zurich; MacTutor names the contact. **Definition of ordinals. [A: MacTutor]** Published 1923, still the standard definition.

**The two degrees of 1926. [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** ETH diploma in chemical engineering, and a Budapest doctorate *summa cum laude* on axiomatic set theory, minors in experimental physics and chemistry.

**Göttingen. [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** Winter 1926-1927 on a Rockefeller fellowship under Hilbert.

*Coordinate note, resolved in favour of the corpus.* The pool's 51.5344 / 9.9366 is the city centre, about 1 km southwest of the Mathematisches Institut (Bunsenstraße 3-5, approx. 51.5410 / 9.9450). The journey nonetheless uses **51.5413 / 9.9158**, the canonical Göttingen pin carried by the Oppenheimer and Dirac journeys, atlas policy being that a shared pin stays byte-identical.

**Habilitation and the Berlin Privatdozentur. [A: Wikipedia]** 13 December 1927; lecturing from 1928, reportedly the youngest Privatdozent in the university's history. That superlative is Wikipedia's and is attributed, not asserted. **The minimax theorem. [A: MacTutor]** "Zur Theorie der Gesellschaftsspiele," 1928.

**Hamburg, then Princeton. [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** Brief Hamburg Privatdozentur; Veblen's invitation; the lectureship taken up **October 1929**. *Coordinate correction:* the pool's 53.5653 / 9.9686 lands about 1.1 km west of the university, near St. Pauli; the Hauptgebäude, Edmund-Siemers-Allee 1, is approximately **53.5646 / 9.9843**.

## 3. Princeton, 1929-1940

**The first marriage: unresolved contradiction. [R]** MacTutor places the marriage to Mariette Kövesi in **1929, before** the move to the United States. Wikipedia places it in **1930**, after the October 1929 crossing, alongside his Catholic baptism, and gives no month. The pool asserted "1930-01, shortly before the couple relocates," which resolves the conflict in a direction neither source supports *and* reverses the sequencing. **The contradiction is preserved**: the stop is dated 1930-01-01, `date_confidence` states the disagreement, and the campa says the question is not settled.

**Full professor, Princeton University. [A: MacTutor]** 1931. Folded into the 1929 arrival stop. *Coordinate note:* the pool's 40.3467 / -74.6559 matches neither the old Fine Hall of that era (now Jones Hall, approx. 40.3487 / -74.6578) nor the modern Fine Hall (40.3457 / -74.6527). The journey uses **40.3487 / -74.6588**, the canonical old-Fine-Hall pin from `dirac.journey.json`.

**Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik. [A: MacTutor]** Springer, 1932. Beyer's English translation, Princeton University Press, **1955** [A: PUP edition, translator's note].

**Institute for Advanced Study. [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** 1933, one of the original six professors of mathematics, the youngest, held for life; German posts resigned as the Nazis take power. Fuld Hall pin **40.3298 / -74.6672** inherited byte-identical from the Oppenheimer journey.

**Marina. [A: Wikipedia, "Marina von Neumann Whitman," correcting the pool]** Born **6 March 1935 in New York City**, not Princeton. Stop moved. **Westcott Road. [A for the street; R for the number]** Wikipedia confirms only "on Westcott Road"; the number 26 is tradition. *Coordinate correction:* the street runs west and southwest of campus at approximately **40.345 / -74.671**; the pool's -74.6485 places the pin east of campus, about a kilometre and a half off.

**Divorce. [A for the fact and the state; R for the city]** 2 November 1937 under Nevada's residency law. Wikipedia references Nevada; **"Reno" specifically is tradition** (Macrae; Poundstone). The campa says the city is tradition and `date_confidence` notes the pin stands for the state.

**Naturalization. [A: Wikipedia]** 1937. **Second marriage. [A: Wikipedia]** Klára Dán, 17 November 1938, Budapest; later a programmer on Los Alamos calculations and the IAS machine. **Bôcher Prize. [A: MacTutor]** 1938.

## 4. Ordnance and the bomb, 1940-1945

**Aberdeen Proving Ground. [A: MacTutor, correcting the pool]** The pool gives "c.1937-1955". MacTutor: "**From 1940** he was a member of the Scientific Advisory Committee at the Ballistic Research Laboratories at the Aberdeen Proving Ground." The separate Navy Bureau of Ordnance consultancy runs 1941-1955.

**The England mission. [R: Macrae (1992)]** A Navy-sponsored 1943 trip to Britain on explosives and mine warfare, unconfirmed against any reachable document. Kept as a stop, tagged tradition in `date_confidence`, with the campa saying the biographers describe the journey and the documentation does not.

**Los Alamos. [A: Wikipedia, "John von Neumann"; Wikipedia, "Trinity (nuclear test)"]** Consultant from 1943; proposes in **September 1943** that the fissile core be compressed by a lens of two high explosives of differing detonation velocity. Canonical pin **35.888 / -106.303** inherited from `robert_oppenheimer.journey.json`.

**Target Committee. [A for membership: Wikipedia; R for the Kyoto advocacy]** The pool tagged the whole item [R]. **Membership is attested**: he is listed from the Manhattan Engineer District. Meetings: 27 April and 2 May 1945 (Washington), 10 May (Los Alamos), so the pool's "1945-05" is wrong. Stimson's removal of Kyoto is attested. **Von Neumann's personal advocacy for Kyoto is not.** The journey states the split inside the campa.

**Trinity. [A: Wikipedia, "Trinity (nuclear test)", with three corrections]** The pool tagged his presence [R]; it should be **[A]**. Ralph Carlisle Smith, on Compania Hill, records him saying "that was at least 5,000 tons and probably a lot more."

(i) *Place naming*: the site is in the Jornada del Muerto, at the north end of the Alamogordo Bombing Range, roughly 60 miles from the town of Alamogordo. (ii) *Coordinate*: the pool's Compania Hill pin 33.9958 / -106.7508 lies about 27 miles nearly due north of ground zero, not 20 miles northwest as claimed. Rather than mint a third Trinity pin the journey uses the **canonical 33.6772 / -106.4753** from `fermi.journey.json`; the Oppenheimer journey's figure differs in the fourth decimal. (iii) *A flourish removed*: the pool called him "reportedly the first to estimate the yield aloud." Wikipedia's named Compania Hill VIP list does not include him at all, and nothing supports "first."

## 5. The machine and the theory of games, 1944-1952

**Moore School. [A: Wikipedia, "Von Neumann architecture"; "ENIAC"]** Joins the EDVAC discussions with Mauchly and Eckert in summer 1944 while still on Los Alamos work. The Aberdeen railway-platform meeting with Goldstine is famous and is **[R]**; the journey frames it as "the story the participants tell." **Theory of Games and Economic Behavior. [A: MacTutor; PUP records]** With Oskar Morgenstern, 1944.

**First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC. [A: Wikipedia]** 1945; circulated by Goldstine, unfinished, bearing only von Neumann's name. Its consequences for the Eckert-Mauchly patent position are standard in the histories and are stated in the campa without a document citation, which is a soft point.

**The 1947 Burks-Goldstine report. [A: Wikipedia]** Parallel machine of roughly 20,000 operations per second. **RAND. [A: MacTutor]** Game theory applied to Cold War strategy; no start date in the pool, so the journey uses 1948-01-01 marked inferred. **Oak Ridge. [R: MacTutor]** Dates vague; **no stop**, too thin to place. **MANIAC. [A: Wikipedia]** Repeated returns to Los Alamos in the early 1950s, first on ENIAC and then on the laboratory machine.

**The IAS machine. [A: Wikipedia, "IAS machine", correcting the pool]** The pool gives "1945-1951". Wikipedia: construction from **1946**, finished 1951, limited operation that summer, **fully operational 10 June 1952**.

## 6. Washington, 1949-1957

**The GAC of October 1949: a contradiction inside the corpus. [A for the meeting and vote; R for von Neumann's role]** `fermi.journey.json` states as [A] that "Fermi and von Neumann both sit on the AEC General Advisory Committee that in October 1949 votes against a crash hydrogen-bomb program." **The standard 1949 GAC membership lists do not include von Neumann** (Oppenheimer chairing, with Conant, DuBridge, Fermi, Rabi, Rowe, Buckley, Seaborg, Smith). Well attested is that he favoured the Super and stood opposite the committee's majority. **Left open**: the campa places him as an advocate outside the committee and `date_confidence` names the corpus file carrying the other reading. The Oppenheimer and Fermi journeys also carry different Washington pins (38.8951 / -77.0364 against 38.9072 / -77.0369); this stop uses the Fermi pin, that file being the one that names von Neumann.

**Teapot Committee. [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** Chairs the Air Force's Strategic Missile Evaluation Committee on ICBM development. The pool gives no date; the work is of 1953-1954 and the stop is dated 1954-02-01, marked a placeholder.

**AEC. [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** Appointed by Eisenhower, sworn 15 March 1955; resigns the IAS professorship to serve full time.

**Awards, with a name correction. [A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]** Navy Distinguished Civilian Service Award **1946**; Medal for Merit **1947** (the pool wrongly placed both in 1946); **Medal of Freedom 1956**, Truman's 1945 civilian decoration and **not** the Presidential Medal of Freedom, which did not exist until Kennedy's 1963 reconstitution; **Enrico Fermi Award 1956**, of which he is the **inaugural recipient**. The pool hedged the Fermi Award as [R]; the award is [A] and only the ceremony is tradition.

*Venue correction.* The pool placed the Fermi Award ceremony at the White House (38.8977 / -77.0365). **No source checked attests that venue**; he was hospitalized at Walter Reed through 1956. The White House stop is **dropped** and the award folded into the Walter Reed stop.

**Diagnosis. [A for the cancer; R for the radiation etiology]** A mass near the collarbone in 1955, diagnosed as metastasized. Primary given as bone in some accounts, pancreatic in others: **contradiction flagged, not resolved.**

**The armed guard. [R]**, downgraded from the pool's [A]. The story that a guard was posted lest he divulge classified material under sedation rests entirely on biographical anecdote (Macrae and those following him); no primary military record is cited anywhere reachable, and it does not appear in the Wikipedia article. **The priest. [R: Macrae; Bhattacharya]** Deathbed request for a Catholic priest, with Pascal's-wager reasoning attributed by friends; no first-person account survives.

**Death. [A: Wikipedia]** 8 February 1957, Walter Reed Army Medical Hospital, aged 53.

**Burial. [A for the cemetery, with a coordinate correction]** Princeton Cemetery. *The corpus contradicts itself*: the pool's geography lens gives 40.3467 / -74.6621, its afterlife lens 40.3538 / -74.6572, for the same grave. Neither matches Wikipedia's 40°21′18″N 74°39′36″W = **40.3550 / -74.6600**, which the journey uses while saying in the campa that two other figures circulate in the atlas's own files. The pool's claim that the grave lies in the "Presidents' Plot" is **wrong** and dropped: that section holds Aaron Burr Sr., Jonathan Edwards, and Aaron Burr Jr.

## 7. Afterlife

- **The Computer and the Brain**, unfinished Silliman Lecture manuscript, Yale UP, 1958. **[A]**
- **Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata**, completed by Arthur W. Burks, Illinois, 1966. **[A]**
- Given no stop, all **[A]**: **Collected Works**, ed. Taub (1961-1963), cited instead as the source of the 1954 alumni address quoted in the journey; **SIAM Lecture** (1959); **IEEE Medal** (1990); **Bhattacharya** (2021).
- **Lunar crater "Von Neumann"**, far side, 40°17′N 153°15′E, c. 75 km across. **[A: Wikipedia]** The pool's "unspecified (mid-20th century naming convention)" is misleading: catalogued as **Crater 58** until the IAU approved the name in **1970**.
- **John von Neumann University (Neumann János Egyetem)**, Kecskemét, founded 2016. **[A]**
- **"The Martians". [R]**, origin corrected. The pool said the nickname came from colleagues baffled by Hungarian brilliance and unintelligible speech. Per Wikipedia, "The Martians (scientists)", it traces to **Szilárd's answer to the Fermi paradox**, recorded in György Marx's *The Voice of the Martians*. The usual list runs wider than the pool's four.

## 8. Quotations: verified and unverified

Thirteen quotations appear in the journey.

Five are **[A]**, four of them with corrections applied to the pool's version:

- "that was at least 5,000 tons and probably a lot more": Ralph Carlisle Smith on Compania Hill, via Wikipedia, "Trinity (nuclear test)".
- Goldstine on absolute recall: the pool ended it "translate it instantly into English if it had been in German, French, or Italian." That language list is **not in Goldstine**. Actual text, p. 167: "at no diminution in speed from its original language into English."
- Klári von Neumann on her husband: the pool's "tolerant and unforgiving" **appears nowhere** in the passage and is dropped. Dyson, p. 63, quoting "Grasshopper in Very Tall Grass".
- Wigner, "A mind of von Neumann's inexorable logic...": not Wikiquote but the 1958 APS memoir, reprinted 1967, p. 261; the truncated ending "and do not even wish to understand" is restored.
- Bigelow, "His mind was just so fast and so accurate...": not Dyson (2012) as the pool had it, but **Regis, *Who Got Einstein's Office?*** (1988), pp. 103-104.

The remaining eight are **[R] as to primary text**, each with its inaccessibility named in the `quote_source` field of its stop: "Any one who considers arithmetical methods..." (NBS AMS-12, 1951; PDF 403/404); "If people do not believe that mathematics is simple..." (attribution chain [A] via Franz L. Alt, *CACM* 15 no. 7, 1972, p. 694, on the 1947 ACM keynote, cited as Alt rather than Wikiquote; ACM PDF 403); "For progress there is no cure..." (*Fortune*, 1955); "The sciences do not try to explain..." (*The Unity of Knowledge*, 1955); "A large part of mathematics which becomes useful..." (source sharpened from a generic "address" to "The Role of Mathematics in the Sciences and in Society," 1954, *Collected Works* vol. VI); "If you say why not bomb them tomorrow..." (*LIFE*, 25 Feb 1957); "There probably is a God..." (Macrae, 1992, secondhand); "You don't have to be responsible for the world that you're in." (to Feynman; source page 404).

The Shannon entropy anecdote (Tribus and McIrvine, 1971, p. 180) was verified and corrected but **is not used**: it belongs to no place on the itinerary.

## 9. Honest gaps

1. **No primary archive was read.** Everything rests on MacTutor, Wikipedia, and a few named books, several cited from the secondary literature rather than opened. The Los Alamos technical series, the AEC records, the Target Committee minutes, and the von Neumann papers at the Library of Congress were out of reach.
2. **The 1929/1930 marriage remains undecided.** Two reputable sources, opposite sequencing.
3. **The 1949 GAC membership question remains open**, and it is a conflict *inside the atlas corpus*, not merely between outside sources.
4. **The Vienna year of 1919 is undated beyond the year**; the length of the family's absence is unknown.
5. **The England mission of 1943 has no reachable documentation at all.** It stands on Macrae.
6. **The cancer's primary site is disputed** and the radiation etiology is unresolvable on present evidence.
7. **Oak Ridge is too vague to place.**
8. **Three offered interlocks are declined**, all for want of a documented presence: the Brussels/Solvay pin (he was not at the 1927 conference; Dirac, Bohr and the Solvay argument are named inside the Grundlagen campa instead), and the canonical Jerusalem and Paris pins.
