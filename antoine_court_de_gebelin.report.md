# Antoine Court de Gébelin (c. 1725-1784): research report

*Slug: `antoine_court_de_gebelin`. Written 2026-08-23 from the verified research pool.
Protestant pastor, agent of the outlawed French Reformed churches at Paris, author of the
nine-volume `Le Monde primitif`, secretary of the Loge des Neuf Soeurs, royal censor, and the
man who in 1781 declared the tarot pack a surviving book of ancient Egypt.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim
whose only source could not be opened.

## 1. Birth: four dates, two cities, unresolved

- **[A]** His father, **Antoine Court (1695-1760)**, was the clandestine Huguenot pastor who
  rebuilt the French Reformed churches (Musée protestant).
- **[R]** Modern authorities give **Nîmes, c. 1725**: Lumières.Lausanne (Univ. of Lausanne,
  notice 43) and BnF authority record cb118979670; the American Academy's page independently
  gives his dates as **1725-1784**.
- **[R]** A competing tradition gives **February 1724 at Geneva**, the boy registered under
  the precautionary name **Antoine Corteiz** (Musée protestant, whose notice is itself
  titled '1724 ou 1728-1784'). **1719** and **1728** also circulate.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** The two exclude each other and both are published by serious
institutions. **No birth register was located**, as one expects for the son of a man under a
capital warrant. The journey carries both as separate stops, each saying in `date_confidence`
that it contradicts the other.

## 2. Lausanne: exile, education, the secretary's desk

- **[A]** The family settled at **Lausanne in 1729** ("La famille de C. quitte la France et
  s'installe à Lausanne en 1729").
- **[A]** Educated at the **Académie de Lausanne**, jointly by his father and the astronomer
  **Jean-Philippe Loys de Cheseaux**; the Lausanne scholarship calls the formation
  encyclopedic. *Gap:* no start date, so 1738 is a declared placeholder.
- **[A]** From **c. 1743** secretary to the jurist and antiquarian **Charles Guillaume Loys
  de Bochat**, then increasingly to his father, until 1760 (Musée protestant;
  Lumières.Lausanne).

## 3. Ordination and the Seminary of the Desert

- **[A]** Theology thesis, cited as ***De prophetiis***, defended **4 June 1754** ("soutient
  une thèse de théologie le 4 juin 1754"). *Gap:* no copy of it reached.
- **[A]** **Consecrated pastor September 1754**, made *lecteur* at the French seminary
  ("Consacré pasteur en septembre 1754, C. devient lecteur au séminaire de Lausanne"), where
  he taught **philosophy, morals, logic and controversy** from the winter of 1754-55 to
  **1763** (Musée protestant; Lumières.Lausanne gives "? - 1763").
- **[A]** His father's **Histoire des troubles des Cévennes** published **1760**, the son
  assisting at press; **the father died at Lausanne in 1760** (Musée protestant). *Gap:* no
  month for the death; June is a stated placeholder.

**Inference flagged.** No source records the son ever serving a clandestine pastorate inside
France, the vocation this seminary trained men for; the file states that as an absence of
record, not a fact.

## 4. Toulouse, and *Les Toulousaines* (1763)

Two executions, three weeks apart, in one city:

- **[A]** **François Rochette**, pastor, **hanged 19 February 1762 at the Place du Salin**
  (**43.5947, 1.4444**), with the **three de Grenier brothers** (Henri, Jean, Joachim),
  Protestant gentlemen-glassmakers of the Comté de Foix, beheaded beside him (fr.wikipedia,
  'François Rochette'; 'Place du Salin': "En 1762, le pasteur François Rochette y est pendu").
- **[A]** **Jean Calas**, merchant, **broken on the wheel 10 March 1762 at the Place
  Saint-Georges** (**43.6019, 1.4478**, renamed Place Calas in 1794; fr.wikipedia).

**Two pool corrections applied.** Rochette died at the **Place du Salin**, not the Calas
square; the pool's hedge that it was "traditionally given as the same site" is false and was
dropped. The pool's Calas coordinate (43.6008, 1.4442) is ~300 m off, near Place Esquirol.

- **[A]** **Les Toulousaines**, fictive letters defending the Reformed religion and the
  Rochette, Calas and Sirven cases, **1763** (Musée protestant).
- **[A]** He left the post after the book's failure ("l'échec des Toulousaines en 1763") and
  **left Lausanne 23 March 1763** (fr.wikipedia).

**Stops deliberately declined.** Court is not recorded at Toulouse for either execution; per
the standing rule those squares are **Rochette's and Calas's** ground. Both events are
narrated inside his **Lausanne** stop, where he actually wrote, with both squares in that
stop's `suggested_refs`. No Calas, Sirven, Rochette or elder-Court file exists here.

## 5. Paris, agent of a church that does not exist (1763-)

- **[A]** In Paris by **October 1763**, his residence until death (fr.wikipedia; Musée
  protestant). *Gap:* first lodging unidentified; a central-Paris coordinate is declared.
- **[A]** Added his paternal grandmother **Marie Gébelin**'s surname **around 1765** (BnF:
  "Autour de 1765, il ajoute à son patronyme Court le nom de sa grand-mère, Marie Gébelin").
- **[R]** A **"Protestant bank"** scheme, c. 1765-1766, to mobilize Protestant capital toward
  a toleration settlement, failing for lack of consensus among notables. Secondary synthesis
  only; **no prospectus, subscriber list or document was reached.**
- **[R]** A **1763 tour of Protestant southern France**; the Musée protestant notice gives
  the transition generally and **no source itemizes the stages**. Nîmes stands for the circuit,
  its coordinate declared a region marker.
- **[A]** **Unmarried, no children** in the record (Lumières.Lausanne, *célibataire*).

## 6. *Le Monde primitif* (1773-1782) and the censorship

- **[A]** **Le Monde primitif**, from **1773**, nine quarto volumes through **1782**
  (Lumières.Lausanne; BnF). Older bibliographies count ten or eleven parts depending on
  binding; modern catalogues normalize to nine.
- **[A]** **Histoire naturelle de la parole**, **1776**, posthumous reprint **1816** (BnF).
  *Gap:* no publisher or city for the reprint. **[R]** **Dictionnaire étymologique et raisonné
  des racines latines**, **1780** (BnF works list; title page not opened).
- **[A]** Appointed **censeur royal in 1781** by the *garde des Sceaux* (Lumières.Lausanne;
  fr.wikipedia; Musée protestant).
- **[A]** Volume I imprint: **rue Poupée, house of M. Boucher, chez l'auteur**, from a
  BnF/CCFr record cited in the pool and **not independently opened**; the street's line and
  1850s demolition are confirmed at fr.wikipedia, and 48.8531 / 2.3417 approximates it.

- **[A]** **4 April 1778: Voltaire's initiation at the Neuf Soeurs, conducted by Benjamin
  Franklin and Court de Gébelin** (en.wikipedia; Lumières.Lausanne), matching

- **[A]** **Secretary of Les Neuf Soeurs in 1779**, under Jérôme de Lalande's presidency

  dropped.
- **[R]** The **letter of 29 June 1778** to Franklin, signed "Secrétaire de la Loge des IX.

- **[R]** **Société Apollonienne** constituted late **1780** with Court as president, becoming

  American Philosophical Society, 1783** (en.wikipedia; as.amphilsoc.org returned **403**).

## 8. *Du Jeu des Tarots* (1781)

**Read in the 1781 first edition**, *Le Monde primitif* tome VIII, via
https://archive.org/details/mondeprimitifana08cour; all six quotations in the journey are
keyed to page numbers there.

- **[A]** The essay stands at **tome VIII, pp. 365ff.** en.wikipedia, 'Occult tarot', cites
  **365-380**; the pool's 365-394 and 365-410 are **not corroborated** and were not used.
- **[A]** The thesis, p. 365: an Egyptian book survived the burning of the libraries, is so
  common no scholar has stooped to it, and is the tarot pack ("Ce Livre est en un mot le Jeu
  des Tarots"). Card-by-card Egyptianizing follows, including trump **N. XVII**, retitled
  **La Canicule** (pp. 374-375), Sirius and Isis, closing "C'est l'Egyptianisme tout pur";
  the **Grand-Prêtre / Grande-Prêtresse** argument against a modern origin (p. 370); and the
  etymology **Tar + Ro/Ros/Rog = "le chemin Royal de la vie"** (p. 380).
- **[R]** The discovery anecdote (**Madame la C. d'H.**, lately from Germany or Switzerland;
  the quarter of an hour) is **known only from Court's own text**. **Demoted from [A]:**
  secondary summaries render it merely as "a group of women playing cards". **The house is
  unidentified**; the file uses the canonical Paris pin and says so.
- **[A] / [R]** The **second dissertation**, "Recherches sur les Tarots, et sur la Divination
  par les Cartes des Tarots", signed only **"M. le C. de M\*\*\*"**, printed immediately after
  Court's essay, is attested, and Court announces it as the work of "un Officier Général,
  Gouverneur de Province" (p. 366). **The comte de Mellet identification is traditional
  attribution, flagged as such and never stated.** The Morgan page returned **403**.

**Translation defects corrected against the French original:** "this marvelous **Map**" is
*cette Carte merveilleuse*, **Card**, the trump; "the rising of the **Dog-Wave**" is *le lever
de la Canicule*, the **Dog-Star**; "the first Bands **from** the Egyptians" is *des
Egyptiens*, **of**. Two further defects belong to a passage not used here. The four-word
quote "This is pure Egyptianism" got **no** stop of its own, per the pool's warning; it sits
in French inside the Star campa, beside the description it judges.

## 9. Mesmer, death, burial, posthumous

- **[R]** **1783: Lettre ... sur le magnétisme animal**, describing his illness (tradition:
  calculi), treatment and recovery, and endorsing investigation of Mesmer. **No copy of the
  pamphlet was opened** (Wood Library-Museum page: 403).
- **[A]** **31 March 1784**: Madame Roland's letter reporting the public course losing its
  audience (French Wikisource, letter 107, read directly). **Distinct from and far better
  evidenced than** the later sexual allegation attached to Roland's name, **not locatable in
  any page reached**.
- **[A] as to month, contradictory as to day.** Died at **Paris, May 1784**.
  Lumières.Lausanne: **10 May**, at Mesmer's residence. BnF: **night of 12-13 May**.
  fr.wikipedia: **12 May**, the date already in
  `jean_baptiste_pitois_paul_christian.journey.json`. **Unresolved**; the date field is
  1784-05-01 as a declared unknown.
- **[R] Contradiction on cause.** Calculi (Lumières.Lausanne and his own pamphlet) vs
  **carcinoma of the upper lip complicated by proteinuria** from a reported autopsy
  (fr.wikipedia) vs an untraceable venereal rumour. **No post-mortem report exists in
  anything reached.** All three ride in one stop.
- **[A]** **10 July 1784**: remains moved from a provisional Paris grave to **Franconville**,
  to a tomb erected by the **comte d'Albon**. **The pool's coordinate was ~450 m off and
  internally inconsistent; corrected to 48.9889 / 2.2256.** *Gap:* exact site and present
  condition unknown.
- **[A]** Papers of father and son at the **Bibliothèque de Genève** (BnF). **[A] 1789**:
  posthumous **Devoirs du prince et du citoyen**, offered as a sequel to the Declaration of
  the Rights of Man (Musée protestant). *Gap:* authorship and state of the text unestablished.

**Iconography.** **[A]** Engraved oval portrait by **André Pujos** and **François Huot**,
**1784** (Commons, citing the Centre de recherche du château de Versailles), the only dated
contemporary likeness. **[R], probably misattributed:** the plaque at the French Seminary of
Lausanne Cathedral (EXIF **46.5228, 6.6347**) is filed on Commons under Court de Gébelin, but
a second photograph of the same stone names **Antoine Court the father** with **Benjamin
Duplan** (1688-1763), and neither page transcribes it. **Reattributed to the father pending a
photograph of the inscription, and therefore not a stop here.**

## Contradictions and gaps

**Seven contradictions, all carried into the file rather than adjudicated:** **birth** (Nîmes
c. 1725 vs Geneva Feb. 1724, with 1719 and 1728 also circulating, no register); **Masonic
initiation** (1771 vs "peu après 1776" vs 1778); **death day** (10 May vs night of 12-13 May
vs 12 May); **cause of death** (calculi vs upper-lip carcinoma with proteinuria vs venereal
rumour); **volume count** of *Le Monde primitif* (nine vs ten or eleven); **essay pagination**
(365-380 vs 365-394 vs 365-410); the **Lausanne plaque** (father or son, almost certainly the
father).

**Gaps, stated as gaps:** no birth register; no copy of *De prophetiis*; no month for the
father's death; no document of the Protestant bank; no itemized 1763 southern itinerary; no
first Paris address; no Neuf Soeurs or Musée de Paris meeting address; no address for the
house where the tarot was declared Egyptian; no post-mortem report; no exact site or present
condition for the Franconville tomb; no publisher for the 1816 reprint; no evidence the 1789
posthumous book is his.

**Two structural gaps for the operator:** the fifteenth-century northern-Italian emergence of
tarot that card historians use to refute Court has **no journey file here**, so the
counter-evidence to this file's central claim has nowhere to live; and Calas, Sirven,
Rochette and Antoine Court the elder are absent too, so the Toulouse geography behind
*Les Toulousaines* has no home of its own.
