# George Gabriel Stokes (1819-1903): research report

*Compiled 2026-09-01 for `george_gabriel_stokes.journey.json`. Sligo rectory to Mill Road Cemetery; Lucasian Professor 1849-1903, the longest tenure the chair has had.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim reaching this file only through a dossier the verifier could not refetch. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved.

## 1. Skreen, County Sligo (1819-1832)

Born **13 August 1819** at the rectory of Skreen, youngest surviving of six children of the Rev. Gabriel Stokes, Rector of Skreen, and Elizabeth Haughton **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**.

**Coordinate corrected.** The dossier's 54.25, -8.8567 lands near Templeboy, 8 km west of the village. Nominatim gives Skreen More, Co. Sligo at **54.2433, -8.7311**, used for both Skreen stops.

**Bearing corrected.** The dossier said "twelve miles south of Sligo town." Sligo is 54.2766, -8.4761; Skreen lies about 17 km on a bearing near 257 degrees, eleven miles west-southwest. The RTE piece cited gives no distance.

Childhood burn, no crying, an uncle's nickname "Wellington": **[R]**, family memoir via Larmor (1907) as reported in the dossier. The link between this coast and Stokes's lifelong subjects is **[R]**, marked in the stop as interpretation, not cause.

## 2. Dublin and the crossing (1832-1835)

**1832**: enters the Rev. R. H. Wall's school in Hume Street, Dublin, a day boy lodging with his uncle John Stokes **[A: MacTutor]**. **Coordinate corrected**: the dossier's 53.3369, -6.2477 falls about 470 m east of the street; Nominatim gives Hume Street, Dublin 2 at **53.3378, -6.2548**.

**1834**: his father dies at Skreen during these school years **[A: MacTutor]**. Nothing attests that the boy travelled back for the burial, so no Skreen stop is staged; the death is carried inside the Dublin stop, which is where Stokes was.

**1835**: crosses from Waterford to Bristol on the packet *Killarney*; the vessel nearly founders and his brother William later recalled George folding away his greatcoat so as to be ready to swim. Move **[A: MacTutor]**, anecdote **[R]**.

## 3. Bristol and Cambridge (1835-1841)

**1835-1837**: Bristol College under principal Joseph Henry Jerrard, Francis William Newman among the teachers **[A: MacTutor for college and dates; teacher names and the Jan 1837 prize per memoir tradition]**.

**Inversion in the pool, corrected.** The dossier's chronology said Stokes "matriculated at Pembroke on Jerrard's advice against Trinity." MacTutor states the opposite: Jerrard urged **Trinity**; Stokes went to **Pembroke**. The dossier's own geography entry had it right. The file follows MacTutor.

**1837**: matriculates at Pembroke, by his own account not having begun the differential calculus **[A: MacTutor, quoting the note printed in Larmor]**. **1838-1839**: reads with the coach **William Hopkins** **[A for the coaching, in Stokes's own words; R for the framing of Hopkins's teaching as the finding of order in nature]**. **January 1841**: Senior Wrangler and first Smith's Prizeman; Pembroke fellowship immediately **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**.

**Canonical pin inherited.** Senate House is carried at **52.2058, 0.1178**, byte-identical to `arthur_cayley.journey.json`. The interlock lens had reported it as 52.2043, 0.1177; the file itself says 52.2058, 0.1178, and the file wins.

## 4. Hydrodynamics and the Lucasian chair (1842-1854)

- **25 Apr 1842**: 'On the Steady Motion of Incompressible Fluids' read to the Cambridge Philosophical Society **[A for paper and year; R for the exact date, standard bibliographic record, not reverified]**.
- **1845**: 'On the Theories of the Internal Friction of Fluids in Motion...' **[A: MacTutor]**, giving the Navier-Stokes equations their final form after Navier (1822) and Poisson's independent derivation **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **1846**: 'Report on Recent Researches in Hydrodynamics' to the British Association **[A: MacTutor]**. **Gap**: no meeting city in the pool for 1846. The stop sits at Cambridge, where the report was written, and says so in `date_confidence` rather than guessing a venue.
- **1849**: Lucasian Professor at thirty; the chair is poorly endowed and he takes London teaching to supplement it. **1851**: Stokes's law, drag on a small sphere in a viscous fluid **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **1852**: names and explains fluorescence; Rumford Medal the same year **[A: MacTutor]**. **Sept 1852**: two-hour public lecture on it at the British Association in Belfast **[A for the meeting city: Wikipedia list; the hall within Belfast is not attested and is not named]**.
- **1854**: sets the curl-surface-integral theorem as a Smith's Prize question, which is how the name attaches **[R: dossier synthesis of examination records, not fetched]**. **July 1850**: Thomson had communicated the theorem to him by letter **[R: dossier synthesis of Larmor's edited Stokes-Thomson correspondence, not fetched in any pass]**. Both halves are therefore [R], and the stop says so.

## 5. The Royal Society and the School of Mines

**1851** FRS; **1854** one of two secretaries, held thirty-one years **[A: MacTutor]**.

**Building corrected.** The dossier put the secretaryship at **Somerset House**. The Society had left it by the 1850s and was at **Burlington House, Piccadilly**, for essentially the whole 1854-1885 secretaryship (Wikipedia: Somerset House 1780-1852, Burlington House from 1857). The file keeps a Somerset House stop only for the **1851 election** (51.5113, -0.1172), inside that period, and moves the secretaryship and presidency to Burlington House.

**Coordinate inconsistency, declared not concealed.** The pool gives Burlington House three ways: 51.5087, -0.1394; 51.5089, -0.1401; and 51.5089, -0.1352 (the interlock lens, under a merged "Burlington House / Somerset House" label, landing 300 m east on Piccadilly and so not inherited). The file uses **51.5089, -0.1401** for both Royal Society stops.

**Contradiction not resolved.** The chronology dates the School of Mines chair **1857**; the geography dates the London teaching **circa 1854-1859**, and MacTutor has him resigning in 1859 on the improved endowment. The stop is dated 1857 and states the spread.

## 6. Mary Susanna Robinson (1856-1857)

**1856**: meets Mary Susanna Robinson, only daughter of Thomas Romney Robinson of Armagh Observatory, at the British Association **[A for the 1856 meeting: skullsinthestars; A for Cheltenham as the city, 26th meeting, president Charles Daubeny]**. **The venue within Cheltenham is an inference**: the Pittville Pump Room is the researcher's guess, in neither source, and the stop says so. **Coordinate corrected** to **51.9124, -2.0674**. Mistaking her for a daughter of Sir David Brewster: **[R]**.

**Birr Castle (1856-1857)**: **[A]** that Stokes advised the Earl of Rosse on the Great Telescope's optics (RTE); **[R]** for the Rosse-circle courtship. The proposal-inside-the-tube legend is **[R]**, called "absolute fiction" by his daughter Isabella per the 1907 memoir; the passage could not be relocated in the archive.org scan.

**Courtship letters, 1857** **[A as printed text; R as to wording, not checked against page images]**. Two corrections:

- **Quote corrected.** The pool rendered the honeymoon letter "and go to Switzerland *as we talked of*". Those words are reconstructed filler inside the source's own ellipsis. Traced text: "If we are married at the time we are at present thinking of, and go to Switzerland...I think I will bring a couple of quartz prisms, a quartz lens, and a piece of uranium glass with me." (11 May 1857.)
- **Quote source corrected.** "George is so fond of lightning. He puts his head under all the waterspouts he can find." is verified at primary level in Larmor vol. 1, where OCR reads "waterspots". It is not a "diary, 1857" in general but **Mary's journal of the Swiss honeymoon**. The next sentence there ("He flew about, now up, now down...") is separate and is not run on.

**4 July 1857**: marries at St Patrick's Cathedral, Armagh, losing his fellowship under Pembroke's celibacy statute **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. **Coordinate corrected** to Nominatim's **54.3478, -6.6562** (about 230 m southwest of the dossier's point).

**Honeymoon**: the Rigi and the Faulhorn, both named in the 11 May letter **[A: skullsinthestars]**. **Coordinate corrected**: the Faulhorn summit is **46.6749, 7.9997**, about 3 km from the dossier's 46.6947, 8.0325.

**From 1857**: Lensfield Cottage, later Stokes Lea **[A: Wikipedia]**, carried at **52.1968, 0.1252**; the afterlife pool gives 52.1983, 0.1264 for the same house, and the geography value is used throughout. **1862**: re-elected to the fellowship under revised statutes **[A: MacTutor]**.

## 7. Light, blood, credit not claimed (1859-1875)

- **1859-1860**: on the Kirchhoff-Bunsen publication Stokes declines to claim priority, though Thomson insisted Stokes had taught him spectral identification orally before summer 1852 **[R: dossier synthesis of the Stokes-Thomson correspondence]**. Kelvin's role as principal circulator is likewise **[R]**.
- **1864**: 'On the Reduction and Oxidation of the Colouring Matter of the Blood' **[A as to paper and result; reached only through dossier synthesis]**. **1869**: President of the British Association at Exeter **[A: MacTutor for the presidency, Wikipedia list for the city; venue not reverified]**.
- **Undated**: consults the lens-maker **Howard Grubb** at Rathmines **[R: RTE states it flatly with no date; no second source found]**. The stop is dated 1875 purely to order the segment and says so.

## 8. Public offices (1880-1893)

**Tay Bridge, date corrected.** The dossier chronology dated the wind-pressure evidence **1879**. The disaster was 28 Dec 1879; Stokes testified to the Board of Trade Court of Inquiry during the **Westminster sessions of 19 April to 8 May 1880** **[A: Wikipedia]**. The dossier's own geography entry had 1880, and the file uses it.

**Burnett Lectures, discrepancy resolved with the resolution shown.** MacTutor dates them 1891-93; the dossier 1883-85. The DNB 1912 supplement separates the series: "Burnett Lecturer at Aberdeen (1883-1885, three courses)" and "Gifford Lecturer at Edinburgh (1891-1893)". MacTutor has conflated them. The file uses **Burnett 1883-1885** (*On Light*) and **Gifford 1891 and 1893** (*Natural Theology*) **[A: giffordlectures.org]**. Marischal College as the Aberdeen venue is **[R]**, flagged in the stop.

**1885-1890**: President of the Royal Society, succeeding T. H. Huxley, having worried in writing about surrendering the secretary's salary. **17 Nov 1887 to 1892**: Conservative MP for Cambridge University, returned unopposed, not contesting 1892 **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor; precise sitting dates from Hansard via the dossier, not refetched]**.

**Baronetcy, designation corrected.** The dossier gave "of Lensfield Cottage in the Borough of Cambridge". Per Wikipedia, 'Stokes baronets', the creation of **6 July 1889** reads "of Lensfield Cottage in the Parish of Saint Paul in the Town of Cambridge". The file uses that wording and the exact date. **1893**: Copley Medal **[A: Wikipedia]**, carried inside another stop rather than given a scene.

**1893**: his younger son **William George Gabriel Stokes**, a physician, dies at thirty of a morphine overdose **[R]**. **Contradiction, not resolved**: the Dictionary of Irish Biography records an accidental overdose; later summaries describe suicide while temporarily insane. The DIB entry was not fetched. No place of death in the pool; the stop sits at Lensfield Cottage and says in `date_confidence` that the location is inferred and the manner disputed. Stated flat, not softened.

## 9. Jubilee, death, burial (1899-1903)

- **1-2 June 1899**: jubilee of the Lucasian appointment; garden party at Pembroke with Kelvin the moving force, dinner at Trinity **[A: Pembroke College; the Times obituary gives "June 1 and 2, 1899"]**. Busts by **Hamo Thornycroft** to college and university **[A: Wikipedia]**; Cambridge gold portrait medal and the Arago medal **[A as a set; standard record, not refetched item by item]**. **Canonical pin inherited**: Trinity at **52.207, 0.1146**, byte-identical to `newton.journey.json`. **30 Dec 1899**: Mary Stokes dies **[A for the date; location inferred]**.
- **1900**: elected one of eight foreign members of the French Academy of Sciences, succeeding Weierstrass **[R: dossier synthesis]**. **No Paris stop is staged**: election to a foreign academy does not put a man in the room, and nothing attests that Stokes travelled there. The Arago medal is carried at the jubilee, where it reached him.
- **Master of Pembroke: both pool dates wrong, and not resolvable.** The chronology gives 12 August 1902 and calls it "the last day of his eighty-third year"; the geography gives 31 December 1902. DNB 1912 says "October 1902"; the Times obituary says he agreed to nomination "in August last, on the death of Dr. Searle"; French Wikipedia gives 26 August 1902. His eighty-third birthday was 13 August 1902, so the "last day" phrase fits neither. The stop is dated **1902-09-01** as an ordering device and states the August-October window.
- **1 Feb 1903**: dies at Lensfield Cottage, aged eighty-three, after fifty-four years in the chair **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. The Times transcript prints the house as "Lamafield", an artefact of Lensfield, noted rather than propagated. **4 Feb 1903**: the body lies overnight in Pembroke Chapel **[R: funeral-custom tradition]**, folded into the funeral stop rather than given its own scene.
- **5 Feb 1903**: procession round Pembroke's court, then Great St Mary's "filled in every part," closing with the Dead March from Handel's *Saul*; burial at Mill Road Cemetery beside Mary and near two children who died young **[A: Times obituary transcript; Wikipedia]**. **Coordinate contradiction flagged**: the pool gives Great St Mary's at 52.2057, 0.1177 and 52.2044, 0.1183; the file uses the latter, from the obituary-transcript pass. **Coordinate corrected**: both pool values for the cemetery (52.1985, 0.142 and 52.2019, 0.144) sit 450-500 m southeast of it; Nominatim gives 52.2029, 0.1375. The file uses **52.2020, 0.1370**.

## 10. Afterward

- **1904**: memorial in the north aisle of Westminster Abbey, commemoration and not burial **[A for the memorial: Wikipedia; R for the "bronze medallion, 1904" specification]**. **Canonical pin inherited**: **51.4993, -0.1273**, byte-identical to `lord_kelvin.journey.json`.
- **1880-1905**: *Mathematical and Physical Papers*, five volumes, first three selected by Stokes, last two completed by **Joseph Larmor**. **1907**: Larmor edits the two-volume *Memoir and Scientific Correspondence*, personal section by Stokes's daughter **Isabella Lucy Humphry**. **1909**: the Stokes Society founded at Pembroke **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**.
- Kinematic viscosity in stokes (St); craters on the Moon (1964) and Mars (1973) **[A as commemorations; R as to the naming dates]**. **2000**: three-dimensional Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness is one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize Problems **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **July 2017**: Dublin City University names a building after him **[A: Wikipedia]**. The Irish-versus-British possession question the dossier raised is **not adjudicated here**.
- **15-18 Sept 2019**: the **Stokes 200 symposium** at Pembroke, co-organised by Silvana Cardoso, Julyan Cartwright and Christopher Ness **[A: Pembroke College]**. **Date corrected**: the resulting *Philosophical Transactions A* theme issue appeared in **2020**, not 2019; DOI 10.1098/rsta.2019.0524 resolves to Paul Ranford, 'Sir George Gabriel Stokes, Bart (1819-1903): his impact on science and scientists', vol. 378 (2020).
