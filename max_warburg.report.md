# Max Moritz Warburg (1867-1946): research report

*2026-08-31. Written from the verified research pool for `max_warburg.journey.json`. Hamburg banker; head of M. M. Warburg & Co. 1910-1938; Versailles expert; Reichsbank Generalrat; chairman of the Hilfsverein der deutschen Juden; emigrant 1938; died New York.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, biographical tradition, or a claim resting on secondary synthesis only. Contradictions are flagged and left standing, not adjudicated. Coordinate fixes are listed because six pool entries carried the same wrong pair.

---

## 1. Hamburg, 1867-1886

- Born **5 June 1867**, one of seven children of Moritz Warburg and Charlotte Oppenheim **[A: en.wiki, de.wiki, deutsche-biographie]**.
- The family is in the **Grindel** quarter at his birth, at **Grindelhof 1a** per de.wiki **[A]**. The same year the bank moves into **Ferdinandstraße 75**, where it still is **[A: mmwarburg.de]**.
- **1871**, move to a villa at **Mittelweg 17, corner Johnsallee** **[A: de.wiki]**. The geography lens tagged this [R]; too weak, since de.wiki states it outright and the chronology lens tags the same fact [A]. Retagged [A].
- **1886**, Abitur at the Realgymnasium of the **Gelehrtenschule des Johanneums** **[A: de.wiki]**. No university follows, ever.
- **c. 1879**, the **birthright-for-books bargain with Aby** **[R as an event, A as a quotation]**. Max's German words survive via Gombrich, reproduced with citation in de.wiki's *Aby Warburg*; he separately called it "den größten Blankoscheck meines Lebens" (Karen Michels, *Es muss besser werden!*, Hamburg University Press 2020). Memoir recollection, not a document, and the journey file says so.

**Coordinate corrections.** Birth house: pool 53.5665, 9.9840; used **53.5636, 9.9908**, the canonical pin inherited from `aby_warburg.journey.json`. Mittelweg 17: pool 53.5665, 9.9890; used **53.5664, 9.9952**. Johanneum: pool 53.5620, 9.9930, which matches neither campus; used **53.5499, 9.9982** (Speersort, occupied until the 1914 move to Winterhude). Ferdinandstraße 75: pool 53.5493, 10.0007; used **53.5524, 9.9978**.

Two pool defects worth recording. The Mittelweg record carried a **malformed JSON key** (`"9.9890": 9.989`) beside the real `lng`, stripped here. And the Ferdinandstraße error is the worst: **the same wrong pair appears on six geography entries**, while the afterlife lens gives a **third, also wrong** pair (53.5497, 9.9954) for the same address.

## 2. Four counting houses, 1886-1893

The most documented thing about his twenties, and it is entirely foreign.

- **1886-1888 Frankfurt**, apprenticeship at **J. Dreyfus & Co.** **[A: de.wiki, deutsche-biographie]**. Firm address not located.
- **1888 Amsterdam**, **Wertheim & Gompertz** **[A for the Amsterdam stage per deutsche-biographie; firm and year from de.wiki]**. Address not located.
- **1888-1890 Munich**, one-year volunteer, **III Royal Bavarian Chevaulegers**, rising to **Vizefeldwebel** **[A]**. The **blocked officer's commission and its antisemitic explanation are [R]**: repeated by every biographer, but no source reached produces the order or refusal. The campa says plainly that nobody produces the document.
- **1890 Paris**, the **Banque Impériale Ottomane** and unenrolled **Sorbonne** attendance **[R: de.wiki and Hoffmann, not refetched]**.
- **1891-1892 London**, **N. M. Rothschild & Sons, New Court** **[A: deutsche-biographie]**.

## 3. The partner, the city, the port, 1893-1914

- **1893**, personally liable partner **[A: mmwarburg.de, deutsche-biographie, dasjuedischehamburg]**. The pool's gloss that he then "ran the bank for forty-five years" is loose and was not carried: partner from 1893, head only from 1910, gone May 1938.
- **1895**, brother **Paul** made partner, later leaving for New York and Kuhn, Loeb **[A]**. **1897**, **Handelsrichter** by Senate appointment **[A]**.
- **1899**, marries **Alice Magnus** **[A]**. The family holds **Kösterberg** from that year, but de.wiki says they *lived* there only to **1907**, moving then to the Alsterufer near the Dammtor. The geography lens's "holds this property from 1899 onward" is true of ownership, not residence; corrected in the campa.
- **South Africa, undated [R]**. Voyage to the Cape, severe dysentery, roughly twenty kilograms lost, then an inland tin-mine inspection in Swaziland. From Hoffmann and Warburg's own memoir. **No reachable source dates it at all.** The journey file uses 1896-01-01 and declares in `date_confidence` that the year is an ordering device, not a claim.
- **1902**, elected to the **Chamber of Commerce**, held to 1933 **[A: de.wiki]**. dasjuedischehamburg's "1903-1933" is a year late at the front.
- **February 1903**, elected to the **Bürgerschaft** for the National Liberals; securities-exchange board; from 1903 occasional financial adviser to Wilhelm II **[A: de.wiki]**.
- **1908**, **Cuxhavener Hochseefischerei** with Albert Ballin **[A]**; the "leading fish supplier by turnover" claim is **[R]**, secondary only. Also **1908**, the **Berliner Börsenausschuss** in the Reichsamt des Innern **[A]**.
- **1910**, Moritz dies, Max leads alone **[A]**. Same year **HAPAG** and the Hamburg **Deutsche Kolonialgesellschaft** boards **[A]**; **Blohm und Voss** **[R: NDB via dossier]**.
- **June 1914**, the **Anglo-German bank with Lord Milner** **[A]**, and the **Kiel Week lunch with Wilhelm II** **[R]**, surviving only in Warburg's posthumous notes and a *Welt* account that could not be fetched. The campa says what it establishes is access, not influence.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** de.wiki dates the **Zentralverband des Deutschen Bank- und Bankiersgewerbes** board seat to **1902** in one place and **1903** in another, and the pool duplicated the same appointment across two chronology entries as if they were two events. The file mentions it once, at 1902, and says in the campa that the reference works date it twice to two different years.

## 4. War and peace, 1915-1919

- **1915**, advisory board of the **Kriegsernährungsamt** **[A]**; the **Reichseinkauf** with Ballin **[R: NDB]**. Antisemitic profiteering charges followed; the pool's own note records that the documentary record does not support them.
- **1916**, the **Judenzählung**. Census attested (DHM); **Warburg's part in the protests is [R]**.
- **October 1918**, adviser to **Prince Max von Baden**; declines the **Reich finance ministry** **[R for the refusal and its reasoning: NDB and Kopper as interpretation]**.
- **12 March 1919**, appointed to the delegation, declines the senior role, recommends his partner **Carl Melchior**, attends as an expert **[A: de.wiki; en.wiki on Melchior's Paris role]**.
- **7 May 1919, Trianon Palace**, handover of the draft and Brockdorff-Rantzau's seated reply **[A: FRUS 1919 Paris XI doc. 245]**. Presence follows from the March appointment; **his private judgment of the scene is [R]**. Coordinate: pool 48.8102, 2.0996 falls in open parkland ~1.6 km west; used **48.8114, 2.1211**.
- **18-19 June 1919**, the unanimous experts' recommendation to reject, signed with Wilhelm Cuno **[R: Bundesarchiv R 43 I/5, not refetched]**. Accepted 23 June under ultimatum, signed 28 June **[A]**.

## 5. The republic's banker, 1920-1932

- **1920**, **Deutsche Warentreuhand AG** with Mendelssohn-Bartholdy **[A]**. **1922**, founds the **Übersee-Club** **[A]**.
- **1921**, **honorary doctorate**, University of Hamburg **[A: NDB]**, after the campaign with senator Werner von Melle (Wissenschaftliche Stiftung 1907, Colonial Institute 1908) **[R: NDB, Bajohr]**. Same year, **honorary life membership of the Verein für Hamburgische Geschichte, revoked 1938 under the racial laws [A: de.wiki]**. The afterlife lens missed this; it is the strongest single afterlife detail in the pool and is now in the file. The 1927 **Chamber of Commerce gold medal** is likewise confirmed by NDB, upgrading a pool [R] to [A].
- **24 June 1922**, **Rathenau assassinated** on the Königsallee **[A]**. **Reported plans against Warburg by the same milieu are [R]**, uncorroborated by anything fetched. Coordinate: pool 52.4870, 13.2917; used **52.4877, 13.2838** near Koenigsallee 65. Nominatim returns nothing for the memorial itself, so this is a best-available fix rather than a fetched authority, and the file says so.
- **1924**, appointed to the eight-member **Reichsbank Generalrat**, held to 1933 **[A]**.
- **4-5 December 1924**, the **Fritsch libel trial**, brought with Melchior. Fritsch convicted, three months **[A: JTA 8 Dec 1924]**; appeal at the Hamburg Court of Appeals October 1926 **[A: JTA 20 Oct 1926]**. His *Mein Streit mit dem Hause Warburg* is catalogued by Open Library, first published 1925 **[A for book and year; "Leipzig" is not carried by that record and stays unverified]**.
- **1925-1931**, **I.G. Farben supervisory board [A: de.wiki, exact dates]**, replacing the pool's vague "c.1926-1931/32". An anachronism is kept explicit in the campa and `date_confidence`: the I.G.-Farben-Haus was completed only in **1930** and cannot stand for a tenure beginning in 1925, but no earlier board address was located. Coordinate: pool 50.1275, 8.6720; used **50.1256, 8.6683**.
- **1931**, the bank survives the banking crisis without direct state rescue, on conservative lending and Kuhn, Loeb support **[R]**; the 1929 figures behind that (382 million marks, 289 employees) are **[A: mmwarburg.de]**. Same year, **leaves the DVP [R]**, widely repeated, not confirmed in either encyclopedia article fetched.

## 6. The emigration years, 1933-1938

- **Summer 1933**, removed from the **HAPAG board**; delivers his own farewell in the third person. German text via Hoffmann **[quote A, scene R]**.
- **1933**, the **Haavara** structure with Melchior, plus the bank's **Paltreu** and **Alltreu** vehicles **[R: Barkai, LBI Year Book 35 (1990), via dossier]**.
- **12 December 1933**, the **library sails** from Hamburg harbour. **[A]** for the shipment, **[R]** for Max's personal authorization and financing. A real conflict: the dossier and en.wiki's *Aby Warburg* credit Max; the Institute's own history, per en.wiki's *Warburg Institute*, credits **Lord Lee of Fareham, Samuel Courtauld and the Warburg family collectively**. Flagged, not resolved.
- **Chairman of the Hilfsverein der deutschen Juden.** **Sources conflict, and the conflict stands:** deutsche-biographie (NDB) says **from 1928**; de.wiki gives **1935-1938**. The pool's two lenses had silently adopted different answers, so the dossier contradicted itself. The stop is dated 1935 and `date_confidence` names both readings.
- **9 January 1938**, opens the **Jewish Community House, Hartungstraße 9-11**, which he financed and argued for against the congregation's own board; the 6.5-page typescript address is in the **Max M. Warburg Collection, Leo Baeck Institute, New York** **[A: keydocuments.net jgo:source-118, every element confirmed]**. Coordinate corrected to **53.5699, 9.9854**, which also answers the afterlife lens's open question: the building stands and is the **Hamburger Kammerspiele**.
- **May 1938**, leaves the bank; partnership to **Rudolf Brinckmann** and **Paul Wirtz** **[A]**. The farewell to about 200 employees survives via Klessmann's 1998 bank history **[quote A, transcript R]**.

## 7. New York and the name, 1938-1949

- **Late August 1938**, emigration with Alice and a daughter **[A]**.
- **November 1938**, **Fritz Moritz Warburg** arrested, held several months, released, eventually a Swedish citizen in Stockholm **[A: en.wiki, *M. M. Warburg & Co.*]**. **Correction carried into the file:** that article calls Fritz "the bank's former head", which is wrong. Max led the firm 1910-1938; Fritz was a partner.
- **1944**, naturalized **[A]**. **1945**, Kösterberg, seized and returned after liberation, given by Max and Fritz to the **American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee** for **200 orphaned children up to the age of sixteen** **[A: JTA obituary]**. Coordinate: pool 53.5620, 9.7970; used **53.5653, 9.7879**.
- **26 December 1946**, dies in New York at 79 **[A]**. Funeral at **Park Avenue Synagogue** (40.7811, -73.9579; the pool's 40.7736, -73.9631 falls inside Central Park). Buried **Sleepy Hollow Cemetery**, used at **41.0954, -73.8603**; the pool's 41.0895, -73.8626 sits on the adjacent Old Dutch Church burying ground, a legally distinct graveyard and exactly the confusion the stop exists to settle.
- **1947**, **Warburgstraße**, Rotherbaum **[A: de.wiki]**. Coordinate **53.5612, 9.9952**; the pool carried two different pairs for this one street, 53.5665/9.9847 and 53.5661/9.9871, both wrong by roughly 700 m and disagreeing with each other.
- **1941 / 1949 / 1969 / 1991**: renamed **Brinckmann, Wirtz & Co.** (27 October 1941); restitution restoring the Warburgs as shareholders through **Eric M. Warburg** (1949); **M.M.Warburg-Brinckmann, Wirtz & Co.** (1969); short form (1991) **[A: mmwarburg.de, de.wiki]**.

**Contradictions flagged here.**

1. **Death date.** JTA gives **29 December 1946**; deutsche-biographie, de.wiki and en.wiki give **26 December**. JTA is the lone outlier and is named as such. The file uses 26 December for the death, 29 December for the funeral and burial, the latter an inference and labelled one.
2. **Burial place.** The afterlife lens called this unresolved. It resolves in one direction only: **Wikidata (Q67580, P119), de.wiki and en.wiki all leave the field blank**, checked directly, and the JTA obituary states Sleepy Hollow. The correct framing is "the reference works are silent, the contemporary obituary names Sleepy Hollow", not "the sources disagree". Separate lead: de.wiki's *Jüdischer Friedhof Ohlsdorf* carries a "Familie Warburg" photo caption, documenting a **family plot in Hamburg, not Max's grave**.
3. **The 1941 renaming.** en.wiki's *M. M. Warburg & Co.* says the name "remained unchanged" through Aryanization. **mmwarburg.de's own history confirms the 1941 renaming and the 1949 restitution.** Not an even contest; the English article is wrong and is recorded as an outlier to discard.
4. **The 1991 date.** The pool called 1991 the year the name returned. mmwarburg.de dates the return of the Warburg *name* to **1969**; 1991 is the short form. Both are stated.

## 8. Interlocks

Three existing travellers are named in campa text, each on a relation Warburg himself had, never on shared geography.

- **`aby_warburg`**, his elder brother, at three stops (birth, the bargain, the library's departure). Pins **53.5636, 9.9908** and **53.5511, 9.9686** inherited byte-identical from that file.
- **`mayer_amschel_rothschild`**, once, at New Court 1891: Warburg apprenticed inside the London house Mayer Amschel's fifth son founded. Pin **51.5133, -0.0888** inherited byte-identical. Cecil Rhodes's 1887 financing at the same address is **not** referenced: same address, no relation.
- **`chaim_weizmann`**, once, at the Hilfsverein stop, for Warburg's seat on the German advisory council of the enlarged Jewish Agency, the Zionist and non-Zionist coalition Weizmann assembled in 1929. **The weakest of the three, labelled [R] in `date_confidence`:** the claim comes from the dossier alone, no fetched source confirms it, and no existing journey file states it. Kept because the relation, if true, is institutional and one Warburg held himself. An operator who wants it out can cut one clause.

**Not used.** The interlock lens correctly flagged non-interlocks and they were left alone: the **Kaaba** and **Temple Mount** canonical pins, **Buenos Aires**, **Paris** for 1890, and **Erich Mühsam** at the Kiel mutiny. Each connects to Warburg by nothing but a map, and none appears in this file.

**A real gap.** No journey file exists for **Walther Rathenau, Albert Ballin, Carl Melchior, Hjalmar Schacht or Wilhelm II**, who are among Warburg's most load-bearing relationships. They appear here as named persons in campa and sources, which is all they can be until they have files. They are the obvious queue candidates coming out of this subject.

## 9. Gaps stated as gaps

- **The South African voyage has no date.** Not a disputed date: none at all, anywhere reachable. The file's 1896 is declared an ordering device.
- **Firm addresses not located**: J. Dreyfus (Frankfurt), Wertheim & Gompertz (Amsterdam), the Paris branch of the Banque Impériale Ottomane. City-centre coordinates used, and said so.
- **The Hilfsverein's Hamburg office address** not located; 53.5560, 9.9930 is a central-Hamburg approximation.
- **The I.G. Farben board address before 1930** unknown; the pin is the later building, anachronistic and labelled so.
- **The 1919 Reichsbank Zentralausschuss appointment** in the pool's chronology is recorded by neither de.wiki nor deutsche-biographie. Not given a stop; noted as unverified at the 1908 stop.
- **The 1933 Dutch first edition** of the forged "Sidney Warburg" book is **not** confirmed. Open Library holds the 1983 English *Hitler's Secret Backers*, the 1983 German and American editions, and a Dutch edition dated **1982**, a reprint. The 1933 date rests on the dossier alone. The forgery gets no stop, since Warburg is not documented reacting to it.
- **The memoir exists, and the pool nearly lost it.** The afterlife lens searched for *Aus meinem Leben*, found nothing, and concluded the memoir might not exist. The correct title is **`Aus meinen Aufzeichnungen`, Max M. Warburg, first published 1952**, held by Open Library. Several [R] items here (Kiel Week, the HAPAG farewell, the May 1938 farewell, the South African voyage) can now be cited to a named, dated, catalogued volume instead of "family transmission". It is in `suggested_refs` at the Table Bay and Kiel stops.
