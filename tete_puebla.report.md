# Teté Puebla (Delsa Esther Puebla Viltres): research report

*2026-08-20. Born Yara, Oriente (now Granma), Cuba, 9 December 1940; living as of the research cutoff. Second in command of the Mariana Grajales Women's Platoon, September 1958 to January 1959. Reserve brigadier general, Hero of the Republic of Cuba, deputy for San Miguel del Padrón, vice president of the Association of Combatants of the Cuban Revolution.*

**Legend.** [A] attested, source named · [R] reconstruction, tradition, or single-source retrospective testimony · [R memoir] appears only in her own book of interviews.

## The problem that governs everything below

Almost everything known about the fifteen months that made her famous comes from one book of interviews given in her sixties: *Marianas en combate* (Pathfinder Press, 2003), built from two sittings in Havana, 18 November 2000 and 1 March 2002 [A Pathfinder; WorldCat]. Neither research pass could read it; the publisher's server returned 403.

So the evidence is a thin documentary spine (a Council of State wartime diary, some Granma and Trabajadores pieces, one obituary, the National Assembly's profile) inside a much thicker body of first-person testimony, retrospective by forty years. Where the two disagree, both are printed; where only the testimony exists it is marked [R memoir] and is not upgraded for being vivid. The first pass tagged several memoir-only items [A]; the verification pass overturned four tags, corrected eleven facts and moved six coordinates.

## Childhood, 1940-1955

Born Delsa Esther Puebla Viltre(s), 9 December 1940, in Yara [A English Wikipedia; 2023 Havana candidate profile]. The National Assembly's page instead gives San Miguel del Padrón, Havana, her current constituency; almost certainly a conflation. **Contradiction flagged.**

Raised chiefly by her grandparents, eldest of eight, the family too poor for toys, two brothers later in the Rebel Army [R memoir]; no vital or genealogical record reached. She names Harry Villegas Tamayo ("Pombo"), later with Guevara in Bolivia, as a childhood friend from Yara [R memoir].

**Coordinate corrected.** The pool gave Yara as 20.3392 / -76.8342, about 14 km out in farmland; Yara, Granma is at 20.2767 / -76.9469. Used at all three Yara stops.

## Clandestinity, 1956-1957

Enters the first year of the Escuela Normal para Maestros de Manzanillo at fifteen [R memoir]; no register reached. Joins the Movimiento 26 de Julio the same year, carrying bonds, messages, medicine, money, dynamite, arms and people [A English Wikipedia + her oral history, cross-checked]. The motive she gives is that her relative Amelia Puebla and Georgina Barbán were raped by Batista soldiers at the Manzanillo barracks [R memoir]; no judicial, medical or press record was found. It is staged at her Manzanillo pin marked uncorroborated, since it is the reason she herself gives.

Two tags downgraded [A] to [R]. **The Yara caltrops, 19 March 1957** (spikes on the roads, wires cut, the town paralysed): the first pass tagged it attested while its own note conceded the memoir was the only source, and nothing reachable holds a contemporaneous police or press record, or any second source for the date. **The Blanquizal departure, 12 July 1957**, after a captured member gave up the Santiago-Manzanillo arms route under torture: the first pass credited *Diario de la Guerra 3* but said plainly it had not fetched the PDF, and the second pass fetched one returning unreadable binary. An unverified citation to a named document is not attestation. Blanquizal could not be located; the file uses the Bartolomé Masó municipal seat (20.1686 / -76.9428), flagged.

## The Sierra Maestra, 1957-1958

*Diario de la Guerra 3*, p. 299, names her among a group of arrivals at Fidel Castro's column and grounds the date 1 August 1957; the second pass extracted that page with pypdf and the sentence is quoted verbatim in the journey file [A]. EcuRed gives only *"julio de 1957"* and quotes her: *"En julio de 1957 subí a la Sierra Maestra."* **Contradiction flagged**: the day is contested, the month is not. Ileana Rodés is in the diary text; Eugenia Verdecia as a companion is memoir-only.

**Coordinate corrected, anachronism caught.** The pool placed the arrival at 20.1686 / -76.9428 and called it "near La Plata". That is the Bartolomé Masó municipal seat; and the Comandancia General de La Plata was only established in 1958, so Column One was not there in August 1957. The file uses an explicitly approximate sierra point (20.05 / -76.9). November 1957, the fever at El Hombrito, Guevara looking in on her and reading poetry to the troops [R memoir], staged at the existing El Hombrito pin.

**The truce mission, 22-23 July 1958**, the strongest wartime episode, and one the pool had partly wrong. She leaves Altos de Mompié about 5.30 a.m. on a mule with a white flag; the destination is not an unidentified "government post near the front" but the enemy camp at Las Vegas de Jibacoa in Bartolomé Masó; Merob Sosa accepted the truce and the answer was back with Castro and Guevara by 6 p.m.; the handover was 23 July, ICRC delegates René Jecquier and Pierre Schoenholzer receiving 257 prisoners, 23 wounded, not the pool's "more than two hundred" [A Cubainformación, publishing her own detailed telling]. "Captain Durán Batista", who she says demanded she remove her armband, appears in no reachable source [R memoir]. Guevara's letter of that date, said to name *"compañera Teté Puebla, del servicio de Sanidad"*, was located by nobody; it is the one document that would make the episode contemporaneously attested. Altos de Mompié is likewise not at 20.15 / -76.95 but near 1,200 m in that municipality's mountains; the file uses an approximate sierra figure.

Three clandestine trips to Santiago de Cuba after the truce, for communications and supplies [R memoir].

## The Mariana Grajales Platoon, September 1958 to January 1959

4 September 1958, La Plata. Castro creates the platoon over his commanders' objections after a meeting of roughly seven hours; a marksmanship trial decides command; Isabel Rielo shoots better and becomes *jefa*, Puebla *segunda al mando* [A Granma, 9 April 2014; Spanish Wikipedia, "Las Marianas (Cuba)"]. Staged at the existing La Plata pin 19.9667 / -76.9. Celia Sánchez chose the platoon's clothing, cut like her own, without buttons down the front [A Bohemia, "Gardenias guerrilleras", quoting Puebla].

**Distance contradiction, not resolved.** Granma 2014 gives 50 metres and the coin-splitting detail, quoting her directly; the 20-30 metres attributed to the Pathfinder oral history could not be confirmed anywhere reachable, since neither pass read the book. Not two equally sourced tellings.

27 September 1958, Cerro Pelado: a three-day action four kilometres from the Estrada Palma mill, under artillery and a Sherman tank [A EcuRed; La Demajagua]. **Casualties corrected:** one lens of the pool said "five male rebels were killed", the other "four rebel men are killed"; both wrong. La Demajagua names five rebel *bajas*, casualties and not all deaths: lieutenants Raúl Verdecia and Luis Arturo Vázquez Matalobos, combatants Miguel López, René Ibarra and Juan Sardiñas Labrada (the last wounded); enemy losses 67. "No Mariana wounded" is Puebla's report, not a flat fact: at least one account has the *jefa* wounded in the opening moments. **Contradiction flagged.**

20 October 1958, the four-woman detachment (Puebla, Isabel Rielo, Lilia Rielo, Edemis Tamayo) reaches Holguín under Eddy Suñol; she names Puerto Padre and La Cadena among the actions [A Periódico 26, fetched directly, quoting her]. 21 October, dawn action near an unnamed reservoir: two trucks and a jeep hit, two rebels wounded, eleven rifles taken, publicised by Radio Rebelde [R]. Suñol's letter singling her out is memoir-only and unproduced.

**Los Güiros: tag downgraded [A] to [R].** Suñol wounded and evacuated, Puebla the ranking fighter left, taking field command; the detachment captures most of the opposing group and its weapons. That much is corroborated in outline by Cuban press, but no reachable source dates it to 2 November 1958 and none locates the hamlet; the file's coordinate (21.0 / -76.45) is a rough Holguín-Velasco midpoint flagged as false precision. The mutual-death pact she describes with Rielo is her testimony alone.

1 January 1959, Bayamo, when Batista flees [A English Wikipedia + Periódico 26, though her location rests on her own account]. Ended the war a first lieutenant with Rielo and five others [R]; no promotion order reached.

## Victory and the war-victim department, 1959-1963

Joins the Caravan of Liberty from Santiago, 2 January, arriving Havana 8 January [A a Wikimedia Commons photograph captioning her as *segunda jefa* of the platoon during the advance].

Appointed by Castro to direct the Departamento de Asistencia a las Víctimas de Guerra y sus Familiares. **Date contradiction resolved downward.** The chronology lens gave 8 January 1959, the geography lens 4 February; neither is supported. English Wikipedia gives January 1959, seated in Santiago de Cuba, and the 8 January date is doubly suspect since that is the day the Caravan reached Havana. [A for month and city; the exact day is a gap]

Promoted captain, 24 May 1959 [R official profiles]. Playa Girón, April 1961: her department served the affected families; she reported being mobilised, not in a field-combat role [A per the dossier]. Married Raúl Castro Mercader (no relation to Raúl Castro Ruz), later FAR brigadier general, served in Angola; three children, Fidel, Raúl and Laura [A English Wikipedia; Granma 2014]. Year 1960 only, no month, day or place.

**FMC, 1960: sourcing error corrected.** The pool said the Assembly profile named her a founding member of the Federation of Cuban Women. It does not: it calls her a *fundadora* of the Communist Party and, separately, a member of the FMC's Dirección Nacional. The Federation was founded 23 August 1960 under Vilma Espín. Her leadership role is attested; founding membership is not [A parlamentocubano.gob.cu, read directly].

## Party, army, agriculture, 1963-1985

- February 1963, heads the education section of the Eastern Army general staff [A official profiles, not refetched]. 1964-1966, children's farms and social-security services for war orphans, Western Army [R memoir]. 1965, founding member of the Communist Party of Cuba [A parlamentocubano.gob.cu].
- April 1966, placed by General Staff chief Sergio del Valle in a FAR unit supporting families of Cubans serving abroad (Algeria, Congo, Vietnam, Angola, Ethiopia) and assisting disabled returnees [A memoir as characterised in the dossier]. No source shows she ever deployed abroad; she appears never to have left Cuba.
- March 1969 to October 1978, directs the Plan Ganadero Guaicanamar at Jaruco on Castro's assignment [A EcuRed]. **Coordinate corrected** to 23.0428 / -82.0094, about 10 km north of the pool's figure; Jaruco was in Havana province throughout the period, Mayabeque only since 2011. Production figures, budgets and the executions she reports there are [R memoir].
- October 1978, heads the military section of the PCC in Havana province [A official profiles]; Central Committee 1980 to 1986, a range rather than the bare "1980" the pool gave.
- Isabel Rielo Rodríguez died 9 April 1989 [A Granma, 9 April 2014]. She gets no stop of her own here: a comrade's death is not a place Puebla travelled to. It is named in one clause of Puebla's 1985 stop.

## Rank and honours, 1985-2001

1985, director of the Department for Care of Veterans and Martyrs [A English Wikipedia]. 1994, colonel. 24 July 1996, brigadier general, first Cuban woman of general officer rank; the same ceremony advanced her husband and Luis Alfonso Zayas [A English Wikipedia, EcuRed, La Demajagua, cross-checked]. The venue is named in no reachable source; the coordinate is an approximate central-Havana government placement, flagged.

1997, credited with a part in the reception of the remains identified as Guevara [R official profiles]. Neither pass could establish whether she was at Santa Clara or at an earlier Havana event, so the stop sits at her own Havana pin with the question open rather than staging her at a ceremony she may not have attended.

2 December 2001, Hero of the Republic of Cuba with the Order of Playa Girón, conferred by Fidel Castro [A EcuRed, Wikipedia, search-corroborated]. Two corrections: the occasion was the 45th anniversary of the Granma landing and Día de las FAR, not the "40th anniversary of the FAR"; and no reachable source places the ceremony in Havana, Granma of that date reporting the national review at the Plaza Antonio Maceo in Santiago de Cuba, where Castro spoke. The file places the stop at Santiago and says there that this is a judgement call.

ACRC vice presidency: undated. The pool gave the Association's founding as 1991; it was 7 December 1993 [A MINFAR; EcuRed].

## The book, the widowhood, the legislature, 2000-2026

Interviews for the memoir, Havana, 18 November 2000 and 1 March 2002; Pathfinder Press, 2003, Spanish edition 119 pp., English 115 pp. (not the 119 the pool gave), introduction by Mary-Alice Waters and a foreword by Juan Almeida Bosque that the pool omitted, plus a Farsi edition [A Pathfinder; WorldCat]. Publication was a New York event, cited on her own Havana interview stop rather than staged as a stop of hers.

2 September 2014, Raúl Castro Mercader dies; funeral honours at the FAR pantheon in the Necrópolis Cristóbal Colón, Granma's caption naming *"la general de brigada Tete Puebla y demás familiares"* among the mourners [A Granma obituary]. **Coordinate corrected** to 23.1231 / -82.3986; the pool's figure landed a kilometre outside the walls. Then 8 January 2015, the Trabajadores interview; 6 March 2015, Mesa Redonda, *"Teté Puebla, la Generala, en persona"*; 19 August 2016, Granma International [all A].

**The National Assembly: the largest single correction.** The profile says she has served *"desde su Primera Legislatura"*. The first pass read that as "since the Assembly's First Legislature" and put her in parliament from 1976. It means since HER first legislature. She entered for the IX Legislature: EcuRed gives ratification 18 April 2018, Wikipedia 2017. **Contradiction flagged.** The venue was wrong too: ordinary ANPP sessions have met at the restored Capitolio Nacional since 2018, not the Palacio de Convenciones. For 2023-2028 she is deputy for San Miguel del Padrón, again on the National Defence Commission [A parlamentocubano.gob.cu].

23 September 2025, Premio del Barrio from the CDR, Zona 10, Consejo Popular Ceiba-Kohly, Playa. **Tag upgraded [R] to [A]**: independently reported by Radio Rebelde and ACN, not only by the municipal notice the first pass could not refetch. **Coordinate corrected:** the two lenses gave mutually inconsistent figures for the same event (23.1136 / -82.4381 and 23.1256 / -82.4292); Reparto Kohly is at about 23.109 / -82.411.

9 December 2025, FAR congratulations on her 85th birthday [R ACN headline, not fetched in full]. 2026: still listed as sitting deputy, reserve brigadier general, Defence Commission member and ACRC vice president [A parlamentocubano.gob.cu, fetched directly].

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Puebla is not in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework-applied-backward stops are licensed here. Three relations from the pool were nonetheless not silently deleted; each is recorded with the direction caught.

1. **El Uvero, 28 May 1957** (*backward, and not hers*): offered because the standard Cuban account credits Celia Sánchez there as the first woman in combat in the Rebel Army. Puebla was still in the lowlands. Dropped; El Uvero already sits on `celia_sanchez.journey.json` and `che_guevara.journey.json`.
2. **The Mariana Grajales monument at Santa Ifigenia, 10 October 2017** (*forward, and not hers*): Alberto Lescay's *"Mariana, Madre Ceiba, Madre Patria"* commemorates the platoon's nineteenth-century namesake, not Puebla, and no source places her at the unveiling. Dropped, and worth restating as a negative finding: there is no monument to Puebla.
3. **Buenos Aires** (*lateral, and not hers*): offered as an oblique link through Guevara's Argentine formation. She has no documented connection to Argentina. Dropped.

The pool also recorded, correctly, that Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris and Santa Clara have no documented connection to her.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- Guevara's letter of 22 July 1958: cited, never produced. It alone would move the truce mission from her word into the record.
- The memoir text: unreachable (403), so the testimonial spine here runs through paraphrase. Of *Diario de la Guerra 3*, only p. 299 was ever read.
- No ledger, roster or administrative record survives in anything reachable for the war-victim department (1959-1963), the children's farms (1964-1966), the internationalist-families unit (1966-1969), or the Guaicanamar plan (1969-1978). Every figure in those four postings is her own.
- No promotion orders for first lieutenant (1958), captain (1959) or colonel (1994). No date anywhere for her ACRC vice presidency. Los Güiros and the reservoir near Holguín are unlocated. Her children are named and otherwise undocumented.
- Monuments, streets, schools, stamps, museum galleries: searched across Wikipedia, EcuRed, Granma, the National Assembly and municipal notices, and none found. Stated as a gap in the reachable record, not proof of non-existence; a Cuban press archive or a visit to Yara could still turn up a local plaque.
