# Étienne-Jules Marey (1830-1904): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-28 from the verified research pool for slug `etienne_jules_marey`. Physician who never practised; Collège de France professor 1869 to his death; inventor of the sphygmograph, the fusil photographique, and chronophotography; founder of the Station physiologique at the edge of the Bois de Boulogne and of the Institut Marey beside it.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, institutional recollection, or a claim reaching me only through the dossier crawl with no source I could hold. Contradictions are flagged, **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. The pool arrived with a verification pass already run over it; its corrections are itemised in section 8 and carried in the journey's `date_confidence` fields.

## 1. Beaune and the medical faculty, 1830-1859

- Born **5 March 1830 at Beaune, Côte-d'Or**, only child of **Claude Charles Marey**, wine-trade representative, and **Thérèse-Joséphine Bernard**. **[A: fr.wikipedia, corroborated in the dossier against the BnF authority record and an Académie de médecine eulogy.]**
- **Collège Monge, Beaune**, literary baccalaureate **1849**. **[A: archivesbeaune.wordpress.com, not re-verified against a school register.]** The three-competing-wishes anecdote (mother for the priesthood, father for medicine, the boy for engineering) recurs in institutional biographies with no contemporary letter behind it. **[R]**
- **Medical studies in Paris from 1849**, to 1859. **[A: fr.wikipedia.]**
- **First in the internship competition, 1854**; **Joseph-Simon Beau's service at the Hôpital Cochin**, four years. **[A for Beau and Cochin: academie-medecine.fr, archivesbeaune; the "placed first" ranking is the dossier's summary of an institutional biography, so [R].]**
- **Thesis 4 March 1859**, *Recherches sur la circulation du sang à l'état physiologique et dans les maladies*, jury presided by **Jules Gavarret**; Rignoux, 1859. **[A: academie-medecine.fr.]**

**Gap.** Nothing reachable says how he lived between 1858 and his first paid post in 1867. The rue Cuvier laboratory was self-funded, implying wine-trade family money; no source says so.

## 2. The graphic method, 1859-1866

- **1859: introduced to Auguste Chauveau at the École vétérinaire d'Alfort by Frans Cornelius Donders.** **[A: academie-medecine.fr states it outright; the lens's [R] tag is wrong and is upgraded.]**
- **Private laboratory, rue Cuvier**, c. 1859-1863. **[A for its existence: archivesbeaune; [R] for the "first private laboratory of experimental physiology in Paris" claim, an institutional boast with no census behind it.]** House number not located; the pin is street-level.
- **The portable sphygmograph**, built with **Breguet**, improving on **Karl von Vierordt's** table-bound instrument. Contradiction over year and presenting body, 8.1.
- **1861 onward: intracardiac cardiography in horses with Chauveau at Alfort.** Probes into right atrium and ventricle, synchronised with an external chest trace, establishing that the palpable heartbeat corresponds to **ventricular contraction**. Memoir *Appareils et expériences cardiographiques*, 1863. **[A: dossier crawl summarising the memoir; the finding is uncontested in every account reached.]**
- **1863: *Physiologie médicale de la circulation du sang*** (Delahaye). **[A: dossier bibliography.]**
- **July 1864: the Académie de médecine controversy closed by Gavarret** for the cardiographic reading, against Beau. **[R: dossier crawl citing proceedings I did not see.]**
- **Second laboratory, rue de l'Ancienne-Comédie**, "1860s", reportedly a former performance space of Molière's company. **[R: archivesbeaune; neither the year nor the Molière detail is confirmed. The journey dates it 1865 purely to order the segment and says so.]**

## 3. The chair and the animal machine, 1867-1878

- **1867: deputy to Pierre Flourens at the Collège de France**; **1869: the chair of Histoire naturelle des corps organisés**, held to his death. **[A: fr.wikipedia.]** End date contested, 8.2.
- **1868: *Du mouvement dans les fonctions de la vie*** (Germer-Baillière), the Collège lectures. **[R: dossier bibliography; no catalogue record reached.]**
- **1869: the "artificial insect,"** gold foil wing and a soot-covered glass fibre, the wingtip inscribing its figure-of-eight **mechanically**. **[A: en.wikipedia.]** No month attested; the journey defaults it to December so it falls after the chair, and says the true order is unknown.
- **1872: elected to the Académie nationale de médecine.** **[A: academie-medecine.fr.]**
- **1873: *La Machine animale*** (Germer-Baillière), stating from mechanical traces alone, before photographic proof, that the galloping horse's unsupported phase occurs with the legs **gathered under the body**. **[A: both wikipedias.]** English translation **1874**, *Animal Mechanism*. **[A: archive.org `animalmechanismt00mare`, read directly for the quotations used.]**
- **1878: *La Méthode graphique dans les sciences expérimentales*** (Masson), and **election to the Académie des sciences in Claude Bernard's seat**, Bernard having died that February. **[A: fr.wikipedia.]**

## 4. Naples, Muybridge, and the photographic gun, 1876-1882

- **1 November 1876: requests live torpedo rays from the Stazione Zoologica Anton Dohrn.** **[A: it.wikipedia.]** Earliest attested Naples connection, four years before the villa; the dossier had it starting in the 1880s.
- **1878: writes to Gaston Tissandier of *La Nature***, praising the Palo Alto plates the journal had reproduced, asking to be put in contact with their maker, and describing a **photographic gun** for a bird in flight. **[R: dossier narrative and hekint.org. I never saw the letter; what I hold is paraphrase, so the dossier's [A] is downgraded.]**
- **1880: Villa Maria at Posillipo, bought with Marie-Antoinette-Elvire Vilbort**, identified by it.wikipedia as the wife of **Joseph Vilbort**, director of the Paris paper *Le Globe*. **[A: it.wikipedia.]**
- **26 September 1881: the soirée for Eadweard Muybridge at 11 boulevard Delessert**, zoopraxiscope shown before Helmholtz and the Paris laboratories; Meissonier hosts a companion evening days later. **[A: `muybridge.journey.json`, which supplies the exact address and date the dossier gave only generically, corroborated by the dossier's account of a September 1881 meeting.]** Muybridge's bird studies do not give Marey the precision he wants.
- **February 1882: the *fusil photographique* completed at Naples** with the shutter-maker **Otto Lund**; clockwork at twelve rotations a second, shutter open about 1/720 s, twelve images on a rotating plate. **[A for gun and mechanism: en.wikipedia, hekint.org; [R] for the Naples completion and for Lund, the dossier's alone.]**
- **13 March 1882: first communication to the Académie des sciences**; **10 April: gull and bat sequences.** **[A: fr.wikipedia; corrected day, 8.6.]** Marey names **Jules Janssen** as the mechanism's precedent in *Movement* (1895), ch. VII. **[A: read directly.]**

## 5. The Station physiologique, 1882-1894

- **1882: some 3,500 m² at the edge of the Bois de Boulogne**, funded by the City of Paris and the Ministry of War, the military interest being soldiers' marching efficiency under load after 1870-71. Long track, black cloth wall, calibrated ground, camera hut on rails. **[A: fr.wikipedia; Marey confirms the State and City endowment in the Preface to *Movement*; the military rationale is the dossier's.]** Site and year corrected, 8.4.
- **3 July 1882: fixed-plate photochronography presented to the Académie des sciences.** **[A for 1882: hekint.org, fr.wikipedia; the day is fr.wikipedia's, unchecked against the *Comptes rendus*.]**
- **1883: geometric chronophotography with Georges Demenÿ**, black velvet suits, bright joint markers and limb rods. **[A: en.wikipedia.]** He had described the ambition a decade earlier in *Animal Mechanism*.
- **1884: president of the Société française de navigation aérienne**, turning it with **Victor Tatin** toward the fixed wing. **[A per academie-medecine.fr; the dossier's table tags the same fact [R]. Flagged; [A] taken.]**
- **14 October 1884: *Les Eaux contaminées et le choléra*** to the Académie de médecine, arguing from case distribution and water conditions during an outbreak reaching Beaune. **[A: academie-medecine.fr.]** **Gap:** no source says whether Marey travelled there, so the journey places **no Beaune stop**.
- **29 October 1888: a camera recording on a moving strip of sensitised paper**, about twenty images a second, for analysis not projection **[R for the day; the 1888 strip work is [A] in en.wikipedia]**; **1889: Eastman celluloid and a pressure-plate camera** halting the unperforated strip during exposure **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **11 August 1889: Thomas Edison visits his laboratory** during the Universal Exposition. **[A: fr.wikipedia; venue corrected, 8.5.]** Its causal weight on Edison and Dickson's later perforated film **remains debated**, and is left debated in the campa.
- **1890: *Le Vol des oiseaux*** (Masson) **[A]**; **1890-1891: the Naples films**, *Scènes d'escrime* and *La Vague*, plus fish, eels and rays at the Stazione Zoologica **[R for titles and dates, from the dossier's reading of the Cinémathèque catalogue; [A] for the Naples water and fish studies generally, en.wikipedia]**.

## 6. Rupture, cinema, and the Institute, 1894-1904

- **1894: Georges Demenÿ dismissed** over Marey's insistence on pure research against Demenÿ's commercial applications; Demenÿ patents the **Biographe on 27 July 1894** and, ruined, sells the rights to **Léon Gaumont**, who launches his film business from 1896. **[A: fr.wikipedia; wording corrected, 8.6.]**
- **1894: *Le Mouvement*** (Masson), tr. **Eric Pritchard** 1895; **honorary president of the Société française de photographie**; **the falling-cat sequence**, whose negative survives as Science Museum Group object 1952-75, 36 frames. **[A: both wikipedias, the translation and the museum record read directly.]**
- **1895: president of the Académie des sciences** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; the **Lumière** Cinématographe is publicly demonstrated in December. Marey's crediting the Lumières rather than himself is **[R]**, the dossier's characterisation with no quotation behind it.
- **1897: proposes an international institute for standardising physiological instruments at the Saint-Étienne congress of the Association française pour l'avancement des sciences**; **1898: ratified at the Fourth International Congress of Physiologists, Cambridge.** **[A: fr.wikipedia; numbering checked against en.wikipedia and the IUPS list. See 8.6.]**
- **1899: with Lucien Bull, high-speed cameras and a patented 35-mm chronophotographic gun.** **[R: the patent was not reached. Carried inside the Institut Marey stop.]**
- **1900: presides over the Académie de médecine [A: academie-medecine.fr]; displays instruments at the Exposition Universelle [R: the dossier's, no catalogue entry]; meets Samuel Pierpont Langley of the Smithsonian [A: en.wikipedia]**, who offers support for airflow research.
- **1901: smoke-filament apparatus, 58 parallel trails**, photographing airflow around obstacles. **[A: en.wikipedia.]** The popular "first wind tunnel in the world" is imprecise and is not repeated.
- **1902: the Institut Marey created** with national assembly funding. **[A: fr.wikipedia; dossier errors at 8.4.]** Marey was **not** director for life: the presidency passed after his death to **Chauveau**, then **Kronecker**, then **Charles Richet 1911-1935**, with **Bull** and **Pierre Noguès** running it in practice.
- **15 May 1904: dies in Paris, 16th arrondissement, aged 74**, reportedly of a liver disease. **[A: both wikipedias, archivesbeaune.]** Date contested, 8.3. **Gap:** whether he died at boulevard Delessert or elsewhere is not pinned; the address is used as the likeliest place, which the journey states.

## 7. Afterlife

- **31 August 1913: monument at Beaune**; **1985: asteroid (3456) Etiennemarey.** **[A: fr.wikipedia.]**
- **3 June 1914: the monument holding his ashes inaugurated beside the Institut Marey by President Raymond Poincaré.** **1968-1978:** CNRS teams move to **Gif-sur-Yvette**. **1978:** the buildings are razed for what became **Court No. 1 of Roland-Garros**; the monument survived on the grounds. **[A: fr.wikipedia, *Institut Marey*.]** **2019: Court No. 1 itself demolished**, and **no accessible source says whether the monument still stands**: an open question in the journey, resolved neither way.
- **Plaque at 11 boulevard Delessert**, full text per fr.wikipedia: "Ici habita de 1881 à 1904 Étienne Jules Marey... Un des fondateurs de la psychologie expérimentale et de la science de l'aviation. Créateur de la chronophotographie, base technique de la cinématographie." The dossier carried only the last clause; the journey quotes the whole.
- **Musée Étienne-Jules Marey, Beaune**, created **1955** in the town hall, installed **2001-2002** in the **Porte Marie de Bourgogne**, **47.0217, 4.8389**. **[A: fr.wikipedia.]** **Sourcing caution:** the archivesbeaune page the dossier cited for museum and monument **mentions neither**; the facts hold elsewhere, the citation was wrong.
- **Science Museum Group** holds four Marey celluloid negatives, 1890-1894, object **1952-75**. **[A: museum record.]**
- **Marta Braun, *Picturing Time*** (Chicago, 1992), in full scan at the Internet Archive, is the field's reference biography. **[A as to its existence; not read, so every claim attributed to Braun here, the Francesca Gallone paternity included, is second-hand and tagged [R].]**
- **1884: *The Vivisectors' Directory***, ed. Bryan, pref. Frances Power Cobbe, names Marey among physiologists who cut into living animals. **[A: verified in the Project Gutenberg text.]** A real one-directional, contemporary engagement with him, carried as a **source on his own Alfort cardiography stop**, with no scene of its own.
- **2025-2026 exhibition**, Musée d'Histoire de la Médecine, Paris, foregrounding lesser-known material including his comparative physiology of peoples. **[R: college-de-france.fr.]** Posthumous, other people's work: **no stop**, a `suggested_ref` only.

## 8. Corrections taken and contradictions carried forward

1. **Sphygmograph. NOT RESOLVED.** Built 1859 or 1860; presented 1860 to the **Académie des sciences** (fr.wikipedia) or the **Société de biologie** (academie-medecine.fr). The journey carries "built 1859, presented 1860", leaves the body unnamed, and notes its Institut pin is right only if the Académie account is.
2. **Chair end date. NOT RESOLVED.** The Collège's succession table reads **1869-1903**; chronologies and obituaries **1869-1904**.
3. **Date of death. NOT RESOLVED.** 15 May 1904 (consensus) against 13 May in one Institut Marey history.
4. **The Station physiologique site, the most consequential correction.** The lens gave **48.8414, 2.253**, the **Parc des Princes football stadium**, about 600 m off, repeated at five indices. fr.wikipedia's *Institut Marey* gives **48.8467, 2.2506**, and its *Stade Roland-Garros* confirms Court No. 1 stands on the former Institut site. Creation year 1882, over the lens's 1881; the institute itself founded **1902**, not "1899-1904", and the lens's claimed film of a 1902 inauguration appears nowhere in the cited article and is unused.
5. **The Edison meeting venue.** fr.wikipedia: Edison visited Marey's **laboratory**. The lens staged it on the **Champ de Mars**, right for Edison's own attested location that August (`thomas_edison.journey.json`, 48.8556, 2.2986), wrong here. Which laboratory is unsaid, so the journey's choice of the Station over the Passy apartment is inference.
6. **Dates and wordings corrected from the dossier:** the gun's first communication 13 March 1882, not 27 March (10 April stands); Demenÿ **lost his job** rather than resigned; the institute proposed at **Saint-Étienne 1897** and only ratified at Cambridge 1898, whose precise "26 August" no source supports and which is dropped; Villa Maria bought 1880, the c.1870 hedge dropped.
7. **11 boulevard Delessert coordinate. NOT RESOLVED, and a deliberate departure.** fr.wikipedia gives **48.85896, 2.28664**, taken here; `muybridge.journey.json` carries **48.858, 2.288**; the afterlife lens **48.858, 2.2817**; the interlock lens reported the corpus value as **48.8566, 2.2833**, matching none of them. I preferred the sourced figure over byte-identical inheritance of an unsourced one, the more so because the lens misreported what the corpus holds. Flagged for the operator to overrule; the values lie about 150 m apart, all inside Passy.
8. **Other coordinates corrected:** Faculté de médecine 48.8516, 2.3411; Cochin 48.8369, 2.3403; Alfort 48.8145, 2.4219; Académie de médecine 48.8564, 2.3342 (wrong at four indices); Collège de France 48.8507, 2.3454; Stazione Zoologica 40.8327, 14.2358; ashes monument 48.8472, 2.2492; Beaune museum 47.0217, 4.8389.
9. **Tag disputes, not resolved:** the Alfort/Donders meeting ([R] in the lens, stated outright by academie-medecine.fr, upgraded); the aeronautical presidency ([A] in one lens, [R] in the other); Langley's offer (1900 with a source, 1897 without; 1900 taken).

## 9. Honest gaps

No income source documented for 1858-1867. No contemporary document behind the 1849 anecdote. No confirmation whether Marey went to Beaune during the 1884 cholera outbreak, so no stop is staged there. The Tissandier letter exists to me only as paraphrase. Which laboratory Edison visited on 11 August 1889 is unspecified. The fate of the ashes monument after 2019 is unknown to every source reached. Braun's monograph was not read, so the Gallone paternity rests on relays. And the **primary *Comptes rendus* were never consulted**: every Académie des sciences date used here, 13 March 1882, 10 April 1882, 3 July 1882 and 29 October 1888, comes from an encyclopaedia or from the dossier.

## 10. Interlocks

Named in the journey's campas, because Marey had a **direct relation** to each:

- **`muybridge`**: three years of correspondence from 1878, opened through Tissandier; the soirée at 11 boulevard Delessert on 26 September 1881; and the prior claim, since *La Machine animale* (1873) predicted mechanically the gathered-legs suspension the Palo Alto plates then photographed. Marey also names Muybridge in *Movement*, ch. VII. The Muybridge file independently stages the same soirée: the richest interlock in the pool.
- **`thomas_edison`**: the 11 August 1889 laboratory visit. The Edison file independently places him at the Paris exposition that August.

Considered and **not** named, being shared geography or milieu only: `gustave_eiffel`, `captain_alfred_dreyfus`, `nikola_tesla`, and the Naples cluster (`ada_lovelace`, `alexandre_dumas`, `auguste_rodin`, `antonio_gramsci`). Also `georges_hebert`: the post-1871 physical-culture and military-efficiency context is genuinely shared and the Station's War Ministry funding sits inside it, but a grep of the Hébert file returns no Marey reference and nothing textual connects them either way.
