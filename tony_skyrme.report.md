# Tony Hilton Royle Skyrme (1922-1987): research report
*2026-08-07. Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `tony_skyrme`. British mathematical physicist; Manhattan Project computer; head of theoretical nuclear physics at Harwell; originator of the soliton model of the nucleon later named the skyrmion.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or tradition.

## 0. Method, and the central limitation

This dossier rests on three English Wikipedia articles and on one secondary source read in full as PDF: **Ian J. R. Aitchison, "Tony Skyrme and the Origins of Skyrmions," arXiv:2001.09944 (2020)**. Full list in §11.

**The standard scholarly life could not be read.** T. W. B. Kibble's Royal Society *Biographical Memoirs* notice (doi 10.1098/rsbm.1988.0022) returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden**. Wikipedia's *Tony Skyrme* is largely a précis of that memoir, so this dossier reads Kibble at one remove, unable to check what the encyclopaedia dropped. Every flat date below is *Kibble as summarised by an anonymous editor*.

Second: **the gathering pass produced a large crop of confidently-dated claims the sources do not date**, plus coordinates wrong by one to two kilometres. Where the pool asserted a year no source supplies, the year is demoted to [R] or dropped rather than carried into the journey as attested. Corrections are itemised in place below.

## 1. Lewisham, 1922-c.1936

**[A: Wikipedia, "Tony Skyrme"]** Born **5 December 1922 in Lewisham, south London**, son of a bank clerk.

*Coordinate corrected.* The pool's 51.4452, -0.0209 lies in Catford, 2.1 km south. Nominatim gives 51.4624, -0.0102 for Lewisham. **That value is used**, in both lenses where the wrong one appeared.

**[A]** He attends a **boarding school in Lewisham**. **[R]** The pool dated this 1930; no source gives a year. The journey's date is a flagged placeholder.

**[A: Skyrme's own 1984 recollection, via Aitchison p.5]** A **Thomson-Doodson-Légé tide-predicting machine** stood in his grandfather's house: *"The ingenuity of its mechanism, whereby it could produce this complicated pattern of tides, had considerable influence on me."* The earliest documented fact about his mind is a fact about a machine.

*Corpus interlock, and a real one.* That machine descends from William Thomson, Lord Kelvin, who has his own file here (`lord_kelvin.journey.json`), and Skyrme quoted Kelvin approvingly in the same 1984 talk. The link is Skyrme's own, not an editor's flourish.

## 2. Eton, c.1936-1940

**[R]** A **scholarship to Eton College**. The year of entry is **not stated anywhere consulted**; the pool asserted c. 1936 from typical entry age, then tagged it [A]. Demoted.

**[A]** **Tomline Prize 1939**, **Russell Prize 1940**. Both are Eton mathematics prizes.

*Contradiction inside the pool, flagged.* One pool reconstruction claimed "the specific subjects of the Tomline and Russell prizes are not detailed," while two other entries called both mathematics prizes. The Tomline is specifically Eton's mathematics prize; the reconstruction is dropped.

*Error removed.* The pool put **Freeman Dyson** beside Skyrme and had them "graduate from Cambridge together within the decade." Dyson was at Winchester, left Cambridge at nineteen for RAF Bomber Command, and took his BA in 1945. False in both halves; absent from the journey.

*Eton coordinate.* 51.4913, -0.6067 retained: it falls between Nominatim's 51.4942, -0.6063 and the historic School Yard at 51.4881, -0.6087.

## 3. Trinity College, Cambridge, 1940-1943

**[R: Wikipedia]** Goes up to **Trinity College, Cambridge**, in **1940** to read mathematics. Kept as [R]: 1940 follows from the Russell Prize, not from a stated matriculation date.

**[A]** **Wrangler in Part I, 1942.** **[A]** **Part III, 1943, starred first.** **[A]** Trinity contemporaries in that cohort include **James Lighthill** and **Fritz Ursell**. (Dyson again does not belong.)

**[R]: a claim that partly collapses on checking.** Wikipedia's *Tony Skyrme* says he was **president of the Archimedeans** and gives **no year**; the pool supplied 1942 as [A]. Worse, Wikipedia's *The Archimedeans* does not list him among past presidents at all. **Not adjudicated:** carried as [R], undated, the discrepancy written into the stop's `date_confidence`.

*Canonical pin inherited.* Trinity is pinned in this corpus at **52.207, 0.1146** (`newton`, `niels_bohr`), reused byte-identical rather than the pool's 52.2081, 0.1183. The Cavendish pin 52.2039, 0.1187 (`dirac`) is noted but unused: no source places Skyrme in the Cavendish.

## 4. The war: Birmingham, New York, Los Alamos, 1943-1945

**[A]** In **1943** Skyrme is drafted into war work as a mathematician **under Rudolf Peierls**, who led the team on the theoretical side of atomic energy.

**[R]: the place is inference.** Wikipedia names Peierls and names no city. The pool asserted **Birmingham** as [A]. Peierls held the Birmingham chair from 1937, so it is a strong inference, and an inference is what it stays.

*Anachronism removed.* The pool had Tube Alloys with "its theoretical heart" at Birmingham "since Peierls's recruitment there in 1937." Tube Alloys was authorised in **August 1941**. The chair dates to 1937; the programme does not.

**[A: "British Mission to the Manhattan Project"]** Arrives in **New York in March 1944**, working with the **Kellex Corporation** and Harold Urey's **SAM Laboratories at Columbia** on gaseous diffusion.

*Companions corrected.* The pool put Peierls and Otto Frisch in New York with him. Both had gone (**Peierls reached Los Alamos 8 February 1944**, **Frisch 13 December 1943**); only **Klaus Fuchs** was still there, leaving in August. The journey names Fuchs alone.

**[A]** In **July 1944** he follows Peierls to **Los Alamos** and works the **plutonium implosion problem** on IBM punched-card tabulating machines.

*Canonical pin inherited.* Los Alamos is pinned across the physics wing at **35.8809, -106.307** (`robert_oppenheimer`, `richard_feynman`, `von_neumann`, `fermi`, `niels_bohr`). Reused exactly.

**Interlocks.** **[A]** Oppenheimer directed the site; Feynman ran the punched-card computing effort in Bethe's division at the same machines, the closest thing to a shared workbench in the corpus; von Neumann worked implosion lens geometry on the same mesa; Fermi arrived 1 September 1944 to head F Division; Bohr arrived 30 December 1943 as Nicholas Baker, the anchor date several corpus files inherit. All are named in campa.

**[R]: flagged, not asserted.** **No corpus file names Skyrme.** A grep for `Birmingham|Bristol|Harwell|Tube Alloys|Peierls` across all 256 journey files returned no hits in physics-relevant files. The overlap is geographic, not textual; nothing shows these men noticing him.

**[R]** His presence at the **Trinity test, 16 July 1945**, is **not documented**; the campa treats the test as the outcome of the arithmetic, not as an event he attended. *Geography error removed:* the pool called Los Alamos "the deserts of New Mexico," but it is a pine-forested mesa at about 2,200 m.

## 5. The postwar circuit, 1946-1950

The least documented stretch of the life, and where the gathering pass invented most.

**[R]** A **postwar fellowship at Oxford**. Wikipedia mentions Oxford; **no source dates it**, names the college, or names the department. The pool gave "1945/46" as [A] while conceding the Clarendon Laboratory was only "a representative physics landmark." Date and building are both reconstructions.

*Error removed.* The pool had Peierls "already reestablished in Birmingham" in 1945. Peierls returned to England in **January 1946**.

**[R]** A **research fellowship at Birmingham**, undated in the sources; the pool's 1947 is unsupported. Campus coordinate 52.4508, -1.9305 is correct.

*Reconstruction dropped.* The pool reasoned about "the gap between Skyrme's 1946 Oxford fellowship and his 1947 Birmingham fellowship." Neither endpoint is dated by any source, so there is no gap to measure; the inference rests on its own invented numbers.

**[A]** **April 1947: skiing at Saas-Fee** (46.1078, 7.9276), documented by surviving photographs. One of very few precisely dated private facts in the life, surviving because he kept negatives.

**[A]** **MIT, 1948-1949** (42.3598, -71.0921), and **member at the Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton, 1949-1950**. What he worked on at either is not recorded anywhere consulted. Of the pool's two Princeton coordinates, **40.3323, -74.6666** is essentially exact against Nominatim's 40.3323, -74.6658 and is used; 40.3292, -74.665 is discarded.

**[A]** **1949: marries Dorothy Mildred Millest**, a lecturer in experimental nuclear physics. **No children.**

**[R]** **The wedding location is unknown.** No source names a venue or even a country, and 1949 straddles his Birmingham base and his American year. The journey pins Birmingham and says in the campa that the pin is a reconstruction.

## 6. Harwell, 1950-1962

**[A: Wikipedia, verbatim]** "Returning to Britain both he and Dorothy gained posts at the **Atomic Energy Research Establishment at Harwell from 1950 to 1962**."

*Coordinate corrected.* The pool's 51.571, -1.317 sits 1.1 km south-west. Nominatim gives 51.5795, -1.3082 for Harwell Campus, and that is used.

**[A]** From **1954**, **head of the theoretical nuclear physics group**, whose members included the young **John Bell**.

**[A]** He develops the **zero-range (effective) potential**, the Skyrme force, still used in nuclear structure calculations. **[R]** Also a **three-body short-range method**, which no source dates: nothing narrower than "the Harwell years" can honestly be said.

**[A] date corrected.** "**The effective nuclear potential**," *Nuclear Physics* **9** (4): 615-634. The pool said 1958; Wikipedia's own citation and the volume both read **1959**.

**[A]** "**A Non-Linear Field Theory**," *Proc. Roy. Soc. A* **260**: 127-138, **February 1961**: the nucleon as a stable topological knot in a continuous field rather than a point. Verbatim, from the third paper of the series, as reproduced by Aitchison:

> "The periodicity in these variables means that they are not uniquely determined (in the classical sense) by the physical state of the system. In regions of weak field the different determinations of angle generate a set of equivalent descriptions, forming separate sheets of a multiple-valued system; but when the fields become strong these sheets may cross one another, forming singularities."

*Citation corrected.* The pool cited Aitchison p.5. It is **p.8**, verified by reading the PDF.

**[A]** "**A Unified Field Theory of Mesons and Baryons**," *Nuclear Physics* **31**: 556-569 (1962). Its published abstract, from the INSPIRE-HEP record, is the journey quote for that stop.

*A fragment declined.* Aitchison reports Skyrme identifying an N=1 solution as "a particle at x0." Genuine, but it is Aitchison's framing rather than continuous Skyrme prose, and four words carry no quotational weight. **Not** used as a `quote` field. (The pool cited p.6; it is p.8.)

## 7. The circumnavigation and Kuala Lumpur, 1958-1964

**[A]** **1958-1959: a one-year overland circumnavigation** by car and Land Rover.

**[R]** **No route survives.** No crossing, campsite, or waypoint is named anywhere consulted. **Dover** (51.1279, 1.3134) is a *symbolic* threshold, declared as such in the campa. *Invented detail removed:* the pool said they returned "thirteen months later"; the source says one year.

**[A: Wikipedia, near-verbatim]** On the tour "**They fell in love with the lush tropical gardens of Malaysia and decided to settle there.**"

*A pool reconstruction that is simply wrong, flagged.* The pool proposed the Malaya move "may reflect a deliberate step back from the Harwell weapons-adjacent establishment," adding that "no source states his motive explicitly." A source states it explicitly, and the motive is gardens. Dropped: the Kuala Lumpur campa does **not** frame the move as conscientious withdrawal.

**[R]** **No garden is named.** **Lake Gardens / Perdana Botanical Garden** (3.1439, 101.6869) stands in as the likeliest landmark, declared as a substitution.

**[A]** **1962-1964: University of Malaya, Kuala Lumpur** (3.1225, 101.6532, about 450 m from the Universiti Malaya centroid and within the campus).

*Two errors removed.* The pool called 1962 "eight years past" independence; Malaya became independent **31 August 1957**, so **five**. It also placed the **Dewan Tunku Canselor** on campus in 1962, before it was built.

## 8. Birmingham, 1964-1987

**[A]** **1964: Professor of Mathematical Physics at Birmingham**, held until death: twenty-three years, the longest posting of his career.

*Framing corrected.* The pool called Birmingham in 1964 "the department Peierls built," implying reunion. **Peierls left for the Wykeham chair at Oxford in 1963.** Skyrme inherited the department; he did not rejoin his patron.

*Coordinate caveat.* The pool's Watson Building value 52.4498, -1.9309 **could not be confirmed** (Nominatim returns nothing); the campus point **52.4508, -1.9305** is used for all Birmingham stops.

**[A]** "**Kinks and the Dirac Equation**," *J. Math. Phys.* **12**: 1735-1743 (1971). Abstract from INSPIRE-HEP, used verbatim as that stop's quote.

**[A] date corrected, and the correction propagates.** The term **"skyrmion" was coined in 1979** by **Pak and Tze**, "Chiral solitons and current algebra," *Annals of Physics* **117** (1): 164-194. The pool asserted 1982 twice and 1979 twice without noticing the conflict. **1979 is correct**, and three figures change with it: the interval from 1961 is **eighteen** years, from 1962 **seventeen**, and Skyrme was **fifty-six**. The 1982 date looks like a confusion with the 1983 paper.

**[A]** **1983: Adkins, Nappi, and Witten** apply the Skyrme model quantitatively to nucleon properties, to roughly 30% accuracy, reviving interest.

*Interlock.* `edward_witten.journey.json` already exists, and this is the one place where the Skyrme story runs directly into a corpus traveller's own published work; Witten is named in that campa. The stop uses the Princeton pin **40.3323, -74.6666**, but the paper's address is the university rather than the Institute, so the pin stands for the town and the campa says so.

**[A: Skyrme's own words, via Aitchison p.4]** In **1984** he gives a talk at **Cosener's House, Abingdon**, reconstructed and published as "The Origins of Skyrmions" in *A Breadth of Physics: Proc. Peierls 80th Birthday Symposium*, eds. Dalitz and Stinchcombe (World Scientific, 1988), pp.193-202.

*Tension, left open.* The talk is dated 1984; Peierls, born 5 June 1907, turned eighty in 1987. Not impossible for a collected volume, but unexplained. The date within 1984 is unknown, and the coordinate 51.6690, -1.2795 is Abingdon, approximate.

In that talk he quoted **Lord Kelvin**:

> "I can never satisfy myself until I can make a mechanical model of a thing. If I can make a mechanical model all the way through I can understand it. As long as I cannot make a mechanical model all the way through I cannot understand; and that is why I cannot get the electromagnetic theory."

*Misquote corrected.* The pool rendered the third sentence "the whole way through"; Aitchison reads "**all** the way through" in both. **[R]** overall: Kelvin's wording at two removes, through Hathaway's 1884 stenographic report and then Skyrme's talk, and the Baltimore lectures were **not** inspected.

**[A]** **1985: Hughes Medal**, cited "for his contributions to theoretical particle and nuclear physics, and his discovery that particle-like entities simulating the properties of baryons can occur in non-linear meson field theories." **[A] He is never elected FRS.**

*Coordinate corrected.* The pool's 51.5014, -0.1419 falls near Green Park, about 1 km off. Nominatim gives **51.5060, -0.1320** for Carlton House Terrace.

**[A]** **Dies 25 June 1987 at Selly Oak Hospital, Birmingham**, of an **embolism following routine surgery**, aged sixty-four, still holding the chair.

*Coordinate corrected, and an internal contradiction resolved at source.* The two pool lenses gave two different and both wrong values for the same building (52.4327, -1.9385 and 52.4344, -1.9484). Wikipedia's *Selly Oak Hospital* gives **52.4370, -1.9371**.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A]** His **photograph collection** passes to the **Bodleian Libraries, Oxford** (51.7534, -1.254): the fullest surviving visual record of a man whose itineraries are otherwise second hand.

**[R] a gap, stated as a gap.** **No source records his grave, ashes, or burial plot.** Buried or cremated, and where, is unresolved. No obituary, memoir, or grave registry was reachable in this session.

**[R] a second gap.** **No monument, plaque, named building, or centenary event** could be confirmed. Lewisham keeps nothing.

**[A]** **1994**: *Selected Papers, with Commentary, of Tony Hilton Royle Skyrme*, World Scientific Series in 20th Century Physics vol. 3.

*Editor corrected.* The pool named **G. E. Brown and A. D. Jackson**; Wikipedia's *Skyrme model* citation and Open Library both give **Brown as sole editor**, so Jackson is removed. Publisher, year, and volume are correct. No translation could be confirmed.

**[A]** **Magnetic skyrmions** are observed at atomic scale in an **iron monolayer on iridium(111) in 2011**; **room-temperature Bloch skyrmion lattices** in cobalt/palladium multilayers follow around **2015**.

**[R] no laboratory named.** The source gives the observations, not the institutions. The journey anchors the stop at Birmingham and says in the campa that the pin stands for the man, not the experiment. Inventing a laboratory city would have been the easy and wrong move.
