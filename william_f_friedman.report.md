## 1. Kishinev and Pittsburgh, 1891-1910

**[A: Friedman's signed personnel history, NSA declassified documents ACC37442; Wikipedia]** Born 24 September 1891 in Kishinev, Bessarabia, to Frederick Friedman and Rosa Trust; the form gives "Kishiniev, Russia." Pittsburgh public schools, Pittsburgh Central High, Michigan Agricultural College in 1910.

- **Contradiction, given name.** Wikipedia has **Wolf**, the NSA Hall of Honor page **Wolfe**. Not adjudicated; the file writes Wolf and flags it.
- **Source misattribution, corrected.** The pool carried father-emigrates-alone-1892 and family-follows-1893, both cited to Wikipedia. The article says neither: it has the whole family leaving in 1892, and puts the father's origin in Bucharest. The split may be true (it is in Fagone) but not on that citation. The file dates the stop 1892 and prints both readings.
- **Contradiction, name change.** Wikipedia's "three years later" gives 1895 from 1892; the personnel history ties it to the father's 1896 naturalization. Both mid-1890s; not adjudicated.
- **Gap.** Eighteen Pittsburgh years leave almost no reachable trace beyond the schooling: no address, no photographs, no account of the household. Stated as a gap in the campa itself.

## 2. The genetics training, 1910-1916

**[A: Irwin Goldman, *Genetics*, PMC5419462]** Six months at Michigan Agricultural College, not the "roughly a year" the pool carried; then Cornell, B.S. in genetics 1914, graduate work and teaching to 1916 under Rollins Emerson. **[R: Goldman]** Summers of 1912 and 1913 at the Carnegie Institution's Station for Experimental Evolution, Cold Spring Harbor, under Charles B. Davenport, alongside George Harrison Shull.

- **Stop dropped.** The pool's 1914 stop at the New York State Agricultural Experiment Station, Geneva, New York, was cited to Goldman, who does not mention that station and has Friedman a Cornell graduate student throughout. Recorded rather than deleted: if a personnel-record citation turns up, restore it at 42.8758, -77.0078, the station campus, not the downtown point the pool used.
- **Eugenics, stated flat.** Davenport was the era's central American promoter of eugenics and the Eugenics Record Office sat on the same Carnegie-funded campus from 1910. No source places Friedman in that office or its work, and none shows him advocating eugenics. The campa states the adjacency and states that he never published for it or against it. That is all the record supports.

## 3. Riverbank, 1915-1918

**[A: Wikipedia, "William F. Friedman," "Riverbank Laboratories," "Elizebeth Smith Friedman"; NSA monograph *DAWNAMERICANCRYPTOLOGY_HATCH.PDF*]** Fabyan hired him **September 1915**, the dossier having the year only. Elizebeth Smith, working with Elizabeth Wells Gallup on the Baconian biliteral cipher, brought him into the cipher department in 1916; married **May 1917**, day and venue unestablished. Riverbank became a de facto federal cryptologic training centre after American entry into the war, and worked traffic tied to the network prosecuted in the Hindu-German Conspiracy trials.

- **Dating error, corrected.** The pool had him formulating the index of coincidence in the 1917-1918 Riverbank stop. He published it in **1922** as Riverbank Publication No. 22, after leaving Fabyan's payroll though under the Riverbank imprint. It is now a 1922 stop at his Washington pin.
- **The pool contradicted itself about the biliteral photograph.** Geography dated the KNOWLEDGE IS POWER group photograph to 1917 at Riverbank, citing a Marshall Foundation portal record it did not load; afterlife dated the same photograph to **winter 1918 at Aurora, Illinois**, citing William H. Sherman's *Cabinet* article, which it did read and which supplies the detail that the line fell four men short of the final R. The file follows the read source; the portal record remains unchecked by anyone in the chain.

## 4. France and the break with Fabyan, 1918-1921

**[A: Wikipedia; NSA monograph]** First lieutenant, National Army, in the Code and Cipher Solving Section of G-2 at AEF General Headquarters, Chaumont; commission generally placed May 1918. Return to Riverbank on promises of back pay, deterioration over withheld salary and pressure to endorse Gallup outright, departure late 1920 or early 1921.

- **Hard contradiction, resolved with the reasoning shown.** Chronology said he returned to the United States in 1920 (Wikipedia); geography said discharged April 1919, back at Riverbank May 1919 (NSA monograph). Both cannot stand. The 1919 reading is the only one under which a 1919-1920 Riverbank period exists at all, and it is the more specific claim, so the file follows it and prints the 1920 figure in `date_confidence` rather than dismissing it.

## 5. Washington and the Signal Intelligence Service, 1921-1936

**[A: Wikipedia; personnel history; dossier works list item 17]** Six-month Signal Corps contract from 1921; sole Signal Corps cryptanalyst by 1922; "Edgar Allan Poe, Cryptographer," *American Literature* 8:3, November 1936. **[R: dossier's citation of the signed personnel statement, not refetched]** Barbara born 14 October 1923, John Ramsay 28 July 1926. **[A: Marshall Foundation photo essay]** Military Road household by 1927; exact address never located.

- **Correction, the founding of SIS.** The dossier's 10 May 1929 date is the War Department directive transferring cryptologic work to the Signal Corps, not the service's operational founding: Wikipedia dates the staffed start to April 1930, with three junior cryptanalysts. The file states both. **[A]** Kullback, Rowlett and Sinkov hired off a civil-service examination in 1930.
- **[R], carried not confirmed.** IBM punched-card tabulating equipment in SIS use from 1936. Every NSA and Army page cited for it returned **HTTP 403**. Kept because it is specific, checkable and consistent with everything around it, and tagged in the stop.
- **[R], and cut for length.** Official travel in 1928 listing France, Belgium, Sweden, and in 1932 Spain and France, on a form whose dates and purposes are partly obscured. Brussels, Stockholm and Madrid came out of a 47-stop draft to reach the 45-stop cap; one Paris stop now names all three 1928 countries and states the illegibility. To restore, at placeholder city centres and not documented sites: Brussels 50.8467, 4.3525; Stockholm 59.3251, 18.0711; Madrid 40.4168, -3.7038.

## 6. PURPLE, 1939-1941

**[A: Marshall Foundation, "Breaking Purple in William Friedman's Own Words"]** February 1939, RED replaced by the "B" machine; April 1939, the six most frequent letters determined; 20 September 1940, first repeating sequences from six messages sharing identical indicator text; 27 September 1940, two full translations; a working machine analog completed in 1940.

- **The largest single-source dependency in the file.** All of the above, and every quotation in the PURPLE segment, comes from that one fetched page, which excerpts Friedman's *Preliminary Historical Report on the Solution of the "B" Machine*, 14 October 1940. **The report itself was not located online**, so nothing here is cross-checked against the document it excerpts.
- **Source misattribution, corrected: Genevieve Grotjan.** The pool cited that page for Grotjan's discovery. **It does not mention her at all**; it deliberately credits the group rather than an individual, which is Friedman's own position in the report it quotes. Her discovery is real, well attested elsewhere, and conventionally dated 20 September 1940, the same day the page dates the first repeating sequences. She is named in the file, folded into that stop, with `date_confidence` saying that the attribution rests on the general literature and not on the source cited beside it.
- **Correction, the hospitalization.** The pool gave 4 January 1941 for admission to Walter Reed's neuropsychiatric section; no fetched source supports the day. Wikipedia's "Elizebeth Smith Friedman" article gives January 1941 and two and a half months in the ward, and no more. The file defaults the day and says so. Stated flat: the record calls it a nervous breakdown, the accounts around it say exhaustion and a depression running back into the 1930s, and the retrospective bipolar label found in later writing is not a contemporary diagnosis and is not asserted. No clinical record is available.

## 7. The war, the alliance, and the agency, 1942-1958

**[A: Wikipedia]** Arlington Hall commandeered 10 June 1942 and made the wartime headquarters of Army cryptanalysis against Japanese systems including PURPLE. Exceptional Civilian Service Award 1944; Medal for Merit from Truman 1946; head of AFSA's cryptographic division 1949; NSA formed 1952; National Security Medal from Eisenhower 1955, the same year as the heart attack and retirement from full-time federal employment.

**[A: National Security Archive briefing book on the Hagelin-Friedman "gentlemen's understanding," 19 February 2020]** Cosmos Club meeting with Boris Hagelin 1951; notice of a coming Zug visit 9 April 1953; USCIB authorization 27 December 1954; letters to Hagelin 7 and 19 January 1955; travel confirmed 11 February; **Zug visit and Hagelin's immediate acceptance, February 1955**; draft report 15 March, final 28 March; active role ends 1958; CIA and BND jointly acquire Crypto AG in 1970, after his death. February 1955 is more specific than the dossier's undated "in 1955" and comes from a page fetched this pass, so it is used.

- **[A] and [R] together, the Bletchley visit.** Spring 1943 is corroborated by Wikipedia and distinguished there from the 1941 Sinkov and Rosen mission Friedman did not join. The diary and Colin Mackinnon's web edition are dossier-sourced; **colinmackinnon.com could not be refetched (certificate error)**, so the edition is [R].

## 8. Shakespeare, the seizure, the papers, 1954-1971

**[R: dossier's citation of NSA's declassified correspondence index; those pages returned HTTP 403]** Special Assistant to the Director 1954-55, organizer of the NSA Scientific Advisory Board, member until 1969; correspondence naming John von Neumann, Claude Shannon and John Tukey. No primary document was read this pass. **[A: Wikipedia]** Folger Shakespeare Library prize 1955 for the manuscript submitted as *The Cryptologist Looks at Shakespeare*.

- **Source misattribution, corrected: the $100,000.** The pool cited Wikipedia's Friedman article for the 1956 congressional authorization. That article mentions no $100,000, no Congress and no settlement; its only patent sentence concerns SIGABA. The claim is plausible and the anti-legend framing, settlement rather than lawsuit, is right, but the citation is wrong. The file cites the 1950 attorneys' settlement memorandum in NSA's declassified correspondence, folder 094, which could not be refetched, and tags the stop accordingly.
- **Contradiction, publication year.** 1957 is the standard first-edition date for *The Shakespearean Ciphers Examined* and what Wikipedia gives; the archive.org record `shakespeareancip00frie` the pool cited for it carries **1958**. The file uses 1957 and prints the discrepancy.
- **Tag upgraded: the 1959 seizure.** Chronology tagged the 26 June 1959 removal of papers [R]; geography and afterlife tagged it [A]. The fetched Marshall Foundation page gives the date explicitly, with the Friedmans' remark, "Even the smallest nations don't care a fig about them." **[A]**; the chronology tag was the outlier. The address is still [R]: that page says only "Capitol Hill" with no street number, so "310 Second Street SE" rests on the dossier alone, and the pool's two lenses put it in two different places.
- **Contradiction of date.** The pool dated the Marshall gift to 1969 while conceding in its own note that the foundation dates the physical move to 1971, Elizebeth supervising the packing. William made the arrangement and did not live to see it done. The file dates the stop **1971**. `diffie.journey.json` puts the same library about 900 m from Wikipedia's figure, so the two files now disagree about one building; flagged for the operator, since editing Diffie's file is not a subagent's call.

## 9. Death and afterlife, 1969-2017

**[A: Wikipedia; official memorial record via dossier]** Died at home on Capitol Hill, Sunday 2 November 1969, aged 78; no medical cause given, and the 12 November date in derivative sources is an error. **[A: Arlington National Cemetery, "Notable Graves"]** Section 8, grave 6379-A, shared with Elizebeth, whose ashes were placed there after her death in 1980; shared epitaph "Knowledge is power." **[A: USPTO via Google Patents]** US 6,097,812, filed 25 July 1933, issued 1 August 2000; US 6,130,946, from a 1936 application, issued 10 October 2000 (the count of nine sealed applications is dossier, **[R]**). **[A: US National Archives, 30 April 2015]** Release of 20 April 2015: about 7,000 records, more than 50,000 pages, roughly 85 percent released in full. **[A: Elonka Dunin, elonka.com/friedman]** 17 April 2017, the alternating serif and sans-serif letters of KNOWLEDGE IS POWER read in fives as Bacon's biliteral cipher give WFF, confirmed by a note in Elizebeth's hand found by Jason Fagone in the Marshall archive.

- **Gap.** The Arlington guide lists the grave under Elizebeth's name only and gives no burial date for William, so the burial stop carries his death date as an ordering device and says so.
- **Stop ownership.** Dunin travelled to the grave; Friedman did not travel to his own solution. The stop is his because the **object** is his monument, and Dunin's work is cited as the source that reads it. She has no journey file here, so no interlock arises.
- **Cut for length.** The 1988 Military Intelligence Corps Hall of Fame induction at Fort Huachuca was drafted and cut to reach the 45-stop cap; the corrected coordinate is in the table. The dossier's 1999 date for the separate NSA/CSS Cryptologic Hall of Honor induction is unverified.

## Legends addressed, not repeated

**[R]**, all of them, and none staged as a stop. **"The man who broke Purple,"** Ronald W. Clark's 1977 title: NSA's institutional history credits Rowlett with daily leadership, Grotjan with the decisive pattern, and Small, Snyder and Rosen with major contributions, and Friedman's own October 1940 report refuses individual credit in words the file quotes. The team achievement itself is not in dispute. **"He sued the government and won $100,000":** no court judgment located, a legislative settlement, section 8. **"He secretly solved the Voynich manuscript or the Beale ciphers":** he ran study groups on both and floated the hypothesis that the Voynich manuscript encodes an artificial or universal language, but no authenticated solution by him exists and both remain open in his own research files. **NSA's OPS1 building** named for both Friedmans, reported as 2002, with the NSA page returning HTTP 403. **PBS's "The Codebreaker,"** premiered 2021 per the dossier and listed April 2023 on the fetched page, which is Elizebeth's film and belongs to her file.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

`EXCEPTIONS.md` was read before anything was dropped. **Friedman is not on that list**, so no framework-applied-backward stops are licensed and none were written. Two relations in the pool are not rooted in his own life, and neither has been silently deleted.

1. **`al_kindi`, direction caught: backward**, the licensable direction. The pool offered al-Kindi's ninth-century *Risala fi Istikhraj al-Mu'amma* as the founding treatise of the discipline Friedman named and professionalized. It is not apparatus in the strict sense, since Friedman applied no theory of al-Kindi's to anything; it is a lineage claim, and the pool's own note calls it "purely thematic ancestry across thirteen centuries." Kept out of the file and recorded here. If the operator wants a cipher-wing lineage framing, adding Friedman to the list would license one stop at his own Washington pin.
2. **`diffie`, direction caught: forward**, which is never licensed. Diffie's 1973 reading at Lexington postdates Friedman's working life; handled without a grant, as a `suggested_ref` on Friedman's own 1971 stop.

One further forward-facing item, disclosed rather than hidden: the **1970 CIA and BND purchase of Crypto AG** and the **2020 publication of the Hagelin documents** are named in the Zug and 1958 campas. These are not apparatus but the ordinary afterlife of an arrangement Friedman made, of the same kind as the 2000 patents and the 2015 declassification, sitting on his own file about his own doings. No earlier subject's file is touched.
