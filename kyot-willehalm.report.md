# Kyot-Willehalm — Toulouse to Dornachbrugg (c. 755-848)

**Shape:** 26 stops in 8 named segments (the Ward of Aquitaine, the Marches of Septimania, Gellone's monk's-turn and vanishing terminus, Alischanz, the Grail Quest, Catalonia and the loss of Schoysiane, the long silence at Le Verdus, Dornachbrugg and the writing of the grail). Calendar julian. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Only 2 of 26 stops carry a quote, both original Middle High German lines from Wolfram, honestly held to what the primary poetic text actually says rather than paraphrased or invented.

## Sources
- **William of Gellone**, Wikipedia and Catholic Encyclopedia ("St. William of Gellone"): the conventional historical layer — Count of Toulouse 790, defeat at the Orbieu 793, capture of Barcelona 801, founding of Gellone abbey 804, retirement as a monk 806, death 812 or 814, canonization 1066.
- **Ermoldus Nigellus, *In Honorem Hludowici*** (826-828): the nearest contemporary Carolingian witness to the Barcelona campaign, naming Willelmus among Louis's captains.
- **Wolfram von Eschenbach, *Willehalm*** and the French *Aliscans*: the epic layer, the battle of Alischanz, Gyburc/Arabel, Vivianz's death, the emperor's relief host.
- **Wolfram von Eschenbach, *Parzival*** and the fragment *Titurel*: Kyot as Wolfram's named source, the Flegetanis/Toledo passage, Schoysiane, Sigune, Tampenteire, Condwiramurs, the Munsalvaesche genealogy, the leash-text of Sigune and Schionatulander.
- **Werner Greub, *Willehalm-Kyot: der Gewährsmann Wolframs von Eschenbach*** (Willehalm Institut, 1991) and the wider Willehalm Institut für Anthroposophie corpus (willehalm.nl, the Munsalvaesche Project, mythische-orte.eu): the identification of Kyot with the historical William of Gellone, the redated chronology extending his life to 848, and the geography (Andlau, the Arlesheim Ermitage and Hornikopf as Munsalvaesche, Dornach/Dornachbrugg).
- **Ellen Schalk, "Kyot and the Stellar Script of Parsifal"** (*Journal of Anthroposophical Work in Germany* 160, 1987, trans. Richard Bloedon, Sophia Foundation): the clearest connected narrative summary of Greub's Kyot-Willehalm biography, used as the backbone for sequencing the grail-quest, Schoysiane, Sigune and Condwiramurs stops.
- **Wilhelm Deinert, *Ritter und Kosmos im Parzival*** (1960): the corrected astronomical reading of Wolfram's Saturn passage that underlies the 13 May 848 dating, independently re-verified here by Julian computus (Easter 848 = 25 March, Pentecost = 13 May).

## Judgment calls
- **The bilingual instruction.** The curator's brief asked for English and Spanish versions of every prose field, but the explicit JSON shape given (and every one of the 108 sibling files, including the Spanish-language lives like Bolívar and Belgrano, which are written single-language in English) carries no `_es` duplicate fields. I followed the exact schema and matched siblings byte-for-byte in shape rather than invent a new field convention unverified anywhere in the corpus; flagging this discrepancy rather than silently resolving it either way.
- **Two historical layers, one register.** Conventional history closes William's life in 812 or 814; Greub's redating carries "Kyot-Willehalm" to 848. I kept both visible by staging the conventional death notice as its own stop (Gellone, 812-05-28) narrated as the terminus the ordinary chronicles give and the tradition refuses, rather than silently overwriting or silently ignoring either layer.
- **Condwiramurs as niece.** Greub's own account (via Schalk) has Kyot deliver Sigune to her aunt Herzeloyde and later support his niece Condwiramurs (daughter of his brother Tampenteire) after her marriage to Parzival. The curator's brief compresses this into "escorts her to Dornachbrugg and hands her to Parzival," implying she had not yet joined Parzival. I followed the curator's brief as the authoritative shape for this dataset, treating it as this atlas's own redaction of the tradition, and noted the divergence here rather than silently harmonizing it back to Schalk's fuller sequence.
- **13 May 848.** A synthesized secondary source stated this exact date; I did not take it on faith and instead recomputed Julian Easter for AD 848 (25 March, via the standard Gauss/Meeus algorithm) and counted 49 days to Pentecost, landing independently on 13 May. The coincidence is a genuine check, not a repeated claim.
- **Andlau.** The curator named it as a waypoint of the 819-821 grail quest; the only verifiable Andlau/grail connection I could find is a modern one (the Route du Gral association founded by Christian d'Andlau in Strasbourg, and Baroness Balbina von Andlau's 1785 landscaping of the Arlesheim Ermitage). I kept the stop, marked traditional, and noted the modern echo honestly in suggested_refs rather than inventing a ninth-century Andlau source I don't have.
- **Coordinates for the epic-only sites** (Alischanz/Oransche, Katelangen, the road to Dornachbrugg) are traditional/legendary placements: Alischanz at the Alyscamps necropolis, Arles, the site the chanson tradition itself names; Katelangen at Girona, standing in for the Catalan march; the road stop anchored concretely at Besançon rather than left as an unnamed midpoint.

## Gaps and time-folds
The whole dataset is built on a genuinely fringe scholarly identification (Werner Greub's anthroposophist redating, never accepted by mainstream medievalists, for whom Kyot remains "probably Wolfram's own invention"). The register instruction is to narrate the tradition's own claims as fact without debunking, so the fold is not hidden, it is the subject: a man the Church's own calendar buries in 812 is walked, by his own later witness, another thirty-six years to a bridge near Basel. The 823-841 stretch at Le Verdus is the thinnest in sources, a near-twenty-year silence bridged with only three stops (the retreat itself, word of Sigune's betrothal, word of Tampenteire's death); I did not pad it with invented incident.

## The five richest episodes
1. **The false terminus at Gellone (812).** The stop where the conventional chronicle's death notice and the Kyot-tradition's continuation are made to stand in the same frame, deliberately, as the dataset's own hinge.
2. **Alischanz, twice fought (818).** The broken first field and Vivianz's death, the ride to Aachen to collect a debt from the emperor he once tutored as a boy, and Gyburc's own plea for mercy toward the defeated at the second field.
3. **Munsalvaesche found (820).** The identification, per Greub, of the actual hill above Arlesheim as Wolfram's grail castle, and the marriage to Schoysiane that makes the old soldier kin to the grail line by blood.
4. **Sigune's leash-text (836), read from Le Verdus.** The unfinished hunting-leash inscription that becomes Wolfram's own unfinished *Titurel*, received by the old man at second hand as one more piece of the larger account he is building.
5. **Dornachbrugg, Whitsun 848.** The astronomically dated hand-off of Condwiramurs to Parzival, the moment the journey's own frame names as its purpose: the traveler becomes the source.

## Connection to the atlas
This journey sits at the joint between the atlas's Carolingian strand and its grail/esoteric strand. Its early segments (Toulouse, the Orbieu, Barcelona, Aachen) run parallel to `charlemagne.journey.json`, sharing Louis the Pious as the connective figure raised at Toulouse under Guilhem's own hand. Its Alischanz segment sits beside the atlas's chanson-de-geste and crusade material the way `bernard_clairvaux.journey.json` sits beside `molay.journey.json`: two lives that face each other across a shared institution, here a shared emperor rather than a shared order. Its grail-quest and Munsalvaesche segments belong with the atlas's esoteric-cosmology cluster (Steiner-adjacent material, `blavatsky.journey.json`, `annie_besant.journey.json`), rendered descriptively as the mythos its own tradition authored, without endorsement. And its final act, an old soldier who becomes the unacknowledged source of the whole grail literature, rhymes with `tolkien.journey.json` and `nizami.journey.json` as journeys whose destination is the writing itself rather than a place on the map.

Parzival 489,24-26 had a real corruption, "daz wan uns bi der wunden kuont" for the canon's "daz wart uns bî der wunden kuont"; restored, with Lachmann's diacritics ("dô der sterne Sâturnus / wider an sîn zil gestuont / ..."). The two new quotes (below) were taken directly from the same edition, not from memory.

**Stops added (canon plainly offered more; corpus norm ~40, file was at 26).** Three additions, all from the tradition's own primary layer, none invented:
1. *Oransche, Arabel won* (Orange, 44.1361, 4.8083; c. 815, traditional) — the Prise d'Orange episode the epic itself presupposes: without the taking of the city and the baptism of Arabel as Gyburc, the "War for Gyburc" segment began in medias res with the marriage unexplained.
2. *Dôlet, the discarded manuscript of Flegetanis* (Toledo, 39.8628, -4.0273; late 818, traditional) — Wolfram's own explicit account of where Kyot found the grail-source (Parzival 453,11-14, quoted). Previously only alluded to in the Andlau campa.
3. *Anschouwe, the chronicles searched* (Angers, 47.4712, -0.5518; early 819, traditional) — Parzival 455,9-12 (quoted): the Latin-chronicle search through Britain, France, Ireland, resolved in Anjou. Completes Wolfram's stated itinerary for his source.
Segment 5 renamed "The Grail Quest: Toledo to Arlesheim" accordingly, and the Andlau campa's opening clause adjusted ("turns north from Anjou") so the route reads continuously.

**Register.** Campa fields spot-read across all segments: present tense, 60-110 words (linter-confirmed), the canon narrated as true throughout; the mythic time-fold (812 death vs. 848 bridge) is carried by `date_confidence` marking, exactly as the register requires. No debunking introduced; nothing removed.
