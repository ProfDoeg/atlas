# John von Neumann: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-09 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `john_von_neumann`. Subject: Neumann János Lajos, margittai Neumann János, John von Neumann; Budapest 1903 to Washington 1957.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or secondhand report. Contradictions are printed as contradictions and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

One caution first. This subject is unusually well documented and unusually badly repeated: anecdotes migrate between physicists (the Trinity paper strips) and dates migrate between years (the Gibbs Lectureship, the Medal for Merit, the Aberdeen appointment). What a document supports is separated here from what tradition supports, and the second is marked as tradition rather than deleted.

## 1. Budapest, 1903-1921

**Birth [A: Wikipedia, "John von Neumann"; Macrae, *John von Neumann* (1992); Bhattacharya, *The Man from the Future* (2021)]**: 28 December 1903, Budapest, to Miksa (Max) Neumann, a lawyer turned banker, and Margit Kann. **Birth order, corrected [A: Wikipedia]**: he was the **eldest** of three brothers, not the third son as the gathered pool had it. "He was the eldest of three brothers; his two younger siblings were Mihály (Michael) and Miklós (Nicholas)."

**The apartment [R]**: Budapest is beyond dispute; the door is not. The address usually cited is Báthory utca 26, in Lipótváros (~47.5065 / 19.0545). The pool's coordinate fell in Belváros, south of it, a district-level error. The journey pins Lipótváros and says so in `date_confidence`.

**Ennoblement [A: Wikipedia]**: 20 February 1913, Franz Joseph, predicate *margittai*, no land. The Hungarian particle becomes a German *von* once the son publishes from Berlin.

**Fasori gymnasium [A: Wikipedia; Macrae; Halmos (1973)]**: he enters in **1914**. *Contradiction flagged, resolved in favour of 1914*: the pool's geography lens carried "1911-1921", but Wikipedia states 1914 and MacTutor agrees. László Rátz recognises him and arranges private university-level tutoring alongside the curriculum. Eugene Wigner is a year ahead in the same school and writes his obituary memoir forty-four years later; Teller is of the same generation.

**Vienna, 1919 [R]**: the family withdraws during Béla Kun's Hungarian Soviet Republic. Carried by the corpus and by general biography; no primary document consulted. **Prodigy anecdotes [R: MacTutor, as anecdote]**: Greek jokes with his father at six, telephone-directory pages recited for guests. In every biography, in no document. Printed as tradition.

**First paper [A: MacTutor, biography of Michael Fekete]**: "Über die Lage der Nullstellen gewisser Minimum Polynome", with Fekete. *Date corrected*: the pool had 1921. MacTutor's Fekete biography says the paper appeared "only one year after he completed his studies at the Gymnasium", and the gymnasium ended in 1921. **Publication year is 1922.**

## 2. The double enrollment, 1921-1929

**The compromise [A: Macrae; MacTutor]**: 1921, registered for the Budapest doctorate, which he attends only for examinations, and enrolled in chemistry at Berlin, which he attends. *Coordinate error in the pool, flagged*: the chronology lens put Budapest coordinates on a sentence whose own text says Berlin (52.517 / 13.3935).

**ETH Zurich [A: Wikipedia; Macrae; Bhattacharya]**: entrance examination September 1923, chemical engineering 1923-1926, diploma taken. **Pólya [A: MacTutor, quoting Pólya]**: the only student he was ever afraid of.

**Doctorate [A: MacTutor; Macrae]**: 1926, Budapest, *summa cum laude*, thesis on the axiomatisation of set theory, minors in experimental physics and chemistry. Macrae gives March. Diploma and doctorate fall in the same year.

**Göttingen [A: MacTutor; Macrae; Sigmund, *Exact Thinking in Demented Times* (2017)]**: 1926-27 on a Rockefeller grant under Hilbert. Oppenheimer arrives the same season. *Coordinate contradiction, flagged and NOT silently resolved*: the corpus's canonical pin is 51.5413 / 9.9158, about 1.4 km west of the city centre; Wikipedia gives 51.5333 / 9.9356. The journey **inherits the canonical pin byte-identically** so the atlas stays coherent, and the disagreement is recorded here rather than quietly fixed in one file out of many.

**Habilitation [A: Wikipedia]**: 13 December 1927, Berlin, described as the youngest Privatdozent in that university's history; the superlative is Wikipedia's, carried as attribution. **Hamburg**: 1929-30, read at the time as a step toward a German chair.

## 3. The crossing, 1929-1938

**Marriage to Mariette Kövesi [A: Macrae]**: *the pool tagged this [R] because MacTutor implies 1929 and Wikipedia says 1930. The contradiction dissolves and is upgraded to [A]*: Budapest, **1 January 1930** (Macrae; the pool's own geography lens carries 1930-01). MacTutor's "1929" refers to the pre-departure period. Baptised Catholic in 1930, reportedly to ease the marriage [A: Wikipedia].

**Princeton, invitation and arrival [A: MacTutor; Macrae]**: October 1929 is when the invitation is accepted; the **arrival** is early 1930, after the wedding. The pool dated the stop 1929-10, which reads as though he were already there. The pier is undocumented, so the harbour is pinned and the gap stated [R for the pier]. Visiting lecturer from 1930, full professor 1931; Dirac spends 1934-35 in the same Fine Hall [A: Regis, *Who Got Einstein's Office?* (1987)].

**Institute for Advanced Study [A: MacTutor; Regis; Macrae]**: 1933, one of the original six professors of mathematics alongside Einstein, co-editor of the *Annals*, chair held twenty-four years. **Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**: Springer, 1932; French (Proca) 1946, Spanish 1949, Beyer's English only in 1955, Russian (ed. Bogolyubov) 1964, re-edited English (ed. Wheeler) 2018.

**Marina [A: Wikipedia, "Marina von Neumann Whitman"]**: *place corrected.* His only child is born **6 March 1935 in New York City**, not Princeton as two lenses of the pool had it (~40.7128 / -74.0060). **Divorce**: 2 November 1937, two years and eight months after the birth; the pool's "three years later" overstates it. **Naturalisation [A: Wikipedia; Macrae]**: 1937; the courthouse is not identified in anything consulted, stated as a gap [R for the venue]. **Bôcher Prize** 1938. **Marriage to Klára Dán** 17 November 1938, Budapest.

## 4. Ordnance, 1940-1945

**Aberdeen [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia, "Ballistic Research Laboratory"]**: *start date corrected.* Named an original member of the Scientific Advisory Committee in **1940** under Hermann Zornig. The pool's geography lens gave "1937-1955", unsupported and contradicting its own chronology lens. The range is 1940-1955. Navy Bureau of Ordnance from 1941.

**Los Alamos [A: MacTutor; Rhodes, *The Making of the Atomic Bomb* (1986); Macrae]**: consultant from 1943 on the explosive-lens geometry that makes implosion, and so the plutonium weapon, feasible. **Chicago Met Lab [R: Rhodes]**: consulting visits attested in general terms only, dates unconfirmed; given no stop on that ground. **Target Committee [A: Rhodes]**: May 1945, arguing from the physics for targets whose terrain would hold and reflect the blast, and for an airburst at a height his own calculations supply. Kyoto heads the list until Stimson strikes it off.

**Trinity [A for presence: Rhodes; Wikipedia, "Trinity (nuclear test)"]**: he observes the 16 July 1945 detonation. ***Misattributed anecdote, removed***: the pool credited him with estimating the yield from dropped paper strips. **That is Enrico Fermi's method**, so credited by Wikipedia's Trinity article. Von Neumann's own prediction, about 5 kilotons, was analytical and entered in the betting pool; his remark on the spot was "that was at least 5,000 tons and probably a lot more." The line is deleted rather than reassigned, and the deletion recorded so it cannot creep back.

## 5. The machine and the game, 1944-1952

**Theory of Games and Economic Behavior [A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**: Princeton, 1944, with Morgenstern, 625 pages; second edition 1947 adds the expected-utility appendix; sixtieth anniversary edition 2004. Translations into five languages exist per the same source, dates unconfirmed. **Moore School and EDVAC [A: Dyson, *Turing's Cathedral* (2012)]**: 1944 he joins the ENIAC consultants; 1945 the *First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC*.

**The IAS machine [A, tag upgraded]**: the pool tagged the project [R] on the ground of "general historical consensus". It is better sourced than that: Dyson's book is a monograph on this machine built from the Institute's own records. Construction from 1946, fully operational 10 June 1952; first serious job a thermonuclear calculation, second the first numerical weather forecast ever computed.

**Monte Carlo [A: von Neumann, NBS Applied Mathematics Series 12 (1951)]**: the state-of-sin line comes from this paper, verified through Wikiquote's full publication citation. **The essay itself was not fetched, being not freely hosted.**

**Shannon and the word entropy [R: Tribus and McIrvine, *Scientific American* 225, no. 3 (September 1971), p. 180]**: genuinely attested, but only as Shannon's recollection reported decades later by third parties, and the popular wording is a paraphrase that gets one term wrong. The attested text says **statistical mechanics**, not thermodynamics. Kept [R]; `claude_shannon.journey.json` already flags it contested and undated and this file does not upgrade it.

**RAND [A: Poundstone, *Prisoner's Dilemma* (1992)]**: consulting from the late 1940s through 1955. MacTutor gives no start year; 1948 is a placeholder in that bracket, marked as such in the journey.

## 6. Washington, 1946-1957

**Medal for Merit and Gibbs Lectureship [A: Wikipedia awards list; Wikipedia, "Josiah Willard Gibbs Lectureship"]**: *the worst error in the pool, corrected in both halves.* MacTutor dates both to 1947; neither is. The **Medal for Merit is 1946**. The **Gibbs Lectureship is 1944**; the 1947 lecturer was Philip M. Morse. MacTutor is not reliable here.

**Congresses and offices [A: MacTutor]**: ICM Cambridge, Massachusetts 1950; ICM Amsterdam 1954; Armed Forces Special Weapons Project; President of the American Mathematical Society 1951. **Oppenheimer hearing [A: Bird and Sherwin (2005)]**: April 1954, he testifies for Oppenheimer while the panel moves against him. **Teapot Committee [A: Macrae]**: chairs the Strategic Missile Evaluation Committee 1953-55; its report drives the Atlas ICBM crash programme. **Atomic Energy Commission [A: Wikipedia; Macrae]**: sworn in 15 March 1955, held until death. **The cancer [A: Wikipedia]**: a mass near the collarbone, 1955; whether it is secondary to radiation exposure is repeated everywhere as a possibility and established nowhere, so it is carried as possibility, not as cause.

**Medal of Freedom [A: American Presidency Project, "Citation Accompanying Medal of Freedom Presented to Dr. John von Neumann"]**: *two corrections.* (1) It is the **Medal of Freedom** established by Truman in 1945, **not** the Presidential Medal of Freedom, which Kennedy created in 1963. (2) The citation is dated **15 February 1956**, months before his admission to Walter Reed in April, so it was not a bedside presentation; he received it in a wheelchair. Eisenhower did present it. **Enrico Fermi Award [A: Wikipedia]**: he is the **first recipient**, 1956, and it was **not** presented by Eisenhower but by AEC chairman Lewis Strauss. The pool had bundled the two awards into one Eisenhower presentation. Unbundled.

**Death [A: Wikipedia; Macrae; Bhattacharya]**: 8 February 1957, Walter Reed, aged 53, still a sitting commissioner, his final months under guard because of the classified material in his memory. Father Anselm Strittmatter attends him and later recalls that the last rites brought him little comfort and that he remained terrified of death. *Coordinate correction, twice over*: the pool gave Walter Reed as 38.9736 / -77.0166 in one lens and 38.9314 / -77.0163 in another; the campus stood at 6900 Georgia Avenue NW, roughly 38.975 / -77.030, so the second is out by 4.7 km. The journey uses the corpus's canonical pin, 38.9743 / -77.0281.

**Burial [A: Wikipedia, "Princeton Cemetery"]**: 40.355 / -74.660, ground already holding Grover Cleveland and Aaron Burr and later Kurt Gödel (1978) and Eugene Wigner (1995). The pool's 40.3548 / -74.6672 is 600 m west, and its "a short walk" from the Institute is wrong: roughly 2.5 km. **The plot could not be confirmed; FindAGrave memorial #2223 returned HTTP 403.** **Rossby Medal** posthumously 1957.

## 7. Afterlife

- ***The Computer and the Brain*** **[A: Wikipedia]**: unfinished at his death, intended for Yale's Silliman Lectures, published 1958 with a preface by Klára Dán von Neumann. Reissued 2000 (Churchlands) and 2012 (Kurzweil, reading it as an anticipation of the singularity).
- **JOHNNIAC [A: Wikipedia]**: built by RAND 1953, named for him, decommissioned 1966, abandoned in a museum parking lot in 1989, rescued, now at the Computer History Museum, Mountain View. **Named prizes [A]**: the IEEE Medal, the INFORMS Theory Prize.
- **Off Earth [A: Wikipedia, "Von Neumann (crater)"]**: crater von Neumann, lunar far side, 40°17′N 153°15′E, 74.83 km across, named by the IAU in 1970, previously "Crater 58"; asteroid 22824. **Kecskemét, 2016 [A]**: a merged university named Neumann János Egyetem.
- **Bhattacharya (2021) [R]**: title, publisher and date could **not** be verified against a live source (author's Wikipedia page HTTP 404, search budget exhausted). Carried as [R] though almost certainly correct, because "almost certainly" is not a citation.

## Quotations: what survived checking

Several of the most-repeated von Neumann lines circulate truncated in ways that change their register. Where a fuller text was verified, the fuller text is used in the journey.

1. **Random digits, 1951 [A]**: the state-of-sin line. Source essay not independently fetched.
2. **"Can We Survive Technology?", *Fortune*, June 1955 [A]**: *contradiction resolved, with reasons.* Wikiquote dates it 1950; most bibliographies say 1955. The reprint in *Collected Works* VI, 504-519, hosted as a file named von_Neumann_1955.pdf, settles it, and Wikipedia's publication list files it under 1955 with the *Fortune* link. The source spells it **judgment**.
3. **"The Mathematician", 1947 [A]**: the circulating version cuts a hedge ("I think that it is a relatively good approximation to truth ...") and turns it into a flat assertion. The journey restores the hedge and marks the cuts with ellipses.
4. **"Method in the Physical Sciences", 1955 [A]**: *The Unity of Knowledge*, ed. Leary, p. 157, via Wikiquote; the collection was not fetched. **The entropy anecdote [R]**: see section 5, statistical mechanics rather than thermodynamics.
5. **"In mathematics you don't understand things. You just get used to them." [R]**: the pool said no dated firsthand account exists. One does, third-hand rather than absent: Zukav, *The Dancing Wu Li Masters* (1979), p. 208 n., reporting Felix T. Smith of Stanford Research Institute, and opening "Young man,". Still [R]. Captioning it under his ID-badge photograph is folklore, not a source.
6. **"What we are creating now is a monster..." [R]**: better sourced than the pool allowed. Klára Dán von Neumann, memoir "Grasshopper in Very Tall Grass", ch. 7, c. 1962-63, Library of Congress MSS 99600, Box 56, quoted in Dyson. Still [R], a wife's recollection of a night in early 1945, but the manuscript fixes the wording.
7. **Wigner, 1958 [A]**: amputated clause restored, "...and do not even wish to understand." **Dyson, 1988, p. 182 [A]**: three dropped words restored, "the reputation **at that time** of being the cleverest man in the world." Also used: **Bethe**, 1957; **Bronowski**, 1974; **Feynman**, 1985 (page unconfirmed). **Ulam** (1991), p. 76, the strongest source in the set, is unused for want of a stop that carries it.
