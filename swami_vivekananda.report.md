# Swami Vivekananda (1863-1902): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Narendranath Datta, in religion Swami Vivekananda; monk of the Ramakrishna order, first Hindu teacher to address a mass Western audience, founder of the Ramakrishna Mission.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or devotional memory repeated in the order's own literature without a documentary anchor. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Register note.** National mythology in the strict sense: India has kept his birthday as National Youth Day since 1984 **[A: Wikipedia]**, and the standard biography is published by his own order. The canon is true in the campa text; divergences between canon and record sit in each stop's `date_confidence` field, not in the prose.

---

## 1. Calcutta and the Datta household, 1863-1884

- Born 12 January 1863 at 3 Gourmohan Mukherjee Street, north Calcutta, to Vishwanath Datta, attorney of the High Court, and Bhuvaneshwari Devi; Kayastha family **[A: Wikipedia, Swami Vivekananda; Belur Math official site]**.
- **Coordinate correction.** The pool carried the birthplace at 22.5958, 88.3639. Wikipedia's article on the site (Ramakrishna Mission Swami Vivekananda's Ancestral House and Cultural Centre) gives **22.58633, 88.36736**, about 1.1 km south-southeast **[A: Wikipedia coordinates API]**.
- Metropolitan Institution 1871; family at Raipur 1877; Presidency College entrance in the first division 1879; Fine Arts examination 1881; Bachelor of Arts 1884. All **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Joined Keshab Chandra Sen's Nava Vidhan, an offshoot of the Brahmo Samaj, c. 1880 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Vishwanath Datta died suddenly in 1884, leaving the family in hardship **[A: Wikipedia; Belur Math]**. **Gap:** no month in either source; the journey file defaults to 01 and says so in `date_confidence`.
- The destitution episodes (the search for clerk's work, the claim to have eaten elsewhere, the three petitions in the Kali shrine that come out as requests for discrimination and renunciation) are **[R]**: order biographies and brother-monk reminiscence, not documents.

## 2. Dakshineswar and Cossipore, 1881-1886

- First meeting with Ramakrishna at the Dakshineswar Kali Temple, 1881 **[A: Belur Math]**. **Contradiction, not resolved:** Wikipedia dates it "likely November" 1881; Belur Math gives the year alone. Both carried in `date_confidence`.
- Regular visits from 1882 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Throat cancer diagnosed 1885 **[A: Wikipedia]**; moved to Shyampukur September 1885 **[R: Belur Math, the only source consulted for that stage]**; then the rented Cossipore garden house, nursed in shifts by the young disciples.
- **Coordinate correction.** Cossipore given in the pool at 22.6167, 88.3667; Wikipedia gives **22.623, 88.375**, about 1.2 km off **[A: Wikipedia coordinates API]**.
- Ramakrishna died at Cossipore 16 August 1886 **[A: Wikipedia; Belur Math]**. Narendra took the ochre cloth there before the death **[A/R: Ramakrishna Mission accounts; carried inside the Cossipore stop without a separate date]**.

## 3. The vow: Antpur and Baranagar, 1886-1890

**The largest unresolved contradiction in this file. Flagged, not settled.**

- **Wikipedia:** sannyasa vows taken **24 December 1886 at the Radha Gobinda Jiu temple, Antpur**, Hooghly district, by Narendra **with eight other disciples**, before a fire, on Christmas Eve **[A]**.
- **Belur Math:** the brotherhood formed at **Baranagar Math in 1887 with fifteen disciples**, in a viraja homa **[A]**.
- The pool as received headlined 1887 and counted fourteen. Both are wrong on their own terms: the primary documented event is 24 December 1886 at Antpur, and Belur Math's own text says fifteen. The journey file gives **two stops** and states the disagreement in each. Neither account is preferred.
- **Coordinate correction.** Antpur carried at 22.7333, 88.0833; Wikipedia gives **22.7834, 88.0436**, about 7 km off **[A: Wikipedia coordinates API]**.
- The Baranagar house is **[A: Wikipedia]** as fact; the domestic detail (cheap for its cremation ground and its ghosts, the poverty, the all-night study) is **[R: order biographies]**.
- **The name.** He used **Vividishananda** and others in the wandering years. **Vivekananda** is traditionally credited to Raja Ajit Singh of Khetri, usually placed at 1893 **[R: Wikipedia carries it as attribution with no fixed date]**. Placed at the Khetri stop and hedged there.

## 4. The wandering years, 1888-1893

Wikipedia dates the wandering broadly 1888-1893 and has a separate article, *Swami Vivekananda's travels in India (1888-1893)*, the best itinerary reachable. Belur Math dates the departure from Baranagar to 1890. These are not the same claim (a tentative first circuit in 1888, a definitive departure in 1890) and both are carried.

- **Varanasi**, repeated visits, staying with the scholar Pramadadas Mitra **[A: *His Eastern and Western Disciples*]**.
- **Hathras station**: assistant stationmaster Sharat Chandra Gupta leaves the railway to follow him and is ordained **Swami Sadananda** at Rishikesh soon after **[A]**. **Date correction:** the pool said August 1890; Wikipedia's travels article says **September 1888**, which the file uses.
- **Vrindavan**, the refused alms and the Bengali woman who fed him **[R: devotional memory; no contemporaneous record]**.
- **Alwar**, February 1891, the argument about images at Mangal Singh's court **[A: travels article]**. **Correction:** the pool had him ask *a minister* to spit on a *photograph*; Wikipedia has him ask **the Maharaja himself** to spit on a **painting** of the Maharaja's own dead father.
- **Mount Abu**, introduction to **Ajit Singh of Khetri**, 1891, the patronage that paid for America **[A]**; **Khetri**, residence 1891 and 1893 **[A]**, the naming **[R]**, the nautch-singer episode **[R]**.
- **Junagadh**, hosted by Diwan Haridas Viharidas Desai **[A]**; **Girnar** climbed from there **[R]**. Girnar's pool coordinates duplicated Junagadh's latitude exactly, a copy artefact; Wikipedia gives 21.49472, 70.50556. Folded into the Junagadh stop.
- **Porbandar**, the long stay at state expense and the never-finished English translation of Sayana's Rig Veda commentary **[A: Complete Works correspondence]**.
- **Khandwa**. The pool called it a stop with "no recorded public incident." Wrong emphasis: the order's itineraries single it out as where he **first heard of the coming Parliament of Religions** **[R: standard in Ramakrishna Mission itineraries, untraceable to a contemporaneous document]**.
- **Poona**, July 1892: he met **Bal Gangadhar Tilak on a train** and stayed with him at Poona **[A: travels article]**. **Correction:** the pool placed a meeting with "Tilak's circle" in Bombay and added **Justice Mahadev Govind Ranade**, unattested in anything reached and **dropped**.
- **Bangalore and Mysore**, late 1892: Diwan K. Seshadri Iyer, then Maharaja Chamarajendra Wadiyar, who offered to fund the passage **[A for the reception; R for the declining of the fare]**.
- **Kanyakumari**, arrival on foot at Christmas Eve 1892 and the resolve of that winter **[A: Complete Works, letters to Alasinga Perumal]**. **Tag correction:** the pool tagged the swim and the three days **[A]** in the geography lens while tagging the same event **[R]** twice elsewhere; Wikipedia's memorial article calls it legend. Carried as **[R]**; coordinates confirmed.
- **Madurai and Ramnad**, January 1893: **Bhaskara Sethupathi, Raja of Ramnad**, urged him to Chicago and backed it **[A]**. **Contradiction, not resolved:** Wikipedia gives Madurai, Rameswaram, Pondicherry, Madras; the order's biographies put Ramnad first.
- **Madras**, February to May 1893: Alasinga Perumal's circle raised the fare by subscription; these letters are the documentary spine of the whole wandering **[A: Complete Works]**.
- **Bombay**, departure aboard the **Peninsular**, 31 May 1893 **[A: Wikipedia; Belur Math]**.

## 5. The Parliament and the first Western years, 1893-1896

- Voyage via Colombo, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Japan (Nagasaki, Kobe, Osaka, Kyoto, Tokyo), with letters admiring Japanese industry and schooling **[A: Complete Works, letters from Japan]**. Arrived Chicago 30 July 1893 via Vancouver **[A]**.
- **11 September 1893**, opening address at the World's Parliament of Religions, Art Institute of Chicago **[A]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's geography lens gave longitude **+87.6237**, which lands in Xinjiang; the correct pin is **41.8796, -87.6237**, which the same pool's afterlife lens had right.
- The ovation: some accounts two minutes, others far longer. **Stated, not resolved [A: Wikipedia, World's Parliament of Religions]**.
- Further addresses 15, 19, 20, and 27 September, all verified against Wikisource (section 9).
- American lecture tours 1894 **[A]**; **Vedanta Society of New York** founded 1894 **[A]**; **Thousand Island Park** classes, summer 1895, printed as *Inspired Talks* **[A]**.
- **UK correction.** The pool gave two UK visits both in 1896 and had Max Muller and Paul Deussen both met in Britain. The visits were **1895 and 1896**, and **Deussen was met at Kiel in Germany** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Margaret Elizabeth Noble** met in London, November 1895; later Sister Nivedita **[A]**. *Raja Yoga* published 1896 **[A]**. Left England 16 December 1896 **[A]**.

## 6. The Mission, 1897-1899

- Landed **Colombo, 15 January 1897**; the return lectures are printed as *Colombo to Almora* **[A]**.
- The Raja of Ramnad pulling his carriage barefoot at Rameswaram **[R: order biographies; no contemporaneous press reached]**.
- Lodged in Madras **6 to 14 February 1897** in the old Triplicane ice house **[A: Wikipedia, Vivekanandar Illam]**. **Correction:** built **1842 by Frederic Tudor as an ice house**; the name **Castle Kernan** came only after 1880, when the advocate Biligiri Iyengar bought and remodelled it. Coordinates corrected from 13.0567, 80.2822 to **13.0492, 80.2803**.
- **Ramakrishna Mission founded, Calcutta, 1 May 1897** **[A: Wikipedia; Belur Math]**. Punjab tour January 1898 **[A]**. Land bought at **Belur** 1898 **[A: Belur Math]**. *Khandana Bhava-Bandhana* composed 1898 **[A]**.
- **Mayavati, corrected twice.** The pool had the Advaita Ashrama founded 1897 at 29.6167, 80.2. It was founded **19 March 1899** by **Captain James Henry Sevier and Charlotte Sevier** at his wish, at **29.373174, 80.061316**. No image is worshipped there, not even Ramakrishna's. He visited once, **3 to 18 January 1901** **[A: Wikipedia, Advaita Ashrama]**.

## 7. The second crossing and the end, 1899-1902

- Departed June 1899 with Sister Nivedita and Swami Turiyananda **[A]**.
- 1900: Vedanta Societies in San Francisco and New York; **Shanti Ashrama** founded **[A]**. **Coordinate correction:** Shanti Ashrama is at **37.3072, -121.4697** (160 acres, upper San Antonio Valley, Santa Clara County, about 40 miles east of San Jose), not 37.2, -121.55 **[A: Wikipedia, Shanti Ashrama]**.
- **Congress of the History of Religions, Paris, September 1900** **[A]**, then the fast tour with **Jules Bois** through Vienna, Constantinople, Athens, and Egypt **[A: Complete Works, late letters]**. Returned to Calcutta **9 December 1900** **[A]**.
- **Correction:** the pool put the Bodh Gaya and Varanasi pilgrimages in 1901. The January 1901 journey was to **Mayavati**; **Bodh Gaya belongs to January 1902**, as the pool's own geography lens had it. Settling at Belur and the 1901 decline from asthma and diabetes are confirmed **[A]**.
- **Died at Belur Math, 4 July 1902, about 9:20 pm** **[A: Wikipedia]**. A ruptured cerebral blood vessel is cited as possible cause; the order records **mahasamadhi**. Both stated, the devotional framing labelled as such.
- **Correction:** the pool's afterlife lens dated the cremation 4 July, contradicting its own geography lens. The body was taken for cremation on the **afternoon of 5 July 1902** **[A: Wikipedia, Sister Nivedita]**.

## 8. Afterlife

- **Swami Vivekananda Temple** on the cremation spot, consecrated **28 January 1924**, alabaster Om on the upper storey **[A: Wikipedia, Belur Math]**.
- **Sri Ramakrishna Temple** at Belur, to his own conception, designed by Swami Vijnanananda: foundation 1929, consecrated **14 January 1938**; a Christian cross in plan under a Renaissance dome, mixing Hindu, Buddhist, Christian, and Islamic forms **[A]**.
- **Vivekananda Rock Memorial**, Kanyakumari: begun January 1962 under Eknath Ranade, inaugurated **2 September 1970**, and contested first, local Catholic fishermen holding that a cross already stood on the rock until a judicial probe named it Vivekananda Rock **[A]**.
- **Vivekanandar Illam** renamed 1963, leased to Ramakrishna Math 1997, opened as a museum **[A]**. **National Youth Day** instituted 1984, observed from 1985 **[A]**.
- Books: *Raja Yoga* (1896), *Karma Yoga*, *Bhakti Yoga*, *Jnana Yoga*; journals *Prabuddha Bharata* (English, publishing since July 1896) and *Udbodhan* (Bengali) **[A]**.
- **Tag upgrade.** The pool tagged the *Complete Works* claim **[R]** because the Wikipedia page for it returned 404. The **Advaita Ashrama** article confirms the ashrama publishes the *Complete Works* in English and Hindi and maintains *Prabuddha Bharata*; the standard edition runs to **nine volumes**. Upgraded to **[A]**.
- **Gaps:** the date the Gourmohan Mukherjee Street birthplace became a museum was not found; reports of statues abroad and of 2013 sesquicentennial commemorations could not be confirmed from anything reached, and are left open rather than answered either way.

## 11. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Sannyasa:** Antpur, 24 December 1886, nine disciples (Wikipedia) versus Baranagar, 1887, fifteen (Belur Math).
2. **First meeting with Ramakrishna:** November 1881, hedged (Wikipedia) versus 1881 without a month (Belur Math).
3. **Departure into the wandering life:** 1888 (Wikipedia's broad period) versus 1890 from Baranagar (Belur Math).
4. **Order of the southern towns, January 1893:** Madurai, Rameswaram, Pondicherry, Madras (Wikipedia) versus Ramnad first and Madurai northward (order biographies).
5. **The ovation of 11 September 1893:** two minutes versus considerably longer.
6. **The Mysore fare:** declined at the palace, or accepted later in another form.
7. **Cause of death:** ruptured cerebral blood vessel versus mahasamadhi. Both stated.

## 12. Gaps, stated as gaps

- No month for the father's death (1884), the Belur land purchase (1898), or several 1891-1892 Rajputana and Kathiawar stops. Defaulted to 01 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
- The Khandwa tradition (first news of the Parliament) has no traceable contemporaneous source.
- The conversion date of the birthplace into a museum was not found.
- Statues abroad and the 2013 commemorations could be neither confirmed nor denied.
- The Sayana Rig Veda translation begun at Porbandar: no manuscript is reported anywhere reached, and its fate beyond "never completed" is unknown.
