# Ann Lee (1736-1784): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Ann Lee, baptized Ann Lees, called Mother Ann; founder in America of the United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing (Shakers). Queued in `QUEUE.md` line 145.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or single-source claim the other reachable sources do not carry. Contradictions are printed and left standing.

**Method.** The evidence is thin and lopsided. Three kinds of source exist: two parish-register entries at the Manchester Collegiate Church, the only contemporaneous documents anything quotes; the *Testimonies* (1816, expanded 1888), a devotional compilation taken from eyewitnesses thirty-odd years after the events and the sole source of every word ever quoted from her; and later encyclopaedia summaries descending unevenly from it. No letter, no signature beyond a mark, no diary, no hostile contemporary account survives in anything reachable. She could not write. Everything in the first person below is somebody else's memory of her speech.

---

## 1. Manchester: birth and the mill years (1736-1758)

- Born Manchester, **29 February 1736**, baptized Ann Lees. **[A: Wikipedia "Ann Lee"; Appletons' Cyclopaedia; NPS biography, all agreeing.]**
- Second of eight children of **John Lees**, blacksmith, and his wife Ann, surname unrecorded. **[A: Wikipedia.]** Some accounts make the father a tailor. **[R: variant noted in Wikipedia, not adjudicated.]**
- Birth on **Toad Lane**. **[R: Shaker tradition; the street is documented nowhere.]**
- Baptized at the **Manchester Collegiate Church** (now the Cathedral), **1 June 1742**, aged six. **[A: parish register, cited in Wikipedia.]**
- Child worker in a **Manchester cotton mill**. **[A: Wikipedia; NPS.]** Mill unnamed, year unknown. **[Gap.]**
- Later **cuts fur for hatters**. **[A: Wikipedia; NPS.]** Workshop unlocated. **[Gap.]**
- Around twenty, **cook at the Manchester Infirmary**, a building that also held the town's lunatic asylum. **[A: Wikipedia; NPS.]** *Pool correction:* the pool labelled this the "Market Street site"; the 1755 infirmary stood on **Lever's Row at Piccadilly**, which is where the supplied coordinate (53.4808, -2.2374) already points.
- **Quaker ancestry** claims are contested; the family name is absent from the relevant meeting records. **[R: flagged as disputed in Wikipedia itself.]**
- **Illiteracy** is universal in the sources; the marriage register carries a mark. **[A.]**

**Gap.** Nothing between the 1742 baptism and the late 1750s is dated by any document. Mill, fur, and kitchen are sequenced from the ages the sources give, not from evidence.

## 2. The Wardley society, marriage, four children (1758-1770)

- Joins the society of **Jane and James Wardley**, the "Shaking Quakers," carrying the ecstatic practice of the French Camisard prophets. **[A as to membership.]**
- **CONTRADICTION, unresolved.** Wikipedia and NPS give **1758**; Appletons' gives **1747**, when she would have been eleven. Incompatible, and neither checkable against a document from this side. *Pool correction:* the pool mis-stated this as a conflict between the sect's founding and her joining; Appletons' 1747 is the year *she joined*.
- The Wardley meeting place is **nowhere recorded**. **[Gap.]** The journey file pins all unaddressed Manchester stops to the canonical city coordinate (53.4794, -2.2453), inherited byte-identical from `theodor_reuss.journey.json`, rather than inventing addresses.
- **Marries Abraham Stanley** (Standerin, Standarin), blacksmith, at the Collegiate Church, **5 January 1761**. **[A: parish register, cited in Wikipedia.]**
- **CONTRADICTION.** Encyclopedia.com gives the marriage year as **1762**. **[R.]**
  - *Pool correction:* the pool attributed 1762 to Appletons'. Appletons' gives **no marriage date**, only "married Abraham Stanley, also a blacksmith." Re-attributed.
- **Four children**, all dead in infancy or early childhood. **[A: Wikipedia; Appletons'.]** **No birth or death date survives for any of them.** **[Gap, and a large one: the central biographical fact of her life is undated.]**
- The causal link from the deaths to the celibacy doctrine is **[R]**: Shaker hagiography and every biography since, not a documented statement by Lee.
- **Repeated Manchester imprisonments** for Sabbath-breaking (dancing, shouting), disturbance, blasphemy. **[A: Wikipedia.]** Building unidentified. **[Gap.]**
- A **fourteen-day imprisonment in 1770** produces the revelation that fixes the doctrine: the sexual relation as root sin, confession as door, celibacy as salvation. **[A: Wikipedia; NPS confirms visions received in confinement.]** The Shakers date the founding of the church to this cell.
- **Single-source claim.** Appletons' describes a *second* imprisonment around 1770 for opposing marriage, plus confinement in a **mad-house**. Wikipedia does not corroborate an asylum. **[R.]**
- Around **1770**, acknowledged as "Mother in spiritual things," thereafter Mother Ann; the Wardleys recede. **[A: Wikipedia; Appletons'.]**

## 3. The vision of America and the crossing (1772-1774)

- **1772:** a vision that a place has been prepared for her people in America. **[A: NPS states it flatly, "In 1772, received a vision from God indicating America was prepared for her community."]** *Pool correction:* one lens downgraded this to tradition, the other tagged it attested; **[A]** retained, with the Manchester *location* left as the city placeholder.
- **1774:** sails aboard the ship **Mariah** with eight others. **[A for ship and party: Wikipedia.]**
- **Port of departure is named in no source consulted.** Liverpool is conventional inference. **[R.]** *Pool correction:* one lens tagged Liverpool attested and the other called it inference; **[R]** wins.
- Party of nine: Lee, **Abraham Stanley** (husband), **William Lee** (brother), **Nancy Lee** (niece), **James Whittaker**, **John Hocknell**, **Richard Hocknell**, **James Shepherd**, **Mary Partington**. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- Mid-ocean leak and the vision calming the crew: **[R: Shaker *Testimonies* only; no log, no independent witness.]**
- **Lands at New York 6 August 1774**, after about three months at sea. **[A: Wikipedia; NPS.]**
- **CONTRADICTION.** Appletons' gives arrival as **May 1774**. The August date is more specific and doubly carried; it is used, and the May variant recorded rather than deleted.
- *Coordinate correction:* the pool put the landing at 40.7033, -74.017, in the Hudson west of Battery Park City, on 1970s landfill. Moved to **40.7017, -74.0129**, the East River end of lower Manhattan.

## 4. The waiting years in New York (1774-1779)

- Lodges with the **Cunningham family on Queen Street** (now Pearl Street); Lee a laundry assistant, Stanley a blacksmith. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- The party disperses across the New York area for work. **[A.]**
  - *Pool correction:* the pool dated the dispersal 1774-1776. Wikipedia keeps the group in the New York area until Hocknell's **1779** lease. The range is **1774-1779**.
- **Abraham Stanley leaves her** over the celibacy rule, after threatening to go to a prostitute if she did not relent. **[A that it happened: Wikipedia.]** **No source gives a year.** **[Gap.]**
  - *Pool correction:* one lens tagged a 1776 separation attested, the other hedged "1776 or 1777"; neither Wikipedia nor Appletons' dates it, so no year is asserted. Two pool details are struck as unsupported: that she **nursed him through an illness** and that he **took another wife**. Wikipedia says neither.

## 5. Niskayuna, the Dark Day, the war arrests (1779-1780)

- **1779: John Hocknell leases land at Niskayuna**, township of Watervliet, about eight miles from Albany. **[A: Wikipedia; Watervliet Shaker Historic District.]** First Shaker settlement in America.
- **CONTRADICTION, unresolved.** Appletons' dates the founding of "The Church of Christ's Second Appearing" at Watervliet, and the dissolution of the marriage, to **spring 1776**, three years before the documented lease. The two chronologies of the American settlement do not reconcile.
- **May 1780, the Dark Day:** midday darkening across New England; Lee opens her testimony publicly and draws the first American seekers. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- **1780, New Lebanon revival:** a New Light revival sends converts to Niskayuna. **[A for the revival: Appletons' places it "at the beginning of 1780".]**
  - *Pool correction:* the pool said a Shaker **branch society was established at New Lebanon in 1780**. False, and struck. The New Lebanon (later Mount Lebanon) society was gathered in **1787** under Joseph Meacham, after Lee's death; in 1780 converts travelled to Niskayuna. Wikipedia's Meacham article does not support his going to Niskayuna in 1780 either, recording only his Enfield ministry and his leadership from 1787, so his presence at the revival is **[R]**.
- **The wartime arrest.** Worst-evidenced episode in the life; the journey file flags it as contested in `date_confidence` rather than choosing. Appletons' (1887) alone gives arrest **summer 1776** at Albany for high treason (loyalism, dissuading militia service), confinement at **Poughkeepsie**, and a pardon by **Governor George Clinton** dated **1777**. **[R: single source.]** Wikipedia's article, fetched and read directly, **never mentions Albany, Poughkeepsie, or any American imprisonment**, only the refusal to sign an oath of allegiance. Appletons' dating is impossible twice over: 1776 conflicts with the group being in New York City until 1779, and Clinton was not inaugurated as first governor until **July 1777**. Standard modern Shaker accounts give arrest **July 1780** and release **December 1780**, after the Dark Day made her public; **this could not be independently confirmed this session** and is reported as probable and unverified.
  - *Pool correction:* one lens tagged both entries **[A]** at 1776/1777, the other dated the same events 1780; the pool contradicted itself. Both are **[R]**. Albany and Poughkeepsie are not competing traditions but a sequence: arrest at Albany, transfer downriver.

## 6. The New England mission (1781-1783)

- Departs Niskayuna **May 1781**; the tour runs roughly two years and four months through Massachusetts and Connecticut. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- **Mob violence follows the party.** **[A in general terms: Wikipedia, which lists no towns.]** Every individual town below therefore carries a weaker tag than the violence itself.
- **Harvard, Massachusetts, the Square House:** Lee takes the house and the millennialist community left by the late Shadrack Ireland; first Shaker settlement in Massachusetts and base of the eastern mission. **[A: Wikipedia "Harvard, Massachusetts" and "Ann Lee"; Appletons'.]**
- **Mount Washington (Benjamin Osborn House):** **[R.]** The coordinate matches the listed Osborn House, but Wikipedia says only that the party "often stayed in homes" and never names the Osborns; the pool's **[A]** is downgraded.
- **Shirley** and **Petersham** (mob attacks), **Ashfield** (preaching and converts), **Richmond** (small convert clusters): all **[R]**, from nineteenth-century Shaker and local histories, none named in the general account.
- **New Marlborough:** hostile crowd. **[R.]** *Coordinate correction:* pool gave 42.0987, -73.2657, southwest toward Southfield; town centre is **42.1198, -73.2242**.
- **Enfield, Connecticut:** **[R]** for her visit, but stronger than the pool allowed: Wikipedia confirms Meacham's New Light ministry at Enfield before conversion, tying the town to the earliest converts independently. The **1793** founding of the Enfield Shaker community is unverified. *Coordinate:* the pool's -72.5507 sits ~3.4 km east of the town centre, near Shaker Pines where the village stood; the journey file uses the town centre, **41.9764, -72.5919**, and says so.
- *Coordinate correction, New Lebanon:* pool used 42.457, -73.3946, ~4 km east of the town. Wikipedia gives **42.4667, -73.4433**; used at both New Lebanon stops.

## 7. Death and burial (1784)

- **William Lee dies 21 July 1784** of injuries sustained in mob violence. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- **Ann Lee dies at Watervliet 8 September 1784**, aged 48. **[A: Wikipedia; Appletons'; NPS.]** Attribution of her decline to the beatings is **[R]**, tradition rather than finding.
- **Buried in the Shaker cemetery at Watervliet** beside her brother, now within the Watervliet Shaker Historic District, Colonie. **[A: Wikipedia; NPS.]** The tombstone was photographed in 2006; **the inscription is transcribed nowhere.** **[Gap.]**
- The **72 tongues** claim and the deathbed singing in unknown tongues are **[A as reported testimony, R as event]**, as are all the reported miracles, which Wikipedia flags as resting on uncited internal Shaker sources.

## 8. Afterlife (1808-2025)

- **1808:** Benjamin Seth Youngs, with Lucy Wright, publishes *The Testimony of Christ's Second Appearing*, the first systematic Shaker theology. **[A for book and year: Wikipedia "Shakers".]** Place of first printing and translation history **not established**. **[Gap.]**
- **1816:** the *Testimonies*, compiled by Rufus Bishop, revised by Seth Y. Wells; the accessible text is the 1888 edition, archive.org `testimoniesoflif00bish`. **[A.]** *Pool correction:* the geography lens cited this work three times as "1827".
- **Post-1784 doctrine:** followers come to hold that Lee embodied "all the perfections of God in female form," the second and female appearing of Christ. Developed **after her death**. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- **1837 to the mid-1850s: the Era of Manifestations**, or Mother Ann's Work, beginning at Watervliet: trance, tongues, messages received as from Lee's spirit. **[A: Wikipedia.]** *Pool correction:* the pool called it a ten-year revival; Wikipedia gives roughly fifteen to eighteen.
- **Gift drawings** by Hannah Cohoon, Rebecca Landon, Polly Collins, Sarah Bates, Polly Anne Reed, Joseph Wicker; some now in the **American Folk Art Museum**. **[A: Wikipedia.]** *Coordinate correction:* the museum is at 2 Lincoln Square, **40.7738, -73.9819**; the pool's 40.7686, -73.9832 sits ~600 m south near Columbus Circle.
- **Albany International Airport was constructed on the Watervliet community's herb garden.** **[A: Wikipedia "Watervliet Shaker Historic District", stated flatly.]** Whether construction disturbed or approached the burial ground is **undocumented**. **[Gap, and only this narrow question is one; the pool over-hedged the whole claim.]**
- **Sabbathday Lake Shaker Village**, New Gloucester, Maine: last active Shaker community, **three members as of 2025** (June Carpenter, Arnold Hadd, April Baxter, who joined in 2025), ~13,000 artifacts, a research library, **Mother Ann's Day** on the first Sunday of August. **[A: Wikipedia.]** *Coordinate correction:* the village is at **43.9864, -70.3663**; the pool's -70.2839 lands ~6.6 km east.
- **1985:** John Fowles appends an afterword memorializing Ann Lee to *A Maggot*. **2010:** Reg Meuross records "The Heart of Ann Lee". **1 September 2025:** *The Testament of Ann Lee*, dir. Mona Fastvold, Amanda Seyfried as Lee, premieres at Venice. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- **No birthplace marker, plaque, or monument in Manchester is documented anywhere consulted.** **[Gap. The commemoration is entirely American.]**

## 9. Quotations: provenance and corrections

Every quotation in the journey file comes from the *Testimonies* (Bishop/Wells, 1888 ed.), read via archive.org. All are **[A]** as printed text of that book and **[R]** as verbatim speech, it being a compilation of recollections gathered decades later. Stated once here rather than at every stop. Three attributions corrected against the text:

1. **"I would not give way to unbelief; it is a damning sin."** The pool hedged the speaker as "Abel/Asa Allen." Chapter VI holds two distinct visitors: Asa Allen at vv. 9-12 (Stafford, October 1782), and **Abel Allen** at v. 13, whose night of doubt at vv. 14-17 produces this rebuke. Correct: **Ch. VI, v. 16, to Abel Allen.**
2. **"God has called you to take up your cross, and obey the gospel."** The pool cited Ch. IV, v. 9; it falls in **v. 8**, v. 9 beginning "The next day Mother came to...". Spoken to **David Meacham** of Enfield, January 1781.
3. **"God has not sent us into this land in vain..."** Ch. II, v. 12, verbatim. The pool presented it as spoken on arrival in 1774; the text introduces it as a **habitual saying of the waiting years**, nearest dated occasion 1779. Placed at the waiting-years stop, not the landing.

## 10. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Checked by grep across `working/journeys/*.journey.json` and against `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md`.

- **James Wardley** (census line 489-490), the Manchester co-founder she joined, is the direct precursor and carries no coordinate of his own.
- **Manchester is a recurring atlas node**: `karl_marx` (Chetham's alcove, 1845, 53.4849, -2.2426), `theodor_reuss` (Yarker's letters-patent, 1902, 53.4794, -2.2453), `niels_bohr` (Rutherford's laboratory, 1912), `laban` (mill studies, 1940s), `nick_land` (Sadie Plant's doctorate, 1990s). The Reuss pair is inherited byte-identical as the canonical city pin for Lee's unaddressed Manchester stops.
- **`edward_bellamy`** supplies the canonical lower-Manhattan pin (Park Row, 40.7115, -74.0055), used at the waiting-years stop, and is the corpus's only other communal-property utopian.
- **`mesmer`**: the Paris commission on animal magnetism sits in **1784**, the year Lee dies. No file connects them; the coincidence of year across a controversy about convulsing bodies and the death of a woman whose church was built on shaking is set down as juxtaposition.
- **`keyserling`** ("Kali and Manchester in one hand") is the exact polarity of Lee's life; **`william_blake`** is an exact contemporary in the same wave of visionary dissent, with no documented contact; **`felix_adler`** is a structural mirror only and is not named in any campa.
- **Explicit gap:** no file mentions Robert Owen, New Harmony, Watervliet, New Lebanon, or the word "Shaker" outside the census entries for Wardley and Lee. Apart from Bellamy, the communitarian thread this subject was queued to join has no other traveler in it.
- None of the atlas's four general canonical pins (Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris, Buenos Aires) fall on Lee's route. None inherited.
