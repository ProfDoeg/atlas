# Mohammad Reza Shah Pahlavi (1919-1980): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `mohammad_reza_shah_pahlavi`. Crown prince from 1926, shah from September 1941, restored by the 1953 coup against Mohammad Mosaddegh, deposed in 1979, dead in Cairo in 1980.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or aggregator-only attribution. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and again in a dedicated section; none is silently adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method.** Four research lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, afterlife) plus an interlock pass over the existing atlas, then adversarial verification, which overturned or corrected eleven submitted items. Each correction is carried below with the superseded version beside it.

---

## I. Crown prince, 1919-1939

- **Birth**, Tehran, 26 October 1919, second child and first son of the Persian Cossack Brigade officer Reza Khan and Tadj ol-Molouk. **[A: Wikipedia, "Mohammad Reza Pahlavi"]** The house is not documented in standard sources, so the pin approximates central Tehran (35.6892, 51.4025); the lens's own hedge is kept.
- **The dynasty**, Golestan Palace, 25 April 1926: Reza Khan crowns himself, and the six-year-old is named crown prince the same year. **[A: Wikipedia, "Pahlavi dynasty" / "Reza Shah"]**
- **Le Rosey**, arriving 7 September 1931. **[A]** *Two corrections.* (1) The submitted pin 46.4675, 6.2167 falls roughly 9 km west of Rolle, near Nyon; the school is at approximately **46.4586, 6.3378**. (2) The submitted "1931/1935", called four years, is wrong: Wikipedia has him there **until 1936**, five years.
- **Military academy**, Tehran. *Correction:* the chronology lens compressed this onto 1936; he was **enrolled 1936, graduating Second Lieutenant 1938**, as the geography lens already read. The two lenses contradicted each other in the raw pool; resolved to the two-year span. **[A]**
- **Fawzia**: married at Abdeen Palace, Cairo, 15 March 1939; second banquet at Saadabad, Tehran, 25 April 1939; daughter Shahnaz 1940; divorced 1948 after her return to Egypt and the failure to produce a male heir. **[A: Wikipedia and chronology extract]**

## II. The throne under occupation, 1941-1949

- **Abdication and oath.** Reza Shah abdicates **16 September 1941** **[A: Wikipedia, "Reza Shah"]**; the oath before the Majlis is **17 September**. *Correction:* the geography entry dated the oath to the abdication day. The entry describes the oath, so it takes the 17th. **[A]**
- **Coordinate conflict, unresolved.** The verifier puts the Majlis, then at Baharestan, at approximately **35.6919, 51.4320**, rejecting the submitted 51.4177 as 1.3 km too far west; but `mohammad_mosaddegh.journey.json` already pins Baharestan at **35.69167, 51.425**, some 700 m away. The atlas pin is inherited byte-identically and the divergence recorded.
- **Tehran Conference**, Soviet Embassy, 28 November to 1 December 1943; the Shah is host and is not at the table. **[A: Wikipedia, "Tehran Conference"]** Embassy coordinates approximate, the compound since rebuilt **[R]**.
- **Reza Shah dies at Johannesburg, 1944**, exiled first to Mauritius then South Africa. **[A: Wikipedia, "Reza Shah"]** *Gap:* year only in the pool; the journey file defaults to 01-01 and says so.
- **Iranian Azerbaijan, 1946**: the army reasserts central control after the Soviet-backed separatist episode. **[A: chronology extract]** Folded into a campa; no pin was supplied.
- **Assassination attempt**, University of Tehran, 4 February 1949: five shots from a gunman with a press card; the Tudeh Party banned in the aftermath **[A]**. *Contradiction left standing:* the chronology lens says "grazed", the geography lens "wounded in the face".
- **First state visit to Washington**, November 1949, seeking arms and aid from Truman. **[A]** Folded into a campa.

## III. The oil and the coup, 1951-1953

- **Soraya**, Marble Palace, 12 February 1951. **[A: Wikipedia, "Soraya Esfandiary-Bakhtiari"]** *Correction affecting three entries:* the submitted Marble Palace pin 35.6912, 51.4223 sits about 2 km east of the palace. Wikipedia gives 35 41 21 N, 51 24 06 E = **35.6891, 51.4018**. Applied to the 1951 marriage, the 1959 marriage, and the 1965 attempt alike.
- **Nationalization**, 1951: Mosaddegh nationalizes the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company, escalating the fight over oil revenue and command of the armed forces **[R: general historical record, not refetched]**. The Majlis confirmation of 28 April 1951 and the July 1952 War Ministry refusal reversed after Si-ye Tir come from the atlas itself **[A: `mohammad_mosaddegh.journey.json`]**.
- **August 1953, corrected in three places.**
  1. *The firman.* The geography lens placed the Shah at Ramsar and had him sign **after** Nassiri's mission failed. Both wrong: the decree is signed **13 August**, at his villa at **Kelardasht** (approx. 36.4833, 51.1333); Ramsar is the transit airfield he flew to at about 4 a.m. on 16 August. The causal order is reversed in the submission. **[A: Wikipedia, "1953 Iranian coup d'etat"]**
  2. *Nassiri* arrives at Kakh Street on 16 August and is arrested; the first attempt collapses within the hour. **[A: `mohammad_mosaddegh.journey.json`, pin 35.715, 51.427]**
  3. *Baghdad and Rome.* He lands 16 August, received by King Faisal II and lodged in the government guest house **[A]**; the submitted Baghdad pin 33.2625/44.345 misses Al-Rashid air base (approx. 33.2603, 44.4525) by about 10 km. He stays until **18 August** and reaches Rome that day, so the window is **18-22 August**, not 17-22, and "five days later" becomes four. The Hotel Excelsior is **[R]**: the coup article does not name it, so the submitted **[A]** was too generous.
- **Restoration**: 19 August, paid crowds and army units under Zahedi shell Mosaddegh's house; return to Mehrabad 22 August; tribunal November-December, sentence 21 December to three years' solitary then lifelong house arrest, the prosecution having asked for death. **[A: atlas file and Wikipedia]**

## IV. The White Revolution, 1958-1967

- **Farah, with a source conflict left standing.** Soraya divorced 1958, childless **[R]**. The submitted geography entry dates the Farah marriage **21 December 1959**, but its own cited source, Wikipedia's "Farah Pahlavi", says **20 December**. The journey file takes the date the source gives and records the conflict in `date_confidence`. Second caveat: Wikipedia's "Marble Palace (Tehran)" calls that palace the site of the **engagement**, so the venue is hedged **[R on venue]**.
- **The heir**, Reza Pahlavi, born 1960. **[R: general historical record, not refetched]** Year only; folded into a campa.
- **The programme**, 1963: land reform, forest nationalization, privatization of state enterprises, worker profit-sharing, women's suffrage, a literacy corps **[A: Wikipedia, "White Revolution"]**; referendum 26 January 1963 returned as **5,598,711 in favour, 4,115 against** amid an opposition boycott **[A: same]**. The margin is reported, not endorsed.
- **Khomeini, split into two events.** The submitted entry fused two arrests eighteen months apart and sent him straight to Iraq. Per Wikipedia, "Ruhollah Khomeini": arrested **3 a.m., 5 June 1963** at Qom after the 3 June Ashura speech; the **capitulations** speech is **26 October 1964**, with a separate arrest that November; exile **4 November 1964 first to Bursa in Turkey**, Najaf only from October 1965 to 1978. **[A]** The fused version is discarded; Qom takes the atlas's canonical 34.6401, 50.8764.
- **Second attempt on his life**, Marble Palace, 10 April 1965, by Imperial Guard conscript Reza Shamsabadi; the Shah unhurt, the royal office afterwards moved to Sahebgharaniyeh. **[A]** The submitted **[R]** is upgraded on event, date, and framing; only the coordinates were wrong.
- **Mosaddegh dies** at Ahmadabad, 7 March 1967, after house arrest from 1956, refused burial at Ibn Babuyeh among the 1952 martyrs and interred under his own dining-room floor. **[A: `mohammad_mosaddegh.journey.json`, pin 35.99361, 50.474722]**
- **Coronation**, Golestan, 26 October 1967, twenty-six years into the reign; Farah crowned Shahbanu. **[A: chronology extract]**

## V. Aryamehr, 1968-1977

- **Niavaran**, designed 1958, completed 1967, occupied from 1968. **[A: Wikipedia, "Niavaran Palace Complex"]** *Two corrections.* (1) The afterlife lens said "five children"; Farah bore **four** (Reza, Farahnaz, Ali-Reza, Leila), the fifth counted being Shahnaz, Fawzia's daughter, born 1940 and grown by 1968. (2) Both lenses' pins are wrong in different directions; Wikipedia gives **35.8119, 51.4725** and both are reconciled to it. The Foroughi architect attribution and the 1994 library opening are **[R]**: not in the article cited.
- **Persepolis**, 12-16 October 1971: a Maison Jansen tent city, roughly 50 tent apartments on 160 acres around a central fountain, a Tent of Honor, a banqueting hall 68 by 24 metres. **[A: Wikipedia, "2,500 year celebration of the Persian Empire"]** The journey file pins 29.9356, 52.8916 and leaves `giuseppe_tucci.journey.json`'s slightly different Persepolis pin untouched, the two marking different events.
- **Shahyad Aryamehr**: Hossein Amanat, 1966 competition, 8,000 blocks of white Isfahan marble, roughly $6 million, inaugurated 16 October 1971, public 14 January 1972, at 35.6997, 51.338. **[A: Wikipedia, "Azadi Tower"]** *Correction:* the basement institution is the **Azadi Museum** (Susa artifacts, a section on the history of photography in Iran), not the "Museum of Persian History".
- **The diagnosis, May 1974.** *Correction, and it matters.* Not non-Hodgkin lymphoma as submitted: the diagnosis made in Tehran by the French haematologists **Jean Bernard and Georges Flandrin** was **chronic lymphocytic leukemia**, later described as Waldenstrom's macroglobulinemia. "Non-Hodgkin lymphoma" is a retrospective gloss. Secrecy from the public and reportedly from US intelligence **[R]**.
- **Rastakhiz**, 1975: parties dissolved, one legal party. **[R: general historical record, not refetched]** No month attested; folded into a campa.
- **Washington and the toast**: tear gas across the South Lawn during the arrival ceremony, 15 November 1977 **[A]**; Carter's "island of stability" toast at Niavaran, 31 December 1977 **[A]**, which Wikipedia's Niavaran article confirms as the occasion he was received there.

## VI. The revolution, 1978-1979

- **Black Friday**, Jaleh Square, 8 September 1978, after martial law. **[A: Wikipedia, "Iranian Revolution"]** *Gap:* no lens supplied a Jaleh Square coordinate, so the journey file uses the canonical Tehran pin 35.6892, 51.389 and says so.
- **Departure**, Mehrabad, 16 January 1979, officially a "vacation"; Bakhtiar kisses his hand, the Shah weeps on the tarmac. **[A]**
- **Neauphle-le-Château**: Khomeini directs the revolution from a rented garden outside Paris and returns to Tehran 1 February 1979; Foucault reports from Jaleh Square for *Corriere della Sera*, is received by clerics at Qom, and writes that a whole people "prefers death to the Shah". **[A: `michel_foucault.journey.json`, inherited Paris pin 48.8566, 2.3522]**
- **11 February 1979**: sources place the constitutional end of the monarchy here **[A: Simple Wikipedia]**. *Contradiction, not resolved:* the atlas's Mosaddegh file dates the final departure to 11 February, while two lenses attest the flight on 16 January. The journey file treats 16 January as the flight and 11 February as the constitutional end.
- **Renaming and looting.** Shahyad becomes **Azadi**, the building unaltered, and hosts the 2009 election protests **[A: Wikipedia, "Azadi Tower"]**. The Persepolis tent city is looted, leaving iron tent-frame rods and access roads, unmarked and open, with a 2005 report of reconstruction being considered for tourism **[A]**. Both are dated only to 1979; `date_confidence` carries that.

## VII. Exile and death, 1979-1980

The itinerary, all from Wikipedia's "Farah Pahlavi": Aswan 16-30 January 1979 **[R]**; Marrakesh 30 January to 30 March **[R]**; Paradise Island, Nassau, 30 March to 10 June **[A]**; Cuernavaca 10 June to 22 October **[A]**; New York Hospital-Cornell 22 October **[A]**; Lackland AFB, San Antonio, December **[A]**; Contadora Island, Panama, December 1979 to March 1980 **[A]**; Cairo from March 1980 **[A]**; death 27 July and funeral 30 July 1980 **[A]**.

- *New York corrected:* admission is **22 October 1979**, not "mid-October"; the embassy is seized **4 November**, thirteen days later, so "days later" understates. Tag raised **[R]** to **[A]**.
- *Cairo pin corrected:* geography gave 30.0894/31.3119, in Heliopolis about 7 km from the centre while claiming to approximate "the city", and the afterlife lens gave downtown 30.0444/31.2357. The residence was **Koubbeh Palace**, approximately **30.0872, 31.2836**, which the journey file uses.
- *Cause of death corrected:* **infections caused by the splenectomy**, per Wikipedia's "Death and state funeral", not the lymphoma or leukemia directly.
- *Funeral:* Sadat presiding, Nixon and exiled King Constantine II attending **[A]**. The submitted "No Iranian government sends a representative" was **not verifiable** and is dropped in favour of who did attend. Of three competing Al-Rifa'i pins the verifier's **30.0316, 31.2565**, on Salah al-Din Square, is taken.
- *Reza Shah's grave corrected:* the afterlife lens had the chamber "also holds part of the grave of his own father". Wikipedia, "Al-Rifa'i Mosque": he was buried there after 1944, but "his body was removed in 1950 and reburied in Iran", leaving a marker. Proximity to the Muhammad Ali tombs, Farouk, Fuad I, Sultan Hussein Kamel, is confirmed **[A]**.

## VIII. Afterlife

- ***Answer to History*** (*Réponse à l'histoire*), written in French while dying, published posthumously by Stein & Day in **1980**; the submitted "September 1980" is unsupported and drops to the year. Blame for SAVAK shifted onto PM Amir Abbas Hoveyda. Abrahamian: "the ramblings of a paranoid"; Martin Kramer in *Commentary*: the "Shah at his very worst". **[A: Wikipedia, "Answer to History"]** The earlier ***Mission for My Country***, 1961, is **[R]**: general historical record, not reverified, the web search budget having been exhausted mid-task.
- **Gaps, stated as gaps.** Nothing consulted describes the tomb's present condition, maintenance, or visitor traffic; Wikipedia's Al-Rifa'i article gives only a photo caption identifying the grave. No primary source was found for any Islamic Republic position on repatriation, so the absence of a request is an unfilled gap, not a policy. Statue-toppling in 1979 is widely reported in secondary literature, but Wikipedia's revolution article is silent on statuary and no session source named a statue, date, or place **[R, unpinned]**.
- **The son and the chant.** Reza Pahlavi declares himself "Reza Shah II" in 1980 and over decades advocates a secular system chosen by referendum rather than restoration; the title is symbolic and unrecognized **[A: Wikipedia, "Reza Pahlavi"]**. Some 2017-18 demonstrators call for the return of Reza Shah, sometimes shouted down within the same crowds; in later rounds reported into 2025-26 the name that carries is his son's. A 2009 Brookings finding cited in the same source holds Pahlavi has little in common with the students and intellectuals driving reform, so this is visible sentiment, not organized restoration **[A on the reporting, R on the meaning]**.
- **Split verdict.** Wikipedia's own framing: "The legacy of Mohammad Reza Shah Pahlavi is divisive." **[R: synthesis of a source's framing, not an event]**

## Quotes: verbatim versus attributed

**Verified to a primary transcription [A]:** the Apollo 11 goodwill message, 1969, from Wikisource's transcription of the booklet carried on the mission. Not used: no stop of this itinerary sits on it.

**Aggregator-only [R], with three corrections that changed the citation rather than the words:**
1. *"You think that Mr Khomeini, [an] uneducated person ..."* The submission claimed no dated primary citation existed; Wikiquote carries one, **David Frost, "The Shah Speaks", January 1980**, shared by three neighbouring quotes. The bracketed "[an]" and the ellipses are the source's own and are preserved.
2. *"As far as we are concerned, we are not the toys of any country, including the United States."* The submitted date, 18 November 1973, is a scraping artifact: Wikiquote dates it only to "an appearance on Meet the Press in 1973", and the day and month come from an adjacent template pointing at an MSNBC page for a **2007** episode. Date dropped; the wording, truncated at the front in the submission, restored.
3. *"One of my chief 'crimes' then, was to have wanted Iran to move from the oil age into the atomic age before it was too late."* The submission silently dropped "then," and "before it was too late" without ellipses, and the dropped phrase carries the sentence's self-justifying force. *The Shah's Story*, 1980, p. 68.

Seven more are used with the primary text unfetched, each carrying its citation in the journey file's `quote_source`: the second Frost line, Fallaci (*Chicago Tribune*, 30 December 1973), *The White Revolution* p. 16, *The Shah's Story* p. 182 and pp. 198-199 (the 3,164 political prisoners, a figure contested by human-rights reporting of the era), Alam's diary p. 233, and Afkhami p. 261 for "Let the dog bark", secondhand in a biography rather than his own words. Gathered and **not used**: de Villiers 1975 p. 259 on SAVAK torture and two *Mission for My Country* lines (pp. 162, 235).

One quote carries its provenance as the point: *"The Allies, after occupying Iran, thought it fitting that I should be in control of affairs..."*, from *Mission for My Country* 1961 but quoted **in Khomeini's "The Crimes of the Shah"**, footnote 5, to argue the Shah admitted foreign installation, with Khomeini adding that the line was struck from later editions. The first edition was not located, so the wording rests on the transcription of his enemy, and the journey file says so.

4. **Cairo pin**: Heliopolis-area versus downtown. Neither used; Koubbeh Palace used, being the building described.

6. **Farah wedding date**: 20 December 1959 in the cited source, 21 December in the submitted entry.
7. **Final departure**: 16 January 1979 in two lenses, 11 February 1979 in the atlas's Mosaddegh file.
8. **The 1949 wound**: "grazed" versus "wounded in the face".
