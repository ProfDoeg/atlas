# Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai (Rashbi): research report

*2026-08-15. Slug `shimon_bar_yochai`. Tanna of the Galilee, second century CE. Compiled from a five-lens research pool, each item re-checked against the cited text where it was reachable.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named and checked. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or later identification, presented as such. Contradictions are flagged and left open; none is adjudicated. Where the first-pass pool was wrong and verification corrected it, the correction is stated in the open and the wrong version named, so the error does not quietly return.

One caution before anything else. Almost nothing in this life is documentary: no inscription, no letter, no contemporary chronicle, no grave archaeology can attribute. What exists is rabbinic narrative composed generations later, medieval identification of sites, and a book written eleven hundred years afterward that claims to be his. Every date below is a placement, not a record. **[A] here means "this is what the source says," never "this happened."**

---

## 1. Origins and schooling

**Birth, c. 90 CE [R].** No primary source dates his birth. The figure is a modern back-calculation from his years under Akiva and his teaching after the Bar Kokhba revolt (New World Encyclopedia).

**Birthplace, Gush Halav [R].** Regional tradition puts the household of Yochai in this Upper Galilee olive town. No Talmudic source names his birthplace; the attribution runs backward from Pesikta de-Rav Kahana's notice that his son Elazar was first buried there. *Correction:* the pool pinned Gush Halav at 33.0056 / 35.4394, about 1.9 km SSW of the town in open hillside. Gush Halav (Jish) is at roughly **33.0217 / 35.4467**. The journey file uses the corrected pin.

**Yavne [A, but the passage is challenged].** Berakhot 28a places him at the coastal academy under Gamliel II and Joshua ben Hananiah. Zecharias Frankel called the passage chronologically impossible: per the Jewish Encyclopedia's summary, about forty-five years *later*, when Akiva was thrown into prison, Shimon's father was still alive, so "Ber. 28a is spurious." *Correction:* the pool ran the forty-five years backward from Akiva's death; they run forward from the Yavne scene to Akiva's imprisonment. *Second correction:* Darkhei ha-Mishnah was published in **Leipzig**, 1859. Breslau (51.1079 / 17.0385, correct for Wroclaw) is where Frankel ran the seminary, not the imprint. Neither city is a place Shimon went, so neither is a stop; the objection lives in the Yavne stop's `date_confidence`.

**Bnei Brak, thirteen years with Akiva [A].** The thirteen-years datum is **Leviticus Rabbah 21**, cited as such by both the Jewish Encyclopedia and Encyclopaedia Judaica. *Correction:* the pool sourced it to Pesachim 68b, which is wrong.

**Who sat beside him: unresolved.** Encyclopaedia Judaica names **Hanina ben Hakhinai** for the thirteen years. The Jewish Encyclopedia pairs him with **Rabbi Meir** in a separate ordination statement. **Yevamot 62b** gives a third arrangement: after Akiva's twenty-four thousand disciples died, he taught **five** in the south (Meir, Yehuda, Yose, Shimon, Elazar ben Shammua). "Alongside Rabbi Meir" is not the standard reading, and the pool's claim that Shimon and Meir *alone* received semikha from Akiva conflates Yevamot 62b with Judah ben Bava's ordination (below). Left open.

## 2. The Hadrianic persecution

**Akiva executed, Caesarea, c. 135 [A].** Berakhot 61b: iron combs, the Shema, death on the word *echad*. Whether Shimon witnessed it is **[R]**; the sources give the scene, not the witness list.

**Ordination between Usha and Shefaram, c. 135 [A].** Sanhedrin 14a, read directly: Judah ben Bava ordains five students in open country between two towns so that neither town be punished, and Roman soldiers kill him for it. This, not any ceremony of Akiva's in a private room at Bnei Brak, is the ordination performed against a capital ban. The pool merged the two events and then wrote that "the text does not record who performed the ceremony's final words." Sanhedrin 14a is explicit that Judah ben Bava performed it.

**The conversation about Roman public works [A as text, R as to place].** Shabbat 33b: Yehuda praises Rome's marketplaces, bathhouses, bridges; Yose is silent; Shimon says Rome built for its own needs. Yehuda ben Gerim repeats it; Yehuda is elevated, Yose exiled to Sepphoris, Shimon sentenced to death. *Correction:* Shabbat 33b names **no location**. I read the passage; it opens only "R. Yehuda, R. Yose and R. Shimon were sitting, and Yehuda ben Gerim was sitting beside them." A Sanhedrin session at Usha is inference, tagged **[R]** in the journey file.

**The first hiding place [A as event, unlocated].** Shabbat 33b: a study hall, his wife bringing bread and a cup of water daily, until they fear she will be made to betray them. No town, no room, no name.

## 3. The cave

**Where it was: two later identifications on a text that names neither.** *This inverts the pool.* Shabbat 33b says only "they went and they hid in a cave." **Gadara** is the reading of the Jewish Encyclopedia and Wikipedia ("hid in a cavern near Gadara"), the biographical-tradition default. **Peki'in** is later Galilean local and pilgrimage tradition, and it is the one with a physical site, a shown carob, and visitors. So this is not "traditionally Peki'in, variant Gadara" but two competing later identifications, both [R]. The journey file pins the Galilean tradition at **32.9814 / 35.3308**, a short walk uphill from the village (32.9742 / 35.3314). The pool's afterlife lens had 33.0122 / 35.3211, about 4.3 km off.

**A third tradition [R].** Zoharic material places the thirteen years in a cave in the desert **near Lod**. The geographies do not reconcile, no source tries, and no cave has ever been proposed at the southern site. Carried as a stop pinned on the town of Lod with the vagueness stated.

**In the cave [A].** Shabbat 33b: a carob tree and a spring; they strip to spare their clothes, sit in sand to the neck, study all day, dress only to pray, and crack with sores.

**Who announced the emperor's death [A, corrected].** **Elijah the Prophet**, not a bat kol: "Elijah the Prophet came and stood at the entrance to the cave and said: Who will inform bar Yochai that the emperor died and his decree has been abrogated?" Only the **second** voice, sending them back for twelve months, is a bat kol ("Have you emerged to destroy My world?"). The pool's chronology lens made both a bat kol while its own geography lens had it right.

**Twelve years plus one [A].** Shabbat 33b.

**Which emperor died is unresolved [R].** The text does not say. Harmonizers offer Hadrian (d. 138) or Antoninus Pius (d. 161). Not settled, and not settled here.

**The two emergences [A].** First: men plowing and sowing, the saying about eternal and temporal life, the burning gaze. Second, after the extra year: the old man running at dusk with two bundles of myrtle, one for "remember," one for "observe," and nothing burns.

## 4. After the cave

**The hot springs [A, one relation corrected].** Shabbat 33b calls Pinchas ben Yair Rabbi Shimon's **son-in-law** (the Davidson text reads "Rabbi Shimon's son-in-law"). The father-in-law reading is a later inversion associated with Zoharic tradition; the pool carried it as "by most readings." Pinchas weeps over the cracked skin; Shimon answers, "Happy are you that you have seen me like this..." The Talmud names only "the bathhouse"; **Hamat Tiberias** is a traditional identification, though the pin (32.7686 / 35.5478) is good.

**Tiberias purified [A].** Yerushalmi Shevi'it 9:1 and Genesis Rabbah 79:6: Antipas's city stood over a burial ground; lupines fail to root where a grave lies, so scattering them charts the hidden dead. *Correction:* the ensuing dispute was **not** "preserved, not settled." Shabbat 34a: an elder scoffs that ben Yochai has purified a cemetery, Shimon directs his eyes at him and the elder dies; then he turns Yehuda ben Gerim into a heap of bones.

**Usha [R, corrected from A].** Encyclopaedia Judaica records **two conflicting traditions**, that he did and that he did not take part in the assembly. The pool presented participation as fact.

## 5. Teaching, travel, rulings

- **Rome [A as narrative].** Me'ilah 17b: three decrees, the demon Ben Temalion, the emperor's daughter, the treasury, and the letter, which they **tore**, not burned (pool corrected). No street, forum, or palace named.
- **Sidon [A].** Pesikta de-Rav Kahana 22:2 (parallel Shir HaShirim Rabbah 1:31): the feast of separation.
- **Two mouths [A, point corrected].** Yerushalmi Berakhot 1:2, 3b: he withdraws the wish not for lack of time but because of denunciation, since with one mouth the world can barely stand for its informers. The pool's gloss inverted the passage.
- **The plowing debate [A].** Berakhot 35b, against Rabbi Yishmael, with the Talmud's own verdict that most who followed Yishmael succeeded and most who followed Shimon did not. No place named.
- **Sukkah 45b [A, chain corrected].** "I am able to absolve the entire world from judgment..." The chain is **Hizkiya, citing Rabbi Yirmeya, citing Rabbi Shimon ben Yohai**, not, as the pool had it, a transmission in his son Elazar's name; the son appears in the adjacent *benei aliyah* saying.
- **Pirkei Avot 4:13 [A].** The three crowns and the crown of a good name.
- **Shabbat 118b [A].** Two consecutive Sabbaths kept properly, and redemption at once.
- **Mishnaic standing [A]; school attributions [R].** Among the most cited authorities in the Mishnah. Later tradition credits his school with the Sifre and the Mekhilta de-Rabbi Shimon; critical scholarship does not confirm it.
- **Kfar Bar'am [R].** Local piety credits the region's best-preserved synagogue facade to his disciples' merit. No inscription, no textual link.
- **Tekoa of the Galilee [R].** Later sources name a Galilean Tekoa where he taught or where Judah HaNasi studied under him; some medieval geographers equate it with Meron. No excavation has fixed it. Folded into the Meron teaching stop rather than given a pin.

## 6. Death and burial

**Death, Meron, mid-to-late second century [A as tradition, no year].** Pesikta de-Rav Kahana describes the day as marked with joy rather than mourning. No source gives a year; c. 160 is placement by generation.

**Elazar's bones [A].** Pesikta de-Rav Kahana: first buried at Gush Halav, later brought to Meron on the petition of its people. The text does not explain the original separation.

**The Idras [R].** The Zohar stages the Great Assembly in a valley below Meron, three of ten companions dying in the transmission, and the Small Assembly as his deathbed, the house full of fire, the master dying mid-word. Thirteenth-century Kabbalah narrating a second-century death it was not present for. The valley has never been matched to a spot.

**Archaeology [A].** Prof. Mordechai Aviam, in the Jerusalem Post, states flatly that no archaeological evidence exists for the tomb's attribution. Mishnaic-period rock-cut burials on the site are real and identify nobody. A medieval tradition once gave the same grave to Hillel the Elder.

**Earliest documentation [A].** The thirteenth-century itinerary of Jacob ben Netanel ha-Kohen, roughly eleven hundred years after the death.

## 7. The Zohar

The Zohar begins circulating in Castile in the 1280s, framed in its own pages as the record of Shimon's teaching, much of it from the cave years; medieval Kabbalah takes the framing literally **[R]**.

Modern scholarship, following **Gershom Scholem**, attributes it to **Moses de Leon** of Castile in the late thirteenth century, on the evidence of artificial Aramaic, Spanish-inflected grammar, and geographic errors about the Land of Israel inconsistent with a second-century Galilean author **[A]**. *Correction:* the pool dated the foundational study to **1934**, which corresponds to no Scholem work on the Zohar. The anchors are his 1926 Hebrew article "Is Moses de Leon the Author of the Zohar?" (Mada'ei ha-Yahadut 1, pp. 16-29) and *Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism* (1941).

**Not resolved here.** Traditional and religious sources continue to hold Shimon as the Zohar's source. The two accounts are incompatible, both are live, and this file records both.

**Printing [A].** Two rival first editions in 1558, Cremona (Conti; colophon 21 November 1558) and Mantua (Ruffinelli), the Mantua text eventually recognized as editio princeps though Cremona served as the base for later editions into the eighteenth century.

**Critical translation [A, corrected].** *The Zohar: Pritzker Edition*, twelve volumes, Stanford University Press, **2004-2017** (not 1998-2017 as the pool had it), Daniel C. Matt for volumes 1-9, completed by Nathan Wolski and Joel Hecker.

## 8. The tomb and its crowds

- **Before 1492 [A].** Musta'arabi (Arabic-speaking, indigenous) Jews are recorded managing the site before the Sephardi refugees arrive.
- **c. 1570 [A].** Isaac Luria is the first writer known to state that the death fell on Lag BaOmer. The Safed circle's adoption of Shimon as patron is what turns a grave into an annual festival: the bonfire on the tomb roof, the *upsherin* first haircut of three-year-old boys. None of it reaches back to the second century (Schechter Institute).
- **c. 1580 [R].** Abraham ben Mordecai Galante "is said to have built" a mausoleum over the tomb.
- **1817, 1850 [A].** Shmuel Abu, a French consular agent arrived from Algeria, buys the land and builds the tomb building that still substantially stands.
- **1881 [A].** The Survey of Western Palestine calls the structure "apparently a modern building."
- **1911 [A].** A balcony collapses during Lag BaOmer, killing 11 among some 10,000 present.
- **1956 [A].** Yosef Shenberger wins a competition to renovate the complex, whose ownership is already contested between Sephardic and Ashkenazic trusts.
- **30 April 2021 [A].** A crowd crush kills 45 and injures about 150 among roughly 100,000 attendees in a narrow, steep stairway slick with spilled drink; the deadliest civil disaster in Israel's history.
- **2024 [A].** The Mount Meron Disaster Commission finds personal responsibility on the part of the Prime Minister, police commanders, and local officials, citing negligence, absent governance, and years of unenforced construction-safety law.

**Flagged contradiction on the building.** One strand (Grokipedia; galilandgolan.com) calls the standing structure a sixteenth-century Galante building renovated in the nineteenth. Wikipedia states that Abu "built the tomb building which now stands on the site." Both cannot be true of the same fabric. The journey file follows Abu, states the Galante claim as the [R] tradition it is, and names the disagreement.

**Peki'in [R].** The cave remains a lesser pilgrimage stop; a large old carob beside it is shown as the tree or its descendant. The village synagogue was restored in 1955 at President Yitzhak Ben-Zvi's request, and two stones inside are said locally to have come from the destroyed Second Temple. Peki'in held one of the Galilee's oldest continuously inhabited Jewish communities until the mid-twentieth century.

## 9. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **The cave's location.** Gadara against Peki'in against a desert cave near Lod, on a text naming none of them.
2. **Which emperor died.** Hadrian (d. 138) or Antoninus Pius (d. 161).
3. **Who studied the thirteen years with him.** Hanina ben Hakhinai, or Rabbi Meir, or Yevamot 62b's five southern students.
4. **Usha.** Encyclopaedia Judaica preserves both that he attended and that he did not.
5. **Berakhot 28a.** Frankel calls it spurious; it is nevertheless the only evidence putting him at Yavne.
6. **The Zohar.** Traditional authorship against Scholem's attribution to Moses de Leon.
7. **The tomb building.** Sixteenth-century Galante fabric against nineteenth-century Abu construction.
8. **Pinchas ben Yair.** Son-in-law in Shabbat 33b, father-in-law in the later inversion.

## 10. Honest gaps

- **No systematic critical study exists** of the full body of traditions attributed to him across rabbinic literature. The gap is stated explicitly in Encyclopaedia Judaica, and it means every synthesis of this life, including this one, is assembled from fragments nobody has audited as a set.
- **No birth date, no death date, no year for anything.** Every date in the journey file is placement by generation, and `date_confidence` says so in every case.
- **No archaeology attributes the tomb.** Aviam's statement is unambiguous.
- **The first hiding place has no location.** The source gives the wife's daily walk and nothing else.
- **Gadara not pursued.** Whether any Gadara-area cave has been proposed by name was not checked; the sources give the reading without a site.
- **Hebrew and Aramaic secondary literature not consulted.** Verification worked from Sefaria's Davidson text plus English-language encyclopedic and scholarly summaries.

## 12. Interlocks

- **adriano** (Hadrian). Direct, and the strongest link here: Hadrian's own file gives the 130 CE foundation of Aelia Capitolina and the circumcision ban as the twin causes of the Bar Kokhba revolt, whose suppression killed Akiva and produced the decree Shimon fled. Named in campa at Caesarea and at the cave, where the identity of the dead emperor is left open between him and Antoninus Pius. The Temple Mount pin (31.778 / 35.2354) belongs to Hadrian's file and is not reused, since Shimon is not recorded at Jerusalem.
- **moses_de_leon** (queued, no journey file yet). Direct and one-directional across eleven hundred years: his book is framed as Shimon's teaching. Named in campa at the Meron stop for the Zohar, where the text sets itself.
- **giovanni_pico_della_mirandola**, **arthur_edward_waite**. Real one-directional engagements with the Zohar tradition, so cited in `sources` and `suggested_refs` on that stop, not staged as scenes: Pico's 1486 theses and Waite's The Holy Kabbalah (1929) are their own travelers' stops.
- **maimonides**, **saadia_gaon**, **hiram_abiff**. Shared Galilee geography only. Not named in any campa; the map already shows co-location.
- **rashi**, **giulio_camillo**, **borges**, **martin_buber**, **christian_rosenkreuz**. Thematic or intermediary links only (transmission structure, Christian Kabbalah, Scholem as go-between). Not named.
