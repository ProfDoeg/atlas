# The Lodge of Rational Knights (Cádiz) and Its Network — A Roster

## What it was

The **Sociedad de Caballeros Racionales** was a secret *political* society of American-born creoles operating in Cádiz c. 1810/11–1812 — not a masonic lodge, though it imitated masonic forms (five grades instead of three, oaths of secrecy, motto "Unión y Beneficencia"). Its best inside witness, fray Servando Teresa de Mier, told the Mexican Inquisition flatly that it "no era de masones." The scholarly consensus from Mitre through Piccirilli ("sobran argumentos y faltan pruebas"), Ferrer Benimeli, Seal-Coon and Cuccorese agrees: patriotic conspiracy in masonic dress. No lodge archive survives in London, Madrid or Cádiz.

**The evidence base is astonishingly thin and astonishingly good at once.** Nearly everything documented rests on one packet: two letters signed by Carlos María de Alvear (London, 28 Oct 1811) to Rafael Diego de Mérida in Caracas, with membership lists attached, intercepted January 1812 aboard the brig *La Rosa* by a Puerto Rican privateer (Archivo "Álvaro de Bazán"; published by Julio Guillén, 1960). The Spanish crown's Cortavarría circular (27 Apr 1812) independently repeats every name. Secondary support: Mier's 1817 Inquisition declarations and Zapiola's old-age testimony to Mitre (the least reliable of the three).

**Founding.** Per Mier, Alvear founded the society at his own house in the barrio de San Carlos, Cádiz (c. early 1811), claiming to act on papers received from **Santa Fe de Bogotá** — a Bogotá origin independently echoed by Gen. Enrique Martínez in 1853. The traditional genealogy (Madrid 1806/07 under José de Moldes, on remnants of an Olavide circle) is late Salta tradition (Frías 1902) and retrojection; Olavide was dead by 1803 and Moldes had left Spain by January 1809, two years before the documented lodge existed.

**Numbering — as documented, not as popularly told.** Cádiz = **No. 3**; Caracas = **No. 4** (founded by Mérida); London = **No. 7**, founded Oct 1811 "por orden de la Logia N.º 3" by Alvear, San Martín, Zapiola, Mier, Villa-Urrutia and Chilavert (motto "Unión, Firmeza y Valor"). A Philadelphia lodge is mentioned as active; a Jalapa (New Spain) filial ran early 1812 until broken up in May. **No document assigns London No. 1** — the "matrix No. 1" (Bogotá? Philadelphia?) is inference only. Note the inversion: Cádiz *ordered* the London foundation, the reverse of the Miranda-mother-lodge legend.

**How it seeded the Lautaro lodges.** The London No. 7 founders sailed on the *George Canning* (19 Jan 1812), reached Buenos Aires 9 Mar 1812, and founded the Buenos Aires lodge mid-1812 (later called Lautaro; reorganized 1816 under Pueyrredón and San Martín around the Chile expedition — "Lautaro" was reportedly the password meaning *expedition to Chile*). The Santiago **Logia Lautarina** followed after Chacabuco (installed 12–13 Mar 1817, presided by O'Higgins — its constitution survives in O'Higgins's own hand, the strongest primary document of any Lautaro chapter). A thinly documented Lima circle ran San Martín's Protectorate (1821–22). Miranda's London "Gran Reunión Americana" (1797/98) as the network's mother lodge is legend: nothing in Miranda's 63-volume archive, nothing in any masonic register (Robertson 1929; Seal-Coon 1982). What is real is Miranda's house at 27 Grafton Street as an open political salon for exiles, 1798–1810 — where No. 7 later met, after Miranda had already left for Venezuela.

## TIER 1 — Documented members (contemporary evidence, chiefly the intercepted Alvear letters + Cortavarría circular)

**The Cádiz–London core:**

- **Carlos María de Alvear** (1789–1852, Buenos Aires) · founder/president, Cádiz No. 3; founder-president, London No. 7 · his own signed letters, Mier's testimony, his 1819 *Refutación* · The organizer of the whole network; later Supreme Director of the United Provinces (1815).
- **José de San Martín** (1778–1850) · Cádiz No. 3; co-founder London No. 7 · named in Alvear's letter and the Cortavarría circular; his own 1848 letter to Castilla admits "una reunión de americanos, en Cádiz" · Liberator of Argentina, Chile and Peru — the network's supreme payoff. (The separate claim of masonic initiation in a Cádiz lodge "Integridad" 1808 is unsupported and chronologically impossible.)
- **José Matías Zapiola** (1780–1874, Buenos Aires) · Cádiz No. 3; co-founder London No. 7 · both Alvear letters; later Mitre's key (and weakest) oral source · Naval officer, Granaderos commander, last surviving witness.
- **Fray Servando Teresa de Mier** (1765–1827, Monterrey) · initiated Cádiz No. 3 (ceremony presided by Alvear); co-founder London No. 7 · Alvear's letter + his own rich Inquisition declarations (Nov 1817) — the best interior account of the lodge · Mexican insurgent priest, propagandist, twice-escaped prisoner.
- **Ramón Eduardo de Anchoris** (Buenos Aires, priest) · Cádiz No. 3 · named in Alvear's letter as the lodge's new **president** on Alvear's departure (Sept 1811) · Third and last known president of No. 3.
- **Rafael Diego de Mérida** (1762–1828, Caracas) · Cádiz No. 3, then founder-**president of Caracas No. 4** · addressee of the intercepted letters · Later Bolívar's secretary of Grace and Justice (1813).
- **Villa-Urrutia** · Cádiz No. 3; co-founder London No. 7 · Alvear's letter; first name unresolved (plausibly of the Mexican Fagoaga-Villaurrutia family).
- **Chilavert** (probably Francisco) · Cádiz No. 3; co-founder London No. 7; *George Canning* passenger · Alvear's letter; Martínez's 1853 testimony · Father of Martiniano Chilavert.

**Admitted in London (No. 7, Lista N.º 4):**

- **Manuel Moreno** (Buenos Aires) · brother of Mariano Moreno; in London after Mariano died at sea · later diplomat and San Martín-faction lodge man in Buenos Aires.
- **Luis López Méndez** (1770–1841, Caracas) · Venezuela's agent in London; Alvear expected him to preside No. 7; the intercepted mail packet was his · later recruiter of the British Legion.
- **Andrés Bello** (1781–1865, Caracas) · admitted 1811 while secretary of the Venezuelan mission · the great humanist — a fact his hagiography routinely omits.
- **Francisco Fagoaga, Marqués del Apartado** (Mexico City) · admitted 1811 (the list mislabels him "de Caracas"; the circular corrects to "de Méjico").

**Received at Cádiz No. 3 after Mérida's departure (Lista N.º 1):**

- **Miguel Santa María** (Veracruz) — later famed Mexican diplomat; **Antonio del Valle**, **José Sotolongo**, **Andrés Arango**, **Vicente Quesada** (Havana); **Vicente Acuña**, **Joaquín La Carrera Ortiz** (⚠ not José Miguel Carrera), **José Herrera** (New Spain); **Juan Vatres/Batres** (Guatemala); **José María Vergara [Lozano]** (Santafé de Bogotá).

**"Hermanos" named in Alvear's personal letter:**

- **Domingo Caicedo** (1783–1843, Santafé) · Cortes deputy; per Mier a co-founder; fled toward Bogotá · later acting president of Colombia.
- **José Álvarez de Toledo** (Havana; deputy for Santo Domingo) · escaped via Philadelphia with a price on his head · later insurgent agent in the US/Texas — and eventual defector back to Spain.
- **Román de la Luz** (Cuba) · jailed at Cádiz for the 1810 Havana conspiracy; a documented freemason before deportation.
- **Murguiondo** (Basque; possibly Prudencio, of Montevideo), **Valbín/Balbín**, **Rada**, **Armenteros**, **Larrea**, **López Conde**, **Roche** (the lodge's ship captain) · surnames only, mostly unidentified.

**Documented refusers (Lista N.º 2 — proof of the recruitment perimeter):** Manuel Rodrigo, Luis Velasco (Buenos Aires), the Marqués de San Felipe y Santiago (Havana), Andrés Savariego, Joaquín Obregón (Mexico) — invited, refused "por temor a los déspotas españoles," barred forever.

**Documented in the successor lodges (local recruits, not Cádiz men):**

- **Bernardo O'Higgins** (1778–1842) · **founder-president of the Santiago Logia Lautarina (1817)** — its constitution survives in his own hand (Vicuña Mackenna 1860), the best primary document of any Lautaro chapter. His Miranda mentorship in London 1798–99 is documented (his own recollections + Miranda's "Consejos" + the fallout of Caro's betrayal). His membership in the Cádiz Caballeros Racionales is chronologically impossible — he sailed for Chile in April 1802.
- **Bernardo de Monteagudo** · Buenos Aires Lautaro (Zapiola's list); conductor of the order to execute the Carrera brothers (1818); Protectorate minister in Lima; assassinated 1825. (His alleged London initiation by Miranda is impossible — he never left South America.)
- **Tomás Guido**, **José Ignacio Zenteno**, **Miguel Zañartu**, **Hilarión de la Quintana**, **Francisco Antonio Pérez / Luis de la Cruz / José Manuel Astorga** (the 1817 Junta Delegada), **Ramón Freire** · Santiago/BA lodge membership attested in the O'Higgins–San Martín–Zañartu correspondence. **Marcos Balcarce** is documented as *rejected* by the Santiago lodge — proof of its veto power.
- **Mariano Rincón** (Veracruz) · Jalapa filial (1812), per Mexican judicial records (Guedea).

## TIER 2 — Probable / network (strong association, initiation less certain)

- **José de Moldes** (1785–1824, Salta) · plausibly of an informal pre-1808 Madrid circle of American conspirators (Frías 1902 tradition); the "president of Cádiz No. 3 until 1808" formula is a retrojection — he left Spain two years before the documented lodge existed.
- **Francisco de Gurruchaga** (1766–1846, Salta) · Cádiz conspiratorial circle pre-1809; helped Pueyrredón escape disguised as a coachman; Madrid "co-founder" claim rests solely on Frías. (The "José de Gurruchaga" who "directed" the lodge in popular lists is a transmission error.)
- **Mier's co-founder list**: **Prada** (Santafé), **Gracida**, **Urriola** (Havana), **Garza** (Caracas) · single late Inquisition testimony, no corroboration.
- **José de Rivadeneira** (Peru) · self-claimed member of a 63-strong Cádiz society; San Martín embraced him at Huaura recalling "nuestros trabajos en la sociedad de Cádiz" and made him governor of Callao — the appointment lends weight. His extended roster of bare surnames (Tobar, Castillo, Pérez, Ruiz, Suárez, Pinedo, Juanos…) is one late source.
- **Alvear's 1819 circle**: Vera, Pablo Zufriátegui, Vallejo, "Olembert" (plausibly Baron **Eduardo Kaunitz de Holmberg**, *George Canning* passenger) · named by Alvear as his Cádiz patriots, membership implied not stated.
- **Buenos Aires Lautaro per Zapiola's list** (single participant testimony, decades later): Julián Álvarez, Valentín Gómez, Posadas (documented as Director governing with lodge support), Juan Larrea, Vieytes, Rodríguez Peña, Nicolás Herrera, Agrelo, Dorrego, Azcuénaga, the Balcarces, Manuel Pinto (Venerable per J.M. Carrera's hostile pamphlet); San Martín's faction: Donado, Álvarez Jonte, Luzuriaga, Vicente López y Planes, Ramón Rojas, Ugarteche, Lezica.
- **Juan Martín de Pueyrredón** · admitted 1816 (Córdoba meeting); as Supreme Director reorganized the lodge for the Chile plan — membership testimonial, behavior confirmatory.
- **Lima circle** (1821–22): García del Río, Paroissien, Blanco Encalada, Las Heras, Alvarado, Necochea · the Protectorate was run by lodge men, but the Lima chapter is the weakest-documented of all.
- **Tomás Godoy Cruz** (Mendoza filial head), **José Antonio Álvarez Condarco** (liaison/spy).

## TIER 3 — Contested or legendary (popularly claimed; scholarship doubts or refutes)

- **Francisco de Miranda** · *Claimed*: freemason Grand Master, founder of the "Gran Reunión Americana" mother lodge of the whole network. · *Why doubted*: no trace in his 24 volumes of diaries or any masonic register (Robertson 1929; Seal-Coon 1982: "neither inventor nor member of any pseudo-masonry"); the network's own documents point to Cádiz — and Bogotá — not London, and never mention him. **What's real**: his Grafton Street salon (1798–1810) as recruiting hub, and his sworn mentorship of O'Higgins. The godfather of the milieu, not the founder of the lodge.
- **Simón Bolívar** · *Claimed*: sworn before Miranda in London 1810; initiated at Cádiz 1803; Caballero Racional. · *The evidence, honestly*: Bolívar **was** a documented regular freemason — initiated, passed and raised in the Paris lodge Saint-Alexandre d'Écosse, 1805–06 (Seal-Coon, AQC 90, on six documents) — and his 1810 London contact with Miranda is fully documented. But that Paris lodge has nothing to do with this network; his name is absent from the intercepted rolls (his own mission companions López Méndez and Bello joined No. 7 — *after* he had sailed home); the "oath before Miranda" is one unsourced Mitre sentence. He later mocked masonry and banned secret societies (1828).
- **Manuel Belgrano** · *Claimed*: mason from 1795, Lautaro member. · *Why doubted*: he left Spain in 1793, 15 years before the society existed; no contemporary document; not in Zapiola's roster; Iriarte's *Memorias* say he **refused** to join, citing Church condemnation; Levene: no document proves membership. Contested-to-rejected.
- **Mariano Moreno** · *Claimed*: initiated by Miranda in London. · *Why doubted*: he died at sea 4 Mar 1811 before reaching London. Impossible. The legend likely grows from conflation with his brother Manuel (who **is** on the No. 7 list).
- **José Miguel Carrera** · *Claimed*: Caballero Racional at Cádiz 1810–11. · *Why doubted*: resident in Cádiz at the right time, but absent from the intercepted lists (beware confusion with the Mexican "Joaquín La Carrera Ortiz"). Later the network's mortal enemy — his hostile 1818 pamphlets are a primary source on the lodge that helped kill his brothers, and killed him (1821).
- **José Cortés Madariaga & Juan Pablo Fretes** · *Claimed*: Cádiz lodge clerics who aided O'Higgins's Miranda commission (1799). · *Why doubted*: both were in America for the entire documented life of the lodge (canonries taken 1803); pure Lappas/Vicuña Mackenna retrojection of an earlier, undocumented "Unión Americana" milieu.
- **Mitre's/Carnicelli's "Gran Reunión Americana initiates"** (Nariño, Rocafuerte, the Montúfars, Pedro José Caro, José Cecilio del Valle, Martínez de Rozas, Argomedo, Rojas) · unsourced roster; several never set foot in Europe (del Valle, Rozas — flatly impossible). Caro was a real Miranda agent (who betrayed him) — associate, not initiate.
- **Pablo de Olavide** · *Claimed*: remote founder of a Madrid precursor circle. · *Why doubted*: in French exile/prison during the alleged years; died a penitent Catholic in 1803, four years before the supposed Madrid lodge. Pure genealogical myth-making.
- **Frías's Madrid 1807 roster** (the Moldes brothers, Pueyrredón, the Gurruchagas, O'Higgins, Zapiola, the Lezicas, Pinto, Alvear…) · one uncited 1902 paragraph, 95 years after the fact, with impossible members (O'Higgins was in Chile from 1802). A kernel of real Madrid malcontents; no documented society.
- **The 1816 "Gran Logia" roster** (French, Beruti, Saavedra, Paso, Chiclana et al.) and **Rivadavia**, **Rondeau**, **Thompson**, **Camilo Henríquez** · circulate in popular lists without stated sources; treat as unverified tradition.

## Richest journeys for the quipu treatment

The arcs with the most documented waypoints, born → revolution → death: **San Martín** (Yapeyú → Spanish army → Cádiz → London → Buenos Aires → the Andes → Lima → renunciation at Guayaquil → death in Boulogne-sur-Mer, 1850); **Alvear** (Misiones → the whole family massacred at sea save him → Cádiz founder → London → Supreme Director → disgrace → Montevideo rival lodge → death in New York, 1852); **fray Servando de Mier** (Monterrey → serial escapes across two continents → Cádiz initiation → London → Inquisition dungeon where he narrates the lodge → Mexican congress, dies 1827) — the single best witness *and* the wildest ride; **Mérida** (Caracas → Cádiz → founding No. 4 → Bolívar's minister → exile); **Miguel Santa María** (Cádiz reception list → Mexican diplomacy); **Domingo Caicedo** (Cortes → flight → presidency of Colombia); **Álvarez de Toledo** (deputy → hunted fugitive → Texas filibuster → traitor); and as tragic counter-thread, **José Miguel Carrera** (Cádiz outsider → Chilean dictator → the lodge kills his brothers, then him). O'Higgins and Bolívar orbit the story magnificently, but their lodge threads are the ones to draw *thin* — mentorship and Paris masonry respectively, not Cádiz initiation.
