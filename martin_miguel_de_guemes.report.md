# Martín Miguel de Güemes (1785-1821) : research report

*Slug: `martin_miguel_de_guemes`. The journey file's register is "national mythology: the canon is true". This report is the evidence layer underneath that register and does not share it.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, approximation, or a claim whose primary document was not reached. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; gaps are stated as gaps.

**What this pass corrected.** The circulating chronology carries a cluster of errors that travel together: Belgrano commanding the 1810 expedition (he did not), Cerrillos dated 1815 (it is 1816), Puesto del Marqués dated 1814 in Salta (it is 1815, in Jujuy), Rincón de Marlopa fought by Güemes near Salta (he was absent; the field is in Tucumán), the 1820 invasion led by De la Serna in February (Ramírez Orozco, in May), San Pedrito dated 1820 (February 1817), and four coordinates off by 24 to 250 km. One quotation in wide circulation as Güemes's own is Belgrano's, and is dropped.

---

## 1. Birth and formation, 1785-1808

**[A, Wikipedia es/en]** Born at Salta, Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata, 8 February 1785, into a family of the local governing class. **Gap:** the birth house has never been identified, so the journey file uses the cathedral square (-24.7883, -65.4103) and says so in the stop.

**[R]** Schooling at the Real Colegio de San Carlos in Buenos Aires, with private instruction in philosophy and the sciences. The college's own article does not name him among its alumni. A genuine gap: the schooling rests on biographers, not on a register.

**[A, es.wikipedia]** Enlists at fourteen in the Regimiento Fijo de Infantería, detachment duty in Salta (1799), and goes down to Buenos Aires with the regiment in 1805. Fights in the defense against the second British invasion, 1807; falls ill; his father dies that November. Transfer back to Salta approved 10 March 1808; by December listed there as lieutenant of the Cuerpo de Granaderos.

**[R, downgraded from the pool's [A]]** The capture of the *Justine*, 1806. en.wikipedia calls her "the armed British merchantman Justine, moored in shallow waters", citing Cornejo, *Historia de Güemes* (1971), p. 46; es.wikipedia says she "había encallado por una bajante repentina del río". An aground merchantman, not a warship at a quay, and the episode descends through traditional biography rather than any contemporary document.

## 2. Upper Peru, 1810-1812

**[A, command corrected]** Joins the first auxiliary expedition to Upper Peru. **The pool's "under Belgrano" is an error carried by the Güemes article itself.** es.wikipedia, "Primera expedición auxiliadora al Alto Perú": Col. Francisco Ortiz de Ocampo commanding, Antonio González Balcarce second, Hipólito Vieytes then Juan José Castelli as political representative. Belgrano was in Paraguay and took the Ejército del Norte only on 26 March 1812.

**[A, coordinate corrected]** Decisive part, as a captain, at Suipacha, 7 November 1810. es.wikipedia gives **-21.5656, -65.6089**, about 25 km from Tupiza; the pool's -20.8167, -65.7333 was roughly 85 km too far north. Two days later Castelli sends him with 150 mounted tarijeños to secure the head of Cinti and pursue fleeing royalist officials; he operates in the Tarija valleys through 1810-11.

**[A, coordinate corrected]** After the rout at Huaqui, 19 June 1811, covers the withdrawal south and holds the Quebrada de Humahuaca. Corrected from -16.5975, -68.6764 to en.wikipedia's **-16.64284, -68.90147**, about 24 km off.

**[A, arithmetic corrected]** Recovers Tarija, 18 January 1812, on Pueyrredón's orders. **The "300 men, 500 rifles and two cannon" are spoils taken from the royalists, not the force he brought:** es.wikipedia has him rejoin the army "con un botín de 300 hombres, 500 fusiles y dos cañones tomados del ejército realista", and dates the start of the "guerra de recursos" to after Huaqui, not to Tarija.

**[A, direction reversed]** The pool reads as though Güemes was ordered to Buenos Aires and Belgrano kept him instead. It is the other way round: "Belgrano había ordenado el traslado de Güemes a Buenos Aires, y dispuso su integración al Estado Mayor General". Belgrano ordered him away, removing him from the northern theatre. The geography lens dated this February 1812, but Belgrano only assumed command on 26 March, so the order falls later in the year; the journey file dates it 1812-04-01 and marks the date nominal.

**[R, contradiction not resolved]** Whether Güemes backed the Éxodo Jujeño (August 1812). es.wikipedia attributes the withdrawal to Belgrano on the Triunvirato's order and places Güemes's removal in the same passage, which would put him out of the province. Not asserted in the journey file.

## 3. The vanguard and the opening of the gaucho war, 1813-1815

**[R, direct contradiction between two Wikipedia articles, left standing]** Presence at the Battle of Salta (Campo de Castañares), 20 February 1813. The Güemes article puts his gaucho cavalry on the field; the article on the battle names only Belgrano and Eustoquio Díaz Vélez and is silent on him. He is by then attached to the General Staff in Buenos Aires, which cuts against his presence without settling it.

**[A, with a one-day discrepancy inside the atlas]** Belgrano hands the Army of the North to San Martín at the Posta de Yatasto. `san_martin.journey.json` dates the meeting 17 January 1814 and the Ciudadela handover 29 January; `belgrano.journey.json` dates the handover 30 January. **Flagged, not adjudicated.** The journey file uses both san_martin dates and says in the campa that the files disagree.

**[A, `san_martin.journey.json`]** From La Ciudadela, San Martín gives the northern mountain frontier to Güemes and his gauchos while he turns toward Chile. **[R]** His Saldán letter of 22 April 1814, that the road to Lima does not run through Upper Peru, implies he treats that frontier as a static shield. Inference, marked as such.

**[A, year corrected]** Promoted lieutenant colonel and given the vanguard. The geography lens dated the Tucumán posting 1813; it belongs to **1814**, after Vilcapugio and Ayohuma and during San Martín's command, January to April 1814. **[R]** The Río Pasaje (Juramento) and the Valle de Lerma are stretches of country, not points; the journey coordinate is approximate and marked so.

**[A, `maria_remedios_del_valle.journey.json`]** María Remedios del Valle joins the frontier war out of Salta from about August 1814, serving also under Arenales; her 1827 certification, signed by Col. Hipólito Videla, names Güemes among her commanders as sworn testimony.

**[A, year and province corrected]** Puesto del Marqués. The pool dated it 1814 in Salta at -24.9, -65.3. Both wrong: es.wikipedia gives **14 April 1815**, in **Jujuy** on the puna near Abra Pampa, commanders Fernández de la Cruz, Martín Rodríguez and Güemes against Antonio Vigil, during Rondeau's third expedition. The pool coordinate was roughly 250 km too far south; the journey file uses -22.72, -65.7, marked approximate. The withdrawal into the puna that follows the rupture with Rondeau therefore belongs to 1815.

## 4. Governor of Salta, 1815-1816

**[A]** Assumes office as governor-intendant on 6 May 1815, the first governor of Salta elected by local authorities rather than appointed from Buenos Aires; the province then took in Jujuy, Tarija and Orán. **[A for the biography; gap in the building's record]** Marries Carmen Puch in the cathedral, 15 July 1815, a wedding the cathedral's own article does not mention.

**[R, attribution not traced]** The División Infernal de Gauchos de Línea, reported to the Supreme Director on 12 September 1815. The unit is documented; the wording of the communication was not located in an archival or edited-correspondence source. Flagged in `quote_source`.

**[A, year corrected]** The Pacto de los Cerrillos. The pool dated it 1815, one entry September 1815. It is **1816**: es.wikipedia, "José Rondeau", has Rondeau besiege Salta in January 1816 and the pact signed "tras algunas semanas", conventionally 22 March 1816. The substance (recognition as governor, charge of the northern frontier) and the coordinate -24.9, -65.4833 are correct.

**[R]** That the frontier war made the Declaration of Independence of 9 July 1816 possible. He is not recorded as present at Tucumán. A claim about a counterfactual, treated as canon-level tradition.

## 5. The line of the Pasaje, 1816-1819

**[A]** Belgrano writes on 3 August 1816 endorsing the strategy of small victories. **[A, `belgrano.journey.json`]** From that month he holds the inner line at La Ciudadela and supplies Güemes; they correspond to 1819, and neither commands the other.

**[R, coordinate corrected]** The Línea del Pasaje, over seven hundred kilometres from Volcán in Jujuy to beyond San Ramón de la Nueva Orán. Volcán corrected from -23.6167, -65.4667 to **-23.91798, -65.46529**, 33 km south. No personal action of Güemes is documented there.

**[R, attribution contradiction]** Humahuaca, 1 March 1817. en.wikipedia credits Güemes; es.wikipedia and the town's article credit **Manuel Eduardo Arias** with 150 men, taking 6 cannon, 500 rifles and prisoners. The date is not in dispute, the command is. Both stated, neither adopted.

**[A, `juana_azurduy.journey.json`]** Juana Azurduy joins Güemes from about April 1817 by way of friar José Indalecio de Salazar and rides with his forces to 1821. **[R]** Her file records a tradition that the relationship went beyond command and subordinate, flagged there as unresolved. Carried across unresolved.

**[A, date and characterization corrected]** De la Serna enters Salta **15 April 1817**, not the 16th (en.wikipedia's date is unsourced), and "repelled" is wrong: Güemes blockaded and starved the occupation, evacuated Salta on 5 May and Jujuy on 21 May. Six days in comes the **Combate del Bañado**, which the pool disputed between 1817 and 1820: es.wikipedia dates it **21 April 1817**, Col. Sardina mortally wounded, while the June 1820 combats were at Chamical, Las Cañas, Cuesta de la Pedrera and Yala. The [R] on the year is retired; the site stays approximate.

**[A]** The moneda de Güemes: debased silver authorized 1817, legalized by countermark 26 October 1817, annulled by Buenos Aires 24 May 1818. **[R]** Roughly 237 engagements across 1816-1821: a traditional tally, unverified.

**[R, attributions not traced]** Pezuela on the method ("Su plan es de no dar ni recibir batalla decisiva en parte alguna...") is plausible and consistent with his documented correspondence but unconfirmed against an archival edition; flagged in `quote_source`. Two more, "Esta provincia no me representa más que un semblante de miseria..." and "He jurado defender la independencia de América...", come through secondary compilations with no document number, the second close enough to standard oath language to be paraphrase; neither is used.

**[R] MISATTRIBUTION, corrected and dropped.** "Hace Ud. muy bien en reírse de los doctores; sus vocinglerías se las lleva el viento..." circulates as a Güemes quotation. **The words are Belgrano's.** es.wikiquote files them under "Citas sobre Güemes", source "Gral. Manuel Belgrano en carta a Güemes", citing Luis Güemes (comp.), *Güemes documentado*, vol. 6 (Plus Ultra, 1980), p. 156. An anonymous, uncited blog (elcaudillodelnorte.blogspot.com) reverses the direction; the documentary citation governs. The pool also carried a stray capital ("Hace Ud. Muy bien").

## 6. The last invasions and the fall, 1820-1821

**[A, month and commander corrected]** The 1820 invasion: es.wikipedia, "Guerra gaucha", has Gen. Juan Ramírez Orozco leave Tupiza on 8 May with 5,000 men, occupy Jujuy on 28 May and Salta on 31 May, and retreat on 5 July. De la Serna ordered it but did not lead it; en.wikipedia's unsourced "In February 1820 another wave of Spanish troops invaded" is not supported. Relatedly, the Combate de San Pedrito, which the pool put in June 1820 against Ramírez Orozco, is dated **6 February 1817** and won by Juan Antonio Rojas near Jujuy. Dropped from the journey for length.

**[A]** Named general-in-chief by San Martín and given the Ejército de Observación over Peru, 1820. Month not established; hedge in `date_confidence`.

**[A, three corrections]** Rincón de Marlopa, 3 April 1821. **(1)** Güemes was absent: "Güemes no participó en la campaña de Heredia"; the column of roughly 2,000 was under Col. Alejandro Heredia. **(2)** The Tucumán field commander was Col. Abraham González, not Bernabé Aráoz, who was the governor being attacked. **(3)** The field lies in Tucumán two leagues south of San Miguel at the confluence of the Lules and Manantial, about **-26.87, -65.24**, some 230 km from the pool's "near Salta". The date is correct. Forces aligned with the expedition then occupy Catamarca before the venture collapses.

**[A]** The Revolución del Comercio, 24 May 1821: the Patria Nueva faction under Evaristo de Uriburu deposes Güemes and signs an armistice with Olañeta. He briefly retakes power before losing control again.

**[A, wound corrected]** 7 June 1821: José María Valdés, called Barbarucho, comes from Yavi on Olañeta's orders with 300 men down the Quebrada del Toro and enters Salta. Güemes is surrounded inside the city at the Tagarete del Tineo, today's Calle Balcarce, and is "herido de bala en un glúteo". en.wikipedia's "shot in the back" and its "near Salta" are uncited loose renderings.

**[A, place corrected]** He is carried to a hacienda "a dos leguas al sudeste de la ciudad", lingers ten days, and dies at the **Cañada de la Horqueta** on 17 June 1821, aged 36. **He did not die at La Chamical:** that chapel is the burial place afterward. The afterlife pool's -24.85, -65.42 lies southwest and contradicts "sudeste"; **-24.85, -65.3** is used. **[R]** The hemophilia speculation is unconfirmed, and es.wikipedia's footnote notes no male relative of the Güemes women was hemophiliac. On his deathbed he refuses a royalist surgeon and evacuation in exchange for ending hostilities, demanding of his officers "ningún tipo de trato para beneficiar al enemigo en suelo patrio": a fragment embedded in article prose, not an independent document. **[R]** His forces retake Salta for good on 22 July 1821.

## 7. Afterlife

**[R, downgraded from the pool's [A]]** The Buenos Aires reception. The pool asserted "open hostility" from the newspapers; es.wikipedia says the opposite of the general reaction, "la noticia de su muerte no causó mayor impacto", then attributes "Murió el abominable Güemes. Ya tenemos un cacique menos" to a single unnamed newspaper with no citation. Singular paper, indifference dominant, quotation uncorroborated.

**[A, upgraded from the geography pool's "tradition"]** Burial and transfers: Capilla del Chamical, then the cathedral, the Cementerio de la Santa Cruz, and finally the Panteón de las Glorias del Norte inside the Catedral Basílica de Salta, beside Carmen Puch and among the honored dead of the Army of the North with Arenales and Rudecindo Alvarado. es.wikipedia's Güemes article carries the category "Exhumados del cementerio de la Santa Cruz de Salta", and its Panteón article documents the transfers. **Gap:** the dates of each move are unestablished, so the journey file's date is an explicit placeholder. **[R]** No forensic exhumation comparable to those done for San Martín or Belgrano is documented: a flagged gap, not a confirmed absence.

**[A]** Historiography: a rural caudillo for most of the nineteenth century, rehabilitated in the early twentieth by Bernardo Frías, with Cornejo and Bidondo deepening the documentation. Antonio Alice paints *La muerte de Güemes* in 1910. The Gauchos de Güemes are disbanded at his death, and since 1970 the 5th Cavalry Regiment has worn the militia's poncho.

**[A, coordinate corrected]** The Monumento a Güemes at the foot of Cerro San Bernardo, Av. Uruguay 4400: 1920-1931, rock quarried on the hill in blocks over 75 kg, bronzes by the Arsenal Esteban de Luca, 25 metres, sculptor Víctor Juan Garino, architect Andrés Iñigo, inaugurated 20 February 1931 with President Uriburu present. es.wikipedia specifies the site as where he **first fell from his horse while already wounded**, not where he was shot. Coordinate corrected from -24.7802, -65.4003 to **-24.78675, -65.39929**.

**[A]** Law 25.172 makes 17 June the National Day of Latin American Liberty; Law 26.125 declares him a National Hero; Law 27.258 makes the day a national holiday, kept in Salta from 2014 and nationwide from 2016. *Guemesia ochoai*, a Cretaceous abelisaurid from Salta, is named for him in 2022. **[R, downgraded]** Tarija adopting the date by decree in 2019: es.wikipedia carries the sentence with a citation-needed tag and calls the instrument a "decreto supremo", a national rather than departmental act.

## 8. Interlocks with the existing atlas

- **`san_martin.journey.json`**: Yatasto, La Ciudadela (the frontier delegated), Saldán (the continental plan). Named in three campas.
- **`belgrano.journey.json`**: Yatasto, 30 January 1814; the 1813-14 retreat down the quebradas screened by Güemes's gauchos; the second command from August 1816 and the 1816-1819 correspondence. Named in five campas.
- **`juana_azurduy.journey.json`**: joins c. April 1817 via friar Salazar, rides with him to 1821; her file dates the end of her military career to his death. Named in two campas.
- **`maria_remedios_del_valle.journey.json`**: joins the gauchos c. August 1814, serves under Arenales, and the 1827 Videla certification names Güemes. Named in one campa.
- **`julio_argentino_roca.journey.json`**: thematic only. Roca was born in 1843. No stop, no campa mention.

**Canonical pins.** Buenos Aires at **-34.6037, -58.3816** is inherited byte-identical, in two stops, and is genuine Güemes geography. The Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), the Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354) and Paris (48.8566, 2.3522) were supplied as cross-atlas anchors but have no connection to a man who never left South America, so **they are deliberately not placed in the journey file**: inventing stops at them would be fabricated geography. The contrast belongs in prose instead: San Martín and Belgrano both spent formative years in Europe, while Güemes's whole life runs between Salta, Buenos Aires, and Upper Peru.

## 9. Sources

**Reachable and used.** Spanish Wikipedia, "Martín Miguel de Güemes", is the spine of the chronology and the source of most corrections to the English account. English Wikipedia, same title, is used but is the origin of several errors corrected above (16 April 1817; "shot in the back"; February 1820 under De la Serna; Humahuaca credited to Güemes; Aráoz at Rincón de Marlopa); where the two conflict the Spanish article is preferred, because at these points it cites its sources and the English one does not. Sixteen further Spanish Wikipedia articles on the individual battles, towns and monuments carry the point corrections and are cited at the place of use above and in each journey stop's `sources`. Also English Wikipedia, "Battle of Huaqui", and es.wikiquote for the "doctores" misattribution, plus the atlas files named in section 8.

**Named but not reached.** Atilio Cornejo, *Historia de Güemes* (1971), cited at second hand for the *Justine* (p. 46); Bernardo Frías, *Historia del general Martín Güemes y de la provincia de Salta*; Emilio Bidondo on the Guerra Gaucha. **Luis Güemes (comp.), *Güemes documentado*, 6+ vols. (Plus Ultra, 1980), the edition that would settle most of the [R] quotations, reached only through the wikiquote citation of vol. 6, p. 156: the largest gap in this pass.** elhistoriador.com.ar-style compilations, source of three unverified quotations, unreliable for attribution; elcaudillodelnorte.blogspot.com, anonymous and uncited, reverses the "doctores" letter and was not followed.

**Open gaps.** Unidentified birth house; no Colegio register; no cathedral record of the 1815 wedding; Chamical and the Horqueta unfixed to modern coordinates; undated transfers of the remains; no known forensic examination. Five circulating quotations lack primary documents: one misattributed and dropped, two unused, two flagged where used. The Battle of Salta presence is left contradicted, and the Yatasto date differs by one day between two atlas files.
