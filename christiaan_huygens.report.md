# Christiaan Huygens (1629-1695): research report

*Slug `christiaan_huygens`. Compiled 2026-08-29 from the verified research pool, adversarial corrections folded in. Journey file: `christiaan_huygens.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-only. Shorthands: *Wik*, *Bri*, *Mac* = the Wikipedia, Britannica and MacTutor articles on Huygens; *geo* / *chron* = the pool's geography and chronology lenses; *dossier* = the background dossier, unverified this pass. Where the lenses disagreed, both readings are printed; nothing is silently resolved.

## 0. Corrections applied to the source record

- **Paris Observatory.** The earlier **48.8051 / 2.2334** is the **Meudon** site founded 1876, **8 km** from the right building, an error both the English and French Wikipedia infoboxes carry. The **Perrault building** is at **48.8364 / 2.3372 [A Wikipedia "Paris meridian", defined by that building's median plane]**.
- **Academiegebouw, Leiden.** 52.1592 / 4.4878 to **52.1569 / 4.4856 [A nl.wikipedia "Academiegebouw (Leiden)"]**. The file keeps 52.1592 / 4.4878 for the Leiden *press* stops, a different and city-level siting **[R]**.
- **Grote Kerk, The Hague.** The afterlife lens gave 52.0793 / 4.3115, *geo* 52.0772 / 4.3123; the church's article gives **52.07722 / 4.30722 [A]**. **Hofwijck**, 52.0667 / 4.3667 to *geo*'s **52.0650 / 4.36194 [A]**, so the lenses now agree. **Bibliothèque du Roi**, 48.8683 / 2.3405 to about **48.8680 / 2.3376 [A]**. **Breda**: no reachable source puts the Orange College inside the castle, so the stop is at **city level, 51.5833 / 4.7667 [R]**, not 51.5906 / 4.7757.
- **Title.** The collision tract is ***De motu corporum ex percussione***, not "De motu corporum ex mutuo impulsu" as *chron* had it **[A Wik]**. **Tag:** the 1689 Hampton Court audience was mis-tagged [A] in the source record; it is dossier-only and **[R]** in the file.
- **Quotation citations.** Seven verified quotations are placed as `quote` fields, text and locus in the journey file. Wikiquote wrongly attributes the two Newton letters to the *Oeuvres complètes*; the Fatio letter's English is **René Dugas, *Mechanics in the Seventeenth Century* (1958), p. 440**, cited so in the file **[A: both letters confirmed against *Oeuvres complètes* IX letter 2473 and X p. 354 letter 2777]**.
- **Two quotations deliberately not used, their circulating citations being wrong.** "I believe that we do not know anything for certain, but everything probably" traces to a real primary (*Oeuvres complètes* VII, p. 298, letter 1944, to Perrault, 1673) **but the published English inverts the negation**. "I had not thought of this regular decrease of gravity..." carries a **wrong date**, being printed in his lifetime in the Addition to the *Discours de la cause de la pesanteur* (Leiden, 1690), pp. 160-161, not an unlocated 1691 remark; it is **paraphrased** in the Fatio campa.

## 1. The house on the Plein, 1629-1645

- Born **14 April 1629 at The Hague**, second son of Constantijn Huygens Sr. and Suzanna van Baerle **[A Wik, Bri]**. Birth house placed near the **Plein [R geo]**: the street address is unconfirmed, so the pin 52.08 / 4.3133 is the quarter, not a doorway.
- Constantijn Sr. is secretary to the Princes of Orange, poet, composer, correspondent of Descartes **[A Wikipedia "Constantijn Huygens"; corroborated inside `rene_descartes.journey.json`, which cites "Descartes's correspondence with Constantijn Huygens on his late mornings"]**. Christiaan is educated at home to sixteen, never at a Latin school **[R: standard in the biographies, not separately fetched]**.
- **Suzanna van Baerle dies when Christiaan is eight.** The fact is attested; the year **1637** is reconstruction-supported only, from an Oeuvres-derived family chronology, not a fetched register **[A fact / R 1637-05-10]**.
- *Gap:* nothing reachable documents the household's daily curriculum; the campa's list is the standard summary, not a document.

## 2. Leiden, Breda, and the Descartes that never happened, 1645-1650

- **Leiden, May 1645 to March 1647**, with Constantijn Jr.; law under Vinnius, mathematics under **Frans van Schooten [A Wik "Enrolled at Leiden University: May 1645 to March 1647"; Bri]**.
- **Breda, from March 1647**, Orange College, **under John Pell [A Wik]**; the earlier phrasing "a milieu that also includes John Pell" understated a direct teacher relation. Studies end **August 1649 [A Wik]**, around the duel his brother Lodewijk fought **[R dossier]**.
- **Danish mission, 1649**: Bentheim, Flensburg, Copenhagen, Helsingør **[A Bri "1649: Diplomatic mission to Denmark"; Wik for the itinerary]**.
- **Contradiction, printed not resolved.** *Chron* says he hoped to reach Descartes at Stockholm "but Descartes died before he could do that", and Descartes died **11 February 1650**, which puts the mission into 1650 and makes the failure a death, not a decision; *geo* dates both Danish stops 1649 and has him home by year's end. The file dates Helsingør 1650-01-01 as an ordering device and prints the disagreement.

## 3. Mathematics without a post, 1651-1659

- **1651**, first book, quadrature of hyperbola, ellipse and circle, against **Grégoire de Saint-Vincent**; **1654**, *De circuli magnitudine inventa* **[A Bri "1651, 1654: Early mathematical publications on circle measurement"]**. Elzevier imprint **[R: title page not seen]**.
- **25 March 1655**, first satellite of Saturn, from lenses ground in his own workshop; he calls it **Luna Saturni [A Wik "Identified Titan: 25 March 1655"; Bri]**. The name **Titan** is nineteenth-century, from John Herschel **[A geo]**.
- **1655**, first Paris journey, into the Gassendi and Boulliau circles; learns of the Pascal-Fermat correspondence on chance **[A Bri]**. Boulliau's urging to publish **[R geo]**. **September 1655, doctorate in law at Angers [R]**: the journey is attested, the degree and date are dossier chronology, the pin city-centre.
- **25 December 1656, the pendulum clock [A Wikipedia "Pendulum clock", which supports the exact date]**; manufacture contracted to **Salomon Coster** (c. 1620-1659), described in *Horologium* (1658) **[A]**. **Privilege to Coster, 16 June 1657 [R]**: the fetched sources give the year alone.
- **1657**, *De ratiociniis in ludo aleae*, inside Van Schooten's *Exercitationum mathematicarum* **[A Bri]**; that he reconstructed the result without seeing the Pascal-Fermat letters is standard in the literature, carried as **[R]**.
- **1659**, *Systema Saturnium*: the ring thin, flat, detached, inclined **[A Wik; Bri]**. Same year, the law of **centrifugal force [A Wik]**, published only posthumously as *De vi centrifuga*, 1703.

## 4. Montmor, Gresham, and the odd sympathy, 1660-1665

- **October 1660**, the **Montmor** private academy at Paris **[A Bri]**; the roster of Roberval, Auzout, Carcavi, Sorbière **[R geo]**. **5 December 1660**, meets **Blaise Pascal**; the correspondence records experiments, mechanics and telescopes, not a sentimental encounter **[A geo, from Huygens's own letters]**.
- **1661**, London, Gresham College, meeting Moray, Boyle, Wallis and the pre-charter Royal Society circle **[A Bri]**; the individual scenes **[R geo]**. The pin is the original Bishopsgate site, now Tower 42 **[A Wikipedia "Gresham College"]**.
- **1663, elected FRS, first foreign member, aged 34 [A Wik]**. **Correction, and it matters:** the day **22 June 1663** in the earlier record is **unsupported**, since the Royal Society catalogue record and Wikipedia's list of fellows elected in 1663 both give the year alone and the claim's own cited source gives no day. It looks invented in transcription; the file dates the stop 1663-01-01 and says so.
- **February 1665, the "odd sympathy" of two coupled pendulum clocks [A for observation and month]**.
- **Contradiction between the pool's own lenses, adjudicated for geography.** *Chron* placed this in **Paris**, *geo* in **The Hague**. The Hague is right: Huygens did not move to Paris until 1666 (Wik, Bri and Mac agree), and the letters went *to* France because his father was there on the Orange mission until 1665 **[A Wikipedia "Constantijn Huygens"]**. The letters were not re-verified **[R as documents]**.

## 5. The Paris academy years, 1666-1681

- **1666**, recruited by **Colbert** to the **Académie royale des sciences**, lodged with the **Bibliothèque du Roi [A Wik, Bri]**; the 6,000-livre salary and its rank **[R dossier]**. **1667**, attends dissections, including of executed bodies, out of interest in the eye and with an initial reaction of disgust **[R dossier]**.
- **1668**, memoir on elastic collision to the Royal Society alongside Wren and Wallis **[A Mac]**; the tract, retitled per section 0, printed only posthumously in *Opuscula postuma*, 1703 **[A Wik]**. **1670**, serious illness, return to Holland **[A Wik, Bri]**; the arrangement preferring the Royal Society as repository **[R dossier]**. **1671** return; **1672** Louis XIV invades the Republic and Huygens stays in French service **[A for the war; R for the exact 1671 return]**.
- **1672**, **Leibniz** arrives and Huygens takes him in hand **[A: `gottfried_wilhelm_leibniz.journey.json`'s 1673-06-01 Paris stop names Huygens as mentor]**; the reading list and the triangular-numbers problem **[R]**. At the **Paris Observatory**: Cassini, Picard, Rømer, whose 1676 timing of Jupiter's satellites gives the finite speed of light Huygens accepts and calculates from **[A Wikipedia "Paris Observatory" for the 1671 completion]**. Pin badly wrong in the source record; see section 0.
- **1673**, *Horologium Oscillatorium*, dedicated to **Louis XIV**: tautochronous cycloid, cycloidal cheeks, compound pendulum, evolutes and involutes **[A Wik, Bri; R at the Muguet imprint level]**. **1674**, **Denis Papin** as assistant, gunpowder-and-atmosphere piston engine **[R dossier]**. **February 1675**, balance-spring watch by **Isaac Thuret**, with **Robert Hooke** asserting prior invention **[A Wik for the 1675 notice; R dossier for Thuret and the quarrel]**.
- **1676**, illness forces him home, return 1678, **Iceland spar** work during recovery **[A Bri; A geo]**. **1679**, wave theory to the Academy **[A at the level of "late 1670s"]**. **September 1681**, final departure to The Hague **[A Wik, Bri]**; **Colbert's 1683 death** and the **1685 revocation of the Edict of Nantes** are a partial, not sole, cause **[R for the causal claim; A for the revocation, dated 18 October 1685 at Fontainebleau in `louis_xiv.journey.json`]**.
- **Contradiction: the date of the *Traité de la lumière*.** Bri dates it **1678**; the bibliographic record puts the printing at **Leiden in 1690**. The preface says he "communicated it in the year 1678 to the learned persons who then composed the Royal Academy of Science" and is itself dated 8 January 1690. Economically that is 1678 reading, 1690 printing, but it is **not silently resolved**: the file carries both stops, each stating the conflict in `date_confidence`.

## 6. Hofwijck and the last decade, 1682-1695

- **1682**, the **planetarium** built to Huygens's gear-ratio design by **Johannes van Ceulen** at Hofwijck **[A geo; Wikipedia "Hofwijck"]**. **1684**, *Astroscopia compendiaria*, the tubeless aerial telescope **[A Wik]**. **1685**, VOC marine-clock sea trial and severe seasickness **[R dossier]**; the **place is a reconstruction** too, since no reachable source names **Texel** as the roadstead.
- **1687**, Constantijn Huygens Sr. dies, estate divided, Christiaan becomes **heer van Zeelhem [A Wik, Bri]**. *Contradiction printed, not resolved:* Wik gives the father's age as **ninety**, Bri **ninety-one**, and the campa says both. The 28,000-guilder quarter share **[R dossier]**.
- **1687-1689**, reads Newton's *Principia* with **Nicolas Fatio de Duillier**, accepts the inverse square, rejects unexplained attraction **[A from his own letters; R dossier for Fatio's assistance]**. **12 June 1689**, meets **Isaac Newton** in person in London **[A Wik]**; the presence of **Edmond Halley** and Fatio **[R dossier]**, and the meeting with **John Locke** at **Lady Mordaunt's house [R dossier]**, whose coordinates could not be located. **10 July 1689**, Cambridge, King's College, over a vacancy Fatio sought **[R dossier only]**. **1689**, Hampton Court, audience with **William III** and **Bentinck [R, retagged per section 0]**. **End of August 1689**, return home **[R: unconfirmed]**.
- **1690**, *Traité de la lumière* printed at Leiden with the *Discours de la cause de la pesanteur* **[A for the work; contested on date; R for the Van der Aa imprint]**. **1691**, the letter proposing the **31-tone division of the octave [R: not fetched at all this pass]**. ***Cosmotheoros***, addressed to his brother Constantijn and withheld from the press, **published posthumously 1698 [A Bri; R for the composition date, set at 1694 to order the segment]**.
- **23 March 1695**, testament executed while ill but certified competent by a notary **[R: not verified against the original]**. **7 July 1695**, the minister **Olivier** at the bedside and Huygens's refusal to be moved **[R: family tradition, from a letter reported by Constantijn Jr. that could not be located]**. **8 July 1695**, dies at The Hague aged sixty-six **[A Wik, Bri, in agreement]**; no modern cause of death can be established, *stated as a gap*.
- Buried in the **Grote of Sint-Jacobskerk**, in an **unmarked grave in the choir**, as his father was, with an **1857 commemorative stone** under one of the windows **[A Wikipedia "Grote Kerk, The Hague"]**. **Contradiction, unresolved and stated in the file: the burial day.** Some tables give **17 July 1695**; a study of the Hague burial register gives **14 July**, recording a grave opened for "the lord of Zelem/Zeelhem" with fourteen periods of bell-ringing. The register itself was not reached.

## 7. Interlocks: who is named, and why

Another traveler is named in a campa **only** where Huygens himself has a direct or indirect relation to that person. Shared geography alone was rejected.

- **`rene_descartes`** His father's friend and correspondent, a guest at the family table, the mathematics he was raised on; the 1649-50 Danish mission was undertaken partly to reach him and failed because he died. **[A]** for the friendship and the Stockholm leg; **[R]** for direct mentorship.
- **`gottfried_wilhelm_leibniz`** Direct mentor-pupil relation, Paris 1672-1676. **[A]**, corroborated on both files. Strongest link in the corpus.
- **`newton`** Read the *Principia*, wrote about it repeatedly, met him 12 June 1689 and argued with him. **[A]**.
- **`john_locke`** Met socially in London, 1689, at Lady Mordaunt's. **[R]**, coordinates unlocatable; named inside the London stop, not staged at an invented pin.
- **`louis_xiv`** Recruited and salaried into the King's academy by Colbert, lodged in the King's library, dedicated the *Horologium Oscillatorium* to him. **[A]** for recruitment and salary; **[R]** at the imprint level for the dedication.
- **`edmond_halley`** In the same company as Huygens and Newton in London, summer 1689. **[R]** dossier only. Named once, and **not** for the Paris Observatory overlap, which is shared geography and nothing more.

**Considered and rejected.** **Spinoza**, reached only through Leibniz's November 1676 visit, which is Leibniz's stop on Leibniz's file. **Leeuwenhoek**, a real documented relation but with no journey file, and neither the Leibniz nor the Locke Delft visits are Huygens's, so no Delft stop was written. **Cassini, Hooke, Papin, Rømer, Thuret**, named freely as historical persons, none with a file.

## 8. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Christiaan Huygens is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`**, so no apparatus stops are licensed and none were written. Per the standing rule the material below is **not silently deleted**. Everything caught runs **forward**, the direction `EXCEPTIONS.md` does not license without the author's say-so: artifacts postdating his death, unembeddable in a file whose clock stops in 1695.

Caught and left out: the **Huygens probe** (Cassini-Huygens), landing on Titan 14 January 2005 near Adiri **[A Wikipedia "Huygens (probe)"]**, with the arc discovery-1655 to Herschel-names-Titan to probe-lands-2005, where the pool's caution holds, since the arc misleads readers into thinking Huygens named Titan; **Huygens crater**, Mars, 467 km, **13.9 S, 55.6 E [A Wikipedia "Huygens (crater)"]**, the earlier record's 450 km and 16.2 S / 54.5 E both being wrong; **asteroid 2801 Huygens [R]**; the **EGU Christiaan Huygens Medal [R egu.eu]**; the **Huygens-Fresnel principle [A: standard history of optics]**, of which the 1676 Iceland spar campa says only that the ellipsoidal wavelet "turns out to be right"; and **failed plans for a public memorial in The Hague [R]**, whose stop already carries the 1857 stone.

Two items are apparatus rather than scene and fall under the standing rule instead. The ***Oeuvres complètes*** **[R: Wikipedia and dbnl.org 404 or incomplete]** appears in `sources` on two stops as the edition through which the letters are cited. **Rijksmuseum Boerhaave**, holding the signed lenses **[R]**, and **Huygens Museum Hofwijck [A huygensmuseum.nl]** are later custodians of objects Huygens made or lived in, so each is a `suggested_ref` on his own stop for the object, never a stop of its own.

**Nothing was caught backward.** Huygens has no apparatus of the kind the exception contemplates: the wave construction and the rules of impact are physics with a claim on nature, not a reading method turnable onto past events. If the operator wants the Titan-probe arc it needs a forward grant of the Burroughs or Derrida type, which under the scope rule would be a sentence or two closing the Luna Saturni stop.

## 9. Honest gaps

Unresolved contradictions: the **day of the FRS election** (1663 only, "22 June" unsupported, needs the Journal Book); the **day of burial** (14 vs 17 July 1695, needs the register); the **date of the *Traité*** (1678 vs 1690); the **father's age at death** (90 vs 91); **whether the Danish mission ran into 1650**.

Missing outright: a document naming **Texel** as the VOC roadstead; the **31-tone letter of 1691**; any **medical cause of death**; the **birth house's street address**.

Dossier-only and unverified, each tagged in its stop's `date_confidence`: the **Angers doctorate**, the **1667 dissections**, the **Papin engine**, the **1689 Cambridge and Hampton Court episodes**, the **Locke meeting**, the **testament**, the **minister Olivier**.
