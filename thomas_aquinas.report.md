# Thomas Aquinas (Tommaso d'Aquino), c. 1224/25 - 1274
*Research report for `thomas_aquinas.journey.json`, compiled 2026-08-29 from the verified research pool with the adversarial corrections applied.*

**[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, hagiography, or a claim resting on one hedged source. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; where the pool's verifier issued a CORRECTION it is followed and said so.

---

## 1. Birth and the Benedictine placement (c. 1225 - 1239)

- Born at Roccasecca to Landulf of Aquino and Theodora **[A - SEP; New Advent]**, in a castle built by Monte Cassino in 994 **[A - Wikipedia]**. Coordinate 41.550 / 13.667 is the **town center**; the castle's own point is in no fetched source. **Gap.**
- **Year contested, not resolved.** New Advent: 1225 *or* 1227. SEP: "around 1225". Critical scholarship: c. 1224/25. No day or month survives; file uses `1225-01-01`.
- Child oblate at Monte Cassino from about five. **[A - New Advent: "At the age of five... sent to receive his first training from the Benedictine monks"]** IEP c. 1229-1239, SEP c. 1231-1239: **two-year disagreement, not adjudicated.** The child asking "What is God?" is **[R]**, canonization-era characterization, written into the campa *as* hagiography.
- **1239. CORRECTED.** The pool asserted Frederick II expelled suspect monks and Thomas left in consequence. Its cited source, Wikipedia "Abbey of Montecassino", says only that in 1239 Frederick II "garrisoned troops in it during his war with the Papacy", and never mentions Aquinas. Garrisoning **[A]**; expulsion-and-consequence **[R]**, Torrell-line.

## 2. Naples, the habit, the custody (1239 - 1245)

- Arts at Frederick II's Naples studium from autumn 1239, reading the newly available Aristotle **[A - IEP]**; masters named as Peter of Ireland and a Master Martin **[A - Wikipedia]**, which also says the medieval site is only "hypothesized" to lie in the Nido district. 40.8508 / 14.2567 approximates the district, not a campus. **Flagged.**
- Dominican habit at Naples. **[A - IEP: "received the Dominican habit in April of 1244"]** **CONTRADICTION, unresolved:** New Advent puts the entry anywhere between 1240 and August 1243. Both stated in the file; neither chosen.
- Intercepted by his brothers near Acquapendente. **[A - New Advent]** **Coordinate CORRECTED:** the pool's 42.7378 / 11.8508 is ~1.3 km off; Wikipedia gives **42.74389 / 11.86444**. The modern comune is *Acquapendente*; "Aquapendente" is New Advent's 1913 form. The exact point on the road is **lost. Gap.**
- Confinement at Monte San Giovanni Campano, then Roccasecca **[A as to detention]**. **CONTRADICTION:** New Advent "nearly two years", SEP 1244-1245, about one. Not adjudicated.
- The temptation episode (woman sent in, driven out with a firebrand) is **[R]**, canonization-era testimony, though Italian Wikipedia's castle article repeats it as established. The sisters won over and the window escape are **[R]**, New Advent's 1913 account. Both campas state the provenance and give the plainer reading alongside. Release **[A - SEP: "his parents yielded and Thomas went to Paris"]**.

## 3. Paris and Cologne under Albert (1245 - 1252)

- To Paris, 1245-1248 **[A - SEP]**. **Coordinate CORRECTED:** the pool's 48.8412 / 2.3417 is ~670 m too far south; French Wikipedia's Couvent Saint-Jacques article gives **48.8472 / 2.3433**, at 158 rue Saint-Jacques, and lists both Thomas and Albert among its teachers.
- To Cologne under Albertus Magnus **[A - SEP: "sent to Cologne, in 1248, under the supervision of Albert the Great"]**. **Coordinate inherited:** the geography lens gave only a city-center point (50.9364 / 6.9528), the Kolner Dominikanerkloster page having 404'd, so the canonical studium-quarter pin **50.9407 / 6.9583** is used.
- **Ordination: DOWNGRADED [A] to [R]**, per the pool's own correction. New Advent alone gives a date and a hand, hedging its own wording: "Probably in 1250, he was raised to the priesthood by Conrad of Hochstaden". Neither SEP nor IEP gives any date or officiant; the file reads "probably 1250-51, officiant not securely recorded". The "dumb ox" nickname and Albert's retort are **[R]**, carried as "is said to have".

## 4. The first Paris regency (1252 - 1259)

- Bachelor of the Bible from 1252 **[A - IEP]**; New Advent gives 1251 or 1252. **Minor contradiction, flagged.** Lectured on Lombard's Sentences, the *Scriptum super libros Sententiarum* **[A - IEP]**; the years of the individual books are **not fixed**. **Gap.**
- *Contra impugnantes Dei cultum et religionem*, against William of Saint-Amour. **[A - IEP and SEP both date the mendicant controversy 1256]**
- Master of Theology. **[A that it happened; DATE contradicted.]** IEP: Master in 1256 at thirty-two. SEP: chair from 1256. New Advent: promotion **23 October 1257**. The file uses New Advent's date and leaves the disagreement open. Regency 1256-1259 **[A - SEP]**; the *De veritate* disputations sit inside it, but **no source consulted dates individual questions**, so `1258-01-01` is an ordering device. **Gap.**

## 5. The Italian decade (1259 - 1268)

- Back to the Roman province 1259 **[A - SEP]**; Naples 1259-61 **[A - IEP]**.
- **Anagni: MISDATED IN THE POOL, CORRECTED.** The pool put an Anagni stop at 1260-61 ("appointed general preacher... present in the papal curia town"), sourced to Wikipedia "Anagni", which contains nothing of the kind. That article and "Santa Sabina" state instead: **"In 1265 a provincial Chapter at Anagni... assigned Thomas Aquinas as regent master"** at Santa Sabina. IEP's sequence is Naples 1259-61, Orvieto 1261-65, Rome 1265-68. The file **redates the stop to 1265**; the Weisheipl-line pre-Orvieto stay is recorded here as **[R]**, unsupported, rather than vanishing.
- Orvieto 1261-1265 at Urban IV's curia: *Summa contra Gentiles* largely finished, *Contra errores Graecorum*, *Catena aurea*. **[A - IEP; New Advent]** **Proxy building rejected:** the pool used Palazzo Soliano (42.7164 / 12.1138), but its own cited Italian Wikipedia article calls that palace *trecentesco*, built from 1297, so it **did not exist under Urban IV**. The coordinate stays as a quarter marker; the file **claims no building**.
- **Corpus Christi 1264: claim SPLIT.** Urban IV extending the feast by *Transiturus de hoc mundo* is **[A - Wikipedia]**, and Thomas's authorship of the office has wide scholarly agreement (BNF lat. 1143) and is carried **[A]**. A papal **commission to him personally** is **[R]**, from Tocco. The campa marks the difference.
- Santa Sabina 1265-1268: new studium, *Summa theologiae* begun there. **[A - IEP; Wikipedia "Santa Sabina"]** Viterbo near Clement IV's court, with William of Moerbeke **[A - IEP; New Advent]**, coordinate 42.4157 / 12.101. **A papal translation project commissioned for Thomas is NOT proven:** the interlock lens flagged it unverifiable and Moerbeke has no journey file. Only contact and *use* of the versions is treated as attested.
- **Uncorroborated episode kept visible:** New Advent alone reports Clement IV offering him the archbishopric of Naples, declined in tears, and the dossier does not mention it at all. **[R, single 1913 source.] Given no stop**, recorded here so the operator sees it was seen.

## 6. The second Paris regency (1268 - 1272), and the hard sayings

- Ordered back for an unusual second term **[A - SEP]**; arrival given variously as late 1268 or early 1269. **Flagged.**
- *De unitate intellectus contra Averroistas*, against Siger of Brabant: **[R as to the year 1270]**, IEP and SEP placing it in the regency and the critical catalogues dating it 1270, no month fixed. *De aeternitate mundi contra murmurantes* **[A as to work and argument]**, c. 1271 by standard dating within 1268-1272, no finer. Summa, disputed questions, quodlibets, and Aristotle commentaries continue **[A - IEP]**.

The hard sayings are in the file in his own voice with verbatim quotes attached, because they are his:

- Heresy: corrupting the faith is graver than forging money, and convicted heretics are "to be not only excommunicated but even put to death". **[A - ST II-II Q.11 A.3, Respondeo, Dominican Fathers translation]**
- Killing for the common good, the rotten-limb analogy. **[A - ST II-II Q.64 A.2, Respondeo]** Secondary literature often attributes this to *Summa contra Gentiles* III.146; the wording used is confirmed in the *Summa Theologiae*.
- Women: "As regards the individual nature, woman is defective and misbegotten..." **[A as to wording; CITATION CORRECTED]** The pool cited ST I Q.92 A.1 *Respondeo*; it is the **reply to the first objection (ad 1)**. The substance survives: it is his own reply, not a quoted opponent, and the qualification (not misbegotten "as regards human nature in general") stands **in that same reply**. The campa gives it its actual weight, one clause.

## 7. Naples 1272-73, the road, and death

- Naples 1272, founding and directing a studium generale. **[A - IEP; New Advent: "In 1272 St. Thomas went to Naples, where the Third Part was written"]** That the chapter at **Florence** let him found it **wherever he chose** is **[R]**; only the year and the school are firm. Vernacular Lenten sermons, Lent 1273 **[A as to the cycle - SEP]**, with a **gap of a specific kind:** only listeners' *reportationes* survive.
- **Crucifix locution: DATE CORRECTED, EPISODES SEPARATED.** The pool dated "You have written well of me, Thomas; what reward will you have?" to 6 December 1273, merging it with the Mass after which he stopped writing. New Advent gives it as **1273 at Naples, after he finished the treatise on the Eucharist**; Wikipedia likewise, in the chapel of Saint Nicholas (*Bene scripsisti de me, Thoma* / *Non nisi te, Domine*). **Two different events.** The file keeps them apart and names the merging in `date_confidence`. Tag stays **[R]**.
- 6 December 1273: he ceases dictating; the Summa breaks off at ST III Q.90. **[A - New Advent: "On 6 December, 1273, he laid aside his pen and would write no more"]** Sacristan named as Dominic of Caserta **[A - Wikipedia]**. **The nature of the experience is explicitly unresolved in both principal sources**; the file leaves it open. "All that I have written seems like straw to me" is **[R]**, a paraphrase from the Tocco and Reginald tradition, **no primary edition of which was fetched**. A crucifix audibly speaking on 6 December is a **separate accretion**, not treated as attested.
- Summoned by Gregory X to Lyon; set out. **[A - IEP]** The **departure date is nowhere given**; `1274-01-15` is inferred from a May opening and a 7 March death. **Gap.**
- **Terracina and Maenza are NOT rival traditions. CORRECTED.** The pool carried them as a "direct contradiction", misreading New Advent, which gives them **in sequence**: he "fell to the ground near Terracina" and was then "conducted to the Castle of Maienza". One itinerary. **Terracina coordinate CORRECTED** from 41.2907 / 13.244 to **41.283 / 13.250**.
- **Maenza: the relative CORRECTED. Not his sister.** Italian Wikipedia has him in "la stanza del castello che apparteneva alla nipote Francesca d'Aquino", his **niece**, the castle belonging to the Conti di Ceccano; Torrell agrees. The dossier's "sister Theodora" is wrong and the campa says so. Coordinate 41.5167 / 13.1833 confirmed. The fresh-herring miracle and the stated motive for moving on stay **[R]**.
- Died at Fossanova, 7 March 1274, aged about 48 or 49. **[A - SEP, IEP, and New Advent independently]** **Coordinate CORRECTED** from the afterlife lens's 41.4306 / 13.2825 (~8 km east) to **41.4381 / 13.1958**. The psalm verse at the gate is attested **as reported**; the Song of Songs exposition is **[R]** with **no surviving text**, stated in the campa as a gap.

**A stop deliberately NOT written: Lyon.** The geography lens proposed a Lyon pin (45.7675 / 4.835) for "the journey's intended endpoint". **It is not in the file:** nobody is staged at a place they never went, and a pin is such a staging however the prose hedges. The summons and the council opening without him sit inside the Naples departure campa instead. Flagged so the operator can overrule.

## 8. Afterlife: the bones, the condemnations, the doctorate

- Buried at Fossanova **[A - New Advent, SEP]**. The boiling of the body and the refusal to surrender it are **[R]**, New Advent alone.
- **1277 Paris.** Tempier condemns 219 propositions; the syllabus **names no author**; modern scholarship disputes any simple claim that Thomas was condemned. **[A - Wikipedia]** The coincidence with the third anniversary of his death is **[R]**, **not re-verified against a primary edition**.
- **1277 Oxford.** Kilwardby prohibits **thirty** theses (corrected upward from the pool's vaguer "a set"), including the unity of substantial form **[A - Wikipedia "Robert Kilwardby"]**, which also flags that Hissette and others **dispute that Kilwardby was targeting Aquinas at all**. The **18 March date appears in no source fetched**, so **[R]**. Folded into the Paris campa, not given an Oxford pin: Aquinas never went there.
- **1323 Avignon.** Canonized by John XXII, 18 July, after inquiries at Naples (1319) and Fossanova (1321). **[A - New Advent, SEP]** The "tot miraculis, quot articulis" retort is **[R]**. **Coordinate note corrected:** the pool's anachronism warning is right in substance but wrong twice. Wikipedia dates the palace groundbreaking to 1252 with Benedict XII's campaign from **1334** (not 1335), and has John XXII lodging **as a guest of the Dominicans**.
- **1369 Toulouse.** Bones translated to the Jacobins, 28 January 1369, by Urban V (decree 1368, arrival 1369). **[A - New Advent, English Wikipedia]** That day becomes his feast in the 1969 reform. **Coordinate:** 43.6039 / 1.4403 (French Wikipedia) vs 43.6039 / 1.4400 (English), **~25 m apart, not adjudicated**; the file uses 1.4403.
- **1567 Rome.** Doctor of the Church: **"15 April 1567 with the Papal bull Mirabilis Deus"** **[A - Wikipedia]**, a detail the pool's bare year lacked. The 1570 Piana edition cited alongside was **not confirmed** and is kept out of the campa.
- **1878.** The 1628 Jacobins shrine destroyed in the Revolution, relics to Saint-Sernin, a gold-and-silver sarcophagus blessed by Cardinal Desprez 24 July 1878. **[A - New Advent]** **Coordinate corrected** 43.6094 / 1.4425 to **43.6084 / 1.4420**.
- **1879 Rome.** *Aeterni Patris*, 4 August 1879 **[A - vatican.va]**. The encyclical **does not name the Leonine edition**; the Commission's 1879 founding is documented separately and stated as a separate fact. **1880**, patron of Catholic schools: **[R, unverified]**, given no stop.
- **1974 Toulouse.** Relics returned for the seventh centenary, under an altar by Yves Boiret. **[A]** The file's 28 January is his feast, used to order the stop: **no source gives the day.** The **2024** tour of the skull relic through fourteen American parishes, 30,000-40,000 venerating it **[A - dominicanfriars.org]**, is one clause in the same campa: the same body still travelling.
- **Competing skull relics, Toulouse and Priverno** **[R for Priverno - Wikipedia]**. Both cannot be genuine; **not adjudicated**. **Arm relics** at Naples Cathedral and Santa Maria sopra Minerva are **[A within New Advent, but UNDATED]**; the fetched Minerva article confirms neither which relic nor its location. No stop, a suggested_ref. **Gap.** The **2024 World Neurosurgery** subdural-haematoma paper is **[R - NOT verified: PubMed 37979685 gave only a cookie-consent screen]**.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Thomas Aquinas is NOT listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** Nothing has been silently deleted. Every relation not rooted in his own life is listed with its direction named, and **all are FORWARD**, which an agent cannot license, so none is written into a campa.

- **Giordano Bruno**, 1565 novitiate at San Domenico Maggiore, "once the school of Aquinas himself", and 1576 shelter at Santa Maria sopra Minerva: Bruno's stops, on Bruno's file. **Frances Yates**, naming Aquinas in the mnemonic chain from the *Ad Herennium* to Camillo, Bruno, Fludd: hers.
- **Ramon Llull**, Paris against Latin Averroism 1298-1311: shared geography only, and his file never names Aquinas. **Jacobus de Voragine**, shared master general John of Vercelli: contemporary, but shared order structure is not a relation. **Pico**, Paris 1485, and **Sor Juana**, *Primero sueno* "through scholastic philosophy" with no name attached: the pool called both thin. **Nicholas of Cusa**, Cologne studium inheritance: only his *coordinate* is inherited, which is a pin, not a name-drop.
- **Dante**, *Paradiso* X-XIII and *Purgatorio* XX.67-69 (the Charles of Anjou poisoning allegation, **[R]**, unsupported by contemporary evidence), and **Roberto Busa**, *Index Thomisticus* from 1946 at Gallarate. Both **cited, not staged**: `suggested_refs` on the Fossanova and Aeterni Patris stops, which is a citation, not a pointer at another atlas file.

## Interlocks named, and coordinate policy

Named only where Aquinas himself has a direct, documented, one-directional relation. **`aristotle`**, in five campas: he commented on the corpus, used Moerbeke's versions, called him the Philosopher, and `aristotle.journey.json` names Aquinas twice. **`maimonides`**, in the Five Ways campa: `maimonides.journey.json` says the *Guide*'s three-part structure "later structur[ed] Aquinas's own engagement with Rabbi Moyses in the Summa". **Considered and not used:** Plato and Augustine of Hippo have files and Aquinas engages both substantively, but **neither appears anywhere in the verified pool**, so naming them would be general knowledge dressed as sourced fact. **The operator may want them added later.**
