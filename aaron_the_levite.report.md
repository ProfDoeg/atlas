# Aaron the Levite: research report

*2026-08-14. Aaron ben Amram, of the tribe of Levi; first high priest of Israel; brother of Moses and Miriam. Slug `aaron_the_levite`. Traditional life span 1396-1273 BCE, Seder Olam reckoning.*

**Method.** Aaron is a wholly textual subject: no Egyptian document names him, no inscription mentions him, nothing excavated touches him. The life comes from four books of the Torah, from the commentary built on them (principally Rashi, 11th century, in the Rosenbaum-Silbermann English on Sefaria), and from later reception: Mishnah, Christian typology, Islamic veneration, the shrine on Jabal Harun. The geography is weaker still. The Exodus itinerary is among the most disputed route-reconstructions in biblical studies, and nearly every place-name below is an identification, not a location.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or identification without independent warrant. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

## 0. The two chronologies

The life sits on a chronological fork this report does not resolve.

- **Seder Olam Rabbah** places the Exodus at **1313 BCE**. Exodus 7:7 gives Aaron as 83 and Moses as 80 before Pharaoh **[A]**; Numbers 33:38-39 gives Aaron's death at 123 in the fortieth year **[A]**. Arithmetic from the 1313 anchor yields birth **1396 BCE**, death **1273 BCE** **[R: arithmetic from a traditional anchor, not an attested date]**.
- The **Ramesside / late-date scheme**, favoured by most modern historians who accept any historicity at all, places the events c. **1290-1250 BCE**, on the Pi-Ramesses store-city reference at Exodus 1:11.

The journey file uses the Seder Olam frame throughout, that being the frame the commentary it quotes uses, and the hedge is carried in every `date_confidence` field. **An error to record:** the pool's afterlife lens dated the Rashi plague material to "c. 1275-1290 BCE narrative setting," the rival scheme, while its chronology lens used 1313. Corrected to one frame; the fork is stated, not hidden.

## 1. Birth and family

- Aaron is son of **Amram** and **Jochebed**, Amram's father's sister, of the tribe of Levi **[A: Exodus 6:20; Numbers 26:59]**.
- He is **three years older than Moses** **[A: Exodus 7:7, by subtraction: 83 and 80]**.
- **CORRECTION, load-bearing.** The pool called Aaron the "elder brother of Miriam and (three years later) Moses." **Miriam is the eldest of the three.** In the plain sense of Exodus 2:4-8 she stands watch over the ark of bulrushes and negotiates with Pharaoh's daughter while Moses is an infant **[A]**; rabbinic tradition (Sotah 12a-13a; Seder Olam) puts her birth roughly six years before Aaron's **[R]**. Numbers 26:59 lists "Aaron and Moses, and Miriam their sister" in **rank order, not birth order**. Aaron is elder to Moses only.
- **Birthplace is a gap.** No verse states where Aaron was born. "Goshen" is inference from the settlement of the Israelites in the eastern Delta (Genesis 47:27) **[R]**; the identification of Goshen with any single site is worse than that (see §7).
- Wife: **Elisheba daughter of Amminadab, sister of Nahshon** (Exodus 6:23) **[A]**. Under-used in the journey file.
- Sons: **Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, Ithamar** **[A: Exodus 6:23]**.

## 2. The commission and the plagues

- **Exodus 4:14 [A]**: God, angry with Moses, names Aaron as the one who "speaks readily" and is already setting out to meet him. The hinge of the public role: Aaron is the mouth of a man who will not speak for himself.
- **CORRECTION.** The pool had "God sends Moses to meet Aaron in the wilderness." Inverted. Exodus 4:27: "GOD said to Aaron, 'Go to meet Moses in the wilderness.'" **God addresses Aaron** **[A: Exodus 4:27, JPS via Sefaria]**. Aaron goes, meets him at the mountain of God, kisses him.
- Aaron speaks to the elders and performs the signs; the people believe **[A: Exodus 4:29-31]**. He and Moses then confront Pharaoh **[A: Exodus 5:1]**; Aaron is 83 **[A: Exodus 7:7]**.
- **The rod.** Aaron casts down his staff and it becomes a serpent; the magicians match him; Aaron's rod swallows theirs **[A: Exodus 7:10-12]**. Rashi, citing Shabbat 97a and Exodus Rabbah 9:7, holds the staff had **first reverted to rod form** before swallowing them **[A: Rashi on Exodus 7:12]**.
- **Why Aaron and not Moses.** Rashi on Exodus 7:19, citing Exodus Rabbah 9:10: "Because the river had protected Moses when he was cast into it, therefore it was not smitten by him neither at the plague of blood nor at that of frogs, but it was smitten by Aaron" **[A]**. The elder brother acts because the younger owes the water a debt.
- Blood: Aaron strikes **[A: Exodus 7:19-20]**. Frogs: Aaron stretches out his hand **[A]**. Lice from the dust: Aaron strikes, and the magicians fail **[A: Exodus 8:12-13 Heb. / 8:16-19 Eng.]**.
- **CORRECTION, citation only.** The pool cited the frog plague as "Exodus 8:1-6," which belongs to neither versification system. Hebrew: 7:26-8:11, Aaron's act at 8:1-2. English: 8:1-15, Aaron's act at 8:5-6. Both are in the journey file's `sources`.

## 3. Before Sinai

- **Rephidim [A: Exodus 17:10-12]**: Aaron and Hur hold up Moses' hands until sunset, and Israel prevails over Amalek. The function is again physical support of another man's authority.
- **Wilderness of Sin [A: Exodus 16:1-15]**: Aaron addresses the whole congregation; the glory appears in the cloud; quail and manna. Event attested, **place not located at all** (see §7).

## 4. Sinai: the vision and the calf

- **Exodus 24:9-11 [A]**: Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders go up, see the God of Israel, and eat and drink. Aaron stands inside the highest theophany the text grants anyone but Moses. **Exodus 24:14 [A]**: Aaron and Hur are left in charge of the camp.
- **Exodus 32:1-6 [A]**: the people gather against Aaron; he collects the gold, makes the calf with a graving tool, **builds the altar himself**, and proclaims a feast "tomorrow."
- **The exculpatory Rashi.** Three separate comments:
  - *On 32:4*, citing Midrash Tanchuma: magicians of the "mixed multitude" worked the calf out of the melted gold by sorcery, some traditions naming a plate inscribed "Come up, ox"; Rashi reads the acclamation "**these** are thy gods" rather than "our gods" as evidence the mixed multitude, not Aaron, is chiefly at fault **[A]**.
  - *On 32:5*: "HE BUILDED AN ALTAR to put them off ... to-morrow, not to-day, for he hoped that Moses might return before they would worship it" **[A]**. Aaron builds it with his own hands to consume time.
  - *On 32:24*: "AND I SAID UNTO THEM one single thing ... but they hastened and broke it off from themselves and gave it to me. AND I CAST IT INTO THE FIRE, and I did not know that this (i. e. this living) calf would come out" **[A]**.
- **CORRECTION, twice in the pool.** Both lenses claimed Rashi "lets Aaron's account stand without further gloss." False, and the falsehood inverts the point: Rashi **does** comment on 32:24, and the comment is what makes the reading exculpatory, the added words "one single thing" and "they hastened" shifting agency onto the people.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Exodus 32:4 has Aaron fashioning the calf **with a graving tool**. Rashi's reading (sorcery by others; stalling by Aaron) softens this without erasing the verb. Both are carried in the journey file.
- **Deuteronomy 9:20 [A]**: "the LORD was very angry with Aaron to have destroyed him; and I prayed for Aaron also the same time." Aaron came within a prayer of being killed for the calf. The strongest counterweight to the exculpatory tradition, and it is scripture, not commentary.

## 5. The priesthood

- **Vestments [A: Exodus 28:1-43; 39:1-31]**: breastplate, ephod, robe, tunic, turban, sash, "for glory and for beauty." **Ordination [A: Leviticus 8]**: seven days at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting. **The eighth day [A: Leviticus 9]**: Aaron's first service; fire comes out from before the LORD and consumes the offering.
- **CORRECTION, dating (twice).** The pool dated the Tabernacle erection "Exodus year 1, month 1 (second year)," self-contradictory, and the eighth day as "year 2, month 1, day 8." Exodus 40:17 fixes the erection on **1 Nisan of the second year** **[A]**; the seven days of *milu'im* **precede** it (23-29 Adar), and the "eighth day" of Leviticus 9 **is** 1 Nisan, the same day the Tabernacle stood up (Rashi on Leviticus 9:1) **[A/R]**. Separately, the census was dated "year 2, month 1"; Numbers 1:1 says **the first day of the second month** **[A]**, which separates it from the ordination cluster. Both corrected.
- **Nadab and Abihu [A: Leviticus 10:1-3]**: strange fire, death by fire, and *vayidom Aharon*, "And Aaron was silent." The two verbs of Aaron's life are *speaking* (Exodus 4:14) and this silence. **Leviticus 10:6-9 [A]**: Aaron and the surviving sons are forbidden to uncover their heads, rend their garments, or drink wine before entering the Tent.

## 6. Revolt, the rod, and Mount Hor

- **Hazeroth [A: Numbers 12:1-13]**: Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses over the Cushite woman. Miriam is struck with scale disease; Aaron is not, and pleads for her. The asymmetry is stated by the text and left unexplained by it, a gap the commentators fill variously and this report does not adjudicate.
- **Korah [A: Numbers 16:1-35]**: the rebellion is against Moses **and** Aaron's priesthood specifically. **The censer [A: Numbers 16:46-48]**: Aaron runs into the assembly with incense and stands between the dead and the living; the plague stops at 14,700 dead. The most active thing Aaron does on his own initiative anywhere in the text, and it is a rescue.
- **The rod that budded [A: Numbers 17:16-23 Heb. / 17:1-8 Eng.]**: twelve staves before the Testimony; Aaron's alone buds, blossoms, bears ripe almonds. Rashi on 17:23: "Because it is a fruit that blossoms earlier than all other fruits; so, too, the punishment of him who sets himself in opposition to the constituted priesthood comes quickly" **[A]**, with King Uzziah's leprosy as the standing example.
- **Meribah [A: Numbers 20:1-13]**: Moses strikes the rock; **both** are barred from the land. Aaron is condemned for an act his brother performed, and the text never says what his own share of the fault was.
- **Mount Hor [A: Numbers 20:22-28; 33:38-39]**: Aaron ascends with Moses and Eleazar in sight of the whole congregation, is stripped of the priestly garments, and dies at 123 on the first of the fifth month of the fortieth year.
- **Rashi on Numbers 20:26 [A]**: Moses dresses Eleazar in Aaron's presence, then walks Aaron into a cave where a bed is already made and a light burning: "'Enter the cave', and he entered ... 'Ascend the bed', and he ascended. 'Stretch out your hands' ... 'Close your mouth' ... 'Close your eyes', and he closed them." Rashi adds that Moses, watching, longed for such a death himself, linking Deuteronomy 32:50 through Sifrei. **Framing correction:** Aaron does not close his own eyes unprompted; Moses instructs at each step and Aaron complies.
- **Numbers 20:29 [A]**: "All the house of Israel bewailed Aaron thirty days." Rashi: "both men and women, because Aaron used to pursue peace and promoted love between contending parties, and between man and wife." He adds that the people disbelieved Aaron was dead until the ministering angels showed them his bier.
- **CORRECTION, citation.** The pool located Rashi's contrast with the mourning for Moses at Numbers 20:29. It is not there. The explicit comparison is at **Rashi on Deuteronomy 34:8** ("THE CHILDREN OF ISRAEL", i.e. the men, against "the whole house of Israel" for Aaron), citing Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer 17 **[A]**. Both citations are in the journey file.

## 8. Afterlife

- **Pirkei Avot 1:12 [A]**: Hillel: "Be of the disciples of Aaron, loving peace and pursuing peace, loving people and bringing them close to the Torah." The same trait Rashi cites for the mass mourning; the reputation runs continuous from Torah to Mishnah to Rashi.
- **Psalm 133:2 [A]**: oil on Aaron's beard as emblem of brotherly unity. Literary afterlife, not event.
- **Jabal Harun, Petra**: Byzantine monastery and pilgrimage complex, 5th-9th century occupation, excavated by the Finnish Jabal Harun Project **[R]**; and a Latin pilgrim account of a shrine there in **Burchard of Mount Sion**, c. 1280 **[R]**. Neither reached directly (see Sources).
- **CORRECTION, significant.** The pool dated the Mamluk shrine to **1458-1459 CE under Sultan Sayf al-Din Inal**, unsupported by anything reachable. It was completed in **719 AH = 1319/1320 CE under Sultan al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun**, the date of renewal standing above the door **[A: inscription, per Wikipedia, "Tomb of Aaron"]**. Sandstone, c. 10 by 8 m, one white dome, courtyard, c. 1,350 m; Mamluk inscriptions alongside Jewish and Greek pilgrim graffiti. Venerated jointly by Jews, Christians, and Muslims, who identify Aaron with the Quranic **Harun**.
- **Typology [A]**: the Biblia Pauperum block-books and cathedral glass cycles (Canterbury, c. 1178-1220) show Aaron vested as high priest among the Old Testament "types" of Christ's priesthood.
- **Poussin, *The Adoration of the Golden Calf*, c. 1633-34, National Gallery [A]**.
- **The Moserah contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** Deuteronomy 10:6 has Aaron die at **Moserah**; Numbers 33:38 has him die on **Mount Hor**. Wikipedia's Aaron article notes the plain difficulty as "a significant amount of travel between these two points." **CORRECTION:** the pool attributed to Rashi a harmonization reading Moserah as *adjacent to* Mount Hor. Rashi does not say that. **Rashi on Deuteronomy 10:6** resolves it by **sequence**: Aaron died on Mount Hor in the fortieth year, the clouds of glory departed, Israel feared war with the king of Arad and turned backwards eight stages as far as Moserah, where the sons of Levi fought them and forced them back onto the road. Moses names Moserah in his rebuke because that is where the retreat turned around. Whoever holds the adjacency reading, it is not Rashi. The attribution is withdrawn; the textual tension is left standing.

## 9. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **No external evidence of any kind.** No Egyptian text, no inscription, no excavated object bears on Aaron.
2. **Birthplace unstated** (Goshen is inference), and **childhood entirely absent**: nothing between birth and Exodus 4:14, when Aaron is already 83.
3. **Elisheba** is named once and never developed; the journey file does little with her, a real thinness.
4. **Aaron's specific fault at Meribah** is never stated. He is condemned alongside Moses for an act Moses performed.
5. **Why Miriam alone was struck at Hazeroth** when both spoke is unexplained by the text.
6. **Temple Mount.** Flagged against the canonical-pin list; **no textual claim** links Aaron to it in any file. Aaron as archetype of the later Jerusalem priesthood would be reconstruction only. **No stop was created for it.**
7. **The year of Korah's revolt** is not fixed anywhere within the forty years.

Writing substantively on a biblical figure is a real one-directional link. **Addendum, 2026-08-15:** per Anthony's instruction ("it doesn't need a Rashi stop, it needs Rashi as source"), the standalone Troyes stop was removed. Rashi was already integrated as a direct source, with quotations, at seven other stops (the rod-to-serpent, the Nile turned to blood, the golden calf altar, the calf out of the fire, the almond rod, Aaron's death, and the thirty days of mourning) plus the Poussin afterlife stop, which contrasts the painter's reading against Rashi's; the Troyes stop's six citations duplicated material already properly sourced there and added nothing beyond a biography of the commentator himself.
- **Abraham [R]**. Ancestral, not geographic: Aaron descends from him through Isaac, Jacob, Levi, Kohath, Amram. Named once, in the birth campa.
