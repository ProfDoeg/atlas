# Evliya Çelebi (1611 to c. 1684): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-13 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `evliya_celebi`. Companion: `evliya_celebi.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried only by a tertiary summary. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**The central problem.** Nearly everything known about Evliya Çelebi comes from the Seyahatname, the same document whose reliability is at issue. He is his own only witness for most of forty years on the road. Ottoman appointment registers can confirm that a pasha held a governorship in a given year, and so that the retinue he claims to have ridden with existed; they cannot confirm he was in it. Every date below inherits that limit.

---

## 1. Birth and family, 1611

- Born in Istanbul, **Unkapanı** quarter, **10 Muharrem 1020 AH = 25 March 1611** **[A: TDV İslam Ansiklopedisi, "Evliya Çelebi", which gives the conversion explicitly; corroborated by Wikipedia]**. Some tertiary sources round to the year. Pin 41.0186 / 28.9689 (Dankoff, *An Ottoman Mentality*, 2004).
- Father **Derviş Mehmed Zıllî**, **kuyumcubaşı**, chief jeweler of the imperial court **[A: TDV; Wikipedia]**. The boy is registered under his father's name; "Evliya" is acquired later.
- Mother an **Abkhazian woman, kin to the future grand vizier Melek Ahmed Pasha** **[R: family tradition repeated in secondary literature, not independently documented]**. It matters downstream: that kinship is the stated engine of the 1655 eastern journeys. If the kinship is invented, the patronage is not.
- Descent claimed from **Ahmed Yesevî** and the **Germiyanid dynasty of Kütahya** **[R: presented in the Seyahatname as ancestral tradition, repeated by TDV and Wikipedia; not verified genealogy]**.

**Gap.** No register entry, waqf document, or court record naming the family was consulted. The father's office is attested only through literature that depends on Evliya.

## 2. Education, 1620s to 1630s

- About **seven years** at the seminary of **Şeyhülislam Hâmid Efendi**, Unkapanı **[A: TDV]**. Start and end years not given; the decade is an estimate.
- Memorized the Qur'an under **Evliya Mehmed Efendi**, whose name he takes **[A: TDV; von Hammer 1834]**, who became imperial chaplain and died in 1045 AH / 1635. Calligraphy from his father **[A: Britannica]**.
- Entered the **Enderun** under **Murad IV**: calligraphy, music, Arabic grammar, tajwid **[A: TDV; Britannica]**. Entry year not given; **1636 [R]**, hedged in `date_confidence`.

**Correction, a merge.** The pool split one man into two. TDV names a music teacher "**Derviş Ömer Efendi**"; English Wikipedia names "a renowned Khalwati dervish by the name of **Umar Gulshani**" whose circle he later entered. Same person: **Derviş Ömer Gülşenî**. Treated as one figure **[A on the merged reading]**.

**Correction, two Istanbul pins dropped.** The pool placed his schooling in the **Bayezid II Mosque** medrese circle and his tajwid and music at the **Süleymaniye**. Both coordinates were accurate for those buildings; neither placement is supported by any consulted source, and both look like generic landmarks chosen to fill a pin. **Cut** rather than carried with a hedge.

## 3. The dream, 1630

- Night of **Ashura, 10 Muharrem 1040 AH = 19 August 1630**. He dreams of the Prophet; meaning to ask for **şefaat** (intercession) he says **seyahat** (travel), and the Prophet grants it **[A: TDV, summarizing the Seyahatname's own preface]**.
- The mosque is the **Ahî Çelebi Mosque near Yemiş İskelesi**, Eminönü **[A: TDV]**. Pin 41.018 / 28.9705. Afterwards he consults **Abdullah Dede**, sheikh of the **Kasımpaşa Mevlevîhânesi**, who tells him to describe **Istanbul first** **[A: TDV]**. Volume I is the city alone **[A: Wikipedia, "Seyahatname"]**.

**Two corrections.** One lens called Abdullah Dede "his teacher" who "confirmed the pun"; TDV makes him neither, only a Mevlevi sheikh consulted after the fact whose advice was the Istanbul-first instruction. A second lens said "his own text places the dream in his family home near the Fatih mosque district" and pinned generic Sultanahmet, 1.2 km off. That contradicts TDV and the geography lens both, and is dropped.

**Open question, not resolved.** Whether this was a dream or a rhetorical origin-device composed retrospectively is debated. What is documented is that he **recorded and circulated it** **[A]**.

## 4. First journeys, 1640 to 1647

This phase carried the pool's worst provenance error.

**The error.** A chronology entry read: "First journey beyond Istanbul takes him into Anatolia (including Bursa) and toward the Caucasus, Crete, and Azerbaijan (1640)." That is not a chronology. It is **Wikipedia's Volume II contents line**, the regions a *volume* covers, dated by its opening. Read as an itinerary it put Crete five years and Azerbaijan six years early.

**Corrected itinerary [A: TDV]:**

| Year | Where |
|---|---|
| 1640 | **Bursa** only, the first journey outside Istanbul |
| 1641 | **Trabzon**, then **Azak** (Azov) and Crimea |
| 1645 | **Crete** with Yusuf Pasha's army, at the fall of **Chania** |
| 1646 | **Erzurum**, **Azerbaijan**, the **Caucasus** |
| 1647 | **Baku**, inside the 1646-47 circuit **[A/R]** |

- **Kütahya**: the ancestral-town visit is plausible and is carried, but **no source fixes a year** **[R]**. Placed 1640, flagged.
- **Erzurum** in the retinue of **Defterdarzade Mehmed Pasha**, and **Kars**, both vol. 2, dated only to "the 1640s" **[A for the passages, R for the years]**.
- **Circassian coast**: the beauty-of-the-women and magpie-speech passage is real; **the landfall is not identifiable to a single site** **[R]**. The pin is representative, not located.
- **Baku**: the "oil congealed on the surface like cream" line is universally attributed to him, but **translator and edition could not be confirmed** **[R]**.

**Azak, 1641: a real doubt, wrongly explained.** The pool said scholars doubt his eyewitness status "since he would have been about thirty and newly attached to the palace." Both halves fail: he entered palace service around 1636, and being thirty is no argument. The genuine doubts concern **textual borrowing and chronological impossibilities inside the campaign narrative** (Dankoff), which is how the journey file states it.

## 5. Syria and Palestine, 1648 to 1649

- **August 1648**, Damascus, in the retinue of **Murtaza Pasha**, newly appointed beylerbeyi **[A: TDV]**.

**Correction, wrong patron.** The pool attributed the Damascus journey to **Melek Ahmed Pasha**. It was Murtaza Pasha; the Melek Ahmed attachment belongs to the **1650s** (Van, 1655). Aleppo inherited the same error, so its campa names no patron and says instead that the identification is loose in his own text.

- **Umayyad Mosque** pinned at the atlas-canonical **33.5117 / 36.3064**, inherited byte-identical from `abdelkader.journey.json`. **Jerusalem**, Dome of the Rock, 1649 **[A: vol. 3]**.

**Contradiction, flagged.** One tertiary summary lists a Syria-Palestine journey in **1648** and a separate Palestine visit in **1649** without reconciling them. They may be one extended journey rendered twice. Both are carried; Jerusalem is dated 1649 with the conflict in `date_confidence`.

- **Konya**, tomb of Rumi: attested for vol. 3's Anatolian material, dated only to "the 1640s" **[A for the visit, R for the year]**. Pin inherited from atlas canon, **37.8706 / 32.5047**, not the pool's 37.8716 / 32.5023.

## 6. Melek Ahmed Pasha's household, 1655 to 1656

- **1655**: Kurdistan, Iraq, and Iran in the entourage of **Melek Ahmed Pasha**, governor of Van **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]**. This is the attachment that lasts. **Diyarbakır**, **Van**, **Tabriz**, **Baghdad**, then south to **Basra** **[A: vol. 4, except Basra]**.

**Four coordinate corrections**, each 0.6 to 1.4 km, verified against the site's own article:

| Site | Pool | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Van Fortress | 38.5018 / 43.3559 | **38.5025 / 43.3400** |
| Blue Mosque, Tabriz | 38.0827 / 46.2919 | **38.0736 / 46.3011** |
| Abu Hanifa Mosque, Baghdad | 33.3739 / 44.3439 | **33.3721 / 44.3584** |
| Koules Fortress, Heraklion | 35.3417 / 25.1424 | **35.3446 / 25.1369** |

**Correction, an anachronism.** The pool had him recording "the ruined Blue Mosque" at Tabriz. The mosque was wrecked by the **earthquake of 1780**, one hundred and twenty-five years after the 1655 visit. He saw it substantially intact; the journey file states the reverse.

- **Basra** carried **[R]**: presence is questioned because the southern Iraq material is sparse against his usual density (Dankoff). Density is a real diagnostic in this text and is used as one.
- **1656, Russia and the Balkans**: thinly documented, tradition only **[R]**. No stop, no locatable pin.

## 7. Balkans, Hungary, and the Habsburg embassy, 1660 to 1667

- **1660** Kosovo **[A]**; **1662** Albania including Tirana, Lezhë, Shkodër, Bushat **[A]**; through the 1660s Sarajevo, Belgrade, Mostar, Edirne as recurring waypoints **[A: vols. 5-7]**. No Kosovo pin was placed: the geography lens supplies none and inventing one is not warranted.
- **1663**: siege and capture of **Uyvar / Nové Zámky** under **Köprülüzade Fazıl Ahmed Pasha** **[A: Seyahatname vol. 6]**.

**Correction, a date error.** The pool dated the Hungarian campaign "including the campaign culminating at Saint Gotthard" to **1663**. **Saint Gotthard was fought 1 August 1664**. 1663 is the Uyvar year; Wikipedia's own volume line reads "1663/64", and the claim collapsed the span onto the wrong end.

- **1665**: **Vienna**, with **Kara Mehmed Pasha's** embassy after the **Peace of Vasvár** **[A: Seyahatname vol. 7; Wikipedia gives 1665-1666]**.

**Contradiction, flagged.** Von Hammer's 1834 sketch says "Evliya accompanied the Turkish Embassy to Vienna **in 1664**, as secretary, whence he proceeded to the **Netherlands and Sweden**." Modern summaries give 1665-66 and do not endorse that extension. Both carried, the discrepancy in `date_confidence`.

- **Rotterdam, 1663**, the Native American episode. That **he wrote it** is documented **[A]**; that it happened is not, and Ottomanist scholarship reads it as fantastical or secondhand **[R]**. Kept as a stop tagged R: it is the best single illustration of the book's method, kept for that reason and not despite it.
- **1667**: **Athens**, the Parthenon still substantially intact as a mosque **[A]**. The Venetian explosion is 1687, after his death.

**Correction, a span narrowed.** The pool had him at Candia "1667-1669, describing the city's fall." TDV records that he witnessed the siege and **returned in May 1668**, so he was probably **not present for the surrender of 27 September 1669**. The journey file says the city surrenders after he has gone.

- **1670**: southern Albania, including **Berat**, coordinate corrected from 40.7058 / 19.9522 to **40.708 / 19.945**. **Ohrid** was **dropped**: the identity and survival of the two-minaret Ali Pasha mosque are not firmly established **[R]**.

## 8. Hajj, Egypt, and the last decade, 1671 to c. 1684

- Leaves Istanbul **May 1671** for the Hajj **[A: TDV]**; **Mecca** and **Medina**, 1671-72 **[A: Seyahatname vol. 9]**.
- Then **Egypt**, south into **Sudan, Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somalia**, the material of **Volume X** **[A for Egypt and Nubia; R for the Horn]**. Summaries give 1672-1675.

**Correction, the Cairo settling date.** The pool inferred settlement in **1675**, at the end of the documented travels. TDV has him leaving Istanbul in May 1671, going on to Egypt, and remaining **over a decade** writing Volume X. Cairo residence begins **c. 1672**, and the 1672-75 travels were run **out of an Egyptian base**. The Nile stops are ordered after it.

- Cairo pin: the **Takiyya of Ibrahim al-Gulshani** stands beside **Bab Zuwayla** at about **30.0447 / 31.2578**. The pool's 30.0367 / 31.2589 falls 0.9 km south. Corrected.
- **Suakin** and the Horn lean heavily on hearsay; firsthand presence unresolved **[R]**.

### Death: a genuine and unresolved conflict

| Source | Says |
|---|---|
| TDV İslam Ansiklopedisi | circa **1095 AH / 1684** |
| English Wikipedia, "Evliya Çelebi" | "Evliya Çelebi died in **1684**. It is unclear whether he was in Constantinople or Cairo at the time." |
| Turkish Wikipedia | "1685 yılından sonra Mısır'da öldüğü tahmin edilmektedir", died in Egypt **after 1685** |
| Wikipedia, "Kütahya" | "Evliya Çelebi (1611-**1682**)" |

The real spread is **c. 1684 to after 1685**, with **1682 an outlier from a non-specialist page**. The pool had sourced 1682 to the Evliya Çelebi article itself, which does not carry it. **Not adjudicated.** The journey file gives 1684-01-01 with the full conflict in `date_confidence`, tagged **[R]**.

**No tomb identified.** Turkish sources state plainly *"ölüm yeri ve mezarı bilinmemektedir"*, death place and grave unknown **[A as a statement of the gap]**. A later tradition places a probable burial near the **Lohusa Sultan (Emine Hatun)** shrine in Beyoğlu, offered as conjecture **[R]**. Carried in `suggested_refs`, not as a stop.

## 9. The book after the man

- Manuscripts preserved principally in the **Topkapı Palace Museum Library** **[A: TDV]**. **No accession date** found in any reachable source.
- **1834**: **von Hammer-Purgstall**'s English rendering of roughly the first two volumes **[A]**, the longest English version until Dankoff. **1896-1938**: the first printed **Ottoman Turkish** edition, six volumes **[R]**, carried on general philological knowledge and a Wikipedia summary, **not confirmed against a primary bibliographic source**. **1996-2007**: the reliable modern **Latin-script Turkish edition**, ten volumes, **Yapı Kredi Yayınları**, by a team including **Dankoff, Seyit Ali Kahraman, Yücel Dağlı** **[A]**. **2010**: **Dankoff and Kim, *An Ottoman Traveller*** (Eland), the standard modern English selection **[A]**. **No complete English translation exists.** Palestine portions by **Stephan Hanna Stephan**, 1935-1940, and excerpts in eight other languages **[R]**.
- **2009**: the **Evliya Çelebi Way**, about 600 km from **Hersek** through Iznik, Yenişehir, Inegöl, Kütahya, Afyonkarahisar, Uşak, Eski Gediz and Simav, retracing the early stage of the 1671 pilgrimage road **[A: Wikipedia, "Evliya Çelebi Way"]**. **2011**: **UNESCO** commemorations calendar **[A]**. A family house at **Kütahya** is preserved as a museum **[A]**, designation date not given; statues at **Eger** **[A]** and **Skopje** **[R]**. None placed as stops.

**Two afterlife corrections.** The **2014** animated film is ***Evliya Çelebi ve Ölümsüzlük Suyu***, "the Water of **Immortality**", not "the Water of Life". And on **Pamuk's *The White Castle* (1985)**, Wikipedia records only that Evliya **is featured**; the claim that the novel "draws on the atmosphere and narrative conventions of the Seyahatname" is the researcher's gloss. Downgraded to **[R]**, not carried into the journey file.

## 10. Quotations used and refused

Eight of forty-four stops carry a quote, all verbatim and unstyled: six from von Hammer 1834 vol. 1, one from Dankoff and Kim 2010 (Mostar), and the Baku oil seeps and Rotterdam curse, both **[R]** for unconfirmed edition and contested attribution.

**A splice was un-spliced.** The pool carried "a design of travelling over the whole earth, to visit the Holy Land, Cairo, Damascus, Mecca and Medina" as one continuous quotation. It is not: the halves sit about ten lines apart on pp. 1-2 of von Hammer vol. 1, joined with no ellipsis. The file uses the full actual sentence, and dates it **A.H. 1041 / A.D. 1631** per the source rather than the pool's "1640s onward". A second quotation, on the Qur'an tutor, had dropped von Hammer's hedge "it appears", converting conjecture into fact. Restored.

**A quotation that is not one, refused.** The pool carried: *"Although many of the descriptions in the Seyahatname were written in an exaggerated manner or were plainly inventive fiction or third-source misinterpretation, his notes remain a useful guide…"* The wording is verbatim on Wikipedia, cited there to Britannica, but it is **Wikipedia's own editorial prose**: not Evliya's words, not any named scholar's. Quoting it would read as an attributed judgement nobody wrote. **Not used.** The point is carried instead in the file's own voice at the Azak, Rotterdam, Basra, and Suakin stops.

## 11. Interlocks named in the journey file

Named where a path crosses an existing atlas traveler at the same pin or institution.

- **`abdelkader`**: the **Umayyad Mosque, Damascus**, where Evliya prays in 1648 and Abdelkader teaches from 1856. Pin inherited byte-identical.
- **`ibn_battuta`**: the **tomb of Rumi at Konya** and the **Kaaba**, his first circuits in 1326. Both pins inherited.
- **`rumi`**: the Konya tomb, and again at Tabriz through Shams.
- **`shams_al_din_of_tabriz`**: **Tabriz**, the city Shams walks out of to find Rumi.
- **`abd_al_qadir_al_jilani`**: **Baghdad**, the Jilani shrine at Bab al-Sheikh a mile south of the Abu Hanifa mosque where Evliya prays.

**Rejected**: the Acropolis against `plato` and `aristotle` (shared city, no shared site); `marco_polo`, `benjamin_de_tudela`, `xuanzang`, `matsuo_basho` (fellow travelers, no crossing path).

## Sources

**Reached and used**

- **TDV İslam Ansiklopedisi**, s.v. "Evliya Çelebi". The specialist reference and the spine of the corrected chronology: birth conversion, the Hâmid Efendi years, the Ahî Çelebi Mosque, the Abdullah Dede consultation, the 1640/1641/1645/1646 split, Murtaza Pasha at Damascus 1648, the May 1671 departure, the May 1668 return from Candia, the decade in Egypt, the c. 1095/1684 death.
- **Evliyâ Çelebi, *Narrative of Travels in Europe, Asia, and Africa in the Seventeenth Century*, trans. Joseph von Hammer** (London: Oriental Translation Fund, 1834), vol. 1. Full text at archive.org, id `narrativeoftrave01evli`. Read directly for all von Hammer quotations, including the pagination check that exposed the splice.
- **Robert Dankoff, *An Ottoman Mentality*** (2004), via summary and citation, not read whole: the Azov authenticity discussion and the Basra caution. **Dankoff and Kim, *An Ottoman Traveller*** (2010) for the Mostar passage, via Wikiquote's citation of the edition. **Britannica**, "Evliya Çelebi": education, Enderun, the Melek Ahmed association.
- **English Wikipedia**: "Evliya Çelebi", "Seyahatname", "Evliya Çelebi Way", "Kütahya", "Battle of Saint Gotthard (1664)", "Van Fortress", "Blue Mosque, Tabriz", "Abu Hanifa Mosque", "Koules Fortress", "Berat Castle", the last six for coordinates and the Tabriz earthquake date. **Turkish Wikipedia**, "Evliya Çelebi": the post-1685 death estimate and the "death place and grave unknown" statement.

**Named as gaps, not reached**

- **The Yapı Kredi ten-volume transliteration (1996-2007)**, the authoritative modern text. Not consulted. Every itinerary date above rests on encyclopedia summaries of it rather than on the text. Largest single limitation of this report.
- **The Topkapı manuscripts.** Not consulted, and no accession date found anywhere reachable.
- **The 1896-1938 printed Ottoman edition.** No primary bibliographic record checked; the censorship claim commonly attached to it was not verified.
- **Ottoman appointment and campaign registers.** Not consulted. These could independently corroborate or break the retinue chronology (Murtaza Pasha 1648, Melek Ahmed Pasha 1655, Kara Mehmed Pasha 1665). Until they are read against the Seyahatname, the itinerary remains a single-witness account.
- **Nuran Tezcan and the Turkish scholarship of the 2010s.** Not searched; likely to settle several dates left open above.

**Coordinates.** Pins are inherited from atlas canon (Mecca, Medina, Konya, the Umayyad Mosque, Jerusalem, Istanbul, Topkapı) or verified against the site's own article (Van, Tabriz, Baghdad, Heraklion, Berat, Cairo). **One is approximate**: the Kasımpaşa Mevlevîhânesi, 41.0363 / 28.9707, placed from the lodge's quarter because no lens supplied a coordinate. **One is representative, not located**: the Circassian coast, because the text does not identify the landfall.
