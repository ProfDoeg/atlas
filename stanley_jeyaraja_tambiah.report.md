# Stanley Jeyaraja Tambiah (1929-2014), research report

*Written 2026-08-22 for `stanley_jeyaraja_tambiah.journey.json`. Anthropologist of ritual
speech and of communal violence, queued as the man who carried J. L. Austin's performative
utterance out of the philosophy of language and into anthropology.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or consensus without a
document reached this pass. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**, except once
(the death date), where one source is disqualified on its own arithmetic, argued in place.
The spine of the file is the **National Academy of Sciences biographical memoir** by Emiko
Ohnuki-Tierney (2015), cited as **NAS memoir**: authoritative on most of the career and
demonstrably wrong on the death date.

---

## 1. Jaffna and the Anglican schoolroom, 1929-1951

- Born **16 January 1929, Jaffna, Northern Province** [A: NAS memoir header]. Earlier passes
  carried a vaguer "British Ceylon"; the town is attested and is used.
- **One of nine children** of an **English-speaking Anglican Tamil family**; father a lawyer
  from a coconut-plantation-owning landed family, mother from a line of "feudal district
  chiefs" [A]. The first pass's generic "Christian" is replaced by the memoir's "Anglican."
  The **house in Colombo** [A] has no recorded address and is folded into the school stop.
- **S. Thomas' College, Mount Lavinia, entered at age eleven**, so c. 1940 [A for the age;
  R for the year]. Tamil, Sinhalese, Burgher and Muslim boys together [A].
- **Wartime displacement to Gurutalawa** [A]. After the fall of Singapore (February 1942) the
  Mount Lavinia campus was requisitioned as a military hospital and he was sent to the school's
  hill-country branch, **S. Thomas' College, Gurutalawa, Uva Province**, for "an idyllic time."
  The gather pass missed this entirely. **Gap:** coordinates carried as approximately **6.35N,
  80.92E**, from the verification pass, **not independently confirmed**. Weakest pin in the file.
- **Undergraduate degree, University of Ceylon, 1951** [A: Wikipedia], Colombo campus.
  **Gap:** the subject is not established anywhere reachable. The campa says only that his
  training was survey sociology, supported downstream by the memoir's description of his
  return as "a rural sociologist using a quantitative approach" [A for the phrase, R for
  reading it back onto the syllabus].

## 2. Cornell, 1951-1954, and the return, 1955-1960

- **PhD, Cornell, 1954** [A: NAS memoir, plus a photo captioned to that year]. Arrival in
  1951 is [R], back-calculated. He returned to teach at the **University of Ceylon** among the
  island's first academic sociologists [A]. The first pass called him "Ceylonese-trained,"
  which is **backwards**: he was Cornell-trained, and the memoir is explicit about the
  quantitative American formation.
- **CONTRADICTION, flagged and not resolved.** NAS memoir dates the post **1951-1960**;
  Wikipedia **1955-1960**. The memoir also places him at Cornell in 1954. The two reconcile
  if 1951 is the appointment (held on study leave) and 1955 the return to residence, but **no
  source says so**; the possibility is offered in `date_confidence` and the disagreement
  stated. Both agree the post ends in 1960 [A]. Located at **Peradeniya** [A for the
  relocation, R for which years sat on which campus]: the transfer up-country was well advanced
  by the mid-1950s and the survey work with **N. K. Sarkar** was Peradeniya-based [A].

## 3. Thailand, 1960-1963

- **UNESCO technical assistance expert in Thailand until 1963** [A], **downgraded to [R] as to
  place.** The gather pass gave this a "UNESCO regional posting, Bangkok" heading, a Bangkok pin
  and an [A] tag; **no source consulted names Bangkok or any city**, the memoir giving country
  and job title only. The stop keeps the Bangkok pin, and both `date_confidence` and the campa
  say the city is a reconstruction.
- **Fieldwork at Baan Phraan Muan, Udon Thani** [R]. The site is universally given for
  *Buddhism and the Spirit Cults in North-East Thailand* (1970), but **exact fieldwork years
  are unconfirmed** and the **village's own coordinates were not verified**. The pin is the
  provincial town, and the file says so.
- The ethnographic content of the second village stop (Pali chanting held effective as sound,
  blessings over water, a rite calling back a fled spirit-essence) is [A] to the 1970 monograph
  and the 1968 article, [R] as to any dated field observation.

## 4. Cambridge, 1963/64-1972/73

- **Edmund Leach brought him to Cambridge** [A], omitted by the gather pass, restored here,

- **CONTRADICTION, flagged and not resolved.** Wikipedia **1963-1972**; NAS memoir
  **1964-1973**, "perhaps the best years of his career" and "ten years at Cambridge
  University." The gather pass's "after nine years" fails on the memoir's own arithmetic and
  is dropped; the campa says a decade, or nine years, according to who counts.
- **Curl Bequest Prize, RAI, 1964** [A for the prize; **R for the location**]. The gather pass
  pinned Bedford Square on its own assumption, and **no source establishes the Institute's 1964

  **no attested ceremony**: a marker, not a scene.
- **Malinowski Memorial Lecture, LSE, 1968** [A], printed as **"The Magical Power of Words,"
  Man, n.s. 3(2): 175-208 (1968)** [A: NAS memoir bibliography, which closes the citation gap
  the afterlife lens had declared open]. ***Buddhism and the Spirit Cults*, 1970** [A].

## 5. Chicago, 1973-1976

- **Chicago professorship 1973-1976** [A: NAS memoir, "three years at the University of
  Chicago, then Harvard, starting in 1976"]. **Gap:** nothing else survives of the three years.
  No lecture, no colleague, no anecdote in anything reached; the campa states the gap.
- ***World Conqueror and World Renouncer*, Cambridge UP, 1976** [A]. **Subtitle corrected:**
  the memoir's bibliography gives *A Study of **Religion** and Polity in Thailand against a
  Historical Background*. The common "A Study of Buddhism and Polity" is a misquotation.
- **Attribution flagged.** The "galactic polity" model is popularly credited to the 1976 book.
  The memoir credits its introduction to **"The galactic polity," Annals of the New York
  Academy of Sciences 293: 69-97 (1977)**, reprinted in *Culture, Thought and Social Action*
  (1985). The file follows the memoir.

## 8. The prizes, 1997-2000

- **Balzan Prize for Social Anthropology, 1997; acceptance speech Bern, 18 November 1997** [A:
  balzan.org, fetched directly]. Corrected from "Bern/Milan": **Milan is the Foundation's
  administrative seat, not the 1997 ceremony city.**
- **HARD CONFLICT, flagged and NOT resolved.** The RAI's Huxley Memorial Medal tabulation
  carries per-year delivery dates and gives **Tambiah's Huxley Memorial Lecture as 18 November
  1997**, titled "Transnational movements, multiculturalism and ethnonationalism": **the same
  day as the Balzan speech in Bern.** He cannot have been in both cities; one date is wrong and
  this pass cannot say which. The Huxley stop is therefore **dated to the year only**, with an
  explicitly arbitrary month for ordering, and **neither stop stages a scene on 18 November**.
  The gather pass's "1997-12" is unsupported and unused.
- **Fukuoka, September 1998** [A], specifically the **Academic Prize (anthropology) of the 9th
  Fukuoka Asian Culture Prizes** [A: official laureate listing], correcting the
  undifferentiated "Fukuoka Asian Culture Prize" of earlier passes. He used the occasion to
  explain his upbringing "in more detail than he had elsewhere" [A], which makes those remarks
  the near-primary source behind the Jaffna family material in §1.
- **Mary Keatinge Das Lecture, Columbia, 1999** [A]; content not recorded anywhere reached.
- **Corresponding Fellow, British Academy, 2000** [A]. **Coordinates corrected for
  anachronism:** the geography lens pinned this at Burlington House, which the Academy left for
  **10-11 Carlton House Terrace in 1998**. The 2000 and 1979 stops sit at two different London
  addresses on purpose.

## 9. Last work, death, afterlife

- ***Edmund Leach: An Anthropological Life*, 2002** [A]. That it is his last book is [R],
  inferred from bibliographies recording nothing after it.
- **DEATH DATE: contradiction resolved, with the argument stated.** The NAS memoir prints **29
  January 2014** in header and text. The **Colombo Telegraph** tribute published **25 January
  2014** states he "passed away on January 19th, 2014." An obituary printed on the 25th cannot
  report a death on the 29th. **19 January 2014 stands [A]; the memoir is in error.** This is
  the one adjudicated conflict in the file, and the reasoning is written into `date_confidence`
  so the adjudication is visible rather than silent. Death at 85 in Cambridge, Massachusetts,
  first at his apartment and then at the Neville Center [A]; survived by former wife **Mary H.
  Tambiah** and sons **Jonathan** and **Mathew** [A].
- **Commemoration, ICES Colombo, 30 November 2015**, with **Gananath Obeyesekere, Sarath
  Amunugama, John D. Rogers** [A: Colombo Telegraph, 3 December 2015]. Posthumous stop,
  declared as such in `date_confidence`. **Gap:** the twenty-two month delay is noted by the
  source and left unexplained by it.
- **Sunday Times (Sri Lanka) editorial, "Buddhism betrayed by extreme monks," 11 May 2014**
  [A: same source], whose timing that source describes confusingly; by the dates given it runs
  **after** the death, and the file measures the interval from the 2015 memorial instead.
- **Translation corrected.** ***Rituali e cultura* (il Mulino, 1995) translates *Culture,
  Thought, and Social Action*, NOT *Magic, Science, Religion*** [A: Open Library OL53626672M,
  "Originally titled: Culture, thought, and social action"]. The gather pass attached it to the
  wrong book; page counts corroborate (397 against 411). Unused, as is the 2016 Turkish edition.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Tambiah is NOT listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** No stop applies his framework backward to
material he never treated, and none reaches forward past his death except two ordinary
afterlife facts (the 2014 editorial, the 2015 memorial), which are reception of his own
published work. **Nothing was silently deleted.** The interlock material that did not enter
the file:

1. **Derrida's reading of Austin and the 1977 Searle-Derrida exchange** (lens, [R]). A parallel
   afterlife of Austin, called "independent of" Tambiah's by the lens itself; no source relates
   the two men in either direction. **Direction: neither. Not an apparatus relation, a shared
   ancestor.** Recorded so the operator can see it was considered.
2. **Austin's 1955 William James Lectures at Harvard and his April 1955 meeting with Chomsky
   in Harvard Yard** (lens, [A]). Real, and on ground Tambiah later worked, but **deliberately
   unused**: the only link is co-location twenty-one years apart, which the map already shows.
   **The Austin relation itself is real and is carried where it belongs**, in the 1968, 1979
   and 1982 stops, on the documented grounds that Tambiah cites him.
3. **Searle.** QUEUE.md queues Tambiah as extending Austin's and, per Anthony, Searle's
   speech-act theory; the chronology pool hedges "per some readings." **No source consulted
   this pass shows Tambiah citing Searle directly**, so Searle is **not named in any campa**.
   That line should rest on a verified citation, not the queue row. **Recorded, not deleted.**
4. **Blavatsky and Olcott's pansil at Kandy, 25 May 1880** (lens, [R]). **Used, with a hedge**,
   in the *Buddhism Betrayed?* campa: the book is about the politicisation of Sinhala Buddhism,
   whose modern revival the Theosophists helped organise, which is subject matter, not
   co-location. But **no source confirms Tambiah names Blavatsky in the book**, and
   `date_confidence` says so. The clause lifts out cleanly.
5. **Levi-Strauss.** Named once, in the *Culture, Thought, and Social Action* stop, because the
   volume argues for rite as performance against a structuralist reading of rite as formal
   system. The lens tags this [R], **no direct citation was verified**, `date_confidence` says so.

**Rejected outright.** **"American Ethnological Association Distinguished Lecture, 1988"**
(geography lens): the body is the American Ethnological **Society**, and the lens admitted its
coordinates were "a stand-in placeholder only" for an unknown host city, which is a fabricated
location. **The fact is kept here instead:** he gave the AES Distinguished Lecture in 1988 [A],
almost certainly published as **"Ethnic conflict in the world today," American Ethnologist 16:
335-349 (1989)** [R]. Establish the 1988 meeting city and this becomes a legitimate stop.
