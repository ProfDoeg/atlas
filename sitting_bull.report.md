# Sitting Bull (Thathanka Iyotake), Hunkpapa Lakota: research report

*2026-08-07. For the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `sitting_bull`. Register: national mythology: the canon is true.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or claim the reachable sources do not settle · *flagged* = sources disagree and this report does not adjudicate.

**Method note.** The pool came through five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife) and the lenses **disagree with each other** on roughly a dozen coordinates and half a dozen dates. Where they disagree this report names both figures; where a correction overturned an earlier claim, the overturned claim is recorded too, so a reader who has met the old figure elsewhere knows why this atlas prints another.

## 1. Birth and naming, c. 1831 to c. 1845

**[A]** Born Hunkpapa near the Grand River. **[R]** The year: 1831 in most accounts, with 1831 to 1837 in circulation. No register was kept.

***Flagged, birthplace.*** Two claims three hundred miles apart, neither documentary: Grand River, South Dakota **[R, print tradition]**, and the Yellowstone south of present Miles City, Montana **[R, family tradition, from great-grandson Ernie LaPointe in 2007]**. The atlas pins both and resolves neither.

**[A]** Childhood names Hunkesni ("Slow") and Hoka Psice ("Jumping Badger"). At about fourteen he rides with his father and his uncle Four Horns against the Crow and counts coup before mounted Lakota witnesses; the raid is dated only by an approximate age itself uncertain by six years, and **no coordinate exists for it**, so the journey anchors it to the Grand River country and says so in `date_confidence`. His father then holds a feast and confers his own name, Thathanka Iyotake, with an eagle feather, a warrior's horse, and a buffalo hide shield.

**Gap.** Wikipedia names five wives (Light Hair, Four-Robes-Woman, Snow-on-Her, Seen-by-her-Nation, Scarlet Woman) and gives **no marriage dates for any of them**. No marriage appears as a stop, because none can be placed in time or space.

## 2. The northern wars, 1864 to 1868

**[A] Killdeer Mountain, 28 to 29 July 1864.** Sully attacks a Lakota, Dakota, and Yanktonai village with roughly 2,200 troops and artillery; Sitting Bull is named among the defenders with Gall and Inkpaduta. Chronology gave only the bare year; the two-day date is firm.

**[A, retagged] Battle of the Badlands** (Little Missouri), 7 to 9 August 1864. Geography carried this as **[R]** tradition. It is a documented engagement and belongs as **[A]**. The Medora pin is a locality anchor; the fighting was south and west of the town.

**[A] Marmarth, September 1864.** Shot through the left hip attacking a wagon train, bullet exiting at the small of the back, wound judged not serious. One of very few injuries attested to him by name and place.

***Flagged, the Missouri forts campaign.*** Chronology dated raids on Forts Berthold, Stevenson, and Buford to **1866**. Impossible as stated: **Fort Stevenson was not built until 1867**. The campaign is properly **1865 to 1868**, in support of Red Cloud's war. Restated as a span.

**Attribution gap.** Wikipedia's article on Sitting Bull carries the raids. The articles on **Fort Berthold** and **Fort Stevenson** do not corroborate him: Berthold records an 1862 Sioux burning without naming him, Stevenson records no attack by him at all. One source, not three, and the journey says so in campa.

## 3. The treaty of 1868

**[A]** He does not accept the Treaty of Fort Laramie, and **he never went to Fort Laramie**. Instead **Pierre-Jean De Smet**, travelling for the government, made a forty-nine-day journey from Saint Louis and reached Sitting Bull's own camp of 500 to 600 lodges in June 1868 (marker hmdb.org/m.asp?m=253651). Two quotations belong to that council and both are placed there:

- **[R]** "I wish all to know that I do not propose to sell any part of my country." Matteoni, *Prairie Man* (2015), p. 41, via Wikipedia; corroborated at teachingamericanhistory.org. ***Correction:*** an earlier pool draft gave the author as "Vincent A. Matteoni." It is **Norman E.**
- **[R]** "I hardly sustain myself beneath the weight of white men's blood that I have shed." Recorded by De Smet after the council of **19 June 1868**; printed in Utley (1993), pp. 79-80. ***Flagged place:*** Wikiquote and the pool put this council "near Fort Rice." Fort Rice was De Smet's departure point, several hundred miles east.

***Flagged, what he did at Fort Rice.*** The northern-band council met there on **2 July 1868**. Wikipedia, "Sitting Bull" is read as saying he refused. Wikipedia, "Fort Rice" states he "did not attend, but sent a replacement to sign the treaty in his stead." **These are materially different acts** and this report does not choose. His opposition is not in doubt; the mechanism is.

## 4. The Sun Dances and the Little Bighorn, 1875 to 1876

***The worst tangle in the pool: there are two Sun Dances and the sources conflate them.***

**The 1875 Sun Dance. [A]** as to the gathering, **[R]** as to place. Wikipedia has the Northern Cheyenne, Hunkpapa, Oglala, Sans Arc, and Minneconjou camped together for a Sun Dance in 1875, with Sitting Bull in alliance with the Cheyenne medicine man Ice, and gives **no location**; the Rosebud pin was imported from the separate 1876 gathering. The journey keeps the stop, because the alliance with Ice is attested, and puts the hedge in `date_confidence`. The revelation quoted there ("The Great Spirit has given our enemies to us...") is Wikipedia's blockquote credited to Utley 1992, pp. 122-24, attached to **1875** and paired with Ice's remark that "No one then knew who the enemy were, of what tribe." Utley's text reads "Sitting Bull intoned," i.e. Utley narrating from late Lakota oral testimony (One Bull, White Bull), **not a contemporaneous transcript**.

**The 1876 Sun Dance. [A]** Rosebud Creek, traditionally Deer Medicine Rocks, about **5 June 1876**: fasting, more than a hundred pieces of flesh cut from the arms, the vision of soldiers falling into camp upside down like grasshoppers. ***Flagged date:*** chronology gave **1876-06-17**, which is the date of the **Battle of the Rosebud**, a different event. Wikipedia's Little Bighorn article puts the dance about three weeks before the battle. The journey uses **1876-06-05 (approx.)**.

**Gap, left open: the Rosebud, 17 June 1876. [R]** Crazy Horse fights Crook. **Wikipedia's article on the battle does not mention Sitting Bull at all.** General accounts place him in camp recovering from the Sun Dance wounds. The journey carries the stop and marks it a gap rather than filling it with the likelier of two guesses.

**[A]** The camp on the Little Bighorn, June 1876: about 1,800 lodges, Hunkpapa circle at the southern end, more than 10,000 people. **25 June**: Custer's scouts find it and Custer attacks with a divided regiment; Sitting Bull takes no direct fighting role and acts as the camp's senior authority while Crazy Horse and Gall lead the counterattack that destroys Custer's battalion.

## 5. Cedar Creek and the flight north, 1876 to 1877

**[A, retagged] Battle of Cedar Creek, 21 October 1876.** Geography flagged this unverifiable. Wikipedia documents it fully: Colonel Nelson A. Miles against Sitting Bull, a parley between the lines, talks broken off, fighting, and 400+ lodges surrendering six days later on 27 October.

**[R] Winter of 1876 to 1877**, Milk River and Frenchman's Creek borderlands. Carried as summary narrative; **no source fixes a camp or a day between October and May**. One stop, reconstruction stated.

**[A] May 1877**: crossing into the North-West Territories, remaining until 1881.

***Flagged, the Walsh meeting.*** Chronology dated the first meeting with NWMP Superintendent **James Morrow Walsh** to 1879. Walsh rode into the Hunkpapa camp near **Pinto Horse Butte in 1877**, weeks after the crossing, and not at Fort Walsh. **Fort Walsh** is where he met the **US Terry Commission in October 1877**, a separate encounter. The Fort Walsh article mentions neither. Both get separate stops so the conflation cannot recur.

**[R] Crowfoot.** The meeting and tobacco exchange fall somewhere in 1877 to 1881, undated in Wikipedia. Placed mid-exile without claiming a year.

**Gap, kept visible: Fort Qu'Appelle.** Secondary accounts mention Qu'Appelle in passing. The Wikipedia article on the town **does not mention Sitting Bull at all**, and no dated event attaches. The stop is retained and labelled in the journey as the weakest point on the map.

## 6. Surrender, imprisonment, agency, 1881 to 1889

**[A]** **19 July 1881, Fort Buford**: surrender with 186 followers, his son Crow Foot handing the Winchester to **Major David H. Brotherton**. **20 July**: he says he wishes to regard soldiers and the white race as friends, and asks who will teach his son "the new ways of the world." **26 August**: census taker **William T. Selwyn** records 12 in the immediate family and 41 families totalling 195 people. **September 1881 to May 1883**: steamboat with 172 followers to **Fort Randall**, twenty months as prisoners of war.

**[R]** "I wish it to be remembered that I was the last man of my tribe to surrender my rifle." Quoted in Wikipedia citing PBS and Utley. **The 1881 dispatch and Brotherton's report could not be reached**: PBS pages reported to host the text returned no matching content on fetch.

**[A, source re-identified]** "There was a Great Spirit who guided and controlled that battle. I could do nothing." The quotes lens called this untraceable folklore. It is not: verbatim in **Johnson, *Life of Sitting Bull* (1891), p. 154**, introduced "Speaking of the Custer fight, Sitting Bull said:" (verified by downloading archive.org `lifeofsittingbul00johnuoft` and grepping the OCR; a first grep missed it because the OCR hyphenates "con-/trolled" over a line break). The passage answers Low Dog's and Gall's published 1881 statements, dating the interview to **1881 or later, at Fort Buford or Fort Randall**, and **not** to the battlefield in 1876 as the pool placed it.

**[R] 1883**: the New York Times reports his Catholic baptism; agent **James McLaughlin** calls it erroneous. No baptismal record from either party.

***Flagged and deliberately unquoted: the Northern Pacific ceremony.*** **Splicing:** Hiltzik gives two separated fragments, not one utterance ("Sitting Bull declared in Lakota, 'I hate all White people.' ... 'You are thieves and liars. You have taken away our land and made us outcasts.'"); the pool ran them together. **Date contested:** Wikipedia says **1884** (Lazarus, p. 106), retellings **September 1883** at Bismarck; neither is asserted. **Substance disputed:** **Utley contends Sitting Bull did not curse his audiences** (1993, p. 263), and Hiltzik's "President Grant" detail is anachronistic. **The journey carries the stop and prints no quotation at it.**

**[A] 1884**: on **Alvaren Allen**'s "Sitting Bull Connection" tour he meets **Annie Oakley** in Minnesota, adopts her in the Lakota manner, names her Watanya Cicilla, "Little Sure Shot."

**[A, dates loosened] 1885**: about four months with **Buffalo Bill's Wild West**, roughly $50 a week for one ride around the arena, charging for autographs and photographs, giving much away. ***Flagged:*** chronology asserted April to August. Wikipedia gives the year without months; standard accounts have him joining in **June** and leaving in **October**. Both narrowings dropped. ***Correction:*** geography claimed the Wikipedia caption on the **Notman** Montreal photograph mis-dates it to 1895. **The caption reads 1885.** The lens invented an error the page does not contain.

**[R]** "The white man knows how to make everything, but he does not know how to distribute it." Dee Brown (1970). ***Flagged:*** the pool put it at Standing Rock c. 1889-1890. Wikiquote reproduces the passage in full: it is said **to Annie Oakley on the Wild West tour, c. 1885**.

**[A] 1889**: **Caroline Weldon** of Brooklyn, National Indian Defense Association, joins the Grand River compound with her son Christie. ***Flagged:*** geography wrote that he "returns to his cabin after the Wild West season" in 1889, misplacing the causality by four years. The season was 1885.

## 7. The Ghost Dance and the killing, 1890

**[A] October 1890**: the Ghost Dance, spread east from Nevada by the Paiute prophet **Wovoka**, reaches Standing Rock. Sitting Bull permits dancers at his camp, does not appear to dance himself, and is treated by the agency as instigator. **14 December**: McLaughlin drafts the arrest instructions and gives the work to the Indian police rather than the Army. **15 December, dawn**: about 39 police and four volunteers under **Lieutenant Bull Head** approach the Grand River house.

**[A, casualties corrected]** Catch-the-Bear shoots Bull Head; Bull Head fires into Sitting Bull's chest; **Red Tomahawk** shoots him in the head; death between 12 and 1 p.m. ***Flagged:*** chronology gave "six policemen and eight of Sitting Bull's men, including his son Crow Foot." Wikipedia has six police killed immediately and two more dead of wounds, and "Sitting Bull and seven of his supporters" killed at the site. So **eight police in total** and **seven supporters besides Sitting Bull himself**, one of them Crow Foot.

**[R]** The grey show horse said to have sat up at the sound of gunfire, trained by the Wild West to treat a shot as a cue. Widely retold, no contemporary documentary source. Carried in campa as legend.

**[A]** The body goes to **Fort Yates**, coffined by an Army carpenter, buried in the post cemetery unmarked. **[R]** Quicklime and the absence of ceremony come down through descendants as grievance, not through the Army as record.

**[R, text corrected]** "What treaty that the whites have kept has the red man broken? Not one. What treaty that the whites ever made with us red men have they kept? Not one." The pool carried a corrupted rendering ("What treaties that the whites have kept, that the red man broken?"). Genuine text at **Johnson 1891, p. 201**, quoted by **W. H. H. "Adirondack" Murray** in a memorial published in the *New York World* just after the killing (Johnson, pp. 195-203). **Caveat that travels with it:** a white memorialist's posthumous recollection, not a transcript, with no occasion or date for the original utterance.

**Doubtful quotations excluded.** "The love of possessions is a disease in them...", attributed to Eastman (Ohiyesa), *Indian Heroes and Great Chieftains* (1918): an archive.org full text was searched and neither "disease" nor "possessions" appeared in the Sitting Bull chapter that loaded, and **the chapter broke off mid-way on fetch**. "Because I am a red man...": Wikiquote cites Deloria, *God Is Red* (2003 ed.), **citing in turn Virginia Armstrong, *I Have Spoken* (1971)**, which the pool omitted; the chain ends in a popular anthology with no primary document behind it.

## 8. Afterlife, 1893 to the present

**[A] 1893**: the log cabin is dismantled and displayed at the **World's Columbian Exposition**, Chicago, while Ghost Dance survivors are still confined at Pine Ridge.

**[A] April 1953**: Lakota family members, backed by a Mobridge business drive, exhume remains from Fort Yates and rebury them near Mobridge under concrete; **Korczak Ziolkowski** carves the granite bust; National Register listing 8 November 2006. **[R]** Whether the correct remains were recovered is disputed; the exhumation is described as hasty and poorly documented. **North Dakota does not accept the removal**; a marker stands at the original grave and both states claim him. Not settled here.

**[A] 5 December 2007**: **Ernie LaPointe** retrieves a lock of hair and a pair of leggings from the Smithsonian **National Museum of Natural History** under NAGPRA; in 2009 he publishes *Sitting Bull: His Life and Legacy*, with a documentary in 2011.

**[A] 27 October 2021**: **Eske Willerslev**'s team publishes confirmation from degraded autosomal DNA in the hair that LaPointe and his three sisters are biological great-grandchildren. ***Flagged, journal wrong in the pool:*** the paper is ***Science Advances*** 7(44), eabh2013, **not *Science***. LaPointe and his sisters have since petitioned to reinter the remains at the **Little Bighorn**; no move has occurred.

**[A] September 1989**: a US Postal Service commemorative stamp. Recorded, given no stop, having no place. **[R] 2016 to 2017**: his name and image are invoked at the Standing Rock camps against the Dakota Access Pipeline; widely reported contemporaneous framing, not verified against a primary document.

**Photographic record. [A]** D. F. Barry photographed him repeatedly in the early 1880s. ***Flagged:*** the afterlife lens also credited "Fiske (1881)." Frank Bennett Fiske **was born in 1883**, two years after the supposed sitting. The 1881 Fort Randall portraits are credited to Orlando Scott Goff and to Bailey, Dix & Mead. "Fiske (1881)" dropped.

## 9. Interlocks with the existing atlas

**Genuine crossing, named in campa.** **Low Dog (Perro Bajo)**, QUEUE.md line 158, the Lakota chief beside Sitting Bull at the Little Bighorn. Same battle, same hour.

**Contemporaneity without contact, named in campa and flagged there as such.** **Julio Argentino Roca** (`julio_argentino_roca.journey.json`): the Conquista del Desierto, 1878 to 1885, running alongside the Canadian exile and the 1881 surrender, two continental campaigns of state conquest with no knowledge of each other; named at Fort Buford. **Gustave Eiffel** (`gustave_eiffel.journey.json`, line 373): Buffalo Bill's Wild West played the 1889 Exposition at the Champ de Mars with Lakota riders, but **Sitting Bull was not there** and never crossed to Europe, having left the show in 1885; named at Montreal, with the absence in the same sentence.

**Recorded, not used.** **Custer** and **Coronel Reno** are queued, not written; Custer is named in campa as the opposing commander, but no slug exists for him. **Lautaro**, **Mark Twain**, **Muybridge**, **Claude Levi-Strauss**, **Tschiffely**: thematic parallel or shared era, no crossing. Pin inheritance was offered for the **Kaaba**, **Temple Mount**, **Paris**, and **Buenos Aires**; none has any attested connection and none is used. The only pin inherited byte-identical is **Little Bighorn, 45.5646 / -107.4319**. Sitting Bull also appears twice in the queueing documents (QUEUE.md line 135, census line 428); the duplicates should be reconciled.
