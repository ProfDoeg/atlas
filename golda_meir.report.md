# Golda Meir (1898-1978), research report

*Written 2026-08-28 from a verified research pool (five lenses) passed through an adversarial verification stage whose corrections are incorporated below. Slug `golda_meir`. Journey file: `golda_meir.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or single-source claim not corroborated · **[C]** contradiction between reachable sources, recorded and not adjudicated.

The problem here is not scarcity but monoculture. One page, the MSU Denver chronology (`msudenver.edu/chronology/`), supplies a large share of the day-level dates in circulation about Meir, several of which no second reachable source confirms; where a claim rests on it alone, it is marked.

## 1. Kyiv, Pinsk, and the crossing (1898-1906)

- Born Goldie Mabovitch, 3 May 1898, Kyiv, 21 April Old Style. **[A: MSU Denver; Wikipedia]** **Corrected:** the geography lens put the birth in **Podil**, 50.4656/30.5236, but Wikipedia says only "downtown Kiev" and the sole documented address is the memorial plaque at **5-A Baseina Street** in Lypky, roughly 50.441/30.522, so that pin sat 2.5 km north. The file uses **50.4409, 30.5203**, the pool's own plaque value. The birth house is undocumented.
- Pinsk c. 1903, after Moshe Mabovitch leaves for America **[A: MSU Denver]**; landing at **Quebec**, 1906 **[A: Wikipedia]**, with ship, pier and month undocumented anywhere reachable. **Gap.**

## 2. Milwaukee and the runaway year (1906-1917)

- Fourth Street School 1906-1912, top of the class; the **American Young Sisters Society**, 1908, a hall rented and a public appeal made at ten for classmates' textbooks. **[A: Wikipedia, "Golda Meir School"; MSU Denver]** Renamed 1979, National Historic Landmark 1990. **Corrected twice over:** two lenses gave two wrong coordinates for one building, 43.0632/-87.9163 (1.4 km north) and 43.0389/-87.9065 (1.4 km south), against the school's own **43.05085, -87.9153917**.
- North Division High School, 1912-1915. **[A]** The article's 43.06678/-87.92497 belongs to the building opened **September 1978**, "just to the east of the old site"; the one Meir attended is not this and was not located. The file uses the modern pin and says so: a pin of convenience. **[R as to place]**
- Denver, **North High School, enrolled 17 February 1913** **[A: MSU Denver]**, coordinate corrected to **39.76007, -105.02261**, the pool's being 700 m off. The pool also wrongly calls the campus unverified: the 1911 Beaux-Arts building still forms the core of the school, so it is the one she entered.
- The **Korngold house, 1606-1608 Julian Street**, where she lodges with Sheyna and meets Morris Meyerson. **[R as to coordinate]**: it was moved to 1301 S Lipan Street on the Auraria campus in 1988, so the pin estimates the original lot, not where the museum stands.
- **Corrected span. [C]** MSU Denver says she attended the Wisconsin State Normal School "for a year" from 1916; UW-Milwaukee states she **graduated from the Milwaukee State Normal School in 1917**, which is why its library carries her name. The file reads 1916-1917. The building was not located. **Gap.**
- Marriage to Morris Meyerson, 24 December 1917, at the Mabovitch home, on her condition of emigration. **[A]** Address undocumented. **Gap.**

## 3. The Pocahontas and Merhavia (1921-1934)

- Sails New York to Naples on the **SS Pocahontas**, then overland to Tel Aviv, with Sheyna's family and Regina Hamburger; rejected, then accepted, at **Merhavia**, whose coordinate **32.60583, 35.30722** is confirmed exactly. **[A: Wikipedia]** Year only for both.
- **A flagged discrepancy that resolves.** The pool set Wikipedia's 1929 founding of Merhavia against a 1921 arrival and left it open. There are two settlements of the name: the **moshav**, established 1911 under Ottoman rule, and the **kibbutz**, founded beside it in 1929 by Hashomer Hatzair members from Galicia. She joined the earlier.
- Menachem 1924, Sara 1926, Jerusalem; brief return to Merhavia 1925 **[R: MSU Denver alone]**; secretary of **Mo'etzet Ha-Po'alot** and the move to Tel Aviv, 1928 **[A]**. No address for any of it. **Gap.**
- 1932-1934 United States emissary tour, doubling as treatment for Sara's kidney illness; Histadrut Executive on return. **[A: MSU Denver]** Itemized by city nowhere reachable, so the file pins New York.

## 4. Évian, the Agency, and the year of the state (1938-1948)

- **Évian Conference, 6-15 July 1938**, Hôtel Royal; she attends as the Jewish observer from Palestine, barred from speaking and from sitting with the delegates, and calls the capacity ludicrous. **[A: Wikipedia, "Évian Conference", quoting *My Life*]** **Corrected:** the pool's 46.4011/6.5906 is the lakefront town centre; the hotel is on the hillside 1.5 km south-southwest, and the file uses **46.393, 6.583**.
- **Operation Agatha, 29 June 1946**; acting then full head of the Jewish Agency's Political Department. **[A]** Coordinate corrected to the Agency headquarters, **48 King George Street, 31.7797, 35.2172**.
- **Cyprus, 1947, a split verdict.** The visit to interned detainees **is** attested by the Wikipedia article on Meir; the pool's verifier checked only the *Cyprus internment camps* article, which does not mention her, and under-rated it. But that article **contradicts** the persuasion narrative: a British-set quota of 750 certificates a month from November 1946, with special allocations decided **by the British** for pregnant women, nursing mothers and the elderly. Visit **[A]**; the persuasion **[R]**.
- **Naharayim, 17 November 1947**, first secret meeting with King Abdullah I. **[A: Wikipedia, "Naharayim", verbatim]** Corrects the pool's bare "1947".
- **Chicago, 22 January 1948**, and **over $30 million** raised across the tour. **[A: Wikipedia]** Upgrades what the pool tagged [R]. The **Council of Jewish Federations and Welfare Funds** as venue is uncorroborated and stays hedged; the **car accident** on the same mission is **[R: MSU Denver alone]**, undated and unplaced.
- Haifa after its capture, 6 May 1948 **[A]**; the disguised journey to **Amman, 10 May 1948** **[A]**; the Declaration signed at **Independence Hall, 16 Rothschild Boulevard, 14 May 1948**, one of 37 signatories and one of two women **[A: MSU Denver, and the Knesset's signatory list; the fetched Wikipedia excerpt for the hall did not name her]**.

## 5. Moscow, the ministries, the premiership (1948-1974)

- Moscow, minister plenipotentiary from September 1948, the Jewish Virtual Library dating the appointment to June. **[A]** The Rosh Hashanah crowd at the **Choral Synagogue** is attested **[A: Wikipedia]** at a figure near 50,000, higher than other tallies. **[C: estimates vary and are not fixed here.]**
- **Two corrections.** "Choral Synagogue, Bolshaya Bronnaya" **conflates two synagogues**: the Choral is at 10 Bolshoy Spasoglinishchevsky Lane, Bolshaya Bronnaya a separate building elsewhere. And **Rosh Hashanah 5709 fell 4-5 October 1948**, so the repeated "3 October" is likely the eve; the file uses 1948-10-04. A separate **Yom Kippur** appearance is nowhere documented and is dropped. **[R]**
- First Knesset **sworn in 14 February 1949**, not 12 February; Labour and National Insurance from 10 March 1949; service to 3 June 1974. **[A]**
- **One Knesset pin would misplace seventeen of those twenty-five years.** Givat Ram (31.7767, 35.2053, confirmed) was dedicated **30 August 1966**; before that the chamber sat at **Froumine House, King George Street**, from 13 March 1950, earlier at the Jewish Agency and in Tel Aviv. So the file pins 1949 at the **Agency**, the 1957 grenade at an approximation for **Froumine House** (31.7789, 35.2178, **[R]**), and only 1969, 1974 and 1977 at Givat Ram.
- **The 1957 grenade.** The pool hedged on whether Meir was in the chamber. She was: Wikipedia records her **foot slightly injured** on 29 October 1957, alongside Ben-Gurion. **[A]**
- **Tel Aviv mayoralty, summer 1955**, lost **by two votes** **[A: Wikipedia, sharper than the pool]**. The pool pinned the city hall on Rabin Square, opened **1965**; in 1955 the council sat on **Bialik Square**, approx. 32.0716, 34.7727. Foreign Minister **18 June 1956 to 12 January 1966**, leaving Labour the day after taking it; Meyerson becomes Meir. **[A]**
- **Sèvres, 22-24 October 1956.** The pool put the secret tripartite coordination in "Sinai / Paris", conflating planning venue with theatre of war; it was a villa at **Sèvres**. **[A as to the conference]** That **Meir was in that room** rests on MSU Denver's general statement that she participated, no reachable source itemizing the delegation. **[R]**
- **The 1958 letter to Katriel Katz**, the pool's strongest primary document: Meir asking Israel's ambassador in Warsaw whether the Poles can be told that Israel wishes to institute **selection in aliyah**, "because we cannot continue accepting sick and handicapped people". **[A: JTA, 9 December 2009, reproducing the text from Rudnicki's discovery]** Its own stop, its own quote field, unsoftened.
- **Lymphoma: [C].** MSU Denver gives 1963, a separately reported account late 1965. No primary medical record is reachable, and the file carries both.
- Prime Minister **17 March 1969** after Eshkol's death on 26 February **[A]**; Black Panthers meeting **13 April 1971** **[A: MSU Denver]**, venue unfixed, the remark attributed to her afterwards **not** verified against a transcript **[R]**.
- **The pool's 1972 entry is a chronology defect independent of accuracy:** it bundles a Socialist International deputy chairmanship, Munich and Sheyna Korngold's death into one line. Munich **[A]** and Sheyna's death **[A: Wikipedia gives 1889-1972]** survive; the Socialist International clause appears in no reachable source and is dropped **[R]**.
- Nixon meetings **1969** and **1 March 1973**, not 28 February **[A]**. **Vienna redated:** the Schönau seizure was 28 September 1973 and Kreisky met her "a few days later", so **early October**, commonly 2 October, not September. **[A as to month, R as to day]**

## 6. The war, the resignation, the hill (1973-1978)

- War opens **6 October 1973**. **[A]** The mobilisation-versus-pre-emption decision is extensively published, but the FRUS and National Security Archive documents cited for the detail were **not independently fetched**. **[A in outline, R as to any particular exchange.]**
- **The unresolved venue. [C]** The pool put the morning consultations with Dayan and Elazar at the **Prime Minister's residence in Jerusalem**, with a coordinate 800 m off even for that (Beit Aghion is at Balfour and Smolenskin, approx. 31.7736, 35.2151), while the verification lens places them **conventionally at her office in the Kirya, Tel Aviv**. Neither could be confirmed. The file pins the Kirya, approx. 32.074, 34.790, and says so in `date_confidence`.
- **The nuclear legend, kept apart from the record.** That Meir ordered thirteen bombs armed and threatened Nixon was popularised by a 1976 *Time* piece and by Seymour Hersh, and is **[R]**: neither figure nor ultimatum appears in any primary document fetched. The National Security Archive states instead that she **rejected** advice urging nuclear use and ordered **Jericho missiles armed as a signal to Washington** **[A]**, and that the American **DEFCON III alert of 24 October answered Brezhnev's letter, not any Israeli nuclear action** **[A]**. *Golda's Balcony* (2003) dramatises the legend and sits in `suggested_refs` as the artefact it is.
- **Resignation dates corrected by a day each:** announcement **11 April 1974**, not 10 April; office left **3 June 1974**, not 4 June, Rabin's government taking office that day. **[A]** *My Life*, 1975, a bestseller **[A]**.
- **Sadat, 21 November 1977: the pool misdescribes the occasion twice.** The visit ran 19-21 November and his **Knesset address was on 20 November**; Meir spoke on **21 November** in the continued debate, not during his address. Both pool entries collapse the two days.
- **Death, 8 December 1978, West Jerusalem, of lymphoma. [A]** Three specifics are **[R: MSU Denver alone]**: hospitalisation on 7 December, the hour of 4.30 p.m., and **Hadassah Ein Kerem** as the hospital; the NYT and JTA fetches failed, so none could be closed. **The worst coordinate in the pool was here:** Hadassah Ein Kerem as 31.7639/35.2278, **7 km off** and on the wrong side of the city. Ein Karem is 31.76806/35.16222 with the centre on a hilltop above, approx. **31.766, 35.150**, which the file uses; the *Hadassah Medical Center* infobox coordinate is the **Mount Scopus** campus.
- **Burial, Mount Herzl**, Great Leaders of the Nation plot, **31.77389, 35.18056** **[A]**; the pool's afterlife lens gave 31.7767/35.1819, 330 m from its own geography lens. The **12 December** date is **[R: MSU Denver alone]**. Plot neighbours attested: Eshkol, Rabin, Shazar, Herzog, Peres, Sprinzak, Kaplan, Kollek.

## Quotations

Nine quotes are carried in the journey file. The quotation lens caught four fidelity problems, all honoured here.

1. **"One's duty was one duty – and it had nothing to do with justice."** The source uses an **en dash**; the pool silently substituted a comma, then described that as removing an em dash. The dash is restored. **[A as to words]** The occasion is not given by the source, so `quote_source` says so rather than letting the Haifa stop imply it.
2. **"I will not write about my private life. I will not settle political or other scores with anyone."** The pool appended a **trailing ellipsis that is not in the source**, and its note claiming otherwise is false. Removed.
3. **"We have been waiting for 2,000 years. Is that hurrying?"** MSU Denver places this at the **secret meeting in Amman**, 1948; the pool's "Naharayim / near Amman" is unsupported by its own cited source. **[C, recorded not resolved:** she met Abdullah at Naharayim in 1947 and Amman in 1948, and accounts split over which produced the line.**]**
4. **The Fallaci quote** had an **ellipsis silently dropped**, joining two separated passages into one statement. **[R]** on provenance regardless, and **not used**.

Excluded: **"Peace will come when the Arabs will love their children more than they hate us"** **[R]**, listed Disputed on Wikiquote, no primary source found by Rachlin, treated as sourceless by Haaretz. **"We can forgive the Arabs for killing our children…"** **[R]**, and worse, the pool's wording matches no source verbatim: it is a hybrid of Syrkin's *A Land of Our Own* (1973, p. 242), which has "sons" not "children", and an Anti-Defamation League advertisement in *The Hollywood Reporter*. **"We don't thrive on military acts…"** (*Vogue*, 1969) and **"I am a Palestinian…"** (Thames Television, 1970), both **[R]**, Wikiquote only. The June 1969 *Sunday Times* line on there being "no such thing as Palestinians" is the reverse case, over-hedged by the pool: it ran on **15 June 1969** and its existence is not in doubt, only its interpretation and the truncation around it. It carries no stop because no stop sits at that interview.
