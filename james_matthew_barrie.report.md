# James Matthew Barrie (1860-1937): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `james_matthew_barrie`. Subject: Sir James Matthew Barrie, 1st Baronet, OM, novelist and playwright, born Kirriemuir, died London.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference: carried because it is widely repeated or structurally probable, not because a document was seen. Contradictions are printed as contradictions and left standing. Coordinate corrections are collected in one section at the end.

---

## I. Kirriemuir and the dead brother, 1860-1873

Barrie is born 9 May 1860 at 9 Brechin Road, Kirriemuir, Angus, ninth of the ten children of David Barrie, a handloom weaver, and Margaret Ogilvy, in a conservative Calvinist household; the cottage survives as a museum in the care of the National Trust for Scotland **[A: Wikipedia, "J. M. Barrie"; NTS, Barrie's Birthplace]**.

In 1867 the elder brother David, their mother's favourite, dies in an ice-skating accident the day before his fourteenth birthday. James, then six, attempts to fill the vacancy by imitating him **[A]**. His account of entering his mother's darkened room is printed in *Margaret Ogilvy* (1896), chapter I **[A: Wikisource]**.

**Gap.** The exact date of David's death is not established: the source gives the year and the phrase "the day before his fourteenth birthday" and nothing more, and no parish or newspaper record was reached. The stop is dated 1867-01-01 and says so in `date_confidence`. The theatricals in the wash-house behind the cottage rest on Barrie's memoir rather than any external record **[A as to his testimony; R as to date]**; the NTS presents the outbuilding as the site of his first plays.

## II. Schooling, 1868-1882

Glasgow Academy c. 1868, where his elder siblings Alexander and Mary Ann are already established; Forfar Academy c. 1870; Dumfries Academy at fourteen in 1874, where he reads Ballantyne and Fenimore Cooper and begins writing and staging plays with friends; the University of Edinburgh 1878-1882, M.A. conferred 21 April 1882 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** The chronology lens dates the Dumfries enrolment to 1874, the geography lens gives 1873-1878, and both derive from the same article. The journey file uses 1873-01-01 for Moat Brae and flags the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

**Anachronism flagged and acted on.** The pool's Glasgow Academy pin points at the modern Colebrooke Street site, but the Academy only moved there in **1878**; the boy Barrie of 1868 attended the original Elmbank Street building, and that is the coordinate used **[A as to the move; R as to the precise point]**. Same problem, smaller, at Forfar: the Turfbeg campus is twentieth-century, while the 1870 school stood in the town centre. The corrected modern pin is used and the caveat is carried in the stop.

**Moat Brae.** The garden of 101 George Street, Dumfries, is the site of the pirate games Barrie later named as the seed of *Peter Pan*; the quotation matches Wikipedia, "Moat Brae", verbatim **[A]**. **Status correction:** an earlier draft called it "now the National Centre for Children's Literature". That centre opened June 2019 and **closed in August 2024** for financial reasons, the property going to bid that September. The present tense has been removed **[A]**.

## III. Journalism and Thrums, 1883-1891

Barrie works for the Nottingham Journal for about a year and a half after leaving university, then from 1884 submits sketches of Kirriemuir life, drawn from his mother's recollections, to the St James's Gazette in London. *Auld Licht Idylls* (1888), *A Window in Thrums* (1889), and *The Little Minister* (1891) follow; "Thrums" is the fictionalised Kirriemuir **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Factual error corrected.** The pool asserted that "the Nottingham Journal folds" and that Barrie therefore went home. The paper published into the 1950s: what ended was Barrie's engagement, not the Journal **[A: negative check against its publication history]**.

## IV. The theatre and the marriage, 1891-1896

**Contradiction, printed rather than resolved.** The pool's chronology lens dates the Ansell meeting to 1891, and its geography lens calls *Walker, London* his first staged London play, opening at Toole's Theatre in 1891. Wikipedia, "J. M. Barrie" and "Toole's Theatre", give a different account: his first London play is *Ibsen's Ghost, or Toole Up-to-Date* (1891), while *Walker, London* is his **third**, opened **1892**, ran 497 performances, and is the production that introduces him to Mary Ansell. The journey file follows Wikipedia and records the disagreement in the stop rather than erasing it **[A for the Wikipedia reading; the 1891 datings are marked as the pool's error]**.

**Toole's Theatre location.** The theatre stood on **William IV Street**, Westminster, not "King William Street" (a different street, in the City). The pool's coordinates were near William IV Street already; only the label was wrong **[A]**.

Barrie marries Mary Ansell at Kirriemuir on 9 July 1894 **[A]**. Biographers report the marriage as unconsummated; there are no children **[R: reported, not documented]**. In 1895 the couple buy a house on Gloucester Road; *Margaret Ogilvy* and *Sentimental Tommy* appear in 1896 **[A]**.

**Not asserted.** An early draft of the Gloucester Road campa claimed Margaret Ogilvy died in 1895. No date for her death is anywhere in the pool, and the claim has been struck rather than sourced from memory. Nico Llewelyn Davies's remark that "Uncle Jim" never experienced "a stirring in the undergrowth" for anyone reaches this pool at two removes, through Birkin as cited in Wikipedia; Birkin was **not** directly consulted **[A as a quotation carried by Wikipedia; R as to its original context]**.

## V. Kensington Gardens and the Llewelyn Davies boys, 1897-1907

Barrie meets the family while walking his Saint Bernard, Porthos, in Kensington Gardens in 1897, befriending George and Jack first **[A: Wikipedia; "Llewelyn Davies boys"]**. Birth years: George 1893, Peter 1897, Michael 1900, Nico 1903 **[A]**. Black Lake Cottage, Tilford, is leased from about 1900, and the boys are photographed there for the private album *The Boy Castaways of Black Lake Island* **[A]**. The family moves to Egerton House, Berkhamsted, in 1904; Arthur Llewelyn Davies dies of cancer in 1907 **[A]**.

Barrie takes rooms at 100 Bayswater Road overlooking the Gardens. **District correction:** the pool labelled this "South Kensington", but the address is in **Bayswater**, on the north side of the Gardens; the coordinates were right and only the label was wrong. The tenancy ran to about 1909, not 1902 **[A]**. Peter Pan appears in print for the first time in *The Little White Bird* (1902); *The Admirable Crichton* is staged the same year **[A]**.

## VI. Peter Pan on stage, in print, and in bronze, 1904-1913

*Peter Pan; or, the Boy Who Wouldn't Grow Up* premieres at the Duke of York's Theatre on 27 December 1904, with Nina Boucicault as the first Peter **[A]**. Shaw's description survives here only as a lowercase fragment inside Wikipedia's own prose, with no footnote and no primary text located: "ostensibly a holiday entertainment for children but really a play for grown-up people". It is used mid-sentence and lowercase in the campa, **not** as a standalone Shaw sentence **[R: secondhand]**.

**Broadway.** Wikipedia says only "A Broadway production was mounted in 1905" at the Empire with Maude Adams; it gives no date and says nothing about Barrie attending **[A for the production; R for the 6 November 1905 date and for his absence]**.

*Peter and Wendy* appears in 1911 **[A]**. Barrie divorces Mary Ansell in October 1909 after her affair with Gilbert Cannan, and moves to 3 Adelphi Terrace, third floor, then from 1917 the fourth, keeping the flat for life **[A: Wikipedia; "Adelphi, London"]**. Sylvia Llewelyn Davies dies in 1910; Barrie becomes guardian to the orphaned brothers alongside their nurse **[A as to the fact]**, though the exact arrangement is described differently across accounts **[R: contested]**.

**The Frampton statue.** Installed in Kensington Gardens without permit overnight and unveiled on May Morning, 30 April 1912, so that children would believe fairies had placed it; Grade II* listed from 1970; six full-size replicas follow between 1924 and 1929 (Brussels, St John's, Liverpool, Perth, Toronto, Camden NJ) **[A: Wikipedia, "Peter Pan statue"]**. Barrie is reported disappointed, complaining that it "doesn't show the devil in Peter"; Frampton had meant to work from photographs of Michael Llewelyn Davies in the costume but used another child **[A]**.

**Scott.** Barrie is one of the seven people to whom Robert Falcon Scott wrote in the final hours of his life, and was godfather to Scott's son Peter **[A]**. Two problems are not papered over: the claim that Barrie wrote the introduction to *Scott's Last Expedition* (1913) is **unverified**, the first edition carrying an introduction by Sir Clements Markham, so it is dropped rather than repeated; and no surveyed coordinate for the final camp exists in anything reached **[A for the 19 March 1912 camp date; R for any coordinate]**.

Barrie is created a baronet by George V, 14 June 1913 **[A]**. The Buckingham Palace stop is tagged **[R]**: the honour is attested, the investiture is not described anywhere reached.

## VII. The war and the water, 1915-1922

George Llewelyn Davies is killed by a German sniper at St Eloi, near Ypres, on **15 March 1915**, with the King's Royal Rifle Corps **[A: Wikipedia, "George Llewelyn Davies"]**. An afterlife item in the pool claimed "the source consulted gives only the year"; that limitation was false and the specifics were retrieved. His remains, first buried near where he fell, were later moved to Voormezeele Enclosure No. 3 **[A]**. **Gap:** the reburial date is not established, so the stop carries the date of his death and says so.

Michael Llewelyn Davies drowns with Rupert Buxton at Sandford Lasher, above Oxford, on **19 May 1921** **[A: Wikipedia, "Michael Llewelyn Davies"]**. A second pool item claimed the exact date was unavailable and hedged around "press coverage appears around 21 May"; that hedge was unnecessary and has been removed.

**Contradiction, left standing.** The coroner found that Davies drowned accidentally and that Buxton drowned trying to save him. Family members later voiced suspicion of a suicide pact. The record does not resolve this and neither does the journey file **[A for the verdict; R for the pact]**.

Michael is buried in St John-at-Hampstead churchyard **[A]**; the funeral date is not recorded anywhere reached, so the stop carries the date of the drowning. Barrie is Rector of St Andrews 1919-1922 and delivers the address *Courage* on 3 May 1922 **[A: Wikipedia; Wikisource]**.

**Quotation discipline.** One *Courage* quotation in the pool carried an unmarked elision: the dashed clause "you remember how Stevenson thumped them" had been silently dropped from "The artistic callings... are merely doing what you are clamorous to be at". It is restored in full in the journey file **[A: Wikisource]**.

## VIII. Late honours, 1922-1930

- Order of Merit, New Year Honours, January 1922 **[A]**.
- 1922: visits University College Dundee with Earl Haig **to open its new playing fields** **[A]**. The pool's vague framing ("in an official capacity connected to his rectorship") was invented around the actual occasion and has been replaced with it.
- 1920s: leases Eilean Shona in Loch Moidart; writes the screenplay for the 1924 *Peter Pan* film and most of *A Kiss for Cinderella* there **[A]**. That the island colours the setting of *Mary Rose* is **[R]**, a widely held identification, not a statement by Barrie.
- April 1929: gives the Peter Pan copyright to Great Ormond Street Hospital, asking that the income not be disclosed **[A]**. 1930: Chancellor of Edinburgh, held to his death, and Freedom of Kirriemuir, 7 June **[A]**.
- **Not carried as stops, for length:** Stanway House, Gloucestershire, where he was a regular guest after the war and paid for the cricket pavilion **[A]**; Glen Prosen, Angus, as the landscape behind *Farewell, Miss Julie Logan* **[R]**. Vailima, Samoa, **is** carried, tagged as tradition: the Stevenson invitation and Barrie's non-appearance are **[R]**, unverified, but a journey that never happened is still a fact about a geography.
- The Allahakbarries cricket club, named in the Dundee campa, is general biographical knowledge and was **not** in the pool: the file's one piece of colour without a pool source, flagged rather than passed off.

## IX. Death and afterlife, 1937-2006

Barrie dies of pneumonia at a nursing home in Manchester Street, Marylebone, on **19 June 1937**, aged seventy-seven, and is buried at Kirriemuir Cemetery beside his parents and two of his siblings **[A]**. "Having declined burial in Westminster Abbey" is a widely repeated tradition that does not appear in the article consulted; the journey file names it as tradition and asserts nothing.

**Estate.** Barrie left the bulk of his estate to his secretary, Lady Cynthia Asquith, **excluding the rights to all Peter Pan works**, which had gone to Great Ormond Street in 1929 **[A]**. The pool's original phrasing omitted the exclusion and was misleading.

**Copyright.** UK copyright expired in 1987, fifty years after death; the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 then granted Great Ormond Street a perpetual UK right to royalties on public performance or publication, called by the Act's commentators the only perpetual copyright-adjacent right in the UK besides the King James Bible. The 1996 EU term extension restored full copyright; when that lapsed again in 2007 the perpetual right resumed as the hospital's sole claim **[A: Wikipedia, "Great Ormond Street Hospital"; "Peter and Wendy"]**.

**Peter Llewelyn Davies.** Two errors in the pool, both corrected. He was **not** the last surviving of the five brothers: Nico lived 1903-1980 and outlived him by twenty years **[A]**. And the date and place were available: he threw himself under a train arriving at **Sloane Square** Underground station on **5 April 1960**, the coroner's jury finding he killed himself "while the balance of his mind was disturbed" **[A: Wikipedia, "Peter Llewelyn Davies"]**.

*Peter Pan in Scarlet* by Geraldine McCaughrean, authorised by the hospital, is published in 2006 **[A]**. That she was chosen through a competition among invited authors is reported elsewhere and is **not** in the source consulted **[R]**. The pool's "at least 27 languages" is a count of Wikipedia language links, an artefact of the counting, and is not used.

## Contradictions carried, not adjudicated

Each is flagged in place in the section above and, where it bears on a stop, in that stop's `date_confidence`.

1. **First London play and the Ansell meeting** (III); **Dumfries dates**, 1874 versus 1873-1878 (II); **Glasgow Academy site**, right institution, wrong building (II).
2. **Michael's drowning.** Coroner's accidental verdict versus family suspicion of a suicide pact.
3. **The guardianship.** Nothing reached settles Barrie's actual authority after Sylvia's death, nor the story about her will and the nurse Mary Hodgson. Relatedly, **which boy is Peter Pan**: Michael is called the favourite and closest model, Peter carried the name and the burden.
4. **Barrie's last weeks.** Adelphi Terrace is attested as his home to 1937, the death at a Marylebone nursing home; whether and when he was moved is not reconciled.
