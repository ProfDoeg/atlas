# Claude Lévi-Strauss (1908-2009): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `claude_levi_strauss`. Journey file: `claude_levi_strauss.journey.json`.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference · contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

Method note. Built from a four-lens research pool (chronology, geography, quotations, afterlife) plus a corpus-interlock pass, then run through adversarial verification, which overturned a number of first-pass findings. Where it did, the corrected value is given and the discarded one preserved beside it: a silently corrected coordinate is indistinguishable from a coordinate nobody checked. Several corrections are coordinate errors of 500 m to 50 km.

## 1. Brussels and the sixteenth arrondissement, 1908-1925

Born 28 November 1908 in Brussels to a French-Jewish family; the father is a portrait painter; the parents are in Belgium temporarily and the family returns to Paris soon after **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia]**.

**Birth name corrected.** The pool's first pass gave "Gustave Claude Lévi", with the hyphen adopted later. Wrong. English Wikipedia's lead and infobox give **Gustave Claude Lévi-Strauss**, the compound surname already the family's; fr.wikipedia names the father **Raymond Lévi-Strauss** **[A]**. The "Lévi" reading is struck.

**Rue Claude-Lorrain.** The family settles "on a street of the 16th arrondissement named after the artist Claude Lorrain" **[A: en.wikipedia]**. That street runs from 82 rue Boileau to 79 rue Michel-Ange, quartier d'Auteuil, at **48.8412, 2.2610** **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Rue Claude-Lorrain"]**. The pool's 48.8637, 2.2769 lies 2.7 km northeast in Passy and is struck.

**Versailles, 1914-1918.** With the father mobilized, mother and son shelter with the maternal grandfather, rabbi of Versailles **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia, "Synagogue de Versailles"]**. The synagogue is at 10 rue Albert Joly, **48.8105, 2.1361**; the pool's 48.8021, 2.1287 is ~1.1 km off and is struck. The pool's gloss that these years left him estranged from observance while teaching him to see ritual as system is **[R]**, unsourced, and is not asserted in the journey.

**Lycée Janson-de-Sailly, 1918-1925**, baccalauréat around 16 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The lycée is at 106 rue de la Pompe, **48.8653, 2.2800** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; the pool's 48.8663, 2.2727 is ~550 m too far west and is struck.

## 2. Condorcet, the Sorbonne, the agrégation, 1925-1931

**Condorcet.** en.wikipedia: "Lycée Condorcet (1925-1926), preparation for École normale supérieure" **[A]**. The pool's 1925-1927 is **struck**: one year.

**A dropped claim.** The pool asserted he declined to sit the ENS entrance examination and so stayed outside the normalien establishment. Neither Wikipedia edition says this. **Dropped as unsupported**; the journey says only that the record is silent.

**Sorbonne 1926-1931**, law and philosophy **[A: en.wikipedia, "from 1926"]**; coordinates **48.848, 2.343** verified. The pool's 1927 start is corrected. **Agrégation de philosophie 1931, placed third, aged 22 [A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia].**

**Marx, Freud, geology.** That he names these three is **[A]**; the causal reading built on them is **[R]**. QUEUE.md lines 62 and 252 group him with Lacan, Barthes, Foucault, and Deleuze, with Lacan's "return to Freud" as the connective tissue **[A: QUEUE.md]**.

## 3. The provincial lycées, 1932-1934

Marries the ethnologist **Dina Dreyfus** in 1932 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction between the pool's own lenses, flagged and not resolved.** Chronology lens: 1932-1935 across Mont-de-Marsan and Laon. Geography lens: Mont-de-Marsan 1931-1933, Laon 1933-1934. fr.wikipedia: *"Après deux ans d'enseignement de la philosophie au lycée Victor-Duruy de Mont-de-Marsan et au lycée de Laon..."*, i.e. **two years in total across both** **[A]**, which after the 1931 agrégation puts the block at roughly 1932-1934. Neither lens matches. The journey uses c. 1932 and c. 1933 and states the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

- Lycée Victor-Duruy, Mont-de-Marsan **43.8897, -0.4988** **[A for the school; coordinates match the town]**
- Lycée de Laon **49.5642, 3.6222** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**

Célestin Bouglé, at the École normale, proposes him for the sociology chair at the new University of São Paulo **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; no date for the offer is given anywhere consulted.

## 4. Brazil: the chair and the two expeditions, 1935-1939

**Departure 1935 [A for appointment and year; R for the port].** The pool places embarkation at Marseille, 43.2965, 5.3698, as the ordinary Mediterranean departure for South America. No source consulted names the port. **Santos, -23.9608, -46.3336 [R]**: reconstruction from 1930s shipping routes.

**Buenos Aires.** Pin **-34.6037, -58.3816** inherited byte-identical from `borges.journey.json` and `victoria_ocampo.journey.json`. A call on the Río de la Plata is **[R]** steamer-route inference, documented nowhere. Carried as an inherited pin, not as a claim that he landed.

**University of São Paulo 1935-1939 [A: en.wikipedia].** Coordinate caveat: the pool's -23.5505, -46.6333 is the city centre, not the Cidade Universitária campus at ≈-23.5595, -46.7313. In 1935 the new Faculdade de Filosofia was downtown, so the point is defensible and is retained, footnoted in the journey.

**First mission 1935-1936, Caduveo and Bororo, Mato Grosso [A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, "Tristes Tropiques"].** No village coordinates survive in the secondary record; the Caduveo point (-21, -56.9, Bodoquena) and Bororo point (-16, -54.9, Rio Vermelho) are regional approximations, tagged as such. Cuiabá (-15.6014, -56.0979) as staging town is **[R]**.

**Second expedition: dating contradiction, left open.** en.wikipedia gives **1938**; fr.wikipedia gives **1938-1939**. Both recorded; the journey dates the leg 1938 and states the conflict.

- Utiariti, Rondon telegraph line, Papagaio river, -13.05, -58.25 **[R]**, reconstructed from the line's geography; no source names it for this trip.
- Nambikwara territory, Serra do Norte, -12.74, -60.15 **[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, "Nambikwara people"]**. Site of the "writing lesson".
- Mundé and Tupi-Kawahib, Ji-Paraná region, Rondônia, -10.88, -61.95 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Return 1939 [A].** The pool's Rio de Janeiro / Museu Nacional waypoint is **[R]** logistics inference and was dropped from the journey rather than carried as a stop.

## 5. War, the racial laws, the ship, 1939-1941

**Maginot Line 1939-1940 [A for the service, fr.wikipedia; R for the sector].** The pool's 49.1, 6.6 is a general position on the Lorraine line; the posting is unspecified and the description of the duty as liaison work is **[R]**.

**Montpellier, October 1940.** Dismissed under Vichy anti-Jewish law **[A: en.wikipedia; fr.wikipedia gives October 1940]**. The school identification holds: fr.wikipedia's "Lycée Joffre" lists him among its teachers alongside Marc Bloch **[A]**. Coordinates corrected to **43.6115, 3.8840**; the pool's 43.6086, 3.8734 is ~900 m off.

**Marseille, 24 March 1941.** Boards the *Capitaine Paul-Lemerle* with some 350 refugees, among them André Breton, Victor Serge, Anna Seghers **[A: en.wikipedia, "André Breton"; standard record of the voyage]**. The Varian Fry network assists; the visa rests on a New School position **[A]**.

**Camp du Lazaret, Fort-de-France, April 1941, 14.5589, -61.0463 [A]:** weeks of internment under guard. **San Juan, Puerto Rico, 1941, 18.4655, -66.1057 [A: en.wikipedia]:** detained and questioned by the FBI, then cleared for New York. No date within the year.

## 6. New York, the exile university, 1941-1948

**New School for Social Research, 40.7355, -73.9975 [A: fr.wikipedia]**, within the University in Exile milieu. Meets Roman Jakobson in New York in 1942; the encounter shapes the structural method **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Conflation corrected.** The pool housed the École libre des hautes études inside the New School. It was not: per fr.wikipedia, "École libre des hautes études", the ELHE stood at **24 West 12th Street**, *"non loin des bâtiments de la New School"*, independent and Rockefeller-financed **[A]**. He is a founding member in 1942 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The journey gives it a separate stop; the coordinate (≈40.7345, -73.9965) is **[R]** geocoding from the street number.

**Embassy posting: contradiction, chronology lens loses.** en.wikipedia: "a brief stint from **1946 to 1947** as a cultural attaché to the French embassy in Washington, DC". fr.wikipedia has him returning to the United States **in 1945** to take up the post. The pool's chronology lens gave 1944-1945 and is **struck**; its geography lens gave 1946-1947. Window 1945/46-1947. Washington **38.9186, -77.0367 [A]**.

Marries **Rose-Marie Ullmo** 1946; son **Laurent** born 1947 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **1948:** state doctorate at the Sorbonne on two theses **[A: en.wikipedia]** and permanent return to France **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

## 7. Kinship, the Indus, *Tristes Tropiques*, 1949-1958

**1949.** *Les Structures élémentaires de la parenté*; sous-directeur of the **Musée de l'Homme**, Palais de Chaillot, **48.8628, 2.2883** **[A: en.wikipedia for the book; fr.wikipedia for both]**.

**1950.** Directeur d'études, École pratique des hautes études, fifth section, chair renamed "Comparative Religion of Non-Literate Peoples" **[chair renaming A: en.wikipedia; the 1950 date is tagged R in the pool, from fr.wikipedia]**.

**The South Asian journey, c. 1950: the weakest chronology in the dossier.** No source consulted dates any leg. UNESCO sponsorship and "c. 1950" are the pool's estimate.

- Karachi **24.8607, 67.0011 [R]**, general chronology of UNESCO missions.
- Mohenjo-daro **27.3294, 68.1382** is **[A]** as a site named in the book: en.wikipedia confirms "Mohenjo-daro and Harappa are specifically mentioned in Chapter 14 comparisons". **The pool's reading is inverted and corrected.** It had him admiring the antiquity of the Indus grid, "finding structure beneath ruin". His point is bleak: the uniform grid prefigures mass, standardized, industrial modernity in the West. The gloss is rewritten in the journey.
- Lahore **31.5905, 74.3151 [R]**, from the book's Pakistan content. Taxila **33.7463, 72.7853 [R]** and Calcutta **22.5726, 88.3639 [R]**, both dropped from the journey for length.
- Chittagong Hill Tracts **22.6533, 92.1729**: tagged **[A]** by the pool, **downgraded to [R]** and dropped. The cited source says only that Part 4 touches "what is now Pakistan and Bangladesh".

**1954** marries **Monique Roman** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **1955** *Tristes Tropiques*, Librairie Plon **[A]**; abridged English by Russell, *A World on the Wane*, 1961 **[A]**; complete Weightman translation under the original title, 1973 **[A, upgraded from the pool's [R]: en.wikipedia does name it, contrary to the pool's note]**. **1956** Royal Netherlands Academy **[A]**. **1958** *Anthropologie structurale* **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

## 8. The Collège de France, 1959-1982

**1959.** Chair of Social Anthropology, place Marcelin Berthelot, **48.8492, 2.3453** **[A]**. The afterlife lens gives 48.8496, 2.3453 for the same building, a difference of metres; the journey uses the former throughout.

**1960** Laboratoire d'anthropologie sociale **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; American Philosophical Society **[A]**. **1961** co-founds *L'Homme* with Benveniste and Gourou **[A: fr.wikipedia]**, folded into the Collège de France campa rather than given a stop. **1962** *La Pensée sauvage* **[A]**. **1967** U.S. National Academy of Sciences **[A]**.

**1964-1971.** *Mythologiques* in four volumes **[A: fr.wikipedia gives 1964-1971; en.wikipedia the looser 1960s-1971]**. English, under *Introduction to a Science of Mythology*: *The Raw and the Cooked* (1969), *From Honey to Ashes* (1973), *The Origin of Table Manners* (1978), *The Naked Man* (1981) **[A, upgraded from the pool's [R]: en.wikipedia, "The Raw and the Cooked"]**.

**1973: date corrected.** The pool gave the election as **14 May 1973** from en.wikipedia. The Académie's own register gives **"le 24 mai 1973"**, reception under the cupola **27 June 1974**, fauteuil 29, succeeding Henry de Montherlant **[A: academie-francaise.fr]**; fr.wikipedia gives only "mai 1973". The 14 May reading is struck, and the pool's conflation of election with reception is separated in the journey. Institut de France, quai de Conti, **48.8566, 2.3376 [A]**. The **Erasmus Prize** the same year, sole laureate, is separately confirmed **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Japan, 1977 onward [R].** Repeated later visits and a late book of essays rest on general biographical knowledge, unconfirmed in any source reached. Tokyo 35.6762, 139.6503, carried with the hedge stated.

**1982** retirement from the chair **[A]**. **2003** Meister Eckhart Prize **[A]**; honorary doctorates at Oxford, Harvard, Yale, Columbia are listed without dates **[A: en.wikipedia]**, which also mentions the 1986 International Nonino Prize.

## 9. The hundredth year and after, 2008-2009

**May 2008.** Gallimard publishes *Oeuvres* in the Bibliothèque de la Pléiade, one of very few living authors admitted, the selection made by him **[A: en.wikipedia and fr.wikipedia; the self-selection detail is on the French page only]**.

**28 November 2008.** Turns 100, first member of the Académie française to do so **[A]**. Centenary exhibitions at the Musée du Quai Branly (**48.8606, 2.2967**) and the Bibliothèque nationale, and a visit from President Sarkozy **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**April 2009, doyen of the Académie** on Druon's death: tagged **[A]** by the pool, but verification **could not confirm it in any source reached**. Recorded as **[R]**, kept out of the journey.

**30 October 2009.** Dies of a heart attack at 2 rue des Marronniers, 16th arrondissement, at 100; the announcement follows four days later **[A: fr.wikipedia for address and cause; en.wikipedia for date, age, delay]**.

**Coordinates: three values, one reconciliation.** Rue des Marronniers runs from 74-78 rue Raynouard to 40 rue de Boulainvilliers at **48.8547, 2.2766** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The geography lens gave 48.8514, 2.2694, the afterlife lens 48.859, 2.268. Both struck, both reconciled in the journey.

**A closing claim corrected.** The pool said the death address lay "only a few streets from the childhood apartment" on rue Claude-Lorrain. It does not: rue des Marronniers is in Passy, rue Claude-Lorrain in Auteuil, ~1.9 km and a different quartier away.

**3 November 2009.** Buried at **Lignerolles, Côte-d'Or**, where he kept a country house called *Les Charmettes* **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Coordinates corrected by ~50 km:** the commune is at 47°54'09"N 4°53'21"E, i.e. **47.9025, 4.8892**. The geography lens gave 47.4667, 4.6167, near Arnay-le-Duc, likely a different Lignerolles or a transposition; the afterlife lens had it right.

**Théâtre Claude Lévi-Strauss**, the lower-level auditorium of the Musée du Quai Branly, is named for him **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The naming date is not in the source; the museum opened in 2006, so it appears to fall in his lifetime. **Gap stated.**

## 10. Quotations

Verbatim, *Tristes Tropiques*, trans. John and Doreen Weightman **[A]**, page numbers from Wikiquote transcription:

- "I hate travelling and explorers." (Ch. 1, p. 17; opening line, cross-checked on two fetched sources.)
- "Adventure has no place in the anthropologist's profession; it is merely one of those unavoidable drawbacks, which detract from his effective work through the incidental loss of weeks or months." (Ch. 1, p. 17; minor punctuation variants.)
- "Teaching and research are not to be confused with training for a profession. Their greatness and their misfortune is that they are a refuge or a mission." (Ch. 6, p. 55.)
- "The first thing we see as we travel round the world is our own filth, thrown into the face of mankind." (Ch. 4, p. 38.) **Wording corrected.** The pool gave "humanity". Weightman reads **mankind**, attested across 66 published books; "humanity" traces to Wilcken's biography and to Russell's 1961 rendering, "the filth, our filth, that we have thrown in the face of humanity", from the French *"au visage de l'humanité"*.
- "One must be very naive or dishonest to imagine that men choose their beliefs independently of their situation." (Ch. 16, p. 148.)
- "The image a society evolves of the relationship between the living and the dead is, in the final analysis, an attempt, on the level of religious thought, to conceal, embellish or justify the actual relationships which prevail among the living." (Ch. 23, p. 246.)
- "Natural man did not precede society, nor is he outside it." (Ch. 38, p. 392.)

Single-source Wikiquote transcriptions, **[R]**, used in the journey only with the hedge carried in `quote_source`:

- "Enthusiastic partisans of the idea of progress are in danger of failing to recognize... the immense riches accumulated by the human race." (*Tristes Tropiques*, Ch. 38, p. 393; possibly abridged at the ellipsis.)
- "I never had, and still do not have, the perception of feeling my personal identity." (*Myth and Meaning*, 1978, Introduction.)
- "I am not far from believing that, in our own societies, history has replaced mythology." (*Myth and Meaning*, 1978, Ch. 4.)
- "We can understand, too, that natural species are chosen not because they are 'good to eat' but because they are 'good to think.'" (*Totemism*, 1962, trans. Needham, p. 89; the *bonnes à penser* formula is universal in the secondary literature, the page unverified.)
- "The scientific mind does not so much provide the right answers as ask the right questions." (*The Raw and the Cooked*, 1975 English edition, p. 7; unverified.)

## 11. Corpus interlocks

All **[R]** unless marked. None is a documented meeting; each is shared ground, decade, or institutional world, and the journey campa says so where it names another traveller.

- **`artaud`**: the 1936 Sierra Tarahumara journey parallels the 1935-1938 Mato Grosso fieldwork, two Frenchmen leaving interwar Europe for indigenous America. Artaud's Left Bank is also the Paris of the 1931 agrégation.
- **`einstein`**: the October 1933 New York arrival is the flight pattern repeated in 1941 into the same refugee-scholar community.
- **`freud`**: the 1938 Vienna-to-London exile falls in the year of the second Brazilian expedition. **[A for the QUEUE.md grouping; R for the biographical link.]**
- **`humboldt`**: the 1799-1804 Orinoco and Amazon expedition is the deep geographic ancestor of the Mato Grosso work. No temporal overlap.
- **`max_muller`**: the 1845 Paris sojourn under Burnouf is the methodological ancestor of *Mythologiques*.
- **`borges`**, **`victoria_ocampo`**: the Buenos Aires pin and the 1930s steamer circuit. Ocampo gives Villa Ocampo to UNESCO in 1973, the world in which Lévi-Strauss wrote *Race et Histoire*; that link is outside-corpus knowledge.
- **`che_guevara`**: continental overlap, opposite purposes. **`karl_marx`**: the corpus Paris pin, and one of the three influences Lévi-Strauss names for himself.

**Corpus footprint [A].** QUEUE.md line 62 names him anchor of the structuralist wing, line 252 restates it from the Lacan entry; `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` line 128 records 3 documents naming him, 1 verified and 2 pending.

1. **The Derrida exchange.** The Nambikwara "writing lesson" and Derrida's critique in *De la grammatologie* is among the most cited disputes in post-structuralist theory, but the en.wikipedia "Tristes Tropiques" page fetched this session explicitly does not cover it and no other source was reached. **[R], needs a dedicated check.** The journey asserts only that the scene is in the book, attributing the Derrida reading as reception.
2. **The South Asian itinerary.** No source dates any leg; "c. 1950" is an estimate, and Karachi, Lahore, Taxila, Calcutta are reconstructions from the book's content.
3. **The 2000s revival.** The pool tagged as [A] a renewal of interest in "transformation" between myth-variants as a quasi-algebraic operation, on an unspecific fr.wikipedia sentence naming no scholars, publications, or dates. **Downgraded to [R], dropped from the journey.**

5. **Quai Branly theatre naming date**: not in the source.
