# George Orwell (Eric Arthur Blair), 1903-1950: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `george_orwell.journey.json`. Slug: `george_orwell`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a dossier claim
not independently re-verified in this pass. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated;
coordinates wrong in the working pool are corrected here with the reason. The file uses both names:
everything before January 1933 is Eric Arthur Blair, and the pen name, chosen for *Down and Out in
Paris and London*, is never his legal name.

## 1. Motihari to Eton, 1903-1921

- **[A: Wikipedia, 'George Orwell']** Born 25 June 1903 at Motihari, Champaran district, Bengal
  Presidency (now Bihar), to Richard Walmesley Blair, sub-deputy agent in the **Opium Department**,
  and Ida Mabel Limouzin. The opium monopoly was a Crown revenue operation, and his own later
  police service put him inside the same machine in another province. Pin 26.651 / 84.917.
- **[A]** 1904: Ida brings Eric and his elder sister Marjorie to England, near Henley-on-Thames;
  Richard stays in Bihar. Ursuline convent day school from about five.
- **[A]** September 1911 to December 1916: **St Cyprian's**, Eastbourne, at reduced fees.
  **Coordinate corrected:** the geography lens gave 50.7729 / **-0.2739**, a longitude sign error
  putting the school in the English Channel; file uses 50.7729 / 0.2891. **[R]** the portrait in
  'Such, Such Were the Joys' (published 1952) was **disputed** by former pupils; used as literature
  and quoted as a quote, never as fact.
- **[A]** January to May 1917: one term at **Wellington College**. **Two pool errors:** the
  chronology lens made it a single month and misplaced the college by 5.6 km, the geography lens by
  1.7 km (Wikipedia: 51.3643 / -0.8067). It is **folded into the Eton stop** rather than pinned.
- **[A]** May 1917 to December 1921: King's Scholar at **Eton**; taught French for a period by
  **Aldous Huxley**; Cyril Connolly a contemporary. Leaves without sitting for a university
  scholarship. December 1921: crammer at **Southwold** for the police examination.

### The 1921 Buddicom claim: carried, not staged

**[R: Dione Venables's postscript to the 2006 edition of Buddicom's *Eric & Us*, as characterized
in the crawled dossier]** A family tradition holds that in late summer 1921 Eric Blair made a
violent unwanted sexual advance toward Jacintha Buddicom, which she resisted, in the Shiplake /
Ticklerton area. Stated flatly: there is **no contemporaneous police report, prosecution, or
trial**; Buddicom's own 1974 memoir does not describe it this way; the postscript rests on family
letters **not independently examined and reported destroyed**. It is **carried here and not made a
stop**, no lens having supplied a coordinate. Recorded so the operator can reverse the decision.

## 2. Burma, 1922-1927

- **[A]** Sailed on the **SS Herefordshire in October 1922**, arrived **Rangoon November 1922**,
  appointed **Assistant District Superintendent of Police on probation, 29 November 1922**, at
  Rs. 525 a month. **Correction:** the chronology lens had this back to front, as though the
  appointment preceded the voyage.
- **[A]** Police Training School, **Mandalay** (21.975 / 96.0836). Postings: **Myaungmya** (early
  1924), **Twante** (mid-1924), **Syriam** (end 1924), **Insein** (September 1925), **Moulmein**
  (April 1926), **Katha** (end 1926 into 1927). Dengue fever at Katha, 1927.
- **[A] Correction, two years:** the chronology lens dated **Katha**, model for Kyauktada in
  *Burmese Days*, to 1924. It belongs to **late 1926-1927**; its coordinate is sound.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the geography lens carries a **Maymyo** posting **[R]**
  dated *early 1924*, colliding with Myaungmya, and biographies differ on the order of the short
  1924 postings. None is pinned; the Insein campa carries them in one sentence and the disagreement
  sits in that stop's `date_confidence`.
- **[A] Correction, causal order:** the geography lens said he returned to England "after
  resigning." He returned on **medical leave in July 1927** and resigned **from England on
  12 March 1928**. **[R]** reading the resignation as expiation for imperial service is Orwell's
  own retrospect in *The Road to Wigan Pier* part two.

## 3. Paris, London, the tramping years, 1927-1933

- **[A]** End of 1927: rooms off **Portobello Road** via Ruth Pitter; casual wards under the name
  **P. S. Burton**. 1928-29: **6 rue du Pot-de-Fer**, Paris 5e (48.8443 / 2.3486), near his aunt
  Nellie Limouzin; work as a plongeur.
- **[A]** **6 October 1928**: first paid article, on English press censorship, in *Monde*. Pinned to
  the **canonical Paris coordinate 48.8566 / 2.3522**, inherited byte-identical.
- **[A] Coordinate corrected:** **Hopital Cochin**, February 1929, is at **48.8368 / 2.3372**
  (47 bd de Port-Royal); the chronology lens sat 330 m east.
- **[A]** December 1929 onward: Southwold as base; tramping, Kent hop-picking, tutoring; 'The
  Spike' in *The Adelphi*, April 1931. April 1932: **The Hawthorns**, Hayes. **Correction:** the
  geography lens dated the move to **Frays College, Uxbridge**, to "1932-1933"; it belongs to
  mid/late **1933**, with pneumonia ending the teaching by January 1934.
- **[A]** **9 January 1933**: *Down and Out in Paris and London*, Gollancz; first use of the pen name.

## 4. Hampstead, Wigan, Wallington, 1934-1936

- **[A]** October 1934 to January 1936: **Booklovers' Corner**, Hampstead. *A Clergyman's Daughter*
  (11 March 1935) and *Keep the Aspidistra Flying* (20 April 1936) get no stop, both absorbed into
  the Hampstead period.
- **[A] Date corrected:** *Burmese Days* appeared in the **US on 25 October 1934** (Harper);
  Gollancz followed **24 June 1935**. The pool's "1934-06" is the 1935 UK month on the 1934 US year.
- **[A]** 1935: meets **Eileen O'Shaughnessy** at a party given by Rosalind Obermeyer. **Gap:**
  sources give "London" and no address; the file pins it at the Hampstead lodgings and says so.
- **[A] Anachronism corrected:** the northern journey was **not commissioned for the Left Book
  Club**, founded in **May 1936**, four months after he left London on **31 January 1936**.
  Gollancz suggested the trip; the Club published the book in 1937.
- **[A] Correction to a hedge:** the chronology lens called Special Branch surveillance
  "independently unconfirmed." Wikipedia states it flatly, from 1936, out of the Wigan research.
  **9 June 1936**: marries Eileen at **St Mary's, Wallington**. **Coordinate corrected:** the
  chronology lens's 51.9647 / **-0.0022** is 8 km east; file uses **51.974 / -0.1195**.

## 5. Spain, December 1936 to June 1937

- **[A]** Left England **23 December 1936**, reaching Barcelona at the end of the month; ILP
  credentials attached him to the **POUM** militia and he trained at the **Lenin Barracks**
  (41.3888 / 2.1873, approximate). January 1937: Aragon front near **Alcubierre**.
- **[A]** **3-7 May 1937**: the May Days; Eileen was working at the ILP office. **[R]** the **Hotel
  Continental** and the rooftop vigil above the Ramblas come from the dossier, the biography
  confirming the episode but not the address. The stop is named for the rooftop, the hotel supplies
  only the pin, and the [R] sits in `date_confidence`.
- **[A] Wounding date corrected, and a conflation unpicked.** The chronology lens dated the sniper
  wound **27 May 1937**. Spanish Wikipedia is explicit: *"recibio un tiro en el cuello en las
  proximidades de Huesca, el 20 de mayo de 1937."* The 27th is a **different event**, the transfer
  from Lleida on to Tarragona. Sequence used in the file: shot about 5 a.m. **20 May** near Huesca;
  field hospital **Sietamo** the same day; Barbastro, then **Lleida** c. 22-27 May; **Tarragona
  27 May**; **POUM sanatorium 29 May**.
- **[A] Coordinate corrected:** **Sietamo** is at **42.1245 / -0.2825**, east of Huesca; the
  geography lens's 42.1167 / -0.5167 is 19 km west, the wrong side of the city. Sietamo is folded
  into the Huesca campa for stop-count reasons; the corrected value is recorded here.
- **[A] Place corrected:** the **13 July 1937 deposition** naming both Blairs as "rabid Trotskyists"
  went to the Tribunal for Espionage and High Treason at **Valencia**, not Barcelona; a trial in
  absentia followed at Barcelona in late 1938. **Stop-ownership note:** Orwell was not in Valencia,
  but the document is an act performed against him rather than another traveler's journey staged in
  his file, so the stop is his and its `date_confidence` says the pin marks where the paper was
  filed (precedent: the Moulouya stop in `abdelkader.journey.json`). **Gap:** no lens gave a
  Valencia coordinate; the file uses the centroid 39.4699 / -0.3763.
- **[A]** *The Road to Wigan Pier*, Left Book Club, 1937, published while he was in Spain.
  *Homage to Catalonia*, Secker and Warburg, April 1938; sold poorly in his lifetime.
- **[A] Quote note corrected.** "I saw newspaper reports which did not bear any relation to the
  facts..." is verbatim from 'Looking Back on the Spanish War' (1943), but the pool's May Days
  framing is **wrong**: section IV is a general statement about war reporting. Framing dropped.

## 6. Kent, Morocco, the war, 1938-1945

- **[A]** March to September 1938: **Preston Hall** sanatorium, Aylesford, after a pulmonary
  haemorrhage. He had respiratory disease since infancy, so the date of tuberculous infection
  cannot be fixed; the campa says so. **[A] Title corrected:** the essay is **'Why I Join the
  I.L.P.'** (present tense), *New Leader*, 24 June 1938, not the long past-tense title the pool
  invented.
- **[A]** September 1938 to March 1939: villa outside **Marrakesh**; *Coming Up for Air* and
  'Marrakech' written there. **[R]** the 15 October 1938 lease at 550 francs a month (dossier only).
  **[A] Date qualified:** the source supports only "back in England on 30 March 1939"; the
  **26 March** sailing would need Crick. **[R]** the 1939 refusal of his offer of military service
  on medical grounds is standard biography, absent from the pool; it closes the Casablanca campa.
- **[A]** **August 1941 to September 1943**: BBC Eastern Service, Talks Producer. **Correction:**
  the geography lens ran the tenure to November 1943; he **resigned in September**. Commissioned
  T. S. Eliot, E. M. Forster, Mulk Raj Anand; called it "two wasted years." 1943-1945: *Tribune* and
  'As I Please', leaving the staff post in early 1945 for the *Observer*.
- **[A]** **14 May 1944**: the Blairs adopt a three-week-old boy, Richard Horatio Blair. June 1944:
  a V-1 destroys 10a Mortimer Crescent, Kilburn, and books are recovered by wheelbarrow. **[R]**
  the crumpled *Animal Farm* typescript pulled from the rubble.
- **[A]** **29 March 1945**: Eileen Blair dies under anaesthetic during a hysterectomy at Fernwood
  House, Newcastle upon Tyne; inquest returns cardiac failure under properly administered ether and
  chloroform. Orwell was in Europe for the *Observer*. She was 39.
- **[A]** **17 August 1945**: *Animal Farm*, Secker and Warburg, after four refusals. **[R]**
  T. S. Eliot's rejection letter for Faber, widely quoted and not re-verified here.

## 7. Jura and the last illness, 1946-1950

- **[A]** May 1946 to January 1949: **Barnhill**, Isle of Jura, first visited September 1945.
  **Coordinate corrected** to **56.1103 / -5.6928**; the chronology lens's value is 16 km southwest,
  in the Sound of Jura.
- **[A] Tag corrected, [R] to [A]:** the **Corryvreckan** accident of **19 August 1947** is in the
  cited biography, not merely the dossier. **[R]** the islet and passing lobster boat. **[A]**
  **7 November 1947**: first draft of *Nineteen Eighty-Four* finished in bed at Barnhill.
- **[A]** **20 December 1947 to July 1948**: **Hairmyres Hospital**, East Kilbride. **Coordinate
  corrected** to **55.75972 / -4.22056**; the geography lens gave the town centroid, 1.9 km off.
  Added from the source: streptomycin began **19 or 20 February 1948**, stopped **after fifty
  days**, and he was the **first patient in Scotland** to receive it. **[R]** the remainder passing
  to two other patients.
- **[A]** January to September 1949: **Cranham**, Gloucestershire; **Celia Kirwan**'s visit for the
  **Information Research Department**, **29 March 1949**, and the list of names, are attested.
  **[R]** 'Reflections on Gandhi', *Partisan Review*, January 1949, is standard bibliography the
  pool did **not** carry; it is the only documented Orwell-to-Gandhi relation in evidence (§8).
- **[A]** **8 June 1949**: *Nineteen Eighty-Four*; British print run 25,575. **13 October 1949**:
  marries **Sonia Brownell** at his bedside at University College Hospital, the party going on to
  the Ritz without him. **21 January 1950**: dies there of a massive pulmonary haemorrhage, aged 46.
  **[R]** the crimson smoking jacket, David Astor as best man, the Swiss charter, the fishing rod.
- **[A]** **26 January 1950**: buried at **All Saints', Sutton Courtenay**, plot arranged by David
  Astor; the headstone reads "Here lies Eric Arthur Blair" and does not name George Orwell.
  **Coordinate corrected** to **51.6442 / -1.2718**; the geography and afterlife lenses both sat
  outside the churchyard.

## 8. Interlocks: what was kept, and why the rest was dropped

Twelve candidates came back. **Two** are named in campas; the rest are shared geography or period,
which the map already shows a viewer.

**Kept**

- **H. G. Wells** (`h_g_wells.journey.json`), in the Broadcasting House campa: Orwell attacked him
  in **'Wells, Hitler and the World State'**, *Horizon*, August 1941, the month he joined the BBC.
  A documented one-directional engagement with another writer's work is a real relation. The Wells
  file does not mention Orwell, and the essay reaches this pass only through the dossier.
- **Mahatma Gandhi** (`mahatma_gandhi.journey.json`), in the Cranham campa, on the strength of
  **'Reflections on Gandhi'** (§7), not the BBC/Quit India overlap the pool proposed, which alone
  would have been shared-period name-dropping.

**Dropped as shared geography or shared period only:** **Durruti** (funeral 24 November 1936,
Orwell arriving a month later; his file never mentions Orwell), **Lucia Sanchez Saornil**, **Jose
Antonio Aguirre** (a queue name with no file), **Gandhi in London 1931**, **Victoria Ocampo**
(whose file does not name Orwell among its translated authors), **Bakunin** (dead since 1876),
**Rudolf Rocker** (gone from England by 1918), and **Elsa Lindenberg**, whose file cites Koestler
for Berlin 1931, years before Koestler and Orwell knew each other; that friendship is real and is
carried **in a quote** that is Orwell's own sentence. Mecca/Kaaba and Temple Mount pins: checked,
no connection. One canonical pin is inherited byte-identical, Paris 48.8566 / 2.3522, on the
*Monde* stop.

### Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found.** Every relation in this file is rooted in Orwell's own life: places he stood,
documents filed against him, books he wrote, people he taught under or wrote about. No stop applies
anyone's framework backward to material they never treated, and none reaches forward past
21 January 1950 to name a later artifact. Orwell is **not** on the `EXCEPTIONS.md` list and, on
this pool, does not need to be.

One item for the operator's eye in the other direction: the **afterlife lens** is rich (§9) and
none of it is in the journey file. Not a deletion but a reading of house convention, since
`h_g_wells`, `mahatma_gandhi` and `buenaventura_durruti` all end at the grave.

## 9. Afterlife (researched, held out of the journey file)

- **[A]** Motihari birth house, 2.48 acres, restored and opened as a **museum, May 2015**.
- **[A]** **Statue by Martin Jennings** outside BBC Broadcasting House, **7 November 2017**, wall
  inscribed from the unused *Animal Farm* preface: "If liberty means anything at all, it means the
  right to tell people what they do not want to hear." **Correction:** the 2012 rejection was under
  **Mark Thompson**; after Ben Whitaker's death in 2014 **Baroness Whitaker** carried the project,
  revived under Tony Hall.
- **[A]** The **1954** animated *Animal Farm*, Britain's first feature-length animated film, was
  covertly financed by the **CIA's Office of Policy Coordination**: Carleton Alsop and Finis Farr
  bought the rights from Sonia Orwell, and the ending was changed so the animals overthrow Napoleon.
  New York premiere 29 December 1954.
- **[A]** **Orwell Prize** founded 1993 by Bernard Crick from his biography's royalties. **Room
  101** (BBC Two, 1994) named from the Ministry of Love, though **[R]** the legend of a real
  disliked room at Broadcasting House is told inconsistently. **[A] Tag corrected, [R] to [A]:**
  the **Big Brother** franchise (Netherlands, 1999) is named for the novel.
- **[A]** **22 January 2017**: after "alternative facts," sales rose more than 9,500 percent in four
  days and Penguin reprinted 75,000. Earlier: 25,575 in the first British run, over 8 million in the
  US by 1970, top of the US bestseller list in 1984.
- **[A] Correction:** the twenty-volume **Complete Works** (Peter Davison) was **begun 1986,
  completed 1998**, revised 2000-2002. "Appeared in 1998" is misleading, and "beyond the nine books
  published in his lifetime" conflates volumes with titles.
- **[R]** The "1984 = 1948 reversed" story is disputed: drafts move the date from 1980 to 1982
  before settling, no document in Orwell's hand states the reversal, and Dorian Lynskey calls the
  story "far too cute for such a serious book." A second theory credits Eileen's 1934 poem 'End of
  the Century, 1984'; no Orwell statement confirms either.

## 10. Honest gaps

1. **No Valencia coordinate** in any lens (centroid used), **no address for the 1935 party** where
   he met Eileen (pinned near his Hampstead lodgings), an approximate **Lenin Barracks** pin, and an
   unconfirmed **Motihari birth plot**, all declared in their stops.
2. **The 1924 Burma posting order** is unresolved, and **the 26 March 1939 Casablanca sailing** is
   unsupported by the cited source.
3. **No primary Orwell manuscript, letter, or diary** was consulted; only the **quotations** come
   from full texts, every biographical fact resting on Wikipedia and the crawled dossier.
4. **The Buddicom postscript** (§1) was not read; it reaches this report at two removes.
5. **Peter Davison's Complete Works**, which would settle most of the [R] items in §6 and §7, is
   the highest-value unreached source for this subject.
