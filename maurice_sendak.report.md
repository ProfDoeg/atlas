# Maurice Sendak (1928-2012), research report
*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: American picture-book author and illustrator, stage designer, Brooklyn and Ridgefield. Companion file: `maurice_sendak.journey.json` (9 segments, 45 stops).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition, no document behind it in reachable material. Where two sources disagree, both readings are printed and the disagreement is left open. Where nothing was reachable, the gap is stated as a gap.

**Method.** Worked from a verified research pool in five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife). Several pool entries carried adversarial correction notes; every correction is applied and marked **[corrected]**. Journey-file coordinates follow the corrected values. Where two lenses give two coordinate pairs for one building, both are printed.

---

## 1. Origins and the Brooklyn household, c. 1911-1932

- **Parents.** Philip Sendak, dressmaker, and Sadie (Sarah) Schindler, from Poland, separately. **Sadie arrives c. 1911, Philip in 1914, naturalized 23 July 1929 [A, corrected]**. The pool's "c. 1908-1913" excluded Philip's crossing entirely. **Ellis Island as the port of entry is [R]**: no manifest for either parent located.
- **Birth: 10 June 1928, Brooklyn [A, Wikipedia; Lanes, *The Art of Maurice Sendak*, 1980; NYT obituary, 8 May 2012]**, youngest after Natalie (b. c. 1919) and Jack (b. c. 1923). Neighborhood given as **Bensonhurst (40.6021, -73.9954)**, with a second family pin at Bath Beach (40.599, -73.992). No street address is documented and the family moved often; treat the two pins as one moving household.
- **The illnesses are [A]** in the aggregate (measles, pneumonia, scarlet fever, long confinements), but **no source fixes a year for any single one**. The bedside stop takes 1930 as nominal.
- **Observance [R].** An observant household at a neighborhood synagogue is standard biography; the congregation is unnamed anywhere reached, so the pool's synagogue pin (40.6018, -73.9962) asserts an unidentified building and is not used.
- **Lindbergh, 1 March 1932 [A].** The kidnapping terrified him, and he attributed the ladder at Ida's window in *Outside Over There* to it repeatedly. Three corrections: **age**, he was three at the crime and turned four that June, so the pool's "four-year-old" ran a year early; **title**, the pool also linked the case to *In the Night Kitchen*, but his own attribution is to *Outside Over There* and the *Night Kitchen* link is [R], dropped; **pin**, Highfields stood in East Amwell Township, roughly **40.40, -74.80**, not the pool's 40.3862, -74.7899.

## 2. Disney, the war, and school, 1934-1946

- **Mickey Mouse and the newspaper comics [A]**, cited by Sendak as foundational. **At twelve he sees *Fantasia* and decides to become an illustrator [A, Wikipedia; Jewish Virtual Library]**; month, day, and theater unrecorded, so the stop defaults to 1940-01-01.
- **Holocaust losses [A as fact, corrected as to date].** The pool dated them 1940. **[corrected]**: his own chronology dates his learning of the destruction of the family's Polish town to **1941**, and the killings fall **1941-1943**, the Jews of **Zambrow** (Philip's town) being ghettoized and then deported to Treblinka and Auschwitz in the winter of 1942-43 [A, Wikipedia, 'Zambrow']. **Which relatives died, and how many, is a gap.**
- **Coney Island [R].** That boardwalk outings imprinted the *In the Night Kitchen* skyline is traditional; Sendak and art historians traced the imagery to Coney Island and 1930s advertising, but no dated visit exists. Folded into the *Night Kitchen* stop rather than pinned.
- **Lafayette High School, graduating 1946 [R].** Widely reported, not confirmed in the fetched sources; the geography lens gives 1942-1946 as standard biography, not from a record. The school-paper comic strips belong to the same account.

## 3. Apprenticeship, 1946-1956

- **All-American Comics, Manhattan, 1946-1948**, backgrounds for *Mutt and Jeff*. Tagged [A] by the geography lens but sourced only to "standard Sendak biographical timeline," naming no document. Carried as attested, thin sourcing recorded.
- ***Atomics for the Millions*, 1947 [A, Wikipedia; Jewish Virtual Library]**, a physics textbook, his first professional illustration work.
- **FAO Schwarz, 745 Fifth Avenue, window dresser, from 1948 [A]**. **Contradiction, unresolved**: the Jewish Virtual Library gives 1948-1952, the geography lens 1948-1951. Both printed in the stop's `date_confidence`. The job produced the introduction to **Ursula Nordstrom** of Harper, the hinge of the career [A].
- **Art Students League, 215 West 57th Street, night classes under John Groth [A]**. **Contradiction, unresolved**: start year 1948 (chronology lens) against 1949 (geography lens).
- **Marcel Ayme, *The Wonderful Farm*, 1951 [A]**, his first children's book illustration. **Ruth Krauss, *A Hole Is to Dig*, 1952 [A]**, the book that made his name. ***Kenny's Window*, 1956 [A]**, first as author and illustrator both.
- **Not used, so the omissions are deliberate**: Italy with Ruth Krauss, 1953 [R], uncorroborated; Central Park Zoo sketching [R], undated tradition.

## 4. Glynn and the Wild Things, 1957-1966

- **Partnership with the psychoanalyst Eugene David Glynn (1926-2007) begins 1957, lasts fifty years [A]**. The pool entry contradicted itself, saying the relationship was revealed "after Sendak's death in a 2008 interview." **[corrected]**: he disclosed it in September 2008, nearly four years before his death.
- **The Nutshell Library, 1962 [A]**. ***Where the Wild Things Are* written and drawn in a basement apartment at 29 West 9th Street, Greenwich Village, 1963 [A, Wikipedia]**. The geography lens tagged the Village [R], hedging "exact address not confirmed," and contradicted its own chronology lens, which had the address. **[corrected]**: promoted to [A], pin moved from 40.7336, -74.0027 (half a kilometre west, in the West Village) to **40.7333, -73.9968**.
- **Publication 13 November 1963, Harper and Row, 40-page hardcover [A]**. **Over 19 million copies worldwide by 2009, about 10 million in the United States [A]**. **[corrected]**: "more than thirty languages" is dropped, since the pool cited Wikipedia's "language-edition list," the sidebar of roughly thirty Wikipedia language versions of the article, not a translation count. The named titles are right (French *Max et les Maximonstres*, German *Wo die wilden Kerle wohnen*, Chinese 野獸國; Spanish *Donde viven los monstruos*). **A sourced count is a gap.**
- **Caldecott Medal, 1964 [A, ALA winners archive]**; the ceremony date within 1964 is not established and the stop defaults to June.
- **Isaac Bashevis Singer, *Zlateh the Goat*, 1966, Newbery Honor [A]**.
- **Rosenbach Museum and Library, Philadelphia, first visit 1966 [A]**. The afterlife lens tagged the holdings [R] because the museum website surfaced no collection page in one session. **[corrected]**, promoted to [A]: the association, the thousands of drawings held there before his death, and the Maurice Sendak Gallery are documented. **Struck as unsupported: that he was a Rosenbach trustee.** **Coordinate contradiction, unresolved**: geography gives 39.9464, -75.1747, afterlife 39.9445, -75.1746, for one address at 2008-2010 Delancey Place. The journey uses the afterlife value.

## 5. The year of the heart, 1967-1971

- **1967: heart attack in England, and Jennie's death [A, Sendak's own chronology; Lanes]**. He was thirty-eight. **The English town is a gap**, so the pin defaults to London and says so. Jennie, the Sealyham terrier, is the heroine of ***Higglety Pigglety Pop!*, 1967 [A]**. **[corrected]**: the pool put her death at Ridgefield, impossible, since he did not buy that house until 1972; repointed to Greenwich Village.
- **Sadie Sendak dies 1968, not 1967 [A, Sendak's chronology: "1968, Mother passes away"]**. **[corrected]**: the pool conflated her death with the 1967 cancer diagnosis. **Philip Sendak dies 1970 [R]**, date and place unconfirmed. **The burial ground is a gap**; the pool's pin at 40.6501, -73.9496 is the Brooklyn borough centroid, not a cemetery, and **is removed**. The stop sits on the family's Bath Beach streets with the missing grave declared.
- ***In the Night Kitchen*, 1970 [A]**, later among the American Library Association's most frequently challenged books, for nudity [A].
- **Hans Christian Andersen Medal for illustration, 1970, first American winner [A, IBBY winners list]**. **Contradiction, unresolved**: the chronology lens says Bologna "per standard award history," the geography lens flags the host city as unconfirmed and calls it a gap. The pin defaults to Bologna, hedged in `date_confidence`.

## 6. Ridgefield and the operas, 1972-1984

- **Move to Ridgefield, Connecticut, 1972, with Glynn [A, Britannica; Maurice Sendak Foundation; Sendak's chronology, "1972, Relocates to Connecticut"]**, keeping a pied-a-terre at **40 Fifth Avenue [A]**. **[corrected]**: the chronology lens dated the move 1985, thirteen years late; that entry is deleted. **The street address is a gap**, so the pin is town-level (41.2815, -73.4979).
- **Weston Woods Studios, Weston, Connecticut, 1960s-1970s [A]**: film adaptations with Morton Schindel, including *Where the Wild Things Are*. No single year documented; the stop takes 1973 as nominal.
- ***Really Rosie*, television special with songs by Carole King, 1975 [A]**. ***Outside Over There*, 1981 [A]**. **National Book Award, 1982 [A, National Book Foundation records]**.
- **Houston Grand Opera, *The Magic Flute*, 1980 [A]**, sets and costumes.
- **The opera *Where the Wild Things Are*, music by Oliver Knussen, libretto by Sendak, premiered 28 November 1980 at the Theatre de la Monnaie, Brussels, as *Max et les Maximonstres*, Ronald Zollman conducting [A, Wikipedia, 'Where the Wild Things Are (opera)']**. **[corrected] twice**: the chronology lens dated a vague "stage adaptation" to 1979, the year the collaboration began, and the geography lens dated the premiere 25 November; the afterlife lens was right. Pin nudged from 50.8467, 4.3554 to **50.8497, 4.3542**.
- **Final revised version, 9 January 1984, National Theatre, London, Glyndebourne Touring Opera, Knussen conducting [A]**. **Glyndebourne double bill with *Higglety Pigglety Pop!*, 1984 [A, Glyndebourne archival production records]**; month not given, defaulted to the summer festival. **Left open, as the afterlife lens left it**: whether the canonical English title is *Where the Wild Things Are* or the Brussels-era *Max et les Maximonstres*.
- **Pacific Northwest Ballet, *The Nutcracker*, Seattle, 1983 [A]**, choreography Kent Stowell. The chronology lens listed a 1983 *Nutcracker* without a company; the geography lens supplies it. One production, one stop.

## 7. Honors and late books, 1983-2011

- **Laura Ingalls Wilder Medal, ALA, 1983 [A]**. Not a stop; the 1964 Caldecott carries the ALA pin.
- **Honorary doctorates: Princeton 1984 [A]; UConn, Storrs, 1990 [A]; Goucher College, 2004 [A]**. Only Princeton is a stop. **[corrected]**: the pool called UConn's Northeast Children's Literature Collection "a principal repository" of his working archive. It holds the Maurice Sendak Collection of James Marshall (1979-1997), one of over 120 author archives there, while the Rosenbach held thousands of his own drawings; the pool's "1970s onward" is unsupported. Downgraded, kept out of the journey file.
- **American Masters, "Mon Cher Papa," 1987 [A]**; ***Caldecott and Co.*, 1988 [A]**. Neither is a stop.
- ***We Are All in the Dumps with Jack and Guy*, 1993 [A]**, on the AIDS crisis and homelessness.
- **National Medal of Arts, White House, 29 September 1996 [A, NEA recipient records]**, presented by President Clinton.
- **Inaugural Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award, 2003 [A]**, administered from Stockholm; **the ceremony date and venue are a gap** and the pin is city-level.
- ***Brundibar*, 2003 [A]**, adapted by Tony Kushner from the opera performed by Jewish children imprisoned at Theresienstadt, illustrated by Sendak.
- **Spike Jonze's live-action film, 2009 [A]**. ***Bumble-Ardy*, 2011 [A]**, his last book in life.

## 8. Disclosure, death, afterlife

- **Eugene Glynn dies 15 May 2007 [A]**; **Sendak gives one million dollars to the Jewish Board of Family and Children's Services in his memory [A]**.
- **September 2008: aged eighty, Sendak publicly acknowledges that he is gay and that Glynn had been his partner [A]**. **[corrected]**: the interviewer was **Patricia Cohen** of The New York Times, whose "Concerns Beyond Just Where the Wild Things Are" ran 9-10 September 2008. The pool credited Terry Gross, conflating this with her NPR *Fresh Air* conversation of 2011.
- **Death: 8 May 2012, Danbury Hospital, aged 83, complications of a stroke four days earlier [A, NYT obituary; Jewish Virtual Library]**. **[corrected]**: both lenses gave wrong coordinates and disagreed with each other, geography 41.3948, -73.4657 and afterlife 41.3948, -73.4909. Danbury Hospital, 24 Hospital Avenue, is at roughly **41.4052, -73.4468**; both are reconciled to that.
- **Cremation, ashes scattered at an undisclosed location, no public grave [A for the absence of a grave, R for the site]**. That the ashes went to the Ridgefield property is tradition; the pin defaults there and declares it.
- ***My Brother's Book*, February 2013 [A]**; **New York Writers Hall of Fame, posthumous, 2013 [A]**; **Jonze dedicates *Her* (2013) to his memory [A]**; **two schools named for him, North Hollywood and P.S. 118 in Brooklyn [A]**; **USPS Wild Things stamp, 2006, and Obama reading the book at White House Easter Egg Rolls [A]**. **A Wicker Park, Chicago, mural, 2012 [R]**: artist and wall unverified beyond a Wikipedia mention, not a stop.
- **Rosenbach litigation [A, corrected in]**: the museum sued the estate in 2014 over promised rare books, and in 2016 a Connecticut probate judge awarded most of the disputed collection to the estate. Very likely why its Sendak holdings are less visible on its own site than the literature implies.
- **Touring retrospective. [corrected]**: the pool put retrospectives at Denver in 2024-2025 and the Skirball in 2024. The exhibition is ***Wild Things Are Happening: The Art of Maurice Sendak***, with the Maurice Sendak Foundation; **Denver Art Museum, 2 October 2022 to 12 February 2023**, and **Skirball Cultural Center, Los Angeles, from 18 April 2024**. Only the Skirball year was right. **Contradiction left open**: the same note gives a Columbus Museum of Art opening of 20 October 2022, overlapping the Denver run rather than preceding it, so the tour's order is unsettled.

## 9. Quotations: verified and repaired

- **"And now," cried Max, "let the wild rumpus start!"** [A, *Where the Wild Things Are*, 1963, Wikiquote, fetched]. The pool spliced this to "And now, let the wild rumpus start!" across the attribution tag without an ellipsis; **full line restored**.
- **"Please don't go. We'll eat you up. We love you so."** [A, same source].
- **"There must be more to life than having everything!"** [A, *Higglety Pigglety Pop!*, 1967, Wikiquote, fetched].
- **"I wish you all good things. Live your life, live your life, live your life."** [A, Terry Gross, NPR *Fresh Air*, September 2011, quoted verbatim in Maria Popova, The Marginalian, 3 January 2013, fetched]. His last words in his last recorded interview.
- **"All children were in the Holocaust. Everybody was in the Holocaust."** [A on Wikiquote from PBS NOW with Bill Moyers, 12 March 2004; **the PBS transcript page was not retrievable**, primary unconfirmed].
- **"I have this idiot name tag which says 'controversial.' I've had it since 1965, with 'Where the Wild Things Are.'"** [A as wording]. **[corrected] citation**: the pool's Marginalian URL returns HTTP 404; replaced with the primary, The New York Times, "Maurice Sendak Sheds Moonlight on a Dark Tale," 20 September 1993, itself paywalled here and confirmed through search-result text and quotation databases. A weak citation, flagged in the stop.
- **"When you hide another story in a story, that's the story I am telling the children."** [A, Rosenbach interview, "Sendak on Sendak," 2007/2008, Wikiquote raw wikitext fetched]. **[corrected]**: the pool dropped "the children," the object of the sentence and the point of the remark.
- **Verified, not used**: "You cannot write for children... They're much too complicated. You can only write books that are of interest to them" (Boston Globe, 4 January 1987, **[corrected]**, restored from the pool's rewrite); "Art has always been my salvation" (Moyers, 2004).

## 11. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. No passenger manifest for either parent; Ellis Island is inference.
2. No street address for the Bensonhurst birth, none for the Ridgefield house.
3. No named relatives among the Polish dead, and no count.
4. No English town for the 1967 heart attack.
5. No host city for the 1970 Hans Christian Andersen ceremony, and the lenses disagree about whether Bologna is even a guess.
6. No cemetery for either parent; no grave for Sendak, by his own arrangement.
7. No sourced count of the translations of *Where the Wild Things Are*.
8. No ceremony date or venue for the 2003 Astrid Lindgren award.
9. The Columbus and Denver openings of the 2022 retrospective overlap; the tour order is unsettled.
10. The Rosenbach coordinates differ between two lenses by about 200 metres, neither citing a survey.
