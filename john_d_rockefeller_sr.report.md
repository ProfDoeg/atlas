## 1. The itinerant father's country (1839-1853)

- Born 8 July 1839 on a hilltop farm three miles east of Richford, Tioga County, New York, second of six children of William Avery and Eliza Davison Rockefeller. **[A, RAC; PBS]** **Gap:** no surveyed marker; the site is inside Michigan Hill State Forest and 42.3605 / -76.142 reads the phrase "three miles east of Richford" rather than fixing a point. **[R for the coordinate]**
- Moravia, Cayuga County, in part of his childhood, one of several upstate removals driven by the father's itinerant trade. **[A, Historical Marker Database]**
- Owego Academy before the move west. **[A, PBS]** **[C]** PBS's own materials disagree on the year of arrival, 1849 against 1851. Unresolved; the file uses 1851 only to order the segment.
- Reaches Ohio 1853, settling first around Strongsville. **[A, RAC]** The boyhood turkeys and the fifty dollars lent at seven percent are **[R]**, standard in the biographies and absent from the pool, carried in one campa as texture.

## 2. Cleveland and Ledger A (1853-1863)

- Central High School, left 1855 without graduating, by PBS about two months short. **[A, PBS]** **[C]** ECH's article on that school does not mention Rockefeller as a pupil; it names his wife as a graduate who gave the clock tower. His attendance rests on PBS and RAC.
- Baptized into the Erie Street Baptist Church, 1854. **[A, ECH]** **[C]** The pool's "clerk by 1858, trustee by 21" credited ECH, which records only that he joined as a teenager and was a trustee in 1883; the earlier detail is Chernow's. Both left standing.
- Folsom's Mercantile College, a nominal six-month course finished in about three. **[A, RAC]**
- First job 26 September 1855, assistant bookkeeper at Hewitt & Tuttle; kept the anniversary as "Job Day" for life. **[A, RAC]** Address unconfirmed; coordinate in the riverfront commission-house district. **[R for the coordinate]**
- Partnership with Maurice B. Clark, 1859, on $1,000 saved plus $1,000 borrowed from his father. **[A, RAC]** The ten percent interest is **[R]**.
- Enters refining 1863 with Clark and Samuel Andrews. **[A, RAC]** Coordinate in the Kingsbury Run corridor; exact plat unverified. **[R for the coordinate]**
- Did not serve in the Union Army. National Archives research on an 1863 Cleveland enrollment record, under the probable clerical error "John W. Rockefeller", concludes the evidence is strong that he paid for a substitute. **[R]**: nobody in this chain viewed the original.

## 3. The refinery (1864-1870)

- Married Laura Celestia Spelman 8 September 1864, Cleveland; honeymoon at Niagara, then Canada and New England. **[A, RAC]** **Gap:** the wedding venue (church or Spelman residence) is unsettled, and the coordinate is a general downtown point, labelled as one.
- 1865, wins the internal auction at $72,500, forms Rockefeller & Andrews; 1867, Flagler joins with capital connected to Stephen V. Harkness. **[A, RAC]** Years only.
- 10 January 1870, Standard Oil incorporated in Ohio, $1 million capital. **[A, RAC]** Initial share reconstructed at about thirty percent **[A as PBS's reconstruction]**. The "tenth of American refining" line in the campa is **[R]**.

## 4. The combination (1872-1882)

- 1872, South Improvement Company contracts with the trunk lines (fixed rates, rebates, drawbacks), collapsing after producer protest before it ever operated. **[A, Tarbell, reproducing charter and contracts]** **Gap:** his presence in the oil region during the uproar is undocumented; the Titusville stop records the revolt's geography and says in `date_confidence` that it is not a visit.
- 1872, the "Cleveland Massacre": nearly all competing city refiners acquired within months. **[A, RAC; Tarbell]** **[C]** Whether particular sales were voluntary or coerced is a live dispute; both readings stated in the campa, neither resolved.
- 29 April 1879, Clarion County grand jury indicts him for conspiracy; ends 5 February 1880 by nolle prosequi. **[A, Tarbell]**
- 1882, the Standard Oil Trust: about $70 million initial capital. **[A, RAC]** **[C]** Sources differ on 41 versus 42 signers; unresolved.
- 1877-1883, the Empire Transportation fight and the Tidewater pipeline war, ending in National Transit and a controlling Tidewater interest. **[A, RAC]** Infrastructural stop; the coordinate marks a corridor, not a visit.

## 5. 26 Broadway (1884-1900)

- 1884, buys 4 West 54th Street **[A, RAC]**; 1885, headquarters at 26 Broadway, landmarked 1995 **[A, LP-1930]**.
- 1888, testifies before a New York State Senate committee on trusts; the trust agreement is produced under compulsion. **[A, RAC; Tarbell]** **Gap:** the hearing room was not identified, so the coordinate is a general lower Manhattan point.
- 1885-1889, the Lima sour-crude commitment; Frasch's desulfurization rescues it in 1888; some 40 million barrels held by then. **[A, PBS]**
- 1892, Ohio Supreme Court dissolves the Trust as a legal form; June 1899, Standard Oil of New Jersey becomes the holding company. **[A, 49 Ohio St. 137; RAC]** The 1899 filing is carried inside the Ohio court stop: he was not present, and the pool gives no coordinate.
- Withdrawal from daily control, Archbold running operations, Rockefeller nominal president of Jersey Standard to 1911. **[A in substance, RAC and PBS]** **[C]** The pool says 1896 and cites PBS as corroborating; the PBS timeline says 1895 ("gradually and secretly, in a move designed to keep the press and the public in the dark"). The range should read 1895-1897. The alopecia and digestive collapse are **[R]**.
- Two personal visits to the University of Chicago, 1896 and 1901; $600,000 of the initial $1 million in 1890 contingent on matching funds; about $35 million by 1910. **[A, RAC citing University of Chicago archival material]** **Gap:** the building entered is unspecified; Cobb Hall stands in as the campus landmark.

## 6. The fortune turned outward (1901-1908)

- 1901, sells the Mesabi ore, railroad and lake-fleet interests into Morgan's United States Steel. **[A, PBS]** **Gap:** nothing places him personally on the Mesabi Range, so the stop sits at 26 Broadway and says why.
- 13 June 1901, Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research incorporated, with Gates and John D. Rockefeller Jr. **[A, RAC]** The Gates textbook story and the grandson's scarlet fever are **[R]**.
- 9 October 1901, John D. Rockefeller Jr. marries Abby Greene Aldrich, daughter of Senator Nelson W. Aldrich. **[A]** **Gap:** whether Sr. attended is not established, and the file says so rather than staging him at the altar.
- November 1902, Tarbell's series begins in McClure's, collected 1904. **[A, Poynter]** The series is Tarbell's own labor and under the standing rule it is her stop; it is carried here as the event his headquarters read and did not answer, with her authorship in `date_confidence`.
- 1902, General Education Board chartered "without distinction of race, sex, or creed", incorporated 1903. **[A, RAC]**
- 1907, Landis fines Standard Oil of Indiana $29,240,000 for rebates on the Chicago & Alton; reversed by the Seventh Circuit in 1908, not restored on retrial. **[A, 164 F. 376]** The day ordering the stop (3 August 1907) is **[R]**. He was not in the courtroom; the coordinate marks the court.
- 2 February 1908, the New York World, reporting by A. B. Macdonald, identifies him as the son of the missing "Dr. William Levingston", exposing the father's double life and bigamous second household. **[A as reproduced in later treatments; the original page was viewed by nobody in this chain.]** **Gap:** nothing records his response.

## 7. The dissolution (1909-1914)

- 1909, *Random Reminiscences of Men and Events*, described in its own pages as neither formal nor complete autobiography. **[A]** 1909, Rockefeller Sanitary Commission for the Eradication of Hookworm Disease, $1 million, eleven Southern states. **[A, RAC]**
- 15 May 1911, *Standard Oil Co. of New Jersey v. United States*, 221 U.S. 1: unreasonable restraint, dissolution within six months, thirty-four successor companies. **[A, Justia]**
- 14 May 1913, Rockefeller Foundation chartered by New York State, Chapter 488, endowment about $100 million; the failed federal charter and the Congressional objection to concentrated private power both **[A, RAC]**.
- 20 April 1914, the Ludlow Massacre. **[A, Britannica]** **Placement decision, deliberate:** Sr. was not at Ludlow, and the pool's own interlock lens says the episode attaches to John D. Rockefeller Jr., who handled Colorado Fuel & Iron policy and testified to Congress. Under the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was there, the file puts **no pin at Ludlow**; it records the controlling interest and the news reaching 26 Broadway. **Gap:** no atlas traveler covers Ludlow, CF&I, or Mother Jones, so this has nowhere to attach yet.
- Peak fortune **[C]**: the press reported the crossing of $1 billion in 1916, producing "first billionaire"; his own advisers disputed the estimates; later historians commonly retain about $900 million circa 1912-1913. **[A, PBS]** Both stated, neither adjudicated. Guinness's "world's richest man ever" is **[R]**.

## 8. The Casements (1915-1937)

- 12 March 1915, Laura dies of a heart attack while he is in Florida. **[A, RAC]** **[C on place]** RAC is ambiguous about where she died; Britannica and Find a Grave give Pocantico Hills, where the funeral was held on 15 March. The 1918 Laura Spelman Rockefeller Memorial, consolidated into the Foundation in 1929, is carried inside this stop. **[A, RAC]**
- 1915-1918, winters increasingly at Lakewood, New Jersey. **[A, RAC]** **Gap:** estate name and address unconfirmed; the coordinate marks the town, and "Golf House" comes from the pool's place-string, not a document.
- 17 December 1917, Forest Hill burns: some 700 acres assembled from 1873 around a converted failed hotel, two empty oil cans found near the ruins, two former employees suspected, cause never proven, and he never returned to Cleveland afterward. **[A, ECH and Cleveland Historical, "Forest Hill"]**
- 1918, buys The Casements at Ormond Beach, built 1913 for the Rev. Dr. Harwood Huntington; hosts Henry Ford, Harvey Firestone, Thomas Edison; hands out dimes. **[A, City of Ormond Beach; Daytona Beach City tourism page]**
- 23 May 1937, dies in bed at The Casements, seven weeks short of ninety-eight. **[A, RAC]** Cause **[R]**: sclerotic myocarditis per grandson David Rockefeller's later recollection, arteriosclerosis in other accounts, no death certificate examined.
- 27 May 1937, buried at Lake View beneath a Barre granite obelisk shipped in 1899 and erected by Joseph Carabelli's firm, the tallest marker on the ground. **[A, Cleveland Historical; Cleveland Magazine]** **[C], height, left open:** the cemetery says 70 feet, Cleveland Magazine 65 feet 10 inches; the pool asserted both silently in different lenses, and the campa states the dispute instead of choosing. The dime-leaving custom is **[A]** as a recent tradition built on a habit the dossier's "Myth 16" calls attested in fact and contested in meaning.

## Quotations

All twelve quotes in the journey file were traced to a fetchable text; three carried errors in general circulation.

- Six from *Random Reminiscences* (1909), chs. I-III, and three characterizations by Tarbell, ch. II: all **[A, verbatim, Gutenberg #17090 and #60692]**. Two notes in `quote_source`: Wikiquote circulates "all the hours of the waking day" for the despicable-and-pathetic line, which the book does not read; and "It was a friendship founded on business" is Flagler's line, reported approvingly.
- **"God gave me my money." [R, but citably so.]** The pool's source line (Women's Home Companion 1915 via Flynn) does not survive checking. Traced verbatim to Chernow, *Titan* (1998), ch. 3, whose endnote cites Flynn, *God's Gold* (1932), p. 395, not a periodical; Chernow's bibliography lists "Woman's Home Companion, January 1907" for a different passage. The file cites Chernow citing Flynn.
- **"I have ways of making money that you know nothing of." [A, verbatim, Tarbell ch. II]**, reporting Isaac Hewitt's testimony. Two cautions kept: Tarbell's text says the committee was of the New York **Senate** in 1879 while her appendix footnotes the Hepburn material to the **Assembly**, so neither should be stated flatly; and Hawke (1980) reports Rockefeller doubted saying it.
- **"I do not think there is any other quality so essential to success..." [A]** Marden, *How They Succeeded* (1901), ch. XII, p. 207, Gutenberg #64059. Wikiquote inserts a "that" after "think" that the printed text lacks. The pool tagged this R for want of a fetch; the fetch was made and it stands as A.

## Interlocks

Named in a campa, on a real relation:

- **`andrew_carnegie`**, in the 1910 stop at 26 Broadway. Basis: co-funding. Three existing atlas files (Carnegie's, Prescott Bush's, Bell's) carry a sentence funding the Eugenics Record Office at Cold Spring Harbor on "Harriman, Rockefeller and Carnegie money" in 1910. An institutional relation between the two men's money, not shared geography. **[R on the attribution to Sr. personally, below.]**
- **`thomas_edison`**, in the 1918 Casements stop, on the attested fact that Edison, with Ford and Firestone, was a guest in the house. **[A, Daytona Beach City tourism page; City of Ormond Beach]**

**The ruling the operator owes.** The ERO sentence in those three files says only "Rockefeller". Wikipedia's ERO article names the funders as Mary Harriman, the Rockefeller **Foundation**, and the Carnegie Institution; the Foundation was not chartered until 1913, three years after the ERO opened, so the 1910 money is very likely Sr.'s personal giving. That is an inference, tagged **[R]** in `date_confidence`. If accepted, the three files should name John D. Rockefeller Sr. instead of bare "Rockefeller", making this a four-way hub. If rejected, this stop loses its ERO half.

1. **`fermi`: FORWARD.** Rockefeller endowed the University of Chicago in 1890, and the Hyde Park campus, Stagg Field, and the Institute for Nuclear Studies later named for him are the exact ground of Fermi's stops for the 1942 pile. 2. **`hillary_clinton`: FORWARD.** Her file has her working for Nelson Rockefeller against Nixon in the New Hampshire snow. Nelson is Sr.'s grandson by John D. Jr., the same bloodline as the queued David. Real dynastic continuity, entirely after 1937. Already where it belongs, in Clinton's file.
3. **`prescott_bush` and `alexander_graham_bell`: CONTEMPORANEOUS, not apparatus.** Both files carry the same 1910 ERO funding sentence, which falls inside Rockefeller's life, so no exception is needed; what is needed is the attribution ruling above. Neither is named in a campa: co-funding an office one of them chaired is thin as a relation to Bell, and no relation at all to Bush, whose tie runs to the Harriman side.
4. **No canonical pin inherited.** The pool checked Rockefeller against the Kaaba, the Temple Mount, Paris, and Buenos Aires, and found nothing. His attested geography is domestic and narrow, with no ocean crossing anywhere in the pool. Standard Oil's kerosene exports reached South America commercially, which could motivate a Buenos Aires pin through corporate rather than personal geography, but nothing found puts him there in person. Stated as a gap; no pin forced.
