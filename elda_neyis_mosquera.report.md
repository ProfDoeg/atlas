# Elda Neyis Mosquera García, alias Karina: research report

*Atlas of Journeys, working file. Slug: `elda_neyis_mosquera`. Subject living as of the sources consulted.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named inline · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or placeholder · gaps and contradictions are recorded, never adjudicated silently.

A note on register first. The brief asked for a "tomb and its fate" and an "afterlife". **She is alive [A: no death record in either Wikipedia edition; October 2025 launch interviews with her in El Colombiano].** There is no grave, no monument, no cult. The brief's framing is recorded as a mismatch with the documented person, and the journey treats the post-2008 material as a second civil life.

---

## 1. Origin: Turbo and the banana belt

**Birth.** Born Elda Neyis Mosquera García on 15 August 1963 in the corregimiento of Nueva Antioquia, municipality of Turbo, Antioquia, into a poor campesino family of twelve children **[A: Spanish and English Wikipedia, "Elda Neyis Mosquera"]**.

**Contradiction 1, unresolved.** Her own 2025 memoir opens with a different birth: *"Nací en Turbo, Antioquia, en el corregimiento de Currulao, el 15 de octubre de 1967"* **[A: Volver a ser Elda, publisher preview fragment, verified on two independent retailer preview pages]**. Both the year and the corregimiento differ from the encyclopedic record. Nothing reachable reconciles them. The journey carries **both as separate stops**, each with the contradiction stated in `date_confidence`.

**An observation, not a resolution.** The memoir's 1967 makes the competing enlistment claims (§2) consistent: sixteen years old in 1984 requires a 1967 birth. This is arithmetic, not evidence, and no source performs it.

**Politics of the household.** At about twelve she encountered the Juventud Comunista and stayed in it until sixteen: *"A los 12 años conocí la Juventud Comunista y estuve en ella hasta los 16"* **[A: El Colombiano launch interview, 4 October 2025]**.

**Corrected upstream error.** An earlier pass paired that with a claim that her family was close to the **Unión Patriótica**. That half is an **anachronism and is dropped**: the UP was not founded until 1985. **[R, refuted]**

**Poverty and motive.** *"Sabía que éramos pobres"* and *"A mí no me motivaba tanto la ideología comunista, me motivaba más estar lejos de casa"* **[A: memoir preview]**. Her own account puts distance from the household above doctrine.

**The father.** He disagreed with her joining and, on losing the argument, asked one thing: *"Lo único que yo le pido es que sea una buena guerrillera"* **[A: memoir preview; the same substance in La FM's interview]**.

**The departure.** *"Una mañana le cociné al muchacho, le dejé la comida en el fogón y me fui para la guerrilla"* **[A: memoir preview]**. Undated in the excerpt.

## 2. Entry into the FARC-EP

**Contradiction 2, unresolved.** Spanish and English Wikipedia date her formal incorporation to **1984** **[A]**. El Colombiano's launch interview says she left the JUCO at sixteen and entered the FARC, and gives **1984** in the same passage **[A]**. Off the 1963 birth these cannot both be true. The atlas records both and resolves neither. An earlier pass inferred "c. 1979-80" from the 1963 birth; that arithmetic is the researcher's, not the source's, and is struck.

**Unit.** Frente 5, in the Urabá region, through the 1990s **[A]**. No source names an enlistment town; the journey pins the entry on **Chigorodó** (7.66972, -76.68139) as a representative Frente 5 municipality and declares the placement in `date_confidence`. An earlier pass carried this pin as attested; that tag was wrong. **[R]**

**The naming.** The nom de guerre was assigned, not chosen, and not to her alone: *"usted se va a llamar Viviana, usted Deisy y usted Karina'. Erika fue quien me bautizó"* **[A: memoir preview]**.

**Corrected upstream error.** An earlier pass quoted this as *"usted se va a llamar Karina"*. Those words never appear contiguously; the line names three women in one breath. The splice is removed and the full clause restored, with the assigner, Erika, named in the campa.

**Early discipline.** The armband sanction and its aftermath are both in the preview: *"usted no se puso el brazalete cuando se le ordenó, por eso va a cargar cinco viajes de leña"*, and her reflection, *"en mi casa cargaba leña porque quería, si no quería no buscaba leña. Aquí todo es por obligación"* **[A]**. Also *"Acababa con la espalda llena de peladuras"*, on an early march with badly packed equipment **[A]**; recorded here, not used as a stop quote.

**The assembly.** *"Todos respondimos que matarlo"* **[A: memoir preview]**, the column's collective verdict after a man named Gonzalo killed a woman named Maribel. **Corroborated by nothing outside the memoir; undated and unlocated there.**

## 3. The Urabá war under Frente 5

**The daughter.** A daughter born in **1991**, kidnapped at age five by paramilitaries, who escaped **[A: El Colombiano launch interview]**.

**Corrected upstream error.** An earlier pass had her abandoning a **newborn** to return to combat. Unsupported by any reachable source; dropped.

**La Chinita, Apartadó, 23 January 1994** **[A]**. Between 35 and 37 killed at a neighborhood gathering in the barrio; attributed to Frente 5 of the Bloque Noroccidental. Spanish Wikipedia's article *Masacre de La Chinita* **names Elda Neyis Mosquera "Karina" explicitly** among the former commanders held to account, alongside Joverman Sánchez Arroyave "Rubén", who led the front.

**Corrected upstream error.** An earlier pass hedged this as "usually assigned to other FARC militia commanders, not confirmed as Karina's". That over-corrected: she is named in the encyclopedia's own article. The hedge is withdrawn.

**Churidó (corregimiento of Apartadó), 1995** **[A]**. **Carepa, 1995** **[A]**; the same town holds the Brigada XVII headquarters where she is later kept (§6). **Finca Osaka, 1996** **[R]**: the farm is not geocoded in any reachable source, and 7.8, -76.65 is an acknowledged Urabá-zone placeholder, labelled as such in the stop.

## 4. Frente 47: the command years

**The eye.** Right eye lost to a grenade explosion in combat at **Pavarandó, 1998** **[A]**.

**Command.** Chief of **Frente 47, Bloque Noroccidental, from 1998** **[A: Spanish and English Wikipedia; Infobae]**.

**Corrected upstream error.** An earlier pass called her "the highest-ranking woman in the organization". Spanish Wikipedia says only *"una de las pocas mujeres en alcanzar una posición destacada"*; the superlative is softened accordingly. **[A, softened]**

**Geographic re-anchoring.** An earlier pass anchored the Frente 47 command on Ituango, northern Antioquia. Wrong twice over: the coordinate given was not Ituango's seat, and Ituango was Frente 18/36 ground. Spanish Wikipedia gives Frente 47's departments as **Antioquia, Caldas and Risaralda**, and her attack record clusters in the eastern Antioquia and Caldas corridor. The journey re-anchors on **Sonsón (5.7139, -75.3106)**, labelled a reconstruction. **[R]**

**Attack record**, all **[A: Spanish and English Wikipedia]** unless noted:

| Date | Place | Recorded outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 1999 | Juradó, Chocó | Fusiliers battalion position taken; 25 soldiers dead, 12 captured |
| Jul 2000 | Arboleda (corregimiento of Pensilvania), Caldas | 13 police killed |
| Sep 2000 | Cerro Montezuma, Pueblo Rico, Risaralda | 13 military dead, 20 wounded |
| Jun 2002 | Caldas, municipality unnamed | 13 police killed |
| Dec 2005 | not stated | 8 police killed, 30 captured |
| 4 Mar 2006 | Montebonito, Marulanda, Caldas | 5 dead including an infant of 7 months, 10 wounded, 10 houses burned |

The **June 2002** and **December 2005** entries keep acknowledged placeholder coordinates, since the sources name no municipality at all; both are labelled as gaps in `date_confidence`. The Montebonito casualty detail is corroborated by El Espectador's contemporary coverage **[A]**.

**Coordinate corrections applied across the file**, against OpenStreetMap or the Spanish Wikipedia municipal articles: Churidó 7.8657, -76.6670; Currulao 7.9970, -76.6361; Pavarandó Grande 7.2776, -76.5573 (the earlier pin matched neither the site nor its own stated justification, Mutatá's seat being 7.24333, -76.43583); Cerro Montezuma 5.2554, -76.1179, the base rather than Pueblo Rico's centre; Montebonito 5.1646, -75.2009, the earlier pin matching neither the corregimiento nor Marulanda's seat.

## 5. The bounty and the desertion

**Contradiction 3 and a refuted figure.** An earlier pass carried a **United States reward of roughly $1,000,000**. **Refuted as stated**: no US government reward for Karina appears in any reachable source. English Wikipedia carries only the bare phrase *"the $900,000 bounty on her head"*, attributed to no government and supported by none of its own cited sources. The million-dollar figure is **dropped**; the $900,000 survives only as an **unsourced press figure [R]**.

**FARC counter-bounty.** The FARC is reported to have placed **300 million pesos** on her after her desertion **[R: Infobae coverage of the memoir; single source]**.

**Iván Ríos.** Her fellow commander was killed by his own security chief in March 2008, the precedent she cites **[A: English Wikipedia, following the Los Angeles Times]**.

**The surrender, 18 May 2008** **[A]**. She gave herself up with **two other guerrilleros, one of them her sentimental partner and chief of security**, presenting **a rifle wound to her right arm** **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, verbatim]**.

**Contradiction 4, unresolved: who received her.** Spanish Wikipedia: *"se entregó frente a una oficina del Departamento Administrativo de Seguridad (DAS)"*, and **names no city**. English Wikipedia: she surrendered to the **Colombian military**, and places only the subsequent **news conference** in Medellín. The receiving body is contested and the Medellín location of the surrender is an inference from the press conference. The stop is **re-tagged as a reconstruction**; an earlier pass presented the Medellín DAS office as attested, and that was wrong.

**Her stated reasons.** English Wikipedia's sentence is **paraphrase, not quotation**, and is footnoted to the **Los Angeles Times, "FARC commander surrenders in Colombia", 20 May 2008**, not, as an earlier pass had it, to BBC Mundo or El Tiempo: army pressure, the deterioration of rebel forces, and her fear of being killed by her own troops for the bounty, in the wake of Iván Ríos being murdered by his bodyguards. Carried as indirect speech, never in quotation marks. **[R at the level of her exact words; A at the encyclopedia layer]**

## 6. The courts

**February 2009: 33 years for Arboleda** **[A]**, on charges of *homicidio agravado, terrorismo, rebelión, hurto calificado* and *daño en bien ajeno*, with a guilty plea taken for sentence reduction.

**Corrected upstream error.** An earlier pass labelled this a **Justicia y Paz** tribunal. It was an **ordinary criminal conviction**; the Justicia y Paz process produced the 2022 ruling. No source names the city, so the canonical Bogotá pin (4.5981, -74.076) stands in for the judicial district and is labelled as such.

**6 March 2009: conditional release** on condition that she act as a *gestora de paz*, a promoter of peace encouraging other rebels to demobilize **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia]**.

**2010: a further 40 years** from a specialized court in **Buga, Valle del Cauca** **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction 5, unresolved.** Spanish Wikipedia says exactly: *"Hasta 2017, estuvo recluida en la sede de la Brigada XVII del Ejército Nacional con sede en Urabá."* **An end year with no start year, and "Urabá" with no municipality.** That detention and the 2010 forty-year sentence both sit awkwardly beside the March 2009 conditional release, and no reachable source explains how the three coexist. An earlier pass invented a 2009 start, colliding with the release, and named Carepa. **Re-tagged [R]**; Carepa is factually the brigade's seat but is not in the source.

**2022: first FARC commander convicted under the Justice and Peace Law**, before the **Sala de Justicia y Paz of the Tribunal Superior de Bogotá**, with a group of other former combatants; she apologised to her victims **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction 6, unresolved.** The number of co-defendants. **El Colombiano** reports ten tried alongside her (eleven in total); **RCN Radio**'s headline says *"Karina y 8 exguerrilleros"*. Written throughout as "a group of other former combatants".

**A quotation that is not a quotation.** The sentence *"En este proceso ha pedido perdón a las víctimas"* exists verbatim, but it is **Spanish Wikipedia's own editorial prose**, footnoted to Semana of 10 June 2022. It is **not Karina speaking** and must never be set in quotation marks or attributed to her. Rendered in the journey as narration.

**Victim scale.** Judicial accounts attribute to her charges including extortive kidnapping, forced displacement, homicide, theft, forced disappearance, illegal recruitment of minors, and gender-based violence, affecting more than **4,500 registered victims** **[R: Infobae and JEP-linked reporting; not verified at the tribunal layer]**.

**The Alberto Uribe Sierra accusation.** She has been repeatedly linked, without proof, to the 1983 killing of the father of president Álvaro Uribe; she denies involvement and it remains unproven **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, which itself frames it as unproven]**. It is **not carried as a journey stop**: the killing predates her entry into the FARC on either dating, and no source geolocates her at it.

## 7. Second civil life

**Alternative sentencing and protection.** She completed eight years of alternative sentencing under the Justice and Peace Law and afterward requested continued protective measures, citing ongoing threats **[R: Infobae, 2016; single source]**.

**Work and public role.** Post-demobilization she has worked as a **dressmaker**; broader reporting frames her public role as an advocate speaking about demobilization and reincorporation **[A: Semana profile, reached only through secondary summary]**.

**Gap: the landmine campaign.** The brief attributed to her a dedicated **anti-landmine advocacy campaign**. **No source located in this pass documents one**, across Spanish and English Wikipedia, La FM, Infobae, El Colombiano, or general search. Widely repeated in secondary characterizations and confirmed by nothing, it is **absent from the journey file**. A corpus-wide grep also found no atlas file containing the word "landmine".

**Gap: the reunion with the daughter.** Sources describe a daughter raised apart during the war and a later reunion. **No source names where or when.** No coordinates were invented; it is not a stop.

**The memoir, October 2025.** *Volver a ser Elda: una biografía íntima de alias Karina*, co-written with the researcher **Gustavo Duncan**, published by **Debate (Penguin Random House)**, built from some **60 hours of recorded conversation** over roughly two years of editing **[A: El Colombiano launch interview, timestamped 2025-10-04 21:23:23]**. An earlier pass dated the release to **2 September 2025**; the timestamp places it in **October**. Spanish Wikipedia's own reference for the book is that same interview, so the bibliographic authority is the newspaper. The earlier "publisher, release date and translations unconfirmed" gap is **closed except for translations**, on which nothing was found.

**Her retrospective judgement.** She states in the launch coverage that she regrets the **24 years** she spent in the FARC and that the organization did harm to the society it claimed to serve **[A as to substance: El Colombiano, where the twenty-four-year figure is confirmed]**. The verbatim wordings *"me arrepiento de haber pasado 24 años en las FARC"* and *"sólo le hizo daño a la sociedad"*, circulated as La FM quotations, **could not be located on any reachable La FM page**, so the journey attributes the sentiment without quotation marks. She has also said she understood the organization's true motives only after some twenty years inside it **[R: La FM, unverified at source]**.

**Attested memoir quotation used instead:** *"Las FARC nunca perdonaron mi deserción y no han dejado de verme como una traidora"* **[A: memoir preview]**.

## 8. Interlocks with the existing corpus

Named in campa where the paths genuinely cross:

- **fidel_castro** and **che_guevara**: in Bogotá in 1948 and 1952 respectively, at the Bogotazo and under "the leaden peace" of the Gómez dictatorship, the violence whose rural aftermath becomes the FARC. **[A at the corpus-file layer]**
- **tamara_bunke**: a woman given a war name by the same practice, dead under it in Bolivia. Juxtaposition, not a documented link. **[R]**
- **camilo_torres_restrepo**: the ELN took his name in 1987, and his file's 2016 stop has Havana hosting the talks that produce the FARC accord. Cited at the 2022 stop. **[A]**
- **policarpa_salavarrieta** and **manuela_saenz**: the corpus's other Bogotá-anchored women of armed struggle, sharing the canonical pin. Cited at the 2009 sentencing. **[R]**

**Canonical pin inherited byte-identically:** Bogotá **4.5981, -74.076**, used at three stops.

**Declined.** `griselda_blanco` (Medellín geography only), `manuel_noriega` (overlap stated in neither file), `nelson_mandela` (structural parallel only). The Havana pins were **not used**: she was not at the talks. Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris and Buenos Aires have no attested link to her.
