# Ferdinand Magellan: research report

*Slug `ferdinand_magellan`. Fernão de Magalhães / Hernando de Magallanes, c. 1480 to 27 April 1521. Journey file: `ferdinand_magellan.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops, calendar `julian`, 11 quotes.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim reaching this file at second hand. Contradictions are listed where found and are **not adjudicated**. Gaps are stated as gaps.

---

## 1. Origin: a beginning that is not documented

Almost nothing of the first twenty-five years is on paper.

- Born in northern Portugal around 1480 **[R]**. No register, no date, no town. Four traditions compete: Sabrosa, Porto, Vila Nova de Gaia, Ponte da Barca.
- Modern Portuguese scholarship (Torre do Tombo materials, *Encyclopaedia of Portuguese Expansion*, José Manuel Garcia) treats **Porto** as the strongest candidate **[R]**. This reaches the file at second hand through a research dossier; I did not check the archive. Best-attested candidate, not fact.
- The **Sabrosa** claim is a documented forgery **[A, Wikipedia "Sabrosa"]**: the documents were manufactured in the eighteenth century by **António Luís Álvares Pereira Coelho da Silva Castelo-Branco e Magalhães (1751-1802)** to present himself as Magellan's heir. The town keeps the claim, the statue, and its civic ties to Cebu City and Puerto San Julián regardless. Both stops are in the file.
- Page in **Queen Leonor's** household from about twelve, c. 1492; crown service from 1495 under **Manuel I** **[R]**, the 1492 placement from the dossier, unverified. No document from the education survives.

**Coordinate correction.** The pool gave Sabrosa twice, as 41.2667, -7.5667 and 41.2844, -7.5950, about 2 km apart. Wikipedia gives 41°17′4″N 7°35′42″W = **41.2844, -7.5950**, which the file uses.

## 2. Eight years in the East, 1505-1512

- **March 1505**: sails from Lisbon in the armada of **Dom Francisco de Almeida**, first viceroy of Portuguese India **[A]**.
- **1506**: wounded at the **Battle of Cannanore** **[A]**. Detail descends through the Portuguese chroniclers, Gaspar Correia among them, not from any log in his own hand.
- **1505-1512**: Malabar service based on **Cochin / Fort Emmanuel**, first European fort in Asia **[A]**. The dossier also names Quilon/Kollam; no landmark confirmed, not in the file.
- **3 February 1509, Battle of Diu.** Honest gap, stated inside the stop. The battle is beyond dispute. Magellan's presence rests on chronicle tradition and on every later biographer, and **no document from the day names him**; the geography lens recorded that it had not re-fetched a source. Tagged **[R]** and written into the campa as unprovable.
- **1509, Malacca** with **Diogo Lopes de Sequeira** and **Francisco Serrão**: ambush, collapse, retreat **[A]**. That Magellan went back for Serrão is **[R]**. Serrão's later letters from Ternate, origin of the whole Moluccas project, are lost and known only by report **[R]**.
- **October 1510, Goa**: sat in Albuquerque's council, took part in the November assault **[R]**, from the *Encyclopaedia of Portuguese Expansion* **as reported inside a dossier, unconfirmed**. The recapture itself is standard **[A]**.
- **1511, Malacca**: took part in Albuquerque's conquest **[A]**; acquired the enslaved man baptised **Enrique** **[A]**; brought him to Portugal in 1512 **[A]**.

## 3. The fall from favour, 1513-1517

**A live contradiction, carried unresolved into the file.** Wikipedia "Ferdinand Magellan" has him sustain a leg wound at Azemmour in August 1513 that left a permanent limp. Wikipedia "Azemmour" says only that he "lost his horse in skirmishes outside the city," no wound. Both statements are in the stop's `date_confidence`; neither is silently adopted. The campa states both and ends on the open question, since a lost horse does not account for the limp everyone reports in Seville four years later.

**Correction applied.** The chronology lens carried "1514-05-15: receives his last offer of employment." That is inverted. Wikipedia states that as of **15 May 1514 he received no further offers**: the date marks the end of offers. The inverted reading also contradicted the pool's own next entry, the 1515 crewman's berth he refused. The file uses the end-of-offers reading **[A]**.

- **1515**: offered, and refuses, a crewman's place on another man's ship **[A]**.
- **1517**: final quarrel with Manuel I, renunciation of Portuguese nationality, departure for Castile **[A]**. The content of the last audience is **[R]**.

## 4. Seville and the contract, 1517-1519

- **October 1517**: arrives in Seville, builds a network around the **Casa de la Contratación**: Diogo Barbosa, Juan de Aranda, Bishop Fonseca, Cristóbal de Haro **[R]**. The Casa's institutional role is solid; its housing inside the Real Alcázar in this period, and the network as a set, come from the dossier unverified.
- **1517**: marries **Beatriz Barbosa** **[R]**. No church, no house, no month located; the file uses the atlas's standing Seville pin and says so. Son **Rodrigo** born in Seville 1518 **[R]**.
- **22 March 1518, Valladolid**: the *Capitulaciones* **[A]**. **Title correction**: the pool said "admiral of the fleet." The contract title is **capitán general**; "admiral" is loose usage in the biography article. Order of Santiago commandership confirmed.
- **24 August 1519**: the will **[A]**. Provision for Beatriz and Rodrigo, an entailed inheritance, religious bequests, a legacy to the page **Cristóbal Rebelo**, and the **emancipation of Enrique effective at Magellan's death**.

**Date and place correction applied.** The pool gave "10 August 1519, Sanlúcar de Barrameda, departure," welding a Seville date to a Sanlúcar place, and its own geography lens contradicted it. The file splits this into **10 August 1519, departure from Seville down the Guadalquivir**, five weeks lying at Sanlúcar, then **20 September 1519, sailing from Sanlúcar into the Atlantic** **[A]**. Crew of about 270 confirmed.

## 5. Atlantic, Patagonia, strait, 1519-1520

- **Canaries call, late September 1519** **[R]**, dossier only, not re-verified. **Late November 1519**, first American landfall near **Cabo de Santo Agostinho** **[A]**. **December 1519, Guanabara Bay** **[A]**.
- **Date correction**: the pool put the garroting of **Antonio Salamón** in January 1520. He was executed **20 December 1519**, and the fleet left the bay on 27 December, so a January garroting there is impossible.
- **Río de la Plata**: sighted **11 January 1520**, worked into February **[A]**. The chronology lens dated the episode February against its own geography lens; corrected.
- **31 March to 21 August 1520, Puerto San Julián** **[A]**. Easter mutiny of **Cartagena, Quesada, Mendoza**; Mendoza killed by **Gonzalo Gómez de Espinosa**; Quesada executed; Cartagena marooned. The legality of Magellan's proceedings was contested by his opponents and the testimony is partisan on both sides. Not adjudicated; noted in the stop.
- **May 1520**: **Santiago** wrecked south of San Julián, crew survives **[A for the wreck]**. **Site unverified**, a fetch returned 404; the pin (-50.13, -68.47) is an approximation and says so.
- **21 October 1520**, entry at **Cabo Vírgenes** **[A for the date]**, the cape as the entrance point being standard geography, not landmark-verified. **c. 20 November 1520**, **San Antonio** deserts under **Estêvão Gomes**, reaching Seville **6 May 1521** with an account hostile to Magellan **[A]**.
- **1 November 1520**: Magellan names the passage **Estrecho de Todos los Santos** **[A]**. **Correction**: the geography lens gave "1-8 November 1520" for the passage while calling it a 38-day crossing in the same sentence. 1 November is the naming, not the entry; the First Narrows were passed in late October; the crossing runs 21 October to 28 November.
- **28 November 1520**, exit into the Pacific at **Cabo Deseado / Cabo Pilar** **[A for the date]**. **Coordinate correction**: the pool gave -52.9309, -74.6963, some 24 km south of the cape in open water. Cabo Pilar, Desolación Island's northwestern point, is approximately 52°43′S 74°42′W = **-52.717, -74.700**, which the file uses. **Needs a gazetteer check before publication**: the article confirms the point but gives no figures.
- Magellan did **not** discover the Pacific in any absolute sense: Indigenous navigators had crossed it for millennia and Balboa saw its eastern shore in 1513. The confidence field says so; the campa does not claim otherwise.

## 6. The Philippines and the death, 1521

- **6 March 1521, Guam** after about three months and twenty days at sea, roughly 30 dead of starvation and scurvy; conflict over stolen property, houses burned, islanders killed **[A]**. The **Umatac Bay** site is "traditionally cited," not certain **[R]**. **16-24 March, Homonhon** **[A]**.
- **28-31 March 1521, Limasawa**: Rajah Kolambu and Rajah Siaui; first recorded Christian Mass in the Philippines by **Fr. Pedro de Valderrama** on Easter Sunday; cross on the high point **[A]**. The identification of Pigafetta's **Mazaua** with Limasawa rather than a site near Butuan is mainstream and was the subject of long Philippine controversy. **Not re-litigated**; flagged in the stop.
- **7 April**, arrival at Cebu and alliance with **Rajah Humabon**. **14 April**, baptism as **Carlos** and **Juana**, gift of the image later venerated as the **Santo Niño de Cebú**. **21 April**, the cross planted at the landing place. All **[A]**.

**Correction applied twice over.** The pool welded these three dates in two different ways: the chronology lens put arrival, baptism and Santo Niño all on 7 April; the geography lens said the 21 April cross-planting "marked the baptism." They are three separate days. The file splits them into three stops.

- The **Santo Niño's 44-year custody gap is real and undocumented**. The image reappears 27-28 April 1565 after Legazpi's men burned the settlement, found by **Juan Camus** in a pine box **[A as the account]**. Continuity with the 1521 gift is devotional tradition, not record.
- **27 April 1521, Mactan** **[A, citing Pigafetta]**: about sixty men, 49 engaged and 11 with the boats; coral shallows holding the boats off; Magellan hit first by a **poisoned arrow through his unarmoured legs**, then speared in the arm, then brought down by a **kampilan**. **Correction**: the pool's wound list omitted the poisoned arrow, Pigafetta's own detail, not a later accretion. The dossier's claim that Magellan's royal instructions counselled avoiding unnecessary local conflict, making Mactan an overreach **[R]**, rests on a text not fetched; carried as a lead in the stop's sources, not asserted in prose.

## 7. After the death

- **The body was never recovered.** Humabon offered merchandise in ransom; Lapulapu's side refused. **No account records what was done with the remains and no grave has ever been found** **[A]**. This sits against the burial clause of the 1519 will, which is why the two are in the same stop.
- **1 May 1521, the Cebu banquet**: Duarte Barbosa, João Serrão and others killed **[A]**. **Source correction**: the pool attributed the rival account of Enrique's role to **Maximilianus Transylvanus**. It is **Giovanni Battista Ramusio** who claimed Enrique warned the chief; Pigafetta has Enrique survive. Left unresolved because it cannot be settled.
- **8 November 1521, Tidore** **[R for the date; the fetched article does not carry it]**. The Moluccas arrival and the judgement that only *Victoria* was seaworthy are **[A]**.
- **6 September 1522**: *Victoria* makes **Sanlúcar**, entering **Seville 8 September** **[A]**. **Place correction**: the pool put the 6 September date at Seville. **Survivors**: 18 Europeans plus 3 Moluccans, 21 in all. Magellan did not complete a circumnavigation; the completed voyage belongs to **Elcano** and the crew, and the file does not blur it.

## 8. Afterlife material: used, folded, and dropped

- **Folded into existing stops, not given stops of their own.** Mactan Shrine: obelisk **1866**, national shrine RA 5695 **1969**, Lapulapu bronze only **1981** **[A]**. Magellan's Cross: pavilion **1834**, tindalo casing **1835** after devotees chipped the relic for its healing power, National Cultural Treasure **14 April 2021**, closed by the **2025 Cebu earthquake** with candles still lit outside **[A]**.
- **Pigafetta's transmission**, in `sources` and `suggested_refs` only: journal lost, four manuscripts (one Italian at the **Biblioteca Ambrosiana**), **Amoretti 1800** with silent alterations, **da Mosto 1894** critical edition, **Robertson 1906** first English from the Ambrosiana Italian. **Publisher correction**: Robertson translated for the **Arthur H. Clark Company of Cleveland**, not the Hakluyt Society; Hakluyt belongs only to **Stanley of Alderley's 1874** volume. **Date dropped**: the pool's "1524" first publication is unsupported, earliest abridgment c. 1525.
- **Unused, with reasons.** Punta Arenas monument: the pool's gap claim is partly wrong, Wikipedia gives "a 1920 memorial" in Plaza Muñoz Gamero, though no sculptor and no toe-rubbing legend. Unused, being a monument in a city Magellan never entered. Magellanic Clouds: recorded by **Vespucci in 1502** and long known under older names to southern observers **[A]**; unused, because no stop has Magellan observing them and a later naming is not a place he stood. The **2019-2022 "Magellan-Elcano" formula [R]**: not asserted in any campa.
- **UNESCO Memory of the World, 2023**, joint Portugal/Spain inscription **[R]**, a `suggested_ref` on the Valladolid stop.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

Magellan is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are licensed and none were written. Per the standing instruction, relations found in research that are **not rooted in Magellan's own life** are recorded here rather than silently deleted. All of the following are **forward**, later people reaching back at Magellan, and a forward reach is not covered by the exception at all.

| relation found | direction | disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **Pedro de Mendoza**, 1535 armada at Sanlúcar; his file names Magellan outright | forward | Not named. The Sanlúcar **pin** is inherited byte-identical: cartography, not a reference. |
| **Charles Darwin** at Puerto San Julián, 1834, "where Magellan hanged a mutineer three hundred years earlier" | forward | Not named. Darwin's file already carries it. |
| **Bernardo O'Higgins**, dying words reported as "¡Magallanes! ¡Magallanes!", Lima 1842 | forward | Not named. The strongest pull in the pool and the hardest to leave out; still 321 years the wrong way. The one worth a ruling. |
| **Roca** (1899), **Sulcic** (1955), **Pinochet** (Dawson Island, 1973), **Saint-Exupéry** (1930) | forward | Not named. Shared geography with a waterway carrying his name, which the map already shows. |
| **Hernandarias**, Río de la Plata gaucho chain | forward, reconstructed | Not named. Neither file mentions Magellan. |
| **Balboa**, first European sighting of the Pacific, 1513 | backward, but **no journey file exists** | Not an interlock. Kept as a corrective inside the Cabo Deseado `date_confidence`. |

**Two relations were judged Magellan's own and are named in campas.**

1. **`columbus`**, once, at the Valladolid stop: the westward route to the Moluccas "is what Columbus promised this same crown and did not deliver." Not shared geography. Magellan's contract is the same proposition Columbus sold to the same monarchy through the same institution.
2. **`saint_erasmus`**, once, at the Puerto San Julián corpo santo stop. Pigafetta recorded the lights on **Magellan's own mastheads**, seen by **his own crews**, read as Saint Erasmus keeping watch. Rooted in his life, not a co-location.

**Three posthumous stops are declared as such.** Tidore (8 Nov 1521), Sanlúcar (6 Sep 1522) and Seville (8 Sep 1522) are places Magellan never reached. They are retained because they are his own fleet, under his own commission, completing the contract he signed at Valladolid, within eighteen months of his death, which is a different case from a later admirer visiting a monument. Each `date_confidence` opens with "posthumous," and the last two add "not Magellan's own stop: the return belongs to Juan Sebastián Elcano and the surviving crew." **On a stricter reading of the ownership rule these three come out and the file ends at the Cebu banquet.**

## 10. Pin inheritance

- **Sanlúcar de Barrameda** `36.7783, -6.3517`, byte-identical from `pedro_de_mendoza.journey.json`, used twice (1519 departure, 1522 return).
- **Puerto San Julián** `-49.3, -67.72`, byte-identical from `saint_erasmus.journey.json`, which had itself moved the corpo santo off an impossible pin at the Strait's Atlantic mouth. Used three times. Geography sources give -67.7167; the atlas pin wins.
- **Seville** `37.3891, -5.9945`, from `columbus.journey.json`, for the marriage and the will. The **Torre del Oro** (`37.3833, -5.9958`) serves separately as the river-mooring proxy for the 1519 departure and 1522 arrival, a landmark proxy, not a documented berth.
- **Canaries: pin NOT inherited.** The atlas's Canaries pin is **La Gomera** (28.0916, -17.1115) on the Columbus file, a different island from the Tenerife call here. The file uses 28.4636, -16.2518 and explains why.
- **Nothing to inherit east of the Cape.** A grep found no pins at Cebu, Mactan, the Marianas or the Moluccas; the whole Philippine and Southeast Asian leg stands alone. No Rio de Janeiro pin was found either, flagged as worth a fresh check rather than confirmed absent; Guanabara Bay is pinned at -22.7903, -43.1556.
- **The Cebu banquet coordinate is a duplicate, flagged as such.** The pool gave 10.293544, 123.901953, which is Magellan's Cross, copied from the previous entry. The banquet site is not independently located.
