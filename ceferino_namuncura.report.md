# Ceferino Namuncurá (1886-1905): research report
*Slug: `ceferino_namuncura`. Compiled 2026-08-14 from the verified research pool, corrections applied. Companion: `ceferino_namuncura.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved; bad pool coordinates are corrected in the appendix, not silently overwritten.

## 0. What this life is

Three generations sit inside eighteen years of biography. Juan Calfucurá builds a confederation of tents on the pampa salt flats and treats with Buenos Aires as a power; his son Manuel Namuncurá inherits it, loses it to Julio Argentino Roca's army, and converts the remainder into a place inside the Argentine state; Manuel's sixth child, Ceferino, is handed to the Salesians at eleven, dies of tuberculosis on an island in the Tiber at eighteen, and is beatified in his birth village a century later. The file's hardest claim is **[R]**, that Manuel placed his son with the missionaries as political adaptation rather than piety. No document has him saying so; it is consensus framing, and the campa says so.

## 1. The line before him (c.1811-1873)

- **Manuel Namuncurá's birth [R].** es.wikipedia: about 1811, in Araucanía. The year is back-calculated from an age of ninety-seven at his death in 1908; no register exists. The pool said "the region of Llaima," a misreading: the source says he crossed with the *llaimache*, a lineage, not a district. Pinned at -38.9833, -72.6333, the Pitrufquén / Lago Colico country given elsewhere as Calfucurá's claimed birthplace, and marked as the lineage's country.
- **The crossing, 1831 [A]** (es.wikipedia). With the llaimache and Calfucurá; year only.
- **The Chilean-blood quote [A as press claim, not as documented speech].** El Mercurio (Santiago), 18 November 2007, reports Calfucurá saying on 27 April 1861: «Yo soy chileno, me vine a estas tierras por orden de don Juan Manuel Rosas». The pool truncated this to «Yo soy chileno [...]», which inverts the sense: the elided half grounds his presence on the pampa in an order from Rosas. Full sentence used. Cautions: a 2007 newspaper reporting an 1861 utterance, no archival document behind it; the same article's dubious claim that he fought «contra las fuerzas realistas»; and the pool's calling him Ceferino's *great*-grandfather where El Mercurio says «abuelo».
- **Baptism at Paraná, 1854 [A]** (es.wikipedia). Takes the name Manuel, godfather Justo José de Urquiza, oath to the 1853 constitution; church unnamed, coordinate the city centre.
- **Succession, 1873 [A].** Calfucurá dies June 1873; Manuel takes the cacicazgo against **José Millaqueucurá**, whom es.wikipedia calls his *brother* (the pool's "half-brother" is unsupported), and his cousin Bernardo Namuncurá. Consolidation dated 1875.

## 2. The frontier war and the surrender (1874-1884)

- **The peace terms [A as to the list; poor source; date corrected].** The demand list («Cuarenta mil pesos oro, cuatro mil seiscientas vacas...», quoted in full in the journey file) needed two corrections. (1) «jabón» was interpolated *inside* the quotation marks by a Wikipedia editor; the source (Instituto Malvinas, via Wayback) puts the soap in its own next sentence, so the campa keeps it as prose outside the quote. (2) That source places the demands before the newly installed Avellaneda government (in office 12 October 1874), while es.wikipedia's «A finales de 1875» belongs to a different sentence; the stop carries 1874-12-01 with the conflict stated. **Source quality flagged:** an amateur free-host page, no archival citation.
- **The malón of 1875 [A, numbers contested].** Tres Arroyos, Tandil, Azul. es.wikipedia "Conquista del Desierto" tabulates «5000 de Namuncurá (1875)»; "Manuel Namuncurá" gives 3,500 to 4,000 and names **Catriel, Pincén and Baigorrita**, not Catriel alone as the pool had it. The file gives the range and all three.
- **Roca's two quotes [A].** «A mi juicio, el mejor sistema para concluir con los indios, ya sea extinguiéndolos o arrojándolos al otro lado del río Negro...», to Avellaneda on 19 October 1875, before Roca was minister of war, in Walther, *La conquista del desierto* (1970), p. 239; and «¡Qué disparate la zanja de Alsina!...», in Lugones, *Roca* (1938), p. 176, undated in the source, as `quote_source` says. Both via es.wikipedia; neither book opened.
- **1879 [A]** (es.wikipedia). Levalle and Freyre kill more than two hundred warriors; Namuncurá abandons the tolderías and goes west. Year only, no day.
- **Surrender at Ñorquín, 21 March 1884 [A].** About three hundred hungry, unarmed warriors. **Overstatement corrected:** the afterlife lens said this "ended armed Mapuche resistance," but es.wikipedia has fighting continuing after March 1884, so the campa says what ends is the confederation.
- **Bahía Blanca and the steamship Pomona to Buenos Aires, June 1884 [A].** Month only; the stop uses 1884-06-15 and calls the day a placeholder in `date_confidence`.
- **San Ignacio and the colonelcy, 1884 [A].** Land at the indigenous colony, honorary rank of colonel. Year only.

## 3. Chimpay: birth, water, font (1886-1894)

- **Birth, 26 August 1886 [A]** (both editions). Chimpay, Río Negro, sixth child of Manuel and Rosario Burgos.
- **Who Rosario Burgos was: unresolved, three readings.** El Mercurio (2007): «la mestiza chilena»; es.wikipedia body text: «la cautiva chilena», a captive; Nicoletti (2007): no qualifier at all. The campa states all three and settles none. Further: the sentence the pool quoted as «...Rosario Burgos [y de] Manuel Namuncurá» **does not exist**; it is a Wikipedia editor's bracket-splice made for the `|cita=` parameter, and the actual archived sentence is quoted whole in the file.
- **The near-drowning, c.1887 [R].** en.wikipedia carries it as legend: no date, place or circumstance. Kept as tradition; year and coordinate flagged as placeholders.
- **Baptism by Domingo Milanesio: direct contradiction, not resolved.** es.wikipedia gives 24 December 1887; en.wikipedia says "age 8" by the same priest, around 1894, and neither reconciles them. The stop takes the es.wikipedia date and flags the conflict.
- **Manuel's Buenos Aires petitions, 1886 / 1894 / 1897 [A].** No stop each; they run in the prose of the Pomona and San Ignacio stops.

## 4. Buenos Aires: the Salesian bargain (c.1896-1902)

- **Talleres Nacionales de Marina, carpenter's apprentice [A as fact, R as to place and date].** en.wikipedia attests the apprenticeship at "the National Workshops of the Navy" with no date and no site.
- **Colegio Pío IX, Almagro, 20 September 1897 [A].** Both editions, to the day.
- **Trap recorded for re-searchers.** es.wikipedia's article titled "Colegio Pío IX" is a **different** Salesian school, in Montevideo (-34.7958, -56.2491), at a deceptively close latitude. Do not follow that title.
- **First communion, 8 September 1898, and confirmation by Bishop Gregorio Romero, 5 November 1899 [A].** Both es.wikipedia, to the day.
- **"Prince of Christian Doctrine," c.1900 [R].** The pool's lenses disagreed on tag ([R] chronology, [A] geography) and date. Carried [R] in the 1901 stop.
- **Carlos Gardel: contested, and the pool contradicted itself.** Chronology tagged it [A] and wrote "sings in the school chorus alongside"; geography tagged the same fact [R]. Carried as **[R]**: es.wikipedia "Carlos Gardel" gives the Pío IX schooling only under the contested *francesista* hypothesis of his birth and in the conditional («habría estudiado... compañero de coro de Ceferino Namuncurá»), against a *uruguayista* hypothesis placing him in Montevideo in 1891-93, with «no total unanimidad»; it.wikipedia does not mention Gardel; en.wikipedia supports only that Ceferino joined the academy on 2 April 1901. The chorus detail is beyond any source and is not written.
- **Tuberculosis diagnosed, 1902 [A].** en.wikipedia: early 1902, no site named.

## 5. Viedma and the crossing (1903-1904)

- **Colegio San Francisco de Sales, Viedma, February 1903 [A]** (all three editions). Transferred for climate and a lighter study load, enrolled as a seminary aspirant.
- **Embarkation, 19 July 1904 [A].** From Buenos Aires with Archbishop Giovanni Cagliero; neither dock nor ship named.
- **Correction: one date was doing double duty.** The pool used 19 July 1904 for **both** the embarkation and the Turin arrival; a 1904 crossing took weeks. The Turin arrival is placed in August and marked inferred.
- **Genoa landfall [R].** No source consulted names the Italian port of arrival; Genoa is the standard 1904 landfall for Salesian traffic between the Plata and Turin. Labelled a reconstruction; the unrecorded three-week passage is itself a fact.

## 6. Italy and the death (1904-1905)

- **Valdocco, Turin, August 1904 [A place, R month]** (it.wikipedia), **and Don Bosco's tomb [R]**, the tomb visit carried only by corazones.org, a devotional site, and written as "according to the devotional accounts."
- **Audience with Pius X, 27 September 1904 [A].** es.wikipedia and corazones.org give the day, en.wikipedia only the month; the poncho presented to the pope is agreed across sources. One Vatican pin (41.9029, 12.4534) serves every Vatican stop.
- **Villa Sora, Frascati, autumn 1904 [A].** Coordinate approximates the town; the villa was not confirmed.
- **Fatebenefratelli, March 1905, and death there on 11 May [A].** The pool gave 28 March for the admission on corazones.org alone; all three encyclopaedias give only "March 1905," and all three agree on the death date, while corazones.org gives «11 de noviembre», apparently conflating it with the beatification anniversary. Dr José Lapponi, physician to Leo XIII and Pius X, attended him (es.wikipedia only).
- **The last words: three sources, one arguing against the other two.** The pool cited the wrong one of three distinct references. Correct chain: the words are cited to **Noriega, *Semblanza* (2000), p. 26**, as «Según la mayor parte de sus biógrafos»; **Pedemonte, *Cartas y escritos*, Carta 37 (1949), p. 46** supports only the claim that the prayer also appears in his correspondence; the thesis that it was moved to the death scene is **Nicoletti, Revista Tefros 7 (1-2), nota 50**, not Wikipedia's own observation. Wording confirmed verbatim in the wikitext. **Neither Noriega nor Pedemonte could be opened.**
- **Burial, 12 May 1905, Campo Verano [A].** es.wikipedia, to the day; the plain-wooden-cross detail rests on that source alone.

## 7. The bones: repatriation and the cause (1908-2007)

- **Manuel Namuncurá dies, 31 July 1908 [A].** At about ninety-seven, at San Ignacio; **the location of his grave is not known**. A permanent gap, and the file's closing figure: the father unlocated, the son moved four times.
- **The 1924 exhumation act [R as document, A as content].** Reproduced in es.wikipedia's "Traslado de sus restos": the mortuary register and two zinc plates, one reading «Ceferino Namuncurá de 18 años, nacido en Chimpay Rio Negro y muerto el 11 de Mayo de 1905»; the act on parchment, sealed in a waxed glass tube in the zinc urn, niche 358, row 2. Only the Wikipedia reproduction was seen.
- **Repatriation, 1924 [A]** (both editions agree). Sponsored by President Marcelo T. de Alvear, to the chapel at the former Fortín Mercedes near Pedro Luro.
- **Opening of the cause, 1944 or 1945: the pool inverted the attribution.** It said en.wikipedia gives 1944 and es.wikipedia 1945; the reverse is true. es.wikipedia: «El 2 de mayo de 1944 se inició la causa». en.wikipedia: "In 1945, a request... was presented to the Holy See." Probably two procedural steps, the Argentine opening and the Roman petition. Not merged; both printed.
- **Testimony and writings [A]** (en.wikipedia). Testimony 13 May to 10 July 1947, approval of his writings 10 June 1949; one stop holds both.
- **Pius XII approves the introduction, 3 March 1957 [A].** The pool attributed this to en.wikipedia with only a year; it is **es.wikipedia**, with a full date.
- **Venerable, 22 June 1972 [A].** Paul VI. The superlative ("first Argentine, first indigenous South American") is en.wikipedia's, **not** checked against a Vatican register.
- **Transfer into the Fortín Mercedes sanctuary, 1991 or 1992 [A, contested].** en.wikipedia 1991, es.wikipedia 1992, neither with a day.
- **The miracle [A, dating contested].** en.wikipedia: in 2000 Vatican-appointed pathologists confirm a medically unexplained recovery from uterine cancer by Valeria Herrera of Córdoba. Perfil (11 November 2007) has her diagnosed «hace ocho años», so around 1999, final dictamen mid-2006.
- **The Perfil headline is not her voice.** The pool quoted «Salvó mi vida y no voy a dejar que digan pavadas» as Herrera's words. The real headline reads «no voy a dejar que **se digan** pavadas», the dropped reflexive turning "I won't let nonsense be talked" into "I won't let them talk nonsense." More importantly it is a **sub-editor's headline**, appearing nowhere in the body. Her own words are used instead.
- **Beatification decree, 6 or 7 July 2007 [contested].** en.wikipedia 6 July, es.wikipedia 7 July; the file uses 7 July and flags it.
- **Beatification, 11 November 2007, Chimpay [A, crowd contested].** Cardinal Tarcisio Bertone presides as papal legate, not Benedict XVI in person. Crowd figures differ by an order of magnitude: es.wikipedia more than 100,000, en.wikipedia more than 10,000.

## 8. The cultrún, and where the body is now

- **12 August 2009 [A, department wrong in the source].** At the family's request the remains leave Fortín Mercedes for the San Ignacio Mapuche community in Neuquén, buried in a mound shaped like a **cultrún**, the ceremonial drum. Date, cultrún and family request confirmed by es.wikipedia.
- **es.wikipedia contradicts itself on the department.** It says «departamento Huiliches... a 60 km de Junín de los Andes». Both cannot hold: Huiliches is the smallest department, with Junín de los Andes as its own capital, and lists six Mapuche communities with no San Ignacio among them, while the Aluminé article **does** list San Ignacio as a paraje, its centroid about 60 km north of Junín. Read as **Aluminé**, the attribution an error inherited from the source.
- **Where the relics are is disputed.** es.wikipedia reports the 2009 removal; en.wikipedia still describes the Fortín Mercedes sanctuary in the present tense as the pilgrimage site, with no transfer mentioned. The architect attribution to Alejandro Santana did not surface in the fetched text and is **unconfirmed**; not used.
- **Chimpay, the Semana Ceferiniana [A, retagged upward].** The geography lens tagged the pilgrimage [R] on "general knowledge... not tied to a single named source." Too conservative: es.wikipedia "Chimpay" describes the annual Semana Ceferiniana, seven days of masses closing with mounted delegations from regional traditionalist centres, attendance around 40,000 on the 26 August feast, and Nicoletti (RUNA XXVII, 2007), read in the journal PDF, documents the same from Diario Río Negro. Retagged **[A]**. The pool's quoted passage splices body text with footnote 10, so the file quotes only the body sentence.
- **The memorial falls on 26 August, his birth date rather than his death date [A]**, so the annual gathering is a birthday. Popular name «El Lirio de la Patagonia».
- **Still Blessed, not canonized [A].** A second miracle is required; no source names a pending case or a date.

## 9. Interlocks written into the campa (mutual gaze)

- **`julio_argentino_roca`**: named at Buenos Aires 1875 (the letter to Avellaneda), Choele Choel and Fisque Menuco 1879, and the Pomona stop of 1884, pins inherited byte-identical (-34.6037/-58.3816, -39.2872/-65.6772, -39.0333/-67.5833). Fisque Menuco is load-bearing, fewer than 100 km from Chimpay: Ceferino is born inside the corridor Roca's column garrisoned.
- **`juan_manuel_de_rosas`**: named at the 1831 crossing, because Calfucurá's quoted claim grounds his presence on the pampa in Rosas's order. Rosas's Campaña al Desierto (Napostá 1834-05-25, -38.75/-62.28) is the precedent Roca's letter cites by name.
- Deliberately **not** written into a campa: **`low_dog`** and **`sitting_bull`** (analogies, not crossings), **`antoine_de_saint_exupery`** (coordinate adjacency), the **Kaaba** pin (21.4225, 39.8262), a category precedent, not a place he went, and **`mussolini`** (boarded with the Salesians at Faenza in 1893, 44.2856, 11.8834, and expelled; the only tie to Ceferino is that both passed through the same order's schools decades apart, no meeting, no relation. Originally named at Frascati; removed under the tightened house style, 2026-08-14).

## Appendix 2: corrections made at the style gate

Four errors of fact survived into the campa prose and were fixed in the journey file. (1) Two stops, the 1924 repatriation and the 2007 beatification, called Ceferino the **great-grandson** of Calfucurá; the line is Calfucurá to Manuel to Ceferino, so he is the **grandson**, exactly as §1 of this report already flagged against the pool. (2) The baptism stop had Manuel baptized "at thirty-three"; he was born c.1811 and baptized at Paraná in 1854, so **forty-three**. (3) The Fortín Mercedes stop said Alvear "was six years old when Ñorquín fell"; Alvear was born 4 October 1868 and was fifteen in March 1884, so the age claim is dropped rather than restated. (4) The Semana Ceferiniana stop, dated 2006 for the attendance figure, sat **after** the 2009 cultrún stop and broke the segment's chronology; it is moved ahead of the July 2007 decree, which is also where its own sentence about "a year before Rome made anything official" belongs. No source reading changed in any of the four.
