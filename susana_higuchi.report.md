# Susana Shizuko Higuchi Miyagawa (1950-2021): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-16. Slug: `susana_higuchi`. Civil engineer, builder, First Lady of
Peru 1990-1994, then the first public accuser of her own husband's government, and twice
a congresswoman of the republic that government had suspended.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline · **[R]** = reconstruction,
tradition, single-source allegation, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not**
adjudicated unless a source resolves them. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**Standing caution for this life.** Much of what is written about Higuchi consists of
*her own allegations*, denied at the time by the Fujimori camp and never adjudicated on
the facts. Those are tagged **[R]** and appear in the journey file as allegations, in her
voice, with the denial noted. They are not attested merely because they are repeated.

---

## 1. Lima origins, 1950-1974

- Born **26 April 1950 in Lima**, youngest of four children of **Tomás Higuchi** and
  **Seki Miyagawa**, Japanese immigrants to Peru. **[A, es/en.wikipedia]** Neither clinic
  nor district of birth is recorded; the journey file pins the city centroid and says so.
- Finished secondary school at **fifteen**. **[A]** The school's name is a live problem:
  Spanish Wikipedia's wording is **"colegio estatal Señora de la Asunción"**, a *state*
  school. The common expansion to "Colegio Nuestra Señora de la Asunción" is a different
  and better-known institution, and is **an embellishment of the source, flagged and not
  adopted** **[R for the name; A for the age and the ~1965 window]**. The San Isidro
  coordinate belongs to the *expanded* name, so the journey file carries it with an
  explicit warning in `date_confidence`.
- Worked before her marriage for the **El Sol tire company**, the family having settled
  in **La Victoria**. **[A, en.wikipedia]** District coordinate only; no street address.
- Enrolled at the **Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería** (Rímac) to read **civil
  engineering**, later specializing in hydraulics. **[A, es/en.wikipedia]** Her father
  Tomás already knew **Naoichi Fujimori**, father of Alberto; the marriage was made
  through that acquaintance. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- **Married Alberto Fujimori on 25 July 1974** at the **Capilla de la Virgen de la O**,
  Basílica de San Pedro, Cercado de Lima. **[A, es/en.wikipedia]** *Coordinate corrected*
  to **-12.0494, -77.0287** (OpenStreetMap); the earlier -12.0468, -77.029 sat ~290 m off,
  nearer the Plaza Mayor.
- **Four children in six years**, all in Lima: **Keiko Sofía** (25 May 1975), **Hiro
  Alberto** (Dec 1976), **Sachi Marcela** (Mar 1979), **Kenji Gerardo** (May 1980).
  **[A, es.wikipedia/Keiko_Fujimori]** *Corrected:* the last birth is 1980, not "the
  1980s" as an earlier draft had it.

## 2. Constructora Fuji, 1970s-1990

- Founded and ran the **Empresa Constructora Fuji** with Fujimori, developing lots in
  **San Borja** and housing in **Santiago de Surco**; English Wikipedia is explicit that
  she was the household's principal earner in these years. **[A, es/en.wikipedia]**
  District-level coordinates only; no lot identified and no company registry document
  located. **Gap.**
- Contributed **Fuji company funds** to the 1990 **Cambio 90** campaign despite early
  misgivings, and campaigned through Lima's **comedores populares**. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
  No individual comedor is named; the stop uses a general Lima coordinate.
- In 2016 she stated flatly that her father, not her husband, had paid for the
  children's schooling: *"Las enseñanzas de mis hijos han sido pagadas íntegramente por
  mi padre. Todos los años y de todos mis hijos. Mi padre era millonario."* **[A,
  RPP Noticias, verbatim]** See §7 for the dating problem attached to this quote.

## 3. First Lady, 1990-1992

- **First Lady of Peru from Fujimori's inauguration, 28 July 1990.** **[A]** Residence:
  the **Palacio de Gobierno (Casa de Pizarro)**. The journey file inherits the atlas's
  existing palace pin **-12.0463, -77.0311** from `pizarro.journey.json` rather than
  minting a new one: same building, same plot.
- Named **president of the Fundación por los Niños del Perú**, the vehicle for most of
  the First Lady's public work 1990-1994. **[A, es.wikipedia]** Office address not found;
  general Lima coordinate used. **Gap.**
- **March 1992: the donated-clothing denunciation.** She publicly accused **Rosa and
  Juana Fujimori, the president's sisters**, of diverting clothing donated from Japan
  through the NGO **Apenkai** and selling it in bazaars. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
  *Corrected relationship:* an earlier pass made Rosa the president's **sister-in-law**;
  Spanish Wikipedia has "Rosa y Juana Fujimori, **hermanas** del presidente", so they were
  Higuchi's sisters-in-law, not his. The press adds **Clorinda Ebisui**, of whom Higuchi
  said *"Eso me indigna."* **[A, El Tiempo (Bogotá), 28 March 1992]**
- Her own framing of the cost: *"Si por eso se me acusa de deslealtad, no sé a qué le
  llaman traición."* **[A, Exitosa Noticias, quoting 1992 statements]**

## 4. The autogolpe and the SIE years, 1992-1994

- **5 April 1992: Fujimori's self-coup.** Higuchi's account is that the family was moved
  **out of the Casa de Pizarro** and that she was then confined in installations of the
  **Servicio de Inteligencia del Ejército**, the Pentagonito. **[R, her own account,
  es.wikipedia]** *Corrected:* an earlier pass placed the confinement *inside the
  presidential palace*. That is wrong on her own telling.
- *Coordinate corrected, twice:* both SIE stops had been pinned at -12.0895, -77.0011,
  in northern San Borja, ~2.6 km from the complex. The **Cuartel General del Ejército /
  Pentagonito** is at **-12.1018, -76.9846** (OpenStreetMap), which the journey file uses.
- She alleged repeated interrogation in the **basements of the SIE**, later putting the
  count at **"fui torturada en más de 500 oportunidades"**, *more* than five hundred, not
  "roughly 500" as an earlier pass had it. **[R, her allegation, es.wikipedia]**
  **Fujimori denied it and attributed her scars to moxibustion.** **[A, that the denial
  was made; the fact itself is unadjudicated]**
- From confinement, in 1992: *"No me dejan salir de la residencia por orden de mi
  esposo, la reja está con cadena."* **[A, Exitosa Noticias, quoting 1992]**
- **23 August 1994: formally stripped of the title and duties of First Lady**, which
  passed to her daughter **Keiko Sofía, then nineteen**, whose tenure ran to 21 Nov 2000.
  **[A, es/en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia/Keiko_Fujimori]**
- **1994: the public condemnation.** English Wikipedia: she "publicly condemned her
  husband as a tyrant and his government as corrupt". *Flagged:* the circulating phrase
  **"corrupt tyrant"** compresses two predicates and is **not a verbatim quotation**; it
  is nowhere set as a quote in the journey file.
- What she did say verbatim, in an October 1994 interview: *"He visto pasar la
  corrupción por el Palacio Presidencial."* and *"Me teme, por eso me hace la vida
  imposible. Sigo estando casada, pero en una lucha por un camino diferente. Mi lucha es
  por principios, no por discrepancias conyugales."* and *"Ni siquiera le temo a la
  muerte."* **[A, El Comercio archive, Carlos Batalla profile, citing an interview to
  Spanish radio, dated 20 October 1994]**

## 5. Divorce and the blocked candidacies, 1994-1999

- **The divorce date is resolvable and has been resolved.** Spanish Wikipedia: she
  divorced **in 1994**, accusing him of *"secuestro y torturas en los sótanos del SIE"*,
  and **"En 1995, el Tribunal de Lima oficializó el divorcio."** English Wikipedia's
  "1995" is that formalization. **One sequence, not two competing dates.** **[A]** An
  earlier pass carried it as an unresolved contradiction; that reading is withdrawn.
- *Coordinate corrected:* the **Palacio de Justicia de Lima** is at **-12.0577,
  -77.0350**; the earlier -12.0505, -77.0347 was Plaza San Martín, ~800 m off.
- **1995: her own party blocked.** Spanish Wikipedia has her attempting a presidential
  run on the **Movimiento Armonía Siglo XXI-Frempol** ticket, with the electoral
  authorities invalidating both it and a later congressional candidacy. English Wikipedia
  instead says the "Harmony" party was ruled ineligible in **December 1994** for failing
  "to muster the required number of signatures".
  **CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED:** the versions disagree on the *grounds*, and an
  earlier rendering disagreed on the *election type* (municipal vs. the April 1995
  general). A third rendering, that she "ran for president in 2000", is simply wrong: her
  2000 candidacy was congressional, on another party's list.
- **A Lima mayoral bid was also rejected** later in 1995, the **ONPE** citing failure to
  meet filing requirements. **[A as to the source's wording; R as to plausibility]**
  *Caveat:* registration and *tachas* are ordinarily the **JNE's** function. The
  attribution follows the source, not the institutional norm.
- **6 October 1999: IACHR Report No. 119/99, Case 11.428, Susana Higuchi Miyagawa**,
  finding that Peru violated her political rights by barring her 1995 candidacies.
  **[R, via es.wikipedia; the Commission's own text was not retrieved]** **Gap:** her
  presence at the OAS in Washington is **not documented**, and the journey file says so
  in `date_confidence`.

## 6. Congress, the vladivideo, and the fall, 2000-2006

- **Elected to Congress in 2000** on the **Frente Independiente Moralizador** list at
  number three, with **126,451 votes**, the ninth-highest tally; sworn in **26 July
  2000**. **[A, es.wikipedia]** *Corrected:* the 2000 election used a **single national
  district**, so "elected representing Lima" is wrong; English Wikipedia gives *National*
  for 2000-2001 and *Lima* only for 2001-2006.
- *Coordinate corrected:* the **Congreso de la República** (Av. Abancay) is at
  **-12.0480, -77.0254**, not -12.0472, -77.0303 (~530 m west). All four Congress stops
  use the corrected pin.
- **26-29 July 2000: the Marcha de los Cuatro Suyos**, against Fujimori's third-term
  inauguration; she took part. **[A, es.wikipedia/Susana Higuchi]** *Flagged:* the
  article on the march does not mention her. *Corrected:* 28 July was its central date,
  the ~250,000 figure attaches principally to **27 July**, and the convergence point was
  a platform near the **Palacio de Justicia**.
- **14 September 2000: the first vladivideo**, showing Vladimiro Montesinos paying
  congressman Alberto Kouri to switch parties, released at the **Gran Hotel Bolívar**,
  Plaza San Martín. **[A, the release and the venue]**
  **CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED:** es.wikipedia's *Vladivideos* article credits the press
  conference to **Luis Iberico and Frank Cayo** and names neither her nor Olivera; her
  presence rests on her own article. *Coordinate corrected* to **-12.0510, -77.0355**
  (Gran Hotel Bolívar, northwest corner of Plaza San Martín); the earlier -12.0486,
  -77.0347 was ~340 m off.
- **November 2000:** she backed the censure of congress president Martha Hildebrandt and
  supported **Valentín Paniagua**, first to the presidency of Congress and days later to
  the presidency of the Republic. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- **Re-elected 2001** for the 2001-2006 term, this time for **Lima**. **[A]**
- **2001: the García allegation.** She stated that Fujimori and Montesinos had ordered
  the killing of former president Alan García on the day of the 1992 self-coup, and that
  she warned him: *"Yo no quise ser partícipe de un asesinato."* / *"Fujimori dio la orden
  de asesinar a García."* **[A as her statements, El Tiempo (Bogotá), 20 September 2001;
  R as to the fact, which she alleged and he denied]**
- **July 2001:** alleged her ex-husband misappropriated a **$12.5 million** donation
  sent from Japan. **[R, her allegation, en.wikipedia]**
- **2005:** resigned from the FIM over internal disagreements and joined the **Perú
  Ahora** bench led by **Luis Guerrero Figueroa**. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- **Term ended 26 July 2006.** **[A, en.wikipedia]** She did not hold office again.
- **The La Cantuta mass.** Spanish Wikipedia says only that she *"participó en una misa
  de los deudos de la masacre de la Cantuta"*. **It does not place that mass at the La
  Cantuta campus**, and an earlier pass that staged it there (7.7 km off the real campus
  at -11.9474, -76.7014) is withdrawn. The journey file keeps the act, drops the invented
  venue, and tags it **[R]** with the year placed by inference.

## 7. Reconciliation and last years, 2016-2021

- **2016: public reconciliation with Keiko Fujimori**, and public support for her
  presidential campaign. **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- **The Barranca swing.** Two verbatim quotes belong to a campaign trip through
  **Barranca, Pativilca and Paramonga**, *not* to Lima city as an earlier pass asserted:
  the *"Mi padre era millonario"* remark above,
  and *"Él ha engañado a todo el Perú con millones de por medio y tiene el descaro de
  presentarse para la presidencia."* **[A, RPP Noticias, verbatim]**
  *Two corrections:* (a) the circulated gloss "Él **(Pedro Pablo Kuczynski)** ha
  engañado..." is an **editorial insertion not in the source text**; the journey file
  quotes the source and identifies him in the prose. (b) The date **2016-05-05** is
  supported by nothing (the RPP page shows only "Actualizado el 10 de Ago, 2016");
  El Comercio supports **May 2016**, so the day is a flagged placeholder.
- **2018:** when Keiko was detained in the Odebrecht-linked investigation, Higuchi took
  on the care of her grandchildren. **[A, es.wikipedia]** Month not given; the household
  address is unpublished, so the San Borja district coordinate is used.
- **2021:** backed Keiko's second presidential run, her last public political act.
  **[A, es.wikipedia]**
- **Died 8 December 2021, of cancer, aged 71**, at the **Oncosalud** clinic in San Borja.
  **[A, es/en.wikipedia]** Keiko announced it the same day: *"Después de una dura lucha
  contra el cáncer, nuestra madre, Susana Higuchi acaba de partir al encuentro de Dios."*
  *Coordinate corrected* to **-12.0906, -77.0076** (Av. Guardia Civil 571); the earlier
  -12.087, -77.002 missed by ~660 m.
- **Wake at the Parroquia Santa María Madre de la Iglesia**, Av. Gregorio Escobedo,
  **Jesús María**. **[A]** *Coordinate corrected* to **-12.0884, -77.0560** (the earlier
  -12.077, -77.049 was a district centroid ~1.5 km away). Condolences came from sitting
  president **Pedro Castillo**, from congress members across parties, and from former
  presidents **Ollanta Humala** and **Pedro Pablo Kuczynski**.
- **Buried at the Cementerio Campo Fe de Huachipa**, Lurigancho, December 2021. **[A]**
  *Coordinate corrected* to **-12.0073, -76.9373** (earlier -11.9815, -76.8893, ~6.2 km
  off). Plot not published; not cremated. **The earlier "burial site unknown" gap is
  closed.**

## Interlocks with existing atlas files

- **The Palacio de Gobierno is a shared pin, not a shared story.** `pizarro.journey.json`
  pins the palace at **-12.0463, -77.0311**; `tupac_amaru_ii.journey.json` attests the
  same building's Salón Pizarro, renamed in 1972. Higuchi lived there 1990-1994, so **the
  coordinate is inherited byte-identical and neither man is named in her campas**: she has
  no relation to either beyond standing on the same ground centuries later, and the map
  already shows co-location.
- **Same ruling for the weak Lima echoes**: `che_guevara.journey.json` (1952),
  `manuel_noriega.journey.json` (Chorrillos, 1962), `isabel_peron.journey.json` (1955),
  `tupac_amaru_ii.journey.json` (the 1996-97 residence siege), and
  `paul_singer.journey.json` (Fujimori's flight to Japan, a stop that file itself flags
  as part legend). **None is a relation of Higuchi's.** The Isabel Perón parallel, First
  Lady later ruptured from a husband's regime, is thematic and not documentary, and is
  excluded rather than smuggled in as resonance.
- **The real family interlocks have no files yet**: no `alberto_fujimori.journey.json`,
  no `keiko_fujimori.journey.json`; both are QUEUE.md rows only, adjacent to hers. That
  interlock must be built when those subjects are researched, not retrofitted now.
- **No canonical hub pins are inherited.** Buenos Aires, Paris, the Kaaba and the Temple
  Mount were offered as placeholders; **no source links Higuchi to any of them**, so none
  appears. Inheriting a pin requires the traveler to have been there.
