# Paracelsus — report

**File:** `paracelsus.journey.json` · 7 segments, 32 stops, 11 quotes · span 1493–1541 · calendar `julian` (all within the Julian era; Gregorian reform is 1582).

## Sources
Core spine from the Zürich Paracelsus Project biography (`paracelsus.uzh.ch/paracelsus-life.html`), Wikipedia, Britannica, and Encyclopedia.com — these agree on the itinerary skeleton (Einsiedeln → Villach → Vienna/Ferrara → 1517–24 wandering → Salzburg 1524 → Strasbourg 1526 → Basel 1527 → Colmar/Nuremberg/Beratzhausen/St Gallen 1528–33 → Tyrol/Pfäfers/Augsburg 1534–36 → Eferding/Villach 1537–38 → Salzburg 1540, †1541). The **esoteric register** — the tradition the atlas honors — comes from A.E. Waite's and Franz Hartmann's Lives of Paracelsus, the Theosophy Wiki, and Philip Ball's *The Devil's Doctor*: the sword with **AZOTH** in the pommel (1567 Rosicrucian portrait), the childhood unmanning, the Trithemius tutelage, the murder-legend. Death details (White Horse Inn / *Zum Weissen Rössl* on the Kaigasse, will of 21 Sept, †24 Sept, St Sebastian's paupers' ground) from the Salzburg city history and Hektoen International. Quotes: the Erasmus letter (PMC), the four-pillars *Paragranum*, the "old wives and gypsies" line, the *Alterius non sit…* Hirschvogel motto, the microcosm from *Astronomia Magna*.

## Judgment calls
- **Register.** Per the brief, the esotericist's cosmology is narrated *descriptively as the mythos he authored* — the Azoth-sword, the elemental spirits of the mines, the signature-doctrine, the microcosm are placed as **events of his life and thought**, not endorsed and not debunked. Legendary matter (the boar/soldier wound, the Eastern tincture, the murder) is carried in the tradition's voice and tagged `traditional`.
- **Confidence tags.** `attested` for documented anchors (Strasbourg citizenship 1526; Froben cure; the 24 June 1527 Avicenna burning; St Gallen/Studer; Pfäfers; Augsburg 1536; the 1541 will and death). `traditional` for the received-but-shaky (Trithemius, the great wandering's Eastern reach, the peasants'-war flight, Meran Anabaptists). `inferred` for undated but necessary way-stations (the mines c.1507, Einsiedeln childhood).
- **Quotes.** Several favorite Paracelsus lines survive only in paraphrase across the biographical tradition rather than in a pinned edition; these are attributed to "the biographical tradition" / named *Defensiones* rather than to a false chapter-and-verse. Where a quote's provenance is a portrait inscription or a correspondent (Erasmus), that is named exactly. Nulls kept where the canon gives no words.

## Gaps / time-folds
- The **1517–1524 wandering** is compressed into three emblematic stops (Venice army-surgery, the European roads, Constantinople/East). The tradition's full list — Spain, Portugal, England, Scandinavia, Poland, Russia, Rhodes, Egypt, Tartary — is impossible to date or route; I fold it into the "roads of Europe" stop and let the East stand as the legendary far edge. Coordinates there are emblematic (Paris, Istanbul), not a claim of a fixed residence.
- **Trithemius/Würzburg** is dated 1508 (before Vienna 1507→ adjusted so the segment reads chronologically); the real tutelage, if any, was by his writings, and the meeting is itself contested — hence `traditional`.
- **Two Salzburgs.** The 1524–25 sojourn (peasants' war) and the 1540–41 return (under Archbishop Ernst) bracket the arc; kept as distinct stops so the wheel visibly closes where the exile began.

## The five richest episodes
1. **The Devil's Bridge birth + the wound at the root** — born by the Teufelsbrücke under the Black Madonna; the childhood unmanning that makes the beardless, chaste wanderer.
2. **The saving of Froben's leg at Basel (1527)** — the one triumph; Erasmus's letter of thanks; the ascent to the chair.
3. **The burning of Avicenna on St John's Day (24 June 1527)** — the millennium of authority cast on the midsummer bonfire; the boldest deed and the seed of his ruin.
4. **The AZOTH sword and the Eastern tincture** — the philosopher's stone in the hollow pommel, the elemental spirits of the mines, the signature-doctrine: the mythos rendered as biography.
5. **The White Horse Inn (1541)** — the will leaving all to the poor, the paupers' grave at St Sebastian, the whispered murder by the physicians' hirelings; the wound healed into legend.

## Connection to the atlas
Paracelsus is a **hinge** among the esoteric siblings. He stands upstream of **Kircher** ("the last man who knew everything," the same one-book-of-nature ambition a century on), **Gurdjieff** and **Blavatsky** (the sacred-wanderer who gathers the world's hidden lore on foot), and **Jung**, who literally read Paracelsus and built alchemical psychology on him — Jung's *Küsnacht* stone answers Paracelsus's Salt-Sulphur-Mercury. He rhymes with **Molay** and **Joan** as another figure the "powers of this world" destroy for loving the poor and speaking against authority (his peasants'-war flight, his book-burning). His medicine-as-charity and reforming theology tie him to the pedagogue-reformers of the atlas (**Froebel**, **Comenius-adjacent Johann Jahn**), and his microcosm/macrocosm cosmology is the direct ancestor of the Goethean root the whole collection grows from — Goethe himself read the Paracelsians. He is the atlas's archetype of the **itinerant adept**: no city his own, every land his home, the university replaced by the road.
