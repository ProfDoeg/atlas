# Joaquín Trincado Mateo (1866-1935): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-21 for `joaquin_trincado_mateo.journey.json`. Electrician of Bilbao, emigrant of 1903, founder in Buenos Aires of the Escuela Magnético-Espiritual de la Comuna Universal (EMECU), author of some forty-six books, founder of two communal colonies in the Argentine interior, correspondent and doctrinal father of Augusto César Sandino.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, movement tradition, or religious claim.

**The standing hazard.** Almost the entire biographical record is written by his own school. `coloniajaime.org`, `emedelacu.org`, `emecu.org.gt`, `cimamovimientoespirita.org`, `grupoatril.com` and `nuevo-renacer.com` are all EMECU-descended; Mendoza Vallejo (2001), the fullest biography and the largest single source of [R] material here, is by an adherent. There is one peer-reviewed study, Galicia Martínez (2024), and it states that the prior literature is apologetic. Several of these sites contradict each other and themselves. Corrections made to the incoming research pool are gathered in §12; unresolved contradictions in §13.

---

## 1. Navarra: birth and the field (1866-1888)

- **Born 19 August 1866 at Cintruénigo, Navarra**, son of Ignacio Trincado Alfaro and Romualda Mateo de Ayala. **[A: UNAM iifl.unam.mx; emedelacu.org, "nació en Navarra de Cintruénigo España, el 19 de agosto de 1866"; cimamovimientoespirita.org; grupoatril.com; en.wikipedia]**
- Born "within the first half hour after one in the morning," and the **fourth child**. **[R: Mendoza Vallejo alone]**
- Parents married 13 April 1856 at Cintruénigo. **[R: emedelacu.org; no civil register located]**
- Field labour from about **age seven**, alongside irregular schooling. **[R]** Traced through Mendoza Vallejo to Trincado's *Profilaxis de la vida* (1931), which could not be opened in any pass.
- Marries **María Dolores Agramonte y Navascués, 24 April 1888**, at the templo de San Juan Bautista, Cintruénigo. **[R: Mendoza Vallejo; grupoatril confirms the marriage without a church or a street; no civil register]**
- Sons **Demetrio Jesús (1890)** and **Máximo (1892)** born at Cintruénigo, Cantón de la Villa. **[R]** Their ages in February 1909 (18 and 16) corroborate the years internally.

## 2. Veruela: the novice (1883-1885)

- Enters religious formation at about seventeen and leaves without vows after **eighteen months or two years**. **[R]** grupoatril says "two years as a novice."
- **Zaragoza, 1883, with the Jesuits**, where he is said to develop his interest in electricity and to join an anticlerical group called the "Cuadrilla del Culebrón." **[A: Galicia Martínez, REHMLAC+ 16.1 (2024)]**
- The house itself, **Santa María de Veruela** at Vera de Moncayo, comes only from Mendoza Vallejo. **[R]** Material support, newly found: es.wikipedia records the **Jesuits held Veruela 1877-1975**, so a Jesuit formation there in 1883 is possible. Possible is not attested.

## 3. Bilbao: the trade of light (1892-1903)

- **Bilbao from about 1892**: mason on buildings tied to an electric company, then his own electrical workshop and shop near the **Plaza de Santiago**. **[A for the Bilbao career: Galicia Martínez 2024. R for the Plaza de Santiago address: Mendoza Vallejo only]**
- **Ramón 1894 and Ángel 1896**, fourth floor, **Calle Dos de Mayo** (48003, Bilbao La Vieja, Ibaiondo, south bank); Ángel dies at about one. **[R]**
- **María 1898**, fourth floor, **Calle Arechaga** (Basque *Aretxaga*, barrio de San Francisco, Ibaiondo, running Martzana to Cortes, named for Cándido de Arechaga, killed by Carlist rioters in 1833); she dies at about two. **[R]**
- **Madrid, May 1902**: credited with the electrical illumination for what movement biography calls the *coronation* of Alfonso XIII. **[R, and misnamed.]** Spanish monarchs are not crowned: the event was the king's coming of age and **jura de la Constitución, 17 May 1902, in the salón de sesiones of the Palacio del Congreso**, with Madrid decorated and over 100,000 visitors arriving on discounted rail fares. No payroll, engineering, or household record naming Trincado.
- **Valencia, 1902**: a hydroelectric installation for a firm named as Saltos y Villora. **[R]** No company record; no site within Valencia is named anywhere, so the pin marks the city centre only and says so.

## 4. The crossing (1903)

- **Sails for Argentina in 1903, arrives at Buenos Aires** under the presidency of Julio Argentino Roca. **[A: Galicia Martínez 2024; grupoatril.com]**
- The ship, the **Berenguer / "Berenguer el grande,"** comes from **emedelacu.org alone**; grupoatril names no vessel. **[R]** A real Spanish steamer *Berenguer el Grande* (3,405 grt) existed, owner Sociedad Anónima de Navegación Trasatlántica, bankrupt 1910. No manifest places Trincado aboard.

## 5. The Orient of Jerusalem (1903 or 1912)

The founding legend, told by Trincado in his own *Buscando a Dios*: he travels to the Near East, learns of the *Cábala Mayor* at Bethlehem, is received in Jerusalem by Kabbalistic elders of a surviving Essene school, is shown archives and mummies, and receives authority to publish a doctrine kept since Moses. **[R throughout.]**

- Sources: **grupoatril.com** ("En Belén tuvo conocimiento de la Cábala Mayor... fue recibido por los Ancianos Cabalísticos") and the first-person narrative in *Buscando a Dios*, roughly pp. 183-211 of the coloniajaime PDF, read directly.
- **grupoatril gives the episode no date.** The "1903 or 1912" conflict comes from elsewhere in the movement literature; both readings are printed in the file's confidence fields and neither is chosen.
- **Gap, stated as a gap:** no passport, itinerary, ship record, named interlocutor, or contemporary report of any kind. A Bilbao-to-Buenos Aires cargo passage does not touch Palestine.

## 6. Buenos Aires: ruin, Constancia, the oratory (1903-1911)

- Business and later residence at **Bartolomé Mitre 3458**. **[R]** No directory or property record.
- **Joins the Sociedad (Asociación) Espiritista Constancia in 1909; breaks with it in 1910** over its mixing of religion and science and its "phenomenism." **[A: Galicia Martínez 2024]** grupoatril gives 1908-1909 for the association.
- Constancia was founded **9 February 1877** by Rafael Hernández (brother of the author of *Martín Fierro*), Ángel Scharnichia and Felipe Senillosa, and is the oldest surviving spiritist society in the city. **[A]**
- The parting line about founding a school "where Jesus stopped" is **[R]**, unsourced beyond tradition.
- **February 1909:** María Dolores and the three sons sail from Santurce on the *Paraná*; the family lives on **Calle Cangallo**; wife and sons return to Spain for good **19 December 1909**. **[R: Mendoza Vallejo]** Bolaños Geyer, hostile, calls the 1903 departure abandonment; this reunion complicates that without settling it.
- Writes ***"Buscando a Dios" y asiento del Dios Amor***, 1909-1911. **[A: primary PDF read]** Chapter VI closes with the dates 24 June 1909 and 8 September 1909.
- **Home oratory, 1910**, with mediums **Mariana (Marciana) Palacios, Pedro Portillo** and the clairvoyant **José González**. **[A: emedelacu.org]**
- A six-month illness called gangrenous, cresting **19 September 1911**, the eve of the founding. **[R]** No medical record of any kind.

## 7. The school (1911-1921)

- **EMECU founded at Buenos Aires, 20 September 1911.** **[A: coloniajaime.org, emedelacu.org, cimamovimientoespirita.org, UNAM (September 1911), Galicia Martínez]** The strongest date-place pair in the dossier.
- Reading that date as an echo of **20 September 1870**, the capture of Rome, is movement interpretation; no statement of intent located. **[R]**
- **Juicio de Mayorías, 5 April 1912**, tied to the signing of the Código de Amor Universal. **[R]** An internal revelatory claim, not an observable event.
- **1914, *La Revolución de México y el crimen de Norteamérica*,** written April 1914. **[A: Galicia Martínez, "escrito en abril de 1914"]** The crime of the title is the **US occupation of Veracruz, April 1914**. Quotable from the same article: "Trincado recupera nuevamente a Juan de Padilla y, bajo el principio de reencarnación, va a declarar que el alma de Padilla reencarnó en Pancho Villa." **[A]**
- **1916, first edition of *El magnetismo en su origen: Método Supremo*.** **[A: UNAM, 1ª ed. 1916, 2ª 1919, 3ª 1928]** The August month and the 6,000-copy print run are in no reachable source. **[R]**
- **January 1919: statutes and regulations approved by Argentine authorities.** **[A: coloniajaime.org; Galicia Martínez]**
- **1920, *Filosofía Austera Racional*,** which named the teaching. **[A: coloniajaime.org; UNAM]**
- **12 October 1921: Unión Hispano-Américo-Oceánica (UHAO)**, with a hymn and a **seven-colour flag**. **[A: coloniajaime.org]** The oak of Guernica appears nowhere on that page **[R]**. coloniajaime itself confirms **no government ever ratified the UHAO**.

## 8. The books and the orders (1922-1931)

- **1922: *El espiritismo estudiado o Política del Creador y gobierno del Espiritismo* and *Los cinco amores. Ética y Sociología*.** **[A: UNAM]**
- **1924, *Conócete a ti mismo: Fisiología, Fisiognosía y Etnología del Universo*** (2nd corrected ed. 1931). **[A: UNAM]** The book contains an explicit **eugenic breeding programme**, pp. 199-201 of the emedelacu PDF, directing the union of white men of the torrid zone with black women and promising "en dos generaciones tendréis el color, la finura de las formas y la raza completamente blanca o adámica." A documented racist and eugenicist passage, not an isolated aside; stated flat in the journey file. Galicia Martínez independently calls the corpus's racial framework Hispanophile, Eurocentric and colonialist.
- **c. 1925, Orden del Templo Azul Racionalista (OTAR)**, for scientists and intellectuals. **[R: Mendoza Vallejo only; coloniajaime does not mention it]** Distinct from the **Círculo Defensor del Maestro**, dated **1930** by coloniajaime **[A]**. Two bodies, not one date-conflict.
- **1929, *Los extremos se tocan: Epílogo de la guerra y prólogo de la paz*.** **[A for 1929: Google Books; a later Venezuelan edition carries ISBN 978-980-07-2086-8]** The 1914-1929 composition span is tradition **[R]**. The book carries his prophecy of a planetary cataclysm: sinking continents, plagues, and part of the Earth breaking away to form a second moon, with no date given **[R]**.
- **1931, *Profilaxis de la vida*.** **[A: UNAM]**
- **1929, Sandino.** Sandino reaches the EMECU through **Francisco Fuentes, who gave him volumes of the school as a memento of his visit to Yucatán on 14 December 1929**; Trincado recognises him as **Celador General** for Nicaragua and advocates his cause in *La Balanza*. **[A for Trincado's advocacy: cimamovimientoespirita.org, "la gesta patriótica de Augusto César Sandino en Nicaragua". R for the mechanism and the title: Amezcua and the Sandino literature, not re-fetched]**
  - **Whose stop:** Sandino's Yucatán encounter and his Río Coco cooperative are **Sandino's stops, not Trincado's**, and are not staged here; they appear only as sources on Trincado's own Buenos Aires stop. The Cooperativa Río Coco, Sección Central Güigüilí, ran **January 1933 to February 1934**, after the February 1933 peace accord. Sandino's documented banner is the **red-and-black bandera rojinegra** taken from Tampico anarcho-syndicalists; a seven-colour EMECU flag at Río Coco is corroborated by no non-EMECU source and is **contested**, flagged not resolved.

## 9. The colonies (1930-1934)

- **First visit to Santiago del Estero, c. 1930.** **[R for the date. A for the search: coloniajaime.org/historia/ records surveying sites "from Jujuy to Buenos Aires" and settling on Santiago del Estero for its irrigation]**
- **Colonia Jaime founded 25 July 1932**, Robles department, **549 hectares** per the colony's own history page (Wikimapia says 607). **[A, twice over: coloniajaime.org/historia/ verbatim, "El 25 de julio de 1932, ante numeroso público, Joaquín Trincado coloca la Piedra Fundamental dando nacimiento a Colonia Jaime"; and Suárez and Infante, *Estudios Rurales* 12:25 (2022), recording the 88th anniversary on 25 July during the pandemic, which also refutes Wikimapia's 1931]**
- **September 1932:** first settlers from La Pampa, twenty-one young people led by **Nazareno Zallocco and Laura Donatti**, joined by local families Paz, Gutiérrez, Gallardo and Manso. **[A: coloniajaime.org/historia/]**
- **31 December 1934: Colonia Comunal Los Franciscos**, near **El Quebrachal, Anta, Salta**, registered as **EMECU Cátedra no. 175**, created "in favor of the School's Founder Don Joaquín Trincado," with the previous owner **Eulalia Gimeno de Álvarez** as internal administrator. **[A: mhistorico.unsa.edu.ar]**

## 10. La Balanza and the last year (1933-1935)

- ***La Balanza*** begins **1 January 1933**, fortnightly, carrying the school's circulars, writings and provisions; **272 issues preserved**, digitised at IAPSOP. **[A: cimamovimientoespirita.org; IAPSOP]**
- **21 February 1934: Sandino assassinated** at Larreynaga, Managua, about 11 p.m., with generals Francisco Estrada and Juan Pablo Umanzor. **[A, independent of all Trincado sources]** The news **reaching Trincado's headquarters**, and his mourning Sandino as disciple and martyr, is **[R]**, Amezcua's and EMECU's telling. Kept in Buenos Aires as Trincado's reception of the news, not allowed to migrate to Nicaragua.
- **April 1935: fourth, photo-engraved edition of *El magnetismo en su origen*.** **[R: bibliographical tradition, not verified against a copy or catalogue]**
- **24 June 1935: Gardel dies at Medellín.** Trincado is reported to say "Ese hombre pone el alma en lo que canta." **[R: family recollection; no contemporary written record]**
- **November-December 1935:** a last rail circuit of thousands of kilometres to advise the colonies, his children recalling him at a desk with his legs wrapped in blankets while handling some 180 cátedras. **[R]** No ticket or itinerary. **Retiro is named only as the principal long-distance terminus of the period**; no source places him there, and the confidence field says so.
- **Died 6 December 1935, 10:30 p.m., calle Sarmiento no. 3137**, of "una vieja afección cardio-pulmonar." **[A for the date: UNAM, cimamovimientoespirita.org, grupoatril, "desencarnó... el 6 de diciembre de 1935". R for address and hour: emecu.org.gt alone, citing *La Balanza* of 15 December 1935 through Mendoza Vallejo]** Last words "En vosotros descanso" **[A: coloniajaime.org and emecu.org.gt]**.
- **Funeral, reported 15 December 1935:** senior officers of Argentina's Grand Masonic Lodge ask leave to render honours; the coffin is draped in the seven-colour flag under an inscription reading "SUMMUM SAPIENTAS DOLORIS SUMSUM." **[R]** Sourced only to the movement's retelling of its own periodical. No lodge register, no non-EMECU obituary, no record of any Masonic degree.
- **Gap, stated as a gap:** **no cemetery, tomb, marker or burial record has surfaced in any source, movement or independent.** Biographies that record his street number, his hour of death and his last sentence are silent on where the body went.

## 11. After (1935-2023)

- **194 cátedras across the Americas at his death, 94 in Mexico. [A: UNAM]** Movement counts elsewhere give 172, 180 and 185.
- **Succession.** He **left the directorship to María Mercedes Riglos Cosis, who delegated it to their eldest son Juan Trincado Donato** (Juan Donato Trincado Riglos, d. 1992). **[A: en.wikipedia]** The Spanish marriage to María Dolores Agramonte is nowhere recorded as dissolved, so "his widow" for Riglos is the movement's usage and not a civil status; the journey file writes "companion" and says why. The **1941 *Boletín Oficial*** listing Riglos as proprietor of *La Balanza* nos. 200-201 could not be reopened **[R]**; her death year is given as 1965 or 1967.
- The third link, **Víctor Rolando Trincado** (Juan Donato's son), is **contested, not settled**: a 1990 document said to grant him the direction has had its authenticity questioned, and EMECU-Puerto Rico's history says a successor Director General was requested of Juan Donato but never appointed. **[R]** Venezuelan court filings recite the chain while litigating institutional identity, which is itself evidence it was disputed; `historico.tsj.gob.ve` could not be opened (TLS certificate verification failure).
- **1938, Mexico:** a regional cátedra breaks from Buenos Aires, announcing that Trincado's spirit had appeared to them, called the Buenos Aires leadership "ofuscada," and dictated corrections to the Código de Amor Universal between 1939 and 1940. **[R: Rojas González, *El movimiento espiritista en México* (UNAM, 2000), through secondary summary; spirit dictation is a religious claim]**
- **1975:** Juan Donato Trincado Riglos, titled "Maestro Nato," publishes **Código de Amor Universal, tomo II**, named in it as Director General. **[A]**
- **2001:** Mendoza Vallejo, *Joaquín Trincado: Algo de su vida y de su obra* (Caracas: Cromotip). **[R as a text: not held; existence and imprint corroborated by citation in Galicia Martínez 2024]**
- **2022:** Suárez and Infante field study of Colonia Jaime, still inhabited and working. **[A as an article]** Its resident count could not be extracted (see §12); a competing count exists (Wikimapia: 25 families, 79 people).
- **2023:** authorised English translation, *Know Thyself*, trans. Wilson Rogelio Kickbush, ISBN 9789878879581, published 15 February 2023. **[A]**
- The **Museo Histórico "Prof. Eduardo Ashur," Universidad Nacional de Salta**, holds the Los Franciscos archive, accessioned in stages (photographs mid-2015, books July 2016, further documents August 2016, two more boxes April 2017): an album of 118 photographs, 58 loose prints, 27 negatives, 4 printed images, and a first batch of **529 documents comprising 427 letters, 47 invitations and 54 telegrams**, plus a second batch of 83 letters, 3 invitations, 6 telegrams and 4 other documents. **[A: mhistorico.unsa.edu.ar]** **Whose stop:** the archive sits at a Salta campus Trincado never saw. It is cited as a source on his own Los Franciscos stop and is not staged as a scene.

## 12. Corrections applied to the incoming research pool

1. **Columbus raid struck** from *La Revolución de México* (April 1914): the raid was 9 March 1916, twenty-three months later. Veracruz substituted.
2. **Coordinates.** Dársena Norte was **+34.5975**, a North Atlantic latitude. Cintruénigo was 4.5 km off on three stops; Veruela 10 km; Colonia Jaime carried three inconsistent values, 14 to 23 km out; El Quebrachal 13 km; Sarmiento 3137 600 m; the Nativity 370 m.
3. **Bilbao districts.** Dos de Mayo and Arechaga were labelled Casco Viejo; both are south-bank Ibaiondo streets. "Arréchega" is Arechaga.
4. ***Profilaxis de la vida*** moved from 1922 to **1931**, per the same UNAM bibliography the pool cited against itself.
5. **Source reattributions.** Zaragoza-1883 to Galicia Martínez, not coloniajaime; the Jerusalem legend to grupoatril, not coloniajaime; the *Berenguer* to emedelacu, not grupoatril; *La Balanza*'s founding to cimamovimientoespirita, not grupoatril; "En vosotros descanso" to coloniajaime and emecu.org.gt, not grupoatril. *Los extremos se tocan* is not in UNAM's bibliography at all.
6. **Retagged.** Hour of birth and birth order, the 1916 print run, the oak of Guernica, Sandino's death-news reaching Buenos Aires, and the third link of the succession, all [A] to **[R]**. The 1910 Constancia break, [R] to **[A]**.
7. **Sandino mechanism.** Seances at Mérida/Progreso replaced by the Fuentes gift of 14 December 1929; Río Coco moved from 1932 to 1933-1934.
8. **Colonia Jaime hectarage** from 600-607 to **549**, which is what the cited page says.
9. **Salta archive arithmetic.** "625 pieces of correspondence" is wrong; the source gives 529 documents comprising 427 letters, 47 invitations and 54 telegrams, which sum to 528. Printed as the source has it.
10. **Alfonso XIII** was never crowned; May 1902 was the jura de la Constitución.
11. **Quote struck**: "la seguridad del riego y las acequias que representan el trabajo del hombre" is not in the fetched text and appears nowhere in the journey file. So is the pool's "rubricated 1911, signed 1912," which matches neither tome of the Código.
12. **Two bodies, not a conflict.** OTAR (undated) and the Círculo Defensor del Maestro (1930) were read as one organisation with two dates.

4. Novitiate **eighteen months** vs **two years**.

7. Cátedras at his death: **194** (UNAM) vs **172 / 180 / 185** (movement counts).

11. The founding date read as deliberately echoing **20 September 1870**: movement interpretation, no statement of intent located.

## 14. Apparatus relations for the operator

Trincado is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing has been silently deleted. These relations were found in the pool and are reported rather than used:

1. **José López Rega** (`jose_lopez_rega.journey.json`). **Direction caught: forward.** Buenos Aires esoteric-political organising decades after Trincado's 1935 death, no documented link. Not used: a 1935 file cannot embed a 1970s hash.
2. **Victor Hugo** (`victor_hugo.journey.json`), Jersey table-turning 1853-1855. **Direction: backward,** so legal, but the relation is lineage resonance only; Trincado is nowhere on record citing Hugo. Dropped as thematic.
3. **Ettore Ferrari** (`ettore_ferrari.journey.json`), Rome and the 1870 anticlerical watershed. **Direction: backward.** A shared calendar date, no relation between the men. Not used.
4. **Benjamin of Tudela** (`benjamin_de_tudela.journey.json`), Tudela 30 km from Cintruénigo across seven centuries. Shared region only. Not used.
5. **Agustí Querol i Subirats** (`agusti_querol_subirats.journey.json`), Bilbao and Buenos Aires in overlapping decades. Shared cities only. Not used.
6. **Carlos Gardel.** A **real one-directional relation**, Trincado speaking about Gardel after his death, though the report of it is **[R]** family recollection, and Gardel is a QUEUE.md subject with **no journey file yet**. Named in the campa because the utterance is Trincado's own; **not** listed as an interlock slug, because no file exists to interlock with.
7. **Moses** (`moses.journey.json`). **Used.** Trincado writes substantively about Moses in *Buscando a Dios*, claiming Moses founded the Kabbalah as the Essene school on withdrawing from the people. A commentator writing on a biblical figure is a real one-directional link, backward, which the block rule permits.
