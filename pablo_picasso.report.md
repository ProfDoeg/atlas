# Pablo Ruiz Picasso (1881-1973): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-24 from the verified research pool for slug `pablo_picasso`. Companion: `pablo_picasso.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops, 12 quoted stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named on the line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim whose wording or citation could not be traced to a reachable source. Contradictions are printed on both sides and not adjudicated. Titles in quotation marks are English Wikipedia articles unless another wiki is named. Governing rule: **a stop belongs to whoever was actually there.**

## 1. Málaga and A Coruña, 1881-1895

Born about 23:15, 25 October 1881, Plaza de la Merced, to José Ruiz Blasco and María Picasso López **[A: "Pablo Picasso," citing the civil birth record]**, at numbers 15-20, the Casas de Campos **[A: es.wikipedia "Plaza de la Merced (Málaga)"]**. A Coruña from 1891 on the father's appointment, Pablo enrolled 1892 **[A: "Pablo Picasso"]**. Conchita dead of diphtheria, January 1895 **[A as to death and month: museum biographical material]**.

- **Correction.** The dossier put the father's post at the **Instituto da Guarda**, a separate secondary institution. The article says the **School of Fine Arts**. The campa states the conflation rather than quietly picking a side.
- **[R]**, tagged, no independent stop: the baptism of 10 November 1881 at Santiago Apóstol (parish register not reached); the vow to give up painting if Conchita were spared; the causal line biographers draw from her death to his preoccupation with mortality.
- **Gap.** No day for Conchita's death; the stop defaults to 1895-01-01.

## 2. Barcelona and Madrid, 1895-1900

Carrer de la Mercè 3 from 1895; the La Llotja entrance examination passed in about a week against a month allowed; *Science and Charity* (1897), the father posed as the doctor, honorable mention at the National Exhibition; San Fernando entered at sixteen and attended badly, the Prado copied instead **[all A: "Pablo Picasso"]**. Horta de Ebro with Manuel Pallarès, 1898-1899, with a return in 1909 **[A: "Horta de Sant Joan"]**. Els Quatre Gats: menu design, first one-man show February 1900 **[A: "Els Quatre Gats"; Museu Picasso Barcelona chronology]**.

- The famous line about having learned everything he knows in Pallarès's village was **not** in the verified quote pool. The campa paraphrases and does not quote.
- **Gaps.** No day for the Quatre Gats opening; no month for the Horta departure.

## 3. Paris and the Blue years, 1900-1906

First Paris trip autumn 1900 with Carles Casagemas, *Last Moments* in the Spanish section of the fair, Max Jacob met **[A: "Pablo Picasso"]**. **17 February 1901:** Casagemas, refused by Germaine Pichot, fired at her and shot himself at the Café de l'Hippodrome, boulevard de Clichy; **Picasso was in Spain** **[A: Musée national Picasso-Paris and standard scholarship synthesized across encyclopedic sources]**. Signature shortened to "Picasso," Blue Period to about 1904. Bateau-Lavoir permanently from 1904, with Fernande Olivier, Max Jacob, Apollinaire **[A: "Bateau-Lavoir"]**. Gósol, the inn Cal Tampanada, spring to mid-August 1906 **[A: "Gósol"]**.

- *La Vie* (1903) is painted over *Last Moments*, the male figure reworked to carry Casagemas's face **[A: technical study, Cleveland Museum of Art]**. What the picture argues is **[R]** and disputed; the campa leaves it open.
- **Correction.** *Portrait of Gertrude Stein* is **1905-1906**, not the dossier's bare 1905: the head painted out after eighty or ninety sittings and repainted from memory in autumn 1906, bequeathed to the Metropolitan at Stein's death, 27 July 1946 **[A: "Portrait of Gertrude Stein"]**.
- Picasso acquired **Alfred Jarry's revolver**, later several manuscripts, and drew his portrait **[A: `alfred_jarry.journey.json`]**. Undated; Jarry died November 1907.

## 4. Cubism, 1907-1915

Trocadéro visited 1907 **[A as to the visit: museum chronologies; the museum's 1882-1928 existence per "Musée de l'Homme"]**; *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon* painted, Braque and Matisse hostile, on record from both **[A: "Les Demoiselles d'Avignon"]**; the Braque exchange 1907-1912, Céret in summer 1911, works at times indistinguishable **[A: "Georges Braque," "Céret"]**; *Still Life with Chair Caning*, 1912 **[A: "Pablo Picasso"]**; mobilization August 1914, Braque and Derain enlisting, Picasso a neutral Spaniard **[A: "Georges Braque"; "Pablo Picasso," "Living there at outbreak of World War I," of Avignon]**; Eva Gouel dead 14 December 1915, cause usually given as tuberculosis complicated by other disease **[A as to date: corroborated across standard chronologies]**.

- **Two corrections.** The *Demoiselles* first public showing sharpened to the **Salon d'Antin, 16-31 July 1916**; MoMA acquisition corrected from 1939 to **November 1937, for $24,000**.
- **[R]** and left open: the Trocadéro account as exorcism, given decades later and dramatized in the telling; priority between his pasted oilcloth and Braque's papier collé; the narrative around the 1911 move from Fernande Olivier to Eva Gouel, whose canvas inscriptions are themselves **[A]**; **Sorgues, summer 1912**, which gets no stop and is folded into the Avignon campa.
- The Avignon stop is dated 1914-08-02, the mobilization date, which is **not** documented for the parting itself.
- **Gap.** The clinic where Eva Gouel died is outside Paris and unnamed in anything reachable; the stop uses a bare city pin and admits it.

## 5. The Ballets Russes and the Olga years, 1917-1935

Rome, February 1917, with Diaghilev, Cocteau, Satie and Massine, Stravinsky met and drawn; *Parade* premiered at the Châtelet 18 May 1917, near-riot before applause, Apollinaire's note coining "sur-réalisme" **[A: "Parade (ballet)"; `sergei_diaghilev`, `erik_satie`, `igor_stravinsky` journey files]**. Olga Khokhlova married 12 July 1918 at rue Daru, postponed from May over her foot surgery; Paulo born 4 February 1921 **[A: "Olga Khokhlova"]**. *El sombrero de tres picos*, Alhambra, 22 July 1919 **[A: "El sombrero de tres picos"]**, pool [R] upgraded; then *Pulcinella* (1920), *Cuadro flamenco* (1921), *Mercure* (1924) and *Le Train Bleu* (1924, curtain from a Picasso gouache, Chanel costumes and partial secret funding) **[A: the same journey files plus `coco_chanel`; dossier synthesis for *Cuadro flamenco*]**. Fontainebleau summer 1921, the two *Three Musicians* **[A: "Pablo Picasso"]**; Boisgeloup bought June 1930 **[A: "Château de Boisgeloup"]**, pool [R] upgraded.

- **Contradiction, flagged not resolved.** Two of the pool's three entries name Cocteau, **Apollinaire** and Max Jacob as witnesses at rue Daru; "Olga Khokhlova" names Cocteau and Max Jacob only. The campa prints both: "the literature adds Apollinaire, the record does not."
- **Removed.** The pool credited the **Julio González** welded-iron collaboration to Boisgeloup. The article does not mention González; that work belongs to his Paris workshop.
- **[R]:** Naples and Pompeii, 1917, confirmed by no fetched source, given no stop and named inside the Rome campa; the Stravinsky customs anecdote; the January 1927 approach to Marie-Thérèse Walter, resting solely on her own recollection given long afterward.
- **1935, split and corrected.** The pool's entry was internally contradictory, dated 1935-09-05 while saying Maya was born "later that year." Sequence: mid-1935 Olga learns of the affair and pregnancy from a friend and files for divorce; **5 September 1935** Maya born at **Boulogne-Billancourt**, not Paris proper, registered "father unknown" because French law barred a married man from acknowledging a child born outside marriage **[A: "Maya Widmaier-Picasso"]**; **no divorce was granted**, Olga staying his wife until her death, 11 February 1955, Cannes **[A: "Olga Khokhlova"]**.

## 6. Guernica, the Occupation, the Party, 1936-1946

Honorary in-absentia directorship of the Prado from September 1936 **[A: "Museo del Prado"]**. Gernika bombed 26 April 1937 on market day; *Guernica* begun at 7 rue des Grands-Augustins after the newspaper reports, George Steer's among them, and Juan Larrea's visit; finished **4 June 1937 after 35 days**, Dora Maar's dated photographs documenting the revisions and credited with pushing the palette to monochrome; Spanish Pavilion July 1937 beside a cast of his own *Woman with a Vase*; then Scandinavia, London on 30 September 1938, MoMA on loan from 1939 under his stipulation about Spanish democracy, Madrid September 1981, Reina Sofía from 1992 **[A: "Guernica (painting)"; the sculpture's pavilion showing per "Château de Vauvenargues"]**. Klee visited at Kistlerweg 6, Bern, late 1937 **[A: `paul_klee.journey.json`]**. Kahlo's seventeen paintings seen at the *Mexique* exhibition, March 1939, the tortoiseshell hand earrings given, a Spanish song taught **[A: `diego_rivera`, `frida_kahlo`, `wassily_kandinsky`]**. Artaud's release funded by the Loeb auction, 13 June 1946 **[A: `artaud.journey.json`]**. Communist Party joined October 1944 **[A: dossier synthesis of party archives and his contemporaneous statement]**.

- **Royan, 1939-1940. [R]**: the article lists Picasso among historical residents and gives no dates. No stop; folded into the Occupation campa.
- The German officer anecdote, "Did you do that?" and "No, you did," is **[R]**: no document, no named witness, postwar circulation, and Wikipedia itself calls it likely apocryphal. The campa carries it as a legend and names it one.
- *Guernica*'s afterlife is carried inside the pavilion campa rather than staged as separate scenes.

## 7. The Midi, 1946-1961

Six months at the Château Grimaldi, Antibes, 1946, 23 paintings and 44 drawings left behind, the building renamed Musée Picasso in December 1966 **[A: "Musée Picasso (Antibes)"]**, pool [R] upgraded. Madoura from 1947, resident in Vallauris 1948-1955, Jacqueline Roque met there in 1952; *La Guerre et la Paix* in the deconsecrated chapel, 1952; married at the town hall 2 March 1961 in great secrecy **[A: "Vallauris"; the marriage day per dossier synthesis of the municipal record]**. Wrocław, 25-28 August 1948, the peace congress at the technical university, some 600 delegates from 46 countries, Picasso in the delegate list and in a photograph **[A: "World Congress of Intellectuals in Defense of Peace"]**. Villa La Californie, Cannes, 1955-1961 **[A: "Villa La Californie"]**. Lump arrives 19 April 1957 with David Douglas Duncan, six years in the house with the boxer Yan and the goat Esmeralda; 44 *Las Meninas* studies between 17 August and 30 December 1957, Lump in fifteen **[A: "Lump (dog)"]**. Vauvenargues bought September 1958 **[A: "Château de Vauvenargues"]**.

- **Upgrade.** The pool's failure to confirm Wrocław came from consulting the *World Peace Council* page rather than the congress's own article, which names him.
- **Auschwitz, 1948.** Asserted in the dossier, **confirmed by nothing reached**. Not a stop; named inside the Wrocław campa as something several accounts report and nothing reachable confirms.
- **The dove, disentangled.** The dossier compressed three events into "1949-04-19": the lithograph *Dove*, **9 January 1949** **[A: "Pablo Picasso"]**; the Salle Pleyel congress, **April 1949**, where Aragon chose *La Colombe* as emblem **[A: "World Peace Council"]**; Paloma born **19 April 1949 at Vallauris**, not Paris **[A: "Paloma Picasso"]**.
- **Kubla is not Kabul.** Duncan's Afghan hound is **Kubla** **[A: "Lump (dog)"]**; Picasso's own is **Kabul**, documented because he said the Chicago Picasso represented the head of an Afghan hound of that name **[A: "Pablo Picasso"]**, placing him in the household by the sculpture's 1962-63 design period. The dossier risked merging them; the file separates them.

## 8. Mougins and after, 1961-1986

Mas Notre-Dame-de-Vie, Mougins, 1961-1973 **[A: "Mougins"]**. Lump dead 29 March 1973, aged 16-17, ten days before Picasso **[A: "Lump (dog)"]**. Picasso dead at Mougins **8 April 1973**, heart failure following pulmonary edema, aged 91, no will **[A: "Pablo Picasso"]**. Buried at Vauvenargues **16 April 1973** (not 10 April, as the dossier had it), "in a sleeping position beneath an earthen mound on the shaded terrace before the main entrance," the 1933 *Woman with a Vase*, second casting, shown at the Spanish Pavilion in 1937, placed on the grave; Jacqueline beside him from 1986 **[A: "Château de Vauvenargues," which also upgrades the sculpture claim the pool had flagged as unverified]**. Estate settled by dation en paiement, ratified 1979, over 5,000 works selected for the Hôtel Salé; second dation after 1986; museum reopened 25 October 2014, his birthday **[A: "Musée national Picasso-Paris"]**.

- **Lump's place of death is unsupported.** The pool put it in Germany, twice; the article records only that Duncan took the ailing dog to Stuttgart in 1964 for several months of treatment, and Duncan's home in this period was Castellaras in the Alpes-Maritimes. The campa states the death, then states that the Germany placement is unsupported.
- May 1973: the 201 paintings he helped assemble that spring opened at the Palais des Papes, Avignon **[A: "Pablo Picasso"]**. He was alive while assembling them and dead when it opened, so "posthumous" is true of the opening only. A **separate 1970 exhibition** at the same venue is asserted in the dossier and **confirmed by nothing reached** **[R]**; it is nowhere in the file.
- The "Picasso curse" reading is **[R]** interpretation, not documented causation.

## Places that are not his stops

1. **Café de l'Hippodrome, 17 February 1901.** Casagemas's death, Casagemas's place. The stop sits at **Madrid**, its `date_confidence` stating that the sources give Picasso's whereabouts only as "in Spain," that the Madrid pin is conventional rather than documented for that day, and that the café is not his stop. The café is a `suggested_ref`.
2. **Gernika, 26 April 1937.** He never went there. The bombing is told from **7 rue des Grands-Augustins**, and that stop's `sources` say so, to stop anyone re-adding a Basque pin later.
3. **Hôtel Salé, MoMA, the Reina Sofía, the UN tapestry, the Harry Ransom Center.** Places his *objects* went; each object's fate is told inside his own stop for the object he made.

Two one-directional relations run the other way, real and citable but not his travel: **Kandinsky and Marc's Blaue Reiter almanac** (1912) and **Klee's viewing of Picasso at the 1912 Sonderbund**. Both are the other man's stop. The almanac is a `suggested_ref` on the *Demoiselles* stop; the Sonderbund is not in the file.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Picasso is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Three relations in the pool are not rooted in his own life. None has been silently deleted.

1. **Augusto Boal, UNESCO Pablo Picasso Medal, 1994.** Direction: **forward** (Picasso d. 1973). An honorific attached to his name, not a relation he had. Not in the file; the pool itself calls it weak and posthumous. Forward reach is not licensable by an agent, so it is flagged, not written.
2. **Vaslav Nijinsky and the Ballets Russes in Buenos Aires, September 1913.** Direction: **lateral, inherited**. The pool is explicit that Picasso never travelled to Buenos Aires and that the pin comes through the shared company. Not in the file; no Argentine pin appears on it anywhere.
3. **Le Corbusier at Wrocław, August 1948.** Direction: **lateral**. Co-attendance at one congress, no attested relation between the two men. Under the rule that shared geography alone is not grounds to name another traveler, he is named here and **not** in any campa; the Wrocław campa lists only non-atlas delegates. If the operator counts co-delegation as a relation, the name restores in one word.

Borderline and **kept**: **Christian Dior's Galerie Jacques Bonjean, 34 rue La Boétie, 1928**, hanging Picasso beside Dalí, Braque, Max Jacob and Giacometti. A dealer showing his work is a real one-directional relation, and it appears in the campa for **Picasso's own home at number 23 on the same street**, which is his stop. The pool flagged the address coincidence as [R]; the campa states both numbers and draws no inference from the proximity.

## Quotes: what survived verification

Twelve stops carry quotes, each in the wording a source actually gives. Repairs:

- **De Zayas, "Picasso Speaks," *The Arts*, May 1923.** The pool **spliced and truncated** the research passage, joining two non-contiguous sentences and dropping "with his eyes fixed on the ground" without ellipsis. The file quotes p. 315 as Wikiquote gives it, and drops the purse sentence rather than splice.
- **Zervos, *Cahiers d'Art*, 1935.** Only "There is no abstract art. You always start with something." is verifiable in that English wording; the pool's third sentence is a Barr-lineage rendering seen in no source reached. The file stops there and puts **Chipp's fuller translation**, cited, in `quote_source`.
- **Herbert Read, *A Coat of Many Colours* (1945).** Truncated mid-sentence with no ellipsis. Restored: "a cry of outrage **and horror amplified by the spirit of genius**."
- **The bull-and-horse refusal.** Wording verified, but the reference is the **now-dead PBS supplement**, not Colm Toibin; Toibin belongs to the separate 1937 statement. No interview, date or translator recoverable. Kept, tagged **[R]**.
- **"Lump, he's not a dog, he's not a little man, he's somebody else."** The pool's citation to The Independent, 1 May 2006 belongs to Duncan's "This was a love affair" instead; the real one is Alan Riding, *The New York Times*, 25 August 2006. **Not used**; the [A]-tagged Duncan quote is.
- **Françoise Gilot, "He thought I would react like all his other women."** Referenced to the **Ursinus College Gilot Collection**, not *Life with Picasso* (1964). Kept at **[R]**.
- **Apollinaire, "une sorte de surréalisme."** Coinage attested; the **full surrounding sentence was never retrieved from a facsimile**. Quoted as the phrase alone.
