# Micaela Bastidas Puyucahua (1744-1781): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-12 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `micaela_bastidas`. Companion file: `micaela_bastidas.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition · contradictions are flagged in place and **not** adjudicated.

**Method.** The primary base (Areche's sentencia, the captured correspondence, the Coleccion Documental de la Independencia del Peru) was not reachable in full. What was: the es/en Wikipedia set, EcuRed, a Wikisource transcription of one letter, Berg 2013 via OpenEdition. Claims are tagged to what was seen, not to what the literature presumably holds. The Spanish articles proved consistently better on dates and places; several errors corrected below originate in English Wikipedia. Wrong coordinates from the research pool are corrected with their magnitude, because those pins have circulated.

---

## 1. Birth: two towns, no register

**[A, en.wikipedia, 'Micaela Bastidas']** Born 23 June 1744 at Pampamarca, province of Canas, viceroyalty of Peru, to Manuel Bastidas and Josefa Puyucahua.

**[R, EcuRed, 'Micaela Bastidas']** An alternate tradition places the birth at Tamburco, outside Abancay in Apurimac, two hundred kilometres west, and makes her the daughter of a Spanish father and an indigenous mother. Both traditions name the same parents.

**Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** Both towns hold the birth as fact; neither is anchored in a surviving parish record. Pampamarca won the textbooks and the monuments through later nationalist historiography and the state, which is a fact about commemoration, not about evidence.

**Coordinate corrections.** Pampamarca (Canas, Cusco) is at **-14.1453, -71.4594** (es.wikipedia, 'Distrito de Pampamarca (Canas)'), not -14.2681, -71.2278: about 28 km out. The district sits at 3,811 m, about 12,500 ft, so the pool's "above thirteen thousand feet" overstates it. Tamburco is at **-13.6169, -72.8711** (es.wikipedia, 'Distrito de Tamburco'), not -13.6425, -72.9061: about 5 km out.

**Gap.** No baptismal certificate located for either town. The ethnic description of the family varies by source and is not settled here.

## 2. Marriage, sons, caciazgo

**[A, en/es.wikipedia, 'Micaela Bastidas']** Married Jose Gabriel Condorcanqui on 25 May 1760 in the church of Nuestra Senora de la Purificacion at Surimana, a month short of her sixteenth birthday.

**Correction.** The groom was **22**, not 20: born 19 March 1738 (es.wikipedia, 'Tupac Amaru II'). Surimana is at **-14.1535, -71.5749** (14°09′13″S 71°34′30″O), not -14.2306, -71.2989: about 32 km out.

**[R]** That the marriage register survives is asserted more strongly by biographies than the reachable sources support: es.wikipedia's Surimana article attests the temple, not the book. Unverified.

**[A, en/es.wikipedia]** Three sons: Hipolito (b. 1761), Mariano (b. 1762), Fernando (b. 1768). **[R]** Birthplaces are not individually documented; Surimana and Tungasuca are inferred from the couple's fixed residence.

**Correction, fates of the sons.** The pool had Mariano dying during the campaign. Per es.wikipedia, 'Tupac Amaru II' he was pardoned after his uncle Diego Cristobal negotiated peace, pensioned at 600 pesos, arrested in a later conspiracy, deported, and died in Brazil in 1784. Fernando was ten at the executions, deported, shipwrecked off Portugal, jailed at Cadiz, and died destitute in Madrid on 30 July 1798.

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Tupac Amaru II']** Jose Gabriel succeeded to the caciazgo of Surimana, Tungasuca and Pampamarca. **Contradiction, flagged:** the Bastidas articles date the succession to **1764**, es.wikipedia's 'Tupac Amaru II' to **1766**, sixteen years after his father's death. Both carried; 1766 is better argued, 1764 is in wider circulation.

**Coordinate correction.** Tungasuca, capital of the Distrito de Tupac Amaru, Canas, is at **-14.157, -71.505** (es.wikipedia, 14°09′25″S 71°30′17″O), not -14.2436, -71.3183: about 22 km out. The wrong pin was repeated at six stops in the pool and is its most contaminating error.

**[A, EcuRed]** By 1780 the household was resident in the Tinta country of Cusco, as protest against the repartos, the mita and corregidor abuses intensified. Tinta (Canchis) is at **-14.1440, -71.4055**, not -14.3467, -71.3536: about 24 km out.

## 3. The rising, November 1780

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Rebelion de Tupac Amaru II'; 'Antonio de Arriaga']** The corregidor Antonio de Arriaga was seized by ambush on **4 November 1780**, then tried and publicly **hanged on 10 November 1780 in the plaza of Tungasuca**. His body was afterward buried in the church of Tinta.

**Correction, major.** The pool gave one event: Arriaga hanged at Tinta on 4 November. Seizure and execution are six days and one town apart, and the Tinta association comes from the burial, not the gallows. English Wikipedia's 'Tupac Amaru II' is the outlier and the origin of the conflation.

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Rebelion de Tupac Amaru II']** On **16 November 1780** Tupac Amaru issued a bando from Tungasuca decreeing the emancipation of enslaved Africans and Afro-Peruvians. **Correction:** the pool dated this proclamation to 26 November; no proclamation of that date appears in any source checked.

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Batalla de Sangarara']** 18 November 1780: about 6,000 rebels defeat about 2,300 royalists at Sangarara; royalist dead given as 395 to 576; the church the royalists and townspeople crowded into is burned.

**Correction, evidentiary.** The pool had **both spouses** excommunicated after Sangarara. es.wikipedia documents only **Tupac Amaru's**, by Bishop Moscoso y Peralta. Micaela's inclusion is **not attested** and is carried as **[R]** or dropped, never as [A].

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Micaela Bastidas']** "Tras el triunfo de Sangarara, Micaela fue constituida jefa interina de la rebelion." Interim chief of the rebellion **after the victory at Sangarara**, November 1780. **Correction, five months:** the pool placed this in April 1781, after the reverse at Cusco, which inverts its meaning. The appointment follows a victory.

**[A, en.wikipedia; EcuRed]** Member of the rebel council of five; ran the Tungasuca headquarters, meaning provisions, discipline, recruitment and correspondence, while her husband campaigned.

## 4. Command: the letters

The strongest documentary basis for her role is her captured correspondence, which survives because the crown needed it to convict her. One letter is transcribed on es.wikisource from Congreso del Peru, Fondo Editorial Bicentenario, 'Tupac Amaru' vol. 6, p. 368.

**[A, es.wikisource transcription]** Verbatim fragments:

- "Chepe mio, tu me has de acabar de pesadumbres, pues andas muy despacio paseandote en los pueblos y mas en Yauri, tardandote dos grandes dias con gran descuido."
- "los soldados tienen razon de aburrirse irse cada uno a sus pueblo."
- "Yo ya no tengo paciencia para aguantar todo esto, pues yo misma soy capaz de entregarme a los enemigos para que me quiten la vida, porque veo el poco anhelo con que ves este asunto tan grave que corroe con detrimento la vida de todos."
- "Estamos en medio de los enemigos, no tenemos segura la vida y por tu causa a pique de peligrar todos mis hijos, los demas de nuestra parte."
- "Bastante advertencia te di, para que inmediatamente fueses al Cuzco pero has dado todo a la barata, dandoles tiempo para que se prevengan como ya lo han hecho."

**Verification gap.** The Congreso.gob.pe page cited by Wikisource (page368.html) returned **404**. The transcription could not be checked against the original scan, and Wikisource marks its own page incomplete. The wording above is Wikisource's.

**[A, es.wikipedia]** Her repeated warnings of a royalist counterattack turned Tupac Amaru back from his southern campaign at Lampa, December 1780. **[A, en.wikipedia]** She criticised his hesitation before Cusco, urging rapid assault over slower encirclement.

**Correction on a quoted phrase.** **"resolucion varonil"** is authentic, but the pool attributed it to **Areche**, which the sources do not support. It traces to Berg 2013, where it reads "la acusaron de «resolucion varonil»": accusers unnamed and plural. es.wikipedia also cites Berg, not Areche. What Areche **is** quoted as writing, per Berg, is the exclusion of "la muger del Rebelde Micaela Bastidas" from the pardon offered to those who would denounce her husband. The two phrases sit at separate stops in the journey file and are never combined. "igual de implacable" is Wikipedia's paraphrase, not quoted words, and is dropped.

## 5. The siege of Cusco, January 1781

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Rebelion de Tupac Amaru II']** The mobilisation order dates to **4 January 1781**; the fighting before Cusco lasted about a week; the siege was lifted **11 January 1781**. **Correction:** the pool dated the order to 15 December 1780, roughly three weeks early, and to a siege that had not begun.

**[A, en.wikipedia, 'Tupac Amaru II']** The delay let Spanish reinforcements arrive; the assault was repelled and the insurgents scattered. **[A, en/es.wikipedia]** By March 1781 the rebel army numbered roughly seven thousand.

**[R]** The corridor towns (Yanaoca, Combapata, Quiquijana, Urcos) are reconstruction: no surviving letter places her at any of them, only her direction of what moved along that road. Corrected pins: Checacupe **-14.0254, -71.4536** (8 km out in the pool); Quiquijana **-13.8220, -71.5416** (15 km out).

## 6. Collapse, betrayal, capture, April 1781

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Rebelion de Tupac Amaru II']** Defeat at Checacupe, 5-6 April 1781.

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Tupac Amaru II']** Tupac Amaru captured **6 April 1781 at Langui**, betrayed by his own officers **Ventura Landaeta** and **Francisco Santa Cruz**.

**[A, es.wikipedia, 'Rebelion de Tupac Amaru II']** "El 7 de abril, el traidor Landaeta tambien entrega a los realistas a Micaela Bastidas", taken **separately, the following day**, while attempting to escape toward La Paz.

**Corrections.** (1) The place is Langui, not "between Tinta and Sangarara", an English-Wikipedia idiosyncrasy. (2) The betrayer is Francisco **Santa Cruz**, not "Francisco Cruz". (3) Langui is at **-14.4326, -71.2727**, not -14.2864, -71.2306: about 17 km out. (4) The pool mis-stated the "minority tradition" as a seizure at Tungasuca during a rear-guard defence; the real divergence is capture *with* the family on the 6th versus *separately, in flight toward La Paz*, on the 7th. Both carried.

**Reconciliation, labelled as such. [R]** The pool treated the Landaeta/Santa Cruz betrayal and the Pumacahua account as rivals. They are not: Pumacahua commanded the offensive that broke the rebellion, while the seizure was made by the couple's own officers. en.wikipedia says he "received fame and prestige for Amaru's capture", which is not the same as having made it.

**Downgrade.** The pool had Micaela **[A]** at Tinta in April 1781 organising the final defence. No source checked places her there; es.wikipedia has her in flight toward La Paz. Carried as **[R]**, tradition only.

## 7. Trial, sentence, execution

**[A]** Tried at Cusco in May 1781 by the tribunal of visitador general Jose Antonio de Areche, with her husband and the captured captains. Torture was within colonial procedure; the sources are markedly fuller on her husband's treatment than on hers.

**Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** The sentence is dated **14 May 1781** by es.wikipedia, 'Micaela Bastidas' and **15 May 1781** by es.wikipedia, 'Rebelion de Tupac Amaru II'. Both carried. Consequence: the interval to execution is three days or four depending on which is taken, and the pool asserted "four days" without flagging the dependency.

**[A, en/es.wikipedia]** Executed in the Plaza de Armas of Cusco on **18 May 1781**, aged 36, made first to watch the execution of her eldest son Hipolito and of other family and allies, among them the cacica Tomasa Tito Condemayta. She was killed **before** her husband, who died the same day.

**Contradiction on the method, flagged, not resolved.** en.wikipedia: tongue cut out, an attempted garrote failed on her slender neck, strangulation by rope, then beating with a club and kicks to the stomach and breasts. Spanish-language sources (EcuRed and historiacultural-derived material) give death by garrote vil after resistance and an attempt to cut out her tongue. Both agree the garrote could not be applied cleanly; they do not agree on sequence or final instrument.

**[R]** She fought her executioners before being subdued (en.wikipedia, 'Tupac Amaru II').

**[R]** A final utterance is attributed to her: "Me perdi de ver a mis hijos seguir creciendo, todo por mi patria, por la igualdad y por la libertad." es.wikipedia carries it without a contemporary source; wording and moment both vary. Carried as attribution, never as record.

**Correction of a source attribution.** The pool sourced the deliberate Crown strategy of extinguishing the Tupac Amaru lineage to EcuRed, which says only that she was "ejecutada por orden de autoridades de ocupacion espanolas". The extirpative intent is real but belongs to **Areche's sentencia**: banishment of the surviving sons, prohibition of Inca names, dress and genealogies.

## 8. The body, and what replaced it

**[A, en.wikipedia]** The bodies were dismembered and the parts distributed to towns across the region as warning. Tupac Amaru's head was displayed at Cusco then sent to Tinta; his limbs went to Tungasuca, Ayapata, Livitaca and Santa Rosa.

**Gap, genuine.** The equivalent detail for **Micaela's** remains does not exist. General accounts describe dismemberment and display without naming which town received which part of her, and some conflate her remains with her husband's. **No grave, tomb or burial site for Micaela Bastidas is documented anywhere.** This is the central absence of the file, and the reason the commemorative geography below exists at all.

Corrected pin: Livitaca (Chumbivilcas) **-14.3144, -71.6887**, not -14.15, -71.9667: about 34 km out.

**Commemoration, [A] unless noted.**

- Effigy in the Panteon de los Proceres, Lima (en.wikipedia). Position within the building unconfirmed; that page returned 404.
- Postal stamp issued by Peru, 1971 (es.wikipedia).
- District of **Micaela Bastidas**, Grau, Apurimac, capital Ayrihuanca, average altitude 3,510 m, created by Law 12861 of 20 December 1957 under Manuel Prado Ugarteche. Pin -14.105, -72.612.
- Law 21705 of 1976, recognising the bicentenary of the rebellion under both their names (en.wikipedia).
- **Universidad Nacional Micaela Bastidas de Apurimac** (UNAMBA), Abancay, created **26 September 2000** by Law 27348. Pin **-13.6170, -72.8680**; the pool gave the generic Abancay centre, 2.3 km off.
- **[R]** Plaza Micaela Bastidas with a central sculpture, Abancay; coordinates unconfirmed.
- **Parque Micaela Bastidas**, Puerto Madero, Buenos Aires, opened 31 January 2003; 7-8 hectares, over 40,000 trees and shrubs. Pin -34.6153, -58.3603.

**[R]** The pattern: with no relic and no grave, the commemorative apparatus substitutes toponymy and sculpture for a tomb, and later Peruvian and Argentine civic culture treats that as restitution for the erasure of 1781. Synthesis, not a claim any single source makes.

## 9. Interlocks with the atlas

Named in the journey's campa text. In each case the other traveller's event is **[A]** in that traveller's file and the tie to Bastidas is **[R]**: no source states a connection.

- **pizarro.** Rode into Huacaypata, the Plaza de Armas of Cusco, in 1533; stripped the Coricancha, installed a puppet Sapa Inca. Bastidas dies on the same stones 248 years later. His file cross-references the plaza for essay 207.
- **bolivar.** Entered Cusco 10 July 1825, refused the crown of the Incas and sent it to Sucre; planted three republican flags on the Cerro Rico, 26 October 1825.
- **belgrano.** Proposed before Congress on 6 July 1816 to restore the Inca dynasty with its capital at Cuzco.
- **che_guevara.** Cusco 1952, "the navel of the world," reading the walls and Sacsayhuaman as accusation.
- **tschiffely.** Rode Potosi and the altiplano in 1925 and into Cusco in 1925/26 on "the road the runners of the Inca used."

- **valientes.** Ana de Urinza and Eustaquia de Souza on the Potosi-Cusco-Lima axis a century earlier, dressed as men, portraits inscribed "peruanas de Potosi."
- **blavatsky.** Read the Inca ruins at Cusco as relics of a vanished race, which is one way of not seeing a rebellion.
- **policarpa_salavarrieta.** Hanged by the same crown at Bogota, 14 November 1817. Structural parallel only: different viceroyalty, unrelated conspiracy.
- **Tupac Amaru II** and **Tupac Amaru I** sit in QUEUE.md without journey files; the queue text itself flags that the 1572 Sapa Inca is a different person whose name the rebels of 1780 took deliberately.

**House-style note.** Cusco, Potosi and Lima pins are inherited byte-identical from the files above rather than re-derived: Plaza de Armas **-13.517, -71.9785**; Coricancha **-13.5183, -71.9773**; Cerro Rico **-19.6097, -65.7538**; Lima **-12.0464, -77.0428**.

## 10. Contradictions carried, not adjudicated

Index of the eight flagged above, each left standing: **birthplace** (Pampamarca vs Tamburco, no parish record for either); **caciazgo date** (1764 vs 1766); **sentencing date** (14 vs 15 May 1781); **method of execution** (rope, beating and kicks vs garrote vil, both agreeing the garrote failed); **excommunication** (documented for her husband, not attested for her, widely repeated as covering both); **capture** (with the family at Langui on 6 April, or separately on 7 April in flight toward La Paz); **the final utterance** (attributed, unsourced, variable); **survival of the 1760 marriage register** (asserted by biographies, unconfirmed).

## 11. Gaps stated as gaps

- No baptismal record, no grave, no tomb, no documented disposition of her remains. No portrait from life: every image of her is posthumous invention.
- Her presence is undocumented at Sangarara, at the corridor towns, at Checacupe, and at Tinta in April 1781. Every such stop is **[R]** and says so in its date_confidence field.
- Her conditions in detention at Cusco are documented only in general terms, far less fully than his.
- The letter transcription could not be checked against the archival scan, and the two obvious scholarly authorities were unreachable. See section 12.
