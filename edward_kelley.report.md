# Edward Kelley (1555 - 1597?), scryer to John Dee, knight of the Bohemian estates

*Research report, 2026-08-15. Companion: `edward_kelley.journey.json` (9 segments, 42 stops).*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting only on Kelley's own testimony. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated.

A structural warning covering the whole file: **almost everything known about Kelley before March 1582 comes through Kelley's mouth, written down by John Dee.** The birth hour, the Oxford years, the buried manuscript and the red powder are his own account of himself, recorded by a man with strong reasons to believe him. The independent record starts at Mortlake and is thereafter thin, hostile, or Bohemian.

---

## 1. England before Dee (1555 - 1582)

**Birth [R].** 1 August 1555, four in the afternoon, at Worcester, known only from the horoscope Dee later cast from what Kelley told him. **No parish record**; some writers give London, and the DNB disputes Worcester. The pool tagged this **[A]** in chronology and **[R]** in geography; **[R]** is defensible and is what the journey file carries. A sister, Elizabeth, born 1558 **[R]**.

**Oxford [R].** Anthony Wood (*Athenae Oxonienses*) reports a Talbot at Gloucester Hall c. 1572-73, leaving without a degree. **University records do not confirm it.** What is independently visible is competence: Latin, some Greek, a hand good enough for enemies to accuse him of forging deeds. Where he learned it is a gap.

**Lancaster [R].** Pilloried c. 1580, both ears cropped. **The charge is misreported in most modern retellings.** DNB: "About 1580 he had his ears cropped in the pillory at Lancaster for forging some ancient title-deeds." Title-deeds is primary; coining and clipping appear only as alternatives. **No court record for either.** The cap is separately attested (Wikipedia: he "usually wore a cap on his head"; every engraving shows one). The inference runs backwards, from cap to shears.

**Walton-le-Dale, the corpse [R as event, A as printed account].** Kelley and **Paul Waring** dig up a recently buried body and make it speak. **Source misattribution corrected:** the story is **John Weever's**, *Ancient Funerall Monuments* (1631), **not** Casaubon's 1659 preface as the pool had it. Weever puts it in "the Parke of Walton in le dale"; the often-repeated "St Leonard's churchyard" is a later embellishment with nothing behind it. He had it at second hand from a servingman, fifty years on, spells the burial ground "Law church-yard" and names Waring (the pool's "Warng" is OCR damage).

**Northwick Hill and the Book of Dunstan [R, Kelley's testimony alone].** He tells Dee that a spiritual creature led him and one John Blockley to a spot in the Gloucestershire hills where they dug up a manuscript attributed to St Dunstan and a quantity of red powder, the powder projected at Trebon in December 1586. **No independent witness exists.**

## 2. Mortlake (1582 - 1583)

**Arrival, March 1582 [A].** He presents himself at Dee's house opposite St Mary the Virgin as **Edward Talbot**, shortly after Dee dismissed Barnabas Saul as a fraud. Dee's diary records the engagement; `john_dee.journey.json` narrates the same scene at the table of practice.

**The Enochian material [A as record, R as to origin]. Date correction:** the pool dated it to 1582. Wikipedia's *Enochian* dates the reception to **1583-1584**, beginning **26 March 1583**, when Kelley reported a twenty-one letter alphabet.

**The instrument is contested and it matters.** Dee's own writings have Kelley seeing **in the crystal**, the shew-stone. The **black obsidian mirror** in the British Museum, Mexican obsidian carried to Europe in the 1520s, is documented as **Dee's possession** with a provenance through Horace Walpole; identifying it as the glass Kelley scryed with is an **eighteenth-century attribution, not a sixteenth-century fact [R]**. The pool tagged it **[A]** twice. It is the most reproduced image of the sessions and it is wrong.

**Named spirits:** Uriel, **Madimi**, Nalvage, Ave, Il. See §5 on the Uriel/Madimi confusion.

**Laski, May 1583 [R].** Olbracht Laski, palatine of Sieradz, sits in on a session and is told through Kelley that he will be king of Poland. His estates were already mortgaged. He invites the household to Poland. **Departure, 21 September 1583 [A]:** Dee, Jane, the children, Kelley, Joan Kelley, servants and Laski leave by night. Kelley had married **Joan (Jane) Cooper**, a widow with two children, shortly before **[R]**.

## 3. Poland (1583 - 1585)

- **Lask, December 1583 [A]** (`john_dee.journey.json`): the party settles at Laski's own town; sessions continue at his expense.
- **Krakow, spring 1584 [A]:** the **forty-eight Keys** received over weeks in lodgings near the market, dictated backwards letter by letter. Largest single block of the Enochian corpus.
- **Krakow, mid-April 1584 [A]:** Kelley's revolt. Dee records him calling their teachers "deluders", saying he could have learned the seven liberal sciences in two years had he begun with logic, and that he would burn the books out of hand. He is back at the stone within days. **Place correction:** the pool assigned this to Prague with Prague coordinates; the folio's headings read "Cracoviae". It is Krakow.
- **Niepolomice, 1584 [A as audience, R as to date]:** audience with **King Stephen Bathory**. Beforehand the spirits through Kelley dictate the exact wording Dee is to use in claiming the stone and asking a place at court, with the marginal note *Responsum pro Stephano* and another recalling they did the same "unto Rodolph in writing". The king commits nothing.

---

## 4. The Emperor's lands, and out of them (1584 - 1586)

- **Prague Castle, September 1584 [A]:** **Rudolf II** receives Dee, who delivers the angelic warning. Rudolf is courteous and hands it to his physician. Note the division of labour: **Kelley produces the message, Dee delivers it.** It inverts after 1589.
- **Expulsion [A as fact; contradiction on date]:** the nuncio **Germanico Malaspina** calls the doctrine heretical and an imperial order gives the pair six days to leave. **Flagged:** geography and interlock date this **May 1586**; chronology records a summons to answer Malaspina on **27 March 1587**, by which date they should already be expelled. Both cannot stand. The journey file carries both stops and says so in each.
- **Kassel, 1586 [R]:** brief refuge with **Landgrave Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel** (French, 1972). Thin trace; no source details what was transacted or how long they stayed.

## 5. Trebon (1586 - 1589)

**Patronage [A].** **Vilem of Rozmberk** takes both households into his castle in south Bohemia. Kelley gets furnaces; Dee gets a study.

**The projection, 19 December 1586 [A that Dee recorded it; R as to what happened].** Kelley projects the red powder on base metal before the household; the product is weighed and taken for gold. Dee enters it without commentary. The transmutation is not independently verifiable and cannot be made so. What *is* verifiable is that the report travelled and that Kelley's price rose.

**The wife-sharing episode.** The most error-prone passage in the biography; the pool contained three separate mistakes.

1. **The angel is Madimi, not Uriel [A].** The pool's chronology named Uriel, from a loose sentence in Wikipedia's *John Dee*, contradicted by its own geography lens, by the DNB, and by the diary, which names Madimi and dates the action **18 April 1587**. (Casaubon's preface separately reports Dee dating the matter to "An. Dom. 1587. April 7, Trebone" and naming **Uriel** "the author of their Conjunction": a fourth date and a competing angel, left open.)
2. **A covenant was drawn up and signed 3 May 1587** by Dee, Kelley, Jane and Joan **[A]**; `john_dee.journey.json` carries this date.
3. **The 22 May 1588 entry is truncated and misread in the pool.** Full text: "May 22nd, Mistris Kelly received the sacrament, and to me and my wife gave her hand in charity; **and we rushed not from her.**" The pool glossed it as reconciliation *after* the episode; the fullest printing treats the date as that of the cross-matching **itself**. The closing clause is restored and the gloss dropped, **no consulted source supporting it.**

**Theodorus Trebonianus, February 1588 [A].** A son born to Jane Dee roughly nine months after the covenant. Paternity privately disputed and **never resolved**; the diary says nothing here.

**The cipher table [A].** Dee records sending Kelley alone to his chamber and receiving a written sheet an hour later, checked against "my book of Enoch his Tables". **The pool's transcription is garbled**: it runs six rows into one string and silently corrects a row number. The rows print as a grid with right-hand totals 24, 48, 72, 96, 120. The journey file quotes Dee's surrounding narration instead.

**The glass handed over, 4 December 1588 [A]** (`john_dee.journey.json`): Dee gives Kelley the crystal shown to Elizabeth I and Rudolf II. **The parting, spring 1589 [A]:** Dee leaves for England, Kelley stays, and **they never meet again.** In November Dee waits at Stade on the Elbe for a man who does not come.

## 6. Sir Edward Kelley (1589 - 1591)

**Knighted 23 February 1590 at Prague [A]. Title correction:** Wikipedia gives "Sir Edward Kelley of **Imany** and **New Luben**"; the pool rendered it variously "New Lueben", "Jimany", "Nova Liben". Czech Wikipedia identifies the estate as the **panstvi Nova Liber**, Prague-West. He also received **inkolat**, citizenship of the Bohemian estates, and was styled Imperial Councillor by 1590. The pool's suggested 1589 alternate date is **unsupported by any consulted source** and is dropped.

**Property [A, Czech Wikipedia]:** the **Faust House** on Charles Square, the fortress of **Vcelni Hradek**, mill and brewery holdings at **Jilove u Prahy**. Of the Faust House the pool's geography lens said "documented ownership history does not securely place Kelley there"; **that overstates the doubt**, Czech Wikipedia listing it among properties he owned. What is legend is the *Faust* attachment: no document connects Faust to Prague at all.

## 7. Prisons and death (1591 - 1597/98)

**First arrest [A as fact; contradictions on reason, date and place].** Wikipedia says Krivoklat, "supposedly for killing an official named Jiri Hunkler in a duel"; the pool led instead with money taken from Rudolf and Peter Vok. **The duel is what the cited source says**; the debt version is what his creditors would have said, and neither is settled. Wikipedia gives **May 1591 at Krivoklat**, Czech Wikipedia **1592 at Sobeslav**: flagged, not smoothed. He **refused to eat** **[A]**.

**Release [contradiction].** English Wikipedia: a bare **1595**. Czech Wikipedia: release **October 1593**, a period at liberty, **re-arrest November 1596 for debts**. The English date compresses three years of his life out of existence. The journey file follows the Czech chronology and flags the variant.

**Second imprisonment, Hnevin Castle above Most [A].** Czech Wikipedia: November 1596 to 1597, not the pool's 1595-1597. Wikipedia's *Hnevin Castle* adds that Kelley and one **Marek Mamugny** were held there with **the town of Most required to provision them at considerable expense**.

**The *Tractatus de lapide philosophorum*, summer 1597 [A, Czech Wikipedia]**, finished in the fortress and dedicated to Rudolf II from prison. **Date correction:** the pool dated the treatises to 1590 and his years of court favour, which turns a plea into a courtesy. Printed posthumously **1676** as *Tractatus duo egregii ... una cum theatro astronomiae* **[A]**.

**The escape [A].** Out of a tower window on a rope; it fails, he falls, the leg breaks. Confirmed independently by Czech Wikipedia. **Citation warning:** the DNB describes an escape attempt but dates the death to 1595, so it cannot be cited for a 1597 fall.

**Death: incompatible accounts, none adjudicated.**

- Died of the fall, **1 November 1597, aged 42** (Czech Wikipedia); or **late 1597 / early 1598** (English Wikipedia; Weever 1631; Browne 1674).
- **Poisoned himself** before wife and children, 1597/98 (Simon Tadeas, Rudolf II's geologist).
- Died **5 February 1595** (Ashmole, per DNB); **October 1595** (Wood, per DNB); **25 November 1595** (**Dee's own diary**, per DNB).

The pool asserted that "standard reference works give Kelley's death year as 1597". **False as stated.** The DNB, the reference work the pool cites elsewhere, gives three variants and all fall in **1595**; the 1597/98 dating is the Czech tradition. The pool also offered Browne (1674) as corroboration; he wrote in England seventy-seven years later from hearsay and is the *same* tradition, not an independent witness. The journey file presents 1595 and 1597 as an open conflict and uses the Czech date only as its spine, saying so. **Burial is a gap:** no tomb, no marked grave, no record anywhere consulted.

## 8. Afterlife

- **1631 [A]:** **Weever** prints the ear-loss, the Prague sojourn, Elizabeth's recall attempt through "Captaine Peter Gwinney", and the fatal fall in one passage - **p. 45, not p. 71** as the pool had it (his index: "Kelley the Alchumift 45. 46."); the necromancy anecdote is **p. 46, not p. 72**. The pool's claim that Weever is "the earliest known printed source for the ear story" is **unverified**, and dropped.
- **1659 [A]:** **Casaubon** prints the diaries meaning to discredit Dee, and instead fixes Kelley in the record as the other half of the operation. He calls him "a great Conjurer". **1676 [A]:** the *Tractatus* printed posthumously. **1785, traditional [R]:** the obsidian mirror reaches the British Museum via Walpole (see §2).
- **1888-1900s [A]:** the **Golden Dawn** rebuilds the Enochian tablets and keys from the British Museum manuscripts into working ritual; **Wynn Westcott** devises a syllabic pronunciation. **1893 [A]: Arthur Edward Waite** publishes *The Alchemical Writings of Edward Kelley* (`arthur_edward_waite.journey.json`).
- **1978 [A]: Laycock**, *The Complete Enochian Dictionary*, Askin, London. **Date correction:** the pool gave 2001, the Weiser reprint; **Laycock died in 1988**, so a 2001 publication is impossible as stated. His sceptical finding: about **250 distinct words** in the whole corpus.
- **Modern, undated [A]:** a statue of Kelley stands at Hnevin Castle (Wikipedia image caption). No installation year in any source; the journey file uses a placeholder and says so in `date_confidence`.

## Citation corrections to the 1659 folio

The pool's Casaubon page numbers are **systematically wrong** because the folio has **two paginations**: Part I runs to p. 448, then Part II restarts at 1 and runs to about p. 45. Pages 499, 566 and 570 **do not exist**. Pool citation → correct citation:

- p. 95 (summary, wives in common) → "The Table", Part II, chap. VI (cross-ref. Part II p. 9)
- p. 566 (Dee on Madimi's words) → Part II, ca. p. 11 · p. 570 (cipher table) → Part II, ca. p. 13
- p. 499 ("Behold (o King)") → Part I, ca. p. 407
- p. 231 ("By August next?") and p. 230 ("our Teachers were deluders") → Part I, ca. p. 90 (Cracow actions, April 1584)
- p. 36 (preface, "Seer, or Skryer") → Preface, sig. C-D, ca. p. 13
- p. 87 (summary, Stone to the Emperor) → "The Table", Part I, chap. XCVIII (p. 240)
- p. 96 (summary, torn covenant) → "The Table", Part II, entry following chap. IX

**One misattribution, the most serious in the pool.** "By August next? What if it were a hundred August? you may be a weary before August next, as the Childres of Israel were of their Manna" was offered as a rare verbatim first-person line from Kelley. **It is not.** The folio labels Kelley "E.K.", Dee with a delta, and prints **the spirit unlabelled in italic**. Only the four words **"By August next?"** are Kelley's. The journey file does not use this quote.

## Honest gaps

1. No parish record of the birth; everything about 1555 is Dee's horoscope note.
2. No Oxford matriculation record for Talbot. No court record for the Lancaster pillory, under either charge.
3. No independent witness to the Northwick Hill find, the powder's origin, or the December 1586 projection.
4. **No account by either wife.** Jane Dee and Joan Kelley subscribed the covenant of 3 May 1587 and left nothing else; the most consequential decision either household made survives only in one husband's hand.
5. No burial place. The paternity of Theodorus Trebonianus is unresolved and unresolvable.
6. **Marek Mamugny**, held with Kelley at Hnevin, appears in the Wikipedia *Hnevin Castle* article and nowhere else consulted.
7. The **1595 / 1597 death conflict** has not been adjudicated by anyone reachable; the English antiquarian and Czech local traditions appear never to have been brought into contact.
