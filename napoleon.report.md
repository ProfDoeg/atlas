# Napoleon Bonaparte — Report

**Sources.** English Wikipedia (Napoleon; battles of Toulon, Lodi, Arcole, the Pyramids, Acre, Marengo, Austerlitz, Jena-Auerstedt, Wagram, Leipzig, Waterloo; Coup of 18 Brumaire; Coronation of Napoleon; Abdications of Bayonne; Joséphine de Beauharnais; Napoleon II; Fire of Moscow; Battle of Berezina; Retour des cendres; Death of Napoleon; Longwood House); napoleon.org (early years, 18 Brumaire, coronation, Austerlitz proclamation, Institut d'Égypte, Erfurt, Retour des cendres, farewell to the Old Guard); shannonselin.com (birth, love letters, Pyramids myth, Elba escape, Waterloo quotes, King of Rome's birth); Las Cases, *Mémorial de Sainte-Hélène*; Bourrienne, *Mémoires*; Englund, *Napoleon: A Political Life*; and the sibling files bolivar.journey.json, fichte.journey.json, goethe_full.journey.json, san_martin.journey.json, johann_jahn.journey.json for the atlas's own prior sightings of him.

**Judgment calls.** (1) Three "attributed" lines — the Pyramids' "forty centuries," Marengo's "lost at five, won at seven," and the Code Civil's "true glory" line — are flagged in their own quote_source as likely later legend or memoir-shaped rather than verbatim battlefield speech; per the register they are kept as canon events (the tradition tells them as his words) but the sourcing is honest about the doubt. (2) Toulon and Vendémiaire carry no quote at all — I could not find a verified contemporaneous line for either, so both are null rather than invented. (3) "Ajaccio — the break with Paoli" reuses Ajaccio's coordinates with a small offset rather than a wholly new site, matching the atlas convention (e.g. joan_of_arc's repeated Domrémy, Gien) of returning to a place the traveler already touched. (4) The Peninsular War is represented by a single hinge (Bayonne) rather than a full Spanish campaign, since Napoleon himself never returned to Spain after 1808 — the war is "his" only at the moment of the abdications; the fighting itself belongs to san_martin's and belgrano's arcs, cross-referenced rather than duplicated.

**Time-folds / gaps.** Auxonne, Valence garrison years, Directory-era Paris salon life, the Consulate's civil reforms beyond the Code, the full Peninsular War, the 1809 Austrian campaign's early stages, and the Hundred Days' domestic maneuvering are compressed or omitted to hold the curator's arc and the 30-45 stop target. The Las Cases "Longwood" dictation stop is dated inferred (mid-January 1816) since the daily routine has no single fixed start; the practice itself, and the shipboard promise ("we will write our Memoirs," August 1815), are attested.

**Three pin-matches, byte-identical per the brief:** Erfurt (50.978, 11.029; 1808-10-02) with goethe_full's "Voilà un homme" stop; Jena-Auerstedt (51.0, 11.6; 1806-10-14) with fichte's Jena; and Notre-Dame (48.853, 2.3499; 1804-12-02) with bolivar's coronation-watching stop.

**Five richest episodes.** Arcole's flag on the bridge (the image outrunning the fact, within a year the Republic's most reproduced picture); the Cairo Institut d'Égypte (savants measuring the Sphinx as the fleet burns behind them at Aboukir); the self-coronation at Notre-Dame (taking the crown from the tray before the Pope can set it, then whispering to Joseph about their father); the Fire of Moscow (the Kremlin held, the wooden city burned by its own governor, "what resoluteness! what people!"); and the retour des cendres (nineteen years dead, still able to draw six hundred thousand Parisians into a snowstorm).

**Connections to the atlas.** He is the hub the curator named him: watched crowning himself by young Bolívar in Paris and reviewed on the Milan plain days later (bolivar.journey.json, both pins shared); the shatterer of Fichte's and Jahn's Jena-Auerstedt world (fichte.journey.json, johann_jahn.journey.json, pin-matched); Goethe's "Voilà un homme" at Erfurt (goethe_full.journey.json, pin-matched); the abdicator of Bayonne whose puppet throne San Martín and Belgrano's generation rose to overthrow at Bailén and after (san_martin.journey.json, belgrano.journey.json); the Code Napoléon that reclassifies salomon_oppenheim as *Banquier et Négociant Notable* on the Rhine; the "ABC" who had no time for Pestalozzi's alphabet in the Tuileries; the amnesty-granting Emperor of barbier's Paris; the strategist Alvear and Juan Perón study at second hand in the classroom of his campaigns; and the Werther he carried through seven campaigns and eight hundred pyramids of sand. Where every other traveler in this atlas moves toward a vision, a shrine, a nation, or a self — Napoleon moves Europe, and Europe's dynasties, marriages, and borders move with him; his only true destination, after Waterloo, is the rock in the South Atlantic where he is finally still enough to dictate the story instead of making it.
