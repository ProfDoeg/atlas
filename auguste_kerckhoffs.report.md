# Auguste Kerckhoffs: research report

*Slug `auguste_kerckhoffs`. Jean Guillaume Auguste Victor Alexandre François Hubert Kerckhoffs, 1835-1903. German master, Volapükist, author of "La cryptographie militaire" (1883).*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, press legend, or a claim its own source flags as unproven. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

Everything rests on one document: **Caraco, Géraud-Stewart and Naccache, "Kerckhoffs' Legacy," IACR ePrint 2020/556**, 64 pp., which reproduces the civil records, the Tübingen diploma, the ministerial personnel file, the Lorient police inquiry and the burial certificate. Before it the biography in circulation was a handful of repeated sentences, several of them wrong. "The study" means this paper.

## 1. Limburg, 1835-1857

**Birth** at the **Nieuwenhof**, a lease farm outside **Nuth** in Dutch Limburg, **19 January 1835**, to Jean Guillaume Kerckhoffs (clerk-registrar, later mayor of Nuth) and Jeannette Elisabeth Lintjens. **[A: Nuth civil birth record]**

**Contradiction, not resolved.** His own **1876 Latin vita**, written for Tübingen, gives **31 January 1835** **[R: self-report]**. The study prefers the civil register and does not explain the twelve-day gap; both dates ride in the journey file's confidence field.

**A second, older error.** Much secondary literature gives **Maastricht** as birthplace. He studied at the gymnasium there in 1852-1853 **[A: 1876 vita]**; he was not born there. The school was compressed into the cradle.

**Rolduc**, the abbey boarding school at **Kerkrade**, roughly October 1847 to August 1852: second and third prizes over several years, no firsts; in one examination 4th of 14, demoted to 7th for conduct "not entirely satisfactory" **[A: Kerkrade municipal archive, study table 2, p. 9]**.

**Liège**, two years of philosophy (1853-1855); **Leuven**, two of natural sciences (1855-1857) **[A: 1876 vita]**. Older biographies inflate Liège into a degree or a doctorate; only coursework is documented, and the one attested doctorate is Tübingen 1876. Then **Eindhoven**, three years teaching after public examinations he describes as passed *cum laude* **[A: his own vita, so the distinction is self-report]**.

## 2. France, 1860-1873

**Meaux**, a private institution from about 1860: his first French post and the end of his Dutch career **[A: 1876 vita]**.

**Melun.** English and German at the collège, **19 October 1863** to **5 March 1873** **[A: Ministry of National Education archives, study pp. 12, 16]**. He substituted in Latin, Greek, history and mathematics; reports praise his learning and note that his classrooms are noisy. In **1867** the departmental council authorized a free winter English course, and he was unpaid secretary-librarian of the **Société d'encouragement à l'instruction** **[A: 1870 prefectural report]**.

**Marriage** to **Marie Émilie Thévenin**, mairie of the **10th arrondissement of Paris, 5 July 1864** **[A: Paris marriage act]**. Her parents were the bailiff Nicolas Jean Baptiste Thévenin and **Émilie Delphine Santerre**, so General **Antoine Joseph Santerre** (1752-1809) is her maternal grandfather; *Le Figaro* of 10 October 1888 independently has Kerckhoffs marrying "la petite-fille du général Santerre" **[A: study fn. 21]**. She was born at Coulommiers **13 February 1835**, three weeks after her husband **[A]**. Before the marriage she was recorded at **118 rue du Faubourg-Saint-Denis**; the investigators raise and cannot prove that she and her mother ran a guesthouse there **[R]**. Their only child, **Wilhelmine Émilie Pauline**, was born at one in the morning, **13 May 1865, 23 quai d'Almont, Melun** **[A: Melun birth register]**.

**War and paperwork.** Registered in the **National Guard** and served consistently in the prison service, certified afterwards by the prefect. Petitioned for domicile under Article 13 of the Napoleonic Code **16 March 1870**, granted **23 May 1870**. Left Melun on leave at his own request **5 March 1873** to study in Germany; rumours that the trip meant dealings with the wartime enemy were investigated and declared unfounded. The **Council of State** ruled him entitled to French citizenship **19 November 1873**, decree published **11 December 1873**. **[A: prefectural reports of 1870 and 1873]**

## 3. Germany, 1873-1876

**Bonn.** Lived by tutoring **the young count Rodrigo Pereira Felício de São Mamede**, later secretary to the king of Portugal, and after 1876 two younger São Mamede sons **[A: study p. 19, career table 3]**. The service brought Portuguese honours: **Knight** of the Order of Christ (*Venloosch weekblad*, 8 August 1874), **Commander** of the same order registered **11 August 1879**, and **Officer of the Military Order of Saint James of the Sword** **[A]**.

**Rhondorf.** Lodged at the **Hôtel Belle Vue** on the Rhine bank; footnote 18 reads it as evidence of comfortable means **[A]**. No property, inheritance or income is documented anywhere: an honest gap.

**Tübingen, 1876.** **Doctor of Philosophy and Master of Liberal Arts**, *bene comprobatam*, under **Adelbert von Keller**; dissertation on the tragedies of **Daniel Casper von Lohenstein**, Paderborn 1877, dedicated to **Alexander Reifferscheid** **[A: the diploma]**.

## 4. Paris, 1876-1883: the chair and the six desiderata

**Neuilly**, **11 boulevard du Château**, from 1876 **[A: study p. 19]**.

**Polytechnique, 1880.** Applied for the chair of German, then on the rue Descartes, and was refused, reportedly over an administrative error about his nationality **[A: study p. 19, appendix A]**. The pool's rider that Kahn dates a second application, to the École Militaire Supérieure, to 1878 is unsupported: no such year, school or application appears anywhere in the study, and **Kahn's *Codebreakers* was not reachable**. It is named as Kahn's in the journey file's confidence field and kept out of the prose.

**HEC.** Chair of German, 108 boulevard Malesherbes, **1 November 1881 to 14 July 1891** **[A: study p. 24, footnote 36]**. Alongside it, German at the **École Arago** on the Place de la Nation, 1881-1883 **[A: CTHS notice]**, and in 1883 the title **Officier d'Académie** **[A]**.

**"La cryptographie militaire."** *Journal des sciences militaires*, n.s. vol. IX: **pp. 5-38, January 1883**; **pp. 161-191, February 1883**; reissued the same year as a 64-page offprint by the Librairie militaire L. Baudoin **[A: study bibliography Ker83 and petitcolas.net, both fetched]**. He was at **62 rue Claude-Bernard** that year, unsuccessfully seeking a normal-school chair **[A]**. Part II works through an authentic intercepted **Havas telegram** on British military affairs in Egypt **[A]**.

**Société d'anthropologie.** Joined **19 July 1883** under the style **Kerckhoffs von Nieuwenhof** **[A: the society's Bulletins et Mémoires, 1885; CTHS]**. The 1883 venue is unconfirmed; the journey file pins the society's rue de l'École-de-Médecine seat and says so.

### The six desiderata, verbatim

From the journal text as transcribed at petitcolas.net **[A]**. The second is what is now called Kerckhoffs's principle.

1. "Le système doit être matériellement, sinon mathématiquement, indéchiffrable."
2. "Il faut qu'il n'exige pas le secret, et qu'il puisse sans inconvénient tomber entre les mains de l'ennemi."
3. "La clef doit pouvoir en être communiquée et retenue sans le secours de notes écrites, et être changée ou modifiée au gré des correspondants."
4. "Il faut qu'il soit applicable à la correspondance télégraphique."
5. "Il faut qu'il soit portatif, et que son maniement ou son fonctionnement n'exige pas le concours de plusieurs personnes."
6. "Enfin, il est nécessaire, vu les circonstances qui en commandent l'application, que le système soit d'un usage facile, ne demandant ni tension d'esprit, ni la connaissance d'une longue série de règles à observer."

### Misattribution, flagged

**"The enemy knows the system being used"** is routinely quoted as Kerckhoffs's. It is not: it is verbatim **Claude Shannon**, "Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems," *BSTJ* 28 (Oct. 1949), p. 662 **[A: Shannon's own text, checked directly]**, and appears nowhere in the 1883 French or in the journey file. "Father of computer security" and "the algorithm must be public" are likewise anachronistic compressions **[R]**: he died before electronic computers, and his second desideratum required only that a system survive its material design falling into enemy hands.

## 5. Volapük, 1884-1892

**Entry, 1884**, after the first congress at Friedrichshafen **[A: Johann Schmidt, *Jenotem Valemapuka Volapük* (1964), quoted in the study]**. **July 1885**: founded the **Association française pour la propagation du Volapük** and published *Volapük ou langue commerciale universelle* **[A: *Le Génie civil*, 11 July 1885]**. **Inaugural lecture** in HEC's grand amphithéâtre, **21 February 1886**, to a crowded house **[A: *Le Correspondant*, 10 March 1886, p. 919]**; the director questioned these lectures on 26 April 1889 **[A: appendix C.3]**.

**La Citoyenne** no. 110, **1 July 1886**, interviewed by André Maguet: he declares himself a strong supporter of women's civil and political rights, praises **Hubertine Auclert**, predicts French women's suffrage **[A]**. Auclert has no journey file in the corpus, so there is nothing to interlock with. A dead end, stated rather than hidden.

**The books.** *Cours complet de Volapük*, 144 pp., Le Soudier, 1886, eight editions; *Dictionnaire volapük-français et français-volapük*, 319 pp., 1887 **[A]**.

**Munich, 6-9 July 1887.** The second congress founded the **International Volapük Academy**, 29 members from 15 nations; **Kerckhoffs was elected its president**, under Schleyer as **datuval**, Grand Master for life **[A: study p. 22 and footnote 5]**.

**Paris, 19-21 August 1889.** He convened the third congress against Schleyer's declared opposition; Volapük served as working language, with the American **Charles E. Sprague** moderating **[A: study; Garvía, *Esperanto and Its Rivals* (2015)]**. Schleyer had already declared the congress and its decisions invalid in *Volapükabled* nos. 105 and 114 **[A: study footnote 60]**.

**Glamat nomik**, the Academy's reform grammar, 1890; Schleyer answered by founding a rival loyalist Academy at **Constance, 18 August 1890** **[A]**, his event at his own place, so it is cited in Kerckhoffs's file rather than staged in it.

**The break, March 1891**: Schleyer removed Kerckhoffs from his list of Volapükists **[A: study p. 23, which states this without a supporting footnote; the confidence field says so]**. **Woldemar Rosenberger** was appointed Academy director **14 December 1892** and Kerckhoffs left both Academy and movement that year **[A]**.

**Unaudited.** A contemporary report claimed the director of the **Grands Magasins du Printemps**, Jules Jaluzot, learned Volapük with 121 employees **[R: flagged in the study as movement propaganda]**. Carried in a campa as a claim, no stop of its own.

## 6. The provincial years, 1891-1900

**HEC ends, 14 July 1891.** He criticized a ministry examiner's grading of a student in the final German examination; the council ended the ten-year collaboration, its letter of 5 August 1891 saying the remark was "interpreted as a criticism by the representative of the Ministry" **[A: appendix C.2]**. Not misconduct. A remark.

**17 rue Vauquelin** during the job-hunting year; **Mont-de-Marsan**, German at the lycée, 1891-1892; **Lorient**, appointed **16 September 1892**, where headmaster reports of March 1893 and March 1895 call his teaching distinguished, conscientious, clear and methodical, and note that at his age he has trouble imposing authority in crowded classrooms. **[A]**

**Pauline's death.** By then **Pauline Mowat**, wife of Alfred Armand Mowat and a certified Volapük teacher, she died at **10 rue des Feuillantines, Paris, 9 January 1893**, aged 27 **[A: Paris death certificate no. 81]**. The cause is **not** stated in the record; cholera in the 1892-1893 epidemic is offered in the literature only as a possibility **[R]**. Left open.

**Ravachol's cauldron.** In March 1894 he anonymously mailed his Lorient colleague **M. Dejean** a novelty explosive, a "chaudron de Ravachol," wrapped in mock-anarchist clippings; Dejean's wife opened it and was frightened by the small blast. The Interior Minister asked Public Education to consider a reprimand on **21 March 1894**; Kerckhoffs admitted sole responsibility in writing; the rector recommended no punishment and criticized the police commissioner's overreaction. **[A: ministry letter and his reply, both reproduced]**

**Picquart.** Signed a published petition protesting Colonel **Georges Picquart**'s persecution, *Le Radical*, **29 November 1898** **[A: study citation; the newspaper page itself was not fetched]**.

**A hole in the record, stated as a hole.** The study's career table is garbled between 1895 and 1900, and its text jumps from the March 1895 report straight to "Kerckhoffs came back to Paris in 1900." The pool carried a sentence saying declining health led him to take renewable leave by 1895; **no such statement exists in the study**, and it has been removed. Five years are undocumented in everything reachable, and the journey file says so in a confidence field.

## 7. Paris again, and Därligen, 1899-1903

**Advertised lectures.** Newspapers advertised weekly Monday public lectures on cryptography associated with the École des Chartes and the Sorbonne, 1899-1900 **[R: the École's archive preserves no parallel institutional file]**. He is recorded in the Société d'anthropologie's account of the **session of 5 April 1900**, and gave free German lectures through the **Société commerciale pour l'étude des langues étrangères** alongside teachers of Spanish, English, Arabic and Russian **[A: Bulletin de la Chambre de commerce de Paris, 1900 and 1901]**. Last address **6 rue Rataud** **[A]**; no pension decision or retirement decree was located, so retirement by 1902-1903 is inference **[R]**.

**Därligen, 9 August 1903.** A guest since **24 July** at the **Hospiz du Lac** on Lake Thun, "Professor Auguste Kerckhoffs from Paris," 68, went down to the **Därligen railway station** on a Sunday evening to greet acquaintances arriving on the train and was struck and fatally injured **[A: *Oberländisches Volksblatt*, Interlaken, 11 August 1903, at study p. 28 and independently at plattformj.ch/artikel/30064/]**. The heavily mutilated body went to the **Interlaken** morgue before transport to Paris **[A]**.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** **Schleyer's diary** records the death as **11 August 1903**; the Swiss death certificate gives **9 August**, and the newspaper of 11 August reports an accident of two days earlier **[A]**. The file carries both.

**Burial, 16 August 1903**, Cimetière du Montparnasse, **division 27, third line north, no. 43 east, perpetual concession no. 141**, bought by his widow the day before **[A: burial certificate]**. The modest stone carries Pauline Mowat, née Kerckhoffs, beside him; her engraved birth date runs two days off the civil register **[A]**.

## 8. Afterlife: other people's journeys, not staged as stops

Cited as sources and suggested refs on his own stops, never as scenes in his file. In **1986** **Haupenthal** asked **Caraco** to find the tomb and recover the archives; Caraco died in 2015 and his collaborators finished in 2020 **[A: p. 2]**. A **1997** photograph shows a white flower pot on the grave, gone by the later visit **[A: p. 30 n. 81]**. On **12 May 2020** the team flowered the grave and notified the Paris cemetery authorities of its significance, so an apparently abandoned concession could not be legally recycled **[A: pp. 29-30]**. **No personal archive survives**: papers bearing his autograph surfaced on the antiquarian market in the late 1990s, read by the study as dispersal through his widow's or daughter's succession **[A: p. 31]**.

Two legends, both false, neither staged: noble descent from the "van den Kerckhove van de Varent" line, invented in a genealogy his great-uncle commissioned around 1839 and marked as fiction by CBG correction sheets **[R for the legend, A for the debunking, pp. 4-7]**; and the claim in *Le XIXe siècle*, 12 September 1887, that he played a forensic cryptographic role in **Vera Zasulich**'s trial **[R: judged implausible by the study]**. The *Cours complet*, not the cryptography, is what was adapted abroad, by Harrison, Pflaumer, Heyligers and Dornbusch, though **none of these names is in the study's bibliography**, which defers to Caraco 1998, **not obtainable in this run** **[R]**.
