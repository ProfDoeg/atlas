# Antonio Mamerto Gil Núñez, called Gauchito Gil: research report
*2026-08-11. Pay Ubre / Mercedes, Corrientes, Argentina. Traditional dates c. 1840s to 8 January 1878.*

**Register decision.** Written under the register *national mythology: the canon is true*. The journey file narrates the legend as the record, without hedging in prose; the hedges are all carried in the `date_confidence` field of the stop they belong to, and every contradiction is recorded here. That distinction matters more here than almost anywhere in the corpus, because for Gauchito Gil there is **no documentary record at all**: no baptismal entry, no muster roll, no police blotter, no death certificate, no grave that can be pointed at. What exists is a devotional tradition of extraordinary vitality and, layered on it, encyclopedic summaries of that tradition. Everything marked [R] below is tradition. The [A] entries are almost entirely about the *cult*, not the man: shrine locations, plaque counts, pilgrim numbers, the Church's posture. That asymmetry is the finding.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested in a named reachable source · **[R]** = reconstruction, oral tradition, or devotional legend. Coordinates to four decimals come from a source; two decimals approximate a described location.

## I. The province before him (context, not biography)

- Corrientes founded 3 April 1588, Hernandarias present as first interim teniente de gobernador. **[A `hernando_arias_de_saavedra.journey.json`; -27.4692, -58.8306]**
- Belgrano's 1810-11 Paraguay expedition passes the Litoral at La Bajada del Paraná. **[A `belgrano.journey.json`; -31.7319, -60.5238]**
- Güemes opens "the gaucho war" in the Valle de Lerma, 1814: irregular mounted militia, red poncho, veneration after death. **[A `martin_miguel_de_guemes.journey.json`; -24.9333, -65.5]**
- Pago Largo, 31 March 1839: federal destruction of the Corrientes army under Berón de Astrada, with the summary field executions typical of the period. **[A `juan_manuel_de_rosas.journey.json`; -29.79, -58.05]**

None of this touches Gil directly. It is here because the legend is unintelligible without it: the montonera, the levy, the throat-cutting of prisoners as ordinary practice, are the furniture of the story.

## II. Birth and the Pay Ubre years

- Born a poor criollo peón at the paraje of Pay Ubre, near (in fact *at*) what becomes Mercedes. **[R es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]**
- **Birth year disputed and unresolved.** The EN infobox reads exactly `birth_date = 1840s, allegedly 1847`: a decade with a hedged year. ES gives only "alrededor de 1840" and carries **no birth date in its infobox at all**. **[A as to what the two articles say]**
- **The precise date 12 August 1847, in wide circulation, is not attributable to either encyclopedia.** It entered the pool sourced to a Wikipedia infobox; it is not there. If retained anywhere it must be sourced to devotional literature, not reachable in text form this pass. **[R, source unlocated]**
- Pay Ubre is the **former name of Mercedes itself** (EN infobox: "Mercedes (formerly Pay Ubre)"), not a separate hamlet. The file therefore pins the birth on the town, -29.20 / -58.083, per the ES coordinate; a pool value of -29.15 / -58.05, about 6.5 km northeast, was discarded as unsourced. **[A on the naming; coordinate corrected]**
- Works as peón and domador on the estancias around Mercedes; known as a horseman; already generous with other men's cattle. **[R oral tradition, devotional pamphlets]**

## III. The comisario and the Triple Alliance war

- The commonest strand gives Gil a love affair with the daughter, or in other tellings the wife, of a local comisario or a landowner, and a resulting vendetta by a man with authority over him. **[R oral tradition in both encyclopedias; the versions differ and no source reconciles them]**
- **Correction carried into the file:** EN says Gil was **conscripted** ("after being conscripted to fight in the War of the Triple Alliance"). The pool had him *enlisting voluntarily to escape the vendetta*, reversing the agency of the cited source. The file follows the source: the comisario procures the levy, and the levy takes him. **[A as to EN wording; R as to the vendetta motive]**
- The war runs 1864-1870. **[A documented history of the war itself, independent of Gil]**
- **Paso de la Patria** was the allied armies' crossing point, at 27°19'02"S 58°34'21"O = **-27.3172, -58.5725** (ES). The pool gave -27.3333 / -58.6167, about 5 km off, and attributed to ES the statement that *Gil* crossed there. **That statement is not in ES.** The crossing is real; his presence at it is an inference imported from general war history. Coordinate corrected, claim demoted. **[A place, R presence]**
- **Gap:** no source places Gil at any named engagement, unit, or regiment. Wartime feats are credited only in the vaguest terms. There is nothing to verify.

## IV. Autonomistas, Liberales, and the desertion

- After the war Gil is recruited into Corrientes's internal conflict. **[R]**
- **Source conflict on the party names, flagged not resolved.** ES gives **Autonomista vs Liberal**, the historically correct pairing for 1870s Corrientes. EN gives **"Federalist Party" vs "Unitarian army"**, labels obsolete by that decade. The pool merged them and sourced the result to EN. The file cites **ES** and drops the Federalist gloss as EN's anachronism. **[A as to what each says; the historical fact not adjudicated]**
- Gil deserts. The legend gives two motives, side by side and unranked: a refusal to go on killing fellow gauchos, and a warning received in a dream against shedding innocent blood. **[R both in en.wikipedia; no source prefers one]**
- Desertion here is a capital matter, and this is the hinge on which the execution turns. **[R framing of the legend, not a citation of any military code]**

## V. The matrero years

- Fugitive; steals cattle from estancieros and gives to the poor. **[R en.wikipedia, oral tradition]**
- Acts as a **curandero** for campesinos with no other medicine, and is credited with foreknowledge. This makes the later miracle legible: he is a healer before he is a martyr. **[R devotional literature, summarized in es.wikipedia]**
- Regional strands range him as far as **Curuzú Cuatiá** (-29.7897, -58.0489) and **Goya** (-29.1362, -59.2661), with long refuges in the monte of **Ensenada Grande** (approx. -29.25, -58.15). **[R regional oral tradition; no document behind any of it]**
- One version has him jailed once and escaping. The jail is Goya in some tellings, Mercedes in others. **Not resolved.** **[R]**

## VI. 8 January 1878

- Captured about **8 km from Mercedes** and executed summarily, without trial. **[R as to event; A as to the distance, en.wikipedia: "about 8 kilometers from the city of Mercedes"]**
- **Coordinate harmonization, the largest single correction this pass.** The pool carried the execution site at three different points: -29.1913 / -58.0508, -29.2 / -57.98, -29.22 / -57.98. The first lies about 3.3 km from Mercedes, contradicting the 8 km figure in its own claim text. The file harmonizes **all** references on **-29.20, -57.98**, roughly 10 km east of town and consistent with the sourced distance. It reconciles a described location; it is not a surveyed point. **[R]**
- Method: hanged upside down from an **espinillo** and killed by a knife to the throat, degollado, the ordinary gaucho-war execution. **[R]** EN says only "hanged upside down from an espinillo tree"; the pool's "by one foot" overspecifies and has been dropped.
- **Death-year contradiction: fabricated, and struck.** Three separate pool entries asserted a 1878 vs 1874 disagreement, one claiming it was "flagged in both" encyclopedias. **Neither EN nor ES mentions 1874.** Both give 8 January 1878 only. The alternative is not carried into the journey file. If it exists it exists in literature not reached here. **[A on what the encyclopedias say]**
- **Last words, corrected.** The pool gave "No me mates, que ya va a llegar la carta de mi indulto." Verification against the raw wikitext (Especial:Exportar/Gauchito_Gil) gives **"No me mates, que ya va a llegar la carta de mi inocencia."** The word *indulto* appears nowhere in the article; a phrase search for "carta de mi indulto" returns nothing. The corrected wording is used. **[A as a quotation of the article; R as an utterance]**
- The comisario answers "Igual no te vas a salvar," and Gil then delivers the prophecy (quoted verbatim in the journey file, stop "The espinillo, the prophecy"). Both lines come from the **same** "Segunda versión" section: one scene, not two competing versions of the last words as the pool had them. **[A as to the article's structure and as quotation; R as utterance]**

## VII. The miracle and the first cross

- The executioner returns to Mercedes to find his son gravely ill, prays in Gil's name as instructed, and the boy recovers. **[R the founding legend, in every devotional account and both encyclopedias]** One pool entry had him return "to find his son alive and recovering, exactly as Gil foretold," deleting the prayer. The prayer is the causal hinge of the cult; without it there is no intercession and no shrine. Restored.
- Shaken, he returns to the body, buries it at the execution site and raises a wooden cross. That cross is the seed of the sanctuary. **[R]**

## VIII. The remains

- ES states plainly that his tomb is in the cemetery of Mercedes ("su tumba se encuentra en el Cementerio de la ciudad de Mercedes"), and adds that the old town cemetery stood where **Plaza Mitre** now is, that remains turned up there during waterworks construction, and that the burials were presumably transferred in the late nineteenth century. **[A as to ES text; R as to Gil being among them]** EN mentions **neither** the cemetery nor Plaza Mitre; the pool attributed both to EN, and the source is corrected to ES. The pool's "empty cenotaph" description of the mausoleum is in neither and is dropped.
- **Gap, stated as such:** no archaeological or documentary confirmation of any burial location exists. The grave cannot be distinguished among the others.

## IX. The sanctuary and the cult

- Red flags and ribbons mark the sanctuary and its imitators, the red popularly explained as the colour of the Partido Autonomista. **[R explanatory tradition]** An alternative folk explanation, that red repels evil or marks blood sacrifice, circulates alongside it. No source adjudicates. **Recorded, not resolved.**
- A permanent complex grows at the execution site: chapels, mausoleum, votive rooms, vendor stalls. **[R as to chronology; no source consulted gives a construction history with dates]**
- The mausoleum walls carry **some 50,000 plaques** of thanks for miracles. **[A en.wikipedia, exact figure]** The pool's claim that the sanctuary "preserves the original espinillo tree" is not in EN and is dropped.
- **Over 250,000 pilgrims a year** (en.wikipedia), **with the largest concentration on 8 January**, the feast day and death anniversary. Two pool entries converted this annual total into a single-day crowd of "hundreds of thousands"; **no source gives a single-day figure**, and the journey file restates it as annual. The pool's claim that the feast "becomes fixed nationally" in 2000 is unsourced and the year anchor is dropped. **[A]**
- A 2019 survey is cited for roughly **23 percent of Argentines** identifying as devotees. **[A en.wikipedia citing survey data; the survey itself was not reached, see Sources]**

## XI. The Church

- Not canonized, not recognized as a saint. Classed as a *santo popular*, explicitly distinguished from cults the Church actively opposes, such as San La Muerte. **[A en.wikipedia]**
- **Bergoglio, corrected to the concrete act.** The pool had a generic claim about "pastoral, non-condemnatory engagement," dated 2005. EN gives something specific and checkable: while still Archbishop of Buenos Aires, Jorge Mario Bergoglio **promoted the publication of a novena to the Cruz Gil**. The 2005 date is unsourced; he held the see 1998-2013, and the journey file anchors the stop at 1998 with the range in `date_confidence`. **[A as to the novena; R as to the year]**
- Ecclesiastical commentary quoted in both editions calls the devotion "A history of freedom, martyrdom, and forgiveness that is penetrating ever more deeply into the hearts of our people, especially among the poor and marginalized." **[A as quotation]** Neither the pool nor this pass established **who** said it or **when**. That attribution is a gap.

## Contradictions flagged, not resolved

1. **Birth year.** EN: "1840s, allegedly 1847". ES: "alrededor de 1840". Neither gives a day or month. The circulating 12 August 1847 is in neither.
2. **The beloved.** Daughter of the comisario, wife of the comisario, or a landowner's woman. Three versions, no ranking.
3. **Party labels.** ES: Autonomista vs Liberal. EN: Federalist vs Unitarian. The ES pairing is historically correct for the 1870s, but the file records both as what the sources say.
4. **Motive for desertion.** Refusal to kill fellow gauchos, or a divine warning in a dream. Both current.
5. **The jail.** Goya in some tellings, Mercedes in others.
6. **Where the remains are.** Old cemetery under Plaza Mitre, transferred to the present Mercedes cemetery, or never located. ES asserts the tomb is in the cemetery and simultaneously that the grave cannot be distinguished.
7. **Why the flags are red.** Autonomista colours, apotropaic, or blood. No adjudication in any source.
8. **Whether he existed at all.** Some historical commentary treats "Gauchito Gil" as a composite of several gaucho deserters executed in Corrientes in the 1870s rather than one man. Devotional sources treat him as a single individual with a biography. Per the house register the journey file narrates the single individual; this note is where the alternative lives.

## Claims struck as unsupported after verification

- 12 August 1847 as a birth date attributed to a Wikipedia infobox. Not there.
- The 1874 death year, and the claim that both encyclopedias flag a 1874/1878 discrepancy. Neither mentions 1874.
- "carta de mi indulto" as the last words. The word is *inocencia*.
- Voluntary enlistment to escape the vendetta. The source says conscripted.
- Gil crossing at Paso de la Patria on ES authority. Not in ES.
- The camino real to Paso de los Libres named by ES as the place of capture. Not in ES; and the pool coordinate, -29.45 / -58.00, lies about 40 km west of that road's axis. The file uses the axis midpoint, about -29.46 / -57.59, marked a reconstruction.
- The sanctuary preserving the original espinillo tree; the mausoleum as an empty cenotaph. Both unsourced.
- "Hundreds of thousands of pilgrims on 8 January." The figure is 250,000 a year.
- The executioner finding his son already recovering. The prayer comes first.
- Feast day "fixed nationally" in 2000, and 2005 as the date of Bergoglio's action. Neither year has a source.
