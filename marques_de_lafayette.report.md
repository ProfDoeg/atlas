# Marquis de Lafayette: research report

*Slug `marques_de_lafayette`. Marie-Joseph Paul Yves Roch Gilbert du Motier, Marquis de Lafayette, 1757-1834. Compiled 2026-08-05 from a five-lens verified pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife) already through an adversarial pass.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition, unconfirmed against a primary or encyclopedic source. **[CORRECTED]** is an error the pass caught, original shown so it stays recoverable. Each phase gives its spine, then its fixes; coordinates sit in section 9.

## 1. Auvergne and the Noailles, 1757-1776

**Spine [A** Auricchio, *The Marquis*; Wikipedia; French Wikipedia, 'Adrienne de La Fayette'**]:** born Château de Chavaniac, 6 September 1757 (45.1572, 3.5792 from geography, chronology giving only the year); father killed at Minden 1759; Musketeer 1771, Noailles Dragoons 1773; marriage to Marie Adrienne Françoise de Noailles, fourteen and a half, at the Hôtel de Noailles, 11 April 1774, corroborated by `madame_de_lafayette.journey.json`; Henriette born December 1775, dead 1778.

- Metz, the Duke of Gloucester dinner, August 1775: **[R** nineteenth-century tradition, e.g. Bardoux, called embellished by modern historians**]**. **[CORRECTED]** Geography gave his age as nineteen; he was **seventeen**.
- **[CORRECTED, my own campa]** The 1779 stop read "bore and buried a daughter while her husband was at Brandywine", compressing Henriette's 1778 death against Anastasie's summer-1777 birth (interlock). Henriette was born December 1775, before he sailed. The campa now reads "buried their first daughter in 1778 while her husband was in America".
- **Gap.** Nothing reachable documents the 1768-1774 Paris education or the Noailles household beyond geography's own **[R]** entry, which admits its address is approximate.

## 2. The crossing, 1776-1777

**Spine [A** Wikipedia; Auricchio; Idzerda, *Lafayette Papers* vol. 1**]:** Deane commissions him major general at Paris 7 December 1776, no address in the pool; London February 1777; the Victoire sails; landing near Georgetown, South Carolina, 13 June 1777.

- **London. [CORRECTED]** Geography said "with his father-in-law". He went with the **Prince de Poix**, hosted by the **Marquis de Noailles**, ambassador and his wife's uncle; the Duc d'Ayen was not there and procured the arrest order weeks later. Dropped: "dines with officers he will soon face", colour not fact.
- **The two departures. [CORRECTED, and the pool contradicts itself.]** Chronology gave one event, "The Victoire departs from Pauillac, 1777-04-26", conflating two. The ship dropped down the Gironde from **Pauillac, 25 March 1777**; Lafayette evaded arrest overland and rejoined her at **Pasaia, Spain**, sailing in late April. **The Spanish date is unsettled: geography 20 April, English Wikipedia 26 April.** The journey takes 20 April, conflict in `date_confidence`.
- **[CORRECTED, tagging]** The paraphrase about the Carolina marshland being beautiful for what it represented was tagged **[A]**; it is **[R]**, and was dropped. The first night with **Major Benjamin Huger** is likewise **[R]**, reachable only because the 1881 pamphlet is by that family.

## 3. Continental service, 1777-1780

**Spine [A** *Journals of the Continental Congress* 1777; Washington's dispatches; Lee court-martial records; Idzerda vols. 1-3**]:** major general 31 July 1777, meets Washington 5 August; wounded at Brandywine 11 September; Valley Forge winter 1777-78; Monmouth 28 June 1778; the Alliance 11 January 1779, with the February 1779 Versailles house arrest and dragoon colonelcy folded in; Georges Washington de La Fayette born December 1779, day approximate; Rochefort March 1780, Boston 27 April, Morristown 10 May.

- **Gloucester, New Jersey. [CORRECTED]** Chronology dated the skirmish 24 November 1777; it was **25 November**, into the early hours of the 26th, Wikipedia's biography article contradicting its own battle article.
- **Rhode Island. [CORRECTED twice.]** Chronology claimed "He engages at the Battle of Rhode Island, 1778-08-09". The battle was **29 August 1778**, 9 August being d'Estaing's Conanicut landing and Sullivan's seizure of Butts Hill, and **Lafayette did not fight in it**: sent to Boston to negotiate with d'Estaing after the fleet withdrew on 22 August, he reached the field as the action closed **[A** Wikipedia, 'Battle of Rhode Island'; Idzerda vol. 2**]**. The journey pins Newport at **1778-08-22**, the Boston errand.

## 4. Virginia and Yorktown, 1781-1783

**Spine [A** Idzerda vol. 4; Cornwallis to Clinton; Washington's siege journal; Auricchio**]:** the Virginia campaign April to June 1781; Green Spring 6 July, two guns abandoned when their horses were shot and roughly 140 Americans lost; the redoubts 14 October; surrender 19 October; Versailles 22 January 1782, no stop.

- "Not strong enough even to get beaten" is a **verbatim fragment, not a sentence**, and is presented as such; the pool gives the letter series, no dateline. **[A** as text, **R** as to the letter**]**.
- **Yorktown, 14 October 1781. [CORRECTED; the pool's most substantive error.]** Chronology said "he leads the storming of Redoubt 9". Both halves fail. **Wrong redoubt:** Lafayette's light infantry took **Redoubt No. 10**; **No. 9** fell the same night to the Royal Deux-Ponts under the Comte de Deux-Ponts **[A** Wikipedia, 'Siege of Yorktown'**]**. **Wrong man:** he commanded the division and gave the order, **Lt. Col. Alexander Hamilton** led the column. Geography compounds it by titling its pin "Redoubt No. 9 line".
- **The Cadiz letter. [CORRECTED: wording, date, provenance.]** The quotes lens carried "Humanity has gained its suit; Liberty will nevermore be without an asylum", assigned to a letter of 1780. The pass grepped all three volumes of the English *Memoirs, Correspondence and Manuscripts of General Lafayette* (Gutenberg #8376; archive.org `memoirscorrespo02unkngoog`, `memoirscorrespo00washgoog`); neither phrase appears. The real passage, unsuperscribed and dated **Cadiz, 1 January 1783**, is **vol. II, p.48**: "humanity has gained her cause, and liberty will never be without a place of refuge."

## 5. Revolution and command, 1789-1792

**Spine [A** Wikipedia; Paris municipal records 1789; Schama, *Citizens*; the Fête proceedings; Assembly records and the 1791 inquiry; interlock**]:** Estates-General 5 May 1789 as a deputy of the nobility; draft Declaration 11 July; Bastille 14 July, Guard command and cockade next day; October days 5-6 October; the Fête 14 July 1790; Varennes June 1791, no stop; the Champ de Mars firing 17 July 1791; resignation October 1791; the Tuileries 10 August 1792.

- The pool does not name his bailliage. **Gap.** Jefferson's help with the draft Declaration is standard in the literature but **[R]** here, unsourced in the lens material; Bonaparte in the Tuileries crowd likewise. No Champ de Mars death toll is fixed in the pool, so the campa says only "dozens".
- **The Bastille key. [CORRECTED]** Geography attached to the 14 July 1789 pin the claim that "Days later Lafayette sends its main key across the Atlantic". The key went in **March 1790**, as that lens's own cited source (Idzerda vol. 5) says; the journey gives it a Mount Vernon stop. Paine as carrier is **[R]**.
- **Metz, 14 December 1791. [CORRECTED, causality reversed.]** Chronology said he took the Army of the Centre "after France declares war on Austria". War was declared **20 April 1792**, four months later.
- **The Jacobin denunciation. [CORRECTED, two events merged.]** Chronology dated to 28 June 1792 "a letter read to the Assembly". The letter was **dated 16 June, read 18 June**; on **28 June** he appeared **in person**, having left his army without leave. The campa keeps both, in order.

## 6. Captivity, 1792-1797

**Spine [A** interlock and both Wikipedias for the capture; Gottschalk for the prisons; the Huger pamphlet for the release**]:** captured in the Austrian Netherlands 19 August 1792; Wesel 1792-93; Magdeburg 1793; Neisse (Nysa) 1794 for the handover to Austria, no stop; Olmütz; release 19 September 1797; Hamburg.

- **The village of capture is not settled:** geography says it "is given differently across accounts, all agreeing only on the border woods", self-tags **[R]**, and its 50.1608, 5.2178 is an approximation.
- **Olmütz, arrival May 1794. [CORRECTED, start year.]** Chronology dated the phase 1795-1797 on a French Wikipedia heading covering the **final** phase only. He was transferred **May 1794**, proved by the escape attempt mounted at Olmütz that November.
- **The escape attempt. [CORRECTED, upgraded from [R] to a dated fact.]** Chronology carried it undated at an "unspecified fortress". It is the **Bollmann-Huger plot of 4 November 1794** (some accounts 8 November), **at Olmütz** **[A** Wikipedia, 'Francis Kinloch Huger'; Huger, *Statement***]**, and its failure produced the harsher confinement of October 1794 to April 1795.
- **Adrienne and Monroe. [CORRECTED, date and cast.]** Chronology put the executions and Elizabeth Monroe's visit all in 1794. Adrienne's **grandmother**, **mother** and **sister** were guillotined **22 July 1794**, the sister missing from the lens claim; **Monroe belongs to January 1795**, since he reached Paris only in August 1794 and Adrienne was released **22 January 1795**. Interlock independently pins the executions and the pit.
- **Adrienne joins the captivity, October 1795: [R].** Chronology says the date "could not be confirmed from the sources reached in this session (widely cited elsewhere as October 1795, but not verified here)"; geography gives 1795-10-15, self-tagged **[R]**, from *Notice sur Madame de Lafayette*. **Unresolved:** Lafayette's own "twelve months later my wife and daughters shared my captivity" **[A** p.45**]** points to November 1795.
- **Release and Hamburg. [CORRECTED, framing and date.]** Chronology credited the release to a condition Napoleon pressed "connected to the Treaty of Campo Formio"; geography dated the Hamburg pin 19 September. **Campo Formio was signed 17 October 1797, a month after the release**, so the framing is anachronistic; the pressure ran through the **Leoben preliminaries**. The 19th is the **release at Olmütz**, the Hamburg handover to the American consul is **4 October**, and he travelled **overland**, so "lands in neutral Hamburg" is wrong.

## 7. La Grange, the Restoration, the tour, 1800-1834

**Spine [A** Lasteyrie and estate records; Levasseur; Webster's oration; Wikipedia; Auricchio and funeral notices**]:** La Grange-Bléneau under Napoleon's amnesty; Adrienne dies December 1807; the 1824-25 tour of all twenty-four states; Yorktown revisited 19 October 1824, no stop; the Bunker Hill cornerstone 17 June 1825; the July Revolution of 1830, declining the dictatorship for Louis-Philippe; death at the rue d'Anjou-Saint-Honoré 20 May 1834, 48.8721, 2.3172 verified.

- **[R]**, standard but unsourced in the lens material: the refusals of Napoleon's senate and the Legion of Honour, the vote against the life consulate, "best of republics", the fatal chill, the guarded funeral. **Humboldt** is **[R]** by interlock's own "no direct Humboldt-La Grange stop is attested", so the campa gives only his 1804 Jefferson dinner.
- **Adrienne's death is contradictory:** French Wikipedia **24 December 1807**, English **25 December**. No stop; the campa says only "dead twenty-six years", true either way. **[CORRECTED]** Geography's "dead eighteen years" is arithmetically wrong; the interval is **twenty-six and a half**.
- **[CORRECTED, Restoration politics]** Chronology said he served in the Chamber of Deputies from 1814. He was **not** in the 1814 Chamber: he sat in the **Chamber of Representatives during the Hundred Days (1815)** and was first elected a Restoration deputy in **October 1818**, for the Sarthe. Wikipedia's lead sentence is a loose summary read as a date. No stop was minted, so it never reached the journey.
- **[CORRECTED, dates]** The lens put the New York arrival at **15 August 1824**: that is Staten Island, the Castle Garden landing being **16 August**. It dated the visit to Washington's tomb **17 August**, "within days of landing": he went **17 October**, reaching Virginia only after New England and Philadelphia, so date and phrase both fail.

## 8. Afterlife

**Spine [A** Wikipedia on Picpus Cemetery, Lafayette Square, the Escadrille, and Lafayette**]:** buried Picpus 22 May 1834 beside Adrienne under Bunker Hill soil; the DAR flag and its Fourth of July renewal; the 1891 Falguière and Mercié monument in Lafayette Square (38.8995, -77.0365), one of four foreign-officer monuments at the corners with Rochambeau, Kościuszko and Steuben; the Escadrille Américaine of April 1916 renamed **Lafayette Escadrille in December 1916** after German protest; Maryland's citizenship grant of 1784; the Metz statue, Pauillac plaza and Pasaia plaque, carried as `suggested_refs`.

- **The flag. [CORRECTED, partially.]** Geography claimed a flag "has flown over the grave without interruption since 1917". The tradition and the renewal are **[A]**; the **1917 start date is not** in any source consulted, and unbroken display through the 1940-44 occupation is a separate legend. The campa claims only the flag and its renewal.
- **"Lafayette, we are here."** Spoken at the grave 4 July 1917 **[A** for the ceremony**]**; **[R, contested]** for the speaker, popularly **Pershing**, generally credited to his aide **Colonel Charles E. Stanton**. Nothing in the pool resolves it; the journey names Stanton and states the contest in `date_confidence` and `quote_source`.
- **[CORRECTED, not equestrian]** The 1891 monument is a **standing** statue with allegorical figures; the equestrian statue in that square is **Clark Mills's Andrew Jackson (1853)**. The rest of the entry is correct.
- **[R]:** the posthumous memoir publication history, unverified by the afterlife lens beyond the 1837 Saunders and Otley edition, and the wave of American places named Lafayette or Fayette, widely cited in toponymy literature but unverified against a consolidated source.

## 10. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Spanish sailing date:** geography 20 April 1777, English Wikipedia 26 April 1777, two lenses of one pool.
2. **Adrienne's death:** French Wikipedia 24 December 1807, English 25 December.
3. **Adrienne's arrival at Olmütz:** October 1795 by tradition, November 1795 by Lafayette's "twelve months later".
4. **Bollmann-Huger date:** 4 November 1794 in most accounts, 8 November in some.
5. **"Lafayette, we are here":** Stanton or Pershing.
6. **The Olmütz pin**, 49.5938, 17.2509 against 49.5955, 17.2517 across two journeys.
