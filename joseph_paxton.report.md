# Sir Joseph Paxton (1803-1865): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `joseph_paxton.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a secondary claim not traced to a document. Contradictions are flagged where they stand; exactly one is closed instead, and that closure is recorded (section 4). Output: **43 stops in 8 segments, 7 quote fields**, register "national mythology: the canon is true," calendar gregorian.

The evidence is lopsided: the public career from 1850 is documented to the day in Hansard and the Gazette, the first twenty years rest on an 1865 obituary, the 1885-1900 DNB, and a tradition Paxton circulated himself.

---

## 1. Bedfordshire and the apprentice years, 1803-1826

**Birth.** "Born at Milton Bryant, near Woburn, Bedfordshire, August 3rd 1803" **[A, Bedfordshire Archives obituary, CRT180/89]**. *Contradiction, left standing:* his Chiswick employment record of 13 November 1823 and the old DNB give **1801** **[A for the record itself]**, the standard explanation being that he overstated his age to get the place **[A as explanation; the intent is inference]**.

*Correction applied.* The dossier claimed "youngest of nine children of William and Ann Paxton," citing Wikipedia, which says only "the seventh son of a farming family". **Dropped, not softened**, and the campa states that the record names neither parents nor a count.

**Woburn free school [A, obituary]**; no year is given anywhere, and the file's 1812 only orders the segment. **Battlesden Park, c. 1818**, garden boy under his brother John at Sir Gregory Page-Turner's seat, returning 1821 and credited with a large lake; **Woodhall Park, c. 1819-1821**, under William Griffin, head gardener to Samuel Smith. Both **[A, DNB]**, both pins wrong in the pool. **Wimbledon, 1823**, in the Duke of Somerset's garden **[R, DNB only]**, cut on stop-count rather than evidentiary grounds, and restorable.

**Chiswick, 13 November 1823.** Entered the Horticultural Society's experimental garden as a labourer, the entry record carrying an age two years too great **[A]**; foreman of the arboretum by 1824, eighteen shillings a week, considering emigration to America **[A, DNB]**. The sixth Duke of Devonshire noticing him there is in the obituary **[A in outline]**, but no dated first conversation survives **[gap]**.

## 2. Chatsworth, 1826-1849

**Appointment 9 May 1826**, aged twenty-two, ~£70 a year and a cottage; **marriage to Sarah Bown, 1827**, the housekeeper's niece, folded into the first-morning stop; **fellowships** of the Horticultural Society 1826 and the Linnean 1833. All **[A, obituary; DNB]**.

**The first morning [R].** Climbing the kitchen-garden wall before dawn, touring the ground, setting the men to work, breakfasting and meeting Sarah Bown, all before nine. Paxton told versions of it himself; hour, sequence and dialogue are undocumented.

**Publishing.** *Horticultural Register* with Joseph Harrison, 1831; *Paxton's Magazine of Botany*, 1834; *Gardeners' Chronicle*, 1841, with Lindley, Bradbury and Dilke. All **[A as reported]**, no title page or founding document inspected. **Pinetum:** *date corrected*, the dossier said 1830-1831, Wikipedia gives "Pinetum established in 1829 using an additional 8 acres of the park" → file **1829**. **Cavendish banana, 1835-1836:** from Mauritius, reportedly £10, flowered November 1835, 100+ fruits by May 1836, named for the family, HS award **[A in outline, Chatsworth; price and count as reported]**. He neither bred nor found it.

**Great Conservatory, 1836-1840.** ~84 x 37 x 19 m, largest glass building in England, eight underground boilers, seven miles of pipe, carriages two abreast; Decimus Burton consulted on the profile, so authorship is collaborative and the campa says so **[A, Chatsworth]**. Forty miles of sash-bar cut by a machine of Paxton's invention **[A, 1865 obituary]**. *Transcription flag:* the transcript reads "were made **be** a machine of his own invention," a slip for "made by"; the file quotes it **unaltered** and explains it in `quote_source`. **Edensor rebuilt 1839-1841** to Paxton's scheme, houses by John Robertson and others **[A, DNB]**; **Emperor Fountain 1843-1844**, for a visit by Tsar Nicholas I that never happened, gravity-fed, advertised as the world's highest, with the Order of St Vladimir following **[A]**.

**Conservative Wall.** *Date corrected by ten years:* the dossier said 1838, Wikipedia gives 1848, "a glass house 331 ft long by 7 ft wide." This reverses the chronology, putting the wall **after** the Great Conservatory. Folded into the 1849 waterlily stop.

**Victoria regia, November 1849**, first English flowering; an image published about then shows his young daughter on a floating leaf **[A]**. *Legend, flagged not resolved:* that the leaf's ribs gave him the Crystal Palace is **[R]**, and the debunking, that ridge-and-furrow glazing predates the flowering, fits the corrected 1848 wall date but rests on the dossier's synthesis, no named source for it being fetchable. The campa cites the 1848 wall.

**1838 continental tour**, ~7 months with the Duke: Switzerland, Italy, Greece, Ottoman Asia Minor, Malta, Spain, Portugal **[A in outline]**. ***The largest gap in the geography:*** no stop-by-stop itinerary survives anywhere reachable, so the whole tour gets one flagged stop at an Alpine waypoint (45.8326, 6.8652).

## 4. Hyde Park, 1850-1851

**11 June 1850, Derby.** Sketch on pink blotting paper during a Midland Railway board meeting **[A for date and episode]**; the precise room and circumstances **[R]**. *Object flag:* the sheet usually exhibited as the sketch is catalogued by the V&A as a **facsimile**, E.941-1983, per Art UK; neither page loaded, both **HTTP 403**, so the designation is unverified. The **29 June** presentation and **15 July** acceptance **[R]** fold into the same stop.

**13 November 1850**, Society of Arts lecture "The Industrial Palace in Hyde-Park" **[A, Royal Commission catalogue; Victorian Web transcript]**, the printed text possibly normalised rather than verbatim.

**Construction and opening [A, V&A]:** length, transept width, pane count (~293,655), tonnage of iron, daily workforce and late-January completion; opening 1 May 1851 at nine a.m. to season-ticket holders, over six million admissions.

**Closing date, contradiction traced and closed.** The dossier left 11 vs 15 October open. It is not a genuine discrepancy: every reachable source gives **15 October 1851** (Wikipedia, "Great Exhibition" and "The Crystal Palace"), while 11 October was attributed to `vam.ac.uk/articles/the-great-exhibition`, **which returns HTTP 404 and supports nothing**. 11 October was the last public day, 15 October the formal ceremony. The file dates the stop 1851-10-15 and records the closure. *The one resolved contradiction here, resolved because one side has no source behind it.*

**Knighthood, place corrected.** The dossier said Buckingham Palace. Primary source: **The London Gazette, issue 21257, 28 October 1851, p. 2812, dateline WINDSOR CASTLE** (repeated in the Edinburgh Gazette, issue 6121, 31 October), which also gives his London address as **36 Gloucester Place, Portman Square**. The file dates the stop 1851-10-28 and notes that this is the notice date **[gap: the day of conferral is not established]**.

## 5. Sydenham, 1852-1865

**1852.** Crystal Palace Company formed, Hyde Park components bought for ~£70,000, Penge Place on Sydenham Hill acquired, Paxton a director and chief designer **[A, Crystal Palace Foundation]**. **10 June 1854**, the enlarged Palace opens, with Owen Jones and Matthew Digby Wyatt on decoration, Richard Owen on prehistoric interpretation, Hawkins on the thirty-three dinosaurs and Brunel on the water towers **[A]**.

**Rockhills**, principal later residence and place of death **[A, Oxford DNB summary; paywalled]**; coordinates approximate, near the historic site on Crystal Palace Parade. ***Correction, biographically load-bearing.*** The dossier said he lived at Rockhills "while retaining his lifelong Chatsworth appointment." **False.** Wikipedia: "He retired from Chatsworth when the Duke died in 1858." The post ended seven years before his death; Sarah remained connected with the estate until 1871, the probable source of the confusion.

## 6. Parliament, 1854-1864

**Elected unopposed for Coventry, 2 December 1854**, returned 1857 and 1859, holding the seat until death **[A]**.

**5 March 1855, Ordnance Estimates (Supply).** The huts sentence in the file was checked against Hansard and matches exactly, the initial "not" capitalised. *Two flags.* (i) Hansard's label is **SIR JOSEPH PAXTON**, not "Mr. Paxton"; he had been knighted four years earlier, and the dossier note was wrong. (ii) "do as little as they could avoid out of their own offices" is verbatim in the same debate, but the dossier placed it in the **Army Works Corps** debate. It is the Ordnance Estimates, on public works and the Aldershot barracks. The campa paraphrases it; the quote field holds the huts sentence.

*Hansard style.* Nineteenth-century Hansard reports in third-person indirect narration, so every Hansard quote in the file sits inside reported speech rather than first-person address. Stated in each `date_confidence`.

**June 1855, Great Victorian Way**, a glass-roofed loop of ten or eleven miles round central London, before a select committee, never built **[A]**. The description quoted in the file is from the **1865 obituary**, not Paxton's submission.

**18 July 1859, misattribution corrected.** Paxton did attack the Kew glasshouse cost, arguing an acre could be roofed for £10,000 and half the £25,000 asked would suffice (quoted verbatim in the file). But **the geraniums were not his**: the 40,000 potted at public expense were raised later in the same debate by **Mr Kinnaird**, and the file names him.

**30 June 1864, "Observations":** last identified contribution, on the Coventry ribbon trade and the French commercial treaty **[A]**.

## 7. Death and afterlife

**Death at Rockhills, 8 June 1865, aged 61 [A, obituary].** The collapse earlier that year taken for a heart attack, the Gladstones among his last visitors, and probate at just under £180,000 are **[A as reported by the Oxford DNB summary; paywalled]**.

**Burial, St Peter's churchyard, Edensor.** Place **[A, Wikipedia: "buried on the Chatsworth Estate in St Peter's Churchyard, Edensor"]**. **Date [R]:** 15 June 1865 appears in no reachable source and rests on the paywalled ODNB; the file says exactly that. The pool's coordinate was wrong twice over, one of the two pins being Chatsworth House itself. **St Peter's was rebuilt by George Gilbert Scott in 1867 [A]**, so the church over the grave postdates the burial by two years.

**Memorials.** Goddard's memorial, 1868, London Road Cemetery **[A]**; Woodington's bust, 1873, Crystal Palace Park **[A]**. *Wikipedia's cemetery article calls Goddard an architect, its Paxton article a sculptor; unresolved, and the file says only "the work of Joseph Goddard."* The dossier pinned the memorial to the city centre, contradicting its own text.

**Great Conservatory demolished 1920** after wartime coal shortages killed the collection; walls kept, maze planted in the footprint 1962 **[A, Chatsworth]**. **Sydenham Palace burned 30 November 1936 [A]:** crowd estimated at 100,000, Churchill reported saying "This is the end of an age," two water towers surviving, the north tower demolished 1941. Cause never conclusively established, an accidental cloakroom fire being the standard account. The 1866 north transept fire is attested but has no stop. *Verified but unused:* the lapsed 2013 ZhongRong proposal, and the park regeneration begun May 2025, which the dossier dated to "late summer 2026" where Wikipedia says "largely completed by 2027." *Open thread:* the claim that the 1838 dahlia treatise had French, German and Swedish editions is **[R]** and unverifiable.

## 8. Interlocks

Two atlas travellers are named in a campa, on a real relation, not shared geography:

1. **`frederick_law_olmsted`**, the strongest link in the corpus. Olmsted's file names Paxton as Birkenhead Park's designer and states that his 3 May 1850 visit shaped his thinking toward Central Park: a direct one-directional influence, inside Paxton's lifetime. Named in the Birkenhead campa as an influence, **not staged as a scene**; the visit stays on Olmsted's own file and appears here only in `suggested_refs` and `sources`.
2. **`charles_dickens`**, Dickens's file attests the *Daily News* founding of 21 January 1846 and his brief editorship; Paxton was an original proprietor and major investor in the same paper. **Tagged [R]:** the proprietorship comes from outside the atlas record.

**Examined and rejected as shared geography only:** `ada_lovelace`, `charles_babbage`, `augustus_henry_julian_le_plongeon` and `blavatsky` all stage a visit to the Great Exhibition inside Paxton's building, and attending a building is not a relation to its architect; Babbage is the closest call, but *The Exposition of 1851* attacks English science rather than Paxton. `dante_gabriel_rossetti` is a different Hyde Park Corner building, `joseph_marie_jacquard` and `marie_taglioni` connect only through Prince Albert, and `eva_peron`'s "Crystal Palace cinema" is a namesake echo.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Crystal Palace / Hyde Park **51.5073, -0.1657** (carried by four other files; the pool's 51.5045, -0.1682 was not used) and Birkenhead Park **53.3856, -3.0166**.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Joseph Paxton is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** No apparatus stops are licensed and none were written. Two things were caught rather than deleted:

1. **Backward direction, not applicable.** Paxton has no theoretical framework in the exception file's sense, only a construction method that could tempt a reading backward onto earlier glasshouses or forward onto curtain-wall construction. **Nothing of the kind was written**; that would need a new `EXCEPTIONS.md` entry.

2. **Forward direction, caught and confined.** The file reaches past his death in five places: St Peter's rebuilt over his grave in 1867, the 1868 Coventry memorial, the 1873 Crystal Palace Park bust, the 1920 demolition of the Great Conservatory, the 1936 Sydenham fire. All five concern **monuments to Paxton or the fate of buildings he made**, the move `abdelkader.journey.json` makes when its burial stop carries the 1966 repatriation, and none points at another subject's file or names a later traveller. **Flagged so the operator can rule**, since a strict reading of the block rule puts the 1936 fire outside Paxton's clock. If the ruling goes against, the fire and the 1873 bust are the two to cut.

Nothing was silently deleted.
