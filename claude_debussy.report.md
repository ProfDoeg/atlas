# Claude Debussy (1862-1918): research report
*Written 2026-08-23 from the verified research pool for slug `claude_debussy`. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or an attribution traced only to a compilation. Contradictions are flagged and left standing, not adjudicated.*

Debussy's life is a Paris life with few departures: a childhood evacuation to the Riviera, three summers as a rich widow's household pianist, twenty-six months in Rome he resented, two Wagner festivals, two English seaside stays, one London season, one Russian tour, one Basque coast farewell. The work of this pass was less finding geography than repairing it: the pool arrived with eleven coordinate errors, several 1-2 km wide.

## 1. Saint-Germain-en-Laye and the crockery shop (1862-1872)

**[A]** Born Achille-Claude Debussy, 22 August 1862, at 38 rue au Pain, above his parents' china and crockery shop (en.wikipedia, 'Claude Debussy'; fr.wikipedia, 'Musée Claude-Debussy'). Petit-bourgeois; the noble particle later attached to the name by admirers is not in the record. **[A]** 1870: sent to his paternal aunt Clémentine Debussy in Cannes during the Franco-Prussian War, where the violinist Jean Cerutti gives him his first piano lessons (*gap:* house not identified, pin is the town centre). **[A]** 1871: his father Manuel-Achille serves the Paris Commune, is arrested and imprisoned after its suppression, and in prison meets Charles de Sivry, who introduces the boy to Antoinette Mauté de Fleurville. **[A]** Admitted to the Conservatoire de Paris, 22 October 1872, aged ten: Marmontel for piano, Lavignac for solfège, Durand for harmony, Guiraud for composition.

*Contradiction, resolved against the pool's own geography entry,* which dated the rue Saint-Honoré apartment "1864-1871": the family reaches Paris in 1864 but en.wikipedia has them lodging first in Clichy with the grandmother, taking their own rooms only **from 1868**. The journey uses 1868.

*Gaps:* no month for the arrest, no prison named. The widely repeated claim that Mauté de Fleurville studied with Chopin is **not in the pool** and is not in the file.

## 2. Other people's houses (1877-1884)

**[A]** 1877: second prize in piano at the Conservatoire, the high-water mark of his prospects as a virtuoso (*gap:* no month; defaulted to January and declared as such). **[A]** Summer 1879: resident pianist at the Château de Chenonceau.

**[A]** 1880-1882: household pianist and music tutor to Nadezhda von Meck, traveling with the family through France, Switzerland, Italy and Russia. *Contradiction, resolved:* the pool's chronology said "the following two summers" after 1880; en.wikipedia gives **1880, 1881 and 1882**, as does the pool's own geography entry.

**[A]** Von Meck forwards his *Danse bohémienne* to Tchaikovsky, who calls it attractive but too short and formally underdeveloped. The sharpest interlock in the file: von Meck is paying Tchaikovsky on the strict condition that the two never meet, and simultaneously feeding a teenage employee at her own table (`tchaikovsky.journey.json`). *Cut for space:* the interlock also names Florence and Venice among the von Meck stops; neither summer is datable.

**[A]** 1880: accompanist to Marie Moreau-Sainti's singing class; meets Marie-Blanche Vasnier, for whom he writes roughly two dozen songs. **[A]** 27 June 1884: Grand Prix de Rome for the cantata *L'Enfant prodigue*, after failing in 1882 and taking second prize in 1883.

## 3. Rome, Bayreuth, and the Champ de Mars (1885-1889)

**[A]** January 1885 to March 1887: Prix de Rome residency at the Villa Medici, with returns to Paris. He hears Liszt play in the building. Envois include *Zuleima*, *Printemps* and *La Damoiselle élue*; the Institut's report on *Printemps* warns against a vague "impressionism," one documentary root of the label later fixed to him. **[A]** 1887: hears *Tristan und Isolde* at the Concerts Lamoureux. **[A]** 1888 and 1889: attends Bayreuth for *Die Meistersinger*, *Tristan* and *Parsifal*; he later calls *Tristan* a supreme achievement while defining his own modernity by escaping Wagner's procedures. One-directional relation to `richard_wagner.journey.json` (Wagner d. 1883).

**[A]** 1889, Exposition Universelle, Champ de Mars: hears Javanese gamelan and Russian repertory conducted by Rimsky-Korsakov. **[R] on the influence claim:** the pool's own lens warns that "Debussy copied gamelan" is too crude, and that the encounter joins Russian, medieval and Symbolist currents already working on him rather than inventing his style. The journey states what he heard and refuses the causal claim. Eiffel, Merson and Isadora Duncan carry the same fairground pin; shared geography is not a relation and none of them is named in a campa.

## 4. Montmartre, rue de Rome, and the private life (1890-1893)

*Contradiction, left standing.* The pool's chronology and en.wikipedia put the meeting with Satie in 1890 ("later in 1890"); `erik_satie.journey.json` places it at the Auberge du Clou, avenue Trudaine, **around 1891**. The journey uses the Satie file's date and pin and says in `date_confidence` that the two disagree.

*Contradiction, resolved.* The pool's chronology bundled "ends his relationship with Marie Vasnier and begins living with Gabrielle Dupont" into 1890. en.wikipedia splits them: the Vasnier liaison ends and the Dupont relationship begins in 1890, but cohabitation only **from July 1893**.

**[A]** Early 1890s: attends Mallarmé's Tuesday gatherings on rue de Rome. *Gap:* street number not reconfirmed.

**[A, through the corpus]** Debussy had an intimate relationship with Camille Claudel in the early 1890s; on its end he wrote *I weep for the disappearance of the Dream of this Dream.* The wording is attested through `camille_claudel.journey.json`, citing Victor I. Seroff, *Debussy: Musician of France* (1970), p. 152. **The printed volume was not consulted**; the quote is carried as the corpus's. Claudel's file puts the end of the Rodin liaison at the Paris centre pin in 1892, where the Debussy stop also sits.

## 5. Pelléas: nine years on one opera (1893-1902)

**[A]** May 1893: attends the premiere of Maeterlinck's play and shortly after secures permission to set it. *Gap:* the pool names no theatre and gives no day. **[A]** 1894: engaged to the singer Thérèse Roger while still living with Gaby Dupont. Anonymous letters alleging debts and misconduct reach the Roger family, the engagement collapses, Ernest Chausson breaks with him. The pool tags this **[A]** but sources it to a cross-check of Lockspeiser and Nichols rather than a fetched page: biography-attested, not document-attested.

*Quote citation corrected.* The pool cited the iron-grey letter as "Letter to Ernest Chausson, 3 September 1894, in *Debussy Letters*, ed. Lesure & Nichols (1987)." Verification found the **wording confirmed verbatim on Wikiquote** and the **citation unsupported**: Wikiquote gives only "Letter to Ernest Chausson (1894)," no editor, page or day, and the volume could not be checked. The journey cites Wikiquote and drops the date. Same caveat for the Dukas, Bardac and Lerolle quotes.

**[A]** 22 December 1894: *Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune* premieres at a Société Nationale de Musique concert under Gustave Doret, Georges Barrère on flute. Mallarmé approves it. **This is a concert work, not a ballet score**, and the pool insists because eighteen years later the confusion becomes load-bearing. *Gap:* the hall was not confirmed; the pool's 48.8683/2.3417 is its own admitted approximation of period venues, published with that hedge. **[A]** 17 August 1895: short score finished. **[A]** 1898: the Opéra-Comique accepts it, orchestration still unfinished. **[A]** 19 October 1899: marries Marie-Rosalie "Lilly" Texier.

**[A]** 13 April 1902: Maeterlinck, angered that Georgette Leblanc was passed over for Mary Garden, publishes an open letter in *Le Figaro* calling the opera strange and hostile to him and wishing it a failure. **[A]** 28 April: the dress rehearsal is disrupted by a parody handbill and by laughter at Garden's Scottish-accented French. **[A]** 30 April: premiere under André Messager, Jean Périer as Pelléas, Garden as Mélisande; Messager's verdict is that it was certainly not a triumph but no longer the disaster of two days before. *Interlock, corpus-attested:* `erik_satie.journey.json` has Satie in the house that night, feeling Debussy had taken an idea he had "sketched first," and re-enrolling at the Conservatoire three years later out of anxiety at Debussy's success.

## 6. The sea, and the woman with the bullet (1904-1905)

**[A]** 15 July 1904: leaves with Emma Bardac for Jersey and the Normandy coast, having sent Lilly to her family. **[A]** 11 August 1904: writes from Dieppe the letter ending the marriage, without at first naming Emma. **[A]** 14 October 1904: Lilly Texier shoots herself in the chest in Paris. She survives; the bullet stays lodged near her spine. Ravel raises money for her; Dukas and Messager break with Debussy. *Location refused:* the pool pins this at Place de la Concorde and **its own note tags the entry [R]**, the street location being inconsistently reported. The same 48.8656/2.3212 was also copied onto the rue Saint-Honoré entry, so the coordinate was floating loose in the source material.

**[A]** 24 July to 30 August 1905: Eastbourne, Grand Hotel, Suite 200 (since renamed the Debussy Suite), correcting the proofs of *La Mer* while Emma is pregnant; the divorce becomes final on 2 August, mid-stay. **[A]** 15 October 1905: *La Mer* premieres, Orchestre Lamoureux under Camille Chevillard, mixed reviews, Pierre Lalo writing that he neither sees nor feels the sea in the score. *Venue refused:* the pool named the Salle du Nouveau-Théâtre, then found no source placing the Lamoureux concerts there in 1905, and found its own 48.8747/2.299 near Parc Monceau, 2.5 km from that theatre's site at 15 rue Blanche.

**[A]** 30 October 1905: Claude-Emma "Chouchou" born. **[A]** 1908: marries Emma Bardac. *Address contradiction, resolved in favour of the French source:* en.wikipedia gives the last household only as a house off the avenue du Bois-de-Boulogne, number 80, while fr.wikipedia 'Square de l'Avenue-Foch' states the street was named square du Bois-de-Boulogne in 1890, renamed square de l'Avenue-Foch by decree on 24 November 1961, and that "Claude Debussy logea dans cette maison de 1905 à sa mort, en 1918" at **no. 24**.

## 7. The Châtelet years (1909-1913)

**[A]** April 1909: conducts the *Prélude* and the *Nocturnes* at Queen's Hall, Langham Place. **[A]** May 1909: attends the first London *Pelléas* at Covent Garden. **[A]** 1909: diagnosed with the cancer that kills him; *contradiction left standing,* accounts differ between colon and rectal.

**[A, corpus]** 22 May 1911: *Le Martyre de saint Sébastien* premieres at the Théâtre du Châtelet, d'Annunzio's text, Bakst's décor, Ida Rubinstein in the title role, Debussy composing under deadline pressure with André Caplet assisting the orchestration. The Archbishop of Paris forbids Catholics to attend; Rome puts d'Annunzio on the Index. **The pool's verifier could not reconfirm the Châtelet as the venue**; that flag is repeated in the file.

**[A]** 29 May 1912: Nijinsky's *L'Après-midi d'un faune*, on Debussy's 1894 concert score, Bakst designing, Monteux conducting, at the Châtelet. Gaston Calmette in *Le Figaro* calls it filthy; Rodin defends it in print and nearly loses his lease on the Hôtel Biron. Debussy, who licensed the music and did not write it for dancing, calls the choreography ugly.

*The corpus carries the Châtelet at two pins*, 2.347 (Nijinsky, Diaghilev, d'Annunzio) and 2.3472 (Rodin); both are inherited as found rather than harmonized. **[A]** 1912: Diaghilev commissions *Jeux*, written expressly for the Ballets Russes. Debussy's first answer survives as a telegram: *Subject ballet Jeux idiotic, not interested.* *Gap:* the article carrying it (classical-music.com, "The story of Debussy's Jeux") gives no date for the telegram.

**[A]** 15 May 1913: *Jeux* premieres at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées, Nijinsky choreographing and dancing with Karsavina and Schollar, Monteux conducting. Reception muted; two weeks later *Le Sacre du printemps* takes the same house. *Contradiction left standing:* some sources give March rather than May 1913. Debussy's word for the *Sacre* is *beautiful nightmare*; for Nijinsky's handling of his rhythms, to Gabriel Pierné, that the choreographer trampled them underfoot like weed.

## 8. Musicien français (1913-1919)

**[A]** November 1913: Saint Petersburg for concerts, staying at the Grand Hotel Europe on Nevsky Prospekt (fr.wikipedia). **[A]** 1914: the *Berceuse héroïque*, described by him as melancholy and discreet, with no pretensions other than to offer a homage to so much patient suffering. **[A]** December 1915: major cancer surgery, often called one of the earliest colostomies; **the pool relays that without a surgical source**. **[R]** He signs himself "Claude Debussy, musicien français" on wartime scores: widely repeated, no manuscript image verified.

**[A] fact, [R] place.** 14 September 1917: last public performance, the Violin Sonata with Gaston Poulet. The pool's chronology said Paris; its own verification rejected that, noting the sonata's Paris premiere was 5 May 1917 at the Salle Gaveau, that fr.wikipedia records the Debussys on the Basque coast that September, and that standard biographies locate the farewell at **Saint-Jean-de-Luz**. The pool's instruction was explicit: do not publish "Paris." The journey uses Saint-Jean-de-Luz (43.3888, -1.6626) and states that the place rests on biography, not on a document seen.

**[A]** 25 March 1918: dies at home under the German long-range gun's bombardment. **[A]** 28 March: provisional burial at Père-Lachaise after a small funeral across a nearly deserted, shelled Paris (*gap:* division and plot not identified). **[A]** 1919: reinterred at Passy, 14th division. **[A]** 14 July 1919: Chouchou dies of diphtheria and is buried in the same grave; Emma follows in 1934.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Debussy is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing was deleted; the relations below are recorded so the operator can decide. **Direction caught: forward, in every case.** No backward-apparatus relation was found, so no listing question arises on that side. The forward material found in the pool and kept out of the journey file:

1. **d'Annunzio invoking Debussy in 1924** (`charlotte_bara.journey.json`), naming Debussy and Ida Rubinstein as his prior model of a total sacred artwork when titling Charlotte Bara's dances. Debussy died in 1918; d'Annunzio's and Bara's material, already on their files.
2. **Satie's 1920 *Élégie***, in the *Quatre petites mélodies*, dedicated to Debussy's memory (`erik_satie.journey.json`). Satie's stop, on Satie's file.
3. **Jerome Robbins's *Afternoon of a Faun***. *Date error in the pool, corrected and not carried forward:* the pool said 1958, from a stray line in en.wikipedia 'Prelude to the Afternoon of a Faun'; the dedicated article gives **14 May 1953**, New York City Ballet.
4. **Namings and posthumous editions**: the Debussy crater on Mercury (85 km, rayed, Turms Planitia, in MESSENGER's first photograph from Mercury orbit, 2011); asteroid 4492 Debussy (found 1988, named 1990, binary 2004); the birthplace museum (monument 1972, opened 1990, closed 2021, reopened July 2025 with Macron attending), which enters the journey only as a `suggested_ref` on the birth stop; *Monsieur Croche antidilettante*, Paris 1921, never authorized in final form **[R]**; the Durand *Œuvres complètes*, begun 1985 **[R, unverified]**.

## Gaps and leads

- **Mallarmé and Maeterlinck have no journey files.** Both are first-order Debussy relations: the *Faune* poet who approved the prelude, the *Pelléas* playwright who publicly wished the opera dead. The largest structural gap around this subject.
- **Stravinsky's file carries no Debussy material**, zero grep hits, despite the mutual admiration, the dedication of *Le Roi des étoiles*, the joint piano reading of the *Rite*, and "beautiful nightmare." A gap on that file.
- **The Fauré / Emma Bardac / Debussy triangle.** Emma Bardac was Fauré's muse and lover before her affair with Debussy. Fauré has no standalone file, appearing only inside `jaques_dalcroze` and `nadia_boulanger`, neither of which mentions Debussy.
- **Two unresolved venues**, hedged in `date_confidence`: the Société Nationale hall for the 1894 *Faune*, the Châtelet for the 1911 *Saint Sébastien*.
- **Kaaba and Temple Mount** are in the interlock array only because the lens was told to list canonical geography. No Debussy connection; neither pin is in the file.
