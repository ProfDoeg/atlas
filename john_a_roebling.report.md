# John A. Roebling (1806-1869): research report

*2026-08-21, for `john_a_roebling.journey.json`. 44 stops, 8 segments. Register: national mythology, the canon is true.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim this pass could not carry to a source. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated, including the several where the dossier's own two lenses (chronology, geography) contradicted each other.

**One warning governs everything below.** The dossier attributed facts to `nps.gov/people/john-a-roebling.htm` that are not on that page: Erfurt, Unger, the *August Eduard*, the 75-thaler fare, the Philadelphia arrival date, the naturalization date. The real source for most of that is **immigrantentrepreneurship.org/entries/john-augustus-roebling**, and the file re-attributes accordingly. Coordinate corrections have their own section.

## 1. Thuringia and Prussia, 1806-1831

**Birth** 12 June 1806, Mühlhausen, Kingdom of Prussia; baptized at Divi Blasii. **[A: en.wikipedia; Deutsche Biographie gnd117533947; baptism per nps.gov]** That the church's Gothic windows shaped the Brooklyn Bridge towers is **[R]**, a German-Wikipedia tradition with no contemporary support, and the file carries it as something said about him, never by him. The birth-house memorial plaque *is* confirmable (de.wikipedia), so the dossier's hedge on it was dropped.

**Erfurt.** Entered Ephraim Salomon Unger's mathematical institute **June 1820** at fifteen, left 1823 after his first examination, qualified Feldmesser. **[A: immigrantentrepreneurship.org, quoting his own "passed my 1st Examination & returned to Mühlhausen in 1823"]** *Dossier-internal contradiction:* chronology said "around 1821," geography 1820-06. Geography is right; 1821 cannot be squared with the three-year span he describes. *Gap:* the pin is the Erfurt centroid.

**Berlin, Bauakademie**, 1824, under Rabe, Dietlein and Eytelwein, about two semesters **[A: en.wikipedia; immigrantentrepreneurship.org]**. *Contradiction flagged and partly disarmed:* the chronology lens offered an even-weighted alternative, "Berlin-Charlottenburg Polytechnic, completed 1826." That is untenable, the Technische Hochschule Charlottenburg not being founded until 1879, so the stop records it as a later back-projection, not an open question. What stays open is when his Berlin studies ended. *Gap:* the pin marks Schinkel's building, finished 1836.

**Hegel.** Attended his lectures during the Berlin years **[A: en.wikipedia; immigrantentrepreneurship.org; cross-checked against `hegel.journey.json`, whose Berlin chair runs Oct 1818 to Nov 1831]**. The one full interlock in the file: he heard Hegel as a student and argued with him on paper for forty-five years. *Pin note:* the interlock lens supplied 52.5170365, 13.3888599, which is **not** what `hegel.journey.json` uses; its canonical lecture-hall pin is **52.517, 13.3935**, and that is what was inherited. *Gap:* no term or date is attested, and 1824-10-01 only orders the stop.

**Westphalia**, Prussian service in the Arnsberg district from about 1825, four years of military-road work around Eslohe, plus unbuilt Ruhr and Lenne bridge sketches. **Mühlhausen, 1829**, a thesis prepared for the second state examination and never sat, and the Etzler colony scheme. **[A: immigrantentrepreneurship.org]** That police surveillance *personally* drove him out is **[R]**, nowhere in the campa; only the general tightening of censorship after 1830 is.

## 2. The crossing and the colony, 1831-1837

**Bremen, 11 May 1831**, aboard the *August Eduard*, fare 75 thalers (about $58), six weeks expected and eleven taken **[A: immigrantentrepreneurship.org]**. English Wikipedia's 22 May is his leaving Prussian territory, a different act, not a conflict. *Error corrected:* the chronology lens gave "Bremen, Prussia." Bremen was a sovereign Free Hanseatic City, never Prussian.

**Philadelphia landing: 3 August 1831** per immigrantentrepreneurship.org, **6 August** per the Rutgers finding aid. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the institution holding his papers and the scholarly biography disagree about the day he entered the country.

**Saxonburg.** 1,582 acres from Mrs. Sarah Collins, **28 October 1831**, laid out as Germania, renamed Saxonburg, founded 1832 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Marriage** to Johanna Herting May 1836 and **Washington Augustus Roebling born 26 May 1837** **[A: Rutgers finding aid]**. **Carl Röbling died 1837** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Naturalization 30 September 1837**, name anglicized in the same act **[A: de.wikipedia]**. The chronology lens cited this to NPS, which carries no naturalization date at all: the date survives, the attribution did not. *Gap:* the court of record is unknown, so the stop sits at the Saxonburg pin. The dossier's naturalization *scene* and its Fairmount competition lost to patronage are **[R]**; Fairmount is kept as tradition, the campa saying no document has been produced.

## 3. Iron wire, 1837-1842

**Sandy and Beaver Canal**, Ohio, 1837 **[A: nps.gov]**. *Gap:* the stretch he worked is unknown, and the pin is approximate.

**Pennsylvania railway surveys** under Charles L. Schlatter, 1839-40, and the repeated failure of hemp haulage rope on the inclined planes **[A: immigrantentrepreneurship.org]**. That Roebling watched two men killed by a snapping hemp rope is **[R]**, no contemporary accident report being found. Flagged on the stop, kept out of the campa.

**Letter to Charles Ellet Jr., 28 January 1840** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, and the rivalry after it: Ellet took Fairmount, Wheeling and the first Niagara contract. There is **no `charles_ellet.journey.json`** here, so the crossing has no atlas file on the other side.

**The quotation, downgraded to [B].** The pool tagged the "study of suspension bridges ... favourite occupation" letter **[A]**. The text is in the en.wikipedia article, whose raw wikitext carries the footnote to Steinman & Watson (1941). But archive.org holds only a lending record for that book, HathiTrust and Google Books full-text were unavailable, and **neither the book nor the letter was seen**. The chain stops at Wikipedia reproducing someone else's citation, and `quote_source` says so.

**Ropewalk** at Saxonburg from 1841 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **US Patent 2,720**, "Method of and Machine for Manufacturing Wire Ropes," **16 July 1842** **[A: patents.google.com/patent/US2720A/en, checked]**, so the dossier's hedge on the number is removed. First test on **Incline 3, Allegheny Portage Railroad**, 1842 **[A: nps.gov]**. The **sabotage story** is **[R]**, kept in the campa as a story that "attaches to the trial."

## 4. Aqueducts and the factory, 1845-50

**Allegheny Aqueduct, Pittsburgh**, low bid among 44, seven spans of about 162 feet, **May 1845** **[A: nps.gov; immigrantentrepreneurship.org]**. *Decoy flagged:* the Wikipedia page titled "Allegheny Aqueduct" is a different 1824 structure in Berks County.

**Smithfield Street Bridge**, opened February 1846 **[A: nps.gov]**. It crosses the **Monongahela**; the chronology lens's "Monongahela/Allegheny road bridge" is wrong, his Allegheny crossings being the 1845 aqueduct and the Sixth Street bridge. en.wikipedia also confirms Wernwag's 1818 bridge lost in the Great Fire of 1845 and Lindenthal's 1883 replacement on Roebling's piers.

**Delaware Aqueduct**, Minisink Ford, 535 feet, 1847 to 1849, $41,750, oldest surviving wire suspension bridge in the country **[A: en.wikipedia; nps.gov/upde]**. The **Lackawaxen** companion, $18,650, abutments only, is folded into the same stop, its coordinates estimated from the confluence. **Trenton works** running by October 1849 under Charles Swan **[A: nps.gov]**.

**High Falls / Rondout** and **Neversink / Cuddebackville**, both completed in the second half of 1850. **Both upgraded from [R] to [A]**: immigrantentrepreneurship.org says the two were "both completed latter half of 1850," and en.wikipedia names the Roebling aqueduct at High Falls. The Neversink pin is 2 km southwest of the hamlet, labelled approximate.

## 5. Niagara, 1851-60

**The bridge**: two levels, rail above and road below, clear span about 825 feet, lower deck open 1854 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Opening date, contradiction flagged.** The 23-ton locomotive *London* was run onto the railway deck **8 March 1855**; "a fully laden passenger train officially opened the completed bridge" **18 March 1855** (en.wikipedia). immigrantentrepreneurship.org says "the first train passed over it on March 16, 1855." **The dossier's two lenses disagreed**, chronology giving 16 March, geography 18 March. The stop is dated 18 March and the campa carries the 16 March account beside it. Dismantled by 27 August 1897.

**The 1852 report to the directors**, read directly in the archive.org OCR `reportjohnaroeb01compgoog` **[A]**; two quotations used. **One repaired, then dropped:** the pool's "The pressure upon the anchor plates will be met with a resistance equal to the ultimate strength of the cables..." is genuine and contiguous but invents a sentence boundary, the original opening "My calculations satisfy me, that the pressure..." Rather than truncate it, the claim is paraphrased in the campa and not quoted.

**The 1860 condition report**, 1 August 1860, read directly, `reportonconditio00roeb` **[A]**: one passage quoted, two paraphrased. **The 1854 cholera outbreak** among the workforce, with Roebling nursing the sick, is **[R]**: en.wikipedia's article on the bridge mentions no outbreak. Kept as one sentence inside the construction stop, attributed to "the family accounts," and given no stop of its own.

**Kentucky River, 1853**, contracted then abandoned on the railroad's collapse: **[R]**, site uncertain, coordinates approximate. Kept as a stop because unbuilt work is part of the record, with the thinness stated in the campa.

## 6. The Ohio and the household, 1856-1867

**Cincinnati-Covington**: contract **18 April 1856**, the Panic of 1857 and the war delaying it a decade, foot traffic **1 December 1866**, all traffic **1 January 1867**, main span 1,057 feet **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The **1983 renaming** is strictly **[R]**, resting on the dossier plus the bridge's public name.

**Sixth Street Bridge, Pittsburgh**: commenced **spring 1858**, opened **2 May 1860**, two main spans of **343 feet**, 179-foot shore spans, dismantled 1892 **[A: immigrantentrepreneurship.org; en.wikipedia]**. The chronology lens's "completed 1859, spans around 344 feet" is both early and imprecise. **Washington joins the practice spring 1858**, out of Rensselaer.

**"Bridging the East River," 1860: [R].** Title and argument are cited throughout the Brooklyn Bridge literature, but **no digitized copy was located**. The campa says outright that no copy was reached.

**Johanna Herting Roebling died at Trenton, 22 November 1864**, after long illness, her husband away **[A: Rutgers finding aid for place and date; immigrantentrepreneurship.org for the circumstance]**. The geography lens called Trenton an assumption from the works' proximity; the Rutgers aid states it, so the hedge is dropped. **Second marriage February 1867, Lucia W. Cooper of Trenton** **[A: immigrantentrepreneurship.org]**, superseding "New Jersey, exact location not confirmed."

**The philosophical manuscripts**: roughly 1,000 pages or roughly 2,000 depending on source, unpublished, on Hegel, Swedenborg and the American spiritualists, split in 1958 between Rensselaer (MC 04) and Rutgers from the estate of John A. Roebling II. **[R]**: the finding aids were not refetched. The largest gap in the subject, and it has its own stop at the Trenton desk.

## 7. The East River, 1867-1869

**New York Bridge Company act approved 16 April 1867** **[A: nps.gov]**. **Contradiction flagged:** NPS implies appointment as chief engineer in **May 1867**, en.wikipedia says **September 1867**. Unresolved, and the campa says so.

**Fulton Ferry, 28 June 1869**, right foot crushed while fixing the Brooklyn tower position, toes amputated **[A: nps.gov; en.wikipedia]**. Accounts differ on the mechanism, an arriving boat or the pilings shifting.

**Death 22 July 1869 of tetanus**, twenty-four days later, at his son's house on Hicks Street, Brooklyn Heights **[A: en.wikipedia; nps.gov gives only "Brooklyn, New York"]**. Two details demoted: **"137" Hicks Street** is in no source reached (en.wikipedia says only "the home of his son on Hicks Street"), and **Emily Warren Roebling's presence at the death** is likewise not in the cited source. The campa says so of the number, and claims only that the house is the one Washington kept with her.

**Hydropathy and the refusal of conventional care** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The wider frame, séances, Swedenborg, Andrew Jackson Davis, mesmerism, is **[R]** from the dossier's religion section.

**Burial, Riverview Cemetery, Trenton**, July 1869 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. *Gap:* the exact day is unknown, so the stop is dated 1869-07-25 to keep it after the death, the one place in the file where an unknown day is not defaulted to 01; the reason is on the stop.

## 8. After 1869

- **Construction began January 1870**, six months after the burial, and the bridge opened **24 May 1883** under Washington, with Emily Warren Roebling as principal intermediary **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The shorthand that he "died while building the Brooklyn Bridge" is inexact and the file corrects it.
- ***Long and Short Span Railway Bridges*, 1869.** Year **[A: Open Library, three editions]**. The D. Van Nostrand imprint was not verifiable and the Lehigh digitization framing returned HTTP 429, so both stay **[R]**. Whether it appeared before or after his death is unknown; the stop is dated 1869-12-01.
- **Cadwalader Park statue, Trenton.** The **seated bronze** and **1908** are confirmed (en.wikipedia), which names no sculptor. The 30 June date, the crowd of 15,000, the marching employees, the governor, the Mühlhausen wreath and the attribution to **William Couper** all come from a todayinsci.com page that **now returns HTTP 404**. The campa states that collapse in its last sentence.
- **Roebling Museum**, Kinkora Works, opened 2010 **[A: roeblingmuseum.org]**; the 1974 closure of the works is not on its About page. **Inventors Hall of Fame 2004** **[A: invent.org]**, **Deutsche Post stamp 2006** **[A]**, the Mühlhausen **Johann-August-Röbling-Schule** anglicized by en.wikipedia **[A]**. No stops: honors are not places he went, and the museum is the company's afterlife.
- Two legends **not used anywhere**: "the curse of the Roeblings," modern tour-culture writing whose cited Museum of the City of New York post returned HTTP 403, and the icebound 1852 ferry ride as the origin of the bridge idea, later legend by every source consulted and includable only as a staged scene.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**John A. Roebling is not in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and this pass found no relation requiring that he be.** No framework of his was applied backward to material he never treated, and nothing forward past 1869. There is nothing to grant. What was dropped is co-location, which the standing rule disposes of without a grant, recorded here rather than deleted silently: **Tocqueville** at Niagara in 1831 against the 1855 bridge, and Tocqueville and Roebling both crossing the Atlantic in 1831; **Calvert Vaux** and **Frederick Law Olmsted** on Prospect Park in the Brooklyn Bridge years; **Theodor Reuss** and **Arthur Edward Waite**, a later European generation. In none is there a meeting, a correspondence or an influence. The 1854 cholera outbreak was also proposed as a period-medicine interlock: no atlas traveler shares it, and it survives as one sentence of his own stop.

An influence relation, not co-location, but its evidence is **[R]**, resting on the dossier's religion section rather than an independently fetched source.
