# Atahualpa (Ataw Wallpa), last Sapa Inca of Tawantinsuyu: research report

*2026-08-23. Slug `atahualpa`. Register: national mythology: the canon is true. Calendar: julian (life ends 1533, half a century before the Gregorian reform).*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or later chronicle without independent support.

A word on the evidentiary floor before anything else. There is no Inca written record. Every date, every place, and every sentence Atahualpa is said to have spoken reaches us through Spaniards who were either killing him, profiting from him, or writing forty years later from other men's memories. The eyewitness layer is thin: Francisco de Xerez (Pizarro's secretary, printed Seville 1534), Hernando Pizarro's 1533 letter to the Audiencia of Santo Domingo, Pedro Sancho de la Hoz's 1534 *Relación*, and Pedro Pizarro's *Relación* of 1571, written as an old man about events he saw as a boy of about sixteen. Everything else, including the entire pre-1532 biography, is chronicle tradition. The file treats that asymmetry as the subject's principal fact rather than smoothing it.

---

## 1. Birth and the northern childhood (c. 1497-1502 to c. 1524)

**Birthplace is unresolved and this file does not resolve it.** Two live traditions:

- **Cuzco [R]**. Favoured by several major colonial testimonies; Cieza de León places the birth there. Pinned at the Qorikancha (-13.52, -71.9756) because no source names a birth chamber. There is no contemporary birth register of any kind, and the conventional date range c. 1497-1502 is a modern convention, not a document.
- **Inca-Caranqui, about 2 km from modern Ibarra, Ecuador [R]**. Atahualpa is himself said to have claimed this on the morning of his death. Pinned at 0.345 / -78.125, which is approximate: the source gives Ibarra's town centre and states Inca-Caranqui lies about 2 km away without a point. The fuller Princess Paccha / Shyri version of the northern tradition traces to Juan de Velasco's *Historia del Reino de Quito* (1789), more than two and a half centuries after the fact, and was not independently verified here.

Both are carried as stops. Neither is presented as settled.

**Upbringing at Tomebamba / Pumapungo, Cuenca (-2.9067, -78.9967) [R].** Huayna Capac built Tomebamba into a second Cuzco. That the city was a northern capital is [A]. That Atahualpa was raised in its court is **inference from his later northern power base, not documentation**: the geography lens concedes "No source names young Atahualpa at this site specifically." The research pool originally carried this claim at **[A]** with a round date of 1520; that tag was **corrected down to [R]** and the date treated as an ordering device only. Flagged here so the correction is visible rather than buried.

**The boy of thirteen in his father's northern campaigns (c. 1515) [R].** Later chronicle tradition, given by en.wikipedia without a visible primary citation. Not independently datable.

## 2. The death of Huayna Capac and the succession (c. 1524-1527)

This is the file's worst-documented hinge and it is flagged hard in `date_confidence`.

**Three-way contradiction, not adjudicated:**

| source | date | place |
|---|---|---|
| en.wikipedia, *Huayna Capac* | 1527 | Tumipampa (Tomebamba) |
| en.wikipedia, *Tumipampa* | "about 1525" | n/a |
| en.wikipedia, *Atahualpa* | "around 1525" | n/a |
| research pool, chronology lens (as submitted) | 1527 | Quito |
| research pool, geography lens (as submitted) | c. 1524-1525 | Quito |

The pool's own two lenses disagreed with each other and both were tagged **[A]**. Corrected to **[R]**, dated c. 1525, place given as **disputed between Quito and Tomebamba**. The stop is pinned at Quito (-0.2201, -78.5123) because that is where the northern court sat and where the succession crisis broke, and the confidence field says so explicitly.

**The smallpox attribution is itself disputed [R].** The same en.wikipedia *Huayna Capac* article records that his mummy showed no pockmarks. The epidemic is attested; its identification as smallpox is a retrospective guess. The file says "a pestilence" and states the dispute.

**Ninan Cuyuchi**, the designated heir, dies in the same epidemic in many accounts [R].

**Huáscar (Topa Cusi Huallpa) installed as Sapa Inca at Cuzco [A]**: en.wikipedia, *Huáscar*. Pinned at Pizarro's inherited Cuzco pin (-13.517, -71.9785).

**Atahualpa governor of the north around Quito [R]**: reported, or self-styled; the sources do not agree on which, and the file does not choose.

**Cause of the breach [R].** Sources disagree entirely: a snubbed embassy, mutual suspicion of separatism, or rival claims to rule. Cieza de León, Betanzos, and Sarmiento de Gamboa each tell it differently. Carried as an open question in the campa; no version privileged.

## 3. The war of the two brothers (1529-1532)

Battle sequence as carried, with the corrections applied:

- **Mocha / Chillopampa (-1.417, -78.667), c. 1529 [R].** A Huascarist victory over Atahualpa's forces, per the en.wikipedia *Atahualpa* battle list. **Coordinate corrected**: the pool gave -1.3654 / -78.6167, about 7.5 km off. en.wikipedia *Mocha Canton* gives 1°25′S 78°40′W = -1.417, -78.667.
- **Tomebamba, capture and escape, c. 1529 [R].** Atahualpa reportedly defeated, captured, and later escaped. This is a dramatic tradition preserved by later chroniclers, not an eyewitness account. Kept as tradition, labelled as such.
- **Mullihambato (Ambato, -1.2543, -78.6229) [R].** Atahualpa's forces defeat Huáscar's general Atoc. **Date contradiction inside the pool, not resolved**: the geography lens gives c. 1530-1531; the en.wikipedia *Atahualpa* battle list groups Mullihambato with Chimborazo and Quipaipán as April 1532 victories. The file follows the April 1532 grouping and states the rival dating in the confidence field. Coordinates are the lens's own estimate for Ambato, unconfirmed by a fetched source.
- **Chimborazo (-1.4692, -78.8175), April 1532 [R]. Date corrected by three years.** The pool's chronology lens dated this 1529; the same site's geography lens and the en.wikipedia battle list both give April 1532. The 1529 value conflated the battle with the war's opening year. The Chimborazo volcano article carries no Inca-war content of its own; the connection rests solely on the *Atahualpa* battle list, which does not describe the engagement.
- **Tomebamba destroyed, 1532 [A/R]. Date corrected.** The pool's chronology lens said 1531; en.wikipedia *Tumipampa* says the city "was largely destroyed" in the civil war before Spanish arrival in 1532, and the geography lens agreed on 1532. Reconciled to 1532. That severe reprisal fell on the Cañari-aligned city is well supported **[A]**; **casualty figures are not recoverable and none is claimed [R]**.
- **Marcahuamachuco (-7.7853, -78.0783), 1532 [R]. Coordinate corrected** by roughly 3.6 km: en.wikipedia *Marcahuamachuco* gives 7°47′07″S 78°04′42″W. The site article does not mention Atahualpa at all; the connection is the *Atahualpa* article's note that "while based in Marcahuamachuco, he sent an emissary to consult the oracle" (Catequil). The widely repeated tradition that he then had the Catequil shrine thrown down does **not** appear in any source fetched for this pool and is therefore **not** written into the file. Recorded here as a gap a future pass could close.
- **Jauja / Xauxa (-11.7797, -75.4972), 1532 [A].** Huáscar's military headquarters, supplying his generals, taken by Quizquiz. Pin inherited byte-identical from `pizarro.journey.json` rather than the lens's rounder -11.775 / -75.5.
- **Quipaipán, near Cuzco (-13.53, -72.01), April 1532 [A].** Chalcuchimac and Quizquiz destroy Huáscar's army and take him. Coordinates approximate: the source says only "a couple of miles west of Cusco." **Atahualpa was not on this field**; he was at Cajamarca. Stated in the stop.
- **Cuzco occupied and the purge, 1532 [A].** Quizquiz holds the capital; Huáscar's lineage and supporters are killed. **Numbers are not recoverable and none is given.** Again, Atahualpa is not present; the stop records action taken in his name.

**Standing structural fact, carried in the file [A]:** at the moment of the Spanish arrival Atahualpa had won the war and had *not* undergone the enthronement rites at Cuzco. He is a victor who has never been crowned in his own capital. Drawn from the dossier's synthesis of Francisco de Xerez, *Verdadera relación* (Seville, 1534).

## 4. Cajamarca: capture, ransom, execution (1532-1533)

**Pins in this section are inherited byte-identical from `pizarro.journey.json`,** as the interlock lens directed, rather than re-geocoded:

- `-7.1638 / -78.515`: the embassy (1532-11-15), the plaza ambush (1532-11-16), the trial and garrote (1533-07-26)
- `-7.1651 / -78.5137`: the ransom promised (1532-11-20), the room filled with gold (1533-05-01), the silver (1533-06-01)

Two Cajamarca pins are *not* inherited, for stated reasons:
- **Los Baños del Inca (-7.1636, -78.4622)**, the hot springs themselves, 6 km from the city. **Corrected** from the pool's -7.1479 / -78.4636, which was about 1.7 km off; es.wikipedia *Distrito de Los Baños del Inca* gives 7°09′49″S 78°27′44″W.
- **Cuarto del Rescate (-7.15778, -78.51667)** for the surviving structure. **Corrected** from the pool's -7.1637 / -78.5128, about 800 m off; en.wikipedia *Ransom Room* gives 7°09′28″S 78°31′00″W. The afterlife lens had this right and the geography lens did not.
- **Burial and afterlife stops use `-7.1611 / -78.5127`**, the pin `guaman_poma_de_ayala.journey.json` already uses for Cajamarca.

**The ransom room's dimensions were wrong in the pool and are corrected.** The submitted text gave "roughly 17-18 feet by 35 feet." **There is no 35-foot dimension in any source.** en.wikipedia *Ransom Room* and es.wikipedia *Atahualpa*, both citing Francisco de Xerez: **6.70 m (22 ft) long by 5.18 m (17 ft) wide, with the gold line marked at 2.75 m (9 ft)**. The file uses the corrected figures. Whether the room was to be filled solid or with an agreed volume of objects is **disputed and left disputed**.

**Casualties in the plaza [R].** Spanish witnesses report Indigenous dead in the low thousands and no Spanish combat deaths. Totals are not verifiable and the file claims none.

**The breviary episode [A as narration, contested as event].** Pedro Pizarro's eyewitness version has Atahualpa ask for the closed book, fail to open it, and throw it down. The famous variant in which he holds it to his ear and remarks that it does not speak **could not be confirmed in any source fetched**. Patricia Seed, "Failing to Marvel: Atahualpa's Encounter with the Word" (1991), treats the whole silent-Bible story as a later literary construction layered onto sparser early accounts; **JSTOR full text is paywalled and was not read**, so Seed is cited as a suggested reference, not relied on. `pizarro.journey.json`'s own campa follows the ear-to-the-book variant; **this file follows Pedro Pizarro and flags the divergence** rather than harmonising the two files.

**Chalcuchimac's capture: contradiction resolved in favour of the Jauja version.** The pool's chronology lens said Atahualpa summoned him to Cajamarca and he was seized on arrival; the geography lens said Hernando Pizarro's party deceived and took him **in the Jauja valley**. en.wikipedia *Chalkuchimac* supports Jauja explicitly: he was "camped with an army of 35,000 in the Jauja Valley" when convinced he had been called to Cajamarca. The file uses the Jauja version and names the rival tradition in the confidence field.

**The death of Huáscar: contradiction resolved in favour of Andamarca.** en.wikipedia's *Huáscar* infobox gives "1532, Cusco" and is the outlier. Against it: es.wikipedia *Huáscar* gives **June 1533 at Andamarca (also Mollebamba), in the modern La Libertad region**, killed on Atahualpa's order with the remains thrown into the river; en.wikipedia *Jauja* independently says he was held in a Xauxa fortress by Quizquiz and "would be later drowned in the nearby Andamarca river." The pool's geography lens had misread the Jauja article as placing the killing *at* Jauja; that misreading is corrected. **Coordinate obtained fresh for this report**: es.wikipedia *Distrito de Mollebamba* gives 8°10′14″S 77°58′26″O = **-8.17058, -77.97396**, and states that the death of Huáscar occurred within the district of Mollebamba and Tulpo. The file uses -8.1706 / -77.974 and notes that a second Andamarca in Junín is a rival candidate not adopted here.

**The ransom total [A]**, from Pedro Sancho de la Hoz's *Relación para S.M.* (1534) and the Cajamarca distribution record: roughly **1,326,539 pesos de oro and 51,610 marks of silver**, melted to bullion, assayed, and divided by military rank with the Crown's fifth deducted.

**The trial [A as event, R as content].** A rapid summary proceeding before Pizarro and Almagro on charges including usurpation, the killing of Huáscar and of royal relatives, idolatry, polygamy, and conspiracy. **No complete authentic trial transcript survives.** The charge list and any recorded vote reach us only through later, inconsistent retellings. The file says so.

**Execution date: two live traditions, both carried.** The file follows **26 July 1533**, on the modern reconstruction that the expedition had already left Cajamarca by 29 August. The older tradition gives **29 August 1533**, feast of the Beheading of John the Baptist, and sometimes the baptismal name **Juan** rather than Francisco. Both circulate in print; the file states both in `date_confidence` and does not pretend the matter is closed. `pizarro.journey.json` uses 26 July, and the file agrees with it.

## 5. Afterlife

- **The burial [A/R].** Clothes and some hair or skin burned to complete symbolically the commuted sentence of fire; a Christian funeral; interment at Cajamarca. **The location was never recorded and is unknown.** No primary source confirms any later disposition.
- **Malqui-Machay [R].** Tamara Estupiñán's hypothesis that Atahualpa's mummy was carried north by Rumiñahui and that the site (Quechua "resting place of the mummy") is "la última morada de Atahualpa." **Location corrected**: the pool placed it "near Sígsig, Azuay province." es.wikipedia *Atahualpa* places it in **Cotopaxi province, canton Sigchos**, approximately **-0.70, -78.88**. Sigchos and Sígsig had been conflated; they are about 260 km apart. **The site has not been formally excavated** and Estupiñán's original publication was not reached. Carried as unresolved.
- **Inkarrí [R].** The Andean legend of the regenerating body, documented among Quechua speakers with variants recorded as far as Huilliche and Mapuche groups; one popular version places the head beneath Lima's Government Palace, the arms in Cuzco's main square, the legs in Ayacucho. **The Wikipedia article on Inkarrí itself flags thin sourcing and names no ethnographer or first-documentation date.** The myth's existence is attested; its documentation history is a gap, stated as one. The stop is dated 1955 purely to order the segment.
- **Cuarto del Rescate, Cajamarca [A].** The structure survives and functions as a heritage site. The World Monuments Fund's own page returned no retrievable content on fetch; the description rests on en.wikipedia *Ransom Room*. The stop's date (1950) is an **ordering device only**; no heritage-designation date was verifiable. It is set at 1950 so the stop precedes the Inkarrí (1955) and stadium (1963) stops without breaking the segment's chronological ordering.
- **Estadio Olímpico Atahualpa, Quito (-0.1775, -78.4767) [A].** Inaugurated 25 November 1951 as the Estadio Olímpico Municipal de El Batán; renamed Atahualpa in 1963.

## Cross-file relations actually used

Named in campa, per the standing rule that a one-directional relation is real and citable:

- **`pizarro`**: direct and reciprocal. He seized, ransomed, tried, and executed Atahualpa. Named throughout.
- **`el_inca_garcilaso_de_la_vega`**: his mother, Isabel Chimpu Ocllo, was Inca panaca nobility of the Cuzco royal line, and his *Historia General del Perú* (Córdoba, 1613) covers the killing at Cajamarca. A blood relation to Atahualpa's dynasty and a writer who treated him substantively. Named once, in the afterlife segment.
- **`guaman_poma_de_ayala`**: drew the seizure at Cajamarca in the *Nueva corónica* (Drawing 174). A one-directional engagement of exactly the licensed kind. Named once, in the afterlife segment.

Cited in `sources` / `suggested_refs` but **not staged as scenes**, because the travelling was somebody else's:

- Guaman Poma's manuscript in **Copenhagen** (Royal Danish Library, digital facsimile 2001, Rolena Adorno editor): that is Guaman Poma's geography, not Atahualpa's.
- The **Palacio Real de Madrid** facade statue of Atawallpa, beside Moctezuma II: the sculptors' stop, not his. He never went to Madrid.
- **Peter Shaffer's *The Royal Hunt of the Sun*** (Chichester 1964, Broadway 1965, film 1969): Shaffer's and the theatre's, not his.
- Painted portraits at **Quito (MuNa), Berlin (Ethnological Museum), Brooklyn Museum**: posthumous, symbolically loaded, none a lifetime likeness. The Brooklyn Museum object page returned HTTP 429 and was not verified directly.

Explicitly **not** used:

- **`cortes`** and the Tenochtitlan arc. Structurally the closest comparandum in the corpus, and the pool flagged it as an intentional editorial pairing (QUEUE.md lines 182-183, where Moctezuma II is queued with a cross-reference to Atahualpa). But Atahualpa had no relation of any kind to Cortés or to Moctezuma: they never met, never corresponded, and neither knew the other existed. **Shared structure is not a relation.** The map shows the parallel to a viewer without any name-drop.
- **`keiko_fujimori`**. Her file names Cajamarca as Pedro Castillo's home region. Place-name resonance only.
- **`micaela_bastidas`** / **`tupac_amaru_ii`**. Their file stages Bolívar refusing the Inca crown at Cuzco, and Guevara reading the Cuzco walls. Downstream Inca-legitimacy material, but nothing tying to Atahualpa personally: Túpac Amaru II claimed descent from Túpac Amaru I, of the Vilcabamba line that ran through Manco Inca, i.e. Huáscar's side of the split, not Atahualpa's.
- **`blas_valera`**. One of the two traditions in his own file makes his mother "a noblewoman of the court of Atahualpa"; the other makes her a Chachapoyas curaca's daughter. His file records both without merging them, and this file does not assert the Atahualpa version by naming him.
- The **Niedzica Castle** legend in Poland. The pool tied it to Atahualpa; the en.wikipedia *Niedzica Castle* article names **Túpac Amaru II** and never mentions Atahualpa at all. **Likely conflation in the crawl.** Dropped, and flagged here rather than silently.
- The canonical pins named in the task brief (Kaaba 21.4225/39.8262, Temple Mount 31.778/35.2354, Paris 48.8566/2.3522, Buenos Aires -34.6037/-58.3816) appear nowhere in this subject's geography and none is inherited.

**Neither Moctezuma II, Charles V, Vicente de Valverde, Manco Inca Yupanqui, Túpac Huallpa, Rumiñahui, Quizquiz, nor Chalcuchimac has a journey file in the checkout.** Several of them carry material this file has to gesture at without a sibling pin to inherit.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Atahualpa is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so nothing here was written into the file on that basis. Two relations were caught that are not rooted in his own life. **Neither was deleted.** Both are recorded here for the operator to rule on.

1. **Chalcuchimac burned alive, mid-November 1533, direction forward.** Atahualpa's own commanding general, executed by the Spaniards about three and a half months after Atahualpa's death, on the road toward Cuzco. It is the end of Atahualpa's war and of his faction, but he was dead and not there, so under the standing rule it is Chalcuchimac's stop, and Chalcuchimac has no file. It is therefore **omitted from the journey and recorded only here**. The place is genuinely three-way contested: en.wikipedia *Chalkuchimac* says Cajamarca; en.wikipedia *Jauja* and *Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire* say the Jauja (Xauxa) valley; John Hemming, *The Conquest of the Incas*, puts it at **Jaquijahuana (Xaquixaguana), on the road just west of Cusco, about 13 November 1533**, immediately before Pizarro entered the capital on 15 November. Approximate coordinate if it is ever wanted: -13.42, -72.13. The pool's own placeholder of -11.9 / -75.6 follows none of the three traditions and should not be used.

2. **Túpac Huallpa installed as puppet Sapa Inca at Cajamarca, then dead at Jauja three months later, direction forward.** Another son of Huayna Capac, raised by the Spaniards to legitimise the march on Cuzco. This *is* carried in the file, as a single afterlife stop at Cajamarca about what became of Atahualpa's title and of his father's remaining sons, not as a stop for Túpac Huallpa's own travel or death. If the operator judges even that too forward, it is the stop to cut. The Jauja death (October 1533) and Manco Inca's installation near Cuzco (14 November 1533) were both left out on the same reasoning: they belong to men who were there.

No backward-apparatus relation arose; there is no theoretical framework in this subject's material to carry back.

## Honest gaps

- **No Indigenous-authored contemporary source exists.** Everything is Spanish, or mestizo and later.
- **The entire pre-1532 life is chronicle tradition.** Not one date before the Spanish arrival is documented in the ordinary sense.
- **Birthplace, birth year, and father's death (date, place, disease) are all open.**
- **The trial transcript does not survive.**
- **The grave is unknown**, and the one modern hypothesis about it rests on an unexcavated site.
- **Catequil.** The tradition that Atahualpa destroyed the oracle's shrine after a bad answer is well known in the literature but appears in nothing fetched for this pool, so it is not in the file.
- **Casualty figures** for the plaza, for Tomebamba, and for the Cuzco purge are all unrecoverable, and none is claimed anywhere in the journey.
- **Every page citation to Pedro Pizarro in the submitted pool was exactly +4 off**, being the archive.org scan leaf index rather than the printed page. All nine quotations were re-verified against the scan and re-paginated. Two were **materially altered** in the pool and are restored: the Pachacama oracle speech had the clause "[in] Quito, he sent to ask him [Pachacama] what should be done for his health" silently deleted, which removes the consultation that makes the oracle's failure a failure; and the plea before the execution had three bracketed connectives inserted that are **not** the translator's and stand unbracketed in the source. The plea is third-person narration by Pedro Pizarro, not Atahualpa's quoted words, and is labelled as narration in the file.
