# Marsilio Ficino (1433-1499): research report

*Slug `marsilio_ficino`. Figline, Florence, Careggi, a year at Bologna, one sermon at Prato: the whole documented itinerary of a man whose books crossed the Alps without him.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or report rather than document · *Flagged* = sources disagree and the disagreement stands. **SEP** = Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Marsilio Ficino*; **DBI** = Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*, same subject, which carries the day-dates SEP smooths to years. Coordinate fixes are from Nominatim.

## I. Figline and the apprenticeship, 1433-1459

- **19 Oct 1433, birth [A].** Figline Valdarno, son of the physician Dietifeci (Diotifeci) d'Agnolo, recorded that year as a student of medicine and later in Cosimo de' Medici's household, and of Alessandra da Montevarchi (SEP; DBI). He fixed the date himself, repeatedly, with an hour, to cast his own nativity. *Flagged:* see contradiction 1. **Coordinate fix:** the dossier's 43.6189 / 11.4631 sits ~700 m west of the town, Piazza Marsilio Ficino being 43.61830 / 11.47185. No birth house documented.
- **24 Oct 1451, the earliest hard date [A].** Giovanni Chellini's account book records lending a logic textbook by Paul of Venice to "maestro Marsilio di maestro Ficino," then *ripetitore* in the household of **Piero de' Pazzi** (DBI; SEP).
- **Before 1454 [R].** The *Invectiva ... ad suum Ghuardavillam Volaterranum*, on the poverty and dependence of tutors: reported, no edition examined. Kept as the one counterweight to the later image of a kept court philosopher.
- **c. 1458, Bologna [R].** Medical study, back after about a year (Treccani, *Enciclopedia Italiana*). No matriculation, curriculum or degree survives; *doctor of medicine* is not firmly documented.
- **1459, Argyropoulos [A] lesson, [R] causation.** He lectures on Greek and Aristotle at the Florentine Studio with Ficino his pupil (Wikipedia; DBI, as "Giovanni Argiropulo"). *Flagged:* that this is **how** he got his Greek does not survive, SEP crediting Francesco da Castiglione instead.
- **Saturn [A] self-diagnosis, [R] early crisis.** He identifies himself with Saturn, black bile and recurrent depression throughout *De vita libri tres* (1489). The youthful-melancholy narrative comes from **Corsi's** *Vita Marsilii Ficini*, dated **1506**, not "shortly after" the death as the dossier had it. The pool's 1450 is a placeholder and is no stop.

**The calendar trap, sprung and un-sprung [A] with correction.** DBI dates the vernacular letter to Francesco Capponi, *Di Dio et anima*, "il 24 genn. '58 (stile fiorentino '57)." Florence began its year on 25 March, so a Florentine date from 1 January to 24 March runs a year behind modern reckoning (Wikipedia, *Florentine calendar*). An earlier pass applied that rule to "1458" and got **1459**: the conversion applied twice. DBI puts the modern year first, the Florentine year bracketed, as its parallel formulas prove. The letter is **24 January 1458** modern, and the "Florentine 1458 = 1459" figure that reached the council relay must not feed a `picatrix_electors` rerun.

## II. Cosimo's commission, 1462-1464

- **4 Dec 1462, the house, sharpened [A].** DBI: "Cosimo acquistò per il F. ... una casa in via S. Egidio," for a *studens in philosophia*. He **purchased** it, stronger than "gave," and the street is named: journey uses **43.7726 / 11.2605**, dropping the "Medici palace district" placeholder. Ficino rented it out and lived at Careggi.
- **1463, Careggi [A].** Land and a dwelling in the parish of **San Piero a Careggi**, beside Cosimo's villa (DBI; SEP); a letter of September 1462 already speaks of an "Academy" on the estate. Pin: Villa Medici, 43.8094 / 11.2495.
- **1463, the Pimander, corrected in extent [A].** Plato set aside at Cosimo's request. **Not** the whole *Corpus Hermeticum*: treatises **I-XIV**, the extent of the manuscript handed him, XVI-XVIII reaching Latin only decades later through Lazzarelli (Wikipedia, *Hermetica*). Printed 1471.
- **1 Aug 1464, Cosimo's death [A] with an [R] inside it.** At Careggi; the deathbed reading of the new Plato rests on Ficino's own later account.

## III. Banquet, whole Plato, new patron, 1468-1469

- **1468, Cavalcanti [A].** Intense letters to the younger patrician **Giovanni Cavalcanti**: Socratic lover and beloved, Eros, Achates (SEP; DBI). The friendship runs decades, and Cavalcanti receives the 1494 Savonarola letter.
- **7 Nov 1468, upgraded and re-attributed.** DBI: "il banchetto che Lorenzo dei Medici aveva voluto organizzare il 7 nov. '68, per onorare Platone," on the traditional day of Plato's birth and death. The date is **[A]**, not traditional, and **Lorenzo** convened it, not "members of Ficino's circle." **[R]** stays on the venue, unnamed in DBI, and on the speeches of *De amore*, which is no transcript even on the attested day.
- **c. 1469 [A].** An initial Latin version of the entire Platonic corpus, revision continuing for years (SEP). **1469, year only [A]:** Lorenzo takes Florence's leading position and becomes chief patron after Piero.

## IV. Orders and the Platonic Theology, 1473-1484

- **5 Jan 1473 [A]** elected *pievano* of **San Bartolomeo a Pomino**, diocese of Fiesole, DBI's "(stile fiorentino '72)" bracket confirming the modern year. **Coordinate fix:** the dossier's 43.838 / 11.524 falls on open hillside ~3.3 km off; Pomino, Rufina is 43.81510 / 11.55064.
- **18 Sep 1473 [A]** ordained **deacon**, no church named. **18 Dec 1473 [A]** ordained **priest** (DBI; SEP). Canonically celibate from this date, with no marriage, partner or child documented at any point in his life.
- **6 Jan 1474 [R]** rector of **San Cristofano a Novoli**, same bracket convention; [R] because the site rests on that one DBI line.
- **1474 [A].** *De Christiana religione*, on scripture, prophecy, miracle and *pia philosophia*, vernacular 1474 and Latin 1476 (Wikipedia); and the *Theologia Platonica de immortalitate animorum* essentially complete, printed only 1482 (SEP).
- **c. 1474, Colucci [R].** Benedetto Colucci records Ficino presiding over disputations among elite young Florentines (DBI), no venue. **Not** a stop; recorded so the operator knows it was seen and left out for want of a place.

## V. Plague, conspiracy, print, 1478-1484

- **26 Apr 1478, Pazzi conspiracy [A] event, gap for Ficino.** Giuliano de' Medici killed, Lorenzo wounded. **No source places Ficino in the cathedral or anywhere specific** that morning, so the stop sits at the generic Florence point. The documentary link is older: the household he tutored in, in 1451, was Pazzi's.
- **1479, Consilio contro la pestilentia [A].** Written in the 1478-1480 plague, in Tuscan deliberately so non-specialists could use it; Galenic advice, called by Ficino an act of charity toward his homeland (SEP). Printed **1481**, year only. *Flagged:* attributing that printing to **San Jacopo a Ripoli**, Via della Scala, rests on one ILIESI-CNR page, unverified. **Coordinate fix:** Via della Scala 85 is 43.77620 / 11.24456, 217 m off.
- **7 Nov 1482 [A].** The *Theologia* printed, day from DBI ("edita ... il 7 nov. '82"): the same traditional Platonic birth-and-death day as the 1468 banquet. No printer identified.
- **Oct 1484 [A].** The complete Latin Plato printed, the first printed translation of the whole surviving corpus into a Western language (SEP). No shop named.
- **Nov 1484, Pico [A], month level.** `giovanni_pico_della_mirandola.journey.json` has Ficino finishing the Latin Plato the day Pico first arrived in Florence, and the two dining with Lorenzo, Poliziano and Benivieni at the Palazzo Medici.

## VI. Canon, De vita, Roman scrutiny, 1486-1492

- **1485-1490, the face [A] portrait, gap for the sitting.** Ficino is in Ghirlandaio's Tornabuoni Chapel cycle at Santa Maria Novella, in *The Angel Appearing to Zacharias*, with Landino, Poliziano and either Gentile de' Becchi or Demetrios Chalkondyles. **Date fix:** the cycle ran 1485 to 1490 (Wikipedia), not 1486-1490. No record of a sitting; the likeness is what is attested.
- **1487, canon [R].** Canon of Santa Maria del Fiore (SEP; DBI), year only, held with his other benefices for life.
- **c. 1489-1492, Lorenzo's table [A] at window level.** `michelangelo.journey.json` has Lorenzo bringing the teenage Michelangelo into the Palazzo Medici to eat at the same table as Ficino and Poliziano, with the two Medici boys who became Leo X and Clement VII. No day.
- **1489, De vita libri tres [A].** Completed at Careggi spring or summer 1489 (DBI); its third book, *De vita coelitus comparanda*, on planetary correspondences, music, stones, images and talismans.
- **The Picatrix line [A] parallel, [R] transmission.** `maslama_al_qurtubi.journey.json` names Book III as working the ground of the *Ghayat al-Hakim*, neither Ficino nor Pico knowing the Arabic compiler's identity; `ahmad_al_majriti.journey.json` calls it "the same art the Ghayat al-Hakim set out in Arabic five centuries before," and **flags the textual debt as unverified against a primary citation**.
- **The quote repaired [A].** The pool's *De vita* 3.8 string opened with "teaches that," SEP's framing verb pulled inside the quotation marks, and dropped the qualifier "just mentioned." SEP also says Ficino is **quoting Thebit**: reported doctrine, not his own voice.
- **15 Sep 1489, the Apologia: contradiction resolved, losing date named [A].** DBI: "il 15 sett. '89, rivolgeva a Piero Nero ... un'Apologia." The dossier's 19 September traces to no reachable document, and its note treating DBI's date as the day accusations *reached* him inverts the source. Its argument, against men "superstitious and blind" who see life in the meanest plants but not in the heavens, is Kaske and Clark, 2002, p. 399, on Acts 17:28; the pool had it mislabelled as Book I.
- **c. Aug 1490, the scrutiny closes [A].** Begins after May 1490, patrons intervene, settled with **no trial, no imprisonment, no recantation, no conviction** (SEP).
- **1492 [A], year only.** The *Enneads* in Latin with commentary printed at Florence, no printer named (SEP; IEP). **8 April 1492, Lorenzo dies at Careggi [A]**, no source placing Ficino at that deathbed.
- **1492, golden-age letter, re-attributed [A] words.** The pool's "letter to a friend, 1492" is only how the uncredited Wikipedia blockquote labels it; it is the **letter to Paul of Middelburg**. The published rendering has **oratory**, not rhetoric, and runs through the Orphic lyre and "all this in Florence" rather than an astrology clause. *Flagged:* Wikipedia ends on "astrology" where the standard English has "astronomy"; the Latin is unreachable, so the journey omits that clause.

## VII. Prophet, invasion, reversal, 1493-1498

- **1493, De sole and De lumine [R].** The sun as visible image of divine goodness, not itself divine (SEP); composition site inferred from his late Careggi habit.
- **1494 [A].** Charles VIII invades, Piero de' Medici is expelled, the Medici collections pass to San Marco, and Ficino withdraws to Careggi and makes his peace with the anti-Medicean republic.
- **12 Dec 1494 [A] by report.** A letter to Cavalcanti praising **Girolamo Savonarola** as a divinely sent voice who helped preserve Florence (DBI). The original text was not fetched; the place is inferred from the withdrawal.
- **11 Mar 1495, Venice [A].** First printed collection of the *Epistolae*, selected, revised and arranged by Ficino himself (DBI, "11 marzo '95"). Ficino never travelled to Venice, so the journey pins the stop at Florence rather than at a press he never entered.
- **31 Aug 1495, upgraded, re-sourced, moved [A].** The pool credited this commission to SEP; **SEP has no such commission**. DBI does, naming its members: "di cui era componente lo stesso Savonarola." Tag rises [R] to [A], and the buried detail is restored: **Savonarola sat on that commission with Ficino**, under three years before Ficino called him the Antichrist in print. **Coordinate fix:** San Marco is 43.77835 / 11.25897, ~316 m from the dossier point.
- **Apr 1496, downgraded, re-sourced [R].** A sermon of Savonarola's at Prato, where the Studio had moved after Pisa's rebellion. The pool tagged this [A] on SEP; **SEP has no "Prato,"** and DBI is conditional, "avrebbe assistito ad una predica." **Coordinate fix:** Santo Stefano is 43.88185 / 11.09815, 182 m off.
- **23 May 1498 [A].** Savonarola is executed. **No evidence places Ficino at the execution**, so that is not a stop here; it belongs on Savonarola's own journey, and he has no atlas file yet.
- **After 23 May 1498, the reversal [A], month-only, deliberately.** The *Apologia contra Savonarolam* to the College of Cardinals reverses the 1494 praise: false prophet, hypocrite, "savage Nero," Antichrist (SEP; Wikipedia; DBI; Passerini 1859, all treating text and reversal as authentic). **No day exists in anything reachable**, matching the council cross-check on the companion dossier pass; 1498-05-24 only orders the segment. Whether this is disillusion, self-protection or accommodation to the victors is **not settled here**.

## VIII. Will, death, burial, 1499

- **27 Sep 1499, and the place the dossier got wrong [A].** DBI: "il F. faceva testamento, a Firenze, nella chiesa di S. Marco," followed by "Morì a Careggi ... il 1º ott. 1499." The **year** is 1499, not 1498, so the will is four days before the death; the **place** is San Marco in Florence, not Careggi, so the geography lens is wrong and the afterlife lens right. He asks for simple rites and thirty masses at San Marco, Savonarola's convent, whose Medici books he had inventoried four years earlier.
- **1 Oct 1499, death at Careggi [A].** **No disease, accident or cause** is documented; poisoning, magical mishap and Savonarolan violence are unsupported. The 1908 Catholic Encyclopedia prints "d. at Correggio, 1 Oct. 1499," an error that then propagated.
- **Burial [A] church, [R] day.** Santa Maria del Fiore, where he had been canon since 1487. No burial date found; journey uses 1499-10-02 to order the segment.

## Contradictions carried forward unresolved

1. **19 vs 20 October 1433**, birth; **1473 vs 1477**, ordination; **Becchi or Chalkondyles** as the fourth humanist in Ghirlandaio's group; **1937 vs 1938** for Kristeller's *Supplementum Ficinianum*, the cited source (IEP) giving 1938.
5. **1495 vs 1492/1494** for the printed *Epistolae*: IEP against DBI's day-dated imprint, preferred here for carrying a day and a place. Venice also reckoned *more veneto*, and which convention the colophon used is unconfirmed.
6. **15 vs 19 September 1489**, the *Apologia*. Resolved for the 15th, losing date named, because nothing reachable supports the 19th. Recorded so a later pass can reopen it against a primary edition.
7. **1521 vs 15 March 1522** for the Ferrucci monument, with 15 February 1520 for its commission. Wikipedia gives 1521 for the bust; the dossier adds the commission and a 1522 installation, which have no accessible source at all, so this stays [R]. Also unconfirmed: whether the bust remains in the south nave or has moved into the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo.
8. **1561 vs 1576**, Basel *Opera omnia*. IEP gives 1576 in two volumes, Wikipedia 1561; both printings are real, and the ranking making one the original was not verifiable.

## Honest gaps

- No birth house; no church for either ordination; no printer for the *Theologia* (1482), the Plato (1484) or the Plotinus (1492). No cause of death, and no document of any kind for the Bologna year.
- No evidence of his whereabouts on 26 April 1478, at Lorenzo's deathbed on 8 April 1492, or at Savonarola's execution on 23 May 1498. All three left as absences.
- The **appearance-after-death legend** (Ficino promising a friend he would appear if the soul survived, then reportedly appearing on a white horse crying that the things were true) is unconfirmed by any page fetched, every reference work consulted being silent, and the only findable "Michele Mercati" is a later Vatican naturalist. Well known in popular and esoteric accounts, so **kept here, labelled, not dropped**, but no stop: nothing in it is something Ficino did alive.
- The **Plethon legend**, that the young Ficino was turned to Platonism by meeting George Gemistos Plethon at the Council of Florence (1438-39), is unsupported: he was five, and nothing places him there. `cosimo_de_medici.journey.json` carries the council as a dated 1439 stop with Plethon present, and treats the "chartered Platonic Academy" as later myth traced to Hankins.

## Afterlife, apparatus, and what is not on this file

No afterlife segment: a journey ends with its subject. **Ficino is not listed in EXCEPTIONS.md**, so no apparatus stops are licensed and none were written, and nothing was silently deleted either. Every relation found that is not rooted in his own life runs **forward**, which the exception file never licenses: a 1499 block cannot embed a later hash. Each hangs instead as a `source` or `suggested_ref` on Ficino's own stop for the work engaged: **Casaubon's 1614 redating** of the Hermetic corpus, **Agrippa** at Pavia (1515) and **Frances Yates** on the 1463 Pimander stop, Yates also on *De vita*; **Bruno's** *De umbris idearum* (1582) and the 1583 Oxford plagiarism charge over Ficino's words on love and light on the 1468 banquet stop, where *De amore* comes from; **Culianu** (1972, 1984) and **Nick Land's** two dated 2025 appearances on the *De vita* stops; **Kristeller** (1943), the **1911 Britannica** verdict of "little better than a mass of ill-digested erudition" and the Basel *Opera omnia* on the Theologia and Plato stops; **Ferrucci's monument** on the burial stop, since it is not Ficino's body but a sculptor's object with unsourced dates. **Reuchlin** in Florence, 1490, stays off the file entirely: `johannes_reuchlin.journey.json` **disputes** that Ficino attended his meeting with Pico, and this file does not resurrect it.

No **backward** relation was found that would need a listing: his apparatus does run backward in his own lifetime, onto Plato, Plotinus, Hermes Trismegistus and the Picatrix tradition, but that is his own documented work. Nothing here for the operator to approve; the entry records that the direction was checked and named.
