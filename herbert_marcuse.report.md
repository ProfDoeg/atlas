## 1. Berlin, 1898-1919

  street of the birth; the stop uses a city-centre pin and says so.
- **Mommsen-Gymnasium**, then **Kaiserin-Augusta-Gymnasium**, Charlottenburg, **1911-1916** **[A**
  SEP names both; en. and de.Wikipedia concur**]**. **CORRECTED:** the pool tagged the Mommsen school
  **[R]**, "dossier assertion only". SEP names it. Promoted to **[A]**. *Coordinate caveat, stated in
  the stop:* 52.5085 / 13.3088 is a generic Charlottenburg point covering both schools, neither at
  its historic site.
- **Drafted 1916**, kept off the front **[A]**. *Two traditions disagree on where, flagged:*
  de.Wikipedia gives **1916-1918 in a Luftschiffer-Ersatz-Abteilung (airship replacement unit) at
  Darmstadt and Berlin**, assigned for an **eye condition**; SEP and the English-language
  (Kellner-derived) tradition give **horse stables in Berlin**. **CORRECTED:** the pool carried only
  the stables and pinned Berlin. **Darmstadt (49.8728, 8.6512) added**; the campa states both.
- **SPD member from 1917**; **elected to a soldiers' council in Berlin-Reinickendorf, November
  1918** **[A** UB Frankfurt vita**]**.
- Murders of **Luxemburg and Liebknecht, 15 January 1919: [A]**. *His resignation and its motive:*

- **Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität**, four semesters. **CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED:** SEP dates

## 2. Freiburg and Berlin, 1920-1928

- Transfers to **Freiburg**, 1920; **1922** dissertation ***Der deutsche Künstlerroman*** accepted
  **[A** UB Frankfurt vita; SEP**]**. Back to Berlin, into the **book trade**; marries the
  mathematician **Sophie Wertheim, 1924** **[A** marcuse.org; SEP**]**; **1928** son **Peter

## 3. Freiburg, 1928-1933: under Heidegger

- Return to Freiburg after *Sein und Zeit*; **Heidegger's seminars**; the **Husserl** circle.
  **CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED:** SEP and en.Wikipedia give **1928**, marcuse.org gives **1929**.
  **CORRECTED:** presented as 1928/29, not a flat 1928.
- A course list records seminars on idealism/realism, truth and certainty, the *Parmenides* and Kant,
  and that Sophie attended some sessions **[A** marcuse.org**]**.
- **1928** "Beiträge zu einer Phänomenologie des Historischen Materialismus"; **1929** "Über
  konkrete Philosophie"; **1932** "Neue Quellen zur Grundlegung des Historischen Materialismus", on
  Marx's newly printed **1844 Manuscripts**. All **[A** SEP bibliography**]**.
- **1932** ***Hegels Ontologie und die Grundlegung einer Theorie der Geschichtlichkeit*** completed.
  He **never completes a Freiburg habilitation** and never enters the German professoriate **[A]**.
  *Whether Heidegger formally rejected it is disputed*, and is left disputed in report and file.

## 4. Exile, 1932-1941

- **CORRECTED, and it changes the map.** The pool carried a Marcuse working stop at the **Frankfurt**
  Institute plus a "1932 vs 1933" source disagreement. That is not a source error but **two events**:
  en.Wikipedia states flatly that he **"never worked in Frankfurt proper"**; he was **hired in 1932**
  (Löwenthal's recommendation, Horkheimer's assessment) and, having **left Germany in May 1933**,
  took up the post at the **Geneva branch**. The Frankfurt working stop was **deleted** and the
  appointment folded into Geneva, whose campa says he never set foot in the Institute as an employee.
- **1933** Geneva; time in **Paris**, where the Institute's documentation office sat at the École
  normale and the *Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung* was printed under the Félix Alcan imprint **[A**
  UB Frankfurt vita; corroborated in `felix_weil.journey.json`**]**. **1934** emigrates; the
  Institute affiliates with **Columbia** **[A** vita; same coordinate and month in `felix_weil` and
  `max_horkheimer`**]**.
- **1936** the long history-of-ideas section of ***Studien über Autorität und Familie*** **[A]**;
  **1937** "Philosophie und kritische Theorie" with **Horkheimer** **[A]**, the Frankfurt Nachlass
  recording which sections are whose where the title page did not. **1940** naturalized **[A** SEP**]**,
  with *no record of ceremony or place*, so the stop uses the Columbia pin; **1941** ***Reason and
  Revolution*** **[A]**.

## 5. Washington, 1942-1951

- **CORRECTED, date.** The pool put the entry into government service at **1941**. It is **1942**
  (en.Wikipedia; SEP; vita "1942-1950"); he was still at the Columbia Institute in 1941. The pool's
  geography lens already said 1942-1943, contradicting its own chronology lens.
- **1943-1945 OSS Research and Analysis Branch** with **Franz Neumann, Otto Kirchheimer, Felix
  Gilbert**; reports later collected as *Secret Reports on Nazi Germany* **[A** Princeton UP**]**.
  *Gap:* exact buildings unconfirmed; 38.8964 / -77.0524 approximates the Foggy Bottom offices.
- **1945 onward, State Department Office of Intelligence Research**, reportedly heading a **Central
  European section** **[A** for the post; the headship is reported, not documented**]**.
  **CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED:** the vita ends the Washington years **1950**, other accounts **1951**.
- **FBI file** kept **1943 into the 1970s**; no indictment ever follows **[A** "Under Surveillance:
  Herbert Marcuse and the FBI"**]**.
- **Spring 1947 Marcuse visits Heidegger at Todtnauberg** **[A** marcuse.org**]**. A **real stop
  missing from the pool's geography lens**, added at the atlas's Todtnauberg pin.
- **CORRECTED, the correspondence.** The pool dated the whole exchange 1947. marcuse.org prints
  **three letters**: 28 Aug 1947, Heidegger's reply 20 Jan 1948, Marcuse again 12 May 1948. The
  demand for a public avowal is in the first; **Heidegger's equation of the murdered Jews with the
  expelled East Germans is in the January 1948 reply**; the "outside of the dimension in which a
  conversation between men is even possible" refusal is **May 1948**. The stop is dated to the first
  letter and its `date_confidence` lays out all three.
- **1950-1951** lectures at the **Washington School of Psychiatry** **[A]**. **1951 Sophie dies of
  cancer**; he leaves government service **[A** marcuse.org**]**. *Gap:* her place of death is not
  independently confirmed.

## 6. Cambridge and Waltham, 1952-1965

- **1954-1965 Brandeis**; students include **Angela Davis** and **Andrew Feenberg** **[A]**.
- **Franz Neumann killed in a Swiss car accident, September 1954** **[A]**; Marcuse marries his widow

- **1958 *Soviet Marxism***; **1964 *One-Dimensional Man*** **[A** SEP**]**.

  chapter of *Negations* (1968). Promoted to **[A]**.
- **1964 Frankfurt guest professorship. [R]** (UB Frankfurt vita only). **Dropped from the file** for

- **1965 "Repressive Tolerance"** in *A Critique of Pure Tolerance* **[A]**.

## 7. La Jolla, 1965-1972

- **1965-1970 chair at the new UC San Diego** **[A]**.
- **July 1967 Berlin lectures**, printed as *Das Ende der Utopie*; engagement with the SDS and **Rudi

  (52.4544, 13.288); marcuse.org dates the lectures **10-13 July 1967**, and the venue is the

- **15-30 July 1967 Congress on the Dialectics of Liberation**, the **Roundhouse**, Camden, with

  **CORRECTED, coordinate:** the Roundhouse is **51.5432, -0.1519**, not -0.1535. **FLAGGED:** 15

- **1968** the governor of California and conservative organizations press UCSD to remove a publicly
  funded Marxist; death threats arrive, students and colleagues guard the house, and **the university
  does not dismiss him** **[A** CSUSM; UCSD flyer bb95354229**]**.
- **1968** unpublished UCSD lecture on the Paris and student revolts, printed by *Jacobin* in 2021
  **[A]**. **1969** *An Essay on Liberation* and the Babeuf essay **[A]**. **1970** formal
