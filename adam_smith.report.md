# Adam Smith (1723-1790): research report

*Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys. Moral philosopher, professor at Glasgow, Commissioner of Customs for Scotland, author of The Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759) and An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations (1776).*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named on the line. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition the reachable sources carry but do not document. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is printed and left standing.

**The shape of the problem.** Smith is badly documented for a figure of his stature, and he arranged it that way. No diary survives; he had sixteen volumes of manuscript burned the week before he died; his two great Glasgow courses exist only because students wrote them down. The spine rests on Stewart (1793), Rae (1895), and the scholarship (Ross) that rechecked them. Rae is the workhorse here, and Rae is a hundred and thirty years old.

---

## I. Kirkcaldy, 1723 to 1737

**Baptism, not birth. [A: Kirkcaldy parish register, cited in Ross, The Life of Adam Smith (2010).]** The register records a baptism on 5 June 1723 (Old Style; 16 June New Style, Britain not adopting the Gregorian calendar until 1752). **No birth date survives** and no source consulted claims one, though reference works routinely print "born 5 June 1723" unqualified.

**The father. [A: Wikipedia; Ross.]** Adam Smith senior, comptroller of customs at Kirkcaldy, died about two months before the son was born. The mother is Margaret Douglas of Strathendry, who lives until 1784, her son under her roof for most of the sixty-one intervening years.

**The abduction. [R]** That the child, aged about three, was carried off by Romani travellers at Strathendry and recovered by an uncle traces to the early biographers with no contemporary document behind it. It is repeated everywhere because it is the only incident anyone can name from Smith's childhood. Flagged as tradition inside the campa itself.

**The burgh school: contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Rae ch. I dates the Latin course from about **1733**; the supplied chronology gives **1729**. Both agree he leaves at about fourteen in 1737. The file uses 1733 and prints the disagreement in `date_confidence`. The schoolhouse does not survive; the site is traditional.

---

## II. Two universities, 1737 to 1746

**Glasgow. [A: Rae; Wikipedia; IEP.]** Enters the Old College **October 1737**, aged fourteen. Moral philosophy under **Francis Hutcheson**, plus mathematics. Leaves spring 1740 on a Snell Exhibition. The university moved to Gilmorehill in 1870 and the High Street site is now a railway goods yard [A: Wikipedia, 'University of Glasgow'].

**Balliol. [A: Balliol College records, cited in Rae ch. IV.]** Matriculates **7 July 1740**, departs **15 August 1746**: six years and five weeks, and he leaves before the scholarship term is out. His dissatisfaction with Oxford teaching is attested twice over, as biography (Rae, IEP) and in his own published words, where the endowed professor paid whether he lectures or not becomes a case study in the Wealth of Nations. The existing Oxford pin in `john_locke.journey.json` (Christ Church) was **not** inherited: different sites in one town.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable documents what Smith did at Oxford for six years beyond reading: no tutor, no course, no friendship attested.

---

## III. Edinburgh and the Glasgow chairs, 1746 to 1763

**Two idle years. [A: Rae.]** Back at Kirkcaldy, no post, no living, and he does not take orders, which is what the Snell Exhibition existed to produce.

**The lectures. [A: Rae; Wikipedia; Lectures on Rhetoric and Belles Lettres, from student notes.]** From **1748**, under Henry Home (Lord Kames): rhetoric, belles-lettres, and afterwards the "progress of opulence." **Gap:** the hall is not identified in any source consulted, so the coordinate marks the Old Town generally.

**Hume. [A: Wikipedia.]** The friendship begins about **1750** and runs to Hume's death in 1776. The meeting itself is not recorded; the date defaults to 1 January and the hedge sits in `date_confidence`.

**The chairs. [A: Wikipedia; Britannica.]** Logic **1751**; Moral Philosophy **1752**; Philosophical Society of Edinburgh, also 1752; LL.D. **1762**; jurisprudence lectures **1762**, surviving only through student notes [A: SEP].

**TMS, 1759: place corrected. [A]** The supplied chronology gave "London/Glasgow." Wikipedia is explicit that the publishers were **Andrew Millar of London and Alexander Kincaid of Edinburgh**. Smith wrote it holding the Glasgow chair, but the imprint is London and Edinburgh. **Correction applied.** Six lifetime editions, two heavily revised, confirmed.

**Townshend. [A: Wikipedia; Rae.]** In **1763** Charles Townshend offers a tutorship for his stepson Henry Scott, 3rd Duke of Buccleuch, above the chair's salary and with a life pension. Smith resigns early in 1764. **Gap:** the month of the offer is nowhere reachable; the file defaults to November 1763 and says so.

## IV. The Continental tour, 1764 to 1766

Best-documented stretch of the life, because Rae reconstructed it from letters. It also needed the most date corrections.

**Departure: month corrected. [A: Rae ch. XII.]** The supplied chronology dated the Dover embarkation and Calais landing to **January 1764**. Rae: "SMITH joined his pupil in London in the end of January 1764, and they set out together for France in the begining of February." **Corrected at both stops: February, not January.**

**Itinerary. [A: Rae chs. XII-XIII.]**
- Paris, **13 February 1764**, brief.
- Toulouse from **4 March 1764** ("they were in Toulouse on the 4th of March") to **the end of August 1765** ("IN the end of August Smith and his pupils left Toulouse"). The supplied range began 1764-02; **front end corrected to 1764-03**.
- Bordeaux and the Pyrenees as far as Bagneres-de-Bigorre, **1764, month unrecorded**. Stated as a gap; the file defaults to January 1764 and prints the defaulting, the convention requiring a full ISO date.
- Montpellier, **Estates of Languedoc**. The supplied entry hedged between winter 1764 and early 1765. Rae settles it: the States "met there in the end of November" 1764. **Corrected to 1764-11, hedge dropped.**
- Geneva, autumn 1765. Ferney, five or six visits to Voltaire in 1765 [A: Rae ch. XIII; Stewart].
- Paris again, roughly **December 1765 to October 1766**: Turgot, Quesnay, Necker, d'Alembert, Helvetius.
- Return by Calais and Dover, **November 1766**, after the Duke's younger brother dies in Paris.

**Canonical pin inherited.** Both Paris stops use **48.8566 / 2.3522**, byte-identical to the pin carried by `benjamin_franklin`, `thomas_jefferson`, `karl_marx` and `alexis_de_tocqueville`. The geography lens proposed 48.8656 / 2.3212; the convention overrides it, and since no lodging address survives, nothing precise is lost.

**The Toulouse letter. [A: Smith to Hume, 5 July 1764, quoted in Rae ch. XII.]** "I have begun to write a book in order to pass away the time." Carried verbatim on that stop.

**Gap.** No address survives for either Paris stay; the Toulouse house is unidentified.

## V. Kirkcaldy, London, and the book, 1767 to 1776

**Duration corrected. [A: Rae ch. XVII, "Kirkcaldy, 1767-1773".]** The supplied chronology said "roughly a decade in Kirkcaldy." That overstates it: **six years, 1767 to 1773**, then about three in London finishing the book.

**The dressing gown: attribution corrected. [R]** The fifteen-mile walk to Dunfermline is real as an anecdote, but the supplied entry credited **Dugald Stewart**. Rae ch. XVII credits **Dr Charles Rogers**. The campa names Rogers via Rae and marks the story as tradition in its own text.

**Other attested points.** Royal Society of London **1767**; Freedom of the City of Edinburgh **1770** [A: Ross]; Suffolk Street lodging 1773-1776 and the Literary Club in **1775** with Johnson, Gibbon, Reynolds [A: Rae]. Publication in two volumes by Strahan and Cadell, London, **9 March 1776**, first edition sold out within six months [A: imprint; Wikipedia].

**Hume's death. [A: Britannica; Smith to William Strahan, 9 November 1776, appended to Hume's My Own Life (1777).]** Smith's account of Hume's composure in dying draws heavier attack than the Wealth of Nations did. The file **paraphrases and does not quote** this letter: its famous sentence was not verified verbatim here.

## VI. The Commissioner of Customs, 1778 to 1790

**Appointment. [A: Ross.]** Commissioner of Customs for Scotland from **January 1778**. **Gap:** the building housing the Board of Customs in 1778 is not certain from the sources consulted; Chessels Court on the Canongate is the traditional area and the coordinate rests on that. **Panmure House** is his residence **1778 to 1790**, with his mother and his cousin Janet Douglas, and is the only house he lived in that still stands. His mother dies in **1784**; Smith never married and left no children.

**Lord Rector: a four-year error, corrected. [A]** The supplied chronology dated the rectorship to **1783**. Wikipedia: "From 1787 to 1789 he occupied the honorary position of Lord Rector of the University of Glasgow." 1783 belongs to a different event, the royal charter of the Philosophical Society of Edinburgh and Smith's founding membership of the Royal Society of Edinburgh. **The two were conflated. Corrected to 1787-11.**

**Editions: content corrected. [A: Wikipedia, 'The Wealth of Nations'.]** Five in Smith's lifetime: 1776, 1778, 1784, 1786, 1789. The supplied entry credited the 1784 third edition with "a new section on the division of labour." Wrong: that material is Book I and was not altered. The 1784 edition first incorporated Additions and Corrections, added an index, and carried new sections in Books IV and V, chiefly "Conclusion of the Mercantile System." **Division-of-labour clause dropped; mercantile and physiocratic review kept.**

**The burning. [A: Wikipedia; IEP; SEP, noting the jurisprudence manuscript was "burned at his death".]** Shortly before dying Smith orders executors **Joseph Black** and **James Hutton** to destroy his unpublished manuscripts, reportedly sixteen volumes. What was in them is disputed and undecidable: a permanent gap, not a question awaiting an answer.

**The deathbed remark. [R]** That Smith said he had meant to do more traces to no first-hand witness located here. Carried in the campa as report, not fact.

**The death date: a false conflict, retired. [A: Rae ch. XXXII.]** The supplied entry presented a plausible conflict: Wikipedia and IEP give 17 July 1790, Econlib's Concise Encyclopedia 19 July. But **Rae, which Econlib itself hosts**, says "Saturday, July 17, 1790." Econlib's own library therefore supports 17 July, and the variant reads as an error in a short reference entry, not an independent tradition. **Hedge retired, reasoning printed in `date_confidence`.**

**The burial date: a real hedge, kept. [A/R]** 22 July 1790 is standard, 24 July circulates in some secondary accounts, and no source adjudicates. The file uses 22 July and records the variant.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The Canongate Kirkyard sources describe the grave incompatibly: a guide calls it "a place of pilgrimage for economists of the world," while a 1930s-era description cited in the same article calls the monument "too small to notice." Both are printed in the campa; neither is chosen.

## VII. Afterlife

**Editions and translations. [A: Wikipedia.]** Edwin Cannan's team collates all five lifetime editions and prints the critical sixth in **1904**, still the scholarly text. French, German, and Spanish translations appear before the century closes.

**The Inquisition claim, corrected.** The supplied entry said the Inquisition banned **Germain Garnier's** French translation. That cannot stand: the ban falls in **1792**, Garnier's version in **1802**. The facts split. Garnier's is judged superior to earlier French attempts (1802); the Inquisition banned an **earlier** French translation (1792), while **Jose Alonso Ortiz's** Spanish rendering (1794) escaped and remains in print. **Correction applied.**

**Monuments and money. [A: Wikipedia.]** Stoddart's ten-foot bronze unveiled on Edinburgh High Street **4 July 2008**; an earlier statue at Burlington Gardens, London, **1867-1870**; Bank of England twenty-pound note from **March 2007**, the first Scotsman on an English banknote, Clydesdale Bank fifties since 1981; the Kirkcaldy theatre renamed **1973**, the 250th anniversary of the birth.

**Panmure House restored. [A: Wikipedia.]** A boys' club, then a ruin; bought by Heriot-Watt's Edinburgh Business School in 2008, opened by Gordon Brown **November 2018**. Restoration archaeology uncovered a well and medieval kilns.

**The Adam Smith Institute. [A as to founding, R as to influence.]** Founded in London **1977** by Madsen Pirie with Eamonn and Stuart Butler. That it was a principal engine of the Thatcher privatisations is **the institute's own framing**; no independent assessment was verified, and the file says so.

## IX. Quotations: what was verified, what was not

Eleven quotations are carried: seven confirmed verbatim against a fetched primary text, four with the failure printed in `quote_source`.

**Confirmed verbatim [A]**, all Econlib unless noted: butcher-brewer-baker (WN I.ii, Gutenberg); the invisible hand (WN IV.ii), the **sole** occurrence of that phrase in the book; the opening sentence of TMS (I.i.1); the opening line of WN Book I; "People of the same trade seldom meet together..." (WN I.x.ii); "the toil and trouble of acquiring it" (WN I.v); the man of system passage (TMS).

**Citation corrected.** The man of system passage was supplied as **TMS VI.ii.3**. econlib.org/library/Smith/smMS6.html returns it under **Section II, Chapter II**, continuing into the chess-board image; Chapter III is "Of universal Benevolence." **Correct citation: TMS VI.ii.2.** Applied.

**Carried with the hedge printed [R]:** "...by the extent of the market" (WN I.iii), carried as the chapter's title-sentence but not re-verified; "Little else is requisite to carry a state to the highest degree of opulence...", transmitted secondhand through **Stewart's** 1793 memoir reporting a lecture of about 1755, with no Smith autograph text traced; "Science is the great antidote to the poison of enthusiasm and superstition", generally placed at WN V.i.iii.iii and probably correctly, but not located on a page actually fetched. The Hume letter of 9 November 1776 is **not** quoted at all, for the same reason.

## X. Interlocks with existing atlas travelers

Named in campa where the paths genuinely cross:

- **benjamin_franklin**, three times. Franklin is made a burgess of Edinburgh in September 1759 and cultivates Hume, Smith's closest friend; **no letter places the two men in a room together**, and the stop says so in prose and hedges it in `date_confidence` [R]. Franklin lodges at 36 Craven Street from 1757, doors from Smith's Suffolk Street lodging of 1773-1776 [A]. Franklin dies in Philadelphia 17 April 1790, Smith in Edinburgh 17 July 1790 [A].
- **john_locke**, twice. Locke came up to Christ Church in 1652, same university town as Smith's Balliol, with the same recorded distaste for its curriculum [R, a parallel and not a contact]. Locke's Royal Society circle elects Smith a Fellow in 1767 [A].
- **thomas_jefferson**, twice. The Declaration goes to press in Philadelphia four months after the Wealth of Nations appears in London, 1776 [A, both files]. Jefferson keeps a Paris house from 1785, on the pin Smith uses in 1764 and 1766 [A].
- **karl_marx**, twice. Marx reads and annotates the Wealth of Nations in Paris, 1843-1845, off the Rue Vanneau, eight decades after Smith formed his physiocrat contacts there [A for the residence, R for the Smith linkage].

**Found but declined.** `alexis_de_tocqueville` shares the canonical Paris pin and the same Franco-Anglo tradition, but no textual link to Smith is attested in either file. `george_washington` touches the Enlightenment institutional world that Smith's Poker Club and Select Society mirror, but the parallel is unevidenced. No name placed in either case.

**Interlocks that do not yet exist.** No atlas file yet pins **Glasgow** or **Kirkcaldy**; Smith is first to anchor both. The queued Vienna cluster (Menger, Mises, Hayek) and Keynes all treat Smith as the founder their work answers, but none has a journey file, so those links are recorded and left unwritten. **alexander_hamilton**, in research at the time of writing, will need to interlock backward: the Report on Manufactures (1791) is built partly against the Wealth of Nations.

**Canonical pins deliberately not inherited.** Smith's itinerary does not touch the Kaaba (21.4225 / 39.8262), Temple Mount (31.778 / 35.2354), or Buenos Aires (-34.6037 / -58.3816), and none were inherited. Stated as a gap rather than silently omitted.

## XI. Sources

**Reached and used.** **John Rae, Life of Adam Smith (1895)**, on Econlib: spine of the tour chronology and the Kirkcaldy and Oxford dating; chapters I, IV, XII, XIII, XVII, XXXII. **Wikipedia**: 'Adam Smith'; 'The Wealth of Nations'; 'Panmure House (Edinburgh)'; 'Canongate Kirkyard'; 'Kirkcaldy'; 'University of Glasgow'; 'Balliol College, Oxford'; 'Adam Smith Institute'. **Econlib** full texts of both books, for quotation checks and the TMS VI.ii.2 correction; **Project Gutenberg (files/3300)** for WN. **SEP** (jurisprudence lectures, 1795 essays); **IEP** (Hutcheson, Oxford, death date); **Britannica** (the chairs, the Hume letter). **Nominatim / OSM** for section VIII. **Atlas files**: `benjamin_franklin`, `john_locke`, `thomas_jefferson`, `karl_marx`, `alexis_de_tocqueville`, `george_washington`.

**Named but not reached, or reached and found wanting**
- **Ian Simpson Ross, The Life of Adam Smith (2010)**, the standard modern biography, reached only at second hand through citation elsewhere. Its authority stands behind the parish-register, Freedom-of-the-City, customs-appointment and Panmure-House entries, but no page was read directly. This is the largest single weakness here.
- **Dugald Stewart, Account (1793)**: reached through Rae's quotation. The "peace, easy taxes" attribution rests entirely on it and could not be pushed back further.
- **Econlib, Concise Encyclopedia entry**: reached, gives 19 July 1790, contradicting the Rae text on the same site. Treated as an error, with the reasoning printed rather than the conclusion asserted.
- **The Correspondence of Adam Smith** (ed. Mossner and Ross): not reached; the 5 July 1764 letter is quoted as Rae quotes it. The **Lectures on Jurisprudence** and **on Rhetoric** were not read either, and are described from SEP and Rae.

**Gaps, restated.** No birth date. No Oxford record beyond matriculation and departure. No Edinburgh lecture hall. No Paris address. No Toulouse house. No certainty about the Edinburgh customs building. No month for the Bordeaux and Pyrenees excursions. No first-hand source for the deathbed remark. And, permanently, no knowledge of what was in the sixteen volumes, because the evidence was the paper that burned.
