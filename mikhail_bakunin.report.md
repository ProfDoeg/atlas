# Mikhail Bakunin (1814-1876) : research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Mikhail Aleksandrovich Bakunin, Russian revolutionary; Pryamukhino 1814, Bern 1876.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named in place. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried by secondary literature with no document behind it in reachable sources. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

The pool arrived with three coordinate pairs for the birthplace and several date slips. Section 11 tabulates every choice made between them.

---

## 1. Pryamukhino and the army (1814-1835)

- Born **30 May 1814 (O.S. 18 May)** at the family estate of **Pryamukhino**, one of eleven children; en.wikipedia calls him "their third child and oldest son." **[A: en.wikipedia "Mikhail Bakunin"; ru.wikipedia "Бакунин, Михаил Александрович"]**
- Estate coordinates **56.9378, 34.4528** (56°56′16″N, 34°27′10″E), Kuvshinovsky District, Tver Oblast. Buildings survive; **not a state museum**. Estate church begun **1808**, probably to a design by N. A. Lvov, completed **1826**, consecrated **October 1836**; the 1760 predecessor was an Intercession church, demolished 1797. **[A: ru.wikipedia "Прямухино"]** The pool's "Trinity church of 1846" appears in no reachable source; no diocesan source was reached to arbitrate.
- Enters the **Mikhailovsky Artillery School** at **Arsenalnaya naberezhnaya 17** on the Vyborg Side (approx. 59.9525, 30.3617) as a cadet in **1829**; commissioned **January 1833**. **[A: ru.wikipedia]** *Contradiction, left standing:* the pool gave entry as **1828**; the journey file uses 1829 and carries the disagreement in date_confidence.
- **Expelled June 1834** for insubordination toward the commandant, **General I. O. Sukhozanet**; posted to an artillery brigade on the **Minsk-Grodno frontier**; deserts that posting; formally **discharged 1835**. **[A: ru.wikipedia]** He deserted the brigade, not the school, which had expelled him a year earlier. Which garrison, and what he saw of the Polish frontier: **[R: E. H. Carr, *Michael Bakunin* (1937). Disputed among biographers.]**

**Gap.** Nothing reachable gives his own contemporary account of the desertion; every version descends from later self-narration or from Carr.

## 2. Moscow and Berlin (1835-1842)

- Moves to **Moscow in 1835**, on leaving the army, and stays until **1840**; tries to live by teaching mathematics; joins the **Stankevich** circle; reads Fichte, then Hegel. **[A: en. and ru.wikipedia agree on the years; standard histories for the circle]**
- **1840, Berlin.** Travel funded in part by **Herzen**; enrols at the university; drifts to the Young Hegelians; takes no degree. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **15 November 1841:** present in the overflow crowd at **Schelling's inaugural Berlin lecture**, named alongside Kierkegaard, Engels, Burckhardt, and Humboldt. **[A: corpus-internal. `schelling.journey.json`, `hegel.journey.json`, and `humboldt.journey.json` each record the same dated, located event and each names Bakunin in the audience.]** Three files on one afternoon: the strongest triangulation in this dossier.
- **1842, Dresden:** meets **Arnold Ruge**; publishes "Die Reaktion in Deutschland" in the *Deutsche Jahrbücher* as **Jules Elysard**. **[A: en.wikipedia; text on marxists.org]** Its closing sentence is quoted verbatim in the journey file.

## 3. The émigré circuit (1843-1847)

- **1843, Zurich.** Relocates as Russian agents take an interest; meets the communist tailor **Wilhelm Weitling**. In **1844** the Senate strips him of rank and property **in absentia** after he refuses the order home. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **Brussels, mid-1840s:** a waystation. **[R: Carr, no document cited. No stop in the journey file.]**
- **Paris, c. 1844-1847:** meets **Proudhon** and **Marx**; in **1847** gives the speech commemorating the **1830 Polish uprising**. **[A: en.wikipedia]** The chronology array dates the itinerant years 1843-1847, the geography array 1844-1847: **not adjudicated**. The consequent expulsion from France is standard in the literature and was not separately verified.

**Canonical pin inherited.** The corpus Paris pin **48.8566, 2.3522** carries the 1844-1847 residence. `gustave_flaubert.journey.json` uses the same pin for his law-student years 1841-1844 in the same quarter. **[R: no file names the two men together. The inheritance is of pin and milieu, not a documented meeting.]**

## 4. 1848-1849: the revolutions

- **February 1848, Paris.** In the February Revolution and its street agitation; then sets out for Prussian Poland, where the rising collapses before he arrives. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **2-12 June 1848, Prague.** Attends the **Slavic Congress**, fights when Austrian artillery bombards the city, writes the **"Appeal to the Slavs."** **[A: en.wikipedia]** The congress met at **Žofín Palace** on the island now called **Slovanský ostrov** (50.0789, 14.4128), renamed in 1925 to commemorate it. **[A: en.wikipedia "Žofín Palace": "The Prague Slavic Congress was held here in 1848"]** The pool's Bethlehem Chapel location is wrong by about 900 m and by building.
- **May 1849, Dresden.** Takes part in the **May Uprising**, advising the provisional government on the barricades. **[A: en.wikipedia]** **Co-presence attested within the corpus:** **Richard Wagner** is on the same barricades, directing Prussian troop-movement reports from the **Kreuzkirche** tower, and Bakunin had been lodging under his roof. **[A: `richard_wagner.journey.json`, stop 1849-05-09, 51.0509, 13.7414; corroborated by Wagner's memoirs]** The journey file inherits that pin byte-identical. Wagner's escape (same file, 1849-05-16, 50.8931, 11.4136) is the direct fork of the event that ends in Bakunin's capture. **[A]**

## 5. The fortresses (1849-1857)

- **Königstein**, 1849-1850: tried, sentenced to death, commuted, extradited to Austria. **[A for the extradition: en.wikipedia. R for the fortress name: Carr.]** **Olmütz (Olomouc)**, 1850-1851: tried again, sentenced to death again, reprieved again; reportedly **chained to the cell wall** for part of it. **[R: Carr; Aileen Kelly (1982). Traditional, repeated across the biographies, no primary document reached.]** Handed to Russia **1851**. **[A]**
- **Peter and Paul Fortress**, Alekseevsky Ravelin, 1851-1854: writes the **Confession** at the direction of **Nicholas I**, a documented archival manuscript. **[A: en.wikipedia "Confession (Bakunin)"]** **Shlisselburg**, 1854-1857: scurvy costs him most of his teeth; his mother petitions **Alexander II**; commutation to Siberian exile. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

## 6. Siberia and the escape (1857-1861)

- **Tomsk, 1857-1859.** Exile begins 1857; marries **Antonina Kwiatkowska in 1858**. **[A for the marriage: en.wikipedia. For the year: geography array and Kelly.]** The pool's Siberian pin, 56, 93, is an empty point in Krasnoyarsk Krai some 500 km from anywhere he lived; the journey file uses **Tomsk, 56.4847, 84.9481**.
- **Irkutsk, 1859-1861.** Clerical work under **Nikolai Muravyov-Amursky**, governor-general of Eastern Siberia and a distant relative. **[R: Carr. Widely reported, no document reached.]**
- **Escape, 1861.** Leaves Irkutsk **5 June**; down the Amur on the steamer **Strelok** to **Nikolaevsk**, reached in **July**; from **De-Kastri** on the American clipper **Vickery** to **Hakodate**; from **Yokohama** on the **SS Carrington**, reaching **San Francisco 15 October 1861**. **[A: the vessels are named in the standard literature.]** *A claimed gap, corrected:* the pool called the vessels and weeks unrecoverable. They are not; the one source consulted simply omitted them. The residual gaps are narrower: the weeks at **Hakodate**, and whom he met at **Yokohama**, the reported **Joseph Heco** meeting included. **[R: Carr; details vary by source.]**
- **Panama.** Crosses the isthmus by the **Panama Railroad** to **Aspinwall (Colón)**. **[A for the route: en.wikipedia. The railroad is standard period transit, not documented for him.]** Then **New York and Boston**, November-December 1861, and **London at the end of December**, rejoining **Herzen** and **Ogarev** at the Free Russian Press. **[A: en.wikipedia]** No trace found of what he did in Boston. **Gap.**

## 7. The Bell, the Baltic, Italy (1862-1867)

- **7 August 1862, Paris:** photographed by **Nadar**, the source of the standard likeness. **[R: one source carries the date]** **Stockholm, 24 February 1863:** arrives to prepare support for the Polish landing; stays in Sweden until autumn. **[A: ru.wikipedia]**
- **The Lapinski expedition.** The steamer **Ward Jackson** carried arms and volunteers under the Polish general **Teofil Lapinski** **from England**, leaving London in March 1863, and was detained by the Swedes at **Malmö** in spring; Bakunin joined en route, not at a Swedish quay. **[A: en.wikipedia, "a failed naval expedition to deliver munitions from England to the insurgents of the January Uprising in the Baltic Sea"]** The pool had him sailing from Sweden in July; port and month both wrong. The conclusion that **he never set foot on Polish or Lithuanian soil** holds.
- **Florence, 1864:** founds the **International Brotherhood**. **[A for Italy 1864-1867: en.wikipedia. R for Florence and the Brotherhood: Carr.]** **Naples, 1865-1867:** recruits among Italian radicals including **Saverio Friscia**; the milieu later produces **Cafiero** and **Malatesta**. **[R: Carr]**

**Ideological transmission, influence and not geography.** `mussolini.journey.json` (stop 1883-07-29, Dovia di Predappio, 44.108, 11.977) records **Alessandro Mussolini**, blacksmith and self-declared internationalist, reading Bakunin at his forge and naming his son partly for revolutionaries of that current. **[A within the corpus]** The journey file folds this into the Naples stop rather than giving it a pin.

## 8. The International and the war with Marx (1867-1872)

- **9 September 1867, Geneva:** inaugural congress of the **League of Peace and Freedom**, opened at 14:00 at the **Palais Électoral**, the League itself founded there **30 May 1867**. On **25 September 1868** at Bern he submits a **collective protest with fifteen signatories** and leaves. **[A: en.wikipedia "League of Peace and Freedom"]**
- **October 1868, Geneva:** founds the **International Alliance of Socialist Democracy** and applies to join the **International Working Men's Association**. **[A: en.wikipedia, in general terms]** Marx had founded the IWMA at **St Martin's Hall, London, 28 September 1864** (`karl_marx.journey.json`, 51.5128, -0.1236). **[R for the link: that file does not name Bakunin at the founding; the connection is later membership.]**
- **1869, the Nechayev affair.** Association with **Sergei Nechayev**, the **"Catechism of a Revolutionary,"** and a fabricated secret network. **[R: general historical consensus, no named source in the pool.]** *Authorship disputed:* older sources credit Bakunin as co-author, most later scholarship (Avrich among others) assigns it to Nechayev alone. **Not adjudicated.** The break-off letter of **2 June 1870** survives. **[A: marxists.org]**
- **September 1870, Lyon:** joins the seizure of the **Hôtel de Ville** as the Second Empire collapses; **October, Marseille:** hides, then returns toward Switzerland. **[A for Lyon 1870: en.wikipedia. R for the seizure, the escape, and Marseille: Carr.]**
- **2-7 September 1872, The Hague:** the Fifth Congress. Bakunin **expelled**; the General Council moved to New York; the anti-authoritarian federations reconstitute at **Saint-Imier** later that month. **[A: `karl_marx.journey.json`, stop 1872-09-02, 52.0753, 4.3038, pin inherited byte-identical; standard First International histories]**

## 9. Last years and death (1873-1876)

- **1873:** writes **Statism and Anarchy**, published anonymously in Russian to evade censorship. **[A: standard bibliographic record]** Based at intervals **1869-1874** at the **Villa Baronata** near Locarno, bought by **Carlo Cafiero** and a financial catastrophe for him. **[R: Carr]**
- **7 August 1874, Bologna:** the planned rising collapses; Bakunin leaves the country **in disguise**, north to Switzerland, not south as the pool had it. Principal organisers were **Cafiero** and **Andrea Costa**, whose arrest days before forced the improvised plan; **Malatesta** led a contingent but did not organise it. **[A: en.wikipedia "Carlo Cafiero"]**
- **1 July 1876, Bern:** dies in a workingmen's hospital, aged 62, having come from Lugano weeks before; refuses broth in the final week, takes only buckwheat porridge. Words reported on arrival: "I came here either for doctors to restore me or to close my eyes forever." **[A: ru.wikipedia, from period accounts]**
- **4 July 1876:** buried at the **Bremgartenfriedhof**, Bern, **plot 9201, grave 68**, coordinates **46.9494, 7.4203**, inside the city rather than outside it; over **200 mourners**, German, Polish, Swiss, Russian; friends raise a headstone. **[A: ru.wikipedia for plot and mourners, verbatim; de.wikipedia "Bremgartenfriedhof" for the coordinates and the burial]** **Headstone inscription unverified: gap.**

## 10. Afterlife

- **1882, Geneva:** *Dieu et l'État* assembled posthumously by **Cafiero** and **Élisée Reclus** from an unfinished 1871 manuscript, translated by **Benjamin Tucker**; his most translated work. **[A: standard publication history]**
- **1921, Moscow:** the **Confession** published in full by **Vyacheslav Polonsky**, seventy years on; first English translation **Howes, Cornell UP, 1977**. **[A: en.wikipedia "Confession (Bakunin)"]** The pool gave 1919, when the manuscript surfaced. *Unresolved:* sources disagree whether contemporaries read the text as tactic or as capitulation.
- **1934-1935, Moscow:** *Sobranie sochinenii i pisem*, four volumes edited by **Yuri Steklov**, then stopped. Steklov **arrested February 1938**, **died 15 September 1941 in Saratov prison**, aged 68. **[A: en.wikipedia "Yuri Steklov"]** The pool flagged his fate as an open gap; it is closed, and it was a prison, not a camp.
- **1936, Barcelona:** CNT-FAI anarcho-syndicalists invoke Bakuninist federalism against Franco. **[R: historiography of Spanish anarchism, no named primary source.]** **1971/1972, New York:** **Sam Dolgoff**, *Bakunin on Anarchy*, dated 1971 by en.wikipedia (revised 1980) and 1972 on the Knopf imprint; both circulate, **not adjudicated**. No stop in the journey file.
- **Archives:** five Russian institutions (Pushkin House, GARF, the Russian State Library, RGALI, the National Library of Russia) plus the **International Institute of Social History**, Amsterdam (approx. 52.3706, 4.9385). The pool counted six Russian ones; six is the total. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **Chomsky** calls Bakunin's forecast that a Marxist vanguard state would harden into dictatorship over the proletariat "one of the few predictions in the social sciences that actually came true." **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **Monuments.** No public statue or state monument found in any source consulted. **[R: an absence in the sources, not a searched negative. Inventories were not checked. A gap.]** What was found is toponymic: **Bakunin Street, Tomsk** (56.4846, 84.9482), by the exile residence, the wooden house later rebuilt in stone. **[A: ru.wikipedia]**
