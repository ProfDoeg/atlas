# Julio Cortázar (1914-1984): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, 2026-08-11. Slug `julio_cortazar`. Companion: `julio_cortazar.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a compilation claim not corroborated here against a primary or encyclopedia-grade account. Contradictions are flagged and left standing.

The life is documented to encyclopedia standard; the errors that turn up are overwhelmingly geographic, coordinates on the right place-name landing in the wrong place. Six were corrected. The biographical corrections are fewer and sharper: the Carol Dunlop chronology, the place of death, and the date of the library donation were all wrong in the material that came in.

## 1. Europe, 1914-1919

- **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica; Fundación Juan March]** Born 26 August 1914, Ixelles, Brussels, to Argentine parents; the father held a commercial post attached to the Argentine legation. **[A: sobrebelgica.com]** The address is Avenue Louis Lepoutre 116, second floor, and the commemoration is two objects, not one: a plaque on the entrance and a bust by the Argentine sculptor **Edmund Valladares** facing it, both 2005. An earlier draft spelled him "Edmundo"; no art-historical source was reached to settle it. **Open.**
- **[A: Wikipedia]** The war traps the family in Europe: occupied Belgium, then **Zurich**, **Geneva**, **Barcelona**, then the crossing. **[R]** Route, order and duration of each leg: no source reached gives a residence, school, incident or month for any of the three cities. He was an infant to a four-year-old throughout, so his later interview accounts of these years are household legend, not memory. Also **[R: Wikipedia ES]**, that he acquired French natively in the Swiss years.

**Contradiction 1, the year of the return (not resolved).** Spanish sketches (buscabiografias.com) give **1918**, the boy aged four; Wikipedia (EN) has the family settled near Buenos Aires **"by the end of 1919."** The incoming chronology said 1918 while the incoming geography lens dated the Banfield house 1919-1932, so the two lenses contradicted each other before any external source was consulted. The file uses **1919** and prints the disagreement. No ship's manifest was consulted.

## 2. Banfield, 1919-1932

- **[A: auno.org.ar]** The family settles at **Rodríguez Peña 585, Banfield**, Lomas de Zamora. The house is demolished; a chalet stands on the site, a plaque marks it from 2004, and the garden's metal door survives at Escuela N.º 10.
- **[A: Wikipedia]** The father abandons the household when Julio is about six and is never seen again; he is raised by mother, sister, aunt and grandmother. The date is unattested; 1920 is a placeholder implied by "about six."
- **[R: Britannica; Wikipedia, both relaying his own account]** The bedridden childhood and voracious reading. **[R] tradition:** the novel written at nine. **[R: auno.org.ar]** That the house is the model for "Casa tomada": plausible, undocumented, recorded not asserted.
- **[A as to wording via Wikipedia EN; letter not read]** The childhood as "full of servitude, excessive touchiness, terrible and frequent sadness," letter to Graciela M. de Solá, 4 December 1963. Second-hand.

## 3. Schooling and the provincial chairs, 1929-1946

- **[R: launion.com.ar; buscabiografias.com]** From about fourteen he commutes from Banfield to the Escuela Normal de Profesores Mariano Acosta, a National Historical Monument since 1999. **[A: same]** **Maestro normal, 1932**; **[R: launion.com.ar]** **profesor normal en letras, 1935**. Popular summaries fuse the two into one date; they are separate examinations three years apart and the file keeps them apart.
- **[A: launion.com.ar; lanacion.com.ar]** Enrols at Filosofía y Letras (UBA) 1935, does not finish, leaves to support his mother and sister.
- **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]** *Presencia*, self-published sonnets as Julio Denis, 1938; **[R]** that he later bought up and destroyed copies. Secondary teaching at **Bolívar** and **Chivilcoy**; **[R]** a third posting at Saladillo, in the Spanish accounts only, carried in a campa without its own stop.
- **[A: Britannica; launion.com.ar]** Professor of French literature, Universidad Nacional de Cuyo, Mendoza, 1944, and **[A: Britannica]** the 1946 resignation under Peronist pressure on the university. **[R]** The brief jailing at an anti-Peronist demonstration: "per some accounts" in the incoming chronology and in elhistoriador.com.ar, absent from Britannica.

**Contradiction 2, when the provincial teaching begins (not resolved).** The chronology lens opens it at **1939**; the geography lens gives **Bolívar 1937-1939** and **Chivilcoy 1939-1944**. Both cannot be right about 1937-1939, and neither is documented with terms of appointment. The file carries Bolívar at 1937 and Chivilcoy at 1939 and states the disagreement. The Chivilcoy town archive would settle it and was not reached.

## 4. Buenos Aires, 1946-1951

- **[A: Wikipedia; ahira.com.ar]** "Casa tomada" published December 1946 in **Los Anales de Buenos Aires no. 11**, edited by Borges, illustrated by Norah Borges. **[A as self-report]** That it came to him in a dream.
- **[R]** The Cámara Argentina del Libro post: Britannica says "director," the Spanish accounts "gerente." Not the same office. **Open.**
- **[R: buscabiografias.com and other sketches]** The 1948 sworn-translator examination in English and French. **Not independently corroborated here;** the register of sworn public translators of the City of Buenos Aires was not consulted. This matters, since the credential is the economic hinge of the whole Paris life.
- **[A: Britannica]** *Los reyes*, 1949, the first book signed with his own name. **[R]** *Divertimento* and *El examen* written 1949-1950 and published 1986: from an earlier draft, unconfirmed this round, **dropped**.
- **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]** French scholarship and permanent departure for Paris, 1951; **[A: cortazarpormiguel.blogspot.com]** *Bestiario* published the same year. The month of sailing is nowhere reached; October 1951 is a placeholder before the November arrival generally cited.

## 5. Paris, 1951-1968

- **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]** Freelance UNESCO translation from 1952, kept up intermittently for most of his life. **[R, consistent across accounts]** That he kept it freelance deliberately; accounts promoting him to staff translator overstate it.
- **[A: Britannica]** Marriage to Aurora Bernárdez, 1953. **[R: heroinas.net]** The day, 22 August, from one secondary source. **Gap:** the arrondissement of the mairie is named nowhere reached.
- **[R]** The Poe translation for the Universidad de Puerto Rico, 1956: carried from the earlier draft and general consensus, not re-verified. Mentioned in a campa rather than given a stop.
- **[A: revistacronopio.com]** India 1956, "the physical and mental terror of having changed planets," with his own admission that he looked only at what was beautiful and did not stop at the misery. **Gap:** the site within Delhi is not named. **[A: dialnet.unirioja.es]** India 1968, the Jantar Mantar observatories at Jaipur, out of which comes *Prosa del observatorio*.
**Contradiction 3, Saignon (not resolved).** mybackpagesblog.wordpress.com has the house bought and occupied at the end of June **1968**, near the end of the marriage; Wikipedia (FR) runs the period roughly **1964-1978**. The file dates the stop 1968-06 and prints the disagreement. Separately, the common description of Saignon as the house shared with **Carol Dunlop** fails under either reading, since Dunlop enters his life in 1977 at the earliest.

**Contradiction 4, the separation (not resolved).** Spanish sources date the divorce 1967; English sources place the separation around 1968. Both **[A]** as published claims, mutually inconsistent. Ugné Karvelis, Lithuanian-born writer and Gallimard editor, is the partner from that point **[A: Britannica]**.

## 6. The political decade, 1963-1976

- **[A: Britannica]** First Cuba trip, 1963, and **[A: newworldencyclopedia.org]** the Casa de las Américas jury connection. **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]** *Rayuela*, 1963.
- **[A: the Cortázar biographical literature]** 1971: he supports the Cuban government and condemns Heberto Padilla's imprisonment, and is estranged from other Latin American writers for it. **Gap:** no dated document was consulted for his own statements.
- **[A: standard bibliographies]** *Libro de Manuel*, 1973, royalties to political prisoners' causes; **[A: Britannica]** Prix Médicis Étranger, 1974. **[R]** That the money went specifically to the **Chilean** resistance: from the earlier draft, not re-verified; the file uses the weaker sourced wording.
- **[A: plancondor.org]** Russell Tribunal II, Rome, 1974, with García Márquez and Armando Uribe under senator Lelio Basso; the inaugural session reviews eleven countries including Argentina.
- **[A: Wikipedia ES, citing the file number]** DIPPBA **legajo 3178**, opened 29 August 1975. The date is load-bearing and routinely reported wrong: it falls under **Isabel Perón's constitutional government**, contemporaneous with López Rega's Triple A, **not** under the junta of March 1976. Compilations dating it 1976 and blaming the military are wrong on both counts.
- **[A: EBSCO]** Repeated Nicaragua journeys from roughly 1976, royalties to the literacy campaign, chronicles gathered as *Nicaragua, tan violentamente dulce*. **Gap:** individual trip dates are consolidated in no single source.

**Dropped for want of corroboration.** **Santiago 1970**, **Mexico City 1975/1980** (Dirección Federal de Seguridad surveillance) and **Solentiname 1976** stood in an earlier draft on compiled timelines only and are absent from the verified pool. Solentiname is the loss worth a second pass: the story and the community's 1977 destruction are documented, and only the visit is thin.

## 7. Carol Dunlop and the last years, 1977-1984

**CORRECTION, the Dunlop chronology.** The incoming chronology dated the relationship to **1979**. Wrong in both directions. **[A: Wikipedia, "Carol Dunlop" and "Julio Cortázar"; culturizando.com; Infobae]** He meets her in Canada in **1977**, at an international writers' meeting in Montreal, and they marry in **1981**. **[A: Wikipedia]** Dunlop was born in Quincy, Massachusetts, 2 April 1946, and naturalized Canadian; "American-born, naturalized Canadian" is more accurate than "Canadian-American." **Gap:** the Montreal venue is named in no source reached.

- **[A: Britannica]** Severe gastric hemorrhage, August 1981; the hospital is not named. The suggestion of infection through transfused blood is treated by the standard accounts as unconfirmed and disputed, and is carried in that state.
- **[A: lalinternadeltraductor.org; La Nación 2022; Unidiversidad]** French citizenship by decree of 24 July 1981, without renouncing Argentine nationality, framed as protest against the junta.
- **[A: standard bibliographies; Wikipedia]** *Los autonautas de la cosmopista*, May-June 1982. **Contradiction 5, not resolved:** Spanish accounts give **33 days**, French **32**. The file says "a month" and prints the disagreement.
- **[A: Britannica]** Carol Dunlop dies in Paris, 2 November 1982. **[A: Infobae 2021]** He designs his own grave marker, writing to the sculptor **Julio Silva on 27 December 1982**; Silva and the painter Luis Tomasello execute it, a grey **cronopio** with a small white face. Dunlop's name is cut in full, deliberately omitting "épouse Cortázar."
- **[A: Britannica]** Last return to Argentina, 1983; Nicaragua, where the Sandinistas award him the **Rubén Darío Order of Cultural Independence**; Aurora Bernárdez returns for the final illness. **[R]** The December dating and the absence of an official reception, from press and compiled timelines.

**CORRECTION, the place of death.** The incoming chronology said he died at home on the rue Martel. He did not. **[A: Rebelión, "Hace 24 años se nos fue Julio Cortázar en Saint-Lazare"; Fábrica de Periodismo, "El largo adiós de Julio Cortázar"]** He dies **12 February 1984 in the Hôpital Saint-Lazare**; the body was moved to the rue Martel apartment **after** death, which is why nearly every account places the death at home. Burial in Montparnasse with Dunlop is confirmed. **Cause:** most accounts say **leukemia**, some only cancer; **not resolved.** The stop keeps the **canonical Paris pin 48.8566 / 2.3522**, not the hospital's coordinate: atlas consistency over point accuracy, stated in `date_confidence`.

## 8. Afterlife

- **[A: Proceso 2024; Infobae 2021]** Burial 14 February 1984, Montparnasse, division 3 (3a/2a, ouest 17 in one account), roughly 300 attending.
- **CORRECTION, who was buried first. [A: Infobae 2021; Proceso 2024]** The claim that Dunlop's ashes were transferred to the grave in **2001** is false. She died 2 November 1982 and was buried there **in 1982, two years before Cortázar**, her name on the upper part of the stone; he was later buried **with her**, not the reverse.
- **[A: Rutas Cervantes; Perfil 2009]** The grave as pilgrimage site: pebbles evoking the hopscotch board, metro and supermarket tickets inscribed with lines from his books, cigarettes, flowers, letters wedged into a crevice. **[R]** Open books and packets of cherries, traveler anecdote.
- **[A: La Nación 2014]** Aurora Bernárdez dies in Paris 2014, is cremated at Père Lachaise on 14 November, and her ashes go into the tomb because both plots are already occupied. She and Cortázar had agreed, after a Hindu funeral in India in 1956, that neither wanted to be burned. **[A: Wikipedia; Diario Río Negro]** As executor she edited *Papeles inesperados* (2009) and five volumes of correspondence.
- **[A: National Book Foundation]** Rabassa's *Hopscotch* (1966) wins the inaugural US National Book Award for translation, 1967; Cortázar recommends Rabassa to García Márquez for *Cien años de soledad*.
- **[A: MNBA; El Universal 2014; soybibliotecario.blogspot.com]** Centenary 2014, an "Año Cortázar": on 26 August the MNBA opens two exhibitions, the National Library gathers more than 40 writers and scholars, and a seated statue by **Yamila Cartannilica**, donated by Susana Rinaldi, is unveiled in the Library gardens.
- **[A: Infobae 2023; WRAL/AP 2023]** October 2023: the **Zorrilla** auction house, Montevideo, sells a 1952 Paris typescript of *Historias de cronopios y de famas* found in a banana box, carrying seven unpublished stories, for **42,100 dollars** against a 12,000-dollar floor.
- **CORRECTION, the Juan March library.** The incoming claim dated the donation **December 1992**. **[A: Fundación Juan March; Centro Virtual Cervantes]** The transfer to Madrid was autumn 1992, but the **donation is dated 1993**, ceremony **April 1993**. "Nearly 4,000 titles" is the physical collection; the **catalogue is 3,588 titles in 28 languages**. The file prints both.
- **CORRECTION, the congress ordinal. [A: RAE; Instituto Cervantes; Télam]** The March 2019 Córdoba event presenting the RAE/ASALE commemorative *Rayuela* was the **VIII** Congreso Internacional de la Lengua Española, not the VII; the VII was San Juan, 2016.
