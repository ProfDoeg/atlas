# Stephen King: research report

*2026-08-09. Slug `stephen_king`. The traveler is alive; the entry is open-ended (`years: "1947-"`) and the afterlife lens returns no tomb.*

**Method.** Four lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock) were run and adversarially verified. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated. Every hedge lives in `date_confidence`, never in the campa. Coordinate repairs are gathered in section 11. Unless another source is named, the authority is the English Wikipedia article on the subject.

**Legend:** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-account testimony.

**Why he is in the atlas.** He was queued out of the locked essay `pet_sematary.md`, which reads the 1983 novel against Operation Condor, 1970s cryptography, Nick Land, and Rudolf Steiner.

## 1. Parents and the moving years, 1939-1958

Nellie Ruth Pillsbury marries Donald Edwin King at **Scarborough, Maine, 23 July 1939** [A]. Donald is a merchant seaman, born in Indiana under the surname **Pollock** [A]. **Stephen Edwin King is born 21 September 1947** at Maine General Hospital, Portland, the second son; brother David was adopted two years earlier [A]. Donald **abandons the family c. 1949**, no month and no place recorded, and Wikipedia notes he was still living [A]. *Claim corrected:* the geography lens wrote that Ruth moved the boys in with "her late husband's relatives" at Fort Wayne. He was not late.

The moves of **1949-1958** run Chicago; Croton-on-Hudson NY; **West De Pere WI** (44.4469, -88.0687); **Fort Wayne IN** (41.0793, -85.1394); Malden MA; **Stratford CT** (41.1845, -73.1332) [A]. Every date inside that range is **[R]**: no months are given and the sequence is reconstructed. At Stratford a friend is **struck and killed by a train**; King says he was present, has no memory of it, and that his family confirmed it **[R: his account in *On Writing*, doubted by some biographers]**. In **1958** the family settles at **Durham, Maine** [A].

## 2. Schools and first publication, 1958-1966

**Durham Elementary School, 1958-1962** [A]: first stories, mimeographed and sold to classmates until a teacher intervenes; brother David runs *Dave's Rag* on the same machine. High school at **Lisbon Falls** (44.0064, -70.0658), **1962-1966**, editing *The Village Vomit* [A]. In **1965** "I Was a Teenage Grave Robber" is serialized in *Comics Review*, his first independently published work [A].

- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** The chronology lens names the school **Lisbon High School**, the geography lens **Lisbon Falls High School**; both forms circulate. The journey stop is titled by the town and the campa prints the disagreement.

**Worumbo Mill, Lisbon Falls** (44.0006, -70.0714), the summer job behind "Graveyard Shift," is **[R]**. *Date corrected, tag lowered:* the lens dated it **c.1965** citing Wikipedia, but the article on "Graveyard Shift" carries **no background section**, so the citation supports nothing. King's own accounts place the work around **1969-1970**; the journey uses 1969 with an explicit hedge. This is the entry's weakest date.

## 3. University of Maine, 1966-1970

Enters **UMaine at Orono** (44.9016, -68.6686) on scholarship in **1966**: English, a student-paper column, anti-Vietnam-War protests, and two mentors, **Edward Holmes** and **Burton Hatlen** [A]. In **1967** he sells "The Glass Floor" to *Startling Mystery Stories*, his first professional sale [A]. He meets **Tabitha Spruce at the Raymond H. Fogler Library** (44.9012, -68.6683), where she holds a work-study job, in 1969 **[A: Wikipedia, "Tabitha King"]**. **1970**: BA in English with teaching certificate; daughter **Naomi Rachel King** born [A].

- **CONTRADICTION.** The chronology lens placed the meeting in **Hatlen's writing workshop**. Both the Tabitha King and Stephen King articles place it at **Fogler Library**. The journey uses the library and prints the disagreement.

## 4. The lean years and Carrie, 1970-1974

**New Franklin Laundry, Bangor** is **[R: King's interviews and *On Writing*]**. *Source failure recorded:* the lens cited Wikipedia's Stephen King article, which does not mention the laundry; the job is attested in his own testimony, so it is kept at [R] with a generic downtown pin. Marriage **2 January 1971** near Orono (44.8832, -68.6706) **[R: "Tabitha King"; no civil record consulted]**. **1971-1973**: teaches at **Hampden Academy** (44.7473, -68.8398) [A]. **1972-1973**: a rented **trailer at Hermon** (44.7973, -68.8862), where *Carrie* is drafted in the laundry room and **Tabitha retrieves the discarded pages from the wastebasket** **[A: "Carrie (novel)"]**.

**November 1973**: **Bill Thompson** accepts the manuscript at **Doubleday**, raising the advance from **$1,500 to $2,500**; **New American Library** later pays **$400,000** for paperback rights, **half of it King's** [A]. *Figure corrected:* the lens wrote "rights later sell for $200,000," which is King's share, not the sale. **18 December 1973**: **Nellie Ruth Pillsbury King dies of uterine cancer** at Durham, before *Carrie* reaches print [A]. **5 April 1974**: *Carrie* published, first printing **30,000 copies**; De Palma's film follows in **1976** [A].

- **CONTRADICTION.** The chronology lens bundles the mother's death into a **1975** entry beside *'Salem's Lot*, following Wikipedia's loose phrasing ("around the time 'Salem's Lot was published"). The geography lens dates it **1973-12**, and the publication calendar agrees. Both readings appear in the campa; 1973 is the stop date.
- A brief residence at **Sanford, Maine**, 1973-1974 **[R, address unconfirmed]**, was **dropped from the journey** for want of a confirmable anchor.

## 5. Colorado, 1974-1975

After the mother's death the family moves to **Boulder** (40.015, -105.2705) [A]. *Date corrected:* the chronology lens said 1975; Wikipedia reads "After his mother's death, King and his family moved to Boulder," i.e. **1974**. On **30 October 1974** the Kings stay at **The Stanley Hotel, Estes Park** (40.3821, -105.5217), **room 217**, the night before it closes for the season, nearly the only guests; a claw-foot tub, empty corridors, and a dream of his son pursued by a fire hose produce *The Shining* **[A: "The Shining (novel)"]**. In **1975** they return east to **Auburn** (44.0979, -70.2312), where *The Stand* is completed, and on **17 October 1975** *'Salem's Lot* is published [A].

- **QUOTE FABRICATION CAUGHT.** The lens carried as first-person speech: *"I pulled back the pink curtain for the tub, which had claw feet, and thought, what if somebody died here?"* No such King quotation exists. Wikipedia's text is third-person narration containing only the fragment **"What if somebody died here? At that moment, I knew I had a book."** The journey quotes that fragment alone, with provenance in `quote_source`. The fire-hose dream is separately attested.

## 6. Orono, Orrington, Pet Sematary, 1977-1983

**1977-1979**: **writer-in-residence at UMaine**; *The Shining* published 1977; third child **Owen Philip King** born; a short residence in England [A]. In **1979** the family rents at **Orrington** beside a heavily trucked road; his daughter's cat is killed on it and buried in a **pet cemetery cut by local children**, the hand-lettered sign carrying the misspelling; his son runs toward the road and is caught; the **first draft is finished May 1979** and King locks the manuscript away **[A: "Pet Sematary"]**. The 1989 film's pet cemetery was shot near **Ellsworth**, not at the real site; the two should not be conflated. **1979** also brings *The Dead Zone*, the first **Castle Rock** novel [A].

*Pet Sematary* is published **14 November 1983**, Doubleday, 374 pages, with a **1984 World Fantasy Award nomination**; the French title is **Simetierre** [A]. *Tag raised:* the afterlife lens had *Simetierre* at [R] as fan-reported; it is stated in the English article, so **[A]**.

## 7. Bangor, 1980-1992

**1980**: King buys the **William Arnold House** on West Broadway and adds the **wrought-iron fence with bat and spider-web motif** himself **[A: Wikipedia, "Bangor, Maine"]**, verbatim: *"The William Arnold House of 1856, an Italianate style mansion and home to author Stephen King. Its wrought-iron fence ... is King's own addition."*

- **CONTRADICTION, printed.** The geography lens dates the house **1858**, the afterlife lens and the article body **1856**. An 1858 photo caption in the same article is the likely source of the error; body text governs, and the journey prints the disagreement.
- *Address tag raised:* the afterlife lens carried **47 West Broadway** at [R] as fan and tourism sourcing. Nominatim's record is cadastral ("Stephen King House, 47, West Broadway, Whitney Park Historic District, Bangor"), so **[A]**. Its asserted **NRHP listing** is nowhere in the Bangor article and stands uncited.

**1980-1985**: King researches Bangor's Victorian storm drains and canal and builds **Derry** on the city he lives in; ***It* is published 15 September 1986**, Viking, five years' writing begun 1980, from an idea traced to a footbridge crossed in **Boulder in 1978** **[A: "It (novel)"]**. **1985**: *Skeleton Crew*, and the **Richard Bachman** pseudonym exposed by bookstore clerk **Steve Brown** [A]. **c. 1987**: **Tabitha King stages an intervention**, treatment completed **1988** **[R]**; *The Eyes of the Dragon* and *Misery* published 1987; a UMaine honorary doctorate, year cited variably **[R]**. **1989**: Mary Lambert films *Pet Sematary*, production at **Ellsworth**, Creed house at **Hancock**, graveyard at **Mount Hope Cemetery, Bangor** (founded 1834), the Mi'kmaq ground near **Sedgwick**, the approach a quarry on **Mount Desert Island** [A]. **1990**: *Four Past Midnight*. **1991**: *Needful Things*, the first novel written sober. **1992**: *Gerald's Game*, *Dolores Claiborne* [A].

- **CONTRADICTION.** The chronology lens dates *It* to **1987**, the year it won the British Fantasy Award (the August Derleth); it collapsed award year into publication year. The geography lens has 1986-09-15 right.
- *Two errors removed.* The chronology lens put the **Creepshow screenwriting debut** at 1985; Wikipedia states verbatim **Creepshow (1982)**. It also put the **miniseries of *The Stand*** at 1990; Wikipedia links **"The Stand (1994 miniseries)."** Both were dropped rather than carried wrongly.

## 8. The accident, On Writing, and the late honors, 1994-2024

**1994**: *The Shawshank Redemption* filmed; "The Man in the Black Suit" in *The New Yorker*'s Halloween issue, taking the **O. Henry Award** in **1996**, the year *The Green Mile* appears as a six-part serial [A]. *Bag of Bones* is published **22 September 1998**, his **first book with Scribner** [A]; *dates corrected:* the chronology lens put its Bram Stoker and British Fantasy awards in the publication year, and both are **1999**.

On **19 June 1999** King is **struck by a van on Maine State Route 5** near his Lovell house; the driver, **Bryan Smith**, lost control of a **Dodge Caravan** reaching for a loose dog; collapsed lung, shattered hip, multiple leg fractures, years of surgery [A]. The **Kezar Lake house at Lovell** is **[R]**, as is the account of recovery there through 1999-2000 in short and painful daily sessions **[R: *On Writing*]**. **2000**: ***On Writing*** (Bram Stoker and Locus awards for nonfiction) and **Riding the Bullet**, an early e-book downloaded **more than 500,000 times** [A].

**19 November 2003**: the **Medal for Distinguished Contribution to American Letters** at the National Book Awards [A]; the **venue** is **[R]**. The winter home on **Casey Key, Florida** is **[R]**, tag lowered because the Casey Key article does not mention King and the residence rests on journalism. **6 June 2005**: reading and signing at the **Harvard Book Store**, Cambridge [A]. **7 December 2012**: a talk at **UMass Lowell** **[R]**, kept here but **dropped from the journey** as the thinnest of the late appearances. **10 September 2015**: the **National Medal of Arts** conferred by President Obama **[A: NEA and White House records]**.

**WZON and WKIT** are owned by Zone Radio Corporation, "a company owned by Bangor resident novelist Stephen King" (verbatim, [A]), and **Tabitha King served on the Bangor Public Library board** ([A]); the claim that *Stephen* King is a longtime library patron and donor is **not in the cited source** and is carried **[R]**, as is the **Stephen and Tabitha King Foundation**. Late books [A]: **2009** *Under the Dome*, **2011** *11/22/63*, **2023** *Holly*, **May 2024** *You Like It Darker* at number one. Adaptations [A]: ***It*** (2017); **Castle Rock** (Hulu, 2018-2019), a shared universe across King's Maine towns; *Pet Sematary* refilmed 2019, prequel **Bloodlines** 2023.

- **CONTRADICTION, printed and not collapsed.** The chronology lens dates the arts medal **2014**, the geography lens the ceremony **2015-09-10**. Both are right about different things: the 2014 award class was conferred at a September 2015 ceremony. The journey states both.

## 9. Quotes

Verified verbatim against the Goodreads author-quotes page (3389.Stephen_King) and, where noted, Wikiquote wikitext. Confirmed **[A]**: four *On Writing* lines ("Books are a uniquely portable magic," "The road to hell is paved with adverbs," "The scariest moment is always just before you start," "Writing is not life, but I think that sometimes it can be a way back to life"), plus "Sometimes dead is better" (*Pet Sematary*), "Grownups are the real monsters, he thought" (*It*), "Get busy living or get busy dying" (*Different Seasons*), and the opening of *The Gunslinger*. Two corrections:

1. ***Bag of Bones* misquoted twice over.** The pool carried "Humor is almost always anger with its make-up on." Verbatim, per Wikiquote wikitext (ch. 8, p. 148) and Goodreads: **"Humor is almost always anger with its makeup on, I think, but in little towns the makeup tends to be thin."** *Makeup* is one word, and the pool cut the sentence in half. The journey uses the full sentence.
2. **The Guardian quote pinned and extended.** The pool carried, at [R], "...Pet Sematary was appalling," from "a Wikiquote summary." Wikiquote's wikitext carries the real citation, **Tim Adams, "Interview: Stephen King," *The Guardian*, 14 September 2000**, and the sentence continues: **"...was appalling when it first came out on to the page."** The Guardian domain refused all fetches, so the article was **not read at primary**; the flag drops from "unverified" to that, and `quote_source` says so.

## 12. Open gaps

1. The **Worumbo Mill summer** is dated by nothing reachable; c.1969-1970 rests on King's recollection alone.
2. **No street address for Durham Elementary School** (the stop uses the town coordinate); the **Sanford residence (1973-1974)** could not be confirmed and was dropped; the **Casey Key purchase**, the **Kezar Lake acquisition** and the **2003 National Book Awards venue** are undated.
3. The **NRHP listing** of the William Arnold House is asserted without a supporting citation.
4. The **Orrington pet cemetery's survival is contested**: fan travelogues and local reporting disagree on whether the original sign and ground persist. Not resolved.
5. **No public monument or statue to King** is documented in anything reached. Bangor commemorates him through his fiction, Derry and the Standpipe, not through plaques. A gap, not a finding.
6. **He is alive.** The afterlife lens is structurally inapplicable: the atlas's standard closing move, the tomb, does not exist. The entry closes on a living author at work and says so.
