# Dalila Puzzovio: research report
*Argentine pop artist, Instituto Di Tella scene, Buenos Aires. Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject living as of every source consulted.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, with the source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference (including inference from date and place proximity). Contradictions between sources are printed side by side and left open; nothing below is adjudicated silently.

**Method.** The reachable evidence base is thin and lopsided. Two Wikipedia editions carry almost the entire skeleton, and they disagree on the birth year, on the venue and date of Cáscaras, and on the rank of both Di Tella prizes. Around that skeleton sit three independent texts: Felisa Pinto's 2008 Página/12 profile, Georgina Lacube's 2009 El Litoral interview, and Laura Podalsky's 2004 monograph, quoted at second hand. Every coordinate was re-derived from a street address rather than inherited from the first pass, which had placed Galería Lirolay 1.3 km from where it stood and MAMBA in the wrong neighbourhood.

---

## 1. Origins: Lecce, and a birth two years wide

**Born in Buenos Aires [A: EN Wikipedia, ES Wikipedia, Wikidata Q5797644, Getty ULAN].** The city is not in dispute. The year is.

**Birth year contradiction, not resolved.** EN and Wikidata Q5797644 give 1943 (Wikidata's "1 January 1943" is a year-precision placeholder, not a recorded day). ES gives 1945 uniformly: infobox `{{fecha|1945}}`, lead "(Buenos Aires, 1945)", persondata `{{NF|1945||Puzzovio, Dalila}}`. The earlier pass called this an internal ES split between prose and infobox; that is wrong, ES is consistent. The real split is EN/Wikidata 1943 against ES 1945. A third variant, 1942, was attributed by the first pass to a "MAMBA catalog"; no such entry was locatable, and it is **dropped as unverified**.

**No hospital, no neighbourhood, no day, no month** is recorded anywhere. **[gap]**

**Italian parentage from Lecce [A: ES only].** ES has "hija de italianos oriundos de Lecce". EN says nothing of Lecce and carries only Category:Argentine people of Italian descent. No emigration year, port, or ship is recorded, and **no source places Dalila herself in Italy [gap]**. Lecce appears in the journey file as the family's point of departure, tagged inheritance rather than travel.

**Encouraged to draw from about age twelve [R: EN, unsourced].** Corroborated in substance, not in date, by her own recollection in Lacube 2009: drawing on the house windows, cutting stamps from erasers, altering her bedroom wallpaper with ink.

## 2. Formation: an apprenticeship the sources disclaim

The first pass tagged the Batlle Planas and Davidovich tutelage **[A]**. That is not defensible, and it is corrected here to **[R]**. EN Wikipedia states the opposite of attestation:

> "Although there is no documentation of formal education, her artistic vocation developed under the mentorship of two artists: surrealist Juan Batlle Planas and conceptual artist Jaime Davidovich."

A source that explicitly disclaims documentation cannot carry an [A]. ES gives the bare sentence "Estudió con Juan Batlle Planas y Jaime Davidovich" with no dates; EN's endpoint "up until 1961" is EN's alone.

**Independent doubt on Davidovich.** Born 1936, roughly seven years Puzzovio's senior, known as a video and installation artist, emigrated to New York in 1963. As a named teacher of a pre-1961 apprenticeship he is chronologically strained. Flagged, not resolved.

**No studio address exists for either man [gap].** The first pass gave a stop called "Studio of Juan Batlle Planas" at -34.6037, -58.3816, which is simply the city centroid. That is false precision; the journey file names the stop for the city and says so.

## 3. Debut and the shells, 1961 to 1964

**First exhibition, Galería Lirolay, 1961 [A: EN and ES Wikipedia].**

**Coordinate correction.** Galería Lirolay was at **Esmeralda 868, Retiro** (moved to Paraguay 794 only in 1969; founded 1960 by Mario and Paulette Fano, closed 1981). It was never on Uruguay Street, as the first pass had it. Nominatim geocodes Esmeralda 868 to **-34.59807, -58.37839**, about 1.3 km east of the first pass's -34.5975, -58.3922.

**Meets Carlos "Charlie" Squirru, 1962 [A: EN and ES].** ES: "a quien conoció en 1962". EN: "whom she had met in 1962". **1962 is the year they met, not the year they married.** No marriage date or place appears in either edition, in Wikidata Q5797644 (bare statement spouse: Carlos Maria Miguel Squirru), or anywhere reachable **[gap]**. The detail that they were "married by an astrologer" is in **none** of these sources and is dropped as unsupported.

**Lirolay again, 1963, curated by Germaine Derbecq [A: ES Wikipedia],** who ES also identifies as the wife of the sculptor Pablo Curatella Manes.

**Cáscaras contradiction, not resolved.** ES and EN describe two different events, and the first research pass collapsed them into one.
- **ES**: a Derbecq-curated return show at Lirolay in **1963**, and separately "en 1964 realizó su exposición Cáscaras en el Museo de Arte Moderno porteño curada por Rafael Squirru."
- **EN**: the Cáscaras / orthopedic-cast collaboration **with Derbecq**, dated **1963**, venue unspecified.

So the two editions disagree on year, curator, and by implication venue. The first pass "inferred" Lirolay as the Cáscaras venue, which runs against the only source that names one at all. The journey file carries both readings as separate stops and says which is which.

**The material itself [A: EN and ES].** Discarded orthopedic plaster casts as sculptural form, read as figures of absence. Her own account, in Pinto 2008: a tortured, documentary material of suffering given a hedonistic side, with Squirru supplying the black and acid humour of the titles.

## 4. The Institute on Florida Street, 1964 to 1968

**Coordinate correction.** The Di Tella Centro de Artes Visuales was at **Florida 936**, which Nominatim geocodes to **-34.59720, -58.37546**. The first pass used -34.5959, -58.3754, about 145 m too far north, at four stops; all four are corrected.

**The Romero Brest circle [A as to membership: ES; R as to date].** ES lists the nucleus as including Berni, Jorge de la Vega, Distéfano, León Ferrari, Kosice, Alberto Greco, Delia Cancela, Pablo Mesejean, Le Parc, Marta Minujín, Luis Wells and Federico Peralta Ramos. **ES gives no year.** The first pass's 1964 is an inference, tagged [R]. Likewise "1965-1970" is the institute's general peak, not a documented span for Puzzovio.

**New Art of Argentina, 1964 [A: EN, citing the Walker Art Center / Di Tella catalogue].** Roughly thirty Argentine artists, shown in Minneapolis and at Di Tella. **Whether Puzzovio personally travelled to Minneapolis is not confirmed [gap];** the journey file tags the Walker stop as a catalogue appearance, not an attested presence.

**Coronas para los habitantes no humanos [A: EN Wikipedia],** white and black cast works on mortality, given the span 1964 to 1998.

## 5. The billboard, 1965

**Attested by both editions [A].** At the corner of Florida and Viamonte, Puzzovio, Charlie Squirru and Edgardo Giménez erect an advertising hoarding carrying their own three portraits under the line **¿Por qué son tan geniales?**

**Wording confirmed verbatim in two independent places.** EN (citing Marina Alejandra Wabnik, "Identidad Gráfica Argentina", 2011, p. 35) and ES: "el cartel publicitario en la esquina de las calles Florida y Viamonte en 1965, con su retrato, el de Squirru y el de Edgardo Giménez debajo de la pregunta: ¿Por qué son tan geniales?"

**Correction to the English gloss.** "son" is third person plural. The line reads **"Why are they so brilliant?"**, not "why are you/we such geniuses". The first pass's gloss and EN's own loose rendering are both wrong.

**Citation withdrawn.** Herrera, *Pop!* (2010), p. 5 was not reachable and is not asserted for the billboard text; cite Wabnik or ES instead.

**What became of the physical billboard after its display is recorded nowhere [gap].**

## 6. Autorretrato en marquesina, and two prizes whose rank is contested

**The work [A: EN, citing Herrera, *Pop!*, 2010, pp. 25-26].** A painting, 223 x 365.5 x 100, executed by artisan cinema-billboard painters, which is why ES titles it *Autorretrato en marquesina*. EN dates it **1966-1967**, not 1966. Herrera as quoted by EN: the tanned body of the model Veruschka on sand in a jewelled bikini, her face replaced by Puzzovio's own. The first pass flagged the Veruschka detail as uncorroborated; it carries a page-level citation and is upgraded to **[A, at second hand through EN]**.

**Prize contradiction, not resolved.** Three readings are in play:
1. **ES prose**: "Su Autorretrato en marquesina le valió el Gran Premio Nacional 1966" (grand national prize).
2. **ES awards list**, in the same article: "1966, 2.º Premio Nacional Di Tella" (**second** national prize).
3. **EN**: "her first Premio Nacional Di Tella", with no year given separately from the work.

ES therefore contradicts itself, and EN offers a third framing. Left open.

**The 1968 "second Premio Nacional" is the weakest claim in the dossier and is not carried as [A].** ES's awards list contains **no 1968 prize at all**; it lists "1967, 2.º Premio Internacional Di Tella", an **international** second prize. EN's 1968 national prize rests entirely on its reference ':4', rendered in the article itself as "LA PLATAFORMA, INFITA DE, and DALILA PUZZOVIO. 'TRABAJO PRÁCTICO FINAL.'", an unpublished student assignment. The first pass's worry about an "unexplained one-year gap" dissolves once the ES reading is preferred, but the journey file states both and prefers neither.

**Premio Bienal de Lima, 1966 [A: ES only].** "1966, Premio Bienal de Lima, Perú." EN never mentions Lima. Venue and personal attendance are unconfirmed **[gap]**; the coordinates are the city centroid, an explicit placeholder.

## 7. Dalila Doble Plataforma, 1967

**[A: EN].** Forty pairs of women's platform shoes shown in glass display cubicles, then exhibited and sold in commercial shoe stores. **[R: EN]** The retailer is given as Grimoldi; **no branch address is confirmed [gap]**, and the Florida Street placement is reconstructed from the brand's period presence there.

ES dates the double-platform shoe with no year ("Creó el zapato de doble plataforma"), and the furniture-and-shoe collaboration with Squirru with no year either. **The first pass's "1965" for that collaboration is unsupported by either edition and is dropped.**

Her own framing, Lacube 2009: "Creo que las grandes afirmaciones necesitan grandes manifestaciones, aquí sí la idea fue convertir la obra de arte en una experiencia de consumo."

Also **[A: EN and ES]**: costumes for *Help Valentino*, 1966. EN gives the endpoint of the fashion-design period as **1985**, not the early 1980s: "Until 1985, she focused on developing herself as a fashion designer by making costumes for film and theater."

**Coordinate corrections.** Teatro General San Martín, Av. Corrientes 1530, geocodes to **-34.60425, -58.38840**, about 430 m from the first pass's figure; the same correction applies to the 1964 MAMBA stop, since the museum was then in that complex. The Teatro Cómico stood at Av. Corrientes 1280, later Teatro Lola Membrives, approximately **-34.6040, -58.3860**. Hotel Bauen, Av. Callao 360, geocodes to **-34.60512, -58.39246**. The Teatro Estrellas address was **not** verifiable and stays an explicit Corrientes-district placeholder **[gap]**.

**Correction of fact.** The Hotel Bauen was recuperated after 2001 by a workers' cooperative as a **hotel**, not a "factory".

## 9. Interiors, Vogue, Híbridos, 1980 to 1998

**Vogue [R: EN, undated and uncited].** EN: "Until the beginning of the 1990s, she also contributed to popular magazines such as Vogue, where she completed tasks as a writer, editor, and illustrator." The Vogue work therefore ends at the **start** of the 1990s; only the interior and architectural advice is placed across the 1980s and 1990s. **Which Vogue is nowhere specified [gap]**; EN says only "popular magazines such as Vogue".

**Premio por la casa mejor reciclada, Museo de la Ciudad de Buenos Aires, 1992 [A: ES awards list].** The first pass recorded year and institution but claimed the prize was unnamed; ES names it. A best-renovated-house award, which independently anchors the interiors strand of these decades.

**Coronas para los habitantes no humanos concludes, 1998 [A: EN].** **Híbridos, digital photographic collage, from 1998 [A: EN].** No studio address, exhibition history, or image list is given for either **[gap]**.

## 10. Rediscovery and honours

**El Deslumbre, arteBA, 2011 [A as to fair and content; R as to venue].** EN's only citation is an arteBA page titled "Proyecto Dalila Puzzovio / PATIO BULLRICH", which points at a Patio Bullrich-linked project rather than confirming La Rural's pavilions. Confirmed: the year, the fair, the recreation of the 1960s shoe-store display environment, and audience participation on a catwalk. **The pavilion is inferred and tagged [R];** La Rural's Predio Ferial, Av. Sarmiento 2704, is approximately -34.5786, -58.4166.

**Personalidad Destacada de la Cultura, 2019 [A: ES, citing a Legislatura porteña statement].** Wikidata lists the honour without a date.

**Collections [A, partially].** EN names the Centro de Artes Visuales del Instituto Di Tella and MAMBA; Wikidata separately names the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes. **The two sets do not overlap and neither was checked against a museum catalogue [gap].** EN also cites Graciela Melgarejo crediting her with opening a path later Argentine artists followed.

**Coordinate correction.** MAMBA's current building is at **Av. San Juan 350, San Telmo**, not La Boca. Nominatim and the museum's own site agree at **-34.62207, -58.37075**; the first pass's -34.6266, -58.3639 is 840 m southeast, in Barracas.

## 11. Afterlife of a living artist

**No death date appears in EN Wikipedia, ES Wikipedia, Wikidata Q5797644, or Getty ULAN.** EN carries Category:Living people and "She currently works and resides in Buenos Aires". **There is no tomb, no grave, no monument, no memorial [A by absence].** Her afterlife, in the atlas's sense, is rediscovery while living.

**Citation withdrawn.** The first pass cited "MAMBA press materials 2025" for her being alive and for a 2025 retrospective. No such material was locatable, and museomoderno.org shows no 2025 Puzzovio cycle. The living claim stands on EN Wikipedia and Wikidata's absence of a death statement; **the 2025 retrospective is dropped.**

## Contradictions carried open into the journey file

1. **Birth year**: EN/Wikidata 1943 vs ES 1945 (uniform across ES infobox, lead, persondata). Not resolved. A "1942" variant is dropped as untraceable.
2. **Cáscaras**: EN 1963, with Derbecq, venue unstated vs ES 1964, Museo de Arte Moderno, curated by Rafael Squirru, with a separate Derbecq-curated Lirolay show in 1963.
3. **The 1966 prize**: ES prose "Gran Premio Nacional" vs ES's own list "2.º Premio Nacional" vs EN "her first Premio Nacional". Not resolved.
4. **The second prize**: ES "1967, 2.º Premio Internacional Di Tella" vs EN "second Premio Nacional, 1968" sourced to a student assignment. Not resolved; the weakness of EN's citation is stated in place.
5. **Autorretrato date**: EN 1966-1967 vs ES 1966 (via the prize year).
6. **Batlle Planas and Davidovich**: attested as mentors in both editions, disclaimed as documented in EN's own sentence, and chronologically strained in Davidovich's case.
