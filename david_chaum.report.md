## 2. Berkeley, 1979 to 1983

- **PhD, Berkeley, 1982 [A: chaum.com].** **Contradiction, unresolved:** bibliographies date the dissertation both April and June 1982. June orders the segment; the disagreement is recorded in the stop.
- **CRYPTO '82, 'Blind Signatures for Untraceable Payments' [A: Wikipedia; Advances in Cryptology 82:199-203]. IACR organized at Chaum's initiative at the same meeting [A: Wikipedia, 'IACR', 'CRYPTO'],** CRYPTO '83 its first sponsored conference [A]. ## 3. New York and Amsterdam, 1984 to 1989

- **Taught at NYU Graduate School of Business Administration [A: chaum.com biography].** **No years given by any source**; 1984 is an ordering placement. **Coordinate correction, and it matters:** the dossier pinned 40.7295/-73.9965, Tisch Hall on Washington Square, which in the 1980s housed the *undergraduate* business college. - **Founded and led the CWI cryptography group [A: chaum.com; Wikipedia, 'CWI', which records DigiCash as a 1990 spinoff].** **No founding date published**; 1984 orders the segment. **Pin inherited byte-identical** from `guido_van_rossum.journey.json`: Kruislaan 413, **52.3563/4.9528**.

The surveillance sentence was a mid-sentence fragment dressed as a whole sentence, carried whole in `suggested_refs` instead. The "tracing information" sentence was truncated in a way that *widened* the claim, and the "mutually advantageous" sentence dropped a clause; neither is used.
- **EUROCRYPT '87 proceedings co-edited with W. L. Price (Springer, 1988) [R].** A Wikipedia page for it returned 404; the Amsterdam host city is **not confirmed**. Pinned to the city coordinate, not a venue.
- **'The Dining Cryptographers Problem', Journal of Cryptology 1(1):65-75, 1988 [A].** Wikipedia records that Chaum first thought about it in the early 1980s, so the concept precedes the paper; the stop is dated to publication and says so.
- **Chaum, Crepeau and Damgard, 'Multiparty unconditionally secure protocols', STOC 1988 [A].** **Dossier contradiction resolved against the dossier's own preference:** it dated the award to 2018, attributed to chaum.com/awards/, reasoning "30 years from 1988". **sigact.org lists STOC Test of Time winners only from 2021 onward** and places this paper in the 2021 30-year category; **chaum.com/awards/ gives no year at all.** File uses **2021**.

## 7. Reception, legend, and what is not a stop

Chaum is alive: no tomb, no estate, no posthumous rediscovery, so the ordinary afterlife lens does not apply.

- **Sherman, Javani, Zhang and Golaszewski, 'On the Origins and Variations of Blockchain Technologies', IEEE Security & Privacy (2019)** reassesses the 1982 vault design as blockchain-adjacent, and **chaum.com/publications/ cross-links the two itself** [A]. A later scholar's engagement with Chaum's object, so it is a source and `suggested_ref` on his own Berkeley stops and **not staged as a scene**. **The "first blockchain" legend [A as a phenomenon]** is that reassessment inflated: no proof of work, no permissionless Sybil resistance, no native monetary unit, no citation link to Nakamoto.
- **The "Chaum is Satoshi" rumor [R, unverifiable].** The dossier asserts it is widespread; **no primary instance could be located or fetched.** **It is not in the journey file.** The checkable negative that is carried, in the Osaka campa: Bitcoin's white paper does not cite him.
- **No full-length biography, memoir or autobiography exists [A as documented absence].** Substantial treatments: Levy, *Crypto* (2001); Blanchette (2012); Brunton, *Digital Cash* (2019); van Wirdum (2024).
