# Simone de Beauvoir (1908-1986): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-18 for `simone_de_beauvoir.journey.json`. 44 stops, 9 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-sourced
claim carried with its status showing. Contradictions are flagged, never adjudicated.
Corrections to the gathered pool are listed in section 9 with the source that overturns each.
Coordinates come from Nominatim where the pool supplied a corrected geocode, and from existing
atlas files where the interlock lens found a canonical pin.

---

## 1. Montparnasse and the Cours Désir, 1908-1925

Born **9 January 1908** at 103 Boulevard du Montparnasse, sixth arrondissement, to Georges de
Beauvoir and Françoise Brasseur [A: en/fr Wikipedia, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "SEP"
below]. Sister Hélène, "Poupette," born 6 June 1910 [A: en]. The family fortune goes after the
First World War and they move in **1919** to a fifth-floor walk-up on the rue de Rennes with no
elevator, heating, running water or bathroom [A: en, SEP]. She rejects Catholicism around
fourteen, c. 1922 [R: SEP calls it a memoir reconstruction and gives no date; placed at the same
address for order only].

Schooling at the **Institut Adeline Désir**, the Cours Désir, from early childhood to the
baccalauréat [R for the site: school confirmed, no street number verifiable, coordinates
approximate to Saint-Germain]. Here she meets **Élisabeth Lacoin**, "Zaza."

## 2. The improvised degree and the Sorbonne, 1925-1929

Baccalauréat in mathematics and philosophy at seventeen, **1925** [A: SEP, en]. Mathematics at
the **Institut Catholique de Paris** 1925-26, literature and languages at the **Institut
Sainte-Marie de Neuilly** 1926-27 [A: fr]; the file folds Neuilly into the Institut Catholique
stop rather than staging a second scene. Diplôme thesis *Le concept chez Leibniz* under **Léon
Brunschvicg**, **1929** [A: en; fr gives the Sorbonne period as 1926-1928].

**Agrégation, July 1929, second in France behind Sartre, youngest to pass at that date** [A: SEP,
en]. Sorbonne coordinates inherited byte-identical from `claude_levi_strauss.journey.json`
(48.8484, 2.3437). **The pact, October 1929** [A for the pact and the quoted "Let's sign a
two-year lease," en; R for the bench outside the Louvre, memoir tradition, not sited].

**Élisabeth Lacoin dies 25 November 1929 at Sceaux** [A: fr, *Élisabeth Lacoin*]: born 25
December 1907 at Tours, died aged 21 of viral encephalitis, first symptoms 11 November,
hospitalised 13 November, Merleau-Ponty's marriage proposal 9 November.

**Olga Kosakiewicz belongs to Rouen, not Molière** [A: en, *Olga Kosakiewicz*]: Beauvoir's pupil
at the Lycée Jeanne d'Arc, introduced 1932, affair with Beauvoir 1934-35 and with Sartre 1935-37;
that article does not mention Molière at all. The Molière pupils were **Bianca Bienenfeld**
(later Lamblin; "In 1937, her teacher was Simone de Beauvoir") and **Natalie Sorokine** [A: en].
Sorokine is the reason the ministry withdraws her teaching licence in **1943**, over conduct
dated to 1939 [A: en]; the 1945 reinstatement stays [R] on en.wikipedia's own showing.

**Berlin 1934 [R, downgraded]**: Sartre had a year at the Institut français reading Husserl.
**Sartre mobilised 2 September 1939** [A], the day before the declaration of war; **which Paris
station is unknown [R]**, approximated to the Gare de l'Est on an assumption of eastern posting,
declared as such in the stop.

## 4. Occupation, Liberation, America, 1940-1948

**Café de Flore 1940-1944** [A for the association: en, *Café de Flore*; the claim that she wrote
there daily is consistent with that source rather than verbatim from it]. **Hôtel La Louisiane,
rue de Seine, 1943 onward** [A: fr]. **L'Invitée 1943; Pyrrhus et Cinéas 1944; Les Temps Modernes
founded with Sartre, Merleau-Ponty and others 1945; Le Sang des autres 1945** [A: en, SEP].
**Brasillach trial January 1945** [A: en]; her essay "Œil pour œil" is 1946 in the same source.
Éditions Gallimard, 5 rue Gaston-Gallimard, 75007, is pinned at Nominatim's **48.8570, 2.3286**
for every publication stop.

**The 1947 American tour** [A: SEP, four months January to May]. en.wikipedia, *America Day by
Day*, lists New York, Boston, Washington, Chicago, Virginia, Los Angeles, San Francisco, New
Mexico, Texas, Georgia, Florida, New Orleans and Niagara, and gives **no ordering and no dates at
all**. **Nelson Algren met in Chicago 1947** [A: en]. *Coordinate caveat:* the pin sits at 1958
West Evergreen Avenue (Nominatim 41.9063, -87.6764), Algren's **last** Chicago address, taken
around 1959, not his 1947 flat on Wabansia Avenue; the stop says so.

## 5. Le Deuxième Sexe, 1949, and after

Serialisation in Les Temps Modernes ahead of the volumes, mid-1949 [R: en, kept at the pool's
tag]. Published **1949 by Gallimard**, two volumes, 978 pages, and on the **Index Librorum
Prohibitorum** until the Index was abolished in 1966 [A: en, *The Second Sex*]. The 1953 Parshley
translation for Knopf was later criticised for extensive cuts and philosophical mistranslation.
**Claude Lanzmann from 1952; Les Mandarins and the Prix Goncourt 1954, dedicated to Nelson
Algren** [A: en].

## 6. China, Cuba, Algeria, 1955-1964

**11 bis rue Victor-Schœlcher, 1955-1986** [A: fr; coordinates corrected to 48.8357, 2.3305].
**China 1955; La Longue Marche 1957** [A: en, a surviving photograph placing the pair in Beijing;
SEP]. **Une mort très douce 1964** [A: en, SEP]. **Cuba 1960 [R, downgraded]**: en.wikipedia,
*Jean-Paul Sartre*, says only "He went to Cuba in the 1960s to meet Fidel Castro and spoke with
Ernesto Che Guevara", with no year and no city, and the Beauvoir caption gives no month.
**Djamila Boupacha** [A: en]: the *Le Monde* front page is **2 June 1960**, copies seized in
Algiers; the book with **Gisèle Halimi** is **1962**; Algeria essays from 1955.

## 7. Cairo, the manifesto years, 1967-1977

**Cairo, February 1967** [A: en, *Jean-Paul Sartre*, photo caption: "Sartre, Simone de Beauvoir
and Claude Lanzmann meeting President Gamal Abdel Nasser at his home in Cairo, February 1967"].

**Tel Aviv, 29 March 1967, received by Levi Eshkol [R], genuinely unresolved.** The claim rests
on a Wikimedia Commons caption attributed to the Israeli Government Press Office. The Levi Eshkol
Wikipedia article makes **no mention of Beauvoir**, and no news source could be reached. The
Cairo leg is well attested, which makes an Israel leg plausible without establishing it; the stop
is kept, tagged [R], and says the sources assert the meeting and do not show it.

**La Vieillesse 1970** [A]. **Manifesto of the 343, April 1971** [A: fr, "publié en avril 1971
par Le Nouvel Observateur"; en]; the magazine's 1971 offices were not located, so its coordinates
are an approximate central-Paris placement, declared in the stop. **Declares herself a feminist
in Le Nouvel Observateur, 1972**; **editorial director of Questions féministes from 1977** [A:
fr]. **Betty Friedan interview**, "Sex, Society, and the Female Dilemma," *The Saturday Review*,
pp. 12-21, **14 June 1975** [A: en raw wikitext]. **The 1977 petition on sexual acts with
minors** [A: en] is stated flat in the file, with no mitigation and no resonance drawn from it;
*gap:* the pool names no other signatories, so the file names none.

## 8. Adieux, 1980-1986

**Sylvie Le Bon adopted 1980** [A: en]: they **met in 1960**, Le Bon nineteen and Beauvoir
fifty-two, and the adoption made her sole heir and executor. Le Bon has publicly disputed the
claim of a sexual relationship, and the file asserts nothing beyond the meeting. **Sartre dies 15
April 1980 of pulmonary oedema; funeral 19 April, fifty thousand on the boulevard du
Montparnasse** [A: en]; Beauvoir's sedation at the hospital is her own account in *La Cérémonie
des adieux*, not independently re-verified. **La Cérémonie des adieux 1981; Legion of Honour
declined 1982** [A].

**Death, 14 April 1986, pneumonia, aged 78 [A on date, cause and age; place CONTESTED].** Not
adjudicated. fr.wikipedia puts the death at **11 bis de la rue Victor-Schœlcher**; es.wikipedia
gives the same address, "accompanied by her adopted daughter and Claude Lanzmann," with no
hospital; English obituaries (Guardian, LA Times, via the crawl) and the pool's geography lens
give **Hôpital Cochin**, 27 rue du Faubourg-Saint-Jacques (Nominatim 48.8370, 2.3402). The file
pins the death at the apartment, following the French and Spanish record, and its
`date_confidence` names the obituary claim, gives Cochin's coordinates, and leaves it open.

**Burial: division 20, Cimetière du Montparnasse, beside Sartre, wearing the silver ring with
Inca motifs given by Nelson Algren** [A: fr and es give the division and grave; fr gives the ring,
"l'anneau en argent aux motifs incas offert par son amant Nelson Algren au matin de leur première
nuit d'amour"]. **The burial date of 18 April 1986 is unsourced [R]**: en, fr, de and es all give
place and division, none gives a funeral date. The five thousand mourners and the headline
"Women, you owe her everything" come from period press summarised in the crawl, not re-fetched.

## 11. Interlocks named in campas

Named only where Beauvoir herself has a direct or one-directional relation, never for shared
geography.

- **`felix_guattari`, `michel_foucault`, `roland_barthes`, `gilles_deleuze`**: all four co-signed
  the **July 1977 Manifesto contro la repressione** with Beauvoir and Sartre, protesting the
  Italian crackdown after the killing of the student Francesco Lorusso.
  `felix_guattari.journey.json` carries the event as an attested stop at the canonical Paris pin
  48.8566 / 2.3522, dated 1977-07-01; the Beauvoir stop inherits both byte-identical. A documented
  co-signature, and the strongest interlock in the file.
- **`che_guevara`**: photographed with Beauvoir, Sartre and Antonio Núñez Jiménez in Cuba, 1960,
  which is a meeting and therefore hers to record. The Che file's Havana stop is dated 1960-03-05
  for Korda's photograph at the La Coubre funeral: same trip plausible, same day not established.
  The Korda photograph is **not** staged in Beauvoir's file, because it is Guevara's event.
- **`mao_zedong`**: one-directional and substantive. She travelled to the People's Republic in
  1955 and wrote *La Longue Marche* (1957) about his revolution. The Mao file's dated stops run
  1945-1950 with no 1955 entry, a gap in that file rather than a confirmed non-crossing.

## 12. Interlocks found and deliberately NOT named

- **`claude_levi_strauss`**: the pool's link is the same agrégation two years later, placed
  third, which is shared institution and not relation. His **Sorbonne pin is inherited** without
  his name appearing in any campa. *Operator note:* Beauvoir is widely held to have reviewed his
  *Les Structures élémentaires de la parenté* in Les Temps Modernes in 1949 and drawn on it in
  *Le Deuxième Sexe*. **Nothing in this pool attests it**, so it is not written in; sourced, it
  would be a genuine two-way interlock.
- **`victoria_ocampo`**: the link runs through Camus, whom both knew separately, and no direct
  Ocampo-Beauvoir meeting is attested. **`julio_cortazar`**: same Left Bank, same years, no
  relation. **`captain_alfred_dreyfus`**: the same cemetery, half a century apart; his pin is
  inherited but his name appears nowhere in the prose, a shared burial ground being exactly the
  co-location the map already shows.

## 13. Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found.** Beauvoir is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no relation in this pool
required applying her framework backward to events she never discussed or forward past 1986.
Every stop is anchored to a place she herself was, or to an object she herself made.

Three adjacent cases were checked and cleared rather than deleted, each handled the same way, as
a `suggested_ref` on the stop for the place or object that is Beauvoir's own:

1. **The Second Sex's reception history** (the Index, the Parshley cuts, the 2009 retranslation,
   Deirdre Bair 1990, Toril Moi 1994, Hazel Rowley 2005) concerns **her own object**, the book,
   and sits on the publication stop. No later scholar is staged as a scene.
2. **The Passerelle Simone-de-Beauvoir** (2006) and the **Prix Simone-de-Beauvoir** (Julia
   Kristeva, after the January 2008 centenary symposium; first laureates Taslima Nasreen and
   Ayaan Hirsi Ali) are posthumous commemorations built by other people and get **no stops**;
   they sit on the burial stop, the last place Beauvoir herself was.
3. **Bianca Lamblin's memoir** (*Mémoires d'une jeune fille dérangée*) is a one-directional later
   engagement with Beauvoir's conduct, and sits on the **Lycée Molière** stop, where the conduct
   happened.

## 14. Honest gaps

1. **The February 1948 Berlin trip** is attested by de.wikipedia and has no stop, because nothing
   in the pool says what happened on it.
2. **No street number** for the Cours Désir, and none for the rue de Rennes apartment; both
   coordinates are approximations, declared as such. **Le Nouvel Observateur's 1971 offices** and
   **Le Monde's 1960 offices** were likewise not located.
3. **The order of the 1947 American itinerary** is unrecoverable from the only source listing it.
4. **The Paris station of 2 September 1939** is unidentified, and **the Cuba trip's month** is
   unrecorded in any source reached.
5. **Beauvoir's funeral date** is unsourced; only the burial place and division are attested.
6. **No page or chapter** for the quotations from *The Blood of Others* or *Force of
   Circumstance*; both cited at book level.
7. **No atlas file exists for any Algerian War figure or for Djamila Boupacha herself.** A lead
   for the queue, not an interlock.
8. **The `mao_zedong` file's dated stops end at 1950-10-19.** Whether it simply does not yet
   cover 1955-1966, or whether no crossing exists, cannot be told from the file.

## 15. Contradictions flagged and left open

Place of death, apartment (fr, es) versus Hôpital Cochin (English obituaries); *Questions
féministes*, 1972 (en) versus 1977 (fr and the journal's own record); the November 2009
translation's publisher, Jonathan Cape (UK) versus Knopf, which is 2010; Berlin 1934, a single
crawl source versus de.wikipedia, which gives 1948 and no 1934; and the four Montparnasse
cemetery coordinates of section 10. In each case both readings are carried in the relevant stop's
`date_confidence`, and none is silently resolved.
