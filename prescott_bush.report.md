# Prescott Sheldon Bush (1895-1972): research report

*Atlas of Journeys working file, 2026-08-11, from the verified research pool for slug `prescott_bush`. Companion: `prescott_bush.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim carried only by a compiled secondary account. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated; unreachable sources are named with the reason. Everything contested about this life is contested about two things: a bank seized in 1942, and a skull.

## 1. Ohio and the schools, 1895-1917

Born in Columbus, Ohio, to Samuel Prescott Bush, a railroad and steel executive, and Flora Sheldon Bush **[A: Wikipedia, "Prescott Bush"]**. The article gives the year; the full date 15 May 1895 is the standard biographical date and is **not** in the fetched text **[R]**, hedged in the stop's `date_confidence`.

St. George's School, Middletown, Rhode Island, from 1908 **[A]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 41.533, -71.2864 falls 4.7 km north, in Portsmouth; the school is at 41.4911, -71.2733 **[A: Wikipedia, "St. George's School (Rhode Island)"]**.

Yale College from 1913; Skull and Bones, Zeta Psi, the Whiffenpoofs, president of the Glee Club, varsity golf and baseball **[A: Wikipedia]**. No source gives a tap date; the file places it in the senior spring of 1917 as a convention and says so **[R]**.

**Coordinate contradiction, the Skull and Bones tomb, unresolved.** Three pins for one building at 64 High Street circulate in the pool: 41.3083, -72.9279 (also the atlas's canonical New Haven pin), 41.3106, -72.9294, and 41.3086, -72.9279. Wikipedia's "Skull and Bones" gives the street address and **no coordinates**, so all three are derived **[R]**. The file splits by function rather than adjudicating: stops about **Yale the institution** carry the canonical **41.3083, -72.9279** byte-identical, as the interlock lens directs; stops about **the tomb** carry **41.3106, -72.9294**.

## 2. The war, 1917-1919

Field artillery captain, American Expeditionary Forces; intelligence training at Verdun; attached for a period to a staff of French officers; under fire in the Meuse-Argonne with the 158th Field Artillery Brigade **[A: Wikipedia]**. No month is given for Verdun, and the file's Meuse-Argonne date, 26 September 1918, is the offensive's opening day, **not** a date attached to Bush personally **[R for both dates]**. Discharged 1919; joins Simmons Hardware, St. Louis **[A]**.

**The Geronimo skull legend.** A 1918 letter from Bones member Winter Mead to F. Trubee Davison states that "the skull of the worthy Geronimo the Terrible, exhumed from its tomb at Fort Sill by your club... is now safe inside the [Tomb]" **[A as a quoted letter: Wikipedia, "Geronimo," citing Marc Wortman]**. That **Prescott Bush** was among the men who did it is **[R]**, disputed on three grounds carried into the file: Mead was not at Fort Sill and witnessed nothing, Geronimo's grave was unmarked in 1918, and Bush is in France for most of that year. Kitty Kelley and the Fort Sill Apache tribal chairman call it a hoax **[R]**. Carried as a legend labelled as one, since its afterlife is attested where its origin is not.

## 3. Marriage, rubber, and Greenwich, 1921-1925

Marries Dorothy Walker, 6 August 1921, Kennebunkport **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Two corrections at Walker's Point.** (1) *Coordinates.* The pool carried two pins for one estate, 43.3948, -70.4183 and 43.3939, -70.4134, roughly 7 km and 9 km off, the second in open Atlantic water; the estate is at 43.3433, -70.4594 **[A: Wikipedia, "Walker's Point Estate"]** and both stops now use it. (2) *Venue.* That the wedding took place here is **unsupported**: Wikipedia says George Herbert Walker bought the property in 1902 and that after the marriage "Walker built an additional smaller residence as a wedding gift" **[A]**. A gift, not a venue; the ceremony is generally placed at St. Ann's Episcopal Church **[R]**. The file states the gift and asserts no wedding site. The pool's "ancestral seat" phrasing is also loose: Walker bought it, Bush married into it.

Columbus and Hupp Products, 1923; president of sales for Stedman Products, South Braintree, November 1923 **[A]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 42.2223, -71.0023 sits 1.8 km north of Braintree centre, the opposite end of town from South Braintree; corrected to approximately 42.2065, -71.0022 **[A: Wikipedia, "Braintree, Massachusetts"]**.

George Herbert Walker Bush born at 173 Adams Street, Milton Hill, 12 June 1924 **[A: Wikipedia, "Milton, Massachusetts," which gives address and date]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 42.2495, -71.0821 lies southwest of town centre, nowhere near Milton Hill; Adams Street runs near 42.262, -71.055 **[A for the address, R for the pin]**.

**Date contradiction, the move to Greenwich, unresolved.** The chronology lens says 1925, with United States Rubber **[A: Wikipedia, "George H. W. Bush": "The Bush family moved to Greenwich, Connecticut, in 1925"]**; the geography lens says 1926 and attaches it to W. A. Harriman instead. The file uses 1925 and records the conflict. The geography lens's added detail that the house was "purchased with help from ... George Herbert Walker" is **not** in the cited article and is dropped rather than carried unsourced.

Children, from the family list in Wikipedia, "Prescott Bush," which gives **no birthplaces and, except for GHWB, no exact dates [R]**: Prescott Jr. (1922), George Herbert Walker (1924), Nancy (1926), Jonathan (1931), William H. T. "Bucky" (1938).

## 4. Wall Street and the Union Banking Corporation, 1926-1942

Vice-president of W. A. Harriman and Co., where his father-in-law is president, 1926; partner of Brown Brothers Harriman on the 1931 merger **[A]**.

**Director of the Union Banking Corporation. The date dispute is the load-bearing fact.** Wikipedia, "Prescott Bush," calls him "a founder and one of seven directors"; Wikipedia, "Union Banking Corporation," says UBC was "incorporated in 1924" and calls him "a founding member ... included among its seven directors" **[A as quotations of both]**. The chronology lens instead anchors the directorship at **1934**, where the Alien Property Custodian and Guardian documentary record puts it **[R for the anchor]**. **The contradiction:** Bush did not join W. A. Harriman until 1926, so he cannot literally have founded a 1924 corporation; either the "founder" language is wrong or the 1926 date is. **Not resolved.** The file uses 1934, writes the conflict into `date_confidence`, and drops "founding" from the campa. That he held **one of the corporation's 4,000 shares** is attested **[A: Wikipedia]**.

UBC's stock is held for the Bank voor Handel en Scheepvaart, Rotterdam, tied to Fritz Thyssen **[A: Wikipedia, "Union Banking Corporation"]**. **No source places Bush in Rotterdam [R; the file says so in the campa itself]**.

**The 1941 exposé, and a genuine source conflict.** The headline "Hitler's Angel has 3 millions in N.Y. Bank" is real; its newspaper is not settled. Wikipedia credits the **Washington Post**, footnoting an undated, unauthored PDF at fleshingoutskullandbones.com **[R, weak carrier]**. A full reproduction credits **M. J. Racusin, New York Herald Tribune, 30 July 1941**, opening "Hidden in the tides of economic warfare now surging over the world, the New York Herald Tribune has discovered that Fritz Thyssen..." **[A as a reproduction; original page not located]**. The Guardian of 25 September 2004 also credits the Herald-Tribune but renders it "Hitler's Angel Has $3m in US Bank" and dates it **1942** **[A as the Guardian's rendering]**. The file uses Racusin / Herald Tribune / 1941 as better supported **and prints all three variants in `quote_source`**, because silent harmonizing would destroy the only interesting thing about the discrepancy.

**The seizure: two documents, routinely conflated.** **5 October 1942** is the APC **investigation report**, which is what Wikipedia cites **[A]**; its conclusion is "Whether any or all part of the funds held by Union Banking Corporation, or companies associated with it, belong to Fritz Thyssen could not be established in this investigation" **[A]**. **Vesting Order No. 248**, generally dated 20 October 1942, is the instrument that executed the seizure **[R for the day; Wikipedia gives no seizure date, saying only "During World War II, under the U.S. Trading with the Enemy Act"]**. An APC memo of 18 August 1941 had already concluded "My investigation produced no evidence as to the ownership of this Dutch bank" **[A]**.

**Correction to the pool's afterlife lens.** That the seizure came "after an investigation confirms the Thyssen family's control of its assets" is contradicted by the document quoted above: the investigation expressly did **not** confirm control. The file says the government seized without having established ownership. Its APC coordinate, 38.8951, -77.0364, is a generic downtown Washington point, not a sourced office address **[R]**.

## 5. Greenwich, the clubs, the League, 1928-1947

Multi-year club champion at Round Hill Club **[A; no years given, R for the dates]**. USGA: "a member of the United States Golf Association, serving successively as secretary, vice-president and president, 1928-1935" **[A: Wikipedia, quoted entire]**. The pool's chronology lens called the article "internally inconsistent on USGA dates." **There is no conflict**: the span covers three offices in succession, the presidency last. The claimed inconsistency was a misreading of one sentence and is dropped.

**Greenwich Representative Town Meeting: a sourcing gap stated as a gap.** The pool labelled one entry "Second Selectman" while its claim text said "moderator of the Greenwich Representative Town Meeting"; these are different offices, and he held the **moderatorship [R]**. Wikipedia's "Prescott Bush" contains **no content whatever** on Greenwich town government, so the citation given cannot support the entry, and the general report of about seventeen years from the mid-1930s **[R]** also makes the pool's 1947/1950 window wrong. The file keeps the stop, labels it moderator, dates it 1935 as a placeholder, and says in both `date_confidence` and `sources` that the cited article does not support it.

American Birth Control League, 1942 **[A]**; treasurer of the first nationwide Planned Parenthood campaign, 1947 **[A]**; Connecticut Republican finance chairman, 1947 **[A: Wikipedia]**. The New York pins on the two birth-control stops are **inferred from headquarters, not sourced [R]**.

Yale Glee Club Associates founded 1937; Yale Corporation 1944-1956 **[A: Wikipedia]**. The pool's geography lens tagged the Corporation membership **[R]**, dated it "1950s," and called it undated in primary sources; that is false here, since Wikipedia gives 1944-1956 plainly and the chronology lens tags it **[A]**. Retagged and redated.

Grandson George Walker Bush born 6 July 1946, New Haven; later Governor of Texas and, in 2001, President **[A]**. The pool tagged this **[R]** as a lineage inference; nothing in it is reconstructed, so it is retagged **[A]**.

## 6. Two elections and the Senate, 1950-1963

1950: loses the Senate special election to William Benton **by 1,102 votes**, a margin later blamed partly on remarks tying him to Planned Parenthood **[A: Wikipedia]**. Westbrook Pegler's column of 22 August 1950 is quoted verbatim in the file **[A as relayed by Wikipedia]**; it contains a period racial slur, reproduced unaltered under the house rule against paraphrasing quotations, and `quote_source` says so.

1952: elected 4 November in a special election, defeating Abraham Ribicoff **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no source reached gives a swearing-in date; the file dates that stop 5 November 1952 and labels it **inferred**.

2 December 1954: votes with the 67-22 majority to censure Joseph McCarthy **[A: Wikipedia]**; his floor remarks are quoted at length. **Provenance caveat:** the wording is Time's, 13 December 1954, relayed by Wikipedia. The **Congressional Record was not retrieved**, so the primary transcript is unverified **[A for the vote and date, R for the exact wording]**.

1956: re-elected with 55 percent **[A]**; the geography lens names Thomas J. Dodd as opponent **[R: uncorroborated in the pool]**. Golf and social intimacy with Eisenhower, Augusta National included **[R: widely reported in secondary Bush-family biographies, not in the Wikipedia extract used, and no round or visit is dated anywhere reached]**; carried as one stop, explicitly tagged. July 1959: publishes "To Preserve Peace Let's Show the Russians How Strong We Are!" in Reader's Digest **[A: Writings section, Wikipedia]**, of which **only the title was retrievable**. 1962: declines re-election **[A]**. 3 January 1963: term ends **[A]**.

**Verification gap on the whole Senate career.** The cross-check against the Congressional Biographical Directory (bioguide.congress.gov) **failed with HTTP 403 Forbidden**. Every Senate date above rests on Wikipedia alone.

## 8. Afterlife: the papers, 1986-2010

- **1986:** Ned Anderson, former San Carlos Apache chairman, negotiates with Skull and Bones representatives, who deny holding the skull, call the 1918 ledger entry a hoax through attorney Endicott P. Davidson, and offer him a glass case holding what appears to be a child's skull, which he refuses **[A: Wikipedia, "Geronimo"]**.
- **17 October 2003:** Fox News, "Documents: Bush's Grandfather Directed Bank Tied to Man Who Funded Hitler" **[A as a headline, relayed by Wikipedia]**, not a sourced statement by a named person.
- **25 September 2004:** Duncan Campbell in the Guardian, "How Bush's grandfather helped Hitler's rise to power" **[A as a headline; body unfetchable, the site blocked retrieval]**. The verb "helped" states as settled what the 1942 APC report said could not be established; the file sets the two side by side without editorializing past them.
- **2007:** Scott Horton in Harper's links Bush to the 1934 Business Plot **[A that the article exists and makes the link]**. That the link is sound is **[R]**; the congressional record on the plot neither named nor cleared him. Place and month unknown, so the pin is a generic Manhattan point **[R]**.
- **2009:** Ramsey Clark sues Skull and Bones, Yale, and the United States for Geronimo's descendants, acknowledging "no hard proof that the story was true" **[A]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 38.8977, -77.0165 is near the National Mall, not a courthouse; the E. Barrett Prettyman courthouse is at approximately 38.8926, -77.0158 **[R for the derived pin]**. The filing day, 17 February, is the commonly reported date **[R]**.
- **2010:** dismissed, the court holding NAGPRA reaches only events after 1990; a Fort Sill spokesman states "there is no evidence to indicate the bones are anywhere but in the grave site" **[A]**.
