# John Everett Millais (1829-1896): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Sir John Everett Millais, 1st Baronet: painter, co-founder of the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood, President of the Royal Academy.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same line. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition, not confirmed against a fetched source this pass. Contradictions are flagged and left open; gaps are stated as gaps.

Principal sources reached: English Wikipedia (eleven articles, listed in section 9), Tate's biography page, and John Guille Millais, *The Life and Letters of Sir John Everett Millais* (Methuen, 1899), via two Internet Archive scans. Dickens's *Household Words* article was checked against Dickens Journals Online.

---

## 1. Birth and the island childhood, 1829-1840

**[A - Wikipedia, John Everett Millais]** Born 8 June 1829 at Southampton, Hampshire, second son of John William Millais and Emily Mary Millais.

**Correction.** The pool named the mother "Emily Mary Hodgkinson", wrong as a maiden name: Wikipedia gives Emily Mary Millais, nee Evermy (historically Evamy), Hodgkinson being her surname by an earlier marriage to Enoch Hodgkinson. The journey file uses Evermy. Date, place and "second son" stand **[A]**.

**[A - Wikipedia]** Most of the early childhood is spent on Jersey, the father's ancestral island, and the family keeps a lifelong attachment to it. The family later relocates for a period to Dinan in Brittany. No exact dates are given for either; the journey file dates them 1830 and c. 1835 with the hedges in `date_confidence`.

**[A - Tate biography; Wikipedia, Henry Sass]** The family comes to London in 1838 and Millais studies at Henry Sass's preparatory academy before sitting the Academy entrance examination. Wikipedia names "Sir John Millais P.R.A." among Sass's pupils and locates the school at No. 6 Charlotte Street, corner of Streatham Street, Bloomsbury, approximately 51.5165 / -0.1265. The pool tagged this **[R]**; it is upgraded to **[A]** on those two sources.

**[A - Wikipedia]** In 1840, aged eleven, Millais enters the Royal Academy Schools, the youngest student ever admitted at that date. There he meets William Holman Hunt and Dante Gabriel Rossetti.

**Correction: the wrong building.** The pool placed the 1840 entry at Somerset House (51.5113 / -0.1176). The Academy left Somerset House in 1837 and occupied the east wing of the National Gallery, Trafalgar Square, until 1868 (Wikipedia, Royal Academy of Arts). The 1840 entry, the 1853 associate election and the 1863 full election all belong at Trafalgar Square, approximately 51.5089 / -0.1283; only the 1896 presidency belongs at Burlington House. Applied throughout the journey file.

**[A - Wikipedia]** Keston (51.355 / 0.033) and Hayes (51.373 / 0.017) in Kent are recorded among the sketching destinations of his youth. The journey file makes one Keston stop and names Hayes inside it.

**[R]** An early deer-stalking trip to Ardverikie on Loch Laggan, sometimes cited from around 1847, is unconfirmed. **Not carried into the journey file.**

## 2. The Brotherhood, 1846-1853

**[A - Wikipedia]** *Pizarro Seizing the Inca of Peru*, 1846.

**Contradiction, flagged.** The pool's chronology dated the founding to September 1847. Wikipedia's Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood article states it "was founded in John Millais's parents' house on Gower Street, London in 1848", Millais, Rossetti and Hunt present at the first meeting. 1848 is used in the journey file. **The month is a genuine gap**: the widely repeated "September" is not in the fetched text, which gives a year only. The stop is dated 1848-09-01 with both hedges in `date_confidence`.

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, p. 51]** Millais at the meeting where the terms of the Brotherhood were settled: "Now, look here, this is what the Pre-Raphaelite clique should follow." Reported by his son from family testimony: **[A] as family record, [R] as verbatim speech**.

**[A - Wikipedia]** *Christ in the House of His Parents*, 1849-50, painted in the Gower Street studio, provokes strong critical and public hostility for its perceived irreverence.

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, p. 75, quoting Blackwood's Magazine, 1850]** "We can hardly imagine anything more ugly, graceless, and unpleasant than Mr. Millais' picture of 'Christ in the Carpenter's Shop.' Such a collection of splay feet, puffed joints, and misshapen limbs was assuredly never before made within so small a compass."

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, pp. 75-76, quoting The Times, 1850]** "Mr. Millais' principal picture is, to speak plainly, revolting... all finished with the same loathsome minuteness, is disgusting." Held here; the journey file carries Blackwood's and Dickens instead.

**Correction: Dickens's word order.** J. G. Millais, vol. 1, p. 75, has Dickens denouncing the picture as "mean, odious, revolting, and repulsive". Dickens's own text, "Old Lamps for New Ones", *Household Words*, 15 June 1850 (Dickens Journals Online, vol. I, p. 265), reads "the lowest depths of what is mean, odious, repulsive, and revolting." The journey file quotes Dickens's order and names *Household Words*; the biography's transposition is recorded here, not corrected away. The same biography notes Dickens "in later years was a firm friend of Millais" **[A]**.

**[A - Wikipedia, Ophelia (Millais)]** The *Ophelia* background is painted over five months in 1851 at Six Acre Meadow beside the Hogsmill River at Old Malden, near Tolworth, up to eleven hours a day and six days a week, from inside a straw-covered hurdle hut Millais builds for shelter. The site was identified by the research of Barbara Webb.

**Gap closed.** The pool's afterlife lens declared the Hogsmill site unresolved because a fetch returned 404. The article exists at `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophelia_(Millais)`: a bad URL, not a missing article, and the location is attested. Whether any modern marker stands at Six Acre Meadow is **still a gap**.

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, p. 119]** Letter to Mrs. Combe, 1851, written from Kingston: "My martyrdom is more trying than any I have hitherto experienced. The flies of Surrey are more muscular, and have a still greater propensity for probing human flesh." **Correction: the scan `lifelettersofsir01mill` reads "tiles of Surrey", an OCR corruption; `lifelettersofsir01milluoft` reads "flies", which alone makes sense of the rest.** The same letter records Hunt working four miles off. Kingston coordinates (51.4123 / -0.3007) are approximate and unsourced.

**[R]** Lodging at Worcester Park Farm near Ewell is general Pre-Raphaelite biography, unconfirmed this pass. **Not carried into the journey file**; the attested Kingston letter is used instead.

**[A - Wikipedia, Ophelia (Millais)]** Elizabeth Siddal poses for the figure in a full bathtub in the Gower Street studio; the lamps warming the water go out unnoticed, she catches a severe cold, and her father demands fifty pounds in medical costs. *Mariana* is dated 1850-51, *A Huguenot* and *Ophelia* 1851-52.

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, pp. 111-112, quoting Ruskin, Modern Painters, vol. 1]** "the admirable, though strange pictures of Mr. Millais and Mr. Holman Hunt... they are endeavouring to paint, with the highest possible degree of completion, what they see in Nature, without reference to conventional or established rules." **Correction: the passage begins on p. 111, not p. 112**; it breaks mid-sentence across the page turn. The *Modern Painters* attribution is the biography's own, introduced with "It was in this year (1851) that Ruskin took up arms in defence of the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood."

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, p. 112, quoting Ruskin, Lectures on Architecture and Painting, Edinburgh, November 1853]** "I question whether even the greatest men of old times possessed more exhaustless invention than either Millais or Rossetti." And: "I thank God that the Pre-Raphaelites are young, and that strength is still with them, and life, with all the war of it, still in front of them."

**[A - Wikipedia]** Elected an associate of the Royal Academy, 1853.

## 3. Glenfinlas, Effie Gray, and Perth, 1853-1862

**[A - Wikipedia, John Ruskin; Effie Gray]** In the summer of 1853 Millais travels to Glen Finglas in the Trossachs with John Ruskin and Effie Ruskin, commissioned to paint the gneiss landscape into which Ruskin's portrait is later set. During the stay Millais and Effie fall in love. The party lodges near Brig o' Turk, named in the title of Millais's sketch "The Waterfall, or Effie at Glenfinlas".

**[A - Wikipedia]** The Ruskin marriage is annulled and Effie marries Millais in 1855. **Gap on the detail.** The fetched text gives the year only. The traditional 3 July 1855 at Bowerswell House, Kinnoull, the same house in which she married Ruskin in 1848, is **[R]** from standard biography. The stop is dated 1855-07-03 with the whole hedge in `date_confidence`.

**[R]** The legend around Ruskin's failure to consummate the marriage runs from revulsion at Effie's body to menstrual blood; Robert Brownell argues the reluctance was financial. **Sources disagree; not adjudicated here, not carried into the journey file.**

**[A - Wikipedia]** The couple settles at Annat Lodge near Perth, whose garden supplies the setting of *Autumn Leaves*.

**[A - Wikipedia, List of paintings by John Everett Millais]** *The Blind Girl* landscape is painted in the fields around Winchelsea, East Sussex, 1854-56.

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, p. 300]** Millais to his wife, 8 May 1856: "I never saw anything more shameless than the treatment by the R.A. of my work. Every year it is the same. The surest sign of a young man's work being worthless is generosity and applause from the Academy!"

**[A - Wikipedia, The Vale of Rest]** *The Vale of Rest*, 1858-59; the gravestones were painted at Kinnoull old churchyard in Perth.

**Coordinate conflict, reconciled and flagged.** The pool put Kinnoull Parish Church at 56.3958 / -3.409 (geography lens) and 56.3958 / -3.4189 (afterlife lens) for the same church. Neither is sourced. The journey file uses 56.3936 / -3.4166 at every Kinnoull stop for internal consistency. **A reconciliation, not an attestation.**

**[A - Wikipedia]** Millais and Effie have eight children. **Correction:** the pool called John Guille Millais their fourth child; Wikipedia gives him as the fourth son and seventh child, born 24 March 1865 at Perth. **Gap:** the birth house is not named; the journey file pins it at Bowerswell and marks the pin inferred.

## 4. Cromwell Place and the Academy, 1862-1870

**[A - Wikipedia]** The family moves to 7 Cromwell Place, Kensington, in 1862.

**[A - Wikipedia]** Elected a full Royal Academician, 1863. Venue Trafalgar Square, per the Royal Academy correction in section 1.

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, p. 275]** Dickens to Millais, 18 December 1864: "You are a generous and true friend to Mrs. Leech." On Millais's effort to win a Civil List pension for the family of the dead illustrator John Leech. Written from Gad's Hill Place, Higham-by-Rochester; the pin (51.4053 / 0.4318) is approximate and unsourced.

**Correction: the Kinnoull window is 1870, not 1864.** Wikipedia, Effie Gray: "Gray's father had donated the Millais window, the West window, to Kinnoull Church in 1870." 1864 is the date of the parable illustrations the designs were based on.

## 5. Chill October and the Perthshire autumns, 1870-1892

**[A - Wikipedia, Chill October]** *Chill October*, 1870, his first large Scottish landscape. **Correction: the pool has the place wrong twice over.** Not an English studio and not Birnam or Dunkeld. Millais's note on the stretcher: "I made no sketch for it, but painted every touch from Nature, on the canvas itself." The site is a backwater of the Tay just below Kinfauns, near Perth, approximately 56.400 / -3.353, and Millais adds: "The traveller between Perth and Dundee passes the spot where I stood." Birnam and Dunkeld lie 30 km upstream and belong to the later sojourns.

**[A - Wikipedia, The Boyhood of Raleigh]** Painted 1870 at Budleigh Salterton, Devon, with the sons Everett and George as the boys; exhibited at the Academy in 1871. The pool gave 1871 and "England".

**[A - Wikipedia]** From 1870 to 1892 he rents houses near Dunkeld and Birnam each year from August into late autumn, for sport alongside landscape work. The journey file makes one Dunkeld stop and names Birnam inside it.

**[A - Wikipedia]** The ground around Murthly Castle supplies backdrops including his first full snow scene, *Christmas Eve*, 1887. **Correction: coordinates.** The pool gave 56.5388 / -3.547, west of the estate; Murthly Castle is approximately 56.552 / -3.517.

**[R]** Fishing the Tay salmon beat at Stobhall is tradition, often cited, **not confirmed by any source fetched this pass**. **Correction: coordinates.** Stobhall is approximately 56.482 / -3.383; the pool's 56.488 / -3.468 falls 5 km west, off the river. Carried as one stop marked traditional.

**[R]** Shooting and fishing on ground near Scone is likewise unverified. **Not carried into the journey file.**

## 6. Palace Gate and the baronetcy, 1874-1892

**[A - Wikipedia]** *The North-West Passage* 1874, *The Princes in the Tower* 1878, *Cherry Ripe* 1879, *Bubbles* 1886, the last a portrait of his grandson later licensed for advertising by A. & F. Pears. Millais builds 2 Palace Gate, Kensington, occupied from 1875, and dies there. President of the Royal Birmingham Society of Artists 1881-82. Created a baronet in July 1885, styled of Palace Gate, Kensington, and of Saint Ouen, Jersey; the first artist given a hereditary title.

## 7. The presidency, the death, and the afterlife, 1896-2007

**[A - Wikipedia]** Elected President of the Royal Academy in 1896 following the death of Frederic Leighton. Venue Burlington House.

**Coordinate conflict, flagged.** The pool gives Burlington House 51.509 / -0.14; the atlas's `edward_burne_jones.journey.json` uses 51.509 / -0.1394. The journey file inherits the existing atlas pin byte for byte so the two files agree.

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, p. 53]** Millais to his son on the eve of the presidency: "I doubt very much whether any man ever gets the credit of being quite square and above-board about his life and work."

**[A - J. G. Millais, vol. 1, pp. 54-55]** "All nonsense! My pictures would have been exactly the same if I had never seen or heard of Rossetti." And: "The Pre-Raphaelites had but one idea, to present on canvas what they saw in Nature; and such productions as these were absolutely foreign to the spirit of their work." Both 1896. The second is held here and not used in the journey file.

**[A - Wikipedia]** Dies of throat cancer at Kensington on 13 August 1896, aged 67; buried in the crypt of St Paul's Cathedral in August 1896. **Gap:** the exact bay or position of the grave within the crypt is not confirmed by any source consulted.

**[A - Wikipedia, quoting the Pall Mall Gazette]** A memorial committee under the Prince of Wales commissions Thomas Brock; the bronze goes up at the garden entrance of the Tate Gallery in 1905, "a breezy statue, representing the man in the characteristic attitude in which we all knew him."

**[A - Wikipedia]** Tate director Norman Reid tries to have it removed in 1953 and 1962, calling its presence "positively harmful" and preferring a Rodin. Ownership passes from the Ministry of Works to English Heritage in 1996, then to Tate; under Stephen Deuchar it is moved to the side of the building in 2000.

**Coordinate conflict, flagged.** Tate Britain appears as 51.491 / -0.1277 (geography lens) and 51.4914 / -0.1279 (afterlife lens). The journey file uses the latter at all three Tate stops.

**[A - Wikipedia]** A Millais retrospective opens at Tate Britain in 2007, drawing 151,000 visitors before travelling to the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam and to Fukuoka and Tokyo, reaching over 660,000 in total.

**[A - Wikipedia, Effie Gray]** Effie is buried in Kinnoull Parish churchyard at Perth, sharing a grave marker with their son George Gray Millais, who died at 21. Her own letters are published only posthumously.

**[A - Wikipedia, Effie Gray]** The triangle is dramatised repeatedly: *The Love of John Ruskin* (1912), *The Love School* (1975), *Modern Painters* (1995), *Desperate Romantics* (2009), *Effie Gray* (2014). Recorded here, not carried into the journey file.

## 8. Crossings with travellers already in the atlas

- **william_morris [A]** Morris meets Edward Jones at Exeter College, Oxford, and the two form their own Birmingham "Brotherhood". The atlas file dates this 1848, the year of the Gower Street founding, without a month. Two brotherhoods, one year, no documented contact. Named in the Gower Street campa as a parallel, not as a meeting.
- **johann_friedrich_overbeck [A]** The Nazarene Lukasbund, Vienna 1809, is named in the atlas as the German precursor of the 1848 revival of pre-Renaissance sincerity. Named in the *Christ in the House of His Parents* campa.
- **jane_morris, william_morris, edward_burne_jones [A]** Rossetti gathers Morris and Burne-Jones for the 1857 Oxford Union murals and spots Jane Burden that October; the Firm is founded at Red Lion Square in 1861. Millais is in none of it: he never joins and stays inside the Academy. Named in the Cromwell Place campa as the fork it is.
- **edward_burne_jones [A]** Burne-Jones resigns his RA associateship in 1893, the Grosvenor Gallery having opened in 1877 as the Academy's rival; Millais never leaves. Named in the Burlington House and Birmingham campas.
- **paul_barbier [A]** The atlas already cites Millais's *Ophelia* as a visual reference for Barbier's Ophelia. Named in the Siddal campa. This is a `suggested_refs` crossing inside the atlas, not a crossing between the two men.
- **moses [A]** Millais's *Victory O Lord!* (1871) is cited as a reference for Moses at Rephidim. Carried as a `suggested_refs` entry only, not named in prose: the lives do not cross.
- **QUEUE.md [A]** Millais and Holman Hunt stand queued as the two unresearched founding PRB members; Christina Rossetti is queued as a contributor to *The Germ* (1850). Millais's own contributions to *The Germ* are general knowledge **[R]**, in no atlas file.
