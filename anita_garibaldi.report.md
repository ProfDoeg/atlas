# Anita Garibaldi (Ana Maria de Jesus Ribeiro), 1821-1849: research report

*2026-08-11, for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `anita_garibaldi`. Method after `bourlemont_roster.md`: every claim carries **[A]** (attested, source named) or **[R]** (reconstruction, tradition, inference). Contradictions are flagged and left standing; inaccessible sources are named with the reason; gaps are stated as gaps.*

**Structural warning.** No scholarly monograph was reachable. The pool is four Wikipedia language editions plus one genuine primary source: Giuseppe Garibaldi's autobiographical sketch of his wife, in Dwight's 1861 translation and in the Italian *Vita e memorie* of 1860. That primary is her husband writing about his dead wife in 1850, for an American publisher, while building a career partly on her legend; the encyclopaedias largely paraphrase him. **Not one sentence written or dictated by Anita Garibaldi survives in the reachable pool.** Every quotation in the journey file is his. Tagging does not fix that; it makes it visible.

---

## 1. Laguna: birth, forced marriage, abandonment (1821-1838)

- Born **30 August 1821** as Ana Maria de Jesus Ribeiro to Bento Ribeiro da Silva and Maria Antônia de Jesus Antunes, at **Morrinhos, Laguna district, Santa Catarina**. **[A: pt. and en.wikipedia, agreeing on the date]** *Corrected geography:* pt.wikipedia's Homenagens section places Morrinhos in what is **today a neighbourhood of Tubarão, not Laguna**.
- Her father dies c. 1833-1835, leaving hardship. **[R: pt.wikipedia, a range only, no document]**
- Married **30 August 1835, on her fourteenth birthday**, to the shoemaker **Manuel Duarte de Aguiar** at the **Igreja Matriz Santo Antônio dos Anjos**. **[A: pt.wikipedia; "forced" is that source's own word]**
- The **Guerra dos Farrapos opens 20 September 1835**, three weeks after the wedding. **[A: pt.wikipedia]**
- Duarte de Aguiar abandons her for the Imperial army, c. 1837-1838. **[R: en.wikipedia, a two-year range only; the journey defaults to 1838-01-01 and says so]**

**Gap.** Nothing on childhood, schooling, literacy or work, 1821-1835; nothing at all 1835-1839 except the husband's departure. Roughly eighteen of her twenty-seven years are undocumented.

**Open question.** Whether Duarte de Aguiar was alive in March 1842, when she married Garibaldi, and whether any annulment or death record exists. No reachable source addresses it; the journey states it as a gap in the marriage stop.

## 2. The Farroupilha navy and the taking of Laguna (1837-1839)

- **1837:** Garibaldi, imprisoned in Rio de Janeiro, meets **Bento Gonçalves** and receives a **letter of marque**. **[A: pt.wikipedia 'Guerra dos Farrapos', year only]**
- **1 September 1838:** commissioned captain-lieutenant, commander of the Farroupilha navy. **[A]**
- **1839:** Garibaldi and **Luigi Rossetti** build two war launches on the **Camaquã** river; when **Francisco Pedro de Abreu ("Moringue")** attacks with over a hundred men, Garibaldi holds the yard with about fourteen entrenched workers. **[A]**
- **22 July 1839:** Farroupilha forces (pt.wikipedia names **Davi Canabarro**) take **Laguna**. **[A]**
- The **República Juliana** is proclaimed there. **CONTRADICTION, UNRESOLVED:** pt.wikipedia 'República Juliana' gives **29 July**; pt.wikipedia 'Museu Anita Garibaldi' gives **24 July**. Two articles in one encyclopaedia, five days apart. The journey names neither day and records both in `date_confidence`.

### The meeting: the file's sharpest date conflict

pt.wikipedia dates the meeting **22 July 1839**; en.wikipedia dates her **boarding his ship 20 October 1839**. The gathered pool carried "Anita, then eighteen, meets Garibaldi, then thirty-two", which is **wrong on the July dating**: Garibaldi (b. 4 July 1807) was 32 in July 1839, but Anita (b. 30 August 1821) was **seventeen** on 22 July and turned eighteen on 30 August. "Then eighteen" holds only for the October dating. **[A: arithmetic on two attested birthdates]** The journey resolves nothing: "seventeen, weeks from her eighteenth birthday", meeting stop 22 July, boarding stop 20 October, conflict recorded in `date_confidence` on both.

### The line he is supposed to have said

"You must be mine" is repeated everywhere and sourced almost nowhere.

- **Not** in the Dwight 1861 translation; full text searched, no match. Dwight has instead the restrained "I one day cast a casual glance at a house in the Burra…". **[A: negative result]**
- It **is** in the Italian memoirs: *"vidi la giovine e gli dissi… « Vergine; tu sarai mia. »"*, with the corroborating continuation *"Con queste sole parole avevo creato un nodo che la morte soltanto poteva disciogliere."* Source: *Vita e memorie*, ed. Dumas (Milan, 1860), archive.org `bub_gb_KdPZtNeocooC`, OCR line 4002. **[A]**
- it.wikipedia gives a third variant, *"Devi essere mia"*, from *Le memorie di Garibaldi* (Milan, 1860), p. 76. Editions differ; the journey quotes the Dumas wording and names both in `quote_source`. en.wikipedia's footnote for the line is **an archived book review with no page citation** and is not cited. **[A: inspected]** **[R]** as to whether the words were spoken.

## 3. The war in Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul (1839-1841)

- **November 1839, off Imbituba:** Anita in action at sea. *"my wife, the incomparable Anita, fired the first shot, putting the match to the gun with her own hand"* (Dwight, Ch. X). **[A: primary, standing caveat above]**
- **15 November 1839, Laguna retaken.** *Corrected:* the pool credited **Frederico Mariath**; pt.wikipedia 'República Juliana' credits **General Francisco José de Sousa Soares de Andrea**, with "mais de 3000 homens por terra, e por mar, uma frota de 13 navios". Mariath commanded naval elements only. The rebel fleet had **already been destroyed at the mouth of the Rio Maciambu**, so "retake Laguna in a naval battle" overstates it. **[A]**
- **25 December 1839, Lages:** the column passes Christmas in the serra. **[R: pt.wikipedia, no document]**
- **12 January 1840, Curitibanos:** the column is routed; Anita, pregnant, is separated and taken prisoner. **[A: en. and pt.wikipedia]**
- **The escape:** a commandeered horse, a ride through the lines, a swim across the **Rio Canoas** while pregnant. **[R: tradition in pt.wikipedia, flagged as legendary by en.wikipedia; the ford is reconstruction, named by no source]**
- **Eight days alone in the campo, then reunion at Vacaria**, Garibaldi having believed her dead. **[R: pt.wikipedia]**
- **16 September 1840, Mostardas:** **Menotti** born in the field. **[A: pt.wikipedia]** His skull is said to have been deformed by a fall during the escape. **[R: en.wikipedia, explicitly family tradition]**
- **c. 28 September 1840:** the Moringue raid; Anita rides out with the twelve-day-old infant.

**Two citation corrections, both offset-verified against the Gutenberg text:**

1. *"taking my wife with them, who mounted the saddle… then only twelve days old"* was cited to **Ch. XIV**; it sits at offset 135532, inside **Chapter XIII** (129516-136089). **Correct: Ch. XIII.** It is also a **distinct episode** from the Ch. XIV forest retreat: twelve days old here, three months old there. Not to be merged. **[A]**
2. *"We had resolved to fight to the last… the Brazilian Amazon on board"* was cited to **Ch. X** and called the same battle as the Imbituba first-shot quote. It sits at offset 92781, inside **Chapter IX**, and belongs to the "FIGHT WITH A BRAZILIAN SHIP OF WAR" of the cruise out of the lagoon: **a different engagement**. With no honest stop to hang it on, it was **left out of the journey**. **[A]**

- **c. December 1840, the woods of "Las Antas":** *"Anna was much distressed by the apprehension of losing her little son, Menotti, who was saved with difficulty, and as if by a miracle."* (Dwight, Ch. XIV). **[A: primary]**

**Gaps.** Her presence at **Piratini** is inferred from the campaign, not documented; the journey tags it `reconstructed`. The "San Simon" camp of the Moringue raid is not resolvable to a coordinate; the journey pins the Mostardas district and states the discrepancy in `date_confidence`.

## 4. Montevideo and the Guerra Grande (1841-1848)

- **1841:** the family relocates to **Montevideo**. **[A: en. and pt.wikipedia]**
- **26 March 1842:** marriage at the **Iglesia de San Francisco de Asís**. **CONTRADICTION:** pt.wikipedia gives 26 March, en.wikipedia gives 1842 without settling the day. **[A for the year; contested for the day]**
  - *Corrected arithmetic:* the pool called this the legalisation of "a union already seven years and one child old". They met in 1839 and married in March 1842: about **two and a half to three years**, one child.
- **Children:** Rosa/Rosita b. 1843, dead of a throat infection at about two; Teresa/Teresita b. 1845 (en.wikipedia: 1845-1903); Ricciotti b. 1847 (en.wikipedia: 1847-1924). **[A]**
- **1847:** Anita "takes part in the defense of Montevideo". **[R as to what she did: en.wikipedia asserts participation and gives no detail whatsoever. The journey says exactly that.]**
- **January 1848:** she sails with the children for **Nice**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

**Gap.** Seven years in Montevideo, the longest settled stretch of her adult life, yield four sourced sentences: a wedding, three births, one death, one unelaborated combat claim.

## 5. Nice and the Roman Republic (1848-1849)

- **1848, Nice:** she meets **Rosa Raimondi Garibaldi** for the first time and leaves the children there. **[A: pt.wikipedia]**
- **24 February 1849, Rieti:** she rejoins Garibaldi. The fact is right; **the pool's attribution was false.** It credited en.wikipedia 'Anita Garibaldi', which **does not mention Rieti at all**. The date is it.wikipedia's: *"Qui Anita lo raggiunse il 24 febbraio 1849, dopo aver lasciato i figli a Nizza."* The journey cites it.wikipedia. **[A]**
- **April to June 1849, the siege of Rome. Downgrade from [A] to [R] for the combat element.** it.wikipedia says only *"In tutto questo periodo anche Anita era a Roma, tornata da Nizza all'arrivo delle truppe francesi"* (from 30 April 1849), occupied **caring for the wounded**. Her **presence** is documented **[A]**; her **fighting on the Janiculum** is Risorgimento tradition **[R]**, carried as fact by en.wikipedia. The journey gives both.
- **The fall of the Republic: the pool's date conflated three events.** Corrected sequence, all **[A]**: French carry the Janiculum **night of 29-30 June**; the Assembly votes to end resistance **30 June**; Garibaldi marches out with about 4,000 **1-2 July**; French troops enter **3 July**; it.wikipedia dates the Republic's end **4 July 1849**. The retreat did not begin on 30 June and the Republic did not fall on 29 June.

## 6. The retreat and the death (July to August 1849)

- **2 July 1849:** out of Rome with about 4,000. **[A: en.wikipedia 'Giuseppe Garibaldi']**
- **Tivoli and Terni:** on the column's route. **[R: route reconstruction; no source names Anita at either. Tivoli is kept and flagged; Terni dropped rather than pad the file with a second unsourced pin.]**
- **31 July 1849, San Marino:** reduced to about 250 men, Garibaldi releases his followers from their oath. **[A]**
- **1 August 1849, Cesenatico:** thirteen **bragozzi** requisitioned; the party puts to sea for Venice. **[A]**
- **Austrian interception off Comacchio. CONTRADICTION:** en.wikipedia dates it 2 August; it.wikipedia puts the landing near Magnavacca on **3 August** (*"Il 3 agosto… approdarono presso Magavacca"*), placing the interception on the night of 2-3 August. Both left standing.
- **Magnavacca:** the renaming to Porto Garibaldi took effect **13 April 1919**; **Colonel Gioacchino Bonnet** is credited with getting Garibaldi away. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **Sant'Alberto:** the pool's hedge ("not confirmed in sources consulted") was **over-cautious**. it.wikipedia does attest the refuge, naming **Ugo Bassi, Giovanni Livraghi and G. B. Culiolo**. **[A]** The **Ravaglia family name** is not in that article and is not used. **[R, dropped]**
- **4 August 1849, 7:45 pm, Fattoria Guiccioli, Mandriole (Ravenna):** Anita dies, pregnant with a fifth child. **[A: en.wikipedia, it.wikipedia concurring]** **Cause of death: contradiction, unresolved.** en. and it.wikipedia give **malaria**, pt.wikipedia **typhoid fever**. The journey states both.
- Garibaldi, writing in 1850: *"on taking her pulse I found her a corpse, and sang the hymn of despair. I prayed for forgiveness, for I thought of the sin of taking her from her home."* (Dwight, Ch. IX, a retrospective aside inside the 1839 meeting narrative). **[A: primary]**

## 7. The graves, and the uses made of her (1849-2012)

- **4 August 1849:** hasty concealed burial **in sand at the "motta della Pastorara"**, no rites, no marker; on **10 August** children find the remains and she is reburied in the **Mandriole cemetery**. **[A: it. and en.wikipedia]**
- **The strangulation allegation:** papal investigators alleged marks of strangulation; ecclesiastical examiners later put them down to decomposition, exonerating the family who had sheltered her. **[R: cross-checked across en/pt/it.wikipedia, thin in each]**
- **The seven exhumations:** pt.wikipedia claims **seven** during Garibaldi's exile, **uncorroborated** by the English or Italian accounts. **[R, flagged as an uncorroborated tally in the journey]**
- **11 November 1859:** exhumed by patriots led by **Giuseppe Deideri**, reburied in the chapel of the castle cemetery at **Nice**; **December 1931**, transferred to the **Pantheon of Staglieno**, Genoa. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **The largest single error in the pool: the date of the Rome interment.** Both the chronology and afterlife lenses gave **2 December 1932**, following it.wikipedia's *biography* article. it.wikipedia's **'Monumento ad Anita Garibaldi'** has the monument *"inaugurato il 4 giugno 1932"* and the remains arriving *"il 2 giugno"*. The correct sequence is **2-4 June 1932**; "2 dicembre" in the biography article is an internal error for "2 giugno" that has propagated widely, into this pipeline included. **[A]**
- **The monument:** equestrian, by **Mario Rutelli**, Anita on a rearing horse, pistol in one hand and infant in the other, remains sealed into the base; **Mussolini presided and spoke**; **ten** delegations attended (Brazil, France, Uruguay, England, Poland, Hungary, Cuba, Greece, Japan, Albania), **not nine** as the pool had it. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **Museu Anita Garibaldi, Laguna.** The Edifício de Câmara e Cadeia (begun 1735, where the República Juliana was proclaimed) was **designated for museum use 31 July 1949** for the centenary but **inaugurated only in 1956**; "opened in 1949" overstates it. It holds Farroupilha arms, furnishings, effects, and **an urn of soil from her burial place at Ravenna**; run by the Fundação Lagunense de Cultura, protected by IPHAN. **[A: pt.wikipedia]**
- **Toponymy:** municipalities of **Anita Garibaldi** and **Anitápolis**; bust in Belo Horizonte, avenue in Salvador, street in Joinville, monument at Morrinhos in what is now Tubarão. **[A: pt.wikipedia]** *Dropped:* the pool's **Praça Garibaldi in Porto Alegre**, unsupported in the passage retrieved. **[R]**
- **April 2012: Law 12.615** inscribes her in the **Livro dos Heróis e Heroínas da Pátria**, Brasília. **[A: pt.wikipedia]**
- **Screen and carnival:** Magnani (*Camicie Rosse*, 1952), Antonelli (*A Casa das Sete Mulheres*, 2003), Solarino (2012), Arósio (*Anita e Garibaldi*, 2013) **[A: en.wikipedia]**; **Unidos do Viradouro**'s 1999 enredo, third place, and **Flávio Aguiar**'s Jabuti-winning novel *Anita* (2000). **[A: pt.wikipedia]**

## 9. Interlocks with the existing atlas

- **`juan_manuel_de_rosas`** and **`sarmiento`**: both files already narrate Garibaldi inside besieged Montevideo, differing on emphasis and agreeing on the core fact, which per house style is convergence from two independent files, not contradiction. **[A]**
- **`san_martin`**: Montevideo 1829, two months at anchor, the refused dictatorship. **[A]** **`alvear`**: commanded the siege lines at the Cerrito in 1814 and took the city, a second layer under the same coordinate for the opposite cause. **[A]**
- **`hernando_arias_de_saavedra`**: his file traces the ethnogenesis of the gaucho (*mozos perdidos*) on the plains Anita is born on the edge of. **[R: shared geography, no textual link]**
- **`dannunzio`**: his 1915 Quarto oration was delivered at Genoa before the monument to Garibaldi's Thousand. Named at the **Staglieno** stop, same city. **[A]**
- **`mussolini`**: he **presided at the 4 June 1932 inauguration** of the monument over her grave. The strongest interlock here, and the only one putting two atlas travellers in one place on one day. **[A: it.wikipedia; `mussolini.journey.json` independently carries the 52nd Garibaldi Brigade at Dongo, 1945]**
- **`gauchito_gil`**: QUEUE.md line 288 links Anita to the same gaucho thread. **Not named in campa**: thematic, not a crossing. **[R]**

**Atlas gaps.** No existing file carried a **Laguna / Santa Catarina** pin (established fresh at -28.4831, -48.7797), and none carries the **Roman Republic of 1849, Comacchio, Nice or Caprera**. **Giuseppe Garibaldi has no journey file** (queued, QUEUE.md line 622). This is therefore the first atlas file to narrate the Guerra dos Farrapos and the Montevideo Italian Legion directly rather than inherit them, and everything about Giuseppe here will need reconciling when his own run happens.
