# Isaac Newton (1642-1727): research report

*2026-08-03. Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys. Register: national mythology, the canon is true.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or anecdote whose base is late, indirect, or contested. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. In the journey file the hedge lives in `date_confidence`, never in the prose.

---

## Two problems that cannot be fixed, only declared

The pool arrived verified across five lenses with an adversarial check run over it. That check overturned roughly a third of the geography lens on coordinates alone, corrected two quotations against their printed originals, and caught one error inherited from English Wikipedia. Every correction is recorded below rather than silently absorbed.

**The calendar.** England kept the Julian calendar until 1752. Newton's birth is 25 December 1642 O.S., 4 January 1643 N.S. **[A, Wikipedia]**; his death 20 March 1727 O.S., 31 March 1727 N.S. **[A, Wikipedia]**. Sources lead with one or the other and the disagreement is not a mistake in any of them. Nearly every English date here (5 June 1661, 29 October 1669, 30 November 1703, 16 April 1705) is Old Style as its source gives it. The journey declares `calendar: "gregorian"`, since the rule for post-1582 lives requires it, then carries Old Style dates as its sources carry them, with the split named in `date_confidence` at birth and death. A compromise, stated as one.

**The pins.** The geography lens used one wrong pair for Trinity College, Cambridge (52.2096, 0.1166) in ten separate entries. Trinity is at 52.2070, 0.1146 **[A, Wikipedia]**. The atlas holds two further Cambridge pins: `dirac` puts the Lucasian chair at 52.2039, 0.1187, `bulwer_lytton` puts Trinity at 52.2043, 0.1149. Four pins, one college. This journey uses the verified pair throughout and does not force agreement with the existing files; the divergence is logged as an atlas-wide problem for a later pass. One pin is inherited byte-identical: **Westminster Abbey, 51.4993, -0.1273**, shared by the Kelvin burial and the Dirac stone.

## I. Lincolnshire, 1642-1661

- **[A, Wikipedia / MacTutor]** Born at Woolsthorpe Manor three months after his father's death, so small he is not expected to live. **[A, Westfall]** The quart-pot remark is his mother's.
- **Coordinates corrected, internal contradiction flagged.** The geography lens gave 52.8156, -0.6136, farmland 1.5 km northeast of the house; the afterlife lens gave 52.8088, -0.6303 and was right (Wikipedia: 52.80917, -0.63056). Two lenses of one pool pinned one building 1.5 km apart. The journey uses 52.8092, -0.6306 for the house, 52.8088, -0.6303 for the orchard.
- **[A, Colsterworth parish register, in Westfall]** Baptised at St John the Baptist, Colsterworth, 1 January 1643 O.S., entered as son of the late Isaac Newton, yeoman. Church at approximately 52.8055, -0.6191; the pool's 52.8033, -0.6094 is 700 m out.
- **[A, corrected]** Hannah Ayscough marries Barnabas Smith, rector of North Witham, **27 January 1646**, not 1645 (Wikipedia; MacTutor's "age two" agrees). North Witham is at 52.7856, -0.6261, **south** of Woolsthorpe, where the pool's 52.8386, -0.5972 put it north, and 2.5 miles off, not the "mile" claimed. Hannah stayed at the rectory until 1653.
- **[A, Fitzwilliam Notebook]** The 1662 list of sins includes "Threatening my father and mother Smith to burn them and the house over them." **Placement disagreement, flagged:** the quotes lens assigned this Woolsthorpe (52.7994, -0.6217); it is a Cambridge notebook. The journey places it at Trinity.
- **[A, Wikipedia; Stukeley]** King's School, Grantham from 1655; the apothecary Clark's house, the carved windowsill, model windmills and sundials. School at approximately 52.9137, -0.6378; the pool's 52.911, -0.6425 is 400 m out. **Contradiction, not resolved:** MacTutor puts the stepfather's death and a return to the school around 1653, Wikipedia dates attendance from 1655, and no reachable source reconciles them. **[A]** Withdrawn to the farm 1659, hopeless at it; sent back in 1660 by Henry Stokes and the Reverend William Ayscough.
- **[A, corrected attribution]** "The lad was not only very expert with his mechanical tools, but he was equally so with his pen." The quotes lens gave this to Stukeley's 1752 *Memoirs*. It belongs to his earlier memoir sent to Richard Mead in 1727 (Keynes Ms. 136.03; Newton Project THEM00158); the 1752 Revised Memoir words the recollection differently. The companion line is also 1727 and reads "birds, beasts, men, ships & mathematical schemes. & very well designd." (not "designed").

## II. Trinity College, 1661-1665

- **[A, Trinity admissions register; Westfall]** Admitted subsizar **5 June 1661**. **Correction:** the pool called that his matriculation; it is the admission date, and he matriculated 8 July.
- **[A, MacTutor]** Elected scholar 1664; BA 1665. Neither carries a month or day in the reachable sources; both default to 01 January with `date_confidence` saying so. **[R, Conduitt, Keynes MS 130]** The tradition that Barrow examined him and found him weak on Euclid is late and disputed.
- **[R, corrected]** The *Quaestiones quaedam philosophicae* (CUL MS Add. 3996) carries, added later above the title, "Amicus Plato amicus Aristoteles magis amica veritas". The pool's English ("my greatest friend is truth") is a translator's rendering, not a manuscript reading, and the tag is a scholastic commonplace Newton copied rather than coined. The journey quotes the Latin and translates in the campa.
- **Three-way contradiction on the prism, not resolved.** Geography lens 1663; Wikipedia 1665; Newton's own 1672 letter, source of "I procured me a triangular glass-prism", says "the beginning of the Year 1666". The journey takes 1665 and names all three in `date_confidence`. Stourbridge Common at 52.2117, 0.1543.

## III. The plague years, 1665-1667

- **[A, Wikipedia / MacTutor]** Cambridge closes; at Woolsthorpe over roughly two years he lays the groundwork for the calculus, the theory of light and colour, and universal gravitation. **[A, CUL MS Add. 3968]** "All this was in the two plague years of 1665 and 1666, for in those days I was in the prime of my age for invention..."
- **[R]** The apple: Stukeley 1752, reporting Newton in old age, and historians dispute how literally to read it. **Gap:** the modern details (grafts to MIT and Cambridge) went unconfirmed, Wikipedia's apple-tree article returning only a truncated excerpt. **[A, Wikipedia, Woolsthorpe Manor]** The tree is a Flower of Kent, over 400 years old by dendrochronology, regrown from the roots of one blown down in 1820.
- **[A, Wikipedia]** Returns to Cambridge 1667.

## IV. The Lucasian chair, 1667-1684

- **[A, Trinity records; MacTutor]** Minor fellow **2 October 1667** (the pool gave 1 October); major fellow July 1668 with the MA.
- **[A, corrected]** The first reflecting telescope is built **1668**, not the pool's 1670-1672; the second instrument went down to the Royal Society at the end of 1671 by Barrow's hand and was shown to Charles II in January 1672 (Wikipedia, Newton's reflector).
- **[A, Cambridge University records; Westfall]** Barrow resigns the Lucasian chair in his favour, 29 October 1669; first lecture course, 1670, on optics.
- **[A, corrected venue]** Elected FRS 11 January 1672; the optical paper read in February. The pool placed both at Gresham College. **The Society was not at Gresham in 1672**: it moved to Arundel House on the Strand in 1666 after the Great Fire and returned only in 1673. Both belong at approximately 51.5113, -0.1157. **Second correction:** 6 February is the letter's date; it was read on 8 February and printed on 19 February.
- **[A, Turnbull ed., *Correspondence*, Vol. I, p. 416]** To Hooke, 5 February 1675/6: "If I have seen further it is by standing on ye sholders of Giants." **Chain stated:** seen via Wikipedia's citation of Turnbull; the volume was not opened in this pass.
- **[A, royal warrant, April 1675; Westfall]** Charles II exempts him from holy orders, letting him keep the fellowship despite an Arian rejection of Trinitarian doctrine documented in the Yahuda papers. Trinity College Chapel is at 52.2074, 0.1175, not the pool's 52.2092, 0.1172. **[A, Keynes MS 7]** From *A Short Scheme of the True Religion*: "Atheism is so senseless and odious to mankind that it never had many professors." Undated.
- **[A, Chymistry of Isaac Newton project, Indiana]** Thousands of hours of alchemy in a laboratory beside his rooms, unpublished, some later burned.
- **[R, weak chain, stated]** The 1675 annotation on *Manna*: "This philosophy, both speculative and active, is not only to be found in the volume of nature, but also in the sacred scriptures." Verbatim in Wikipedia's "Isaac Newton's occult studies", whose only reference is Laurence Gardner, *The Shadow of Solomon* (2007), p. 146, a popular history. The Newton Project corpus returned no hit. **No manuscript-grade source was reached.**
- **[A, MacTutor]** 1679: correspondence with Hooke on orbital motion and the inverse-square law, later central to their priority dispute.

## V. The Principia, 1684-1696

- **[A, De Moivre via Conduitt, Keynes MS 130; Westfall]** August 1684: Halley's question, the instant answer (an ellipse), the lost paper. **[A, Humphrey Newton, in Westfall]** Eighteen months of writing, forgotten meals.
- **[A, Royal Society Council minutes]** Book I presented 28 April 1686; Halley underwrites the printing. The Society had returned to Gresham College by then, approximately 51.5155, -0.0838; the pool's 51.5155, -0.0864 is a loose Bishopsgate pin.
- **[A, first edition imprint; corrected]** Published July 1687, printed by Joseph Streater, roughly 500 to 750 copies, licensed by Pepys as president. **The day is unsafe:** Wikipedia gives none, and the pool's "5 July" appears to come from confusion with the licensing a year earlier (ordered printed 30 June 1686, imprimatur July 1686). The journey uses 1687-07-01 with the day declared unattested.
- **[A, Grace Book; Westfall]** 1687: among the fellows resisting James II over the Benedictine's degree. **[A, MacTutor]** 1689: Convention Parliament for Cambridge University. **[R, Brewster, disputed as apocryphal]** The window anecdote.
- **[A, Four Letters to Richard Bentley]** 25 February 1692/3: "Tis inconceivable that inanimate brute matter should (without the mediation of something else which is not material) operate upon & affect other matter without mutual contact."
- **[A, letters to Pepys and Locke, September 1693]** The breakdown, later apologised for. **[R]** Cause disputed: overwork, a fire among his papers, mercury. **Not adjudicated.**

## VI. The Mint and the presidency, 1696-1713

- **[A, Treasury warrant, April 1696; Westfall; Levenson (2009)]** Warden of the Mint, lodgings in the Tower, the Great Recoinage; he interrogates coiners in taverns and prisons and sends several to the gallows.
- **[A, corrected]** Chaloner was **held at Newgate but not hanged there**: tried at the Old Bailey 3 March 1699, hanged at Tyburn 22 March (Wikipedia, William Chaloner). The pool conflated the two. Tyburn's approximate 51.5132, -0.1590 is this compiler's, not the pool's; the journey pins the stop at Newgate and names Tyburn in the campa.
- **[A, Treasury warrant, December 1699]** Master of the Mint to his death; the 1717 gold-silver ratio effectively puts England on the gold standard. **[A, Trinity records]** 1701: resignation of chair and fellowship, **month not given in the reachable sources**.
- **[A, Journal Book, 30 November 1703; corrected venue]** Elected President on St Andrew's Day 1703. The pool placed this at Crane Court. **Anachronism:** the Society did not buy Crane Court until 26 October 1710; in 1703 it still met at Gresham College.
- **[A, corrected house]** Newton kept a Jermyn Street house from his early Mint years and moved to St Martin's Street only in 1710, so *Opticks* (1704) belongs to Jermyn Street. **Internal contradiction inside the pool, resolved in favour of its own later entry.**
- **[A, corrected wording]** The Query 31 comets passage had drifted. Against *Opticks* 4th ed. (Innys, 1730; Project Gutenberg 33504): Newton wrote "may have risen", not "may have arisen", and "upon one another", not "on one another".
- **[A, Cambridge University records, 1705; Westfall]** Knighted by Queen Anne at Trinity, 16 April 1705, **not at Whitehall** as popular accounts have it, and reportedly for political rather than scientific service.
- **[A, Commercium Epistolicum, 1712]** The Royal Society committee rules for its own president in the calculus dispute; Newton drafts much of the supposedly impartial report. Crane Court is correct as venue from 1710.
- **[A, corrected place]** The second edition, edited by Roger Cotes under Bentley, appears 30 June 1713, 750 copies, **printed at Cambridge**: the imprint reads "Cantabrigiae". The geography lens said London, the chronology lens said Cambridge. **Internal contradiction, resolved by the imprint.**
- **[A, corrected translation]** The General Scholium's famous sentence was given as Motte's. It is not: Motte (Internet Archive, newtonspmathema00newtrich) has "and I frame no hypotheses; for whatever is not deduced from the phenomena is to be called an hypothesis...", where the pool carried a modern rendering from Wikipedia. The journey quotes Motte verbatim. Latin: *hypotheses non fingo*. Likewise the pool's "an intelligent Being" drops two words: Motte has "an intelligent **and powerful** Being".

## VII. Chronology, Kensington, death, 1713-1727

- **[A, Wikipedia; the Newton Project]** Decades of alchemy and a biblical chronology, printed posthumously in 1728 as *The Chronology of Ancient Kingdoms Amended*. **[A, Yahuda MS 7.3g, f. 13v]** The 2060 passage is a limit against speculation, not a prophecy: he mentions it "to put a stop to the rash conjectures of fancifull men".
- **[A, Survey of London; Westfall]** 1710-1725: St Martin's Street near Leicester Fields, rooftop observatory. **[A, Brewster (1855), Vol. II, Ch. 27]** The sea-shore remark, undated. **Brewster's report, not a manuscript in Newton's hand: secondhand but attributed.**
- **[A, Conduitt, Keynes MS 130; Westfall]** 1725-1727 at Kensington with the Conduitts; dies in his sleep aged eighty-four after months of bladder stones, refusing last rites.
- **[R, corrected]** The mercury. **There was no autopsy finding.** The evidence is a 1979 analysis of preserved hair samples (Johnson and Wolbarsht) reporting mercury roughly fifteen times normal levels (Wikipedia, Later life of Isaac Newton). The pool's "autopsy-adjacent report" is wrong on its face and is reworded; the inference to 1693 stays **[R]**.
- **[A, Westminster Abbey]** State funeral, burial 28 March 1727; pallbearers the Lord Chancellor, the Dukes of Montrose and Roxburgh, and the Earls of Pembroke, Sussex, and Macclesfield.

## VIII. Afterlife

- **[A, Westminster Abbey]** The Rysbrack monument to Kent's design is **finished August 1730 and unveiled the following year**. Geography lens: 1731 only. Afterlife lens: 1730. **Contradiction resolved by the Abbey's own page**, which distinguishes the two acts. Pope's couplet was **refused**; the monument carries a Latin inscription instead.
- **[A, corrected date]** Du Chatelet completed the only complete French *Principia* in **1749**, the year she died; first published **1756**, second edition 1759. The pool gave 1759 for the translation itself.
- **[A, Wikipedia; Tate Britain]** Blake's monotype *Newton*, 1795, reworked 1805: criticism, not homage. 1995: Paolozzi's bronze in the British Library piazza. Roubiliac's 1755 marble in the Trinity antechapel is not given a stop, for length.
- **[A, Wikipedia, Newton's occult studies]** 1936: Sotheby's sells the Portsmouth Papers, roughly 329 lots, more than a third alchemical. **Gap: the sale's month and day are not in the reachable sources.** Keynes buys much of the alchemy and the death mask and in 1946 calls Newton "the last of the magicians".
- **[A, corrected: a Wikipedia error the pool inherited]** Keynes's papers did **not** pass to Yahuda. Keynes bequeathed his collection to King's College, Cambridge in 1946, where the Keynes MSS remain; Keynes MS 130, cited throughout, is a King's shelfmark. Yahuda bought a **separate**, chiefly theological portion at the same sale, now at the National Library of Israel. The pool's unexplained "1998" is dropped; the journey uses 1946 for the bequest and 1969 for the Jerusalem deposit.

## Contradictions logged, not adjudicated

Old Style versus New Style throughout, birth and death each carrying two dates in circulation; the stepfather's death (1653, MacTutor) against the start of King's School attendance (1655, Wikipedia); the prism, 1663 or 1665 or "the beginning of the Year 1666"; four Cambridge pins across the pool and the existing atlas files; Woolsthorpe pinned 1.5 km apart by two lenses of the same pool; the cause of the 1693 breakdown; whether the Conduitt Euclid anecdote reflects anything Barrow said; the Sotheby's sale date.

## Gaps, stated as gaps

No manuscript transcription reached for the 1675 *Manna* annotation, the chain stopping at a 2007 popular history. Wikipedia's apple-tree article returned a truncated excerpt, leaving the MIT and Cambridge grafts unconfirmed. Turnbull's *Correspondence* Vol. I was not opened directly, so the shoulders-of-giants text stands at one remove, and Westfall's *Never at Rest* is cited throughout the pool without being opened here. Exact dates are missing for the 1664 scholarship election and the 1701 resignation.
