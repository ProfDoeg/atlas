# Victoria Ocampo — report

**File:** `victoria_ocampo.journey.json` — 9 segments, 41 stops, 8 quoted stops, span 1890-1979.

## Sources
Primary spine: Wikipedia (EN/ES) and Encyclopedia.com for the vital dates and honors; Doris Meyer's biography *Against the Wind and the Tide* and Ocampo's own posthumous *Autobiografía* (vol. I, *El archipiélago*) for childhood and interior life; the published correspondences — with Tagore (via Ketaki Kushari Dyson's essay and *Purabi*'s dedication), Ortega y Gasset (*Entre el corazón y la razón*, 2023), Virginia Woolf (*Correspondencia Ocampo/Woolf*), Albert Camus (*Correspondencia 1946-1959*), Drieu la Rochelle (*Lettres d'un amour défunt*), and Keyserling (*El viajero y una de sus sombras*) — for the love-and-letters throughline; La Nación, Infobae, and Página/12 features for the 1953 imprisonment, the Stravinsky/Colón episode, and the Sur founding narrative; UNESCO's own page for Villa Ocampo's building history and 1973 donation.

## Judgment calls
- **Register.** Ocampo's canon has no miracles or visions — it is "national mythology" in the secular sense the schema also licenses for figures like Alvear: a life narrated as founding-myth of Argentine letters, not sanctity. I kept everything at the level of documented fact or well-attested testimony (her own memoirs, published letters), erring toward "traditional" confidence only for domestic scenes (childhood summers at the villa, the Dante reading) that are consensus but not date-stamped in the sources.
- **Coordinates.** Villa Ocampo's exact plot (Elortondo, Beccar) is used for all San Isidro stops including the Tagore convalescence, since Villa Miralrío itself was demolished and no surviving coordinate exists; this is a small, acknowledged fold — the poet's actual rented house stood nearby on the same bluff.
- **The Estrada marriage and Julián Martínez affair** are placed as separate stops with only inferred/traditional dates, since the sources give ranges ("eighteen years," "around 1919-1920") rather than fixed days — honest under the schema's date_confidence system.
- **Drieu la Rochelle's Buenos Aires tour (1932)** had to be re-sequenced ahead of the Woolf meeting (1934) and the Mussolini interview (1935) after an initial chronology error caught by the linter — fixed before finishing.

## Time-folds / gaps
Not included, for space: her sister Silvina Ocampo's own literary career (a natural adjacent journey, referenced once as an edge in the Academy stop); the full roster of Sur contributors beyond Borges; her wartime activity in detail beyond *Les Lettres Françaises*; and several of her own books (*Testimonios*, ten volumes across her life) are referenced only as the source of quotes, not narrated as discrete stops.

## Five richest episodes
1. **Tagore at Villa Miralrío (1924-25)** — the convalescence, the naming as Vijaya, the *Purabi* dedication: the only book of his life dedicated entirely to someone outside his family and country.
2. **The founding of Sur (1931)** and Editorial Sur (1933) — the single act that makes her indispensable rather than merely wealthy and cultured.
3. **The Perón imprisonment (1953)** — twenty-six days in a colonial prison for "fallen women," with Camus, Huxley, Mistral, and Nehru cabling for her release: the starkest inversion in the whole life.
4. **Dancing Perséphone at the Colón (1936)** — the one night the theatrical vocation her father forbade at gunpoint-threat was publicly permitted her, which she called her most painfully happy memory.
5. **The Woolf friendship and its 1939 rupture** over an unwanted photograph — intimate, literary, and quietly heartbreaking, ending the year before Woolf's suicide.
