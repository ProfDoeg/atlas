# Francesco Tamburini (1846/1848 to 1890/1891): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-21 for `francesco_tamburini.journey.json`. Ingeniero-arquitecto of the
Marche; from July 1883 a contracted employee of the Argentine Departamento de Ingenieros
Civiles; author of the arch that made the Casa Rosada one building, and of the general
idea of the present Teatro Colón, which he did not live to see rise past its foundations.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition,
or a claim carried by a single source. Contradictions are recorded and left standing; none
is adjudicated. Where the verified pool corrected an earlier draft, the correction is what
is reported, and the superseded reading is named so it cannot creep back in. The pool also
moved a dozen coordinates, several by kilometres; those corrections are carried in the
journey file's `date_confidence` fields and are only summarised here.

---

## 1. Birth, and the year that will not settle

Born **29 January at Ascoli Piceno**, then in the Papal States. Day and city are stable
across every source reached **[A: es.wikipedia; Argentina.gob.ar confirms the city]**. The
**year is not**: es.wikipedia gives **1846**, it.wikipedia's raw wikitext gives
`AnnoNascita = 1848` and its body reads "nacque ad Ascoli Piceno nel 1848" **[A on both
readings; the disagreement is the finding]**. The Istituto Italiano di Cultura di Córdoba
holds both at once: its 29 January 2016 tribute was billed as the **170th anniversary**,
which only 1846 supports, while the same text prints **1848-1891** **[A: IIC Córdoba]**.

**Correction.** An earlier draft gave **Jesi** as a rival birthplace. Dropped: no Italian
source reached gives Jesi as a birthplace, and Jesi enters it.wikipedia only as the home
of his correspondent, "il marchese Colocci di Jesi". The live dispute is **1846 vs 1848,
same city, same day** **[A]**. Garibaldian family sympathies are **[R]**, uncorroborated.
No civil or baptismal record was consulted; neither year is preferred, the birth stop is
dated 1846-01-29 for ordering, and the dispute sits in `date_confidence`.

## 2. Ancona and Bologna

At about **eleven** the family moves to **Ancona**: liceo, then the **Istituto Tecnico
Benincasa**, where the naturalist **Francesco De Bosis** takes him "come assistente al
laboratorio di fisica e chimica" **[A: it.wikipedia, verbatim]**. An earlier draft called
that mentorship unconfirmed tradition and added *meteorological observation*: the
mentorship is in the source, the meteorology is not **[R on the meteorology]**. He studies
at Ancona and Bologna, qualifying as *ingeniero arquitecto* **[A: Argentina.gob.ar]**, and
graduates from **Bologna in 1872** **[A: es.wikipedia, corroborated by it.wikipedia]**.
The Benincasa building was **not located**; its coordinate in the pool merely duplicates
the Ancona city point, so the file pins it to the city and says so. **Gap.**

## 3. The decade of posts: Urbino, Pisa, Naples, Rome

- **Architecture at Urbino** **[A: it.wikipedia]**; years not established. **Gap.**
- **Architectural drawing at the Accademia delle belle arti di Pisa**, where he measures
  the **Duomo** and, it.wikipedia says, **publishes** the surveys, "di notevole dettaglio
  e rifinitura" **[A]**. An earlier draft denied a published outcome, overstating the gap.
  The 1879 Pisa newspaper notice of six large pen plates could not be fetched **[R]**. The
  same passage pairs Pisa with his surveys of **Giorgio Orsini di Sebenico's Ancona
  churches, San Francesco alle Scale and Sant'Agostino**, which the dossier omitted
  entirely **[A]**. Neither church located; pinned to the Ancona city point. **Gap.**
- **Ornament at the Accademia di Belle Arti di Napoli** before sailing **[A: Shmidt p.9]**.
  **Correction:** it.wikipedia does **not** list a Naples post (its list is Urbino, Pisa,
  Rome). The Naples post and the **Pisanti/Alvino lineage** are Shmidt's, not this
  dossier's inference: Pisanti "en continuidad con los preceptos de Errico Alvino".
- **Scuola di Applicazione per gli Ingegneri, Rome, until 1883** **[A: it.wikipedia]**;
  coordinate approximate, the school later moved to Via Eudossiana.
- **Palazzo Rheinold, Ancona**, "all'angolo tra corso Garibaldi e piazza del Teatro", his
  "probabile primo lavoro", dated by it.wikipedia to the Rome years **[A on address; R on
  the year]**; coordinate corrected by about 700 m. Likewise the **palazzina Marulli,
  Chiaravalle (AN)**, now the municipal library **[A: it.wikipedia]**, corrected by about
  7.4 km: the source says *palazzina*, not villa, and "fine '800", not 1881, and the
  **1907 municipal purchase** stays **[R]**.

## 4. The contract from Rome, 1883

"Al comenzar 1883 el gobierno del General Roca indicó al embajador argentino en Roma que
contratase un arquitecto" **[A: Argentina.gob.ar; Shmidt fn.12 after Alberto De Paula]**.
The **contract is signed 1 July 1883**, into the Departamento de Ingenieros Civiles as a
specially engaged government employee **[A: Shmidt fn.21]**; neither source says where
**[R on the place]**. On **17 August 1883** Roca writes to Congress on the urgency of the
Casa de Gobierno works **[A: Shmidt fn.15]**, and on **17 October 1883** the Chamber debates
a Palace of Justice, a police building, a military hospital, a normal school and an
immigrants' asylum, the whole future workload of the office **[A: Shmidt, Diario de
Sesiones]**. Tamburini is still in Italy, so that debate is context, carried inside the
arrival stop rather than staged as a scene. **Enrique (Henrik) Aberg**, the incumbent whose
Government House design he is hired to revise, **resigns** soon after **[A: Shmidt]**.

He **arrives February 1884** **[A: Shmidt fn.22 after De Paula]**; es.wikipedia gives the
bare year, so the month is De Paula's reconstruction **[R]**, and the 1880s landing point
predates Puerto Madero. **Gap.** Length of service is disputed: **seven years** (Shmidt)
against **six** (Argentina.gob.ar), for the identical span **[A on both]**.

## 5. The Casa de Gobierno

He designs the **central archway** joining the former Post and Telegraph building to the
Government House, inheriting Carl Kihlberg's post office (1873-1879) and Aberg's proposal
**[A: en.wikipedia "Casa Rosada"; es. and it.wikipedia]**; accounts making him sole
architect of the whole palace are contradicted by that same source. His own memoir calls
the assignment "desagradable y demasiado complejo" because it had to use "la antigua casa
de Correos y Telégrafos y parte de la antigua residencia del Gobierno" **[A: Memorias, in
Shmidt pp.7-8]**, and, on the collision of German ground-floor windows with Renaissance
columns and Venetian first-floor windows under heavy German roofs, gives the fullest
statement of method he left: "he tratado de armonizar sin ligarme con esas partes"
**[A: Shmidt p.8 n.23, which also carries his note on the Rivadavia front]**.

## 6. The competitions and the office, 1884-1890

- **Palacio de Justicia, Plaza Lavalle.** Tamburini and the French engineer **Norbert
  Maillart** are the **only two invited** by the Executive; a Congressional commission
  picks Maillart and faults Tamburini's plan distribution **[A: Shmidt p.8]**. Date
  **1884-1885**, not "late 1880s" as an earlier draft had it. His defence asks for "el
  sello de la Edad Media", citing Vienna and Berlin **[A: Shmidt p.9]**. Shmidt fn.25 adds
  that he projected **three buildings for that one square**: the Palacio de Justicia, the
  Teatro Colón, and a **Museo de Productos Argentinos** at Libertad y Lavalle **[A]**.
- **Biblioteca Nacional**, unbuilt, on the old Cuartel de Bomberos site at Plaza Lorea. He
  takes an updated Hellenic vocabulary over an Egyptian one because neither the Parthenon
  nor the monuments of Pompeii "podían adoptarse en nuestros días" **[A: Memorias p.39, in
  Shmidt p.13]**. Coordinate derived from Plaza Lorea. **Gap.**
- **Departamento Central de Policía.** Shmidt p.16 resolves the division of labour the
  dossier left open: **Juan Antonio Buschiazzo**, public-works chief under intendente
  Torcuato de Alvear, drew the building in 1884 for a Plaza Lorea site; when the state
  bought a site a few blocks south the job passed to the national department, where
  "Tamburini confeccionó un nuevo proyecto que finalmente se construyó", and fn.46 adds
  "Paradójicamente, a la muerte de Tamburini, lo terminó Juan A Buschiazzo" **[A]**. An
  earlier draft attached fn.46 to the **Escuela Superior de Medicina**, which carries fn.45
  instead; the completion belongs to the **police building**.
- **Escuela Normal de Maestras de Buenos Aires**, extended in matching neo-Gothic with
  gardens near the future Palacio de Aguas Corrientes, the 1883 original being by the
  architect Bunge **[A: Shmidt fn.44]**; coordinate derived from the Av. Córdoba block.
  **Gap.** The **Escuela Normal de Maestras de Rosario** is in Shmidt's inventory under
  built works **[A]**, replacing a vague es.wikipedia listing of "multiple schools"; no
  address. **Gap.**
- **Hospital Militar Central**, "proyectado y construido en 1889 en la calle Pozos y
  Caseros", Tollet pavilion system **[A: Shmidt fn.47]**; the pool's coordinate pointed at
  the modern Palermo hospital, a twentieth-century relocation 6 km away. The **Aberg
  co-attribution caveat is dropped**: Shmidt lists it under built works and never raises it.
- **Congreso de la Nación**: filed by Shmidt under *otros proyectos*, not works. He drafts
  an unofficial scheme while the site is undecided, then enters **neither** competition,
  not Law 2204 of 1887 nor the one after Law 2461 of 1889 **[A: Shmidt fn.35]**; Meano wins
  the Congress by competition in 1895 **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The contradiction between
  it.wikipedia listing "Congress Palace" among his works and en.wikipedia crediting Meano
  therefore **dissolves**: one is a project, the other the built building.
- Private practice, distinct from the office: a **residence for President Miguel Juárez
  Celman** at 25 de Mayo 551, plus the **Banco Provincial** and **Mercado San Vicente** in
  Córdoba **[A: Shmidt inventory]**. The estate **La Elisa** is **[R]**, dossier only.

## 7. Córdoba

**Ampliación y reforma de la Casa de Gobierno de Córdoba**, 1886 **[A: Shmidt]**. The
**Teatro Rivera Indarte**, now Teatro del Libertador General San Martín, is designed 1887
for the Calle Ancha, built by **José Franceschi**, and proposed to Governor **Ambrosio
Olmos** by **Ramón J. Cárcano** **[A: muva-faud-unc.org; es.wikipedia]**; it is
**inaugurated 26 April 1891** **[A: es.wikipedia]**, the strongest circumstantial argument
in the file for a death before that date, since no source records him there. Its coordinate
is corrected by about 500 m. The claim that the façade copies Naples's Teatro di San Carlo
is **[R]**, carried by no source reached, and is omitted from the journey.

The **Banco Provincial de Córdoba** is built in two years under Franceschi and inaugurated
**17 May 1889**, its interior compared by the bank to Versailles, with a stained-glass
ceiling over the central hall and plasterwork by **Arturo Nembrini Gonzaga**
**[A: Bancor]**. Two coordinates for it circulate about 40 m apart; one is used throughout
and the fact is stated. The **Hospital Nacional de Clínicas**, Santa Rosa 1564, leaves
**[R]** because es.wikipedia names Tamburini as designer of the original building **[A]**;
Shmidt confirms the commission passed to the national department after the province
rejected local proposals, describes the pavilion type sited away from the city, records
that he himself flagged the ornament as *suntuoso*, and adds in fn.49: "según el Registro
Oficial, el autor sería el Ingeniero Camici-Roncoroni." **Left standing.** The building
opened **24 May 1913**; its coordinate is corrected by about 2.5 km.

## 8. The Colón, the crash, the death

He **resigns** the national post to practise independently and take the Colón, which
Shmidt says he had "almost won in advance"; no exact date exists **[A on the fact; R on
the day]**. Shmidt attributes the new theatre to intendente **Torcuato de Alvear**'s
initiative to move the old Colón, "realizado por el ingeniero italiano Enrique Pellegrini
y situado al norte de la Casa Rosada, frente a la Plaza de Mayo" **[A]**, which makes
Pellegrini his predecessor on the function, not a collaborator.

**A claim withdrawn.** The dossier's line that impresario **Angelo (Ángel) Ferrari** won
an 1889 concession to build the Colón is **not in the source it was attributed to**:
teatrocolon.org.ar/el-teatro/ was fetched twice with targeted prompts and contains no
Ferrari, no empresario and no 1889 licitación. Shmidt never names him either. **Dropped
from the journey**, recorded here as needing a source **[R, unsourced]**.

**Cornerstone 25 May 1890**, completion targeted for **12 October 1892**, the fourth
centenary of Columbus **[A: teatrocolon.org.ar]**; en.wikipedia's looser 1889 start year
is recorded, not preferred. Two months later comes the **Revolución del Parque, 26-29 July
1890**, and with it the **worst coordinate in the pool**: the rising took the **Parque de
Artillería in San Nicolás, the site of today's Plaza Lavalle**, not Parque Tres de Febrero
in Palermo, 4.5 km away, and the same article notes the park stood "frente a las obras
recién iniciadas del Teatro Colón" **[A: es.wikipedia]**. His own presence at the rising is
nowhere attested, so the stop sits at his building site, not at the park. His ruin is
stated directly by it.wikipedia, the crisis and inflation having made him "perdere tutte le
ricchezze investite sino ad allora" **[A]**, though no estate inventory exists to quantify
it. **Gap.**

**The letter to Colocci**, verbatim in it.wikipedia: "E fede ci vuole! Torneranno tempi
migliori, si rifarà qualche cosa, e allora... vento alle vele!" The cited authority is
Fabio Mariano, not fetched **[A on the wording; R on date and place of writing]**; the
dossier had truncated the quotation and the full form is used. **Death year unresolved**:
es.wikipedia's infobox says **1890**, its own category files him under **Fallecidos en
1891**, teatrocolon.org.ar says **1891**, IIC Córdoba prints **1848-1891**, and no primary
record was reached. **Not adjudicated**; the stop is dated 1891 for ordering.

## 9. Afterlife

His technical report **"Teatro Colón"** is printed posthumously in *Memoria del
Departamento de Obras Públicas de la Nación 1889-1891*, t.3, pp.59-76 (1892), and is still
cited as a primary source **[A: Signos Históricos view/538]**. **Ascoli Piceno dedicates
commemorations to him in 1892** **[A: it.wikipedia]**; what they consisted of is not
described. **Gap.** **Meano's tribute**: "a él solo pertenece el mérito de la idea general
del proyecto" **[A: Brandariz p.37]**.

**Late 1899**: Buschiazzo leads a campaign to demolish the unfinished Colón **[A: Shmidt]**.
A **municipal inspection commission** then calls the project "reñido con todas las reglas
de la estética" and the theatre "el producto de una empresa de lucro y de especulación en
que poco se ha tenido absolutamente en cuenta la seguridad de los concurrentes" **[A]**.
**Correction, two parts.** (1) This is **not** the 1899 commission: Shmidt writes "Años
después" of that campaign and Susini published in July 1904, so it falls in **1899-1904**.
(2) The periodical is **Arquitectura N°6, 1904, p.59**, not "Revista Arquitectura", and
transmission is two-stage, Shmidt quoting Susini quoting the commission; the words are the
commission's. Meanwhile construction stalls after **1894** for money, **Meano is murdered
in 1904**, the Belgian **Jules Dormal** completes the house with structural changes and a
stronger French character, and it opens **25 May 1908** with Aida, the Salón Dorado
unfinished **[A: teatrocolon.org.ar]**. en.wikipedia records **no curse legend by name**,
but its own sequence, the death mid-project, Meano's murder, and a **1910 anarchist
bombing** absent from the dossier entirely, is what popular retellings fuse into a "curse
of the Colón" **[A on the sequence; R on the legend]**.

A **watercolour of his original Colón project** in the **Archivo General de la Nación** was
scanned in **December 2006** and released on Wikimedia Commons **[A: Commons]**. The
**Museo Arquitecto Francisco Tamburini** occupies the Córdoba bank **[A: Bancor]**; a
tribute there on **29 January 2016** is described by the IIC Córdoba as inaugurating the
museum space **[A]**, conflicting with the bank's own page dating the naming to **2009**.
**Not resolved.** The standard modern edition, the 1997 Casa Rosada catalogue *La obra de
Francesco Tamburini en Argentina*, survives in the CPAU catalogue as a **single
non-circulating copy, inventory 07798** **[R: CPAU.1.15689]**: the field's central reference
text is effectively an archival object. A 23-minute docufiction, *La costruzione del potere*
(2011), is **[R: Marche Film Commission 375, synopsis and cast unpopulated]**. Finally the
**Masonic claim, impossible as stated**: the
Grand Lodge of Argentina calls him "el masón italiano Francesco Tamburini" and says he
completed its headquarters, while the same page dates that building to **1872**, twelve
years before he reached Argentina **[R]**. Its second architect is **Charles Pellegrini**,
father of the president, credited with the earlier Plaza de Mayo building, **not** "Jacques
Henry Pellegrini" and **not** a collaborator. No lodge register found. Kept here, kept out
of the journey.

## Contradictions recorded and left standing

Birth year 1846 vs 1848; death year 1890 vs 1891; service seven years vs six; Colón start
1889 vs cornerstone 1890; Clínicas authorship Tamburini vs Camici-Roncoroni; museum founded
2009 vs 2016; the Masonic headquarters dated 1872 on the page that credits him; arrival
February 1884 vs bare 1884 vs late 1883. All eight are sourced on both sides above.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Francesco Tamburini is **not** on `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing found requires adding him:
no theoretical framework of his is applied backward to material he never treated. Two
things are flagged rather than silently dropped, both **forward-facing**.

**1. The posthumous life of his own objects.** The last segment carries stops for the 1892
printing of his Colón report, the 1899-1904 demolition campaign and inspection commission,
the 1908 opening under Dormal, the 2006 digitisation of his watercolour, and the Córdoba
museum: later hands on **his own buildings and papers**, which the standing rule allows on
his own pins, and which the corpus already does, since
`juan_antonio_buschiazzo.journey.json` closes on his nephew's 1940 restoration and a 1991
excavation. No later **atlas subject** is named in any of them. Buschiazzo, who is named,
died in 1917 and overlapped Tamburini in life and in office, so that is a live relation,
not a pointer at a file stamped after his clock stopped.

**2. Relations dropped for shared geography alone**, listed so the operator can see where
they went: **Carlos Thays** (same city, same years, no attested contact), **Pio
Collivadino**, **Anna Pavlova**, **Eugène Py**, **Max Glucksmann** (later users of the
Colón), **Francisco Gianotti** and **Viktor Sulcic** (later events at the Casa Rosada).
None is named in a campa; the map already shows co-location.

Named in campas, each with a direct relation: **Julio Argentino Roca** (recruited and
employed him), **Torcuato de Alvear** (whose initiative to move the old Colón produced the
commission), **Juan Antonio Buschiazzo** (superseded on the police headquarters, finished
it at his death, led the 1899 campaign against the Colón). Meano, Dormal, Maillart, Aberg,
Kihlberg, Franceschi, Juárez Celman, Olmos, Cárcano, Pellegrini and De Bosis are also
named; none has a journey file.

## Honest gaps

- **No civil or baptismal record** for the birth, neither year preferred; **no exact
  resignation date**, only "after seven years".
- **No burial record, cemetery listing, or grave marker.** Both Wikipedias end at the
  death with no mention of interment, and a Find A Grave search returned no accessible
  result. The journey closes on this as a stated gap rather than inventing a grave.
- **Buildings not located**: the Istituto Tecnico Benincasa; San Francesco alle Scale and
  Sant'Agostino; the Rosario normal school. Derived from named streets: the Biblioteca
  Nacional, the Buenos Aires normal school, the Hospital Militar, the Juárez Celman house.
- **The Ferrari concession** has no source and is withdrawn; **the Masonic affiliation**
  has no register and stays out of the journey; **the 1907 Marulli purchase** and **the six
  Pisa plates** rest on documents not fetched.
