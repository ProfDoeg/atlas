# David Sassoon (1792-1864): research report

*Baghdad treasurer, Bombay merchant, founder of David Sassoon & Co. Compiled 2026-08 from the English Wikipedia articles listed at the end; Cecil Roth, "The Sassoon Dynasty" (1941), full-text OCR via Internet Archive, identifier `in.ernet.dli.2015.209865`; and the Jewish Encyclopedia (1901-1906) entry "Sassoon".*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Where a claim in the research pool was corrected during verification, the correction is stated in place rather than applied silently.

---

## 1. Baghdad, 1792-1829

**Birth.** Baghdad, Ottoman Iraq, October 1792, son of Sheikh Sassoon ben Saleh, chief treasurer (*sarraf bashi*) to the pashas of Baghdad and head of the city's Jewish community, and of Amam Gabbai **[A: Wikipedia, "David Sassoon (treasurer)"]**. No day of the month is reachable; the journey file defaults to the first and says so in `date_confidence`.

**Office.** He served as treasurer of Baghdad under the Ottoman governors, c. 1817-1829, the post effectively inherited through his father's standing **[A: same; the family's two-generation hold also in "Sassoon family"]**. It is the hinge of the whole life: both the source of the capital that crossed the Gulf and the reason the family had to cross it.

**Marriages and children.** Hannah Joseph, married 1818, four children, died 1826 **[A]**. Farha Hyeem, married 1828, ten more children **[A]**. The second wife's name is rendered variously (Farha Hyeem, Farha Hai Gubbay); recorded, not resolved.

**Elias.** Second son, born Baghdad 27 March 1820 **[A: Wikipedia, "Elias David Sassoon"]**. The pool's claim that he was born "shortly before the family's flight" is **wrong**: the flight is 1828 and the move to Bombay 1832, so Elias was eight at the flight and twelve on arrival in India, not an infant.

**Dawud Pasha.** The persecution of the Jewish notables of Baghdad by the wali Dawud Pasha, last of the Mamluk governors, is given as the direct cause of the family's flight **[A: Wikipedia, "David Sassoon (treasurer)" and "Sassoon family"]**. Roth's characterisation, fetched verbatim:

> Daoud Pasha was not only the last, but also the most tyrannical of the Slave Governors. His maltreatment of the Jews in his Pashalik was particularly brutal.
> (Roth, p. 33)

**The death sentence and the escape.** Roth, p. 33, unbroken:

> But in the meantime, in 1829, the news of what had happened became known in the city. The Vali swore revenge, began a thorough-going persecution of the Jews on a more systematic plan than before, and, of course, gave orders for this arch-enemy to be hanged.

The pool quoted this sentence with an internal ellipsis and glossed it as an OCR artefact. It is not: the ellipsis deleted a substantive clause (the systematic persecution of the community). The full sentence is used here and in the journey file. **Roth himself footnotes "some other, slightly less dramatic, accounts of the reasons that led David Sassoon to leave Baghdad."** That concession is carried into the journey file's prose rather than suppressed **[A for the words; R for the escape narrative they support]**.

And p. 34:

> Accompanied by his octogenarian father, and probably assisted by disguise and judicious bribery, David Sassoon escaped from Baghdad by night.

Note the hedge inside Roth's own sentence. No contemporary document of the escape was reachable; the night flight is **[R]**, resting on a 1941 secondary account.

## 2. The Gulf, 1828-1832

**Basra.** The flight route down the Tigris is described only in general terms; no reachable source names Basra as a stop. It is kept in the journey file as an explicitly tagged **[R]** waypoint, with the reason in `date_confidence`.

**Bushehr.** The family relocated to Bushehr (Bushire) on the Persian shore, c. 1828/1829 to 1832 **[A: "Sassoon family"]**. The flight date (1828 in some passages, 1829 in others) is inconsistent across articles and within the pool; flagged, not resolved.

**The father's death.** Sassoon ben Saleh died 1830 **[A for the year]**. The place is **not** attested: "probably Bushehr or the Basra region" is the best the sources permit, and no grave is named. Recorded as a gap.

## 3. Bombay, 1832-1864

**Arrival, firm, method.** Arrived 1832 as a refugee; founded David Sassoon & Co. (later & Sons) the same year as a middleman between British textile firms and Persian Gulf commodity merchants; reinvested profits into Bombay harbour properties; learned working Hindustani to deal directly **[A: Wikipedia, "David Sassoon (treasurer)"]**. "By the 1830s, Sassoon had become one of the wealthiest people in India" **[A: same]**; the pool's "by the later 1830s" is an unsourced narrowing, reverted. His own account of the cause, per the Jewish Encyclopedia (1901-1906):

> the employment of his sons as his agents and to his strict observance of the law of tithe

**Residences.** 9 Tamarind Street, Fort, on arrival **[R: address given in Wikipedia, "David Sassoon (treasurer)", but the building is not extant and the plot is locatable only to the Fort district]**. Later Sans Souci, a former palace in Byculla, afterwards donated and now Masina Hospital **[A]**. The year of the move is not given anywhere reachable; a gap.

**Naturalisation.** British subject 1853, signing the Oath of Allegiance in Hebrew "due to his poor command of English" **[A: Wikipedia, "David Sassoon (treasurer)"]**. **Correction:** the pool placed the signing at Pune. No source supports this; his base in 1853 was Bombay, and the attribution is dropped, with the correction stated in the journey file's `date_confidence`.

**Observance.** Kept Baghdadi dress, the Sabbath, and Orthodox practice throughout, while permitting his sons English manners **[A]**. Roth preserves two contemporary descriptions, one attributed and one not:

> He was then advanced in years, and his grave countenance, commanding figure, rich turban and flowing robes made up a picture worth beholding.
> (Sir Richard Temple, quoted in Roth, p. 53)

> Everything in the outward man heightened the dignity of his presence
> (unattributed contemporary, quoted in Roth, p. 53)

Temple's memoir was not located; the words reach us only through Roth **[A for Roth's page, R for the underlying attribution]**.

## 4. The China line, 1842-1864

**Opening.** The Treaty of Nanking (1842) opened the treaty ports and the firm moved in immediately after **[A]**.

**Hong Kong: a flat contradiction, flagged.** Wikipedia, "David Sassoon & Co.", lists the branch as **est. 1843**. The pool and several summaries give **1844** and tie the opening to Elias's voyage, which the earlier date makes impossible since Elias reached Canton in 1844. The Keyserling interlock file gives a third date, **1858**. All three are recorded; the journey file uses 1843 and states the conflict.

**Canton.** Elias reached Canton in 1844, aged about twenty-four **[A: "Elias David Sassoon"]**. Roth, p. 48:

> As a result, in 1844, after he had been settled in Bombay for a little more than ten years, he had the idea of dispatching one of his sons to the Far East to supervise the operations of the firm. It was the second and (apparently) most enterprising of the house, Elias, whom he chose for the purpose.

**Shanghai.** Elias judged Shanghai's prospects superior and moved the China operation there in 1845, opening an office in the British concession, partnered with his elder brother Albert; the family fleet came to carry roughly one fifth of all opium imported into China **[A: "Elias David Sassoon"]**.

**The triangular trade.** Indian opium and raw cotton east; silver, tea, and silk back; English manufactures sold into India on the proceeds **[A]**. Roth, p. 49:

> the most lucrative part of the trade from British India to China and beyond (and, later on, much of the direct trade from England to the Far East) came into the hands of the firm of David Sassoon, Sons, & Company

**England.** Roth, p. 50:

> London now became a main centre of the firm's operations, and as a natural consequence branches were established also in Liverpool and Manchester.

Roth's context places this c. 1858; no precise month is given **[A for the fact, R for the year]**.

**Reach.** The Jewish Encyclopedia summarises the house as having

> branches at Calcutta, Shanghai, Canton, and Hongkong; and his business, which included a monopoly of the opium-trade, extended as far as Yokohama, Nagasaki, and other cities in Japan.

"Monopoly" is the encyclopedia's word, written forty years after his death: a period judgement, not a measured share **[A for the quotation, R for its accuracy]**.

**Cotton.** With American exports disrupted 1861-1865, the firm expanded its cotton trade into the Lancashire shortage; by the 1860s the family had outstripped the established Parsi houses, and by the 1870s dominated the China opium trade against rivals including Jardine Matheson **[A]**.

## 5. Philanthropy and community, 1850s-1864

- **Magen David Synagogue, Byculla.** Funded and built by him; extended c. 1910 by his grandson Jacob **[A]**. **Date contested:** Roth, p. 59, says 1861; the article on the synagogue gives a construction year of 1864 while elsewhere implying 1861 through a "150th anniversary" in 2011. The journey file uses 1861 and flags the conflict. **Coordinate correction:** 18.96696, 72.83207 (340 Sir J.J. Road), not the pool's 18.9767, 72.8322, about 1.1 km north. Roth, p. 59, on what he attached to it: "he erected a hospice for travellers, a ritual bath, and a Talmud Torah, or institution for the education of Jewish youth" **[A]**.
- **David Sassoon Benevolent Institution**, schooling poor Jewish immigrants arriving in Bombay, alongside trusts for hospitals, synagogues, libraries, and cemeteries **[A]**. No founding year is reachable; the journey file dates it to mid-decade and says so.
- **Victoria and Albert Museum, Byculla** (now Dr Bhau Daji Lad). The pool tagged this **[R]**; it should be **[A]**: the museum article names David Sassoon among the funding patrons with Sir Jamsetjee Jejeebhoy and Jagannath Shunkerseth. Established 1855, opened 2 May 1872, renamed 1 November 1975. Coordinate corrected to 18.97947, 72.83481.
- **Poona:** Ohel David Synagogue, a school, a Mechanics' Institute, a library, a convalescent home, and the **Elderly and Destitute Persons Home** (1863) **[A]**. A **Lady Rachel Sassoon Dispensary** is named in the pool but unlocated **[R]**; no stop.
- **"Institute of Science / Elphinstone precinct"**: a summary inference in the pool with no building and no sum **[R]**. Not used; recorded as a gap.

**The sons' education.** Roth, p. 55, quoting a visitor from Ottoman Palestine in 1854: "All of them are pious and learned." **Two corrections:** the passage is on p. 55, not p. 54, and the remark is about **his sons**, not about the Baghdadi community of Bombay at large (Roth's frame: "he saw to it that his sons ... all had a thorough Jewish education in the traditional style"). The speaker is "an ecstatic visitor from Palestine", a Jewish visitor from Ottoman Palestine; the pool's label "Palestinian visitor" is avoided.

## 6. Death, 1864

Died at Poona, 7 November 1864, aged 72 **[A]**. Buried in a Gothic-revival mausoleum inside the compound of the Ohel David Synagogue he had endowed, architect Henry Saint Clair Wilkins; the synagogue was built 1863-1867 and consecrated 29 September 1867, three years after his death; locally the Laal Deval, the red temple **[A: Wikipedia, "Ohel David Synagogue"]**. **Coordinate correction:** 18.5192, 73.8747; the pool's afterlife lens carried 18.5314, 73.8446, about 3.5 km off and contradicting its own geography lens. The congregation shrank from over a hundred families to a remnant, with controlled access **[A]**; "largest synagogue in Asia outside Israel" is unattributed at source and is stated in the journey file as a description **[R]**.

## 7. The estate and the afterlife of the name

- **Succession.** Albert (Abdullah) Sassoon took control of the firm, 1864 **[A]**. **The split:** Elias broke away in 1867 after a dispute with Albert over leadership, founding E.D. Sassoon & Co. with offices in Bombay and Shanghai in direct competition **[A]**.
- **Statue and library.** Woolner's marble statue completed 1865, commissioned by **Sir Bartle Frere** as Governor of Bombay, funded by subscription from the Jewish community, traders, and Englishmen; a Woolner bust above the portico. Building 1867-1870, architects J. Campbell and G. E. Gosling; Sassoon gave Rs. 60,000 of a Rs. 125,000 cost, the idea reportedly Albert's. UNESCO listing 2018; restored 2023 under Abha Narain Lambah **[A]**. **Coordinate correction:** 18.9278, 72.8311, not 18.9327, 72.8347.
- **Sassoon General Hospital, Pune.** Founded 1867 on his donation, initially 144 patients, affiliated with Byramjee Jeejeebhoy Government Medical College **[A]**. **Correction:** 1,296 beds per the infobox, not "over 1,500". Coordinate 18.5258, 73.8719. Gandhi's appendectomy here, **12 January 1924** **[A]**.
  - Meher Baba's birth here (25 February 1894) is asserted by the hospital article but **not** by the Meher Baba article, which says only "in Pune"; standard biographies place it at the family home. Downgraded to **[R]**, omitted from the journey file. The pool's "musician-mystic" is also wrong.
  - **Jacob Sassoon Hospital**, adjacent, foundation stone 1906, opened 1909, Royal Doulton tiles by Margaret Thompson **[A]**. **Kinship correction:** Jacob was David's **grandson** (son of Elias), not great-nephew; the Sassoon General Hospital article contradicts "Knesseth Eliyahoo Synagogue", which says plainly "grandson of David Sassoon".
- **Sassoon Docks**, Colaba, built by Albert, opened 1875, bought for the Bombay Port Trust 1879, still a major fish market **[A]**. Coordinate corrected to 18.9116, 72.8262.
- **Chinchpokli Jewish Cemetery**, founded 1878 by Elias for his son Joseph (died Shanghai 1868) **[A]**. No stop: no coordinate was verifiable.
- **Elias's death.** 21 March 1880 at **Galle**, not Colombo as the pool had it (a 100 km error); buried Bombay **[A]**.
- **Knesseth Eliyahoo Synagogue**, Fort, 1884, built by Jacob Elias Sassoon for his father; Gostling & Morris; 18.92811, 72.83257 **[A]**. The pool's plural "Elias's sons" is wrong.

## 8. The dynasty after India

- **Arthur Sassoon** (1840-1912), fifth son, at 6 Queen's Gardens (1881) and from 1883 until his death at **8 King's Gardens, Hove** (Grade II listed, 50.8225, -0.1636); Edward VII stayed with him in 1898, 1907, 1908, and 1910, often to see Alice Keppel **[A]**. **Correction:** the pool put this house on Eastern Terrace, Kemptown, which was **Albert's** address ("of Kensington Gore and of Eastern Terrace").
- **Sir Edward Albert Sassoon**, grandson, Conservative MP 1899-1912, married Aline Caroline de Rothschild of the Paris house **[A for both facts; no marriage date in the sources consulted, so 1887, the year generally cited, is carried as [R]]**. **Sir Philip Sassoon** inherited the seat and became Under-Secretary of State for Air, 1912-1939 **[A]**.
- **Siegfried Sassoon**, born 8 September 1886 at Weirleigh, Matfield, Kent, David's great-grandson by David > Sassoon David Sassoon > Alfred Ezra Sassoon (1861-1895) > Siegfried **[A]**. The pool's coordinate is about 2.8 km off; Matfield is 51.1488, 0.3648. Recorded here, not given a stop.
- **David Solomon Sassoon** (1880-1942), bibliophile. **Material correction:** the pool said his collection "comes to be housed at the British Library". False. The dedicated article records auction by Sotheby's in Zurich and New York between 1975 and 1994, most of the residue now at the **University of Toronto**, only a small cluster at the British Library. No stop: this is a dispersal, not a place.
- **Sassoon House, Shanghai.** Built by **Sir Victor Sassoon** of **E.D. Sassoon & Co.**, Elias's breakaway house and not the parent firm; 1926-1929, architects Palmer and Turner, Cathay Hotel inside, renamed Peace Hotel 1956 **[A]**. Coordinate 31.2411, 121.4846, not the pool's 31.2396, 121.4907. The pool's **[R]** hedge on the Victor Sassoon attribution can be retired.
- **Egypt and Curacao.** Elias Nissim Joseph Sassoon co-founded Banque du Caire, 1952; nationalisations from 1957; expulsion through Alexandria, 1966 **[A]**. The Providence Group formed in **1961 in Switzerland**, moving to **Curacao only in 1983**; the pool's "1961 onward" for Willemstad collapses a 22-year gap **[A]**.
- **Collapse of the network.** "The wealthier followed the path of the Sassoon family to Britain"; Calcutta fell from about 500 Baghdadi families to under 100 by the 1970s; Burma's rabbinate left by 1969 **[A: "Baghdadi Jews"]**.

## 9. Contradictions recorded and not resolved

1. **Hong Kong branch:** 1843 vs 1844 vs 1858. The 1843 date breaks the common framing tying the branch to Elias's 1844 voyage.
2. **Magen David Synagogue:** 1861 (Roth, and a 150th anniversary in 2011) vs 1864 (the construction year in the same article).
3. **Flight from Baghdad:** 1828 vs 1829; and Roth's footnote concedes explanations that do not involve a death sentence.
4. **Farha Hyeem / Farha Hai Gubbay:** two renderings of the second wife's name.
5. **Jacob Sassoon's kinship:** grandson vs great-nephew, across two Wikipedia articles.
6. **Meher Baba's birthplace:** Sassoon Hospital vs unspecified Pune.
7. **Death place of Sassoon ben Saleh:** Bushehr or the Basra region; unsettled.
8. **David Solomon Sassoon's collection:** British Library vs auction dispersal with a Toronto residue.

## 10. Gaps stated as gaps

- No contemporary document of the flight from Baghdad. The night escape, the disguise, and the bribery rest on Roth (1941), who hedges them himself.
- The Basra leg is unattested and tagged **[R]** wherever used.
- No year for the move from Tamarind Street to Sans Souci; no founding year for the Benevolent Institution.
- Temple's memoir was not located; his description reaches us only through Roth. Two further vivid Roth quotations ("Everything in the outward man...", "In public life, he was ever fervent...") are printed with no named speaker, and are period voice rather than identified testimony.
- No verifiable coordinate for the Chinchpokli Jewish Cemetery or the Lady Rachel Sassoon Dispensary; neither gets a stop rather than an invented pin.
- His specific contribution to the Byculla museum is not itemised, only his membership among the funding patrons.
- "Largest synagogue in Asia outside Israel" is unattributed at source.
