# Parzival: Soltane to the Grail Throne: research report

## Sources
Primary canon: Wolfram von Eschenbach, *Parzival* (Books I-XVI), in Jessie L. Weston's 1894 public-domain verse translation (Project Gutenberg #47297, Books I-IX verified directly against the source text; Books X-XVI summarized from Weston's Book II and secondary synopses since that volume was not directly fetchable in this session). Geography and chronology: Werner Greub, *How the Grail Sites Were Found: Wolfram von Eschenbach as a Historian* (Goetheanum, Dornach, 1974; Eng. trans. Robert J. Kelder, Willehalm Institute, Amsterdam, 1999/2013), read via the Willehalm Institute's blog serialization (chapters 11 "Bertane," 12 "Terre de Salvaesche," 13 "Schastel Marveil," and the closing chronological summary "Wolfram the Historian"). Confirming/supplementary: Rudolf Steiner's 1913 Arlesheim identification as reported by Emil Bock; local-history material on Rengersmatt/Ränggersmatt, the Ermitage Arlesheim, and the Proserpina Grotto.

## Judgment calls
- **Chronology skeleton is Greub's, not mine.** Every date in the file (826 tournament, 827 birth, May 841 departure, June 841 Pelrapeire, Sept 19 842 failed question, Sept 21 842 Round Table, spring 848 return, May 9-13 848 Whitsun climax) is lifted directly from Greub's own summary table, which he in turn derives from Wolfram's internal planetary-position references. I did not invent a single date; I only distributed the plot's own scenes across it.
- **The redemption scene (Book XVI) has no Weston text I could verify verbatim.** Weston's Vol. I (Gutenberg #47297) covers only Books I-IX; Vol. II (Books X-XVI, containing the Illberg/Mönchsberg duels, Feirefiss, Cundrie's second coming, and the healing of Anfortas) was not reachable in this session. Those five stops are built from Greub's prose summary plus the well-attested plot beats of the poem; the one quote I give for the healing ("Uncle, what ails thee?") is the canonical form of the question as summarized by Greub, flagged accordingly in `quote_source` rather than cited to a specific verse line.
- **Coordinates inside the Arlesheim Terre de salvaesche cluster are estimates.** Rengersmatt farm, the rock arch, Hornikopf/Munsalvaesche, the Proserpina Grotto, Stollenrain and the Birs camp all sit within about 1.5 km of each other; Greub's book gives a numbered site map, not GPS, so I placed each point by relative position on that map against known modern landmarks (the real Ermitage Arlesheim park, the real Ränggersmatt farmhouse on Schönmattweg, the real Proserpinagrotte). All are marked `traditional`.
- **"National mythology" register extended to an esoteric-research tradition.** This is neither scripture nor a folk epic in living cult use; it is a medieval poem read, per Steiner and Greub, as literal ninth-century history. I narrated it exactly that way, present tense, the poem's own miracles (the bleeding lance, the Grail's self-feeding, Sigune's incorruption, the sword mended at a spring) as plain fact, per the brief's instruction to treat an esotericist's claimed cosmology descriptively without editorializing.
- **No Spanish duplicate fields.** The brief asked for English and Spanish prose; the fixed schema (matched byte-for-byte to 109 sibling files, none of which carry bilingual fields, including Spanish-canon subjects like Alvear and Bolívar) has no room for a second-language `campa`. I kept the JSON English-only, consistent with the whole corpus's actual practice, and did not fork a duplicate file since only one `.journey.json` was requested.
- **Register not registered in `index.json`.** The task specified two deliverables; I left the index untouched since a `parzival` entry isn't there yet and a build script (`encode_journeys.py`) may regenerate it.

## Time-folds / gaps
Segment 7 ("The Years of Wandering") compresses roughly five and a half years (Sept 842 to March 848) into three stops, matching how Wolfram himself treats the period: two named tournaments (Bearosche, Schampfanzun) in the first six weeks, then a jump-cut straight to the mended sword at the spring, with the intervening years narrated only as an aggregate ("four years and a half and three days," per Trevrizent's own psalter-count, which I used as the load-bearing verification of the gap rather than inventing incident to fill it).

## The five richest episodes
1. **The birds and the question "Who may God be?"** (Soltane, c. 831): the whole Grail failure is seeded here, in a child's first theological question, years before he can know it matters.
2. **The bleeding lance and the failed question** (Munsalvaesche, Sept 19, 842): Gurnemanz's good manners become Parzival's fatal silence at the one table where silence is the sin.
3. **Cundrie's curse and the renunciation of God** (the Birs camp, Sept 21, 842): Parzival's own furious "doth He hate me, His hate I'll face!" is the hinge the entire six-year wandering swings on.
4. **Trevrizent's grotto, Good Friday** (848): the confession scene doubles as a geography lesson: the spear Parzival unknowingly carried out of this same cave after the Orilus fight is dated to the day by the hermit's psalter, which is also how Wolfram (and Greub) pin down the "five and a half years."
5. **The three blood-drops, twice, six years apart** (Stollenrain, Sept 21, 842 and May 13, 848): failure and redemption sharing one coordinate, which is the whole register of this dataset in miniature, per the curator's brief.

## Connections to the atlas
Parzival's uncle **Kyot** is, per Greub's identification, the historical **Willehalm of Toulouse and Orange**: meaning this journey shares a real family tree and a real landscape (Arlesheim, Montpellier, the Rhine) with any future `willehalm.journey.json` built from the companion volume of Greub's research, and it explains why Kyot's biography (Grail quest 819-822, Sigune's birth, Schoysiane's death) is summarized rather than dramatized here: it belongs to his own dataset. The **Joan of Arc** journey and this one never touch geographically, but both treat a medieval war-epic (trial record vs. verse romance) as literal history under this atlas's "the canon is true" register, and both hinge a whole life on a single spoken/unspoken sentence (Joan's testimony vs. Parzival's silence). Anyone building **Rudolf Steiner** or a Goetheanum-adjacent figure later should note the direct link already placed in the final stop: Steiner's 1913 foundation-stone speech at Dornach explicitly invokes this same Grail stream.

## Verification pass (2026-07-20)

A second session verified the dataset end to end and repaired it in place. `json_check.py` now passes with zero WARN lines: 9 segments, 43 stops, 23 quoted.

**Chronology confirmed against Greub directly.** The Blogger dynamic-views wall that blocked the first session yields to the Blogger JSON feed (`/feeds/posts/default?alt=json`); Greub's chapter 12 text was pulled whole from it. His own words confirm the skeleton: "On September 19, 842 ... the day Parzival was invited by Anfortas to come to Munsalvaesche"; "Good Friday, March 23, 848"; "On Whitsun Saturday in the year 848 Cundrie along with Parzival and Feirefiss also returned on this road"; "rode away from home in the spring of 841". The computus checks: Julian Easter 848 falls on March 25, so Good Friday is March 23 and Whitsunday May 13, exactly the dataset's dates. Trevrizent's psalter-count also closes exactly under Greub's reading of "fuenfthalp jar" as five and a half years: September 20, 842 plus five years, six months and three days is March 23, 848, to the day. Weston translates the same phrase "four years and a half"; the quote stays as Weston wrote it, the dates stay as Greub computed them, and the divergence is a translator's fork, not an error.

**Chronology fixes.** Two: (1) Pelrapeire's first two stops were dated June 1-2, 841, before the June 4 parting from Gurnemanz they follow; moved to June 5-6 (canon has the Graharz-Pelrapeire ride take a day). (2) The third Sigune meeting, which the campa correctly calls a Saturday (Cundrie provisions her cell each Saturday), was dated March 18, a Sunday in 848; moved to Saturday March 17.

**Quotes.** Sixteen of the nineteen quotes were grepped verbatim against Gutenberg #47297; all check out (earlier misses were line-wrap artifacts of the verse layout). One touch-up: Weston capitalizes "Bon fils, Cher fils, Beau fils." More importantly, Weston's Vol. II IS on Gutenberg, as #47298, contrary to the first session's note above. The Whitsuntide stops were re-grounded against it: the healing question is now Weston's verbatim "What aileth thee here, mine uncle?" instead of the Greub paraphrase, and four formerly quote-less stops gained verbatim canon: Parzival casting away his weapon on learning his foe is Gawain (Book XIV), Feirefiss refusing to fight an unarmed knight (Book XV), Cundrie's "read is the mystic writing" proclamation (Book XV, and that stop's source was corrected from Book XVI to XV), and the burial of Sigune beside Schionatulander (Book XVI). The departure-from-Pelrapeire quote was likewise re-attributed from Book V to Book IV, where Weston places it.

**Canon corrections.** Two scene details had drifted: (1) the sword that breaks in the Feirefiss duel is the one Parzival robbed from dead Ither, breaking on the heathen's helmet because God no longer willed him lord of a stolen blade (Weston XV.175-177), not the mended Lac sword against a magic blade; (2) at the second Sigune meeting (Book V) she sits aloft in a linden tree with the embalmed body, not yet walled into the hermit cell, which belongs to Book IX. Both campas corrected; the myth itself was not touched anywhere, and every miracle stands as told.

**Coordinates.** Fourteen stops spot-checked (Nominatim plus Greub's own site list): Colmar, Besancon, Champagnole, Nantua, Grenoble, Montpellier, Porrentruy, Kembs, the Mulhouse Illberg and the whole Arlesheim cluster (Ermitage, Hornikopf, Rengersmatt, Winterhalde, Stollenrain) all verify. Three fixed: the Cluse viewpoint sat east of Nantua on the wrong side of town, moved into the actual defile between Montreal-la-Cluse and Nantua (46.178, 5.588); the fountain of Lac sat in the Courtavon valley, moved to the Largue's true source at the foot of the Glaserberg by Oberlarg (47.446, 7.238), with the ref reworded to match; Breisacherwald sat 4 km north of its own ref's "between Niffer and Petit-Landau", moved to 47.693, 7.518.

**Stop count.** Left at 43. Books X-XIII and most of XIV are Gawain's journey, not Parzival's; every named station of Parzival's own itinerary in Books III-XVI is already present, so padding would dilute, not complete.

### Quotes
All 23 quotes were checked mechanically, character by character, against the full Weston text downloaded from Project Gutenberg (#47297 and #47298 both, whitespace, verse line-numbers, accents and OCR noise normalised out). Twenty-one were already verbatim. Three repairs:

- **Stop 11 was a paraphrase, not canon.** "If my mother had not advised me to come here that day when I left her" is Greub's sentence, cited to Parzival 169:11-12, and appears nowhere in Wolfram's parting scene at Graharz. Replaced with what Wolfram actually has Parzival say there (Weston, Book III): *"Little wisdom have I, yet if ever the day I know when I win knightly fame and honour, so that maiden I well may woo, thou shalt give unto me Liassé, thy daughter so fair and true."* This is a better scene as well as a true one: the boy does not renounce Liassé, he pledges to earn her.
- **Stop 17 contained an unmarked elision.** The quote silently spliced out Weston's parenthetical "(And thus did he wake their weeping)" between "he bore" and "from the point". Ellipsis inserted.
- **Stops 30 and 34**: restored Weston's accented "turnèd" and "Accursèd".

Stop 20 is verbatim; the one apparent divergence is Gutenberg's OCR reading "'Alas I'" for "'Alas!'", which the file already has right.

### Canon corrections
- **Karidoel is not in this poem.** "Karidoel" occurs nowhere in Wolfram's *Parzival*; Arthur's chief seat throughout is **Nantes** ("slain before Nantes he lieth", III; and Weston's own note: Nantes "is always treated as Arthur's chief" residence). Greub's identification is Nantes = Nantua. Segment 1 renamed "Through Brizljan Forest to **Nantes**" and stop 9 to "Nantua (**Nantes**)". *Note for the Spanish twin: these two name strings say Karidoel in `es/` and need the same correction; no realignment is required.*
- **Stop 11's campa contradicted the canon twice.** It said "Nineteen days" at Graharz where Wolfram says "For twice seven days in honour Parzival with his host did dwell" (Weston III, l. 6006), and it made Parzival tell Gurnemanz he would never have come but for his mother — which is Greub's gloss, not the poem. Campa rewritten to the canon: fourteen days, the pledge to return for Liassé, and the prince's grief going "from threefold to fourfold".
- **Dates followed.** Arrival stays 17 May 841; fourteen days' dwelling puts the parting at **31 May** (was 4 June), and Wolfram has the ride to Pelrapeire take exactly one day ("thus from the land of Graharz he rode through the livelong day"), so Pelrapeire's first two stops move to **1 and 2 June** (were 5 and 6 June). This restores the file's original June 1-2 dating; the previous pass had moved Pelrapeire *forward* to clear a parting date that was itself the error. Chronology is ordered within every segment; nothing else moved.

### Register
Three campas ran over the 110-word ceiling (stops 10, 36, 42 at 112, 114, 114) and were trimmed to 107, 108 and 108 without losing an image. All 43 are now 60-110 words, present tense. No miracle, theophany, curse or vision was touched: the bleeding lance, the Grail's self-feeding, Sigune's incorruption, Cundrie's curse, the sword mended in the spring of Lac and the healing of Anfortas all stand exactly as the poem tells them.
