# John Maynard Keynes (1883-1946): research report

*For `john_maynard_keynes.journey.json`. Register: national mythology, the canon is true.
Calendar: gregorian. 43 stops in 7 segments. Compiled 2026-08-25.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or
standard-biography commonplace no reachable source confirmed. Contradictions are flagged,
not adjudicated; gaps are stated as gaps. The verified pool arrived already adversarially
corrected, about a dozen entries carrying `CORRECTED:` notes that overturn the first-pass
dossier; those corrections are carried into the journey and recorded below.

---

## 1. Harvey Road, Eton, King's: 1883-1905

- Born **5 June 1883 at 6 Harvey Road, Cambridge**, to John Neville Keynes and Florence Ada
  Keynes. **[A: MacTutor; King's College Cambridge]** The father lectured in logic and
  political economy, the mother later became mayor of Cambridge; household texture **[R]**.
  **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's longitude 0.1367 lands ~500 m east of Harvey Road;
  OSM gives **52.1988, 0.1294**, a street-level estimate, not a rooftop fix.
- Kindergarten, then **St Faith's**, c. 1889-1897, masters reporting him far ahead of his
  class. **[A: MacTutor]** **Conflation flagged:** the pool made "Perse kindergarten /
  St Faith's" one stop; two institutions. The journey pins **St Faith's only** at OSM's
  **52.1886, 0.1232** (the pool's 52.2016, 0.1218 was ~1.4 km north).
- **Eton, 1897**, King's Scholar, **tenth of twenty accepted**, **"first equal in
  mathematics"** **[A: MacTutor, verbatim on both]**; **Tomline Prize** 1901 **[A:
  Wikipedia]**; **Daniel Macmillan** **[A]**. **Correction carried:** the pool attached
  "after winning a scholarship in mathematics and classics" to the 1897 Eton entry; MacTutor
  assigns it to the **1902 King's** scholarship. Moved.
- **October 1902**, King's, tutored by **E. W. Hobson**; **twelfth Wrangler**, 1905.
  **[A: MacTutor; King's]** **February 1903**, elected to the **Cambridge Conversazione
  Society (the Apostles)** **[A: King's College, "Cambridge secret revealed"]**; the pool
  tags this R for framing, but the membership is college-documented, so it is treated as A.
- **[R]** Marshall pressing him toward economics, the brevity of his formal training, and
  Moore's *Principia Ethica* as the Apostles' creed: standard biography, unverified here. The
  campas state them plainly; the hedge lives in `date_confidence`, per house rule.

## 2. The India Office and the return to King's, 1906-1913

- **August 1906**: Civil Service examination, **placed second**, sent to the **India Office**
  rather than the Treasury he wanted; **his lowest paper was economics**. Enters **October
  1906**; **never served in India itself**. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**
- **5 June 1908**: resigns, bored by routine, to finish the probability dissertation.
  **[A: MacTutor]** That this is also his twenty-fifth birthday is arithmetic from the birth
  date, not a documented remark of his; the stop's confidence field says so.
- **March 1909**: **Fellow of King's** **[A: MacTutor; King's]**. The **Political Economy
  Club** from 1909 is **[R]**, folded into that stop. **1911**: editor of the **Economic
  Journal** until **1945**; **1913**: *Indian Currency and Finance*. **[A]**
- **1913-14, the Royal Commission. CONTRADICTION, resolved in favour of the correction.**
  The dossier made him the Commission's **secretary**. Wikipedia states he was **appointed
  to** it, i.e. a **member**, its youngest; the secretary was **Basil Blackett**, whom the
  journey names. **[A]** That he drafted the report and its central-bank annexe is **[R]**.

## 3. The Treasury, Bloomsbury, and Paris, 1915-1919

- **January 1915**: HM Treasury, external war finance, allied credits, exchange controls;
  **1917**: the **CB**. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**
- **46 Gordon Square. Address corrected and evidence upgraded.** The dossier gave **41**
  Gordon Square at R strength. English Heritage's blue plaque gives **46 Gordon Square**,
  "lived here 1916-1946", with the OSM memorial node at **51.5244, -0.1302**: this fixes the
  number and **upgrades the stop from [R] to [A]**, 1916 included.
- **Charleston Farmhouse** from 1916: **[R]**, Bloomsbury scholarship, no primary source
  fetched; coordinate corrected from 50.863, 0.0947 to OSM's **50.8427, 0.1154** (~2.7 km).
  **Garsington Manor** (51.7145, -1.1454, "1910s", **[R]**) was in the pool and is **dropped
  from the journey** for thinness, not suspicion. Recorded here so it is not lost.
- **1919, Paris**: Treasury representative and deputy for the Chancellor on the **Supreme
  Economic Council** **[A: Wikipedia; King's]**; **June 1919**: resigns over the reparations
  figure **[A: MacTutor]**. **Pin at [R]:** the **Hôtel Majestic, 19 avenue Kléber** housed
  the British delegation, but that **Keynes's own office** was there rather than a Treasury
  annexe could not be confirmed. Coordinate corrected to OSM's **48.8707, 2.2933**.
- **December 1919**: *The Economic Consequences of the Peace*. **[A]** Pinned at Gordon
  Square, his house and **not** a documented place of composition (standard biography puts
  much of the writing in Sussex). *A Revision of the Treaty*, 1922 **[A]**, is a ref only.

**The Clemenceau quotations: chapter citation corrected.** Three passages were cited in the
pool to "ch. 2". The wording is verbatim; the **chapter is wrong**. By offset in Gutenberg
15776, Chapter III begins at char 34011 and Chapter IV at 72025, and the passages sit at
38448, 38861 and 38980, inside **Chapter III, "The Conference"** (ch. 2 is "Europe before the
War"). The journey cites ch. III. **[A]** The "gray gloves" quotation is a truncation that
also **begins mid-sentence**, and says so. The Wilson passage "a generously intentioned
man..." is flagged in the pool as **not word-for-word re-verified**, so it is **not used**.

## 4. Probability, money, and Lydia, 1921-1929

- **1921**: *A Treatise on Probability*. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** **Frank Ramsey's**
  criticism and Keynes's concession: **[R]**, dossier-only, with **no Ramsey and no
  Wittgenstein file** to cross-check. An open thread and a gap.
- **1923**: *A Tract on Monetary Reform*. **[A]** **Source indirection flagged:** "In the
  long run we are all dead" is verified only via **Wikiquote's sourced citation** (ch. 3,
  p. 80); no full text located, Gutenberg Canada, Gutenberg.net.au and archive.org's djvu
  links all 404'd. The `quote_source` says so.
- **1924**: **First Bursar** of King's, after Second Bursar from 1919; endowment moved toward
  equities. **[A: King's]** Chairman of the **National Mutual**, 1921-1938 **[R:
  Wikipedia]**, folded into that campa. **1925**: *The Economic Consequences of Mr.
  Churchill* **[A]**; the overvaluation figure and the coal mechanism are **[R]**.
- **1925: marriage to Lydia Lopokova. [A: King's: "married Lydia Lopokova in 1925"]**
  **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED:** standard biographies give **4 August 1925**, some
  genealogical summaries **7 August**, neither checkable against a primary record here. The
  journey uses 1925-08-04 and states the disagreement; **St Pancras Register Office is [R]**,
  tradition only. **Russia and *A Short View of Russia*, 1925: [R: Wikipedia]**, month, day
  and itinerary unconfirmed, the pin a Moscow city coordinate.
- **1926**: *The End of Laissez-Faire*. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Place corrected:** the pool gave
  Cambridge; it began as the **1924 Sidney Ball Lecture at Oxford** and was published by the
  **Hogarth Press in London**. Pinned in London. **1929**: *Can Lloyd George Do It?* with
  **Hubert Henderson**, and the **Macmillan Committee**. **[A]**

## 5. The Treatise, the quarrel with Hayek, the General Theory, 1930-1937

- **1930**: *A Treatise on Money*, two volumes **[A]**; and "Economic Possibilities for our
  Grandchildren", source of the "love of money as a possession" passage, **quoted via
  Wikiquote** because the essay's full text could not be fetched (the Yale econ PDF refused
  the connection). Flagged in `quote_source`.
- **1931-32, the Hayek exchange.** Hayek's two-part *Economica* review (Aug 1931, Feb 1932);
  Keynes calling *Prices and Production* "a frightful muddle"; **Sraffa's** attack in the
  *Economic Journal* (1932), which Keynes edited; Hayek writing no review of the *General
  Theory* and calling the silence a regret. **[A: `friedrich_hayek.journey.json`]**
  **Placement note:** the interlock lens carries a canonical pin at 51.5144, -0.116 (the
  LSE/Economica milieu), where Keynes was not, so the exchange is staged at **Keynes's own
  King's pin**, the counterattack having run in his own journal; the LSE pin stays Hayek's.
  The "frightful muddle" phrase carries the Hayek file as provenance, unverified in print.
- **September 1931**: Britain leaves gold. **[A: Wikipedia]** The **21 September** day is
  conventional and not in the pool. **GAP: his whereabouts that day are not established**;
  the stop is pinned at Gordon Square and says so.
- **October 1934**: Wells's Kremlin interview printed in the *New Statesman* "with replies
  from Shaw and Keynes printed alongside it". **[A: `h_g_wells.journey.json`]** Canonical
  London pin **51.5074, -0.1278** inherited byte-identical; the issue day is conventional.
- **1 February 1936: the Cambridge Arts Theatre opens** with a Vic-Wells Ballet gala; a share
  scheme **supervised by Keynes** at a cost of £15,000, of which the town subscribed £2,300,
  **Keynes underwriting the rest**. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Correction carried:** the pool's
  "serves as its first chairman" is **unsupported**; the same article names **Norman Higgins**
  as first manager and **Dadie Rylands** as chairman from 1946.
- **February 1936**: *The General Theory*. **[A]** The **4 February** day is standard and not
  in the pool; flagged. **"Animal spirits" punctuation corrected:** the dossier had it
  backwards. A raw string match against marxists.org ch. 12 gives "...as a result of animal
  spirits — of a spontaneous urge...". The **em dash is correct**, the dossier's comma the
  deviation. (House style forbids em dashes in **authored** prose; quotation is exempt.)
  **Schumpeter**, from his own file: Harvard chair 1932, "promotes Keynesians whose
  conclusions he rejects". **[A]** One sentence in the campa, and **no Harvard stop**.
- **13-14 July 1936: the Sotheby sale. CONTRADICTION, resolved in favour of the cited
  source.** The pool's chronology said he "attended and bid". The Newton Project, which that
  chronology itself cites, says he "failed to realise the full significance of what was on
  offer until the sale had been largely completed", then "immediately set about buying up
  Newton manuscripts from both individuals and dealers", **Maggs Brothers** notably. **[A]**
  **COORDINATE CONFLICT, LEFT UNRESOLVED:** the geography lens gave 51.5136, -0.142 and
  corrected itself to OSM's **51.5119, -0.1434**, while `newton.journey.json` carries the
  canonical pin **51.5101, -0.1354**, which the byte-identical inheritance rule requires, so
  the journey uses it and states the ~700 m gap. **To prefer geographic accuracy, this file
  and `newton.journey.json` must move together.**
- **1937**: severe heart attack (coronary thrombosis), workload restricted, Lydia guarding
  the recovery. **[A: Wikipedia]** The streptococcal diagnosis is **[R]**.

**Tilton: three coordinates, none agreeing.** The pool gave Tilton twice and the two lenses
**contradicted each other**, geography at 50.8577, 0.1015 and afterlife at 50.8577, 0.0862,
for one house. OSM puts **Tilton House at 50.8405, 0.1228**, used at both Tilton stops.

## 6. The Second War, the Lords, Bretton Woods, 1939-1945

- **Autumn 1939**: the LSE evacuates to Cambridge, **Hayek at Peterhouse** next door to
  King's, the two spending the war as neighbours. **[A: `friedrich_hayek.journey.json`]** The
  **fire-watching** is flagged **in that file itself** as "a widely repeated recollection
  rather than a documented duty roster", and the hedge is carried intact. Staged at Keynes's
  own King's pin; the Peterhouse canonical pin stays on Hayek's file.
- **1940**: *How to Pay for the War*. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** The 1941 budget on
  national income accounting is **[R]**. **From 1941**: Washington missions on Lend-Lease and
  a new monetary order with **Harry Dexter White** **[A: IMF eLibrary]**; crossing dates were
  not established, and the stop's date is a placeholder that says so.
- **September 1941, the Bank of England. Correction carried.** The pool said flatly "Became a
  director... 1941". Wikipedia states he was **"proposed to fill a vacancy"** in the Court of
  Directors that September, the seat taken up the following year. **[A]** Montagu Norman
  still Governor: **[R]**. **7 July 1942**: **Baron Keynes of Tilton**, Liberal benches
  **[A: Wikipedia gives the day, MacTutor the year]**; "first economist ennobled" is **[R]**.
- **1-22 July 1944, Bretton Woods**: leads the British delegation, chairs the World Bank
  commission; **bancor** and the clearing union not adopted, White's plan adopted. **[A:
  MacTutor; Wikipedia]** The pool gave 44.2601, -71.4478 and 44.2601, -71.4762, both wide of
  the building; OSM's **44.2574, -71.4394** is used. The interlock value came from a "no
  matching atlas file" entry, so **it is not a canonical pin** and carries no inheritance
  obligation. **GAP:** no file for **White, Morgenthau, or the conference**.
- **1945**: the **Anglo-American Financial Agreement** after the abrupt end of Lend-Lease.
  **[A: Wikipedia, no precise date in the pool]** The **6 December** signing date, the rate,
  the convertibility clause and the 1947 collapse are **[R]**; the stop says so.

## 7. Savannah, Tilton, and the posthumous lecture, 1946

- **March 1946, Savannah**: inaugural meeting of the Fund and Bank governors; clashes over
  location and governance; health visibly failing. **[R]**, and honestly so: **imf.org
  returned HTTP 403** and no primary institutional source could be loaded. Vinson and the
  New York versus Washington argument are likewise **[R]**.
- **21 April 1946, Easter Sunday**: dies of a heart attack at **Tilton**, aged 62. **[A:
  MacTutor; Wikipedia; King's]** That both parents outlived him is **[R]**.
- **The ashes: RE-TAGGED FROM [A] TO [R].** The pool carried the cremation and scattering on
  the Downs, the geography lens marking it **[A]** and citing the Wikipedia article on
  Keynes. **That article's raw wikitext contains no cremation or ashes detail whatever.**
  The journey tags it R and names **no** source, saying plainly that none was found; the
  family disagreement over whether it contradicted his wish to rest at King's is likewise
  **[R]**. Coordinate corrected to OSM's **Firle Beacon, 50.8338, 0.1082** (~1.7 km).
- **1946**: "Newton, the Man", written for the tercentenary, **delivered posthumously by his
  brother Geoffrey**; the manuscripts bequeathed to **King's**. **[A: Newton Project]**
  **Unresolved:** the interlock lens says it was delivered **to the Royal Society Club**, the
  afterlife lens that it was prepared for the Royal Society's tercentenary. The journey pins
  it at King's, whose bequest is the attested part, and says the setting is unresolved. Text
  from MacTutor's reproduction, fetched directly.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Keynes is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** No apparatus stops were written. Two pool
relations are not rooted in Keynes's own life and are **flagged, not silently deleted**:

1. **FORWARD, Javier Milei (b. 1970).** `javier_milei.journey.json` records Milei as
   "General AnCap" at a Vox event in Spain in 2022, naming Keynes's school as his target: to
   "kick Keynesians and collectivists". A real one-directional relation pointing the **wrong
   way** for this file, since a 2022 artifact cannot go in a block whose clock stopped in
   April 1946. **Omitted**; it belongs on Milei's own file, where it already is. **Direction
   caught: forward. Not licensable without an author's grant.**
2. **TWO-STEP, no direct relation, Victoria Ocampo.** Offered via Virginia Woolf: Ocampo met
   Woolf in 1934, Woolf was close to Keynes. Ocampo's file **never mentions Keynes**, and the
   Woolf-Keynes link comes from the dossier, not either file. **Omitted, not proposed for
   licensing.** Woolf herself is named in the Gordon Square campa as a direct friend, which
   is different and attested.

Noted, not a relation: **Ludwig von Mises**, who lectured at Harvard in December 1940 into a
profession his own file calls "Keynesian and becoming more so by the term". A fact about the
profession, not about Keynes; his polemics postdate 1946.
