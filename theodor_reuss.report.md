# Theodor Reuss (1855-1923): research report
*2026-08-05. Albert Karl Theodor Reuss: druggist's apprentice, variety singer as Charles Theodore, Freemason, Theosophist, Socialist League librarian, paid informer, founder-head of the Ordo Templi Orientis. Slug `theodor_reuss`; census row 281.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the source itself calls unverifiable. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Holes are stated as holes.

One warning governs everything below. Reuss is a subject whose biography was largely written by Reuss: the Wagner connection, the Ludwig II introduction, the correspondence for The Times. Each comes from his own self-presentation, was repeated by later occult writers, and reached Wikipedia uncited. The English article's wikitext was read directly here; several of its best-known sentences carry no inline reference at all.

---

## 1. Augsburg and the stage (1855 to c. 1876)

- **Born in Augsburg, son of the innkeeper Franz Xaver Reuss and Eva Barbara Margaret Wagner. [A]** Both Wikipedias agree on city, year, parents.
- **The birth date is contradicted.** en.wikipedia: 28 June 1855. de.wikipedia: 28 July 1855. Neither cites a register. *Not resolved;* the journey carries the June date with the disagreement in `date_confidence`.
- **Commercial section of the Kreis-Gewerbeschule; apprenticeship as a druggist. [A, de.wikipedia]** No year given; the journey uses 1870 as a stated placeholder for the decade.
- **Operatic and variety singer as Charles Theodore; later loses the voice to illness. [A, de.wikipedia]**
- **Circuit of Vienna, Paris, Amsterdam, Quedlinburg. [R, Koenig]** Towns named without venues or dates. No playbill or notice from any of the four was found. Three were dropped rather than pinned as attested stops; Vienna carries the itinerary in one campa.
- **Introduced to King Ludwig II. [R]** The largest tagging correction to the pool, which had it [A]. de.wikipedia verbatim: *"Angebliche Bekanntschaften mit Richard Wagner und mit Ludwig II. ... können historisch nicht belegt werden."* en.wikipedia dates it 1873 and cites nothing; Koenig puts the court contact at 1883. **Two dates ten years apart for an event the German source says cannot be substantiated at all.** Kept as labelled legend.
- **Sang in the Parsifal premiere at Bayreuth, 26 July 1882. [R]** The premiere is attested (Hermann Levi conducting). Reuss's participation is *angeblich* in de.wikipedia and uncited in en.wikipedia. The single footnote in that paragraph, a Grand Lodge of British Columbia and Yukon biography, says only that Reuss "was a singer, druggest, journalist and possibly a spy for the Prussian Police". No Ludwig, no Bayreuth. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool pinned 49.9445/11.5866, about 1.8 km off; the Festspielhaus is at 49.9600/11.5797.

## 2. London, Munich, the first orders (1876 to 1882)

- **Initiated in Pilger Lodge No. 238, United Grand Lodge of England. [A]** de.wikipedia: 9 November 1876; en.wikipedia: the year only. The meeting venue is *inferred* from Freemasons' Hall, Great Queen Street, and stated nowhere. Flagged in the stop.
- **Marries Delphina Garbois of Dublin; annulled later for bigamy. [A, en.wikipedia]** Neither wedding place nor day recorded; whose prior marriage was concealed is not stated. **Gap.** The journey pins Dublin as her origin only and says so.
- **Moves to Munich, 1878. [A, en.wikipedia]**
- **Son Albert Franz Theodor Reuss born 1879 (1879-1958), self-educated zoologist. [A]** The pool gave Berlin as birthplace; en.wikipedia says the son *lived* there. **Birthplace unknown; no stop created.**
- **War correspondent for The Times, Balkans 1878 and Bosnia-Herzegovina 1882. [R]** de.wikipedia carries this among his self-reported credentials, naming no city or dateline. The pool turned it into an [A] pin on Baščaršija, Sarajevo (43.8586/18.4312). **That pin invents a location the record does not contain and was removed.** The claim survives in the Munich 1878 campa as his own account.
- **Illuminati revival attempt at Munich, 1880. [A, both Wikipedias]**
- **Expelled from Freemasonry for left-wing activity, 1881. [A, de.wikipedia only]** English sourcing records no such expulsion and has him in the Socialist League into 1886. **Contradiction between language editions, not resolved;** recorded inside the 1880 stop.

## 3. The Socialist League and the informer (1885 to 1887)

- **Joins the London Lodge of the Theosophical Society, 1885. [A, de.wikipedia]**
- **Meets Blavatsky there. [R, and impossible as dated]** de.wikipedia gives no date; 1885 was the research model's inference. Blavatsky left India in March 1885, was in Naples from April, then Würzburg, Ostend in 1886, and "arrived in London in May 1887". **Lodge membership in 1885 is possible; a London meeting that year is not.** The pool also pinned 17 Lansdowne Road, her later residence, anachronistic by two years. The journey uses the canonical pin 51.515/-0.14 and states the impossibility in `date_confidence`.
- **Executive committee, librarian and labour secretary of Morris's Socialist League. [A, en.wikipedia]**
- **Address correction.** The pool pinned Farringdon Road (51.5206/-0.1067); the 1885-86 headquarters was **24 Great Queen Street** (~51.5148/-0.1223), Farringdon Road belonging to the 1887-88 conferences. Corrected, which puts the League a few hundred yards from the hall of his initiation.
- **Expelled as a police spy in Prussian pay. [A on the expulsion; date contradicted]** en.wikipedia: 7 May 1886. de.wikipedia: 10 May 1886, wording *"wegen ehrenrühriger Handlungen"* and *"internationaler Polizeischuft"*. **Neither sentence carries a citation in its wikitext**, contrary to the pool's note that en.wikipedia cites an expulsion notice. It does not. *Not resolved.*
- **Context. [A, en.wikipedia]** The charge arose inside the Peukert/Dave feud "where such accusations were often made without substance," and the Gruppe Autonomie rebutted it in the Anarchist.
- **February 1887: uses Peukert to track Johann Neve, seized in Belgium, dead in German custody. [A, both Wikipedias]** This is what turns the accusation into something the record supports.
- **Location correction.** The pool pinned Berlin (52.517/13.3888), contradicting its own chronology, which says Belgium. No source names the town, street or day. The journey pins Brussels **explicitly as a country-level placeholder**, in both campa and confidence field.

## 4. The collecting of charters (1887 to 1902)

- **First pamphlet, "The Matrimonial Question," 1887; over thirty works in all. [A, de.wikipedia]**
- **Joins Leopold Engel in Berlin for a second Illuminati revival, 1888; separation by 1902. [A, both Wikipedias]**
- **30 August 1896: vice-president of the German offshoot of the Theosophical Society in America, under Franz Hartmann. [A, de.wikipedia, to the day]** The pool had "Theosophical Society for Europe." **Body misidentified.** de.wikipedia verbatim: *"am 30. August 1896 unter Franz Hartmann Vizepräsident des TGinA-Ablegers."* The city of appointment is **not given**; the journey pins Berlin as his residence and says the source is silent.
- **c. 1895: discussions with Carl Kellner. [R, en.wikipedia]** Vienna is Kellner's city and the conventional site; no source documents a Reuss journey there.
- **24 June 1901: Papus names him Special Inspector for the Martinist Order in Germany. [A, to the day]** No Paris address recorded.
- **26 July 1901: Westcott's charter for a Swedenborgian Rite; letter of authorization 24 February 1902. [A, to the day]** No venue documented. The journey uses the canonical Aldermanbury pin (51.515/-0.093) from the Bulwer-Lytton journey and states that the pin marks the milieu, not the transaction.
- **24 September 1902: Yarker's letters-patent, 33°, with German rights to Memphis and Misraim. [A, to the day]** Building not named; Manchester was Yarker's base.
- **1902: the Sovereign Sanctuary "Valley of Berlin." [A, Koenig]** No day. It may postdate the September patent; the journey says so.

## 5. The Ordo Templi Orientis (1904 to 1913)

- **1 July 1904: an Orient styled "Phoenix of Truth" at Hamburg. [R, Koenig only]** No address, roll, or subsequent working found.
- **1905: Kellner dies; Reuss takes the Academia Masonica. [A, en.wikipedia]** Kellner's place of death not recorded in sources consulted.
- **1906: "Lingam-Yoni," after Hargrave Jennings. [A, en.wikipedia]** No month, no place of publication.
- **1906: warrants Rudolf Steiner as Deputy Grand Master of "Mystica Aeterna," Berlin. [A, en.wikipedia]** No day. Steiner's excision of the sexual material was not re-verified from his side.
- **21 June 1906: OTO constitution promulgated. July 1906: proclaims himself O.H.O. [A, en.wikipedia, which separates the two]** The pool had merged them into one date. Corrected.
- **A constitution contradiction that is not one.** de.wikipedia genuinely distinguishes an **English** constitution of 22 January 1906 from a **German** one of 21 June 1906; it does not duplicate the 1917 Verità Mystica date, as the pool suspected.
- **A residency contradiction that is real.** en.wikipedia has him in London from January 1906; Koenig dates the settlement to the **end of 1913**. *Not resolved.*
- **24 June 1908: raises Papus to the X° of the Ecclesia Gnostica Catholica at the Paris conference. [A, to the day]** Venue inferred from the Grand Orient in the rue Cadet, not stated.
- **c. 1909: Carl Lauer of Ludwigshafen takes the higher degrees. [R, Koenig]** The pool dated it c. 1905 and pinned 49.4809/8.4463, on the Rhine rather than in Ludwigshafen (~49.4741/8.4400). Stop **cut for length**.
- **11 November 1906: A.P. Eberhardt receives authority at Leipzig. [R, Koenig]** Also cut; recorded here so it is not lost.
- **1910: confers the VII° on Crowley. [A]** No day, no venue.
- **1 June 1912: confers the IX° and appoints Crowley National Grand Master General X°. [A, to the day]** **Territory corrected:** the pool's geography lens said "Slavic-language territories"; it is **Great Britain and Ireland**, styled "Supreme and Holy King of Ireland, Iona and all the Britains within the Sanctuary of the Gnosis." 124 Victoria Street is Crowley's documented address, not a documented meeting site.
- **1913: Crowley writes Liber XV, the Gnostic Mass, at Reuss's wish. [A]**
- **1913: Grand Master of Memphis-Misraïm. [A, corrected from 1914]** en.wikipedia verbatim: "In 1913 he became Grand Master of the Rite of Memphis-Misraïm."

## 6. The war, Basel, the mountain (1914 to 1919)

- **1914: leaves England for Germany; Red Cross work in Berlin. [A, en.wikipedia]** No month.
- **1916: moves to Basel, founds an "Anational Grand Lodge." [A, corrected from 1914]** The pool's geography lens dated the move 1914; en.wikipedia has him in Germany from 1914 and at Basel only in 1916. de.wikipedia's wartime shift of headquarters to neutral Switzerland concerns the order, not the man.
- **January 1916: arrives at Monte Verità and founds the lodge "Verità Mistica." [A, Koenig]**
- **Coordinate reconciliation.** The pool's afterlife lens pinned Monte Verità at 46.1601/8.759, about 2.6 km east of the hill and at odds with its own geography lens. The journey uses **46.1585/8.7248** for the hill and the canonical **46.1544/8.7681** for Casa Anatta, inherited byte-identical from `ida_hoffmann.journey.json`.
- **Lodge names disentangled.** The pool conflated two bodies. Monte Verità's lodge is **Verità Mistica**; **Libertas et Fraternitas** is the Grand Lodge chartered at **Zürich, 24 October 1917**.
- **22 January 1917: manifesto "Verità Mystica" and a revised constitution. [A, to the day]**
- **15 to 25 August 1917: the anational congress at Monte Verità. [A]** Programme reported (nationalism, women's rights, mystical freemasonry, dance; a "cult of Mary" on brotherhood and sisterhood), not minuted.
- **1918 or 1919: leaves Monte Verità. [contradiction, flagged]** The plain en.wikipedia chronology gives a departure before November 1918 or in 1919 with no cause; the "Monte Verità" article reports expulsion by Henri Oedenkoven after midnight erotic rituals, with the charge "a charlatan and devourer of women." **Date and manner both contradicted; not resolved.**
- **1918: the Gnostic Mass in German translation. [A]**
- **18 September 1919: reconsecrated by Jean Bricaud, the "Antioch Succession." [A, to the day]** **Where this happened is recorded nowhere.** The journey pins Lyon, Bricaud's see, and says in both campa and confidence field that it is a placeholder.

## 7. The break with Crowley, and the end (1920 to 1923)

- **Spring 1920: possibly a stroke. [R]** en.wikipedia flags it "not entirely certain"; Crowley later refers to a "first stroke of paralysis" and to questionable diplomas issued afterwards.
- **17 July 1920: World Federation of Universal Freemasonry congress, Zürich. [A, to the day]**
- **1920: "Aufbauprogramm der gnostischen Neo-Christen." [A, de.wikipedia, no footnote]**
- **10 May 1921: X° charters to Charles Stansfeld Jones (US) and Heinrich Tränker (Germany). 30 July 1921: "Gauge of Amity" to H. Spencer Lewis of A.M.O.R.C. [A, both to the day]** Places of issue not recorded; the journey pins Basel as his base and says so.
- **September 1921: returns to Munich, employed by the municipal travel agency. [A, both Wikipedias and Koenig]**
- **9 November 1921: writes to Crowley repudiating Thelema's identification with OTO doctrine. [A, to the day]**
- **23 November 1921: Crowley claims the will to be O.H.O. and avails himself of the "abdication." 27 November: his diary records the self-proclamation. [A, both to the day]** Reuss's reply, if any, **is not extant** (en.wikipedia says so directly).
- **1922: an alleged resignation in Crowley's favour. [R]** Crowley, quoted by en.wikipedia from the Confessions, says Reuss "resigned the office [of O.H.O.] in 1922 in my favour"; the bracket is Wikipedia's. **No resignation document is extant, and the successor bodies have disagreed ever since about whether it was made, made under duress, or invented.** Lawrence Sutin, cited in the OTO article, doubts it for want of corroboration. *Not resolved.*
- **1925: Crowley elected O.H.O. at a contentious Conference of Grand Masters. [A, en.wikipedia, OTO article]**
- **28 October 1923: dies in Munich, aged 68. [A, every source agrees on date, age, city]**
- **Herbert Fritsche as successor "in 1923" is a fabricated date. [removed]** Fritsche (order name Basilius) lived **1911-1960**: twelve years old in 1923. de.wikipedia attaches **no date** to the sentence naming him a successor; his leadership falls in the 1950s. **Dropped from the journey rather than carried with a corrected guess.**

## 8. Afterlife, and what does not exist

- **No grave. [gap]** Neither Wikipedia nor Koenig records a cemetery, plot or stone. This is an **absence of evidence in the reachable sources**, not a claim of a lost or destroyed grave.
- **No monument and no plaque** was found in any source.
- **The rediscovery is archival.** Koenig's parareligion.ch assembles charters and organizational records from roughly 1905 to 1923. His framing of Reuss as the axis of a "paper empire" is a **characterization [R]**, not a documentary finding, and is used as such in the closing stop.
- **Oriflamme**, the order's journal: 1900, 1902, 1906, 1912, 1914, with a 1912 "Jubilee Number." [A, Koenig]
