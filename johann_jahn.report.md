# Friedrich Ludwig Jahn — Turnvater: research report

**File:** `johann_jahn.journey.json` · 8 segments · 28 stops · 1778–1852 · gregorian · 6 quotes.

## Which Jahn, and why

The requester wrote "Johann Jahn," which is ambiguous. I chose **Friedrich Ludwig "Turnvater" Jahn (1778–1852)**, the father of German gymnastics (*Turnen*), NOT Johann Jahn (1750–1816) the Austrian orientalist/Hebraist. Reason: the atlas. This fleet is building a **physical-culture / movement lineage** (Delsarte, Stebbins, Laban, Muybridge, Steiner), and Turnvater Jahn is its German root — body-nationalism, open-air apparatus, the leaping body as national education. Decisively, the corpus already contains **Fröbel**, and Fröbel's own journey places him in the **Lützow Free Corps at Dresden, Easter 1813** — the exact corps Jahn led older gymnasts into. The two journeys physically intersect in 1813. The orientalist Jahn would connect only faintly to the Max-Müller philology thread and shares no scene with anyone in the fleet. Movement-cluster fit is overwhelming.

## Judgment calls

- **Quotes kept honest.** Six quotes, all genuinely attested: the *Volkstum* definition, the F.F.F.F. motto, the *Turnkunst* Vorbericht phrase (*"die mühsam wiedergefundene und erweckte deutsche Turnkunst"* — the strongest verifiable fragment I could confirm; the DTA page kept returning empty, so I did not fabricate the fuller "verlorne Gleichmaß" sentence), Körner's *"Lützows wilde… Jagd,"* the museum's "unity and freedom of Germany," and the deer-and-hares line. The Jena "resolve" is a paraphrase the museum records, so I attributed it as such rather than as direct speech.
- **Hasenheide date:** sources split between 18 and 19 June 1811. I used **19 June** (the German gymnastics tradition's canonical "Geburtsstunde" date; the museum's "18 June" is the minority reading).
- **Coordinates:** actual sites throughout — Lanz parsonage, Berlin Hasenheide (Jahndenkmal area), the fortresses (Küstrin/Kostrzyn, Kolberg/Kołobrzeg), Freyburg (house vs. grave given slightly distinct points), Frankfurt Paulskirche.
- **Register restraint:** Jahn's nationalism is rendered *descriptively as his own mythos* (the body forged for a fatherland-to-be), narrated as event, without endorsement — same handling the brief prescribes for the esoteric figures.

## The tradition's own folds and gaps

- The **Wartburgfest book-burning** is a genuine historiographic fold: tradition credits Jahn with the burn-list; modern historians (Malettke) find no evidence and note he wasn't there. I placed the stop but narrated the deed as *laid at his door* — true to how the state read it, honest about his absence.
- **No degree, ever.** Thirteen semesters, banned from the universities in 1800, studying under false names — the "Wanderjahre" are poorly dated (mostly `traditional`/`inferred`); the anchors (Halle 1796, Greifswald/Arndt 1802, Jena 1806) are firm.
- The **grave moved** (reburied 1936 at the honor-court of his house); I used the memorial-gymnasium/grave point at Freyburg.

## The five richest episodes

1. **Jena, 14 Oct 1806** — the shame of the shattered Prussian army and the founding vow; the hinge of the whole life.
2. **Hasenheide, 19 June 1811** — the first Turnplatz; the birth-hour of the movement and the coining of *Turnen* itself.
3. **The Göhrde, 16 Sept 1813** — the Lützow charge and Eleonore Prochaska, the maiden-soldier mortally wounded; the corps of poets and gymnasts spent in blood.
4. **The Turnsperre & Kolberg, 1819–1823** — the Father of Movement held motionless in a Baltic fortress, denied even his wife's funeral; the movement roped off across Germany.
5. **Paulskirche, 1848** — the seventy-year-old prophet seated in the parliament of the Germany he dreamed, disowned by the younger radicals — the fire outrunning its keeper.

## How it connects to the atlas

- **Fröbel** — direct intersection: both in the **Lützow Free Corps, 1813**. Jahn's Breslau-muster stop is the mirror of Fröbel's Dresden-muster stop. Two journeys, one corps.
- **The movement lineage** — Jahn is the German headwater feeding **Delsarte, Stebbins, Laban, Steiner**: the body as public, trainable, expressive, national. *Turnen* is the ancestor of the physical-culture fleet.
- **Fichte** — already in the corpus; Fichte's *Reden an die deutsche Nation* (1808, Berlin) and Jahn's *Deutsches Volkstum* (1810) are twin scriptures of the same Napoleonic-era German awakening, blocks apart in the same city.
- **Napoleon-era nodes** — Jahn's war stations (Jena, Leipzig/Battle of the Nations) tie into any Napoleonic strand of the atlas, viewed from the German patriot's side of the ledger.
