# Robert Maxwell: research report

*Slug `robert_maxwell`, 2026-08-11. Jan Ludvik Hyman Binyamin Hoch, b. Slatinske Doly (Solotvyno), Czechoslovakia, 10 June 1923; d. Atlantic off the Canary Islands, 5 November 1991. Names carried in life: Hoch, Ivan du Maurier, Leslie du Maurier, Ian Robert Maxwell.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or contested allegation. Conflicts are printed and left standing; unopened sources are named with the reason. "The gather" means the first-pass research pool, before verification.

**Method note.** The evidentiary base is thin in a specific way, and the thinness should be visible first. Almost the whole chronology rests on one tertiary source, the English Wikipedia article "Robert Maxwell", and on sources that article cites. The three primary documents that matter most (the 1971 HMSO inspectors' report, the 1968 *Times* interview, the December 1991 Spanish inquest file) were **not read**. Verification corrected fourteen defects in the gather, recorded in place rather than silently absorbed.

---

## 1. The salt village and the flight west (1923-1940)

**[A, Wikipedia "Robert Maxwell"]** Born 10 June 1923 in Slatinske Doly, Carpathian Ruthenia, then Czechoslovakia, now Solotvyno, Ukraine, to a poor Yiddish-speaking Orthodox Jewish family. Most of his family were murdered at Auschwitz in 1944 after the region's occupation. May 1940: joins the Czechoslovak Army in exile at Marseille.

**[R], tag corrected.** The gather placed him in Budapest at about fifteen, sent away by his parents c. 1938-39, and tagged it **[A]**. Rejected: the cited article does not say this, so the coordinates (47.4979, 19.0402, correct for the city) anchored an unsourced detail under an attested label. The stop is kept, with `date_confidence` saying the journey is carried by later profiles and not by the cited article.

**[R], dropped.** The Czechoslovak evacuation route through Gibraltar after the fall of France is general historical account, not confirmed for Maxwell. The direction of travel sits inside the Marseille campa; the Gibraltar pin is reserved for 1991.

**[R] for place, [A] for units, error corrected.** Cholmondeley Park, Cheshire, was genuinely the Czechoslovak army camp in England in 1940 (approx 53.066, -2.686); no source reached puts Maxwell there. The gather also erred inside that hedged pin: it called the Royal Pioneer Corps "part of the reconstituted Czechoslovak forces". It was a British Army corps. Maxwell transferred out of the Czechoslovak army-in-exile into the British Pioneer Corps, then in 1943 into the North Staffordshire Regiment. **[A]** Served under the aliases "Ivan du Maurier" and later "Leslie du Maurier".

## 2. The war, the Military Cross, the new name (1943-1948)

**[A]** June 1944: the regiment crosses to France in the Normandy campaign; **[R]** which beach, and on what day, is not documented for him personally, and the stop is pinned at Gold Beach with the hedge stated. **[A]** 1945: commissioned captain, having risen from sergeant; posted to Berlin, Foreign Office press section, 1945-1947. 19 June 1946: naturalized a British subject. 30 June 1948: name changed by deed poll to Ian Robert Maxwell.

**[A] for the action, [R] for the place, coordinate rejected.** January 1945: storms a German machine-gun nest; Military Cross presented by Field Marshal Bernard Montgomery. The gather gave this an [A]-tagged coordinate of 51.6, 6.1, invented and anachronistic: it sits east of the Rhine near Wesel and Duisburg, territory 21st Army Group did not reach until March 1945, two months after the action. The journey file pins the 21st Army Group winter line between the Maas and the German border (51.0, 5.87), states that the town is undocumented, and records the rejected coordinate so the error cannot quietly return.

**[A], family record corrected.** Married Elisabeth "Betty" Meynard, a French Protestant, in 1945; nine children. The gather said "Elisabeth Maxwell earned an Oxford University degree" in 1981, conflating two achievements. Her BA in modern languages from St Hugh's College was taken in **1974**; in **1981**, aged sixty, she received a **PhD in French literature**, thesis "The Art of Letter Writing in France, 1789-1830" (Wikipedia, "Elisabeth Maxwell").

**Gap.** The place and date of the 1945 marriage are not given anywhere reached. The journey file inherits the canonical Paris pin (48.8566, 2.3522) and says so in `date_confidence`: an admitted placeholder, not a claim about a venue.

## 3. Pergamon, Headington, the family (1951-1968)

**[A, Wikipedia "Robert Maxwell" and "Pergamon Press"]** 1951: acquires three quarters of Butterworth-Springer, renames it Pergamon Press. 1959-1991: Pergamon relocates to Headington Hill Hall, an Oxford City Council mansion which Maxwell also rents as the family home for the rest of his life. 1957: daughter Karine dies of leukaemia aged three. 1961: daughter Ghislaine born at Maisons-Laffitte ("Ghislaine Maxwell"). 1968: son Michael dies of injuries sustained in a 1961 car crash.

**Gaps.** No source reached gives the place of Karine's death; Oxford is pinned as a stated default. None gives Ghislaine's month or day of birth; defaulted to 01-01.

## 4. Parliament and the inspectors (1964-1974)

**[A]** 15 October 1964: elected Labour MP for Buckingham; re-elected 1966; loses to the Conservative William Benyon in 1970; loses the seat again in both 1974 elections. 1969: bid for the News of the World rejected in favour of Rupert Murdoch, and in October ousted from Pergamon amid the Saul Steinberg and Leasco takeover attempt. 1970: establishes the Maxwell Foundation in Liechtenstein. 1974: reacquires Pergamon.

**[A], quotation and title both corrected.** The gather rendered the 1971 verdict as "not a person who can be relied **upon** to exercise proper stewardship of a publicly quoted company" and named the document an "Inspectors' Report into Pergamon Press Ltd". Both are wrong. The wording is "relied **on**", in the sentence *"We regret having to conclude that, notwithstanding Mr Maxwell's acknowledged abilities and energy, he is not in our opinion a person who can be relied on to exercise proper stewardship of a publicly quoted company."* The circulating fragment also drops "in our opinion", removing the inspectors' own hedge. The document is R. O. C. Stable and Sir Ronald Leach, *Report on the Affairs of the International Learning Systems Corporation Limited: And Interim Report on the Affairs of Pergamon Press Limited* (HMSO, 1971): a joint ILSC and Pergamon **interim** report, inspectors appointed by the Board of Trade / DTI. The HMSO original is not freely available online, so "relied on" rests on Wikipedia's citation of the report rather than on the report.

## 5. The press empire (1981-1991)

All **[A]**, Wikipedia "Robert Maxwell": 1981, acquires the British Printing Corporation, later BPCC, later Maxwell Communication Corporation. July 1984, buys Mirror Group Newspapers from Reed International for £113 million. 1984, bids for Manchester United. 1985, takes Oxford United into the top flight; 1986, it wins the League Cup; 1987, buys into Derby County. August 1985, aborted bid for Sinclair Research. February 1987, launches the London Daily News, closing it in July after roughly £25 million in losses; May 1987, hostile bid for Harcourt Brace Jovanovich defeated; 1987, buys part of IPC Media to form Fleetway. 1988, acquires Macmillan Inc. for about $2.6 billion. 1991, buys the New York Daily News and sells Pergamon Press and Maxwell Directories to Elsevier for £440 million.

**Coordinate drift corrected.** The Daily News Building at 220 East 42nd Street is at approximately 40.7497, -73.9713; the gather gave -73.9738, two blocks west near Grand Central.

**A false gap corrected.** The gather pinned the 1986 naming of the *Lady Ghislaine* at an unnamed "Dutch shipyard" at 52.4, 4.9 (Amsterdam and Haarlem, no shipyard involved) and called the yard "a gap". It is not: the vessel was built in 1986 by **Amels of Makkum, Friesland** (approx 53.053, 5.405) to a Jon Bannenberg design (Wikipedia, "Dancing Hare", its later name). The pin moved about 120 km.

**[R], attribution failure, downgraded.** The gather carried an [A]-tagged *Ma'ariv* stake sourced to Wikipedia's "Robert Maxwell". That article never mentions Ma'ariv, and the Ma'ariv article's ownership history begins with the Nimrodi family, Maxwell nowhere in it. The stake is reported elsewhere (bought 1989, sold to the Nimrodis after his death) but cannot be attested on the cited source.

**[R]** The Eastern Bloc courtship (state publishing deals, commissioned biographies, access to Gorbachev, Ceausescu and Zhivkov) is general historical record; no visit dates were established. Bucharest is carried for the Ceausescu question; Sofia was dropped to hold the stop count in house range, recorded here rather than concealed.

## 6. The Mossad allegations

Nothing here is settled, and the tagging is the point. **[R]** That Maxwell worked for Israeli intelligence, and for other services, is a widely repeated journalistic allegation (Seymour Hersh; Gordon Thomas and Martin Dillon; Ari Ben-Menashe), never officially confirmed. It stays [R] throughout.

**[A]** 21 October 1991: MPs George Galloway and Rupert Allason name Maxwell under parliamentary privilege in connection with the Vanunu affair. Hersh repeats similar claims at a press conference for *The Samson Option*; the *Washington Post* reports sources disputing his account.

**Tag conflict flagged, not resolved.** The privilege *event* of 21 October 1991 is [A]; the *substance* named under it, the tip-off, is Ben-Menashe's contested allegation and is [R]. The gather put both under one [A] pin; the journey file separates them.

**Wrong newspaper corrected.** The gather had Maxwell tipping the Israeli Embassy to Vanunu's disclosures "to the Sunday Times and Daily Mirror". They went to the **Sunday Times**; the freelance Oscar Guerrero then sold the story onward to the **Sunday Mirror**, Maxwell's own title (Wikipedia, "Mordechai Vanunu"). Nicholas Davies was separately the *Daily Mirror*'s foreign editor; that point stands.

**Context of "ludicrous, a total invention" corrected.** The gather presented this as Maxwell denying allegations about his own espionage ties. Wikipedia's sentence is: *"Maxwell called the claims 'ludicrous, a total invention' and fired Davies."* The claims concerned **Davies**, and trace to Laurance, Hooper and Sharrock, "Maxwell's body found in sea", *The Guardian*, 6 November 1991.

**[A]** The Foreign Office assessed him internally as "a thoroughly bad character and almost certainly financed by Russia", per Wikipedia on later-declassified files not located here.

**[A]** Buried on the Mount of Olives in November 1991, at a funeral attended by Prime Minister Yitzhak Shamir, President Chaim Herzog (who gave the eulogy), and at least six serving or former heads of Israeli intelligence; Samuel Pisar said the Kaddish. The hardest fact here, and it proves nothing either way.

## 7. The death, 4-5 November 1991

**[A]** Last contact with the crew of the *Lady Ghislaine* at 4:25 a.m. local time on 5 November; found missing that morning; body recovered from the Atlantic and landed at Las Palmas, naked, with a graze to the left shoulder and no other noticeable wounds.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Two coordinates for the same event: the geography lens gives 28.3, -16.5, south of Tenerife; the afterlife lens gives 28.4, -15.6, north of Gran Canaria. About 90 km apart. The journey file takes Gran Canaria, because the yacht was making for Gran Canaria and the body was landed at Las Palmas, and records the conflict in `date_confidence` rather than deleting the losing figure. No source reached settles it.

**[R]** The heated telephone call with Kevin Maxwell on the night of 4 November, over £50 million in defaulted loans and an impending Bank of England meeting, is not in the cited article; the gather carried it under an [A] pin. **[R]** The detail that he went overboard while urinating over the side is press speculation, nowhere carried as attested.

**[A], finding corrected.** December 1991: the inquest rules the cause of death a heart attack combined with accidental drowning, in a man with serious heart and lung disease; three pathologists could not agree. Murder was ruled out by the judge. **Suicide was not formally ruled out.** Wikipedia's wording: *"Murder was ruled out by the judge and, in effect, so was suicide."* The gather said both were "formally ruled out", which overstates the finding, and that difference is the whole subsequent argument.

**Wrong son corrected.** The remark that suicide was "highly unlikely" was given by the gather to **Kevin** Maxwell. It is **Ian** Maxwell's: *"I think it is highly unlikely that he would have taken his own life, it wasn't in his makeup or his mentality."*

**Terminology.** The proceeding was a Spanish judicial inquiry. The gather called it a "coroner's court", an English institution with no Spanish equivalent; rejected as an anglicism.

## 8. Afterwards (1991-2022)

All **[A]**: 1991-1992, massive unauthorized transfers from company pension funds to prop up Mirror Group shares come to light after his death, leaving pensioners roughly half their entitlements before partial restitution through banks and government. 1992, the companies file for bankruptcy protection and Kevin Maxwell is declared bankrupt with debts of about £400 million. 1995-1996, Kevin and Ian Maxwell and two other directors stand trial for conspiracy to defraud and are unanimously acquitted. November 1994, Elisabeth Maxwell publishes *A Mind of My Own*. 1996, Archer's *The Fourth Estate*; 1997, Elliot Carver in *Tomorrow Never Dies*; 2007, the BBC's *Maxwell*, David Suchet, International Emmy; 2023, *Tetris*, Roger Allam. July 2020, Ghislaine Maxwell arrested; 29 December 2021, convicted; 28 June 2022, sentenced to twenty years.

**Haines corrected.** The gather said Joe Haines "wrote a highly critical biography of Maxwell after his death" and implied "a crook and a liar" came from that book. It did not. The line was spoken **in 1984**, at a meeting of Maxwell's new Mirror Group employees, when Haines was opposing the takeover and before he became Maxwell's aide; his 1988 biography was authorised and largely sympathetic, and the hostile account came only after Maxwell died. (Wikipedia cites *The Spectator*, 22 February 1992, p. 15, subscription-gated.)

**Gap, stated as a gap.** No exhumation and no new forensic re-examination of the grave is documented. The attention since 2020 is biographical and journalistic; nothing physical or archival has been reopened.

## 9. Quotations: condition after verification

**The three 1968 Times lines.** All verbatim on Wikiquote (raw wikitext confirmed via Special:Export), but unevenly sourced: that page holds three bullets and **one** citation line, attached to the **third** ("Britain will never have a woman Prime Minister..."). Bullets one and two carry no source, so Wikiquote does not cite the *Times* interview for them; the Stella King attribution is an inference, and the paywalled *Times* page was never read. The gather also claimed confirmation of quote 1 via Wikipedia; that passage is not in Wikipedia's article at all. Quote 2 was **altered**: the source reads "...I do my best to get it—so I have trained them myself", and the gather replaced the em dash with a comma "per house style". A verbatim quotation is not restyled; the em dash is restored in the `quote` field, exempt by rule.

**The rest.** The 1971 HMSO verdict was misquoted and its report misnamed (section 4); "ludicrous, a total invention" and "a crook and a liar" are verbatim with contexts corrected (sections 6, 8). "the best council house" had its quotation marks drawn too wide: Wikipedia encloses only those four words, with "in the country" outside them, and the underlying source (headington.org.uk) renders the whole thing as reported speech; undated. "free to produce the news without interference", the Ceausescu question, the Foreign Office line and Galloway's "one of the worst criminals of the century" are all [R]: no transcript, no declassified original, no Hansard entry located. The twelfth, John Major's "a great character" (November 1991), is **dropped**: Wikipedia renders it "great character" with the article outside the quotation, and its only source is a 2020 *Daily Express* piece that could not be opened.
