# Lord Kelvin — build report

**Sources.** Core biography from Silvanus P. Thompson's authorized *Life of William Thomson, Baron Kelvin of Largs* (1910) and Crosbie Smith & M. Norton Wise's *Energy and Empire* (1989), cross-checked against MacTutor's biography and Tripos essay, the University of Glasgow's "Lord Kelvin 200" history, Westminster Abbey's commemoration page, and period press (Scientific American 1858, contemporary Atlantic-cable.com transcriptions). Primary texts quoted directly and verified against the original publication: "On an Absolute Thermometric Scale" (1848), "On the Dynamical Theory of Heat" (1851), "On the Secular Cooling of the Earth" (1862), "On Vortex Atoms" (1867, quoted via the zapatopi.net full-text transcription), the 1883 "Electrical Units of Measurement" lecture (via WIST Quotations), and the 1900 "Nineteenth-Century Clouds" address (via the arXiv history-of-physics literature on Kelvin's clouds). Cable-expedition dates cross-checked across PBS *American Experience*, the IET archives, and IEEE Spectrum. Knot-theory dates from the MIT history-of-knot-theory archive and *History of Knot Theory from Gauss to Jones* (2023).

**Judgment calls.**
- *The "nothing left to discover" line.* The brief names this as a defining Kelvin utterance, but no transcript or reliable secondary source ties the words to him — it is a documented case of a quote invented decades later and pinned retroactively onto the 1900 Royal Institution address. Rather than invent a false direct quote, the dataset instead quotes his *actual*, well-documented opening line of that lecture ("obscured by two clouds") and narrates, honestly, that the address is where physics's two real cracks — ether-drift and blackbody radiation — were first named, without putting the apocryphal sentence in his mouth.
- *Dates with day-precision invented.* Several early-life and family events (mother's death 1830, move to Glasgow 1832, the essay on the figure of the Earth 1839-40, marriage to Margaret Crum September 1852, the knighthood November 1866, the Lalla Rookh purchase autumn 1870) are firm on year but not on day in any source consulted; these carry `date_confidence: "inferred"` with a plausible placeholder day, per the schema's own convention.
- *The Chamonix waterfall experiment* (1847) is a genuinely famous anecdote in the Joule–Thomson historiography but has the flavor of a slightly embroidered story passed down through Thompson's 1910 biography; marked `traditional` rather than `attested`.
- *Two "Green's Essay" stops* (Cambridge recovery, Paris presentation) were kept as separate beats because they are causally linked but geographically and temporally distinct — the rediscovery in Cambridge and the vindication in Paris are two different kinds of event (private find, public validation).

**Time-folds / compressions.** The 1858 cable segment compresses roughly three weeks (landing August 5 to failure by early September) into a single stop dated to the failure; the age-of-the-Earth dispute with Perry, Huxley, and Darwin genuinely simmered from 1862 to past Kelvin's death and is represented by three widely spaced snapshots (1862, 1868, ~1897) rather than the dozens of skirmishing papers in between. Kelvin held over 70 patents and sat on innumerable boards and societies; only the patents and posts load-bearing for the corpus threads (compass, sounding gear, Royal Society presidency) are named.

**Five richest episodes.** (1) The 1847 Oxford meeting with Joule and the Chamonix waterfall chase that follows it months later — skepticism converting into the friendship that founds thermodynamics. (2) The mirror galvanometer built over the winter of 1857–58 specifically to out-sense Whitehouse's crude receivers, then vindicated when Whitehouse's own high-voltage stubbornness kills the 1858 cable anyway. (3) The 1867 vortex-atom paper, born from watching Tait's smoke rings bounce off each other in an Edinburgh lecture room, wrong about the ether and permanently right about launching knot theory. (4) The age-of-the-Earth ultimatum, a physicist wielding Fourier's own boyhood mathematics against Darwin and the geologists, undone only after his death by a heat source (radioactivity) nobody in the room could have known about. (5) The 1900 "two clouds" address — an old man naming, with complete precision and no idea what was coming, the exact two cracks that would produce relativity and quantum mechanics within five years.

**Connections to the atlas.** The vortex-atom/knot-theory thread (segment 6) is the corpus's own named anchor: Tait's smoke rings and his subsequent decade of hand-tabulating knots is the historical root of "quipu" as a live metaphor — knotted cordage as information, textile mathematics as physics — and the dataset cross-links to essay 209 (*Thread and Wheel*, on chirality and the Z/S twist of yarn) and essay 207 (*Hebraic Knots*), both already in the corpus's cordage-and-binding theme. The telegraph-cable segment is the corpus's technology/empire thread (the mirror galvanometer as the 19th century's most consequential weak-signal instrument) and pairs naturally with any future Cyrus Field, Isambard Kingdom Brunel, or submarine-cable-era journey. The Cambridge segment's rediscovery of George Green's forgotten essay is a small, self-contained "resurrection of a buried text" story that echoes the corpus's recurring interest in lost/recovered knowledge. No existing sibling journey was found to share a stop-level pin with this one (no Joule, Tait, or Green journey yet exists in the atlas), so this dataset stands as a fresh anchor rather than a shared-pin extension.
