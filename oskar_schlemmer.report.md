# Oskar Schlemmer (1888-1943): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-24 for `oskar_schlemmer.journey.json`.*

Painter, sculptor, choreographer, stage designer. Born Stuttgart 4 September 1888, died Baden-Baden 13 April 1943. Bauhaus form master 1921-1929 (Weimar, then Dessau), head of the Bauhausbühne 1923-1929; professor at Breslau 1929-1932; dismissed from state teaching 1933; five or six works in the 1937 *Entartete Kunst* show; last years as a commercial painter, a camouflage painter, and a lacquer technician at Wuppertal.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, **[R]** reconstruction or seen only in secondary form. **Where no source is named, an [A] claim rests on Karin von Maur's Neue Deutsche Biographie entry and de.wikipedia's *Oskar Schlemmer*, both fetched directly.** Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Blocked sources are named with the reason in Sources. Full name **Oskar Alfred Victor Schlemmer**; "Oscar" is a common catalogue variant, search both. Coordinate work is collected in one block near the end rather than repeated per event.

---

## 1. Origins and formation, 1888-1912

- Born Stuttgart 4 Sept 1888 to Carl Leopold Schlemmer and Luise Wilhelmine née Neuhaus **[A]**. Father dies 1902, mother (1849-1906) dies 1906 **[A]**.
- **Contradiction, flagged.** The dossier said he lived with his married sister Wilhelmine in Göppingen *since his father's death*. de.wikipedia dates the move to **1899** and the departure to Stuttgart to **1903**, aged fifteen **[A]**: on that chronology he was in Göppingen three years before the father died and gone three years before the mother died. Both sit in `date_confidence`; neither preferred.
- Apprentice draughtsman, **Wölfel und Kiessling** marquetry workshop, 1903-1905 **[A]**.
- A semester at the Kunstgewerbeschule, then a scholarship to the **Königliche Akademie der Bildenden Künste**, 1906, under Landenberger, von Keller, then Hölzel **[A]**.
- **Contradiction, flagged.** The meeting with **Otto Meyer** (later Meyer-Amden): the pool said **June 1911, Landenberger's class**, impossible since he was in Berlin then **[A]**. de.wikipedia says only that they met while studying at the academy, Meyer under Hölzel from 1909. Journey carries **c.1906-1909, month unknown**.
- Berlin, freelance painting, the **Der Sturm** circle around Herwarth Walden, Cubism **[A]**. **Contradiction:** de.wikipedia dates the move 1910/1911, the NDB 1911/1912. Both carried.
- Meyer relocates to **Amden, Switzerland, 1912**, invited by Baumeister and Hermann Huber **[A]**. The pool's month, and its implication that Schlemmer prompted the move, are dropped. **Not a Schlemmer stop:** Meyer travelled. The correspondence is folded into Schlemmer's own 1913 Stuttgart stop.

## 2. Hölzel's master pupil and the ballet's origin, 1913-1914

- **Correction.** Master-pupil status: the pool said 1912; de.wikipedia says **1913**, and the NDB independently dates it to **end of 1913**, when three Hölzel pupils are picked for the Werkbund murals **[A]**.
- The cohort: Schlemmer, **Baumeister, Hermann Stenner, Ida Kerkovius**, and from Oct 1913 **Johannes Itten** **[A: `itten.journey.json` stop 1913-10-01, which names Schlemmer directly; Museo Thyssen]**. Earliest documented crossing with an atlas traveler; named in campa.
- **Neuer Kunstsalon am Neckartor**, April 1913, about fifteen months **[A]**. **Contradiction:** de.wikipedia names the co-founding brother **Wilhelm**, the dossier **Carl**. Unresolved.
- Meeting with dancers **Albert Burger** and **Elsa Hötzel** **[A: Museo Thyssen; de.wikipedia]**, dated **c.1912-13, more probably 1913**, since he only returns from Berlin in 1913. **[R] and dropped:** that they came from **Jaques-Dalcroze's Hellerau institute** is in nothing I could reach; kept out of the campa, flagged below.
- **Cologne Werkbund, 1914:** Hölzel hands the main hall of van de Velde's theatre to Schlemmer, Baumeister and Stenner; **twelve murals**; building demolished 1922 with the paintings on it **[A]**.

## 3. War and the three dances, 1914-1920

- Volunteers 1914, Western Front, wounded twice **[A: en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]**. **Contradiction, open:** one account transfers him to a **cartographic unit at Colmar** **[R: dossier only]**; **de.wikipedia sends him east to Russia** and never mentions Colmar. The stop states the disagreement. No source consulted names a battle: **gap**, and the pin says so.
- **17 Dec 1916, Stuttgart:** three dances on leave, reportedly at a regimental charity evening, music by **Enrico Bossi** **[R: the day-level date reaches me only via the NDB-derived chronology; the venue is named nowhere]**. Strongest documented precursor to 1922. The same leave produces **Bild K, Komposition auf Rosa, Geteilte Figur** **[A: Bauhaus Kooperation]**.
- Nov 1918: elected students' representative at the Stuttgart academy; pushes curriculum reform and tries unsuccessfully to bring **Paul Klee** to Stuttgart **[A]**, a genuine one-directional relation to an atlas traveler, named in campa. 1919: founds the **Üecht** group with Baumeister **[A]**.
- Jan 1920: **Der Sturm** show, Berlin: *Plan mit Figuren*, *Relief JG*, *Bauplastik R* **[A]**; also noticed at **Galerie Arnold, Dresden**, c.1919-20 **[A]**, no stop of its own.
- Marries **Helena "Tut" Tutein**, Stuttgart 1920 **[A]**, October per de.wikipedia. Economics-trained, from Mannheim; outlives him to 1987 and edits the letters and diaries that are now the primary source base.

## 4. Weimar, 1921-1924

- **Corrections.** The NDB gives the 1921 appointment as form master **for wall design and sculpture together**, not sculpture "later" **[A]**; "one of the first masters" overstates it, the founding masters being appointed **1919** and he arriving **January 1921** **[A: Bauhaus Kooperation]**. **Contradiction:** de.wikipedia dates **Gropius's call** to 1920 and **active service** to 1921; the journey keeps them distinct.
- Children: **Karin** b. 1921 Weimar, **Ute-Jaïna** b. 1922 Weimar, **Tilman** b. 1925 **[A]**. **Correction:** the pool's question mark on Tilman's death is unnecessary, the NDB Genealogie gives **Tilman (1925-1944)** flat. **Doubt:** the Weimar birthplace for Tilman is unsupported and improbable, the school moving to Dessau that year; the journey names the year only.
- **June 1922: Kandinsky** joins and takes over wall painting, freeing Schlemmer for sculpture, drawing and the stage **[A: `wassily_kandinsky.journey.json` stop 1922-06-01]**. **Klee** was appointed 10 Jan 1921, the same month as Schlemmer's start of service **[A: `paul_klee.journey.json` stop 1921-01-10]**. Both named in campa.
- **Triadisches Ballett premiere: 30 Sept 1922, Kleines Haus, Württembergisches Landestheater** **[A: de.wikipedia, 'Triadisches Ballett']**. The pool's hedge on the day is dropped: the day is documented, as is Schlemmer dancing it himself **under the pseudonym "Walter Schoppe"** with Burger and Hötzel, the programme crediting all three jointly.
- **1923:** takes the stage workshop after **Lothar Schreyer's** departure, to 1929 **[A: Bauhaus Kooperation]**; paints the Weimar workshop-building wall **[A]**, painted over in **1930** on the order of Thuringian minister **Wilhelm Frick** **[A]**.
- **16 Aug 1923, Bauhaus Week: venue corrected.** The pool put this at the Bauhaus building; de.wikipedia places it at the **Deutsches Nationaltheater Weimar**, at a theatre and not at the school **[A]**.

## 5. Dessau, 1925-1929

- Relocation 1925; the Bauhausbühne becomes an independent department; **"Mensch und Kunstfigur"** appears in the fourth Bauhaus book; he paints **Römisches (Fünf Figuren im Raum)** and **Konzentrische Gruppe** **[A]**.
- **3 Jan 1926**, letter to Meyer-Amden calling **Mondrian** "eigentlich der Gott des Bauhauses" **[A: full citation to the Hüneke edition on de.wikipedia]**. Mondrian is not an atlas traveler; no interlock.
- **Museum Folkwang, Essen, 1928-1930:** nine murals for a fountain room; director **Ernst Gosebruch** engaged Kirchner for the festival hall and Schlemmer for this **[A]**. Destroyed **1934** **[A]**, resolving the pool's vaguer "later removed."
- **Darmstadt 1929:** gold medal and honour prize at *Der schöne Mensch in der neuen Kunst* **[A]**, promoted from the pool's [R].
- **Leaves the Bauhaus 11 July 1929** **[A: Bauhaus Kooperation, "Er verließ das Bauhaus am 11.7.1929"]**.

## 6. Breslau and the closing doors, 1929-1933

- Professor at the **Staatliche Akademie für Kunst und Kunstgewerbe, Breslau**; stage-art class; "Mensch und Raum" pedagogy; chairs the Silesian artists' association 1931-32 **[A]**. **Correction:** the director was **Oskar Moll**, not "Otto Moll". **Contradiction:** NDB says he accepted in **1928**, de.wikipedia dates the appointment **June 1929**. Both carried.
- **Stravinsky double bill, Breslau:** *Le Rossignol* and *Renard* **[A for two short Stravinsky operas with his designs: de.wikipedia; R for the Dec 1929 date and the titles, which reach me only through the Thyssen/NDB chronologies]**. Staging Stravinsky's works is a real one-directional relation, so `igor_stravinsky.journey.json` is named in campa.
- **Kroll Opera, Schoenberg's *Die glückliche Hand*: contradiction resolved, reasoning stated in the stop.** The pool offered 1930 or 1932. de.wikipedia gives the German premiere **7 June 1930**, Klemperer conducting, sets by Schlemmer; the Krolloper's **final performance was 3 July 1931**. 1932 is impossible at that venue, so de.wikipedia's own Schlemmer article, calling this his last staged work "in 1932," is the outlier.
- **Paris, Concours de Chorégraphie, 4 July 1932, Théâtre des Champs-Élysées**, to Pachernegg's *Deutsches Barock* **[A: de.wikipedia]**. Promoted from [R]: the venue is documented, not guessed.
- **Bauhaustreppe** painted at Breslau in 1932 on news of the Dessau closure **[A]**. Austerity then closes Breslau and he transfers to the **Vereinigte Staatsschulen**, Berlin-Charlottenburg, teaching perspective, from summer 1932 **[A: Bauhaus Kooperation]**. **Contradiction:** dismissal dated **May 1933** by the NDB, **March** by other accounts. Both carried.
- **March 1933, Stuttgart:** the retrospective at the **Württembergischer Kunstverein** is closed before opening **[A]**. In the same month **Philip Johnson** is said to have bought *Bauhaustreppe* from him **[R]**: MoMA's provenance page returned **403** on every attempt across two passes, and en.wikipedia's *Bauhaus Stairway* stub has no provenance section. The stop says so.
- **Real, cut from the journey for length only:** the 1926 **Donaueschingen** revival **[A]**; the 1930/31 **Haus Rabe, Zwenkau** interior at 51.2181/12.3343 **[A: de.wikipedia, promoted from the pool's R]**; the **Galerie Flechtheim** show, Berlin, early 1931 **[A]**.

## 7. Exclusion, survival work, death, 1933-1943

- **Dettighofen-Eichberg**, rented farmhouse near the Swiss border, subsistence farming, 1933/34-1937 **[A]**. Detail worth keeping: **Eichberg was not one of the commune's three Ortsteile**, but Bühl territory joined to the district only in **1936** **[A: de.wikipedia, Dettighofen]**.
- **Entartete Kunst, Munich, opening 19 July 1937.** **Contradiction, both counts carried:** NDB **six** works, de.wikipedia **five paintings**; both agree on roughly **fifty further confiscations**. Named in the first ground-floor room: *Römisches* and *Konzentrische Gruppe*. **Venue:** the **Archäologisches Institut in the Hofgartenarkaden**, not the Haus der Kunst, which held the rival Große Deutsche Kunstausstellung; the journey states this in `date_confidence`. The pool's "19-20 July" range is wrong: one opening day.
- **Sehringen (Badenweiler), 30 Sept 1937:** wooden house and studio bought with Tut's inheritance **[A]**.
- **1938:** the Stuttgart painting firm **Albrecht Kämmerer**; **1939-40:** camouflage painting of military barracks **[A]**. Stated flat in the journey.
- **From Nov 1940:** the **Kurt Herberts** lacquer works at Wuppertal and its Maltechnikum, with Baumeister, Georg Muche, Gerhard Marcks, Carl Grossberg **[A]**.
- **6 Dec 1941:** the **Lackballett / "Reigen in Lack"** for the firm's seventy-fifth anniversary at the **Barmer Theater**, six dancers in lacquered cardboard, glass beads and beer mats **[A]**, described in his letter to Tut of **9 Dec 1941** **[A: Briefe und Tagebücher, ed. Tut Schlemmer, Hatje 1977, pp. 176-177]**. The file's strongest primary quotation.
- **1942:** the eighteen **Fensterbilder** under blackout **[A]**; diabetes and jaundice, repeated hospitalization at Stuttgart and Freiburg **[A]**.
- **13 April 1943, Baden-Baden**, cardiac failure (**Herzlähmung**) **[A: NDB]**; family accounts add malnutrition and inadequate care against the shorthand of a plain heart attack **[R for the family attribution]**.

## 8. Afterlife

- **Grave.** Waldfriedhof Stuttgart-Degerloch, plot given exactly: **section 14 f2**, with **Tut Schlemmer (1890-1987)** in the same grave **[A: de.wikipedia]**, which answers the pool's "no specific grave section" caveat. **Gap:** the burial date is nowhere reached; the journey's date is an explicit placeholder.
- ***Bauhaustreppe***: 1932, MoMA, accession **597.1942** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, consistent with a 1942 Johnson gift; the **1933 purchase clause stays [R]**.
- **Letters and diaries:** *Briefe und Tagebücher*, ed. Tut Schlemmer, **1958** **[R: no catalogue record fetched]**; English translation, Wesleyan 1972, ISBN 0819540471 **[A: Internet Archive]**.
- **Triadic Ballet reconstructions.** No film of his own production and no complete notation survive **[R: this negative is on no page I fetched]**. The circulating colour film is a **1970 television version**, 29 min, Bavaria Atelier for Südfunk Stuttgart, **directed by Helmut Amann**, advised by Grote, Schawinsky and Tut Schlemmer **[A: whitney.org]**; **contradiction, not adjudicated:** de.wikipedia credits it to **Margarete Hasting**. **Gerhard Bohner's 1977** version was commissioned by the **Akademie der Künste** **[A: tanzfonds.de]**. **Corrections:** Bohner, not Dietrich, made the 1977 reconstruction (Dietrich is credited for 2014), and "toured about twelve years" is unsupported, tanzfonds giving "more than 80 international guest performances." The 2014 revival, 4 June at the Reithalle Munich and 27-29 June in Berlin, is folded into the same stop.
- **Estate litigation:** settlement **17 Dec 2024**, OLG Stuttgart **19 U 61/23** (from LG Stuttgart 7 O 266/09), roughly 1,600 works divided, some to museums, forced auction avoided **[A: OLG press release]**.
- **Catalogue raisonné warning, half true.** schlemmer.org **does** say the 1952, 1965 and 1979 catalogues "may be incorrect, outdated or even false" and that lost works have re-emerged "some with falsified provenance" **[A: fetched]**. It carries **no** forged-stage-design claim and no 1994 von Maur forgery documentation; that clause is **deleted**, not softened.
- **Verified but kept out of the journey:** the 2018 Google Doodle and Complex's 2013 ranking **[A: en.wikipedia]**, and the 1998 Sotheby's sale of *Idealistic Encounter* for **$1.47 million** **[A: sothebys.com]**, whose page does not support the pool's "£150,000 to £1.5 million" range.

## 10. Quotations, and on what footing

Four are used on a firm footing: **"Ich will Menschen-Typen schaffen und keine Porträts..."** (von Maur 1977, p. 9); the **9 Dec 1941 letter to Tut** on the Lackballett (*Briefe und Tagebücher*, 1977, pp. 176-177); **"eigentlich der Gott des Bauhauses"** on Mondrian (letter to Meyer-Amden, 3 Jan 1926, Hüneke edition); and **"The history of the theater is the history of the transfiguration of the human form"** **[A on the corrected wording]**. The pool dropped "the" before "human form"; the corrected line is verified by full-text search of the Wensinger translation (*The Theater of the Bauhaus*, ed. Gropius, Wesleyan 1961) and eleven independent printings, but **no page number is verified and none is stated**, and TheArtStory, which mis-renders it, is dropped as a source.

One is weaker: **"Wir brauchen Zahl, Maß und Gesetz..."** **[R]**, given as Schlemmer's on de.wikipedia with **no book, letter or page cited**; that weakness is stated in the stop itself. Two more, "My themes, the human figure in space..." and "The stage is after all architectonic...", are **dropped**: TheArtStory only, no primary citation, all routes to the primary editions blocked.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Schlemmer is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stop was written. Nothing was silently deleted. What was caught, and where it went:

1. **Forward, kept as the subject's own object.** The posthumous material (1942 MoMA gift, 1970 film, Bohner 1977, 2024 settlement) postdates his 1943 death. It sits in a closing segment because the traveller in each case is **Schlemmer's own work**, as `paul_klee.journey.json` carries the Zentrum Paul Klee and `wassily_kandinsky.journey.json` the Guggenheim *Yellow Sound*. No later person's visit is a scene there, and no other traveler's slug appears. If the operator reads the block rule more strictly than the corpus does, segment 8 is what to cut.
2. **Forward, refused.** The 1977 Berlin and 2014 Munich premieres are **Bohner's, Liška's and Scott's** travelling, so they are compressed into one stop about the ballet's survival rather than given scenes of their own. The 2000 Neue Nationalgalerie ownership incident (dossier only, unconfirmable) is out entirely.
3. **Not a Schlemmer stop, deliberately.** Otto Meyer's 1912 move to **Amden** is Meyer's travelling; the correspondence it produced sits inside Schlemmer's own Stuttgart stop.
4. **Rejected for shared geography only, recorded not deleted.** `laban.journey.json` (Stuttgart, 1920) and Kurt Jooss at the Folkwang in Essen overlap Schlemmer in city and year; the interlock lens flagged both [R] with no documented meeting, so neither is named. A source putting Schlemmer and Laban in one room in 1920 Stuttgart would make that a real interlock.
5. **The link worth a second pass.** That Burger and Hötzel came to Schlemmer **from Jaques-Dalcroze's Hellerau institute** would tie this file to `jaques_dalcroze.journey.json` at the ballet's origin. It is in no source I could reach, and is the single most valuable missing citation here.
