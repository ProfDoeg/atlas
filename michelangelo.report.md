# Michelangelo di Lodovico Buonarroti Simoni (1475-1564): research report

*2026-08-15. **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or near-contemporary anecdote no document confirms. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Gaps are stated as gaps.*

Three bodies of evidence exist here, of very different weight. **Documents**: the 1488 apprenticeship record in Lodovico's own hand, the 1498 Pietà contract, the 1501 David commission, the 1508 ceiling contract, six Julius-tomb contracts (1505-1545), Opera del Duomo and Fabbrica di San Pietro records, Bolognese payments, republic records of 1528-29, some five hundred letters. **Condivi's *Vita* (1553)**: near-contemporary, but written under Michelangelo's supervision, so a source for what Michelangelo wanted said, which is not a source for what happened. **Vasari's *Vite* (1550, 1568)**: he knew the man and organized his funeral, and is also the century's great manufacturer of workshop anecdote.

## 1. Birth and infancy, 1475-1488

- Born **6 March 1475 at Caprese**, in the Casa del Podestà, while his father **Lodovico Buonarroti Simoni** served a six-month term as podestà of the joined communes of Caprese and Chiusi della Verna **[A: Condivi; Vasari]**. Family returns to Florence within weeks. Put out to a **wet nurse at Settignano**, wife of a stonecutter working Buonarroti marble; mother **Francesca di Neri** dies when he is six (1481). **[A]**
- **Geography correction.** Caprese Michelangelo is **not in the Casentino**; it lies in the **Valtiberina**, province of Arezzo, and Chiusi della Verna is the Casentino half of the podesteria. Both passes erred. **[A]**
- **Coordinate correction.** The passes gave two *different and both wrong* positions, 43.6669/11.9302 and 43.6803/11.9269, on empty hillside 6-7 km off. The village sits near **43.633-43.642 N, 11.983 E**; the file uses **43.6417, 11.9833**.
- Schooled in grammar under **Francesco da Urbino**; learns little, copies pictures in churches, is beaten for it **[A: Condivi and Vasari agree]**. No year is reachable; the file dates it 1485, hedged in `date_confidence`.
- **Source correction.** The nurse's-milk remark is **Vasari's, spoken to Vasari**: the speaker addresses "George" and names "your keen climate of Arezzo," and Vasari was the Aretine. The geography pass credited Condivi. **[A: Symonds #11242, Ch. I]**

## 2. The Torment of Saint Anthony, c. 1487-1488

The atlas's strongest interlock runs through this panel, so the evidence is set out in full.

- **Martin Schongauer** cut *The Temptation of Saint Anthony* at Colmar, **c. 1470-1475**, among the most copied prints of its era. Condivi and Vasari both record that the boy copied a Schongauer Saint Anthony; Condivi has **Francesco Granacci** showing him the sheet. **[A]**
- **The fish-market detail is Condivi's, not Vasari's.** The geography pass credited Vasari **[A]**: the exact mirror of the nurse's-milk error above.
- **Whether the surviving Kimbell panel is that work is disputed.** Sold at Sotheby's London, July 2008, as workshop of Ghirlandaio, c. two million dollars; examined at the Metropolitan; acquired by the **Kimbell, Fort Worth, 2009**. **[A for sale, examination, acquisition; [R] for the attribution**, accepted by many and not all, as the campa says**]**
- Whether he painted it inside the Ghirlandaio shop or just before is **not determinable** (§12).

## 3. Ghirlandaio and the Medici garden, 1488-1493

- **1 April 1488**: Lodovico binds his son to **Domenico and Davide Ghirlandaio** for three years. The terms run backwards: the boy is to be *paid* twenty-four florins over the term, amended in his favour in 1489. **[A: Archivio Buonarroti, verbatim in Symonds Ch. I]** He leaves after about a year and insisted ever after that he learned nothing there **[R: Condivi's line, which flatters the subject; Vasari is warmer on the shop, and the two conflict]**.
- **c. 1489-1490**: Lorenzo asks for Ghirlandaio's two best pupils and gets Michelangelo and Granacci; the **San Marco** garden under **Bertoldo di Giovanni**, formerly Donatello's assistant. Taken into the **Palazzo Medici** to eat at the family table among **Ficino**, **Pico**, **Poliziano**, and the boys who became **Leo X** and **Clement VII**. **[A; the later papal patronage corroborates the tie]** The **faun's-head anecdote** is **[R: Condivi]**.
- **Pietro Torrigiano** breaks his nose, c. 1491. **[A as to the injury**, visible in every portrait; the account rests on Torrigiano's own boast reported by **Cellini** decades later, a hostile witness at one remove, so the episode is **[R]**.**]**
- **8 April 1492**: Lorenzo dies **[A]**. **c. 1492-93**: prior **Niccolò Bichiellini** of **Santo Spirito** gives him hospital cadavers; he carves a wooden **Crucifix** in return **[A]**. Condivi's claim that dissection ruined his digestion is **[R]**.

## 4. Flight, Bologna, Rome, 1494-1500

- **October 1494**: leaves for Venice weeks before the Medici expulsion, moving partly on omens **[A for the flight; [R] for the motive]**. **1494-95, Bologna**: fined on entry for lacking the wax thumb-seal, housed by **Gianfrancesco Aldrovandi**; carves the **angel with candlestick, Saint Petronius, Saint Proculus** for the **Arca di San Domenico** **[A: Bolognese payments]**. That he studied **Jacopo della Quercia's** San Petronio reliefs, later legible in the Sistine, is stylistic inference, nowhere documented **[R]**.
- **1496, the sleeping Cupid. Correction:** the chronology pass had Michelangelo committing the fraud. The statue went to Rome through **Lorenzo di Pierfrancesco de' Medici**; a **middleman** buried, aged and sold it and pocketed the difference, cheating both seller and sculptor. **Cardinal Riario** discovered the fraud, was impressed, and sent for the sculptor. Condivi and Vasari differ on details. **[R: the whole episode]**
- **25 June 1496**: arrives in Rome, aged 21. **4 July**: begins the **Bacchus** *for Riario*, who later rejected it; it passed afterwards to **Jacopo Galli**. **Correction** to the geography pass, which had it carved for Galli's garden from the start. **[A]**
- **August 1498**: Pietà contract with **Cardinal Jean Bilhères de Lagraulas**, 450 gold ducats; he appears elsewhere as **Jean de Villiers de la Groslaye, cardinal of Saint-Denis**, two forms of one man, flagged **[A]**. **1499**: finished. The **Solari misattribution and the signature on the sash** is **[R: Vasari via Symonds, who hedges "It is said"]**; that it is his only signed work is **[A]**.
- **Citation correction.** Three Pietà quotations were supplied as Symonds **Ch. III**; all are **Ch. II, sec. VII**. `quote_source` corrected in the journey file.

## 5. David and the republic, 1501-1505

- **16 August 1501**: commission from the Wool Guild consuls and the Opera del Duomo for a colossal David from a block abandoned by **Agostino di Duccio** (**Antonio Rossellino** is named as a second earlier hand). **13 September**: begins cutting. **[A]**
- **25 January 1504**: a committee of some thirty, **Leonardo**, **Botticelli**, **Filippino Lippi** among them, debates placement; the intended cathedral roofline is dropped. **[A: Archivio di Stato di Firenze]**
- **Two-stage installation, conflated by both passes.** Installed at the **Signoria entrance in June 1504**, replacing Donatello's *Judith*; **formally unveiled 8 September 1504**. The journey file uses 8 September, matching the canonical `king_david` pin, and carries both stages in `date_confidence`. **[A]**
- **Autumn 1504**: the **Battle of Cascina** against Leonardo's **Anghiari** opposite. Only the cartoon was finished; both works are lost and survive in copies. **[A]**

## 6. Julius II: tomb, flight, bronze, ceiling, 1505-1513

- **1505**: summoned to Rome, tomb of some forty statues ordered, eight months and more at **Carrara** choosing marble **[A: his letters]**. **17 April 1506**: turned away from the pope's presence, sells up, rides for Florence the same night **[A]**. The parting message ("if he wants me he must look for me elsewhere") is **reported speech** **[Condivi via Holroyd Ch. IV]**.
- **Late 1506 - Feb 1508, Bologna**: reconciliation after Julius took the city; the rebuke "You ought to have come to us" **[A as reported speech: Holroyd Ch. V]**. The **bronze Julius II** for San Petronio was cast twice and pulled down by Bentivoglio partisans in 1511, melted into a cannon **[R]**.
- **8 May 1508**: ceiling contract, three thousand ducats, original subject twelve apostles **[A]**. That **Bramante** engineered it expecting a sculptor to fail at fresco is **[R: Condivi, and self-serving]**.
- **Quote correction, important.** "It was an easy thing to pile brick on brick, but to make such a column was most difficult" was supplied glossed as Michelangelo *defending* Bramante's or his own architecture. **The sense is inverted.** In Holroyd Ch. VI (Condivi sec. XXXVIII) it is Michelangelo **accusing Bramante before Julius II** of throwing down the ancient columns of old St Peter's instead of lowering them whole; it belongs to the ceiling period, not the later rebuilding, and is indirect narration. Recast in the journey file.
- **1510**: work stalls about a year over payment already earned. **15 Aug 1511**: first half unveiled. **31 Oct 1512**: whole vault unveiled, public viewing All Saints' Day. **[A]**
- **Scale correction.** The geography pass gave "12,000 square feet," roughly double: the vault is **over 500 square metres**, with more than 300 figures. **[A]**

## 7. Medici Florence, 1513-1534

- **San Lorenzo facade**: commissioned by **Leo X** 1518, cancelled March 1520, never built. Two years went into opening a new quarry at **Monte Altissimo above Pietrasanta** and a hauling road, at the pope's insistence and against his judgment; his letters curse it **[A: Carteggio]**. **New Sacristy**, from 1520, for **Giuliano duke of Nemours** and **Lorenzo duke of Urbino**, with **Day, Night, Dawn, Dusk**; left unassembled in 1534 **[A]**.
- **Laurentian Library**: **commissioned 1523**, not 1524 as the chronology pass had it; vestibule stair by **Ammannati, 1559**, after **Tribolo's** failed attempt in 1550; opened 1571. **The claimed "not fully finished until 1904" is unsupported** and is dropped. **[A]**
- **1528-29**: **governor general of fortifications** of the besieged republic; bastions of earth and brushwood, protection of the **San Miniato** campanile used as a gun platform **[A: republic records]**, the wool mattresses **[R: Vasari]**. **Sept-Nov 1529**: abandons his post, flees to **Venice**, considers France under Francis I, is declared a rebel, pardoned, returns **[A]**.
- **1530, the hidden room. Correction:** the afterlife pass dated this to "the 1527 expulsion." It belongs to **1530**, after the republic fell, as the chronology pass has it: about two months in a chamber beneath the Medici Chapel, walls covered in charcoal. **[R: no contemporary document; reconstructed backwards from the room, rediscovered 1975, opened 2023, both [A]]**
- **1534**: leaves Florence permanently. **[A]**

## 8. Rome, 1532-1564

- **1532**: meets **Tommaso de' Cavalieri**, about twenty-three, when he is fifty-seven; the longest sequence of poems he addressed to one person, out of c. 300 surviving, and Cavalieri was present at his death **[A: the correspondence survives]**. **c. 1536-1547**: **Vittoria Colonna** at **San Silvestro in Capite**, drawings for her alone, sonnets both ways **[A]**; the remark about kissing her hand, not her face, is **[R]**.
- **The Last Judgment. Correction:** commissioned by **Clement VII** in 1533-34, only confirmed under **Paul III**, painted **1536-1541**, unveiled 31 October 1541. The chronology pass gave the commission to Paul III alone; the geography pass had it right **[A]**. The self-portrait on Bartholomew's flayed skin is universally accepted **[A]**, though Michelangelo never said so.
- **Draperies, sequence corrected.** Trent's decree came in its **final session, 1563**, the covering was ordered in **January 1564**, and **Daniele da Volterra** painted it "probably mostly after Michelangelo died." The chronology pass implied only the nickname was posthumous. **[A]**
- **From 1536**: **Campidoglio**, reoriented toward St Peter's, **Marcus Aurelius** at the centre, pavement laid 1940. **1546**: on **Sangallo the Younger's** death, takes over the **Palazzo Farnese** cornice and is made **chief architect of St Peter's** at 71, refusing payment **[A: his letters]**. **1550**: **Cappella Paolina**. **1561**: **Santa Maria degli Angeli**, **Porta Pia**. *The last three got no stop, for stop-count reasons.*
- **18 February 1564**: dies on **Macel de' Corvi**, aged 88, having worked on the **Rondanini Pietà** days before **[A]**. **Deathbed correction:** the geography pass named **Condivi** present and called him an assistant. He was neither, but an acquaintance from c. 1545 who after 1553 returned to Ripatransone and died there in 1574. Attested present, per **Daniele da Volterra's letter to Vasari**: **Cavalieri, Daniele, Diomede Leoni, the physicians Donati and Fidelissimi, the servant Antonio del Franzese**. **[A]**
- **Dome correction.** Not "carried through essentially unchanged": **della Porta** and **Fontana** built it **steeper**, nearer Brunelleschi's curve, with added chains. Closed **1590**. **[A]**

## 9. Afterlife

- **March 1564**: body removed from Rome against **Pius IV's** wish, at the instance of nephew **Leonardo Buonarroti**; the bale of merchandise is **[R]**, the removal, the San Lorenzo funeral organized by Vasari and the new **Accademia del Disegno**, and the **Santa Croce** tomb (Vasari, figures by **Lorenzi, Cioli, Bandini**, 1570s) are **[A]**.
- **1623**: **Buonarroti the Younger** prints the poems and **changes the gender of the pronouns** in the Cavalieri poems **[A]**; restored from manuscript in the nineteenth century, notably by **Symonds**, who had his own reasons for wanting them as written **[R]**.
- **1873**: **David** moved to the **Accademia** after cracks in the left leg (rotunda 1882, replica outdoors 1910); **1991**: **Cannata** damages a toe with a concealed hammer. **21 May 1972**: **Toth** strikes the **Pietà** fifteen times; glass since, barrier renewed 2024. **[A]**
- **1980-1994**: ceiling restoration; a brighter palette recovered, the *giornate* made countable **[A]**. Whether it removed *secco* finishing shadows is contested, and left contested.

## 11. Contradictions flagged, not resolved

1. **Vasari vs Condivi, twice, in opposite directions** (nurse's milk is Vasari's, the fish market is Condivi's). Anecdotes here migrate freely between the two and should never be cited without checking which carries it.
2. **How much he owed the Ghirlandaio shop** (§3), and **the Cupid fraud** (§4), where Condivi, Vasari and the modern summary differ on who aged the statue, who knew, and what the dealer did.
3. **The Pietà patron's name**: Bilhères de Lagraulas / de Villiers de la Groslaye, one man, two forms.
4. **Symonds chapter citations** (§4) and **the Bramante columns quotation**, which circulates with its sense inverted (§6).
5. **David: June installation vs 8 September unveiling**, two different events routinely conflated.
6. **Laurentian vestibule completion**: "1904" is unsupported, the record gives Ammannati 1559.
7. **Caprese coordinates**: two passes, two different wrong positions, neither matching the other.
8. **The Sistine cleaning controversy** remains open among specialists.

## 12. Honest gaps

- **No source places Michelangelo at La Verna**, ever, despite his father's magistracy over the commune. The connection is geographic and thematic, not biographical.
- **No month or day** survives for the start of grammar school, the Settignano fostering, entry to the Medici garden, the Torrigiano assault, the Santo Spirito arrangement, the Cavalieri meeting, or the first meeting with Colonna. All defaulted to 01-01 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
- **The Torment of Saint Anthony cannot be dated** relative to the Ghirlandaio contract: the c. 1487-88 range straddles it.
- **The 1530 hiding under the Medici Chapel has no contemporary document at all.** The room and its drawings are real; the story attached to them is reconstruction.
- **Nothing in this pass consulted an archive directly.** Every document above reaches this report through a printed edition or an encyclopaedia article; the contracts are quoted at second hand.
