# Count Orlando di Chiusi (Orlando Catani): research report

*Slug: `count_orlando_di_chiusi_orlando_catani`. Subject: the lord of the castle of Chiusi in the upper Casentino who, by Franciscan tradition, gave the mountain of La Verna to Francis of Assisi. Died between 6 May 1236 and 4 September 1237.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or later attribution · gaps are stated as gaps and not filled by inference.

**The one-line finding.** Almost everything the books say about this man is fourteenth-century narrative, and almost everything that is documentary about him says something different. Two acts in a notarial codex give him a style (*dominus*, *nobilis vir*, *spectabilis vir*), a purchase, a son, and a death window sixteen months wide. Every other familiar element (the count's title, the date 8 May 1213, the wife, the financing of the chapel, the podesteria, the quarrel with the bishop, the letters to Guittone, the counsel before Campaldino) is either later tradition, a different man of the same name, or unsupported in anything reachable.

---

## 1. Origins and the family name (10th to 12th c.)

**[A]** An imperial act of **Otto I, 967**, grants a castle and fief in the upper Casentino (Verghereto, Pieve Santo Stefano, Badia Tedalda, Sarna, Compito, Vezzano, Chitignano) to **Goffredo del fu Ildebrando di Catenaia** (it.wikipedia, *Chiusi della Verna*).

**Contradiction flagged, and resolved against the gathered brief.** The gathering pass reported the act as issued **at Ostia on 7 December 967**. Neither the place nor the day is attested in anything reachable, and Ostia is hard to reconcile with Otto I's itinerary: per en.wikipedia (*Otto II*), Otto II joined his father at Verona in October 967 and the two marched through Ravenna to Rome for the coronation of 25 December, a road that does not pass the Tiber mouth. **The journey file carries "967" with no day and no place of issue.**

**[R]** The link between this Goffredo and the later Catani/Cattani of Chiusi della Verna **cannot be demonstrated** (attributed to Pierluigi Licciardello's reconstruction; see §8 on the accessibility of that study). Treated throughout as unproven ancestry.

**[R]** The toponymic root is the **Alpe di Catenaia**, the massif south of the castle, summit Il Castello, 1414 m, at **43.6241, 11.9417** (it.wikipedia, *Alpe di Catenaia*). *Coordinate correction:* the gathering pass gave 43.72, 11.90, roughly 11 km north of the massif. The corrected pin is used.

**[R]** The surname **Catani/Cattani** derives from medieval Latin *capitanei*, the major episcopal vassals and military retainers of the bishop of Arezzo; the office hardens into a hereditary name across the twelfth and thirteenth centuries. The family sits inside the wider **Rolandi-Ildebrandi** aristocratic milieu of the Aretine countryside. **[R], attributed to Licciardello, not independently verified.** No act of Orlando's own homage to the bishop survives; the feudal tie is inferred from the family's documented class, not from a record of him in the city.

**Spelling.** The Aretine documents write *di Catenaia*; the codex of La Verna writes *de Cataniis*; the Franciscan books write *Cattani*. Not reconciled here.

## 2. Birth, parents, wife, education: the honest gaps

**Nothing is recorded.** No birth date, no birthplace, no parents, no education, no individual burial place. This is stated in the journey file as absence, not filled.

**[R] The wife.** An old genealogical tradition names **Maria di Pietramala**, of the **Tarlati**, as the mother of Orlando's sons. The Franciscan historian **Zeffirino Lazzeri**, who reported the identification in 1913, **explicitly declined to affirm it**. No marriage contract, dowry instrument, or witness list carries her name in anything reachable, and **Lazzeri's 1913 study itself could not be retrieved this pass**; the tradition is reported at second hand from the brief.

*Coordinate correction:* Pietramala of the Tarlati is at **43.5050, 11.9559** (OpenStreetMap; it.wikipedia, *Tarlati*, places the seat "tra Arezzo e Anghiari", i.e. east/north-east of Arezzo). The gathering pass gave 43.55, 11.75, which lies **west** of Arezzo, the wrong quadrant. Not to be confused with the Pietramala near Firenzuola in the Apennines (~44.1 N).

## 3. The feast, the sermon, the gift (traditional 1213; the source says 1224)

**[R] The whole scene is fourteenth-century narrative.** It is first told in the **Actus beati Francisci et sociorum eius**, attributed to **Ugolino Brunforte da Montegiorgio** (b. c.1255-60, d. c.1344-45, of Montegiorgio in the Marche), composed **c.1320s-1340s**: it.wikipedia gives "tra il 1327 e il 1337" on the *Atti* page and "tra il 1322 e il 1328" on the *Ugolino Brunforte* page, so the brief's tighter "c.1327-1340" is narrowed past what the sources support. The vernacular adaptation is the **Fioretti**, and the donation scene sits in the **Considerazioni sopra le sacre sante stimmate, Prima Considerazione**, the appendix to the Fioretti, and **not** in Fioretti ch. XVI, as the gathering pass cited it.

**Contradiction flagged, NOT resolved.** The brief asserted that the oldest surviving narrative "gives no exact date, only *in that year*." **This is false.** The Prima Considerazione opens: *san Francesco essendo in età di quarantatrè anni, nel mille dugento ventiquattro, ispirato da Dio, si mosse della Valle di Spuleto*, and then narrates the arrival at the feast and the offer of La Verna. **The earliest narrative dates the episode to 1224, Francis aged 43, the year of the stigmata.** The traditional **8 May 1213** is therefore not merely imprecise; it is irreconcilable with the text it is supposed to rest on. Both dates are carried in the journey file, in the `date_confidence` fields, and neither is adjudicated. (Source: it.wikisource, *Delle sacre sante stimmate di San Francesco*.)

**[R] The place.** The source text names the setting **Castello di Montefeltro**, not San Leo. The San Leo identification is a standard and defensible later gloss, San Leo being the seat of the Montefeltro. The occasion, *uno grande convito, e corteo per la cavalleria nuova d'uno di quelli Conti di Montefeltro*, the knighting of a Montefeltro count, is what the brief got right.

**Canonical pin inherited byte-identical** from `saint_francis.journey.json`: San Leo **43.8962, 12.343**. The afterlife lens had drifted to 43.9075, 12.3419, ~1.2 km north of the rock; not used.

**[R] The quotation.** The brief's Italian text of the offer is a composite. The Wikisource text of the Prima Considerazione reads *"Io ho in Toscana uno Monte divotissimo, il quale si chiama il Monte della Vernia, il quale è molto solitario, ed è troppo bene atto a chi volesse fare penitenza... s'egli ti piacesse, volentieri lo ti donerei a te, e a' tuoi compagni"*. It **lacks** "e salvatico" and "in luogo rimosso dalle gente". The journey file quotes the Wikisource wording, elided, and says so.

## 4. La Verna after the gift

**[A]** The **Cappella di Santa Maria degli Angeli** was begun in **1216** at Francis's request and built **1216-1218**; enlarged from c.1250 under Cardinal Rainaldo da Segni / Innocent IV, and **consecrated in 1260 with Bonaventure present** (it.wikipedia, *Santuario della Verna*). The brief's phrasing risked reading "1216-1218" as the finished building; the file distinguishes the first chapel from the enlargement.

**[R] The financing claim is unsupported.** Popular accounts have Orlando helping to pay for the chapel. **No reachable account of the chapel names a lay financier**, and later compilers do not say which Orlando is meant. Carried as tradition, flagged.

**[A]** **September 1224, the stigmata.** Francis's own event, on Francis's own file. In Orlando's file it is present once, as the consequence of the gift, at the canonical pin **43.7064, 11.9297** inherited from `saint_francis.journey.json`. The Chartula of Assisi and `essays/207_hebraic_knots.md`, which are Francis's file's apparatus for the wounds, are **not** re-cited or restaged here.

**Dropped: the Caterina Tarlati chapel.** The gathering pass had the **Cappella di Santa Maria Maddalena** built by "Countess Caterina Tarlati, a descendant of the Chiusi lords." Both it.wikipedia (*Santuario della Verna*) and the sanctuary's own history date it to the **end of the 14th century**, on the site of Francis's first cell, and **name no patron**. The attribution is also circular: the Tarlati are lords of Pietramala, a distinct Aretine house, so calling a Tarlati a descendant of the Chiusi lords presupposes exactly the Catani-Tarlati marriage that §2 flags as an unconfirmed 1913 tradition. **Not carried as a stop**, and recorded here so the operator can see it was dropped for cause.

**[A] The 1877 canvases.** Two paintings by the Florentine **Ferdinando Folchi**, 1877, in the chapel of Santa Maria degli Angeli, on the walls before the tramezzo; one shows the meeting of Francis and Orlando at San Leo. Carried as the last stop: the sanctuary's principal commemorative image of a donation nobody wrote down.

## 5. The documented man: two parchments

This is the entire secure biography.

**[A] 6 May 1236.** Verified **directly in the primary edition**, which the gathering pass could not open: **Saturnino Mencherini, *Codice diplomatico della Verna e delle SS. Stimate* (Florence, 1924)**, searchable at archive.org, item `codicediplomatic0000disf`. The regesto on **p. 685** reads:

> *1236, 6 maggio, "Instrumentum venditionis unius praedii, seu podere, existentis in territorio Castri Clusii factae a spectabili et nobili viro domino Guidone de Puppio ad favorem spectabilis ac nobilis viri Orlandi de Cataniis de Castro Clusii, cum casamentis, plateis..."*

Date, transaction (sale of a *podere*), and counterparty (**Guido de Puppio**, Guido of Poppi) all confirmed.

**Correction to the brief's Latin, flagged loudly.** The gathering pass quoted the nominative *"nobilis vir Orlandus de Catanis de castro Clusii."* That string **appears nowhere in the edition**: a full-text search for *nobilis vir* returns **one** hit, p. 181, in an unrelated letter salutation. The correct wording is the **genitive** governed by *ad favorem*: *spectabilis ac nobilis viri Orlandi de Cataniis de Castro Clusii*, with the spelling **Cataniis**. The journey file quotes the genitive.

**[A] 4 September 1237.** A document records **dominus Orlandus quondam domini Orlandi**, Orlando, son of the late lord Orlando, lending money to the **abbey of Santa Fiora**. The phrase *quondam domini Orlandi* fixes the father's death. **Orlando senior died between 6 May 1236 and 4 September 1237.** No cause, no exact date, no place of death, no burial site is recorded.

**[A] The title.** Secure medieval terminology **never calls him count**. He is *dominus* (messere), *nobilis vir*, *spectabilis vir*, *de Cataniis*, *de Castro Clusii*. *De castro Clusii* occurs only four times in the whole codex (pp. 123, 199, 685, 690), and on three of those it is a **place-of-action formula** (*acta in castro Clusii*, 1442; *habitante in castro Clusii*, 1399), not a style of the lords; the family style used elsewhere is *comes de Clusio* (p. 64) or *de Clusi* (pp. 62, 66). So the phrase is correct for Orlando but documented **once**, in 1236, and not, as the brief framed it, as general "medieval documentary Latin for the Chiusi lords."

**[R] The 1274 confirmation** of the donation's terms is regarded by modern diplomatic analysis as a **forgery**, though assembled from genuine underlying family documentation rather than invented. Attributed to Licciardello; not independently verified.

**[A] Nothing he wrote.** No book, treatise, poem, or letter is attributed to him or said by anyone to have existed. Every document naming him is a legal record of a transaction involving him.

## 6. Not his: the son, and four claims that belong to another man

Licciardello's reconstruction (as relayed in the brief) places a **same-named son, Orlando junior**, with **Alberto** and **Nicola**, in the generation after the donor, and later descendants named Cungio, Guglielmino, Bandino. Four episodes routinely folded into the donor's biography **cannot be his**:

1. **[R] The podesteria of Sarna, 1250.** Thirteen years after his death. *Coordinate correction:* Sarna is a frazione of Chiusi della Verna at **43.6839, 11.8716** (OSM); the brief's 11.8167 put it outside the comune. it.wikipedia lists Sarna among the communities in the 967 fief, so the tie is real. **The 1250 podesteria itself remains unverified.**
2. **[A for the act, R for the man] The Ubertini dispute, 1261.** Bishop **Guglielmino Ubertini** of Arezzo asserts overlordship over the wider holding, leaving the brothers Orlando, Alberto and Niccolò di Catenaia the castle of Chiusi alone. Twenty-four years after the donor's death.
3. **[R] The Guittone d'Arezzo correspondence.** The chronological exclusion is **stronger** than the brief stated and needs no Licciardello: **Guittone was born between 1230 and 1240** (Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*), so he was at most seven when the donor died. But the premise is also **shakier**: the DBI entry lists Guittone's known correspondents and dedicatees (Marzucco Iscornigiano, Guido Guidi, Loderengo Andalo, others) and includes **no Orlando of Chiusi**, noting only that "the recipients of some letters remain obscure." Stated in the file as: no such correspondence is attested at all, and Guittone's birth rules the donor out in any case.
4. **[R] The counsel before Campaldino, 11 June 1289.** Date and place confirmed (en./it.wikipedia; plain between Poppi and Pratovecchio, near the church of Certomondo, left bank of the Arno; corrected pin **43.7376, 11.7526**, the brief's 43.7181/11.7375 being ~2.4 km off). **But no account of the battle, English or Italian, mentions any Orlando di Chiusi advising the Florentines**; the named Florentine commanders and advisers are Amerigo di Narbona, Guillaume de Durfort, Corso Donati, Vieri de' Cerchi, Barone dei Mangiadori, Bindo degli Adimari, Ugolino de' Rossi. This is therefore **a popular tradition with no traceable source**, not a real episode reassigned to the son. Kept because it is a standing misattribution, and because Guglielmino Ubertini, the bishop who stripped the family in 1261, died in this battle.

**House rule applied.** These four are written as corrections, at the family's own places, with the campa's subject being the misattribution and the absent donor, never the son staged as a protagonist.

  - **Archivio di Stato di Firenze.** The gathering pass proposed a stop asserting the La Verna parchments are preserved there. **Dropped on two grounds:** it is an archivist's place, not Orlando's; and **the claim is unsupported**, since Mencherini's 1924 edition draws on the sanctuary's own archive, and no reachable source puts these parchments in Florence. *(ASFi is at 43.7690, 11.2702, not the brief's 43.7803, 11.2547.)*
