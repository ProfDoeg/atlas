# Arthur of Lotharingia — research report

**Sources.** Primary canon is Wolfram von Eschenbach's *Parzival* (c. 1200-1210), quoted throughout in Jessie L. Weston's 1894 English verse translation (public domain, Project Gutenberg #47297/#47298). The geographic and chronological spine — Bertane = Burgundy/Franche-Comté, Karidoel = Nantua, Karminal = Dorneck castle in the Brizljan forest, the Birs/Plimizoel camp, Gstadbrunnen, Bems = Beaune, Joflanze = the Rhine plain at Kembs, Landser = Logrois, Dianazdrun = Dijon, Schamilot = Champmol — comes from Werner Greub's *How the Grail Sites Were Found: Wolfram von Eschenbach as a Historian*, specifically the chapter "Bertane, the Land of King Arthur" and the summary chronology "Wolfram the Historian," both hosted at willehalminstitute.blogspot.com (the chapter-11 URL itself resolves to a JS redirect and would not render its body text for either WebFetch or the browser tool; the companion chronology post did render and supplied the verified place-name/date spine). Supplementary confirmation of Dianazdrun/Schamilot = Dijon/Champmol came from independent web search. Dorneck Castle's coordinates are from its Wikipedia/Wikidata entry. "Gstadbrunnen" itself I could not independently pin to a map source beyond the Gstadstrasse quarter of modern Dornach; I placed it there on trust of the curator's brief.

**Judgment calls.** (1) Greub's own chronology gives Gawain's messenger "eight days" travel time from Kembs to Beaune, but the curator's brief specifies a five-day march for Arthur's own column — I read this as Greub's finer geographic calculation (real road-distance) against the herald's rounder poetic figure, and used both: the poem's "eight days" appears verbatim in the messenger's quote, while the narrated march itself runs the five days the brief specifies. (2) The 843 Treaty of Verdun is not itself dated within any Parzival episode; I placed the mention of Bertane's absorption into Lothair's Middle Kingdom at the Bems/Beaune stop (843, a two-year narrative gap Greub's own chronology leaves open) rather than forcing it onto the 841-842 material that precedes the partition. (3) I added "quote_es"/"campa_es"/title_es/register_es fields beyond the sibling schema to satisfy the curator's explicit bilingual instruction without breaking json_check.py or the required key set — this is new to the fleet as far as I can see and worth a note for whoever indexes the corpus next.

**Gaps and time-folds.** The six-year silence between the Birs camp (Sept. 842) and the Beaune muster (May 848) is real in Wolfram's own text — Gawain's and Parzival's adventures fill it, Arthur's court simply waits — and I narrated it as waiting rather than inventing incident. Schamilot/Champmol is the deliberate time-fold the curator asked for: the poem sets the Round Table on ground that will not become the Chartreuse de Champmol, necropolis of the Valois Dukes of Burgundy, for another five centuries. I named the fold openly in the campa rather than concealing it.

**Five richest episodes.** Ither's death before Karidoel/Nantua (armor and identity transferred in one stroke); the blood-drops trance and the successive unhorsing of Segramors and breaking of Kay's arm and leg; Cundrie's curse at the Round Table; the Feirefiss recognition-duel (black-and-white brother, broken sword, thrown-away blade); Arthur's peacemaking at Joflanze, ending the Gramoflanz blood-feud with a marriage rather than a duel.

**Connections in the atlas.** This is the first Arthurian/Grail-cycle dataset in the fleet — no prior Parzival, Gawain, or Guinevere journey exists to cross-reference, though the gazetteer (Arlesheim/Birseck as Munsalvaesche, the Isteiner Klotz as Chastel Marveil) is exactly the ground a future Parzival journey would need, and several suggested_refs here are written to be shared pins if that journey is ever built. Schamilot/Champmol also anticipates any future Valois-Burgundy dataset (Philip the Bold, Claus Sluter) the same way joffrey_bourlemont.journey.json anticipates Joan of Arc's Bois-Chenu.

## Verification pass, 2026-07-20 (Opus)

This dataset had never been checked: the original verify agent died on spend before running. It needed substantial correction. Both files were repaired in place and re-validated.

**Method.** Weston's *Parzival* (Gutenberg #47297/#47298, the 1894 translation this dataset quotes) was downloaded whole and grepped line by line; every quote was matched against the actual verse rather than trusted. Coordinates were resolved against OpenStreetMap/Nominatim. The already-verified sibling `parzival.journey.json` was used as the cross-reference for shared episodes, since it covers the same Greub geography and the same Book VI and XIV scenes.

**Structural defect (both files).** The dataset carried `title_es`, `register_es`, `name_es`, `campa_es` and `quote_es` fields in *both* the English and the Spanish file, i.e. it was bilingual-in-one-file plus a duplicate translation, diverging from all 117 siblings. The earlier report flagged this as a deliberate innovation; it is not the fleet convention. All `*_es` keys were removed from both files. The key shape now matches `joan_of_arc.journey.json` exactly (verified programmatically). The Spanish canonical fields were kept and the redundant `campa_es` variants discarded.

**Canon errors corrected.**

1. **The Gawain/Parzival duel was reversed.** The file had Gawain magnanimously stopping the fight over an unhorsed Parzival. Book XIV's own argument reads "how Gawain was defeated," and in the text Gawain says outright "the victory is thine this hour"; Parzival cries "'Tis myself whom I here have vanquished" and throws his weapon away when Arthur's pages shout Gawain's name. Campa rewritten; the verbatim lament added as a quote.
2. **The garland.** The file said Gawain "won a garland from King Gramoflanz in single combat." Gawain broke a bough from Gramoflanz's tree while Gramoflanz stood unarmed and refused to fight one man alone ("Thou art free from strife, I fight not till thou bring with thee two or more"); Gramoflanz later rides "to wreak vengeance in strife for the broken tree." Corrected.
3. **Who named the day.** The file credited Gawain with setting the sixteenth day at Ioflanz. It is Gramoflanz who sets it, and Gramoflanz who says Arthur may come up from Bems in eight days. The quote attached to that stop is in fact *Gramoflanz's* speech; `quote_source` now says so.
4. **Ekuba.** The file called her "messenger of a queen in the Orient." She is herself the young heathen queen of Janfuse, and Feirefis's mother's cousin. She also does not break the news: Cundrie names the brother first ("Kondrie a man hath named," Ekuba's own opening words), and Ekuba vouches. Corrected in both files.
5. **Gawain's silk.** He does not hold a cloth "before Parzival's eyes"; he covers the blood-drops with it ("With cloth of fair silk and sendal, he covered the blood-drops bright"). Corrected.
6. **The Round Table came before the jousts.** The file staged the Table on 09-20, with Kay "biting his tongue" — but Kay's arm and leg are broken on 09-21, and Wolfram sets the Table up *after* the jousts, expressly to honour Parzival. The stop was re-dated to 09-21 and moved after Keye's fall.
7. **Gstadbrunnen.** Unattested and unmappable (as the original report admitted). It is not a Wolfram place-name and returns nothing from OSM or search. Rather than pin a phantom, the stop was rebuilt on what the canon actually gives at that moment, which is better material anyway: the Table cannot be carried out of Nantes, so a great round silk woven in Acraton is spread on the meadow, with no high seat and no low, ladies seated with knights. Verbatim quote added in both languages. The stop's own claim that the Table is a ring wherever the king pitches it turned out to be exactly right and is retained.
8. **The camp's extent.** "A full mile, from Dornachbrugg to the mills of Münchenstein" is self-contradictory: those points are 4.3 km apart. Münchenstein is also 4 km downstream of Greub's Grail centre at Arlesheim, outside the reach the sibling dataset uses. Narrowed to the Birs meadows below Dornachbrugg.
9. Minor: Orgeluse gives Gramoflanz the kiss of forgiveness, she does not receive a hand-kiss; Brandelidelein of Punturtois shares the peacemaking with Arthur and was missing; Arthur's embassy to Gramoflanz was refused before the reconciliation, which the "Hosts Assembled" stop now carries.

**Dates.** Two fixes. Ither's death at Karidoel was dated 0841-05-12, but the verified sibling puts Parzival's arrival at Nantua on 0841-05-16, after Bromhübel (05-14) and the Cluse (05-15) — corrected to 05-16. The "Ascension Day" framing of the messenger's arrival was dropped: "Ascension" appears nowhere in Weston, and Easter 848 (Julian) falls on 25 March, putting Ascension on 3 May, which is incompatible with the anchored 6 May arrival at Kembs. The 05-01 date is kept as the herald's arrival without the false liturgical label. Whitsunday 848 = 13 May and Whit Saturday = 12 May both check out against the sibling's Grail-crowning date, so Cundrie's second coming stands. The final segment's first stop was named "Departure for Dianazdrun" but pinned at Dijon and dated 05-14, which is physically impossible from the Rhine; it is now the arrival at Dianazdrun on 05-20, one week's march, with Schamilot on 05-21. All dates now sort monotonically across the whole journey, not merely within segments.

**Quotes.** All twelve now verified verbatim against the Gutenberg text, line-located. Ten pre-existed; the four Book VI/XIV/XV quotes that were already present and correct (Ither's death, the blood-drops, Segramors, Kay, Cundrie's curse, Bems by the Korka, the Gramoflanz reconciliation) were confirmed word-for-word. Fixes: the oath stop carried Arthur's *refusal to Segramors* ("bethink thee of the oath... Anfortas of Monsalväsch with a mighty host doth stand"), which is spoken days later at the Plimizoel — replaced with the actual oath speech ("like hounds untrainèd whose leash shall have slipped the hand..."). The Feirefis quote silently dropped "for so hath Ekuba told me," restored. **The Camelot line was cited to Book XV; it is Book XVI** (p. 183, l. 580), and Weston's own note marks it as the poem's only mention of Camelot. Two quotes added, to the Round Table stop and the Gawain/Parzival duel.

**House style.** No em-dashes in either file, before or after; a programmatic sweep over every string (all Unicode dash-punctuation, hyphen excepted) is clean. No em-dash was found inside a verbatim quote, so no exception had to be invoked.

**Spanish sibling.** Rebuilt to the same shape: indent=1, ensure_ascii=False (no `\u` escapes present), same 7 segments and 24 stops, quote parity 12/12, lat/lng/date/date_confidence identical to English (checked field by field), `quote_source` translated and normalised to the fleet's capitalised "Libro" form. Four campas came out at 121-123 words and were trimmed; the Spanish now sits at 106-120 words, inside the band the other 117 siblings occupy. Gawain is "Galván" throughout, matching `es/parzival.journey.json`.

**Still uncertain.** (a) Greub's identifications are Greub's, not Wolfram's, and remain the load-bearing assumption of the whole dataset; the campas now say "Greub sets" rather than "Wolfram sets" where the siting is Greub's rather than the poem's. (b) The Landser=Logrois equation is weak on terrain, which the suggested_ref already concedes. (c) The five-day march from Beaune is the curator's figure against the poem's eight days; the tension is now named inside the campa instead of being quietly resolved. (d) Whether the Plimizoel camp belongs at Dornachbrugg or further upstream at Arlesheim is not settled between this dataset and the sibling; both now sit within ~700 m of each other, which is as close as the sources warrant. (e) The 843 Verdun mention was reworded to "this year" since the stop is dated 843, not two years after.

## Second verification pass, 2026-07-20 (independent re-check)

A prior pass is recorded above; this one re-checked its claims from scratch rather than trusting them. Weston's *Parzival* (Gutenberg #47297/#47298) was downloaded again and every quote located by line number in the actual verse. Every coordinate was resolved or reverse-resolved against OpenStreetMap/Nominatim. **No stop was added, removed or reordered: 7 segments, 24 stops, before and after.**

**Structure.** Key shape is identical to `joan_of_arc.journey.json` at every level (top, segment, stop) — verified programmatically. Dates sort monotonically across the whole journey, not merely within segments. `date_confidence` values are drawn only from attested/traditional/inferred. Campa word counts now run 95-110, all present tense. No em-dash or other Unicode dash-punctuation anywhere in the file.

**Quotes: 12 of 12 verified verbatim, line-located.** Not a spot check, the whole set. Nothing was nulled and nothing was invented. Located at: Ither's death pz1 ll. 5301, 5344; Arthur's oath pz1 l. 9329; the blood-drops pz1 ll. 9399, 9409; Segramors pz1 ll. 9547, 9560; Kay's halter pz1 ll. 9796, 9813; the Acraton silk pz1 ll. 10331-10341; Cundrie's curse pz1 ll. 10529 ff.; Gramoflanz naming the day pz2 ll. 3563-3573; the Gawain/Parzival lament pz2 ll. 6102, 6126; the reconciliation pz2 ll. 7382, 7424; Feirefis pz2 ll. 8051-8053; Camelot pz2 l. 10468. The prior pass's two most load-bearing claims both hold: Book XIV's text does read "the victory is thine this hour" from Gawain's mouth, and Weston's own endnote to p. 183, l. 580 confirms the Camelot line as the poem's only mention.

**Canon errors corrected.**

1. **The winged dragon was on the wrong tent.** "Down to the Birs" gave Guinevere a tent with "a winged dragon worked in gold above it". That tent is Cunneware of Lalande's, pitched near the royal pavilion, and the dragon is her brother Orilus's device, which is exactly how Orilus recognises it (pz1 ll. 9211-9216, "above it a wingèd dragon hung... And Orilus thereby knew it, for the self-same arms he bare"). Attaching Orilus's arms to the queen inverts a recognition scene. Replaced with the detail the poem does give at the pitching: a thousand tents and more, on either side of the water (pz1 ll. 9044-9051).
2. **An invented battle.** The stop "The Skirmish Past Landser" narrated garrison outriders falling on Arthur's foragers, with `date_confidence: traditional`. No such engagement exists anywhere in Wolfram; the march from Bems to Ioflanz is not narrated at all. An invented episode carrying a confidence label that says the tradition attests it is the one thing this register cannot afford. The stop was rebuilt on what is actually true of the place: Logrois is Orgeluse's castle, and Orgeluse's slain love is the root of the very feud Arthur is riding to settle. Renamed "Past Logrois in the Sundgau" and re-labelled `inferred`. The coordinate is unchanged, so the stop keeps its position and its pin.

**Trimmed.** Four campas ran 111-115 words; all now sit inside the 60-110 band.

**Could not confirm.**

- **Greub himself.** The willehalminstitute.blogspot.com chapter pages were not re-fetched in this pass, so the Greub identifications (Karidoel = Nantua, Karminal = Dorneck, Bems = Beaune, Joflanze = Kembs, Logrois = Landser, Dianazdrun = Dijon, Schamilot = Champmol) rest on the earlier pass's reading plus their agreement with the verified sibling `parzival.journey.json`. They remain the load-bearing assumption of the dataset and are Greub's, not Wolfram's; the campas already say so.
- **Stollenrain.** The blood-drops pin at 47.486/7.614 reverse-resolves to Dornachbrugg, about 750 m south of the streets actually named Stollenrain in Arlesheim (47.492-47.493, 7.6135-7.6142). Left in place: it keeps the stop inside its own camp cluster and matches the sibling dataset, and Greub's Stollenrain is a slope rather than a street centroid. Flagged rather than moved.
- **Moenchsberg.** The Gawain/Parzival duel at 47.729/7.331 is about 700 m from the OSM node for Moenchsberg, Brunstatt (47.7241/7.3368), on the same wooded ridge. Left in place for the same reason.
- **Ekuba's parchment.** Weston's own endnote to p. 142, l. 230 says Ekuba did *not* say "as written parchment" in Wolfram's German, and guesses the simile came from the French source. The quote is verbatim in Weston, which is this dataset's stated canon, so it stands; but the campa's "Parzival answers on Ekuba's word" is true of the translation rather than of Wolfram.
