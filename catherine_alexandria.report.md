# Catherine of Alexandria — Famagusta to Sinai, and the Long Echo into France: research report

**Dataset:** `catherine_alexandria.journey.json` — 9 segments, 33 stops, 0290 to 1431 (Julian, traditional throughout), 8 stops carrying canon words (Golden Legend passages and Joan of Arc's own attested trial testimony).

## Sources
Primary canon per the curator's brief: essays/213_las_tres_virgenes_martires.md (the full hagiography — Famagusta birth, Costus, queen at 14, the move to Alexandria for husband and libraries, the hermit, the desert baptism, Maxentius, the fifty scholars, Empress Valeria, the wheel, the milk); essays/216_la_espada.md and essays/instagram/2022-11-13-espadadejoanadarc... (the sword of Fierbois, Charles Martel's cache, Hypatia as the historical shadow behind the legend); essays/instagram/2022-12-29-santa-catalina-era-hija-del-gobernador.md and 2023-01-03-los-14-santos-ayudantes-se-originaron.md (the Fourteen Helpers, the Rhineland origin, the fairy-field at Domrémy); essays/212_domremy_por_katrin_vates.md (the embroidery, the Barbara interpolation). Deep background: Jacobus de Voragine's *Golden Legend* (Caxton's 1483 English translation, via christianiconography.info) for the mystical-marriage vision, the debate speech, the wheel prayer, and the beheading prayer — all quoted verbatim, public domain. Joan of Arc's own trial testimony (Procès de condamnation, 22–27 Feb 1431) supplies the quotes at every shared French pin, since those are literally the record of Catherine's voice being heard. Coordinates for the Anna-Kapelle were resolved by web search to St. Anna-Kapelle, Obernburg am Main (Kapellengasse), whose Nothelferaltar depicts Barbara, Margareta, and Katharina together — matching the essay's "50 km outside Frankfurt" description exactly.

## Judgment calls
- **Internal chronology fixed to Maxentius's reign (306–312 AD)**, per the essay's own instagram gloss, rather than the more common "305" martyrdom date — so birth (290) + confrontation "at eighteen" lands the debate/wheel/beheading at 308, keeping the essay's own numbers internally consistent.
- **The desert hermitage pinned near Lake Mareotis**, south of Alexandria — the canon names no exact site, so I chose the historically attested desert margin closest to the city rather than inventing a toponym or reaching for the (better-known but textually unconnected) Wadi El Natrun.
- **Sinai pin is byte-identical with `moses.journey.json`'s "Sinai — the theophany" stop (28.539, 33.975)** — confirmed that file has a clean, well-sourced Jebel Musa pin, so no fallback to the monastery-only pin was needed. A second, distinct pin (28.5563, 33.9750, the monastery itself) is used for the founding (555) and the relic's rediscovery (~800), since those postdate the body's arrival on the bare summit by centuries.
- **Fierbois, Domrémy (father's garden and Ladies' Tree), and both Rouen stops are byte-identical pins with `joan_of_arc.journey.json`**, confirmed by direct coordinate comparison against that file. Where the event is literally the same historical moment (the sword-finding, the trial naming, the stake), I kept Joan's exact date too, not just her coordinates.
- **The Anna-Kapelle coda is dated 1350 (traditional)** — the essay's own account of the Fourteen Helpers' Rhineland origin in the 14th-century Black Death/famine — rather than a modern date, since Catherine is not a living traveler and the corpus reserves present-day dating for that case.
- **`margarita_pelagia.journey.json` and `saint_barbara.journey.json` do not yet exist** in this working directory as of this run. The Anna-Kapelle pin (49.842, 9.148) and the Famagusta-triad framing at the birth stop are written to be shared/coordinated once those two files land; `sources` on both stops flags this explicitly so a later pass can byte-match.

## Gaps and time-folds
The Sinai segment openly folds three and a half centuries into three stops (angels carry the body in 308; Justinian's monastery rises in 555; the undecayed body is found in 800) — flagged in-file by date, not smoothed over. The Fierbois segment folds seven centuries between Charles Martel's chapel (732) and the sword's recovery for Joan (1429). Both folds are legitimate within the linter's rule (chronological order is checked only *within* each segment, never across segments), and both are the curator's own designed welds: Catherine's relic-body completing Moses's mountain, and her sword completing the Maid's arming.

## Five richest episodes
1. **The mystical marriage** (desert oratory, 305) — the Child turning his face away for want of baptism, then the ring set on her finger before the whole celestial court; the two-vision structure straight from the Golden Legend.
2. **The debate with the fifty scholars** (Alexandria, 308) — an eighteen-year-old queen dismantling Rome's best rhetoricians with the trivium she began at age eight, then losing them all to Maxentius's fire as martyrs of her own making.
3. **The dungeon** — the dove that feeds her twelve days, then Empress Valeria and the captain Porphyrius kneeling in a cell lit by no torch, converting two hundred soldiers before Maxentius even comes home.
4. **The wheel and the milk** — the machine built to answer argument with iron, shattered instead by a single prayer; the healing milk at her death set explicitly beside Saint Christopher, Vishnu, and Hayagriva, per the essay's own comparative-mythology move.
5. **The angels bearing her to Sinai** — Moses's mountain and Catherine's body meeting across six centuries of empty rock, then Justinian's monks unknowingly building a house to receive a relic they don't yet know is coming.

## Connections to the atlas
This journey is designed as two welds rather than a single life-line: it completes `moses.journey.json`'s Sinai pin (previously the atlas's isolated "pin-island") with a second holy body on the same summit, and it threads through `joan_of_arc.journey.json` at five separate byte-identical pins — the father's garden, the Ladies' Tree (where the essay's own honesty about Barbara's interpolation is preserved in the campa text), Fierbois twice (chapel-founding and sword-finding), and both Rouen stops (the trial naming and the stake). It is written to sit beside `margarita_pelagia` and `saint_barbara` as the third leg of the triad essay 213 describes — the birth stop and the Anna-Kapelle coda both name and wait for those two files by coordinate and by the shared plaque rhyme.
