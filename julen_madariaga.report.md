# Julen Madariaga: research report

*Julen Kerman Madariaga Agirre, 1932-2021. Co-founder of Euskadi Ta Askatasuna and first head of its military branch; lawyer; French citizen from 1986; from 1993 the most senior founder saying in public that the armed campaign had failed. Compiled 2026-08-31 for `julen_madariaga.journey.json`, 43 stops in 7 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, single-source testimony, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. Wikipedia is cited by language code.

**Standing caution.** Most detail on this subject descends from Madariaga himself: the 2012 Ahotsak oral history, the Berria interview of 15 June 2014, the memoir *Egiari zor*, and Batista's 2007 biography, built on access to him. He was consistent across sixty years and frequently the only witness. Anything tracing only to him is **[R]** on that ground.

## 1. Birth and family, 1932

- Born **11 October 1932**, Abando, Bilbao [A: en/es/eu.wikipedia; the obituaries of 6 April 2021 give the district], no street address located. Father a PNV militant [A]. Married twice; children **eleven** per es.wikipedia, **ten** per eu.wikipedia, a **contradiction** the journey states rather than settles.
- Grandfather Ramón Genaro de Madariaga, and the tradition that the 1931 draft autonomy statute was worked out on his table at Zamaldegia [R: family tradition, in the dossier's own framing].
- Uncle Juan de Madariaga married María Teresa Aguirre Lekube, sister of **José Antonio Aguirre** [R: dossier only; the interlock lens warned "Confirm the marriage independently before writing", and it was not confirmed]. This is the link to `jose_antonio_aguirre.journey.json` and the weakest-sourced load-bearing claim in the file. See §9.
- Claim that Ramón Genaro was floated as an alternative candidate at Aguirre's 1936 election under the Gernika oak [R: dossier only]. **Not used**: Madariaga was four and not present.

## 2. Chilean exile, 1937-1946

- Exiled to Chile at about five [A: en.wikipedia]. Sheltering under the 1936-37 bombing of Bilbao [R: Berria 2014; the raids are documented, the memory is his]. Embarkation at La Rochelle [R].
- Arrival on his fifth birthday, 11 October 1937 [R: uncorroborated]. **No source names the port of disembarkation**, Valparaíso being inference from its status as principal immigrant port, and **no source names the Chilean city** of the Marist schooling and the corporal punishment he remembered [R: Ahotsak, no primary transcript consulted]; Santiago is inference.
- Return via flight to Buenos Aires, then ship to Bilbao [R: dossier-only; the lens asked for confirmation, none obtained]. Grey postwar Bilbao against "technicolor" Chile [A for the words as reported: Berria 2014, consonant with the 2009 El Colombiano interview, fetched directly].

**Contradiction: the year of return.** en/eu.wikipedia say **1942**; es.wikipedia says **1946**, with his brother Jon, for secondary schooling; the dossier prefers 1946 on his own testimony. **Not resolved**; the journey follows 1946 and says so in both affected confidence fields. A **pool inconsistency is corrected**: its chronology tagged the return [R], its geography tagged the Buenos Aires stop [A], on the same contested date. Both are [R].

## 3. Formation and Ekin, 1948-1958

- Meeting **Txillardegi** at about sixteen in Donostia through an older cousin's circle [R: no address or firm year]. Law at Deusto, degree completed at Oviedo [A: es.wikipedia], no years of enrolment established; the Father Urrutia commercial-law class as the room where the founders met is [R], the historiography corroborating the personnel, not the room.
- **Sireika**, travel agency, partly financing Ekin [A: es.wikipedia]. Ekin at the World Basque Congress, Paris, 1956 [R: unconfirmed].

**Correction: Ekin's date and its relation to EGI.** The pool, following en.wikipedia, gave 1953 and called Ekin a splinter of EGI. Both wrong. es.wikipedia's ETA article: *"Ekin, grupo de estudio fundado en 1952 en Bilbao"*, and the relationship is inverted, since Ekin **predated** contact with EGI, **merged** with it in **1956**, and the direct-action faction **split away in May 1958** [A]. Its premises are documented nowhere; the coordinate is central Bilbao.

**Correction: Deba, December 1958.** The meeting is [A]: es.wikipedia's ETA article confirms it and names Benito del Valle, Madariaga, Larramendi and Txillardegi. But the pool inverted its content: that source makes Deba where the **acronym** was chosen, *Aberri Ta Askatasuna* rejected because ATA means "duck" in Gipuzkoan Basque. The pool had it commissioning Txillardegi to find a name and dated the organisational split to it; the split is **May 1958**.

**Open dispute, left open: when does ETA begin?** Ekin's 1952 founding (Madariaga's own continuity thesis); the December 1958 Deba decision, which the dossier calls the strongest scholarly candidate, citing Santiago de Pablo Contreras (2019); or the 31 July 1959 letter. [R; no adjudicating source fetched.] The journey takes no side.

## 4. Founding, first actions, prison, 1959-1962

- Co-founders: with José María Benito del Valle, Rafael Albisu, José (Manuel) Agirre and Txillardegi, among others [A: en/es.wikipedia]. Public founding dated to the **31 July 1959 letter to Jesús María Leizaola**, first public appearance of the initials [A: en/eu.wikipedia]. **New contradiction, not in the pool:** those sources call Leizaola *lehendakari* at that date, but Aguirre held the office until his death in March 1960. Either the office or the date is loosely stated. Not resolved.
- **October-November 1959 bombings**: the newspaper *Alerta* in Santander, the Civil Government in Vitoria, police headquarters in Bilbao [A: es.wikipedia's ETA article]. This **upgrades** the pool, which rested them on Madariaga's 1964 manuscript and a December 1959 US consular report, dated only to "late 1959". **His presence at any of the buildings is unestablished [R].** The Bilbao action gets **no stop**, its pool coordinate sitting at no police building; it is a `suggested_ref` on the Vitoria stop, attested and unlocated.
- Torture under **Melitón Manzanas**, 1960-61. The fact is [A, upgraded]: Berria's 2021 obituary reports it independently of Madariaga. The specifics, four days and three broken ribs, are [R], his 2012 Ahotsak testimony only; no medical certificate survives, no building is named. **Pool inconsistency resolved downward**: its geography tagged this [A], its chronology [R]; the journey uses [R]. Carabanchel followed, about six months, with Larramendi, Laspiur and Albisu [R].

**Correction and open question: Cambridge.** That he studied there is [A]. The **doctorate is overstated in the pool's source line**: en/eu.wikipedia say doctorate, es.wikipedia says only *"un postgrado en Reino Unido, en Cambridge"* and is dropped from that citation. **No thesis title, college, field or conferral date exists in any source.** The 1962 date comes from the Ahotsak biography page alone and strains against the 1960-61 arrest cycle and the May 1962 assembly alike. **Not resolved.**

**Correction: the First Assembly.** May 1962, monastery of Nuestra Señora de Belloc, **Urt** [A: es.wikipedia's ETA article gives venue and month; en.wikipedia's loose "in Bayonne" is what the pool followed]. Madariaga takes the military branch [A/R].

## 5. Itsasu and the search for allies, 1963-1966

- **Itsasuko Agiria, Aberri Eguna, 15 April 1963** [A: eu.wikipedia, verbatim, "Ximun Haranek eta Julen Madariagak, Ipar eta Hego Euskal Herriaren izenean, hurrenez hurren": each planted a shoot of the Gernika oak, Haran for the North, Madariaga for the South]. The date upgrades the pool's bare "1963". The sapling was later destroyed and a stone raised outside the church of San Fruktuoso [A: same source]; it commemorates a collective declaration and two planters, not Madariaga alone.
- Paris, autumn 1963, approach to the Begin, Herut and Irgun milieu with Juan Luis Irusta and an engineer named Jaime; Begin declines [R: later reconstruction via Alberto de Onaindia and Elie Meisi; no contemporaneous record]. His stated admiration for Irgun methods is [R], dossier-only. Antwerp and Brussels, 1965, with Eneko Irigarai after expulsion from France, and contact with **Ernest Mandel** and Fourth International militants that cooled when Mandel began advising ETA on Spanish strategy [R: Irigarai via El Salto Diario]. No venue or dates for any of it.
- Algiers, early 1965 to March 1966: a post found by Bachir Boumaza, the clandestine name "Ahmed", witness to Che Guevara's speech, the June 1965 coup, and a March 1966 request for weapons, money, training and a radio station, refused. [A for the period and the name; R for his presence in the hall and for the request.] **Correction**: the pool called Boumaza "communications minister" in early 1965; per fr.wikipedia he took Information only in 1965-66, after the coup. Boumédiène took power 19 June 1965 [A]; Che's address to the Afro-Asian Solidarity seminar was 24 February 1965 [A: carried at the same pin in `che_guevara.journey.json`].

## 6. Burgos, Aturri, the French prosecution, 1970-1991

- Burgos process, December 1970: indicted, tried in absentia, declared in rebellion [A: en/es.wikipedia]. Prominent in Herri Batasuna [A], no joining date given.
- Aturri S.A., Biarritz, 1973, eighteen to twenty employees at its height [R: his own account]. Bayonne cathedral hunger strike, 1974, with Marc Legasse interpreting [R, demoted from the pool's [A]: uncorroborated Enbata testimony]. Naturalized French, 1986 [A: es.wikipedia].
- Arrest 30 May 1988: magnet invoices from Aturri read as detonator components, plus material found in a cavity at Zamaldegia; held at Fresnes through trial [R for the date]. Weapons and explosives counts later dismissed by *non-lieu*.
- His daughter **Iraia**, nineteen months, drowns in the pool at Zamaldegia, September 1988, while he is in Fresnes; body viewed at Bayonne under guard; refused permission to attend the funeral; he later said he contemplated suicide. [R: Berria 2014, verbatim for the core fact, "urte eta erdirekin zendu zen, etxeko igerilekuan, 1988an"; the day, the morgue scene and the guard are uncorroborated, and no morgue is named.]
- Trial 22-23 June 1989; verdict 13 July 1989. Sentence **four years' imprisonment and a ten-year ban on residing in the northern Basque Country** [A: es.wikipedia verbatim, eu.wikipedia, Berria]. **Source-attribution error corrected:** es.wikipedia gives no date beyond the year and no appeal reduction, so the 13 July date and the reduction to five years are **Enbata-only**. Co-defendants and the Sokoa link are dossier-level; the **courthouse is inferred**.
- Bois-d'Arcy, June to August 1991; released **18 August 1991 after 1,170 days** [A: Enbata's obituary, fetched directly, confirms both verbatim, so this need not stay "August 1991"]. 1989: severs links with ETA [A]. 1991: relocates to Bilbao to practise law [A: es.wikipedia], sharing an office with Txema Montero that failed for lack of clients [R].

## 7. After ETA, 1993-2017

- 1993: declares publicly that ETA has lost the struggle for self-determination as an armed organization [A: es.wikipedia]. Later close to or active in Elkarri [A: eu.wikipedia], with no dates and **no stop**, because no place or occasion attaches to it.
- January 1995: leaves Herri Batasuna after it refuses to condemn ETA's killing of **Gregorio Ordóñez** [A: en/es.wikipedia]. The killing was in Donostia on 23 January 1995; **no venue or date is documented for his own resignation** (see §8).
- 2001: joins Aralar, heads its Guarantees Commission, sits on its executive; May 2003, candidate for deputy general of Biscay; 2004, on its Senate and European Parliament lists [A: es.wikipedia]. 2003: appears as himself in Julio Medem's *La pelota vasca* [A, upgraded from [R]: es.wikipedia's article on the film lists him as "Cofundador de ETA. Miembro de Aralar"; the pool's un-refetched Variety review is dropped].
- June 2006: detained in a French-Spanish operation against an alleged ETA extortion-finance network, then released under judicial supervision, confined to Paris. **Genuine contradiction, left open:** es.wikipedia names Judge **Grande-Marlaska** as ordering the detention, the dossier names Judge **Laurence Le Vert**; en.wikipedia says charges were later dropped, the dossier says no dismissal order was located. No located conviction is not exoneration.
- 2012: stroke, thereafter a cane [R]. 2013: returns to Itsasu for the fiftieth anniversary [A: Enbata]. April 2017: appears at the Baiona disarmament gathering, favouring handover to an international body or Basque institutions rather than to Madrid or Paris [A: press via dossier]; no venue, and **no day within April is given for either event**, so the journey states none.

## 8. Books, death, afterlife, and two scenes dropped

- *Egiari zor*, Erein, Donostia, 13 May 2014, ISBN 978-84-9746-893-0 [A: txalaparta.eus; eu.wikipedia]; that he had to write it before dying is his own statement [R]. *En honor a la verdad*, trans. - **Death: 6 April 2021, at home at Senpere, aged 88, after long illness** [A: Berria, es/en/eu.wikipedia, El País, RTVE, La Vanguardia]. **Contradiction: Enbata alone says 5 April**, verified verbatim. - **Gap, stated as a gap: no funeral, burial site or grave marker appears in any source consulted.** Berria, Wikipedia and Enbata report the death and are silent on interment.
- *La línea invisible* (Movistar+, 2020): **Asier Etxeandia's "El inglés" IS Julen Madariaga** [A, upgraded from [R]: es.wikipedia's cast list names him explicitly; en.wikipedia's silence is why the dossier hedged it as a composite]. Reception, not travel; no stop. *Traidores* (Jon Viar, 2021) carries Mikel Azurmendi's allegation that Madariaga organised a cell vote on whether to kill the dissident Patxi Iturrioz [R: retrospective testimony in a film not viewed; no killing occurred, no order has surfaced]. **Not written**: no place, date or second witness.

**Two scenes dropped on the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there.**

1. **Anglet, 2 August 1979, the killing of "Pantu" Lopategui by the Batallón Vasco Español.** The pool staged it as an attack on Madariaga's company milieu; es.wikipedia's *Batallón Vasco Español* disproves that. The name is Juan José **Lopategui** Carrasco; he was machine-gunned on the **beach** and died next day in Bayonne; the wounded are Txomin Iturbe and Sasiain Etxabe; and the source calls him **a militant of ETA-m**, not an Aturri employee. Madariaga was not present, and with the Aturri link gone nothing ties the scene to him.
2. **Donostia, 23 January 1995, the killing of Gregorio Ordóñez.** The killing is not his stop; his resignation is. The journey stages that at Bilbao, its confidence field noting that the killing was in Donostia's Parte Vieja and that no venue or day is documented for the resignation.
