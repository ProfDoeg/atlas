# Paul Berthon (1872-1934), research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Paul Louis Joseph Berthon, French designer and lithographer, pupil of Eugène Grasset and Luc-Olivier Merson.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named on the spot. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or an attribution resting on catalogue metadata rather than a document. Contradictions are flagged and not silently reconciled; the two resolved in this pass are marked RESOLVED. Gaps are stated as gaps.

**Take away first.** English Wikipedia's dates for this man are wrong in a specific, traceable way: they belong to another artist. The dates that hold are 15 March 1872, Villefranche-sur-Saône, to 27 February 1934, Sceaux. See section 9.

---

## 1. Villefranche-sur-Saône, 1872 to c. 1893

- Born **15 March 1872 at Villefranche-sur-Saône** (Rhône), as **Paul Louis Joseph Berthon**. **[A, Wikidata Q2178796; fr.wikipedia, 'Paul Berthon'; corroborated by de.wikipedia. en.wikipedia gives the same date and place, so the editions differ only over the death.]**
- Son of a **primary-education inspector** (inspecteur de l'enseignement primaire). **[A, fr.wikipedia]**
- Takes his **first painting lessons in his native town** before moving to Paris. **[A as to the fact, en.wikipedia; R as to date, teacher, and institution: the source gives none of the three.]** In the journey file this is placed at a nominal 1885 purely to keep the sequence ahead of the 1893 Paris enrollment.

**Discrepancy, RESOLVED.** A Wikidata search snippet calls Berthon a lithographer "from Chartrettes" (Seine-et-Marne). It is a description-string artefact: the structured statement P19 on Q2178796 is Q208770, Villefranche-sur-Saône, and the "born in Chartrettes" clause belongs to Paul Émile Berton, born there 4 May 1846. No Chartrettes connection to Berthon. **[A, Wikidata Q2178796, structured statement vs. description string]**

## 2. The school on the rue Vavin, 1893 to c. 1896

- **From 1893 he attends the École normale d'enseignement du dessin in Paris.** **[A, fr.wikipedia]**
- **Correction, twice over.** Despite the name it was **not** a state school for drawing teachers but the private academy founded in 1881, the **École Guérin**, training designers for industry, at **19 rue Vavin and 9 rue Bréa, Paris 6e** (approx. **48.8435, 2.3298**). An earlier pass placed it at 48.85 / 2.345, some 1.3 km away near the Île de la Cité. **[A, fr.wikipedia, 'École normale d'enseignement du dessin']**
- Studies **painting under Luc-Olivier Merson**. **[A, en.wikipedia and fr.wikipedia]** Merson is himself an atlas traveler (`luc_olivier_merson.journey.json`), which makes this the one hard personal interlock the subject has.
- Also studies under **Puvis de Chavannes** at the same school. **[A, fr.wikipedia and de.wikipedia]**
- Studies **decorative art under Eugène Grasset**, who becomes the principal influence and turns him toward Art Nouveau. **[A, en, fr, and de.wikipedia, convergent]** en.wikipedia: Grasset "had a far greater influence on him, and he may be regarded as his pupil." He held the chair of decorative composition there **from 1891 to 1903 [A, fr.wikipedia]**; no source dates the classes, and the journey's 1895 is a midpoint. **[R as to year]**
- **Around 1896** he moves past direct imitation of Grasset and commits fully to Art Nouveau decorative design. **[A, de.wikipedia; the approximation is the source's own]**
- The stylistic comparison with **Maurice Pillard Verneuil**, a fellow Grasset pupil, is **fr.wikipedia's**, not en.wikipedia's, and it is dated rather than vague: "Tout comme Maurice Pillard Verneuil, les premières œuvres de Paul Berthon (vers 1896) montrent un style proche de celui de Grasset." **[A, fr.wikipedia]** An earlier pass attributed this to en.wikipedia, which does not mention Verneuil at all. Correction recorded.

## 3. The Salons, 1895 to 1896

- **Exhibits furniture designs and book bindings at the Salon, 1895.** **[A as to the fact, fr.wikipedia]** **[R as to venue.]** fr.wikipedia names no venue; an earlier pass inferred the Palais de l'Industrie and tagged it [A], which is a house-style violation on a reconstructed place and is probably wrong: applied art in 1895 was shown by the **Société nationale des beaux-arts at the Palais du Champ-de-Mars** (approx. 48.8556, 2.2986). **[R]** No source names the pieces. Gap.
- **Exhibits at the Salon des Cent**, the gallery run out of the offices of the review *La Plume* at **31 rue Bonaparte** (48.8546, 2.3336). **[A as to the venue address, fr.wikipedia, 'Salon des Cent'; A as to his participation]**
- **CONTRADICTION, left open.** en.wikipedia dates a Berthon Salon des Cent poster to **1895**; fr.wikipedia dates his participation to **1896**. The poster is for exhibition number 17, which falls across the turn of those two years. Neither reading is preferred here.
- Contributes illustrations to ***La Plume*** itself, associated with **1895 and 1899**. **[R, Wikimedia Commons, Category:Paul Berthon, file metadata only; no article text and no printed issue was consulted]**
- **Poster of Liane de Pougy at the Folies Bergère, 1896.** **[A, fr.wikipedia gallery caption: "Liane de Pougy aux Folies Bergère en 1896 par Paul Berthon"]** An earlier pass called this undated Folies Bergères work; the date exists, the subject is identified, and the venue is **Folies Bergère**, singular. Theatre coordinates (32 rue Richer, approx. 48.8752, 2.3444) are supplied here and are **[R]**.

## 4. The printers and the output, c. 1896 to 1900

- Prints **lithographs notably at Bourgerie et Cie** and **chromolithographs at Chaix et Cie**. **[A, fr.wikipedia]** Chaix's works were in the **rue Bergère, Paris 9e** (approx. 48.8727, 2.3450) **[R for the pin]**; **Bourgerie's address is not confirmed in any source reached**, a gap, and the reason the printers' pin is tagged [R] rather than [A].
- **Total output: about a hundred lithographs, chromolithographs, and woodcuts, two thirds of them decorative panels rather than commercial posters.** **[A, fr.wikipedia ONLY:** "une centaine de lithographies (imprimées notamment chez Bourgerie et Cie), de chromolithographies (chez Chaix & Cie) et gravures sur bois, dont deux tiers qualifiées de panneaux décoratifs"**]**
- **CONTRADICTION, left open.** de.wikipedia is not a witness for that figure and contradicts it, saying he left "hunderte von verschiedenartigen schwarzweißen und mehrfarbigen Lithographien", hundreds. An earlier pass cited both encyclopedias in support of "about a hundred." Cite French only, and record the German dissent.

### Les Maîtres de l'Affiche

The series ran **December 1895 to November 1900 [A, fr.wikipedia, 'Les Maîtres de l'affiche']**. Berthon's involvement, corrected in three particulars:

1. **The binding, January 1897.** He did **not** design the series cover in 1895; that is impossible, since the series only began in December 1895. He supplied the design for the **binding of the bound albums**: "relié par Jean Engel d'après la maquette de Paul Berthon". **[A, fr.wikipedia, 'Les Maîtres de l'affiche']** An earlier pass had this as a cover, dated 1895; corrected.
2. **Plate 175**, in volume 4, which ran to **November 1899**. **[A, fr.wikipedia]**
3. **Plate 227**, in volume 5, which ran to **November 1900**, plus **unpublished drawings** for that volume. **[A, fr.wikipedia]**

An earlier pass read "13 Les Maîtres de l'Affiche items" in the Commons category as thirteen plates. That is a file count, and it overstates him sixfold. **Two plates**, plus the binding, plus unpublished drawings.

## 5. The year 1897

fr.wikipedia groups three commissions in this one year:

- **Wood-engraved cover for the magazine *L'Image*, July 1897.** **[A, fr.wikipedia]** The publisher's address was not established; the journey uses the canonical Paris anchor.
- **Poster "Normandie-Bretagne" for the Compagnie des chemins de fer de l'Ouest.** **[A, en.wikipedia and fr.wikipedia]** Pinned at the company's terminus district near the Gare Saint-Lazare (48.8757, 2.3244) **[R for the pin]**. **No evidence he travelled to Normandy or Brittany**; period practice was to design from Paris.
- **Poster for the literary review *L'Ermitage*.** **[A, fr.wikipedia body text and gallery caption, both 1897; en.wikipedia's gallery also gives 1897, citing LACMA M.87.294.2]** An earlier chronology pass said **1898** while its own geography lens said 1897. **1897 is correct**; corrected.

## 9. The conflation with Paul Émile Berton, RESOLVED

An earlier pass carried this as an open contradiction. Publishing it as open would preserve an error.

- **en.wikipedia** states Berthon died **15 February 1909 in Paris, aged 36**, and opens: "Paul Berthon (15 March 1872 – 15 February 1909) was a French artist, who primarily produced posters and lithographs."
- **fr.wikipedia** opens: "Paul Berthon (à l'état-civil Paul Louis Joseph Berthon), né le 15 mars 1872 à Villefranche-sur-Saône et mort le 27 février 1934 à Sceaux."
- **The 1909 date belongs to a different man.** **Paul Émile Berton**: born **4 May 1846 at Chartrettes** (Seine-et-Marne), died **15 February 1909 in Paris 16e**, buried **Père-Lachaise, division 29**. **[A, fr.wikipedia, 'Paul Émile Berton']** That is exactly the day, month, city, and year en.wikipedia assigns to Berthon.
- fr.wikipedia's Berthon article opens with a **{{Confusion}} hatnote** naming Berton and Paul Bertho; Berton's article states "Il est parfois confondu avec le lithographe français Paul Berthon (1872-1934)" and notes that **Berthon lithographs are routinely misattributed to Berton on the market**. **[A]** Wikidata Q22132058 carries the same note. **[A]**
- The error is still propagating in museum labelling: **clevelandart.org/art/1991.175 gives "French, 1872-1909".** **[A]**

**Conclusion for the atlas: 1872-1934. The 1909 date is reported as an identified error, not as an open dispute.** An earlier pass folded a "Grasset confusion in dealer catalogs" claim into this; it is not in the cited source. The documented market confusion is with **Berton**. Corrected.
