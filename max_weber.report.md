# Max Weber (1864-1920): research report

*2026-08-04. Prepared for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `max_weber`. The queue line gave only "German sociologist" and a documents count of 2, so this is the corpus's first pass at the subject.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim in secondary literature not traceable to a source reachable this session. Where two reachable sources disagree, both readings are printed and the disagreement is left standing. A wrong pin is treated as a wrong fact.

One warning. Weber's life is well documented in German and badly geocoded in English. Almost every address in the reachable material is right as an address and wrong as a coordinate, sometimes by a kilometre. The largest category of correction below is cartographic, not biographical.

---

## 1. Erfurt and Charlottenburg, 1864-1882

**[A: German Wikipedia, "Max Weber"]** Born 21 April 1864 at Am Karthäuser Mühlsteg 44, Erfurt, today Juri-Gagarin-Ring 10. Eldest of eight children of Max Weber Sr., lawyer, municipal official and National Liberal parliamentarian, and Helene Fallenstein.

**Coordinate contradiction, flagged.** Two lenses of the pool gave two different pins for this one building and both are wrong: geography 50.977 / 11.036, afterlife 50.9787 / 11.0328. Nominatim places Juri-Gagarin-Ring 10 at **50.9709 / 11.0269**, used in the journey file. The two internal figures are about 900 m and 950 m off in different directions, the signature of independent guessing rather than of a shared bad source.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 1869, the family moves to Charlottenburg, then a town of its own beside Berlin, where the father's political salon runs through the house. 1870, the Doebbelinsche Privatschule. 1872-1882, the Kaiserin-Augusta-Gymnasium. **[R]** The Gymnasium pin (52.515 / 13.305) is a district approximation, not a surveyed address, and is tagged as such.

## 2. The law faculties, 1882-1893

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 1882, matriculates at Heidelberg in law. 1883-1884, military service at Strasbourg with lectures at the Kaiser-Wilhelms-Universität and evenings in the household of his uncle, the historian Hermann Baumgarten. 1884, Berlin and Göttingen. 15 May 1886, the Referendar examination. 1887, a semi-engagement to his cousin Emmy Baumgarten that does not reach marriage.

**Correction carried.** The pool had Weber joining **Corps Suevia**. German Wikipedia states flatly: "Weber war Mitglied der Studentenverbindung Burschenschaft Allemannia Heidelberg." The file names Allemannia. The duelling scar is standard in the literature downstream of Marianne Weber's *Lebensbild* and is retained as [A] on that basis; no fencing record was consulted.

**Correction carried.** The pool's geography lens made **August Meitzen** supervisor of the 1889 doctorate. He was not: the dissertation on medieval commercial partnerships was supervised by **Levin Goldschmidt and Rudolf von Gneist** [A: SEP; English Wikipedia]. Meitzen supervised the **1891 habilitation**. The pool contradicted itself across two lenses; the chronology lens had it right.

**[A: SEP]** 1891, habilitation, Privatdozent at Berlin. 1892, the East Elbian survey of agrarian labour for the Verein für Sozialpolitik, his first large empirical study. **[A: English Wikipedia; Marianne Weber, *A Biography*]** 20 September 1893, marries his second cousin Marianne Schnitger at Oerlinghausen; the marriage brings financial independence and produces no children.

**Coordinate correction.** Pool gave Oerlinghausen as 51.9575 / 8.7817, roughly 8 km east, in open country toward Detmold. Wikipedia's figure is 51°58′00″N 8°40′00″E = **51.9667 / 8.6667**, used in the file.

## 3. Freiburg and the Heidelberg chair, 1894-1897

**[A: German and English Wikipedia]** 1894, first full appointment, professor of economics at Freiburg, residence Schillerstraße 22. 1895, the inaugural address "The Nation State and Economic Policy," an aggressively nationalist statement that Weber's later admirers handle at arm's length and that Weber never withdraws.

**Coordinate correction.** Pool gave 47.999 / 7.842 for Schillerstraße 22. Nominatim puts it, in Mittelwiehre, at **47.9898 / 7.8515**, about 1.25 km southeast.

**[A: German Wikipedia]** 1896, called to Heidelberg to the chair of economics and finance vacated by Karl Knies.

**Correction carried, and it matters for dating.** The pool attached the "Weber Circle" of Jellinek, Troeltsch and Sombart to the 1896 appointment. German Wikipedia locates the Gesprächszirkel at the Fallensteinvilla only **after the Webers move there in 1910**: "nach Webers Umzug ... 1910 in die großelterliche Fallensteinvilla in der Ziegelhäuser Landstraße 17." Sombart is a poor fit in any case, being at Breslau then Berlin. The file dates the circle to 1910 and drops Sombart.

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 1897, a violent quarrel with his father over the father's treatment of Helene. Weeks later the father dies on a journey to Riga. No reconciliation.

**Gap.** No reachable source gives the room, the words or the day of the quarrel, and the Riga death has no address either. The file pins it to the Heidelberg university coordinate as a city-level marker, and deliberately **not** to the Fallensteinvilla, which the Webers do not occupy for another thirteen years; the pool's geography lens invited that error.

## 4. The collapse and the southern years, 1898-1903

**[A: SEP; German Wikipedia]** 1898, severe nervous collapse and admission to the Heilanstalt für Nervenkranke "Konstanzer Hof." Years in which he can neither lecture nor write for long stretches. **[R]** The building was not located; 47.6603 / 9.1758 is a Konstanz-area approximation, tagged in the file.

**Correction carried.** The pool's chronology lens folded Corsica and Italy into 1898. They belong later: English Wikipedia gives Bad Urach in **1900** and the Corsica and Italy travels across **1899-1903**; German Wikipedia gives Italian journeys 1899-1913 and a Rome stay from **October 1901 to March 1902** at Via Cicerone 3.

**Coordinate decision, a deliberate override.** Geography gave Via Cicerone 3 at 41.9053 / 12.4642. The interlock lens identified a **canonical corpus Rome pin at 41.8886 / 12.5064**, from `martin_luther.journey.json`. Canonical pins are inherited byte-identical, so the file uses it and names the address in prose: a knowing loss of about 3 km for corpus coherence, recorded so a later editor can reverse it.

**[A: German Wikipedia]** Florence is among the recovery stations of the same years; it is folded into the Corsica campa rather than given its own stop. **[A: SEP]** 1903, formal withdrawal from regular teaching; from here he is a private scholar with an honorary title.

## 5. America, 1904

**[A: English Wikipedia]** August to roughly November 1904: the Webers cross for the Congress of Arts and Science at the Louisiana Purchase Exposition in St. Louis, where Weber reads a paper on rural society. His only visit to the country and his only formal academic appearance there.

**[A: German Wikipedia]** The itinerary includes the Union Stock Yards at Chicago, Tuskegee Institute, and Indian Territory near Muskogee shortly before Oklahoma statehood. **[A: English Wikipedia]** Mount Airy, North Carolina, where emigrated Fallenstein relatives give him first-hand sight of German-American Protestant sectarianism; the visit feeds into "The Protestant Sects and the Spirit of Capitalism."

**[R]** Niagara Falls rests on Marianne Weber's biography as relayed in secondary itineraries, not confirmed against a fetched text. Omitted from the file.

**[R], and a coordinate problem on top of it.** Mount Vernon is repeated in secondary itineraries and attested in neither Wikipedia article nor any primary letter reachable here. The pool's coordinate (38.7292 / -77.1075) is also wrong for the named place: that is the modern census-designated place, about 2.9 km north of Washington's estate at roughly 38.7076 / -77.0861. Doubly weak, so **omitted from the journey file**.

**Sequence gap.** No reachable source fixes the order of the American stops after the September congress; the file's order is a reconstruction and the month values say so.

## 6. The private scholar at Heidelberg, 1904-1914

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 1904, associate editor of the *Archiv für Sozialwissenschaft und Sozialpolitik*, where the *Protestant Ethic* appears in two parts across 1904-1905. 1905, he learns Russian to follow the revolution and publishes two long essays on it. 1907, an inheritance secures him permanently against the need for a salary.

**[A: English Wikipedia; SEP]** 1909, co-founder and first treasurer of the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Soziologie, resigning the treasurership in 1912; the Venice journey with Marianne, Else von Richthofen and Mina Tobler. 1911, the beginning of his relationship with Tobler. 1913 and spring 1914, holidays at Monte Verità above Ascona.

**[A: German Wikipedia]** 1910, the move into the grandparents' Fallensteinvilla at Ziegelhäuser Landstraße 17, and the circle that gathers there.

**Coordinate correction.** Pool gave the villa at 49.4077 / 8.7194, south of the Neckar. Nominatim puts the address north of the river at **49.4158 / 8.7124**, about a kilometre away.

**Gap, and a real one.** Simmel, Troeltsch, Jaspers, Jellinek and Lukács, the actual population of the Heidelberg circle, are absent from the entire corpus, as are Friedrich Naumann and the Verein für Sozialpolitik "socialists of the chair." Gaps, not contradictions, and they limit how much mutual gaze the Heidelberg stops can honestly carry.

## 7. War, Lauenstein, and the two vocations, 1914-1919

**[A: English Wikipedia; SEP]** August 1914, Weber volunteers as a reserve officer and directs army hospitals in the Heidelberg district through the end of 1915, supporting the war and then turning hard against the Kaiser's conduct of it. 1915, his brother Karl is killed near Brest-Litovsk, and "The Economic Ethics of the World Religions" begins appearing.

**[A: English and German Wikipedia]** 1917, the Lauenstein Conferences; Weber is photographed in discussion at the castle in May 1917 and clashes with the younger romantics over rationalization.

**Coordinate correction.** Pool gave 50.484 / 11.457 for Schloss Lauenstein. German Wikipedia gives Burg Lauenstein at 50°30′47.9″N 11°22′11″E = **50.5133 / 11.3697**, about 7 km northwest.

**Correction carried, and a pin left knowingly imprecise.** The pool attributed both vocation lectures to Weber's Munich chair. Neither belongs to it: "Wissenschaft als Beruf" came in **November 1917** and "Politik als Beruf" on **28 January 1919**, both to the Freistudentischer Bund and both **before** the appointment. No reachable source names the hall, so both carry the LMU coordinate as a Munich city marker, flagged rather than hidden.

**[A: English Wikipedia; SEP]** 1918, a summer-semester course at Vienna, his first regular lecturing in about twenty years. 1918-1919, the Weimar constitutional committee, the founding of the German Democratic Party, the delegation at Versailles, and an unsuccessful DDP candidacy in January 1919.

## 8. Munich and the death, 1919-1920

**[A: English Wikipedia]** 1919, chair at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität. The lecture course is published posthumously in 1923 as *Wirtschaftsgeschichte*, in English *General Economic History*, compiled by Siegmund Hellmann and Melchior Palyi from students' transcripts.

**Correction carried.** The pool called that course "Economy and Society material." It is not: *Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft* is the separate unfinished Grundriss manuscript Marianne edited in 1921-1922.

**Dating note.** The pool put the Munich appointment at April 1919, before the Versailles delegation of 13 May. No reachable source says whether he lectured before he travelled, so the file dates the chair stop to June 1919, the summer semester after the return, rather than asserting an April start the sources do not support.

**[A: German Wikipedia]** The Webers take an apartment at Seestraße 3c in Munich-Schwabing. 7 April 1920, his sister Lili Schäfer takes her own life and the Webers adopt her four children. 14 June 1920, Weber dies of pneumonia at the apartment, aged fifty-six, probably in the tail of the influenza pandemic.

**Coordinate correction.** Pool gave 48.1633 / 11.5838 in one lens and 48.16 / 11.585 in another for the same address. Nominatim puts Seestraße 3, Alt-Schwabing, at **48.1590 / 11.5890**; both pool figures sit several hundred metres northwest.

**[R], removed.** The afterlife lens added that Seestraße 3c is "now numbered 16." That appears nowhere in the German Wikipedia passage it cites; dropped.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A: English and German Wikipedia]** June 1920, a secular funeral at the Munich Ostfriedhof, Marianne delivering the eulogy; the body is cremated. Eduard Baumgarten, Karl Loewenstein and Lujo Brentano are recorded among those present.

**Coordinate contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** Geography gave 48.117 / 11.612, which lands in Ramersdorf about 1.6 km too far east; the afterlife lens gave 48.1181 / 11.5931. German Wikipedia's figure for the cemetery is 48°7′4″N 11°35′20″E = **48.1178 / 11.5889**, in Obergiesing, and the file uses it. The pool's verifier called the afterlife figure "the closer one" while supplying the Wikipedia figure that differs from it; the remark is internally inconsistent and is recorded, not adjudicated.

**[A: German Wikipedia]** 1921, the urn is interred at the Heidelberger Bergfriedhof, whose article lists "Max Weber (1864-1920)" and "Marianne Weber, geb. Schnitger (1870-1954)" together in Abteilung E.

**Coordinate correction.** Pool gave 49.4045 / 8.6924 and 49.402 / 8.687 in two lenses. The cemetery's own coordinate is **49.3972 / 8.6903**; the pool figures sit up to 800 m north, near or outside its boundary.

**[R], demoted.** "Roughly one thousand mourners" at the 1921 interment appears in neither Wikipedia article. It survives in the file only as a phrase attributed to tradition, not as a count.

**[A: German Wikipedia]** *Gesammelte Aufsätze zur Religionssoziologie*, three volumes 1920-1921; *Gesammelte Aufsätze zur Wissenschaftslehre*, 1922; *Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft*, 1921-1922; Marianne's biography, 1926; *Wirtschaftsgeschichte*, 1923.

**Correction carried.** The pool credited all three GARS volumes to Marianne. Weber assembled and revised **volume I himself** before his death and it appeared later in 1920; her editorial work covers volumes II and III and the *Wissenschaftslehre*.

**[A: English Wikipedia; Wikipedia, "Iron cage"]** Parsons's 1930 translation; the Scribner's edition of 1958 with Parsons's added section titles; the Baehr and Wells Penguin retranslation of 2002. Parsons renders *stahlhartes Gehäuse* as "iron cage," a phrase that outlives every objection to it; later translators prefer "shell as hard as steel."

**[A: German Wikipedia]** A memorial plaque marks the Erfurt birth house. **Gap:** no reachable source gives its installation date, so it is not a stop; a stop needs a date, and inventing one would be worse than omitting the plaque.

## Quotations: verified, corrected, and not verified

Two attributions in the pool were wrong and are corrected in the journey file.

1. **"Politics is a strong and slow boring of hard boards. It takes both passion and perspective."** Gerth and Mills, *From Max Weber: Essays in Sociology* (1946), p. 128, verified by full-text search in the scanned 1946 edition (archive.org item `frommaxweberessa00webe`, scan p. 158). The pool's wording ("a slow, strong drilling through hard boards ... with both passion and a sense of judgment") is the later Livingstone rendering in *The Vocation Lectures* (Hackett 2004), which could not be opened this session, yet the pool cited it to Gerth and Mills.

2. **"Bureaucracy has a 'rational' character: rules, means, ends, and matter-of-factness dominate its bearing."** Verbatim, but from Gerth and Mills 1946, "Bureaucracy," **p. 244**, not Roth and Wittich's *Economy and Society* (1968) as the pool claimed. Same underlying German, different English.

Two *Protestant Ethic* quotations were silently truncated in the pool and are restored. Parsons ch. 2 reads "**And in truth this** peculiar idea, so familiar to us to-day ...", where the pool began at "The peculiar idea"; ch. 4 reads "On the other hand, in order to attain that self-confidence **intense worldly activity is recommended** as the most suitable means," where the pool presented the fragment as a whole sentence. The file marks the elision and gives the full sentence in `quote_source`.

**[R], carried as such.** "The state is a relation of men dominating men, a relation supported by means of legitimate (i.e. considered to be legitimate) violence." Commonly cited, not verified against any fetched text; used once, with a `quote_source` that says so. The Westminster Confession passage on double predestination is **not Weber's own words** and is not used as a stop quote.

## Interlock with the corpus

Named in campa where paths genuinely cross: **martin_luther** (Erfurt matriculation, cloister and first mass, a few streets from the birth house; the *Beruf* doctrine the *Protestant Ethic* argues from directly, a real documented intellectual through-line), **hegel** (Heidelberg chair, shared streets), **goethe** (read directly at the Gymnasium, a real documented citation — separate from the coincidental Marianne von Willemer walk, cut below), **humboldt** (the Berlin university), **karl_marx** (the contrast point, kept as a real intellectual dialogue: Weber inverts Marx's materialist thesis, but the "elsewhere in this atlas" self-reference was cut).

**Considered and not used**, removed under the house-style tightening of 2026-08-14, each a coincidental crossing or bare self-reference with no relation to Weber: **martin_luther** a second time at the Heidelberg-Knies-chair stop and the Rome pin stop (both pure city-sharing coincidence, distinct from the real Erfurt/*Beruf* connection which stays), **felix_adler** and **samuel_oppenheimer** (Heidelberg in other centuries), **keyserling** (Fallensteinvilla Sunday circle, "same intellectual weather" only), **symmes** and **tschiffely** (St. Louis, pure coincidence), **napoleon** and **mussolini** (both cut to remove the named reference while keeping the real point about later reception of Weber's own typology/charismatic-authority concept), **itten** and **engelmann** (Munich-Schwabing, other dates, other districts, no relation), **goethe**/Marianne von Willemer at the Burschenschaft matriculation stop (a different Marianne, an unrelated walk, a coincidence of first name and city only).

**Non-connections, checked and rejected.** The canonical Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354) and Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816) pins have no attested link to Weber's itinerary. He wrote about Confucianism, Hinduism, Buddhism and ancient Judaism from desks in Heidelberg and Munich and travelled to none of those places; his only transatlantic crossing is 1904 and northbound. Those pins are absent from the file.
