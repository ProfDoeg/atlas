# Claude-Henri de Rouvroy, comte de Saint-Simon (1760-1825)
*Research report, 2026-08-23, for `henri_de_saint_simon.journey.json`: 45 stops, 8 segments, 11 quotations.*

Legend: **[A]** attested · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or self-report · contradictions flagged and left standing.

Saint-Simon's record is thinnest exactly where the legend is thickest. The middle years,
1798-1813, survive almost entirely through tradition assembled by his own disciples after his
death, and those disciples had a doctrinal interest in the shape of the story.

---

## 1. Birth and boyhood, 1760-1777

**Born Paris, 17 October 1760 [A]**: BnF authority record `ark:/12148/cb11923405t`.
Sandricourt branch of the Rouvroy de Saint-Simon family, distant kin of the memoirist duc.
**Exact birth address: not established [gap]**; the file uses the canonical Paris pin.
**Charlemagne descent via the counts of Vermandois [R]**: family tradition, held unproven or
disproved by modern genealogists. Retained because it is load-bearing for the 1794 vision.

**Childhood between Paris and Falvy, Picardy [R]**: later tradition; no primary childhood
document located. The family's *tenure* of Falvy is documented independently: fr.wikipedia's
commune page names **Balthazar de Saint-Simon Rouvroy (1780)** and **Claude Henri de Saint
Simon (1789)** in its seigneur list.

*Coordinate correction:* an earlier value of 49.8256 / 2.9958 is ~2.6 km off, in farmland
east of the village. fr.wikipedia gives 49°49'30"N, 2°57'35"E = **49.8250 / 2.9597**, used.

**Rousseau as his tutor [R, legendary]**: circulates in the tradition; the dossier itself
calls it unreliable and nothing in this pass confirms it. **Kept, not deleted**, with
`date_confidence` naming it legend: it is a claim about Saint-Simon's own formation.

**Military commission [contradiction]**: January 1777 per his own later account and Musso;
1775 per an Encyclopedia of the American Revolution entry. No muster roll consulted decides
it. File dates 1777, names both.

## 2. The American war, 1779-1783

**Régiment de Touraine, sailing from Brest, 1779 [A for regiment and port]**: New World
Encyclopedia; fr.wikipedia's *Régiment de Touraine* lists Yorktown and Martinique 1780 among
its honours. *Correction:* the "age seventeen" gloss on the 1779 sailing is impossible. Born
17 October 1760, he is **eighteen** through most of 1779, and the 1911 Britannica puts him in
America "at nineteen"; seventeen fits the 1777 enlistment and has been dropped here.
fr.wikipedia attaches him to the **régiment d'Aquitaine** for the later Mézières period, so
Touraine is specific to the American service.

**Saint-Domingue [R]**: no port, no date, no landing site; the coordinate is Cap-Français as
a stand-in. **Barbados, 1780 [R]**: summaries "connect him with operations around"
Barbados; no engagement named, nothing verified past that phrasing.

**Yorktown, October 1781 [A for the siege; R for his role]**: the gunnery command rests on
military-reference tradition, not a muster document. *Identity warning, carried into the
file:* the **marquis Claude-Anne de Rouvroy de Saint-Simon-Montbléru**, an older relative,
commanded some 3,500 men at this same siege; material about him (e.g.
americanrevolutioninstitute.org) is routinely misfiled under Henri and is **excluded** here.
**Pin inherited byte-identical**: 37.2384 / -76.5074, not the lens's 37.2192 / -76.5172.

**Departure with de Grasse, 4 November 1781 [R]**: the fleet movement is documented, his
presence aboard follows the chronology. **Brimstone Hill [A, window corrected]**: "1782-02"
is too narrow: per en.wikipedia the siege **began 11 January 1782** and the garrison
surrendered after about a month, standard date **12 February**. **Saintes, 12 April 1782, and
capture [A]**. **Prisoner of war in Jamaica to the 1783 peace [A]**, but **no place of detention
on the island is named anywhere consulted [gap]**; coordinate is Kingston as a stand-in.

## 3. The projector years, 1783-1789

**Interoceanic canal proposal to the viceroy of New Spain, 1783 [A]**: the **1911
Britannica** corroborates the scheme independently, hence the [A]. **No engineering dossier
and no viceregal reply located [gap].**

**Mézières [R], with a well-loved claim removed.** The line that he studied under **Gaspard
Monge at Mézières is chronologically impossible**: Monge took the post in January 1769 and
had **left by 1783**, while Saint-Simon was in the Caribbean and then a British prisoner
until the 1783 peace; fr.wikipedia puts him at Mézières with the régiment d'Aquitaine only
*after* 1783. Monge is **not deleted, but moved** to the c. 1798 Polytechnique stop, where he
was in fact teaching descriptive geometry.

**Netherlands, c. 1785 [R]**: tradition only, no city, no document; Amsterdam coordinate as
a generic landmark, admitted in the file. **Madrid, Canal del Guadarrama and the Banco de San
Carlos, 1787-1789 [A for involvement]**: entrepreneurial involvement, **not** authorship of
the design; the Guadarrama project itself is documented infrastructure history.

## 4. Revolution, speculation, prison, 1789-1797

**Endorsement of the Revolution and the name Claude Bonhomme, 1789 [R for the pseudonyms]**.
Note the friction: the Falvy seigneur list still carries his name that year.

**Speculation in biens nationaux with the comte de Redern, from 1790 [A]**, though the **size of
the fortune is nowhere quantified [gap]**. **The Notre-Dame roof scheme [R]**: reported in
modern Oxford scholarship, sometimes linked to Talleyrand; **not established that it was
executed**, and Talleyrand's part uncertain.

**Arrest 1793, Sainte-Pélagie, transfer to the Luxembourg [A]**: a general-security measure,
per Dautry's archival study as characterized in the dossier.

**Release [contradiction, unresolved].** A precise date of **28 August 1794 is unsupported**
and is inconsistent with the "roughly eleven months" figure carried alongside it: arrest in
November 1793 plus eleven months runs to about October 1794. fr.wikipedia says only "après le
9 thermidor" (27 July 1794); en.wikipedia says 1794 at the Terror's end. File dates
**1794-07-28** as the earliest defensible marker and asserts neither the day nor the months.

**The Charlemagne vision [R, self-report]**: via Rodrigues's 1832 edition and the disciple
tradition (Fournel, 1865). No independent witness exists.

**Palais-Royal mansion, ~twenty servants, the salon, from October 1795 [R]**: Musso's 2025
correspondence edition. **Collapse of the Redern partnership, 1797 [A]**: settlement terms
not verified [gap].

## 5. The science of man, 1798-1813

**Self-education programme from c. 1798 [R]**: Musso / Swedberg; attendance at either school
**not documented [gap]**. This is where Monge belongs (see §3). **Marriage, 7 Aug 1801 [A];
divorce within about a year, 1802 [A]**; the reported motive, wanting a salon hostess, is
tradition. **The Madame de Staël proposal anecdote [R]** comes from Halévy's 1832
recollections, seven years posthumous, and is attributed there in the campa itself.

**Lettres d'un habitant de Genève [contradiction].** The BnF holds records dated **both 1802
and 1803** (Gallica's copy is 1803); most modern scholarship says 1803, en.wikipedia and the
1911 Britannica 1802, and **the imprint is effectively undated**. File dates 1803, names the
conflict. The persona is documented by the BnF author form **"Habitant de Genève, Un."**

**The racial-hierarchy passage [A as quoted; attribution corrected].** It was circulating
**truncated**, beginning mid-thought with "The negro" recapitalized; Wikipedia's wording is
two sentences, opening "The revolutionaries applied the principles of equality to negroes."
Wikipedia also does **not** attribute it to *Lettres d'un habitant de Genève*: it says only
"Lettres," unspecified. The file carries both sentences verbatim, cites the work as
unspecified, and states the content flat in the campa rather than softening it.

**Travels in Britain, Germany, Switzerland, 1802-1804 [R]**: no itinerary, no address, no
document; kept as a gap-shaped stop at the London pin that says it is a gap.

**Letter to Fourcroy, 1804 [R]**. **Appeals toward Napoleon [R]** are **petitionary, not a
documented meeting**. **Introduction aux travaux scientifiques [contradiction]**: Wikipedia
1803, **BnF 1807** (J.-L. Scherff, in-4, pp. 1-176); file follows the BnF. **Copyist at the
Mont-de-Piété, c. 1807 [R]**: Larousse tradition, **no employment record [gap]**; **Diard
the ex-servant [R]**, standard accounts, no address [gap]. **Letter to Duméril, 6 November
1813 [R]**: FranceArchives catalogue record; **the letter's text was not read in this
pass**, only the record, and the file says so.

## 6. The industrial age named, 1814-1821

**De la réorganisation de la société européenne, 1814, with Augustin Thierry [A]**: BnF
entry, both names on the title page.

**The colonization quotation [A, corrected from truncation].** The ellipsis was ours, not the
citing source's; Wikipedia gives the complete sentence, which the file now carries, sourced
to the 1814 work co-written with Thierry and quoted via **Richard Swedberg, "Saint-Simon's
vision of a united Europe," *European Journal of Sociology***.

**Arsenal librarianship during the Hundred Days [contradiction, flagged in the file].** The
Larousse tradition has him with Benjamin Constant and briefly librarian at the Arsenal,
losing the post after Waterloo. **His own 1821 letter to Robin Scévole claims he accepted no
government office since 1789.** Both cannot be true; neither is dropped.

**L'Industrie, from December 1816 [A]**: New World Encyclopedia says 1817, the dossier's
bibliography December 1816; minor conflict, noted, no publishing address [gap]. **Comte as
secretary from c. 1817 [A]**: reference entries date it from *L'Organisateur* (1819),
Bourdeau from c. 1817, which the file follows while naming the discrepancy.

**L'Organisateur prospectus, August 1819; the Parable [A]**: the **"Parabole de
Saint-Simon" label was fixed later**, by Rodrigues's 1832 edition; the passage was never
published as a standalone pamphlet in 1819. That distinction is carried into the stop.

**Prosecution after the duc de Berry's assassination; acquittal 23 March 1820 [R for the
date]**. **Second arrest over the "double vote" law, June 1820 [R]**: Larousse tradition,
separate matter, unconfirmed; folded into the acquittal stop.

**Du système industriel, 1821 [A]**: the "administration of things" formula is **not a
verbatim Saint-Simon sentence** but a later crystallization, principally by Engels, of
arguments present in these pages. Both the campa and `date_confidence` say so.

## 7. The last years, 1823-1825

**Suicide attempt, 9 March 1823 [A]**: date and the loss of sight in one eye; the **widely
repeated "six bullets" detail is unverified and excluded**, and the address is not
established [gap]. **Rodrigues's support from 1823 [R]**: tradition, corroborated by his
documented editorial role after 1825. **Catéchisme des industriels, 1823-1824 [A]**, third
cahier substantially Comte's [A]. **Break with Comte, 1824 [A]**. **Halévy as final
secretary, 1824-1825 [R]**; his 1832 memoir is the major anecdote source, and therefore the
reason much of that tradition cannot be independently checked.

**Nouveau Christianisme, 1825 [A for work and year; month unsupported].** A sourcing error
worth stating loudly: the BnF ark circulating for this work, **`ark:/12148/cb31285237m`, is
the wrong book**. That record is *Introduction aux travaux scientifiques*, 1807, J.-L.
Scherff, **double-assigned** to both. The right record is *Nouveau christianisme, dialogues
entre un conservateur et un novateur. Premier dialogue*, **Bossange père**, 1825. The BnF
record does **not** support the month; the file's April is tradition only, and it says so.

**Death, 19 May 1825, 9 rue du Faubourg-Montmartre, about 10 p.m. [A date; R hour].**
*Coordinate correction:* a working 48.8737 / 2.3425 sits mid-street near nos. 27-30, ~190 m
off; geocoding against the French national address base returns **48.872032 / 2.343056**, so
the file uses **48.8720 / 2.3431**. The address survives scrutiny (fr.wikipedia puts the
death in the ancien 2e arrondissement, which held the quartier du Faubourg-Montmartre).
**The hour is uncorroborated**, and france-memoire.fr **could not be fetched (HTTP 403)**, so
address and hour rest on the Musso characterization, not a page read directly.

**Civil funeral at Père-Lachaise, 22 May 1825 [A]**, per appl-lachaise.net, fetched directly:
Division 28, chemin Camille Jordan 1ère ligne, ref M=Q-28, family absent, Rodrigues / Comte /
Thierry / Enfantin behind the cortège. **Attendance lists vary among later accounts.**
*Coordinate reconciled:* the two lenses gave 2.3956 and 2.3936 for one grave, and 2.3956
drifts east of Division 28; the file uses the **corpus pin 48.8614 / 2.3931**. **No
restoration history or patron for the tomb was found [gap]**.

## 8. Posthumous: how the canon was made

**Le Producteur [A, chronology corrected].** "Immediately after the funeral" compresses four
months. Per fr.wikipedia's *Saint-simonisme*: proposed **at the funeral**; **partnership
signed 1 June 1825**; **first issue 1 October 1825**, epigraph *"L'âge d'or, qu'une aveugle
tradition a placé jusqu'ici dans le passé, est devant nous"*; **ceased October 1826**.
Founding party corrected: Rodrigues **with Prosper Enfantin** as managing partners,
**Laffitte, Ternaux and Halévy** as shareholders. Documented, so [A].

**Rodrigues's 1832 anthology [A]**: prefixed by "Fragmens de l'histoire de sa vie écrite par
lui-même"; the volume that fixed the canonical corpus. **The Suez legend [R]**: the
organized Suez advocacy belonged to the **posthumous movement under Enfantin**, while
Saint-Simon's own 1780s proposal concerned **Spanish America**. Not a stop here.

**1918 Soviet monumental-propaganda list [A for the list]**: ru.wikipedia names him **27th**
among the 66-70 figures under Lenin's decree, with Fourier and Owen. **The source does not
confirm a monument was built**; a French secondary claim that a statue went up alongside
Lenin's is unconfirmed and dropped.

**Modern editions [A]**: *Oeuvres complètes*, 4 vols., eds. Grange / Musso / Régnier /
Yonnet, PUF, from 2012. Bicentenary 2025: Musso's *Correspondance (1782-1825)*, Manucius,
446 pp.; and his biography, Fayard, 15 October 2025, 704 pp., ISBN 9782213716893, whose
*title* is ***Saint-Simon. Le philosophe de la société industrielle***, the "Le" belonging to
the subtitle and having been dropped in circulation. **Markham's 1952 *Selected Writings*
[A]**: Open Library; **publisher not confirmable** from the record alone, so omitted.

## 9. Whose stop is it

**Kept, rooted in his own life, named in campas:** **`jean_jacques_rousseau`** (the tutor
claim, heavily hedged, `date_confidence` calling it legend) and **`napoleon`** (petitionary
only, **no meeting documented**; he also appears twice as historical weather, which is
context, not an interlock claim).

**Refused as campa material, kept as sources or refs:**

- **Engels**: named in the *Du système industriel* campa and two quote attributions; see
  §10. No Engels file exists in the corpus.
- **`karl_marx`**: his file already stages the 1834 Trier scene where Westphalen "speaks of
  Saint-Simon and of the coming reign of association": **Marx's stop on Marx's file**, not
  restaged here. The 1840s Paris overlap is inference only. **`charles_fourier`**: the
  Manifesto's "utopian socialism" filing is a posthumous classification by third parties, not
  contact between the two men.
- **`marques_de_lafayette`, Miranda**: both at Yorktown, but shared geography is not a
  relation and the map already shows co-location; the **pin** is inherited, the name is not.
  **`marcel_proust`**: Père-Lachaise pin only. **`thomas_more`, `edward_bellamy`**:
  thematic QUEUE.md thread, no contact possible. **`louis_xvi`**: backdrop. **Madame de
  Staël**: no file, so no pin to inherit. **Enfantin and Suez** covers his own
  traveling and his own decades, so report only, no stop.

**Corpus gap, noted not asserted:** there is **no `auguste_comte.journey.json`**, despite
Comte being central to the last decade. He is named in four campas purely as a man in
Saint-Simon's own room. Whether he warrants a file is the operator's.

## 10. Apparatus relations for the operator

Saint-Simon is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing was silently deleted; these are
recorded for a human ruling.

1. **Forward direction, Engels (d. 1895).** The *Du système industriel* campa names Engels
   and the closing stop carries an Engels quotation, because the famous formulas are his
   compressions and stating them without him would be a fabrication. Under the block rule
   this is a **forward reference**, not licensed by the exception. Flagged, not removed: to
   remove it, move both mentions into `sources` / `suggested_refs`, which already carry them.

2. **Forward direction, afterlife refs on the closing stop.** Its `suggested_refs` point at
   the **1918 Soviet monument list**, the **PUF *Oeuvres complètes* (2012-)**, and **Musso's
   2025 Fayard biography**. The corpus does normally let a final stop look at its subject's
   afterlife (cf. `abdelkader.journey.json`, closing on a 1966 repatriation), but these are
   strictly post-1825 artifacts, kept in refs rather than prose. Flagged for a ruling.

3. **No backward-apparatus material found**, so no `EXCEPTIONS.md` listing is requested.
