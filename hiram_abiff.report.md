# Hiram Abiff - research report

*2026-08-06. Subject: the bronze craftsman of 1 Kings 7 and 2 Chronicles 2-4, called Hiram, Huram, or Huram-abi in scripture, and Hiram Abif or Hiram Abiff in Freemasonry. Slug `hiram_abiff`. Companion artifact: `hiram_abiff.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

**Legend: [A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or ritual legend with no external attestation.

One finding governs everything below. The man and the legend are two objects, twenty-seven centuries apart. The man is two paragraphs of scripture about metalwork. The legend, meaning the murder, the three ruffians, the acacia, and the lost word, is an English composition of the early 1720s. No source between them carries a death. This report keeps the two apart and marks the seam.

---

## 1. The two Hirams, and the two mothers

Scripture presents two figures named Hiram in the Solomonic material, and the tradition confuses them constantly.

- **Hiram I, king of Tyre** [A: Josephus via Menander of Ephesus; 1 Kings 5]. A documented monarch with a king-list; a thirty-four year reign conventionally from c. 980 BC; dies c. 947 aged about fifty-three by Josephus's reckoning, succeeded by Baal-Eser I.
- **Hiram / Huram-abi, the bronze craftsman** [A: 1 Kings 7:13-14; 2 Chronicles 2:13-14]. A widow's son, sent by the king to Solomon. This is the atlas subject.

The corpus already keeps them apart: `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` lists Hiram Abiff and Hiram de Tiro as separate entries (lines 375, 390), and `solomon.journey.json` names "another Hiram, cunning in brass."

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved: the mother's tribe.** 1 Kings 7:14 makes her a widow of the tribe of **Naphtali**. 2 Chronicles 2:14 makes her a woman of the daughters of **Dan**. Naphtali is upper Galilee; the Danite association in Judges (Manoah of Zorah, "of the family of the Danites") is the western foothills a hundred miles south. Both are pinned in the journey, Hazor 33.0201/35.5664 and Zorah 31.7751/34.9853, and neither is preferred.

**Corrections applied.** (a) The pool put Zorah at 31.7616/34.9987, about two kilometres off the tell; Wikipedia 'Zorah' gives 31.7751/34.9853. (b) The pool cited 2 Samuel 5:11 for Hiram calling David "brother." That verse, read verbatim in the KJV, contains no such word; it is at 1 Kings 9:13, addressed to Solomon. The same entry dated a David alliance to 1000 BC, which precedes Hiram's own accession at 980 in the entry above it. Neither claim is carried into the journey.

**Gaps.** No birth year, no house, no name for the mother, no death, no burial. Tyre is the only fixed point for his origin, and the journey's -0990 birth date is a placeholder marked as one in `date_confidence`.

**[R], and cut.** Antiquarian tradition apprentices him to a bronze-workers' guild at Tyre's temple of Melqart. No ancient source names him in any guild; the link is reasoned backward from his trade. Recorded here, given no stop.

## 2. The work: Tyre to Jerusalem, and the casting ground

- **The league** [A: 1 Kings 5:1-10; 2 Chronicles 2:12-14]. Hiram of Tyre renews the alliance on Solomon's succession and supplies cedar, cypress, and labor; the craftsman comes with the bargain. Solomon's Tyre pin 33.271/35.196 at -0966-01-01 is inherited byte-identical.
- **The cedars** [A for the timber, R for the man]. Solomon's cedars pin 34.245/36.049 at -0966-06-01, inherited. No verse puts the craftsman on the mountain, and the stop says so.
- **Sidon** [R, cut]. The pool proposed Sidon as a waypoint on a route running "south past Sidon" from Tyre to Jerusalem. That is backwards: Sidon at 33.56 N lies north of Tyre at 33.27 N. The inference has no textual support and an error in its own reasoning, so it gets no stop.
- **Joppa** [A: 2 Chronicles 2:16]. Timber comes ashore in floats and goes up overland. The craftsman's own passage through the port is inference and is marked so.
- **The building** [A: 1 Kings 6:1, 6:38]. Begun in Ziv of the fourth year, c. 966; finished in Bul of the eleventh, c. 959; seven years. Traditional chronology puts completion at 959 or 958.
- **The castings** [A: 1 Kings 7:15-47; 2 Chronicles 3:15-17]. Jachin and Boaz; the molten sea on twelve oxen; ten lavers; pots, shovels, basins, fleshhooks.
- **The casting ground** [A: 1 Kings 7:46]. "In the plain of Jordan, in the clay ground between Succoth and Zarethan." Succoth is conventionally Tell Deir Alla, 32.1889/35.6167; the identification is convention, not proof.

**Contradiction flagged: Zarethan.** The pool coordinate 32.1219/35.5794 matches neither candidate. Wikipedia 'Zaretan' gives 32.0958/35.4614, Tell el-Mazar on the west bank in Wadi Far'a, as the now-preferred site; Nelson Glueck's older east-bank proposal, Tell es-Sa'idiyeh, sits near 32.2668/35.5556 and is flagged as superseded. The journey takes Tell el-Mazar and names the rejected alternative in `date_confidence`.

**Divergence from the interlock lens.** The lens reported that `solomon.journey.json` pins "the plain of Jordan at Zarethan" at 34.245/36.049. Direct inspection shows that pin belongs to Solomon's cedars of Lebanon stop. This journey inherits it as the cedars and uses Jordan-valley coordinates for the casting ground.

**The silence after the bronze** [A, by absence: 1 Kings 8]. Hiram is not at the dedication and is not mentioned in it. No payment, no return to Tyre, no death. That absence is where the legend later gets its room, and it is the only feature of the biblical record the eighteenth century needed.

## 3. The legend. Everything in this section is [R]

The developed third-degree narrative, in brief: fifteen fellowcrafts conspire, twelve recant, three waylay the master at the south, west, and east gates at high twelve with gauge, square, and setting maul; the body is hidden under rubbish, reburied on a hillside under acacia, found by the twelve searching in parties of three; the killers are taken in a cave near Joppa, confess, and are executed; the body is raised after fourteen days, and a substitute word is adopted. The journey stages it stop by stop.

Four corrections, each a case where the pool contradicted itself.

1. **Joint holder, not sole holder.** One entry made Hiram sole possessor of the word. The ritual, and the pool's own East Gate entry, have him say the word is held by three (Solomon, Hiram king of Tyre, himself) and cannot be given by one alone. The joint reading is the only one that works: his death loses the word rather than withholding it.
2. **Twelve search, not fifteen.** Solomon sends the twelve who recanted, in four parties of three. Fifteen is the number who conspired.
3. **The ruffians' names are American.** Jubela, Jubelo, and Jubelum come through the Preston-Webb monitorial line. English Emulation ritual leaves the three unnamed, so the pool's attribution of the names to "Emulation and related workings" is wrong.
4. **The Preston misattribution.** Six pool entries sourced the noon devotions, the three gates, the named ruffians, the acacia, and the lion's paw to William Preston, *Illustrations of Masonry* (1772). Preston's book is a history and a lecture system, twelve lectures per degree given for a fee at the Mitre Tavern from 1774, and it does not print ritual. Those elements come from the exposure literature (Prichard 1730; *Three Distinct Knocks* 1760; *Jachin and Boaz* 1762) and, for the American wording, Webb (1797). Every affected stop was re-sourced.

**Fabricated precision, removed.** The pool gave the ruffians' cave 31.98/34.85 and the well 31.9812/34.8523 while stating in the same entries that neither can be located. The journey collapses both into one stop pinned at the port of Joppa, 32.0533/34.7514, a real place, and says in the campa that the cave and the well are given no coordinates because they have none. The hillside grave, 31.7735/35.2275, is likewise marked notional.

**Left unresolved.** The execution site varies between workings: on the spot where the body lay, or in Jerusalem by the king's order. Mackey recorded the divergence and this record keeps it.

**The absence claim** [A, by comparative silence across 1 Kings, 2 Chronicles, and Josephus]. No ancient Near Eastern, biblical, or Josephan source records the craftsman's death.

## 4. The long interval, c. 947 BC to AD 1717

- **c. -947** [A: Josephus]. Hiram I of Tyre dies. The Qabr Hiram monument near Hanaouay, six kilometres southeast of Tyre, is popularly called his tomb.
  **Corrections applied.** The pool put it at 33.1587/35.2301, about nine kilometres off; Wikipedia 'Tomb of Hiram' gives 33.2261/35.2739. The pool dated it "Iron Age or Persian period"; Wikipedia dates it specifically to the Persian period, four to six centuries after any Solomonic Hiram, on parallels with the Tomb of Cyrus and the Gur-e-Dokhtar.
  **Contradiction inside the pool.** The afterlife lens asserted that no tomb of Hiram Abiff "exists or is claimed," while the geography lens documents Qabr Hiram, visited by Masonic pilgrims as the craftsman's grave. The afterlife claim survives only on the word "mainstream." The journey states the narrow true version: there is a tomb, it is the wrong man and the wrong century.
- **-587** [A: 2 Kings 25:13-17]. Nebuchadnezzar's men break up the pillars, the sea, and the bases and carry the brass to Babylon. Nothing he made survives.
- **AD 93** [A: Josephus, *Antiquities*]. Josephus preserves Tyrian court records through Menander of Ephesus and mentions no murdered master-builder. This is the load-bearing silence of the dossier.
- **c. 1119** [R]. The Templars headquarter on the Temple platform and take the name of Solomon's Temple. The claim that they carried the legend to Europe is popular, undocumented, and rejected by mainstream Masonic historiography.
- **1400s-1600s** [R]. Operative guilds hand down building lore. No primary guild document naming Hiram Abiff or narrating a murder was located. Stated as a gap.
- **1599** [A]. **Corrections applied.** The pool dated Lodge Mother Kilwinning's documentation to the seventeenth century; the Second Schaw Statutes of 1599 are sixteenth. The coordinate is corrected from 55.6597/-4.7128 to 55.6539/-4.6981. Wikipedia says of the antiquity claim that "there is no existing evidence to support this claim"; the lodge could not prove it when it petitioned in 1736.

## 5. The invention, 1717 to 1738

- **24 June 1717** [A]. Four lodges at the Goose and Gridiron form the Grand Lodge of England; Anthony Sayer elected Grand Master.
- **1723** [A]. Anderson's *Constitutions of the Free-Masons*, compiled to Desaguliers's order and approved by a Grand Lodge committee, fixes "Hiram Abif" as a proper name by resolving the Hebrew *abiv*, "his father," as a surname.
  **Correction applied, three times over.** The pool attributed three genuine Anderson passages to the 1738 second edition. All three are from the 1723 first edition, verified in Internet Archive scans. The 1738 text returns zero full-text hits for "Prince of Architects" and for "Grand Master of the Lodge at Jerusalem," having reworded both. The pool's "wise King Solomon" quotation also drops the clause "and the learned King Hiram was Grand Master of the Lodge at Tyre," and its "None since Hiram Abif" clips a subordinate "though" from the front. Only verified 1723 wording is used.
- **Early 1720s** [A for the appearance, R for authorship]. The murder legend enters Anglo-American ritual. Christopher Powell (2021) attributes it to John Theophilus Desaguliers. Paul Naudon traced its structure to Renaud de Montauban in the twelfth-century *Four Sons of Aymon*, killed by a hammer blow while working as a mason at Cologne, his body hidden and later found.
  **Correction applied.** The pool called Naudon's theory "a minority comparative theory." Wikipedia 'Hiram Abiff' calls it the leading theory supported by many scholars, and Powell draws on the same chanson de geste, so the two are complementary rather than rival. The pool also pinned Naudon at 46.2276/2.2137, the geographic centroid of France, a null anchor. Cologne is the meaningful place, and neither coordinate is used.
- **c. 1725** [R]. The third degree separates from the two craft degrees. Exact year disputed.
- **1726** [R]. The Graham Manuscript, Edinburgh, has Noah's sons raise their father's body by grip and word. Read by many as the legend's ancestor; the manuscript makes no such claim itself.
- **1730** [A]. Prichard's *Masonry Dissected*, the earliest substantial print witness: master workman killed, hidden under rubbish, found by fifteen brothers. No gates, no names for the killers. The apparatus grows after this, not before it.
- **1736** [A]. **Correction applied.** Ramsay's oration was delivered at Paris in 1736, not 1737; the 1737 item is the written Discourse sent to Cardinal Fleury on 20 March, whose delivery is unconfirmed. Ramsay connected Freemasonry to the Hospitallers, not the Templars; the Templar inference was his listeners'.
- **1738** [A]. The second *Constitutions*. **Correction applied.** The pool called this the first appearance of the name in an official Grand Lodge publication. False, and self-contradicting against its own 1723 entry: the 1723 edition was authorized too and already carried the name.

## 6. Transmission and afterlife, 1772 to 2013

- **1772** [A]. Preston's *Illustrations*: lecture system and moral apparatus, not ritual drama. See section 3, correction 4.
- **14 September 1797** [A]. Webb's *Freemason's Monitor*, Albany, New York, fixed by the copyright date. **Correction applied:** the pool hedged that "some bibliographies give a different American city." Wikipedia records no such dispute. The hedge manufactured a controversy the sources do not show, and is dropped.
- **1801** [A]. Supreme Council of the Scottish Rite, Southern Jurisdiction, Charleston. Day within the year not fixed by sources consulted here.
- **c. 1780** [A for the legend, R for its date]. Rosslyn Chapel's Apprentice Pillar story: a master mason kills his apprentice out of jealousy. Eighteenth-century, local, no Masonic origin. Robert L. D. Cooper dates the third degree to c. 1720 and attributes the Rosslyn-Hiram connection to speculative writing from the 1980s. Popular esoteric writers say otherwise; the disagreement is flagged.
- **1851** [A]. Nerval, *Voyage en Orient*: Adoniram, Balkis, Soliman, framed as a coffeehouse tale over two weeks, reportedly incorporating real Masonic passwords.
- **28 February 1862** [A]. Gounod's *La Reine de Saba*, libretto Barbier and Carre after Nerval, Salle Le Peletier.
- **c. 1866** [A]. Duncan's *Masonic Ritual and Monitor*, New York, Dick & Fitzgerald. **Correction applied:** the pool quoted the Grand Hailing Sign as "Is there no help for the widow's son?" Duncan reads "O Lord my God! is there no help for the widow's son?" The journey uses the full wording.
- **1871** [A]. Pike, *Morals and Dogma*: Hiram absorbed into solar and mystery-religion symbolism.
- **1873** [A]. Mackey's *Encyclopedia of Freemasonry*: Hiram as symbol of the initiate's death and rebirth, variant workings recorded rather than harmonized. **Corrections applied:** the pool dated it 1874 (Wikipedia gives 1873) and placed Mackey "working from Charleston," though he moved to Washington in 1870. The journey pins Washington, 38.9072/-77.0369.
- **1885** [A]. Gould's *History of Freemasonry* treats the narrative explicitly as an eighteenth-century literary construction. Cut from the journey for stop budget; recorded here.
- **1900** [R, downgraded from A]. **Correction applied.** The pool asserted a "statue titled 'Architect Hiram'" was "installed" at St John's Church, Chester. The Wikipedia caption reads only "Architect Hiram, St John's Church, Chester (1900)": no medium, no title, no installation. The church's own article inventories its period works and mentions no Hiram. Carried as a deliberately thin pin.
- **2013** [A]. Bronze statue of Hiram Abiff by Nickolaus-Otto Kruch, Berlin.

**Honest gaps**
1. No birth date, death date, grave, or end of any kind for the craftsman exists in any source.
2. The mother's tribe is contradicted between 1 Kings and 2 Chronicles and cannot be resolved.
3. Zarethan cannot be located with confidence, and Succoth only by convention.
4. The ruffians' cave and the well have no locatable sites and are given none.
5. Nothing he made survives; the bronze was broken up and carried to Babylon in 587.
6. The author of the Hiramic legend is unknown. Powell's Desaguliers attribution is a 2021 argument, not a document.
7. The Chester 1900 item's medium, position, and maker are all unknown.
