# Herbert Yardley (1889-1958): research report

*Written 2026-08-30 from the verified research pool supplied for slug `herbert_yardley`. Companion file: `herbert_yardley.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops, 8 quotations.*

Legend: **[A source]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim carried without a reachable citation. Where the pool's verifier notes contradict its own claims, the correction is followed and both readings recorded. Unsettled contradictions are left open.

## 1. Worthington, the telegrapher's son (1889-1912)

- Born Worthington, Indiana, 13 April 1889 **[A Wikipedia; NSA listing]**. Son of Robert Kirkbride Yardley, station agent and telegrapher **[R Kahn]**. **Coordinate corrected**: pool 39.1225/-86.9758 to **39.1208/-86.9811**.
- Mother's death c. 1903, church membership August 1905, 127 West Union Street (kept in `suggested_refs`, not in the campa), the 1906 Denver freight-riding: all **[R Kahn]**.
- **Open contradiction, not adjudicated.** The flag-prank expulsion **[R Kahn]** is contradicted by the source the pool cites for everything around it. Wikipedia verbatim: *"After graduation from high school in 1907, Yardley went to the University of Chicago, but dropped out after one year."* No flag, no expulsion, no Eaton Rapids. The file keeps the stop, states Kahn's version, prints the contradicting sentence in `date_confidence`, and chooses neither.
- Eaton Rapids **[R Kahn]**; coordinate corrected 42.5084/-84.6605 to **42.5092/-84.6533**. University of Chicago, one year, dropped out **[A Wikipedia]**: no place detail in the pool, so folded into that campa, not pinned at an invented campus point.
- Indiana railroad telegraphy including Indianapolis **[R Kahn]**; coordinate corrected from 39.7647/-86.1733, which is 1.2 km west of the station, to **39.7631/-86.1594**. Civil service examination and State Department clerkship, 1912 **[A Wikipedia]**.

## 2. The code room (1912-1917)

- Oath as code clerk 1912 **[A Wikipedia]**; the day (23 December) and the $900 salary **[R Kahn]**. Coordinate is the State, War and Navy Building.
- **The origin story is [A] but the pool described it wrongly.** The quote lens called the two-hour solution a foreign diplomatic cipher. Yardley's Chapter I says he copied a 500-word coded telegram from **Colonel Edward M. House to President Wilson** off the wire State shared with the White House and broke it in under two hours: an **American** code. The campa says so. The episode is **undated [R gap]**; 1915 and 1916 are ordering dates inside the clerkship.
- Marriage to Hazel Milam, 20 May 1914, at the Reverend James H. Taylor's Washington home, and the address at 1009 Seventh Street NE: **[R Kahn]**. The Taylor house is unlocated, so the point is a general Washington one.

## 4. The American Black Chamber (1919-1929)

- Peacetime Cipher Bureau, jointly funded, moved to New York 20 May 1919 under Yardley **[A Wikipedia; 'Black Chamber']**. **Cover-company name corrected**: the pool's "Code Compiling Company" is the **Code Compilation Company** in the article it cites. Mendelsohn's co-ownership **[R]**, kept out of the campa. Street address **[R gap]**, sometimes given as East 38th Street; 40.7503/-73.9822 is an acknowledged Midtown placeholder.
- Washington Naval Conference, 5:5:3, Hughes as Secretary of State **[A State Department Office of the Historian]**; decrypted Japanese instructions passed to American officials during the talks **[A]**. **Venue name inverted, coordinate corrected**: the building is **Memorial Continental Hall**, 1776 D Street NW, and en.wikipedia states it "was the site of the Washington Naval Conference in 1921-22", which lifts the pool's own caution. File uses **38.8939/-77.0400**; the pool had "Continental Memorial Hall" at 38.8951/-77.0412. **Legend flagged, not resolved**: Kahn has Japan accepting the ratio on 12 December 1921 with some celebrated decrypts arriving afterward, against the story of one decisive telegram **[R]**. The campa carries both.
- **DSM upgraded [R] to [A]**: valor.militarytimes.com record 18395 prints the citation to Major (Signal Corps) Herbert O. Yardley, WD General Orders No. 56 (1922), *"as Chief of the Communication Section of the Military Intelligence Division, War Department General Staff"*. The unit wording is **Communication Section**, not MI-8; the campa follows it.
- Tuberculosis and Arizona recuperation 1922 **[R]**, no town established. **The Tucson placeholder stop was dropped** rather than pin a city the pool never attests he visited; the fact is folded into the DSM campa, an add-or-leave call for the operator. Queens property through Cody Realty **[R Kahn]**, 1920s; 1926 orders it.
- **Closure date contradiction inside the pool**: chronology gave 31 October 1929 **[A Wikipedia]**, geography gave 1 November. File uses **31 October**. That 1929 compressed a longer Army reorganization begun before Stimson took office is **[R Kahn]**, in `date_confidence` only.
- **Stimson line upgraded [R] to [A] and re-attributed.** Primary source located: Stimson and Bundy, *On Active Service in Peace and War* (1948), **p. 188**, as reported speech, "as he later said"; corroborated in Kahn. The pool's negative finding also holds: **the phrase is nowhere in Yardley's own book**, zero hits across the full OCR text. Quoted as memoir, not as a documented 1929 utterance.

## 5. The book and the ruin (1929-1937)

- Return to Worthington **[R Kahn/Hannah]**; the Northwestern crime detection laboratory, Chicago, c. 1930 **[R]**, is folded into that campa, not pinned at a generic Chicago point.
- *The American Black Chamber*, Bobbs-Merrill, 1931, 375 pp. **[A en.wikipedia]**. **The pool's 1 June 1931 is unsupported**; downgraded to the year, with the default declared. Saturday Evening Post excerpts **[R]**. Sales 17,931 US, 5,480 UK, 33,119 Japanese **[A]**; the pool's translator names for the French and Swedish editions **[R]**. Friedman's damage assessment against Kahn's finding of no worldwide blackout: both **[R]**, both in the campa, neither adjudicated.
- **Roberts's review is [A] and was truncated in the pool**, cut at "of the war", which changes the scope of the superlative. Full sentence, from Kahn, in `suggested_refs` on the Bobbs-Merrill stop: *"I think it the most sensational contribution to the secret history of the war, as well as the immediate post-war period, which has yet been written by an American."* Kahn names no periodical, so none is invented.
- Seizure by US marshals, never published in his lifetime, declassified 1979 **[A Wikipedia]**. The 20 February date, the Macmillan venue and MacArthur's intervention **[R Kahn]**. **Title varies**: Wikipedia has *Japanese Diplomatic Codes: 1921-1922*, the pool *Japanese Diplomatic Secrets*. Flagged, not resolved.
- **Causation corrected.** The afterlife lens had the manuscript seized "under threat of the newly passed secrecy statute". That statute (ch. 57, 48 Stat. 122, ancestor of 18 U.S.C. 952) was enacted **10 June 1933** **[A Law Revision Counsel]**, four months after the seizure: it postdates the episode and was prompted by it. The pool contradicted itself between lenses; the file follows the two that had it right.
- Secret-ink accident and amputation, 21 May 1933, place unestablished; M. J. Nolder distillery, Colton, 1935-1937, 1936 orders it: both **[R Kahn]**.
- **Rendezvous (1935): the pool's claim is contradicted by the accessible record.** en.wikipedia gives MGM, US release 24 October 1935, Powell and Russell, adapted from the novel *The Blonde Countess* with credits also drawing on *The American Black Chamber*, and does **not** support the "technical adviser" role, which stays **[R]** and out of the campa. **Coordinate corrected** to the Culver City lot, **34.0172/-118.4017**, from the pool's 34.0247/-118.4055, about 900 m north in residential streets.

## 6. The long way east (1938-1940)

- China 1938-1940, assisting Nationalist cryptanalysis against Japanese codes **[A Wikipedia]**. The alias "Herbert Osborn", the Le Havre departure, the sea route, the 12 October 1938 Hong Kong arrival and Dai Li as patron via Major Hsiao Hsin-ju: **[R Kahn]**.
- **Transit stops reduced.** The pool offered nine transit points, all **[R]**, identically sourced. Four are staged (Le Havre, Port Said, Bombay, Hong Kong); Gibraltar, Marseille, Valletta, Aden, Colombo, Penang and Singapore are named inside those campas. A reversible density judgment. **Canonical pin inherited byte-identical**: Port Said **31.2653/32.3019**, as `abdelkader.journey.json` has it.
- Chungking: the decayed mansion, roughly fifteen trainees, nineteen claimed Japanese systems, all **[R]**; fragmentary Chinese record-keeping leaves the claim unverifiable either way, and the campa says exactly that.
- **The Chungking allegations.** Orgies, and a coerced arrangement supplying women to foreign advisers, appear in expatriate recollection and correspond to no investigation, charge or finding in any record reached. Stated flatly in the campa as uncorroborated.
- **Two books disentangled.** The pool's chronology conflated the seized manuscript with *The Chinese Black Chamber*. Wikipedia is explicit: the former declassified **1979**, never published; the latter declassified and published **1983**.

The same page confirms Coxeter and Robinson going to Washington in April 1941 and Mauborgne naming Yardley.
