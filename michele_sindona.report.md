# Michele Sindona (1920-1986): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-18 for `michele_sindona.journey.json`. Subject: Sicilian tax lawyer, banker, Vatican financial adviser, P2 member no. 0501; convicted of the fraudulent bankruptcy of the Banca Privata Italiana and of ordering the murder of the liquidator Giorgio Ambrosoli; died of potassium cyanide in Voghera prison.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-dossier claim not corroborated in this pass. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**, except where one side cites a source that says the opposite of what was attributed to it, in which case the misattribution is corrected and both readings stay on the record.

Evidence base: four reachable pillars, **Treccani's DBI**, **it.wikipedia** (much the fullest, and the only one with street addresses, the kidnap itinerary, and the burial plot), **en.wikipedia**, **Britannica**, plus one newspaper original through la Repubblica's archive. No archive or court docket was opened. Every coordinate was re-derived from OSM/Nominatim or an it.wikipedia geotag, and **nine pins inherited from earlier passes were wrong by 300 m to 6.5 km**, the largest single category of error found; they are tabulated at the end.

## 1. Patti and Messina, 1920-1944

- Born **8 May 1920 at Patti (Messina)**, eldest son of **Antonino Sindona** and **Maria Castelnuovo**, family of modest means **[A: Treccani, "Nacque l'8 maggio 1920 a Patti (Messina), figlio primogenito di Antonino e di Maria Castelnuovo"; corroborated by Britannica]**; the father a florist trading largely in funeral wreaths **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- Worked **from age fourteen**, so from 1934, as typist, assistant in accounts, and employee of the **Messina tax office**. **[A as to substance: it.wikipedia]** *Correction:* an earlier pass dated this "early 1940s", late by most of a decade, and glossed it as learning to read balance sheets "and evade them", which is editorial and is not carried as fact.
- **Law degree, University of Messina, 1942.** **[A: Britannica; Treccani; en.wikipedia]** No month or day found anywhere.
- **Black-market smuggling with AMGOT officers and Mafia contacts, 1943.** **[A: it.wikipedia, dating the fortune to just after the Allied landing]** *Correction:* an earlier pass dated this 1946, apparently back-formed from the Milan move, and dropped the Allied Military Government officers.
- **Marriage to Caterina Cilio, 4 September 1944**; three children, Maria Elisa, Nino, Marco. **[A: Britannica]** *Gap:* the place is given only as "Sicily", so the stop is pinned at Patti and says so. Nothing else about her was recoverable from any pillar.

## 2. Milan and the apparatus, 1946-1959

- **Milan, law office opened, summer 1946**, tax and financial adviser to industrialists. **[A: Treccani; Britannica]** No street address found.
- **Joe Adonis's attic on Via Albricci** inside Sindona's Milanese circle in the 1950s. **[A: it.wikipedia]** No source dates any crossing from proximity into partnership; recorded as proximity.
- **Fasco AG founded at Vaduz, c. 1950**, with the back-to-back financing technique. **[A: en.wikipedia; it.wikipedia]**
- **Meets Cardinal Giovanni Battista Montini, 1954**, introduced by **Mons. Amleto Tondini**. **[A: it.wikipedia]** *Correction:* an earlier pass had the Vatican relationship starting vaguely "in the 1960s", conflating two events fifteen years apart. The introduction and the IOR relationship (1968-69, en.wikipedia) are separate stops.
- **Chosen by the Gambino family, 1957**, to manage heroin profits. **[A: en.wikipedia]** *Resolved against the citing lens:* a parallel pass gave "early 1950s" while citing the same article, which says 1957.
- His own denial survives verbatim, to his biographer Tosches: *"Come sai le mie banche italiane erano istituti di prim'ordine con soci di prim'ordine... La mafia invece si serve sempre di istituti e professionisti di second'ordine."* **[A: it.wikipedia note 68]** A snobbery defence, not an alibi; carried as a quote rather than paraphrased.

## 3. The banks, 1960-1971

- **Banca Privata Finanziaria acquired: 1960 [A: Treccani, "Nel 1960 diventò un banchiere, poiché acquisì il controllo della Banca privata"] versus 1961 [A: it.wikipedia]. Unresolved**, neither giving a day. The file dates the stop 1960 for ordering and states the conflict in the campa itself; an earlier pass presented 1960 as settled.
- **Hambros Bank (London)** and **Continental Illinois (Chicago)** among his backers. **[A: it.wikipedia]**, which adds that Continental Illinois was headed by **David M. Kennedy** before he became Nixon's Treasury Secretary, a stronger detail than the earlier pass drew from that sentence. **Major Eurodollar deal, London, 1964. [A: en.wikipedia]**
- **IOR:** adviser to the Holy See from 1968, working relationship dated 1969; the IOR's seat in the **Torrione di Niccolò V** confirmed. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Michele Sindona" and "Institute for the Works of Religion"]**
- **Banca Unione bought from the Feltrinelli family, 1968**, with Vatican approval **[A: Treccani; Britannica]**; **Società Generale Immobiliare bought from the IOR, 1968 [A: Treccani; it.wikipedia]**.
- **Bastogi takeover attempted and failed, 1971.** **[A: Britannica]** Sindona's account of who blocked him is on record five years later: *"Ambrosoli è una creatura di Cuccia."* **[A: L'Espresso, 29 February 1976, via it.wikiquote]**

## 4. The pyramid at full height, 1971-1974

- **Cisalpine Overseas Bank, Nassau, founded 1971 with Roberto Calvi [A: it.wikipedia]; Paul Marcinkus on its board c. 1971-1982 [A: `paul_marcinkus.journey.json`]**, whose Nassau pin is inherited byte-identical.
- **Franklin National Bank of Mineola, controlling interest: 1972 [A: en.wikipedia] versus 1974 [A: Britannica]. Unresolved**, both being named encyclopedic sources; the file uses 1972 for ordering.
- **P2 membership number 0501. [A: en.wikipedia AND it.wikipedia, independently.]** *Tag corrected upward:* an earlier pass tagged this [R] because Wikipedia's *Propaganda Due* article omits it, but both *Sindona* articles carry it. **The join year is a separate question and is not attested.** The earlier pass's "1974" has no source and is strained: Gelli became Venerable Master only in 1975, and the roll surfaced only in the March 1981 raid. The file dates the stop 1975 purely to order the segment. The **place** is likewise unattested; Rome is used for want of any recorded location.

## 5. Il crack Sindona, 1974

- **The crash, April 1974** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; **Banca Privata Italiana into forced liquidation, 24 September 1974 [A: Treccani]**.
- **Ambrosoli appointed liquidator by the Bank of Italy, 27 September 1974 [A: Treccani; en.wikipedia], by Governor Guido Carli personally [A: it.wikipedia].** *Inference flagged:* siting the stop in Rome is an inference from "appointed by the Banca d'Italia"; no source says where, and Ambrosoli worked in Milan.
- **Franklin National declared insolvent, 8 October 1974**, largest US bank failure to that date. **[A: Treccani; en.wikipedia; Britannica]** Same day, Sindona **transferred $30 million to Europe [A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **IOR losses on Franklin estimated at 35 billion lire (£20 million). [A: en.wikipedia, "Institute for the Works of Religion", explicitly "estimated by one source".]** *Correction:* an earlier pass appended "$30 million" as a gloss; no source gives that figure for the IOR loss, which belongs to Sindona's own same-day transfer. Deleted.
- **Indicted in Italy for fraudulent bankruptcy 1974-75, a fugitive in New York. [A: Treccani]** *Correction:* an earlier pass also had a US indictment pending "by late 1974". The American indictment over Franklin was returned in **March 1979**. The file says he had not been indicted in the United States during these years.

## 6. Fugitive, and the murder of Ambrosoli, 1975-1979

- **Apartment at the Hotel Pierre [A: it.wikipedia]**, used in place of the earlier pass's generic lower-Manhattan "fugitive residence" pin.
- **Interview with Gianfranco Modolo, L'Espresso, 29 February 1976. [A: it.wikiquote, three passages]** *Place unresolved:* the quotes lens assigned them to Milan, but Sindona had been a fugitive in New York since 1974-75 and no source places him in Italy that February. The file pins the Pierre and records the discrepancy.
- **The $5.6 million commission** paid by Sindona to *"an American bishop and a Milanese banker"*, identified by Italian sources as **Marcinkus and Calvi**. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Giorgio Ambrosoli"]** *Date is a gap:* no source dates Ambrosoli's discovery, and the earlier pass's 1978 is its own. The file places the stop inside the 1974-79 liquidation and asserts no year.
- **The contract on Ambrosoli [R].** The recruitment of **William Aricò** through the trafficker **Robert Venetucci**, a former cellmate, for about **$115,000**, is dossier-only. Attested by contrast: Aricò's involvement and a threatening call invoking **Giulio Andreotti**'s name that Ambrosoli recorded **[A: en.wikipedia]**, and Aricò named in the trial narrative **[A: Montanelli and Cervi]**.
- **Ambrosoli shot dead, evening of 11 July 1979, at via Morozzo della Rocca 1, Milan**, hours after meeting US Justice Department investigators; four shots from a .357 Magnum. **[A: en. and it.wikipedia, "Giorgio Ambrosoli", the address being it.wikipedia's; the shots, Montanelli and Cervi]** *Correction:* an earlier pass declared the address unconfirmable and pinned the murder 1.2 km away.
- Ambrosoli's letter to his wife Anna, **25 February 1975**, is the strongest document in this file. **[A: it.wikiquote]** *Misquotation corrected:* an earlier pass dropped *di buono* and swapped a preposition. Correct: *"Pagherò a molto caro prezzo l'incarico: lo sapevo prima di accettarlo e quindi non mi lamento affatto perché per me è stata un'occasione unica **per** fare qualcosa **di buono** per il paese [...]"*, and the citation is **Stajano, *Un eroe borghese*, Einaudi, pp. 102-3, 149**, not the Robiglio volume.

## 7. The kidnapping that was not, August to October 1979

Route, all **[A: it.wikipedia]**: **Vienna, Athens, Brindisi, Caltanissetta, Palermo (17 August 1979), the villa of Rosario Spatola at Torretta**, with a self-arranged gunshot wound to the leg inflicted by the surgeon **Joseph Miceli Crimi**. The file carries Vienna, Caltanissetta, Palermo and Torretta; **Athens and Brindisi are omitted for length only, not for doubt**, and the Caltanissetta stop says so. it.wikipedia frames Spatola within the **Gambino / Bontate / Inzerillo** organising group, so "Mafia boss" as a flat gloss is fair but loose and is not used as such.

- **Miceli Crimi sent twice to Arezzo. [A: it.wikipedia, "mandò Miceli Crimi almeno due volte ad Arezzo per convincere Licio Gelli"]** *Correction:* an earlier pass gave the purpose as "coordinating P2's part in the hoax". The source says it was pressing Gelli over the rescue of the bank, and that Sindona *sent* the surgeon rather than that the surgeon travelled.
- **Vincenzo Spatola arrested 9 October 1979** delivering the fabricated captivity letter. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **Reappearance: 16 October 1979, a Manhattan telephone booth. [A: it.wikipedia]** *Two corrections.* An earlier pass gave 20 October, which no source supports, Treccani saying only "in ottobre"; and it placed the booth at **Times Square**, which no fetched source does. The file uses 16 October and a generic Manhattan pin, and says the booth's location is unknown.

## 8. The courts, 1980-1986

- **Conviction on 65 counts, Manhattan, 1980; 25 years and a $207,000 fine. [A: en.wikipedia; it.wikipedia]** The month of **March** is dossier-only. **Two suicide attempts by wrist-cutting during the proceedings [R: it.wikipedia and dossier].** The federal facility could not be identified in any fetched source, so no custody stop is staged: an honest gap in the itinerary between 1980 and 1984.
- **April 1981 bail-jumping and perjury conviction [R].** Dossier-only: en.wikipedia's full article contains **no sentence dated 1981**, and it.wikipedia records only the 1980 American conviction and the 1985-86 Italian ones. Perjury already sits among the 65 counts of 1980, so this may duplicate the same case; the "five-hour deliberation" is unsourced. *Tag conflict:* one lens tagged the event [A] and another [R]. The lower tag is used, the stop is kept rather than deleted, and its campa says outright that the record does not support it.
- **Extradited and imprisoned at Voghera, 25 September 1984. [A: en. and it.wikipedia for the day; Treccani gives only "September 1984"]**
- **Fraudulent bankruptcy conviction, Milan: 16 March 1985, twelve years [A: en. AND it.wikipedia] versus 1985, fifteen years [A: Treccani, "una condanna a 15 anni per il dissesto della Privata"]. Unresolved, but weighted:** two sources to one for twelve, and only the sources giving twelve give a day. An earlier pass attributed "fifteen years" to it.wikipedia, which says twelve.
- **Life sentence for ordering Ambrosoli's murder, 18 March 1986. [A: Treccani; en. and it.wikipedia]** The Montanelli and Cervi passage quoted in the file dates the murder to 12 July 1979 against the encyclopedic 11 July; the quote is verbatim and the discrepancy is left standing inside it.

## 9. The coffee, and after

- **Potassium cyanide in his prison coffee, morning of 20 March 1986. [A: it.wikipedia]** *Internal contradiction corrected:* two earlier passes dated the poisoning **18 March**, the day of the verdict, while their own prose said "two days after" sentencing. Both cannot be true.
- **"Mi hanno avvelenato!"**, shouted to the officers after he came out of the bathroom where he had sugared the coffee. The words are real; the earlier pass's **date (18 March) and citation (Simoni and Turone) are both wrong**. Traced instead to **la Repubblica, "Ecco gli ultimi istanti di Sindona", 4 November 1986**, reporting that he said it **twice**, at 8:30 on 20 March. **[A]**
- **Died 22 March 1986, aged 65. [A: Treccani; en. and it.wikipedia]** *Two flags.* One lens put the death in the hospital *of the prison*, another in the **Ospedale Civile di Voghera**; it.wikipedia supports the civil hospital and the file follows it while recording the conflict. And **"53 hours on life support" appears in no fetched source**: it.wikipedia says a two-day coma, and so does the file.
- **The suicide framing.** Treccani is more assertive than the earlier pass allowed: *"si suicidò con il cianuro... simulando un omicidio"*, a suicide staged to look like a murder. The softer framing about persistent suspicion is the pipeline's own. The file gives Treccani's wording and leaves the genuinely open question open: how cyanide got inside a maximum-security prison is unanswered in every source consulted.
- **Buried in the Cimitero Monumentale di Milano, tomb 430, Circondario di Levante. [A: it.wikipedia]** No burial date found.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Michele Sindona is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`**, and no apparatus relation was found that would require adding him: he had no theoretical framework, and nothing in the pool applies anyone's framework backward to him or his to anyone.

One relation not rooted in his own presence *was* caught, and the direction is **forward**: **Calvi hanged under Blackfriars Bridge, 18 June 1982**, having fled **Rome** via Venice as Gian Roberto Calvini **[A: en.wikipedia; an earlier pass had him fleeing Milan, which is wrong]**, and **Marcinkus named in a 1987 Italian warrant over Banco Ambrosiano [A: en.wikipedia]**. Both postdate Sindona's presence: American custody in 1982, dead in 1987. **No stop is staged for either.** They survive only as a clause inside campas at places where Sindona himself acted (Nassau; the Vatican losses) and as `suggested_refs` marked "his own stop, not this traveler's". If the operator judges even that too far forward, the clauses come out without touching a stop.
