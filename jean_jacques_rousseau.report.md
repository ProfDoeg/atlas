# Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1712-1778): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-08. Approved on QUEUE.md as "the Genevan Enlightenment philosopher of education and society" **[A - QUEUE.md]**.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in place; **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim circulating without a locatable primary. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Short forms: enwiki, frwiki, SEP, Conf. (*Confessions*, book in roman).

**Method and its limits.** Two passes: gathering (enwiki, frwiki, SEP, Project Gutenberg full texts, fr.wikisource search) and adversarial verification, which rechecked coordinates, dates, and quotation wording. No manuscript archive, no Leigh *Correspondance complète*, no Pléiade apparatus. Biographies named below are cited **at second hand**, as the pool cited them.

**The structural problem of this subject.** Almost every intimate episode reaches us through the *Confessions*, written 1765-1770, decades after the events narrated, by a man who by then believed a conspiracy was arranging his ruin. Where an episode exists only there, read **[A - Conf. N]** as "attested in his own account", not "corroborated".

## 1. Geneva, 1712-1728

- Born 28 June 1712 to Isaac Rousseau, watchmaker, and Suzanne Bernard, on the Grand-Rue, 46.201 / 6.1466, house now a museum **[A - enwiki; Conf. I]**.
- Baptized 4 July 1712 at Saint-Pierre, 46.2013 / 6.1487 **[A - Geneva parish register, second hand through Cranston and Damrosch]**.
- The mother dies of puerperal fever within days **[A - enwiki; Conf. I]**.
- 1717: Isaac sells the house, moves to a craftsmen's quarter **[A - enwiki]**. No coordinate found: a gap.
- 1722: Isaac flees after a legal quarrel; the boy is boarded with the pastor Lambercier at Bossey with his cousin Abraham Bernard **[A - enwiki; SEP; Conf. I]**. The broken-comb punishment belongs here **[A - Conf. I only]**.
- 1725-1728: apprenticed to a notary, then to the engraver Abel Ducommun **[A - enwiki; Conf. I]**.
- 14 March 1728: finds the gates shut at curfew a third time and leaves **[A - enwiki; Conf. II]**.

**C1, age at flight (open).** Chronology lens and enwiki: **fifteen**. Geography lens: **sixteen**. Born 28 June 1712, he was fifteen; the journey file says so and carries the dispute in `date_confidence`.

**C2, Bossey coordinates (open).** 46.1667 / 6.15 (chronology), 46.1706 / 6.2394 (geography, six kilometres east on the Salève slope), 46.1525 / 6.16 (enwiki "Bossey", which also confirms residence 1722-1724, used).

## 2. Savoy, Turin, Madame de Warens, 1728-1742

- March 1728: sheltered at Confignon by the priest Benoît de Pontverre, sent to Annecy with a letter **[A - Conf. II]**.
- 21 March 1728, Palm Sunday: meets Françoise-Louise de Warens **[A - Conf. II; Annecy plaque, Cour Saint-Maurice]**.
- April 1728: Hospice des Catéchumènes, Turin, 45.0682 / 7.6864; abjures Protestantism, loses his citizenship **[A - SEP; Conf. II]**.
- 1728-1729: footman to Madame de Vercellis, present at her death; in a second post steals a ribbon and names the servant girl Marion, who is dismissed **[A - Conf. II only]**.
- June 1729: walks back over the mountains to Annecy **[A - Conf. III]**.
- 1731: to Chambéry with her household; about a year as a clerk in the royal land survey **[A - SEP; Conf. IV-V]**.
- c. 1732: becomes her lover, on her initiative **[R - enwiki, ultimately Conf. V; no external corroboration exists or could]**.
- Les Charmettes, later the Musée Jean-Jacques Rousseau **[A - Conf. VI]**.
- 1740: tutor at Lyon to the children of Jean Bonnot de Mably **[A - SEP; Conf. V]**; earlier Lyon lodgings 1730-1731 **[A - Conf. III-V]**.

**C3, the seminary (explained, not resolved).** The chronology lens puts both the domestic service and the training toward the priesthood in **Turin, 1728**. Conf. III and the SEP put the seminary at **Annecy, 1729**, after his return walk. The pool's verification calls this a conflation of two places; the journey file separates them.

**C4, Les Charmettes dates (open).** **1735-1736** (enwiki), **1735-1738** (geography), **1736-1742** (frwiki: summers 1736 and 1737, winter 1738-39, first half of 1742). Nothing reachable reconciles them; the stop is dated 1736-01-01 and records all three.

**C5, Les Charmettes coordinates (open).** 45.5646 / 5.9178 (chronology, which is Chambéry town centre), 45.5581 / 5.9207 (geography), 45.5533 / 5.9297 (surveyed, used).

## 3. Paris and Venice, 1742-1749

- 1742: arrives in Paris with a system of musical notation by numbers; the Académie des Sciences receives it and does not adopt it **[A - enwiki; Conf. VII]**. Befriends Diderot the same year **[A - enwiki]**.
- 1743-1744: secretary to the Comte de Montaigu at Venice; dismissed unpaid after a quarrel **[A - SEP; Conf. VII]**. **Gap:** the embassy building was not identified; central-Venice coordinates (45.4342 / 12.34) are used and flagged.
- 1745: meets Thérèse Levasseur **[A - enwiki; Conf. VIII]**. From 1746 their children, five by the standard count, are left at the Enfants-Trouvés **[A - same]**. **Gap:** number and dates rest on his own retrospective statement and on hostile polemic; the institution recorded no names by design.
- 1749: begins contributing articles on music to the *Encyclopédie* **[A - enwiki]**.

## 4. The illumination and the fame, 1749-1758

- October 1749: on the road to Vincennes to visit the imprisoned Diderot, reads the Dijon prize question and experiences the illumination **[A - Conf. VIII; letter to Malesherbes, 1762]**.
- First prize for the *Discourse on the Sciences and the Arts* **[A - enwiki; SEP]**.
- October 1752: *Le Devin du village* before Louis XV at Fontainebleau; pension and presentation both declined **[A - enwiki; Conf. VIII]**.
- August 1754: at Geneva, abjures Catholicism, is readmitted to church and citizenship, and signs himself *citoyen de Genève* thereafter **[A - enwiki; SEP; Conf. VIII]**.
- 1755: *Discourse on the Origin and Foundations of Inequality among Men* **[A - enwiki]**.
- 1756: installed by Madame d'Épinay at L'Ermitage, Montmorency; begins *Julie* **[A - Conf. IX-X]**.
- 1758: *Letter to d'Alembert on the Theater* **[A - enwiki]**. No stop.

**C6, the Dijon prize (open).** The chronology lens tags the award to **Paris, 1750**. It was made by the **Académie de Dijon** (47.322 / 5.041), and the *Discourse* was published in 1751; SEP dates prize and publication to 1751, enwiki dates the prize to 1750. The journey file pins Dijon, dates 1750-01-01, records both.

**C7, Sophie d'Houdetot (open).** Chronology lens: **1756**, with the break from the Encyclopédistes. Enwiki ("Sophie d'Houdetot"): meeting and declaration **1757**, on 24 May, affair over after July, break following 1757-58. The journey file uses the Ermitage coordinates (48.988 / 2.313), not the chronology lens's "near Montmorency" (49.0033 / 2.3247, several kilometres off the estate).

## 5. Mont-Louis and the condemnation, 1758-1762

- 1758-1762: at Mont-Louis, Montmorency, in a house lent by the Duc de Luxembourg, he completes *Julie*, *Émile*, and *Du contrat social*; the house survives as a museum **[A - Conf. X-XI]**.
- 1761: *Julie, ou la Nouvelle Héloïse*, an immediate commercial success **[A - enwiki]**.
- April 1762: *Du contrat social* printed at Amsterdam by Marc-Michel Rey; distribution banned in France, burned at Geneva, placed on the Index; Voltaire mocks the censorship in *Idées républicaines*, 1765 **[A - enwiki, "The Social Contract"]**.
- May 1762: *Émile* published in France and Geneva; burned that year in both cities over the "Profession of Faith of the Savoyard Vicar"; also on the Index; first English translation 1763 **[A - enwiki, "Émile, or On Education"]**.
- 9 June 1762: the Parlement of Paris orders *Émile* burned and issues the arrest warrant; warned by the Prince de Conti, Rousseau leaves Montmorency that morning and reaches Yverdon on 14 June **[A - standard biography at second hand; Conf. XI]**.
- 1794: the Jesuit Alfonso Muzzarelli publishes a refutation of the *Contrat social*, the polemic continuing decades after the burning **[A - enwiki]**.

**C8, the flight (open in the pool).** Chronology lens: **July 1762**. Geography lens and the documented 9 June decree: **June 1762**, Yverdon on the 14th, used.

## 6. The Swiss exile, 1762-1765

- July 1762: Yverdon, with his friend Roguin, in Bernese territory; ordered out within weeks **[A - Conf. XII]**.
- 1762-1765: Môtiers, in the Prussian principality of Neuchâtel, under George Keith, Lord Marischal, governor for Frederick the Great; adopts Armenian dress **[A - enwiki; Conf. XII]**.
- May 1763: renounces his Genevan citizenship **[A - SEP]**.
- December 1764: James Boswell visits at Môtiers **[A - enwiki]**. No stop.
- September 1765: parishioners stone the house, allegedly incited by the pastor Montmollin **[A - enwiki; Conf. XII]**.
- 10 September 1765: to the Île de Saint-Pierre in the Lake of Biel **[A - enwiki; Conf. XII; Rêveries, Fifth Walk]**.
- 17 October 1765: the Senate of Bern orders him off its territory; he leaves the island 29 October **[A - enwiki]**.
- Late October to 9 December 1765: Strasbourg, weighing Corsica, Prussia, England **[A - 1765 correspondence, second hand through Cranston, *The Solitary Self*]**.
- December 1765: Paris, lodged in the Temple under Conti's jurisdiction **[A - Conf. XII]**.

**C9, Môtiers coordinates (open).** 46.9333 / 6.7333 (chronology, nine kilometres east of the Val-de-Travers), 46.9319 / 6.6231 (geography, used as closest to the village centre), 46.917 / 6.617 (verification pass).

**C10, Île de Saint-Pierre (open).** 47.0964 / 7.0967 (chronology) and 47.085 / 7.1667 (geography) both fall in open water off the island; surveyed 47.0708 / 7.1425, used. Duration: six weeks (geography), about seven weeks by the dates, two months (enwiki).

**C11, Strasbourg (open).** The geography lens gives October to November 1765; its own verification note gives departure 9 December. The stop is dated 1765-11-02 with the December departure in prose.

## 7. England, 1766-1767

- 4 January 1766: leaves Paris with David Hume; 13 January, arrives in London **[A - enwiki; Hume-Rousseau correspondence, second hand]**.
- February 1766: briefly at Chiswick **[A - Hume's 1766 letters, second hand through Cranston]**. No stop.
- 22 March 1766 to May 1767: Wootton Hall, Staffordshire, lent by Richard Davenport; begins the *Confessions*; the quarrel with Hume becomes public and is printed **[A - enwiki; letters 1766-67]**.
- Embarks from Dover for Calais **[A - enwiki]**.

**C12, Dover (open).** Geography lens **21 May 1767**; chronology lens and enwiki **22 May 1767** ("On 22 May 1767, Rousseau and Thérèse embarked from Dover for Calais"), used.

**C13, Wootton coordinates (open).** 53.0333 / -1.8667 (chronology), 53.0167 / -1.8667 (interlock), 52.9932 / -1.8394 (geography, used). The interlock value is **not** a canonical corpus pin: that lens records the English exile as absent from the existing corpus, so there was nothing to inherit.

## 8. Renou: France under a false name, 1767-1770

- June 1767 to June 1768: on the Prince de Conti's estate at Trie-Château, under the name Renou **[A - frwiki "Trie-Château"; correspondence]**. **C14 (open):** geography lens 49.3128 / 1.8306, three kilometres north of the village; surveyed 49.285 / 1.8222, used.
- 30 August 1768: marries Thérèse at Bourgoin under the alias, without legal or religious standing **[A - enwiki; Bourgoin municipal record, second hand through Cranston]**.
- January 1769 to 10 April 1770: the farmhouse of Monquin near Bourgoin **[A - enwiki, which records both dates; correspondence 1768-70]**.

**C15, Monquin tag.** The geography lens tagged it **[R]**, the exact site being uncertain in secondary sources. The verification pass argues **[A]**, the residence being well attested even where the building is not fixed. This report follows the verification pass: residence [A], exact site a gap, coordinates 45.61 / 5.32 approximate.

## 9. Last Paris years and afterlife, 1770-1794

- 24 June 1770: returns to Paris, settles on the Rue Plâtrière, later renamed Rue Jean-Jacques-Rousseau; copies music by the page; finishes the *Confessions* and reads it aloud in private houses; begins the *Rêveries* **[A - enwiki; Conf. XII; Rêveries]**.
- 1772: *Considerations on the Government of Poland* **[A - enwiki]**. No stop.
- 24 October 1776: knocked down in a Paris street by a Great Dane running ahead of a carriage **[A - enwiki]**.
- 20 May 1778: moves to a cottage on Girardin's estate at Ermenonville **[A - enwiki]**.
- 2 July 1778: dies of cerebral haemorrhage, aged sixty-six, the morning after a concert and a hearty meal **[A - enwiki]**. Suicide rumours circulate; all who saw him in his last days describe a serene state of mind **[R - enwiki, reporting rumour and refutation both]**.
- 4 July 1778: buried on the Île des Peupliers; a pilgrimage site at once, drawing Marie Antoinette and, later, Napoleon **[A - Girardin's 1778 account; visitor accounts, second hand]**.
- 11 October 1794: the Convention has the remains exhumed and transferred to the Panthéon, near Voltaire's **[A - enwiki; frwiki]**. December 1821: both tombs are moved out of public view, remaining in the building **[A - frwiki "Panthéon (Paris)"]**. The Ermenonville mausoleum stands empty, a cenotaph **[A - frwiki]**.
- Île Rousseau, Geneva: the bronze statue was commissioned from **James Pradier in 1830, inaugurated 1835** **[A - frwiki "Île Rousseau"]**. The gathering pass had declared sculptor and date an unclosable gap; the French page closes it. Recorded because the gap claim was wrong.

**C16, Ermenonville coordinates (open).** 49.1667 / 2.6833 (chronology), 49.15 / 2.6733 (geography), 49.1451 / 2.6772 (afterlife), all two to four kilometres from the village and its park; 49.1267 / 2.6967 (frwiki, surveyed, used).

**C17, Île des Peupliers coordinates (open).** Geography lens 49.1494 / 2.6742, some 3.5 kilometres outside the park; afterlife lens 49.121111 / 2.690833, matching frwiki and used. Two lenses of the same pool contradict each other.

**C18, the empty tomb, 1793 or 1794 (open).** Frwiki's "Île des Peupliers" says "Depuis 1793, sa tombe est vide". Every other source in the pool dates exhumation and transfer to 11 October 1794. Inherited defect, recorded not corrected.

**C19, the posthumous *Confessions*.** The afterlife lens tagged this **[R]**, an inference from a general publishing pattern. It is documented: Part I written 1765-1767, published 1782; Part II written 1769-1770, published 1789 **[A - enwiki]**. Its phrase "written in his last years" is loose: the writing ended eight years before he died.

## 10. Quotations: what survives verification

**Verified verbatim against a fetched full text [A]:**

- "Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains." *Du contrat social* I.1 (Cole translation, Gutenberg 46333).
- "I mean to inquire if, in the civil order, there can be any sure and legitimate rule of administration..." Same translation, wording exact throughout. **Citation corrected:** the unnumbered **opening paragraph of Book I**, preceding Chapter I, not Chapter I itself.
- "I have entered upon a performance which is without example..." Conf. I opening, verified in full (Gutenberg 3913).
- "God makes all things good; man meddles with them and they become evil." *Émile* I opening line (Gutenberg 5427, Foxley).
- "The first man, who, after enclosing a piece of ground, took it into his head to say, 'This is mine'..." *Discourse on the Origin of Inequality*, Part Two, opening, verified in full (Gutenberg 11136).

**Verified but misattributed in the gathering pass [A, with the correction]:**

- "It is too difficult to think nobly when we think for a livelihood." Conf. **IX** (Gutenberg 3913): "Il est trop difficile de penser noblement quand on ne pense que pour vivre." The circulating variant "when one thinks only of earning a living" is a loose retranslation, not the text.
- "Virtue is a state of war, and to live in it means one always has some battle to wage against oneself." **Not correspondence.** *Julie, ou la Nouvelle Héloïse* (1761), Part VI, Letter VII, verified in French via fr.wikisource (Page:Oeuvres completes de Jean-Jacques Rousseau - II.djvu/346): "ne savez-vous pas que la vertu est un etat de guerre, et que, pour y vivre, on a toujours quelque combat a rendre contre soi?" It is **spoken by a character in a novel**, not by Rousseau in his own voice; the journey file says so in the quote_source.

**Unverified, circulating attributions only [R], used nowhere in the journey file:** "To live is not to breathe" and "Nature never deceives us; we deceive ourselves", given to *Émile*; "The money that we possess is the instrument of liberty", given to the *Confessions*; "I love liberty, and I loathe constraint", given to the letters.

## 11. Interlocks with the existing corpus

Named in campa where the crossing is documented, all **[A]**:

- **kant**: *Émile* is the acknowledged jolt that reoriented Kant's ethical thought, and in one telling his fixed afternoon walk was missed only for its arrival. Named at the *Émile* stop; the Königsberg pin 54.7104 / 20.4522 already exists and is not duplicated.
- **pestalozzi**: his son, born 13 August 1770 at Neuhof near Birr while Rousseau lives, is named Jean-Jacques for him. Named at the Rue Plâtrière stop, dated 1770.
- **bolivar**: absorbs the *Contrat social* in the Caracas house of his tutor Simón Rodríguez, 1795. Named at the Amsterdam stop, with **juana_azurduy**, who courts Manuel Padilla over borrowed Rousseau in colonial Charcas.
- **braille**: interred in the Panthéon in 1952 among Voltaire, Rousseau, and Hugo. Named at the Panthéon stop; that pin is inherited **byte-identical**, 48.8462 / 2.3464.
- **napoleon**: a recorded visitor to the Île des Peupliers tomb before the remains are moved. Named at the burial stop.

Not named: **mikhail_bakunin** (his father teaches Rousseau at Pryamukhino) and **mao_zedong** (Rousseau read at the Hunan First Normal School) are genuine [A] crossings, but as readers only. **jaques_dalcroze** shares only the city of Geneva, which the interlock lens itself tags **[R]**. Kaaba, Temple Mount, and Buenos Aires returned no hits across all `*.journey.json` **[A - null result]**; not force-fitted.

## 12. Honest gaps

1. **No archival access.** Every "record" citation is a citation of a biography citing a record, so anything resting only on "standard biography" is weaker than its tag suggests.
2. **The five children.** Number, dates, and sexes are not independently documented and by the nature of the Enfants-Trouvés cannot be.
3. **Unlocated buildings.** The Venice embassy; the Monquin farmhouse; Isaac Rousseau's second Geneva house of 1717.
4. **Coordinates.** Six of the pool's forty-odd were wrong by two to nine kilometres, across both lenses, and twice the two lenses disagreed about the same place. Any coordinate not flagged above is "best of the reachable values", not surveyed.
5. **The suicide rumour.** [R] on both sides: the rumour attested as a rumour, the serenity of his last days as the report of witnesses with an interest in the answer.
