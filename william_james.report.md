# William James (1842-1910): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-28 for `william_james.journey.json`. 45 stops in 9 segments; gregorian; register "national mythology: the canon is true".*

Legend: **[A]** attested, fetched source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-only claim carried without independent confirmation. Contradictions are printed and left standing.

One structural fact governs everything below. **The popular chronology of James's life is welded together wrong in several places**, consistently across the derivative sources: three items in the pool fused two events into one year, and eight coordinates were wrong, two of them putting his grave in Harvard Yard.

---

## 1. Childhood and the itinerant household, 1842-1861

- Born **11 January 1842 at the Astor House**, Broadway at Vesey Street; brother **Henry** in the same city in 1843; the children tutored and moved rather than schooled. **[A: Wikipedia, "William James" and "Astor House"; Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy]** The usual coordinate 40.7128/-74.006 is the New York centroid, 1.1 km off. File uses **40.7118/-74.0086**.
- **May 1844, Windsor**: Henry James Sr.'s vastation, the terror at table that turned him to Swedenborg and made Swedenborgian theology the household's air. **[A: Wikipedia, "Henry James Sr.", "In May 1844, while living in Windsor, in England"]** *Correction:* the pool carried this as "London (Piccadilly / West End lodgings)" at 51.5074/-0.1419, tagged [A]. The lodgings are unsupported invention; the stop moves to Windsor, **51.4839/-0.6044**.
- **European schooling 1855-58**: Geneva, Paris, Boulogne. **[A: SEP timeline, which gives the span without allotting months to cities]** Boulogne is pinned to No. 20 Rue Neuve Chaussée, summer 1857 **[A: Wikipedia, "Henry James"]**. *Correction:* the pool gave 1857-1859; the family had crossed to Newport by 1858.
- **First fusion unpicked: the two Newports.** The pool welded "family settled in Newport 1858" to "studied painting under William Morris Hunt" as one 1858 item. They are separate residences: Newport **1858** **[A: SEP]**; **Geneva, at the Academy, 1859-60** **[A: SEP]**; a summer at **Bonn, 1860** **[A on place, R on year: Wikipedia, "Henry James", puts Bonn in the 1855-1860 sweep and dates the return to Newport to 1860; the pool's 1859 belongs to Geneva]**; then **Newport again from 1860**, where the **Hunt apprenticeship falls, 1860-61**. The file gives the second Newport a stop and the first none, since they share a coordinate; the split is stated in that stop's `date_confidence`. The year ends with James choosing science **[A: Britannica]**, on explanations the campa names and does not adjudicate.

**Gap.** The pool's "Washington Place" stop for the infant's first months is **[R]**, sourced to Perry (1935) with the address unverified. Dropped rather than staged: where the family lived in Manhattan between the birth and the first crossing is not established in anything reachable.

## 2. Science, medicine, Brazil, 1861-1866

- **Lawrence Scientific School 1861**, chemistry then comparative anatomy. **[A: Britannica]**
- **Museum of Comparative Zoology under Louis Agassiz**, c. 1861-65 **[A: Wikipedia; dossier]**. Agassiz's anti-Darwinism and polygenism are attested; James's inner reckoning with them is interpretation and is written as such.
- **Harvard Medical School from 1864**, North Grove Street **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool carried 42.3626/-71.0656 twice; Nominatim gives **42.3617/-71.0687**. Both stops use the corrected pair.
- **Spring 1865, the Thayer Expedition to Brazil.** James joins as collector and withdraws after about eight months with severe seasickness and a mild smallpox. **[A: Britannica; corroborated by Encyclopedia.com]** Rio, the plantations and Manaus are **[R: revista.drclas.harvard.edu plus dossier, months not pinned]**, and the Tefé stretch specifically is **[R]**: the ascent is documented, that reach is dossier-only, and the stop says so.

## 3. Germany, the M.D., the crisis, 1867-1872

- **1867-68 Germany**: Dresden, Bad Teplitz, Berlin; Helmholtz, Wundt, Fechner; physiology at Berlin. **[A: Britannica]** The order of the three cities is not fixed by any fetched source; the file folds Teplitz into the Berlin stop. **M.D. June 1869**, never practised **[A: Britannica]**.
- **The crisis, 1869 into the early 1870s.** Depression, insomnia, bodily fear; the terror before the remembered epileptic patient printed later in *Varieties* as a "French correspondent". **[A: SEP, sourcing the narrative to *Varieties*]**
- **30 April 1870, the Renouvier entry**, verbatim confirmed **[A: *Letters* I, Gutenberg 40307]**, quoted by the editor inside his narrative rather than reproduced as facsimile. *Myth flagged, not repeated:* the instant-cure reading is a later simplification, symptoms continued for years **[A: SEP]**, and the entry's own text is provisional and self-limited to one year.
- **The Quincy Street address** is inferred from standard biography and could not be reconfirmed. Marked **[R]** in `date_confidence`.

## 4. Harvard, the club, the textbook, 1872-1890

- **Instructorship in physiology 1872/73 under president Eliot; full professorship 1885; chair in psychology 1889; *Principles* 1890.** **[A: Britannica]**
- **The Metaphysical Club**, with Peirce, Chauncey Wright, Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. **[A: Wikipedia]** *Correction:* the pool dated it "early 1870s"; it ran **one year only, January to December 1872**, and the same source records that **no club of this name appears in any member's writings except Peirce's, written decades later** **[A: Wikipedia, "Metaphysical Club"]**. The pool's hedge that the pragmatism-birthplace framing is "partly retrospective" is therefore the state of the evidence, and the campa says so.
- **1875-76, the physiology-and-psychology course with a demonstration space** **[A: New England Historical Society]**. *Contradiction left standing:* whether this is the first American psychology laboratory is contested, Hall's later Johns Hopkins laboratory holding the rival claim.
- **Marriage to Alice Howe Gibbens 1878** **[A on the year: Encyclopedia.com]**; the **day, 10 July, is [R]**, dossier-only, kept because the schema needs an ISO date and flagged in the stop. The **Holt contract** of the same year was delivered in 1890. **Assistant professorship: contradiction, unresolved**, Britannica giving 1881 and the dossier 1880; no stop is dated to either.
- **1882, nitrous oxide**, after Blood's 1874 tract: "a tremendously exciting sense of intense metaphysical illumination". **[A: Wikipedia]**
- The **"loathsome, distended, tumefied, bloated, dropsical mass"** verdict on his own textbook is **[R]**: from Richardson (2006) via the dossier, the letter not re-fetched. It appears unquoted in the campa, with the weakness recorded in `date_confidence`.

## 5. Psychical research, 1884-1910

The pool's worst tangle.

- **The American Society for Psychical Research was founded in December 1884, not 1885**, first meeting "in the rooms of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences" in Boston. Its first **president was Simon Newcomb**; **James was a founder and one of the initial vice-presidents**, with G. Stanley Hall, Fullerton, Pickering and Bowditch. **[A: Wikipedia, "American Society for Psychical Research"]**
- **The London SPR presidency is 1894-95**, between Arthur Balfour and William Crookes in the Society's own succession. **[A: Wikipedia, "Society for Psychical Research"]** *Contradiction resolved, losing source named:* Wikipedia's "William James" article calls him a founding member and president of the British SPR in 1884-85, and is wrong twice. He did not found the London society (Sidgwick, Myers and Barrett did, in 1882), and 1884-85 conflates that presidency with the founding of the **American** society, where he was a vice-president.
- **Leonora Piper**: controls arranged, honesty accepted, the "white crow", assent withheld from her spirit controls **[A: dossier; the formula is James's own]**. Wikipedia confirms an infant son died before 1885 but does not name him, so **the identification as Herman James (1884-1885) is dossier-only, [R]**, and the campa says "their infant son is dead" without the name.
- **Richard Hodgson** joins the two halves: he wrote the 1885 report finding the Theosophical marvels at Adyar fraudulent, then moved to Boston and ran the Piper work for James. That is the only reason any Blavatsky material touches this file, and it touches it as a `suggested_ref`.

## 6. The public philosopher, 1896-1909

- **"The Will to Believe", 1896**, to the Yale and Brown philosophical clubs; the right defended only for options live, forced and momentous and not decidable by evidence in time. **[A: Wikipedia]** Both deliveries share one stop at the Yale pin. The volume and *Human Immortality* follow in 1897 **[R: Britannica's timeline; no imprint page fetched]**.
- **26 August 1898, Berkeley**: "Philosophical Conceptions and Practical Results", the first use in print of *pragmatism*, credited to Peirce. **[A: Wikipedia, "Pragmatism"]** *Coordinate corrected* from 37.8715/-122.273, 1.3 km off, to **37.8719/-122.2585**.
- **Anti-Imperialist League, 1898-99** **[R: SEP, no precise date]**. **Gifford Lectures, Edinburgh 1901-02** **[A: Britannica]**, hall unconfirmed. **Radical-empiricism essays 1904-05** **[A: SEP]**, collection posthumous. **1906, Stanford, "The Moral Equivalent of War"** **[R: dossier only]**. **January 1907 emeritus; *Pragmatism* 1907; *The Meaning of Truth* 1909** **[A: Britannica]**.
- **1908, Hibbert Lectures at Manchester College, Oxford**, published 1909 as *A Pluralistic Universe*. **[A: SEP; New England Historical Society names the venue]** *Two coordinates, one chosen:* the pool's 51.752/-1.2577 is central Oxford, 500 m off; the file uses the geocoded **51.7556/-1.2523** over the geography lens's **51.7548/-1.2544**.
- **Mary Whiton Calkins.** Upgraded from hedged [R] to **[A]** and re-dated: Harvard refused her the doctorate **in 1895** despite unanimous departmental recommendation, president Eliot opposing degrees for women; James called it "the most brilliant examination for the Ph.D. that we have had at Harvard"; she declined the Radcliffe substitute in **1902**. **[A: Wikipedia]** The pool's 1890 is the year she began studying with him.
- **W. E. B. Du Bois** among his students **[A: Wikipedia]**. No stop: the pool supplies no place, date or scene, and one is not invented.

## 7. Last illness, death, afterlife

- **September 1909, Clark University's twentieth anniversary**, Freud lecturing with Jung beside him. Conference confirmed **[A: Wikipedia]**, but **James's attendance and both attributed remarks are [R] externally**: Clark's article does not name him. Two atlas files stage him there independently.
- **28 September 1909, the letter to Theodore Flournoy**, verbatim confirmed **[A: *Letters* II, Gutenberg 38091]**. This letter, not the reported conversation, is the primary evidence of what James thought of Freud, and the file quotes it whole.
- **Spring 1910, Europe for heart treatment.** *Sourcing overreach corrected; downgraded to [R].* Wikipedia says only that James "sailed to Europe to take experimental treatments for his heart ailment that proved unsuccessful, and returned home on August 18". It does **not** name Bad Nauheim, the regimen, or a meeting with Henry in England. All three are dossier-only and marked so in the stop.
- **Returned 18 August 1910; died of heart failure at Chocorua 26 August, aged 68.** **[A: Wikipedia verbatim; Britannica]** *Coordinate corrected* from 43.8531/-71.2495, 4.5 km off in empty country, to **43.8886/-71.2204**, Chocorua Lake, where the farm stood.
- **Buried in the family plot, Cambridge Cemetery** **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**. **Three coordinates circulated for one grave and two were Harvard Yard**: 42.3736/-71.1097 and 42.3735/-71.1096, 2.4 km east, plus 42.3736/-71.1362, 600 m north. The file standardizes on **42.3682/-71.1388**. Do not reintroduce the others.
- **95 Irving Street.** Upgraded to **[A]** and corrected on three counts. Built **1889**, moved into that autumn, lived in until his death; Alice a decade longer, children and grandchildren until 1968; brown cedar shingles, gambrel roof, library with floor-to-ceiling bookcases, small upstairs study with a stand-up desk; and "the contractor told James he could save several thousand dollars simply by moving to Europe for the summer". **[A: news.harvard.edu/gazette, 2016; Wikipedia, "William Ralph Emerson"]** *A false kinship removed:* the pool called Emerson "his brother-in-law", when he was a cousin of Ralph Waldo Emerson with no marital tie to James. *Coordinate corrected* from two wrong pool values, 42.3823/-71.1195 and 42.3803/-71.1176, to **42.3781/-71.1111**.
- **Afterlife carried into the file, in the burial campa only**: **William James Hall**, 1963 **[A: Wikipedia]**, and the **ten-percent-of-the-brain misattribution**, fixed on James by Lowell Thomas in 1936 **[A: Wikipedia]**. James's own claim carried no percentage.
- **Afterlife left out, recorded here**: the Harvard UP editions **[R]**, the Rorty/Putnam revival **[R]**, Blum (2006), Knapp (2017) and the 2014 PBS program **[A, runtime dossier-only]**, the psychedelic-studies adoption **[R]**, the *Varieties* reception quotes **[A]**. The pool's "15th floor" for the invisible-gorilla filming is unsupported.
- **Negative finding, carried as a warning**: no verified footage or authentic sound recording of James survives in standard archives, so any online video presenting an animated portrait or synthetic narration as archival James is not genuine. **[R: dossier, not sourced externally]**

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

**William James is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** Two relations not rooted in his own life were caught, and **both point forward**, the direction the exception never licenses. Neither was deleted silently.

1. **`j_l_austin` and the William James Lectures.** `j_l_austin.journey.json` has Austin crossing in 1955 to deliver the William James Lectures at Harvard, the series behind *How to Do Things with Words*. The lectureship is named and endowed for James, so the relation is real, but it postdates his death by forty-five years. **That is Austin's stop, on Austin's file, and it is already there.** Nothing about Austin or 1955 is written here: a 1910 block cannot embed a 1955 hash. Direction: **forward**.
2. **William James Hall and its modern folklore.** The 1963 tower, the Department of Social Relations that held it 1964-86, Henry Murray's stress experiments there with the young Theodore Kaczynski among the subjects, the disputed MKUltra links, the "invisible gorilla" filming: accretions on a name, not events in a life. One sentence on the tower and one on the 1936 misattribution sit in the burial campa, as the Abdelkader file's closing stop carries the 1966 repatriation; **the rest is left out.** Direction: **forward**, no atlas-traveler pointer either way.

## 10. Contradictions left standing

Assistant professorship, 1880 (dossier) against 1881 (Britannica). Marriage day, 10 July 1878 (dossier only) against the bare year. First American psychology laboratory, James's 1875-76 room against Hall's. Harris Manchester College, two coordinates 130 m apart.
