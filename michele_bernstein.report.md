# Michèle Bernstein: research report

*2026-08-28. Slug `michele_bernstein`. Born Paris 1932; no death record located, so the journey file is written for a living subject and carries no tomb.*

**Method.** Every claim comes from a research pool in which each item was re-fetched and upheld, corrected, promoted, or demoted. Where the verifier contradicted the dossier, the correction stands and the dossier's reading is kept as a flagged contradiction. Eight pool coordinates were wrong, by 180 m to 5.5 km, and are fixed in the journey file. **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or single-source report not independently confirmed.

## 1. Birth and the occupation (1932-1945)

- Born Paris, 1932. **[A: Yale, Guide to the Michèle Bernstein Papers, GEN MSS 1556, which gives the year and nothing more.]** Day 28 April 1932: publisher-blurb only in the dossier, now corroborated by fr. and en.wikipedia. **[A, tertiary only.]** No primary document giving the day was reached.
- 16th arrondissement, from a fr.wikipedia category. **[R: the only arrondissement-level evidence found, and a weak instrument.]** Russian Jewish parents. **[A: en.wikipedia; absent from Yale.]**
- Father Michel Bernstein, antiquarian bookseller, born Lyon 13 January 1906, died Auxerre 15 August 2003; maquis in Seine-et-Oise, June 1944. **[R: Musée de la Résistance en ligne, not re-fetched.]**
- From 1942 he was involved in mass production of false papers for Défense de la France, the workshops sited with printers and booksellers of the 15e on rue de Lourmel. **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Défense de la France" and "15e arrondissement de Paris", both naming him.]**
- **Dropped:** February as start month, the collaborator Monique Rollin, the apartment as base, the forging manual. Dossier-only, confirmed nowhere. **[R]**

**Gap.** Her whereabouts 1939-1945 are undocumented everywhere reached; the dossier placed her with her mother near Le Havre, and nothing supports it. The file's one occupation stop says in `date_confidence` that the household is documented and the child is not.

## 2. The Latin Quarter and the Lettrist International (1952-1956)

- Sorbonne, 1952, and bored there. **[A: fr.wikipedia, "elle étudie à la Sorbonne mais s'y ennuie"; Yale has the studies without a year.]** Field, duration, degree: undocumented.
- Chez Moineau, 22 rue du Four, 6e; her closest companions there were Jean-Claude Guilbert and Patrick Straram, not Debord, whom she met the same year. **[A: fr.wikipedia; Yale for the meeting.]** The pool had put the bar on rue de la Huchette, 900 m east.
- August 1952: Le Havre with Guilbert and Straram, to see the settings of Sartre's *La Nausée*. **[A: fr.wikipedia.]** Her only documented journey outside Paris in these years, and the file's link to `jean_paul_sartre.journey.json`.
- *Potlatch* 3, 6 July 1954, issued from 15 rue Duguay-Trouin, 6e: her "Tout s'explique", signed Michèle-Ivich Bernstein, comparing the Lettrists to Jacobins and Cordeliers, and her signature on the collective declaration on Guatemala. The masthead address is André-Frank Conord's own, not an office. **[A: facsimile at oeuvresouvertes.wordpress.com.]**
- Married Debord 17 August 1954; witnesses Mohamed Dahou for him, Conord for her, who was excluded from the Lettrist International twelve days later. **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia.]** Yale gives no date; the pool cited Yale for it and was wrong to. No source names the mairie.
- "Square des Missions-Étrangères", *Potlatch* 16, 26 January 1955. **[A: notbored.org.]** What happened at the square is nowhere described.

## 3. The founding, and the lumpensecretariat (1957-1958)

- SI founded at Cosio d'Arroscia, Liguria, 28 July 1957. **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia.]** The file dates the stop 1957-07-28 and states the one-day/two-day disagreement (contradiction 1) rather than harmonising with Debord's file.
- She and Debord represented the Lettrist International, with Asger Jorn, Giuseppe Pinot-Gallizio, Piero Simondo, and Ralph Rumney of the London Psychogeographical Association. **[A: Yale for the group; fr.wikipedia for her presence.]** Debord's stop does not name her.
- 1957: "my husband and I were rather skint. In those days I was still a member of the lumpensecretariat, working for tuppence ha'penny at a humdrum publishing house. Guy Debord, naturally, wasn't working." **[A: her 2013 preface, theanarchistlibrary.org.]** **Correction:** she was *a member of* the lumpensecretariat, and did not call the period "her lumpen secretariat" as the dossier had it. Same page: the journal "sold five or six copies, and we sent the rest to people we found interesting."
- She wrote *Tous les chevaux du roi* in 1957-58 for money: "The solution was simple: I would fabricate a 'fake' popular novel." **[A: same.]** The dossier's "end of 1957" is false precision, dropped.
- "Pas d'indulgences inutiles", *Internationale Situationniste* 1, Paris, June 1958. **[A: bopsecrets.org, Knabb translation, headed "(excerpts)".]** The dossier dropped her scare quotes from the opening; they are restored: "Intellectual" or "artistic" collaboration involves our everyday life.
- October 1958: "responsable artistique" of the cabaret La Méthode, rue Descartes, founded by the singer-guitarist Jacques Florencie facing the École polytechnique, backed by Debord. **[A: fr.wikipedia, plus the Beinecke page, which has her "the manager during several months in 1958".]** **Dropped:** the house number 2, the 10 October poster, "drinks from 300 francs", none of them sourced. **[R]**

## 4. The two novels (1960-1962)

- *Tous les chevaux du roi*, Buchet/Chastel, 1960. **[A: year from Yale; publisher from fr.wikipedia, which adds that the house later published Debord's own book.]** The pool cited Yale for the publisher; Yale names none.
- 14 September 1960: interviewed about the novel on *Lectures pour tous*, 8 min 08 s. **[A: INA record; the pool cited editions-allia.com, which says nothing of the broadcast.]** Pierre Dumayet as interviewer is **[R]**, dossier-supplied.
- With Asger Jorn, the monograph *Pinot-Gallizio*, Bibliothèque d'Alexandrie, an imprint of the Skandinavisk institut for sammenlignende vandalisme under Jorn's direction; Yale's proof is dated 1960. **[A: Yale, Series II.]** **Cataloguing dispute resolved against the dossier:** Yale lists *Le long voyage* under Jorn and Pierre Wemaëre, not her, so it is dropped from her bibliography.
- *La Nuit*, Buchet/Chastel, 1961. **[A: year from Yale, publisher from fr.wikipedia.]** Yale says nothing of Robbe-Grillet; the nouveau-roman framing is re-sourced to Éditions Allia and fr.wikipedia's "pastiche du nouveau roman". **[A]**
- Her preface has the characters covering "almost every street in the fifth and sixth arrondissements", rue Monge singled out. **[A: 2013 preface.]** Fictionalised geography drawn from real dérive, not one event at an address; the stop says so.
- "Sunset Boulevard", *Internationale situationniste* 7, April 1962. **[A: notbored.org.]**
- A dated photograph places her in Antwerp, November 1962, occasion unstated. **[A: notbored.org.]** The SI's sixth conference met at Antwerp 12-16 November 1962 **[A: fr.wikipedia, as carried in Debord's file]**, but no source puts her at it. **[R]**, flagged in `date_confidence`, not asserted in the campa.

## 5. Denmark, the proofs, the poems (1963-1967)

- June 1963, Denmark: "Destruction of RSG-6" under J. V. Martin, with Martin's Thermonuclear Maps, Debord's painted Directives, Bernstein's *Victories*, a reconstructed bomb shelter, and a clandestine reissue of the tract "Danger: Official Secret - RSG 6". **[A: Debord's own catalogue text, bopsecrets.org.]**
- Her contribution recast defeats as victories: the Paris Commune, the Spanish Republicans, the Grande Jacquerie, in painted toy-soldier reliefs, reported lost. **[A for the works: tidsskrift.dk, abstract. R for the loss.]**
- Yale holds proofs of *Internationale Situationniste* 1-9, 1958-1964, most corrected in both Debord's hand and hers, her corrections to his *Contre le cinéma*, 1964, and a proof of her "Note on the Situationist International" matching the *Times Literary Supplement* text of 3 September 1964. **[A: Yale, Series II.]** The Note was written in Paris and published in London; the stop sits in Paris.
- 1964: Debord sent her 22 autograph poems, 17 gathered as *Épopée léwinienne*, after a breakup that followed her relationship with the journalist Walter Lewino; her notebook explaining them survives with them, and his letters run circa 1956-1968, bulk 1963. **[A: Yale, Series I.]**
- March 1967, Aarhus: she exhibited "Victory of the Spanish Republicans" at the "Ny-irrealisme" operation, a second and distinct Danish SI exhibition. **[A: the SI chronology at cddc.vt.edu.]** Absent from the dossier; found in the interlock pass.

## 6. Leaving (1967-1973)

- Resigned from the SI's French section, June 1967, and went on exhibiting and associating informally for about three years afterward. **[A: SI chronology.]**
- Her own account: the Six-Day War dispute, over Egyptian versus Israeli responsibility for starting it. "The arguments over this war were long and everyone was in agreement apart from me." And afterward: "Guy said to me, you stay clandestine, like Jorn." **[A: Frieze, "The Game", 2013, via text proxy after frieze.com returned 403.]**
- No contemporaneous 1967 resignation letter is known in any source consulted (contradiction 4).
- May 1968, her own prose: "Everyone who was there will tell you that it was, first and foremost, a festival." **[A: "Hot Water on All Floors", her preface to Walter Lewino, *L'Imagination au pouvoir*, Allia 2018, notbored.org PDF.]** The dossier's May 1968 details, bicycling for cigarettes and a banner for the occupied Sorbonne, were never confirmed **[R]**, so the stop rests on the preface instead.
- Divorce 5 January 1972, after long separation. **[A: en.wikipedia; Yale gives the year only.]** Her reason: "I was buying my flat and no longer wanted to be responsible, as Guy's wife, for any financial costs or fines that the IS journal could have." **[A: Frieze.]** The tribunal is unnamed.
- Married Ralph Rumney at the end of 1973: "At the end of '73, Ralph and I married. Debord went into a rage, and this time excluded me for good." **[A: Frieze, which settles contradiction 3 against the 1974 notices.]**
- Rumney had married Pegeen Vail Guggenheim in 1958, widowed 1 March 1967, and taught at the Canterbury College of Art. **[A: en.wikipedia.]** That Bernstein lived in Canterbury is **[R]**: it recurs in secondary accounts, and the Frieze interview names only Salisbury. The marriage stop sits at Canterbury with the residence flagged.

## 7. Salisbury and Libération (1982-1996)

- "En 1982, Michèle Bernstein s'installe à Salisbury et devient critique littéraire pour le journal Libération." **[A: fr.wikipedia.]** The dossier's "Old Parsonage" address is uncorroborated and dropped; the bicycle survives. **[A: partisanhotel.co.uk.]**
- **Sequence corrected.** The pool had her moving to Salisbury after the divorce and marrying Rumney there. Correct order: divorce January 1972, Rumney end of 1973 elsewhere in England, Salisbury from 1982, which Frieze presents as where she went *after* leaving Rumney.
- "I worked there for 14 years, until 1996 when I was 64, the longest job I ever had, and I liked it." **[A: Frieze.]** Sixty-four in 1996 checks against a 1932 birth.
- **Correction to the July anecdote.** The dossier said Serge July did not know of her Situationist past until Lebovici's 1984 murder. She says "he did not know for two years that I was the ex-wife of Guy Debord", and that the murder prompted July to ask her to write about him.
- She championed Marie NDiaye, Lydie Salvayre, Hervé Guibert. **[A: postwarcultureatbeinecke.org.]** The 21 February 1991 piece on Guibert's *Le Protocole compassionnel* is **[R]**, dossier bibliography only. The pin is Libération's later rue Béranger office; the stop says it was not there throughout.

## 8. Normandy and the late voice (2000-2024)

- About twenty years in a house in a hamlet outside a small Normandy village, whose large windows she called "depressing". **[A: partisanhotel.co.uk.]** The village is named nowhere; the coordinate is a declared placeholder. **Dropped:** the basket of provisions from a neighbour, the magnifying glass, the visual distortions after reading, none on the page fetched. **[R]**
- Winter 2020: telephone contact with her translator Clodagh Kinsella; a planned Normandy meeting never happened; they met instead near the Jewish section of Montparnasse Cemetery, by a plaque marking a former Simone de Beauvoir residence. "I HAVE FORGOTTEN TO DIE", capitals in the source, titled both the profile and the 2021 film, whose title, *On the Passage of Michèle Bernstein Through Time*, détourns Debord's own of 1959. Also: "I am an orange that's already been sucked." **[A: partisanhotel.co.uk.]**
- The horse horoscopes legend: that she wrote astrology for racehorses to fund the SI was Debord's joke to Henri Lefebvre, repeated by Lefebvre as fact. Her real paid work was copy for a horse-racing newspaper. **[A: partisanhotel.co.uk, with Frieze.]**
- 2013: "Preface in the Guise of an Autobiography, or Vice Versa" for the English *The Night*, ending "It's a love story, a story of lost love. For the streets." **[A: theanarchistlibrary.org.]**
- 3 August 2014: letter to Raoul Vaneigem, "Lefebvre and Cheype", in *Rien n'est fini, tout commence* (Allia). "Of all of Guy's little lovers, Cheype is the only one who had antipathy for me." **[A: notbored.org PDF.]** **Framing corrected:** the pool called "If it were me, I would not want a child. If it is you, do what you want and God bless." advice she gave Nicole. She writes "I was already far away... If not, my advice would have been what it has always been", so it is counterfactual and is used only so.
- 2019: papers sold to the Beinecke with the bookseller Didier Lecointre-Dominique Drouet; GEN MSS 1556, seven boxes. **[A: Yale.]** The 22 Debord poems were "recently made public" as of the Beinecke's 2022 notice. **[A: postwarculture.]**
- Late 2024: Jean-Philippe Delhomme, "a friend and studio neighbor", painted two portraits of her from life for "Model Resting", Galerie Perrotin, 76 rue de Turenne, 23 November 2024 to 18 January 2025. Valérie Da Costa's press text calls her "the cofounder of one of the last avant-garde movements, the Situationist International" and reads the sittings, about three hours each and often a single session, as opposition to "the society of the spectacle". **[A: Perrotin press release PDF.]** **Titles corrected:** the release names neither portrait, and the titles the dossier gave belong to separate canvases of another model.
- **No tomb.** No death record or obituary located; the 2024 sittings are the most recent confirmed evidence she was alive. The file ends there and makes no death claim.

## Contradictions flagged, not resolved

1. **Cosio d'Arroscia, one day or two.** 28 July 1957 confirmable; 27-28 July in the dossier and Debord's file; "July 1957" only in en.wikipedia's SI article.
2. **Rumney's expulsion interval.** Seven months versus nine, both landing in 1958.
3. **Rumney marriage year.** End of 1973 in her words; 1974 in secondary notices.
4. **Resignation or expulsion, 1967.** Her testimony and the SI chronology say resignation; the "Zionism" rumour persists; no contemporaneous document settles it.
5. **The Galerie EXI venue.** Debord says only "Denmark"; the two pages that would confirm Odense and the gallery both 404.
6. **Birth arrondissement.** Yale says "Paris", a Wikipedia category the 16e, and no document gives an address.
7. **Le long voyage.** Dossier treated it as possibly hers; Yale attributes it to Jorn and Wemaëre.

## Honest gaps

Her whereabouts 1939-1945. Her mother, unnamed everywhere. Her Sorbonne field and any degree. The "humdrum publishing house" of 1957. The name of the horse-racing newspaper. The Normandy village, withheld in every source deliberately. Any English address between the 1973 marriage and the 1982 Salisbury move. Whether she attended the Antwerp conference or was only photographed in Antwerp that month. The interviewer on *Lectures pour tous*. Whether the *Victoires du prolétariat* reliefs survive. The exact wording around "Paddington is a bastard", which two fetches of the Kinsella profile rendered differently, so the line is left out of the journey file.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Bernstein is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are written. Two relations were caught and are reported rather than deleted. Both run **forward**, which the exception file never licenses, so both fall to the standing rule: sources on her own stops, no scene of their own.

1. **The translations and the rewrite.** Kelsey's *All the King's Horses* (Semiotext(e), 2008), Kinsella's *The Night* (Book Works, 2013, second edition 2021), and Everyone Agrees' *After the Night* (Book Works, 2013), a détournement of *La Nuit* set in London. Other people's acts in cities she did not travel to; cited on her own novel stops. The pool's claim that *After the Night* had "Bernstein's permission and prompting" is confirmed nowhere and is dropped. Her own 2021 postface **is** her act and does get a stop, at her Normandy pin: "I am a fraud. Anyone who buys The Night (thank you) and so much as skims it might think that I used to spend my time walking the streets of Paris..." **[A: bookworks.org.uk.]**
2. **The Beinecke acquisition and the 2024 portraits.** Yale's purchase is Yale's act at a place she did not go, so it is written as her act of selling, at her own Paris pin. Delhomme's paintings are his, but she sat for them in Paris, so the sitting is hers and stays.

Staging any of this as scenes would require adding her to `EXCEPTIONS.md`; the direction caught is forward.
