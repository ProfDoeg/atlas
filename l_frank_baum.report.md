# L. Frank Baum (1856-1919): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-08 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Lyman Frank Baum, author of The Wonderful Wizard of Oz and thirteen Oz sequels; also poultry breeder, actor, shopkeeper, newspaper editor, trade-magazine founder, film producer. Queued in `QUEUE.md` line 155.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a single-source claim the other reachable sources do not carry. Contradictions are printed and left standing.

**Method.** The evidence is lopsided in the opposite direction from most atlas subjects. Baum's *published* words survive completely and free: the prefaces in which he explains himself to his readers are on Project Gutenberg and were read directly, and every quotation in the journey file is verbatim from one of them. His *life* reaches this session almost entirely through three Wikipedia articles (the biography, `Maud Gage Baum`, `The Wonderful Wizard of Oz`) plus place articles used to fix coordinates. No archive, no letter, no manuscript, no scholarly biography was reachable. The pool arrived with corrections attached to about a third of its entries; those are adopted and printed rather than applied silently, several of them reversing the pool outright. Ten pool coordinates were also wrong; each fix, with old value, new value, and authority, sits in its own stop's `date_confidence`. Only six stops carry a quote, below the house target of about a third, because every quotable Baum text reachable here is a preface and the pool's ten quotations come from six books with several pairs sharing one preface.

## 1. Chittenango and Rose Lawn (1856-1873)

- Born **Chittenango, New York, 15 May 1856**, seventh of nine children of **Benjamin Ward Baum** and **Cynthia Ann Stanton Baum**; money from oil and barrel-making; German, Scots-Irish, English ancestry; Methodist household. **[A: Wikipedia "L. Frank Baum"; "Chittenango, New York".]**
- Childhood at **Rose Lawn**, near Syracuse, at what is now **Mattydale**; recalled by him as a paradise. **[A.]** The Mattydale article localizes him better than the pool did: he "spent much of his childhood on the site of Roxboro Road Middle School" **[A]**. The estate footprint is gone, so the pin marks the hamlet.
- **Home-tutored** for a reputed **weak heart**. **[A.]** What the condition actually was is nowhere established. **[Gap.]**
- **Peekskill Military Academy** from about age twelve (1868), withdrawn after roughly two years following what biographers call a **psychogenic heart attack** brought on by punishment for daydreaming. **[A.]** The academy stood at Elm and Hudson Streets, founded 1833, closed June 1968, Baum on its alumni list; the pool's hedge that the site was unknown was unnecessary.
- **Amateur journals** on his father's press with brother **Henry Clay Baum**, including **The Rose Lawn Home Journal** (1870s); **The Stamp Collector** and a stamp dealership by seventeen (1873). **[A.]**
- **Gap.** No document from before the 1880s is quoted anywhere reachable; the first seventeen years arrive as summary.

## 2. Hamburgs, footlights, and the Richburg fire (1875-1886)

- Clerks in his **brother-in-law's Syracuse dry goods company**, c. 1875-1880 **[A]**; breeds **fancy Hamburg chickens** from about age twenty **[A]**; founds **The Poultry Record**, March 1880 **[A]**. The pool locates the last two only as "New York State."
- **CORRECTION.** Pool dated the Richburg theater **1880**. Wikipedia "Richburg, New York": Baum "was manager of a theater, Baum's Opera House, in Richburg **from 1881 to 1882**." Stage names **Louis F. Baum** and **George Brooks** confirmed. **[A.]** The theater burned in **March 1882** during Baum's play **Matches**. **[A.]**
- **CORRECTION, reversing the pool.** The pool asserted the fire destroyed "the only script of *The Maid of Arran*." False on the pool's own evidence: the play **premiered 15 May 1882**, two months after the fire, and toured to June 1883. Wikipedia says the script survived, "written in the box office with the charred sides." The play actually lost was **The Mackrummins**.
- **The Maid of Arran**: premiere **15 May 1882**, his twenty-sixth birthday; music, lyrics, and book by Baum, who starred as **Hugh Holcomb**; two Broadway stints on tour; closed **7 June 1883 at Richmond, Indiana**. **[A.]** **Gap: the premiere venue is named in nothing reached**, so the stop takes the canonical New York pin.
- **Marries Maud Gage, 9 November 1882**, in her mother's parlor at Fayetteville; officiant **W. H. Hawley**, Fayetteville Baptist Church. **[A: Wikipedia "Maud Gage Baum".]** **Matilda Joslyn Gage** co-wrote the *History of Woman Suffrage* with Stanton and Anthony **[A]**; the common claim that her ideas shaped Oz directly is **[R]**, standard in Baum scholarship, carried by no source reached.

## 3. The Syracuse houses (1883-1886)

- **Shonnard Street**, 1882-1884, first married home; **Frank Joslyn Baum** born there **4 December 1883**. **[A.]** **No street number given** **[Gap]**; the pin approximates the corridor. Move to **28 Slocum Avenue**, **1 May 1884**. **[A.]**
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** The pool placed the birth of **Robert Stanton Baum (1 February 1886)** at Slocum Avenue. Wikipedia "Maud Gage Baum" places it at a **Holland Street** residence and records that it left Maud with **peritonitis**. **No coordinate for Holland Street exists in the pool.** The Slocum pin is kept and both street names printed, neither dismissed.
- **The Book of the Hamburgs** (1886), a poultry manual, is his first published book. **[A.]**

## 4. Aberdeen: the Bazaar, the Pioneer, the two editorials (1888-1891)

The pool's errors were densest here.

- **CONTRADICTION, resolved on the stronger source and said out loud.** Chronology lens gave the move as **July 1888**; Wikipedia "Maud Gage Baum" is specific, the family "moved to Aberdeen, South Dakota, on **September 20, 1888**." September is used; July, if anything, is Baum's advance trip. **[A / R.]**
- **Baum's Bazaar**, Main Street, opened 1 October 1888, bankrupt within about a year on extended customer credit. **[A.]** **Storefront address unconfirmed.** **[Gap.]** **CORRECTION:** Harry Neal Baum born **18 December 1889**, not the pool's 17 December.
- **The Aberdeen Saturday Pioneer.** Pool gave a bare "1890." Attested and tighter: **owned and edited 25 January 1890 to 21 March 1891**, with the **Our Landlady** column. **[A.]** **Office address not recorded.** **[Gap.]**
- **Editorial of 20 December 1890**, five days after Sitting Bull's killing, calling for the "total annihilation of the few remaining Indians." **[A: Wikipedia "L. Frank Baum", quoting the Pioneer.]** **Editorial of 3 January 1891**, after Wounded Knee, repeating the call. **[A, same source.]** The **reading** of both (satire, sincerity, or ordinary frontier-weekly rhetoric) is contested among historians. **The atlas does not adjudicate it.** The texts are not in dispute and the journey states their substance without softening.
- **CORRECTION, inverting the pool.** The pool said "the Pine Ridge and Standing Rock Sioux tribes formally sought a retraction" in 2006. **No such demand is documented anywhere reached.** Wikipedia records the opposite motion: "In 2006, two descendants of Baum apologized to the Sioux nation for any hurt that their ancestor had caused." **[A.]** **The location of the apology is nowhere recorded** **[Gap]**, so that stop is pinned to the massacre ground.

## 5. Chicago: the window trade and the Emerald City (1891-1904)

- **Humboldt Park** from 1891, four sons. **[A.]** **Weak point: Wikipedia's Humboldt Park article does not mention Baum.** The residence rests on the two Baum articles and the Chicago Literary Hall of Fame.
- **Chicago Evening Post** reporter, 1891-1892 **[A]**. **Pitkin & Brooks**, china and glassware wholesalers, traveling salesman c. 1893-1897: **[R]**, from Baum biographies, **not confirmed by any source reached**, and left unnamed in the campa.
- **Theosophy.** The interlock lens asserts Baum joined the **Chicago branch in 1892** and drew on it for Oz. **[R]**, and the lens itself concedes `blavatsky.journey.json` never mentions Baum and that the link is "a reconstruction from outside the corpus." No source reached confirms membership.
- **Mother Goose in Prose** (1897), illustrated by **Maxfield Parrish**; **The Show Window** founded the same year, later **Merchants Record and Show Window**. **[A.]**
- **68 Humboldt Boulevard** **[A]**, modern address uncertain after **Chicago's 1909 renumbering** **[Gap]**. **Matilda Joslyn Gage dies there 18 March 1898** **[A]**. **Father Goose: His Book** (1899), with **W. W. Denslow**, is the bestselling children's book of the year **[A]**, and buys the **Sign of a Goose** cottage at Macatawa Park, Michigan **[A]**; **CORRECTION:** the name is "Sign of a Goose", not the pool's "Sign of the Goose."
- **The Wonderful Wizard of Oz** written at Humboldt Boulevard; introduction signed "L. Frank Baum, Chicago, April, 1900"; published **1 September 1900**, George M. Hill Company, Denslow co-copyrighted. **[A.]** **CORRECTION:** the pool said the 10,000-copy first and 15,000-copy second printings "both sell out by October 1900"; Wikipedia says the first sold out and the second was **"nearly depleted."**
- **The stage musical** with **Paul Tietjens** and **Julian Mitchell**; Broadway **January 1903**, **293 performances**; returns 1904; tours to 1911. **[A.]** **Venue not named in the pool.** **[Gap.]** **The Marvelous Land of Oz**, Reilly & Britton, June 1904. **[A; Author's Note verbatim in Gutenberg 54.]**

## 6. The four failures (1905-1911)

- **Pedloe Island**, an Oz amusement park off the California coast, abandoned when the musical **The Woggle-Bug** flopped, 1905. **[A that both happened.]** **The island is located only as "off the California coast" and no theater is named.** **[Gap, twice.]** Pinned to Chicago, his home city in 1905.
- **The 1906 expedition:** six months through **Egypt, Greece, Italy, North Africa, Switzerland, France**. **[A: Wikipedia "Maud Gage Baum".]** **The source names countries and never cities.** The pool made this six pinned stops (Giza, Sorrento, Geneva, Paris, the Acropolis, Algiers), each conceding the city was "chosen as representative, not independently confirmed": six invented coordinates for one attested fact. **Giza alone is kept, marked [R].**
- **Ozma of Oz** (1907), Author's Note datelined Macatawa **[A; Gutenberg 33361]**. **The Road to Oz** (1909), preface datelined Coronado **[A; Gutenberg 26624]**.
- **The Fairylogue and Radio-Plays** (1908): hand-tinted slides, film, live actors, orchestra, Baum on stage in evening dress; financially disastrous. **[A.]** **Place given only as "United States (touring)"; no opening house named** **[Gap]**, so the September date is a season, not an opening night.
- **Hotel del Coronado**: he "did much of his writing at the hotel." **[A.]** **CONTRADICTION, not resolved:** hotel tradition holds the **Emerald City** was modeled on the building; most biographers cite the **1893 Chicago World's Fair "White City."** Wikipedia presents it as contested; both are printed and neither chosen.
- **Bankruptcy, August 1911**: property already transferred to Maud; left with clothes, a typewriter, and his library; rights to the early books, *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* included, signed away. **[A.]**

## 7. Ozcot and the film company (1910-1915)

- **CORRECTION.** The pool dated the move and Ozcot to **1911**. Wikipedia "Maud Gage Baum": the house was **custom-built in 1910** with Maud's inheritance, the move prompted by failing health. **1911 is the bankruptcy year.** **CORRECTION:** the pool called Hollywood then "a small independent town outside Los Angeles"; it was **consolidated into the City of Los Angeles on 7 February 1910**.
- **LENS CONTRADICTION.** Geography lens put Ozcot at 34.1004, -118.3389 (North Cherokee Ave); afterlife lens put the death at 34.0928, -118.3287, generic mid-Hollywood, 1.2 km south. **Cherokee Avenue is used at all four Ozcot stops.** The other value appears once, at the "move west" stop, where it is the canonical Hollywood pin.
- **1749 N. Cherokee Avenue** is widely cited in Baum literature but **appears in no source reached, and Wikipedia's own "Ozcot" article gives no address**. **[R.]**
- **Oz Film Manufacturing Company**, 1914: Baum president, **Louis F. Gottschalk** VP, **Harry Marston Haldeman** secretary, **Clarence R. Rundel** treasurer; studio on **Santa Monica Boulevard between Gower Street and Lodi Place**. **[A.]**
- **CORRECTION.** The pool said the company "later renames itself Dramatic Feature Films." It did not. It **failed in 1915 and was absorbed by Metro Pictures**, after which **Baum's son Frank Joslyn Baum** renamed the remnant. The lot later held Famous Players-Lasky (Paramount) and National Film Corporation. **[A.]**

## 8. Death and burial (1919)

- **Stroke 5 May 1919** at Ozcot, then coma; **died 6 May 1919**, nine days short of sixty-three. **[A.]**
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** A passage in Wikipedia's **"Ozcot"** article gives the death as **16 May 1919**. Every other reachable account gives 6 May. The journey uses 6 May and prints the variant.
- **Last words**, "Now we can cross the Shifting Sands." **[R.]** Universally repeated, no independent witness. Set as prose in the campa and deliberately **not** in a `quote` field, which here is reserved for printed text.
- **Buried at Forest Lawn Memorial Park, Glendale**, May 1919. **[A.]** **Forest Lawn's own article does not list him among its named notable interments**; confirmation comes only from the biography. **Gap: the day of burial is recorded nowhere.** 8 May is set so the burial does not precede the death, and `date_confidence` says so.
- **The Magic of Oz** published posthumously 1919; **Glinda of Oz** 10 July 1920. **[A.]**

## 9. Afterlife (1920-2015)

- **Ruth Plumly Thompson** continues the series from 1921 with **21** further books **[A]**; Baum wrote **13** sequels, and the original entered the **US public domain in 1956** after three million copies sold **[A]**. **Alexander Volkov**, *The Wizard of the Emerald City* (1939), begins a six-book Soviet series with **Ellie** and **Totoshka** **[A]**.
- **Hebrew** editions render Oz as **"Eretz Uz,"** echoing the biblical land; some **Indian** editions replace the Tin Woodman with a horse. **[A.]** **CORRECTION:** the pool's "at least 56 languages" **is in no source reached**; Wikipedia says only "nearly every major language."
- **Chittenango:** yellow brick sidewalk **1982**, replaced with stamped colored concrete in a **2007** revitalization; the **All Things Oz Museum**; the **Oz-Stravaganza** festival, first Friday and Saturday of June, historically drawing surviving 1939-film Munchkin actors. **[A.]** No founding dates for museum or festival. **[Gap.]** **Yellow Brick Road Casino**, Oneida Indian Nation, **2 June 2015** **[A]**; **Chicago Literary Hall of Fame**, **2013** **[A]**.

## 10. The Bronxville problem

`QUEUE.md` line 155: **"L. Frank Baum | American author of The Wonderful Wizard of Oz; lived in Bronxville, New York, the same town as Sara Rudner and Viola Farber already queued."** The subject was queued for a reason that does not survive contact with the sources.

- **No attestation for a Bronxville residence was found.** Both lenses checked independently and came back negative. Wikipedia's biography names Rose Lawn, Aberdeen, Chicago, and Ozcot and no Bronxville period, and **Bronxville's own notable-residents list does not include him.** The documented sequence is **Chittenango, Mattydale, Peekskill, Syracuse, Richburg, Aberdeen, Chicago, Macatawa (summers), Coronado (winters), Hollywood.**
- **Handling.** The journey neither drops the claim quietly nor pretends he lived there: it carries **one final stop**, dated to the compilation day and labelled as such, naming the queue entry, Rudner, Farber, and Cunningham, and stating that the pin stands over nothing. Precedent for a non-biographical stop: `ann_lee.journey.json`.

## 11. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Verified by grep across `working/journeys/*.journey.json`. **No existing journey file mentions Baum or the Wizard of Oz.** He enters the corpus cold, through other people's ground.

**Inherited byte-identical.** Grand River, Standing Rock (45.7667, -100.9333) and Wounded Knee Creek (43.1462, -102.3555) from `sitting_bull` and `low_dog`; Chicago (41.8781, -87.6298) from `maurice_sendak`, `otto_hanish`, `fyodor_yevtishchev`; Hollywood (34.0928, -118.3287) from `fritz_lang`; New York (40.7128, -74.006) from `mark_twain`; Bronxville (40.9387, -73.8323) from the interlock lens, the afterlife lens giving -73.8326.

**Named in campa (mutual gaze).** **`sitting_bull`** and **`low_dog`** are the strongest interlock here, and the interlock lens missed them: it searched the queue's Bronxville framing and found dancers, while the corpus already holds two Lakota journeys crossing Baum's route in the exact week his worst writing was published. Sitting Bull dies at Grand River 15 December 1890 and the editorial runs on the 20th; Wounded Knee falls on the 29th and the second editorial on 3 January. **Two of the atlas's travelers are the subject of the third's editorials.** Baum is routed through both pins for that reason, and each campa says he is not standing there.

Also named: **`tolkien`** and **`maurice_sendak`** at the April 1900 introduction, where Baum's stated purpose is the reverse of Tolkien's 1939 St Andrews argument (**[R]**; no source connects the three men); **`blavatsky`** and **`annie_besant`** at Humboldt Park, on the [R] Theosophy claim; **`mark_twain`** at the 1903 Broadway stop, **no meeting attested**; **`fritz_lang`** at the 1910 arrival and at the grave, where the two Forest Lawns are distinguished; **`merce_cunningham`** at the Bronxville stop, via Viola Farber.

**Not inherited:** the Kaaba, Buenos Aires, and Paris canonical pins, none of them on Baum's route once the five unlocated 1906 tour guesses were cut. **Queue-only, named in no campa:** Rudner, Farber, John Cage, Walt Disney, the Brothers Grimm, Hans Christian Andersen, H. P. Lovecraft.
