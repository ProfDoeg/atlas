# Le Corbusier (Charles-Edouard Jeanneret-Gris), 1887-1965: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `le_corbusier`. Companion artifact: `le_corbusier.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or widely repeated attribution that no source fetched in this pass confirms. Where sources disagree, both readings are printed and the disagreement is left standing. Where the adversarial verification pass overturned a gathered claim, the overturn is printed as a **CORRECTION**; the original is not quietly deleted.

**On coordinates.** The gathering pass supplied a pin for every geographic entry. Verification checked them against Wikipedia's own coordinate fields and found many wrong by between 350 metres and 22 kilometres, one of them in the wrong city. Corrections are tabulated below and applied in the journey file. Treat any uncorrected pin as merely plausible.

---

## 1. La Chaux-de-Fonds, 1887-1907

Born 6 October 1887 in La Chaux-de-Fonds, in the Swiss Jura, to a family of watch-engravers **[A: Wikipedia, "Le Corbusier"]**. Enters the municipal Ecole d'Art at fifteen, in 1902, for engraving and watch-case decoration **[A]**. From 1905 studies in the higher decoration course under the painter Charles L'Eplattenier, who turns him toward architecture **[A]**. Designs his first executed building, the Villa Fallet, in 1905 with the builder Rene Chapallaz **[A]**.

- **CORRECTION.** The gathered entry gave a flat "1905" and called him seventeen. Wikipedia's Villa Fallet article calls the designer "the eighteen-year-old Charles-Edouard Jeanneret" and gives completion 1906; some sources give 1907. Honest range: design 1905, completion 1906 or 1907. Carried in the stop's `date_confidence`.
- His own account: "I had a horror of architecture and architects... I was sixteen, I accepted the verdict and I obeyed. I moved into architecture." **[A as a translation in the Wikipedia article, footnote 21; French original not fetched]**. Note the friction: he says sixteen, the school record puts the turn at eighteen. Not adjudicated.

## 2. 1907-1911: other men's offices

- **September 1907, Florence.** First trip outside Switzerland; visits the Certosa del Galluzzo, the Charterhouse of Ema, whose private cells opening onto a shared cloister he cites for life as the model for collective housing **[A: Wikipedia; Fondation Le Corbusier biography]**.
- **Winter 1907-1908.** On through Budapest to Vienna; meets Gustav Klimt among others **[A]**. Months not fixed by anything fetched.
- **1908-1910, Paris.** Roughly fourteen months with Auguste Perret, pioneer of reinforced concrete, at 25bis rue Franklin **[A]**.
- **October 1910 to March 1911, Berlin.** In Peter Behrens's office **[A]**.
  - **CORRECTION.** The gathered entry put Walter Gropius alongside him there. Gropius had left Behrens in 1910, before or as Jeanneret arrived in October. Mies van der Rohe was there. The Gropius overlap is **not supportable** and is dropped from the journey text.
- Retrospective verdict, letter to Perret, July 1916: "You can see, that Auguste Perret left more in me than Peter Behrens." **[A as a translation in the Wikipedia article, footnote 33]**.

## 3. 1911: the Voyage d'Orient

Five months through the Balkans, Serbia, Bulgaria, Turkey and Greece, with Pompeii and Rome on the return; extensive Parthenon sketches; some eighty sketchbooks **[A: Wikipedia]**. Companion: August Klipstein. Route: Berlin and Dresden, Prague, Vienna, the Danube, Istanbul, Mount Athos, Athens, home through Italy.

- **CORRECTION.** Gathering tagged the companion and the route **[R]**, on the ground that only Le Corbusier scholarship carried them. Verification found both in the English Wikipedia article and corroborated by the dossier's own itinerary entries. **Retagged [A].** Documentation, not legend.

Itinerary stops carried into the journey file, sourced to Elke Krasny's account of the Voyage (which follows *Journey to the East*) or to *Journey to the East* itself: Prague, Vienna, Budapest, Belgrade and Bucharest, May and June 1911; Istanbul, roughly six weeks across June and July, with repeated returns to Hagia Sophia, the Suleymaniye and Topkapi; Mount Athos, August, Great Lavra and Simonopetra; Athens, September, weeks on the Acropolis; Pompeii and Naples, October; Rome, October into November; Florence, November; home in November **[A for the sequence; the month assignments are itinerary reconstruction, not a diary, and every Voyage stop is hedged in `date_confidence`]**.

*Journey to the East*, written 1911, is not published in his lifetime; it appears in 1966 edited by Jean Petit, with Ivan Zaknic's English translation in 1987 from MIT Press **[R: publication history as reported in the pool; neither edition held in hand]**.

## 4. 1912-1922: the Jura, concrete, Paris

- **1912.** Begins a house for his parents in La Chaux-de-Fonds; designs the Villa Favre-Jacot at Le Locle **[A]**.
- **December 1914.** Begins serious study of reinforced concrete with the engineer Max Dubois **[A]**. **1914-1915.** Develops the Dom-Ino system, an open floor plan on slender concrete columns **[A]**.
- **August 1916.** Commission for the Anatole Schwob house, the Villa Turque **[A: Wikipedia, "Le Corbusier"]**.
  - **CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** Wikipedia's Villa Schwob article dates the commission to 1912 and completion to 1917, against the main article's August 1916. Both printed; the stop is dated 1916-08-01 with the conflict in `date_confidence`.
  - Reported reply to the client: "Whether you like it or not, my presence is inscribed in every corner of your house." **[A as a translation in the Wikipedia article, footnote 34]**.
- **1917.** Moves permanently to Paris **[A]**.
  - **CORRECTION.** The gathered entry fused two events: the 1917 move *and* the start of the partnership with Pierre Jeanneret. Wikipedia: "In 1922, he and his cousin Pierre Jeanneret ... opened a studio in Paris." **The partnership dates from 1922.** The gathered geography entry for 35 rue de Sevres carried the same error as a date range, "1917 to 1965"; corrected to 1922 to 1965.
- **1918.** Meets Amedee Ozenfant; they publish *Apres le Cubisme* and launch Purism **[A]**. **1920.** Adopts "Le Corbusier", adapted from a maternal ancestor named Lecorbesier, in the first issue of *L'Esprit Nouveau* **[A]**. **1922.** Opens the atelier at 35 rue de Sevres with Pierre Jeanneret **[A]**.

## 5. 1923-1935: manifesto and Radiant City

- **1923.** *Vers une architecture* **[A]**. Print run of 3,000 from Cres, second edition with new preface 1924 **[R: publication history as reported, not verified bibliographically]**. **1923-1925.** Maison La Roche / Jeanneret, later the Fondation Le Corbusier **[A]**.
- **1925.** Pavillon de l'Esprit Nouveau and the Plan Voisin, the scheme to rebuild central Paris as towers in a park **[A]**.
- **1926.** Cite Fruges at Pessac for the industrialist Henry Fruges **[A]**. That residents modified the houses and that he blamed the client is **[R]** in the pool and stays [R].
- **1926-1927, Geneva.** League of Nations competition.
  - **CORRECTION.** The gathered entry said "rejected on a technicality", the printer's-ink anecdote. Wikipedia says the jury "declared it was unable to pick a single winner, and the project was given instead to the top five architects, who were all neoclassicists." The technicality is **[R] tradition**; the jury's failure to choose is **[A]**. Competition ran 1926-1927, judged 1927. Both printed. Cut from the journey for want of a verified pin.
- **1927.** Weissenhof Estate, Stuttgart, for the Werkbund **[A]**. Wikipedia says **three** houses, nos. 13 to 15; the gathered entry said two. Corrected.
- **26 June 1928.** Presides at the first CIAM at the Chateau de la Sarraz **[A]**. **1928-1931.** Villa Savoye, Poissy **[A]**.
- **Tsentrosoyuz, Moscow. CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** Wikipedia's Le Corbusier article: "between 1928 and 1932". Its Tsentrosoyuz article: completed 1933. Much of the literature: 1936. Best stated as begun 1928 or 1929, completed between 1933 and 1936; the spread is in `date_confidence`. **1932.** Palace of the Soviets competition, passed over **[A]**.
- **1929, South America.** Lectures in Buenos Aires and Rio, published as *Precisions*.
  - **CORRECTION.** Gathered **[R]**. The tour is the documented origin of *Precisions sur un etat present de l'architecture et de l'urbanisme* (1930), the transcript of the Buenos Aires lectures. **Retagged [A].**
  - **The Victoria Ocampo meeting stays [R].** It is not in the Wikipedia article on Victoria Ocampo, checked this session, nor in the atlas's own `victoria_ocampo.journey.json`. Well reported elsewhere; unattested here.
- **1929-1933.** Swiss Pavilion at the Cite Universitaire and the Cite de Refuge **[A]**. Neither carried a coordinate; both omitted from the journey rather than pinned by guess.
- **1930. CORRECTION.** Marriage to Yvonne Gallis and French naturalization were both gathered **[R]**. French Wikipedia's infobox lists the spouse as Yvonne Gallis-Jeanneret from 1930, and the opening line reads "naturalise francais en 1930". **Both retagged [A].**
- **1931-1942.** Plan Obus for colonial Algiers, unbuilt **[R, and left [R]]**. **1935.** *La Ville Radieuse* **[A]**; first American visit, skyscrapers judged "much too small" **[A]**. The better-known line, "A hundred times I have thought: New York is a catastrophe, and fifty times: it is a beautiful catastrophe", is from *When the Cathedrals Were White*, 1947 **[A]**.

## 6. 1940-1947: Ozon, Vichy, and the later reckoning

- **1940-1942.** Retreats to the Pyrenean village of Ozon, largely idle for over a year **[A]**. **1943.** Founds Ascoral to plan reconstruction, under Vichy then in Paris **[A]**. **20 April 1944.** Resigns from Alexis Carrel's Vichy-era eugenics foundation **[A]**. The resignation is attested to the day; the depth of the prior involvement is exactly what is contested, and this dossier does not settle it.
- **2015.** Xavier de Jarcy's *Le Corbusier, un fascisme francais* and Francois Chaslin's *Un Corbusier* revive documentary evidence of interwar fascist and antisemitic sympathies and of the Carrel role **[A: Wikipedia, controversies section; French press coverage of the anniversary year]**. Neither book was read here: what is attested is that the controversy occurred and what it concerned, not the strength of its evidence.

## 7. 1947-1962: concrete, and India

- **1947-1952.** United Nations headquarters with Oscar Niemeyer and Wallace K. Harrison **[A]**. That the credit dispute embittered him for years sits inside an [A] entry but is interpretive; treat as **[R]**.
- **1947-1952.** Unite d'Habitation, Marseille: 337 apartments, interior streets, roof with running track and nursery, on pilotis **[A]**.
- **Reze-les-Nantes. CORRECTION.** Gathered as 1948-1952. Wikipedia's Unite d'habitation article: "The Unite d'habitation of Nantes-Reze was completed in 1955." 1948-1952 is design; construction ran 1953-1955. Cut from the journey for space; the correction is recorded so it is not lost.
- **1952.** Commander of the Legion d'Honneur **[A]**. Builds Le Cabanon at Roquebrune, 3.66 metres square, sized to the Modulor **[A: Fondation Le Corbusier / UNESCO documentation]**.
- **November to December 1950.** Recruited to head a four-member team for the new Punjabi capital, replacing the original American planner **[A: Wikipedia, "Chandigarh"]**. **February 1951.** First visit **[A]**; engaged with the city until his death **[A]**. **March 1955.** High Court functional **[A]**. **1962.** Palace of Assembly completed **[A]**.
- **1950-1955.** Notre-Dame du Haut, Ronchamp **[A]**. **1953-1960.** Sainte Marie de La Tourette, Eveux: design from May 1953, construction 1956-1960 **[A, with the refinement supplied by verification]**. The cell-and-cloister logic returns to the Charterhouse of Ema, 1907; the journey states the return and leaves the reader to draw it.
- **1954.** Mill Owners' Association Building, Ahmedabad, with villas for the Sarabhai and Shodhan families **[A]**.
- **1949-1953. CORRECTION.** Casa Curutchet, La Plata, was gathered as "his only executed work in the Americas". False: the Carpenter Center at Harvard, 1962, is also his, and Wikipedia's Carpenter Center article says the two are the only Le Corbusier designs in the Americas. Casa Curutchet is his only executed work in **South** America.

## 8. 27 August 1965 and after

Dies 27 August 1965, aged 77, swimming in the Mediterranean off Roquebrune-Cap-Martin against his doctor's warning after a heart attack **[A]**. Buried in the hillside cemetery of Roquebrune beside Yvonne Gallis **[A]**, in a tomb said to be his own design, made in 1957 for her **[R: repeated in the biographies, e.g. Nicholas Fox Weber, *Le Corbusier: A Life*, 2008; self-authorship unconfirmed against any primary document here]**.

A recurring legend says he drowned deliberately, to "die in beauty" **[R: oral tradition among biographers and local guides; no contemporary document confirms intent, and the documented fact is only the doctors' warning]**. Printed as legend, not resolved.

- **1968.** Fondation Le Corbusier established: roughly 8,000 drawings, some 450 paintings, about 30 enamels, in the Maison La Roche **[A]**.
- **1985.** The Open Hand Monument at Chandigarh, conceived 1948, completed twenty years after his death: 26 metres, a 14-metre steel vane of 50 tons rotating in the wind **[A: Wikipedia, "Open Hand Monument"]**. Cut from the journey for space.
- **17 July 2016.** UNESCO inscribes seventeen works across seven countries as one transnational site **[A]**.
  - **CORRECTION.** The gathered entry placed the inscription at UNESCO headquarters in Paris. It was made at the 40th session of the World Heritage Committee, held in **Istanbul**. The journey pins it there, which produces the circuit the atlas cares about: the city he walked through in 1911 is the city where his work is canonized 105 years later.

## Quotations

Verified and carried into the journey file: "A house is a machine for living in."; "Architecture is the masterly, correct and magnificent play of masses brought together in light."; "The 'styles' are a lie."; "Modern life demands, and is waiting for, a new kind of plan, both for the house and the city." All *Vers une architecture* (1923), Etchells translation **[A]**. "The Despot is not a man. It is the Plan. The correct, realistic, exact plan.", *La Ville Radieuse* (1933) **[A]**. "The age of personal statues is gone." and "The city of Chandigarh is planned to human scale.", *The Edict of Chandigarh* (1959) **[A]**. "A hundred times I have thought: New York is a catastrophe...", *When the Cathedrals Were White* (1947) **[A]**. Three surviving only as English translations inside the Wikipedia article, footnotes 21, 33 and 34: the "horror of architecture" passage, the July 1916 letter to Perret, the reply to Schwob **[A as translations; French originals not fetched]**. The Jenger passage on raising people out of misery, via Wikiquote **[A as a secondary quotation]**.

- **CORRECTION.** "It is a question of building which is at the root of the social unrest of to-day: architecture or revolution." The words are verbatim Etchells, which spells it "to-day". The gathered citation called it the book's closing line. It is not: it sits in the argument of the final chapter, "Architecture or Revolution", and the actual closing lines are "Architecture or Revolution. / Revolution can be avoided."
- **REJECTED.** "Our epoch determines each day its style." Not Etchells. Every occurrence of "epoch" in the Etchells full text reads "Our own epoch is determining, day by day, its own style" (twice) or "Our epoch is fixing its own style day by day". The quoted form appears verbatim in the English Wikipedia article, in a spliced passage referenced to the Taylor & Francis 2017 reprint of *Towards an Architecture*, a different translation. The journey uses the Etchells wording and says so.
- **[R], carried with its attribution stated.** "Space and light and order. Those are the things that men need just as much as they need bread or a place to sleep." Attributed via the New York Times obituary of 28 August 1965; circulates only in this posthumous English form and is untraced to any work in his own hand.

## Honest gaps

1. **No primary archive was consulted.** Nothing from the Fondation Le Corbusier's document holdings was fetched; the Fondation appears only through published biography pages, at second hand.
2. **"Some eighty sketchbooks"** is repeated everywhere and verified nowhere here. Treated as canon, which this register licenses, but it is not evidence.
3. **Day-level dates on the Voyage d'Orient.** The city sequence is solid; the month assignments come from a secondary reconstruction, not from dated letters read here.
4. **The Ocampo hospitality of 1929**: the most attractive interlock in the dossier and the weakest supported in files actually opened. Left [R] in both artifacts.
5. **Yvonne Gallis** appears here as a marriage date, a summer at the Cabanon and a grave. No source consulted gives her a life. A gap in the sources, and now in the journey.
6. **Chandigarh's other authors.** Maxwell Fry, Jane Drew and Pierre Jeanneret were the other three of the four. The pool names none of them. The journey therefore says "a team of four" and under-credits them; a known distortion, not an editorial judgement.
7. **The Swiss Pavilion, the Cite de Refuge and the League of Nations competition** are attested but uncoordinated in the pool; omitted rather than pinned by guess.
