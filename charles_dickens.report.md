# Charles Dickens: the research report

*2026-08-23. Written from a verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock and afterlife lenses), already adversarially corrected before it reached this desk. Flags contradictions; does not adjudicate them.*

**Subject.** Charles John Huffam Dickens, b. 7 February 1812, Portsea; d. 9 June 1870, Gad's Hill Place, Higham, Kent; buried Poets' Corner, Westminster Abbey, 14 June 1870.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or standard chronology not confirmed against a fetched source this pass.

## The attested spine

**[A], all Letters Project unless noted.** The spine of record is the Charles Dickens Letters Project chronology (dickensletters.com/content/dickens-timeline), which this report follows wherever the dossier's lenses disagreed with it. The journey file carries that spine stop by stop with the source named on each stop rather than repeating it here; the birth entry reads verbatim, "(7 Feb.) Charles Dickens born at Mile End Terrace, Landport, Portsea". Every date this report has had to argue with is below under Corrections. Two [A] stops rest on something other than the Letters Project and say so: Eastern State Penitentiary, 8 March 1842 (the penitentiary's own history; Victorian Web), and the September 1860 Gad's Hill letter-burning (contemporary witness accounts in standard biography, against which nothing was found).

**[R], relayed and not fetched this pass.** Bayham Street 1822-23, 4 Gower Street North from December 1823, the Lant Street lodging (Forster, dated only to 1824) · the hulks, marshes and Dover road as recurring furniture of the fiction · Field Lane Ragged School September 1843 and the Burdett-Coutts link · Richmond, Washington and the Tyler meeting, Niagara · Preston January 1854 and *Hard Times* · the 600-pounds-a-year separation allowance.

## Corrections applied, by phase

**Procedural notes for the whole file.** No verified coordinate existed for the Beadnell house, Seymour's Islington death, or the Wellington Street office; rather than invent pins, each is folded into a neighbouring stop, stated in date_confidence. Day-level dates for Richmond, Washington and the Poe meeting are **assigned to order the itinerary and carry no authority**. Gad's Hill was occupied from June 1857, so "permanent home from 1860" is a narrowing, not the start of occupancy.

### Portsea and the Medway, 1812-1822

- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The house number is 1 or 13 Mile End Terrace depending which page of Portsmouth's Birthplace Museum you read. The journey carries neither.
- **Coordinate corrected.** Chronology lens had 50.7989, -1.0913: Guildhall Square, a kilometre south, and in fact the 2014 statue's site. The house (393 Old Commercial Road) is 50.807046, -1.087238 (Wikipedia), matching the geography lens: **50.8070, -1.0872**.

### The warehouse and the Marshalsea, 1822-1825

- **[A] Correction to the popular account.** Popular versions end the warehouse episode with the imprisonment; the Letters Project runs the employment into March or April 1825, so the boy worked at Warren's roughly ten months *after* his father walked out of prison.
- **[A] Quote modification, flagged.** Both autobiographical-fragment passages verify verbatim against Forster's *Life* (Gutenberg 25851). The second carries dashes in Forster around "a child of singular abilities... soon hurt, bodily or mentally"; the journey **regularises them to commas** and says so in quote_source, house style forbidding the em dash. A knowing change to a verbatim quote.
- **[A] Quote upgraded R to A.** Micawber's budget speech verified against Gutenberg 766 Ch. 12, one wording error fixed: "nineteen nineteen **and** six", not "nineteen nineteen six".

### Law, shorthand, the gallery, 1827-1836

- **[A] Date corrected.** Ellis and Blackmore. The chronology lens said March 1827; **that date is not in the cited source**, the Letters Project entry being undated within 1827. Wikipedia's "May 1827 to November 1828" is standard and matches the geography lens: journey **1827-05**.
- **Coordinate corrected.** Gray's Inn is 51.51944, -0.11222 (Wikipedia); chronology lens 51.5215, -0.1121, geography lens 51.522, -0.1132. Journey: **51.5194, -0.1122**.
- **[A], causal claim refused.** 20 April 1836, Robert Seymour dies by suicide amid disagreement over Pickwick's direction. The suicide is documented; **that Dickens drove him to it is contested and unproved**, and the journey asserts no cause.

### Doughty Street and the first fame, 1837-1841

- **Coordinate corrected, three-way.** 48 Doughty Street appears **three times with three different pairs** (51.5231/-0.1173, 51.5233/-0.1178, 51.523/-0.1174). Wikipedia gives 51.523921, -0.116902; journey unifies on **51.5239, -0.1169**, tenancy sharpened to 25 March 1837 to December 1839.
- **Contradiction, flagged.** Geography lens credits the house with "Oliver Twist and the end of Pickwick", afterlife lens with "Oliver Twist and Nicholas Nickleby". Both true, neither complete; stated in date_confidence, not chosen between.
- **Coordinate corrected.** Bowes: dossier 54.5122, -1.9808, about 2 km off the village. Wikipedia gives 54.516, -2.009. Journey: **54.5160, -2.0090**.
- **[R], attribution limited.** Wikipedia's Bowes article confirms the village as the Dotheboys Hall model and George Ashton Taylor's grave as Smike's, but **does not name William Shaw's Academy and gives no 1838 date**; that stays [R]. Bowes is County Durham today, Yorkshire's North Riding in 1838.
- **Reception legend, not staged.** The "Is Little Nell dead?" pier scene was flagged unverified by the dossier's own compilers and no contemporary account was found. Beyond sourcing, a placement problem: **Dickens was not on that pier.** The real reader mail sits in the Devonshire Terrace campa, the scene named as legend.

### America, 1842

- **[A] Quote verified.** "I hold this slow and daily tampering with the mysteries of the brain, to be immeasurably worse than any torture of the body." *American Notes* Ch. VII.
- **[A] Quote, citation corrected.** "that most hideous blot and foul disgrace—Slavery" was attributed by the dossier to a dedicatory epistle to Dr Channing. **There is no such dedication.** The phrase is verbatim in **Ch. III, "Boston"** (Gutenberg 675, lines 1088-1093). The journey carries the corrected citation and keeps the original dash, quote fields being verbatim.
- **Date corrected.** Niagara: dossier said "May 1842"; arrival was 26 April, stay about ten days into early May. Wikipedia confirms the Great Lakes leg but gives no day-level dates, so this rests on the standard itinerary and the journey says so. Horseshoe Falls (43.0779, -79.0747) is named as the alternative to the city pin.

### The Carol, Italy, the magazines, 1843-1850

- **Date corrected.** The geography lens dated the Peschiere tenancy "July 1844-1845". The family first took the Villa Bagnerello at Albaro, entering the Peschiere only at the end of September 1844 and staying to June 1845. Journey dates the stop **1844-09**.
- **Inaccessible, coordinates unconfirmed.** The Palazzo delle Peschiere and Villa Bagnerello pages both 404'd; *Pictures from Italy* is a stub. The Peschiere coordinates rest on the dossier alone, stated in date_confidence.
- **Founding year corrected.** Urania Cottage: dossier said 1846, Wikipedia gives 1847, the home opening that November, so the journey dates it 1847-11. The terminus needs care too: 1858 is when **Dickens** withdrew, not when the institution closed.

### Gad's Hill and the separation, 1851-1859

- **[A] The worst coordinate problem in the dossier.** Gad's Hill Place carries **three pairs across three lenses**: 51.3966/0.5011 (chronology, four entries, 5 km east), 51.4111/0.4578 (geography, correct), 51.3936/0.5219 (afterlife, 5.5 km east). Wikipedia gives 51.4112, 0.4579; **all four stops are unified on it.**
- **[A] Writing attribution corrected.** Fort House, Broadstairs (51.3598, 1.4450, confirmed). Two dossier claims fail: the tenancy ran "from the 1840s till 1852", not 1837-1859; and **the novel written there was David Copperfield, not Bleak House**, written at Tavistock House in 1852-53. The renaming came in the early twentieth century on a disputed attribution.
- **[A] Two publications un-inverted.** The dossier had the "Personal" statement in *Household Words* and "also circulated through The Times." The Letters Project reads "(7 and 12 June) publishes 'Personal' statement about it": **two dates**, and Wikipedia's Catherine Dickens article pins *Household Words* to 12 June, the newspapers first. **7 June is the newspaper date, 12 June the magazine, the reverse of the dossier.**

### The readings, Staplehurst, the end, 1860-1870

- **[A] Coordinate corrected.** Staplehurst, 9 June 1865 at 3:13 pm, Beult viaduct, confirmed by Wikipedia and railwaysarchive.co.uk. Chronology lens had 51.1594, 0.5647, 1.5 km short of the viaduct. Journey: **51.1692, 0.5802**, plus two verified details, that he lost his voice for a fortnight and died five years to the day after.
- **[A] Month corrected.** The second American tour: the dossier said 1867-12 while citing a source that says November. The Letters Project reads "(Nov.) begins American reading tour in Boston"; he sailed 9 November, landed on the 19th, first read 2 December. Journey dates the stop **1867-12-02** and states both. The just-under-20,000-pound profit is the Museum's estimate.
- **[A], primary not pulled.** The Royal Archives journal entry for 9 March 1870 was **not fetched**; the audience is uncontroversial, and the inscribed *Leaves from the Journal of Our Life in the Highlands* rests on the dossier.
- **[A] Internal contradiction resolved.** The chronology lens said he suffered a stroke and "dies the next day" while stamping the entry **1870-06-09**, the death date. That cannot stand. The afterlife lens has it right: collapse 8 June, death 9 June at about 6:10 pm, so the journey dates the stop **1870-06-08**. The time, the apoplexy reading and the vascular warning signs were **on no page fetched this pass**.
- **Contradiction, flagged.** Geography lens has him die "at his desk"; Wikipedia says "on a couch in the dining room". Incompatible. The journey follows the fetched source and names the conflict.

### The burial and after

- **[A] Mourner count corrected.** 14 June 1870, 9:30 am, Poets' Corner. The dossier said fourteen mourners; Westminster Abbey's commemoration page says "only twelve".
- **[A] and a complication the chronology lens omitted.** The Abbey page records the grave left open through mid-morning on 15 June for thousands of mourners and closed on 16 June, which complicates the strictly private funeral the will asked for. The journey writes both halves.
- **[A]** He wished burial at Rochester and a grave was prepared there before *The Times* led a campaign for the Abbey and the family gave way. sdrfoundation.org and medway.gov.uk, relayed, **no document fetched**. The stop is dated 1870-06-11, a placeholder between death and burial.
- **[A]** Portsmouth's Martin Jennings bronze, 7 February 2014, his 202nd birthday, about forty descendants present (ITV News; Atlas Obscura). Closes the birth stop's campa.
- **The Philadelphia statue is a mess and is NOT in the journey.** Coordinates wrong (39.9503/-75.2205 against Clark Park's 39.94767/-75.20983); year disputed (1897 per the park article, not 1901, the Association for Public Art page **404'd**); creation 1890 or 1891, predating the 1893 Exposition; the "world's first" and "only statue with his own character" claims unsourceable. **And Dickens was never there.**
- **[A]** T. P. James's spirit-pen completion of *Edwin Drood*, Brattleboro, Vermont, 1873, corroborated across Wikipedia, the LOC catalogue (loc.gov/item/06026417) and VTDigger. Dickens was three years dead and never in Brattleboro, so it is no stop; it closes the death campa in a sentence.
- **[A]** Charles Dickens Museum: demolition threat 1923, freehold bought by the Dickens Fellowship 1924, opened 1925. The date **9 June 1925, his death anniversary**, is only on the museum's centenary page; kept attributed. **[R]** The 3.1 million pound Heritage Fund restoration, approximately 2012; year unpinned, not in the journey.
- **[R], useful for the Spanish edition.** John Stone (University of Barcelona) identified the earliest known direct English-to-Spanish Dickens translation: "A Madman's Manuscript," a *Pickwick* chapter, in the Cadiz periodical *El Pasatiempo*, 1844, probably by William Macpherson; an earlier 1847 text came second hand from French. **Not in the dossier; found independently at verification.** In the Pickwick stop's suggested_refs.
- **[A], left unreconciled.** "Dickensian" entered English as an adjective for institutional neglect and extreme wealth-and-poverty contrast, detached from his actual politics: he subscribed to the pro-flogging Eyre Defence Committee while opposing slavery outright. The journey states both halves.

## Interlocks with the existing corpus

**Named in a campa**, each on a personal relation, never shared geography:

- **edgar_allan_poe**. Poe requested and got an audience at the United States Hotel, Philadelphia, March 1842; Dickens then tried and failed to place his work with an English publisher. **[R]**: the meeting is **in neither edgar_allan_poe.journey.json nor Dickens's own dossier**, which never mentions Poe. Philadelphia pin 39.953, -75.165 inherited byte-identical from Poe's file.
- **charles_darwin** and **john_ruskin**. The 1865-66 Eyre controversy: Dickens subscribed to the Eyre Defence and Aid Committee with Carlyle, Ruskin, Kingsley and Tennyson, against the Jamaica Committee of Mill, Darwin and Huxley. Direct opposition on a named question, not co-location. **[R]**, dossier only. **Gap: john_ruskin.journey.json has nothing on Eyre, Jamaica, or Dickens.**
- **victor_hugo**. The call at Hugo's Place Royale apartment, Paris winter 1846-47. **[R]** and doubly flagged: **not in the dossier at all**, standard biography on my own knowledge, not fetched. If the operator wants it out it is one clause in the Paris campa.
- **mesmer**. Dickens practised animal magnetism on Augusta de la Rue at Genoa: practice lineage, not a meeting or citation, which the interlock lens left to the writer. **Judgement made: included**, one clause naming Mesmer as the practice's origin, plus a suggested_ref. **mesmer.journey.json has no reference to Dickens**, correct and must stay so: he died in 1815 and cannot reach forward.

**Found and deliberately not named:**

- **hans_christian_andersen**. Gore House 1847 and the five-week Gad's Hill stay of summer 1857 (invited for a fortnight), after which the friendship curdled and Dickens stopped answering. Both are **already stops on Andersen's own file**: he did the travelling. A Dickens-side 1857 stop would be defensible but duplicates a scene told from the traveller's end.
- **bulwer_lytton**. Co-founder with Dickens of the Guild of Literature and Art (1854), donating land beside Knebworth while Dickens performed the fundraising theatricals. Documented both ways, **cut only for length**, and the first thing to restore if a stop is freed.
- **john_everett_millais**. Both directions: the *Household Words* leader of 15 June 1850, "Old Lamps for New Ones," calling *Christ in the House of His Parents* "mean, odious, repulsive, and revolting"; then a friendly December 1864 letter over a pension for John Leech's widow. **Cut for length**, and restorable in one stop.
- **edward_burne_jones**, **max_muller**, **halley**, **dirac**: Westminster Abbey co-location only, not named per the rule. **balzac**: QUEUE.md pairs the two as literature-gap entries, editorial and not biographical, since **they never met**. **mark_twain**: not in the pool, a lead only. Twain attended a Dickens reading in New York on 31 December 1867; if it holds it is **Twain's stop on his own file**, appearing here at most as a suggested_ref.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Westminster Abbey **51.4993, -0.1273** (charles_darwin, bulwer_lytton, edward_burne_jones, max_muller) · Paris **48.8566, 2.3522** · Philadelphia **39.953, -75.165** (edgar_allan_poe).

**Pin conflict the operator must rule on.** The interlock lens reports **Gad's Hill Place at 51.4053, 0.4318** in hans_christian_andersen.journey.json and john_everett_millais.journey.json; the pool's Wikipedia-sourced correction is **51.4112, 0.4579**, about 3 km off. This file uses the corrected value, byte-identical inheritance existing to stop drift on shared hubs rather than to carry a known error into the subject's own house. **Andersen's and Millais's stops therefore now sit apart from Dickens's house on the map.** Those two files should be corrected; I have not touched them.

## Honest gaps

- **Ellen Ternan and Georgina Hogarth have no journey files.** The will of 12 May 1869, codicil 2 June 1870, leaves Ternan 1,000 pounds and Georgina Hogarth 8,000 plus his papers; a grep found neither name, so this branch has **no corpus interlock to inherit**. **Catherine Dickens likewise has no file**: twenty-two years, ten children, and the "Personal" statement are told here entirely from his end.
- **No connection to Buenos Aires, the Kaaba, or the Temple Mount.** His travel reaches Britain, France, Italy, Switzerland, the United States and Canada. A gap, not forced.
- **Coordinates on the dossier's word, not re-fetched:** Ordnance Terrace, Hungerford Stairs (gone under Charing Cross, so the pin is approximate to a vanished site), Lant Street, Wellington House Academy, Doctors' Commons, Furnival's Inn, Devonshire Terrace, Tavistock House, Urania Cottage, the Peschiere, Rosemont, St James's Hall, Free Trade Hall, Field Lane, Tremont Temple.
