# John Brown (1800-1859): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `john_brown.journey.json`. Slug: `john_brown`. Register: national mythology: the canon is true. Calendar: gregorian. 45 stops, 9 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source recollection. *Contradiction* means two reachable sources disagree and the disagreement is recorded, not adjudicated. Every hedge below has a matching `date_confidence` field in the journey file.

---

## 0. Corrections applied before writing

Thirteen coordinate corrections came with the pool and all were applied; the corrected pins are in the journey file, stop by stop. Worst cases: Kennedy Farm was out by ~12 km, landing near Antietam, now 39.3797, -77.7156. John Brown's Fort was out by ~740 m onto the building's 1909-1968 Camp Hill position, one wrong pair reused four times across the pool, now 39.32304, -77.72956. North Elba carried three mutually inconsistent values, all wrong, now 44.2520, -73.9714 at every use; Osawatomie carried two, now 38.4994, -94.9589. New Richmond and Peterboro were out by ~8.5 km each, Pottawatomie Creek and Black Jack by ~5.6 km, the Harpers Ferry armory by ~700 m onto the wrong landform.

Two non-geographic corrections also carried through. Perkins & Brown was formed 1844 at Akron and the Springfield relocation is 1846, where the pool merged them under one date. And 18 men entered the town, out of a company of 21 plus Brown, not "a force of twenty-one seizing the armory."

## 1. Connecticut and the Western Reserve, 1800-1825

- Born 9 May 1800 at Torrington to Owen Brown, tanner, and Ruth Mills Brown; the birthplace house burned in a 1918 chimney fire, leaving a marker and a clearing. **[A: Torrington Historical Society, confirmed verbatim; Wikipedia; Brown's 1857 letter to Henry L. Stearns]**
- Hudson, Ohio, from 1805; Ruth Mills Brown dies December 1808, Brown aged eight. **[A]** That he never got over it is his own retrospect at fifty-seven about age eight, his account rather than an independent finding.
- Marries Dianthe Lusk, 1820, Hudson **[A to the year; no day in the pool]**; John Brown Jr. born 25 July 1821. **[A]**
- Raised a Master Mason, Hudson Lodge No. 68, 11 May 1824. **[A: Grand Lodge of BC & Yukon biography, citing lodge records]** *Over-specification removed:* the dossier had him taking all three degrees that year, but only the Master Mason raising is recorded, with no dates for Entered Apprentice or Fellow Craft. The withdrawal follows the William Morgan affair of 1826.

## 2. Pennsylvania, 1825-1835

- New Richmond, Crawford County: tannery built 1825, worked to 1835, correcting the pool's 1826-1835. Church organized, school opened, postmaster under John Quincy Adams. **[A: Wikipedia; National Archives *Prologue*, Spring 2011, verbatim on the postmastership]**
- Sheltering of freedom-seekers at the tannery: **[R]**, local tradition with no contemporary document naming anyone. Explicitly rejected: the figure of ~2,500 people assisted, on the Wikipedia site article, is an unsourced round number and appears nowhere in the file.
- Dianthe Lusk Brown dies 1832 after the birth and death of an infant son **[A to the year]**; Brown marries Mary Ann Day, about seventeen, 14 July 1833, probably at New Richmond. **[A as to date; the place is "probably" in the pool and is hedged]**

## 3. Ruin in Ohio, 1836-1844

- Franklin Mills (Kent) from 1836; Franklin Land Company, roughly 700 acres. **[A for the move; R for the acreage and the brief jailing, from *Prologue* citing Paul Finkelman]** Federal bankruptcy 28 September 1842. **[A]**
- Elijah P. Lovejoy killed at Alton, 7 November 1837. **[A]** **The vow at the Hudson memorial meeting: [R]**, resting on recollection recorded decades later with no contemporaneous transcript. The most-quoted moment of Brown's early life has no primary source, and the campa says so.
- Four children (Sarah I, Charles, Peter, Austin) dead of dysentery within weeks, 1843. **[A: family genealogical and NPS records via the dossier]** The pool gives the location only as "Ohio (Akron area)"; the pin is approximate.
- Perkins & Brown formed by 1844, Akron. **[A: *Prologue*, "By 1844, he was raising sheep with a wealthy business partner in Akron"]** Brown rents 514 Diagonal Road, opposite the Perkins Stone Mansion, 1844-1854. **[A: summithistory.org]**

## 4. Springfield, England, and North Elba, 1846-1851

- 1846: the wool operation moves to Springfield, bringing contacts with the city's Black community, abolitionist clergy, and Frederick Douglass. **[A]** No date in the pool for the Douglass visit; *Life and Times* leaves it undated, and 1847 is conventional rather than attested.
- English wool voyage 1849/1850, a failure; the whole enterprise reckoned at roughly $40,000 in losses. **[A: dossier citing Summit County material; *Prologue*]** No sailing date exists.
- 244 acres at $1/acre at North Elba, near Gerrit Smith's grants to Black New Yorkers ("Timbuctoo"). **[A for purchase and acreage]** *Contradiction:* 1848 vs 1849. The grants and the arrangement with Smith are **[A]**; that Brown "meets repeatedly" with him at Peterboro 1848-1858 is **[R]** and stays so.
- League of Gileadites, 15 January 1851. **[A: springfieldmuseums.org]** *Transmission caveat:* the "Words of Advice" manuscript was first printed years later and survives through Brown's biographers, not a contemporary imprint.

## 5. Kansas Territory, 1855-1856

- Sons migrate 1855; Brown follows that year with arms. **[A]**
- Wakarusa War, near Lawrence, December 1855. **[A to the month]** Brown's specific part comes from general Kansas historiography, not from a document placing him at an hour.
- Sack of Lawrence, 21 May 1856, and the caning of Sumner, 22 May 1856. **[A]** Written into the Pottawatomie campa as immediate cause and deliberately given no stops: Brown was in neither place.
- Pottawatomie Creek, night of 24-25 May 1856. Five killed with broadswords: James P. Doyle, William Doyle, Drury Doyle, Allen Wilkinson, William Sherman. **[A: Wikipedia; PBS]** *Contradiction flagged, not resolved:* Brown's command responsibility is not seriously disputed, but which men wielded the blades is, and Brown never admitted striking a blow. PBS has him firing a final shot into the wounded James Doyle; other accounts differ. Nothing in the pool supports the common claim that none of the five owned slaves, so it is not written.
- **Battle of Black Jack, 2 June 1856. Tag upgraded [R] to [A].** Pate offered conditional surrender, Brown refused, and after roughly three hours Pate and 28 men were taken. Only troop counts on Brown's side remain contested.
- Osawatomie, 30 August 1856. Frederick Brown shot dead by Martin White, the town burned, the epithet fixed. **[A]** The size of the attacking force varies and is not asserted.

## 6. Eastern money and Chatham, 1857-1858

- Boston, January 1857: Sanborn brings Brown east and introduces him to George L. Stearns. **[A: Tufts, "John Brown and the Secret Six"]** "Secret Six" is a retrospective label, not in use at this date. Days later at Medford, Mary Stearns urges her husband to sell the estate and their eleven-year-old son gives Brown his savings. **[A]** Exact day unrecorded.
- St. Catharines, April 1858: first documented meeting with Harriet Tubman; Brown calls her "General Tubman." **[A: NPS]** Pin inherited byte-identical from `harriet_tubman.journey.json`: 43.1594, -79.2467. The quote is the Wendell Phillips recollection in Bradford (1869), the Tubman file's own source; the `quote_source` names the Phillips introduction as its setting, not the St. Catharines meeting.
- Chatham, May 1858: the Provisional Constitution adopted, Brown elected commander in chief. **[A]** *Contradiction flagged:* Wikipedia dates it 10 May, the dossier 8 May, most accounts have it sitting 8-10 May, so the file uses month precision rather than picking a day. The First Baptist Church is inherited as a `suggested_ref` from the Tubman file.

## 7. The Missouri raid and the flight north, 1858-1859

- 20-21 December 1858: raid into Vernon County, Missouri; eleven people freed; slaveholder David Cruise shot dead by one of Brown's men. **[A]** Identifying the two properties as the Cruise and Larue farms, and the finer sequence of the night, is **[R]** in the pool.
- Grinnell, Iowa, 25 February 1859: the party housed and resupplied by Josiah Grinnell and local ministers. **[A: PBS]**
- Chicago, 9 March 1859: Allan Pinkerton raises funds and arranges transport onward. **[A: PBS]**
- Detroit, 12 March 1859: thirteen people, including an infant born on the road, put aboard a ferry into Canada after eighty-two days and more than a thousand miles. **[A: PBS]** Note the arithmetic the sources carry: eleven freed in December, thirteen aboard in March.

## 8. Harpers Ferry, October 1859

- 3 July 1859: the Kennedy Farm rented under the alias Isaac Smith. **[A: NPS]** Distance corrected from the pool's "about seven miles" to about five, the NPS figure.
- August 1859, near Chambersburg: Douglass refuses, calling the town a "perfect steel-trap"; Shields Green goes with Brown. **[R as to the day]** Attested only through Douglass's own recollection in *Life and Times*, which is undated.
- 16 October, 8:00 pm: Brown leads 18 men out; Owen Brown, Barclay Coppoc, and Francis Merriam stay as rear guard. By 10:00 pm both bridges, the armory, the arsenal, and Hall's Island rifle works are held; Lewis Washington and John Allstadt seized after midnight. **[A: NPS, "John Brown's Raid"]**
- 17 October: Heyward Shepherd, a free Black baggage master, shot near the station, the first person killed by the raid. Mayor Fontaine Beckham killed; the crowd extrajudicially kills the captured raider William Thompson; Watson and Oliver Brown mortally wounded. **[A: general Harpers Ferry historiography]**
- 18 October, 7:00 am: Marines under Robert E. Lee, with J. E. B. Stuart as aide, storm the engine house; Israel Green wounds and captures Brown; Jeremiah Anderson and Dauphin Thompson killed. **[A]** Later that day, the interview with Senator James Mason, source of the "great wrong against God and humanity" statement. **[A: famous-trials.com]**

## 9. Trial and execution, October to December 1859

Chronology corrected. The dossier's "trial proceeds from October 25" is wrong, and the error is common. Per famous-trials.com: 25 Oct, preliminary examination before magistrates, not the trial; 26 Oct, indictment and jury selection; 27 Oct, testimony opens; 31 Oct, verdict; 2 Nov, allocution and sentence; 2 Dec, execution. Judge Richard Parker presided; prosecution Andrew Hunter and Charles B. Harding, where the dossier named Hunter alone. **[A]**

- The allocution, 2 November. *Contradiction flagged and preserved, never blended.* Two transmission lines exist: Drew, *The John Brown Invasion* (1860), p. 82, reading "mingle my blood further" and ending "I say let it be done"; and Sanborn p. 585 with famous-trials.com, ending "I submit; so let it be done!" The pool's text had drifted to "farther," which no traced text supports. The file quotes a different, undisputed sentence and records both lines.
- The 8 November letter to Mary and the children. *Citation date corrected:* the pool dated it "late Nov. 1859," but Sanborn pp. 585-86 prints it headed "Charlestown, Jefferson County, Va., Nov. 8, 1859," the first letter home after conviction. The quotation itself is verbatim.
- 1 December: Mary Ann Day Brown's final visit. **[A as to visit and date]** The supper, the duration, and the conversation come from press reports and family recollection, and vary.
- 2 December: the final note ("the crimes of this guilty land will never be purged away but with blood"), authentic, Sanborn p. 518, confirmed verbatim in the archive.org OCR where it appears twice. **[A]** Execution under 1,500 soldiers ordered by Governor Wise, no civilians within hearing; Jackson with VMI cadets; Booth with the Richmond Grays. **[A: PBS, every element confirmed]** The clock time of the drop varies and is not asserted.
- 8 December: buried at North Elba in front of the house, near the boulder. **[A: parks.ny.gov]**

## 10. Afterlife

- Watson Brown's remains. **Tag upgraded [R] to [A]**, documented rather than tradition: Winchester Medical College took the body after his death on 19 October 1859, prepared it as an anatomical specimen and displayed it labelled as John Brown's son; an army surgeon recovered it when Union forces entered Winchester in March 1862, and it was reburied at North Elba in October 1882.
- 30 August 1899: ten raiders, Oliver Brown among them, reburied at North Elba in one town-donated coffin, on the Osawatomie anniversary. **[A]** *Contradiction:* an older summary says twelve, parks.ny.gov says "several." Ten has the best support; the variants are recorded.
- State acquisition of the farm. *Contradiction, resolved against the pool's preference:* the Historic Site article gives 1896, the biography article 1895, and 1896 is the better figure.
- John Brown's Fort, four addresses: sold by the B&O in 1891 and shipped to Chicago for the 1893 Columbian Exposition, where the exhibit failed; brought home free by the railroad through Kate Field's campaign and rebuilt on the Murphy Farm, November 1895; onto the Storer College campus in 1909; moved by the NPS in 1968 to within 150 feet of its original site. **[A]** *Contradiction:* the Fort article carries both 1968 and 1969, the Storer College article states 1909 through 1968, and 1968 is used. The Chicago episode is written inside the Harpers Ferry stop, at Brown's own pin: he was never at the exposition.
- 1931: the Heyward Shepherd Memorial dedicated by the UDC and the Sons of Confederate Veterans, framing Shepherd as an example of Black fidelity to slaveholders, after Storer's president had warned it could provoke "unpleasant racial feeling." The NAACP counter-tablet drafted under Du Bois was refused by Storer as "too militant" and installed in 2006. **[A]**
- The last-kiss legend, from Edward H. House's *New-York Daily Tribune* account of 5 December 1859, fabricated as the Charles Town jailer's testimony established by 1885, after Hovenden's 1884 painting drew scrutiny to it. Ransom, Currier & Ives, and Hovenden all painted or printed the scene between 1863 and 1884. **[A: graphicarts.princeton.edu]** Written as a stop at Charles Town, where the alleged event and the debunking both belong; the painters' studios are not staged as scenes.
- Osborne P. Anderson, the only Black raider to escape, published *A Voice from Harper's Ferry* (1861), describing local enslaved men taking up arms with Brown. **[A]** A `suggested_ref`, not a stop. McBride's *The Good Lord Bird* (2013) and its adaptation are not in the file: reception with no place attached to Brown.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

John Brown is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Relations in the pool not rooted in Brown's own life are not silently deleted; they are named here with the direction caught. **All three are FORWARD, which the exception file never licenses.** None is written into the journey file, and none should be without a ruling.

1. **Abraham Lincoln, Cooper Union, 27 February 1860. FORWARD.** The lens proposes the address was shaped by Republicans needing to disavow Harpers Ferry. It postdates Brown's death by nearly three months, and `abraham_lincoln.journey.json` names neither Brown nor the raid, a gap in *that* file.
2. **James Montgomery and the Combahee River Raid, June 1863. FORWARD.** `harriet_tubman.journey.json` calls Montgomery a man out of Kansas and out of John Brown's war: Brown's network persisting four years past his execution, and Tubman's scene.
3. **Douglass's 1868 letter measuring Tubman against Brown. FORWARD.** A posthumous comparison already on the Tubman file.

Two further items fell under the ordinary stop-ownership rule and need no ruling. Victor Hugo's open letter of 2 December 1859 is one-directional engagement, real and citable, so it rides as a `suggested_ref` and source on Brown's own gallows stop, with the pool's garbled wording corrected ("There is something more terrible than Cain slaying Abel: It is Washington slaying Spartacus!") and its framing corrected: the letter is dated 2 December and reached the London News in the days following, not on the morning of the execution. Hugo's Guernsey scene is not staged inside Brown's file, and `victor_hugo.journey.json` has no stop for December 1859 at all, jumping from August 1859 to 1861. The Kansas-Nebraska Act as shared root cause with Lincoln's Peoria speech is causation, not a relation between two people.

## Honest gaps

- No Frederick Douglass journey file exists. Springfield, the Rochester lodging, and the Chambersburg refusal hang off Brown's file with no counterpart: the most conspicuous absence this research turned up. No file contains Thoreau, Emerson, or John Brown Jr. either, and Thoreau's "A Plea for Captain John Brown" and Emerson's "new saint" remark were not in the quote pool.
- The Hudson vow has no primary source. The Springfield Douglass meeting is undated. The Chambersburg meeting is single-source, Douglass recalling himself thirty-three years later.
- No day is fixed for the 1820 marriage, Dianthe's 1832 death, the 1836 Franklin Mills move, the 1843 deaths, the 1844 partnership, the 1846 Springfield move, the 1849 North Elba purchase, the English voyage, the Medford dinner, or the 1931 Shepherd dedication; all default to the first of month or year. The 1843 deaths also have no location finer than "Ohio (Akron area)."
- Brown's exact physical role at Pottawatomie is not recoverable from the pool, and probably not from anywhere.
- No scholarly monograph was in the pool: Oates, Reynolds, Villard, and Du Bois were all unavailable, which is this report's largest single limitation.
