# William Blake — report

**Sources.** Alexander Gilchrist's *Life of William Blake* (1863, the foundational biography, drawing on Catherine Blake, Samuel Palmer, and other direct witnesses) anchors nearly every visionary anecdote: the head of God at the window, the tree of angels at Peckham Rye, Robert's spirit clapping its hands for joy, the deathbed singing as relayed by George Richmond. G. E. Bentley Jr.'s *Blake Records* and *The Stranger from Paradise* supply documentary dates (apprenticeship indenture, Royal Academy registration, the 1803 indictment). Blake's own letters to Flaxman and Butts, and the texts of *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell*, *Milton*, *Jerusalem*, and the *Songs*, are quoted directly from the William Blake Archive wherever the canon puts words in his mouth. West Sussex Record Office and Tate/Britannica accounts cover the Scofield affray and Chichester trial; Cunningham's *Lives of the Most Eminent British Painters* (1830) is the source for the fairy's-funeral anecdote, secondhand but canonical to Blake studies. Job and Dante commission dates come from the Blake Archive and Tate.

**Judgment calls.** Childhood visions (God at the window, the Peckham Rye tree) have no fixed calendar date in any source — I placed them at plausible ages (4 and 8) within Blake's known childhood years and marked them `traditional`. The Westminster Abbey "vision of Christ and the Apostles" and the schoolboy quarrel are undated in the record; I distributed them across the 1774–1776 Abbey years to avoid stacking everything on one day. The fairy's-funeral date is unfixed within 1800–1803; I set it just after the fourfold-vision letter (Nov. 1802) to keep segment order clean — a small time-fold, not a canon claim. Blake's own account of the Scofield scuffle survives in a letter to Butts, but I could not verify exact wording with confidence, so that stop carries no quote field rather than risk a fabricated line.

**Gaps / time-folds.** Six Lambeth prophetic books (*Marriage*, *Visions*, *America*, *Songs of Experience*/*Urizen*, *Europe*, *The Four Zoas*) are compressed into single-year stops though composition and etching often ran across two or three years each; treat their dates as "the book arrives," not a single day's labor. *Milton* and *Jerusalem* are each given one narrative stop though both were worked on for over a decade spanning Felpham and South Molton Street.

**Five richest episodes.** (1) The head of God at the four-year-old's window. (2) Robert's spirit rising through the ceiling "clapping its hands for joy," then returning a year later to teach illuminated printing in a dream. (3) Milton's shade entering Blake's left foot at Felpham, the vision that structures the whole epic *Milton*. (4) The fairy's funeral on a rose-leaf in the Felpham garden. (5) The last day: the unfinished portrait of Catherine ("you have ever been an angel to me") and the death "singing of the things he saw in Heaven."

**Connections to the atlas.** Direct edge to **Dante** — Blake's 102 Divine Comedy watercolours, worked to his last day, are the atlas's most literal handshake between two journeys. Three prior journeys already cite Blake in passing (`jung`, `moses`, `ohiggins`) via his art or his name; this file gives that citation a home. The register — "the canon is true," visions narrated as plain fact — sits beside `blavatsky` and `steiner` as the atlas's other descriptive-cosmology entries, though Blake's mythology (Albion, Urizen, Los, Orc) is authored poetry rather than a claimed esoteric doctrine, closer in kind to `joan_of_arc`'s voices than to a lodge initiation. Bunhill Fields, his burial ground, is a small stone the atlas can return to.
