# Miguel de Cervantes Saavedra (1547-1616), research report
*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `miguel_de_cervantes.journey.json`. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Calendar: gregorian, section 8.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition not confirmed against a fetched source this pass. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**.

## 0. The shape of the evidence

Cervantes is documented almost entirely by **administrative paper he generated as a debtor's son, a soldier, a slave, a commissioner, and a defendant**: a baptismal register, an arrest warrant, muster rolls, twelve depositions taken in Algiers, wheat requisitions, two prison orders, a homicide file, a burial entry. No letters, no diary, no manuscript of any of his books in his hand. **The best-documented years are the ones in which he was in trouble.** The years in which he wrote Don Quixote are the thinnest, and the document that fixes his Valladolid address is a murder investigation.

Two consequences. **His prologues are primary sources of a particular kind**: they hold the only physical description of him that exists, and they are polemic. Quoted verbatim as [A], treated as testimony rather than fact. And **he commissioned his own testimonial**, the *Información de Argel* (1580). **Every escape narrative in the Algiers segment rests on it and it was not read in the original this pass**, the largest evidential weakness in the file.

## 1. Origins and the warrant (1547-1569)

- **[A, parish register of Santa María la Mayor; en.wikipedia; Cervantes Project (TAMU)]** Baptized 9 October 1547, son of Rodrigo de Cervantes, a *cirujano sangrador* or surgeon-barber ranked below the university physicians, and Leonor de Cortinas. Hidalgo status was claimed by the family and confirmed by no paper.
- **[R]** The birth date of **29 September** is a back-formation from the name Miguel and the feast of Saint Michael. **No birth record exists**; the file carries the baptism.
- **[A, en.wikipedia, dating it "October 1553 to April 1554"]** Rodrigo jailed for debt at Valladolid. **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED:** the dossier's geography lens dated this **1551**, its chronology lens **1553**; es.wikipedia dates the family's *move* c. 1551 with the jailing following. Stop dated 1553-10-01, split stated in `date_confidence`.
- **[A/R, es.wikipedia: "1556: Father moves to Córdoba to claim inheritance and escape creditors"]** Córdoba c. 1556-1564; the dossier's range starting 1553 is **wrong at the front end**, corrected. Seville c. 1564-1566 is **[R]**.
- **[R, and it stays R]** **Jesuit schooling** at Córdoba or Seville rests on a schoolroom scene in *El coloquio de los perros* and nothing else; **no register at either college carries his name**. **HONEST GAP:** no documented formal education of any kind anywhere.
- **[A, Cervantes Project]** Pupil in Madrid of **Juan López de Hoyos**, who in the 1569 memorial volume for Queen Isabel de Valois calls him "nuestro caro y amado discípulo" and prints four of his poems: **first appearance of the name in print**. The school's location is unknown, so the stop uses the canonical Madrid pin.
- **[A as a document, R as an identification, en.wikipedia]** Royal warrant of **15 September 1569** for a Miguel de Cervantes who wounded **Antonio de Sigura**; sentence, public amputation of the right hand and ten years' banishment. **FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED:** most biographers identify the fugitive with the writer, but **the name is not unique and the identification cannot be proved**. Carried with the uncertainty in `date_confidence`.

## 2. Italy and the fleet (1569-1575)

- **[A, Cervantes Project; en.wikipedia]** Rome, chamber servant to **Giulio Acquaviva**, made cardinal 1570.
- **[A/R, UNIT ATTRIBUTION CONTRADICTED]** Enlisted 1570 at Naples. es.wikipedia: company of **Diego de Urbina**, tercio of **Miguel de Moncada**. en.wikipedia: "Tercio of Sicily under the **Marqués de Santa Cruz**." Never reconciled in anything reachable; the file follows the Spanish reading **and names the English one inside the campa** rather than choosing silently.
- **[A, en.wikipedia; "Battle of Lepanto"; corroborated by his own 1613 preface]** **Lepanto, 7 October 1571**, on the galley **Marquesa**. Feverish, refused to stay below, given twelve men at the skiff. Three arquebus wounds, two chest, one permanently disabling the **left** hand. **PERSISTENT ERROR FLAGGED:** the hand was **maimed, not amputated**. Then Messina, hospital into spring 1572, extra pay, and a conduct certificate he presents to officials for the rest of his life.
- **[R]** Post-Lepanto Greek service, **Corfu, Navarino, Modon** (1572), under Lope de Figueroa among others. Not independently fetched.
- **[A/R, DATE CORRECTED]** **Tunis and La Goleta, 1573.** The dossier's "1573-1574" is wrong: the 1574 events there are the **Ottoman recapture**, which he had no part in; es.wikipedia has him in 1574-1575 in Sicily, Sardinia, Genoa, Lombardy, Naples. He served **four more years after Lepanto**, and that the battle ended his soldiering is not in the record.

## 3. Algiers (1575-1580)

- **[A, en.wikipedia: capture "approaching Barcelona on 26 September" 1575]** Taken with his brother **Rodrigo** aboard the galley **El Sol** by corsairs under **Arnaut Mamí**, carrying letters of recommendation from **Don Juan of Austria** and the **Duke of Sessa**; the captors read them and priced him high.
- **[A]** Five years captive, held by **Dali Mamí**, later by **Hasan Pasha**. Four escape attempts. Rodrigo ransomed 1577; Miguel stays.
- **[R, the 1580 depositions, not independently fetched]** The attempts: **1576**, overland toward Oran, the hired guide abandons them; **1577**, the coastal cave, betrayed by **El Dorador**; **1578**, a letter carried overland to Oran, the messenger impaled (thinner than the others, folded into the bagno stop rather than given a scene); **1579**, sixty captives and a frigate funded by **Onofre Exarque**, betrayed by the Dominican **Juan Blanco de Paz**.
- **[A, es.wikipedia]** Ransomed **19 September 1580** for **500 gold escudos** raised by the Trinitarians **Fray Juan Gil** and **Fray Antón de la Bella** out of his mother's savings, his sister Andrea's dowry money, alms, and merchants' credit.
- **[A, DATE CORRECTED]** Landed at **Denia, 24 October 1580** (es.wikipedia); the dossier's 27 October is three days out. **Canonical Denia pin 38.842, 0.1057 inherited byte-identical** from `maslama_al_qurtubi.journey.json`.
- **OPEN QUESTION, LEFT OPEN.** At each betrayal he told the pasha the plan was his alone and named nobody, and was not executed. **No source explains why Hasan Pasha spared him.** The campa states the fact and supplies no motive.

## 4. Petitioner and commissioner (1580-1600)

- **[A]** Madrid late 1580; files the *Información*; no durable post follows, and a later request for a post in the Indies is refused. **[R]** An errand to **Oran** and presence at the **Lisbon** court, both 1581, neither independently fetched.
- **[A, DATE AND MOTHER'S NAME BOTH CORRECTED, TAG RAISED]** Daughter **Isabel** baptized at **Santos Justo y Pastor**, Madrid, **9 April 1584**, born the month before to **Ana de Villafranca y Rojas** (1563-1598), wife of the tavernkeeper Alonso Rodríguez. The dossier had "November 1584" and "Ana Franca de Rojas", a superseded name form; the record located by **Emilio Maganto Pavón** settles it, so the item goes [R] to **[A]**.
- **[A, en.wikipedia; "Esquivias"]** Marries **Catalina de Salazar y Palacios** at Esquivias, December 1584; he thirty-seven, she about nineteen. **No children of the marriage.** **[A]** *La Galatea*, Alcalá, 1585, its second part promised across thirty-one years and never written; Rodrigo dies the same year.
- **[A, en.wikipedia]** From 1587, royal **commissioner of provisions** in Andalusia under **Antonio de Guevara**. **[R]** Excommunications for seizing church grain, Écija among other towns. **[A]** Jailed briefly at **Castro del Río**, 1592, by the corregidor of Écija: **accusation in the file, no conviction**. **[R]** Poetry prize at Zaragoza, 1595: too thin for a stop, dropped for length, recorded here.
- **[R]** 1594 commission for tax arrears in **Granada**, funds deposited with the Seville banker **Simón Freire de Lima**. **[R, and the tag matters]** 1597, held in the **royal jail of Seville** by order of Judge **Gaspar de Vallejo** after Freire's bank failed. **No documented conviction for embezzlement.** The difference between "held while accounts were examined for a shortfall he did not cause" and "jailed for stealing" is his whole financial reputation, and general sources blur it constantly.
- **[R, popular and unprovable]** The Part 1 prologue says the book was "begotten in a prison". Four centuries of readers have attached that to Seville 1597 **with no evidence it names that building**; the campa says so.

## 5. Valladolid and the book (1603-1606)

- **[A, COORDINATES AND STREET NAME CORRECTED]** The household follows the court to Valladolid, an upper floor beside the slaughterhouse. The street is the **Calle del Rastro**, **not "Rastro de los Carneros"**, and the house is **41.64833, -4.72722** per es.wikipedia "Casa de Cervantes (Valladolid)"; the dossier's 41.6558, -4.7278 is **830 m off** and was discarded.
- **[A, en.wikipedia]** **Don Quixote Part 1**, Madrid, January 1605, printed by **Juan de la Cuesta** for **Francisco de Robles**. Immediate success, immediately pirated in Lisbon and Valencia. He had sold the rights outright.
- **[A, DEATH DETAIL CORRECTED, TAG RAISED FROM R]** **27 June 1605**: **Gaspar de Ezpeleta** mortally wounded in the street outside the building, carried into **an adjoining dwelling in the same building, not the Cervantes lodging**, and **dead two days later**; the dossier had him dying inside their rooms on the 27th. The whole building was questioned by Judge **Cristóbal de Villarroel** and **all released**. The surviving proceeding is what fixes the address, so the item is raised to [A]. **The killing was never solved.**

## 6. The last decade (1606-1616)

- **[A, Cervantes Project]** Madrid from 1606-1607, rented rooms in the **Barrio de las Letras** (street list [R]). **[R]** Franciscan Third Order, 1613.
- **[A]** ***Novelas ejemplares***, 1613, whose preface holds **the only physical description of Cervantes by anyone who saw him**. **[R, a genuine negative]** **No authenticated lifetime portrait exists.** The ruff-collared image attributed to **Juan de Jáuregui** rests only on a line in that preface saying Jáuregui *could* have painted him; the inscriptions on it are **later additions**, and El Greco identifications are conjectural. Every image of Cervantes descends from a paragraph of his own prose.
- **[A]** ***Viaje del Parnaso***, 1614. The false ***Segundo tomo*** under the name **Alonso Fernández de Avellaneda**, Tarragona 1614; **Avellaneda has never been identified**. Cervantes answers in the Part 2 preface, then absorbs the false book into the true one, sending his knight to Barcelona precisely because Avellaneda sent him to Zaragoza. **[A]** ***Part 2*** and ***Ocho comedias y ocho entremeses nuevos, nunca representados***, both 1615; the plays' title page says plainly that not one has been staged.
- **[A]** Dies **22 April 1616** of what the century called **hidropesía**, dropsy: a description of fluid retention, **not a diagnosis**. Retrospective guesses **cannot be proven** and are not stated in the file. **[A, Project Gutenberg #61561]** The **Persiles dedication to the Count of Lemos**, 19 April 1616, one day after extreme unction. **TWO CORRECTIONS TO THE DOSSIER'S NOTES:** (a) it claimed the Gutenberg edition does not name its translator, and it does, **Louisa Dorothea Stanley**; (b) Cervantes says himself that the stirrup line is **an old couplet he is quoting**. The `quote_source` field says both.

## 8. The calendar problem

`"calendar": "gregorian"`, deliberately. Spain adopted the Gregorian calendar in **October 1582, inside his lifetime**. Dates from 1583 on are Gregorian as recorded; earlier ones are Julian as recorded in Spanish sources and are not converted.

**The Shakespeare legend, stated as a legend.** UNESCO's World Book Day (since 1995) rests on the claim that the two died the same day, 23 April 1616. They did not. Cervantes died **22 April 1616 Gregorian**, buried the 23rd; Shakespeare died **23 April 1616 Julian**, England not having reformed, which is **3 May Gregorian**. The deaths are about **eleven days apart**, and the shared "23 April" pairs Cervantes's *funeral* with Shakespeare's *death* in two calendars. `el_inca_garcilaso_de_la_vega.journey.json` carries the same correction for its own subject, whose Córdoba tombstone gives 22 April 1616 with 23 April by convention. No relation between Cervantes and El Inca is attested either way, so he is named in no campa.

## 9. Interlocks

A stop belongs to whoever was actually there, and another traveler is named in a campa only where **Cervantes himself** stands in a relation to them.

**Named in a campa (2).** **`borges`**, who wrote "Pierre Menard, autor del Quijote" (1939) about this book and read Don Quixote first in English as a boy in his father's library: one sentence, 1605 stop. **`alberto_gerchunoff`**, who built a literary identity on Cervantes across four decades, 1913 to 1951: one sentence, 1615 stop.

**Found, real, deliberately NOT staged as stops.** **`borges` at Alcalá de Henares, 23 April 1979**, receiving the Premio Cervantes in the city where Cervantes was baptized: **that is Borges's stop, on Borges's file**, cited only in `sources` on Cervantes's own baptism stop, since staging it here would be the Rashi-at-Troyes error exactly. **`thomas_jefferson`**, reading a borrowed Don Quixote aboard the *Ceres* in 1784: his stop, attaching to no Cervantes place. **`mark_twain`**, reachable only through Borges: a Borges relation, not a Cervantes one. Both dropped. **`el_inca_garcilaso_de_la_vega`**: command overlap under Don Juan de Austria in two campaigns two years apart is not a meeting, and a shared date pattern is not a relation.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical** from `maslama_al_qurtubi.journey.json`: **Denia 38.842, 0.1057** and **Madrid 40.4168, -3.7038**. Site-specific pins (Trinitarias, Barrio de las Letras, Casa de Cervantes) are distinct places rather than the city node and keep their own documented values. Valladolid recurs across the corpus (Columbus, Magellan, Belgrano, Moses de León); **shared geography is not an interlock** and none of them is named.

**A gap stated as a gap:** a corpus-wide grep finds **no file connecting to Cervantes through Lepanto, Algiers, the Ottoman Mediterranean, corsairs, or Philip II**. The soldier and the slave have no crossing point to inherit; that half of the life is written freestanding and no interlock was manufactured for it.
