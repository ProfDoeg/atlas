# Mircea Eliade (1907-1986), research report

**[A]** attested, source named inline · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, inference, or dossier-only claim no source reached this pass confirms. Contradictions are flagged and left open. ## 1. Bucharest childhood, 1907-1925

- **[A: ro.wikipedia raw]** Birth 13 March 1907, given as 28 February / 13 March, Old Style and New. **[A: en.wikipedia]** father Gheorghe Eliade, born Ieremia, army officer; mother Jeana Vasilescu; garrison postings including **Tecuci [R on the date]**; Bucharest from 1914; 1 Strada Melodiei, now Radu Cristian; the Mantuleasa Street primary school; Spiru Haret College; the 1921 first article on the enemy of the silkworm; University of Bucharest 1925-1928 under Nae Ionescu, diploma on Campanella.
- **Contradiction, flagged, not resolved: the birth date.** en.wikipedia gives 13 March, New World Encyclopedia 9 March. The 9 March variant is not merely "older sources": Eliade's own *Autobiography* opens *la 9 martie 1907 (25 februarie stil vechi)*. Registry against the subject's lifelong self-dating, four days apart, with two different Old Style equivalents attached. The Forty Martyrs feast explanation is [R].
- **Birth address not established.** The dossier tied it to Piata Rosetti; ro.wikipedia dates the Melodiei purchase to **1914**, seven years later. The stop pins the quarter.
- **The golden room [R].** His own memoir, corroborated by no one; en.wikipedia carries no golden-room passage at all, so placing it in this house is inference and the stop says so. Kept because Bellow read the text at the 1986 memorial [A: en.wikipedia].
- **Quotation corrected [A: verified in the book].** Wikipedia renders "[the] exercise of recapturing that epiphanic moment"; Open Library full-text search of *Autobiography, Volume I* gives **"this exercise of recapturing the epiphanic moment"**, corroborated in Ellwood. The file uses the book.
- **Correction:** the "Ficino to Bruno" thesis scope is **[R]**, since a term search of the en.wikipedia raw wikitext returns **no "Ficino"**. Cite Ricketts or Turcanu, or drop it. The file drops it. Separately, the dossier's two lenses put the Mantuleasa block 800 m apart; resolved to Nominatim.

## 2. Italy, the passage, India, 1927-1931

- **[A: en.wikipedia]** Italy 1927 and the Papini meeting; the Kassimbazar stipend; study under Dasgupta and residence in his household, 1928-1931; the Maitreyi Devi relationship and the expulsion; return end of 1931; doctorate 1933 for *Yoga. Psihologia meditatiei indiene*.
- **Correction, dossier downgraded.** "Met Tucci in Italy in 1927" is **[R]**: the article says only that Eliade "collaborated with the scholar Giuseppe Tucci", no place, date or meeting. The Italy leg is attested; the compound is not. Tucci stays out of the Florence stop.
- **Where the Tucci relation is attested:** `giuseppe_tucci.journey.json` line 142 has Tucci meeting "a young Romanian student of Sanskrit named Mircea Eliade" at Dasgupta's Calcutta house in **1929**. The file puts it there, at that canonical pin, inherited byte-identical.
- **"the return to the Church" [R]:** 1927, on Wikipedia, uncited.
- **Egypt transit [R].** en.wikipedia gives only "a brief visit to Egypt", no port. The dossier chose Alexandria, which is not on the Suez route the claim itself invokes; a Calcutta-bound steamer calls at **Port Said**. The file uses Port Said and labels the stop a route inference.
- **Gap:** "3 Bakul Bagan Road" occurs **nowhere** in the cited article. Dossier-derived; the file uses the canonical corpus pin rather than any geocode of it.
- **Contradiction, flagged, not resolved: what happened with Maitreyi Devi.** Eliade's 1933 *Maitreyi* narrates a consummated affair; Devi's *Na Hanyate* (1974; English *It Does Not Die*, Sahitya Akademi Award 1976) denies it. Both are novels; there is no third document. **Correction to the dossier's framing of her:** not "his student", but Dasgupta's daughter and a Tagore scholar. "His student and lover" miscasts her and asserts the thing her book disputes.
- **University of Calcutta enrolment [R].** The only support was the *Surendranath Dasgupta* article's "famous students includes scholars like Mircea Eliade", which establishes a teacher-student relation, not matriculation. No stop written.
- **Rishikesh and Sivananda [R].** Term search of en.wikipedia returns **no "Rishikesh"** and no "Shivananda", only "a short period at a Himalayan ashram". **"Ananda Kutir" is anachronistic**, founded 1936, five years after Eliade left India; and the dossier said "spends months" against its own source's "short period". Kept, tagged [R], the ashram name dropped.

## 3. Bucharest, 1932-1936

- **[A: en.wikipedia]** the Criterion symposia, their cast (Comarnescu, Sebastian, Cioran, Noica, Ionesco) and the far-right disruptions; the apartment at 141 Dacia Boulevard; the 1933 manifesto "opposing Nazi Germany's state-enforced racism". **[A: ro.wikipedia]** marriage to Nina Mares, 1934. Bejan (2019), not fetched.
- **Correction, a substantive dossier error.** The dossier had Eliade writing the preface to Sebastian's *De doua mii de ani*. **He did not. Nae Ionescu did**, on the "eternal damnation" of the Jews, and Eliade spoke publicly *against* it. His 1934 intervention answered the preface; it was not one.
- **Venue [R].** A term search returns **no "Dalles"**. The dossier named Sala Dalles *and* put it on Calea Victoriei; it stands on Bulevardul Nicolae Balcescu. Coordinate corrected, attribution still [R].
- **"neither fascism, nor chauvinism" [R]** and **"All I wish for is a deep change..." [R]:** both on Wikipedia, uncited.
- **Contradiction, flagged: the assistant lectureship.** en.wikipedia dates the formal role narrowly to **1936-1937**; the broader literature runs it **1933-1939**. Both recorded; neither adjudicated.

## 4. The Legion and the camp, 1937-1938

- **[A: en.wikipedia]** Iron Guard support, enrolment in "Everything for the Fatherland", writing for *Sfarma-Piatra* and *Buna Vestire*; Prahova campaign responsibilities, December 1937; arrest 14 July 1938, three weeks at Siguranta Statului, transfer early August to Miercurea-Ciuc; illness, the Moroeni clinic, release 12 November 1938 (date corroborated by New World Encyclopedia).
- **Quotation corrected.** "A Christian revolution aimed at creating a new Romania" is **not a published Eliade sentence**: that string appears only in Lachman, *Politics and the Occult*, as his narration. Verified instead: Bosworth, ed., *The Oxford Handbook of Fascism* quotes Eliade in **Vremea, December 1936**, on giving birth to a "**Christian revolution**". The file quotes that phrase only and prefers 1936/Vremea to the vague "1937 article".
- **"to reconcile Romania with God" [A, secondary chain]:** Wikipedia citing Ornea, *Anii treizeci*, p. 203. Ornea not fetched; the stop says so.
- **Correction, two details the dossier hedged away.** en.wikipedia says Eliade flatly **"refused"** to sign the declaration of dissociation, against the dossier's "refusing or delaying... whose exact terms are not securely established". And the release was **unconditional**, "simply released on November 12".
- **Nae Ionescu in the same camp [R].** Frequently stated, not in the cited article, not attested anywhere reached. Kept out of the campa.

## 5. London and Lisbon, 1940-1945

- **[A: en.wikipedia]** London Legation April 1940; Lisbon from February 1941, through the National Legionary State then Antonescu; the Hotel Palacio at Estoril; the Cascais house on Rua da Saudade; *Salazar si revolutia in Portugalia* (1942) and *Romanii, latinii Orientului* (1943); departure for France **16 September 1945** with his adopted daughter **Giza**. Both the exact date and Giza improve on the dossier, which had only the year; she is the one member of the Lisbon household to survive into the Paris years.
- **Gap:** neither legation address could be located; both pins are city approximations.
- **Cascais coordinate flagged, not asserted.** Nominatim finds **no Rua da Saudade in Cascais town**; the three in the municipality sit 5-9 km east of the dossier's point, which is simply the town centre. Either the street was renamed or the point was invented. The stop uses the town centre and says the house is not located.
- **Nina Mares's death [A for the month: ro.wikipedia, "d. noiembrie 1944"]. The exact day, 20 November, is [R]:** dossier-only, and a dedicated page fetch returned 404.

## 8. Chicago, 1956-1986

- **[A: UChicago finding aid]** the 1956 visiting professorship and the Haskell Lectures. **[A: New World Encyclopedia and en.wikipedia]** settlement 1957, Divinity School professorship, Committee on Social Thought, Avery Distinguished Service Professor 1964, death 22 April 1986 in Chicago aged 79 from stroke complications.
- **Joachim Wach's role [R]:** Wach is **not mentioned anywhere** in that finding aid. The campa gives it as what is usually credited, not as fact.
- **Quotation corrected [A, verified in the book].** Open Library full-text search of the Trask translation gives **"The manifestation of the sacred ontologically founds the world."**, p. 21, with **no comma after "sacred"**, as in at least six other books quoting it. The dossier's comma is spurious.
- ***History of Religions*, 1961 [A for year and role].** Wikipedia: "founded in 1961 by Mircea Eliade"; the finding aid says only "co-founded and edited". **Kitagawa and Charles Long are [R]**, named in neither. The campa keeps them and says the reachable sources name only Eliade.
- **Frye quotation [A as quoted, venue unknown]**, from the Contemporary Authors entry at Encyclopedia.com, which does not give Frye's original publication.
- **Maitreyi Devi in Chicago.** The encounter is [A: en.wikipedia, "Maitreyi Devi"], which has her travelling to Chicago to lecture and meeting Eliade several times. **The year 1973 is [R]:** it appears in no source reached.
- **Contradiction, flagged, not resolved: the year of the office fire.** en.wikipedia dates it four months before his death, i.e. **December 1985**; the UChicago finding aid dates it to **1986**, twice. The stop keeps December 1985 for segment order and states the conflict. The finding aid also supplies a consequence the dossier lacked: **roughly one fifth of the correspondence destroyed**, the remnant an unidentifiable subseries, which the campa leads with. **"Omen" [R as interpretation, A as report]:** en.wikipedia, "an event which he had interpreted as an omen".

- **"mahaparinirvana", attribution corrected.** The term is real and citable, but **not to Culianu**. Two searches pairing Culianu with the term returned nothing; Anton's *Eros, Magic, and the Murder of Professor Culianu* yields no such passage. The quote_source names Rennie and records the mis-attribution.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Mircea Eliade is NOT listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** One relation in the pool is not rooted in his own travel, so it is reported rather than silently deleted. **Direction caught: BACKWARD**, the licensable direction.

**The relation.** The interlock lens proposed connecting Eliade's theory of sacred space (axis mundi, hierophany, the omphalos-type center of the world) to the corpus's canonical pins at the **Kaaba, 21.4225, 39.8262** and the **Temple Mount, 31.778, 35.2354**. Both sites long predate him; the framework would be applied backward to material he did not narrate as travel. The lens flagged it as its own reasoning: a grep for "axis mundi", "omphalos" and "Eliade" matched five journey files, **none at those two pins**.

**What I did.** Wrote **no** stop for it. Under `EXCEPTIONS.md` such a stop would sit at Eliade's own pin, dated only to order its segment, declaring its status in `date_confidence`, per the `j_l_austin.journey.json` Briar Rose template. That is licensable only if the author adds Eliade to the list, and he is not on it. Nothing was written into any other file at those pins: that would be a forward reference, never licensed.

**To rule on.** Eliade may be the corpus's strongest candidate for this exception, since "the center of the world" is a category he built to read sites he never visited. The row would read: *Mircea Eliade | hierophany, axis mundi, the center of the world | the Kaaba and the Temple Mount as omphalos-type centers*.

## Naming discipline inside campas

Each named traveller has a direct or one-directional relation to Eliade, never mere co-location: **`giuseppe_tucci`** (met at Dasgupta's house, 1929, per that file's line 142; later collaborators), **`olga_frobe_kapteyn`** (convened Eranos, invited him, he attended from 1950), **`jung`** (met at the Eranos table from 1950), **`rene_guenon`** (Eliade wrote about him in the *Portugal Journal*, per that file's lines 492-493), and **`wolfgang_pauli`** and **`martin_buber`**, named only inside the Eranos roster sentence, on the basis that Eranos was one continuing invitation-only seminar binding its members into the same yearbooks, not a place they both merely passed through.

**Not named, deliberately:** `rabindranath_tagore` (Devi lectured on Tagore in Chicago; that is *her* relation, and no Eliade-Tagore meeting is attested), `otto_hanish` (his file names Eliade; the reverse relation does not exist), `keyserling` (Darmstadt is the prototype of Eranos, institutional genealogy, not a contact), `savitri_devi`, `andre_breton` (Paris pin only).
