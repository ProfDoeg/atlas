# Polybius of Megalopolis, son of Lycortas (c. 200 - c. 118 BCE)
*Research report for `polybius.journey.json`. 44 stops, 8 segments, julian.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Gaps are stated as gaps.

The governing difficulty: Polybius is his own principal source and most of him is lost. Five of forty books survive whole, the sixth nearly; Books 7 to 39 exist only as Byzantine excerpts. The life comes from a few autobiographical passages in Books 30, 31 and 38, from Plutarch and Pausanias, and from one late compilation for the death. Nine dates or citations in the dossier were wrong; each correction is argued at its own point below.

---

## 1. Megalopolis and the house of Lycortas (c. 200 - 182 BCE)

- Born at Megalopolis in Arcadia, son of Lycortas, a leading Achaean statesman allied with Philopoemen. **[A: his self-identification throughout the Histories; Pausanias 8.9 and 8.30, inscriptions naming him "Polybius of Megalopolis, son of Lycortas"]**
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** No ancient text gives a birth year. The standard reconstruction works back from his offices to c. 200 BCE **[R: Wikipedia, 'Polybius']**; James Lloyd, for the World History Encyclopedia, prefers c. 208 BCE **[R]**. Both are carried in the file: in the birth stop's `date_confidence`, and again in the hipparchy campa, "about thirty, or about forty if the earlier birth date is right."
- Education in riding, hunting, weapons, camp discipline, and in federal documents. **[R: inferred backward from his own list, Book 12, of the competencies a historian must hold. No tutor, school or curriculum survives. Gap, and the campa says so.]**
- 182 BCE: Philopoemen killed by the Messenians; Polybius carries the funerary urn in the procession. **[A: Plutarch, Life of Philopoemen 21, which also supplies the stoning of the Messenian prisoners at the tomb]** Earliest securely datable event of his life.
- Aigion as the training ground in federal procedure. **[R: its role as federal capital is documented, but no passage places the young Polybius there on any day; the only evidence is the procedural exactness of Books 2 and 4.]**

## 2. Office, and the year of Pydna (171 - 167 BCE)

- 171 BCE: the League offers Rome a force for the Third Macedonian War; Polybius makes ready; the Roman commanders never call the troops up. **[A: his own fragmentary account of the debate, via Walbank]** Meeting site inferred **[R]**.
- Elected hipparchos, commander of the federal cavalry, second annual office of the League. **[A: Histories, his own record; standard in Walbank]** **Contradiction, unresolved**: 170/169 BCE **[R: Wikipedia; Walbank]** against c. 178 BCE **[R: World History Encyclopedia]**. The file uses -0169 and states the conflict.
- 22 June 168 BCE, Pydna. **[A]** His whereabouts that day are **not recorded**, so the stop is written as receipt of the news in Achaea, not presence at the battle.
- 167 BCE: Callicrates supplies the names; roughly a thousand Achaeans deported to Italy without trial. **[A]** **Citation corrected**: the dossier cited Polybius 30.13, which covers the congratulatory embassies after Perseus's fall and names Callicrates but is not the seizure passage. The detention without trial is **30.32**; the thousand is **Pausanias 7.10**. Verified against the Loeb Book 30.

## 3. Rome, seventeen years without a trial (167 - 150 BCE)

- Most detainees dispersed to Italian towns; Polybius kept in Rome, in the household of Lucius Aemilius Paullus. **[A: Book 31]** Palatine coordinates conventional **[R]**. Fabius and Scipio then petition the praetor to let this one Achaean stay, and it is granted **[A: Book 31]**.
- **Date corrected by about six years.** The dossier dated Scipio Aemilianus's approach to 161 BCE. Polybius 31.24 says Scipio was "not more than eighteen"; Scipio was born 185 BCE **[A: Wikipedia, 'Scipio Aemilianus']** and Polybius reached Rome in 167, so the conversation falls in 167/166. The file uses -0166 and shows the arithmetic. Everything else is verbatim in 31.23-24: the walk from Fabius's house, the complaint, the answer, "never left his side," and the acquaintance beginning through "the loan of some books and conversation about them," the stop's quote.
- Paullus keeps nothing of the Macedonian treasure except Perseus's library, given to his sons. **[A: Plutarch, Aemilius Paullus 28, quoted verbatim]** That **Polybius read in it** is **[R]**: he lived in the house, but no text connects him to that collection.
- Access to Roman treaty texts, including the disputed "Philinus treaty". **[A: 3.26 for the analysis, Book 12 for the method]** That they sat in the **Temple of Saturn** is **[R]**, a reasonable identification of the state archive, unstated in antiquity.
- The Lacinian bronze: Hannibal's inscription in the temple of Hera Lacinia, inspected and used for the army numbers. **[A: 3.33 and 3.56]** The **year** is unrecorded; the file places it at -0158, inside the Italian detention, and flags that **[R]**.
- Lilybaeum. **[R: inferred from his insistence on autopsy and the length of the Book 1 narrative. No passage names him there, and Sicily was a province rather than a detainee town, so this is the file's weakest travel stop.]**
- The fire-signal: the alphabet in five groups of five, one torch set for the group and one for the position, read through sighting tubes. **[A: 10.45-47]** **Citation corrected**: dossier and QUEUE brief both give 10.43-47, but 43-44 cover the older, cruder methods he is superseding. Verified against the Loeb Book 10.
- He credits the method to Cleoxenus and Democleitus, claiming only its perfection: "the most recent method, devised by Cleoxenus and Democleitus and perfected by myself." **[A: 10.45.6]** The "Polybius square" of the cryptography manuals is therefore a collaborative improvement to an **open signalling** scheme, not a secrecy cipher and not his invention. No date survives for the other two. Gap.
- Repeated Achaean embassies over the years, with many detainees dying first **[A: Wikipedia, with Pausanias 7.10 behind it]**; then in 150 BCE Cato closes the debate with the line about Greek or Italian undertakers, some three hundred of the thousand are released, and he refuses Polybius's request to restore the freed men's honours with the Cyclops joke. **[A: Plutarch, Cato the Elder 9, naming Polybius in both exchanges]** Plutarch writes two and a half centuries later and the wording of both jokes is his; noted in the stop.

## 4. Africa (150 - 146 BCE)

- Released after roughly seventeen years, he returns to the Peloponnese **[A]**, and by 149 has rejoined Scipio for the Third Punic War **[A: Book 38 fragment, "Polybius the Achaean, though he was then with Scipio in Africa..."]**. His **capacity** is a gap: no rank, title or command survives **[R for anything past presence]**.
- The naval mission for Masinissa's elephants. **[R: a fragment, outcome not preserved; Cirta given as the Numidian royal seat, not an attested endpoint]**
- Utica as the base. **[R: its role as Roman headquarters is documented; no passage names him there, only "in the theatre of war," and the campa says exactly that.]**
- 146 BCE, present at the destruction of Carthage. **[A]**
- Scipio weeping on the Byrsa, the Homeric lines on Troy, the turn to Polybius. **[A in transmission, R in status]**: Appian, Punic Wars 132, says "Polybius wrote this down just as he heard it," and 38.21-22 preserves the words, but Book 38 survives only in excerpt and modern scholarship reads the scene as possibly authentic, possibly literary shaping. Both readings are in the stop's confidence field.
- In Africa he learns the League has gone to war under Critolaus and Diaeus, and writes that he could not be indifferent to it. **[A: Book 38]**

## 5. The settlement of Greece (146 - c. 140 BCE)

- Mummius destroys Corinth the same year as Carthage. **[A: Pausanias, Strabo, standard tradition]** Polybius is in Africa when it happens; the date of his arrival in the ruins is **not recorded**, and the stop's autumn date only orders the segment.
- After 146 he uses his Roman connections in the postwar settlement of the Achaean cities. **[A in general terms: Wikipedia]** The fuller version, Roman permission to frame their laws and the verses saying he "roamed every land and sea" and stayed Rome's anger, is **[R: Pausanias, consistently reported in secondary literature; exact chapter not re-verified]**. Livius.org adds statues in **at least six cities [R: unnamed, a modern aggregate]**, not written into the file as a count.
- The Kleitor relief, excavated 1880, published by L. Gurlitt 1881, "thought to depict the historian." **[R: no inscription names him; the identification is the excavator's proposal]** **Coordinates corrected by about 17 km**: the dossier gave 37.95 / 21.9167, but ancient Cleitor is **37.8927 / 22.1032**, near modern Kleitoria in the Aroanius valley **[A: Wikipedia, 'Cleitor']**. The dossier's longitude sat in the next valley west.

## 6. The research travel: other men's ground

- He personally inspects the Rhone crossing and the Alpine terrain of Hannibal's 218 BCE march, rejecting writers who invoke gods or impossibility. **[A: Histories 3.42-56, where he states the inspection himself]** **Overstatement corrected**: the dossier said he was born "about eighteen years after" the crossing, which holds only on the c. 200 birth date; on c. 208 the gap is ten. The file says "a decade or two." That he was no eyewitness is unaffected and stated.
- Which pass he describes is still disputed; the Traversette is one candidate and was **rejected** by Walbank. **[R: contested modern scholarship, not an ancient statement; the pin is a hypothesis and says so.]**
- The Atlantic voyage beyond the Pillars, possibly provided for by Scipio, reported by Pliny. **[R, thin: the Pliny citation was **not** independently located here and is treated as a named scholarly tradition. Book 34 is lost except in quotation.]** Gades as staging point is **[R]**, pure geographic inference; no ancient text places him there, and the stop opens by saying so.
- Book 34 against Pytheas of Massalia: Britain's circumference, Thule, the sea-lung, and the objection that a poor private man could not have travelled from Iberia to the Tanais. **[A in substance, indirect in transmission: the polemic survives only through Strabo, Geography 2.4]** **Citation corrected**: the "not even of Hermes himself" line is **34.5.9**, not 34.5.10 (34.5.10-11 is the following passage on Eratosthenes trusting Pytheas against Dicaearchus).

## 7. The last roads, and the death

- Said to have accompanied Scipio to the fall of Numantia in 133, and credited with a lost monograph on the war. **[R: tradition via Wikipedia; presence and duties unconfirmed, and no Polybian passage located here places him at the siege.]**
- A fragment mentions the Via Domitia, conventionally dated to the campaigns of 121-118 BCE. **[R: attribution and dating both second hand. If genuine, the last marker of his writing life.]**
- Pseudo-Lucian, Macrobii 22: thrown from his horse returning from his farm, fell ill, died at eighty-two. **[A as a text, the only ancient death narrative. R as a fact: an imperial-period compilation written centuries later, with no contemporary corroboration.]** The c. 118 BCE date is arithmetic, eighty-two subtracted from a birth estimate that is itself a reconstruction **[R]**.
- **Total gap: the grave.** No tomb, burial site or grave inscription is attested anywhere. Not an unresolved question but an absence, and the file says so.

## 8. The work, and the text after him

- The Histories, designed to explain how Rome brought nearly the whole Mediterranean under its rule. **[A in substance]** **Two overstatements corrected**: 1.1.5 reads "in less than fifty-three years", the emphasis being shortness, not a round span; and the **forty-book count is not programmatic in Book 1**, which gives the scope and the 140th Olympiad starting point and says nothing of forty books. That figure is transmission tradition, and the file never puts it in his mouth.
- Books 1-5 complete, Book 6 substantially, the rest in excerpts, chiefly the tenth-century Excerpta Constantiniana under Constantine VII. **[A: standard transmission account]**
- Book 6: the constitution as a mixture of consuls, Senate and assembly, inside anakyklosis. **[A: with 6.9.10 and 6.18.1 quoted verbatim in the file]**
- Perotti's Latin for Nicholas V, five hundred ducats **[A: Wikipedia, 'Niccolo Perotti'; no exact year, inferred from the pontificate 1447-1455]**; editio princeps, Hagenau 1530, Secerius, ed. Obsopoeus, Books I-V with Perotti's Latin **[A: Wikipedia, 'Editio princeps']**.
- **Negative claim overturned.** The dossier recorded that Casaubon left his Polybius unfinished and that no completed 1609 edition exists. **False, and it would have planted an error.** The DNB: the work "cost him more than four years' labour, though he lived only to finish the translation, the fragment of the commentary being published after his death." The **commentary** is what was unfinished, and the Gronovius (1670) and Ernesti (1763) editions carry Casaubon as translator. Corrected in the file and in that stop's `date_confidence`.
- Sheeres's English translation with Dryden's preface, 1693 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the Loeb Polybius, W. R. Paton, six volumes, 1922-1927 **[A]**. The Dryden campa's framing against post-1688 English constitutional argument is juxtaposition, not a sourced claim.
- Madison cites Polybius by name in Federalist No. 63. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Mixed government']** That the framers' checks-and-balances reasoning derives substantially from Book 6 is argued by **some** scholars (Winkler named), not all **[R]**; the stop leaves it argued over.
- Ortega y Gasset's "one of the few great minds that the turbid human species has managed to produce." **[A as a quotation; source work Meditations on Hunting, prologue 1942-43, correcting the dossier's note that no work is given.]**
- The arcade urban legend: a seizure-inducing cabinet supposedly in Portland, Oregon around 1981. Earliest traceable source a coinop.org posting of 1998 whose own date appears fabricated; first print mention GamePro, September 2003; no evidence the machine existed. **[R; the stop says flatly "No such machine existed."]**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Polybius is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing was silently deleted. Three relations are not rooted in his own life, and all three are **forward**, which an agent may never license on its own judgement.

1. **The cipher lineage. Direction caught: forward.** The QUEUE brief says it is "this Polybius entry's job" to supply the link from his five-by-five signalling square to Caesar's shift cipher (`julius_caesar.journey.json`, "Bibracte: the alphabet moved three places," 52/51 BCE) and on to al-Kindi's frequency analysis (`al_kindi.journey.json`, c. 843-844 CE). Both are forward references from a file whose clock stops around 118 BCE, and the corpus does not itself assert the lineage. The signalling stop says everything his own text supports, including that the grid was Cleoxenus's and Democleitus's first and that it was open signalling rather than secrecy; the pointer forward is left out. Stating the lineage needs either a forward grant for Polybius in `EXCEPTIONS.md` or a backward-reaching sentence in the Caesar and al-Kindi files, where it would be legal.
2. **The final segment, "The Text After the Man." Direction caught: forward, but about the subject's own object.** Every stop in it postdates him by centuries. It is **in** the file, on the reasoning that these are engagements with his own book rather than someone else's framework applied to him, that Madison cites him by name **[A]**, and that `abdelkader.journey.json` makes the same move with the 1966 repatriation in its closing stop. By the strict letter of the block rule they are later artifacts inside an earlier file. If the author reads it strictly the segment should be cut or reduced to `suggested_refs`. I have not cut it; the operator should rule.
3. **Freud at Trasimene and Cicero's Somnium Scipionis. Direction caught: forward.** Real one-directional engagements with material Polybius supplied, but neither file names him and the relation runs the wrong way here. Not written; if wanted they belong in those two files.

No **backward** apparatus relation was found: Polybius has no framework of the kind `EXCEPTIONS.md` contemplates awaiting application to material he never treated.
