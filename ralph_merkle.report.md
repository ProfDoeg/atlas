# Ralph Charles Merkle: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-09 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject living (b. 1952). Slug `ralph_merkle`. Companion: `ralph_merkle.journey.json`, 8 segments, 43 stops.*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or inference, stated as such. *Gap* = looked for, not found, recorded as absent rather than filled. Every correction and hedge below also lives in the relevant stop's `date_confidence`.

Much of the primary record here is **self-hosted by the subject**. `ralphmerkle.com/1974/` reproduces as scans the two CS 244 project proposals, the anonymous referee report, and Susan Graham's rejection letter, alongside Merkle's own narration: contemporaneous documents, served from a single non-institutional host with no independent mirror found.

## 1. Livermore, 1952 to 1970

- **[A]** Born **2 February 1952, Berkeley, California** (Wikipedia; Computer History Museum Fellow profile). **[R]** *Birth hospital:* no source names a birthplace within Berkeley, and the pin rests only on Alta Bates having been the city's principal maternity hospital of the period.
- **[A]** Father **Theodore Charles Merkle**, aeronautical engineer, later director of **Project Pluto** (nuclear ramjet) at Livermore. *Chronology corrected:* the directorship begins in **1957**, five years after Ralph's birth, so phrasing that makes him already director at the birth is wrong.
- *Gaps.* Mother not named anywhere reached. Siblings not established. The family's move to Livermore is undated; their presence there is inferred backwards from the schooling.
- **[A]** **Livermore High School, graduated 1970**, primary-sourced from Merkle's own CV rather than a crypto-blog biography. **[R]** The often-repeated span "1963 to 1970" is an unsourced inference, and a four-year 1966 to 1970 attendance is the ordinary assumption. Neither is attested, so the journey carries only the graduation.

## 2. Berkeley and CS 244, 1970 to 1975

- **[A]** Enters UC Berkeley **1970**; **BA in computer science, 1974**.
- **[A]** Enrolls in **CS 244, Computer Security, taught by Lance Hoffman, Fall 1974**, and submits a **seven-page** proposal covering "Method 1" and "Method 2". Hoffman rejects it. Merkle drops the course, cuts the proposal to **two pages**, and resubmits.
- **[R]** *Building.* No source places CS 244 in **Evans Hall**; the pin is an inference from Evans housing Berkeley computer science in the 1970s, and is tagged as reconstruction in the stop.
- **[A]** *Provenance corrected.* The two quotations usually attached to the *first* proposal are from the **second**: "One of the major concerns, in the field of security, is how to communicate without fear of being overheard" opens `SecondCS244projectProposal.pdf`, and "It might seem intuitively obvious that if two people have never had the opportunity to prearrange an encryption method..." spans pp. 1-2 of that same document. `FirstCS244projectProposal.pdf` opens "Topic: Establishing secure communications between seperate secure sites over insecure communication lines" (sic) and contains neither sentence.
- **[A]** Hoffman's marginal comment on the resubmission is **in red pen**, not pencil, and as scanned reads "It's getting there, but not good enough yet - see comments." The words "I enjoy it," present in some retellings, are **not on the page**.
- **Contradiction flagged: when Hellman and Merkle first make contact.** The atlas's own `hellman.journey.json` dates it **1974-06-01** ("Berkeley, the third fool"), noting that sources give a 1974 to 1975 range and no month. Merkle's account puts the written CS 244 work first, in the autumn. The journey places contact at **1975-01-01** and records the disagreement rather than picking a winner. Hellman's phrase for him, from that file: "another fool crazy enough to work in the area."

## 3. The CACM fight, 1975 to 1978

The best-documented episode of the life, because Merkle kept the paper trail and then published it.

- **[A]** **August 1975:** submits "Secure Communications over Insecure Channels" to **Susan L. Graham**, editor for Programming Techniques at *Communications of the ACM*.
- **[A]** Anonymous referee report (`ExpertLetter.pdf`): "I am sorry to have to inform you that the paper is not in the main stream of present cryptography thinking and I would not recommend that it be published in the Communications of the ACM." *The referee is unidentified in everything reached, probably permanently.*
- **[A]** Graham's covering letter, **22 October 1975** (`RejectionLetter.pdf`), carries her own substantive objection about an eavesdropper setting N codebreakers on N puzzles in parallel. The separately reported complaint that the paper lacks literature references comes from Merkle's narration, not from the scanned letters.
- **[A]** Revised version dated **7 December 1975**. Merkle on why he did not withdraw: "I persisted for the simple reason that (a) the idea was sound, so CACM would eventually have to concede this fact and publish the article and (b) they would then have to include the original submission date."
- **[A]** Published **April 1978**, *CACM* 21:4, pp. 294-299, carrying the 1975 submission date. Read verbatim from `PuzzlesAsPublished.pdf`: abstract; Introduction opening; the definition of a puzzle (p. 296); the eavesdropper's hypothetical sign (p. 298); Conclusion (p. 299); Addenda naming Merkle-Hellman and RSA. *Truncation noted:* Merkle's sentence beginning "CACM eventually published the paper, though only after almost three years of delay..." is truncated on the source page as fetched, so it is not used as a journey quote.

## 4. Stanford, 1975/1976 to 1979

- **Contradiction flagged: when Merkle arrives at Stanford.** One biography (Bitnovo, summarising the standard account) gives a **summer 1976** stint with the Diffie and Hellman group, doctorate that autumn. The atlas's own `hellman.journey.json` and `diffie.journey.json` both date his arrival to **June 1975**, alongside Diffie's enrolment. The journey uses 1976-06-01 and records the atlas date. Not adjudicated.
- **Contradiction flagged: the master's degree.** Wikipedia gives **MS, UC Berkeley, 1977**; the interlock lens, reading `hellman.journey.json`, says he "earned his master's at Berkeley (1975) before being brought to Stanford." Two years for one degree, from two sources the atlas otherwise relies on. Because it cannot be placed without choosing, **the MS is given no stop**: a deliberate omission, recorded here.
- **[A]** **November 1976:** Diffie and Hellman publish "New Directions in Cryptography" (IEEE Trans. IT), crediting Merkle's independently developed puzzle scheme. **[A]** In **2002** Hellman publicly proposes the protocol be renamed **Diffie-Hellman-Merkle key exchange** (AMS *Notices*, June/July 2016).
- **[A]** **October 1977, Cornell:** at the IEEE International Symposium on Information Theory, a joint Hellman and Merkle paper is read with Merkle at the podium, amid export-control anxiety following the Meyer letter to the IEEE. *Two accounts kept side by side:* `hellman.journey.json` also records that Hellman's students read the papers aloud on legal advice. Not merged.
- **[A]** **1978:** Merkle and Hellman, "Hiding Information and Signatures in Trapdoor Knapsacks." Broken by **Adi Shamir in 1982**.
- **[A]** **PhD, Stanford, conferred 17 June 1979**, dissertation "Secrecy, Authentication, and Public Key Systems," introducing **tree authentication**. *Single-copy dependency:* the thesis PDF is self-hosted; no independent library mirror was found.
- **[A]** **US 4,200,770**, "Cryptographic Apparatus and Method," filed 6 September 1977, granted 29 April 1980, names **Diffie, Hellman and Merkle** as co-inventors, assigned to Stanford.

## 5. Elxsi and Xerox PARC, 1980 to 1999

- **[A]** **Elxsi**, San Jose, from 1980, rising to manager of compiler development. *Contradiction noted:* the Merkle biography gives 1980, while `hellman.journey.json` records that the Hellman oral history places the dispersal in 1979. Left standing. Elxsi's street address is *not confirmed*; the pin is central San Jose.
- **[A]** *Hedge retracted.* **US 4,309,569**, "Method of providing digital signatures," filed **5 September 1979**, **granted 5 January 1982**, inventor Merkle, assignee Leland Stanford Junior University. The grant date is on the patent record itself; an earlier note treating 1982 as unverified secondary reporting was over-cautious.
- **[A]** **1983:** marries **Carol Shaw**, video game designer (*River Raid*, Activision; earlier Atari titles). *Gap:* no source names a wedding location.
- **[A]** **CRYPTO '87:** "A Digital Signature Based on a Conventional Encryption Function," proceedings 1988. **[A]** **CRYPTO '89:** "A Certified Digital Signature," jointly credited as **Merkle-Damgard**. *Priority left open:* Damgard published an equivalent construction independently the same year and sources do not agree on sole priority.
- **[A]** **Xerox PARC, 1988 to 1999**, research scientist (CV). There he designs **Khufu**, **Khafre** and **Snefru**, dated 1990 in the pool; the work plausibly spans several years, so 1990 is a default.

## 6. Nanotechnology and cryonics, 1986 to 2003

- **[R]** **Foresight Institute** advisor, later vice president for technology assessment, given as **1986 to 1999** on a single biographical page (ethw.org), hence the tag.
- **[A]** **9 November 1995:** chairs the **Fourth Foresight Conference on Molecular Nanotechnology**, Palo Alto, 300 participants from 16 nations. The venue within Palo Alto is not named, a *gap*.
- **[A]** **1992:** "The Technical Feasibility of Cryonics" (*Medical Hypotheses*), coining **information-theoretic death**.
- **[A]** **1996 ACM Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award**, shared with Adleman, Diffie, Hellman, Rivest, Shamir. The banquet venue is unconfirmed, a *gap*.
- **[A]** **1989:** joins **Alcor** (his own home page). *Gap closed:* an earlier note treated his personal membership as unconfirmed; it is primary-sourced. *Still open:* whether the contract is neuro or whole body. The claim that he "has arranged to have his head cryopreserved" was **not confirmed in any source checked** and is not carried.
- **[A]** **21 May 1998:** joins Alcor's **board of directors** ("Director, Alcor," same page). A Wikipedia-derived date of **2006** is eight years late and is not followed.
- **[A]** **1998 Feynman Prize in Nanotechnology** (theory), at the **Sixth Foresight Conference, Westin Hotel, Santa Clara**. *Date corrected:* the conference ran **13 to 15 November 1998** and the prize goes at the banquet; the 12 November date in the pool is the **tutorial day**. Which evening carried the banquet is not fixed.
- **[A]** **Zyvex**, Richardson, Texas, **1999 to 2003** (CV). The **1999 IEEE Koji Kobayashi Award** has no venue attested anywhere reached and is folded into that stop rather than given a false pin. **[R]** The **2000 RSA Award for Excellence in Mathematics** is attested as to award and year; presentation at that year's RSA Conference in San Jose is inference, not confirmed.

## 7. Georgia Tech, the return, and the medals, 2003 to 2020

- **[A]** **2003 to 2006:** Distinguished Professor, Georgia Tech, leading the **Georgia Tech Information Security Center** (CV). *Anachronism corrected:* the **Klaus Advanced Computing Building did not open until 2006**, at the end of the tenure, so it cannot be where he worked from 2003. **[A]** **2004:** *Kinematic Self-Replicating Machines*, with Robert Freitas, folded into the same stop.
- **[A]** **2006:** returns to the Bay Area. Two claims usually bundled into that line are **split apart** here: the **IMM senior research fellowship begins in 2001** (CV), overlapping Zyvex and Georgia Tech; and **Singularity University did not exist in 2006**, being founded 20 September 2008 and launched at NASA Ames in February 2009, so a 2006 SU appointment is impossible. **[A]** He joins the SU faculty in **2009**, later Chair Emeritus of Nanotechnology.
- *Unresolved:* an IEEE biography places the **Institute for Molecular Manufacturing in Cupertino**, other sources in **Palo Alto**. Not adjudicated; the journey uses Palo Alto.
- **[A]** **2008:** IACR Fellow. **2010:** IEEE **Hamming Medal**, with Diffie and Hellman.
- **Contradiction flagged: the Hamming ceremony.** The frequently cited **20 August 2011** event at the San Francisco Marriott Marquis, held with the IEEE Sections Congress, was the **2011** Honors Ceremony; it recognised the 2011 Medal of Honor laureate Morris Chang. An `ieee.tv` listing for it carries a misleading `2010-IEEE-honors-ceremony` URL slug, the likely origin of the error. **No ceremony is located for the 2010 Hamming Medal**, and the journey says its pin carries no evidential weight.
- **[A]** **2011 National Inventors Hall of Fame**, jointly with Diffie and Hellman, for the 1977 patent. *Date corrected:* 3 March 2011 was the **announcement** of the class; the **ceremony was 4 May 2011** (IPWatchdog on-site report; PRNewswire "Honors 2011 Inductees").
- **Contradiction flagged: the NIHF venue.** Several sources including that on-site report place the ceremony in the **old US Patent Office Building, Washington, D.C.** (about 38.8977, -77.0219). But `diffie.journey.json` and `hellman.journey.json` both pin this stop at **Alexandria, Virginia (38.8014, -77.0639)**, dated 2011-01-01. The journey **inherits the atlas pin byte-identical** and records the documentary venue in the stop note. A candidate for a corpus-wide fix across three files at once.
- **[A]** **2011 Computer History Museum Fellow**; **2012 National Cyber Security Hall of Fame**, no venue attested, named in campa and given no pin; **2020 Levchin Prize**, Real World Crypto Symposium at Columbia, Alfred Lerner Hall.

## 8. Afterlife, which does not exist yet

- **[R]**, *stated as a gap.* As of August 2026 Merkle is **living**. No obituary, grave, memorial marker or tomb was found across Wikipedia, ETHW, the Computer History Museum, Alcor and cryonics-wiki pages, and none was expected. The closing segment is titled "The Medals, and No Tomb," and its final stop is prospective: the Alcor facility, under the 1989 contract.
- **[A]** **31 October 2008:** the Bitcoin white paper lists among its eight references *R. C. Merkle, "Protocols for public key cryptosystems," Proc. 1980 Symposium on Security and Privacy*, and hashes transactions into a Merkle tree with only the root in each block header. The paper has **no place**; the journey pins Palo Alto for where Merkle is living and says the pin carries no evidential weight.
- **[R]** The framing of Merkle as "the father of" blockchain, common on crypto-industry explainer sites, is a retrospective popular characterisation appearing only after 2009; the 1979 thesis and the 1980 paper beneath it are separately [A]. **[A]** The **Satoshi Nakamoto Institute** keeps an author archive of his papers as primary sources for Bitcoin's lineage.
