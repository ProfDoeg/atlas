# John Dee (1527-1608/09): research report

*For the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `john_dee`. Companion: `john_dee.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or legend. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**.

**Calendar.** England kept the Julian reckoning, its civil year beginning 25 March, until 1752. The journey is stamped `"calendar": "julian"` although most of Dee's life falls after 1582, because the Gregorian reform he computed for England is the one England refused. Three dates turn on that and are flagged in place: both wives' deaths and his own death window.

---

## 1. Birth and schooling, 1527-1546

- **Born 13 July 1527, Tower ward, London**, to Rowland Dee, Welsh mercer with a small place about Henry VIII's court, and Johanna Wild. **[A: Wikipedia 'John Dee', citing Fell Smith 1909]** The parish is documented; **no source names the house or street**.
- **Chelmsford school, c. 1535-1542. [A]** *Correction applied:* the pool's "King Edward VI Grammar School" is anachronistic. KEGS was founded by warrant of 24 March 1551, nine years after Dee left; the school's own list files him under "the Chelmsford Chantry School (before the Royal Charter of 1551)," with the chantry elsewhere in town. **No stop**; the chantry is named in the Cambridge campa.
- **St John's College, November 1542**, aged fifteen **[A]**. **BA "1545 or early 1546" [A as a range]**. *Contradiction flagged:* the pool gave a flat 1546 for both degree and fellowship. Only the fellowship is firm, **Trinity having been founded in December 1546 with Dee among its original fellows [A]**. The journey dates the Trinity stop 1546-12-01 and carries the BA ambiguity in words, not in a date field.
- **The flying scarabaeus** for the college *Peace* of Aristophanes. **[A: Dee's 'Compendious Rehearsall', via DNB]** Undated within the fellowship; hedge in `date_confidence`.

## 2. The Continent and the two queens, 1548-1564

- **Louvain from 1548 [A: French 1972, Woolley 2001]**: Gemma Frisius, acquaintance with Mercator, globes and instruments bought. **Paris 1550 [A]**: Euclid at the Collège de Rheims, crowds at the windows, a French post declined. The crowd is Dee's own self-account, **[A]** for transmission and **[R]** for any figure, so the journey gives no number.
- **17 February 1554**: Catholic orders by dispensation of Bishop **Edmund Bonner**. **1555**: arrest for "lewd and vain practices of calculating and conjuring," **exoneration by the Star Chamber**, then examination on faith before Bonner, ending as his chaplain. **[A]**
- **1556**: the supplication to Mary for a **national library**, refused. **1558**: elects **15 January 1559** as the coronation date. **[A]** The consultation itself is undated, and `date_confidence` says so.
- **1563, Antwerp**: hand-copies **Trithemius's *Steganographia* in ten days [A: Dee's diary via Fell Smith]**, the strongest documented interlock in the dossier. **1564**: the **Monas Hieroglyphica**, printed by Willem Silvius for Maximilian II. **[A]**

## 3. Mortlake, 1565-1583

- **From 1565 at Mortlake, in a house opposite the parish church [A: Wikipedia]**, with a library of some **four thousand printed books and hundreds of manuscripts [A: Roberts and Watson 1990]**, larger than either university then held.
- **1570, the Mathematicall Praeface. [A]** **Quote rejected:** the pool's second Euclid quote ("I haue for their sakes ... faithfully translated into our vulgare tounge") is **misattributed**; it sits in "The Translator to the Reader," **Billingsley's** epistle preceding the Praeface.
- **3 October 1574, letter to Lord Burghley.** *Correction applied:* the pool sourced these words to the *Compendious Rehearsall* (1592); the DNB gives the Burghley letter. Re-dated and re-sourced.
- **16 March 1574/75: Elizabeth at the church wall. [A: Dee's *Compendious Memorial*, in Halliwell's 1842 notes]** *Three corrections.* (a) The pool had the queen "return soon after to inspect the collection." She did not: the glass was shown **the same day, outdoors at the church wall**. (b) Two lenses split the same day into entries dated 1574 and 1575; **Old Style / New Style flagged**, since Halliwell's 16 March 1575 is 16 March 1574 by the English year. The journey uses 1575-03-16, hedged in `date_confidence`. (c) "Newly widowed" understates it: Katherine Constable had been carried out for burial **four hours earlier**.
- **1576-77 [A]**: Frobisher's pilots at **Ratcliff**, the *General and Rare Memorials*, and **"British Empire"** in English print, merged into one stop; the 1577 comet read at Windsor, **what he told the queen unrecorded**.
- **17 September 1580, the queen at the gate.** *Correction applied, splice removed:* the pool's quote ("...and than did she pulled off her glove...") never existed as continuous text. About forty words separate the two fragments, and the bridging words are invented and ungrammatical. The journey quotes **one continuous fragment**.

## 4. Kelley and the actions, 1582-1583

**March 1582: Edward Kelley**, then using the name Edward Talbot, arrives at Mortlake. **[A]**

**The instrument. Correction applied twice over.** The pool described "a wax-sealed obsidian mirror" and had the scrying "centre on" an Aztec mirror. Both are wrong. The documented apparatus is a **crystal shew-stone** on the wax **Sigillum Dei Aemeth**, with smaller seals under the table of practice, held by the British Museum as **separate objects [A]**. The **obsidian mirror is genuinely Aztec**, not merely "traditionally" so: Wikipedia calls it flatly an Aztec cult object brought to Europe in the late 1520s, and *Antiquity* (2021) tied it geochemically to Mexican obsidian **[A]**, so the pool's [R] sat on the wrong half. What is **[R]** is the **link to Dee**, which rests on **Horace Walpole**, since "there is no explicit reference to the mirror in any of Dee's surviving writings." The mirror is kept **out of the 1582 scene**, with one afterlife stop where the attribution is narrated as one.

**26 March 1583**: Kelley's alphabet visions, the language later called **Enochian**; whether he invented it is open **[R on authorship]**. Folded into the Kraków Keys stop. **1583**: **Albert (Olbracht) Łaski** becomes patron and the party leaves England that **September**. **[A]**

## 5. Poland and Bohemia, 1583-1589

- **Kraków, c. 1584: the forty-eight Angelic Keys. [A: Wikipedia 'Enochian']** *Correction applied:* the pool staged them "at the court of King Stephen Báthory in Kraków," conflating a long residence in lodgings with a discrete audience. Wikipedia places them "about a year later" than the March 1583 alphabet and dates the language 1583-1584, so the reception straddles both years. The stop is dated 1584-04-01, hedged in `date_confidence`, with no audience staged.
- **1584, Prague Castle**: audience with **Rudolf II**, the warning of judgement, no result **[A: Evans 1973]**. **19 December 1586**: Kelley's first reported transmutation **[A as a report]**, hedged as "reported, not verified." **1586**: expulsion from imperial lands within six days under nuncio pressure **[A]**. A **separate** summons before the nuncio **Malaspina** is dated 27 March 1587 **[R]**; the journey keeps the expulsion as the stop and notes the summons in `date_confidence` rather than merging the two.
- **The Faust House, Charles Square** (pin inherited byte-identical from `johann_faust.journey.json`): **[R]**, as the Faust file itself says. The stop is kept and **Faust named** because the relation runs the right way: a legend attached itself **to Dee**, a reception fact rather than co-location.
- **Třeboň from 1586, under Vilém of Rožmberk. [A]** *Correction applied:* Kelley was **not** "left at Třeboň in the service of Rudolf II." Třeboň was Rožmberk's seat; Kelley entered Rudolf's service **in Prague**, shuttling there from at least October 1586 ("E. K. recessit a Trebona versus Pragam").
- **4 December 1588**: Dee gives Kelley the glass **[A: diary]**. The **place of the entry is not stated**; inferred from residence and flagged as such.

**The covenant of all things common. Correction applied; the pool's own lenses disagreed by a year and reversed a quotation.** The instruction belongs to **April-May 1587**, not 22 May 1588: the diary has "1587 ... April 4th, actio tertia incepit," those actions run to 23 May 1587, and the DNB dates the subscribed covenant **3 May 1587**. The pool's sibling claim, **Theodore born 28 February 1588**, only works from a 1587 pact. The **22 May 1588** entry is a **reconciliation**, hands the other way about: *"Mistris Kelly ... to me and my wife gave her hand in charity."* **Contradiction flagged:** the angel is **Uriel** in one source, **Madimi** in another; the journey states both. **[R] not narrated:** that Kelley fathered Theodore.

## 6. The road home and the last years, 1589-1609

- **27 May 1589, Bremen: Heinrich Khunrath meets Dee** "when Dee was on his way back to England from Bohemia." **[A: Wikipedia 'Heinrich Khunrath']** **Missing from the pool's Khunrath entry**, restored as documented contact. *Correction applied:* the pool dated Khunrath's *Amphitheatrum* to **1609, Hamburg**; both cannot hold. First printed **Hamburg 1595**, the 1609 posthumous edition at **Hanau** (Khunrath died 1605).
- **3 November 1589, Stade**: the diary has Dee "hoping to mete Mr. Edward Kelly at Stade." It did not happen; the two never meet again. **[A]**
- **The homecoming, corrected.** The pool dated the Mortlake return "1589-11." That is the **departure**: "Nov. 19th, toke ship ... Dec. 2nd, we cam into the Terns to Gravesende ... Dec. 25th, I lay this night first at my howse." **[A: diary]**
- **The ruined library. Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The pool carried both an **1583 mob attack** (Fell Smith) and an **1589 discovery of vandalism**. Wikipedia gives only the 1589 homecoming; Roberts and Watson attribute much of the loss to **sale and dispersal by the custodians Saunder and Davis across the whole absence**. The journey has **one stop**, describing six years of piecemeal loss; the 1583 mob is **[R]**, not narrated.
- **4 December 1590: "I thank thee, Dee."** *Correction applied:* the pool inserted "she sayd," not in the text, and cut the second half of the remark, which is its point: *"did say with mery chere, 'I thank thee, Dee; there was never promisse made but it was broken or kept'"*, alluding to the hundred angels she had promised him.
- **1594**: the seven children before the queen at Isleworth. **1595**: warden of **Christ's College, Manchester**, cheated by his fellows, nominal warden until death. **[A]**
- **Jane Dee's death. Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Wikipedia 'John Dee' gives **March 1604**; 'Jane Dee' says **1605** while its own lead reads "1555-1604/5," almost certainly an Old Style / New Style split on a March date. The journey uses 1605-03-01, hedged in `date_confidence`; burial in the collegiate church is attested. **[A]**
- **Kelley's death.** *Correction applied:* a bare "1597" overstates the record. Wikipedia 'Edward Kelley': he "died a prisoner in **late 1597/early 1598** of injuries received while attempting to escape," with an alternate account of poison. **[A]**
- **Death between December 1608 and March 1609** at Mortlake, aged about eighty-one, buried "in an unmarked spot beneath the chancel." **[A]** The **parish register and the gravestone are both lost**, which is why the date is a window; the journey uses 1609-01-01, hedged in `date_confidence`.

## 7. Afterlife

- **1614-1616, the Rosicrucian manifestos.** The **Chymical Wedding (1616)** opens its invitation with **Dee's Monas Hieroglyphica symbol**. **[A: Wikipedia 'Rosicrucianism']** *Correction applied:* the pool dated it **1609**, bundled it with Khunrath's *Amphitheatrum*, and put Rosicrucian appropriation of Dee at "the same mid-17th-century moment" as Casaubon. It begins **from the 1610s**. **[R] retained:** no organised fraternity is evidenced in Dee's lifetime, and no evidence has been found that he belonged to any secret society.
- **British Museum objects**: the obsidian mirror (Walpole provenance, **[R]** as to Dee), the wax **Sigillum Dei Aemeth**, the table seals, a gold amulet engraved with Kelley's visions, a 6 cm crystal globe. **[A for the holdings]** The stop is dated **1785** for Walpole's ownership: **the date of Campbell's visit to him is not recorded** and no accession date was reachable. An acknowledged placeholder, flagged in `date_confidence`.
- **1659**: Casaubon's ***A True and Faithful Relation*** fixed Dee's name for three centuries as a deluded necromancer while putting the whole angelic record into print. **1880s-1893**: **Mathers** builds the Enochian tablets into the Golden Dawn's Adeptus Minor curriculum, members invent Enochian chess, **Waite** publishes *The Alchemical Writings of Edward Kelly* (1893). **[A]**
- **June 2013**: memorial unveiled **inside** the church at Mortlake. **[A]** The pool said "south wall"; the source says only "inside the church," so the wall is **dropped as unsupported**, the month **June** added as verifiable.

## Deliberate omissions

Dropped to keep the file inside 45 stops, or on evidence grounds, and recorded so none reads as oversight: the **1552 Cardano** meeting **[A]**; the **1553 livings** of Upton-upon-Severn and Long Leadenham **[A]**, at neither of which Dee resided; the **Báthory audience at Niepołomice**, **[R]** with an unresolved year, which would force false precision on date and place; **Golden Lane**, folklore with the lodgings evidenced elsewhere; **Kelley's prisons at Křivoklát (from May 1591) and Hněvín [A]**, since Dee was in England, the death being carried in the Manchester campa; the **2004** Science Museum theft **[A]**; the **Ashmolean portrait [A]**; and **Crowley**, who has no file, and whose Aethyr workings the pool misdated: "only as far as his own level of initiation permitted" belongs to the **Mexico 1900** attempt, not the **1909 Algerian** series, which ran through to the 1st Aethyr.

## Quotes: used, repaired, thrown out

Thirteen of forty-five stops carry a quote, each verbatim. Four candidates were repaired or rejected: **Billingsley's epistle** (misattributed to Dee, dropped); **the 1580 glove passage** (a splice; one fragment used); **the 1590 "I thank thee, Dee"** ("she sayd" not in the text); **the 1574 Burghley words** (wrongly sourced to the 1592 *Compendious Rehearsall*).

**Two Aubrey quotations were not used at all.** Both were cited in the pool to the DNB entry 'Dee, John', and **neither appears there**: the DNB quotes Aubrey exactly once, for the Meredith Lloyd memorandum on Dee's book of Spirits. They trace to **Aubrey, *Brief Lives*, ed. Clark (1898), vol. 1, p. 214**. Worse, the description read "a very fair, clear, **sanguine** complexion," and **"sanguine" is not Aubrey's word**; he has "a very fair, cleare complexione ... a long beard as white as milke." Clark was not opened in this pass, so both are left out rather than carried corrected-but-unverified. **The Fell Smith epigraph was likewise altered**, its closing words "as Dr. John Dee" **appended** to make the fragment self-contained.

## Honest gaps

- No street address for the birthplace, ward only, and no site for the Chelmsford chantry school.
- What Dee told Elizabeth about the 1577 comet is not recorded, and the place of the 4 December 1588 diary entry is not stated in the diary.
- The exact death date, the parish register, and the gravestone are all lost, so no stop rests on a precise 1608/09 date.
- The date the obsidian mirror entered the British Museum, and that of Campbell's visit to Walpole, were not reachable.
- Whether Kelley invented Enochian is open. The journey narrates the language's reception and takes no position on authorship.
