# Carlos Thays: research report

*2026-08-21. Jules Charles Thays, born Paris 20 August 1849, died Buenos Aires 31 January 1934. Director de Paseos of the City of Buenos Aires from 1891 to the end of 1913; proposer and builder of the Jardín Botánico; surveyor of the Iguazú falls; the man Argentine commemorative writing calls "el jardinero de la Nación."*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or one uncorroborated source. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Standing caution.** Thays is a name that absorbs parks: the popular figure designed the green space of Buenos Aires single-handed, the archival figure ran a department with a large staff, students who succeeded him, and a son and grandson of the same surname in the same posts for another half century.

## 1. Paris, 1849 to 1889

- **Birth [A].** Paris, 20 August 1849, to Simon Thays, a Belgian-born typographer, and Ernestine Paris, of a Versailles commercial family (Historical Archive, "Quién fue Carlos Thays"; es.wikipedia). No street or arrondissement located; the stop uses the canonical Paris pin.
- **Baptism [R].** Family recollection via La Nación (2018): father dead when the boy was seven, baptism at Saint-Germain-des-Prés at about ten, so around 1859. No parish register consulted; the La Nación page returned HTTP 404.
- **Apprenticeship with Édouard François André [A as to fact, R as to length].** en.wikipedia's André article calls Thays "his pupil and assistant", es.wikipedia calls him André's "secretary". **The dossier's "more than a decade" and the span "c. 1870-1888" are supported by nothing reached and are downgraded to [R].** The stop dates itself 1870 openly as an ordering device.
- **Commission and contract [A].** Miguel Crisol commissioned a park for the Nueva Córdoba expansion in 1888 through the André and Alphand network (Historical Archive); the contract was signed in Paris 23 May 1889 (Botanical Garden, "Biografía Julio Carlos Thays"). Compatible in sequence with the arrival of June 1889 [R, same page, which names neither port nor ship]; no primary document inspected.

## 2. Córdoba and the appointment, 1889 to 1891

- **Parque Crisol [R for the month].** A signed general plan dated October 1889 by the Historical Archive, cross-referenced to Carlos A. Page's archival study, not inspected here. es.wikipedia supplies the frame [A]: commissioned 1889, works begun 1890 on 17 hectares, 3 square kilometres, renamed for Sarmiento in 1911.
- **The competition [A].** Took office as Director de Paseos on 15 May 1891 after an open competition (Historical Archive).
- **Correction applied.** The dossier's "twenty-two new promenades against twenty-seven that had existed before" is half sourced. The Archivo Histórico says verbatim that between 1891 and 1895 the department finished 22 new paseos; **the comparator of 27 appears nowhere in the fetched text and is dropped.** Its career totals, 69 in the city plus 16 in the provinces [A], are what the file uses.

## 3. Parks and the Botanical Garden, 1891 to 1898

- **The proposal [A, with the primary text].** 22 February 1892 (Botanical Garden). The letter, Nota A, expediente 2661 D.1892, to Intendente Bollini, is printed in the appendices of Thays's 1910 book and was verified in that book's OCR, as was his letter of 6 September 1898 to Intendente Alcobendas announcing the opening. Both are quoted in the file. **Opening [A]**: 7 September 1898.
- **The house on the grounds [A as to building, contradicted as to residence].** The brick casona was built 1881 by Jordán Wysocki [A]. The dossier says the family lived there 1892-1898; **no source supports the 1898 terminus**, and es.wikipedia has Thays there throughout the directorship. **"Hup, hup" [R]**: family folklore, no contemporaneous witness.
- **Marriage to Cora Venturino [R, three-way contradiction].** (1) La Nación: 1 January 1892, he 43, she 18. (2) A genealogical index: 30 November 1893. (3) es.wikipedia: she Uruguayan and 16, he 41, putting it between August 1890 and August 1891. Version 1 is internally impossible: born 20 August 1849, he was 42 on 1 January 1892. No record inspected; the file asserts no date.
- **Yerba mate [A].** Seed and cuttings from Honorio Leguizamón in 1895; cuttings failed; germination achieved by prolonged hot-water treatment (Botanical Garden). No dated note, paper or patent by Thays on the method was reached, and diffusion into commercial Misiones plantations is not claimed.
- **Carrasco [A].** Thays directed Benito Javier Carrasco's agronomy thesis in 1900; Carrasco ran the department 1914-1918 and created the Rosedal on 24 November 1914, routinely misattributed to Thays. The file corrects this twice.
- **USDA record [A, verified directly].** Inventory no. 33 covers October to December 1912 but was **issued 23 January 1915**. Entries 34605-34608 (four Opuntia species) and 34655 (Ilex paraguariensis) read "Presented by Dr. Carlos Thays, director, Jardin Botanico. Received November 14, 1912." The "Dr." is courtesy, not a doctorate.

## 4. The provinces and the River Plate

- **Parque Urquiza, Paraná [A authorship, R date].** es.wikipedia credits the design to Thays and the forestation to Julio Kumagae, 44 hectares, land donated January 1894. **The dossier's "1895 inauguration" is supported by nothing reached.** The Paraná municipal page and the academia.edu study returned HTTP 403; no coordinates are published, so the pin is flagged unverified.
- **Mendoza [A].** Ley 19 of 6 November 1896; plans presented four days later; 394 hectares (mendoza.gov.ar). Cerro del Pilar purchased 1910. **Estancia La Paz, Ascochinga [A]**: the park of Julio Argentino Roca's estancia, 1901 (La Nación, via the Roca material in this corpus, which names Thays).
- **Santa Ana (Tucumán), Salta, San Juan, Sierra de la Ventana, Glew, Tigre, Mar del Plata [R].** Single-line entries in es.wikipedia's works list or the dossier's archive-derived list, dates uncheckable; none received stops. es.wikipedia gives Sierra de la Ventana as **three phases, 1905, 1916 and 1919**, not the continuous "1905-1919" the dossier printed.
- **Montevideo [A as to involvement, dateless].** The municipal page says Thays "intervino en la delineación y ornamentación" of Carrasco, the Parque Urbano (now Rodó), Parque Batlle and Plaza Independencia, and **gives no dates whatever**. The dossier's "1905-1912", repeated across four entries, is invented and dropped; the file uses 1907 openly as an ordering device.
- **São Luís do Maranhão, 1901 [R].** The dossier cites an 1901 Diário do Maranhão notice and USP scholarship for a plan for the Largo do Palácio; the USP page returned only its title. **Whether Thays travelled to Brazil or only posted drawings is not established**, and the file says so rather than staging an arrival.
- **Rosario [R, contested].** Parque de la Independencia inaugurated 1 January 1902 [A]. Municipal tourism credits Thays; es.wikipedia states the attribution "no cuenta con respaldo documental en estudios históricos especializados" and specialists credit Mayor Luis Lamas and municipal engineering. The stop picks no side.

## 5. Iguazú and the national parks argument

- **The 1902 survey [A as to year and commissioner, R as to detail].** The National Parks history page attests the survey in 1902 at the national government's request; es.wikipedia adds the Ministry of the Interior. **The arrival date of 6 April, the two-month duration, and Governor Juan José Lanusse as co-commissioner appear in NO fetched source**; es.wikipedia has a "Salto Lanusse" but never calls him governor. **1909 [A]**: the lands around the falls reserved by law.
- **The 1912 report [A year and content, R recipient].** es.wikipedia dates the detailed survey to March 1912 and gives 103 photographs. **"Agriculture Minister Adolfo Mujica" as recipient is unsupported by anything fetched and is dropped.**
- **Paris, 19 June 1913 [R].** The lecture at the International Forestry Congress and the 47-page Touring-Club de France pamphlet Les forêts naturelles de la République Argentine come from es.wikipedia plus an unfetched WorldCat record; the venue address was confirmed by no page reached.
- **Nahuel Huapi: a claim dropped.** The dossier carried an **[A]-tagged entry** for Thays conducting studies around Nahuel Huapi c. 1910. No fetched source supports it; the National Parks page ties Thays only to Iguazú. **No stop was written for a visit that may never have happened.** Nahuel Huapi appears only where attested: the 1913 lecture, and Moreno's donation of 6 November 1903 [A].
- **Law 12,103 [A].** 29 September 1934, establishing Iguazú and Nahuel Huapi; preceded by the 1928 purchase of 75,000 hectares.

## 6. The centennial city, 1901 to 1913

- **The caricature [A], the epithet [R].** José María Cao Luaces published a caricature in Caras y Caretas no. 169 on 28 December 1901; the Wikimedia record attests date and issue. **That "el jardinero de la Nación" derives from this image is NOT attested**: the record carries no such caption, and the file splits the tag.
- **Parque Patricios [A, two corrections].** es.wikipedia has the plan presented in March 1902 and the park inaugurated the same month; the Matadero de los Corrales operated on the site 1872-1902. **The yellow fever monument is in the adjacent Parque Ameghino**, and the dossier's merging of the two sites is undone.
- **Comisión del Plano [A].** Appointed by Intendente Güiraldes, 6 July 1908. **Palermo Chico [A]**, the curved street plan, 1912. **Boy Scouts [A]**, founded with Francisco P. Moreno and others, 4 July 1912; no source names the venue.
- **Plaza del Congreso [A].** Rodin's Thinker was inaugurated in the eastern half in November 1907, signed, one of two casts from the original mould; Thays redesigned the plaza around the already-placed bronze for the 1910 centennial. **The 1910 book [A, verified directly]**: Peuser, 180 pages, from a University of Illinois copy, **digitized 14 May 2008, not 15 May as the dossier had it**; full OCR fetched and six passages verified.

## 7. Private practice, death, 1913 to 1934

- **End of the directorship: a contradiction that resolves.** Both municipal pages give "fines de 1913"; es.wikipedia's infobox runs it "1891-1920". **The dossier left this open; it should not be.** The family firm has his son Carlos León Thays in the post "from 1920 until 1945", and Carrasco held it 1914-1918. The 1920 terminus belongs to the son's start: a father-and-son conflation, not a dispute.
- **Death [A, arithmetic contradiction left standing].** 31 January 1934. The Historical Archive says "a la edad de 85 años"; arithmetic from 20 August 1849 gives 84. The dossier's two lenses differed without noticing; the journey file states both. No cause of death found.
- **Burial [A/R].** Chacarita; "a multitude accompanied his remains" (La Nación, HTTP 404). es.wikipedia's Chacarita article does not list Thays among notable burials, so the burial rests on his own article and an unreachable newspaper. **Neither the plot nor the marker's condition was established.** Karman's eulogy, cited to Le Courrier de la Plata of 3 February 1934, is unverified.

## 8. Afterlife and the shape of the legend

- **Naming [A].** 31 August 1937 (es.wikipedia). **The argentina.gob.ar page the dossier cited does NOT contain the naming date**; misattributed, corrected. **Monument [A]**, Decreto 366/1996. **Bust and plaque [A as to existence]**, sculptor and date unknown.
- **Parque Carlos Thays, Recoleta [A].** Opened 1998 on the former Italpark site, closed 1990 after a fatal ride accident; he did not design it. **Plaza Carlos Thays, Carrasco [A]**, by Rubén Dufau. **Archivo Carlos Thays [A]**, donated by the heirs in 2014.
- **The Berjman edition [A, hedge resolved].** Carlos Thays: sus escritos sobre jardines y paisajes (2002), **376 pages, not "375/376"**; that it is the only such edition cannot be established from the publisher's page. **The 2009 Recoleta exhibition [A in part]**: soniaberjman.com confirms it and its catalogue but **gives NO page count and NO visitor figure, so the "224-page catalogue" and "roughly 50,000 visitors" are dropped.**
- **The 80-to-90-percent legend [R].** Denominator and survival criteria unpublished; the file uses the archive's 69 plus 16 instead. **The cabin-and-palace saying [R]** has no located first publication and is labelled traditional attribution in its `quote_source`.
- **Generational conflation [A].** The family firm distinguishes Carlos León Thays (1894-1962, director 1920-1945) and Carlos Julio Thays (b. 1926, National Parks until 1982) from the founder. **The same page carries its own error**, describing Charles Thays as remodelling Paris alongside Alphand and André under Napoleon III, impossible for a man born in 1849.

## Quotations: what was verified and how

- **Thays's own words**, verified in the Internet Archive OCR of the 1910 book: the three-styles passage (p. 24), the climate passage (p. 18), the 1892 and 1898 letters, plus **Olivera's signed preface** (pp. 9-10). OCR ligature errors were silently corrected, wording untouched.
- **Huret, En Argentine (1911)**, verified directly, with two page corrections: "la section argentine est une véritable création de M. Thays" is on **p. 66**, not 67, and "il créa l'ombre à Buenos Aires" on **p. 67**, not 68, both fixed by running heads. "Quatre cents rues" needed a second scan: enargentinedebue00hure drops "cents".
- **Clemenceau [upgraded from R].** The passage is in the authorized English translation, South America To-day (1911), pp. 39-40, verified in three scans; the file quotes the English and cites Clemenceau, not Spanish Wikipedia. **The French original was not seen.** **Bunge (1916)** and **Karman (1934)** remain [R]: unreachable originals, caveated in `quote_source`.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Carlos Thays is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so nothing was licensed and nothing was silently deleted. Three relations not rooted in his own life were found; each is recorded with its direction.

1. **aime_bonpland. Direction: backward.** Bonpland studied cultivated yerba mate at Santa Ana on the Paraná decades before Thays's 1895 germination work. **But no source establishes that Thays knew of, cited or worked from Bonpland**, and the Bonpland file makes no connection to Thays. A shared problem, not a citation chain, so he is named in no campa. If the operator judges that a real relation, its home is a source line on the yerbal stop, not a scene.
2. **francisco_salamone. Direction: forward.** The dossier states that Salamone's 1930s redesigns altered Thays's Parque Sarmiento in Azul: Salamone's engagement with Thays's object, so his stop on his file. **There is no Azul stop**, the claim is uncorroborated by anything fetched, and the Salamone file mentions neither Azul nor Thays. Nothing was written in either direction.
3. **Sonia Berjman and the 2009 Recoleta retrospective. Direction: forward.** Real, but the person who travelled to the Centro Cultural Recoleta was Berjman. **No stop was staged there.** The exhibition and the 2002 edition are carried as `suggested_refs` and `sources` on the Archivo Histórico stop, where Thays's own papers are.

Also: **Alphand, André, Barillet-Deschamps and Crisol**, the network that formed Thays, have **no atlas files**. A gap in the corpus, not in this subject.

## Contradictions

Marriage date (1892 / 1893 / a 1890-91 window), residence in the casona (1892-1898 / 1891-1913), age at death (85 / 84), the Rosario attribution, and the 1889 contract against the October 1889 plan are all flagged in place above and left standing. The end of the directorship (1913 against 1920) is the only one this report closes, on positive evidence.

## Honest gaps

- No birth address in Paris, no school, no account of how a fatherless boy entered the nursery trade; no parish record for the baptism. The length of the André apprenticeship is unknown, and "more than a decade" is somebody's guess.
- No cause of death, no plot number at Chacarita, no photograph of the marker; the sculptor and date of the garden bust are unknown to every source reached.
- Whether Thays personally travelled to São Luís do Maranhão is unknown. No translation of the 1910 book was located: an argument from silence, stated as one.
- Of 69 city and 16 provincial projects claimed by the archive, this research could name and place fewer than half. The journey file carries 42 stops, not a project catalogue.
