# Sophie Taeuber-Arp (1889-1943): research report

*Written 2026-09-01 for `sophie_taeuber_arp.journey.json`. Swiss textile designer, dancer, teacher,
interior architect, editor; among the first Europeans to make wholly abstract work, in 1915.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a dossier-only
claim. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Inaccessible sources are named with the reason.
Every correction below is repeated in the `date_confidence` of its stop, so the journey file carries
its own errata. Short cites: GW
= German Wikipedia "Sophie Taeuber-Arp"; EW = English Wikipedia, same; FMA = Fondazione Marguerite
Arp chronology; KMB = Kunstmuseum Basel retrospective page.

## 1. Davos, Trogen, the schools (1889-1914)

- Born Sophie Henriette Gertrud Taeuber, Davos-Platz, 19 January 1889 [A: GW/EW]. Father Emil
  Taeuber, Prussian-born pharmacist, dies of tuberculosis 1891; her mother reacquires the Swiss
  citizenship she lost by marrying a foreigner [A: GW].
- 1895, family resettles at Trogen, mother running a guesthouse [A: GW/EW]. The name "Pension
  Taeuber" and an opening c.1902 are [R].
- 1905-06, Mesdames van Muyden boarding school, Vevey [R], not located on any accessible source.
- 1906-1910, textile department, St. Gallen, teacher Johannes Stauffacher (1850-1916) [A: GW].
  **CORRECTION:** the school is the École des arts décoratifs, founded 1867 by the Industrie- und
  Handelskammer St. Gallen-Appenzell, not the "Industrie- und Gewerbemuseum" the dossier names.
  Stauffacher is attested, so the dossier's hedge on him is dropped.
- 1910-1914, Debschitz School, Munich [A: GW]. *Contradiction, unresolved:* EW compresses attendance
  to "1911" and "again in 1913"; the October 1910 start is [R]. 1912-13, weaving at the
  Kunstgewerbeschule Hamburg [A: GW/EW]. Summer 1914, Debschitz diploma qualifying her to teach
  [A: GW], the month of June being [R].

## 2. Zurich in wartime (1914-1915)

- Returns to neutral Switzerland, moves in with her sister Erika Schlegel [A: GW]. The month,
  November 1914, is [R]: dossier-only precision, GW giving no month.
- 1915: joins the Schweizerischer Werkbund and trains in Bewegungskunst under Laban [A: GW/EW],
  "from summer 1915" being an ordering device, [R]; and makes the first fully abstract
  vertical-horizontal colored-pencil compositions [A: GW], for which no month is recorded.
- November 1915: meets Hans (Jean) Arp, at an exhibition commonly identified as the Galerie Tanner
  [A: EW]. **Gap:** the gallery's 1915 address was not located, so a central-Zurich coordinate is
  used and declared approximate.

## 3. Dada (1916-1920)

- Cabaret Voltaire opens 5 February 1916 under Hugo Ball and Emmy Hennings; she is associated with
  the circle's dance evenings [A: GW]. In May she takes the textile design class at the Zurich
  Kunstgewerbeschule, held to 1929 [A: GW/EW], the exact day, 5 May, being [R].
- 29 March 1917: abstract solos to Ball's phonetic poetry at the Galerie Dada opening [A: GW twice;
  EW]. **CORRECTION (mask):** the dossier gives the mask to Arp; both Wikipedias give it to Marcel
  Janco, "in einer schamanischen Maske von Marcel Janco". **CORRECTION (coordinate):** the dossier's
  47.3757/8.5402 sits by the Hauptbahnhof, 450-500 m too far north; the gallery was the former
  Galerie Corray, placed by GW at the Paradeplatz end of the Bahnhofstrasse, so the file uses
  47.3705/8.5389. GW also records only two documented solo Dada appearances in total and credits
  Emmy Hennings with founding the gallery; both are carried into the stop.
- August 1917: Laban's Sonnenfest at Monte Verità, with Wigman, Perrottet and Wulff [A, loosely:
  EW's Laban and Monte Verità material; the dossier cites the Arp foundations]. The exact festival
  date needs caution.
- 1918: painted turned-wood Dada Heads, including an Arp portrait [A: GW]. 1918-1920: the group Das
  Neue Leben with Janco and Arp [R], corroborated only through the dossier's foundations.
- 1918: the König Hirsch marionettes [A: GW]. **CORRECTION (two names restored):** the commission
  came from Alfred Altherr, director of both the Kunstgewerbeschule and Kunstgewerbemuseum Zurich,
  for Gozzi's play in René Morax's adaptation. Premiere 11 September 1918, ignored by the Zurich
  press; no second performance until 1965, in St. Gallen. The dossier named neither man.

## 4. Marriage, Strasbourg, the Aubette (1922-1929)

- 20 October 1922, marries Arp at Pura, Ticino [A: GW]. **CORRECTION (coordinate):** the dossier's
  45.9847/8.9236 lands near Agno/Bioggio, 4.4 km east across the valley. EW gives Pura as
  45.9833/8.8667.
- Summer 1923, Rügen with Kurt Schwitters and Hannah Höch [R], dossier only.
- 1925, Paris Exposition internationale des arts décoratifs: member of the Swiss division [A: FMA].
  **CORRECTION (two demotions):** the prize is unattested, FMA saying only "Member of the Swiss
  division" and GW not mentioning 1925 at all; the pairing with Johannes Itten is likewise [R].
- 1926: Strasbourg, French citizenship, first interiors [A: GW; EW on the residency requirement].
  The developers André and Paul Horn are [R]. Hotel Hannong [A, up from the dossier's R]: GW, "Ihr
  erstes grosses Projekt war die Wandgestaltung des Hotel Hannong"; its later destruction by fire
  stays [R]. Villa Heimendinger stairwell mural [R] throughout: villa unlocated, mural
  unphotographed, the doctor named without a first name.
- The Aubette, Place Kléber: renovation 1926-1928, opening 16 February 1928 [A: French Wikipedia].
  She is the original artistic contact and brings in Arp and Theo van Doesburg. **CONTRADICTION
  FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED (room attribution):** the dossier gives her the tea room, bar, billiard room,
  entrance and staircase; English Wikipedia gives her the entrance hall, tearoom and two bars, Arp
  the basement, passage and billiard room, and the stairwell to all three.
- Arosa, the cure with Gala and Paul Éluard [A: GW]. **CORRECTION (decade inverted):** the dossier
  staged an unattested c.1919-20 sanatorium stay as a stop and demoted the attested late-1920s one
  to a parenthesis. No source supports 1919-20; GW puts the stay in the Aubette years, when
  commuting between Zurich, Paris and Strasbourg exhausted her.

## 5. Clamart and the Paris decade (1929-1939)

- 1929: resigns the Zurich post, moves permanently to France, to the house she designed at
  Clamart/Meudon [A: GW; French Wikipedia "Fondation Arp", giving 48.8036/2.2444 and dating
  construction to 1927]. The address 21 rue des Châtaigniers is dossier lead.
- 1930: exhibits with Cercle et Carré [A via foundation material relayed by the dossier; her 1930s
  Paris activity corroborated by both Wikipedias]. Seuphor's invitation is [R].
- 1931: Muzeum Sztuki, Łódź, opened 15 February 1931 as the International Collection of Modern Art
  assembled by the a.r. group (Strzemiński, Kobro, Stażewski), the first such museum in Europe [A:
  English Wikipedia]. **CORRECTION:** the dossier gave "1931-32" for a single dated opening. Her own
  contribution and her travel to it remain [R].
- 1930s: Abstraction-Création, exhibiting and designing catalogues and invitations [A: GW, which
  dates only to the decade]. The dossier's tighter 1931-34 range is unconfirmed.
- 1936: MoMA, "Fantastic Art, Dada, Surrealism" [R], dossier only; moma.org returned HTTP 403 on
  every route.
- 1937: Konstruktivisten, Kunsthalle Basel, twenty-four works by her [A: GW exhibition list], so the
  dossier's hedge is dropped. **CORRECTION (Allianz founders):** GW, "Allianz (Künstlergruppe)",
  gives the 1937 founders as Richard Paul Lohse and Leo Leuppi; Max Bill was a member, not a founder
  (confirmed on the Lohse article), and Allianz's first group show was 1938 at the same Kunsthalle, a
  separate event the dossier ran together with the 1937 one. **[R], held apart:** that a 1932 Basel
  show hung ten-plus of her wooden sculptures under Arp's name alone. Unverified.
- 1937-1939: founds and edits *plastique / PLASTIC*, trilingual, Paris and New York, five issues to
  1939 [A: GW]. **CORRECTION (a founder restored):** the founders are A. E. Gallatin, César Domela,
  George L. K. Morris and Taeuber-Arp, who acted as editor. The dossier omitted Domela.

## 6. Flight, Grasse, death (1940-1943)

- June 1940: flees Clamart, abandoning both studios [A: GW; KMB]. Route: "Flees to Grasse in
  southern France via Nérac and Veyrier" [A: FMA].
  **CORRECTION (host):** Nérac rises from [R] to [A]; the Gabrielle Buffet-Picabia host detail is in
  no source reached and stays [R]; Peggy Guggenheim sheltering them in 1940 is [A: GW], but that the
  shelter was at Veyrier is [R], the two being named separately. **CORRECTION (coordinate, wrong
  country):** the dossier's 46.1667/6.2167 is Veyrier in the canton of Geneva, Switzerland,
  impossible as a 1940 waypoint into unoccupied France, since they reached Switzerland only in late
  1942. Veyrier-du-Lac, Haute-Savoie, 45.8822/6.1781, is used.
- 1940-1942: Grasse [A: GW; FMA], in poverty, on food parcels from Erika Schlegel and Marguerite
  Hagenbach, with Sonia Delaunay-Terk as her working companion; she works in pencil, producing
  *Lignes géométriques et ondoyantes*. **CORRECTION (residence):** "Château Folie" is unattested in
  every source reached, FMA included, which says only "Grasse"; Magnelli's
  presence is plausible but uncited. The stop is named for the town and the coordinate corrected
  to 43.658/6.9253 (GW) from the dossier's 43.6667/6.9167.
- End of 1942: escapes to Switzerland before the Germans reach Grasse [A: GW/EW]; last New Year,
  1942/43, at Max Bill's house in Zurich-Höngg [A: GW]. **[R]:** the November 1942 dating, the
  household split (Sophie with Erika, Arp with the Bills), and the Johannes Itten welcome, for which
  `itten.journey.json` stages no scene. **Internal tension flagged:** the split sits awkwardly with
  her dying at the Bills' two months later.
- 5 January 1943: "Exhibition by 31 Women" opens at Peggy Guggenheim's Art of This Century, New York,
  with Taeuber-Arp among the thirty-one [A: English Wikipedia; GW's exhibition list].
  **CORRECTION (not posthumous):** English Wikipedia states "Taeuber-Arp died a week after the
  exhibition opened", so "posthumously" goes, the tag moves [R] to [A], and the coordinate is
  corrected to 40.7636/-73.9758.
- Death: accidental carbon monoxide poisoning from a badly handled stove in the Bills' garden house,
  Zurich-Höngg [A].

## 7. Afterlife

- Grave, Locarno: she, Jean Arp (d. 1966) and Marguerite Arp-Hagenbach (d. 1994) share one plot [A:
  FMA, Hagenbach page]. **Gap:** that page names no cemetery and no monument. The 1943 Zurich *Les
  derniers 9 dessins* is [R], dossier only, and is given no stop.
- 1955: documenta 1, Museum Fridericianum, Kassel, 16 July to 18 September [A: English Wikipedia
  "documenta"; her inclusion via GW relayed by KMB].
- 1981: MoMA retrospective [A: GW exhibition list]. The tour to Chicago, Houston and Montreal, and
  "MoMA's first for the artist", are [R]; moma.org returned 403. **CORRECTION (address):** the
  dossier flagged the 1936 show as at "MoMA's prior location", but MoMA has held 11 West 53rd Street
  since 1932, the 1939 building going up on that site.
- 1995-2021: Swiss 50-franc note, eighth series, issued 3 October 1995, legal tender to 30 April
  2021, four of her works on the reverse; the only woman as principal subject in the series [A:
  Swiss National Bank]. On 8 April 2025 Christie's Paris sold one of those reliefs for about EUR
  3.06 million, an auction record [A: heni.com, Christie's own page not re-fetched].
  **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED:** English Wikipedia gives the circulation window as 1995 to 2016; the SNB
  is primary and preferred, and the discrepancy stands.
- 2021-22: "Living Abstraction", Kunstmuseum Basel 20 March to 20 June 2021 and Tate Modern 15 July
  to 17 October 2021, her first UK retrospective, both [A: pages fetched]; MoMA 21 November 2021 to
  12 March 2022 [A per dossier and press; MoMA 403].
- 2021-22: the correspondence editions [A: Hyperallergic]. **CORRECTION (two details):** the Nimbus
  set is *Sophie Taeuber-Arp: Briefe 1905-1942* (2021), not the dossier's three volume titles; and
  the Müller-Widmann volume holds 35 letters written 1932-1942, so "her 35 wartime letters" is wrong,
  most predating the war. GW adds the provenance: about 450 unpublished letters bought by the
  Zentralbibliothek Zürich in 2012, edited by Hoch, Krupp and Schade. The dossier's husband-shaped
  1948 catalogue is unverified.
- Arp Museum Bahnhof Rolandseck, Remagen [A: arpmuseum.org]. **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED:** GW's search
  abstract gives the opening as 28 September 2007, the body of her own article 29 September; the
  museum's page gives no date. **CORRECTION (coordinate):** the dossier's 50.5822/7.19 points at
  Remagen town centre, 5.7 km from the museum, which GW puts at 50.6314/7.2069. Given no stop.

## 8. Quotations: provenance

Eight stops carry quotes, all verbatim from the pool. Two need declaring.

1. "Only when we go into ourselves..." is [A] as a quotation, but the dossier's source is wrong: it
   is absent from sophietaeuberarp.org's biography page (checked at `/en/biografie/` and
   `/biografie/`) and appears verbatim, uncited, at Tate, "Five things to know about Sophie
   Taeuber-Arp". The dossier's attribution to her 1922 essay *Bemerkungen über den Unterricht im
   ornamentalen Entwerfen* is unsupported: the MoMA catalogue (Lanchner, 1981) quotes one unrelated
   sentence from that essay, and Wikiquote does not carry it. **Gap stated in the file: no primary
   text is named for it by any source reached.**
2. Rotzler's "Schlüsselfigur und vielleicht eigentliche Initiantin" is upgraded from [R] to [A].
   Rotzler (1917-1994) honoured her as key figure and perhaps actual initiator of Swiss Concrete Art
   specifically, not of her career at large. **Two gaps:** the publication behind
   sophietaeuberarp.org's footnote marker was not visible, and the English wording is a translation.
   Being a third party's verdict, not words said at a place, it is not used as a stop quote.

Ball's line and the Arp dance descriptions come from Tate Etc, issue 52. The dossier's paraphrase of
Ball as "flashes, fishbones, dazzling light" could not be confirmed there and is unused; of the Arp
lines only the vertical-horizontal-influence sentence is used, on the 1915 stop.
