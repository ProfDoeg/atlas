# Dion Fortune (Violet Mary Firth), 1890-1946: research report

*2026-08-09. Slug `dion_fortune`. Born Llandudno, Caernarfonshire; died Middlesex Hospital, London; buried Glastonbury Cemetery, Wells Road. Founder of the Fraternity (later Society) of the Inner Light; author of Psychic Self-Defence, The Mystical Qabalah, The Sea Priestess.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, self-report, or an inference this report is making. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Coordinate fixes are marked *[corrected]* with the discarded value given, so the correction can be audited.

## 1. The water cure, 1888-1900

The family money is Sheffield steel two generations back; Arthur Firth trained as a Sheffield solicitor before leaving the law for the hydropathic trade **[A: Wikipedia, 'Dion Fortune']**. His first establishment is at Limpley Stoke, Wiltshire, before the move to Wales **[A]**. No year is given anywhere reached; 1888 is a placeholder in the "before 1890" window. *[corrected]* pool 51.354/-2.31 sits 1.2 km north of the village; OSM gives 51.3440/-2.3154.

Born 6 December 1890 at the family home on Bryn-y-Bia Road, Llandudno **[A: Wikipedia, citing Alan Richardson, *Priestess*; corroborated by the Society of the Inner Light's own site]**. Gap: the street is named, the house is not; the pin is a street point, not a doorway.

Her father then establishes the Craigside Hydrotherapeutic Establishment at Llandudno **[A]**, the business anchoring the family in North Wales through her childhood. *[corrected]* pool 53.3268/-3.792 sits 545 m north on the Little Orme slope; OSM (LL30 3AR) gives 53.3219/-3.7917.

*[corrected: a claim, not a coordinate]* The pool carried "Family living in Llandudno at time of 1900 census." There was no 1900 UK census; the decennial censuses ran 1891, 1901, 1911. Wikipedia says only that the Firths were still in Llandudno in 1900. The invented census is deleted; the journey states residence.

The magical motto *Deo, non fortuna*, "by God, not by chance," is the family motto, from which the pen name is contracted **[A as to derivation]**; that it was cut into the family plate is **[R]**.

## 3. Studley Castle, and the broken year, 1911-1916

She studies at Studley Agricultural College, Warwickshire, January 1911 to December 1912, and serves on its staff January to April 1913 **[A]**. A confrontation at the college is followed by a mental collapse and a return to her parents' home **[A]**. *[corrected]* pool 52.2814/-1.8830 is 1.0 km northwest in open farmland; OSM (Hardwick Lane, B80 7AJ) gives 52.2748/-1.8722.

The psychic attack is **[R]**, and the distinction matters more here than anywhere else in the file. The breakdown is attested. Her explanation, published seventeen years later in *Psychic Self-Defence*, is that the principal held her under sustained hypnotic assault until her nerve gave way: a retrospective self-report with no independent corroboration, and the founding narrative of her whole career. The journey states the collapse as event and the attack as her later reading, with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

The principal is named here where the pool said only "her employer": Lillias Hamilton **[A: Wikipedia]**, a physician who had served as court doctor to the Emir of Afghanistan.

Recovery becomes a curriculum: psychology and psychoanalysis under John Flügel at the University of London **[A]**, then lay counsellor and psychotherapist at a clinic in Brunswick Square, Bloomsbury, 1914-1916 **[A]**, likely under the London School of Medicine for Women. Gap: no building is named for the Flügel study; University College, Gower Street is used and that precision is called reconstruction.

Wartime: Women's Land Army service on a farm near Bishop's Stortford **[A]**, later work at an experimental food facility of the Letchworth-based Garden City Pure Food Company **[A]**. Gap: the farm is unnamed, the years unfixed; the pin marks the town.

## 4. The orders, 1915-1923

She begins attending the Theosophical Society's lunchtime lectures, dated by Wikipedia only as circa 1915 **[A as to fact, R as to date]**. Initiation into the London temple of the Alpha et Omega, 1919 **[A: Wikipedia; Society of the Inner Light]**, later transferring to a Stella Matutina lodge **[A]**.

*[corrected: grade misnamed]* Wikipedia's 'Alpha et Omega' records that she reached 2=9; the pool glossed that as Practicus. In the Golden Dawn ladder (0=0 Neophyte, 1=10 Zelator, 2=9 Theoricus, 3=8 Practicus, 4=7 Philosophus, 5=6 Adeptus Minor) 2=9 is Theoricus. The same source records that she later attained 5=6, Adeptus Minor, in the Stella Matutina **[A]**, the more consequential grade, absent from the pool and restored here.

Gap, with a conflict about it. No temple address was located. The geography lens supplied a central-London placeholder (51.5074/-0.1278) and honestly flagged it; the interlock lens, drawing on `arthur_edward_waite.journey.json`, states she was "initiated 1919 in its Bayswater temple," a striking coincidence with the Queensborough Terrace headquarters five years later that should not rest on one unverified line. The journey uses the placeholder and records the Bayswater tradition as unconfirmed.

Theodore Moriarty's Masonic-influenced lodge at Hammersmith **[A]**, and residence in his circle at Gwen Stafford-Allen's house in Bishop's Stortford **[A]**. Her account of watching Moriarty exorcise a young man possessed by the soul of a dead East European soldier is **[R]**: her own narrative, no other witness. Moriarty dies in August 1923 **[A]**, carried in the Cosmic Doctrine stop's campa for stop budget. *[corrected]* pool 51.4927/-0.2339 sits 715 m west near Ravenscourt Park; OSM gives 51.4920/-0.2236.

### The 1921/1922 correction, which reorders the Glastonbury story

The pool dated the trance mediumship at Glastonbury with her mother and the archaeologist Frederick Bligh Bond to 1922. Wikipedia dates the Maiya Curtis-Webb experiments to January to March 1921 and says they "culminated in an act of trance mediumship that Fortune conducted in the Somerset town of Glastonbury with her mother and Frederick Bligh Bond." The correct year is 1921 **[A]**, and the consequence is structural: Bligh Bond, not Charles Loveday, is her first Glastonbury contact, a year before the September 1922 Loveday visit, so her introduction to the town comes through the man who excavated the abbey by automatic writing. The journey is built on the corrected sequence.

September 1922: she returns to Glastonbury to visit Charles Loveday **[A]**, thereafter collaborator and financial backer. 1922: *The Machinery of the Mind* published **[A]** under her birth name Violet M. Firth. 1923: the text later published as *The Cosmic Doctrine* is received through mediumship with Loveday **[A]**; her parents having moved to Letchworth in 1922, trance sittings run there 1923-1925 **[A]**. Publication waits until 1949 **[A]**.

## 6. The novels, 1933-1938

1933: the marriage grows strained amid rumours of Evans's affairs **[A that Wikipedia reports this; the rumours themselves [R]]**. Gap: no date for a separation or divorce was found anywhere reached.

1934: Avalon of the Heart **[A]** · 1935: The Mystical Qabalah and The Winged Bull **[A]** · 1936: The Goat-Foot God **[A]** · 1938: The Sea Priestess, self-published **[A]** · late 1930s: rents and takes up residence in the Belfry, a converted Presbyterian chapel in West Halkin Street **[A, undated within the decade]**.

The Mystical Qabalah quotation, promoted from **[R]** to **[A]** and corrected. The pool carried "Symbols are to the mind what tools are to the hand, an extended application of its powers" as unverifiable, on the grounds that archive.org copies returned HTTP 401/404. Wrong on both counts: open-access scans exist, five were downloaded, all agree. Best OCR (`dion-fortune-the-mystical-qabalah`), ch. III 'The Negative Existence', §3, p. 31:

> ...but the initiate will go back furthest of all because he has learnt to do his thinking in symbols, and symbols are to the mind what tools are to the hand—an extended application of its powers.

It is a subordinate clause, not a standalone sentence; "symbols" is lowercase; the original punctuation is an em dash, which the pool had silently converted to a comma to satisfy house style. House style does not reach inside a quote field, so the journey carries the clause verbatim, lowercase, dash intact.

The Sea Priestess line: still **[R]**. "All gods are one God, and all goddesses are one Goddess, and there is one Initiator" is the most quoted sentence attributed to her and is echoed in later Wiccan liturgy. It could not be verified: the archive.org copies reached (`moonmagic0000dion` and related identifiers) returned HTTP 401, lending-restricted. `quote_source` reads UNVERIFIED and names the reason: the one quote field in the file that is not known-good text.

## 7. The Magical Battle of Britain, 1939-1944

October 1939: weekly group meditations for peace organised after the outbreak of war **[A]**, part of a nationwide programme circulated by weekly letters to members across Britain from the London headquarters **[A: Wikipedia and the Society of the Inner Light; the letters were later published as The Magical Battle of Britain]**.

Contradiction inside the pool, flagged and split. The afterlife lens asserted the whole campaign including "imagined angelic forces patrolling the coast" as **[A]**; the chronology lens tagged the February 1940 angelic-patrol visualisation as **[R]**, a group-internal claim. One claim cannot hold two tags. The journey splits it: campaign, letters and synchronised weekly practice **[A]** at one stop; the content of the visualisation **[R]** at a separate stop, whose campa says that what is on record is the instruction and the fact of its being followed, not the effect. The 1941 "Arthurian Formula" material **[R]** is the same category and gets no stop.

August 1940: The Inner Light suspended for wartime paper shortages **[A]**; a further project of trance mediumship begins with her Alpha et Omega mentor Curtis-Webb **[A]**. *[corrected: two women conflated]* The pool glossed the collaborator as "Christine Hartley/Tranchell-Hayes." Maiya Curtis-Webb is the woman who later became Maiya Tranchell-Hayes: one person, two surnames. Christine Hartley (Christine Campbell Thomson) is a different occultist entirely. The journey names Curtis-Webb. 1940-1941: the Bayswater headquarters is damaged in the Blitz and back in use within a week **[A]**, folded into the same stop.

Netherwood, the Crowley claim, recast. The pool carried "Fortune is said to have visited Aleister Crowley at his residence in Hastings," tagged **[R]**. Verifiable: Crowley lived at the Netherwood boarding house on The Ridge, Hastings, from 1944 until his death there on 1 December 1947 **[A: Wikipedia, 'Aleister Crowley']**, and a Fortune-Crowley correspondence is well attested **[A]**. Not verifiable: no primary source was reached documenting a visit, and Wikipedia's Crowley article mentions none. The stop is built on the correspondence and says so in campa. *[corrected]* pool 50.8721/0.5941 is nearly a kilometre south of The Ridge; OSM (TN34 2FP) gives about 50.8801/0.5909.

## 8. Death and disposition, 1945-1959

Late 1945: falls ill, unable to deliver the Fraternity's winter solstice address **[A]**.

The death date is contradicted, and the pool contradicted itself about it. Wikipedia gives 6 or 8 January 1946 and does not decide; the chronology lens anchored 1946-01-08, the afterlife lens 1946-01-06, for the same death in the same document. The Society of the Inner Light, her own organisation and the strongest available witness, states 8 January 1946. The journey anchors that and records 6 January as the standing variant: a choice of anchor, not a resolution.

Died of leukaemia at the Middlesex Hospital, aged 55 **[A]**. Pool coordinates spread about 130 m (51.5189/-0.1367, 51.5194/-0.1385, Wikipedia 51.5190/-0.1377); 51.5189/-0.1367 is used. The hospital was demolished in the early 2000s; the pin marks the former Mortimer Street site.

Body transferred to Glastonbury, funeral overseen by the Reverend L. S. Lewis, vicar of St John the Baptist **[A]**; buried in Glastonbury Cemetery **[A]**; Charles Loveday dies soon after and is buried near her **[A]**. That the grave is modest and easy to overlook is **[R]**, a pattern in later biographical and pilgrimage accounts (Richardson 1991, Chapman 1993), not verified here.

Gap: no source reached gives the calendar day of the funeral or burial. The journey uses 1946-01-12 for both, a placeholder set after the 8 January death to hold travel order. This is the one place where the rule of defaulting an unknown day to 01 was overridden, since defaulting would have put the burial before the death.

*[corrected: the largest geographic error in the pool]* The geography lens placed Glastonbury Cemetery at 51.1441/-2.7186, southwest of the town out over the Levels, about 1.4 km off. The cemetery is on the Wells Road, northeast of the town; OSM (BA6 9BB) gives about 51.1564/-2.7135, and the pool's own afterlife lens already carried a near-correct 51.1558/-2.7128, to which the journey conforms.

1946: renamed the Society of the Inner Light **[A]** · 1949: The Cosmic Doctrine published **[A]** · 1956: Moon Magic, unfinished at her death, published **[A]** · 1959: the Society sells the Queensborough Terrace house and relocates to north London **[A]** · Chalice Orchard sold after her death and eventually bought by the writer Geoffrey Ashe **[A, undated]**, carried on the 1959 pin with a note that the year belongs strictly to the London sale.

Reception, verified, not carried as stops: Urban and Drury identify Fortune as a key link between ceremonial magic and the later development of Wicca, crediting her singular Great Goddess concept with anticipating feminist currents in it **[A]**; Gareth Knight leaves the Society in 1961 and returns by the 1990s to publish unpublished Fortune material **[A]**; Richardson (1991) and Chapman (1993) mark the modern rediscovery **[A]**.
