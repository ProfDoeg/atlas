# Moses de León (Moshe ben Shem Tov de León), c.1240-1305

*Research report for the Atlas of Journeys. Compiled 2026-08-18 from the verified research pool. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or scholarly inference. Contradictions are flagged and left open, not adjudicated.*

The difficulty of this file is structural: almost nothing about his life is documented, while almost everything about his book is. There are perhaps six firm dates in seventy years, five of them colophons on treatises he signed. The richest source for the life is a report by a man who came to interview him, and it survives only as an excerpt inside a chronicle written two centuries later, printed in 1566, censored out in 1580, and not restored until 1857.

---

## 1. Origins: León, or the name of León (c.1240)

- Traditionally born c.1240 at León, Kingdom of Castile. **[R]** Standard summaries (Scholem and successors), resting on the surname. Exact birth date, birthplace, mother, childhood teachers: unknown, and not estimated here. Father named Shem Tov, known only from the patronymic. **[A]** Jewish Encyclopedia; Wikipedia "Moses de León".
- **Live disagreement.** Bar-Asher and Brown, *Light is Sown* (2025), prefer "Moses de León of Guadalajara," arguing that only the *family*, not necessarily Moses himself, is documented as coming from León. **[R]** A preference by the most recent monograph, not a settled correction. The journey file states both and chooses neither.
- The Guadalajara bust's plaque asserts he was *born* in Guadalajara: a municipal claim, not the scholarly position, flagged as such rather than cited as evidence. **[A]** es.wikipedia "Luis Sanguino".

## 2. First firm date: the Guide (1264)

- A manuscript of Maimonides' *Guide of the Perplexed* was copied **for** Moses de León in 1264, the first solid chronological marker in his life. **[A]** Colophon evidence cited in Scholem and in Bar-Asher and Brown (2025). The manuscript itself was not located and no shelfmark was found.
- It shows philosophical formation well before any documented turn to Kabbalah, and fixes a floor under the birth year. Everything earlier is undated.
- The relation to Maimonides is textual and one-directional: he died at Fustat in 1204 and never travelled to Castile. The 1264 copy is posthumous circulation of the *Guide*, not a meeting. **[A]**

## 3. Castilian Kabbalah: Gikatilla, Girona, Burgos, Toledo (1270s-1280s)

- By the 1270s and 1280s he is in close contact with **Joseph Gikatilla** (1248-after 1305), with an early disagreement over alphabetic versus theosophical models, followed by convergence. **[R]** Bar-Asher and Brown (2025). Gikatilla had studied under **Abraham Abulafia** c.1272-74 **[A]**; Abraham Abulafia stands in QUEUE.md as approved but unresearched, has no journey file, and is not cited as an atlas interlock.
- **Correction carried from the pool.** The pool staged this friendship as a Guadalajara scene tagged [A]. Gikatilla was born at Medinaceli, worked chiefly at Segovia, and died at Peñafiel; no source places him at Guadalajara. Downgraded to **[R]** and written as a doctrinal relation traced through parallel passages, not a scene in one room.
- He also draws on **Gerona Kabbalah** (Ezra and Azriel) and on **Todros Abulafia** and **Moses of Burgos**. **[R]** He is not recorded travelling to Girona or Burgos, and per the standing stop rule no stop is staged at either; the debts are stated in a campa at his own Guadalajara pin.
- **Patron:** Joseph ben Todros ha-Levi Abulafia of Toledo. **[A]** No source places Moses physically at Toledo; the journey file says so in `date_confidence` rather than implying residence. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's Toledo pin (39.8578, -4.0264) sits ~360 m northeast of the Sinagoga del Tránsito it names; the file uses **39.8557, -4.0296** (OpenStreetMap).

## 4. The Nameless Composition and the question of the court (c.1284)

An unsigned "Nameless Composition," reconstructed by recent scholarship as a major early work of his, is placed by Bar-Asher and Brown in a patronage context. **[R]**

**Contradiction, not resolved.** Both pool lenses attached this to the court of **Sancho IV** (r. 4 April 1284 - 25 April 1295). The OUP abstract for *Light is Sown* situates it instead in **"Alfonsine Castile's cultural context"**, i.e. Alfonso X (r. 1252-1284). The date 1284 falls exactly on the reign boundary and cannot arbitrate. **The journey file drops the Sancho IV name and writes "Alfonsine Castile"**, and states in `date_confidence` that the patronage context is reconstructed and that no source names Moses as holding office at any court.

## 5. The signed Hebrew corpus (1286-1294)

The only tightly dated stretch of the life, all at Guadalajara, the best-attested centre of his mature activity **[A]**.

| Work | Date | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| *Shoshan Edut* | 1286 | [A] dated colophon |
| *Sefer ha-Rimmon* | 1287 | [A] ed. Wolfson 1988 |
| *Or Zaru'a* | 1288-89 | [A] colophon |
| *Ha-Nefesh ha-Hakhamah* | 1290 | [R] colophon names the pupil Jacob |
| *Mishkan ha-Edut* | 1290 **or** 1293 | contested, see below |
| *Shekel ha-Kodesh* | 1292 | [A] closes the tight sequence |
| *Maskiyyot Kesef* | after 1293 | [A] last securely signed |

- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The pool's chronology lens dates *Mishkan ha-Edut* to **1290**; its own geography lens hedges to "early 1290s"; the conventional dating is **1293**. No source consulted supports 1290. The journey file uses 1293 as an ordering date and states the disagreement in `date_confidence`. Bar-Asher's 2013 critical edition of the same work **could not be confirmed** in Crossref or Open Library; cited as the pool gives it, with the failure noted.
- **Lost works: the bibliographies disagree, and one is wrong.** The pool listed *Sefer Pardes*, *Sha'arei Tzedek*, a Song of Songs commentary, and an anti-Sadducee/Karaite polemic **[R]**. he.wikipedia "משה דה ליאון" instead lists *Tappuḥei Zahav*, *Sefer ha-Pardes*, and *Mashal ha-Kadmoni*; only *Sefer ha-Pardes* overlaps, and *Mashal ha-Kadmoni* is a work of **Isaac ibn Sahula**, so that list is itself unreliable. The journey file names the disagreement rather than picking a list. Needs a named bibliographic authority the research did not reach.
- ***Sefer ha-Shem***, printed 1605 under his name, is assigned by **Scholem** to a different, later Moses. **[A]** Later catalogues list it as his regardless.

## 6. The Zoharic material (1280s, undated)

No colophon, no date, no signature. Everything here is inference from the shape of the corpus and from later testimony.

- Aramaic material circulates in Castile presented as the work of **Shimon bar Yochai**. **[A]** Scholem noted frequent errors in Aramaic grammar and traces of Arabic and Castilian sentence patterns as evidence against genuine antiquity. **[A]** *Major Trends* (1941).
- **Alinda Damsma** (2018) found Zoharic Aramaic archaizing and literary rather than a simple medieval speaker's fingerprint, separating dating from individual authorship. **[A]** **Citation corrected:** the actual work is the chapter "The Aramaic of the Zohar: The Status Quaestionis," in *Jewish Languages in Historical Perspective*, 28 June 2018 (Crossref), not a standalone survey.
- **Daniel Abrams** (2009) argued no single bounded "Zohar" existed as a book before later manuscript collection and the 1558-60 printings; more than twenty distinguishable groupings circulated as separate units. **[R]** **Eitan Fishbane** read the Zohar as mystical fiction constructing an imagined ancient Galilee **[A]**; **citation corrected**, the 2009 date belongs to "The Scent of the Rose," *Prooftexts*, while the sustained literary reading is *The Art of Mystical Narrative*, Oxford, **2018**.
- Some informants attributed his productivity to **shem ha-kotev**, "the Name of the Writer." **[R]** An early emic supernatural explanation, recorded as a period belief about him, not as evidence of a technique.

## 7. The road years and Ávila (1291-1305)

- Around 1291-92 his travel increases **[R]**; by the 1290s and 1300s he is moving through **Valladolid** and **Ávila** **[A]**, roughly thirty years in total across Guadalajara and Valladolid before the final move to Ávila **[R]**. No source records his reason for the shift. Age, patronage, and the general itinerancy of Castilian Jewish scholars have all been proposed; none is his own stated motive. Stated as a gap.
- **Joseph ha-Levi at Talavera** claims to have tested him, asking him to reproduce from memory a passage missing from his supposed source copy, and finding the replacement identical. **[R]** The tradition preserving the story preserves the counter-reading with it: a man who wrote the passage would need only his own master copy. Left contested.

## 8. Isaac of Acre, and the death at Arévalo (1305)

- **Isaac's own history, corrected.** The pool framed him as setting out from Acre for Castile "drawn by reports of the new mystical texts." Per en.wikipedia "Isaac ben Samuel of Acre" he was at Crusader-held Acre when Al-Ashraf Khalil's Mamluk army took it in **1291**, was arrested and imprisoned, escaped the massacre, and **went to Spain in 1305**. Flight and investigation are separate events fourteen years apart. **Acre gets no stop**: it is Isaac's ground, not Moses de León's.
- In 1305 the two meet at **Valladolid**; Moses swears he possesses an old manuscript written by Shimon bar Yochai himself, at his home in Ávila, and promises to show it; he then falls ill and dies at **Arévalo** travelling home, before Isaac can inspect anything. On the surviving record he never confessed and maintained the claim to the end. **[A]** Isaac via Zacuto, *Sefer Yuhasin*, ed. Filipowski 1857, pp. 88-89.
- **Coordinate correction.** The pool's afterlife lens put Arévalo at 41.0631, -4.7256, about 500 m west of the town, contradicting its own geography lens (-4.7195). The journey file uses **41.0632, -4.7199** (OpenStreetMap) at both stops.
- **No grave.** No headstone, burial record, or identified site; the medieval Jewish cemetery did not survive 1492 intact. **[A]** as a stated gap, confirmed across en./es./de. wikipedia.

## 9. The testimony about the house (after 1305)

Everything here reaches us through one channel and should be read as one channel.

- **Transmission.** Isaac's account survives only as an excerpt inside Zacuto's *Sefer Yuhasin*, and it **breaks off before Isaac states his own conclusion**. Printing and censorship history as in the header above. **[A]**
- **The widow's confession.** Via an intermediary at Ávila, given by Wikipedia as "David Dafan Korpo" and elsewhere as **David de Pancorbo**, the widow and daughter are reported to have told the wife of a certain Joseph of Ávila that there was no ancient manuscript and that Moses wrote the work himself, using Shimon bar Yochai's name because his own would not sell. **[R]** Not a direct quotation: it passes through Pancorbo, then Isaac, then Zacuto. **Pancorbo's own verdict** is distinct from the widow's reported words and is in the first person. **[A]**
- **The countervailing legend.** One tradition held the manuscript descended from **Nahmanides** via the Land of Israel and Catalonia, a variant having it carried by wind toward Aragon and Alicante. **[R]** The miraculous detail marks it as legend. Written in as one competing testimony, at Ávila, with **no stop at Alicante**: nobody travelled that route in the record.
- **Isaac's later position contradicts his own investigation:** *Otzar HaHayim* concludes the Zohar was written by Shimon bar Yochai. **[A]** Not resolved here. **Graetz** later turned the ambiguous widow material into a confident "forger and impostor" portrait **[A]**, a gloss the underlying testimony does not carry: a documented case of a later historian resolving an ambiguity the source left open.

## 10. Printing, translation, and the authorship debate (1557-2025)

Staged in the journey file as **two stops at his own Guadalajara pin**, describing what became of the object he made, with every scholar and printer confined to `sources` and `suggested_refs`. See section 12.

- **First printings, corrected.** The pool called Mantua "the first printed edition." en.wikipedia "Zohar": Cremona 1558 (one vol.), Mantua 1558-1560 (three vols.), Salonika 1597 (two vols.); it.wikipedia dates Cremona to **1560**; and *Tikkunei haZohar* was printed at Mantua in **1557**, before any of them. The "rival near-simultaneous editions" argument survives; the span "1559-1560" and the word "first" do not. Mantua pagination became the standard citation system, and still is. **[A]** ***Zohar Hadash*, corrected:** the pool put its first printing at Kraków 1603; en.wikipedia and it.wikipedia both give **Salonika, 5357 (1596/97)** first, Kraków (5363, 1602/03) second.
- **Leone Modena**, *Ari Nohem*, completed Venice 1639, first printed 1840: internal Jewish historical criticism connecting the Zohar to his circle two centuries before Wissenschaft **[A]**; see Dweck (2011). **Jellinek** (Leipzig, 1851): first sustained historical-critical study connecting the signed corpus to the Zohar **[A]**. **Luria**, *Qadmut Sefer ha-Zohar* (Königsberg, 1855): the traditional defence **[A]**. **Soncino**, first full English translation, 1931-34 **[A]**.
- **Luzzatto: sequence corrected.** The pool dated him to 1852 and had his case running "alongside Jellinek's." Per en.wikipedia "Zohar," Luzzatto had concluded by **1813** that the texts were "utter forgeries," arguing from cantillation marks invented in the ninth century, published arguments in **1817**, and issued the fuller treatise only in 1852, at **Gorizia**, not Padua. His case **predates** Jellinek by decades.
- **Scholem**, *Major Trends* (1941): sole principal authorship of the main body, excluding later strata such as *Ra'aya Meheimna* and *Tikkunei Zohar*. **[A]** **Correction:** the lectures were given at the Jewish Institute of Religion, **New York, 1938**; the book was published at Jerusalem in 1941. The pool's Mount Scopus coordinate (31.7767, 35.2345) falls near Sheikh Jarrah, 1.9 km from the campus at 31.7927, 35.2450. Neither is used; no stop is staged there.
- **Tishby** (1989) systematized Scholem **[A]**; **Liebes**, *Studies in the Zohar* (1993), proposed a creative fellowship rather than a solitary author **[A]**; **Meroz** (manuscript stratigraphy, confirmed to 2007) and **Wolfson** extend the shift toward multiple circles in Castile and Aragon **[R]**. The pool staged all three at one Jerusalem campus; Meroz was at Tel Aviv. Not used. **Huss** (2016) shifts the question from composition to how later communities built the Zohar's authority **[A]**. **Pritzker Edition**, Stanford, 2004-2017 **[A]**. **Bar-Asher and Brown** (2025) find reciprocal development between the signed corpus and Zoharic literature, strengthening his centrality without requiring sole authorship **[A]**.
- **Current position.** He is very likely an indispensable, probably principal, contributor or redactor; single-handed authorship of every major Zoharic composition is no longer consensus. Four hypotheses remain live: he wrote it, he compiled it, he transmitted an ancient original, he worked within a Castilian circle. **[R]** The journey file does not choose.

## 11. Atlas interlocks used

Named in a campa, because Moses de León himself stands in a real relation to them:

- **Maimonides** (`maimonides.journey.json`). The 1264 copy of the *Guide* made for him. One-directional and posthumous: Maimonides died at Fustat in 1204 and never came to Castile. The relation is that Moses de León read him.
- **Shimon bar Yochai** (`shimon_bar_yochai.journey.json` and `rabbi_shimon_bar_yochai_rashbi.journey.json`). The central relation of the file: he wrote in Rashbi's voice, attributed the corpus to him, and swore to it. **Note for the operator: two live Rashbi files exist in working/journeys simultaneously**, both carrying an independent Moses-de-León-authorship stop, and `rabbi_shimon_bar_yochai_rashbi.journey.json` cites `moses_de_leon.journey.json` by filename as a source. Dating and the framing of the widow's testimony here are kept consistent with both rather than re-litigated. The duplication is not something this file can fix.

Nobody else is named. Shared geography or a shared century is not a relation.

## 12. Apparatus relations for the operator

Moses de León is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Per the standing instruction, relations not rooted in his own life are kept and flagged here rather than silently deleted.

**All of the following are FORWARD, which the exception file does not license in general.** Each is a later person engaging with the corpus he put into circulation; none is a relation he could have had. 2. **Giulio Camillo** (`giulio_camillo.journey.json`). Rome, March 1519, Christian Kabbalah through Egidio da Viterbo's circle, descending from Pico. **Forward**, third generation removed.
3. **Arthur Edward Waite** (`arthur_edward_waite.journey.json`). London, 1929, *The Holy Kabbalah*. **Forward.**
4. **Borges** (`borges.journey.json`). Madrid, February 1920, the Café Colonial tertulia of Rafael Cansinos-Assens, translator of the Zohar into Spanish. **Forward.**
5. **Martin Buber** (`martin_buber.journey.json`). Scholem in Buber's circle, *Der Jude* 1916 and the Frankfurt Lehrhaus from 1923. **Forward**, and doubly indirect, since Scholem has no journey file.
6. **The printing and scholarship history itself** (Cremona, Mantua, Salonika, Kraków, Venice, Königsberg, Leipzig, Gorizia, London, Jerusalem, Stanford). **Forward.** This material *is* in the journey file, but only as two stops at his **own Guadalajara pin**, describing what became of the object he made, with no later person staged as a scene.
