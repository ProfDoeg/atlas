# Ernest Shackleton (1874-1922): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-22 for `ernest_shackleton.journey.json`. 44 stops, 8 segments. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Calendar: gregorian.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting only on secondary biography. **[C]** arrived in the pool wrong and was corrected; every **[C]** is itemised in §9.

The pool arrived adversarially annotated: fourteen claims carried explicit `CORRECTED:` notes, several of them latitude sign errors that would have plotted Antarctic stops in the Arctic Ocean. The corrections are listed rather than silently absorbed.

---

## 1. Kildare, Dublin, Sydenham (1874-1890)

- Born 15 February 1874 at Kilkea House, County Kildare, second of ten children of Henry Shackleton and Henrietta Gavan. **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]** Pin (52.9478, -6.8928) is Kilkea village; the house has no separate fix. **[R: placement]**
- 1880, the father leaves farming for medicine at Trinity College Dublin and the family moves to Dublin **[A to the city]**; the dossier's street address, 35 Marlborough Road, could not be confirmed **[R]**, so no Dublin stop was written and the move sits inside the Sydenham campa. 1884, Sydenham, where the father sets up practice. **[A: Huntford]**
- 1887-1890, Dulwich College as a day boy. **[A: Britannica]** The year of entry differs across sources; the file dates the stop 1887-01-01 and says so. **[R: the year]** The Keats and Browning reading, and the later recitation in the field, is standard biography. **[R]**

## 2. The merchant marine (1890-1900)

- Leaves school at sixteen for the North Western Shipping Company, first ship the *Hoghton Tower*. **[A: Britannica; Dulwich archives via dossier]** Sailing month not fixed; the file defaults to 1890-01-01.
- Second mate August 1894; first mate 1896; master mariner 1898. **[A: Britannica, from the Board of Trade record]**
- 1900, aboard the troopship *Tintagel Castle*, meets Cedric Longstaff, son of Discovery's principal private backer, and gets through him the introduction onto the expedition. **[R]** Standard in Huntford and in Fisher and Fisher; no ship's paper or letter documenting the meeting was reached, so the hinge of his whole career rests on biography alone.

## 3. Discovery (1901-1903)

- Third officer confirmed 17 February 1901 **[A]**; RNR commission 4 June 1901 **[A: service record via dossier]**; sails 31 July 1901, reaches the Antarctic 9 January 1902; winter quarters at Hut Point, where he edits the *South Polar Times*. **[A: Britannica]**
- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED: the date of the Discovery Farthest South.** The chronology lens gave 14 January 1903; the geography lens, in the same pool, gave 30 December 1902 for the same event. Wikipedia 'Discovery Expedition' supports 30 December 1902 (journey 2 November 1902 to 3 February 1903). The file uses **1902-12-30** and states the rival date in `date_confidence`. 14 January falls inside the return march.
- **Further complication, recorded.** Wikipedia 'Farthest South' notes that modern maps correlated against Shackleton's photograph and Wilson's drawing put the final camp at 82°06'S and the furthest point reached at 82°11'S, not 82°17'S. Both figures are in `date_confidence`; the pin keeps the canonical latitude.
- Early 1903, Scott sends Shackleton home on the relief ship *Morning*, invalided. **[A: Scott's own account]** The departure date is not fixed in reachable sources; the file uses 1903-03-02 with the hedge stated. **[R: the date]** The reading of the decision, prudence against the removal of a rival, is a century-old dispute and the campa presents it as one.

## 4. The years ashore (1904-1907)

- RSGS secretary 11 January 1904 to 10 November 1905. **[A: Britannica]** Pin is an approximate central Edinburgh placement. **[R: placement]**
- Marries Emily Mary Dorman at Christ Church, Westminster, 9 April 1904; Cyril Longhurst best man, breakfast at Sir Clements Markham's house. **[A: Britannica; marriage record via dossier]** Church coordinates unconfirmed. **[R: placement]** Children: Raymond (2 February 1905), Cecily (23 December 1906), Edward (15 July 1911, later Baron Shackleton). **[R: family record cited in the dossier]**
- 1906, stands for Dundee as a Liberal Unionist, fourth of five with 3,865 votes. **[A: 1906 election returns via dossier]** The failing ventures of these years, cigarettes, Hungarian mining stock, a Russian transport scheme, are standard biography, not individually documented. **[R]**

## 5. Nimrod (1907-1909)

- Departs England 7 August 1907; towed out of Lyttelton by the *Koonya* 1 January 1908; base at Cape Royds established 3 February 1908. **[A]** *Aurora Australis*, printed there that winter with Shackleton as editor, is generally regarded as the first book printed in Antarctica. **[A]**
- First ascent of Mount Erebus, March 1908, by a party that did **not** include Shackleton. **[A]** See §11.
- Beardmore Glacier found and climbed December 1908 by Shackleton with Wild, Marshall and Adams, named for William Beardmore. **[A]** The pony Socks lost in a crevasse is standard record. **[R]**
- 9 January 1909, the southern party turns back at approximately 88°23'S, about 97 geographical miles from the Pole. **[A: Britannica]** Longitude approximate. **[R: placement]** On 16 January David, Mawson and Mackay reach the calculated South Magnetic Pole **[A]**; Shackleton not present, see §11.
- Received by Edward VII 10 July 1909; knighted November 1909. **[A]** No Buckingham Palace stop: the pool's palace entry was **[R]** with no confirmed ceremony location, and both facts sit in the Tidebrook campa.

## 6. Endurance (1914-1916)

- January 1914, buys *Polaris*, built at Framnaes, Sandefjord, and renames her *Endurance* for the family motto *Fortitudine vincimus*. **[A]**
- **Canonical pin inherited.** The interlock lens identified **-34.6037, -58.3816** as an existing atlas anchor (Vito Dumas; Saint-Exupery's Aeroposta directorship). Both Buenos Aires stops use it byte-identically, not the -58.3691 the geography lens supplied. Grytviken 5 November 1914; departure for the Weddell Sea 5 December. **[A]**
- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED: the besetment date.** Wikipedia 'Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition' gives 18 January 1915, 'Endurance (1912 ship)' 18-19, Britannica 19. The file uses 1915-01-18 and states all three.
- Hull critically damaged 24 October 1915; abandoned 27 October at 69°05'S 51°30'W; Ocean Camp pitched 1 November a mile and a half from the wreck; sinks 21 November; Patience Camp from 29 December. **[A]**
- Three boats launched 9 April 1916; *James Caird* departs Point Wild 24 April; South Georgia sighted 8 May, landfall King Haakon Bay 10 May; the island crossed 19-20 May in roughly 36 hours with no map; Stromness 20 May; *Yelcho* under Luis Pardo takes off the remaining 22 on 30 August, all 28 alive. **[A]** Patience Camp (-62.5, -53.5) and the interior waypoint (-54.13, -36.85) are approximate placements on a drift track and an unsurveyed route, and say so. **[R]** Sorlle's failure to recognise them is standard record. **[R]**

## 7. The Ross Sea party (1914-1917)

In the file only where Shackleton was himself present, at Cape Evans on 10 January 1917 with the repaired *Aurora*; the deaths there are stated as what he found, not staged as scenes he witnessed. *Aurora* wrenched from her moorings 7 May 1915, stranding ten men; Spencer-Smith dies on the Barrier, Mackintosh and Hayward disappear on rotten sea ice 8 May 1916; the ship returns 10 January 1917. **[A]** That Shackleton sailed with her, after a public dispute over his role, is standard biography. **[R]**

## 8. War, book, last ship (1917-1922)

- 1917-18 South American propaganda mission **[R: dossier-only]**, carried at the inherited Buenos Aires pin. April 1918, illness at Tromso en route to a Spitsbergen venture. **[R: secondary biography, no medical record reached]**
- Temporary major 22 July 1918 **[A]**; North Russia service October 1918 to March 1919, Murmansk and Archangel **[A: Britannica; SPRI notebook via dossier]**; OBE (Military) 1919 **[A]**. The Shackleton jerkin and the Kola minerals scheme are standard record. **[R]** No separate Archangel stop, to hold the file inside its stop budget; both places are named in the Murmansk campa.
- *South* published by Heinemann 1919, second edition that December; dictated to Edward Saunders, edited unpaid by Leonard Hussey; the Ross Sea chapters assembled from other men's diaries. **[A: Wikipedia, 'South (book)']**
- **CONTRADICTION, RESOLVED AND RECORDED.** Britannica dates *Quest*'s departure 24 September 1921, the dossier 17 September. These are two departures, not a disagreement: St Katharine Docks 17 September (after inspection by George V) and Plymouth 24 September. The file uses 17 September.
- Death aboard *Quest* at Grytviken, early hours of 5 January 1922. **[A]** **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED:** the time is "about 2:50 a.m." in one source, "shortly after 02:00" in another. Both in `date_confidence`. Macklin's finding was "atheroma of the coronary arteries and heart failure." Buried Grytviken cemetery 5 March 1922 on Emily's instruction, after a Lutheran service read by Edward Binnie. **[A]**

## 9. Corrections applied to the pool [C]

| # | Pool | File, and why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Four southern-hemisphere latitudes given positive: Cape Town `33.9036`, Lyttelton `43.6033`, Hut Point `77.8419`, Cape Royds `77.5529` | All four sign-flipped: **-33.9036**/18.4232, **-43.6033**/172.7194, **-77.8419**/166.6381, **-77.5529**/166.1683. As given they plotted in the Mediterranean off Libya, off northern Japan, and twice in the Arctic Ocean. Magnitudes were right (Discovery Hut is 77°50'50"S 166°38'12"E), and the Cape Royds error was visible from inside the pool, whose neighbouring Erebus entry correctly used -77.5297. |
| 2 | Discovery Farthest South `lng -163` | **+163.5**. 163°W puts the 1902 turn near the Bay of Whales, 1,000 km east of the party, which ran down the western Barrier and named Cape Wilson (82°15'S, **164°E**). The pool's own Nimrod Farthest South used +162. |
| 3 | 1914 departure from East India Docks | **Plymouth, 8 August 1914** (50.37, -4.14). The pool reused the 1901 Discovery pin. Also: **Shackleton was not aboard**, following on 27 September and joining at Buenos Aires. |
| 4 | "not as strong as the Nimrod" letter: c. 1914, England, source Huntford | Shackleton to Emily, **26 October 1914, Buenos Aires**, SPRI **MS 1537/2/31/6;D**, printed in Tuhkuri (2025). Not findable in Huntford. The file quotes the unpunctuated primary text. |
| 5 | Besetment `-76.34, -31.5`, noted 76°34'S 31°30'W | **-76.567**, -31.5. The 34 arc-minutes were read as a decimal, about 25 km north. The longitude was right only because 30' converts to .5. |
| 6 | Mrs Chippy shot 30 October 1915 | **29 October**, per Shackleton's own diary for that date, which also names the animals: Sallie's three youngest pups and Sue's Sirius, four named dogs, not unspecified puppies. |
| 7 | Ocean Camp `-68.5, -30` | About **-69.0, -51.5**, folded into the abandonment stop. -30 is the January 1915 besetment longitude, an error of some 900 km. |
| 8 | Sinking at 69°05'S 51°30'W, "matches the 2022 wreck coordinates" | It does not; that is the **abandonment** position. Worsley's logged sinking fix, used in the file, is **-68.658, -52.442**; the wreck lies 5.4 nautical miles off at -68.739, -52.330, recorded in `date_confidence`. The pool's "six weeks after" is also wrong: 25 days. |
| 9 | Cape Valentine `-61.0333, -54.75`, "five days in open lifeboats" | About **-61.10, -54.66**: Point Wild lies 11 km west of the cape, so the pool's fix sat 5 km away and too far north. The boats launched 9 April, landed 15 April: **seven days**. The 497 days is correct. |
| 10 | Peggotty Camp `-54.13, -36.9` | About **-54.15, -37.13**. The pool put the camp 14 km east of the bay it is described as being in, and 25 km from its own King Haakon Bay pin. |
| 11 | Rio heart attack, November 1921 | **17 December 1921.** *Quest* arrived 22 November and lay a month for an overhaul; the pool dated the attack to the arrival. (For the *Quest* departure date, see §8.) |
| 12 | Last words: "You always want me to give up something. What do you want me to give up now?" | A paraphrase. Attested, from Macklin via Fisher and Fisher, *Shackleton* (1957), pp. 476-478: **"You are always wanting me to give up things, what is it I ought to give up?"**, answered "Chiefly alcohol, Boss." **[A via Fisher; R as to the notebook]** |
| 13 | "Better a live donkey than a dead lion," written to Emily | Form and medium both wrong: spoken, not written. Two renderings, not reconciled. Mill (1923), on a Sunday walk at **Tidebrook, June 1909**: "Anyway, darling, I thought you would like a living donkey better than a dead lion." Huntford (1985) p. 300, from Emily's own record: "a live donkey is better than a dead lion, isn't it?" The file uses Mill at a Tidebrook pin (51.048, 0.311, approximate). |
| 14 | "She went down bows first, her stern raised in the air" as Shackleton's words | Not his: a crew diarist's 21 November 1915 entry that Shackleton quotes in *South*, ch. V. The campa paraphrases it and says whose it is. Only "She's going, boys!" is used as a quote. |
| 15 | Grave inscription "I hold that a man should strive to the uttermost for his life's set prize," ascribed to Browning | Not Browning's text and not locatable anywhere reachable. His actual lines, verified twice (Gutenberg 4253, 'The Statue and the Bust'; the epigraph of Mill 1923): "Let a man contend to the uttermost / For his life's set prize, be it what it will!" ## 10. Afterlife material, and where it was put

Afterlife events are cited in `sources` or `suggested_refs` on Shackleton's **own** stop for the place or object, never staged as their own scenes.

- **James Caird at Dulwich College** (donated 1922 by Rowett) on the Dulwich stop, his own school; its three replicas on the Caird launch stop. **[A]**
- **Endurance22, 5 March 2022**, Falklands Maritime Heritage Trust aboard *S.A. Agulhas II*, wreck at 3,008 m, Mensun Bound directing, protected under the Antarctic Treaty System with nothing recovered: on the sinking stop. **[A; perimeter detail R]**
- **Frank Wild's ashes**, interred beside the grave 27 November 2011: on the burial stop, not a campa scene, since that is Wild's interment **[A]**. **The 2016 JRSM cardiac hypothesis**: on the Rio stop, labelled retrospective **[R]**. **The Shackleton Museum at Athy**: on the birth stop **[A]**.
- **Not carried anywhere:** the Jagger bronze at the Royal Geographical Society (1927-1932); the James Caird Society (1994). Both attested, but neither attaches to a place Shackleton himself was, and manufacturing an RGS stop for a statue is the error the rule names.

## 11. Two events where Shackleton was not physically present

1. **Mount Erebus, first ascent, March 1908.** No Erebus stop written. The ascent is named inside the Cape Royds campa, at Shackleton's own pin, with the summit party in that stop's `suggested_refs`. The conservative handling.
2. **The South Magnetic Pole, 16 January 1909.** A stop **was** written, at -72.25, 155.16, because no other pin fits it and because it is an achievement of the expedition Shackleton raised, provisioned and commanded. Its `date_confidence` states that he was not there, being twelve hundred miles away on the southern march. If the operator prefers the Erebus handling applied consistently, this is the one stop a strict reading of the rule would remove; fold it into Cape Royds and delete it.

## 12. Apparatus relations for the operator

Shackleton is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops were licensed and none written. Per the instruction not to delete silently, the relations the interlock lens surfaced that are not rooted in his own life are listed here with the direction of each.

**Forward** (never licensable without an author's grant; not in any campa): **Miguel Serrano** at Base Soberania; **Maria Orsic** and the Neuschwabenland / Base 211 mythos; **Vito Dumas**'s Southern Ocean circumnavigation; **Antoine de Saint-Exupery**'s Aeroposta line into Patagonia. The first two share only a continent with him, the last two only a Buenos Aires port, which the map already shows. **Endurance22** (2022) is forward of a different kind: it engages Shackleton's own object at his own position, so it is cited on his own sinking stop, which the rule permits.

**Backward** (licensable in principle if he were ever added to the list; not written, because none is attested as anything he read or cited): **Edmond Halley**'s 1700 "islands of ice" at 52°S, a latitude band he crossed twice; **Piri Reis** and the Mallery/Hapgood pre-ice Queen Maud Land argument; **John Cleves Symmes Jr.** and the open polar sea; **Edgar Allan Poe**'s *Arthur Gordon Pym*; **Charles Darwin**'s *Beagle* work in the Falklands and Tierra del Fuego. Any could be argued as a framework he worked inside; none is attested as something he engaged with.

**Result: `interlocks` is empty**, an honest outcome rather than an oversight. The people Shackleton actually had relations with (Scott, Wilson, Wild, Worsley, Crean, Mawson, Emily Dorman, Markham, Beardmore, Rowett, Macklin) have no journey files; the people who do have files share only latitude with him. Scott is the sharpest loss, and having no atlas file he is named freely in these campas. If he is ever queued, the Discovery and Nimrod segments are the anchor for a real two-directional interlock.

## 13. Gaps, stated as gaps

- **No primary archive was fetched.** SPRI's holdings (the North Russia notebook; MS 1537/2/31/6;D) reach this file through the dossier and, for the letter, through Tuhkuri's printed transcription. Nothing was read from a manuscript. **Tuhkuri (2025)** and the **2016 JRSM paper** were both unfetched, so their arguments are **[R]** throughout. Of Huntford, Fisher and Fisher, Mill and Caroline Alexander, only Mill was read at length.
- **The recruitment advertisement** ("Men wanted for hazardous journey...") has never been located in any contemporary newspaper; the earliest printed versions date from decades later and the South Georgia Heritage Trust treats it as apocryphal. It is not in the journey file in any form. **[R, and the assessment rests on the dossier's account]**
- **Street-level addresses** (35 Marlborough Road, Dublin; the RSGS premises; Christ Church, Westminster) were not confirmed; each pin carries the hedge. **The grave inscription** remains open, the last row of §9.
