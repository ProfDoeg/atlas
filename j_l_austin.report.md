# J. L. Austin (1911-1960): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-17 for `j_l_austin.journey.json`. Subject: White's Professor of Moral Philosophy at Oxford; author, posthumously, of How to Do Things with Words; and from 1942 head of the Advanced Intelligence Section at GHQ, the unit called the Martians, which compiled the beach intelligence for Overlord.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or a pin standing in for an undocumented place. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated, unless one source supersedes another; where that happens it is said so and both readings stay on the page.

One fact governs the evidence: **Austin published almost nothing while alive and told nobody about the war.** Three of the four books under his name were assembled from his notes by others after his death, and the military half of the biography surfaced only with Rowe's 2023 volume. Both halves are mediated, neither by him.

---

## 1. Lancashire and Fife, 1911-1924

- Born **26 March 1911 in Lancaster**, second son of **Geoffrey Langshaw Austin** and **Mary Hutton Bowes-Wilson**. **[A: SEP; Britannica; Wikipedia]**
- **Date contradiction, flagged.** encyclopedia.com gives **28 March 1911**; SEP and Britannica give 26 March. Not adjudicated; the file carries 26 March with the discrepancy in `date_confidence`.
- **Father's occupation corrected.** The afterlife lens said "schoolmaster-turned-architect," reversing the order and mislabelling the role: he was an **architect** (1884-1971), and the school connection was administrative, not teaching. He became **secretary of St Leonards School** in 1921, when the family moved to St Andrews; the school is the *family's* address, and John is sent away at thirteen. **[A: Wikipedia; HandWiki]**
- **No street or house is named** anywhere reachable for the birth. The town-centre pin is all the record supports. **[R: gap]**

## 2. Shrewsbury and Oxford, 1924-1935

- **1924, Classics scholarship to Shrewsbury School**, Kingsland site since 1882. **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**
- **1929, up to Balliol to read Literae Humaniores**; **1930, First in Classical Moderations**; **1931, Gaisford Prize for Greek Prose**; **1933, First in Literae Humaniores**, sat at the Examination Schools; **1933, Prize Fellowship at All Souls**, then the Aristotle years; **1935, Fellow and Tutor in Philosophy at Magdalen**. **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia; Nagel, LRB; Rowe]**
- Two hedges. The **Sheldonian as the prize venue** is **[R]**: customary for Oxford ceremony, undocumented for this award. The **months** of Finals (Trinity) and of the All Souls election (Michaelmas) are **[R]**, inferred from the standard calendar. No college or school archive was consulted; this stretch rests on encyclopedia entries alone.

## 3. The young dons, 1936-1940

- **c. 1936-1939, a weekly discussion group** run by Austin, dismantling Ayer's logical positivism sentence by sentence. **[A: Nagel, LRB, following Rowe]**
  - **Membership corrected.** The pool named only **Berlin** and **Ayer**; sources name **Stuart Hampshire** as a core member. Added. **[A]**
  - **Rooms not specified.** All Souls stands as the setting because Berlin held his fellowship there. **[R]** Berlin's assessment of Austin as the sharpest close reader he ever watched work reaches us through Nagel as reported speech. **[A, reported]**
- **February 1940, 'The Meaning of a Word' to the Cambridge Moral Sciences Club**, engaging Wittgenstein's Blue Book, and shortly after to Oxford's Jowett Society. **[A: IEP; Harris and Unnsteinsson]** Day and room unrecorded; city-centre pin. **[R for the pin]**

## 4. The war, 1940-1945

- **July 1940, called up**; **1 January 1941, reports to GHQ Home Forces, Storey's Gate, Westminster.** **[A: Nagel, summarising Rowe]** The call-up is folded into the GHQ stop.
- **March 1941, marries Jean Coutts.** Four children. **[A: Wikipedia; Grokipedia, 'Jean Austin']** **Description corrected:** the chronology lens called her "formerly one of his philosophy students," but she was a **current Somerville undergraduate reading Greats** (b. December 1918), and the marriage made her one of the first Oxford women allowed to stay up while married.
- **1941, seconded to MI14**, working the Afrika Korps order of battle from the War Office; inside two months deduces that Germany is about to reinforce the Italians in North Africa. **[A: Rowe via WebSearch summary; EBSCO]** Month **[R]**, inferred from the postings.
- **March 1942, promoted captain, head of the Advanced Intelligence Section, GHQ**, the unit nicknamed **the Martians**. **[A: Nagel]** **Figure corrected:** the chronology lens said it grew "to nearly five hundred," where Nagel gives **between three hundred and five hundred** from an original six or seven. **The section's address is undocumented**; central-London pin. **[R]**
- **1943, Norfolk House, St James's Square**, collating Normandy coastal-defence intelligence: beach gradients, sand load-bearing capacity, reefs, gun positions. **[A: Nagel; EBSCO; Rowe]**
  - **Anachronism corrected.** The geography lens had SHAEF at Norfolk House in 1943. The building housed **COSSAC** that year; **SHAEF was not constituted until mid-January 1944**, also there. The Torch precedent (1942) is correct. **Ultra clearance** is tagged **[R]** in the pool as a widely reported paraphrase of Rowe rather than a verbatim quotation, and that tag stands. See section 8.
- **March 1944, SHAEF moves to Camp Griffiss, Bushy Park**, and the section with it. **[A: The D-Day Story; The Royal Parks; Nagel]**
- **6 June 1944, Omaha Beach.** The section **misidentified the German 916th Grenadier Regiment** on that sector, a failure that contributed to the severity of casualties there. **[A: Nagel]**
  - **Whose stop is this.** Austin was in England on 6 June; the pin holds the intelligence product his section compiled, in the hands of officers going ashore, and the stop says so in `date_confidence`. The counterweight in the same pool, that he "more than anybody was responsible for the life-saving accuracy of the D-Day intelligence," is a later assessment with the attribution unverified **[R]**. Both are kept.
- **1944, the Martians brought under SHAEF.** **[A: Nagel]** **Corrected:** the pool's "absorbed into SHAEF" overstates it. Nagel says the unit became part of SHAEF **while retaining its independence**.
- **Summer 1944, crosses into Normandy** with Eisenhower's advancing headquarters. **[A: Nagel]** **The forward site is not named** anywhere consulted; Bayeux is a proxy pin and the month inferred. **[R]**
- **1944-45, work through Arnhem and the Ardennes**; leaves the army a **lieutenant colonel** with the **OBE**, **Croix de Guerre** and American **Legion of Merit**, and **keeps the whole record secret, including from his wife**. **[A: Nagel; Wikipedia]** The pool gives only "Continental Europe" with a regional centroid; the file uses it and flags it as not a documented posting. **[R]**
- **Invade Mecum**, the guidebook issued to Allied officers, co-produced by the section **[A: Rowe; Military Wiki; EBSCO]**, has no place of its own and is folded into the Norfolk House and Omaha stops.

## 5. Oxford, 1945-1954

- **October 1945, returns to Magdalen and to teaching**, with a more commanding, less patient manner than the don who left. **[A: Nagel]** **Gilbert Ryle: "We were all frightened of him."** **[A: quoted in Nagel, LRB]**
- **July 1946, 'Other Minds' at the Joint Session**, in symposium with John Wisdom and A. J. Ayer; PAS Supp. Vol. 20, pp. 148-187. **[A: SEP]** **Place corrected:** the chronology lens put this in **London**, but the 1946 Joint Session met at **Manchester**.
- **1947, the Saturday Mornings begin**: invitation-only, one text or problem worked slowly, sometimes a whole term; Aristotle's Ethics and Frege's Grundlagen among the texts; Grice, Strawson, Hart, Warnock among the attenders; running in term time for over a decade. **[A: EBSCO; SEP; Longworth]**
  - **Date contradiction, adjudicated.** Chronology lens **1953** against geography lens **1947**. EBSCO: "In 1947, Austin brought together a group of philosophy dons... they met on Saturday mornings during term." The file takes 1947, the reading carried by a source describing the founding, and records the gap in `date_confidence`. **Rooms fixed nowhere**; All Souls is representative. **[R]**
- **1950, Frege's Die Grundlagen der Arithmetik published as The Foundations of Arithmetic** in Austin's translation. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Publisher corrected, the pool's largest factual error outside the dates:** the geography lens gave **Clarendon Press, Walton Street** and built a scene on "Oxford University's own imprint," but it was published by **Basil Blackwell, Oxford**, with Philosophical Library, New York, for the US edition: facing-page German and English, 16s. **[A: Journal of Symbolic Logic and philosophy review records]** The pin moves to Blackwell's Broad Street premises.
- **1952, White's Professor of Moral Philosophy**, professorial fellowship at Corpus Christi, held until his death. **[A: Britannica; SEP; Wikipedia]**
- **1952-1954, the lecture course 'Words and Deeds'**, where the constative and performative distinction is first worked out. **[A: SEP; Urmson]** **Range corrected at the front:** geography lens 1951-54, against Urmson's "in each of the years **1952-4**." **Venue unsupported:** the pool tagged **Examination Schools lecture rooms** as **[A]**, but Austin's rooms are documented nowhere consulted. Demoted to a city pin. **[R]**

## 6. Harvard and Berkeley, 1955-1958

- **1955, the William James Lectures at Harvard**, twelve, delivered without a finished manuscript. **[A: SEP; Oxford Academic; Urmson]**
  - **Venue is inference, not attestation.** The pool carried **Emerson Hall** as **[A]** with no note; no source consulted documents the hall, which is inferred from the department's address since 1905. Demoted to **[R]**.
  - **Months are invented granularity.** The pool assigned 1955-02, 1955-03 and 1955-05 to the opening, the felicity lecture and the closing lectures. **Only the year is attested.** The file keeps the months **solely as ordering devices**, and says so in each `date_confidence`, since the content of Lectures I, II and XI-XII is attested and must appear in sequence.
- **1955, meets Noam Chomsky at Harvard** **[A: Wikipedia, a bare fact only]**: no date, no campus location, no account of what passed; Harvard Yard is representative and the month an ordering device **[R]**. **1956, 'Ifs and Cans' in Proceedings of the British Academy.** **[A: SEP]**
- **October 1956, 'A Plea for Excuses' as the presidential address** to the Aristotelian Society at the opening of the 1956-57 session; PAS vol. 57, pp. 1-30. **[A]**
  - **Date contradiction, flagged.** The chronology lens dates the delivery to **1957** while dating the election to 1956; the geography lens dates the address to **1956**. The session opens in October 1956, and the file follows the geography lens. **Venue undocumented:** the pool gave **Senate House, Malet Street** as **[A]**, but Wikipedia says only that the Society *continues* to meet there. Demoted to **[R]**.
  - **Quotation corrected: the pool misrendered Austin's most-quoted sentence**, opening it "Ordinary language embodies" and closing it "in the lifetime of many generations." Austin wrote: **"our common stock of words embodies all the distinctions men have found worth drawing, and the connexions they have found worth marking, in the lifetimes of many generations of men."** The corrected text is what the file carries. **[A]**
- **Autumn 1958, lectures at Berkeley**, giving the excuses seminar and the perception material later published as Sense and Sensibilia. **[A: Wikipedia]** Lecture building undocumented; Wheeler Hall stands in **[R]**.

## 7. Illness, death, and the posthumous books

- **Early October 1959, falls ill returning from Scandinavia**; tests quickly show advanced lung cancer. **[A: Rowe, 'Final Illness: 1959-1960', via Oxford Academic]** **Date contradiction, adjudicated:** both lenses date the diagnosis to **December 1959**, where Rowe puts the illness in **early October**, "very ill" by Christmas, dying by mid-January. October is taken and December printed in `date_confidence`.
- **Radiation treatment at the Churchill Hospital, Oxford.** **[A: Rowe]** **Location contradiction, adjudicated:** the geography lens placed the diagnosis at the **Radcliffe Infirmary**, flagged in that lens itself as an inference from "period Oxford medical geography" rather than a source. Rowe names the **Churchill**; the inference is superseded.
- **Dies in Oxford, 8 February 1960, aged 48**; estate later valued at **15,049 pounds, 0 shillings, 5 pence**. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP; Nagel]** **Arithmetic corrected:** the geography lens says "seven weeks after his diagnosis," which does not survive the October date; the interval is roughly **four months**. **Count corrected:** the same lens says the material was "delivered twice, at Oxford and at Harvard," but the Oxford course ran in each of 1952, 1953 and 1954, so the count is four.
- **No grave, memorial stone, cremation record, burial register entry, or Find a Grave memorial was located** in Wikipedia, SEP, Rowe's biography summaries, or a targeted search of Wolvercote Cemetery records. Dons of his generation commonly lie at Wolvercote, but **nothing found puts Austin there**: a gap in the record as searched, not a settled fact either way, and stated as a gap inside the stop rather than guessed. No memorial service location is documented either. **[R: gap]**
- **1961, Philosophical Papers**, ed. Urmson and Warnock. **[A: SEP]**
- **1962, How to Do Things with Words**, ed. Urmson from the Harvard notes, published simultaneously by **Clarendon Press** and **Harvard University Press**; inside it the performative and constative distinction is abandoned for the general theory of illocutionary force. **[A: Cambridge and Oxford Academic publication records]** **Reception, cited but never staged as stops:** per the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, **Derrida's 'Signature Event Context' (1972)** and **Searle's reply (1977)**, **Limited Inc**, **Rae Langton** on uptake and **Maggie Nelson** on Proposition 8 appear as `sources` and `suggested_refs` on this stop and never as scenes in Austin's itinerary. **[A: primary texts; Aeon Essays, essay date unconfirmed]**
- **1962, Sense and Sensibilia**, reconstructed by Warnock from the manuscript notes: a critique of sense-data theory offering no replacement. **[A: SEP]** The **title's pun on Jane Austen** is standard in the literature but appears nowhere in the pool; carried as **[R]**. **Quotation source corrected:** the pool attributed *"Usually it is uses of words, not words in themselves, that are properly called 'vague.'"* to **Philosophical Papers (1961)**; it is genuine but comes from **Sense and Sensibilia (1962), p. 126**, per two independent sources. **[A]**
- **1975, corrected second edition of How to Do Things with Words**, ed. Urmson and Marina Sbisa from the original papers, with an appendix of marginal notes. **[A: HUP listing; CUP record]**

## 8. Interlocks, and coordinate corrections

The lens proposed a dozen connections. Most are **shared geography or shared institution and nothing more**, which the map already shows a viewer without prose saying so, and are **rejected**: **Tolkien**, **Richard Dawkins**, **John Ruskin**, **John Locke** (Oxford colleges, the endowed-chair pattern; the lens's Austin-Lewis Socratic Club claim is tagged **[R]** there and unsupported here); **Ian Fleming** (the same wartime apparatus and Ultra, but no contact attested); **al-Kindi**, **Johannes Trithemius**, **Claude Shannon** (the language-as-cipher lineage in the queue brief: a curatorial thread, not a relation between people). **No canonical pin applies**: the Kaaba (21.4225 / 39.8262) and Temple Mount pins belong to a Prague or Maharal figure, not to Austin.

**Accepted, one: `alan_turing`.** Austin's section was **cleared to receive Ultra**, the decrypt product of Bletchley Park, where Turing built the machinery that produced it: a dependency running one way, from Turing's output into Austin's assessments. Named once, in the Norfolk House campa, with `alan_turing.journey.json` in that stop's `sources`; the clearance carries the pool's **[R]** tag. Named in campas but not atlas travellers, so not interlocks, though real relations: **Chomsky**, met at Harvard in 1955, and **Ayer**, **Berlin** and **Hampshire**, his interlocutors from 1936 **[A]**. The golem framing in QUEUE.md line 908 is the **stated reason Austin was queued** **[A: QUEUE.md]**; it is a curatorial frame and belongs nowhere inside the campas.

**Coordinate corrections.** Nine pins were wrong or contested in the pool and are corrected in the file, each stated in the relevant `date_confidence`: St Leonards School, Shrewsbury School, Balliol, Somerville and Emerson Hall were misplaced by 200 to 860 m; **Magdalen carried two values, both wrong**, reconciled to **51.7524 / -1.2471**; and Corpus Christi, the Churchill and Clarendon Press each carried two plausible values 60 to 130 m apart, all on their own sites, the second of each used and not adjudicated on evidence.
