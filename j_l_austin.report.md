# J. L. Austin (1911-1960): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-17 for `j_l_austin.journey.json`. Subject: White's Professor of Moral Philosophy at Oxford; author, posthumously, of How to Do Things with Words; and from 1942 head of the Advanced Intelligence Section at GHQ, the Martians, which compiled the beach intelligence for Overlord.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or a pin standing in for an undocumented place. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated, unless one source supersedes another; where that happens both readings stay on the page.

One fact governs the evidence: **Austin published almost nothing while alive and told nobody about the war.** Three of the four books under his name were assembled from his notes by others after his death, and the military half of the biography surfaced only with Rowe's 2023 volume. Both halves are mediated.

---

## 1. Lancashire and Fife, 1911-1924

- Born **26 March 1911 in Lancaster**, second son of **Geoffrey Langshaw Austin** and **Mary Hutton Bowes-Wilson**. **[A: SEP; Britannica]**
- **Date contradiction, flagged.** encyclopedia.com gives **28 March 1911** against SEP and Britannica's 26 March. Not adjudicated; the file carries 26 March, the discrepancy in `date_confidence`.
- **Father's occupation corrected.** Not a "schoolmaster-turned-architect": he was an **architect** (1884-1971) who became **secretary of St Leonards School** in 1921, an administrative post, when the family moved to St Andrews. **[A: Wikipedia; HandWiki]**
- **No street or house is named** anywhere reachable for the birth; the town-centre pin is all the record supports. **[R: gap]**

## 2. Shrewsbury and Oxford, 1924-1935

- **1924, Classics scholarship to Shrewsbury School**, on the Kingsland site since 1882. **[A: Britannica]**
- **1929, up to Balliol to read Literae Humaniores**; **1930, First in Classical Moderations**; **1931, Gaisford Prize for Greek Prose**; **1933, First in Greats**, sat at the Examination Schools, and **Prize Fellowship at All Souls**; **1935, Fellow and Tutor in Philosophy at Magdalen**. **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia; Nagel; Rowe]**
- Two hedges. The **Sheldonian as the prize venue** is **[R]**, customary for Oxford ceremony and undocumented for this award; the **months** of Finals and of the All Souls election are inferred from the standard calendar. No college archive was consulted; this stretch rests on encyclopedia entries.

## 3. The young dons, 1936-1940

- **c. 1936-1939, a weekly discussion group** run by Austin, dismantling Ayer's logical positivism sentence by sentence. **[A: Nagel, following Rowe]**
  - **Membership corrected:** the pool named only **Berlin** and **Ayer**; **Stuart Hampshire** was a core member too. **Rooms not specified**, All Souls standing as the setting because Berlin held his fellowship there **[R]**; Berlin's assessment of Austin as the sharpest close reader he ever watched work reaches us through Nagel as reported speech. **[A, reported]**
- **February 1940, 'The Meaning of a Word' to the Cambridge Moral Sciences Club**, engaging Wittgenstein's Blue Book, and shortly after to Oxford's Jowett Society. **[A: IEP; Harris and Unnsteinsson]** Day and room unrecorded. **[R for the pin]**

## 4. The war, 1940-1945

- **July 1940, called up**; **1 January 1941, reports to GHQ Home Forces, Storey's Gate**. **[A: Nagel, summarising Rowe]**
- **March 1941, marries Jean Coutts.** Four children. **[A: Wikipedia; Grokipedia, 'Jean Austin']** **Description corrected:** not a former student but a **current Somerville undergraduate reading Greats** (b. December 1918), the marriage making her one of the first Oxford women allowed to stay up while married.
- **1941, seconded to MI14**, working the Afrika Korps order of battle from the War Office; inside two months deduces that Germany is about to reinforce the Italians in North Africa. **[A: Rowe via WebSearch summary; EBSCO]** Month inferred. **[R]**
- **March 1942, promoted captain, head of the Advanced Intelligence Section, GHQ**, the unit he nicknamed **the Martians**. **[A: Nagel]** **Date corrected:** one pool item dates the Martians to 1944, which is only the year of the landings. **Figure corrected:** not "nearly five hundred" but **three to five hundred**, from an original six or seven. **Address undocumented**; central-London pin. **[R]**
- **1943, Norfolk House, St James's Square**, collating Normandy coastal-defence intelligence: beach gradients, sand load-bearing capacity, reefs, gun positions; the section enlarged that year, renamed the Theatre Intelligence Section, transferred to 21st Army Group. **[A: Nagel; EBSCO; Rowe]** **Anachronism corrected:** the pool had SHAEF at Norfolk House in 1943, where the building housed **COSSAC**, **SHAEF not being constituted until mid-January 1944**. **Ultra clearance** keeps its **[R]** tag, being a paraphrase of Rowe.
- **March 1944, SHAEF moves to Camp Griffiss, Bushy Park**, and the section with it, **brought under SHAEF while retaining its independence** against the pool's "absorbed into". **[A: The D-Day Story; The Royal Parks; Nagel]**
- **6 June 1944, Omaha Beach.** The section **misidentified the German 916th Grenadier Regiment** on that sector, a failure that contributed to the severity of casualties. **[A: Nagel]** Austin was in England: the pin holds the intelligence product, in the hands of officers going ashore. The counterweight, that he was "more than anybody responsible for the life-saving accuracy of the D-Day intelligence," is a later assessment with the attribution unverified, and Wikipedia's casualty claim is its own wording; Rowe documents instead the **Invade Mecum** handbooks issued to some ten thousand officers and two official reports on the section's accuracy. Both kept. **[R]**
- **Summer 1944, crosses into Normandy** with Eisenhower's advancing headquarters; **the forward site is named nowhere consulted**, Bayeux is a proxy pin, the month inferred. **[A: Nagel; R for the pin]**
- **1944-45, work through Arnhem and the Ardennes**; leaves the army a **lieutenant colonel** with the **OBE**, **Croix de Guerre** and American **Legion of Merit**, and **keeps the whole record secret, including from his wife**. **[A: Nagel; Wikipedia]** The pool gives only "Continental Europe" with a centroid, used and flagged as not a posting. **[R]**

## 5. Oxford, 1945-1954

- **October 1945, returns to Magdalen and to teaching**, more commanding and less patient than the don who left. **Gilbert Ryle: "We were all frightened of him."** **[A: quoted in Nagel, LRB]**
- **July 1946, 'Other Minds' at the Joint Session**, in symposium with John Wisdom and A. J. Ayer; PAS Supp. Vol. 20, pp. 148-187. **[A: SEP]** **Place corrected:** the pool put this in **London**; the 1946 Joint Session met at **Manchester**.
- **1947, the Saturday Mornings begin**: invitation-only, one text or problem worked slowly, sometimes a whole term; Aristotle's Ethics and Frege's Grundlagen among the texts; Grice, Strawson, Hart and Warnock among the attenders. **[A: EBSCO; SEP; Longworth]** **Date contradiction, adjudicated:** **1953** in one lens and **1950** in a third pool item, against EBSCO on the founding, "In 1947, Austin brought together a group of philosophy dons... they met on Saturday mornings during term." The file takes 1947. **Rooms fixed nowhere**, All Souls standing in. **[R]**
- **1950, Frege's Die Grundlagen der Arithmetik published as The Foundations of Arithmetic** in Austin's translation, facing-page German and English, 16s. **Publisher corrected:** not Clarendon Press as the pool had it, but **Basil Blackwell, Oxford**, with Philosophical Library, New York, for the US edition. **[A: Wikipedia; Journal of Symbolic Logic review records]**
- **1952, White's Professor of Moral Philosophy**, with the professorial fellowship at Corpus Christi, held until his death. **[A: Britannica; SEP]**
- **1952-1954, the lecture course 'Words and Deeds'**, where the constative and performative distinction is first worked out. **[A: SEP; Urmson]** **Range corrected at the front:** the pool's 1951-54 against Urmson's "in each of the years **1952-4**." The **Examination Schools** venue, tagged **[A]** in the pool, is documented nowhere consulted; demoted to a city pin. **[R]**

## 6. Harvard and Berkeley, 1955-1958

- **1955, the William James Lectures at Harvard**, twelve, delivered without a finished manuscript. **[A: SEP; Oxford Academic; Urmson]** **Venue is inference:** **Emerson Hall** came through the pool as **[A]**, but no source consulted documents the hall; it is inferred from the department's address since 1905, and demoted to **[R]**. **Months are invented granularity:** only the year is attested, and 1955-02, -03 and -05 stay **solely as ordering devices**.
- **1955, meets Noam Chomsky at Harvard** **[A: Wikipedia, a bare fact only]**: no date, no campus location, no account of what passed. **1956, 'Ifs and Cans' in Proceedings of the British Academy.** **[A: SEP]**
- **October 1956, 'A Plea for Excuses' as the presidential address** to the Aristotelian Society at the opening of the 1956-57 session; PAS vol. 57, pp. 1-30. **[A]**
  - **Date contradiction, flagged.** Chronology lens **1957** against geography lens **1956**; the session opens in October 1956 and the file follows the latter. **Venue undocumented:** the pool gave **Senate House** as **[A]**, but Wikipedia says only that the Society *continues* to meet there. Demoted to **[R]**.
  - **Quotation corrected:** the pool opened Austin's most-quoted sentence "Ordinary language embodies" and closed it "in the lifetime of many generations." He wrote **"our common stock of words embodies all the distinctions men have found worth drawing, and the connexions they have found worth marking, in the lifetimes of many generations of men."** **[A]**
- **Autumn 1958, lectures at Berkeley**, giving the excuses seminar and the perception material later published as Sense and Sensibilia. **[A: Wikipedia]** Building undocumented; Wheeler Hall stands in **[R]**.

## 7. The formula that does not describe: magic and prophecy

- **Austin never wrote about magic, spells, curses, prophecy, or fairy tales**, and no source consulted places him near the subject. The three stops of this segment apply his published categories (Lectures I-II) to a class of utterance he does not name; they are pinned to central Oxford with dates that order the segment and attest nothing, each stop saying so in its `date_confidence`. Everything argued here is **[R]**; only the texts quoted are **[A]**.
- **The scholarly bridge exists and is not his.** S. J. Tambiah's Malinowski Memorial Lecture, 'The Magical Power of Words', *Man* n.s. 3 no. 2 (1968), pp. 175-208, reads ritual spell-language explicitly through Austin's illocutionary framework, and his 1979 Radcliffe-Brown Lecture develops magical ritual as an "imperative transfer". Cited as a source on Austin's own stop, staged nowhere. **[A]**
- **The Briar Rose texts, and four corrections to how the pool rendered them.** **[A: Grimm, KHM 50, trans. Margaret Hunt, 1884; Perrault, 1697]** (1) **No christening in Grimm**: the occasion is a feast for the birth, the christening being Perrault's, the two blended in the pool. (2) **"Wise women" in the German**, fairies being Perrault's and reaching Grimm through loose translations. (3) **Perrault's mitigating fairy is not summoned from afar**: she is one of the seven invited godmothers, hidden behind the hangings so as to speak last, and the old fairy was omitted because she was believed dead after fifty years shut in a tower, not out of a snub. (4) **Quotations restored to one translation**, Hunt for both the curse and its amendment, where the pool blended Hunt with Ashliman.
- **Contradiction printed, not resolved.** The translations diverge: Hunt's "fall down dead" against Ashliman's "fall over dead" and Taylor/Edwardes's merely "wounded by a spindle". The file follows Hunt and names the translator in `quote_source`.
- **Nick Land and hyperstition.** Attested: Land coined the term in a 1995 issue of *Catacomic* at Warwick's Cybernetic Culture Research Unit, defining a hyperstition as "an element of effective culture that makes itself real". **[A]** Interpretive: that it is built on Austinian ground. **No citation of Austin has been located in Land's hyperstition writings**, and the two never met, Austin dying in February 1960 and Land born in 1962, two years later and not one as the pool had it. **[R]**
- **Quotation provenance corrected.** "Hyperstitions by their very existence as ideas function causally to bring about their own reality" is not Carstens's commentary: he is the interviewer and Land the speaker, in 'Hyperstition: An Introduction', 0rphan Drift Archive (2009), and the sentence opens "Superstitions are merely false beliefs, but hyperstitions...". The ***Fanged Noumena* locus for the "Digital Hyperstition" fragment stays unverified**, only secondary commentary having been reached and the full-text host returning HTTP 403; kept out of the file. **[R]**
- **Three corrections inside Austin's own text.** (a) The ship formula is uttered "when smashing the bottle against the **stem**", not the hull. (b) The felicity-conditions sentence was truncated at both ends in the pool, losing "Speaking generally, it is always necessary that" and the closing clause about mental acts and further utterances; the file quotes Lecture I's performative sentence in full instead. (c) **"Stating is performing an act" is a clause, not a sentence**; the whole of it, from p. 139, sits in `suggested_refs`. **[A]**
- **Lecture numbering corrected.** The locutionary, illocutionary and perlocutionary trichotomy is introduced in **Lecture VIII**, not IX, and elaborated in IX and X; the collapse of the performative and constative distinction runs through **XI and XII**. **[A]**

## 8. Illness, death, and the posthumous books

- **Early October 1959, falls ill returning from Scandinavia**; tests quickly show advanced lung cancer. **[A: Rowe, 'Final Illness: 1959-1960']** **Date contradiction, adjudicated:** the pool dates the diagnosis to **December 1959**, where Rowe has the illness in **early October**, "very ill" by Christmas, dying by mid-January. October is taken, December printed in `date_confidence`.
- **Radiation treatment at the Churchill Hospital, Oxford.** **[A: Rowe]** **Location contradiction, adjudicated:** the pool placed the diagnosis at the **Radcliffe Infirmary**, an inference from "period Oxford medical geography"; Rowe names the **Churchill**, superseding it.
- **Dies in Oxford, 8 February 1960, aged 48**; estate later valued at **15,049 pounds, 0 shillings, 5 pence**. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP; Nagel]** **Arithmetic corrected:** "seven weeks after his diagnosis" does not survive the October date; the interval is roughly **four months**. The lecture material was delivered four times, not twice.
- **No grave, memorial stone, cremation record, burial register entry, or Find a Grave memorial was located** in Wikipedia, SEP, Rowe's summaries, or a targeted search of Wolvercote Cemetery, where dons of his generation commonly lie. Stated as a gap inside the stop rather than guessed; no memorial service location is documented either. **[R: gap]**
- **1961, Philosophical Papers**, ed. Urmson and Warnock; **1962, How to Do Things with Words**, ed. Urmson from the Harvard notes, published simultaneously by **Clarendon Press** and **Harvard University Press**; **1975, the corrected second edition**, ed. Urmson and Marina Sbisa, with an appendix of marginal notes. **[A: SEP; Cambridge and Oxford Academic records; HUP]**
- **1962, Sense and Sensibilia**, reconstructed by Warnock from the manuscript notes: a critique of sense-data theory offering no replacement. **[A: SEP]** The **title's pun on Jane Austen** is standard in the literature but appears nowhere in the pool; carried as **[R]**. **Quotation source corrected:** the "uses of words, not words in themselves" line is from **Sense and Sensibilia, p. 126**, not Philosophical Papers. **[A]**
- **Reception, cited but never staged as stops:** **Derrida (1972)**, **Searle's reply (1977)**, **Rae Langton** on uptake, all as `sources` and `suggested_refs` only. **[A]**

## 9. Interlocks, and coordinate corrections

**Rejected, as shared geography or shared institution and nothing more**, which the map already shows a viewer: **Tolkien**, **Dawkins**, **Ruskin**, **Locke** (Oxford colleges and the endowed chair; the pool's Austin-Lewis Socratic Club claim is unsupported here); **Ian Fleming** (the same wartime apparatus, no contact attested); **al-Kindi**, **Trithemius**, **Shannon** (the queue brief's language-as-cipher lineage, a curatorial thread and not a relation between people). **No canonical pin applies.**

**Accepted, two.** `alan_turing`: Austin's section was **cleared to receive Ultra**, the decrypt product of Bletchley Park, where Turing built the machinery that produced it, a dependency running one way into Austin's assessments; named once, in the Norfolk House campa. `nick_land`: named once, in the closing Briar Rose campa, for the attested 1995 coinage only, the genealogical claim confined to `date_confidence` and `suggested_refs`, and **no Land scene staged in Austin's itinerary**.

**Deliberately not named in any campa.** `brothers_grimm` has a journey file and the Kinder- und Hausmarchen is quoted twice, but Austin has no relation to the brothers: the tale enters through this pass's own reading, so the text is cited and the men stay out of the prose. `john_searle`, queued and unresearched, did his doctorate under Austin: a real relation with no file yet to point at.

**Coordinate corrections.** Nine pins were wrong or contested, each correction stated in the relevant `date_confidence`: St Leonards, Shrewsbury, Balliol, Somerville and Emerson Hall were out by 200 to 860 m; **Magdalen carried two values, both wrong**, reconciled to **51.7524 / -1.2471** (a third pool value, 51.7523 / -1.2495, is a further regression and is not used); Corpus Christi, the Churchill and Clarendon Press each carried two plausible values 60 to 130 m apart, the second of each used.
