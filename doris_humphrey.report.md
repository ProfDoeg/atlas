# Doris Humphrey (1895-1958): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-29 for `doris_humphrey.journey.json`. Slug: `doris_humphrey`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or
single-source chronology not independently confirmed. Contradictions are flagged and left
open. Gaps are stated as gaps.

Three grades of evidence: **(1)** two general encyclopaedias (Britannica, English
Wikipedia) agreeing on roughly twenty dated facts, **[A]**; **(2)** the NYPL Doris
Humphrey Collection finding aid (`archives.nypl.org/dan/19673`), quoting her letters
verbatim with citation codes, where a quotation recovered word for word is **[A]** and the
narrative built on it **[R]**; **(3)** an institutional chronology of premieres reachable
only as a secondary compilation, on which almost every performance date below is **[R]**.

---

## 1. Oak Park and Chicago, 1895-1917

Born **Doris Batcheller Humphrey, 17 October 1895, Oak Park, Illinois**, only child of
Horace Buckingham Humphrey and Julia Ellen Wells Humphrey **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia,
independently]**. Attended the **Francis W. Parker School**, Chicago, and trained under
**Mary Wood Hinman** **[A: Wikipedia for the school; Britannica names Hinman among her
early teachers]**; the 1900 entry year and the "around age eight" start of the Hinman
training are **[R]**. **1913, age 18: opened her own dance school [A: Britannica,
verbatim]**, with Julia managing and playing for it, and Humphrey finding it limiting,
both **[R]**.

Three quotations recovered verbatim and used in the journey file. **C91, 1897**, Julia to
"Auntie May", the circus prediction and the watchword "upity" **[A as text]**; *caveat:*
two fetches of the same page returned it with minor wording differences, so the string
carries a transcription caution despite the A tag. **C160, 1903**, Julia to Horace from
**Green Lake, Wisconsin** **[A as text]**; *gap:* the finding aid establishes where the
letter was written, not that Doris was there, and her presence is inference flagged in
`date_confidence`. A Parker teacher's "very ambitious very dependable child" **[A as text,
speaker unnamed in the finding aid]**.

**Gap.** No birth address, no address for the Oak Park school, no Chicago residence. All
Illinois coordinates are town or neighbourhood centres and say so.

## 2. Denishawn, 1917-1928

**Entered the Denishawn School, Los Angeles, 1917 [A: Wikipedia; encyclopedia.com,
"Joined the Denishawn dance school and company in Los Angeles in 1917"; Britannica]**, at
**600 St. Paul Avenue, established summer 1915 [A: Wikipedia, "Denishawn School of Dancing
and Related Arts"]**.

**Coordinate corrected.** The pool carried two wrong pins, `34.0522/-118.2437` (City Hall,
~1.6 km off) and `34.0592/-118.2822` (~2.1 km off, not on the street). Nominatim gives
Saint Paul Avenue at **34.0526/-118.2615**, used in the journey file.

Early works named without premiere dates: **Valse Caprice (Scarf Dance), Soaring, Scherzo
Waltz [A: Britannica]**. Every specific premiere is **[R]**: Valse Caprice, 7 Jan 1920,
Egan Little Theatre; Bourree, 5 Apr 1920, Potter Theatre, Santa Barbara (demolished,
coordinates approximate); Soaring and Sonata Pathetique, 20 Sep 1920, Spreckels Theatre,
San Diego, the theatre itself **[A]** and open in 1920, coordinate refined to
**32.7153/-117.1634**.

**The Asian tour. CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED NOT RESOLVED.** The institutional chronology
gives departure **August 1925**, return **26 November 1926**: roughly **fifteen months**.
Wikipedia and the popular literature say **"toured the Orient for two years."** The
journey file states the fifteen-month reconstruction, names the two-year figure as what
was repeated afterward, and does not adjudicate. Both in-tour works (Singapore, 15 Jul
1926; Kobe, 23 Oct 1926) are **[R]**.

**Denishawn House, Stevenson Place, the Bronx, opened 1927**, Humphrey and Weidman running
much of the teaching while St. Denis and Shawn toured **[A: Wikipedia; corroborated in
`ruth_st_denis.journey.json`]**. **COORDINATE CONFLICT, FLAGGED NOT RESOLVED.** The
canonical atlas pin, inherited byte-identical, is **40.8448/-73.8648**; Nominatim's fix on
Stevenson Place gives **40.8825/-73.8945**, about **4.6 km north**. Byte-identical
inheritance wins, so the journey file uses the canonical value and states the discrepancy
in `date_confidence`. **This is a live defect in the atlas, not in this file:** if
Nominatim is right, `ruth_st_denis.journey.json` needs the same fix and both should move
together. Operator decision.

**The break, 1928**: left Denishawn with Weidman, moved to New York **[A: Britannica]**.
The June 1928 **Westport, Connecticut** letter (C270) is **[A as text]** and is the
strongest single document in this life: "More important to me than any organization, is
the dance, and more important than loyalty is sincerity."

## 3. Humphrey-Weidman, 1928-1936

**First independent concert, 28 October 1928, Civic Repertory Theatre**, with Sarabande
and Water Study **[R for the date; A for the address, 107 West 14th Street, Wikipedia]**.
Water Study danced without music is attested across the general literature and is among
the Dance Notation Bureau's notated works.

**Quote dates corrected against the finding aid's container list.** All four are **[A
verbatim]**; only the dates the pool attached were wrong. C271 "The group is the flowering
of the dance" is **1928** (the pool's "October 1928" was narrative context, not a date on
the letter). C274 "art... is after all really a disease" is **1929**, not 1928-29. C267
"dinky little dances..." is **1927**, not 1927-28, and belongs to the origin of Color
Harmony. C396 "Charles and I try to convince the rest of America..." is **1937**, not
1936-37; unused in the journey file, recorded as available.

**Guild Theatre programme, 31 March 1929** (Air on a Ground Bass, Gigue, Concerto in A
Minor, Speed, Life of the Bee) **[R]**. **VENUE CORRECTED.** The pool gave "33 West 52nd
Street". The Guild Theatre stood, and stands, at **245 West 52nd Street**, opened 1925 for
the Theatre Guild and since 2005 the **August Wilson Theatre [A: Wikipedia]**. The pool's
coordinate was ~430 m east, consistent with the bad address; the journey file uses
~**40.7622/-73.9855**.

**Jose Limon's enrolment. CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED.** The pool and
`jose_limon.journey.json` both give **1930**; English Wikipedia's Limon article gives
**1929**, after a performance that turned him from painting to dance. The journey file
uses 1929 and names the conflict. **If the atlas prefers internal consistency,
`jose_limon.journey.json` is the file that would move.** Operator decision. The rest is
agreed: most consequential alumnus, own company 1946 with Humphrey as artistic director,
twenty-nine years in her orbit.

**Dance Repertory Theatre** with Weidman, **Martha Graham** and Helen Tamiris, first
season 5-9 January 1930, Maxine Elliott's Theatre **[R for dates and venue]**. **The
Shakers (Dance of the Chosen)**, second season, 1-4 February 1931 **[R for the premiere; A
that the work exists and is still danced, being among the DNB's 24 notated works]**; the
journey prose treats it as a theatre piece about bodies under a rule, not an ethnographic
document, which is the reading the pool itself recommends.

**Charles Francis Woodford**, met aboard the **S.S. Dominica**, West Indies, May 1931
**[R]**. Married **1932 [A: Britannica; encyclopedia.com independently gives June 1932]**;
the day (10 June) and Morrisville, Pennsylvania come from Humphrey's own postscript (C302)
**[A as text]**, not checked against a civil record. Coordinate corrected to
**40.2082/-74.7755**.

## 4. Broadway, Bennington, the trilogy, 1932-1938

**Run Little Chillun**, Hall Johnson's Black folk opera, Lyric Theatre, 1 March 1933 **[R
for date and venue]**. She later said Bahamian movement observed on the 1931 voyage
informed a ritual dance. The evidence supports cultural borrowing and mediated authorship
by a white choreographer working with Black and Bahamian source material; it does not
support any claim by her of sole invention, or any known legal dispute. The journey file
states that flat and claims nothing further.

**Son Charles Humphrey Woodford born 1933 [A: Britannica]**, 8 July **[R]**; **Broadway
debut 1933, The School for Husbands [A: Britannica]**; **Life Begins at 8:40, 1934 [A:
Britannica]**. **Horace Humphrey's death, 17 March 1934**, after a 1930 stroke, is **[R]**
only: the place is "Illinois" with no town, and **no reachable source says whether
Humphrey travelled for it**, so the journey file places the news at her own New York pin
rather than staging her at a graveside she may not have attended.

**Bennington School of the Dance, founding faculty, 1934**, with **Martha Graham**,
Weidman and **Hanya Holm**, under **Martha Hill** **[A twice over: Wikipedia, "Doris
Humphrey"; Wikipedia, "Martha Hill", which dates the school to 1934, names the four, and
runs it to 1942]**. **Coordinate note:** the pool's Bennington pins (`42.8779/-73.1968`,
`42.893/-73.204`) are both wrong, the college being in **North** Bennington at Nominatim's
`42.9204/-73.2385`; the atlas canonical pin, **42.9247/-73.2367**, is ~500 m from that and
is what the journey file inherits.

**New Dance, Bennington, 3 August 1935 [R]**; **Theatre Piece** and **With My Red Fires**,
1936 **[R]**; **Wallingford Riegger** as composer **[R]**. **TAG DEFLATION APPLIED:** the
pool tagged this block **[A]** on the Wikipedia Doris Humphrey article, whose own note
admits it only "corroborates Bennington's centrality" and which carries neither the date
nor the trilogy detail. **"Never performed together as one evening in her lifetime" is
unsourced and could not be checked**; the journey file hedges it as "so far as the record
shows". **"Theatre Piece is generally considered lost" is [R], supported indirectly** by
its absence from the DNB's 24 notated works, which include the other two.

**Limon named a Bennington Fellow, 1937**, with Humphrey, Weidman and Holm teaching the
same six weeks **[A via `jose_limon.journey.json`]**.

**Passacaglia and Fugue in C Minor, 1938. TITLE CORRECTED.** The pool called it
"Passacaglia in C Minor" in both lenses; the work is **Passacaglia and Fugue in C Minor**,
so given by encyclopedia.com and the DNB score list. **[A for title and year; R for the
5 August date and the Bennington venue.]** **To the Dance** (Bloomington, 23 Feb 1937) and
its Dance Magazine prize are **[R]**, with sources disagreeing on whether the prize is
dated 1937 or 1938.

**Carried in the pool, not written into the journey file, for length not doubt**, all
**[R]**: Sonata Tragica (1923), Hoop Dance (1924), Lake Placid (1929), Die glueckliche
Hand (1930), Bloch String Quartet (1931), Cleveland stadium Carmen and Aida (1932), To the
Dance (1937), Green Mansions (1947).

## 5. The Studio Theatre and the end of dancing, 1939-1945

**Mills College, Oakland, 1939 [R]**. **Perry-Mansfield Camp**, teaching from 1932 and the
1939 meeting with **Helen Mary ("Hay") Robinson**, both **[R]**; the Robinson
correspondence is **[A]** as a series in the finding aid. **Humphrey-Weidman Studio
Theatre opened 26 December 1940 [R]**, with **no street address found**, so the journey
file uses an explicit midtown placeholder and says so. **Inquest, 5 March 1944, her last
performing role [R for the date]**.

**Retirement from performing: 1944. STATED FLAT, HEDGE DELETED.** The pool hedged that
"the dossier instead dates formal retirement to 1945". **Three independent sources give
1944** and none gives 1945: Wikipedia ("After arthritis forced her retirement from
performing in 1944"), Britannica, and encyclopedia.com, which calls Inquest her final
public performance. The 1945 claim is dropped from both artifacts.

## 6. Artistic director to another body, 1946-1953

**1946: Director of Dance at the 92nd Street YM-YWHA**, and **artistic director of the new
Jose Limon Dance Company**, Limon keeping only principal dancer and choreographer **[A for
the company and her directorship: Wikipedia, "Jose Limon"; `jose_limon.journey.json`. R
for the Y post.]** Coordinate corrected to **40.7830/-73.9528**.

**Lament for Ignacio Sanchez Mejias**, after **Federico Garcia Lorca**'s elegy, and **The
Story of Mankind**, said to premiere the same day at Bennington, 11 July 1946 **[R]**; the
company's formal Bennington debut, Humphrey directing from the wings and Pauline Lawrence
making costumes, is **[A via `jose_limon.journey.json`]**. **Day on Earth**, New York debut
at the **Belasco Theatre**, 1947 **[A via the same file]**. **Connecticut College faculty,

faculty, 1951**, again under Martha Hill **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**, with Limon on the

**Mexico City, December 1951, mounting Passacaglia at the Instituto Nacional de Bellas

## 7. The last years and the afterlife, 1952-1959

**Merry-Go-Rounders, founded 1952 [A: Britannica]**; **Capezio Award, 9 March 1954 [R]**;
**Dance Magazine Award, 1958 [R]**.

**March 1956 letter to Hay Robinson (C707). QUOTATION CORRECTED.** The pool rendered it as
one utterance with an ellipsis: "alright... that's all I ask of them". The finding aid
reads: *Doris reported to Hay in March 1956 that her legs were "alright," they got her
around, and "that's all I ask of them" (C707).* **The words between the fragments are the
compiler's narration, not Humphrey's.** The journey file quotes only the second fragment.
Recipient, date and citation code all **[A]**.

**European tour with the Limon company, August 1957, despite chronic illness [R]. Gap:**
no itinerary, no city, no venue in any reachable source. The journey file uses the
canonical Paris pin to place a tour whose route is undocumented, and says exactly that in
`date_confidence`. **If the operator would rather have no European stop than an
undocumented one, this is the stop to cut.**

**Died 29 December 1958, New York City, aged 63, of cancer [A: Britannica and Wikipedia,
independently]**. **Flower and Fifth Avenue Hospital: [R], downgraded** from the pool's
[A], whose cited source gives only "December 29, 1958, in New York City". **The 1959
misdating claim: DROPPED as uncheckable.** The pool asserted that a minor NYPL finding aid
gives 1959 and that the main chronology corrects it; neither half could be verified. The
death year is solid **[A]**, so nothing load-bearing is lost.

**Buried at Forest Home Cemetery (historically German Waldheim), Forest Park, Illinois [A:
Wikipedia]. COORDINATES RECONCILED:** the pool carried **three different** pins,
41.8744/-87.8134, 41.8697/-87.8156 and 41.8697/-87.8197; the journey file uses Wikipedia's
own coordinate for the grounds, **41.8698/-87.8198**. Burial month unverified, plot not
located. **The ground itself [A: Wikipedia, "Forest Home Cemetery (Chicago)"]:** a
Potawatomi village and burial ground until 1835; German Waldheim (1873) and Forest Home
(1876) merged 28 February 1969; the Haymarket martyrs' monument, a National Historic
Landmark, stands on the same grounds. Absent from the pool, and it is why the last stop
closes where it does.

**The Art of Making Dances**, finished 1958, published posthumously 1959 (Rinehart, ed.
Barbara Pollack) **[A: Britannica]**; in print since through Grove (1959, 1962, 1978), UK
Dance Books Ltd (1979, 1997), Grove/Atlantic (1987) and Princeton Book Company (1991) **[A:
Open Library, AbeBooks]**. **No foreign-language edition surfaced**, which is absence of
evidence, not evidence of absence. **Dance Notation Bureau [A: dancenotation.org]**:
Labanotation scores for **24** of her works, many stagings taught by Humphrey herself, and
a **December 2023** licensing agreement with **Charles Woodford**, her heir, covering those
24. Her remark on the New Dance score, "New Dance is no longer a legend, it is history", is
**[A verbatim, undated]** and is the quote on the 1958 stop.

**National Museum of Dance Hall of Fame, inducted 1987**, inaugural class with Fred
Astaire, George Balanchine, Martha Graham and Isadora Duncan; museum **indefinitely closed
since March 2020 [A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**. No stop is made of it: it postdates her and
is not her travelling. **Doris Humphrey Foundation** at **Goucher College**, Towson **[A:
Wikipedia]**, **founded 2008 [R, unconfirmed]**. **Charles Woodford's 1967 donation of the
collection to NYPL: [R], unconfirmed**, kept rather than dropped because it is
load-bearing for the archive's provenance.
