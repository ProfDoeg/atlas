# Moshé Feldenkrais (1904-1984), research report
*Prepared 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `moshe_feldenkrais`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named inline · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or the subject's own retrospective narration · **GAP** = looked for, not found, recorded as missing.

A note before anything else. This biography arrives pre-polished, because the movement he founded needed a founding story and he was a vivid teller of it. Three set pieces recur in nearly every retelling: the walk to Palestine at fourteen, Marie Curie as his teacher, and the heavy water carried out of falling France. None survives contact with a primary record. All three are kept below, tagged [R], because deleting them would misrepresent what the record is: a hard scientific and military career with a legend growing on it.

---

## 1. Slavuta and Baranovichi, 1904-1918

- Born in Slavuta, Volhynia Governorate, Russian Empire, to a Ukrainian Jewish family. **[A: Wikipedia, "Moshé Feldenkrais"]**
- Birth date 6 May 1904; eldest child of Sheindel and Mordechai Feldenkrais, a bookbinder and grain merchant active in Hovevei Zion circles. **[A: Mark Reese, *Marvelous Beyond Counting: Moshe Feldenkrais, A Life*, Reese Kress Somatics Press, 2015]** A minority of encyclopedic entries give 1900 or 1902; 1904 is Reese's date and the Guild's. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.**
- The family relocates to Baranovichi (present-day Belarus), where he has his bar mitzvah, completes two years of high school, and studies Hebrew and Zionist philosophy. **[A: Wikipedia]** for the substance.
  - **The year is not sourced.** Wikipedia attaches no date to the relocation. An earlier research pass produced "1912" as an interpolation; it is not in the cited article. Reese covers the Baranovichi childhood (cheder, Russian gymnasium, self-taught geometry from library books, local Zionist youth movement) as **[A: Reese]** but with the loose range c.1912-1918. The journey file carries c.1912 with the interpolation declared in `date_confidence`.

## 2. The walk to Palestine, 1918-1919

- Emigration year 1918, destination Palestine. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- The overland trek: departure from Baranovichi at about fourteen, after the bar mitzvah, amid postwar chaos and regional pogroms, with a small group of Zionist youths whom he is said to have guided. **[R]** This rests on Feldenkrais's own later retelling, in interviews and in remarks inside his Awareness Through Movement teaching, relayed by Reese. It is not independently documented.
  - **The two research lenses disagreed on the tag here**, and the disagreement is itself informative: the chronology lens marked the trek [R], the geography lens marked the identical event [A] citing Reese. Harmonized down to **[R]**, because Reese's own basis is the subject's narration.
- Odessa as a transit point on the route south to the Black Sea. **[R]** Reconstructed from standard period emigration routes plus secondhand material in Reese; the actual stages of his route are not documented. **GAP.**
- Landing at Jaffa with no money and no family to receive him, after roughly a year on the road, in the Second and Third Aliyah wave. **[A: Reese]** for the landing. **Sources disagree on whether it is late 1918 or 1919. Contradiction flagged, not resolved.**

## 3. The Yishuv years, 1919-1930

- Works as a laborer in Palestine among the halutzim. **[R: Wikipedia]**
- Tel Aviv, manual labor including land surveying and construction, teaching himself mathematics, physics and Hebrew at night from borrowed books. **[R: Reese]**
- Completes his high school diploma, self-taught, at the Gymnasia Herzliya, 1925. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Works as a cartographer for the British survey office in Palestine, and begins studying self-defense and jiu-jitsu, 1925. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Develops and teaches self-defense to members of the Haganah, publishing an early pamphlet on the subject, drawing on jiu-jitsu learned from manuals before any formal training, c.1929-1930. **[R: Reese; Feldenkrais's own early self-defense pamphlets]**
- Knee injury playing soccer, 1929, later re-aggravated and central to the self-treatment work. **[R: Wikipedia]**
- Sails from Palestine to France in 1930 to pursue higher education. **[A: Wikipedia]** for the emigration; **[R]** for Marseille as the port of arrival, which Reese's account and period convention suggest but which is not independently confirmed for this particular voyage.

## 4. Paris, 1930-1940

This is the densest and best-documented decade, and also the one where two dates that are usually printed together do not both hold.

- Enrolls at the École Spéciale des Travaux Publics; engineering diploma 1933. **[A: Wikipedia; Reese]**
- Doctor of Science in physics, University of Paris. **[A: Wikipedia]** for the degree. **The year is not sourced.** Wikipedia says only "during the 1930s". Dating both the ESTP diploma and a Sorbonne D.Sc. to 1933, three years after a 1930 arrival, is not supported by anything reachable; Guild biographies put the engineering degree at 1933 and the doctorate later. **GAP: no thesis title, defense date, or faculty record located.**
- **Marie Curie as one of his teachers: [R], not [A].** This line is uncited in Wikipedia and is legend-adjacent. It is chronologically possible, since she held her Sorbonne chair until her death on 4 July 1934, but no roster, course list, or memoir places him in her lecture room. Kept as tradition, tagged.
- Research assistant to Frédéric Joliot-Curie at the Institut du Radium from 1933. **[A: Wikipedia; Reese; Institut Curie institutional histories of the laboratory staff]** Present in the laboratory across the years of the Joliot-Curies' Nobel work and the early fission experiments.
- Meets Jigoro Kano, founder of judo, in Paris, September 1933; the meeting spurs his serious study of judo. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Trains under Mikinosuke Kawaishi. **[A: Wikipedia; Reese]** for the fact. **The usual date range is anachronistic at the front end.** Wikipedia's own Kawaishi article has Kawaishi in Liverpool from c.1928 and London from 1931, moving to Paris only in **1935**. Training "under Kawaishi" therefore cannot begin in 1933. The 1933 to 1935 portion of his judo training must be accounted for by other teachers in Kano's Paris orbit, unnamed in reachable sources. **GAP.**
- Black belt (shodan) 1936, among the first Europeans to hold one; co-founds the Jiu-Jitsu Club de France. **[A: Wikipedia; French judo federation historical accounts]** Wikipedia itself flags these dates as uncited within the article. The club's continued existence is **[R]**.
- Second-degree black belt 1938; teaches judo to Frédéric and Irène Joliot-Curie and to the physicist Bertrand Goldschmidt. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- The old knee worsens under judo practice; refusing surgery with a poor prognosis, he begins the systematic self-study of his own movement that seeds the Method, c.1937. **[R]** The geography lens tagged this [A] while conceding in its own note that the chronology comes from Feldenkrais's retrospective narration and is not independently documented. Corrected down to **[R]**.

## 5. The fall of France, 1939-1940

- Joliot-Curie's laboratory and personnel disperse from Paris ahead of the German advance, with French nuclear material moved toward Clermont-Ferrand. **[R]** for Feldenkrais's presence in that dispersal specifically.
- Flees France via the Atlantic port area of Bordeaux in the general exodus of June 1940. **[R: Reese]** The vessel and date of his own crossing are not established. **GAP.**
- **The heavy water: [R], not [A]. This is the single most important correction in this file.** Wikipedia asserts that Feldenkrais fled carrying heavy-water research material for the Admiralty and War Office, a claim that recurs across popular retellings, often decorated into a jar carried in a coat. The documented evacuation was carried out by **Hans von Halban and Lew Kowarski** on Joliot-Curie's instruction, leaving Paris with the heavy water, a gram of radium and the research documentation, sailing on the **SS Broompark from Bordeaux on 19 June 1940** and landing at **Falmouth on 21 June 1940**. Feldenkrais is named in none of the standard accounts. He may genuinely have been among the thirty-three unnamed scientists and technicians aboard the Broompark, and that is the most that can honestly be said. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.**
- Reaches England by sea on the Cornish coast. **[R]**

## 6. The Admiralty, 1940-1946

- Science officer for the British Admiralty until **1946**, working on anti-submarine weaponry at **Fairlie, Scotland**, developing sonar-related improvements for which he later holds several patents. **[A: Wikipedia]**
  - Both earlier lenses got this wrong in opposite directions: one gave the end date as 1945 and pinned the work in London, the other downgraded the Fairlie posting to "traditionally reported" [R]. Wikipedia states Fairlie and 1946 plainly. Corrected to **[A]**, 1940-1946, pinned at Fairlie, with a separate London pin for the Admiralty as administrative address. **GAP: the patents are referred to but never enumerated.**
- Publishes *Practical Unarmed Combat* (1942) for Home Guard volunteers. **[R: Feldenkrais, *Practical Unarmed Combat*, 1942; Reese]**
- The old knee is re-aggravated on pitching decks during the war work, prompting systematic self-rehabilitation experiments in movement and awareness, c.1943. **[R: Wikipedia]**
- Continues sharing the discoveries through lectures and experimental classes in Britain from 1946. **[R: Wikipedia]** After leaving Admiralty service he works in private industry in London. **[R: Wikipedia]**
- Serves on the International Judo Committee, studying judo kinesthetically, c.1948. **[R: Wikipedia]**
- Publishes *Body and Mature Behavior: A Study of Anxiety, Sex, Gravitation and Learning*, London, 1949. **[A: Wikipedia]** **GAP: later editions and translations not verified this pass.**

## 7. The somatic lineage

- During the London years he studies the work of G. I. Gurdjieff, F. Matthias Alexander, Elsa Gindler and William Bates, and trains directly with **Heinrich Jacoby** in Switzerland. **[A: Wikipedia]** This is the load-bearing link into the Gindler wing, and it is attested, not inferred.
- The relationship to Gindler is **study of her material, not discipleship**. No documented meeting or direct transmission between Gindler and Feldenkrais exists. **[R]** for any stronger reading. Comparative somatics scholarship (Don Hanlon Johnson and others) groups Feldenkrais, Gindler, F. M. Alexander and Gerda Alexander as parallel roots of the same field, which is a classification, not a lineage.
- **Contradiction with the existing corpus, flagged and not resolved.** `elsa_gindler.journey.json` carries a stop, "Zurich, Feldenkrais comes to Jacoby", dated **1954-01-01**, noting that no year is given anywhere and the date is a placement inside Jacoby's settled Zurich period. The research pool for this file placed the same study in **1948**, alongside the London reading. Both cannot be right and neither is sourced. This file follows the corpus placement so the two journeys agree on the map, and declares the conflict in `date_confidence`.

## 8. Israel, 1951-1981

- Returns to Israel, 1951. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Directs the Israel Defense Forces' Electronics Department before settling in Tel Aviv to teach full-time; 1954 is the date given for the transition. **[A: Wikipedia; Reese]**
- Opens a Tel Aviv practice and refines the two forms of the Method, Functional Integration and Awareness Through Movement. **[A: Reese]**
- Meets Mia Segal, 1957, who becomes his close assistant for thirty years. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Becomes personal movement trainer to Prime Minister David Ben-Gurion, 1957. **[A: Wikipedia]** The famous headstand photograph is on a **Tel Aviv beach**. **[R]** for the exact beach; the central beachfront is used as the best-supported approximation.
  - **Corrected: an earlier pass placed this at Sde Boker in the Negev.** Ben-Gurion was serving Prime Minister from 1955 to 1963, based in Jerusalem and Tel Aviv; the hut at Sde Boker was his from 1953, but the lessons and the photograph are Tel Aviv. The Negev stop is dropped, not re-tagged.
- Trains his first cohort of thirteen teachers in Tel Aviv, 1969-1971. **[A: Wikipedia]**

## 9. America, 1972-1981

- Presents Awareness Through Movement at the **Esalen Institute**, Big Sur, 1972. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Trains 65 teachers over four summers at **Lone Mountain College**, San Francisco, 1975-1978. **[A: Wikipedia; Feldenkrais Guild of North America records]** The name is period-correct: USF acquired the campus in 1978.
- 235 students begin the summer training at **Hampshire College**, Amherst, Massachusetts, 1980, his largest cohort. **[A: Wikipedia]**
  - **Corrected: one lens named the University of Massachusetts Amherst.** Wikipedia names Hampshire College explicitly, and the same lens's own chronology said Hampshire. The two campuses are about seven kilometres apart.
- Becomes seriously ill and stops public teaching after two of four planned summer sessions, 1981. **[A: Wikipedia]**

## 10. Death and afterlife

- Dies in Tel Aviv, 1 July 1984, aged 80, after declining health following a subdural hematoma and subsequent strokes that began with a fall in 1981. **[A: Wikipedia; Reese; contemporary obituaries]** **GAP: the underlying primary obituary was not read directly.**
- **Burial: unresolved.** **Nahalat Yitzhak Cemetery** (Givatayim) is sourced only to a Wikipedia *category tag*, with nothing in the article body, and the cemetery's own article does not list him among notable burials. A separate assertion in circulation names **Kiryat Shaul**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The journey file pins Nahalat Yitzhak with the uncertainty declared, because it is the better-attested of two weak claims, not because it is settled.
- A memorial plaque marks his former house in Tel Aviv. **[R: Wikipedia]** **GAP: no street address or dedication date given.**
- Norman Doidge's *The Brain's Way of Healing* (2015) is widely credited with reviving popular interest in the Method. **[R]** **GAP: web budget exhausted before this could be checked against the book's contents.**
- Modern evidence reviews are mixed: a 2015 Australian Government review of natural therapies finds no clear evidence of effectiveness; a 2022 German health-institute report finds only a "hint" of benefit for Parkinson's disease and inconsistent evidence for chronic low back pain. **[A: Wikipedia, "Feldenkrais Method"]**

## 11. Quotations

Nine quotations were gathered. **Eight of the nine are unverified aggregator attributions** (quotefancy.com and similar), each commonly assigned to *Body and Mature Behavior* (1949), *Awareness Through Movement* (1972) or *The Elusive Obvious* (1981) without edition or page. None was confirmed against a fetched primary text. Each carries its status in `quote_source`.

One is traceable, and was being **misquoted**. Source: interview with Moshé Feldenkrais, *New Realities* magazine, vol. 1 (1977), via Google Books snippet. Three alterations had crept into the circulating version: "freeing the body of" for "freeing it of", "wired-patterns" for "wired patterns", and British "behaviour" for the printed "behavior". The circulating opener, "My purpose is to allow people to", returns zero hits and appears to be aggregator framing; it is not presented here as his words.
