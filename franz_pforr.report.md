# Franz Pforr (1788-1812): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Franz Pforr, painter, co-founder of the Lukasbund (Brotherhood of Saint Luke), the group later nicknamed the Nazarenes. Born Frankfurt am Main 5 April 1788, died Albano Laziale 16 June 1812.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or interpretive framing. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**One structural fact governs this dossier.** The life is 24 years long, the working career about four, the documentary footprint correspondingly thin. Almost every sentence in Pforr's own voice reaches us through a single channel: a long autobiographical report written in 1810 for his guardian Johann Jakob Sarasin, preserved because Overbeck kept a copy, printed in 1886 inside a biography of Overbeck. Nearly everything else is written by people describing Overbeck, the Lukasbund, or the Nazarene movement. This report treats that asymmetry as a finding.

---

## 1. Frankfurt: birth and the horse painter's house (1788-1801)

- Born Franz Pforr in Frankfurt am Main, 5 April 1788. **[A: de.wikipedia, en.wikipedia, Wikidata Q705163, agreeing]**
- Son of **Johann Georg Pforr** (1745-1798), painter of horses and animals, and **Johanna Christiane Pforr, née Tischbein**, which puts him inside the Hessian Tischbein painting dynasty on his mother's side. **[A: de.wikipedia; Wikidata]**
- **Earliest instruction from his father.** **[A: en.wikipedia, "received his earliest training from his father"]** No source dates its start; the journey file places it c. 1793, marked inferred. **Gap.**
- **The father died in 1798** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, year only. German Wikipedia states instead that Pforr had lost **both parents by about age twelve (c. 1800)**, one compressed milestone with no separate date, cause, or place for the mother. **[A as the source's own framing]** **Gap: the death year of Johanna Christiane Pforr is nowhere stated in anything reached.**
- **About a year after the parents, his only brother died (c. 1801).** **[A for the sequence: de.wikipedia. R for everything else.]** Unnamed, undated, unplaced.

### Correction carried out of the research pool
The pool tagged the brother's death **[A]** at **Kassel (51.3127, 9.4797)**. German Wikipedia names no place, and Pforr only goes to Kassel in 1801, so Frankfurt is at least as likely. The journey file pins the event at Frankfurt (50.1109, 8.6821), says in the campa that neither place is documented, and downgrades it to a reconstruction. The Kassel coordinate is **withdrawn**.

## 2. Kassel: the gallery inspector's household (1801-1805)

- From **1801**, his maternal uncle **Johann Heinrich Tischbein the Younger** (b. Haina 28 November 1742, d. Kassel 22 December 1808), painter, engraver, **inspector of the Kassel picture gallery from 1775**, took the orphan into his household and directed his training. **[A: de.wikipedia "Johann Heinrich Tischbein der Jüngere": "Ab 1801 förderte er die Ausbildung von Franz Pforr ... in Kassel", and Pforr as "Sohn seiner Schwester"]** The kinship is exact: the uncle is the brother of Pforr's mother.

### Flagged wording change
The pool asserted that Tischbein "**assumed guardianship**". No source consulted says so; the German article says he *förderte die Ausbildung*, supported the training. The journey file says "took him into his household and directed his training". **[R for guardianship; not asserted.]** In tension with it: Pforr's 1810 letter is addressed to **Johann Jakob Sarasin** in Frankfurt, whom Howitt calls his *Vormund*. **[A: Howitt 1886, I.77]** A guardian existed and was not the uncle; the two are compatible, but no source states the arrangement and it is not resolved here.

### Reconstruction, marked as such
That Pforr studied the old masters of the Kassel gallery follows from his uncle's post and nothing else; no document places him in those rooms. **[R]** The pool's pairing of "father's instruction, 1798, Kassel" is impossible and has been split: the father in Frankfurt to 1798, the uncle in Kassel from 1801.

## 3. Vienna: the Academy, the friendship, the Lukasbund (1805-1810)

- Admitted to the **Academy of Fine Arts, Vienna, in 1805**, under **Heinrich Friedrich Füger**, in a strict Neoclassical tradition. **[A: de.wikipedia]** Year only.
- Met fellow student **Friedrich Overbeck** there. **[A for the fact; R for the date.]** No source dates the meeting; the journey file places it at 1806 inside the Academy span and hedges in `date_confidence`.
- **A self-portrait exists.** Commons dates one to **1807**; the pool's **1810** is unsupported, and the Städel lists two without adjudicating. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The journey file carries it at 1807, which places it in Vienna.
- **The Entry of King Rudolf of Habsburg into Basel in 1273** was begun in Vienna: the **Städel dates it 1808-1810** and Commons agrees. **[A: sammlung.staedelmuseum.de]** The Web Gallery of Art's "1809-10" is the outlier and is not followed.
- **The Lukasbund was founded in Vienna in 1809** **[A: de.wikipedia "Lukasbund" and "Nazarener (Kunst)"; en.wikipedia "Nazarene movement"]**, and Pforr's own letter dates the founding supper to **10 July 1809**, a year after the circle began meeting regularly. **[A: Howitt 1886, I.86]**
- **The six founders** are **Franz Pforr, Friedrich Overbeck, Joseph Sutter, Josef Wintergerst, Johann Konrad Hottinger, Ludwig Vogel**. **[A: de.wikipedia "Lukasbund" and "Nazarener (Kunst)", agreeing]**

### Correction carried out of the research pool (roster)
The pool's founder list, twice, gave "Hottinger, Vogel, and **Johann Nepomuk Höfel**" and omitted **Sutter** and **Wintergerst**. Höfel is in neither German article's six; he appears in the German Pforr article as a Vienna associate. The journey file prints the correct six and demotes Höfel explicitly, because the wrong roster circulates widely.

### Correction carried out of the research pool (the exit from the Academy)
The pool had both men "excluded from the academy over their break with its classicist program" in 1809. de.wikipedia "Lukasbund": when the Academy **had to reduce its student numbers in 1809**, the Lukasbrüder were **not readmitted**, an administrative non-renewal rather than a doctrinal expulsion. **[A]** de.wikipedia "Johann Friedrich Overbeck": "Aus Unzufriedenheit mit dem an der Akademie gelehrten Klassizismus brach Overbeck **1810** sein dortiges Studium ab", so Overbeck left a year later and by his own decision. **[A]** Two mechanisms, two years; the journey file keeps them apart and does not adopt the later legend of expulsion for heresy against the antique. **[R for the legend.]**

### The 1810 letter to Sarasin: the primary voice

Titled in Howitt "Geschichte des Studiums in Wien von F. Pforr an seinen Vormund Herrn Sarasin in Frankfurt geschrieben", written 1810, surviving as a copy in Overbeck's hands, verified verbatim against the scanned first edition (archive.org/details/friedrichoverbec01howi). All quotations in the journey file are the atlas's translations; **no published English edition was located**.

**Page numbers corrected against the scan.** Three pool citations were one page out: "I came here with a great longing" is **I.77**; "During this endeavor Overbeck came here" is **I.78**; "My inclination draws me to the age of the Middle Ages" is **I.87**, running onto I.88 where the signature appears. Verified as given: the envy passage at I.79, the confession at I.80-81, the terms of the friendship and the Lessing remark at I.81, the drift from the Academy and the sealing of the bond at I.86. **Translation note:** the pool's "I came here so far" mistranslates *bisher*, "hitherto"; dropped.

**What the letter contains that the secondary literature does not.** A first-person admission of envy: Pforr could not keep pace with Overbeck, resented him, and watched the friendship die "almost as it began", before confessing it and asking pardon. The reconciliation came with terms, notably that outside praise of one should strike both exactly as blame would, and the Lukasbund is that principle enlarged to six. This is the interpretive spine of the journey file, and it rests on Pforr's own text. **[A]**

## 4. Rome: Sant'Isidoro and the working years (1810-1812)

- **1810: travels to Rome** with Overbeck, Ludwig Vogel, and Johann Konrad Hottinger. **[A: en.wikipedia "Franz Pforr", "Nazarene movement"]** **Route: total gap.** No source names the road, the stopping towns, the month, or the mode of travel; the journey file's Brenner Pass stop (47.0022, 11.5058) is labelled **[R]**, a corridor reconstruction and nothing more.
- **Settled in the disused Franciscan convent of Sant'Isidoro a Capo le Case** on the Pincian slope, living communally. **[A: en.wikipedia, occupation given as 1810-1820]**
- **The nickname.** Long hair and plain dress in imitation of old German models; Romans mocked them as *Nazarener*. de.wikipedia "Nazarener (Kunst)" derives the word from the Roman hairstyle *alla nazarena* and calls this **one theory** ("eine Theorie besagt"). **[A that the article says it; R as etymology.]** The pool cited the Sant'Isidoro article, which says nothing of the kind; **corrected**.
- **Overbeck's Portrait of the Painter Franz Pforr, 1810** **[A: en.wikipedia "Johann Friedrich Overbeck"]**; **Raphael, Fra Angelico and Michelangelo over Rome**, pencil, 1810 **[A: Web Gallery of Art]**.
- **The Entry of King Rudolf of Habsburg into Basel in 1273**, oil on canvas, **90.5 x 118.9 cm**, dated **1808-1810**, **Städel Museum, Frankfurt, on permanent loan from the Historisches Museum Frankfurt am Main since 1922**. **[A: sammlung.staedelmuseum.de]** Dates and dimensions follow the museum, not the Web Gallery of Art. The Städel displays it and does not own it.
- **Sulamith und Maria**, c. 1810-11, oil on panel, **34 x 32 cm**, contrasting a sensuous Italian and a chaste German ideal **[A: Web Gallery of Art]**; German Wikipedia calls it archetypal for Nazarene sentiment **[A as the source's characterization; R as evaluation]**.
- **Sankt Georg der Drachentöter**, c. 1811, oil on wood, **28 x 21 cm** **[A: Web Gallery of Art]**, lost to view for over a century and **rediscovered in 1923** **[A for the year: de.wikipedia]**. Finder and circumstances: **gap**.
- **Vatican study.** Use of Raphael's Stanze is documented as a group practice; **no source places Pforr individually in the Stanze or the Sistine Chapel on any date.** **[R]**

### Contradiction resolved in favour of the museum, and why
Three listings for *Sulamith und Maria*: Wikidata puts the **Städel** among Pforr's collections, a summary of the **Museum Georg Schäfer, Schweinfurt** lists a work of that title and date, and the Web Gallery of Art records a **private collection**. The Schäfer collection was precisely such a private collection until it opened as a museum in **2000**, so two of the three are one holding at different dates, and the Städel's database returns no *Sulamith und Maria* among its 76 Pforr works. The journey file states **Museum Georg Schäfer, Schweinfurt**. **[A/R: reasoned, with the reasoning shown.]**

## 5. Illness, death, and the missing grave (1811-1812)

- **Tuberculosis** in the final Roman period. **[A: en.wikipedia, de.wikipedia]** Progress of the disease, physicians, treatment: **gap in every source consulted**.
- **Overbeck's Roman diary, Christmas Eve, Tuesday 24 December 1811.** Pforr surprises the brotherhood with a *Bescherung*: Overbeck's little Madonna on a lighted table dressed with foliage, a present for each of them. **[A: Howitt 1886, I.223, verbatim in the scan]** **Correction: the pool dated this Christmas 1810.** The running head reads "1811-1812", the next dated section is "A° 1812", and Howitt calls it the surprise "welche der kranke Pforr seinen Freunden und Pflegern bereitete", the sick Pforr already being nursed. The year is **1811**.
- **Overbeck's diary, Sunday 16 February 1812.** An hour of talk with Pforr recalling "our old relationship... our sweet conversations about art". **[A: Howitt 1886, I.225]** **Corrections: the page is I.225, not I.224** (I.224 carries Pforr's verses "Johannes, du junger Adler...", the New Year and Ash Wednesday entries), and **the chapter is "Düstere Schatten", not "Äußere Schatten"**. A footnote fixing Ash Wednesday at 12 February confirms the 16th as a Sunday.
- **Moved to Albano Laziale** in the Castelli Romani, and **died there of tuberculosis, aged 24**. **[A for the death; R for the move as a health measure, which is the standard reading and not a documented decision.]** Date of the move: **gap**.

### The death date, and why the "contradiction" is asymmetric
Three records give **16 June 1812**: de.wikipedia, **Wikidata Q705163**, and the **body text of the English article itself**. Only the **English infobox** gives 12 June. That is an error in one infobox, not a split between English and German scholarship, and the pool's framing overstated the doubt. The journey file states 16 June and records the outlier in `date_confidence`. **[A]**

### The grave: a documented gap
**No source located in this research states where Pforr is buried.** Targeted queries across German and English Wikipedia, Wikidata Q705163, and the open web (including "Pforr Grab Albano") returned no church, no cemetery, no plaque, no tomb. The journey file's coordinate marks the town, not a grave. A **gap, not an absence**: Albano parish and cemetery records were not consulted and may settle it.

## 6. Afterlife

- **Overbeck converts to Catholicism, April 1813**, ten months after the death. **[A: de.wikipedia "Johann Friedrich Overbeck"]** That article credits the future cardinal **Pietro Ostini** and **does not mention Pforr**. The pool tagged the causal link **[A]**; **demoted to [R]**.
- **Sant'Isidoro empties c. 1820**; the movement disperses **by 1830**, when all except Overbeck have returned to Germany. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Sant'Isidoro a Capo le Case" and "Nazarene movement"]** The pool conflated the two dispersals; **separated here.**
- **Overbeck, Italia und Germania**, 1811-1828, oil on canvas, **94.4 x 104.7 cm**, **Neue Pinakothek, Munich**, which de.wikipedia "Italia und Germania" derives from Pforr's drawing *Allegorie der Freundschaft*, his text, and *Sulamith und Maria*. **[A]** The pool tagged this **[R]** for want of a citation; **promoted to [A]** on that article.
- **Pforr's papers**: **Universitätsbibliothek Johann Christian Senckenberg**, Frankfurt (**Ms. Ff. F. Pforr**), and the **Lübeck city library**. **[A: Wikidata Q705163]** **Coordinates fixed:** the pool's 50.1247, 8.6674 for the Senckenberg is about 1.1 km off (**50.1204, 8.6531**), its Städel 50.1017, 8.6712 about 250 m off (**50.1032, 8.6739**).
- **Size of the surviving oeuvre.** The **Städel's database documents 76 works**. **[A: sammlung.staedelmuseum.de]** de.wikipedia repeats "a handful of paintings and several hundred drawings", but **no source gives a total across all collections**; the 76 is the only hard number located.
- **Nazarene influence** on the Beuron Art School and on the Pre-Raphaelites, William Dyce and Frederic Leighton. **[A: en.wikipedia "Nazarene movement"]** This traces the movement, not Pforr's contribution, and is not claimed for him.
- **No monument, plaque, or Pforr-specific exhibition** was located, as distinct from group Nazarene shows. **A gap, not an absence.** **[R]**

### Named and not reached
- **Städel exhibition pages** for Pforr: **HTTP 404** in this session, so a Pforr-specific exhibition can be neither confirmed nor excluded.
- **Albano Laziale parish and cemetery records, 1812**, and **Frankfurt baptismal and death registers** (the 1788 birth, the mother's death year): not consulted.
- **A published English edition of Pforr's letters**: none located; all translations are the atlas's own, with the German kept in the quote sources.
- **German monographs on the Lukasbund beyond Howitt**: not reached. The dossier rests on Howitt, museum catalogues, and encyclopaedia articles, and would be materially improved by them.

### Standing contradictions, left unresolved
1. **Self-portrait date**: 1807 (Commons) against a circulating 1810 with no located evidence; the Städel lists two without settling it.
2. **Guardianship**: Sarasin is Pforr's *Vormund* in Frankfurt (Howitt), while Tischbein houses and trains him in Kassel. No source describes the arrangement.
3. **Death date**: 16 June (de.wikipedia, Wikidata, English body text) against 12 June (English infobox only), asymmetric and recorded as such.
4. **Place of the brother's death**: unnamed by any source; the pool's Kassel coordinate is withdrawn.
5. **Overbeck's conversion and Pforr's death**: causal link widely assumed, attributed by the German article to Pietro Ostini instead. Carried as **[R]**.
