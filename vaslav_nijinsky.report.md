# Vaslav Nijinsky (1889/1890 - 1950): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14. Slug `vaslav_nijinsky`; journey file `vaslav_nijinsky.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim whose chain could not be closed · *flag* = contradiction recorded and left open.

**Method note.** Compiled from a verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotations, interlock, afterlife) resting on English Wikipedia and its bibliography, on Encyclopedia.com, and on books whose relevant pages were confirmed by full-text search rather than read in full. Where the pool's lenses contradicted each other, the contradiction is printed rather than adjudicated. Several claims tagged [A] have been corrected or demoted and two tagged [R] promoted; both directions are logged.

## 1. The touring family, 1886-1897

- Parents **Tomasz Niżyński** and **Eleonora Bereda**, Polish dancers on the provincial opera circuit of the Russian Empire with the Setov company **[A]**.
- Elder brother **Stanislav** born Tiflis (Tbilisi), 29 December 1886 **[A]**. Sister **Bronislava Nijinska** born Minsk, 8 January 1891 **[A]**.
- **Vaslav born in Kiev, 12 March**; *year contested*, 1889 or 1890 **[neither adjudicated]**. The Montmartre gravestone reads 1889, which is evidence of a preference and not of the fact; his age at death is therefore 60 or 61.
- Christened at **Warsaw**, the parents' home city; parish not named anywhere reachable **[A for city]**.
- Tomasz danced soloist roles at **Odessa** in the 1890s **[A: no year fixed]**. *Coordinate corrected*: the pool's pin (46.4825, 30.7233) sat a kilometre southwest of the opera house; the journey uses 46.4857, 30.7346.
- A Christmas show at **Nizhny Novgorod, c. 1894**, with the child on stage **[A]**; no stop, recorded here.
- Parents separate; **Eleonora settles the children at 20 Mokhovaya Street, St Petersburg, 1897** **[A]**. Month unrecorded.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable documents the family's income between the separation and Vaslav's admission to the Imperial school.

## 2. Theatre Street, 1900-1907

- **Admitted to the Imperial Ballet School, 1900**, aged ten; teachers include **Sergei and Nikolai Legat** **[A]** and **Enrico Cecchetti** **[A: Buckle, at second hand]**.
- **1902**: Stanislav institutionalized for mental illness **[A]**. "Some accounts place Vaslav's own brief asylum stay around this period" **[R: no account named, developed nowhere reachable, not used]**.
- **1903**: expelled after a catapult incident, readmitted a month later **[A]**.
- **1904**: **Marius Petipa** casts him in a principal role for a performance that never took place **[A]**.
- **9 January 1905**: present on **Nevsky Prospect** during the Bloody Sunday massacre; head wound **[A]**. No street coordinate in the pool; the journey uses 59.9343, 30.3351.
- **Graduation with top marks**; *contradiction*: Wikipedia and Buckle give **April 1907**, Encyclopedia.com **1908** **[not resolved; the journey uses 1907-04-01 and carries the disagreement in `date_confidence`]**.
- **September 1907**: engaged as **coryphée at the Mariinsky, 780 roubles a year** **[A]**; that summer he had danced the court season at **Krasnoe Selo** **[A]**, which has no stop.
- **1908** at the Mariinsky: *La fille mal gardée*, *Le Pavillon d'Armide*, the **Bluebird pas de deux with Lydia Kyasht** **[R: roles secure, season less so]**.

## 3. Meeting Diaghilev: a contradiction inside the pool

The chronology lens dated the introduction to **1909**, the geography lens to **c. 1908**, through the dancer's patron **Prince Pavel Lvov** **[Ostwald 1991; Buckle 1971]**. The pool's verification resolves against 1909, the year of the Paris debut rather than of the meeting; Wikipedia treats the **1908** season as the turning point. The journey uses 1908, disagreement noted. Venue nowhere fixed **[gap]**.

## 4. Ballets Russes, 1909-1913

- **May 1909, Théâtre du Châtelet**: first Paris season, *Le Pavillon d'Armide*, *Cléopâtre*, *Le Festin* **[A]**.
- **1909-1911**: lodges with Diaghilev at the Hôtel Daunou, then the Hôtel de Hollande **[R]**; folded into the Venice Lido campa.
- **1910, Venice Lido**: *correction*. The pool said Bakst **photographed** him; Wikipedia's caption reads **painted** **[A]**.
- **1910**: *Giselle*, *Carnaval*, *Scheherazade*; *correction*. The chronology lens called all three Mariinsky revivals. **Scheherazade** premiered 4 June 1910 at the Palais Garnier as a Fokine work for the **Ballets Russes**, and **Carnaval** likewise entered that repertory in 1910. Only *Giselle* is Mariinsky **[A]**.
- **January 1911, Mariinsky dismissal** over the *Giselle* costume worn without trunks **[A]**. *Unresolved*: Wikipedia places the **Tsarina Alexandra Feodorovna** in the house, Buckle the **Dowager Empress Maria Feodorovna**; the campa says "the imperial box". Whether Diaghilev engineered the scandal to free his dancer is tradition without document **[R]**.
- **1911-1913, Salle Garnier, Monte Carlo**: the company's winter workshop **[A]**. **Le Spectre de la Rose** premiered there in spring 1911 **[A for year and city; no day in the pool, so the journey's 19 April is hedged]**.
- **13 June 1911, Châtelet**: creates the title role in **Petrushka**, with **Tamara Karsavina** **[A]**.
- **1911-1913**: London seasons at Covent Garden **[A]**, no stop. **October 1912**: his father **Tomasz dies while touring** **[A; place unrecorded, hence no stop]**.

## 5. The four ballets, 1912-1913

- **29 May 1912, Châtelet: *L'Après-midi d'un Faune***, his first choreography. Final tableau, mimed masturbation on the nymph's scarf; a *Figaro* editorial calling it filthy; police at the second performance, no action; **Rodin defends it in print** **[A]**.
- **1913: *Jeux***, reworked by Diaghilev from Nijinsky's plan for three male dancers into one man and two women **[A]**. *Gap*: the pool fixes only "1913, Paris"; the journey's 1913-05-15 is hedged in `date_confidence`.
- **29 May 1913, Théâtre des Champs-Élysées: *Le Sacre du Printemps*** **[A]**. Wikipedia's caution is carried into the campa: **"riot"** for this evening enters print only with reviews of a **1924 revival**, contemporary accounts describing uproar and catcalling **[A]**.
- **26 July 1913**: the London production reviewed by *The Times* **[A: see quotations]**.

## 6. The crossing, the marriage, the telegram, 1913

- **August 1913**: the company sails for South America **without Diaghilev**, who avoided sea travel **[A]**. *Contradiction*: Buckle and Ostwald differ on **Cherbourg or Southampton** **[the journey pins Cherbourg and says so]**.
- **Rio de Janeiro, port of call** **[A]**, twice corrected: the latitude was sign-flipped (+22.9035 lands in the Red Sea), corrected to **-22.9035**; and the rings were bought on arrival at **Buenos Aires**, not ashore at Rio.
- **10 September 1913: marries Romola de Pulszky in Buenos Aires** **[A]**. Church or registry not named **[gap]**; the journey uses the canonical Buenos Aires pin (-34.6037, -58.3816).
- **September 1913: Diaghilev dismisses him by telegram** **[A]**. The date of the wire is not fixed **[gap]**.

## 7. Enemy alien, 1914-1917

- **March 1914, Palace Theatre, London**: his own eight-week season; observers describe him nervous and distraught on opening night **[A]**.
- **19 June 1914: Kyra Nijinsky born**; *correction*. One lens said "place unspecified", the other Budapest. Wikipedia, 'Romola de Pulszky': born in **Vienna** on 19 June 1914 **[A]**.
- **1914-1916: house arrest in Budapest** as an enemy Russian civilian, at the villa of Romola's mother, the actress **Emilia Márkus** **[A]**. Kyra was born in Vienna, not here.
- **1916: released through diplomatic and prisoner-exchange channels** at Diaghilev's instigation; **arrives New York 4 April 1916** **[A]**.
- **1916, Metropolitan Opera House**: dances and briefly directs the autumn season for a reported **$60,000** **[A]**; choreographs **Till Eulenspiegel** **[A for work and year; the 23 October date is outside the pool, hedged]**. *Coordinate corrected*: the pool's pin matched neither Met house; the **old house** (1411 Broadway, 1883-1966) is at **40.7542, -73.9875**.
- **1917: Stanislav dies in a Petrograd hospital** **[A; month and institution unrecorded]**.
- **30 September 1917, Montevideo**: last professional performance, a benefit with **Arthur Rubinstein** **[A for date, city and Rubinstein; R for the Teatro Solís attribution]**.

## 8. St Moritz, the diary, the diagnosis, 1917-1919

- **1917**: company members notice signs of illness; the couple move to **St Moritz** for two years **[A]**.
- **19 January 1919, Suvretta House**: last public appearance, an improvised solo before an invited audience **[A, confirmed independently by Wikipedia, 'Suvretta House']**. *Coordinate corrected*: both lenses put the hotel 1.5-1.8 km off and disagreed with each other; it is at **46.4846, 9.8201**. Witness accounts of what he danced differ **[flagged]**.
- **January to early March 1919: the notebooks**: four books, drawings of eyes, fear of hospitalization written down in advance **[A]**.
- **1919: examined in Zurich by Eugen Bleuler, diagnosed schizophrenia; committed to the Burghölzli, then to Bellevue, Kreuzlingen, under Ludwig Binswanger** **[A]**. Months not fixed; March and June are defaults **[gap]**.
- **1920: second daughter Tamara born** **[A; place unrecorded, folded into the Kreuzlingen campa]**.

## 9. The long silence, 1920-1950

- **1936: Romola publishes the diary**, cutting roughly 40 percent: bodily function, sexuality, homosexuality, unflattering remarks about herself **[A]**. Encyclopedia.com gives **1968** for an English edition **[R]**; both dates stand. The **unexpurgated edition, ed. Acocella, trans. FitzLyon**, is dated **1995** by two lenses and **1999** by a third **[flagged; no date in campa prose]**.
- **Early-mid 1940s: moved to Sopron**, western Hungary **[A; no year fixed]**.
- **1945, Vienna**: encounters occupying Russian soldiers and, after long near-silence, **begins speaking again** **[A]**. That he danced for them is widely repeated **[R]**.
- **1947-1950: Virginia Water, Surrey** **[A; some sources place the residence differently within Surrey, flagged]**.
- **8 April 1950: dies of kidney failure in a London clinic**, aged 60 or 61 **[A; the clinic is not named]**. First burial site in London **[R: inconsistently reported, unverified]**.

## 10. Afterlife

- **1953: reinterred at the Cimetière de Montmartre** **[A]**. *Correction, applied twice*: two lenses named **Gaétan Vestris** as the neighbouring grave, but he died in 1808, seventeen years before the cemetery opened in 1825. The dancer there is **Auguste Vestris (1760-1842)**, with **Gautier** and **Emma Livry**. The lenses also give two pins, 48.8897, 2.3317 and 48.8869, 2.3316; the journey uses the second.
- **Post-1953**: a bronze of him as **Petrushka** added to the grave by a Russian group from **Perm without the family's permission**; the stone gives 1889 **[A]**.
- **No film of him dancing is known to survive**; Diaghilev is said to have discouraged filming on the grounds that the technology would misrepresent him **[R, near-legend]**.
- **Rodin's sculptural study, c. 1912**, called posthumously cast by some sources **[R: Rodin died in 1917, so "posthumous" most plausibly refers to his own death. Unresolved]**.
- Stage and screen **[all A]**: Béjart, *Nijinsky, Clown of God* (1971); Neumeier, *Vaslav* (Hamburg, 1979) and a cycle revived into the 2020s; Herbert Ross, *Nijinsky* (1980), with de la Peña and Alan Bates; Paul Cox, *The Diaries of Vaslav Nijinsky* (2001).
- **11 June 2011**: Poland's first sculpture of him unveiled in the foyer of the **Teatr Wielki, Warsaw**, a bronze by **Giennadij Jerszow** of Nijinsky and **Bronislava Nijinska** as the Faun and the Nymph **[A]**.
- **Vaslav and Romola Nijinsky Foundation** founded by Romola, with daughter **Tamara** later its executive director **[A; founding date unrecorded]**.

## Interlocks with existing atlas journeys

Named in campa, on a direct relation to Nijinsky himself:

- **`nicholas_roerich`**: designed the sets and costumes for *Le Sacre du Printemps*, and Roerich's own file names Nijinsky for the 29 May 1913 premiere. Named at the Rite stop.
- **`marcel_proust`**: placed in that same audience by a report the Proust file itself calls "repeated for a century and never established". Named at the Rite stop with the non-establishment stated in the prose.

Present in the interlock lens and deliberately **not** named, because the relation runs to someone else or is bare co-location:

- **`george_balanchine`**: succeeded **Bronislava Nijinska** at Monte Carlo in 1925 and inherited Diaghilev's company. Both links run to Nijinsky's sister and his former employer; no meeting with Vaslav is attested. **`coco_chanel`**: paid for Diaghilev's funeral in 1929 and dressed *Le Train Bleu* in 1924, eleven years after Nijinsky left the company.
- **`gurdjieff`**, **`erik_satie`**, **`tchaikovsky`**, **`isadora_duncan`**, **`victoria_ocampo`**, **`suzanne_perrottet`**, **`dannunzio`**: all connect through the Champs-Élysées building, the Mariinsky, the Teatro Colón, or the Ballets Russes orbit. Shared address, no relation.

**Diaghilev, Stravinsky, Debussy and Picasso** are queued and unresearched, so the Ballets Russes cluster is at present one-directional out of this file.

## Honest gaps

1. **Birth year**, 1889 or 1890: unresolved, and the parish records at Warsaw and Kiev were not reached.
2. **Christening church, Warsaw**: not named anywhere reachable.
3. **The 1902 asylum claim about Vaslav himself**: no named account behind it. Not used.
4. **Tomasz Niżyński's death, October 1912**: place unrecorded, hence no stop.
5. **Tamara Nijinsky's birthplace, 1920**: unrecorded.
6. **The 1950 London clinic and the first burial site**: neither named, the second inconsistent across sources.
7. **Venue of the Diaghilev introduction** and **date of the dismissal telegram**: both unrecorded.
8. **Premiere dates for *Jeux* and *Till Eulenspiegel***: not in the pool; the journey's 15 May and 23 October are outside figures, hedged.
9. **Departure port**: Cherbourg or Southampton.
10. **Which royal saw the Giselle costume**: Wikipedia's Tsarina against Buckle's Dowager Empress.
11. **The notation system** of the Budapest years: referenced in the wider literature, unverified here, so the campa says only "plans for a system of dance notation".
