# Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi (1804-1851): research report

*Slug `carl_gustav_jacob_jacobi`. Written 2026-08-28 from a verified research pool
(chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife), whose verify-pass corrections are
carried below. Companion: `carl_gustav_jacob_jacobi.journey.json`, 9 segments, 42 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, later
testimony, or a claim resting on a compilation rather than a document. Contradictions are
flagged and **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

The base is narrow: MacTutor's Jacobi biography, English and German Wikipedia, Deutsche
Biographie, two quotation compilations, one archive.org record. **No archival material
was consulted, and the standard biography, Leo Koenigsberger, *Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi*
(Leipzig, 1904), was not read.** Details descending from Koenigsberger through the
dossier are marked [R] on that ground alone.

## 1. Potsdam, 1804-1821

- Born **10 December 1804 at Potsdam**, second son of **Simon Jacobi**, banker and
  money-changer, and **Rachel Lehmann**, in a prosperous Jewish household. **[A: MacTutor;
  Wikipedia en, citing NDB material]** Original first names sometimes given as *Jacques
  Simon* **[R: Wikipedia de]**.
- **Home-educated by a maternal uncle**, a Lehmann, in classical languages and elementary
  mathematics **[A as to the fact: MacTutor]**. The uncle's given name and the dates of the
  instruction are established by no source consulted. **Gap.**
- **Entered the Potsdam Gymnasium 1816**, shortly before turning twelve; promoted to the
  senior class in about six months; **held there roughly four years** because Berlin would
  not matriculate anyone under sixteen **[A: MacTutor]**. The school's own history lists him
  as a pupil who "erlangte mit 13 Jahren die Hochschulreife" **[A: Wikipedia de,
  'Helmholtz-Gymnasium Potsdam']**.
- In the wait he read **Euler's *Introductio in analysin infinitorum*** and attempted the
  **general quintic by radicals**, failing **[A: MacTutor]**; the attempt was necessarily
  hopeless, as Abel and Galois proved shortly after.

**Coordinate correction.** The pool's Gymnasium pin (52.398 / 13.0645) was loose. His
school, the Grosse Stadtschule, later Viktoria-Gymnasium, now Helmholtz-Gymnasium, stood on
today's Friedrich-Ebert-Strasse and moved to Kurfuerstenstrasse 53 only **in 1878**. File
uses **52.3995 / 13.0595**.

## 2. Berlin, 1821-1826

- **Matriculated at Berlin 1821**, dividing study among philosophy, classical philology,
  mathematics **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia en, de]**; **concentrated on mathematics alone
  1823** **[R: Wikipedia en]**.
- **Oberlehrer examination passed autumn 1824** (mathematics, Greek, Latin, history)
  **[A: Wikipedia en; MacTutor gives 1823/24 without the season]**; **offered a post at
  the Joachimsthalsches Gymnasium 1825** **[A: Wikipedia en]**.
- **Doctorate 1825**, *Disquisitiones analyticae de fractionibus simplicibus*;
  habilitation soon after. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia en]**
- **An 1825 paper on iterated functions was rejected by the Berlin Academy** and was
  published only in **1961**, with commentary by **Kurt-R. Biermann**. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **Lectured on curves and surfaces in space, winter semester 1825-26**; ministerial
  advice then pointed him east to Königsberg. **[A: MacTutor]**

**CONTRADICTION 1, unresolved: the year of the conversion.** MacTutor and Wikipedia en put
it **around 1825**, at the doctorate. Wikipedia de says he took the name Carl Gustav Jacob
"nach dem Uebertritt zum evangelisch-christlichen Glauben, den er im ersten Studiensemester
**1821** in Berlin vollzog". Deutsche Biographie gives "israelitisch, dann evangelisch" with
**no year**. Not a trivial four years: on the 1825 reading the baptism is a career act at
the threshold of a professorship closed to Jews; on the 1821 reading it is a
seventeen-year-old's act before any career exists. The file enters the stop at 1825 **only
because a segment must order on something**, and says so in `date_confidence`.

**CONTRADICTION 2, flagged.** The chronology lens said "age twenty-one" at the first
lectures, the geography lens "aged twenty". MacTutor places them in the academic year
**1825-26**, which opened before his twenty-first birthday of 10 December 1825, so the file
uses **aged twenty**. **Coordinate note.** The pool's Joachimsthalsches Gymnasium pin
(52.5192 / 13.4014) is **right** (Wikipedia de: Burgstrasse 21-23, Mitte, 1717 to 1880), but
its *note* was wrong and is rewritten: the school moved to Wilmersdorf in **1880**, to
Templin only in **1912**.

## 3. Königsberg, 1826-1844

- **Arrived May 1826 as salaried Privatdozent** **[A: MacTutor]**. **Gauss wrote to Bessel
  20 November 1826** asking after "diesen wie es scheint sehr talentvollen jungen Mann";
  **Bessel answered 12 December 1826**, "Jacobi ist seit Anfang des Sommers hier und bezieht,
  als Privatdocent, ein kleines Gehalt." **[A: both at deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118775766]**
- **Wrote to Legendre 5 August 1827**; **Legendre replied 9 February 1828.** **[A: MacTutor
  states both dates verbatim.]** The pool's geography lens flagged the August date as
  unconfirmable; that flag was wrong and is dropped.
- **Extraordinary professor 28 December 1827** **[A: MacTutor]**; **correspondence with
  Alexander von Humboldt from 1828** **[R: MacTutor]**; **ordinary professor 1829**, with
  ***Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum*** **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia en]**.
- **Married Marie Schwinck 11 September 1831**, and had **eight children**, all named in
  Wikipedia de and in the journey file's marriage stop. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia de for
  the day and the names]** Marie died **1902** at Cannstatt per Wikipedia de; an unverified
  **1901** circulates and is not followed.
- **Seminar founded with Franz Ernst Neumann, 1833.** **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia en]**
  **Kirchhoff attended 1843-1846**, trained chiefly by Neumann because Jacobi was by then
  ill. **[A: MacTutor, 'Kirchhoff']**
- **Ten hours a week on elliptic functions, 1835-36.** **[R: Dictionary of Scientific
  Biography via the dossier, not refetched]**
- **Extended Hamilton's 1834-35 general method in dynamics**: three-body work 1836, an
  1837 paper on first-order partial differential equations absorbing the characteristic
  function. **[R: Wikipedia en]**
- **Reintroduced Legendre's ∂ for the partial derivative, 1841**; he did not invent the
  symbol **[A: Wikipedia en]**. ***De determinantibus functionalibus*, 1841**, the
  functional (Jacobian) determinant **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **1842**: theorem on the spherical image of the normals of a closed space curve, prompted
  by Thomas Clausen's correction of an earlier Jacobi paper **[R: Wikipedia en]**; given no
  stop, dropped for length, recorded here. **Bankruptcy, autumn 1841**, losing all his money
  **[A: MacTutor, a Prussia- and Europe-wide depression; Deutsche Biographie, "1841 einen
  starken Bankerott"]**.

**CONTRADICTION 3, unresolved: 1829 or 1832 for the full professorship.** The pool held both
as [A], which is incoherent. Wikipedia en, Wikipedia de and Deutsche Biographie date the
ordinary professorship to **1829**; MacTutor alone gives **May 1832**, after a four-hour
Latin disputation. Both cannot be a first appointment. The file keeps **both stops**, tags
1832 **[R]**, and says that reading 1832 as a *formal confirmation* would reconcile them but
that **no source consulted says so**.

**Claims removed, with the reason.** The **brother Eduard's failed bank**, or a family bank
failure, as the cause of the 1841 ruin: unsupported, MacTutor blames a general depression
and Deutsche Biographie names no brother and no bank. **Marie travelling to Manchester**:
unsupported by anything consulted. **A meeting with Hamilton at Manchester in 1842**:
unsupported, since MacTutor's Hamilton biography never mentions Jacobi and Wikipedia en's
Hamilton article names him only among successors; the Manchester stop says in prose that
every retelling asserts the meeting and no source supports it.

### The two journeys abroad

**Summer 1829**: travelled west, **visited Gauss at Göttingen**, then **Paris**, meeting
**Legendre, Fourier, Poisson** **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia de]**. **July 1842**: Jacobi and
**Bessel at the British Association meeting in Manchester as representatives of Prussia**
**[A: MacTutor, verbatim]**; **lectured at Paris on the return, August 1842** **[R, and
thin: in the dossier's itinerary table only, corroborated by nothing independently fetched]**,
a stop kept and labelled as such in prose. **Coordinate correction**: the pool's Göttingen
pin (51.5328 / 9.9349) was about 750 m off, and the file inherits the corpus figure
byte-identically from `gauss.journey.json`, **51.5285 / 9.9433**, the Alte Sternwarte.

## 4. Illness and Italy, 1843-1844

- **Seriously ill 1843.** MacTutor and Wikipedia de record **diabetes**; Wikipedia en
  instead describes a **breakdown from overwork**. **[A as to the diabetes; the two
  descriptions are reconciled by no source consulted.]**
- **PLACE CORRECTION.** The pool put the onset at **Berlin**. It belongs at **Königsberg**:
  MacTutor has Dirichlet travelling east to him, Wikipedia de has him giving up his
  Königsberg lectures for health before the Italian journey.
- **FALSE CONTRADICTION, deleted.** The pool flagged a conflict between the 1843 diabetes
  and the 1851 smallpox death. There is none: MacTutor carries both, as successive
  illnesses eight years apart. The flag is removed, not propagated.
- **Dirichlet sat with him; the appeal went through Humboldt to Friedrich Wilhelm IV, who
  funded the convalescence** **[A: MacTutor]**. "Roughly two weeks" and April are **[R]**.
- **Lucca**: a congress with Dirichlet, Dirichlet's wife, **Carl Wilhelm Borchardt**
  **[A: MacTutor]**; that few Italians had read his papers is **[R]**; no month is given
  anywhere, so the file sets 10 to order the stop before Rome and says so.
- **Rome, arrived 16 November 1843**, **Jakob Steiner** and **Ludwig Schläfli** joining,
  Schläfli interpreting **[A: MacTutor]**; **collated Diophantus manuscripts in the Vatican
  Library** **[A: Wikipedia de]**, undated within the stay.
- **Back in Berlin by end of June 1844**, permitted a permanent transfer from Königsberg as
  a state pensioner on medical grounds. **[A: Wikipedia en; MacTutor]**

## 5. Berlin, 1844-1851

- ***Opuscula mathematica* vol. I dedicated to Friedrich Wilhelm IV, 1846** **[R: dossier,
  not refetched]**. **Lectured on analytical mechanics 1847-48** **[R: MacTutor]**; the
  course survives only through **Wilhelm Scheibner's** notes, published **1996**.
- **1848**: the liberal **Constitutional Club**, an impromptu speech offending monarchists
  and republicans alike, an unsuccessful **Liberal candidacy**, and afterwards a refusal of
  his request to join the Berlin university staff. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia en for the
  candidacy]** **DATE CORRECTION**: the pool gave "1848-02". Rejected. MacTutor gives no
  month and the Berlin rising began in **March** (Paris rose 22-24 February).
- **Summer 1849**: supplement withdrawn, **Marie and the eight children moved to Gotha**,
  he stayed in Berlin **[A: MacTutor, "by the summer of 1849"; Wikipedia en]**. **REMOVED**:
  that he gave up his house and lodged in an inn; MacTutor mentions neither.
- **Vienna chair provisionally accepted, then withdrawn** when Prussia made concessions
  **[A as to the event: MacTutor]**. MacTutor gives only "a few months later" after summer
  1849, Wikipedia de dates the offer to **1850**, and the file gives the range unnarrowed.
- **Number theory, summer semester 1850**, and the **summer vacation at Gotha** **[R:
  MacTutor for both]**.
- **January 1851**: influenza, then **smallpox** **[A: MacTutor]**; **died at Berlin
  18 February 1851, aged 46** **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia en, de]**. **REMOVED**: that the
  influenza was caught visiting Gotha. Wikipedia en says only "In 1851, Jacobi died after
  contracting a smallpox infection", and MacTutor gives no location.

## 6. Grave, works, afterlife

- **Buried at Friedhof I der Dreifaltigkeits-Kirchengemeinde**, Baruther Strasse 61,
  Berlin-Kreuzberg; grave **DV2-SA-1T**, a **pedestalled iron cross**; **Ehrengrab of the
  State of Berlin from 1980**, renewed 2001 **[A: Wikipedia de, en]**. **H. A. Schwarz,
  "Die Grabstätte J.'s", Jahresbericht der DMV, 1904**, established where the grave was, in
  Koenigsberger's centenary year **[A: Deutsche Biographie]**.
- **CORRECTION.** The pool said the grave lies "close to the grave of Johann Encke". Encke
  (1791-1865) is **not in Jacobi's cemetery**: he is an Ehrengrab of the **Friedhof II der
  Jerusalems- und Neuen Kirchengemeinde**, a different ground adjacent within the Hallesches
  Tor complex **[A: Wikipedia de, 'Friedhöfe vor dem Halleschen Tor']**. The file says "in
  the neighbouring ground".
- **Dirichlet's *Gedächtnissrede***, published in the Academy's *Abhandlungen* for **1852**
  **[A: Deutsche Biographie]**. **REMOVED**: the delivery date 1 July 1852 and the
  "greatest since Lagrange" wording, neither in any source consulted.
- **Gesammelte Werke. CORRECTION.** The pool said eight volumes, 1881-1891, Borchardt and
  Weierstrass. Deutsche Biographie gives "hrsg. v. C. W. Borchardt, A. Clebsch u.
  K. Weierstrass, **7 Bde., 1881 ff.**": seven volumes, with **Alfred Clebsch** an editor
  the pool had dropped. The commonly counted eighth is the **Supplementband**, the
  *Vorlesungen über Dynamik*, which Clebsch had already edited in **1866**. Vol. 2 was
  digitized on the Internet Archive, scan completed 30 March 2006 **[R: archive.org
  `gesammelwerk02jacorich`]**.
- **Lunar crater Jacobi** **[A: Wikipedia en, corroborated by MacTutor]**; **asteroid
  (12040) Jacobi** **[A: Wikipedia de only]**; **FRS 1833, FRSE 1845** **[A: MacTutor]**;
  **International Honorary Member, American Academy of Arts and Sciences, 1850** **[R:
  dossier only, NOT checked against the Academy's register, and not used in the file]**.

## 7. The quotations

**Used, [A]:** the **2 July 1830 letter to Legendre** ("the unique end of science is the
honor of the human mind"), English translation in *Science*, 10 March 1911, vol. 33 no. 845,
p. 359, read at Wikiquote; **translations of the key sentence differ** (sole / unique /
real; mind / intellect / spirit) and a shorter one is in *The Mathematical Gazette*, July
1905, p. 200; the archival original was not consulted. Two lecture texts, the ***Vorlesungen
über Dynamik*** (1842-43, ed. Clebsch, 1884) and the ***Vorlesungen über analytische
Mechanik: Berlin 1847/48*** (Scheibner's notes, published 1996). Two **Legendre** letters,
9 February 1828 and early 1829, in MacTutor's translations, not checked against the
originals. **Bessel** (12 December 1826, 11 November 1841) and **Gauss** (20 November 1826)
from Deutsche Biographie, quoted in the German as printed and deliberately **not
translated**, so no paraphrase passes as a quotation.

**Used, [R]:** "Dirichlet alone, not I, nor Cauchy, nor Gauss knows what a completely
rigorous mathematical proof is...", attributed in G. Schubring, *Zur Modernisierung des
Studiums der Mathematik in Berlin*, printed on MacTutor's quotations page. **The pool's
wording was a paraphrase and has been replaced with MacTutor's exact text**, ellipsis
included; three departures corrected ("but we learn it only from him" to "Rather we learn it
first from him"; "it is very likely" to "it is very clear"; "it is a fifty-fifty bet" to
"one can wager as much pro as con"). Later testimony, not an autograph, and tagged so in
`date_confidence`.

**Excluded, all [R], none traced past a twentieth-century quotation book:** "God ever
arithmetizes" (Eves 1971; Bell 1937); "It is often more convenient to possess the ashes of
great men..." (Bell 1937, Eves 1977); "Mathematics is the science of what is clear by
itself" (Newman 1956 from Simon 1908, wording confirmed, so **the pool's note that no
earlier source underlies it was wrong**, but Simon is still a report); "There is none", the
alleged reply about England's greatest mathematician (Bell and Boyer only). Also **"Man muss
immer generalisieren": this German sentence appears in no source reached.** Davis and Hersh
print "One should always generalise", Dresden (1936) "One must always generalize" without
citation; his genuinely circulated German maxim is the different "man muss immer umkehren".

## 8. Interlocks

Before this pass, **no journey file in the checkout contained the name "Jacobi" except
one**, and neither QUEUE.md nor the census recorded prior work on him **[A: grep across
`*.journey.json`]**. That one is decisive: **`william_rowan_hamilton.journey.json`**,
Dunsink Observatory stop, states verbatim *"Jacobi at Konigsberg takes the method up and
completes it, and the equations go under both names."* **[A]** Jacobi's file now carries its
own side of that, at the Albertina pin, dated to the 1836-37 papers.

Five travelers are named in campa text, each on a **direct or one-directional relation of
Jacobi's own**, never on shared geography: **`leonard_euler`** (read the *Introductio* as a
schoolboy and formed himself on it), **`gauss`** (corresponded from 1827 on cubic residues;
Gauss asked Bessel after him in 1826; visited at Göttingen 1829), **`joseph_fourier`** (met
at Paris 1829, and the 1830 Legendre letter answers Fourier's position explicitly),
**`william_rowan_hamilton`** (took up and completed his general method in dynamics),
**`humboldt`** (correspondent from 1828, and the patron who took the 1843 appeal to the
king).

**Deliberately not named**, being shared geography and nothing else, which the map already
shows a viewer: **`kant`** (same Albertina, but Kant died two years before Jacobi was born
and no relation is documented; the Albertina coordinate is still inherited from
`kant.journey.json`, a pin inheritance and not a name-drop) and **`brothers_grimm`** (Jacob
Grimm a Berlin professor from 1841 and an Academy member, with no documented personal
interaction in any source consulted).

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identically:** Albertina **54.7065 / 20.5112** from
`kant.journey.json`; Göttingen observatory **51.5285 / 9.9433** from `gauss.journey.json`;
Institut de France **48.8566 / 2.3376** from `joseph_fourier.journey.json`; Prussian Academy
of Sciences **52.517 / 13.3888** from `leonard_euler.journey.json`. Pins named in the brief
that do **not** belong here and were not forced in: the Kaaba, the Temple Mount, Buenos
Aires. His itinerary is Potsdam, Berlin, Königsberg, Göttingen, Paris, Manchester, Lucca,
Rome, Gotha, and nothing else.
