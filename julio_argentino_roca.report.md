# Julio Argentino Roca: research report

*2026-08-03. Slug `julio_argentino_roca`. General; twice president of Argentina, 1880-1886 and 1898-1904; commander of the Conquest of the Desert. Journey register: national mythology: the canon is true.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition or inference.

About a quarter of the verified pool's chronology and geography entries carried a `note` beginning "CORRECTED". Several of those corrections reverse the *causal order* of famous events rather than merely a date, so all are reproduced below. Where a correction leaves two sources disagreeing, the disagreement is printed and not adjudicated.

## 1. Tucumán and the Colegio (1843-1858)

- Born **17 July 1843, San Miguel de Tucumán**, son of **Colonel José Segundo Roca**, veteran of the independence wars **[A**, Wikipedia; Félix Luna, *Soy Roca***]**.
- Childhood on the family's rural properties near Tucumán **[R]**. The pool offers "Estancia El Bracho / La Fronterita" at -27.05, -65.35 and flags the identification as *disputed between the two names*. Placeholder pin, unresolved identification: **stop dropped** rather than pinned falsely. Gap.
- **Colegio Nacional de Concepción del Uruguay**, Entre Ríos, c. 1856-1857 **[A** for the schooling, **R** for the dates**]**. Coordinates -32.4825, -58.2372 verify.
- **Joins the army of the Confederation before turning fifteen, 19 March 1858** **[A]**. The pool gives no place. Paraná, the Confederation capital, is the obvious inference but only an inference, so no stop was created; the fact sits in the Colegio campa. **Gap: place of enlistment.**

## 2. The wars of the Confederation (1859-1861)

Two corrections, both raising the evidentiary grade.

- **Cepeda, 23 October 1859** **[A, corrected from R]**. (a) His presence is documented, not traditional, and he was there as a **commissioned artillery officer, teniente segundo**, not a cadet with his father's kit (es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Cepeda (1859)"; standard biographies). (b) The Cañada del arroyo Cepeda lies on the **Santa Fe side of the Arroyo del Medio, ~25 km north of Pergamino**, at **-33.3814, -60.5831**, about 45 km from the pool's original -33.55, -60.15. Corrected pin used.
- **Pavón, 17 September 1861** **[A, corrected from R]**. Presence documented in the service record: promoted **teniente primero in the hours after the defeat**. Coordinates corrected from -33.05, -60.72 (well west of the Paraná corridor) to **-33.25, -60.2167**, near the Domingo Palacios estancia between Rueda and Godoy, ~40 km south of Rosario.
- **National Guard regiment, Salta, 1865** **[A]**. Year only. Salta city (-24.7859, -65.4117) is the journey file's pin, not the pool's, which gives only the province; `date_confidence` says both.

## 3. Paraguay and the montoneras (1865-1871)

- **Tuyutí, 24 May 1866** **[A]**, with promotion to **sargento mayor at twenty-two**. Coordinates corrected to **-27.2044, -58.5481**, ~2 km north of Paso de Patria, from -27.2833, -58.1333 (~41 km east).
- **Curupayty, 22 September 1866** **[A]**. Leads the Salta battalion in the assault and **loses half his force**. Coordinates corrected to **-27.1131, -58.5744**, ~8 km from Humaitá, from -27.1667, -58.15 (~42 km east).
- **Father and brother(s) dead, Humaitá campaign: CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** English Wikipedia says father **and two brothers**; Spanish Wikipedia says father José Segundo Roca **and one brother**. Both counts are printed in the campa; no stop was built on the disputed number.
- **Varela: corrected by a year and relocated.** The pool had Roca "sent to the Puna to suppress Varela" in 1868. Varela's final campaign ran from late 1868 into January 1869; Roca, then lieutenant colonel, brought reinforcements from Jujuy and Salta, and Varela was broken at the **Combate de Pastos Grandes, Puna de Salta, 12 January 1869**, with **some sources giving 14 January** (revisionistas.com.ar; lagazeta.com.ar). Both dates sit in `date_confidence`. **Coordinate caution:** no pin in the pool; **-24.55, -66.75** (the salar, Los Andes department) is my approximation, flagged in the stop.
- The same correction voids a second claim: the pool had Roca fighting Varela's montoneras **from Río Cuarto, 1867-1873**. He did not. The Varela campaign was in Salta, Jujuy and the Puna; his Río Cuarto and Villa Mercedes frontier command against indigenous raiding is a **separate, later assignment, c. 1872-1877**. The journey file separates them.
- **Ñaembé and the colonelcy, 26 January 1871** **[A]**. **Coordinate caution:** no pin in the pool; **-29.20, -59.30**, near Goya, is an approximation. Flagged.

## 4. Córdoba, the frontier command, the 1874 revolt

- **Marriage to Clara Funes, August 1872, Córdoba** **[A]**: eighteen, daughter of the estanciero **Tomás Funes** and **Eloísa Díaz**, a Córdoba-resident family of San Luis origin.
- **Children: CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** The Archivo General de la Nación and most Argentine sources say **six** (Julio Argentino Pascual, Elisa, María Marcela, Clara, Agustina Eloísa, Josefina Elena); Wikipedia says **seven**. Both counts printed. **Julio Argentino Pascual Roca as future vice president** is confirmed **[A]**.
- **Juárez Celman is his *concuñado***, not his brother-in-law: he married Elisa Funes, sister of Roca's wife. English usage flattens this; the campa uses "his wife's sister's husband".
- **La Verde, 26 November 1874: two errors corrected, then the stop dropped.** (1) The president was **Nicolás Avellaneda, not Sarmiento**, who had left office 12 October 1874. (2) The pool's troop numbers (800 against 7,000, from lagaceta.com.ar) are inflated: es.wikipedia gives **~900 government troops under Lt. Col. José Inocencio Arias against ~4,500 rebels under Mitre**. The pool's own caveat that **Roca was not present** is correct and stands. Presence negative, and no reliable coordinate for the estancia (7 km north of Del Valle) could be fixed without guessing: **not a stop**, one clause in the Santa Rosa campa. Deliberate omission.
- **Santa Rosa, Mendoza, 7 December 1874** **[A]**: defeats **José Miguel Arredondo**, ends the Mitrista revolt. **Correction:** Avellaneda was **not president-elect**; inaugurated 12 October 1874, with the revolt breaking out the day after. The campa states this.
- **Rank corrected.** The pool called the promotion "brigadier general (general de brigada) at 31". Date and age hold (7 December 1874, aged 31), but Argentine sources give **coronel mayor**, then the top rank below general. This dissolves the pool's internal tension with the separate promotion **to brigadier general on 28 September 1880**.
- **Villa Mercedes, Fifth Division, 1875-1877** **[A]**, coordinates verify. **Azul (1876-1878)** was offered but tagged **[R]**, "Roca's specific presence is inferred, not individually documented": dropped. **Carhué** **[A]** as Alsina's 1876 line: dropped for space, its ditch-versus-advance argument folded into the Villa Mercedes campa.

## 5. The Conquest of the Desert (1878-1885)

- **Minister of War and Navy, 4 January 1878** **[A]**.
- **Legislative basis: two events conflated in the pool.** It dated to **14 August 1878** a claim that "Congress funds execution of the 1867 law". Per El Historiador, the cited source itself, 14 August is when **Avellaneda sent Congress the bill** to execute **Ley 215 of 23 August 1867**; **Congress voted the 1,600,000-peso credit in October 1878 (Ley 947)**. Both dates, in order, in the ministry campa.
- **Preliminary operations, July 1878** **[A]**: prisoners taken, captives recovered. Folded into the Trenque Lauquen campa.
- **Campaign opens 16 April 1879 in five columns** **[A]**, one from **Trenque Lauquen** (-35.9756, -62.7339, verifies).
- **Authorship error corrected, and it is the important one.** The pool sourced Trenque Lauquen to "Roca's own campaign memoir, *La conquista de quince mil leguas*". **Roca did not write it.** It is **Estanislao Zeballos**, 1878, corrected edition 1879, offered to the war minister **without payment** and dedicated to him (Wikisource, "Oríjen de esta obra"; UCEL catalog; Academia.edu, "El viaje a la guerra"). The pool's afterlife lens had this right while its geography lens had it wrong: an internal contradiction, resolved in favour of the sourced entry. Zeballos is named in the campa.
- **Choele Choel, 25 May 1879** **[A]**: reveille, flag, band, mass. The file inherits the **canonical corpus pin -39.2872, -65.6772** from `che_guevara.journey.json` rather than the pool's -39.28, -65.68.
- **The 25 May telegram: upgraded [R] to [A] on corrected wording.** The pool carried a paraphrase ("Dos grupos de indios que se preparaban para malonear fueron batidos..."). Attested text, from Roca's telegram to the interim war minister as recorded by **Col. Manuel José Olascoaga** (*Estudio topográfico de la Pampa y Río Negro*, 1880) and quoted in La Izquierda Diario: *"Se han abatido dos grupos de indios que se preparaban para dar malón, muertos 18 y tomados 20 como prisioneros, entre ellos cuatro cautivas."* Used verbatim.
- **The "close to God in the desert" line: [R], wrong wording and wrong date in the pool.** No source gives the pool's phrasing. Two attested wordings: Infobae, 16 April 2022, *"en ninguna parte se siente uno tan cerca de Dios como en el desierto"*, and elarcondelahistoria.com, *"En ninguna parte como en el desierto se siente uno más cerca de Dios."* Date is **2 June 1879**, not 25 May, and elarcondelahistoria calls it a **letter after a religious ceremony**, not a telegram. Recipient and place hold. File uses the Infobae wording, 2 June, tag kept at **[R]**: no archival original reached.
- **Fisque Menuco, 8 June 1879** **[A]**, site of the future Fuerte General Roca (-39.0333, -67.5833).
- **Confluence of Limay and Neuquén, 11 June 1879** **[A]**. El Historiador supports only the arrival; the pool's **"escort of 100 men" is not in the cited source and was dropped**. **Coordinate caution: -38.9833, -68.05** is an approximation of the junction. **Return to Choele Choel, 13 June, garrison left** **[R]**: one clause, not a stop. **End of the ministry, 9 October 1879** **[A]**: folded into the 1880 election campa.
- **Campaign declared concluded, 1885** **[A]**, the source itself noting fighting continued. The 1886 campa says the paperwork and the violence keep different calendars.

## 6. The Revolution of 1880

- **Election 11 April 1880** **[A]**; **Electoral College 12-13 June** **[A]**, with **Francisco Bernabé Madero**.
- **The revolution came AFTER, not before.** The pool said Tejedor's rising was "put down... **before** the Electoral College formally seats Roca". Reversed: the College seated him 12-13 June; the fighting at **Los Corrales, Puente Alsina and Barracas fell 20-21 June**, armistice ~24-25 June. The rising was a **response** to the seating. The ~3,000 casualty figure is conventional and carried as conventional. Both pins verify; the file merges them at Los Corrales (-34.6389, -58.4064).
- **Federalization: attribution corrected.** The pool carried it twice, once as "[R] general historiography" and once as "Under Roca's presidency". Both wrong the same way. **Ley 1029 was sanctioned 20-21 September 1880 under Avellaneda**, three weeks before Roca's inauguration (Infoleg; Museo Roca; Museo Histórico Sarmiento); some sources note a complementary act Roca promulgated 6 December 1880. Tag rises to [A], framing falls: the campa says he inherited the settlement.
- **Brigadier general, 28 September 1880** **[A]**.

## 7. First presidency (1880-1886)

- **Inaugurated 12 October 1880** **[A]**. The inaugural address is the best-sourced material in the dossier, fetched directly from the **Academia Nacional de la Historia** blog page "Roca, el constructor" and **gentedelpasado.blogspot.com**. Three lines are used, each with a `quote_source` naming the 12 October 1880 address even where the stop it sits on is later.
- **Ley 1130** (currency, 1881), **boundary treaty with Chile** (1881) and **lieutenant general** (1882) are all **[A]**; space forced them into campa clauses rather than stops.
- **La Plata, 19 November 1882: attribution corrected.** Not "Roca's government": founded by **Dardo Rocha, governor of Buenos Aires province**, planned by **Pedro Benoit**. A provincial act. The motive (the province needing a capital after federalization) is correct; coordinates verify.
- **Ley 1532, nine National Territories, 1884** **[A]**. Pool gives the year; standard accounts date it 16 October 1884, and `date_confidence` says so.
- **Convertibility: off by a year.** Established **1883**, suspended **January 1885**, not 1884, when the executive ordered the Banco Nacional to stop converting notes (es.wikipedia, "Caja de Conversión"; BCR informativo; todo-argentina, "Año 1885").
- **Avellaneda Law, 25 June 1885** **[A]**, in a campa clause. **Assassination attempt, May 1886** **[A]**, month only.
- **Handover: off by one day.** The pool ended the term 11 October 1886; **Juárez Celman took office 12 October**. Same error at the other end: **Quintana assumed 12 October 1904**, not the 11th. Both corrected.

## 8. Unicato, second presidency, last years (1890-1914)

- **Minister of the Interior under Pellegrini, 6 August 1890 to 1 May 1891** **[A]**; **acting president 28 October 1895 to 8 February 1896** during Uriburu's illness **[A]**, folded into the same campa.
- All **[A]**: **second term from 12 October 1898**; **Abrazo del Estrecho with Federico Errázuriz off Punta Arenas, 15 February 1899**; **conscription 1901**; **Pactos de Mayo 28 May 1902**; **Ley 4144, Residence Law, 1902**; **Drago Doctrine, December 1902**, by foreign minister **Luis María Drago**; **retirement-fund law 20 September 1904**; **special ambassador to Brazil, 1912**. The **1914 return is [R]**, and the campa claims nothing more than a return.
- **The estancias: corrected outright.** The pool invented an **"Estancia La Argentina"** at -37.5, -59, round numbers that betray a placeholder. No source supports the name. The documented properties are **La Larga near Daireaux** (autumns) and **La Paz near Ascochinga, Córdoba** (summers for the last two decades of the century, park by **Charles Thays**, 1901): La Nación, "Los veranos cordobeses de Julio Argentino Roca"; Diario Río Negro. The invented estancia is dropped; **La Larga takes the corpus pin -36.6748, -61.9266** inherited byte-identical from `emin_arslan.journey.json`; **La Paz is approximated at -30.95, -64.2833**, flagged.
- **Death 19 October 1914, aged 71** **[A]**; burial in the **Recoleta** family vault, Mannerist front, wrought-iron gates with arabesques, angels and inverted torches, his father Colonel Segundo Roca in the same vault **[A]**. The stern bronze bust is **[R]**, a visitors' and guides' observation, undated.

## 9. Afterlife

- **Equestrian monument, Diagonal Sur and Perú, 1941** **[A]**. SciELO Argentina (2019), "Julio A. Roca y la Conquista del desierto: monumentalización, patrimonio y usos del pasado durante las décadas de 1930 y 1940", places it inside a deliberate **1930s-1940s state-patrimony campaign**, not in late post-mortem grief. The campa says so.
- **Vandalism, "Roca genocida", thrown objects, attempted demolition, documented from the early 2000s** **[A]** (La Nación; Kalewche). **Osvaldo Bayer's Desmonumentar campaign** **[A]**, 2000s-2010s: statues down, streets renamed, a monument to indigenous women in place of the Buenos Aires one.
- **Banknote: overstated in the pool.** Roca's portrait was **not removed in 2012**. The Evita note was announced late July 2012 and circulated from **20 September 2012**, Argentina's first commemorative circulating note and first with a woman as main figure, but **the Roca note kept being printed and circulating alongside it for years** (BCRA; La Nación, September 2012). The campa says "begins the phase-out".
- **Bariloche 2023: corrected twice, and the correction is the point.** The municipality ordered the monument off the centre of the Centro Cívico, but (1) the destination was the **barrancas above Lake Nahuel Huapi**, not "a side row of próceres statues", and (2) **the move never happened**: the courts blocked it before execution (Diario Río Negro; Infobae, 3 August 2023; Ámbito). A Change.org petition, "El monumento a Roca en Bariloche no se toca", opposed it **[A]**. The statue still stands where it stood.
- **Pichetto's homage, 19 October 2021** **[A]** (Infobae) and the 2023 opinion piece "Con Roca, no" **[R]** are the two poles of a live argument. The pool declines to adjudicate genocide against nation-building, and so does the journey file: the campa says both sides agree on the events and disagree about the man on the horse.
- **Honest negative finding:** no source in any pass documents a **grave-desecration or relic legend** at Roca's tomb, unlike Facundo Quiroga's severed hands. Stated in the Recoleta campa as a gap ("nobody has ever come for the body"), not as a proven absence.
