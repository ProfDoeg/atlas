# Jacques Derrida (1930-2004): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-17 from the verified research pool. Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or apparatus applied interpretively.* Spine term: **iterability**, the mark that functions in the absence of both its author and its addressee and is altered by every return to it.

## 1. El Biar and Algiers, 1930-1949

**[A]** Jackie Élie Derrida, born 15 July 1930 at El Biar near Algiers into a Sephardic family traced to Toledo; father Aimé Derrida, traveller for a wine-and-spirits firm, mother Georgette Sultana Esther Safar; third of five, an elder brother Paul Moïse dead in infancy. French citizens under the Crémieux Decree of 1870, which naturalized Algeria's Jews collectively, a point Derrida works in *Le monolinguisme de l'autre* (1996): what a decree confers a decree can withdraw, as in 1940. **The expulsion [A]:** first day of the 1942 school year, expelled under a Vichy quota cutting Jewish enrolment (pool: 14% to 7%); he refused the improvised Jewish lycée staffed by dismissed Jewish teachers and played truant for roughly a year. (Wikipedia; Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy; Peeters ch. 1; JewAge.)

**Contradiction, decided not resolved.** The geography lens labels the pin "El Biar / Lycée de Ben Aknoun" while carrying El Biar's coordinate; the school is in the separate commune of Ben Aknoun, approx. 36.7522/3.0122. The file **moves the stop to Ben Aknoun and names the school**. No source consulted gives the building's own coordinate. **Three coordinates for one birthplace [A/R]:** chronology 36.7667/3.0333, geography 36.7628/3.0308, afterlife 36.7686/3.0247, spread ~600 m; the geography value is used throughout, the disagreement flagged, not adjudicated on evidence.

**Self-description, corrected [A].** The quotes lens carried "un peu noir et tres arabe," which is **wrong and is not printed**. Derrida's phrase in "Circonfession" is *un petit Juif noir et très arabe*; the corrupt version turns *un petit Juif* into *un peu* and deletes the noun the adjectives modify (Rue Descartes 2014/2; CIELAM; Bennington's "a little black and very Arab Jew," 1993). **Gap:** no Seuil page verified, so the French is not used in the journey file.

## 2. Paris, Leuven, Harvard, Koléa, 1949-1959

**[A]** Paris 1949 for the khâgne at Louis-le-Grand under Étienne Borne. **Contradiction, adjudicated [A vs A]:** en.wikipedia says he failed the ENS entrance once and was admitted on the second attempt; Peeters, via NYRB (25 Mar 2013) and Book Haven (2015), has him failing in 1950, failing again in May 1951, and admitted at the end of 1951-52, the **third attempt after three years of khâgne**. The pool's own verification prefers Peeters; the file follows Peeters and records the disagreement.

**Foucault, corrected [A].** The geography lens calls Foucault "then a senior student" at the ENS in 1952. False: Foucault (b. 1926) entered in 1946, had finished by 1951, and in 1952-53 was at the rue d'Ulm as a *répétiteur* in psychology whose courses Derrida attended. Teacher, not classmate.

**[A]** Leuven 1953, Husserl Archives, for the diplôme thesis *Le problème de la genèse dans la philosophie de Husserl* (1954, published 1990). Harvard 1956-57 as a "special student," Husserl microfilms in Widener, reading *Ulysses*; marries Marguerite Aucouturier in June 1957. Two pool coordinates for that one episode (42.377/-71.1167 Harvard; 42.3736/-71.1097 the marriage), ~700 m apart, merged at the Harvard pin and flagged; the agrégation (1956) gets no stop. **Koléa [A]:** military service 1957-59, teaching French and English to soldiers' and settlers' children rather than serving as a combatant, which he called a stroke of luck. **Gap:** month not established, so an ordering month is used and declared.

## 3. The Sorbonne years and the year of three books, 1960-1968

**Posts [A].** Sorbonne assistant 1960-64 (arranged through Bachelard, Canguilhem, Ricoeur, Wahl; also active with the Union of French Christian Students without being a believer). *Agrégé-répétiteur* at the ENS from 1964 on Althusser's and Hyppolite's recommendation, held to 1984. Sons Pierre (1963) and Jean (1967). **L'origine de la géométrie [A], split:** translation and long introduction, PUF, 1962; the chronology lens joins publication to the Prix Cavaillès, but the prize came **29 May 1964** (Encyclopedia.com). Two years apart, kept apart.

**Cogito et histoire de la folie [A].** 4 March 1963, Collège philosophique: a critique of Foucault's *Histoire de la folie* (1961) on Descartes, opening a public rift that lasts until a partial reconciliation in the 1980s.

**Johns Hopkins [A, canonical pin inherited].** 18-21 October 1966, "The Languages of Criticism and the Sciences of Man," organized by Girard and Macksey; "Structure, Sign, and Play" delivered 21 October. Four corpus files already pin it at **39.3299/-76.6205**, dated 1966-10-18; inherited byte-identical. Barthes and Lacan attended, Deleuze and Foucault did not. Derrida meets Paul de Man here.

**1967-1968 [A].** Three books in one year: *De la grammatologie* (Minuit), *La voix et le phénomène* (PUF), *L'écriture et la différence* (Seuil). "La différance" delivered 27 January 1968, *Bulletin de la Société française de philosophie* 62. Participation in the May events, and "La pharmacie de Platon" in *Tel Quel*; the Tel Quel association runs roughly seven years from 1965.

## 4. Iterability: Montreal, Paris, Baltimore, Evanston, 1971-1988

**Montreal [A, soft].** "Signature événement contexte," August 1971, Congrès international des Sociétés de philosophie de langue française, Université de Montréal. Searle's file flags this delivery as wanting confirmation; the flag is carried forward rather than resolved. Collected in *Marges de la philosophie* (Minuit, 1972); Weber's translation in *Glyph* 1, 1977. **The etymology, corrected [A]:** the afterlife lens says Derrida "grounds iterability etymologically... he derives" *iter* from Sanskrit *itara*; he proposes it in a hedged parenthesis, Latin *iter* is from the root of *ire*, and no philologist accepts the link. The file presents it as his proposal and disowns the philology.

**Baltimore, twice [A, pin inherited].** Searle's "Reiterating the Differences," *Glyph* 1 (1977), at **39.2904/-76.6122**, 1977-01-01, inherited byte-identical from Searle's file; Derrida's "Limited Inc a b c..." in *Glyph* 2 the same year at the same pin. That pin is the press, not the 1966 campus: the file's two Baltimore coordinates differ deliberately. **Evanston [A]:** *Limited Inc*, Northwestern UP, 1988, at **42.0451/-87.6877**, also inherited byte-identical; Searle refused permission to reprint his 1977 reply, so the book carries Derrida's account of the argument instead of the argument.

**Attested negative [A].** The two never met in person; the file states this rather than staging a meeting. **Austin [A]:** "Signature Event Context" reads *How to Do Things with Words*; Austin died in 1960 and never read Derrida, so he is named on the Montreal stop as a one-directional relation.

## 5. The apparatus segment: Hansel and Gretel

`EXCEPTIONS.md` **names Jacques Derrida explicitly**, licensing his iterability apparatus applied backward to "Hansel and Gretel: the trail that exists only because it can be returned to, and is deformed by each return." Already ruled; nothing here needs the operator's decision. Three stops at Derrida's own ENS pin (48.8414/2.3444), dated 1972-06-01/02/03 only to order the segment, on the template of `j_l_austin.journey.json`.

**Attested [A].** Grimm, *Kinder- und Hausmärchen*, 1812, no. 15, rev. through 1857: white pebbles catching moonlight; breadcrumbs eaten by birds; the house of bread and cake with sugar windows; the crossing of the water on a duck's back. **Not attested, stated plainly [R]:** Derrida is not on record discussing "Hänsel und Gretel" anywhere in the material consulted, and each stop says so in its own `date_confidence`. **Contradiction resolved on the primary text [A]:** the afterlife lens says a **swan**, the geography lens a **duck**; the Grimms have *ein weisses Entchen*, so the duck stands.

### Apparatus relations for the operator

The Hansel and Gretel material is the only relation in the pool not rooted in Derrida's own life, and it is **caught backward**, which is exactly what EXCEPTIONS.md licenses for Derrida by name. Nothing was deleted. **No forward-direction relation appeared in the pool at all**, so the brief's "no LLM" instruction had nothing to bite on: nothing is written into any earlier file, and nothing later than 2004 appears in Derrida's own.

## 6. Ris-Orangis, Cerisy, the two columns, 1970-1980

**[A]** The house in the Essonne where he lived and wrote for over three decades; the pool dates the move only to a lens default of 1970-01-01, declared as such. *Glas* (1974), Hegel down one column and Genet down the other. Cerisy 1972, "Nietzsche aujourd'hui": `nick_land.journey.json` pins it at **49.0219/-1.2886** (from `gilles_deleuze.journey.json`) and names Derrida among the attendees with Deleuze, Lyotard, Klossowski; inherited byte-identical.

**Cerisy coordinates: four values, flagged loudly [A].** The pool gives 49.1319/-1.1698 (geography, twice, ~12 km north of the commune), 48.9333/-1.2833 (chronology, ~11 km south), 49.0269/-1.2811 (commune) and 49.0227/-1.2884 (the château); the corpus uses a fifth, 49.0219/-1.2886, within 100 m of the château. **Decision:** 1972 inherits the corpus pin exactly; 1980 and 1997 use 49.0227/-1.2884. The two bad lens values are discarded and named as discarded; worth checking against other Cerisy-bearing files before a globe rebuild.

**The doctorat d'État [A, jury corrected].** Defended 2 June 1980 at the Sorbonne. The geography lens puts Paul Ricoeur on the jury; he was not on it. Chair Maurice de Gandillac, with Aubenque, Desanti (supervisor), Joly, Lascault, Levinas (aphelis.net; Peeters). A *defense sur travaux*: no manuscript submitted. Address: "Ponctuations: le temps d'une thèse." Same year, first honorary doctorate, Columbia; Cerisy hosts "Les fins de l'homme" 23 July - 2 August 1980.

## 7. Prague and the institutions, 1981-1989

**Prague [A, detention understated in the pool].** Arrested at Prague airport 30 December 1981 after clandestine seminars for Czech dissidents; state security planted narcotics and charged him with trafficking. Held roughly **three days**, not overnight as the geography lens says, released on or about 2 January 1982 after French government pressure including an appeal from Mitterrand (*Washington Post*, 2 Jan 1982). **The foundation, corrected [A]:** the chronology lens has Derrida and Jean-Pierre Vernant founding the Jan Hus Educational Foundation in 1981; it was founded at Oxford in 1980 after Jan Patočka's death, and in summer 1981 Vernant became president and Derrida vice-president of its **French branch**.

**Institutions [A, two corrections].** The Collège international de philosophie was founded in 1983 by Derrida, Châtelet, Faye and Lecourt (ciph.org); the geography lens lists Châtelet twice and is one founder short. The EHESS post is misdated there too: elected 1983, chair taken up 1984, held to 2003. From 1986, Professor of Humanities at UC Irvine, part of each year until shortly before his death. *De l'esprit* (1987) on Heidegger's *Geist*. **Nice [A]:** his mother's dying, c. 1989-91, feeding into "Circonfession" (1991), the marginal band beneath Bennington's *Derridabase*; the pool dates this only as "1989-12-01," an ordering approximation, declared as such.

**Dropped for cause [A].** The pool carries a 1983 *Ghost Dance* stop (Ken McMullen, Derrida opposite Pascale Ogier) with a famous line on cinema as the art of ghosts. The film is confirmed; the quotation is **not stable** and circulates in at least three incompatible English translations. The episode was cut rather than print a paraphrase as a quote, and can be restored with a sourced subtitle.

## 8. The language that was never his, 1992-1997

**Baton Rouge, corrected by four years [A].** The geography lens dates *Le monolinguisme de l'autre* to a 1996 LSU conference; the oral version was delivered **23-25 April 1992** at "Echoes from Elsewhere / Renvois d'ailleurs," organized by Mensah and Wills, hosted by Édouard Glissant at LSU. French book 1996, translation 1998.

**Cambridge [A, signatories unresolved].** May 1992, honorary doctorate conferred after public protest and a ballot. **Two incompatible signatory lists in the pool:** chronology names Quine, Ruth Barcan Marcus, David Armstrong; geography names Barry Smith, Quine, René Thom. Only Quine is on both, so only Quine is named, the rest declared disputed. Pin 52.2043/0.1149 is the corpus's Cambridge coordinate from `john_searle.journey.json`.

**Riverside and Capri [A].** April 1993, UC Riverside's "Whither Marxism?", the lectures that become *Spectres de Marx*. Late February 1994, the Capri religion colloquium with Vattimo, Gadamer and Trías, his contribution published as "Foi et savoir."

**Villanova, corrected then cut [A].** The "Religion and Postmodernism" roundtable with Caputo and others was **2 October 1994**, not September (Caputo, ed., *Deconstruction in a Nutshell*, 1997). The stop was cut for length and can be restored.

**The Deleuze tribute, corrected by one day [A].** "Il me faudra errer tout seul," *Libération*, **7 November 1995** (not 6); Deleuze died 4 November. Collected in *Chaque fois unique, la fin du monde* (2003) / *The Work of Mourning* (2001), with the memorial essays for Barthes (1980), Foucault (1984), Deleuze (1995), Lyotard (1998). **Cerisy 1997 [A]:** "L'animal autobiographique," 11-21 July 1997, the ten-hour address behind *L'animal que donc je suis*.

## 9. The last years and the grave, 1999-2004

**Algeria [A].** Derrida appears in Safaa Fathy's *D'ailleurs, Derrida* (1999), filmed partly at his childhood sites. **Gap:** no shooting dates, sites or coordinate in the pool; the El Biar pin is reused and the year is the release year.

**Frankfurt [A].** 22 September 2001, Adorno Prize, address "Fichus" at the Paulskirche, eleven days after the September 11 attacks; two Frankfurt coordinates in the pool ~50 m apart, the Paulskirche value used. **Diagnosis [A vs A]:** pancreatic cancer, 2002 per the chronology lens, "the previous year" (2003) per the geography lens, **not resolved**; the campa states the ambiguity.

**Last interview and death [A].** *Le Monde*, 19 August 2004, "Je suis en guerre contre moi-même," by Jean Birnbaum, English as *Learning to Live Finally* (2007); its subject is survival. Death 9 October 2004 in a Paris hospital, aged 74; he had agreed to take up the Gadamer visiting professorship at Heidelberg. **Gap:** the hospital is not consistently named, so the citywide coordinate stands.

**The grave [A, coordinate contested].** Buried at Ris-Orangis 12 October 2004; son Pierre read a text Derrida had prepared. **Three lenses, three coordinates:** 48.6572/2.4275 (geography), 48.6474/2.4114 (afterlife), and the pool's correction putting the cemetery at approx. 48.660/2.408, up to 1.4 km apart. **Decision:** the house stop uses 48.6572/2.4275, the cemetery stop 48.6600/2.4080; both flagged, neither survey-verified. The tomb is a plain marble slab and the first name cut into it is **Jackie**, not Jacques; he began signing "Jacques Derrida" only on publishing his first work on Husserl (Find a Grave 9580205; *New Statesman*, 2021). **Two graveside endings [A vs A]:** "Always prefer life and never cease affirming survival" versus "I smile at you, I bless you, I love you, wherever I am," both reported as the close of the same address, **not resolved**; the file quotes the second and names the first in `date_confidence`.
