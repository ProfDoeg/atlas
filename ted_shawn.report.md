# Ted Shawn (1891-1972): research report

*2026-08-29, for `ted_shawn.journey.json`. Edwin Myers Shawn, called Ted Shawn: co-founder with Ruth St. Denis of Denishawn (1915-1931), founder of Ted Shawn and His Men Dancers (1933-1940), founder and to his death founding director of Jacob's Pillow, Becket, Massachusetts.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested; **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim reaching this file only through the research dossier. **An [A] with no source named is Wikipedia's "Ted Shawn", "Denishawn" or "Jacob's Pillow" article.** Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

**Structural caution.** Shawn was among the most self-documenting figures in American dance, but the abundance is deceptive: almost everything reachable runs back through two institutional narrators, Wikipedia and Jacob's Pillow itself, and **jacobspillow.org returns HTTP 403 to WebFetch**, so the institution's account of the institution could not be checked at any point.

## 1. Kansas City and Denver: the ministry that did not happen (1891-1911)

- Born **Edwin Myers Shawn, Kansas City, 21 October 1891**, second son of the newspaperman Ellsworth Shawn and Mary Lee Booth Shawn **[A]**. In **1903** his elder brother died, his mother died about eight months later, and he and his father lived in boarding houses **[R: NYPL Ted Shawn Papers note via the dossier]**; a **c. 1906** move to Denver to his remarried father rests on the same trail **[R]** and is **given no stop**, nothing independent supporting the year.
- **University of Denver, ministerial course, not completed [A]. Dating corrected.** The dossier pinned enrolment to 1911 while dating the diphtheria to "age nineteen", flagging its own inconsistency as unresolved. NYPL resolves it: he was "in his third year of studying for the ministry at the University of Denver" when he contracted diphtheria. So c. **1908/09-1911**, the illness breaking off a course already three years old.
- **c. 1910: diphtheria, temporary lower-body paralysis, therapeutic study with Hazel Wallack**, former Metropolitan Opera dancer **[A]**. **Flagged, not carried as fact:** that dance *cured* the paralysis, a conversion narrative Shawn propagated for fifty years with no clinical record behind it.
- **1911, Denver: he saw Ruth St. Denis dance**, dating his artistic birth to that night. **[A: his own account in *Pioneer and Prophet* (1920) ch. IV, read with its surrounding paragraph in the archive.org OCR rather than as an isolated snippet]** Nothing was mutual and nothing is recorded on her side.

## 2. Los Angeles, and the meeting (1912-1914)

- **1912:** Los Angeles, exhibition ballroom with **Norma Gould** **[A; geography lens gave 1912-13]**, no venue being named anywhere reachable, so the pin is a city centroid. The **1914 honeymoon dance tour, Saratoga Springs to San Francisco**, is attested **[A]**.
- **Married St. Denis 13 August 1914 [A]. Two corrections.** The dossier's "married roughly two months later" is wrong: NYPL has "On April 13, 1914, Ruth St. Denis and Ted Shawn began a tour of the southern United States. In August of the same year they were married", so four months of joint work preceded the wedding and the meeting predates April; the file inherits `ruth_st_denis`'s March 1914 arrival. The dossier's "within weeks the two are engaged" overstates the same record; dropped.
- **Honest gap: nobody knows where they married.** No source reached names the ceremony's city, let alone a venue; the marker sits at the inherited New York pin as a convention.

## 3. Denishawn (1915-1926)

- **Summer 1915, 600 St. Paul Avenue, Los Angeles: the school opened [A on year and address]. The pool had the name wrong.** Wikipedia raw: "the two established their first official school, the **Ruth St. Denis School of Dancing and Its Related Arts** at 600 St. Paul Avenue." It opened under her name alone; "Denishawn" came afterward. `ruth_st_denis.journey.json` already carries this correction.
- **Uncontroversial and attested [A]:** the Portland naming contest of 6 February 1915 that produced "The Denishawn Rose Mazurka" and the official short form; the 1916 Palace Theatre bill, with an **all-male Pyrrhic Dance** set inside the Egyptian Ballet, the first appearance of the male-company idea; Martha Graham entering June 1916 and Doris Humphrey in 1917 (`martha_graham`, `doris_humphrey`); the later-1910s West Lake Park second house (ordering date 1918 from `ruth_st_denis`); *Xochitl* in 1920, on a purported Aztec subject, made for Graham; and *Ruth St. Denis: Pioneer and Prophet* (San Francisco: John Howell, 1920), full text fetched at archive.org `ruthstdenis01shawrich`. That *Xochitl*'s scholarship is invented is a judgment, not sourced.
- ***Invocation to the Thunderbird*. Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** Wikipedia's *Ted Shawn* dates it 1917, its *Denishawn* article dates the same piece 1916. File uses 1917 and names the disagreement. **Gap:** no Indigenous collaborator is named in any account reached, here or anywhere in Shawn's Native-American repertory.
- **1923: *Danse Américaine*, a solo for Charles Weidman [A]. Given no stop**: no place established, and the file was at its stop ceiling. Recorded so it is not lost.

### The Asian tour, 1925-26: the weakest evidentiary floor in the file

Wikipedia's Denishawn article: the company was "the first American company to present 'serious Western dance' in **Japan, Burma, China, India, Ceylon, Java, Malaya and the Philippines (1925-26)**." The dossier dated the tour **1925**; the source says **1925-26**. The "first American company" line is **the company's own marketing claim**, not a verified first, and stays attributed in the campa.

**The cities are inference, not attestation.** The source names *countries only*; the eight city stops and their month order are inherited from `ruth_st_denis.journey.json` and `doris_humphrey.journey.json`. The verify lens was right that eight `[A]` city stops cannot rest on a country list, and no itinerary source was found. **Every stop in that segment carries the caution in its own `date_confidence` and reads as `[R]` on city and month.** The geography lens proposed **Calcutta**; **Bombay** is used for continuity with the three files already carrying it. *Sinhalese Devil Dance* and *Dyak Spear Dance*, attributed to tour observations, come from the dossier's repertory listing and are **[R]**.

## 4. The books, and the collapse (1926-1931)

- **1926: *The American Ballet* (New York: Henry Holt). [A: catalogue record; full text fetched, archive.org `americanballet0000teds`]** Shawn's nationalist programme, carrying a social-evolutionary racial argument later scholarship reads as eugenic. The racial passage ("There is no living white man today...") is quoted **precisely rather than softened**, on the *Thunderbird* stop.
- **Three damaged quotes in the pool were repaired.** The "Ballet Russe... American born and American trained dancers" quote **splices two sentences across an intervening passage**, the elision hiding that the middle names *Russians*, not Americans; the file quotes the second whole. The "God-consciousness" quote **deletes a clause with no ellipsis**; restored. In *Gods Who Dance*, "...nor without possession of infinite forms of beauty." **truncates mid-clause and closes with a period**, making a fragment read as complete; restored.
- **1927: Denishawn House, Stevenson Place, the Bronx. [A on year and street]**
- **1929: *Gods Who Dance* (New York: E. P. Dutton). [A: catalogue record; full text fetched, archive.org `bwb_W9-BTJ-415`]**
- **1931: Denishawn disintegrated as marriage and partnership broke down; they never divorced. [A]** The pool gave the place only as "United States"; a conventional New York pin is used.

## 5. Jacob's Pillow and the Men Dancers (1931-1940)

- **1931: bought the hill farm on George Carter Road, Becket.** The name *Jacob's Pillow* was already on it, from Jacob Carter III (1790), the switchback called Jacob's Ladder, and a pillow-shaped boulder **[A]**. **Date corrected** from the geography lens's "1930-1931" to the source's flat 1931.
- **1931: the relationship with Barton Mumaw began**, to about 1948: star soloist, household member, lover **[A]**. **The pool contained a false fact:** the geography lens called Mumaw "foremost among" the Springfield College athletic recruits, and he was not recruited there at all. Per Wikipedia, "Barton Mumaw", he was reared in Eustis, Florida, and came to Shawn after seeing a Denishawn performance in 1930 at eighteen.
- **Springfield College**, all-male, muscular Christianity and physical education: Shawn taught there and recruited its athletes **[A]**. **Sourcing correction:** the pool cited the NEA feature for this, and that article **mentions Springfield College nowhere**.
- **Uncontroversial and attested [A]:** the 1933 Friday "tea" lecture-demonstrations at 75 cents, the first drawing 57 people to seating built for 25 (arts.gov, "The House That Ted Built", 2015); the **14 July 1933** performance from which the festival counts itself continuous, with company members building many of the campus buildings themselves; and the **31 August 1940** final homecoming performance ending the company, partly with American entry into the war approaching.
- **1935: *Kinetic Molpai* premiered on the Men Dancers. [R: dossier citing the Jacob's Pillow Dance Interactive catalogue]** **Date contradiction, flagged:** Wikipedia, citing the same archive, describes a restored **1937** film. Premiere and film need not be inconsistent, but neither is confirmed.
- **11 August 1939: Ruth St. Denis's five-page letter** accusing her estranged husband of attracting "defenseless boys" who sought friendship or mothering and met "an impossible lover" **[R: dossier citing a University of Florida finding-aid excerpt]**. Because the wording is at one remove it is reported in the campa and **not** set in a `quote` field. Neither place of writing nor of receipt is established.

## 6. The theatre, and the long directorship (1940-1972)

- **Uncontroversial and attested [A]:** the 1940-41 lease to **Mary Washington Ball**, whose festival did not pay, taken in severe debt and while considering a sale; the October 1941 meeting of seven local citizens at **Mabel Dwight Busey's** home in Lee with the Pillow about **$20,000** in debt, setting a **$50,000** goal (theberkshireedge.com), with **Markova and Dolin** helping organize the campaign backed by **Reginald Wright**; **Inge Sand's 1954 United States debut** at the Pillow, on short notice, bringing Bournonville before an American audience for the first time (pointemagazine.com); and the **1965 National Dance Association Heritage Award**.
- **1942: the Ted Shawn Theatre**, 514 seats, "the first facility in the United States built specifically for dance" **[A: arts.gov]**. July as the month is the pool's, unconfirmed.
  - **Item split, half of it dropped.** The pool bundled a **University of the Dance**, founded 1942 as successor to the Shawn School of Dance for Men, into this entry. **Nothing reachable supports it, at 1942 or any date.** Not in the journey file. **Recorded as an unresolved claim for the operator, not as a fact and not as a silent deletion.**
- **1943: Joseph Pilates demonstrated his system and taught Pillow students**, with photographs of Shawn on the apparatus and a later correspondence **[R]**. The Berkshire Edge article whose **own URL title names Pilates as a Jacob's Pillow instructor** was reachable, real but oblique support; the 1943 demonstration was not confirmed firsthand. **A real gap between dossier and atlas:** `joseph_pilates.journey.json` was checked in full for "Shawn", "Pillow" and "Becket" and contains **none of them**. Either that file is missing a stop it should have, or this story is thinner than it looks. **Operator decision, not mine.**
- **c. 1949-1972: partnership with the designer and stage manager John Christian Ammann**, after Mumaw's relationship with Christian led to Mumaw's separation from Shawn **[A]**.
- **1954: *Every Little Movement*, the Delsarte study [A].** The French translation *Chaque petit mouvement*, which the afterlife lens could not confirm, **does exist**: OpenLibrary carries it, Éditions Complexe, **Brussels**, 2005, alongside the original (Pittsfield/Brooklyn, 1954) and Dance Horizons reprints from 1968. **Upgraded [R] to [A], the translation being Belgian, not French.**
- **1957: knighted by the King of Denmark** for bringing Danish ballet to America through Jacob's Pillow **[A on the fact: pointemagazine.com]**. **The place was invented by the pool** (see the coordinate audit). The source does not name the king; he was **Frederik IX**.
- **1964: *Siddhas of the Upper Air* with Ruth St. Denis** at the Ted Shawn Theatre, fiftieth anniversary of the partnership, his last appearance on that stage **[A: `ruth_st_denis.journey.json`, which corrected an earlier 1941 dating in the pool by about 23 years]**. Written as **his side** of a scene her file already stages.
- **1967: John Christian appointed executive director; Shawn kept the title of founding director [A].** The geography lens carried this as an unverified "**1966** onward"; the article states 1967 and names Christian as "Shawn's domestic partner and longtime collaborator". **Upgraded [R] to [A], date corrected.**
- **9 January 1972: died, aged eighty, after a lengthy illness the public finding aids do not specify [A on date and age]. Place of death is [R]:** Orlando traces to an NYPL biographical record cited by the dossier; Wikipedia's infobox leaves death place blank.
  - **Honest gap: there is no confirmed grave.** No burial site, cremation record or marker surfaced in anything reached; treat any "buried at X" claim as unconfirmed until a certificate or cemetery record surfaces. An odd hole for a man who documented himself so thoroughly, and it stands in relief against **Ruth St. Denis**, who died in Los Angeles on **21 July 1968** and lies at Forest Lawn, Hollywood Hills, under a stone **[A]**. The marriage never ended in divorce, so she was his wife at her death.

## 8. Posthumous

Facts about Shawn's own honours and estate are carried in campas, on the `abdelkader.journey.json` precedent (the 1966 repatriation inside an 1883 death stop). Other people's later journeys are **not** staged as scenes; see the next section.

- **1987: inducted into the Whitney Hall of Fame, National Museum of Dance, Saratoga Springs [A]**, carried in the campa of the 1914 Saratoga Springs stop, the only Saratoga pin his life supplies. **27 May 2003: Jacob's Pillow designated a National Historic Landmark District [A]**, closing the death stop.
- **America's Irreplaceable Dance Treasures: The First 100.** Shawn is on the list, **[R] on any year**. The dossier said 2000; the afterlife lens "corrected" it to 1999. **Neither is established:** danceheritage.org/treasures.html says only that "In the Fall of 1999, the Dance Heritage Coalition solicited nominations", which is when *nominations opened*, not when honorees were selected. The `suggested_ref` carries the name with no year.
- **Scolieri, *Ted Shawn* (Oxford, 2019)** and **Walter Terry, *Ted Shawn: Father of American Dance*:** both books exist; the characterizations of Scolieri as the definitive archival life and of Terry as having fixed the epithet are **[R]**, neither checked against a review.
- The Jacob's Pillow paraphrase that Shawn's choreography both "celebrated and othered" the forms it drew on is **nowhere presented in quotation marks**: unverified, behind the 403.
