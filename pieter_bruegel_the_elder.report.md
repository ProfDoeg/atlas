# Pieter Bruegel the Elder (c. 1525-1569): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `pieter_bruegel_the_elder`. Journey file: `pieter_bruegel_the_elder.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops, calendar `julian`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, document or object named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged and left unresolved.

**The shape of the problem.** Bruegel is a major painter with a thin documentary spine. The hard record is: one guild register entry (1551), one Mechelen subcontract (1550), one Roman probate inventory (Clovio, 1577), one Antwerp collateral inventory (Jonghelinck, 1566), one Brussels marriage deposition (25 July 1563), one burial record (9 September 1569), plus roughly forty signed or dated pictures and the print inscriptions. Everything else, meaning birthplace, teacher, the Italian route, the man's temperament, and the burnt drawings, descends from Karel van Mander's *Het Schilder-boeck* (1604), written thirty-five years after the death by someone who never met him. Where van Mander is the only source, this report says so and the journey file tags the stop `reconstruction`.

---

## 1. Birth and the unlocatable village (c. 1525-1530)

- Born in or near **Breda**, Duchy of Brabant, in a village van Mander calls "Brueghel". **[R: van Mander 1604]**. No baptismal record survives and no village of that name has been identified on any map of Brabant.
- The year **c. 1525** is attested nowhere. It is computed backward from the 1551 guild entry; estimates run c. 1525-1530. **[R]**
- Rival claim: birth at **Grote-Brogel**, a hamlet beside **Bree** in Belgian Limburg, whose older name could as easily have given the painter his. **[R: Flemish local tradition, per Sellink 2007]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Breda (van Mander, followed by most of the literature) and Bree/Grote-Brogel are mutually exclusive, and neither carries a document. The journey file gives both as separate stops with matching hedges rather than picking a winner.
- **Coordinate errors in the pool, corrected.** Its Breda point (51.5719, 4.7683) sits about 1.9 km SSW of the Grote Markt it names; corrected to **51.5889, 4.7758**. Its Limburg point (51.1378, 5.5964) sits on Bree, not on Grote-Brogel (about 51.156, 5.549), and misspelled the village "Groot-Brogel"; the stop is renamed **Bree** and pinned at **51.1417, 5.5967**.
- Breda's Brabant status is **[A]**: sold to Duke Johannes III of Brabant in 1327.

**Gap.** Nothing is known of the first twenty years: no parents, no schooling, no early work. Van Mander's framing of him as low-born is a literary opening, not a document.

## 2. Training and first work (c. 1545-1551)

- Apprenticeship with **Pieter Coecke van Aelst**. **[R: van Mander 1604]**. No contract, and the guild registers do not enter him as anyone's pupil. Circumstantial support: the later marriage to Coecke's daughter, the print-designer habits learned young, and a workshop that was publisher as well as studio.
- **Coecke dies in Brussels, 6 December 1550. [A]**, before his supposed pupil signs anything.
- **1550-1551, Mechelen.** Documented assisting **Peeter Baltens** on an altarpiece for the glovemakers' guild, Bruegel on the wings, Baltens on the centre. **[A: Mechelen guild and city archives, per Sellink 2007]**. The altarpiece is lost. Earliest firm trace of professional activity.
- **1551, Antwerp.** Entered in the **Liggeren** of the Guild of Saint Luke as "Peeter Brueghels", free master. **[A: Antwerp guild register]**. First hard date in the career.

**Gap.** No work from before 1551 has been identified and the Mechelen wings are lost, so the training period is documented only by an entry recording that it ended.

## 3. The Italian journey (c. 1552-1554)

The journey is real; the itinerary is reconstructed from dated drawings. No travel diary, letter, or payment survives for any leg except Rome.

- **Route south via Lyon. [R: traditional reconstruction, Silver 2011]**. No document names him there; Clovio's inventory later lists a *view of Lyon* by him, which dates nothing.
- **Alpine crossing, 1552. [A for the crossing, R for the pass.]** The pool tagged the Great Saint Bernard **[A]**; that is unsupportable, since no document places him on any named pass. The dated Alpine pen drawings (British Museum, Louvre) establish that he crossed, not where. Coordinates 45.8697, 7.1706 fall within about 120 m of the real pass and are retained with the tag downgraded.
- **Waltensburg, Grisons. [R]**, with a contradiction inherited from the pool and flagged rather than fixed: **Waltensburg/Vuorz is in the Surselva at 46.767, 9.117**, but the pool's coordinate (46.6764, 9.4394) lands about 28 km southeast near Thusis, in the **Domleschg**, which is the valley the pool's own claim text names. Label and pin cannot both be right. The stop is pinned at the real Waltensburg and the contradiction stated in the campa.
- **Reggio Calabria, 1552. [R, corrected down from the pool's [A].]** The pool asserted a witnessed Ottoman bombardment in 1552; no such event is documented. Wikipedia 'Reggio Calabria' lists Turkish attacks in **1534** (Barbarossa burns the town) and **1558** (sack, inhabitants carried to Tripoli). 'Turgut Reis' jumps from 1551 to **May 1553** (Crotone, Castello) and dates the sack of Reggio to **June 1558**. The evidence for Bruegel here is a drawing of the town burning, known through copies.
- **Sicily and Messina. [R]**. A lost drawing of a naval battle in the strait, surviving in copies, is the only argument, and it works equally well if he drew from the Calabrian shore. Palermo rests on tradition alone.
- **Naples. [R, corrected.]** The pool dated the Doria Pamphilj *Naval Battle in the Gulf of Naples* to the journey; Wikipedia dates the panel **1558-1562**, painted in Antwerp from sketches and memory, attribution not unanimous. The reasoning is circular: Naples is on the itinerary because of the picture, and the picture is his because of the itinerary.
- **Rome, 1553. [A]**. The strongest anchor of the journey: **Giulio Clovio's testamentary inventory** lists works by "Pietro Brugole", including a joint picture, a Tower of Babel on ivory, and a view of Lyon. **Date correction:** the pool gave 1578; Clovio died 5 January 1578 and the will and inventory are normally cited as **1577**. All six Bruegels named in it have disappeared.
- **Return over the Brenner, 1554-1555. [R]**, route inferred.

**Gap.** No painting can be assigned to the Italian years. He returns at about thirty with a portfolio of drawings and no demonstrable paintings at all.

## 4. The Cock years: Antwerp print designer (1555-1563)

- **Hieronymus Cock's shop In de Vier Winden / Aux Quatre Vents**, near Antwerp cathedral, commissions **more than forty print designs [A]** across the decade, beginning with the **Large Landscapes, 1555 [A: Cock's publishing records; Van Bastelaer and Lebeer]**, cut from the Alpine sheets.
- **1557: *Big Fish Eat Little Fish* published inscribed "Hieronymus Bos inventor". [A: surviving print inscription; Van Bastelaer, New Hollstein]**. Drawn 1556, engraved 1557, forty-one years after Bosch's death. The documented physical instance of Bruegel's early work marketed under Bosch's name, because Bosch's name sold and Bruegel's did not yet.
- **The hell and vice series, c. 1556-1562. [A as works, R as to the exact character of the Bosch relation.]** Vasari and van Mander both frame Bruegel as a comic successor to Bosch **[A]**, and Wikipedia's Bosch article calls Bruegel "his best-known follower".
- **1557: first securely signed and dated painting. [A]**. The painting career therefore runs twelve years.
- **1559: he begins signing "Bruegel", dropping the h. [A: signature analysis]**. Folded into the Proverbs stop rather than given its own pin.

## 5. The Antwerp panels (1559-1563)

- **1559 *Netherlandish Proverbs***, signed and dated, Gemäldegalerie Berlin. **[A]**. Over a hundred proverbs enacted literally. The contemporary caption was verified against the **raw wikitext** of Wikipedia 'Netherlandish Proverbs': "Die blau huicke is dit meest ghenaemt / Maer des weerelts abuisen het beter betaempt", from **Frans Hogenberg's 1558 engraving**, not from Bruegel's panel. The pool's transcription had "he beter betaempt"; corrected to **"het"**. A rendered-page fetch returned "abvisen"; the wikitext confirms **"abuisen"**.
- **1559 *The Battle Between Carnival and Lent***, signed and dated, KHM Vienna. **[A]**
- **1560 *Children's Games***, signed and dated, KHM Vienna. **[A]**. **Correction:** the count of about 80 catalogued games is supported (Wikipedia: "over 90 different games… the detailed catalog identifying 80 distinct games"); the pool's **"230 children" is not**, Wikipedia giving no count at all, and the figure usually quoted elsewhere being about 246. The journey file states the game count and declines the child count.
- **c. 1562 *The Triumph of Death***, Museo del Prado. **[A as work, R as to date]**, undated.
- **1562 *The Fall of the Rebel Angels***, signed and dated, Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium, Brussels. **[A]**. Long catalogued as a Bosch.
- **The Antwerp maidservant and the tally stick. [R: van Mander only.]** He says Bruegel kept house with a servant girl given to lying, notched a stick for each lie, and that Mayken Verhulst made removal to Brussels a condition of the marriage. **The pool tagged this [A]; retagged [R] here.**

## 6. Brussels: marriage, tower, months (1563-1567)

- **Marriage to Mayken Coecke, 1563, Brussels. [A]**. **Correction:** the pool gave the wedding as 25 July 1563. Wikipedia states that "their deposition for marriage was **registered** on 25 July 1563", the banns and not the ceremony; the wedding itself is dated only to **1563**. The journey file dates the stop 1563-07-25 and says in `date_confidence` exactly what that date is.
- **Kapellekerk coordinates.** The pool used **50.841, 4.3459** in the geography lens and **50.8417, 4.3511** in the afterlife lens for the same church. Wikipedia gives 50°50'30"N 4°21'04"E, that is **50.8417, 4.3511**, and both stops are harmonized on it.
- **Relocation to Brussels, 1563; house on the Hoogstraat. [A for the move, R for the specific house.]** The Bruegelhuis identification rests on later property and institutional documentation.
- **1563 *Dulle Griet*. [A, redated.]** The pool placed it in Antwerp, c. 1561-1562. The **2018 restoration** read the date as **1563**, "shortly after the painter had moved to Brussels" (Wikipedia 'Dull Gret'), making it a Brussels work. It hangs in Antwerp, which is where it is, not where it was made.
- **1563 *The Tower of Babel* (Vienna). [A]**, signed "Brvegel. FE. M.CCCCC.LXIII".
  - *One-directional citation, not a stop:* **Jan van Eyck's 1437 *Saint Barbara* (KMSKA, Antwerp)**, showing the saint before a tower still under construction, is read as a pictorial ancestor of the motif. That is van Eyck's object at van Eyck's place; it appears in `suggested_refs` on Bruegel's Babel stop and is not staged as a scene inside Bruegel's journey.
- **1564 *The Procession to Calvary*** (KHM Vienna), **1564 *The Adoration of the Magi*** (National Gallery, London), and the birth of **Pieter Brueghel the Younger** in Brussels. **[A]**
- **The Months cycle, 1565.** Commissioned by **Niclaes Jonghelinck** for his villa **Ter Beke**, recorded in the **1566** inventory of his collection pledged to the city of Antwerp as security. **[A for the inventory, R for Ter Beke as the hanging place.]** **Correction:** the pool framed the cycle's size as an open "five or six". The 1566 document names "the twelve Months of the Year", and the standard reading is now **six bi-monthly panels, one missing, five surviving**.
  - Surviving: *Hunters in the Snow*, *The Gloomy Day*, *The Return of the Herd* (KHM Vienna), *The Harvesters* (Metropolitan Museum), *The Hay Harvest* (Lobkowicz Collection, Prague). **[A]**
- **c. 1565-1567 *The Massacre of the Innocents*. [A as work, R as to date.]** **Location correction:** the Royal Collection original **moved to Windsor Castle in 2017** and is no longer at Hampton Court as the pool had it. Rudolf II had the slaughtered infants overpainted as bundles, food, and animals; the overpaint was confirmed in **1998** conservation and left in place. The Vienna version is now considered a copy by Pieter Brueghel the Younger or his studio.

## 7. The last works (1566-1569)

- **c. 1565-1568 *The Wine of Saint Martin's Day***, Museo del Prado. **[R as to date.]** **Corrections:** not securely 1566 as the pool had it, but c. 1565-1568 on stylistic grounds; **not a panel** but glue-size tempera on linen; identified as a Bruegel only in **2010**, when X-rays revealed signature fragments.
- **1567 *The Land of Cockaigne***, Alte Pinakothek, Munich, signed and dated. **[A]**
- **1567 *The Peasant Wedding***, KHM Vienna, oil on panel, 114 by 164 cm. **[A]**. The pool dated it 1568; Wikipedia gives **1567**. **c. 1567 *The Peasant Dance***, KHM Vienna **[A/R]**, which the pool wrongly bracketed with the 1568 *Blind Leading the Blind*.
- **1568 *The Blind Leading the Blind***, distemper on linen, Museo di Capodimonte, Naples. **[A]**
- **1568 *The Magpie on the Gallows***, Hessisches Landesmuseum Darmstadt. **[A as work, R as to the bequest]**: van Mander alone reports that Bruegel left it to his wife.
- **1568: Jan Brueghel the Elder born, Brussels. [A]**

## 8. Death, tomb, afterlife (1569 onward)

- **Deathbed instruction to burn drawings "too sharp or sarcastic". [R: van Mander only.]** No list, no corroboration, no surviving fragments. The attested fact is that van Mander wrote it in 1604; whether the drawings existed is not independently confirmed.
- **Died Brussels, 9 September 1569. [A: burial record and epitaph, Church of Notre-Dame de la Chapelle]**
- **Buried in the Kapellekerk. [A]**. **Correction, retagged [R]:** the pool asserted **[A]** that Jan Brueghel commissioned a memorial epitaph with a Rubens contribution. The church's own article says only that "a modest funerary monument commemorates Pieter Brueghel the Elder, who was buried there in 1569", and separately that the church holds a **copy** of *Christ handing over the keys to Saint Peter*, with no link asserted to the tomb.
- **The sons raised and first taught by their grandmother Mayken Verhulst. [A: van Mander]**. Pieter the Younger is five, Jan an infant.
- **Pieter Brueghel the Younger's Antwerp copying workshop. [A, range corrected.]** The pool gave "1588 to 1626/27"; Wikipedia has the workshop in Antwerp "from the 1580s onward", Brueghel born 1564 and dead **1638**, with "approximately 1,400 pictures… with plausible connections to Brueghel and his studio". *Winter Landscape with Skaters and a Bird Trap* alone survives in at least 60 studio versions, ten signed and four dated.
- **Abraham Ortelius, 1574:** "the most perfect painter of his century", in his *album amicorum*. **[A]**
- **Reputation.** Read chiefly as a comic peasant painter for roughly three centuries; rehabilitation dates from **Henri Hymans, 1890-91**, and gathered force as photographic and colour reproductions of the landscapes circulated. **[A]**

## 9. Interlocks: what was used and what was refused

**Used, named in a campa.** **Hieronymus Bosch** (`hieronymus_bosch`, queued, not yet researched), at three stops: the *Big Fish Eat Little Fish* print issued as "Hieronymus Bos inventor", the hell and vice series drawn for Cock, and *The Fall of the Rebel Angels*, long catalogued as a Bosch. A genuine one-directional inheritance, and QUEUE.md already frames the two as "the two poles of Netherlandish painting".

**Refused, shared geography only.** **John Dee** (`john_dee`) was in Antwerp in 1563 copying Trithemius and printing the *Monas Hieroglyphica* there in 1564, the same city and years as Bruegel's departure for Brussels: no contact, no document, no influence either way, and the Mercator-Ortelius chain that puts Dee two links away is a fact about Ortelius. **Dante Gabriel Rossetti** (`dante_gabriel_rossetti`) visited the Brussels museum that now holds *The Fall of the Rebel Angels* in October 1849, but a later admirer's museum visit is that admirer's stop. **Jan van Eyck** (via `saint_barbara`) supplies a tower motif that is an art-historical reading rather than an attested influence, so he is cited in `suggested_refs` on the Babel stop and nowhere in prose. **Michelangelo**, **W.G. Sebald**, **Karl Marx**, and **San Antonio Abad** connect only through shared cities, shared iconography, or co-location centuries apart; a draft line describing Clovio by his contemporary epithet, "the Michelangelo of small works", was cut for exactly that reason. Canonical pins with no Bruegel tie, recorded as negative results: Paris, the Kaaba, Temple Mount, Buenos Aires.

**Pins inherited byte-identical from the pool and the corpus,** except where section 1, 3, and 6 record a correction: Antwerp 51.2194, 4.4025 (the Cock press and Coecke workshop pin, shared with `john_dee` and the afterlife lens), Rome, Lyon, Reggio, Messina, Naples, Mechelen, the two Alpine passes, the Antwerp guild, Ter Beke, and the Hoogstraat.

## 10. Honest gaps

1. The first twenty-five years are empty, and the birthplace is contested between two towns about 90 km apart.
2. No painting survives from before 1557, six years after he became a master.
3. The Italian itinerary outside Rome is entirely inferential, and the Reggio chronology does not close.
4. The Waltensburg sheet contradicts itself between name (Surselva) and topography (Domleschg); the literature has carried both.
5. The burnt drawings cannot be assessed: one source, thirty-five years late, no list, no survivors.
6. No portrait of Bruegel is securely identified and no autograph letter survives. Everything about his temperament comes from van Mander.
7. The sixth Months panel is unlocated, and the 1566 document's "twelve Months" is the only contemporary description of the set.
8. The Bruegelhuis on the Hoogstraat is respectable tradition rather than a documented address.
