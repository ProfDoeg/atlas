# Sophie von Kühn (1782-1797): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, 2026-08-11. Companion: `sophie_von_kuhn.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim whose source offers no evidence. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; unreachable sources are named.

**The shape of the problem.** Sophie von Kühn died at fifteen having written, so far as anything reachable shows, five short diary entries. Everything else is testimony by other people, most of it after her death, much of it by men who never saw her. The evidence splits into two bodies that must not be mixed: a thin documentary record (dates, a death notice, an inscription, the diary) and a thick literary one (Novalis's journal, Tieck's 1815 preface, two centuries of scholarship). This report keeps them apart and flags where the second has been read into the first.

---

## 1. Name, birth, household (1782-1794)

- Born **17 March 1782**, full name **Christiane Wilhelmine Sophie von Kühn**. **[A]** de/en.wikipedia, "Sophie von Kühn" ("* 17. März 1782; † 19. März 1797 in Grüningen"); the date is repeated on the memorial medallion at Grüningen.
- **Birthplace is NOT attested. [R]** The German lead gives a death place only. Birth at Schloss Grüningen is inference: her father **Johann Georg von Kühn** rebuilt the castle in 1772 and it was the family seat. de.wikipedia, "Schloss Grüningen (Thüringen)".
- **Grüningen is an Ortsteil of Greußen, Kyffhäuserkreis, Thuringia**, at 51.229564 N, 10.974752 E. **[A]** same article. *Correction logged:* two pool entries placed it at 51.2333 / 11.8667, near Bad Lauchstädt in Saxony-Anhalt, and called the churchyard Saxon-Anhaltine. Both wrong.
- Mother **Sophie Wilhelmine von Kühn**; stepfather **Captain Johann Rudolph von Rockenthien**, who holds Schloss Grüningen **[A]** en.wikipedia, "Sophie von Kühn", and who acquired Grüningen and Niedertopfstedt by marrying the widow in **late 1786**, the castle standing entailed to her two underage sons **[A]** de.wikipedia, "Rockenthien". No exact date; the journey file defaults month and day.
- Siblings: Friederike, Karoline (Caroline), George, Hans von Kühn; half-sister Wilhelmine von Kühn / von Thümmel from the father's first marriage; further Rockenthien half-siblings. **[A]** en.wikipedia, "Sophie von Kühn".
- **Whether Sophie ever resided at Niedertopfstedt is not established [R]**; nothing consulted describes her education, reading, or health before 1795.

## 2. The counterpart childhood: Hardenberg to 1794

Carried as the ground the meeting comes out of, not as Sophie's biography. All **[A]** de.wikipedia, "Novalis", except as noted.

- **Georg Philipp Friedrich von Hardenberg** born **2 May 1772** at Schloss Oberwiederstedt, Mansfeld (also de.wikipedia, "Oberwiederstedt"); childhood at the moated manor of **Schlöben** to 1786 (de.wikipedia, "Schlöben"; dropped from the journey file for stop budget); then with his uncle **Friedrich Wilhelm von Hardenberg**, Landkomtur of the Teutonic Order, at the commandery of **Lucklum**, "als Zwölfjähriger". *Corrections logged:* the pool gave the name order as "Georg Friedrich Philipp", and dated Lucklum "c. 1782"; he turned twelve in May 1784, and the exact date is unknown **[R]**.
- Gymnasium at **Eisleben** under Rektor Christian David Jani (1790); law at **Jena** (1790), **Leipzig** (1791-92, where he meets Friedrich Schlegel), **Wittenberg** (1793-94); **Aktuarius at Tennstedt** under Kreisamtmann **Coelestin August Just** from **October 1794**, day unattested (also de.wikipedia, "Bad Tennstedt"). *Flagged:* `novalis.journey.json` dates the same posting 1794-11-01.

## 3. The meeting and the engagement (1794-1795)

- **17 November 1794, Schloss Grüningen: Novalis (22) meets Sophie (12). [A]** de.wikipedia, "Sophie von Kühn" ("begegneten sich am 17. November 1794 die zwölfjährige Sophie und der 22-jährige Novalis zum ersten Mal") and "Schloss Grüningen (Thüringen)".
  - **Contradiction flagged:** en.wikipedia, "Novalis" dates the meeting to **1795**. The October 1794 posting makes a November 1794 visit possible and two German articles give the day; the English date is treated as an error and recorded as one.
- **The "quarter of an hour."** The words are real; the standard framing is wrong on three counts. **[A]** O'Brien 1995, 49-50, citing *Schriften* IV, 367:
  1. **Novalis's letter to Erasmus does not survive** (O'Brien, 49: "a now lost letter").
  2. The surviving text is **Erasmus von Hardenberg's sceptical reply**, not Novalis's own sentence: "You write me that a quarter hour has determined you; how can you see through a girl in a quarter of an hour? ... a quarter hour sounds too miraculous."
  3. It is **not dated 17 November 1794**; that is the date of the meeting. The lost letter came "a few days later," the reply later still. Neither O'Brien ("the nearby town of Gruningen") nor Karl von Hardenberg (O'Brien, 35: "a neighboring estate in Gruningen") says "Grüningen Castle" of the letter, though the meeting at the castle is separately attested.
- **Engagement, March 1795, unofficial. Contradiction NOT resolved:** **15 March** ("kurz vor ihrem 13. Geburtstag"), de.wikipedia **[A]**; **17 March**, her birthday itself, en.wikipedia **[A]**, both in "Novalis" and "Sophie von Kühn". The journey file pins the German date and carries both. *Correction logged:* the pool claimed "one cited source gives the year as 1785, evidently a transcription error." No consulted source gives 1785 anywhere; the sub-claim invents an error and corrects it. Deleted.
- **Her parents consent later in 1795. [A]** en.wikipedia, "Novalis". No month; the journey file defaults to 1795-01-01 and says the default lands earlier in the year than the event. **Repeated courtship visits from Tennstedt and later Weißenfels, 1795-1796 [R]**: pattern inferred, no source itemizes visits, folded into the engagement stop rather than pinned.

## 4. Sophie's diary

Five entries, quoted by O'Brien 1995 and reproduced on en.wikipedia. The only words of hers this research reached.

| Entry | Text as used here |
|---|---|
| 1 March | "Today Hartenberch visited again nothing happened." |
| 11 March | "We were all alone today and nothing at all happened." (O'Brien 1995, 54) |
| 12 March | "Today was like yesterday nothing at all happened." |
| 13 March | "Today was repentance day and Hartenb. was here." |
| 14 March | "Today Hartenber. was still here he got a letter from his brother." |

- **Correction logged, and it matters.** English Wikipedia prints the 11 March entry as "We were alone today." O'Brien, 54 reads **"We were all alone today,"** verified by two full-text searches inside archive.org item `novalissignsofre01obri`. "All alone" is the household party; "alone" is the two of them, and the second is the reading an atlas is tempted to lean on. O'Brien's wording is used.
- Only the 11 March entry is tied to a year (**1795**); the others are printed under day and month alone, and the journey file's assignment of 1795 to them is **[R]**. All are quoted **via O'Brien and via Wikipedia's reproduction of O'Brien**; *Schriften* IV, 586f. was not read directly.
- **Open question, not resolved:** "repentance day" renders Buß- und Bettag, which in the Saxon and Prussian calendars of the period ordinarily fell in **November**, not March. Nothing consulted addresses this. **[R]**

## 5. Illness, surgery, death (1795-1797)

- **November 1795:** falls ill at Grüningen, liver inflammation understood as connected with pulmonary tuberculosis, with an apparent recovery that does not hold. **[A]** de/en.wikipedia; anthrowiki.at.
- **May-July 1796:** three severe operations at **Jena**, without anesthesia. **[A]** de/en.wikipedia, "Sophie von Kühn". They are not individually dated, and no source names the clinic or house, so the pin marks the city **[R]**. **Contradiction flagged, NOT resolved:** the surgeon is **Johann (Christian) Stark the Elder** in the pool (en.wikipedia) and **Justus Christian Loder** in the atlas's own `novalis.journey.json`; both named, neither chosen.
- **January 1796, not 1797: Novalis is already at Weißenfels. [A]** de.wikipedia, "Novalis": "Januar 1796 wurde er Akzessist bei der Salinendirektion in Weißenfels." **Contradiction flagged:** en.wikipedia dates the auditor appointment to **January 1797**. The German date is followed because it explains the geography of the death; the English one is recorded, not deleted.
- **March 1797: his parents' formal approval of the engagement, shortly before her death. [R]** en.wikipedia, "Novalis" only; the month is inferred from "shortly before."
- **19 March 1797: Sophie dies at Schloss Grüningen**, two days after her fifteenth birthday, with Novalis at Weißenfels; the **death notice runs in the Leipziger Zeitung on 25 March**. **[A]** de/en.wikipedia, "Sophie von Kühn"; de.wikipedia, "Novalis".
- **Burial: attested, contrary to the incoming pool.** A medallion in the wall of the Grüningen village church (**St. Petri**) reads: "Auf diesem Gottesacker ruht Sophie von Kühn, geb. 17. März 1782, gest. 19. März 1797 auf Schloß Grüningen. Die Braut des Dichters Friedrich von Hardenberg (Novalis)." **[A]** de.wikipedia, "Schloss Grüningen (Thüringen)" and "Sophie von Kühn" (image caption). *Correction logged:* two pool entries called the burial an unresolved gap; it is not. Unknown are the **exact grave plot** (stated as unknown by the same source), the **burial date**, and the tablet's installation date, all **[R]**.

## 6. The grave, the journal, the Hymns (1797-1800)

- **13 May 1797, the grave-vision. [A]** de.wikipedia, "Hymnen an die Nacht", quoting the 1797 Tagebuch: "Abends ging ich zu Sophieen. Dort war ich unbeschreiblich freudig – aufblitzende Enthusiasmus Momente – Das Grab blies ich wie Staub, vor mir hin – Jahrhunderte waren wie Momente – ihre Nähe war fühlbar – ich glaubte sie solle immer vortreten." (verbatim)
- **The Third Hymn quotes the 1797 journal nearly verbatim [A]**, but **current scholarship rejects dating its composition to 1797**, since no preliminary versions exist and the memory was reworked retrospectively into an initiation narrative **[A]** (de.wikipedia, "Hymnen an die Nacht"; en.wikipedia, "Hymns to the Night"). The popular "the diary entry is the seed of the poem" is therefore **[R]**.
- **Erasmus von Hardenberg dies of consumption in 1797. Interval NOT resolved:** en.wikipedia says "four months" after 19 March, i.e. July **[A as a source claim]**; the verification pass gives **14 April 1797**, under four weeks, but **could not produce a citable page in session** and warns that de.wikipedia names only the father, Heinrich Ulrich Erasmus von Hardenberg (1738-1814), an easy confusion **[R]**. The journey file pins 14 April, carries the dispute, and marks the Weißenfels place-pin as inference.
- **The 1797 journal** in English is **Bruce Donehower, *The Birth of Novalis* (SUNY Press, 2007)**. *Correction logged:* the pool cited a "Contra Mundum Press, 2012" edition titled *The Novalis Journal*, unverifiable. Neither was read here; the journal is used only where de.wikipedia quotes it directly.
- **End of 1797: matriculates at the Bergakademie Freiberg** under Abraham Gottlob Werner and W. A. Lampadius. **[A]** de/en.wikipedia, "Novalis". **Contradiction, resolved and recorded:** the Stanford Encyclopedia places the mining studies "just prior to Sophie's death"; both Wikipedias place them after. The SEP sentence is treated as an error, and the same paragraph carries the unsupported Goethe claim below.
- **December 1798:** second engagement, to Julie von Charpentier, at Freiberg **[A]**; **December 1799:** assessor of the salt works at Weißenfels **[A]**. en/de.wikipedia, "Novalis"; en.wikipedia, "Sophie von Kühn". *Correction logged:* en.wikipedia's "assessor and director" is unsupported by the German source; "director" dropped.
- **1800: Hymnen an die Nacht printed in the final issue of the Athenäum** (Band 3, Stück 2), edited by August Wilhelm and Friedrich Schlegel at Berlin. **[A]** de.wikipedia, "Athenäum (Schlegel)" and "Hymnen an die Nacht". *Corrections logged:* "August 1800" is supported by nothing consulted and is dropped; composition is better given as verse manuscript late 1799 / early 1800, prose print version January-February 1800. "The only major work Novalis saw published in his lifetime" is **false**: Blüthenstaub and Glauben und Liebe (both 1798) preceded it, so the only major *poetic* work.
- **6 December 1800:** appointed **Supernumerar-Amtshauptmann** for the Thuringian district. **[A]** de.wikipedia, "Novalis". *Correction logged:* not "Amtmann," and not a vague "end of 1800."

## 7. His death (1800-1801)

**August 1800:** begins coughing blood, tuberculosis diagnosed, with a later scholarly suspicion of cystic fibrosis. **November 1800:** severe hemorrhage, moved to Dresden. **January 1801:** asks to be returned to his parents' house at Weißenfels. **25 March 1801:** dies there, aged 28, and is buried in the Alter Friedhof. All **[A]** en/de.wikipedia, "Novalis"; the burial date 29 March is inherited from `novalis.journey.json`.

## 8. The making of the Sophie myth

- **Tieck's 1815 biographical preface is the source of the public Sophie**, and it is myth-making, not testimony. **[A]** via O'Brien 1995, 36 (S* I, xvi-xvii; reprinted *Schriften* IV, 552f.).
  - **Tieck never saw her. [A]** O'Brien, 35: "he never once saw the girl. Sophie von Kuhn had died more than two years before Tieck met Hardenberg in July 1799." The passage is also **a generalisation about a type**, not a memory: "these transfigured and nearly transparent faces," "such creatures." Tieck applies it to her afterwards.
  - **REJECTED wording.** The English text circulating as Tieck on Sophie ("Even as a child, she gave an impression which ... we must call superearthly or heavenly") is **untraceable**: an insource search of all English Wikipedia returns one hit, the "Sophie von Kühn" article itself, tagged "citation needed" since **April 2008**; Wikisource returns zero; "superearthly" and "delicately woven" return zero matches inside O'Brien. It also converts a plural generalisation into a singular eyewitness memory. O'Brien's translation is used instead.
  - **Contradiction flagged:** the preface says "She had completed her thirteenth year when he met her," against an attested age of **twelve**. Not resolved; recorded as one more sign the preface is a construction.
- **Goethe and "a whole generation of poets and artists in Germany" were moved by her death. [R]**, downgraded from [A]. The quotation is accurate to the Stanford Encyclopedia, "Novalis", but SEP gives no evidence, no contemporary source, and no date, and the same paragraph carries the Freiberg error above.
- **The "cult of the beloved dead" framing** is an interpretive tradition, not a single attested claim **[R]**; **George MacDonald's** translation of the Hymns in *Rampolli* (1897) is standard bibliography, not re-verified here **[R]**.
- **Penelope Fitzgerald, *The Blue Flower*:** UK 1995 (Flamingo), US 1997, **1997 National Book Critics Circle Award for Fiction**. **[A]** en.wikipedia, "The Blue Flower", "Penelope Fitzgerald". *Corrections logged:* the pool's "UK title / US title" construction is spurious; the **BBC list of the 100 greatest British novels is unsupported** and is dropped. Recorded instead: a **1999 BBC Radio dramatisation by Peter Wolf** and the **Observer's 2012 "ten best historical novels"**.
- **Present-day sites. [A]** Schloss Grüningen, confiscated 1945, operates as a care facility named **"Schloss Sophie von Kühn"** under the Novalis-Diakonie-Gesellschaft. The Hardenberg house at **Klosterstraße 24, Weißenfels** (built 1680) holds the city library and the Novalis museum. **Schloss Oberwiederstedt** opened as museum and research centre in **May 1989**, after preservationists prevented its demolition. de.wikipedia, "Schloss Grüningen (Thüringen)", "Weißenfels", "Oberwiederstedt".

## Contradictions carried, not resolved

**Engagement day**, 15 March 1795 (de) vs 17 March, her birthday (en). **Jena surgeon**, Stark the Elder (en.wikipedia) vs Loder (`novalis.journey.json`). **Erasmus's death**, July 1797 (en.wikipedia) vs 14 April 1797 (verification pass, no citable page). **Weissenfels appointment**, January 1796 (de) vs January 1797 (en). **Meeting year**, 17 November 1794 (two German articles) vs 1795 (en). **Freiberg sequence**, after her death (both Wikipedias) vs "just prior" (SEP). **Her age at the meeting**, twelve (de.wikipedia) vs "had completed her thirteenth year" (Tieck 1815). **Tennstedt posting**, October 1794 (de.wikipedia) vs 1794-11-01 (`novalis.journey.json`). **"Repentance day"** in a March diary entry against a November Buß- und Bettag.

## Gaps stated as gaps

Her **birthplace**; her **burial date** and **exact grave plot** (churchyard attested by inscription, plot stated as unknown by the same source); the **installation date of the medallion**; the **clinic or house in Jena**, so that pin marks the city; **Erasmus's place of death**, so that pin is inference; the **year on four of the five diary entries**; **whether she ever set foot on Niedertopfstedt**; and **her education, reading, health before November 1795, and any description of her written by someone who saw her**. On the last, nothing at all was reached: Tieck, the usual source for her appearance, never saw her.
