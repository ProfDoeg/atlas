# Charles Ponzi (1882-1949): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-20 for `charles_ponzi.journey.json`. Carlo Pietro Giovanni Guglielmo Tebaldo Ponzi, born Lugo (Ravenna), died Rio de Janeiro; the man whose surname became the U.S. regulatory term for paying old investors out of new investors' money.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in place. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or uncorroborated self-report. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Gaps are labelled in place. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method.** Well served by courts, badly served by everything else. Four spines carry the dated findings: *In re Ponzi* (1920), *Ponzi v. Fessenden* (1922), *Cunningham v. Commissioner of Banks* (1924), and the NARA Atlanta inmate case file. Nearly everything else descends from Ponzi's memoir and from Zuckoff via Smithsonian. Anything tracing only to the memoir is tagged [R] however confidently secondaries repeat it, the memoir being a professional persuader writing about himself.

---

## 1. Italy, 1882-1903

- Born 3 March 1882, Lugo, province of Ravenna, to Oreste Ponzi and Imelda Tommasini. [A: Wikipedia; Britannica] *Contradiction:* tertiary accounts give Parma, where he was raised and which is the city Ponzi gave reporters, probably the origin of the error. Not resolved beyond preferring Lugo.
- Family remembers itself as fallen from higher comfort on the Tommasini side. [R: Zuckoff via Smithsonian; no archival evidence of the prosperity located]
- University of Rome, La Sapienza, left without a degree after spending family money. [R: the memoir] Duration disputed, about a year versus a longer dissipation.
- *Coordinate correction.* Sapienza's seat then was the Palazzo della Sapienza near Piazza Navona, not the Citta Universitaria (1935). File pins 41.8982, 12.4747 (working 41.8985, 12.4728 was 160 m west).
- "In my college days, I was what you would call here a spendthrift." [A as quotation: Smithsonian] *Attribution corrected:* Smithsonian credits a New York Times interview, not the memoir.

## 2. Crossing and itinerancy, 1903-1907

- Sailed from Naples on the Dominion Line SS Vancouver, arriving Boston 15 Nov 1903, aged 21. [A: Wikipedia; Smithsonian]
- *Azores call upgraded.* GG Archives' Dominion Line holdings record the Vancouver's 1903 service as Naples-Azores-Boston. [A for the routing; R for Ponzi on deck at that call] Coordinate corrected to 37.7412, -25.6756 (working -25.6693 was 550 m offshore).
- "$2.50 in cash and $1 million in hopes." [A as quotation; R as accounting.] The sum and the shipboard cardsharp are self-report, corroborated by nothing.
- *Gap.* The Boston pier is undocumented. Long Wharf is used as the period's principal passenger landing and declared a placeholder; the stop is [R] on placement though date and ship are [A].
- Itinerant work 1903-1907: dishwasher, waiter, sign painter, labourer, interpreter, clerk. [R] No employer, address, wage, or date established. The Florida town of the sign-painting is unknown; a state centroid is used and declared.

## 3. Montreal, Atlanta, Blocton, 1907-1913

- 1907: Banco Zarossi, Saint-Jacques Street, Montreal, run by Luigi Zarossi, paying 6 percent out of incoming deposits. [A: Wikipedia; Duhaime] *Two corrections:* the alias is Charles Bianchi, no source supporting the initial "P"; and he entered as teller or clerk, the rise to manager being Zuckoff's reconstruction. [A alias; R promotion]
- 1908: bank failed, Zarossi fled to Mexico with funds [R: reconstruction, not court record]. Ponzi forged a check for $423.58 on the Canadian Warehousing Company over director Damien Fournier's signature and was confined at Saint-Vincent-de-Paul Penitentiary, inmate 6660 [A].
- *Date contradiction, decided against Wikipedia and flagged.* Wikipedia gives 1908-1911, incompatible with the NARA file dating the federal term 1910-1912 with a 1910 mug shot. File uses 1908-1910, saying why in `date_confidence`.
- *Coordinate corrections.* Saint-Vincent-de-Paul (Laval NHS) is 45.6178, -73.6459 (working 45.5813, -73.6890 was 4.5 km off, on Montreal island). USP Atlanta, 601 McDonough Blvd SE, is 33.71111, -84.36861 (working 33.7328, -84.3495 was 3.5 km off).
- 1910: federal conviction for smuggling five Italian migrants across the border; two years plus about a month for the unpaid fine. 1910-1912 at USP Atlanta; the case file holds the mug shot and measurements. [A: *Prologue*, 2010]
- c. 1912-13, Blocton, Bibb County, Alabama: donated a large area of his own skin for grafts after nurse Pearl Gossett was burned by an exploding gasoline stove. [R: only source is the autobiography, ch. VII] *Name corrected* from "Pearl Gosid." *Contradiction:* 50, 122, or ~200 sq in, by retelling.

## 4. Boston, the clerk, 1917-1919

- 1917: hired as clerk and stenographer by export broker J. R. Poole off a newspaper advertisement; his languages were the qualification. [A for the employer: Smithsonian] *Gap, and a deliberate pin choice:* Poole's office address is unattested; a working note put it on School Street, then conceded it could not be told apart from the Niles Building address. The file pins it separately at 42.3579, -71.0596, tags placement [R], and says the address is unknown.
- 4 Feb 1918: married Rose Maria Gnecco (1895-1993) in Somerville, Middlesex County. [A: WikiTree Gnecco-10; month via Smithsonian] *"Boston-born" dropped:* no source locates her birthplace. Venue unidentified; city-centre coordinates declared as placeholder.
- 1919: built the Trader's Guide, an advertising-funded international commercial directory, and was refused a $2,000 loan by Hanover Trust. [A for the refusal: Wikipedia, "Hanover Trust Company"] *Contradiction:* that source attaches the request to the reply-coupon scheme, while the Trader's-Guide framing is Zuckoff's; `date_confidence` carries the disagreement rather than picking one. *Gap:* no complete copy of the Guide located.
- August 1919: conceived the International Reply Coupon arbitrage after an IRC arrived from a Spanish correspondent. [R: self-report via Smithsonian]

## 5. The Securities Exchange Company, 1919-1920

- 26 Dec 1919: business certificate filed, sole manager, Securities Exchange Company, 27 School Street, the Niles Building (1915, Charles Howard Walker); the referee found a one-man sole proprietorship. Joseph Daniels's $200, that month, ruled a loan, not a partnership investment. [A: *In re Ponzi*; aknextphase.com]
- 12 Mar 1920: second certificate naming John S. Dondero and Guglielmo Bertollotti, ruled a fictitious partnership got up to forestall a Fidelity Trust attachment; Dondero an agent, Bertollotti probably already dead. [A that the court so ruled]
- Growth: ~$5,000 in February, $2.5m by June, ~$15m gross from 30,000-40,000 people over about eight months. [A] *Irreconcilable:* cash received, face value outstanding, and reinvested principal are different measures, not points on one curve.
- Hanover Trust: accounts in his own and fictitious names, one as "Lucy Martelli"; a $1,500,000 CD dated 22 July 1920, split into three of $500,000; overdraft $441,878.07 as of the morning of 10 August. [A: *Cunningham*] *Correction:* the account-opening date of 24 May 1920 is not in the opinion and could not be sourced; day-precision dropped, the stop dated to the 22 July certificate.
- *Gap, soft coordinate.* Wikipedia says only that the bank's offices were "located in Boston"; no street number is given anywhere. 42.3646, -71.0546 is an unconfirmed North End placeholder, declared at every stop using it.
- *Coordinate conflict, new to this pass.* 27 School Street is given as 42.3585, -71.0596 by one source and 42.3579, -71.0596 by another, 65 m apart. The file uses the former for the Niles Building and the latter for the unlocated Poole office (section 4), so no reader sees two identical pins implying an address that does not exist.
- 1920: bought 19 Slocum Road, Lexington from Richard Engstrom for $39,000, conditioned on Engstrom investing in the company; occupied about six weeks. [A: Boston Globe, 2023, on public records]

## 6. Collapse, July-November 1920

- 24 July: *Boston Post* admiring front-page feature; deposits rose. 26 July: the paper turned skeptical, having consulted Clarence Barron; a run followed and Ponzi paid out roughly $1-2 million over days. [A: Smithsonian] *Coordinate correction:* the *Post* was at 259-261 Washington Street on Newspaper Row, about 42.3573, -71.0578 (working 42.3554, -71.0598 was 250 m off).
- *Sourcing correction, important.* Mass Moments does not mention Grozier or Barron; that page was fetched and checked. Both are real (Grozier acting publisher; Barron's analysis ran 26 July) but must be cited elsewhere.
- *Context correction, load-bearing.* The Columbus-and-Marconi exchange circulates as a late-life interview with a reporter asking whether Ponzi thought himself great. It is neither: it is late July 1920 in the street outside his own office, the questioner an admirer who called him "the greatest Italian of them all." The file stages it at the bank run. *Contradiction:* the retort is "But you discovered money!" (NEHS) or "But Charlie, you discovered where the money is!" (Post via Smithsonian).
- 2 Aug: William McMasters, the publicity agent Ponzi had hired, gave the *Post* an exposé calling him a hopeless financial incompetent millions in deficit. 9 Aug: creditors filed an involuntary bankruptcy petition against Ponzi individually; the federal building is not identified in sources reached, so a downtown placeholder is used. [A: Smithsonian; *In re Ponzi*]
- 11 Aug: the *Post* printed the Montreal forgery and Banco Zarossi past, its inquiry including a wire to the Atlanta warden; Commissioner Joseph C. Allen took possession of Hanover Trust. The paper took the 1921 Pulitzer for Public Service. [A: Smithsonian; *Cunningham*]
- 12 Aug: surrendered, charged with mail fraud. Auditor Edwin L. Pride found him $3 million in the red, revised to $7 million. [A: Smithsonian] *Correction:* an earlier note said "higher once interest was included"; Smithsonian gives the number.
- 11 Sep: grand jury returned 22 indictments, 11 larceny, 10 accessory before the fact, 1 conspiracy. 30 Nov: pleaded guilty to the first count; five years at the House of Correction at Plymouth as a federal prisoner. [A: *Fessenden*, both verbatim] *Contradiction:* secondaries give 1 November; the Court's statement of facts is preferred. *Gap:* the 1920s Plymouth building cannot be distinguished from the modern Long Pond Road facility; downtown Plymouth used and declared.

## 7. Fessenden, the trials, Florida, 1922-1927

- 27 Mar 1922: *Ponzi v. Fessenden*, 258 U.S. 254, holding a federal prisoner may be produced for state trial with federal consent and returned. [A] Routinely mis-described as double jeopardy; it is comity and jurisdiction. *Pin note:* the Court still sat in the Capitol's Old Senate Chamber, its own building opening 1935; pinned accordingly and declared. 1924: released after about three and a half of the five federal years.
- State trials: first Oct 1924, acquitted on the first ten larceny counts; second hung; third Feb 1925, convicted, adjudged a "common and notorious thief," seven to nine years, affirmed 1926. [A: American Heritage, Feb 1973; *Commonwealth v. Ponzi*] *Date correction:* an earlier draft dated the third trial "1927," which is when he began serving, after affirmance. Two sums are often conflated: $14,000 Massachusetts appellate bail and $1,500 Florida bond.
- 28 Sep 1925: arrived in Jacksonville with Rose as Charles Borelli, Springfield, 1300 block of Main Street. 9 Nov: first Charpon Land Syndicate sale, with Rose and the Alviatis: $10 lots off about 100 acres in Columbia County bought at roughly $16 an acre, part swamp, tracts named Rosa Maria, Oreste, Imelde; 200 percent in 60 days promised, about $7,000 collected. [A: The Jaxson; R for the parcel, not pinpointed]
- 26 Apr 1926: convicted under Florida trust and securities law; one year hard labour, $1,500 appeal bond. [A] *Building correction:* the 1926 courthouse was the third Duval County Courthouse (1902, Rutledge Holmes), 330 East Bay Street, about 30.3247, -81.6531 (working 30.3211, -81.6557 was several hundred metres south).
- June 1926: staged a suicide with clothes and a note on a beach, went to Tampa, shaved his head, shipped as a waiter under the name Andrea Luciana aboard the Sic Vos Non Vobis, and was arrested at her New Orleans call before she cleared jurisdiction. He appealed to Coolidge and to Mussolini for deportation instead of prison; neither intervened. [A: The Jaxson; A that the appeals were made] The joke that the two "agreed on nothing except refusing Ponzi" is folklore, in no record reached.
- Feb 1927: began the state term at Charlestown State Prison. [A: American Heritage] *Gap and explicit compromise:* no coordinate for Charlestown is established in this pool, so rather than invent one the stop is pinned at the Suffolk County courthouse from which the sentence issued, with `date_confidence` saying the prison is unpinned.

## 8. Deportation, Rome, Rio, 1934-1949

- July 1934: Governor Joseph B. Ely denied pardon and relief from deportation. [A that it happened; R that it issued from the State House, which is inferred] 7 Oct 1934: deported as an undesirable alien. *Correction with a named artifact:* the departure is documented, not open. The Immigration Service put him aboard the SS Vulcania in Boston Harbor for one passage to Italy; UMBC Special Collections holds the press photograph, captioned "[Charles Ponzi is deported on board the S.S. Vulcania to Italy.]" [A]
- The memoir: *The Rise of Mr. Ponzi*, printed by Pacific Printing in 1937 under Ponzi's own imprint, the first 1,000 copies withheld against $235, released 1938 after a public appeal for a backer; the planned sequel *The Fall of Mr. Ponzi* was never written. [A: thefirstedition.com] *Bibliographic contradiction:* the cited 1936 is the cover date of the 2001 Inkwell reprint.
- 1939-1941: commercial representative in Brazil for LATI, off Ala Littoria assets, on the Rome-Seville-Ilha do Sal-Recife-Rio route, obtained through his reported second cousin Attilio Biseo, LATI director from 1939 and Mussolini's pilot. [A for Biseo and route; R for placement, no Rio address existing] Appointment by Mussolini himself is unverified; currency-smuggling claims are allegation, not conviction.
- Early 1948: stroke, partial paralysis. *Correction:* the eyesight detail circulates as "nearly blind"; Wikipedia gives blind in one eye, Smithsonian failing sight. The file says he loses the sight of one eye. 18 Jan 1949: died aged 66 in the charity ward, Hospital Sao Francisco de Assis, Rio, leaving about $75, latterly dependent on friends including the barber Francisco Nonato Nunes. [A]
- *Deathbed quotation, corrected.* To an AP reporter months before his death: "Those were confused, money-mad days. Everybody wanted to make a killing... My business was simple. It was the old game of robbing Peter to pay Paul." [A: TIME, Waxman, 2017, fetched] The bare fragment "The old game of robbing Peter to pay Paul," capitalized as a standalone aphorism, appears in no source. No source names the reporter or dates the dispatch. The "best show since the landing of the Pilgrims" line likewise survives only through transmission, worded differently in Smithsonian and NEHS. [R as to wording]
- *Burial, unresolved gap.* Unmarked grave in a paupers' cemetery in Rio. [R: NNDB, tertiary, no primary register located] Caju Cemetery pages and Find a Grave could not confirm cemetery or plot; the file names none.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Charles Ponzi is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Nothing has been silently deleted. Four relations were found that are not rooted in his own life, and all four are **forward**, which the exception never licenses. Recorded here; they appear nowhere in the journey file.

- *Forward.* `jeffrey_epstein.journey.json` uses "Ponzi scheme" for Steven Hoffenberg's Towers Financial Corporation, collapsed 1993: a lexical descendant of 1920, not a relation Ponzi had. He died in 1949.
- *Forward.* Madoff and Kenneth Lay are queued in `QUEUE.md` grouped with Ponzi, and Zuckoff's "What Madoff could learn from Ponzi" links them. `bernie_madoff.journey.json` may point back at Ponzi; Ponzi's file may not point forward, so it does not.
- *Forward.* The SEC and Investor.gov still define "Ponzi scheme" as the standard regulatory term. The file touches this only in the closing stop, cited as a reference and not staged as a scene. *URL correction:* cite Investor.gov; `sec.gov/answers/ponzi.htm` is retired.
- *Forward.* Rediscovery artifacts, all excluded: *White Rabbit Project* s1e4 (2016); *Murdoch Mysteries: Home for the Holidays* (2017, chronology fictionalized); Panda Edizioni's Italian edition, ISBN 978-88-93782-49-4 (16 July 2021; `pandaedizioni.it/2021/10/06/...` is HTTP 404, cite the ISBN); Apple's *Easy Money: The Charles Ponzi Story*, live at Tribeca 13 June 2025.

Three further interlock findings are co-location or typology rather than relation, excluded on the standing rule: Arthur Bispo do Rosario at Colonia Juliano Moreira in Rio across the same 1939-1949 decade; Lucky Luciano deported in 1946 on the same statutory footing; Marcus Garvey prosecuted 1922-1923 under the same mail-fraud statute. Cagliostro is typological only, and died in 1795.

**The one real relation, and it is in the file:** Ponzi appealed to Mussolini in 1926 for deportation instead of imprisonment, and from 1939 drew his living from the Fascist state airline network through Mussolini's own pilot. Attested, one-directional, outward from his own life. `mussolini` is named in the campa for the New Orleans arrest and again at the LATI desk.
