# Johann Jost Textor (1739-1792): research report

*Frankfurt am Main, 29 September 1739 - Frankfurt am Main, 19 September 1792. Jurist; Ratsherr 1771, Jüngerer Bürgermeister 1783, Schöffe of the Erste Bank 1788. Brother of Catharina Elisabeth Goethe, born Textor; maternal uncle of Johann Wolfgang von Goethe.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or tradition. An untagged claim here is a statement about the evidence, not about the world.

The governing difficulty is stated at the outset. Johann Jost Textor is documented as an officeholder and as a relative. He held four ranks in one building over twenty-one years, married once, fathered five sons, sat for one portrait, and is named exactly once in his nephew's autobiography, in a subordinate clause. There is no correspondence, no legal opinion, no published work, no travel outside two towns, no anecdote. The temptation is to borrow substance from the famous relatives; the report names the borrowing wherever it occurs.

---

## 1. The name and the generations before him

**[A: Frankfurter Personenlexikon, entry "Textor, Familie", node/1464]** The family originates at Weikersheim an der Tauber in Hohenlohe and is called **Weber**, weaver. The name is Latinized to **Textor** around 1620, on the family's entry into state service.

**[A: FPL node/1467]** **Wolfgang Textor (1588-1650)** is **Hofkanzler**, court chancellor, of Hohenlohe. *Correction: an earlier draft made him a court councillor; node/1467 gives chancellor.*

**[A: FPL node/1467]** **Johann Wolfgang Textor (1638-1701)**, born at Neuenstein on 20 January 1638, is the jurist of the family: professor of law at **Altdorf** from 1666, at **Heidelberg** 1673-1690, then **Syndicus primarius**, Konsulent and Stadtadvokat, of Frankfurt am Main from early 1691 **[A: FPL node/1464 and node/1467]**. He dies 27 December 1701 and is buried in the **Katharinenkirche**. He is the author of the *Synopsis Juris Gentium* (1680).

**Relationship correction, flagged because the research pool made the error and then caught it.** This Johann Wolfgang Textor (1638-1701) is **Goethe's great-great-grandfather** and **Johann Jost Textor's great-grandfather**, not Goethe's grandfather. The line runs: JWT 1638-1701 > **Christoph Heinrich Textor 1666-1716**, Palatinate court judge, married **Maria Catharina Appel 1665-1741** **[A: FPL node/1468]** > **Johann Wolfgang Textor 1693-1771**, the Stadtschultheiß **[A: FPL node/1468]** > **Johann Jost Textor 1739-1792**. Three men of this name are routinely conflated in secondary literature; the *Synopsis Juris Gentium* belongs to the first and to no other.

## 2. The father: Johann Wolfgang Textor (1693-1771)

**Contradiction, not resolved.** His birth date: **[A: FPL node/1468]** gives **12 December 1693**, a separate encyclopedia extract **11 December 1693**. Neither is preferred here; the journey file carries 12 December with the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

- **[A: FPL node/1468]** Law at **Altdorf**, 1712-1717; advocate at the **Reichskammergericht in Wetzlar** from 1721 (the year corroborated by a separate encyclopedia extract), doctorate afterwards at **Giessen**.
- **[A: FPL node/1468; exact date from a separate extract]** Marries **Anna Margaretha Lindheimer (1711-1783)** on **2 February 1726**. She is fifteen or sixteen at the wedding and outlives him by twelve years.
- **[A: Deutsche Biographie, GND 117414133; FPL node/1468]** Elected to the **Rat** 16 December 1727; **Schöffe** from 1731; **Älterer Bürgermeister** in three one-year terms, **1738, 1741, 1743**; **Reichs- und Stadtschultheiß**, the city's highest civic office and held for life, from **10 August 1747**, with the title *kaiserlicher wirklicher Rat*.
- **[A: same]** Stroke **August 1768**, leaving him partly paralysed; resigns the Schultheißenamt **June 1770** on grounds of disability; dies **6 February 1771** at Frankfurt am Main.

Of nine children, five reach adulthood; **[A: FPL node/1466; Deutsche Biographie summary]** Johann Jost is the only son among them.

## 3. Birth and childhood, 1739-1755

**[A: FPL node/1466]** **Johann Jost Textor is born 29 September 1739 at Frankfurt am Main.**

**[R]** *That he was born in the Textor house on the Friedberger Gasse.* The house is his father's documented residence; no source states the birth took place in it. Tagged [A] earlier, corrected here. **[A: de.wikipedia, "Johann Wolfgang Textor"]** It stood in the Neustadt, was **demolished in 1863**, and a hotel occupies the site; approximate coordinate 50.1146, 8.687.

**[R]** *That the house was castle-like with a fortified gate.* Not in the cited articles, withdrawn. What Goethe describes in *Dichtung und Wahrheit* is the **garden**, with peaches trained on the wall.

**Baptism: a gap. [R]** No register entry has been located. A Lutheran patrician child would be baptized within days, and the plausible church is the **Katharinenkirche**, *die evangelische Hauptkirche in Frankfurt am Main* per de.wikipedia (50.1133, 8.6797), whose pastor married his sister. *Correction flagged:* an earlier stage placed the presumed baptism at the **Kaiserdom St. Bartholomäus** (50.1106, 8.6845), which stayed **Catholic** after the Reformation and served as the coronation church. Not a candidate.

**Schooling: a gap. [R]** No matriculation record at the Frankfurt **Städtisches Gymnasium** has surfaced. His father was schooled there **[A: de.wikipedia, "Johann Wolfgang Textor"]** and his son enrolled in 1784 **[A: FPL node/1465]**; the boy between them is placed there by pattern only. *Coordinate correction:* the Gymnasium held the cloister of the **Barfüßerkloster** from 1542 to 1838, today's **Paulskirche**, about **50.1114, 8.6800** **[A: de.wikipedia, "Lessing-Gymnasium"]**. An earlier 50.1094, 8.681 was some 230 m off.

**Family events of his boyhood, both [A: FPL node/1468, with the day from de.wikipedia]:**

- **20 August 1748.** His sister **Catharina Elisabeth**, seventeen, marries **Johann Caspar Goethe**. The Katharinenkirche pastor performs it, at the garden house of the bride's uncle **Johann Michael von Loën**, "Auf der Windmühle", outside the gates. **Contradiction flagged:** sources differ on the venue. The year is firm; the day comes from de.wikipedia on both spouses.
- **28 August 1749.** **Johann Wolfgang von Goethe** is born in the house on the Grosser Hirschgraben. Johann Jost, one month short of ten, becomes his maternal uncle.

## 4. Altdorf, c.1756-1760

**[A: FPL node/1466; Deutsche Biographie summary; de.wikipedia "Johann Jost Textor"]** He takes the degree of **Juris Utriusque Doctor** at the **University of Altdorf** in **1760**.

**[R]** *The preceding matriculation.* Not documented anywhere consulted. A mid-1750s entry is plausible from customary ages of study; the journey file carries 1756 with the inference stated in `date_confidence`.

**Two corrections.** Altdorf bei Nürnberg is **49.3875, 11.3569** (de.wikipedia: 49° 23' N, 11° 21' E); an afterlife-stage draft carried 49.4009, 11.3536, about 1.5 km north in open country. And the Altdorf **Kollegien- und Hörsaalgebäude was not demolished**: it survives as the Wichernhaus **[A: de.wikipedia, "Altdorf bei Nürnberg"]**. **Maximilian I dissolved the university in 1809** and its 35,000 volumes went to Erlangen.

## 6. The council, 1771-1788

**[A: FPL node/1466 and node/1464; de.wikipedia]** Following his father's death in February 1771, he is **elected to the Frankfurt Rat in 1771**.

This is the one moment of his life that a literary source touches. **[A: Goethe, *Truth and Poetry: From My Own Life*, trans. John Oxenford, London: George Bell and Sons, 1897, Project Gutenberg ebook #52654, Part Three]**:

> After the death of my grandfather, my uncle Textor had come into the council, and consigned to me the little offices to which I was equal; while the brothers Schlosser did the same.

**Provenance, given in full because it is the only quote.** The Gutenberg text (about 1.68M characters) was searched programmatically for every occurrence of "Textor". This is the only passage in the autobiography referring to Johann Jost; two others refer to his father. Goethe gives no first name, and the identification rests on external biography (Deutsche Biographie GND 117296333; FPL/ADB via Alexander Dietz) placing his entry to the council after his father's death in 1771. **Flagged:** an earlier stage logged the anecdote as a gap after a projekt-gutenberg.org 404; a later stage recovered it in Oxenford. The German original has **not** been collated, so the quote above is a translation, not Goethe's words.

**[A: FPL node/1466 and node/1464; de.wikipedia]** **Jüngerer Bürgermeister**, junior mayor, **1783**; **Schöffe** and member of the **Erste Bank**, the senior judicial bench, **from 1788**, the office his father held from 1731.

He does not receive the Schultheißenamt. No source consulted comments on this or suggests it was expected of him.

**Deaths in the family during these years:**

- **25 May 1782**, **Johann Caspar Goethe** dies **[A: de.wikipedia, "Johann Caspar Goethe": "29. Juli 1710 in Frankfurt am Main; † 25. Mai 1782 ebenda"]**. *Correction flagged:* an earlier stage gave 30 May and placed the death at the Textor house; it is 25 May, at the Goethe house on the Grosser Hirschgraben.
- **1782**, Johann Caspar Goethe is interred at the **Peterskirchhof** **[A: de.wikipedia, "Peterskirche (Frankfurt am Main)"]**.
- **1783**, **Anna Margaretha Textor**, born Lindheimer, dies at seventy-two **[A: de.wikipedia, "Johann Wolfgang Textor"]**. Burial in the family plot is **[R]**, by pattern.

## 7. Death, 1792

**[A: FPL node/1466 and node/1464; de.wikipedia]** **Johann Jost Textor dies at Frankfurt am Main on 19 September 1792**, ten days short of his fifty-third birthday.

**Burial: a gap. [R]** No gravestone record, register entry, or burial notice for him has been located. Three attested things are the sole basis for placing him at the **Peterskirchhof**: it was the compulsory burial ground for the Protestant dead of the Altstadt and Neustadt from the Reformation until 1828 **[A: de.wikipedia, "Alter Peterskirchhof"]**; the Textors held a family plot there **[A: de.wikipedia, "Catharina Elisabeth Goethe"]**; and his parents and sister lie in it or are presumed to. The atlas records the placement as inference.

**Coordinate correction.** Peterskirchhof: **50.1174, 8.6838** (de.wikipedia gives 50° 7' 2.8" N, 8° 41' 1.6" E). An afterlife-stage draft carried **50.1146, 8.6841** on six consecutive items, about 310 m south; all six corrected.

## 8. Afterlife

**No monument, no legend cycle, no publications.** **[R: gap across the Deutsche Biographie and Wikipedia entries consulted]** He is documented strictly as jurist and officeholder, nothing attributed to him in print. The **portrait pair does exist** (section 5) and is the single item of iconography.

**The family after him, all [A: FPL node/1465 unless noted]:**

- **1784** Friedrich Karl Ludwig enters the Frankfurt Gymnasium, attending irregularly; **1794** writes *Der Prorector, Ein Lustspiel in vier Aufzügen*, the earliest surviving comedy in the Frankfurt dialect, reprinted 1839 and 1893.
- **1797-1800** at **Tübingen**: doctorate and habilitation 1800, then Privatdozent and extraordinary professor. **1813** back in Frankfurt with a practice; **1816** closes it for want of income and lives by tutoring, one pupil being the dialect poet **Friedrich Stoltze**. **31 December 1851** dies in Frankfurt, his last years spent in a charitable hospital.
- **13 September 1808** **[A: de.wikipedia, "Catharina Elisabeth Goethe"]** Goethe's mother dies and is buried at the Peterskirchhof *im Familiengrab der Textors*.
- **1925** **[A: FPL node/1464]** the Frankfurt line ends with **Karl Wolfgang Textor (1840-1925)**. A **Textorstraße** and a **Textorschule** in Sachsenhausen commemorate the family collectively, no individual.

**The ground itself, all [A: de.wikipedia, "Alter Peterskirchhof"]:** founded 1508; last burial Elisabeth Mauer, 30 June 1828; about two thirds of the area lost to nineteenth-century Prussian street and building projects and to the 1974 Diamond Exchange; Hans Backoffen's Crucifixion group of 1511 damaged in the wartime bombing; the surviving stones conserved from 1999 as an open-air museum; since 1994 a section of the retaining wall is Frankfurt's AIDS Memorial.

## 10. Contradictions carried forward unresolved

1. **Father's birth date:** 12 December 1693 (FPL node/1468) against 11 December 1693 (separate encyclopedia extract).
2. **Sister's wedding venue, 1748:** the Katharinenkirche pastor officiated, but sources differ on whether the ceremony was in the church or at von Loën's garden house outside the gates. The day, 20 August 1748, is not in dispute; the house's location is nowhere given, and 50.1195, 8.695 is approximate.
3. **The identity behind "my uncle Textor"** in Goethe: secure by circumstance, not by naming, and the German original has not been collated against the Oxenford translation used here.
4. **The census "great-uncle"** against the documented **uncle** relationship.
