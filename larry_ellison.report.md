# Larry Ellison, research report
*Lawrence Joseph Ellison, b. Bronx, New York, 17 August 1944. Living subject; the file closes at the research cutoff of August 2026.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, single-source allegation, or a claim resting only on the subject's own retelling. Contradictions are printed side by side and **not adjudicated**.

One caution governs the whole file. Much of the Ellison canon is autobiography: the CIA as first customer, the drive west with the guitar, the storm at sea. He has told these from stages for decades. That does not make them false; it makes them single-sourced to the man they flatter, and where the record is silent this report says so instead of borrowing his confidence.

---

## 1. Origins, 1944-1966

- **Birth. [A: Britannica Money; EBSCO Research Starters; Notable Biographies]** 17 August 1944, the Bronx, to Florence Spellman, nineteen and unmarried. The father is nowhere identified beyond a first name. *Gap:* no hospital, street, or attending physician in any source; the coordinate marks the borough and `date_confidence` says so.
- **Adoption. [A: Britannica; Notable Biographies]** At about nine months, after a pneumonia, the child was given to Florence's aunt and uncle, Lillian Spellman Ellison and Louis Ellison, who adopted him and raised him in South Shore, Chicago. **[R]** for the exact month: the "ninth month" is a biographers' formula with no document behind it. He learned he was adopted at about twelve **[A: Britannica]**.
- **Schooling. [A: Notable Biographies; Academy of Achievement]** South Shore High School. Illinois at Urbana-Champaign from 1962, named science student of the year, left in his second year without sitting finals after Lillian's death. One term at the University of Chicago in 1966, physics and mathematics, first contact with computer design and programming, no degree.
- *Not verified:* the repeated claim of a pre-medical track at Illinois. It comes from the subject and reports the family's ambition; no registrar record was reachable, and the file carries it as the family's understanding, not a declared major.

## 2. The Bay Area and Ampex, 1966-1976

- **Move west and contract work. [A: Britannica]** Permanent move to the Bay Area in 1966; contract programming for Fireman's Fund, Wells Fargo, Amdahl and Ampex. **[A: USC commencement address, 13 May 2016]** for his own account of the drive from Chicago. **[A: Biography.com]** Adda Quinn, married 1967, met through an employment agency; divorced 1974.
- **Project ORACLE. [A: CIA Reading Room (FOIA), CIA-RDP83T00573R000500080009-3 and ...040011-4]** Ampex ran a CIA-sponsored mass-storage effort of that code name for the Agency's Office of Joint Computer Support; a 1975 acceptance report records serious failures transferring data between disk and tape memory.
- **[R] Ellison's personal involvement. The released pages do not name him.** His presence at Ampex rests on company histories and his own accounts, not on the CIA documents; the acceptance-report stop says so in `date_confidence`. **[A: Business Insider, "The CIA Made Larry Ellison A Billionaire"]** that Miner and Oates were fellow Ampex employees.
- **[R], flagged as legend:** that the CIA created, financed, or has ever owned Oracle. No filing, no declassified record, no equity document; the primary documents predate Oracle's existence.

## 3. Founding and Version 2, 1977-1986

- **Incorporation. [A: Academy of Achievement; History of Information; Computer History Museum]** Software Development Laboratories, 16 June 1977, Santa Clara, about $2,000 in capital, roughly $1,200 of it Ellison's. *Address correction applied:* the pool placed the founding at "20 Davis Drive, Belmont," which is Oracle's mid-1980s headquarters; the company left Belmont for Redwood Shores in 1989 and no source gives Davis Drive as a 1977 premises. The file founds the company at Santa Clara (37.3541, -121.9552) and gives Belmont its own 1980s stop.
- **The first customer. [R]** "Our very first customer was the Central Intelligence Agency" is Ellison's own line, most quoted from the OpenWorld 2014 keynote. **No contract, price, or signing date has ever been published.** As printed by Business Insider (Bort, 29 September 2014): "The very first version was Oracle version 2. We knew no one would buy version 1. Low and behold the CIA was our first customer." The pool silently corrected "Low and behold" to "Lo and behold" and "would buy" to "would want to buy." The file quotes it as printed; correcting a source inside quotation marks is a fabrication.
- **Version 2. [A: Britannica; Computer History Museum]** Released 1979 as Relational Software, Inc. There was no commercial Version 1; the museum states plainly that "V2" was a marketing device.
- **SCOTT / TIGER. [A: orafaq.com/wiki/SCOTT; oralytics.com]** The demonstration credential commemorates Bruce Scott, first employee, and his daughter's cat. Two independent community sources agree; no corporate document was reachable, so this sits at the weak end of [A].
- **Prior art, which is not his.** [R] as popularly told, [A] as to the history **[ACM Digital Library; ibm.com/history/relational-database]**: Ellison invented neither the relational database nor SQL. Codd published "A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks" in *CACM*, June 1970; IBM's System R team built SQL from 1974 at 5600 Cottle Road, San Jose (~37.238, -121.793). The pool's 37.2751, -121.9006 is about 10 km off and is unused.
- **Family. [A: Biography.com; CNN Fast Facts]** Nancy Wheeler Jenkins, married 1977, gave up her interest for $500 in the divorce. Barbara Boothe, an early Oracle employee, married 1983; son David born 1983, daughter Megan 1986.
- **IPO. [A: Begin To Invest]** NASDAQ, 12 March 1986, $15 a share, one day before Microsoft's IPO. First-day close contested (3 below); the file says "near twenty dollars."

## 4. Redwood Shores: the restatement and the injuries, 1989-1998

- **Headquarters. [A: Fortune, December 2020, retrospective]** Relocation to Redwood Shores in 1989; his base for three decades.
- **The 1990 crisis. [A: In re Oracle Securities Litigation, 829 F. Supp. 1176 (N.D. Cal. 1993); financial press]** FY1990, ending 31 May 1990, was first reported at $970.8M revenue and $117.4M net. An internal audit led Oracle to restate **three of the four fiscal 1990 quarters**, cutting revenue to about $916M and earnings to about $80M, after switching from booking revenue at contract signature to booking on delivery. The first quarterly loss in company history followed within weeks, about $36M. The stock fell from about $28 to $6.25. Litigation against Oracle and auditor Arthur Andersen followed. (Correction 8 below.)
- **Oracle7. [A: Oracle product history; industry press]** Released 1992, stored procedures and triggers, the product that restored the company. Folded into the Miner stop.
- **The surfing accident. [R], contradiction not resolved (1 below).** Broken 16th vertebra, punctured lung, board in three pieces. *Gap:* the beach is unnamed everywhere; Forbes gives only "the beach was closed, there were red flags out, 12- to 15-foot waves," so the North Shore coordinate is a flagged guess. **[A: Academy of Achievement; UC Davis Health]** for the bicycle crash during recovery, the shattered left elbow, four operations under Murray Chapman, and the named research center and 1998 ambulatory care building.
- **Bob Miner. [A: Wikipedia "Bob Miner"; Find a Grave 125595824; orafaq]** Died 11 November 1994, aged 52, in **San Francisco** (correction 5 below), of pleural mesothelioma diagnosed 1993, having stepped back from daily leadership in 1992.

## 5. Rivalry, sailing, Apple, 1997-2003

- **Apple board. [A: Apple Newsroom, 20 September 2002; technology press, 1997]** Joined 1997 after Jobs's return; resigned September 2002 citing his schedule. **[R]** that he publicly contemplated financing a takeover to restore Jobs and dropped it on Jobs's advice: widely reported, no primary document.
- **Sydney-Hobart 1998. [A: France 24; The Register, 30 October 2000; Wired, December 1998]** Sayonara started 26 December; a hurricane-force depression struck the fleet on the 27th; six died, 55 were helicoptered off, five yachts abandoned. *Correction applied:* line honours at **08:06 on 29 December 1998**, elapsed 2d 19h 6m, not 28 December as the pool had it. The Constitution Dock pin also moved about 750 m north.
- **Microsoft opposition research. [A: Time, "Peeping Larry," 2000; NYT]** Oracle admitted hiring Investigative Group International; investigators sought discarded documents from Microsoft-allied advocacy groups. Ellison called the tactics unsavory and defended the object.
- **Marriage to Melanie Craft. [A: society and business press]** 18 December 2003 at Woodside, officiated by Rep. Tom Lantos, photographed by Steve Jobs.

## 6. Consolidation and the security state, 2001-2014

- **National ID proposal. [A: national press, September and October 2001]** Consolidated federal database, national digital identity card, Oracle software offered free to the government.
- **Insider-trading litigation. [A: Delaware Chancery record as reported 2001-2005]** Plaintiffs alleged Ellison sold nearly $900M of stock in January 2001 ahead of a weak quarter; settled 2005 with a $100M charitable donation in Oracle's name and about $22M in fees, no admission of wrongdoing. *No stop*, for length.
- **PeopleSoft. [A: financial press; United States v. Oracle Corp., N.D. Cal. 2004]** About $10.3bn, December 2004, after an eighteen-month hostile campaign; the DOJ antitrust suit failed at trial.
- **Harvard. [A: The Harvard Crimson, 30 June 2006]** $115M pledged for a world-health institute, withdrawn unpaid after Lawrence Summers resigned.
- **Sun Microsystems. [A: Oracle, "Oracle Buys Sun"]** $7.4bn, closed 27 January 2010. **[A: *Google LLC v. Oracle America* (2021)]** for the Java litigation and Oracle's loss on fair use.
- **America's Cup. [A: America's Cup official history]** Won at Valencia, February 2010; defended on San Francisco Bay September 2013, 9-8, after a two-point jury penalty for illegal modifications to smaller test boats, which Ellison was not found to have ordered.
- **NSA remarks. [A: CBS News, 13 August 2013]** Bulk collection called essential, citing the Boston Marathon bombing; political targeting named as the one condition that would make it objectionable.
- **Lana'i. [A: Honolulu Civil Beat, June 2012; NPR]** About 98 percent of the island from Castle & Cooke, reported near $300M, held through Pulama Lana'i.
- **CEO transition. [A: Oracle, 18 September 2014]** Stepped down after 37 years; Catz and Hurd co-CEOs; Ellison executive chairman and CTO.

## 7. Israel, 2007-2021

- **Sderot. [A: Haaretz, 10 August 2007]** Visited with Tzipi Livni and Tom Lantos; pledged $500,000 to fortify an unprotected community center.
- **FIDF giving. [A: Business Wire, November 2014; JTA, 5 November 2017]** $9M at the 2014 Western Region gala; **$16.6M** in 2017, then the largest single gift in FIDF history, funding well-being facilities on a training campus for co-ed infantry units, at a gala raising $53.8M. *Date correction:* the 2017 gala was Thursday **2 November**; JTA published on 5 November, and both the pool's chronology and quote entries took the publication date for the event.
- **The quote. [A: The Jewish Chronicle; fuller text in JTA]** "For 2,000 years, we were a stateless people, but now, we have a country we can call our own..." Verified verbatim, British "honour" as printed. Companion statements from the same piece, that he does not subscribe to the dogmas of Judaism and takes the scriptures for "interesting mythology," are used in the file as prose, not as a second quote.
- **Netanyahu witness list. [A: Times of Israel, December 2019]** Among 333 potential prosecution witnesses, on Case 2000. Being listed made him neither suspect nor defendant, and he was never called. *Coordinate correction applied:* the court is at 40 Salah ad-Din Street (~31.7877, 35.2301); the pool's 31.7768, 35.2007 lands about 3 km west-southwest.
- **Board seat offer. [R: Haaretz, 16 September 2021]** A reported Oracle board seat worth about $450,000 a year plus stock. **Netanyahu denied receiving any such offer** and never joined. One report, one on-record denial, no Oracle confirmation. Printed with the denial attached.
- **Lana'i holiday. [A: Times of Israel, August 2021]** The Netanyahu family vacationed on the island and stated it paid its own expenses. No public evidence connects the trip to the witness list.
- **Project Nimbus. [A: Haaretz, 21 April 2021; DCD; Calcalist/CTech]** Israel awarded Nimbus to Google and AWS in April 2021 (~$1.2bn); Oracle's petition to the Jerusalem District Court was dismissed, Oracle having been disqualified partly on information-security grounds. Oracle does not run Nimbus, a claim that circulates widely. (Pool contradiction 6 below.)
- **Jerusalem cloud region. [A: Times of Israel; The Register, 14 October 2021; DCD]** Inaugurated **13 October 2021**, not 11 October as the pool's geography entry had it. 14,000 m² over four floors, 50 m below a Bynet server farm at Har Hotzvim, hardened against missile strike, the first hyperscale cloud region inside Israel.

## 8. The late institutions, 2016-2026

- **USC. [A: USC News]** Commencement address and honorary degree, 13 May 2016; about $200M in the same period for a cancer institute under David Agus.
- **Cerner. [A: Oracle newsroom]** $28.3bn, closed 8 June 2022, Oracle's largest acquisition. *Coordinate correction applied:* the headquarters is 2800 Rock Creek Parkway, **North** Kansas City (~39.1545, -94.546); the pool's 39.1032, -94.6003 sits about 7 km southwest.
- **Oracle and Palantir. [A: Oracle / PR Newswire, 4 April 2024]** Foundry and AI Platform on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, government, dedicated and air-gapped regions included. Carried as a clause in the 2024 analyst-meeting stop.
- **Surveillance remarks. [A: Business Insider, September 2024; TechRadar Pro]** Body cameras, vehicle cameras and doorbells continuously analyzed; drones instead of pursuit vehicles; "Citizens will be on their best behavior."
- **Oxford. [A: Oxford news, 3 December 2024; Times Higher Education, 2025]** Partnership with the Ellison Institute of Technology, over £1bn; campus at the Oxford Science Park, Littlemore, a further £890M pledged in 2025, opening targeted for 2027, up to 7,000 researchers. Incomplete at cutoff.
- **Stargate. [A: CNN Business; AP News, 21 January 2025]** White House announcement with Trump, Altman and Son; up to $500bn over four years, an ambition, not committed capital.
- **NetSuite litigation. [A: Delaware Supreme Court No. 139, 2024, affirmed 21 January 2025]** Judgment for Ellison and Catz; the court found influence but not control, and no material misleading of the special committee. *No stop*, for length.
- **Paramount. [A: Los Angeles Times, 24 July 2025]** Skydance-Paramount closed August 2025, financed largely from Ellison family capital and trusts; David Ellison holds voting and operational control.
- **PCAST. [A: White House release, 25 March 2026]** Appointed alongside Safra Catz, Mark Zuckerberg, Sergey Brin, Jensen Huang and Lisa Su.
- **Pentagon. [A: Reuters, 23 July 2026]** DoD enterprise software agreement potentially worth nearly $7bn over ten years, covering the department, Coast Guard and intelligence community; Oracle already on the Joint Warfighting Cloud Capability vehicle, ceiling $9bn through 2028. Ceilings are not guaranteed expenditure.

## Attribution problem, flagged and acted on

The pool carried Safra Catz's Calcalist remark, "For employees, it's clear: if you're not for America or Israel, don't work here, this is a free country" (November 2024, reproduced in The Intercept, 18 February 2025), framed as "shared company leadership policy with Ellison." **No source states that.** It is Catz on her own company's policy, and The Intercept attributes nothing comparable to Ellison. The quote is **excluded from the journey file** rather than attached to Ellison by proximity.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Ellison is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing here required the exception: he has no theoretical framework to apply backward, and no stop reads a later thinker's apparatus onto earlier events. **Neither a backward nor a forward apparatus relation was found or dropped.**

What *was* dropped, so the deletion is not silent: the pool's interlock lens surfaced **Robert Maxwell, Paul Singer, Adi Shamir, Jeffrey Epstein, Ghislaine Maxwell, William Colby and Allen Dulles**, marking every one as shared geography, shared era, or structural parallel with no documented contact. The strongest of them is the weakest kind of link: Colby was DCI when the 1975 Ampex acceptance report was written, and no document places Ellison at Langley at all. Shared geography being no reason to name-drop, **none of the seven is named in any campa**. No stop stages another person's later visit as a scene inside Ellison's journey.

## Honest gaps

- **No birth record, no adoption document.** Hospital, address, attending physician unlocated; the nine-month transfer is a biographers' formula.
- **No CIA contract.** The most-repeated fact about Oracle's founding rests on the founder's word alone. Fifty years on, no purchase order, price, or date has surfaced; the declassified ORACLE files establish an Ampex contract and nothing about Ellison.
- **The beach is unnamed**, and no OpenWorld 2014 keynote video or transcript was reachable.
- **Addresses unconfirmed:** the South Shore apartment, the Woodside parcel, the Rancho Mirage estate, the 1977 SDL office.
- **Living subject.** Net worth, residence, and the Oxford campus are all moving at cutoff and are stated only where a dated source supports them.
