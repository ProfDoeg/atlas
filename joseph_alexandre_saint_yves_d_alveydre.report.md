# Joseph Alexandre Saint-Yves d'Alveydre (1842-1909): research report

*Slug: `joseph_alexandre_saint_yves_d_alveydre`. 2026-09-01, from the verified research
pool (gather + adversarial verification), for the journey file of the same slug.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or
single-source dossier claim. Mixed sentences in the pool are split, not averaged;
contradictions are flagged and left standing.

The subject coined **synarchy** and **Agarttha**: a ministry clerk and would-be
constitutional architect who wrote a subterranean kingdom into existence in 1886 and
destroyed the book inside a fortnight.

## 2. Mettray (adolescence, undated)

- Detention for a period at the **agricultural penal colony for young offenders near
  Tours**, whose founder made a lasting impression on him. **[A: fr.wikipedia attests it
  directly]**; the gather note claiming "neither Wikipedia biography names his stay" was
  **wrong**. Demetz's credit for redirecting his life stays **[R]**. **Gap:** dates, age,

## 3. Brest and the smallpox (early 1860s)

- Pushed toward the service by his father, enters the **naval medical school at Brest**
  **[A: both Wikipedias]**. Volunteers to replace an intern struck by **variole noire**,
  contracts it, nearly dies; the training ends **[A: fr.wikipedia in its own voice]**. The
  gather lens tagged this [R] and called it absent from the Wikipedia biographies;
  **incorrect**. **No medical diploma documented**, stated as a gap.

## 4. Jersey (1863-1870)

- Relocation to **Jersey in 1863**, teaching science for his living. **[A: fr.wikipedia, which also gives the 1870 departure]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: Victor Hugo.** Both Wikipedias say he "connected
  with Victor Hugo" on Jersey. `victor_hugo.journey.json` has Hugo at **Marine Terrace
  from 1852-08-16** to **expulsion 1855-10-31**, then Guernsey. Saint-Yves arrives eight
  years into that absence; no meeting is possible as the dates stand, and the file states
  the contradiction in the campa rather than smoothing it.
- **Antoine Fabre d'Olivet (1767-1825)**: access to his legacy and manuscripts through
  the Jersey acquaintance **Virginie Faure** is **[R: dossier synthesis of Cellier and
  Strube, in neither Wikipedia biography]**. That his system underlies the *Missions* is
  the scholarship's settled judgement, load-bearing for §7.

## 5. War and the ministry (1870-1878)

- Returns 1870, enlists in the **marine infantry**, **wounded during reconnaissance**
  **[A: both Wikipedias]**. **Dropped as unsupported:** the "**171st marching battalion**"
  and the wound "before a fort," in no reachable source, and French *bataillons de marche*
  of 1870-71 were not numbered that high.
- **Commis at the Ministry of the Interior**, 1871 to **1878** **[A: both Wikipedias]**;
  Place Beauvau is the historic seat, his own office unidentified **[R]**.

## 6. The marriage, the money, the title (1877-1880)

- **6 September 1877**, marriage to **Marie Victoire de Riznitch Rzewuska (1827-1895)**,
  Countess Keller **[A: fr.wikipedia, date and bride]**. **Venue demoted to [R]:**
  "Westminster, London" is dossier-only; fr.wikipedia gives no venue. Birth at Odessa
  likewise **[R]**. The file keeps the pin and calls the venue a reconstruction.
- **She is not a widow.** First husband **Count Eduard Fyodorovich Keller (1819-1903)**,
  senator, outlived them both **[A: ru.wikipedia]**; that marriage ended by divorce or
  annulment.
- **Balzac.** en.wikipedia calls her "a relative of Honoré de Balzac," fr.wikipedia a
  relative of Madame Hanska **[A at that level]**. The mechanism (her mother Polina
  Adamovna Rzewuska a sister of Ewelina Hańska) is **[R: dossier only]**; the file names
  Balzac only as far as the sources go.
- The marriage ends salaried work; from 1878 he lives on her fortune **[A]**.
- **1878** *Mystère du progrès*, "tragédie historique en 5 actes, avec choeurs et
  ballets," Didier **[A: BnF, upgraded; the phrase is the catalogue's]**. **1879** *De
  l'utilité des algues marines*, the venture failing for want of capital **[A]**.
- **1880**, **San Marino** confers (or sells) **Marquis d'Alveydre** **[A: both
  Wikipedias]**. **Contradictions flagged:** grant vs. sale-with-property, and a rival
  Vatican-conferral claim conflicting with the better-sourced account. **No decree

## 8. Agarttha (1885-1886)

- **1885**, Sanskrit instruction from **Haji Sharif** / **Hardjji Scharipf**, origins
  unknown, who introduced him to Agartha **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Agartha']**.
- **Demoted from [A] to [R]:** the date **8 June 1885**, the address **108 rue de
  Cormeille, Levallois-Perret**, and the lesson heading naming "guru Pandit H. S.
  Bagwandass of the Great Agartthian School." All rest on the dossier's report of
  **Godwin** on **Sorbonne MS 1824**, which was unreachable (see Sources).
- **Source contradiction flagged.** en.wikipedia's *biography* article frames 1885 not as
  paid lessons but as a visit from "a group of Eastern Initiates, one of them being named
  Prince Hardjij Scharipf." The file carries both.
- **Vattanian**, the language Sharif presented as Agartha's tongue, was **Sharif's own
  invention** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, and still becomes the base of the *Archéomètre*.
- **1886**, private printing of *Mission de l'Inde en Europe… la question du Mahatma et
  sa solution*, recalled and destroyed within about two weeks, one copy kept by the author,
  one secretly retained by the printer **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Motive: contradiction flagged.** en.wikipedia carries two strands generically (he said
  too much; Indian informants threatened him). The attribution to **Jules Bois** and the
  "threatened litigation over unauthorized publication" could not be located and are
  credited to the dossier by name. Calling the assassination legends "unverified
  embellishments" is an assessment, not a finding.

**Quotations.** Eight stops quote verbatim: seven from the Inner Traditions translation
(trans. Graham, 2008), read in the archive.org full text with line numbers; one French.
**French OCR caveat:** `missiondelindeeneurope` `_djvu.txt` at offset 36367 reads `Ce nom,
l'Agarttha, grygasignilie insaisissable 7 mystique, / à la violence, inaccessible à
l'anarchie.` Two artifacts: `grygasignilie` = hierogram + *signifie*, and `mystique` is a
marginal-gloss bleed-in falling *after* `insaisissable`, inside the researcher's unmarked
span rather than the ellipsis. The file quotes only the clean fragment. The
1848-enthronement line was seen only in grep output: **[R]**, unused.

## 9. The Syndicat and the last public books (1886-1893)

- **The Syndicat existed and he addressed it.** BnF holds *Les États-généraux du suffrage
  universel: discours prononcé au banquet du Syndicat de la presse économique*, **1888**,
  "au siège du Syndicat," Paris **[A: upgraded]**. Still **[R]:** the 1886 founding year,
  his founder role, the fuller name "et professionnelle de France," and the *Conseil de
  l'économie nationale* campaign with **Baron Théodore de Cambourg** addressed to **Grévy**
  and **Carnot**.
- **1889** *Le Poème de la Reine* (Lahure) and **1892** *The poem of the Queen Victoria*,
  trans. the **Earl of Lytton** (Lahure) **[A: BnF holds both, upgraded]**. The English
  version was printed **in Paris by the same house**, and **Lytton died November 1891**,
  so the 1892 issue is posthumous for its translator. The twenty-two Hebrew-alphabet
  stanzas remain **[R]**.
- **1889**, donation of **Cabanel's 1873 *Portrait de la comtesse de Keller*** with life
  usufruct. Painting, sitter and present home at the Musée d'Orsay **[A: en.wikipedia]**;
  donation and usufruct **[R]**, the Orsay page 403. **No stop**: a `suggested_ref` on the
  marriage stop.
- **1890** *Jeanne d'Arc victorieuse, épopée nationale, dédiée à l'armée française*,
  Sauvaitre **[A: BnF, dedication in the catalogue's words]**. The preface's announced end
  of synarchic campaigning is **[R]**: the preface could not be read.
- **1893**, **Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Reading it as
  official encouragement is interpretation; Base Léonore could not be consulted.
- **1893**, move from **27 rue Vernet** to **9 rue Colbert, Versailles** **[R: dossier
  only]**. Street coordinates verified (48.8722, 2.2975; 48.8048, 2.1256), house numbers
  not. **Correction:** the street is **rue Vernet**, so named since 1864 for the Vernet
  painters collectively; never "rue Horace-Vernet."

## 10. Widowhood, the Archéomètre, death (1895-1909)

- **1895**, death of Marie Victoire at 67 or 68 **[A for the year, fr.wikipedia's
  1827-1895; the arithmetic checks]**. **Death at Versailles, in the rue Colbert house, at
  about one in the morning: [R]**, fr.wikipedia gives no place of death for her. His
  withdrawal from politics afterward is **[R]**.
- **Correction to the pool's account of the late years.** The gather lens said he produced
  "diagrams, musical scores, and patent applications rather than a finished book." Wrong:
  he printed finished works catalogued at the BnF, the **Delanchy sheets 1900-1902**
  (*Salutation angélique* in Latin and Slavonic, *Kyrie* in Greek, *Pater noster*,
  *Amrita*, *L'Étoile des Mages*, *Isola Bella*), *Diatonie archéométrique* (1907) and
  *L'Archéomètre musical* (1909). What he did **not** finish was the compiled
  *Archéomètre*.
- **1901** *Notes sur la tradition cabalistique*, édition de l'Initiation **[A: BnF]**,
  carried into **Papus**'s *La Cabbale*, Chacornac, **1903**, beside a letter of **Adolphe
  Franck** **[A: BnF contents list]**. **The carrying volume is 1903, not 1901.**
- Archeometric patents from **1903**: **[R]**, no numbers or dispositions recovered; that
  no instrument was made and no patent earned money is a **negative finding**.
- **5 February 1909**, dies at **Pau** **[A: both Wikipedias]**; cause **not documented**.
  **Correction:** Pau is **not** thermal and has no springs, it was a **climatic** winter
  resort, the thermal stations being Eaux-Bonnes and Eaux-Chaudes. The cure motive is
  unattested.
- **February 1909**, buried at the **Cimetière Notre-Dame, Versailles**, in the shared
  tomb **[A: fr.wikipedia, which prints a photograph of the grave]**. Division, plot and
  inscription unconfirmed (403s).

## 11. Posthumous (1910-)

- **1910**, *Mission de l'Inde en Europe* published publicly for the first time; the copy
  that made it possible reached **Papus** by way of **Count Keller** **[A: en.wikipedia;
  fr.wikipedia for the Keller route]**.
- ***L'Archéomètre***, compiled by **Papus** "et quelques amis et collaborateurs,"
  Dorbon-Aîné. **Year contested, flagged, not resolved:** fr.wikipedia 1910; the
  archive.org item (`archeometre`) is catalogued 1911 while its own note says the book
  appeared "l'année suivante" after his 1909 death; BnF holds a Dorbon aîné printing with
  **no date at all**. The pool's "dated 1911 on its preface but issued in 1913" has **no
  support** and is dropped.
- **1939**, **Sorbonne MS 1823/5**, *Cahier de notes I: notes sur l'existence historique
  de Jésus*, 303 leaves, enters the Sorbonne library as a gift from Papus, dead since
  **1916**; intermediate custody **unestablished**. Digitized on NuBIS under an open
  licence **[R: single archival record]**.
- **2008**, first English translation, Inner Traditions, Rochester, Vermont **[A]**; the
  hollow-earth subtitle is a 2008 editorial addition, not his title.
- **Digitisation, narrowed.** archive.org has the 1882, 1884, 1909, 1910 and 1911
  printings above, Gallica has Barlet's 1910 study; *La France vraie* and the Delanchy
  sheets are **not** confirmed online. "Much of," not "the full run."

## Interlocks: who is named, and who is not

**Named in a campa**, Saint-Yves standing in a documented relation to them:

- **`victor_hugo`**, twice: for the Jersey claim *as a contradiction*, the encyclopedias
  asserting a connection the dates forbid; and for *Les Funérailles de Victor Hugo* (1885),
  which he wrote and signed "par l'auteur des Missions." The second is a real
  one-directional engagement and carries the relation.
- **`honore_de_balzac`**, through the marriage; his wife is a relative of Balzac (or of
  Madame Hanska) out of the Rzewuski connection. Family, not co-location.

**Deliberately not named**, the only tie being shared theme or shared map: `kircher`,
`symmes`, `jules_verne`, `edgar_allan_poe`, `william_r_bradshaw`, `john_uri_lloyd`,
`arthur_edward_waite`. No contact, citation or influence in either direction for any of
them, only a hollow-earth lineage the map already shows.

**`blavatsky`, an unpursued lead, recorded as a gap.** The 1886 subtitle is *la question
du Mahatma et sa solution*; "Mahatma" in 1886 was a Theosophical word in a Theosophical
quarrel, and Saint-Yves also uses it as an office of Agarttha's triangle. Nothing in the
pool settles whether the subtitle answers Blavatsky, and it is asserted nowhere.

## Honest gaps

1. **He never went to Asia.** No evidence of travel to India, Tibet or Central Asia, where
   he set Agarttha; his documented geography is Paris, Tours, Brest, Jersey, London and San
   Marino (both unverified as visits), Versailles, Pau. The Mecca and Temple Mount stops
   are marked in their confidence fields as thematic pins for books he wrote, explicitly
   not journeys, on the precedent of `rene_guenon`'s qibla stop.
2. **Sorbonne MS 1824 never seen**; the Sanskrit-lesson detail rests on Godwin at one
   remove through the dossier.
3. **No San Marino decree**, the most-repeated fact about him after Agarttha.
4. **Never read or opened:** the Meunier review, Base Léonore, the Cabanel provenance, the
   grave to a plot. **Cause of death unknown**, and the reason for going to Pau unknown.
5. ***Le Retour du Christ* (1874)** is in every bibliography and no catalogue; possibly a
   ghost title propagating by citation.
