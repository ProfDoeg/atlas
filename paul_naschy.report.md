# Paul Naschy (Jacinto Molina Álvarez), 1934-2009: research report

*Compiled 2026-09-01 for `paul_naschy.journey.json`. **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-witness first-person testimony. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Inaccessible sources are named with the reason. Gaps are stated as gaps.*

The evidence base first. Naschy is his own principal source: two memoirs and a run of long interviews, which the secondary literature, fan scholarship, and obituaries largely recirculate. The hard anchors are narrow. The state gazette for the 2001 medal, both Wikipedias for the film chronology, the weightlifting record via proyectonaschy.com, and a 2013 civil judgment known only through press summaries. Everything else is one man's account of himself, told well and told often. That is not a reason to discard it. It is a reason to tag it.

---

## 1. Origins and the war, 1934-1943

- **Born 6 September 1934, Madrid, as Jacinto Molina Álvarez. [A]** es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia, EcuRed agree.
- **Father Enrique Molina, furrier near the Plaza Mayor; mother Pilar Álvarez. [R]** Wikipedia gives only "Madrid," no address; the workshop and the ruined prewar business come from later profiles and his own retellings. The coordinate is the Plaza Mayor, labelled as such.
- **Wartime displacement including Burgos. [A for Burgos]** es.wikipedia; the rest of the itinerary [R].
- **The father in the doorway. [R]** The central scene of his self-account: hiding under a bed from his own armed, bearded father, fleeing a threatened execution, taking him for a monster. His first-person telling, El Duende 2000, with the line *"El primer monstruo de mi vida fue mi propio padre."* **The village is never named in any source consulted**; the file uses a rough Cantabrian centroid. A half-brother alleged to have denounced the father could not be confirmed and is omitted.
- **Snow White's witch as his first cinematic fright. [R]** Dossier only. **The war-delayed Spanish release date is not established**, so the viewing date is unfixed.
- **Frankenstein Meets the Wolf Man as the founding image. [R]** **He varied his own age in the telling: eight, ten, eleven**, and no source fixes the Spanish screening. The file uses 1943, the American release year, as an ordering device.
- **Gap:** nothing on his schooling, his reported architecture studies, or his adolescence survives in sourced form.

## 2. The weightlifter, 1950-1971

- **Gimnasio Guzmán el Bueno, then Gimnasio Juventud on Calle Fuencarral. [A]** proyectonaschy.com, "Jacinto Molina y su primera pasión: la halterofilia." Neither start year given; the file's dates order the segment.
- **Spanish lightweight champion (67.5 kg), Valencia, 1958. [A]** es.wikipedia (champion multiple times from that year) plus proyectonaschy.com for city and class. Venue unspecified; coordinate is the city centre.
- **Real Madrid Olympic weightlifting section, 1958-1971; twenty-eight records credited; six consecutive team titles. [A per proyectonaschy.com]**, no club record checked.
- **First Spanish lightweight to total 295.5 kg, 7 June 1959. [R]** Dossier only. **No results sheet located; asserted nowhere in the journey file.**
- **Vienna, 1961, world and European championships, reportedly sixth in Europe and ninth overall. [R]** **No IWF result book found**, and **the Wiener Stadthalle attribution is a researcher's inference.** The file says both.
- **Considered for Rome 1960 and Tokyo 1964, never competed. [R]** es.wikipedia cites a hand injury and a federation decision; other profiles cite compressed vertebrae or federation politics. **No source reconciles which applied to which year.**

## 4. The manufacture of Paul Naschy, 1967-1975

- ***La marca del hombre lobo* (1968, dir. Enrique López Eguiluz), Spanish-West German, 70mm 3-D, written by and starring Molina. [A]** es.wikipedia.
- **The name: *Paul* from a photograph of Pope Paul VI, *Naschy* from the weightlifter Imre Nagy. [A per es.wikipedia]** **Open contradiction:** proyectonaschy.com names a Hungarian competitor **literally surnamed Naschy** who won the 60 kg class. No source reconciles them; both are in the stop's confidence field.
- **Daninsky named for the Polish champion Waldemar Baszanowski. [R]** His own account only.
- ***Las noches del hombre lobo*, claimed shot in Paris under René Govar, who allegedly died with the negative. [R, disputed]** **No print, contract, festival record, trade notice, or still has ever surfaced**, and filmographers disagree on whether it was completed. Stop at the canonical Paris pin (48.8566, 2.3522).
- **Marriage to Elvira Primavera. [A for month and year; day contradicted.]** en.wikipedia: **24 October 1969**. The dossier, citing a scholarly history of Spanish fantastic cinema: **4 October 1969**. **Not resolved.**
- ***La noche de Walpurgis* (dir. León Klimovsky, 1970, wide release generally the following year), the breakthrough. [A]** Territory release dates vary, not adjudicated.
- **Georges Méliès award, Paris fantastic-film festival, 1973. [R]** es.wikipedia lists it; **no primary palmarès located.**
- **Sitges best actor, October 1975, for *La maldición de la bestia*. [A]** en.wikipedia. **Open discrepancy:** *Clavel de Plata* (dossier, via the Sitges archive) against "Silver Nail" (es.wikipedia). **Inaccessible:** the festival's 1975 palmarès page, HTTP 403.

- **Julia Saly and the Japanese patron Masurao Takeda; Aconito Films; documentaries including *El museo del Prado*. [A for the documentary, R for the year.]** revistaelduende.com confirms the film, the financier, and a reported 42-million-peseta budget (**his own unaudited claim**), **but gives no year. Open contradiction:** geography lens 1980, chronology lens 1981 for the same activity.
- **Japanese crews inside the Palacio de Oriente, 1981 [R]**, the dossier citing an El País report; **elpais.com could not be fetched.** **Altamira among the same commissions [R]**, dossier list only.
- ***La bestia y la espada mágica* (1983), Daninsky in sixteenth-century Japan. [A for the film.]** **The Kurosawa meeting and the samurai helmet are [R], single-witness: no photograph, letter, provenance, or second witness, and no source fixes where or when.** The Tokyo stop declares its placement and date as ordering devices.
- **Aconito collapses; financial ruin and prolonged depression. [R]** His memoirs. Retrospective claims of suicidal thought are his own; **no suicide attempt is documented.**
- **Father's death, 20 June 1984, heart attack while fishing, aged 74. [A per en.wikipedia]**, not verified against a primary record. ***Operación Mantis* (1984), a Bond parody he directed. [R]**

## 6. The heart, and the slow rehabilitation, 1987-1996

- ***El aullido del diablo* (1987), written, directed, starring him opposite his son Sergio, with Caroline Munro and Howard Vernon; very poor distribution. [A]** The reading as self-portrait is critical consensus, not documented intent.
- **Myocardial infarction after weight training, 27 August 1991; bypass surgery 5 September 1991; three grafts by his own account. [A]** en.wikipedia. The city is **presumed** Madrid, not stated by the source.
- **Spielberg's telephone calls during the recovery. [R]** His anecdote, El Duende 2000. **No independent confirmation; `stephen_spielberg.journey.json` does not mention Naschy.** Kept, flagged unconfirmed.
- **Filmoteca Española retrospective, 1993, reported at 45 films. [R]** **No programme or press notice fetched, no venue named**; generic Madrid pin.
- **Fantastic film festival founded or co-founded at Burgos, 1994. [R, weakly sourced.]** Support is later legal and obituary reporting, neither fetched. **Open gap: a source that actually says Burgos.**
- ***Licántropo: el asesino de la luna llena* (1996), written in convalescence; thin distribution, poor box office, best actor at the Madrid fantastic film festival the following year. [R]** No primary festival record checked.

## 7. American recognition and the Spanish medal, 1997-2007

- ***Memorias de un hombre lobo* (Alberto Santos/Imágica, 1997), 304 pp., prologue by Christopher Lee; English edition *Memoirs of a Wolfman*. [A]**
- **Fangoria Weekend of Horrors, New York, January 1998, with Elvira, Sergio, and Bruno. [A]** proyectonaschy.com; El País covered the trip. The Hall of Fame induction is placed "two years after" **with no year given**, so the campa says "a year or two later."
- **Carl Laemmle lifetime achievement award, Washington, alongside Roger Corman and Janet Leigh. [A]** proyectonaschy.com. **Neither year nor venue given.**
- **Gold Medal of Merit in the Fine Arts, Real Decreto 159/2001 de 16 de febrero, BOE núm. 42 of 17 February 2001. [A, verified against the gazette.]** The decree names "Don Jacinto Molina Álvarez (Paul Naschy), Actor" and grants **sixteen medals: thirteen individuals, three institutions. No ceremony is evidenced; it is a gazette act.**
- **The Honorary Goya he did not get. [R]** Genre-press tradition, including a claim that Pilar Miró championed it. **No Academy minute confirms it.** What is [A] is his own remark about the asymmetry.
- **Los Angeles, 2003, about two months at the Hollywood Roosevelt filming *Countess Dracula's Orgy of Blood* (dir. Don F. Glut) and *Tomb of the Werewolf* (dir. Fred Olen Ray). [A]** proyectonaschy.com, which also carries the Ackerman visit **[R, single source, no date]** and the Mission San Juan Capistrano shoot **[A, though the source says near the mission, not at it]**.
- **Fangoria convention at Universal Studios, 2007; met Elena Verdugo of *House of Frankenstein* (1944). [A]** Canonical Universal City pin.
- **Honorary doctorate at Algeciras. [R, thin.]** One undated sentence on revistaelduende.com, **absent from every other source. Dropped from the journey file**; recorded here so it is not lost.

## 8. Last work, death, and afterlife, 2008-2025

- ***Cuando las luces se apagan* (Fundación AISGE / T&B, 2008), closing text by Quentin Tarantino [A]; *Alaric de Marnac* (2009), his sole novel [A, es.wikipedia].** The novel is folded into the 2008 stop.
- **Final months: Sitges, and acting for José Luis Alemán in *La herencia Valdemar* and its sequel. [A in substance]** eldiario.es and academiadecine.com. **The offered cameo in Joe Johnston's *The Wolfman*, prevented by scheduling, is [R].**
- **Died 30 November 2009 in Madrid, aged 75. [A]** en.wikipedia, EcuRed, eldiario.es. Some notices and the Real Academia de la Historia give **1 December**, the death having become public the following morning.
- **Cause of death: unresolved, not adjudicated.** en.wikipedia: **pancreatic cancer**, diagnosed 2008. es.wikipedia: **"cáncer"**, no type. The 2013 judgment as reported: **metastatic ductal adenocarcinoma of the prostate**, found during an unrelated operation. **This pass could not read the judgment.**
- **2013: Madrid Court of First Instance No. 6 partly upheld a negligence suit by his widow and son against his urologist, awarding 43,682 euros for loss of therapeutic opportunity. [A per press reporting relayed by the dossier]**; judgment text not read. **The stop is deliberately absent:** that is his widow's and son's action, not his. It is carried as a `suggested_ref` and in the death campa.
- **Buried in the Cementerio Municipal de San José, Burgos, 2 December 2009. [A]** es.wikipedia infobox. **No plot identified.**
- **Afterlife, folded into the burial campa and its refs rather than given stops**, posthumous events being other people's scenes: the Madrid wax museum Daninsky figure (2016, [R]); the *El huerto del francés* restoration, Sitges circa 2020 [A]; the Mondo Macabro and Shout Factory Blu-rays [A]; Howarth's *Human Beasts* (2018) [A via a review site]; *Muchas Gracias, Señor Lobo* [A, **no year**]; *Call Me Paul*, Sitges 2024 [A].

## Corrections applied to the research pool

Each was carried in as settled and does not survive checking. All are fixed in the journey file, with reasons in the relevant `date_confidence` fields.

**Source misattributions.** ***El Cid*** and ***55 Days at Peking*** were tagged [A] on en.wikipedia and **appear nowhere in that article** (`Special:Export`); both drop to [R]. The **name origin** was credited to revistaelduende.com, which **does not mention it**; es.wikipedia carries it, and the rival "Hungarian surnamed Naschy" derivation stays an open contradiction. The **Burgos festival** was [A] on revistaelduende.com, which **has no mention of it**, plus an inference; now [R]. The **cause of death** was credited to es.wikipedia for "metastatic ductal prostate cancer"; **"prostate" is in neither Wikipedia article.** Conversely, **Sitges 1975** was [R] only because the palmarès page 403'd, and **BOE 159/2001** was called unverifiable; both are now [A], the decree showing sixteen medals, not the "fifteen" or "about a dozen" earlier passes gave.

**Quotations.** "Paul Naschy es Dios" was called a Fangoria tribute with the words in the press's mouth; the source reads *"la prensa preguntó cuál era la causa, y les contestaron"*, so **the press asked and bystanders answered**, at a New York homage, and *Fangoria* is not on that page. "Mi etiqueta terrorífica..." had an unmarked ellipsis swallowing the comparison to Karloff, Lugosi, and Cushing. The Rollán memoir passage had the source's "(...)" dropped. Both restored.

**Facts.** The Peñaflor murders, called "nineteenth-century," are **1898-1904**, the film's year settled at **1978**. Mission San Juan Capistrano, called "near the Mexican border," is in Orange County about 130 km north of it. The Palacio Real "Royal Decree ceremony" is an invented setting: **the decree evidences no ceremony.** The *Call Me Paul* framing was given as the 1991 heart attack and an "ambulance journey"; **cineconn.es gives the director's thesis as formative childhood viewing**, with 1991 appearing nowhere.

**Coordinates.** Wiener Stadthalle, about 400 m off, becomes **48.2019, 16.3328**. Peñaflor, about 3 km off, becomes **37.7072, -5.3469**. Hollywood Roosevelt, about 1.4 km east, becomes **34.1011, -118.3417**. The Cementerio de San José, Burgos, had **two different wrong coordinates from two passes**, 42.355, -3.681 and 42.3439, -3.6969, and becomes **42.3567, -3.7043**. The Méliès award, staged at Sitges a thousand kilometres from the Paris festival the source names, moves to the canonical Paris pin. The Madrid civil court coordinate is moot: no stop was built, the lawsuit not being his scene.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Paul Naschy is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Relations not rooted in his own life have not been silently deleted. What was caught, and which direction it runs:

**Backward or contemporaneous, and used**, each being a genuine one-directional relation of his own: he saw the film, named the influence, made the parody, told the story.

- **Walt Disney**, backward: the *Snow White* witch, named by Naschy as his first cinematic fright. In the Madrid cinema campa. The Carthay Circle premiere pin is **not** inherited: he was not there.
- **Mary Shelley**, backward: the creature in *Frankenstein Meets the Wolf Man*, plus the US retitling of his first film as *Frankenstein's Bloody Terror*. Named once, in the 1943 campa. **Villa Diodati (46.2203, 6.1833) and Finsbury Square are not inherited**: Shelley's stops.
- **Ian Fleming**, backward: *Operación Mantis* (1984) cites his creation. One clause in the 1984 campa, no London stop.
- **Steven Spielberg**, contemporaneous and single-witness: the claimed telephone calls of 1991, named in that campa and flagged unconfirmed. The Universal City pin (34.1381, -118.3534) is inherited byte-identical, for Naschy's **own** 2007 visit.

**Caught and dropped as shared geography only**, co-location not being a relation:

- **George Orwell.** Same Civil War, opposite fronts. No contact, citation, or influence either way.
- **Federico García Lorca.** Shot near Granada in 1936, with real thematic kinship to the *España negra* of *El caminante* and *El huerto del francés*, but **no documented crossing between the two men.** Corpus resonance is essay material, not a journey interlock.
- **H. P. Lovecraft.** **Genre lineage only**: no adaptation, correspondence, or citation by Naschy in the pool. The QUEUE.md phrase "the corpus's horror/Gothic wing (Lovecraft already queued)" is the atlas's framing for a viewer, **not a fact about Naschy's life**.

**Direction summary: everything caught runs backward or contemporaneous. Nothing forward-facing was found or written**, and no framework of Naschy's is applied to material he never treated, so nothing calls for adding him to `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Argentina is answered negatively: **no Argentine or Buenos Aires connection exists in the pool or was found independently, and none has been invented.**
