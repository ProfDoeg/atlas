# Emma Kunz (1892-1963): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14. Swiss healer, radiesthesist, and maker of large-format pendulum-guided drawings on millimetre paper. Born Brittnau (Aargau), died Waldstatt (Appenzell Ausserrhoden). Namer of the mineral AION A at the Roman-era quarry of Würenlos.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference · *gap* = the sources do not answer, and the question is left open rather than filled.

One warning governs the whole file. **The record for Kunz is thin, late, and institutionally interested.** Almost everything before 1938 reaches us through the Emma Kunz Zentrum, a foundation set up twenty-three years after her death by a man who believes her mineral cured his polio, and through Meier's 1994 biography, which is that foundation's own account. Wikipedia relays it. No Kunz letters, no diary, and no contemporary press were reachable in this pass.

---

## 1. Brittnau, 1892-1911: the weaver's house

- Born **23 May 1892** in Brittnau, Aargau, to **Stephan Oswald Kunz**, a hand weaver, and **Rosina Kunz née Gubelmann**. One of **ten children**, three of whom die in infancy. **[A: English Wikipedia; German Wikipedia; emma-kunz.com]**
- Village primary schooling; an early "sensitivity" to what others do not perceive. **[R]** Family-derived, undated, no document behind it: the standard opening move of a visionary's *vita*, and evidence of what the family later said rather than of what happened.
- **In her seventeenth year (1909) her father and one brother die by suicide.** **[A: German Wikipedia, at age 17; English Wikipedia citing family biography]** No month or day for either. *Gap.*
  - **Overreach flagged.** The geography lens said the deaths fell "within the same short span": **no source says this**, and the interval is a gap. The same lens implied five distinct sibling deaths, an arithmetic error, since one of the two suicides is the father.
- **c. 1910, at eighteen:** takes up a pendulum and explores what her contemporaries call telepathy and prophecy. **[A: German Wikipedia]** She names no teacher, then or later.
- **1911: travels to the United States, reportedly following a romantic interest, and returns disappointed** after roughly a year. **[A: German Wikipedia]**
  - **The destination is a documentary gap.** No city, port, employer, or household is named anywhere. The least documented year of her life, carried inside a Brittnau stop rather than pinned, because pinning it would mean inventing a place.

## 2. Strengelbach and Engelberg, 1912-1939

- **1912-1923: knitting factory at Strengelbach.** **[A: German Wikipedia]** Eleven years, unremarked in every account: no incident, no correspondence, no notice.
- **1923-1939: housekeeper and companion to the painter Jakob Welti.** **[A: German Wikipedia]**
  - **Correction.** German Wikipedia specifies that the Engelberg part ran "während der Sommermonate", summer months only: sixteen years, and *seasonal*, not the continuous residence with "unhurried time, a mountain village's quiet" that the geography lens described, and not the "seventeen years" it elsewhere claimed. Whether Welti had any view of her research is **unrecorded**. *Gap.*
- **1930: publishes a poetry collection, *Leben* (Life).** **[A: English and German Wikipedia]**
  - **Place of writing and publication is a gap.** The geography lens pinned it to Engelberg and tagged it [A]: reconstruction wearing an attestation tag. The journey file pins it to Brittnau as her home of record, hedge in `date_confidence`. The same lens called *Leben* "her only book to reach print in her lifetime"; two further books appear in 1953.
- **1933: begins investigating the earth's energy currents with the pendulum.** **[A as to date: German Wikipedia]** **Location unattested.** Siting the work "in and around Engelberg", or having her "walking Engelberg's slopes", is reconstruction and cannot carry [A].

## 3. 1938: the first sheets

- **1938: begins large-format geometric drawings on millimetre paper, guided by a pendulum held over the sheet.** **[A as to date and method: German Wikipedia; English Wikipedia; Serpentine Galleries]**
  - **Age correction.** Born 23 May 1892, she is **forty-five turning forty-six**. The repeated "at around age forty" is wrong by five or six years.
  - **Location unattested.** *Gap.* Engelberg is a defensible inference (the Welti arrangement runs to 1939; she is at Brittnau only from 1940) but stays inference, and the journey file says so in `date_confidence`.
- **Method:** sustained sessions **beyond twenty-four hours**, graphite and coloured pencil on graph paper, recording patterns generated by pendulum movement. **[A: Serpentine Galleries]**
- **She does not sign, date, or title the works.** **[A: English Wikipedia]**
  - **Overreach dropped:** the chronology lens added that she "records their medical or research meanings separately in her published books". **No source consulted supports this.**
- **Self-description: researcher and healer, not artist.** **[A: Serpentine Galleries; emma-kunz.com]**
  - **Quotation problem flagged.** The line circulating as "Emma Kunz, one of the most exceptional women of the 20th century... described herself as a researcher" is **not a Kunz quotation.** It is the Zentrum's own third-person prose, with a clause elided by the ellipsis, made to read as her voice. **No verbatim first-person Kunz sentence to this effect was located.** Carried as unquoted biographical fact, never in a quote field.
- **Undated: the marigold experiment.** She uses the pendulum to polarise marigolds in her garden, producing multiple flower heads, documented photographically. **[A: Serpentine Galleries]** **No source assigns it a year**; the circulating 1940 date is unsourced. Placed inside the Brittnau period, undatedness stated in `date_confidence`.

## 4. Würenlos and AION A, 1941/1942 onward

- **1942: identifies healing properties in a stone at a Roman-era quarry near Würenlos and names it AION A.** **[A: English Wikipedia; emma-kunz.com]** No month.
- **1941: Anton C. Meier, of the quarry family, is healed of the aftereffects of childhood polio by the stone.** **[A: German Wikipedia; Emma Kunz Zentrum]**
  - **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** The stone is recorded curing someone in **1941**, a year before its curative property is discovered in **1942**. No source addresses the sequence and neither does this file; it is flagged in that stop's `date_confidence`. Both readings stay open: a loose year in the account of Meier, or a longer investigation that only crystallised into the name in 1942.
- **Through the 1940s and 1950s** the pit is worked to her instruction: AION A extracted, ground to powder, made into paste, given to patients and increasingly sold. **[A: emma-kunz.com, giving the address Steinbruchstrasse 5, CH-5436 Würenlos]**
- **The stone is still mined at the same quarry and sold commercially in Switzerland.** **[A: Serpentine Galleries, "still sold in chemists in Switzerland, mined from the same quarry"; English Wikipedia concurs]**
  - **Overreach dropped:** the marketing indications in circulation ("joint pain, muscular complaints, and inflammatory skin conditions") appear in **neither** the Serpentine page **nor** the Zentrum material consulted. Note also that this is a present-tense fact which the pool filed under a 1963 date; the journey file carries it as present practice.
- **The grotto.** She returns repeatedly to the cut face, saying she goes to "recharge like a battery" body and soul, calling it "this place of strength" and wishing it might become a meeting place for cultural, spiritual, and healing work. **[A: emma-kunz.com, quoting both phrases in situ]** Both are fragments inside the foundation's prose, not sentences from a Kunz text; marked as such in `quote_source`.

## 5. Waldstatt, 1951-1963: the last years

- **1951: relocates to Waldstatt** near the Säntis, a place she regards as spiritually significant. **[A: German Wikipedia]** She is fifty-nine.
- **Practises as healer and naturopath**, working the pendulum over the body or over a drawing made in the patient's name, and calling the capacity latent in everyone rather than exclusive to her. **[A: emma-kunz.com; Aargauer Kunsthaus text; Meier 1994 via English Wikipedia]** Start date: *gap*.
- **1953: self-publishes two books.** *Neuartige Zeichnungsmethode* **[A: German and English Wikipedia]** and a companion given in English sources as *The Miracle of Creative Revelation* (*Das Wunder schöpfender Offenbarung*) **[A: English Wikipedia]**. No contemporary review located. *Gap.*
- **Died 16 January 1963 at Waldstatt, aged 70.** **[A: English and German Wikipedia; emma-kunz.com]**
- **Burial place: unknown. [R, gap]** No location, marker, or grave reference in English or German Wikipedia, the Zentrum's history, or Serpentine materials. Carried as its own stop stating the absence rather than asserting a tomb. Waldstatt parish registers were not consulted.

### The size of the corpus

- **English Wikipedia:** "At the time of her death, Kunz had left behind a substantial body of work of **400** drawings." **[A]**
- **Emma Kunz Zentrum:** around **600**. **[A]**

**Not reconciled by any source, and not reconciled here.** Both figures are given with attribution in the death stop. Related error: the geography lens described the 1973 Aarau show as hanging "six hundred sheets". **600 is the estimated surviving corpus, not an exhibition count**, and no source gives the number hung in 1973.

### The five-hundred-year prophecy

Art press and popular accounts repeat that Kunz predicted her work would only be understood in about five hundred years.

- **Verifiable [A]:** the figure is **Professor H. Larcher of Paris**, writing *about* her on the Zentrum biography page: "People such as Emma Kunz live at best once every 500 years, and even then we do not always have the good fortune to receive such a witness as that delivered in her bequeathed work." A claim about her rarity as a person, made after her death.
- **What Kunz is recorded saying [A: emma-kunz.com Museum page]:** "My drawings are designed for the 21st century." Decades, not centuries.
- **Therefore [R]:** the popular prophecy conflates Larcher's tribute with her own narrower statement. Carried as tradition, attributed to Larcher, never put in her mouth.

## 6. Posthumous

- **1973: first public exhibition, *Der Fall Emma Kunz*, Aargauer Kunsthaus, Aarau**, curated by **Heiny Widmer**. **[A: German Wikipedia; emma-kunz.com; aargauerkunsthaus.ch]** No month. *Gap.* English Wikipedia renders the title *Der Fall von Emma Kunz*; the German form is used.
- **1970s: Harald Szeemann** brings critical attention to the work. **[A: Serpentine Galleries, quoting him verbatim]** Original publication and date of that passage **not given** there. *Gap.*
- **Düsseldorf and Paris:** German Wikipedia records "Weitere Ausstellungen folgten unter anderem in Düsseldorf und Paris". **[A as to occurrence; R as to all else]** **No venue, no curator, no year for either.** *Gap.* Both are carried as stops with placeholder dates and city-centroid pins, the emptiness stated in `date_confidence`. The pool credited Düsseldorf to English Wikipedia, where it does not appear.
- **1986: Anton C. Meier founds the Emma Kunz Zentrum** at the quarry. **[A: emma-kunz.com; English and German Wikipedia]**
- **17 October 1991: the Emma Kunz Museum opens**, showing roughly seventy major works. **[A: German Wikipedia, date; English Wikipedia, figure]**
- **1999: *Richtkräfte für das 21. Jahrhundert*, Kunsthaus Zürich, 21 May to 1 August**, with Rudolf Steiner, Joseph Beuys, and Andrej Belyj. **[A: German Wikipedia]**
- **2005: *3 x Abstraction*, Santa Monica Museum of Art**, Catherine de Zegher's pairing of Kunz with **Hilma af Klint** and **Agnes Martin**, with a Yale University Press book. **[A: English Wikipedia]** Not a solo show, and the af Klint pairing is established **here, in 2005**, not 2010 as the pool's interlock anchor had it.
- **2013: *The Encyclopedic Palace*, 55th Venice Biennale, Central Pavilion**, curated by Massimiliano Gioni. **[A: English Wikipedia names the 55th Biennale and that pavilion; the 55th is 2013, the pavilion is in the Giardini]** The pool's [R] hedge is unnecessary; promoted to [A].
- **2018-2019: *Weltempfänger*, Städtische Galerie im Lenbachhaus und Kunstbau, Munich**, with Georgiana Houghton and Hilma af Klint. **[A: German Wikipedia]** The geography lens filed both Zurich and Munich as "date uncertain, post-1973" with venue unknown; German Wikipedia fixes both, and Munich is not part of the post-1973 wave at all.
- **23 March to 19 May 2019: *Emma Kunz: Visionary Drawings*, Serpentine Galleries, London.** First UK solo show, over forty drawings, conceived with **Christodoulos Panayiotou**, who had benches quarried from AION A made for it. **[A: Serpentine Galleries]**
- **2021: *Emma Kunz Cosmos*, Aargauer Kunsthaus.** Around sixty drawings with fourteen contemporary artists, many shown for the first time. **[A: aargauerkunsthaus.ch; English Wikipedia]** No month. *Gap.*
- **2021: Aargau heritage-protection award** to the Emma Kunz Foundation for the Würenlos site. **[A: German Wikipedia]** No month. *Gap.*

## 7. Interlocks with the existing corpus

Only relations running *through Emma Kunz or her work* are named in the journey prose. Shared geography and shared century are not relations.

**Named in the journey file**

- **Hilma af Klint** (`hilma_af_klint.journey.json`). A real relation, established posthumously and repeatedly: de Zegher's *3 x Abstraction* (2005) yokes them in one book and one exhibition, and *Weltempfänger* (2018-2019) hangs them together with Georgiana Houghton. Both made large geometric abstraction by a method they refused to call invention, both called the result research, both addressed the work to a later public. **They never met and no source suggests either knew of the other.** [A as to the joint exhibitions; R as to deeper kinship]
- **Rudolf Steiner** (`steiner.journey.json`). Named only at the 1999 Kunsthaus Zürich stop, where her sheets hang beside his blackboard drawings by the curators' arrangement. **No documented contact or awareness either way**; the link is a fact about that exhibition, not about her life.

**Recorded here, deliberately NOT named in any campa.** All are parallels or shared milieu, none a documented relation: **Carl Gustav Jung** (`jung.journey.json`: contemporaneous, 25-40 km off, mandalas and synchronicity rhyming with the pendulum grids, but no meeting or mutual awareness recorded), **Paracelsus** (`paracelsus.journey.json`: the Swiss precedent for an untrained mineral-healer, and a precedent is not a relation), **Paul Klee** (`paul_klee.journey.json`), **Arthur Bispo do Rosário** (`arthur_bispo_do_rosario.journey.json`: the identical posthumous arc), **Charlotte Bara** and Monte Verità (`charlotte_bara.journey.json`), and **Hermann Rorschach** (a close diagnostic parallel; no dedicated file, mentioned inside `salomon_trismosin.journey.json`). Two naming hazards: `carl_jung_of_mainz.journey.json` is an early-modern alchemist, so the psychiatrist must always be *Carl Gustav Jung of Küsnacht* in full; and **George F. Kunz** in `robert_oppenheimer.journey.json` has **no relation whatsoever** to Emma Kunz.

## 9. Contradictions carried forward unresolved

1. **400 vs 600 drawings** at her death. Both given with attribution. Not adjudicated.
2. **Meier healed 1941, stone discovered 1942.** Flagged in the stop's `date_confidence`. Not adjudicated.
3. **The five-hundred-year prophecy** against the Larcher quote and her own "designed for the 21st century". Attributed to Larcher; the popular version marked tradition.
4. **Sixteen vs seventeen years** with Welti. Resolved arithmetically: 1923-1939 is sixteen.
5. ***Der Fall Emma Kunz* vs *Der Fall von Emma Kunz***. German form used.
6. **Coordinates for six places**, where the lenses disagreed with each other and with gazetteers. Corrected in the journey file: Waldstatt (2.5-3 km off) to 47.3563362 / 9.2838414; the 1973 exhibition (pinned in central Zurich, 75 km off) to the Aargauer Kunsthaus 47.3903329 / 8.0445261; Santa Monica (Ocean Park, 3 km off) to Bergamot Station 34.029 / -118.469; Venice (1.5 km off) to 45.4297 / 12.3542; Kunsthaus Zürich and Lenbachhaus centroids to the venues, 47.3703 / 8.5484 and 48.1469 / 11.5636.
