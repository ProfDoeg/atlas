# Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756-1791): research report

*2026-08-28, for `wolfgang_amadeus_mozart.journey.json`. 9 segments, 45 stops, 8 quoted stops.*

**[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or secondhand report. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Where a pool claim proved wrong on inspection, the correction is stated with its source and the original recorded, so nothing is silently overwritten.

Naming: he signed himself **Wolfgang Amadè**, rendering the register's Greek *Theophilus* variously; the fixed "Amadeus" is largely posthumous editorial usage.

## Evidence ledger by phase

The journey file carries the narrative, each stop stating its own status in `date_confidence`. This ledger records what is attested, what is not, and where the sources fight each other.

**Salzburg and the courts, 1756-1762.** Birth, baptism, parentage, the seven children of whom five died in infancy, the Munich trip of 1762 and the Vienna audience of October 1762 are all **[A: Mozarteum; Wikipedia; German Wikipedia for the itinerary]**. The Schonbrunn lap-and-proposal anecdote is court folklore attached to a documented performance. **[R: no primary source]**

**The Grand Tour, 1763-1766.** Dates of departure and return, the itinerary, the Brussels wait and its 7 November 1763 concert, the Paris sonatas K. 6-9, Versailles, the fifteen London months with George III and Johann Christian Bach, the first symphonies written in Chelsea, and the Dutch illnesses are **[A: Wikipedia; dossier via the interlock lens for K. 6-9]**. Report rather than document, so **[R]**: Wolfgang standing at the queen's side fed from her hand; the Ebury Street address, traditional among several London lodgings; Nannerl's last rites at The Hague.

**Smallpox and Italy, 1767-1773.** Olomouc and the Podstatsky deanery, the *Miserere* written out from memory with Leopold reporting within days, the Golden Spur, Martini and the Bologna examination with its waived age limit and its exercise corrected in another hand, the three Milan operas, and the 1773 Salzburg appointment are **[A: Wikipedia; dossier; DME/NMA]**. **[R]**: the papal-secrecy and single-hearing embellishments on the *Miserere*.
- ***Bastien und Bastienne*, 1768. [R]** Commission and work attested; the staging in Mesmer's garden theatre rests on **Nissen's posthumous biography alone**, with no contemporary programme, letter, or score annotation, Nissen himself finding no proof, the commission also claimed for Mesmer's cousin Joseph Conrad, and the earliest secure staging Berlin 1890. **[R: Steptoe, jstor.org/stable/735887; Carus-Verlag note]**
- ***La finta semplice*** was **never staged in Vienna**; premiere Salzburg, 1 May 1769. The Vienna intrigue is in the family correspondence but has no building to sit on, so the stop was cut rather than mis-pinned. **[A]**
- Maria Theresa's advice to Ferdinand against employing itinerant musicians is **[A]** but **paraphrased** in the file, not quoted: I did not have the letter's text.

**Away from Colloredo, 1775-1779.** *La finta giardiniera*, the discharge and the departure with his mother, Augsburg, Mannheim, K. 297, Anna Maria's death and the two letters of that night, the Saint-Eustache burial, and the 450-florin organist's post are **[A: Wikipedia; Mozarteum]**. **Whether the Basle relationship was also sexual is unproven**; the letters are the only evidence and do not settle it.

**Vienna, 1781-1787.** *Idomeneo*, the 1781 rupture at the Deutschordenshaus, *Die Entfuhrung*, the marriage one day before Leopold's consent arrived, the two children (Raimund Leopold, nine weeks; Carl Thomas, d. 1858 without issue), both Masonic degrees under Ignaz von Born with Leopold initiated in 1785, Domgasse 5, *Figaro* and its nine performances, and Leopold's death are **[A: Mozarteum; Wikipedia; King's College London, "True Concord", mmc.kdl.kcl.ac.uk; Museum of Freemasonry]**.
- **[R]**: the **kick from Arco**, Mozart's own account in his own letter, corroborated by nobody; Joseph II's "too many notes", reaching us only through Niemetschek's later biography; **Haydn's remark to Leopold**, which the interlock lens told the writer to verify independently and I did not.
- **The lodge premises are not fixed in the record**; the file's pin marks central Vienna and says so. Mozart's line about nothing being talked of but *Figaro* is **paraphrased**: not in the verified quote pool. **He did not attend Leopold's funeral and the record does not single out a reason**; none is supplied.

**Prague, Berlin, the money, 1787-1790.** *Don Giovanni* and the Bertramka lodging, the Kammermusicus post at 800 florins succeeding Gluck, the three catalogued symphonies of summer 1788, the Puchberg letters, the 1789 northern journey with Lichnowsky whose documented return was far smaller than legend, *Cosi fan tutte* cut short by Joseph II's death, and the self-financed Frankfurt trip are **[A: Wikipedia; Dexter Edge, "Mozart and Finances", *Mozart in Context*, CUP]**. **[R]**: the skittles at Bertramka; the Thomaskirche organ improvisation, which survives in Bach-circle memoir, not a contemporary letter. **No lifetime performance of the three symphonies is on record**, which the file states as an absence in the record, not as proof none occurred.

**The last year and the burial, 1791.** *Tito*, *Die Zauberflote*, K. 623 as the last catalogue entry, the November illness with Closset and Sallaba attending, the death shortly before 1 a.m. on 5 December with the register's "hitziges Frieselfieber", a symptom name and not a disease, the absence of an autopsy, and the third-class shaft grave that was normal for his rank and **not** a pauper's mass grave are **[A: Mozarteum; Hirschmann, "What Killed Mozart?", JAMA Internal Medicine; Wikipedia]**.
- **Flag preserved from the pool**: an automated extraction rendered the *Tito* premiere as 6 December 1791, impossible, since he is dead on 5 December.
- **[R]**: the Hofer / Schikaneder casting, standard but unconfirmed from the fetched source; the Crucifix Chapel identification, from later accounts; the storm and abandoned mourners, nineteenth-century additions.
- **The two-date contradiction, flagged and left standing.** The chronology lens has burial at St Marx on **6 December**; the geography and afterlife lenses, and Wikipedia's "Death of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart", have **7 December**. The Mozarteum has the coffin consecrated at St Stephen's on 6 December at about 3 p.m. and transported that evening. The file carries **two stops**, consecration 6 December and interment 7 December, with `date_confidence` saying the sources disagree. A reconciliation, not a resolution: the sources do not supply one date.

**Afterlife.** Eybler's abandoned completion and Sussmayr's finished one for the anonymous Count Walsegg, the failed grave searches by Constanze and Novello, the 1855 stone and its removal to the Zentralfriedhof, the replacement tablet at St Marx refurbished in 1950 by Florian Josephu-Drouot, the Kochel catalogue of 1862, the Neue Mozart-Ausgabe of 1956-2007, and the three letters editions, the last of which restored scatological passages earlier editions silently censored, are **[A: Wikipedia, "St. Marx Cemetery"; standard bibliographic record]**.
- **Salieri poisoning legend**: no autopsy, toxicology, confession, or witness. Cultural form from Pushkin (1830), Rimsky-Korsakov (1897/98), Shaffer (1979), Forman (1984). **[R as legend, A as reception history]**
- **"Mozart effect"**: the 1993 Rauscher / Shaw / Ky study in *Nature* reported a roughly fifteen-minute spatial-reasoning boost after K. 448, which the press inflated into a general-intelligence claim the authors disavowed; later meta-analysis found little Mozart-specific benefit beyond arousal and mood. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Deliberately not a stop**: UC Irvine is a place Mozart has no connection to whatever, so under the standing rule it is Rauscher's and Shaw's stop. Carried in `suggested_refs` on the Kochel stop.
- Cut from the file for length, all **[A]** but the last two: the Tilgner monument (1896, moved to the Burggarten 1953); the birthplace museum (1880); Mozarthaus Vienna (2006); the Verona academy admission; the Naples magic-ring anecdote **[R]**; and the Mozarteum skull, whose 2006 mtDNA test **neither confirmed nor excluded** because the comparison remains were unrelated to each other **[R: the pool could not re-fetch the Live Science summary]**.

## Corrections applied to the research pool

Nine claims in the pool were wrong on inspection. Each is corrected in the journey file with the reason in `date_confidence`.

1. **Birthplace coordinate.** Three lenses gave three pins for one house: 47.7981/13.0450, 47.7998/13.0464, 47.8000/13.04333. Wikipedia, "Mozart's Birthplace", gives **47.80000, 13.04333**; the first two land 200-300 m southeast. File uses 47.8000/13.0433.
2. **Schönbrunn date.** The chronology lens gave "1762-09". German Wikipedia: left Salzburg 20 September, arrived Vienna 6 October, presented **13 October 1762**. September is the departure, not the audience.
3. **Ludwigsburg, claim withdrawn.** The lens had the family playing for the Württemberg ducal court in July 1763. **Not supported**: English Wikipedia's itinerary omits it from the performance stops and German Wikipedia lists it only as a station passed through, Duke Carl Eugen being unavailable. Stop dropped, recorded here so it is not lost.
4. **Brussels venue anachronistic.** The Coudenberg Palace burned in 1731 and lay in ruins; Charles of Lorraine received them at his own new palace on the hill (~50.8437, 4.3564).
5. **Olomouc.** The lens called the shelter the "Archbishop's Palace"; Olomouc was a **bishopric** until 1777 and the house was the **deanery**. Its 49.5955/17.2517 is the town centre, not the cathedral quarter (~49.5960, 17.2597). German Wikipedia has **both** children take smallpox.
6. **Burgtheater, three stops.** The eighteenth-century house stood on **Michaelerplatz** by the Hofburg (~48.2085, 16.3668); the Ringstrasse building dates from **1888**. The pool's 48.2065/16.3663 matches neither.
7. **Milan, wrong building and site.** The lens put all three Milan operas at 45.4676/9.1897, "near Piazza della Scala", which is **La Scala**, inaugurated 1778. The **Teatro Regio Ducale** was a wing of the Royal Palace beside the Duomo at **45.4632, 9.19114**, burned 25 February 1776.
8. **Concert Spirituel venue.** The lens gave "Palais-Royal". Wikipedia: the concerts took place for many years in the **Salle des Cent Suisses**, **Tuileries Palace**, moving to the Salle des Machines (also the Tuileries) only in 1784. Coordinate ~48.8626, 2.3324.
9. **Four more coordinates.** Estates Theatre **50.08611, 14.42389**, the pool's lying ~300 m east and the same wrong pair reused for *Tito*; Theater auf der Wieden **48.19778, 16.36611**, the pool's ~120 m west; St Marx **48.18278, 16.40167**, where all three lenses were wrong and mutually inconsistent (48.1958/16.4030; 48.1875/16.4025; 48.1814/16.3986); Zentralfriedhof **48.15250, 16.44000**, not 48.1489/16.4436, and its **1891 cenotaph year is unsupported**, the source giving no year and 1888 referring to Beethoven's and Schubert's actual graves. **The file asserts no year.**

## Quotations: verified, corrected, excluded

Eight of forty-five stops carry a quote; every one is verbatim from a fetched text.

**Verified verbatim, Project Gutenberg #5307, Wallace (1866), Vol. I:**

1. "I can't possibly write better, for my pen is only fit to write music and not a letter." **Addressee corrected**: the pool cited a letter to *Leopold*, who was travelling *with* Wolfgang in Italy in 1770. Letter 19, "Bologna, August 4, 1770", is internally addressed to his sister. Correct citation: **to Nannerl**. **[A]**
2. "This is famous for a composer - it inspires so many fine thoughts." Milan, 23 August 1771, ironic, on the noise of neighbouring musicians. **[A]**
3. "My head feels as light as a feather since I got away from that chicanery." Munich, 26 September 1777; secondary retellings truncate before "from that chicanery". **[A]**
4. "Mourn with me! This has been the most melancholy day of my life..." To Bullinger, Paris, 3 July 1778. **[A]**
5. "Just as I love Mannheim, so Mannheim loves me..." Mannheim, 12 November 1778. The circulating "As I love Mannheim, Mannheim loves me" is a **later compression**, not this translation. **[A]**

**Verified in a different edition, citation changed:**

6. **The death letter, 4 April 1787.** The famous wording ("As death, when we come to consider it closely, is the true goal of our existence...") is **Anderson's**, and no Anderson text could be fetched. What was fetched is Wallace's Vol. II, Letter 243 (archive.org, `lettersofwolfgan02moza`). The file quotes **Wallace**, verifiable end to end. **[A]** Wallace drops *seit ein paar Jahren*, which Anderson renders; Anderson is the better translation and cannot be cited without a page from a physical copy.
7. **The Kucharz remark.** **Attribution corrected**: the pool cited Niemetschek, but that name appears nowhere in the volume where the text lives, which is **Kerst, *Mozart: The Man and the Artist*, trans. Krehbiel (1906), no. 4** (Gutenberg #4042, fetched), glossed there as a remark to the conductor who led the *Don Giovanni* rehearsals in Prague. The pool's version also **drops Kerst's opening "Moreover"**; the file begins at "It is a mistake", a clean sentence boundary. Still a **secondhand report of speech**, so **[R]**.

**Carried as [R], unverified:** "Stay with me to-night; you must see me die.", reported to **Sophie Haibel** per her later letter to Nissen, set down years after.

**Excluded:** "Melody is the essence of music..." attributed to Michael Kelly's *Reminiscences* (1826). **No fetchable full text found**; widely repeated, unverified, kept out. **[R]** Also cut, though verified **[A]**: two Munich letters of 29 September 1777, when those stops were consolidated.

## Interlocks with existing atlas files

A grep of all 781 `*.journey.json` files and of `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` returned Mozart by name in exactly **three**: `mesmer`, `nadia_boulanger`, `tchaikovsky`. The census has zero mentions. Everything else is inference.

**Named in a campa (2):**

1. **`mesmer`**, *direct relation*. Mozart knew Mesmer; the *Bastien und Bastienne* tradition sits in `mesmer.journey.json`'s own text with the same caveat, and Mozart put a mesmeric magnet on stage in *Così fan tutte* twenty-two years later. The file **inherits the pin byte-identical**, `48.203163, 16.391392`, and the stop's `date_confidence` restates the caveat rather than asserting the staging.
2. **`tchaikovsky`**, *one-directional influence, forward in time*. That file's birth stop is attested for the Votkinsk household orchestrion playing Mozart fragments the child heard before he could name them; *Mozartiana* (1887) reworks **K. 355, K. 592, K. 618, K. 455**. Named on the **Köchel stop**, about the numbering that made such borrowing legible. **The direction is forward and the operator should know it**: see the flag below.

**Real, deliberately kept out of prose (1):** **`nadia_boulanger`**, whose late-life stop has pupils singing Mozart to her in her final teaching months. Born 96 years after his death, this is **her** engagement with **him**, so it is her stop. Carried as a **`suggested_ref`** on the Köchel stop only.

**Checked and negative:** `richard_wagner`, `freud`, `jung`, `paracelsus`, `kircher` do not mention Mozart, and QUEUE.md's note that Wagner and Tchaikovsky are queued alongside him creates none. **Milieu only, correctly unused:** `comte_de_saint_germain` shares Vienna and the courtly/Masonic milieu with **no documented meeting**; `hiram_abiff`, `albert_pike`, `prince_hall`, `theodor_reuss` have **no grep hit**. Shared city and shared institution are not relations; the map already shows co-location.

**Not in the atlas at all**, candidate queue subjects: **Haydn**, **Salieri**, **Da Ponte**, **J. C. Bach**, **Padre Martini**, **Schikaneder**, **Constanze**, **Nannerl**, **Leopold Mozart**. All are named in the file's prose as people in Mozart's own life, which the rule permits; none is a slug yet.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Mozart is **not** on the `EXCEPTIONS.md` list, so no apparatus stops are licensed and none were written. Two items are flagged rather than silently deleted:

1. **Forward, and used: `tchaikovsky` named in the Köchel campa (1862 stop).** Tchaikovsky (1840-1893) postdates Mozart, and under the block rule a strict reading says Mozart's file should carry no pointer to a later musician. I judged it to stand on the same footing as the file's other afterlife material (the Salieri legend, the Köchel catalogue, the failed grave searches, the Requiem completion), which the atlas already carries as reception, and which `abdelkader.journey.json` does too, with the 1966 repatriation inside a campa. It is one sentence, in the afterlife segment, at a place tied to the works. **If the operator reads the block rule as binding on reception too, this is the sentence to cut**, and the interlock count drops to one.
2. **Forward, and not used: `nadia_boulanger`**, kept as a `suggested_ref` only, per the interlock lens's own instruction. Putting it in prose needs the same ruling as item 1.

Nothing backward-facing needed licensing: Mozart is not a theorist and has no framework to apply.

## Gaps stated as gaps

In one place: **why he did not attend Leopold's funeral** (no reason in the record, none supplied); **the lodge premises** (not fixed, so the pin marks central Vienna and says so); **the burial date**, 6 vs 7 December, a live contradiction, not adjudicated; **the cause of death** (over a hundred modern diagnoses, no autopsy, a register entry naming symptoms, no favourite implied); **the Zentralfriedhof cenotaph's date** (unknown, so no year asserted); **Constanze's second marriage to Nissen** (1809 in the pool, day unconfirmed, so not used); **Haydn's remark to Leopold** and the **Hofer / Schikaneder casting** (standard, unconfirmed from the sources fetched, presented as report); **the Basle relationship** (whether it was also sexual is unproven, the letters being the only evidence).
