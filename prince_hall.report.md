# Prince Hall: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `prince_hall`. Companion: `prince_hall.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or contested claim. Gaps and blocked sources are named, not smoothed over.

The research pool carried several errors a verification pass caught. Three are structural: they collapse a three-year Atlantic transit into one point, relocate two of Hall's four surviving public texts into the wrong towns, and date his best-known petition fourteen years early. All three originate in the English Wikipedia article on Prince Hall, the most-copied source on him. This report states the corrections rather than quietly writing the right thing, so a reader comparing it to Wikipedia knows the disagreements are deliberate.

---

## 1. Birth: four years, two islands, no register

No birth record has ever been produced. Four years circulate, each with something behind it, none of them a register:

- **1735**: death notices give his age at death as 72 **[A]**; the Massachusetts Historical Society renders his dates (1735-1807) **[A]**.
- **1737 or 1738**: a letter of Rev. Jeremy Belknap, 1795, gives a *range* **[A]**. The Prince Hall Grand Lodge of Massachusetts adopted 1738 on this basis **[A]**. *Flag:* the pool attributed the single year 1738 to Belknap. The Grand Lodge chose; Belknap did not.
- **c. 1737**: a deposition of 31 August 1807 records his age as "about 70 years" **[A]**. A third data point, pulling against both 1735 and 1748.
- **1748**: William H. Grimshaw, *Official History of Freemasonry Among the Colored People* (1903) **[R]**.

**Birthplace unfixed.** Grimshaw asserts Bridgetown, Barbados, son of an Englishman and a free Black woman of French descent **[R]**; no primary document supports it. Hall in his own printed charges names **New England** as his homeland **[A]**, and James Sidbury reads him as a native of the region **[R, argument not document]**. The Prince Hall Grand Lodge of the Caribbean, at Christ Church, Barbados, keeps the Bridgetown tradition and cites it as fact **[A that they cite it; R as to the fact]**.

**Contradiction left standing.** Grimshaw is the load-bearing source for Barbados and also the source later Masonic scholars warn against; Hinks and others treat him as embellishing and in places fabricating **[R]**. An unreliable sole source is not the same as a false claim. Not adjudicated. The file pins Bridgetown *and* Massachusetts and states the defect of each in its `date_confidence`.

**Coordinate flag.** The pool gave "New England" at 42.4072, -71.3824, the centroid of the **Commonwealth of Massachusetts**, not of New England. That label narrows Hall's own word. The file keeps the coordinate and relabels the place.

## 2. Enslavement, trade, household (c. 1749-1769)

- Charles H. Wesley theorized Hall was enslaved from about age eleven to a Boston tanner named **William Hall**, learning leatherworking in his shop **[R, Wesley, *Prince Hall: Life and Legacy*, 1977]**. The trade is corroborated downstream by the 1777 drumhead record; the enslavement to William Hall is not.
- **1762:** Hall joins a Congregational church in Boston **[A]**.
- **1756, Delia and Primus.** PBS's *Africans in America* and other secondary sources give Hall a wife named Delia and a son, Primus Hall, born 1756 **[R]**. David L. Gray found no records supporting it **[R]**. Primus Hall is independently documented; whose son he was is the open question, not resolved here.
- **1769:** first wife **Sarah Ritchie** (also Ritchery) dies **[A]**. Name and status, nothing further.
- **Later marriages.** **Sylvia (Zilpha) Ward Hall** is executrix of the 1807 estate **[A]**. The sequence of Hall's marriages is contested, notably by Gray **[R]**, and is not ordered here.

## 3. Freedom (1770)

An April 1770 certificate describes Hall as "no longer Reckoned a slave, but always accounted as a free man" **[A, text quoted in the biographical literature; transcribed in Wesley 1977, dated 9 April 1770]**.

**Caveat carried into the file:** Gray notes that **no official Massachusetts manumission record has been located** corroborating the certificate's own text **[R]**. The document says he is free; the state's paper trail for the freeing has not been found.

The same year he marries **Flora Gibbs of Gloucester** **[A]**. Only the year is recorded, so whether the marriage precedes or follows the April certificate is not established. The file says so rather than choosing.

## 4. The lodge refused, the lodge founded (1775-1776)

- Hall and **fourteen** other free Black men petition Boston's **St. John's Lodge** and are **refused** **[A]**.
- **6 March 1775:** the fifteen are initiated by members of **Lodge No. 441, Grand Lodge of Ireland**, a military lodge attached to a British regiment in occupied Boston **[A]**; the initiation is credited to Sergeant John Batt **[R, Wesley]**. **Gap:** the itinerant lodge's meeting building is unrecorded. Handled per the precedent the corpus already sets for Franklin's 1731 initiation in `benjamin_franklin.journey.json`: the event is carried in a Boston stop, not given a false address. Five weeks later, Lexington and Concord.
- **3 July 1776:** they form **African Lodge No. 1**, on the permit, without a charter **[A]**. One day before the Continental Congress declares independence.

**Revolutionary service: [R], probably unresolvable.** At least **six men named Prince Hall** served from Massachusetts **[A, Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War, 1896-1908]**. Whether any is this Prince Hall is "believed, but not certain." Bunker Hill is a genealogists' inference; the file carries it as a Charlestown stop tagged unproven.

**Naming order, corrected.** Wikipedia's *Prince Hall* article says No. 459 later became No. 1. Backwards: the lodge was **No. 1** first (1776, on the permit), chartered **No. 459** (1784), and **reverted to No. 1** after declaring independence from the English register in 1827. Wikipedia's *Prince Hall Freemasonry* article has it right. A contradiction internal to one source, flagged, resolved in favor of the documents.

## 5. The warrant: London 1784, Boston 1787

The largest correction in the pool, and it is geographic.

- **29 September 1784, LONDON:** the **Premier Grand Lodge of England**, under Grand Master H.R.H. the **Duke of Cumberland**, issues the warrant chartering **African Lodge No. 459** **[A]**.
- **Prince Hall never crossed the Atlantic** **[A: no source places him in England]**. The warrant is carried to him, not collected.
- **29 April 1787:** **Captain James Scott** delivers it to Hall in Boston **[A]**.
- **6 May 1787:** African Lodge No. 459 is **constituted at the Golden Fleece on Water Street** **[A]**.

The pool tagged the 1784 charter to "Boston." That one wrong place-field folds a British Grand Master's signature, a three-year sea passage and a captain's errand into a single pin. Corrected to two stops.

**The Golden Fleece was not a waterfront tavern.** It was **Hall's own leather-dressing shop**, "at the sign of the Golden Fleece," on **Water Street**, approximately 42.3575, -71.0565 **[A: MasterMason.com; Medford Historical Society; MWUGL of Florida; PBS, "The lodge met at the sine of the Golden Fleece"]**. The lodge met at its founder's place of business, a different and larger fact than a tavern. The pool's two entries for the same premises, "Hall's leather shop" at 42.3601, -71.0589 and "the Golden Fleece" at 42.3608, -71.0535, were mutually inconsistent. The file uses one coordinate for both.

## 6. Petitions to the General Court (1777-1788)

**a) 1777, abolition [A].** Hall joins a petition for abolition in the state **[A, Massachusetts Archives]**.

**b) 1787, emigration, seventy-three signers [A]. Not January 1773.** PBS, *Africans in America / The Boston Plan*: "In 1787, a committee of twelve of the African Lodge, headed by Grand Master Prince Hall, drafted a petition to the General Court (state legislature) of Massachusetts. The document was signed by seventy-three 'African Blacks' who proposed a detailed plan 'to return to Africa, our native country...'" **[A]**.

The pool carried this as **January 1773**, tagged **[A]**, in two lenses. The Wikipedia sentence it came from is doubly defective: it also refers the petition to the "Massachusetts Senate," a body that did not exist in 1773, created only by the 1780 state constitution (the colonial upper house was the Governor's Council). The seventy-three signers fix the document to 1787. **Corrected.**

**c) 1787, schools [A].** A committee of twelve led by Hall petitions for access to public schooling for Black children on a **"taxation without representation"** argument **[A]**. It fails, as does a second attempt; Hall then teaches out of his own house **[R, Wesley; the address has not survived]**. The pool had merged this with (d) under one 1788 date. **Split.**

**d) 27 February 1788, kidnapping [A].** Hall petitions against the seizure of free Black Bostonians for sale in the Caribbean **[A: Massachusetts Archives; printed in *The American Museum, or Universal Magazine*, vol. 3, Philadelphia: Mathew Carey, 1788; manuscript transcription at Massachusetts Historical Society, item 670]**.

The pool's "irons" passage was a paraphrase presented as verbatim (it opened "The petitioners describe the kidnapping" and read "he put them in irons," neither of which is in the source), and its seamen passage silently dropped the clause "and some of them we have heard from." Both are restored to the printed text in the journey file's quote fields. The MHS manuscript preserves Hall's phonetic spelling of the same lines. **Citation flag:** the pool cited *American Museum* pp. 410-411; the passages sit at **scan pp. 414-415** of Internet Archive item `americanmuseumor03care`. Verify the printed folio before publishing the citation.

## 7. The Charges (1792, 1797), both delivered outside Boston

**25 June 1792, CHARLESTOWN.** Title page: "A Charge Delivered to the Brethren of the African Lodge on the 25th of June, 1792. At the Hall of Brother William Smith, in Charlestown." **[A: catalog records at the American Antiquarian Society, University of Michigan, NYPL, Oxford Text Archive]**. Charlestown was a separate municipality until 1874; approximately 42.3782, -71.0602.

**24 June 1797, MENOTOMY.** Title page: "A Charge, delivered to the African Lodge, June 24, 1797, at Menotomy." **[A: Open Library and Internet Archive catalog records]**. Menotomy is present-day **Arlington**, approximately 42.4154, -71.1565, about ten kilometres northwest of the coordinates the pool gave.

This is not pedantry about pins. The most-quoted sentence in the 1797 Charge is about the daily insults met with **in the streets of Boston**. Hall is speaking about Boston from outside it, to a lodge assembled in a country town.

**That sentence, corrected.** The version in wide circulation, including on Wikipedia, is first person ("we could not bear up under the daily insults we meet with"). The pamphlet is **second person** throughout:

> "Patience I say, for were we not possess'd of a great measure of it you could not bear up under the daily insults you meet with in the streets of Boston..."

**[A: Internet Archive scan, item `tract-c-74-charge-delivered-african-lodge`]**. The file quotes the pamphlet in full; the modernized text is flagged, not used.

Two further repairs from the same scan: "the charity or love to all mankind" reads "**and** charity or love to all mankind," and "how necessary it is to have a fellow feeling" reads "how **becoming and** necessary."

**And one on 1792.** "Ethiopia shall stretch forth her hands" is present but not in that form: "Hear what the great Architect of the universal world saith, **Aethiopia** shall stretch forth her hands **unto me**" **[A, Evans-TCP N18755]**. Hall's own rendering, not KJV Psalm 68:31 ("Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto God"). The often-confused 1797 line is different again: "Thus doth Ethiopia begin to stretch forth her **hand**, from a sink of slavery to freedom and equality," singular.

**Content [A]:** 1792 addresses abolition in Massachusetts and Masonic duty; 1797 addresses mob violence against Black Bostonians and praises the **Haitian Revolution**.

## 8. Expansion, office, death (1791-1808)

- **1791:** named **Provincial Grand Master** **[A]**.
- **22 March 1797:** organizes a lodge under his warrant in **Philadelphia** **[A]**. Hall unidentified.
- **25 June 1797:** organizes in **Providence** what becomes **Hiram Lodge #3** **[A]**. Also unidentified.
- **early 1800s:** tradition places the lodge in a room on **Kirby Street**, Boston **[R: neither the date of the move nor the site is established in surviving minutes]**.
- **4 December 1807:** Hall dies in Boston **[A]**. Estate inventoried at **$47.22**, no real property, Sylvia Ward Hall executrix **[A, Suffolk County probate]**. Buried at **Copp's Hill Burying Ground** **[A]**.
- **24 June 1808:** the brethren organize the **African Grand Lodge**, uniting Boston, Philadelphia and Providence **[A]**.

**December 4 versus December 7.** The 1835 stone reads "Died Dec. 7, 1807"; the death record gives 4 December. The pool called this an unresolved discrepancy in one lens and a carving error in another. Contemporary newspaper accounts report that Hall died December 4 and was buried three days later **[A]**, so the stone most likely carries the burial date. Stated as the likely explanation, not as proof. The file dates death to the 4th and burial to the 7th, which is what the two documents jointly say.

**Coordinate contradiction inside the pool.** Its geography lens gave Copp's Hill as 42.3673, -71.0558, its afterlife lens 42.3656, -71.0552, some 200 m south in the street grid. The correct figure is **42.367322, -71.055844**; the file uses the geography lens.

## 9. Afterlife

- **24 June 1835:** monument erected at the grave **[A]**, inscribed "Here lies ye body of Prince Hall, first Grand Master of the colored Grand Lodge in Mass. Died Dec. 7, 1807." **[A, verbatim]**.
- **1871:** Albert Pike's *Morals and Dogma* rewrites the Hiramic legend as comparative-religion myth from the Southern Jurisdiction's seat **[A per `hiram_abiff.journey.json`]**. Pike's Scottish Rite did not recognize Prince Hall Freemasonry **[R: the racial split is not stated in the corpus file, it is inferred]**.
- **1903:** Grimshaw's *Official History*, later judged embellished and in places fabricated **[R]**.
- **May 2010:** **Ted Clausen's** Prince Hall Monument is unveiled on **Cambridge Common**, beside the Washington Elm and the Revolutionary War cannons, at **42.37636, -71.12125** **[A, Wikipedia "Prince Hall Monument"]**. The pool's geography lens dated it **1998** and its afterlife lens called the date unconfirmed: two lenses, two answers, both wrong. Its coordinate, 42.3736, -71.1097, is a kilometre southeast and is in fact **the corpus's canonical Cambridge pin for Washington's headquarters**, borrowed by accident.
- **2021:** Danielle Allen, "A Forgotten Black Founding Father," *The Atlantic* **[A]**.
- **2026:** **47 of 51** mainstream U.S. Grand Lodges recognize Prince Hall Grand Lodges as regular; **Mississippi, Louisiana, South Carolina, West Virginia** hold out **[A, Wikipedia "Prince Hall Freemasonry"]**.
- **Barbados:** a bust stands at the **Prince Hall Masonic Centre, Graeme Hall, Christ Church**, approximately **13.0745, -59.5760** **[A that it exists]**. The pool gave 13.1939, -59.5432, in the uplands 14 km inland from any Grand Lodge building, tagged **[A]**. Corrected. **No reachable source gives a year of erection**, so the file's date field carries the survey year and says so.

## 10. Corpus interlocks

Canonical pins inherited byte-identical from the files named.

- `hiram_abiff.journey.json`, **Goose and Gridiron, 51.5138, -0.0984**: four London lodges constitute the Grand Lodge of England in 1717, the body whose warrant Hall petitions in 1784 **[A at both ends]**.
- `benjamin_franklin.journey.json`, **Philadelphia, 39.9526, -75.1652**: Franklin reprints Anderson's *Constitutions* in 1734, carrying the Hiramic legend into the colonies, and is initiated at St John's Lodge in 1731, forty-four years before African Lodge **[A]**. Used for the 1797 Philadelphia stop in preference to the pool's 39.9496, -75.1503.
- `george_washington.journey.json`, **Cambridge, 42.3736, -71.1097**: Washington holds the siege headquarters July 1775 to April 1776, encircling the British-held Boston whose garrison lodge had initiated Hall that March **[A at both ends; the juxtaposition is the atlas's]**.
- `hiram_abiff.journey.json`, **Charleston, 32.7765, -79.9311**: the Scottish Rite Southern Jurisdiction constitutes itself 1801 **[A for each date; R for the connection, temporal overlap only]**.
- `hiram_abiff.journey.json`, **Washington, D.C., 38.9126, -77.0361**: Pike 1871, and the reading lineage running on into `rene_guenon.journey.json` and `theodor_reuss.journey.json` **[A per the corpus file; R as to any bearing on Prince Hall Freemasonry]**.
- `solomon.journey.json`: named, not pinned. The Temple and its two pillars are what the third-degree ritual points back to, and Prince Hall's lodges use the same furniture **[R]**.

**Examined and rejected.** `mikhail_bakunin.journey.json` (grep hit "mason") and `isadora_duncan.journey.json` (grep hit "boston") carry no Prince Hall-era connection: a search dead end, recorded rather than dressed up. `san_martin.journey.json` and `alvear.journey.json` share the 1717 London machinery at four removes; no contact claimed, no stop written. `QUEUE.md` line 227 names Prince Hall as joining the corpus's Masonic material "from its Black-American branch," alongside `hiram_abiff` and the queued Albert Pike; Pike has no journey file yet and is named only as a figure inside `hiram_abiff.journey.json`.
