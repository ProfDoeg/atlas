# Constantine XI Palaiologos: research report

*Slug `constantine_xi_palaiologos`. Compiled 2026-08-28 from the verified research pool, whose five lenses had each already been run adversarially against their own dossier. Nothing is resolved here that the sources leave open.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or an inference the record permits but does not compel. "The article" is the Wikipedia page named in the same sentence, fetched 2026-08-28.

## Two structural warnings

1. **The Sphrantzes problem.** The authentic *Chronicon Minus* is terse. The long *Chronicon Maius*, source of the famous farewell speech of 28 May 1453, was written in the sixteenth century by Makarios Melissenos and separated from the genuine text only by scholarship from 1934 onward **[A]**. No sentence of that oration is used in the file.
2. **Two misattributed quotations, removed.** The dossier carried "the city will take me, dead or alive" and "The city is all I want, even if it is empty" as Constantine's, from Leonard of Chios. Both are **Mehmed II's**, from the Sultan's ultimatum via Doukas as rendered by Nicol, whose next sentence is "Constantine did not trouble to reply" **[A: Nicol, myriobiblos.gr]**.

## 1. Birth and the first regency (1404-1425)

Born **8 February**, Constantinople, fourth surviving son of Manuel II Palaiologos and Helena Dragas **[A]**. The **day** is firm, the **year contested between 1404 and 1405** and left so; the birthplace within the city is undocumented, Blachernae being inference **[R]**. Sphrantzes gives the lifespan as "forty-nine years, three months, and twenty days" **[A: De Re Militari]**, while the chronology lens reports the same passage as **49 years, 4 months, 20 days** and uses it to favour 1404. **Contradiction flagged**, and a lever on the birth year.

**The 1422 siege.** That article names Manuel II and John VIII as defending commanders and **does not place the seventeen-or-eighteen-year-old Constantine at the walls at all** **[A on the silence]**. No stop written; a gap left as one.

**First regency, corrected.** Not November 1423 to February 1424: John VIII "returned from his journey in November 1424 after failing to procure help," so it ran **roughly a full year, with the February 1424 peace inside it** **[A]**. Manuel II died **21 July 1425**; John VIII became senior emperor **[A]**.

## 2. The Thracian appanage (from 1425)

Three separated places: **Mesembria** (Nesebar, restored to Byzantine hands by the 1403 Treaty of Gallipoli), **Selymbria** (Silivri), **Derkos** **[A]**. Derkos's identification with the Terkos/Durusu lake fortress is standard gazetteer usage that **could not be re-verified by direct fetch** **[R]**.

## 3. The Morea won (1427-1432)

**26 December 1427:** reaches **Mystras** with John VIII's expedition against Carlo I Tocco **[A]**.

**Echinades, corrected to 1427.** The chronology lens said 1428; the article dates the skirmish to **1427**, the **transfer of Glarentza following 1 May 1428** **[A]**. The dossier contradicted itself: its geography lens had 1427.

**1 July 1428, Glarentza:** marriage to **Maddalena Tocco**, renamed **Theodora**; proclaimed despot about this date **[A]**. **Coordinates corrected** to 37.9400, 21.1371, about 1.6 km northwest of the dossier's pin. **1428-1430: Chlemoutsi** becomes residence and base for the Patras campaign **[A]**.

**From about 20 March 1429, Patras:** lower city surrenders, citadel holds. **His horse is shot under him; Sphrantzes is captured** saving him **[A: timeline]**, though the Patras Castle article **does not narrate that incident** **[R on it]**.

**1429, Glarentza:** Catalans seize it; Constantine ransoms it back and razes the fortifications **[A]**. **Two corrections:** the article dates the demolition to **1429**, not 1429-1430, and **gives no sum**; the dossier's **6,000 Venetian ducats is not in the cited page** and is not printed in the file. **November 1429: Theodora dies**, buried at Glarentza, later translated to Mystras **[A]**; **the receiving church is not established** **[R]**.

**July 1430:** the Patras citadel surrenders, ending two centuries of Latin rule; Sphrantzes later governs it **[A]**. **March 1432:** exchange with Thomas, Constantine taking **Kalavryta** and Thomas **Elis** **[A; not re-verified]**.

## 4. Regent for the union, two widowings (1437-1443)

**Second regency, corrected.** Not November 1437: he reached Constantinople **24 September 1437**, two months before John VIII sailed; the **February 1440** end date stands **[A]**. The **Union of Florence** was proclaimed **6 July 1439**; Constantine was in Constantinople, inherited its consequences, and **never signed** **[A]**. No stop puts him in Italy.

**December 1440:** Sphrantzes sent to **Lesbos** to arrange the second marriage **[A]**. **August 1441, Mytilene:** marriage to **Caterina Gattilusio**, daughter of Dorino I **[A]**; that he left her with her family is pool synthesis, **not re-verified** **[R]**. **April 1442:** Demetrios attacks Constantinople with Ottoman backing; Constantine, summoned, is **blockaded at Lemnos** **[A]**.

**August 1442, two corrections.** She died **at Palaiokastron on the north of Lemnos**, not at Myrina, which is the **burial** place **[A: "Caterina Gattilusio"]**. The cause is not a late inference: **Sphrantzes, who negotiated the marriage, records that she fell ill and suffered a miscarriage that August** **[A]**; the dossier's hedge understated the record. No precise coordinate for Palaiokastron; the file uses an approximate north-coast pin and says so.

**November 1442:** he reaches the capital after the attack has already failed **[A]**. **November 1443:** Theodore II gives up the Morea for Selymbria; Constantine takes up residence at **Mystras**, ruling with Thomas **[A]**.

## 5. Despot at Mystras (1443-1448)

**March 1444:** the **Hexamilion** rebuilt across the Isthmus after coercive fiscal and labour demands on Moreot landowners **[A]**. **Coordinates corrected** to 37.9261, 22.9725, about 1.4 km west of the dossier's pin.

**Summer 1444, corrected.** The dossier claimed **Athens and Thebes**. He did not take Athens: Nerio II Acciaioli "lost Thebes to Constantine and was forced to pay him tribute and become his vassal," keeping the duchy, and Murad helped him retake Thebes in 1446 **[A: "Nerio II Acciaioli"]**. The geography lens had it right. **Thebes only.**

**10 November 1444, Varna. Constantine was not there**; he was campaigning in central Greece and afterwards refused Ottoman demands to withdraw **[A]**. The stop stands where he stood. **End November to 10 December 1446:** Murad breaches the rebuilt wall with artillery within days; the brothers escape to Mystras and accept overlordship, tribute, and a ban on rebuilding **[A]**. **June 1448:** Theodore II dies **[A]**.

## 6. Succession and the strait closed (1448-1452)

**31 October 1448:** John VIII dies childless **[A]**. **6 December 1448: Murad II assents** to the succession **[A]**; Constantine is not recorded travelling to the Ottoman court, the business was done by envoys, and the file says so rather than staging him there. **6 January 1449, Mystras:** proclaimed and invested; **no patriarch crowned him, and it is unclear whether a formal coronation took place at all** **[A]**. Helena Dragas had arbitrated the claim against Demetrios **[A]**. **12 March 1449:** enters Constantinople on a Catalan vessel **[A]**; **the landing point is undocumented** **[R]**. **23 March 1450:** Helena Dragas, the nun Hypomone, dies **[A]**.

**3 February 1451:** Murad II dies; **Mehmed II succeeds at nineteen**; envoys renew the peace **[A]**. **15 April to August 1452: Rumeli Hisari** built opposite Anadolu Hisari; Constantine protests by embassy, is ignored, briefly shuts the gates and detains Ottoman subjects; in November the guns sink a Venetian grain ship **[A]**. **He himself is not recorded at the site**: the embassy is his, the journey is not. **26 October 1452: Isidore of Kiev** arrives with **Leonard of Chios** and about **200 Neapolitan archers** **[A]**. **12 December 1452, Hagia Sophia:** the union proclaimed and the pope commemorated, Constantine participating, over the objection of Gennadios Scholarios **[A]**.

## 7. The siege (April to May 1453)

**A conflation corrected.** The dossier put both the encampment and Constantine's taking position at the Mesoteichion on **2 April**. The article separates them: the army encamped on 2 April, but "On 5 April, the Sultan himself arrived with his last troops, and the defenders took up their positions." Siege proper: **6 April to 29 May** **[A]**.

**2 April:** the **chain** raised across the Golden Horn, holding until Mehmed hauls ships overland behind Galata on **22 April** **[A]**. **5 April onward:** command at the **Mesoteichion**, where the walls cross the Lycus, with **Giustiniani Longo** redeployed there from the northern gates **[A/R: the geography lens tags the joint command R]**.

**Barbaro's record of the address to the Venetian colony** gives two verbatim lines from a single speech, the appeal beginning "Since the whole of Christendom has been unwilling to help me" and the order to send a gripo toward Negroponte **[A: Barbaro, De Re Militari]**. Eyewitness rendering of reported speech. The file splits them across two stops and says in the second that they are one address.

**The returning ship, and a citation that does not hold.** The dossier dated the return to **23 May**, citing "Fall of Constantinople." **That article instead says** "a small Venetian fleet of 12 ships... reached Constantinople on 27 May." The **23 May single-brigantine** story (twelve volunteers out on 3 May under Ottoman colours, voting to return rather than flee) is **Barbaro via Runciman**, not that page. **Printed, not resolved:** the file carries the brigantine, credits Barbaro and Runciman, and names the 27 May alternative in `date_confidence`.

**Late May:** the surrender demand rejected, reportedly with a reply that the decision to die had been taken collectively; **the wording survives only through later authored narrative** **[A on the tradition, R on the wording]**. **28 May: Vespers in Hagia Sophia**, Latin and Greek clergy together, Constantine present **[A]**. The farewell to the household at Blachernae comes through **Leonard of Chios** and its later expansion: pattern credible, intimate detail unverifiable **[R]**. The tradition that he prayed the same night at St Theodosia (later the Gul Mosque) may derive from that church's rose-decked feast falling on the eve of the assault **[R]**; a suggested reference, not a scene.

## 8. The death, and the accounts that disagree

Died **29 May 1453**, most probably in the **Mesoteichion / St Romanos** sector. **The body was never securely identified**; the article states flatly that "his fate remains unknown" **[A: Sphrantzes, Barbaro, Doukas, Kritovoulos and the Ottoman chroniclers agree only that he died fighting]**. The versions, unadjudicated:

- **Killed in the fighting, terse, no scene.** Sphrantzes, *Chronicon Minus*. **[A]**, the reliable core.
- **"he was caught up in the melee and fell. He got up, only to fall again, and he was trampled underfoot."** Leonard of Chios to Nicholas V, 16 August 1453, via Nicol. **[A]**, and closest to the event on Nicol's reading.
- **Cries that the city is taken and charges.** Kritovoulos, via Nicol. **[A]** as a quotation of Nicol's rendering, **[R]** as history: a pro-Mehmed non-eyewitness in Thucydidean manner; his own text could not be fetched.
- **"Is there no Christian here to take my head from me?"** Doukas, via Nicol. Same secondhand caveat.
- **He hanged himself** at the San Romano breach. Barbaro, reporting camp rumour: **[A]** that he reports it, **[R]** as fact; he is explicit it is what "was said."
- **Killed near the sea or the Golden Gate by a marine who did not know him, and beheaded.** Tursun Beg, Ibn Kemal. **[R]**; hostile, and at odds with the Greek and Latin traditions.
- **He stripped off the purple to die as an ordinary soldier.** **[R]**. No source secures it, and the identification reports conflict: some say the body was known by purple shoes worked with eagles, others that it was never recognized.

**The Kerkoporta.** Doukas reports a postern near tower 96 left open by accident, admitting fifty Ottoman soldiers. **Modern excavation has found no corresponding gate in the inner wall**, and current opinion puts the decisive breach at the Mesoteichion after Giustiniani's withdrawal **[A on the doubt]**. Constantine was not there: a source on his own sector stop, **not a scene**.

## 9. Afterlife

**Nicol's conclusion:** the grave was "nowhere to be found"; the body probably went into a common grave, and the Istanbul tombs shown to tourists were likely guides' inventions **[A: Nicol, myriobiblos.gr]**.

**Three competing graves, none confirmed.** (i) **Gul Camii** (former St Theodosia, Fatih), reported by the Patriarch in **1844**, which the building's own record calls nineteenth-century and groundless **[R]**; **coordinates harmonized to 41.0267, 28.9565**, about 800 m from the afterlife lens's pin, which disagreed with its own geography lens. (ii) **Sulu Manastir** (Peribleptos), Kocamustafapasa, per **Evliya Celebi** **[R]**. (iii) **Vefa Kilise Camii**, whose **own article mentions neither Constantine XI nor any burial tradition**, so this rests on Nicol alone **[R]**; coordinates corrected to 41.0164, 28.9603.

**The Marble Emperor.** An angel turns him to marble and hides him near the **Golden Gate**, to wake and restore the empire; the legend develops from the late fifteenth century and fuses with the Megali Idea **[R]**. **Coordinates harmonized to 40.9933, 28.9231** (Yedikule), about 250 m from the afterlife lens's pin. **Isidore of Kiev**, summer 1453, gives the symmetry it rests on: a city founded by a Constantine son of Helena, lost by another **[A: Nicol's rendering]**.

**Coinage.** A hoard published 1991 held **no fewer than 90 coins of Constantine XI**, revealing the unrecorded **half-stavraton**, undocumented control-marks, and a die-link with a coin of John VIII **[A: *Revue numismatique* 1991]**. **Find-site not given.** Gap.

**No stop for later hands' work** (see the apparatus section). The **2024 fresco** at the **Holy Monastery of the Taxiarchs**, Aigialeia is mid-fifteenth-century: crowned, cruciform sceptre, purple cloak with double-headed eagles. The painter's sight of him at Mystras is the officials' **inference**, and the lens's coordinate is **Aigio town centre, not the monastery** **[A: Archaeology.org, 2024]**. It is a suggested reference on the Mystras investiture stop. Biography: Nicol, *The Immortal Emperor*, 1992 **[A]**.

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

**Birth year** 1404 against 1405. **Lifespan:** "three months" against "4 months," two renderings of one Sphrantzes passage. **The relief ship:** one brigantine on 23 May (Barbaro via Runciman) against twelve on 27 May (Wikipedia). **The death:** last charge (Kritovoulos, Doukas, Leonard) against hanging (Barbaro's rumour) against an unknowing marine near the Golden Gate (Tursun Beg, Ibn Kemal). **The body:** known by purple shoes worked with eagles, or never recognized. **The grave:** Gul Camii, Sulu Manastir, Vefa, or Nicol's common grave and none. **The Kerkoporta:** Doukas's postern against the archaeological absence of any such gate.

## Gaps stated as gaps

**The 1422 siege:** his role, if any, undocumented. **Palaiokastron, Lemnos:** no precise coordinate; the file uses an approximate north-coast pin and says so. **Theodora Tocco's grave at Mystras:** church unidentified. **The landing point, 12 March 1449:** undocumented. **Derkos:** gazetteer identification not re-fetched. **The 1991 hoard's find-site:** absent from the text retrieved. **The Aigialeia monastery's coordinates:** Greek Wikipedia 404. **Kritovoulos and Doukas in their own texts:** used only through Nicol.

**Western diplomacy.** The dossier asserts embassies to Venice, Genoa, the papacy, Aragon-Naples and Hungary before 1453. **No corroborating source turned up for the Hunyadi, Naples or papal legs**; the search budget was exhausted. Only the October 1452 Isidore arrival is attested, and it alone is in the file. **Buenos Aires:** the canonical-geography list suggested a Greek-diaspora Marmaromenos Vasilias link licensing the pin at -34.6037, -58.3816. **Unverified; no source, no file. The pin is not used.**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None require the exception, and Constantine XI is not on the EXCEPTIONS.md list.** Nothing has been silently deleted.

- **Backward direction: nothing.** He is not a theorist and has no framework to apply to earlier material.
- **Forward direction, caught and kept.** Relations running from later people toward him, which would be forward references if staged as scenes: **Cusa's** *De pace fidei* and *De visione Dei*, written in the Tirol on news of the fall; the **relics of Saint Pantaleon** carried out by Armenian refugees in 1453 to Porto; **Mehmed II's** demolition of the Holy Apostles around 1461 and the iron hoops later binding Constantine the Great's burnt porphyry column; the **2024 fresco**, the **Athens statue**, the **1991 hoard**, the **2012 film**. All are `sources` or `suggested_refs` on his own stops; none is a scene.
- **Two travellers are named in a campa, both on a relation and not on shared pavement.** **Constantine the Great** (`constantino_el_grande`), whose name Constantine XI bears and whose city and office he holds as the last of the line, a symmetry Isidore of Kiev drew in his own lifetime. **Nicholas of Cusa** (`nicholas_of_cusa`), whose 1437 delegation came to arrange the council whose union Constantine as regent inherited and as emperor proclaimed in 1452, and who answered the fall with *De pace fidei*. **No meeting between them is attested**, and the file says so in `date_confidence`; if the operator judges this too thin, it is one clause and cuts cleanly.
- **Deliberately not named**, on the shared-geography rule: **Empress Theodora** (her Hagia Sophia is the building he prayed in, but there is no relation between the persons), **Marco Polo**, **Ibn Battuta** (received in 1332 by Andronikos III, Constantine's great-grandfather, which is a relation to Andronikos), **Piri Reis**.
