# Jakob Fugger "the Rich" (1459-1525): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-30 from the verified research pool, adversarial corrections folded in. Slug: `jakob_fugger_the_rich`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim whose chain does not reach a document. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is printed and left standing.

---

## 1. Augsburg and the Fondaco, 1459-1487

**[A]** Born 6 March 1459 at Augsburg, tenth of eleven children of Jakob Fugger the Elder and Barbara Bäsinger (Deutsche Biographie gnd118694022; en.wikipedia). The father, who had moved the family from the weavers' guild into the merchants', dies 1469 **[A]**.

**[R]** Older biographies place the boy in minor orders with a prebend at the Herrieden collegiate foundation, some lodging him there as resident canon until 1478. *Correction:* de.wikipedia reports that post-2009 research refutes the residency, documents showing him represent the family at Venice from 1473. The first-pass coordinate (49.1964, 10.8879) is wrong by roughly 30 km; Herrieden is at 49.2331, 10.4978.

**[A]** Brother Peter dies at Nuremberg 1473, brother Markus at Rome 1478 (de.wikipedia). *Tag split:* the deaths are [A]; the causal claim that Peter's death turned Jakob from church to counting house is traditional and stays **[R]**.

**[A]** From 1473, aged fourteen, trained through the Fondaco dei Tedeschi at Venice, in Italian bookkeeping, bills of exchange, credit assessment, and the fustian trade; resident for most of the period to 1487 (en.wikipedia; Fugger Foundation). **[A]** At Rome for commercial purposes by 1478 (Deutsche Biographie), with Markus in the papal supplications chancery from 1471.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The chronology lens carries a 1487 "return from Venice" as a leadership marker; Deutsche Biographie gives the Innsbruck factory from 1485. Both cannot be first. The file follows 1485 and says so in `date_confidence`; 1487 survives only as the end of the Venetian residence.

## 2. Tyrolean silver, 1480-1494

**[A]** Mining in the Gastein and Schladming areas from 1480 (Fugger Foundation): the first working of the model Jakob extends everywhere, unsecured loans to cash-poor mine owners converted into Kuxe and rights to output. **[A]** Leads the Innsbruck factory from 1485 (Deutsche Biographie). **[A]** By 1488 the firm handles all silver production at Schwaz (Deutsche Biographie; Fugger Foundation chronology, reached through secondary compilation, the primary page not opening).

**[R]** First meeting with Maximilian, traditionally at the Frankfurt fair of 1489 (Fugger Foundation); the dossier calls the dating traditional, not documented. **[A]** 16 March 1490: Sigismund of Tyrol abdicates the county to Maximilian, who assumes the debts and promises the Augsburgers repayment (en.wikipedia). Fugger's presence at the act is not documented; he had run Innsbruck for five years and held the paper.

**[R]** Loans to Sigismund said to have bought metal access, export concessions, and influence over the Hall mint. Too thin for a stop; folded into Innsbruck and Schwaz.

**[A/R]** 1494: the brothers constitute "Ulrich Fugger and Brothers of Augsburg," described by en.wikipedia as one of the first open trading companies in Europe **[A]**. Jakob's 15,552-guilder contribution under the first agreement is **[R]**, from the Fugger Foundation's partnership-record review, not a document seen.

## 3. Copper, pepper, and the Curia, 1494-1510

**[A]** Partnership with Johann (Jan) Thurzo the Elder of Kraków, dated 1494-1495 across the sources; the copper company founded 1495 at Neusohl (Banská Bystrica), with drainage, smelting, and Saiger separation at industrial scale (Fugger Foundation; en.wikipedia).

**[A, coordinate and date corrected]** Fuggerau. The first pass gave 46.6111, 13.8558 ("near Villach") and "early 1500s"; both wrong. de.wikipedia places the works at 46.5481, 13.6935 near Arnoldstein, built from 1495, with furnaces, hammer mill, brass foundry, and cannon works; the Fuggers quit Hungarian mining in 1547 and sold the site back to Arnoldstein monastery in 1570 for 2,500 gulden. Upgraded from [R] to [A].

**[R]** Married Sibylle Arzt, 29 January 1498 (en.wikipedia; Fugger Foundation). The pool tags it [R]; treated as well transmitted, not as a document read. **[R]** Encyclopedia.com: he held no office in Augsburg's government.

**[A]** 1503: Lisbon factor's house, Casa da Índia permission for pepper, spices, pearls, gems; **[A]** 1505, about 3,000 guilders into a Portuguese India voyage with Augsburg, Nuremberg, and Genoese partners. Folded into one stop. **[A, weakly]** 1505-1506: the firm helps recruit and transport the first Swiss Guard to Rome under Julius II; the Guard's founding is well documented, the Fugger transport detail rests on the dossier and was not re-verified.

**[A, span corrected]** The Roman mint. First pass: "c.1508-1524, with interruptions." en.wikipedia ("Fugger"): "From 1508 to 1515, they leased the Roman mint." Corrected span used; the mint-building coordinate (41.9024, 12.4658) remains **[R]**. **Rejected, not merely omitted:** a Wikipedia-repeated claim of a c.1500 Fugger loan toward St Peter's and the Sistine Chapel, which the pool flags as chronologically confused, the chapel having been built in the 1470s-80s before Jakob led the firm.

**[A]** 1510: after Georg (d. 1506) and Ulrich (d. 1510), Jakob takes sole leadership of the whole firm (Fugger Foundation).

## 4. Lordship and title, 1507-1515

**[A, coordinate corrected]** 15 July 1507: Maximilian sells him Kirchberg with Weißenhorn, Wullenstetten, and Pfaffenhofen for about 50,000 guilders, financing the Italian expedition (en.wikipedia; Deutsche Biographie; Historisches Lexikon Bayerns). Kirchberg an der Iller is at 48.1322, 10.0806, not the first pass's 48.1167, 10.1167, about 3.5 km off. **[A]** Weißenhorn acquired with it; a Fugger palace built there later; the town's quiet in 1525 attributed to Fugger support for its textile trade (Fugger Foundation), a traditional explanation rather than a documented one.

**[A, dates sharpened]** The Fugger Chapel at St Anna: contract 4 April 1509, papal confirmation 19 November 1509, built 1509/10-1512 (cartouches carry MDXII), consecrated 17 January 1518 (de.wikipedia, "Fuggerkapelle"); Dürer, Burgkmair, Breu, Adolf Daucher. Coordinate corrected to 48.3678, 10.8953 (first pass: 10.8974).

**[A]** 8 May 1511, imperial nobility; **[A]** 17 July 1514, imperial count, the "first German merchant so raised" claim credited in the pool to the Neue Deutsche Biographie, not consulted directly. **[A]** 1512-1515: the Fuggerhäuser built on the Weinmarkt, expanded 1523. **Coordinate conflict, see section 8.**

**[A]** 1514: about 20,000 guilders to Albrecht of Brandenburg for the dispensations to hold Mainz alongside his other sees, repayment structured through indulgence revenue (en.wikipedia). This runs straight into the Ninety-Five Theses three years later.

**[A, but not Fugger's stop]** 1515: Johannes Eck, sponsored by Fugger, argues at Bologna for the legitimacy of certain five-percent commercial deposit returns under canon law, defending the firm against the usury prohibition. Eck was at Bologna; Fugger was not. Under the standing rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, this is Eck's travel. It appears in `suggested_refs` on the Augsburg loan stop, not as a scene.

**Downgraded on verification.** The geography lens placed Jakob personally at the 1515 Vienna congress, at the Hofburg. The congress opened at Pressburg and concluded at Vienna, its double wedding of 22 July 1515 in St Stephen's rather than the Hofburg; and neither en.wikipedia's article on it nor Deutsche Biographie puts Fugger there. Stop dropped.

## 5. Illness, indulgence, and the hearing of 1518

**[A]** 1516: the Fuggerei begun (en.wikipedia; Fugger Foundation). *Coordinate corrected:* the two lenses disagreed with each other, 48.3667/10.9022 and 48.3729/10.9029, and both were wrong; de.wikipedia gives 48.3688, 10.9041. Content confirmed: 52 houses, 102 dwellings, 1516-1523.

**[A]** Summer 1517: Fugger and the provost of Bamberg taken gravely ill visiting Prince-Bishop Christoph von Stadion at Dillingen. Sole direct record: a letter of the canon Bernhard Adelmann von Adelmannsfelden, 19 July 1517 (Fugger Foundation fact-check). **[R]** Poison rumours, later noted by Pölnitz; no contemporary source names a poison, a culprit, or a motive, and the Foundation concludes natural illness, food poisoning, and deliberate attack cannot be distinguished.

**[A]** 1517: Leo X grants patronage rights at St Moritz, Augsburg. *Gap:* no verified coordinate for St Moritz in the pool, so the stop uses the city pin and says so.

**[A for the venue]** October 1518: Cajetan examines Luther over three days in the Fuggerhäuser, where the cardinal lodges for the diet. Jakob's participation is unattested; Pölnitz argues he kept away. The file's strongest interlock: `martin_luther.journey.json` states it at its own Augsburg pin, inherited byte-identical here.

**[A, with a live date dispute]** 28 June 1519: Charles V elected king of the Romans at Frankfurt, unanimously. Deutsche Biographie describes over 850,000 concentrated there; the Fugger share is roughly 543,000 of about 852,000 in one lens, over 500,000 of about 850,000 in another. **Contradiction:** en.wikipedia dates the election 28 July, flagged by the dossier as a documented error for 28 June, which the file uses. Jakob's own presence at Frankfurt is not documented; the firm's agents and money were there.

**Not Fugger's stops.** The Ninety-Five Theses (Wittenberg, 1517), Maximilian's death at Wels (1519), and Charles V's Aachen coronation (1520) are all in the pool. None is a place Fugger stood; none is staged.

## 6. The bridle, the charter, and the letter, 1520-1523

**[A, wording corrected]** 1520: Luther, in *An den christlichen Adel deutscher Nation*, calls for a bridle on the Fuggers. The first pass could not find the passage; it is in the treatise. English Wikisource (Harvard Classics vol. 36, 1910): "Doubtless we should also find some bridle for the Fuggers and similar companies." *Correction:* de.wikipedia's modernized German gives the singular, "dem Fugger," framing it as a personal attack on Jakob by name; Luther writes the plural, and de.wikipedia's footnote for the line is not Luther but Aloys Schulte (1904). The file quotes the English and does not say Luther attacked Fugger by name.

**[A, source traced]** The Tyrolean chronicler on Schwaz. de.wikipedia's raw wikitext shows the footnote is Eike Eberhard Unger, *Die Fugger in Hall i.T.* (Tübingen, 1967), which the first pass could not identify. Two caveats survive into the file: the chronicler is unnamed even in Unger, and the quoted German is modern orthography, so it is reported text, not verbatim period wording. Undated beyond "early sixteenth century"; the file dates it 1520 to order the segment.

**[A, weak citation chain declared]** 23 August 1521: the foundation charter binds the St Anna chapel, the St Moritz preachership, and the Fuggerei into one endowment on a 10,000-florin account in memory of Georg and Ulrich (Fugger Foundation; Encyclopedia.com). The quoted wording is verbatim on de.wikipedia ("Fuggerei"), *but* that article's footnote is a tourism website (visit-augsburg.eu, 2018); fugger.de carries no charter text and en.wikipedia only paraphrases. The file cites it as transmitted and says the archival edition was not consulted.

**[A]** 1523: 52 houses standing in the Fuggerei. **[A]** 1522-1523: the estates and fiscal officers attack the great trading companies at Nuremberg for monopoly, the Fuggers named first; Charles V orders the proceedings stopped and the case is suspended in 1524 with no conviction. Jakob's attendance is not documented; he intervened by letter. **[A, wording caveat]** That 1523 letter to Charles V is given in German by Franz Herre, *Die Fugger in ihrer Zeit* (Augsburg, 2000), quoted on de.wikipedia; the dossier flags that the wording should be checked against the archival edition, and that check was not made.

**Flagged against misuse.** "Kauf dir einen Kaiser" is not a period quotation. It is Günter Ogger's 1978 book title, circulated online as though contemporary. Not used.

## 7. The last year, 1524-1525

**[A, with a discrepancy]** From 1 January 1525 Charles V leases the Fuggers the revenues and properties of Santiago, Calatrava, and Alcántara, the Maestrazgos, including Almadén. *Discrepancy:* the Fugger Foundation dates a quicksilver rights grant to 1523 while the dossier dates the broader lease to 1 January 1525; possibly two stages of one arrangement. Flagged, not resolved.

**[A in substance, [R] in wording]** The "lazy rabble" remark on the rebel peasants. *Chain traced:* de.wikipedia's footnote is Greg Steinmetz, *Der reichste Mann der Weltgeschichte* (Munich, 2016), p. 306, the German translation of an English book, so the German is a translator's rendering rather than a documented sixteenth-century utterance, and no primary letter or chronicle appears anywhere in the chain. The file states the substance in English and sets no German in quotation marks.

**[A]** 1525: Tyrolean miners at Schwaz strike over conditions, pay, and Fugger privileges; Ferdinand I threatens punishment and leaves the rights in place (habsburger.net, "Anti-Fuggerians: mines").

**[R]** 1525: the Fugger house at Buda stormed, goods seized, a submission agreement refused, answered with a blockade. Unconfirmed beyond the dossier; habsburger.net's 1525 unrest page covers Tyrol only. Kept as a stop, with the failure to corroborate stated inside the campa. **[R], dropped as a stop:** "Fugger won Pavia" (February 1525), Pölnitz's attribution. Fugger was in Augsburg, and the scale of his contribution could not be verified.

**[R]** 28 December 1525: the second and final testament naming Anton, whose succession is well documented though the 28 December dating rests on the dossier. **[A]** 30 December 1525: dies at Augsburg, aged sixty-six; inventory of about 3,000,058 guilders in assets against about 867,797 in liabilities. **[A]** Buried in the Fugger Chapel at St Anna.

**The retirement remark, recovered.** "I will keep on gaining as long as I can," tagged [R] on the first pass and marked "skip unless corroborated," should not be skipped and is not a deathbed line. Max Weber, *Die protestantische Ethik und der Geist des Kapitalismus* (1904/05), reports Fugger's reply to a business associate urging him to retire: "er hätte viel einen anderen Sinn, wollte gewinnen, dieweil er könnte," Weber's contrast case against Franklin. No date attached; the attribution runs to Weber relaying an anecdote, not to Fugger's pen. Used on the testament stop, with all of that in `date_confidence`.

## 8. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

The one not already flagged above is **the Fuggerhäuser coordinate**: `martin_luther.journey.json` pins the Fugger townhouse on the Maximilianstrasse at **48.36778, 10.89528**, de.wikipedia gives **48.3658, 10.8989**, about 250 m south-east. The file inherits the atlas pin byte-identical, as instructed, and prints the disagreement in `date_confidence`. Five stops ride on it: the 1494 firm seat, the 1512 building, the 1514 Albrecht loan, the 1518 Cajetan hearing, and the 1525 testament and death. The others stand where they were flagged above: the 1519 election date, the 1487 return against the 1485 Innsbruck leadership, Almadén 1523 against 1525, the Fugger share of the 1519 spend, and the Sistine Chapel loan.

## 9. Honest gaps

Unplaceable or unverifiable, each stated as such in the file: the 1515 Vienna congress (dropped); Bologna 1515 (secondary consensus only, and Eck's stop); St Moritz, Augsburg (no verified coordinate, so the city pin is used); the Roman mint building (location unverified); Buda 1525 (single line of transmission); the Aachen 1520 and Worms 1521 Fugger representations (both [R], neither staged).

The Fugger archive itself was never reached: the 1521 Stiftungsbrief was verified only as text transmitted on de.wikipedia, whose footnote is a tourism website, and no scholarly edition was found online. Also **not read:** Pölnitz's biography; Schulte, *Die Fugger in Rom 1495-1523* (1904); Herre, *Die Fugger in ihrer Zeit* (2000), seen only as quoted; the NDB entry behind the "first merchant made count" claim; Unger (1967); Steinmetz (2015/2016); Strieder (1926/1931); Häberlein (2012). Every claim resting on them is marked. Steinmetz's two-percent-of-European-GDP figure (about US$400 billion in 2015 terms) is a modern reconstruction, not a contemporary account or a scholarly consensus, and appears nowhere in the journey file.

## 10. Apparatus relations for the operator

Jakob Fugger is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are licensed here. Two relations in the pool are not rooted in his own life, and per the standing instruction they are flagged rather than silently deleted.

- **Samuel Oppenheimer, named in `joseph_oppenheimer.journey.json` as "the Fugger of his age."** Direction caught: **forward**. The comparison is made by the Oppenheimer file itself, at Vienna in 1703, 178 years after Fugger's death. It is a real, citable one-directional relation and it already lives where it belongs. Nothing about Oppenheimer is written into Fugger's file and no Vienna 1703 stop is staged. If the operator wants the reverse pointer, that is an EXCEPTIONS decision.
- **The afterlife material generally** (the Dürer portrait, the busts taken to Berlin in 1848-1850, the 1967 Walhalla bust, the 2025-2026 Augsburg State Theatre production *Tatort Augsburg*, the Strieder, Häberlein, Steinmetz, and Ogger books). Direction: **forward**, all of it. The one piece admitted is the chapel's survival as Catholic property inside a Lutheran parish from 1548 and the removal of the choir-stall busts: the afterlife of an object Fugger commissioned, attached to his own burial stop rather than staged as anybody's visit.
- *Correction recorded in case the object is wanted elsewhere:* the afterlife lens put Dürer's c.1520 portrait in the Alte Pinakothek, Munich (48.1487, 11.5703). It hangs in the Staatsgalerie Altdeutsche Meister, Schaezlerpalais, Augsburg, about 48.3655, 10.8960 (en.wikipedia, "Portrait of Jakob Fugger").

## 11. Interlocks named in the journey file

One traveler is named in a campa: **`martin_luther`**, on three direct grounds. Luther is examined by Cajetan inside Fugger's own house in October 1518; the indulgence campaign he attacks in 1517 serviced Fugger's loan to Albrecht of Brandenburg, with a Fugger agent at the chest; and he names the Fuggers in print in 1520.

Considered and **not** named, the relation being shared geography or shared century only: `salomon_trismosin` (Venice 1476; that file itself disclaims the Fondaco identification), `cortes` (Veracruz 1519, same year, no relation), `molay` (the pool raises a Templar-to-Fugger continuity myth and rejects it; the only real link is the 1525 military-order lease, which sits on Fugger's own Almadén stop unnamed), `thomas_more` (Henry VIII was one of three 1519 candidates; More was party to nothing of Fugger's), `joseph_oppenheimer` (forward, section 10).
