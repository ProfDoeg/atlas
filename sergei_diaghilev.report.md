# Sergei Diaghilev (1872-1929): research report
*Compiled for `sergei_diaghilev.journey.json`. 44 stops, 8 segments, register "national mythology: the canon is true", calendar gregorian.*

**Method.** Built from a verified pool across five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock with existing atlas files, afterlife). Every row arrived adversarially checked or with a correction attached; the corrections are honoured and recorded here rather than silently absorbed. Backbone: English Wikipedia, with ru.wikipedia for the birthplace and fr.wikipedia for Le Train Bleu. The quotations rest almost entirely on **Serge Lifar, *Serge Diaghilev* (Putnam, 1940)**, page-checked against its own index. **Buckle (1979)** and **Garafola (1989)** were unreachable in full text and are cited only as tradition, always [R].

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-reporter attribution. Contradictions are flagged and left open; where the journey had to pick a value, the pick is stated as one. Hedging lives in `date_confidence`, never in campa prose.

---

## I. Origins, 1872-1890

- Born on the family estate at **Selishchi, Novgorod Governorate**, 31 March 1872 NS (19 March OS); his mother dead of childbed fever within days. **[A: Wikipedia; birth at Selishchi confirmed independently by ru.wikipedia, 'Селищи (Чудовский район)']**
- Father Pavel Pavlovich, Chevalier Guard officer, remarried **Elena Panaeva**, who raised him; family later at **Perm**, where the house on **Sibirskaya Street** was a musical salon through his boyhood. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **First romance composed around age fifteen**, giving 1887. **[R as to date]** Age is the only anchor: no month, day, or manuscript in reachable sources.
- *Coordinates corrected:* the pool put Selishchi at 58.65 / 31.35, some 33 km southwest of the village; ru.wikipedia gives 58°53′46″N 31°39′54″E, so the journey uses **58.8961 / 31.665**, the village and not the unlocated estate house. **Gap.**

## II. Petersburg and the World of Art, 1890-1898

- **St Petersburg Imperial University** from 1890 to read law; six years over a four-year degree. **[A: Wikipedia]** Benois's account of their first meeting that summer is quoted verbatim. **[A: Lifar 1940, p. 27]**
- **Private composition lessons with Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov**; never satisfied himself as a composer. **[A: Wikipedia]** *Correction:* the raw row said "at the Conservatory," where he was **never enrolled**. The **year** is unestablished; the stop sits at 1894-01-01, flagged as reconstruction.
- **1897**: an exhibition of **British and German watercolourists**, his first curatorial venture, month and day unestablished. **1898**: co-founded the **Mir iskusstva** society and journal, editing it himself, plus a Russian-Finnish exhibition; the first number's month is not in the pool. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Gap.**
- "One must climb the pinnacles of Florentine art to pass judgment on contemporary art," from the journal in **1899** **[A: Lifar 1940, p. 30]**, is attached to the 1898 stop with the 1899 date in `quote_source`. **Nicholas Roerich** was later elected president of the society. **[A: nicholas_roerich.journey.json:106]**

## III. The Imperial Theatres, 1899-1901

- **1899**: Prince **Sergei Volkonsky** appointed him officer on special duty. **1900**: **entrusted with** a new production of Delibes' **Sylvia**. **1901**: **discharged after production disagreements**, closing official theatrical work in Russia to him. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- *Two corrections.* The geography row said he "stages Sylvia in 1900": he did not, **the production was never realised**. Its further claim that he met Nijinsky on this stage is **unsourced** and is not used (see gap 4).

## IV. Russia carried west, 1905-1908

- **6 March 1905**: Russian historical portraiture at the **Tauride Palace**, more than 4,000 paintings. **[A: Wikipedia]** *Coordinates corrected* from 59.9469 / 30.3853, which fell inside the Tauride Garden, to **59.9479 / 30.3759**. The campa's claim that many canvases were later destroyed is **[R]**.
- **Isadora Duncan** danced barefoot in St Petersburg soon after Bloody Sunday, with Diaghilev, **Fokine**, **Pavlova**, and **Kschessinska** in the audience, Kschessinska recording the effect on Fokine. **[A: isadora_duncan.journey.json:407-412]** Dated 1905-01-01 there; the evening is not fixed. **[R as to day]**
- **October 1906**: **Two Centuries of Russian Art**, some 750 works, Salon d'Automne, **Grand Palais**. **May 1907**: **Concerts historiques russes** at the Opera with Rimsky-Korsakov and Chaliapin, individual dates unestablished. **[A: Wikipedia]** **Gap.**
- **May 1908**: **Boris Godunov** with Chaliapin; triumph, loss of roughly **85,000 francs**. **[A: Wikipedia]** The **only attested money figure in the file**; every other financial statement in the campas is [R].

## V. The Ballets Russes founded, 1909-1911

- **18 March 1909**: a Grand Duke wrote to Nicholas II about "the whole Diaghilev business" and its "vilest flatteries," quoted verbatim. **[A: Lifar 1940, pp. 132-133]** Relations with **Kschessinska**, who had ears at court, deteriorated and the treasury money fell away. **[R: so tagged in the pool]**
- **19 May 1909**: first Saison Russe at the **Théâtre du Châtelet**. **[A: Wikipedia; 'Ballets Russes']** *Correction:* the pool put **Les Sylphides** on the opening bill; the 19 May bill was **Le Pavillon d'Armide, the Polovtsian Dances, and Le Festin**, Les Sylphides coming on the second programme, **2 June 1909**. The Comtesse de Noailles's account is quoted in full; *citation corrected* from p. 137 to **p. 158**, opening Chapter IX, per the book's own index. **[A: Lifar 1940, p. 158]**
- **25 June 1910**: **The Firebird**, commissioned from the unknown **Igor Stravinsky**. *Venue corrected:* the pool placed it at the Châtelet; it premiered at the **Palais Garnier**, and the whole 1910 season was at the Opéra, which moves Schéhérazade with it. **[A: 'The Firebird']**
- **1911**: first **Monte Carlo** season, the company constituted as standing. **[A for the season; R for reading 1911 as the founding of a permanent troupe]** *Correction:* the pool called Monte Carlo the winter base "1911-1929," but the wartime company was in Spain, Switzerland, Italy, and America, and Monte Carlo became the **formal winter base only from 1922**; the journey **splits this into two stops**.
- **June 1911**: **Petrushka** at the Châtelet, day unestablished, and the London debut at **Covent Garden** in the coronation season, exact date not in the pool. **[A: 'Ballets Russes']** The campa's line about living in hotels thereafter is **[R]**.

## VI. Faune, the Rite, and the rupture, 1912-1913

- **29 May 1912**: **L'Après-midi d'un faune**, Nijinsky's first ballet, to Debussy; the final gesture scandalised Paris. **[A: Wikipedia]** That Diaghilev had it repeated is **[R]**.
- **June 1912, Hellerau**: Suzanne Perrottet performed before an audience the atlas file lists as running "from Diaghilev to Shaw to Kafka." **[A: suzanne_perrottet.journey.json:190]** The campa's reading of **why** he went is **[R]**, inference from sequence.
- **29 May 1913**: **The Rite of Spring**, Théâtre des Champs-Élysées; Stravinsky, Nijinsky, **Roerich**. **[A: 'Ballets Russes'; nicholas_roerich.journey.json:142]** *Two corrections.* The theatre was not "barely two weeks old": it opened **2 April 1913**. And **riot** does not appear in first-night reviews; the word enters the record with the **1924 revival**.
- **August 1913**: the company sailed for South America; he did not. *Correction of substance:* he stayed **in Venice**, and the same article carries the alternative reading, that the reason "had more to do with wanting to spend time away from Nijinsky." **[A: 'Vaslav Nijinsky']** **Both are in the campa; neither is resolved.**
- **10 September 1913**: Nijinsky married **Romola de Pulszky** in Buenos Aires, days after proposing at sea. **[A: 'Vaslav Nijinsky'; 'Romola Nijinsky']** *Correction:* "civil ceremony" is **unsupported**, the sources saying only that they "married in port," so the qualifier is dropped; the Gunsbourg proposal story is **not in Wikipedia either way** and is unused. **Gap.** Diaghilev's "wild orgy of dissipation" and "sobbing shamelessly in Russian despair" is Wikipedia quoting a biographer. **[A as a quotation; R as to the event]**
- The telegram: **"Le Ballet Russe n'a plus besoin de vos services. Ne nous rejoignez pas. Serge de Diaghilev."** **[A: Lifar 1940, p. 148]** *Corrections:* the pool dropped the leading "Le," restored here; and its place field said "sent to South America," where Lifar writes "About to start back from **America**."

**Two contradictions left open.** *Rio:* the geography lens dates Nijinsky's missed performance to **August 1913**, the standard chronology puts the Brazilian dates **after** the 10 September wedding. The journey does not adjudicate: there is **no separate Rio stop**, and the missed performance sits inside the Buenos Aires campa, which holds either way. The pool corrected the Teatro Municipal coordinates to about **-22.9088 / -43.1766**. *The telegram:* Diaghilev's location that day is unspecified anywhere, so the canonical Paris pin is used with the uncertainty in `date_confidence`.

## VII. The war years, 1914-1918

- A period based at **Ouchy, Lausanne**, working with **Massine** as Nijinsky's successor. **[R: Buckle 1979; the pool tags the row R]** Month and day defaulted. **Alfonso XIII** became patron of the stranded company, with repeated Madrid seasons **[A: Wikipedia]**; the El Greco and cante jondo detail is **[R]**, and a San Sebastián base, **[R]**, was given **no stop**.
- **1916, the United States.** *Correction carried in full:* the pool said he crossed "to secure Nijinsky's release," and elsewhere that he "never crossed the Atlantic." Both wrong. The tour opened at the **Century Theatre** in January 1916, not the Met; the **Met** engagement was in **April**; he did sail; and Nijinsky's release was a **separate** negotiation begun October 1914 and completed in 1916, reportedly involving the Pope and President Wilson, Nijinsky reaching New York **4 April 1916**. **[A: 'Vaslav Nijinsky']** The old Met pin (40.7539 / -73.9877) is confirmed. Romola's "Great courtesy was shown to me... Diaghilev must have given his orders" **[A: Lifar 1940, p. 149]** and Nijinsky's "I do not regret my relations with Serge Pavlovitch" **[A via Lifar, p. 148; R as to occasion]** are quoted here.
- **1917, Rome**: rehearsals with Picasso, Cocteau, and Massine on **Parade**, **Erik Satie** fetched in at Cocteau's urging **[A: erik_satie.journey.json:402-405]**; *coordinates corrected* to **41.9009 / 12.4955**. **18 May 1917**: **Parade** at the Châtelet, Apollinaire's note coining "surréalisme." **[A: erik_satie.journey.json:426]**
- **1917**: the Revolution cut the company off from Russia for good. **[A: Wikipedia]** The stop is dated 1917-11-07 (October Revolution NS), the severance attested to the year, not the day. The Soviet denunciation as "bourgeois decadence" is **[R]**, kept only in `suggested_refs`.

## VIII. The Paris company, 1918-1924

- The **Alhambra, London**, as principal postwar venue, and the **1921 Sleeping Princess**, prestige project and severe financial disaster **[A: Wikipedia]**; opening night unestablished. The **Tchaikovsky** link is one-directional in the way the atlas allows: Diaghilev staged his score, and `tchaikovsky.journey.json:448` carries the reverse view.
- **1920**: he introduced **Stravinsky** to **Coco Chanel** in the spring; by September the Stravinskys were at her villa at **Garches**; that year Chanel **secretly underwrote** the revival of Le Sacre du Printemps. **[A: coco_chanel.journey.json:170-171]** The December 1920 date is a placing, not a document, and says so.
- **13 June 1923**: **Les Noces**, by **Bronislava Nijinska**. **[A: 'Ballets Russes']** **20 June 1924**: **Le Train Bleu**; *correction:* the pool placed it in Monte Carlo, fr.wikipedia gives "au **Théâtre des Champs-Élysées de Paris le 20 juin 1924**." Chanel's costumes tied her name publicly to a company she was financing unacknowledged. **[A: coco_chanel.journey.json:224]**
- **November 1924**: **George Balanchine** auditioned in Paris, hired on the spot, soon ballet master. *Correction:* the pool said **1925**; Wikipedia and `george_balanchine.journey.json:240-252` give **November 1924**. Diaghilev on **Massine** the same year: "that essential though indefinable something which made the creator in Massine... for all too short a time, alas!" **[A: Lifar 1940, p. 208]**
- *Contradiction flagged:* a pool row date-stamped 1920 listed Prokofiev, Ravel, Satie, Matisse, and Braque as 1920s commissions, wrong for several (Parade 1917, Daphnis et Chloé 1912). Unused; each appears at its own date.

## IX. Last seasons, death, burial

- **12 June 1928**: **Apollon musagète**, Balanchine to Stravinsky, **Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt** **[A: george_balanchine.journey.json:299]**; that Diaghilev recognised a new language at the final rehearsal is **[R]**. **21 May 1929**: **The Prodigal Son**, Prokofiev, Balanchine, Rouault, his last. **[A: 'Ballets Russes']** **July 1929, Vichy**: **[R: Buckle 1979]**.
- **19 August 1929**: died at the **Grand Hôtel des Bains** on the Venice **Lido**, aged 57, of complications of diabetes. **[A: Wikipedia; hotel confirmed independently by Wikipedia, 'Grand Hotel des Bains']** *Coordinates corrected:* the afterlife lens gave 45.4408 / 12.3155 (2.7 km off), the geography lens 45.4041 / 12.3663 (1.2 km off). The journey uses **45.4134 / 12.3748**, keeping 45.4408 / 12.3155 only as the **city** pin, from `george_balanchine.journey.json:316`.
- **Deathbed attendance is not settled.** **Kochno**, **Lifar**, and **Misia Sert** at the bedside is tradition, not in the cited article. `coco_chanel.journey.json:308-317` records Misia paying for the funeral and carries **unresolved** the claim that **Chanel paid secretly and stood at the grave beside her**; the campa names the three and says Misia paid, adjudicating nothing further.
- Buried in the **Orthodox section, Isola di San Michele**; the **21 August interment date is traditional**, not documented. **[A: 'San Michele Cemetery, Venice']** Lifar's "the body was wafted to the island of San Michele, and there borne on our arms to the grave" is used **[A: Lifar 1940, p. 373]**; the graveside "Seriozha, my dear, they are about to lower the coffin..." **[A: same page]** is unused. **Stravinsky** was buried near him **15 April 1971** (died 6 April), **Vera de Bosset** in 1982 **[A: same article]**; *correction:* the pool treated 15 April as the death date, but it is the interment. The company dissolved within the year.

**Coordinate reconciliation, San Michele.** The corpus held **four** pins: 45.4548 / 12.3459 and 45.4586 / 12.3417 (both in open lagoon), 45.4508 / 12.3444 (`coco_chanel`), 45.4408 / 12.3155 (`george_balanchine`, the city). The journey uses **45.4466 / 12.3456** (OSM) for both island stops, a **deliberate divergence from the `coco_chanel` pin**, which is off by roughly 500 m and needs the same fix.

## X. Quotations examined and rejected

- **"Étonne-moi."** To Cocteau. **[R]** Unverifiable in a primary source, circulating through Cocteau's reminiscences. **Unused.**
- **"Tchaikovsky thought of committing suicide for fear of being discovered as a homosexual, but today, if you are a composer and not homosexual, you might as well put a bullet through your head."** **[R]** Traced past Wikiquote and the Oxford Dictionary of Quotations to its reported origin, **Vernon Duke, *Listen Here!* (Obolensky, 1963)**, whom later dictionaries credit downstream. **No date, place, or occasion anywhere, and it rests on one reporter.** Unused.
- **"Recinto XIV, consecrated 1816"** and the Bagration / Golitsyn / Potemkin-Tavrichesky burial list are unverifiable. Dropped.

## XI. Honest gaps

1. **No archival Diaghilev.** Letters, contracts, and company books were not consulted; every financial statement except the 85,000-franc loss is tradition.
2. **Lifar is the sole quotation source and is not disinterested.** He was Diaghilev's last protégé and was present at the funeral he narrates. His pages were checked and one corrected; his framing was not.
3. **Buckle (1979) and Garafola (1989) unreachable in full text**, so everything from them is [R]; and no Russian-language archival material was used, ru.wikipedia serving only for the birthplace coordinates.
4. **The meeting with Nijinsky is undocumented**: no place, no date, no occasion. **Massine's discovery** is likewise undated and unplaced.
5. **The Rio ordering problem is open** (Section VI) and was routed around, not solved.
6. **Kschessinska's memoir** not read directly; it reaches this file through `isadora_duncan.journey.json`.
7. **Defaults.** Twenty-six stops carry a day defaulted to 01, eleven of those a month defaulted to 01 as well. Every one says so in `date_confidence`.
8. **The reception layer was gathered and not mapped**, since the journey closes on the island: V&A 2010-11 (pin 51.4966 / -0.1722) with the Ekstrom Collection, the National Gallery of Australia costume show (coordinates corrected to -35.3008 / 149.1367), Washington 2013, Morgan Library 2024, a 1928 Montreux newsreel surfaced in 2011, and Nijinsky's 1953 Montmartre reinterment (48.8872 / 2.3325).

## XII. Atlas relations named in the campas

Named only for a direct or one-directional relation, never for shared geography: **isadora_duncan** (he was in the Petersburg house in 1905 and took the choreographer she changed to Paris four years later); **nicholas_roerich** (president of his own World of Art society, exhibited by him in 1906, designer of Prince Igor 1909, the Rite 1913, and work at Covent Garden in exile); **suzanne_perrottet** (he attended the 1912 Hellerau demonstrations in which she performed); **erik_satie** (commissioned through Cocteau to score Parade); **coco_chanel** (introduced by him to Stravinsky, secret financier of the 1920 Rite revival, costumier of Le Train Bleu, present in the accounts of his funeral); **george_balanchine** (hired on the spot in November 1924, his last chief choreographer); **tchaikovsky** (his Sleeping Beauty was the score Diaghilev staged whole in London in 1921, out of the repertoire he had been thrown out of in 1901).

Deliberately **not** named, though present in the interlock lens: **marcel_proust**, **gurdjieff**, **victoria_ocampo**, **dannunzio**. Each is a co-location, which the map already shows.
