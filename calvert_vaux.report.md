# Calvert Vaux (1824-1895): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-21 from the verified research pool for slug `calvert_vaux`. Written for `calvert_vaux.journey.json`.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named and reachable. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition, or a claim whose only carrier is a secondary work the verify pass could not open. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

The verify pass overturned or re-dated **fourteen** dossier items: four coordinates 200 m to 1.5 km off, three sources cited for something they do not say, two duplicated or merged stops, and several dates off by a year or a day. Each is recorded below under its phase.

---

## 1. London, 1824-1850

**Birth, 20 December 1824 [A]** (NPS, fetched). The birth at or near **36 Pudding Lane**, where his father the surgeon Calvert Bowyer Vaux practised, is **[R]**, carried by the NYC Municipal Archives birthday post; no primary record reached. Pin kept at 51.5097, -0.0865, street flagged as tradition.

**Baptism, 9 February 1825, St Benet Gracechurch [A]** (Wikipedia, citing the parish record). *Correction:* the dossier cited this to NPS, whose page was fetched in full and contains **only** birth and death dates; citation withdrawn. Coordinate moved ~110 m east to 51.5117, -0.0850. This date is the commonest error about Vaux online, reprinted as his birthday.

**Father's death, 1833 [R].** Kowsky/Rybczynski tradition, unconfirmed. **Merchant Taylors' School [R].** The school held the Suffolk Lane site 1561-1875, which fits the period. *Correction:* the dossier's 51.5109, -0.0912 is ~220 m northwest, near Cannon Street station; corrected to 51.5099, -0.0884. Vaux appears in no published Old Merchant Taylors list, so the attendance stays **[R]**.

**Articled to Lewis Nockalls Cottingham, 1843 [R]** (Kowsky; Rybczynski). *Contradiction, unresolved:* the dossier rejects the widely reprinted "entered training at age nine" version as implausible; neither it nor 1843 was traced to a primary record.

**Architectural Association: a seventy-year anachronism corrected.** Vaux exhibited watercolors in the Association's milieu in the late 1840s **[A** Rybczynski, NYRB, 13 Aug 1998**]**. *Correction:* the dossier pinned 51.5171, -0.1341, i.e. 34-36 Bedford Square, which the AA did not occupy **until 1917**. In 1847-1850 it had no Bedford Square presence: its first formal meeting was May 1847 at **Lyons Inn Hall** off the Strand, and it shared 9 Conduit Street with the RIBA only from 1859. File uses ~51.5133, -0.1141.

**Continental tour with George Truefitt, 1846 [R].** France, Belgium, Germany. **Not one town on the route is named in any source in the pool.** Pinned on Brussels per the geography lens. On the declined Paris pin see §9.

**Recruitment by Downing, 1850 [R].** Downing seeing the exhibited watercolors and hiring on the spot is the standard telling. What is **[A]** is only the 1850 emigration to work with Downing at Newburgh (olmsted.org, fetched).

## 2. The Hudson and the Downing inheritance, 1850-1857

**Highland Gardens, Newburgh, autumn 1850 [A]** (Kowsky, olmsted.org; NPS). **Washington federal grounds: re-dated and upgraded.** The dossier had 1851 and tagged Vaux **[R]**. *Correction:* Wikipedia has Downing "commissioned in late 1850", presenting to the Smithsonian Regents 27 February 1851; LPC **LP-2274** independently states that Vaux and Downing "began the planning of the grounds around many government buildings in Washington, D.C." Re-dated **1850-1852**, upgraded to **[A]**.

**Henry Clay, 28 July 1852.** Standard history, tagged **[R]** in the pool because no primary account was refetched; the caution is kept. That Vaux was not aboard is uncontested everywhere.

**Marriage to Mary Swan McEntee, 4 May 1854 [A/R].** Marriage and McEntee kinship **[A]** via the Smithsonian AAA finding aid (not refetched); the exact date therefore **[R]**. Place given in the pool only as "probably Kingston/Newburgh area"; pinned Rondout/Kingston and said so.

**Hoyt House, Staatsburg: collaborator and coordinate both wrong.** *(a)* The dossier gives it to Vaux "with Frederick Clarke Withers"; Wikipedia's works list gives it "(1855), **independently**". **Contradiction left open in the campa**, both stated, neither chosen. *(b)* The dossier's 41.8145, -73.921 is ~4 km south-southeast, off the property; corrected to ~41.849, -73.940 in Mills-Norrie State Park. Re-dated to 1855.

**Idlewild and Wodenethe [R].** Real Vaux jobs of these years. **No stops**, trimmed for count, not a judgement on soundness. **110 Broadway [A for the address, R for 1856].** The address is attested by the letterhead of the 12 May 1865 letter. The move year and the naturalization year are reconstruction; **no primary naturalization record was located.** **Villas and Cottages, 1857 [A]** (Internet Archive, 1874 Harper edition, Getty copy).

**AIA founding membership, 1857: fact holds, citation replaced.** *Correction:* the dossier cited TCLF; the TCLF pioneer page was fetched and **does not mention the AIA at all**, listing only partners. Re-cited to Wikipedia: "Became founding member of the American Institute of Architects in 1857."

## 3. Greensward, 1857-1873

**The approach to Olmsted, 1857 [A]** (NPS: Vaux "convinced New York City to hold a competition ... and partnered with Frederick Law Olmsted"). That the entry was drafted at Vaux's house, often at night, is **[R]**, and the house coordinate is approximate.

**Competition won, 28 April 1858 [A]** (Central Park Conservancy). Thirty-three entries, $2,000 shared; primary text at gutenberg.org/cache/epub/73830. The Greensward report is jointly authored and does not divide authorship sentence by sentence, so its two quotations are Vaux's only in the co-authored sense; both `quote_source` fields say so.

**Bethesda Terrace 1859-1873 [A]; Bow Bridge 1862 [A]; The Dairy 1870 [A]** (Central Park Conservancy; NYC Municipal Archives, which records that all thirty-four park bridges were designed to be unique). The "Nature first, and 2nd & 3rd" line is attributed to Vaux by the Conservancy with **no manuscript citation**, and is carried on that footing.

**The Green conflict, 1861-1865.** The 1862 fever episode (Vaux raving that Green's name must not be spoken) is **[R]**: later historians, no medical record. The 1865 letters are the strongest primary material in the file: three in the Rotunda Olmsted Papers (12 May, 20 May, 3 June), quoted verbatim. The abbreviations "a p.s. and an m.p." applied to Green are **left unglossed by the source itself** and are not expanded here.

## 4. Prospect Park and the firm, 1865-1872

**Site shift, 1865 [A]** (LPC **LP-2558**). **Long Meadow: coordinate corrected.** The dossier's 40.658, -73.976 is ~800 m south of the meadow, near the Bandshell and Parade Ground; Wikipedia places the Long Meadow in the park's northern and western portions. Corrected to ~40.6650, -73.9720.

**Buffalo, 1868 onward [A/R].** TCLF confirms the Olmsted-Vaux Buffalo system; the exact 1868 start is **[R]**. Kowsky's 2013 book not fetched.

**Riverside, Illinois: re-dated to a range.** Wikipedia: "designed in 1869 by Calvert Vaux and Frederick Law Olmsted". Firm engaged 1868, General Plan dated 1869; the dossier's flat 1868 is a year early for the plan. **Eastern Parkway, 1870s [A]** (through LP-2558).

**Olana: the largest single overstatement in the dossier.** *(a)* The dossier dates Vaux's advice to Church at 1870, or "c. 1870-1872". The cited source, Bergdoll on olana.org, was fetched: it gives **no dates at all** and says only that Olana was "created in consultation at the outset with the architect and landscape designer Calvert Vaux". "At the outset" points earlier (Church bought the land in 1860). *(b)* The dossier attributes to Vaux personal on-site guidance about "window openings, bay windows, and porches as places to frame views"; Bergdoll attributes that to **Vaux's 1857 book**, not to advice in person. The campa states it as Bergdoll does. *(c)* Coordinate moved ~690 m west to 42.21778, -73.82944; the dossier's point sat off the hill toward the river.

**Dissolution, 1872 [R]** (olmsted.org: "1872: Olmsted-Vaux partnership ended"). **No source in the pool gives a reason.** Stated as a gap in the campa.

## 5. The independent practice, 1873-1886

**Parliament Hill, Ottawa, 1873 [R].** Only sustained study is J. J. Stewart (1976), JSTOR 1493555, **not fetched** (paywall). **Jefferson Market Courthouse, 1874-1877 [A]** (Village Preservation, fetched; the fifth-most-beautiful-building poll is on that page). **Riverside Park and Drive [A involvement, R phasing]:** two phases, 1873-1875 and the 1880s, and no fetched source separates the hands.

**AMNH, 1877 [R]** (AMNH Research Library records, not fetched). **Metropolitan Museum, opened 30 March 1880 [A]** (metmuseum.org history; corroborated by TCLF, fetched).

**Ruskin.** NYC Landmarks reports call both museum buildings "Ruskinian Gothic". The influence of John Ruskin's published argument (truth to materials, the workman's hand in the ornament; *The Seven Lamps*, "The Nature of Gothic") is standard architectural history, tagged **[R]** because WebSearch quota was exhausted and it could not be re-verified. Ruskin is a researched traveler here and **is named in the AMNH campa** on that basis: a one-directional relation, books read by Vaux. **No meeting is documented, and the campa says so.**

**Stuyvesant Apartments, 142 East 18th Street.** Vaux's own residence for a period. **No stop**, trimmed for count.

## 6. The Children's Aid Society buildings

**Substance confirmed, dates wrong.** LPC **LP-2274**, fetched as PDF and read with pypdf, confirms approximately twelve CAS buildings, the firm Vaux & Radford, and verbatim: "Vaux worked to evoke the imagery of a snug country inn in the middle of New York City." *Correction:* the same report dates the Radford partnership to "around 1873", formalized 1876, and places the buildings "in the 1880s and 90s", **not beginning 1876** as the dossier has it.

**Two buildings merged into one stop.** The dossier's geography lens has "Tompkins Square Lodging House and Industrial School, **221 Sullivan Street**". These are two buildings. LP-2274 puts the Tompkins Square Lodging House at 8th Street and Avenue B (1885; own report LP-2055), which Village Preservation gives as 295 East 8th Street / 127 Avenue B, opened 1887; it lists **221 Sullivan Street separately as the Sullivan Street Industrial School, 1891-1892**. The coordinate given was Sullivan Street, so the **name** was the error. Neither carries a stop; the Elizabeth Home carries the CAS material.

**Elizabeth Home for Girls, 307 East 12th Street, 1891-1892 [A]** (LP-2274: "Built 1891-2, Vaux & Radford, architects"; Dutch-influenced stepped gable). *Correction:* coordinate moved from 40.7285, -73.9822 (~280 m southeast, near East 7th and Avenue A) to ~40.7305, -73.9840 per the report's tax map.

## 7. The return to the parks, 1887-1895

**A duplicate stop removed.** The dossier chronology has **two** returns to the Department of Public Parks, 1881 and 1887, both tagged **[A]**. The 1881 tag is unsupported and its own source note admits it: every fetched source gives 1887 (NYC Municipal Archives, "starting in 1887"; olmsted.org, "1887: Returned to designing landscape plans for the Parks Department under Samuel Parsons Jr."). **One** return stop, at 1887.

**The 1879 abolition of the landscape-architect office [R]**, from Olmsted's 1882 *The Spoils of the Park*, unconfirmed. Referenced inside the 1887 campa.

**Niagara, 1887 [A]** (NPS). Plan catalogued at LoC (Supplemental Report of the Commissioners, 1887). *Lens disagreement:* geography dates the work 1885-1887, chronology 1887; both are in `date_confidence`.

**Downing Park, Newburgh, 1889-1897 [R].** Fee waiver, dedication to Downing, and the sons' participation all dossier reconstruction, consistent with olmsted.org and TCLF but not fetched. **Morningside Park** and **New York Botanical Garden (1895)**: thin sourcing, **no stops**, trimmed and recorded here.

**Mary Vaux's death, 13 August 1892 [R].** LoC Olmsted Papers timeline, not refetched. Carriage accident near Closter, skull fracture, roughly a week's survival. Nothing Vaux wrote about it has been located. **Mulberry Bend Park, 1895 [A per the dossier project register]**, citing NYC Parks history, which **returned 403**. Opened after his death, later renamed Columbus Park.

**Rockwood Park, Saint John, 1894.** *Contradiction inside the pool, left open:* chronology tags it **[R]** as tradition, geography tags the same claim **[A]** citing TCLF. `date_confidence` says so.

## 8. Gravesend Bay, November 1895

**The morning [A]** (Bowery Boys History, fetched): the fog, the watch and chain and vest left behind, about two dollars carried, the son's house on Twentieth Avenue in Bath Beach.

**The last sighting: over-stated in the dossier.** The dossier has Vaux "last seen alive by Captain Walter Earl Ditmar on a pier, the two discussing possible improvements to the shore before Vaux walks on alone." Bowery Boys hedges twice and contains **no conversation**: he was spotted by a "Captain Ditmar", possibly Captain Walter Earl Ditmar, on a pier near the water. The name sits in scare quotes there and the full identification is the writer's conjecture. **The shoreline conversation is not in the source at all.**

**The recovery: date off by one day.** The dossier gives 21 November in all three lenses. Bowery Boys dates the discovery to **20 November**; the 21st is the publication date of the linked New York Times report. Two further fixes from the same source: the finder was "a workman on Fry's coal dock [later identified as Benjamin Butler]", and the missing shoe was specifically the **left** shoe.

**Manner of death: the pool contradicts itself.** The dossier repeatedly calls the manner "unresolved in the historical record" and attributes that to Wikipedia and Bowery Boys. **Neither source leaves it open.** Wikipedia states flatly "by accidental drowning in Gravesend Bay"; Bowery Boys concludes he "likely fainted or tripped". The genuine ambiguity is in **the 1895 press**, where the Tribune allowed a suicide theory that "prevails with some people" and Vaux's son rejected it. The campa says it that way and adds that nothing found on the body settles it.

**Three coordinates for two places.** Recovery site: 40.5906, -74.0009 vs 40.599, -74.005 across two lenses; the file uses **40.5990, -74.0050** and flags it. Calvert Vaux Park: three values in the pool; the file uses Wikipedia's **40.5849, -73.9944**. Montrepose: two pool values ~1.4 km from Wikipedia's **41.9181396, -73.9979157**, which the file uses.

**Burial [A cemetery, R plot].** Wikipedia confirms Montrepose. The McEntee-plot specification is uncited by anything fetchable. The **park-foliage funeral gesture** had been sitting under an **[A]** tag in the pool with no retrievable source; downgraded to tradition and marked as such inside the campa.

**Calvert Vaux Park, 1998 [A]** (Wikipedia). **No personal memorial in Central Park or Prospect Park [R]**: a negative finding off the Conservancy's Bethesda Terrace page, tagged R because an absence is hard to source positively.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Calvert Vaux is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so these are not written into his file; per the anti-silent-deletion rule they are recorded here for decision. **All are FORWARD-direction catches**, which the exceptions file does not license by default.

1. **Nikola Tesla / Niagara.** The lens proposes a `suggested_ref` from Vaux's Niagara stop to Tesla's Adams Power Station scene (`nikola_tesla.journey.json`, 43.0896, -79.0378), AC transmission beginning August 1895, eight years after the General Plan. A real tension, conservation against industrial harnessing of the same water, but no relation between the men and the station postdates the plan. **Forward. Not written in.**
2. **Edward Burne-Jones / the Metropolitan Museum.** The 1998 centenary retrospective opened at the Met, whose first building Vaux designed. Vaux died 1895. **Forward by more than a century. Not written in.**
3. **Pure place-overlap, none written in:** Jules Verne at Niagara (1867, the sole *backward* item, still only co-location), Jose Limon at the Delacorte, Fidel Castro at the Central Park Zoo, Alphonse Mucha's studio near the park, Antoine de Saint-Exupery on Central Park South, Felix Adler's and Robert Oppenheimer's Ethical Culture School, Maurice Sendak's negated line to that school, Jeffrey Epstein's and Michele Sindona's Central Park addresses.
4. **A false cognate, flagged so it is not picked up later:** `lord_kelvin.journey.json`'s "Niagara" stops refer to the **cable ship USS Niagara**, not the falls. Not an interlock.

The one relation rooted in Vaux's own life and therefore **written in: John Ruskin**, in the AMNH campa. See §5.

## Corpus gaps worth queueing

No journey file and no QUEUE.md entry for **Andrew Jackson Downing**, **Frederick Clarke Withers**, **Jacob Wrey Mould**, **Frederic Edwin Church**, or **Andrew Haswell Green**: Vaux's mentor, partner, ornamentalist, most famous client, and adversary. ## §9. The canonical Paris pin, declined

The interlock lens offers Paris (48.8566, 2.3522) because the 1846 tour and the 1865 European study trip "plausibly route through Paris", while conceding that **the dossier does not name Paris**. No stop was created: it would stage travel no source records. The decision is stated in the Continental-tour stop's `date_confidence` rather than made silently. The lens also closed off Buenos Aires, the Kaaba, and the Temple Mount; none inherited.
