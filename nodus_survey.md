### #1 — HANNIBAL BARCA (247–181 BC)

**Where he appears:** Essay 199, *HANIBAL*, the entire text. The essay opens:

> "Hamilcar held his son Hannibal over fire and made him promise to vanquish Rome to avenge the loss of the First Punic War. Twenty years later… the 29-year-old Hannibal would lead infantry, cavalry, and African war elephants, up the eastern Iberian coast, through Provence and across the Swiss Alps, into Italy."

And the mythic framing:

> "We can identify a star-gazing bull-headed monster who consumes children in fire, embedded in the culture and worship of the old gods of the Mediterranean from the Age of Taurus… it represents an atavist thread that is formative in the collective unconscious of Western civilization."

**Journey arc:**  
- Childhood oath sworn over fire at the Pillars of Hercules (237 BC) — the fire-vow as mythic origin  
- New Carthage (Cartagena, Spain) → along the Via Heraclea up the Iberian coast → Pyrenees → Provence → **crossing the Swiss Alps with 37 war elephants** (the supreme mythic moment) → descent into the Po Valley → victories at the Trebia, Lake Trasimene, Cannae → 15 years of Italian occupation without ever taking Rome → recall to Africa → defeat at Zama (202 BC) → exile in the East (Ephesus, Bithynia) → death by self-administered poison at Libyssa (181 BC), rather than be surrendered to Rome  
- Standard he marched under: **the upward crescent of Tanit, bull-horn goddess of Tyre**

**Mythology:** Semitic/Canaanite. The corpus places him inside the Moloch/Cronus/child-sacrifice cluster — he is himself a child consecrated by fire (held over the flame by Hamilcar), who becomes a flame that consumes armies. His arc is Dido's Carthage reversed: where Aeneas left Carthage for Italy, Hannibal left Iberia for Italy to undo Aeneas's Rome.

**Connections to existing corpus:**  
- His Alpine crossing enters northern Italy at the same Alpine gates Goethe exited (Splügen, Chiavenna, the Po plain) — two journeys through the same passes, 2,000 years apart, in opposite directions  
- The Canaanite mythology connects directly to Saint Christopher (essay 208: "he was a Canaanite"), and to essay 201 (Moriah-Qorikancha child sacrifice axis)  
- The Punic path through Iberia (Cartagena, Sagunto, the Ebro) is the same corridor Columbus navigated northward before his Atlantic crossing  
- The Tanit bull-horn standard is kin to the Libertadores' own standard imagery — and the essay 217 (*El Estandarte*) makes the estandarte itself a protagonist

### #2 — CHRISTOPHER COLUMBUS / CRISTÓBAL COLÓN (1451–1506)

**Where he appears:** Essay 197, *COLUMBUS CRYPTO JUDIO*, the entire text. The defining passage:

> "Four months after the [Alhambra] decree, on August 3rd, the day after Tisha B'Av, the Jewish day of remembrance marking the destruction of the First and Second Temple, Christopher Columbus set off toward a New World. The journey was funded with an interest-free loan of 17,000 ducats provided by two conversos… The first two letters Columbus sent back to Spain… were written to Santángel and Sánchez, not the Spanish monarchs whose flag he sailed under."

And his encoded signature:

> "It is believed to encode the Kaddish, allowing the Jewish prayer for the dead to be recited over his body at burial without the goyim understanding."

And the explicit Joan/Columbus link:

> "While Marianne was the new form of Joan of Arc… Miss Columbia would represent those ideals in the Americas. Both figures adopted representations of neo-Roman Republican ideals, often donning a Phrygian cap…"

**Journey arc:**  
- Born Genoa → youth at sea, arrives in Lisbon (1476) → Lisbon ghetto, financial responsibility for a Jewish elder → Spain: Granada, Seville, La Rábida monastery → **Palos de la Frontera, August 3, 1492** (Tisha B'Av +1 day) → Atlantic crossing with Niña, Pinta, Santa María → landfall at Guanahaní/San Salvador → Cuba, Hispaniola → return to Spain (1493, triumphant) → three more voyages → stripped of governorship → dies in Valladolid (1506), in poverty, still insisting he had reached Asia  
- The hidden journey beneath the visible one: the marrano's flight from Europe, coded in every document he signed

**Mythology:** The dove/Columbia constellation — the dove of Noah's return — encoded as his name. The crypto-Jewish messianic project (Jerusalem via the Americas). The Lucifer/Morning Star/Statue of Liberty chain that runs from him to Miss Columbia to the Rioplatense republic (a Bartholdi Statue of Liberty stands in Belgrano, Buenos Aires, per essay 197).

**Connections:**  
- Essay 197 makes the explicit argument: Columbus → Columbia → Marianne → Joan. The two figures are already in the same mythological orbit; their quipus would knot  
- The Alhambra Decree (1492) + departure date (Tisha B'Av) ties him to essay 190 (*Al-Andalus and the Reconquista*) and essay 185 (*Seder Structuralism*)  
- The Statue of Liberty replica in Belgrano, Buenos Aires (essay 197): Columbus's symbol lands physically in the neighborhood of the Libertadores geography  
- His Genoese origin places him adjacent to Goethe's Italian arc (Genoa is one hour south of the Ligurian coast Goethe traversed)

### #3 — HYPATIA OF ALEXANDRIA (c. 350–415 CE), folded as Saint Catherine

**Where she appears:** Essay 216, *LA ESPADA*, with precision:

> "The hagiography of Saint Catherine of Alexandria is said to be a Christianized version of the story of Hypatia of Alexandria (350–415 CE). Hypatia, an emblem of reason and Hellenistic values, lived during this shift to Christianity in the Roman Empire and died as a 'virgin martyr for philosophy.' She and her father, Theon, professed Plotinian Neoplatonism at the Mouseion of Alexandria… Hypatia became involved in the politics of Alexandria, advising the Roman prefect in his negotiations with the bishop. In 415 CE, she met a violent end by a mob of Christians displeased by her involvement in church politics."

And essay 213, *LAS TRES VIRGENES MARTIRES*, gives the hagiographic version (Saint Catherine): born in Cyprus (Famagusta), queen at 14, travels to Alexandria to study, defeats 50 imperial scholars in public debate, the wheel that shatters at her touch, milk flowing from her severed neck.

**Journey arc (Hypatia/Catherine composite):**  
- Born Cyprus (Famagusta) or Alexandria → childhood and education at the Mouseion of Alexandria → the Library/Serapeum research years → entry into civic politics as advisor to the Roman prefect Orestes → confrontation with Bishop Cyril's power → Lent 415: abducted by the *parabalani*, murdered on the steps of the Caesareum → her ghost becomes Saint Catherine, who then haunts Joan of Arc's sword at Fierbois  
- The sword buried in the church in France was rumored to have belonged to Charles Martel — Hypatia's martyrdom in Alexandria, travelling through Catherine's death, re-emerging as the blade Joan pulls from the earth: a thousand-year relay of the rational-feminine sacred

**Mythology:** Alexandrian Neoplatonism / Christian martyrology. The corpus treats her as the original archetype behind Joan's patron saint — the philosopher killed at the intersection of church and state power, whose story is then laundered into hagiography. She is the ghost in Joan of Arc's sword.

**Connections:**  
- Essay 216 (*La Espada*) is literally about Joan's sword coming from Catherine/Hypatia's tradition — the quipus knot directly  
- The Neoplatonist thread runs: Plotinus → Hypatia → Porphyry → (via Goethe's Neoplatonic aesthetics) → Goethe's Italy  
- The Library of Alexandria is the ancestor of every hermit's library in the Bohemian forests (essay 180) and Goethe's own scholarly wandering  
- Her milk-at-decapitation connects to Saint Christopher's and Vishnu's/Hayagriva's decapitation mythology (essays 208, 210) — she belongs to the same sacrificial economy as the on-chain Certificate Authority's three keys (Hayagriva/Christophia/Anthony)

### #4 — GEORGE IVANOVICH GURDJIEFF (1866–1949)

**Where he appears:** Essay 175, *MORNING MAGI, MARIA ORSIC & GURDJIEFF*, fully:

> "The Russian mystic George Ivanoff Gurdjieff (1866–1949) was an important character in the development of period esoteric spiritual narratives. As a young man, Gurdjieff traveled to Central Asia, Egypt, Iran, Tibet, and Rome. After an exhaustive study of religious traditions, he characterized three approaches of spiritual cultivation… Gurdjieff proposed 'The Fourth Way,' that of the new magician, with specially composed music and a collection of ritual dances from the secret Sufi mystery school of the Sarmoung Brotherhood."

And essay 191 (*Goethe Italian Exit*) places his spiritual descendant Rudolf von Laban at Monte Verità:

> "Before coming to Monte Verità, Rudolf von Laban was steeped in a culture of magic and movement similar to that of George Gurdjieff. As a young man in Bosnia, he was taken by sword dancing the whirling dervishes, and converted to a minority Islam."

**Journey arc:**  
- Born Alexandropol/Gyumri, Armenia (Caucasus) → adolescent wanderings through the Steppe → Central Asian sacred sites, alleged encounter with the Sarmoung Brotherhood in Bokhara or Afghanistan → Egypt (sacred sites study) → Iran/Persia (Zoroastrian sources) → India/Tibet → Constantinople (1912) → Moscow, St. Petersburg (Institute) → flight from Russia through the Caucasus and Constantinople (1917–1919) → Tiflis → Paris/Fontainebleau (Prieuré des Basses-Loges, 1922) → New York teaching tours → death at the American Hospital of Paris, 1949  
- The arc is the one of the *Fourth Way* itself: the first three ways are abandoned at each major stop; the fourth is assembled from all of them

**Mythology:** The hidden Brotherhood, the traveling magician, the synthetic mystic who claims no single tradition but subsumes all of them. His mythology belongs to the same family as the Hermit (essay 180) but routed through the Steppe and Islamic world rather than the Egyptian desert.

**Connections:**  
- Gurdjieff's student and movement heir **Rudolf von Laban** intersects Goethe's undocumented exit (essay 191: Monte Verità is in the exact *lacuna* of Goethe's Italian Journey at Lago di Lugano) — these two quipus would share a node  
- Essay 175 places him alongside **Steiner** and the Theosophists, both of whom are embedded in the Goethe/Jung lineage already on-chain  
- Essay 196 (*Night Writing*) explicitly foregrounds the **Keyserling *ganas* vs. *voluntad*** axis (inscribed on chain); Gurdjieff's Fourth Way is in the same spiritual field — his quipu would reinforce the on-chain Keyserling essays  
- His route through Persia/Zoroastrianism connects to essays 177 (*Sufi Spin*) and 178 (*Mithraic subterranea*)

### #5 — ANA LEZAMA DE URINZA & DOÑA EUSTAQUIA DE SONZA, "Las Valientes Damas Peruanas de Potosí" (17th century)

**Where they appear:** Essay 204, *HORNERO*, at the climax:

> "Ana Lezama de Urinza and Doña Eustaquia de Sonza came to be the cross-dressing lesbian heroines known as 'The Valiant Peruvian Ladies of Potosí'. Ana grew up as an orphan and was adopted at age 12 into the noble Eustaquia family, as a sibling to Doña. The two were raised together in a villa overlooking the city. They were taught dance and embroidery and in secret observed the swordplay and horseback riding of their elder brother. The two would sneak out of the villa, dressed as caballeros, and would protect the weak from silver-thieving bandits that plagued the city. The two became lovers and vigilantes, and grew to leave Potosí to seek adventure on horseback, fighting bulls, playing cards, and drawing swords on any man who crossed them. Legend says they stored the recovered gold and silver retrieved from their duels with villains in the clay abodes of the red-hatted hornero."

**Journey arc:**  
- Orphan adoption → villa education in dance, embroidery, and (secret) swordplay → nocturnal vigilantism in Potosí's silver-haunted streets (the *Micu Runa Urqu*, man-eating mountain, as backdrop) → departure on horseback → itinerant adventure across the Andean altiplano, bullfights, card tables, duels → ultimate fate unknown (legend, not record)  
- The gold and silver hidden in the *hornero*'s clay oven-nest: the $ sign recovered and redistributed; dead labor made living again

**Mythology:** The runaway bride meets the Amazonian warrior woman of the Andes — they are the Andean version of the same gender-transgressive arc as Joan of Arc (essay 215: Articles 8–16), Thecla, and Mama Huaco (essay 220: the violent founding woman of Cusco). Their story condenses the entire corpus's argument about women, embroidery, swordplay, and subterranean wealth into a single myth.

**Connections:**  
- Potosí's *mit'a* system, the $-sign etymology, and General Belgrano's seizure of the Potosí mines (essay 204) place them literally at the birth of the Rioplatense money economy — their quipu would knot directly into Belgrano's  
- The gender-transgression pattern (dance + embroidery → sword + horse) rhymes exactly with Joan of Arc's transformation (essays 215–217): a deliberate structural echo the corpus has already laid  
- The **hornero** (national bird of Argentina) as their treasure-chest links their Bolivian-Andean story to the Buenos Aires geography of the Libertadores  
- Essay 220 (*La Coronación*) makes Mama Huaco the mythic ancestor of Peruvian women guerrilla leaders; Las Valientes are the historical midpoint in that lineage, between the Inca founding mother and Edith Lagos of Sendero Luminoso

## FINAL RANKING WITH RATIONALE

1. **HANNIBAL** — the clearest geographic itinerary of any non-listed figure in the corpus; the mythic register (fire-oath, Tanit, Alps crossing) is fully developed in essay 199; connects to Goethe's Italy, Columbus's Iberia, and the Canaanite saint-chain; the corpus explicitly presents him as a thread "formative in the collective unconscious of Western civilization."

2. **COLUMBUS** — the essay argues explicitly for his journey as a mythic-Jewish diaspora act, not merely a discovery; the Columbus/Joan/Marianne/Miss Columbia chain is already drawn in essay 197; the Libertadores connection is literal (the Bartholdi *Libertas* replica in Belgrano, Buenos Aires).

3. **HYPATIA / CATHERINE** — she is the hidden ancestor of the entire Joan of Arc cycle; the corpus traces the sword of Fierbois to her; her death in Alexandria is the mirror-image of Joan's death in Rouen — philosopher-woman killed at the border of church and civil power; her quipu would be the deep-time prequel to Joan's.

4. **GURDJIEFF** — the traveling magician figure that Goethe's wandering anticipates and Laban's body-work extends; his quipu through Central Asia, Egypt, Iran, Tibet, and Paris is the only itinerary in the corpus that rivals the Goethe/Francis model of "the journey as self-transformation"; directly knots into the on-chain Keyserling essays.

5. **LAS VALIENTES DE POTOSÍ** — the most unexpected recommendation and the one the corpus most quietly earns: two women who learned embroidery and dance, secretly watched swordplay, and became cross-dressing vigilantes of the man-eating silver mountain. Their story is the Andean folding of Joan of Arc, and their treasure hidden in the hornero's clay oven is a perfect closing image — dead silver made living again, hidden in the national bird of Argentina. The Colegio Invisible would recognize them.
