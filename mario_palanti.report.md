# Mario Palanti, 1885-1978: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Milanese architect of the Rio de la Plata; builder of the Palacio Barolo (Buenos Aires, 1919-1923) and the Palacio Salvo (Montevideo, 1923-1928), the twin Dante-coded towers on opposite banks of the same river.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or placement, not documented in any reachable source. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; where the research pool was wrong or invented, the correction is recorded under its phase rather than applied silently.

The evidentiary base is thin and almost entirely tertiary: the en, es and it Wikipedia articles on Palanti, plus those on the Barolo, the Salvo, the Palacio Alcorta and the Civico Mausoleo Palanti. The three editions disagree on at least five points. There is no reachable archival layer, no correspondence, no text by Palanti himself, and the single monograph, Cresti and Cruciani Fabozzi (2015), was not reached.

---

## 1. Milan: the carpenter's son, the Brera, the Politecnico (1885-1909)

- Born in Milan, 20 September 1885 **[A: en.wikipedia and it.wikipedia, "Mario Palanti"]**.
- Son of Giovanni Palanti, a carpenter, and Virginia De Gasperi, a dressmaker; family origins in the province of Cremona **[A: it.wikipedia]**. Spelling drift, not adjudicated: it.wikipedia gives De Gaspari in the Civico Mausoleo article and De Gasperi in the Palanti article.
- Brother: Giuseppe Palanti, painter of the Milanese Divisionist and poster scene **[A: it.wikipedia]**. Cremona is a genealogical tie only: **no source records Mario Palanti visiting it [R]**, and the atlas gives it no stop.
- Enters the Accademia di Brera at nineteen to study painting. **Contradiction, not resolved:** it.wikipedia dates the entry 1904, es.wikipedia 1905 **[A on both sides, incompatible]**.
- Also studies architecture at the Politecnico di Milano; it.wikipedia dates the degree 1909, the year he leaves for Argentina **[A: en.wikipedia, it.wikipedia]**. The school's address in those years is unreachable, so the pin is the generic Milan coordinate **[R on the pin]**.
- Gold medal at an International Exhibition in Brussels **[A as fact: en.wikipedia; no year given]**. The atlas places it at 1910, the Brussels Exposition Universelle, as a placement and not an attestation, and notes that it collides with his documented presence in Buenos Aires that year. **Unresolved.** The stop is ordered after the 1909 Genoa departure so the segment reads in date order; that ordering is a display convention and does not make the year attested.

## 2. The crossing and the Centenario (1909-1911)

- Arrives in Buenos Aires in 1909, aged twenty-four, to work on the Italian Pavilion for the coming centenary of the May Revolution **[A: en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia]**.
- **Port of departure is a reconstruction [R].** No manifest, ship name or sailing date is reachable. Genoa is the standard 1909 emigrant port for northern Italy and stands in the atlas as a reconstructed stop.
- Works first in the studio of Arturo Prins and Oskar Ranzenhofer **[A: it.wikipedia, "lavorò in primo luogo nello studio di Arturo Prins ed Oskar Ranzenhofer"; es.wikipedia]**. The spelling Razenhofer appears in one source.
  - **Correction.** The pool's "1909-1914" span and "for five years" are inventions: **no source gives an end date [R]**, and the address is unrecorded, so the atlas uses the canonical city pin.
- 1910: works on the Italian Pavilion of the Railway and Land Transport section of the Centenario exposition **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool placed this in Palermo park. es.wikipedia, "Exposición Internacional del Centenario", puts the Ferrocarriles y Transportes Terrestres exposition "en los predios del Regimiento 1 de Patricios", inaugurated 17 July 1910; Palermo hosted Agriculture and Industry. The pin moves to the Patricios grounds and is tagged **[R]**, since the 1910 barracks perimeter is itself undocumented.
- 1911: collaborates on the new Faculty of Law building **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool called the building's fate unrecoverable. it.wikipedia identifies it as the neo-Gothic Facultad de Derecho of the Prins and Ranzenhofer studio, "un edificio simbolo dello stile neogotico", never completed as a law faculty, today the UBA Facultad de Ingeniería, Las Heras 2214. Pin corrected to -34.58876, -58.39663 (OSM way 18613538), 1.6 km from the pool's.

## 3. The first Buenos Aires practice (1913-1915)

- 1913: the Costaguta residence and a rental building for F. Cassotana **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Addresses unrecorded; no atlas stop.
- 1914: the Edificio de los Atlantes on Avenida Rivadavia, residential blocks, and the Cinema Presidente Roca **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** Av. Rivadavia 1916 geocodes to -34.60943, -58.39370 (OSM way 265311278, Balvanera). The pool's -34.6079, -58.3847 lands in Monserrat, six blocks east.
- 1915: the Grimoldi chalet, since demolished **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Address unrecorded; pinned on the canonical city coordinate. Style of the whole 1909-1919 period: variations of Renaissance Revival and Art Nouveau **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 4. The Genio militare (1915-1918)

- Returns to Italy and serves as a lieutenant in the Genio militare on the Trentino front, to the end of the war **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **His sector is named in no reachable source [R].** The atlas uses the Asiago plateau, a principal engineering theatre of that front, as an explicitly reconstructed placeholder.
- The armistice stop is dated 4 November 1918, when the Villa Giusti armistice takes effect; that is not a date attested for Palanti, and his location is unknown **[R]**.
- **Gap.** Nothing reachable records how or when he crossed back to Italy in 1915, or back to Argentina afterwards.

## 5. The Barolo (1919-1923)

- Construction of the Palacio Barolo, Avenida de Mayo 1370, begins in 1919 **[A: en.wikipedia infobox, construction_start_date; es.wikipedia gives the span 1919-1923]**.
- **Correction.** The pool's "discharged from the Italian army, returns to Buenos Aires in 1919" **is attested nowhere in en, es or it Wikipedia [R]**: not the discharge, not the voyage, not the year. It is inferred backwards from the Barolo start, and the stop is tagged a reconstruction. Luis Barolo was a "empresario textil" (es.wikipedia), not the pool's "knitwear magnate".
- **Correction.** Barolo coordinate. en.wikipedia carries `{{coord|34|36|34.4|S|58|23|9.1|W}}` = -34.609556, -58.385861. The pool's geography lens gave -34.6092, -58.3822, about 330 m east, disagreeing with its own afterlife lens.
- The Dante scheme **[A as design intent: en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia]**: 100 m for the 100 cantos; 22 floors, basement and ground as Inferno, 1 to 14 Purgatorio, 15 to 22 Paradiso; nine vaulted bays with Latin inscriptions for the nine circles; the beacon for the nine choirs of angels; the spire on the Southern Cross on 9 July. **Caveat [R]:** the full symbolic elaboration is an interpretive synthesis of the encyclopaedia articles, and no reachable source quotes Palanti's own words for it.
- 1920: the Edificio Roccatagliata **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** it.wikipedia places it at Avenida Santa Fe and Avenida Callao, roughly -34.59474, -58.39304. The pool's pin sat 1.9 km away in Monserrat.
- The tradition that the tower was meant as a refuge for Dante's remains is **[R]**: widely circulated, not documented in the reachable articles, and dante.journey.json records the remains at Ravenna without interruption.
- **Correction.** The pool made Barolo the tallest in South America until the Kavanagh of the mid-1930s. it.wikipedia gives the Salvo the Latin American record from 1928 to 1935; es.wikipedia: "Hasta los años 1930 fue el más alto de la ciudad y segundo de América del Sur ya que su hermano gemelo el Palacio Salvo, midiendo 105 metros era el primero". Correct: tallest in South America 1923 to 1928, tallest in Buenos Aires until 1935 or 1936.

## 6. Rome, the Mole Littoria, the decoration (1924-1927)

- 1924: proposes the Mole Littoria to Mussolini, an eighty-eight-storey, 335 m monument for Rome; Marcello Piacentini's criticism sinks it **[A: it.wikipedia]**. 1926: publishes *L'Eternale: mole littoria - Roma MCMXXVI* **[A: it.wikipedia, Scritti]**.
- **The venue of the proposal is a reconstruction [R].** it.wikipedia confirms the city (the tower was to stand "in Roma") but not the room. The atlas pins Palazzo Chigi, Mussolini's seat at that date. Coordinate corrected to 41.90141, 12.48002 (OSM relation 325553).
- Commander of the Order of the Crown of Italy **[A: en.wikipedia, it.wikipedia; no date in either]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool left the date blank twice. A terminus ante quem is recoverable: it.wikipedia's honours citation is the Gazzetta Ufficiale of 6 July 1927, printing R.D. 12 maggio 1927 n. 999, which styles him "comm. Mario Palanti", so he held the rank by May 1927. The same decree authorises the Fondazione Mario Palanti at the Regia scuola d'architettura in Rome to accept two donations from him, a 1927 Rome tie the pool missed. The stop is dated 12 May 1927, a decree date and not a ceremony date.
- Academician of San Luca and of the Accademia dei Virtuosi al Pantheon **[A: en.wikipedia; no dates]**. Folded into the same stop, pinned at the Virtuosi's Pantheon seat; **the pin is a stand-in [R]**, the Regia scuola's 1927 address being unreachable.

## 7. The twin on the other bank, and the late Argentine work (1919-1929)

- 1919: the Salvo brothers, Angel, Jose and Lorenzo, buy the Plaza Independencia plot held by the confectionery La Giralda, where "La cumparsita" premiered in 1917, and commission Palanti **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** Design or commission 1922 (es, it); cornerstone 1923 (it); construction start 1925 (en). Probably three different facts rather than one disputed fact, but no reachable source says so, and all three are left standing.
- **Salvo coordinate.** Three pool candidates: -34.9067/-56.1983, -34.906725/-56.198308 (matching the en.wikipedia coord exactly), -34.9057/-56.1958. The atlas uses the second byte-identically at every Salvo stop.
- 1925-1928: construction; twenty-seven floors, 105 m to the tip, Art Deco massing with Gothic and Neoclassical ornament **[A: it.wikipedia]**. Inaugurated 1928 **[A: en.wikipedia, it.wikipedia]**.
- 1927: the Tienda London París, Paysandú **[A: es.wikipedia work list]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool said no name or address survives and left the date blank. Both name and year are in the source it cites.
- 1927-1928: the Palacio Automotor, later Palacio Alcorta, Av. Figueroa Alcorta 3351, a showroom for the Resta brothers' Chrysler dealership with a rooftop test track, the Estadio Olimpo **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool logged 1927 against 1928 as an unresolved discrepancy. It is neither: es.wikipedia gives "proyectado originalmente en 1927" and "inaugurado el 1 de diciembre de 1928", carried here as two stops. Coordinate corrected to -34.57741, -58.40309 (OSM relation 12403783).
- 1927-1928: the Hotel Excelsior, Avenida de Mayo 1152, later the Hotel Castelar **[A: es.wikipedia, it.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** Coordinate corrected to -34.60936, -58.38272 (OSM way 476772574). The pool also attached "the exiled poet Federico Garcia Lorca" to the hotel: unsourced in the consulted pages, and Lorca was in Buenos Aires in 1933-34 on a lecture tour, not in exile. **Dropped**, not hedged.
- 1924-1928: the funerary aedicule at the Cimitero Monumentale di Milano, inaugurated 1929, Riparto V, Edicola 83, in rose granite from Baveno **[A: it.wikipedia, "Civico Mausoleo Palanti"]**.
  - **Correction.** Not designed as his own tomb: "Venne progettata inizialmente come tomba per i genitori" (it.wikipedia), Giovanni Palanti and Virginia De Gaspari. The threshold reads "PER ETERNARE LA MEMORIA DE'MIEI CARI GENITORI HO ERETTO QUESTO TEMPIO"; the year is cut on the rear face as MCMXXIX, anno VII.
  - **Gap.** Whether he travelled to Milan between 1924 and 1928 to oversee the work, or worked by drawing and post, is nowhere stated.
  - **Coordinate note.** The pool's two lenses disagree by about 280 m here, 45.4842/9.1783 against 45.4847/9.1819; the cemetery's OSM centroid is 45.4877/9.1778. The atlas uses 45.4842, 9.1783, effectively the Famedio entrance, rather than pretending to a plot-level fix.

## 8. Milan again (1930-1946)

- Leaves Argentina and returns to live in Italy, 1930 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Port and vessel unrecorded **[R]**.
- Palandomus, a patented dry-construction system **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool dated it to the 1930s and to "back in Milan". it.wikipedia: "A cavallo degli anni '20-'30 progettò un sistema, brevettato, per erigere costruzioni a secco: il Palandomus". It straddles the decade, so it begins while he is still in South America. The patent date is a gap.
- 1934: *Palazzo del Littorio: progetto*. 1935: *Auditorium: progetti*. 1946: *Architettura per tutti* **[A: it.wikipedia, Scritti]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool's list carries a title absent from the source, "Torre Littoria: progetti (1935)", and omits one present in it, *Palazzo del Littorio: progetto* (1934).
- 1942: the ramps for Milano Centrale station **[A: it.wikipedia]**. 7 January 1943: marries Nahir Marazzi Castiglioni in Milan, aged fifty-seven **[A: it.wikipedia]**; no church or registry named **[R]**.

## 9. Death and burial (1974-1981)

- 1974: donates the Edicola to the Comune di Milano **[A: it.wikipedia, "Civico Mausoleo Palanti"]**.
- 4 September 1978: dies in Milan, aged 92 **[A: en.wikipedia, it.wikipedia]**.
  - **Contradiction, not resolved.** es.wikipedia hedges the year as "4 de septiembre de 1978 o 4 de septiembre de 1979"; neither figure is verified against a registry.
- Buried in the family aedicule **[A: en.wikipedia, it.wikipedia]**. **The day of burial is a gap**; the atlas defaults the stop to 1978-01-01 rather than inventing a September date.
- **Correction.** The pool twice called the 1978 tomb the "Civico Mausoleo Palanti", which is anachronistic: "Il 4 febbraio 1981 l'Edicola venne ufficialmente denominata 'Civico Mausoleo Palanti'" (it.wikipedia). At the burial it was still the Edicola Palanti; the renaming is a separate 1981 stop.

## 10. Afterlife (1996-2017)

- 1996: the Palacio Salvo declared a National Historic Monument of Uruguay **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- 1997: the Palacio Barolo declared a National Historic Monument of Argentina, Decreto 437/97 **[A: es.wikipedia, citing infoleg.gov.ar]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool logged 1989 against 1997 and put 1989 in the date field. The 1989 figure appears only in the unsourced en.wikipedia infobox `designation_date`; en.wikipedia's body text says 1997 and es.wikipedia says it twice with the decree number.
- 25 September 2009 to 25 May 2010: the Barolo beacon relit as the Faro del Bicentenario, a 5,000 W lamp in place of an original arc of 300,000 candelas **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** Not "lit nightly": "cada día 25 de cada mes se iluminó durante media hora el cielo nocturno porteño desde la torre, hasta el 25 de mayo de 2010". The 25th of each month, half an hour.
- Since 2015: the Barolo beacon lit pink on October nights for breast cancer awareness **[A: es.wikipedia]**. No atlas stop; recorded here.
- 28 April 2017: a new LED beacon on the Salvo dome; the gates reinstalled 13 July 2017 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
  - **Correction.** The pool bundled the stained glass and facade restoration into 2017; en.wikipedia puts that programme at "since 2021".
- November 2012: the television antenna that had replaced the Salvo's original beacon is permanently removed **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **When the original lighthouse came down is unknown, and en.wikipedia says so [R, gap declared by the source]**.
- 2012: the documentary *El rascacielos latino*; 2023: the novel *La otra orilla* **[A: es.wikipedia, titles and years only]**.

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **Brera entry: 1904 (it) against 1905 (es).**
2. **Death year: 1978 (en, it) against "1978 or 1979" (es).**
3. **Salvo: design or commission 1922, cornerstone 1923, construction start 1925.** Three dates, three sources, none reconciling them.
4. **Barolo monument year: 1997** in both bodies of text and with a decree number, **1989** in the en.wikipedia infobox. Adjudicated for 1997, stray recorded.
5. **The mother's surname: De Gasperi against De Gaspari**, across two it.wikipedia articles. **Ranzenhofer against Razenhofer.**
6. **Two coordinate disagreements inside the research pool itself**, for the Barolo and the Milan tomb, both fixed above to the source coordinate where one exists.

## Gaps, stated as gaps

- No text by Palanti was reached. Six titles are catalogued by it.wikipedia, none digitised anywhere reachable, and no translation was found. Absence of translations is a gap, not proof.
- No quotation *by* Palanti was found anywhere. The single candidate, "un enamorado de las curvas", is about him, comes from a 2023 La Nación centenary piece, and two fetches of that article disagreed on whether the phrase belongs to the heritage historian Leonel Contreras or to the reporter. Carried once, tagged low confidence.
- The 1909 crossing, the 1915 return to Italy, the return to Argentina after the war and the 1930 departure all lack ship, port and date. The Trentino sector is unnamed. Whether he supervised the Milan tomb in person is unknown.
- His fascist sympathies are asserted by the 2015 monograph's subtitle and by the Mole Littoria and Palazzo del Littorio projects. Beyond the projects, no reachable source documents a political affiliation, and the atlas asserts none.

## Atlas interlocks

Palanti, Barolo and Palacio Salvo appear in no existing journey file; a grep across all `*.journey.json` returns nothing outside `QUEUE.md` and the census. Every crossing below is geographic and thematic, not attested contact. All are named in campa.

- **dante** and **virgil**: the Comedy is the Barolo's template and the tower's ascent literalises the Inferno and Purgatorio itinerary. **victoria_ocampo**: *De Francesca a Beatrice*, 1924, the year after the Barolo opens. **borges**: writes on Dante all his life at the same Buenos Aires pin.
- **le_corbusier** and **keyserling**: both at Villa Ocampo in 1929. **mussolini** and **dannunzio**: the Mole Littoria and the Fiume years; **guido_keller** shares the same 1919-1922 calendar and is deliberately not named, to keep the stop from becoming a list.
- **alvear**: president when the Barolo opens. **julio_argentino_roca**: dies 1914, the year of the Atlantes and of the cinema carrying his name. **alberto_gerchunoff**: the immigrant-writer generation. **claude_levi_strauss**: the 1935 landing his own file flags as unconfirmed.
- **Canonical pin inherited byte-identically:** Buenos Aires -34.6037, -58.3816, verified against `victoria_ocampo.journey.json`, already load-bearing for at least eleven journeys, and used here for every stop that is "Buenos Aires" rather than a building. **Montevideo is originated here:** no existing journey pins Plaza Independencia.
