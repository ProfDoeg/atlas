# Benjamin Franklin (1706-1790): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Printer, currency theorist, postmaster, natural philosopher, Freemason, and the only man to sign the Declaration, the Treaty of Alliance, the Treaty of Paris, and the Constitution.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim circulating without a firsthand source. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Coordinate disagreements between research lenses are recorded where they occurred.

**Calendar.** Born 6 January 1705 Julian, the calendar then in use in the British colonies; 17 January 1706 is the Gregorian equivalent, and is the date used in the journey file. **[A]** The doubled year is the Old Style / New Style shift, not a source conflict.

**Sole-witness caution.** Much of the pre-1750 material rests on the *Autobiography*, written decades later by a man arranging his own legend. The geography lens tagged some of it **[R]** where the chronology lens tagged it **[A]**. Both tags are preserved below.

## 1. Boston, 1706-1723

- Born on Milk Street, fifteenth of seventeen children of Josiah Franklin, tallow chandler and soap boiler. **[A: *Autobiography*; Wikipedia; Britannica]**
- Baptized the same day at Old South, across the street. **[A: Old South records; Isaacson]** *Coordinate flag:* two positions produced, 42.3567, -71.0603 and Wikipedia's 42.35694, -71.05861, about 140 m apart; the verified value is used. Second correction: the meeting house standing today was built in 1729, so the 1706 baptism was in the earlier cedar Third Church building on the same ground. **[A]**
- Boston Latin School under two years; withdrawn before ten for cost. **[A: *Autobiography*]** Folded into the birth stop, no separate pin.
- 1718: apprenticed at twelve to his brother James, printer. **[A]**
- 1721: James founds *The New-England Courant*, Boston's third newspaper; Benjamin sets type. **[A]**
- 1722: writes anonymous Silence Dogood letters at sixteen; runs the paper during James's three-week imprisonment. **[A: Wikipedia; *Autobiography*]** Tagged **[R]** by the geography lens on sole-witness grounds. Both recorded.
- 1723: breaks the indenture and runs to Philadelphia. **[A: Wikipedia; History.com]**

## 2. Philadelphia and London, 1723-1728

- Arrives by boat and walks up Market Street with rolls under his arm. **[A: *Autobiography*]**
- 1724-1726: London, reportedly on Governor William Keith's promise of backing, which never materializes. **[A as to trip and failure; the Keith promise reaches this report through History.com and the *Autobiography*, Franklin again sole narrator]**
- Compositor at Samuel Palmer's house, Bartholomew Close, then at John Watts's near Lincoln's Inn Fields. **[A: *Autobiography*]** Merged into one stop. 1726: returns to Philadelphia and the trade. **[A]**
- 1727: founds the Junto. **[A per chronology lens; [R] per geography lens, the *Autobiography* in both cases]** Flagged, not resolved.
- 1728: partnership with Hugh Meredith. **[A]**
- 1728-1729: prints New Jersey's currency at Burlington. *Tag contradiction corrected:* the chronology lens tagged this **[R]** on "general consensus." It is directly attested in the *Autobiography* and in Van Doren, and the geography lens tagged it **[A]** independently. Carried as **[A]**.

## 3. Paper money, Poor Richard, civic machinery, 1729-1748

A grep of every existing `*.journey.json` for "paper currency" and "Modest Enquiry" returns no hits; this material is new to the corpus. **[A: absence confirmed by grep]**

- April 1729: publishes *A Modest Enquiry into the Nature and Necessity of a Paper Currency* anonymously, arguing that an adequate paper supply lowers interest and speeds trade in a colony starved of coin. **[A: the pamphlet; History.com]**
- **Contradiction corrected: the Gazette.** The chronology lens made him "sole owner and publisher" in 1729. He and Meredith bought the paper from Samuel Keimer on 2 October 1729; he became sole owner in July 1730 on buying Meredith out. The file carries the two-stage account. **[A]**
- The pamphlet helps win the contract to print Pennsylvania's currency. Chronology lens: **[R]**, no fetched primary source. The *Autobiography* states it plainly. Carried **[A]**, Franklin again his own witness.
- Nature-printed leaf impressions on the currency as a counterfeiting defense. **[A per the afterlife lens, sourced to Isaacson; no primary citation reached, so biography-standard rather than document-verified.]**
- **Contradiction corrected: William Franklin.** The chronology lens dates a public acknowledgement of the illegitimate son to 22 February 1730. That is the traditional birth date, itself disputed across sources ranging 1728 to 1731, and no acknowledgement event is dated anywhere reachable. The file says only that William is born about 1730 and raised in the household. **[R as to date; A as to household]**
- 1 September 1730: common-law marriage with Deborah Read. **[A]**
- 1731: initiated at St John's Lodge, Philadelphia. **[A: Wikipedia]** No secure location reached for the lodge; folded into the Library Company stop rather than pinned. Gap.
- July 1731: founds the Library Company of Philadelphia, first subscription library in the colonies. **[A: institutional history]**
- 1732: begins *Poor Richard's Almanack*, running to 1758. **[A]** The December 1732 publication date circulates but is unconfirmed; the stop is hedged.
- 1734: Grand Master of the Pennsylvania Freemasons; reprints Anderson's *Constitutions of the Free-Masons*, the first Masonic book printed in the Americas. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- December 1736: founds the Union Fire Company. **[A: *Autobiography*]** Cut from the journey file for length. 1737: postmaster of Philadelphia. **[A: History.com; USPS]**
- 1743: founds the American Philosophical Society. **[A: APS institutional history]**
- 1748: leaves active management of the printing business. **[A: History.com]** The partner who took over is not named by any source fetched this session. Gap.

## 4. Lightning, the post, the first union, 1749-1754

- 1749: first president of the Academy and College of Philadelphia, to 1754. **[A: Wikipedia]** Cut from the journey file: no reliable coordinate for its first building, and inventing one would be worse than omitting the stop. Gap.
- 1751: co-founds Pennsylvania Hospital with Thomas Bond on a matching-funds scheme. **[A: *Autobiography*]**
- **June 1752: the kite. Contradiction flagged.** The chronology lens tagged the experiment **[A]** and put the lightning rod after it as a consequence. Geography and afterlife lenses both tagged it **[R]**. Verification supports **[R]** on two grounds. *Sequence reversed:* Franklin published the pointed-rod proposal in 1750, before the kite, and it appeared in *Poor Richard's* for 1753; the June 1752 kite was a workaround for lacking a tall enough structure. *Testimony:* the earliest surviving account is Priestley's (1767), fifteen years later and secondhand, Franklin left no firsthand narrative, and Dalibard had already drawn lightning at Marly-la-Ville in May 1752. Carried **[R]**, hedge in `date_confidence`, sequence stated in the campa.
- 1753: joint deputy postmaster general for the British colonies. **[A]** Dismissed 1774, days after the Cockpit. **[A]**
- **Contradiction corrected: the Albany Plan.** Both lenses dated it June 1754. The congress sat 19 June to 11 July and the Plan was approved 10 July 1754. Place (Stadt Huys, Albany city hall) correct in both. The file carries 1754-07-10. **[A]**

## 5. Craven Street, 1757-1775

- 1757: agent for the Pennsylvania Assembly against the Penn family's tax exemptions; lodges with the Stevensons at 36 Craven Street to 1762. **[A: Benjamin Franklin House archives]** *Coordinate flag:* geography lens 51.5079, -0.1234; Wikipedia 51.5075, -0.1250, about 110 m apart. Verified value used.
- February 1759: St Andrews awards an honorary Doctor of Laws for the electrical work, origin of "Doctor Franklin." **[A: degree records]**
- September 1759: made a burgess of Edinburgh; meets David Hume on a Scottish tour. **[A: Hume correspondence; Isaacson]**
- April 1762: Oxford honorary doctorate; the 1753 Copley Medal had already made him a scientific celebrity. **[A]** Cut from the journey file for length.
- 1764-1775: second Craven Street residency, agent for Pennsylvania and later Georgia, New Jersey, Massachusetts. **[A]**
- February 1766: testifies before the Commons against the Stamp Act; repeal follows within weeks. **[A: History.com; Journals of the House of Commons]** The standard sitting date of 13 February was not confirmed against the Journals this session; hedged.
- 29 January 1774: Wedderburn berates him for about an hour before the Privy Council in the Cockpit over the Hutchinson letters; Franklin stands silent and is dismissed as deputy postmaster general days later. **[A: contemporary accounts in Van Doren]** The velvet suit worn again at the 1778 signing is **[R]**; the campa marks it as tradition.

## 6. Congress, the Atlantic, Passy, 1775-1777

- May 1775: returns and is elected to the Second Continental Congress. **[A: History.com]**
- April 1776: Montreal commissioner at seventy, in poor health; the mission fails and he falls seriously ill. **[A: Journals of the Continental Congress]** The Château de Ramezay is the commission's known headquarters.
- June-July 1776: on the committee of five; he and Adams mark Jefferson's draft lightly at the Graff house, and he sits beside Jefferson during Congress's cuts on 4 July. **[A: `thomas_jefferson.journey.json`; Journals]**
- **Contradiction corrected: the signing.** The geography lens dates his signature to 4 July 1776. The engrossed parchment was signed by most delegates, Franklin among them, on 2 August 1776. The file dates the stop 1776-08-02 and treats 4 July as adoption. **[A]**
- 11 September 1776: meets Lord Howe with Adams and Rutledge at the Billopp house, Staten Island; the conference fails because Howe cannot treat with Congress as independent. **[A: Adams, *Autobiography*]** Cut for length. *Coordinate flag for whoever restores it:* geography lens 40.4996, -74.2544, about 370 m south of the house; Wikipedia 40.50286, -74.25378.
- **Contradiction corrected: the covert French aid.** The chronology lens credits Franklin with securing secret loans and clandestine assistance from Vergennes in 1777. Covert aid was authorized in May 1776 through Beaumarchais's front company Roderigue Hortalez et Cie, seven months before Franklin reached France. He negotiated further loans in 1777 but extended an existing pipeline rather than opening one. The file says so.
- 3 December 1776: lands near Auray in Brittany after six weeks on the sloop *Reprisal*. **[A: Franklin Papers; Van Doren]** He rests at Nantes before Paris; that stop was cut for length.
- 1777-1785: Hôtel de Valentinois at Passy, lent rent-free by Le Ray de Chaumont; private press installed. **[A: Franklin correspondence]**
  - *Pin inherited.* `mesmer.journey.json` pins Franklin's Passy residence at 48.8546, 2.2789. Carried byte-identical into both Passy stops, in preference to the interlock lens's 48.8567, 2.276 and the geography lens's 48.8558, 2.2749.

## 7. The alliance and the return, 1778-1785

- **Contradiction corrected: where the treaties were signed.** The largest geographic error the research produced. The geography lens places the 6 February 1778 signing at Franklin's own Passy residence (48.8558, 2.2749). The treaties were signed at the Hôtel de Coislin, the Hôtel de Crillon in English sources, 4 Place de la Concorde, where a plaque records it. The file uses approximately 48.8672, 2.3216. Signatories Franklin, Deane, Lee. **[A: Wikipedia, "Treaty of Alliance (1778)"]** His standing in French salon society is credited with helping win the alliance; Jefferson's eyewitness testimony is quoted in the file.
- 20 March 1778: Louis XVI receives the commissioners at Versailles. **[A: French court records]** Pin inherited: 48.8049, 2.1204, the corpus Versailles pin from `louis_xvi.journey.json` and `marques_de_lafayette.journey.json`.
- **Tag contradiction corrected: the Nine Sisters.** The chronology lens tagged the lodge membership **[R]**, "not independently confirmed." Wikipedia's "Les Neuf Soeurs" documents acceptance in 1778, election as Master in 1779, re-election in 1780. Carried **[A]**. John Paul Jones and Bailly admitted the same year. **[A]**
- 4 April 1778: Franklin with Court de Gébelin conducts Voltaire's initiation; Voltaire dies a month later. **[A: Wikipedia, "Les Neuf Soeurs"]**
- 3 September 1783: Treaty of Paris signed with Adams and Jay at the Hôtel d'York. **[A: National Archives]**
- **Contradiction corrected: the balloon and the baby.** The 1 December 1783 crewed hydrogen launch from the Tuileries with Franklin present is **[A]**. "What use is a newborn baby" has no contemporary source and is variously attached to the unmanned Champ de Mars flight of 27 August 1783 instead. The file carries the flight as fact and the remark as **[R]**, unquoted.
- March to August 1784: named by Louis XVI to the commission on animal magnetism; too gouty to be carried into Paris, so the blindfolded-boy tree trial runs in his own Passy garden. The report of 11 August, signed by Franklin, Bailly, Lavoisier, Guillotin and others, finds no fluid. **[A: `mesmer.journey.json`; the report]**
- 1785: Jefferson succeeds him and leases the Hôtel de Langeac. **[A: `thomas_jefferson.journey.json`]** Pin inherited byte-identical: 48.8721, 2.305. July 1785: Southampton, the last meeting with his estranged son William. **[A: Franklin correspondence]**

## 8. Franklin Court and after, 1785-1790

- September 1785: returns to Philadelphia. **[A]**
- 18 October 1785: President of Pennsylvania, chief executive of the Supreme Executive Council, to 1788. **[A]**
- May to September 1787: oldest delegate to the Constitutional Convention at eighty-one, carried in a sedan chair; closing speech urges unanimous support despite his own doubts. **[A: Madison's *Notes*]**
- **Contradiction corrected: "one of only three men."** The chronology lens claims Franklin was one of three to sign the Declaration, the Treaty of Paris, and the Constitution. The Treaty of Paris was signed by Adams, Franklin and Jay; neither Adams (then in London) nor Jay signed the Constitution. Franklin is the **only** man to sign all three, and the only signer of all four founding documents once the 1778 Treaty of Alliance is counted. **[A]**
- 17 April 1790: dies at home at eighty-four; roughly twenty thousand attend the funeral. **[A: *Pennsylvania Gazette* obituary]**
- 21 April 1790: buried beside Deborah in Christ Church Burial Ground under a flat slab reading "Benjamin and Deborah Franklin, 1790," per his own instruction. **[A: Christ Church Preservation Trust; his will of 1788]** *Coordinate flag, three-way:* geography lens 39.9526, -75.1489; afterlife lens 39.9528, -75.1499, which lands outside the burial ground; Wikipedia 39.95194, -75.14861. The file uses 39.9519, -75.1486.
- **The self-composed epitaph was truncated in the pool without an ellipsis.** The pool ends "CORRECTED AND AMENDED." Gutenberg e-text #48136 ends "CORRECTED AND AMENDED BY THE AUTHOR." and reads "PRINTER." with a period, not a comma. The file carries the full text. **[A]**
- Pennies tossed onto the grave through the sidewalk railings, popularly tied to "a penny saved is a penny earned." **[R: the practice is attested by Wikipedia; when and why it began is documented nowhere reachable.]**
- 1858: bronze rails set into the brick wall for public viewing, arranged for the Franklin Institute after Bostonians criticized Philadelphia for neglecting the grave. **[A]**
- 1791: the *Autobiography* first appears in Paris as *Mémoires de la vie privée*, **Part One only**, from a flawed transcript predating Franklin's revisions. **[A: Wikipedia; the pool implied the whole work and is corrected.]** London retranslations 1793, French 1798; William Temple Franklin's three-part English edition 1818; Bigelow's first accurate complete edition 1868. **[A]**
- 1998: renovation of 36 Craven Street uncovers bones from roughly ten bodies, several children, traced to William Hewson, an anatomist who dissected in the house in secret, not to Franklin. Museum opened January 2006. **[A: Wikipedia, "Benjamin Franklin House"]**
- Franklin's portrait on the hundred-dollar bill, a face on American paper currency who was never president. **[A]** Not a stop.

## Honest gaps

1. **St John's Lodge, Philadelphia**: no securely located meeting place in any reachable source. The 1731 initiation is attested; the pin is not. Folded into another stop rather than invented.
2. **The Academy and College of Philadelphia**, 1749: no reliable coordinate for its first building. Stop omitted rather than guessed.
3. **First *Poor Richard***: attested to 1732, December advertisement date unconfirmed.
4. **Stamp Act testimony date**, 13 February 1766: standard but unconfirmed against the Journals.
5. **The 1748 press partner**: not named by any source fetched this session.
6. **William Franklin's birth date**: disputed 1728 to 1731, no acknowledgement event dated.
7. **The Junto's meeting place** and **the kite site**: both reconstructed placeholders, not documented addresses.
