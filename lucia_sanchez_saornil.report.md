# Lucía Sánchez Saornil (1895-1970): research report

*2026-08-21. Poet of ultraísmo under the male pseudonym Luciano de San-Saor; telephone operator; CNT editor; co-founder of Mujeres Libres; thirty years clandestine under Franco.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the sources hedge. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Where the research pool's verification stage overturned a claim, the correction is carried here and into the journey file, and the overturned version is recorded so the error cannot come back. The two Wikipedias are the spine; there is no reachable monograph, and the one substantial secondary work touched, Ackelsberg's *Free Women of Spain*, is reached only through quotation.

---

## 1. Origins and the Peñuelas, 1895-1915

- Born **13 December 1895**, Madrid, **Peñuelas** quarter, Calle del Labrador, to **Eugenio Sánchez Rodríguez** and **Gabriela Saornil Serrano** **[A es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**.
- **Pozal de Gallinas** (Valladolid, 41.3667 / -4.8333) is the rural origin **[A]**, but the source attaches it **to the father specifically**; the pool's "her parents come from this village" is one parent too many. The wave-of-rural-migration framing is the writer's gloss, **[R]**.
- Deaths in the household: brother **José, 1900**; sister **Eleuteria**, at one year, **1901**; **mother, 1908** **[A]**. She is twelve at her mother's death. 1910s residences include **Calle Hortaleza** and **Paseo de Santa María de la Cabeza** **[A, as a residence list]**; self-taught reading beyond her schooling is **[R]**.
- **1913**: attends the **Centro Hijos de Madrid**, the "Casa de los Gatos," studying art while working **[A]**; that this was one of very few routes open to a girl of her class is **[R]**. **January 1914**: first published poem, **"Nieve,"** in the weekly **Avante** of **Ciudad Rodrigo** **[A]**. She lives in Madrid and there is no record she ever went there; the journey file pins the press, not her.

## 2. Luciano de San-Saor: the switchboard and ultraísmo, 1916-1931

- **1916**: operator at the **Compañía Madrileña de Teléfonos**, later Telefónica; the same year she begins publishing in **Los Quijotes** as **Luciano de San-Saor**, kept for most of her literary career **[A both Wikipedias]**. The exchange building is not identified anywhere reachable.
- **1916-1918**, corrected: es.wikipedia says she published *"principalmente en **Cádiz-San Fernando**, así como en la colección **Los Noveles** o en **El Progreso de Lugo**"*. **Cádiz-San Fernando is a periodical, not a place**, no individual year is assigned, and the pool's "San Fernando 1916 / Lugo 1917" is invented precision. The Lugo pieces also **predate** her ultraísta phase, so calling them ultraist verse was wrong.
- **January 1919**: adheres to **ultraísmo**; poems in **Grecia** (Seville), **Cervantes**, **Ultra**, **Tableros**, **Vértices** **[A]**. **1925**, corrected: Plural closes the **strictly ultraísta** phase (the movement ends c. 1923), but she keeps publishing six more years, in **Manantial (1929)** and **La Gaceta Literaria (1930-1931)** **[A, verbatim]**. The pool's "last contributions" overstated it.
- **March 1927**: first-class operator at the **Madrid-Alcalá interurban centre**. **September 1927**: transferred to **Valencia** on service requirements **[A]**.

## 3. The card, number 26: CNT, 1931-1935

- **29 January 1931**: she leaves the company. es.wikipedia, verbatim: *"causó baja en la Compañía el 29 de enero de 1931; readmitida en octubre de 1936."* **[A]** **14 April 1931**: Republic proclaimed. **July 1931**: CNT telephone strike; she takes part and is **briefly detained** **[A both Wikipedias]**.
- **Correction, the pool's worst error.** It made the departure a **consequence** of the Republic and the strike. It cannot be: she was gone ten weeks before the one and six months before the other, and it was not final, since she was **readmitted in October 1936**.
- **1931**: joins the **CNT Sindicato Nacional de Teléfonos**, **member number 26** **[A]**. **Party, corrected**: not "the Radical Socialist Republican Party" but *"el **Partido Radical Socialista Revolucionario**, de Balbontín, al que abandonó en 1932"* **[A]**, a different organisation. See section 11 for the chronological oddity this leaves open.
- **1932-1933**: meetings in **Cuenca, Manzanares, León, Burgos, La Horra, Talavera de la Reina, Madrid, etc.** **[A]**. The pool dated towns individually and called Talavera the last stop. **All invented**: the source gives **one range for the whole list** and ends with "etc.," explicitly non-exhaustive. The journey file names the towns in one stop and says no town can be dated.
- **1932**: manifestos on working-class women; begins working with **Mercedes Comaposada** and **Amparo Poch y Gascón** **[R]**. **1933**: talked over at a CNT meeting, she and Comaposada leave together and begin organising **[A en.wikipedia]**, the **Retiro Park** setting coming from en.wikipedia after Ackelsberg; the same year she heads the editorial department of the daily **CNT**, writing as **La Compañera X, Vigía, El Observador** **[A]**.
- **Autumn 1935**: the series **"La cuestión femenina en nuestros medios"** in **Solidaridad Obrera** **[A]**. Two corrections: (i) **venue**, en.wikipedia adds FAI's *Tierra y Libertad*, which es.wikipedia does not corroborate; **flagged, not resolved**; (ii) **rank**, **Mariano R. Vázquez was not CNT general secretary in 1935**. He headed the **Catalan regional committee** and became national general secretary only in **November 1936**.

## 4. Mujeres Libres and the war, 1936-1938

- **Founding date: contradiction, left standing.** es.wikipedia's Saornil article puts the magazine's first issue and the Madrid Agrupación in **May 1936**; its *Mujeres Libres* article says the organisation was co-founded in **April 1936**; **en.wikipedia dates the organisation to 1937**. All three are stated in the stop's `date_confidence`.
- **Issue count: disputed inside one encyclopedia.** *Mujeres Libres* says **14**; the Saornil article says **números 1-13**. Written as thirteen published and a fourteenth set but never distributed. **Founding address, corrected**: the pool had "Paseo de Santa María de la Cabeza **26**, her own residence," but es.wikipedia confirms the Paseo only as a **family address**, gives **no house number**, and does **not** say the organisation was founded from her flat.
- **19-20 July 1936, Cuartel de la Montaña.** The Saornil article says she took part in the assault on the 19th; es.wikipedia's **own** account of the rising has the crowd besieging on the 19th and the storming on the **20th**, *"pocos minutos antes del mediodía,"* after bombardment from Getafe. The journey file has her in the crowd on the 19th and dates the stop to the fall.
- **August 1936**: the Madrid Agrupación merges with Barcelona's **Grupo Cultural Femenino** **[A]**. **November 1936**: writes **"Madrid, Madrid, mi Madrid"** under bombardment and reads it on **Radio Madrid** **[A; the Gran Vía studio is R]**. No text or recording reachable.
- **Spring-June 1937**: sent by the CNT to **Valencia**; **SIA** founded in June; **secretary of press and propaganda** of the Spanish section and **editor-in-chief of Umbral** **[A, sharpened from the pool's flat "1937"]**. That year she meets **América Barroso García**, "Mery," lifelong partner **[A]**.
- **20 August 1937**: first national congress, Valencia; federation constituted; she becomes **national secretary** **[A both]**. **Corrected:** the pool wrote "well over a hundred local groups" for this date. The figure is **90**; the **147** belongs to **1938**.
- **May 1938**: **international general secretary of SIA**; by 1938, **147 agrupaciones** and roughly **20,000 members** (the 20,000 dated October) **[A]**. That year she publishes **Romancero de Mujeres Libres**, the only collection of her lifetime **[A]**, whose **place of publication is not given anywhere reachable** and which is therefore pinned to no city.
- **December 1938**: the **Casal de la Dona Treballadora** in Barcelona is cleared by police and handed to the Bank of Spain **[A en.wikipedia]**; her protest letter to the CNT national committee **[A via Ackelsberg; the letter not consulted]**.

## 5. Exile, 1939-1942

- **January 1939**: crosses into France with **América Barroso, Mateo Baruta, Cristina Kong** **[A es.wikipedia]**. **en.wikipedia dates the flight to February 1939** and adds a brief internment at **Argelès-sur-Mer**, which **es.wikipedia does not mention**. Both carried, not reconciled.
- **Perpignan**: organises distribution of arriving refugees **[A]**. **Ordering corrected**: the pool's January 1939 date puts this **before** the February camp; re-dated February-spring 1939. **Coordinate corrected** to 42.6986 / 2.8955.
- **1939**: relief work continues around **Paris** **[A]**; the canonical Paris pin (48.8566, 2.3522) is inherited byte-identical. **1940**: directs **España Expatriada** from **Orléans** **[R, loosely dated]**. **June 1940**: moves to **Montauban** after the French collapse **[A, sharpened from a bare "1940"]**, Mery working with a **Quaker organisation** while Sánchez Saornil **retouches photographs at home** **[A]**.
- **Late 1941 / early 1942: two events, not one.** The pool conflated them and dated the arrest 1941. es.wikipedia has a **late-1941 notice** pressing them toward a forced *albergue*, and a **separate brief arrest over suspicious mail in early 1942**; en.wikipedia instead has her **escaping arrest** before a planned deportation. None reconciled.
- **March 1942**: returns clandestinely with the help of **Electra**, Mery's sister **[A]**. The **La Jonquera** crossing is a geographic inference, not an explicit statement, and is treated as such.

## 6. Clandestinity, 1942-1970

- **July 1942**: residence contract on **Calle de Donoso Cortés**, Madrid; she works as a **comisionista** and makes **hairnets** **[A]**. **1945 padrón**, corrected: the pool said she lived "under an assumed identity," but es.wikipedia says only *"figura como cabeza de familia, acompañada de **su prima Mery**."* The fiction in the record is that **Mery is entered as her cousin**, not that Lucía used a false name.
- **1946**: a manifesto survives; she is seen at clandestine confederal meetings **[A for the manifesto, R for the extent]**. **Attribution corrected**: es.wikipedia does not say *presuntamente*, it says flatly *"se conoce un manifiesto de 1946."*
- **January 1948**: moves to **Valencia**, into a **Russafa** flat provided by **Elena Samada**, Mery's niece **[A]**. **Coordinate corrected** from 39.4633 / -0.3773 (nearer the Estació del Nord) to **39.4625 / -0.3719**.
- **1948**: opens **Gabriren**, representing knitwear; private lessons **[A]**. **June 1953**: first Spanish identity document since the exile, profession **fan painter** **[A]**.
- **October 1955**: **"Quiero en mi ley cumplirme"** in **Estrofa**, *"revista burgalesa de la juventud falangista,"* published in **Burgos** **[A]**. The pool pinned it to Valencia, where she lived; the file pins the press. Whether anyone in that office knew whose verse they were setting is **not established** and is not asserted.
- **1950s-1960s**: small literary gatherings in Russafa **[R]**; unpublished late poetry accumulates, dated **c. 1960** **[A]**. **2 June 1970**: dies of **breast cancer**, aged 74, four months after her sister **Concha** **[A both]**.

## 7. Afterlife

- **Gravestone**, Cementerio General de Valencia: Mery has a line of Lucía's own verse cut into it, *"Pero... es verdad que la esperanza ha muerto?"* **[A, verbatim; the Spanish as printed has no opening inverted question mark]**. **Coordinate corrected by about 4 km**, from 39.4838 / -0.3958 (Campanar/Beniferri) to **39.4475 / -0.3965**. **Mery died in 1977**, of a heart attack **[A]**; that she lies in the **same tomb** is **not sourced**, and is stated as unsourced.
- **December 1963, London**: the **Federación de Mujeres Libres de España en el Exilio** is formed under **Suceso Portales**; 47 bulletins to 1976 **[A]**. **Corrected**: the pool gave **Paris** as the founding city. It is **London**. She is unconnected to it, and it appears in the journey file only inside a stop at her own Russafa pin.
- **1996**: **Poesía**, ed. Rosa Martín Casamitjana (Pre-Textos), the first posthumous gathering; the same year the film **Libertarias** renews attention to Mujeres Libres. **2016**: *La cuestión femenina en nuestros medios*. **2019**: *Horas de revolución* reprinted. **2020**: *Corcel de fuego*, ed. Nuria Capdevila-Argüelles, poems 1914-1931 **[A]**.
- **2022**: *Siempre puede volver la esperanza*, the late manuscripts. **Attribution corrected**: es.wikipedia credits **Jesús Gallego Montero** with *"edición, introducción y notas"* (Fundación Emilio Hurtado). The pool credited the recovery to **Michela Cimbalo**, **not named anywhere in that article**; the credit is dropped.
- **2019**: **Espacio de Igualdad Lucía Sánchez Saornil**, San Blas-Canillejas, Madrid. **September 2023**: Correos stamp. **Annual homage** at the grave in early June **[A, recurring; the pool hard-coded "2026" as its year, which a recurring event should not carry]**.

## 8. Quotations: what is verbatim and what is not

- The 1935 quotations are traceable: **"La cuestión femenina en nuestros medios,"** *Solidaridad Obrera*, September-October 1935, trans. **Paul Sharkey**, in Robert Graham, ed., *Anarchism: A Documentary History*, vol. 1; excerpted at libcom.org. **"The Question of Feminism" is libcom's page title, not the article's**, and the journey file cites the series title and the translator.
- **One pool quotation was silently doctored and is not used.** It gave: "The vast majority of male comrades have minds infected by the most typical bourgeois prejudices," cutting a clause from the middle: "...male comrades [with the exception of a half dozen right-thinking types] have minds infected...". Two other lines from the series are used instead.
- The Ackelsberg quotations ("This feminine force...", "not interested in 'feminine unity'...", the 1938 letter) are **seen quoted in en.wikipedia citing the book**, which was not fetched. The `quote_source` fields say so.
- **The Louise Michel comparison is not a quotation and is not used as one.** The pool carried an en.wikipedia sentence ("a firebrand, a keen organiser and a powerful orator, reminding many libertarian women of... Louise Michel") as a quote. It is **Wikipedia's editorial prose**, neither Saornil's words nor verbatim Ackelsberg, and the compression loses three things present on p. 122: that this is **reported interview memory** from surviving comrades, that the Michel resemblance is **physical as well as temperamental**, and that "keen organiser" flattens a portrait which also calls her **diffident**. The journey file **paraphrases p. 122 with attribution** and quotes nothing.
- *"el concepto de madre [está] absorbiendo el de mujer, la función, anulando al individuo"* (Solidaridad Obrera, 15 October 1935) is cited in es.wikipedia and **not verified against the periodical**. Carried as paraphrase inside a campa, never as a quote field.
- The gravestone line is used as a quote field but is **[R]** for attribution: repeated everywhere as her verse, with no poem text fetched to confirm which poem.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

Sánchez Saornil is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework-applied-backward stops are licensed. Three relations in the interlock pool are not rooted in her own life; per the standing rule they are not deleted silently.

1. **Noam Chomsky. Direction caught: forward.** His first political writing at ten on the fall of Barcelona (1938/39), and his later role as the foremost English-language defender of the CNT-FAI collectives. He never met her and never wrote about her; the relation runs from him toward the movement. Not staged as a scene, not named in a campa, not carried at all, since even as a citation it points at his engagement rather than at any object of hers. Note that his major writing on Spanish anarchism (1969) **predates her death**.
2. **Victoria Ocampo and José Ortega y Gasset. Direction caught: lateral, no attested link either way.** *Sur* aligning with the Republican cause in 1936, and Revista de Occidente anchoring a Madrid establishment adjacent to the Cansinos-Assens circle, are shared cause and shared city, exactly the co-location the map shows by itself. Neither is named in a campa; neither gets a stop.
3. **Buenaventura Durruti: pending.** QUEUE.md states the intended interlock ("joins buenaventura_durruti"), but he has **no journey.json** and no personal relation is attested beyond common CNT membership. Flagged for revisiting if he is researched.
4. **Canonical pins.** Kaaba, Temple Mount and Buenos Aires were surfaced as framing devices; none has any connection to this subject and **none is used**. The **Paris** pin **is** used, byte-identical, her 1939 relief work around Paris being attested in her own right.

## 10. Interlocks named in the journey file

Two, both resting on a real relation rather than shared geography:

- **borges**: contributors to the **same movement and the same magazine**. Her poems run in *Grecia* from January 1919; his first appears there on 31 December 1919 (attested on `borges.journey.json`). Named once, in the Seville/Grecia stop, the campa stating that **neither is recorded as having met the other**; the Café Colonial stop says plainly that **her own attendance there is not attested**.
- **mikhail_bakunin**: doctrinal descent, not acquaintance. The CNT she joins in 1931 descends from the Spanish movement seeded by Fanelli on his behalf (the 1868 Alliance is attested on `mikhail_bakunin.journey.json`). Named once, in the membership-card stop.

## 11. Honest gaps

- **No monograph reachable**, and **Ackelsberg not fetched**: every Ackelsberg quotation and the p. 122 portrait arrive through en.wikipedia.
- **No primary periodical consulted**: not one issue of *Mujeres Libres*, *Umbral*, *Solidaridad Obrera*, *Grecia*, *Los Quijotes*, *Avante*, *España Expatriada* or *Estrofa*. **No poem text**: not one poem read in full, including "Nieve," "Madrid, Madrid, mi Madrid," "Quiero en mi ley cumplirme," and whichever poem the epitaph comes from. The file quotes her prose and her inscription, never a line of her verse.
- **Romancero de Mujeres Libres**: no imprint, no place, no contents. **CNT card vs party membership**: 1931 for the card, 1932 for leaving a party founded in 1932, unresolved. **The Cuartel de la Montaña**: her presence rests on one sentence in es.wikipedia and no reachable eyewitness source.
- **Exact addresses**: the CNT paper's offices, the Radio Madrid studio, both exchanges, the founding flat's number. All approximate, all declared in `date_confidence`.
- **The thirty clandestine years** are documented by a lease, a census line, an identity card, a business name and one magazine appearance. The file does not invent the rest.
