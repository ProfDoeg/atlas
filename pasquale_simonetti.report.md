# Pasquale Simonetti, "Pascalone 'e Nola" (1926-1955): research report

*Prepared for the journey file `pasquale_simonetti.journey.json`. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or an anecdote the source itself flags as such. Contradictions between sources are recorded, not adjudicated.*

A produce racketeer of the Naples market economy, famous for twenty-nine months and a character in other people's fiction for seventy years. The documentary base is thin: two Wikipedia articles in two languages that disagree on three separate dates, one Italian local-history chronicle, one paranormal-adjacent Italian site, and a single scholarly work (Allum on postwar Naples) reached only through footnotes. Nearly everything vivid about him comes through his widow's legend, not his own record.

---

## 1. Palma Campania, 1926-1943: origin and the empty years

**[A]** Born **26 February 1926 in Palma Campania**, province of Naples, son of a carter and a housewife (EN Wikipedia, *Pasquale Simonetti*; IT Wikipedia, *Pasquale Simonetti*). Both language versions agree on date and place.

**Coordinate correction, load-bearing.** Earlier working coordinates for Palma Campania (40.9089, 14.5433 and 40.8969, 14.5417) both fall **outside the comune boundary** (OSM relation 40807, max latitude 40.8932); the first lands ~4.6 km north, in Saviano/San Vitaliano territory. The journey file uses **40.8674, 14.5500** (Nominatim centroid; IT Wikipedia infobox 40°52'N 14°33'E) for the birth, the childhood, the wedding, and the presumed burial.

**[A]** The nickname is half physique and half geography. IT Wikipedia (*Assunta Maresca*) explains **Pascalone** as an augmentative given "per la sua mole," for his bulk; **'e Nola** means *of Nola*, the market town where he traded. An earlier draft of this dossier claimed the nickname was "about physique, not origin"; that overcorrects a folk etymology into a second error. The accurate statement: he was born in Palma Campania and worked at Nola.

**Geographic correction.** Nola is not "ten kilometres east" of Palma Campania. It lies roughly **7.5 km north-northwest** (Nola 40.9333, 14.5333; Palma Campania 40.8674, 14.5500).

**[R]: GAP.** Nothing whatever is recorded of his boyhood: no school, no incident, no first job, no first arrest. The interval between the birth register and the war is blank, and the journey file's childhood stop states it as a gap.

## 2. The war: ration books

**[A]** During the Second World War he forged ration books and sold them on the Naples black market (EN Wikipedia, verbatim: "falsifying ration books for food which he then sold on the black market").

**[R]: DATE NOT ATTESTED.** No source consulted gives any year within the war. **1943**, carried in earlier working notes under an **[A]** tag, is a researcher's midpoint, not a record; it is demoted here, and the journey file's `date_confidence` says so in the stop.

**[R]: GAP.** No street, quarter, or accomplice is named for the wartime racket.

---

## 3. The contraband years, late 1940s

**[A]** He moved into **cigarette smuggling**, with the traffic fed from **Marseille, Gibraltar, Nice, and Tangiers**, competing violently with rival gangs for control of the routes (EN Wikipedia, confirmed verbatim on all four port names and on the rivalry).

**[R]: DATE NOT ATTESTED.** The **1948** date used in earlier drafts appears in no source. Rendered in the journey file as a late-1940s placement, flagged in `date_confidence`.

**[A]: IMPORTANT NEGATIVE.** The four ports are attested as **nodes of the trade**, not as places Simonetti personally travelled; no source consulted places him on any foreign quay. Each port gets a stop in the journey file, and each stop says so.

## 4. Corso Novara: the presidency of prices, c. 1950-1952

**[A]** He took control of the fruit and vegetable market at **Corso Novara**, beside Naples Central Station, and was known with **Antonio Esposito** as one of the market's "price presidents" (EN Wikipedia; TerranostraNews, *Gomorra dalla A alla Z, i guappi del secondo dopoguerra*, 2016).

**[A]** EN Wikipedia supplies the two figures that make the business legible: the region exported **thirty percent of Italy's produce**, and "the potato transactions alone, the price president earned three or four million per day."

**[A]** At the **Nola** market he took **100 lire per quintal** of potatoes sold (TerranostraNews, verbatim).

**[R]: REMOVED CLAIM.** "Some forty thousand quintals move through in a day" appears in **no source**: it is EN Wikipedia's three-to-four-million-a-day divided by the 100-lire toll, an inference promoted to a reported fact. Struck; the journey file names the back-calculation as a back-calculation.

**Coordinate correction.** Corso Novara is **not** 40.8518, 14.2724; that point sits ~430 m south, inside Piazza Garibaldi / Napoli Centrale. The street runs 40.8543-40.8570 N, 14.2709-14.2714 E (OSM way 326263990, Rione Vasto). The journey file uses the canonical pin **40.8557, 14.2712**, inside that run.

**[A]** Esposito is described by TerranostraNews as a **producer of dried fruit and nuts** ("produttore di frutta secca"), not a general produce dealer, and on good terms with politicians, "tanto che al suo funerale si disse che ben dodici deputati inviarono corone di fiori." The source hedges the twelve deputies with *si disse*, "it was said."

**[A]: the physical description.** EN Wikipedia quotes a contemporary: *"He was a tall man, nearly two meters, a gigantic man who always had a way of behaving, a 'guappaesica' way of doing things, a self-assured man."* Tagged **[R]** in the journey file, because it reaches print through **Allum 2000, p. 370** (Wikipedia footnote 7) quoting an unnamed informant, not through a primary document.

**[R]: the flowers.** EN Wikipedia: "Simonetti asked the young man, if he preferred spending ten thousand lire in flowers on his marriage or his funeral," of a man who had abandoned a pregnant girlfriend. This is **reported speech, not a transcribed line**, so the journey file renders it as paraphrase and leaves the `quote` field null. Wikipedia cites Allum 2000, pp. 142-45, and the memorial site tinosimonetti.it.

**[R]: the Luciano anecdote.** IT Wikipedia, citing a book titled *Il guappo*, carries a story that Simonetti slapped **Lucky Luciano** somewhere in Naples during Luciano's deportation years. No date, no location, no corroboration; the source flags it as anecdote. Carried in the journey file as anecdote, and it is the only direct personal link between Simonetti and another atlas traveller besides his wife.

**Territorial geography [A, TerranostraNews]:** **Pomigliano d'Arco** (Esposito's nickname town), **Giugliano in Campania** (Maisto's ground, where his family held a mill and land), **Scafati**, **Pagani**, and **Marano di Napoli** as parts of the trading network. No Simonetti incident attaches to Scafati or Pagani.

**Attribution correction.** **Marano** is not Maisto's territory, as earlier working notes had it. TerranostraNews names it as the home town of **Raffaele Baiano**, Simonetti's **ally** in the 1952 Melito fighting, and as the ground the Nuvoletta family later took. An allied stronghold, not a rival frontier; Maisto's base is Giugliano.

## 5. Melito, the sentence, and Poggioreale, 1952-1954

**[A]** In **1952** armed conflict broke out between Simonetti (with Raffaele Baiano) and **Alfredo Maisto's** gang over market territory. TerranostraNews names the place verbatim: *"I due gruppi si affrontarono a colpi di pistola nelle strade di Melito."* The town **is** given, against earlier notes that defaulted the stop to Corso Novara for want of a location; the journey file places it at **Melito di Napoli (40.9200, 14.2300)**.

**[A]** Convicted of attempted murder for the shoot-out: **eight years and three months** (EN Wikipedia). Year 1953; no month or day in any source.

**[R]: FACILITY NOT ATTESTED.** No source consulted names the prison. **Poggioreale** is the plausible Naples facility and is used, flagged as inference, at the canonical atlas pin **40.86, 14.2789**. (An earlier coordinate, 40.865, 14.298, is ~1.7 km east of the prison's true position.)

**[A]** Released **at Christmas 1954**, "after serving two years of his sentence" (EN Wikipedia). No pardon or amnesty is named.

**Arithmetic note.** Wedding 27 April 1955, killing 16 July 1955: **eighty days**, under three months. Accounts that put five months between them are wrong.

**[A/R]** While he was inside, **Antonio Esposito** took over the market business and reportedly failed to keep the promised share of profits, seeding the rivalry (EN Wikipedia, *Pupetta Maresca*). The withheld share is reported; no confrontation, no dated quarrel, and no exchange of words between the two men is documented. Everything between the release and the killing is inference from the ending, and the journey file says so.

## 6. The marriage, 27 April 1955

**[A]** He married **Assunta "Pupetta" Maresca** on **27 April 1955**, before more than **500 guests**, in **Palma Campania**. EN Wikipedia settles the venue against the Naples variant carried elsewhere: *"The whole town of Palma Campania turned out to wish them well."*

**[A]** She was already pregnant (IT Wikipedia: "già incinta"). **"Three months pregnant" is not in either Wikipedia article** and is dropped from the file's prose.

**AGE CORRECTION.** Born **19 January 1935**, married 27 April 1955, she was **20 years and 3 months old**, not "barely eighteen" and not nineteen. Neither Wikipedia states an age; "barely eighteen" looks like legend accretion.

**[A]** **Antonio Esposito stood as a witness to the marriage** (IT Wikipedia: "Testimone di nozze fu Antonio Esposito"). Eighty days later he commissioned the groom's killing.

**[R]: HEDGED.** **Alfredo Maisto's presence at the wedding** is repeated in some accounts and confirmed by none consulted. EN Wikipedia says only that the 500 guests included rivals.

## 7. Corso Novara, 16 July 1955

**[A]** Shot dead by **Carlo Gaetano Orlando**, called **Tanino 'e Bastimento**, on a commission from **Antonio Esposito** ("Totonno 'e Pomigliano"), who wanted the market (EN and IT Wikipedia both confirm triggerman and mandante).

**LOCATION CORRECTION.** Not "a central square of Naples" (EN Wikipedia, *Pupetta Maresca*). EN Wikipedia's *Pasquale Simonetti* article is specific: *"in the Corso Novara, the busy market place of Naples."* Corso Novara is a street in the Rione Vasto, not a square.

**CONTRADICTION 1: the date, unresolved.** EN Wikipedia and IT Wikipedia *Pasquale Simonetti* give **16 July 1955**; IT Wikipedia *Assunta Maresca* gives **15 July**. Not adjudicated; the journey file uses 16 July and records the conflict in `date_confidence`.

**CONTRADICTION 2: the second gunman.** **Gaetano Nuvoletta** as a second shooter is attested **only** by EN Wikipedia and appears in no Italian source consulted. Flagged in the file, not resolved.

**[R]: the pretext.** TerranostraNews gives the killer a line before he fires: *"E tu saresti Pascalone 'e Nola?"* ("And you would be Pascalone 'e Nola?"), and gives the motive as a perceived slight, a greeting not returned. Secondary chronicle, no trial record or eyewitness memoir behind it: **[R]**.

**CONTRADICTION 3: the aftermath.** The sources reviewed here name **no hospital, no ambulance, no doctor**: the record goes from the shots to silence. A separate account in circulation carries him to the **Ospedale degli Incurabili** and has him die of **peritonitis** on 16/17 July. Both versions stand side by side in the journey file; nothing consulted decides between them.

**[R]: GAP: the grave.** **No source consulted names his burial place.** Not a cemetery, not a chapel, not a stone. Palma Campania is a likelihood (born there, married there), not a record. IT Wikipedia's *Nola* article lists the city's notable figures (Giordano Bruno, San Paolino) without reference to Simonetti; no monument, street name, or civic marker was found for him in either town. Absence in one tertiary source is not proof of absence, and is recorded as a gap.

## 8. The widow's answer, October 1955 - 1959

**DATE CORRECTION, the killing of Esposito.** Both IT Wikipedia articles give **4 October 1955** ("il 4 ottobre, Assunta - al sesto mese di gravidanza - uccise il presunto mandante dell'omicidio: Antonio Esposito"). **EN Wikipedia's 4 August is the outlier**, and the Spanish article merely translates it; Italiamistero.it gives a third date, **16 September**. October is the only date consistent with the undisputed **14 October arrest**, universally reported as ten days later. Earlier working notes had this backwards, carrying 4 August as [A] and 4 October as an unverified search summary tagged [R]. Corrected: 4 October is **[A]** on it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assunta_Maresca; 4 August and 16 September are the variants.

**CONTRADICTION 4: how many guns.** EN Wikipedia has her firing **29 shots from a Smith & Wesson .38** held in both hands, "I was afraid I would miss." IT Wikipedia reports investigators who concluded the shots came from **at least four pistols** ("i colpi partirono da almeno quattro pistole"), which would mean she did not act alone. Not resolved.

**[R]: GAP.** No source names the street where Esposito was killed. Placing it **at Poggioreale prison**, as earlier notes did, conflates the crime scene with the place of her later detention; the journey file uses the canonical Naples pin.

**[R]: her stated motive.** That she believed the police already knew the mandante and would not act is repeated in popular accounts but appears in **neither** the EN nor the IT Wikipedia article as retrieved. Downgraded to [R].

**[A]** **Arrested 14 October 1955** in Naples, ten days after the killing. No account of flight or hiding.

**[A/R]** The son, **Pasquale "Pasqualino" Simonetti**, was born **in detention**; EN Wikipedia says "in prison," IT Wikipedia "nel corso della sua detenzione." **Neither gives a date.** Year 1956 is retained, derived from a sixth month of pregnancy in October 1955, implying a birth around January 1956. The sixth month belongs to the October killing, not to the July one: in July she would have been roughly three months along.

**[A]** Trial before the **Court of Assizes, Naples, April 1959**; **18 years**, reduced on appeal to **13 years and 4 months**. Her line in court, **"I would do it again!"**, is reported by EN Wikipedia together with the courtroom bursting into cheers. **[R]** that the court sat physically in **Castel Capuano** (canonical atlas pin 40.8508, 14.2622): likely, not separately confirmed.

## 9. Afterlife

**[A]** **La sfida** (1958), Francesco Rosi's debut, "liberamente ispirato" by the Simonetti-Maresca story; the couple renamed Vito Polara and Assunta; Rosanna Schiaffino and José Suárez. **Silver Lion at Venice 1958**, three **Nastri d'argento** in 1959 (IT Wikipedia, *La sfida (film)*).

**[A]** **Pasqualino Simonetti, 1974.** He disappeared at **eighteen**; the body was never found. EN Wikipedia says "abducted and murdered"; IT Wikipedia says he vanished in an ambush ("scomparve"). He had opposed his mother's relationship with the camorrista **Umberto Ammaturo**, and **she suspected Ammaturo** of the killing: she did not reject that theory, she held it. Nor was he "an adult, decades after" his father's death, as earlier working notes had it; he was 18, and 1974 is 19 years after 1955.

**[A]** **Pupetta - Il coraggio e la passione** (Canale 5, 2013), **Manuela Arcuri** as Pupetta. Simonetti is refracted into **Michele De Nicola**, the *nipote* of a murdered fruit vendor, the Italian **ambiguous between nephew and grandson** and not to be resolved silently either way. Played by **Massimiliano Morra**; the character dies in Pupetta's arms, killed on the orders of a rival boss (Don Luigi Vitiello). **Maresca consulted on set and afterwards said her biography had been distorted**; the broadcast was delayed by the controversy.

**[R]** That the killing and the vendetta became a **sceneggiata**-shaped civic legend, retold in song, film, and television, is a composite characterization drawn from Italiamistero.it, Napolitan.it framing, and the cultural record itself; not tied to a single named source.

**[R]: NO WRITINGS.** Simonetti left no known writings; the "editions and translations" line of inquiry does not apply, and is flagged rather than filled with an invented bibliography.

## 10. Interlocks with other travellers

Two other atlas travellers are named inside the journey file's prose, each because Simonetti stands in a relation to them:

- **Assunta "Pupetta" Maresca** (`pupetta_maresca`): his wife, and the reason the story survived.
- **Charles "Lucky" Luciano** (`charles_lucky_luciano`): via the **[R]** slap anecdote, undated and uncorroborated.

Deliberately **not** named in prose, though the interlock lens surfaced them, because each connection is shared geography or editorial adjacency rather than a relation Simonetti stood in: **Vito Genovese** (occupied-Naples black market 1943-44, the same underworld, but no document places them together); **Meyer Lansky** (co-presence only); **Benito Mussolini** (oblique, via Alessandra Mussolini's casting in the blocked 1982 RAI film *Il caso Pupetta Maresca*); **Griselda Blanco** (QUEUE.md line 307 places her in the same editorial "organized-crime cluster," which is a curatorial grouping, not a historical link).

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identically:** Naples 40.8518, 14.2681 · Corso Novara 40.8557, 14.2712 · Castel Capuano 40.8508, 14.2622 · Poggioreale 40.86, 14.2789. No connection to the Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris, or Buenos Aires pins appears in the Simonetti or Maresca material.
