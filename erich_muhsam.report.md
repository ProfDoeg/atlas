# Erich Mühsam (1878-1934): research report

*2026-08-21, for `erich_muhsam.journey.json`. 45 stops in 9 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, single-source report,
or inference. Contradictions are flagged and left open, never adjudicated silently.
Every correction below is also carried in a `date_confidence` field in the journey file, as are

## 1. Lübeck, the pharmacist's son, 1878-1900

Born Berlin, 6 April 1878, third child of **Siegfried Seligmann Mühsam**, pharmacist and Lübeck
councillor 1887-1915, and **Rosalie née Cohn** [A: de.wikipedia; GDW Berlin]. Family moves to
**Lübeck, Mengstraße 4** [A: Stolpersteine Lübeck dossier]. Attends the **Katharineum** [A as to
the school; R as to 1888-1896, inferred from age], **expelled 11 January 1896** for
*sozialdemokratische Umtriebe* after passing school matters to the *Lübecker Volksbote* [A: de.
and en.wikipedia]. Finishes at the **Friedrich-Franz-Gymnasium, Parchim** [A: Unser Lübeck;
erichinenglish], then apprentices at the **Adler-Apotheke, Lübeck** [A: de.wikipedia].

*Corrections:* the mother is **Cohn**, not "Seligmann", which is the father's middle name; he was
**seventeen** at the expulsion, not sixteen; the apprenticeship was at the Adler-Apotheke, not
the Ratsapotheke. A second apprenticeship at **Blomberg (Lippe)** rests on the erichinenglish
chronology alone [R], kept inside the campa as single-source rather than given a stop.
*Contradiction, open:* the Lübeck move is dated "soon after birth", "at six weeks" and "at one
year"; the stop uses 1879 and records all three.
*Gaps:* birth address not established, so that pin marks central Berlin and says so; the
Adler-Apotheke address not found, so the stop still carries the Ratsapotheke coordinate.

*Quote correction.* The Autobiography passage on the Katharineum stop is **verbatim**, Kuhn
reader p. 26 [A], but the pool dated it 1927. Kuhn's headnote (p. 25) establishes the
*Selbstbiographie* as **written 1919**, in Bavarian custody; its Lübeck tag is the childhood
narrated, not the place of writing.

## 2. Berlin, the trade abandoned, 1900-1903

Autumn 1900, a post at a pharmacy on the **Weddingplatz**, quit before year's end, pharmacy
left for good [A: erichinenglish]. Rooms on the **Augsburger Straße**
[A: de.wikipedia]. First meeting with Heinrich Hart December 1900; **Neue Gemeinschaft**
involvement and the friendship with **Gustav Landauer** both begin **1901** [A: erichinenglish].

*Corrections:* the pool dated the Landauer meeting 1900, and placed the Neue Gemeinschaft in
Friedrichshagen; the Hart brothers' colony was founded 1902 at **Schlachtensee, Seestraße
33-37**, on Berlin's **western** edge, Friedrichshagen being only the predecessor milieu, and
Mühsam moved there in 1902 as a residence [A: de.wikipedia]. The file moves the pin to
Schlachtensee and puts the Friedrichshagen residence in `date_confidence` rather than spending a
second stop on it. "Wedingplatz" is a typo in the chronology.

## 3. Monte Verità and the wander years, 1904-1908

**1904** Ascona / **Monte Verità**; **1905** publishes *Ascona* (Locarno), the first sustained
inside account of the colony [A: de.wikipedia; Spartacus; karl_graser file]. **19 April 1906**,
death of **Lotte Hattemer** at Ascona, aged 29 [A as to death and naming: karl_graser file].
**Zürich, Vienna, Paris**, 1904-1908 [R: de.wikipedia; no addresses or durations], merged into
one Paris stop for budget. **1908**, the pamphlet *Die Jagd auf Harden* [A: Souchy] and the
founding of the **Sozialistischer Bund** with Landauer, Buber among the members.

*Correction:* the friendship begun at Ascona is **Karl Gräser's**, not Landauer's, met in 1901.
*Contradictions, open:* Hattemer's manner of death (suicide per the 1909 police report,
poisoning by a physician per another account), neither adopted; and the Bund's founding month,
GDW giving **May 1908** against the Landauer file's **June 1908**, the stop using 1908-06-01 and
recording both.
*Gaps:* Mühsam's whereabouts on 19 April 1906 are **not established**, and the stop says so; it
sits on his file because he and Johannes Nohl are both named in the rumour around the death. The
Bund's founding venue is unspecified anywhere reachable.

## 4. Schwabing and *Kain*, 1909-1918

**1909**, settles in **Munich-Schwabing**, publishes *Der Krater*, runs the Bund's **Tat** and
**Anarchist** subgroups [A: de.wikipedia]; a documented *Stammgast* of the **Café Stefanie**,
named in Becher's 1912 poem on it [A: de.wikipedia]. A Tat demonstration of the unemployed on
the **Marienplatz** [R: Souchy only, uncorroborated in the German record]. **April 1911**,
founds **Kain: Zeitschrift für Menschlichkeit** [A: GDW; de.wikipedia]. **1914**, turns from
early ambivalence about the war to open antiwar agitation under argument from anarchist peers
[A: en.wikipedia]. **September 1915**, marries **Kreszentia "Zenzl" Elfinger** [A: Souchy for
the month]. **15 July 1916**, the Kakaostube meeting arranged by **Guttzeit** [A: Mühsam's own
diary, via the Guttzeit file], single-source, and the diary does not name the friend he meets.
**1918**, six months' **Festungshaft at Traunstein** [A: de.wikipedia].

*Corrections, three years:* the pool dated the Munich move, *Der Krater* **and** the Tat group
all to 1906; all three belong to **1909**. The Café Stefanie stood in **Maxvorstadt**, not
Schwabing, and the *Stammgast* attestation covers the Munich years **from 1909**, not the
1905-1908 window claimed. *Kain* ran **1911 to 1919**, not "until 1914".
*Correction, with a duplication resolved:* the pool held **two entries for one event**, "banished
to Traunstein, 24 April 1918" and "arrested as an antiwar agitator, April 1918". It was **not
banishment**: he was arrested for breaching his political-activity ban and refusing the
*Vaterländischer Hilfsdienst*, and 24 April 1918 is unattested and not used.
*Gap:* the 1915 registry office is unnamed.

## 5. The six days, 1918-1919

**7 November 1918**, takes part in the overthrow of the Wittelsbach monarchy alongside Kurt
Eisner's USPD [A: GDW; Souchy]. **10 January 1919**, arrested with other members of the
**Revolutionäre Arbeiterrat** on Eisner's own order [A: Souchy], place unspecified.
**21 February**, **Eisner assassinated** by Arco auf Valley [A: de.wikipedia]. **25 February**,
the **Bayerischer Rätekongress** votes down Mühsam's motion to proclaim a Räterepublik
[A: de.wikipedia]. **7 April**, the **Bavarian Council Republic** proclaimed at the
**Wittelsbacher Palais**; Toller leads, **Landauer** takes public enlightenment, Mühsam sits in
the same government, six days [A: gustav_landauer file; Souchy; de.wikipedia]. **13 April**,
arrested during the **Palmsonntagsputsch**, taken to **Ebrach** [A: de.wikipedia]. **2 May**,
**Landauer** beaten and shot at Stadelheim [A: en.wikipedia].

*Corrections:* the pool gave 20 February for Eisner's death (it is the 21st) and 28 February for
the Rätekongress (the 25th). It also had Mühsam arrested **after** the Freikorps suppression of
the second, communist-led council republic; he was arrested **before** it, which is why he took
no part in it, the suppression coming on 2-3 May.
*Unsupported figure dropped:* the Rätekongress tally **"234 to 70"** appears in the pool's search
summary and **could not be traced to any reachable source**. It is omitted, the omission stated
in `date_confidence`. *Rule applied:* Eisner's and Landauer's deaths are their own events and
are not staged as scenes here.

## 6. Five and a half years of fortress, 1919-1924

All diary and trial quotations here were **verified in extracted PDF text** of the Kuhn reader
with printed page numbers [A]: **"Gustav Landauer's Death"**, Ebrach, May 1919, p. 128;
**"Zenzl"**, 16 May, p. 129, with her own letter transcribed 19 May, p. 130; the **Final Court
Statement** of **11 July 1919**, printed 12 July in the *Münchner neueste Nachrichten*,
pp. 131-133, four passages verified, one used as the stop's quote and three in `suggested_refs`;
and **"Sentenced"**, 12 July, p. 134, the day of the **fifteen-year Festungshaft** sentence
[A: Souchy; Spartacus]. **15 October 1920**, transferred to **Niederschönenfeld** until release
[A: de.wikipedia]; that year he writes *Judas* and publishes *Brennende Erde* [A].

*Quote correction, and a rule.* Kuhn's **footnote 4** to p. 128 carries Landauer's reported last
words: the source reads **"Finish me off-to be human!"** (dash), not the pool's comma, and the
dash carries the sense. These are **Landauer's** words, via an unnamed eyewitness through Rocker
[R], so they sit in `suggested_refs` on the Ebrach stop, never in a quote field.

*Two corrections to the Nexø letter of **27 October 1919***. **Place wrong:** the pool put it at
Ebrach, which held him April to July 1919 only; Kuhn p. 141 has him writing of a letter sent "on
September 24 from the **Ansbach Fortress**, where I am currently detained". **Tag upgraded [R]
to [A]:** verbatim at Kuhn p. 9, with **footnote 63** giving the primary citation and the date
exactly (Mühsam, *Briefe 1900-1934*, ed. Jungblut, Vaduz: Topos, 1984, 365).

*Contradiction resolved in the German record's favour, and said so:* sentence suspended
19 December 1924, **released 20 December**, met in Berlin by a large gathering [A: de.wikipedia;
Spartacus]. Souchy's **March 1925** is wrong. Total confinement **five and a half years**, not
the "roughly six" of several accounts. *Gap:* arrival station unconfirmed, so the pin marks the
Anhalter Bahnhof as likely and says so.

## 7. Berlin and *Fanal*, 1925-1933

**1925**, restarts *Kain*, which fails after a few issues [A: en.wikipedia], and is expelled from
the **Föderation Kommunistischer Anarchisten Deutschlands** over alleged KPD collaboration
[A: Spartacus]; merged into one stop. **1926**, *Gerechtigkeit für Max Hölz* [A: Souchy], and in
**October** founds **Fanal**, running to 1931 and banned by the Berlin police for four months in
that final year [A: Souchy]; the ban is folded into the *Fanal* stop. **1928**, **Piscator**
stages *Staatsräson*, on the execution of Sacco and Vanzetti [A: en.wikipedia].
**1929** *Von Eisner bis Leviné*, **1930** the last play *Alle Wetter*, **1933** *Die Befreiung
der Gesellschaft vom Staat* [A: Souchy; en.wikipedia]. Residence **Dörchläuchtingstraße 48**,
Hufeisensiedlung, Berlin-Britz, per the memorial stone unveiled 13 September 1947
[R: gedenktafeln-in-berlin.de; kulturverein-eden.de].

*Gaps:* no editorial address confirmed for *Kain* or *Fanal*; Piscator's 1928 theatre unnamed;
the expulsion venue unspecified; the year he moved into the Britz house not established, so that
stop's date orders the segment only.

## 8. Seventeen months in custody, 1933-1934

**28 February 1933**, early hours: arrested at his Berlin flat by the SA, hours after the
Reichstag fire, Goebbels singling him out publicly as a "Jewish subversive" [A: USHMM; GDW;
Encyclopedia.com]. **Custody sequence** per GDW: **Lehrter Straße** prison, **KZ Sonnenburg**,
**Berlin-Plötzensee**, **KZ Brandenburg**, **KZ Oranienburg** [A]; the pool omitted Lehrter
Straße and Plötzensee, and Mühsam is named in the prisoner lists of both Sonnenburg and
Brandenburg [A: de.wikipedia]. **3 February 1934**, identification photograph at Oranienburg,
**prisoner 2651** [A: USHMM photo archive]. **Night of 9-10 July 1934**, beaten, strangled and
hanged in a latrine; the administration recorded suicide, prisoner eyewitnesses reported the
hanging was staged, and the camp was dissolved on 14 July [A].

*Three contradictions, all open.* **(1) The 1933 address.** The geography lens put the arrest at
a generic central-Berlin pin (52.51, 13.39), the afterlife lens at Dörchläuchtingstraße 48 in
Britz. **One is wrong.** The file uses the Britz pin and states the conflict; **the address
should be confirmed before this stop is treated as settled**. The arrest itself is solid. **(2) Arrival at Oranienburg.** GDW gives **early October 1933**; Goyens,
following Rocker, places the Brandenburg exchange in **October or November 1933** and the
transfer in **February 1934**. Both cannot be right, and the USHMM photograph fits either; the
file orders on GDW and flags this twice. **(3) Date of death.** en.wikipedia gives the night of
9-10 July, Spartacus and Encyclopedia.com 10 July, USHMM 11 July; the file uses 1934-07-10 and
prints all three.

*Camp quotations*, all through **Zenzl's testimony relayed by Rudolf Rocker**, *Der Leidensweg
von Zensl Mühsam* (1949), trans. **Tom Goyens**, 'I Will Never Be a Coward', *Anarchistories*.
All correctly [R], all verbatim, all **misdated in the pool** and corrected: "I will certainly
never be a coward" is Sonnenburg **May 1933**, not 1934 (he had left by November 1933); "this
slow murdering" is Brandenburg **October or November 1933**, not 1934; "never believe that I
committed suicide" is Oranienburg **8 July 1934**, confirmed, but the pool prepended a vocative
Goyens's text does not carry, so it is used **without** it. Kuhn p. 16 also gives an [A]-grade
eyewitness line from the inmate **John Stone**, used on the 2651 stop.

## 9. The grave and the papers

**16 July 1934**, buried at the **Waldfriedhof Dahlem**, Zenzl barred from the funeral [A: GDW;
Spartacus]. She flees to Prague that same day, helped by **Dorothy Thompson**, then goes on to
**Moscow 8 August 1935** with the diaries; several volumes (1910-11, 1916-19, vols. 2-4 and
18-21) go missing in the USSR and remain lost [A: de.wikipedia, 'Zenzl Mühsam';
R: muehsam-tagebuch.de]. Zenzl is arrested in Moscow **23 April 1936**, re-arrested **November
1938** for eight years under Article 58 at **Potma**, exiled to Ust-Tarka in 1946, re-imprisoned
1949, freed **1954** into East Germany, and dies **10 March 1962**; her remains join Erich's
grave in **autumn 1992** [A: de.wikipedia]. Then the **Erich-Mühsam-Gesellschaft**, Lübeck,
**6 April 1989** [A: erich-muehsam.de]; the **2003** Stolperstein before the Buddenbrookhaus
[A: 2mecs.de]; the diaries in **15 volumes, Verbrecher Verlag, 2011-2019** [A: de.wikipedia];
Slime's **2012** *Sich fügen heißt lügen* [A: kritisch-lesen.de].

*Corrections:* the grave is **Feld 015-479, Abteilung 2A, Nr. 144**, the afterlife lens's
"006-21-15" being wrong; Zenzl did not go straight to Moscow in 1935; the institute is the Gorky
Institute of **World** Literature (IMLI), not "for International Literature"; the Nachlass went
into an archive, not to press; her return is **1954**, not 1955; and the Kuhn reader is **2011,
not 2009** [A: Open Library], which matters as the citation on nine quotations in the file.
*Contradiction, open:* the two lenses give **52.4544, 13.2632** and **52.4547, 13.2764** for the
Dahlem cemetery; the file uses the former and records the discrepancy.
*Rule applied:* all of this is other people's travelling and other people's acts, so each item
is stated inside the campa of a stop that is Mühsam's own, the Dahlem grave or the Lübeck
childhood city, or carried in `sources`. Nothing after 1934 is staged as a scene.

## Interlocks

Named in a campa, each on a direct relation of Mühsam's own:

- `gustav_landauer`: met 1901 through the Hart brothers, friend and teacher for eighteen years,
  co-founder of the Sozialistischer Bund, same Räterepublik government, the Ebrach diary entry.
- `karl_graser`: met at Monte Verità 1904, praised by name in Mühsam's 1905 *Ascona*.
- `gusto_graeser`: co-founder of the colony Mühsam lived in and wrote about in 1905.
- `martin_buber`: co-member of the Bund from 1908, tagged [R] in the Landauer file, inherited so.
- `johannes_friedrich_guttzeit`: telephoned Mühsam's flat 15 July 1916, attested in his diary.

**Deliberately not named**, having only shared geography, which the map already shows:
`heisenberg` (dispatch runner in the May 1919 retaking of Munich, while Mühsam was already
imprisoned at Ebrach); `max_weber` (rooms in Schwabing 1919-1920); `johann_jahn` (the "mühsam"
hit there is the German adjective, laborious, from an 1816 *Turnkunst* quotation).

**Canonical pins offered and not used.** The lens supplied **Kaaba** (21.4225, 39.8262) and
**Buenos Aires** (-34.6037, -58.3816), its own notes stating that no attested Mühsam link exists
to either; inventing a stop at either would be fabrication. The wider anarcho-syndicalist cluster
(Durruti, Sánchez Saornil, Lucy Parsons) has no journey file yet, so that interlock stays
queue-level.
