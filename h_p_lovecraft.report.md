# H. P. Lovecraft (1890-1937): research report
*Providence, Rhode Island. Journey file: `h_p_lovecraft.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a dossier claim
not independently confirmed. Corrections the verified pool made are stated, not absorbed
silently; the positional ones are gathered in §7.

## 1. Angell Street, 1890-1904

- Born 20 August 1890, 9 a.m., in his maternal grandfather's house, 454 Angell Street
  **[A: hplovecraft.com chronology and birth certificate]**. Only child of Winfield Scott
  Lovecraft, salesman for Gorham & Co., and Sarah Susan Phillips Lovecraft. hplovecraft.com
  gives the address as "454 (then 194) Angell Street"; the street was renumbered.
- April 1893: the father collapses on business in Chicago and is committed to Butler
  Hospital **[A: Wikipedia, citing the admission record]**. He never leaves.
- 19 July 1898: dies at Butler, death record giving general paresis **[A: Wikipedia, citing
  the death record; hplovecraft.com]**. That this means tertiary syphilis is the standard
  translation of the period's term, not a separate document; whether Lovecraft ever learned it
  is not recoverable **[gap]**. Grandmother Robie Alzada Place Phillips had died in 1896
  **[A: Wikipedia]**, and the earliest surviving work, "The Poem of Ulysses," is 1897
  **[R: hplovecraft.com]**.
- 1904: Whipple Van Buren Phillips's ventures fail, he dies, and the family loses 454 Angell
  Street for half a duplex at 598 Angell **[A: hplovecraft.com; Wikipedia]**. The corrected
  coordinates matter: 598 Angell lies *further east*, away from College Hill, the displacement
  Lovecraft described feeling. The dossier's pin had him moving toward Brown.

**Astronomy periodical, date corrected.** The dossier gave 1902 for *The Rhode Island Journal
of Astronomy*; Open Library full text gives the run as **1903-1907** and Lovecraft's own words
as "by pencil, pen, and hectograph... In 1903 astronomy became my..." **[A]**. Wikipedia's
1902 refers to astronomy taking hold of his worldview, which the dossier conflated with the
founding of the paper. First published letter, *Providence Sunday Journal*, 1906
**[R: hplovecraft.com]**, folded into the same stop.

## 2. Hope Street and the shut years, 1904-1913

- Attends Hope Street High School intermittently, strong in chemistry, astronomy and history,
  weak in mathematics **[A: hplovecraft.com; Wikipedia]**. A collapse in 1908 ends his
  schooling without a diploma **[A]**; its medical character is **undocumented**, per
  Wikipedia, and the suicide-attempt claims found elsewhere are unsupported.
- Seclusion 1908-1913 **[R: dossier synthesis of Joshi-derived accounts; Lovecraft himself
  exaggerated the isolation later]**, with the Providence Athenaeum as habitual reading room
  **[R: consensus of Providence Lovecraft topography, Beckwith; no dated visit confirmed
  anywhere fetched]**. Kept as a stop, flagged as reconstruction.
- Poe as earliest decisive influence, found around age eight **[R: dossier claim, not
  reconfirmed]**, though *Supernatural Horror in Literature* (1927) makes the debt explicit
  **[A: hplovecraft.com text]**. See §10.
- 1913: the letter attacking Fred Jackson in *Argosy*, then a year of verse polemic in the
  letters column, bringing him to Edward F. Daas's notice **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 3. The amateur press, 1914-1921

Four attested events at one address, gathered into one 1916 stop rather than four
near-identical pins, all four dates being listed in its `date_confidence`:

- Joins the UAPA at Daas's invitation, April 1914 **[A: Wikipedia; hplovecraft.com]**.
- *The Conservative* begun 1915, thirteen issues to 1923 **[A: hplovecraft.com; Wikipedia]**.
- "The Alchemist" (written 1908) published 1916 **[R: hplovecraft.com, tagged R in the pool]**.
- UAPA president 1917-1918 **[A: dossier table, corroborated by the officer record in
  Wikipedia]**. "Dagon" written 1917, printed in *The Vagrant* 1919 **[A]**.

**Dunsany, Boston, 1919.** Trip and effect well established **[A: dossier chronology
cross-checked against Joshi]**; the hall is **not** identified in anything fetched, so the
coordinate marks central Boston and says so. **Mother** committed to Butler March 1919
**[A: Wikipedia, citing the admission record]**, dies there 24 May 1921 after gallbladder
surgery **[A: hplovecraft.com; Wikipedia]**; no modern diagnosis is establishable **[gap]**.
**Sonia Haft Greene** met at an amateur convention in Boston, July 1921 **[A: hplovecraft.com
gives 4 July; Wikipedia gives July]**; hall unidentified.

## 4. Kingsport, Weird Tales, New York, 1922-1926

**Marblehead, 17 December 1922: date corrected, and it matters.** The dossier dated the visits
"1926-1930, recurring" while carrying the superlative that belongs to the first sight of the
town. Wikipedia's 'Kingsport (Lovecraft)' documents that sight on **17 December 1922, about
4.05-4.10 p.m.**, quotes "the most powerful single emotional climax during my nearly forty
years of existence," and states that Kingsport is based on Marblehead **[A]**.

- 1923: *Weird Tales* launches, Edwin Baird accepting several early stories including "Dagon"
  and "The Rats in the Walls" **[A: Wikipedia]**. Pinned at Lovecraft's Providence address,
  not the Chicago office he never visited.
- 3 March 1924: marries Sonia at St. Paul's Chapel without notice to the aunts; they settle
  at 259 Parkside Avenue, Brooklyn **[A: marriage certificate; Wikipedia]**. Ghostwrites
  "Imprisoned with the Pharaohs" for Harry Houdini that year **[R: dossier synthesis, with the
  bibliographies on hplovecraft.com]**.
- 1 January 1925: Sonia leaves for Cleveland, Lovecraft moves alone to 169 Clinton Street;
  clothes stolen in May, severe economies, and the letters record intensifying xenophobia
  toward his neighbours **[A: Wikipedia; hplovecraft.com]**. Clinton Street is in
  **Brooklyn Heights**, not Red Hook; the dossier's text blurred the two, and the file keeps
  Red Hook a separate walked-to district, as Wikipedia has it. Red Hook supplies the setting
  and title of "The Horror at Red Hook," a story his letters show was fed by real prejudice
  **[A: Wikipedia]**, recorded as a story's referent rather than a dated visit.
- 17 April 1926: returns to 10 Barnes Street with aunts Lillian Clark and Annie Gamwell;
  mood and productivity recover sharply **[A: hplovecraft.com]**.

## 5. The Providence work and the circuit, 1926-1931

- "The Call of Cthulhu" written 1926, published February 1928; *The Case of Charles Dexter
  Ward* written 1927, unpublished in his lifetime **[A: hplovecraft.com]**.
- Divorce: Sonia signs in 1929 **[A: hplovecraft.com]**, but Lovecraft apparently never
  completed the decree, so the marriage's formal end is **unresolved in the record**
  **[R for the non-completion: Joshi-derived synthesis]**, and the file leaves it unresolved.
- *At the Mountains of Madness* completed 1931, rejected by Farnsworth Wright, serialized by
  *Astounding Stories* 1936 **[A: hplovecraft.com; Wikipedia]**.

**Destinations in the file**: Salem (1920s recurring, [A] as destination, [R] as to date);
Newburyport (1923 and fall 1931, **[A]**, below); Brattleboro and Charleston (1930, [A], the
latter with *An Account of Charleston*); Quebec City (1930, [A], with *A Description of the
Town of Quebeck*); St. Augustine (1931, [A] for the essay's date only); DeLand (1934 and 1935,
[A], homestead unlocated so the pin marks the town centre).
**Dropped** for stop count, recorded so nothing is lost: Portsmouth, Philadelphia and
Elizabeth, N.J. ("1920s, recurring," dates [R], Elizabeth [R] throughout), and Wilbraham
(1930, [A]: Wikipedia), the best evidenced of the four and first candidate for expansion.

**Newburyport, corrected in tag and date.** The dossier tagged the Innsmouth identification
[R], "a widely repeated scholarly and fan tradition." Wikipedia states it without hedge:
"Lovecraft based the town of Innsmouth on his impressions of Newburyport, Massachusetts, which
he had visited in 1923 and fall 1931." Upgraded to **[A]**, dates replaced; the fall 1931
visit is load-bearing, the novella being written that November and December.

## 6. College Street and the last years, 1932-1937

- Aunt Lillian Clark dies 1932; economic pressure moves Lovecraft and Annie Gamwell to
  66 College Street in 1933 **[A: hplovecraft.com]**. "The Shadow Out of Time" written
  1934-35, published 1936 **[A]**.
- 1936: *The Shadow over Innsmouth* in book form from William L. Crawford's Visionary
  Publishing Co., Everett, Pennsylvania, about 400 copies printed and roughly 200 bound, his
  only lifetime fiction book **[A: standard bibliographic record; hplovecraft.com]**. Pinned
  at his own address; he never visited Everett.
- 11 June 1936: Robert E. Howard dies by suicide at Cross Plains, Texas; Lovecraft writes
  "In Memoriam: Robert Ervin Howard" **[A: standard record reflected in Wikipedia]**.
  **Pinned at 66 College Street, not Cross Plains**: he was never in Texas, and the stop
  belongs where he got the news and wrote the obituary.
- Admitted to Jane Brown Memorial Hospital 10 March 1937 **[A]**; dies **15 March 1937 at
  7.15 a.m.**, carcinoma of the small intestine with chronic nephritis contributory
  **[A: death certificate]**; buried 18 March **[A: hplovecraft.com]**. The clinical notes he
  kept in his last weeks, sometimes called a "death diary," are a factual record, not a
  testament.

**Address contradiction resolved, the dossier's open flag withdrawn.** The dossier flagged
"66 College Street vs. 66 Prospect Street" as live and could not confirm the relocation story.
Open Library settles both: Klinger, "originally built in 1825 at 66 College Street. In 1959 it
was moved to 65 Prospect Street," and De Camp to the same effect. So the
1933-37 address was **66 College Street**, the house was **physically relocated** in 1959
(OSM: Samuel B. Mumford House), and Wikipedia's "66 Prospect Street" conflates the old street
with the new number **[A]**. The relocation moves from [R] to [A].

**A fabricated physician caught.** The dossier named "Dr. Cecil B. Leet" as the doctor who
found the terminal cancer in February 1937. **No such person exists in the Lovecraft
literature**: Open Library returns zero hits across its scanned corpus. Two independent hits
give **Dr. Cecil Calvert Dustin**, an internal-medicine doctor, Klinger glossing Lovecraft's
own note as "tel. [Dr. Cecil Calvert] Dustin ... about specialist" **[A]**. hplovecraft.com
names no physician at all, so the dossier's cited source supported the claim neither way. The
diagnosis itself is sound **[A: Joshi-derived, not in a primary document]**.

**Funeral home located.** Horace B. Knowles' Sons Inc., established 1854, **187 Benefit
Street**, per the 1937 and 1938 Providence directories via Open Library; an earlier directory
gives 188. The file follows the directory's spelling, "Knowles' Sons."

## 8. Afterlife: what is in, and what belongs to someone else

Four posthumous stops, each anchored to his own body, papers, or house: the Phillips plot at
Swan Point (18 March 1937, listed on the family monument with no stone of his own) **[A]**;
the John Hay Library **[A: riamco finding aid US-RPB-mslovecraft; Wikipedia, 'R. H.
Barlow']**; the 1959 house move **[A]**; the 1977 fan-funded headstone reading "I AM
PROVIDENCE" **[A: Wikipedia]**, its line from the letter to Lillian D. Clark of 29 March 1926
**[A: Wikiquote]**. *John Hay date corrected*: the dossier dated the deposit 1937, but
Barlow's 1937 act was the **gathering** under the "Instructions in Case of Decease" and
Wikipedia gives no year for the donation, so the stop reads "from 1937 onward."

Excluded because the travelling was someone else's; each belongs on another file, or none.
**Arkham House**, Sauk City, 1939, founded by Derleth and Wandrei to put
Lovecraft in hardcover, *The Outsider and Others* drawing about 150 advance orders
**[A: Wikipedia]**. **The estate documents**, Annie Gamwell's will **1940** and the
Morrish-Lewis letter **1941**, which the dossier dated both to 1940 and conflated while
hplovecraft.com lists them separately **[R: instruments not examined]**; its summary holds the
letter conveyed no exclusive right and no copyright, which would put Arkham House's long
control on weaker ground than assumed. Then, on the same ground: **Llopis, *Los mitos de
Cthulhu*, Madrid, 1969**, the first Spanish panorama of the Mythos **[A: Wikipedia (es)]**;
**Houellebecq, 1991** **[A]**; **Library of America, 2005** **[R as to the canonization
reading]**; **NecronomiCon Providence**, since 2013 **[A]**; **Chaosium's game, 1981**
**[R: uncited critical opinion]**; **occult "Necronomicons," the 1977 Simon edition**
**[R: not verified]**.

**World Fantasy Award**: a Gahan Wilson bust, 1975-2015, retired November 2015 after
campaigning by Daniel José Older, Nnedi Okorafor, China Miéville, Sofia Samatar and Kevin J.
Maroney, Joshi returning his two awards and Wandrei having refused his in 1984 as a "demeaning
caricature" **[A: Wikipedia]**. The 2015 convention at Saratoga Springs is the campaigners'
place, not his, so only one sentence of this reaches the headstone campa, as a fact about
his name.

## 9. Quotations: verified and corrected

Thirteen of 44 stops carry a quote, all [A]. Corrections applied:

- **"Only a cynic can create horror..."** was the worst defect: the dossier gave it to a
  letter to James F. Morton, 8 March 1923. It is Lovecraft's letter to Edwin Baird, *Weird
  Tales* 3, no. 3 (March 1924), pp. 89-92, reprinted in *Lord of a Visible World*, p. 122;
  the Morton letter is an adjacent Wikiquote entry that also discusses cynicism, and the two
  were conflated. The file uses the corrected attribution and the full text.
- **"It is good to be a cynic..."**: the truncation inverted the point, the line ending "and
  it is best not to exist at all." Redated to *The Rainbow* I.1, **October 1921**, which with
  the full text moves it from [R] to **[A]**.
- Restored past their cuts: **"...neither affirming nor denying anything"** (Moe, 1915);
  **"There is a real restfulness..."** past the semicolon the dossier made a full stop
  (Kleiner, 1919); the **tripartite** passage, rewritten from one lettered sentence into
  three, dropping "and of scientific logick" and "the ancient and the permanent" (Kleiner,
  1920).
- Verified verbatim at hplovecraft.com: the openings of "The Call of Cthulhu" and
  *Supernatural Horror in Literature*, the Necronomicon couplet, Jedediah Orne's letter.
- **Not used**: "From even the greatest of horrors irony is seldom absent" and "Memories and
  possibilities are ever more hideous than realities," both **[R]**, Wikiquote-only.

Wikiquote letters with named recipient and date (Galpin 1918, Clark 1926, Harris 1929) are
[A] on that basis; the volumes were not opened, and each `quote_source` names letter and date.

## 10. Apparatus relations for the operator

Per `EXCEPTIONS.md`, relations not rooted in the subject's own life are reported rather than
deleted, **with the direction named**. All below run **forward** from Lovecraft (d. 1937),
which his file is not licensed to carry, so none was written into the journey.

**1. Nick Land, hyperstition, the Necronomicon. FORWARD, and licensed on the other side.**
`EXCEPTIONS.md` lists **Land** with "hyperstition: the fiction that makes itself real,"
applied backward to "H. P. Lovecraft's Necronomicon (Lovecraft d. 1937): the book nobody
wrote, quoted into a bibliography by circulation alone." That stop belongs on
`nick_land.journey.json` at Land's Warwick pin and appears to be there: it discusses the
Necronomicon and quotes Graham Harman crediting Land as "the first philosopher in recent times
to seriously engage with Lovecraft" **[A, 1997-11]**, plus the CCRU "Origins of the Cthulhu
Club" letters **[A, 1998-03]**. Land is in no campa and no source list here; the occult
"Necronomicons" are omitted with it **[R]**.

**2. Mark Fisher. FORWARD, weak.** *Flatline Constructs* (1999), same CCRU milieu, the thesis
**not** shown naming Lovecraft **[R: inferred]**. **3. Stephen King. FORWARD.** *It* (1986)
wins the award called "the August Derleth" **[A: stephen_king.journey.json]**, a tie running
through Derleth, not Lovecraft. **4. Ocampo, Borges and Bioy's *Antología* (1940) and
Cortázar's 1956 Poe translation. FORWARD, indirect**, reaching Lovecraft only through Poe and
Dunsany **[A as to contents]**; still worth attention, since with Llopis 1969 they bridge to
the Spanish-reading side of the corpus. **5. Ahmad al-Majríti's Picatrix at Brown: no
relation**, only the university that holds the papers **[A: ahmad_al_majriti.journey.json]**,
excluded on the standing rule, not the block rule.

**The one backward relation, and it is in the file: Edgar Allan Poe** (d. 1849), read from
about age eight and treated at length in *Supernatural Horror in Literature* **[A as to the
essay; R as to the age-eight discovery]**. Poe is named in one campa, the Athenaeum stop, on
the ground of influence, and **not** because he courted Sarah Helen Whitman in Providence in
1848 **[A: edgar_allan_poe.journey.json]** or served near Charleston: co-location is shared
geography, which the map shows by itself. **No canonical pin was inherited.** The lens offered
Providence (41.8240, -71.4128, from that file), Warwick, Buenos Aires and Paris; this file has
no generic "Providence" stop, every Providence stop being a specific building with its own
geocode, and a city-centre pin would undo §7.

## 11. Gaps stated as gaps

What the 1908 collapse was, undocumented. Whether Lovecraft knew his father's diagnosis, not
recoverable. Sarah Susan Lovecraft's condition in modern terms, not establishable. Whether the
divorce was completed, unresolved. The grave plot within Swan Point's 200 acres, the two
Boston venues, the Barlow homestead outside DeLand, the year of the John Hay deposit as
distinct from the 1937 gathering, and the dates of the recurring New England and mid-Atlantic
visits: all attested as places, unfixed as points or dates.
