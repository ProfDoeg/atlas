# Felipe Guamán Poma de Ayala (Waman Puma), c.1535 - c.1616
*Research report for the Atlas of Journeys. Compiled 2026-08-12 from the verified research pool (five lenses: chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife), with the verifier's coordinate and attribution corrections applied.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference from route or from silence, or tradition. Contradictions are flagged and left open, not adjudicated. Coordinates in the journey file are the corrected ones; superseded values are recorded below so the correction is auditable.

**The central evidentiary fact about this life:** almost the entire biography rests on the subject's own self-narration inside a single manuscript, plus a thin band of colonial court and visita records fixing him to six years (1587, 1588, 1594, 1597, 1600, 1606). **[R]** No portrait, no physical description, no baptismal entry, no burial entry, and no independent document establishing his birth or death date has been located by any consulted source. A stated gap, not a documented fact.

---

## 1. Origins: the valley, the lineage, the house

**Birth.** Born in San Cristóbal de Suntuntu (also called Sondondo), Valle de Sondondo, Lucanas province, Ayacucho. **[R]** The year is contested: c.1534 (es.wikipedia), c.1535 (en.wikipedia), possibly as late as c.1550 in some scholarship. He gives no birth date anywhere in the Nueva corónica, and no baptismal or civil record survives.

**Birthplace contested twice over.** Some sources give Suntuntu/Sondondo in Ayacucho; other traditions place his origins in the Huánuco/Yarowilca homeland to the north, the tension being between his stated paternal lineage and the region where he is documented for his whole recorded life. **[R]** Neither Wikipedia edition reconciles them. Separately, the literature does not agree whether the natal village belongs to Cabana or to Andamarca **[R]**; the chronicle names the valley far more often than any settlement, though the GeoNames village actually named Sondondo lies in Cabana district. Both left open.

**COORDINATE CORRECTION (three lenses gave three different birthplaces).** The geography lens gave -14.6833/-73.4667, empty puna ~65 km southeast of the valley. The afterlife lens gave -14.7/-74.1667, which is Puquio, the Lucanas provincial capital ~85 km away, not an approximation but a different town. Both wrong. **Used: -14.2978 / -73.9392**, GeoNames Sondondo, Cabana district (Cabana seat -14.289/-73.967; Aucará -14.2802/-73.9765; Andamarca ≈ -14.39/-73.97).

**Parents.** Don Martín Guamán Malqui de Ayala and doña Juana Curi Ocllo. **[A** poma.kb.dk p. 15, verified directly against the Royal Danish Library transcription**]**

**The father's war service.** The manuscript describes him fighting the rebel encomendero Francisco Hernández Girón. **[A** poma.kb.dk pp. 434-435, ch. 19, Drawing 174, at "Uata Cocha", 100 of his men against Girón's 300**]** *Citation correction:* the pool cited pp. 749-751, which are the chapter of moral judgments of the father (ch. 27) and mention no Girón. **Uata Cocha is not locatable in any gazetteer consulted**, so no pin was created; the claim rides in the Cajamarca campa.

**The half-brother.** Father Martín de Ayala, described in the manuscript as a mestizo priest and "a great and holy man" who taught him Christian doctrine and to read. **[A** poma.kb.dk**]**

**The lineage claim.** Descent from the Yarowilca nobility of Huánuco, and through his mother from Inca royal blood; en.wikipedia relays his self-description as "direct descendant of Huaman-Chava-Ayauca Yarovilca-Huanuco". **RETAGGED [A] → [R].** The substance is reported correctly, but the claim is his own genealogical self-assertion inside his own manuscript, with no independent colonial corroboration, exactly as the geography lens had it.

**COORDINATE CORRECTION.** Huánuco Pampa (Huánuco Viejo) was given as -9.9086 / -76.5325, ~33 km off in longitude, landing in the wrong valley. **Used: -9.8767 / -76.8292** (GeoNames, Pampa Huánuco Viejo, near La Unión, Dos de Mayo province, Huánuco).

## 2. The interpreter, c.1560s-1590s

**Taki Onqoy.** Served as Quechua-Spanish interpreter to Cristóbal de Albornoz during the campaign to extirpate the Taki Onqoy revival. **[A** en.wikipedia; Mills, *Idolatry and Its Enemies* (2004) on the visitas**]**

**ATTRIBUTION CORRECTION.** Albornoz was **not** an Augustinian friar (geography lens) and **not** a Dominican (en.wikipedia). Per es.wikipedia he was a secular cleric, "canónigo", "presbítero católico de rito latino", of no religious order, titled *visitador eclesiástico* and twice provisor and vicar general of the Cuzco diocese. The journey file calls him "secular priest and ecclesiastical visitador". **Date narrowed:** es.wikipedia places him in Huamanga 1569-1571, against the pool's looser "c.1564-1570s".

**Parinacochas.** The visita's extension south, and his interpreting across the wider Huamanga hinterland rather than the city alone. **[R]** Drawn from campaign scope (Mills; Adorno), not from a paper naming him there.

**Alberto de Acuña.** Interpreter for Acuña in the 1560s-1570s. **[A** es.wikipedia**]** **ATTRIBUTION CORRECTION:** es.wikipedia does **not** call him an *oidor*, but "Alberto de Acuña, conocido como el «Abogado General de los Indios»". The judicial title is dropped. No year is given; the stop carries a nominal 1575 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

**Martín de Murúa.** Assistant and informant to the Mercedarian chronicler in the late 1580s or early 1590s, thought to have contributed drawings or information to Murúa's chronicle. **[A** en.wikipedia; Adorno and Boserup**]** The direction of borrowing between the two manuscripts, and the falling-out, remain contested.

## 3. The visita years, 1587-1594

**1587, Juan Pérez de Gamboa.** Served in his retinue during official inspection visits. **[A** es.wikipedia, which identifies Pérez de Gamboa as "corregidor de Huancavelica y luego visitador"**]**
**CORRECTION:** the pool attached Cuzco and Huamanga to this commission as its destinations. **es.wikipedia names no destinations**, so the itinerary is dropped from the claim and the campa says so. The Cuzco, Vilcabamba, Jauja and Andahuaylas stops rest instead on Adorno and Pease, tagged as reconstruction in `date_confidence` where that is what they are.

**1588, Huancavelica.** Translator during the visita to the mercury mines under Pedro de Arteaga Mendiola. **[A** es.wikipedia; Adorno ch. 2**]**
**CORRECTIONS, two.** (1) *oidor* is unsupported: es.wikipedia gives Arteaga Mendiola no title, only "la visita a las minas de Huancavelica en 1588". A visitador, not an audiencia judge. Dropped. (2) Coordinate off ~5 km: the given -12.8206/-74.9294 sits east of the mine. **Used: -12.8099 / -74.9725** (GeoNames, Santa Bárbara settlement).

**The circuit.** Castrovirreyna, Jauja and Vilcabamba are **[A]** on Adorno and Pease; Huaytará, Andahuaylas, Cuzco and Vilcashuamán **[R]**, from route geography or the chronicle's internal evidence rather than a document placing him there on a day.

**COORDINATE CORRECTIONS.**
- Huaytará given -13.7667/-75.0333, ~40 km off into empty highland. **Used: -13.6044 / -75.3531** (GeoNames). It is today the capital of its own province, created 1984 out of Castrovirreyna, so the journey file says "the corregimiento of Castrovirreyna".
- Vitcos given -13.15/-72.9333, ~6 km off. **Used: -13.0983 / -72.9319** (en.wikipedia, the ridge between Huancacalle and Pucyura). The 1572 end of the neo-Inca state is confirmed.
- Cuzco: **canonical atlas pin inherited byte for byte, -13.5319 / -71.9675**. The geography lens's -13.5183/-71.9781 was **not** used.

**1594, Huamanga.** Employed by a Spanish judge overseeing composición de tierras proceedings. **[A** en.wikipedia**]** The officer is not named by the source.

## 4. The suits, the ruin, the exiles, 1597-1608

**Late 1590s.** Lawsuits to recover the cacicazgo and lands in the Valle de Chupas near Huamanga; the suits fail. **[A** es.wikipedia; Adorno; Lohmann Villena on Huamanga land litigation**]**

**COORDINATE CORRECTION.** Chupas was given as -13.05/-74.1667, on the wrong side of the city, ~22 km north-northeast. GeoNames places Chupas in Chiara district at -13.2308/-74.2183 and -13.2599/-74.2263, i.e. **south** of Ayacucho, and es.wikipedia on the 1542 battle confirms Vaca de Castro moved "hacia Chupas, al sur de la ciudad". **Used: -13.24 / -74.22.**

**"Concepción de Chupas" dropped.** GeoNames returns no entry for a reducción of that exact name; its existence is unverified, so no stop asserts it, and the reducción policy survives only as context. **[R]** on the policy, gap on the toponym.

**The Battle of Chupas, 16 September 1542** (Vaca de Castro against Diego de Almagro el Mozo), fought in the same valley. **[A** es.wikipedia**]**

**Santa Ana parish, Huamanga.** Local tradition connects the Ayala family to this indigenous parish outside the Spanish traza. **[R** regional histories; not attested in the Nueva corónica**]** **No stop was created for it**: the association rests on later tradition alone.

**1600, judgment and banishment.** Having lost the litigation he is stripped of property and banished from Huamanga by the corregidor. **[A** en.wikipedia and es.wikipedia both carry the expulsion; Adorno**]** **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED:** some popular syntheses add 200 public lashes, a number circulating without a clear primary citation, and accounts disagree both on the number and on whether any flogging happened. The banishment is attested on every side; the campa states the disagreement rather than picking a side.

**1606 (or 1608), second exile,** imposed by the corregidor of Lucanas. **[A** es.wikipedia, citing Adorno**]** **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED:** circulating scholarship gives the year both ways and does not reconcile; the stop is dated 1606 with the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

## 5. The road and the book, c.1600-1615

**The itinerant composition.** Between roughly 1600 and 1615, impoverished and without settled residence, he walks much of the highlands and coast, gathering, drawing and writing. **[A** en.wikipedia; the manuscript's chapter on how he was able to write this book, poma.kb.dk pp. 916-921**]**

**Huarochirí.** He denounces Francisco de Ávila's idolatry trials there, sharply enough that scholars read direct knowledge, whether by travel or through informants inside the same extirpation networks that once employed him under Albornoz. **[R** Adorno; Salomon on the Huarochirí Manuscript**]** **COORDINATE CORRECTION:** given -11.9036/-76.2333, ~26 km off, nearer the province centroid. **Used: -12.1364 / -76.2350** (en.wikipedia, Huarochirí district capital).

**The manuscript.** *El primer nueva corónica y buen gobierno*, roughly 1,189 pages (some counts give 1,180) with about 397-398 full-page drawings, addressed to King Philip III. **[A** en.wikipedia; MS GKS 2232 4°, Royal Danish Library**]** Composition usually placed at 1615, the closing date read by some scholars as 1616, the library cataloguing the work "1615/1616". **Contradiction flagged, left open.**

**The journey to Lima chapter,** pp. 1104-1139. **[A** poma.kb.dk**]**
**CITATION CORRECTION:** the pool quoted a chapter title "Camino y visita de los pueblos". **No such heading exists**; poma.kb.dk gives ch. 36 as "The chapter of the author's journey to Lima". Whether the narrated journey is a literal itinerary or a device closing the book's argument is debated, and no notarial trace confirms the towns **[R]**; the -12.4/-75.7 route marker is arbitrary and labelled so in `date_confidence`.

**Huánuco honorific.** The manuscript ties the author to Huánuco and to the honorific "falcon and royal lion, waman puma". **[A** poma.kb.dk pp. 1037-1038**]**

## 6. Lima, death, and the gap

**Lima arrival.** By inference he reaches the viceregal capital in his last years, seeking a channel to the crown. **[R]** No document records an audience or any reception of the text. **Canonical pin inherited: -12.0464 / -77.0428** (pizarro.journey.json); the geography lens's -12.0464/-77.03 was not used.

**Cercado de Lima.** The walled Jesuit parish outside the Spanish traza, named as the plausible indigenous lodging. **[R]** No document confirms his presence.

**Death. DIRECT CONTRADICTION, LEFT OPEN.** en.wikipedia: "after 1616", in Lima. es.wikipedia: 1615, in Lima, the year it presumes the manuscript finished. Neither offers a burial site. **RETAGGED [A] → [R]** on the place: that he died *in Lima* is itself an inference with no burial, parish or notarial record behind it, as the geography and afterlife lenses both had it. No tomb, grave or marker is known to survive or ever to have been recorded **[R]**: reconstruction from silence, no source affirming a tomb and none stating one was destroyed.

**The king never read it.** No evidence Philip III received, opened or answered the Nueva corónica, and no record it reached the Spanish court. **[A** en.wikipedia; Britannica via Wikipedia synthesis**]** The attested fact is the silence, not a claim about the king's mind.

**The transit gap.** How the manuscript left Peru and entered a European collection is unresolved: no manifest, no ecclesiastical correspondence, no royal acknowledgment, half a century of silence between the author's death and the first documented European owner. **[R]** Given its own stop rather than smoothed over.

## 7. Afterlife

**Copenhagen provenance.** The manuscript is in the Danish royal collection, GKS 2232 4°, one account holding it there since at least the early 1660s. **[R]**
**MAJOR CORRECTION, tag and content.** The geography lens carried at **[A]** that "the Danish royal historiographer Cornelius Pedersen Lerche" acquired it, "documented in a 1662 inventory". Both parts fail. Lerche was no royal historiographer: per en.wikipedia he was Danish resident in Madrid 1650-1653 and envoy to the Spanish court 1658-1662, and **that article records no book collection and no Guamán Poma connection at all**. The inventory is documented nowhere reachable; en.wikipedia says only that the book may have travelled "from Spain to Denmark via the library of the Count-Duke of Olivares" and been "sold to Cornelius Pedersen Lerche", adding **"this is only speculation"**. **Retagged [R]**. Coordinate **55.6746 / 12.5822**.

**Rediscovery.** Richard Pietschmann identifies the manuscript in the Danish collection and returns it to scholarship. **[A]** **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED:** en.wikipedia dates this 1908, es.wikipedia 1907. Unresolved; the stop is dated 1908 and names the disagreement.

**Editions.** 1936, Paul Rivet's first facsimile in Paris, heavily retouched **[A]**. 1980, Murra and Adorno with Urioste, the critical transcription from direct autopsy, now standard **[A]**. The Royal Danish Library digital facsimile, Adorno editing: **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED**, en.wikipedia 2001, es.wikipedia 2011, the site itself silent in the sections retrieved. Left open. 1992, Herman Braun-Vega appropriates pages of the chronicle in a series on colonial violence and Church evangelization, shown in Madrid **[A** all four: en.wikipedia**]**.

**Commemoration.** Bolivia's Premio Guamán Poma de Ayala for narrative in indigenous languages **[A]**; the Centro Guamán Poma de Ayala in Cusco, whose site offers no explanation of the naming **[A** guamanpoma.org, fetched directly**]**. **No statues or physical monuments were found documented in any source checked**: the institutional naming is the clearest commemoration located, and the monument record is a gap.

**Prophecy.** An Andean tradition holds that his descendants are protected and hidden to one day retake the Peruvian Empire, tied to a prophecy of a hawk that will fly high where the sun surrenders. **[R** en.wikipedia, citing Andean oral tradition and presenting it as legend, not history**]**

- **micaela_bastidas.journey.json**: the 1780-81 rising, a cacique claiming Inca descent, mita drafts from Canas y Canchis, Areche's 1781 sentencia banning Inca names, dress and genealogies. **[A]** Same sierra, same cacique network, same grievance, 170 years later. Pin -14.8/-71.4 from that file.

- **hernando_arias_de_saavedra.journey.json**: the mita and the encomienda as "the two instruments by which the conquerors" abused the Indians. **[A]** Named at Castrovirreyna.

## 9. Quotations used

Nine passages (ff. 1, 8, 8-9, 9, 11, 1085 twice, 1105, 1189), all from the Royal Danish Library transcription at poma.kb.dk, in the original orthography. **The site showed no published English translation on the pages fetched**, so all nine stand untranslated: a limit of what was reachable, not an editorial choice.
