# Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa von Nettesheim (1486-1535): research report
*2026-08-24, for `heinrich_cornelius_agrippa.journey.json`. Slug: `heinrich_cornelius_agrippa`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or specialist
synthesis not confirmed in the sources reachable this session. Contradictions are flagged and
**not** adjudicated. Hedges live in each stop's `date_confidence`, never in campa prose.

Reachable this session: the Stanford Encyclopedia entry (Perrone Compagni), the English, German,
and French Wikipedia articles, the 1651 John French translation of *De Occulta Philosophia*, and
two archive.org scans of the English *Of the Vanity of Arts and Sciences*. **No primary or archival
document was consulted:** every [A] rests on a tertiary encyclopaedia entry or an early-modern
printed translation, and the [A]/[R] split should be read in that light.

---

## 1. Name, birth, and the two villages

**Born 14 September 1486 [A]**, all four sources agreeing on the day. **Born at Cologne [A, three
of four]**: SEP, de.wikipedia ("* 14. September 1486 in Köln"), and fr.wikipedia, which derives the
surname from Colonia Agrippina. His matriculation record calls him a citizen of Cologne.

**Contradiction flagged.** Only the *infobox* of en.wikipedia gives "Nettersheim, Electorate of
Cologne", a **different village** from the Nettesheim of the surname: Nettersheim is in the Eifel,
c.40 km south; Nettesheim is in Rommerskirchen, Rhein-Kreis Neuss, north-west of Cologne, at
51.0347, 6.6914. A circulating note claiming "German Wikipedia gives Nettersheim" is false;
de.wikipedia gives Köln. The file pins the birth at Cologne and gives Nettesheim a separate stop at
the **Rommerskirchen** coordinates, flagged in `date_confidence` as the source of the surname and
not an attested birthplace. No source places him physically at the village.

## 2. Cologne and Paris (1499-1507)

Matriculated in arts at Cologne c.1499, aged about thirteen **[A: matriculation record, cited by
en.wikipedia and SEP]**; baccalaureate 1500, *magister artium* 1502 **[A: de.wikipedia]**. In Paris
c.1502-1507 among humanist friends reading Hebrew, alchemy, and the Hermetic books **[A: SEP]**.
**Contradiction flagged:** SEP calls the circle a *sodalitium*, en.wikipedia "a secret society".
The named membership (Champier, Ganay and others) is **[R]** and is not asserted.

## 3. Spain (1508-1509)

An expedition with two Paris associates on military or political business **[A that it happened:
SEP, which dates it 1508-1509, not 1508 alone]**. Purpose and employer are unrecoverable and no
source attempts a reconstruction. en.wikipedia strings an unsourced itinerary (Valencia, the
Balearics, Sardinia, Naples, Avignon, Lyon); that chain is **[R]**, and Valencia is the pin only
because it is the one named starting point.

## 4. Dôle and the Reuchlin lectures (1509-1510)

Public lectures at Dôle on Reuchlin's *De verbo mirifico*, and *De nobilitate et praecellentia
foeminei sexus* composed there under Margaret of Austria's nominal patronage **[A: SEP;
de.wikipedia]**.

**Place contradiction, flagged and resolved against the common synthesis.** The denunciation as a
"judaizing heretic" by the Franciscan provincial Jean Catilinet was **not** made at Dôle. SEP:
denounced "at Ghent court, ending Dôle position". de.wikipedia: "Während einer Fastenpredigt in
Gent." Catilinet preached the Lenten sermon at the Habsburg court in Ghent, before Margaret;
Agrippa was the absent target, and two widely circulated summaries put the scene at Dôle and are
wrong. Since he was not in Ghent, the file stages no Ghent scene: the episode is narrated at
**Dôle**, as the loss of his position, with Ghent named in the campa as where the sermon was
preached. **No source gives a year**; 1510 orders the segment.

## 5. Würzburg and Trithemius (winter 1509-1510)

Visit to Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the Schottenkloster St James's **[A: en.wikipedia; already
written and sourced in `johannes_trithemius.journey.json`]**. First draft of *De occulta
philosophia* sent with a dedicatory letter dated **8 April 1510** **[A: en.wikipedia; SEP; the
letter printed in the 1651 edition]**. Trithemius's reply counselling secrecy is **[A]** as text
("Give Hey to an Ox, Sugar to a Parret only..."), but it is **by Trithemius**, and the file
attributes it so.

**Coordinate correction carried.** The Schottenkloster (today the Don-Bosco-Kirche, Mainviertel,
**left** bank) is at 49.7957, 9.9205 per de.wikipedia, not the right-bank 49.7913, 9.937 that
circulates. `johannes_trithemius.journey.json` already uses it, and this file inherits it
byte-identically.

## 6. England (1510)

Travelled to England on a political and secret assignment in imperial service **[A: SEP;
en.wikipedia]**. **Met John Colet [A: SEP; fr.wikipedia].** **NOT attested, and flagged:** that he
"attended Colet's lectures on the Pauline epistles at St Paul's". No source places him at lectures
in that building; de.wikipedia says instead he heard Colet "an der Universität Oxford",
chronologically doubtful, since Colet's Oxford Pauline lectures fall c.1497-1504 and by 1510 he was
Dean of St Paul's. Two sources, two venues, neither being "St Paul's, 1510". The file says he
studied the epistles with Colet in England, asserts **no building**, and pins generic London. The
English commentaries do not survive **[R]**.

## 8. Metz (1518-1520)

Civic orator and legal advocate of Metz from **February 1518** **[A: de.wikipedia; office confirmed
by en.wikipedia and SEP]**. Defended a woman **from Woippy** accused of witchcraft before a
Dominican inquisitor, attacking the procedure and the use of her mother's conviction as inherited
proof **[A: SEP; de.wikipedia for Woippy, Metz, and the argument]**.

**Name contradiction flagged, not resolved.** de.wikipedia names the inquisitor **Claudius Salini**;
a widely repeated synthesis names **Nicolas Savin**; SEP and fr.wikipedia name nobody. A note
claiming "German Wikipedia names Savin" is false. The file gives both and settles neither.

*De beatissimae Annae monogamia*, supporting Lefèvre d'Étaples against the tradition of Saint
Anne's three marriages, with heresy accusations from mendicant preachers **[R]**, not confirmed.
**Left Metz 25 January 1520; at Cologne by July 1520 [A: de.wikipedia]**, against en.wikipedia's
bare "resigned 1520".

## 9. Cologne, 1520

Met Ulrich von Hutten in July 1520 **[A: de.wikipedia]**. Received Trithemius's magical writings
**[A for the year: SEP, and carried in `johannes_trithemius.journey.json`, whose stop marks the
*place* as a guess pinned to Cologne]**. The file repeats that hedge.

## 10. Geneva, Fribourg, Lyon (1521-1528)

- Geneva from 1521: medical licence and hospital-connected work **[A: en.wikipedia; SEP]**.
- Second marriage to **Jana Luisa Tissie**, aged eighteen, **end of 1521** **[A: de.wikipedia, which
  names her and gives the age]**. **Contradiction flagged:** one synthesis puts it in spring 1521,
  supported by no source. Name spelled variously (Iana Loysa Tytia).
- City physician of **Fribourg** 1523, **resigned July 1523** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. en.wikipedia's
  "until 1524" is contradicted by that resignation; with the May 1524 Lyon arrival, the tenure was a
  few months with a gap after. A synthesis giving "March-July 1523 ... roughly six months" is
  arithmetically wrong; that is four to five.
- **Lyon, May 1524**, physician to Louise of Savoy **[A: SEP; de.wikipedia]**.
- Refused a judicial horoscope for Francis I on canon-law grounds **[R]**: SEP as fetched contains
  neither the scene nor the October 1526 pension-cancellation date that travels with it.
- *De incertitudine et vanitate scientiarum et artium* composed 1526 during pension removal and
  travel restriction **[A: SEP; en.wikipedia gives 1526-1527]**. Gave up the post 1528 for Antwerp
  **[A: en.wikipedia; arrival "end of 1528" per de.wikipedia]**; the July 1528 transit date is
  **[R]**.

## 11. Antwerp, Mechelen, Brussels (1528-1532)

- Second wife dead of plague at Antwerp, shortly after childbirth, **17 August 1529** **[A:
  de.wikipedia, flatly]**. A March 1529 date circulates with **no** source support; August followed.
- Imperial archivist and historiographer at Mechelen, taken up **1529** **[A: de.wikipedia, "noch
  1529 antrat"]**. en.wikipedia places it vaguely "in the 1530s"; a reconstruction gives 27 December
  1529, a day **no source confirms**, which the file drops.
- Account of Charles V's Bologna coronation **[R]**; funeral oration for Margaret of Austria, dead
  at Mechelen 1 December 1530 **[A for her death: en.wikipedia]**. *De vanitate* printed at Antwerp
  1530 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- Briefly imprisoned for debt at **Brussels**, end of 1530 **[A: en. and de.wikipedia both name
  Brussels]**, against the vaguer "in the Netherlands" of synthesis. The public book-burning is
  **single-sourced to de.wikipedia**.
- *De vanitate* condemned by **Louvain** *and by the Sorbonne* **[A: SEP]**; the Paris condemnation
  is missing from syntheses naming only Louvain. The **Great Council of Mechelen** dismissal is
  **[R]**: two research passes tagged this episode differently, and the split adopted is [A] for
  the condemnations, [R] for the dismissal.

## 12. Bonn and the printing of the complete book (1532-1534)

- Received into Hermann von Wied's household at Bonn, 1532 **[A: SEP; de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia
  'Johann Weyer']**. **Month contradiction flagged, not resolved:** SEP gives **June 1532**,
  de.wikipedia **"Ab März 1532"**. The file writes 1532 and states both.
- Book I printed separately 1531. **en.wikipedia's *De occulta philosophia libri tres* gives three
  cities, "Paris, Cologne, and Antwerp"; SEP gives only Cologne.** The circulated "Antwerp/Paris"
  omits Cologne, putting a Cologne printing of Book I two years before the 1533 complete edition.
- **November 1532:** Johannes Soter typesetting the complete work at Cologne **[A: SEP, which names
  the printer]**. **December 1532:** the Dominican **Conrad Köllin** denounced it as heretical;
  Hermann's intervention allowed publication with an appendix from the anti-magic chapters of *De
  vanitate* **[A: SEP]**. A protest letter dated **11 January 1533** appears in synthesis, is
  **confirmed by no source**, and is dropped for SEP's December dating.
- Complete *De occulta philosophia libri tres* at Cologne 1533 **[A: en.wikipedia]**; SEP notes the
  surviving correspondence ends July 1533.
- 1534, from **Bonn**, a legal memorandum to the **Parliament of the Low Countries** **[A: SEP]**.
  Synthesis calls the addressee "the Mechelen tribunal" and dates it 22 February; **neither is
  confirmed**.

## 13. Arrest and death (1534-1535)

**Arrested by order of Francis I on return to France [A: SEP]**, and released **[A: SEP]**. **NOT
confirmed by any source:** that it happened at Lyon; that the cause was insults to Louise of Savoy
in *De vanitate*; that Johann Weyer is the source; that friends secured the release. The claim is
split: arrest and release [A], particulars [R], the Lyon pin itself a reconstruction used because
no other city is named.

**Died at Grenoble [A: all four sources].** Three of four give **18 February 1535**, aged 48; SEP
alone hedges to "1535 or, at the latest, in 1536". The file uses the day and carries the hedge.

## 14. Afterlife

- **Burial in the Dominican convent church at Grenoble [A: de.wikipedia]**, gravestone inscribed
  that the fitting place for Agrippa would be **Hades**, the stone moved between monasteries until
  its trail is lost. **Correction carried:** synthesis adds "Hades/Cerberus"; de.wikipedia does
  **not** mention Cerberus, and that dog-shaped embellishment is dropped.
- **Dominican church destroyed by Protestants, 1562 [A: de.wikipedia]**; no primary confirmation.
- **Three surviving sons, citizens of Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye (Isère), not carrying the Nettesheim
  name [A: de.wikipedia]**. **Coordinate correction:** the commune is at 45.1758, 5.2172, not the
  45.2372, 5.2969 that circulates, 9 km off.
- **The black-dog legend [R throughout].** Giovio's *Elogia* and Manlius's 1563 commonplace book as
  its print origins were **not** verified. Naudé's 1625 *Apologie* is confirmed via fr.wikipedia,
  and his version puts the death at **Lyon**, contradicting the Grenoble burial tradition; recorded,
  not resolved. Weyer's reported denial was **not** found in the pages consulted.
- **Marlowe, *Doctor Faustus* I.i [A: Gutenberg 811]**; **Shelley, *Frankenstein* chs. 2-3 [A:
  Gutenberg 84]**; **Apollinaire, "Cortège", *Alcools* 1913 [A: fr.wikisource]**.
- **Twentieth-century rehabilitation [A: fr.wikipedia; SEP's bibliography]**: Yates 1964, then
  Nauert, Perrone Compagni, Zambelli; the critical Latin edition, Brill, 1992. **Purdue's
  translation, Inner Traditions, 2021 [A, upgraded]**, confirmed by Open Library and en.wikipedia,
  which also lists Paul Summers Young (Black Letter Press, 2020).
- **The Cologne Rathausturm statue [R, partially rescued]:** fr.wikipedia carries a *photograph*
  captioned as Agrippa's statue beside Fygen Lutzenkirchen (1450-1515), corroborating the adjacency
  exactly. de.wikipedia's *Kölner Rathaus* confirms 124 figures fitted **1988-1995**, all removed in
  2006 and replaced in Savonnière stone from July 2008: so **1988 is the programme's start year, not
  an installation date**, and any figure there today is a post-2008 replacement. Neither Rathaus
  article lists Agrippa, but neither enumerates all 124.

## 15. Apparatus relations for the operator, and reception placed as citations

Agrippa is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework-applied-backward stops are licensed
and none were written. **Nothing was silently deleted.** Two classes of relation were caught that
are not rooted in his own biography:

1. **Forward, and therefore not licensable without the author's say-so.** The reception chain
   (Giovio; Manlius 1563; Marlowe 1604; Naudé 1625; Shelley 1818; Apollinaire 1913; Yates 1964;
   Perrone Compagni 1992; Purdue 2021) all postdates his 1535 death: new hashes in an old block if
   written as scenes. Since a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, each survives only as a
   `source` or `suggested_ref` on the Agrippa stop for the place or object it engages. Marlowe is
   the quote on the black-dog stop; Shelley is named once at the Cologne statue stop, with no
   Ingolstadt scene; Naudé sits at the deathbed, which is Agrippa's own; Apollinaire's "Cortège" is
   the quote on the Cologne stop, the poem addressing Agrippa and naming his city; Yates, Nauert,
   Perrone Compagni, Purdue, and Whitehead are `suggested_refs` on the 1533 publication stop.
   Writing the reception properly needs a forward grant, or stops on the later writers' own files.
2. **Shared-patron and shared-geography relations, dropped from campa prose but recorded here.**
   Three candidates were rejected as co-location rather than relation and are named in no campa:
   **Hieronymus Bosch** (a Bosch *Temptation of Saint Anthony* in Margaret of Austria's 1516
   Mechelen inventory; Agrippa reaches that court in 1529 and the two never met); **Thomas More**
   (Louise of Savoy negotiated the 1529 Treaty of Cambrai, which More may have attended; Agrippa was
   her physician 1524-1528 with no evidence he was there); **Hildegard von Bingen** (preached at
   Cologne three and a half centuries earlier). If the operator wants any surfaced, they belong in
   the other subject's file.
