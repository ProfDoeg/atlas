# Nicholas Roerich (1874-1947): research report

*2026-08-14. Painter, archaeologist, jurist, expedition leader, author of the first international treaty for the protection of cultural property. Slug `nicholas_roerich`. Journey file: `nicholas_roerich.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or contested detail. Contradictions are flagged and left open, not adjudicated. Where a pool coordinate was checked and found wrong, both the corrected value and the wrong one are recorded, so the correction is auditable.

The through-line is one legal idea carried across five continents: that a monument has no standing in law, and that the law must be written. Roerich formulates it at twenty-five over an opened burial mound in the Petersburg province, and it is signed in the Oval Office thirty-six years later while he is in Inner Mongolia collecting grass seed.

## 1. Saint Petersburg and Izvara, 1874-1901

- **Born 9 October 1874 in Saint Petersburg**, eldest son of a notary of Baltic German descent and a Russian mother. **[A: Wikipedia, "Nicholas Roerich"; roerich.org]**
- **Birthplace coordinate corrected.** The pool gave 59.9343, 30.3351, which falls on the mainland near the Admiralty, some 2.3 km southeast of Vasilievsky Island (whose eastern tip reaches only about 30.305 E). The birth house is Nikolaevskaya, now Universitetskaya, Embankment 25, on the island: **59.9375, 30.2915**, used in the journey file.
- **Izvara estate.** Childhood and youth; hunting, sketching, and from 1892 amateur excavation of local kurgans. **[A: Wikipedia; roerich.org]** **Coordinate corrected**: pool gave 59.3697, 29.4676, about 3.5 km northwest of the village; ru.wikipedia gives **59.3520, 29.5160**. The museum-estate there (opened 1984) is the house where he spent his first twenty-six years. **[A: ru.wikipedia]**
- **1893: simultaneous enrollment** at the university's law faculty and the Imperial Academy of Arts, studying under Arkhip Kuindzhi. **[A]** Title of "artist" **1897 [A]**; law degree **1898 [A]**.
- **1899: first formulation of the idea of legal protection for monuments**, during archaeological work in the Saint Petersburg province. **[A: Wikipedia, "Roerich Pact"]** The most load-bearing dated claim in the file, and it rests on one article; no primary text of the 1899 formulation was reached.
- **1899, Bologoye: meets Helena Ivanovna Shaposhnikova.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Helena Roerich"**, "In 1899, at Bologoye, she met the painter and archeologist Nicholas Konstantinovich Roerich"**]**
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: Helena and Mussorgsky.** The brief said "grand-niece." English Wikipedia says she descended through her mother from the Golenishchev-Kutuzov family and was *distantly related* to Mussorgsky. roerich.org calls her "niece of the composer Mussorgsky." Three degrees from three sources; the campa uses the neutral "a relative of the composer Mussorgsky." **[R]**
- **1900-1901, Paris: venue contradiction flagged.** The pool placed him at the **Académie Julian, rue du Dragon**. Nothing checked supports this: not English or Russian Wikipedia, not roerich.org, which says only that he planned "a year in Europe to visit the museums, exhibitions, studios, and salons of Paris and Berlin." The documented attachment is **Fernand Cormon**, who taught in his own studio, not at Julian's annexe. The stop is relabelled and pinned to the canonical Paris coordinate. **[A for Cormon; R for any address]**
- **1901: marriage in Saint Petersburg.** **[A: Wikipedia, "Helena Roerich"]**

**Gap:** nothing reachable documents what Roerich did on the 1892-1899 digs beyond the fact of them. No excavation report, no finds list, no institutional affiliation. That the digs produced the protection idea is his own retrospective framing as relayed by Wikipedia.

## 2. The Russian decade, 1903-1917

- **1903-1904: tour of some forty ancient Russian cities**, nearly ninety paintings of architectural heritage. **[A: Wikipedia, "Roerich Pact"]**
- **"Architectural Studies": date corrected.** Pool gave 1904; Wikipedia gives **1904-1905** ("consisting of dozens of paintings"). The range sits in `date_confidence`. **[A]**
- **1906: Florence [A]**, cut from the journey file for stop budget. **1906: directs the school of the Imperial Society for the Encouragement of the Arts [A]**; **1910: president of the World of Art (Mir Iskusstva) [A]**, merged into one stop pinned to the Society's Bolshaya Morskaya premises (59.9339, 30.3122). That coordinate is **[R]**: standard address, not a pool figure.
- **Talashkino, Church of the Holy Spirit: three corrections.** (i) Coordinate: pool gave 54.7278, 32.2917, roughly 10 km off; Wikipedia gives Flyonovo at **54.6561, 32.1853**. ## 3. Exile: Karelia, London, New York, 1916-1923

- **The emigration date is a compression, and has been recast.** The pool's chronology said "emigrates to Finland, 1918." Russian Wikipedia establishes the move to **Serdobol (Sortavala), Karelia, in December 1916** for Roerich's lung illness; Finnish independence and the 1918 border turned a medical residence into exile. Stop dated **1916-12-01**. **[A: ru.wikipedia, and the pool's geography lens, which dates the site 1916-1918]**
- **1919: London.** Diaghilev designs; Theosophical Society. **[A]**
- **1920: the collaborative esoteric work that becomes Agni Yoga / Living Ethics begins. [A: Wikipedia, "Helena Roerich"]** First volume *Leaves of Morya's Garden, Book One: The Call* (Paris, 1923); fourteen volumes to *Supermundane* (1938). **[A: Wikipedia, "Agni Yoga"]**
- **1920-1922: United States exhibition tour**, New York to San Francisco, Chicago on the way. **[A]** The opening gallery is not named in any reachable source; the stop uses a neutral Manhattan pin and says so. **Gap.**
- **Santa Fe, 1921.** Tagged **[R]** in the pool itself ("contested detail of itinerary"). Kept, because the mesas-to-plateau recognition is a claim the later expedition literature leans on, with the contest carried in `date_confidence`.
- **1921: Master Institute of United Arts founded, with Corona Mundi. [A: roerich.org]**
- **Anachronism corrected.** The pool pinned the **1920-1923 New York period to the Master Apartments, 310 Riverside Drive**, a building that opened **17 October 1929** on lots Horch began buying only in 1925. Its coordinate is off too (pool 40.7974, -73.9724; actual **40.8006, -73.9714**). The file splits them: neutral Manhattan pin for 1920-1921, separate 1929-10-17 stop at the corrected coordinate. **[A]**

## 4. The Central Asian Expedition, 1923-1928

- **December 1923**: the Roerichs land at Bombay and are in Sikkim by month's end **[A: roerich.org]**; Darjeeling base at **Talai Pho-Brang**, villa of the Thirteenth Dalai Lama in exile **[A]**.
- **Route**: Sikkim, Punjab, Kashmir, Ladakh, Karakoram, Chinese Turkestan (Khotan, Kashgar, Qara Shar, Urumchi), then the Altai, the Oyrot region, the central Gobi, Kansu, Tsaidam, Tibet. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Kashmir coordinate corrected.** The pool pinned "Shalimar Bagh, Srinagar" at 34.1281, 74.8823, about 2.4 km south of the garden (roughly 34.1478, 74.8722). Rather than choose, the file inherits the corpus's **canonical Srinagar pin, 34.0837, 74.7973**, byte-identical from `xuanzang.journey.json`, and relabels the stop.
- **Khotan, 1925-1926**: weeks under Chinese provincial guard, weapons confiscated. **[A: Wikipedia; Roerich, *Altai-Himalaya*]**
- **Moscow detour, 1926: sourcing and tag corrected.** English Wikipedia says only that the expedition made "a detour through Siberia to Moscow in 1926," and contains **nothing** about Georgy Chicherin, the casket of Himalayan soil, or Lenin's tomb; those come from the reconstruction literature (Znamenski, *Red Shambhala*; Andreyev, *The Myth of the Masters Revived*). The pool tagged this **[A]** citing Wikipedia, which is wrong. Retagged **[R]**, with `date_confidence` naming what is attested (the detour) and what is not (everything else).
- **The Shambhala dimension is [R] throughout.** That the Mongolia-Tibet leg was pursued partly as a Shambhala search, entangled with Soviet contacts and Buryat-Mongol messianism, is reconstruction (Znamenski, Andreyev, McCannon). Roerich's published accounts record Shambhala as *ethnographic material he collected*, which is a weaker and different claim, and is all the *Altai-Himalaya* text supports. Neither is asserted as the other.
- **Verkh-Uimon: coordinate corrected.** Pool gave 50.2333, 85.7833; ru.wikipedia gives **50.2133, 85.7406**, and confirms the August 1926 stay and the house-museum. **[A]**
- **Tibet detention, autumn 1927 to March 1928**: halted near Nagchu, roughly five months above 15,000 feet, five members dead, most animals dead. **[A]** The campsite coordinate (31.48, 92.05) is explicitly **[R]**, reconstructed from route descriptions.
- **Expedition length: contradiction flagged.** The pool's geography lens called it "three-year, roughly 15,500-mile"; the pool's own chronology and Wikipedia both say **five-year**. The file says five years and drops the **15,500-mile figure, which is not in Wikipedia**; roerich.org gives thirty-five passes of fourteen to twenty-one thousand feet and about 500 paintings, which the campa carries instead. **`Roerich's_Central_Asian_expedition` returns HTTP 404**, so there is no leg-by-leg chronology to check against: the months for Khotan, Kashgar, Urumchi, Urga, and Tsaidam are **[R]** inferences from route order and marching season.

## 5. Kullu, Urusvati, and the Pact, 1928-1935

- **1928: the family settles at Naggar and founds the Urusvati Himalayan Research Institute**, Helena as Honorary President-Founder. **[A: roerich.org; Wikipedia, "Helena Roerich"]** Correspondents including Einstein and Millikan **[A: Wikipedia, "Naggar"]**, though no correspondence itself was reached. **Gap.**
- **1929: Nobel Peace Prize nomination by the University of Paris [A]**; again **1932 and 1935 [A]**.
- **1929: the treaty drafted with G. G. Shklyaver in Paris**, with the Banner of Peace emblem. **[A: Wikipedia, "Roerich Pact"]**
- **Emblem description corrected.** The pool called it a "tricolor Banner of Peace." It is not tricolor: the article describes "a white cloth with a red circle and three red circles inscribed in it," two colors. The Museum reads the three circles as Art, Science and Religion within the circle of culture, and as past, present and future. **[A]**
- **1930: the draft Pact Cultura circulates internationally.** Tagged **[R]** in the pool and left **[R]**; the committee list was not reached.
- **1934-1935: the USDA Manchurian expedition** under Henry Wallace's sponsorship, collecting drought-resistant grasses in the Greater Khingan and Barga plateau, then the Gobi, Ordos, and Helan Mountains. **[A]** **Harbin coordinate corrected**: pool gave 45.753, 126.642, about 12 km southeast of the centre; the city is at **45.8038, 126.5350**.
- **The Wallace break is [R] as narrated.** That the venture ended in acrimony, that government botanists complained, and that the "Guru letters" reached the press is standard in the secondary literature but is not established at that level of detail from anything reachable here. The campa states the cancellation; the mechanism is reconstruction.
- **15 April 1935: the Roerich Pact signed in the Oval Office** by the United States and twenty other Pan-American Union states, the first treaty signed in that room; Wallace signs for the United States; Roerich is in Asia. **[A]** **In force 26 August 1935** on Cuba's ratification; ten of twenty-one signatories ratify. **[A]**

## 6. Last years and afterlife, 1942-2000

- **1942: Nehru and Indira Gandhi at the Kullu house [A]**; **the American-Russian Cultural Association founded in New York [A]**.
- **13 December 1947: dies at Kullu, aged 73 [A]**; cremated on the hillside above the house, memorial stone in place **[A: roerich.org]**.
- **Helena's cremation-day quote: truncation corrected.** The pool cut it mid-sentence and closed with a period. The source (Wikipedia, citing Kamalakaran, RBTH 2012) reads: "The day of cremation was exceptionally beautiful. Not a single breath of wind and all surrounding mountains were clad in fresh snowy attire." The full sentence is used.
- **1949: refounded in New York as the Nicholas Roerich Museum under Sina Fosdick [A: roerich.org]**, later at 319 West 107th Street, about 150 paintings plus archive **[A]**.
- **5 October 1955: Helena Roerich dies at Kalimpong, aged 76. [A]**
- **1954: the Hague Convention cites the Pact as a foundational precedent [A]**; its emblem supersedes the Banner internationally while the Pact stays valid among its ten ratifying states **[A]**. The file dates the stop 14 May 1954, the standard signing date; that day is **[R]** relative to the pool, which gives only the year.
- **Banner flights: dates corrected in both directions.** The pool said "Mir, 1990-1999" plus undated poles. The article gives a **single Mir flight, 11 February 1990** (Balandin and Solovyev), no 1999 terminus, and dates the poles: **North Pole 6 May 1988**, **South Pole 8 January 2000**. The pole flight therefore precedes the Mir flight.
- **The Fifth Dalai Lama claim, corrected and split.** Wikipedia, citing British intelligence records, says Moru monastery lamas recognized Roerich as the Fifth Dalai Lama's incarnation by a mole on his **right** cheek; the pool omitted the side. The **Panchen Lama / Maitreya / "Age of Shambhala"** element is **not** in that passage, belongs to the Znamenski-McCannon literature, and must not carry the intelligence-file citation. The **UFO** half is accurate. All **[R]**, none of it in any campa.
- **Institutional custody splits [R]**: two rival Moscow bodies (the Roerich Department of the State Museum of Oriental Arts, and the International Centre of the Roerichs), plus the Russian Museum, the Tretyakov, Sofia, Thiruvananthapuram, and Bangalore. The Moscow rivalry is litigated and no reachable source on it is neutral. Recorded, not adjudicated.
- **4426 Roerich (minor planet), a crater near Mercury's south pole, Altai peaks and passes named for the family. [A]**

2. **No primary text** of the 1899 formulation, the 1914 appeal, or the excavation reports; each is a summary sentence in a secondary article.
