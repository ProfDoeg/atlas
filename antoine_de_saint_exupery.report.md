# Antoine de Saint-Exupéry (1900-1944), research report

*Atlas of Journeys, working file. Compiled 2026-08-14 from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses), with the pool's own adversarial corrections folded in and flagged where they contradict the first-pass claim.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including anything carried by a single encyclopedia and uncorroborated elsewhere. Where sources disagree, both readings are printed and the disagreement is left open.

He was an airline employee before he was a novelist, and the geography of his books is the geography of a mail contract. The journey is well documented in its middle and vague at both ends: the childhood châteaux are a biographer's composite, and the last flight ends in water.

---

## 1. Lyon and the impoverished aristocracy (1900-1917)

- Born 29 June 1900 in Lyon to Viscount Jean de Saint-Exupéry and Marie Boyer de Fonscolombe, third of five children **[A: en. and fr.wikipedia]**. The birth apartment is placed on the rue du Peyrat, second arrondissement **[A: fr.wikipedia, following biographical consensus incl. Schiff]**. The father dies of a stroke before Antoine's fourth birthday, 1904, leaving the family titled and poor **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- Childhood between properties: Saint-Maurice-de-Rémens in the Ain, La Mole in the Var on the maternal side, and after 1909 a house at Le Mans and the Jesuit Collège Notre-Dame-de-Sainte-Croix **[A: fr.wikipedia; Cate, *His Life and Times*, 1970]**.
- **Correction, coordinate and date range.** The pool's pin for Saint-Maurice-de-Rémens (45.9494 / 5.3288) is about 4 km east of the château; fr.wikipedia gives 45°57'23"N 5°16'31"E = **45.9564 / 5.2753** **[A]**. "c. 1904-1917" understates the tie: baptized in the chapel in 1900, the estate passing to his mother in 1920, sold to the Lyon school board 20 December 1932 after a last family Christmas **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Château de Saint-Maurice-de-Rémens"]**. That it is "the emotional landscape behind the walled garden and roses of The Little Prince" is **[R]**: Schiff's and Cate's reading, never his own statement.
- Sent with his brother François to the Villa Saint-Jean at Fribourg during the war; baccalauréat there in 1917 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** François dies in summer 1917 of rheumatic fever with pericarditis, aged fifteen **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**. The *place* is disputed: en.wikipedia associates the death with the Villa Saint-Jean period, which would put it at Fribourg (46.8065 / 7.1619); fr.wikipedia gives only the season; Schiff and Cate place it at Saint-Maurice-de-Rémens on 10 July 1917, Antoine at the bedside, the scene retold in *Pilote de guerre* **[R either way]**. The journey file follows the biographers, hedge in `date_confidence`, and attaches no Fribourg coordinate to the death.

## 2. The failed sailor, the conscript pilot (1919-1923)

- Twice fails the Naval Academy entrance examination; enrolls at the École des Beaux-Arts as an architecture auditor, 1919 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Correction, unit name.** The pool had a "2nd Chasseurs regiment," and en.wikipedia gives "2e Régiment de chasseurs à cheval," cavalry. fr.wikipedia contradicts both: called up April 1921 to the **2e régiment d'aviation de chasse** at Neuhof, outside Strasbourg **[A]**.
- Brevet sequence per fr.wikipedia **[A]**: self-funded civilian lessons from June 1921; civil brevet 9 July 1921 at Strasbourg; assignment to the 37e régiment d'aviation at Casablanca 2 August 1921; **military** brevet 23 December 1921. The pool's geography lens made the Casablanca brevet the civil one; wrong, corrected.
- Briefly at Istres, January 1922, then the 34e régiment d'aviation at Le Bourget, where he crashes 1 May 1923 and fractures his skull, ending his military flying **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

## 3. The Line: Toulouse, Spain, the Sahara (1926-1929)

- Hired by Latécoère October 1926, posted to Toulouse-Montaudran **[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, "Didier Daurat"]**. First published fiction, the novella *L'Aviateur*, in *Le Navire d'Argent*, Paris, 1926 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Correction, and it matters twice.** Daurat hired and trained him **at Toulouse-Montaudran in 1926-27** and was never in Buenos Aires. The pool's chronology (index 10) and its 1954 memoir entry both have Saint-Exupéry "working under" Daurat in Argentina, or Daurat "overseeing him on the Patagonia routes"; both wrong **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Didier Daurat"]**. The Daurat *character* details hold: new pilots made to clean engines first ("le royal cambouis ... forme le caractère"), and the line to Jean Mermoz, "Je n'ai pas besoin d'artistes de cirque mais de conducteurs d'autobus" **[A: same]**.
- Flies Toulouse-Casablanca-Dakar through the Spanish legs (Alicante, Málaga) and the Moroccan fields (Agadir) **[A for the route; [R] for which stopovers he worked in which months, itemized nowhere reachable]**.
- Chief of the Cap Juby (Tarfaya) stopover, 1927-1928, negotiating in person with Sahrawi tribesmen for fliers held for ransom, and writing what becomes *Courrier Sud* **[A: fr.wikipedia; Cate]**. Légion d'honneur 1930 for the negotiations; *Courrier Sud* published 1929 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- Villa Cisneros (Dakhla), Port-Étienne (Nouadhibou), Saint-Louis and Dakar are the southward chain of the same line **[A for the chain; [R] for his presence at each]**.

## 4. Aeroposta Argentina (1929-1931)

- **Contradiction, partially resolved and left visible.** fr.wikipedia has him joining Guillaumet in South America **September 1929**; the geography lens dates the directorship to **October 1929**; es.wikipedia's biography says **1928**. What is dated is operational: es.wikipedia's Aeroposta Argentina article records him personally flying the final inspection runs of the Buenos Aires-Bahía Blanca-Comodoro Rivadavia line **29 October to 1 November 1929**, the line opening on 1 November **[A]**. The journey file uses autumn 1929 for the arrival and those flights for the first firmly dated Argentine action.
- Title per company records: **Director de Explotación** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Apartment in the Galería Güemes, Florida 165 / San Martín 170 **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**; the pool's pin (-34.6076 / -58.3735) sits about 500 m south of the building, corrected to approximately **-34.6033 / -58.3745**.
- **Correction, Comodoro Rivadavia.** The pool tagged this **[R]**; under-tagged. The field and his inspection of it are **[A]** (es.wikipedia). Only "the atmosphere behind *Vol de Nuit*" is **[R]**: the novel names no real town.
- **Correction, Punta Arenas.** The southern line reached Río Gallegos March 1930 and only later Río Grande, September 1935. No Punta Arenas service in his tenure, no Chilean leg **[A: es.wikipedia]**. It survives here only as the intended destination of the failed 1938 New York raid.
- **Correction, Asunción.** The first official Buenos Aires-Asunción flight was Pedro Ficarelli's, 22 March 1929, months before he arrived; not extended "under his direction" **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The pool's coordinate is also about 10 km off.
- Guillaumet crashes a Potez 25 in the Andes on his 92nd crossing, 13 June 1930, and is found alive 19 June after walking out across three passes; his line to Saint-Exupéry, "What I have done, I swear to you, no animal would have done," is retold in *Terre des hommes* **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- Meets Consuelo Suncín here **[A: es.wikipedia]**; civil marriage Nice 22 April 1931, religious ceremony Agay 23 April 1931 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

## 5. Vol de Nuit and the record attempts (1931-1939)

- *Vol de Nuit* published by Gallimard 19 September 1931, preface by André Gide; Prix Femina the same year **[A: fr.wikipedia, "Vol de nuit"]**.
- **A hedge the pool asked for and the sources do not support.** The afterlife lens carried an [R] claim that Rivière may be "a composite rather than a direct portrait of Daurat." fr.wikipedia on both *Vol de nuit* and Daurat states flatly that Rivière was inspired by Daurat, and the novel is dedicated to him **[A]**. Dropped as unsupported; the correction that matters is geographic (section 3).
- Journalism: Indochina 1934, the Mediterranean and Soviet Union 1935, Spain 1936 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. No itineraries are reachable, so the journey file carries no stop for them.
- **Correction, the Sahara crash.** 30 December 1935, Caudron C-630 Simoun, Paris-Saigon speed attempt, with his **mechanic** André Prévot; four days near-waterless; rescued by a Bedouin on a camel **[A: fr.wikipedia; en.wikipedia, "Wind, Sand and Stars"]**. The pool misdescribed the place as "near the Egypt border." The crash was in the Libyan (Western) Desert of Egypt, Wadi Natrun country, roughly 200 km northwest of Cairo **[A]**.
- **Contradiction between the pool's own lenses.** Geography lens 30.4 / 29.9, afterlife lens 30.0 / 29.5, Wadi El Natrun itself near **30.40 / 30.33**. All three disagree and the crash point is undocumented **[R]**. The journey file uses 30.40 / 30.33 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
- Guatemala City, La Aurora, 15 February 1938: crashes on takeoff attempting the New York-Punta Arenas record, lasting injuries **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- *Terre des hommes* February 1939; *Wind, Sand and Stars* the same year from Reynal & Hitchcock, dedicated to Guillaumet; the U.S. National Book Award and the Grand Prix du roman de l'Académie française **[A: en.wikipedia, "Wind, Sand and Stars"]**.

## 6. War, exile, the Little Prince (1940-1943)

- Reconnaissance in a Bloch MB.174 with Groupe de reconnaissance II/33 during the invasion, 1940 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Guillaumet is shot down over the Mediterranean 27 November 1940 flying a Farman F.220 to Syria **[A: en.wikipedia]**; Saint-Exupéry mourns him in *Lettre à un otage*, in a sentence the journey file carries verbatim **[A: fr.wikiquote]**.
- Hotel Palácio, Estoril, 28 November to 20 December 1940 **[A: Schiff, via the geography lens]**; New York aboard the S.S. Siboney 31 December 1940; National Book Award at the Hotel Astor 14 January 1941 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- Lecture tour of French Canada, May-June 1942, staying with the family of the philosopher Charles De Koninck, rue Sainte-Geneviève, Quebec City **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- *Pilote de guerre* 1942 **[A]**. *Le Petit Prince* completed October 1942, largely at the Bevin House, Asharoken, Long Island, published New York April 1943 in English and French at once by Reynal & Hitchcock **[A: en.wikipedia, "The Little Prince"]**.
- **Correction, Bevin House coordinate.** Pool: 40.9187 / -73.3357. en.wikipedia's Delamater-Bevin Mansion article: 40°56'05"N 73°22'43"W = **40.9347 / -73.3786** **[A]**, dating the rental to "late 1942" and noting that a sketch made there appeared on the French 50-franc note, 1993 to 2002.
- **Quote misattribution, corrected.** "What makes the desert beautiful is that it hides, somewhere, a well" sits on en.wikiquote's author page under the *Terre des hommes* heading, and the pool inherited that error. It is dialogue in *Le Petit Prince* (1943), spoken by the prince, ch. XXIV in the Katherine Woods translation (numbering varies by edition) **[A: en.wikiquote, "The Little Prince"]**.
- **Citation tightened.** "It seems that perfection is attained not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing more to take away" is genuine *Terre des hommes*, ch. III, "L'Avion" ("The Tool") **[A: fr.wikiquote]**, whose French original the journey file carries in the quote_source. The submitted English is one faithful rendering among several; en.wikiquote's differs slightly. Not adjudicated.

## 7. The last squadron (1943-1944)

- **Correction, split date.** April 1943 is the **departure**: he leaves North America with an American military convoy for Algiers in April **[A: en.wikipedia]** and presents himself to General Giraud in Algiers 5 May 1943 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The pool collapsed the two.
- Rejoins Groupe de reconnaissance 2/33 "Savoie" on unarmed Lockheed F-5B photo-reconnaissance Lightnings, eight years over the age limit, on an exemption approved by General Eisenhower **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The group moves to Borgo-Poretta, Bastia, Corsica, 17 July 1944 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Correction, the takeoff hour.** The pool twice said "before dawn." He took off in daylight: fr.wikipedia 08:25, en.wikipedia 08:45, hours after a sunrise near 06:10 on 31 July 1944. The twenty-minute discrepancy is left standing. Aircraft F-5B serial 42-68223, mission over the Rhône valley ahead of the Provence landings; he did not return and no crash was witnessed **[A]**.

## 8. The sea gives it back (1945-2008)

- Declared "mort pour la France" 1945 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.
- **Correction, the sharpest one.** He was **not** pantheonized. fr.wikipedia's list of transfers to the Panthéon omits him and no remains were placed there. What exists is a wall inscription: "À LA MEMOIRE DE ANTOINE DE SAINT EXUPERY POÈTE ROMANCIER AVIATEUR DISPARU AU COURS D'UNE MISSION DE RECONNAISSANCE AÉRIENNE LE 31 JUILLET 1944." **[A]**. The November 1967 legislative act rests on en.wikipedia alone, uncorroborated in French sources: **[R]**.
- September 1998: a fisherman off Marseille recovers a silver identity bracelet engraved with his name, Consuelo's, and Reynal & Hitchcock's **[A: fr. and en.wikipedia]**.
- May 2000: a diver locates P-38 wreckage off the northeast face of the Île de Riou **[A]**. The pool's chronology coordinate (43.25 / 5.3667) sits on the coastline near Les Goudes, about 7 km north; the journey file uses the geography and afterlife lens values instead.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** fr.wikipedia dates the raising to **1-3 September 2003**, en.wikipedia to **October 2003**. Formal identification in 2004, by airframe serial number 42-68223. The pool's "tail number confirmed against Lockheed production records" is an embellishment absent from the sources and is dropped.
- Cause of the loss remains unresolved: mechanical failure, fighter attack, pilot blackout from old injuries **[R]**.
- 2008: Horst Rippert (1922-2013), former Luftwaffe pilot, claims publicly to have shot the aircraft down in a Messerschmitt Bf 109 **[A that the claim was made; [R] as to its truth]**. **Correction:** the pool had Rippert privately suspecting it for decades; his stated position was the opposite, that he had no idea at the time whom he had hit ("if I had known it was Saint-Exupéry, I would not have fired"). fr.wikipedia notes the claim came "sans fournir de preuve matérielle." No corroborating radio records; comrades expressed skepticism.
- **[R], not verified:** the Père Lachaise family tomb carrying his name. Kept in the report, kept out of the journey file.

## 9. Afterlife objects

- The Latécoère 25 (F-AIEH) he flew for Aeroposta Argentina survives at the Museo Nacional de Aeronáutica, Morón **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Undated in the sources, so it is left out of the journey file rather than pinned to an invented year.
- Asteroid 2578 Saint-Exupéry, about 17 km across, citation published 11 July 1987, the choice echoing the prince's B-612 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Lyon's airport renamed Aéroport Lyon-Saint-Exupéry in 2000 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; no day is given, so the journey file uses his birthday as an explicit placeholder.
- *Le Petit Prince* is dedicated to Léon Werth, "When he was a little boy"; en.wikipedia gives 600 languages and dialects as of November 2024, second only to the Bible among translated works, about 140 million copies sold **[A]**. The pool's "300 to 600" hedge is stale and dropped.
- **Demoted to [R].** That the Musée de l'Air et de l'Espace keeps a permanent Saint-Exupéry exhibit and recovered aircraft material is **not supported** by either cited source; the museum's own article mentions neither. Its coordinate is also wrong by about 2 km. Left out of the journey file.

## Honest gaps

1. **No crash coordinate exists for December 1935.** Three values circulate in the pool alone; anything pinned approximates a region, not a site.
2. **The Spanish and West African stopovers are route knowledge, not biography.** They are documented as fields on the line he flew; no reachable source names him at any of them on a given date.
3. **The journalism years (1934-1936) are blank.** Three assignments named, no itineraries, no journey stop.
4. **François's death place**, **the takeoff minute of 31 July 1944** (08:25 against 08:45) and **the 2003 recovery month** (September against October) are each contested between sources and left contested.
5. **Consuelo Suncín is thin in this pool.** Marriage dates and Salvadoran origin attested, almost nothing of her own movement. A queue subject in her own right.
6. **The Père Lachaise inscription** was never checked against cemetery records.
