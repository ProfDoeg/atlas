# Marta María Ezcurra Real de Azúa: research report

*Atlas of Journeys, working file. Compiled 2026-08-06 from the verified research pool. Subject:
Argentine Catholic social worker; president of the Primer Congreso Superior de las Jóvenes de la
Acción Católica (1931); president and vice-president of ALPI (1944-1950); Directora de Asistencia
Integral of the Ministerio de Asistencia Social y Salud Pública under the 1955 de facto
government, charged with dissolving the Fundación Eva Perón.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or
single-partisan-source claim. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Gaps are stated as gaps.

---

## 0. The shape of the record

A subject with no dedicated encyclopedia entry in any language, whose name nonetheless appears in
four Spanish Wikipedia articles. That combination governs everything below.

The four articles (*Fundación Eva Perón*, *Comandos civiles*, *Revolución Libertadora
(Argentina)*, *Hospital Escuela Eva Perón*) are **not four sources**. They descend from one root
text: **Sandra Russo, "Un lugar seguro", *Página/12* contratapa, 17 September 2016**, cited by
name in two of them. The vivid material that makes Ezcurra memorable, the burning blankets, the
smashed blood-bank flasks, the destroyed iron lungs, the casino at Termas de Reyes, the
"suntuosa" quotation, descends from that single signed opinion column. Two partisan blogs recycle
it. Sentences were compared line by line against the Wikipedia wikitext.

Against that stands one independent academic account: **Daniela Testa (UBA/UNSAM), "La lucha
contra la poliomielitis y la figura de Marta Ezcurra. ¿Huellas del catolicismo social?", X
JIDEEP, Facultad de Trabajo Social, UNLP, 14-15 September 2017, SEDICI handle 10915/64267**. Testa
worked Catholic Action's archive and an ALPI institutional record; she supplies dates, titles, an
education and a job description the Russo lineage lacks, and contradicts it on two points of
substance. Almost every correction below is Testa against Russo, or a gazetteer against a guess.

## 1. Name, birth, family

- **Marta María Ezcurra Real de Azúa** **[A** *Las Balas del Campanario*, "Revista Nro. 11", 16
  June 2020, verbatim**]**. Fourth of twelve children **[R** same blog only**]**.
- **Birth year contested, left open.** The blog gives **2 May 1900**, Buenos Aires. es.wikipedia
  *Hospital Escuela Eva Perón* gives **"Marta María Ezcurra (1901-¿?)"**, footnoted to her
  electoral-roll entry (CUIT 27-00227996-2). Neither is a birth registry. **Not resolved.** The
  journey carries 1900-05-02 with the split declared in `date_confidence`.
- Father **Pedro Dionisio Ezcurra** (9 Oct 1860 - 8 Oct 1913), engineer, **Minister of Agriculture
  under José Figueroa Alcorta, 4 Nov 1907 - 12 Oct 1910** **[A** es.wikipedia *Pedro Ezcurra***]**.
  The blog's "Pedro Tomás Dionisio de Ezcurra Pardo" is a **garble** conflating him with his own
  father, **Pedro Tomás de Ezcurra Fuentes**. Corrected, and the garble recorded rather than
  silently smoothed.
- Mother **Marta Real de Azúa Muñoz**, married 6 March 1894 **[A** same**]**, which independently
  corroborates the blog's account of the household.
- **Rosas lineage: upgraded from [R] to [A].** The pool tagged the Encarnación Ezcurra connection
  an uncorroborated blog claim. It is not: es.wikipedia *Pedro Ezcurra* states that Pedro
  Dionisio's grandfather **José María Ezcurra** was *cuñado* of **Juan Manuel de Rosas**, that is,
  brother of **Encarnación Ezcurra**.

**Gap.** No baptismal record, civil registry entry or birth certificate located. The only
document-grade trace of her as a person rather than an office-holder is an electoral-roll number
cited at second hand in a Wikipedia footnote.

## 2. Formation, 1915-1933 (the section Testa rebuilt)

The pool arrived with this phase almost entirely **[R]**, sourced to "aggregated DuckDuckGo
snippets". Testa attests nearly all of it directly. Retagged **[A]**, all Testa 2017:

| Year | Fact |
|---|---|
| 1915 | Leaves the **Colegio Sagrado Corazón de Almagro** ("recién egresada del Colegio Sagrado Corazón de Almagro") |
| 1915 | Enters the **Congregación Hijas de María** |
| c. 1915-1925 | Apostolic-social formation with the **Hermanas de la Asunción** |
| 1925 | Joins **El Centavo**, assisting needy women through food and sewing workshops; later on its Consejo Superior |
| 1930 | Enters the **Escuela de Servicio Social del Museo Social Argentino** |
| 1933 | Obtains the **título de asistente social** |

**Institutional correction.** The pool said "Universidad del Museo Social Argentino". In 1930 the
body was the **Escuela de Servicio Social**; UMSA was not constituted as a university until 1956,
so the pool's name is an anachronism of twenty-six years.

**Gap.** No enrolment file, thesis title or class list. Testa's dates are taken on her authority;
the ACA and MSA archive references behind her paper were not consulted in this pass.

## 3. Acción Católica, 1931-1939: the split at the centre of her public identity

**The file's most consequential unresolved contradiction, and it is not resolved here.**

- **Version A (Russo lineage).** Ezcurra **founds the youth branch of Acción Católica Argentina in
  1931**. Near-identical wording in all four Spanish Wikipedia articles, traced to Russo,
  *Página/12*, 17 Sept 2016. **[R]**: four copies of one column are one source.
- **Version B (Testa 2017).** "En 1931 fue nombrada **presidenta del Primer Congreso Superior de
  las Jóvenes de la Acción Católica** por el arzobispo **Santiago Copello**." Not the founder of a
  branch: the presiding officer of its first congress. **[A]**, archive-based.

Every popular account opens with Version A. The only scholar who worked the archive gives Version
B. **Flagged in the journey's `date_confidence`, not adjudicated.**

Further, from Testa **[A]**: **1932**, travels to Europe, private audience with **Pope Pius XI**,
"en representación de la flamante Asociación de Jóvenes de la Acción Católica". **1937**,
publishes ***Primeras Nociones de Doctrina Social Católica***, a manual of the **Junta Central de
la ACA**, prologue by **Francisco Valsecchi**; **second edition 1939**, prompted by "la favorable
aceptación dispensada a este opúsculo" (fn. 5). Also publishes **lecciones de Sociología
Cristiana** in the Catholic supplement ***Ideales***.

**Correction.** *"Manual de Doctrina Social de la Iglesia"*, carried by the pool, **is not the
title of anything she wrote**. It is a search-engine synthesis. Deleted.

**Nickname.** "**el azote de Dios**", given by *Las Balas del Campanario* as an internal Catholic
Action nickname. **[R]**: no earlier print carrier, and the blog is hostile.

## 4. ALPI, 1943-1954

- **ALPI** (Asociación para la Lucha contra la Parálisis Infantil) founded **17 December 1943**,
  Buenos Aires, a women-founded body assisting poor children with poliomyelitis **[A** Efemérides
  de Salta; corroborated by the SEDICI record of Testa 2017**]**.
- **"Ocupó los cargos de presidenta y vicepresidenta alternadamente durante los primeros seis
  ejercicios (1944-1950)"** **[A** Testa, full text**]**. The pool's "president from 1943" was
  wrong on both the date and the shape of the office: she alternated.
- Testa, verbatim: "galenos y señoras constituyeron una **alianza médico-social sostenida en un
  andamiaje de ideas cristianas**".
- **ALPI Salta filial opens 12 June 1954**, first president **María Antonieta Day** **[A** *El
  Tribuno*, 2014; Salta efemérides**]**. **The pool's causal clause, "under Ezcurra's presidency of
  the parent association", is FALSE** and is struck: her last term ended four years earlier.

**The irony is documentary, not editorial.** The woman who ran Argentina's polio charity from 1944
to 1950 is the woman reported in 1955 to have ordered the destruction of the Fundación's
**pulmotores** because their plates bore the Foundation's initials, after which a severe polio
epidemic obliged Argentina to import twenty-one iron lungs from the United States (**[R]**, Russo
lineage via *Revolución Libertadora*). Both halves are in the record. Neither is stated by the
other's source.

## 5. The Fundación Eva Perón: the ground she is later given

All **[A]** from es.wikipedia *Fundación Eva Perón* unless noted. **No source places Ezcurra at
any of these**; they are the institutions she is later put in charge of dismantling, and the
journey file says so at each stop.

**3 April 1948**, the Foundation's first public act, the **Hogar de Tránsito N.º 1, Carlos Calvo
102**. **19 June 1948**, the **Fundación Eva Perón** founded (corpus: `eva_peron.journey.json`);
personería jurídica **8 July 1948**, seat at **Avenida Paseo Colón 850**. **1949**, **Ciudad
Infantil Amanda Allen**, entrance at **Echeverría 955**, Belgrano. **17 October 1949**, the first
**Hogar para Ancianos** at **Burzaco**, four more by 1950. **30 December 1949**, the **Hogar de la
Empleada General San Martín**, Avenida de Mayo 869, eleven floors of which nine are dormitories,
lodging for 500 women earning under $500. **Ciudad Universitaria de Córdoba**, $70,000,000
budgeted, under construction at Eva Perón's death in 1952, planned for 400 Argentine and 150
foreign students in 15 pavilions.

Two corrections. **The pool's claim that the Córdoba construction "is interrupted by the
Revolución Libertadora" is NOT in the cited source** and wore an [A] tag; downgraded to **[R]**.
And no source says Carlos Calvo 102 or Avenida de Mayo 869 specifically was occupied on 24
September: Wikipedia says only "cada uno de los institutos", so site-level occupation is inference
and is tagged **[R]** throughout the journey.

## 6. September 1955: coup, appointment, order

**Date correction.** The pool had "16 September 1955: a military coup overthrows Perón". Wrong in
three steps, all **[A]** from *Comandos civiles* / *Revolución Libertadora*: **16 Sept, 00:00**,
**General Eduardo Lonardi** raises the **Escuela de Artillería de Córdoba**, beginning the coup;
**20 Sept**, Perón leaves power; **23 Sept**, Lonardi sworn in as provisional president; **13
Nov**, displaced by **Aramburu**.

**Her title, corrected.** Testa: "en 1955 fue nombrada por el gobierno de facto como **Directora
de Asistencia Integral del Ministerio de Asistencia Social y Salud Pública**" **[A]**. Not
"Director of Social Assistance" (the pool), not Grassi's looser "directora de asistencia social"
(**Grassi 1989, p. 220**). She headed the **Dirección** itself; the ministry was then under
**Colonel Ernesto Alfredo Rottger**. **The day of her appointment is given by no source**; the
journey defaults it to the swearing-in date with the hedge declared.

**The two orders, as the sources actually distribute them.** **23 September 1955**: "Marta Ezcurra
ordena el día 23 de septiembre la ocupación militar de cada una de las Escuelas Hogar". **24
September 1955**: "Marta Ezcurra dispone la intervención inmediata de cada uno de los institutos
el día 24 de septiembre. Convoca para ello, a los miembros de los 'comandos civiles' de la Acción
Católica Argentina, ordena el desalojo inmediato de todos los niños y niñas..." Both were verified
present verbatim in *Fundación Eva Perón*, and **both are uncited**: no footnote hangs on any
Ezcurra sentence in that section. Text **[A]**, fact **[R]**. Then **23 December 1955**, a
**different event** under **Aramburu**: the military occupation of the escuelas hogares, at which
Ezcurra convenes the comandos civiles (*Hospital Escuela Eva Perón*).

**The pool recorded these as a September-versus-December contradiction. They are not.** That
article gives both, for two different events.

**Who gave her the order: three incompatible answers, unresolved.** *Hospital Escuela Eva Perón*:
"bajo las órdenes del general golpista Eduardo Lonardi". *Comandos civiles*: "siguiendo supuestas
órdenes de **Aramburu**". *Fundación Eva Perón*, wikitext pulled and read, **names neither man
anywhere in its Disolución section**. Russo 2016 names no general at all.

## 7. The dismantling: what is claimed, and how thin the chain is

Every item below is **[R]**, for one shared reason: all of it descends from Russo 2016, a signed
opinion column, with no archival citation anywhere in the chain.

- Blankets, sheets, mattresses, balls and toys bearing the Foundation's initials are burned.
- **Pulmotores** (iron lungs) whose plates bore the FEP initials are destroyed. The pool said
  "expensive medical equipment"; the sources say iron lungs specifically.
- Blood-bank stock is **destroyed**, not confiscated as the pool had it: "manda destruir todos los
  frascos de los Bancos de Sangre de los Hospitales de la Fundación porque contenían sangre
  'peronista'". The two words **"sangre peronista"** are the only part any source marks as a
  literal quotation.
- At the **Clínica de Recuperación Infantil, Termas de Reyes, Jujuy**, interned children are
  expelled and **a luxury casino is inaugurated on the site shortly afterwards**. The pool excluded
  this as uncorroborated; that was an error. It is in the pool's own principal source, *Fundación
  Eva Perón*, and in *Revolución Libertadora*. Restored.
- Furniture ends up as the personal property of comandos civiles members. Second hand to **Néstor
  Ferioli, *La Fundación Eva Perón*, vol. 1, CEAL, 1990**, not accessed.
- Former facilities converted to detention centres **[R** blog only**]**.

**Struck from the pool.** "More than two hundred food-distribution centres are closed under the
intervention Ezcurra directs." Wrong number and wrong actor: every source says "**casi 200
proveedurías**", and *Revolución Libertadora* attributes the closure to **Aramburu**.

**The "suntuosa" passage: attribution corrected.** The pool presented the passage beginning "Desde
el punto de vista material, la atención de los menores era múltiple y casi suntuosa" as
**Ezcurra's own intervention report**. The wording is confirmed verbatim in
*lasbalasdelcampanario* and in the parallel reproduction at *presmanhugo.blogspot.com*, but
Presman's introducing sentence reads: "**Cuando los interventores le envían los primeros informes
de las Escuelas Hogar**, Marta Ezcurra descubre con escándalo que..." It is language from the
**interventores' reports sent TO her**. Re-attributed, kept **[R]**: neither carrier gives a file
number or an archive.

**Removed from the quote set.** "Marta Ezcurra dispone la intervención inmediata de cada uno de
los institutos el día 24 de septiembre" was carried in the pool as a quotation. It is anonymous,
footnote-free Wikipedia narration: verified present, verified uncited. It appears in no `quote`
field in the journey.

## 8. December 1955 and Decreto-Ley 4161/1956

- **December 1955**: Aramburu's government names a **liquidating commission**. Among its members,
  **Adela Caprile** and **María Delfina Matilde Salomé Caprile de Ezcurra** **[A** *Fundación Eva
  Perón*, verbatim**]**. **Whether Caprile de Ezcurra is a relative of the subject is established
  by no source. Treated as a distinct, unconfirmed person, never merged.** The pool's added clause
  "seated at its former Paseo Colón headquarters" is **not in the cited source**, so **[R]**; the
  building was assigned to the Facultad de Ingeniería in 1956.
- **27 December 1955**: confiscation of furnishings ordered **[A** *Comandos civiles*, citing
  Ferioli**]**.
- **Decreto-Ley 4161/1956**. **Primary text now in hand** (Wikisource), which allows an upgrade
  from [R] to **[A]** and a correction. The pool quoted "**ofendían** el sentimiento democrático
  del pueblo argentino". The second considerando reads: "una doctrina y una posición política que
  **ofende el sentimiento democrático del pueblo Argentino**". Singular present, and the subject is
  the doctrine, not the banned objects. The day of promulgation appears nowhere in the pool; the
  journey defaults to 1956-01-01 and says so.

## 9. Death, afterlife, historiography

- **Death: two sources, one open bracket. Not resolved.** *Las Balas del Campanario*, verbatim:
  "Murió, o descendió a los infiernos, en 1996, durante la segunda presidencia de Menem, a sus 95
  años." es.wikipedia leaves it open: "**Marta María Ezcurra (1901-¿?)**". The pool tagged the 1996
  death **[A]**; **downgraded to [R]**, since it rests on a single hostile blog with no obituary,
  cemetery record or registry entry behind it. Internal strain: 2 May 1900 plus "aged 95" requires
  death **before 2 May 1996**.
- **Gaps.** No tomb, cemetery record, plaque, street, monument, translation, critical or annotated
  edition was located. The Recoleta vault appears here only as an **inherited family pin, tagged
  [R]**, and the campa says so: no source places her in it.
- **Edition history does exist**, against the pool's blanket gap statement: *Primeras Nociones*,
  Junta Central de la ACA, 1937, second edition 1939 **[A** Testa fn. 5**]**.
- **Her own voice, once, unreachable.** A **1964 interview in *Revista Diálogo***, archived by
  Acción Católica Argentina, cited by Testa as
  `accioncatolica.org.ar/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/MARTA-EZCURRA.pdf`. **The URL returns HTTP
  500.** The only located document in which she speaks at length in her own person.
- **Modern rediscovery, corrected.** The pool named a YouTube documentary. The chain is **Russo,
  *Página/12*, 17 Sept 2016**, which is what put her into Spanish Wikipedia at all, then **Testa,
  JIDEEP/UNLP, 14-15 Sept 2017** (SEDICI 10915/64267, UNPAZ project A00317), then the blogs.
- **Blog dating corrected by eight years.** Walter Onorato, "Marta Ezcurra: La ejecutora de
  intervenir, desmantelar y disolver la Fundación Eva Perón", is dated **28 February 2026**, not
  2018, per the blog's own listing. Its line, accurate as quoted: "El 23 de septiembre de 1955 no
  fue un día administrativo. Fue un acto fundacional del odio."

## 10. Contradictions flagged, not resolved

1. **Birth year**: 1900 (blog, with a day) vs 1901 (Wikipedia, from an electoral roll).
2. **Acción Católica 1931**: founder of the youth branch (Russo lineage) vs president of its first
   congress, named by Archbishop Copello (Testa).
3. **Who ordered the intervention**: Lonardi, Aramburu, or nobody named.
4. **Death**: 1996 aged 95 (blog) vs open bracket (Wikipedia), plus the arithmetic strain in §9.
5. **Caprile de Ezcurra**: relative, or unrelated bearer of the married surname. Undecided.
6. **Ciudad Infantil coordinate, inside the corpus**: `eva_peron.journey.json` pins the Ciudad
   Infantil at **-34.627, -58.438**; the attested address, **Echeverría 955**, geocodes to
   **-34.5535, -58.4412**, roughly 8 km north. This file uses the geocoded address and records the
   discrepancy rather than propagate a pin landing in the wrong half of the city. Recommend
   correcting `eva_peron.journey.json`.
