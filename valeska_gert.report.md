# Valeska Gert (1892-1978): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-31 for `valeska_gert.journey.json`. Subject: Gertrud Valesca Samosch, stage name Valeska Gert. Dancer, film actress, cabaret proprietor; Berlin, Munich, London, New York, Provincetown, Zurich, Kampen auf Sylt.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source assertion carried without independent confirmation. Contradictions between sources are flagged and left open. Sources that could not be opened are named with the reason.

Two structural cautions govern everything below.

1. **The Deutsches Tanzarchiv Köln calls its own Gert work catalogue (Werkverzeichnis) "vorläufig", provisional**, and the URL cited for it in the research pool now returns 404. Every premiere date sourced to that catalogue (Tanz in Orange, Canaille, Tod, Salome, the 1930 Paris program) is therefore **[A] at one remove and unre-verifiable this pass**. Where a second source disagrees, the disagreement stands unresolved.
2. **Gert's own memoirs are the sole source for a large share of the American material.** *Ich bin eine Hexe* (1968), *Die Bettlerbar von New York* (1950), and *Katze von Kampen* are autobiography written decades after the fact by a performer who reordered her life freely. Where a claim rests on them alone it is marked **[R, memoir]**, even when the wording is verbatim.

---

## 1. Berlin childhood and training (1892-1916)

- **Born 11 January 1892 in Berlin** as Gertrud Valesca Samosch, eldest child of the merchant Theodor Samosch (associated with the firm Zade und Falk) and Augusta Samosch née Rosenthal, a prosperous assimilated Jewish commercial family. **[A: Deutsche Biographie gnd118538888; filmportal.de person record; both Wikipedias]**. **Gap:** no birth address was located. The journey file uses the central Berlin pin (52.52, 13.405) and says so in `date_confidence`.
- **Ballet lessons arranged by her mother.** **[A]** as fact, **contradiction on the age**: German Wikipedia says seven, English Wikipedia says nine. Not adjudicated; the file dates the stop 1899 and names both readings.
- **The Anna Pavlova stage door.** Gert's memoir: as a child she waited at a Berlin stage door with a friend named Else, and Pavlova handed her a lily of the valley from her own bouquet, which she pressed into her diary and later called her "third love." **[R, memoir]**. Three separate problems, all flagged in the file's `date_confidence`:
  - No independent source for the episode was reached.
  - The venue is unknown. The research pool's placeholder coordinate (52.5158, 13.3184, the Deutsches Opernhaus in Charlottenburg) is **anachronistic and was discarded**: that house did not open until 7 November 1912 and cannot be the site of an encounter dated c. 1899-1901. The stop uses the central Berlin pin with the venue declared unknown.
  - Pavlova's first Berlin appearances are generally dated 1908-09, well after the memoir's implied date. The file dates the stop 1908 to hold segment order and states the contradiction rather than resolving it.
- **Studied acting with Maria Moissi and Alfred Breiderhoff, dance with Rita Sacchetto**, c. 1915. **[A: munich-dance-histories.de; both Wikipedias]**. Exact addresses of study not given by any source.
- **Gert on Isadora Duncan**: she credited Duncan with breaking ballet open and rejected Duncan's "return to Greek harmony" as too romantic. **[R: dossier summary of Gert's writing; not traced to a page of Gert this pass]**. This is a genuine one-directional relation (Gert wrote about Duncan) and is carried in a campa on that basis, not on shared geography.

## 2. First stages: Berlin and Munich (1916-1918)

- **Tanz in Orange, U.T. Lichtspiele, Nollendorfplatz, Berlin, 27 February 1916**, on a Rita Sacchetto program: orange silk, whitened face, bare feet. **[A: Deutsches Tanzarchiv Köln work catalogue, with the provisional-catalogue caveat above]**. **Contradiction, unresolved:** German Wikipedia reports an earlier showing a few days prior, probably at the Apollo-Theater; a third account names the Blüthner-Saal. Only the U.T. Lichtspiele date has a program behind it.
- **Engaged by Otto Falckenberg at the Münchner Kammerspiele for the 1916/17 season.** **[A: munich-dance-histories.de]**. Month not established; the file sets September to open the season and says so.
- **Bonbonniere cabaret, Munich, March 1917**, a program of "dance caricatures"; **new dances at the Hotel Vier Jahreszeiten on Maximilianstraße, October 1917.** **[A: munich-dance-histories.de]**. The Bonbonniere's exact street address was not located; the coordinate approximates the Munich cabaret district.
- **Bucharest tour with Erik Charell, March 1918**, her first known foreign engagement. **[R: dossier summary of Frank-Manuel Peter's documentary biography; no independent source reached]**.

## 3. The expressionist stages and Canaille (1918-1922)

- **Married the physician Helmuth von Krause, 28 March 1918.** **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **Reinhardt engagement, Deutsches Theater / Kammerspiele, Berlin, in Oskar Kokoschka's Hiob (1918).** **[R: German Wikipedia; widely reported, no primary document reached]**. **Correction applied:** the research pool bundled Hiob, Toller's *Die Wandlung*, and Wedekind's *Franziska* into a single Reinhardt stop. That merges at least two theatres and misattributes the most famous of the three. *Die Wandlung* premiered **30 September 1919 at Die Tribüne, Berlin, directed by Karlheinz Martin**, an independent expressionist house, not Reinhardt's **[A: German Wikipedia, "Ernst Toller"]**. The file splits them into two stops. **Coordinate corrected:** Deutsches Theater is Schumannstraße 13a, 52.5244, 13.3819 (OpenStreetMap); the pool's 52.5258, 13.3874 sits about 410 m northeast across the Charité grounds. **Gap:** no coordinate for Die Tribüne was established, so that stop uses the central Berlin pin and declares it.
- **Premiere of Canaille (Kanaille), Weißer Saal, Hamburg, 30 January 1919.** **[A: Deutsches Tanzarchiv Köln work catalogue]**, again with the provisional-catalogue caveat. **Contradiction, unresolved:** English Wikipedia and most secondary summaries place Canaille's premiere and notoriety in the early 1920s, and some summaries date the piece 1915 or 1916. The archive's program-based date is preferred in the file only for ordering; the disagreement is stated in `date_confidence`.
- **The 1922 police anecdote.** English Wikipedia: "When she danced Canaille with an orgasm in Berlin in 1922, the audience called the police." **[R]**. The research pool's phrasing, that the dance "prompted a police response," **overstates the source in direction**: the audience summoned police; nothing in any reachable source describes an intervention, a report, or a charge. **No contemporary police record was located.** The file states it as the audience calling, and as anecdote.
- **Final documented Tanz in Orange, Städtisches Schauspiel, Baden-Baden, 15 June 1921** **[A: Tanzarchiv catalogue]**.
- **Salome, reduced version.** **Contradiction, unresolved and material.** The Tanzarchiv catalogue records the premiere on **26 November 1921 at the Klindworth-Scharwenka-Saal, Berlin**; the dossier and other secondary summaries give **20 April 1923 at Berlin's Tribüne**. Both cannot be the premiere. The cited catalogue URL 404s and the archive itself calls that catalogue provisional, so **neither date can currently be preferred**. The file carries the 1921 date for ordering and flags the alternative. **Coordinate corrected:** the Klindworth-Scharwenka-Saal was a hall of the Klindworth-Scharwenka-Konservatorium at Lützowstraße 76, Berlin-Tiergarten, 52.5040, 13.3583 (OpenStreetMap, via German Wikipedia on the conservatory); the pool's 52.5008, 13.3667 lands several blocks southeast near the Landwehrkanal. The "exact address unconfirmed" caveat is dropped: the address is known.
- **Tod (Death), first documented performance, Schwechtensaal, Berlin, 14 November 1922** **[A: Tanzarchiv catalogue]**. English Wikipedia gives November 1922 without a day.
- **Relationship with the actor and writer Aribert Wäscher from 1923 to 1938** **[A: English Wikipedia]**.
- **Mary Wigman as rival.** Gert publicly caricatured Wigman's stamping and dust-raising as still "pseudo-classical." **[R: dossier; the Wigman journey file does not mention Gert]**. Carried as a real one-directional relation (Gert on Wigman). What the Wigman file does attest **[A]** is the opposite trajectory after 1933: Wigman stayed, took local leadership in the regime's dance-teachers league, and her Jewish pupils and colleagues vanished from the school rolls.

## 4. Film and cabaret in the Weimar years (1924-1932)

- **Puck in Ein Sommernachtstraum, directed by Hans Neumann, 1925.** **[A: both Wikipedias; silentfilm.org, "A Pungent Presence: Valeska Gert in Silent Film"]** for the role and the hostile set; Gert: "I was tired because the fear of shooting prevented me from sleeping."
- **Frau Greifer, the procuress, in G. W. Pabst's Die freudlose Gasse, 1925**, with Asta Nielsen and Greta Garbo **[A: both Wikipedias; Arsenal Berlin screens the film 11 July 2026]**. Suse Byk's photographic sessions are credited with converting her fear of the camera into "pleasure in what had pained me before: being captured by the camera" **[A: silentfilm.org]**.
- **Studio locations: an invented precision, removed.** The research pool placed both films at "Neubabelsberg film studios" at 52.4064, 13.1372. Two errors. (a) That coordinate is about 3.1 km from the studio it claims, in open land near Kohlhasenbrück; Studio Babelsberg is 52.3820, 13.1136 (OpenStreetMap). (b) More seriously, **no source names a shooting location for either film**, and *Die freudlose Gasse* was a Sofar-Film production with no located Neubabelsberg link. The file keeps one Babelsberg pin for the Neumann shoot with the inference declared in `date_confidence`, and places the Pabst film at the central Berlin pin as film work of unrecorded location. This is a downgrade of precision, deliberately.
- **Zoe, Nana's maid, in Jean Renoir's Nana, 1926** **[A: English Wikipedia; Arsenal screens it 10 July 2026]**. Shooting location not established; central Berlin pin.
- **Tontänze (sound dances) from 1926**, voice, cries, and distorted speech joined to movement **[A: English Wikipedia]**.
- **Reformatory director in Pabst's Tagebuch einer Verlorenen (1929)** with Louise Brooks, and **Frau Peachum in Pabst's Die 3-Groschen-Oper (1931)** **[A: English Wikipedia]**.
- **Chansonette and Opus I, Komposition auf ausgeleiertem Klavier, Comédie des Champs-Élysées, Paris, 22 March 1930** **[A: Tanzarchiv catalogue]**.
- **First visit to Kampen auf Sylt, 1930**, on friends' recommendation; she liked it enough that a house was built for her there **[A: sylt.de, "Kunststele Valeska Gert"]**. **Correction:** sylt.de does **not** say her husband had it built; the research pool added that. Dropped.
- **Kohlkopp ("Cabbage Head"), her first own Berlin cabaret, 1932, short-lived** **[A: kabarett.de; both Wikipedias]**. Street address not given by any source.

## 5. Exclusion, London, and emigration (1933-1939)

- **1933: barred from the mainstream.** **Correction to the research pool, which was too absolute.** She was declared *entartet* and shut out of German stage and screen; Jews were excluded from the Reich cultural chambers from September 1933. But German Wikipedia records that she could still perform through the **Kulturbund Deutscher Juden**, the segregated Jewish cultural association. It was not a total ban on performing; it was expulsion from the mainstream with one remaining outlet. **[A: German Wikipedia; English Wikipedia for the exclusion]**. The claim that Goebbels's *Das erwachende Berlin* carried her photograph among the rejected Weimar theatre is **[R: dossier only]**.
- **Pett and Pott: A Fairy Story of the Suburbs, Alberto Cavalcanti, GPO Film Unit, 1934.** **Upgraded [R] to [A]**: German Wikipedia's filmography lists "1934: Pett and Pott; Regie: Alberto Cavalcanti" outright, and the Arsenal Berlin 2026 retrospective screens it on 6 July. The earlier hedge (filmportal.de did not list the title) is no longer needed. Note the item sits out of chronological order in the source array, after the 1936 marriage.
- **Divorce from Helmuth von Krause finalized 23 November 1935** **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **Married the British writer and cultural organizer Robin Hay Anderson in London, 24 April 1936**, securing British passport protection **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **Performed at Raymond Duncan's Paris theatre, 31 Rue de Seine, 1938**, after which an American manager offered a round-trip ticket, a contract, and $500 **[R: dossier / memoir only]**. Raymond Duncan was Isadora Duncan's brother; the Duncan journey file documents his Greek-revival institute at Kopanos, Athens, and **does not corroborate a Paris venue or any Gert connection**.
- **Sailed for New York aboard the Île de France, early 1939** **[R: memoir]**. Some summaries date the crossing to 1938; the dossier judged early 1939 better supported. Not adjudicated here.

## 6. America: Hollywood, the Cherry Lane, the Beggar Bar (1939-1946)

Nearly all of this phase is **[R, memoir]**, from *Die Bettlerbar von New York* and *Ich bin eine Hexe*, read here through Karen A. Mozingo's dissertation, which quotes and translates them.

- **Los Angeles, 1939.** Travelled by bus, contacted Salka Viertel at Mabery Road, Santa Monica, met émigré film figures without success; the producer Walter Wanger told her she was "not the type," and she angered Ernst Lubitsch by telling him American actors looked like picture postcards. **[R, memoir]**.
- **Provincetown, 1940.** A shed room at $25 a month, dishwashing at $0.30 an hour ("a classic beginning in America for everyone who wants to become a millionare"), then posing nude for Hans Hofmann's painting school at $12.50 for five morning sessions, which she turned into a sequence of theatrical postures. **[A for the fact of the posing: English Wikipedia; R, memoir for the detail and the sums]**.
- **Cherry Lane Theatre, 38 Commerce Street, New York, October 1940**: Boxen, Baby, Tod, Kupplerin, Koloratursängerin. Critics admired Tod and often found the program too European. **[A for the program: English Wikipedia; R for the critical reception]**.
- **The White Whale, Provincetown, summer 1941**; Tennessee Williams urged her to open a New York cabaret **[A: English Wikipedia]**.
- **Beggar Bar, 3 Morton Street at Bleecker, Greenwich Village, opened late 1941.** **[A: Künste im Exil, "Valeska Gert: Beggar Bar"]** for the bar, its mixed wartime clientele of émigrés, artists, sailors, gay and Black patrons, and Tennessee Williams working there as waiter and reciter; Kadidja Wedekind and Maria Collm performed. Judith Malina in the cloakroom is **[A: English Wikipedia]**. **Julian Beck, Jackson Pollock, and Jack Kerouac in the staff or circle are [R] and explicitly weak**: no dates, jobs, or duration are established in any source consulted, and the Burroughs journey file, which covers the same New York years, does not mention Gert or the bar at all.
  - **Source failure, recorded:** kuenste-im-exil.de is currently unreachable (DNS failure on `www`, 404 on the bare host). The Künste im Exil citation therefore **backs nothing that can presently be opened**, including the bar's closure account.
- **Closure, early 1945.** **Contradiction, unresolved:** English Wikipedia gives February 1945; German Wikipedia gives "Frühjahr 1945," spring. The file writes it as the first months of 1945 and names both. The stated cause, loss of the cabaret licence after serving the alcoholic "Beggar Sip" (coffee with egg liqueur) without a liquor licence, **could not be re-checked** for the reason above and is carried **[R]**.
- **Valeska's, Provincetown waterfront studio, May 1946**: light blue walls under pink bulbs, stools from nail barrels, a stage of two drifted crates **[R, memoir]**. Afterwards, disputes with neighbours over noise and a dance partner's unpaid wages, contempt of court for refusing to pay a fine, one night in jail, released when regulars intervened **[A for the jail night: English Wikipedia; R for the docket, which the dossier itself flags as unlocated]**.

## 7. Return: Zurich, Baden-Baden, Berlin, Sylt (1947-1978)

- **Paris and Zurich, 1947**, while seeking permission to enter Berlin **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **Valeska und ihr Küchenpersonal, Zurich, 1948**, roughly six months **[A: English Wikipedia]**.
- **Re-entry to Germany through Baden-Baden, December 1948**, on a six-month American occupation permit **[R: dossier; the year 1949 is given in the English Wikipedia summary]**.
- **Bei Valeska, in the former Opernkeller of the Theater des Westens, Kantstraße 12, Berlin, 1949.** **[R]**, and the siting is weaker than the pool suggested: German Wikipedia's article on the Theater des Westens **does not mention an Opernkeller or "Bei Valeska"**, recording the Wilde Bühne (1921-23) and the Tingel-Tangel-Theater (from 1931) in the basement instead. The building did house the Städtische Oper after 1945, so an Opernkeller is plausible; it is not sourced. **Coordinate corrected** to 52.5059, 13.3290 (OpenStreetMap); the pool's 52.5049, 13.3324 falls about 250 m east, short of Uhlandstraße.
- **Hexenküche ("Witch's Kitchen"), Berlin, from 1950**, a winter cabaret running into the mid-1950s, where the young Klaus Kinski appeared and worked; her postwar numbers mocked German amnesia about the Nazi past and included the "KZ-Kommandeuse" / Ilse Koch routine **[A: kabarett.de; both Wikipedias]**. Street address not given.
- **Ziegenstall ("Goat Shed"), Kampen auf Sylt, from 1951**, at her own house: hay on the floors, milking stools, mangers, waitresses who sang and recited between drinks; run until her death **[A: both Wikipedias; sylt.de]**. **Contradiction, resolved with the disagreement recorded:** kabarett.de, itself affiliated with the Deutsches Tanzarchiv, dates the Ziegenstall's continuous run "von 1956 an." Three independent sources give 1951, so the file uses 1951 and names kabarett.de's 1956 in `date_confidence`. Whether 1956 marks a later formalization is not established.
  - **Coordinate corrected:** Kampen is 54.954, 8.344 (OpenStreetMap 54.9538, 8.3441; German Wikipedia 54° 57' N, 8° 21' E). The pool's 54.9394, 8.3389 lands about 1.6 km south in the dune belt between Wenningstedt and Kampen, outside the village.
- **Giulietta degli spiriti, Federico Fellini, 1965**, an international screen comeback after decades **[A: both Wikipedias; filmportal.de; Arsenal screens it 13 July 2026]**. **Coordinate corrected:** Studi di Cinecittà is 41.8525, 12.5782 (OpenStreetMap); the pool's 41.8577, 12.5687 falls about 1 km west-northwest in residential Don Bosco. Note also that Fellini shot location work elsewhere in Rome and **the shooting site for her scenes is not established**.
- **Filmband in Gold, 28 June 1970**, for long-standing and outstanding contribution to German film **[A: German Wikipedia]**.
- **Guest role in Rainer Werner Fassbinder's Acht Stunden sind kein Tag, 1973** **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **Hansjürgen Rosenbauer's talk show Je später der Abend, dated 19 November 1975** in the research pool, later cited by the artist Wolfgang Müller as his discovery of Gert as a proto-punk figure. **[R]**: the late-career television appearances are attested in general outline on both Wikipedias, but **the exact 19 November 1975 date was not independently reconfirmed**, and the smb.museum page that would corroborate the Müller connection is gone (404).
- **Aunt Praskovia in Volker Schlöndorff's Der Fangschuss, 1976**, her final fiction-film role **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **Signed to play the estate agent Knock in Werner Herzog's Nosferatu: Phantom der Nacht, 1 March 1978**, shortly before her death; she died before filming and the role went to another actor **[A: both Wikipedias]**.
- **Death at Kampen.** **Correction to the research pool's anchor.** The pool used 1978-03-18 as the death date; that is the **forced-entry** date. German Wikipedia: death "zwischen dem 15. und 18. März 1978 (wahrscheinlich am 16. März)," found after four days unseen. kabarett.de gives the range 15-18 March; filmportal.de gives 15 March. The file dates the stop 1978-03-16 and states the range and the disagreement. sylt.de supplies the trigger: a neighbour alerted police after hearing her cat crying. **The medical cause of death is not established in any source consulted.**
- **Burial, Friedhof Ruhleben (Am Hain), Berlin-Westend, field XVI-175**, black stone with her name in pink signature-style lettering and the word "Tänzerin"; Ehrengrab of the City of Berlin from 1978 **[A: German Wikipedia, verbatim "Die Grabstelle befindet sich im Feld XVI-175"]**. The **[R]** hedge in the research pool can be dropped. She had wanted a bright red coffin; the burial used a conventional coffin draped in red cloth **[A: German Wikipedia]**. Volker Schlöndorff giving the funeral address is **[R: dossier only]**.
  - **Coordinate corrected, and a duplication removed.** The research pool carried **three different points for one grave**: 52.5187/13.2601 (geography lens), 52.5219/13.2178 (afterlife lens), and neither is right. German Wikipedia's article on the Friedhof Ruhleben gives 52° 31' 25.1" N, 13° 13' 47.8" E = **52.5236, 13.2299**. The file carries one point only. **Exact burial date not established**; the file dates the stop 1978-04-01 and says so.

## 9. Interlocks: named and refused

**Named in a campa**, on the strength of a direct or one-directional relation, never on shared geography:

- **`anna_pavlova`**: Gert's memoir account of the stage door and the lily of the valley, and her calling Pavlova her "third love." One-directional and unverifiable, flagged as such in the stop.
- **`isadora_duncan`**: Gert wrote about Duncan, crediting her with breaking ballet open and rejecting her Greek harmony as romantic. One-directional.
- **`mary_wigman`**: Gert publicly caricatured Wigman's style; the two are the standard Weimar antithesis, and their 1933 trajectories diverge completely, which the Wigman file attests from its own side.

**Refused**, because the only link is time and place, which the map already shows:

- **`hanya_holm`**: same German-trained New York dance world from 1931; no source ties them.
- **`theodor_adorno`**: the Pacific Palisades émigré colony from November 1941. The Gert/Brecht "epic theater" anecdote is flagged as lore in the dossier itself, and Gert's Los Angeles trip predates the colony's assembly.
- **`william_s_burroughs`**: Columbia and Morningside Heights, 1943-45, against Gert's Greenwich Village. The Burroughs file does not mention Gert or the bar; the Kerouac-at-the-Beggar-Bar claim is unconfirmed on its own terms.
- **`andre_breton`**: shared Paris coordinate convention only.
- **`victoria_ocampo`**: checked, no Gert reference.

**No Argentina connection exists and none was invented.** The dossier records no South American travel for Gert at all. The Kaaba, the Temple Mount, and the other standing canonical pins are not applicable to this subject.
