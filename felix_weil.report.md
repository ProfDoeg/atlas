# Felix Weil (1898-1975): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for `felix_weil.journey.json`. Felix José Weil, Argentine-German heir who endowed the Institut für Sozialforschung at Frankfurt in 1923 and spent his last working years on the payroll of the United States Air Force.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or inference. Contradictions are printed and not adjudicated, except where one side is a demonstrable artefact (marked, with the demonstration). Gaps are stated as gaps. **de/en/es** = German, English, Spanish Wikipedia.

**Method.** One monograph exists, Jeanette Erazo Heufelder's *Der argentinische Krösus* (2017), and it was **not** reachable in full text; it appears here only through the p. 180 sentence de quotes and through review notices on Perlentaucher.de. Everything else rests on encyclopedic articles plus marxists.org. That base is unusually self-contradictory, and the contradictions are the most useful thing in it.

---

## 1. Origins: the grain fortune, 1868-1898

The father is the material premise of the subject and is better documented than the son.

- **18 September 1868**: Hermann Weil born at **Steinsfurt**, Baden (now Sinsheim), tenth of thirteen children of a Jewish family **[A: en "Hermann Weil"]**. The gloss "livestock-trading family" is not in the cited article and is dropped.
- **1883**: apprenticed to the grain trader **Isidor Weismann** at Mannheim, trading across Switzerland, Antwerp and the Balkans; **1888**: emigrates to the **United States** after his brothers Leopold and Gustav **[A: same]**. The usual "emigrates to Buenos Aires 1888" **merges two migrations ten years apart**; de dates the Argentine move to **1898** **[A: de "Hermann Weil (Unternehmer)"]**.
- **1896** marries **Rosa Weismann**; **1898** founds **Weil Hermanos & Cia.** at Buenos Aires with his brothers; by **c. 1900** the firm has some **3,000 employees**, European subsidiaries and **60 ships**, making him the world's largest grain trader **[A: en "Hermann Weil"]**.

### The birthplace contradiction (open)

- **[A: en and de "Felix Weil"]** Felix José Weil born **8 February 1898 in Buenos Aires**.
- **[R, contradicted]** The queue entry and family tradition give **Concepción del Uruguay, Entre Ríos**. Nothing located corroborates it; neither article mentions the town. Both birthplaces are stopped in the journey file, the second with the contradiction printed in `date_confidence`.
- **[A: es "Félix Weil"]** gives a birth year of **1895**. Not harmless: the same entry computes his age at death as **80**, so the repeated "16 of his 80 years in Argentina" imports the error. Against 1898 he lived **77 years**, and the ratio is **16 of 77**.
- The given name **"Lucio"** is in no source and is dropped; de on the father names him "Felix José (1898-1975)".

## 2. The German schooling, 1907-1919

- **1907**: sent to Germany at nine for the **Goethe-Gymnasium**, Frankfurt **[A: en and de "Felix Weil"]**. The school stood on the Bahnstrasse, today Friedrich-Ebert-Anlage, from its 1897 foundation, so the 1907 stop pins on the surviving site.
- **1907**: Hermann returns to Germany for health reasons, Samuel keeping Buenos Aires while Hermann directs the business **from Rotterdam**; he settles at a villa at **Zeppelinallee 77**, Frankfurt, and spends some **120 million marks** on relief organizations in the following years **[A: en and de on Hermann Weil]**. The clause "directing trade across Switzerland, Antwerp and the Balkans from there" belongs to the **1883-1888 Mannheim apprenticeship** and is removed. No coordinate for the villa was verified, so the stop uses the canonical Frankfurt pin.
- **16 April 1912**: **Rosa Weil dies of cancer** **[A: en "Hermann Weil"]**. **Gap:** no source names the city or circumstances. Felix is fourteen.
- **1919**: his **promotion at Tübingen is forbidden** for revolutionary political engagement **[A: de "Felix Weil"]**. The German is *verboten*, stronger than "denied"; the subject was *Nationalökonomie*. **Gap:** no start year for the Tübingen studies exists, so "c. 1917-1919" is unsourced.

## 3. Doctorate and radicalization, 1919-1922

- Doctorate taken **at Frankfurt**, dissertation rendered in English as **"Socialization: An Attempt at a Conceptual Foundation, with a Critique of the Plans for Socialization"** (German short form *Sozialisierung: Versuch einer begrifflichen Grundlegung*) **[A: en and de "Felix Weil"]**. **The year 1920 is [R]**: neither article gives a year for the Frankfurt doctorate.
- **[A: en "Felix Weil"; en "Karl Korsch"]** He met **Karl Korsch** and studied Marxist economic theory. **The date is a gap**; the circulating "1920" is in no source, and the file marks the stop `undated in every source consulted`.
- **[A: Jörg Später, "Zuerst kommt die Geldanlage, dann die Theorie", FAZ, 7 March 2017, p. 10, quoted in de]** Weil belongs "zur Generation der um die Jahrhundertwende geborenen und aus bürgerlichen, meist jüdischen Familien stammenden Intellektuellen", drawn in the twenties to "einem philosophischen Marxismus jenseits der Arbeiterparteien".
- **1921**: marries **Käthe Bachert**, moves to Argentina for about a year; the marriage runs to **1929** **[A: en and de]**. **Flag:** the sources say only *Argentinien*, so **Buenos Aires as the city of that stay is [R]**, marked as such in the file.

## 4. The founding, 1923-1924

- **3 February 1923**: the **Institut für Sozialforschung** is founded at the University of Frankfurt **[A: en "Frankfurt School", corroborated by de "Erste Marxistische Arbeitswoche"]**, financed through a **Stiftung set up by Hermann and Felix Weil** **[A: de "Institut für Sozialforschung"]**. **Removed as unsourced:** "by decree of the Prussian ministry of education", and the related claim of a ministry negotiation over university affiliation.
- **Date contradiction, open:** de "Felix Weil" says he "war **1924** Mitgründer", and an SZ notice says he endowed the institute "im Jahr **1924**". The likeliest reading is **1923 founding, 1924 opening of the premises**, but the German entry describes *his role*, not the building, so this is a reconciliation and not a resolution.
- **20 May 1923 (Pentecost), eight days**: the **Erste Marxistische Arbeitswoche** at a railway hotel in **Geraberg** near Arnstadt, owned by the communist **Friedrich Henne**. Korsch probably conceived it, Sorge probably coordinated invitations, **Weil financed it** **[A: de "Erste Marxistische Arbeitswoche"]**. Pentecost 1923 did fall on 20 May.
  - **Place and year contradictions, open:** en "Felix Weil" puts the gathering at **Ilmenau**, de at **Geraberg**; en "Frankfurt School" dates it **1922** against three other articles. The dedicated German article, built on Buckmiller's photographic research, is followed on both counts, with the English readings flagged in the file.
  - Attendees **[A: de]**: Lukács, Karl and Hedda Korsch, Pollock, Wittfogel with his wife Rose, Sorge, Eduard Ludwig Alexander, Konstantin Zetkin, Fritz Sternberg, Heinrich Süßkind, Käthe Weil, and **Fukumoto Kazuo** (1894-1983), who photographed it. **Buckmiller identified 19 of roughly 21 people visible** in the plates, so the usual "19 participants" is loose; Weil's memoirs recall about 25.
  - **Causality corrected.** "The conference's success is the direct occasion for founding the Institute" is **false**: the Institute was founded three months earlier, and de calls the week *das erste Theorieseminar* of it.
- **22 June 1924**: the Institute **officially opens** at **Viktoria-Allee**, now Senckenberganlage, **Carl Grünberg** from Vienna as founding director, declaring for the Marxist research method **[A: de]**.
- **Weil's role** **[A: en "Felix Weil"; de; marxists.org]**: patron and organizer, not a leading theorist. marxists.org calls him an orthodox Marxist who took Marxism for a science and wanted social and historical research on the workers' movement: a secondary summary, quoted as such in the journey file.

## 5. Weimar, 1927-1932

- **3 October 1927**: **Hermann Weil dies**, aged 59 **[A: en "Hermann Weil"]**.
- **After 1927**: part of the inheritance goes into **left-wing cultural initiatives in Berlin** **[A: de "Felix Weil"]**. **Gap:** no named recipient. **Flagged and not quoted:** the phrase calling Weil a "Vertrauensmann der Kommunistischen Internationale" is from the **Süddeutsche Zeitung review notice** on Perlentaucher.de, not publisher copy, and as filed it is a stitched fragment without a grammatical subject that cannot stand as a quotation. It is a reviewer's compression of Heufelder; the file states this in prose and carries **no quote** there.
- **1928**: **Grünberg suffers a stroke**; **Pollock** takes interim leadership **[A: de]**.
- **1929**: the marriage to Käthe Bachert ends **[A: de]**. **Place is [R]**, inferred from the couple's German residence.
- **1930 or 1931**: **Horkheimer** becomes director and professor of social philosophy and begins recruiting Adorno, Fromm, Marcuse. **Open contradiction:** 1930 (en) against 1931 (de). The file uses 1931 and prints the conflict.
- **Summer 1932**: a **branch office at the International Labour Organization, Geneva** **[A: de]**. **Resolved against the intake material**, which dated this 1933-1934: a year late, and it destroys the point of the move, which is that it preceded the seizure of power.

## 6. Confiscation and exile, 1933-1941

- **February 1933**: the **Société Internationale de Recherches Sociales**, Geneva, replaces the Frankfurt foundation. **13 March 1933**: the Institute is **closed**. **13 July 1933**: the **Gestapo confiscates and dissolves** the Frankfurt property under the law on the confiscation of communist assets. **[A, all three: de]**
- **Paris**: an *Außenstelle* in the **Centre de Documentation at the École normale supérieure** under **Paul Honigsheim**, the new publisher **Félix Alcan** also seated in Paris **[A: de]**. **Gap:** no year in the source, so "1933-1934" is a placement; Honigsheim is usually omitted and is restored.
- **1934**: most staff relocate to **New York**, affiliated with **Columbia** **[A: en "University of Frankfurt Institute for Social Research"]**. **Open contradiction:** en "Frankfurt School" gives 1935, de says "mid-1930s". **Removed as unsourced:** the received-sounding "the only research institution transplanted whole from Germany to the United States", and "Columbia provided housing".
- **1935**: Weil **transfers his personal estate to the Institute's foundation** **[A: de]**. **Flag:** the source names only *die Stiftung*, so "the Institute's American foundation" is an unsupported gloss, the successor foundation being **Geneva**-seated. The file places the stop at New York and says in `date_confidence` that the placement is an inference.
- **1939-1941**: the *Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung* appears as **Studies in Philosophy and Social Science** **[A: de]**. **Corrected:** a **1935** renaming for the American move does not survive checking, the ZfS having run under its German title from Alcan in Paris through 1939; the implied causal link is dropped.

## 7. Argentina again, and the American decades, 1920s-1951

- **[A: es "Félix Weil"]** Argentine research trips in the **1920s** (early labour-movement research) and the **1930s** (a hand in "la creación del impuesto a los Réditos"). **No exact years given.** **Corrected:** a reconstruction placing a trip at **c. 1943** "during the early Peronist period" is unsupported; no 1943 trip is sourced, the 1944 book does not require one, and the label is anachronistic, Perón not being president until 1946.
- **1944**: publishes **Argentine Riddle**, in Spanish **El enigma argentino** **[A: en and es]**. **Corrected in the campa at the style pass:** it read "the year before Perón takes power", which is wrong on either reckoning (Perón was already Secretary of Labour from October 1943 and was not elected president until **1946**); the campa now reads "two years before Perón is elected president".
- **1945**: settles permanently in **California** **[A: de]**. **Gap:** state only, no city; the marker sits on Los Angeles and says so.
- **By the 1950s**: **Horkheimer, Adorno and Pollock** return to West Germany, **Marcuse, Löwenthal and Kirchheimer** stay in the United States **[A: en "Frankfurt School"]**. Pollock is regularly dropped from that list and is restored; the source says "by the 1950s", not 1950.
- **14 November 1951**: the Institute **reopens** in a new building by **Hermann Mäckler and Alois Giefer**, Horkheimer again at its head **[A: de]**. Weil does not return to it.

## 8. The last years, 1963-1975

- **1963**: on his 65th birthday Frankfurt awards him its **Ehrenplakette** and a **bronze portrait relief** goes up in the Institute's entrance hall **[A: de]**.
- **1969-1973**: teaches **taxation and municipal budget questions** to USAF personnel at **Ramstein**, "im Range eines Majors" **[A: de]**.
- **1970 and 1973**: lectures in Frankfurt, once at **Iring Fetscher's** invitation and once at the Institute's **[A: de]**. Two corrections carried into the file: the German *beziehungsweise* **distributes** the invitations rather than giving both to Fetscher, and the lectures were "in Frankfurt", not specifically at the Institute. **Gap:** no record of what he said.
- **After 1973**: teaches **Geschichte der Luftfahrt** at **Dover Air Force Base**, Delaware **[A: de]**. The Heufelder passage about this employment was carried in the intake material **truncated mid-sentence and presented as complete**; the restored ending, used in the file, runs "...**und ist das passende Schlussbild am Ende eines widersprüchlichen Lebens**" (*Der argentinische Krösus*, p. 180). **Flag:** that sentence names the **Civil Air Patrol cadets** and **no base**; Dover comes from de separately, and the campa says so.
- **18 September 1975**: dies of a **heart attack in Dover, Delaware**, aged **77** **[A: en and de]**. Memoirs begun **1971**, refused by Fischer Verlag, parts at the **Institut für Stadtgeschichte**, Frankfurt **[A: de]**.
- **Gap, genuine:** **no burial place** is recorded in any source consulted, English, German or Spanish.

## 9. Interlocks

The brief proposed three. Two do not survive checking; the third is real but not yet written.

1. **Foucault and Deleuze: no interlock. [A as an absence]** Neither `michel_foucault.journey.json` nor `gilles_deleuze.journey.json` mentions Weil, the Institute, Horkheimer, Adorno or Frankfurt. No meeting, correspondence or citation links Weil to either man; the school's actual contact with French post-structuralism runs through **Habermas's** later debate with Foucault, a generation after Weil. Reported as a gap, and **no stop is built on it**. An intake entry carrying this finding as a geography stop with place "n/a" and live Frankfurt coordinates was malformed and is dropped.
2. **The Argentine-Jewish geography: shared ground, not relation.** `alberto_gerchunoff.journey.json` runs an Entre Ríos arc through the JCA colonies, 1889-1893, in the province of Weil's *unconfirmed* birthplace; `victoria_ocampo.journey.json` is a structural mirror, a patron bankrolling an institution (*Sur*, 1931) that becomes a gate for European thought into Argentina. Neither is a documented relation between persons, and the house rule licenses naming another traveler on neither shared geography nor structural parallel. **No atlas traveler is named in any campa here, and the interlock list returned is empty.** That is the honest result, not an oversight.
3. **The Frankfurt cluster is the real interlock, and it is queued rather than written.** QUEUE.md stages **Horkheimer, Marcuse, Benjamin, Adorno, Habermas**, and **Erich Fromm** as the bridge from `freud.journey.json` to that cluster. Each will have a direct personal relation to Weil: he paid their salaries, Horkheimer directed the institute he endowed, Pollock was his friend from Geraberg. **None exists yet**, and no journey file contains "Institute for Social Research", "Frankfurt School" or "critical theory". Weil enters as the first carrier of all three.

**Pins.** Inherited byte-identical: Buenos Aires **-34.6037 / -58.3816** (Gerchunoff, Ocampo), Paris **48.8566 / 2.3522** (Foucault, Deleuze), Frankfurt am Main **50.114 / 8.688** (Rothschild) for generic Frankfurt stops. Declined: Kaaba and Temple Mount, proposed for inheritance with no connection here.

**Coordinates corrected** at seven places, the worst being **Geraberg 50.7167 / 10.8458**, where three passes produced three different pairs 5 to 12 km off, and the Institute on the Senckenberganlage **50.119 / 8.653**, replacing a wrong pair reused across six intake stops. The others: Steinsfurt, the Goethe-Gymnasium, the Centre William Rappard, Ramstein Air Base rather than the town, and the Institut für Stadtgeschichte.
