# Enrico Fermi (1901-1954): research report

*2026-08-02. For `fermi.journey.json`: 9 segments, 44 stops, 3 quotes. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Calendar: gregorian.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source anecdote no reachable document confirms. Contradictions are printed and left standing. The scholarly hedge lives in each stop's `date_confidence`, never in campa prose. Where the pool's verifier flagged an error, the correction is followed and the superseded figure named.

## 1. Rome, 1901-1918

- Born 29 September 1901, Via Gaeta 19, Rome; father Alberto Fermi of the Railways Ministry, mother Ida de Gattis, schoolteacher **[A: Wikipedia "Enrico Fermi"; AHF/Nuclear Museum]**. Street coordinate for the birth, generic Rome for undifferentiated Roman events.
- Brother Giulio dies 1915 after a throat-abscess operation, standardly the turn toward physics **[A: Wikipedia]**. No month or day in any reachable source; the stop defaults to 1 January and says so.
- Campo de' Fiori book stalls, the self-teaching from cheap Latin treatises **[R: Segre, 1970, from family memory]**. Undated; dated 1916 for sequence only, and flagged.
- Classical diploma, Liceo Ginnasio Umberto I, 1918, a year skipped **[A: Wikipedia]**; plaque at Via Daniele Manin 72, now Liceo Classico "Pilo Albertelli" **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- *Excluded as outside the pool:* finishing a treatise before noticing it was in Latin; Adolfo Amidei's books and advice.

## 2. Pisa, Gottingen, Leiden, Florence, 1918-1926

- Scuola Normale entrance competition, first place, on an essay treating a vibrating rod by Fourier analysis. The theme was "Specific characteristics of Sounds," not the looser "theory of sound" **[A: AHF; Wikipedia]**. The examiner talking to him as an equal is **[A, single-source: AHF]**.
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** Exam sat November 1918. en.wikipedia puts entry in 1918, right after the July diploma; it.wikipedia says "entro nella Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa nel 1919." Dated to the examination both agree on.
- Doctorate, Pisa, July 1922, at twenty **[A: Wikipedia; Nobel note]**. **Conflation flagged:** the laurea rested on an experimental thesis on X-ray diffraction in curved crystals, while "Un teorema di calcolo delle probabilita ed alcune sue applicazioni" was the separate Scuola Normale diploma thesis. Wikipedia merges them, Segre and the journey separate them.
- Semester under Max Born at Gottingen **[A: Wikipedia; AHF]**. **Contradiction, one reading impossible:** pool chronology says 1923, pool geography 1922-1923, Wikipedia "In 1923-1924." 1922 is impossible, he was at Pisa until July. Dated 1923-01-01, all three printed. Feeling overshadowed there is **[A, single-source: AHF]**.
- Leiden with Ehrenfest on a Rockefeller fellowship, autumn 1924 **[A: Wikipedia; Nobel note]**.
- Florence with Rasetti, and the derivation of Fermi-Dirac statistics **[A: Wikipedia; AHF]**. **Two corrections:** the post runs January 1925 to late 1926, not 1924-1926; the institute was at Arcetri, not the city centre the pool pinned.
- **[R]** in campa, outside the pool: Arcetri's nearness to the villa where Galileo died under house arrest; Dirac's independent derivation.

## 3. Via Panisperna, 1926-1938

- Chair of theoretical physics at twenty-four, one of the first three in Italy; forms the boys of Via Panisperna, Amaldi, D'Agostino, Majorana, Pontecorvo, Rasetti, Segre **[A: Wikipedia "Enrico Fermi" and "Via Panisperna boys"]**.
- Nicknames: Fermi "Il Papa" for infallibility, Corbino "Padreterno," Majorana "Il Gran Inquisitore," Amaldi "Fanciulletto" **[A: Wikipedia]**. In campa, not in a `quote` field: a nickname is not verbatim source text.
- Volta centenary congress, Como, September 1927; Bohr reads the complementarity paper on 16 September; Fermi listed among the Rome participants, the congress having "boosted Enrico Fermi's reputation outside Italy" **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Venue corrected:** the pool titled this Villa Olmo, but the congress met at the Istituto Carducci, and the pool's coordinate is not Villa Olmo either. Kept as a generic Como pin, declared as such.
- Marriage to Laura Capon, 19 July 1928 **[A: Wikipedia; Laura Fermi, *Atoms in the Family*, 1954]**; Nella born January 1931, Giulio February 1936 **[A: Wikipedia]**, both folded into the marriage stop.
- Royal Academy of Italy, appointed by Mussolini 18 March 1929 **[A: Wikipedia]**; National Fascist Party card 27 April 1929, read as the condition of the seat **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Building corrected:** the Academy sat in the Villa Farnesina, not the Palazzo Corsini the pool named, which housed the Lincei the regime suppressed in its favour **[A: Wikipedia "Royal Academy of Italy"]**. **Date texture:** decreed 7 January 1926, list approved 13 March 1929, appointment 18 March, inauguration 28 October 1929. Title, doubled salary and feathered hat are **[R]**.
- Beta decay: tentative paper 1933, longer paper 1934 **[A: Wikipedia, verbatim]**. **Attribution corrected:** sole-authored, not "Fermi and collaborators" as the pool had it; neutrino is coined in conversation around 1932-33, before the paper. *Excluded as outside the pool:* the rejection by *Nature*.
- Neutron-induced radioactivity reported to *La Ricerca Scientifica*, 25 March 1934 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Slow neutrons, 22 October 1934: paraffin in the beam, silver a hundred times more radioactive **[A: Wikipedia; Nobel note; AHF]**. **Two pool errors corrected:** the chronology lens dated slow neutrons to 25 March, which belongs to neutron-induced radioactivity; and the geography lens set the discovery in the garden, when the paraffin work was indoors and the goldfish pond served that afternoon to repeat it with water.
- **Cut for the stop budget, both real:** the Michigan summer symposia of 1930, 1933, 1935 **[R: Michigan physics department accounts]**; the sixth Solvay Conference, October 1930 **[A: Wikipedia, attendee photograph]**, kept as one sentence in the Como campa.

## 4. Stockholm and the crossing, 1938-1939

- Nobel Prize 1938, for the new radioactive elements produced by neutron irradiation and the related discovery of nuclear reactions brought about by slow neutrons; ceremony 10 December **[A: Nobel archive; Wikipedia; AHF]**. The family does not return, the trip serving as the exit from the racial laws that threatened Laura Fermi as a Jew **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **[R]:** the handshake instead of the Roman salute, and the Italian press noticing.
- Southampton embarkation, December 1938 **[R: Segre 1970; Schwartz 2017, the pool stating that port and ship were not verified]**. No manifest consulted; dated 1938-12-24 for sequence. The Copenhagen stopover with Bohr is **[R]**, attributed in campa to "the biographies": the pool could pin no Bohr-Fermi meeting at the institute to a date, so no Copenhagen stop was made. Leaving the prize money abroad is **[R]**.
- Arrival in New York, 2 January 1939 **[A: AHF; Wikipedia]**. Bohr lands a fortnight later with the Meitner-Frisch interpretation **[A: `niels_bohr.journey.json`]**. The interlock lens says "within days"; two weeks is closer, and is what the campa says.

## 5. Columbia, 1939-1942

- First fission experiment in the United States, Pupin Hall basement, 25 January 1939 **[A: Wikipedia, whose wording is collective, "an experimental team including Fermi"]**. **Attribution corrected, and it matters:** the team was Dunning, Anderson, Booth, Fermi, Glasoe and Slack **[A: Wikipedia "John R. Dunning"]**, and Fermi was in Washington that night. Co-author, not operator.
- Fifth Washington Conference on Theoretical Physics, opening 26 January 1939, GWU with the Carnegie Institution **[A: Wikipedia "Nuclear fission"]**. **Gap flagged:** the interlock lens has Bohr and Fermi "jointly announce" fission here, but the article never names Bohr as announcer, saying only that "the news on nuclear fission was spread even further." The campa stages no announcement.
- Navy Department warning, 18 March 1939 **[A: Wikipedia]**. The Einstein-Szilard letter of 2 August 1939 **[A: `einstein.journey.json`]** is named in that campa; no Peconic stop was made, since Fermi was not there.
- Columbia graphite-uranium exponential piles, 1941 **[A: AHF]**. The boron-in-graphite problem and Szilard's part in it are **[R]**.
- **Cut for the stop budget:** the Leonia, New Jersey residence 1939-1942 **[R: Laura Fermi; address unverified]**.

## 6. Chicago Pile-1, 1942

- Move to the Metallurgical Laboratory, Chicago, 1942, Eckhart Hall offices **[A: Wikipedia; AHF]**.
- **Contradiction, majority followed, minority printed.** Construction began **16 November 1942** per both "Chicago Pile-1" ("Construction Start: November 16, 1942") and "Metallurgical Laboratory" ("began on November 16, 1942 ... with Herbert Anderson and Walter Zinn leading construction shifts"). The "Enrico Fermi" article alone says 6 November.
- Criticality 2 December 1942, 3:25 pm, about 4.5 minutes at roughly 0.5 watts, in a converted rackets court under the west stands **[A: Wikipedia; AHF]**.
- **[R]** in campa: forty-nine people present, Wigner's Chianti, the paper cups, the silence. Standard in every chronicle, absent from the pool.
- **Quote [R].** "The Italian navigator has landed in the New World," attributed to Compton telephoning Conant. No primary transcript, no passage located in *Atomic Quest*; the `quote_source` says so. Words about Fermi, not by him.

## 7. The bomb roads, 1943-1945

- X-10 Graphite Reactor critical at Oak Ridge, 4 November 1943, Fermi present with Compton **[A: Wikipedia "X-10 Graphite Reactor"; AHF]**. Reactor coordinate preferred over generic Oak Ridge.
- Naturalization, July 1944, at the earliest date the residence requirement allowed **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Honest gap:** no court or city is named in any source reached, so the stop carries the geographic centre of the United States (39.8283 / -98.5795) as a declared placeholder. The Mussolini sentence in that campa is **[R]**, context from outside the pool.
- Los Alamos, associate director, head of F Division. **Contradiction:** Wikipedia September 1944, AHF 16 July 1944. Dated 1944-09-01, both named.
- B Reactor first fuel slug. **Contradiction, one figure eliminated:** the Fermi article says only September 1944; the B Reactor article gives 13 September for loading and 26 September for criticality; AHF says 27 September, after criticality, so it cannot be the first slug. Dated 1944-09-13, all three printed. Whether the slug was Fermi's own hand is unconfirmed: Wikipedia credits him the design, AHF the act.
- Trinity, 16 July 1945 **[A: Fermi's own written report]**. **Two pool errors corrected:** place, since the "Alamogordo" coordinate lies north of ground zero in the Jornada del Muerto and Alamogordo itself is sixty miles away; and yield, since 18.6 kt is a stale figure kept in the Fermi article while the Trinity article now gives 24.8 plus or minus 2 kt. The campa names no number.
- **Quote discipline.** The pool flagged "I estimated to correspond to the blast..." as a subject-less mid-sentence cut; the journey quotes the full sentence, from "The shift was about 2 1/2 meters."
- Departure from Los Alamos, 31 December 1945 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the scientific-panel sentence in that campa is **[R]**.

## 8. Hyde Park, 1945-1954

- Charles H. Swift Distinguished Service Professor, Institute for Nuclear Studies, dated 1 July 1945 **[A: Wikipedia; AHF]**, though he is on the mesa until December.
- Argonne National Laboratory, the reorganised Met Lab, 1 July 1946, folded into the Institute stop; election to the National Academy of Sciences, 1945, cut for the stop budget **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Chicago doctoral students: Chamberlain, Lee, Yang, Garwin, Steinberger, Friedman, Rosenbluth **[A: the pool's correction to a lens that had garbled this into training "Chandrasekhar's and Teller's students"]**.
- **Tag upgraded, upgrade itself flagged.** The pool tagged the AEC General Advisory Committee service **[R]** because the AHF profile lacks the hydrogen-bomb detail. The verifier argues for **[A]**: membership 1946-1950 and the Fermi-Rabi minority annex to the 30 October 1949 report are documented history, and AHF's silence is a gap in one source. The journey treats it as attested. **Caveat in `date_confidence`:** both Wikipedia GAC URLs returned 404, so nothing fresh stands behind the annex's wording, and the campa paraphrases.
- "Where is everybody?", Los Alamos, summer 1950 **[R]**. No contemporaneous transcript; the fullest account is Eric Jones, LA-10311-MS (1985), compiling recollections of Teller, York and Konopinski thirty-five years later. The `quote_source` says so, and the campa ends on it.
- Villa Monastero, Varenna, July 1954 **[A/R mixed]**. **Contradiction:** the Italian Physical Society treats this as the school's founding session, but the conference centre was created in 1953, and Wikipedia confirms Fermi among the laureates who met there without documenting the 1954 lectures.
- Death of stomach cancer, Chicago, 28 November 1954, aged 53 **[A: Wikipedia and AHF agreeing independently]**. **[R]:** dying at home, the address 5327 South University Avenue, the stopwatch on the intravenous drip. **Claim withdrawn:** the pool called the house a preserved Chicago landmark, and it is not one. No Enrico Fermi House appears among Illinois National Historic Landmarks, and the Hyde Park article designates the Compton and Millikan houses instead. The campa states the negative.
- Burial, Oak Woods Cemetery, December 1954 **[A: Wikipedia "Oak Woods Cemetery"; Find a Grave]**. **Gap:** no section or plot number in the article, and the campa says so.

## 9. Afterlife

- Fermium **[A: Wikipedia "Fermium"]**. **Conflation corrected:** Ivy Mike is 1 November 1952, but element 100 was identified in the debris in December 1952 at Berkeley by Ghiorso's group. The naming, proposed while Fermi lived and official after his death, is verbatim. Pinned at Enewetak, source of the material.
- Institute renamed Enrico Fermi Institute for Nuclear Studies, 20 November 1955, shortened 1968 **[A]**.
- Enrico Fermi Award established 1956: $100,000, certificate, gold medal bearing his likeness **[A: Wikipedia "Enrico Fermi Award"]**; given to Oppenheimer by Johnson in the East Room, 2 December 1963 **[A: `robert_oppenheimer.journey.json`]**. **[R]:** Kennedy having chosen him, and dying ten days before the ceremony.
- Stagg Field stands demolished August 1957, Regenstein 1970, Mansueto 2011 **[A: Wikipedia "Chicago Pile-1"]**. **Mislabel corrected:** 1952 and 1967 are plaques, 18 February 1965 is the National Historic Landmark designation, not a third plaque.
- Henry Moore's *Nuclear Energy* dedicated 2 December 1967 over the buried rackets court **[A: same]**. The skull-and-mushroom-cloud reading is **[R]**.
- Via Panisperna plaque near the Via Milano corner; the building houses the Enrico Fermi Museum / CREF; Amelio's *I ragazzi di via Panisperna* (1989), first television, then feature **[A: Wikipedia "Via Panisperna boys"]**. **[R]:** Majorana's 1938 disappearance, Pontecorvo's 1950 defection.
- Namesakes: Fermilab, the Enrico Fermi Nuclear Generating Station, the Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope, fermions, the Fermi paradox **[A: Wikipedia, undated, cumulative]**. Folded into the Institute-renaming campa, the pool giving no coordinate. The lower-case unit, the fermi, is **[R]**.
