# Viola Farber (1931-1998), research report

*Founding member of the Merce Cunningham Dance Company; choreographer; director of the CNDC, Angers; chair of dance at Sarah Lawrence. Born Heidelberg, died Bronxville, New York.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single tertiary source · contradictions flagged, never resolved · unreachable sources named with the reason.

**The one-line problem with this subject.** Almost everything written about Farber traces back to one man. Jeff Slayton was her dancer, her husband, her stage partner for twenty-five years, her biographer, and the author of the LA Dance Chronicle essays (LADC below) carrying the bulk of the narrative. He is a first-hand witness and on many points the only witness. Where a claim rests on Slayton alone, this report says so.

## 1. Germany and the flight (1931-1938)

- Born **25 February 1931 in Heidelberg** **[A: LADC; Wikipedia]**, two independently fetched pages agreeing. Father **Eduard Farber**, chemist, Jewish; family fled Nazi persecution **[A: LADC]**. Slayton adds that Eduard's employer helped arrange the exit before emigration got harder; that has **no second source**.
- **Correction to the pool.** The geography lens sourced the parents' names and a "began dancing as a small child in Heidelberg" claim to the Black Mountain College Museum + Arts Center page. **That page was refetched and contains none of it**, carrying only a one-line description, the 1951 and 1953 summer sessions, the company dates and Sarah Lawrence from 1988. The mother's name **Dora Schmidt Farber** has no reachable source and is **dropped**.
- **1938: New Haven, then the Washington, D.C. area** **[A: LADC]**. Nothing about the crossing survives: no ship, no port of entry, no papers, no date beyond the year.

**Gap.** Eleven years in Washington (1938 to c.1949) produce one named fact, modern dance study with **Erika Thimey** **[A: LADC]**, whose studio address was not located. No school, no recital, no photograph, no account of a refugee childhood in the wartime capital.

## 2. The music major (c.1948-1951)

- **University of Illinois, Urbana-Champaign**, one year as a **music major**, first formal dance classes under **Margaret Erlanger** **[A: Wikipedia, cross-checked against LADC, same Illinois-then-Washington sequence]**.
- **Contradiction, unresolved.** The second school is **American University** per LADC and **George Washington** per the crawled dossier. Neither shows a completed degree. The journey file names both in the campa and pins the AU campus, the alternative that appears in a fetched source.
- **Black Mountain College.** The pool calls this a "transfer" and treats it as continuous enrollment. **That is wrong.** The BMC Museum + Arts Center states she attended the **summer sessions of 1951 and 1953**, two discrete summers, and Wikipedia's "by 1952" is vague tertiary phrasing, not a third source for residence. The journey file says "summer session" and never "transfer".
- **The photograph story is unsourced and has been cut.** The much-repeated detail that she came after seeing a photograph of Cunningham dancing with **Anneliese Widman** is attributed in the pool to the Rockburne oral history. **It is not in that interview**, read in full, and could not be sourced anywhere else. What *is* there **[A]**: the stone-house rooming, going into Cunningham's classes together, the family quartet, and Katherine Litz moving "sort of like a broken butterfly."

## 3. Cunningham, 1953-1965

- **21 August 1953, Black Mountain College, the first program of the Merce Cunningham Dance Company.** Farber dances **Banjo** with Carolyn Brown, Remy Charlip, Cunningham, Jo Anne Melsher and Paul Taylor; David Tudor plays the Gottschalk **[A: Cunningham Trust production record]**. She is a **founding member**, not a later recruit: the most load-bearing fact in the file.
- **July 1955, Jacob's Pillow:** Banjo, filmed, Farber with Marianne Preger, Charlip and Anita Dencks **[A: Jacob's Pillow Dance Interactive]**.
- **Roles originated:** Crises, Rune, Nocturnes **[A: LADC]**, which also notes that Wikipedia's "Paired Rune" is probably a conflation of *Paired* and *Rune*. Flagged, not resolved; no such title is used.
- **1959, Katherine Litz's *Dracula*,** Farber as the vampire, with Charles Weidman **[A: LADC; BMC Museum]**. No source names the venue, so the generic New York pin is used. Further ballet study with **Margaret Craske** and **Alfred Corvino** is **[R: Wikipedia, echoed by LADC, no primary record]**.
- **9-10 September 1963, Pocket Theatre, 100 Third Avenue:** one of the pianists in **John Cage's relay performance of Satie's *Vexations*** **[A]**. Three corrections. **(a) Venue:** the pool put the theatre on **East Third Street** at 40.7259 / -73.9857, about 900 m south of the real one; Wikipedia's *Vexations* article gives **100 Third Avenue**, and the journey file inherits the canonical pin **40.7328 / -73.986** byte for byte from `john_cage` and `erik_satie`. **(b)** The pool misquotes the error it is flagging: it says Wikipedia dates her appearance to 1953, but **Wikipedia says 1952**, so the gap against the documented relay is **eleven years, not ten**. **(c) The "only woman" detail is better supported than the pool tagged it:** the published roster of twelve (Cage, Tudor, Wolff, Corner, Farber, Wood, Cook, Cale, Del Tredici, Tenney, Klein, Rifkin) names exactly one woman. **Upgraded [R] to [A]**.
- **1964 world tour:** Paris in June and **Museum Event No. 1, Vienna, 24 June 1964** **[A, cross-checked against `merce_cunningham.journey.json`, which pins both]**.
- **1965: leaves the company** after thirteen years **[A: LADC; Wikipedia]**. **No source documents a quarrel, a firing or a scandal;** both describe a transition to independent choreography. The five following years are thinly documented.

## 4. Teaching, and the company of her own (1961-1980)

- **Adelphi University. Contradiction, unresolved.** LADC gives **1966**; Wikipedia gives **1959-1967**, beginning six years before she left Cunningham. 1966 orders the segment; both are named.
- **Cunningham studio, c.1961-1969** **[R: Wikipedia's chronology alone]**. The pool's pin approximated the **Westbeth** studio, which did not open until **1970**, after the claimed window; the 1960s studio was on West Fourteenth Street. Rather than pin a building that did not yet exist as a studio, the journey file uses the generic New York pin.
- **Bennington College, 1967-1968** **[R, downgraded from the pool's A]**. The pool cited the BMC Museum page; **it does not mention Bennington at all**. The range is Wikipedia's alone.
- **The Viola Farber Dance Company. Contradiction.** Wikipedia gives **1968**, LADC **1970**; 1970 orders the segment.
- **1971: prize at the Ninth International Dance Festival, Paris,** with Slayton **[A for the prize; R for edition and date, which the source omits]**.
- **1971: marries Jeff Slayton [A].** The pool's **Garden Room at Judson Church** is **unsourced**: the cited article says only "Viola and I were married from 1971 to 1980," and Judson appears nowhere in it. Venue dropped.
- **Separation. Contradiction.** LADC: "married 1971-1980, separated 1978." Other secondary accounts say 1980.
- **Brazos River (1976 production, 1977 preview and broadcast)** **[A: Rauschenberg Foundation performance history]**. Fort Worth Art Museum and KERA Channel 13; sets and costumes **Robert Rauschenberg**, score **David Tudor**, direction **Dan Parr**; previewed at **Leo Castelli Gallery, 28 March 1977** and at **Galerie Ileana Sonnabend, Paris**, cast of nine from her company. **Year conflict on the Foundation's own page:** it labels the production **1976** while preview and broadcast fall in **1977**; both reported. **The Sonnabend showing is undated by the source**, so the journey file's date is flagged as inference.
- **Rauschenberg also costumed *Dandelion* (1978) and *Tracks* (1980)** **[A, upgraded from the pool's R]**. The Foundation lists both with casts, and its "unconfirmed" flag attaches to **premiere date, venue and cast**, not to the costume attribution, which is the claim being made.

## 5. Angers (1981-1984)

- Appointed **director of the CNDC, Angers**, succeeding **Alwin Nikolais** **[A: LADC]**. Tenure **1981-1984** per the fr.wikipedia directors list, which places her between Nikolais (1978-1981) and **Michel Reilhac** (1984-1988); the pool's "1981-1983/84" is tightened on that basis.
- **Mathilde Monnier confirmed [A]:** LADC carries the caption "Viola Farber coaching Mathilde Monnier at the Centre National de Danse Contemporaine in Angers, France." **Angelin Preljocaj dropped:** the pool named him an Angers protege, but he does not appear in the cited article and no connection could be confirmed anywhere reachable.
- **The Arles story was inverted in the pool and is corrected here.** The pool has Farber and Slayton performing *Doublewalk* "essentially unrehearsed" after the scaffolding collapse. Slayton's actual account: the scaffolding collapsed **during rehearsal**, damaging the floor, whereupon the two of them **boycotted the performance** and the company danced without them. The Paris critics then praised their duet, calling them "some of the best dance artists that America had to offer," **for a performance they did not give.** Also dropped: that this was the French company's last performance, and the **Théâtre Antique** venue, a guess by the pool's own admission.
- **Departure terms are a gap.** Whether she left in 1983 or 1984, and why, is not established; the record that would settle it is behind a 403. The pool's claim of a premature departure for undocumented cause is **not asserted**.
- **The Place (London Contemporary Dance School), 1984** **[A: LADC, year only]**. The 1987 end of the London period is **inferred** from the Sarah Lawrence start.

**Coordinate corrections.** Six pool pins were wrong and were fixed: Jacob's Pillow (2.9 km off, in woods; now 42.2644 / -73.1181), Adelphi (950 m east of campus; now 40.720 / -73.652), Bennington (the *town*, 4.6 km from the college; now 42.9248 / -73.2367), Sonnabend (600 m west, near the Louvre; now 48.8546 / 2.3417), The Place (now 51.5272 / -0.1286), and the Cunningham studio (anachronistic, replaced by the city pin). Pocket Theatre and Black Mountain are **inherited** from the corpus, both pool lenses having been off.

## 6. Sarah Lawrence (1985/1987-1998)

- **The pool's 1987-versus-1988 "conflict" is not one.** The Sarah Lawrence magazine index settles it: "Fall/Winter 1987, New faculty member" and "Spring/Summer 1988, Appointed new dance department chair." Two events, split into two stops. **"Succeeding Bessie Schonberg" dropped**: not in the index, and Schonberg had left the faculty well before 1988.
- **Newfane Flea Market photographs, exhibited in the college library, Winter 1985** **[A: same index]**. **New oddity, not in the pool:** the exhibit is dated **two years before** the same index's new-faculty notice for her, and nothing reachable explains her connection to the college in 1985. Recorded as an open question in `date_confidence`.
- **Pam Tanowitz [A for the relation, R for any date].** Wikipedia confirms the Sarah Lawrence MFA and that she "was mentored by Viola Farber Slayton," with **no year**; the pool's placeholder **1990** is unsourced, sits awkwardly against Farber arriving only in 1987, and is **dropped**. The quotation **"Viola Farber changed my life"** is verbatim and real; the pool's URL 404s over an apostrophe in the slug, and the live page is dancereflections-vancleefarpels.com. Fjord Review corroborates: "I got the last two good years of Viola, who retaught me how to dance."
- **Ainsi de suite with Mathilde Monnier, Avignon / Paris / Brest, 1992** **[R]**. From LADC's chronology and the dossier; no program, cast, venue or review fetched. The **Brest** leg rests on the dossier alone and got no stop. **Tenure, 1995 [A: magazine index]**.
- **Return to performing with Ralph Lemon [A for the collaboration, R for the rest].** The index places it in the **Winter 1996** issue, with photographs and **no venue**. The pool's **Joyce Theater**, its **1995** date and its title **"Threestep (Shipwreck)"** are uncorroborated and dropped. **Last performance with Slayton, ADF, 1996 [A: LADC]**.
- **Officier de l'Ordre des Arts et des Lettres, recorded Fall 1998** **[A: magazine index]**, that is, **before** her death. The idea that the honour was posthumous looks like an artifact of the announcement being reprinted afterwards.

## 7. Death and afterlife

- **Cerebral hemorrhage the day after Thanksgiving 1998** (27 November), a second a week later, month-long coma with Slayton at the bedside, **death 24 December 1998, aged 67**, still chairing the department **[A]**. **Citation correction:** the pool sourced this to "Stories from the Inside," which **contains nothing about her death**; it is in "She Had a Great Sense of Humor, That Viola Farber."
- **Burial: an absence-of-evidence finding, not a fact.** No cemetery or interment record located; Find a Grave returned 403, the obituary could not be fetched, Wikipedia has no burial section. The journey file states a gap and does **not** say she has no grave.
- **The archive.** Her will left the personal archival material to Slayton; he reports finding **no copies of her work in her apartment**, and says what he has fits in **three small boxes** on a guest-bedroom floor in Long Beach, California, still unhoused **[A: LADC]**.
- **The Prickly Rose** (2006), Slayton's self-published biography, the only book-length life **[A that it exists]**, **not located or read this pass**. Its title comes from an anecdote Farber told him around 1995 about a critic who called her a prickly rose; **he could never identify the critic or the article** **[R, unverified at its own source]**.
- **Viola Farber Artists in Residence Program, Sarah Lawrence, created Summer 2001** **[A, upgraded from R and re-dated]**. The pool's 1998 date looks read off her death date, and its R tag came from a bad URL returning 404, not an absent program.
- **Viola Farber Award, Foundation for Contemporary Arts, created 2024** **[A: the Foundation's page]**: $45,000 annually to a dancer working in New York, endowed by **Pam Tanowitz** plus gala proceeds, honouring her **board service 1969-1988**. Recipients: **Hsiao-Jou Tang** (2024), **Shayla-Vie Jenkins** and **Jennifer Lafferty** (2025), **Maggie Cloud** (2026).
- **Restagings [A, partial]:** *Tendency* for Repertory Dance Theater, Salt Lake City, and the 1979 solo *Clearing* for Nancy Evans Dance Theatre; the pool's "Sunday Afternoon and Doublewalk discussed for Rome" is not in the retrieved text and is not carried. **Films** including *January* (1984) and *Brazos River* were screened at the Fort Worth Dance Festival **[A; the year is an open gap]**.

## Material moved out, and why

The rule applied: **a stop belongs to whoever was actually there.**

1. **Mexico City, the falling mirror. Cut.** Slayton says the mirror fell during rehearsal preparation, striking **Chase Robinson**, who needed emergency treatment for leg lacerations, and Slayton, unhurt. **Farber's presence is not stated.** As a stop it staged another dancer's injury on her journey.
2. **Spoleto, the reception at Menotti's. Cut.** The pool made this a Viola Farber Dance Company engagement; the source describes a reception for the **Merce Cunningham Dance Company**, relayed by Slayton, who was never a Cunningham dancer. Her presence is not asserted, so it was cut rather than relocated.
3. **San Francisco, the Janice Ross interview. Cut as a pin, kept as a reference.** The pool concedes San Francisco was the researcher's inference, not a stated location, and an admittedly invented location should not be a map pin. The title is verbatim in LADC, so it rides as a **`suggested_ref`**.
4. **Fort Worth screenings; the Salt Lake City and Nancy Evans restagings.** Posthumous, Farber not present. Carried as `sources` and `suggested_refs` on her own stops, not as scenes.
5. **Theater Piece No. 1, Cage's "first Happening". Dropped.** The event was **1952**; the BMC Museum records her attendance as the summers of **1951 and 1953**, and no participant list names her.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found requiring a ruling.** Every relation in this file is rooted in Farber's own life: she danced in Cunningham's company, played in Cage's Vexations relay, and thereby played Satie's score. No framework from a subject listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md` was applied to her, and nothing here required reaching backward or forward outside her life. She is not on the exceptions list and, on this pass, does not need to be.
