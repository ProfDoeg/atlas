# Mohammad Mosaddegh (1882-1967): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Prime minister of Iran 1951-1953, who nationalized the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company and was removed by a joint CIA/MI6 operation. Slug: `mohammad_mosaddegh`.*

**Evidence legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or secondary-literature consensus not run down to a document in this pass. Where sources disagree, both readings are printed and the disagreement is left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**On the pool.** The pool arrived with a verification pass applied, and that pass overturned sixteen first-gathering items. Those overturns are treated here as findings, not housekeeping: several had already crossed between lenses of this project. Each is annotated in place; §8 collects the coordinate fixes.

---

## 1. Origins and education, 1882-1914

- Born 16 June 1882 at the family estate of Ahmadabad, into a Qajar administrative family. Father **Mirza Hedayatollah**, a treasury official; mother **Malek Taj Khanom** (Najm al-Saltaneh), of Qajar descent. **[A: Wikipedia, "Mohammad Mosaddegh"]**
- The estate is **Ahmadabad-e Mosaddeq**, Nazarabad County, **Alborz Province**, 7.5 km southwest of Abyek, at **35.99361 N, 50.474722 E**, **west/northwest of Tehran**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Ahmadabad-e Mosaddeq"]** *Overturned:* the first pass called it "east of Tehran" and pinned 35.7 / 52.05, ~145 km off. It also attached him to an "Ashtiani family," unsupported by the cited article. Dropped.
- **1892**: father dies of cholera; the boy is raised by his mother. **[A]** Upbringing in a Tehran household of the bureaucratic caste, privately tutored; no address located, journey coordinates 35.687 / 51.42 an **estimate for central old Tehran**. **[R]**
- **1901**: marries **Zahra Emami** (Khadijeh), a Qajar princess, 1879-1965. **[A]** **1906**: elected to the Majlis from **Isfahan** at twenty-four; barred from the seat, the legal minimum age being thirty. **[A]**
- **1909**: begins study in Paris. *Name corrected:* the school in 1909 was the **Ecole libre des sciences politiques**; the Institut d'etudes politiques de Paris (Sciences Po) is its 1945 successor, and the first pass carried the anachronism. **[A on the date]** **1911**: returns to Iran ill after roughly two years, then resumes doctoral work at **Neuchatel**. **[A]**
- **June 1913**: doctorate in law from Neuchatel, on wills and testaments in Islamic law; by most accounts the first Iranian to hold a European law doctorate. **[A on the doctorate; the "first Iranian" claim is hedged in the source itself]**
- Teaches afterward at the **Tehran School of Political Science**. **[A]** *Overturned:* the first pass placed him there as a *student*, c. 1897-1901, but the school was **not founded until 1899** and the source supports only the teaching, post-1914. The journey file carries only the teaching.

## 2. Provinces and ministries, 1920-1925

- **1920**: Minister of Justice under **Hassan Pirnia**. **[A]** **1920-1921**: **Governor-general of Fars** at Shiraz; disputes with the British-officered **South Persia Rifles**. **[A on the office]** The **Arg-e Karim Khan** as his seat is inference from the customary residence of Fars governors. **[R]** *Overturned:* the first pass anchored the governorships at 1922, which covers only Azerbaijan.
- **21 Nov 1921 to 7 Jan 1922**: **Finance Minister** under **Ahmad Qavam**. **[A]** **17 Feb to 12 July 1922**: **Vali of Azerbaijan**, from Tabriz. **[A on office and dates]** Tabriz inferred as seat; no building attested. **[R]** **30 May to 23 Sept 1923**: **Foreign Minister** under Mostowfi ol-Mamalek; re-elected to the Majlis in June. **[A]**
- **1925**: votes **against** the bill elevating Reza Khan to the throne, on constitutional grounds; withdraws from politics for over a decade. **[A on the opposition and withdrawal; R on the October dating and the speech's content]**, which come from Abrahamian, not from a Majlis record. A quotation attached to this episode by mohammadmossadegh.com is **dated 1923 there while the vote is 1925**: **contradiction flagged, not resolved**, and carried into quote_source.

## 3. Silence and return, 1925-1949

- **1930s-1941**: withdrawal to Ahmadabad; farming; a reported expectation of arrest that never came. **[R: Abrahamian, Kinzer; specific dates unverified]** **1941**: Reza Shah forced to abdicate under Allied occupation; **Mohammad Reza Pahlavi** installed. **[A]** **1944**: re-elected to the Majlis. **[A]** **1947**: announces a retirement, after an electoral-reform bill fails, that proves temporary. **[A]**
- **15 October 1949**: leads a procession from Kakh Street to the **Marble Palace** and takes **bast** with nineteen others for **four days**; the Shah "gave in and promised fair and honest elections." **[A: Wikipedia, "National Front (Iran)"]** *Overturned twice:* the first pass claimed the sit-in forced "a partial recount," which the source does not say; and the palace coordinates were ~1.5 km off.
- **12 November 1949**: the **National Front (Jebhe-ye Melli)** is founded with nineteen other figures. **[A]** *Overturned:* the first pass dated it October and merged it with the bast. They are separate events three to four weeks apart; the journey file splits them. The Kakh Street address is approximate. **[R]**

## 4. Nationalization and premiership, 1951-1952

- **15 March 1951**: the Majlis nationalizes the **Anglo-Iranian Oil Company**. **[A]** **28 April 1951**: confirmed Prime Minister after a **79-12** confidence vote. **[A]** Whether the confirmation was at the palace or in the chamber is **not specified anywhere consulted**. **[gap]**
- **June 1951**: Iranian officials take control of the **Abadan refinery**; British technicians withdraw; output collapses under the boycott. **[A]** *Honest gap:* **no evidence was found that Mosaddegh ever travelled to Abadan.** The stop marks the event, not a footfall, and its date_confidence field says so. **July 1951**: breaks off AIOC negotiations. **[R; month-level]**
- **15 October 1951**: addresses the **UN Security Council**. **[A]** Venue given as **Lake Success**, its temporary Long Island home in 1951; exact meeting site not re-verified. He then meets **Truman** and **Acheson** in Washington: sympathy for Iranian sovereignty, no action against the boycott. **[R: Kinzer; Gasiorowski and Byrne]** The press describe him as frail, steadied or carried, near collapse. **[R]** **Contradiction left open:** accounts place the episodes at **different Washington venues**, and sources divide on whether the fragility was genuine or staged. A parallel tradition holds he fainted or wept at the 1953 tribunal; also **[R]**, unverified.
- **October 1951**: a **Cairo stopover** including **al-Azhar** is cited across secondary accounts but fixed to no verified itinerary. **[R]** **November 1951**: return to very large crowds, the height of his popularity. **[R; the Mehrabad arrival unverified]** **1952**: **Land Reform Act**, 20 percent of landlord revenues to development and worker welfare. **[A; year-level]**
- **1952**: the seventeenth Majlis elections are **halted partway through**. **[A: Wikipedia, "1952 Iranian legislative election"]** *Overturned, and materially.* The first pass had the reason backwards, saying he stopped the voting because opposition candidates were gaining in **urban** districts. The reverse is true: National Front candidates won **all twelve Tehran seats on doubled turnout**, and he halted the count once **79 of 136** deputies had been returned, a bare quorum, to keep the **rural, landlord-controlled** districts from seating a majority against him. The journey file states this plainly rather than softening it.
- **9-23 June and 22 July 1952**: sittings at the **Peace Palace**, The Hague, in *Anglo-Iranian Oil Co. (UK v. Iran)*; judgment of **22 July 1952** declines jurisdiction. Application 26 May 1951; interim measures 5 July 1951. **[A: ICJ case record, icj-cij.org/case/16]** *Overturned by a full year:* the first pass dated his appearance to June 1951, before the October 1951 UN trip. It came after. Its own clause "Iran prevails the following July" is coherent only on the 1952 date.
- **16 July 1952**: resigns after the Shah refuses him the **Ministry of War**. **[A]** **Ahmad Qavam** appointed. **[A]**
- **21 July 1952 (30 Tir)**: the **Si-ye Tir** uprising; the military withdraws from the streets; Mosaddegh reappointed the same day **with** the War Ministry. **[A: Wikipedia; Abrahamian; Katouzian]**

## 5. Operation Ajax, August 1953

- **Early 1953**: **Churchill** seeks US backing for a covert operation, citing Tudeh influence. **[A: Wikipedia, "1953 Iranian coup d'etat"]** *Refined:* the approach was to the **Eisenhower** administration, in office from January 1953; **Truman had opposed** the operation. The first pass omitted this, and the omission removes the causal hinge.
- **15 August**: Colonel **Nematollah Nassiri** and Imperial Guard units move to arrest the government; the army takes the **central telephone exchange**; Foreign Minister **Hossein Fatemi** and others are detained. **[A]** **16 August, early hours**: Nassiri serves the Shah's **firman**; Mosaddegh questions its authenticity and has him arrested. **[A]**
- The Shah's flight, **corrected on two points**: he was **not in Tehran** but at **Kelardasht**, informed at about 4 a.m. that the mission had failed; and he did not fly straight to Baghdad but went by small plane to **Ramsar**, then **Baghdad**, then **Rome**. **[A]**
- **16-19 August**: **Zahedi** in a CIA safehouse; the agency organizes and pays the crowds; **Kermit Roosevelt Jr.** runs TPAJAX with **MI6**; dead in the unrest roughly **200-300**. **19 August**: the government falls, **Zahedi** installed. **[A]**
- **20 August**: **Mosaddegh surrenders.** **[A on the date; R on the address]** *Overturned:* the first pass had him **arrested on the 19th**, an error that also contradicted this project's own geography lens. He went over the garden wall as the house was shelled and gave himself up the next morning; the journey file splits the two days. The **pajamas** detail is **[R]**: repeated everywhere, confirmed nowhere.
- **Nov-Dec 1953**: military tribunal; he conducts much of his own defence. **[A: Gasiorowski and Byrne; Kinzer]** The **Officers' Club** as venue is a common identification in the coup literature; the building and its coordinates are **an estimate**. **[R]** **21 December**: sentenced to **three years' solitary**. **[A]**
- **1953-1956**: served in a Tehran military prison. **Term [A]; building and coordinates [R]**. The first pass tagged this [A] while conceding in its own note that the location was a guess; a self-declared estimate cannot carry [A]. **1954**: National Front candidates suppressed at the polls. **[R]**

## 6. Ahmadabad, death, burial

- **1956-1967**: house arrest at Ahmadabad for the rest of his life. **[A; the 1956 month is not fixed]**
- **5 March 1967**: dies at **Najmieh Hospital, Tehran**, aged 84, brought from Ahmadabad for treatment. **[A]** *Overturned, and the pool's two lenses disagreed:* the afterlife lens had him dying **at Ahmadabad**, which is where he was held and buried, not where he died. Hospital coordinates (35.702 / 51.411) remain **an estimate**.
- He had asked for burial among the 30 Tir martyrs at **Ibn Babuyeh, Rey** (35.603233 / 51.4439); refused, to keep the funeral from becoming a demonstration. **[R: Persian Wikipedia]** Buried instead **inside his own house at Ahmadabad, under the dining-room floor**; the source is specific. **[A]**

## 7. Afterlife

- **1979**: Ahmadabad residents vote to name a high school after him; later renamed **Emam Khomeini High School** after Khomeini's denunciations. **[A]** The compound has fallen into **serious disrepair** under restrictions on the site. **[A; ongoing condition, no date given]**
- Memoirs circulate in Persian as **Khaterat va Ta'allomat-e Mosaddegh**. **[A: Persian Wikipedia]** A catalogued **Musaddiq's Memoirs**, English and Persian, **1988**. **[A: Open Library]** The record names **no editor, translator, or publisher**; the common attribution to **Homa Katouzian** could not be confirmed and is **omitted rather than asserted**. **[gap]** Streets named for him in **Cairo** (attributed to Nasser) and **Tehran**. **[R; no coordinates in source, city centres used]**
- **2013**: the United States formally acknowledges the CIA's role, in documents declassified under the Obama administration. **[A]**
- **31 August 2019**: **Coup 53** (dir. Taghi Amirani; Walter Murch co-writer and editor) premieres at **Telluride**, built on a rediscovered transcript in which MI6 officer **Norman Darbyshire** admits plotting Ajax jointly with the CIA; **Ralph Fiennes** voices the passages. It also draws on footage from the **1985** Granada series **End of Empire**. **[A]** **"Mosaddeghism"** is named as a current within Iranian social democracy and liberalism. **[R]**

## 9. Quotations: what is verbatim, and what is not

Ten were gathered; eight are placed at stops. Notes on the ones that needed work:

- **"Yes, my sin, my greater sin..."** Tribunal defence, **19 Dec 1953**, carried by mohammadmossadegh.com and en.wikiquote.org. **The Persian court record was not located.** [A to the carriers, not the record.]
- **"...the petroleum industry has contributed nothing to well-being of the people..."** UN Security Council, 15 Oct 1951. **Two corrections.** The first pass wrote "to **the** well-being"; the cited text has no article, and silently smoothing the grammar of a translated primary record is not permitted. Reverted. And the citation is the **single record S/PV.560**, not the range S/PV.559-560, whose body text **could not be retrieved** (see §12), so the wording is verified to Wikipedia's citation of the record, not to the record itself. Mid-sentence fragment, hence the leading ellipsis. **Flagged for a pass that reads S/PV.560 directly.**
- **"Our long years of negotiations... have yielded no results thus far..."** Speech of **21 June 1951**; the Wikipedia footnote resolves to **M. Fateh, Panjah Sal-e Naft-e Iran, p. 525**, cited accordingly. **The internal ellipsis is in the sourced text**; the first pass deleted it, presenting elided material as continuous speech. Restored.
- **"There is no political or moral yardstick..."** Refusal of ICJ jurisdiction, per wikiquote; **pleadings not retrieved**. **"The moral aspect of oil nationalization..."** Attributed, **no transcript and no date**. **"...one man is to be king, Prime Minister and magistrate!"** The site dates it **1923**; the vote is **1925**; **contradiction flagged, carried into quote_source**.
- **"A person's worth in society is dependent on how much one endures for the sake of the people."** These are **his mother's words, recounted by him**. The first-pass sourcing was **wrong**: mohammadmossadegh.com does not carry the sentence in quotation marks, only a paraphrase of her counsel that anyone in politics "should be ready to suffer all types of slander and insults." The wording is carried verbatim by **tmj.news, "The Overthrow of Mossadegh"** and **The Friday Times (27 Feb 2015)**. **Variant warning:** those two split on "the people" vs "his people," and both The Friday Times and a Medium reprint **misattribute it to Mosaddegh himself**. Attribution to his mother preserved; memoirs citation dropped; no translator claimed.
- **Not carried:** "I put my trust in the support of the Iranian people. That is all." (undated, too thin) and "The Iranian state prefers to take over the production of petroleum itself..."
