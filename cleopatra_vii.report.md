# Cleopatra VII Thea Philopator, research report
*2026-08-30. Slug `cleopatra_vii`. Alexandria c. 69 BC to Alexandria, 10 or 12 August 30 BC. Last ruling monarch of the Ptolemaic house.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition · **[gap]** a hole in
the record, or a source I could not reach. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**.

The difficulty here is not scarcity but distortion. The narrative record is Strabo, Plutarch, Dio
and Suetonius, writing sixty to two hundred and fifty years after her death, plus Josephus, hostile
and Herodian, plus Augustan poets writing for the man who beat her. The documentary record is
Egyptian: inscriptions, coins, dated papyri, reliefs. Where the two disagree the documents are safer,
and the journey file is weighted that way.

## 1. Birth and inheritance (69-52 BC)

- **[R]** Born early 69 BC, possibly late 70. *No ancient source records the birth*; the year is
  reconstructed from her attested age at accession and death (Wikipedia, 'Cleopatra'; Livius.org).
  Day unknown, defaulted to 01-01.
- **[A]** Father: Ptolemy XII Auletes, restored 55 BC by Aulus Gabinius on Roman loans. **[R]** That
  she was present aged about fourteen during that restoration is reported but unconfirmed by
  contemporary record, and is not staged as a stop.
- **[A, re-tagged]** 31 May 52 BC: an inscription at the Temple of Hathor, Dendera, makes her regent
  beside her father. The pool tagged this **[R]**; Wikipedia's 'Cleopatra' and 'Ptolemy XII Auletes'
  both state it flatly, so **re-tagged [A]**. **[gap]** Nothing says she went to Dendera; the stop is
  placed there because that is where the document is, and says so.
- **[A]** Her languages, and being the first Ptolemy to learn Egyptian: Plutarch, *Antony* 27,
  fetched and quoted. **A report about her written roughly a century and a half later**, not a
  document of her schooling. Flagged in the stop.

## 2. Sole rule, then expulsion (51-48 BC)

- **[A, corrected]** Ptolemy XII died **before 22 March 51 BC**, leaving the throne jointly to
  Cleopatra (about 18) and Ptolemy XIII (about 10), with the Roman people named executors. The pool
  gave "March 51"; Wikipedia gives only a terminus ante quem from the Buchis stela. The stop is dated
  03-01 to order the segment, and says so.
- **[A]** 22 March 51 BC: she voyages to Hermonthis near Thebes to install a new Buchis bull. Same
  stela, and it is what fixes her father's death.
- **[R] place, [A] substance.** 2 July 51 BC: a Faiyum dedication names her alone as Thea Philopator.
  Findspot uncertain and the relief may be recut, so the coordinate is the nome capital. **[A]** that
  by 29 August 51 BC documents list her as sole ruler.
- **[A]** c. 50 BC: she arrests the Gabiniani who killed Bibulus's two sons and sends them to him in
  chains; he refers the matter to Rome. Same year, she is forced to accept Ptolemy XIII as co-ruler
  under Pothinus, Achillas and Theodotus. **[A]** 49 BC: the pair supply Pompey with ships and
  troops. **[A]** 48 BC: driven out, she withdraws to the Thebaid, then to Roman Syria to raise a
  mercenary army, and camps outside Pelusium.
- **[R]** Ashkelon struck dated bronze in her name in these years: standard modern numismatics
  (Roller). **[gap]** No primary coin catalogue reached. Kept as the only geographical anchor for the
  exile year.
- **[A]** 28 September 48 BC: Pompey murdered near Pelusium by Ptolemy XIII's advisers. **She is in
  the region but no source places her at the killing**, and the file does not put her there.

## 3. Caesar (48-44 BC)

- **[A]** Early October 48 BC: Caesar arrives and undertakes to arbitrate the succession. She is
  smuggled in past the guards in bedding by Apollodorus the Sicilian; the rolled carpet is a later
  popularisation. **[gap]** The Penelope page for Plutarch's *Caesar* **truncated before chapter
  49**; the claim rests on the standard citation and the World History Encyclopedia summary, and the
  stop says so.
- **[A]** The Alexandrian War: Achillas besieges Caesar, Arsinoe IV is proclaimed rival queen,
  harbour fire destroys ships and book storage on the docks. **The accounts disagree on what the fire
  reached; none is by a witness.** Not resolved. **[gap]** *Bellum Alexandrinum* not fetched.
- **[A, corrected]** **13 January 47 BC**: Ptolemy XIII drowns crossing the Nile. The pool gave a
  loose "-0047-01"; Wikipedia gives the day and adds that **whether he was fleeing or negotiating is
  uncertain**. Flagged, not resolved. **[gap]** The battle site is not securely located, so the
  coordinate is an approximate delta point, explicitly not a landmark.
- **[A/R]** 23 June 47 BC: Caesarion born. **[A]** the birth and her public presentation of him as
  Caesar's; **[R]** the day, proposed from Egyptian evidence and not universally accepted; **[R]**
  paternity, disputed in Rome in her lifetime and not establishable.
- **[A]** Spring/early summer 47: Caesar leaves for Pontus, legions remaining. 46 BC: she travels to
  Rome with Ptolemy XIV, lodges in Caesar's gardens across the Tiber, and the Senate recognises the
  pair as friends and allies of the Roman people.
- **[A]** 26 September 46 BC: the gilded statue in the Temple of Venus Genetrix, "a controversial
  golden statue of Queen Cleopatra as the goddess Isis" (Wikipedia). **[A]** 15 March 44 BC: Caesar
  assassinated, the will adopting Octavian and providing nothing for her or Caesarion. **[gap]** Her
  lodging in those weeks and her departure date are unrecorded.

## 4. A child colleague, then Antony (44-41 BC)

- **[A, corrected]** Ptolemy XIV alive by inscription **26 July 44 BC**, dead by **August 44**. The
  pool gave only "-0044."
- **Misattribution flagged.** The pool credited the poisoning charge to Josephus. Wikipedia says only
  "It has been assumed but remains uncertain that Cleopatra poisoned her co-ruler, with aconite."
  Josephus (*Against Apion* 2.56-58) charges her with killing a brother, but **the aconite is modern
  conjecture, not Josephus**. Carried as an assumption, **[R]**, unresolved.
- **[A, corrected]** **2 September 44 BC**: Caesarion proclaimed co-ruler as Ptolemy XV Caesar, aged
  three. The pool gave only "-0044."
- **[R, re-dated]** Cyprus. The pool asserted **[A]** recovery in 43 BC. Wikipedia: "The exact date at
  which Cyprus was returned to her control is not known, although she had a governor there by 42 BC."
  **Re-tagged [R], re-dated "by 42 BC"**, and folded into the Antioch stop rather than given one.
- **[R]** Low Nile floods, famine, plague and a reported decree holding Upper Egyptian grain back
  from Alexandria, 44-41 BC: argued from papyri and ice-core climate work, **narrated by no ancient
  source**, and **[gap]** no primary decree text verified.
- **[A]** 41 BC: Antony summons her to Tarsus. The barge on the Cydnus is Plutarch, *Antony* 26,
  **fetched directly** and quoted in the stop.
- **[A]** At her request Antony has Arsinoe IV killed at the Artemision, where she was a suppliant.
  Broadly attested across the Josephus/Appian/Dio traditions. **[gap]** Legal mechanism unknown, no
  primary passage fetched.
- **[R]** Aradus: a pretender claiming to be the surviving Ptolemy XIII removed at her instigation.
  **[gap]** Probably Appian, *Civil Wars*, but the passage could not be located. Kept with the gap
  stated in the stop's own prose.
- **[A]** Winter 41/40: Antony winters at Alexandria; the Inimitable Livers material is Plutarch's,
  **[gap]** not refetched to the chapter. **[R]** The pearl in vinegar is Pliny, *NH* 9.119-121, a
  century later and uncorroborated: pearls dissolve in acetic acid, but not at the anecdote's speed
  without crushing or long soaking. Recorded as luxury anecdote inside the stop.

## 5. The eastern kingdom (40-33 BC)

- **[A]** 40 BC: twins Alexander Helios and Cleopatra Selene II born; Antony marries Octavia in Rome
  the same year. **[R]** That she did not contest the marriage is inference from silence. **[A]**
  Herod takes refuge at her court and declines a command; source is Josephus, **[gap]** not refetched.
- **[A]** 37 BC, Antioch: the twins acknowledged; grants of Phoenicia, Coele-Syria, Cilicia, Crete,
  Cyrenaica and the Jericho balsam. **[R]** Her personal visit to Judaea and meeting with Herod:
  Josephus only, hostile tradition, not independently confirmed. Tagged in the stop.
- **Contradiction inside the pool, resolved against it.** The chronology lens dated Antony's
  separation from Octavia to 37 BC, wrong by five years and contradicting the pool's own later entry.
  Wikipedia, 'Mark Antony' gives the marriage as 40-32 BC; 'War of Actium' has him marrying Cleopatra
  in 32 BC before divorcing Octavia. **The 37 BC entry is discarded**; the divorce appears at the
  Athens stop, 32 BC.
- **[A]** 36 BC: the Parthian disaster she financed; she meets the retreating army on the Syrian coast
  with supplies (Plutarch, *Antony* 51, **[gap]** not refetched). Ptolemy Philadelphus born the same
  year. **[R]** Double-dating, "year 16, also year 1": papyrological synthesis in the pool, **[gap]**
  not verified against a published papyrus, and reading it as a claim to a new era is interpretation,
  which the stop says.
- **[A]** Autumn 34 BC, the Donations: "gymnasium of Alexandria, autumn 34 BC... Antony as
  Dionysus-Osiris, Cleopatra as Isis-Aphrodite" (Wikipedia). She is proclaimed Queen of Kings,
  Caesarion King of Kings.
- **[A/R] P.Bingen 45** (Berlin P. 25239), a royal ordinance granting a Roman absentee landlord tax
  privileges, received at Alexandria **23 February 33 BC**, with one Greek word subscribed in a
  second hand. **[A]** the papyrus, its date and content. **[R]** van Minnen's attribution of the
  subscription to her own hand: Bagnall, Derow and Sarri question whether the word is in a different
  hand at all, and with no surviving signature of her name there is **no palaeographic control**.
  **Correction applied**: the pool's "ginesthoi" is not a real form. It is **γινέσθω**, *ginesthō*,
  "let it be done" (papyrus-stories.com, Cromwell on van Minnen).

## 6. Actium and the end (32-30 BC)

- **[A]** 32 BC: Antony divorces Octavia, triumviral powers lapse, senators and both consuls leave
  Rome for Antony, and war is declared against Cleopatra rather than Antony. Ephesus muster, Samos
  festival, Athens winter with honours voted her, all Plutarch. **[gap]** Chapters not refetched, and
  the Athenian honours reach us mainly through hostile sources.
- **[A, corrected]** **2 September 31 BC, Actium.** Coordinate corrected, see Pins below.
  **Characterisation corrected too**: Wikipedia puts her squadron in the rear, retreating "to the
  open sea without engaging," with Antony following because he believed "it was mere panic and all
  was lost." The pool's "broke through the line" is dropped, and plan or panic is **left open**.
- **[A, re-dated]** Artavasdes II beheaded, his head sent to Artavasdes I of Media Atropatene. The
  pool had "probably 30 BCE"; Wikipedia has him dying **in 31 BC**, with Cleopatra named as the
  agent. Caution still: the episode rests on **Cassius Dio alone**, with no second witness.
- **[R]** Ships hauled overland toward the Red Sea and burned by the Nabataeans, reportedly at
  Octavian's urging through Herod. **Her presence at the haul site is not stated in any source** and
  is not claimed.
- **[A]** 31 July 30 BC: Antony's last cavalry success; his fleet and cavalry defect the next
  morning. **[A]** 1 August: on a false report of her death Antony stabs himself and is hauled
  through an upper window into the sealed mausoleum. **[gap]** Plutarch 76-77 **not independently
  retrieved** this pass; flagged in the stop.
- **[A] Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Plutarch (*Antony* 83, Perseus, fetched) and Dio
  (51.12, LacusCurtius, fetched) give different exchanges for what may be the same interview with
  Octavian. Both are carried in the stop; neither is preferred.
- **[A] The central contradiction, how she died.** Wikipedia gives "10 or 12 August 30 BC,"
  Livius.org 12 August, the variation following from calendar conversion. Mechanism: **Strabo** asp
  bite *or* poisoned ointment; **Plutarch** the asp in the figs *and also* a hollow implement;
  **Dio** puncture marks and a needle; **Suetonius** the Psylli snake-charmers called in and failing.
  **No eyewitness account survives**, and Olympos's memoir, which Plutarch says he used, is lost. The
  disagreement is itself the finding; the stop states all four positions rather than choosing.
- **[A]** Burial beside Antony "in royal fashion" permitted by Octavian (Plutarch, Dio). **Neither
  says where.** **[gap]** Tomb never located; Wikipedia's infobox reads "Unlocated tomb (probably in
  Egypt)."
- **[A]** Caesarion sent up the Red Sea road, recalled through his tutor Rhodon, killed. The three
  children by Antony spared, taken to Rome, walked in the triumph. Egypt annexed as a province.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Cleopatra VII is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are licensed and none were
written. Per the standing instruction, relations not rooted in her own life are kept and flagged
rather than deleted. **All of the following are FORWARD**: the artifact did not exist when her clock
stopped in 30 BC. None is staged as a stop; where the relation is one-directional and citable it sits
as a `source` or `suggested_ref` on **her own** stop for that place or object.

- **Kathleen Martinez at Taposiris Magna**, 2002-2025: 27 tombs, ~300 coins (22 with her image),
  gold tongue amulets, a 1,305 m tunnel, a submerged harbour, no artefact naming either ruler. And
  **Franck Goddio's** underwater survey of the royal quarters and Antirhodos, 1996-2003. *No stops:
  the digging is theirs.* Both `suggested_ref` on the burial stop, with Wikipedia's "No definitive
  evidence of Cleopatra's actual burial has been confirmed." 
- **Shakespeare, *Antony and Cleopatra*** (c. 1606-07) via North's 1579 Plutarch. *Dropped*: a fact
  about Plutarch's English reception, not about her. Flagged in case the operator wants it.
- **"Cleopatra's Needles"**, obelisks of Thutmose III and Ramesses II moved to the Caesareum
  c. 13-12 BC, *after her death*; London 1878, New York 1881; the name a Western misattribution.
  Their arrival is stated as the **building's** later history in the final stop, whose confidence
  field says outright that it reaches past her clock; London and New York are `suggested_ref` only.
- **The "Berlin Cleopatra"**, Altes Museum, mid-1st century BC. *Not used.* The pool tagged it
  **[R]** after 404s on smb.museum; re-tagged **[A]** here, Wikipedia's 'Cleopatra' describing the
  object directly. Available as a `suggested_ref`.
- **Cleopatra Selene II** in Mauretania with Juba II, 25-5 BC, minting as "Queen Cleopatra"; the line
  ends with Ptolemy of Mauretania, executed 40 CE. *No stop, the daughter's own journey.*
  `suggested_ref` on the final stop.
- **"Cleopatra the Alchemist"** and the *Chrysopoeia*, a pseudonymous figure of Roman Egypt conflated
  with the queen. *Dropped.* It is **not her**. Noted so the conflation is on record.
- **Plutarch, Dio, Suetonius, Josephus, Pliny** as authors: each wrote substantively about her, a
  real one-directional relation. Named in prose where the evidentiary question is the point, which
  follows house practice in `julius_caesar.journey.json`, where Suetonius and Pliny are named inside
  campa text. **But `flavius_josephus.journey.json`, `plinio_el_viejo.journey.json` and
  `strabo.journey.json` also appear as slugs in stops' source apparatus, and under the block rule a
  `suggested_ref` is a hash pointing forward.** Kept and flagged rather than deleted; the operator
  should rule on stripping the slugs and leaving the names in prose only.

**Backward and contemporary relations, legal without exception, and used:** Alexander, founder of the
city and of the dynasty she ends, named once in the birth campa; `julius_caesar` and `cicero`

## Honest gaps

**Nothing survives in her own voice** except, arguably, one Greek word on P.Bingen 45, and that
attribution is disputed. Every quotation in the file is Plutarch's or Dio's rendering of her, in
translation, written between a century and a quarter and two and a half centuries later. Her mother,
her birthday, her tomb and the mechanism of her death are unknown, and are stated as unknown.

Beyond the texts listed as unreached under Sources: no primary coin catalogue for the Ashkelon
issues, no published papyrus for the double dating, no primary decree text for the famine-year grain
restriction.

**Cicero's letter numbering** follows attalus.org's own sequence in Book 15, not cross-checked
against Loeb or Shackleton Bailey. The pool's second Cicero item ("I hope it may be true about the
queen") is a **fragment**: Shuckburgh's full sentence is "I wish it had been true about Menedemus,
and I hope it may be true about the queen," and the pool's gloss states as settled something the
letter does not say. **That quote is not used**; only the Ammonius line, which stands whole.
