# Agustí Querol i Subirats (Agustín Querol Subirats), 1860-1909
*Research report for the Atlas of Journeys, compiled 2026-08-14 from the verified pool. Sculptor; the state's monument-maker in the decades Spain stops being an empire.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named on the line. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, tradition, or a brief's claim no source supports. Contradictions are flagged and not adjudicated, except where a dedicated article outranks a biography's passing mention; that rule is declared each time.

**Structural warning.** Nearly everything here reaches the file through **Wikipedia in three languages** (es, ca, en) plus **one 1910 monograph** (Rodolfo Gil). The three editions disagree on at least five dates, and the Spanish biography disagrees with the Spanish edition's own dedicated monument articles on at least four more. Coordinate corrections and unsourced pins are consolidated in section 10.

---

## 1. Tortosa: the baker's son, 1860 to c.1878

- Born **17 May 1860 in Tortosa** (Baix Ebre, Tarragona), son of a baker from Ulldecona. **[A: ca.wikipedia 'Agustí Querol i Subirats'; en.wikipedia 'Agustín Querol'. Both give the same date.]**
- First instruction from **Ramon Cerveto Bestraten**, founder of a Tortosa artistic dynasty. **[A: ca and en.wikipedia]** *Undated in every source:* no year, duration, or description. Placed at a boyhood default of 1874 with the hedge in `date_confidence`. **Gap.**
- Boyhood modelling in bread dough at the oven. **[A as Gil's report, R as fact]** An origin topos, but a contemporary biographer's; quoted in the journey as Gil's words rather than asserted.
- **Leaves the bakery for Barcelona at eighteen.** **[A for the age: en.wikipedia; R for the year]** Sources give the age, not the year; 1878 is arithmetic and is marked inferred.

## 2. Barcelona: the Llotja and the dissection room, c.1878 to 1884

Four facts, **all undated in every source consulted.** The journey defaults them across 1879-1880 and flags each.

- Apprenticeship with **Domènec (Domingo) Talarn** and with the **Vallmitjana brothers, Agapit and Venanci** **[A: es, ca and en.wikipedia, all three]**, and sculpture classes at the **Escola Provincial de Belles Arts** in the **Llotja de Mar** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. No years, no order between the studios, no duration.
- **Dissection and anatomy at the Hospital de la Santa Creu.** **[A: en.wikipedia, single source]** Neither es nor ca mentions it. Flagged as single-source.
- **'Cabeza de Dolorosa', 1881** **[A: en.wikipedia]** and a **clay statue of the soprano Elena Theodorini, 1883** **[A: ca.wikipedia]**. Years only. No source says whether the Theodorini survives or where it is. **Gap.**

## 3. The pension, Rome, the first medals, 1884 to 1890

- **1884: wins the competitive state pension to Rome** with **Eduardo Barrón**. **[A: es, ca, en]** Year only; no source says how long he stayed. The Roman stops pin to the **Real Academia de España** on the Janiculum; the texture of study there is period framing, not a documented itinerary. **[R for texture, A for institution]**
- **1885: bust of Eduard Toda i Güell** **[A: ca.wikipedia]**, assigned to the Roman period as attribution, not as documented place of execution; and **1886: 'Sagunto'**, now in the Prado **[A for existence, date, location: en.wikipedia; R for place of execution]**, since sources do not say whether it was modelled in Rome or Madrid. Both share one stop, with the ambiguity carried there.
- **1887: 'La Tradición' takes a first-class medal at the Exposición Nacional**, Madrid, and later enters the Prado **[A: es, ca, en]**. **1888: first prize at the Barcelona Universal Exposition** **[A: ca.wikipedia]**. **1889: 'San Félix y Santa Eulalia' for the cathedral facade** **[A: es, ca, en]**, where the campa's detail of Eulalia's martyrdom is Barcelona's canonical account of its co-patroness, not sourced from the Querol material **[R, register-level]**.
- **1890: Monumento a Casto Méndez Núñez, Vigo.** **[A: ca.wikipedia for the work; es.wikipedia works list for the city]** The pool's first pass called the city unspecified; the correction is right. Original siting within Vigo unconfirmed.

## 4. Madrid: the studio as a factory, 1890 to 1903

- **1890: moves from Rome to Madrid**, opens the studio at **paseo del Cisne** (today paseo de Eduardo Dato), employing dozens of assistants including **Lorenzo Coullaut Valera** and **Jacinto Higueras**. **[A: en.wikipedia, corroborated by es.wikipedia]** **Correction carried:** the pool dated the move "late 1890s"; it is 1890. **No house number in any source. Gap.**
- **1891 and 1895: gold medals at Munich. 1898: gold at Vienna.** **[A: es, ca, en]** **Limit, stated in each stop:** the medals are attested; **no source places Querol himself in Munich or Vienna**, only his sculpture. Stops of the work, not of the man.
- **1892 to 1895: vice-director of the Museo de Arte Moderno** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; from the same single source and uncorroborated, **Carrara marble dealing and the pseudonym 'El Plutarco del Pueblo'**.
- **1892-1903: sculpture for the front of the Biblioteca Nacional de España**, including the crowning figure of Spain. **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]** **Correction carried:** the pool's **"nineteen separate figures" is unsupported** by the BNE article, which does not enumerate. **Dropped.**
- **1896 or 1897: Monumento a José Elduayen, Vigo.** **[A for the work: es.wikipedia]** **Contradiction, unresolved:** the biography's narrative gives 1896, the same article's works list 1897. It stands in the **Jardines de Elduayen**. **1897: 'Moisès i les lleis' for the Palace of Justice, Barcelona** **[A: ca.wikipedia]**.
- **'La Gloria y los Pegasos'. Contradiction, three-way, unresolved:** 1897 (es biography, en.wikipedia); 1897-1902 (ca.wikipedia); **1905** (es.wikipedia's dedicated article). The stop is dated 1897 and prints the spread rather than applying the dedicated-article rule, since here that article disagrees with two editions by eight years. **Second correction:** the building is the **Palacio de Fomento**, so the pool's "Ministry of Agriculture" is anachronistic for 1897 and was dropped.

## 5. The overseas commissions

Querol's monuments crossed oceans; **Querol, so far as anything consulted shows, did not.** No source places him in Havana, Manila, Lima, Mexico City, Buenos Aires, Paraná or Guayaquil. A studio exporting allegory by ship is the central fact of the late career.

- **Monumento a los Bomberos, Necrópolis de Colón, Havana.** Designed **1892**, inaugurated **1897**. **[A: en and ca.wikipedia]**
- **Monumento a Legazpi y Urdaneta, Intramuros, Manila.** **[A: es.wikipedia, dedicated article]** **Two corrections carried.** (1) **Date: 1893**, not a vague "1890s". (2) The claim that **American sculptors completed it is wrong**: Querol made the bronzes in Barcelona (architect **Luis María Cabello**), and in 1901 the American provost marshal **Dwight F. Davis** merely *authorised its erection*.
- **Monumento a Francisco Bolognesi, Lima.** Designed **1902**, inaugurated **6 November 1905**. **[A: en and ca.wikipedia; es.wikipedia 'Plaza Bolognesi']** **Manuel González Prada** among others called the bowed-head pose undignified; in **1954** it was replaced by a Bolognesi by **Artemio Ocaña**, the original going to the Real Felipe, Callao.
- **Pegasos for the Palacio de Bellas Artes, Mexico City.** **[A for the attribution: es and en.wikipedia; R for the date]** No source dates the groups; "c.1904-1909" comes from the building's construction years, and the stop declares the placeholder. **Gap.** The **Columna de los Próceres, Guayaquil**, completed posthumously in **1920** **[A: ca.wikipedia]**, gets **no stop**: no coordinate in the pool, none invented, so it is carried in a campa.

## 6. Marble, decorations, the Cortes, 1900 to 1909

- **Monumento a Claudio Moyano, Madrid. Contradiction, resolved with the rule stated:** the biography gives 1899, the **dedicated** article inauguration **11 November 1900**, and the pool's chronology lens uses 1900. Dedicated article followed. The **Gran Cruz de la Orden Civil de Alfonso XII, 1902** **[A: es, ca, en]** is attested to the year only, with no site. **Gap, flagged.**
- **Monumento a Francisco de Quevedo, Madrid, 1902. The pool's [R] gap is closed here.** The dedicated article gives inauguration **5 June 1902** in the **Plaza de Alonso Martínez**, and a **move to the Glorieta de Quevedo in the 1960s**. Retagged **[A]**; the stop uses the present coordinate and narrates both sitings.
- **Monumento a los mártires de la religión y de la patria, Zaragoza.** **[A: es.wikipedia dedicated article; Querol with the architect Ricardo Magdalena]** **Site gap closed:** it stands in the **Plaza de España**. **Contradiction, unresolved:** **1904** (es biography) against **1906** (ca.wikipedia and the dedicated article); the stop uses 1906 and prints both.
- **Mausoleum of Antonio Cánovas del Castillo, Panteón de Hombres Ilustres, Madrid. Contradiction, four-way, partially resolved:** **1903** (ca.wikipedia); **1906** (en.wikipedia **and** the dedicated article, which names Querol); **1908** (es biography). The stop uses **1906** on the dedicated-article rule and prints all four. The pool framed this as a two-way conflict; the spread is wider.
- **Monumento a Casilda Iturrizar, Bilbao, 1906.** **[A: en.wikipedia]** **Naming discrepancy, flagged:** es.wikipedia's works list calls it 'Monumento a la viuda de Epalza', the same woman under another designation.
- **Monument to Frederic Soler, 'Serafí Pitarra', Barcelona.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** With **Pere Falqués**, by subscription, inaugurated **26 December 1906**, mayor **Domènec Sanllehy** presiding. **Correction carried:** the claim that **Soler's son Ernest gave the inaugural address is unsupported** by the dedicated article, which credits Sanllehy. **Dropped**, and noted so it is not reintroduced.
- **Deputy to the Cortes for Roquetes**, Conservative, **April 1907 to April 1910**, cut short by death. **[A: en and ca.wikipedia]** No building named. **Gap.**
- **Monumento a los Sitios, Zaragoza, inaugurated 28 October 1908**, Alfonso XIII presiding, with the **Ballarín** foundry late, plaster stand-ins at the ceremony and the bronzes arriving **eight days before the Pilar festivities of 1909**. **[A: es.wikipedia]** The best-documented episode in the pool, and the anchor for the letter to Moret. The **Monumento a Segismundo Moret, Cádiz, 1909** **[A: en.wikipedia]** gets **no stop**: no coordinate in the pool, none invented.

## 7. The Argentine gift, death, afterlife

- **Monumento a la Carta Magna y las Cuatro Regiones Argentinas ('Monumento de los Españoles')**, Palermo, Buenos Aires: a gift from the Spanish community for the centenary of the May Revolution, with Liberty crowning it and bronzes for the four regions. **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]** **Contradiction, unresolved:** en.wikipedia dates the design **1900**, the pool's afterlife lens **1909-1910**; the **foundation stone** is laid **1910** under President **José Figueroa Alcorta** **[A]**. The stop uses 1900 and prints the conflict.
- **Dies 14 December 1909 in Madrid**, aged 49. **[A: es, ca, en]** **No source gives an address of death**; the stop pins to the studio and says so. **Gap.**
- **Burial. Contradiction, flagged not resolved:** en.wikipedia names the **Cementerio de San Isidro**; es.wikipedia's article on the **Cementerio de San Justo** lists Querol **by name** in its **Patio de San Justo**. The more specific, naming source is followed and the conflict printed. **The date of interment is not attested**; the death date stands in. **Open gap:** nothing describes the tomb's design, condition, or survival.
- **Posthumous completions. [A: en and es.wikipedia]** The **Panteón Guirao** is finished **1911**; the **Urquiza** at Paraná is inaugurated **1920, completed by Mariano Benlliure**. In Buenos Aires **Cipriano Folgueras** takes over and dies **1911**, a **1914 storm** breaks the marble Liberty's arm, the steamer **Príncipe de Asturias** sinks off Brazil in **1916** with marble and bronze aboard, and a **Carrara strike** and bureaucratic delay push inauguration to **13 March 1927**. **Restoration 2019-2023; bronze stolen May 2023.** The **wreck has no stop**: the pool locates it only as "off the coast of Brazil".
- **'El caso Querol'.** **[A as an attributed judgment: en and es.wikipedia, citing Juan Antonio Gaya Nuño]** The phrase and the 'psicosis colectiva' sentence are quoted **at second hand, not from the original 'Arte del siglo XIX'**, and are **undated** in everything consulted; the stop carries a **placeholder year of 1966**, declared as such. The weakest date in the file. The **Prado** holds several works. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

## 8. The two refuted attributions

The brief that opened this subject credited Querol with two monuments he did not make. Both are **affirmatively refuted**, not merely uncorroborated, and neither is a stop.

1. **Monument to Alfonso XII, Retiro Park, Madrid. [R, refuted]** Won in an open 1902 competition by the architect **José Grases Riera** and executed by a team of about twenty sculptors (Benlliure, Clarà, Inurria, Blay, Marinas, Trilles, Vallmitjana, Estany and others), inaugurated **22 July 1922**, thirteen years after Querol's death. **Querol appears nowhere in the es or en articles on it.**
2. **Monumento de los Dos Congresos, Plaza del Congreso, Buenos Aires. [R, refuted]** By the Belgian sculptors **Jules Lagae** and **Louis-Charles D'Huicque**, inaugurated **9 July 1914**, per es.wikipedia's 'Plaza del Congreso'. No Querol involvement.

**Consequence.** The error looks like a conflation of the genuine Palermo monument with a different Buenos Aires one, and of the **decoration** Querol did receive with a **monument** to the king of that name. The journey keeps the decoration and drops both monuments. Downstream, the interlock pool built connections on the premise that the Retiro monument was Querol's signature work; that premise is false.

## 9. Interlocks carried and rejected

Fifteen candidates returned. **Fourteen rejected, one carried.**

**Rejected as shared geography or date only:** Mario Palanti, Pedro Subercaseaux and Alberto Gerchunoff (all present at Argentina's 1910 centenary, as the monument was, with no relation to Querol), and the QUEUE.md Buenos Aires mural and monument cluster (Ron, Fasoli, Segatori, Carbone, Puzzovio, Ezcurra). Co-location is what the map already shows a viewer. **Rejected as resting on the refuted Retiro attribution:** Sergei Diaghilev and Albert Einstein, linked only through Alfonso XIII to a monument Querol did not make. **Rejected as name-collisions, recorded as a standing caution:** San Martín, Perón and Che Guevara, all attaching to **Retiro, Buenos Aires**, unrelated to Madrid's Retiro Park.

**Carried: Moses** (`moses.journey.json`), named in the campa for the 1897 **Moisès i les lleis** at the Palau de Justícia de Barcelona. A genuine one-directional relation: a sculptor taking a scriptural figure as subject and setting him, tablets and all, over a courthouse. Querol treats Moses; the reverse could never hold; that is enough. No other atlas traveller is named in any campa.

## 10. Honest gaps, and the coordinate record

1. **No archival source was reached.** Three Wikipedia editions and one digitised 1910 monograph. No Spanish state archive, no Prado catalogue entry, no Diccionario Biográfico Español, no municipal subscription records.
2. **The Barcelona years are wholly undated.** Four facts, no year in any source.
3. **No source describes the studio practice from the inside.** The division of labour between Querol and dozens of assistants, on works signed with his name and shipped to four continents, is undocumented. For a sculptor whose interest is the *industrial* production of monuments, this is the largest hole.
4. **The personal life is absent.** No marriage, no children, no household, no friendships outside the professional.
5. **The travel question is unanswered.** Beyond Tortosa, Barcelona, Rome and Madrid, no source places him physically anywhere. Whether he ever crossed the Atlantic is simply not stated, and every foreign stop says so.
6. **Three attested items have no stop** for want of a sourced coordinate: the Cádiz **Moret** (1909), the Guayaquil **Columna de los Próceres** (1920) and the **1916 wreck of the Príncipe de Asturias**, all narrated inside campas instead. **Gaya Nuño's date is a placeholder** (section 7).
7. **Ten pool coordinates were wrong and were corrected**; the values used are in the journey file. Worst first: **Cementerio de San Justo**, about 5 km off; **Plaza Bolognesi**, where the pool gave Lima's Plaza Mayor; **Necrópolis de Colón**, 1.5 km; **Frederic Soler**, about 1 km and the wrong end of the Rambla; then **Parque de Doña Casilda Iturrizar** (640 m), **paseo de Eduardo Dato** (530 m), **Biblioteca Nacional** (380 m) and **Panteón de Hombres Ilustres** (250 m); plus **Claudio Moyano** and **Quevedo**, central-Madrid placeholders replaced with the real sites.
8. **Pins that remain unsourced or approximate, each flagged in its stop:** the Palau de Justícia, the Palacio de Fomento, the Plaza de España at Zaragoza and the inherited Jardines de Elduayen pin are the compiler's placements; also approximate are Méndez Núñez, the Museo de Arte Moderno, the Gran Cruz civic pin, Roquetes, the Panteón Guirao and the Parque Urquiza. And the pool carries **two coordinates for the same Buenos Aires monument**, 340 m apart; the journey uses **-34.575, -58.4153** across all three Palermo stops.

## Sources

**Reached and used**

- **es.wikipedia, 'Agustín Querol'** (biography and works list): spine of the chronology, and the source most often in conflict with the dedicated articles.
- **ca.wikipedia, 'Agustí Querol i Subirats'**: sole source for the Theodorini statue, the Toda bust, the Moisès i les lleis, the Guayaquil column. **en.wikipedia, 'Agustín Querol'**: sole source for the anatomy training, the marble dealing, the pseudonym, the Panteón Guirao, the Cádiz Moret; and its **'Monument to the Spanish'** for the delay narrative, the 1914 storm, the 1916 wreck, the restoration and the 2023 theft.
- **Eighteen dedicated es.wikipedia articles** on the individual monuments, cemeteries, parks and streets, each named at its point of use above and each correcting the biography on at least one date, site or attribution.
- **Rodolfo Gil, *Agustín Querol*, Monografías de Arte Universal vol. V, Madrid, Sáenz de Jubera Hermanos, 1910**: the one contemporary monograph, published within a year of the death. Source of the letter to Moret (p. 7), the private remark on politics (p. 16), the credo (p. 5), the epigraphs from Arsenio Alexandre, Delahaye and León Thevenin (p. 3), the boyhood-dough passage, and the Argentine monument's guiding idea (p. 41). **Register warning:** Gil is a eulogist, used for the sculptor's own words and contemporary judgment, never as a check on dates. **Two quotation corrections carried:** the 'confraternidad' line is a truncated fragment of Gil's own prose and is quoted lowercase as printed; and a passage the pool gave as one quotation is two sentences from pages 14 and 15 spliced by an ellipsis, so it was **not used**.
- **Nicasio Mariscal, in *Obras Completas de Eusebio Blasco*, tomo IV** (es.wikisource.org): the period usage pairing Querol with Benlliure.

**Reached and negative:** the two articles used to refute the brief's attributions, plus the Querol article grepped for "Retiro" and "Congresos" with zero hits (section 8).

**Not reached, named with the reason:** **Gaya Nuño's *Arte del siglo XIX***, quoted at second hand from es.wikipedia with wording, page and year unverified; **Spanish state and municipal archives** and any **Diccionario Biográfico Español** entry, not attempted within this pool; **a Prado catalogue entry** for Sagunto or La Tradición, so the holdings are attested only through Wikipedia; and **any Peruvian, Cuban, Philippine, Mexican, Ecuadorian or Argentine local source** on how the monuments were received in their own cities, which leaves the whole overseas half of this career resting on Spanish and English Wikipedia.
