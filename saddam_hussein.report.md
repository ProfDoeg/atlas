# Saddam Hussein al-Tikriti (1937-2006)

*2026-08-11. Slug `saddam_hussein`; companion `saddam_hussein.journey.json`*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or state hagiography · *gap* unresolved.

**On the evidence base.** He spent thirty-five years inside a state that manufactured his biography as an instrument of rule. Sources fall into three unequal piles: contemporaneous documents (FBI interview reports, court transcripts, UN and HRW findings), Western biographies written from exile testimony (Aburish, Coughlin, Karsh and Rautsi), and Iraqi state accounts that are propaganda by design. The famous youth episodes, the swim across the Tigris, the 1968 tank, the leg wound on Rashid Street, belong to the third pile and are **[R]** however often repeated as fact. This report carries evidence and corrections; the narrative lives in the journey file.

---

## 1. Al-Awja and the uncle's house (1937-1957)

Born 28 April 1937 in al-Awja, 13 km south of Tikrit **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**. His father Hussein Abd al-Majid had died or disappeared before or shortly after the birth **[R: the sources say "died or disappeared" and cannot decide]**. His mother Subha named him Saddam, "one who confronts" **[A]**; she reportedly attempted suicide after the disappearance and the death of an older son **[R: unattributed accounts]**, and remarried Ibrahim al-Hassan, remembered in exile accounts as abusive **[R: Aburish (2000); Coughlin (2002); relatives dispute the severity]**. Primary school at Tikrit, then Karkh secondary school in Baghdad **[R: Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]**; joined the Ba'ath Party at about twenty, 1957, later abandoning law study for party work **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Britannica]**.

**CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** Where did the ten-year-old go when he ran? Wikipedia puts Khairallah Talfah's house in **Baghdad** **[A as to the article's claim]**; Aburish and Coughlin put the first refuge at **Tikrit**, Baghdad later **[R]**. The pool carried both as [A], which cannot be right. The journey file pins Baghdad and names the Tikrit version inside the campa rather than deleting it.

**Marriage, five-year error.** He married his first cousin Sajida Khairallah Talfah. The pool dated this 1963 in two lenses, tying it to the return from Cairo. It was **1958**, arranged in childhood, preceding the 1959 flight **[A: Wikipedia, "Sajida Talfah"]**. Children: Uday (1964), Qusay (1966), Raghad (1968), Rana (1969), Hala (1972) **[A]**; the pool's "Rina" misrenders Rana.

## 2. Rashid Street and the Cairo years (1958-1963)

The monarchy fell to Qasim's coup on 14 July 1958; the Ba'ath turned against him over his tilt toward the Iraqi Communist Party **[A: Britannica]**. On 7 October 1959 a Ba'athist ambush on Rashid Street wounded Qasim and failed. Saddam took part **[A: Wikipedia; Coughlin]**, but his precise role and his own wound are **[R]**: Karsh and Rautsi treat the heroic framing as mythologization built after 1968, and the sources cannot separate the gunman's acts from the president's later account of them.

He fled overland through Tikrit toward Syria, reaching Cairo by early 1960 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the Damascus leg is **[R: Coughlin]**, duration unclear. The swim across the Tigris with a knife in his teeth is **[R: Ba'athist hagiography, treated by historians as embellishment]** and appears in the journey file as a story the state told, not an event. In Cairo he enrolled at law school, never finished, and lived under Egyptian protection among exiled Iraqi Ba'athists **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Wikipedia]**. The pool's "near Ain Shams University" is an approximation, not a documented address.

## 3. Prison, party, palace (1963-1972)

Qasim was overthrown and killed in the Ramadan Revolution of February 1963; Saddam returned within weeks into the party's regional command **[A: Wikipedia]** and worked in its internal security and interrogation apparatus **[A: Karsh and Rautsi (1991)]**. Arrested October 1964 **[A: Wikipedia]**; accounts place part of the imprisonment near Qasr al-Nihayah, the former royal Palace of the End **[R: Aburish; Coughlin; others put the main confinement elsewhere in Baghdad, and the sources do not agree]**. Escaped 1966 **[A as to the escape; R as to every detail, which state media inflated]**. On 17 July 1968 the Ba'ath took power from Abd al-Rahman Arif: al-Bakr president, Saddam deputy and deputy chairman of the RCC; the Iraq Petroleum Company was nationalized 1 June 1972 **[A: Britannica]**. Through the 1970s he built the security services from RCC offices and used them against rivals **[R as to the characterization]**.

**Title error corrected.** The pool called al-Bakr "deputy secretary-general." The titles are reversed: al-Bakr was Secretary-General of the Iraqi Regional Command, Saddam the deputy, appointed 1966 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**TAG DOWNGRADE.** The pool carried as **[A]** the claim that Saddam personally commanded the tank that stormed the palace in 1968. It is incompatible with the same sources' bloodless coup, Arif having been put on a plane to London; it rests on his own later account, and the pool's own note concedes it is self-mythologized. **[R]** here and in the journey file.

## 4. The deputy becomes the president (1975-1979)

Nothing in this phase is contested in the reachable sources: the Algiers Agreement of 6 March 1975 settling the Shatt al-Arab boundary against Iran's withdrawal of support from the Kurdish rebels, a concession he later called humiliating; the Osirak negotiations in France that September, Chirac receiving him; promotion to general in 1976; al-Bakr's resignation on 16 July 1979, leaving him the presidency, the RCC chairmanship, and the armed forces; and six days later the filmed Al-Khuld Hall assembly, 68 named as conspirators, 22 executed, surviving party members compelled onto the firing squads. **[A throughout: Britannica; Wikipedia; Coughlin; the purge film is documented in BBC and CNN retrospectives]**

## 5. The eight years' war (1980-1988)

Uncontested and carried in the journey file: the invasion of 22 September 1980, eight years, roughly a million dead, no territorial change, ceasefire 20 August 1988 presented domestically as victory; chemical weapons from 1983; the Victory Arch opened 8 August 1989 **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**. Dujail, 148 killed, 8 July 1982, the first trial charge **[A: Wikipedia, "Trial of Saddam Hussein"]**.

**16 March 1988, Halabja.** Chemical bombardment during Anfal, 3,200 to 5,000 civilians killed, the deadliest chemical attack on a civilian population in modern history; Ali Hassan al-Majid directed Anfal **[A: Britannica; HRW, *Genocide in Iraq* (1993)]**. Whether Saddam personally ordered Halabja, as against general Anfal authorization, **remains debated and is left open**.

**Babylon.** He rebuilt it through the 1980s with bricks stamped with his own name, casting himself as heir to Nebuchadnezzar II **[A: documented history; `nabucodonosor.journey.json` attests Nebuchadnezzar's Babylon but says nothing of Saddam, so the identification is external]**.

**Helmet claim corrected.** The pool said the Victory Arch's bronze helmets were cast from melted-down *Iraqi* helmets. They are 5,000 actual helmets which Saddam claimed had belonged to *Iranian* dead; it was the sword blades that were partly cast from Iraqi guns and tanks. The hands were cast from his own forearms **[A: Wikipedia, "Victory Arch"]**.

## 6. Kuwait and the Mother of All Battles (1990-1991)

Uncontested and carried in the journey file: the invasion of Kuwait, 2 August 1990; Desert Storm from 17 January 1991; the Amiriyah shelter, 13 February, 400-plus civilians dead, Iraq calling it atrocity and the US a strike on a believed command bunker; the Safwan ceasefire, 3 March, Saddam absent; the March-April risings crushed, the Imam Ali Shrine precinct at Najaf shelled **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia, "Invasion of Kuwait", "Amiriyah shelter bombing", "Gulf War", "1991 uprisings in Iraq"]**.

**Date correction.** "Allahu Akbar" was added to the flag on 13 or 14 January 1991 **[A: Wikipedia, "Flag of Iraq"]**. The pool filed it under 1994 alongside the Faith Campaign; both halves were misdated, which disposes of the implied causal sequence: the takbir precedes the campaign by two years.

## 7. The sanctions years (1991-2002)

The Faith Campaign, al-Hamla al-Imaniya, was announced mid-June 1993, not 1994 **[A: Wikipedia, "Faith Campaign"]**; the economy collapsed through the decade under UN sanctions **[A: Britannica]**. Dozens of palaces were built meanwhile, Al-Faw Palace at Radwaniyah later serving as MNF-I headquarters at Camp Victory **[A: Wikipedia, "Al Faw Palace"]**.

**28 April 1998.** Groundbreaking of the Umm al-Ma'arik / Umm al-Qura Mosque on his birthday, the building completed 2001; its minarets measure 37 m for his birth year and 43 m for the 43 days of the 1991 war **[A: Wikipedia]**. The pool dated the *inauguration* to his birthday, which is wrong.

**2000, Zabibah and the King.** Published in Iraq 2000, English translation 2004, adapted as a 20-part television series and a National Theatre musical **[A: Wikipedia]**. The pool's "serialized on Iraqi state radio" is unsupported and dropped. Authorship disputed, the CIA initially judging it not his work **[R]**.

**QUOTE ATTRIBUTION FAILURE, flagged.** The pool supplied two lines as Saddam's own speech, sourced to "FBI Interview Session 1, quoted by Hussein himself": "Long live Zabibah. Long live the people." and "I'm a great leader. You must obey me. Not only that, you must love me." Both fail the same way. The report states Hussein **was quoted** these passages, meaning Piro read them **to** him from the novel; they are the lines of fictional deputies and a fictional king, the first truncated (the full shout ends "Long live the army"). The document records that Saddam **rejected** the second, replying that "fear will not make a ruler and will not make people love a ruler." Publishing either as his creed would invert the record. **Neither appears in the journey file.**

## 8. The fall (2003)

Uncontested and carried in the journey file: the invasion of 20 March citing alleged WMD and the fall of Baghdad on 9 April; the toppling of the Firdos Square statue that day by a US Marine M88 recovery vehicle; the killing of Uday, Qusay, and Qusay's son Mustafa at Mosul on 22 July, identities confirmed by dental records; the capture in a spider hole near Ad-Dawr on 13 December without resistance, and the flight to Baghdad International Airport that night **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia, "Uday Hussein", "Operation Red Dawn", "Capture of Saddam Hussein"]**.

**Firdos Square, contested.** Later analysis found the crowd smaller than the television framing implied **[A: Los Angeles Times, 2004]**; staged versus spontaneous **remains contested and is not adjudicated here**.

**Four pool errors corrected.** He was a fugitive eight months, not nine, Baghdad having fallen 9 April; those months are **[R]** as to sequence, only partly documented. Ad-Dawr is **not his hometown**, as the pool called it; al-Awja is. The examination footage was broadcast 14 December, but the flight was the 13th. And the pool names two capturing forces, Task Force 20 with the 101st Airborne for Mosul against Task Force 121 with 1st BCT, 4th Infantry Division for Red Dawn; different operations two months apart, so not necessarily in conflict, but nothing reconciles them and neither enters the journey file.

## 9. Camp Cropper to al-Awja (2004-2015)

Held at Camp Cropper for most of the period to and through trial **[A: Wikipedia, "Camp Cropper"]**, though the same source notes he was kept outside the main complex, isolated from other high-value detainees; the journey file hedges rather than flattens this. Interviewed there from 7 February 2004 by SSA George L. Piro, the reports declassified as National Security Archive EBB No. 279 **[A]**. Seven verbatim Session 1 lines are safe to use, the journey file taking "Currently, the only political parties existing in Iraq are the ones with the weapons." Two further lines attributed to that session are not his; see §7.

**The trial.** He first appeared before an investigating judge on 1 July 2004, challenging the tribunal's legitimacy and calling himself still president of Iraq; the Dujail trial opened 19 October 2005; the verdict of 5 November 2006 convicted him of crimes against humanity and sentenced him to hang, upheld on appeal 26 December **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**THREE QUOTE CORRECTIONS.** (1) The pool gave "This is all theater. The real criminal is Bush." sourced to an "English-language court record" of the arraignment. No transcript was released; CNN (1 July 2004) quotes only **"This is all a theater"**, the Bush element being the reporter's paraphrase, and the two-sentence form traces to a 2005 opinion column. The journey file quotes the attested words only. (2) The pool's rendering of the 19 October 2005 refusal ran two sentences together with a spurious "because" and a mangled article; that is Wikipedia's paraphrase and does not match the BBC source Wikipedia cites: "Neither do I recognise the body that has designated and authorised you, nor the aggression. All that is built on a false basis is false." **[A: BBC]**. (3) The pool's verdict shout contains "Ummah," a word found in no traced source. The NYT (Semple, 5 November 2006) prints "Long live the people! Long live the Arab nation! Down with the spies!" with "God is great!" chanted separately after; the BBC excerpt renders the moment differently again ("Long live the nation. Down with the traitors..."). **The two are left in contradiction**; the journey file uses the NYT wording and names it.

Also from the proceedings, undated within 2005: "I have been beaten on every place of my body, and the signs are all over my body." **[A as a courtroom statement; the allegation is not adjudicated]**. *Gap:* no session date in the pool.

**30 December 2006, Camp Justice.** Hanged at a former military intelligence facility in Kadhimiya on the first morning of Eid al-Adha; a cellphone video of guards taunting him leaked and circulated **[A: Reuters, AP, Al Jazeera; Wikipedia]**. Gallows speech: "I have saved you from destitution and misery and destroyed your enemies, the Persians and Americans." **[A: translated video and eyewitness accounts]**. A second line, "No, I am a militant and I have no fear for myself...", is **[R]**: reported in secondary press, not verified against a primary transcript here, and so not used as a quote field.

**31 December 2006, burial.** Buried before dawn at al-Awja, under 24 hours after execution, in a tent near the plot holding Uday and Qusay **[A: Wikipedia, "Al-Awja"; Reuters, BBC]**, which became an informal shrine for tribal loyalists and Ba'athists **[A: Reuters and Guardian features, 2007-2013]**. A story that the real body was never buried there circulated before 2015 **[R: unverified local tradition, no official source]**.

**March-April 2015, the tomb destroyed. CONTRADICTION, not resolved.** Wikipedia's "Al-Awja" states the tomb was demolished **by ISIS** during the Second Battle of Tikrit, Shia Popular Mobilization fighters afterwards marking the site with their insignia, including Qasem Soleimani's, once Iraqi forces retook the village. Contemporaneous Western press (Independent, Newsweek, regional Arabic media, late March 2015) reported **PMF** burning the mausoleum. The journey file tags this stop **[R]**, dates it March-April 2015 rather than the pool's precise 31 March, and names both versions without choosing.

***Gap, unresolved.*** Whether the tomb was rebuilt, the remains relocated, or the site left in ruins was not established by any reachable source, and no stop in the journey file asserts a present-day state.

## Appendix: coordinates

Eleven pins were corrected against Wikipedia's article on the place, the pool's figures being wrong by between 600 m and 22 km: al-Awja (three wrong pairs, the first Tikrit itself), the Amiriyah shelter, Safwan, the Imam Ali Shrine, Al-Faw Palace (two, one the airport), Umm al-Qura Mosque, Firdos Square (two), Ad-Dawr, the Red Dawn capture site (two), Camp Cropper, and Camp Justice (the pool's pair sits across the Tigris from Kadhimiya). The corrected values are in the journey file. Two further pins are used **unverified**. The Republican Palace at 33.3243 / 44.366 is the pool's own figure; standard references place it about 3 km away near 33.303 / 44.390, and no authoritative pair was established here. Dujail at 33.85 / 44.2333 is a gazetteer figure filling a *gap*, the pool having supplied no coordinates for the town at all.
