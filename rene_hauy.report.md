# René Just Haüy (1743-1822): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-07. Queued in `QUEUE.md` as "French mineralogist, founder of crystallography". Companion: `rene_hauy.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim the corroborating source does not carry · *gap*, looked for and not found.

Thin biography, thinner geography. Both Wikipedias are dense with dates and silent on rooms: no prison named, no ordination church named, no address for the cabinet where the founding crystal broke. The primary text is reachable, though: the 1784 *Essai* was read directly in the archive.org Wellcome scan, so the quotations are the strongest evidence here and the coordinates the weakest. Several pool coordinates were wrong by 300 m to 7 km.

## 1. Picardy, 1743-1762

**Born 28 February 1743, Saint-Just-en-Chaussée, Oise, to a family of weavers. [A: en and fr Wikipedia agree.]** Commune point per fr.wikipedia: **49.5067, 2.4317**. Pool value 49.5044, 2.4469 sits ~1.1 km east in farmland; **corrected**. The geographic pass gave 49.5085, 2.4328 for the family-house site (now a stele), used for the tonsure and monument stops.

**Baptism: *gap*.** No source names church, officiant or day, and the present Church of Saint-Just is nineteenth-century, so not the fabric he was baptized in **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The journey's baptism stop is dated 1743-03-01, a placed day-after-birth, **[R]**.

**1762, tonsured under the Premonstratensians. [A for year and order: fr.wikipedia.]** Place **[R]**: no abbey named. The pool tagged this [A] while reusing the birthplace coordinate; the geographic pass tagged the same event [R]. **[R] is correct for the location.**

**Younger brother Valentin Haüy (1745-1822)**, founder of the first school for the blind **[A]**: the most productive interlock here (§8).

## 3. The broken spar and the theory, 1781-1788

**The founding anecdote. [R].** Four divergences, none resolved:

1. **Date.** fr.wikipedia 1781; en.wikipedia "1783 or later". The journey uses 1781 and carries the disagreement.
2. **Agency.** The 1911 Britannica has Haüy "happening to let fall a specimen of calcareous spar belonging to a friend". Haüy himself, twice, says the crystal had detached from a group by chance and was found broken: "qui s'étoit détaché par hazard d'un groupe, se trouvoit cassé obliquement" (1784), and in 1801 that Defrance gave it to him "just after it had broken off from a group". Both carried; the Britannica line is quoted at the 1863 statue stop, [R].
3. **Tag inconsistency in the pool.** Chronology and afterlife passes tagged it [R], the geographic pass [A]. **[R] is correct.**
4. **Owner of the cabinet.** "Jacques de France de Croisset" in the pool, "the citizen Defrance" in Haüy's 1801 text. Probably one man, but no source says so, and nothing is merged.

**12 February 1783, elected to the Académie royale des sciences as *adjoint botaniste*. [A: en.wikipedia for the day; fr.wikipedia for the year and the rank *associé-botaniste*.]** The pool's 48.8534, 2.3376 is ~800 m south across the Seine, matching no institution; fr.wikipedia states the Academy sat in the Louvre, **48.8606, 2.3376**. **Corrected**, as at three further pool entries carrying the same bad point (1788, 1793, 1795).

**1784, *Essai d'une théorie sur la structure des crystaux*. [A: en and fr Wikipedia.]** Read directly at archive.org, `b28762411_0002`. Doctrine attested from the text itself: the primitive form, the *molécules constituantes* (later *intégrantes*), the law of decrements.

**Quotation discipline.** Six passages verified word for word against the OCR. Two problems in the pool's presentation are carried as caveats in `quote_source` rather than silently fixed. (1) The pool claimed its only change was long-s to s; it had also modernized verb morphology throughout (*avois/avais*, *étoit/était*) and *hazard/hasard*. For the two passages verified letter by letter, the journey prints the 1784 forms with long-s modernized only, and says so. (2) **Page numbers are unreliable**: *molécules constituantes* reads at p. 49, not p. 50; the *décroissemens* passage at p. 64 by one OCR read and p. 66 by another against the claimed p. 65; the Daubenton thanks at pp. 37-38, not 38-39. No page cite here is final without a check against the printed folio. That *décroissemens* sentence also sits inside a worked example, so quoting it bare overstates it as a general law.

**1785, pyroelectricity in calamine, later tourmaline; 1787, *Exposition raisonnée de la théorie de l'électricité et du magnétisme*; 1788, associate of the Academy in natural history and mineralogy; 1792, parallelepipeds identified as primitive forms. [A: en.wikipedia for all four.]** The first two are one stop; the last gets none, the *Essai* having stated the doctrine in 1784.

## 4. The Revolution, 1792-1795

**10 August 1792, arrested as a *prêtre réfractaire*. [A: en.wikipedia gives the day, tied to the fall of the Tuileries; fr.wikipedia the month only.]** *Gap*: **the prison is named by neither source.** The journey says so rather than guessing.

**September 1792, released before the massacres, reportedly through Étienne Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire. [R, downgraded from the pool's [A].]** fr.wikipedia names Geoffroy and specifies he was Haüy's student ("son élève"); en.wikipedia corroborates the timing without naming anyone; **en.wikipedia's article on Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire does not narrate the rescue at all**. A rescue story the rescuer's biography omits is tradition, not attestation.

**4 January 1793, tasked with Lavoisier to determine the density of water. [A: en.wikipedia.] 8 August 1793, the academies abolished. [A: en.wikipedia.] 11 September 1793, appointed to the Temporary Commission of Weights and Measures. [A: en.wikipedia for the day, fr.wikipedia for the year.]** The last two are one stop, both at the Louvre.

**8 May 1794, Lavoisier guillotined; Haüy stays on as secretary. [A: en.wikipedia.]** Pool coordinate 48.8666, 2.3419 is ~1.5 km east, near the Palais-Royal. The execution was on the Place de la Révolution, today the Place de la Concorde, **48.8656, 2.3212** (en.wikipedia names Lavoisier among those executed there). **Corrected.**

**7 April 1795, the metric system formally established. [A: en.wikipedia.]**

## 5. The mineralogical establishment, 1794-1809

**12 July 1794, decree reorganizing the École des Mines and establishing a Cabinet of Mineralogy; October 1794, Haüy first curator. [A: en.wikipedia.] Contradiction, unresolved:** fr.wikipedia dates his conservatorship and chair of crystallography to **1795**. Both carried, neither picked. The **1795 first lectures on the law of symmetry** are [R] (en.wikipedia, undated), folded into the same stop.

**Coordinate wrong twice over.** The pool's 48.8493, 2.3336 for 1794 is neither the modern site (60 boulevard Saint-Michel, 48.845, 2.339) nor the 1794 one, fr.wikipedia dating the move to the hôtel de Vendôme to the ordinance of **6 December 1816**. For 1794 the seat is the **Hôtel des Monnaies, Quai de Conti, 48.8567, 2.34**; the boulevard point (48.846, 2.3399) serves the commemoration stop.

**Professor of physics, École normale de l'an III. [A, framing corrected.]** The pool called it "the École normale supérieure of Year III", conflating two foundations, and treated 9 November 1794 as a teaching date. fr.wikipedia gives the run as 20 January to 19 May 1795, lectures in an amphitheatre of the Muséum, cohort ~1,500. The journey dates the stop to the opening.

**22 August 1795, the Institut National created; Haüy a member from the start. [A: en.wikipedia for the day, fr.wikipedia for the year.]** First installed in the Louvre **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**1800 vs 1802 at the Muséum. Contradiction flagged; the reconciliation is not asserted as sourced.** fr.wikipedia: teaching from 1800. en.wikipedia: professor from 1802. **Daubenton died 31 December 1799; Dolomieu, his successor, was imprisoned in Italy until March 1801 and died 28 November 1801 [A: fr.wikipedia]**, so Haüy could have taught from 1800 in Dolomieu's absence and been appointed in 1802, both dates right about different things. **No source says this**; the journey carries 1800 with the contradiction.

**1801, *Traité de minéralogie*. [A.]** The four-versus-five volume clash is **not real**: four text volumes plus a separately bound atlas, counted as four by en.wikipedia and five by fr, whose "1801-1823" span covers the atlas and later issues.

**5 April 1802, honorary canon of Notre-Dame by Napoleon [A: en.wikipedia day, fr.wikipedia year]; 28 November 1803, Légion d'Honneur [A: en.wikipedia], folded into the 1815 stop; 1805, the Institut moves to the Collège des Quatre-Nations, Quai de Conti [A: fr.wikipedia].**

**1807, haüyne named for him by Tønnes Christian Bruun-Neergaard. [A: en and fr Wikipedia.]** The pool placed this at 41.9028, 12.4964, "Vesuvius area": that is central Rome, neither Vesuvius nor the type locality, which en.wikipedia (Haüyne) gives as **Lake Nemi, Alban Hills, Latium**, ~41.67, 12.70. **Corrected on place-string and coordinate.**

**1808, teaching at the École normale supérieure. [R, downgraded from [A]; excluded from the journey.]** fr.wikipedia lists 1808 for Haüy, but its article on the school shows Napoleon's decree of 17 March 1808 created the *pensionnat normal* and **the school opened only in 1810**, in the former Collège du Plessis, rue Saint-Jacques (~48.8472, 2.3437). 1808 is a decree year, the pool coordinate 48.8446, 2.3407 matches no candidate site, and **no source shows Haüy teaching there**. Kept out rather than pinned to a building he may never have entered.

**1809, chair of mineralogy created for him at the Faculté des Sciences. [A: fr.wikipedia.]** Pool 48.8462, 2.3444 is ~270 m south of the Sorbonne; journey uses **48.848, 2.3437**.

## 6. Restoration, death, burial, 1814-1822

**Deprived of appointments, left poor. [A that it happened; [R] on detail.]** 1911 Britannica: "after 1814 he was deprived of his appointments by the government of the Restoration. His latter days were consequently clouded by poverty." **The anchor is 1814, not the pool's 1815**: the first Restoration is April 1814. **Corrected.** The geographic pass overreached by naming the *Muséum professorship* as the loss; the Britannica names no post, and he was still lodged at the Muséum in 1822 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Restated as a general loss of appointments and income, [R].

**1815, officer of the Légion d'Honneur during the Hundred Days; the law of symmetry published in full. [A: en.wikipedia for both.]**

**1817, honorary member of the "New York Academy of Sciences". [R.]** Anachronistic name: en.wikipedia dates the founding to 29 January 1817 **as the Lyceum of Natural History**, and that article does not list Haüy. The claim rests on the Haüy article alone; phrased in the journey as the Lyceum.

**1822, *Traité de cristallographie*, 2 vols with atlas. [A.]** The pool called it a second edition of a book of that title. **It is not**: it is the recast second edition of the *Traité de minéralogie*. **Corrected.**

**19 March 1822, Valentin dies in the shared Muséum lodging. [A: fr.wikipedia.]** **1 June 1822, Haüy dies at 79 after a fall that fractured his femur. [A: en and fr Wikipedia converge, both flagging 3 June as a common error.]** The error has a pedigree: **Cuvier's 1823 eulogy gave 3 June and it stood until Alfred Lacroix corrected it in 1944 [A: en.wikipedia, corroborated by fr]**. The 1911 Britannica still prints 3 June, which is why the geographic pass logged an EN/FR contradiction that is really a resolved error with a named corrector. *Gap*: exact address not given; 48.845, 2.356 is a placeholder.

**3 June 1822, buried at Père-Lachaise, division 60, chemin Gosselin, in his brother's grave. [A: fr.wikipedia; appl-lachaise.net.]** Coordinate **48.8605, 2.3921**, division-level, preferred over the pool's 48.8619, 2.3934.

## 7. Afterlife
