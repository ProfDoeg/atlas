# Charles Rennie Mackintosh (1868-1928): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-16 for `charles_rennie_mackintosh.journey.json`. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or claim carried by the general biographical literature without a source reachable in this pass. Coordinate corrections are recorded inline; contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.*

---

## 1. Townhead and the evening classes (1868-1889)

Born **7 June 1868 at 70 Parson Street, Townhead, Glasgow**, fourth of eleven children of a police superintendent **[A: Wikipedia, "Charles Rennie Mackintosh"]**. Pin used: 55.8628 / -4.2367.

Attends **Allan Glen's Institution**, a school with a technical rather than a classical bent, from about 1880 **[A for the school; the 1880-1883 span is given without an act or register reference, so the start month and day are placeholders]**. The tradition that his father intended him for the ministry and that architecture was the son's own choice is carried by the biographical literature **[R]**.

**1884: articled to the architect John Hutchison** in Glasgow **[A]**. The single most persistent error in the secondary material attaches here: the name is repeatedly printed **Hutchinson**, with an intrusive n. The correct form is **Hutchison**. The office address is not fixed by any source reached; the pin 55.859 / -4.251 is a central-Glasgow placeholder and is marked **[R]** in the journey file's `date_confidence` and prose alike.

Concurrently he enrols in **evening classes at the Glasgow School of Art**, then housed on Sauchiehall Street, and becomes a prize-winning student **[A]**. **Gap:** no source reached gives the year the evening classes began. Wikipedia states only that the classes run alongside the apprenticeship through the 1880s. The journey file dates this stop 1885-01-01 and says so in `date_confidence`.

**1889: joins Honeyman and Keppie as draughtsman [A].** Office pin 55.8597 / -4.2537 is **[R]**, a city-centre placeholder rather than a documented address.

At the school he meets **Margaret and Frances Macdonald and Herbert MacNair**, the group later called the Glasgow Four. Wikipedia dates the meeting with Margaret to **c. 1892 [A, with the circa carried through]**; the journey file's evening-class stop names the group without dating the meeting, and the marriage stop carries the firm 1900 date.

**Influence lineage [R].** The Arts and Crafts inheritance behind the school's teaching (Ruskin on honest ornament, Morris on making) is standard art history; no letter, lecture, or citation linking Mackintosh to either man by name was reached. See section 12.

## 2. The Thomson Studentship: Italy, 1891

The **Alexander Thomson Travelling Studentship** takes him to Italy: **Naples, Siena, Ravenna (San Vitale), Venice [R throughout]**. The pool's source is the general biography (Alan Crawford, *Charles Rennie Mackintosh*), with the itinerary reconstructed from surviving sketchbooks rather than from correspondence. No individual leg is independently confirmed here, and the months are placeholders inside the travelling season; the journey file says so in all four `date_confidence` fields.

The claim that Ravenna's mosaic feeds the flattened, jewelled surfaces of the later decorative work is **the biographers' reconstruction drawn backward from the finished work [R]**, and the campa states it as theirs, not his.

## 3. The Glasgow Style: early interiors and the first buildings (1893-1900)

- **Craigie Hall, Dumbreck**, interior remodelling for a client of Honeyman's, 1893 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's 55.8358 / -4.3097 is about 1.26 km off and lands in the wrong district. Craigie Hall stands at 6 Rowan Road, Dumbreck; **55.8471 / -4.3057** used.
- **Glasgow Art Club, Bath Street**, rooms redesigned, 1893 **[A]**. Pin 55.8631 / -4.2653 uncorrected.
- **Glasgow Herald building and water tower, Mitchell Lane**, 1893-1895 **[A: Wikipedia, "The Lighthouse, Glasgow"]**. Pin 55.8597 / -4.2556. Now The Lighthouse, Scotland's centre for design and architecture.
- **Martyrs' Public School, Parson Street**, 1895-1898. **Tag held at [R]:** the 1895-1898 dates were not independently verifiable in the verification pass, and the pool's own correction note says to keep it **[R]** until sourced. **Coordinate corrected:** 55.8654 / -4.2377, not 55.8631 / -4.2352 (about 300 m). The school stands on the street he was born in, which is confirmed by the two addresses themselves.
- **Buchanan Street tea rooms for Catherine Cranston, 1896 [A: Wikipedia, "Willow Tearooms"]**. Pin 55.8617 / -4.2531.
- **Ruchill Church Hall, Maryhill, 1898-1899 [R]**, coordinate corrected to 55.8855 / -4.2863. **Dropped from the journey file** for length, not for doubt; it belongs in any later expansion.
- **Queen's Cross Church, Maryhill [A: Wikipedia, "Queen's Cross Church, Glasgow"]**. Memorial stone 23 June 1898, first service 10 September 1899, and it is the **only church of his design ever built**, the Liverpool scheme being an unbuilt competition entry. **Coordinate corrected:** 55.8803 / -4.2716, not 55.8834 / -4.2799 (about 620 m).
- **Glasgow School of Art, east wing, Renfrew Street, 1897-1899 [A: Wikipedia, "Glasgow School of Art"]**. **Coordinate corrected everywhere it appears:** the Mackintosh Building is at **55.8661 / -4.2637**. The pool carried three wrong variants for this one building (55.8664 / -4.2578 in the chronology, 55.8637 / -4.2634 in the afterlife section); all are replaced.
- **Marriage to Margaret Macdonald, 22 August 1900 [A]**, and the flat at **120 Mains Street** (later Blythswood Street), pin 55.862 / -4.2664.

## 4. Vienna, Turin, and the competitions (1900-1902)

**Vienna Secession, eighth exhibition, 1900 [A].** A Mackintosh-designed room is shown and the reception is warmer than Glasgow's; the influence on Josef Hoffmann and the circle that becomes the Wiener Werkstatte is the standard reading. **Coordinate corrected:** the Secession Building is at **48.2005 / 16.3657**, not 48.201 / 16.3707 (about 370 m).

**Honeyman, Keppie and Mackintosh, 1901 [A]:** partnership and name on the door, twelve years after joining as a draughtsman.

**House for an Art Lover, 1901 [A: Wikipedia, "House for an Art Lover"].** Competition of the German magazine *Zeitschrift für Innendekoration*; 36 entries; the Mackintosh portfolio arrives late and lacks the required interior perspectives, is **disqualified on those grounds**, and is given a special prize regardless. The judges' assessment ("pronounced personal quality, novel and austere form and the uniform configuration of interior and exterior") is quoted in the journey file, sourced through Graham Roxburgh, *Building the Dream* (2006), as cited on Wikipedia; **Roxburgh's book itself was not reached**.

**Placement decision.** The pool pins this stop at Bellahouston Park (55.8474 / -4.3139), the site where the house was eventually built in 1989-1996. That is a 1980s decision, not a 1901 one: the competition was for an imaginary house with **no site at all**. The journey file therefore pins the 1901 portfolio at the Mains Street flat where it was drawn and names Bellahouston in the prose and `suggested_refs`. This is the same discipline as the standing rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there.

**Turin International Exhibition, 1902 [R]:** a Scottish room designed and furnished by Mackintosh and Margaret. The pool's source is the general biography, not independently confirmed.

## 5. The great commissions (1901-1909)

- **Windyhill, Kilmacolm**, for William Davidson, 1900-1901 **[R]**; address and coordinates not independently confirmed. Pin 55.9014 / -4.6172.
- **Hill House, Helensburgh**, for the publisher Walter Blackie, **design 1902, completed 1904, cost £5,000 [A: Wikipedia, "Hill House, Helensburgh"]**. The pool had this tagged **[R]** on the strength of a 404 on a wrongly titled article; the correctly titled one exists, so it is **upgraded to [A]**. **Coordinate corrected:** 56.0171 / -4.7281, not 56.0011 / -4.7333 (about 1.9 km). Blackie's recollection of the method is quoted in the journey file; it reaches us through Wikipedia citing a 2018 Dezeen article citing Blackie's memoir, and **the memoir itself was not reached**.
- **Liverpool Anglican Cathedral competition [A that he entered: Wikipedia, "Liverpool Cathedral", naming him among 103 entrants].** **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the pool dates his entry 1903, but the competition opened in late 1901 and Giles Gilbert Scott was selected in 1903. Dating the entry to 1903 conflates submission with verdict. The journey file dates the stop 1902-01-01 and states the ambiguity in both `date_confidence` and prose. **Coordinate corrected:** 53.3975 / -2.9731, not 53.3985 / -2.9666 (about 450 m). **Gap:** no source reached says whether Mackintosh ever visited the site.
- **Willow Tea Rooms, 217 Sauchiehall Street**, commissioned 1903, opened October 1903, full interior and exterior control **[A: Wikipedia, "Willow Tearooms"]**. **Coordinate corrected:** 55.865 / -4.2612 (55°51'54.105"N 4°15'40.179"W). The pool carried two different wrong pins for this building (55.8654 / -4.265 and 55.8657 / -4.2635). Restored and reopened 2018.
- **Scotland Street School, Kingston**, designed and built 1903-1906, opened 1906, closed as a school 1979 **[A: Wikipedia, "Scotland Street School Museum"]**; pool tag **[R] upgraded to [A]**. **Coordinate corrected:** 55.8495 / -4.2737, not 55.8447 / -4.2686 (about 600 m).
- **Move to 6 Florentine Terrace, later 78 Southpark Avenue, Hillhead, 1906 [A]**. **Coordinate corrected:** about 55.8737 / -4.2867, not 55.8748 / -4.2917.
- **Glasgow School of Art, west wing and library, completed 1909 [A]**. Same corrected pin as the east wing.

The pool's summary claim that the major commissions are concentrated in a single decade is Wikipedia's own framing, given there as **1895-1906 [A]**.

## 6. The practice fails (1910-1915)

Profitability of the partnership declines in the early 1910s **[R, Wikipedia]**; **resignation from Honeyman, Keppie and Mackintosh, 1913 [A]**, followed by an independent practice that produces almost nothing.

**Not asserted here:** the explanations usually offered (temperament, drink, a turn of fashion) are all supplied by later writers and none was verifiable in this pass. The campa says exactly that and attributes them to no one.

**1914: move to Walberswick, Suffolk [A]**, pin 52.313 / 1.667. **1915: brief arrest and questioning as a suspected German spy [A in the chronology pool, Wikipedia; the geography pool downgrades it to [R] because the Wikipedia "Walberswick" article fetched in that pass did not confirm it].** **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the two halves of the pool disagree on the tier of this claim. The journey file treats it as widely repeated biographical tradition and says so.

**The Sedding motto.** "There is hope in honest error, none in the icy perfection of the mere stylist." This is quoted in the journey file at the resignation stop and is **explicitly not given as Mackintosh's own words**: Wikiquote states it originates with the English architect **J. D. Sedding** and is sometimes misattributed to Mackintosh, who adopted it as a personal motto. The pool also carried a corrupted wording, "icy perfections" plural; the standard singular form is used. This is the single most likely error to reappear downstream, since the line is printed on Glasgow merchandise over Mackintosh's name.

## 7. Chelsea and Derngate (1915-1923)

**Glebe Place, Chelsea [R].** **Address contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the pool's "78 Glebe Place" is unsupported and looks like contamination from 78 Derngate and 78 Southpark Avenue. Wikipedia's "Glebe Place" article lists Mackintosh at **No. 49**; the biographical literature more often gives **43A**. The journey file names the street and not the number, and the campa states the disagreement. **Coordinate corrected:** 51.4857 / -0.1699.

**78 Derngate, Northampton, 1916-1917 [A: Wikipedia, "78 Derngate"]**, for the model-engineering manufacturer **Wenman Bassett-Lowke**; later called one of the first Art Deco interiors in Britain; the Bassett-Lowkes left it in 1926 for a modernist house. Pin 52.2356 / -0.89.

**Textile and book-binding work for Blackie and Son, 1920-1923.** The pool's geography section tagged this **[A]** and its own correction note **downgrades it to [R]**: it was not confirmed in the Wikipedia article in the verification pass. Carried as **[R]** and the campa says the attribution rests on little that can be checked.

## 8. Roussillon (1923-1927)

**Port Vendres, from 1923 [A: CRM Society website, Port Vendres page]**, pin 42.5192 / 3.1058. Architecture abandoned; watercolour full time.

**Collioure [R]**, 42.5259 / 3.0831. **Mont-Louis [R]**, 42.5083 / 2.1219. **Ille-sur-Têt [R]**, 42.6667 / 2.6167. **Amélie-les-Bains [R]**, 42.4667 / 2.6667, is in the pool and is **omitted from the journey file for length**, not for doubt. All four rest on the general Roussillon-watercolour literature; none was independently confirmed, and which year each sheet belongs to is largely guesswork.

**1927: the tongue tumour worsens through the winter and he leaves France [A for the return, R for the scene in the port]**.

## 9. London, the last year (1927-1928)

- **Westminster Hospital**, diagnosis of tongue cancer, surgery and radium **[A]**, pin 51.4975 / -0.1357. The surgery leaves his voice a whisper.
- **Willow Road, Hampstead [A]**, convalescence as guest of the painter **Randolph Schwabe** and his wife Birdie, pin 51.5566 / -0.1651.
- **12 Porchester Square, Bayswater [A]**, arranged by his friend **Desmond Chapman-Huston**, and death at the **nursing home at 26 Porchester Square on 10 December 1928, aged 60 [A]**. **Coordinate corrected:** 51.5176 / -0.1874; the pool carried 51.5152 / -0.1877 and 51.5145 / -0.1834. **Two pool errors struck out:** the phrase "three years after leaving the coast" is wrong (he left Port Vendres in 1927 and died in December 1928, about thirteen months later), and "Margaret at his side" is unsourced and is not asserted in the journey file.
- **Cremation at Golders Green Crematorium, 11 December 1928 [A]**. **Coordinate corrected:** 51.5772 / -0.1936 (62 Hoop Lane, NW11 7NL); the pool carried three wrong variants. **Pool error struck out:** a campa draft in the pool referred to "his grave", which cannot stand, since he was cremated and the ashes scattered. The "no memorial service" detail is unsourced and is omitted.
- **Ashes scattered on the Mediterranean off Port Vendres, from rocks he had painted [A for the fact, CRM Society; date unrecorded].** The journey file dates the stop 1929-01-01 as an explicit placeholder and says so.

## 10. Afterlife (recorded, mostly not staged as stops)

Handled inside the campas of the works themselves rather than as separate scenes, since Mackintosh was present at none of it:

- **1981:** the Mackintosh House, a reconstruction of the Southpark Avenue interiors, opens inside the Hunterian, University of Glasgow (55.8721 / -4.2887) **[A]**.
- **1989-1996:** House for an Art Lover built in Bellahouston Park, 55.8474 / -4.3139 **[A]**. Pool coordinate corrected from 55.8347 / -4.3186, about 1.5 km off and outside the park.
- **1990:** Glasgow as European City of Culture, the hinge of the modern rediscovery **[A]**. **1996-1997:** Metropolitan Museum of Art retrospective, New York **[A]**.
- **23 May 2014:** fire destroys the library; about 90% of the structure survives, roughly 70% of contents lost **[A]**.
- **15 June 2018:** second fire guts the building; **[A]**. Approach is faithful reinstatement and the building is **due to reopen fully as a graduate school in 2030 [A]**.
- **2018:** Willow Tea Rooms restored and reopened; Andy Scott statue unveiled in Glasgow on the ninetieth anniversary of his death; Pendolino 390008 named for him in March **[A]**.
- **2019:** the Hill House Box, a steel-mesh shelter by Carmody Groarke for the National Trust for Scotland, installed to let the failed Portland cement harling dry out, scheduled to remain until 2028 **[A, upgraded from the pool's [R]]**.
- **2009:** Clydesdale Bank £100 note carries his portrait and rose motif **[A]**.
- **1986-1992: rolling-stock error corrected.** Locomotive **86226**, named *Charles Rennie Mackintosh*, is a **Class 86 AC electric**, not an InterCity 125 power car: Wikipedia's "InterCity locomotive 86226" uses InterCity as the sector brand, and HST power cars are diesel, numbered 43xxx. The span is right.

## 11. Argentina

**No documented connection between Mackintosh and Argentina was found [A as an absence, noted across all consulted sources].** His career runs Scotland, England, France, with exhibition contact in Vienna and Turin. Expected for an aesthetic-ancestry entry: the Argentine link is stylistic, through the Buenos Aires Art Nouveau architects, and belongs to their files.

## 12. Interlocks

Every candidate relation surfaced by the interlock pass is **geographic or generational overlap**, which is not a reason to name a name in a campa. A grep across all `*.journey.json` returns **no direct hits for "Mackintosh"** in any existing file **[A]**.

**Named in campas (two, both influence lineage, both [R]):**

- **john_ruskin**, at the Glasgow School of Art evening classes: Ruskin's argument that ornament must be made by the hand that thought of it is the theory the school taught under. Influence, not contact.
- **william_morris**, at the Glasgow School of Art east wing: the Arts and Crafts inheritance is the acknowledged ancestor of the Glasgow Style. Influence, not contact.

**Deliberately not named**, despite being in the atlas: Adam Smith, Lord Kelvin, J. M. Barrie (Glasgow co-location only); Eugene Grasset, Paul Berthon, Jules Felix Coutan, Loie Fuller (the same 1900 decorative-arts moment in another capital, no documented contact); Francisco Gianotti, Virginio Colombo, Eduardo Rodriguez Ortega (the Argentine branch of the same style-family, no contact); Antoni Gaudi, Le Corbusier (contemporary and successor, no textual link reached); **Ida Hoffmann**, whose file is Vienna-Secession-adjacent through Josef Hoffmann's circle but who is not Josef Hoffmann and has no relation to Mackintosh. **Josef Hoffmann himself is named in the Vienna campa** as a historical figure influenced by the exhibited room; he has no file of his own, so he is not an interlock.
