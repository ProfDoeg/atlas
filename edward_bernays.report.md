# Edward L. Bernays (1891-1995): research report

*Written 2026-08-28 from the verified research pool. Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline; **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim resting on Bernays's own retrospective account. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated; unreachable sources are named with the reason.*

One evidentiary problem governs this subject. Bernays wrote an 849-page memoir, told the same handful of anecdotes for sixty years, and outlived everyone who could have contradicted him, so a large share of the standard biography is **his own testimony about himself, repeated until it acquired the texture of record**. Wikipedia says so of the most famous episode: "with each retelling the tale got more colorful and his claims more sweeping" **[A]**. Where a claim rests only on him it is **[R]** below, however universally repeated.

## 1. Vienna and New York, 1891-1912

- **Born 22 November 1891, Vienna [A, Wikipedia]**. The **birth address is not established** in anything reached; the journey uses the canonical Vienna pin (48.2191, 16.3632, inherited from `freud.journey.json`) and says in `date_confidence` that it is an inherited city pin, not a located birthplace.
- **The double kinship [A, Wikipedia; and Bernays to Moyers: "Well my mother was his younger sister and my father was the brother of his wife"]**. Mother Anna Freud Bernays, Freud's sister; father Ely Bernays, brother of Martha Bernays, Freud's wife.
- **Emigration 1892 [A]**; Ely ahead of the family in the Manhattan grain trade at the New York Produce Exchange **[A for the trade, R for the years on the floor]**. The 2 Broadway building is a general identification.
- **DeWitt Clinton High School, 1908 [A, Wikipedia; Cornell CALS]**. Building attested separately: Tenth Avenue between 58th and 59th, C. B. J. Snyder's H-shaped building "said to be the largest high school building then in the United States", occupied 1906-1929 **[A]**. **Coordinate corrected**: the pool's 40.769, -73.9885 sat ~150 m south of the block; the journey uses 40.7702, -73.9884.
- **Cornell, A.B. in agriculture, 1912, at his father's direction [A, Wikipedia; cals.cornell.edu]**. **Louis Dreyfus & Company, Paris, back in New York by December 1912 [R]**, LOC chronology only, no address located; canonical Paris pin, placeholder month.

## 2. The trade journals and the third party, 1912-1917

- **Co-editor with Fred Robinson of the Medical Review of Reviews and the Dietetic and Hygienic Gazette [A, Wikipedia]**. No address located; midtown placeholder. **Caruso tour, Toledo, 1917 [A, prmuseum.org, which holds the Shriners Temple photograph]**; month and day unestablished.
- **Damaged Goods, 1913 [A, upgraded from the pool's R]**. The pool hedged the Fulton Theatre as general knowledge; Wikipedia's article on the play in fact **names the Fulton, the first US performance of 14 March 1913, the public premiere of 14 May, and Bernays and the Sociological Fund by name**. **Coordinate corrected** a block west to the Fulton's site at 210 West 46th Street. **Not asserted**: the widely repeated sponsor list (Rockefeller Jr., Franklin Roosevelt) is not in the pool and stays out of the campa, which says only clergymen, physicians, philanthropists.
- **Ballets Russes from 1915 [A, prmuseum.org, including his own "I was given a job about which I knew nothing"]**. The 81-page advance-agent guide is **[R]**; prmuseum confirms only that he met the dancers at the docks.
  - **Chronology corrected.** The pool put **Nijinsky** in the 1915 sentence. He was interned in Austria-Hungary; "Nijinsky arrived in New York on 4 April 1916" **[A, Wikipedia]**. The US season opened January 1916.
  - **Venue corrected.** The pool put the 1916 season at the old Metropolitan Opera House; the January 1916 debut was at the **Century Theatre**, and the Metropolitan link is Otto Kahn's negotiation of the *autumn* tour. The journey uses the Century for January **[R for venue and day]** and the `vaslav_nijinsky.journey.json` pin for 4 April **[A for the arrival, R for Bernays on that pier that day]**.

## 4. Counsel on public relations, 1919-1929

- **Office opened 1919, title "counsel on public relations" adopted [A]**; no street address located, midtown placeholder. **First university course in public relations, NYU, 1923 [R]**, LOC chronology only. ***Crystallizing Public Opinion*, 1923 [A]**. **Coolidge breakfast, 1924 [A, Wikipedia; prmuseum; Moyers]**, month and day unestablished. **Liggett & Myers and the Tobacco Society for Voice Culture, 1927, then hired by Hill [A]**. ***Propaganda*, Liveright, 1928 [A]**, read from the archive.org scans.
- **Doris Fleischman** (all **[A, Wikipedia]** except as noted): Barnard 1913, New York Tribune, first employee 1919; married 1922 at City Hall (**[R]** for the venue, the jwa.org page returned an access error); registered at the Waldorf-Astoria in her own name; 1925 passport in her birth name, reported the first to a married American woman; daughters 1929 and 1930; died of a stroke 10 July 1980 aged 88, place of death Cambridge **[R, inferred]**.
- **NAACP eleventh annual conference, Atlanta, 1920 [A, New Georgia Encyclopedia; the Museum of Public Relations catalog holds the letter of appreciation, which supplies 30 May to 2 June]**.
  - **Source error corrected**: the pool cited the Wikipedia article on Bernays, which **contains no mention of the NAACP or Atlanta at all**. Citation withdrawn.
  - **One-sidedness corrected, the most consequential fix in the file.** The New Georgia Encyclopedia continues: the publicity "triggered a backlash that hurt the Atlanta branch and virtually paralyzed most branches throughout the state for the next two decades." "Proceeded without the anticipated violence" is true and radically incomplete; the campa carries both halves.
- **Procter & Gamble, Ivory, the National Soap Sculpture Competition, from 1923 [A, prmuseum.org]**. **The stop was moved to New York**: P&G sat in Cincinnati, no record places Bernays there, and a stop belongs to whoever was actually there. **1925 Paris Exposition, Cheney Brothers silks in the Louvre [A, prmuseum.org, "exhibited in the Louvre for the first time ever"]**; **"Grand Palais" dropped from the place name**, a separate site whose inclusion made the coordinate ambiguous.
- **Torches of freedom, Easter Sunday 31 March 1929, Fifth Avenue [A, Wikipedia; Moyers]**. Brill's consultation and the phrase **[A via Bernays]**; Bertha Hunt **[A]**; cross street unestablished. **The causal claim is not asserted**: that theatre smoking rooms opened to women within five weeks is **[R]**, his own account, and Murphree's peer-reviewed study found later tellings amplified it.
- **Light's Golden Jubilee, Dearborn, 21 October 1929 [A, Wikipedia: Hoover, Curie, Orville Wright, Chrysler, Eastman, Mellon, Rockefeller Jr., Will Rogers present; Einstein by radio; up to 140 stations]**. Pin inherited from `thomas_edison.journey.json`. **Scope corrected**: GE organized it and **"Henry Ford took control and relocated the ceremony from Schenectady, New York to Dearborn"**, and Bernays is **not named in that article's text**, only in a navigation template. The campa attributes the publicity to him, not the event.

## 6. United Fruit and Guatemala, 1943-1959

- **The Middle America Information Bureau.** Tye confirms it: Bernays "set up one of his trademark front groups, the Middle America Information Bureau" **[A, cabinetmagazine.org/issues/23/tye.php]**. **Tye gives no founding date**, so **1943 is [R]**, on the LOC finding aid alone. "Front group" is Tye's word, better sourced than the pool's "distributing company-supplied information"; the campa uses it and attributes it to him.
- **The press tour: January 1952, two weeks [A, Tye]**: "the publishers of **Newsweek, the Cincinnati Enquirer, the Nashville Banner, and the New Orleans Item**." **Time, present in the pool's geography entry, is not among Tye's outlets and has been removed.** McCann as Tye quotes him: "what the press would hear and see was carefully staged and regulated by the host" **[A]**.
- **The 1952 memo** on scientific counter-communist propaganda and the plant disease **[A, Tye, from the Bernays papers]**; month not given, set to June. **The Redmond letter [A, Tye]**: "to carry forward the strong aggressive tactics of the United Fruit Company in pointing the finger at Communism in Guatemala."
- **The "responsible correspondent" [A, datable more precisely than the pool's "1951-1954"]**: Tye has Bernays in **May 1954** citing "a very responsible correspondent of ours in Guatemala City" on Soviet arms; the stop is narrowed accordingly.
- **PBSUCCESS [A, CIA/State memorandum, history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54Guat/d287]**: authorized August 1953. **Árbenz resigned 27 June 1954**, handing government to armed-forces chief Carlos Enrique Díaz; Castillo Armas headed the junta in early July. **What is not claimed**: no document reached places Bernays inside the CIA command structure, and the campa says so outright.
- **Terminated by United Fruit, 1959 [A]**. *Public Relations* (1952) and *The Engineering of Consent* (1955, ed.) **[A]**.

**Two stops stand where no document puts him**, following the exemplar rather than breaking the rule: `abdelkader.journey.json` has "The deira on the Moulouya, the prisoners' deaths", set three hundred miles from where the emir was fighting. A stop for an agent acting under the subject's direction and at his expense is his stop; the correspondent was his employee, and both `date_confidence` fields say plainly he was not present. **Tegucigalpa was dropped** on that test: nothing connects Bernays to the city except that the operation staged through it.

## 7. Cambridge, 1962/63-1995

- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Tye's biography is the usual source for a **1962** move; Wikipedia has him in New York **"from 1919 to 1963"**. The journey writes 1963 and records 1962 as the competing reading; nothing reached settles it. ***Biography of an Idea*, Simon & Schuster, 1965, 849 pp. [A]**; that it minimizes Doris Fleischman's authorship is **[A]**, and where it was written is **[R, inferential]**.
- **NYU instructor 1948-1950; Hawaii visiting professor 1950; Boston University adjunct 1968-1969**: all **[R]**, from the LOC chronology, finding aid and a 1951 annotated bibliography as summarized in earlier research, none confirmed this pass.
- **Cornell Outstanding Alumni Award, 1985 [R]**: the CALS page was fetched and confirms his standing there, calling him "the father of public relations" who "used his expertise in psychology to manipulate public opinion" and noting his place on *Life*'s 100 Most Influential Americans **[A]**, but the awardees page was not reached.
- **Northeastern honorary doctorate, 17 June 1989 [A, archivesspace.library.northeastern.edu/subjects/2627]**, which also records the Bernays papers, M099, 1982-1998. **Massachusetts Senate Bill 374, 1992, failed [A, prmuseum.org]**; no record places him in the State House, and the coordinate marks the legislature and says so.
- **Death: Cambridge, 1995, aged 103 [A, Wikipedia]**. **The day (9 March) and the cause (bladder cancer) are [R]**, from the Washington Post obituary, which **returned HTTP 403**; Wikipedia's text gives neither.
- **No burial place could be located.** Findagrave and the NYT return 403; Wikipedia EN/DE/ES, Alchetron, Prabook and **Mount Auburn Cemetery's own site search (zero results)** all lack it. Stated in the file as a gap, which is itself striking for a man who curated everything else about his posthumous record.

## 9. Quote transcription errors corrected

1. **Dropped word**: "our minds molded" is a web variant, both 1928 scans read **"our minds are molded"**. This quote did not survive into the final file; the correction is recorded here.
2. **Wrong number**: "its stereotypes, as Walter Lippmann calls it" should be **"its stereotype"**, singular, corroborated by the singular pronoun in the same clause. Used at the 1923 NYU stop, correction stated in `quote_source`.
3. **Spliced quote**: the Moyers dinner-table quote stitched across Moyers's interjection "Taboos?", merging two turns into one sentence. The journey marks the elision with an ellipsis and says in `quote_source` what it replaces.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Bernays is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stop is licensed and none was written. One relation found is **not rooted in his own life** and is flagged here rather than deleted, per the adversary's duty.

- **Che Guevara in Guatemala, 1953-1954. Direction: neither backward nor forward, but sideways; pure co-location.** `che_guevara.journey.json` has Guevara in Guatemala City from Christmas Eve 1953, witnessing the fall of Árbenz and writing of "these capitalist octopuses". Same coup, same city, same company, from the opposite side, and the most vivid interlock the research turned up. **It is not a relation between Guevara and Bernays**: Guevara wrote against United Fruit, not against Bernays, and Bernays never mentions Guevara. Shared geography alone is not grounds to name a traveler in a campa, so he is **not named** here. Flagged in case the operator wants it added in the reverse direction, on Guevara's own file, where it would be legal. The same reasoning excluded **Fidel Castro** and **Marcus Garvey**.
- **Goebbels.** Bernays's memoir attributes to Karl von Wiegand a secondhand 1933 report that Goebbels used *Crystallizing Public Opinion* as a blueprint. This **is** rooted in his own life, being his own memoir claim, and the *Propaganda* campa carries it as exactly that: second hand, one journalist, thirty-seven years after **[R]**. No atlas file exists for any Nazi propaganda figure to check it against; Emerald *CCIJ* 29(1), "Bernays and Goebbels", which interrogates the legend, is **paywalled and its verdict unread**.
