# Robert Fludd (1574-1637): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15. Robert Fludd, alias Robertus de Fluctibus: English physician, Fellow of the College of Physicians, Paracelsian, apologist for the Rosicrucian fraternity, author of the Utriusque Cosmi Historia.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named on the spot. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or inference, including anything a source states without a document behind it. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and **not** adjudicated. Coordinate problems are findings in their own right here, because the journey file is a map before it is a text.

**Method.** Everything below comes from a verified research pool built on the English Wikipedia article "Robert Fludd" and its bibliography, the Dictionary of National Biography (1885-1900) entry on Wikisource, the New World Encyclopedia article, a set of Wikipedia cross-check articles, Nominatim for church and college coordinates, and the atlas's own journey files and QUEUE.md. The full list is in Sources. Nothing rests on Fludd's own folios read directly; see Gaps.

---

## 1. Kent and Oxford, 1574-1598

Robert Fludd is born 17 January 1574 at Milgate House in the parish of Bearsted, Kent, third son of Sir Thomas Fludd, treasurer-at-war to Elizabeth I in the Low Countries and later MP for Kent **[A: Wikipedia; DNB]**. The identification of the birth house as Milgate, and the "third son" rank, rest on the DNB and tradition relayed through it **[R]**.

*Coordinate finding.* Two lenses of the pool carried 51.2667 / 0.6167 for Bearsted, a point about 4.5 km east of the village toward Hollingbourne. Wikipedia gives **51.2738 / 0.5789** for the village; Milgate House is separately placed at **51.2634 / 0.5606** **[R: uncorroborated]**. The journey file uses the Milgate figure for the birth stop and records the discrepancy here rather than averaging it away.

He matriculates at St John's College, Oxford as a commoner in 1591 **[A: Wikipedia]**. The Medical Fellow in residence is Matthew Gwinne, who had produced a tract showing that, while he practised Galenic medicine, he knew the principal Paracelsian work **[A: Wikipedia]**. Whether Gwinne turned Fludd toward medicine is inference; the sources place the two in the same college and no further **[R]**.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** Wikipedia gives the B.A. as 1597; the DNB, quoting Oxford records, gives "graduated B.A. on 3 Feb. 1596" **[A: DNB]**. Old-style dating, with the year turning on 25 March, would reconcile them exactly, but no consulted source performs that reconciliation, so it is offered as an explanation and not as a finding. The M.A. of 1598 is uncontested **[A]**.

*Coordinate finding.* Two lenses disagree on St John's College: 51.7562 / -1.2585 against 51.759 / -1.254. The second lands about 330 m north-east of the college, near the University Parks. Nominatim gives **51.7573 / -1.2577**, which the journey file uses.

## 2. The continental years, 1598-1604

After the M.A., Fludd spends roughly six years on the Continent. The DNB's summary is the whole documentary basis: "visiting France, Spain, Italy, and Germany, and teaching in noble families" **[A: DNB, verbatim]**. To this Wikipedia adds that he spent a winter in the Pyrenees studying theurgy with Jesuits, a claim descending from Fludd's own later autobiographical remarks **[R]**.

Everything more specific is tradition. The DNB has him tutoring the sons of the Duke of Guise in mathematics at Avignon **[R]**. Marseille, Madrid, Rome and the German Paracelsian centres appear in later biographies without documents behind any single leg **[R]**; Wikipedia states outright that the itinerary is undocumented in detail. Marburg was assigned to the German leg by the research pool as a stand-in for a documented centre of Paracelsian teaching, a modern editorial choice labelled as one **[R]**. No town within the Pyrenees is named anywhere; 42.95 / 0.15 marks the range, not a place Fludd is known to have slept.

The result is better attested than the route: Fludd returns a Paracelsian and defends Paracelsian medicine for life, against Galenists first and mechanists later **[R: synthesis of Wikipedia's biographical and doctrinal sections; no dated encounter with Paracelsus's texts is pinpointed anywhere consulted]**.

## 3. Physician in London, 1604-1618

Fludd matriculates at Christ Church, Oxford in 1604 **[A: Wikipedia]**, supplicates for his medical degree on 14 May 1605 **[R: date given by Wikipedia without a document cited]**, and graduates M.B. and M.D. on 16 May 1605 **[A: Wikipedia]**. He settles in London, in a house on Fenchurch Street **[A: Wikipedia]**.

Admission to the College of Physicians follows only after at least six failed attempts **[A: Wikipedia]**; the DNB gives election as fellow on 20 September 1609, the examiners' verdict "dignus" on a 22 December, and "on 21 March 1608 he was again admonished" **[A: DNB, verbatim]**. What the admonishments were for is nowhere stated.

*Coordinate and venue finding.* Pool entries placed the 1609 admission at Amen Corner (51.5138 / -0.0996) or at 51.525 / -0.142, which is the College's Regent's Park building opened in 1964. The College did not move to Amen Corner until 1614; before that it occupied Linacre's house in Knightrider Street, for which no consulted source gives a coordinate. The journey file drops the building name for 1609 and uses the Amen Corner pin **51.51445 / -0.10090** as a City locator, keeping the venue name only from 1614. "Royal" is likewise anachronistic for 1609 and is not used.

He takes part in the College's inspection of London apothecaries in 1614, helps author the Pharmacopoeia Londinensis in 1618, and serves as Censor in 1618, 1627, 1633 and 1634 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

Thomas Fuller explains his success in practice by "a 'faith-natural' which aided the well working of his drugs"; John Webster calls him "one of the most Christian philosophers that ever writ" **[A: both quoted in the DNB]**.

## 4. The Rosicrucian controversy, 1614-1617

The Fama Fraternitatis is printed at Kassel in 1614 and the Confessio follows in 1615 **[A: as recorded in the atlas's own christian_rosenkreuz.journey.json, which the interlock lens read directly]**. Andreas Libavius publishes his Analysis confessionis Fraternitatis de Rosea Cruce against the manifestos in 1615 **[A: Wikipedia, "Andreas Libavius"]**.

*Coordinate finding.* The pool placed Libavius at 51.1657 / 10.4515, which is the geometric centroid of modern Germany, an empty field in Thuringia. Libavius lived at Coburg from 1607 until his death in 1616 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the journey file uses **50.2612 / 10.9627**.

Fludd answers with the Apologia Compendiaria, Leiden 1616, and the Tractatus Apologeticus integritatem Societatis de Rosea Cruce defendens, Leiden 1617 **[A: Wikipedia bibliography; the atlas's christian_rosenkreuz file carries the same Leiden placement and flags the common attribution to England as wrong]**. The Tractatus Theologo-philosophicus, a different work, is printed at Oppenheim the same year **[A]**. One pool entry had merged the two at Oppenheim; that merge is undone here.

Fludd is not a member of the fraternity and no source claims he ever met one **[A: Wikipedia]**. The DNB preserves Justus Helt's testimony of 20 April 1617 that the brotherhood was neither popish nor Lutheran, "Fratrum theosophiam esse Calvinistarum theologiam" **[A: DNB, verbatim]**. A pool note had glossed the contrast as "not popery or diabolism"; diabolism does not appear in the DNB's sentence. Correction recorded.

Fludd's own dismissive term for Aristotelian and pagan learning, set against revealed wisdom, is "ethnic philosophy" **[A: Fludd, quoted in DNB]**.

## 5. Utriusque Cosmi Historia, 1617-1624

The Utriusque Cosmi, Maioris scilicet et Minoris, Metaphysica, Physica atque Technica Historia begins publication at Oppenheim in 1617 and continues at Frankfurt, printed by Johann Theodor de Bry and later Caspar Rötel **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction, not resolved.** Two lenses date the run 1617-1624 and 1617-1621. Wikipedia's imprint statement supports the longer span, which the journey file uses; the shorter is recorded here rather than deleted.

**Attribution downgraded.** The pool asserted the plates were "engraved chiefly by Matthaeus Merian" **[originally A]**. Neither Wikipedia article, on Fludd or on Merian, connects him to the Utriusque Cosmi. Merian did work for de Bry at Oppenheim from 1616 to 1620 and married de Bry's daughter in 1617 **[A: Wikipedia, "Matthäus Merian"]**, so the traditional attribution is possible. It is carried as **[R]**, and the journey file names the workshop rather than the hand.

De Musica Mundana, the cosmic-harmony section, appears within the project in 1618 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Redating.** The pool dated Fludd's water-screw machine (a wheel driving an Archimedes screw that returns the water to the header tank) to 1630. Wikipedia's "Perpetual motion" article dates that device to **1618** and calls it the first such description; the Fludd article separately says that in 1630 he proposed many perpetual motion machines. Both are kept, distinct **[A for each, once separated]**.

## 6. The quarrel with Kepler, 1619-1623

Johannes Kepler, at Linz, publishes Harmonice Mundi in 1619; the DNB locates his attack on Fludd in the "appendix to 'Harmonice Mundi,' 1619" **[A: DNB, verbatim]**. Fludd answers with Veritatis Proscenium, Frankfurt 1621, arguing that the hermetic or chemical approach reaches deeper than the mathematical, and again with Monochordon Mundi Symphoniacum, Frankfurt 1622 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Correction.** The pool described the Monochordon as a reply to "Kepler's Mathematice", which is not a work. The phrase is a misreading of Wikipedia's terse bibliography line. Kepler's 1622 rejoinder is Pro suo opere Harmonices mundi apologia adversus demonstrationem analyticam Roberti de Fluctibus **[A]**. Title and place of the Monochordon are unaffected.

Anatomiae Amphitheatrum, Frankfurt 1623, reprints the Monochordon inside a wider anatomical and cosmological treatise **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 7. Mersenne, Gassendi, and the mechanist front, 1623-1631

Marin Mersenne attacks Fludd at length in Quaestiones Celeberrimae in Genesim, Paris 1623, accusing him of magic; at Mersenne's request Pierre Gassendi publishes Examen Philosophiae Fluddanae, Paris 1630, against Fludd's neo-Platonic syncretism **[A: Wikipedia for both; neither polemic was read, so both are carried as titles]**. Fludd answers in 1629 with Sophiae cum Moria Certamen under his own name and Summum Bonum under the pseudonym Joachim Frizius, both Frankfurt **[A: Wikipedia]**.

Philosophia Sacra et vere Christiana, seu Meteorologia Cosmica, Frankfurt 1626, is dedicated to John Williams **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the pool styled Williams "Archbishop of York". In 1626 he was Bishop of Lincoln and Dean of Westminster; York came only in 1641 **[A: Wikipedia, "John Williams"]**.

The five-part Medicina Catholica is published at Frankfurt 1629-1631 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

*Coordinate note.* Two Paris pins were in play, 48.8558 / 2.3665 (chosen by the geography lens to mark the Minim convent quarter) and 48.8566 / 2.3522 (already canonical in the atlas through the christian_rosenkreuz file's 1622 Paris placard episode). The journey file inherits the canonical pin byte-for-byte.

## 8. London: the weapon-salve, the last years, 1623-1637

**Correction, substantial.** The pool stated that Fludd "publishes a response to Patrick Scot's Tillage of Light" in 1623 **[originally A]**. Wikipedia says only that he "wrote against" Scot's book, and he did not print it: the reply survives as a manuscript at Trinity College, Cambridge, first put into print by Allen G. Debus as *Robert Fludd and his Philosophicall Key* (1979) **[A]**. The journey file records the writing, not a publication.

In 1630 Fludd proposes perpetual motion machines **[A: Wikipedia]**; see section 5 for the redating of the water-screw design to 1618.

In 1631 he publishes Doctor Fludds Answer vnto M. Foster in London, defending the weapon-salve, a sympathetic remedy applied to the weapon rather than the wound, against William Foster's Hoplocrisma-Spongus **[A: Wikipedia]**.

He dies in London on 8 September 1637, aged 63 **[A: Wikipedia]**, and is buried in Holy Cross Church, Bearsted **[A: Wikipedia]**; the DNB specifies the chancel and a monument erected 10 August 1638 **[A: DNB]**.

*Coordinate finding.* Three pool entries gave three different points for the burial church: 51.2667 / 0.6167, 51.2711 / 0.5696, and 51.2738 / 0.5789 (the village centre, with a false note claiming no separate church coordinate exists). Nominatim resolves "Holy Cross Church, Church Lane, Bearsted, Maidstone, Kent ME14 4EE" to **51.2702 / 0.5805**, which the journey file uses; Wikipedia places the church on Church Lane south of the green.

An early thermometer or vertical thermoscope design is sometimes credited to him with a date of 1638 **[R: New World Encyclopedia]**, which postdates his death by a year. Carried as reconstruction only, and given no stop in the journey.

## 9. Posthumous publication and modern rediscovery

Philosophia Moysaica appears at Gouda in 1638 with a Latin edition of the weapon-salve defence, Responsum ad Hoplocrisma-Spongum, and the folios are collected into six volumes at Frankfurt the same year **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the appended Clavis Philosophiae et Alchimiae Fluddanae is a separate Frankfurt publication of **1633**, not written or issued in 1638; Wikipedia and the DNB agree. An English Mosaicall Philosophy appears in London in 1659 **[A]**.

Many contemporaries label him a magician, and the caricature persists for centuries **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Attribution recovered.** The pool carried the assessment "the great English mystical philosopher of the seventeenth century, a man of immense erudition, of exalted mind, and, to judge by his writings, of extreme personal sanctity" as a possibly contemporary verdict of unverified authorship. Wikipedia credits it to **Arthur Edward Waite, The Real History of the Rosicrucians (1887)** **[A]**: an occult-revivalist judgement written 250 years after Fludd's death, so the "contemporary or near-contemporary" framing is dropped.

Wolfgang Pauli's 1952 essay "The Influence of Archetypal Ideas on the Scientific Theories of Kepler", published by Rascher Verlag in Zurich alongside Jung's synchronicity essay, takes the Kepler-Fludd exchange as its central case **[A: historical record; the atlas's wolfgang_pauli.journey.json describes the essay without naming Fludd, a gap in that file rather than this one]**. Frances Yates analyses Fludd's memory system in The Art of Memory (1966), ch. XVI, and proposes that it may reflect the layout of Shakespeare's Globe **[A: Wikipedia, summarising Yates]**. Huffman and Godwin later reassess Fludd as a figure of Renaissance Christian neo-Platonism rather than a Rosicrucian or a charlatan **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **No text of Fludd's was read.** The Utriusque Cosmi, the Medicina Catholica, the Apologia, and the Answer unto M. Foster are described entirely through Wikipedia and the DNB. Every characterisation of Fludd's argument in this report is therefore second-hand.
2. **No text of his opponents was read either.** Kepler's Harmonice Mundi appendix and Apologia, Mersenne's Quaestiones, and Gassendi's Examen are carried as titles with summarised contents. The pool itself marked the Mersenne and Gassendi entries [R] on that ground.
3. **The continental itinerary has no documents.** Avignon, Marseille, Madrid, Rome, and the German stops are biographical tradition; Marburg in particular is a modern stand-in. Any of these stops could be wrong in a way no consulted source would catch.
4. **The Guise tutorship** rests on the DNB alone, uncorroborated.
5. **A Philosophicall Key** is known here only through the Debus 1979 edition's catalogue record; the Trinity College manuscript was not consulted.
6. **The College admonishments** of 1608 and the "dignus" verdict come from the DNB without the College annals behind them; the reasons for the six rejections are nowhere stated.
7. **The engraver of the Utriusque Cosmi plates** is unresolved. Merian is tradition, not attestation.
8. **Calendar.** England used Old Style dating throughout Fludd's life, and the DNB's "3 Feb. 1596" is almost certainly Old Style. The journey file is labelled gregorian per house convention for a life running past 1582; its ISO dates are not New Style conversions.
