# John Ruskin (1819-1900): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `john_ruskin`. Companion: `john_ruskin.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops.*

Ruskin enters this atlas from the inside out. Before he had a file of his own he was in six others as a cited author: the man Morris read at Oxford, the man Millais painted on a rock and whose wife Millais then married, the man Proust translated, the man who put a book into Gandhi's hands on a night train. **Neither QUEUE.md nor `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` holds an entry for him [A: grep]**.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested with source named; **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim unchecked against a primary text this session. Contradictions are flagged, **not adjudicated**; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

## 1. The walled garden, 1819-1835

- Born **8 February 1819, 54 Hunter Street, Brunswick Square, London**, only child of the sherry merchant John James Ruskin and Margaret Ruskin **[A: Wikipedia, 'John Ruskin']**.
- **1823**, the family moves to **28 Herne Hill** **[A: Wikipedia; Praeterita]**: home tutoring, no toys, free run of a walled garden.
- Boyhood holidays at **Bowerswell, Perth** **[A: Wikipedia]**, dated in the pool only to "1820s-1830s", so folded into the Herne Hill campa.
- **1825**, first Continental tour, **France and Belgium**, entering at **Calais** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Month unrecorded; the journey defaults to May and says so.
- **August 1829**, first publication, the poem **'On Skiddaw and Derwent Water'**, *Spiritual Times* **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **1833**, the long tour: **Strasbourg, Milan, Genoa, Turin** **[A: Wikipedia]**, plus **Schaffhausen** and the first view of the Alps **[A: geography lens, citing Wikipedia and Praeterita]**. Months are reconstructions from the itinerary.
- **1834**, **Thomas Dale's evangelical school, Peckham** **[A: Wikipedia]**. No coordinate in the pool, so folded into the Christ Church campa rather than given an invented pin.
- **1835**, first sight of **Venice** **[A: Wikipedia]**, pinned at Piazza San Marco 45.4341/12.3388.

**Coordinate contradiction, flagged.** The lenses disagree on both London houses: birth house **51.5246/-0.1219** (chronology) against **51.5262/-0.1225** (geography), about 180 m apart; Herne Hill **51.4514/-0.1027** against **51.4530/-0.1027**. Geography figures used, disagreement written into `date_confidence`, neither checked at street level. **Gap:** the Skiddaw poem's place of writing is unrecorded, pinned at Herne Hill as a labelled inference.

## 2. Oxford and Modern Painters, 1836-1846

- **Matriculation Michaelmas 1836; residence at Christ Church from January 1837** **[A: Wikipedia]**. His mother's High Street lodgings and the nightly tea are **[R]**: repeated everywhere, unchecked here.
- **1837-38**, *The Poetry of Architecture* serialised as **Kata Phusin** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Primary text fetched, eight passages confirmed verbatim (Gutenberg 17774).
- **1839**, the **Newdigate Prize** at the third attempt, and **Wordsworth** **[A]**. **1842**, the **honorary double fourth** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **1842**, the **Fontainebleau aspen** **[R]**: told in *Praeterita*, no fixed date. The journey's August date is a labelled reconstruction.
- **1842-1871**, **163 Denmark Hill** as the working base **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **1843**, ***Modern Painters* I**, anonymous, defending Turner **[A]**; **April 1846**, volume II **[A: Wikipedia]**, folded into the marriage campa.
- **1845**, first Continental journey without his parents: **Lucca** (the Ilaria del Carretto tomb), **Pisa**, **Florence** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
  - *Correction applied.* Santa Maria Novella sits at **43.7745/11.2494**, not the pool's 43.7766/11.2486, about 240 m north of the church. Folded into the Lucca campa, so no pin is exposed.

## 3. Marriage, Venice, and the Stones, 1847-1853

- **October 1847** engagement to **Euphemia "Effie" Gray**; **10 April 1848** marriage at **Bowerswell, Perth** **[A: Wikipedia; 'Effie Gray']**.
- **1848 and 1868**, **Abbeville** and Saint-Wulfram **[A: Wikipedia]**, folded into the marriage campa.
- **1848-1852**, **31 Park Street, Mayfair** **[R: WebFetch summary of Effie Gray's residences]**; **1849**, ***The Seven Lamps of Architecture*** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **November 1849**, Venice, the **Hotel Danieli** **[A: WebFetch summary]**; **1849-50**, the survey of the **Doge's Palace** capitals, the **Ca' d'Oro**, **San Marco** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **1851**, letters to **The Times** for the **Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood'; the Millais file, which dates it May 1851 and pins London 51.5074/-0.1278]**. Pin inherited byte-identical.

**Correction applied: *The Stones of Venice*.** The pool dated all three volumes to 1851. Wikipedia's article on the book gives **volume I 1851, volumes II and III both 1853** **[A]**. The journey pins the stop at the opening of the span and states the split in `date_confidence`.

**Internal atlas contradiction, flagged, not adjudicated.** 'The Nature of Gothic' is **volume II, chapter VI** **[A: Wikipedia]**, reprinted by **Morris at Kelmscott, 1892** **[A: `william_morris.journey.json`]**. That same Morris file puts the chapter in **volume III** and dates Morris's Oxford reading to **summer 1853**, while the Burne-Jones file records the date as contested between the Morris file (1853-1856) and the Proust file (1856), already flagged there as unadjudicated. Not settled here: the campa says only that Morris read the chapter at Oxford with Burne-Jones and printed it in 1892.

## 4. Glen Finglas and the annulment, 1853-1858

- **Summer 1853**, the Highland holiday with **Millais** and Effie; the portrait against the gneiss at **Glen Finglas**; Millais falls in love with Effie **[A: Wikipedia, 'John Ruskin'; the Millais file]**. The party lodges at **Brig o' Turk** **[A: same file]**.
- **November 1853**, the **Edinburgh lectures on architecture and painting** **[A: millais file, quoting Ruskin's published lectures]**. Pin inherited, 55.9533/-3.1883.
- **April 1854**, Effie files for annulment on the ground of non-consummation **[R in the pool's own tagging: Wikipedia]**; **July 1854**, granted **[A]**; **1855**, she marries Millais **[A]**. The medical examination and virginity finding are **[R]**: standard in the literature, no document checked.
- **1854-1858**, drawing classes at the **Working Men's College** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Working Men's College']**.

**Correction applied: the college's address and Ruskin's role.** The pool put the college at Great Ormond Street and had Ruskin teaching "alongside Dante Gabriel Rossetti" as though he had brought him in. It was founded **1854 at 31 Red Lion Square**, moving to Great Ormond Street **only in 1857** **[A: Wikipedia]**, so the pin is **51.5197/-0.1183**; and **Rossetti, Brown, Burne-Jones, Munro and Prinsep all taught art there from 1854** **[A]**. The campa says "teach beside him".

**Coordinate contradiction, not resolved.** Geography corrects Glen Finglas to **56.230/-4.362**, calling the pool's 56.2333/-4.4333 wrong by about 4.5 km; the canonical pin, from the Millais file, is **56.24/-4.42**, labelled there "approximate for Trossachs region". Canonical pin inherited, correction written into `date_confidence`.

## 5. Rose, Turin, and the turn to economy, 1857-1868

- **3 January 1858**, first meeting with **Rose La Touche** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Rose La Touche']**.
- **1858**, the **Turin unconversion**: Veronese's *Solomon and the Queen of Sheba* set against a narrow sermon heard the same day in a Waldensian chapel **[R for the narrated episode: WebFetch of Wikipedia's religious-views section; A for a religious crisis at Turin in 1858]**.
- **1860**, *Modern Painters* V; **August to November 1860**, ***Unto This Last*** in the *Cornhill*; **1862**, issued as a volume after hostile reaction **[A: Wikipedia]**. One stop.
- **3 March 1864**, death of **John James Ruskin**, estate reported between roughly **£120,000 and £157,000** **[A: Wikipedia; the range is the source's]**.
- **1859-1868**, **Winnington Hall** **[R: general biography, visit dates unconfirmed]**.

**Correction applied: Rose's age.** The pool made her ten; the cited source quotes Ruskin himself, "Nine years old, on 3 January 1858, thus now rising towards ten." She was **nine**. His own age, a month short of thirty-nine, is right.

**Contradiction inside a single source, flagged.** The same article gives an **1848 birth year** and **age 27 at death in 1875**, which cannot both hold with a nine-year-old in January 1858. Not adjudicated: the campa says plainly that the sources disagree about her birth year, her age at the meeting, and her age at death. Her **cause of death is likewise disputed**.

**Correction applied: Winnington.** The pool tied the Winnington dialogues to *Sesame and Lilies*; they produced ***The Ethics of the Dust* (1866)**, the *Sesame and Lilies* lectures being given at **Manchester in 1864** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Coordinates corrected to **53.2686/-2.5336** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Winnington Hall']**. Also: the Waldensian temple in Turin is at approximately **45.0645/7.6787**, about 1 km off the pool's figure, and the journey merges chapel and gallery into one stop pinned at the gallery. **Gap:** the *place* of the first meeting with Rose is not fixed by the pool, which says only "England", and is pinned at Denmark Hill as a labelled inference.

## 6. The Slade chair, the Guild, and the trial, 1869-1878

- **1869**, **first Slade Professor of Fine Art at Oxford** **[A: Wikipedia]**; **1871-1872**, buys and occupies **Brantwood** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Brantwood']**.
- **1871**, **Fors Clavigera** begins, running to 1884, Letter I dated **1 January 1871** **[A: Wikipedia]**; the **Guild of St George** is founded the same year as St George's Company, renamed and reconstituted 1878 **[A: Wikipedia, 'Guild of St George']**.
- **1875**, **St George's Museum, Walkley, Sheffield** **[A: general record; Wikipedia, 'Guild of St George' for the collection's present home in the Millennium Galleries]**. The April date is a labelled reconstruction.
- **25 May 1875**, **Rose La Touche dies in Dublin at 27** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Rose La Touche']**.
- **2 July 1877**, **Fors Clavigera Letter LXXIX**, the attack on Whistler **[A: verified verbatim, archive.org `forsclavigera04ruskiala`, p. 73]**.
- **1878**, the mental breakdown **[A]**. **25 November 1878**, **Whistler v. Ruskin** in the **Exchequer Division before Baron Huddleston**, jury awarding **one farthing** without costs **[A: Wikipedia, 'Whistler v. Ruskin'; 'Nocturne in Black and Gold' gives 25-26 November]**.

**Corrections applied.** (1) *The drawing school.* The pool folded the **Ruskin School of Drawing** into the 1869 appointment; it was **founded 1871** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ruskin School of Art']**. One stop dated 1869, "two years later" in the campa, the split named in `date_confidence`. (2) *The Guild's founding date.* The pool's "5 January 1871" is supported by nothing reachable: Fors Letter I is dated 1 January, the St George's Fund appeal came later that year. The day is dropped; the pool's Guild coordinates (52.3555/-1.1743) are a generic England centroid, not used. (3) *The trial's location.* The pool's 51.5136/-0.1132 is the **Royal Courts of Justice on the Strand, opened only on 4 December 1882**; in 1878 the superior common-law courts still sat at **Westminster Hall**. Pin changed to **51.4995/-0.1246**. (4) *The Whistler quotation.* The pool's version opened "It is not enough that Whistler's own splashes are known to be picturesque": **that clause is not in Ruskin**, a grep for "splashes" across the volume returning nothing. Deleted; the p. 73 text is used verbatim.

**Contradiction flagged, met by rewording only.** The pool called 1878 "the first of a series of mental breakdowns"; Wikipedia's own article says the first severe bout came **at Matlock, Derbyshire, in 1871**. The campa calls 1878 the first of the *violent delirious* attacks and names Matlock 1871 outright. Which counts as "first" depends on the definition, and the sources are not forced to agree. **Gap:** the place of writing of Letter LXXIX is not established by anything reachable, and is pinned at Brantwood by default, said so in `date_confidence`.

## 7. Praeterita and the far shore, 1879-1900

- **1880-1885**, ***The Bible of Amiens*** **[A: general record]**; **1885-1889**, ***Praeterita*** issued in parts, left unfinished **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **20 January 1900**, dies **at Brantwood**, aged 80 **[A: Wikipedia, 'John Ruskin'; corroborated by 'Coniston, Cumbria']**. Buried **five days later at St Andrew's, Coniston**, **Westminster Abbey declined** **[A]**.
- **1901**, the **Ruskin Museum** opens at Coniston, a Bluebird K7 wing following in **2009** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Coniston, Cumbria']**. Named in the burial campa.
- **Brantwood afterwards** **[A in part: Wikipedia, 'Brantwood']**: the Severn family inherit, **John Howard Whitehouse** acquires it to preserve as a museum, the **Brantwood Trust** is founded in **1951**. **The pool's 1931 (Arthur Severn's death) and its thirty-days-a-year public-access wish appear nowhere in the cited source and are stripped as unsupported.**
- The **Library Edition**, ed. **Cook and Wedderburn** **[R]**: cited throughout modern scholarship, but the usual span 1903-1912 in 39 volumes **could not be confirmed**.

**Correction applied: the two resignations, which the pool had reversed.** Wikipedia: he **resigned in 1879**, forced by declining health and "finally precipitated by the Whistler trial"; he **resumed in 1883 and resigned again in 1884** over **vivisection** **[A]**. The vivisection resignation is the second. A further disagreement: the Slade Professor list ends the second term in 1885, the Ruskin article in 1884; not adjudicated, 1884 used. **Death coordinates corrected**: the pool's 54.3697/-3.0778 is **Coniston village**; Brantwood is **54.3535/-3.0592**, the figure the geography and afterlife lenses already carry.

**Contradiction flagged.** The pool had the *family and executors* declining Westminster Abbey; Wikipedia attributes the refusal to **Ruskin's own wishes** ("according to his wishes"). The campa uses the passive and names the wish; `date_confidence` records the disagreement.

**[R] and a named gap: the memorial cross.** Green Tilberthwaite slate, knotwork, relief panels alluding to his books, commonly attributed to **W. G. Collingwood**. The attribution **could not be checked**: ruskin.org.uk returned 403, the Historic England listing was not reached. Collingwood at Brantwood from 1873 is itself **[A: Wikipedia, 'W. G. Collingwood']**; the carving is not.

**Cut from the journey for length** (geography lens, Wikipedia): **Chamonix** 1842-1863 **[R]**, folded into the *Modern Painters* campa; the **Scaliger tombs** **[A]** and **Assisi** 1874 **[R]**, whose pool pins both need correcting, to **45.4434/10.9985** and **43.0748/12.6053**; *The Elements of Drawing* and *The Political Economy of Art* **[A]**; **Harristown House** **[R]**; **Rouen** **[A]**.

## 8. Where this life crosses the atlas

Named in campa (mutual gaze), from each traveler's own file:

- **william_morris**: 'The Nature of Gothic' read at Oxford, printed at Kelmscott 1892; Venice with Jane, 1876.
- **edward_burne_jones**: reads the chapter with Morris; teaches beside Ruskin at the Working Men's College from 1854.
- **john_everett_millais**: defended in The Times 1851; Glen Finglas 1853; marries Effie 1855.
- **jane_morris**: Venice 1876, "the city out of whose stones Ruskin built the book that converted her husband".
- **marcel_proust**: the Danieli, May 1900; translates *La Bible d'Amiens* 1904, *Sésame et les lys* 1906.
- **mahatma_gandhi**: handed *Unto This Last* for the night train to Durban, January 1904; founds Phoenix.

**Absence noted, not treated as evidence.** Ruskin appears nowhere in the Christina Rossetti, Holman Hunt, May Morris, or Bessie Burden files **[R]**, though all four sit inside the same circles: a gap in atlas coverage, not a fact about their lives.

**Pins inherited byte-identical:** London 51.5074/-0.1278, Bowerswell 56.404/-3.44, Glen Finglas 56.24/-4.42, Edinburgh 55.9533/-3.1883, Venice/Danieli 45.4336/12.3411, Durban -29.8587/31.0218. Two, **Bowerswell and Glen Finglas, are wrong by this dossier's own checking** (56.396/-3.416 and 56.230/-4.362), and are inherited anyway with the discrepancy written into each stop's `date_confidence`. A deliberate choice for atlas consistency; it should be corrected across all affected files at once, not silently here.

## 9. Quotations: verified and unverified

**Verified verbatim against primary text, used.** Eight passages from ***The Poetry of Architecture*** (1837-38), Gutenberg 17774 **[A]**. ***The Two Paths*** (1859), "Then FINE ART is that in which the hand, the head, and the _heart_ of man go together." **[A]**, **citation corrected** to **Lecture II, 'The Unity of Art'** (Manchester, 14 March 1859), not Lecture III: Gutenberg 7291, lines 1221-1222. ***Lectures on Art*** (1870), "life without industry is guilt, and industry without art is brutality...", **citation corrected** to **Lecture III, 'The Relation of Art to Morals', section 95**, not Lecture II: Gutenberg 19164, line 2379. Both tags raised R to A on the corrected citations. ***Fors Clavigera*, Letter LXXIX** (July 1877) **[A]**, archive.org `forsclavigera04ruskiala`, p. 73.

**Used with the attribution hedged inside `quote_source`, primary text unreached.** "When we build, let us think that we build for ever.", attributed to *The Seven Lamps of Architecture*, 'The Lamp of Memory' **[R]**: fetches returned truncated or wrong documents. "You must either make a tool of the creature, or a man of him. You cannot make both.", attributed to *The Stones of Venice* II.vi **[R]**: Gutenberg, Wikisource, and mirrors failed or served earlier chapters only. The single most consequential sentence in Ruskin's afterlife, and **not** verified here. "I do not claim to know English, I claim to know Ruskin.", attributed to Proust, from `marcel_proust.journey.json` **[R]**. Two further pool quotations, given to *Stones of Venice* and *Modern Painters* III, were left off the stops: neither was located in primary text, and for the second the volume was fetched (Gutenberg 38923) without the phrase in it **[R]**.
