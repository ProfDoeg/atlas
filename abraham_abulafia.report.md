# Abraham ben Samuel Abulafia (1240 - after 1291): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-28 for `abraham_abulafia.journey.json`. Subject: the Aragonese founder of prophetic (ecstatic) Kabbalah, the letter-permutation technique (tzeruf), and the only Jew on record to have set out to convert a reigning pope.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Coordinates are the ones written into the journey file.

The journey file carries **33 stops in 8 segments**, calendar `julian`, register `national mythology: the canon is true`. Six stops carry verbatim quotes.

---
## 0. Identity, and a surname that is not a lineage

Several unrelated or distantly related medieval families bear the name Abulafia. `moses_de_leon.journey.json` documents a Toledo patron, **Joseph ben Todros ha-Levi Abulafia**, kin to the poet-courtier Todros Abulafia and to Meir ha-Levi Abulafia the Talmudist **[A: file-stated, "Toledo, the patron in the house of Abulafia," 1285]**, and already flags that no secure pedigree joins the Toledo Abulafias to Abraham of Zaragoza. Treated here as a **disambiguation flag, not a connection**. The birth stop states the collision and claims no kinship.

---

## 1. Zaragoza and Tudela, 1240 to 1258

- Born at **Zaragoza** in 1240, the Hebrew year 5000 **[A: Encyclopaedia Judaica via Encyclopedia.com; English Wikipedia]**. No month, day, quarter or house.
- Family moves to **Tudela**, Navarre, in his childhood **[A: Encyclopedia.com]**, undated; the stop's 1248 date is an ordering device and says so.
- His father **Samuel**, who taught him Bible and Talmud, dies when he is eighteen, so 1258 **[A: Encyclopedia.com]**.

**Gaps.** The mother is never named. No teacher but Samuel is named for the childhood. No grave site. The two years between the death and the departure east are blank.

## 2. The road to the Sambation, 1260

- At twenty he leaves Iberia for the Land of Israel, seeking the **Sambation** and the Ten Lost Tribes **[A: Encyclopedia.com; English Wikipedia]**. The Sambation is legendary travel lore, not a geographic river; no evidence he found it or any Lost Tribe **[R]**.
- **The port is not stated in any source consulted.** Barcelona is used as the plausible Aragonese departure point, marked as inference, at the canonical pin 41.3851, 2.1734 the atlas already uses for Ramon Llull.
- He reaches **Acre** and goes no further **[A: English Wikipedia, "reached only Akko before returning to Europe"]**. **Correction applied:** the pool attributed the turn-back to the Mongol-Mamluk war decided at Ayn Jalut, and **no fetched source makes that attribution**. The war is in the campa as context, not as the reason.
- Returning through **Greece**, he marries; the wife's name and later fate unrecorded **[R]**. **Retag applied:** the pool carried this **[A]** citing the Jewish Encyclopedia and the Jewish Virtual Library, and it is in **neither**; it rests on one line in the Encyclopaedia Judaica notice. Carried **[R]** at a central-Greek placeholder (38.5, 23.0) labelled as such.

## 3. Capua and the Guide of the Perplexed, early 1260s

- At **Capua** he studies philosophy and Maimonides' *Guide of the Perplexed* under a teacher named **Hillel** **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Jewish Encyclopedia]**.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** Encyclopaedia Judaica identifies him flatly as "R. Hillel of Verona"; the Jewish Encyclopedia calls him only "a philosopher and physician named Hillel." The file names Hillel, puts Verona in `suggested_refs` as the Encyclopaedia Judaica identification, and does not assert it in the campa.
- **The date 1262 is reconstruction.** Encyclopaedia Judaica gives only "the early 1260s."

This is the most consequential episode of his formation and the reason **Maimonides** is named in the campa. Abulafia writes **three** commentaries on the *Guide* over twenty years **[A: Encyclopedia.com]**: *Sefer ha-Ge'ullah* (1273), *Hayyei ha-Nefesh* (undated), *Sitrei Torah* (1280).

## 4. Barcelona, c. 1270-71

- At about thirty-one he takes up a Kabbalah centred on *Sefer Yetzirah* and receives a revelation with messianic weight **[A: Encyclopedia.com; English Wikipedia]**.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** Encyclopaedia Judaica dates it **1270**; Wikipedia's "at the age of thirty-one" computes to **1271**. Both circulate; neither is preferred.
- **Baruch Togarmi.** Second contradiction, also left standing. Encyclopaedia Judaica says he "studied under Baruch Togarmi," which is personal instruction; English Wikipedia says only that Togarmi was the "most important representative" of that Kabbalah, which is textual influence. The pool's first pass called personal contact "unestablished," understating Encyclopaedia Judaica.
- **The site within Barcelona is undocumented.** No synagogue, no house.
- *Sefer ha-Ge'ullah*, 1273 **[A for the year: Encyclopedia.com, English Wikipedia, Sefaria]**. **The city of composition is given nowhere**; placed at Barcelona and declared unlocated inference.

## 5. Castile, mid-1270s

- He teaches in Castile; pupils named are **Joseph Gikatilla** and **Moses ben Simeon of Burgos** **[A]**. **Upgrade applied:** the pool had this [R], an Idel reconstruction, but Encyclopaedia Judaica, English Wikipedia and the Jewish Encyclopedia all state it outright, the last two calling Gikatilla "his most important disciple." **The residual gap is in the campa:** Gikatilla's own surviving works do not name Abulafia as teacher, so the claim runs one way.
- **No city is fixed.** A central-Castile placeholder, 40.0, -4.0, declared as such.
- *Hayyei ha-Nefesh*, the second commentary, is **undated and unplaced** in every source consulted. It gets a stop only so the three commentaries appear in series, with date and place both declared ordering devices.

## 6. Greece again, 1279

- At **Patras** he begins *Sefer ha-Yashar*, first of the self-declared **prophetic books** **[A: Jewish Encyclopedia, "in 1279 wrote at Patras, in Greece, the first of his prophetic books"; corroborated by English Wikipedia and the Jewish Virtual Library]**.
- **Dropped: Thebes.** The pool carried teaching in Greek cities "including Thebes and Patras" in 1275. **Thebes is in none of the fetched sources** and the 1275 date is unsourced. No stop written; recorded so the deletion is visible.
- **Every prophetic book is lost** except *Sefer ha-Ot*. What survives is titles and his own back-references.
- He returns through **Trani** to Capua **[A: English Wikipedia]**.

The pool had it in a `quote` field; removed, and the campa now says the phrase circulates in modern retellings only.
- *Sitrei Torah*, third and largest commentary on the *Guide*, 1280 **[A: Encyclopedia.com; Sefaria]**.
- *Hayyei ha-Olam ha-Ba*, the manual of the seventy-two-letter Name, 1280 **[A: English Wikipedia; Sefaria]**. Place not given; Capua is inference from the same year's teaching circle.

## 8. Rome and Soriano, 1280: the pope who died first

Best-documented episode of his life, and the one where the sources most visibly disagree.

- **Purpose [A].** English Wikipedia: an attempt "to convert Pope Nicholas III to Judaism." Jewish Encyclopedia: "in order to effect the conversion of Pope Nicholas III." The pool's softer framing, a confrontation over the meaning of redemption, understated it.
- **The burning order [A, single-channel].** Wikipedia's *Pope Nicholas III*, quoting Abulafia's biography: the pope "issued orders to burn the fanatic as soon as he reached Soriano," and "the stake was erected in preparation close to the **inner** gate." The pool said outer gate. Caveat: this reaches us through Abulafia's own retrospective account and later retellings, with no chancery document behind it.
- **The pope's death [A].** Nicholas III died on the night of **22 August 1280** at Castro Soriano, diocese of Viterbo. Cause given as apoplectic stroke in some accounts, heart attack in others; the file carries both.
- **Coordinate correction applied.** The pool gave 42.4167, 12.2833 and elsewhere 42.4172, 12.2969, both 4 to 5 km east of the town in open country. **The file uses 42.4194, 12.2342**, per Wikipedia's 42°25'10"N 12°14'3"E.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** Wikipedia's *Pope Nicholas III* has him arrive **22 August**, learning the pope had died "during the preceding night." Wikipedia's *Abraham Abulafia* has him go "on the day before Rosh Hashanah," and 1 Tishrei 5041 = 27 August 1280 Julian, so the eve is **26 August**, four days after the death. Both are in `date_confidence`; neither is preferred.
- **The detention [A].** Held by the **Franciscan Minorites for four weeks**, per both the Jewish Encyclopedia and English Wikipedia. The pool's "roughly two to four weeks" is a hedge the sources do not require.
- **Gap.** No trial, no charge, no sentence, and **no source names which Franciscan house held him**. The file pins the detention to the Rome city coordinate and states the site is unlocated; the pool's 41.8931, 12.4828 implied a known prison and was not used.

## 9. Sicily, 1281-1291

- Relocates to **Sicily**, active at **Messina** through the 1280s **[A: Encyclopedia.com; English Wikipedia]**.
- **Prophet, messiah, and in some formulations "son of God" [A: English Wikipedia]**, attached by that source to **Messina**, 1281-1291. **Correction applied:** the pool staged this at **Palermo** and Wikipedia does not. Palermo appears in the sources only as the community that **appealed against him**.
- **The Rashba's letter [A].** Palermo's leaders appeal to **Solomon ben Abraham ibn Adret** of Barcelona, who writes a letter "which most energetically condemned Abulafia's conduct" (Jewish Encyclopedia), preserved as responsum 1:548.
  - **Quotes removed.** "Villain and liar" and "closed the door," carried in the pool as quoted text, appear in **neither** cited entry, and responsum 1:548 itself was not fetched. No quotation from the Rashba appears in the file.
  - **Stop ownership.** The pool proposed a Barcelona stop for Ibn Adret's counter-campaign. **Abulafia was not in Barcelona in 1285.** That is Ibn Adret's action at Ibn Adret's location, so it is carried as the content of the Palermo stop and as a `suggested_ref` naming the responsum, with no Barcelona scene staged.
- **His reply**, *Ve-Zot li-Yehudah* **[A: English Wikipedia]**. Undated and unplaced; the file's date and coordinate are declared ordering devices.
- *Otzar Eden Ganuz* **[A]**, dated **1285/6** by both Encyclopaedia Judaica and English Wikipedia. Upgraded from the pool's [R] at a bare 1285.
- *Or ha-Sekhel* **[R]**. The pool had this [A], dated 1283, dedicated to a pupil called "Nathan the Wise." **Wikipedia lists it among his handbooks with no date, no dedicatee and no city**, and the dedication is in none of the fetched sources. Retagged [R], all three declared unattested.
- *Mafteah ha-Torah*, **1289 [A]**, listed under that year by both Encyclopaedia Judaica and English Wikipedia. Upgraded from [R]. **The place is named by nobody**; Messina is inference.
- **Sha'arei Tzedeq / Nathan ben Sa'adyah Harar [R], excluded.** The attribution to Harar is attested (English Wikipedia), but "before 1290" and the Messina location are in **no** fetched source, and Idel's reconstruction commonly puts the work at **c. 1295**, after Abulafia's disappearance. It would then postdate the subject, and it is Harar's own text in any case. **No stop written**; recorded rather than dropped silently.

## 10. Comino, and the silence after 1291

- **Comino [A]**, where he compiles *Sefer ha-Ot* in distress. **Date range corrected:** both the Jewish Encyclopedia and English Wikipedia give **1285-88** for the composition, and Wikipedia's *Comino* article extends the residence to "from 1285 until some time after 1290." The pool's flat 1285 was too narrow. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's 36.0, 14.3333 and 36.0058, 14.3333 both fall in open sea south-west of the island; **the file uses 36.0114, 14.3367**.
- **Contradiction, not resolved.** The Comino residence (1285 to after 1290) **overlaps** the attested Messina activity (1281-1291), and no source explains how he divided the years. The file lets the overlap stand visibly, interleaving the two segments in date order.
- ***Imrei Shefer*, 1291 [A for the year]**. **Place corrected:** the pool placed it in Sicily by inference; Wikipedia's *Comino* article contradicts that directly, "It was on Comino that Abulafia composed his *Sefer ha-Ot* ... and his last work, *Imre Shefer*."
- **Death unknown [R].** No death notice, grave, burial record or contemporary report in any source consulted. "Died on Comino" is shorthand for the last place he can be proved to have been. English Wikipedia: after *Imrei Shefer*, "all trace of him is lost." The final stop's 1292 date closes the file and says so.
- **The 2003 Maltese reburial [R, unverified].** Circulates in popular and tourist accounts, unconfirmed against English Wikipedia, jewishencyclopedia.com or jewishvirtuallibrary.org, all of which give his death and burial as unknown. **No stop written.** A modern commemorative act is not an archaeological identification of a grave, and would in any case belong to whoever performed it.

## 11. The corpus, and the numbers people quote for it

**Roughly thirty works survive**, whole or in fragments **[A: English Wikipedia, "only thirty survive"]**. **His own count was twenty-six**, of which he said **twenty-two were prophetic** **[A: Jewish Encyclopedia]**. **Encyclopaedia Judaica says "several dozen books"** for the activity of 1271-91. **"Nearly fifty" is dropped**: the pool carried it as a hard figure and it is in no fetched source. Twenty-two is also the count of the Hebrew alphabet, the substrate of his whole technique; the file states the coincidence and does not interpret it.

## 12. Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. Barcelona: **1270** (Encyclopaedia Judaica) vs **1271** (Wikipedia's "age thirty-one").
2. Togarmi: **personal teacher** (Encyclopaedia Judaica) vs **textual influence only** (Wikipedia).
3. Rabbi Hillel: **"Hillel of Verona"** (Encyclopaedia Judaica) vs **an unidentified physician named Hillel** (Jewish Encyclopedia).
4. Soriano arrival: **22 August 1280** vs **26 August 1280**, the eve of Rosh Hashanah, straddling the pope's death. Both from Wikipedia, in different articles.
5. Nicholas III's cause of death: **apoplectic stroke** vs **heart attack**.
6. Detention: **four weeks** (Jewish Encyclopedia, Wikipedia) vs **two weeks** (one modern retelling).
7. **Comino 1285 to after 1290** vs **continuous Messina activity 1281-1291**. Both attested, neither reconciled.
8. *Imrei Shefer* 1291: **Comino** (Wikipedia) vs **Sicily** (inference in the project's earlier dossier).
9. Corpus size: **c. 30 surviving** / **26 by his own count** / **"several dozen" composed**.

## 13. Honest gaps

The mother, never named. The wife, never named, her fate after the 1260s unrecorded, absent from all thirty surviving books. The port he sailed from in 1260. The four Capua students, unnamed. The Franciscan house in Rome, unidentified. The composition cities of *Sefer ha-Ge'ullah*, *Hayyei ha-Nefesh*, *Ve-Zot li-Yehudah*, *Or ha-Sekhel*, *Otzar Eden Ganuz* and *Mafteah ha-Torah*, six works with no place for any, each stop declaring its coordinate as inference. *Hayyei ha-Nefesh* has neither date nor place. What he said, if anything, when 1290 passed. Death, date, cause, grave. And the prophetic books, all lost but one, so the largest category of his output cannot be read at all.

## 14. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Abraham Abulafia is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** Per the standing instruction the following were found and **kept for the operator's decision rather than silently deleted**. **Direction caught: FORWARD in every case**, which `EXCEPTIONS.md` does not license in general and never by an agent's own judgement. **None were written into any campa.** Some appear in `sources` or `suggested_refs` where the later engagement documents an object Abulafia himself made, which the standing rule does permit.

1. **Pico della Mirandola and Flavius Mithridates, c. 1486.** English Wikipedia states that Abulafia's treatises were "translated into Latin and Italian in the circle of Giovanni Pico della Mirandola, mostly by Flavius Mithridates, and Pico's vision of Kabbalah was significantly influenced by his views." `giovanni_pico_della_mirandola.journey.json` carries Mithridates translating Kabbalah at Perugia and Fratta in 1486 without naming Abulafia. Strongest reception link in the dossier, and it is **Pico's stop, on Pico's file**. Place correction for whoever writes it up: Wikipedia's *Flavius Mithridates* puts the work at **Fratta near Perugia**, not Rome, where the pool's afterlife lens had it.
2. **Giulio Camillo's Theatro (Venice, 1519-1544)** and **Reuchlin's *De arte cabalistica* (1517)**, both downstream of Pico by their own files' cross-references. Two steps removed; neither is shown citing Abulafia by name.
3. **Umberto Eco, *Foucault's Pendulum* (1988)**, where Belbo's computer is named **Abulafia** and runs a program that permutes text at random **[A: English Wikipedia]**. Eco's artifact, not his, and the best-known modern icon linking Abulafia to computation.
4. **Borges**, "Una vindicación de la Cábala" (1932) and "El Golem" (1958), and **Xul Solar**, the kabbalah paintings and neocriollo/panlengua. Both files state the letter-combinatorial material; neither names Abulafia.
5. **Nick Land**, the Warwick numogram (1995) and the 2025 Shanghai stops grounding its alphanumeric method in the Pico and Bruno hermetic-Kabbalah lineage via Frances Yates. Abulafia is not named there. Land is living and already on the `EXCEPTIONS.md` list for his own apparatus, which may make his file the right home for the "letter-permutation read as proto-computational" thread.
6. **Gershom Scholem**, *Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism* (1941), most credited with restoring Abulafia to standing after Graetz **[A: English Wikipedia]**. Upgraded from the pool's [R]. The lectures behind it were given in **New York** in 1938, so the pool's Jerusalem pin is a choice, not a given.
7. **Chaim Wirszubski's discovery** that a Mithridates Latin translation preserves an Abulafia work more fully than the fragmentary Hebrew **[R]**. Its sole cited source, a seforimblog post, **returns HTTP 404**; unverified.
8. **David Aronson, "Abraham Abulafia," 1962**, Worcester Art Museum object 19661 **[A: Worcester eMuseum]**. Aronson's object at Aronson's museum. Coordinate correction: 55 Salisbury Street, **42.2731, -71.8022**; the pool's is 1.16 km off.
9. **Jellinek's Leipzig printings (1853-1887)**, the **Gross Jerusalem edition (chiefly 1999-2004)**, and the **Vatican holdings** (Mithridates' autograph Latin at Vat. MSS 189-191, confirmed as "thirty-eight fragments" by Wikipedia's *Flavius Mithridates*; Vat. ebr. 597 confirmed by the Center for Jewish Art; **Chigi A.VI.190 and the *Mafteah ha-Re'ayon* claim unverified**, resting only on the dead seforimblog link). These document **Abulafia's own texts**, so they are cited as `suggested_refs` on the stops for the works themselves and **not** staged as scenes at Leipzig, Jerusalem or the Vatican.

**No backward apparatus relation was found.** His engagement with the past (Maimonides, *Sefer Yetzirah*, the Torah) is ordinary citation inside his own biography.

## 15. Interlocks

**Named in a campa (2):**

- **`maimonides`.** Direct and load-bearing: Abulafia studied the *Guide* at Capua in the early 1260s and wrote three commentaries on it over twenty years. `maimonides.journey.json` carries the *Guide*'s completion at Fustat, 1190. Named at the Capua study stop and again at *Sefer ha-Ge'ullah*.
- **`moses_de_leon`.** Indirect, but through a person and not a place. `moses_de_leon.journey.json` states that **Joseph Gikatilla** studied directly under Abulafia and built his early work from Abulafia's letter-permutation and gematria before diverging toward sefirotic theosophy, and that Gikatilla was de Leon's chief kabbalistic interlocutor in Castile. The shared pupil is the link. Named once, in the Castile stop, phrased so the relation runs Abulafia to Gikatilla to de Leon's circle.

**Considered and declined (shared geography or period only, per the house rule):**

- **`benjamin_de_tudela`.** Same home town, and an itinerary a century earlier through Barcelona, Rome, the Apulian coast, Acre and Palermo, almost exactly Abulafia's adult map. **No contact is possible and none is claimed.** The Kaaba pin (21.4225, 39.8262) is **not inherited**: Abulafia never went to Mecca and the offered connection was genre-level only.
- **`ramon_llull`.** Contemporary, same western Mediterranean, structurally similar combinatorial art of rotating alphabetic wheels in the 1270s and 1280s. **No document places the two in contact or shows either aware of the other.** The most tempting parallel in the dossier and exactly what the house rule forbids; worth a researcher's follow-up, not asserted. Llull's canonical Barcelona pin (41.3851, 2.1734) **is** inherited byte-identical for Abulafia's three Barcelona stops.
- **`nicholas_of_cusa`**, **`juan_eduardo_cirlot`.** Thematic resonance only, both later, neither naming him.
