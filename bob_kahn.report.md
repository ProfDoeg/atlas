# Robert Elliot Kahn (b. 1938): research report for the Atlas of Journeys

*Compiled 2026-08-13 from the verified pool for slug `bob_kahn`. Companion: `bob_kahn.journey.json`, 42 stops in 9 segments.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same clause. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference, including anything the research pass could not confirm against a source it reached. Where sources disagree, both readings are printed and neither adopted. Where a fact is missing, the gap is named and left open.

**A structural note.** Kahn is alive. The atlas's closing lens, the afterlife (tomb, cult, posthumous legend), has no object here. Everything that would fill it is living-honoree recognition: medals handed to a man who walked up and took them. The journey ends not at a tomb but at an office in Reston he has not vacated in almost four decades, and at a 2024 medal whose ceremony city no source names.

---

## 1. Brooklyn and the schools, 1938 to 1964

**Born 23 December 1938 in Brooklyn, New York, to Lawrence and Beatrice Pauline Kahn, an Ashkenazi Jewish family. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]** No source names the hospital or the neighborhood; the journey file pins the birth on the Brooklyn centroid (40.6782, -73.9442) and says so. **Bachelor's degree in electrical engineering, City College of New York, 1960. [A, same source]** The Convent Avenue campus in Harlem (40.82, -73.9493), to which he returns forty-five years later for the Townsend Harris Medal.

**Bell Telephone Laboratories, member of technical staff. [A as employment, CNRI institutional biography, cnri.reston.va.us/bios/kahn.html, which states he "worked on the Technical Staff at Bell Laboratories"] [R as to date and site]** The pool's chronology lens dated this 1964 and tagged the whole thing [R]; both halves are wrong in opposite directions. The employment is better attested than [R] implies, CNRI being a primary institutional source that says it plainly. The date is worse: 1964 is the year of the Princeton doctorate, and CNRI places Bell Labs *before* the MIT professorship, which precedes the 1966 move to BBN. The geography lens reconstructs c. 1960 to 1962, the only reading consistent with the sequence. **The site is unconfirmed. [R]** Murray Hill (40.687, -74.3986) is used and flagged; Holmdel and Whippany are equally available and no source chooses.

**Master of Arts 1962 and PhD 1964, electrical engineering, Princeton, advised by Bede Liu; dissertation "Some problems in the sampling and modulation of signals." [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]** Engineering Quadrangle (40.348, -74.6555).

## 2. MIT and BBN, 1964-1972

**Assistant Professor of Electrical Engineering, MIT. [A as appointment, CNRI institutional biography, which states he "became an Assistant Professor of Electrical Engineering at MIT"] [R as to dates]** Both pool lenses tagged this [R], one dating it 1966 and one c. 1964 to 1966; both under-claim the appointment. CNRI settles the fact. Standard accounts have him at MIT from roughly 1964, taking leave in 1966 for BBN, so 1966 is a departure year. The journey file dates the appointment 1964-09-01 and marks the year as reconstruction. MIT main campus, 42.3593, -71.0935, confirmed by the pool.

**Bolt Beranek and Newman, Cambridge; principal designer on the ARPANET, author of much of the technical proposal that wins BBN the Interface Message Processor contract. [A as role, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"] [R as to the 1966 date]** The pool tagged a date [A] to a source that does not contain it: Wikipedia's Career section says only "Kahn first worked at Bolt Beranek and Newman Inc." and gives no year. The 1966 figure comes from elsewhere and is carried as reconstruction.

**Address, corrected.** The pool's geography lens gave "50 Moulton Street" at 42.3875, -71.119. BBN's Cambridge headquarters, the IMP building, was **10 Moulton Street**, and the atlas holds a pin for it: `vint_cerf.journey.json`, stop "Cambridge, the working group at BBN", at **42.3906, -71.1418**. The journey file inherits that pair byte-identical and discards the pool's coordinates.

**A cross-file gap, stated.** `vint_cerf.journey.json` describes BBN as host of the Network Working Group but **does not name Kahn at that stop at all**, and Kahn's 1966 to 1972 tenure is general biography, not sourced inside that file. The coordinate is inherited; the tenure is not.

## 3. The first nodes and the demonstration, 1969 to 1972

**First ARPANET node at UCLA, September 1969; Kahn works on system architecture and meets Vint Cerf there. [A, Wikipedia "Vint Cerf" and "Bob Kahn"]** The journey file uses the canonical Boelter Hall pin **34.0689, -118.4452**, not the pool's -118.4432. **Second node at SRI International, Menlo Park, 29 October 1969, first host-to-host message from UCLA. [A, Wikipedia "History of the Internet"]** Canonical pin 37.453, -122.1817.

**International Conference on Computer Communications, Washington, 24 to 26 October 1972: Kahn organizes the first public ARPANET demonstration. [A, Wikipedia "International Conference on Computer Communications" for date, venue, and Kahn's organizing role with Larry Roberts as conference chair; also Wikipedia "Bob Kahn" and the Internet Hall of Fame]** Two pool corrections carried. (a) The geography lens wrote "some forty terminals and computers"; the chronology lens, Wikipedia, and the Internet Hall of Fame all say **20 computers**. Twenty is used. (b) The geography lens hedged the Washington Hilton as [R]; the ICCC article confirms it, upgrading it to [A]. The pool's 38.9207, -77.0431 falls roughly 500 m northeast of the hotel; the journey file uses **38.9169, -77.0455**.

## 4. DARPA and the protocol, 1972 to 1978

**Kahn leaves BBN for DARPA's IPTO, 1972; on SATNET he develops the initial concepts of the Transmission Control Protocol. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]** The canonical DARPA Arlington pin **38.8951, -77.0722** is used, not the pool's 38.8956, -77.0715. **He brings Cerf the internetworking problem directly, 1972 to 1973. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"; `vint_cerf.journey.json`, stop "Stanford, the assistant professorship", which reads "Robert Kahn brings him the problem directly from DARPA."]**

**The Palo Alto hotel session, 1973. [R]** Tradition places an early Kahn and Cerf working session in a Bay Area hotel, sketching the layered architecture that will split into TCP and IP. The pool could not confirm the anecdote against a primary source, and `vint_cerf.journey.json` flags its own coordinate for the same stop as disputed. **A three-way coordinate contradiction, flagged and not silently fixed:** the geography lens gives 37.4177, -122.1255, naming the Hyatt Cabana; its own note places the Hyatt Cabana at 4290 El Camino Real, roughly 37.405, -122.115, with the search budget exhausted before that address could be re-fetched; the atlas pin is 37.4075, -122.125. The journey file inherits the atlas pin and records all three readings.

**International Network Working Group, University of Sussex, September 1973. [A for the meeting, R for Kahn's personal attendance]** The pool is explicit that Kahn's presence, as distinct from Cerf's, is unconfirmed. Atlas pin 50.8664, -0.0866 inherited over the pool's 50.8674, -0.0879.

**"A Protocol for Packet Network Intercommunication," Cerf and Kahn, IEEE Transactions on Communications COM-22 no. 5, May 1974, pp. 637 to 648. [A, PDF fetched and read directly during the research pass]** Verbatim passages available:

- "It is thus apparent that the interface between networks must play a central role in the development of any network interconnection strategy. We give a special name to this interface that performs these functions and call it a GATEWAY." (p. 638; used in the journey file.)
- Also verified verbatim: the abstract's opening sentence on sharing resources across different packet switching networks; the p. 637 definition of such a network as hosts, packet switches, and interconnecting media; and the p. 638 statement that the unacceptable alternative is for every host to implement every protocol.

**One pool quotation was transcribed short and is corrected.** The pool gives "The GATEWAY does not modify the text and merely forwards the check sum along without recomputing it." The paper (p. 639) reads **"The GATEWAY does not modify the text and merely forwards the check sum along without computing or recomputing it."** Three words were dropped.

**RFC 675, "Specification of Internet Transmission Control Program," Cerf, Dalal, and Sunshine, December 1974: Kahn named in the acknowledgments. [A, rfc-editor.org, fetched]** A documentary trace, not an utterance of his. **TCP is split into TCP and IP, 1978. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]** No location attested; the journey file places it at the DARPA pin and calls the placement administrative, not ceremonial.

## 5. The three-network demonstration, 1977

**The demonstration itself. [A, Wikipedia "History of the Internet": "On November 22, 1977, a three network demonstration was conducted including the ARPANET, the SRI's Packet Radio Van on the Packet Radio Network and the Atlantic Packet Satellite Network (SATNET) including a node at UCL."]** **University College London as the far leg. [A, same source]** Canonical pin 51.5246, -0.134; the pool gives -0.1339, and the canonical pair is used.

**What is NOT attested, and what the pool wrongly presented as attested.** The geography lens ran the confirmed core and several unconfirmed specifics under a single [A]. All downgraded here: the Bayshore Freeway as the van's route, widely repeated but not in the cited source; the figure of "roughly ninety-four thousand miles round trip without losing a bit," traditional and in no source reached; Goonhilly Downs as the SATNET earth station, common in secondary retellings and unconfirmed against a primary technical account; and USC's Information Sciences Institute as the return leg, same status.

**Two coordinate notes.** The pool's packet-radio-van entry duplicates the fixed SRI coordinate, a stationary pin standing for a moving vehicle; the atlas resolves this with a separate stop, "The Bayshore Freeway, the van with a radio," at **37.42, -122.1**, which is used for the van while SRI keeps the building. For USC ISI the pool gives 33.9803, -118.4517; the atlas's canonical Marina del Rey pin, used twice in `vint_cerf.journey.json` for ICANN at 4676 Admiralty Way, is **33.98, -118.44**, the same address block, and is inherited with the reuse stated in the stop.

## 6. The directorship, 1979 to 1986

**Director of IPTO at DARPA, 1979. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]** **Strategic Computing Initiative. [A as his initiative, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"] [R as to 1983]** The pool's chronology lens welded two events onto one 1979 date. SCI launched in 1983 and ran to about 1993; the pool flags that the search budget was exhausted before 1983 could be re-fetched, so it is carried as high confidence but unrefetched. The journey file splits them into stops at 1979 and 1983 and prints the caveat.

[A as event] [R as to Kahn's presence]** `vint_cerf.journey.json` states the cutover is administered from DARPA Arlington with no ceremonial site documented and does not attest Kahn's presence. That hedge is carried intact.

## 7. Reston and the Corporation, 1986 onward

**Leaves DARPA after roughly thirteen years, 1986. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]**

**Founds the Corporation for National Research Initiatives, Reston, 1986; chairman, CEO, and president. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]** **Cerf joins him there as vice president the same year. [A, `vint_cerf.journey.json`, stop "Reston, back with Kahn at CNRI"]** The pool gives three different Reston coordinates: 38.9522, -77.357 (geography, twice), 38.9586, -77.357 (afterlife), and 38.9558, -77.355 (interlock). The atlas pin **38.9558, -77.355** is inherited; the divergence is recorded rather than averaged. The street address 1895 Preston White Drive is the pool's own [R].

**Coins "National Information Infrastructure," popularly the "Information Super Highway." [A as the coinage, Internet Hall of Fame inductee biography] [R as to any single year]** The pool tagged 1986 as [A] to a source that says only "in the mid 1980s." The year is downgraded; the journey file dates the stop 1986-06-01 and says the source supports a range and nothing narrower, and that nothing orders this against the CNRI founding.

**Elected to the National Academy of Engineering, 1987, and Founding Fellow of the American Association for Artificial Intelligence, 1990. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]** Neither gets a stop; an election is not a journey. The pool garbled the second body as "American Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence," which conflates two names and denotes no real organization: it is the American Association for Artificial Intelligence, since 2007 the Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence.

**Co-founds the Internet Society with Cerf, 1992. [A, Wikipedia "Bob Kahn"]** **Location [R]:** no source names a founding site or event; Reston is the reconstruction, stated as such. **The Digital Object Architecture and the Handle System at CNRI. [A as CNRI work, same source] [R as to date]** No year is attested; the journey file dates it 1995-01-01 and calls the year a placeholder inside a decade of work.

**"Al Gore and the Internet," joint statement by Cerf and Kahn. [A, Wikipedia "Al Gore and information technology", dating it 29 September 2000; republished in The Register on 2 October]** The pool dated it 28 September, which nothing supports. Corrected, with the republication noted as the version that circulated. No verbatim line is in the pool, so the stop carries no quote.

## Contradictions logged and left open

1. **National Inventors Hall of Fame, 2006 ceremony city.** Geography lens: Akron, Ohio (41.0814, -81.519), hedged [R]. Afterlife lens: 600 Dulany Street, Alexandria, Virginia (38.8048, -77.0469), tagged [A]. Both cannot stand, and Alexandria is anachronistic: the Hall's museum did not occupy the USPTO campus until 2008, while by the mid-2000s the induction ceremony had generally moved out of Akron toward Washington. The journey file inherits the atlas's Akron pin (**41.0798, -81.5219**) for continuity with the Cerf file and states that the city is unresolved.
2. **Coordinate disputes**, each left open with every competing reading printed at the point it arises: Reston, three pairs (§7); the Palo Alto hotel, three readings (§4); the Bell Labs site (§1); the Zimmermann San Francisco pin, whose claimed sharing is not borne out by the file named (§8).
3. **The "fathers of the internet" title. [R]** A popular formula that later historiography contests by pointing to Paul Baran, Donald Davies, and Leonard Kleinrock on packet switching. No primary source calls it a legend; it is recorded as a simplification neither Kahn nor Cerf originates.

**RFC 1000 attribution, corrected**
The pool attributed an RFC 1000 passage to "Leiner, Cerf, Clark, Kahn, et al." and presented a paraphrase inside quotation marks. RFC 1000 is by J. Reynolds and J. Postel; the passage sits in "THE ORIGINS OF RFCs," by Stephen D. Crocker. The actual text reads: "The BBN folks, led by Frank Heart, Bob Kahn, Severo Ornstein and Will Crowther, found themselves talking to a crew of graduate students they hadn't anticipated." The pool's clause "met with graduate students to discuss the host-IMP interface" appears nowhere in it. Since the passage is narrative about Kahn rather than words of his, no RFC 1000 line enters the journey file.
