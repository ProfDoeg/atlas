# Ian Lancaster Fleming (1908-1964): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11. Companion: `ian_fleming.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or contested claim. Contradictions are flagged in place and **not adjudicated**; unreachable material is named with the reason; gaps are stated as gaps.

The distortion here is not distance in time but the man's own trade. Fleming spent the war inside a deception organisation and the rest of his life in publicity; many standard anecdotes trace back to him telling them about himself to journalists who did not check. Those are kept as **[R]** rather than dropped, since dropping them leaves a reader unable to see why they are everywhere.

## 2. Life, by phase

### Green Street and the Purbeck coast (1908-1927)

**Attested backbone. [A: Wikipedia; Lycett 1995]** Born 28 May 1908, 27 Green Street, Mayfair, second son of Valentine Fleming, MP for Henley, and Evelyn Ste Croix Rose; the money one generation old, from the grandfather Robert Fleming's merchant bank. Durnford School from 1914; the Langton Matravers article names Fleming explicitly as a pupil. Valentine killed 20 May 1917 at Gillemont Farm near Epehy, Churchill's appreciation in *The Times* of 25 May 1917.

**Braziers Park. [A: Wikipedia, 'Valentine Fleming'; 'Braziers Park']** *Upgraded from the pool's [R].* The pool called this undocumented family recollection of a leased house, c. 1909. Both articles state it flatly: Valentine bought the house in 1906, the family stayed to 1911, and Ian's nursery is now the house library. "Leases" corrected to "owns"; range 1908-1911.

**Eton, 1921-1926. [A: Wikipedia]** The pool's "Victor Ludorum 1925-1927" implies three wins; Wikipedia has him holding the title "for two years between 1925 and 1927", which is two. Leaving year 1926 preferred, since he left "a term early for a crammer course".

**Sandhurst, 1926-1927. [A: Wikipedia; Lycett 1995]** **Two corrections.** (1) Chronology placed the Royal Military College in **Surrey**; it is in **Berkshire**. (2) Chronology dated entry to 1927, but "spent less than a year there, leaving in 1927" requires a 1926 entry, as geography has. **[R: Lycett]** for the gonorrhoea and the withdrawal; no institutional record reached.

### The continental repair (1927-1931)

**Attested backbone. [A: Wikipedia; Lycett 1995]** The Tennerhof at Kitzbühel, academy of Ernan Forbes Dennis, formerly a passport control officer in Vienna, and his wife the novelist Phyllis Bottome, who encouraged him to write; then Munich and the University of Geneva, with an engagement to Monique Panchaud de Bottomes. The period is given loosely as 1927-1931; no month-level dates were reachable.

**Foreign Office examination. [A as to failure / R as to the ranking]** **Contradiction flagged, the pool's figure rejected.** Geography claimed seventh of sixty-two with only five places offered, a near-miss reading. Wikipedia says only that "he scored an adequate pass standard, but failed to get a job offer", giving no rank; the biographies give **25th of 62**. The "seventh" version is not carried. **Gap:** no results list was reachable.

### Fleet Street, Moscow, the City (1931-1939)

**Reuters, October 1931. [A: Wikipedia]** Sub-editor and journalist. **Address anachronism flagged:** geography pinned the stop to "Reuters, 85 Fleet Street", but Reuters did not occupy that building in 1931-1933; the Lutyens headquarters was finished at the end of the decade. The stop is renamed "Reuters, London", the coordinate kept as an approximate City pin. **Gap:** the earlier City address was not established.

**Moscow, Metro-Vickers trial, April 1933. [A: Wikipedia; Lycett; Reuters archive]** Trial of six British engineers in the House of the Unions. **[R]** for the Stalin interview request and signed refusal, which reach this report through Lycett only. **Cull & Co 1933 and Rowe and Pitman 1935 [A]**, merged into one stop at the Rowe and Pitman pin. **Kitzbühel visits and Muriel Wright [R: Lycett]**, no firm year, pinned 1935 with the hedge in `date_confidence`. **Ann O'Neill [A]**, affair from early 1939; **[R]** for the 1948 stillborn daughter Mary.

### Room 39 (1939-1941)

**Recruitment. [A: Wikipedia]** Recruited by Rear Admiral John Godfrey, DNI, May 1939; commissioned lieutenant RNVR July 1939; NID full-time from August 1939 in Room 39 as 17F; lieutenant commander "months after", undated.

**The Trout Memo, 29 September 1939. [A as to the document / R as to Fleming's authorship]** **The pool's [A] on "Fleming circulates the Trout Memo" is demoted.** Wikipedia states it was **issued under Godfrey's name** and merely "bore the hallmarks of having been written by Godfrey's assistant Ian Fleming"; Macintyre attributes it to Fleming. Date and the item-28 anticipation of Mincemeat are confirmed. The campa gives attribution and signature both, and resolves neither.

**Operation Ruthless, 12 September 1940. [A: Wikipedia; Batey 2008]** Fleming's text survives in Batey, *From Bletchley with Love* (2008), pp. 2-6. Cancelled. Frank Birch's letter of 20 October 1940, printed alongside it, records Turing and Twinn arriving "like undertakers cheated of a nice corpse": a contemporary primary source, kept out of the journey file only because each stop carries at most one quote.

**Operation Golden Eye. [A: Wikipedia]** **Both dates and purpose in the pool were wrong.** Chronology gave 1941 through 1942 and called it a response to "a German invasion of Spain". Wikipedia has it running **1940 to August 1943**, Fleming assigned to draw up the plan in August 1940, and the purpose as monitoring Spain against a Franco-Axis alliance and keeping communications if Germany occupied **Gibraltar**.

**Gibraltar, 16-26 February 1941; Madrid, February 1941. [A: Golden Eye files via Lycett]** The Convent; liaison with Alan Hillgarth. **Tangier [A: same]** carries a year only, pinned 1941-01-01 with the hedge in `date_confidence`. **Internal tension flagged:** the pool dates Gibraltar and Madrid to February 1941 but Estoril to May 1941 on the same tour. Not resolved. **Casino Estoril [R: Pearson; Lycett]:** the gambling-against-German-agents story is repeated by every biographer and sourced by none beyond Fleming's own retelling.

### The American blueprint (1941-1942)

**Washington, May 1941. [A: Wikipedia; Lycett]** Travel with Godfrey; meeting with William Donovan; the memorandum on the structure of a future American service. The pool's own hedge that its founding-document status is "credited, with some overstatement" is retained. The **St Regis blackjack anecdote of June 1941 [R: Pearson]** is unverifiable against any record and is **dropped from the journey file**, noted here so the omission is deliberate rather than silent.

**Camp X, Whitby, Ontario. [R: demoted from the pool's [A]]** Wikipedia treats the connection as contested, reporting Hodgson's claim that Fleming "may have been" a trainee "or at least a visitor" and adding "There is however evidence against this claim." Stafford undercuts the story, and Stevenson's *A Man Called Intrepid* (1976) cannot carry [A] alone. Kept at [R] because Fleming's published tribute to Stephenson is attested (foreword to Hyde, *Room 3603*).

### 30 Assault Unit (1942-1945)

**Proposal, March 1942. [A: Wikipedia, '30 Assault Unit', citing BBC Magazine, 5 March 2013]** Fleming's memo to Godfrey survives in quotation.

**Dieppe, 19 August 1942. [A as to the raid / R as to the unit's presence]** **Two corrections.** (1) Fleming observed from the destroyer **HMS Fernie**, not "a landing craft offshore". (2) The pool's causal claim is inverted: it had the pinch concept becoming "the founding idea of 30 Assault Unit" after Dieppe, but Fleming proposed the unit in **March 1942, five months before** the raid. Wikipedia also flags the deployment itself as disputed, so the tag is softened.

**Jamaica conference. [A: Parker 2014]** **Year corrected from 1942 to July 1943.** The pool's closing line, "the promise becomes Goldeneye four years later", only works from 1942; from 1943 the interval is three years. **Contradiction retained in `date_confidence`:** chronology still gives 1942 and is not silently overwritten.

**Algiers 1943, Palermo July 1943, Naples 1944. [R: Rankin 2011]** Unit operations, Fleming directing from London. Algiers and Palermo **dropped** for stop-count reasons; Naples kept for its atlas interlock. All three remain [R].

**6 June 1944. [A: Wikipedia; Rankin]** **Place corrected; the most consequential fix in the pool.** Chronology tagged the loss of operational command to **Normandy, France, on 1944-06-06**. He was replaced on that date precisely because he was barred from the field; he did not go to Normandy on D-Day, and the row as given would put a pin exactly where he demonstrably was not. Corrected to **London**.

**Cherbourg, June-July 1944 [A: Rankin]; Kandy, December 1944 to January 1945 [A: Lycett]. Schloss Tambach [A: German Wikipedia; Rankin]:** the bare year 1945 is replaced by the datable seizure of **11 April 1945**, the archive having been moved there in April 1943; "hundreds of years of records" overstates it, the German navy dating from 1848/1871, though Prussian material and part of the Reich Air Ministry archive were included. **Demobilisation and Kemsley, May 1945 [A].**

### Goldeneye and the last years (1946-1964)

**Year of acquisition: contradiction flagged, not resolved.** The Fleming article says "in 1945, Fleming had a house built"; the estate article says he "bought 15 acres adjacent to the Golden Clouds estate in 1946". The file uses 1946 and states the conflict in `date_confidence`.

**Attested backbone. [A]** *Casino Royale* written 15 January to 16 February 1952; marriage to Ann Charteris in Jamaica 24 March 1952; Caspar born in London that August. Published 13 April 1953: 4,728 copies, cover by Fleming, sold out in a month, three print runs; the 1955 US paperback renamed it *You Asked for It* and the hero *Jimmy Bond*. Istanbul June 1955, the Interpol conference, Nazim Kalkavan transcribed into Darko Kerim, out on the Orient Express from Sirkeci. Tokyo 1959 and 1962, three days then twelve with Richard Hughes and Torao Saito. *Life* prints Kennedy's list 17 March 1961; the heart attack follows in April. Final Goldeneye stay January 1964.

**The writing-method quotation. [A as to wording / corrected as to source]** The "2,000 words a day" passage is verbatim in Wikipedia, cited to Faulks and Fleming, *Devil May Care* (2009), p. 320. **The pool's source descriptor is wrong:** it was not an interview but a piece Fleming wrote for *Books and Bookmen* in May 1963. Corrected, and the place is phrased as the Goldeneye routine he describes rather than as an utterance made in Jamaica.

**Research trips. [R: Lycett]** Saratoga and Las Vegas (1954, *Diamonds Are Forever*); Nassau (1956-1963, *Thunderball*); Hong Kong (1959, *Thrilling Cities*). None dated to the month. Nassau and Hong Kong **dropped**; Saratoga and Las Vegas merged into one stop.

**11 August 1964. [A: Wikipedia]** **Place corrected.** Chronology said Fleming "collapses with a heart attack at a hotel in Canterbury". He was *staying* at a Canterbury hotel, "went to the Royal St George's Golf Club for lunch", and "collapsed with another heart attack shortly after the meal": the collapse was at the club in Sandwich, ~20 km away. **The afterlife lens compounded this by calling the meal dinner; it was lunch.** He had been elected Captain that year by Eric Pemberton.

**Death, 12 August 1964. [A]** Kent and Canterbury Hospital, aged 56. **Caspar's birthday: two pool errors.** Chronology said "on what would have been his son Caspar's twelfth birthday", but Caspar was alive in 1964 and died in 1975; geography said "three days after", also wrong. Caspar was born 12 August 1952: Fleming died **on the birthday itself**.

**Last words. [R: Lycett 1996, p. 443]** **Attribution corrected.** The pool credited the wording to Pearson's 1966 biography; Wikipedia's underlying citation is Lycett. Wording confirmed verbatim; the tag stays [R] because it is reported speech recorded by a biographer.

**Burial, Sevenhampton. [A: Wikipedia]** St James's Church, built 1846 by W. Pedley. **Two gaps.** No source reached establishes what is inscribed on the obelisk, nor whether any blue plaque marks a London address of Fleming's; the campa says so in prose rather than guessing. No source gives the funeral date, so the burial stop is pinned to the day of death, stated in `date_confidence`.

## 3. Afterlife

**Posthumous publication. [A: Wikipedia]** **Date corrected.** Afterlife gave December 1964; publication was **1 April 1965**, Jonathan Cape, and geography's "the following year" is right. *Octopussy and The Living Daylights* followed on 23 June 1966, so *Golden Gun* was neither the last Bond book nor strictly the thirteenth: twelfth **novel**, thirteenth **book**.

**The "thin and feeble" quotation. [R: unverified]** Attributed to Cape by the pool. The sources record only that Cape thought the novel below standard and paid Kingsley Amis £35 15s for an assessment whose suggestions were not adopted. Not carried.

**Continuation authors. [A with three corrections: Wikipedia, 'James Bond']** The pool said "nine authors" and named eight; the ninth is **Kim Sherwood** (Double O trilogy, 2022-2026). **Gardner wrote fourteen original novels plus two film novelisations**, sixteen books, not sixteen novels. **Benson's original novels run 1997-2002**, not 1996-2002. Higson's "another announced" has appeared as *King Zero* (2026).

**Goldeneye after Fleming. [A: Wikipedia]** **Ownership corrected.** The pool had Chris Blackwell buying the estate in 1976. **Bob Marley bought it in 1976 and sold it to Blackwell the following year**; Marley as intervening owner is worth keeping rather than eliding. Blanche Blackwell as Fleming's companion and Chris's mother is correct. The pool's "all fourteen Bond books written between 1946 and 1964" is also wrong: the span is **1952-1964**.

**Ian Fleming Publications. [A: Wikipedia]** Glidrose Productions, bought in 1952, renamed Ian Fleming (Glidrose) Publications in 1998 and Ian Fleming Publications Ltd in 2002; still family-owned.

**Not carried into the journey file.** **Bronisław Urbański [R]**, named by 2020 press reporting a "physical and operational model" for Bond: late, contested, one entry in a parlour game running also through Peter Fleming, Patrick Dalzel-Job, and Duško Popov. **Ian Fleming International Airport, Boscobel [R]**: exists and is named for him, but the 2011 opening date could not be re-verified. **Imperial War Museum, 'For Your Eyes Only' [R]**: the 2008 centenary exhibition could not be confirmed.

## 4. Atlas interlocks

Four are named in the campa; one is named here in order to be excluded.

1. **`antoinette_de_saint_leger`, Naples, the false friend. [A]** She married an Anglo-Irish officer named Richard Fleming (also Flemyng) in 1881; he withdrew to Naples in 1897 and died there in 1922. **No source connects the two families.** A surname coincidence, and the campa says so outright. The canonical Naples pin is inherited byte-identical.

2. **`meyer_lansky`, 1942. [A as to Lansky / R as to the linkage]** The American Office of Naval Intelligence recruited Lansky in 1942 as go-between for Operation Underworld on the New York waterfront, the year Fleming's NID stood up 30 Commando. A structural parallel between two navies, not a meeting; the campa sets both facts side by side and asserts no contact.

3. **`dion_fortune` and `theodor_reuss`, via Aleister Crowley. [R]** Reuss admitted Crowley to the O.T.O. in London in 1910; Fortune corresponded with Crowley at Netherwood, Hastings, from 1944. Crowley is the figure several biographers attach to Le Chiffre, and the 1941 suggestion that NID use him to help interrogate Hess is credited to Fleming. Named in the Trout Memo campa, hedged.

4. **`adnan_khashoggi`, deliberately excluded.** Offered by the pool as a thematic parallel (arms broker, the *Goldfinger* and *Thunderball* milieu), with the Kaaba pin 21.4225, 39.8262 and the Paris pin 48.8566, 2.3522 flagged as inheritable. Fleming has **no documented stop in Mecca or Paris**, and the parallel is anachronistic: Khashoggi's career postdates his death. No pin inherited, no mention in the campa.

5. **Pin inheritance elsewhere.** The generic **London pin 51.5074, -0.1278** comes from `theodor_reuss.journey.json`, used for the four stops located only as "London". The Jerusalem/Temple Mount pin (31.778, 35.2354) is unused: Fleming has no Jerusalem reference in any source reached.

6. **Documented gap.** A grep across every `*.journey.json` for "Ian Fleming", "James Bond", "Casino Royale", "SMERSH", and "Le Chiffre" returns only unrelated numeric coincidences: Fleming has **no directly attested crossing with any traveller already in the atlas**. The QUEUE.md grouping with the cipher wing (`diffie`, `hellman`, `hal_finney`) is curatorial, not biographical.
