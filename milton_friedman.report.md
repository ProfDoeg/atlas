# Milton Friedman (1912-2006), research report

*Compiled 2026-08-25 for `milton_friedman.journey.json`. 44 stops, 8 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-only report not independently confirmed. Disagreements between reachable sources are printed, not harmonized. Coordinate and dating fixes are gathered in "Corrections carried."

## 1. Brooklyn and Rahway, 1912-1928

- Born 31 July 1912 in Brooklyn to Sarah Ethel Landau and Jeno Saul Friedman, Jewish immigrants from Beregszasz in Carpathian Ruthenia (now Berehove, Ukraine); fourth child, only son. **[A: Wikipedia, "Milton Friedman"; the Hoover/OAC chronology opens at 1912.]**
- Birth address 502 Barbey Street, East New York, and the move to Rahway "at about thirteen months." **[R** on both, from a Libertarianism.org reconstruction cited by the earlier crawl and never fetched; **A** on the Rahway upbringing itself: Wikipedia.**]** The pin is East New York generally, declared a neighborhood and not a house.
- **Contradiction, not resolved: the father's death.** Family material gives **1 November 1927**, other family material **1929**; neither checked against a certificate. Attested only that he died during or just before Milton's last year of school. **[R on both.]** The graduation stop states both and leaves it open.
- Graduated Rahway High School 1928, before his sixteenth birthday. **[A: Wikipedia.]** Coordinate corrected; `date_confidence` keeps the caveat that the Madison Avenue building may not be the 1928 one.

## 2. Rutgers, Chicago, Columbia, 1928-1935

- Rutgers on scholarship from 1928, intending to be an actuary; B.A. in mathematics and economics 1932; Arthur Burns and Homer Jones the decisive teachers, Jones pushing him to Chicago. **[A: Wikipedia; Econlib; Hoover/OAC.]** Actuarial examinations partly failed: **[A in outline; R** for the self-deprecating framing.**]**
- Chicago M.A. 1933; met Rose Director through alphabetical seating in an economics class. **[A: Wikipedia; year corroborated by Hoover/OAC.]** Wikipedia elsewhere attributes the seating to Viner's course; the pool says only "an economics class," so the campa names no course.
- Columbia fellowship under Harold Hotelling and Wesley Clair Mitchell, 1933-1934, corrected from the pool's 1934. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- Research assistant to Henry Schultz, Chicago, 1934-1935. **[A: Wikipedia.]** **Sourcing error recorded:** the pool credited this to the Hoover/OAC finding aid, whose chronology was fetched in full, holds eleven entries and never mentions Schultz. The stop's `date_confidence` says so.

## 3. Washington, New York, the war, 1935-1946

- National Resources Committee, Washington, from 1935, on a large consumer budget survey; his later position that WPA/CCC/PWA employment suited the crisis while price and wage fixing did not. **[A: Hoover/OAC; Wikipedia.]** The Committee's building is unestablished; the pin is central Washington.
- NBER research staff from 1937, affiliation to 1981 **[A: Wikipedia; Hoover/OAC]**; lecturer at Columbia from 1937 **[R:** Hoover/OAC, tagged R in the pool.**]** The NBER's address was never pinned down; the coordinate is midtown.
- **Contradiction, decided three to one: the marriage year.** English Wikipedia's *infobox* says 1936; its own *article body* says he wed "six years later, in 1938"; the Hoover/OAC chronology says "1938 Married Rose Director"; Simple Wikipedia says 1938. Journey uses **1938 [A]**.
- **Gap: the marriage location is unknown.** No source names a city, and the pool's Hyde Park pin is contradicted by his being NBER staff in New York from autumn 1937. The marriage gets **no stop of its own**: it is folded into the New York stop, location declared unestablished.
- Wisconsin visiting professor 1940, not renewed amid departmental controversy. **[A: Wikipedia; OAC.]** No faculty minutes reachable. The common reading that antisemitism was a factor is **not in the pool** and is not asserted.
- Treasury 1941-1943 on wartime taxation, helped develop income tax withholding, later regretted that it made a large peacetime government financeable. **[A: Wikipedia; OAC.]**
- Statistical Research Group, Columbia, 1943-1945, associate director, with Wallis, Wald and Mosteller. **[A: Wikipedia; OAC.]**
- Income from Independent Professional Practice, with Kuznets, published 1945 after NBER delay; the dissertation for the 1946 Columbia Ph.D.; Minnesota associate professor 1945-1946. **[A: Econlib; Wikipedia; Hoover/OAC.]** The usual account of the board's objection appears in the pool only as "controversy around the professional-income research," so the campas keep to that.

## 4. Chicago, Mont Pelerin, Cambridge, 1946-1957

- Chicago faculty 1946-1977, full professor from 1948. **[A: Hoover/OAC; Wikipedia.]**
- Aaron Director, Rose's brother, pressed Hayek's The Road to Serfdom on the University of Chicago Press in 1943-44, after three American publishers refused it. **[A:** `friedrich_hayek.journey.json` 271-272.**]** Embedded in the 1946 stop as a prior event.
- Mont Pelerin Society founding meeting, Hotel du Parc near Vevey, 10 April 1947, convened by Hayek, thirty-nine scholars. **[A: Wikipedia, "Mont Pelerin Society"; PBS for Friedman's attendance.]** The pool's pin falls in Lake Geneva, so the file inherits the canonical corpus pin byte-identical.
- The Mises walkout, "a bunch of socialists." **[R, explicitly.]** Friedman's own recollection decades later via Hoover, already carried as such in `ludwig_von_mises.journey.json` 483-487; the campa says no transcript confirms it.
- John Bates Clark Medal 1951 **[A: Econlib; PBS]**; A Theory of the Consumption Function 1957 **[A: Wikipedia; Econlib]**. Neither has an established venue, so both are folded into the Chicago workshop stop.
- Fulbright Visiting Fellow, Gonville and Caius, Cambridge, 1954-1955. **[A: Wikipedia, verbatim.]** The pool's geography lens said 1953-1954; its own chronology lens says 1954, so the flagged discrepancy dissolves.
- The lectures behind Capitalism and Freedom were given at **Wabash College, Crawfordsville, Indiana, June 1956**, not at Chicago as the pool had it. **[A: Wikipedia, "Capitalism and Freedom."]** **Volker Fund financing and Rose's editing are [R],** corroborated but not re-verified.

## 5. The public decades, 1962-1972

- Capitalism and Freedom 1962; past half a million copies, eighteen languages, later on Time's and National Review's lists. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- A Monetary History of the United States, 1867-1960, with Anna Jacobson Schwartz, 1963. **[A: Wikipedia; Econlib.]** Pinned to the NBER in New York, where Schwartz's series lived, not to the Princeton imprint.
- Newsweek column 1966-1984. **[A: Wikipedia; Econlib.]** The rotation with Samuelson and Wallich is **not in the pool**, so no name is used.
- President of the American Economic Association, 1967. **[A: PBS; Econlib.]** **Largest substantive gap in the pool:** the December 1967 presidential address, the natural-rate argument against a stable Phillips curve, is absent from the research entirely, so nothing about it is asserted.
- Gates Commission on an All-Volunteer Armed Force, 1969, Nixon appointment, conscription as a tax in kind. **[A** for the Nixon role: PBS; **R** for commission dates.**]** The Westmoreland exchange about mercenaries and slaves is not in the pool and is not used.
- Coronary bypass at the Mayo Clinic c. 1972 and the Nixon telephone remark. **[R on both.]** Family recollection; the pool itself calls the joke anecdotal rather than a transcript, and `quote_source` says so.

## 6. Chile 1975 and 1981, Stockholm 1976

- March 1975: traveled to Chile with Arnold Harberger at a private Chilean institution's invitation, lectured publicly including "The Fragility of Freedom," met Pinochet. **[A** for the advisory contact: PBS; **A** for the lecture text: hoover.org, fetched; **R** for the 21 March date and the 45-60 minute length, which rest on Edwards and Montes, *JHET* 42 (2020), not fetched here.**]**
- 21 April 1975: the follow-up letter to Pinochet. **[A** for the date: Wikipedia, "Miracle of Chile"; **R** for the contents, via the Friedmans' memoir as summarized by Edwards and Montes.**]** Placed at **Chicago**, where it was written; the pool pinned it to Santiago while conceding it was mailed.
- **Structural finding for the operator:** `augusto_pinochet.journey.json` has **no stop for the Friedman meeting**, though this file inherits its Santiago pin byte-identical. Grepping it for "Friedman," "shock" and "Harberger" returns nothing. Nothing was written into it.
- Orlando Letelier, "The Chicago Boys in Chile: Economic Freedom's Awful Toll," The Nation, 1976. **[R** on title and venue, dossier line 626; **A** that his Sheridan Circle assassination is a worked stop in `augusto_pinochet.journey.json` 307-317.**]** A one-directional engagement published in Friedman's lifetime, named in the letter stop's campa. **The assassination is not staged here**: it is Letelier's death and Pinochet's stop.
- Nobel Memorial Prize 1976, citation corrected to the official British spelling, "the complexity of **stabilisation** policy." **[A: Wikipedia; Econlib; PBS.]** Venue **[A by documented practice, not by a 1976 page]**: nobelprize.org was unreachable, and Wikipedia's Nobel and Concert Hall articles establish the annual 10 December ceremony there; coordinate inherited from the Hayek file.
- **Contradiction corrected: who protested.** The pool credited it to Gunnar Myrdal. Wikipedia names four laureates, **Wald, Pauling, Baltimore and Luria**, who wrote to the New York Times in October 1976. Myrdal's documented position was that the prize should be *abolished*, having gone to "such reactionaries as Hayek (and afterwards Milton Friedman)": not a protest at the ceremony. The journey names the four and drops Myrdal.
- November 1981: second Chile visit and second Pinochet meeting before the 1982 crash; Friedman opposed the fixed exchange rate Chile was running. **[R:** Edwards and Montes via the dossier.**]**

## 7. San Francisco, 1977-2006

- Retired from Chicago 1977; Russian Hill apartment until death; visiting scholar at the San Francisco Fed; Hoover senior research fellow 1977-2006. **[A: Wikipedia; Hoover/OAC.]**
- Capitaf, the Fairlee, Vermont cottage. **[A** that they built one there in the 1960s: Wikipedia; **R** for the name.**]** Dated 1965 only to order the segment.
- Free to Choose, ten-part PBS series and companion book with Rose, 1980; five weeks atop the US bestseller list; about two dozen languages; updated 1990. **[A: Wikipedia; Econlib.]** **Attribution corrected:** the voucher line quoted at that stop is verbatim at hoover.org/research/free-choose, but that page is a Hoover Digest essay by Friedman alone, 2005 no. 3, not the 1980 book.
- China, three visits: 1980 official lectures; 1988 conference and a nearly two-hour meeting with Zhao Ziyang urging rapid price liberalization, later used against Zhao by factional enemies as contamination by "extreme liberal" foreign ideas; 1993 meeting with Jiang Zemin. **[A** that he visited and advised there: Wikipedia; **R** for each trip.**]** **Zhongnanhai is an inference**, declared as one.
- Reykjavik 1984, lecture and televised debate. **[R, weakly.]** No transcript, no broadcast date, no named interlocutor. Kept, with the gap declared.
- Presidential Medal of Freedom and National Medal of Science 1988, the Medal in the White House East Room. **[A** for the awards: Wikipedia, Hoover/OAC; **A** for the East Room via a photo caption; **R** for the 17 October date.**]**
- Friedman Foundation for Educational Choice founded 1996, renamed EdChoice 2016. **[A on both dates: Wikipedia.]** **Motive corrected:** not posthumous housekeeping but EdChoice's own reason, the Friedmans' "desire to separate their personal legacy from the intellectual legacy of educational choice." **Indianapolis is [R],** inferred from the later headquarters.
- Died of heart failure in San Francisco 16 November 2006, aged 94, taken to a hospital near his home. **[A: Wikipedia infobox.]** The hospital is nowhere named.
- **Gap: no recorded resting place.** Wikipedia's infobox, which normally carries a resting-place field, leaves it empty; Find a Grave was blocked or empty. Suggestive of cremation without a memorial and **not evidence of it**, and the death stop says so.

## 8. Afterlife

- Milton Friedman Institute founded at Chicago 2008 with a $200 million commitment; more than 170 faculty signed a protest letter; merged in June 2011 with the Becker Center into the Becker Friedman Institute. **[A: Wikipedia.]**
- The papers: 227 boxes, about 100 linear feet, Hoover, open from 1977 with increments to 2008, digitized as the Collected Works. **[A: OAC finding aid.]**
- Naomi Klein's The Shock Doctrine (2007) made Friedman the emblem of "disaster capitalism" with Chile as case study; Johan Norberg of Cato called that a "straw man... hopelessly flawed at virtually every level." **[A: Wikipedia.]** Both named as commentary, neither staged as a traveler.
- "Chicago Boys" persists in Chilean discourse; a 2015 Chilean documentary carries it as a title. **[A: Wikipedia.]** The film's page 404'd, so no director or reception detail is given.
- A 2011 survey ranked him second most influential economist of the century after Keynes; commentators credited the Friedman-Schwartz work with shaping the Fed's 2008 response. **[A: Wikipedia.]** Neither given a stop.
