# Shams al-Din of Tabriz: research report

*2026-08-09. Slug `shams_al_din_of_tabriz`. Shams al-Din Muhammad of Tabriz (Shams-e Tabrizi, "Shams-e Parande", the Flier), wandering dervish, c. 1185 to 1247/1248. Companion of Jalal al-Din Rumi; author at one remove of the Maqalat; namesake of a book he did not write.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Where two sources disagree, both are printed and neither is adjudicated.

---

## The evidentiary situation, stated first

Everything known about Shams before Konya comes from three Mevlevi devotional compilations, none contemporary with the events **[R, from Franklin Lewis, *Rumi: Past and Present, East and West*]**:

1. **Sultan Walad, *Ibtidanama***: verse chronicle by Rumi's son, born 25 April 1226, present in the household through the Shams years and the man who rode to Damascus to fetch him back. Earliest and closest. It is a poem, and **gives no calendar dates at all**.
2. **Sepahsalar, *Resaleh***: a generation later.
3. **Aflaki, *Manaqib al-'Arifin***: fourteenth century, source of nearly every vivid detail now repeated. Lewis reads him as an unreliable dramatizer.

Against these stands one text in Shams's own register, the **Maqalat-e Shams-e Tabrizi**, discourses **taken down by disciples rather than composed by him** **[A, consensus, e.g. Chittick]**: invaluable for what Shams thought, useless for when anything happened. **No contemporary thirteenth-century document independent of this corpus records his biography** **[R, Lewis]**. Birth year, itinerary of the wandering decades, and circumstances of the death are all unresolved in modern scholarship.

## Phase 1: Tabriz, c. 1185 to c. 1221

**Birth** at Tabriz, in the Azerbaijan of the Seljuk-ruled Persian world **[A, en.wikipedia 'Shams Tabrizi'; tr.wikipedia 'Şems-i Tebrizi']**. **No contemporary record fixes the year** **[R]**. Circa 1185 is standard, tr.wikipedia gives 1186, some accounts push a decade earlier. The estimate counts back from a death placed at 1247 or 1248 and an age of 62 or 63: one uncertain figure derived from two others **[R]**.

**The father's name, a contradiction left standing.** Sepahsalar's *Resaleh* makes him son of **Imam Ala al-Din** **[A]**; Aflaki names the father **Ali** and the grandfather **Malikdad** **[A]**, and the Turkish tradition carries the full nasab *Şemsüddîn Muhammed bin Alî bin Melikdâd Tebrîzî* **[A, tr.wikipedia]**. Two earliest traditions of one order, two fathers, no way to check either. Both go into the journey file.

**Abu Bakr Sallabaf, the basket-weaver.** Shams attaches himself to **Ebubekir Selebâf**, a Tabriz shaykh of the futuwwa and Malamati current, formally unlettered, at age **22**, serving **14 years** **[A, tr.wikipedia; corroborated in outline by Chittick, at second hand]**. Two corrections to the pool: its coordinate 38.0827 / 46.2946 for the workshop is **invented precision**, since no source locates it and no site survives; and its claim that **the master's death** released Shams to wander is said by **no source checked**, and was cut.

**The teacher list disagrees about geography, not only names.** fa.wikipedia's Tabriz teachers are **Pir Sele-baf, Shams Khunji, Shaykh Rukn al-Din Sujasi** **[A]**, and Sujas is near Zanjan, not Tabriz. tr.wikipedia adds **Salahaddin Mahmud** **[A]** and gives "Şeyh Rükneddin (Sacaslı)", the same man. en.wikipedia's "**Baba Kamal al-Din Jumdi**" is a **spelling error [R]**, existing in that form only there. He is **Baba Kamal al-Din Jandi (Jondi)** of **Jand on the Syr Darya**, a **Kubrawi** in the line of Najm al-Din Kubra, and **not a Tabriz master at all**, so the pool's placement of this training in Tabriz is unsupported. The journey names the garble rather than repeating it.

**Trade.** Basket-weaving and the selling of girdles are given as his own trade in some accounts, possibly a bleed-through from Sallabaf's craft, while other manuscripts give **zarduz**, embroiderer **[R]**. **Epithet: Shams-e Parande**, the Flier, because he never settles anywhere for long **[A, Aflaki]**.

## Phase 2: the wandering decades, c. 1221 to 1244

The weakest-attested stretch of the life, and the journey says so at every pin.

**In his own voice** the Maqalat names **Baghdad, Damascus, Aleppo, Erzurum/Erzincan, Sivas, Kayseri** **[A, Maqalat, primary]**, cities passed through in search of a companion able to endure his company. **It gives no dates and no order** **[R]**. Aksaray, Isfahan and Mecca come from secondary tradition only **[R]**. The journey's dates for these stops, 1224 through mid-1244, are **spaced inference**, not evidence: they carry `reconstructed` in `date_confidence`, and the campa prose says outright that these years are a list of city names with the connective tissue missing.

**Coordinate correction.** The pool gave Baghdad as 33.3406 / 44.3922, about 5 km NE of the old Rusafa city the claim describes. The journey uses **33.3344 / 44.3747**, the pin already in `rumi.journey.json`.

**Awhad al-Din Kirmani.** In the Maqalat, Shams faults Kirmani's practice of contemplating beauty in created forms **[A]**. Where they met is **not fixed**: Kayseri, Konya, or unspecified, by account **[R]**.

**Ibn Arabi.** A persistent tradition has Shams dismissing him as a man of intellect rather than a lover of God, on an earlier Damascus pass **[R, repeated across secondary biographies; the wording could not be verified against a primary citation]**. **No source states the two met.**

**Mecca.** Credited to Shams by diffuse hagiographical strands only **[R, no specific citation located, a gap]**. The Kaaba pin, 21.4225 / 39.8262, is carried on inherited ground: the Bayazid paradox Shams uses at Konya is Bayazid's Kaaba paradox, not a documented hajj of his own.

## Phase 3: Konya, November 1244

**The arrival date, a conversion error rather than a rival tradition.** **26 Jumada al-Thani 642 AH = 29 November 1244 Julian = 6 December 1244 Gregorian** **[A, fa.wikipedia gives exactly "26 Jumada al-Thaniyah 642 / 6 December 1244"; tabular conversion agrees]**. en.wikipedia's **15 November 1244** appears nowhere else and is a **bad conversion of the same AH date** **[R]**. The pool attributed the AH date to the **Ibtidanama**, which is **wrong**: a verse chronicle giving no calendar date. The carrier is fa.wikipedia and the Mevlevi tradition. The journey uses **1244-11-29**, matching `rumi.journey.json`.

**Lodging:** the **Inn of the Sugar Merchants**, Khan al-Shakariyya / Şekerciler Han **[A, traditional]**. The pool offered 37.874 / 32.493; the journey adopts `rumi.journey.json`'s **37.8719 / 32.5089** so the two files share the pin.

**The first encounter: two incompatible accounts, both printed.** In one, Shams seizes the bridle of Rumi's mount and asks which is greater, the Prophet's *we have not known You as You should be known*, or Bayazid Bistami's *glory be to me, how great is my station*, and the professor falls from the horse **[R, Aflaki and Sultan Walad, hagiographic elaboration]**. In the other, en.wikipedia has Shams find Rumi reading and open with "**What are you doing?**" **[A]**. The journey flags the split at the arrival stop.

**The forty days.** Rumi takes Shams into his household and the two shut themselves in seclusion for forty days, food passed through the door **[R, Aflaki, Mevlevi oral tradition]**. Nothing of what passed between them is recorded by either man. **The consequence:** Rumi abandons law lecturing and the muftiship **[A, Sultan Walad; Aflaki]**.

## Phase 4: the first flight, 1246, and the return, 1247

**Hostility.** Resentment builds among Rumi's disciples and household against an outsider of no lineage or school **[A, Sultan Walad]**. The pool dated this 1245; `rumi.journey.json` dates the murmuring **1246-01-01**, and 1245 leaves an unexplained year-long gap before the flight. **Corrected to 1246-01-01.**

**The departure, corrected by a month.** fa.wikipedia dates it to **21 Shawwal 643 AH = 11 March 1246 Julian** **[A]**. The pool said "around February 1246" and `rumi.journey.json` has 1246-02-15: **both early**. The journey uses **1246-03-11**.

**Damascus.** He surfaces in the Umayyad quarter **[R, Sultan Walad]**. **Rumi's grief** during the absence, silence and verses composed and destroyed and students turned away, is recorded almost as illness **[A, Sultan Walad]**, with no duration given.

**Sultan Walad's errand, corrected.** The pool called him "teenage". **He was not.** Born **25 April 1226 at Larende** (`rumi.journey.json` carries the date; en.wikipedia says "around 1227"), he was **20 or 21** in 1247. He locates Shams and delivers his father's verse-letters **[A, Ibtidanama, the son's own account]**, and on the return refuses the offered horse to walk beside Shams's mount **[R]**. Honestly on the dating: **en.wikipedia gives no date for the errand at all**, and 1247 rests entirely on `rumi.journey.json`'s 1247-04-01. **The reunion**, spring 1247, is remembered as both men collapsing at the other's feet, onlookers unable to say who fell first **[A, Sultan Walad]**.

## Phase 5: the second Konya season, 1247

**Marriage to Kimya Khatun, over-specified in the pool and hedged here.** Rumi marries Shams to **Kimya Khatun** to anchor him in the city **[A, Sultan Walad; Lewis]**. The pool made her "the daughter of his second wife Kira Khatun by an earlier marriage". **No Wikipedia-language edition checked (en, tr, fa) states this**; tr.wikipedia says only that she was *"Celaleddin Rumi'nin evinde evlatlık olan"*, a foster child raised in Rumi's house, and en.wikipedia has no article on either woman. The journey **prints the Kerra Khatun parentage as unsupported**.

**Kimya's decline and death.** Some accounts have her failing under household strain, with her death close in time to Shams's disappearance; **the sequence is inconsistent across accounts** **[R, Lewis]**, and is not resolved here. She is the only figure in the story with no preserved voice.

**The Maqalat.** Compiled from disciples' notes during the Konya years, possibly including transcription by Sultan Walad **[A, consensus, Chittick]**. In it Shams speaks of his search for a spiritual equal, his impatience with jurisprudence, and his assessment of Rumi **[R, Maqalat, primary]**. One wrinkle: en.wikipedia calls the Maqalat flatly "a Persian prose book written by Shams", against the consensus that it is transcribed talk. The journey follows the consensus and says so in prose.

## Phase 6: the disappearance, December 1247

**The year, resolved toward 1247 with the outlier named.** tr.wikipedia gives **1247 (hicri 645)** **[A]**, and **645 AH runs 8 May 1247 to 26 April 1248**, so a December inside it can only be **December 1247**. en.wikipedia's Rumi article gives **5 December 1248** and appears to be a bad AH conversion of the same tradition **[A as text, R as fact]**. The journey adopts 1247-12-05, naming the variant in `date_confidence`.

**The night.** Shams is called to the door by an unnamed visitor, says "I am called to be killed", and walks out, never seen alive again **[R, Mevlevi oral tradition and later Ottoman-era chroniclers]**.

**The decisive negative.** **Sultan Walad's *Ibtidanama*, the earliest and most authoritative source, records no account of Shams's fate at all.** The murder narrative is a later accretion **[R, Lewis]**, and the journey states this at the stop itself. The rumor names seven men, in some versions including Rumi's second son **Ala al-Din Muhammad**, and a body found in a well near the house **[R, absent from the Ibtidanama]**. **The counter-reading:** Lewis allows that Shams simply resumed his wandering and died of natural causes elsewhere **[R]**.

## Phase 7: four graves and no body

Mutually exclusive burial traditions, all printed, none adjudicated.

| Claim | Coordinate used | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Konya, beside Baha al-Din Walad in the rose garden, under the Green Dome | 37.8706 / 32.5047 | **[R]** no marker, no witness |
| Konya, the **Makam-i Şems** (Şems-i Tebrizi Camii ve Türbesi) | **37.8737 / 32.4975** | **[R]** contents uncertain even locally |
| **Khoy**, natural death, tomb extant and a pilgrimage site | town **38.5464 / 44.9542**, tomb **38.5561 / 44.9350** | **[A]** monument, **[R]** identification |
| **Tabriz, Geçil cemetery** | 38.0827 / 46.2789, approximate | **[R]** tr.wikipedia list only |
| **Niğde, Kesikbaş Türbesi** (Tomb of the Beheaded) | 37.9667 / 34.6833, town centre | **[R]** a death fitting none of the others |
| **Multan, Shah Shams Tabrez** | 30.1955 / 71.4763 | **[R]** conflation with a later saint |

**Two coordinate corrections were required, besides Baghdad.**

1. **The Makam-i Şems was pointed at the wrong monument.** The pool gave 37.8706 / 32.5047, which en.wikipedia gives as the **Mevlana Museum**, the Green Dome, **Rumi's own tomb**; a third value, 37.8746 / 32.4932, appeared elsewhere in the pool for the same building. The Şems-i Tebrizi Camii ve Türbesi is at **37.8737 / 32.4975** (Wikidata **Q131390887**; OSM 37.87368 / 32.49755), about **700 m NW**, so "near the Green Dome" is a stretch but not false.
2. **The Khoy tomb coordinate was wrong in all three lenses** (38.5503 / 44.9522, 38.5502 / 44.9525, 38.5504 / 44.954, the last 1.8 km SE of the monument). Khoy **city** is 38.54639 / 44.95417; the **tomb** is **38.5561 / 44.9350** (Wikidata **Q16044966**, corroborated by OSM at 38.5566 / 44.9347), 2 km NW of the centre. The journey pins the settled-and-died claim on the city and the tomb-monument claim on the tomb.

**The Khoy tomb is otherwise well attested**: a grave beside a **tower monument in a memorial park**, forwarded by Iran for **UNESCO World Heritage** listing, with **three Timurid-era skeletons** found near the minaret **[A, en.wikipedia 'Tomb of Shams Tabrizi' and 'Khoy']**. The skeletons date the ground's use as a burial site and settle nothing about the identification.

## Phase 8: the afterlife

- **Rumi rides to Damascus twice** **[A, Aflaki; Lewis]** and there abandons the outward search: "Why should I seek? I am the same as He." **[R, within the transmitted teaching of the Divan]**.
- **The Divan-e Shams-e Tabrizi**: over **40,000 verses** across more than **3,000 ghazals**, mostly Persian with Arabic, Turkish and Greek lines, composed by **Rumi** under Shams's name and compiled by students after his death **[A, en.wikipedia; Lewis]**. **This is Rumi's literary act, not Shams's writing.** Critical edition **Foruzanfar, 1957**; **Nicholson**, fifty ghazals, **1898**, some since judged not authentically Rumi's; **Barks**, free reinterpretations from **Nevit Ergin's** versions rather than the Persian, disputed and dominant in North America; **Osborne**, twenty volumes from the Persian, **2020** **[A]**.
- **Maqalat**: two-volume critical Persian edition by **Movahhed**, Khwarazmi, Tehran, **1990**; English as **Chittick, *Me and Rumi*, Fons Vitae, 2004** **[A]**, the first substantial passage of Shams's own voice into English.
- **Elif Shafak, *The Forty Rules of Love*, March 2009**: over 750,000 copies across Turkey and France, on the BBC's 2019 list of 100 most influential novels **[A]**. **No contemporary portrait or description survives**; the black-cloaked figure of miniature painting is retrospective, chiefly downstream of Aflaki **[R]**.

## Interlocks with existing atlas travelers

Verified against the atlas's own files, not invented.

- **`rumi`**: the spine. Its file carries a dozen stops naming Shams directly; arrival, forty days, murmuring, Damascus flight, Sultan Walad's errand, marriage, last night and Damascus search are all inherited, with the two date corrections above.
- **`abu_yazid_al_bistami`**: Shams's opening question sets **Bayazid** against the Prophet, and Bayazid's own Kaaba stop, "he went round the House and did not see the Lord of the House, only the House", is the same paradox. Kaaba pin **21.4225 / 39.8262** inherited byte-identical.
- **`ibn_arabi`**: died and buried in Damascus, 1240-11-09, four to eight years before Shams twice took refuge there, and Kirmani belongs to his circle. **No source states the two men met**, and the atlas does not claim it.
- **`baha_ud_din_walad`**: Rumi's father, whose household fled Balkh ahead of the Mongols and reached Konya in 1229 along the same corridor, fifteen years early. His grave in the rose garden is the one Shams is said to have been laid beside.
- **`junayd_al_baghdadi`**: named at the Baghdad stop because the atlas records Rumi, a boy travelling with his father in 1219, spending thirty days at Junayd's tomb there. Shams passes through eight years later, and the two do not meet.

In the pool but **not** promoted to campa for lack of an atlas file: Sultan Walad, Kimya Khatun, Kirmani, Najm al-Din Kubra, Jandi. Salah al-Din Zarkub and Husam al-Din Chelebi are named in one campa, on `rumi.journey.json`'s own framing of them as Shams's successors.

## Honest gaps

1. **No birth year.** Every downstream figure inherits it: age at death, age at entering Sallabaf's service, "near sixty at Konya".
2. **No itinerary.** The Maqalat names cities and fixes no order or dates; the 1224 to 1244 dates are spaced inference, labelled as such at every stop.
3. **No location for the Sallabaf workshop** and no surviving site; the pool's coordinate was invented precision and was dropped.
4. **No primary citation for the Ibn Arabi insult**, and **none for a hajj**; both traditional and unverified.
5. **No account of the death in the earliest source.** Everything about the murder is later.
6. **No body, and four graves.** No source reconciles Konya with Khoy, and the Tabriz and Niğde claims are reconciled with nothing at all.
7. **Kimya Khatun has no voice in any source** and no en.wikipedia article; her stated parentage is unsupported and her death unfixed relative to the disappearance.
8. **The Multan identification** rests on consensus **not independently re-verified this session**.
