# Baha ud-Din Walad (Baha' al-Din Muhammad ibn Husayn al-Khatibi, "Sultan al-Ulama")
*Research report, 2026-08-09. Preacher and Hanafi jurist of Wakhsh and Balkh; author of the Ma'arif; father of Jalal al-Din Rumi. Traditional dates c. 1148-1231.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or hagiography · a claim with no tag has not been assessed and should not be used.

The governing difficulty of this subject is stated once here and not repeated: **Baha ud-Din Walad has no independent biography.** Almost everything known about him is preserved because it explains his son. The two document classes that are genuinely his own are (1) a waqf-nama naming him preacher and jurisconsult at Wakhsh, and (2) his own book, the Ma'arif. Everything else descends from Sultan Walad's *Ibtida-nama* (composed c. 1291, sixty years after the death), Sepahsalar's *Resaleh*, and above all Aflaki's *Manaqib al-'Arifin* (14th c.), which is a hagiography of the Mevlevi order and treats its founder's father accordingly. Where this report says "the tradition holds," it means Aflaki or a text downstream of him.

---

## I. Origins: Balkh, the name, the title

**[R]** Born in or near **Balkh**, in the Khwarazmian empire, into a line of Hanafi jurists and preachers (nisba *al-Khatibi*, "the preacher's"). Persian Wikipedia gives 543 AH / **1148**; Turkish Wikipedia gives a range, **"1148/1152"**; **Encyclopaedia Iranica** gives 546-628 AH / **1151-1231**. *Contradiction flagged, not resolved.* The Iranica figure is the standard reference-work date and is named as such in the journey file's `date_confidence`; the journey's `date` field carries 1148 because that is the date the Persian tradition, which is the tradition this register follows, actually gives.

**[R]** Claimed as a disciple of **Najm al-Din Kubra**, placing him in the Kubrawi silsila of Transoxiana. Persian Wikipedia itself notes that modern scholars dispute the documentary basis for the link. No document supports it; it is recorded here as a lineage claim, not a fact.

**[R]** The title **Sultan al-Ulama** ("Sultan of the Scholars"). Later Mevlevi hagiography has the Prophet confer it directly in a dream. **[A]** Franklin Lewis (*Rumi: Past and Present, East and West*, p. 54, quoted via Ibrahim Gamard, "Common Misconceptions About Rumi," dar-al-masnavi.org) reads the Ma'arif itself as recording only that *one spiritually elect person* dreamt of an "illumined elder" addressing him with the title:

> "O Sultan al-Ulama, come out so that the world may be filled with light..."

Lewis treats the honorific as **retrospective**, applied backward by his son's followers, and not attested in his own lifetime. **This report follows Lewis and does not treat the title as contemporary.**

## II. Wakhsh: the one documentary anchor

**[A]** A **waqf-nama discovered by Fereydun Nafiz Uzluk**, discussed by Franklin Lewis, names Baha ud-Din Walad preacher and jurisconsult of **Wakhsh**, a small town on the Vakhsh river in Khuttalan, for a long stretch of years. This is the strongest single attestation in the dossier and the basis of Fritz Meier's philological finding that **Wakhsh, not Balkh, was the family's actual home base** in the relevant period.

**[A]** English Wikipedia ("Rumi", "Sangtuda") states that Baha ud-Din "lived and worked in the town, then known as Wakhsh, as a jurist and preacher until 1212."

**[A]** **Jalal al-Din Rumi born 30 September 1207**, at Wakhsh. En-wiki: "born to Persian parents, in Wakhsh, a village on the East bank of the Wakhsh River known as Sangtuda in present-day Tajikistan." **[R]** The hagiographic tradition places the birth at Balkh instead; the two accounts cannot both be literally true. *Contradiction flagged. The journey file follows the documentary account (Wakhsh) and says so.*

### Coordinate correction, applied across all lenses
The research pool gave Wakhsh as **37.95, 69.75** (variously 37.95/69.7333, 37.96/69.75) in **three separate lenses**. All three are wrong by roughly 60 km: that point lands essentially on **Kulob**. Medieval Wakhsh (Lewis: "the medieval town of Wakhsh or Lewkand") is identified by en-wiki "Sangtuda" with modern **Sangtuda, Danghara District, Khatlon**, at 38°02'N 69°05'E = **38.033, 69.083**. Note also that this is *not* the modern town called Vakhsh, Tajikistan (37.711, 68.831), a different place ~35 km away with no Rumi connection. **The journey file uses 38.033, 69.083 throughout.**

**[R]** The **mother's name** is not settled. Persian Wikipedia gives **Mu'mina Khatun**; a separate Persian Wikipedia redirect page gives **Gohara/Gohar Khatun, daughter of Sharaf al-Din Lala of Samarkand**. The Gohar Khatun detail probably belongs to a later generation (Rumi's own wife, Gowhar Khatun, mother of Sultan Walad). *Recorded as an open conflation; not resolved.*

## III. The quarrel and the departure

**[A]** Baha ud-Din's own denunciation of **Fakhr al-Din Razi** survives in his own words (*Ma'aref*, vol. I, p. 82, quoted in Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Baha'-al-Din Mohammad Walad"): the theologians have

> "taken refuge in two or three dark corners while abandoning the numerous miracles and proofs [of positive religion]"

This is attested polemic. **[R]** What is *not* attested is a face-to-face confrontation, or the further chain in which the court scholars complain that Baha ud-Din is drawing away the Khwarazmshah's following. That chain is Aflaki-derived.

**[R]** **Muhammad II Khwarazmshah** allegedly orders him out of Balkh with the line "two rulers cannot coexist in one region." Legendary; Lewis treats the causal story with skepticism.

**[R]** **The motive for leaving.** Three incompatible explanations circulate and none is documented:
1. the Mongol threat (the standard telling; but the Mongol sack of Balkh is **1220-1221**, after most chronologies already have the family gone);
2. the quarrel with the Khwarazmshah;
3. personal injury. Semazen's English page, "The Life of Baha al-Din Valad" (w1.semazen.net), renders Sultan Walad's account as: *"Sultan Valad wrote that his grandfather had migrated because the people of Balkh offended him and broke his heart."* **[R]**. This wording is confirmed present on that page, but it is semazen's own English prose summarising Sultan Walad, **not a verbatim translation of the Ibtida-nama**, and the journey file quotes it with the attributive frame intact for exactly that reason. I could not reach the Ibtida-nama text itself.

## IV. The road west

**[A]** The **route** is the best-attested thing in the whole dossier, better attested than any of its dates. En-wiki "Rumi" gives: **Samarkand - Nishapur - Baghdad - Mecca - Damascus - Malatya - Erzincan - Sivas - Kayseri - Nigde - Larende - Konya.** Every stop in the journey file's middle segments comes from that list.

**[A]** **Samarkand, c. 1212**, "when Rumi was around five" (en-wiki "Rumi", en-wiki "Sangtuda"). *The pool's chronology lens gave 1213; corrected to 1212.*

**[R]** **Nishapur and Attar.** The aged **Farid al-Din Attar** receives the family and gives the boy a copy of the *Asrar-nama*, predicting he will "set fire to the souls of the world's lovers." Sources: Dawlatshah Samarqandi, *Tadhkirat al-Shu'ara*; Aflaki. No contemporary record. Modern scholars variously date it to the 1210s, the 1220s, or doubt it entirely.

**[A]** **Baghdad** as a route stop (en-wiki "Rumi"; fa-wiki). **[R]** Everything attached to the stop: the reception, the refusal of lodging in the great khanqahs, and the meeting with **Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi** (Aflaki). **Date [R]:** the pool gave 1216; *no source consulted fixes a year*, and that interpolation is marked as reconstruction, not attestation.

**[R]** The line attributed to Baha ud-Din at Baghdad, "We came from God. We again are going to Him. We have no strength except for God." (w1.semazen.net) has **no named translator and no critical edition**, and I could not trace it to Aflaki, Sepahsalar, or the Ma'arif. Used in the journey file as tradition, tagged as such.

**[A]** **Mecca**, the hajj (en-wiki "Rumi"; fa-wiki). **Date unfixed by any contemporary source**; the pool's "1217" is unsourced interpolation. **[R]** **Medina** and the Prophet's tomb (Sepahsalar, *Resaleh*): customary, undocumented.

**[R]** **Damascus.** The city is on en-wiki's route list **[A]**, but the added detail that local authorities wished to retain him and he declined is Aflaki-derived. *The pool tagged this [A]; corrected down to [R].* Later tellings also fold Rumi's own later study years in Damascus into this same visit, a conflation modern scholars separate out.

**[R]** **"An ocean is walking behind a lake."** Attributed to **Ibn al-Arabi** watching Baha ud-Din pass with his son. Aflaki and later Mevlevi tradition. Lewis regards it as apocryphal or unverifiable; Ibn al-Arabi's own dated presence in Damascus does not align with every version of the route.

**[R]** **Aleppo** appears in some reconstructions on **geographic grounds alone**. No named primary source places the family there. Kept on the map, marked as inference, because a land route from Damascus into Anatolia has few alternatives.

## V. Into Rum

**[A]** **Malatya**, then **Erzincan**, **Sivas**, **Kayseri**, **Nigde** (en-wiki "Rumi" route list). Three corrections applied to the pool's geography lens:

1. **Malatya coordinates.** The Great Mosque (Ulu Cami) of Malatya stands at **Battalgazi / Eski Malatya**, 38°25'14"N 38°22'00"E = **38.4205, 38.3668** per the en-wiki infobox. The pool's 38.3987, 38.2734 is ~10 km SW, in modern Malatya's outskirts. The journey file instead inherits the atlas's own established crossing pin, **38.4228, 38.3656** (rumi.journey.json), which is the same place. *Also flagged: the mosque is conventionally dated to c. 1224 under Kayqubad I, i.e. AFTER the crossing. The "newly built mosque" framing in the pool is struck and the mismatch is stated in the campa.* (The building was destroyed in the 6 February 2023 earthquake.)
2. **Sivas Ulu Cami was not "a century old."** En-wiki, "Great Mosque of Sivas": built **1196-1197** under Qutbuddin Malik Shah, minaret added **c. 1213**. In the 1220s it was ~25 years old. Only Michael Meinecke's minority redating to 1109 would support "a century," and that would have to be named. Corrected.
3. **Erzincan: tag dropped [A] → [R].** The pool claimed Malika **Ismati Khatun**'s patronage had "corroborating note in regional Seljuk histories." **I could not substantiate this.** En-wiki "Erzincan" mentions neither her nor a madrasa for Baha ud-Din; en-wiki "Mengujekids" names no wife of Fakhr al-Din Bahramshah by that name (only a daughter, Turan Melik, who built the Divrigi Darussifa in 1228). Bahramshah's reign, 1162-1225, makes the visit chronologically possible, and no more than possible. **The claim of corroboration is struck as unverified.**

**Direct contradiction at Erzincan, not resolved.** Three mutually exclusive accounts of the same town:
- **hosted**: Ismati Khatun builds him a madrasa (Aflaki, and rumi.journey.json dates it September 1221);
- **refused entry**: Aflaki himself, *Manaqeb al-'arefin* vol. I p. 22 (ed. Yazici, Ankara 1959), quoted in Encyclopaedia Iranica, says the party would not enter the city **"because of the evil nature of its people"**;
- **resident four years**: Jami's later claim, cited and contradicted in the same Iranica passage.

**[R]** **Aksehir.** Some reconstructions insert a stop before Larende; en-wiki "Rumi" routes Nigde straight to Larende. The two route traditions are not reconciled; Aksehir is the weaker. Retained on the map, marked disputed, because an omitted stage is as much a claim as an added one.

## VI. Larende (Karaman), the seven years

**[A]** **Arrival 1222.** En-wiki "Karaman": "In 1222 the Sufi preacher Bahaeddin Veled arrived in town with his family," and a madrasah was built to accommodate them. *The pool's chronology lens gave 1221 while its geography lens gave 1222; the two lenses contradicted each other. Corrected to 1222 on the en-wiki wording.* The **seven-year stay** is confirmed by en-wiki "Rumi": the longest stop of the entire journey.

**[A]** **Death of Rumi's mother, 1224.** En-wiki "Karaman" states it flatly: "Rumi's mother died in 1224. She was buried, along with other family members, in the Aktekke Mosque." *The pool framed the year as disputed; that overstates the disagreement, and the framing is corrected.* **But the mosque did not exist.** En-wiki dates the **Aktekke Mosque (Mader-i Mevlana Camii) to 1370**, built by Alaeddin Ali Bey "to replace the original madrasah." She was buried at the site of the madrasa; the mosque was raised over it ~146 years later. The journey file says this rather than implying a contemporaneous mosque burial.

**[A]** **Marriage of Jalal al-Din to Gowhar Khatun** at Larende, c. 1225 (en-wiki "Karaman"; Ibtida-nama).

**[R]** **Birth of Sultan Walad.** *Source-attribution error in the pool, corrected.* En-wiki "Sultan Walad" gives **1227** in both lead and infobox and **names no birthplace at all**: "Sultan Walad was born around 1227 to Rumi and his wife Jowhar Khatun." **Larende as birthplace, and the date 623 AH / 1226, come from the Persian and Turkish tradition, not from English Wikipedia.** Tag dropped [A] → [R] for the birthplace; the 1226/1227 divergence is shown, not smoothed.

**[R]** **Other family burials** at the same Larende site. En-wiki "Karaman" says "other family members" without a list; one account names a brother of Rumi. No complete or consistent roster survives. **Recorded as a gap.**

## VII. Konya

**[R]** The **invitation** from **Ala al-Din Kayqubad I** is standard in every Mevlevi source and corroborated by none outside them.

**[A]** **Arrival and settlement.** En-wiki "Rumi": **1 May 1228**. Persian Wikipedia: **626 AH**, roughly 1229. The atlas's own **rumi.journey.json**: **May 1229**. *Three-way divergence of about a year, flagged in `date_confidence`, not resolved.* The journey file uses 1228-05-01 because that is the most precise attested figure.

**[A]** **Head of a madrasa** in Konya, preaching under Seljuk patronage (en-wiki "Rumi").

**[A]** **Death.** Most commonly **628 AH / 1231**, with **12 January 1231** given by en-wiki "Mevlana Museum". **Turkish Wikipedia gives 1230.** **Persian Wikipedia additionally flags a much earlier alternative dating, 618-619 AH.** The atlas's **rumi.journey.json gives February 1231.** *Four-way divergence, flagged, not resolved.* Journey file uses 1231-01-12.

**[R]** Kayqubad mourns deeply and distributes alms for a week (Aflaki).

**[A]** **Burial** in a **rose garden** given by Kayqubad (en-wiki "Mevlana Museum"); Rumi is buried beside him in 1273.

**[A]** **Rumi inherits the position**, aged about twenty-four (en-wiki "Rumi").

## VIII. The book, and the book that is not it

**[A]** The **Ma'arif**: Baha ud-Din's collected sermons, visions and mystical reflections in Persian prose, which Rumi is said to have carried and drawn on lifelong.

**Conflation corrected.** The pool's chronology lens called the father's book "Ma'arif (also called Ma'arif-i Waladi)." **This is wrong and the alias is struck.** The **Ma'arif-i Waladi** ("The Waladi Gnosis") is a **separate work by Sultan Walad**, the grandson: en-wiki "Sultan Walad" describes it as "a Persian prose work featuring 56 sermons and lectures," with a scholarly edition by **Nadjib Mayil-i Hirawi, Tehran 1367/1988** (independent bibliographic search returns the same: Tehran, Mowla, 1988). The **1988 Hirawi edition is of the son's book, not the father's.** Secondary sources conflate the two constantly; the pool's afterlife lens flagged the conflation as unresolved and it is resolved here in favour of Sultan Walad.

**[A]** Translated selections of the father's Ma'arif, attributed on dar-al-masnavi.org to **A. J. Arberry** and reproduced by Ibrahim Gamard ("Selections from Baha'uddin Walad's Book"), supply the journey file's five direct quotations (chs. 1, 6, 13). **Gap:** I could not independently confirm Arberry's publication details beyond that reproduction.

**[A]** **English trade translation:** Coleman Barks and John Moyne, *The Drowned Book: Ecstatic and Earthly Reflections of Bahauddin, the Father of Rumi.* *Pool correction applied:* Open Library returns publisher and more editions than the pool's first query found: **HarperSanFrancisco (2003, 2004), HarperOne / HarperCollins (2005, 2008, 2009)**, author credited as Baha al-Din Valad. The canonical first trade edition is **HarperSanFrancisco, 2004**; 2003 is an e-book/advance record and 2005 the HarperOne reissue. The journey file gives 2004.

**[R] and unverified:** the **Foruzanfar critical Persian edition** of the Ma'arif, commonly cited as standard in later scholarship. **Encyclopaedia Iranica's article returned HTTP 403 on WebFetch and the web.archive.org mirror could not be retrieved.** This rests on general recollection of the secondary literature, not on a source read for this report. **Do not treat as attested.** (Note: the Iranica quotations used elsewhere in this report reached me through the research pool, not through a live fetch.)

## IX. Afterlife

**[A]** Mausoleum over the two graves completed **1274** under architect **Badr al-Din Tabrizi**, commissioned by Sultan Walad; expansions continue to **1854** (en-wiki "Mevlana Museum").

**[A]** **Gold-embroidered Koranic brocade**, gift of **Sultan Abdul Hamid II, 1894**. *Pool correction:* the pool flagged a gap over which tomb it covers. **There is no gap**. En-wiki "Mevlana Museum" attributes it to **Mevlana's (Rumi's) sarcophagus**, not his father's. Caveat removed.

**[A]** **1926** Republican decree; the complex reopens **2 March 1927** as a museum; formally designated the **Mevlana Museum in 1954**.

**[R]** Mevlevi legend that at Rumi's funeral his father's grave opened, or his father's body rose to greet the new coffin, commonly traced to Aflaki. **Unverified this session:** WebFetch checks against en-wiki "Shams-i Tabrizi" and a nonexistent dedicated Aflaki article did not surface it. Recorded as a claim in circulation.

**[A]** **The bibliographic gap itself, stated as a finding:** there is **no dedicated English Wikipedia article on Baha ud-Din Walad**. He appears only inside the articles on Rumi, Sultan Walad, the Mevlana Museum, the Seljuk dynasty, and Derbe; the search interface flags the absence and invites article creation. His son has articles in dozens of languages. This asymmetry is the closing stop of the journey file.

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. **Birth year:** 1148 (fa-wiki) / 1151 (Iranica) / "1148-1152" (tr-wiki).
2. **Birthplace of Rumi:** Wakhsh (documentary, Meier/Lewis) vs Balkh (hagiography). Irreconcilable.
3. **Departure year and motive:** 609-610 AH ≈ 1212-1214, vs the c. 1219 departure the atlas's own rumi.journey.json narrates. Motive: Mongols / the shah / personal injury.
4. **The Samarkand siege.** Aflaki has the family inside Samarkand during a Mongol siege dated 1220, years after other accounts have them west of Khorasan. genghis_khan.journey.json dates the fall of Samarkand to **16 March 1220**. The two cannot both hold. The journey file keeps the stop, out of geographic order, and says why.
5. **Erzincan:** hosted / refused entry / resident four years (see §V).
6. **Ibn al-Arabi in Damascus:** rumi.journey.json dates the meeting **August 1220**; ibn_arabi.journey.json dates it **1224**. Two existing atlas files contradict each other on the same three people in the same street. **Flagged here, not silently harmonised.**
7. **Sultan Walad's birth:** 1226 (Persian/Turkish tradition, with Larende named) vs 1227 (en-wiki, no birthplace named).
8. **Konya arrival:** 1 May 1228 / 626 AH ≈ 1229 / May 1229 (rumi.journey.json).
9. **Death:** 12 January 1231 / 1230 (tr-wiki) / February 1231 (rumi.journey.json) / 618-619 AH (fa-wiki's alternative).
10. **The mother's name:** Mu'mina Khatun vs Gohar Khatun, the latter probably a generational conflation.
11. **Aksehir:** on the route or not on the route.
