# Duwenavue Sante' Johnson: research report

*2026-08-12, for the Atlas of Journeys. Living subject: no death record, no tomb. Calendar gregorian.*

**[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or corpus assertion without independent corroboration. Sections 1 to 7 state the record; 8 to 11 carry the corrections, contradictions and gaps. **dsj** = duwenavuesantejohnson.com, a stale portfolio, nothing after 2021, no V&A, no Masonic role. **axs** = artxstitch.com, the current professional record.

**The fact governing the dossier: she keeps two self-published sites that disagree with each other.** Where they conflict, both readings are recorded and neither is chosen; a biography built from dsj alone is materially wrong, and an earlier pass was built that way. A second hazard: the dsj CV is one undifferentiated column, lectures, performances, exhibitions and certificates in a single list with no category marks. Most of the misreadings in section 8 originate there.

## 5. The Masonic research: documented against reconstructed

**[A, essay 203_masonic_embroidery.md; axs/duwenavue]** She teaches embroidery at the Masonic Library and Museum of Pennsylvania, curates the student work, researches on site. Essay 203: "an artist, curator, researcher and teacher of Masonic embroidery in Philadelphia, USA."

**[R, essay 203 only]** That the research concerns the **Washington-Lafayette correspondence** specifically. This appears in essay 203 and on no external source reached. Her documented archival research is on the composer Francis Johnson, at a different institution (section 7).

**[R, corpus tradition, contradicted by the museum] The apron.** Essay 203 states the Library displays a ceremonial apron of **Chinese silk**, embroidered by **Madame de Lafayette**, presented to **General Washington in 1771**. The Masonic Temple's own page says only: "A Masonic apron thought to be made for Brother George Washington, which was presented to the Grand Lodge of Pennsylvania in 1829 by the Washington Benevolent Society." No Lafayette, no Chinese silk, no 1771.

> **The 1771 date is impossible on three counts; flagged, not adjudicated.** Washington was not a general until June 1775; Lafayette was 13 and did not meet him until August 1777; Adrienne de Noailles did not marry Lafayette until 11 April 1774, and born 2 November 1759 she was 11 turning 12 in 1771, not thirteen as an earlier note said.

**Interlock, [A] within the atlas.** `madame_de_lafayette.journey.json` (154-163) puts the embroidering at the rue de Bourbon house in Paris and quotes the Grand Lodge verbatim, "This cannot be documented as fact": the only stop in the searched corpus whose central claim is apron embroidery by a **named needlewoman**. `george_washington.journey.json` (402-410) narrates the same apron as "the Marques de Lafayette apron," carried by John Rutledge Jr. **Johnson's employer is already a quoted primary source in this atlas, before she has a file of her own.**

**Reconstructed research geography [R]**, sited from subject matter and not from any record of her presence, each tagged in `date_confidence`. Canonical pins are inherited **byte-identical**: **Mount Vernon 38.7292, -77.0864** from `george_washington.journey.json`; the **Philadelphia Masonic pin 39.95, -75.1477** from `benjamin_franklin.journey.json` (190), reused in `prince_hall.journey.json` (373-381), which documents the inheritance rule; **Tyre 33.2704, 35.1937** from `hiram_abiff.journey.json` (12-14); and the corpus **Temple Mount 31.778, 35.2354**. **Fredericksburg Lodge No. 4** (38.3032, -77.4605) holds the lodge-attested 1752 initiation on which the unattested apron legends were later hung, the fault line `george_washington.journey.json` line 77 states exactly. The Jerusalem and Tyre stops carry the apron's iconographic origin, with the Exodus 28:39 and 39:29 girdle of the Kohanim as the second lineage essay 203 names: genealogical, not biographical.

## 6. The looted lodges

All of this is in **essay 203_masonic_embroidery.md, lines 12 and 14**, not essay 205 as an earlier pass had it.

- **[A]** 1919: Masonic properties seized across **Hungary**, lodges outlawed the following year; 1922, **the Soviet Union**; 1924-1925, **Italy**, where Mussolini forbids co-membership then disbands the order. All three are country-level in the essay, so the Budapest pin is the atlas's own localization and the journey says so, while the Soviet and Italian items get no city stop.
- **[A]** 1934: the Reich Ministry of the Interior dissolves all German lodges. 1937: **Goebbels inaugurates the first Anti-Masonic Exposition**; venue not named in the source. **Counter-fact recovered:** dsj carries a **Berlin 5x5** project page and her bio lists Berlin among her venues, so Berlin is not purely reconstructed for her; the journey keeps the threads distinct.
- **[A]** 1941, **Belgrade**: the **Grand Anti-Masonic Exhibition**, "the largest collection of Masonic embroideries in history." **Gap:** city and event named, no venue building; the site is unidentified and left so.
- **[A]** **Museo di Salo**, Via Brunati 9 (45.6072, 10.5222), "L'ultimo Fascismo: La Repubblica Sociale Italiana": hand embroidered flags and uniform details, **fascist-era** and not Masonic. **KreisMuseum Wewelsburg**, "Ideology and Terror of SS": the essay says the collection includes "similar German artifacts of fascist fabric culture," and does **not** say it holds looted Masonic embroidery.
- **[R, essay 205]** What 205 does contain: Johnson's explanation, as the narrator recalls it, that lodge records kept the chain of **owners** meticulously while the **woman who made the object** went unnamed; and the open question whether "secret stitches on the reverse side or hidden in plain view" encode messages from anonymous makers. Corpus speculation, not scholarship.
