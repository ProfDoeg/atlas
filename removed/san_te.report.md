# San Te (San-De, 三德): research report
*2026-08-28. Legendary Shaolin monk of Xichan Temple, Guangzhou; in the canon a disciple of Zhi Shan and founder of the thirty-sixth chamber. Born, in the postwar telling, Liu Yude (劉裕德) of Huiyang, Guangdong.*

**Legend: [A]** = attested, source named and reachable · **[R]** = reconstruction or literary tradition · chapter references to 《聖朝鼎盛萬年青》 are to the Chinese Wikisource text.

---

## The evidentiary frame, which governs everything below

There is no documented San Te. **[R, and the most important finding in the file]** No eighteenth-century ordination register, Xichan inscription or abbatial list, Fujian or Henan Shaolin roll, Qing memorial, criminal file, wanted notice, military report, execution record, gazetteer entry, Liu-family genealogy, merchant record, or datable martial manual naming San Te or Liu Yude has been located by any research consulted. The completeness of the search behind that statement could not itself be verified by me, so it is a negative finding, not a proof.

What exists instead is a two-stage textual invention:

1. **1893 [A]**: the anonymous Cantonese court-case novel 《聖朝鼎盛萬年青》, 76 chapters, first printed in parts by Shanghai's Wucai (Five Colors) Company. Earliest securely datable appearance of the name. San Te counsels restraint, fails, and is killed by government troops in chapter 63; the narrator is loyal to the Qing and hostile to him.
2. **1947-48 [R]**: 我是山人 (I Am a Mountain Man)'s 《三德和尚三探西禪寺》, which invents the civil name, the birthplace, the merchant father, the killings, the flight, the ordeal, and the tonsure verse, and reverses the politics of the whole cycle.

Everything now believed about San Te's *life* comes from stage two, everything about his *death* from stage one, and the thirty-six chambers from neither: they are a 1978 screenplay. The journey file is written in the register **"national mythology: the canon is true"**, so the campas narrate the canon straight and the evidentiary apparatus lives in `date_confidence`, stop by stop.

## Phase 1: the merchant's son at Shuangmendi (story time: late Yongzheng, c. 1733)

- Born in **Huiyang**, Guangdong, to a family trading tea and paper in the provincial capital; **four hundred jin of arm strength**, the "Iron Arm" epithet. **[R:** 我是山人, ch. 1, qimao.com per the dossier, not re-fetched.**]**
- The birth year **c. 1718-1719 is calculated, not stated [R]**: the novel makes him sixteen in Yongzheng's final years. No source gives a year.
- The shop stands at **Shuangmendi near Hui'ai Street [R]**, a quarter with Banner households among Cantonese shopkeepers. The journey file's 23.128, 113.262 places that quarter approximately inside present Yuexiu District and is **not** a located address.
- Kills a bystanding shopkeeper in the fight with the Banner martial artist **Bai Feilong** (ch. 2) **[R]**, then kills **Shang Hu**, deputy commander of the Yellow Banner, with a chair (ch. 3) **[R]**. No Qing judicial or Banner record of either death has been found.

## Phase 2: the flight east (story time: c. 1733-1734)

- Flees to **Huiyang**, then **Zhangzhou, Fujian**, where the tea grower **Lin Boping**, a friend of his late father, shelters him about three months and gives him **500 taels** after a **100-tael bounty** is posted. He then meets the monk **Yuankong**, who recognizes his connection to the teacher **Lin Tiegui** and leads him to the Fujian Shaolin monastery. **[R:** ch. 3.**]**
- Zhangzhou coordinates are city-centre only; no residence is named. A gap.

## Phase 3: three years at the southern monastery (story time: c. 1734-1737)

- **Zhi Shan** accepts him on trial. Year one: menial labour. Year two: **300 jin of firewood and 50 loads of water daily**. Year three: striking a tree until it breaks. Then the admission test with **Hong Xiguan** and **Tong Qianjin**, tonsure, and the name. **[R:** ch. 3.**]**
- The **tonsure verse** ("the boundless Buddha-dharma aids the completion of my work; one spark of numinous light: Three Virtues shall be his name") is a **literary etymology found only in the 1947-48 novel [R]**; the 1893 text never explains it.
- **Site of the monastery: unresolved [R].** Coordinate **corrected**: Linquan Yuan (林泉院) is in 林山村, a village of 西天尾鎮, Putian, whose centre Chinese Wikipedia gives as 25.48782, 119.0377, putting the site near **25.47, 118.96**, not the dossier's 25.42, 118.95 (6 km too far south).
- Excavations **[A:** zh.wikipedia 南少林寺**]**: Putian/Linquan 1987, announced 25 April 1992 at the Great Hall of the People; the rival Fuqing site 4 June 1993; Quanzhou's Dongchan named 鎮國東禪少林寺 in 880. **None is confirmed as San Te's monastery.**

### "Main Shaolin" versus "Southern Shaolin"

The queue brief's phrasing (San Te "moved from the main Shaolin Monastery to Xichan Monastery") conflates two traditions and has been corrected in the drafting. **[A:** Shahar 2008, author/title/publisher/year confirmed, 1641 and 1735 corroborated on English Wikipedia.**]** Henan Shaolin's major devastation was **1641**, late-Ming warfare associated with Li Zicheng, **before the Qing conquest**; the **Yongzheng** court **financed a reconstruction in 1735**. The story of a vengeful Qing state burning Shaolin is spurious as history of the Henan institution. The Fujian question remains open.

One journey stop (Songshan, 1735) records the Henan monastery at its own pin and **explicitly not as a visit**; its `date_confidence` says no source places San Te there. Coordinate **corrected** to 34.5081, 112.9354; the dossier's afterlife lens carried 34.5086, 113.0359, 9 km east, contradicting its own geography lens.

## Phase 4: Xichan Temple, Guangzhou (story time: Qianlong era)

- **Zhi Shan dispatches San Te and Tong Qianjin to Guangzhou** to correct the Xichan monks' discipline. **[R:** later chapters of the 1947-48 novel, per the dossier, not re-fetched.**]**
- At Xichan, **Hu Huiqian** of Xinhui hangs a lantern announcing he will fight the weaving workshops (機房) and kills thirteen men. **[A:** ch. 14, fetched directly: 新會胡惠乾專打機房, 共計打死十三人.**]**
- San Te repeatedly counsels restraint. **[A:** ch. 14, verbatim 凡事知足不辱.**]** He is the *brake* on the violence in the earliest text, not its author, worth saying because the screen tradition later makes him a militant.
- **Lei Dapeng** coerces the letters out of him. **[A:** ch. 16, fetched directly; the dossier left the episode unchaptered.**]** Framing corrected: San Te does *not* convene a network. Lei threatens the monks collectively (若不寫信，莫怪我得罪你各僧人) and he writes under duress.
- The brothers gather not at Xichan but at **Guangxiao Temple** (光孝寺). **[A:** ch. 16.**]**

## Phase 5: the siege and the death (story time: Qianlong era; text: 1893)

- Governor **Zeng Bizhong** orders a thousand guards and a thousand archers to surround Xichan at the third watch; the court sends **Gao Jinzhong**. **[R:** ch. 61, literary; no Qing military record of such an operation exists.**]**
- San Te urges flight to Fujian Shaolin to consult Zhi Shan. **[R:** ch. 62.**]**
- Gao beheads Hu Huiqian and throws the head, which strikes San Te on the head. **[A:** ch. 63: 剛剛打在三德和尚頭上.**]** The dossier's addition, that San Te splits the head with his own blade before realising Hu is dead, **is not in the text and has been dropped.**
- Fang De cuts his left shoulder (在他的左膊之上); he falls and the attackers 將三德砍為肉餅. **[A:** ch. 63, fetched directly.**]**
- **No burial, no date, no successor, no relic**, here or anywhere later.

## Phase 6: the ground itself

The temple was real, and its documented fate has nothing to do with San Te. **[A:** Sohu local history, sohu.com/a/683168220_121124386, dates confirmed verbatim.**]**

- Founded 唐末五代初; renovated 北宋太平興國年間 (976-984); on the turtle-shaped hill 龜岡, hence 龜峰 and 靈峰.
- **1907** (Guangxu 33): converted to the 巡警西關第二分局, Second Sub-Bureau of the Xiguan police.
- **1924**: auctioned to fund the Northern Expedition, reserve 150 yuan, then demolished.
- **1933**: 廣州市立第五十一小學, Municipal **No. 51 Primary School**, built on the site.

The 1933 point **corrects the dossier**, which made that school today's Guangzhou No. 4 Middle School. Different institutions, and the merge concealed a contradiction (see below). The Xichan coordinate is reconciled across the whole file to **23.1314, 113.2433**, what Chinese Wikipedia gives for No. 4 Middle School at 廣東省廣州市荔灣區康王北路蘿荻街1號; the dossier carried three inconsistent values (23.1257/113.2465, 23.1291/113.2644, 23.1224/113.244), the second about 2 km east in Yuexiu. Also confirmed **[A]**: this is the **Guangzhou** Xichan, not the surviving Fuzhou temple of the same name.

### The name 三德 ("Three Virtues")

**[A:** Ding Fubao, 佛學大辭典, entry 三德, fetched directly.**]** The entry gives **four** senses, and the dossier conflated two:

1. The Nirvana triad **法身德 / 般若德 / 解脫德**, glossed 如伊字之三點, like the three dots of the letter *i*.
2. A separate 自利利他 triad, **智德 / 斷德 / 恩德** (恩德，具大悲而救濟一切眾生者). 智德 and 斷德 are **not** alternate labels for 般若德 and 解脫德.
3. 因圓德 / 果圓德 / 恩圓德. 4. The Samkhya guna triad.

**No sense resembling "harmony" appears anywhere in the entry.** The queue brief's gloss "Three Harmonies" is folk etymology. The 1922 compilation date is carried from the dossier, unverified by me.

## Contradictions (flagged, not adjudicated)

1. **Is San Te the 住持 of Xichan?** The quotes lens supplies a verbatim ch. 14 string beginning 西禪寺住持三德和尚及洪熙官, reading him as resident abbot. A verification pass on the same chapter reports that ch. 14 treats 西禪寺住持 as a *distinct* role and calls him only 三德和尚, ch. 16 styling him 三德禪師. **The research pool contradicts itself about its own primary text.** Quotation used as supplied, dispute flagged in `date_confidence`; somebody should re-read ch. 14 with this question in hand.
2. **The magistrate's detention.** Two lenses claim San Te and Hong Xiguan were detained after the thirteen killings and released pending inquiry; a direct read of ch. 16 found no such arrest. Not narrated as fact, not dropped from the record either.
3. **When did Xichan Temple disappear?** Sohu: a police station until the 1924 auction and demolition. Chinese Wikipedia's 西禪寺 disambiguation page: 南漢入宋後毀, tenth century. Not both.
4. **The school on the site.** Sohu: No. 51 Primary School, 1933. No. 4 Middle School's own entry: founded 1917, 於佛教名剎西禪寺舊址開辦, seven years *before* Sohu's demolition.
5. **The 1947-48 book editions.** Xiongming, five volumes, 1948 (collector's copyright-page survey) versus Hong Kong Dazhong, 1947 (Chen Longting), preface dated March 1948 by a third source. Not reconcilable.
6. **The TVB serial's year.** 1987 versus 1988; 1986 has no support at all.
7. **The 1978 film.** Runtime 111 minutes (HKFA) versus 115 (English Wikipedia); HKFA dates production 1977 against the 2 February 1978 premiere. Both dates are in the file.
