# Eduardo S. Rodríguez Ortega: research report

*Written 2026-08-17 for `eduardo_s_rodriguez_ortega.journey.json`. Subject: the engineer credited with two adjacent buildings on one block of Avenida Rivadavia, Balvanera, Buenos Aires: the Casa de los Lirios (2027/2031) and the corner building at Ayacucho (2001), popularly "No hi ha somnis impossibles".*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, family testimony, or an inference of this pass. Contradictions are flagged and left open; gaps stated as gaps.

### 0.2 A claim in `antoni_gaudi.journey.json` is contradicted by this pass

That file's Casa Batlló stop says Rodríguez Ortega "casts an iron dragon of his own onto Avenida Rivadavia", with a `suggested_ref` and source citation pointing at a stop `"the iron dragon", ~1907-12`.

**The dragon may not be his.** Noticias Abasto and La Nación (nid922238) both place the two scaled cast-iron Dragon Gate replicas on the *terrace*, added during Fernando Lorenzi's 1999-2001 restoration. If that is right, Gaudí's file credits Rodríguez Ortega with an object made sixty years after his death, and, worse under the block rule, a file closing in 1926 carries a pointer to a 2026 artifact. This pass does **not** resolve it: no source states unambiguously whether any dragon was on the original fabric. Only the operator can edit the Gaudí file.

## 1. Origins, name, and dates: an empty biography

- Documented as an Argentine **ingeniero**, not a formally titled architect. **[A: both La Nación articles, El Cronista, El Ojo del Arte, Noticias Abasto, La Divina, Huellas; Síntesis Comuna 3 has "ingeniero civil"]** **Contradiction:** Spanish Wikipedia "Balvanera" calls him *arquitecto*, the lone outlier against seven. **[A for the disagreement]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved: birth and death years.** **1871-1938** (La Nación nid922238, Noticias Abasto, La Divina, Huellas), the majority; **1831-1938** (Spanish Wikipedia, El Ojo del Arte), a 107-year span that would seat him at 72 for his first commission, very likely a slip for 1871, though **no source says so** and this pass does not assert it **[R]**; **1878-1931** (El Cronista). No civil registry entry, parish record or obituary was located. **[A: gap confirmed by search]** The journey file uses 1871 to order the segments and says so.
- **Gap.** No birthplace, parents, marriage record or nationality document. Spanish Wikipedia calls him Argentine; Barriada.com.ar (403 to this pass) calls him a Spanish engineer. Unadjudicated. **[R]**
- **Gap.** No institution, date or graduation year for his training. A Brave Search query for "Rodríguez Ortega ingeniero Universidad de Buenos Aires" ran successfully and returned no UBA or professional-register record. **[A: a negative result, not a tool failure]**

**Correction of the prior pass's own note:** an earlier round hedged this gap as possibly a tool artifact, the search budget having run out before the query. Withdrawn. Brave Search was reachable and used; it returns highly specific results for him and no biographical document at all, and Spanish Wikipedia full-text search returns exactly three articles naming him. **The gap is a finding: abundant press coverage of two buildings, zero documentation of the man.**

## 2. Europe: one family's word, and nothing else

Everything here rests on one testimony: the engineer's grandson and namesake, interviewed by Germán Wille for La Nación, 10 February 2021.

- Studied in **Berlin**, returned an *ingeniero civil*. Asked what his grandfather built there, the grandson says only *hizo muchas cosas*. **[R: family testimony, uncorroborated; no register, diploma, commission, drawing or employer traced]** The Charlottenburg polytechnic in the journey file is this pass's inference from the period's usual route for foreign engineers, attested by nothing. **[R, flagged in `date_confidence`]**
- **Barcelona: no source places him there, or in Spain at all.** Spanish Wikipedia says only "ha sido catalogado como un admirador del arquitecto Antonio Gaudí" and does not say who catalogued him. The grandson, asked whether the two men met, does not know. **[R: the journey file keeps a Barcelona stop as an explicitly-flagged open question; see §8]**
- Gaudí's own dates: degree and Barcelona studio **1878**, died **10 June 1926**. **[A: El Cronista]** He never crossed the Atlantic.

## 3. The Palacio / Casa de los Lirios, 1903-1905

- Built **1903-1905**. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, El Ojo del Arte, La Nación (Wille, 10 Feb 2021)]** **Contradiction:** the older La Nación piece (nid922238) instead dates *both* buildings together, 1903-1907.
- Commissioned by **Miguel Capurro**, "un empresario relacionado con lo textil y con la industria del vino", who also invested in property. **[A: La Nación 2021, El Ojo del Arte, El Cronista]** *Corrected: an earlier round had him as textiles only.*
- **Contradiction, not resolved: the street number.** Spanish Wikipedia "2027 y 2031"; El Ojo del Arte and La Nación 2021 "2031"; El Cronista "203" (a truncation); the older La Nación piece "2009". OpenStreetMap's node "Casa de los Lirios" carries **2031**. The 2009 reading appears only in the older article, which reaches it by transposing the two buildings. **[A for the readings; R for the transposition diagnosis]**
- Facade in **"piedra París"**. **Not imported limestone.** La Nación: "una copia de la piedra sólida que se usaba en Europa"; El Ojo del Arte the same, a render worked to simulate cut stone. **[A]** *Corrected: an earlier round called it imported limestone.*
- Ornament: organic Art Nouveau relief, **iris/lily stems and leaves**, hyacinth bunches beneath the capital, curved balconies, decorative iron door. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, El Ojo del Arte, La Nación]** No sculptor, smith, workshop or contract is named anywhere. **[A: gap]**
- A large **plaster head** of a bearded old man crowns the cornice, hair and beard becoming the rooftop railing; Spanish Wikipedia specifies *yeso*, moulded and not carved. **[A]** *Corrected: an earlier round called it sculpted stone.* **Contradiction, not resolved:** El Ojo del Arte reads him as Eolo or Poseidón, Spanish Wikipedia and La Nación as Eolo or Neptuno.
- Plan: two ground-floor commercial spaces flanking a central residential entrance, three upper storeys of two apartments each. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia]** A further conflict, flagged in the existing atlas file: La Nación reports the third floor held back for the owning family, which the Wikipedia count does not accommodate.
- **Ramón Gómez de la Serna**, in Buenos Aires from 1936, called the building "el lugar del pelo de cemento". **[A: La Nación]** *His engagement with the object, not a journey of his own: carried as a quote and source on the cornice-head stop.*

## 4. The corner at Rivadavia and Ayacucho

- **Contradiction, not resolved, the largest in the dossier: the construction date.** Four incompatible families across eleven years. **1903-1907**, run together with the Lirios as one campaign (La Nación nid922238); **1907** (Huellas, Noticias Abasto, La Divina, Spanish Wikipedia "Balvanera" and "Avenida Rivadavia"); **begun 1912, finished 1914** (Síntesis Comuna 3); **1914** (La Nación 2021, on the grandson). No permit or expediente exists in public. **This must not carry an [A] tag on any single date.** The journey file orders by 1907, the most widely printed, and says so.
- Built as a **renta** property, **five storeys**. **[A: Huellas, Noticias Abasto, La Divina, Spanish Wikipedia; the storey count also in La Nación nid922238, both "cinco pisos"]** The finer subdivision (ground floor plus entresuelo commercial, four residential floors, terrace) is single-sourced and is softened in the journey file. **[R]**
- **Ferrocemento**, iron in cement, a precursor of reinforced concrete, an unusual technique for Buenos Aires at the time. **[A: La Nación, Revista Para Ti]** **Contradiction, not resolved: the superlative.** La Nación 2021: "la primera edificación de Buenos Aires hecha con hormigón armado". Síntesis Comuna 3 enlarges this to the first in Latin America and is alone in it. Barriada.com.ar makes a competing "first" for the *Lirios*. No engineering history was located that mentions either building. **[R for all three firsts]**
- Ornamental capitals carved with **women's faces** rather than acanthus; faceted glass streetlamps on the facade. **[A: Noticias Abasto, single-sourced]** A **three-level dome** crowned by a small cupola with an onion-shaped construction and an iron weathervane. **[A: Huellas, La Nación nid922238]**
- Dome interior: lower polygonal reception room, middle bedroom lit by eight oval windows, upper observatory reached by retractable stairs, with a telescope. **[A: Revista Para Ti, La Divina, Noticias Abasto]** **Caveat, in the journey file too:** every one of these descriptions post-dates the 1999-2001 restoration, and how much is original fabric is documented nowhere. **[R]**
- Ornament unmistakably Catalan, read universally as homage to Gaudí **[A: Spanish Wikipedia and every press account]**, but **contradiction, not resolved: which Gaudí.** The Finca Güell dragon gate at Pedralbes, 1884-87 (Noticias Abasto, La Divina, Huellas); the Parc Güell dragon (Spanish Wikipedia); the Casa Batlló chimneys with warrior silhouettes (Huellas). **The task brief's own framing, that the ornament replicates the Casa Batlló *dragon door*, is corroborated by no source reached and is a fourth, wrong reading.** And see §0.2: the replicas may themselves be 1999 work.

## 5. The empty decades, and the death

## 6. The afterlife of the two buildings

*Everything here is somebody else's act on his object, carried in the journey file as sources and `suggested_refs` on his own stops and as stops about the fabric, never as scenes staging another person's travel.*

- By the 1990s the corner building, dome and terrace had deteriorated badly. **[A: Noticias Abasto, La Divina, Revista Para Ti]**
- **1999**: a company bought the fourth floor with terrace and dome and commissioned **Fernando Lorenzi** to restore it; La Nación dates the work late 1999 through **2001**. **[A: Noticias Abasto, La Divina, Revista Para Ti, La Nación]**
- Lorenzi faced the dome with **952 pieces of mirrored glass** and added an artistic wrought-iron lift. **[A: Huellas, repeated by Noticias Abasto and El Cronista]** *Corrected: an earlier round attached the Casa Batlló to the refacing; per Huellas the Batlló borrowings are the chimney forms with warrior silhouettes. Noticias Abasto (2023) reports the onion finial clad in trencadís said to echo Kremlin domes, a journalist's comparison.* **[R for the Kremlin reading]**
- The terrace, **300 m² (La Nación) or ~350 m² (Noticias Abasto)**, disputed, carries the Dragon Gate replicas, a cascade crossed by a wooden footbridge, cement pergolas and a large red umbrella, all from the single La Nación article. **[A/R]** *The "219 m² apartment" figure from an earlier round is dropped as uncorroborated.*
- **The Catalan inscription "No hi ha somnis impossibles" was added by Lorenzi during or after the 1999-2001 restoration**, as homage to Gaudí, and gives the building its popular name. **[A: Noticias Abasto, La Nación, La Divina]** **The most consequential correction in this pass:** the existing atlas file dates it to 1907 and gives it a stop as Rodríguez Ortega's own act.
- The legend attached to it retells a Gaudí anecdote of 1883: told the Sagrada Família would take many years, he answered that his client had all the time in the world. **[R: Vedek / Huellas, the building's own mythology]** Two corrections: 1883 is the year Gaudí *took over* the Sagrada Família from Francisco de Paula del Villar on a work already begun; and the "client in no hurry" line is apocryphal.
- **2006**: Senate expediente **PL 2993/06** (Sen. Luis A. Falcó) proposed declaring the Lirios a Monumento Histórico Nacional. **[A: archived Senate listing via Spanish Wikipedia]** Its fate is recorded nowhere reached **[gap]**, and La Nación adds that both buildings have at various points been nominated, with no dates or outcomes. **[R]**
- The Casa de los Lirios is catalogued **"Representativo"** by the city government with an **"integral" (comprehensive) level of protection**. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, La Nación 2021, El Ojo del Arte]** *Corrected: an earlier round wrote "cautelar integral". Cautelar / Estructural / Integral are three mutually exclusive tiers; every source read says only "nivel integral". The primary GCBA record could not be retrieved and no source gives the year of listing.* The corner building carries no protection tier in any article that mentions it. **[A]**
