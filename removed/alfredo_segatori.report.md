# Alfredo Segatori, "Pelado" (b. 1970): research report

*Research pass closed 2026-08-13. Subject living; no death record found. Argentine muralist, aerosol and recycled-metal artist, Buenos Aires.*

**Method.** Spanish Wikipedia's "Alfredo Segatori" and Wikidata Q70874003 supply the spine; the press citations behind Wikipedia (Perfil 2014 and 2019, Clarin 2017, Infobae 2018) were chased to the primary where the outlet allowed it. Trade press (buenosairesstreetart.com, streetartlatam.com, arteforadomuseu.com.br, muralesbuenosaires.com.ar) supplies the datable mural log. Diario Contexto's profile is the only source found for the childhood material. Coordinates were re-derived from Nominatim wherever a street or barrio is named; several carried in the first gathering pass were wrong by 0.5 to 25 km (see **Coordinate corrections**).

**Legend: [A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source assertion not corroborated. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

---

## 1. Floresta: birth and formation (1970 to c. 1993)

- Born 1970 in **Buenos Aires**. **[A]** es.wikipedia infobox; Wikidata Q70874003. No source found names a birth hospital, barrio of birth, or a month and day. A 2015 interview (ojosalcuadro.tumblr.com) quotes him as forty-four, which implies 1970 or 1971. *Minor discrepancy, unresolved.*
- Known publicly as **"Pelado"** (Baldy). **[A]** Wikidata "also known as", citing arteforadomuseu.com.br; Street Art Latam profile.
- Grows up in **Floresta**, a working-class barrio in the city's west, which he names as his identity before his trade. **[A]** Diario Contexto. **Floresta is not attested as his birthplace**: every birth source says only "Buenos Aires". Recorded as two separate facts.
- Expelled from three schools; describes himself as a *vivo* who could not sit through classroom discipline. Studies **Physical Education**, not art; works as a gym teacher and a medical sales representative before painting becomes his living. **[A]** Diario Contexto.
- Family detail from the first gathering pass (mother a painter and sculptor, father a psychiatrist of Italian descent, a sister named Carla) **is not in the retrieved Diario Contexto text**. Unverified, kept out of the journey file. **[R]**

## 2. Sao Paulo and the first can (1994 or 1996)

- Travels to the **Bienal de Sao Paulo** (Ibirapuera Park), meets Brazilian graffiti writers, and picks up an aerosol can for the first time; the spray does not feel like his medium at first. **[A]** Diario Contexto.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** Diario Contexto dates the trip to **1994**; ojosalcuadro.tumblr.com (2015) dates his entry into street art to a **1996** Biennial. No third source adjudicates, and both are Biennial years, which removes the easy tiebreak.
- **Correction to an earlier framing:** "freehand aerosol street interventions from 1989" conflates two things. Street Art Latam and arteforadomuseu say only *muralista urbano desde finales de los 80*: wall work from the late 1980s, with aerosol dating from the Biennial trip. That start is an **era**, not a year, and the journey file treats it as such.

## 3. The 1990s: first murals, the method, the classroom

- First Buenos Aires mural, **"El Vomito Publicitario"**, painted on his return from Brazil, self-funded and unpaid. **[R]** Single source: ojosalcuadro.tumblr.com (2015). No date beyond 1996, no address, no photograph located.
- Wider press diffusion begins **mid-1990s**. **[A]** es.wikipedia, citing Perfil (Calvo, 13 September 2019). No exact year anywhere; "mediados de la decada de 1990" is the whole of it.
- Fixed working method, in his own words: *"ver una pared, pedir permiso, rasquetearla y trabajarla"*, plus a stated preference for rough walls over smooth. **[A]** Clarin, Hernan Firpo, 23 December 2017. *Access note: the Clarin page was blocked in this pass; the wording is Wikipedia's verbatim reproduction of it.*
- Links his practice to **action painting** and cites **David Alfaro Siqueiros** and **Jackson Pollock** as influences. **[A]** es.wikipedia, citing Perfil 2019.
- Teaches **"Aerosol Urbano"** at the UBA's **Centro Cultural Ricardo Rojas**, Avenida Corrientes. **[R]** Single source: ojosalcuadro.tumblr.com; undated, and no confirmation the course still runs.
- Paints three twenty-five-metre building facades in **Villa Soldati** in a single month. **[R]** Single source: La Nacion (nid2208090), **404 in this pass**, known only through the earlier gathering record. All La Nacion items below share that defect.

## 4. Barracas: "El regreso de Quinquela" (2013 to 2019)

The largest knot of contradiction in the file, and largely resolvable.

- **October 2013**: inaugurates **"El regreso de Quinquela"** on a party wall in **Barracas**, on the camino de sirga of the Matanza-Riachuelo, fusing four works by **Benito Quinquela Martin** and setting in more than seventy portraits of neighbourhood residents, dockworkers among them. **[A]** Turismo Buenos Aires; es.wikipedia citing Perfil, Claudio Corsalini, 28 December 2014.
- **Two apparent contradictions, reconciled here, the reconciliation itself flagged as reconstruction:**
  1. *Location.* es.wikipedia says **calle Lavadero**; Turismo Buenos Aires says **Pedro de Mendoza y San Antonio**; arteforadomuseu lists **"Lavadero & San Antonio"**. Same corner: the two streets both run down to the Riachuelo and meet near Pedro de Mendoza. **[R]**, though arteforadomuseu's listing effectively states it.
  2. *Size.* es.wikipedia gives **100 m x 13 m** (1,300 m2) and "largest in Argentina at the time"; Turismo Buenos Aires gives **2,000 m2** and "largest in the world by a single artist in freehand aerosol"; Perfil's 28 December 2014 headline says "el mural mas grande del mundo". The wall was worked in stages, per buenosairesstreetart.com posts of 24 August 2014 and 17 March 2015: 1,300 m2 is the October 2013 state, 2,000 m2 the finished work. **[R]**, reconstructed from post dates. Earlier framing of this as an unresolved contradiction is withdrawn.
- **The Guinness question, open.** Street Art Latam states he *"llego a ostentar un record Guinness"* for one of the largest murals painted by a single artist. **No Guinness entry for Segatori or any of his murals was located**; es.wikipedia has no Guinness mention, and the city page uses only descriptive superlative language. One secondary source asserts a record; no certification, date or record ID exists anywhere reachable. **[R]**
- **October 2019**: the city legislature declares the mural of **Cultural Interest**. **[A]** Turismo Buenos Aires. The work belongs to the **camino de sirga** recovery under the Matanza-Riachuelo plan. **[A]** es.wikipedia citing Perfil 2014.
- His own son is said to be among the faces painted into the wall. **[R]** Turismo Buenos Aires, via the gathering record; see the name discrepancy in section 9.

## 5. Scrap metal: the desarmadero and the recyclers (c. 2015 to 2018)

- Works from an atelier at **Anden 2222**, tied to the **Cooperativa de Recicladores Urbanos El Ceibo**, whose salvage feeds the metal works. **[R]** La Nacion (404).
- **"El viejo del desarmadero"**, Gorriti and Lavalleja, **Palermo**: a figure assembled from car bumpers, exhaust pipes and scrapyard metal, counted by 2017 among the most-visited street artworks in Buenos Aires. **[A]** es.wikipedia; Clarin, 23 December 2017. **No source gives a year of making**: Wikipedia says only that it was most-visited *in* 2017, placing the making at or before that. An earlier "installed 2017" was inference presented as fact, withdrawn. **Gap.**
- On the public's readings of the figure, the comment he liked best was that it looked like *"un dios desorientado"*. **[A]** Clarin, 23 December 2017, via es.wikipedia's verbatim quotation.
- **December 2018**: faces the **La Covacha** bar, Humboldt and Cabrera, Palermo, entirely in recycled material. **[A]** buenosairesstreetart.com, 6 December 2018.
- **2018**: said to organise **"Lunatica Arte & Turf"** at the **Hipodromo Argentino de Palermo**, curating over 450 artists. **[R] and weak:** absent from the retrieved Diario Contexto text, surviving only in the gathering record. Kept with the hedge in `date_confidence`, and flagged here as the weakest item retained.

## 6. The port: "Fauna Portuaria" and "Exabrupto de Color" (2018 to 2020)

- **21 December 2018**: inaugurates **"Fauna Portuaria"**, 1,200 m2, at **Avenida Rafael Obligado y calle 1, Darsena F, Puerto Nuevo**, depicting native Argentine species. **[A]** es.wikipedia (date, size, address), citing Infobae, 23 December 2018.
  - *Unverified within that:* **the cited Infobae URL returns 404**, so the Administracion General de Puertos role, the species list, and above all the claim that **four sand-transport companies chose the species** are uncorroborated. That last detail is dropped from the journey file.
  - *Correction:* Darsena F is the **Puerto Arenero**, the sand basin, not a grain terminal. "Grain silos" in an earlier pass was wrong.
- **13 September 2019**: third edition of **"Exabrupto de Color"** at the port, on the **Agrecom silos** in the sand-extraction sector. **[A]** Perfil, Calvo, 13 September 2019, **fetched directly**, which also records a technique shift: exterior latex applied with paint machines and compressors rather than aerosol.
- The same piece carries his line on ephemerality: *"esto es arte perecedero y lo que queda basicamente son los registros fotograficos y de video"*. **[A], primary verified.** **Attribution correction:** previously credited to Clarin/Firpo 2017; it is Perfil/Calvo 2019, and es.wikipedia footnotes the sentence to that reference. Wikipedia's rendering drops the opening *"esto"*; the journey file carries the primary wording.
- **January 2020**: the series travels to **Punta del Este, Uruguay**, where he intervenes several city spaces including **Mario Irarrazabal's** "La Mano" on Playa Brava; the initiative is credited to producer Carlos Mazalan and businessman Hector Liberman. **[R]** Bacanal, 2 January 2020, cited by es.wikipedia; **bacanal.com.ar failed DNS resolution in this pass**, so the content is known only secondhand. (The sculptor is Irarrazabal; "Irrazabal" in the gathering record is a misspelling.)

## 7. The barrios, the pandemic, and the country beyond (2019 to 2023)

- **28 February 2019**: **"Mirando Miranda"**, a giraffe the full height of an eleven-storey building at **Miranda 4778, Monte Castro**. **[A]** Diario Contexto; buenosairesstreetart.com post of that date; arteforadomuseu for the address.
- **2021**: **"San Diego de la Boca"**, tribute to **Diego Maradona** (died 25 November 2020), at **Aristobulo del Valle 50, La Boca**. **[A]** es.wikipedia, citing Perfil.
- **2022**: **"La vaca que se fue volando"** on grain silos at **San Francisco, Cordoba**. **[A]** es.wikipedia; Infonegocios San Francisco. Same year, **"Panda Pandemial"** on a ten-storey party wall in **Villa del Parque**, documented complete on **17 June 2022**. **[A]** muralesbuenosaires.com.ar; es.wikipedia.
- **c. 2022**: a staircase mural nicknamed **"La Scaloneta"** after the World Cup-winning squad. **[R]** conexionshow.com.ar; **no street named**, so the journey file uses the canonical city pin. Gap.
- **20 April 2023**: two dinosaur murals in **Parque Chacabuco**, among them **"Dino Rex"**, a Tyrannosaurus on a skateboard in a backwards cap numbered 10, a Maradona reference. **[A]** buenosairesstreetart.com; streetartlatam.com.
- **Rio Gallegos** (Santa Cruz) and **Puerto Esperanza** (Misiones): named as painting stops with no wall, subject or date. **[R]** La Nacion (404). Bare place-names, all the sourcing supports.
- More than **one hundred works** across Argentina. **[A]** es.wikipedia ("mas de un centenar las obras que ha hecho hasta el presente"); Street Art Latam.

## 8. Abroad

- **August 2016, Baltimore:** paints over three days on a shuttered tire factory beside the Jones Falls in **Woodberry**, with Uruguayan-born Baltimore artist **Pablo Machioli**, under the **Roots/Raices** exchange organised by Matt Fox-Tucker and Richard Best with Gallery 788. **[A]** Baltimore Sun, 17 August 2016; buenosairesstreetart.com post of 30 August 2016. **Correction:** the figure is **Homo naledi**, per the Sun. "Neanderthal" in an earlier pass was wrong.
- **New York**, **Ciudad de Mexico**, **Playa del Carmen**, **Cancun**: named as international stops by Street Art Latam, La Nacion (404) and conexionshow.com.ar. **[R] for all four. No wall, district, commissioner, or year anywhere.** Recorded as named cities only.
- **Miami, 2023:** a party built on the "exabrupto de color" recycling aesthetic around a Martin Fierro awards event, and a collaboration referencing **Inter Miami CF**. **[A]** for Inter Miami (Diario Contexto). **[R] and contested for the award:** the Martin Fierro **de Oro** is APTRA's top prize, given in Buenos Aires; the Miami ceremony is the separate **Martin Fierro Latino**. tiempoar.com.ar, which would settle it, was not retrieved, so the journey file names no award.
- **December 2023:** two Palermo murals honouring **Carlos Gardel** and **Charly Garcia**. **[A]** buenosairesstreetart.com, post of 14 December 2023. No street address in the source.

## 9. Institutions, family, self-description

- Founds and chairs the **Fundacion Cultural Anden 2222**, oriented to art, culture, education, solidarity and science. **[A]** arteforadomuseu.com.br. **Gap: no founding year in any source.**
- Takes part in **BADA** (Buenos Aires Directo de Artistas), a direct-from-artists fair. **[A]** Diario Contexto names the participation, undated. **Correction:** an earlier pass glossed BADA as "the Buenos Aires Design Biennial" at the Buenos Aires Design centre in Recoleta. BADA is no design biennial, has no fixed venue, and that centre closed at the end of 2018. Venue, gloss and coordinate were fabricated connective tissue, and are cut.
- Develops **"La Boca 5.0"** with Visit Caminito, and takes part in reopening the **Galeria Social Rimini** and **Il Piccolo Vapore** in La Boca. **[A]** Diario Contexto. Undated; the Rimini reopening is given only as "opening 23 August", year unspecified.
- Married to the sculptor **Paula Pons**; they have a son with an early hand for drawing. **[R]** ojosalcuadro.tumblr.com (2015) names him **Mateo**; the city page on the Quinquela mural names him **Theo**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.**
- Instagram bio, verified account **@alfredosegatori**: *"Del aerosol a mano alzada al Exabrupto de Color Street. Murales trash: dandole una segunda vida a la chatarra"*. **[A]**
- **alfredosegatori.com.ar returns an account-suspension notice** as of August 2026, so his self-published archive is offline. **[A]** direct observation; cause and duration unknown, a **gap**.

1. **Birth year 1970 vs 1971** (infobox vs a 2015 interview's "forty-four").
2. **Sao Paulo Biennial 1994 vs 1996** (Diario Contexto vs ojosalcuadro).
3. **Guinness record**: asserted by Street Art Latam, absent from Wikipedia, Wikidata and the city page, unlocatable in the Guinness database.
4. **The son's name, Mateo vs Theo.**

## Corpus interlocks

Segatori has **no attested crossing with any traveller already in this corpus**: a grep of every existing journey file returns zero occurrences of "Segatori". Two links are real and one-directional, and both are made of his own work:

- **Diego Maradona**, painted twice: "San Diego de la Boca" (La Boca, 2021) as an explicit tribute after Maradona's death, and "Dino Rex" (Parque Chacabuco, 2023), a dinosaur in a backwards cap numbered 10.
- **Charly Garcia**, portrait mural in Palermo, December 2023.

Other Buenos Aires figures already researched (Franco Fasoli, Milu Correch, Matias Carbone) share the city, the barrios and the same trade press, but no source records a meeting, a collaboration or a mention in either direction. Shared scene is not a crossing, and they are deliberately unnamed in the campas. **Benito Quinquela Martin**, **Siqueiros** and **Pollock** are genuine one-directional links with no journey file.
