# Franco Fasoli, called Jaz: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14 for `franco_fasoli_jaz.journey.json`. Subject: Argentine muralist and scenographer, born 1981, alive and working. There is no burial or posthumous-cult section, because there is no death; the afterlife phase is here a section on documentation and reputation.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in place. **[R]** reconstruction, inference, or a claim carried by a single weak or aggregating source. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**A caution that colours everything below.** Four bodies of material carry the whole record: StreetArtNews post archives, graffitimundo.com, buenosairesstreetart.com and Brooklyn Street Art's tag page, plus one MutualArt index line. They are contemporaneous and specific, which is their strength; they are promotional, unedited, and usually dated to the post rather than the event, which is their weakness. Nothing scholarly sits above them to reconcile what they disagree about.

---

## 1. Origin and formation (1981 to c. 2004)

**Birth.** Born 1981, Argentine. **[A, MutualArt artist index: "Franco Fasoli, Argentinian, born 1981"]**. Exact birth date and birthplace within Argentina are **not documented anywhere reachable**, and are recorded as a gap.

> **Contradiction inside the research pool itself.** Its chronology lens says no source gives a birth year; its afterlife lens carries the MutualArt line giving 1981. Resolved in favour of MutualArt as to the year, with the rest of the gap preserved in `date_confidence`.

**Family.** Born into a family of artists. **[A, streetartnews.net, "Artist Retrospective: Franco Fasoli aka JAZ", 16 February 2022]**. No address, no parents named, no description of the workshop. The "family workshop" framing of the journey's first stop is **[R]** built on an attested fact, pinned to the canonical Buenos Aires coordinate.

**When he began painting in the street: contradiction, unresolved.** graffitimundo: "recognized as one of the first major graffiti writers to begin painting in the streets of Buenos Aires in the mid 1990's" **[A, verified verbatim]**. Brooklyn Street Art independently: "since the mid-nineties and his mid-teens" **[A]**, consistent with a 1981 birth. But Fasoli himself, in the Buenos Aires Street Art interview of 1 June 2011, says he began in **1999**, alongside Poeta, Caru, Nerf, Roma and Pastel **[A]**. Three sources, two answers, and the later date is the one the subject gives in his own words. Both are preserved: the journey dates the stop 1995-01-01 with a "contested" confidence string naming both figures. **No source names 1995**; "mid 1990s" is the strongest phrasing available, and the pin is a placement convenience, not a claim.

**Training.** Scenography at the Instituto del Teatro Colón **[A, graffitimundo profile, corroborated by the 2011 interview]**, with **no year given by any source**; the journey's 1998 is inferred and flagged. Painting with José Marchi, Nahuel Vecino and Diana Aisemberg **[A, graffitimundo names all three]**. **IUNA (painting, drawing, ceramics) is [R], and weakly:** the pool attributes it to both the 2011 interview and graffitimundo, but the graffitimundo profile names no institution except the Teatro Colón and the interview page is unreachable. **Not carried into the journey file at all**, its only surviving support being a secondhand summary of an inaccessible page.

**Fileteado and graffiti.** He and other writers, Poeta and Caru among them, discussed "the idea of mixing the theme of fileteado with graffitis." **[A, Fasoli's own words, 1 June 2011]**. Quoted verbatim in the journey.

**Barcelona, the first crossing.** graffitimundo attributes his turn from hip-hop lettering toward public art to an encounter with public art on a visit to Barcelona as a young adult. **[A as to fact and causal claim, graffitimundo, "Jaz: new residence and global tour"]**. **No year is given**; the pool's 2003-2004 window is **[R]**, and the journey's stop carries an "approximate" string saying so. graffitimundo also states he later relocated to Barcelona; **no date for that relocation exists [R as to when]**.

## 2. The Buenos Aires decade (c. 2005 to 2012)

**c. 2005, letters to figures.** The street work shifts to figurative subjects, chiefly musicians, mainly around Palermo Hollywood. He calls the graffiti of this period "still very naïve." **[A, his own words, 1 June 2011]**; the date is his own and approximate.

**January 2008, Valparaíso.** Collaborative mural of musicians with Fisek, Inti and Charquipunk; reported still intact when photographed October 2011. **[A, Buenos Aires Street Art, 22 October 2011]**. The earliest attested work outside Argentina in the record.

**11 August 2010, "Aguante", Hollywood in Cambodia, Buenos Aires.** Solo show on football hooliganism: abstracted barra brava violence, portraits of notorious hooligans, and a room where visitors added tattoo designs to painted figures. **[A, graffitimundo, "Aguante by Jaz @ HIC"]**. No street address is reachable, so the stop takes the canonical Buenos Aires coordinate.

**2009-2011, the iconography settles.** Donkeys, then fighting scenes of human bodies with animal heads, drawing on lucha libre, sumo and football; he says he is driven by "the act of painting rather than an idea or a concept," and treats animals as subjects free of inherent social attitude. In June 2011 he is working as a set designer, sharing a workshop with Ever (Nicolás Romero), and collaborating regularly with Ever and Poeta. **[A as quotation and as fact, 1 June 2011 interview; R as to dating the stylistic shift, which the interview describes as a period, not an event]**.

**Dated Buenos Aires walls and shows**, all **[A]** from the Buenos Aires Street Art and graffitimundo posts of those dates: 6 Oct 2011, Villa Crespo, two torsos in a wrestling hold (a *documentation* date, the painting date being unrecorded); 14 Jan 2012, Villa Crespo bus depot, two bulls in mud, brick and coal sourced on site, with a companion wall on Thames and Camargo a week earlier in the same materials; 20 Mar 2012, "Jaz + Ever" at The Oasis Clubhouse; 22 Aug 2012, Villa Martelli, two smilodons with the painted text "Jaz was here". A solo show at Ras Gallery, Barcelona, 17 May 2012, is **[R]**, carried only by Brooklyn Street Art; not carried as a stop.

**27 August and 3 September 2012, the depot at Castillo and Fitz Roy: barrio contradiction, unresolved.** Buenos Aires Street Art, 27 August 2012, reports a bus depot at Castillo and Fitz Roy with four wildcats and a horseback rider, filed under **Chacarita**, plus a mural at Tecnópolis the same week. streetartnews.net, 3 September 2012, reports "Los Guardianes" with Dötz, "six of Jaz's lions trying to fight off a gigantic insect painted by Dötz," located "between Calle Fitz Roy and Calle Castille in the **Palermo** District." Both **[A]**. Same corner, two barrio names, a week apart; the boundary runs near there and either label may be locally defensible, and **no adjudication is attempted**. Both stops take the corner coordinate (-34.5885, -58.4405) from the streetartnews description, and the campa states the disagreement. The pool had pinned "Los Guardianes" to the city centre, about 5.5 km off, and called Dötz "the Brazilian painter"; **no source gives Dötz a nationality**, so it is dropped rather than downgraded.

**29 November 2012, Saavedra.** Photographed painting a new mural. **[A, Buenos Aires Street Art, 30 November 2012]**. No subject described, no work named; not carried as a stop.

## 3. The bitumen years (October 2012 to 2014)

The technical signature is bitumen: road tar cut with gasoline, applied with rags and brushes, giving a watercolour-like stain rather than an aerosol surface. Nineteen walls of this phase are carried as journey stops, each **[A]** from the StreetArtNews or Buenos Aires Street Art post of its date, with the citation attached to the stop.

**Attested in the same run but not carried as stops**, listed so the itinerary is complete: 14 Jan 2013, two indoor pieces at the Woodstock Exchange, 66-68 Albert Rd, Cape Town. Feb 2013, Tigre (Tigre 100x100). 20 Apr 2013, Shoreditch, London, a large bear. 28 Apr 2013, Berlin, with Moneyless. 10 May 2013, Vitry-sur-Seine, headless beasts. 1 Aug 2013, Villa Crespo and Chacarita, two wildcats and the Espacio Forest facade. 29 Oct 2013, Villa Soriano, Uruguay. 26 Apr 2014, Melbourne laneways. 19 May 2014, Punta del Este, "Mito Rioplatense". 1 Jun 2014, Rome, two wrestlers (Avanguardie Urbane). 4 Sep 2014, Aalborg, "Something Remind" (first We Aart Festival). All **[A]**, same source basis.

**Two contradictions in this phase.**

1. **"First international festival."** In the 2015 StreetArtNews interview he calls **Atlanta, August 2013**, his first international festival invitation. Johannesburg (Dec 2012), Cape Town (Jan 2013) and San Juan (Oct 2012) precede it, and Valparaíso precedes them by four years. It may turn on what he counts as a festival, or be compression in a retrospective interview. **Preserved, not resolved**, and stated inside the Atlanta campa.
2. **The South Africa trip's year.** Brooklyn Street Art files "Acrylic Walls, Johannesburg and Cape Town" under **2013**; the posts date Johannesburg to **31 December 2012** and Cape Town to **11, 14 and 18 January 2013**. The trip straddles the boundary and cannot be filed wholly under either. **The name "Acrylic Walls" is also not visible in the readable post titles**, only in BSA and the pool: a soft attribution.

**Two wording corrections.** Djerba: the post reads "four of the civilisations that conquered **Tunisia**, the Greeks, the Romans, the Vandals and the Arabs", where the pool had **Djerba**; the source's wording is used, and **the post never names Erriadh**, which is correct as the Djerbahood village but comes from general knowledge **[A for the wall and the civilisations; R for the village]**. Villa Soriano: the pool's coordinates sat about 3.7 km south of the town and it asserted a population "in the hundreds", against roughly 1,150 in the 2011 Uruguayan census; the figure and the sentence built on it are dropped, and the stop is not carried.

**Four pin corrections carried into the journey.** Vienna (pool gave Stephansplatz, about 1.9 km from Inoperable Gallery; journey uses 48.203 / 16.349 and names the stop simply "Vienna"); Kyiv (pool gave Maidan Nezalezhnosti, about 11 km from Gymnasium 267; journey uses 50.41 / 30.64); Río San Juan (pool about 9 km east; journey uses 19.63 / -70.06); Erriadh (pool about 1.2 km west; journey uses 33.808 / 10.858). The Puente Bosch coordinate is **approximate**: no source gives a precise position under the bridge.

**Barracas, 1 November 2014.** Tattooed half-man half-beast figures under the Puente Bosch bridge, plant elements by Pastel, palette from the strips of Deportivo Barracas and Barracas Central; their first joint mural since Saavedra in 2011. **[A, Buenos Aires Street Art; graffitimundo, "Interview: Jaz & Pastel"]**.

**Río San Juan and the collage question. [R]** The pool attributed paper collage to this mural. **The ArteSano post does not describe the technique**, and the 2015 interview places the collage development at a Miami residency he was still working through on 17 December 2014, four days earlier. The campa gives the residency as context and says outright that whether this wall used it is unrecorded.

## 4. Paper, festivals, and the museum door (2015 to 2017)

**2015 was the year of maximum motion.** By then he was spending more than six months a year travelling to festivals, residencies and exhibitions. **[A, graffitimundo, "Jaz: new residence and global tour"]**, which also supplies the year's itinerary: "SUBEN" in London (June); PangeaSeed, Cozumel (July); the Diego Rivera Project of MAF, Antwerp (August); Cityleaks, Cologne, and a group show at Athen B. Gallery, Oakland (September); a solo show at Elsi del Río, Buenos Aires (November); and the Mural Festival, Montreal.

Four walls of that year are carried as stops, each **[A]** from the post named: **May, Rabat**, "The Shoe Thief" at the Jidar Festival, in layered coloured-paper collage, exploring "tension, violence and tribal identity" (graffitimundo 20 May; streetartnews 21 May); **July, Cozumel**, "Eat This" for SeaWalls (21 and 27 July); **October, São Paulo**, Largo do Arouche, ten days beside Conor Harrington (30 October); **December, Madrid**, "The Rapture," a diptych of half-human half-animal figures in combat (30 December). *Cozumel correction:* the pool described "a dragon locked in combat with a shark." **The source describes a shark and says the piece "warns us against harvesting sharks fins for soup." There is no dragon**, and the flourish depending on it is cut. The Elsi del Río show is not carried as a stop, no venue coordinate being reachable, and **"Mito Fundacional" (Founding Myth), 2015, [R]** is asserted by Brooklyn Street Art with **no venue and no city**; both are unresolved.

**The 2015 studio interview.** In graffitimundo's "Interview: Jaz & Pastel" he describes sharing a Villa Crespo studio with Pastel, self-identifies as middle-class and university-educated, says he is about to leave Buenos Aires again, and describes moving from gasoline and tar toward latex for commissioned work and paper collage for gallery pieces. **[A]**. The clearest single statement of the arc from tar to paper.

**12 April 2016, Ostend.** The Crystal Ship; the work draws explicitly on James Ensor, the town's own painter of masked crowds. **[A, streetartnews.net]**.

**19 May to 28 July 2016, Córdoba.** "Pioneers of Trip to Nowhere," curated by Kosovo Gallery at the Museo Provincial de Bellas Artes Emilio Caraffa, including his "First free territory of America," on the identity and violence of Córdoba's provincial history. He worked first with residents of the **Alberdi** neighbourhood on the piece's development, then completed it independently; **after the close it was reinstalled in its original neighbourhood location in Alberdi**. **[A, graffitimundo, "Exhibition Review: Pioneers of Trip to Nowhere"]**. The reinstallation is dated only as after the close; the journey uses the closing date, with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

**22 July 2017, Kyiv.** Seven days at Gymnasium No. 267, Verbytskoho Street, for Mural Social Club's "Back to School! 2017." Subject: "two symmetric felines in a forest environment, communicating through patterns on their skin." **[A, streetartnews.net]**. The pool had rendered this as generic "animals in their natural environments"; the source's wording is used. Also in 2017, **Mexico City with Elian and Ever, and the Up North Festival at Røst, Norway [R]**: both carried only by Brooklyn Street Art, undated beyond the year, uncorroborated, and neither carried as a stop.

## 5. Commissions, a book, and the present (2020 to 2022)

**"Público Privado", 2020: category correction.** The pool recorded this as an exhibition with an unfindable venue. It is **not an exhibition**. Brooklyn Street Art lists it as a **monograph** documenting his 2009-2019 work, published 2020; BSA's June 2020 article is titled "Artist Franco JAZ Fasoli Goes Publico Privado." **[A as a book, Brooklyn Street Art]**. No venue could be found because there was no show. Not carried as a stop, a book having no coordinate.

**8 June 2021, Buenos Aires.** "TODO 2021," ten metres by three, with Jorge Pomar, in vivid colour. **[A, streetartnews.net]**. **31 August 2021, Basildon, Essex:** a large mural for the Our Towns initiative, BasildON Creative People and Places **[A, streetartnews.net]**.

**6 July 2021, Trinitat Nova, Barcelona: date correction.** Mural for the TIME project, a joint initiative of Pla de Barris with Difusor and B-Murals, called by the organisers "a tool for social transformation through art"; made with local residents and students of the Escuela Superior de Restauración y Conservación de Bienes Culturales de Cataluña; imagery includes "bulls that recall the past of the area tied to agriculture." **[A, streetartnews.net, 5 August 2021]**. The pool dated this 2021-08-05, which is the **post** date; **the source says the mural was inaugurated 6 July 2021**.

**2022, latest activity: correction.** The pool states that 2021 is the latest dated item. It is not. StreetArtNews published "Artist Retrospective: Franco Fasoli aka JAZ" on **16 February 2022**, and Brooklyn Street Art lists **Crystal Ship murals at Ostend in 2022**, a second appearance six years after 2016. **[A]**. The final stop uses 16 February 2022 and says in `date_confidence` that the 2022 Ostend murals carry no day or month.

**Reputation and documentation.**

- Before the Jaz name, "the most prolific graffiti writer in Argentina"; the alias evoking "jazz," for the improvisational character of the practice; recurring iconography of "folcloric fighters and cats," criollo and gaucho figures against a self-described "Battlefield." All three **[R, Brooklyn Street Art, aggregated, no primary attribution, and he is nowhere quoted directly on them]**.
- graffitimundo's own summary: "large-scale figurative muralism" using techniques from his scenography training; latex, tar, gasoline, acrylic, aerosol and paper collage; hybrid part-man part-beast figures exploring identity, conflict and duality against popular culture, local tradition and Latin American themes. **[A, graffitimundo profile]**.
- **No Wikipedia article exists in English or Spanish**, both URLs returning HTTP 404 **[A as a negative, direct check]**, which explains the shape of the record: trade press and gallery self-description, nothing above them. And **he is alive and working**: no death notice, obituary, grave, memorial or posthumous cult **[A as a negative, absence across every source checked]**, so the brief's ordinary afterlife frame does not apply and is not forced.

## 6. Interlocks

The interlock survey returned fourteen candidate links. **Thirteen are shared geography, shared decade, or a queue grouping, and nothing more**: the Buenos Aires pin (Puzzovio, Ocampo, Borges, Spinetta, Charly García, Chiachio, Giannone), the Paris pin (Chiachio, Giannone), Gauchito Gil in Corrientes, the Fernández de Kirchner and Milei presidencies, the textile thread with Bispo do Rosário, and one the survey itself offered in order to rule out (the Nobel-science cluster of Houssay, Leloir and Milstein). **None is a relation between persons**, co-location is already visible on the map, and none is named in a campa. Julio Cortázar, noted in QUEUE.md as absent from the corpus, would give the same parallel and the same absence of any relation; not used.

**One link is carried, hedged.** Diego Maradona is named once, in the "Aguante" campa of August 2010, because the exhibition's subject is barra brava football culture and Fasoli is credited with large-scale Maradona portrait murals. **This is [R] and thin:** the claim comes from the survey's own external knowledge, flagged there as "connection inferred," and **no source in the research pool attests any Maradona mural by Fasoli**. The campa therefore says "credited with," names no wall and gives no date. If the attribution is sourced the hedge comes out; if it is disproved, one clause goes and nothing else changes.

## 7. Sources

**Reachable and used**

- **graffitimundo.com**: artist profile "Jaz" (verified verbatim), plus the "Aguante", "Jaz + Ever", "Jaz in Morocco", "new residence and global tour", "Interview: Jaz & Pastel" and "Pioneers of Trip to Nowhere" pages.
- **streetartnews.net**: roughly forty dated posts, September 2012 to August 2021, plus "Artist Interview: Jaz", 23 June 2015, and "Artist Retrospective", 16 February 2022. The backbone of the itinerary, and the only source giving day-level dates for most walls.
- **buenosairesstreetart.com**: dated mural posts October 2011 to December 2013; profile page; the 1 June 2011 interview, secondhand (below).
- **brooklynstreetart.com**: Fasoli tag page. Good for the "Publico Privado" monograph, the 2022 Ostend murals and the mid-nineties start date; unreliable on years (see the South Africa misfiling), unattributed in its characterisations.
- **MutualArt.com**: artist index line, the only source for 1981. **en.wikipedia.org and es.wikipedia.org**: checked, both 404, an attested negative.

**Not reachable, with the reason**

- **Buenos Aires Street Art, "All that Jaz and tango: Interview with street artist Franco Fasoli", 1 June 2011.** The single richest source in the record: the fileteado quote, the 1999 start date, "still very naïve", "the act of painting rather than an idea or a concept", the set-design and shared-workshop details. **It could not be fetched during this pass**, and everything drawn from it passes through the pool's summary. That is why the IUNA claim was dropped rather than carried.
- **jaz-art.com.ar** (the artist's own site), **widewalls.space, crystalshipoostende.be, b-murals.org, mojeek.com**, and StreetArtNews full-text search: did not resolve or returned nothing usable.
- **Live web search was unavailable for part of the gathering pass**: the session's WebSearch quota was exhausted (200 of 200 calls) before any Fasoli-specific query ran, so findings rest on pages reachable by direct URL fetch. **Spanish-language Argentine press was not searched at all**, and it is the likeliest place to settle the birth date, the birthplace, the IUNA question and the two undocumented 2015 shows. This is the largest single gap in the file.

**Unresolved, for a later pass**

Birth date and birthplace within Argentina. The start in the street: mid-1990s (graffitimundo, BSA) against 1999 (his own 2011 account). IUNA attendance, asserted by the pool and unsupported by any reachable page. The years of the Teatro Colón training, the first Barcelona trip, and the relocation to Barcelona. The barrio of the Castillo and Fitz Roy depot, Chacarita or Palermo. "First international festival," Atlanta 2013 by his own account against four earlier foreign walls. Venue and city for "Mito Fundacional" (2015). Ras Gallery, Barcelona, 17 May 2012, single-source. "Acrylic Walls" as the South African project's actual name. And whether any Maradona mural by Fasoli is documented anywhere.
