*Compiled 2026-08-12, revised 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Embroidery artist, born Seattle 1991, living and working in Bogotá; maker of the 2022 diptych "Encoded Threads."*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named on the same line · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**.

**Scope note (2026-08-14 revision).** This report and the accompanying journey were narrowed at Anthony's direction to what the artist states about herself on her own site and CV, plus verifiable facts about the "Encoded Threads" pieces themselves. Material drawn from separate personal essay writing about her work (studio visits, thematic comparisons to other figures, a workshop presentation) has been removed as out of scope for her journey; it belongs to that other writing, not to her own self-report.

## Phase 3: the diptych "Encoded Threads" (2022)

**Exhibitions on the CV for 2022**: WTF at Galería La Cometa, Bogotá; Dechado at Casa Hoffmann, Bogotá; Mexican Modernism at the Portland Art Museum; Pinta Miami in person. **[A: CV]**

**MAGDALENA, 2022**, thread on silk, garment, 70 x 42 in / 178 x 107 cm, described as "Commission for the Portland Art Museum." **[A: laurareneemaier.art/wildwoman, verbatim]** The source states a commission and says nothing about accession, so museum ownership stays off the record.

**The diptych, 2022:**
- Two canvas panels, black thread. One: nude, four-armed, horse-headed feminized Hayagriva facing right, 114 x 102 cm, worked in Topanga Canyon, California. Two: nude, dog-headed feminized Anubis / cynocephalic Saint Christopher, gazing and gesturing left, 72 x 102 cm, worked in Bogotá. Each nimbus is bordered with hexadecimal characters of a 32-byte SHA256 hash, first 16 bytes on the horse-headed panel and last 16 on the dog-headed panel, so the hash is verifiable only across the pair. Each panel carries a gilded fold secured by overlay stitches, said to conceal the private-key counterparts of the certificate's public keys.
- **12 December 2022:** two digital images, "Laura's Hayagriva" and "Laura's Christophia," created the same day in the two different locations, 512x512 px, black and white, generated from the certificate data each nimbus encodes, published via two 2-of-2 Dogecoin multisignature addresses: AD28...ozuw (Hayagriva + a third key named Anthony) and A7pf...aefV (Christophia + the same Anthony key). Neither image can be inscribed by one keyholder alone.
- The public description of "Encoded Threads" on her own site (laurareneemaier.art/encoded-threads) independently confirms the project explores "the extension of textile processes into blockchain technology," with "cryptographic proofs, stitched into the physical and on-chain canvas," and lists two catalogued pieces, Encoded Threads I and II (2022), both in private collections. **[A: laurareneemaier.art/encoded-threads]** Her site does not use the names Hayagriva or Christophia for the pieces; those names, and the on-chain/multisignature detail, come from a separate record of the certificate itself, not from her public artist statement.

### Standing notes on this phase

- **Where each panel was made is stated directly here** (Topanga Canyon and Bogotá respectively) rather than reconstructed; it is not on her public site.
- **On-chain verification.** No pass in this research queried a Dogecoin node directly against the addresses or hash named above; the technical detail comes from the certificate record, not from an independent chain query.
