# Research report: Los Catorce Santos Ayudantes (the Fourteen Holy Helpers / Vierzehn Nothelfer)

*2026-08-08. Subject: not a person but a devotional company. Fourteen late-antique martyrs (plus one non-martyr) assembled in the German dioceses of the fourteenth century and invoked as a single body against plague and unprepared death. Principal shrine: Vierzehnheiligen, above Bad Staffelstein, Upper Franconia.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or devotional literature · contradictions are flagged where found and **not adjudicated**.

A structural note first. This subject has no biography. Every other journey in the atlas follows one body through space; this one follows a *count*. The fourteen died between roughly 250 and 316 in Gaul, Rome, Palestine, Bithynia, Armenia and Lucania, and never met. The traveler tracked here comes into existence around 1300 in Bavaria, when the habit of invoking these dead *together* hardens into a fixed set. The report is organised by the phases of the company's life, so the first two phases are the prehistory of an object that does not yet exist.

---

## Phase I: the graves, c. 250 to 316

- **Denis**, first bishop of Lutetia, beheaded above Paris c. 250; the cephalophore legend **[R]**. The basilica over the grave, later the royal necropolis, **[A]**.
- **Cyriacus**, deacon, tortured, beheaded and buried on the Via Salaria in 303 **[A]**. His ministering to the enslaved labourers at the Baths of Diocletian is hagiographic **[R]**.
- **Acacius / Agathius**, d. 303 **[R]**. **Correction:** the pool framed this as an open contest between Agathius of Byzantium (Cappadocian centurion) and Acacius of Sebaste (Armenian priest, d. c. 304). It is not. The Nothelfer member is standardly Agathius; what is unsettled is which martyr of that name owns the early church of St Acacius at Constantinople.
- **George**, martyred at Diospolis (Lydda / Lod) 303, titular church built under Constantine **[A]**. The source calls the crypt sarcophagus a "symbolic tomb", weaker than the pool's "traditionally said to contain his relics"; downgraded in the campa.
- **Vitus**, born Mazara del Vallo c. 290, child-martyr in Lucania c. 303, **[R]** on both counts. Already travels the atlas as `san_vito`; his death sites are not re-pinned here, only his relic route.
- **Pantaleon**, imperial physician, born and martyred at Nicomedia c. 275 to c. 305 **[A]**; the liquefaction of his blood at Ravello **[R]**.
- **Blaise**, bishop of Sebastea, torn with wool-combs and beheaded under governor Agricola during the persecution of Licinius, 316 **[A]**. His dying request about the throat is quoted from Caxton, 1483: **[A] as text, [R] as event**.
- **Eustace**, the weakest member. **Correction:** the pool tagged the Roman church and the martyrdom together as [A]. Split them. Sant'Eustachio attested under Gregory II (731-741) is **[A]**; the martyrdom under Hadrian c. 118 is **[R]**, since en.wikipedia calls Eustace a "fictitious saint" whose veneration cannot be documented before the seventh century.
- **Barbara**, **Catherine of Alexandria**, **Christopher**, **Margaret of Antioch**, **Erasmus**, **Giles**: four of these already travel the atlas individually (see Interlocks).

Scholarly split, reported by en.wikipedia as a synthesis rather than from one document **[R]**: seven (Blaise, Cyriacus, Erasmus, George, Giles, Pantaleon, Vitus) are generally treated as historical, the other seven rest on legendary vitae. All but Giles are venerated as martyrs.

## Phase II: the relics move west and north, c. 450 to 1300

By 1300 most of these eastern and Roman dead have addresses in Frankish, German and Adriatic ground. That is what makes the later grouping physically possible.

- **Chalcedon**, martyrium to Christopher, 450-452 **[A]** (en.wikipedia, 'Saint Christopher').
- **Sant'Eustachio, Rome**, attested 731-741 **[A]**.
- **Saint-Denis**: Fulrad brings Vitus in 756 **[A]**; Suger records the first Eustace relics to reach Europe there in the twelfth century **[A]**.
- **Corvey Abbey**, receives Vitus from Saint-Denis in 836 **[A]** (en.wikipedia, 'Corvey Abbey'). **Correction:** pool coordinates about 800 m off; corrected to 51.77885, 9.40943.
- **Formia / Gaeta**: Erasmus bishop of Formia; after Saracens raze the town in 842 the cult and relics move to Gaeta **[A]** (en.wikipedia, 'Erasmus of Formia').
- **Dubrovnik**: the canon Stojko's 971 vision of a Venetian attack; skull, throat bone and both hands paraded each 3 February **[A]** (en.wikipedia, 'Saint Blaise'). **Correction:** the pool put the relics in Dubrovnik *Cathedral*. They are in the Church of St Blaise on the Stradun; the cathedral is dedicated to the Assumption. Renamed.
- **Cologne, St Pantaleon**: **correction applied twice.** The pool claimed a ninth-century dedication, at coordinates 720 m off. En.wikipedia records a church over a Roman villa "around 870" without naming its dedication; the Pantaleon dedication belongs to the monastery church begun 966, consecrated 980. Re-dated to 980, tagged **[R]**, coordinates corrected to 50.92861, 6.94806.

Recorded but **cut from the journey for length**, so a later expansion need not re-research them: Prague 925, Vitus's hand-bone to Wenceslas **[A]**; Maratea, relics of Blaise **[R]**, where it.wikipedia dates a transfer to 732 with no wreck while en.wikipedia has the ships wrecking under Leo III (**contradiction flagged**); Altorf in Alsace and Santa Maria in Via Lata, both receiving Cyriacus **[A]**, Altorf's pool coordinates 850 m off (correct 48.5231, 7.5292); Rab 1075, skull of Christopher **[R]**; Šibenik 1484, leg relic from Constantinople **[A]**; St Peter's Munich, skull of Erasmus **[A]**.

## Phase III: the company forms, c. 1300 to 1400

**The two standard encyclopedias flatly disagree here, and that disagreement is the most important thing in the dossier.**

- **En.wikipedia, 'Fourteen Holy Helpers'**: the cult "originated in the 14th century at first in the Rhineland, largely as a result of the epidemic ... known as the Black Death". No year given.
- **De.wikipedia, 'Vierzehn Nothelfer'**: the fixed group emerges "im Spätmittelalter in den Diözesen Regensburg, Bamberg und Würzburg sowie in Nürnberg", and the spread runs *from there* ("von dort") into the German-speaking world and on to Sweden, Hungary and Italy. **The Rhineland is not named as the origin at all.**

**Not adjudicated.** The journey pins Cologne's St Pantaleon on the Rhine and the Franconian and Bavarian sees, and lets both claims stand in the campa. **Correction applied:** the pool dated the Rhineland origin to **1330**, an anachronism, since the Black Death reached Europe in 1347-48 and no source gives a year at all.

Waypoints:

- **c. 1320**, fresco in the Dominican church of St Blasius, Regensburg: oldest known group image, ten saints around Christopher **[A]** (de.wikipedia). **Correction applied twice:** the pool's coordinates were 650 m off, and the pool's *afterlife* lens gave the cathedral's coordinates for the Dominican church, collapsing two distinct sites into one pin. Corrected to 49.01806, 12.09083.
- **c. 1350**, fixed company invoked against plague and sudden death in the dioceses of Regensburg, Bamberg and Würzburg, with Barbara, Margaret and Catherine as the virgin-martyr core: **[A] within the corpus**, inherited from `saint_barbara.journey.json`, pin 49.0134, 12.1016 taken byte-identical.
- **1360**, Regensburg Cathedral windows showing Oswald and Leonhard **[A]** (de.wikipedia). **Correction applied twice.** The pool wrote that these saints appear "alongside the group"; the source says only that the depictions "weisen auf die frühe Verehrung der Nothelfer". And the pool's afterlife lens attached a self-generated "contradiction flag" claiming a clash with the canonical list. There is no clash: neither man is among the eventual fourteen, which is exactly the fluidity the source is describing. Flag removed.
- **c. 1400**, the fourteen appear in closed, standardised sequence across Regensburg, Bamberg, Würzburg and Nuremberg **[A]** (de.wikipedia). Canonical membership: Acacius, Barbara, Blaise, Catherine, Christopher, Cyriacus, Denis, Erasmus, Eustace, George, Giles, Margaret, Pantaleon, Vitus **[R]**, with regional lists still substituting Leonard or Sebastian before the set hardens.

The functional logic of the membership (each saint matched to a feared mode of dying without last rites: throat for Blaise, belly for Erasmus, sudden death for Christopher, Barbara and Catherine, good confession for Giles, livestock for George, Erasmus, Pantaleon and Vitus) is **[R]**. No primary source states it as a scheme. The closest thing is Bonaventure Hammer, 1908, a devotional compiler who is a witness to the tradition's account of itself and not to the fourteenth century.

## Phase IV: the meadow at Langheim, 1445 to 1450

- **24 September 1445**: Hermann Leicht, shepherd on a field of the Cistercian abbey of Langheim, hears a child crying and sees a child sitting in the field; it vanishes when he approaches **[A]** (en.wikipedia). **Contradiction flagged:** Hammer 1908 gives **22 September** and spells him **Herman Leicht** with one m. Not resolved. The journey carries 24 September in `date`, the discrepancy in `date_confidence`, and Hammer's sentence as the verbatim quote so the reader meets the other date in the source's own words.
- **1446**: a second apparition **[A]**, folded into the third stop for length.
- **28 June 1446**: the child returns with thirteen others, a red cross and two floating candles, and asks for a chapel **[A]** (de.wikipedia). The words come from Hammer: "We are the Fourteen Helpers, and desire that a chapel be built for us. Be thou our servant, and we shall serve thee." **[A] as text of the tradition**, not as transcript.
- **Eighteen days later**, a mortally ill maidservant of Langheim is reported healed and pilgrimage begins **[R]** (en.wikipedia, explicitly tradition, not independently attested).
- **1448**: altar consecrated on the site **[A]**.
- **Nicholas V attaches indulgences** to devotion of the Fourteen **[R]**. **Correction applied:** the pool dated this 1450; no year is in the source. En.wikipedia verbatim: "As the saints' joint cultus spread in the fifteenth century, Pope Nicholas V attached indulgences to devotion of the Fourteen Holy Helpers, though these no longer apply." The pontificate ran 1447 to 1455. Tag downgraded [A] to [R]; hedge moved into `date_confidence`.

## Phase V: saturation and ruin, 1480 to 1632

- **Late fifteenth century, undated**: the cult spreads through the German-speaking lands and to Sweden, Hungary and Italy **[A]** (de.wikipedia). **Correction:** the pool's year **1480** is invented; the source gives none.
- **1498**: altar at Heilsbronn Minster depicting the assembled fourteen **[A]**. **Correction:** coordinates 2.4 km off; corrected to 49.33778, 10.79194.
- **By c. 1500**: roughly 800 churches in the German-speaking lands hold the patronage **[A]** (de.wikipedia).
- **1503**: winged altar with Christ as Man of Sorrows and the Fourteen on the outer wings. **Three corrections.** (a) Painted for **St Bartholomew's, Bindlach**, and moved to Lindenhardt only in **1687** after a fire (de.wikipedia, 'Lindenhardt'); the pool placed it at Lindenhardt from the start. (b) The **Grünewald** attribution dates only from 1915 and recent scholarship proposes **Hans von Kulmbach**; tag downgraded to **[R]**. (c) The pool's geography lens claimed Kloster Langheim commissioned it. Nothing supports that; Bindlach is roughly 50 km away and no source connects the two. Clause deleted.
- **1525**: the first chapel destroyed in the German Peasants' War **[A]**.
- **Thirty Years' War**: rebuilt church destroyed again and restored, **[A] as event, year unattested**. **Correction:** the pool's **1632** is not in the source, which says only "nachdem diese im Dreißigjährigen Krieg ebenfalls zerstört worden war". The journey keeps 1632 in `date` for map ordering and says plainly in `date_confidence` that no year is attested.

## Phase VI: Neumann's three ovals, 1735 to 1772

- **1735**: Abbot Stephan Mösinger of Langheim obtains permission for a new church **[A]**.
- **April 1743**: cornerstone laid to Balthasar Neumann's design **[A]**.
- **1744**: after foundations and the three apses are built, Langheim demands cost reductions; the Altar of Grace, which marks the apparition spot and cannot be moved, ends up mid-nave, and Neumann reorganises the interior into three interlocking ovals with the altar at the centre of the largest **[A]** (en.wikipedia, 'Vierzehnheiligen'). The best-documented episode in the dossier, and the only one where an artistic form is demonstrably produced by a devotional constraint.
- **19 August 1753**: Neumann dies at Würzburg; Johann Thomas Nissler completes the vaulting **[A]**.
- **14 September 1772**: consecration by Prince-Bishop Adam Friedrich von Seinsheim **[A]**.

## Phase VII: secularisation, fire, restoration, 1803 to 1900

- **1803**: Bavarian secularisation dissolves Kloster Langheim **[A]**. **Correction:** pool coordinates 3.3 km off; corrected to 50.11219, 11.11011.
- **1835**: lightning destroys organ, roof structure and both towers **[A]**.
- **1839**: Ludwig I commissions Franciscans to take charge and restore **[A]**. Folded into the 1835 stop.
- **1897**: Leo XIII raises the church to minor basilica, the second in Germany **[A]** (both encyclopedias agree).

## Phase VIII: the calendar, 1969 to the present

- **1969**: the revised General Roman Calendar drops the individual feasts of **four** of the fourteen (Barbara, Catherine of Alexandria, Christopher, Margaret of Antioch) from the universal calendar; none is decanonised and all keep local and votive observance **[A]**. **Correction applied, and an internal contradiction inside the research pool flagged:** the pool's afterlife lens said the revision "removes most of the Fourteen". That is wrong and it contradicts the pool's own chronology lens, which gives the correct figure of four.
- **1999**: Rieger organ of 68 registers installed **[A]**.
- **2004**: John Paul II restores an optional memorial for Catherine of Alexandria on 25 November **[A]**.
- **Present**: pilgrimage roughly May to October; the basilica and the former abbey of Banz across the Main valley are locally called the "Goldene Pforte" **[A]**.

## Contradictions flagged and left open

1. **Rhineland or Franconia.** En.wikipedia says the Rhineland; de.wikipedia says Regensburg, Bamberg, Würzburg and Nuremberg, and makes Regensburg the point of departure for the spread. These are two different origins, not two aspects of one.
2. **The first apparition.** Hammer 1908: 22 September 1445, "Herman Leicht". En.wikipedia: 24 September 1445, "Hermann Leicht".
3. **The culminating apparition, inside this atlas.** `saint_barbara.journey.json` and `san_vito.journey.json` date it **1446-06-24**; `giles.journey.json` dates the same vision **1446-07-28**; German sources give **28 June 1446**. `san_vito.journey.json` already carries an internal note that the corpus files are unreconciled. This journey uses 1446-06-28. **The atlas now carries three dates for one vision.** Fixing it means editing three approved files and is a decision for Anthony, not for this run.
4. **The shrine pin.** Four existing files pin Vierzehnheiligen at **50.1157, 11.0541**; `giles.journey.json` pins **50.1156, 11.0544**. This journey inherits the majority pin byte-identically, per the interlock lens.
5. **Blaise's relics at Maratea.** It.wikipedia: transfer, 732, no wreck. En.wikipedia: shipwreck under the iconoclast persecutions.
6. **The Bindlach/Lindenhardt painter.** Grünewald (ascribed 1915) versus Hans von Kulmbach (recent).
7. **The dedicatee of the early church of St Acacius at Constantinople.** Several candidates; the identification of the *Nothelfer* Acacius with Agathius of Byzantium is not itself contested.

## Honest gaps

- **No primary medieval document was reached.** Everything before 1908 comes through encyclopedia articles or Caxton, 1483. The Regensburg fresco, the 1360 windows, the Heilsbronn altar, the 1448 consecration and the Nicholas V indulgence are known here only at second hand, with no archival shelfmark for any of them.
- **The folk rhyme** ("Margaretha mit dem Wurm, Barbara mit dem Turm, Katharina mit dem Radl, das sind die drei heiligen Madl") could not be traced to a printed original or dated to a chronicle. Tagged **[R]**, with that caveat in the quote's source field.
- **The symptom-matching scheme** is a reconstruction. Hammer 1908 asserts something close to it, five hundred years after the fact.
- **No date could be established** for the Nicholas V indulgence, the Thirty Years' War destruction, the spread to Sweden, Hungary and Italy, the translation of Cyriacus to Altorf, or the arrival of Erasmus's skull in Munich.
- **Membership before c. 1400** is not recoverable beyond the note that Leonard and Sebastian sometimes appear. No pre-fixation regional list was found.
