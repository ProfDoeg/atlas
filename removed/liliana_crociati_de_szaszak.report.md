# Liliana Crociati de Szaszak (1944-1970): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Liliana Crociati de Szaszak, born Buenos Aires 1944, died Innsbruck 27 February 1970; buried in the Cementerio de la Recoleta, Buenos Aires, under a neo-Gothic chapel designed by her mother and a life-size bronze by Wilfredo Viladrich.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference; the source of the inference is named. Contradictions between sources are flagged and left standing unless a source of clearly higher authority resolves them, in which case both readings are recorded and the resolution is stated as such.

**The governing source problem.** This subject has no biography. She has a tomb, and the tomb has an encyclopaedia article. Almost everything in general circulation, in both English and Spanish, descends from two Wikipedia articles ("Tomb of Liliana Crociati de Szaszak" / "Tumba de Liliana Crociati de Szaszak") and from tour-guide repetition of those articles. A single further source, **recoletacemetery.com (post 646, with a 2021 update at post 312)**, run by a cemetery historian working from testimony and photographs supplied by the husband's own family, contradicts the encyclopaedia on at least six points of fact. Where the family testimony and the encyclopaedia disagree, this report records both and says which is better placed to know. It does not pretend the disagreement is settled in the sources themselves.

---

## 1. Birth and childhood, Buenos Aires 1944-c.1960

- Liliana Crociati is born in **1944**, in Buenos Aires, the only child of the family. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Tumba de Liliana Crociati de Szaszak"]**
- Her father works as a **hairdresser to Buenos Aires society**; her mother is described as artistically inclined, later as the designer of the tomb. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **No street address, no clinic, no parish of birth has been located** in any source consulted. The Barrio Norte / Recoleta pin used in the journey file is a neighbourhood proxy for a family of this class and trade, not an address. **[R: stated as reconstruction]**
- The father's salon is placed on or near **Avenida Alvear** on the same reasoning: the trade is attested, the premises are not. **[R]**
- **Schooling.** Earlier drafts of this material reconstructed a convent classroom from class custom. That reconstruction is **withdrawn** in favour of an attested detail: recoletacemetery.com records that she **"estudiaba Bellas Artes"**, and that the face of her statue was modelled on a friend from the School of Fine Arts. **[A: recoletacemetery.com/?p=646, from family testimony]**. No enrolment record, no dated matriculation, and no work of her own hand has been located. **Gap.**
- The **Teatro Colón** stop in the journey file is explicitly speculative: no programme, guest list, or press notice places her there. It is retained as an era-typical venue and flagged as such in `date_confidence`. **[R]**

**Age contradiction, flagged and unresolved.** The tomb gives her age at death as **26**, consistent with a 1944 birth. The **La Nación** death notice of 27 February 1970, as transcribed by recoletacemetery.com, gives **24**. Both are reported here. Neither can be checked against a birth certificate, which has not been located. This propagates backwards through every date in her early life.

## 2. Marriage, 1966

- She marries **Janos "Jancsi" Szaszak**. The husband's name and nickname are **[A: es.wikipedia]**.
- The marriage date is **17 October 1966**. **[A: recoletacemetery.com/?p=646, from family testimony]**
- **The wedding church is not attested.** The Basílica Nuestra Señora del Pilar is named in the journey file as a guess, the default high-society Recoleta church of the period, standing some two hundred metres from the grave. No parish register or newspaper notice has been located. **[R: explicitly a guess]**
- **The reception venue is not attested.** The Plaza Hotel appears in the journey file on the same footing. **[R]**

**Correction propagated through the whole file.** Earlier working material dated the courtship c. 1968-1969 and the wedding c. 1969-1970, i.e. immediately before the avalanche. That dating is **wrong by more than three years**. The couple had been married **three years and four months** when the avalanche struck.

**The honeymoon error, flagged.** English Wikipedia and essentially every downstream retelling describe the February 1970 trip as the couple's **honeymoon**. Given a wedding of 17 October 1966 this cannot be true. The error is durable because a bride killed on her wedding journey is a more transmissible story than a married couple on a winter holiday. Both versions are recorded; the family testimony is the better-placed source and the journey file follows it, with the discrepancy stated in the campa.

- **The married years, 1966-1970, are a total gap.** No address, no employment, no children, no travel, nothing. Four years of an adult life with no record located.

## 3. The Alps, February 1970

- The holiday **begins at Val d'Isère, in the French Alps**. **[A: recoletacemetery.com/?p=646]**. This contradicts every summary that has the couple in Austria for the whole trip.
- At some point they move east to **Zürs am Arlberg, Vorarlberg, Austria**. The reason for the move, the road taken, and the date are **not recorded anywhere located. Gap.**
- On **26 February 1970 an avalanche struck the hotel and came through the window of their second-floor room while they slept.** **[A]** Verbatim: *"On 26 Feb 1970, an avalanche covered their hotel & broke through their second story room window"* (recoletacemetery.com/?p=646). es.wikipedia independently attests the second-floor window and the date.
- **Janos Szaszak** is recovered with **hypothermia**, quickly reversed. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **Liliana is recovered alive and gravely injured.** She is **airlifted to Innsbruck**, declared **brain dead**, and her husband **authorizes withdrawal of life support**. She dies **27 February 1970**, the day after the avalanche. **[A: La Nación obituary of 27 Feb 1970, transcribed at recoletacemetery.com/?p=646]**

### Contradictions in this section, flagged

1. **Where the avalanche struck.** es.wikipedia places the hotel **"64 km west of Innsbruck"**, which points to the Paznaun/Silvretta area (Ischgl, Galtür) and matches the hotel name **Piz Buin** carried in several accounts. The family-sourced account places it at **Zürs am Arlberg, in Vorarlberg**, which is roughly **90 km west in a straight line and ~120 km by road**, on the far side of the Arlberg and in a different province. **The hotel name and the village do not sit comfortably together and this report does not reconcile them.** The journey file pins Zürs (47.1689, 10.1633) on the strength of the family testimony and records the Paznaun reading in the stop sources.
2. **Interlock contradiction with the existing corpus.** `viladrich.journey.json` already carries a stop **"Hotel Piz Buin, Tyrol, the avalanche"** pinned at **46.9681, 10.1869 (Galtür, Tyrol)**. That pin follows the es.wikipedia reading. This file departs from it. **The two files now disagree on where Liliana Crociati died** and the disagreement is recorded here rather than silently harmonized. If Zürs is accepted, the Viladrich stop needs re-pinning; that is an editorial decision, not a research one.
3. **When and where she died.** English-language summaries have her **killed outright at the hotel on 26 February**; es.wikipedia has her dying in Innsbruck **"days later"**. Both are wrong against the obituary, which gives **27 February in Innsbruck, one day after**. This is the one contradiction this report does treat as resolved, and it says so openly: the resolving source is a dated newspaper death notice, which outranks both encyclopaedia summaries.
4. **Her age.** 26 (tomb) against 24 (La Nación). **Unresolved.**

- **The notification of the parents in Buenos Aires** is reconstructed. No transcript, cable, or account of that day exists. What is attested is the aftermath: both parents are described as having lost interest in living. **[A: es.wikipedia; the scene itself R]**

## 4. Repatriation and burial, March 1970

- Her body is **returned to Argentina and interred in La Recoleta Cemetery**. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Tomb of Liliana Crociati de Szaszak"]**
- **Every logistical detail of the repatriation is reconstruction**: the departure through Innsbruck-Kranebitten, the arrival at Ezeiza, the date, the carrier. No manifest, no notice, no photograph located. **[R]**
- The **outbound flight legs** in the journey file (Ezeiza, Rio de Janeiro, Dakar, Madrid) are likewise **reconstruction from the 1970 South Atlantic route structure**, not from any document of this trip. They are marked as such in `date_confidence` on every stop. Two legs carried in earlier working material, **Zurich** and an **Innsbruck arrival by air**, have been **dropped**: with the holiday now known to have begun at Val d'Isère in France, an overland move to the Arlberg is likelier than a flight, and the Zurich leg served a route that no longer exists in the corrected itinerary. **Landeck** is retained as a route town but its rationale is corrected: it lies on the Innsbruck-St Anton-Flexenpass road to Zürs, not on any road to Ischgl, and it is only on the itinerary if they approached from the east at all.
- The funeral mass at the Basílica del Pilar is **reconstruction**, paired with the equally unattested wedding venue. **[R]**

## 5. The tomb

- **The mother designs the mausoleum herself.** es.wikipedia is explicit that she designed it; it does not say the parents commissioned a design. **[A]** The distinction matters and has been flattened in most retellings.
- The chapel is **neo-Gothic, of wood and glass, with large stained-glass windows admitting abundant light**, in deliberate contrast to the shut marble of the surrounding vaults. **[A: es.wikipedia; en.wikipedia describes the same contrast]**
- **No construction or completion date is attested.** The "1970" carried in several summaries for the tomb's completion is unsupported. **Gap.**
- **Wilfredo Viladrich** casts a **life-size bronze of Liliana, standing, in a wedding dress, wearing her engagement ring**, her hand resting on the head of her dog. **[A: en.wikipedia and es.wikipedia]**
  - **Name spelling, flagged.** en.wikipedia: *Wilfredo Viladrich*. es.wikipedia: *Wílfredo Viladrich*. Several downstream sources and earlier working material: *Wifredo*. The form used here is **Wilfredo**. recoletacemetery.com, working from family testimony, **does not name a sculptor at all**.
  - **The dress is not an exact replica** of her wedding dress, per photographs supplied by the family. **[A: recoletacemetery.com/?p=646]** This contradicts the standard description.
  - **The face is a portrait of one of Liliana's friends from the School of Fine Arts**, not of Liliana. **[A: recoletacemetery.com/?p=646]** This too contradicts the standard description, and it is the single most consequential correction in the file: the statue everyone photographs as her likeness is not her likeness.
- The detail that the bronze holds **"flowers given by an unknown admirer"** appears in Wikipedia without attribution and is carried here as **[R]**, tradition of unknown origin.
- **The poem.** Her father composes an Italian-language poem, **"A Mia Figlia"**, set on a plaque at the base. **[A: en.wikipedia, which reproduces the full Italian text with an English translation]**. Earlier working material asserted that no copy of the text existed outside the tomb; that assertion was **false** and is withdrawn. The poem opens:

  > Solo mi chiedo il perché / Tu se partita e distrutto hai lasciato il mio cuore.

  The characterization of the poem as resolving grief "into fate and enduring love in the pattern typical of Italian-Argentine mourning verse" is **[R]**, a reading, not a source.
- **Both parents are buried in the same tomb.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** **Neither death year is attested. Gap.** The claim that they died "within a few years" is unsourced beyond the general statement that they died some years later.

## 6. Sabú

- **Sabú was Liliana's living dog**, and appears at her side in the finished bronze with her hand on his head. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **The bronze of the dog was added after the dog's death**, as a second casting. **[A: en.wikipedia]** **No date for the second casting has been located. Gap.**
- **Sabú died many years after Liliana.** **[A: the husband's family to recoletacemetery.com, 2021 update, post 312]**
- **The grief-death legend is refuted, not merely unverified.** The widely told version, that Sabú refused food and died of grief on or near the day of her funeral, and that the statue was commissioned partly in his memory at the time, is **contradicted by the family's own statement**. Earlier working material flagged it as "unverified"; that is too weak. It is **false**, and it is included in the journey file as a false story that is nonetheless part of how the site is told, labelled as such.

## 7. Afterwards

- **Janos Szaszak** survived. Per the family account: he **authorized the removal of life support at Innsbruck**, later **married a woman called Trixie**, had **two children**, and **died in 1996**. **[A: recoletacemetery.com/?p=646]**. Earlier working material recorded his later life as an unbridgeable gap; that gap is now closed. **Where he lived after 1970 is still not stated. Gap.**
- **The polished snout.** Popular belief holds that touching the bronze dog's nose brings luck; the metal there is visibly bright against the patina of the rest. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **The "most photographed tomb" claim is not supported by the source usually cited for it.** en.wikipedia's *La Recoleta Cemetery* article says only that her tomb, **"due to its unusual design, is of special interest"**, and separately describes the statue and the poem. It does **not** rank it among the most photographed or most visited monuments, and does not call it "a notable tourist attraction". Working material that tagged this claim **[A]** was **overstating its own citation**; it is downgraded to **[R]**, tour-guide tradition.
- **Wikimedia Commons** catalogues the tomb at approximately **34°35'18.22"S, 58°23'35.36"W**, sharing a stretch of pathway with the tomb of **R. Fuchs-Ritrovato**. **[A: commons.wikimedia.org, Category:Liliana Crociati de Szaszak]**
- **There is no biography.** No Spanish Wikipedia article treats her as a person; the only article is titled for the tomb. **[A: es.wikipedia search]** Her reputation survives as cemetery iconography rather than as a life.

## 8. Interlock notes

The interlock lens returned a large number of Recoleta co-occurrences: Jules-Félix Coutan, Torcuato de Alvear, Eva Perón, Julio Argentino Roca, Tschiffely, Victoria and Silvina Ocampo, Alejandro Christophersen, Marta Ezcurra, Juan Manuel de Rosas, Isabel Perón. **These are shared ground, not relations.** None of them met, corresponded with, influenced, or wrote about Liliana Crociati, and she predeceased or postdeceased them without contact. The map already shows co-location; naming them in the prose would be decoration. **None is named in a campa.**

**The one real interlock is Wilfredo Viladrich**, who made her effigy and her dog's. He is named in the campas of the two casting stops. `viladrich.journey.json` carries a whole segment, "The Bride of Recoleta", on this commission, and dates it 1972; that date is inherited here for the bride casting.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** the Recoleta cemetery pin **-34.5877, -58.3931** (as used in `viladrich.journey.json`, `jules_felix_coutan.journey.json`, `julio_argentino_roca.journey.json`, and others) and the Buenos Aires pin **-34.6037, -58.3816**.

**Pin departure, recorded:** the avalanche site, for the reasons given in section 3.

## Sources

**Reachable and used**

- **en.wikipedia.org, "Tomb of Liliana Crociati de Szaszak"**: the fullest general account; carries the complete Italian text of "A Mia Figlia" with translation, the sculptor's name, the wedding-dress and engagement-ring description, the two-casting sequence for the dog.
- **es.wikipedia.org, "Tumba de Liliana Crociati de Szaszak"**: the mother as designer (not commissioner), the neo-Gothic chapel with stained glass, the second-floor window and the date of the avalanche, the husband's hypothermia, the polished snout, the parents' decline and burial in the same tomb, the "64 km west of Innsbruck" location.
- **en.wikipedia.org, "La Recoleta Cemetery"**: the phrase "due to its unusual design, is of special interest"; used here chiefly to *disprove* a claim commonly attributed to it.
- **recoletacemetery.com/?p=646, with the 2021 update at post 312**: the decisive source, working from testimony and photographs supplied by Janos Szaszak's family. Sole source for: the marriage date of 17 October 1966; the trip beginning at Val d'Isère; Zürs am Arlberg; the airlift, the brain-death declaration, the withdrawal of life support, and the death on 27 February 1970; the La Nación obituary and its age of 24; the dress not being an exact replica; the face being a friend's; Sabú dying many years later; Jancsi's remarriage to Trixie, two children, and death in 1996.
- **commons.wikimedia.org, Category:Liliana Crociati de Szaszak**: coordinates to the second of arc; the neighbouring Fuchs-Ritrovato vault.
- **Corpus files consulted for interlock and pins:** `viladrich.journey.json`, `jules_felix_coutan.journey.json`, `torcuato_de_alvear.journey.json`, `eva_peron.journey.json`, `marta_ezcurra.journey.json`, `QUEUE.md` (lines 891-897), `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` (lines 213, 223).

**Not reachable (recorded as gaps, not as absence of evidence)**

- **Argentine press of 1970 in the original.** The La Nación death notice of 27 February 1970 is used here **only as transcribed by recoletacemetery.com**; the paper itself was not consulted. Its age figure of 24, which contradicts the tomb, therefore rests on a single transcription.
- **Austrian press of February 1970** on the Arlberg / Paznaun avalanches. Nothing was accessible. This is the source class that would settle the Zürs-versus-Galtür question and it was not read.
- **Infobae, La Nación online, Clarín, Atlas Obscura, Taringa**: URLs guessed and fetched; returned 404 or were blocked.
- **The WebSearch tool's session budget was exhausted before this subject could be searched**, so the source set is narrower than is usual for this corpus. Recorded for the editorial record: **this file should not be promoted to canon without a search pass against Argentine and Austrian press.**
- **No civil records**: no birth certificate, no marriage register entry, no death certificate, no cemetery internment record has been seen. Every date in this file except 17 October 1966, 26 February 1970, and 27 February 1970 rests on secondary report or on inference.
