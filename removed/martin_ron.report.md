# Martín Ron (b. 1981): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-14. Muralist, b. Caseros, Partido de Tres de Febrero. Living subject: no death, no grave, no posthumous cult. The "monument" is a dispersed inventory of walls, some already demolished.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, single-source assertion, or press superlative with no verifiable body behind it. Contradictions are printed and left standing, not adjudicated.

**Method note.** The evidence is thin and top-heavy. Nearly the whole pre-2020 chronology descends from one lightly cited document, the Spanish Wikipedia article *Martín Ron (artista)*, demonstrably wrong on at least three points (Contradictions 2, 3, 6). Wherever a second source was reachable it **corrected** Wikipedia rather than confirming it, which is the main finding of this pass. Clarín's site was unfetchable throughout, so every Clarín citation below reaches this report **through** Wikipedia's footnotes.

---

## 1. Caseros and the formation (1981 - c. 2010)

- **Born 13 March 1981 in Caseros**, cabecera of the Partido de Tres de Febrero, Buenos Aires Province. **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Martín Ron (artista)']**
  - *Coordinate conflict, flagged.* Three values for the birthplace across the lenses: -34.6055/-58.5651, -34.6031/-58.5636, and es.wikipedia's own town article, **-34.6167/-58.5667**. The journey uses the last; the others are ~1.3 and ~1.5 km off with no source behind them.
- **Studies *gestión en arte y cultura* at the Universidad Nacional de Tres de Febrero (UNTREF).** **[A: es.wikipedia]** No years given anywhere. The journey carries a placeholder year of 2000, hedged in `date_confidence`; it is not evidence.
- **Later teaches *muralismo* at UNTREF**, then moves to independent seminars. **[A: es.wikipedia]** Undated; placed before 2010 in the journey on sequence only.
- **Paints a mural along the Ferrocarril General San Martín tracks in Caseros.** **[A: es.wikipedia, image caption]** Undated: a genuine gap, the source being a photograph caption.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable documents how he learned to paint, who trained him, when he first worked a wall, or whether he came out of the 1990s Buenos Aires graffiti scene at all. Circulating description of a "graffiti-covered childhood Caseros" is atmospheric writing, not sourced fact, and is not carried into the journey.

## 2. Embellecimiento Urbano and the icon murals (2010 - 2012)

- **From 2010, artistic director of the *Embellecimiento Urbano* program**, eventually 250+ murals with various collaborators. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
  - *Jurisdiction error, flagged.* One summary placed these murals "en la ciudad de Buenos Aires." The source says **Partido de Tres de Febrero**, in Buenos Aires **Province**. The distinction is the substance of the story: his own suburban district, not the capital.
- **From 2011, a series of large-scale murals honouring popular-culture icons**, with other artists. **[A: Clarín, 'Arte urbano: homenajes en los muros a los ídolos populares', Nahuel Gallot, via es.wikipedia]** The **series** is dated 2011. **No individual mural in it is dated by any source.**
  - **Carlos Tévez**, in Barrio Ejército de los Andes (Fuerte Apache), Tévez's home barrio. **[A for the location]**: the source says so outright ("Carlos Tévez, que se encuentra en su barrio natal Ejército de los Andes"). One lens tagged it **[R]**, thinking the site inferred from Tévez's biography; it is sourced, so the tag is corrected. Date unknown.
  - **Diego Maradona.** **[A that he is among the figures; R for any date or location]** *Conflation risk, flagged:* Ron's best-known Maradona work is the 2022 Constitución mural (section 6), a different piece entirely. Do not merge the two.
  - **Isabel Sarli.** **[A that she is among the figures]** No date, no location, no confirmation the wall survives. **Stated gap.**
  - **La Mona Jiménez.** **[A that he is among the figures]** No date, no location. The singer's base is Córdoba, hundreds of kilometres from Ron's working ground, so even the province is open. **Stated gap.**
- **Meeting of Styles, Buenos Aires: 'Pedro Luján y su perro'.** **[A: buenosairesstreetart.com interview, November 2012]** The richest correction of the pass. The interview establishes: the **second** edition of Meeting of Styles BA (he painted the first too); a **factory facade on Pedro de Luján street, Barracas**, hence the title; a **flying sea turtle coming out of a hole in the wall above an elderly man seated with his dog**, not a turtle alone. It also records that **the wall was demolished in 2017 and the mural no longer exists**, an afterlife fact absent from every other lens.
- **First trip abroad: the Ibug Culture and Urban Art Festival, Glauchau, Germany, 2012.** **[A: es.wikipedia]**

## 3. The subte centennial and Villa Urquiza (2013)

The Buenos Aires underground turned a hundred in 2013, and Ron's year is almost entirely inside that anniversary.

- **An entire Línea A formation painted with a hundred Argentine figures**, including Julio Cortázar, Mercedes Sosa, Carlos Tévez, Juan Manuel Fangio, Luis Alberto Spinetta. **[A: Clarín, 'Pintan retratos de ídolos de la cultura popular en los vagones de la línea A', Nahuel Gallot, via es.wikipedia]**
  - *Flagged:* one summary in the pool reduced this to "un coche," a single car. The source says "una formación completa," an entire train. Corrected.
- **Station murals, 2013: Plaza Miserere, Medrano, Plaza de Mayo.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** Individually undated within the year.
- **Support columns of Línea B in a chromatic gradient**, an effect for passengers on moving trains. **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **Hospitales station: Tita Merello and Ángel Villoldo**, for the city government's *Paseo Cultural del Tango*. **[A: Buenos Aires Ciudad, 'Línea H' page]** Two errors corrected here:
  1. **Hospitales is on Línea H, not Línea A** (es.wikipedia, 'Hospitales (Subte de Buenos Aires)', now Hospitales-Ringo Bonavena, Av. Almafuerte 300, Parque Patricios). Wikipedia's own footnote here is the GCBA **Línea H** page, so the article contradicts its citation.
  2. **The work is not solely Ron's.** The station article credits the Villoldo and Merello homages to "Martín Ron y Leandro Frizzero."
- **'El cuento de los loros', Villa Urquiza: 412 m², a four-storey medianera**, an initiative of BA Street Art with the city government. **[A: Clarín, 'El mural más grande, en Villa Urquiza', Nora Sánchez, via es.wikipedia]**
  - *Overstatement corrected.* Wikipedia says "el mural más grande hasta el momento **en el barrio de Villa Urquiza**": largest in that barrio, not in the city. The wider claim is dropped.
  - *Coordinate corrected.* One lens gave -34.5763/-58.5000, outside the barrio, ~1.3 km WSW toward Villa Pueyrredón. The journey uses -34.5711/-58.4869. **Gap:** the street address could not be recovered (clarin.com blocked, BA Street Art tag page empty).

## 4. Export (2014 - 2015)

- **'Proyecto Dúo', twelve artists paired into duos; his partner is Nase Pop, 2014.** **[A: es.wikipedia]**
- **'From Argentina with Love', Vice Gallery, Miami, 2014.** **[A: Vice Gallery page, Wayback copy, via es.wikipedia]** Wynwood address unverified; coordinates are neighbourhood-level.
- **Rock Werchter, Belgium: North West Walls, curated by Arne Quinze.** A portrait on a tower of stacked shipping containers: a man with a parrot on a branch in his mouth. **[A for the event; date NOT established]**
  - *Date contradiction, unresolved.* One lens dated it **2015**, another **2014**; the cited passage **gives no year at all**, sitting between the dated 2014 Miami and dated 2015 England paragraphs, which is presumably how both guesses arose. arnequinze.com and its Wayback copy were unreachable. The journey uses 2014 only to hold sequence.
  - *Coordinate corrected.* Rock Werchter is at 50.9681/4.6842 (en.wikipedia); one lens gave 4.7717, about 6 km east.
- **Three murals in England in 2015.** **[A: es.wikipedia, "En el año 2015 pintó tres murales en Inglaterra"]** The only dated anchor for the England work; everything else grouped around it inherits the year by inference.
  - **Old Truman Brewery, London.** **[A: London Calling Blog, 5 August 2015]** Two corrections:
    1. **Location.** Wikipedia says "Colbert Place"; the blog it cites says **Ely's Place / Ely's Yard**, off Hanbury Street inside the brewery grounds. The Wikipedia form has propagated into every downstream summary.
    2. **Date.** 5 August 2015 is the **publication** date; the post says the wall was painted "last Saturday and Sunday," i.e. late July or 1-2 August.
    - Subject: a man's head in a circular checkerboard, crown engulfed in flames. The post also notes Ron had painted in the area **before** (with ROA on Hanbury Street, and at Village Underground), which undercuts 2015 as his arrival in London.
  - **Number 90, Hackney Wick: 'Machaco'.** **[A for the wall; the sole attribution is wrong]** London Calling, 5 August 2015: "Martin Ron is currently working on a large scale collaboration with fellow Argentinian Street Artist **Jiant** in Hackney Wick." Title and year come from Wikipedia alone. Address corrected to 90 Wallis Road, E9 (~51.5447/-0.0225).
  - **'Andromeda', Urban Paint Festival, Bristol.** **[R]** upfest.co.uk names **neither Ron nor 'Andromeda'**, so the attribution rests on one Wikipedia line. Upfest runs in **Bedminster and Southville** (~51.4406/-2.5988), not the city centre.
- **Solo exhibition, Mead Carney Gallery, London.** **[A that it happened; date and street both unresolved]** Wikipedia gives **no year** ("Su segunda exposición fue en Londres, Inglaterra en la galería Mead Carney esta vez en solitario"), so grouping it with 2015 is inference; it also calls it his **second exhibition**, full stop, not his second overseas one.
  - *Street contradiction, flagged:* one lens put the gallery on **Cork Street**, the trade record on **Dover Street, Mayfair**. Unsettled: meadcarney.com serves a parked WordPress listing, artsy.net returned HTTP 403.
- **'Mudra', Tallinn, Estonia, 2015.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** Wall unlocated; city-centre coordinates used.
- **Three hyperrealist 3D sea turtles, George Town, Penang, 2015.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** *Flagged:* one summary reduced "tres tortugas marinas" to a single turtle. Wall unlocated.
- **'Hay canarios...y canarios', Puerto de la Cruz, Tenerife, 2015, via the Festival Periplo.** **[A: es.wikipedia]** Crossed fingers and two canaries.

## 5. Concrete at home (2016 - 2017)

- **'Parque Cromo', 3 July 2016:** Ron leading 25 other artists painting the pillars of the Acceso Oeste-Perito Moreno on the Ciudadela/Liniers boundary. **[A: El 1 Digital, 'Intervención artística: pintan los pilares de la autopista entre Ciudadela y Liniers']** One of the few precisely dated items in the file.
- **Puente Saavedra, 2017:** intervention on the bridge's **lower concrete structure**. **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Puente Saavedra']**
  - *Flagged:* one summary narrowed this to "los pilares de hormigón"; the source says "estructura inferior de concreto." *Coordinate conflict:* two lens values, -34.5445/-58.4735 and -34.554/-58.489. The bridge is at the Cabildo / General Paz junction, ~-34.5435/-58.4880, which the journey uses.

## 6. The idols at full height (2020 - 2023)

- **The Constitución Maradona mural**, Av. San Juan and Sáenz Peña: 40 m by 45 m on a twelve-storey building, called the largest existing portrait of Maradona, unveiled **October 2022** for what would have been his 62nd birthday, from a photograph of the 1990 World Cup final. **[R]**: reached only through a **Brave Search summary** of El País and TyC Sports coverage. The quoted line about choosing "el Diego combativo" is a **paraphrase carried through that summary**, so it is **not** used as a quote field, only as reported sense in the campa.
- **A Ron Maradona mural photographed in Buenos Aires by an AP photographer (Natacha Pisarenko) on 10 March 2025**, during the trial of Maradona's medical team. **[A for the photograph; R for everything about the mural]**
  - **Open gap.** It is widely said the mural dates from around Maradona's death in November 2020, but no creation date or address could be verified, and whether the photographed wall is the Constitución one is unresolved. The journey carries this as its own stop on the canonical Buenos Aires pin, gap and all.
- **The Miramar water tower, 2023:** children swimming painted over a working 35 m tower, named **Best Mural in the World** by Street Art Cities. **[R]**: coverage reached through a search summary, URLs unverified, except the Infobae quotation below, which is direct.
  - Ron's own account of the image is attested: "Inspirado en las vacaciones en la costa cuando era chico, retraté la esencia de la felicidad a través de la imagen de dos niños, Nina y Salvi deleitándose en un refrescante chapuzón." **[A: Infobae, 20 June 2023]**

## 7. The trilogy (2025 - 2026)

The best-sourced part of the file: Infobae, 8 July 2025, 22 July 2025 and 10 May 2026, directly reached, carrying verbatim quotation.

- **The Pope Francis mural, La Plata, 50 m high**, reported in progress 8 July 2025 and as finished on 22 July 2025, at which point the press counts it as completing a **trilogy of popular idols**. **[A: Infobae, both dates]**
- **The 'hola, mamá' signature**, hidden in every mural: "Inicio con un 'hola, mamá', que es un guiño que hago en cada mural"; "Empezó como un mensaje para mi vieja, pero a la gente le generó ternura y curiosidad. Hoy es una marca registrada." **[A: Infobae, 8 July 2025]**
- **His stated theory of the work**, all **[A: Infobae, 8 July 2025]**: "Mis murales se convierten en amigos de la gente." / "Los murales tienen una vida: nacen, envejecen, se desgastan. Como la vida misma." / "El arte urbano tiene eso: genera identidad, despierta emociones, queda en la memoria colectiva."
- **The Rosario Belgrano mural.** *Attribution flagged.* The quotation "Quise traerlo al barrio, que volviera al lugar como un vecino más" is verbatim in Infobae, 22 July 2025, but does **not** concern the La Plata pope mural: two independent reads place it earlier in the article, on a **Manuel Belgrano mural in Rosario facing the Monumento a la Bandera**. The journey re-anchors it there (~-32.9468/-60.6393), with neither year nor address established.
- **The Charly García mural, Corrientes y Callao**, reported 10 May 2026. **[A: Infobae]** Ron, posting after finishing: "Gracias a todos los que pasaron, miraron, tocaron bocina, frenaron un rato o compartieron una charla durante estos días de pintura. La calle y todo lo que allí sucede, también forma parte importante de la obra."

## 8. Living subject; no afterlife to document

Ron is alive at the date of this research: no death, no tomb, no funerary site. **[R, argument from silence: no death recorded in es.wikipedia or associated searches]** The functional monument is the dispersed body of walls, itself unstable. The Barracas Meeting of Styles wall was demolished in 2017, and by his own account murals "nacen, envejecen, se desgastan."

**The one legend already forming.** Ron is routinely called "uno de los diez mejores muralistas del mundo." **[R, flagged.]** The phrase sits unattributed in the lead of the Wikipedia article, footnoted to a dead Clarín URL, and **no ranking body is named anywhere**. Two lenses disagreed, one [A], one [R]. It is a press superlative, not a datum, and is not carried into the journey in any form.

## Contradictions between sources (printed, not adjudicated)

1. **Birthplace coordinates.** Three values for Caseros (-34.6055/-58.5651, -34.6031/-58.5636, -34.6167/-58.5667); only the last is sourced.
2. **Hospitales station line.** The career article implies Línea A; its own cited GCBA page and the station article say **Línea H**.
3. **Hospitales authorship.** Career article: Ron. Station article: **Ron and Leandro Frizzero**.
4. **Rock Werchter year.** 2014 vs 2015 across lenses; the cited source gives no year. Unresolved.
5. **Mead Carney: year and street.** No year anywhere; Cork Street vs Dover Street unresolved (gallery site dead, Artsy 403).
6. **Truman Brewery address.** Wikipedia's "Colbert Place" vs **Ely's Yard** in the blog Wikipedia itself cites.
7. **Villa Urquiza superlative.** Largest in the barrio (source) vs largest in the city (pool).
8. **Embellecimiento Urbano jurisdiction.** Partido de Tres de Febrero vs Ciudad de Buenos Aires.
9. **The centennial train.** "Un coche" vs "una formación completa."
10. **Penang turtles.** One vs three.
11. **Puente Saavedra coordinates.** Two lens values 1.3 km apart; neither matches the Cabildo / General Paz junction.
12. **"Ten best muralists in the world."** [A] in one lens, [R] in another. Recorded as [R].

## Stated gaps

Nothing on his training or first walls before 2010. No date or location for the Sarli and La Mona Jiménez murals, and no sign either survives. No date for the Tévez mural. No address for 'El cuento de los loros'. No located wall for 'Andromeda', 'Mudra', or the Penang turtles. No creation date or address for the Maradona mural photographed in March 2025. No year for the Mead Carney show or the Werchter tower. Whether Ron ever painted in Paris: checked, not found.

## Relations carried into the journey

Others are named in a campa only where Ron has a documented direct relation, which here means one thing: he painted their portrait. **Diego Maradona** (2011 series, 2022 Constitución mural, 2025 photograph); **Julio Cortázar** and **Luis Alberto Spinetta** (the 2013 Línea A formation); **Manuel Belgrano** (Rosario, hedged); **Charly García** (Corrientes y Callao, 2026). Not named: Segatori, Fasoli, Correch, Gardel, Xul Solar, Borges, Carbone, Puzzovio. Scene, city and tradition are not relations, and no source puts Ron on a wall or program shared with any of them. One lead, unverified: Correch's teacher Leandro Frizzera may be the Leandro Frizzero credited beside Ron at Hospitales.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** Buenos Aires **-34.6037, -58.3816** (icon series, Proyecto Dúo, AP photograph). Paris (48.8566, 2.3522) was **not** used: nothing places Ron there. Kaaba and Temple Mount show no intersection, a checked absence.
