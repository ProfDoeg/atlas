# Abu Yazid al-Bistami (Bayazid Bastami): research report

*Slug: `abu_yazid_al_bistami`. Bestam (modern Bastam), Qumes, Iran. Died 234/848 or 261/875; both dates are in the sources and this report does not choose.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction or later tradition. Contradictions are flagged and left standing.

**The controlling source.** Gerhard Böwering, "Bestami, Bayazid," *Encyclopaedia Iranica* (1989), which opens by saying that "historical evidence for his life is sparse." Almost every date attached to Bistami in popular reference works is a back-calculation from one of the two death dates. The journey treats the canon as true, as the register requires; this report records what the canon rests on.

## 1. Origins

- Grandfather **Surushan, a Zoroastrian, converts to Islam**; father Isa and uncles Adam and Ali live as ascetics at Bestam. **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji]**. No date given, stated only as family background. He is born **Abu Yazid Tayfur b. Isa b. Surushan in the Mo'bedan quarter**, the quarter of the fire-priests. **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji, *Ketab al-nur*, p. 47]**. **No birth year survives in any primary source Iranica cites.**
- The printed **c. 804 CE is [R]**, not [A]: a back-calculation from the 261/875 death date. The **TDV *İslâm Ansiklopedisi*** preserves two rival figures that contradict it, Sahlaji's (born 161/777, dead at seventy-three in 234/848) and Abd al-Rafi's (103 years from 131/748), and calls Sahlaji's the closest to the truth. **Flagged, not resolved.** The pool tagged "c. 804" as [A]; that tag is demoted here and in the journey's `date_confidence`.
- The family moves to an Arab street called **Wafedan, afterward renamed Buyadan in his honor**. **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji p. 47]**.
- Bestam is in **Qumes**, not Khorasan proper **[A: Iranica]**; secondary sources calling him Khorasani are loose.

## 2. Formation

- **Studies Hanafite law** in his youth. **[A: Iranica]**.
- Meets **Shaqiq al-Balkhi (d. 194/810)** in his youth, receives him with **Abu Torab al-Nakhshabi (d. 245/859)**, later corresponds by messenger. **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji pp. 91, 95; Sebt b. al-Jawzi p. 163; Attar I p. 147]**. **Load-bearing:** Iranica cites this as circumstantial evidence for the 848 death date, since a man born c. 804 cannot receive a shaykh who died in 810. The journey carries the strain in `date_confidence`.
- Sufi teacher **Abu Ali al-Sindi** introduces him to "the experience of divine oneness and the mystical realities in pure form" (*al-tawhid wa'l-haqa'eq sarfan*); Bistami teaches him the obligatory duties in exchange. **[A: Iranica, citing Sarraj, *Ketab al-loma'*, pp. 177, 325]**.
- **Correction.** That al-Sindi taught him *fana'* is **[R] and contradicted by the controlling source**: Iranica says in the same article that *fana'* was first advanced by **Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz**. The journey uses Iranica's actual phrase.
- TDV calls al-Sindi an **illiterate shaykh** **[A]**. English Wikipedia's "Buddhist convert from Sindh" is the contested Tholuck-Zaehner thesis, **[R]**.
- **Teacher count.** Attar reports "over one hundred" masters **[A: TDV]**. The figures 113 and 103 are **[R] and partly a conflation**: 103 belongs to Abd al-Rafi's claim about his *lifespan*. The pool presented them twice as rival teacher-totals; the journey does not.
- Nishapur and Merv as study-towns are **[R: Attar]**, no named teacher, no date. Merv is not pinned: nothing but the general claim of wide study supports it.

## 3. The pilgrimage

- **At least one hajj to Mecca** **[A: Iranica]**; once or several times, sources disagree **[R]**. **The load lifted from his camel** en route is one of his *karamat* **[R: Iranica, citing Sahlaji pp. 89-90; Attar I p. 137]**, and the saying that he circled the House but did not see the Lord of the House is **[R: Attar; Hujwiri]**, framed variously by the two.
- **Kufa** as a waystation is **[R]**: the Darb Zubayda route is solid geography, nothing is recorded of him there. Pinned at the corpus coordinate for Attar's Kufa, with the silence stated in the campa.
- **Fayd**, the beggar who says true worship is not circling a house of stone: **[R: Attar]**. **Coordinate correction:** the Darb Zubayda station of Fayd is in Ha'il Province at about **27.117 N, 42.533 E**; the pool's 27.093 / 42.423 is 11 km southwest. Warning: English Wikipedia's "Fayd" describes a different village in Makkah Province at 21°55′02″N 39°23′18″E.
- **Medina** after Mecca is **[R]**, implied by ninth-century pilgrim practice, not isolated by any source.
- **The mother's threshold**, turning back from the road for her sake: **[R: Attar; retold by Rumi]**. Rumi is a literary echo, not independent attestation.

## 4. The recluse and the ecstatic sayings

- Lives as a **recluse between house, mosque, and an isolated cell (*sawma'a*)**, exiled for short periods when the ulama turn hostile, still holding sessions and receiving visitors. **[A: Iranica]**.
- **Practice:** closes every opening so no noise reaches him; stands on tiptoe; rests his head on his knees, lifting it only to sigh; a visitor once fails to notice he is praying. He **ejects a man from the mosque for praying without ablution** and **rinses mouth and tongue before uttering God's name**. **[A: Iranica, citing Attar I, pp. 140, 143, 154, 157; Solami, *Tabaqat*, pp. 62-63; Sahlaji p. 106; Esfahani X p. 35]**.
- **The shathiyat.** Credited as the first to use ecstatic utterance consistently as an expression of Sufi experience. **[A: Iranica]**. Includes:
  - *Sobhani! Ma a'zama sha'ni*, "Glory be to Me! How great is My Majesty!"
  - "I am I; there is no God but I; so worship me!"; "All humans are my servants except you."
  - The Ka'ba circles around him; the qibla turns from God toward himself.
  - Answering "God is great!" with "I am greater!", and reworking Qur'an 85:12 into "By my life, my grasp is firmer than His."
  - Containing the seven *abdal*; being the preserved tablet, the limitless ocean, the throne.
  - The wish for hell, enlarged so no other soul fits beside him; hell extinguished by his glance or smothered by his coat-hem.
  All six **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji pp. 80, 88, 108, 111, 113, 114-15, 119, 122; Ruzbehan Baqli p. 101; Esfahani X p. 37; Ebn al-Jawzi pp. 329-34; Attar I pp. 161, 171]**.
- **He downplays miracles**: wood floats, fish swim, birds fly, demons cross distances fast, so none of it proves sanctity; he once pays a ferryman rather than take the miraculous shortcut. **[A: Iranica, citing Sarraj p. 324; Qushayri p. 164; Attar I pp. 169-70]**. The *karamat* themselves are **[R]**, literary tradition in Iranica's framing.

## 5. The jurists, the exile, the ascension

- Sayings brought before the jurists as accusations of unbelief: **[R: Attar; Hujwiri]**. Repeated exile itself is **[A: Iranica]**.
- **The one named exile destination is Jurjan (Gorgan).** Accused by a hadith scholar of claiming a mi'raj like the Prophet's, he performed the hajj, went to Jurjan, and returned only after the accuser died. **[A: TDV, following Sahlaji]**. **Correction:** the pool placed these years in unnamed villages around Bestam at 36.42 / 54.95, conceding the point had no landmark under it. That pin is dropped for Gorgan.
- **"Seven times" expelled** appears in neither Iranica nor TDV. **[R], unsourced even as tradition.** Not stated as a count in the journey.
- **The mi'raj**, a first-person ascent through seven heavens: **[R]** as event, **[A]** as text. Transmitted by **Sarraj (d. 378/988)** in the *Kitab al-Luma'*; the embellished *Resalat al-qasd ela'llah*, wrongly ascribed to Junayd, is dated **395/1005**, compiled by a certain Abu'l-Qasem. **[A: Iranica, citing Nicholson, *Islamica* 2 (1926), pp. 402-15]**. The pool's "post-10th century accretion" is too late by its own evidence.

## 6. Correspondence and visitors

- **Ahmad b. Khezruya of Balkh (d. 240/854)** visits while on pilgrimage, and an **emissary of Dhu'l-Nun al-Misri (d. 245/860)** comes and receives a reply. **[A: Iranica, citing Qushayri p. 38; Hojviri pp. 322, 331-32; Sahlaji pp. 65, 73, 117, 131; Attar I p. 156]**.
- Advises **Yahya b. Mo'adh al-Razi (d. 258/872)** by letter, including the exchange on the oceans of love. **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji p. 136; Esfahani X p. 40; Attar I pp. 143-44]**. **Geographic correction:** Yahya is a native of Rayy but left it and died at Nishapur, so Rayy is his origin, not his base during the correspondence **[A: Wikipedia]**. The journey pins Nishapur.
- Sends **Abu Musa al-Dabili** into his native **Armenia**. **[A: Iranica, citing Sam'ani V p. 313; Sahlaji pp. 54-55]**. **Gap:** no town is named. The journey uses Dvin as a declared representative marker, said so in `date_confidence` and in the campa.
- **He never meets Junayd of Baghdad (d. 298/910)**, who becomes the principal interpreter of his sayings, though he is on familiar terms with Junayd's uncle and teacher **Sari al-Saqati (d. 251/865)**. **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji p. 81]**. The school that canonized him had no contact with him.

## 7. Death: the two dates

1. **234 AH / 848 CE**, recorded by Sahlaji (p. 63) and Solami (p. 60) on the authority of Hosayn b. Yahya Shafe'i via Ja'far Kholdi (d. 348/959). Iranica notes "considerable circumstantial evidence" behind it, chiefly the meetings with Shaqiq al-Balkhi (d. 810) and Ahmad b. Khezruya (d. 854).
2. **261 AH / 875 CE**, recorded by Solami elsewhere and by most later sources on a family *esnad* modern scholars treat as more reliable; Iranica calls it "commonly accepted by scholars."

**[A: Iranica, summarizing Sahlaji and Solami, *Tabaqat*]**. The journey pins 875 for the globe and states 848 in `date_confidence`, which is where the hedge belongs. Burial at Bestam is undisputed; the grave draws pilgrims within a generation or two, and by the 4th-5th / 10th-11th centuries a small group of followers keeps itself together around it. **[A: Iranica]**.

## 8. Transmission

- **He writes nothing.** Roughly five hundred sayings survive through two chains: a family line beginning with his nephew and attendant **Abu Musa Isa b. Adam**, and a visitors' line through **Abu Eshaq Ebrahim Heravi** ("Satanba") and **Ahmad b. Khezruya**; **Junayd translated the bulk of them from Persian into Arabic** on the family line's authority. **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji pp. 56, 108-109, 122-23; Esfahani X pp. 43-44]**.
- **Sahlaji (389-476 / 998-1084)** compiles *Ketab al-nur men kalemat Abi Tayfur*, drawing on Dastani (d. 417/1026) and Ibn Babuya Shirazi, biographer of Hallaj (d. 442/1050). The most detailed source there is. **[A: Iranica]**.
- **Hojviri** classifies the followers as the **Tayfuriya** of intoxication (*sokr*) against the **Junaydiya** of sobriety (*sahw*). **[A: Iranica, citing Hojviri pp. 228-35]**.
- **Kharaqani (d. 425/1033)**, who never met him, becomes his principal heir in Iran and a frequent pilgrim to the tomb; through Abu Ali al-Farmadi (d. 477/1084) he links Bistami into the silsila later claimed by the Naqshbandiyya. **[A: Iranica, citing Attar II pp. 201-55; Minovi pp. 11-36]**; the Naqshbandi claim itself is **[R]**. **Correction:** the pool called him "a generation after" Bistami. He was born 352/963, Bistami died 261/875: eighty-eight years to Kharaqani's *birth*, which is why the transmission has to be *uwaysi*.
- The honorific **soltan al-'arefin** is first attested in Shawarabadi, repeated by Attar, popularized by **Aflaki in the 8th/14th century**. **[A: Iranica, citing Sahlaji p. 147; Attar I p. 134; Aflaki I p. 259]**. **Date correction:** the pool dated this to 1300; Aflaki composed the *Manaqeb al-'arefin* between 1318 and 1353.

## 9. Afterlife in the corpus and beyond

- **Attar** turns aside to Bastam, kneels at the tomb, gathers sayings for the *Tazkirat al-Awliya*, in which Bistami gets one of the two longest lives; at Shadiyakh the notebooks hold him beside Hallaj and Rabia. **[A: `attar.journey.json`, stops dated 1176 and 1186]**.
- **Ruzbihan Baqli** (1128-1209, back in Shiraz 1165) completes the *Sharh-i Shathiyat* in 1174 **[A]**. **Correction:** it is a commentary on the shathiyat genre across many Sufis, with **Hallaj** as its primary example, not a defense of Bistami alone. The pool overstated his share.
- **Rumi**: at the Inn of the Sugar Merchants in Konya, Shams sets Bistami's "glory be to me, how great is my station" against the Prophet's "we have not known You as You should be known." **[A: `rumi.journey.json`, dated 1244-11-29, from Aflaki and Sultan Walad]**. The corpus's only verbatim citation of Bistami before this journey.
- **Ibn Arabi** discusses the mi'raj in *al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya*: **[R]**, standard in the scholarship, not confirmable in reachable sources. The book's geography is: begun at Mecca 1202, second recension 1238 at Damascus, where he died 16 November 1240 **[A: Claude Addas, via Wikipedia]**. The pool presented the *Futuhat* as a Damascus work, which is half right.
- **Oljeitu builds a dome over the tomb in 713/1313** **[A: Iranica]**; the complex accretes Seljuk through Ilkhanid to Qajar **[A]**. **Correction:** the **Kashaneh is not a minaret** but a 24 m thirty-sided tower southeast of the Jami mosque, inscribed 700 AH.

## 10. The tombs he never reached

- **Chittagong.** Shrine and mosque at **Nasirabad**: sarcophagus uncovered 1831, three-domed mosque under Aurangzeb, traditional date c. 850, a pond of black softshell turtles (*Nilssonia nigricans*, extinct in the wild since 2002) held to be evil spirits that incurred the saint's wrath. **[R: Wikipedia, "Shrine of Bayazid Bostami"]**, which states there is no significant historical evidence he visited Bengal and that the site may be a *jawab*, an answering tomb. **Attribution correction:** that scepticism is Wikipedia's, not Iranica's, which reports only that "a popular form of Indian Sufism lives on at Bayazid's memorial in Nasirabad." **Coordinates: 22.38904 N, 91.80919 E**; the pool gave three different points for one shrine, all reconciled in the journey.
- **Kirikhan, Hatay.** The **Beyazıd-ı Bestamî Külliyesi** at Alabeyli village, inside Darb-ı Sak castle on the Kırıkhan-Hassa road, venerated locally as his tomb. **[R: English and Turkish Wikipedia]**. **Coordinates: 36.4975, 36.3608**; the pool's afterlife lens was 2.3 km off, its geography lens right. Bastam to Kırıkhan is about **1,670 km**, not the pool's "roughly 2,000." A nearby tomb of **Şeyh İsa**, said to be his descendant, plausibly explains how the name migrated.

## 11. Modern scholarship

- **The Indian-influence quarrel, 1821 onward.** Tholuck proposes a Hindu or Buddhist channel via al-Sindi (1821); Nicholson (1916) and Horten (1927) reaffirm; Ritter (1954) cautiously accepts; Massignon (1922) and Arberry (1957) object, Arberry arguing the imagery is Qur'anic and Sufi rather than Upanishadic; Zaehner builds the case in 1957 and 1960, judged unconvincing since. **[A: Iranica]**. **Unresolved, and left unresolved.**
- **Massignon** (Paris, 1922) **[A]**; **Ernst**, *Words of Ecstasy in Sufism* (SUNY, 1985) **[A]**; **Meddeb**, *Les dits de Bistami* (Paris, 1989) **[A]**; **Abdur Rabb** (Dacca, 1971) **[A]**, cited by Iranica for the Chittagong shrine.
- **Nicholson: correction.** The pool cited *Studies in Islamic Mysticism* (1921), whose three studies treat Abu Sa'id ibn Abi'l-Khayr, Ibn al-Farid, and al-Jili, not Bistami. His Bistami work is the *Islamica* 2 (1926) mi'raj article and his 1916 intervention in the influence debate.

## 12. Quotations traced or corrected

Each pool quotation was chased to a named edition and page, or dropped.

- **"How great is My presence!"** Wrong: *subhani, ma a'zama sha'ni*. Journey prints Schimmel's **"My Majesty"** (p. 146); Arberry p. 141 "my dignity," Zaehner p. 98 "my glory," who notes Sarraj records only *subhani*.
- **"The sea of good works for 40 years... a zunnar."** **Untraceable as worded**, after checking Attar/Arberry, Hujwiri/Nicholson, Sulami and Sarraj in Sells, Schimmel, Zaehner. Replaced by the Arberry passage with the same zunnar image (p. 139).
- **"I shed my nafs as a snake sheds its skin."** Substance confirmed, wording loose; the attached question is attested nowhere and is dropped. Printed in Zaehner's wording, p. 98.
- **"One who drinks a single drop from the ocean of love."** **Wrong speaker**: Yahya b. Mu'adh's words *to* Bistami (Hujwiri/Nicholson p. 187), so given in the campa as his question. Bistami's reply corrected from "cry out from thirst" to **"cry for more to slake his thirst."**
- **"Drunk the oceans of heaven and earth."** Real, Abu Nu'aym attribution correct, wording paraphrased, **and the same exchange in another recension**. Not printed.
- **"I am four years old."** Genuine but **not Attar**: Hujwiri/Nicholson p. 331, chapter on contemplation, whose wording the journey prints. Skip-risk cleared.
- **"Never found by seeking, yet only seekers find it."** Nicholson, *Translations of Eastern Poetry and Prose* (1922), p. 140.
- **"Bayazid and his impious sayings when beside himself"** is a **story title**: Rumi, *Masnavi* IV: 2102-2153. Not used.

## 13. Contradictions and gaps

**Contradictions, flagged and not resolved.** (1) Death date: 234/848 against 261/875, Iranica adjudicating neither. (2) Birth: 161/777 (Sahlaji), 131/748 (Abd al-Rafi), c. 804 (back-calculation), mutually exclusive. (3) Who originated *fana'*: popular works say Bistami, Böwering says Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz. (4) Indian influence via al-Sindi, two centuries old and still open. (5) One hajj or several. (6) Three tombs, one man: Bestam, Nasirabad, Alabeyli, unreconciled in any source. (7) The Kashaneh: Godard gives it to Ghazan, the inscription names Oljeitu.

**Gaps, stated as gaps.** No year of birth, of the conversion, of the move to Wafedan, or of any single saying: the journey's ninth-century dates are ordering devices and each says so in `date_confidence`. No town in Armenia for the Dabili mission. No named teacher at Nishapur or Merv. No text by his own hand, and both transmission chains begin after his death. Ibn Arabi's discussion of the mi'raj in the *Futuhat* was not confirmable. The number and duration of the expulsions is unrecorded; only Jurjan is named.
