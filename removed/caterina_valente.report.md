# Caterina Valente (1931-2024): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-12 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `caterina_valente`. Prepared from a verified research pool in which an adversarial pass had overturned a number of first-pass claims; those overturns are carried here as the corrected reading, with the discarded version named so the correction can be audited.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, placeholder, or uncorroborated single-source claim. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved; absences are recorded as absences.

**Standing caveat.** This life is documented almost entirely by two Wikipedia editions that do not agree and do not cite the same things. DE is richer on the German career, the television series, the honours and the residences; EN is richer on the American decade and carries at least two outright errors. No printed biography, discography, or archival finding aid was reachable.

---

## 1. Paris and the family trade (1931-1947)

Born in Paris, 14 January 1931 **[A: both editions]**. Father Giuseppe Valente, accordionist (DE: 1891-1957); mother Maria, called Siri, musical clown (DE: 1897-1977) **[A: DE]**; family in show business for generations. No arrondissement, street, or clinic in either edition: **gap**, and the journey pin is a city coordinate. **Paternal origin**: San Biagio Saracinisco, Frosinone **[A: EN]**, corroborated by *Il Messaggero*, 11 September 2024 **[A]**. The first-pass coordinate (41.6667, 13.8333) was **wrong by about 9.5 km**; DE gives 41.6133, 13.9272.

She decides at about five, on hearing Billie Holiday, to become a singer **[R: EN, uncorroborated]**. 1936: debut with her siblings, reportedly in a circus **[A: DE]**; the family sextet, Caterina on guitar, plays the Friedrichsbau in Stuttgart **[A: EN and DE agree on venue, city and year]**.

*Coordinate warning.* The coordinate DE attaches to the Friedrichsbau (48.7806, 9.1762) is the **current** Friedrichsbau-Varieté on the Pragsattel, opened 4 December 2014. The 1936 house was the original of 1898/1900 near the Oberer Schlossgarten; it burned out in the raid of 25-26 July 1944 and was demolished in 1955 for street widening, so it was **not** rebuilt on its site and the first pass's "and rebuilt" is struck. The journey uses 48.7761, 9.1755, about 500 m south, and flags it.

**The war. Not reconciled.** Both editions have the family touring occupied Europe for German troops. EN then has the thirteen-year-old surviving the bombing of Breslau and helping with rescue work; DE has the family imprisoned there and deported to the Soviet Union **[both R, mutually unconfirmed]**. No camp, city, date, or route home is named. The journey carries a Moscow placeholder and says so.

**Return.** By 1947 the family is in Paris and the teenager sings in nightclubs **[A: DE]**, with chansons written for her by the then-unknown Gilbert Bécaud **[A: both; the first pass wrongly called this EN-only]**. The same DE sentence supplies a Scandinavian tour the first pass omitted, which explains how she reached Copenhagen. No club is named: **gap**.

## 2. Copenhagen, Zurich, Baden-Baden (1948-1954)

**1948, Copenhagen**: first vocal recordings with a quartet **[A: DE only; EN omits the session]**. No label, studio, or titles: **gap**.

**1952, Zurich**: Walo Linder, head of entertainment at Radio Zurich, had heard her sing at the Circus Grock and brings her to the station, where her first record is made and distributed onward **[A: DE]**. The first pass had the clown Grock personally encouraging her; DE says only that Linder "sie im Zirkus Grock hatte singen hören". The circus is the venue, not the mentor.

**Marriage to Gerd Scholz**, juggler as Erik van Aro, later her manager. **EN gives 1951, DE 1952 [contradiction, unresolved]**. The first pass overstated this: DE dates the Budd marriage to "Ein Jahr nach der Scheidung von Scholz", putting that divorce in 1971 and agreeing with EN's 1951-1971 span. **Only the start year is disputed.** No location given by either.

**1953, the audition. The largest correction in the file.** EN places the Kurt Edelhagen audition at Süddeutscher Rundfunk, Stuttgart. That is wrong. DE's Valente article places it "beim Südwestfunk Baden-Baden", and DE's Edelhagen article settles it: he directed the SWF Baden-Baden orchestra from 1 January 1952 to 31 March 1957 (WDR Cologne from 1 April 1957), and "entdeckte Edelhagen 1953 in Baden-Baden Caterina Valente". **He was never at SDR Stuttgart.** The first-pass coordinate was about 68 km off; the journey uses Baden-Baden, approximately 48.7606, 8.2400.

**What Edelhagen said.** EN renders it as indirect speech, "the most musical woman he had ever heard", and the first pass put that inside quotation marks, misattributing a paraphrase to him. The cited SWR4 page (11 September 2024), fetched directly, gives his words as first-person German: **"Das ist die musikalischste Frau, die ich je gehört habe!"** That is what the journey quotes. The page carries no "Hans-Jürgen Finger" byline, which comes only from EN's citation, so the credit is dropped.

**First single**: "Istanbul", recorded 29 March 1954 for Brunswick, organised by Edelhagen with Heinz Gietz and Kurt Feltz; a failure **[A: date and personnel EN, label DE]**. **No source names a recording city.** The first pass asserted Berlin as attested; downgraded to **[R]**, and since Edelhagen worked from Baden-Baden while Feltz and Gietz worked from Cologne, Berlin is the weakest guess available. The journey pins the session at the canonical Cologne coordinate and flags it. Feltz then steers her to Schlager and "O Mama, O Mama, O Mamajo" is an immediate hit **[EN]**.

## 3. Breakthrough (1954-1957)

**"Ganz Paris träumt von der Liebe"**, after Cole Porter's "I Love Paris". **Release year is 1954, not 1955**: DE lists "(Polydor, 1954)". The first pass carried 1955 and a figure of 500,000 copies in the first year; **that figure appears in neither edition and is dropped as unsourced**. The attested figure is over 900,000, from *Der Spiegel* 3/1959, which calls it the Polydor sales record and states that no Valente title had passed a million:

> Die Valente zählte unbestritten zu den Spitzenstars des Polydor-Repertoires, obgleich bislang kein Valente-Titel die legendäre Auflage von einer Million überschreiten konnte – den Verkaufsrekord hält die Nummer Ganz Paris träumt von der Liebe mit einer Auflage von über 900.000 Platten.

**[A: reproduced in DE Wikipedia's `quote=` parameter, confirmed against the original.]**

**"Malagueña"** with the Werner Müller orchestra is **1954, not 1955** (DE). **"The Breeze and I"** is the 1955 Polydor release, fourteen weeks on the US charts, past a million by 1978. The first pass conflated the two. Recording city unattested for both; Berlin is a reconstruction from Müller's base **[R]**.

**1955, American television debut**, *The Colgate Comedy Hour* with Gordon MacRae **[A: EN]**. **Venue is [R], not [A]**: the series ran units in New York, Chicago and Los Angeles, and nothing ties Valente to Sunset and Vine. The first-pass coordinate was also 0.4 km north of that intersection; corrected to 34.0980, -118.3267 and flagged as a placeholder.

**Jazz.** Major success at the 2nd German Jazz Festival, Frankfurt, 1955 **[A: DE]**; hall not named. In 1956 she records with Chet Baker **[A: DE]**: **two singles, "I'll Remember April" and "Ev'ry Time We Say Goodbye", not an album**, and the LPs the first pass bundled in are separately dated, *The Hi-Fi Nightingale* 1956 and *Plenty Valente!* 1957. **No city for the Baker session**; a declared Munich placeholder.

**1957.** *Bonsoir, Kathrin!* begins, the first German personality show, with Peter Alexander, to 1964 **[A: both; they disagree on the episode count, one giving ten, left standing]**. No production city: a declared Frankfurt placeholder for the ARD. A villa is built at Oberflockenbach in the Odenwald, occupied with Scholz 1957-1962, later passing via Curt Engelhorn to the University of Heidelberg **[A: DE]**. First-pass coordinate 2.2 km off; DE gives 49.5111, 8.7303.

## 4. The American decade (1958-1966)

- **1958**: films *Hier bin ich, hier bleib ich*, released 1959, with Bill Haley and His Comets and a duet on a re-recorded "Vive la Rock and Roll" **[A: EN for filming and duet, DE for the release]**. A son is born from the Scholz marriage **[A: both; no place]**.
- **1958-1959, Teldec**: *Der Spiegel* 3/1959 reports her secretary Günter Arendt announcing, the same day as the Polydor split, that she had already signed with Teldec. **The verbatim text was corrected against the original**: the string circulating through Wikipedia carries an editorial insertion, "(29. Dezember 1958)", which **does not appear in the article**, and substitutes single quotes for the magazine's guillemets. The December date is a gloss and belongs in prose, not in quotation marks.
- **1959, Grammy**: nominated for "La strada dell'amore". **Wrong ceremony in the first pass**: the **second** Grammy Awards, 29 November 1959, not the first (4 May 1959, honouring 1958 recordings, which did not include her). The category reads Fitzgerald (winner), Horne, Valente, Suzuki, Lee **[A]**. Attendance and ceremony city are **[R]**.
- **1961-1966**: eight appearances on the Perry Como show **[A: EN]**; Rai series *Bonsoir Catherine* (6 episodes, 1961) and *Nata per la musica* (9 episodes, 1962) **[A: DE]**. IT Wikipedia gives the Via Teulada 66 centre at 41.9201, 12.4551, 520 m east of the first-pass coordinate; the building is **[R]**.
- **Three appearances on *The Ed Sullivan Show*** **[A: EN]** at 1697 Broadway. **The name is an anachronism for her run**: the building was CBS Studio 50 until 10 December 1967. **No broadcast dates for any of the three**, so the journey's date is a declared placeholder.
- **1964-1965, *The Entertainers***, CBS. **Episode count corrected from 22 to 26**, 25 September 1964 to 27 March 1965; Newhart and several regulars left in January 1965 on the move from Friday 8:30 to Saturday 9:00. **Produced in New York at CBS Studio 50, not CBS Television City**: the first pass had the wrong coast, and the fix collapses this stop onto the Ed Sullivan address.
- **1964**: first Las Vegas performance and her own Broadway show **[A: EN]**. **No hotel, showroom, theatre, run, or review is named anywhere reached: a substantial gap.** Same year, *The Caterina Valente Singers*, with Jimmy Page said to have played on it: **downgraded to [R]**, DE's only citation being the "Trivia" page of her site.
- **1965**: FAME award as best female vocalist on American television, from Sammy Davis Jr **[A: both]**. **1966-1968**: *Die Caterina Valente-Show*, 8 episodes, ZDF and AVRO **[A: DE]**, **not a single 1966 season**. Goldene Kamera 1966 **[A]**.

## 5. The circuit (1968-1979)

- **1968**: Order of Merit of the Federal Republic, reportedly the first show-business star to receive it **[A for the award, R for the "first", unsourced in both editions]**. No presentation site named; a Bonn placeholder.
- **1970**: Royal Variety Performance at the London Palladium, "Before the Parade Passes By" and "The Breeze and I", per *Radio Times*, 15 November 1970 **[A]**. First-pass coordinate 0.4 km off; corrected to 51.5146, -0.1405. **Undated recurrences**: the Talk of the Town in the old Hippodrome, and **seven appearances at the Olympia in Paris** **[A: DE; neither dated, a gap]**.
- **1972**: marries Roy Budd. **No location named**; the first pass gave London an **[A]** tag from Budd's career base, downgraded to **[R]**. Budd's own EN article confirms only year and duration and **does not mention the 1974 son**, which is DE-only. Divorce 1979 **[A: DE]**.
- **International touring**: DE lists the USSR, Japan, Australia, South Africa and South America **without one city, venue, or year**; all are declared placeholders in the journey. A performer whose whole claim is internationalism has almost no attested itinerary outside Europe and America.
- **1978**: "Manuel" is a hit, with ZDF-Hitparade appearances **[A: DE]**.

## 6. Jubilee (1981-1995)

- **1981**: begins hosting *Wünsche, die ich mir erfülle* **[A: EN only]**; no broadcaster, no run, and DE does not list it at all.
- **1982-1983**: *Caterina*, 8 episodes, ORF **[A: DE]**. First-pass coordinate 4.5 km off, in the wrong district; DE gives the ORF-Zentrum Küniglberg at 48.1777, 16.2906.
- **1984**: "Männer brauchen Liebe". **Not a national chart position.** DE's awards list is explicit where its body text is not: "Platz 2 ... in der ZDF-Hitparade (von Zuschauern gewählt, Verleihung einer 'Goldenen 2' als Trophäe)", cited to the broadcast of 26 May 1984. **Second in a viewer vote, with a trophy. Not number two in Germany.**
- **1986, the fiftieth stage year**: ARD broadcasts *Bravo, Catrin!*, nearly 17 million viewers **[A]**; the Großes Bundesverdienstkreuz is presented by Lothar Späth at Sindelfingen **[A: DE]**; the Guinness entry is made. **The unit is recordings, not albums**: "mehr als 1350 Aufnahmen". DE's citation is a photograph of the certificate on caterinavalente.com, artist-supplied rather than a Guinness listing, so caution stands even on the corrected figure.
- **1987**: *Ich bin...* **[A: DE]**. **1989**: *A briglia sciolta*, reissued as *Fantastica* and *Platinum Deluxe*, her best-selling CD worldwide **[A: both]**. **1990**: jazz concerts with the WDR Big Band and the Cologne Philharmonic, later issued as *Kurt Weill: American Songs* **[A: DE]**. Bambi 1990, Goldene Europa 1991, Ehren-Prix Walo 1993, Bambi lifetime 1995, Platinum Romy 1998 **[A]**.

## 7. Withdrawal, death, afterlife (2000-2024)

- **2000**: *Girltalk* with harpist Catherine Michel, final studio album **[A]**. **2002**: Echo lifetime achievement **[A]**. **2003**: retires from performing **[A for the year, EN]**. **The place attribution is dropped**: no edition attaches Bissone or anywhere else to the retirement; the first pass invented it. DE also undercuts a hard 2003 line, recording interviews in 2009 and 2019 and an appearance in December 2005.
- **1 December 2005, Munich, 57th Bambi gala**: Ehren-Bambi for lifetime achievement. **Stronger than the first pass allowed**: DE calls this her last appearance before a mass audience, not one of the last.
- **Later years**: "sehr zurückgezogen", contact with the public chiefly through a Facebook page of memories and photographs **[A: DE]**. Long residence at Bissone, death at her house in Lugano. **Not the contradiction the first pass called it**: DE asserts both in the same article, making it a sequence. The Bissone coordinate was about 2 km off, corrected to 45.9500, 8.9660. An unused DE detail: she sold a California second home, saying it was out of antipathy to the Trump administration.
- **2019**: "Bongo Cha Cha Cha" (1959) on the *Spider-Man: Far from Home* soundtrack **[A: EN]**. **2021**: the same track goes viral on TikTok, over 250 million views, beginning in Latin American accounts before spreading to Italy and Germany **[A: EN]**. **9 September 2024**: dies at home in Lugano, 93 **[A: both]**.
- **Afterlife, and it is thin.** **No burial site is documented anywhere reached.** A handwritten testament, opened by the Magistrate of Lugano, is reported to specify her funeral wishes **[R: secondary press summary]**. Focus ran a headline on an "Eklat nach Beerdigung von Caterina Valente", her former manager alleging he learned of her death from the media **[R: headline only]**. **Targeted searching turned up no street, square, walk-of-fame star, museum exhibition, or Ehrengrab.** Her monumental afterlife is close to nonexistent, in flat contrast to her algorithmic one.

## Contradictions flagged and left standing

1. **The war years.** EN: bombing of Breslau and rescue work. DE: imprisonment there and Soviet deportation. Neither corroborates the other.
2. **Marriage to Scholz: 1951 (EN) vs 1952 (DE).** The 1971 end is agreed; only the start year differs.
3. **Episode count of *Bonsoir, Kathrin!***, one edition giving ten across 1957-1964.
4. **Sales window for "Ganz Paris"**: over 900,000 by December 1958 (EN) or within four years (DE).
5. **Language count.** "Spoke six, sang in thirteen", or "sang in eleven", or the journalistic "10+". **No number is consistently attested and no record certifies a language-count superlative.**

**Errors in English Wikipedia found here**: Süddeutscher Rundfunk for the 1953 audition (it was Südwestfunk, Baden-Baden); 22 episodes of *The Entertainers* (the dedicated article gives 26); Edelhagen's remark rendered as indirect speech in a form that reads as a quotation.

## Gaps stated as gaps

No clinic, arrondissement, or street for the 1931 birth. No camp or city for the Soviet internment, and no route home. No Paris nightclub for 1947-1948. No label, studio, or titles for the 1948 Copenhagen session. No recording city for "Istanbul", "O Mama", "Ganz Paris", "Malagueña", "The Breeze and I", or the Baker singles. No production city for *Bonsoir, Kathrin!* No hotel for Las Vegas 1964; no theatre or notice for the Broadway show. No broadcast dates for the Ed Sullivan appearances; no years for the seven Olympia engagements or the Talk of the Town residencies. No location for either marriage. No city, venue, or year for the USSR, Japanese, Australian, South African, or South American tours. No grave, no monument, no toponym.

## Atlas interlock

A grep across all 412 journey files in `working/journeys` returns **no attested crossing of Valente's career by any existing atlas traveler**. No file mentions Sanremo, Eurovision, Schlager, Ed Sullivan, Sacha Distel, Vico Torriani, Peter Alexander, or Freddy Quinn. She existed in the atlas only as a zero-document row in `QUEUE.md`. Three pins are inherited byte-identically; the two crossings named in campa are geographic, with no claim of contact:

- **Paris, 48.8566, 2.3522**, from `coco_chanel.journey.json`, where it carries the house at 31 rue Cambon. Valente is born on this pin and returns at sixteen.
- **Cologne, 50.938, 6.957**, from `salomon_oppenheim.journey.json`. Cologne is Edelhagen's base from 1 April 1957, the working city of Feltz and Gietz, and the seat of the WDR Big Band of 1990.
- **Buenos Aires, -34.6037, -58.3816**, the canonical South American pin, a labelled placeholder for the unspecified South American touring DE lists.

The Kaaba and Temple Mount pins were checked per instruction and have **no connection to this life**; not inherited.
