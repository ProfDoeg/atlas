# St. Anthony of Padua (Fernando Martins de Bulhões), 1195-1231
*Research report. Slug: `st_anthony_of_padua`. Compiled 2026-08-16 from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock and afterlife lenses), with the pool's own correction notes applied.*

Legend: **[A source]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = tradition or legend, no contemporary attestation. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and **not** adjudicated.

A general caution governs everything below. The whole life is transmitted through hagiography written for a cult already in motion within a year of the man's death. There is no archive of charters here. Almost every date before 1227 rests on a single narrative line repeated by later compilers, and where two encyclopedias disagree, neither is standing on a document.

---

## 1. Lisbon and Coimbra, 1195-1220

Born in Lisbon, in a house near the cathedral, to a family of the city's minor nobility **[A: Wikipedia, "Anthony of Padua"; New Advent Catholic Encyclopedia]**. The parents are given as Vicente Martins and Teresa Pais Taveira, but the historian Niccolò Dal-Gal, cited in the same article, calls the parentage uncertain. The **year** 1195 and the **city** are the firm part; the day, 15 August, is traditional and attested by no contemporary document **[R for the day]**. The Church of Santo António stands on the traditional site of the house **[A]**.

At about fifteen he enters the Canons Regular of the Holy Cross at São Vicente de Fora, outside Lisbon, c. 1210 **[A: Wikipedia; New Advent]**. Around 1212 he transfers to the Augustinian motherhouse of Santa Cruz at Coimbra, reportedly to escape visits from friends and family **[A]**, and spends roughly eight years there in study **[A: New Advent]**.

In 1220 the bodies of five Franciscans martyred in Morocco are brought back to Coimbra; he resolves to leave the Augustinians **[A: Wikipedia; New Advent]**, and receives the Franciscan habit and the name Anthony at the hermitage chapel of Saint Anthony the Great at Olivais **[A: Wikipedia]**. His baptismal name had been Fernando.

**Coordinate correction applied.** The pool's Olivais pin (40.2201, -8.4297) was a copy of the Santa Cruz longitude and landed 2.2 km west, in Coimbra's Baixa. The place is **Santo António dos Olivais** (not "Santo Antão"), OSM 40.2181, -8.4038.

## 2. The failed mission and the shipwreck, 1220-1221

He sails for Morocco intending to preach to Muslims and to be martyred as the five were **[A: Wikipedia; New Advent]**.

**Date contradiction, flagged.** English Wikipedia dates the departure to 1221; Italian Wikipedia ("Antonio di Padova") dates it to **autumn 1220**, with the wreck on the Sicilian coast in late 1220 and arrival at Messina in spring 1221. The Italian sequence is coherent with the 30 May 1221 chapter and is followed in the journey file; the English date stands here as the competing reading.

He falls seriously ill in Morocco, sources varying on the cause, and boards a ship home **[A: Wikipedia; New Advent]**. Storm drives it to Sicily instead **[A: Wikipedia; New Advent; Italian Wikipedia gives the coast between Tusa and Caronia]**. He recovers among the Franciscans at Messina **[A: Italian Wikipedia]**. **The Moroccan port of landing is named by no reachable source**: Marrakesh appears only as the intended destination of the Almohad court, not as a confirmed stop.

On 30 May 1221 he is present at the general chapter at Assisi, the "Chapter of Mats," where Francis of Assisi presides. He is unnoticed and holds no post **[A: New Advent]**. He is then assigned to the rural hermitage of Montepaolo di Dovadola, near Forlì **[A: Wikipedia; New Advent]**.

**Coordinate corrections applied.** Porziuncola: the pool gave 43.0709, 12.5988, about 2 km NE of the site, up the hillside toward the town. The canonical pin inherited byte-identical from `saint_francis.journey.json`, **43.0578, 12.5804**, is used instead. Montepaolo: the pool gave 44.1103, 11.7864, about 9.5 km SW in open countryside; OSM places the Eremo di Montepaolo at **44.1546, 11.8806**.

## 3. Romagna: the voice discovered, 1222-1224

At an ordination at Forlì, with no one else prepared to preach, he is called on and delivers an unplanned sermon that reveals his learning **[A: Wikipedia; New Advent]**. He is sent to preach through Romagna **[A: Wikipedia; the minister is named Brother Gratian only in some accounts]**.

Francis of Assisi entrusts him with teaching theology to the friars, in a short letter that survives **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), via Wikisource]**:

> To Brother Anthony, my bishop, Brother Francis sends his greetings. It is my pleasure that thou teach theology to the brethren, provided, however, that as the Rule prescribes, the spirit of prayer and devotion may not be extinguished. Farewell.

Italian Wikipedia dates the first Franciscan theological studium, at the convent of Santa Maria della Pugliola in Bologna, to **1223** **[A]**; the pool's English-derived entry gave 1223-1224 and the earlier date is used. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's Bologna pin (44.4949, 11.3426) is Piazza Maggiore, a city centroid, not the convent; the Pugliola site is roughly 44.4996, 11.3393 **[R for the exact pin, the convent no longer standing]**.

Three Romagna legends belong to this period and none has contemporary attestation:

- **The sermon to the fish at Rimini** **[R: Wikipedia; Italian Wikipedia]**. Wikipedia's line "a great crowd of fish was seen gathered before him" is its own paraphrase, not a quotation from a chronicle. The early source (the Chronicle of the Twenty-Four Generals, or a translated *Legenda*) could not be fetched, so **no verbatim words of Anthony to the fish can be verified**.
- **The miracle of the mule** **[R]**. The heretic is Bononillo in some tellings, Bonovillo in others; the site is Rimini in the Italian tradition, Toulouse or Bourges in others. Flagged, not resolved.
- **The stolen psalter at Bologna** **[R: Wikipedia, traditional account]**: a departing novice takes the psalter holding Anthony's teaching notes and returns it after Anthony's prayer. This is the traditional origin of his patronage of lost things, and that origin is itself a legend. The book is reported preserved in a Bologna friary.

A fourth, the eating of poisoned food without harm as a fulfilment of Mark 16:18 **[R]**, has neither a named city nor a year and is not staged in the journey file.

## 4. France, 1224-1226

He teaches at Montpellier **[A for the teaching: Wikipedia, New Advent; R for the bilocation legend attached to it]**; is guardian at Le Puy, dated 1224 by New Advent **[A]**; attends the chapter at Arles **[A that he was there]**; preaches at Toulouse **[A: Italian Wikipedia]** and before Archbishop Simon de Sully at a synod at Bourges in November 1225 **[A]**; is named custos of the Limousin **[A: New Advent, 1226]**, where local memory keeps caves at Limoges and near Brive **[R]**.

**Three contradictions at Arles, flagged.**
1. **What kind of chapter.** The pool's chronology lens calls it general; Italian Wikipedia says "capitolo provinciale della Provenza." The provincial reading is used.
2. **Who saw the vision.** The pool called it "the St. Bonaventure vision." Bonaventure was born c. 1221 and was not the visionary: the vision of Francis in the doorway is reported of **Brother Monaldo**, and merely narrated by Bonaventure in the *Legenda Maior*.
3. **The year.** English Wikipedia gives 1226; standard Franciscan chronology and the pool's geography lens give 1224-1225. The two lenses contradict each other. 1224 is used and the disagreement is recorded in the stop's `date_confidence`.

A fourth internal disagreement, at Le Puy: the pool's geography lens said 1225, its chronology lens and New Advent 1224. 1224 is used.

## 5. Provincial minister of northern Italy, 1227-1230

At the Pentecost chapter at Assisi, **30 May 1227**, he is named provincial minister for northern Italy, aged 32 **[A: Italian Wikipedia]**.

**Date contradiction, flagged and corrected.** English Wikipedia gives a bare 1226 for the appointment, contradicting the pool's own geography lens (30 May 1227) and New Advent, which says he resigned "at the General Chapter of Assisi, 30 May, 1230." The pool's chronology entry compounding this ("elected and resigned the same year, 1230") is wrong on its face: New Advent gives only the resignation date. The reading used is **elected 30 May 1227, resigned 30 May 1230**. New Advent names the province "Emilia (Romagna)" against Italian Wikipedia's "Italia settentrionale": recorded, not resolved.

His province reaches the Adriatic's eastern shore. Foundations are recorded at Trieste, Pola (Pula), Muggia, Parenzo (Poreč) **[A: Italian Wikipedia]**, and visitation circuits at Udine, Cividale del Friuli, Gorizia, Gemona, Treviso, Venice, Milan, Vicenza **[R: Italian Wikipedia lists these as places, not as dated scenes]**. The journey file pins a representative subset; the full list is kept here so it is not lost.

The Ferrara legend of the newborn who speaks to clear his mother **[R: Italian Wikipedia]** belongs to these years.

## 6. Rome, and the last chapters, 1228-1230

**Two Rome episodes are conflated in the pool and are separated here.**
- **March 1228**: he preaches Lenten meditations before Gregory IX and the papal court, and this is where the pope's praise belongs. The wording is contested: the Catholic Encyclopedia gives "Ark of the Covenant," English Wikipedia "jewel case of the Bible," Italian Wikipedia the title *Doctor Arca testamenti*. **Three wordings for one anecdote; flagged, not resolved [R for the phrase, A for the occasion].**
- **1230**: the mission as envoy over the disputes about the Rule and Francis's Testament, producing the bull *Quo elongati*, 28 September 1230 **[A: Italian Wikipedia]**.

The commissioned collection is named by Wikipedia only as **Sermons for Feast Days (*Sermones in Festivitates*)** **[A]**. The pool's equation of it with the *Sermones Dominicales* is wrong: the Sunday sermons are a separate, earlier collection begun c. 1227 at Cardinal Rainaldo's request, and *Sermones de Sanctis* is not an alternate title for the same book.

On **25 May 1230**, Pentecost, he is present at the translation of Francis's body into the new basilica at Assisi **[A: English Wikipedia, "Basilica of San Francesco d'Assisi"]**. The pool gave 25 June, taken from Italian Wikipedia's "inaugurata il 25 giugno 1230"; the Pentecost date is used. **Coordinate correction:** the basilica is at OSM 43.0748, 12.6058, not the pool's 43.0707, 12.6089, a placeholder doing duty for two different Assisi sites.

He then withdraws for some months to La Verna **[A: Italian Wikipedia]**, corrected to OSM 43.7073, 11.9311.

## 7. Padua and death, 1230-1231

He settles at the convent by the church of Santa Maria Mater Domini in Padua and preaches the Lent of 1231 to crowds the hagiographers put at 30,000 or more **[A for the preaching; R for the numbers: New Advent]**. **Coordinate correction:** Mater Domini was incorporated into the later basilica as the Cappella della Madonna Mora, so its site is the basilica's, OSM 45.4015, 11.8808, not the pool's 45.4064, 11.8768, which points at no related building **[A: Wikipedia]**.

In May 1231 he goes to Verona to plead with Ezzelino da Romano for the release of prisoners, among them Count Rizzardo. The plea fails **[A: Italian Wikipedia]**.

He falls ill and retreats to Camposampiero, to a cell in a walnut tree on Count Tiso's land **[A for the retreat, R for the cell and the vision of the Christ child]**. **Coordinate correction:** Santuario del Noce is at OSM 45.5760, 11.9322, about 3 km north of the pool's pin.

**Diagnosis contradiction, flagged.** English Wikipedia states flatly that "Anthony became sick with ergotism in 1231." Older accounts give dropsy (edema). These are different conditions and competing diagnoses, not, as the pool's gloss had it, the same thing. Neither is adjudicated here.

He dies on **13 June 1231** at the Poor Clare house at Arcella, outside Padua, while being carried back to the city **[A: Wikipedia; Italian Wikipedia]**. His last words, on seeing the Host, are given by tradition as "Video Dominum meum" **[R]**.

**Two further contradictions at the death, flagged.**
- **Age.** Wikipedia's dates give 35. New Advent states 36 and offers a life tally of 15 years with his parents, 10 with the Canons Regular, 11 with the Franciscans, which does not reconcile cleanly with an 1195 birth and a 1210 entry to São Vicente either.
- **Place.** The New Advent text as fetched names **Vercelli** as the place of death, flatly contradicting Wikipedia, Italian Wikipedia, and the entire Arcella tradition. This is recorded as a contradiction in a major reference work and is not resolved here.

**Internal coordinate inconsistency, resolved.** Arcella was given three pins across the pool's lenses: 45.4213, 11.8779 (geography) and 45.4176, 11.8686 (afterlife), against OSM's Santuario dell'Arcella at **45.4216, 11.8866**, which is used.

He is buried on 17 June 1231 at Santa Maria Mater Domini, as he had asked **[A: Italian Wikipedia]**.

## 8. Tongue, basilica, cult

Canonized by Gregory IX at Spoleto, **30 May 1232**, less than a year after his death, among the fastest canonizations on record **[A: Wikipedia; New Advent]**.

Construction of the basilica begins c. 1232 and the building is substantially complete by 1301-1310, absorbing the original burial church as the Chapel of the Dark Madonna **[A: Wikipedia, "Basilica of Saint Anthony of Padua"]**.

**8 April 1263**: the relics are translated to the finished basilica in the presence of Bonaventure, minister general. The body is found reduced to dust and the tongue reported incorrupt **[A for the event and Bonaventure's presence: New Advent; R for the state of the tongue, which is a report inside a cult, not an examination]**. Bonaventure's exclamation is recorded as: "O Blessed Tongue that always praised the Lord, and made others bless Him, now it is evident what great merit thou hast before God." **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), via Wikisource]**.

**Duplicate entry corrected.** The pool carried a separate "1261 exhumation, thirty years after death" alongside the 1263 translation. There was no 1261 opening: English Wikipedia's "thirty years after" is a round number for the 1263 event, which Italian Wikipedia calls "dopo 32 anni." The entries are merged.

**Anachronism corrected.** The pool's 1263 entry listed "tongue, jaw, and vocal cords" set aside as relics. The vocal apparatus was recovered at the **1981** examination, not in 1263; the modern reliquary displays the chin and tongue.

Later: relics moved to a dedicated chapel from 1350; the Baroque Treasury Chapel begun 1691 for the gold reliquaries **[A]**. Titian paints three fresco scenes from the miracle legends for the Scuola del Santo in 1511 **[A]**; that is Titian's work at Padua, cited as a reference on Anthony's own basilica stop rather than staged as a scene in his journey. Pius XII declares him Doctor of the Church, *Doctor Evangelicus*, **16 January 1946** **[A]**. John Paul II authorizes an examination and the tomb is opened **6 January 1981** **[A]**; the pool's two lenses give 1981-01-01 for the authorization and 1981-01-06 for the opening, which are compatible. Fourteenth-century fresco cycles in the complex enter a UNESCO listing in 2021 **[A]**.

The cult of the finder of lost things spreads well past Padua: a Tuesday novena at the Shrine Church of St. Anthony of Padua in Greenwich Village, New York **[A]**; patronage of more than 105 cities across ten Brazilian states **[A]**. Neither is staged as a stop, and no Brazilian coordinate was supplied, so none is invented.

The responsory *Si quaeris miracula* by Friar Julian of Speyer, from which the patronage of lost things liturgically descends, is verified only in fragment: the fetched source gives "Si quaeris miracula . . . resque perditas," and **the complete Latin text could not be fetched** **[A for the fragment, gap for the whole]**.

## Interlocks with existing atlas files

Only relations running through Anthony himself are carried into the journey file's prose.

- **`saint_francis`**: direct, both ways. Francis presides at the Chapter of Mats where Anthony stands unnoticed, 30 May 1221, and Francis's own file already names "an unknown young Portuguese, lately of the Augustinian canons: Anthony, whom Padua will claim" in that crowd; Francis writes him the letter authorizing him to teach; Anthony walks in the translation of Francis's body in 1230. The Porziuncola pin **43.0578, 12.5804** is inherited byte-identical from that file.
- **`san_antonio_abad`**: direct and one-directional. Anthony of Padua takes his religious name from the chapel of Saint Anthony the Great at Olivais. The two are also the standing namesake hazard of Spanish American toponymy and iconography (the pig and the tau cross against the lily, the book, and the Christ Child); any cross-check of the two files should start there.
- **`count_orlando_di_chiusi`**: indirect but causal. Anthony's 1230 retreat is on the mountain Orlando gave Francis at San Leo on 8 May 1213; the retreat exists because the gift did.
- **`abd_al_rahman_al_ghafiqi`**: no personal relation; the existing **Toulouse** pin, 43.6045, 1.4442, is inherited byte-identical.

Deliberately **not** named in any campa, though present in the interlock lens: `al_kamil` (the Damietta parallel is thematic; they never met), `hernando_arias_de_saavedra` and `gauchito_gil` (Franciscan descent and later folk-devotion of lost things, downstream of Anthony rather than relations of his), `margery_kempe`, `roger_bacon`, `matteo_ricci`, `catorce_santos_ayudantes` (later institutions, no personal link), `michelangelo` (the *Torment of Saint Anthony* depicts Anthony Abbot, a different saint).

## Honest gaps

1. **No contemporary document for the birth day**, and none for the parentage; Dal-Gal calls the latter uncertain outright.
2. **The Moroccan landfall is unlocated.** No reachable source names the port.
3. **The exact site of the Santa Maria della Pugliola convent** in Bologna is approximate; the building is gone.
4. **No verbatim words of Anthony survive from any of the legends** (the fish, the mule, the psalter, the poisoned food). The sermon quotations carried into the journey file are from his written corpus, never from reported speech.
5. **The full text of *Si quaeris miracula*** could not be fetched.
6. **The early Franciscan chronicles were not read directly.** Everything here passes through Wikipedia (English and Italian), the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia, and Wikiquote. This is a report on modern encyclopedias, not on medieval sources.
7. **New Advent's "Vercelli"** for the place of death is unexplained and left standing as a contradiction.
8. The pool's line "He knew everything in Scriptures and used to quote it by heart" is by **Plinio Corrêa de Oliveira**, a twentieth-century commentator. Not used.
