# Rabbi Moses de León (Moshe ben Shem Tov de León), c.1240-1305

*Research report, 2026-08-15. Castile: León, Guadalajara, Toledo, Valladolid, Ávila, Arévalo. The man who put the Zohar into the world and never, in any surviving document, said he wrote it.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or scholarly inference · contradictions are flagged where they stand, not adjudicated · gaps are stated as gaps.

A structural warning governing the whole file: **almost everything anyone claims to know about this man's intentions comes from one chain of secondhand testimony**, collected by Isaac ben Samuel of Acre just after de León's death and preserved only in Abraham Zacut's sixteenth-century *Sefer Yuhasin*. The books, dates, and dedications are documentary. The confession is not. The two tiers are kept apart below on purpose.

---

## 1. Origins: León, and a disputed birth

- Born most probably in the city of **León**, kingdom of Castile-León **[A** EJ via Encyclopedia.com, "apparently born in Leon"; JE, s.v. "Leon, Moses ben Shem-Tob de"**]**, and the strongest evidence for it is his own hand: he signs *Shekel ha-Kodesh* as Moses "of the town of León" **[A** EJ, citing the work itself**]**.
- **Birth year, contradiction flagged.** EJ and Wikipedia give c.1240; the Jewish Encyclopedia "about 1250"; Britannica the range c.1240-50, containing both; New World Encyclopedia c.1250 **and Guadalajara as the birthplace**. Nothing reconciles them; the journey file carries 1240 with the dispute in `date_confidence`. **[A** for the disagreement itself**]**
- **Geography correction, made here.** An earlier draft placed the León judería "near the Real Colegiata de San Isidoro," which stands in the northwest of the walled city. After Castilian and Leonese forces destroyed the extramural aljama of **Puente Castro** in 1196, the surviving Jews resettled *intramuros*, in the south, in the Santa Ana quarter by the parish of San Martín, on what is now Calle Santa Ana (then Calle Silvana). **[A** Red de Juderías de España, León; Junta de Castilla y León, Judería de Puente Castro**]**
- Nothing survives about his family, his father Shem Tov, his trade, his schooling in León, or the age at which he left it. **Gap.**

## 2. Formation: philosophy first, Kabbalah after

- **1264:** a copy of Maimonides' *Guide of the Perplexed* is prepared for him, the earliest dated fact of his life, fixing the direction of his formation: philosophy before mysticism. **[A** EJ**]**
- **c.1270:** studies philosophy and draws close to the Geronese tradition and the gnostic circle around **Moses of Burgos** and **Todros ha-Levi Abulafia**. **[A** EJ**]**
- A **Burgos** training in person is sometimes reconstructed from the doctrinal descent. **No source places him in Burgos.** **[R]**

## 3. Guadalajara, c.1270-1291

- Takes up residence in **Guadalajara**, by the last third of the century the leading center of Castilian Kabbalah; its judería lay below the ground where the Palacio del Infantado was later raised. **[A** Encyclopaedia Judaica via Encyclopedia.com; Jewish Virtual Library, "Guadalajara"**]**
- **Duration, corrected.** Wikipedia and New World Encyclopedia assign roughly thirty years to **Guadalajara and Valladolid jointly**, not Guadalajara alone; EJ has him there "at least until 1291." An earlier draft gave "1286-1293": both ends are wrong, since he was writing there through the 1270s and 1280s and the wandering had begun by 1293. **[A** EJ; Wikipedia; New World Encyclopedia**]**
- **Isaac ibn Sahula**, born Guadalajara 1244, pupil of Moses of Burgos, is in the same town. **Personal contact is nowhere attested [R].** What is attested is stronger than acquaintance: his *Meshal ha-Kadmoni*, begun c.1281, carries three quotations of the *Midrash ha-Ne'elam* and is **the first work anywhere to quote the Zohar**. **[A]**
- **Joseph Gikatilla**: born Medinaceli 1248, pupil of Abraham Abulafia 1272-74, longest resident at Segovia, died Peñafiel 1325. EJ dates the closeness to the 1270s-80s and describes a mutual interdependence of thought. **[A** EJ, s.v. "Gikatilla"**]** An earlier draft called Medinaceli his career base and "nearby": it was his birthplace, 110 km off. **Corrected.**

## 4. The composition of the Zohar

- **c.1276 [R]:** begins the pseudepigraphic writing, of which the Zohar becomes the central product, to promote kabbalistic teaching. EJ's reconstruction, not a document.
- ***Midrash ha-Ne'elam***, the earliest stratum, completed **before 1286**: the source gives only that limit, so the journey file's year is a *terminus ante quem*. **[A** EJ**]**
- **c.1280-1281:** hand copies circulate among students of Kabbalah, presented as transcriptions of an ancient book by **Shimon bar Yochai**. **[A** JE, s.v. "Leon, Moses ben Shem-Tob de"**]** **Dating correction:** an earlier draft credited the year 1281 to New World Encyclopedia, which gives no circulation date at all. The anchor is external: ibn Sahula's quotations, in a work begun c.1281.
- Accusations that the ancient pedigree was invented for money circulate **in his own lifetime**. **[A** contemporaries quoted in *Segula*, "Mystery of the Zohar"**]**

## 5. The books he signed

Dated as the sources date them, with their own hedges preserved:

| Work | Date | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| *Shoshan Edut* | 1286 | first independently signed work **[A** EJ**]** |
| *Sefer ha-Rimmon* (Book of the Pomegranate), dedicated to **Levi ben Todros Abulafia** | 1287 | **[A** Jewish Encyclopedia; EJ; Brown Judaic Studies edition**]** |
| *Or Zaru'a* | **1288/89**, not flatly 1288 | **[A** EJ**]**; the Jewish Encyclopedia entry does not mention this work at all |
| *Ha-Nefesh ha-Hakhamah* (*Ha-Mishkal*) | 1290 | soul as likeness of its heavenly prototype, death, resurrection, transmigration **[A** JE; EJ**]** |
| *Shekel ha-Kodesh*, dedicated to **Todros ha-Levi Abulafia** | 1292 | **[A** JE; EJ**]**; **place unknown**, see below |
| *Mishkan ha-Edut* (*Sefer ha-Sodot*), on heaven, hell, atonement | 1293 | **[A** Jewish Encyclopedia**]**; EJ leaves it undated |
| *Maskiyyot Kesef* | **after 1293** | **[A** EJ**]**; a *terminus post quem*, so it cannot be dated *to* 1293 |
| Kabbalistic explanation of Ezekiel ch. 1 (the Ma'aseh Merkavah) | **undated** | **[A** that he wrote it: Jewish Encyclopedia**]**; **[R]** any date or place assigned to it |

- **Dedicatee correction.** An earlier draft named **Joseph ben Todros Abulafia** as a dedicatee and early recipient of Zohar material. The JE entry names exactly two dedicatees, Levi ben Todros and Todros ha-Levi; Joseph ben Todros appears in a *different* JE article ("Zohar"), only as a man who **swore that de León did not write the Zohar**. The recipient claim has no source and is dropped.
- **Placement correction.** An earlier draft put *Shekel ha-Kodesh* (1292) and *Mishkan ha-Edut* (1293) in Ávila. Both fall inside the wandering interval, and the cited entries give no place for any of his works. Locations recorded as unknown.
- The overlap between signed Hebrew books and unsigned Aramaic sheets is the whole later argument: same images, same phrases, same doctrines. In his lifetime that is a man reusing his own material; after 1941 it is evidence.

## 6. The wandering years, 1291/92-1295

- Resident in Guadalajara **at least until 1291**; "after 1292 Moses led a wandering life." **[A** Encyclopaedia Judaica via Encyclopedia.com**]**
- **No source names a single town visited in this interval.** **Gap.** A visit to **Segovia**, where Gikatilla lived for years, is plausible and entirely unattested **[R]**.
- Settles in **Ávila**, where he lives the rest of his life. **Contradiction flagged:** Encyclopaedia Judaica gives no year; the Junta de Castilla y León and Ávila Turismo give **1295**, and specify the house of **Yuçaf de Ávila**, a wealthy Jew of the city. An earlier draft gave 1292, which is the year the *wandering* began, not the year it ended. The file uses 1295. **[A** for the fact of the Ávila settlement, across all four; **contradiction** on the year**]**

## 7. Ávila, 1295-1305

- Lives in Ávila by the Puerta de la Malaventura, producing and circulating further copies of the Zohar. **[A** JE; Ávila Turismo**]**
- Married, with a daughter; the household is left without means at his death. **[A** for the family, through the Isaac of Acre tradition; **[R]** for the poverty, which belongs to the same disputed testimony**]**
- **Tag correction.** An earlier draft had him telling visitors "from the 1290s" that the ancient original was in his house at Ávila, tagged [A]. There is exactly **one** attestation of that assertion, the oath sworn at Valladolid in 1305; a fifteen-year habit extrapolated from it is unsourced. Lowered to **[R]** for the habit, kept **[A]** for the oath. The often-quoted "the original from which he copied was in his own house at Avila" is a paraphrase of Graetz relayed by a blog, not a primary text.

## 8. Valladolid, the oath, and the road to Arévalo, 1305

- **Isaac ben Samuel of Acre** reaches Spain in 1305, investigating the Zohar's origins. **Correction:** not "newly arrived from the Land of Israel"; he escaped the fall of Acre in **1291** and spent fourteen years elsewhere, Italy among them. **[A** Wikipedia; Jewish Encyclopedia**]**
- The two men meet at **Valladolid**. De León swears an oath that he holds at his house in Ávila a copy of the Zohar written by Shimon bar Yochai himself, and invites Isaac to come and see it. **[A** Jewish Encyclopedia; Encyclopaedia Judaica via Encyclopedia.com; *Segula*, quoting Isaac's account**]**
- On the road home he falls ill and dies at **Arévalo**, roughly midway between Valladolid and Ávila, in **1305**, before the inspection can take place. **[A** Jewish Encyclopedia; Encyclopaedia Judaica; Britannica; New World Encyclopedia**]**
- **No burial site is recorded**, for Arévalo or anywhere else, and no grave or marker is known to survive. **Gap, stated as a gap.**

## 9. After the death: the widow's testimony

This entire section rests on one chain: widow (and daughter) to an informant at Ávila, to Isaac of Acre, to Abraham Zacut's *Sefer Yuhasin* two centuries later. It is therefore **[R] throughout**, including the parts an earlier draft tagged [A].

- A wealthy man of Ávila named **Joseph** offers the widow a large sum for the ancient original; in the fuller version of the story he offers a marriage between his son and de León's daughter, with a substantial dowry. **[R** Jewish Encyclopedia, s.v. "Zohar"; the Kotzk Blog version reports only the money**]**
- The widow answers that no such manuscript ever existed and that her husband wrote the book himself, out of his own head. **[R]**
- She reports asking him why he ascribed his own teaching to another, and his answer: that doctrines in the mouth of the miracle-working Shimon bar Yochai would be a rich source of profit, whereas his own name would draw nothing. **[R]**
- **Chain correction.** Per Isaac's own account the denial was **not made to him directly**: wife and daughter revealed it to the wife of a certain Joseph of Ávila, and it reached Isaac at third hand.
- **The counter-testimony, usually omitted.** Isaac also collected sworn statements from **Joseph ben Todros** and from **Jacob, a pupil of de León**, both insisting under oath that de León did *not* write the Zohar; Isaac credited them. **[A** as a record of what Isaac collected; the content remains contested**]**
- **Authenticity dispute, not adjudicated.** The JE's own "Zohar" article calls the story barely credible ("inconceivable that a woman should own that her deceased husband had committed forgery for the sake of lucre"); Landauer judged it apocryphal, against Graetz, who used it.

## 10. The book without its author

- **1486:** Flavius Mithridates translates thousands of pages of Aramaic Kabbalah, Zoharic material among it, into Latin for **Giovanni Pico della Mirandola**, whose teacher Elia del Medigo warns him that what he is buying is not what he has been told it is. This is the channel by which de León's book enters Christian Europe. **[A** giovanni_pico_della_mirandola.journey.json, lines ~188-203**]**
- **1492:** the Alhambra Decree expels the Jews of Castile and Aragon; Sephardim carry the Zohar out in their baggage, and it takes root in the Galilee, above all at Safed. **[A** shimon_bar_yochai.journey.json line ~431; columbus.journey.json line ~379**]**
- **First printings:** rival editions from **Mantua** (three volumes, 1558-1560) and **Cremona** (Vincenzo Conti's press, one volume, usually 1559-1560, some sources 1558), after a long rabbinic controversy over printing the book at all. **[A** Sephardic U; Jewish Currents; michaeld.ca**]** *Coordinate note:* the two cities are 60 km apart and must not share a pin (Cremona 45.13/10.04, Mantua 45.16/10.79).
- **1941: Gershom Scholem**, in *Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism*, having once promised to refute the theory, concludes that de León is the author: the Aramaic's grammatical errors, its Spanish-inflected syntax, the author's ignorance of the real geography of the Land of Israel, the phrases shared with the signed Hebrew books. **[A]** *(shimon_bar_yochai.journey.json dates the argument to Scholem's 1926 Jerusalem lecture; both dates stand.)*
- **1993: Yehuda Liebes**, *Studies in the Zohar*, argues against the single-author model for a **circle** around de León with distinct voices across sections; this is now the dominant position. **[A] Contradiction with Scholem preserved, not resolved.**
- **2017:** the twelve-volume **Pritzker Edition** concludes (Daniel C. Matt, with Nathan Wolski and Joel Hecker), the first English translation from a critical Aramaic text built from manuscripts. **[A** Stanford University Press; *Moment***]**
- **Commemoration:** the **Jardín de Moshé de León**, Ávila, opposite the Puerta de la Malaventura. **[A** Junta de Castilla y León; Ávila Turismo**]** **No monument, plaque, or street naming him is documented in Guadalajara**, where he lived longest **[R** one blog author recalls a photograph of a possible monument and could not relocate it**]**.
- Roughly two dozen further Hebrew works are credited to him; they are the linguistic control group authorship studies compare against the Zohar's Aramaic. **[R** Ávila Turismo; juderiasdesefarad.blogspot.com**]**

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. **Birth year**: c.1240 (EJ, Wikipedia) vs. about 1250 (JE, New World Encyclopedia) vs. c.1240-50 (Britannica).
2. **Birthplace**: León (EJ, JE, his own signature) vs. Guadalajara (New World Encyclopedia).
3. **The Ávila settlement**: undated in EJ, 1295 in the Castilla y León sources, 1292 in retellings that confuse it with the start of the wandering.
4. **The widow's confession**: decisive for Graetz, apocryphal for Landauer, doubted by the JE's own "Zohar" article, set aside by Isaac of Acre for two sworn denials.
5. **Authorship**: Scholem's single author (1941) vs. Liebes's circle (1993) vs. the traditional attribution to Shimon bar Yochai, never formally withdrawn.
6. **Scholem's date in the corpus**: 1926 (shimon_bar_yochai file) vs. 1941 (*Major Trends*): a lecture and a book, both stand.
7. **Cremona's printing year**: 1558 in some sources, 1559-1560 in most.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- Father, family, trade, income, standing in any aljama: unknown throughout. The years between birth and 1264: no evidence of any kind.
- Every town of the wandering interval, 1292 to 1295: unnamed in every source.
- Place of composition of *Shekel ha-Kodesh*, *Mishkan ha-Edut*, *Maskiyyot Kesef*, and the Ezekiel commentary: unrecorded.
- Whether he ever traveled to Toledo, where his dedicatees lived: unrecorded. The tie to the Abulafia household is attested; the journey is not.
- His burial place: no tomb, no marker, no cemetery named.
- The names of the students who received the first Zohar sheets, beyond what the posthumous oaths imply.

## Interlocks with the existing corpus

- **shimon_bar_yochai** [A]: that file already has the Zohar circulating in Castile as the record of bar Yochai's teaching, and names de León as the real author on Scholem's philology. One-directional, and the central fact of both lives.
- **maimonides** [A]: the 1264 copy of the *Guide of the Perplexed* prepared for de León. A documented reader-to-book relation.
- **giovanni_pico_della_mirandola** [A]: Mithridates' 1486 Latin translations of Zoharic material for Pico, the route the book took into Christian Europe.
- **borges** [A, downstream]: Cansinos-Assens, who translated the Zohar into Spanish, appears in the Borges file; Borges takes up "the hermeneutic machinery of the Kabbalists" in *Discusión* and names Scholem. Cited, not named in a campa.
- **arthur_edward_waite** [A, one-directional]: *The Holy Kabbalah* (1929) on what the Renaissance magicians took out of the Zohar. A source on de León's own stops; Waite's reading room belongs to Waite.
- **giulio_camillo** [R, indirect]: the Theatre's Sephirot reach him through Pico's Christian Kabbalah, two removes from Castile.
- Deliberately **not** claimed: jacob_boehme (diffuse "Kabbalistic material"), juan_eduardo_cirlot (general Kabbalah), benjamin_de_tudela and Maimonides' Iberian flight as mere geography. Shared ground is not a relation.
- **Pin warning**: the atlas's other "Guadalajara" entries are the Mexican city. De León's is in Castile, 40.6333 / -3.1669.
