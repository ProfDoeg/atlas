# Edward Bulwer-Lytton (1803-1873): research report
*2026-08-14, for `edward_bulwer_lytton.journey.json`. 45 stops, 8 segments, gregorian throughout.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or weakly sourced claim. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated; where the journey had to choose one date or coordinate, the choice is stated and the loser recorded. Hedges live in the journey's `date_confidence` field, never in campa prose.

## 1. Norfolk, London, the schools (1803-1821)

- Born **25 May 1803**, third son of General William Earle Bulwer and Elizabeth Barbara Lytton **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica; Victorian Web]**. The address **31 Baker Street** comes from the Victorian Web and the old DNB; the Oxford DNB gives only London **[A/R]**.
- Father dies **7 July 1807**; Norfolk estates (Heydon Hall, Wood Dalling) pass entire to the eldest brother **[A: Victorian Web to the day; Britannica gives only "when the boy was four"]**. The removal to London and the mother's lifelong control of the allowance **[A: Oxford DNB]**.
- **Dr Ruddock's school, Fulham, c.1812**, year only **[A: Victorian Web, citing DNB]**.
- **Dr Thomas Hooker's school, Rottingdean.** Two pool errors corrected. (1) Dates: Oxford DNB has him arriving "at eleven" (c.1814) and leaving **September 1818**; the pool's "c.1812-1818" is too early, and the journey uses 1814. (2) Coordinates: the supplied 50.8004/-0.0453 falls in the English Channel, ~1.1 km SE of the village; Hooker kept the school in the **vicarage house**, so the journey anchors on the village at **50.8059/-0.0585**. Henry Manning a fellow pupil **[A: Oxford DNB]**.
- **Rev. Charles Wallington at Ealing, 1819-1821**, and fencing under **Henry Angelo**, Byron's master **[A: Oxford DNB]**. The Victorian Web's "summer 1820" is a point inside that span.
- **Ismael: An Oriental Tale, with Other Poems**, April 1820 **[A: Victorian Web; Wikipedia]**; Wikipedia spells it *Ishmael*, and the campa names both spellings rather than choosing. **The girl at Ealing**, called in the family biography only "Lucy D-", wrote to him from her deathbed **[A for the attachment and the letter; R for any identification]**.

## 2. Cambridge, Ullswater, Paris (1822-1826)

- Goes up **January 1822**, one term at Trinity, then Trinity Hall as fellow-commoner **[A: Oxford DNB]**. **Contradiction flagged:** the Victorian Web says **Easter term 1822**; January is Lent term. Journey takes January, records the divergence in `date_confidence`, keeps year, colleges, and rank, on which all agree. The earlier bulwer_lytton file also carried Easter term, with a Trinity pin dated 1822-04-01.
- Secretary, treasurer, president of the Cambridge Union **[A: Oxford DNB]**. ***Delmour* is 1823, not 1825**: Oxford DNB names Carpenter & Son's 1823 printing and DNB 1885-1900 agrees, and 1825 would place it after he left Cambridge. Corrected, folded into the Trinity stop.
- **Ullswater, long vacation 1824.** The pool dated this 1833-1834 and called it a Wordsworth pilgrimage; both wrong. Oxford DNB: a pilgrimage "to visit the grave of the girl he had loved and lost at Ealing", an overnight vigil on her tomb, then weeks in a solitude "similar to that of Erasmus Falkland, the hero of his first novel, which he was drafting at the time." V. A. Lytton's *Life* titles the chapter "THE GRAVE OF A DREAM. 1824." Journey dates it 1824-07-01 as a graveside vigil; it returns as the Lily Mordaunt strand of *Kenelm Chillingly* **[A: Oxford DNB]**.
- **Chancellor's Gold Medal**, *Sculpture*, **July 1825**; **BA 1826** **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]**. Army commission bought and sold c.1829 **[R: Wikipedia only, and the pool flags the sale as uncorroborated]**.
- **Paris, Faubourg Saint-Germain**, the Marquise de la Rochejaquelein, the attachment broken by his mother on anti-Catholic grounds **[A: Victorian Web]**. **Versailles, *Weeds and Wild Flowers*:** Oxford DNB has it "privately printed later in 1825", Victorian Web 1826, and since he did not return to London until **25 April 1826** the 1825 printing is the coherent reading. Stop dated 1825-10-01, both readings in `date_confidence`.

## 3. Marriage, the silver fork, Parliament (1827-1832)

- **Marriage to Rosina Doyle Wheeler, 29 August 1827** **[A: Oxford DNB; Wikipedia; Dictionary of Irish Biography]**. The pool contradicted itself, its chronology array giving 30 August and its geography array 29 August; the Victorian Web (source of the 30th) is the outlier. **The venue "St James's Church" is dropped**: no source corroborates it. Mother refuses to attend and terminates the allowance **[A: Oxford DNB]**.
- **Household: Woodcot House, Oxfordshire, not Reading, Berkshire.** Oxford DNB: "at Woodcot House in Oxfordshire, 6 miles from Reading, an imposing residence whose rent and upkeep accounted for virtually their entire combined income". The pool's separate "Reading 1827-1829 household" entry is a duplicate and a displacement; Reading is the reference town only and **is not a stop**. The pool's coordinates (51.4885/-1.0955) sit ~3.7 km off, on the Berkshire side of the Thames; the journey uses **51.522/-1.096**. **Emily born there June 1828** **[A: Oxford DNB]**.
- *Pelham* (1828), *The Disowned* (1828), *Devereux* (1829) **[A: Wikipedia]**; payments of £900 and £1500 **[A: Oxford DNB]**. **Vine Cottage, Fulham, Sept-Dec 1829** **[A: Oxford DNB]**: no usable coordinate, so no stop.
- **36 Hertford Street, Mayfair, bought late 1829** **[A: Oxford DNB; History of Parliament Online heads his entry "of 36 Hertford Street, Mdx."]**. The pool's range "by 1820s-1832" is wrong at both ends: nothing before late 1829, and he kept Mayfair houses for decades after. *Paul Clifford* (1830), its opening sentence and "the great unwashed" **[A: Wikipedia; Victorian Web; opening verified against Gutenberg]**.
- **First constituency, 1831: CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** Wikipedia gives St Ives 1831 then Lincoln from 1832; a History of Parliament / Britannica-derived summary names **Lincoln** as the first seat in 1831. The journey keeps both stops in Wikipedia's order and states the conflict in `date_confidence` on the St Ives stop. The file's most consequential open question. **Lincoln 1832-1841** **[A]**; *Eugene Aram* (1832), *Godolphin* and *Asmodeus at Large* (1833) **[A: Wikipedia]**; the *New Monthly* editorship resigned August 1833 **[A: Oxford DNB]**.

## 4. Italy, Pompeii, the separation (1833-1836)

- **The Italian journey ran September 1833 to February 1834**, returning with "the near-completed manuscript" **[A: Oxford DNB]**. The pool hedged between the winters of 1832-33 and 1833-34; the DNB settles it and the hedge is dropped.
- **Naples, study with Sir William Gell**, while Rosina "flirted outrageously with a Neapolitan prince" **[A: Oxford DNB]**. The pool's Posillipo attribution is not in the DNB, and its coordinates (40.8272/14.1949) sit ~2 km NW of Posillipo; the stop is retitled simply **Naples** on the canonical pin **40.8518/14.2681**. **Pompeii** follows, his own preface quoted at that stop **[A: via Victorian Web and Gutenberg front matter]**.
- **Milan, the Brera, Bryullov's *The Last Day of Pompeii*, 1833.** The pool tagged this **[R]** in geography while asserting the same fact as **[A]** in afterlife: an internal contradiction. The exhibition and his viewing are stated as fact by Wikipedia's article on the painting and by the Getty essay, so this report treats it as **[A]**. The tension with his own preface, which credits the site visit as the trigger, is left open.
- *The Last Days of Pompeii* (1834), published by Richard Bentley; *The Pilgrims of the Rhine* (1834), *Rienzi* (1835) **[A: Getty; Wikipedia]**.
- **The separation: pool sequence misdated.** Oxford DNB puts the decision **after** the Italian trip, so **1834**, not 1833; 1833 is the year of the affair with Mrs Robert Stanhope that precipitated it. The **February 1836** flashpoint was Rosina raiding his **Albany** apartments and accusing him publicly of keeping his mistress **Laura Deacon** there; the legal deed followed **April 1836** **[A: Oxford DNB; Victorian Web for April; a Britannica-derived summary collapses it into one 1836 event]**. One stop at 1836-02-01, pinned at the Albany (51.508/-0.1391, my own pin, the Albany being unpinned in the pool).

## 5. Plays, Knebworth, the water cure (1837-1851)

- *Ernest Maltravers* (1837); *Alice* and *Leila* (1838); **baronet 1838** **[A: Wikipedia]**. ***Richelieu*** (1839) and "the pen is mightier than the sword" **[A as text: reproduced in full by multiple citing sources]**. **Gap:** the play text was not re-fetched from a primary source, and neither premiere date nor venue is established, so the stop takes the year and a generic London pin rather than asserting Covent Garden. ***Money***, Theatre Royal Haymarket, **8 December 1840** **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Craven Cottage, Fulham, c.1840-1846.** The pool tagged this **[R]** and "thin and secondhand"; too pessimistic. He took it in 1840 and held it six years, entertaining Prince Louis Napoleon; documented in Fulham local-history sources and club ground histories. Treated as **[A]**, dates firm, coordinates 51.4749/-0.2216 good. ***Zanoni*** (1842) **[A: Wikipedia; Victorian Web]** is pinned there on residence grounds, the composition address itself **[R]** and flagged in `date_confidence`.
- **Mother dies 1843**, leaving Knebworth and the Lytton estate; **royal licence 20 February 1844** for name and arms; "thoroughly shattered", and the mantelpiece inscription **[A: Victorian Web]**.
- **Great Malvern water cure**, Dr James Wilson's establishment, nine or ten weeks, same circle of patients as Darwin, Dickens, Carlyle, Tennyson **[A: explorethepast.co.uk; Wikipedia]**. Year within the 1840s not fixed **[gap]**. **Boppard** and **Petersham** from 1844 **[R: Victorian Web only]** get no stop and are named inside the Malvern campa.
- *The Last of the Barons* (1843), *Harold* (1848), *The Caxtons* (1849) **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 6. The second parliamentary career (1852-1866)

- **Hertfordshire, Conservative, 1852-1866**; **Rector of Glasgow from 1856** (Wikipedia gives 1856-1859, the Victorian Web only the start year) **[A]**.
- **Secretary of State for the Colonies, 5 June 1858 to 11 June 1859**; selects **Richard Clement Moody** for British Columbia; **Lytton, BC**, Fort Dallas renamed 1858, never visited **[A: Wikipedia; canonical pin 50.2317/-121.5817 inherited]**. Month not established **[gap]**.
- **The hustings and the asylum, 1858.** Rosina denounces him at the Hertfordshire election; he has her committed to a private asylum; she is released after public outcry **[A: Wikipedia; *A Blighted Life*]**. Placed at 1858-06-20 inside the attested June-July window, the missing day stated in `date_confidence`. Pool caution observed: the "Hertford Street" of the Mayfair address must not contaminate this stop, which belongs at **Hertford (51.7959/-0.078)**.
- *A Strange Story* (1862), no stop. **Baron Lytton of Knebworth, 1866**; **GCMG 15 January 1870**; son **Edward Robert**, b. 8 November 1831, Viceroy of India 1876-1880 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 7. Vril and death (1867-1873)

- **Argyll Hall, Warren Road, Torquay, taken 1867**, built 1849, later the Roseland Hotel, now Marine Palms above Rock Walk **[A: The PRSD; English Riviera]**. **Coordinate reconciliation:** the pool gave 50.472/-3.5175 in geography and 50.4619/-3.5253 in afterlife for the *same building*, the former ~1.2 km inland. The journey uses **50.4619/-3.5253** for both Torquay stops.
- **Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia founded at Aldermanbury, 1867** **[A]**. **His patronage is [R]:** the founding record does not name him, and the claim rests on *Zanoni* alone. The campa states the absence of the record rather than asserting patronage.
- ***The Coming Race*, published anonymously by Blackwood, May 1871** **[A: Oxford DNB]**. **The pool's title is anachronistic:** "Vril: The Power of the Coming Race" is an **1873 retitling**, made when his authorship was acknowledged; the journey uses *The Coming Race*, with the retitling in `date_confidence`. Eight editions in eighteen months, and the word briefly entering the language **[A: Oxford DNB]**.
- **Robert and his wife visit Torquay in 1872** **[R: Victorian Web only]**, folded into the death campa rather than given a stop. **Dies at Argyll Hall, 18 January 1873**, aged 69, of **cerebral abscess** from chronic ear disease **[A: Oxford DNB]**.
- **Buried Westminster Abbey, Chapel of St Edmund, 25 January 1873** **[A: Westminster Abbey commemorations]**. Dean **Stanley** hesitated, **John Forster** pressed him, a letter in *The Times* followed. Placed near **Sir Humphrey Bourchier**, killed at Barnet, a character in one of his romances **[A; the pool spells him Bourgchier]**. He had asked to lie simply at Knebworth **[A: Poets' Corner accounts]**.
- **Posthumous publication corrected:** *Kenelm Chillingly* and *The Parisians* are 1873; ***Pausanias the Spartan* is 1876** **[A: Oxford DNB; Wikipedia]**. *Kenelm Chillingly*, issued days after his death, sold out its first impression of 3150 three-volume sets on the day of publication **[A: Oxford DNB]**.

## 8. Afterlives

- **Statue, niche S05, Colonial Office, Whitehall** **[A for niche, Scott's building of 1861-68, and the subject]**; sculptor **Henry Hugh Armstead**. The **1874 placement date is the weak link**, resting on vanderkrogt.net and londonremembers, the latter returning 403 this session **[flagged in `date_confidence`]**. vanderkrogt records that the figure reportedly resembles his son Robert more than the man himself **[R]**. The building postdates the tenure, served in the old Downing Street houses.
- **Bovril: pool wrong on person, probably on date.** The creator was **John** Lawson Johnston (1839-1900), not **George** (1873-1943), his son and later 1st Baron Luke. Wikipedia puts development in the 1870s, the renaming at **1886** with trademark **1887**, Bovril Ltd at 1889; **1884 is unsupported**. Journey uses 1886. Etymology confirmed by Oxford DNB. Minor: vril is a general vital force, **not** telepathy, and the journey does not call it telepathic.
- **17 Lansdowne Road, 20 October 1888**, Blavatsky finishing *The Secret Doctrine*, vril passing into it as fact **[A: blavatsky.journey.json]**.
- **Munich, June 1919** **[pin and date inherited from maria_orsic.journey.json]**. The Vril Society's historicity is **[R], treated by historians as pseudohistory**: the earliest identifiable source is **Willy Ley's 1947 article**, sceptical rather than confirmatory, and the Hitler-at-Landsberg claim rests on occult-history sites alone. The campa reports what the circle did with the word, nothing more.
- **Bulwer-Lytton Fiction Contest**, founded **1982** by **Scott E. Rice**, San Jose State, discontinued January 2025 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
