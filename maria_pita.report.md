# María Pita (Mayor Fernández da Cámara Pita), c.1556-1643

*Research report, 2026-08-17, for `maria_pita.journey.json`. A Coruña; Madrid, San Lorenzo de El Escorial, El Pardo; Sigrás in the parish of Cambre.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim standing on the subject's own petition rather than independent record. Contradictions are flagged and left standing.

The biography rests on two modern transmissions of the archive, **López Rodríguez (2023)** and **Briante Benítez (2020)**, both read in full, plus **Martínez Salazar (1889)**, reached only through them. **Saavedra Vázquez**'s monograph of 2003 would not yield text (FlateDecode binary) and reaches this file at second hand. Full references in Sources.

---

## 1. The shop in the Peixería, 1554-1587

**[A]** **1554**: Simón Arnao and María Pita "la vieja" marry and keep a shop in the Peixería, outside the wall of the Cidade Vella; their daughter works the counter in her youth *(Saavedra via Briante p.104)*.

**[R] Birth: unsettled on two axes, not one.** Year: Saavedra argues c.**1556**, Martínez Salazar **1562-1568**, popular usage 1565 *(López p.156)*. Place: the same sentence has Martínez Salazar putting the birth at **Sigrás, Cambre**, and López p.172 endorses it; Briante and Saavedra put it in A Coruña. **Correction applied:** the pool tagged the Peixería birthplace [A] and treated only the year as open. What is documented is the **parents' shop**, not the birth. The stop is [R] and states both axes in `date_confidence`.

**Gap:** no baptismal entry located, in the Cidade Vella parishes or at Sigrás; the old-town origin is an inference from the shop.

**[A]** **1581**, first marriage to **Juan Alonso de Rois**, butcher of San Cristóbal das Viñas; daughter **María Alonso de Rois**, who later renames herself María Alonso de Pita after her mother *(López pp.158-159)*. Three addresses (López p.158): **Herrerías 24, today 28, the Casa Museo**; Santa María 26; Cortaduría 9. López attributes the Herrerías house to **her**; that Rois owned it rests on es.wikipedia alone. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 43.3708/-8.3961 is 300 m off; the file uses **43.3711/-8.3924**.

**[A]** **1583**, death of María Pita "la vieja" *(Saavedra 2011 pp.38-39 via Briante p.109)*. The date matters later: Robello's 1833 play gives the heroine a living mother in 1589.

**[A]** **1587**, death of Juan Alonso de Rois *(López p.157)*. **Correction:** the pool carried "widowed c.1585" in two lenses, unsupported by either source, and offered Briante's "antes de julio de 1587" as corroboration; that phrase (p.105) refers to the **second marriage**. She then sues her son-in-law **Gregorio Vázquez de Verbía** over an inheritance from **Elena de Rois**, her first husband's grandmother, and wins lands and property *(López p.159)*.

**Contradiction flagged: the second marriage**, to **Gregorio de Rocamonde**, butcher. López p.157: "noviembre de 1587." Briante p.105: "antes de julio de 1587." Mutually exclusive; the pool presented them as agreeing. The file pins November and states the conflict. The pool's alternative, es.wikipedia's 1585, is a phantom: es.wikipedia gives no year here.

## 2. The siege, May 1589

**[A]** **4 May**: the fleet of **Francis Drake** and **Sir John Norris**, 180-200 sail and 5,000-6,000 troops, appears off A Coruña; the English land at **Santa María de Oza** and take the unfortified Pescadería *(Briante p.110, citing Saavedra and the diary of Capt. Juan Varela)*. **7 May**: they set a firing position in the **convent of Santo Domingo** *(López p.173)*, the same building her testament will name fifty-four years later, which is why it has a stop.

**[A]** **14 May**: a mine brings down the wall and the assault comes through; **Gregorio de Rocamonde is killed**, in his wife's sight *(Briante p.106; López p.154)*.

**Correction applied, the largest in the dossier.** The pool's geography lens dated four stops (the husband's death, the feat, the women's work, the battle cry) to **3 May**, a figure from es.wikipedia that is out of step with both scholars and is not even the arrival date. All four are moved to **14 May**.

**[R] The individual feat** (the ensign, the pike, the killing blow, the seized flag) is the central evidentiary problem. It is **absent from the surviving official siege documentation**, per Saavedra Vázquez, and **present in her own petitions from 1596**: "peleo varonilmente, de manera que mato al General de la Inglesa" (AGS CC 772, López p.162). The narrative version quoted in the file ("Adelantose entonces a la brecha un alferez enemigo...") is offered by López p.154 as reflecting "las crónicas de la época," but his footnote leads to **a modern blog retelling**, not a named period chronicle; carried as unverified transmission, said so in `quote_source`.

**[A] The collective evidence, of a different order.** The **Cofradía de San Andrés** deposition of **25 September 1589** (Archivo General de Galicia, P. sig. 16-nº I; López pp.154-155) describes the women of A Coruña in the plural: fighting beside husbands and soldiers, killed at the wall while filling the towers with earth, hauling material, burying the dead. It names a servant of Juan Jaspe and a shoemaker's wife among the dead, names no heroine, describes no individual feat. This is the contradiction Saavedra points at, left open.

**[A]** **19 May**: the English withdraw and sail for Lisbon *(Briante pp.110-111)*.

## 3. Third widowhood and Captain Peralta, 1590-1596

**[A]** **1590**, third marriage to **Sancho de Arratia**; daughter **Francisca de Arratia**, still named in an instrument of 1620 *(Briante pp.106, 111)*. **Contradiction flagged:** Briante p.106 has "el hidalgo y oficial de la marina"; López p.157 has "el capitán de Infantería de la guarnición." **[A]** Arratia dies between May and November **1592** *(Briante p.111)*.

**Detail dropped as unsupported, recorded so the deletion is visible.** The pool made Arratia a shipmaster of **Sanlúcar de Barrameda** carrying timber and munitions to **Ferrol**, and gave Sanlúcar a stop. Neither cited source contains Sanlúcar, timber, or Ferrol, and the Ferrol arsenal postdates this by a century and a half. **No Sanlúcar stop is in the file.**

**[A] The Peralta affair.** Captain **Francisco de Peralta** is billeted on her house; she is sentenced by the **Licenciado Haro** to two years' exile from A Coruña and its jurisdiction plus a **4,000-maravedí** fine payable before release from prison; she takes the case to court *(López pp.158, 160-161, 164-165, from Martínez Salazar 1988 pp.142-201 and AGS Secretaría de Guerra 660)*.

**Transcription corrected** against López p.158 at two points ("Vistos: Fallo atento le procesado que debo de condenar," "dentro de tercero dia que como salga"); the file carries the corrected text, and the page reference belongs to footnote 10 (pp.142-201), not footnote 9. **Dating gap:** the sentencia is undated. The file uses an ordering date of 1595 and says so; the only anchor is that the redress of July 1596 answers it.

## 4. The first road to Madrid, 1596-1597

**[A]** **May 1596**, first journey to Madrid with informes and certifications *(López p.159, citing Saavedra pp.145-146)*.

**[A] The memorial to Philip II**, AGS Cámara de Castilla leg. 772, transcribed in full at López p.162 and carried verbatim in the journey file: she went in her own person to fight and fought "varonilmente, de manera que mato al General de la Inglesa"; she spent and gave much property, food, cord, bullets, munitions, mattresses; her husband was killed there leaving two unmarried daughters. **Transcription corrected:** the pool dropped four words, "y dio mucha hazienda" after "gasto"; restored. The memorial asks a licence for **2,000 mules**; **300** are granted.

**[A]** **24 July 1596**, cédula ordering the Captain General of Galicia to redress Peralta's grievances and confirming her house's exemption from billeting *(via Saavedra p.146, López p.163)*. **4 September 1596**, licence for 300 mules, Galicia to Portugal; **28 October 1596**, supplement for 200 more at **San Lorenzo**; **1597**, a further petition on the same licence, quoted in the file *(all AGS CC 794; López pp.159-160)*.

## 5. Philip III, 1598-1608

**[A]** **30 May 1598**, licence renewed *(AGS CC 934; López p.166)*.

**Contradiction flagged: the fourth marriage**, to **Gil Bermúdez de Figueroa**, escudero of the Real Audiencia, land at Ledoño and San Pedro de Nos; sons **Juan** and **Francisco Pita de Figueroa**; his testament strips her of tutorship and usufruct should she remarry. Briante p.106: **1598**. López p.157: **1599**. The file pins 1598 and states the conflict.

**[R]** **1599**, second Madrid petition, to Philip III *(AGS CC 901; López pp.163-164)*: "peleando entre los soldados con pica y murrión," supply of cloth, cord and provisions, aid for her children, the office of **alguacil mayor of Betanzos** for a daughter. Tagged [R]: the document fuses the combat and supplier framings without distinguishing them, and the combat claim is the uncorroborated one.

**[A], redated and re-sourced: the stipend petition** (5 escudos a month; raise it, make it heritable to the daughters, place the two young sons in the A Coruña infantry). **Source:** not AGS CC 917 but **Martínez Salazar 1988 pp.122-123** (López's footnote 16; CC 917 is his footnote 22, on the clerics' goods of 1607). **Date:** the text speaks of "Su Magestad, **que esta en el cielo**," and Philip II died 13 September 1598, so 15 July **1596** is impossible; López places it in his 1596 narrative anyway. The file uses 1599-07-15 as an ordering date and states the problem in `date_confidence`.

**[A]** **17 August 1606**, renewed memorial on Peralta *(AGS Secretaría de Guerra leg. 660; López p.160)*. Three corrections to the pool's transcription: the abuses are dated in the document to **1596**, not 1589; the phrase is "di quenta a vtra. mag.d en su rreal consejo de guerra destos agravios," she **gave** account, not "le quito la quenta"; and "me lleno todos mis bienes muebles y" was missing. **Further contradiction, unresolved:** López prints this memorial **twice with conflicting dates**, p.160 as of 17 August **1606**, p.164 as of 17 August **1596**.

**[A]** **26 Aug 1606**, licence prorogued two years, 200 plus 300 head, San Lorenzo *(CC 934; López pp.166-167)*. **12 May 1607**, petition for the **escribanía de las entradas** of the Cárcel de la Corte, after ten months at court, "muy gastada y empeñada" *(CC 926; López p.168)*. **2 July 1607**, petition for the reverted goods of three dead clerics *(CC 917; López pp.168-169)*. **28 Jan 1608**, cédula at **El Pardo** to Don Diego de las Mariñas, governor of Galicia, to enforce the billeting exemption *(Libros de Guerra 105; López p.165)*: the last court document of hers reached.

## 6. Sigrás: the last thirty years, 1613-1643

**[A]** **1613**, Gil Bermúdez de Figueroa dies. She holds considerable property at **Sigrás (Cambre)**: arable and farms, wheat, vines whose wine she sells by the pipe to A Coruña taverners with at least one resulting suit, and she lends money at interest *(López p.158; Briante p.106)*.

**Coordinate correction, systematic.** The three lenses of the pool each gave a **different and each time wrong** point for the same parish: 43.2764/-8.3667, 43.2431/-8.3667 (5.1 km south, beyond Cambre), 43.276/-8.35. Sigrás is at **43.2893/-8.3606**, used by all Sigrás stops.

**[A]** **1616-1619**, the Real Audiencia suit *Martín de Cavalar con Maior Fernández Pita de Cámara* over Ledoño, fol. 17, preserves Galician verses beginning "María Pita está remendada..." *(transcribed by María del Pilar Rodríguez Suárez in Saavedra 2003 p.226, via Arquivos de Galicia)*. The most valuable item in the afterlife: her name in vernacular circulation **while she is alive**, a century and a half before the heroic ballads.

**Contradiction flagged: how many lawsuits.** **27** is the sourced figure: Briante, printed **p.106** (not p.111 as the pool had it), 27 suits of which 16 criminal querellas, 9 as plaintiff and 7 as defendant. The competing figure is **lower, not higher**: López p.173 has "una veintena de procesos judiciales." **The pool's "~35" could not be traced to any source**, and two of its lenses led with it. The file gives 20 to 27, leading with 27.

**[R] The 1633 stipend.** The pool claimed a 1633 notarial document recording **ten escudos a month**; what is documented (López pp.163, 175) is **five escudos plus an alférez's pay**, and no reference for the ten-escudo instrument could be produced. The file keeps it as [R], untraced, and does not choose between the figures.

## 7. Death, 1643

**[A]** **21 February 1643**, sudden death at Sigrás. The burial entry, found by **Xosé Antón García Ledo** in the Archivo Histórico Diocesano de Santiago, names no illness and no accident: "se murió Mayor Fernández Pita de repente" *(López pp.171-173, via Martínez Salazar 1988 pp.188-189)*. The same entry copies her testamentary request: "tenia echo testamento por el qual semando enterrar en santo Domingo dela çiudad dela Coruña," beside her last husband. The parish of Sigrás does not honour it.

**Gap:** no grave has been securely identified, at Sigrás, at Santo Domingo, or anywhere else. For the register: the death record names her **Mayor**, never María.

## 8. The heroine assembled, 1726-1998

**[R] The battle cry.** "Quen teña honra, que me siga" appears in **no** 1589 source. The earliest attestation located is **Feijoo, *Teatro crítico universal*, Tomo I, Discurso XVI, "Defensa de las mujeres" (1726)**, quoted at López p.174 and carried verbatim in the journey file: she snatches sword and buckler from a soldier's hands, reproaches the men with their cowardice, "clamando que quien tuviese honra la siguiese." That is 137 years after the breach; the Galician wording is later still. **Correction:** the pool, following an error at López p.174, called it "discurso sexto"; it is **Discurso XVI**.

**[A]** **1784**, Valladares de Sotomayor, *Defensa de La Coruña por la heroica María Pita*: the siege fused with an invented love triangle and her origin **raised**, single and highborn, a governor's daughter. **1833**, Robello y Vasconi, *María Pita o la heroína de Galicia* (BNE MSS/17427): closer to the record, still wrong in detail (Rocamonde a baker; a living mother in 1589, six years after the mother died). *(Briante pp.105, 108-110, 116-118.)*

**[A]** **1889**, Martínez Salazar's foundational study does the archival work and in the same breath invents a face: "debió ser de estatura elevada y de nada vulgar belleza" *(López p.174)*. No portrait from life exists.

**[A]** **1990**, Cabaleiro: Romantic historiography concentrated the collective agency of many A Coruña women into one exceptional, masculinized heroine, intensified by Galician identity-building and the city's need for a civic symbol *(pp.99-100, via López p.175)*.

**[A], redated:** the bronze by **José Castiñeiras Iglesias** in the Praza de María Pita is of **1998**, not 1968; the 1968 in the pool is a typographical error in the caption of López p.155, Fig. 1. es.wikipedia and gl.wikipedia both give 1998 with dimensions (total 9.31 m, 30 tonnes). Coordinates corrected to **43.3709/-8.3959**; the pool's 43.3717/-8.4004 sits 370 m west, off the square.

**Claim dropped for want of any source: the 1801 medal**, carried in two lenses of the pool as a Museo del Ejército piece whose account calls the dead Englishman only "believed" to be Drake's brother. Neither medal, date, nor qualifier could be located; the only museum object in the reachable literature is a María Pita image in the **Museo Militar de La Coruña** (López p.176, Fig. 11). **No medal stop is in the file.**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Nothing here requires the EXCEPTIONS.md list, and nothing was silently deleted.** Reporting the direction anyway. María Pita is not on the list and no apparatus stop was written. The relations in the interlock pool sort into two kinds.

1. **Backward-gazing engagements by later people with her**: Feijoo 1726, Valladares 1784, Robello 1833, Martínez Salazar 1889, Cabaleiro 1990. Real one-directional relations, so per the standing rule they appear **as sources and suggested_refs on her own stops**, never as scenes of those authors travelling or writing. Feijoo's convent at Oviedo, proposed as a stop by the pool, is **Feijoo's** place; his text is cited on her breach stop instead. The pool's 1784 Madrid theatre stop is handled the same way.

2. **Shared geography with no relation at all**: A Coruña's port in `humboldt.journey.json` and `aime_bonpland.journey.json` (the Pizarro, 1799), Galician pilgrim geography in `margery_kempe.journey.json`, `ramon_llull.journey.json`, `egeria.journey.json`, a named-place echo of Bayona in `columbus.journey.json`. **None is a relation to María Pita**, and none is named in any campa.

**Canonical pin inherited byte-identical:** the A Coruña port pin **43.3623 / -8.4115**, as it stands in the Humboldt and Bonpland files, is used unchanged on the two harbour stops (4 and 19 May 1589). For the operator: that value is the municipality centroid, 2.4 km southwest of the sixteenth-century Pescadería, which is why every **other** A Coruña stop uses street-level coordinates. If the corpus pin is normalised, those two move.

**No forward reference was written**, and no later traveler's slug appears. Segment 9 is reception **of her**, at her own pins, the shape of the statue stops already in `egeria.journey.json` and `margery_kempe.journey.json`. **No Argentina link, by design:** the Buenos Aires pin was checked and left out.
