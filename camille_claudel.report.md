# Camille Claudel (1864-1943): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-14.*

**Legend. [A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or contested attribution. **FR** = fr.wikipedia "Camille Claudel", **EN** = en.wikipedia "Camille Claudel", **AC** = Odile Ayral-Clause, *Camille Claudel: A Life* (Abrams, 2002). Contradictions are flagged and left open, not adjudicated.

Sculptor. Born in the Aisne; trained in Paris outside the academy that would not admit her; ten years inside Auguste Rodin's studio as praticienne, collaborator and lover, then twenty alone; committed by her family in March 1913 and held thirty years and seven months until her death. The documentary spine is clean at both ends (civil registry, Salon records, hospital registers, a death certificate) and thin in the middle, where the most-repeated claims come from biographers working with privately held family papers.

The pool arrived with **eight wrong coordinates** (one off by 9 km), **four misattributed quotations**, one **fabricated quotation** found in no Claudel source, and **three genuine source contradictions**.

**Coordinates corrected** (pool value, then the figure the journey file uses, each from the relevant fr.wikipedia commune or institution article): Fère-en-Tardenois 49.1937, 3.5271 to **49.1994, 3.5172**; Villeneuve-sur-Fère 49.1502, 3.5967 or 49.1667, 3.5667 to **49.1783, 3.4758** (the first is ~9 km east, in open country); Nogent-sur-Seine 48.4886, 3.5075 to **48.4939, 3.5031**; Château de l'Islette 47.2564, 0.4653 to **47.2675, 0.4342**; Ville-Évrard 48.8672, 2.5389 to **48.8589, 2.5489**; Montdevergues 43.9394, 4.8886 or 43.928, 4.935 to **43.9203, 4.8758**; Montfavet cemetery 43.937, 4.894 or 43.9294, 4.9256 to **43.9360, 4.8760**; Musée Camille Claudel 48.4886, 3.5064 or 48.4897, 3.4967 to **48.4942, 3.5003**.

## 1. The Tardenois childhood (1864-1876)

- Born **8 December 1864** at Fère-en-Tardenois, Aisne, second child of Louis-Prosper Claudel, registrar of mortgages, and Louise-Athanaïse Cerveaux. An elder brother, Charles-Henri, died in infancy in 1863. **[A: FR/EN]**
- Sister Louise born February 1866; brother Paul born **6 August 1868 at Villeneuve-sur-Fère**. **[A: fr.wikipedia "Paul Claudel"]**
- The family holds property at Villeneuve; Camille spends her early years and childhood summers there, modeling figures from local clay. **[A: FR/EN; Musée Camille Claudel materials]**
- **La Montagne**, the limestone escarpment above the village, cited as the landscape that taught her mass and cavity and as the source of her first clay. **[R: family tradition relayed by Reine-Marie Paris and Paul Claudel's writings; no document fixes it]** No coordinate exists in any reachable source; the journey pin sits beside the village, approximate and not surveyed.
- **Gap:** no work survives from before Paris. The childhood modeling rests entirely on later recollection, mostly Paul's, written after his sister had become a subject rather than a sibling.

## 2. The father's postings (1870-1881)

- Bar-le-Duc from around 1870. **[A: EN; standard chronologies]** Nothing recorded of her there beyond residence.
- Nogent-sur-Seine, 1876. At twelve she meets **Alfred Boucher**, who gives her real instruction. **[A: FR/EN; Musée Camille Claudel official history]**
- **Two pool errors corrected.** (1) The geography lens has Paul born "close to this period" at Nogent: false, he was born in 1868 at Villeneuve, eight years earlier. (2) The pool calls Boucher a "Prix de Rome laureate"; fr.wikipedia records only a **second** prix de Rome (1876) and notes "malgré un double échec au premier prix de Rome". **[A: fr.wikipedia "Alfred Boucher"]**
- Wassy-sur-Blaise, 1879. **[A: FR/EN]** No surviving work, no anecdote beyond residence.

## 3. Paris and the workaround (1881-1883)

**The 1881 move: the pool's two lenses contradict each other, and one contradicts Wikipedia.** The chronology lens has the mother bringing the children to Paris, to Montparnasse (135 bis boulevard Montparnasse) **[A: FR]**; the geography lens has the father winning a years-long argument with his wife and moving them, pinned to boulevard de Port-Royal. FR's residence sequence is Montparnasse 1882-1886, 31 boulevard de Port-Royal 1886-1892, 19 quai de Bourbon 1899-1913, so **Port-Royal belongs to the later period, not the arrival**, and the "father won the argument" reading is a biographer's, not Wikipedia's. **Flagged, not resolved:** the journey file pins the arrival to Montparnasse and carries the disputed agency in `date_confidence`.

- **Académie Colarossi**, rue de la Grande-Chaumière, 1882, entered because the École des Beaux-Arts does not admit women to its sculpture ateliers. **[A: FR/EN; Musée Rodin notes]**
- Studio at **117 rue Notre-Dame-des-Champs** shared with English sculptors including **Jessie Lipscomb**; Boucher instructs the group free until he leaves for Italy. **[A: FR/EN]**
- Salon des Artistes Français submissions in the early 1880s, including a bust of the family servant known as Vieille Hélène. **[A: Salon records cited in standard biographies]**

**Two quotations reattributed.** The pool assigned four Claudel letters wholesale to Rodin; search inside AC shows two are misattributed. "Don't fear anything for your letters, they are burnt one by one and I hope you do the same with mine" went **to Florence Jeans**, her English friend, not to Rodin **[A: AC, which quotes it with its frame, "she wrote to Florence in February"]**. "When you left on Saturday, I felt a horrible void, I saw you everywhere, on the beach, in your room, in the garden" is verbatim in AC but its **addressee is not given**, and the seaside setting fits the English correspondence **[A as to text; R as to recipient]**; the journey file calls it neutrally a letter of Claudel's.

## 4. The atelier (1883-1892)

- **1883:** Boucher, leaving for Italy, passes his students to **Auguste Rodin**; Claudel enters his atelier at the Dépôt des marbres, 182 rue de l'Université, as praticienne. **[A: FR/EN; Musée Rodin archives]**
- By 1884 she is fully integrated among the praticiens, working on **The Gates of Hell** and **The Burghers of Calais**. **[A for the studio integration: FR]** **[R for attributing particular hands and faces to her chisel, a claim recurring in biographies without a document to separate her carving from the studio's]**
- **1886:** the liaison; residence at 31 boulevard de Port-Royal. **[A: FR]**
- **c. 1888-1892, the Folie Neubourg / Clos Payen**, a ruined folly off the boulevard d'Italie secured for her by Rodin. **[R: AC; address precision differs and the building is demolished]**
- **1888:** honorable mention at the Salon; *Sakuntala* in marble; a bust of Rodin. **[A for the works: EN]** **[R for the Salon distinction, so tagged in the pool]**
- **1891, Château de l'Islette. Three corrections** besides the coordinate: the château is in the commune of **Cheillé**, not Azay-le-Rideau (postal address only); the rental period is **the summers of 1890, 1891 and 1892**, not "c. 1886-1892"; and the work documented as made there is ***La Petite Châtelaine***, not "much of La Valse". **[A: fr.wikipedia "Château de l'Islette"]**
- **1891:** jurist for the Société Nationale des Beaux-Arts. **[A: EN]**
- **1892, the break.** After an abortion she ends the intimate relationship. **Sources contradict on when contact ceases:** EN says the two keep seeing each other until **1898**; FR dates the definitive separation, with Rodin choosing to remain with Rose Beuret, to **1892**. **Flagged, not resolved;** the stop is dated 1892 and carries the disagreement in `date_confidence`.
- **Claude Debussy.** One attested line, "I weep for the disappearance of the Dream of this Dream," from Seroff (1970), p. 152, glossed as Debussy on the end of his relationship with Claudel. **[A for the quotation and its ascription; R for any dating of the attachment]** No chronology entry places Debussy in her life and Seroff's own sourcing was not checked, so the journey file uses the line once and builds no stop on it.

## 5. Her own hand (1893-1899)

- **1893:** *Clotho* at the Salon of the Société Nationale, Champ-de-Mars; *La Valse* conceived and worked. **[A: EN]** The story that the jury demanded the figures be clothed before a state purchase stays **[R]**: the jury correspondence is cited in biographies but was not reachable this session.
- **1895:** the Direction des Beaux-Arts commissions a marble *L'Âge mûr*; it stalls, then is **cancelled in 1899, reportedly at Rodin's intervention**. **[A for commission and cancellation; R for Rodin's role, which AC notes has a thinner documentary trail than the story attached to it]**
- **1897:** the comtesse Arthur de Maigret becomes a patron until a rupture in 1905 **[A: FR]**; *La Vague* completed in onyx and bronze **[A: EN]**.
- **Meudon.** The geography lens dates Rodin's Meudon household "1892-1898". **Rodin did not acquire the Villa des Brillants until 1895**, so the range is wrong at its front end; narrowed to 1895-1898. **[A]**
- **1899:** Rodin reacts with shock and anger to *L'Âge mûr*, read as depicting the breakup, and withdraws his support. **[A: EN]**
- **1899:** she takes studio and apartment at **19 quai de Bourbon**, Hôtel de Jassaud, Île Saint-Louis, her address until 1913. **[A: FR]**

## 6. The quai de Bourbon (1900-1912)

- **1900:** bronze medal at the Exposition Universelle. **[R as tagged in the pool]** *L'Âge mûr* completed. **[A]**
- **1902:** *Persée et la Gorgone* completed. **[A: EN]**
- **December 1905:** solo exhibition at the **Galerie Eugène Blot**, boulevard de la Madeleine; *La Valse* and *L'Implorante* cast in bronze by Blot. **[A: EN; AC; press notices cited in standard biographies]** Octave Mirbeau's "A revolt against nature: a woman genius" reaches this file only through *Connaissance des Arts*, 2 July 2020, via Wikipedia. **[A at second hand; the original Mirbeau publication was not identified]**
- A **second Blot exhibition around 1907-1908** is reported, date uncertain, biographers disagreeing on whether one showing or two took place. **[R: AC notes the uncertainty]** Given no stop in the journey file, precisely because the date cannot be fixed.
- **1905:** break with the comtesse de Maigret; paranoid ideation and isolation intensify. **[A: FR]**
- **1906:** after Paul's marriage and departure for China she lives secluded and begins destroying her own work. **[A: EN]** The line for these years, confirmed inside four biographies (AC; Paris; Delbée; Butler): "Last night, two men tried to force my shutters. I recognized them: they are two of Rodin's Italian models. He told them to kill me." **[A: AC]** The pool's BrainyQuote sourcing is dropped.
- **1910:** studio damaged in the great flood of the Seine; **1912:** she destroys many plasters and personal works. **[A: FR]**
- **Gap:** the scale of the destruction is nowhere quantified. "The majority of her life's work" is a biographers' phrase, not a tally; the 1996 catalogue raisonné lists what survives, not what was broken.

## 7. The certificate (1913-1915)

- **2 March 1913:** Louis-Prosper Claudel dies at Villeneuve-sur-Fère; sources differ by a day, EN giving 3 March and other biographies 2 March. **Flagged.** Camille is not informed in time to attend the funeral. **[A: FR/EN]**
- **7 March 1913:** Dr Michaux certifies serious mental disturbance. **[A: FR]**
- **10 March 1913:** committed to **Ville-Évrard**, Neuilly-sur-Marne, at Paul's request. **[A: FR/EN; fr.wikipedia "Hôpital de Ville-Évrard" confirms the date from the register]**

**The transfer to Montdevergues: a real contradiction, weighed and recorded in full.** EN gives 7 September 1914, with an admittance certificate dated 22 September 1914. Against it stand three French sources: FR gives 12 February 1915; fr.wikipedia "Hôpital de Ville-Évrard" records her resident "10 mars 1913 au 12 février 1915", citing Éric Favereau's *Libération* investigation; and fr.wikipedia "Centre hospitalier de Montfavet" says "transférée à Montdevergues le douze février 1915". A fourth variant, es.wikipedia's July 1913, is refuted outright by the Ville-Évrard register.

The journey file dates the transfer **1915-02-12** and states the September 1914 reading inside `date_confidence` rather than dropping it silently. The certificate quotation is retained: the document's own date is a different question from the date of the move.

## 8. Montdevergues, thirty years (1915-1943)

- **25 February 1917:** she writes to Dr Michaux asking for her liberation. **[A: FR]**
- **1920:** Dr Brunet advises her mother that she could be reintegrated into family life; the mother refuses. **[A: EN]**
- **Jessie Lipscomb visits.** One source gives a first visit in 1924, another December 1929; both agree Lipscomb insisted afterward that Claudel "was not insane". **[A, count and dates left open]**
- **June 1929:** Louise-Athanaïse dies without having taken her back. Her sister Louise visits **once in her life, in 1929**. **[A: EN]** The geography lens's claim that "her mother and sister never once make the journey" is **false as to the sister**, and is corrected.
- **Paul's visits: sources differ sharply.** EN gives seven (1913, 1920, 1925, 1927, 1933, 1936, 1943); fr.wikipedia "Paul Claudel" gives twelve. **Flagged, not resolved:** the journey file says "between seven and twelve".
- **19 October 1943:** she dies at Montdevergues, aged 78, reportedly of a stroke, wartime malnutrition cited as contributing. No relative attends. She is buried in the **carré des indigents** of the Montfavet cemetery; within roughly a decade the remains are moved to a communal ossuary, unclaimed, so that no grave survives to be visited. **[A: FR/EN]**
- **Morhardt's judgment, corrected.** The pool carried "shut away his genius of a sister" as a Morhardt quotation. It is not; that phrase is the Wikipedia editor's own gloss. Morhardt's actual sentence, per the Tehan citation the article rests on, is what the journey file quotes: "Paul Claudel is a simpleton. When one has a sister who is a genius, one doesn't abandon her. But he always thought he was the one who had genius." **[A: Tehan, *The Gates of Hell*, ch. 3 (Xlibris, 2010)]**

## 9. Afterlife (1951-2017)

- **1951:** Paul organizes a retrospective of about forty works at the **Musée Rodin**. **[A]**
- **28 September 1968:** commemorative plaque at the cemetery of Villeneuve-sur-Fère, with no remains beneath it. **[A: FR]**
- The rediscovery, in order: Anne Delbée's *Une femme* (**1982**), credited with sparking it; the state's purchase of a posthumous bronze of *L'Âge mûr* for the **Musée d'Orsay** (**1983**), eighty-four years after letting the marble lapse; a retrospective from the Musée Rodin (15 February to 11 June **1984**) to the Musée Sainte-Croix, Poitiers; and the catalogue raisonné of Rivière, Gaudichon and Ghanassia (**1996**). **[A]**
- **1988:** Bruno **Nuytten**'s film with Isabelle Adjani. **Two corrections:** the director is Nuytten, not Bruno Dumont (a misattribution the pool caught); and the two Academy Award nominations, Best Actress and Best Foreign Language Film, came at the 62nd ceremony held in **1990**, not 1989. The five Césars were won in 1989, the likely source of the confusion. **[A]**
- **2008:** cenotaph at Montfavet, erected by her great-niece Reine-Marie Paris. **[A: FR]**
- **26 March 2017:** the **Musée Camille Claudel** opens at Nogent-sur-Seine. **Two corrections:** it is built around **la Maison Claudel**, the family's house, not Alfred Boucher's studio (Boucher's link is institutional, as founder of the 1902 predecessor museum); and EN says it displays about **half of Claudel's 90 surviving works**, roughly 43 pieces, where the afterlife lens misread 90 as the museum's holding. **[A: fr.wikipedia "Musée Camille-Claudel"]**

## 10. Argentina: a connection kept thin on purpose

The single documented Argentine appearance is a **2008 loan exhibition at the Museo Nacional de Arte Decorativo, Buenos Aires**: *L'Implorante* and *La Vague* (*La Ola*), borrowed from Mexican and Puerto Rican collections. **No Claudel work is or was held in any Argentine collection. [R]**, and the [R] is load-bearing. The claim entered the pool from the research brief itself, and the verifying pass located no catalogue, museum record or period press coverage. **It should be checked against the MNAD exhibition archive or 2008 Buenos Aires press before being treated as documented.** The journey file carries that hedge in `date_confidence`.

Rodin's Argentine footprint is by contrast thick and attested (*Sarmiento*, *El Pensador*, nineteen sculptures at the Bellas Artes, a named inheritance in Troiani's *El Arquero*). The asymmetry is the point and is not smoothed over.

## Interlocks used, and interlocks declined

**Used** (Claudel has a direct or one-directional relation to the person):

- **Auguste Rodin** (queued, QUEUE.md line 895): teacher, employer, collaborator, lover, and in her own later account her persecutor. There is no telling this life without him.
- **Dante** (dante.journey.json): Claudel modeled and carved figures for *The Gates of Hell*, Rodin's commission built out of the *Inferno*. Her hands are on a monument to a poem, a one-directional link between a sculptor and a poet six centuries dead. Named once.

**Declined**, all Rodin adjacencies rather than Claudel relations, none of the files mentioning her: **Isadora Duncan**, **Loïe Fuller**, **Vaslav Nijinsky**, **Troiano Troiani**, **John Everett Millais**, the **Solvay Congress pin at Parc Léopold**, and **Falconetti**, whose 1944 Buenos Aires staging of *L'Échange* links to the brother, a year after the sister's death.
