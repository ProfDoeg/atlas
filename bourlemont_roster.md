# The seigneurs de Bourlémont — generation-by-generation census
*2026-07-12. Château de Bourlémont, commune of Frebécourt (Vosges, arr. Neufchâteau, canton Coussey), overlooking the Meuse valley; the family were also lords of Greux and Domrémy (Joan of Arc's village).*

The standard monograph is **Georges Poull, *Le château et les seigneurs de Bourlémont***, 2 vols. (I: *Les sires de Bourlémont, 1149–1412*, 1962; II: *Les barons d'Anglure. Les d'Alsace, princes d'Hénin, 1412–1964*, 1964) — text not directly accessible (Google Books no-preview; the Numilog excerpt PDF Cloudflare-gated), so Poull reaches this list through the French Wikipedia article on the château (which follows him) and booksellers' descriptions ("catalogue of nearly 300 acts of the lords of Bourlémont"). The **attested backbone** comes from Siméon Luce, *Jeanne d'Arc à Domremy* (1886), whose *Preuves* print the actual charters. **MedLands (fmg.ac) does not treat the family at all** — LORRAINE and BAR pages downloaded and grepped: zero occurrences (an honest gap).

Legend: **[A]** = attested, document cited · **[R]** = genealogist's reconstruction (Poull-derived or Racines & Histoire) · fl. = floruit only.

## Origin (context)

A first castle was built by the **bishops of Toul**, probably 1100–1130, garrisoned by men-at-arms led by a member of the **Brixey family** (avoués of Brixey-aux-Chanoines); that branch took the name of the seigneurie, whose **first known mention is 1184** (no individual named in reachable sources for that act). **[R — fr.wiki château, after Poull]**. Kinsman of the first generation: **Pierre de Brixey, bishop of Toul 1165–1192**, said to be a cousin of Simon de Brixey **[R — fr.wiki "Thomas de Bourlémont"]**. Nothing earlier than Hersende (below) is claimed; no 11th-century Bourlémont is attested in anything reachable.

## First house (Brixey de Bourlémont), c. 1125 – 1412

**Gen. 1 [R].** **Simon Ier de Brixey**, seigneur de Brixey (c. 1090 – †1149) ∞ c. 1125 **Hersende de Bourlémont, dame de Bourlémont** (c. 1100 – †?). The earliest name-bearer in any source; entirely Poull's reconstruction as relayed by fr.wiki. Poull's vol. I opens in 1149 (Simon's death).

**Gen. 2 [R].** **Pierre Ier** (c. 1130 – †1170) ∞ **Ide de Froville** (c. 1135 – †?).

**Gen. 3 [R].** **Simon II** (c. 1149 – †1197) ∞ **Agnès de Broyes** (de Neufchâteau) (c. 1160 – †1221). The 1184 first mention of the seigneurie falls in his tenure.

**Gen. 4 [R].** **Pierre II** (c. 1170 – †c. 1226) ∞ **Félicité de Joinville** (c. 1185 – †1240) — the first Joinville tie.

**Gen. 5 [A].** **Joffroy de Bourlémont** ("Jofridus, dominus de Borlenmont"): charter of **28 May 1248**, giving the abbey of Mureau all the *arages* of Domremy-sur-Meuse, Greux and Neuville-lez-Greux, "laude et assensu **Sibille uxoris mee** et heredum meorum"; confirmed by Thibaud, comte de Bar, May 1248 (Arch. des Vosges, cartulaire de Mureaux, f° 226; printed in Luce, *Preuves*, Suppl. I). Poull/fr.wiki add **[R]**: c. 1210 – †1268, **sénéchal de Navarre** of comte Thibaut IV (King Thibaut I of Navarre), rebuilt the castle after returning from the Holy Land; wife **Sibylle de Saulxures** (c. 1215 – †1275).
- Son (per fr.wiki "Thomas de Bourlémont"): **Thomas de Bourlémont, 59th bishop of Toul, elected 22 July 1330 – †April 1353** **[A as bishop; R as to filiation]** — archdeacon, then dean of Verdun cathedral; issued an early penal regulation for the Toulois. *Chronological strain flagged:* a son of Joffroy (†1268) and Sibylle becoming bishop in 1330 and dying 1353 would be 85–100+ years old; the filiation (fr.wiki, citing A. D. Thierry, *Histoire de la ville de Toul et de ses évêques*, 1841) probably skips a generation. Not adjudicated.
- Also **[A]**: a **Thomas de Bourlémont, seigneur de Bras**, present at an act of 1320 in the church of Saint-Remy of Domremy (BnF, coll. de Lorraine, vol. 129, n° 166; Luce). Whether he is the future bishop or a kinsman, the sources do not say.

**Gen. 6 [A 1300 / R details].** **Pierre III** — "Pierre, seigneur de Bourlemont," charter of **May 1300** re the Mureau monks' holdings at Greux and Domremy (BnF, coll. de Lorraine, t. 82, n° 58; Luce) **[A]**. Poull/fr.wiki: c. 1255 – †1310, ∞ **Jeanne de Choiseul** (c. 1255 – †after 1311) **[R]**.

**Gen. 7 [A].** **Jean Ier, sire de Bourlémont** — takes Poirel of Greux under his safeguard **4 Nov 1327** (BnF, coll. de Lorraine, vol. 129, n° 162; Luce); renders a full **aveu to Edouard Ier, comte de Bar, Monday 18 July 1334** (Arch. de Meurthe-et-Moselle, layette Gondrecourt, n° 112; printed Luce, Suppl. II): liege man of Bar *except* his homage to the bishop of Toul and to the king of France "pour cause de la contey de Champaigne"; holds **the forte maison of Domremy and half that village** (the church side, bounded by the brook), half of Maxey-sur-Vaise, and fiefs at Reffroy, Orquevaux, Haillainville. Poull/fr.wiki **[R]**: 1285 – †15 Nov 1337, ∞ **Isabelle (Jeanne) de Grancey** (c. 1295 – †1357) — Racines & Histoire independently names "Jean et Isabelle de Grancey" as Henri's parents.
- His sister **[A]**: **Agnès de Bourlémont**, "li dame dou Chastelleir, ma suers" in the 1334 aveu — wife of **Girard, sire du Châtelet**, her Bourlémont share (partage) at Reffroy.

**Gen. 8 [A].** Three brothers, jointly attested in a charter of **Monday 22 March 1350**: "**Pierre, sire de Bourlemont, et Henri, son frère**, donnèrent à **Jean de Bourlemont, leur frère**, la vigne que on dit la vigne de Greux" (BnF, coll. de Lorraine, vol. 82, n° 46; Luce):
1. **Pierre (IV), sire de Bourlémont**, fl. 1350 **[A]** — the reigning sire in 1350; *omitted from the fr.wiki chain* (flagged below). Luce's calling the last lord "Pierre V" presupposes him.
2. **Henri (Ier) de Bourlémont**, sire de Bourlémont et de **Donjeux** — fl. 1350 **[A]**; per Racines & Histoire **[R]**: ° from 1321, †before 7 May 1403 (fr.wiki: †7 May 1403), ∞ **Alix (alias Béatrix) de Joinville, dame de Donjeux** (†1406 per R&H; †1413 per fr.wiki — flagged). Father of Jeanne (→ Anglure, below).
3. **Jean de Bourlémont** (numbered **Jean I** in Luce's index but **Jean II** in Luce's own text and footnotes — internal inconsistency, flagged), écuyer, **seigneur de Bourlémont "en partie" et sire de Domremy (et Greux)**: consents 26 Sept 1370 to Duke Jean I of Lorraine's safeguard over the men of Domremy (Luce, Suppl. III) **[A]**; aveu 12 Feb 1398 **[A]**; **testament 3 Oct 1399 + codicil 23 Oct 1399** (printed in Luce, *Preuves* XIII): "Je Jehans de Boullaimmont, escuiers, signour d'icellui lieu en partie et sire de Dompremey…", electing burial at Saint-Eloi de Gerbonvaux "en la fosse mon père", instituting heirs **Pierre and Jean his sons**, with provision for **frère Liebaut, religieux, his third son**, and the famous clause remitting the Domremy goslings-rent **[A]**. †1399/1403 (Luce has Pierre lord "de 1399 à 1412"; the Geneanet tree flemoine2 gives †1402/03 — unverifiable, Cloudflare). ∞ **Catherine de Bauffremont-Ruppes** (Luce, from the Bauffremont genealogy compiled for the prince de Bauffremont-Courtenay and J.-Ch. Chapellier) **[A/R]**.

**Gen. 9 — the last generation:**
- **Pierre (V) de Bourlémont** **[A]** — eldest son and principal heir in the 1399 testament; **"seigneur de Greux et de Domremy de 1399 à 1412"** (Luce); buried in the Cordeliers (Franciscan) church of Neufchâteau (Luce). ∞ **Béatrix**, "originaire du royaume de France" — the châtelaine whom the 1456 rehabilitation-trial witnesses remembered picnicking at the **Arbre des Dames / Fairy Tree** with her husband and her mother-in-law Catherine de Bauffremont-Ruppes (Luce, from the *Procès*). **Died without children** — extinction of the male line, 1412 (Luce: "certainly during the first quarter of the 15th century"; Poull/fr.wiki: 1412, the year of Joan's birth). *Filiation contradiction flagged below (fr.wiki makes him son of Henri).*
- **Jean**, second son (1399 testament) **[A]** — nothing further found; evidently died without succeeding. A gap, left as such.
- **Liebaut**, third son, in religion (1399 testament) **[A]**.
- **Jeanne de Bourlémont** (daughter of Jean & Catherine de Bauffremont) **[A]** — ∞ **André de Joinville, seigneur de Beaupré et de Pulligny**; †before 1414 (Luce). Mother of Jeanne de Joinville (below).
- **Jeanne de Bourlémont, dame de Donjeux** (daughter of **Henri** & Alix/Béatrix de Joinville) **[R — R&H, fr.wiki]** — c. 1375 – †1433; ∞ 1) **1400 Jean-Saladin d'Anglure** (°~1370, †before 7 May 1403), seigneur d'Etoges et de Raucourt, capitaine de Reims; ∞ 2) 1405 **Pierre de Belloy dit "Le Baudrand"** (†killed at **Agincourt**, 25 Oct 1415). Through her, Bourlémont (castle) and Donjeux passed to the **maison d'Anglure**.

## The transition, and the lords of Domrémy in Joan of Arc's lifetime (c. 1412–1431)

The inheritance split between the two Jeannes:

**Greux and Domrémy** → **Jeanne de Joinville** (dite **de Joinville-Bourlémont**), daughter of André de Joinville and Jeanne de Bourlémont, niece and heiress of Pierre (V) **[A — Luce, with BnF coll. de Lorraine refs]**. **She is the actual dame of Domrémy of Joan of Arc's childhood.** Kinswoman (through her father) of Antoine de Lorraine-Vaudémont, seigneur de Joinville, descendant of the chronicler-seneschal. ∞ 1) before 24 March 1415 (elsewhere Luce says "avant 1413" — flagged) **Henri, seigneur d'Ogéviller**, bailli de Vosges, conseiller, chambellan & maître d'hôtel of Duke Charles II of Lorraine (attested in an act of 12 July 1424, BnF coll. de Lorraine t. 90 bis n° 15; still alive 6 Feb 1428 o.s.); ∞ 2) by 16 Sept 1429 (BnF coll. de Lorraine vol. 223, n°s 21, 23) — elsewhere in Luce "un peu avant le 15 octobre 1430" (flagged) — **Jean (III), comte de Salm**, killed at the **battle of Bulgnéville, 2 July 1431**, a month after Joan's execution. As "dame d'Ogéviller" she obtained restitution of cattle raided from Greux and Domrémy (letter of remission, before 1428; Luce, *Preuves* CCXXXVI).

**The Château de l'Île lease [A]** — notarized act of **2 April 1420**: a **nine-year farm lease (14 June 1419 – 14 June 1428)** of the "forte maison" de l'Île at Domremy with its dependencies, concluded through **Aubry dit Jannel, mayor for the lords**, to **Jean Biget (Bigot) of Neufchâteau and Jacques d'Arc** (Joan's father, elsewhere "doyen" of Domremy — so attested 7 Oct 1423, Luce *Preuves* LI) with other villagers; **14 livres tournois** a year plus grain; **Jacquemin d'Arc** (Joan's eldest brother) among the guarantors. Original: Trésor des chartes de Lorraine, Arch. de Meurthe-et-Moselle, **layette Ruppes 2, n° 28** (vidimus on parchment). Published/communicated by Luce (CRAI, 1889).

**Bourlémont castle and Donjeux** → the **d'Anglure** (Poull's vol. II runs from 1412; fr.wiki "Famille d'Anglure" dates the acquisition 1403).

## Contradictions between sources (not adjudicated)

1. **Filiation of the last Pierre (†1412).** Luce (grounded in the 3 Oct 1399 testament) makes him **son of Jean** (∞ Catherine de Bauffremont), lord of Greux-Domremy 1399–1412, wife **Béatrix**. fr.wiki *Château de Bourlémont* makes Pierre †1412 **son of Henri Ier** (†1403) and brother of Jeanne d'Anglure. A reconciliation (Pierre son of Jean succeeding his uncle Henri in the castle) is plausible and would fit Poull's 1412 volume-break, but unverifiable in Poull directly — recorded as open.
2. **Luce's own numbering** is internally inconsistent: the testator of 1399 is "Jean I" in his index but "Jean II" in his text and footnotes (because of the earlier Jean fl. 1327–1337).
3. **The two Béatrix.** R&H: Henri's wife "Alix *alias* Béatrix de Joinville, †1406, dame de Donjeux"; fr.wiki: "†1413". Luce: **Béatrix** is the *wife of Pierre V* seen at the Fairy Tree (1456 depositions). Risk of conflation across sources is high; fr.wiki's "Pierre's widow Béatrice cited in the 1456 trial" matches Luce, its filiation does not.
4. **Jeanne de Joinville's marriage dates**: Luce gives both "married before 1413" and "before 24 March 1415"; remarriage to Salm "by 16 Sept 1429" and "a little before 15 Oct 1430" in different notes.
5. **Date the Anglure took Bourlémont**: 1400 (marriage), 1403 (Henri's death; fr.wiki Anglure), or 1412 (Pierre's death; Poull vol. II, fr.wiki château).
6. **Post-1732 devolution**: Lignéville (R&H, fr.wiki Anglure) vs Bauffremont (fr.wiki château).
7. **Pierre IV (fl. 1350)** is attested by charter but absent from the fr.wiki succession chain.
8. **Bishop Thomas** as son of Joffroy (†1268): chronologically strained (see Gen. 5).
9. A "Hedwige de Bourlémont" appears in one Geneanet tree (dupuisl); page Cloudflare-blocked, unverifiable — recorded as a stray, nothing more.

## Secondary flag: the Provence/Italy — "Pica de Bourlémont" claim

- **English Wikipedia, "Francis of Assisi"**: "a French mother, Pica di Bourlemont, about whom little is known except that she was a noblewoman originally from Provence" — footnoted only to "Lives" (i.e., the early *vitae*, which do **not** contain the surname).
- The earliest sources on Francis's mother (Thomas of Celano's *vitae*; the *Legend of the Three Companions*) give only the name **"Pica"** with no family name and no origin; none of the reachable material dates the appearance of "de Bourlemont" — no source found even attempts to. (But see pica_dossier.md: Frassen 1666 is the earliest verified carrier.)
- **Provençal-tradition carriers**: provence7.com and a Beaucaire parish page (now HTTP 410, seen via search snippet) claim she was "of the noble French family of Bourlemont, **originally from Tarascon**," her marriage to Pietro Bernardone c. 1180 explained by the Beaucaire fairs; devotional sites (roman-catholic-saints.com "Blessed Pica Bernardone"; sanfrancescopatronoditalia.it) repeat the Provence claim.
- **Genealogy-crowd carriers**: Geni ("Donna Pica Petronella Bernardone (Bourlemont), 1160–1230"), WikiTree, Wikidata Q110856621.
- **No source found connects her to the Lorraine seigneurs de Bourlémont of Frebécourt**, and none of the medieval-documented family members above has any Provence or Italy link in the 12th century. An alternative etymology in circulation derives "Pica" from *Picardy*. Recorded, not adjudicated.
- **The taller corpus supplies the connection this census lacks** (taken on faith; mapped in pica_dossier.md, Layer 6): the essays' saga makes Pica (Giovanna) a cousin of **Pierre** — who sits where Gen. 4's Pierre II (father of Joffroy) sits — and its **Joffrey** (born ~1211, Greccio 1223, Treaty of 1229, Thibault de Champagne's crusade, home 1244 to build the well and tower at the fairy tree) lands almost exactly on Gen. 5's attested **Joffroy** (c. 1210–†1268, sénéchal de Navarre of Thibaut IV, Holy Land return, and the 28 May 1248 charter over Domremy and Greux). The saga's **Lois de Conte** (Twain's narrator, born Neufchâteau ~1411) closes on Gen. 9's seam: Pierre V dead childless in 1412, buried with the Franciscans of Neufchâteau, the year of Joan's birth.
