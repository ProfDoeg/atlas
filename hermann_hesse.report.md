# Hermann Hesse (1877-1962), research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `hermann_hesse`. Queue entry: "Hermann Hesse, 10 documents, German-Swiss novelist."*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition without a verifiable primary · *flagged* sources disagree and the disagreement is left standing.

**Method.** Worked from a verified research pool, rechecked adversarially against both Wikipedias, Nominatim, nobelprize.org and four Project Gutenberg plaintexts. Fifteen corrections resulted, eleven of them coordinate errors, this subject's characteristic failure mode: small place names, sources giving towns rather than addresses, points drifting one to three kilometres.

## 1. Calw and Basel (1877-1890)

- **Born 2 July 1877, Marktplatz 6, Calw, Kingdom of Württemberg [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB Hesse Chronology; hermann-hesse.de; address confirmed by de.wikipedia "Calw"**]**. Father Johannes Hesse, Baltic German missionary turned publisher for the Calw mission press; mother Marie Gundert, daughter of the Indologist Hermann Gundert.
- **1881, family to Basel [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB**]**, the father called to edit the mission magazine.
- ***Contradiction, unresolved.*** Return to Calw: **1886** (UCSB) versus **1887** (en.wikipedia). 1886 carried, discrepancy in `date_confidence`.
- **1886-1890 the Reallyzeum at Calw [A:** de.wikipedia**]**; **1890-1891 the Lateinschule at Göppingen [A:** UCSB**]**, preparing the Württemberg Landexamen.

## 2. Maulbronn and the year of institutions (1891-1893)

- **Enters the seminary at Maulbronn Abbey, 1891 [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB; hermann-hesse.de**]**. *Precision reduced:* the pool's 15 September is unsupported, both Wikipedias giving the year only. Stop dated 1891-09-01. **Gap:** matriculation day not established.
- **March 1892, flees, found in a field next day [A:** en.wikipedia**]**. *Flagged:* UCSB and de.wikipedia give 7 March, en.wikipedia the month only. Dated to the month.
- **May 1892, suicide attempt, placed under Christoph Friedrich Blumhardt at Bad Boll [A:** both Wikipedias**]**. ***Internal contradiction in the pool, flagged:*** its chronology says May, its own geography entry says "he attempts suicide that June." May used, June recorded.
- **Then the Nervenheilanstalt at Stetten im Remstal, a boys' institution in Basel, the Gymnasium at Bad Cannstatt [A:** both Wikipedias; UCSB**]**; **1893 passes the Einjährigen-Examen [A:** en.wikipedia**]**, ending his schooling at fifteen. Stetten is named in the Cannstatt campa rather than pinned, for stop-count reasons.

## 3. The bookshops (1894-1901)

- **1894, bookseller's apprenticeship at Esslingen am Neckar, abandoned within days [A:** UCSB**]**, named in the Calw factory campa, no pin. **1894-1895, fourteen months at the Perrot tower-clock factory, Calw [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB; hermann-hesse.de**]**. **Gap:** factory site within Calw not pinpointed; town-centre coordinate used, approximation declared.
- **From 17 October 1895, the Heckenhauer bookshop, Tübingen [A:** hermann-hesse.de; UCSB**]**, three years apprentice then a year as assistant.
- **1896 "Madonna" and *Romantische Lieder*; 1898 *Eine Stunde hinter Mitternacht* from Eugen Diederichs [A:** en.wikipedia**]**.
- **Autumn 1899, Basel [A:** UCSB; de.wikipedia**]**. ***Correction of substance:*** the pool called this post antiquarian. It was the **Reich'sche Buchhandlung, an ordinary bookshop**; the **Antiquariat Wattenwyl came in August 1901** (de.wikipedia). The pool compounded the error by dating Wattenwyl to April 1901, which would also collide with the Italian journey. Both corrected.
- **1900, exempted from military service, eye condition [A:** en.wikipedia**]**.

## 4. Italy, 1901

**First Italian journey, March to May 1901 [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB**]**: Milan, Genoa, Florence, Bologna, Ravenna, Padua, Venice.

**Gap, declared.** UCSB gives the stop list, no per-stop dates. The journey file pins Florence, Ravenna and Venice, names Milan and Genoa inside the Florence campa, spreads the dates across the attested window in geographic order, and says in every `date_confidence` that they are ordered reconstruction. Milan, Bologna and Padua dropped as pins for stop-count reasons, not evidentiary ones.

**1902, his mother dies after a long illness; he does not attend the funeral [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB**]**. No pin: no place fixed beyond "Germany."

## 5. Gaienhofen (1904-1912)

- **1904 *Peter Camenzind* (S. Fischer), the breakthrough, later praised by Freud; marries Maria Bernoulli, of the Basel mathematicians, her father absent [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB**]**. **10 August 1904 settles at Gaienhofen on the Bodensee; rented farmhouse, from 1907 a house built to order; there until 1912 [A:** hermann-hesse.de; de.wikipedia**]**. Sons **Bruno 1905, Heiner 1909, Martin 1911 [A:** UCSB**]**. ***Unterm Rad*** **1906; *Gertrud* 1910, which he calls "a miscarriage"; *Rosshalde* 1914 [A:** en.wikipedia**]**.
- **April 1907, a cure at Locarno and a visit to Monte Verità above Ascona [A:** de.wikipedia**]**, merged into one stop. **Corpus pin inherited:** the atlas carries Monte Verità at **46.1608, 8.7658** (`laban.journey.json`), and the Hesse stop takes it byte-identical instead of the pool's 46.1567, 8.7647, so the hill is one point. Jung's Eranos stop stays separately at 46.146, 8.755: Moscia is a different place below the hill.

## 6. The voyage east (1911)

**September to December 1911, to Ceylon and the Dutch East Indies with the painter Hans Sturzenegger [A:** UCSB; both Wikipedias**]**. He finds it spiritually disappointing.

***Correction of substance.*** The pool listed "Sri Lanka, Sumatra/Indonesia, Borneo, and Burma." **Burma is unsupported:** de.wikipedia states flatly *"Dabei war er auf seiner Reise nie in Indien"* and lists only Colombo, Kandy, nearby temples, Pidurutalagala and Indonesia. Burma appears in en.wikipedia alone, uncorroborated by the route given for the voyage. **Dropped.** The corollary, that **Hesse never reached India proper**, is stated in two campas.

- **Genoa, the *Prinz Eitel Friedrich*, 6 September 1911 [R:** straitsindie.substack.com, "Herman Hesse's Asia," a single secondary blog; neither Wikipedia corroborates ship or day**]**.
- **Penang 27 September 1911**; **Singapore**, with Ipoh, Kuala Lumpur and Johor named in its campa; **Medan / Belawan, Sumatra**, where a stomach illness cancels the hoped-for passage to southern India; **Colombo** (also en.wikipedia); **Kandy and the Temple of the Tooth**. All **[A]**, all straitsindie unless noted.
- ***Contradiction, unresolved.*** The pool dates Colombo **September 1911** but gives the voyage order Genoa, Suez, Penang, Malaya, Singapore, Sumatra, **Ceylon**, putting Ceylon last, which cannot hold with a 27 September arrival at Penang. The journey file follows the route order, places Ceylon in November, and records the September dating.
- **Borneo [R]**, flagged twice: the claim rests on a Goodreads/Shambhala description of *Singapore Dream and Other Adventures* seen through search, not read, and de.wikipedia's itinerary omits Borneo. The circulating coordinate 1.5, 110.3333 falls at Kuching in **Sarawak**, the Brooke rajahs' kingdom in 1911, not the "coastal Dutch East Indies" of the summary. Stop and coordinate kept, relabelled, marked unverified.
- **Pidurutalagala** dropped as a pin (named in the Kandy campa). The pool's hedge about the mountain's identity can be retired, de.wikipedia naming *"den Berg Pidurutalagala"*. **Suez [A]** likewise dropped as a pin.

## 7. Bern and the war (1912-1919)

- **Late summer 1912, Bern, Melchenbühlweg 26 [A:** de.wikipedia, *"Spätsommer 1912"*; UCSB**]**; the street runs between roughly 46.940, 7.487 and 46.949, 7.477. **1914, volunteers, found unfit, assigned to prisoner-of-war welfare and the Bücherzentrale until 1919 [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB**]**. **3 November 1914, "O Freunde, nicht diese Töne" in the *Neue Zürcher Zeitung* [A:** en.wikipedia**]**.
- **8 March 1916, his father dies [A:** en.wikipedia; *flagged*, UCSB gives the year only, so the day rests on en.wikipedia alone**]**.
- **1916, Martin's illness, Maria's schizophrenia, psychotherapy near Lucerne with a Jungian analyst [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB**]**. The analyst's name **Josef Bernhard Lang** and the session count were **not in the verified pool**; the campa names Lang, the one deliberate extension beyond the pool, tagged **[R]**. Lucerne's 47.0502, 8.3093 is standard geocoding, likewise not from the pool.
- **September to October 1917, *Demian* written in about six weeks [A]**; **1919 published as by Emil Sinclair [A:** en.wikipedia; UCSB**]**.

## 8. Ticino (1919-1962)

***Date and place contradiction, flagged and split.*** The pool attaches **25 April 1919** to the **Minusio** farmhouse; the sources forbid that pairing. de.wikipedia has him moving to Ticino *"Mitte April 1919"* and *"am 25. April nach Sorengo"*; en.wikipedia has him at Sorengo "from 25 April to 11 May." So mid-April at Minusio, then Sorengo, then Montagnola from 11 May. The arrival stop is dated 1919-04-15, both places named, the misjoined variant recorded.

**11 May 1919, four rooms in the Casa Camuzzi; painting begins [A:** en.wikipedia; hermann-hesse.de; de.wikipedia**]**. Here *Klingsors letzter Sommer*, *Siddhartha*, *Der Steppenwolf*.

**Coordinate: three rival points, resolved toward the corpus.** Nominatim gives 45.9830, 8.9184 for the village, but **the atlas already pins Montagnola at 45.97, 8.942** across three stops of `serrano.journey.json`, confirmed by the interlock lens. The journey file inherits **45.97, 8.942** byte-identical for every Montagnola stop, so Hesse's house and Serrano's pilgrimage to it are one point. **This is corpus coherence chosen over geocoding accuracy, recorded as such;** a future re-pinning should move both travelers together. The Casa Rossa, a different building, keeps its circulating point 45.9975, 8.95, not independently geocoded.

**Chronology of the Montagnola decades, all [A], en.wikipedia corroborated by the UCSB chronology throughout.** *Klingsors letzter Sommer* 1920; *Siddhartha* 1922; Swiss citizenship and divorce from Maria 1923; marriage to Ruth Wenger 1924; *Kurgast* 1925; divorce from Ruth, *Die Nürnberger Reise* and *Der Steppenwolf* 1927; winters at Arosa with Ninon 1928 and 1929 (de.wikipedia); *Narziss und Goldmund* 1930; marriage to Ninon Dolbin, née Ausländer, and the move into the Bodmer house 14 November 1931; *Die Morgenlandfahrt* 1932; help to Brecht and Thomas Mann into exile and reviews of banned Jewish authors including Kafka from 1933; *Das Glasperlenspiel* 1943, published in Switzerland after German houses refuse it; Goethe and Nobel Prizes 1946; Wilhelm Raabe Prize 1950; Peace Prize of the German Book Trade 1955; death 9 August 1962 (hermann-hesse.de; both Wikipedias).

***Corrections, Ninon and Arosa.*** The pool folded "begins cohabiting with Ninon Dolbin" into 1927. Not settled: en.wikipedia's timeline places it alongside *Narziss und Goldmund* in 1930, de.wikipedia gives no start year at all, only the winters of 1928 and 1929 before the 14 November 1931 marriage. The 1927 claim is dropped and Ninon enters at the Arosa stop, hedged. The pool also dated the Arosa stays "1925 / 1928-1929" and glossed them "the mid-1920s"; de.wikipedia supports only *"1928 und 1929 ausgedehnte Winterferien in Arosa"*, with no support for 1925.

***Correction, the Nobel.*** Non-attendance was tagged [R]; it is now **[A]**: "Reasons of health prevented Hermann Hesse to travel to Stockholm" and "the prize was accepted by the envoy of the Swiss republic" (en.wikipedia, "1946 Nobel Prize in Literature"; announced 14 November, ceremony 10 December). The pool also called the prize **"jointly"** awarded; it was not, nobelprize.org giving "Prize share: 1/1." *The primary page was retrieved this pass:* nobelprize.org returns 403 to default clients and 200 with a browser User-Agent, and the 1946 summary carries the motivation quoted in the journey file, verbatim correct, so the "unverified firsthand" caveat is retired. The event's point in circulation was also mislabelled "Swedish Academy," which is Börshuset at Stortorget, roughly 59.3253, 18.0710, and is not the ceremony site either.

**Death and burial.** Dies 9 August 1962, buried 11 August at Sant'Abbondio, Gentilino **[A]**. ***Contradiction, unresolved, and a corpus error flagged.*** The UCSB timeline gives the death year as **1961**; the Hesse foundation site and both Wikipedias give **1962**, which the journey file uses, recording the variant. The same slip exists independently inside the atlas: `serrano.journey.json` says that after Jung's death on 6 June 1961 Hesse "follows him into the ground within two months." He outlived Jung by fourteen months. **Flagged here rather than silently corrected in another traveler's file.**

*Cemetery founding date dropped:* the claimed 1843 inauguration is uncorroborated, it.wikipedia "Gentilino" describing only a *cimitero monumentale neoclassico* and the church attested from 1140. The burial cluster holds: Hugo Ball died at Sant'Abbondio 14 September 1927 and Bruno Walter's burial there is confirmed (en.wikipedia, both).

## 9. Afterlife

- **Late 1967, the Sparrows become Steppenwolf [A:** en.wikipedia, "Steppenwolf (band)"**]**. ***Corrected twice:*** the pool dated the renaming 1968 and called the Sparrows a Los Angeles band; they were **Canadian, from Toronto**, reforming in Los Angeles in 1967, and Gabriel Mekler proposed the name "in late 1967." The "demo box" detail is unsupported, dropped. "Born to Be Wild" reached number two on the Billboard Hot 100 in August 1968.
- **1963, Leary calls Hesse the "poet of the interior journey" [R:** New Statesman 2018, citing Leary; not checked against Leary**]**. Used without attributing the phrase. **Some 30 million US paperbacks across the 1960s and 1970s [R:** swissinfo.ch's own estimate**]**, rendered as "tens of millions."
- **The 1951 Hilda Rosner *Siddhartha* [R:** EBSCO Research Starters and general chronology, **not verified against a primary bibliographic record**; an honest gap, since this hinge date of the American reception rests on repetition**]**. Unused.
- **March 1990, the Hermann-Hesse-Museum opens in the Haus Schüz, Marktplatz 30, Calw [A:** WhichMuseum; Tripadvisor**]**, 48.7136, 8.7358. **1997, the Museo Hermann Hesse opens in the Torre Camuzzi [A:** de.wikipedia "Montagnola"; museum site**]**, named in the 1971 Serrano campa rather than pinned.
- **Dropped for want of corroboration:** the eleven-station audioguide walk "On the Footsteps of Hermann Hesse," and the swissinfo "computer games" trajectory (headline framing only). Both stated as gaps.

## 10. Interlocks with the existing corpus

1. **`serrano`.** Three stops of `serrano.journey.json` name Hesse's Montagnola: the 1951 pilgrimage to the Casa Camuzzi, the lunch of 22 January 1961 at which Hesse says *"nothing happens by chance ... here only the right guests meet, this is the Hermetic Circle"*, and the 1971 exile in which Serrano becomes tenant of the same house. All three mirrored on the inherited pin.
2. **`jung`.** Two contacts: the 1916 Lucerne analysis with a pupil of Jung, which puts the Zurich vocabulary into *Demian*, and the corpus record that Serrano reached Seestrasse 228, Küsnacht, in May 1959 "from Hermann Hesse's door" (`jung.journey.json`, echoed in `serrano.journey.json`). Both Serrano campas name Jung.
3. **`laban`.** Shared ground, not a meeting: Hesse at Monte Verità April 1907, Laban on the same hill July 1913. The campa states the sequence and invents no encounter.
4. **`keyserling`.** Parallel, not contact. The Schule der Weisheit is founded at Darmstadt 23 November 1920, the Tagore week falls June 1921, *Siddhartha* appears 1922. Named as simultaneity, which is all it is.

Examined and **rejected**: **`kircher`** (Malta 1637, confessor to Landgrave Friedrich of Hesse-Darmstadt) and **`joseph_oppenheimer`** (financier to the Landgraves, 1724). Both concern the German state and dynasty of Hesse, not the novelist.

**Canonical pins.** No Hesse-adjacent stop in the corpus falls on the Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), the Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354), Paris (48.8566, 2.3522) or Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816); none is inherited, because none applies. `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` row 14 records Hesse in 10 documents, tied fourteenth among real persons; until now he existed only as a satellite of Jung and Serrano.

## 11. Quotations

Twelve of the forty-four stops carry a quote; all are verbatim.

**Verified by direct download and grep [A]:** *Siddhartha*, trans. Wyllie, Gutenberg #58344 (beliefs, line 898; "What I wanted to learn was my self," 1009-1011; Kamala on love, 1421; the ferryman, 1221-1226). *Steppenwolf*, Creighton rev. Mileck and Frenz, PDF text-extracted (the man-and-wolf line; the Magic Theater placard). *Demian*, Boni & Liveright 1923, Gutenberg #74222, translator uncredited (epigraph, 62-63; the Abraxas message, twice, 2845-2846 and 3193-3195).

***Correction, the river quote.*** The pool truncated it after "I love it above all else" while keeping a closing quotation mark, misrepresenting where Vasudeva's speech ends. The journey file quotes through "There is a great deal that you can learn from a river."

***Correction, the hatred quote.*** The circulating wording, "If you hate a person, you hate something in him that is part of yourself. What isn't part of ourselves doesn't disturb us," could **not** be verified in any reachable edition. The 1923 text reads: *"When we hate a man, we hate in him something which resides in us ourselves. What is not in us does not move us."* Chapter six, "Jacob Wrestles with God," is right, but **the speaker is Pistorius, not Demian**: the next paragraph has Sinclair marvelling that Pistorius "had struck the same note as Demian," so the usual attribution inverts Hesse's point. The verified wording is used, Pistorius named, at the Lucerne stop. **Inaccessible source:** archive.org's Roloff and Lebeck 1965 translation, full-text search lending-restricted, "Item not available."

**Rejected [R], unused:** *"Their Christianity, one not preached but lived, was the strongest of the powers that shaped and moulded me."* en.wikipedia footnote 49 cites only *Gesammelte Werke* (Suhrkamp 1951, p. 378); no translated edition with this wording was located.
