# Juan Antonio Buschiazzo (1845-1917) — research report
*Compiled 2026-08-14 for the Atlas of Journeys. Municipal architect of Buenos Aires under Torcuato de Alvear; the man who built the Recoleta gate and cut three arches off the Cabildo.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, attribution without a document, or an inference the cited source does not itself make. Contradictions between sources are printed side by side and left standing. Where a source could not be reached, it is named together with the reason.

A note on the base sources. Almost the whole biographical spine of this subject rests on two encyclopedia articles, es.wikipedia "Juan Antonio Buschiazzo" and en.wikipedia "Juan Antonio Buschiazzo", which disagree with each other on his qualification and are thin on everything before 1880. Where a building has its own article, that article is more specific than the biography and sometimes contradicts it; those cases are flagged individually below. The only material in this dossier that is Buschiazzo's own written voice comes from the tables of contents of the *Anales de la Sociedad Científica Argentina* on Wikisource. The article bodies behind those headings were not retrievable; only the index lines were.

---

## 1. Pontinvrea and the crossing

- Born **29 October 1845 in Pontinvrea**, in the Kingdom of Sardinia. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Juan Antonio Buschiazzo"]**
- **Correction to an earlier working note:** Pontinvrea is in **Liguria, province of Savona**, not Piedmont. **[A — it.wikipedia, "Pontinvrea"]** Both regions sat inside the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1845, so the political unit is right and only the regional label was wrong.
- **Coordinates.** it.wikipedia gives 44°26'41.15"N, 8°26'04.69"E = **44.4448, 8.4346**. Two different working coordinates were in circulation for this one town (44.4822 / 8.4658 and 44.4394 / 8.4728), off by roughly 4.8 km and 3.1 km respectively. Both are discarded. **[A]**
- The family **emigrates to Argentina when he is about four**. es.wikipedia says only "a los cuatro años" and gives no year; en.wikipedia gives **1850**. Born October 1845, age four spans October 1849 to October 1850. **The 1849 date used in earlier drafts is an inference no source makes; use 1850 or c.1850.** **[R]**
- Father a **maestro mayor albañil** (master mason); the family settles in the then-independent pueblo of **Belgrano**. **[A — es.wikipedia]**
- The parents' given names (Carlos; Margarita Bresciani) appear in working notes but **could not be found in the es.wikipedia text retrieved**. Recorded as unverified. **[R]**
- The **landing point** is a reconstruction. Buenos Aires in 1850 had no docks; passengers were rowed and carted ashore at the Bajo below the Plaza de Mayo, roughly -34.6035, -58.3680. Earlier working coordinates placed the arrival at the Dársena Sur, a basin dug decades later, 3.4 km south. **[R]**
- **Gap:** no passenger manifest naming this crossing has surfaced in anything reachable.

## 2. Apprenticeship, and a diploma nobody can date

- **1862**, aged seventeen, enters the studio of the Italian architect **Nicolás Canale and his son José**. **[A — en.wikipedia]** The studio's address does not survive in the sources consulted. **Gap.**
- **The qualification is a straight contradiction and is left unresolved:**
  - **en.wikipedia:** graduates **1869** from the Universidad de Buenos Aires in architecture and construction engineering, qualifying formally as architect in 1878.
  - **es.wikipedia:** graduates **1878** from the Facultad de Ciencias Físico-Matemáticas (founded 1874), the **second person ever to take a diploma from it**.
  - Both cannot be first. Nothing reachable adjudicates it. **[R]**
- **1873:** designs the **Casa Municipal de Belgrano** on Cuba street, a petit hôtel with six Roman Doric columns, Italian Renaissance and Mannerist in style. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Museo Histórico Sarmiento"]** Earlier notes gave 1874; the museum's own article says 1873. Either way the building precedes his 1878 diploma.
- Coordinates for that building: the museum article gives 34°33'39"S, 58°27'16"O = -34.5608, -58.4545. The atlas already carries a pin for this building at **-34.5628, -58.4566** through the Sarmiento file; the canonical corpus pin is kept for continuity and the discrepancy noted here. **[A / flagged]**
- Also lays out the **Saavedra** neighborhood in this early period; no date in any source consulted. **[A for the work, R for any date]**
- **1875:** joins the **Municipal Commission of Public Works of Belgrano**, his first public office. **[A — en.wikipedia]**
- **1879:** son **Juan Carlos Buschiazzo** born, later also an architect. **[A — en.wikipedia]**

## 3. The Alvear department, 1880

- **1880:** appointed director of the **Departamento de Obras Públicas** of the newly federalized Municipality of Buenos Aires. **[A — es. and en.wikipedia]**
- **Correction, load-bearing:** Torcuato de Alvear was **not intendente in 1880**. es.wikipedia, "Torcuato de Alvear", gives his intendencia as **10 May 1883 to 10 May 1887**; in 1880 he presided over the **Comisión Municipal** and became the city's first intendente only in 1883. Any sentence calling him "intendente" in 1880 is anachronistic. **[A]**
- **1881:** joins the commission overseeing construction of the new provincial capital at **La Plata**. **[A — en.wikipedia]**
- Designs the **Banco de la Provincia de Buenos Aires** building in La Plata and several provincial branches. **No date in any source consulted.** **[R]**

## 4. Recoleta

- **15 December 1880:** Alvear calls Buschiazzo in and hands him the Recoleta burial ground to survey and lay out as a planned necropolis. **[A — torcuato_de_alvear.journey.json, an attested direct link between the two men]**
- **1881:** brick perimeter wall, paved internal streets, neoclassical entrance portal with **four Doric columns**, inscribed *Requiescant in pace* and *Expectamus Dominum*. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Cementerio de la Recoleta"]**
- The cemetery pin **-34.5875, -58.3936** is already fixed and reused across at least sixteen journey files. Inherited byte-identical here. **[A]**
- **The Asilo de Mendigos site (today Centro Cultural Recoleta):**
  - The institution opened on the site **17 October 1858 as the Asilo de Mendigos**, asylum for **beggars**, under governor Valentín Alsina; it was renamed *Asilo de Ancianos General Viamonte* only in **1944**. Working notes that call it an "Asylum for the Elderly" in 1858 collapse the two names. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Centro Cultural Recoleta"]**
  - Buschiazzo, as municipal architect, builds the **Italianate access pavilion and the neo-Gothic chapel, 1881-1885**, with further interventions **1893-94 and 1897**. The date is not missing from the record, as earlier notes claimed. **[A]**
  - This also corrects a point-dated "1881" for the Paseo de la Recoleta reform: es.wikipedia, "Torcuato de Alvear", gives **no year at all** for that work. Use the 1881-1885 span. **[A / correction]**

## 5. The plaza, and the two demolitions of the Recova

The Recova demolition is the single most tangled date in this dossier and is **not** harmonized here.

- The Recova was built **1802-1804** (design by Agustín Conde, construction directed by Juan Bautista Segismundo). It had therefore divided the Plaza Mayor for **eighty years**, since the nineteenth century, not since the eighteenth. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Recova de Buenos Aires"]**
- **Stage one:** demolition of the municipally-owned **central arch begins 9 August 1883** and takes eight days. **[A — same source]**
- **Stage two:** the main structure, owned by the Anchorena family and requiring expropriation, comes down **8 to 14 May 1884**. **[A — same source]**
- The two plazas are united under a single name from **17 May 1884**, with 700 workers finishing in nine days. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Plaza de Mayo"]**
- The **contradiction in the biographies:** es.wikipedia "Torcuato de Alvear" gives **25 May 1883**, which matches neither stage; es.wikipedia "Juan Antonio Buschiazzo" gives **1884**. **Flagged, not resolved. [R]**
- **Hospital San Roque** (today Hospital Ramos Mejía) is firmer than earlier notes allowed: the complex "fue diseñado por el arquitecto municipal Juan Antonio Buschiazzo" and was inaugurated **12 August 1883**, the site in provisional use since an 1868 cholera outbreak. Coordinates 34°37'04"S, 58°24'35"O = **-34.6178, -58.4097**; earlier working coordinates were 1.3 km east, in the wrong part of the city. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Hospital General de Agudos Dr. José María Ramos Mejía"]**
- **1885:** builds the **second headquarters of the Bolsa de Comercio**, later absorbed by the Banco de la Nación and eventually demolished. **[R — en.wikipedia; no independent confirmation found, and no photograph located]**

## 6. The profession invents itself

- **18 March 1886:** founding member of the **Sociedad Central de Arquitectos**, one of nine, alongside Ernesto Bunge, Joaquín Mariano Belgrano and Adolfo Buttner. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Sociedad Central de Arquitectos"; en.wikipedia dates the founding to 1886]**
- **President 1888-1891 and again 1901-1902**; honorary president until his death. **[A — en.wikipedia]**
- **No award, medal, or hall within the society is documented as bearing his name.** **[R — argument from absence]**
- **His own written voice.** Five index headings from the *Anales de la Sociedad Científica Argentina* on Wikisource. The article bodies were **not retrievable**; only these lines were. Quoted here as printed, including one typesetting corruption:
  - Tomo 21 (1886): "Memoria descriptiva del proyecto para la construccion de una gran casa de inquilinato para el Banco Constructor de la Plata, por Juan A. Buschiazzo"
  - Tomo 27 (1889): "Informe sobre pavimentacion de la ciudad, presentado al señor Intendente de la Capital por los señores arquitectos Juan A. Buschiazzo y Pablo Biot é ingenieros Luis A. Huergo, Rómulo Otamendi y Guillermo White" — a five-signatory commission report, not a two-man document; earlier drafts truncated it mid-attribution without an ellipsis
  - Tomo 30 (1890): "Hospital de la Bolsa, por los señores Juan A. Buschiazzo y Telémaco Susiui" — "Susiui" is confirmed identically in the rendered page and the MediaWiki search index; it is an OCR or typesetting corruption of the physician **Telémaco Susini**, and is quoted as printed rather than silently emended
  - Tomo 37 (1894): "Memoria descriptiva del anteproyecto de hospital y asilo de niños expósitos, por Juan A. Buschiazzo"
  - Tomo 41 (1896): "Memoria descriptiva del proyecto de Nuevo Hospital Italiano actualmente en ejecucion, por Juan A. Buschiazzo"
  - The volume years are supplied from the volumes themselves; the individual index lines carry no dates. **[A for the headings, R for the per-article dating]**

## 7. The avenue and the Cabildo

- **Law 1,583 of 31 October 1884** authorizes Avenida de Mayo. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Avenida de Mayo"]**
- **25 May 1888:** demolition begins on the buildings ringing the plaza, including the Police House, **three arches of the Cabildo's north side**, and the Fire Barracks. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Avenida de Mayo"]**
- **1889:** es.wikipedia, "Cabildo de Buenos Aires", dates the same cut to 1889 and names "the engineer Juan Antonio Buschiazzo" as the man who ordered it, together with the removal of the ornamental tower Pedro Benoit had added a decade earlier. **The 1888 and 1889 dates for one event are left standing side by side. [R]**
- A third source disagrees again: torcuato_de_alvear.journey.json dates the Cabildo truncation to **1888-05-25** under Alvear's successors. Same event, three datings. **Flagged.**
- **Avenida de Mayo is not a diagonal.** It runs straight west from the plaza along the old Rivadavia and Victoria line, which is exactly why it had to be cut through the middle of built blocks; Buschiazzo championed that alignment. Any description of it as a diagonal is wrong. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Avenida de Mayo"]**
- **Inaugurated 9 July 1894.** **[A]**
- **Serious misidentification corrected.** The building es.wikipedia "Avenida de Mayo" credits to Buschiazzo for 1891-1902, "de impronta italiana y mansarda afrancesada", is the **municipal Palacio de Gobierno at Bolívar and Avenida de Mayo**, the city hall. It is **not** the Casa Rosada. es.wikipedia "Casa Rosada" credits Francesco Tamburini, Henrik Åberg and Carl Kihlberg and never mentions Buschiazzo. **[A]**

## 8. The disputed attributions

Three works are widely credited to him on grounds that the buildings' own articles do not support. None is resolved here.

1. **Cementerio de la Chacarita (1887).** es.wikipedia's Chacarita article **never names Buschiazzo at all**, crediting only Itala Fulvia Villa for the underground mausoleums built nearly a century later. The attribution comes from the research brief and general repute. **[R]**
2. **Villa Devoto (13 April 1889).** es.wikipedia, "Villa Devoto", credits the plan to engineers "**Carlos Buschiazzo y José Poggi**", not to Juan Antonio. The same article separately and explicitly credits the 1913 **Palacio Devoto** to "Juan Antonio Buschiazzo", so it distinguishes the two names deliberately. That weakens the convenient reading that "Carlos" is a transcription slip. **[R]**
3. **Hospital Italiano (1901 or 1902).** The hospital's own article gives **1902** for the inauguration of the Almagro headquarters, built on land that had belonged to Dalmacio Vélez Sarsfield, and **names no architect in its body text**; Buschiazzo appears only in its category tags. The 1901 date comes from the research brief. **[R]**

## 9. Works with firm dates, later career

- **Palacio de Aguas Corrientes**, built **1887-1894** with 400 workers, faced in 300,000 enameled terracotta pieces imported from Great Britain; National Historic Monument in 1989 by decree 325. Buschiazzo is one of the "renombrados arquitectos locales" who worked with the Norwegian Olaf Boye and the Swedish engineer Carlos Nyströmer, **a collaborator and not the author**. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Palacio de Aguas Corrientes"]**
- **Iglesia de la Merced:** "En 1894, comenzó una remodelación total del edificio, cuya fachada fue modificada por el arquitecto Juan Antonio Buschiazzo"; facade inaugurated **24 April 1900**. The date is documented, contrary to earlier notes that called it lost. Coordinates -34.6054, -58.3721 (Reconquista 207). **[A — es.wikipedia, "Basílica de Nuestra Señora de la Merced (Buenos Aires)"]**
- **Mercado de San Telmo, 1897**, central body for Antonio Devoto, entrance at Bolívar and Carlos Calvo, Tuscan order, iron and glass roof; two further wings added 1930. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Mercado de San Telmo"]**
- **1908-1910:** sits on the **International Centennial Exposition Commission**. **[R — en.wikipedia only]**
- **1909-1912:** **Hospital Durand**. **[A — es. and en.wikipedia]**
- **1913:** **Palacio Devoto** for Antonio Devoto and Elina Pombo, over 10,000 square metres. Devoto dies in 1916, Pombo in 1923, and **neither ever lives in the finished house**. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Antonio Devoto"]** Its demolition date and cause are documented nowhere reachable. **Gap.**
- **1913:** **Hospital Centenario, Gualeguaychú**. **[A — es.wikipedia]**
- **Undated attributions**, listed without dates in the biography: Hospital Muñiz, Hospital Rivadavia, Iglesia de la Inmaculada Concepción de Belgrano, Iglesia de la Piedad, Patronato de la Infancia, Hospital San Martín at Paraná, Hospital San Martín at Mar del Plata, the cemetery of Gualeguay, Hospital Vicente López y Planes at General Rodríguez, Villa Alvear (later Palermo Viejo), and early planning toward Avenida 9 de Julio. **[A for the list, R for every date]** For the provincial works, coordinates in this dossier are **town centres, not confirmed building sites**, and are marked as such in the journey file.
- One window needs widening: the summary that Buschiazzo's hospital work runs "between 1888 and 1910" opens too late, since Hospital San Roque was inaugurated **12 August 1883**. Use c.1883-1912. **[A]**

## 10. Death and afterlife

- Dies in **Buenos Aires, 13 May 1917**, aged 71. **[A — es. and en.wikipedia]**
- Buried in the **Cementerio de la Recoleta**, behind his own gate. **[A]** **No source located names a plot number, describes a bóveda, or documents the tomb's present condition. Gap.**
- His will contains a **special provision for the education of his nephew Mario Buschiazzo**. es.wikipedia calls Juan Antonio "su tío", his uncle, not a looser relative. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Mario Buschiazzo"]**
- **An impossible sentence corrected.** Mario Buschiazzo was born **10 December 1902**, so in 1917 he was **fourteen**, a schoolboy. Any text describing him in 1917 as "an architectural historian barely into his career" is chronologically impossible. **[A]**
- Mario carries out the **first scientific restoration of a national historical monument in Argentina** on the Cabildo. Work runs **1940-1960**; **11 October 1940** is the inauguration of the first phase, not the completion. He rebuilt the tower in reinforced concrete, **deliberately reduced in size relative to the colonial original** because full colonial height would have looked disproportionate. He did **not** rebuild "from Benoit's original plans": Benoit's tower was the 1879 addition the restoration was undoing. **[A — es.wikipedia, "Cabildo de Buenos Aires"]**
- In **1931** the remaining three southern arches were demolished too, over a press campaign by La Nación. **[A — same source]**
- **1991-1992:** the archaeologist Daniel Schavelzon excavates beneath the Cabildo, describing the building above ground as "cut back twice by street widening". One of those cuts is Buschiazzo's. **[A — daniel_schavelzon.journey.json]**
- **No street, plaza, museum or public monument in Buenos Aires bearing his own name** was located in any source consulted; nor does the Italian Wikipedia article on Pontinvrea mention him. Both are **arguments from absence, not confirmed negatives**: a plaque or minor street could exist that these sources omit. **[R]**
