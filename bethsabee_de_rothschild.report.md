# Bethsabée (Batsheva) de Rothschild, 1914-1999, research report

*Prepared 2026-08-09 for the Atlas of Journeys. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named inline · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or a pin supplied by the compiler rather than a source. Contradictions are flagged and left open, not adjudicated.*

She existed in the corpus only inside one stop of `martha_graham.journey.json`, the 24 December 1964 founding of Batsheva. `QUEUE.md` line 180 calls her "a direct interlock between the banking cluster and the dance wing." Everything on either side of that stop was an atlas gap until this pass.

---

## 1. The Paris house (1905-1914)

- Parents **Édouard Alphonse James de Rothschild** and **Germaine Alice Halphen** marry in Paris, 1 March 1905. **[A: English Wikipedia]**
- Édouard takes over **de Rothschild Frères** in 1905, on the death of his father Alphonse a few months after the wedding. **[A: same]**
- He is **régent of the Banque de France, 1906-1936**, succeeding his father in the post. **[A: French Wikipedia, same subject]** *Corrected:* the pool called the office a directorship and a late-career addition. It is the regency, and it begins right after the 1905 succession.
- 1911: he sells the family's **Azerbaijan oil fields to Royal Dutch Shell**. **[A: English Wikipedia]** Year only.
- 1911: her elder brother **Édouard Alphonse Émile Lionel (1906-1911)** dies of appendicitis after emergency surgery at Ferrières. **[A: English Wikipedia for cause and age, French Wikipedia for place and operation]** *Corrected:* the pool said age four. He died at **five**. "Three years before her birth" is right.
- **Bethsabée Louise Émilie Béatrice de Rothschild** born **London, 23 September 1914**, fourth child. **[A: English and French Wikipedia]**
  - **Gap:** no reachable source explains why a French Rothschild is born in London seven weeks into the war. The pool's claim that the war "scatters the French branch from Paris" is in neither Wikipedia and has been struck. **[R at best; not carried into the journey file]**
- The family returns to **Paris shortly after the birth**. **[A: French Wikipedia]** *Corrected:* the pool dated this to 1918-1919, after the armistice; the source says shortly after September 1914.

## 2. Ferrières and the Hôtel de Talleyrand (1920s-1930s)

- Childhood split between the **Château de Ferrières** and the Paris residence, with siblings **Guy** and **Jacqueline** (later Piatigorsky). **[A: English Wikipedia]**
- Ferrières: built by **Joseph Paxton** for Baron James on the Mentmore Towers model; **central hall 120 ft long and 60 ft high under a glass skylight**; **library of 8,000 volumes**. **[A: English Wikipedia, "Château de Ferrières"]** *Corrected:* the pool put the 8,000 volumes in the central hall, conflating two rooms, and pinned the chateau 5.9 km ENE of itself. Correct point **48.8197, 2.7119**.
- The Paris house is the **Hôtel de Saint-Florentin / Hôtel de Talleyrand, 2 rue Saint-Florentin**, on the Place de la Concorde: bought by James 1838, to Alphonse 1857, **inherited by Édouard 1906 and lived in until the occupation**; leased by the United States from 1948 and bought outright 14 November 1950. **[A: French Wikipedia, "Hôtel de Saint-Florentin"]** *Corrected:* the pool tagged this **[R]**, "remembered as". It is corroborated. Upgraded to **[A]**, coordinates **48.8666, 2.3236**.
- **Biology degree at the Sorbonne.** **[A: English and French Wikipedia]** No source gives the **year**; c. 1935 is the compiler's estimate. **[R for the date]**

## 3. The fall of France and the war (1940-1944)

- June 1940: the family flees, reaching **New York via Lisbon**. **[A: English Wikipedia]** Ship, port and day unrecorded. **[R for anything finer]**
- The Nazis **confiscate her father's art collection**. **[A: English Wikipedia]**
- In New York she obtains **attestations** for European refugees settling in the United States. **[A: French Wikipedia]**
- **Columbia University**: chemistry, biochemistry, biology, no degree completed. **[A: English Wikipedia]** Dates c. 1940-1942 **[R]**.
- She **enlists with the Free French** in New York. **[A: French Wikipedia]** *Tag downgraded:* the pool pinned this to Rockefeller Center at **[A]** while its own note conceded that no source gives an address. **[R]**
- Posted to **London** as liaison officer between French and American forces. **[A: French Wikipedia]** *Tag downgraded:* the pool placed her at **4 Carlton Gardens** at **[A]** while conceding the source does not name the building. **[R for the building]**
- **Normandy, summer 1944.** English Wikipedia: "part of the landing force for the Battle of Normandy". The Batsheva site: she "participated in the Normandy invasion".
  - **Largest correction in the pass.** The pool dated this **1944-06-06** and framed it as D-Day. No source places her ashore on 6 June. The battle runs 6 June to late August; women were not in the 6 June assault waves, and Free French women such as the Rochambelles landed in August. Invented precision. The journey file carries summer 1944, day unrecorded, no D-Day framing. **[A for the campaign, [R] for anything narrower]**
- **August 1944:** liaison during the **liberation of Paris**; her parents return to France after it. **[A: English and French Wikipedia]**

## 4. The Graham studio and the American marriage (1946-1954)

- After the war she enrols at the **Martha Graham school** in New York and becomes the company's patron: operations, filmed documentation, and eventually a permanent home. **[A: English and French Wikipedia; Batsheva site]** Enrolment year unattested; c. 1946 **[R]**.
- **The jade necklace.** Robert Cohan, one of Graham's leading dancers, describes first seeing her at the studio, noticing "a huge, beautiful jade necklace" and being told: "This jade is one of the few things I managed to save from our house in Paris when Hitler conquered France..." The full passage is carried verbatim in the journey file. **[A: verified verbatim against the source PDF; Manor, *Dance Today* no. 27 (2014), p. 71, full citation under Sources]**
  - *Date corrected:* the pool said "c. early 1940s". Manor has Cohan in military service during the war and back in the company **after demobilization** before this meeting. **c. 1946.** The early 1940s is her enrolment, a different event.
- **The cheque.** Manor: "Batsheva simply took out her checquebook and wrote a cheque for the sum needed. She was, after all, 'one of the real Rothschilds'..." One year in the late 1940s, rescuing a threatened Graham season. **[A: verified verbatim]**
  - *Citation corrected:* p. **70**, not p. 71. "One of the real Rothschilds" is **Manor's own narratorial phrase**, not anybody's speech. **[R as an utterance]**
- She marries **Donald Bloomingdale**, grandson of Lyman G. Bloomingdale, co-founder of the store, in **1948**. **[A: English Wikipedia]**
  - **Struck:** the pool called him "an attaché at the American embassy in Paris" and inferred a Paris wedding from that posting. Neither Wikipedia gives him an occupation; French Wikipedia gives only 1913-1954. Paris pin dropped, the stop placed in New York with the location flagged unattested. **[R]** "American businessman" is likewise unsupported.
- **CONTRADICTION, LEFT OPEN, the divorce.** English Wikipedia: **1951**. French Wikipedia: "Donald Bloomingdale (né en 1913, mort en 1954). Elle en divorce par la suite", placing the divorce **after his 1954 death**, incoherent as written. The journey file carries 1951 with the contradiction in `date_confidence`.
- Her father **dies in Paris, 30 June 1949**. **[A: English Wikipedia]**

## 5. The turn to Israel (1951-1962)

- **1951:** first visit to Israel. **[A: English and French Wikipedia]**
- **1956:** assistant costumer on a State Department-financed Graham tour of the Far East, she pays personally to extend it to Israel. **[A: French Wikipedia]**
  - *Venue downgraded:* the pool named the **Habima Theatre** at **[A]** while conceding the source names no venue and Habima was supplied as "the era's principal stage". A reconstruction; **[R]**, not carried. Its coordinates were wrong too: Habima is at **32.0728, 34.7790**. **[A: English Wikipedia]**
  - Cohan on the arrival: at every airport on the tour the photographers looked at her "for two seconds and concentrate on Martha again", but at Lod, "when they realized they were in front of a genuine Rothschild the cameras kept clicking... She realized that her name was a name to conjure with in Israel..." Full passage verbatim in the journey file. **[A: Manor, p. 70]**
- **1957:** founds the **Israeli Chamber Music Society** under **Gary Bertini**. **[A: French Wikipedia]**
- **1958:** establishes the first **Martha Graham summer course** at the Jerusalem music academy. **[A: French Wikipedia]**
  - *Location corrected, anachronism:* the pool placed this at **Givat Ram**, where the academy did not yet stand. Per English Wikipedia it was on Hillel Street until 1958, when Samuel Rubin's money bought a Smolenskin Street building in **Rehavia** and it became the Rubin Academy. Repinned to central Jerusalem, c. 31.7746, 35.2145 **[R for the point]**.
- **1962:** settles permanently in Israel, takes the Hebrew name **Batsheva**. **[A: English and French Wikipedia]**
- **1962:** the **Fondation Batsheva de Rothschild** under **François Shapira**, buying music libraries and funding translation of ancient Hebrew texts; the company site calls it the **Batsheva de Rothschild Foundation for Arts and Sciences**. **[A: French Wikipedia; Batsheva site]**
  - *Corrected:* the pool dated two foundations "for science and technology" to 1965 with no source. English Wikipedia gives two foundations and **no year**. The dated, named one is the 1962 arts-and-sciences foundation. **Gap:** the second is unidentified.
- **CONTRADICTION, LEFT OPEN, the year she moved.** English Wikipedia: visit 1951, permanent settlement 1962. **Batsheva company site: moved to Israel in 1956.**
  - *Note:* the pool framed this as 1951 versus 1956, a misreading; English Wikipedia never gives 1951 as an immigration date. The divergence is **1962 versus 1956**.

## 6. Batsheva and Bat-Dor (1964-1975)

- **24 December 1964:** the **Batsheva Dance Company** founded in Tel Aviv, **Martha Graham** artistic adviser, de Rothschild chief executive and sole endowment. **[A: English Wikipedia; corroborated in `martha_graham.journey.json`]**
- Graham gives **seven works outright** and choreographs **one exclusively**; the dancers are **the first outside her own company licensed to perform her repertory**. **[A: English Wikipedia]**
- **1965:** **Jeannette Ordman**, South African-born, London-trained, arrives in Israel. **[A: English Wikipedia]** *Corrected:* the pool used 1965 as the year the two **met**. The source reads "In the mid-1960s, she met" Ordman, "who had come to Israel in 1965 from London". Two facts; the meeting date is **not given**. **[R]**
- **1967:** the **Bat-Dor studios**, with Ordman. **[A: French Wikipedia]**
- **1968:** the **Bat-Dor Dance Company** debuts with Ordman as leading dancer and artistic director, taking work from **Alvin Ailey** and **Hans van Manen** alongside Israelis. **[A: English Wikipedia]**
- **1969:** **Jerome Robbins** stages *Moves* for Batsheva and tells Manor that the companies he works with are usually terrified of him and this one is not, quoted verbatim in the journey file. **[A: Manor, p. 67]** About the company, not her.
- **1973, her Tel Aviv office:** she asks Manor, in his account imperiously, "How could you write I had no right to terminate the existence of the company I had founded and am financing?" He answers with an analogy of a mother killing her own infant and the police coming for her: "after ten years it has a right to live and you as its 'parent' have no right to kill it." **[A: Manor, pp. 65-66]** Manor states he recounts the exchange **from memory**; the caveat is his and is preserved.
- **1974:** she appoints **Ordman artistic director of Batsheva**; **1975:** she **withdraws her funding**, ends the Graham relationship and redirects everything to Bat-Dor. **[A: English Wikipedia; Batsheva site]**
- **CONTRADICTION OF CAUSE, LEFT OPEN.** English Wikipedia: the trigger is **dancers complaining to Graham about Ordman**. The Batsheva company's own site: "When plans to consolidate Batsheva with the Bat-Dor Company failed, she ceased supporting Batsheva Dance Company in 1975." Complementary rather than exclusive, but the **failed merger is the proximate cause on the company's own account** and the pool omitted it. Both carried.
- Withdrawal of Graham rights: **Ron Protas** and **Linda Hodes**, as characterized by Manor, "if you wish to see Graham works performed, you'll have to come to us." **[R: Manor gives this as stated policy and the phrasing may be paraphrase; flagged as such in the journey file]**
- Batsheva then runs under Israeli directors, judged technically strong and choreographically unadventurous. **[A: English Wikipedia]**

## 7. Later foundations, the prize, the death (1975-1999)

- **1975:** a **Pilates institute in Beer-Sheva**. **[A: French Wikipedia]** *Source corrected:* the pool credited English Wikipedia, where it does not appear. Its coordinates were right.
- **1985:** the **Israeli Center for Dance Therapy**. **[A: French Wikipedia]** Same source misattribution corrected. **Gap:** no source gives the city.
- **1989:** the **Israel Prize**, for special contribution to society and the State of Israel. **[A: English and French Wikipedia]** The wording quoted as the official citation ("for her unique contribution to the society and country") is secondary-site repetition; the **gazette was not fetched**, so it is **[R]**. Venue unspecified.
- **1989:** the **Suzanne Dellal Centre** opens in Neve Tzedek and becomes Batsheva's home; it takes its own Israel Prize in 2010, for having "succeeded in uplifting the art of dance in Israel". **[A: English Wikipedia]** *Coordinates corrected to **32.0608, 34.7642**.* The pool carried two different wrong values, one of them outside Neve Tzedek.
- **1990:** **Ohad Naharin** becomes artistic director and develops **Gaga**. **[A: English Wikipedia]**
- **20 April 1999:** dies at home in **Tel Aviv**, aged 84, after a lengthy illness. **[A: English and French Wikipedia]**
- Buried at **Yarkon Cemetery**, Petah Tikva, a secular civil cemetery. **[A: French and English Wikipedia]** *Coordinates corrected to **32.1156, 34.8700**.* The pool's afterlife lens was 3.2 km off and contradicted its own geography lens, which had it right. **Gap:** no plot, marker, photograph or epitaph located.
- **2000:** the collection from her grandfather **Alphonse** is sold at **Christie's, King Street, London, Sale 6407**: Rembrandt's *Portrait of a Lady* to **Robert Noortman** for **US$28.7 m**, a thirteenth-century **Mamluk jug** at **£3,307,750**, a world record for Islamic glass, two mosque lamps at £1,763,750 and £641,750. **[A: English Wikipedia]** *Corrected:* the pool dated the sale 1999 in one lens, "circa 1999-2000, unknown venue" in another, tagged **[R]**. The year is **2000**, the venue known, the detail on a page already cited. Upgraded to **[A]**; Christie's lot records remain unfetched.
- **July 2006:** the **Bat-Dor Dance Company ceases operations** for want of her funding. **[A: English Wikipedia]** Batsheva, cut off in 1975, survives her; Bat-Dor, funded to the end, does not.
- Her sister **Jacqueline de Rothschild Piatigorsky** dies 15 July 2012 at her **Brentwood** home in Los Angeles, aged 100; married to **Gregor Piatigorsky** from 1937, played chess for the United States at the first Women's Olympiad (Emmen, 1957). **[A: English Wikipedia, "Jacqueline Piatigorsky"]** *Corrected:* the pool had this unverified **[R]**, in Pacific Palisades. Family context; not a stop.

## Open gaps, stated as gaps

1. Why she was born in London. No source explains it; the pool's wartime explanation is unsourced and struck.
2. The **second foundation**. English Wikipedia says two; only one is named and dated anywhere.
3. The **city of the 1985 dance therapy centre**.
4. Her **grave**: cemetery only, no plot, marker or epitaph.
5. The **date and unit** of her Normandy service inside June-August 1944.
6. The **date she and Ordman met**, as distinct from Ordman's 1965 arrival.
7. **Donald Bloomingdale's occupation** and the **place of the 1948 marriage**.
8. The **Sorbonne graduation year**.
