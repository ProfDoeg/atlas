# Hesiod of Ascra: research report

*2026-08-22. Boeotia. Floruit c. 700 BCE, range c. 750-650 BCE. Journey file: `hesiod.journey.json`, 9 segments, 39 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** tradition or attributed scholarly position · **[gap]** nothing found.

Two structural facts govern everything below. **The biography is autobiography, in verse:** four passages supply nearly every fact, the father's migration (WD 633-640), the quarrel with Perses (WD 27-41), the Muses on Helicon (Theogony 22-34), and the crossing to Chalcis (WD 646-662). No contemporary inscription survives, and no third-party notice earlier than Thucydides. **The death legend is a snowball:** Thucydides 3.96 gives only a killing at a Locrian sanctuary of Nemean Zeus and an oracle foretelling it, while the seduced girl, the named killers, the dolphins, Troilus, the crow, and the plague at Orchomenus are attested five to nine centuries later and contradict each other. They are kept apart here by attestation date.

---

## 1. Dating: two incompatible chronologies

- Floruit conventionally c. **700 BCE**, range **c. 750-650 BCE**; upper bound from Delphi's rise and Euxine geography, lower from apparent imitations in Alcaeus, Semonides, and Archilochus. **[A, Britannica]**
- Herodotus 2.53: Homer and Hesiod lived "four hundred years before my time and not more," and made the Greeks' theogony and the gods' titles, offices, forms. His arithmetic points to the **mid-9th century**. **[A, Macaulay, Gutenberg 2707, verbatim]**
- **Contradiction, not adjudicated.** Both stand in the opening stop's `date_confidence`.
- Janko favoured an early-7th-century Theogony on formulaic diction, others late-8th; no convergence on a date, none on the order of Homer and Hesiod. Aulus Gellius, reporting Varro, used the Chalcis tripod inscription to argue the two were contemporaries. **[R, both via secondary literature]**
- **Correction applied.** The pool attached "730-705 BCE" to the Lelantine War; that range is a proposed date for the **Amphidamas games**, while Wikipedia dates the war "between c. 710 and 650 BC." Correctly stated, the two *widen* the circularity, and the file says so.

## 2. Cyme, and the crossing

- The father left **Aeolian Cyme** for poverty and settled at **Ascra**, "bad in winter, sultry in summer, and good at no time." **[A, WD 633-640, checked verbatim in Gutenberg 348 and carried in full as the opening stop's quote]**
- The father is **never named in the poems**; **Dios** and **Pycimede** appear only in the Suda and scholia, over a millennium later. **[R]**
- **[gap]** Birthplace, Cyme or Ascra: the poems do not say, no ancient source settles it, and the file leaves it open. No date, route, ship, or trade at Cyme either.

---

## 3. Ascra: the farm, the brother, the lords

- The household economy of Works and Days runs to oxen, mules, hired labour, **enslaved people** (including a woman recommended as childless for practical reasons), seed, tools, storage, and assumes slaveholding and patriarchal control as normal. **[A, WD household passages]** This is the world the poem depicts, not Hesiod's own station.
- Hesiod charges that **Perses** took the greater share of the estate by currying favour with bribe-swallowing local basileis. **[A as text, WD 27-41]** **Historicity of Perses unresolved:** real brother and grievance, stylized memory, or pure didactic frame **[R]**, which is why the file tags that stop tradition. **[gap]** No names for the lords, no court, no acreage, no sum, no outcome, no external corroboration.
- Two quotes were checked verbatim in Gutenberg 348 and are carried in full in the file: the address to Perses about the two Strifes (WD 25-29), and the "noise when Justice is being dragged" passage. **Citation correction:** the pool cited the second as "lines ~220-228," but Evelyn-White prints it in the block ll. 212-224 and the sentence falls at c. 220-224.
- The **Iron Race** places the poem's voice in an era of failing kinship, oath, hospitality, justice: a mythic frame, not a datable description. The poem also tells a man to marry a virgin so he can "teach her careful ways," and via Pandora casts woman as a costly evil. **[A as text]** **[gap]** No wife, child, or marital status of his own is documented anywhere.

---

## 4. Helicon: the commissioning

- Shepherding lambs, Hesiod says the Muses appeared, gave him a staff, and breathed a divine voice into him. **[A as text, Theogony 22-34; R as biography]** The file tags it tradition, the defensible tag for a first-person commissioning scene. The Muses' address (Theogony 26-28, "mere bellies... we know how to speak many false things as though they were true") was checked verbatim in Gutenberg 348 and is carried in full at that stop.
- **Correction 1, laurel not olive, but do not cite Evelyn-White for it.** Theogony 30 reads *skeptron daphnes erithaleos ozon*, daphne being laurel, where Evelyn-White renders "a rod, a shoot of sturdy olive." The file cites the Greek and Pausanias 9.30.3. **Correction 2:** "as a boy or young man" is not in the text, Theogony 22-23 giving no age.
- **Correction 3, the Pausanias detail was inverted.** The pool had the Helicon statue holding "a laurel branch rather than a lyre." Pausanias 9.30.3 says it shows Hesiod "holding a harp upon his knees," which he objects to because the verses give him a laurel wand. Correct citations are **9.30.1** (tripod) and **9.30.3** (statues), not 9.31.
- **Nagy's etymology** (Hesiodos from *hienai* + *aude*) is used as an attributed reading. **[R, not verified against a primary Nagy text]**

## 5. Aulis to Chalcis: the only journey in his own voice

- One sea voyage, **Aulis** to **Chalcis**, for funeral games held for **Amphidamas** by his sons; he won a handled tripod and dedicated it to the Muses of Helicon. **[A, WD 646-662, verbatim]** He names **no rival**, no judge, no category, and does not say what he sang. **Amphidamas cannot be securely identified or dated**, and the Lelantine War tie is circular (see §1). **[R]**
- The **Certamen** supplies the rival. Extant text usually dated **2nd century CE**, on older material associated with **Alcidamas**. **[A as to the text's character]** Reception, not record; the file dates that stop 0150 CE at Chalcis.
- **Citation correction.** The pool cited the Certamen quotes to sacred-texts.com, filename "homrhes.htm": Cloudflare-gated, and a typo for homerhes.htm. All three were re-verified in **Gutenberg 348** and cited there, with the oracle quote's source punctuation restored.

## 6. Delphi, Nemea, Acarnania

- **The oracle.** Delphi warns he will die "at Nemea"; he avoids the Peloponnesian Nemea, not knowing a lesser Locrian sanctuary carries the same epithet. **[R, Certamen; the bare oracle is in Thucydides 3.96]** A folktale mechanism, not datable to his lifetime.
- **The Pythian exclusion.** Pausanias 10.7.3 is said to record that he was barred for not having learned to accompany himself. **[R, unverified at first hand: fetches failed against Perseus, sacred-texts, topostext]** In tension with later art giving him a stringed instrument.
- **Acarnania.** Acarnanians claimed to have taught him divination, and prophecy and bird-omen poems circulated under his name. **[R, Pausanias via the pool]** **[gap]** No itinerary, no named town, no evidence of any western journey before the last one. Likelier back-formation from the omen and calendar material already in Works and Days.

## 7. Death in Locris

- **Thucydides 3.96** (verbatim, Crawley, Wikisource): the army bivouacs "in the precinct of Nemean Zeus, in which the poet Hesiod is said to have been killed by the people of the country, an oracle having foretold that he should suffer this in Nemea." **[A]** No culprit, no sexual charge, no dolphins. **Date correction:** the pool's anchor of **-0413** matches nothing in the passage; the narrated events are **Demosthenes' Aetolian campaign of 426 BCE**, and the stop carries the corrected framing.
- **Plutarch, Banquet of the Seven Sages 19** (penelope.uchicago.edu): a man from Miletus seduces the host's daughter and Hesiod falls under suspicion of having known "although he was in nowise guilty." Also **Troilus**, the river **Daphnus**, the rock still called Troilus, and the dolphins carrying the body "to Rhium hard by Molycreia." **[A as Plutarch's text, R as history]**
- **The Certamen** (Gutenberg 348) has the young men kill him at Oenoe on suspicion of seducing their sister and cast the body "into the sea which separates Achaea and Locris," while separately naming the real seducer as a companion, **Demodes**, and saying the girl hanged herself. **Correction:** the pool flagged that sentence as a paraphrase and urged dropping it; it is verbatim, so the file uses it in full and re-tags it attested.
- **Later lives** (pseudo-Hesiodic Lives, Suda, Tzetzes) name the killers **Ganyctor** or **Phegeus** and sons **[R]**; **Alcaeus of Messene**, Greek Anthology 7.55, has nymphs wash the corpse and goatherds pour milk with honey **[R, not verified against the epigram]**.

**Contradictions flagged, not resolved:** (1) **Guilt**: Plutarch exculpates him, other versions make him the seducer, the Certamen gives the deed to Demodes, Thucydides mentions no sexual charge. (2) **Stesichorus**: one late strand makes the woman his mother, which the arithmetic will not carry. (3) **The Daphnus** is an East Locrian toponym, yet Plutarch places the events in the west; the file follows Plutarch and says so. (4) **Burial**: Thucydides at the Locrian precinct, Pausanias at Naupactus and then moved, Chersias at Orchomenus. Three placements, harmonized by nobody.

## 8. The bones, the tomb, the village

- **The relic transfer.** Plague at Orchomenus, a Delphic order to fetch the bones from Naupactian territory, a **crow** showing the searchers a hidden rock cleft. **[R, Pausanias 9.38; Aristotle's lost *Constitution of the Orchomenians*, known only by quotation]** Standard relic-legitimation machinery, untested by excavation. A variant from the same lost work has the people of **Ascra**, on its destruction by Thespiae, carrying his ashes to the Orchomenian agora beside the **tomb of Minyas**. **[R]**
- **The epitaph**, credited to **Chersias of Orchomenus**, naming Ascra as his native land and the Minyan country as holder of his bones. **[A as text, Certamen, carried in full at that stop; variant in Pausanias 9.38.9-10]** **Correction:** the pool dated it **-0625**, but no source gives a year; it is *attributed to the 7th century BCE*, and Pausanias says the poem no longer survived in his day. The pool's "within about a century of his death date" is unsupported and was dropped.
- **Ascra's end.** Pausanias saw a single tower still standing in the 2nd century CE. **[A]** Wikipedia dates the destruction 700-650 BCE **[R, flagged]**, a bare assertion sitting awkwardly inside Hesiod's own lifetime, and says the site is "less than seven and a half miles west of Thespiae" where its own coordinates give about **7.6 km**, matching **Strabo's forty stadia**.

## 9. The corpus and its ancient authorship disputes

- **Theogony**, 1,022 lines, generally the earlier core poem, with the ending and some passages suspected of interpolation; **Works and Days**, 828 lines, its closing "Days" section questioned since antiquity as a later addition. **[A as text, R as to the Days]**
- **Shield of Heracles**, now generally pseudo-Hesiodic, suspected spurious already by Aristophanes of Byzantium for its borrowing from the Catalogue. **Correction:** the pool asserted "probably 6th century BCE" with a date field of -0550, but Wikipedia gives "end of the 7th to the middle of the 6th century BCE," so that precision is unsupported. **[A/R]**
- **Precepts of Chiron**, athetized by Aristophanes of Byzantium (Quintilian 1.1.15) **[R]**; **Marriage of Ceyx**, reported by Plutarch as interpolated though Athenaeus thought it ancient **[R]**; **Aegimius**, given by Athenaeus to **Cercops of Miletus** **[A as attribution dispute]**; **Catalogue of Women**, now generally later and composite **[A]**. The **Suda** adds a lost lament for one **Batrachus**, his *eromenos* **[R, isolated, 10th century CE]**, not used as a stop for want of any earlier witness.
- **Near Eastern comparison** to the Kumarbi cycle and Enuma Elish, recent work cautioning against direct borrowing **[R, following West 1997 via secondary literature]**: a modern frame, marked as such in the stop rather than staged as an episode.

## 10. Confirmed negatives, stated as gaps

No school, literacy training, named teacher, coinage, investment, patent, priesthood, guild membership, military command, or government office can be attached to Hesiod by any surviving source; the only economic history for the family is the father's poverty-driven migration. **[A, negative finding across the poems, Britannica, Wikipedia]** Coinage would in any case be anachronistic here.

**[gap]** No contemporaneous portrait. The only ancient artwork that securely names its subject is the late-3rd-century-CE **Monnus mosaic** from Trier, labelled ESIODVS; the "Pseudo-Seneca" bronze from Herculaneum was reidentified as an imagined Hesiod by **Gisela Richter**. **[A as attribution history]** Neither is staged as a stop; see §12.

## 11. Geography: pins, and what could not be pinned

**Inherited byte-identical:** **Delphi 38.482 / 22.501**, the pin the interlock lens named (via `cyrus`, `diogenes_of_sinope`, `savitri_devi`, which are not mutually consistent). **Chalcis 38.4625 / 23.5950**, matching `aristotle.journey.json`.

- **Ascra vs Mount Helicon.** Wikipedia's Ascra coordinates (38.327032/23.074249) sit roughly **22 km east** of its own Mount Helicon coordinates (38.35278/22.82250), and the pool used the Ascra pair for a stop *titled* Helicon. The file separates them, Ascra at 38.327/23.0742 and mountain at 38.3528/22.8225, stating the discrepancy rather than averaging it away.
- **Approximate or regional, each saying so in its own stop:** Valley of the Muses, Hippocrene, Oenoe, the Locrian Nemean Zeus sanctuary, the Daphnus mouth, Acarnania. For **Orchomenus** the lenses disagreed (38.483/22.983 vs 38.4919/22.9762, same article), and the file uses the latter. **Aganippe was dropped**: the pool put it on top of Thespiae where Pausanias 9.29.5 puts it in the Muses' grove, and conceded anyway that neither spring is named in Hesiod's text.
- **Unsourced claims not asserted.** The pool cited en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thespiae for claims it does not make: no control of the Helicon sanctuary, no "Mouseia" (it describes the **Erotidia**), no archaeological dating. Thespiae appears only as the town that destroyed Ascra. Neither the **Molycreia** nor the **Naupactus** article mentions Hesiod at all.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Hesiod is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and under the block rule stated there a file can only embed what already existed at its own moment. **Direction caught: FORWARD, all three.** Each is a later person's one-directional engagement with Hesiod or a text bearing his name, embedded as a named pointer. Flagged, not deleted, per the adversary's duty.

1. **`strabo`**, in "Boeotia, the women in the catalogue." Geography 1.2.35 (verbatim, tr. Jones) defends Hesiod beside Homer as a deliberate mythmaker: "no one could charge Hesiod with ignorance when he speaks of men who are half-dog, of long-headed men and of Pygmies." c. 700 years later.
2. **`giulio_camillo`**, in "Boeotia, the Shield and the poems that gather to a name." Camillo assigned the Gorgon Sisters of the pseudo-Hesiodic *Shield of Heracles* (l. 230) to the fourth grade of his 1528 Venetian memory theatre, still crediting Hesiod; sourced in `giulio_camillo.journey.json` to Yates ch. VI. c. 2,200 years later.
3. **`aristotle`**, in "Orchomenus, laid by the tomb of Minyas" and in a `suggested_ref` on the crow stop. His lost *Constitution of the Orchomenians* is the traceable source for the ash transfer and the burial beside Minyas. c. 350 years later.

**Precedent, for the operator's decision.** `aristotle.journey.json` already names Averroes, Aquinas, and Dante inside Aristotle's own file, with stops staged at Córdoba, Fustat, Toledo, and Oxford. This file is more conservative: the three later figures are named **only in campas anchored at Hesiod's own pins**. If even that is out of bounds, the names move into `suggested_refs` without touching a stop.

**Considered and dropped on the research lens's own recommendation:** `max_muller`, whose 1856 *Comparative Mythology* uses "theogony" generically rather than citing Hesiod, "a weak/generic lexical echo only."

**Homer.** No `homer.journey.json` exists, so no slug is named. Homer appears only where a source names him, in Herodotus 2.53 and the Certamen; Hesiod's own Chalcis lines name no opponent.

**Negative interlock finding, recorded so nobody hunts for it again:** no connection to the Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris, or Buenos Aires pins. His geography is central Greece and western Anatolia only.
