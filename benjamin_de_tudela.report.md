# Benjamin de Tudela: research report

*Rabbi Benjamin son of Jonah of Tudela. Departure from Tudela in Navarre c. 1159/1165; return to Castile 1173. Death undated.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

The controlling text is **M. N. Adler, *The Itinerary of Benjamin of Tudela* (Oxford University Press, 1907)**, in the Project Gutenberg edition 14981-h. All quotations are Adler's English. Where a claim in circulation does not survive against Adler, that is said plainly.

---

## 1. Before the road

**[A: Adler p. 1; Jewish Encyclopedia]** The book names him Benjamin son of Jonah of Tudela and gives no birth date, no parentage beyond the father's name, no trade, no teachers, no family left behind. He is a voice and an itinerary.

**Flagged, a persistent error.** Summaries in circulation say he sets out **from Saragossa**, and place Saragossa **in Navarre**. Both are wrong. **[A: Adler p. 1]** "The said Rabbi Benjamin set forth from Tudela, his native city ... I journeyed first from my native town to the city of Saragossa." Tudela is the departure, Saragossa the first stage, and Saragossa lay in **Aragon**. The journey begins in one Iberian kingdom and crosses at once into another.

**Flagged, unresolved: the departure year.** **[A: Jewish Encyclopedia]** the traditional 1160, carried in the title of Spanish editions as "1160-1173." **[A: Adler n. 2]** rejects it, placing Benjamin in Rome only between late 1165 and 1167 and bracketing his absence from Europe between **1166 and 1171**. **[R: EBSCO Research Starters; Wikipedia]** modern consensus puts departure around **1165**; **[R: Adler, Introduction]** editions differ between 1159 and 1165. Consequence: the repeated "thirteen-year journey" holds only on the 1160 start. On c. 1165 and the attested 1173 return the circuit is about **eight years**. Neither is adjudicated here.

**Purpose: a gap.** Nothing says why he went. Gem trading, a survey of refuges and pilgrimage have all been proposed. The manuscript offers no statement of purpose, and neither does this report.

## 2. Iberia and Provence (c. 1165)

**[A: Adler]** Tudela, Saragossa, then the Catalan coast: Tortosa, Tarragona, Barcelona, Girona. Barcelona is "a small city and beautiful, lying upon the sea-coast"; he names R. Shesheth, R. Shealtiel, R. Solomon, R. Abraham son of Chisdai.

**Flagged, tag correction.** Identifying Benjamin's "R. Shesheth" with **Sheshet ben Isaac Benveniste, royal physician**, is a modern inference, not in the text. It is **[R]**, not [A].

**Flagged.** "Two days from Tudela to Saragossa" is not in Adler, who gives no interval for that leg; the two-day stages are Tortosa-Tarragona and Tarragona-Barcelona.

**[A: Adler]** Narbonne, about 300 Jews under R. Kalonymos son of R. Todros of the House of David; "thence the Torah (Law) goes forth to all countries." Lunel, about 300, an academy under Rabbenu Meshullam and his five sons, with R. Judah ibn Tibbon among them; students from distant lands are "taught, boarded, lodged and clothed by the congregation." Posquières (medieval Vauvert, Gard), about forty Jews and the academy of "the great Rabbi, R. Abraham, son of David." Saint-Gilles, about a hundred, three miles from the sea on the Rhone, R. Abba Mari bailiff of Count Raymond. Marseille, about 300 in two congregations. He takes ship there.

**Flagged, two corrections.** The RABaD is named by Benjamin himself, so Posquières is [A]; hedging it as a later identification is wrong. And calling the Saint-Gilles community port merchants is not in the text; it is a pilgrimage town in Adler, with the sea three miles off.

## 3. Italy (1165-1166)

**[A: Adler]** Genoa, two Jewish residents only, brothers, R. Samuel son of Salim of Ceuta; the city governed by consuls, not a king. Pisa about 20, Lucca about 40. Rome, "the head of the kingdoms of Christendom," about 200 Jews paying no tribute, chief rabbi R. Daniel, and R. Jechiel an official of Pope Alexander, "the steward of his house and of all that he has," grandson of the R. Nathan who compiled the Arukh.

**Flagged, two corrections.** R. Jechiel was **not a cardinal**; Adler has him a lay household steward and "a handsome young man." And Benjamin's Roman community lived in **Trastevere**: the Portico d'Ottavia coordinates used for Jewish Rome mark the **1555 ghetto**, four centuries later. The pin is canonical; it is not his quarter.

**[A: Adler]** The southern census: Capua ~300, Naples ~500, Salerno ~600 beside the medical school, Amalfi ~20, Benevento ~200, Melfi ~200, Trani ~200 and an embarkation port for Jerusalem, Taranto ~300, Brindisi ~10 dyers, Otranto ~50. **Flagged:** one lens of the research pool gives Otranto ~500, another ~50. Adler reads "about 50 Jews"; the 500 is an error.

## 4. Greece and Constantinople (1166-1167)

**[A: Adler]** Corfu: "only one Jew of the name of R. Joseph lives, and here ends the kingdom of Sicily." **Flagged, two corrections:** Corfu ends the **Sicilian** kingdom, and the Byzantine dominion begins one stage later, at "the land of Larta (Arta), which is the beginning of the dominions of Emanuel, Sovereign of the Greeks"; and R. Joseph is **not** called a dyer. That trade is invented.

**[A: Adler]** Patras ~50, Corinth ~300, Negroponte ~200, Thessalonica ~500 in silk work and oppressed by the Greek populace. Thebes ~2,000, the largest in Greece: "the most skilled artificers in silk and purple cloth throughout Greece." Constantinople: tribute of 20,000 gold pieces a year; "No Jews live in the city, for they have been placed behind an inlet of the sea"; in the quarter "about 2,000 Rabbanite Jews and about 500 Karaites, and a fence divides them." R. Solomon Hamitsri, the court physician, alone may ride horseback. The Aegean: Rhaedestus ~400, Gallipoli ~200, Chios ~400 with its mastic, Samos ~300, Rhodes ~400.

## 5. Cyprus, Cilicia, the Syrian coast (1167)

**[A: Adler]** Cyprus: a sect he calls the Epikursin, who profane the Sabbath eve and keep it only from the following morning. **[R]** No port or city is named; any Cyprus pin is a placement. Cilician Armenia under "Thoros, king of the mountains" (Toros II). Antioch: about ten Jews, all glass-makers, the city fortified under "Prince Boemond." **[R: Adler's notes]** Whether he passed through Antioch himself or relayed the coastal list secondhand is debated by his editors. Near Gebal, the Nizari Ismailis, the "Sheik Al Hashishim," territory eight days across centred on Kadmus, at war with the Franks and with Tripoli.

**Flagged, correction.** Benjamin gives **no Jewish population for Tripoli**. The quoted "about 200 Jews at Tripoli" belongs to **Gebal/Gubail (Byblos)**, which Adler places under Genoese rule with Guillelmus Embriacus as governor. Tripoli appears only as the Assassins' enemy and as an earthquake site: more than 20,000 dead there, and at Hamah about 25,000 including roughly 130 of the town's Jews, of whom about 70 survived. Both quakes are conventionally dated 1157, before his arrival.

**[A: Adler]** Beirut ~50. Tyre ~500, harbour closed nightly with iron chains, sugar, and the glass "which is prized in all countries." Acre ~200, where pilgrims land for Jerusalem. Haifa, "which is Hahepher on the seaboard," and on Mount Carmel "the cave of Elijah, where the Christians have erected a structure called St. Elias." **Flagged, three corrections:** the pool gives Tyre 400 in one lens and 500 in another, and Adler reads 500; Haifa is not "called Sepphoris by some," Sepphoris being a separate town in his list; the Christian element at Carmel is a building over the cave, not a spring.

## 6. The Kingdom of Jerusalem

**[A: Adler]** "There are about 200 Jews who dwell under the Tower of David in one corner of the city," holding "a dyeing-house, for which the Jews pay a small rent annually to the king, on condition that besides the Jews no other dyers be allowed in Jerusalem." The Hospitallers keep 400 knights, the Templars 300 in the former Temple of Solomon; the Dome of the Rock is a place of Muslim prayer without images. Bethlehem, two parasangs off, and "the pillar of Rachel's grave, which is made up of eleven stones." Hebron: a fee paid to descend beneath the Church of St. Abraham to the tombs of the patriarchs and their wives, under a perpetual lamp.

**Flagged, three corrections.** The widely circulated "about four Jewish dyers" is a **variant reading**, not Adler's critical text, which gives 200 Jews plus the dyeing-house. Rachel's pillar is **eleven** stones, not twelve, and Benjamin gives the reason himself; the "six miles" distance is also not his. And two pool entries pin Machpelah at 31.5256, 35.1004 and 31.5326, 35.0998, both about a kilometre off the Cave of the Patriarchs at **31.5247, 35.1108**, which the journey file uses.

**Flagged, unresolved: when.** The pool's two lenses date the Jerusalem visit **c. 1169** and **1170**. Both postdate the Raqqa synchronism below, which the Itinerary's route order places **after** Jerusalem. No arrangement satisfies all three. The journey file follows route order and the Raqqa anchor, and says so in date_confidence at the stop.

## 7. Syria, Mesopotamia, Baghdad (1167-1170)

**[A: Adler]** Damascus, about 3,000 Jews under R. Azariah's academy, with roughly 100 Karaites and 400 Samaritans at peace and not intermarrying. Tadmor (Palmyra), about 2,000 warlike Jews who fight alongside their Arab neighbours. Aleppo, Aram Zoba, a royal city of Nur al-Din, about 5,000. Mosul, "Assur the Great," about 7,000, with R. Zakkai Nasi of the line of David and R. Joseph Burhan-al-Mulk, astronomer to Sayf al-Din. Basra, 10,000. Susa and the tomb of Daniel, where two riverbanks dispute the relic until Sultan Sanjar hangs the coffin in glass from the bridge.

**[R: synchronism reported in the scholarship on the itinerary's dating]** Benjamin is at **Raqqa no earlier than late summer 1167**, when Nur al-Din ceded the city to his youngest brother, and **no later than 26 October 1168**. The firmest single anchor on the eastward leg. It reaches this report through a search summary of that scholarship rather than the article itself, hence [R].

**[A: Adler; corroborated in Jewish Encyclopedia, "Alroy"]** **David Alroy** (Menahem ben Solomon) of Amadia, who tried to raise the Jews of the Kurdish mountains against the Seljuk authorities and march on Jerusalem, and was killed. Benjamin is the primary historical source for the episode.

**[A: Adler]** Baghdad: "In Bagdad there are about 40,000 Jews ... In this city there are ten Academies," and twenty-eight synagogues. The caliph "knows all languages, and is well versed in the law of Israel," and comes out once a year at the feast ending Ramadan. The Exilarch Daniel son of Hisdai, "Head of the Captivity of all Israel," is received every fifth day and seated opposite the caliph per Genesis 49:10; his authority runs from Persia to Yemen and to the land of the Alans. The great academy is headed by R. Samuel son of Eli. Ezekiel's tomb nearby draws pilgrims from Media and Persia; **[R: Jewish Encyclopedia, "Kifl, Al-"]** its siting at Al-Kifl is a later reconstruction.

**Flagged, four points.** The caliph is **not al-Mustadi**: footnote 123 reads "Benjamin here refers to the Caliph El Mostanshed," that is **al-Mustanjid, r. 1160-1170**. Footnote 127 records **40,000** as the British Museum and Casanatense reading against **1,000** in Asher and the printed editions, so the most-quoted number in the book rests on a manuscript choice. Describing Basra as an Indian Ocean trading community is embroidery; the Indian-trade emporium in his text is **Kish**. And "Hadara/al-Hilla, about 15,000" runs two places together: footnote 122 identifies **Hadara** as Alhathr or **Hatra** (15,000), while **Hillah** is a separate later stage with 10,000.

## 8. Persia, the Turks, Arabia: where the eyewitness ends

**Flagged, three corrections to a widely repeated summary.**

1. The Persian section is **not** "fragmentary in the manuscript tradition." It is continuous and detailed. Footnote 122 says the figures from Hadara onward look exaggerated, lending "colour to the view that Benjamin did not proceed beyond Ispahan," the intervening places resting "upon hearsay information."
2. The Ghuz (Kofar-al-Turak) are **not** Sanjar's subjects. They are his enemies, allied with the mountain Jews, and they rout him: "the king of Persia fled with only a few followers to his own country."
3. The hostile ethnography is confirmed: "they eat no bread and drink no wine, but feed on uncooked meat." Khuzistan, the tomb of Daniel, Hamadan, Ispahan and Shiraz are all in the text.

**[R: Jewish Encyclopedia; EBSCO Research Starters]** Whether he travelled east of Baghdad at all, or compiled those sections from merchants, remains disputed; some hold he turned south toward Arabia instead. Not resolved here; the journey file marks the eastward stops as reported rather than walked, in date_confidence. Kish in the Gulf is his emporium of international trade.

**Flagged, correction that reverses the sense.** The Jews of Aden are **not** "living under tribal protection." Adler: they "are not under the yoke of the Gentiles, but possess cities and castles on the summits of the mountains, from which they make descents into the plain-country ... and no man can prevail against them."

**Gap.** Mecca and the Arabian interior are in the Itinerary at second hand. No stop there is a stop.

## 9. Egypt, the return, the book (1171-1173)

**Flagged, two consequential corrections.** Benjamin describes Egypt under **Fatimid** rule, not Ayyubid: "Here resides the Emir al Muminin, a descendant of Abu Talib," and Adler's footnote 2 confirms that "In Cairo he found that the Fatimite Caliph was the acknowledged ruler." And the text contains **no allusion** to the deposition of al-Adid; Adler's preface has Saladin keeping "himself so studiously in the background, that not even his name is mentioned in the Itinerary." The deposition date is right (10 September 1171) but postdates the visit.

**[A: Adler]** Alexandria: the Pharos with its glass mirror; Aristotle's academy "standing between other academies to the number of twenty, with a column of marble between each"; a roster of trading nations; "about 3,000 Jews." Fustat: "Nethanel the Prince of Princes and the head of the Academy, who is the head of all the congregations in Egypt." Sicily on the return: Messina ~200, Palermo ~1,500 with the palace of King William.

**Flagged, three corrections.** There is **no cistern attributed to Joseph** in the Alexandria passage. Benjamin calls the royal city **"Zoan el-Medina,"** not al-Qahira, and Fustat **"Mizraim"**, a usage Adler flags himself; the title **Nagid** is Adler's footnote, not Benjamin's. And the homeward leg does **not** run through Italy and southern France to Iberia: Adler's sequence is Sicily, Rome, Lucca, "the mountain of Jean de Maurienne, and the passes of Italy," Verdun, and the Rhineland communities of Alamannia.

**[A: Adler, colophon]** "He brought this book with him on his return to the country of Castile, in the year 4933 (C.E. 1173)." The text names the country, not the town.

## 10. Death and afterlife

**Gap.** No primary source records place, date or cause of death. **[R: Wikipedia infobox, uncited]** 1175 circulates; **[A: Jewish Encyclopedia; Encyclopedia.com]** both state the death date is unknown. No tomb attributed to him is documented anywhere.

**Gap.** No contemporary portrait, seal or image survives or is claimed. Every likeness, the Tudela bust included, is modern and has no iconographic model.

**Printing and translation [A: Jewish Encyclopedia; Adler, Introduction]:** editio princeps, Hebrew, **Constantinople 1543**, Eliezer ben Gershon, 64 pages octavo in rabbinic script; **Ferrara 1556**, **Abraham ben Usque**, textually different and preferred later for fewer scribal errors; eight further Hebrew printings, Freiburg 1583 to Warsaw 1884. Latin: **Benito Arias Montano** (Antwerp 1575), **Constantijn l'Empereur** (Leiden 1633). English: **A. Asher 1840**; **M. N. Adler 1907** from a British Museum manuscript, with selections in the *Palestine Exploration Fund Quarterly Statement* in 1894, against **L. Grünhut**'s Jerusalem edition of 1902. Spanish: **González Llubera 1916**, **Riber 1956** from Arias Montano's Latin, **Magdalena Nom de Déu 1982**.

**Flagged, four attribution errors in circulation.** Ferrara 1556 was **not Benjamin Musafia's**; Musafia was born c. 1606 and his name attaches to **Amsterdam 1697**. "Benjamin Arias Montanus" is a corruption of **Benito Arias Montano**. González Llubera is **1916**, not 1918, and a 2002 revision of Magdalena is not in the source consulted. The 1993 Pangloss Press volume reprints Adler's translation with added introductions; it is **not** a new critical edition.

**[A: Jewish Encyclopedia]** Its verdict: "preponderatingly marked by sobriety and clearness" yet "perhaps not altogether free from fiction," and a first-importance source for twelfth-century Jewish history. It records roughly **248 community leaders**. **[R: Medieval Fair blog; ExplorersWeb]** "The Jewish Marco Polo" is a modern label, absent from the medieval reception of either man.

**Commemoration [A: Wikipedia; Universidad de Navarra, Cátedra de Patrimonio y Arte Navarro]:** a bronze bust by **Ricardo Varela Andrés** in the Plaza de la Judería, Tudela; streets in Tudela's former judería, in Jerusalem's Rehavia, and in Tel Aviv; a secondary school in Tudela. **Flagged, and narrowed against the pool:** the pool presents 1984 and 1994 as an open contradiction. Wikipedia in both languages and the Navarre heritage sources date the **bust to 1994**; **1984** in the Cátedra itinerary belongs to a **different work**, a monument to the Sephardic community. The inscription itself was not recovered, so this rests on secondary agreement.

## 11. Crossings with travellers already in the atlas

Each file's own facts are **[A]**; every crossing with Benjamin is **[R]**, since no source pairs them.

- **maimonides**: Temple Mount 1165, Fustat from 1166. Both cities Benjamin describes; no meeting recorded either side.
- **bernard_clairvaux**: the mob turned back at Mainz, 1146, on the Rhineland leg of Benjamin's road home, load-bearing since it protects the very congregations Benjamin later travels among, and kept in campa. Considered and not used: his crusade also breaks at Damascus 1148, a generation before Benjamin counts 3,000 Jews there; that mention was cut from the Damascus campa under the house-style tightening of 2026-08-14, since the failed siege has no bearing on Benjamin's own visit or purpose there.
- **richard_lionheart**: marries Berengaria of Navarre and takes Acre, 1191. Same kingdom of origin, same port.
- **marco_polo**: leaves through Acre in 1271, along the same Levantine string.
- **ibn_battuta**: retraces the route city for city; his Jerusalem and Kaaba pins are inherited byte-identical, the second for the one place Benjamin never reached.
- **ramon_llull**: licensed at Barcelona 1299 to preach in the synagogues Benjamin catalogued; Famagusta 1301.
- **ziryab**: Baghdad to Córdoba, Benjamin's two poles travelled the other way.
- **abd_al_rahman_al_ghafiqi**: musters at Pamplona, 732, in the kingdom that would hold Tudela.
- **martin_buber**: dies in Jerusalem 1965, eight centuries after the count of 200.
