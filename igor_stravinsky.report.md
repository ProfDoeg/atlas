# Igor Fyodorovich Stravinsky (1882-1971): research report

*2026-08-23. From the verified research pool. Journey file: `igor_stravinsky.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops, calendar `gregorian`, register "national mythology: the canon is true".*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim this pass could not confirm. Contradictions are printed, never adjudicated. Every hedge also lives in the relevant stop's `date_confidence`, never in campa prose. He is born under the Julian calendar and dies under the Gregorian, 5 June O.S. / 17 June N.S. 1882 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the file is `gregorian`, all dates New Style.

## 1. Petersburg, 1882-1909

- Born 17 June 1882 at Oranienbaum, third son of Fyodor Stravinsky, bass of the Mariinsky, and Anna; baptised Orthodox at St Nicholas Cathedral; piano lessons from about age nine **[A: Wikipedia; Boosey & Hawkes composer timeline]**.
- Second Saint Petersburg Gymnasium 1892-1901 **[A: ru.wikipedia, "Вторая Санкт-Петербургская гимназия", whose pupil list names him]**; law faculty 1901-1905, no final examination, no practice **[A: Boosey]**.
- Summer 1902, Heidelberg: his father dying of cancer, Igor shows his manuscripts to Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov, who advises against the Conservatory and takes him privately. Tuition runs to the teacher's death, June 1908 **[A: Wikipedia; Boosey]**.
- 1905: university closure in the Bloody Sunday year, half-diploma without examination, engagement to his first cousin Yekaterina Nosenko; marriage 24 January 1906 near Saint Petersburg under a dispensation for a union Orthodox canon prohibits **[A: Wikipedia]**. Ustyluh, the Volhynia estate, is visited frequently 1890-1914 **[A: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustyluh]**.
- Symphony in E-flat and *The Faun and the Shepherdess* played through privately by the Court Orchestra under H. Wahrlich, 27 April 1907; first public performance 22 January 1908 under Felix Blumenfeld **[A: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_in_E-flat]**.
- *Chant funèbre*, Op. 5, for the dead Rimsky-Korsakov, premieres 17 January 1909 in the Conservatory Grand Hall under Blumenfeld; parts found spring 2015 by the librarian Irina Sidorenko, identified by the musicologist Natalya Braginskaya, played again 2 December 2016 under Gergiev **[A: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral_Song]**.
- February 1909: the Scherzo fantastique and Feu d'artifice are performed, Diaghilev hears them and engages him, first for Chopin orchestrations for *Les Sylphides*, then for a full ballet **[A: Wikipedia; `sergei_diaghilev.journey.json:212`]**.

Two rule decisions here. The 1909 premiere is his stop; the 2015 rediscovery is not staged as a scene, since Sidorenko and Braginskaya travelled and he did not. And the sighting of Tchaikovsky at the Mariinsky is **[R]**, flagged by Boosey as later recollection with no second witness; the campa carries it as his own story and links it, inside his own life, to *Le Baiser de la fée* (1928). Nothing written into `tchaikovsky.journey.json`.

## 2. Paris, 1910-1914

- **The Firebird**, 25 June 1910, Palais Garnier, Fokine, Karsavina **[A: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Firebird]**. Anna Pavlova was offered the lead and refused it, unable to hear the dance in the score **[A: `anna_pavlova.journey.json:158-159`]**: named in the campa because she engaged with his score, not because she was in the same city.
- **Petrushka**, 13 June 1911, Théâtre du Châtelet, Nijinsky, Fokine, Benois, Monteux **[A: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrushka_(ballet)]**, orchestrated in Rome that spring **[A: Boosey]**.
- **The Rite of Spring**, 29 May 1913, Théâtre des Champs-Élysées, 99 players, Monteux, Nijinsky, Roerich **[A: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rite_of_Spring; `vaslav_nijinsky.journey.json:248`; `nicholas_roerich.journey.json:142`]**; first concert performance 18 February 1914 under Koussevitzky.
- July 1914, the last crossing into Russia, to Ustyluh and Kyiv, where he buys the Kireyevsky song collection that becomes *Les Noces* **[A: Boosey]**; *Le Rossignol* opened at the Paris Opéra in May.

Four things about the Rite that the record refuses to tidy, all preserved in the file. **The riot is partly retrospective:** disruption began in the Introduction, intensified at the curtain, about forty people were ejected, the performance ran to its end, and contemporary press did not use the word *riot*, which scholarship traces to reviews of the 1924 revival. **Causation is disputed:** Taruskin argues it was Nijinsky's "ugly earthbound lurching and stomping", not the score, that shocked the house **[A: Wikipedia quoting Taruskin; the primary text was not located]**. **He contradicts himself about Nijinsky:** in 1913 he called the choreography "incomparable: with the exception of a few places, everything was as I wanted it" **[A: letter to Maximilien Steinberg, quoted in Wikipedia]**, while his later remarks are hostile. Both stand. And the 1936 autobiography's "fleeting vision" of "sage elders, seated in a circle" watching "a young girl dance herself to death" conflicts with the documented Roerich collaboration, so the file quotes the February 1914 note to Koussevitzky instead.

## 3. Switzerland, 1914-1920

Clarens 1914-1915, Morges 1915-1920, the working circle Ansermet, Ramuz and Werner Reinhart; *Renard* finished 1916; *L'Histoire du soldat* premiered 28 September 1918 at Lausanne, the planned tour collapsing under the influenza epidemic **[A: Boosey]**. Mika was born at Leysin in 1914, where Catherine was in a tuberculosis sanatorium **[A: Wikipedia]**. He met Picasso in Italy in 1917 and was drawn by him **[A: Boosey]**; the customs "spy map" anecdote is **[R]**, told differently by different memoirists, and the campa tells it as such.

## 4. The French years, 1920-1936

- **Pulcinella**, 15 May 1920, Palais Garnier, Picasso designs, Massine choreography, on music then given to Pergolesi and later shown to be by several hands. September 1920 to May 1921 the family lives at Chanel's Villa Bel Respiro at Garches at her expense; she had also underwritten the 1920 revival of *Le Sacre* with 300,000 francs, anonymously **[A: Wikipedia; Boosey; `coco_chanel.journey.json:166-171`]**.
- Biarritz from May 1921 with the Pleyel piano-roll contract; Vera de Bosset becomes his companion that year and the two households run in parallel until Catherine's death **[A: Wikipedia; Boosey]**.
- **Les Noces**, conceived 1913, short score complete by October 1917, premiered 13 June 1923 at the Théâtre de la Gaîté, Nijinska choreographing. **Concerto for Piano and Wind Instruments**, 22 May 1924, Palais Garnier, Stravinsky at the piano, Koussevitzky conducting **[A: Wikipedia, the articles on both works]**.
- Nice from September 1924; Father Nicolas and the return to Orthodox practice, 1926; *Oedipus Rex* in concert form, Paris, May 1927, Latin after Cocteau; **Le Baiser de la fée**, November 1928, for Ida Rubinstein, poorly received, Diaghilev reportedly furious; Symphony of Psalms, December 1930 **[A: Boosey; Wikipedia]**.
- **Apollon musagète**: world premiere 27 April 1928, Library of Congress, Washington, Adolph Bolm choreographing; the Balanchine version 12 June 1928 at the Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt **[A: Wikipedia; `george_balanchine.journey.json:299,303`]**. The stop is the Paris evening, the one he attended.
- French citizenship June 1934, Faubourg Saint-Honoré apartment 1933-34 **[A: Wikipedia; Boosey]**. That stop carries "Music is, by its very nature, essentially powerless to express anything at all" **[A: An Autobiography, 1936, pp. 53-54, via a fetched Wikiquote entry carrying that citation; the book was not opened]**.

**The Chanel affair is not settled and is not settled here:** Chanel asserted it in old age to Paul Morand, Vera Stravinsky and Robert Craft denied it, no contemporary correspondence decides it, and a 2002 novel and Kounen's 2009 film dramatised it **[R]**.

**Diaghilev dies 19 August 1929 in Venice, aged 57, buried in the Orthodox section of San Michele [A: Wikipedia, "Sergei Diaghilev"].** Stravinsky was not there, so this is deliberately not a stop on his file. It closes the *Baiser de la fée* campa, which is where his own relation to it sits.

## 5. Buenos Aires 1936, and 1939

**Victoria Ocampo.** Introduced in Paris in 1932 by Ernest Ansermet; hosted him and his son Soulima at Villa Ocampo before the Colón premiere of *Perséphone*; spoke the title role on opening night; received the manuscript dedicated 17 May 1936 **[A: `victoria_ocampo.journey.json:33,346,414,417,419-420`]**. Two stops, pins inherited byte-identical from her file. A two-directional relation, not co-location: he stayed in her house, rehearsed there, gave her a manuscript. *Left out of the campas on purpose:* `george_balanchine.journey.json:535` links the same Colón stage to the 1941 Ballet Caravan tour, which is Balanchine's observation on his own file.

Then Ludmila, Catherine and his mother Anna die within months, tuberculosis in the first two cases, and he sails for the United States in September 1939. That stop carries Catherine's letter of 11 March 1935, "So you plan to go to America again next winter.... When will you ever have time to rest and to compose?" **[A: NYRB, 22 November 1979]**: not his words, but a primary document about the marriage the stop is about. **Norton Lectures**, Harvard, October 1939 to April 1940, published as *Poétique musicale* (1941) and *Poetics of Music* (1947), the text substantially drafted by Alexis Roland-Manuel and Pierre Souvtchinsky from his notes, which the campa states rather than hides **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 6. America, 1940-1971

- Marriage to Vera de Bosset, Bedford, Massachusetts, 9 March 1940; 1260 North Wetherly Drive, West Hollywood, 1941-1969, in the émigré Los Angeles of Mann, Huxley and Schoenberg, the Schoenberg relation famously cool; citizenship and the Boosey & Hawkes contract, 1945. *Fantasia*, 1940: Rite excerpts used without a live performance, his first reaction reportedly less hostile than his later statements **[A: Wikipedia; Boosey]**. No stop for it, since no travel attaches to it.
- **Star-Spangled Banner, Boston, 15 January 1944: [R]** throughout. The police warning is treated as real in the scholarship, but this pass could not load starspangledmusic.org (HTTP 403) or confirm the venue; the circulated "mug shot" is an unrelated 1940 alien-registration photograph **[R: dossier]**.
- *Orpheus* with Balanchine and Isamu Noguchi for Ballet Society, 1948 **[A: `george_balanchine.journey.json:574`]**; Robert Craft met around the same time and became assistant, collaborator and executor. **The Rake's Progress**, La Fenice, 11 September 1951, Auden and Kallman after Hogarth; **In Memoriam Dylan Thomas, 1954**, his first fully serial work **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- White House dinner 18 January 1962; *Elegy for J.F.K.* composed 1964; Soviet return September-October 1962, six performances in Moscow and Leningrad, received by Khrushchev, first return since 1914; **Requiem Canticles, 1966**, later performed at his funeral **[A: Wikipedia; Boosey]**. **Massey Hall, Toronto, 1967**, last public conducting appearance, leading *Pulcinella*: **[R]** on venue and day, the appearance attested while the hall is the dossier's estimate. Evian-les-Bains, 1970, folded into that stop.
- Hospitalised with pulmonary edema 18-28 March 1971 **[R on the hospital: the Lenox Hill identification comes from general New York medical geography, not a re-verified source]**; edema recurs 4 April; dies 6 April at 5.20 a.m. at 920 Fifth Avenue, aged 88. Buried on San Michele, Orthodox section, near Diaghilev, whose article carries the only independent confirmation of the adjacency **[A: Wikipedia]**. Vera joins him in 1982.

## 9. Contradictions left open

1. City of the first concert Rite, 18 February 1914: the pool's chronology says unspecified, its geography lens Saint Petersburg, standard accounts Moscow first. The file follows the pool and prints the conflict.
2. City of the Symphony of Psalms premiere, December 1930: Brussels or Boston. No stop for it; the work sits inside the Nice stop.
3. Whether the 15 April 1971 funeral at Santi Giovanni e Paolo is sourced at all: two lenses of the pool carry it, a third says the date and service "remain unsourced by any page I could fetch".
4. Also open, and stated at their own stops: the Chanel affair; Nijinsky's choreography, praised in 1913 and attacked later; the Rite's genesis, the 1936 "vision" against the documented Roerich collaboration; and whether his first reaction to *Fantasia* was as hostile as his later statements.

## 10. Afterlife, monuments, editions

## 11. The documented antisemitism

Two letters of 1933, traced to their text as reproduced in "Jews and Geniuses," *The New York Review of Books*, 16 February 1989 **[A: page fetched directly]**:

> "I am surprised to have received no proposals from Germany for next season, since my negative attitude toward communism and Judaism, not to put it in stronger terms, is a matter of common knowledge."

> "Is it politically wise vis-à-vis Germany to identify myself with Jews like Klemperer and Walter?"

His own words in his own correspondence, attested, not alleged. They are in no campa because the pool supplies no coordinate for either letter; the right home for them is a stop at whatever address the correspondence can be tied to, once a source establishing one is reachable.

## 12. Apparatus relations for the operator

Stravinsky is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no framework has been applied backward or forward on his file. Per the adversary's duty, relations in the pool that are **not** rooted in his own life are named here rather than silently deleted, with the direction of each:

1. **Marcel Proust at the Rite premiere, 29 May 1913.** Direction: **contemporaneous**. The pool records that the report has been repeated for a century and never established as fact, and `vaslav_nijinsky.journey.json:248,252` already treats it as unresolved. Left out of the campas, since unestablished shared attendance is co-location at best. The two did meet at the Majestic dinner of May 1922 in some accounts, a real relation, but that meeting is not in this pool. No change to `marcel_proust.journey.json`.
2. **Mary Wigman staging her last major work to *Le Sacre*, Berlin, c. 1957** (`george_balanchine.journey.json:175`). Direction: **forward**, score to later choreographer. He is alive in 1957, but the pool records no relation between them. It is Wigman's engagement with his score and belongs on her file.
3. **Erik Satie** (`erik_satie.journey.json:503`): Balanchine arriving at Monte Carlo to work with Stravinsky, Picasso and Satie's scores is co-presence in one company, not an attested relation, so Satie is named nowhere. Direction: **contemporaneous**.
4. **Claude Debussy and Pablo Picasso**, queued in the same cluster per `QUEUE.md:107-109`. Both are real direct relations: Debussy played through the Rite at the piano with him, Picasso drew him in 1917 and dressed *Pulcinella*. Picasso is named in two campas as an ordinary historical person, having no journey file yet. Debussy is not named: the piano-duet reading is dossier framing, not in this pool with a source.

The only forward-looking material inside his own file concerns his own works and his own grave: the 2015-2016 Funeral Song rediscovery, the 1983 fountain, the 1987 Hodson reconstruction.
