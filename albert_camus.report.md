# Albert Camus (1913-1960), research report
*Compiled 2026-08-18 for `albert_camus.journey.json`. Born Mondovi (Dréan), French Algeria, 7 November 1913; died Villeblevin, Yonne, 4 January 1960.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim circulating widely without a citation I could reach. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. **CORRECTED** marks where verification overturned the gathering pass, with the correcting source.

---

## 1. Mondovi and Saint-Brieuc

- Born 7 November 1913 at Mondovi (today Dréan), second son of Lucien Auguste Camus, cellarman for a wine shipper, and Catherine Hélène Sintès, deaf and illiterate. **[A**: IEP; en.wikipedia**]**
- **CORRECTED, coordinates.** Gathering pass gave 36.7333 / 7.7667. Dréan is **36.6833 / 7.7500** (en.wikipedia, "Dréan"), 5.5 km south of it. The birth house is not identified anywhere reachable; the pin is town-level and the stop says so. **[A town, gap house]**
- **CORRECTED, the father's death.** Every English summary says Lucien Camus died "of wounds from the First Battle of the Marne," and the pool pinned him on the battlefield (48.9564 / 3.6069). fr.wikipedia: "Blessé à la tête par un éclat d'obus... meurt le 11 octobre 1914 à l'hôpital auxiliaire de la rue Saint-Benoît, à Saint-Brieuc," where he is buried. Wounded in September, evacuated to Brittany, died there. The file gives the death its own stop at **Saint-Brieuc, 48.5136 / -2.7653**, with the wounding in the campa rather than pinned twice. **[A]**

## 2. Belcourt

- The widow moves to Algiers, to Belcourt, into an apartment on the rue de Lyon shared with her mother and a brother. No water, no books; the grandmother beats the children; the mother cleans houses. **[A**: IEP; fr.wikipedia**]** **CORRECTED, coordinates, twice:** the pool gave 36.7628 / 3.0764 (1914) and 36.7625 / 3.0760 (1921, 93 rue de Lyon), two pins four metres apart and both ~1.7 km north-west of the street, in Hamma. Rue de Lyon is today's **rue Mohamed Belouizdad**, **36.7489 / 3.0684** (Nominatim/OSM). The two stops are **merged into one**; the 1921 move within the street is in the campa. **[A street, R building]**
- Louis Germain, teacher at the école communale of the rue Aumerat, coaches him free for the scholarship examination and argues down the grandmother, who wanted him earning. **[A**: Britannica; IEP**]** Camus wrote to Germain in the week of the Nobel; the letter is an appendix to *Le Premier Homme*, a print edition I did not open, so nothing from it is quoted. **[R for wording]** **School coordinates unresolved:** the pool's 36.7631 / 3.0771 is outside Belcourt; the file uses **36.7470 / 3.0720** and says the exact site is unverified. **[R]**

## 4. University, party, theatre, Tipasa

- University of Algiers, 1933-1936; *diplôme* on Plotinus, Neoplatonism, and Saint Augustine. **[A**: IEP; en.wikipedia for campus-level coordinates 36.7708 / 3.0556**]**
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved: Simone Hié.** The pool's chronology says **1934**, its geography **1935** plus "collapses within a year... and they divorce." fr.wikipedia: *mariage juin 1934, divorce 1940*, separation 1936. The file follows fr.wikipedia and names the disagreement in `date_confidence`. **[A for 1934 and 1940, R for June]** Her morphine addiction, prescribed for menstrual pain, and her affair with the prescribing doctor are stated flat. **[A**: en.wikipedia**]**
- **CONTRADICTION, flagged: the Communist Party.** Britannica: entry "early 1935." en.wikipedia: "left the PCF a year later." fr.wikipedia: *adhésion 1935, exclusion 1937*. The pool's arrays disagree for that reason. The file uses **joined 1935, expelled 1937**. **[A years, R month]**
- Théâtre du Travail 1936, later Théâtre de l'Équipe **[A**: IEP**]**. **CORRECTED, and it matters:** the pool had *Révolte dans les Asturies* "performed, his first staged theatrical work," May 1936. It was **never staged in his lifetime**: written 1935 with three collaborators, censored early 1936 by the mayor of Algiers, published instead by Charlot in May 1936 with no author names on the title page; first productions 2011-2012. **[A**: fr.wikipedia**]**
- Tipasa, repeated visits, source of "Noces à Tipasa" **[A**: en.wikipedia**]**; the French quote in the file comes from that page, untranslated. *L'Envers et l'Endroit*, Charlot, May 1937, first book **[A**: Britannica**]**.

## 5. The newspapers

- *Alger républicain* from October 1938 under Pascal Pia: reviews, then the courts. **[A**: Britannica; en.wikipedia**]** Office pin 36.7850 / 3.0590 is **[R]**, unverified.
- *Misère de la Kabylie*, **5-15 June 1939**. **[A**: fr.wikipedia**]** **CORRECTED, coordinates:** the pool's 36.4667 / 4.9000 sits near Bouira, outside the Grande Kabylie of the series (Tizi-Ouzou, Fort-National, Michelet, Azazga, Bougie); the file uses **Tizi Ouzou, 36.7170 / 4.0500**.
- **CORRECTED, the sequence of bans.** Not "banned 1940, then Paris." *Alger républicain* suppressed **October 1939**; the evening *Le Soir républicain*, founded by Camus and Pia **September 1939**, banned **January 1940**; Paris **March 1940**. **[A**: fr.wikipedia**]** Two stops. That the Government General then made him unemployable in the Algerian press is **[R]**. *Noces* is **CORRECTED** to **1939** (Charlot), not 1938, the year he joined the paper; no stop, folded into the Tipasa campa.

## 6. Exodus, Oran, the Absurd

- *Paris-Soir* layout desk, March 1940; pin 48.8677 / 2.3428 **[R]**, approximate. **CORRECTED, the flight south:** the paper fled Paris in June 1940 first to **Clermont-Ferrand** (45.7772 / 3.0870), where Camus stayed months, and only then to **Lyon** (45.7640 / 4.8357) in the autumn; the file folds Clermont-Ferrand into the Lyon campa. **[A]**
- **CONTRADICTION, flagged.** The pool's geography row puts the marriage to Francine Faure at **Oran**; its chronology and Britannica put it at **Lyon, 3 December 1940**. She was from Oran; the wedding was in exile. The file uses Lyon. **[A]**
- **CORRECTED, the return to Oran.** *Paris-Soir* dismissed him **January 1941**; the Oran teaching runs **1941 to August 1942**, not 1940. **[A**: fr.wikipedia**]** That some pupils were Jewish children excluded under Vichy is **[R]**, not in the file.
- *L'Étranger* and *Le Mythe de Sisyphe*, Gallimard, 1942. **[A**: IEP**]** The cut Kafka chapter (German censor) is **[R]**, universally repeated, unverified, carried only in `date_confidence`. **CORRECTED:** Sartre's "Explication de *L'Étranger*" appeared in *Cahiers du Sud* in **February 1943**, not 1942. **[A]**
- **CORRECTED, the return to France.** Not 1943: **August 1942**, for a TB relapse, to Le Panelier above Le Chambon-sur-Lignon, then cut off from Algeria by the November 1942 landings; *Combat* only in late 1943. **[A**: fr.wikipedia, which confirms only the 1942-1943 stay at Le Chambon**]** **Coordinates CORRECTED:** the pool's 44.9569 / 4.2481 is ~12 km south; the village is 45.0622 / 4.3019 and the hamlet **~45.0850 / 4.3200**, which the file uses. **[A village, R hamlet]**

## 7. Combat, the theatre, the Liberation

- Clandestine *Combat* from late 1943 under a false identity; Gallimard reader from 1943 until his death; meets Sartre in Paris 1943. **[A**: Britannica; IEP**]** The pseudonym "Beauchard" is **[R]**.
- **CORRECTED, the two plays.** Not both 1944. *Le Malentendu* premiered **24 June 1944**, Théâtre des Mathurins, dir. Marcel Herrand, **María Casarès** as Martha. *Caligula* premiered **June 1945 at the Comédie de Genève** and reached Paris **26 September 1945**, Théâtre Hébertot, with Gérard Philipe. **[A**: fr.wikipedia**]** Two stops; **no Geneva stop**, an honest gap.
- The Casarès affair from 1944, intermittent, until his death **[A**: en.wikipedia**]**; her father's premiership of the Spanish Republic is **[R]** here, true but outside the pool.
- *Combat* above ground August 1944 in the former *L'Intransigeant* offices; editorials on the purge and, in 1945, on the bomb. **[A**: en.wikipedia**]** Circulation figures **[R]**, hedged as "six figures."
- **CORRECTED: a stop-ownership problem, not a date problem.** The pool staged Camus at **Sétif** (36.1898 / 5.4108) in May 1945 as "one of the few mainland French journalists" reporting the massacres. He was not there: his Algerian trip ran through **April 1945**, before the 8 May uprising, and "Crise en Algérie" ran in *Combat* **13-23 May 1945** on famine and colonial policy, not as eyewitness reporting. **[A**: fr.wikipedia, "Massacres de Sétif, Guelma et Kherrata"**]** "Mainland French" is also wrong for an Algerian-born writer. The file **drops the Sétif pin** and reframes the stop as **Algiers, April 1945**.
- Twins Catherine and Jean born September 1945. **[A**: Britannica**]** No stop; the birth sits inside the Théâtre Hébertot campa, which is where their father was that month.

## 9. Algeria, Stockholm, Lourmarin, Villeblevin

- Algiers appeal for a civilian truce, January 1956, disrupted from both sides. **[R]**, widely reported, hall unconfirmed. *La Chute* 1956; *L'Exil et le royaume* 1957 **[A**: IEP**]**, the collection with **no stop**, cut for length.
- Nobel Prize, 10 December 1957, at 44. **[A**: IEP**]** The citation wording could **not** be reverified: nobelprize.org returned 403 to every attempt. The file paraphrases and never quotes it.
- **CORRECTED, the most consequential textual fix in the file.** The famous answer is usually given as "People are now planting bombs in the tramways of Algiers... If that is justice, then I prefer my mother." That is a **popular reconstruction**. Le Monde's **Dominique Birman**, present at the Maison des étudiants, Stockholm, **12 December 1957**, reported: *"J'ai toujours condamné la terreur... Je crois à la justice, mais je défendrai ma mère avant la justice."* **[A**: fr.wikipedia citing Birman**]** The file quotes **Birman** and says in the campa that the tramway version is the shortened form that travelled.
- Uppsala, "L'artiste et son temps" / "Create Dangerously," **14 December 1957**. **[A**: sv.wikipedia**]** The galley-and-herring image from that lecture is famous but outside the pool: paraphrased and hedged, not quoted.
- Lourmarin house bought with the prize money, 1958 **[A**: en.wikipedia**]**, the Char recommendation **[R]**. *Les Possédés*, Théâtre Antoine, January 1959 **[A**: Britannica**]**, with scene count, running time, and the state-theatre negotiation all **[R]**, hedged.
- **CORRECTED, the crash.** **Route nationale 5**, lieu-dit **Le Grand Fossard**, Villeblevin; renumbered RN 6 later, which is where the literature's "Nationale 6" comes from. **[A**: fr.wikipedia; es.wikipedia**]** Coordinates **48.32444 / 3.08233**; the pool's afterlife array had 48.2967 / 3.2506 for both crash and stele, ~15 km off.
- **CONTRADICTION, not resolved: the car.** fr.wikipedia, "Michel Gallimard": **FV3B**. en.wikipedia, "Facel Vega FV": **FV2**. The file says "the Facel Vega" and names the dispute in `date_confidence`. Michel Gallimard died **9 January 1960**, five days later, at the clinique Lyautey, Paris 16e; Janine and Anne Gallimard survived. **[A**: fr.wikipedia**]**
- **The unused train ticket.** Flagged **[R]** three times in the pool, including by the verifier: no primary citation anywhere reachable. The file **keeps it** and marks it unconfirmed in `date_confidence` instead of asserting it flat, as the gathering pass did.
- Manuscript of *Le Premier Homme*, 144 pages, recovered from the wreck; published 1994, ed. Catherine Camus. **[A**: Britannica; IEP**]** Buried Lourmarin, 6 January 1960; Francine later beside him **[A**: Britannica; en.wikipedia**]**. Sartre published a eulogy **[A**: Britannica**]**; the sentence quoted in the file is the standard English rendering, labelled as such in `quote_source`.

## 10. Afterlife carried as sources, never as stops

Each is a **later person's engagement with Camus**, so it stays out of the segments and rides in `sources` / `suggested_refs` on the stop for the work concerned: on the 1942 stop, the English translations (Gilbert 1946, sole version for thirty years; Laredo and Griffith 1982; **Ward 1988**, whose "the gentle indifference of the world" is closer to *la tendre indifférence du monde*) and **Kamel Daoud, *Meursault, contre-enquête*** (2013/2014) **[A**: en.wikipedia**]**, on which see §12; on the burial stop, Sarkozy's 2009-2010 Panthéon proposal, opposed by the family and not carried out **[A**: en.wikipedia**]**; on the crash stop, **Catelli's KGB theory** (Italian 2013, English 2020) **[R]**, whose gathering-pass sourcing (a Willsher byline in The Guardian, a rebuttal by Olivier Todd) **could not be verified**, so both are dropped and the stop states what did happen. The gathering pass had filed that theory in the *quotes* array; it is not a quotation. Attested but unused for want of a place: *La Mort heureuse* (1971), *Carnets* 1962-1989, the Casarès correspondence 2017.

## 11. Interlocks

**Named in a campa**, both running through Camus's own life:

- **`victoria_ocampo`**, twice. (1) **New York, 1946**: first meeting in person; she had already translated *Caligula* for *Sur*. (2) **Paris, 20 May 1953**: after her arrest in Buenos Aires, Camus, who had been her guest at Villa Ocampo, drafts a protest letter himself and takes it to the Argentine ambassador, alongside Huxley, Waldo Frank, Gabriela Mistral, and Nehru. **[A**: both stated directly in `victoria_ocampo.journey.json`, not inferred**]** Three pins inherited **byte-identical** from that file: New York **40.7128 / -74.006**, Paris **48.8566 / 2.3522**, Villa Ocampo **-34.45806 / -58.51833** (the pool offered a city-centre -34.6037 / -58.3816 for the villa; the Ocampo file's own villa stop governs).
- **`nietzsche`**, once, at the rue du Languedoc stop: Acault's shelves are where Camus meets him, and the reading runs into *Le Mythe de Sisyphe* and *L'Homme révolté*. An influence relation, not a co-location.

**Deliberately not named**, though the pool offered them: `jacques_derrida`, `julio_cortazar`, `isabelle_eberhardt`, `le_corbusier`, `abdelkader`, `dihya`. Each is shared geography or period and nothing else, which the map already shows; the pool tags most of them **[R]** itself. **María Casarès** is not an atlas traveler at all.
