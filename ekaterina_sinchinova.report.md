# Ekaterina Sinchinova (Katrin Vates): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-12 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `ekaterina_sinchinova`. Queue entry: QUEUE.md line 301.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim reaching us only through a search summary rather than a primary fetch. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; gaps are stated as gaps.

A structural note first. This subject is a **living artist whose documentary base is almost entirely self-published**: her own site, CV, blog, Instagram. One independent press notice exists (Bethesda Magazine / MoCo360); there is no catalogue, no museum record, no scholarly treatment. Nearly every biographical [A] below is therefore *attested to the artist's own account*, a weaker thing than attested to a document. The reverse holds for the *artwork*, which two existing atlas journeys cite by name.

## 5. "Domrémy": the corpus's title work

The piece the whole subject exists in the atlas for, and the organizing object of the 52-essay corpus (essay 212, `212_domremy_por_katrin_vates.md`), per `nodus_survey.md`.

**[A] The work is a commission.** Blog post "Domrémy. The story of the picture": "This picture is my first commissioned art." The client is named as Dr. Doeg (@drdoeg) **[R: WebFetch summary, not read verbatim]**.

**[R] Physical description.** 20x30 inches on linen, no hoop or frame; straight stitch, chain stitch, custom stitches, French knots; a well beside a large tree on a green hillside, a small tower with one window, a pink-and-blue sky. Same blog post, **via WebFetch summary rather than raw HTML**, so tagged [R] throughout, dimensions included.

**[A] Completion announced 19 January 2023.** Instagram @katrin.vates, reel `instagram.com/reel/CnmoJnOB2Nq`, caption verbatim:

> Domrémy / Сегодня окончила картину, ура ) / 💙💛 / Today I finished the picture ^^ / Domrémy (the town in France) / Later 'll take a photos and show the picture in details

Roughly 11,300 likes, multilingual comments. **Correction to an earlier pass**, which dated this post to December 2022, called it work "in progress", and hedged it as search aggregation. All three are wrong: it is a reel, dated 19 January 2023, announcing completion, read directly.

**[A] A separate, earlier in-progress post exists,** dated 22 December 2022, caption "The picture is called Domrémy, this is the town in France in the process". **[R] on wording:** this one was retrieved through DuckDuckGo results rather than the Instagram page, so its phrasing remains provisional. The two posts are distinct and are not merged.

**[A] The artist's own historical note,** verbatim from the blog post, in sequence (two further sentences of the same block, on the name la Pucelle and on the survival of the Church documents, are in the research pool and elided here for length):

- "Domrémy is a small village in the Champagne region of France which was the birthplace of Joan of Arc (1412-1431)."
- "Since Joan of Arc was tried for heresy under oath, we know much about her life from her own words, and those of people close to her."
- "This embroidery represents the landscape where Joan, starting at the age of 13, received visions of the archangels Michael and Gabriel, and the three virgin martyr saints: Catherine, Barbara and Margaret."
- "Testimony reveals the children of Domrémy would sing and dance around the giant beechwood 'fairy tree', and leave flower garlands on its branches, as gifts for the fairies."
- "The beechwood was also called the Tree of Bourlémont, named after the family of Joffrey Bourlémont."
- "Depicted in the image is Bourlémont tower overlooking the fairy tree, wellspring, and forest reported to be home to dragons."
- "A singular floral wreath is placed on a low-hanging branch of the giant 1000-year-old tree."

Two notes. First, **the artist names Barbara herself.** The interpolation the corpus credits her with is not the corpus reading a triad into her thread; it is written out in her own words on her own site, and it is the most load-bearing verified fact here. Second, **"the Champagne region" is her framing, not a fact**: Domrémy fell in the bailliage of Chaumont, an older convention, but lies in the Vosges. Recorded, not corrected inside her quotation.

**TWO CORRECTIONS TO EARLIER PASSES.** The Champagne / Joan of Arc / visions-at-thirteen framing was attributed to the **Instagram caption**, which read verbatim says only "Domrémy (the town in France)"; the framing belongs to the blog post and the misattribution is removed. And the fairy-tree reference, earlier recorded as an unattested gap, is closed: the blog post names the fairy tree, the wellspring, the tower, the Bourlémont family, and the garland. The queue brief's claim is confirmed from the artist's own mouth.

## 7. Substantive corrections to the Joan material

Three pool claims are wrong on the history, not just the placement, and are not carried into the journey file as written:

1. **Patay.** The pool has her "present with the vanguard". English Wikipedia states the reverse: "she was in fact not present for the battle as she had remained with the main body of the French army." The vanguard was La Hire's and Xaintrailles's. Patay was dropped from the journey file rather than restated.
2. **Bois Chenu.** The pool has her first hearing Saint Michael at the wood's edge. Her own testimony places the first vision c. 1425, aged thirteen, in her **father's garden**, the voice coming from the direction of the church. Bois-Chenu is the commemorative site the nineteenth-century basilica marks, tied to the prophecy of a maid from that wood. The journey stop is tagged commemorative.
3. **Rouen, the trial's length.** The pool has "some seventy sessions", conflating the seventy articles of accusation (later cut to twelve) with the sittings. The trial ran fifteen interrogation sessions, 21 February to 17 March 1431, then the ordinary trial from 26 March.

Two of date: **Troyes** capitulated with Charles VII entering 10 July 1429, not 9 July; **Chinon** received her 6 March 1429, Charles meeting her two days later. No "Sainte-Catherine hall" is documented at Chinon, the Catherine association belonging to Fierbois.
