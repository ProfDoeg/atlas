# Wifredo Viladrich, sculptor (Buenos Aires 1923 to Buenos Aires 1976)
*Research report, 2026-08-14. Queue entry: "Viladrich", listed as "Wíeredovol Viladrich", sculptor of the Crociati tomb at Recoleta.*

**The queue's name is wrong, and so are two of the three corrections in circulation.** The subject is **Wifredo Viladrich**, Catalan spelling, no L in the first syllable: son of the Catalan painter Miguel Viladrich, born Buenos Aires 28 April 1923, died Buenos Aires 13 May 1976. Four spellings circulate (see *Contradictions*, item 1); the estate site and Wikidata agree on **Wifredo**, and his own 1958 statuette of the count **Wifredo el Velloso** corroborates the form. Record him as *Wifredo Viladrich*, with the other three as variants.

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or tradition, not documented as stated.

---

## 1. Origins and childhood, 1923 to 1934

- Born Buenos Aires, **28 April 1923**, son of the Catalan painter **Miguel Viladrich** and **Ana Morera**, described as a *profesora* (teacher of painting). **[A: wifredoviladrich.com, "Síntesis biográfica"; birth date independently in Wikidata Q110450265, sourced there to artedelaargentina.com]**
- The family moved to Spain in **1925**, settling at the **Castillo de Urganda, Fraga, Aragón**, the house the painter had made his own. **[A: wifredoviladrich.com]**
- **1932 to 1934**: the household lived in **Morocco**. No city, no reason, no detail. **[A for the years: wifredoviladrich.com] [gap: place unknown]**
- The journey file's claim that the boy learned the trade from his father's sitters (reapers, water carriers, girls with pitchers) is **[R]**: Miguel Viladrich's Aragonese peasant subjects are well known, but no source describes Wifredo watching him work at Fraga. His schooling and first teacher: **[gap]**

## 2. Madrid, Barcelona, the war, and the Pyrenees, 1934 to 1939

- After Morocco the family lived **in Madrid and then Barcelona**, "donde el artista completó su formación artística". No years are attached to either city. **[A for the sequence and the cities; R for the 1934 and 1936 dates used in the journey file, which are placed for ordering only: wifredoviladrich.com]**
- He experienced the Spanish Civil War directly, "cuando tenía entre 13 y 15 años", that is 1936 to 1938 inclusive. **[A: wifredoviladrich.com]**
- **1939**: "horas antes de que las tropas franquistas la ocuparan, salió con su familia de Barcelona. Cruzaron a pie los Pirineos, rumbo al exilio en la Argentina." Barcelona fell 26 January 1939; the journey file uses that day, which the family account does not state. **[A for the flight and the crossing: wifredoviladrich.com] [R for the day]**
- **Which pass, the internment (if any) in the French camps, the port, the ship, and the arrival date in Argentina are all unrecorded.** The journey file pins Le Perthus (42.4671, 2.8639), the principal Retirada crossing, as a declared placeholder. **[gap]**

## 3. Catamarca and the salons, 1939 to 1950

- The family settled in **Catamarca**, "donde comenzó a esculpir". He was sixteen. **[A: wifredoviladrich.com]** Whether the whole family stayed, how they lived, and who if anyone taught him there: **[gap]**.
- **El Guaica, muchacho catamarqueño**, the bust that launched him. The pool dates the modelling 1940, at seventeen; the prize list dates only the award, and the catalogue dates a museum version 1949. See *Contradictions*, item 2. **[A for the 1942 prize and 1949 acquisition; R for 1940]**
- **1942**: "PREMIO ESTÍMULO, XXXII SALÓN ANUAL DE BELLAS ARTES" (sculpture), for *El Guaica*. **[A: wifredoviladrich.com, "Premios obtenidos"]**
- **From 1944 he lived in Buenos Aires**, "donde continuó su carrera artística y docente". The teaching is asserted without detail: no institution, no years. **[A for the residence and the fact of teaching; gap for where he taught]**
- **1944**: 2nd prize with acquisition, **Salón Municipal**, for *Hijo de la tierra, Jujuy*; the bust is catalogued in the **Museo Sívori**, Buenos Aires. **[A: "Premios obtenidos" and "Listado de obras en lugares públicos"]**
- **c. 1945 to 1946, Pueblo Español.** His first full statue, made at twenty-two: "imagen de un hombre desnudo y encadenado, que adquirió en 1946 gran repercusión: Wifredo se negó a cambiarle el nombre, como le exigiera el jurado como condición para ser expuesta y premiada en el Salón Municipal." **[A: wifredoviladrich.com]** The prize list shows no award for 1946, which is consistent. The journey file's gloss (that Franco's Spain was a government Buenos Aires did business with, hence the intolerable title) is **[R]**: no source states the jury's motive. Present whereabouts of the statue: **[gap]**. The research pool put this episode at the **National** Salon; the source says **Salón Municipal**. Corrected.
- **1948**: special mention, Sociedad Hebraica, for *Retrato de A.M.* and *Sarita*. **[A]** (not given a stop in the journey file.)
- **1949**: acquisition prize, VIII Salón de Arte de Mar del Plata, *El Guaica*; 2nd prize, IV Salón de Bahía Blanca, *Hombre de Belén*. **[A]**
- **1950**: encouragement prize, IX Salón de Arte de Mar del Plata, *Chacho, el boyero*. **[A]**

The pattern of these years is uniform and documented: northwestern types (a Catamarcan boy, a man of Belén, an ox driver, a son of the earth from Jujuy), sent to provincial and national salons, bought one at a time by municipalities and state collections.

## 4. The working decades, 1951 to 1969

- **1951**: 2nd prize, Sociedad Argentina de Artistas Plásticos (work not named). **[A]**
- **1958**: two works installed at the **Argentine consulate in Barcelona**, a relief of **General San Martín** and a statuette of **Wifredo el Velloso**. **[A: "Listado de obras en lugares públicos"]** The second is the strongest internal evidence for the spelling of his own name.
- **1960**: honorable mention, historical art, XLIX Salón Nacional, for *Viejo Criollo* ("Martín Fierro"). **[A]**
- **1962**: *Fragatina, moza de cántaro*, statuette, **Hispanic Society, New York**. **[A]** The title means a girl of Fraga, the town of his childhood.
- **1963 to 1970**: five **Monumentos a la Madre**: Christophersen (1963), Lima (1965), María Teresa (1968), Vicente López (1970), Sancti Spíritu (1970). **[A]**
- **1964**: **Monumento a Sarmiento**, Lanús. **[A]**
- **1966**: his best prize year. *Pacha Mama*, stone head: Premio Justina y Manuel Quinquela, LV Salón Nacional, and an acquisition by the Dirección Nacional de Cultura. *Tamborilera*: Premio Sesostris Vitulo, Salón Municipal. **[A]**
- **1970**: decorative panel *Juventud, Armonía y Belleza*, "Saverio", Buenos Aires. **[A]**

No source reached gives anything personal from these two decades: no marriage, no children, no address, no studio, no illness, no politics after 1946, no travel (the Barcelona and New York works were shipped; nothing says he accompanied them). **[gap, and a large one: roughly 1951 to 1969 is a career record with no life in it]**

## 5. The competition years, 1970 to 1973

The whole of his monumental career falls inside four years.

- **1970**: 1st prize and award, **Monumento General Pueyrredón**; 2nd prize, **Monumento General Güemes**. **[A: "Premios obtenidos"]**
- **1971**: 1st prize and award, **Monumento Almirante Brown**, Mar del Plata, and, listed separately in the same year, **2nd prize** for the same monument. He appears twice on one result. **[A, and odd; reproduced as given]**
- **1971**: **Monumento a J. M. de Pueyrredón**, Plaza República de Chile, Buenos Aires. **[A: catalogue]**
- **1972**: 1st prize and award, **Monumento "Alas de la Patria"**, Mar del Plata; and the **Monumento Almirante G. Brown** catalogued in this year. See *Contradictions*, item 3. **[A]**
- **1973**: **Monumento "A las Alas de la Patria"**, Mar del Plata. **[A]**

The research pool's claim that *Alas de la Patria* was replicated in three cities including Río Cuarto is **wrong**: the catalogue shows two (Mar del Plata 1973, Buenos Aires 1981), and the Río Cuarto work is a different subject, *Mto. a los Héroes de Malvinas*, 1984.

## 6. The Crociati tomb, Recoleta

- **Liliana Crociati de Szaszak**, born 1944, died **26 February 1970**, aged 26, when an avalanche struck the **Hotel Piz Buin** in the Tyrol; her husband **Janos Szaszak** survived. **[A: en.wikipedia, "Tomb of Liliana Crociati de Szaszak"; es.wikipedia, "Tumba de Liliana Crociati de Szaszak"]** en.wikipedia calls the trip a honeymoon ski trip; es.wikipedia does not characterise it. **[contradiction of emphasis, flagged]**
- **Location of the hotel**: es.wikipedia places it "64 kilómetros al oeste de Innsbruck", but the Piz Buin massif is reached from **Galtür**, roughly 98 km out. Distance and name do not agree; the journey file pins Galtür (46.9681, 10.1869) and says so. **[unresolved]**
- **The tomb**: neo-Gothic, of wood and glass with wide windows, **designed by Liliana's mother**; a **dais with an Italian poem by her father**, *A Mia Figlia*, beside the figure. **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]** No construction or unveiling year is given anywhere. **[gap]**
- **The statue**: life size, green-patinated bronze, Liliana in her wedding dress wearing her engagement ring. **[A: en.wikipedia, citing Cansino, "Cemetery For the Elite of Argentina", *NYT*, 10 January 1999]**
- **The statue is dated 1972** by the sculptor's own catalogue: "Liliana Crociati (1972) – Cementerio Recoleta". **[A: wifredoviladrich.com]** The pool's "1970" was an unsourced inference from the death; en.wikipedia gives no year. See *Contradictions*, item 4.
- **Sabú**: after the dog's death a second bronze was made and added, the bride's right hand resting on its head. **[A: en.wikipedia]** No date for the dog's death or the second casting exists in any source reached. **[gap]**
- **The luck belief**: touching the dog's snout brings good luck, and the muzzle is visibly polished. This is **not** in the English article, contrary to the research pool's sourcing; it is in **es.wikipedia** ("El hocico del perro suele verse brillante ya que existe la creencia popular de que tocarlo trae buena suerte") and in Argentine press. **[A, re-sourced]**
- **Coordinates**: es.wikipedia gives the tomb -34.588395, -58.3931563. The journey file nevertheless uses the shared Recoleta pin **-34.5877, -58.3931** for all cemetery stops, per the byte-identical convention. The two differ by about 80 m. Noted, not silent.

## 7. Death and posthumous installations

- **Died Buenos Aires, 13 May 1976, aged 53**, leaving "una producción escultórica de trescientas ochenta y tres obras entre relieves, estatuillas, retratos, bustos, estatuas y monumentos". **[A: wifredoviladrich.com; Wikidata Q110450265]** Cause of death and burial place: **[gap]**
- **1977**: the Brown monument installed in the *plaza seca* of the **Rambla Casino** complex, Mar del Plata, in the spot **Alejandro Bustillo** had designed for a monolithic obelisk. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Rambla Casino", unfootnoted within that article]**
- **1979**: *Humahuaqueño Indio de la quena*, Ayuntamiento de Barcelona. **1981**: *Monumento "Alas de la Patria"*, Buenos Aires. **1984**: *Mto. a los Héroes de Malvinas*, Río Cuarto, Córdoba. **1996**: *Monumento a Martín Fierro*, General Madariaga. **2001** and **2018**: *Adelante Juventud en marcha*, Pinamar and Isla del Golf, Pinamar. **[A: public-works catalogue]**
- Everything after 1973 was raised posthumously. **Which were finished models left in the studio, which were later casts, and who executed them, is nowhere stated.** **[gap]**
- The 1981 stop's framing (a monument to the fliers being useful to the government of that year, a year before the South Atlantic war) is **[R]**, a juxtaposition, not a sourced claim about the commission.

## 8. Afterlife of the work

- The tomb is popularly **la novia de la Recoleta** and is among the cemetery's most visited and photographed. **[A: infobae, 2019]** That this status accumulated through guidebooks and press rather than at one rediscovery moment is **[R]**, inferred from the sequence NYT 1999, Calgary Herald 2007, infobae 2019.
- One secondary account says the statue's left hand holds flowers left by an unknown admirer. Uncorroborated, possibly folkloric. **[R]** Not given a stop.
- **Both parents were eventually interred with their daughter.** **[A: infobae]** No years given. **[gap]**
- No monograph, catalogue raisonné, or published study of Viladrich was found; the estate site is the compiled archive. **[R, an absence]**

## Contradictions, flagged and not resolved

1. **The name, four spellings.** *Wíeredovol* (en.wikipedia body text, traceable to the dead blog touristtravel.com) · *Wilfredo* (Calgary Herald 2007 via en.wikipedia; and es.wikipedia "Rambla Casino") · *Wílfredo* (es.wikipedia, "Tumba de Liliana Crociati de Szaszak") · *Wifredo* (Wikidata Q110450265; wifredoviladrich.com). es.wikipedia is inconsistent between its own two articles. The estate and Wikidata are preferred here for a stated reason: the Catalan form matches his Catalan father and his own *Wifredo el Velloso*.
2. **El Guaica, three dates.** Modelled 1940 at seventeen (research pool, unsourced) · prize 1942 (artist's prize list) · "El Guaica (busto), 1949, Museo Municipal, Mar del Plata" (artist's public-works catalogue) plus an acquisition prize at the VIII Salón de Mar del Plata in 1949 (prize list). Most likely one bust, one prize, one later museum acquisition, but no source says so.
3. **The Brown monument, 1972 or 1977.** The artist's catalogue: "Monumento Almirante G. Brown, 1972, Mar del Plata". es.wikipedia "Rambla Casino": installed in the plaza seca in 1977. Both can be true of a work completed in one year and erected in another, and no source reconciles them. The 1971 prize list is consistent with either.
4. **The Crociati commission, 1970 or 1972.** Press accounts (Calgary Herald and others, via en.wikipedia) describe a grieving father commissioning the statue in the aftermath of the February 1970 death. The artist's catalogue dates the bronze 1972. Two years are unaccounted for. Left open.
5. **The Brown competition result, 1971.** The prize list records both a 1st prize with award and a 2nd prize to Viladrich for the same monument in the same year. Reproduced as printed; unexplained.
6. **The avalanche site.** "64 km west of Innsbruck" (es.wikipedia) against the Piz Buin name, whose gateway village Galtür is roughly 98 km out. Unresolved.
7. **The honeymoon.** en.wikipedia: honeymoon ski trip. es.wikipedia: no characterisation of the trip. Minor, but the romantic framing of the tomb rests partly on it.

## Gaps, stated as gaps

- No address, marriage, children, or descendants named anywhere reached; nothing personal at all from 1951 to 1969, only prizes and installations. The estate site is a professional record, not a biography.
- Training: "completó su formación artística" in Madrid and Barcelona, with no school, teacher, or dates.
- The 1939 route: pass, camps, port, ship, arrival date, all unknown.
- Cause of death, funeral, burial place: unknown, no obituary retrieved. No photograph or physical description of him retrieved either.
- The execution of the posthumous works (1977 to 2018) is undocumented, as are the dates of Sabú's death, the second casting, the tomb's completion, and the parents' burials.
