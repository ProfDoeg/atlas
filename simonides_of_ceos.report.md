# Simonides of Ceos (c. 556 - 468/467 BCE): research report

*Slug: `simonides_of_ceos`. Compiled 2026-08-29 from the verified research pool, with the pool's own correction notes applied. Journey file: `simonides_of_ceos.journey.json`, 7 segments, 32 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Conflicting sources are both printed, neither preferred. In the journey file the hedge always lives in `date_confidence`, never in the campa prose.

The governing difficulty: almost nothing about Simonides reaches us from his own century. One epitaph is attributed to him by a historian writing two generations later; one long poem was pulled out of an Egyptian rubbish mound in torn strips. Everything else is Hellenistic biography, Roman rhetoric, and anecdote, all of it working on a name that had already become a type: the poet who charged.

---

## 1. Identity, dates, family

- Born at **Ioulis on Ceos (Kea)**, Cyclades. **[A]** Suda sigma 439: "A citizen of the city Iulis on the island Ceos."
- **Birth date, unresolved. [R]** Suda sigma 439 gives the 56th Olympiad (556/552 BCE) and in the same sentence reports a variant, "though some have written the 62nd" (532/528). The pool's original framing, two Olympiads given "in one place and another" of the entry, was wrong and is corrected: one sentence, one reported variant.
- **Lifespan, unresolved. [A vs A]** Parian Marble A 57 says he died aged **ninety**; Suda sigma 439 says **eighty-nine**. One year apart, from the two best ancient chronological witnesses. The conventional birth date of c. 556 is derived by counting ninety years back from 468/467, so it is not independent evidence.
- **Father Leoprepes. [A]** Herodotus 7.228, Marmor Parium A 54, and the tomb epitaph in Callimachus all carry the patronymic.
- **Bacchylides as nephew. [R]** Later tradition; kinship not secured contemporaneously.
- **Mother, siblings, spouse, children: no record. [A as absence]** No ancient source names any of them.
- **No trial, indictment, or exile decree** is recorded anywhere in the surviving testimonia. **[A as absence]**

## 2. Ceos: the island phase

- **Birth at Ioulis [A]**, coordinates corrected (section 10).
- **Chorus training at Karthaia [R].** No contemporary source names the post; it is inferred from his profession and Ceos's documented choral culture, via the Suda and Molyneux (1992). The site itself is excavated and securely identified.
- **Delos [R].** Cean choral delegations to Apollo's Delian festival are documented; no Simonidean text or testimonium puts Simonides there. Included as the working circuit of a Cean chorus master, and the stop says exactly that.

## 3. Athens under the Peisistratids

- **Hipparchus brings him to Athens [A].** Aristotle (or pseudo-Aristotle), *Ath. Pol.* 18, and pseudo-Plato, *Hipparchus* 228b-c: Hipparchus drew Simonides, with Anacreon, to Athens. The *Hipparchus* is not Plato's and is a defence of the Peisistratids, so it is interested testimony.
- **Age thirty at arrival [R].** A biographical round number, absent from the primary texts.
- **Rivalry with Lasus of Hermione [R].** The pool tagged this **[A]** together with the invitation; the verification pass caught the over-reach, since neither text mentions Lasus, festivals, or commissions. The file splits them: invitation attested, Lasus rivalry later tradition.
- **Assassination of Hipparchus, 514 [A]:** Thucydides 6.54-59, *Ath. Pol.* 18, Herodotus 5.55-57. **Simonides' presence that day is [R]**, inferred from the patronage ending.
- **Topography, unresolved.** Thucydides 6.57 puts the killing at the **Leokoreion**, whose site is disputed between the Kerameikos and the Agora, so the pin is a scholarly choice. The pool had given the Kerameikos the Agora's coordinates outright (section 10).

## 4. Thessaly, and the memory story

The most cited and least secure material in the life. The strata, oldest first:

1. **Rhetorica ad Herennium 3.16-24** (c. 80s BCE) **[A]**: earliest surviving full technical exposition of ordered *loci* and *imagines* in Latin. **Names no inventor, tells no story.**
2. **Marmor Parium A 54** (264/263 BCE) **[A]**: calls him *ho to mnemonikon heuron*, the man who found the art of memory. The invention claim, with no banquet.
3. **Cicero, De Oratore 2.351-354** (written 55 BCE, set 91 BCE) **[A as text, R as history]**: earliest surviving full narrative of the Crannon banquet, hedged by Cicero himself as "is said to have." Verified verbatim by the pool, including "at Crannon in Thessaly," the two youths, and the identification of the dead by seat.
4. **Pliny, Natural History 7.24** (77 CE) **[A]**: Simonides devised the art, Metrodorus of Scepsis perfected it. **No banquet.**
5. **Quintilian, Inst. Or. 11.2.11-17** (c. 95 CE) **[A]**: reports the disagreements, rejects the miracle.
6. **Suda sigma 439** **[A]**: "He also invented the art of artificial memory."

**Contradictions inside the tradition, printed and not resolved:**

| point | version A | version B |
|---|---|---|
| town | Crannon (Apollas, Callimachus, Cicero) | Pharsalus (Apollodorus, Eratosthenes, Euphorion, Eurypylus, citing Simonides' own verse) |
| the dead patron | Scopas | Glaucus of Carystus / Leocrates / Agatharcus |
| the rescue | Castor and Pollux call him out (Cicero) | "purely fictitious," since the poet nowhere mentions so flattering an event (Quintilian) |
| the invention | Simonides invented it (Parian Marble, Pliny, Suda) | method expounded with no inventor named (Rhetorica ad Herennium) |

Quintilian notes only that Cicero's version "gained wider circulation." The journey file gives **both towns their own stop**, each `date_confidence` naming the other as its rival. It does not adjudicate.

**Modern revision [R].** Joe Grimwade, *Memory in Roman Oratory* (Cambridge PhD thesis, 2021), argues Simonides should be removed from the literal invention history, an Alexandrian legend of a hall collapse having been fused with a genuinely later Hellenistic doctrine. A significant recent thesis, **not** confirmed against the thesis text and **not** settled consensus. Carried only as a `suggested_ref`.

**Other Thessalian material.** The Scopadae and Aleuadae as patrons: **[R]**, plausible and multiply attested as tradition, no securely dated commission. The ode to Scopas: **[A as text]**, quoted at length in Plato, *Protagoras* 339a-347a. **Correction applied:** at 339b the poem is produced by **Protagoras**, to trap Socrates; Socrates dissects it only afterwards. The file's `quote_source` names Protagoras.

## 5. The Persian Wars

- **Marathon competition against Aeschylus [R].** Only in the anonymous *Vita Aeschyli*, late and shaped like a rivalry topos. Establishes neither the contest nor Aeschylus' motive. The epigram does not survive.
- **Megistias epitaph [A].** Herodotus 7.228 names Simonides son of Leoprepes as author, composed out of guest-friendship. **The single most secure attribution in the corpus.**
- **"Stranger, tell the Lacedaemonians" [R].** Herodotus quotes the couplet in the same chapter and **names no author**, three lines from where he does name Simonides. The attribution is later and modern editors stay cautious. The file splits the two Thermopylae epitaphs into separate stops so the difference in evidence is visible.
- **Artemisium and Salamis poems [R].** Attested by title only (*He ep' Artemisioi naumachia*, *He en Salamini naumachia*); nothing substantial survives; ancient sources describe the genre inconsistently.
- **Athenian, Spartan, Corinthian epitaphs [R].** Transmitted under his name in the Greek Anthology; modern editors judge only a minority securely his, the rest attracted to the most famous name in the genre.
- **Plataea elegy [A].** P.Oxy. LIX 3965 (2nd c. CE roll, ed. Parsons, 1992), with P.Oxy. 2327 and a Plutarch quotation: roughly forty-seven lines, opening on Achilles and Troy, then the Muse, then Pausanias and the campaign. Existence and content secure; **performance context debated and unrecoverable**. See the fetch failure in Sources.

## 6. The professional career

- **Chorus victory at Athens [A].** Marmor Parium A 54, archonship of **Adeimantos, 477/476 BCE**. Three corrections applied: the entry is **A 54**, not the "§59-60" the pool cited (the death is A 57); the year is 477/476, not 476/475; and **the stone does not call him "of Cos."** The Greek reads *Simonides ho Leoprepous ho Keios ho to mnemonikon heuron*, "the Cean." The Cos error appeared in three of the pool's lenses and is false in all three. Struck from the file entirely.
- **Confusable entry, flagged.** Another Parian Marble entry gives an Athenian poetic victory in 489/488 to a Simonides commonly identified as a homonymous **grandfather**. The stop warns of it.
- **Fifty-seven dithyrambic victories [R].** One anonymous Greek Anthology epigram, of unknown date and author. No archive can audit the figure.
- **The mule-cart ode [A for the fragment, R for the anecdote].** PMG 515 is quoted as his by Aristotle, *Rhetoric* 3.2.1405b; the refusal-then-fee story is Aristotle's report. No victor, festival, or year named, so the Olympia pin is the likeliest venue and not an attested one.
- **"Painting is silent poetry" [R].** Attributed by Plutarch, *De Gloria Atheniensium* 346f, five and a half centuries later, in more than one formulation. Reception fact solid, wording not.
- **Danae fragment, PMG 543 [A].** Preserved entire by Dionysius of Halicarnassus as a specimen of word arrangement; the longest continuous lyric passage of his to survive. **Placeless and undated**, pinned at the Athenian coordinates purely to order the segment.
- **The mercenary reputation [A as reputation].** Aristophanes, *Peace* (421 BCE), uses his name as shorthand for an aged poet who would do anything for money: the stereotype is attested within sixty years of his death, whatever any single anecdote is worth.

## 7. Sicily, death, and the tomb

- **Hieron's court at Syracuse [R].** Broadly accepted as tradition, itinerary unrecoverable. The court-rivalry stories involving Pindar and Bacchylides are **late scholiastic inference**, as `pindar.journey.json` flags on its own side.
- **Wealth or wisdom [A as anecdote].** Aristotle, *Rhetoric* 2.16.2 (1391a), attributes the exchange to Simonides directly, a century-plus later, as a genre piece. Hieron's wife is not named.
- **The provisions sold [A as report].** Athenaeus, c. 200 CE, six and a half centuries after the fact.
- **Reconciliation of Hieron and Theron [R].** Scholia to Pindar credit him with the mediation; **Diodorus Siculus narrates the same reconciliation without mentioning Simonides at all.** Printed in the stop's `date_confidence`, not resolved.
- **Death [A for year and age, R for place].** Marmor Parium A 57: aged ninety, archonship of Theagenides, 468/467. **No ancient source gives a place.** Sicily, and Acragas specifically, is inferred backward from the tomb tradition. Day, house, cause, and funeral unknown.
- **The tomb at Acragas [R].** Callimachus, *Aetia* fr. 64 Harder (= 64 Pfeiffer = 163 Massimilla): the Acragantines build it "fearing Zeus" the god of strangers, a general named **Phoenix** demolishes it into the city wall, the ghost speaks. **Archaeology has authenticated neither tomb nor demolition.** This Hellenistic fiction is the entire ancient basis for locating the grave at Acragas at all.
- **The drowned man and the warning ghost [R].** Two Greek Anthology epigrams of contested authorship. The pool could not confirm the epigram numbers. **Not used** in the journey file, having no place and no verified text; recorded here so it is not lost.

## 8. Quotations: verified, and three corrections

Verified verbatim and used: Palatine Anthology 7.677, 7.249, 7.248, 7.250, 7.253 (attalus.org, the security of each attribution stated in its own stop); Plato, *Protagoras* 339b (Jowett), speaker corrected per section 4; Cicero, *De Oratore* 2.354; Aristotle, *Rhetoric* 3.2.1405b; Plutarch 346f, with a quote_source naming Plutarch as reporter since the wording is his; Callimachus' tomb inscription; Xenophon, *Hiero*, Dakyns. **Locus correction on the Xenophon:** the Wikisource page prints no section numbers and the question falls at 1.1, not the pool's 1.2, so the file cites *Hiero* 1 and states that the dialogue is a fourth-century fiction, not a record of speech.

**Two pool quotations rejected:**

1. "To tell the truth and pay your debts? No more than this? Or must we admit exceptions?", attributed in the pool to *Republic* 331d-e. The words are **Jowett's own Introduction and Analysis** summarizing Book I, **not the dialogue**. Quoting it as Plato would attribute a translator's paraphrase to the dialogue. Dropped, not substituted: Simonides' definition of justice is Cephalus' citation and belongs to a discussion of him, not to a place on a map.
2. "Would it not be simpler if you, on your side, who are still to-day a private person...", attributed in the pool to Simonides at *Hiero* 1.3. The speaker is **Hiero**. Dropped entirely.

## 11. Apparatus relations for the operator

Simonides is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops were licensed and none were written. Two clusters of relation are not rooted in his own life; neither has been silently deleted.

**Direction: forward. The art-of-memory descent.** Everything from the *Rhetorica ad Herennium* onward is downstream of him: Cicero, Quintilian, Pliny, Callimachus' tomb poem, the Parian Marble's invention clause, then Llull, Camillo, Bruno, Ricci, Warburg, Yates. `cicero.journey.json` already carries the whole lineage as a suggested_ref on its own Crannon stop. A forward reach is not licensable by an agent and none was attempted; the material appears here only as sources and suggested_refs on Simonides' own stops, which the standing rule permits. If the operator wants the descent narrated rather than cited, that is a ruling for `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and it would be a forward grant of the Burroughs and Derrida kind, not the ordinary backward exception.

**Direction: forward, minor. The reception of the mercenary type.** Aristophanes' *Peace*, Plato's two citations, Aristotle's two anecdotes, Xenophon's *Hiero*, and Athenaeus all postdate him and all shape what "Simonides" means. Cited as sources and one quote_source, none staged. Flagged because the *Hiero* quotation puts words in his mouth that he never spoke; its stop says so.

**No backward relation was found** that would need the exception. Simonides has no framework to apply to anything, and the art of memory is attributed to him by antiquity rather than read onto him later.

## 12. Honest gaps

- **Where he died.** Undocumented. Sicily is an inference from a poem written two centuries later.
- **When anything was written.** Not one poem can be dated to a year by a contemporary source. Every date in the journey file except 477/476 and 468/467 orders a segment rather than records an event, and each `date_confidence` says so.
- **The family.** Mother, wife, children: nothing. Bacchylides as nephew is tradition.
- **The Marathon epigram.** The winning text does not survive.
- **Corpus proportions.** Of the epigrams under his name, only a minority are judged securely his, and no criterion separates them cleanly.
- **P.Oxy. LIX 3965**, **Grimwade 2021**, **Yates 1966 ch. 1**: not read directly, see Sources.
