# Marie Curie: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `marie_curie.journey.json`. Maria Salomea Skłodowska-Curie, Warsaw 7 November 1867 to Passy, Haute-Savoie, 4 July 1934. Calendar gregorian: the life postdates 1582, no conversion arises. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. File: 9 segments, 45 stops, 13 carrying quotations.*

**[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition, or a claim from one compiled source not pushed back to a primary. Contradictions are printed and left open unless a named source settles them, in which case the losing reading is still printed. The pool came in five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife); an unusual share of their notes are coordinate corrections, all applied and tabulated in §7.

**The Sorbonne pin, wrong four times over.** The pool gave 48.8462/2.3444 in four items (enrolment, Lippmann's laboratory, the 1903 defence, the 1906 lecture). 48.8462 is the latitude of the **Panthéon**, itself a stop in this file: uncorrected, the error would have stacked her university life on her tomb.

**London, June 1903 [A for the invitation and the exclusion, R for the venue].** en.wikipedia "Pierre Curie": the Royal Society invited Pierre to present the work, **Marie Curie was not permitted to deliver it**, Kelvin sat beside her while Pierre spoke, the Davy Medal followed later in 1903. The pool called the whole thing unconfirmed; genuinely [R] is only the venue, the Royal Institution at 21 Albemarle Street.

**1903 Nobel Prize in Physics [A], and a pool stop removed over it.** December 1903, half to the Curies jointly and half to Becquerel; **neither travelled to Stockholm that year**. The geography lens carried "Swedish Academy, Stockholm," dated 1905-06, at 59.3251/18.0711. Three faults: the prize is awarded by the **Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences**, not the Swedish Academy, which is the literature body; 59.3251/18.0711 is the Börshuset, the Swedish Academy's building; and the lecture was given **by Pierre**, 6 June 1905, Marie present but not speaking. The lecture is stated inside the 1903 prize stop, at Paris, and no 1905 Stockholm stop is staged.

**Fuller and the refused costume [A, `loie_fuller.journey.json`].** Fuller proposes a radium-treated luminous costume, the Curies refuse, she makes the dance from salts she already knew. That file places it at Rue Lhomond, 48.8419/2.3453, dated 1904-01-01, and flags as legend the story that the Curies gave her radium which burned her hands. Pin inherited byte-identical.

**The chair: a contradiction resolved by a named source.** The chronology lens gave **13 May 1906** for "awarded the chair and delivers her first lecture"; the geography lens gave **5 November 1906** for the lecture. 13 May is the date the physics department decided to keep Pierre's chair and offer it to her (en.wikipedia); the lecture was 5 November, per pl.wikipedia "Maria Skłodowska-Curie", *"Pierwszy wykład prowadziła 5 listopada 1906 r."* One stop, dated 1906-11-05, conflation stated in `date_confidence`.

**Rodin at Meudon [R, and weak].** The dossier cites NYPL Loie Fuller correspondence arranging a Curie visit to Rodin, undated, with no distinction between the Meudon villa and the Paris studio, and `auguste_rodin.journey.json` has no mention of Curie. Kept as a stop because it is Curie's own alleged visit and not another person's scene, and because silent deletion is the failure mode this pipeline is guarded against. Pin inherited byte-identical from the Fuller file (48.8123/2.2367); the confidence field states the visit is unverified and the 1908 date an ordering device.

**Académie des sciences, 23 January 1911 [A for date and defeat, contested as to margin].** Lost to Édouard Branly amid a press campaign invoking her sex, her nationality, and a false allegation of Jewishness. The dossier says "by two votes"; en.wikipedia says "failed, **by one or two votes**, to elect her," and a 30 to 28 tally circulates cited to no named source. The file says "by one or two votes." The doorkeeper instructed to admit everyone except women is **[R]**.

**First Solvay [A, Wikipedia "Solvay Conference"].** Hotel Metropole, Brussels, 30 October to 3 November 1911; **Einstein met her in person here for the first time [A, `einstein.journey.json`]**. Pin inherited byte-identical from the Einstein file, 50.8503/4.3517, over 50.8497/4.3547. During the conference the nationalist press, including Gustave Téry's paper, publishes the stolen Langevin letters; a mob at her house **[A]**.

**The Fuller letter of 24 November 1911: tag downgraded, the file's most consequential evidence decision.** Both lenses tagged it **[A]** and the geography lens attached a **verbatim quotation**, "I love you and beg you to pay no attention to the lies." But the chronology lens's own source field concedes it could not be verified against a primary archive, and en.wikipedia "Loïe Fuller" records no correspondence with Curie at all, only that Fuller's work "attracted the attention, respect, and friendship of many French artists and scientists, including ... Marie Curie." A dated letter with a quoted line cannot carry [A] on a dossier's say-so. **The file tags it [R], keeps the quotation, and names in `quote_source` that it is dossier-cited to the NYPL collection and unverified.** The pin is the canonical Paris coordinate, declared as such, because the lens had used the Hôtel de Ville centroid as though it were a place.

**Petites Curies: figures split.** en.wikipedia gives **20 mobile radiological vehicles and 200 units at field hospitals in the first year**, which is what the campa uses. The often-quoted **150 women trained as technicians** is the dossier's and is **not** in the encyclopaedia entry; it appears only in `date_confidence`, labelled as such. The pin (49.0472/3.9564) is a representative Marne point: the units moved constantly, no site is documented, and 1914-11-01 orders a stop covering four years.

**Palais Wilson: a contradiction the interlock wins, flagged not resolved.** The geography lens gave 46.2136/6.1478 and its own verifier corrected that to 46.2231/6.1479 (the Palais des Nations opened only in 1936), but `henri_bergson.journey.json` already holds the event at **46.2149/6.1511**, and interlock pins are inherited byte-identical, so the file uses the Bergson figure. The two differ by roughly 900 m; an operator wanting one pin across the corpus must pick one and change both files, and this pass will not unilaterally move a pin another journey owns. Also flagged: the committee's executive institute sat in **Paris** from 1926, so one Geneva pin for 1922 to 1934 overstates the geography, and the stop says so.

**Not staged, recorded rather than deleted.** Smith College 1921 **[R]** (the dossier says "numerous honorary degrees" without naming Smith); a Carnegie Institution or Washington reception 1921 **[R]** (venue unconfirmed, pool coordinate 1.3 km wrong even for the representative venue); L'Arcouest in the 1920s **[A]**; the 1930 weights committee, to which the pool attaches no place.

## 5. Death and afterlife

**[A]** Died 4 July 1934 at the Sancellemoz sanatorium, Passy, of aplastic pernicious anemia; buried at Sceaux 6 July beside Pierre; transferred with him to the Panthéon 20 April 1995 under Mitterrand and Wałęsa, coffins lead-lined, first woman interred there on her own merit. Attribution of the anemia to cumulative radiation exposure is **later medical-historical consensus**, not a finding on the certificate, and the file states that in `date_confidence`, not in the prose. Both lenses had put the burial on the town-hall pin, which would have stacked it on the 1895 wedding. The Panthéon pin is inherited from `victor_hugo.journey.json`. **The 1995 exhumation finding [R]:** that the remains carried no dangerous radium rests on the dossier alone, and the campa states it conditionally.

**The notebooks [A, bnf.fr].** Two displayed after certified radiation measurement, the rest consultable under a strict monitoring protocol, protected by **plastic covers and not lead**; about 2 nanosieverts for a ten-minute viewing, some 5,000 times below a day of natural background; radium-226 half-life about 1,600 years. This overturns the popular "lead boxes, 1,500 years, sign a waiver" story and gets the closing stop. Site contradiction resolved: the afterlife lens said Site Richelieu, the BnF's own page says Manuscripts at François-Mitterrand.

**Curium [A as to element and naming analogy, contested as to place and date].** en.wikipedia "Curium": plutonium-239 bombarded in the Berkeley 60-inch cyclotron, July and August 1944, but **chemical identification at the Metallurgical Laboratory, University of Chicago**; named for both Curies on the model of gadolinium for Johan Gadolin. The Quiz Kids leak is **disputed and nowhere stated flat**: Wikipedia dates the ACS presentation to 11 November 1945 with a leak "five days before," while the standard account has the broadcast on 11 November and the announcement on 16 November. **Curium is not a Curie stop:** it belongs to Seaborg, Ghiorso, and James, and appears only as a `suggested_ref` on her own Panthéon stop.

**Later retellings.** Ève Curie's *Madame Curie* (1937) and the 1943 MGM film **[A]** omit the Langevin affair and fixed the austere image; cited as sources, not staged. *Radioactive* (2019) **[R]**: its Fuller séance scene has no support in the documented correspondence and one medical historian criticized the film as unsuitable for education; a `suggested_ref` on the BnF stop. French banknote and stamp iconography **[R]**: no Banque de France source refetched, unused. Dava Sobel, *The Elements of Marie Curie* (2024) **[R]**: unused, noted as the best modern corrective to the solitary-genius reading.

## 6. Quotations: a systematic trimming fault

Five of the fourteen pooled quotations were verbatim in their words but **cut at one or both ends in ways that changed the sentence**. All restored to the printed text.

| As pooled | As printed |
|---|---|
| "There is not even one part of radium in a million parts of good ore." | "**But we now know that** there is not even one part..." |
| "...a laboratory worthy of him." | "...worthy of him, **which he had never had, but where others would be able to work to develop his idea.**" |
| "It was in this miserable old shed..." | "**Yet** it was in this miserable old shed... **devoting our entire days to our work.**" |
| "...different from the solitary life." | "...**that I had known during the preceding years.**" |
| "I still believe that we have done right." | "**Yet,** I still believe that we have done right." |

Sources: Marie Curie, *Pierre Curie* with "Autobiographical Notes," trans. Charlotte and Vernon Kellogg (Macmillan, 1923), Wikisource; "The Discovery of Radium," Vassar College lecture, 1921, Wikisource. Both fetched directly by the quote lens. Two quotations are **[R]** and labelled where used: "Life is not easy for any of us..." and "I am among those who think that science has great beauty," via Wikiquote to Ève Curie (1937), unconfirmed against the book.

## 8. Interlocks used and refused

Named in a campa, because Curie had a real relation to the person:

- **Loïe Fuller.** Friendship and correspondence; the refused radium costume; the 1911 letter [R]; the arranged Rodin visit [R]. Three stops.
- **Albert Einstein.** Met at the first Solvay 1911; ICIC colleague; seated beside her in the 1927 Solvay photograph. Three stops.
- **Henri Bergson.** Inaugural chair of the ICIC on which she was vice-president. One stop, at the Bergson file's own pin.
- **Auguste Rodin.** The Fuller-arranged visit, [R] and flagged.
- **Enrico Fermi.** One clause on the 1934 Institut du Radium stop. Real and one-directional: Fermi's Rome report of 25 March 1934 opens by crediting Irène Curie and Frédéric Joliot's discovery, made in Marie Curie's own laboratory, three months before her death.

Refused as shared geography only, on the rule that the map already shows co-location: **Paul Dirac** (present at the 1927 Solvay, standing behind her, Pauli's jibe at the same table, but no relation beyond co-attendance; his file's pin is inherited for Parc Leopold and he is not named); **Victor Hugo, Jean-Jacques Rousseau, Louis Braille** (same crypt, no relation; the `victor_hugo` pin is inherited, no name in the campa); **Marie of Romania** (network adjacency via Fuller, no documented contact conceded, nothing written).

**Open geography now closed.** The interlock lens reported that no atlas journey anchored a Warsaw-era Polish-partition education stop and that Curie's Warsaw material stood alone. This file supplies five Warsaw-region stops, two on Wawelska Street, and the last visit of 1934.

**Canonical pins.** Paris 48.8566/2.3522 is used once, on the Fuller-letter stop, declared as a canonical pin and not an address. **Kaaba (21.4225/39.8262) and Buenos Aires (-34.6037/-58.3816) are not used**: the interlock lens checked both explicitly and found no connection of any kind. Per standing convention an absent Argentina link is an acceptable gap in an aesthetic-ancestry entry and not something to invent around; recorded so the check is on the record.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Marie Curie is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** No apparatus stop is licensed and none is written. Nothing in the pool required one: she has no theoretical framework in the sense Austin, Searle, Derrida, Burroughs, or Land do, only a discovery and a body of measurement, and every item in the pool is rooted in her own life, her own laboratory, or her own remains. **There is no backward relation to report.**

Two categories were inspected and are reported rather than dropped, so the operator can rule if he disagrees.

1. **Forward in time, but not framework-forward.** Posthumous material about her own body, papers, and name is carried as ordinary afterlife: the 1995 Panthéon transfer, the BnF notebooks, and, as a `suggested_ref` only, the 1944 naming of curium. Same shape as the 1966 repatriation inside `abdelkader.journey.json`. These are facts about the traveler's own remains and instruments, not a framework applied forward to events she never treated, and the block rule is not strained: the file's clock stops when the file is written and these artifacts exist by then. If the 2026-dated BnF stop reads as overreach, the fix is to cut that one stop; nothing depends on it.

2. **Relations belonging to other travelers, cited instead of staged.** Curium (Seaborg, Ghiorso, and James), *Madame Curie* and the 1943 film (Ève's and Hollywood's), *Radioactive* (Satrapi's and Redniss's), Fermi's Rome report (Fermi's). Each is a source or `suggested_ref` on Curie's own stop for the relevant place or object, and none is staged as a scene. Fermi alone is named in a campa, because the citation runs through her own daughter working in her own institute. **Direction: none is an apparatus relation; all are one-directional engagements by later people.**
