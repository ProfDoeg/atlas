# Leon Battista Alberti (1404-1472): research report

*Written 2026-08-29 for `leon_battista_alberti.journey.json`, from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses, corrections folded in).*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim the pool could not corroborate. Conflicting sources are both printed and neither is chosen. Coordinate fixes are listed near the end.

**The problem of the subject.** Alberti is the standing exhibit for the Renaissance universal man, and nearly every colourful detail of that portrait comes from one anonymous fifteenth-century *Vita* that scholars widely believe he wrote about himself. The attested Alberti is narrower: a papal clerk with a canon-law doctorate, no workshop training, no guild membership and no title of architect, who drafted other men's documents for thirty-two years and is nowhere documented standing on the site of either building he is most famous for.

---

## 1. Birth and exile, 1404-1416

- Born **Genoa, 14 February 1404**, illegitimate second son of the exiled Florentine merchant-banker **Lorenzo di Benedetto Alberti** and **Bianca Fieschi**. **[A: Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani* (DBI), s.v. "Leon Battista Alberti," Grayson-Argan]**
- **Contradiction, not resolved:** some English sources give **18 February**. Nothing reachable settles it.
- Household relocates to **Venice**, Lorenzo continuing the business under the standing ban. **[A: DBI]** Some accounts tie the move to plague at Genoa; the pool treats the move as better attested than the reason. **[R for the causation]**
- **Gaps:** no parish, street or house of birth is recorded; the Venetian residence is unknown. Both pins are cities, and the file says so.

## 2. Padua, and the father's death, 1416-1421

- Sent to the school of **Gasparino Barzizza**, with **Francesco Barbaro**, **Francesco Filelfo** and **Antonio Beccadelli (Panormita)** among his fellows. Literary and rhetorical formation throughout. **[A: DBI]** **Gap:** Barzizza kept a private boarding school, not a faculty, and its schoolroom is unlocated.
- **Lorenzo di Benedetto Alberti dies at Padua, 28 May 1421**; a prolonged inheritance dispute with relatives follows. **[A: DBI]**
- **The murder tradition. [R]** That relatives plotted or attempted to kill Alberti rests **solely** on the anonymous *Vita*, plus one document Girolamo Mancini read as circumstantial support. No named perpetrator, no judicial finding, no prosecution. Carried in the journey as tradition, never as event.

## 3. Bologna: canon law, illness, a forged comedy, 1421-1428

- **Canon law at the Studium** from about 1421; **doctorate 1428**. **[A: DBI; English Wikipedia]**
- **Recurrent illness, blamed by the anonymous *Vita* on overwork, memory strain and family hostility. [R]** A self-fashioning source, not a medical record; **no modern diagnosis is possible from the evidence**. Said inside the campa rather than smoothed away.
- ***Philodoxeos fabula***, c. 1424, circulated by Beccadelli as an ancient work by **"Lepidus"**; Alberti let the attribution stand about ten years. **[A: DBI]** A literary deception, no known victim, no legal action.
- **The rejected embassy. [R, and rejected]** Older biographies send him after 1428 into the service of **Cardinals Aleman and Albergati**, through France and Germany and home by **Basel**. The DBI rejects it: he appears in no documented suite at Arras or Basel. **No stop given**, and the gap that **where he was 1428-1431 is not known** is stated instead.

## 4. The Roman curia, 1431/32-1434

- In Rome by 1432, possibly 1431, as secretary to **Biagio Molin**, patriarch of Grado and regent of the chancery, who secures his appointment as **apostolic abbreviator**. **[A: DBI]**
- **Before October 1432** Eugenius IV issues the **dispensation removing the canonical disability of illegitimate birth**. **[A: DBI; English Wikipedia]** The exact date inside the window is undocumented; the journey's 1432-09-01 is a stated ordering default.
- ***Vita Sancti Potiti***, 1433, for Molin, first of an abandoned series; in a letter to **Leonardo Dati** he voices historical doubts about the legendary material. **[A: DBI]**
- ***Descriptio urbis Romae***, encoding the city plan in polar coordinates so the map regenerates from numbers rather than from copying. **[A: DBI; architectural-history consensus]** Dated only loosely to the early-to-mid 1430s. Pinned at the Forum as the ruins he worked among, not as a station point; the treatise's centre is generally taken as the Capitoline.
- **Flight of Eugenius IV: contradiction in the pool, corrected.** The chronology lens dated it 1434-06 but placed it at **Florence**. Eugenius fled Rome **4 June 1434**, reached Pisa 12 June, and reached **Florence only in October**. Split into two stops. **[A: English Wikipedia, "Pope Eugene IV"]**

## 5. Florence, 1434-1436

- Renews or forms relations with **Brunelleschi, Donatello, Ghiberti, Luca della Robbia**; encounters a world altered by **Masaccio**. **[A: DBI]**
- ***De pictura / Della pittura***, 1435, Latin and Tuscan; Latin dedicated to **Gianfrancesco Gonzaga**, Tuscan prologue praising the four. **[A: DBI; English Wikipedia]** **Open question, not adjudicated:** the priority of the Latin over the Tuscan is disputed (Bertolini).
- ***I libri della famiglia*: the pool contradicts itself, and the contradiction stands.** Its chronology lens places the first three books at **Rome, 1433-34**, its geography lens at **Florence, 1434-36**. Both derive from the same DBI entry. The journey stops at Florence and prints the conflict in `date_confidence`. The **ninety days in his thirtieth year** is the anonymous *Vita*'s claim **[R]**; Book IV c. 1440/41 **[A: DBI]**.

## 6. The itinerant court, 1436-1443

- **Bologna, April 1436**, with the pope. **[A: DBI]**
- **Ferrara, January 1438**: the Latin-Greek reunion council; renews his connection with **Leonello d'Este**. **[A: DBI]**
- **Florence, 1439**: plague forces the council's transfer. **[A: DBI]** Pinned at **Santa Maria Novella**, where the sessions are traditionally sited; this predates his own 1458-1470 facade commission there.
- **The Florentine literary period**, c. 1441: *De equo animante*, *Teogenio*, *Della tranquillità dell'animo*, *Villa*, the *Intercenales*. **[A: DBI]** Individual datings vary in the scholarship and are not fixed.
- **Certame Coronario, October 1441**, organized with **Piero di Cosimo de' Medici**, in Tuscan, on friendship; **the judges award no crown**. **[A: DBI]**
- **Ferrara, from c. 1443**: advisory work on the monument to **Niccolò III** (Arco del Cavallo) and the **cathedral campanile**. **[R]**; the pool itself calls the split between his conception and local execution unresolved. The campanile was built **1451-1493**, so it cannot belong to a c. 1443 dating, and the stop says so.
- **Siena, summer 1443.** **[A: DBI, which gives "a stop at Siena" and nothing more]**
- **Return to Rome: contradiction corrected.** The DBI gives "June 1443." Eugenius IV made his **formal entry 28 September 1443**; the court left Florence in spring and lingered at Siena through the summer. Redated, with the June date printed as the departure leg.

## 7. Rome under Nicholas V, 1443-1455

- Advisory role in the humanist rebuilding under **Nicholas V** (1447-1455). **[A: DBI]** The DBI itself warns that the boundary between Alberti's advice and **Bernardo Rossellino**'s execution is **often undocumentable**; the journey repeats the warning rather than assigning authorship. **Old St Peter's:** "He commissioned work on the old building from Leone Battista Alberti and Bernardo Rossellino," with no detail of Alberti's own contribution **[A: English Wikipedia]**. **Santo Stefano Rotondo:** "restored in the 1450s by Bernardo Rossellino, probably under the guidance of Leon Battista Alberti" **[A, with the source's own hedge]**.
- **Santa Prassede: [R upgraded to A].** The pool downgraded it after checking the wrong article; it **is** listed among Nicholas V's restorations. *Cut from the journey for thinness, no attested personal act, not for failed attestation. Restorable at 41.8961/12.4986.* **Santa Maria Maggiore: dropped**, being **not** among the documented restorations though the pool listed it.
- **Acqua Vergine: [R upgraded to A].** Nicholas V renovated the Aqua Virgo's channels in **1453**, and the terminus was "a simple basin that **Alberti designed**, the predecessor of the Trevi Fountain." **[A: English Wikipedia]** Attested, not merely "sometimes counted."
- **Lake Nemi salvage, mid-1440s. [R in practice]** The DBI tradition has him attempting to raise the sunken ships; it fails, and **no drawing of the apparatus survives**. The Lake Nemi article confirms the ships but **does not surface Alberti's role**.
- ***De re aedificatoria*** presented c. 1450-1452, **in manuscript through his lifetime**, first printed **Florence 1485**. **[A: DBI; English Wikipedia]**
- ***De Porcaria coniuratione***, 1453: sympathy for Porcari's republican ambition, not his method. **[A: DBI]** **No charge, investigation or punishment of Alberti is documented**, and the journey says so rather than implying jeopardy.
- **Benefices:** the priorate of **San Martino a Gangalandi** and the parish of **Borgo San Lorenzo** from 1448. **[A: DBI]** **No document places him in residence at either**, so Borgo San Lorenzo gets **no stop**.

## 8. The buildings, 1446-1472

- **Palazzo Rucellai. [R]** The attribution is Vasari's and is held by most scholars, but early sixteenth-century sources gave the design to **Bernardo Rossellino**; **Schlosser, Planiscig and Mack** argued for Rossellino, **Brenda Preyer (1981)** for Alberti. **Open question, printed as open.**
- **Tempio Malatestiano, Rimini**, for **Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta** from c. 1450; **Alberti works largely from Rome by letter**, including a detailed **1454** correction to **Matteo de' Pasti**. **[A: DBI; English Wikipedia]** Dome and upper facade never built; Sigismondo's 1460 excommunication and collapse curtailed it.
  - **Method note.** Alberti is **not documented on the Rimini site**. The Rimini stop is kept because the building is his own object, with the non-attendance stated in `date_confidence`; the **1454 letter gets its own stop at Rome**, where he wrote it. **Gap:** no verbatim text of that letter was reachable, so the journey paraphrases and quotes nothing.
- **Rucellai Sepulchre, San Pancrazio**, completed **1467** by the door inscription, modelled on the Holy Sepulchre. **[A]** **Santa Maria Novella facade**, begun no earlier than 1458, frieze dating completion to **1470**, with the S-shaped volutes. **[A]** **Santissima Annunziata tribuna**, commission **1469**, replacing Michelozzo, Gonzaga-funded, constrained by existing foundations, **concluded 1481** after his death. **[A]**
- **San Sebastiano, Mantua**, begun **1460**, Greek cross, crypt intended as a Gonzaga mausoleum, **Luca Fancelli** on site; abandoned partway in the mid-1470s once Alberti no longer directed it, so the present front is a reconstruction and the original facade design is not securely known. **[A]**
- **Sant'Andrea, Mantua**, designed 1470-71, **construction begins 1472, the year he dies**. **[A]** **Arithmetic corrected:** the pool says "finished 328 years later"; 1472 to **1790** is **318 years**. Juvarra's dome 1732-1782 is right.

## 9. The cipher, 1466-1467

- ***De componendis cifris***, at **Leonardo Dati**'s prompting: tables of **Latin letter frequencies** and the **Formula**, a fixed outer disk (*Stabilis*, capitals and four numerals) with a movable inner disk (*Mobilis*, mixed lowercase), so that turning the inner disk changes the substitution alphabet mid-message. **[A: DBI; English Wikipedia, "Alberti cipher," "Alberti cipher disk"]** The treatise's own opening frames the device as arising from a conversation with Dati about the **recently developed printing press**. **[A]**
- **The priority claim, stated precisely.** First known **European mechanical polyalphabetic cipher device**, not the first cipher or the first cryptanalysis in world history: **al-Kindi** and later Arabic cryptanalysts had frequency analysis centuries earlier. **[A]** The campa says this and adds the gap: **nothing in Alberti names al-Kindi, and no transmission is documented.**
- **"Father of Western cryptology": [R upgraded to A], wording enforced.** History of cryptography: "most clearly explained by Leon Battista Alberti around AD 1467, for which he was called the 'father of Western **cryptology**'." Not "cryptography." The journey uses neither phrase in its own voice. **Descent flagged, not asserted:** the later Vigenère cipher descends through **Trithemius** and especially **Bellaso**.
- **Print history:** never printed in the fifteenth century; first as "La cifra" in **Cosimo Bartoli's *Opuscoli morali*, Venice 1568**. **[A]**

## 10. Loss of office, last years, death

- **1464:** Paul II abolishes or reorganizes the **College of Abbreviators**; Alberti loses the office. **[A: DBI]** He is **not documented as arrested, tried or charged**, unlike other humanists later imprisoned and accused of conspiracy or heterodoxy, and stays in Rome on his benefices.
- **1468:** *De iciarchia*. **[A: DBI]** *Editorial: cut for stop-count, not for evidence.*
- **1471:** guides **Lorenzo de' Medici, Bernardo Rucellai and Donato Acciaiuoli** through the **Roman Forum**. **[A: DBI]** **Gap:** nothing of what was said is recorded.
- **Death: contradiction printed, not resolved.** The **will is dated 19 April 1472**; by **25 April** a successor was presented to his vacant benefice. The DBI treats **only that window** as securely documented. English Wikipedia gives **25 April**; other modern sources give **20 April**. All three positions are printed in the stop.
- **Burial: [R], and the pool's own overstatement corrected.** The afterlife lens asserted he "was first interred at Sant'Agostino, then his remains were later moved" to Padua. Italian Wikipedia says he **wished** it by testament: "volle essere sepolto in Sant'Agostino e poi trasferito nella tomba del padre a Padova, **ma non sono rimaste tracce della sepoltura**." A documented instruction of unverified execution, not a documented interment.
- **Cause of death is unknown** in everything reachable.

## 11. Afterlife of the works

Posthumous, but concerning his own body and works, so used only inside his own stops, never as scenes.

- ***De re aedificatoria*** printed **Florence 1485**, the **first printed book on architecture**, a year before the first printed Vitruvius. **[A]**
- ***De statua*: the pool's claim was inverted.** It said the Latin waited until the late 1800s and the vernacular came "much later still." In fact **Bartoli's Italian appeared in 1568** and the **Latin** only at the end of the nineteenth century. **[A: it.wikipedia]** *(Not used.)*
- In `suggested_refs` only **[A]**: the **Tempio Malatestiano** bombed in WWII and rebuilt from rubble by the Monuments, Fine Arts and Archives unit, with **Matteo de' Pasti's 1450 foundation medal** the only record of the unbuilt dome; **Santa Maria Novella**'s right-hand scroll clad in marble only in **1920**; **San Pancrazio** deconsecrated, now the **Museo Marino Marini**, its Rucellai chapel enterable since **February 2013**.
- **The bronze self-portrait plaquette**, winged eye and **"Quid tum?"**, his only securely attributed sculpture, sometimes given instead to **Pisanello** or **Matteo de' Pasti**. **[R]** The National Gallery of Art page returned **403/429**, so the location is unconfirmed; the object has no stop.
- **Not used:** a statue in the Uffizi colonnade **[A: it.wikipedia]**, sculptor and date unreachable, a nineteenth-century statue of him being not his stop; and **Burckhardt's 1860 canonization of Alberti as *uomo universale* [R]**, unconfirmed outside the pool.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Alberti is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Relations not rooted in his own life are kept and flagged here rather than deleted. Direction is named in every case.

**Caught FORWARD (not licensable; correctly excluded).** All four are real one-directional engagements *by later people with Alberti's work*, and under the block rule they belong on **those people's own files**. A 1472 file cannot embed a 1567 hash.

1. **`blaise_de_vigenere`.** At the papal court c. 1567 Vigenère read "the cipher disk Leon Battista Alberti described a hundred years before," alongside Bellaso (1553) and Della Porta (1563); the 1586 *Traicté des chiffres* synthesizes them with Trithemius. **The Vigenère file already names Alberti directly**, so the relation is carried where it belongs. **[A]**
2. **`johannes_trithemius`.** *Polygraphia*, Sponheim 1508, printed 1518: the next European step after the disk. His file does not name Alberti; the Vigenère file states the succession. **[R]**
3. **`charles_babbage`.** Breaks Vigenère-family ciphers in the nineteenth century, but the genealogy runs through Trithemius and Bellaso, not Alberti's disk. Weak and forward.
4. **`columbus`.** He obtains Toscanelli's chart at Lisbon in 1481, nine years posthumous, so the link cannot be written here. Alberti's real relation is to **Paolo dal Pozzo Toscanelli** himself: close friend from the Bologna years, dedicatee of the *Intercenales*, lifelong interlocutor. **Operator note: that friendship is attested in the DBI and has no stop only because the pool gave it no place or date. It is the strongest unused biographical thread in the file.**

**Caught BACKWARD (licensable in principle; one used, with the gap stated).**

5. **`al_kindi`.** The *Risala fi Istikhraj al-Mu'amma*, Baghdad, ninth century: frequency analysis some six centuries before Alberti's tables. The al-Kindi file **already names Alberti** in a `suggested_ref`. This is the **only interlock named in a campa** here, at "Rome, the two disks on a pin," used because the priority claim is a statement about **Alberti's own object** and is standard in the historiography of it. The campa says outright that **nothing in Alberti names al-Kindi**, and `date_confidence` adds that no transmission is documented. If that is judged too strong for a relation Alberti never articulated, the clause is one sentence and lifts cleanly.
6. **`ibn_al_durayhim`** and **`ibn_adlan`.** The same Arabic lineage; neither file mentions Alberti and no direct link exists. **[R]** Kept out, recorded here.

**Rejected as shared geography only (co-location is not a relation).**

7. **`cosimo_de_medici`.** Cosimo received the Council of Florence at Santa Maria Novella in 1439 and saw the Duomo consecrated in 1436; Alberti was in both places at both times. The Cosimo file does not mention him and the pool supplies no personal relation. **Not named.** Alberti's actual Medici relations are with **Piero di Cosimo** (the Certame) and **Lorenzo** (the Forum): Piero appears as a person without an interlock, Lorenzo with one.
8. **`nicholas_of_cusa`.** Cardinal from December 1448, same curia, same pope, no attested contact. **Not named.**
9. **`st_anthony_of_padua_1195_1231`.** The Basilica del Santo is where Alberti's testament asked his body finally be laid, beside his father. That is a fact about **Alberti's will**, and it sits in his file at Rome, where the will was written. Saint Anthony has no relation to Alberti, so he is **not named**, and no Padua-burial scene is staged: the transfer is not documented to have happened at all.

## Corrections applied to the pool

Twelve pins were moved, each against the named landmark article and each recorded in the relevant stop's `date_confidence`: Genoa, Padua, Bologna (the pool's value was Piazza Maggiore, not Piazza Galvani), Castello Estense and Ferrara Cathedral (whose two pins had been effectively swapped), Siena, Palazzo Ducale Mantua, Tempio Malatestiano, Sant'Andrea, San Pancrazio, Santa Maria Novella and Sant'Agostino. In the last four the pool's geography lens was right and its afterlife lens wrong. Three pins are also anachronistic as buildings and say so in the file: Palazzo Bo became the university's seat only in 1493, the Archiginnasio was built in 1562, and Genoa Cathedral is a landmark, not a birth site. Substantive corrections are stated in the sections above.
