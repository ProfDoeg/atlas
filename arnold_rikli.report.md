# Arnold Rikli (1823-1906): the atmospheric cure at Veldes

*Research report, 2026-08-02. Slug `arnold_rikli`. Companion: `arnold_rikli.journey.json`, 9 segments, 39 stops, 11 quotes.*

Swiss dyer turned lay healer; founder of the Naturheilanstalt Mallnerbrunn at Veldes (now Bled, Slovenia); originator of the "atmospheric cure" of air, light and sun baths; the transmitting figure between Central European hydropathy and the Lebensreform sun cult of Monte Verita.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition or inference, including anything the atlas interlock lens derived from theme rather than text. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Gaps are stated as gaps.

---

## 1. Wangen an der Aare: the dye works, 1823-1845

Born 13 February 1823 at Wangen an der Aare, Canton Bern, fourth of ten children of a dye-works owner **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. Works in the family dyeing business and completes an apprenticeship as a dyer **[A: de.wikipedia]**. Marries at twenty-one, 1844 **[A: en.wikipedia]**; the wife is not named anywhere reachable. **Gap.**

de.wikipedia places him in the parental works "im zwanzigsten Lebensjahr", about 1842-43, with departure for Carinthia in 1845, so the pool's "c.1840-1843" is early; the journey uses 1842 as a stated placeholder **[R: arithmetic from de.wikipedia's own dates]**.

**Coordinate correction.** The pool gave 47.1672, 7.6486, wrong by about 7.4 km and sitting in the hills south of the Aare. The municipality is at 47 deg 14 min N, 7 deg 39 min E = **47.2333, 7.6500** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. The journey uses the corrected pair.

**The Carinthian venture.** With his brothers Karl and Rudolf he founds a dyeing works at Seebach on the Millstatter See in 1845 **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**.

> **Double-counting corrected.** The pool listed two ventures, a yarn works at Seebach/Seeboden and a leather factory near Spittal, and flagged them as contradictory. They are one venture in one place: en.wikipedia's wikitext writes it `[[Seeboden am Millstätter See|Seebach]]`, and Seebach is an Ortschaft of the municipality Seeboden am Millstatter See, some 5 km from Spittal an der Drau **[A: de.wikipedia, Seeboden]**. Merged into one stop.

> **Surviving contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** The **commodity** genuinely diverges: German says **yarn** dyeing, English and Slovene say **leather** dyeing. Nothing reachable adjudicates. The journey states both.

Coordinates for the works corrected from the pool's two wrong pairs (46.7975, 13.47 and 46.8032, 13.5959) to **46.8189, 13.5183** **[A: de.wikipedia]**.

## 2. Illness and the first lake, c. 1850-1854

Suffers serious health problems, attributed by him to chemical exposure in the dyeing trade **[A: en.wikipedia]**. No diagnosis, no symptoms, no year in any reachable source. **Gap**; the journey dates the stop 1850 as an explicit placeholder before the 1852 journey.

First travels to Veldes in 1852 and finds the stay restorative **[A: en.wikipedia, citing Berner Zeitung, 29 May 2023; sl.wikipedia]**.

> **Attribution corrected.** The pool credited the 1852 visit to de.wikipedia's Rikli article, which does not carry the date. It is English and Slovene Wikipedia only.

Relocates his family permanently to Veldes in 1854 **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**.

## 3. Mallnerbrunn: institute and doctrine, 1855 to c. 1870

Establishes at Veldes a natural healing institute for atmospheric treatment, patients coming chiefly in summer **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia; sl.wikipedia]**. Publishes the sun-bathing method in 1855 **[A: de.wikipedia]**. The cure combines water, air and light therapies with outdoor activity, intensive sun baths and a vegetarian diet **[A: de.wikipedia]**.

> **Proper name recovered.** The pool never gave the institute's name: it is the **Naturheilanstalt Mallnerbrunn**, after the Mallner Brunn spring at the lake's western end **[A: de.wikipedia "Bled"; de.wikipedia "Ida Hofmann"]**. This fixes the site too. The institute stood on the north-western shore near **46.3697, 14.1041**, at Veslaska promenada where Riklijeva vila still stands **[A: OpenStreetMap/Nominatim]**, not at the town centroid 46.3683, 14.1146 that the pool used for nearly every Bled stop.

> **Founding year contradicted, not resolved.** en and sl give **1855**; de.wikipedia "Bled" gives **1858**; de.wikipedia "Arnold Rikli" implies **1854**. The journey states 1855, which Rikli's own 1905 text independently supports, and carries 1858 in `date_confidence`.

**The maxim.** "Water is good, air is better, but light is best" **[A: en.wikipedia, cited to BBC Travel, "The birthplace of modern nature healing", 14 Aug 2019]**. Slovene verbatim: "Voda je dobra, se boljsi je zrak, a najboljsa je svetloba" **[A: sl.wikipedia]**.

> **German wording rejected.** The pool's German original, "Wasser ist gut, Luft ist besser, aber Licht ist am besten", does not appear in the cited de.wikipedia article. It is a back-translation **[R]** and is absent from the journey, which quotes only the French primary text.

> **Competing Slovene variant.** "Voda je dobra, zrak boljsi, sonce pa najboljse" puts **sun** where the other puts **light**. It is the title of a 2004 *Finance* advertising supplement listed in en.wikipedia's own bibliography **[A]**, and is apparently the form in Slovene commercial use. Recorded in the journey's last segment.

**Nickname.** Local usage: *Sonnendoktor*, the Sun Doctor **[R: de.wikipedia describing a popular nickname, not a document]**. "Heliopath" appears in the Monte Verita articles **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

### The primary text

A French translation of the eighth German edition was reached and quoted: *Medecine naturelle et bains de soleil*, from *Die Grundlehren der Naturheilkunde* (Lausanne: Georges Bridel & Cie, 1905), translator uncredited. All of the following are **[A: Rikli 1905]** and appear verbatim in the journey's `quote` fields: the Avant-propos on the book's purpose; the four axioms of § 1 (life as molecular movement, health as normal movement, disease as abnormal); the one-line definition of the cure in § 38 ("A se baigner dans l'atmosphere au lieu de le faire dans l'eau!"); the botanical anatomy of § 38 (man as "la plante terrestre la plus developpee", isolated from the atmosphere by housing, clothing, shade "et pour les parasols!", with "L'eau est a ses pieds!"); the ranking by molecular speed in § 45, light fastest, then air, then water; and Hufeland at § 40.

**Hufeland.** § 40 quotes Christoph Wilhelm Hufeland, *Macrobiotik*, 6th ed., p. 87, on light at the summit of the four heavenly gifts. **Not Rikli's own words**, and tagged as such in the journey's `quote_source`. The Hufeland edition itself was not consulted; it is reached only through Rikli.

**§ 39 quotation corrected.** The pool rendered it "La lumiere solaire ... sera donc toujours, en sa qualite de mere, sa nourriture la meilleure." The printed text (pp. 40-41) has "...le systeme nerveux est le produit organique le plus perfectionne de la lumiere solaire. (Voir § 4.) Celle-ci sera donc toujours, en sa qualite de mere, sa nourriture la meilleure et la plus fortifiante...". The pool silently swapped a noun phrase for the pronoun "Celle-ci" and dropped "et la plus fortifiante" without an ellipsis. The journey restores the full clause and marks the bracketed substitution in `quote_source`.

### Three section locators corrected

The pool cited three first-person passages as "§ 35" and "§ 36". All three are wrong the same way: **printed page numbers mistaken for section numbers**. §§ 35 and 36 sit in Chapitre II; the passages are on printed pp. 35-36, inside the unnumbered "Souvenirs historiques" opening Chapitre III **[A: Rikli 1905, ch. III]**.

1. **The dry wrap, p. 35.** "...lors de l'inauguration de mon etablissement de medecine naturelle a Veldes, en 1855, j'introduisis le maillot sec a la fin de ce bain..." This is Rikli's own first-person corroboration of **1855**, the strongest evidence against de.wikipedia's 1858.
2. **The four-year self-experiment, p. 35.** "Durant quatre annees, de 1865 a 1868, je pratiquai les bains de lumiere comme experience de rafraichissement, hiver et ete, chaque matin, sur moi-meme." The pool truncated at "de rafraichissement" without an ellipsis, cutting the clause that establishes the self-experiment. The journey quotes the whole sentence. Corroborated p. 36: "Au bout de la quatrieme annee, confiant en mes experiences, resolu a en tenter l'essai sur mes hotes, j'introduisis le bain de lumiere dans le plan de ma cure."
3. **Guest resistance, p. 36.** "Que de paroles pour reussir a entrainer quelques-uns de mes hotes, a moitie persuades seulement..." The pool cut at "mes hotes" and made the comma a full stop with no ellipsis; the journey quotes it with a trailing ellipsis.

## 5. The resort at its height, 1872-1903

**Publications.** Five original editions survive at one institution, dated 1872, 1889, 1890 and 1894, plus an undated offprint **[A: en.wikipedia; sl.wikipedia]**. The pool's "1872-1894" therefore describes four of the five.

**1895.** "In 1895, he constructed a villa and baths in the Swiss architectural style, along with a hospital featuring his own examination office." **[A: en.wikipedia, verbatim]**

> **Invented detail struck.** The pool described "a wooden bathing facility... opposite what will become the Hotel Park, built in Swiss chalet style." Neither the wooden construction nor the Hotel Park siting is in any source **[R, rejected]**. The geography was wrong too: Park Hotel is at 46.3676, 14.1093 and the complex stood at Mallner Brunn near 46.3697, 14.1041, while the pool's 46.3627, 14.1096 falls on the far southern side of the lake.

**The Kursalon.** The villa served as Kursalon and is a listed cultural monument **[A: sl.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. The pool's note that the address is unknown is wrong: Riklijeva vila is mapped at **Veslaska promenada 12, 46.3697, 14.1041** **[A: OpenStreetMap]**. Its construction **year** genuinely is unrecorded. **Gap.**

**Straza path.** A walking route to Straza Hill is among those laid out for the cure **[A: en.wikipedia, "one that led to Straza Hill"; sl.wikipedia]**, undated. **Gap.** Coordinates corrected from the pool's 46.3567, 14.1256, about 1.7 km east and off the hill, to the summit at **46.3600, 14.1047** **[A: OpenStreetMap]**.

**1899.** "A larger swimming area with promenade was added in 1899." **[A: en.wikipedia]** The pool's gloss that it "replaced the modest 1895 structure" is the pool's inference **[R, dropped]**; the source says added.

**1903.** Bled takes a gold medal at an international exposition for healing destinations at Vienna **[A: en.wikipedia; sl.wikipedia]**. The exposition is not named and the venue not identified anywhere reachable, so the journey pins the city centre and says so. **Gap.**

## 6. The guests of 1899 and the Monte Verita transmission

Ida Hofmann and Henri Oedenkoven meet at Bled in 1899; Karl Graser is also there taking the cure under Rikli **[A: en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia, "Monte Verita"]**. Year, persons and the Sonnendoktor/heliopath epithets check out in both language versions.

> **Overstatement corrected.** The pool wrote "a joint stay undergoing cure therapy". de.wikipedia's "Ida Hofmann" says she came to Bled in 1899 to visit her gravely ill father and took lodgings at the institute: "Sie selbst mietete sich in der nahe gelegenen Naturheilanstalt Mallnerbrunn ein" **[A]**. She was a lodger; Oedenkoven (25) and Graser were patients. The journey states both readings and leaves the divergence visible.

> **Founding tightened.** The group met at **Munich in October 1900** and bought land on **Monte Monescia** above Ascona that autumn; the name **Monte Verita was adopted only in 1902**; **Gustav** Graser belongs in the founder list beside Karl **[A: de.wikipedia "Monte Verita"]**.

**The line of descent itself is [R].** That Rikli's institute transmitted Lebensreform health ideals into the Monte Verita founding circle is an inference from the attested Bled meeting; no reachable source says it in those words, and none places Rikli at Ascona ever. This is the dossier's most consequential gap: the subject's entire posthumous significance runs through a link the sources imply and never state.

## 8. Death and afterlife

**Death.** 30 April 1906 at Sankt Thomas, Carinthia, aged 83 **[A: de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. de.wikipedia wikilinks the death place to Wolfsberg and Wikidata Q124423 gives place of death Wolfsberg **[A]**. Coordinates corrected from the pool's 46.8194, 14.8567, about 2.4 km out in the hills southeast, to **46.8269, 14.8275**, St. Thomas in the Katastralgemeinde St. Marein **[A: OpenStreetMap]**. Why he was there rather than at Bled: no source addresses it. **Gap.**

**Burial.** No burial site in the German, English or Slovenian biographies **[R: absence across all three]**. FindAGrave returned 403. The pool framed this as a source-access gap; it is worse. **Wikidata carries no place-of-burial statement at all** **[A: Q124423]**, so it is an open question, not a retrieval failure. **Gap**, stated as such in the journey campa.

**Monument.** A monument stands in the Bled spa park, raised by supporters to mark fifty years of his practice, registered as **ESD 12999** **[A: sl.wikipedia; en.wikipedia; Wikimedia Commons, "Klavdija Peljhan ESD 12999 Riklijev spomenik Bled"]**. Two corrections: the park is **Zdraviliski park**, not "Zdravilski park" **[A: OpenStreetMap, "Zdraviliski park, Pot okoli jezera, Bled"]**, and the coordinates are **46.3696, 14.1063**, not the town centroid 650 m off. The **year** of erection is stated by no source; fifty years from 1855 gives 1904-05, used in the journey as a declared placeholder. **Gap.**

**Loss of the property.** After the collapse of Austrian authority in 1918 the family lost all its property at Bled and the complex declined **[A: en.wikipedia, citing Berner Zeitung]**. Two framings tightened: en dates the decline of the complex from Rikli's **death in 1906**, with 1918 an acceleration rather than the sole cause; and en's "the establishment of Slovenia" is loose, since the state formed in 1918 was the **Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes**.

**The villa today.** Bought 23 December 2024 by the Slovenian entrepreneur Tim Mitja Zagar, who announced restoration as a symbol of Slovenian health tourism **[A: en.wikipedia; sl.wikipedia, cited to *Delo*, 6 Jan 2025]**. The surname appears as both Zagar and Zagar-with-hacek across the two articles; flagged, not resolved.

**Rikli Sports Days.** *Riklijevi sportni dnevi* every July at Bled, retracing his therapy trails **[A: sl.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. No founding year anywhere. **Gap.**

**Library holding.** Five originals at the **National and University Library of Slovenia**, Ljubljana, described as the only library keeping them **[A: en.wikipedia; sl.wikipedia]**. Coordinates corrected from 46.0511, 14.5051 to **46.0475, 14.5037** (Turjaska ulica 1) **[A: OpenStreetMap]**. Acquisition date unknown. **Gap.**

**The prizes.** The pool was wrong here twice.

> **Correction.** The pool said the Arnold-Rikli-Preis has been awarded annually since 1989, and that "two prizes carry Rikli's name in Germany". Per Jung and Bocionek, "25 Jahre Arnold-Rikli-Preis", *Aktuelle Dermatologie* 40 (2014), 47-49, cited by en.wikipedia: the 1989 prize was awarded by the **Light Symposium Foundation in Atlanta, USA**, and only "from 1989 until its dissolution in **2005**" **[A]**. Neither German nor current. Only the **Jorg-Wolff-Stiftung** prize, from **2016**, 10,000 euros, under the patronage of the European Society for Photobiology, is a German prize still awarded **[A: en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia]**. de.wikipedia's looser "seit 1989 ... jahrlich" is contradicted by the cited article and not followed. The journey pins the 1989 prize at Atlanta and gives the 2016 successor no pin, because no reachable source names its awarding city. **Gap.**
