# Giulio Camillo — research report

**Traveler:** Giulio Camillo Delminio (c. 1480–1544). **Span rendered:** birth in Friuli to the posthumous printing of *L'Idea del Theatro* (Florence/Venice, 1550). 7 segments, 36 stops, 11 quoted.

## Sources
Primary spine: Frances Yates, *The Art of Memory* (1966), ch. VI — full text pulled from the Internet Archive OCR (`archive.org/stream/ArtOfMemory1/`), which preserves Yates's translations of Viglius Zuichemus's two 1532 letters to Erasmus (Erasmus, *Epistolae*, ed. Allen, IX p.479 and X pp.29-30) and long verbatim passages of *L'Idea del Theatro* itself (the seven grades: pillars/planets, Banquet, Cave, Gorgon Sisters, Pasiphae, Sandals of Mercury, Prometheus). Secondary spine: Judita Uremović, *Backstage at Camillo's Esoteric Theatre* (CEU MA thesis, 2021, read via `pdf_text.py`), which supplies the tightest sourced curriculum vitae (Padua, San Vito, Pordenone 1508, Bologna chair ~1521-25, the 1530 departure with Rangone and Muzio, the d'Avalos negotiation, the seven-mornings dictation, death 15 May 1544, burial at Santa Maria delle Grazie, publication history including the 1550 Jubilee reprint). Cross-checked against Wikipedia (EN/IT) and Lu Beery Wenneker's 1970 dissertation (cited at second hand throughout Uremović and Yates).

## Judgment calls
- **Birthplace and exact birth year are genuinely disputed** (Portogruaro, Udine, or Zoppola castle; "c. 1480" is the scholarly consensus). I picked Zoppola as the single traditional site per the eighteenth-century memoirists, flagged `traditional`.
- **The seven "grade" stops (12-19 in the file) are not dated events** — they are the intellectual construction of the Theatre, which Camillo worked on continuously from roughly 1519 to his death. I placed them as a sequence of invented dates (1526-1529) inside the Venice years, purely to give the lint tool a chronological spine; every one is marked `traditional` and the campa text is honest that this is architecture-of-an-idea, not a dated visit. This is the dataset's one deliberate time-fold, flagged per the register rules — it lets the Theatre's actual content (its most interesting feature) get seven full stops instead of being summarized in one.
- **The 1543 d'Avalos courtship has two datings in the sources** (Wenneker suggests the Marchese may have first sounded Camillo out as early as 1539; Muzio's own account puts the decisive approach in 1543). I followed Muzio/Yates's 1543 dating as primary and noted the 1539 possibility inside the campa text rather than as a separate stop.
- **Quotes**: every quote is Camillo's own words — either dictated text from *L'Idea del Theatro* itself (via Yates's translation of the 1550 edition), his *Discorso in materia del suo theatro* (1532) and *Trattato dell'Imitatione*, or his own description of the Theatre as directly reported by Viglius ("a windowed one... a built or constructed mind and soul"). I did not quote Viglius's or Erasmus's *own* commentary as if it were Camillo's — those appear only in campa prose, attributed.
- **The lion story** (Betussi, 1544) is legend even by contemporary report — Betussi's own telling notes Camillo stood still from bulk, not courage, and other witnesses supplied the "solar virtue" gloss after the fact. Marked `traditional`, framed in the text as a story Camillo himself was glad to repeat rather than as flatly attested fact.

## Gaps
No surviving image of the Theatre itself and no physical trace after the mid-16th century (Montfaucon searched and found nothing in the following century) — so every "suggested_ref" for the France and Milan segments points to a document (a letter, a dedication, a printed page) rather than a relic, which is honest to how little survived. Exact itinerary and dates of the two French sojourns (1530 and again c.1534-37) are not fixed in the scholarship; I used the best-attested fragments (the 1530 audience, Bording's 1534 letter, the 1537 falling-off) and left the gaps as gaps rather than inventing waypoints.

## The five richest episodes
1. **Viglius inside the Theatre, Venice, 8 June 1532** — the only eyewitness account of the object itself, standing, entered, touched: wood, little boxes, "a windowed one."
2. **The seven grades themselves** (stops 13-19) — Camillo's own cosmology in his own dictated words, the actual argument of his life compressed into names (Banquet, Cave, Gorgon Sisters, Pasiphae, Sandals of Mercury, Prometheus).
3. **Erasmus's reply from Freiburg, 5 July 1532** — an old Venetian friend turning, in one letter, into the first and sharpest critic, comparing Camillo's stir to Luther's.
4. **The seven mornings on adjoining beds, Milan, February 1544** — the entire forty-year project rescued from oblivion by dictation to Muzio in what scholars suspect was a deliberate echo of Solomon's own "bed of wisdom."
5. **The 1550 Jubilee reprint** — seven-times-seven years made canonical law, arriving the same year as a book built on seven-times-seven gates; the coincidence the Venetian printer Costantini clearly meant readers to catch.

## Connections in the atlas
Direct edges named in the brief: **giordano_bruno** and **kircher** already have journeys in this corpus (`giordano_bruno.journey.json`, `kircher.journey.json`) — both are named in the closing stop as figures who inherited pieces of Camillo's room (Bruno borrowed the Sun-grade imagery for his own art of shadows; Kircher's encyclopedic theatre-engravings and the memory palaces the Jesuits carried to Matteo Ricci's China both descend, at a distance, from the same wooden idea). Ramon Llull, Matteo Ricci, and Francis Bacon are named as thematic kin in the text and report but have no journey file yet in `working/journeys/` — natural next additions if the memory-arts cluster keeps growing. More broadly, Camillo's Venice (Aldus Manutius's press, Bembo, Aretino, Titian, Serlio) sits in the same decades and city as several other Venetian-adjacent figures already in the atlas, and his death at Milan under Alfonso d'Avalos (patron also of Ariosto, painted repeatedly by Titian) gives a second point of contact into the same web of Italian courts.
