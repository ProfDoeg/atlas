# Henryk Erlich (Hersh-Wolf Erlikh), 1882-1942: research report

*2026-08-29, for `henryk_erlich.journey.json`. Slug: `henryk_erlich`.*

**[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction or Bundist tradition, not
confirmed against a document this pass. Contradictions are flagged and not adjudicated, unless
one reading is demonstrably impossible. Where a claim was corrected or cut, the reason is given.
Four reference works carry most of this life and they disagree: English Wikipedia, Polish
Wikipedia, the Congress for Jewish Culture translation of the *Leksikon fun der nayer yidisher
literatur* entry, and Open Library.

## 1. Lublin and the double education (1882-1902)

Born in Lublin, Congress Poland, into a prosperous Jewish family **[A, Wikipedia]**. Father
**Moyshe Erlich**, a mill owner, in the *Leksikon* "in his younger years a fervent Gerer Hassid"
who "over time freed himself from Hassidism, became a Hovev-tsiyon, a member of *Bene Moshe*,
and a follower of Ahad-Haam" **[A]**. The dossier's gloss of this as a passage "through the
Haskalah" is in no source; the sourced detail is **Bene Moshe**, Ahad Ha'am's inner order.

**Contradiction, unresolved: the birth month.** *Leksikon*, day-precise **13 April 1882**
**[A]**; Polish Wikipedia, "ur. w maju 1882 w Lublinie", **May 1882**, month only **[A]**. The
**1881** variant circulating in some catalogue summaries was **not encountered in any source
checked** and is dropped.

Heder at six alongside a secular tutor **[R]**: no institution named, coordinates a city-centre
stand-in. Gymnasium finished with a gold medal, then the law faculty of the Imperial University
of Warsaw **[A, Leksikon]**; the full university sequence (Warsaw, Berlin, Saint Petersburg,
graduating around 1908) is **[R]**, no academic record opened.

## 2. Prison, expulsion, wandering studies (1902-1908)

**The theatre demonstration, 1902**: during a performance of the antisemitic play **Zloty
Cielak** at the Warsaw municipal theatre, arrest, three weeks in jail **[A, Leksikon]**. The
dossier tagged this **[R]**, putting the tag on the wrong half: the *event* is attested, the
*building* is inference. No source names Teatr Wielki, and "municipal theatre" in 1902 Warsaw
could equally mean Rozmaitosci or Teatr Letni. Joining the Bund, 1903 **[R]**, no primary party
record checked.

**The funeral arrest.** Polish Wikipedia: arrested in Warsaw **4 May 1904** at the funeral of
the socialist **Jozef Birencwajg**, trying to lay a bouquet of red flowers on the coffin
**[A]**; the *Leksikon* confirms three months in jail and expulsion from the university and
gives the name as **Birntsvayg** **[A]**. The dossier's "winter 1904" is wrong. **Pawiak** is
inference only; the coordinates are a placeholder.

**Berlin** (political economy, 1904-1905), **Nasze Haslo** (the Bund's illegal Polish-language
organ, co-edited with **Bronislaw Grosser**, 1905-1906) and the **Saint Petersburg** law degree
of about 1908 are all **[R]**, resting on tradition alone; no editorial address survives.

## 3. The fugitive years (1909-1913)

Three months' imprisonment for Social Democratic party membership, administrative expulsion from
the Kingdom of Poland, a period in **Paris** **[R]**; no address anywhere in the accounts. The
*Leksikon* confirms that in 1911 he "hid in Kiev Province, departed for Munich" **[A]**, but the
dossier's trailing clause, that the case was *discontinued* and that this is why he moved to
Saint Petersburg, is supported by nothing reached and is cut.

**Marriage, September 1911**, to **Zofia Dubnow**, daughter of the historian **Simon Dubnov**
**[A, Polish Wikipedia; Leksikon gives 1911 independently]**. Sons **Aleksander** (1912-1985)
and **Wiktor** (1914-2007), both born in Petersburg **[A]**. The widely circulated day-precise
**22 November 1914** for Wiktor is carried by **neither** Polish Wikipedia **nor** the
*Leksikon*, and English Wikipedia's article on Victor Erlich returns 404; the day is dropped.
**Gap.** **Den'** contributions from 1912 **[R]**; **Moscow**, arrest at the All-Russian
Conference of commercial employees, three months in prison, summer 1913 **[R]**.

## 4. Petrograd, 1917

On the executive committee of the Petrograd Soviet after the February Revolution **[A,
Wikipedia]**, from **March 1917** **[A, Leksikon]**. *Coordinates corrected:* the Tauride Palace
from the dossier's 59.9505, 30.3809, some 450 m off in the Tauride Garden, to **59.9477,
30.3757**; the **Mariinsky Palace** from 59.928, 30.3062, about 400 m off across St Isaac's
Square, to **59.9312, 30.3085**.

**The Bund's tenth conference**, Petrograd, **14-17 April 1917**, the first held openly inside
Russia; Erlich among the Central Committee delegates and named to the committee formed after it
alongside **Goldman, Vladimir Medem and Jeremiah Weinstein** **[A, Wikipedia `General Jewish
Labour Bund`]**. *Flag:* the same source dates the new committee to **May** 1917 and states that
**Medem remained in Poland and could not attend**. Colleagues on one committee, which is why
Medem is named in that campa; not in the room together, and the campa says so.

**The Stockholm mission**: a Soviet delegation to **England, France and Italy**, part of the
failed effort to convene a socialist peace conference at Stockholm **[A, Wikipedia]**. **Sweden
and Norway**, and Stockholm as intended venue, are **[R]**; the interlock lens flagged the full
itinerary as dossier-derived. Return in early August **[R]**.

**The Pre-Parliament.** *Leksikon*: **15 August 1917** for the selection, **October 1917** for
the withdrawal with the Bundist faction and the Mensheviks **[A]**, better attested than the
dossier's **[R]**; the venue comes from the general history of the body. **Rabochaia gazeta**
editorial board from November 1917 **[R]**; delegate for the Bund's **Northern bureau** to the
Menshevik conference of **May 1918** **[A, Wikipedia]**, folded into that stop since no venue is
named anywhere reached.

**Calendar.** Russia was Julian until February 1918; the file is `gregorian` and prints 1917
dates as the sources give them, so Old Style against New Style is open on every 1917 stop.

## 5. Warsaw of the Bund (1918-1938)

Return from Russia via Lublin (August 1918) to Warsaw (October 1918) **[R]**, itemised in no
encyclopedia entry checked. **City council, March 1919:** *Leksikon*, "Named to head Bundist
candidate list for Warsaw city council" **[A]**; the repeated result, **four Bundists seated**,
is in **no source reached** and is dropped. **The 1920 arrest:** *Leksikon*, "1920 Russo-Polish
War: Arrested, 3 months jail for anti-war speech at city council" **[A]**; the dossier's second
half, a **separate Bund-membership prosecution dragging three years before dismissal**, is in no
source reached and is cut.

**Krakow 1920**, unification conference, entry to the Central Committee, emergence alongside
**Wiktor Alter**, **[R]** as to the conference itself. **The press:** co-editor of the Yiddish
daily **Folkstsaytung** from 1921, later the **Naye Folkstsaytung**, eventually editor-in-chief
**[A, Wikipedia]**; the **Glos Bundu** material (confiscations, two convictions as responsible
editor, both extinguished by amnesty) is **[R]**. No address located for either paper.

**Kehilla 1924**: one of **five Bundists among fifty** **[A, Wikipedia]**. *A dossier
self-contradiction, corrected:* the geography lens attached to this stop a presidential run
against Agudath Israel's **Eliahu Kirszbraun**. No source reached has Erlich standing for the
presidency in 1924, or running against Kirszbraun ever; the only documented contest is **1936,
against Jakub Trokenheim**, as the same dossier's chronology says. Removed.

**Kehilla 1936**: the Bund took **fifteen of fifty** seats, Erlich **sixteen votes**,
**Trokenheim** the presidency with **nineteen** **[A, Wikipedia]**. **Yitzhak Schipper's ten
votes**, often quoted alongside, is in no source reached and is dropped. **The accusation
against Grunbaum and Jabotinsky**, that they aided antisemitic agitation by accepting the
premise that Polish Jews should emigrate, is **[R]**: consistent across summaries, **not
verified against a primary 1936 press account**.

**The International.** The dossier's framing, that the Polish Bund was admitted to the Labour
and Socialist International around 1930-31 with Erlich central to it, does not survive the
*Leksikon*, which has him at Socialist International congresses as a Bund delegate at **Hamburg
1923** and **Marseille 1925**, before that window, then **Vienna 1931** **[A]**. What does date
to 1930 is more precise: Polish Wikipedia records membership of the **Socialist International
Executive from 1930** **[A]**.

**The 1934 pamphlet**, *In kamf farn revolutsyonern sotsyalizm* **[R]**: catalogued in the Bund
bibliography and by the Yiddish Book Center, record **not opened**.

**The 1938 reply to Dubnov.** The **text** is **[A]**, fetched verbatim in Samuel A. Portnoy's
translation; its **October 1938 publication in Di Tsukunft** is **[R]**, asserted by secondary
summaries, unconfirmed against the periodical. Di Tsukunft was published in **New York** while
Erlich wrote in **Warsaw**; the stop is placed where he wrote. **Two quotations were altered in
the dossier and are restored to source wording:** "Zionism has over the years evolved into an
open ally..." rewrites **"Over the years, Zionism has evolved into an open ally of our deadly
enemy—anti-Semitism."**, and "For this struggle we must seek partners..." drops the opening
conjunction of **"But for this struggle..."**.

## 6. Arrest, prison, release, second arrest, death (1939-1942)

**Brest, 4 October 1939.** All three sources agree: English Wikipedia, Polish Wikipedia ("4
pazdziernika 1939"), *Leksikon* ("Arrested by Soviets in Brisk") **[A]**. The "4 or 6 October"
hedge should go: **no source reached offers 6 October**. Polish Wikipedia adds the **Brest
railway station** **[A]**; the pin is central Brest. **Victor Alter** was arrested **29
September 1939** **[A]**, five days earlier.

**Lubyanka from October 1939** **[A, Polish Wikipedia]**, better than the dossier's **[R]**. The
atmospheric detail (isolation, sleep deprivation, interrogation by Beria in person) is in **none
of the four sources reached**; the campa names it as tradition. Coordinates corrected to
**55.7607, 37.6283**.

**Butyrka** detention **[A, Wikipedia]**. The claim that the NKVD **compelled him to write
extensive narratives of Bund history and personnel** is confirmed by nothing reached and is
demoted out of the campa. *Coordinate decision, flagged:* the dossier gave 55.7897, 37.5875 and
corrected it to about 55.7936, 37.5847; the file uses **neither**, inheriting **55.78444,
37.59389** byte-identical from `victor_alter.journey.json` so both men's Butyrka stops sit on
one point. That pin is roughly 500 m from the walls; if corrected, **both files must move**.

**Saratov, 2 August 1941**, death sentence commuted to ten years. The dossier preserved a
1940-against-1941 discrepancy that should be closed: Polish Wikipedia gives "2 sierpnia 1941",
the *Leksikon* "August 2, 1941: Sentenced to death; sentence commuted to 10 years" **[A]**, and
**English Wikipedia's "2 August 1940" is the lone outlier and internally impossible**, since the
Saratov transfer was **summer 1941** and a 1940 sentence cannot sit six weeks before a release
granted under a July 1941 agreement. Coordinates moved from 51.5924, 46.0348, about 6.5 km
north, to central Saratov.

**Release under Sikorski-Mayski**, signed in **London 30 July 1941** **[A]**. **Contradiction,
unresolved:** the *Leksikon* gives **11 September 1941** for Erlich with Alter freed on the
12th; Polish Wikipedia gives **12 September** for Erlich himself; `victor_alter.journey.json`
dates Alter's release to **13 September**. The **release city is named by no source**; Moscow is
inference. Beria's reported acknowledgement of a fatal error is in **none** of the sources
reached, and English Wikipedia's 1942 window for the release is wrong on the others' evidence.

**Kuibyshev, autumn 1941**, evacuation east with the Polish mission **[A]**. The dossier's
**Grand Hotel** is confirmed by nothing reached and conflicts with English Wikipedia's own
"while residing in the Polish embassy". Hotel dropped, embassy kept.

**Re-arrest, night of 3-4 December 1941** **[A, English Wikipedia on both men; Polish Wikipedia
"4 grudnia 1941"]**. **Minority variant flagged:** the *Leksikon* dissents with "October 1941:
Re-arrested in Kuibyshev during German advance". Two details are carried by **no source
reached** and are cut rather than tagged **[A]** against citations that do not support them:
that their conversations about the killing of Polish officers were **secretly recorded and
reported to Stalin** (**[R]** at best), and that the arrest followed **a Soviet decree
reclassifying annexed-territory Jews as Soviet citizens** (an unsourced causal link that had
carried an **[A]** tag).

**Death, 14 or 15 May 1942, Kuibyshev. Contradiction, unresolved:** English Wikipedia and the
*Leksikon* give **15 May 1942**, Polish Wikipedia **14 May 1942**; the file takes 15 May and
prints both. **The manner is stated flat.** "Died in NKVD custody" is a formula the sources do
not use: *Leksikon*, "Hanged himself in Kuibyshev cell"; Polish Wikipedia, suicide by hanging
from the bars of his cell window **[A]**. No grave, no body, no autopsy seen outside the
ministry, no family identification **[A]**. The dossier's claim that catalogues "had long treated
1941 as his year of death" is unsupported: the older erroneous date is **December 1942**.

## 7. Afterlife

**23 February 1943:** ambassador **Maxim Litvinov** tells American labour leaders including
**William Green** of the AFL that both men had been executed as enemy agents **[A, Wikipedia]**,
merging Erlich's May 1942 death with Alter's later execution into one fictitious event. In the
**Warsaw ghetto** that February, letters signed **"Henryk Wiktor"** circulate as proof that both
are alive **[R]**, a wartime rumour presented as such.

**Memorial publishing.** Open Library holds *The living record of two leaders of labor*
(Erlich-Alter Memorial Conference) in **two records, 1942 and 1943**, the latter subtitled
"Henryk Erlich, born 1882, Victor Alter, born 1890, executed -- December, 1942" **[A]**; *The
Case of Henryk Erlich and Victor Alter* (New York, 1943), the Yiddish *Henrik Erlikh un Viktor
Alter* (1943 and 1951, contributors including **J. S. Hertz**) and Portnoy's English edition
(1990), all **[R]**. The erroneous December 1942 date was thus fixed in print as early as 1942.
**Buenos Aires, flagged:** the interlock lens reported a **Buenos Aires 1943** *gedenk-bukh* and
marked it dossier-derived, not refetched. It is the only Argentine anchor in this life, so it is
used at the canonical pin with the provenance stated. **It is the weakest sourced item here.**

**The cenotaph, 17 April 1988**, Jewish Cemetery, Okopowa Street, Warsaw **[A, English and
Polish Wikipedia]**, erected against the opposition of Poland's communist government through
**Marek Edelman** and Solidarity, over three thousand at the unveiling **[R]**. Inscription
**[A, Wikipedia `Victor Alter`]**: "Leaders of the Bund, Henryk Erlich, b. 1882, and Wiktor
Alter, b. 1890. Executed in the Soviet Union." The stone enshrines the 1943 propaganda version.
*Coordinate mess:* one monument, four points (52.2529, 20.982; 52.2478, 20.9838; 52.2475,
20.9747; `victor_alter.journey.json` **52.2439, 20.9777**). The file inherits Alter's point so
one cenotaph sits in one place. **If corrected, correct both.**

**Rehabilitation, 8 February 1991** **[A, Wikipedia `Victor Alter`]**, with **Victor Erlich**
informed. **The standard attribution is anachronistic twice over:** on 8 February 1991 the USSR
still existed, dissolving that December, and Yeltsin was not president but **Chairman of the
Supreme Soviet of the RSFSR**, President of Russia only from **10 July 1991**. English
Wikipedia's "under Russian president Boris Yeltsin" is not inherited.

**Documented absence:** no street, square or building named for Erlich is recorded in the sources
checked, a checked negative rather than an inference. The dossier's "slated for publication" for
**Molly Crabapple**, *Here Where We Live Is Our Country*, is out of date: published April 2026,
a New York Times bestseller **[A]**.

## Interlocks named in the journey file

Two, both direct relations. **`victor_alter`**: co-leader from 1920, arrested in the same sweep,
held in the same prison, released a day or two apart, re-arrested on the same night, entered in
Soviet paperwork as one case, dead in the same building, memorialised on the same stone; named
at Krakow 1920, Brest 1939, Butyrka, Kuibyshev and the cenotaph. **`vladimir_medem`**: named to
the same Bund Central Committee after the tenth conference, April/May 1917.

**Declined**, each shared geography or a parallel institutional position with **no documented
contact**: `rosa_luxemburg` (same Warsaw milieu, opposed parties on the national question);
`leon_trotsky` (the Petrograd Soviet months after Erlich sat on its executive, no meeting in the
record); `golda_meir` (no Warsaw stop, no mention of Erlich, Grunbaum or Jabotinsky in her
file); **Arkadi Kremer** (in QUEUE.md, **no journey.json** to interlock with).
