# Christian Dior (1905-1957): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, 2026-08-08. Slug `christian_dior`. Paired by QUEUE.md with `cristobal_balenciaga`, inside a planned fashion cluster with Vuitton, Hugo Boss and Chanel.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, house legend, or a claim carried from general knowledge with no source opened in this pass. Contradictions are flagged in place and left standing. Where a pool coordinate was checked against Nominatim or a national Wikipedia and found wrong, the corrected figure is given and the wrong one recorded, since the wrong one still circulates.

## 1. Granville and Passy, 1905-1926

**Birth.** 21 January 1905, Villa Les Rhumbs, Granville, Manche; second of five children of Maurice Dior, fertilizer manufacturer working through the port, and Madeleine Martin. **[A: en.wikipedia "Christian Dior"; Musée Christian Dior]**

*Coordinate.* The pool gave the villa twice, wrongly (48.8373 / -1.6116, ~1.5 km off near the Pointe du Roc; 48.8378 / -1.6083, ~1.2 km southwest). Corrected to **48.8430, -1.5925** (Nominatim; fr.wikipedia gives 48.84193, -1.59251). All three villa stops use it.

**The villa and its garden.** Bought by his parents 1906, sold after the 1929 crash, acquired by the town of Granville in 1938 for the garden, opened as the Musée Christian Dior in 1997, the only museum in France carrying the *Musée de France* label and devoted to one couturier. Madeleine Dior planted the garden with roses and lily of the valley, both of which recur through Dior's couture, perfume and jewellery. **[A: Musée Christian Dior; fr.wikipedia; Normandy Tourism]** The garden is dated only across a spread, c. 1906-1920s, so the stop is pinned at 1910 as a placement. A statue stands in the grounds near a compass-rose motif. **[R: Alamy caption]**

**Paris.** The family takes an apartment in Passy, keeping Granville for holidays. **[A]** *Contradiction:* chronology lens 1911, geography lens 1910; 1911 adopted. **Gap:** no source gives the street, so the pin (48.8574, 2.2764) is an approximate placement inside the quarter, labelled as such.

**Schooling.** Lycée Janson-de-Sailly, 106 rue de la Pompe, to 1923; then the École Libre des Sciences Politiques, 27 rue Saint-Guillaume, aimed at the foreign service; leaves 1926 with no degree. **[A: en.wikipedia; Sciences Po alumni page]** The **fortune-teller** of c. 1919 is house legend **[R: WWD; MOJEH]** and gets no stop, being folded into the star-on-the-pavement stop.

## 2. The galleries and the ruin, 1928-1934

**Galerie Jacques Bonjean**, 34 rue La Boétie, 1928, on his father's money and on condition the Dior name not appear on the sign; shows Picasso, Dalí, Braque, Max Jacob, Giacometti, Christian Bérard. **[A: en.wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Pin 48.8737, 2.3106.

**The ruin, 1931.** The Depression and Maurice Dior's property speculation destroy the business; Madeleine and one son die; the villa is sold; the gallery closes. **[A: en.wikipedia; WWD timeline]** **Gap:** the site of the fertilizer works is unconfirmed everywhere, so the ruin is pinned at the villa.

**Galerie Pierre Colle**, 29 rue Cambacérès, Surrealist programme, closing at the end of 1933. **[A for the gallery, R for the opening year]** *Contradiction, unresolved:* the chronology lens dates the move 1932, the geography lens and the correction note 1931, immediately on the Bonjean closure; the journey adopts 1931 with the disagreement in `date_confidence`. *Detail set aside:* the geography lens placed the gallery "in an alley connecting the two streets," which are ~250 m apart with no named passage joining them in anything consulted, so the phrase is unsourced embellishment and is not repeated. Pin **48.8738, 2.3176**.

**Illness.** Tuberculosis, roughly a year of convalescence. *Two contradictions, neither resolved.* (i) **Date:** en.wikipedia places both closure and illness in 1933, the chronology lens 1934; the journey uses 1934 and flags 1933. (ii) **Place:** the pool says Callian, Var, while the correction note observes that biographies put much of the convalescence in Ibiza and the Balearics and that "roughly a year in Callian" overstates the source. Both are in the campa. *Coordinate:* Callian, given twice as 43.6672, 6.7517 (~5 km north, open hillside), corrected to **43.6224, 6.7528** (Nominatim). *Unverified:* "Les Nayses" as the name of the Callian property is uncorroborated and not used.

## 3. The draughtsman, 1935-1940

Sells fashion sketches to *Le Figaro*, *Le Jardin des Modes*, and directly to houses including Piguet, Patou, Nina Ricci, Maggy Rouff, Molyneux, Schiaparelli and Balenciaga, from 1935. **[A: WWD timeline; en.wikipedia; Biography.com]** **Gap:** no office address, because there was none; the stop uses the canonical Paris pin 48.8566, 2.3522. Hired by **Robert Piguet** as design assistant, 1938, 3 Rond-Point des Champs-Élysées, for three collections. **[A: en.wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]**

**Mobilized 1939**, French 47th Infantry Division. **[A: en.wikipedia]** **Gap, stated as a gap:** garrison, sector and length of service are absent from every source reached, which makes this the largest true blank in the life. The stop uses the canonical Paris pin, says so in the campa, and repeats the warning in `date_confidence`. **Callian, 1940:** after the armistice he works the family land with his father and, by some accounts, sells vegetables. **[A]**

## 4. The Occupation, 1941-1945

**Engagement by Lucien Lelong**, 6 October 1941; Pierre Balmain follows 1 December. **[A: fr.wikipedia "Lucien Lelong"]** Sharper than the pool's bare "1941."

*Three corrections applied.* **Givenchy removed:** the pool had Dior working "per some accounts" alongside a young Hubert de Givenchy, who did not reach Lelong until 1946, as Dior was leaving; the journey states this explicitly rather than dropping it silently, because the false version is widespread. **Address:** the pool twice wrote "16 rue Matignon"; the house was at **16 avenue Matignon**, Paris 8e, from 1924 (fr.wikipedia), pin **48.8709, 2.3136**, the afterlife lens's 48.8687, 2.3009 being ~1 km west and discarded. **Dates:** the afterlife lens gave 1942-1945; the span is 1941-1946.

**The politics of staying open.** As president of the Chambre Syndicale de la Haute Couture Parisienne, Lelong negotiated with the German authorities to keep couture in Paris rather than see it moved to Berlin and Vienna and thousands of fashion workers conscripted into German labour. The houses that stayed open, his among them, dressed the wives of German officers and of French collaborators, as did several others. **[A: Forbes on Lelong; en.wikipedia]** Carried inside the Lelong stop; the record does not adjudicate a moral argument the sources themselves do not settle. **Film costume** begins 1942 with *Le lit à colonnes*. **[A]**

**Catherine Dior.** The pool tagged this **[R]** as unverified; it upgrades to **[A]** with specifics from en.wikipedia, "Catherine Dior": F2 network from November 1941; arrested in Paris 6 July 1944 with 26 others; deported by 15 August 1944 to Ravensbrück, then Torgau, Abteroda and Leipzig-Markkleeberg; freed near Dresden 19 April 1945; back in Paris 28 May 1945; died at Callian 17 June 2008. **[A]** *Coordinates:* 180 rue de la Pompe, seat of the French Gestapo unit known as the *bande de la rue de la Pompe*, is **48.8714, 2.2838** (pool ~900 m off, beside the lycée); Ravensbrück is **53.1890, 13.1700** (pool ~28 km west, in open countryside).

**The Miss Dior naming** stays **[R]**: the perfume is universally said to honour Catherine, the house has never endorsed it, and no statement from Christian or the family supports it. The hedge sits on that clause alone.

## 5. Thirty avenue Montaigne, 1946-1947

**The star.** On his way to meet Marcel Boussac, Dior is said to have found a cast-iron star loose on the pavement and taken it as an omen. **[R: La Galerie Dior, "The star"]** *Contradiction inside the pool:* the afterlife lens places it on the **rue Saint-Honoré** (48.8656, 2.3212), the geography lens and the house's own version on the **rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré** (48.8697, 2.3155), two different streets. The house version is adopted, tag [R], this being brand legend rather than contemporaneous documentation. The charms and the clairvoyant Madame Delahaye are the same order of claim **[R: ICON-ICON; MOJEH]**; a dress named *Étoile* appears in Haute Couture Spring-Summer 1952 **[A]**.

**The house opens** at 30 avenue Montaigne, ~85 staff, seven fit models. **[A]** *Contradiction, unresolved:* 8 October 1946 is given as the founding and 16 December 1946 as the physical opening, both circulating; the journey uses 1946-10-08 with the disagreement in `date_confidence`. Maurice Dior dies the same year **[A]**, no stop.

**Corolle**, 12 February 1947: ~90 garments, rounded shoulder, cinched waist, full skirt; Carmel Snow of *Harper's Bazaar* is credited with the coinage. **[A for the collection; the wording of Snow's remark is R]** *Provenance recovered:* the pool flagged the Snow quote as untraceable, cited only as "p. 135" on Wikiquote. That bare page number sits under a Wikiquote heading naming **Marie France Pochna, *Christian Dior: The Man Who Made the World Look New* (Arcade, 1996)**, so the citation is Snow in Pochna (1996), p. 135. *Caution:* Wikiquote assigns three overlapping Snow quotations to that page, so it may aggregate rather than quote continuously, and Pochna was **not opened**.

**Miss Dior** and the **Colifichets** boutique, 1947. **[A]** **Eva Perón at the Ritz**, 22 July 1947: days after the Legion of Honour ceremony she is shown gowns by Dior, with Fath and Rochas, in her suite. **[A: eva_peron.journey.json]** Canonical Ritz pin **48.8681, 2.3286** inherited byte-identical, shared with `marcel_proust.journey.json`. **Neiman Marcus Award**, Dallas, September 1947, alongside Ferragamo and Hartnell, his first crossing to the United States. **[A: WWD]** **The export figure**, "about 75 percent of French fashion export revenue" by the first anniversary, is **[R: WWD timeline]**: no audited source supports it.

## 7. The last years, 1955-1957

**"The master of us all."** The pool tagged this **[A]** twice. Retagged **[R]**: no contemporary source records Dior saying it, and it survives by repetition. `cristobal_balenciaga.journey.json` already carries the tribute (V&A record O1435930, plus Blume 2013) and flags that Dior has no journey to point back at; that is now closed. The afterlife lens attached a generic Île de la Cité pin to the quotation; the stop instead uses **48.8657, 2.3011**, the canonical Balenciaga avenue George V pin, being about the geography of the rivalry rather than a sentence with no location. The second line, "with fabrics, we do what we can, Balenciaga does what he wants," is likewise undated and known only from that file; a third, the orchestra-and-conductor quotation, sits there at a 1959 stop, after Dior's death, and is **not** used.

**Yves Saint Laurent hired**, 20 June 1955, born 1 August 1936, therefore **eighteen**, not twenty-one. **[A: en.wikipedia]** The pool's "then twenty-one" conflates his age at hiring with his age at succession in 1957; the correction is stated in the campa because the error is everywhere.

**Oscar nomination** for *Terminal Station*, 1955 **[A]**: no stop, the place field being "USA / Italy" with no defensible coordinate. **Parsons award** and **Diorissimo**, 1956 **[A]**. **7 boulevard Jules-Sandeau** **[A]**: coordinate corrected from 48.8637, 2.268 (~530 m off) to **48.8613, 2.2733**; **gap**, the year of the move is not given, only "1950s."

**Time cover**, 4 March 1957 **[A: content.time.com]**. The attached claim that he was the **first** fashion designer on a Time cover is **false**: Elsa Schiaparelli was on the cover of 13 August 1934. The journey states both.

**Death**, 24 October 1957, Montecatini Terme, heart attack reportedly his third, aged 52. **[A]** *Contradiction, explicitly unresolved:* the trigger is disputed rather than undocumented, a card game, a fish bone at dinner, or, per Paris gossip written into Baron de Redé's memoirs, a strenuous private encounter. **[A that the accounts differ; each version R: Musée YSL Paris; Nicki Swift]** The two lenses also give the spa hotel as 43.8827, 10.7684 and 43.8858, 10.7772, ~1.1 km apart; the first is used.

**Funeral**, Église Saint-Honoré-d'Eylau; ~9,000 attend; coffin covered in lily of the valley. **[A]** *Coordinate resolved:* the parish has three sites, and in 1957 it was the older building on **place Victor-Hugo, 48.8697, 2.2836**, the avenue Raymond-Poincaré church taking over only in 1974. The geography lens gave 48.8703, 2.2865, matching neither. **Gap:** the exact day is fixed nowhere consulted, a strange blank for an event that size.

**Burial**, cemetery of Callian, near the chapel of Saint-Barthélemy, beside his father, later beside his housekeeper and Catherine (d. 2008). **[A: Alamy, Getty and Bridgeman grave captions; Find a Grave #6749]** *Contradiction, flagged not resolved:* one automated summary names the Château de la Colle Noire instead; photographs of the stone outweigh it, and the campa says so. *Note corrected:* the afterlife lens glossed Callian as the site of "Le Moulin du Coudret," which was at **Milly-la-Forêt, Essonne**. **Succession:** Yves Saint Laurent, 21, named head designer **[A]**; the story that Dior had privately named him is **[R: reported tradition]**.

## 8. Afterlife

***Dior by Dior***: French 1956, Fraser translation 1957, V&A/Abrams reissue 2018 with plates by Avedon, Beaton and Horst. **[A]** ***Christian Dior: Designer of Dreams***, V&A, 2 February to 1 September 2019, curated by Oriole Cullen, designed by Nathalie Crinière: ~500 objects, 200 haute couture garments, **594,994 visitors**, the museum's most-visited exhibition, past *Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty* by over 100,000. **[A: The Art Newspaper; BBC News]**

## 9. Quotations: what was checked, and how far

Nine of the 44 stops carry a quote. Three from *Dior by Dior* are **[A]** but seen only as search excerpts, no page image; one, "It is one of the great secrets of haute couture...," is **corrected**, since the book reads "the dress **which** contains the least cuts," not "that", the pool's Goodreads transcription having modernised the pronoun (around p. 77). The *Little Dictionary* opening is **restored** to "...a dictionary **on the subject**." Two Pochna quotations (pp. 48, 51) come via Wikiquote, ellipses as transcribed and "bougeois" corrected. Snow's coinage is **[R]**, provenance recovered as Pochna p. 135. The Dallas line is **[A]** via WWD; "with fabrics, we do what we can..." is **[R]**, undated, via the Balenciaga file.

**Not used.** The *LIFE* 1948 and McNair-Hansen 1949 lines are **[A]** by citation but seen only through Wikiquote's raw wikitext. "A painter may risk only his canvas and his beefsteak..." (*Talking About Fashion*, 1954) is **[R]**, search-result summary only. Jean Cocteau's line on Dior belongs in a Cocteau file if one is ever made; its provenance is now recoverable as **Pochna (1996), p. 1**, wording "Dior is that nimble genius unique to our age with the magical name - combining God and gold [dieu et or]," the bracketed gloss restored, since the pun *Dior = dieu + or* is the whole line.
