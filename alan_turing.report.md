# Alan Turing (1912-1954): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, 2026-08-12. Slug `alan_turing`. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Calendar: gregorian.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried only by secondary literature this pass could not put against a primary. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated; wrong pool dates and coordinates are corrected with the reason printed.

Output: 44 stops in 9 segments. Every hedge below lives in the matching stop's `date_confidence` field, never in the campa prose.

## 1. Birth and the boarded childhood (1912-1926)

- Born 23 June 1912 at 2 Warrington Crescent, Maida Vale, son of Julius Mathison Turing of the Indian Civil Service and Ethel Sara Turing **[A: Wikipedia; English Heritage plaque]**. The building was then the Warrington Lodge Medical and Surgery Home for Ladies, a hotel from 1935 **[A: Wikipedia, "Colonnade Hotel"]**. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool carried two pins for one doorway, 51.5228, -0.1859 and 51.5262, -0.1859, both wrong; the address is **51.5243, -0.1846**, and both lenses are reconciled to it.
- Boarded during holidays with Colonel and Mrs Ward at Baston Lodge, Upper Maze Hill, St Leonards-on-Sea **[A: Hodges, *Alan Turing: The Enigma*]**; **corrected** from 50.8546, 0.568 (1.1 km too far east) to **50.8560, 0.5522**. Day school St Michael's, 20 Charles Road **[A: Hodges]**, **corrected** from 50.8558, 0.573 to **50.8560, 0.5564** and folded into that stop, 150 m away.
- Hazelhurst Preparatory School, Frant, 1922-1926 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Corrected**: the pool's 51.087, 0.307 is 2.8 km southeast of the village, and "Hazelhurst, Frant" returns **no OpenStreetMap match**, so the school is **not independently locatable**; the pin is the village, 51.0961, 0.2711. Stated as a gap. The pool's lenses also disagree on the county; Frant is East Sussex.

## 2. Sherborne and Christopher Morcom (1926-1930)

- Entered Sherborne aged 13, boarding in Westcott House **[A: Wikipedia; Hodges]**; coordinate 50.9474, -2.5175 checked and retained.
- The sixty-mile cycle ride from Southampton during the General Strike, with an overnight stop at an inn **[R: Hodges; route and inn documented nowhere reachable]**. The **day** is inferred from the strike (3-12 May 1926), not attested.
- Headmaster's report: "If he is to be solely a Scientific Specialist, he is wasting his time at a Public School." **[R]**: seen only through a page summary of turing.org.uk, not a raw scan. Used as a quote with the flag preserved; it is a line about Turing, not by him.
- Christopher Morcom, a year above him, was the central attachment of the school years; he died February 1930 of complications of bovine tuberculosis, and Turing's later preoccupation with mind, matter and survival after death is documented **[A: turing.org.uk]**. **Gap:** no day given; the stop defaults to 1930-02-01.
- Parents bought a house on Ennismore Avenue, Guildford, 1927 **[A: Wikipedia; Hodges]**. **Corrected** from 51.2362, -0.5851 to **51.2433, -0.5593**; no stop spent on it, logged for the coordinate ledger.

## 3. Cambridge and the universal machine (1931-1936)

- Failed the Trinity scholarship December 1929 **[A: Hodges]**; up to King's February 1931, first-class honours 1934 **[A: Wikipedia]**; photographed on Sark 1931 **[A: turing.org.uk]**, folded into the King's campa. Fellow of King's 1935 on a dissertation proving a version of the central limit theorem, in ignorance of Lindeberg's 1922 proof **[A: Wikipedia]**; Smith's Prize 1936 **[A: turing.org.uk]**.
- **"On Computable Numbers": date corrected.** The pool gave 1936-08, which matches nothing. Wikipedia's sequence: **submitted 28 May 1936**, read to the London Mathematical Society **12 November 1936**, printed in *Proc. LMS* ser. 2 vol. 42 in two parts, **30 November** and **23 December 1936**, with a Correction in vol. 43 (1938) after Bernays found errors. The stop is dated to submission, the whole sequence printed in `date_confidence`, and the opening line is verified verbatim against PDF text of the received paper, p. 230. **[A]**

## 4. Princeton (1936-1938)

- Under Alonzo Church, September 1936 to July 1938; PhD June 1938 for "Systems of Logic Based on Ordinals" **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **Building anachronism corrected.** The pool pinned "Fine Hall" at 40.343, -74.6551, which is neither the 1930s building nor the 1970 tower. In 1936-1938 the mathematics department **and** the Institute for Advanced Study were both in the building then called Fine Hall, today's **Jones Hall** **[A: Wikipedia, "Institute for Advanced Study"]**.
- **Second anachronism, self-undercutting.** The pool carried a separate IAS stop at 40.33, -74.6656 because Turing kept company with the Institute men there. But the IAS had **no campus until Fuld Hall opened in 1939**; through 1933-1939 it sat inside Fine Hall, so those men were in Turing's own building. The file uses the Fine Hall pin for the 1936 arrival and reserves the IAS pin for the **1938 offer**, the campa saying Fuld Hall does not exist yet.
- **Coordinate inheritance.** Fine Hall takes **40.3487, -74.6591** byte-identical from `dirac` and `einstein`; the Institute stop takes **40.3298, -74.6672** from `john_von_neumann`, `robert_oppenheimer`, `claude_shannon`. **[R]** The 1938 offer from von Neumann, declined: his file establishes the chair from 1933 but does **not** state the offer.

## 5. Bletchley Park (1938-1942)

- Part-time GC&CS work from September 1938, trained by Dilly Knox at Broadway Buildings **[A: Wikipedia; Hodges]**. **Corrected** from 51.4996, -0.131 to **51.4995, -0.1338**, about 215 m.
- Reported to Bletchley 4 September 1939, working first from the stable yard cottage; naval indicator solved and Banburismus conceived December 1939; headed Hut 8; bombes built at the British Tabulating Machine Company, Letchworth, under Harold Keen **[A: Wikipedia, "Bombe"; Bletchley Park Trust]**.
- **Attribution corrected.** The pool credited the 18 March 1940 machine to Turing's design "with Gordon Welchman's improvements". Wrong: the first bombe, **Victory**, **lacked** the diagonal board; the first machine with it was **Agnus Dei**, 8 August 1940 **[A: Wikipedia, "Bombe"]**.
- Operation Ruthless, September 1940: Fleming's plan to ditch a captured bomber in the Channel and take the Enigma material from the rescue boat, cancelled when no boat appeared; Turing and Peter Twinn came to Frank Birch about it "like undertakers cheated of a corpse" **[A: `ian_fleming.journey.json`, whose Bletchley coordinate 51.9974, -0.7402 is inherited byte-identical]**.
- **Tag dispute flagged.** The pool tagged the Joan Clarke engagement **[R]**, claiming Wikipedia and turing.org.uk conflict over a Bletchley or Manchester setting. Wikipedia treats it as fact, **spring 1941 in Hut 8**, and the DNB entry does not contradict that. Carried as **[A]**, with the pool's hedge recorded rather than deleted.
- Letter to Churchill, 28 October 1941, with Alexander, Welchman and Milner-Barry; Turingery, the hand method for the Lorenz wheel patterns, July 1942 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **[R]** Churchill's "Action this day" minute in reply is standard in the literature but **not** in the pool; the campa carries it and `date_confidence` says so.

## 6. The Atlantic crossing (1942-1945)

**Itinerary corrected three ways.**

1. The crossing is **November 1942** **[A: Wikipedia; turing.org.uk]**, but the pool folded Dayton into it; per Wikipedia (USNCML), **Turing visited Dayton in December 1942**.
2. The Washington venue is anachronistic. The pool pinned the **Naval Communications Annex, Nebraska Avenue** at 38.9515, -77.0716; that site is really 38.9389, -77.0831, and the Navy took it over only by the end of 1942, buying the land 20 July 1943. In November 1942 OP-20-G was still in the **Main Navy Building on Constitution Avenue** **[A: Wikipedia, "Nebraska Avenue Complex"]**; the stop is retitled the Navy Department. **[R]** Its coordinate 38.8917, -77.0430 is that building's site, reconstructed and **not** supplied by any pool source.
3. The Dayton pin 39.7589, -84.1916 is the **city centroid**; the laboratory occupied **NCR Building 26** at Patterson Boulevard and Stewart Street, approx. **39.733, -84.188**, demolished January 2008 **[A: Wikipedia, USNCML]**.

- Bell Labs, 463 West Street, early 1943, on speech enciphering feeding SIGSALY **[A: Wikipedia; Hodges]**. **Coordinate inherited**: geography gave 40.7367, -74.0086, but `claude_shannon.journey.json` pins the building at **40.7196, -74.0089**, and the atlas rule is byte-identical inheritance; the discrepancy is recorded, not averaged. That file is also the strongest interlock here **[A]**: two months of daily tea, both forbidden to discuss their own classified work, Turing handing Shannon "On Computable Numbers", with von Neumann's Los Alamos season stated as contemporaneity, not a meeting.
- Returned to Bletchley March 1943, Hugh Alexander formally taking Hut 8; Hanslope Park from 1944, building Delilah with Donald Bayley; OBE 1946, citation unpublished **[A: Wikipedia; Hodges]**. Hanslope **corrected** from 52.0733, -0.8004 to **52.1050, -0.8090**, a shift of 3.6 km.

## 7. Teddington (1945-1950)

- Lodged at 78 High Street, Hampton; ACE design read to the NPL executive committee 19 February 1946 with costings and sample programs; Pilot ACE ran its first program May 1950 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Corrected**: the pool's 51.4257, -0.34 is the NPL site pin, 590 m north of Bushy House itself at **51.4204, -0.3392**.
- **Marathon: date, venue and race all corrected, and the correction is itself only partly secure.** The pool gave August 1949 at Windsor, sourcing the venue to "the conventional Polytechnic Marathon course", which conflates two races: the Polytechnic ran Windsor to Chiswick, the **AAA championship did not**, and Wikipedia rules out 1949 by framing the run as a tryout for the **1948** Olympic team. The standard secondary account gives **August 1947 at Loughborough**, fifth in **2:46:03**, used here as **[R]** since no primary results archive was reachable. Walton Athletic Club membership is not in dispute **[A: Hodges]**.

## 8. Manchester (1948-1952)

- **Sequence flagged.** The Manchester "Baby" ran the first stored program in **June 1948** **[A: turing.org.uk]**; Turing arrived in **October**, so he was not present. **[R]** The precise date 21 June 1948 and the seventeen-instruction program lie **outside** the pool, which gives the month only.
- **Appointment contradiction, not resolved.** turing.org.uk gives **October 1948** for the move as deputy director of the Computing Machine Laboratory; Wikipedia gives a **1948** reader appointment and **1949** for the deputy directorship. Both printed in the stop. Turochamp, with D. G. Champernowne, was conceived because no machine existed to run it **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- "Computing Machinery and Intelligence", *Mind* n.s. LIX/236, pp. 433-460, October 1950 **[A]**. Eight quotations verified verbatim against PDF text from courses.cs.umbc.edu/471/papers/turing.pdf (local copy at /home/drdoeg/instance2_home/turing1950.txt). **Place corrected**: the pool attached the opening quotation to the National Physical Laboratory, but Turing left the NPL in 1948 and was at **Manchester** when the paper was written and published.
- FRS 1951, on a citation resting principally on the 1936 paper **[A: Wikipedia]**; BBC talk on machine intelligence, 1951 **[A: Britannica]**. **Gap:** year only for both; both default to 1951-01-01.
- **"The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis": date corrected.** The pool gave 1952-01; the paper appeared in *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 237/641, pp. 37-72, issue dated **14 August 1952**, while turing.org.uk gives **November 1951** as the submission, the likeliest source of the drift. Both printed. **[R]** That the numerical work ran on the Ferranti Mark 1 is standard but not in the pool.

## 9. Wilmslow, the conviction, the death (1952-1954)

- **House name corrected.** The pool called the house "Copper Folly"; Wikipedia records the 2004 plaque at Turing's former residence **Hollymeade**. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool's 53.3251, -2.2367 is essentially the Wilmslow town centroid, while Adlington Road geocodes to 53.328-53.331, -2.205 to -2.191; the stop uses **53.3282, -2.2050**, 2.2 to 3.0 km east.
- **Arrest and conviction were conflated in the pool.** Burglary **23 January 1952**, reported by Turing, who acknowledged the relationship with Arnold Murray (19) during the investigation; both charged **February 1952**; **31 March 1952** is **trial and conviction** at Knutsford Quarter Sessions **[A: Wikipedia]**. Carried as two stops. Sentence: a year's probation conditional on oestrogen treatment in place of prison, plus loss of clearance and consultancy **[A: turing.org.uk]**.
- **The Routledge letter.** The pool carried the syllogism as **[R]** and misquoted its opening ("I'm rather afraid"); the corrected text, with its three lines unpunctuated, is in the stop's quote field. Letter to Norman Routledge, **February 1952**, between charge and plea, closing "Yours in distress, Alan"; original in the Turing Archive, King's College, quoted in that form by Hodges, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, §9, citing the archive **[A]**. **No manuscript image was fetched**; the upgrade from [R] rests on the SEP's citation, not the archive.
- Norway, summer 1952, a young man met in Bergen **[R: Hodges; itinerary undocumented]**. Further security scare over a foreign visitor to the house, March 1953 **[A: turing.org.uk]**.
- **Death: carried as a contradiction, not resolved.** Cyanide poisoning, a partly eaten apple beside the bed, inquest verdict of suicide **[A: Wikipedia]**; Sara Turing maintained to her death that it was accidental poisoning from his home electroplating **[A: Hodges]**. **The apple was never tested**, which is the crux. **Date discrepancy in the pool:** two lenses give **1954-06-07**, the geography lens has him found on 8 June "a day after taking cyanide". Consistent, but never reconciled there; the stop is dated 1954-06-07 with the discrepancy printed.
- **Cremation: three coordinates in the pool for one crematorium.** Geography 51.3273, -0.5804; afterlife 51.3168, -0.5623; Wikipedia's Hermitage Road site **51.3118, -0.6027**, to which both are reconciled. Cremation 12 June 1954 and the scattering confirmed **[A]**; **[R]** his father's ashes being scattered there could not be.

## 10. Afterlife (1966-2021)

- Given no stop: the Turing Award (1966), Hodges (1983), *The Imitation Game* (2014). **Collected Works corrected:** the pool gave "1990s"; the volumes ran **1992-2001**, and [R] upgrades to [A].
- **Alan Turing Memorial**, Sackville Gardens, bronze by Glyn Hughes, unveiled 23 June 2001, £15,000 raised by Richard Humphry's fund, Derek Jacobi patron, coordinates 53.476722, -2.236028 confirmed **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Two corrections carried into the campa:** the ciphertext IEKYF ROMSI ADXUO KVKZC GUBJ is on the **bench**, not the plinth; and the popular decoding "founder of computer science" is **impossible**, since Enigma cannot encipher a letter as itself and that reading shares letters with the ciphertext at matching positions. Planning documents record "pioneer of digital computing" instead; the true plaintext is unresolved.
- Slate statue by Stephen Kettle at Bletchley Park, commissioned by Sidney Frank, 2007 **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Gap:** exact date not in the pool nor confirmed against Trust material; defaults to 2007-01-01.
- Brown's apology 10 September 2009, by e-petition response rather than parliamentary act; royal pardon 24 December 2013; Policing and Crime Act 2017, the "Alan Turing law", automatic pardons for an estimated 49,000 men **[A: Wikipedia; UK Parliament, commencement date not re-verified]**.
- Bank of England £50 note, 23 June 2021, with his signature from the Bletchley visitors' book, a table from the 1936 paper, a drawing of the ACE, and binary ticker tape encoding his birth date **[A: Wikipedia, confirmed by fetch]**. Plaques: birthplace 23 June 1998, Wilmslow 7 June 2004 **[A]**. The Guildford commemoration, which the pool listed as a plaque, is principally a **bronze statue by John W. Mills, University of Surrey, 28 October 2004**.
