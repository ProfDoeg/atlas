# Vladimir Lenin - research report
*Slug `vladimir_lenin`. Pool verified 2026-08; report written 2026-08-24 for `vladimir_lenin.journey.json` (45 stops, 9 segments).*
*Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov, b. Simbirsk 22 April 1870 (10 April O.S.), d. Gorki 21 January 1924.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or standard consensus not independently re-fetched at verification. Russia switched Julian to Gregorian on 1/14 February 1918, which is why almost every pre-1918 event carries two numbers; the file is Gregorian throughout and says so in `calendar`. Two industries of falsification ran on this biography in sequence, the Soviet hagiographic apparatus and the post-1991 reaction. Where sources conflict this report says so, and adjudicates only where marked.

## 1. Simbirsk, 1870-1887

- Born 22 April 1870 N.S. / 10 April O.S.; birth house on **Streletskaya Street**, a museum since 16 April 1970. **[A, ru.wikipedia "Istoriya Ulyanovska"]** *Correction:* the pool conflated three Ulyanovsk sites, the Streletskaya birth house, the Lenin Memorial complex, and the Moskovskaya Street family house-museum of 1878-1887. The file names only the first.
- Father **Ilya Nikolayevich Ulyanov**, schools inspector, dies of cerebral hemorrhage January 1886. **[A]**
- Gymnasium under headmaster **Fyodor Kerensky**, father of Alexander Kerensky; gold medal 1887. **[A]**
- Brother **Alexander Ulyanov** hanged at Shlisselburg 20 May 1887 O.S. for a Narodnaya Volya plot against Alexander III. **[A]** "We shall not take that road" is **[R]**, a later hagiographic formula by scholarly consensus. Not quoted; the flag sits in `date_confidence`.

## 2. Kazan, Kokushkino, Samara, 1887-1893

- Law faculty at Kazan August 1887; arrested, expelled, struck off after the December student protest; confined at **Kokushkino** under open police supervision. **[A]** Reads Chernyshevsky then Marx **[A/R: attested in outline, the order is standard biography]**.
- **The law degree: the pool's two lenses contradicted each other and one was wrong.** Chronology said May 1890, geography said 1891. Chronology misread its own source: "In May 1890, Maria ... persuaded the authorities to **allow** Lenin to take his exams externally" records permission, not sitting. He sat in two rounds (autumn 1890, spring 1891); first-class diploma **November 1891**. **[A]** The file uses 1891.
- Assistant barrister to **Andrei Khardin**, Samara, 1892-93 **[A; docket detail [R]]**. Alakaevka (~53.0833/50.6167), where he read *Capital*, is folded into that stop to hold the file at 45.

## 3. Petersburg, prison, Siberia, 1893-1900

- St Petersburg autumn 1893; workers' circles; League of Struggle 1895; arrested 20 December 1895 O.S.; ~14 months in the **House of Preliminary Detention, Shpalernaya Street**. **[A]** The milk-ink letters are **[R]**, resting on Krupskaya's memoir.
- Three years' administrative exile, no trial, at **Shushenskoye** from February 1897. **[A]**
- Marries **Krupskaya** in the village church July 1898, the ceremony being the legal condition of her transfer. **[A]** *Contradiction, unresolved:* one source gives 10 July, another 22 July. Consistent with O.S./N.S., but no source says which is which. File dates 1898-07-10 and flags the alternative.

## 4. Iskra and the split, 1900-1907

- **Iskra** editorial base at **Munich** from September 1900 (cover name Meyer) with Plekhanov, Martov, Axelrod, Zasulich, Potresov. **[A]** *Correction:* the pool said the paper was "launched from Munich" in September. It was not. Issue No. 1 was printed in **Leipzig**, dated 1 December 1900 (some sources 11 December), appearing 24 December.
- Pseudonym "Lenin" first in print December 1901. **[R]** Origin genuinely unresolved (the Lena; a borrowed passport; others). Left open.
- ***What Is to Be Done?*** written 1901, published March 1902 by **J. H. W. Dietz, Stuttgart**. **[A]** *Correction:* the pool gave London. Both en.wikipedia "Iskra" and "Revolutionary activity" date the London move to **April 1902**, after publication.
- **Second Congress** July-August 1903, Brussels then London, splitting over the membership clause; Iskra board to Geneva and Lenin loses the paper; Bloody Sunday January 1905; clandestine return to St Petersburg November 1905. **[A]**
- **Tampere, December 1905, first meeting with Stalin. [R]** Not confirmable: en.wikipedia "Lenin Museum, Tampere" returned **404**. Tertiary sources only; the failure is named in `date_confidence`.
- **Kuokkala**, Grand Duchy of Finland, 1906-07, Bolshevik Centre in a dacha. **[A]**
- **Tiflis expropriation** 1907, ~341,000 roubles, senior sanction, bystanders killed. **[R]** No primary or academic source obtained; carried from dossier synthesis and flagged in the stop.

## 5. The long emigration, 1908-1917

- **Capri 1908**, Gorky's villa, during the rupture with Bogdanov. **[A, Service]** Coordinate is Capri town; the villa was not re-verified. *Materialism and Empirio-Criticism* 1908-09 **[R]**.
- **Paris, December 1908-1912**, rooms off the **Rue Beaunier**, "a foul hole". **[A]**
- **Longjumeau party school**, summer 1911: 18 students, 56 lectures by Lenin, staff Semashko, Riazanov, Rappoport, Armand, Lunacharsky. *Tag raised [R] to [A]*: the pool downgraded it for want of a fetched source, but the Wikipedia article documents it directly.
- **Prague Conference** January 1912 **[R]**; *Pravda* founded May 1912 **[R]**; **Krakow** 1912-13 and summers at **Bialy Dunajec / Poronin** 1913-14 **[A]**.
- **The August 1914 arrest: contradiction, adjudicated against the geography lens.** Geography put the arrest at Poronin; chronology and en.wikipedia "Revolutionary activity" put it at **Nowy Targ**. Reconciliation used: the Bialy Dunajec house was searched, he was held at **Nowy Targ** (~49.4817/20.0303), and **Victor Adler**'s intercession got him released and deported to Switzerland. *The reconciliation is this report's, not a source's.* The usual eleven-day figure is **[R]**, softened in prose.
- **Zimmerwald**, 5-8 September 1915, Hotel Beau Sejour, delegates registered as an ornithological society. **[A]** **Kienthal**, **24-30 April 1916** (not simply "April 1916"), Lenin with Zinoviev and Inessa Armand **[A]**; dropped as a separate stop for stop-count reasons and folded into the Zimmerwald campa, its corrected coordinate logged in section 0 for a later pass.
- **Zurich, Spiegelgasse**, 1916 to March 1917. **[R]** The Wikipedia page for the street returned **404**; the residence is everywhere in the secondary literature but the pool saw no page confirming it. The sausage-factory detail is [R] and the stop says so.

## 6. 1917

- **Zurich departure.** *Correction, calendar error:* the pool dated it "27 March 1917", the **Old Style** date, while dating Finland Station 16 April **New Style**: twenty days apart for an eight-day journey. Switzerland was Gregorian. The train left **9 April 1917 N.S. (27 March O.S.)**. **[A]** The carriage was **not sealed** in the legendary sense; passengers disembarked overnight in Frankfurt **[A]**. Terms negotiated through **Fritz Platten**.
- **Finland Station** 16 April N.S. / 3 April O.S.; **April Theses** next day. **[A** for arrival; **[R]** for the exact chronology of first delivery]
- July Days, the German-agent charge, flight to **Razliv**. **[R]** *Correction, twice:* the pool had the hut in Finland and 24 km from where it stands. en.wikipedia "Lenin's Hovel" places it in **Sestroretsk, Kurortny District, Saint Petersburg** and states the site "was located in Russia at the time"; he crossed into Finland only **after** leaving Razliv in late July. The hut was built by **Nikolai Emelianov**, who first hid him and Zinoviev in a shed. *The State and Revolution* begun there, continued in Finland.
- **Smolny** as command post from October; **Winter Palace** taken 7 November N.S. / 25-26 October O.S.; Sovnarkom elected next day with Lenin as chairman. **[A]** **Cheka** under Dzerzhinsky 20 December 1917 **[R]**.

## 7. The state, 1918-1921

- **Constituent Assembly** meets once at the **Tauride Palace** 18-19 January 1918 and is dispersed. **[R** for the narrative, dates confirmed]
- **Brest-Litovsk**, 3 March 1918: ~34% of population, 54% of industrial land, 89% of coalfields, 26% of railways. **[A]** **Lenin was not present.** Kept as a stop because the act and the ratification fight are his; `date_confidence` states the absence. Capital moves to Moscow March 1918 **[R]**.
- **The Penza telegram**, 11 August 1918. **[A]** To Kurayev, Bosch, Minkin and other Penza communists; four numbered points; "Yours, Lenin"; postscript "Find some truly hard men."
  - *Provenance flagged.* `loc.gov/exhibits/archives/ad2kulak.html` returned **HTTP 403** and `web.archive.org` is blocked, so the text was confirmed against en.wikipedia "Lenin's hanging order", which reproduces the LOC translation in full and cites it. **Nobody in this pipeline read the LOC page directly.** LOC reads "hundreds of **verst**", singular. The pool's ellipses silently swallow points 2-4 ("Publish their names. / Seize all grain from them. / Designate hostages.") and the closing "Telegraph the receipt and implementation."; the hanging order is **item 1 of four**, the campa says so, and the quote keeps its ellipses visible. Lenin never travelled to Penza; the stop is the place the order targeted, and says so.
- **The shooting, 30 August 1918.** *Correction, and it matters.* The pool named the **"Hammer and Sickle factory"**, following en.wikipedia "Fanny Kaplan", which is **wrong here**. He was shot at the **Michelson plant**, Partiyny pereulok 1, Zamoskvorechye, later the Moscow Electromechanical Plant "named after Vladimir Ilyich" (ru.wikipedia on that plant; en.wikipedia "Assassination attempts on Vladimir Lenin" agrees). **Serp i Molot** is a different works, the former Guzhon plant in the east of the city.
- **Fanny Kaplan** shot in a Kremlin courtyard without public trial, 3 September 1918. **[A]** Not a stop; see section 9.
- **Comintern** founding congress **2-6 March 1919, in the Kremlin, Moscow**. **[A]** *Correction:* the pool's entry was headed "Smolny/Petrograd" while carrying Moscow coordinates.
- **H. G. Wells** interviews Lenin in a Kremlin office **October 1920**, arranged by Gorky; *Russia in the Shadows*, "the dreamer in the Kremlin". **[A, `h_g_wells.journey.json`, pin 55.752/37.6175, inherited byte-identically]**
- **Kronstadt**, 1-18 March 1921, suppressed under Tukhachevsky. **[A]** Lenin directed from Moscow, so the March 1921 stop sits at the **Tenth Congress in Moscow**, from which delegates left the hall to join the assault, not at the fortress. That congress adopts NEP and the ban on factions **[R]**; the concurrence with Kronstadt is confirmed. USSR created by treaty December 1922 **[R]**.

## 8. Illness, Testament, death, 1922-1924

- Strokes: May 1922 (aphasia, right-sided weakness), December 1922, 10 March 1923. All **[R]**.
- **Testament** ("Letter to the Congress") from December 1922; the addendum of **4 January 1923** proposes removing Stalin as General Secretary. **[A]** The Krupskaya telephone incident as trigger is **[R]**. Read to regional delegations at the Thirteenth Congress May 1924 under rules barring notes and discussion **[A]**; not a stop, carried in `suggested_refs`.
- Moved to Gorki 15 May 1923; dies **21 January 1924 at 18:50**, aged 53, after falling into a coma; autopsy finds severe cerebral sclerosis; brain sectioned 1925. **[A]**
- Lies in state, **Hall of Columns**, 23-27 January 1924. **[A]** Family objections including Krupskaya's, and a reported wish for burial beside his mother in Petrograd, overridden; the Funeral Commission renamed itself, before the funeral, the **Commission for the Immortalization of the Memory of Vladimir Ulyanov Lenin**. **[A]**
- *Corrections:* the **initial** embalming was by **Alexei Abrikosov**, **Vorobiev and Zbarsky** developing the long-term method afterwards; and on maintenance, the pool asserted "roughly every eighteen months" while en.wikipedia says glycerol and potassium acetate **yearly** and ru.wikipedia gives no schedule. The file states a schedule and declines the number.
- **Shchusev**: wooden mausoleum in days, larger wooden one in spring, permanent granite/labradorite/porphyry structure **October 1930**. **[A]**
- **Mausoleum closed 2025 for repair said to run to June 2027**, body reportedly undisturbed. **[R]** Reuters (10 June 2025) **could not be fetched**; hedged in the last stop.
- **Leninopad**: 2,000+ monuments felled in western Ukraine in the 1990s, 600+ more 2005-08, 552 in 2013-14; the Kyiv statue falls **8 December 2013**; decommunization law passed 9 April, signed 15 May 2015; 1,320 dismantled by January 2017. **[A]**

## 11. Contradictions

Left open: (1) marriage date, 10 vs 22 July 1898, probably calendar, but no source says which; (2) the origin of the name "Lenin", rival explanations, none decisive; (3) the mausoleum re-treatment interval, yearly vs eighteen months, number declined. Adjudicated and marked as adjudications: (4) the plant name of 30 August 1918, Michelson over Hammer and Sickle; (5) the August 1914 arrest, reconciled as Poronin raid then Nowy Targ detention; (6) the law degree, May 1890 permission vs November 1891 completion. Two quotation findings:

7. **A misattribution the pool caught on itself.** "Between capitalist and communist society lies the period of the revolutionary transformation of the one into the other" is verbatim in *The State and Revolution* ch. 5, but the page reads "Marx continued:" immediately before it. **It is Marx's sentence, from the *Critique of the Gotha Programme*, quoted by Lenin.** Not used as a Lenin quote anywhere, on the same rule that had already excluded "From each according to his ability."
8. **"Politics begins where the masses are".** Dropped to [R] by the first verification pass, located and raised to [A] by the second, in the **Political Report of the Central Committee, 7 March 1918, Extraordinary Seventh Congress of the R.C.P.(B.)**, not the 1919 Eighth Congress report (checked, absent) and not *"Left-Wing" Communism*. The MIA wording differs from the paraphrase in circulation and is used verbatim, plural verb and all.
