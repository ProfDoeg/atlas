# El Inca Garcilaso de la Vega (1539-1616): research report

*Slug: `el_inca_garcilaso_de_la_vega`. Born Gómez Suárez de Figueroa at Cusco; died at Córdoba. Translator of Leon Hebreo, historian of Soto's Florida, author of the Comentarios Reales de los Incas.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Calendar.** The journey is marked `julian`. Spain went Gregorian in October 1582, so his Peruvian and military years are Julian and his Córdoba years are not. Dates are reported as the sources give them, unconverted. An unresolved seam, noted rather than fixed.

---

## 1. Cusco, 1539-1560

**Birth.** Cusco, 12 April 1539 **[A: es.wikipedia; RAH Diccionario Biográfico Electrónico]**. Father: captain **Sebastián Garcilaso de la Vega y Vargas**, conquistador **[A]**. Mother: **Palla Chimpu Ocllo**, baptized **Isabel Suárez Chimpu Ocllo** **[A]**.

**The mother's lineage is the worst-transmitted fact in the file. Four readings conflict:** (1) Garcilaso's own Comentarios Reales makes her the **daughter of Huallpa Tupac Inca Yupanqui**, a legitimate son of **Tupac Inca Yupanqui** and a **brother of Huayna Capac**, so granddaughter of Tupac Inca Yupanqui and **niece** of Huayna Capac **[A]**; (2) es.wikipedia, "Inca Garcilaso de la Vega": "bisnieta de Tupac Yupanqui y nieta de Huayna Capac"; (3) es.wikipedia, "Chimpu Ocllo": daughter of **Tupac Hualpa**, the 1533 puppet Inca, a different man from Huallpa Tupac Inca Yupanqui; (4) the research pool contradicted itself internally, granddaughter of Huayna Capac in one entry and his niece in another.

**Adopted:** the first reading, on the authority of Garcilaso's own text, the earliest witness with a claim to family knowledge. The variants stand recorded.

**Birth house.** The Casa del Inca Garcilaso, now the **Museo Histórico Regional del Cusco**, at **Calle Garcilaso con Heladeros**, **-13.5176, -71.9804** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Identifying the surviving building with his actual childhood house is a **traditional attribution [R]**; the address is **[A]**. The pool put the house "near the Plazoleta de las Nazarenas", a different square about 500 m northeast. Not used.

**Coordinate note.** The atlas disagrees with itself on a generic Cusco pin: -13.517/-71.9785 in `pizarro.journey.json`, -13.5319/-71.9675 in `blas_valera` and `guaman_poma_de_ayala`, the latter about 2.3 km southeast of the historic centre. **Flagged, not resolved.** These stops use site-specific pins instead.

**Baptism.** Baptized **Gómez Suárez de Figueroa** **[A]**. The place is a problem. The pool put the rite "in the young cathedral rising on the Inca plaza" and closed "the font already stands", but **Cusco Cathedral was not begun until 1560 [A: es.wikipedia, 'Catedral del Cusco']**, after he had sailed. In 1539 the plaza church was the provisional **Iglesia del Triunfo**. The journey places the baptism there and tags it **[R]**: no baptismal register is cited anywhere, and the only authority offered was "local parish tradition". The **Kiswarkancha / Viracocha Inca palace** site identification is **[A]**.

**Household.** Quechua is his first language; he is raised among his mother's kin, including **Paullu Inca** and **Alonso Tito Atauchi** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. As a boy he listens to amautas and elderly kinsmen, including surviving **quipucamayocs**, recite imperial history **[R, on his own repeated testimony]**, and he names this the primary source of the Comentarios.

**Schooling, c.1548.** The **tutor's name is contested**: **Juan de Alcobaza** (es.wikipedia) versus **Juan de Cuéllar**, cathedral canon (en.wikipedia). **Not resolved**; the campa names both. Fellow pupils included the children of Francisco and Gonzalo Pizarro **[A]**.

**Where the famous scene happens.** The pool sited the uncle's rebuke and the turn to the quipu at **Colcampata**, an inference that would be [R]. **Garcilaso sets it at his mother's house** (Libro I, cap. XV). The Colcampata stop was dropped and the scene placed there. (Colcampata sits at roughly -13.5113, -71.9772; the pool's -13.5133/-71.98 was about 300 m off.)

**Charcas, c.1550 [R: es.wikipedia; Miró Quesada 1948]**. Undated, undocumented, repeated from inference. Kept as a stop with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

**Hernández Girón, 1553 [A: es.wikipedia]**: he witnesses the rebellion and helps his father escape it. **Secretary, 1554 [A: en.wikipedia]**: to his father as corregidor. **Father's death, 1559 [A: en. and es.wikipedia]**: lands at **Paucartambo** and **4,000 pesos** for study in Spain. No day given anywhere.

## 2. The crossing, January 1560

Embarkation at **Callao, 20 January 1560 [A: es.wikipedia gives the day; en.wikipedia only the year]**. Route: Callao, near-shipwreck off **Isla Gorgona**, the **Panama isthmus**, **Cartagena**, Iberia **[A for the route as a whole: es.wikipedia]**.

The individual legs are **[R]**, placed by the ordinary pattern of the 1560 Indies fleets and not by any surviving letter; he never wrote about the isthmus. Two pool coordinates were wrong: **Panamá Viejo** (8.9714/-79.4831, about 4 km south of the site and offshore of the ruins) corrected to **9.0072, -79.4816**; **Nombre de Dios** (9.5833/-79.8333, roughly 40 km west in open water) corrected to **9.5829, -79.4703** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Callao** is inherited byte-identical from `blas_valera.journey.json` at **-12.0566, -77.1181**, already canonical there (Valera ships from the same port in 1592); the pool offered -12.0553/-77.1181. **Havana and the Azores** appear in the pool as pure route reconstruction, sourced only to "standard Indies fleet route", and were **dropped rather than pinned**.

**The landfall is contested.** es.wikipedia brings him in at **Lisbon**; other traditions land him at **Sanlúcar de Barrameda**, the customary Indies port, where `blas_valera.journey.json` also lands Valera in 1595. **Unsettled by the biographers and unsettled here.** The journey uses the Lisbon pin with the contradiction in `date_confidence`.

## 3. Spain, 1560-1569

**Seville, 1560-61 [A]**. Latin study under **Pedro Sánchez de Herrera** is **[R], downgraded from the pool's [A]**: en.wikipedia hedges it as possible, the pool's own prose said "he may study Latin here", and es.wikipedia does not mention the man. Coordinate corrected from 37.386/-5.9926 to **37.3891, -5.9845**.

**Montilla, from 1561**, under **Alonso de Vargas [A]**, his **paternal uncle** ("su tío Alonso de Vargas", es.wikipedia), **not his father's cousin**. The pool's geography entry said cousin while its own chronology entry said uncle; resolved to the source wording.

**Madrid, 1561-63.** He petitions for his father's unpaid wages and for recognition. **The petition is denied [R: es.wikipedia; RAH]**. Later biographers attribute the refusal to his father's conduct in the civil wars; the campa gives both readings without choosing. **The name, c.1563 [A: es.wikipedia]**: Gómez Suárez de Figueroa is dropped for **Garcilaso de la Vega**, later prefixed **Inca**. No dated act survives; the year is conventional.

## 4. The Alpujarras, 1569-1571

Service under **Don Juan de Austria**, rank of **captain [A]**. **Year of entry contested**: es.wikipedia dates the campaign service to 1569, en.wikipedia dates his entry to 1570. **Flagged**; the journey uses 1569.

**Granada.** The pool labelled the stop "Granada (Alhambra, war headquarters)" but gave 37.1773/-3.5986, which is **Granada city centre, not the Alhambra** (37.1761/-3.5881, about 0.9 km east). Retitled Granada rather than keep a label the pin does not support.

**Galera**, three corrections. **Coordinate**: the pool gave 37.4667/-2.8333, roughly 40 km southwest in empty country toward Baza; **Galera is at 37.7431, -2.5514 [A: es.wikipedia]**. **Tag**: his **personal presence at the siege is undocumented [R, downgraded from the pool's A]**, since es.wikipedia's Garcilaso article names no engagement, only the campaign, and the campa says so. **Chronology**: the siege did **not** end the main phase of the war, Galera falling 10 February 1570, **Serón** in March, with the rebellion running into spring **1571 [A]**.

**Órgiva** is **[R]**: campaign geography, no muster roll.

## 5. Montilla and Córdoba, 1570-1609

**Two deaths, not one.** The pool collapsed both into 1571; es.wikipedia gives a window, "Entre 1570 y 1571". Separated: **Alonso de Vargas dies 1570 [A]**; **Isabel Chimpu Ocllo dies 1571 in Cusco [A: es.wikipedia, 'Chimpu Ocllo']**.

**Luisa Ponce, 1586.** Year confirmed **[A]**, relationship wrong in the pool: **an aunt by marriage, the widow of Alonso de Vargas**, not a blood paternal aunt.

**Humanist circles, c.1580.** Contact with **Luis de Góngora** and, "by some accounts", **Miguel de Cervantes**, is **[R: es.wikipedia]**. Only Góngora appears in the campas. **The Cervantes claim is left out entirely: "by some accounts" is the whole of the sourcing. A gap.**

**Leon Hebreo.** The **Dialoghi d'amore** translation completed at Montilla **1586 [A]**; printed at Madrid **1590** as *La traduzion del indio*, signed "el Indio" **[A]**. That it is the first work of major literary value produced in Europe by an American-born author is **[R]**, a judgement rather than a fact.

**Children.** Thin and partly contradictory.

- A **first son**, name and fate uncertain, possibly died young, c.1570 **[R: en.wikipedia]**. No stop: nothing to say beyond his possible existence.
- A son by **Beatriz de Vega**, household servant, c.1590. **The surname is not settled**: en.wikipedia gives **Diego de Vargas**, es.wikipedia **Diego de Vega**. **[A]** that he existed, copied the Comentarios manuscript, and was living in **1651**; **[R]** on the surname. The campa names both.
- **Admiral Lope de Vega**, possibly a son, possibly lost with Mendaña in 1595 **[R: en.wikipedia]**. The pool pinned this at -9.6/160.2, **Guadalcanal, which the 1595 voyage never reached**: that second voyage made the Marquesas and then **Santa Cruz (Nendo)**, where Mendaña died in October 1595 without relocating the Solomons. Corrected to approximately **-10.7, 166.0**, the paternity flagged as undocumented inside the campa.

**Córdoba, 1591 [A]**: permanent relocation. **Genealogía de Garci Pérez de Vargas, 1596 [A]**, unpublished until the twentieth century.

**Blas Valera's papers, c.1600. The strongest interlock in the atlas.** `blas_valera.journey.json` states that three years after Valera's death, Father **Pedro Maldonado de Saavedra** delivers the rescued fragments to "Inca Garcilaso de la Vega at Córdoba". Garcilaso quotes Valera by name and at length, saying outright that he translates him from the Latin **[A: Comentarios Reales, Libro I cap. VI, Libro V caps. XI-XIV]**. Canonical pin **37.8882, -4.7794** inherited byte-identical.

**La Florida del Inca**, Lisbon, **Crasbeeck, 1605 [A: title page]**, built on the testimony of **Gonzalo Silvestre**, named in its own prologue **[A]**. **Comentarios Reales**, first part, Lisbon, Crasbeeck, **1609 [A]**; canonical pin **38.7223, -9.1393** inherited byte-identical from `blas_valera.journey.json`, which already fixes this printing as an atlas event.

## 6. Death, burial, second part

**The chapel.** Purchase of the **Capilla de las Ánimas del Purgatorio** in the Mezquita-Catedral, **1612 [A]**. **The second part**, printed later as the **Historia General del Perú**, is dated by es.wikipedia to **c.1613**, not 1612; the pool's 1612 looks like a conflation with the chapel purchase. **Corrected to c.1613.**

**A pool assertion that is false, and was cut.** The pool said the Historia General "sits unfinished in manuscript at his death, its account of the conquest's violence too pointed to have found a printer in his lifetime". es.wikipedia dates completion to c.1613, records that it passed licence and approval, was already at press, and that a rare surviving issue carries the date **1616**. The printing outlived him, not the manuscript, and the motive about the printer is **invented**.

**The death date is contested, and the strongest witness is the less-cited one.** The **tomb inscription** reads **"Falleció a 22 de abril de 1616"** (quoted in full on es.wikipedia): a physical epitaph, the oldest available witness. **en.wikipedia gives 23 April**, the conventional date, and the day tradition attaches, inaccurately, to Cervantes and Shakespeare; **es.wikipedia's prose gives a 22-24 April range**. The journey **leads with 22 April on the epitaph's authority**, recording 23 April as the alternative in `date_confidence` and in the campa. **Age 77 holds on either reading.**

**The burial day is a gap.** No reachable source gives it; the burial stop is dated 1616-04-23 with `date_confidence` saying plainly that the day is unrecorded.

**Historia General del Perú**, Córdoba, **widow of Andrés de Barrera, 1617 [A: title page]**.

## 7. Afterlife

- **Paul Rycaut's English translation**, *The Royal Commentaries of Peru*, London. **Sources disagree**: en.wikipedia's "Paul Rycaut" gives **1688**, its "Royal Commentaries of the Incas" gives **1685**. 1688 preferred, because the 1685 figure sits in the same article that carries a demonstrably wrong date for the Peruvian ban. **Disagreement recorded in `date_confidence`, not erased.**
- **The ban, 1782 [A].** After the **Túpac Amaru II rebellion of 1780-81**, Charles III restricts and then bans circulation of the Comentarios in the viceroyalty. The pool's **1758** for the rising is wrong; flagged, not quietly fixed.
- **1918 [A]**: a Peruvian edition of the second part, after more than a century without an American reprint. No stop: no city is given.
- **Universidad Inca Garcilaso de la Vega, Lima. Founding date contested**: es.wikipedia **21 December 1964**, en.wikipedia **1965**. The journey uses 1964-12-21 with the disagreement flagged. **Coordinate corrected**: the pool's -12.0797/-76.986 sits about 5 km east of the university. The rectorate is on Av. Arequipa 1841, roughly -12.0797/-77.0330; es.wikipedia gives **-12.0828, -77.1046**, which the journey uses. Either is defensible; -76.986 is not.
- **Statues**: Rome, by the Villa Borghese, **1967 [A]**; Buenos Aires, Plaza República del Perú, **1973 [A]**.
- **The ashes, 25 November 1978 [A]**: Juan Carlos I presides over the return of a portion of the remains to **Cusco Cathedral**; the rest stays in Córdoba. **The division has been disputed since, and no reachable source states how much ash was transferred. A gap.**
- **Twentieth-century indigenismo** adopts him as the founding figure of Andean mestizaje, a role he never claimed in those terms **[R: general historiography]**. Kept out of the campas: a reading of him, not an event in his life.

## 8. Quotations

**Verified as supplied [A]**: Libro I caps. I, XV and XIX, the prologue's glossator declaration, the Advertencias note on Quechua accentuation, the 1617 dedication, and the tomb epitaph.

**Corrected before use:**

- **Prologue, "El autor al lector" [A]**, one OCR slip fixed: the pool read "que la que **basta** ahora los escritores han dado"; the text reads **"hasta ahora"**. The odd-looking "que lo he notado" is correct as printed.
- **Libro I, cap. XV, the boy listening. HALF THE POOL'S VERSION WAS FABRICATED.** The pool gave "...donde ellos estaban, **y les escuchaba con la atencion que despues acá tengo puesta en escribir en esta historia**." The real text ends **"y me holgaba de las oir, como huelgan los tales de oir fábulas."** The supplied ending returns zero hits in Wikisource full-text search and looks like a conflation with the nearby "ninguna con la atencion que entónces", from another sentence. **The fabricated clause was used nowhere.** The real ending is paraphrased in the campa.
- **"Yo protesto decir llanamente la relación que mamé en la leche..."**: the pool flagged it **[R]** and put it in the prologue. **Both wrong. Upgraded to [A]**, relocated to **Libro I, cap. XIX**. The pool's ending "pedida a los proprios Incas" returns zero hits; the real reading is **"pedida a los propios míos"**. Quoted on through the promise of impartiality, which is the point of the sentence.

**About him, not by him.** **John Hemming's verdict** on Garcilaso's reliability **[A: The Conquest of the Incas, 1993 ed., via en.wikipedia]** sits at the stop about his interviewing method, as a counterweight to the register of the rest of the file.

## 9. Interlocks named in the campas

Two travellers are named, each with a direct relation to Garcilaso. Shared geography was not treated as a reason to name anyone.

- **`blas_valera`**, at the Córdoba 1600 stop. Documentary and direct: Valera's salvaged manuscripts are delivered to Garcilaso, who then quotes Valera by name in the Comentarios and states that he translates him from the Latin. `blas_valera.journey.json` names Garcilaso outright and pins both the delivery and the 1609 printing.
- **`pizarro`**, at the Cusco schoolroom (he studied alongside the children of Francisco and Gonzalo Pizarro), at Lima and Isla Gorgona, and at the Córdoba 1613 stop, where he completes a history of the conquest and of Pizarro's generation. The relation runs both biographically and authorially.

**Deliberately not named**, though the interlock lens surfaced them: **`guaman_poma_de_ayala`**, a contemporary on the same material, with no evidence either man knew of or read the other, so naming him would be co-location rather than relation; **`cecilia_vicuna`**, at two removes through Guaman Poma's quipu thread; **`giovanni_pico_della_mirandola`**, a shared Neoplatonist current (Pico to Ficino to Leone Ebreo to the translation) that the pool itself tags [R] with no direct textual link.
