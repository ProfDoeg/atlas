# Rivest, Shamir, Adleman (b. 1945, 1947, 1952): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-09 for the Atlas of Journeys. Three living subjects travelling as one entry: no death dates, no tombs, so the "afterlife" material covers monuments to the work. Second half of a pair; the atlas already holds `diffie`, `hellman`, and `ralph_merkle`, who wrote the other founding public-key patent in the same year.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, single-source self-report, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason; gaps are stated as gaps.

**Caution.** **The verified pool arrived carrying fourteen corrections against its own gathered material**: five wrong or fabricated coordinates, one award given to the wrong man, two publication places predating the author's arrival there, one misquoted citation, one conflated document, one quotation dated three years wrong, two inverted tags, one anachronism, one founder who was not a founder. Each is stated below where it bites. The founding story itself, examined in section 4, is tradition rather than record.

## 1. Three origins, 1945 to 1973

**Adleman**, born San Francisco 31 December 1945 to a bank teller and an appliance salesman **[A: ACM; MacTutor; Wikipedia]**; entered Berkeley intending chemistry, changed to mathematics, B.A. 1968 **[A]**. **Rivest**, born Schenectady 6 May 1947 **[A: MIT CSAIL; Britannica]**; raised in Niskayuna, high school 1965 **[A: History Tools, citing an MIT oral history]**; B.A. mathematics, Yale, 1969 **[A: ACM]**. **Shamir**, born Tel Aviv 6 July 1952 **[A: ACM; Britannica]**; B.Sc. mathematics, Tel Aviv University, 1973 **[A]**.

*Coordinate corrected.* The pool pinned Niskayuna at 42.8119, -73.8494, about 3 km off; Wikipedia gives 42.80028, -73.88083, and the school on Balltown Road is near 42.806, -73.856.

*Honest gap, all three.* No source reached names a teacher, a class, a book, or a formative incident for any of the three before university. Three childhoods, no detail.

*Interlock, Berkeley.* Adleman is there 1964 to 1968 and again to 1976; **Ralph Merkle** is an undergraduate 1970 to 1974 and writes the CS 244 key-distribution proposal in 1974 **[A: `ralph_merkle.journey.json`]**. Any overlap in a room is **[R]** and is not claimed. The canonical pin is inherited; the pool's Evans Hall alternative (37.8740, -122.2586) is not used.

## 2. The separate trainings, 1973 to 1977

**Rivest, Stanford doctorate, advisor Robert W. Floyd.** *Contradiction flagged, not resolved.* Rivest's own CHM oral history has the interviewer ask "you graduated from Stanford in '73?" and Rivest answer "73, yeah" **[A]**; Wikipedia and Britannica give **1974** **[A]**. The journey dates the stop 1973 and prints the disagreement in `date_confidence`.

**Rivest, INRIA at Rocquencourt, 1973 to 1974** **[R: History Tools only, a secondary compiler]**. *Honest gap:* no source says what he worked on there, with whom, or whether anything came of it. **He joins the MIT EECS faculty in 1974** **[A: MIT CSAIL]** and is still there. **Shamir, M.Sc. Weizmann 1975, Ph.D. 1977, advisor Zohar Manna** **[A: ACM; Britannica; Wikipedia]**. **Adleman, Ph.D. Berkeley 1976, advisor Manuel Blum; MIT mathematics 1976 to 1980** **[A: ACM]**.

Those cannot all be sequential, and the constraint that breaks them is documentary: **Shamir co-authors MIT/LCS/TM-82 in April 1977** **[A]**. The pool tagged Warwick **[A]** while conceding that "some accounts place this year immediately before Weizmann rather than after." An entry whose own prose says the sources conflict cannot carry an **[A]**: **downgraded to [R]**. The stop is kept at 52.3793, -1.5610, and the campa says plainly that no ordering reached here works.

## 3. New Directions, and the winter of broken functions, 1976

**Diffie and Hellman, "New Directions in Cryptography," *IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory* IT-22(6), November 1976** **[A: `hellman.journey.json`]**: poses the concept, supplies no construction.

**The RSA team's motivation by that paper is attested from the horse's mouth**: the 1978 paper says in its own first section, "Their article motivated our research, since they presented the concept but not any practical implementation of such a system" **[A: CACM 21(2), p. 121, verified in the PDF at people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/Rsapaper.pdf]**. The pool tagged this causal link **[R]**; **upgraded to [A]**.

**The division of labour.** Rivest and Shamir propose candidate one-way functions; Adleman breaks them **[A: ACM biographies of all three]**. The repeated figure of **forty-two broken proposals** is **[R]**: it appears in secondary retellings (Encryption Consulting, Splunk) and in no primary account, and the campa labels it unconfirmed.

*Interlock, MIT.* The atlas has been to this pin three times: **Whitfield Diffie**'s bachelor's degree 1961 to 1965; **Martin Hellman** as assistant professor 1969 to 1971, receiving the Shannon paper from Peter Elias; **Robert Oppenheimer** on the same stretch of Cambridge four decades earlier **[A]**. **Claude Shannon** and **Gauss** are named travellers already, and the modular exponentiation both public-key traditions run on is Gauss's **[A for their presence; R for any conscious debt]**.

## 4. April 1977: the documented month, the undocumented night

**Attested [A].** In April 1977, at the MIT Laboratory for Computer Science, the three have a working trapdoor one-way function built on the difficulty of factoring a product of two large primes. Evidence: **MIT/LCS/TM-82**, issued that month from 545 Technology Square, and the received stamp of **4 April 1977** on the journal version, revised 1 September 1977 **[A: the paper itself]**.

**Tradition [R].** The seder at a student's house; the wine; midnight; Rivest sleepless on the couch with a mathematics textbook; the dawn call to Adleman; most of a paper by daybreak. The pool tagged this **[A]** while its own source note said the specifics were "flagged R," and the journey **splits it** into one attested April stop and two reconstructed narrative stops.

*Honest gap, twice.* **The seder house is not identified in any source reached, and neither is Rivest's April 1977 residence.** Both stops carry the **canonical MIT coordinate as an acknowledged stand-in**, disclosed in `date_confidence`; no address was invented. This follows `hellman.journey.json`, where a fabricated home coordinate for Joseph Meyer was refused and the Fort Meade pin substituted.

**Adleman's authorship [A, upgraded]** from the pool's **[R]**, both sentences confirmed verbatim on the Goodreads *The Code Book* quotes index: "I told Ron to take my name off the paper," recalls Adleman. "I told him that it was his invention, not mine. But Ron refused and we got into a discussion about it." The passage continues that Adleman proposed being listed third, which is why the initials read R, S, A.

**The document that was mischaracterised.** The pool called the April 1977 write-up a report "submitted to the Office of Naval Research." **Wrong.** It is an MIT technical memorandum, mailed free to anyone who sent Rivest a self-addressed envelope; ONR funded the research and did not receive the report **[A]**. The pool also ran two titles together: the memo is "On Digital Signatures and Public-Key Crypto-Systems," the 1978 article "A Method for Obtaining Digital Signatures and Public-Key Cryptosystems" **[A]**. Separate stops, separate titles.

## 5. Publication, and two patents in one autumn, 1977 to 1978

**Gardner's column, *Scientific American*, August 1977** **[A: the column PDF]**. First public description; RSA-129 printed; $100 prize. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool put *Scientific American* at 40.7484, -73.9857, the **Empire State Building**; the offices were at 415 Madison Avenue, approximately **40.7566, -73.9752**. The same coordinate appeared again labelled "ACM headquarters," and is not used there either.

**MIT files US 4,405,829, 14 December 1977; granted 20 September 1983** **[A: patent record at cr.yp.to]**. Because Gardner published first, the invention was public before filing and the patent has no standing outside the United States **[A: Wikipedia, "RSA (cryptosystem)"]**. *Interlock:* Stanford filed the Diffie-Hellman-Merkle application, **US 4,200,770, on 6 September 1977** **[A: `hellman.journey.json`]**, ninety-nine days earlier.

*Interlock, Fort Meade and Cornell, and their limits.* **Joseph A. Meyer's July 1977 letter** warning that publishing cryptography could carry prison is already an atlas event **[A: `diffie`, `hellman`]**; that it was aimed at or read by the RSA team is **[R]**, and the campa claims only that letter and column belong to one summer and one objection. Cornell is softer: Hellman reads Merkle's and Pohlig's papers at Ithaca in October 1977 **[A]**, but the RSA result's circulation through that community is **[R]**, the weakest inference here, and the campa says no source places anyone from Cambridge there.

## 6. The dispersal, 1979 to 1983

**Shamir, "How to Share a Secret," CACM 22(11), pp. 612 to 613, November 1979** **[A]**; Blakley's independent geometric equivalent the same year **[A]**. *Place corrected, twice:* both the pool's chronology and afterlife lenses filed this at the **Weizmann Institute**, which Shamir does not join until **1980**, as the same chronology states two entries later. He wrote and published it **at MIT** **[A]**, and the journey pins it at 545 Technology Square.

**Adleman to USC 1980**, thirty-six years, eventually Henry Salvatori Professor of Computer Science and Professor of Molecular Biology at once **[A: ACM; USC]**. **Shamir to the Weizmann Institute 1980**, permanently **[A]**. *Coordinate harmonised:* the pool carried two Weizmann values, 31.9046, 34.8078 and 31.9074, 34.8092 (Wikipedia's infobox), 350 m apart; the journey uses the second throughout.

**RSA Data Security incorporated 1982**, reportedly out of Adleman's apartment **[A for the founding; R for the apartment, whose city no source specifies]**. *Correction:* the pool named **Jim Bidzos as a co-founder**. He was not; Bidzos joined **1 February 1986** as chief executive with the company near bankruptcy, and created the RSA Conference in 1991 **[A]**. *Interlock:* the atlas already holds RSA Data Security at Redwood City (`hellman.journey.json`, "the royalty dispute"), where the company credits Diffie, Hellman, and Merkle as the inventors of public-key cryptography while selling the RSA implementation. That pin is inherited rather than guessing at the apartment's city.

**Shamir breaks the Merkle-Hellman knapsack, 1982** **[A: Odlyzko on knapsack cryptosystems]**, an atlas event already, at Stanford, in `hellman.journey.json`. The Chicago FOCS venue that November is **[R]** and unverified.

**The computer virus named, 10 November 1983** **[A: Fred Cohen, "Computer Viruses: Theory and Experiments"; Adleman's 2017 WeLiveSecurity interview]**. *Place corrected:* the pool's place field read "Los Angeles (Cohen's demonstration at Lehigh University, Pennsylvania)," conflating two institutions. Cohen was **Adleman's doctoral student at USC** and the seminar was there; both men say so.

## 7. The cipher trade, 1986 to 2000

- **Fiat and Shamir, "How to Prove Yourself," CRYPTO '86** **[A: proceedings]**; the August month is **[R]**, inferred from the conference's schedule. **Biham and Shamir, differential cryptanalysis, CRYPTO '90** **[A: proceedings; *J. Cryptology* 1991]**. **Rivest designs RC4, 1987**, trade secret until an anonymous 1994 posting leaks the source **[A: Wikipedia, "RC4"]**.
- **First RSA Conference 1991** **[A]**; **RSA Factoring Challenge 1991 to 2007**, largest prizes unclaimed **[A]**. *Place corrected:* the pool put the challenge at Bedford, Massachusetts, which becomes headquarters only around 1999 to 2001; RSA was California-based for most of that span, so the journey attaches the challenge to the 1991 San Francisco stop.
- **Pius XI Gold Medal 1992.** *Wrong person, corrected:* the pool gave it to **Rivest**, in an entry whose own text said it was "an award to Shamir specifically." It went to **Adi Shamir** **[A: Wikipedia's Shamir article lists it; Rivest's does not]**.
- **RSA-129 factored, completed 26 April 1994** **[A]**, by roughly **1,600 computers operated by more than 600 volunteers**, coordinated by Atkins, Graff, Lenstra, and Leyland. *Numbers corrected:* the pool had "about 600 computers" and Lenstra as sole lead. Plaintext: **THE MAGIC WORDS ARE SQUEAMISH OSSIFRAGE** **[A]**. The "forty quadrillion years" estimate is **[R]**.
- **Adleman, "Molecular Computation of Solutions to Combinatorial Problems," *Science* 266, 11 November 1994** **[A]**, founding DNA computing.
- **GCHQ declassifies Clifford Cocks's 1973 note, 1997.** *Tag inverted, corrected:* the pool had **[R]** on the reasoning that Cocks's priority is "treated in the literature as tradition/legend." It is not; Wikipedia's RSA article states the 1973 document plainly and observes that had it been known the US patent would have been invalid. **Upgraded to [A]**.
- **RSA Security releases the patent into the public domain 6 September 2000**, a fortnight before its expiry **[A: CNN, 7 September 2000]**.

## 8. The award, and the long after

**2002 A. M. Turing Award, jointly** **[A: awards.acm.org]**. *Citation corrected:* the official wording is **"For their ingenious contribution for making public-key cryptography useful in practice."** The pool's afterlife lens misquoted it as "for their seminal contributions to the theory and practical application of public-key cryptography"; the chronology lens had it right. *Contradiction flagged, not resolved:* AMS *Notices*, July 2003, puts the presentation at the ACM Awards Banquet in **June 2003**, while the contemporaneous report of Shamir's Turing lecture dates the lecture **June 2002**. Both stand in `date_confidence`; the venue is unconfirmed and the pin is the canonical San Francisco banquet coordinate.

**Shamir's three laws [A, upgraded].** The pool had "Cryptography is typically bypassed, not penetrated" at **[R]** as an untraceable aggregator attribution. It is traceable: the third of three laws in Shamir's Turing lecture, verbatim in the financialcryptography.com report, alongside "Absolutely secure systems do not exist" and "To halve your vulnerability, you have to double your expenditure." That report is not an ACM transcript, and the `quote_source` says so.

**Later honours and work.** Rivest: Kanellakis Award 1996, Marconi Prize 2007, **MIT Institute Professor announced June 2015** **[A: MIT CSAIL]**; CSAIL moved to the Stata Center in 2004 **[A]**. *Anachronism corrected:* the pool had Rivest "continuing as an MIT Institute Professor after" the 2004 move, eleven years before he held the rank. Shamir: TWIRL 2003, Israel Prize 2008, Japan Prize 2017 **[A]**. Adleman: emeritus, USC, 2016 **[A]**. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool put the National Theatre of Japan at 35.6852, 139.7528, inside the Imperial Palace grounds; it is at 4-1 Hayabusacho, Chiyoda, **35.6817, 139.7433**.

**National Inventors Hall of Fame, 2018** **[A: invent.org pages for all three]**; a stray snippet dated it 2003. The pool gave the museum as 38.813, -77.048 in one lens and 38.8048, -77.0469 in another; it is at 600 Dulany Street, and the journey inherits the atlas coordinate. The 2018 **ceremony** was in **Washington, D.C.**, not at the museum. *Interlock:* Diffie, Hellman, and Merkle were inducted here in 2011.

**RSA Conference panel, San Francisco, 29 April 2025** **[A]**. *Date corrected by three years:* the pool dated Shamir's cryptocurrency remark **2022**, but Decrypt (decrypt.co/317115) of 30 April 2025 reports a panel held Tuesday 29 April 2025, and The Register covered it the same week.

**Status, August 2026.** All three alive **[A]**: Rivest at MIT, Shamir at the Weizmann Institute, Adleman emeritus at USC. *Honest gap:* **no plaque, marker, room, or preserved house was found for the events of April 1977.** The final stop records the absence as an absence.
