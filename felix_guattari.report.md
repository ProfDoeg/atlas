# Félix Guattari (1930-1992): research report
*2026-08-15. Born Villeneuve-lès-Sablons (Oise), died at the Clinique de La Borde, Cour-Cheverny (Loir-et-Cher). Psychiatrist without a medical degree, militant, co-author with Gilles Deleuze of Capitalisme et schizophrénie.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or secondary-literature recollection not re-verified against a fetched source. Contradictions are flagged, not silently resolved. Where a verification pass caught an error in the research pool, the correction is recorded with both readings visible: the wrong version circulates widely (English Wikipedia in several cases) and a reader will meet it.

---

## 1. Oise, Paris, and the two Oury brothers (1930-1953)

**Birth. [A]** Pierre-Félix Guattari is born 30 March 1930 at Villeneuve-lès-Sablons, in the Oise (EN and FR, "Félix Guattari"). **Correction on the birthplace, twice over. [A]** First, coordinates: French Wikipedia gives the commune at 49.2375 / 2.0775; the pair 49.2833 / 1.9167 in gathered material lands roughly 13 km west-northwest, in open country. Second, character of the place: English Wikipedia's "working-class suburb of northwest Paris" is wrong. French Wikipedia classes Villeneuve-lès-Sablons a *bourg rural* outside any urban unit, about 50 km from Paris and 26 km from Beauvais. The journey file uses the corrected pin and drops the suburb framing.

**Family. [R, and thin]** The claim that his father Louis founded the Monbana chocolate company, and that he had brothers Jean and Paul, appears in neither cited Wikipedia article. Carried here as unsourced and **not** used in the journey file.

**Jean Oury, met in 1945 in Paris. [A]** French Wikipedia's "Jean Oury" states that Oury met Guattari in 1945 in Paris, where Guattari was a pupil of Oury's brother Fernand, the Freinet-tradition pedagogue. **This displaces a widely repeated version [R]** in which the two meet at the Clinique de Saumery around 1953. Oury was *médecin-chef* at Saumery from 1949 and left in 1953 to found La Borde, so Guattari's visits there post-date the 1945 introduction rather than constituting it. Both readings are recorded; the file marks Saumery a visit, not a first meeting, and pins it approximately, no coordinates having been reached.

**Militancy and analysis. [A]** Wikipedia (EN) records teenage Trotskyist activism preceding the psychiatric career; French Wikipedia records PCF membership through his youth and early twenties. No precise dates; the file places these loosely and hedges. He attends the University of Paris and, in the early 1950s, becomes Lacan's analysand and something close to an apprentice, the training analysis running for years (Wikipedia EN).

**Correction on Sainte-Anne, twice over. [A]** Coordinates: French Wikipedia gives the hospital at 48.8286 / 2.3383, not the 48.8236 / 2.3350 that circulates. Seminar dates: Lacan's public seminar ran at Sainte-Anne 1953 to 1963-64, moved to the École normale supérieure in January 1964 and to the Faculté de Droit in 1969; the pool's "1952-1969" is wrong. **Pin decision:** the file inherits the corpus value 48.829 / 2.3396, carried by `artaud.journey.json` and used by `jacques_lacan.journey.json`, which already records three circulating pins without resolving them.

## 2. La Borde (1953-1992)

**Founding. [A]** Oury founds La Borde in the château at Cour-Cheverny on **3 April 1953** with 33 patients; Social Security accreditation follows that June, 29 approved beds (French Wikipedia, "Clinique de La Borde", citing Oury's own account). **Correction. [A]** The repeated "Oury founds La Borde in 1955 and Guattari follows him" conflates two events: 1953 is the founding, 1955 the year Guattari arrives.

**Contradiction, flagged and argued down. [A/R]** English Wikipedia says Guattari joined in 1955; French Wikipedia's *Guattari* article calls the clinic "co-founded with Jean Oury in 1953," while its own article on *the clinic* credits Oury alone. With the 1957 appointment to the administrative direction ("Jean Oury"), the documented sequence is: Oury founds 1953, Guattari arrives 1955, takes the administration 1957. The "co-founder" wording is treated as loose formulation, not a rival account, and the stop's `date_confidence` says so.

**Correction on the coordinates, three times over. [A]** French Wikipedia's "Clinique de La Borde" gives **47.5336 / 1.4628**. Two wrong pairs circulate for the site, 47.4926 / 1.4536 (which sits on Cour-Cheverny village, south of the clinic) and 47.4808 / 1.4674. The corrected value is the one already in `gilles_deleuze.journey.json`, inherited byte-identical at every La Borde stop.

**GTPSI. [A]** He takes part in the Groupe de Travail de Psychothérapie et de Sociothérapie Institutionnelles alongside Oury and François Tosquelles (FR); the Association pour la Psychothérapie Institutionnelle is formally established November 1965 (EN). He dies in the building thirty-seven years after arriving.

## 3. The militant decade (1955-1968)

**La Voie Communiste. [A]** He edits and writes for the Trotskyist-leaning paper across roughly 1955-1965 (EN and FR). No office address is documented; generic Paris pin, flagged. **FGERI, 1965. [A]** He helps found the Fédération des Groupes d'Études et de Recherches Institutionnelles.

**Correction on CERFI and *Recherches*. [A]** (i) CERFI was founded in **1967** by Guattari and dissolved 1987 (French Wikipedia's dedicated article); English Wikipedia's 1970 and the 1965 given elsewhere are unsupported, 1965 belonging to FGERI. (ii) *Recherches* first appeared in **1966**, ran 49 issues to 1982-83, and is CERFI's organ rather than FGERI's.

**OSARLA, 1967. [A]** He is named a founder of this Latin America solidarity body (EN). Nothing further reached. **May 1968. [A]** He takes part, meets Daniel Cohn-Bendit, Jean-Jacques Lebel and Julian Beck, and joins the Mouvement du 22 Mars. **Correction on coordinates:** French Wikipedia gives Université Paris-Nanterre at 48.90394 / 2.21378; the circulating 48.8926 / 2.2136 falls about 1.3 km south.

## 4. The meeting with Deleuze (1969), and where it happened

The single most contested point in the file, contested in three directions at once.

- **[A]** Deleuze is appointed to the philosophy department of the Centre universitaire expérimental de Vincennes in 1969, Foucault having suggested the hiring (Stanford Encyclopedia; Wikipedia EN).
- **[A]** Guattari held **no post at Vincennes** in 1969 and the two never ran joint seminars there. The pool's "a hub for their joint seminars" is wrong and is dropped.
- **[R]** The Vincennes-meeting narrative, standard in English accounts, is therefore demoted from attested to tradition: Dosse's dual biography places the first meeting away from campus.
- **[R]** The pool then over-corrected, putting the first sustained conversation at Deleuze's **Paris apartment**. Dosse places the June 1969 meeting at Deleuze's country house in the **Limousin**, where he was convalescing after his 1968 lung surgery. No primary Dosse source could be reached.
- **[A]** The introduction through the La Borde psychiatrist **Jean-Pierre Muyard** is well attested.

**Pin decision.** `gilles_deleuze.journey.json` already carries "Paris: the meeting with Felix Guattari" at the placeholder pin 48.8566 / 2.3522, its `date_confidence` naming the conflict outright. The Guattari file inherits that pin byte-identical and the flag with it, rather than choosing a side. Vincennes and Saint-Léonard-de-Noblat each get their own [R] stop, both inheriting the Deleuze pins (48.8434 / 2.4415 and 45.8383 / 1.4914). No source reached gives coordinates for the razed Vincennes campus; the corpus value is used and its unsourced status noted.

## 5. Capitalism and Schizophrenia (1972-1980)

**Anti-Oedipus. [A]** *Capitalisme et schizophrénie 1: L'Anti-Œdipe*, Les Éditions de Minuit, 1972. Minuit's address, 7 rue Bernard-Palissy in the 6th, is confirmed by French Wikipedia as its home since 1951; the pin 48.8531 / 2.3317 is inherited from `gilles_deleuze.journey.json`.

**Correction on Foucault's preface. [A]** It was written for the **1977 American edition** (Viking Penguin), not the 1972 French one, and his much-quoted phrase is a proposed alternative title. The file therefore attaches preface and quotation to a New York stop dated 1977.

**The Recherches trial. [A]** In 1973 Guattari is tried and fined for "outrage to public decency" over the *Trois milliards de pervers* issue of *Recherches*, devoted to homosexuality (Wikipedia EN).

**Kafka, 1975; Rhizome, 1976; Mille Plateaux, 1980. [A]** All Minuit, all jointly signed. **La Révolution moléculaire, 1977. [A]** His own collection, outside the collaboration.

**Schizo-Culture, Columbia, November 1975. [A]** He speaks at Sylvère Lotringer's conference, on a programme with Michel Foucault and R. D. Laing, before more than two thousand attendees; later accounts describe confrontational exchanges between speakers and audience (Wikipedia EN, "Sylvère Lotringer").

## 6. Italy, free radio, and the raids (1977-1981)

**Correction on the 1977 appeal, decisive. [A]** Gathered material dated "The Call of French Intellectuals Against Repression in Italy" to November 1977 and described it as protesting Antonio Negri's imprisonment. Both parts fail. Italian Wikipedia records a *Manifesto contro la repressione* published in **July 1977**, signed by 28 intellectuals including Sartre, Beauvoir, Foucault, Barthes, Sollers, Deleuze and Guattari. And it cannot have protested Negri's imprisonment: **Negri was not arrested until 7 April 1979.** The appeal responded to the killing of Francesco Lorusso in Bologna and the general crackdown. The Negri clause is removed.

**Radio Alice. [A]** Guattari writes the preface to *Radio Alice, radio libre*, the collective account of the Bolognese pirate station shut down by police in March 1977 (French Wikipedia).

**Bologna, September 1977. [R]** His attendance at the international conference against repression is recollection from general secondary literature. No dedicated source page could be located or fetched; attempted URLs returned 404. Recorded as unverified.

**CINEL. [A]** Guattari directs the Centre d'Initiative pour de Nouveaux Espaces de Liberté with Gisèle Donnard, 1977-1981: autonomist solidarity, free radio, the 1978 "Antibrouille" festival, the "ALFREDO" radio conference (French Wikipedia).

**The 1979 searches. [A]** Police search his Paris residence over his support for Charlie Bauer, a former convict he had helped in rehabilitation work; La Borde is searched the same year over a kidnapping case. No residence address is documented. **Radio Tomate and Coluche, 1981. [A]** He co-creates the free radio station and supports the comedian's satirical presidential candidacy (FR).

## 7. Brazil, Negri, Japan (1982-1985)

**Correction on "the winter years." [A]** Gathered material described a quiet stretch of the early 1980s that "biographers call his winter years." Wrong in kind. *Les années d'hiver: 1980-1985* (Bernard Barrault, 1985) is **Guattari's own book title**, naming the political reaction of the period, not a lull in his activity. The years were among his busiest: Radio Tomate (1981), the Brazil tour (1982), Negri into exile (1983), *Les nouveaux espaces de liberté* (1985). The characterization is rewritten in the journey file, not repeated.

**Brazil, September 1982. [R]** He travels with Suely Rolnik, meeting Workers' Party militants and psychiatric reformers during the democratic opening. **Correction on the citation:** the record of the trip is *Micropolítica: Cartografias do Desejo* (Portuguese, 1986), in English as *Molecular Revolution in Brazil*, Semiotext(e), **2008**, not 2007. Because the city-by-city itinerary was not verified against any source fetched this session, São Paulo and Rio are tagged **[R]**, not [A], contrary to the gathered material's own tagging.

**Negri's exile, 1983. [A]** Guattari helps Antonio Negri, freed from Italian custody on claiming parliamentary immunity, into France, with Amnesty International's assistance (Wikipedia EN, "Antonio Negri").

**Correction on Communists Like Us. [A]** The book was written in French as *Les nouveaux espaces de liberté*, Paris, Dominique Bedou, **1985**. *Communists Like Us* is the English edition, Semiotext(e), 1990, published in the United States. Dating a Paris stop to 1990 conflates the two editions; the file dates it 1985.

**Japan, 1985. [R, weak]** No dedicated source page was located or fetched. The stop rests on background knowledge and on the posthumous *Machinic Eros: Writings on Japan* (2015), which anchors a Japanese engagement without dating any trip. Carried as reconstruction, with the gap stated in the stop itself.

## 8. Ecosophy, the last books, the death (1989-1992)

**Cartographies schizoanalytiques, 1989. [A]** **Les trois écologies, 1989. [A]** Published in **Paris by Éditions Galilée**. **Correction:** the pool anchored the publication to La Borde, a place error. The file puts the book in Paris.

**Correction on the ecosophy quotation, the largest in this file. [A]** The much-quoted sentence beginning "Without modifications to the social and material environment, there can be no change in mentalities" is attributed almost everywhere, Wikipedia's ecosophy article included, to *The Three Ecologies* in the Pindar/Sutton translation. It is **not in that book**. The French original (1989) was downloaded from archive.org (item `1989-felix-guattari-les-trois-ecologies`) and the sentence does not occur: no *postuler*, no *milieu social et matériel*. It comes from a later text, "Pour une refondation des pratiques sociales," *Le Monde diplomatique*, October 1992, pp. 26-27 (page live at monde-diplomatique.fr/1992/10/GUATTARI/44749), in English as "Remaking Social Practices," trans. Sophie Thomas, rev. Brian Holmes, in *The Guattari Reader* (Blackwell, 1996). The published wording, verified in the PDF at raumstation.cc, reads "...to social ecology and mental ecology," without the second "to" the Wikipedia variant inserts. The file quotes the published wording and cites *Le Monde diplomatique*, which appeared two months after his death.

**Qu'est-ce que la philosophie?, 1991. [A]** The last book signed jointly, closing twenty-two years. **Chaosmose, 1992. [A]** **Correction:** published in **Paris by Galilée**, not at Cour-Cheverny as the pool had it; only the year was right there.

**Death and burial. [A]** Heart attack at La Borde, 29 August 1992, age 62. Buried at Père-Lachaise, division 42 (the division number given only by the French article). Two pairs circulate for the grave, 48.8619 / 2.3936 and 48.8606 / 2.3934; the file uses the first and notes that the corpus already carries 48.8614 / 2.3931 on `marcel_proust.journey.json` for division 85. A plot difference inside one cemetery, left unresolved.

**Posthumous English collections. [A]** *Chaosophy* (1995) and *Soft Subversions* (1996) are correctly dated. **Correction:** *The Anti-Oedipus Papers* is 2006 in English (Semiotext(e)); 2004 is the French original, *Écrits pour L'Anti-Œdipe*. *Machinic Eros: Writings on Japan* (2015) belongs on the same list.

**Quotation chapter corrections. [A]** Two verbatim Anti-Oedipus passages carried wrong chapter attributions in the pool, corrected against the Hurley/Seem/Lane PDF: "The truth is that sexuality is everywhere..." is ch. 4, "Introduction to Schizoanalysis," p. 293, not ch. 2; "The body without organs is an egg..." is ch. 1, "The Desiring-Machines," p. 19, not ch. 2.

## Contradictions left open

1. **Where Guattari and Deleuze first met, June 1969.** Vincennes campus (English accounts) versus Deleuze's Limousin country house during the convalescence (Dosse). A third version, a Paris apartment, is supported by nothing reached. Not adjudicated; the placeholder Paris pin inherited from the Deleuze file carries the flag.
2. **Guattari's founder status at La Borde.** French Wikipedia's Guattari article says co-founder 1953; its own clinic article credits Oury alone. Argued down above, but the two sentences remain in print and contradict each other.
3. **1955 versus 1957 at La Borde.** English Wikipedia dates his following Oury to 1955; French Wikipedia's Oury article dates the administrative-director invitation to 1957. Compatible, but no source states both.
4. **The Père-Lachaise coordinate.** Three pairs in circulation, two from the pool and one already in the corpus for a different division.
5. **Sainte-Anne coordinates.** Three pairs circulate inside this corpus alone, as `jacques_lacan.journey.json` already records. Inherited, not resolved.

- No street address for the Paris residence raided in 1979, for CERFI, for the *La Voie Communiste* office, or for the school of Fernand Oury. Generic Paris pins, flagged in each stop.
- No coordinates for the Clinique de Saumery, nor for the razed Vincennes campus from any source reached; Saumery is pinned approximately, Vincennes takes the corpus value with its unsourced status recorded.
- The 1982 Brazil itinerary is not verified city by city; São Paulo and Rio are [R]. The 1985 Japan trip is undated and uncorroborated [R]. The September 1977 Bologna attendance is uncorroborated [R].
