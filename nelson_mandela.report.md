# Nelson Mandela: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `nelson_mandela`. Register: national mythology: the canon is true.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a source unreachable this session · **[R coord]** coordinate absent from the verified pool or conceded approximate by it. Bare coordinates come from the pool's geography lens, tagged [A] there.

The pool spans five lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife) which contradict each other repeatedly. Every conflict found is listed; none is adjudicated. Narrative detail lives in the journey campa, not here.

## 3. Defiance, treason, the turn to arms, 1952-1961

**CORRECTION: the Defiance Campaign date.** The campaign **launched 26 June 1952 [A: Wikipedia, "Defiance Campaign"]**. The chronology lens attaches that date to Mandela's election as ANC Transvaal President and Deputy National President; those offices came **later in 1952** (SAHO gives the year only; Wikipedia says October). **[flagged and split]**

**CORRECTION: Marshall Square.** Geography lens: 22 June 1952, tied to a Durban rally. He addressed that rally but was not detained; he was arrested in Johannesburg on the night of **26 June 1952**, the launch date, for a curfew violation, with Sisulu and some fifty others. **[flagged and redated]** -26.2041, 28.0435. Conviction under the Suppression of Communism Act, December 1952: nine-month suspended sentence and a banning order **[A: SAHO]**.

**Kliptown, Congress of the People**, 25-26 June 1955, the Freedom Charter adopted, Mandela present but banned from attending openly **[A: SAHO]** -26.2856, 27.9048.

**CORRECTION: the Treason Trial, two dates merged.** Dawn raids **5 December 1956** (140 taken that day, 156 charged in all); the **Drill Hall** proceedings opened **19 December 1956**, 153 prisoners in a wire cage. The geography lens dates the Drill Hall opening to the arrest date. **[A: Wikipedia, "1956 Treason Trial"; flagged and split]** -26.2023, 28.0436, byte-identical to the canonical Johannesburg pin in `mahatma_gandhi.journey.json` for the 1906 Empire Theatre satyagraha oath; inherited unchanged.

**CORRECTION: the outcome.** Charges were **not dropped**. On **29 March 1961** the court, down to 28 remaining defendants including Mandela, found them **not guilty** and discharged them. Charges were withdrawn against 61 of the original accused in 1958; Mandela was not among them. **[A]**

**MAJOR ERROR: Sharpeville, off by one year.** The massacre was **21 March 1960**, not 1961; 69 killed by the apartheid-era police figure, recent research at 91+ killed and 238+ wounded **[A: Wikipedia, "Sharpeville massacre"]**. The 1961 dating puts the event after the events it caused and contradicts the pool's own Pretoria Local Prison entry. Chain: Sharpeville March 1960, ANC banned April 1960, state of emergency and five months' detention March-August 1960, MK founded 1961. **[flagged and corrected]** Pretoria Local Prison -25.7295, 28.1826. **No Sharpeville coordinate is in the pool**, so none is minted; the massacre is narrated at the prison stop.

Marriage to Nomzamo Winifred Madikizela 1958 **[A]**. **Umkhonto we Sizwe** founded 1961, Mandela first Commander-in-Chief **[A: SAHO]**. **Liliesleaf Farm, Rivonia**, 1961-1962, Mandela living there as a caretaker named David Motsamayi **[A: Liliesleaf Trust]** -26.0439, 28.0536.

## 9. Quotes: verbatim, altered, fabricated

All courtroom quotes come from the **Statement from the Dock, Rivonia Trial, 20 April 1964**, as archived by the United Nations. Three problems in the pool:

1. **Punctuation altered.** The UN text reads *"...serving five years for leaving the country without a permit and for inciting people to go on strike at the end of May 1961."* No comma before "and for inciting". **Corrected.**
2. **Misattribution.** *"The time comes in the life of any nation when there remain only two choices - submit or fight."* Not Mandela's formulation: he reads aloud from the **Manifesto of Umkhonto we Sizwe, 16 December 1961**, which the UN transcript sets in quotation marks. **Not used as a standalone quote.**
3. **Fabricated fusion.** *"I have never been a member of the Communist Party, and I have always regarded myself as an African patriot first and foremost."* **No such sentence exists.** It fuses, with altered wording, *"Although I have never been a member of the Communist Party, I myself have been named under that pernicious Act..."* and, from elsewhere in the statement, *"I have always regarded myself, in the first place, as an African patriot."* **Rejected, not used.** Further: post-1994 archival work (Simons papers, SACP records) indicates Mandela **had** been a Communist Party member around 1960-62, so the courtroom denial is his testimony, not established fact. **[flagged, not resolved]**

Seven passages from the same statement are confirmed verbatim against the UN transcript and carried in the journey file's quote fields, from *"I am the First Accused."* to the closing *"I have cherished the ideal..."* paragraph. Two *Long Walk to Freedom* aphorisms ("no one is born hating another person", "courage was not the absence of fear") are **[R]**: widely quoted, but the exact page text **could not be fetched this session**. Used sparingly, quote_source naming the book without a page.

## 11. Honest gaps

- **1919 against 1926** for the move to Qunu, and the **Pretoria courtroom coordinate** (quotes lens against geography lens, 4 km apart): both unresolved.
- Approximate coordinates, marked in the journey file: **Mqhekezweni, Tyhalarha, Healdtown, Oujda, Pollsmoor, Jabulani Stadium, FNB Stadium**.
- **Dakar**, **Lagos**, the **1995 Rugby World Cup final**, the **White House visits**: attested, given no pin, for want of a usable coordinate or date or by editorial choice.
- The **intelligence tip-off at Howick**: tradition, not documentation.
- Mandela's **Communist Party membership** around 1960-62 is indicated by post-1994 archival work and denied under oath by Mandela himself. Both recorded, neither adopted.
- The **ghostwriter** claim for *Long Walk to Freedom*'s later chapters is Wikipedia quoting The Times of London: reported, not verified. The "21 languages" figure conflates Wikipedia interlanguage links with book translations; rejected, no real count found.
