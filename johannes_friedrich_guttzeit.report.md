# Johannes Friedrich Guttzeit (1853-1935) research report
*2026-08-18. Prussian officer, vegetarian, wandering Naturprediger, pamphleteer of "der volle Mensch". Born Königsberg, died Olching (Neu-Esting) near Munich.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or unverified self-report · contradictions flagged and left open, never adjudicated. Short forms: **DDB** = Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek authority record GND 116936053 · **dewiki** = de.wikipedia "Johannes Friedrich Guttzeit" unless another article is named · **GG-info** / **GG-pdf** = the Gusto-Graeser-circle page `gusto-graeser.info/Diefenbach/guttzeit.html` and Hermann Müller's `gusto-graeser.de` PDF on the 1885 Bruder-Bund.

Standing caution: GG-info and GG-pdf hold the densest Guttzeit biography and are at the same time a sympathetic memorial project with a thesis about the ancestry of Monte Verità. Everything from them is marked. Second is dewiki, thin but disciplined. Beyond those, no monograph, no critical edition, no reprint.

## 1. Origins and the officer's years, 1853-1879

- Born **6 August 1853, Königsberg** **[A: DDB]**, fixed to the day. Contrast §9.
- Father an army officer turned postal official; childhood moves through **Breslau, Neuruppin, Höxter** and Königsberg **[A as to the towns: dewiki, GG-info]**, **[R as to order and years: no source dates any leg]**. Three placeholder-dated stops, each declaring itself.
- **Cadet institution at about thirteen, 1866** **[A: dewiki]**. **Gap:** no cadet house, no city; pinned to Berlin as the system's administrative centre.
- **Commissioned 1871** **[A: dewiki]**. **Franco-Prussian War service and a long stint as Bezirksadjutant at Stolp** **[R: later Lebensreform and Hauptmann-adjacent sketches, not checked against Prussian military rolls]**. **Contradiction flagged:** part of that literature, inherited into modern Hauptmann scholarship, makes him a *Bavarian* officer, and an 1871 commission leaves little room for service in a war ending that year.
- **Resigned 1879 after his parents' deaths** **[A: dewiki, "Nach dem Tod seiner Eltern quittierte er 1879 den Militärdienst"]**. **Correction:** the pool's "parallel tradition" adding personal illness has **no located source**; illness-as-conversion-crisis is a stock Lebensreform pattern applied retrospectively. Dropped as unsourced accretion. **Gap:** place undocumented; pinned to Stolp, his last attested posting.

## 2. Italy and the turn from Church to Nature, 1881-1882

- **Roughly two years in Italy, 1881-1883**, decisive for vegetarianism and natural living **[A: dewiki]**. **Gap:** no city, address, route or companion; national placeholder coordinate.
- **Von der Kirche zur Natur** **[A as to the work]**. **Contradiction flagged:** Literaturport dates it 1882, other catalogues 1881. The file uses 1881 and says so.
- **Unsinn und Unmoral im Alten Testament** **[R]**. **Correction:** the pool title *"in biblischen Geschichten"* is wrong; dewiki gives **"im Alten Testament"**. The 1882 date, the confiscation of early editions and the imprisonment rest on **Guttzeit's own later account** alone; the pool tagged this [A] and it is **downgraded to [R]**. It does fix sequence: the quarrel with scripture predates Diefenbach by three years, against a Graeser-circle literature that reads him as Diefenbach's product.

## 3. The Pythagorean Brotherhood, Berlin, 1884-1885

- **1884, Berlin: Bund der Pythagoräer founded by Robert Springer, Guttzeit, Otto Rabe, Karl Max Engelmann** **[A: dewiki; GG-pdf; Linse, *Barfüßige Propheten* (1983)]**, corroborated by `engelmann.journey.json`, whose 1884 stop already calls Guttzeit "the officer turned nature-preacher". No day or month anywhere.
- The motto ("Ni Fleisch, ni Fisch") is quoted verbatim in the journey file **[A: GG-pdf p.1]**. **Correction:** the pool attributed it to **Otto Rabe** via a "(Rabe, S.15)" citation that in the PDF attaches to a *different, following* quotation; the file cites it to the Bruder-Bund page itself.
- **The same four also recorded founding an Internationaler Bund für konsequente Humanität, 1884** **[A: dewiki "Robert Springer (Schriftsteller)"; `engelmann.journey.json`]**. **Gap:** no source says whether it duplicates, subsumes or stands apart from the Pythagoräer-Bund. June placeholder inherited from the Engelmann file.
- **Springer dies in Berlin, 21 October 1885** **[A]**; Guttzeit takes over what is left.
- **Renamed Bund für volle Menschlichkeit, motto Verbrüderung / Vernatürlichung, journal Der Bruder** **[A: GG-pdf p.2, verbatim]**; members used *du*, and Guttzeit refused *Herr* as encoding domination **[A]**. **Correction:** the pool called the phrase a *masthead*; the PDF calls it the organisation's new name and motto.
- **Contradiction flagged, three-way.** (a) dewiki and GG-pdf put the renaming in **1885**, right after Springer's death. (b) `engelmann.journey.json` dates renaming and journal to **1886**. (c) Diefenbach's letter of **June 1885** already speaks of reading the Bruder-Bund's journal, four months before Springer died. Nothing reconciles these; the file states all three.

## 4. Höllriegelskreuth and Diefenbach, 1885

- **Diefenbach opens contact with the Bruder-Bund, reads Der Bruder, wants coverage, asks Guttzeit to visit** **[A: GG-pdf p.3, letter verbatim in the journey file]**. **Correction:** the pool's orthography of the quote is corrected to the PDF's (*ausserhalb*, not *außerhalb*). The separate Diefenbach letter of 22 June 1885 is addressed **to Max Engelmann** and is already a stop in `engelmann.journey.json`. **Placement:** the invitation stop sits at **Berlin**, where Guttzeit read it, not Munich-Thalkirchen, where Diefenbach wrote it.
- **Elisabeth Guttzeit arrives 10 July 1885, renamed "Fidelis"** **[A: GG-info; Schirn museum panels]**.
- **Johannes follows 17 September 1885 as secretary and household record-keeper** **[A: Schirn panels; dewiki]**. The exchange runs both ways: he takes on elements of the apostolic dress, his Brüderlichkeit language runs into Diefenbach's developing Humanitas.
- **Departure 24 December 1885** **[R: GG-info, sympathetic]**. That he was gone by year's end is well supported; the Christmas Eve date comes only from the sympathetic tradition. **Gap:** nobody records the substance of the quarrel.
- **Coordinate correction, three times over.** The pool carried three wrong Höllriegelskreuth values (48.0264/11.4728, 48.0206/11.5192, and a third), all 2.6-3.3 km off; dewiki gives **48.0431 / 11.5094**. Applied to all three stops.

## 5. The wandering years, 1886-1892

- **Wandering Naturprediger before vegetarian and natural-healing societies, c.1886-1888; reportedly compared his wandering to Ahasuerus** **[R: later Lebensreform histories, Graeser-circle sourcing]**. Reported self-mythologising, folded into the Zürich stop for want of a place to pin it to.
- **1888, Zürich: meets Gerhart Hauptmann** **[A: dewiki]**: coarse haircloth, sandals, reddish shoulder-length hair, a cord round a bare head, and the epithet **"Pfingstapostel"** **[A: Hauptmann, *Das Abenteuer meiner Jugend*, via dewiki]**.
  - **Correction, and it matters.** The pool dated this **20 May 1888**, Pentecost. **No source places the meeting at Pentecost**; the date was back-derived from the *epithet*, which describes the man's manner, not the calendar. Reduced to a **year-only 1888 placeholder**. Second correction: the dress-and-hair description, credited in the pool to Neymeyr 2022, is **Hauptmann's own memoir**. Reassigned.
- **1890: Hauptmann's novella *Der Apostel***, whose protagonist combines characteristics of **two** wandering preachers, **Guttzeit and Diefenbach**; **Guttzeit protested** **[A: Neymeyr, *Weimarer Beiträge* 68 (2022) 3, pp.390-415, verified from the DNB PDF, citing Hauptmann, *Notiz-Kalender 1889 bis 1891*, ed. Machatzke, pp.278f.]**. **Correction:** the pool's subtitle *"Zu Hauptmanns Der Apostel"* is the article's **running head**. **Contradiction flagged:** Linse sees Guttzeit "porträtiert" in the Apostle (*Barfüßige Propheten* p.19), Cowen names first Guttzeit then Diefenbach as "Urbild" (*Hauptmann-Kommentar*, 1981, pp.50-51). **Placement:** the novella is Hauptmann's act and gets no stop; it is carried on Guttzeit's Zürich stop, with his protest, which is his act.
- **1890: prosecuted for grober Unfug over his dress** **[A: dewiki]**. **Correction:** the pool tagged the **acquittal** [A], but *freigesprochen* appears nowhere in the cited article; the acquittal is **unsourced** and downgraded to [R]. (The pool's own two lenses contradicted each other here.) **Gap:** city and court undocumented; Berlin placeholder.
- **1891: Der volle Mensch**; **1892: Das Prügeln in der Schule** and **Über Soldatenmisshandlungen und Beschwerderecht** **[A as to the works: working bibliography from library catalogues; none checked against a copy]**.
- **Residences at Stuttgart and Söflingen near Ulm** **[R: named among his 1890s addresses without dates]**; the decade's residence sequence is imperfectly reconstructed in every source.

## 6. Dresden-Loschwitz and the full human being, 1893-1899

- **Settles at Dresden-Loschwitz** **[A as to residence: Uwe Wolfram, *Elbhang-Kurier* 3/2014, pp.12-13; dewiki, which also names a Viktoriastraße address]**, **[R as to 1893]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool carried **three** Loschwitz values (51.0578/13.8253, 51.0603/13.8153, 51.0587/13.8215); dewiki gives **51.0539 / 13.8150**, applied to all Loschwitz stops.
- Enormous pamphlet output on corporal punishment, soldiers, fashion, spiritualism, love, immortality, child-rearing, marriage and free love, and same-sex love, under the programme of **der volle Mensch** **[A: dewiki; Wolfram]**. Emblem: **an apple breaking a sword**, against **"Schlachthaus-Zivilisation"** **[R: GG-info]**. Holbein's comparison to 1960s flowers-in-rifle-barrels (*Narratorium*, 2008) is analogy only and is in no campa.
- **Naturrecht oder Verbrechen? Über Liebe zum gleichen Geschlecht** **[R as to a c.1895 first edition]**. The dating corrects the standard literature: Beachy dates it 1900 (*JMH* 82, 2010); a **trade advertisement proves circulation by 1897 [A]**; the **1899 *Bibliographie der homosexuellen Literatur* already lists it [A]**. So 1899/1900 is too late; 1895 is reconstruction, not an attested imprint.
  - **Standing caution, applied in the campa.** No reliable evidence establishes **Guttzeit's own orientation**; he is an early advocate for the legitimacy of same-sex love, never a documented gay man. `engelmann.journey.json` already flags the parallel Graeser-circle speculation as assumption.
- **1896: Meine Ehe** **[A as to the work]**.
- **c.1898: Was will der Naturprediger? 40 Lehrsätze des reinen Menschentums** **[A as to work and content: dewiki, secondary Lebensreform scholarship; two theses verbatim at GG-info]**, **[R as to 1898; some material suggests an earlier version circulated]**.
- **1898: Anton Losert and two other Guttzeit followers enter Diefenbach's Himmelhof near Vienna**, preaching primitive Christianity and the rejection of money, winning the young **Gusto Gräser** and joining a revolt against Diefenbach **[A: Linse; GG-info, sympathetic]**: the documented bridge toward the network that produced Monte Verità.
  - **Placement, a deliberate departure from the pool**, which put the stop at the Himmelhof (48.1908/16.2508). **Guttzeit is not recorded there.** Losert travelled; Guttzeit did not. The stop sits at **Guttzeit's own Loschwitz pin**. Losert has no file.
- **1899: an Alma Guttzeit documented in Dresden publishing on children in the Dresden art exhibition** **[A as to the publication]**, **[R as to her being his wife: family and Graeser-circle tradition]**. The tradition also has the two running an alternative book trade near Trieste after 1900 **[R]**, carried in the campa as tradition with no stop. **Gap, stated as a gap: no marriage certificate has been located for any of Guttzeit's marriages**; the "second marriage" of 1905 leaves a first undated and the bride unnamed.

## 7. The sentence, 1900-1903

The best-evidenced episode in the life, because he reported it himself, contemporaneously.

- **Ordered to report at Hoyerswerda on 6 April 1900 for a three-month sentence for "Beschimpfung der jüdischen Religion"**, incurred by his attacks on the Old Testament and its "Blut- und Eisenreligion"; he did not report and went on lecturing and publishing **[A: his autobiographical notice in his own journal, summer 1900]**.
  - **The antisemitism question is flagged as unresolved, not exonerated.** His 1900 defence, that no Jew had felt insulted, is **advocacy by the defendant**. Whether his anti-biblical polemic crossed into the antisemitic discursive environment of the period is an **open question requiring dedicated textual investigation**; the campa says the defence settles nothing.
- **Launched Der Neue Mensch, volume 1 dated May-June 1900** **[A: his own surviving notice]**, correcting the standard 1901 start, independently supported by a photograph on GG-info filed as `Guttzeit1900DerNeueMensch.jpg`. (The pool counted two Guttzeit photographs there; there are **three**, and this is the useful one.)
  - **Correction to the quoted verse.** The pool gave a truncated, mis-spelled fragment and speculated a source in *Jugendblüten* (1889) or *Der Bruder*. Neither is right: the full block is on the same page and cited to ***Der Neue Mensch*, 1. Jg.**
- **Arrested within an hour on the Breslau prosecutor's instructions, 4 July 1900, sent to Ratibor and serving the term there** **[A: same notice]**. **Gap:** nothing survives about the term itself; the 5 July stop date follows from the arrest and is not separately documented.
- **Der Neue Mensch failed commercially and ended, 1903**; **moved to Upper Silesia 1903** **[A: dewiki, "1903 verzog Guttzeit nach Oberschlesien und heiratete 1905 zum zweiten Mal"]**. **Correction:** the pool conflated move and marriage into one 1905 event; they are two years apart and are split into two stops. **Gap:** the Upper Silesian town is never named.

## 8. Neu-Esting: the robe laid aside, 1905-1927

- **Second marriage, 1905** **[R: dewiki states it; no place, no name, no certificate]**. Regional placeholder coordinate.
- **Ein dunkler Punkt**, on abortion, Max Spohr, Leipzig **[R as to a 1905 first edition: contemporary legal bibliography, alexandria.unisg.ch]**, correcting standard bibliographies that date it 1922; the edition sequence recorded there (fourth by 1911, fifth 1914, sixth by 1919) is itself the argument against 1922. Recorded as a **structural feature of his thought, not an inconsistency**: he defends sexual freedom, same-sex love and women's emancipation and opposes abortion, because his master value is Life or Nature rather than individual autonomy.
- **From 1908 resident at Neu-Esting (Olching); abandons the apostolic robe for conventional author's dress** **[A: dewiki, "Ab 1908 wohnte Guttzeit in Neu-Esting"]**, the second of two clothing-and-identity transformations. **Coordinate correction:** the pool's 48.2036/11.3378 sits ~2 km east of Esting, and its afterlife lens used a fourth value, 48.1793/11.3388, outside the built-up area. dewiki gives Esting = 48.2008/11.3086, Olching centre = 48.2086/11.3356; Neu-Esting lies between, and **48.198 / 11.320** is used for all Neu-Esting/Olching stops including the death.
- **Schamgefühl, Anstand und Sittlichkeit**, circulating from 1908 with a **documented 1911 edition** **[R]**, correcting the familiar 1922 date.
- **By 1908 writing approvingly of Isadora Duncan**; **Dickinson, *Dancing in the Blood* (2017), calls him a German "sex-radical" in that context** **[A]**. **Direction stated explicitly, in campa and `date_confidence`:** one-directional. `isadora_duncan.journey.json` contains **no mention of Guttzeit** (absence check run deliberately). No meeting, no correspondence, no discipleship; the link to later free dance and body culture is genealogical, not a chain of instruction.
- **1914: Die Tyrannei der Mode** **[A as to the work]**, continuing the polemic begun with the 1890 prosecution.
- **1914: Schöpfung und Sündenfall**, from Neu-Esting **[A: surviving copy at the Heinrich-Heine-Institut, Düsseldorf, object HHI.NLB.4382, in the estate library of the poet Karl Röttger]**: Swedenborg-indebted spiritual hermeneutics, attacking conventional theology and ridiculing Theosophical excesses. His religious road runs church Christianity, natural religion, radical humanism, then this, and is not a Christian-to-atheist line.
- **15 July 1916: telephones Erich Mühsam to the Kakaostube at Karlsplatz, Munich, to meet a friend visiting from Zürich** **[R: Mühsam's diary; a single source, Mühsam's not Guttzeit's; the friend unnamed]**. Proves him alive, socially active in Munich's radical milieu, known to Mühsam, and still holding Swiss connections in wartime.
- **11 January 1917: surviving handwritten letter to Michael Georg Conrad** **[A: Kalliope DE-611-HS-1397315, Conrad papers, Monacensia]**, contents not reproduced in the catalogue entry. **1917: Die Macht des Glaubens und des Willens re-circulated** and **further 1920 correspondence** **[A: Kalliope]**, both folded into this stop.
- **Letter to the writer Wilhelm Walloth, postmarked 10 June 1927** **[A: DDB]**. **Correction:** the pool described this as an unspecified "surviving autograph"; the authority record names recipient and postmark. **Explicit gap, left open: no reliable evidence establishes his attitude toward the Nazi regime after 1933.** Eight years with nothing in them, not filled with speculation.

## 9. Death and afterlife

- **Died 1935 at Olching (Esting), aged 81-82** **[A as to year and place: DDB, recording "XX.XX.1935, Olching"]**. Day and month were never established anywhere, and the campa says so.
- **Contradiction flagged.** **Literaturport** gives the death as **"after 1913, place unknown"**, flatly contradicting DDB. Two respectable catalogues, one live disagreement, carried in `date_confidence`.
- **No grave, headstone, plaque, street name or monument has been located anywhere** **[R: an absence across DDB, dewiki, literaturport, GG-info]**. The afterlife is textual and archival, not monumental.
- **Iconography:** a Wikimedia Commons category holds a portrait photograph and a signature reproduction **[A]**; GG-info reproduces three more **[R]**.
- **Modern rediscovery** runs through four channels that do not talk to each other, all **[A]**: Neymeyr 2022 (literary scholarship); Weir, *Red Secularism*, pp.97-101 (religious and political history); Dickinson 2017 (dance history); Wolfram 2014 (local history).
- **No modern reprint, critical edition or translation has surfaced** **[R: an absence across emuseum.duesseldorf.de, kalliope-verbund.info, literaturport.de, DDB]**; the output survives as scattered originals and secondary citation.
- **Overclaim flagged and rejected.** GG-info states flatly that *"Guttzeit had two students: Gusto Graeser and Gerhart Hauptmann"* **[R]**. Unsupported: the documented chain to Gräser runs **through Losert** (§6), and Hauptmann's contact was one 1888 encounter followed by a novella Guttzeit protested against. In no campa.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Guttzeit is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no framework-applied-backward stops were written. Per the adversary's duty in that file, the relations in the pool **not rooted in Guttzeit's own life** are named here rather than silently deleted, with the direction stated.

**Direction caught: forward, in every case.** All of the following postdate or lie outside his documented movements and were dropped. Forward reach is not licensable by an agent's judgement; they are listed only so the operator sees what was in the pool.

1. **Monte Verità after 1900**: the Gräser brothers' Ticino reconnaissance (Nov 1900) and purchase of Monte Monescia (Dec 1900); the Munich founding meeting (Oct 1900, `ida_hoffmann.journey.json`); Gusto Gräser's expulsion (Jun 1901). Guttzeit's connection is two removes away, via Losert to Gräser and via Engelmann's pension on Monte Trinità. He never went to Ascona.
2. **Laban's School for Art (Jul 1913) and the Sang an die Sonne rite (Aug 1917)** (`laban.journey.json`); **Wigman and Perrottet's 1913 Hellerau-to-Ascona pivot** (`mary_wigman.journey.json`). Thematic descent from the body-as-manifesto template, no personal link, all after 1908.
3. **Theodor Reuss's OTO lodge Verita Mistica (1916) and anational congress (1917)** (`theodor_reuss.journey.json`): programmatic resonance with the 40 Theses only, already [R] in the pool.
4. **Otto Gross at Monte Verità (Apr 1906)** (`otto_gross.journey.json`). Note the near-miss: Gross met **Erich Mühsam** there, and Mühsam is the man Guttzeit telephoned in 1916. The **Guttzeit-Mühsam link is real and is in the file**; the Gross link is not, and no source connects them.
5. **Hermann Hesse's 1907 climb** (`otto_hanish.journey.json`), offered in the pool as a *structural parallel* to Hauptmann publicising Guttzeit. A parallel is not a relation.
6. **Wolfgang Pauli at Ascona, 1952** (`robert_fludd.journey.json`), seventeen years after Guttzeit's death.

One further placement decision, not an apparatus question: the **Himmelhof stop** was moved off Vienna onto Guttzeit's own Loschwitz pin, because his followers travelled and he did not. If **Anton Losert** is ever researched, that scene belongs on his file.

Two named interlocks survive into the campas, both direct relations: **Karl Max Engelmann** (co-founder, 1884, `engelmann.journey.json`) and **Isadora Duncan** (Guttzeit wrote about her in 1908; the campa says the direction out loud, `isadora_duncan.journey.json`). **Karl Wilhelm Diefenbach, Gerhart Hauptmann, Gusto Gräser and Karl Gräser** appear in campas as historical persons but have **no files**; the Graesers sit in QUEUE.md rows 519-520, queued and unresearched. None is cited as a file.
