# John Locke (1632-1704): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `john_locke`. Sources are the pipeline's five verified lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife) with the verifier's corrections carried through.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference: carried, not documented. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

## 0. Two dating problems that affect everything below

**Calendar.** The journey is marked `gregorian` per the atlas rule for post-1582 lives: a convenience, not a fact about the documents. England kept the Julian calendar until 1752, so every English date here is Old Style and every Dutch or French date New Style, ten days ahead. The pool preserves one double form: the Calais crossing, **1675-11-12/24** [A: *Studi Lockiani*]. Dates are reproduced as the sources give them, **not** normalised. Stated as a gap.

**Coordinates.** The lenses disagreed with each other about the same places, in some cases badly; corrections are in §8. Worst case: **High Laver carried four different pairs**, **Wrington three, all wrong.**

## 1. Somerset, 1632-1647

- Born **29 August 1632** at **Wrington, Somerset**, baptised at **All Saints the same day**. Father a country attorney and clerk to the justices; mother a tanner's daughter; household Puritan. **[A: Wikipedia; Fox Bourne 1876]**
- Family **removes to Pensford**, seven miles south of Bristol, where the father practises law. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED.** The **geography lens dates the move to 1632**, "shortly after Locke's birth", following Wikipedia's own phrasing; the **chronology lens dates it to 1642**, bundling it with the father's Parliamentary service. Ten years apart. The journey dates it **1633** as a reading of "shortly after" and carries the contradiction verbatim in `date_confidence`. No third source was reached that would settle it.
- The father serves as **captain of horse for Parliament** under **Alexander Popham**. **[A: Wikipedia]** The 1642 date attaches securely to *this* fact, which is probably how it migrated onto the house move.
- Boyhood at a **Tudor farmhouse at Belluton** near Pensford. **[R: antiquarian tradition via county histories]** No document fixes the house; it survives in fragments; coordinates approximate.
- **1647**: entered at **Westminster School** under **Richard Busby**, sponsored by Popham, now an MP. **[A: Wikipedia]** The standard link between Busby's flogging and Locke's later argument against corporal punishment is **[R]**, a commonplace rather than anything Locke wrote.

## 2. Oxford, 1652-1666

- **November 1652**: admitted to **Christ Church** at twenty; finds the scholastic curriculum tedious and turns to natural philosophy and medicine. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP]**
- **BA February 1656**; **MA June 1658 with election as Senior Student**. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP]** Both lenses agree; the firmest pair in the Oxford stretch.
- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED: the lectureships.**

  | Post | chronology lens | geography lens | SEP / standard chronology |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Lecturer in Greek | 1660 | 1660 | December 1660 |
  | Lecturer in Rhetoric | **1663** | **1662** | **1663** |
  | Censor of Moral Philosophy | absent | **1663** | **1664** (Cranston, Woolhouse) |

  The journey dates Greek 1660-12 and Rhetoric 1663 (SEP and the chronology lens against the geography lens), and states the 1663-vs-1664 censorship split in `date_confidence` without choosing. **[A for all four posts; the years contested]**
- **November 1665 to February 1666**: **secretary to Sir Walter Vane** on a failed mission to the **Elector of Brandenburg** at **Kleve**, resident at the **Schwanenburg**. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP]** Reading Kleve as a seed of the toleration argument is **[R]**: an interpretation the sources invite but do not assert.
- **1666**: meets **Anthony Ashley Cooper**, at Oxford to take the waters at **Astrop** and wanting medical advice. **[A: Wikipedia; johnlocke.net]**

## 3. Exeter House, 1667-1675

- **May 1667**: moves into **Exeter House on the Strand** as physician, tutor, secretary. **[A: Wikipedia, both articles]** The building is gone; the coordinates are approximated from period maps of Exeter Street and are **[R]** even though the residency is **[A]**.
- **May 1668**: oversees the **liver-abscess drainage** that probably saves Ashley's life; a drainage tap is left in the wound. **[A: Wikipedia, Shaftesbury]** **CORRECTED TWICE:** the gather said the tap was **silver** and that **Whigs** coined "Tapski". Both wrong: the tap was **copper**, and "Tapski" was a **Tory** taunt, the original having Shaftesbury's own party mock him; the "-ski" alluded to the charge that he wanted a Polish-style elective monarchy.
- **1668**: elected **Fellow of the Royal Society**, with **Boyle** and **Sydenham** in the circle. **[A: Wikipedia; johnlocke.net]** **VENUE CORRECTED:** the geography lens put the election at **Gresham College**, but the Society quit Gresham after the Fire of 1666 for **Arundel House** and did not return until **1673** **[A: Wikipedia "Royal Society"]**. The journey uses **Arundel House, 51.5117, -0.1157**, the **canonical pin in `newton.journey.json`**, which flags the identical error for the 1672 optical paper. Inherited byte-identical.
- **1669**: directed to help draft the **Fundamental Constitutions of Carolina**; **October 1673**, **Secretary to the Council of Trade and Plantations**. **[A: Wikipedia]** The *content* of the Constitutions (landgraves, caciques, the article on masters and slaves) is **not in the verified pool** and is therefore **not asserted** in the journey. See §10, gap 4.
- **1672**: Ashley Cooper made **1st Earl of Shaftesbury, 23 April 1672** **[A: Wikipedia, Shaftesbury]** and Lord Chancellor the same year. **CONTRADICTION RECORDED:** the chronology lens dated the earldom **1673**, after the Chancellorship, following the Locke article against the dedicated one. No stop turns on it.
- **1671**: an evening discussion of morality and revealed religion stalls at Exeter House; Locke resolves to examine the understanding first and begins the *Essay*. **[A: Locke's Epistle to the Reader; SEP]** One of the few episodes with the subject himself as source.
- **February 1675**: **Bachelor of Medicine**, Oxford. **[A: Wikipedia; johnlocke.net]**

## 4. France, 1675-1679

- **CORRECTION CARRIED.** The chronology lens described **two tours**, one from 1675 and a "second tour" in 1679. Wrong: **one continuous stay, November 1675 to spring 1679**; **1679 is the year of return, not of departure**. **[A: Wikipedia; the *Studi Lockiani* article treats 1675-1679 as one span]**
- **12/24 November 1675**: sails without his **commonplace books**, lands at **Calais**, switches to a running dated **journal**. **[A: *Studi Lockiani*]** The geography lens tagged this [R]; the article is specific enough to carry the practice change as [A].
- **Paris**, southbound and revisited. **[A: Lough]** The **canonical atlas Paris pin 48.8566, 2.3522** is inherited byte-identical (from `alexis_de_tocqueville.journey.json`), overriding the geography lens's 48.853, 2.3499.
- **Montpellier**, roughly **fifteen months, 1676-1677**: medical faculty, English and Huguenot residents, botany, journals of Protestant life. **[A: SEP]**
- **27 February 1677 (NS)**: the journal records **three leagues to Gigean**, then **five leagues on to Pezenas**. **[A for the route; R for the journal date]**, which the verifier could not confirm without Lough. **Gigean corrected** from 43.5486, 3.8232 (which lands near Cournonsec/Fabregues) to **43.5003, 3.7117**.
- **Lyon, Toulouse, Bordeaux, Blois** are named in the pool from Lough **[A]** but **cut from the journey** to stay inside the stop range: a deliberate omission, recorded.
- **Spring 1679**: returns. The **port is not attested**; **Dover** is reconstructed from the Calais packet route. **[R: Wikipedia gives only "returned to England" in 1679]**

## 5. Exclusion Crisis and flight, 1679-1683

- **1680-1683**: divides time between Oxford and London; **royal agents monitor him at Christ Church**, suspecting anti-court tracts. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP]**
- **COMPOSITION OF THE TWO TREATISES, CONTRADICTION FLAGGED.** The chronology lens dated the drafting **1682**. **Laslett's dating**, followed by Wikipedia and SEP, puts the bulk in **1679-1681** with revision after the 1683 flight; **SEP gives 1681**; **1682 falls outside the accepted window.** The journey attaches the composition to the 1680 surveillance stop, names the 1679-1681 window in `date_confidence`, and identifies 1682 as the outlier rather than dropping it silently.
- **1682**: meets **Damaris Cudworth**, later Lady Masham, a philosopher in her own right. **[R: tradition; no primary correspondence in any reachable source]** **CORRECTED:** the pool had the attachment forming "decades before settling permanently in her household". Met 1682, moved to Oates 1691: **nine years**.
- **September 1683**: the **Rye House Plot** is discovered; Locke leaves for the Dutch Republic. **[A: SEP; Wikipedia]** **LENGTH OF EXILE, CONTRADICTION:** chronology says "five-year exile", geography "nearly six years"; September 1683 to February 1689 is **five years and five months**, and the journey uses that rather than either round number.

## 6. The Dutch Republic, 1683-1689

- **September 1684 to May 1685**: moves among **Amsterdam, Leiden, Utrecht**, six weeks of it continuous travel in August-September 1684, under the alias **"Dr. van Linden"** amid extradition fears after the **Monmouth Rebellion**. **[R for the sequence; A by weak convergence for the alias: littlehouseofconcrete.net + lensonleeuwenhoek.net]** Sources agree on the set of cities, not the order; both are amateur-scholarly sites and no archival citation was reached. Carried as **one** stop, not three false-precision pins.
- **November 1684**: **Charles II has his name struck from the Christ Church student list.** **[A: SEP]** No charge, no hearing, and he is abroad.
- **Winter 1685-86, Amsterdam**: writes the **Epistola de Tolerantia**. **[A: Wikipedia; IEP]** **CONTRADICTION FLAGGED:** chronology dates it **1685, "in the Netherlands"**, geography **Rotterdam, 1686-1689**. The verifier fixes **Amsterdam, winter 1685-86**; the journey uses that and names the Rotterdam claim in `date_confidence` as the discarded alternative.
- **22 June 1685**: visits **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek** on the **Hippolytusbuurt, Delft**; observes red blood cells, a section of human tooth, and dog spermatozoa. **Precisely dated in Locke's own journal** **[A: lensonleeuwenhoek.net]**: the best-fixed day of the Dutch years.
- **1686, Amsterdam**: associates with **Philipp van Limborch** and completes the ***Essay***. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP]**
- **1686-1689, Rotterdam**: lodges with the Quaker merchant **Benjamin Furly** at **De Lantaarn**. **[A: Wikipedia "Benjamin Furly"]** **CLAIM DEMOTED:** the Erasmus University Library page has Locke **chairing** a society of that name; the Furly article confirms the lodging and mentions **no club and no office**, and **De Lantaarn was the house**. The journey claims lodging and circle membership only. **[R]**
- **1687-1688, The Hague**: Orangist circle **[R: general accounts; no dated visit]**, **cut from the journey** as the weaker of the two [R] Dutch pins. **February 1689**: embarks from **Hellevoetsluis** with Princess Mary's fleet **[R: general histories of the fleet; Wikipedia names only the Netherlands departure]**, folded into the Greenwich stop.

## 7. England again, and the end, 1689-1704

- **12 February 1689**: lands at **Greenwich**. **[A: Wikipedia]** **AGE CORRECTED:** the geography lens said fifty-seven; born August 1632, he was fifty-six.
- **1689: the Latin *Epistola* is printed at GOUDA, not London**, by **Justus van der Hoeve** **[A: verifier's correction against IEP]**. The chronology lens placed it in London, putting a Dutch imprint in England; **Popple's English translation** is what appeared there. The journey splits the two. *Gap: no coordinate for Gouda in the pool; the journey uses 52.0115, 4.7104, a town-centre lookup, the only pin with no lens behind it.*
- **December 1689**: ***Two Treatises*** anonymously, title page backdated **1690**; the ***Essay*** under his name. **[A: Wikipedia, both articles]** **CLAIM SOFTENED:** the afterlife lens said he "**never**" acknowledged authorship in his lifetime; Wikipedia says not **until the end of his life**, in a **codicil to his will** public only when the will was read. He **complains of printing errors**, hand-corrects a third edition, leaves a fourth with friends, and **more than half the draft manuscript is lost**.
- **1691**: moves to **Oates**, High Laver, Essex, home of Sir Francis and Lady **Damaris Masham**, for life. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP]** The manor is gone; its footprint is approximate.
- **16 September 1693**: **Newton writes to Locke by name**, accusing him of endeavouring to "embroil him with woemen" and asking pardon. **[A: quoted in `newton.journey.json`]** The strongest interlock in the pool: dated correspondence between two atlas travellers, Fellows of the same Society. Pin inherited byte-identical, **52.207, 0.1146**.
- **1693** ***Some Thoughts Concerning Education***, out of letters to a friend; **1695** ***The Reasonableness of Christianity*** anonymously, at once accused of **Socinianism** **[A: IEP]**. **1696-1700**: **Commissioner for Trade and Plantations**, lodging near **Whitehall**; **resigns in 1700 citing asthma** **[A: Wikipedia; johnlocke.net]**.
- **28 October 1704**: dies at Oates aged **seventy-two**, **while Lady Masham reads him the Psalms**; buried in **November** at **All Saints', High Laver**, in a **brick altar tomb outside the south nave wall**. The **Latin epitaph is his own**, friends adding only the dates; it opens **"Siste Viator, Hic juxta situs est JOHANNES LOCKE"** and sends the reader to his writings for his character. In **1932** the **tablet is moved indoors**, separating epitaph from burial: the journey's closing stop. **[A: Wikipedia; SEP]**
- **1691**: **David Mazel**'s **French translation**, printed in the Netherlands, **drops the preface, the whole First Treatise, and the opening chapter of the Second**; **Montesquieu, Voltaire, and Rousseau all first meet Locke's politics in it**. **[A: Wikipedia, Two Treatises]** *Gap: the pool gives "Netherlands", no city; the journey uses the canonical Amsterdam pin and says so.* **1773**: the **only eighteenth-century American printing**, at **Boston**, carries the **same omissions**, none following until the twentieth century. **1789**: **Jefferson ranks Locke with Bacon and Newton**.
- Attested but not pinned, for stop count: the **Greenhill portrait c. 1672-73** and **Kneller 1697**; the early-19th-c. **Wrington busts of Locke and Hannah More**; the **Christ Church floor memorial** **[R; undated]**. The last two are named inside other stops.

## 10. Honest gaps

1. **Lough's *Locke's Travels in France* (1953) was not reachable.** Every French waypoint below Paris rests on secondary report; the 27 February 1677 date is unverified at first hand.
2. **No primary correspondence supports the Damaris Cudworth attachment.** Tradition only; kept [R]. **The Belluton farmhouse has no deed.**
3. **The Fundamental Constitutions of Carolina are named but not characterised in the pool.** Locke's involvement with the slave colonies, the hardest question about him, is therefore stated in the journey rather than examined. The largest single gap in the file.
4. **The alias "Dr. van Linden" and the Dutch city sequence** rest on two amateur-scholarly websites, no archival citation reached.
5. **Hellevoetsluis and Dover are both reconstructed**, from fleet histories and packet-route convention; neither is Locke-specific. **Gouda has no source-supplied coordinate.** **Old Style and New Style are not normalised** (§0).
6. **The Two Tracts on Government (c. 1660-62), the Stillingfleet controversy, the 1697 poor-law report, and the posthumous *Paraphrase* of Paul's epistles** are absent from the pool and so from the journey. A limit of the gather, not a judgement of importance.
