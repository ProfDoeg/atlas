# Gustave Flaubert (1821-1880): research report

*2026-08-02. Subject 3 on `working/journeys/QUEUE.md`, approved and previously unresearched. Output: `gustave_flaubert.journey.json`, 9 segments, 43 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference, with the weakness stated. Contradictions are flagged and left standing.

**Structural note.** The pool arrived in three lenses (chronology, geography, afterlife) that disagreed with each other on coordinates. Croisset appears three times with three different pins; the Cimetière monumental de Rouen three times, none correct; the Hôtel-Dieu twice, wrong both times. Section 7 records every value adopted.

## 1. Rouen, 1821-1840

Born **12 December 1821** in the staff apartment on the first floor of the Hôtel-Dieu of Rouen, where his father Achille-Cléophas was chief surgeon; mother Anne Justine Caroline Fleuriot; second son. **[A: Wikipedia EN and FR; Wikipedia FR, "Musée Flaubert et d'histoire de la médecine"]** Boarder at the **Collège royal of Rouen from 1832**; manuscript journal *Art et Progrès* with Ernest Chevalier, 1834; **expelled December 1839**; baccalauréat 1840 as an external candidate **[A: chronologie]**. The school was Collège royal from 1762 and took Corneille's name only in April 1873 **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Lycée Pierre-Corneille de Rouen"]**. **Trouville, 1836**: meets Élisa Schlésinger, unrequited, recurring as Madame Arnoux in *L'Éducation sentimentale* **[A: chronologie; Wikipedia EN]**.

**[R] in this section**, marked as tradition in the campa: the children watching cadavers from the garden wall (Sartre's material in *L'Idiot de la famille*), and the boy retrieving Élisa's cloak from the tide line, universal in the biographies and absent from both chronologies.

> **Contradiction inside the pool, settled by arithmetic.** Chronology lens says fourteen, geography lens says fifteen. Born 12 December 1821, so in August 1836 he was fourteen. The journey uses fourteen.

## 2. 1840-1846: the journey south, the law, the seizure, the deaths

**1840 journey** to the Pyrenees and Corsica. **[A for the itinerary: Wikipedia EN and FR]**

> **Three corrections.** (1) The pool said he travelled "with his parents". Wikipedia FR says only that they *funded* it (`ses parents lui financent un voyage`); the standard account has him accompanied by Dr Jules Cloquet. **[A funding; R Cloquet]** (2) The pool gave one point, 42.6, 1.0, for "Pyrenees / Corsica": a meaningless midpoint in the Ariège massif, in neither the spa towns nor Corsica. Split into Luchon (42.7906, 0.5936) and Ajaccio (41.9192, 8.7386). (3) The geography lens put "a violent, almost animal love affair" in Corsica; the documented 1840 episode is Eulalie Foucaud de Langlade at the Hôtel Richelieu in **Marseille**, on the return leg, and it is moved there inside the Ajaccio campa. **[R: standard biography, not in the chronologie]**

**Paris, 1841-1844.** Reads law without conviction; never finishes. **[A]** **First seizure** on a road near Pont-l'Évêque; the law is abandoned in consequence.

> **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Wikipedia FR dates the seizure January 1844; Wikipedia EN gives 1846. The journey uses 1844-01-01 and states the disagreement in `date_confidence` and in the campa. Neither is preferred on evidence; the French chronology is merely more granular. The coordinate 49.3, 0.4 is approximate; no spot is attested.

**Croisset, June 1844.** The family settles on the Seine below Rouen; thirty-five years there. **[A: chronologie; Wikipedia FR, "Pavillon Flaubert"]** **1846**: Achille-Cléophas dies, Caroline the sister within weeks **[A: chronologie]**, year only in the pool, month and day defaulted. The plaster death mask is **[R]** tradition; the inheritance funding Egypt is **[R]** inference, softened in the campa.

## 3. 1846-1849: Colet, Brittany, the first Temptation

**Meeting**, 1846, in James Pradier's studio. **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Louise Colet": `En 1846, elle rencontre Gustave Flaubert ... dans l'atelier de James Pradier`]** **Brittany, 1847**: three months on foot and by carriage with Maxime Du Camp from May, Mont Saint-Michel, and the co-written *Par les champs et par les grèves* **[A: chronologie and the book's own article]**. **September 1849**: the first *Tentation de saint Antoine* completed, Bouilhet and Du Camp advising everyday subject matter **[A: Wikipedia EN]**; the four-day reading aloud is **[R]**, universal tradition, presented without a document behind it.

> **Contradiction inside the pool, settled by arithmetic.** The chronology lens dates the Colet liaison 1846 to 6 March 1855 and calls it decade-long; the geography lens calls it eight-year. That span is nine years. The journey uses nine and says so.

> **Error corrected, three times over.** The pool asserted at three separate geography indices that *Par les champs et par les grèves* was published in 1886. It was not. Du Camp's sections appeared in the *Revue de Paris* from 1 April 1852; Flaubert's chapters on 15 October 1881, Charpentier. **[A: Wikipedia FR]**

**Mantes-la-Jolie**, the Colet meeting point, is **[R]**: no reachable source fixes a dated night there, this being tradition from the correspondence. Tagged a reconstruction in `date_confidence`, with the campa stating the gap; the date 1848-01-01 is a placeholder inside the 1846-1854 span, not evidence. **Quiberon and Brest** were tagged **[A]** in the pool on the strength of the travel book itself, which the pool's own note conceded was generous; both **dropped** rather than carried at an inflated tag.

## 4. 1849-1851: Egypt and the Near East

The densest attested stretch of the life, and the one that supplies the atlas its interlocks. All **[A]**, from Wikipedia FR "Voyage en Égypte" and "Maxime Du Camp", Wikipedia EN "Kuchuk Hanem" and "Gustave Flaubert", and Steegmuller: **Croisset 22 October 1849**, **Marseille** aboard *Le Nil* **4 November**, **Alexandria** mid-November with Pompey's Pillar, **Giza** December, **Cairo** through the winter, **Esna 6 March 1850** and Kuchuk Hanem (the only Egyptian stop dated to the day), **Karnak** and **Abu Simbel** in March, **Beirut** in July with the syphilis, **Jerusalem** in August, **Rhodes** in October, five weeks in **Constantinople**.

> **Departure: pool error corrected, tag upgraded.** The chronology lens paired date 1849-10 with place Marseille, a mismatch; the journey splits it in two. The pool tagged the departure **[R]**; Du Camp's `mission archéologique` from the Ministère de l'Instruction publique is confirmed by Wikipedia FR, "Maxime Du Camp", so it is carried **[A]**.

> **Pompey's Pillar: two corrections.** The pool's coordinate 31.179, 29.8853 is ~1.1 km off (correct: 31.1825, 29.8964), and the pillar does **not** overlook the harbour: it stands inland in the eastern temenos of the Serapeum. **[A: Wikipedia, "Pompey's Pillar (column)"]** The stop's pin is not the pillar's but the canonical atlas Alexandria pin (section 6).

> **Giza and Cairo: two claims struck.** The pool's "He climbs the Great Pyramid and carves nothing, unlike Du Camp, who photographs the monuments" is a non-sequitur, Du Camp not being recorded as carving; it is replaced by the documented anecdote, the Rouen wallpaper manufacturer's card nailed at the summit. And the Mosque of Muhammad Ali was not "still raw from construction finished only the year before": built 1832-1857, decoration unfinished until 1857, so in January 1850 an active building site. **[A: Wikipedia, "Mosque of Muhammad Ali"]**

> **Philae: internal impossibility corrected.** The geography lens dated it **February 1850**. Philae lies ~140 km upriver of Esna, dated 6 March by the same lens, and below Abu Simbel, dated March. A February Philae cannot precede a 6 March Esna on one upstream run that left Cairo on 6 February. Placed **mid-to-late March 1850**, reasoning in `date_confidence`. Coordinate corrected from 24.0258, 32.8845 to **24.0208, 32.8894** (the temple as re-erected on Agilkia), relocation dated 1977-1979 rather than "later".

> **Abu Simbel and Rhodes.** Abu Simbel was **not** the furthest point; the pair continued to Wadi Halfa and the Second Cataract **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Maxime Du Camp", covering Nubie]**. The Rhodes pin was corrected: the pool's 36.4341, 28.2176 sits ~1.3 km southwest of the medieval city, Old Town being **36.4447, 28.2258**.

**Damascus, August 1850, kept as [R].** Syria is on the confirmed itinerary; no reachable source puts him inside the Umayyad Mosque on a named day. Kept because it carries the `abdelkader` interlock, and tagged a reconstruction in both `date_confidence` and the campa. **Dendera** **[A: Steegmuller]** and the separate Luxor Temple item were dropped for length; **Bethlehem**, the **Dead Sea**, and **Baalbek**, all **[R]** and explicitly reconstructed from the standard 1850 circuit, were dropped outright.

> **Greece and Italy: tag inflation flagged.** The itinerary summary (`Liban, Palestine, Rhodes, Constantinople, Grèce, Italie`) is **[A]**; the monuments are not. The pool tagged the **Colosseum** stop **[A]** on a source supporting only "Italie", where the Vesuvius stop beside it was honestly **[R]**; that Vesuvius stop also named two sites and pinned one (40.8224, 14.4289 is Vesuvius, Pompeii is 9 km off at 40.7497, 14.4869). **Athens, Naples, and Rome were all dropped**, the legs named in prose inside the Constantinople campa. Largest deliberate gap in the journey.

> **Return date, contradiction flagged.** Wikipedia EN has him back by 1850 and starting *Madame Bovary* that year; Wikipedia FR dates the voyage 1849-1852, has him at Croisset by June 1851, and gives 19 September 1851 for the first page, which is the firmest thing in the dispute. The journey follows the French chronology and states the disagreement in the Constantinople campa.

## 5. 1851-1880

All **[A]**, attested to the day unless noted, from Wikipedia FR chronologie or Wikipedia EN "Madame Bovary" / "Salammbô": *Madame Bovary* begun **19 September 1851** and finished May 1856 after roughly fifty-six months; Colet liaison ends **6 March 1855**; serialization **1 October to 15 December 1856**; acquittal for outrage to public and religious morality **7 February 1857**; Michel Lévy edition **12 April 1857**; *Salammbô* begun **1 September 1857**, Tunisia trip **12 April to 5 June 1858**, Byrsa found nearly bare, published **1862** (year only); Légion d'honneur 1866; *L'Éducation sentimentale* begun **1 September 1864**, finished **16 May 1869**, published **November 1869** to a reserved reception; Bouilhet dies **July 1869**; Prussians occupy Croisset and he takes refuge in Rouen with his niece Caroline, 1870-1871 (month inferred); mother dies **6 April 1872**; *Le Candidat* fails March 1874; final *Tentation de saint Antoine* **1874**; *Trois Contes* **24 April 1877**; death at Croisset **8 May 1880**, cerebral hemorrhage, aged 58, *Bouvard et Pécuchet* unfinished; burial in the family vault, **Cimetière monumental de Rouen, 11 May 1880**, Zola, Maupassant, and Goncourt attending; Pavillon Flaubert classified **18 April 1914**; some **310** French streets named for him as of 2023.

**[R] inside that stretch**, tagged accordingly: Colet's *Lui* (1859); the prosecutor Ernest Pinard; Sainte-Beuve's attack and Flaubert's reply; the Commanville bankruptcy and Flaubert's self-ruin; Daudet at the funeral, named in the campa alongside the three attested mourners without being distinguished from them, a looseness recorded here; the Rouen legend that the coffin would not fit the plot, carried as legend in those words. A Berlioz letter present in an early draft of the *Salammbô* campa was **cut** as unverified. The Légion d'honneur, *Le Candidat*, and the 1857 book edition got no stops of their own.

> **Embellishment struck, twice.** Two lenses had him dying "at his writing desk", one adding "shortly before a planned visit to Paris". Neither is in any consulted source: Wikipedia FR gives only a sudden cerebral hemorrhage at Croisset, the 1911 Britannica only "a stroke of apoplexy". The campa states the absence explicitly, because the desk detail is repeated widely enough that a reader will expect it.

**Unused afterlife material**, recorded so the omission is visible: Chapu's 1890 monument; Barnes's *Flaubert's Parrot* (1984) and the two-museum "parrot wars" **[A for the novel; R for the dispute]**; the Marx / Steegmuller / Davis translation lineage; the 1884-1885 collected editions.

## 8. Honest gaps

1. **Stops dropped, reasons given above**: Greece and Italy entire (Athens, Naples, Rome), the largest gap; Quiberon and Brest; Bethlehem, the Dead Sea, Baalbek; Dendera and Luxor Temple (both **[A]**, cut for length, first to restore if the budget grew); London and Juliet Herbert, **[R]** tradition with no granularity beyond "1860s".
2. **The 1867 Exposition Universelle dropped.** Wikipedia FR says only that Flaubert was `vraisemblablement` photographed there with Princesse Mathilde: a probable identification of a photograph, not a documented visit. The pool tagged it **[A]** and gave it a month; both unsupported.
3. **The afterlife lens's best material is unused** (section 5). A future editor may want a Rouen reception stop; none was built, the register here being a life of journeys.
4. **No day is fixed** for the 1862 *Salammbô* publication, the 1846 deaths, the 1832 school entry, or the 1841 arrival in Paris. All default to 01 and say so.
5. **The Colet correspondence was not fetched**, so every claim resting on it, including the "God in the universe" formulation, is **[R]** via Wikiquote. **No French critical biography was consulted**: Sartre, the Pléiade *Correspondance*, and Steegmuller are cited at second hand through the research lenses.

## 9. Quotes used

Nine of the twelve are **[A]**, fetched from Project Gutenberg: the *Madame Bovary* opening (#2413) and eight from *The George Sand-Gustave Flaubert Letters* (#5115). Three are **[R]** and say so inside `quote_source` itself: "An author in his book must be like God in the universe..." (attributed to Colet, 9 December 1852, via Wikiquote; its primary-verified cousin is Letter CCCI to Sand, and the journey uses both and marks which is which); "Be regular and orderly in your life like a bourgeois..." (attributed to Gertrude Tennant, 1876); Zola's "Nervous analysis of the smallest facts..." (*La Tribune*, 28 November 1869), which is *about* Flaubert rather than by him and is labelled so. The "Axiom: Hatred of the bourgeois..." line is widely paraphrased as "the beginning of *wisdom*"; the primary translated text reads "virtue", and the journey uses the primary text.
