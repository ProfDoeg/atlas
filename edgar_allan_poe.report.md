# Edgar Allan Poe (1809-1849): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `edgar_allan_poe`. Companion: `edgar_allan_poe.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, present in the verified pool · **[R]** reconstruction or inference · **[R-out]** standard in the Poe literature but **not** in the pool, carried at reduced confidence and flagged wherever it enters the journey file.

The [A] / [R-out] split is load-bearing. The pool carries nine CORRECTED notes, several overturning dates that circulate everywhere. Where it corrects a common claim, the correction is followed and the discarded version named, not quietly dropped.

## 1. Birth and the Allan household (1809-1815)

- Born Boston, 19 January 1809, to the actors David Poe Jr. and Elizabeth "Eliza" Arnold Hopkins Poe. **[A: Wikipedia, "Edgar Allan Poe"]** Father abandoned the family, 1810, place given as "Richmond (probable)". **[A]** Folded into the 1811 stop rather than pinned as a probable place.
- Mother died of pulmonary tuberculosis, 1811; the orphan was unofficially fostered, never legally adopted, by John and Frances Allan of Richmond. **[A]** *Gap:* the pool gives the year only; the stop is dated `1811-12-01`, with `date_confidence` stating December comes from outside the pool. **[R-out for the month]**
- Baptized Episcopal, 1812. **[A]** Separation of the three children: **[R-out]**.

## 2. The British years (1815-1820)

- The Allans sailed for the United Kingdom, 1815. **[A]** The pool's original entry compressed two schools into that year; its note **corrects** this. Irvine, Ayrshire is 1815-16; the Misses Dubourg's school at 146 Sloane Street, Chelsea is 1816-17, dated under 1816 by eapoe.org, with Wikipedia saying he studied there "until summer 1817". **[A, corrected]** The file gives the pin to Chelsea and carries Irvine in the campa, one pin dropped for stop count.
- Manor House School, Stoke Newington, under the Reverend John Bransby. **Contradiction, not resolved:** Wikipedia implies 1817, eapoe.org dates entry under 1818. The pool says the entry "should not assert 1817 flatly" and recommends "1817/1818 (sources differ)". **[A for the fact, contradictory for the year]** Dated `1817-01-01`, disagreement in `date_confidence`. "Near London" is right: Stoke Newington was then about four miles north. **[A]**
- **[R-out], both in campa:** Dubourg is the laundress witness in "The Murders in the Rue Morgue" (1841); the schoolmaster of "William Wilson" (1839) is named Bransby.

## 3. Richmond, the University, the rupture (1820-1827)

- Returned to Richmond, 1820. **[A]** Lieutenant of a Richmond youth honor guard, 1824. **[A]** William Galt died 1825 leaving a substantial estate; Allan bought Moldavia. **[A]** Registered at the University of Virginia, February 1826, ancient and modern languages. **[A]**
- **Corrected by the pool:** the original entry said "one year" and that Poe "lost touch with" Elmira Royster. Both overturned: he entered 14 February 1826 and left that December, roughly ten months, one session, and eapoe.org has him leaving on *discovering Royster was engaged to another man*. Gambling debts and Allan's refusal to cover expenses are both cited causes. **[A, corrected]** The intercepted letters are **[R-out]**.
- Boston, April 1827, on odd jobs. **[A]**

## 4. The Army, under a false name (1827-1829)

- Enlisted 27 May 1827 at Boston as "Edgar A. Perry", claiming 22 when he was 18. **[A]** *Tamerlane and Other Poems*, anonymous, "A Bostonian", about 50 copies, 1827. **[A]** *Sequencing problem, stated as such:* the month is missing and convention defaults to 01, putting the book before the May enlistment though the printing almost certainly followed it. `date_confidence` says so; convention beat an invented month. The printer's name was cut as unverified; the surviving-copies sentence is **[R-out]**.
- Fort Independence, Boston Harbor, then Fort Moultrie, Charleston, on the brig *Waltham*, 8 November 1827. **[A]** Promoted to sergeant major, the highest non-commissioned rank, in roughly two years. **[A]**
- Frances Allan died 28 February 1829. **[A]** Discharged 15 April 1829 at Fort Monroe after arranging a replacement enlistee. **[A]** That Poe reached Richmond the day after her funeral is **[R-out]** and was cut; only the sequence is asserted. "The Gold-Bug" (1843) set on Sullivan's Island: **[R-out]**.

## 5. Baltimore, West Point, the third book (1829-1833)

- Before West Point, Baltimore, to Maria Clemm's household: Virginia Eliza Clemm, brother Henry, grandmother Elizabeth Cairnes Poe. **[A]** Year only. **Corrected by the pool:** the original entry welded John Neal's notice and *Al Aaraaf, Tamerlane and Minor Poems* onto September 1829. Split: Neal is September, the book **December 1829**, Hatch & Dunning of Baltimore, per eapoe.org. **[A, corrected]** The stop is dated to the book and carries both.
- West Point 1 July 1830. **[A]** John Allan married Louisa Patterson, October 1830. **[A]** Court-martialled 8 February 1831 for gross neglect of duty and disobedience of orders, having deliberately courted expulsion. **[A]** New York, *Poems*, February 1831, back to Baltimore in March. **[A]** *Sequencing problem again:* February with no day, default 01, placing the volume before the court-martial. Stated in `date_confidence`, not silently fixed. Elam Bliss, the cadets' subscription, the dedication to the Corps: **[R-out]**.
- William Henry Leonard Poe died 1 August 1831, complications attributed to alcoholism. **[A]** *Baltimore Saturday Visiter* prize for "MS. Found in a Bottle", 1833. **[A]** The $50 figure is **[R-out]** and was cut.
- 203 North Amity Street, where Poe lived around age 23, now the Poe House and Museum. **[A]** **Coordinate correction:** the pool flags its own 39.2925, -76.6339 as off by ~150 m and gives Wikipedia's **39.29129, -76.63310**.

## 6. The hollow-earth lineage (genealogy segment, 1692-1829)

Five stops that are not events in Poe's life: the doctrine arriving in *Pym*. All pins inherited byte-identical, three from `symmes.journey.json` (St. Louis 1818, Cincinnati 1820, Hamilton 1829), Paris 1818 from `humboldt.journey.json`, London/Halley 1692 at **51.5072, -0.1276** from the interlock lens.

- Halley's 1692 nested-shell model is called "the real proposal of the period" in `leonard_euler.journey.json`. **[A]** Euler was posthumously and wrongly folded into the lineage beside Symmes and Kircher, debunked by Edward Sandifer (2007). **[A, same file]** The campa states the misattribution as one.
- Circular No. 1 named Humboldt, Humphry Davy and Samuel Mitchill as "protectors" without consent. **[A]** Humboldt answered with total silence. **[A, via `humboldt.journey.json`]** Two quotes are reused byte-identical from `symmes.journey.json`, the Circular declaration and the Americus Symmes title line: the only pool-backed quotes here. **The Poe end of the link is [R] and the pool says so:** "Poe-Humboldt-Eureka link is R (not stated in file); Symmes-Humboldt naming is A."
- **Contradiction internal to the scholarship, left open.** Per the pool, "the extent to which Poe 'believed' the theory as opposed to using it as fictional material is debated among biographers," his admiration being generally read as speculative fascination rather than settled conviction. **[R, explicitly]** The file never says he believed it, only that the doctrine is the machinery under the last chapter.
- Reynolds broke from the doctrine at Philadelphia in 1826 and campaigned for a national polar expedition. **[A]** That stop was cut for stop count, Reynolds carried in the 1837 Richmond stop instead: a real loss, recorded.

## 7. The Messenger, the marriage, *Pym* (1835-1838)

- Assistant editor, *Southern Literary Messenger*, 1835, discharged within weeks and later rehired. **[A]** Month not given; the editor's name is **[R-out]** and was cut. Licence 22 September 1835 at Baltimore to marry his cousin Virginia Clemm, then 13; Poe was 26. **[A]** Presbyterian ceremony at a Richmond boarding house, 16 May 1836. **[A]** That Virginia was sworn to be 21 is **[R-out]**, in campa.
- Reviewed Reynolds's 1836 *Address* on a surveying and exploring expedition to the Pacific and South Seas in the January 1837 *Messenger*, advocacy itself entangled with Symmes-derived polar speculation. **[A: Wikipedia, "Jeremiah N. Reynolds"]** He left the *Messenger* that month, **claiming** to have raised circulation from about 700 to 3,500, which pool and campa both keep as a claim. **[A]** The second installment of *Pym* ran there in February. **[A]**
- Harper & Brothers published *Pym*, July 1838. It builds Symmes's hollow Earth, an unnaturally warm polar region opening on an interior world, into its Antarctic climax, and lifts roughly 700 words of Reynolds's *Address* into Chapter XVI. **[A: eapoe.org; Wikipedia, "The Narrative of Arthur Gordon Pym of Nantucket"]**
- The Antarctic verge stop (-66.5, 140) is **not a dated event**: an inherited composite pin for Pym's fictional latitude and the Wilkes Expedition of the same year. The pool dates the Wilkes pin 1838-01-01 and tags the parallel [R]; the file dates the stop July 1838, beside the novel, and says why. Tsalal details come from the novel, with no quote field, because the pool supplies **no verbatim Poe quotation**.

## 8. Philadelphia (1838-1844)

- Relocated 1838, five residences by 1844. **[A]** Assistant editor, *Burton's Gentleman's Magazine*, June 1839. **[A]** *Tales of the Grotesque and Arabesque*, two volumes, 1839. **[A]** Lea & Blanchard, twenty-five stories, payment in copies: **[R-out]**. Prospectus for *The Penn*, later *The Stylus*, June 1840, never realized. **[A]** *Graham's Magazine*, 1841, month not given. **[A]** Virginia ruptured a blood vessel at the piano, January 1842. **[A]**
- Failed to appear for the Philadelphia Custom House meeting, September 1842, **reportedly** because he was drinking. **[A]** The campa keeps the pool's "reportedly" and declines to adjudicate illness against grief against intolerance of alcohol.
- "The Murders in the Rue Morgue", *Graham's*, April 1841, and the invention of the detective story: **not in the pool's chronology at all**. They enter because the Borges interlock depends on them and the pool's Borges entry calls the Dupin tales the founding template. Carried **[R]**.
- **Honest gap.** The pool holds almost nothing on 1843-1844: no Philadelphia addresses, no "The Tell-Tale Heart", no *Dollar Newspaper* prize, no lecture tours. One stop crosses the years. Thin because the pool is thin, not because the years were.

## 9. New York, the Raven, Fordham, Eureka (1844-1848)

- After *Graham's*: briefly the *Evening Mirror*, then editor and eventually owner of the *Broadway Journal*, 1844. **[A]** "The Raven" in the *Mirror* and, as by "Quarles", in *The American Review*, 29 January 1845; paid $9. **[A]**
- The *Broadway Journal* failed; the family moved to a cottage at Fordham, then countryside, now the Bronx, 1846. **[A]** The tortoiseshell cat on Virginia's chest and the greatcoat over the straw come from Mary Gove Nichols's visit account: **[R-out]**, flagged in campa. Virginia died of tuberculosis there, 30 January 1847. **[A]** The posthumous watercolor as her only likeness: **[R-out]**.
- *Eureka*, 15 July 1848; reception mixed, praised as brilliant and denounced as pantheistic. **[A: eapoe.org; Wikipedia]** The dedication to Humboldt is **[R]** by the pool's note, though well established in the *Eureka* bibliography; the February 1848 Society Library lecture is **[R-out]**. The Berlin stop (52.517, 13.3945, *Kosmos* vol. 1, 1845) is inherited byte-identical from `humboldt.journey.json`; the pool tags the Kosmos-to-Eureka relation **[R]**, and the campa states the sequence while claiming no influence.

## 10. The last year and the death (1848-1849)

- Courted Sarah Helen Whitman at Providence, 1848; the engagement failed. **[A]** Month not given, so September 1848 is called a reconstruction. Cemetery proposal, settlement of property against him, abstinence pledge: **[R-out]**.
- Resumed with Sarah Elmira Royster, by then the widowed Mrs. Shelton, Richmond, 1849, reportedly planning marriage. **[A]** *Convention artifact, stated:* the courtship belongs to the summer, but the pool gives the year only, so the file defaults to `1849-01-01` and says the month is convention, not a source. Sons of Temperance and the Richmond lectures: **[R-out]**.
- Found semiconscious and in distress on the streets of Baltimore, in clothes not his own, 3 October 1849, taken to Washington Medical College. **[A]** Ryan's Fourth Ward polls, the palm-leaf hat, Joseph Walker's note, Dr. Snodgrass: all **[R-out]**, the tavern appearing only in `suggested_refs`. The delirious calls for "Reynolds" through the last night are **[R-out]** and the most tantalizing detail in the file, since Jeremiah N. Reynolds is a documented interlock. No reachable source connects the two. Left unexplained in campa.
- Died about 5:00 a.m., 7 October 1849; the attending physician later reported the final words as "Lord help my poor soul"; the cause remains disputed. **[A]** The **only verbatim quote in the journey file**, attributed by `quote_source` to the physician's report rather than to Poe, since John Joseph Moran varied his account later. That it varied is itself **[R-out]**. **Coordinate correction:** the pool flags 39.2904, -76.6122 as the Baltimore centroid, not the death site, and gives the hospital at Broadway and Fayette as roughly **39.2955, -76.5930**. Used for the October stops; the centroid is kept only for 1829 and 1831.
- Buried aged 40 at what is now Westminster Hall and Burying Ground, October 1849: family plot, southeastern corner, plot #27, now under a raven-engraved headstone, with David Poe Sr. and Henry; Neilson Poe arranged a small marker. **[A]** That the marker was smashed by a derailed train: **[R-out]**. The burial day is a reconstruction, the pool giving "1849-10" only.

## 11. Afterlife: slander, translation, monuments

- Griswold's hostile "Ludwig" obituary in the *New York Tribune* immediately after the death, then a three-volume edition from 1850 with a "Memoir" carrying forged letters and fabricated vices. **[A: Wikipedia, "Rufus Wilmot Griswold"]** The pool dates the sequence "1849-1850" without days; the file uses 9 October 1849, day called a reconstruction. The Memoir held the popular image of Poe for two decades despite Whitman and George Rex Graham denouncing it in print, and paradoxically boosted sales. **[A]**
- Baudelaire discovered Poe in 1847 and translated for roughly two decades: *Histoires extraordinaires* (1856), *Nouvelles histoires extraordinaires* (1857), *Histoires grotesques et sérieuses* (1865), plus *Eureka* and *Pym*. The versions became definitive and raised his continental standing above what he had at home. **[A: Wikipedia, "Charles Baudelaire"]** His remark about finding sentences he had himself imagined is **[R-out]**.
- "Pennies for Poe": Baltimore schoolteachers and students opened a subscription in October 1865 through the Public School Teachers Association. **[A]** **Corrected by the pool:** the ~$587 total is **March 1871**, not 1872; the 1865 launch stands. Childs gave $650, Hugh Sisson carved the monument, Adalbert Volck made the medallion, and the remains were reinterred at Greene and Fayette, the stone dedicated 17 November 1875 with an oration by John H. B. Latrobe. **[A]** Coordinates 39.29, -76.6239 unchanged. Walt Whitman's attendance is **[R-out]** and was **cut**.
- **The Poe Toaster: the largest correction in the file.** The usual claim, a visitor from 19 January 1949 for six decades, cannot stand as [A]. Per the pool: Wikipedia dates the visits to "sometime in the 1930s (though no report appeared in print until 1950)"; a 1950 *Baltimore Evening Sun* article already describes the visitor; the 1949 date circulates because Sam Porpora **admitted falsely telling a reporter** it began then; and in 2007 he claimed instead to have invented the tradition **in the 1960s**, which the 1950 report predates by a decade. Surviving as **[A]**: the cognac, the three roses, the original-grave cenotaph, and the final confirmed appearance on 19 January 2009 with no verified visitor since. The stop is dated `1930-01-01` as a floor, not a date. Porpora's claim stays **[R]**.
- "Poe Returning to Boston" by Stefanie Rocknak, unveiled 5 October 2014 at Edgar Allan Poe Square, Charles and Boylston, Robert Pinsky attending. **[A]** **Coordinate correction:** the pool flags 42.3496, -71.0745 as ~700 m southwest, near Bay Village, and gives Wikipedia's **42.352333, -71.067306**.
