# William Morris — Walthamstow to Kelmscott (1834–1896)

**33 stops · 7 segments · gregorian.** Register: the canon is true — the poems, romances, lectures, letters, the *Icelandic Journals*, and the sagas Morris Englished are narrated as events. The saga-sites (Þingvellir, Bergþórshvoll, Hlíðarendi) are marked *traditional*: Morris walked the traditional geography of *Njáls saga* as if it were living fact, and so do we.

## Judgment calls
- **The 1857 inscription** "I cannot paint you, but I love you" is traditional (widely reported of the *La Belle Iseult* canvas; some place it on the frame); dated to the murals/courtship, marked attested-adjacent via the event.
- **Saga quotes** at Bergþórshvoll and Hlíðarendi are from *Njáls saga* (Dasent's rendering, which Morris knew and worked beside Magnússon on), attributed to the saga, not to Morris — honest sourcing.
- **Iceland leg dates** follow the Iowa archive's reconstruction; individual saga-site days are traditional (Morris's pencil journal is not always precisely dated stop-to-stop).
- Kelmscott Manor appears **twice** by design — as the 1871 refuge and as the utopia's destination in *News from Nowhere* — because the journey folds real geography onto the mythic river. Its churchyard is the true terminus.
- Elm House / Kelmscott House / Merton coordinates are to the sites; a few (Willow Bough at Queen Square, the tapestry loom) are placed at the workplace, dated to the design's flowering rather than a single day (traditional).

## The tradition's own folds and gaps
Morris authored his own myth twice over: once as the "idle singer of an empty day" (the *Earthly Paradise* self-portrait he later repudiated), and once as the socialist prophet. The canon carries a **silence at the center** — Jane's love for Rossetti, the wound behind the flight to Iceland — which Morris never narrates directly; the journals go north instead of inward. I let the shadow fall (St Michael's, Kelmscott 1871) without inventing scenes the canon withholds.

## The five richest episodes
1. **The Nature of Gothic read at Oxford (1853)** — the revelation that seeds everything: joy-in-labour, hatred of the machine, the whole arc to socialism.
2. **Bergþórshvoll / Hlíðarendi (1871)** — the burning of Njáll and Gunnar's refusal to leave the fair hillside; the saga-land narrated as living fact, source of "the grey minster of lands."
3. **News from Nowhere's river (1890)** — the utopia walked as geography, Hammersmith to Kelmscott, the England-to-come reached through a real door.
4. **The Kelmscott Chaucer, "a pocket cathedral" (1896)** — the two Oxford brothers, both dying, closing the vow of their twenties in a single book.
5. **The hay-wain funeral to Kelmscott churchyard (1896)** — the maker carried home on an open farm-cart wreathed in willow, buried as one of the country-folk who built his house.

## Connections across the atlas
- **The book-as-art** (Kelmscott Press, the Golden type, the Chaucer) faces **Braille**'s raised books and the notation thread, and the corpus's own **book quipu** — the printed page dreaming it is the illuminated manuscript, the atlas's counter-image to the machine.
- **The sagas Englished** (Sigurd, the Iceland journals) face **Tolkien** — the Northern myth carried into modern English as a living inheritance; Morris is Tolkien's forefather in this.
- **News from Nowhere** faces **every utopia in the atlas** — it is the pastoral, hand-work answer to the machine-utopias, the river-journey utopia against the city-plan utopia.
- **The Cause** (Socialist League, Bloody Sunday, *John Ball*) faces the atlas's revolutionaries — Morris the gentle maker who crossed "the river of fire," dying, his doctor says, a victim of his socialism.

## Canonical-pin pass (2026-08-11)

This is that coordinated pass: **six stops here, 39 stops across nine journey files**, English and Spanish kept identical.

- **[A Wikipedia geotag 51.753871/-1.256046; OSM `Exeter College Chapel`, Turl Street, 51.7539367/-1.2561066] Exeter College, Oxford** (stop "the meeting with Burne-Jones"). **51.7548, -1.2554 → 51.7539, -1.256**, a move of ~108 m. The old point sat on the college's northern boundary against Broad Street; the new one is 5 m from the article geotag and lands in the front quad beside the chapel, which is where the rooms and the 1853 meeting belong — and where the *Adoration of the Magi* tapestry of the 1890 stop now hangs.

The old pin lay south of the square altogether; the new one is the modern no. 17 door on the square's south-east side, the rooms Morris shared with Burne-Jones. **The 1856 and 1861 stops are now separated**: they were two different houses, and the file had been giving them one pin.
