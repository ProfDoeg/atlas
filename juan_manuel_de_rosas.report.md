# Juan Manuel de Rosas (1793-1877) - research report

*2026-08-03. For the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `juan_manuel_de_rosas`; queue item 4, census entry 87. Companion artifact: `juan_manuel_de_rosas.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

Subject: Juan Manuel José Domingo Ortiz de Rozas y López de Osornio. Estanciero of the Salado frontier, twice governor of Buenos Aires province (1829-1832, 1835-1852), holder of the *suma del poder público*, tenant farmer at Swaythling, Hampshire, for the last twenty-five years of his life.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference no named source carries. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated. Where the research pool itself carried an error that verification caught, it is said plainly.

---

## 1. Birth and the invaded city (1793-1815)

- Born 30 March 1793 in his mother's house on the calle Santa Lucía (today Sarmiento) between Florida and San Martín, to León Ortiz de Rosas and Agustina López de Osornio **[A es.wikipedia, "Juan Manuel de Rosas"]**.
- Age eight, enrolled in Francisco Javier Argerich's private school **[A es.wikipedia]**. Nothing further on his schooling. That he showed early skill managing rural labour on the family estancias is **[R]**, recorded in the pool as undated tradition.
- Age thirteen, distributes ammunition during the Reconquista, 1806; fights in the defence of the city during the second invasion, 1807 **[A en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia]**. No day for either, so the stop defaults to 1806-01-01.
- On the López de Osornio estancias south of the río Salado, c. 1807-1815 **[R es.wikipedia]**: the source gives the region and no coordinate, so the pin -35.4 / -58.03 is approximate.
- Marries Encarnación Ezcurra y Arguibel, 1813 **[A both Wikipedias, year only]**. Juan Bautista born 30 July 1814, Manuela born 24 May 1817 **[A es.wikipedia]**.

## 2. The estanciero of the Salado (1819-1828)

- 1819: buys Los Cerrillos at San Miguel del Monte and organizes the Colorados del Monte **[A es.wikipedia]**.
- 1820: enlists with the Fifth Regiment of Militia, later called Colorados del Monte **[R en.wikipedia]**. Sits awkwardly beside the attested 1819 organization of the same body; left as found, see contradiction 8.
- 1 October 1820: helps defeat Colonel Manuel Pagola's mutiny **[A es.wikipedia]**.
- 1821: acquires the Estancia del Pino in La Matanza, renamed San Martín, held 1821-1824 **[A es.wikipedia]**. Coordinate approximate to the partido; the casco is unlocated.
- Estancia Camarones, 1820s: tagged **[A]** by the pool at -36.35 / -58.4, downgraded to **[R]**, since es.wikipedia places it only "al sur del río Salado" and the coordinate is a guess. Mentioned in a campa, not pinned.
- 1827: Dorrego appoints him comandante general de milicias **[A es.wikipedia, year only]**.
- 9 December 1828, Navarro: **the pool said Rosas was defeated here. He was not present.** Dorrego abandoned his plan to join Rosas and the northern militias, gave battle alone, and Lavalle's six hundred cuirassiers routed his two thousand gauchos; he was shot shortly after **[A es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Navarro"]**. The journey states the corrected version.

## 3. The first governorship (1829-1832)

- 26 April 1829: Rosas and Estanislao López beat Lavalle at Puente de Márquez **[A es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Puente de Márquez"]**.
- 24 June 1829: Pacto de Cañuelas signed with Lavalle at an estancia near the Cañuelas post held by an Englishman named Miller **[A es.wikipedia]**. Coordinate approximate to the partido.
- 8 December 1829: proclaimed governor, titled Restaurador de las Leyes **[A both Wikipedias]**. **Correction:** the pool said he received the *suma del poder público* in 1829. He received *facultades extraordinarias*; the suma came on 7 March 1835, as the same lens states correctly elsewhere.
- 4 January 1831, Pacto Federal: **Rosas did not sign it**, contrary to the pool. Signed at Santa Fe by José María Roxas y Patrón for Buenos Aires, Domingo Cullen for Santa Fe, Diego Miranda for Entre Ríos, Rosas instructing Roxas y Patrón from Buenos Aires. Corrientes acceded 19 August 1831 **[A es.wikipedia, "Pacto Federal (Argentina)"]**.
- End of first term: **three dates, not adjudicated.** 17 December 1832 (es.wikipedia biography); 5 December (en.wikipedia and es.wikipedia "Anexo:Gobernadores de la provincia de Buenos Aires"); 18 December (the pool, unsupported). The journey uses 17 December. Succeeded by Juan Ramón Balcarce **[A es.wikipedia]**.

## 4. Desert Campaign and the suma del poder público (1833-1835)

- 23 March 1833: leaves Los Cerrillos commanding a column of the Campaña al Desierto **[A es.wikipedia]**.
- 1833, río Colorado: Darwin, crossing the pampa overland from the Beagle, reaches the camp and gets a passport **[A Darwin, *Voyage of the Beagle*, ch. 4]**.
- **Coordinate unresolved.** Three points compete: the geography lens gives -39.0 / -62.9 while naming Pedro Luro, which sits at roughly -39.507 / -62.683, sixty km away and off the river; the quotes lens gives -39.0 / -64.0. The journey uses -39.5 / -62.7 and flags it.
- 25 May 1834: campaign concludes **[A es.wikipedia: "La Campaña al Desierto finalizó el 25 de mayo de 1834"]**. The pool's lenses contradicted each other, geography giving 1833. The source also mentions the Napostá farewell without a date, so pairing Napostá with 25 May is an **[R]** inference, flagged in the stop.
- 16 February 1835: Quiroga assassinated at Barranca Yaco, Córdoba **[A es.wikipedia]**. **No stop made:** no lens carries a coordinate for it, and inventing one is worse than folding the event into the campa it explains.
- 7 March 1835: the Legislatura grants the suma del poder público. 28 March: plebiscite, 9,713 for and 7 against. 13 April: the Sala names him governor **[A es.wikipedia for all three]**. **The pool conflated these into one act dated 13 April; en.wikipedia dates the term from 7 March.** The journey carries all three in one stop dated 7 March.
- 16 April 1835: María Remedios del Valle put on the army's inactive roster as sargento mayor by his decree; she takes his surname and signs as Doña Remedios Rosas until her death, 8 November 1847 **[A maria_remedios_del_valle.journey.json]**.

## 5. The long government (1835-1845)

- 28 March 1838: the French blockade begins **[A es.wikipedia, "Bloqueo francés del Río de la Plata": "miércoles 28 de marzo de 1838"]**. **The pool's chronology lens dated it 28 February 1839, wrong by nearly a year, contradicting its own geography lens.** Lifted 29 October 1840 **[A en.wikipedia]**.
- 1838-1852: Carlos María de Alvear serves as Rosas's minister plenipotentiary in Washington, defending the government to five US presidents **[A alvear.journey.json]**.
- 20 October 1838: Encarnación Ezcurra dies **[A es.wikipedia, "Encarnación Ezcurra"]**. **The pool pinned her death at the Palermo caserón, which did not yet exist.** The cortege ran from the Fuerte to San Francisco and she was buried at the Recoleta, so the city-centre coordinate is the anchor. Rosas afterwards takes María Eugenia Castro into the household **[A en.wikipedia]**.
- c. 1838 onward: the Caserón de San Benito is built at Palermo, begun after her death and continuing into the mid-1840s, at the eastern corner of today's Av. General Sarmiento and Av. del Libertador **[A es.wikipedia]**.
- 31 March 1839, Pago Largo: Pascual Echagüe of Entre Ríos destroys Genaro Berón de Astrada, brought down with boleadoras and lanced on the field **[A es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Pago Largo"]**. **The pool's coordinate -28.05 / -58.7 is roughly 200 km out of position**; the field is near Curuzú Cuatiá, approximately -29.79 / -58.05, used here.
- June 1839: the Maza conspiracy uncovered; Manuel Vicente Maza killed in the Sala de Representantes, those implicated shot **[A en.wikipedia, es.wikipedia]**. Month only.
- October 1841: Lavalle killed in Jujuy **[A en.wikipedia, month only]**. Folded into the Pago Largo campa to stay under the stop ceiling.
- 1843-1851: Oribe besieges Montevideo from the Cerrito de la Victoria. **Sourcing seam:** the es.wikipedia article on the Cerrito documents the hill but does not narrate the siege, which comes from the general biography.

## 6. The rivers and the chains (1845-1851)

- 27 March 1845, India Muerta: **the pool tagged this [R] and credited "tropas orientales".** Upgraded to **[A es.wikipedia, "Batalla de India Muerta (1845)"]**, attribution corrected: Urquiza commanded, with roughly 4,000 Entre Ríos federals and 2,000 Uruguayan blancos, against Rivera, who fled into Brazil with six to ten thousand followers. Pin approximate to the arroyo in northern Rocha.
- 17 September 1845: the Anglo-French blockade begins **[A en.wikipedia]**.
- 1844: San Martín at Grand Bourg offers his services to Rosas against the blockade and in his Paris testament leaves him the sabre of the Andes **[A san_martin.journey.json]**.
- 20 November 1845, Vuelta de Obligado: **the pool stated the outcome backwards.** It was a tactical Anglo-French victory; the fleet broke the chains and forced the passage. It is an Argentine victory only in the diplomatic sense, the cost leading the two powers to recognize sovereignty over the interior rivers. Mansilla commanded, with batteries under Alzogaray, Eduardo Brown, Palacios and Thorne **[A es.wikipedia, "Batalla de la Vuelta de Obligado"]**. **The pool carried two different wrong coordinates for this one site**; the Spanish article gives -33.5922 / -59.8072, used for both battle and monument.
- 15 July 1847 Arana-Southern, Britain ends hostilities; 12 June 1848 Arana-Lepredour, France lifts its blockade; 1850, all of it over **[A en.wikipedia]**. One stop.
- 18 August 1848: Camila O'Gorman and Ladislao Gutiérrez shot at Santos Lugares on Rosas's personal order, without trial **[A en.wikipedia, "Camila O'Gorman", citing John Lynch]**. **No coordinate for Santos Lugares in the pool**; an approximate -34.57 / -58.56 is supplied from the named locality and flagged. Widely circulated details the pool does not carry, her age and her pregnancy, are kept out of the campa.
- 18 August 1851: declares war on the Empire of Brazil, opening the Platine War **[A en.wikipedia]**.

## 7. Caseros and the flight (1852)

- 3 February 1852: defeated by Urquiza's allied army at Caseros **[A both Wikipedias]**.
- **Coordinate conflict, unresolved in favour of atlas coherence.** es.wikipedia sites the battle at El Palomar, -34.6028 / -58.6122; the corpus carries Caseros at -34.7833 / -58.5167 in `sarmiento.journey.json`, 20 km away. Canonical pins are inherited byte-identical, so the journey uses the Sarmiento pin. If the atlas reconciles these, both journeys move together.
- 3 February 1852, Hueco de los Sauces (today Plaza Garay): writes his resignation **[A es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Caseros"]**. **Quote correction:** the pool gave "Creo haber cumplido con mi deber para con mis conciudadanos y compañeros." The source reads "Creo haber llenado mi deber con mis conciudadanos", used in the journey.
- 3 February 1852: sheltered by the British consul Robert Gore; embarks on HMS Centaur at the port that February **[A es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Caseros"]**, month only.
- 1852: properties confiscated, condemned to death in absentia, declared a traitor **[A en.wikipedia]**, year only.
- 26 April 1852: lands at Plymouth, received with twenty-one guns **[A en.wikipedia]**. **The pool's geography lens still carried latitude -50.3714, which puts the landing in the South Atlantic 1,500 km east of the Falklands; its own note diagnosed the sign error and the value was never fixed.** The afterlife lens has 50.3755 / -4.1427, used here.

## 8. The exile (1852-1877)

- From 1852: tenant of a farm at Swaythling near Southampton, employing local labourers paid above the going rate **[A en.wikipedia]**. Two pool coordinates, 50.9412 / -1.3856 and 50.9515 / -1.3809; the journey uses the first. **Quote correction:** the pool truncated him as "living in modest circumstances." The source ends "...as you see." Restored.
- 22 October 1852: Manuela Rosas marries Máximo Terrero in a Catholic church in Southampton **[A es.wikipedia, "Manuela Rosas"]**. The church is unidentified, so the pin is central Southampton. **Gap.**
- 14 March 1877: dies at Southampton of pneumonia after a walk in cold weather, aged 83 **[A both Wikipedias]**. **The pool gave 84 and claimed the sources disagreed. They do not; both give 83.** He died sixteen days short of his eighty-fourth birthday.
- March 1877: private funeral mass, burial in Southampton Old Cemetery **[A en.wikipedia]**, no day given. Two pool coordinates, 50.9186 / -1.4222 and 50.9209 / -1.4184; the journey uses the first.

## 9. The afterlife (1877-1989)

- 17 September 1898: Manuela Rosas de Terrero dies in London at eighty-one, never having returned **[A es.wikipedia, "Manuela Rosas"]**.
- Night of 2 to 3 February 1899: the caserón dynamited by order of the intendente Adolfo Bullrich, timed so nothing stood at dawn on the anniversary of Caseros **[A es.wikipedia, "Caserón de Rosas"]**. **This corrects the pool, which said it was demolished after Caseros in 1852**; it stood forty-seven further years.
- The Rosas monument in Parque Tres de Febrero stands on the site of the caserón's Maestranza building, demolished 1924, raised roughly a century after the Sarmiento monument inaugurated on the same grounds on 25 May 1900 **[A es.wikipedia, "Caserón de Rosas"]**. **Gap: sculptor and inauguration date unknown**, the dedicated article 404 in both title forms. The journey dates it only as "a century after 1900" and pins it to the caserón site.
- 1880s to 1930s: revisionismo histórico reworks Rosas from condemned dictator into nationalist hero; academic history largely rejects the rehabilitation **[A en.wikipedia]**.
- **Quote correction:** Dusenberry (1961) was truncated mid-argument. Full: "a negative memory in Argentina. He left behind him the black legend of Argentine history, a legend which Argentines in general wish to forget." The original prints a dash where this rendering has a comma; the substitution is declared in the journey's `quote_source`.
- 20 November declared Day of Sovereignty 1974, a national holiday 2010; a sculpture bearing Rosas's image stands at the Vuelta de Obligado monument **[A en.wikipedia, "Vuelta de Obligado" and "Juan Manuel de Rosas"]**. He has also appeared on Argentine currency and postage, contested **[A en.wikipedia]**.
- 30 September 1989: exhumed from Southampton Old Cemetery and reburied in the family vault at the Recoleta under Menem, framed as reconciliation **[A en.wikipedia, corroborated by "Southampton Old Cemetery" and "Recoleta Cemetery"]**. **The pool's chronology lens tagged this [R] with an empty date while its own geography and afterlife lenses carried the exact date; the [R] is upgraded to [A].** The lenses differ in the fourth decimal of longitude, about four metres.
- **[R] and left as [R], none carried as a stop:** the published editions of his correspondence and archive, no title, editor or year confirmed; the folklore of buried treasure, cursed estates and ghosts on his former properties, no named collector or text; the pre-1989 pilgrimage of expatriates to the Southampton grave, no source naming pilgrims or dating visits.

## 11. Contradictions and gaps

Carried forward, not adjudicated: (1) end of the first governorship, 17 December 1832 against 5 December against the pool's unsupported 18 December; (2) start of the second term, 7 March 1835 (the suma) against 13 April (the naming), both acts real; (3) Caseros, -34.6028 / -58.6122 against the corpus pin; (4) Swaythling and (5) Southampton Old Cemetery, two pins each; (6) Recoleta, -58.3927 against -58.3931; (7) the Río Colorado camp, three points, none matching its own stated referent; (8) whether the 1820 enlistment as the Fifth Regiment **[R]** is a separate event from the 1819 organization of the Colorados **[A]**.

Gaps: no coordinate anywhere in the pool for Barranca Yaco, Santos Lugares or the city of Santa Fe (the last two supplied approximately and flagged, Barranca Yaco not made a stop); the church of Manuelita's marriage unidentified; sculptor and inauguration date of both the Palermo and the Obligado monuments unknown; the repatriation date resting on the biography article alone. No primary Rosas document was reached: the base is encyclopaedic apart from Darwin and Sarmiento, read directly.

## 12. Quotes: used and held

Seven of forty-four stops carry a quote, limited by how little of the man's own voice the sources print: two of Darwin's eight passages, two of Sarmiento's six, and the only two lines of Rosas himself in the pool, both corrected against the pool's wording. The other ten are held for the Spanish edition. **The apostrophe "Rosas! Rosas! I bow before thy mighty wisdom" is Sarmiento's irony, not a Rosas utterance, and is flagged so nobody later mistakes it for one.**
