# Max Horkheimer (1895-1973): research report

*Written 2026-08-25 from the verified research pool for slug `max_horkheimer`. Companion artifact: `max_horkheimer.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or dossier-only assertion not independently confirmed. Where sources disagree, both are printed and neither is adjudicated.

## 1. Zuffenhausen and the textile house (1895-1918)

Born **14 February 1895** at Zuffenhausen, a separate town north of Stuttgart, annexed only in 1931; only son of textile manufacturer Moses/Moritz Horkheimer and Babette née Lauchheimer, in an affluent Jewish house **[A: Frankfurt UB archival chronology; Stanford; Wikipedia]**. No birth-house address exists in anything reachable; the pin marks the town centre and says so.

Leaves the Gymnasium before the Abitur in 1910/11 and enters the family works, trainee then junior manager; meets **Friedrich Pollock**, and the friendship runs to Pollock's death in 1970 **[A: Frankfurt UB]**. No factory address recovered. Commercial training travel through Belgium, France and England **[A as to countries only]**; the pool proposed **Manchester** as a stand-in English stop and admitted in its own note that Manchester is not documented, so **I dropped that stop** rather than pin an invented city and folded the countries into the Stuttgart campa.

**The war: contradiction, not resolved.** German Wikipedia has him conscripted spring 1917, spending months in a **Munich sanatorium** with a lung illness, discharged spring 1918 **on medical grounds**. English Wikipedia has him **avoiding service**, rejected on medical grounds, **failing an army physical** before enrolling in spring 1919. **[A on both sides: de.wikipedia and en.wikipedia, 'Max Horkheimer']**. Both appear in the stop's `date_confidence`; the campa carries the German version as the specific one and names the English dissent in its own sentence. No unit, diagnosis, or exact date is established anywhere reachable. The pool cites **John Abromeit's** caution against overstating the draft-avoidance reading; Abromeit was not himself retrieved **[R]**.

## 2. The late student (1919-1926)

External Abitur in Munich; enrols at LMU spring 1919 in philosophy, psychology, economics, with Pollock **[A]**. **Freiburg**, winter 1920/21 through summer 1921, with Pollock, hearing **Husserl** and **Heidegger** **[A: Frankfurt UB; Wikipedia]**; the asymmetry is stated honestly, since `martin_heidegger.journey.json` does not mention Horkheimer and no class register was consulted. **Doctorate 1922**, *Zur Antinomie der teleologischen Urteilskraft*, under **Hans Cornelius**, after an abandoned Gestalt-psychology project; **habilitation 1925**, *Über Kants Kritik der Urteilskraft als Bindeglied…*, again under Cornelius; Privatdozent 1926 **[A: Stanford; Frankfurt UB]**. **Marriage 1926, Kronberg im Taunus**, to **Rosa Christine Riekher ("Maidon")**, his father's former secretary, Christian, over his parents' objections **[A: LEO-BW, "1926 in Cronberg am Taunus"]**.

**The Toller arrest. [R]** Mistaken for the revolutionary writer **Ernst Toller** during the Bavarian crisis and briefly detained, no lasting charge. Stated as fact in Wikipedia after the standard biographies, but **no arrest file, date, site, or duration has been located**, the pool's own gloss being that the evidentiary chain "terminates in later biographical narration." Carried as tradition, with the missing police register stated in the campa itself and not only in the hedge field.

> **Contradiction, not resolved.** English and German Wikipedia both make Maidon **eight years his senior, born 1887**. LEO-BW gives **1897**, two years younger. The pool's geography note originally preferred LEO-BW; the verified pool reversed that, reading 1897 as a probable transposition. The journey prints 1887 as the majority reading and LEO-BW's 1897 as the lone outlier, in campa and hedge field both. **No civil certificate was consulted by anyone in this chain.**

## 3. The chair and the program (1930-1932)

**The Institute is not his.** Founded 1923 and financed by **Felix Weil** out of *Weil Hermanos y Cia*, his father Hermann Weil's Buenos Aires grain house (1898); the building opened 22 June 1924 under **Carl Grünberg**, architect **Franz Roeckle** **[A: Historical Materialism interview with Hans-Peter Gruber; de.wikipedia IfS; IfS centenary page]**.

**First Marxist Work Week: corrected, then dropped as a stop.** The pool placed it at **Ilmenau**. It was held at **Geraberg bei Arnstadt, Thuringia**, in a railway hotel run by the communist Friedrich Henne, from **20 May 1923**, over Pentecost, eight days; **Karl Korsch** conceived it, Weil financed it, **Richard Sorge** sent the invitations **[A: de.wikipedia; IfS centenary page]**. **Horkheimer is not documented as present**, and Geraberg is already a stop on `felix_weil.journey.json`, so it is not staged as a scene here and appears only as a `suggested_ref` on his 1930 stop.

**Chair and directorship 1930**: Grünberg has had a stroke, Pollock holds the post provisionally; the ministry appoints Horkheimer to the new chair in **July**, the directorship follows in **October** at Weil's instigation **[A: Stanford; Historical Materialism]**.

> **Contradiction, flagged.** Sources variously date the appointment 1929, 1930 or 1931; `felix_weil.journey.json` records the German date **1931-01-01** for "Horkheimer takes the chair" while English sources give 1930. The journey uses 1930-10-01 for ordering and names the disagreement.

**Inaugural lecture 24 January 1931**, *Die gegenwärtige Lage der Sozialphilosophie…* **[A: Stanford; IfS centenary; text at marxists.org]**. **Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung** founded and edited 1932 **[A]**. **Erich Fromm** entered the Institute 1930 to run social psychology on a Marx-and-Freud synthesis **[A: `erich_fromm.journey.json`]**; his Weimar worker study stayed unpublished for decades, but the archival history of that suppression was not retrieved **[R as to cause]**.

## 4. Driven out (1933-1934)

Sequence, all **[A: IfS history page; Frankfurt UB; Wikipedia]**: **January 1933**, SA occupy the Kronberg residence shared with Pollock; **March**, police close the Institute; **May**, the building passes to the Nazi student organisation; **July**, the Gestapo records formal closure for "subversive activities." Horkheimer is dismissed under anti-Jewish civil-service law and leaves for Switzerland. The Roeckle building is destroyed in Allied bombing in **1944**.

**Geneva**: directs from the **Société Internationale de Recherches Sociales**, the foundation into which Weil had moved his fortune and out of which the exile payroll was paid **[A: `felix_weil.journey.json`]**. No Geneva street address, **gap**. The claim that the *Zeitschrift* "appeared from Paris" in the mid-1930s is **dossier-only [R]**, not reconfirmed; it sits as a subordinate clause in the Geneva campa and gets no Paris stop, the canonical Paris pin being used only for the documented 1937 Benjamin trip.

***Dämmerung*, Zurich 1934**, as *Heinrich Regius* **[A: Frankfurt UB bibliography]**; whether he was in Zurich is not established, and the stop marks the publication and says so. **Columbia 1934**: Nicholas Murray Butler provides affiliation and premises at **429 West 117th Street** **[A]**. `felix_weil.journey.json` names Horkheimer, Pollock, Adorno, Marcuse, Löwenthal and Fromm crossing together, keeping name, director and Geneva endowment, with **Weil not travelling with them**; `erich_fromm.journey.json` dates Fromm's crossing **31 May 1934**.

## 5. Morningside Heights (1936-1940)

*Studien über Autorität und Familie* (1936), edited by Horkheimer, major contributions from Fromm and Marcuse, printed in Paris by Alcan; *Traditionelle und kritische Theorie* (1937), postscript co-written with Marcuse; *Die Juden und Europa* (1939); US citizenship 1940 **[A]**. **Benjamin, Paris 1937**: Institute stipend plus editorial pressure to revise **[A as to the relationship]**, venue not documented, **gap**. **Fromm leaves, 1939**, under a financial settlement, amid theoretical divergence and contracting funds **[A: Stanford; McLaughlin; NYPL finding aid]**; the further claim that **later Institute-centred histories deliberately minimised Fromm** is contested, and the pool's own interlock lens flags it unconfirmed, so the journey says instead that no single-cause account of the break holds up.

## 6. Los Angeles (1940-1947)

> **Three corrections applied.** (1) **Year off by one**: the pool dated the move **1941**; en.wikipedia has him receiving citizenship and moving to Pacific Palisades in **1940**, and 1941 contradicted the pool's own geography span of 1940-1949. (2) **The émigré roll-call**: the pool put **Brecht** and **Schoenberg** in Pacific Palisades; Brecht lived in Santa Monica, Schoenberg in Brentwood. The district holds **Thomas Mann**, **Feuchtwanger** (Villa Aurora), **Adorno**, Vicki Baum, Emil Ludwig, Herbert Zipper **[A: en.wikipedia]**, and the campa puts each man in his own suburb. (3) **Coordinates**: **34.0481 / -118.5269**, not the pool's -118.5171, some 900 m too far east.

Drafting 1942-1944 daily with Adorno, with **Gretel Adorno** typing and keeping notes, her contribution acknowledged for the first time in the **1969 Fischer reissue preface** **[A]**. Mimeograph 1944 as *Philosophische Fragmente*; Columbia lectures on reason 1944, published 1947 as *Eclipse of Reason* **[A]**. **AJC, 1943(?)-1949**, scientific direction of the antisemitism research department, planning *Studies in Prejudice* **[R as to start date: sources give 1942 and 1943/44, unreconciled; the pin marks the AJC's historic Manhattan headquarters area, not a confirmed office]**.

> **Correction: wrong canal.** The pool put Querido on the **Singel**, pinned 52.3728 / 4.8907. Querido's exile house was **Keizersgracht 333** (de.wikipedia), about **52.3697 / 4.8836**, some 600 m off; "Singel" almost certainly leaked in from *Singel Uitgeverijen*, the group Querido joined **in 2014**, an anachronism of eighty years. The stop declares itself as the book's place of publication, not his travel.

## 7. The twice-rebuilt Institute (1948-1960)

April 1948, Rockefeller-supported first postwar return trip **[R as to planning venue: none documented; the pin marks the Rockefeller Center area]**. 1949, return and restoration of the chair **[A]**. **14 November 1951**, Institute reopens on the Senckenberganlage with money from **John J. McCloy's** High Commission fund, the city and private donors **[A: Wikipedia IfS; date and pin inherited from `felix_weil.journey.json`]**. 1951-1953 rector, the first Jewish rector in the university's postwar history **[A]**. 1953 Goethe Plaque, 1960 honorary citizenship **[A: award list]**. Mid-1950s Chicago visiting professorships **[A as to the fact]**, semesters not established, **gap**; folded into the retirement campa rather than given a stop, to hold the file at 45.

**1954 Mannesmann workforce study. [R]** Reported in Institute institutional histories; **the primary contract record was not retrieved in any pass**. Wilhelm Zangen's rearmament and forced-labour record is not in doubt, but the claim that the study's purpose was to forestall socialist organising is an **interpretation, not a demonstrated clause**, and the journey says so in those words.

> Three contradictions left open. **Adorno handover**: 1958, 1959, or "in stages." **Goethe Plaque**: 1953 (archive award list) vs 1951 (popular sources); archive preferred, dissent printed. **Montagnola move**: 1957 (Deutsche Biographie/LEO-BW) vs 1959 (Frankfurt UB short chronology); 1957 used for ordering, both named.

## 8. Montagnola, the late positions, death (1957-1973)

*Theismus und Atheismus* 1963 **[A: marxists.org]**. 1969: Maidon dies, **Adorno** dies the same year; 1970: **Pollock** dies **[A]**. **Maidon's place of death is not established**, stated as a gap in the campa itself.

***Die Sehnsucht nach dem ganz Anderen***, interview with **Hellmut Gumnior**, 1970. The pool gave the place of publication as "West Germany"; it was **Hamburg**, **Furche-Verlag** (Frankfurt UB bibliography), and the stop names Hamburg. The book states his sympathy with **Paul VI** against artificial contraception, on the ground that the pill would instrumentalise sexuality and extinguish love: documented, much criticised, and stated flat in the campa.

1970 Stuttgart Citizens' Medal, 1971 Hamburg Lessing Prize **[A: award list]**, no venues confirmed, **gap**. **Death: Nuremberg, 7 July 1973, aged 78** **[A]**; **no cause of death and no clinic established**, and the "routine medical examination" account is labelled unverified. **Burial: Jewish cemetery, Papiermühlestrasse 112, Bern-Wankdorf**, beside Maidon and near his parents' graves from their own Swiss exile, on a written request to the cemetery commission; **Pollock lies in the same ground** **[A: de.wikipedia, 'Max Horkheimer' and 'Jüdischer Friedhof Bern']**.

> **Correction: the worst positional error in the pool.** The geography lens pinned the burial at **46.953 / 7.4238**, the **Bremgartenfriedhof** area in western Bern, and guessed the grave was in "the Jewish section" of that complex. It is a **separate cemetery**, opened 5 September 1871, at **46.9678 / 7.4711** in Bern-Wankdorf, about **3.6 km** away; the pool's own afterlife lens already had the right figure, so the two lenses contradicted each other. The journey uses **46.96782 / 7.4712**.

## 9. Afterward

Two English *Dialectic of Enlightenment*: **Cumming** (1972; from 1979 the Continuum/Verso reissues reversed the author order to put Adorno first) and **Jephcott** (Stanford 2002, from the *Gesammelte Schriften* text) **[A]**. **Max-Horkheimer-Strasse**, formerly Affensteiner Weg, Campus Westend, addresses assigned 2015 **[A]**; a second at Wuppertal **[R, passing mention only]**; Gedenktafel on **Westendstrasse** **[A]**; **Max-Horkheimer-Kabinett**, Stadtbibliothek Stuttgart, level 3, section "Leben" **[A, cited from the Horkheimer article, the library's own not mentioning it]**. ## 10. Interlocks

Named in a campa, each with a direct or one-directional relation rooted in Horkheimer's own life: **`felix_weil`**, who founded and financed the Institute, had him made director in 1930, and whose Geneva foundation paid the exile payroll · **`erich_fromm`**, Institute colleague from 1930, ran its social psychology, crossed to Columbia with it, broke with him in 1939 · **`martin_heidegger`**, whose Freiburg lectures he attended in 1920/21 (one-directional; Heidegger's file does not mention him) · **`kant`**, subject of both the 1922 dissertation and the 1925 habilitation, the antinomy of teleological judgment seeding the later question about instrumental reason · **`freud`**, whose psychoanalysis is the instrument the Institute's social psychology runs on, and over whose drive theory the Fromm break partly turns · **`max_weber`**, from whose *Zweckrationalität* the instrumental reason of *Traditional and Critical Theory* and *Eclipse of Reason* is taken.

**Deliberately not named**, the only link being shared geography or period, which the map already shows: `hermann_hesse` (Montagnola until 1962, no source documents a meeting), `martin_buber` (Frankfurt Lehrhaus milieu, no file states they met), `gustav_landauer` (the same Munich crisis of 1919, no documented relation), `paul_klee` (the *Angelus Novus* was **Benjamin's**; the relation is to Benjamin, who has no journey file).
