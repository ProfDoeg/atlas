# John F. Kennedy: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. John Fitzgerald Kennedy, 1917-1963, 35th President of the United States. Slug: john_f_kennedy.*

**Method.** Built only from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses); nothing was re-fetched from the web. Where the verification pass appended a correction, it is carried here and the corrected value is what the journey file uses.

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named in the pool. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, legend, or general-literature knowledge with no pooled source, mostly kept out of the journey file. **[C]** correction from the verification pass. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

---

## I. Brookline and the schools, 1917-1935

- Born 29 May 1917 at 83 Beals Street, Brookline, second of nine children of Joseph P. Kennedy Sr. and Rose Fitzgerald Kennedy **[A: JFK Library; Britannica; NPS]**. **[C]** Pool coordinates (42.3373, -71.1219) fall about 1.1 km south; the JFK National Historic Site is at **42.3469, -71.1233**. Edward Devotion and Dexter schools, Brookline, c. 1923-1927; Riverdale Country School, Bronx, 1927-1930 **[A]**.
- Canterbury School, New Milford, September 1930: first boarding school, the only Catholic school he ever attends, the year cut short by illness the pool does not name **[A: Wikipedia; Hamilton, *JFK: Reckless Youth*]**. The usual account (appendicitis) is **[R]**, unused. **[C]** Pool coordinates (41.6702, -73.4133) sit about 9 km north, outside New Milford; the school is at **41.5864, -73.4126**.
- Choate, September 1931 to June 1935; graduates 64th of 112 **[A: Wikipedia]**; founds the "Muckers" **[A: JFK Library; Hamilton]**. The membership count of thirteen is **[R]**, unused.

## II. London, Princeton, Harvard, 1935-1940

The phase where the pool contradicted itself, and the correction matters more than the facts corrected.

- The geography lens gave Princeton first (October 1935, withdrawal for jaundice), then: "Recovering from illness, Kennedy briefly studies under Harold Laski at the London School of Economics."
- **[C]** The sequence is inverted and the illness framing backwards. Kennedy sailed 25 September 1935, enrolled at LSE under Laski, was hospitalised with jaundice within a week without effectively attending, and sailed home about three weeks after arriving. **Only then** did he matriculate at Princeton, 26 October 1935 (LSE History, "The almost alumnus"). The illness ended the LSE attempt rather than preceding it. LSE coordinates 51.5144, -0.1160 are correct.
- Princeton: withdraws after roughly two months, jaundice again **[A: JFK Library / Britannica]**. Harvard from September 1936; B.A. in government *cum laude* 1940; thesis published the same year as *Why England Slept*; audits business courses at Stanford one term, autumn 1940 **[A: Wikipedia; JFK Library]**. Embassy residence, Prince's Gate, London, 1938-1939, while his father is ambassador; travels Europe and watches the descent to war **[A: JFK Library; Hamilton]**.
- **Gap:** nothing pooled on the ambassador's appeasement politics, the father-son break over it, or the book's reception. All **[R]**, unused.

## III. The Navy and Blackett Strait, 1941-1945

- Naval Reserve 24 September 1941; ensign 26 October; trains at Melville, Rhode Island, 1942 (year only in the pool); commands PT-109 from Tulagi, 24 April 1943 **[A: Wikipedia; US Navy]**. On 2 August 1943 the *Amagiri* rams and splits PT-109 in Blackett Strait; two of the crew die; Kennedy tows an injured crewman by a life-jacket strap held in his teeth **[A: Navy action report; Donovan, *PT 109*]**.
- **[C]** Four Solomons coordinate sets were wrong by 7 to 22 km, each placing the point in open water or on the wrong island:
  - Blackett Strait: -8.1167, 157.2 → **-8.1167, 157.000**
  - Plum Pudding / Kasolo / Kennedy Island: -8.0578, 156.9469 → **-8.1128, 156.9056**
  - Olasana: -8.0644, 156.955 → **-8.1320, 156.9087**
  - Naru: -8.0489, 157.1 → **-8.1388, 156.9173**
  The corrected set puts Naru about 1.2 km from Olasana, which the swim narrative requires. The uncorrected set did not.
- Found on Olasana by the Solomon Islander scouts **Biuku Gasa and Eroni Kumana**, working for the coastwatchers; the coconut-shell message is cut on Naru and carried by them **[A: Donovan; JFK Library]**.
- **Date discipline:** the pool dates the sinking and the swim to Plum Pudding to 2 August **[A]** but gives only "1943-08" for Olasana and Naru. The file uses 4 and 5 August on the standard rescue chronology and says so in `date_confidence` **[R for the days]**.
- PT-59 rescue 2 November 1943; relieved of command 18 November; Navy and Marine Corps Medal; retires 1 March 1945 **[A]**. Joseph P. Kennedy Jr. killed 12 August 1944, his explosives-laden bomber detonating over England **[A: Britannica / JFK Library]**. No coordinates in the pool for the death or for where the news reached the family; the file pins the Hyannis Port compound as reconstruction.

## IV. The House, the Senate, the body, 1946-1957

- House seat for Massachusetts's 11th district November 1946, taken 3 January 1947, three terms; Senate seat November 1952 over the incumbent Henry Cabot Lodge Jr., the campaign organised from Boston largely by Robert Kennedy, taken 3 January 1953 **[A: JFK Library]**.
- Marries Jacqueline Lee Bouvier at St Mary's, Newport, 12 September 1953, reception at Hammersmith Farm **[A: JFK Library; History.com; Time]**. Guest counts and the bride's newspaper job are **[R]**, unused. Spinal surgery, New York, October 1954 **[A]**.
- **[C]** The pool said *Profiles in Courage* was "published the following year." Publication was **January 1956**, two years after the surgery and after a third operation on 10 February 1955; the Pulitzer for Biography followed in **1957**.
- **Contradiction, unresolved, on authorship.** The book appears under Kennedy's name and wins the Pulitzer **[A]**; Ted Sorensen states in his 2008 memoir that he wrote first drafts of most chapters and helped choose the wording of many sentences, with research by Jules Davids **[A: Wikipedia; Straight Dope]**; Drew Pearson charged in 1957 that Kennedy was the only man to win a Pulitzer for a ghostwritten book **[A]**. Nothing settles the proportion; the file states the drafting claim with its date and stops there.
- Loses the vice-presidential nomination to Kefauver, Chicago, August 1956 **[A]**, cut for length. Caroline Bouvier Kennedy born 27 November 1957, New York City **[A]**.
- **Coordinate gap:** the pool gives "New York City" for both the surgery and the birth, with no hospital coordinates. The file uses a generic Manhattan pin (40.7128, -74.0060) and says so in `date_confidence`. This is its weakest pin.
- **Gap:** Addison's disease. Nothing pooled on the diagnosis, the steroid regime, or the campaign's denials. A real hole in any medical account, left as a hole.

## V. 1960

- Nomination on the first ballot at Los Angeles, 13 July, Johnson selected; "New Frontier" acceptance at the Coliseum two days later **[A]**. First televised presidential debate against Nixon, 26 September, pinned by the pool at the WBBM-TV studios (41.8896, -87.6217), with Theodore White cited for its effect on the race **[A]**. Elected 8 November, "one of the closest elections in U.S. history" **[A]**; the pool gives no vote margin, so neither does the file. John F. Kennedy Jr. born 25 November **[A]**.
- **[R, flagged, unused]** The radio-versus-television scoring claim about that debate rests on thin contemporary polling and is contested; not pooled, not in the file.

## VI. Cuba, 1961-1962

- Inaugurated 20 January 1961 in bitter cold after a snowstorm; the address closes with "ask not what your country can do for you, ask what you can do for your country" **[A: American Presidency Project / JFK Library]**. Frost's poem and the fourteen-minute length are **[R]**, unused.
- Bay of Pigs, 17 April 1961 **[A: History.com; JFK Library]**. From the Castro file: Brigade 2506 lands, Castro directs the defence in person, air cover is withdrawn, about 1,200 prisoners later ransomed for baby food and medicine **[A: fidel_castro.journey.json]**. "Victory has 100 fathers and defeat is an orphan" belongs to the news conference of 21 April **[A]**; the file names that four-day gap in `quote_source`.
- Paris and Versailles, 31 May to 2 June 1961 **[A]**, folded into the Vienna stop. Vienna, "the roughest thing in his life" **[A: Britannica; Kempe, *Berlin 1961*]**: the chronology lens gives "1961-06", the geography lens "1961-06-03/04", and the file uses 3 June. Rice University, 12 September 1962, the moon commitment, quoted verbatim **[A: JFK Library]**.
- Missile crisis: U-2 photographs 16 October; quarantine address 22 October; Khrushchev agrees to withdraw 28 October, the US secretly agreeing to remove the Jupiters from Turkey **[A: State Department Office of the Historian]**. Khrushchev's letter of 26 October (the knot of war) and Kennedy's reply of 27 October are both pooled verbatim **[A: JFK Library CMC microsite; FRUS 1961-63 vol. VI]**. From the Castro file: Castro is consulted about neither the installation nor the removal and learns of the agreement from the radio **[A]**.
- **Gap:** civil rights. The pool reaches the 11 June 1963 address only by implication, through a note on the American University speech; nothing on Birmingham, Alabama, the March on Washington, or the bill. A file built from this pool under-represents the domestic presidency, and does.

## VII. June and July 1963

- **4 June 1963.** Kennedy signs Public Law 88-36, repealing the Silver Purchase Act of 1934, and the same day signs Executive Order 11110 **[A: Wikipedia; American Presidency Project]**. **[C]** The pool said Congress "passes" PL 88-36 on 4 June; Congress passed the bill in April and May, and 4 June is the signing.
- EO 11110 amends EO 10289 to delegate to the Treasury Secretary the authority to issue silver certificates during the transition away from silver toward Federal Reserve notes; the 1963 Economic Report states that "except for its use in coins, silver serves no useful monetary function." By March 1964 the Treasury stops redeeming certificates for silver dollars, and redemption ends entirely 24 June 1968 **[A]**.
- **Contradiction, flagged, left standing.** Jim Marrs, in *Crossfire*, and writers after him hold that EO 11110 was a covert move to strip the Federal Reserve of its currency-issuing power, and a motive for the assassination **[R: Marrs, per Wikipedia]**. PolitiFact, citing the biographer Ira Stoll, and economic historians generally find no evidence Kennedy intended to end the Fed, and note that the order *reduced* silver certificates in circulation, expanding rather than curbing Federal Reserve note dominance **[A: PolitiFact]**. The file states the order's content and direction, never the legend as fact.
- American University, 10 June, the "Strategy of Peace" address, two verbatim passages pooled and one carried **[A: American Presidency Project]**. Frankfurt, 25 June **[A]**, named in the Berlin campa rather than given a stop. West Berlin, Rathaus Schöneberg, 26 June, quoted verbatim **[A: JFK Library]**. New Ross and Dunganstown, 27 June **[A: JFK Library; RTÉ]**; the great-grandfather's name and emigration year are **[R]**, unused.
- **[C]** Vatican, 2 July 1963. Date, pope (Paul VI, elected days earlier) and coordinates are right, but the pool's "first sitting US president to be so received" is **false**: Wilson was received by Benedict XV in January 1919, Eisenhower by John XXIII in December 1959. Kennedy was the first *Roman Catholic* US president received by a pope, and shook hands rather than kissing the ring **[A: NPR; Religion News Service]**.
- **[C]** Patrick Bouvier Kennedy, born 7 August 1963, died 9 August of hyaline membrane disease **[A]**. The pool's place was wrong: delivered by emergency caesarean at **Otis Air Force Base Hospital, Bourne**, not Hyannis Port, and died at **Boston Children's Hospital**. No coordinates for either; the file pins the compound and states the correction in the campa.

## VIII. Dallas, 22 November 1963

- **[C]** Hotel Texas, Fort Worth. The pool dated this 21 November. Kennedy arrived the night of the 21st and slept in Suite 850, but the rain-soaked parking-lot speech and the Chamber of Commerce breakfast both took place on the **morning of 22 November**, and in that order; the pool had the sequence reversed as well **[A: National Archives *Prologue*; Sixth Floor Museum]**.
- Air Force One lands at Love Field; shots are fired from the sixth floor of the Texas School Book Depository as the motorcade passes Dealey Plaza at 12:30 pm; pronounced dead at Parkland at 1:00 pm; Johnson sworn in aboard Air Force One that afternoon **[A: Warren Commission]**. The body goes to Bethesda for an autopsy "later criticized as rushed and contested in forensic detail" **[A: Warren Commission; HSCA]**. **[C]** Bethesda coordinates: pool 38.9847, -77.0961, about 1.9 km south; the hospital at 8901 Rockville Pike is at **39.0017, -77.0947**.
- **[R, flagged, unused]** The autopsy failures usually cited (burned notes, missing photographs, pathologists lacking the Dallas trauma notes) are not pooled, and the Zapruder film does not appear in the pool at all; the file says only what the pool says.

## IX. Afterlife

- Lying in state in the Rotunda 24 November, an estimated 250,000 filing past; Requiem Mass at St Matthew's and burial at Arlington 25 November **[A: JFK Library; press]**. **[R, flagged]** The Lincoln catafalque of 1865 is standard but not pooled; the campa says only that the same rotunda held Lincoln's coffin.
- **[C]** The first eternal flame was improvised in about a day: a propane tiki torch from the Washington Gas Light Company, fed by lines run from a tank **200 yards** away, not 300 feet as the pool had it **[A: Wikipedia, JFK Eternal Flame]**.
- **Coordinate contradiction, flagged, not adjudicated.** Arlington: geography lens -77.0703, afterlife lens -77.0716, about 110 m apart; the file uses -77.0716 at both stops. Dealey Plaza: -96.8085 against -96.8083; the file uses -96.8085 so assassination and museum share a pin.
- Permanent gravesite: casket moved March 1967, opened 15 March, complete by 20 July; the flame burns at the centre of a five-foot circular granite stone from a nozzle whose constantly flashing spark relights it automatically **[A: History.com; JFK Library]**.
- **[C]** **John F. Kennedy Jr. is not buried at Arlington.** He was cremated, his ashes scattered at sea off Martha's Vineyard from the USS *Briscoe* on 22 July 1999 with Carolyn Bessette Kennedy's and Lauren Bessette's. At the gravesite are the president, Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis (d. 1994), the infant Patrick and an unnamed stillborn daughter, both reinterred December 1963 **[A: Britannica; Arlington]**.
- Runnymede: a symbolic acre by the Magna Carta meadow, memorial by Geoffrey Jellicoe, dedicated by Elizabeth II and Jacqueline Kennedy 14 May 1965; ceremonial only, the UK retaining sovereignty **[A: National Trust; Big Think]**.
- Camelot: Theodore White interviews Jacqueline Kennedy at Hyannis Port 29 November 1963; the *Life* piece runs 6 December with "one brief shining moment." White writes later that she shaped it and overruled his editors, fixing a frame before bitter accounts could be written; his transcripts go to the JFK Library, opened 1979, sealed until a year after her death **[A: Time; Washington Post]**.
- The Sixth Floor Museum opens 1989 in the former Depository, grassy-knoll legends persisting despite both official findings **[A: jfk.org]**. On 2 November 2021 hundreds of QAnon adherents gather at Dealey Plaza expecting John F. Kennedy Jr., dead since 1999, to reappear alive at 12:30 pm and announce Trump's reinstatement, on the prophecy of the gematria practitioner Michael Brian Protzman; nothing occurs and the crowd pivots to a Rolling Stones concert **[A: Snopes; Rolling Stone]**.

## X. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Named in campa text where the paths genuinely cross:

- **Fidel Castro** (`fidel_castro`): Bay of Pigs; the missile crisis, his file recording that he was consulted about neither the installation nor the removal; the address of 23 November 1963.
- **Meyer Lansky** (`meyer_lansky`): the Havana casinos expropriated 1959-1960, the rupture hardened into the embargo. At the Havana stop.
- **Andrew Jackson** (`andrew_jackson`) and **Abraham Lincoln** (`abraham_lincoln`): the central-banking thread the QUEUE entry names. Jackson's 1832 Bank veto and Lincoln's Legal Tender Act are the earlier fixed points, EO 11110 the twentieth-century one. Both at the 1963-06-04 stop, Lincoln again at the Rotunda.
- **Robert Oppenheimer** (`robert_oppenheimer`), **Enrico Fermi** (`fermi`) and **John von Neumann** (`john_von_neumann`), all at the Fermi Award stop: Kennedy selects Oppenheimer for the award and is killed ten days before the ceremony, Johnson presenting the medal on 2 December 1963, a date two files carry independently; the Medal of Freedom created in 1963 supersedes the Truman-era medal Eisenhower gave von Neumann in 1956.
- **Richard Feynman** (`richard_feynman`): his file pins the 1986 Challenger disaster at Kennedy Space Center, renamed for Kennedy after his death. At the Rice stop.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical:** the White House, **38.8977, -77.0365**, shared across five Lincoln stops, used for the Bay of Pigs, the quarantine, the letters of 27 October, EO 11110 and the Fermi Award; Havana, **23.1136, -82.3666**, shared by the Castro and Lansky files. The Kaaba, Temple Mount, Paris and Buenos Aires pins named in the brief have no Kennedy crossing, as the interlock lens found.

**Structural notes.** 45 stops in 8 segments, travel order. Quotes appear at 9 of them, fewer than the usual third: the quote lens returned thirteen items covering nine occasions, and none was invented or moved. Hedges live in `date_confidence`. Stops cut for length: Dexter, Riverdale, Stanford, Melville, PT-59, the seat-takings of 1947 and 1953, the 1956 convention, Caroline's birth, Paris, Frankfurt, St Matthew's, the library.
