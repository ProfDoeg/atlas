# William Holman Hunt (1827-1910): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, working file. Slug `william_holman_hunt`. Compiled 2026-08-11 from a verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses), with the verifier's corrections applied in place and the surviving contradictions left standing.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried by a general biographical account rather than a document reached this session. Where two sources disagree, both are printed and neither is adjudicated. Where nothing was reachable, the gap is named as a gap.

**One standing warning.** Hunt wrote his own history. *Pre-Raphaelitism and the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood* (Macmillan, 1905) is the principal narrative source for the Brotherhood's founding, and it is a memoir composed fifty-seven years after the event by the last surviving founder. Wikipedia characterises the late writings as an attempt to control the interpretation of his own work and the Brotherhood's history **[A: Wikipedia, "William Holman Hunt"]**. Every claim tracing back to the autobiography alone carries that weight, several of the quotes below included.

## 1. Cheapside and the clerk's desk (1827-1844)

- Born in Wood Street, Cheapside, City of London, son of the warehouse manager William Hunt and Sarah née Hobman, 2 April 1827 **[A: Wikipedia, "William Holman Hunt"]**.
- The name "Holman." The pool's first draft claimed a clerk duplicated his father's middle name. **That mechanism is wrong and has been corrected.** Wikipedia states that Hunt "adopted the name Holman instead of Hobman when he discovered that a clerk had misspelled the name" after his baptism at St Mary the Virgin, Ewell. The misspelled word was therefore his **mother's maiden name** **[R: family tradition, not independently documented]**. The baptism date is not given in anything reached. **Gap.**
- Leaves school in his thirteenth year for clerical work: assistant to a surveyor and estate agent, then the London agent of **Richard Cobden**, a calico printer **[A: DNB 1912 supplement]**. The pool's "textile merchant" anonymised Cobden's agency and is corrected to name it.
- Admitted as a probationer to the Royal Academy Schools after repeated rejection, 1844; meets Millais there the same year **[A: Wikipedia]**. The Schools' location in the National Gallery building on Trafalgar Square is **[R: standard biographies; not verified this session]**.
- *Little Nell and Her Grandfather*, from Dickens, 1845 **[A]**. A first *Christ and the Two Marys*, 1847 **[A]**.

## 2. Gower Street and the Brotherhood (1848-1850)

- Shares lodgings on Cleveland Street, Fitzrovia, with Dante Gabriel Rossetti before the founding **[A: Wikipedia, "Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood"]**. Rossetti admires *The Eve of St Agnes* at the Academy exhibition and seeks Hunt out, 1848 **[A]**.
- The Brotherhood is founded at the Millais family house, 83 Gower Street, September 1848: Hunt, Millais, Dante Gabriel Rossetti, joined by William Michael Rossetti, James Collinson, Frederic George Stephens, Thomas Woolner. Seven members **[A: Wikipedia, "Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood"]**. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool gave 51.5219, -0.1308, about 130 m east of the building and off the street; 83 Gower Street (today the Arosfa Hotel) geocodes to **51.5222, -0.1327** **[A: nominatim.openstreetmap.org]**.
- *Rienzi* exhibited 1849, among the first public works signed PRB; *The Haunted Manor* the same year **[A]**. *The Germ* runs four issues January to April 1850 **[A]**.

## 3. The Hogsmill and the lantern (1851-1854)

- *The Hireling Shepherd* is painted on the banks of the Hogsmill at Ewell in **1851**, alongside Millais painting *Ophelia* at the same spot; a local woman the group called "the Coptic" models the shepherdess **[A: Wikipedia, "The Hireling Shepherd"]**. *Date corrected:* the chronology lens dated it to 1852 together with *Our English Coasts*; Wikipedia's works list gives the Shepherd 1851 and only *Our English Coasts* 1852. Splitting the entry also removes an internal contradiction with the geography lens.
- **Where the first *Light of the World* was painted is unresolved and left so here.** Wikipedia names three rival locations: a hut at **Worcester Park Farm**, the **Oxford University Press** garden, and a site **near Bethlehem** **[R]**. The pool presented Bethlehem as a separate, independently attested site; that framing was wrong, and it is re-tagged **[R]** as one limb of the same dispute.
- **The first version's dates are corrected.** The pool asserted a start in 1851. Wikipedia dates version one to 1849/1850-1854 and the Keble article says completed 1853. Restated: begun c. 1849-51, completed 1853, exhibited 1854 **[A]**.
- Works from a home studio at Prospect Place, Chelsea, later renumbered into Cheyne Walk **[A]**. *Our English Coasts* 1852 **[A]**. *The Awakening Conscience* 1853 **[A]**.
- **A misreading corrected.** The pool listed "a portrait of the 22-year-old Rossetti" for 1853. That is the picture's traditional title, *Portrait of Dante Gabriel Rossetti at 22 Years of Age*. Rossetti was born 12 May 1828 and was 25 in 1853; the title is not a statement of the sitter's age **[A: Wikipedia works list]**.
- Engaged to the model Annie Miller during the 1850s; the engagement fails **[A]**. No date is given for either the engagement or its end. **Gap.**

## 4. The first journey east (1854-1856)

Hunt travels to the Holy Land in search of "accurate topographical and ethnographical material" for biblical subjects, prompted by a personal religious crisis **[A: Wikipedia]**.

The **route out is almost entirely reconstruction.** The geography lens gives Paris, a packet from **Marseille**, landing at **Alexandria**, weeks in **Old Cairo** among the Coptic community, the **Giza** pyramids, and entry by the **Jaffa Gate**. Every one carries the same note: "general account of the 1854 journey; not independently confirmed this session" **[R]**. They are printed in the journey file with the hedge in `date_confidence`, not in the prose.

Attested, by contrast:

- He eventually builds his own house in Jerusalem and uses it as base and studio **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- He meets the millenarian preacher **Henry Wentworth Monk**, whose reading of the scapegoat as a figure of Christ shapes the painting's theology **[A: Wikipedia, "The Scapegoat (painting)"]**.
- He paints most of *The Scapegoat* on location at Sodom on the Dead Sea shore, the mountains of Edom beyond **[A: same, quoting Hunt's Royal Academy catalogue note]**.
- He returns to London, finishes *The Scapegoat*, touches it up into 1856 and exhibits it that year. Two versions exist: the smaller at Manchester, the larger at the Lady Lever Art Gallery, Port Sunlight **[A]**. **The return route is a gap.**
- *The Finding of the Saviour in the Temple* is begun in this period and spans several years **[A]**.

**Mar Elias coordinate corrected, twice.** The geography lens gave 31.7245, 35.1949 and the afterlife lens 31.7304, 35.1994 for the same monastery, wrong by roughly 1.9 km and 1.1 km. Mar Elias stands at **31.7347, 35.2108**, on a hill in south Jerusalem overlooking Bethlehem and the Herodium **[A: Wikipedia, "Mar Elias Monastery"]**. Both values are reconciled to that.

## 5. Florence, and the two sisters (1864-1875)

- *London Bridge on the Night of the Marriage of the Prince and Princess of Wales*, 1864 **[A]**.
- **Marriage date: a contradiction now resolved, the day still missing.** The Wikipedia summary gave 1861. The DNB states Hunt married "in 1865 to Fanny, daughter of George Waugh" **[A: DNB 1912 supplement]**. Wikipedia's 1861 is an error and is not carried forward. The commonly repeated 28 December 1865 could not be confirmed against any source reached, so the **year alone** is given. **Gap: the day.**
- The couple travel to Italy 1866; Fanny models for *Isabella* **[A]**. A son, Cyril Benoni Holman Hunt, is born in Florence, October 1866 **[R: standard biographies; not confirmed via a source fetched this session]**.
- **Cause of death corrected.** The chronology lens said Fanny died in childbirth. She did not. Wikipedia states she "died in December 1866 from fever after giving birth" in Florence: the death followed the birth by some weeks and was not a death in labour **[A: Wikipedia, "Isabella and the Pot of Basil"]**.
- Hunt cuts her tomb at Fiesole; it is placed in the English Cemetery, Piazzale Donatello, beside Elizabeth Barrett Browning's. He keeps a connection to St Mark's English Church, Via Maggio, and pays for a communion chalice in her memory **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- *Isabella and the Pot of Basil* and *The Birthday*, 1868 **[A]**. *The Shadow of Death*, 1873 **[A]**.
- Marries **Edith Waugh**, Fanny's younger sister, at Neuchâtel in November 1875, marriage to a deceased wife's sister being illegal under British law. It causes a rift with Thomas Woolner and with family members **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 6. The second and third journeys (1869-1878)

- **Second journey dates corrected.** The pool gave 1869-1872 for the Jerusalem residence and 1872 for the return. The DNB gives a landing at Jaffa in **autumn 1869** and a return in **1871** **[A: DNB 1912 supplement]**. Since *The Shadow of Death* is dated 1873, the corrected 1871 return sits better with the painting than 1872 did.
- Whether Hunt travelled to **Nazareth** during this stay, as against reconstructing its interior in his Jerusalem studio, is unresolved in everything reached. **Gap, left open.**
- Third Near Eastern journey, c. 1875-1878, with Edith, toward *The Triumph of the Innocents*, with recurring illness through the period; returns to England 1878 and completes the picture in London **[R: general account; neither the itinerary beyond Cairo and Jerusalem nor the return date confirmed]**.
- A memorial **stone bench**, cut with biblical verses in Hebrew, Greek, Arabic and English, was erected by Hunt's wife facing Mar Elias Monastery, at a site where he painted major works **[A: Wikipedia, "William Holman Hunt" and "Mar Elias Monastery", independently]**. **Gaps: the bench is undated, and which wife is not named.** Edith survived him and is the likelier candidate, but nothing reached says so.

## 7. Melbury Road and the third lantern (1884-1910)

- *Amaryllis* 1884 **[A]**. *May Morning on Magdalen Tower* 1890, Oxford **[A]**.
- The third, life-sized version of *The Light of the World* is painted 1900-1904, with **Edward Robert Hughes** assisting because of Hunt's advancing age and glaucoma **[A: Wikipedia, "The Light of the World (Hunt)"]**.
- Hunt moves his residence and studio to **18 Melbury Road**, Holland Park, 1903 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
  - *Coordinate corrected.* The pool carried three different values for this one address: 51.5011, -0.2059 (geography, three entries), 51.5008, -0.202 (blue plaque), and 51.5074, -0.1278 (death, a generic central-London point near Charing Cross). The house geocodes to **51.4992, -0.2022** **[A: nominatim.openstreetmap.org]**. All Melbury Road pins are standardised to that.
- Publishes *Pre-Raphaelitism and the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood* and is appointed to the Order of Merit by Edward VII, both 1905 **[A]**.
- The third version tours the world **1905 to 1907**, not the single year the pool gave, and Wikipedia calls it a world tour rather than strictly a British Empire tour **[A]**. The Australian viewing figure is reported as a period claim: "it was claimed that four-fifths of Australia's population viewed it." **The number is [R]; the tour is [A].**
- Dedicated at St Paul's 1908; the painting was given by **Charles Booth** **[A]**. *Wording corrected:* Booth was a shipowner and social reformer, a donor and patron, not "Hunt's colleague" and not a fellow artist.
- **Place of death settled.** The afterlife lens flagged Kensington versus Sonning-on-Thames as unresolved. The DNB closes it: Hunt "died at his residence, 18 Melbury Road, Kensington, on 7 Sept. 1910," aged 83 **[A: DNB 1912 supplement]**.
- **Cremation settled.** The lens hedged burial against cremation because St Paul's own history and collections material, checked directly, says nothing about Hunt's grave, ashes, or memorial; that silence stands as an institutional gap. The DNB states he was **cremated at Golders Green and his ashes interred in the crypt of St Paul's** **[A]**. The claim no longer rests on a single Wikipedia sentence.

## 8. Afterlives

- The first version of *The Light of the World* is at **Keble College, Oxford**, donated by Martha Combe in 1873 after her husband Thomas Combe's death, held in the library from 1878, moved to the purpose-built side chapel 1892-95 **[A: Wikipedia, "Keble College, Oxford"]**. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool gave 51.761, -1.265, about 600 m north-west of the college; Keble is at **51.7589, -1.2577**.
- The second, smaller version (1851-1856) is at **Manchester Art Gallery**, purchased 1912 **[A]**. A blue plaque was placed at 18 Melbury Road in 1923 **[A]**.
- Hunt's granddaughter **Diana Holman-Hunt** published *My Grandfather, His Wives and Loves* in 1969 **[A: Wikipedia, "Diana Holman-Hunt"]**. That the book shaped a later, more intimate popular image distinct from the reputation he cultivated in his own autobiography is **[R]**: an inference from the book's subject, not stated in any entry fetched.
- The Brotherhood was dramatised in the BBC's *The Love School* (1975) and *Desperate Romantics* (BBC Two, 21 July to 25 August 2009) **[A]**. *Correction:* Rafe Spall did not play a "Hunt-adjacent Brotherhood role." He played **Hunt himself**, alongside Aidan Turner as Rossetti and Samuel Barnett as Millais. Wikipedia records the 2009 series as "greatly influenced by" the 1975 serial **[A: Wikipedia, "Desperate Romantics"]**.

## 9. Quotes: what is verbatim and what is not

Six quotes were fetched directly from the Internet Archive full-text scan of *Pre-Raphaelitism and the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood* (`preraphaelitism01hunt_djvu.txt`), volume 1, Preface. Those are **[A]** and are used verbatim.

Three further lines are **[R]**, flagged because they are easy to mistake for narrative prose: "A newspaper publishes the secret of the initials P.R.B."; "Rossetti enthusiastic over my picture. Proposes to come and see me. He visits my studio. Confesses being disheartened at his position."; "Am driven to abandon picture and determine to paint 'The Scapegoat'." All three are **chapter-heading synopsis lines** from the contents or running headers, not confirmed as continuous prose; the surrounding narrative was not retrieved. The second is telegraphic in the original, with dashes between clauses, rendered with periods per house style.

**One quote was truncated and is repaired.** The pool carried "To whom but the artist is relegated the task of giving a tangible and worthy image of the national body and mind?" The scan has no question mark at that point. The original reads: "To whom but the artist is relegated the task of giving a tangible and worthy image of the national body and mind, who else may select and uphold the visible sign of that beauty in his race which is most heroic physically and mentally ?" The full sentence is used in the journey file.

## 10. Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. **Where the first *Light of the World* was painted.** Worcester Park Farm, the Oxford University Press garden, or near Bethlehem. Wikipedia lists all three and reconciles none. Both the Worcester Park and Bethlehem stops carry the dispute in `date_confidence`.
2. **The marriage year.** Wikipedia 1861 against DNB 1865. The DNB is preferred with the reason stated; the Wikipedia figure is recorded rather than deleted. The day remains unconfirmed.
3. **Nazareth.** Visited on the second sojourn, or reconstructed in the Jerusalem studio? Nothing reached decides it.
4. **St Paul's institutional silence.** The cathedral's published material references the painting but not the ashes; the DNB carries the interment claim alone.
5. **The bench at Mar Elias.** Undated; the wife unnamed.

## 12. Interlocks with the atlas

- **Founding.** Hunt co-founds the PRB in 1848 with Rossetti and Millais. QUEUE.md line 276 names the intersections itself. Millais has no `journey.json` yet (line 277); Rossetti, Christina Rossetti and Ruskin are queued, not written **[A: QUEUE.md]**.
- **johann_friedrich_overbeck.** Overbeck's journey names the Nazarene Lukasbund (Rome, 1809-1810) as the direct precursor two generations before Burne-Jones goes hunting the same pre-Renaissance sincerity, the lineage the PRB claimed for itself in 1848 **[A: johann_friedrich_overbeck.journey.json]**.
- **The Oxford Union murals, 1857: a documented absence.** Rossetti, Morris and Burne-Jones painted Arthurian scenes there; Jane Burden and Bessie Burden were recruited as models. Hunt is not among them **[R: inferred from four atlas files naming the participants; his own whereabouts in 1857 are not established by anything reached, and the pool's claim that he was in the Holy Land that year does not fit the 1854-56 dates]**.
- **Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co., 11 April 1861: a second documented absence.** The partners are Morris, Rossetti, Burne-Jones, Webb, Ford Madox Brown, Marshall and Faulkner. Hunt, a founding Brother with Rossetti, is not among them **[A: `william_morris`, `edward_burne_jones`, `jane_morris` journey files, all at stop 1861-04-11]**.
- **muhammad.** The Haram al-Sharif pin at **31.778, 35.2354** is inherited byte-identical. Nothing reached states that Hunt entered the Haram. **House-style pass, 2026-08-14:** the campa named Muhammad only because the pin coincides with the Haram al-Sharif, with a bare "the atlas" self-reference and its own admission that "the pin is inherited here, not earned" — a coincidental crossing with zero documented connection to Hunt. Both the self-reference and the name are cut; the stop now describes the Temple Mount generically.
- **abraham.** Abraham's journey carries a Dead Sea pin; Hunt painted *The Scapegoat* on the same shore in 1854. No coordinate was available from that file in the pool, so the Sodom stop uses the geography value 31.0699, 35.3799. **House-style pass, 2026-08-14:** the named "Abraham" reference and its "the atlas already stands" self-reference are cut for the same reason as above; the Genesis narrative context is kept generically since it is what Hunt's own painting depicts.
- **Paris**, canonical pin **48.8566, 2.3522**, inherited byte-identical for the outbound stop, tagged **[R]**.
