# Johannes Cusanus (Johannes Enclen de Cusa): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-17. Subject: the itinerant teacher of artificial memory and arithmetic who put his name on the* Tractatulus artificiose memorie *(Frankfurt an der Oder 1510; Vienna 1514; Leipzig 1519), and who described himself at Cracow in 1529 as lecturer in artificial memory in nineteen universities and seven kingdoms.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or a claim resting only on secondary summary. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

**The disambiguation this file exists to protect.** Johannes Cusanus is **not** Nicolaus Cusanus, Nicholas of Cusa (1401-1464), the cardinal-philosopher, who has his own journey file. The two share only the toponymic element *de Cusa*, from Kues on the Mosel. Nicholas died in 1464 at Todi **[A: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy]**; Johannes is on record 1502-1529. No source draws any link of kinship, discipleship or influence between them, and none of the scholarship on the *Tractatulus* mentions Nicholas at all **[A: cross-check of Kiss 2022, Wójcik 2012, and the 2016 volume's front matter]**. The journey file therefore does **not** name him in any campa: a shared toponym is not a relation.

---

## 1. Name, origin, and the one full biographical record

Everything known about who he was comes from one line in the faculty record of the University of Cracow, 1529 **[A: Rafał Wójcik, *Opusculum*, 80; quoted in the 2016 volume, 303, 307]**:

> Johannes Kusanus Petri Henklen de Kusan d. Triverensis, magister Coloniensis..., Lector artificiose memorie in decem novem Universitatibus et septem regnis.

That is: Johannes Kusanus, son of Petrus Henklen of Kues, of the diocese of Trier, master of Cologne, lecturer in artificial memory in nineteen universities and seven kingdoms.

From this line, **[A]**: father's name (Petrus Henklen), family origin (Kues, on the Mosel, diocese of Trier), degree and university (master, Cologne), self-declared career scale (nineteen universities, seven kingdoms).

**[R]** and flagged as such:
- **Birth c. 1480.** The Ossoliński / Dolnośląska Biblioteka Cyfrowa authority record gives "Enclen de Cusa, Johannes (1480-)": a cataloguer's assignment, not a birth entry. No birthplace, baptism or birth date located.
- **Born at Kues.** The 1529 record makes Kues the *family's* place, not demonstrably his birthplace. **Enclen / Henklen is a family name, not a toponym**; only *de Cusa* is toponymic.
- **Death: entirely unknown.** No death date, place, grave, portrait or will in any source consulted.
- **Nineteen universities and seven kingdoms.** His own claim to a clerk. Documents place him with certainty in **five** towns (Zwolle, Frankfurt an der Oder, Ingolstadt, Copenhagen, Cracow) and probably two more (Cologne, Vienna). The rest is unchecked and, as far as this research can tell, uncheckable.

**Contradiction flagged (name form).** The Ingolstadt matriculation of 6 May 1517 reads "Ioannes Cusanus Trevirensis dioecesis Coloniensis" **[A: *Die Matrikel der Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität*, Bd. 1, 407; 2016 volume, 305 n.16]**. The research pool renders this "of Trier, diocese of Cologne", which no map allows. The 1529 Cracow line unpicks it the other way: **of the diocese of Trier, master of Cologne**. Both readings recorded; the register's own wording is left as it stands.

## 2. Cologne, and the authorship problem (c. 1500-1501)

- **[A]** He is a *magister Coloniensis* (1529 Cracow record; corroborated by the 1517 Ingolstadt entry). Date of the degree **not attested**; placed c. 1500 in the journey file with an explicit hedge in `date_confidence`.
- **[R]** A memory treatise associated with **Henricus Vibicetus** is connected with the University of Cologne from about 1500 (2016 volume, 303-310, via secondary summary).
- **[R]** An edition titled ***Aureum reminiscendi memorandique prebreve opusculum*** is recorded at **Cologne, 1501**, with a prefatory epigram by **Hermann von dem Busche**. **No copy survives.** The epigram was signed and the text was not, so the work was for a time misattributed to Busche, who supplied only the verse.
- **[R]** The *Aureum reminiscendi* shares its **incipit** with the later *Tractatulus*. This is the hinge of the authorship question.

**Contradiction flagged (authorship), not resolved.** All three printed editions name **Johannes Cusanus** on the title page as author **[A: 2016 volume, 304 n.9; Kiss 2022, 74]**. Modern scholarship credits the underlying text chiefly to **Henricus Vibicetus**, casting Cusanus as teacher, editor and print-sponsor rather than composer, and titles the edition with a bracketed dual attribution, "[Henricus Vibicetus-Johannes Cusanus]" **[R: 2016 volume, 303-310, via secondary summary]**. One reconstruction goes further and describes an outright appropriation, possibly after Vibicetus's death **[R: secondary summary only]**. The sources disagree; neither the title pages nor the editors' brackets settle it, and this report does not.

**Corrections carried in from the verification pass:**
- A **Zwolle 1502** imprint was described in one lens as a second, lost printing of the memory treatise. Almost certainly a **conflation**: the Zwolle 1502 book under this name is an *extant arithmetic textbook* (section 3).
- "Masters, Pupils, Friends, and Thieves," *Daphnis* 41 (2012): 399-418, is by **Rafał Wójcik alone**, not Kiss. Several of the sharpest findings below (the word-for-word Publicius borrowing, the plagiarism verdict, the roster) belong to that article.
- Wójcik's roster, from his abstract: **Matheolus Perusinus, Jacobus Publicius, Conrad Celtis, Petrus de Ravenna, Jodocus Wetzdorf, Conrad Umhauser, Hermann von dem Busche, Johannes Cusanus**, c. 1474-1530. Earlier drafts dropped Perusinus and misnamed Umhauser as "Christian".

## 3. Zwolle, 1502: the arithmetic, and the start of the wandering

- **[A]** **Johannes Enclen de Cusa, *Algorismus proiectilium de integris novus*, Zwolle: Arnold Kempen, 20 October 1502.** Catalogued and extant. Studied in Smeur, *Scientiarum Historia* 4 (1962): 63-75; listed as extant by Heeffer (logica.ugent.be/albrecht/georgius.pdf, p. 4).
- His **first surviving book**, and not about memory. Direct documentary support for Kiss's characterisation of him as a teacher of "mnemonics and arithmetic" **[A: Kiss 2022, 54]**.
- **[R]** *Algorismus proiectilium* denotes reckoning with counters (jetons) cast on ruled lines, each line a place value: the standard sense of the genre title. The book itself was not read, and Smeur 1962 was not reachable.
- **[R, well grounded]** His own two datings of the *peregrinatio* (section 7) count back to **1501 or 1502**, exactly where the Zwolle imprint sits.

## 4. Frankfurt an der Oder, 1510: the editio princeps

- **[A]** *Tractatulus artificiose memorie* published at Frankfurt an der Oder in 1510 **[Kiss 2022, 54]**.
- **[A]** Full title and imprint, 2016 volume, 304 n.9: "Tractatulus artificiose memorie omnibus cuiuscumque etatis studiosis admodisque utilis et necessarius (Impressus Francophordie ad Oderam: per Joannem Hanaw, 1510)." The formula is *Impressus*, not *Impressum*. The same volume's critical text (p. 311) normalises to "cuiuscunque ... admodum"; variant recorded, not resolved.
- **[A]** The printer **Johann Hanau** is a real Frankfurt-an-der-Oder printer, active 1509-1528 (Deutsche Biographie, sfz25782). **Contradiction flagged:** the 2016 volume's n.114 (p. 144) gives the 1510 imprint as "s.t.", no printer named; its n.9 (p. 304) names Hanaw. Not adjudicated.
- **[R], demoted from [A]:** a **unique copy of the 1510 edition at Uppsala** ("Ink. 31.230", from a search summary). An incunabula shelfmark is inconsistent with a 1510 imprint, so that copy may be a different treatise. Not used in the journey file.
- **[A]** On f. 1r-1v he prints an epigraph from **Cicero, *De oratore* 2.88**, "Vidi ego summos homines et diuina prope memoria Athenis Carneadem..." (full text quoted verbatim in the journey file) **[2016 volume, 144 n.114]**. The critical text at p. 311 prints "in Asiam" for "in Asia" under the rubric "Cicero artis detractoribus"; the journey file follows the footnote form and the variant is recorded here.
- **[A]** On f. 1v, an epigram signed **"Eberhardus Verberius Dantiscus auditor artificiose memorie M. Joannis Cusani"** (2016 volume, 304 n.11). In this edition Cusanus's own name is set in small letters, less prominent than his pupils'.
- **[R]** That the *Tractatulus* carries epigrams by **three** humanists (Busche, Trebelius, Verberius) is stated of the tradition generally, **without a per-edition breakdown**. Kiss 2022 mentions no epigram at all; that Busche's appears in all three editions is unverified.
- **[R]** That Cusanus used the treatise as his own lecture textbook is Wójcik's formulation, hedged in the scholarship itself ("most probably"), and is **not** anchored to Frankfurt an der Oder or to 1510 anywhere readable.
- **Context [A]**: the Viadrina was chartered in 1506 by Elector Joachim I with papal permission and opened 26 April 1506, first university of the Margraviate of Brandenburg. The gloss "still building its Latin-textbook market" is this report's own, **[R]**.

## 5. What the treatise actually teaches

- **[A]** **Tiered loci.** Memory-space divided into *loci maximi*, *maiores*, *minores*, nested one within another; the tiering is taken from earlier fifteenth-century treatises, not invented **[2016 volume / Wójcik, via secondary summary]**.
- **[A]** **The alphabet is copied** "word for word" from the woodcuts of **Jacobus Publicius** **[Wójcik, *Daphnis* 41 (2012)]**. Publicius's *Ars memorativa* was printed at Toulouse c. 1475-76 and Paris c. 1475-80 before the Venice 1482 Ratdolt printing of the *Oratoriae artis epitomata* (GW M36431), the edition carrying the woodcut alphabet **[AUC Philologica 2020/2, 85ff.]**. An earlier draft's "first printed Venice, c. 1482" was wrong.
- **[A]** **A second, rival alphabet.** The treatise also adopts the image-alphabet of **Conrad Celtis**, from his *Epitoma in utramque Ciceronis rhetoricam*, although Celtis had explicitly rejected and criticised Publicius's method **[Wójcik 2012, via search summary]**. Cusanus teaches both without reconciling them.
- **[A]** **The fivefold sermon scheme**, in his own words, Vienna 1514, f. B1v-B2r **[2016 volume, 153 n.139]**, quoted verbatim in the journey file. Adapted from the anonymous *Memoria fecunda*, reworked from a threefold to a fivefold division.
- **[A]** **The woodcuts do not explain themselves.** A student with the book but not the oral instruction could not grasp the technique; surviving copies carry marginalia and inserted sheets supplying what was said aloud **[Kiss 2022, 54 and fig. 3]**. This is the commercial mechanism of the whole career: the book advertises a course the book cannot replace.
- **[A]** **The Budapest copy**: Vienna 1514, Hungarian National Library, Ant. 10008, f. a3r, a woodcut loci diagram with marginalia headed "De auctoritatibus et allegationibus" and repeated annotation of *imagines* **[Kiss 2022, 55, fig. 3 caption]**. Calling the diagram "house-shaped" is **[R]**, from a lens summary.
- **[A]** **The verdict.** Wójcik calls it "an interesting example of a borrowing, or indeed, a theft of ideas conceived by his predecessors, in some instances transformed, in some others, simply plagiarized."
- **[R], cut from the journey file:** a claim that "the preface positions the work as a practical aid, not an original contribution." No source says this and nobody in this research has seen the preface.

## 6. Vienna 1514, Ingolstadt 1517, Leipzig 1519

- **[A]** **Vienna, 1514**, second edition, printed by **Hieronymus Vietor and Johann Singriener (Singrenius)** **[Kiss 2022, 54-55 and bibliography 74; fig. 3 caption]**.
- **[R], demoted:** that **Cusanus taught at the University of Vienna**. This rests on a single search summary. Kiss 2022 documents only that he *published* at Vienna in 1514 and was itinerant.
- **[A] context:** the **Collegium poetarum et mathematicorum** was founded at Vienna in 1501 by **Maximilian I**, on Conrad Celtis's initiative; Celtis directed it 1502-1508 and died at Vienna on 4 February 1508 (AEIOU; Wien Geschichte Wiki; University of Vienna "650 plus"). An earlier draft made Celtis the founder; corrected.
- **[A]** **Ingolstadt, 6 May 1517**: matriculation, under rector Martinus Otingensis and vice-rector **Johannes Eck** **[*Die Matrikel der Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität*, Bd. 1, 407; 2016 volume, 305 n.16]**.
- **[A]** **The fee.** Marginal note by the student **Georg Jobst the Elder** in his copy of the Vienna 1514 edition, Wolfenbüttel, Herzog August Bibliothek, A: 104.11 Quod. (5), quoted verbatim in the journey file **[2016 volume, 306 n.18]**. Note that **the year 1517 stands inside the quotation**; an earlier draft's claim that the source is silent on the date is wrong, and the place is supplied by Cusanus's own Ingolstadt registration in the same year.
- **[R], demoted:** the **Leipzig 1519** third edition. Kiss 2022, read in full, names only Frankfurt an der Oder 1510 and Vienna 1514, and lists only Vienna in its bibliography. Leipzig rests solely on search summaries of the 2016 volume. Carried in the journey file with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

## 7. Lübeck 1523, Copenhagen 1524, Cracow 1529

- **[A]** **Lübeck, 1523**: *Textus lecture arborum consanguinitatis, affinitatis, cognationis spiritualis et legalis*, printed by **Vitus Blancken**; preface opens "Johannes Cusanus pio lectori salutem" **[2016 volume, 307]**, described there as the first work in which he plainly claims authorship in his own preface. Same leaf, his own dating, twenty-second year of the *peregrinatio* in 1523, counting back to **1501/1502** **[304 n.5, 307]**.
- **[A]** **Copenhagen, 9 October 1524**: matriculation and licence to teach, from the *Matricula Academiae Hafniensis* in Albertus Thura, *Regiae Academiae Hafniensis infantia et pueritia* (Flensburg: Korte, 1734), 37 **[2016 volume, 307 n.24]**: "admissus ad communicandam mensuram quatuor arborum ... una cum mensura memoriae artificialis". Both subjects, one licence.
- **[A]** **Cracow, 1529**: *Textus lecture quatuor arborum...*, printed by **Maciej Szarfenberg** for **Marek Szarfenberg**; copy at the Zakład Narodowy im. Ossolińskich, sig. XVI.Qu.2347 **[Dolnośląska Biblioteka Cyfrowa]**. Same year, the preface dated to the twenty-seventh year of the wandering **[307 n.28]**, and the faculty record quoted in section 1.
- **[R]** That the four kinship trees function as a spatial mnemonic, the loci habit applied to canon law, is this research's inference. **No source states it**, and the journey file marks the inference in its own prose.
- **[R]** That **Cracow was his base**, or that he taught there thirty years, is **not** supported. The "thirty years" figure traces to a section heading in the 2016 scholarship, "Thirty years of teaching artificial memory in Europe", not to Cracow. What is attested is that Cracow's *ars memorativa* milieu counted him among its teachers, alongside Publicius, Celtis, Thomas Murner, Jan Szklarek, Stanisław Korzybski and Antoni z Radomska.
- **[R], corrected:** an earlier draft placed the Cracow university-mendicant circuit at the **Church of St Francis of Assisi**, which is **Conventual**, not Observant. The Cracow Observants are the Bernardines on Stradom, the milieu that produced Jan Szklarek's *Opusculum de arte memorativa* (Cracow: Hochfeder, 1504). No document places Cusanus in either house, so **no Cracow friary stop appears in the journey file**.

## 8. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **No death date, place, or grave.** The record simply stops after 1529.
2. **No birth record.** The 1480 birth year is a cataloguer's assignment, nothing more.
3. **Twelve of the nineteen universities are unidentified.** Documents locate him at Zwolle, Frankfurt an der Oder, Ingolstadt, Copenhagen and Cracow; Cologne (degree) and Vienna (imprint) are strongly implied. The rest is his own word.
4. **Vibicetus is a name and nothing else.** No biography, no dates, no other work found for him.
5. **The 2016 critical edition, pages 303-310 and 311ff., could not be read directly.** Much of the authorship argument and the print history reaches this report only through search summaries. Every claim resting on it is tagged [R] above unless Kiss 2022 carries it.
6. **No portrait, no monument, no memorial** under either name form.

## 10. Interlocks named in the journey file

Two, each on a one-directional relation rather than shared geography:

- **Simonides of Ceos** (queued): Cusanus prints Cicero, *De oratore* 2.88 as the epigraph to his treatise. The banquet at Crannon, from which Cicero derives the invention of the method of loci and credits Simonides, stands a few paragraphs earlier in that same book. **[A** for the epigraph; **R** for the framing.**]**
- **Peter of Ravenna** (queued): Wójcik's *Daphnis* article places Petrus de Ravenna and Johannes Cusanus inside one named environment of *ars memorativa* authors, c. 1474-1530, whose defining feature in the article's own argument is that they borrowed from one another. **[A** for the roster; **R** for any direct Cusanus-to-Peter borrowing, which no source asserts.**]**

Deliberately **not** named: Nicholas of Cusa (toponym only), Martin Luther (Leipzig 1519 is a coincidence of city and year), Paracelsus, Giulio Camillo, Matteo Ricci, Giordano Bruno, Athanasius Kircher (later inheritors, none with documented engagement with Cusanus), Arthur Ben (thematic cluster only).
