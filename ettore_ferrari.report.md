# Ettore Ferrari (Rome, 1845 - Rome, 1929): research report

*Sculptor of the Giordano Bruno of Campo de' Fiori; deputy of the extreme left; Grand Master of the Grande Oriente d'Italia 1904-1917; Sovereign Grand Commander of the Scottish Rite 1918-1929.*

## Method and legend

Two spines carry almost everything: the **Treccani *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*** entry "Ferrari, Ettore" (footnoted, fullest monument list) and the **Italian Wikipedia** article, closer to the monument literature and often carrying inauguration dates Treccani gets wrong. English Wikipedia adds the Masonic offices and one internal error. Five verbatim passages come from **Emma Perodi, *Roma italiana 1870-1895*** (1896) on it.wikisource, a chronicle written while Ferrari sat on the Rome city council, one from ***Ars et Labor*** (1906).

- **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, single-source, tradition, or inference.
- Contradictions are **flagged and left open** unless one side is a demonstrable arithmetic or chronological impossibility, in which case the reasoning is shown and the defective source named. Where an earlier pass produced a coordinate or date that a check falsified, the falsification is recorded rather than silently corrected: the wrong value is what the next reader would otherwise reproduce.

## 1. Origins and formation (1845-1874)

**Born in Rome, 25 March 1845**, son of **Filippo Ferrari** and **Maria Luisa Pasini** **[A - Treccani; it.wikipedia]**. Filippo was a **sculptor and engraver** (Treccani: "scultore e incisore", 1814-1897), a carbonaro and republican who took part in defending the Roman Republic **[A - Treccani]**. An earlier pass made him a **painter**: wrong. The same pass gave the mother Florentine nobility, which is **not in Treccani** (which gives only her name) and **not in it.wikipedia** (which never mentions her): **[R], uncorroborated**, unused in the journey file.

**22 October 1867**: at twenty-two he takes part in the failed republican insurrection against the papal government **[A - Treccani]**. No source gives his role or any arrest; **a gap**.

**1868**: debuts at the **Albacini competition of the Accademia di San Luca**, winning a two-year scholarship **[A - Treccani]**; it.wikipedia attaches the terracotta *Labano accoglie amorevolmente Giacobbe* to this year **[A]**. **1870**: *Ildegonda*, *Bruto*, *Stefano Porcari*; **1872**: graduates **[A - it.wikipedia]**; **1874**: *Ermengarda* **[A - Treccani]**.

**Institutional caution, load-bearing.** The Accademia di San Luca, where he trained, and the **Regio Istituto di belle arti**, where he held the chair of sculpture from 1896 and the presidency from 1906, are **different institutions** **[A - Treccani]**; an earlier pass merged them. No reachable source gives an address for the Regio Istituto, so that stop takes a generic Rome pin and says so.

## 2. Council, Chamber, and the first commissions abroad (1875-1888)

**1877** is the hinge year: *Iacopo Ortis* takes an honourable mention at the **Naples National Exhibition**, and the democrats elect him to the **Rome city council**, where he sits, with interruptions, **to 1907** **[A - Treccani]**.

**Bucharest, Ion Heliade Radulescu.** Treccani dates the monument **1877**; it.wikipedia gives **no year** **[A/R]**. An earlier pass asserted an **1875 commission** that no source supports. **Site corrected**: the statue stands **in front of the University building**, Piata Universitatii, approx. **44.4353, 26.1017** **[A - en.wikipedia]**, not in the Cismigiu Gardens (44.4361, 26.0913, 850 m west).

**The Ovid for Constanta.** Treccani: executed **1879**. it.wikipedia: monument **1887**, cast by Nelli. ro.wikipedia: unveiled **August 1887** before prefect **Remus Opreanu**, in **Piata Ovidiu**, at **44.17433 N, 28.65854 E** **[A]**. The contradiction **resolves**: model 1879, unveiling August 1887. Two earlier coordinates were wrong: 44.1792, 28.6551 (550 m north) and 44.1733, 28.6383 (1.6 km inland).

**Venice, Vittorio Emanuele II.** Competition announced **September 1879**, **48 entries** **[A - it.wikipedia, "Monumento a Vittorio Emanuele II (Venezia)"]**; an earlier pass dated it 1880 and credited "preliminary designs with **Pio Piacentini**, 1884". The Venice article **never mentions Piacentini**, naming only **Alessandro Nelli** as founder; Piacentini belongs to the **Rome** competition, where he and Ferrari took the **second prize of 30,000 lire in 1882** **[A - Perodi, "Il 1882"]**. Inauguration **1 May 1887** before Umberto I and Margherita **[A]**, Perodi's 1887 chapter confirming Ferrari as modeller and Nelli as founder. Ferrari's own presence is undocumented: a gap.

**1880**: *Cum Spartaco pugnavit* takes **first prize at the Turin National Exhibition**; he marries **Maria Carolina Frey von Freienstein** **[A - Treccani]**.

**Vicenza, Garibaldi in marble**: Treccani **1886**, it.wikipedia **1887**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.**

## 3. Freemasonry: initiation to the Grand Mastership (1881-1917)

**Summer 1881**: initiated into the lodge **Rienzi** of the Grande Oriente d'Italia, proposed by **U. Bacci** **[A - Treccani; en./it.wikipedia]**.

**Anachronism corrected, twice.** An earlier pass put the 1881 initiation and the 1892 Venerable Mastership at **Palazzo Giustiniani**, calling it the GOI seat. The GOI did **not occupy it until 21 April 1901** **[A - it.wikipedia]**. The 1881 venue is **unknown**: a gap. The journey file uses that palace only from 1904 on.

**1892**: **Maestro Venerabile** of the lodge Rienzi **[A - it.wikipedia]**. **Grand Secretary**: en.wikipedia says **1897, under Adriano Lemmi and Ernesto Nathan**, which is **impossible**, Lemmi's grand mastership having ended **1 June 1896** when Nathan succeeded **[A - it.wikipedia, "Grande Oriente d'Italia"]**; it.wikipedia's Ferrari article dates the office **1896**. The journey file uses **1896 under Nathan**, year flagged.

**1900**: elected **assistant grand master** **[A - Treccani]**. **15 February 1904**: elected **Grand Master**, succeeding **Nathan** **[A - Treccani]**, serving to **25 November 1917**; the Order takes an explicitly democratic and anticlerical line (secular schooling, civil marriage, divorce), and the politicisation contributes to the **1908 schism** **[A - en.wikipedia]**. **23 February 1906**: re-elected **131 of 149**, **Engel** assistant grand master with **110 of 132** **[A - *Ars et Labor* 1906 n. 3]**.

**June 1917**: attends the **Paris congress of Entente masonic bodies** seeking a league of nations, then is forced to resign amid a hostile press campaign **[A - Treccani]**. **1918**: **Sovereign Grand Commander** of the Supreme Council of the Scottish Rite **[A - Treccani]**, held until death **[A - en.wikipedia]**. **1919**: honorary Grand Master for life; **refuses a senatorship for life**, citing political consistency **[A - Treccani]**.

## 4. Campo de' Fiori (1879-1891)

**1879**: his **first design** for the Bruno monument is **rejected as too polemical** toward the Catholic Church **[A - it.wikipedia]**.

**9 June 1889**: the bronze is inaugurated in **Campo de' Fiori**, on the site of Bruno's burning of **17 February 1600**, before anticlerical and radical delegations; the subscription was driven by **students of the University of Rome** **[A - it.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**. Coordinate **41.8955, 12.4723**, inherited byte-identically from `giordano_bruno.journey.json`.

- **Date contradiction, open.** Perodi gives **9 July 1889**; it.wikipedia and the 1911 *Encyclopaedia Britannica* give **9 June 1889**. The journey file uses 9 June and states the conflict in `date_confidence`.
- **"Exactly 289 years after" is wrong** and removed: 1600 to 1889 is 289 years and nearly four months, and the date-matched observance is the **17 February** commemoration.
- The figure faces the **Vatican** **[A - it.wikipedia, "Monumento a Giordano Bruno"]**. The pedestal carries **eight medallions**: Paolo Sarpi, **Tommaso Campanella**, Petrus Ramus, **Lucilio Vanini**, Aonio Paleario, Miguel Servet, John Wycliffe, Jan Hus **[A]**. Worked into the **Vanini** relief is a concealed portrait of **Martin Luther**, unnoticed until **Lars Berggren** identified it in **1991** **[A - en./it.wikipedia]**.
- **October 1890**: **Leo XIII** condemns the monument by name in ***Ab Apostolici***, calling it "eminently sectarian" and charging it with aiming to insult the papacy; on the unveiling day he had fasted and prayed against it **[A]**.

**May 1890**: organises the Rome congress closing with the **"Pact of Rome"** (radicals, socialists, irredentists, republicans). **30 June 1890**: speaks in the Chamber on article 11 of the bill on Rome's municipal provisions. **1 May 1891**: joins the labour demonstration at **Santa Croce in Gerusalemme**, addressing the Chamber on it days later **[A - Treccani for all three]**.

## 5. Parliament and the Garibaldi series (1882-1904)

**29 October 1882**: elected deputy for **Perugia II (Spoleto)** with **3,003 preferences**; **reconfirmed 1886 and 1890**, sitting through the XV, XVI and XVII legislatures, and opposing colonial expansion, the Triple Alliance and Crispi **[A - Treccani]**. An earlier pass ended the tenure in 1890; the XVII legislature ran **to 1892**.

Garibaldi monuments with their sourcing: **Vicenza** marble, 1886 (Treccani) / 1887 (it.wikipedia) **[open]**; **Pisa** 1892 **[A]**; **Cortona** and **Tortona** 1895 **[R - Treccani only]**; **Macerata** 1895 **[A]**; **Massa Marittima** 1904 **[R - Treccani only]**; **Bevagna** undated **[R]**; **Rovigo** equestrian, **inaugurated 15 November 1896** **[A - it.wikipedia, "Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi (Rovigo)"]**, against Treccani's **erroneous 1897**.

**The republican detail was misdescribed in every earlier pass**: not crowns "beneath the horse's hooves", "beneath the saddle" or "beneath the horse's stirrups" (horses do not wear stirrups). it.wikipedia: "la particolare foggia delle staffe su cui poggiano gli stivali che richiamano la corona di Casa Savoia", **the stirrups under Garibaldi's boots being shaped as the Savoy crown**. The statue was **destined for Rome** and sent to Rovigo for this irreverence **[A]**.

Other works: **Quintino Sella**, Rome, **2 March 1893**, before the Ministry of Finance **[A - Perodi, "Il 1893"]**; **Terenzio Mamiani**, Pesaro, **1896**; **Alberto Mario**, Lendinara, 1897 **[R - en.wikipedia]**; **Engel**, **Vicosoprano, Switzerland, 1899**; **Giovanni Bovio**, Trani, **1914**; **Gabriele Rosa**, Iseo, **1914** **[A - Treccani, except Lendinara]**. Earlier passes called Bovio, Mamiani and Rosa "undated in the sources"; all three dates sit in Treccani's list, so those were research failures, not source gaps. The Iseo pin was wrong too (45.6478, 10.0517 falls in the lake); Iseo is approx. **45.6592, 10.0538**.

**Carlo Cattaneo, Milan: a genuine open contradiction.** Treccani dates the monument **1893**; it.wikipedia puts it in **Via Santa Margherita** (approx. **45.4671, 9.1893**) and dates the inauguration **23 June 1901**, Cattaneo's birth centenary. **Not resolved**; the journey file uses 1901 and flags 1893. An earlier pin, 45.4489, 9.1706, was 2.5 km off. In the same city and year, the **Universita Popolare di Milano**: it.wikipedia's Ferrari article calls him a founder, but **Treccani never mentions any universita popolare** and it.wikipedia's own "Universita popolare" article credits it to **Luigi Molinari**. **Downgraded [A] to [R]**; an earlier pass sourced the claim to Treccani, which does not carry it.

**Mazzini on the Aventine.** One coherent chronology in it.wikipedia: commission **1902**, sketch **1905**, siting approved **1914**, first stone **1922**, stalled under Fascism, resumed **1948** under Ferrari's son, **inaugurated 2 June 1949**, at **41.8852, 12.4843** **[A]**. An earlier pass called Treccani's "1902-1911" and the 1949 date a contradiction; they are the work period and the unveiling of one project. Its pin (41.8836, 12.4806) was about 350 m off.

## 6. Fascism, the ban, death (1922-1929)

**8 August 1922**: his wife **Maria Carolina** dies. **November 1923**: founds ***Lux***, merging political and esoteric masonic concerns. **End of 1924**: urges his brothers to defend the secular inheritance of the Risorgimento, in the passage Treccani quotes verbatim **[A - Treccani, quotation unfootnoted]**.

**The year of the anti-Masonic law: corrected, and it matters.** Two earlier passes gave **November 1926**. The law against secret societies is **n. 2029 of 26 November 1925**, passed by the Chamber **19 May 1925**; the November 1926 measures dissolved non-Fascist parties and associations generally, a different instrument. The **GOI officially suspended its works in November 1925** **[A - it.wikipedia, "Grande Oriente d'Italia"]**. So "Ferrari does not dissolve his lodge" **overstates**: the Order suspended its works without formal dissolution, and Ferrari went on acting as its head under surveillance while lodges were sacked **[A/R]**.

**25 May 1929**: denounced for attempting to reorganise Freemasonry under Fascism, given a formal warning, transmits his full Masonic powers to **Giuseppe Leti** in France **[A - Treccani; en.wikipedia]**.

**19 August 1929**: dies in Rome; buried in the **Cimitero del Verano** **[A]**, at **41.9015, 12.5203** (it.wikipedia); two earlier passes used 41.8975, 12.5252 and 41.9034, 12.5234, both off the cemetery. it.wikipedia's "Cimitero del Verano" lists Ferrari among sculptors of funerary monuments there but does **not** say he is buried there; the burial rests on his own article **[A/R]**. **Age: 84**; en.wikipedia's "aged 86" and its stray death year **1931** are one error with two faces (1931 minus 1845 = 86).

## 7. Afterlife

- **1929, Lateran Pacts**: the Church presses for the statue's removal and proposes an **expiatory chapel**; Mussolini refuses, saying it "will remain where it is", while **banning commemorative gatherings** there **[A - it.wikipedia, "Monumento a Giordano Bruno"]**.
- **After Fascism**, freethinkers resume gathering each **17 February**, as Masons, atheists and pantheists still do, with a city representative laying a wreath **[A]**. The year of resumption is **not** given in the sources, and the journey file says so.
- **2 June 1949**: the **Mazzini** is inaugurated **[A]**. **1991**: **Lars Berggren** identifies the concealed **Luther** **[A]**. His second son **Giordano Bruno Ferrari** (b. 1887), painter and anti-fascist, saw the Mazzini work resumed in 1948 **[A - Treccani; it.wikipedia]**.

## 8. Argentina: a negative finding, stated as a gap

No commission, monument, visit or installation by **Ettore Ferrari** in **Argentina or anywhere in Latin America** appears in any source consulted: Treccani, the work-lists on both Wikipedias, and a corpus-wide grep of every `*.journey.json`. The only Argentine-footprint "Ferrari" hits in the corpus are **Ivo Ferrari**, a Buenos Aires cantina owner (`emin_arslan`), and the artist **Leon Ferrari** (`dalila_puzzovio`). Frequently confused with him: the **Uruguayan sculptor Juan Manuel Ferrari**, who did work in **Mendoza**.

His **catalogued foreign work**: **Romania** (Bucharest, Constanta, Cluj-Napoca), the **United States** (Staten Island; a Verdi attributed to Philadelphia; a Lincoln and a *Lesbia* thinly attributed to New York and Chicago), **Egypt** (Alexandria), **Switzerland** (Vicosoprano, omitted by earlier passes, which made their absence statement read as a fuller survey than it was).

## 9. Contradictions and defects, listed

1. **Age at death**: en.wikipedia 86 and a stray 1931 vs it.wikipedia 84. **Resolved to 84.**
2. **Bruno unveiling**: Perodi 9 July 1889 vs it.wikipedia and 1911 Britannica 9 June. **Open.**
3. **Vicenza Garibaldi**: Treccani 1886 vs it.wikipedia 1887. **Open.**
4. **Rovigo Garibaldi**: Treccani 1897 vs it.wikipedia 15 November 1896. **Resolved to 1896.**
5. **Cattaneo, Milan**: Treccani 1893 vs it.wikipedia 23 June 1901. **Open.**
6. **Grand Secretary**: en.wikipedia 1897 "under Lemmi and Nathan" (impossible) vs it.wikipedia 1896. **Year open, Lemmi removed.**
7. **Ovid, Constanta**: Treccani 1879 vs it.wikipedia 1887. **Resolved**: model 1879, unveiling August 1887.
8. **Verdi, Philadelphia**: Treccani 1887, unconfirmed by any Philadelphia source. **Open** (the earlier "predates Verdi's death" objection is a non sequitur, withdrawn).
9. **Universita Popolare di Milano**: it.wikipedia's Ferrari article vs its own "Universita popolare" article. **[R], open.**
10. **Presence at his own unveilings**: Bucharest, Constanta, Venice and Staten Island unattested. A **gap**.

## 10. Gaps left open

- The **venue of the 1881 initiation** (not Palazzo Giustiniani), and the **address of the Regio Istituto di belle arti**.
- The **site within Alexandria** of the war memorial; the **site within Philadelphia** of the Verdi; the **nature and date** of the New York Lincoln; the **date and site** of the Chicago *Lesbia*; the **dates** of the Bevagna Garibaldi and the Marino war memorial; the exact site at Vicosoprano (a village pin is used).
- Ferrari's **role in the October 1867 rising**: nothing beyond participation.
- Whether the **Engel** of the Vicosoprano monument (1899) is the Engel elected assistant grand master in 1906. The sources do not say; **not asserted in the journey file**.

## 11. Interlocks with existing atlas travelers

Named inside campas, each on a **direct relation of Ferrari to that person**, never on shared geography:

- **giordano_bruno** - sculpted the monument on the site of Bruno's execution, and is named as its sculptor in `giordano_bruno.journey.json`. Canonical pin **41.8955, 12.4723** inherited byte-identically.
- **tommaso_campanella** - carved his medallion portrait into the Bruno pedestal.

Also named though not yet a journey file: **Lucilio Vanini**, whose medallion Ferrari carved and in whose relief the Luther hides; Vanini stands in QUEUE.md (row 892) as the thematic echo of the Ferrari entry. **Deliberately not named**, the tie being co-location or career pattern only: Licio Gelli, Frederic Auguste Bartholdi, Auguste Rodin, Agusti Querol Subirats, Mario Palanti, Theodor Reuss, Emin Arslan.
