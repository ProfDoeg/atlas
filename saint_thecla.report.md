# Saint Thecla of Iconium: research report
*2026-08-23. Slug `saint_thecla`. Traditional life c. AD 30 to c. AD 120, Iconium (Konya) to Seleucia ad Calycadnum (Silifke). Feast 23 September (Roman, Episcopal), 24 September (Eastern Orthodox), Thout 23 (Coptic). No formal canonization; the cult predates the procedure.*

**Legend. [A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim this pass could not confirm. Unreachable sources are named with the reason; contradictions are collected in §7 and not adjudicated.

---

## 0. The shape of the problem

Thecla's whole biography rests on one work of fiction, so the house register ("national mythology: the canon is true") does more work here than usual. The journey file narrates the Acts of Paul and Thecla as true; this report states what is underneath it.

- **Not named in the canonical New Testament**, not in any undisputed Pauline letter, not in canonical Acts, and no securely first-century source attests her. The earliest narrative is the **Acts of Paul and Thecla**, Greek, **later second century** (§1); the earliest *external* notice is **Tertullian, c. 198-203**, and it is hostile (§3). **[A: Wikipedia, "Thecla"]**
- Birth c. 30, age eighteen at conversion, death at ninety: **[R]** in every case, back-calculated from the text's own arithmetic, which does not close (§2).

Segments 1 to 4 of the journey file contain no documented events, and every stop carries its hedge in `date_confidence`. Segments 5 to 7 are different: shrine, relic, manuscripts and the 2013 Maaloula kidnapping are documented.

## 1. The text and its transmission

Later second century, within the Acts of Paul; never canonized, despite circulating in Greek, Latin, Syriac, Armenian, Coptic, Ethiopic and Arabic. **[A]**

**Standard edition:** Lipsius and Bonnet, *Acta Apostolorum Apocrypha* (Leipzig, **1891-1903**, not 1891 alone), from manuscripts **sigla A to M, all 10th-12th century**. **[A: NASSCAL e-Clavis, fetched]** Tischendorf 1851 precedes it, Vouaux 1913 follows.

**The earliest witnesses are not those manuscripts.** They are papyri: **P.Schøyen MS 2634.1 (3rd c.), P.Antinoopolis 13 (4th c.), P.Oxyrhynchus I 6 (5th c.)**. **[A: NASSCAL]** The dossier's "earliest surviving Greek critical edition base text ... 1891" conflated an edition with a witness. Corrected likewise: Schmidt's Coptic edition is ***Acta Pauli aus der Heidelberger koptischen Papyrushandschrift Nr. 1*, Leipzig: Hinrichs, 1904**; Heidelberg is where the papyrus is *conserved*, not where it was published, and Wikipedia elides the two. **[A]**

**Spread:** 45+ Byzantine Greek manuscripts with **eight distinct alternate endings**; 135+ Latin in recensions A-F plus epitomes; seven West Syriac and four East Syriac; independent Coptic, Armenian, Arabic, Slavonic, Ethiopic. **[A: NASSCAL]** Her ending is not one ending, and the file says so in `date_confidence`. Every quote in the journey file comes from **Walker, *Ante-Nicene Fathers* vol. 8 (1886)**.

## 2. The narrative (journey segments 1 to 4)

**Iconium.** Plot **[R]** as event throughout, per Wikipedia's summary cross-checked against Walker. Two details worth the record: the governor is **Castelius / Cestilius** in variant manuscripts, and Thamyris's complaint in the text is about marriage contracts and bride-price, not doctrine; and **Paul refuses her baptism at the tomb**, which is what makes the later self-baptism a self-baptism. Onesiphorus's sons are a manuscript variant (§7.4).

Two corrections: the **bracelets go to the gatekeeper and the silver mirror to the jailor**, which the dossier reversed **[A: Walker ch. 18]**; and **the text says SEVENTEEN at the fire**, "She was cast ... into the fire when seventeen years old," eighteen being a modern back-calculation from the same text's 72 ascetic years and 90 years of life (§7.2). The file uses seventeen. **[A]**

**Antioch.** The text says only "Antioch." **Which Antioch is textually unstable.** This pass identifies it, per tradition, as **Pisidian Antioch, 38.3061 / 31.1892, near Yalvaç** **[A on the coordinates: Wikipedia, "Pisidian Antioch"; R on the identification]**. That contradicts the atlas as it stands: §7.1, a map-visible disagreement and the most consequential open item here.

Episode content **[R]** as event: Alexander's assault and prosecution, Tryphaena's household and Falconilla's dream, the lioness, the self-baptism in the pool of seals, the bulls, and the eight days of teaching before she leaves in a mantle recut as a man's cloak. Tryphaena's identification with the Pontic client queen Antonia Tryphaena is a contested conjecture (§7.9). **Formula corrected:** Walker ch. 34 reads **"In the name of Jesus Christ I am baptized on my last day,"** looser in both the dossier and the atlas's current quotation (§7.10). **[A]**

**Myra and the return.** Reunited at **Myra**; the common Greek ending has Paul commission her: **"Go, and teach the word of God."** **[A on the wording: Walker ch. 41]** The hinge of the entire later reception, and what Tertullian writes against (§3). **Coordinates corrected:** The dossier's 36.2472 / 29.9828 is modern Demre town centre, about 1.4 km from the ancient site. Wikipedia's "Myra" infobox gives **36.2592 / 29.9853**, used in the file. **[A]** Returns to Iconium; Thamyris is dead; she seeks out Theocleia. **The text does not clearly narrate a conversion, and the file leaves the door open as the text does.** **[R]**

**Seleucia.** **The earliest and shortest ending says only that she "enlightens many" and then "slept a good sleep."** Everything else is later expansion, including the appendix Walker prints: "she departed to Seleucia, and dwelt in a cave seventy-two years, living upon herbs and water." **[A on the wording, R as event]** **The arithmetic does not close:** 17 at the fire plus 72 in the cave is 89, against the same text's "all the years of her life were ninety." This replaces the dossier's framing of a round-number convention; the numbers are in the text and they disagree with each other. **[A]**

**Physicians' legend:** doctors resentful that her free cures undercut their trade send men to assault her; the rock opens and closes around her, leaving a fragment of cloth, an aetiology for an empty tomb. All **four incompatible endings** are in the file, at their own pins, each flagged (§7.5). **[R]**

## 3. The earliest external witness

**Tertullian, De Baptismo 17, c. 198-203, Carthage. [A: newadvent.org/fathers/0321.htm, fetched]**

> But if the writings which wrongly go under Paul's name, claim Thecla's example as a licence for women's teaching and baptizing, let them know that, in Asia, the presbyter who composed that writing, as if he were augmenting Paul's fame from his own store, after being convicted, and confessing that he had done it from love of Paul, was removed from his office.

Attested by this and nothing more: the text existed by c. 200; it was already being used as precedent for women teaching and baptizing; its author was a presbyter who was deposed, in a place given only as "Asia." **No Carthage stop appears in the journey file:** Tertullian travelled nowhere on Thecla's account, so his testimony is carried as a `source` on her Myra commission stop, the claim he attacks. See §8.

## 4. Reception, and who is not in the file

- **Methodius of Olympus** (d. c. 311), *Symposium (Banquet of the Ten Virgins)*, late 3rd or early 4th century. **[A on work and date: Wikipedia]** **Correction and tag upgrade:** Thecla is the **eighth and climactic** virgin-speaker (Discourse VIII), and at the close she leads the concluding hymn, **Discourse XI ch. 2**, whose recurring **chorus**, sung by the virgins after each of her stanzas, is **"I keep myself pure for Thee, O Bridegroom, and holding a lighted torch I go to meet Thee."** Verified at newadvent.org/fathers/062311.htm (ANF vol. 6). The dossier had it as the hymn's opening line in Discourse VIII. **R moves to A.**
- **Gregory of Nyssa**, *Life of Macrina*: their mother secretly named Macrina "Thecla" before birth. **[R:** in the *Life*, but not in the Wikipedia Macrina article, and no primary text fetched.**]** **Regional correction:** the dossier placed the household in Cappadocia at 38.64 / 34.83; the estate was **Annisa on the river Iris, in Pontus**, modern Amasya province, roughly 40.6 / 36.6, some 250 km north.
- **Gregory of Nazianzus** (retreat at the sanctuary, a pilgrimage site by 374 **[A: Wikipedia, "Aya Tekla Church"]**) and **Egeria** (c. 384, with the deaconess **Marthana** and the reading-aloud of the Acts, both **[R]** here, §9) travelled to her shrine themselves. Those are their stops, not hers: see §8.

## 5. The shrine (journey segment 5)

- **Hagia Thekla / Meryemlik / Ayatekla**, **36.362857 / 33.930988**, **1.5-2 km south of Silifke**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Aya Tekla Church"]** The dossier's "2.5 km" is overstated: the distance computed from its own coordinates is about 1.48 km, agreeing with Egeria's "about fifteen hundred paces" outside the city.
- **By 374** a pilgrimage site exists; **by 384** monastic cells for men and women, a central church, an enclosing wall. **[A]**
- **Zeno** reigned January 474 to April 491 with an interruption **[A: Wikipedia, "Zeno (emperor)"]** and commissioned the hilltop basilica, about 55 by 37 m, dated 460-470 by one article and "5th century" by another (§7.8). Wikipedia's biography of Zeno mentions neither Thecla nor the shrine, so the patronage is **[R]**.
- Surviving remains (cave church, basilica ruins, six cisterns, aqueduct and bath fragments, tombs, monastic and defensive structures) and the claim that systematic excavation began in 1907 come from a source that is now dead, §9. **Herzfeld and Guyer's 1907 survey, published as *Meriamlik und Korykos*, MAMA II (1930)**, should replace it. **[R]**
- **Life and Miracles of Saint Thecla**, anonymous (the ascription to Basil of Seleucia is rejected), c. 445-474: **forty-six miracles** at the sanctuary, including her appearing to correct its own clergy. **[A on existence and setting: Wikipedia, "Thecla"; R on the dating, §9]**

## 6. The places that claim her (journey segments 6 and 7)

**Iconium, 354.** Later tradition credits her with turning back an Isaurian raid, absent from the second-century Acts. **[R: Wikipedia, "Konya", unverifiable beyond that mention]**

**Maaloula, Syria (33.8442 / 36.5467).** Convent of **Mar Takla** around a cave and spring; local tradition has the mountain splitting open at her prayer, giving the town its Aramaic name, roughly "entrance," which is incompatible with the Seleucian ending. **[R: Wikipedia, "Maaloula"]** **The 2013 seizure, corrected:** twelve nuns, not the dossier's thirteen plus three attendants; released **9 March 2014** via Lebanese officials for roughly **150 Syrian women** held by the government. Date discrepancy at §7.6. **[A]**

**Rome (catacomb 41.8377 / 12.482, approximate; Paul's tomb 41.8587 / 12.4767).** A fourth-century catacomb in a reused pagan necropolis carries her name, with no claim that she is buried in it; rediscovered 1870, its frescoes laser-cleaned 2008-2010, yielding what is believed to be the oldest surviving image of Paul. **[A: Wikipedia, "Catacomb of Saint Thecla"]** A separate late tradition has her die or be buried near Paul's grave; the basilica's article contains **no mention of Thecla**, so this rests on the variant endings alone. **[R]**

**Mosfiloti, Cyprus (34.9519 / 33.4247), corrected.** Wikipedia "Thecla" names **Mosfiloti**, not the Larnaca coast: nuns, a healing spring, "reputedly founded by Saint Helena," relics said to have come with refugees from the **Mamluk conquest of Cilicia**. The dossier's 34.965 / 33.965 appears guessed from the unrelated Agia Thekla beach near Ayia Napa. **[A on the site; R on relics and foundation]** **The Cypriot Saint Menas inscription is dropped from the file and kept here:** "of the martyr Thecla," read by early-twentieth-century scholars as evidence for a historical first-century Thecla, but Wikipedia gives no location and the dossier's 34.8 / 32.4 lands unsupported in the Troodos foothills. **No pin placed**: a real claim with no place attached. **[R]**

**The Mareotis, Egypt (31.15 / 29.85, regional).** Coptic women take her as patron of virginity and healing, on wall paintings, stelae, textiles, lamps, combs and ampullae. **The flasks pairing Menas and Thecla are the mass-produced ampullae of ABU MENA in Egypt**, not Cypriot as the dossier had it. **[R on the corpus; the Abu Mena attribution is the correction]**

**Ephesus, the Grotto of Paul (37.9411 / 27.3419).** Disputed late-fourth-century fresco: Paul beside a woman usually identified as Thecla, her eyes and raised hand deliberately destroyed, the man untouched. Kept at the general Ephesus pin, with the doubt in `date_confidence`. **[R, §9]**

**Tarragona (41.1192 / 1.2581).** **Relic corrected:** not a bare 1321. NASSCAL cites *La traslación del brazo de Santa Tecla desde Armenia a Tarragona (1319-1323)*, so the translation is **1319-1323** and the arm came **from Armenia**, Armenian Cilicia, the same refugee dispersal Wikipedia invokes for the Cyprus relics; never scientifically tested. **[A]** A marble altar c. 1220 and Vallfogona's alabaster retable 1426-1434 follow **[A: NASSCAL]**, one relief showing **her arm coming out of the cave into the hands of clergy**, a legend built to explain the relic and present in no manuscript of the Acts **[R]**.

## 7. Contradictions between sources (not adjudicated)

1. **Which Antioch.** This pass: Pisidian Antioch, 38.3061 / 31.1892. The atlas: Antioch on the Orontes, 36.205 / 36.16 in `margarita_pelagia`, a pin shared with `constantino_el_grande`, `al_kamil`, `saint_erasmus` and `san_cristobal`. About 700 km apart, and visible on the map.
2. **Her age at the fire.** Seventeen (Walker) against eighteen (modern retellings).
3. **The text's own arithmetic.** 17 + 72 = 89, against "all the years of her life were ninety."
4. **The sons of Onesiphorus.** Silas and Zeno (Walker) against Simmias and Zeno (Lipsius, James).
5. **How she ends.** Rock-fissure at Seleucia; natural death and burial at Seleucia; rock-fissure at Maaloula; death or burial at Rome. Four traditions behind eight distinct Greek endings.
6. **Maaloula count and date.** Twelve nuns seized Monday 2 December 2013 (Wikipedia "Thecla") against the town captured 3 December 2013 with twelve nuns taken (Wikipedia "Maaloula"); the dossier's thirteen plus three attendants unsupported by either.
7. **Tarragona chapel.** Josep Prat i Delorta, 1760-1775 (NASSCAL) against 1777, late Baroque over the former baptistery (Wikipedia). The stop is dated 1777.
8. **Zeno's basilica.** Dated 460-470, but Zeno's reign begins January 474.
9. **Tryphaena.** Contested conjecture, stated as settled fact in existing atlas prose.
10. **The self-baptism formula.** ANF "I am baptized on my last day" against the atlas's "do I baptize myself on the last day."

## 8. Apparatus relations for the operator

**Saint Thecla is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** The relations below are not rooted in her own life and are **not silently deleted**. They are recorded here and carried into the journey file only as `sources` and `suggested_refs`, never staged as scenes.

**Direction caught: FORWARD in every case.** These are later people reaching back to cite, defend, depict or visit her. As stops in her file they would be new hashes in an old block, which the exceptions file says cannot be written; as sources on her own stops they are legitimate one-directional citations, and that is where they are.

| relation | what it is | where it went |
|---|---|---|
| **Tertullian**, De Baptismo 17, c. 198-203 | earliest external witness; attacks the use of her example to license women teaching and baptizing | source on the Myra commission stop; no Carthage stop |
| **Methodius of Olympus**, Symposium, late 3rd c. | makes her the eighth and climactic virgin-speaker, gives her the concluding hymn | source on the Seleucia cave stop; no Olympus stop, a literary setting is not a place |

To put any of this on the map as Thecla's own, the route is to add her to `EXCEPTIONS.md`, which would be a novel use of it: she has no apparatus to apply backward, and what would be licensed instead is *reception* material reaching forward. The author's call.

**Interlocks named in campa: none.** Thecla engages with nobody who has an atlas file. Paul has no `paul_of_tarsus.journey.json` (QUEUE.md pairs the subjects; the file does not exist), so every Paul appearance here is self-contained rather than shared-pinned. Everyone else in the record engages *her*, one-directionally and posthumously, which by the standing rule is a source, not a name in prose. Shared geography alone (Konya's later Sufi layer; Reus for Gaudí) was checked and rejected as a naming reason.

## 9. Honest gaps

- **No first-century evidence of any kind.** Not a gap in the research; a gap in the world.
- **Egeria's *Itinerarium*, full text.** 404s at tertullian.org, ccel.org, fourthcentury.com and wikisource, so Marthana and the reading-aloud are **[R]** here.
- **turkisharchaeonews.net, "Saint Thecla Church and Cave in Silifke."** 404 at every URL form; everything drawn from it is unverified.
- **"Meryemlik" and "Ayatekla" as Wikipedia titles:** 404. The site is covered under "Aya Tekla Church."
- **The Ephesus grotto fresco**, **the Egyptian material corpus** (stelae, textiles, lamps, combs), **the Tryphaena identification** and **the c. 445-474 dating of the Life and Miracles**: all four rest on the dossier alone, with no academic source reached for any of them.
- **Methodius:** the hymn and its Discourse XI placement verified directly; her position as eighth speaker is standard reference-work knowledge, not re-fetched.
