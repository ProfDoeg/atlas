# Josefina Ayerza: research report

*2026-08-16. Josefina Ayerza (b. 1950, Buenos Aires): psychoanalyst, art critic, founder and editor of* Lacanian Ink *(1990), builder of lacan.com (1997). Living subject; no death record exists.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, no document. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method note.** Most of this subject's attested geography comes from her own dated captions at `lacan.com/josefinaslide/`: at once the best source (dated to the day, venue-named) and the weakest (self-authored, unverifiable, internally contradictory in at least one place). The two Wikipedia articles are thin; skeleton facts only.

## 2. Paris, the 1970s

**The move. [A, decade only]** "In the 1970s, she relocated to Paris" (`en.wikipedia`; `es.wikipedia` agrees). **No year, no arrondissement, no reason, no companion.**

> **Correction applied.** An earlier pass entered `date: 1970` and a stop at "Left Bank, Paris (48.8499 / 2.3441)". Both unsupported: the sources give a decade and a city. The file pins generic Paris (48.8566, 2.3522) and marks the January-1970 stamp a placeholder.

**Lacan's seminar. [R, explicitly not attested]** The seminar ran in Paris throughout the decade (Faculté de Droit, Place du Panthéon, among its venues). **No document places Ayerza in the room.** The relation is textual and runs backward from New York: she later hosts the seminars online and writes of "my love for Jacques Lacan". Per the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, the file does **not** stage her inside the seminar; it records the relation at a generic Paris pin and cites the venue only in `suggested_refs`.

**Training and analysis. [R]** By the 1990s she is a member of the **World Association of Psychoanalysis** and affiliated with the **New Lacanian School** (`lacan.com/perfume/josefinaayerza.htm`; `en.wikipedia`). A 1970s Paris residence plus a Miller-circle home by the 1990s makes formation there, during or after Lacan's January 1980 dissolution of the École Freudienne, the natural inference. **No source names an institution, an analyst, a supervisor, or a date.**

**Paris-New York Psychoanalytic Workshop, 2 April 1989. [A on date; location demoted]** `lacan.com/frameI1.htm` carries her piece *Jacques-Alain Miller's Perversion*, datelined "Paris-New York Psychoanalytic Workshop, April 2, 1989".

> **Three corrections applied.** (1) **Genre:** the page is **not an interview with Miller** but Ayerza's own account of his closing address; "Ayerza interviews Miller" is withdrawn. (2) **Venue:** the dateline names the workshop and the date and **no city**; the earlier pin at the École de la Cause freudienne, 1 rue Huysmans, is unsupported and removed. (3) **Coordinate:** the values formerly used for 1 rue Huysmans (48.8482 / 2.3283) sit ~350 m north of the actual address (approx. 48.8452 / 2.3288); the corrected value is used on the affiliation stop, the only place rue Huysmans still appears.

**Affiliation vs. attendance. [A / gap]** WAP membership is attested; **attendance at any Paris congress or meeting is not** (no congress list, no minute, no photograph), and `date_confidence` says so.

- **LI 17, 30 Nov 2000.** Called "unspecified" with a generic midtown pin by an earlier pass. The caption names **Deitch Projects, West Broadway**, theme **Aesthetics** (not "Esthetics"). *Residual contradiction:* Deitch's principal SoHo space 1996–2010 was **18 Wooster Street**. The file pins SoHo (40.7233 / -74.0030) and adjudicates nothing.
- **Tilton, 14 Nov 2001.** An earlier pass gave "8 Wooster Street". **No Jack Tilton Gallery existed there**; the gallery moved to **Greene Street** in 1993 and stayed to 2005, and the "8" is contamination from the later 8 East 76th Street address. Pin moved to approx. **40.7229 / -74.0013**.
- **LI 24/25, wrong by two to four years.** An earlier pass dated it "c.2007-2009" and said the caption gave no date. It reads "Deitch Projects, NYC, April 1, 2005 - Lacanian Ink 24/25 launching on Eroticism". Corrected to **2005-04-01**; no street number, so the pin follows 18 Wooster Street.

## 6. Cadaqués: the one autobiographical text

**"When I Come to Cadaques I Always Stay Here". [A]** *The Symptom* 18 (`lacan.com/symptom/alex-kayser/`): first-person memoir of the Costa Brava town, of photographer **Alex Kayser**, and of meeting **H. R. Giger**. Verbatim lines carried into the journey file: "You are…Hans Giger, who did Alien?"; "The place was in fact very nice, the Mediterranean blue as ever, the stones, oh well, no sand, we loved, enjoyed every part of it."; "Because of my love for Jacques Lacan I was promptly taken by the way Alex would focus on body parts." (A fourth, on Kayser's height and hair, is quoted in the report only.)

**Dating. [gap]** Neither the events nor the essay carries a year in any reachable source. *The Symptom* 18 is the archive's last issue (Fall 2019) but the essay recalls an earlier period. The three Cadaqués stops carry placeholder 1998 dates whose `date_confidence` calls the placement editorial.

**The photographs. [A, an earlier pass had the sense inverted]**

> **Correction applied.** An earlier pass quoted Gueguen praising "wonderful pictures" and speculated, as a flagged gap, that Ayerza was the photographer. The verbatim testimonial at `lacan.com/lacan1.htm` reads: *"A beautiful issue. A perfect choice of texts and wonderful pictures. I especially noticed ones taken of you by Alex Kayser in our beloved Cadaques."* The photographs were taken **of Ayerza, by Kayser**. She is the sitter. The speculation is deleted. **Adrian Dannatt**, on the same page, adds "especially love the Dali photos of Dali and you".

**Recurrence. [R]** The title implies a habit of returning that predates the essay. Not corroborated. *L'Âne, listed among her Paris outlets, is likewise undated as to whether contributing needed any travel.*

**Books. [A, thin]** *Voyeurism* (2002) and *Lacanian Ink 37* (2011), both in the `es.wikipedia` bibliography only. No publisher, page count, or review; **no copy of *Voyeurism* located**. Entered in the journey file as a book that exists and has not been read.
