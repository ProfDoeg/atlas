# Abu al-Qasim al-Qushayri (376-465 AH / 986-1072 CE): research report

*Subject: Abu 'l-Qasim 'Abd al-Karim b. Hawazin al-Qushayri, of Ustuwa and Nishapur. Author of al-Risala al-Qushayriyya, the epistle that turned the sayings of the early Sufis into a teachable, defensible science. Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys, 2026-08.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, with the source named in the line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference (including road stages nobody records him on). Contradictions between sources are printed as contradictions and left standing where the evidence does not settle them; where the pool's verification stage did settle one, the correction is stated with its reason.

The spine of this report is Alexander Knysh's translator's introduction to *Al-Qushayri's Epistle on Sufism* (Garnet, 2007), pp. xxi-xxiv, read against English and Arabic Wikipedia, the TDV *Islam Ansiklopedisi*, and the devotional biography at imamghazali.org. Where those four disagree, the disagreement is recorded below.

---

## 1. Name, birth, and the district that is not Nishapur

- Born 376 AH / 986 CE **[A: Knysh, p. xxi]**. One line of the pool gives the month as Rabi al-Awwal **[R: Arabic Wikipedia]**; no day is preserved anywhere reached.
- Birthplace: a village of the **Ustuwa** district, northern Khurasan, a grain-growing country **[A: Knysh, p. xxi]**. Ustuwa is the district of Khabushan, modern **Quchan**, 37.105 N / 58.506 E **[A: TDV, "Ustuwa region near Kuchan"; Wikipedia, "Quchan, also known as Gochan and Khabushan"]**.
  - *Correction recorded, not silent:* the pool's geography lens originally pinned Ustuwa at 36.4 / 59.2 and called it "northeast of Nishapur." That pin sits 99.8 km southeast of Quchan and 41.7 km from Nishapur, and Quchan is north-northwest of Nishapur, not northeast. The afterlife lens separately placed the birth "in the Nishapur region" at the city pin, following English Wikipedia's loose "Nishapur, Khorasan." Three lenses of the same pool disagreed by about 100 km. Resolved to Quchan on TDV plus Knysh; English Wikipedia's district-level looseness is the weaker reading, and is named here rather than quietly dropped.
  - The **specific village is not named in any source reached.** That is a gap, not a coordinate to be invented. The pin marks the district.
- Family: Arabs of the **Banu Qushayr**, descended from the conquest armies, granted Khurasani land in compensation for military service **[R: Knysh, p. xxi]**. Knysh judges the household likely Persian-speaking despite the Arab descent **[R]**.
- Arabic Wikipedia adds that his father died in his childhood and his mother raised him **[A: Arabic Wikipedia]**. Knysh does not mention it. Not a contradiction, an asymmetry of coverage.
- Boyhood training: Arabic language, poetry, *adab*; also martial arts, horsemanship, archery, "as befits a country squire's son" **[R: Knysh, p. xxi]**.

## 2. Arrival in Nishapur and the master al-Daqqaq

- He travels to Nishapur as a youth; some sources say at fifteen, and say the errand was a petition to reduce the tax on a village he owned. **Knysh flags the tax story as unverifiable [R]**, and this report keeps that flag. The journey itself is attested; the motive is tradition.
- In Nishapur he attends **Abu 'Ali al-Hasan al-Daqqaq [A: Knysh, p. xxi; Arabic and English Wikipedia]**, who heads a madrasa and a preaching seat.
- English Wikipedia dates the madrasa's foundation to **1001 CE**. This is a **single-source claim not corroborated in Knysh [R]**, and it is also the date the afterlife lens uses for al-Daqqaq's building of the school later called al-Madrasa al-Qushayriyya.
- Al-Daqqaq's own line: student of **Ibrahim b. Muhammad al-Nasrabadhi (d. 367/977)**, standing in a chain that reaches back through **Abu Bakr al-Shibli (d. 334/946)** to **al-Junayd al-Baghdadi (d. 297/910) [A: Knysh, p. xxi]**.
- Al-Qushayri becomes al-Daqqaq's foremost disciple and marries his daughter **Fatima [A: Knysh; Wikipedia]**. **No source gives the marriage year**; the journey file places it c. 1010 as an approximation before al-Daqqaq's death and says so in `date_confidence`.
- Al-Daqqaq introduces him to **Abu 'Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami (d. 412/1021) [A: Knysh, p. xxi]**, quoted on almost every page of the later Epistle.
- On al-Daqqaq's death he succeeds to the circle and becomes leader of Nishapur's Sufi assemblies **[A: Knysh]**. Knysh dates the death only as "around 405/1015 **or somewhat later**" and this report does not sharpen it. The geography lens independently drifted to "1020s-1030s" for the succession, which conflicts with its own chronology lens; the earlier bracket (1015-1021) is the one the sources support.

## 3. The three sciences

- **Ash'arite kalam**: under **Abu Bakr ibn Furak (d. 406/1015)** and **Abu Ishaq al-Isfarayini (d. 418/1027) [A: Knysh, p. xxii]**; Arabic Wikipedia adds **'Abd al-Qahir al-Baghdadi**, the heresiographer relocated from Baghdad **[A: Arabic Wikipedia]**.
- **Shafi'i jurisprudence**: under **Abu Bakr al-Tusi (d. 420/1029) [A: Arabic Wikipedia; Knysh, p. xxii, in the fuller form "Abu Bakr Muhammad b. Abi Bakr al-Tusi"]**.
  - *Correction recorded:* the pool's chronology entry read "Muhammad b. Bakr al-Tusi," a form attested nowhere. The date (420 AH began 19 January 1029) is right; the name was garbled in transmission into the pool. Fixed to Abu Bakr al-Tusi.
- **Hadith**: he hears **al-Hakim al-Nishaburi [A: Arabic and English Wikipedia]**, and per **Hamid Algar** studies under at least **seventeen** authorities while transmitting to as many as **sixty-six** students **[R: Algar, introduction to *Principles of Sufism*, p. iii, cited by Knysh, p. xxii]**. Algar's figures are reported at one remove and are marked [R] for that reason, not because they are doubted.

## 4. The pilgrimage

- He performs the hajj with the hadith scholars **Abu Muhammad al-Juwayni (d. 438/1047)** and **Ahmad al-Bayhaqi (d. 458/1066) [A: Knysh, p. xxii]**.
- **The year is not fixed by any source reached.** This is the single largest dating gap in the life. The pool's chronology lens places it loosely at c. 1030 (before al-Juwayni's 1047 death); the geography lens says "c. 410 AH / early 11th century, exact year not confirmed," i.e. c. 1019-20. **The two lenses differ by roughly a decade and this report does not adjudicate.** The journey file uses 1030 and carries the contradiction in `date_confidence`.
- **The road stages are reconstruction, not testimony [R].** No source places al-Qushayri at Rayy, Hamadan, Hulwan, or Kufa. They are the fixed stages of the Khurasan road and the Darb Zubayda, and the atlas pins them as itinerary, labelled as such.
  - *Correction recorded:* Hulwan was pinned at 34.2167 / 45.65, which is 33 km off the pass road. Wikipedia ("Hulwan, Iran") gives 34°27'54"N 45°51'18"E, identifying the site with modern Sarpol-e Zahab at the mouth of the Paytak pass. Fixed to 34.465 / 45.855.
- **Medina** is inference **[R]**: the Hijaz journey is attested, the specific stop at the Prophet's mosque is customary practice and is not itemized in the sources.
- **Mecca** is attested **[A: Knysh; Wikipedia]**. Later further pilgrimages are asserted by the traditional biography **[R]** and are not given separate stops here.

## 5. The books

- **al-Taysir fi'l-tafsir**, a conventional historical, philological, and legal Qur'an commentary, reportedly composed **before 410/1019 [A: Knysh, p. xxiv]**.
- **Lata'if al-isharat**, the mystical commentary: **begun 434 AH / 1042-43 [A: TDV Islam Ansiklopedisi]**.
  - *Correction recorded:* the pool's chronology entry dated Lata'if to 410/1019, the same year as al-Taysir, and the coincidence looks like contamination from the adjacent line. Two consecutive works cannot both be 410 AH; TDV gives 434 AH as the year he began it, immediately before the Risala. Corrected, with the error's likely mechanism stated.
- **al-Risala al-Qushayriyya**, completed **437 AH / 1045 [A: Knysh, p. xxiv]**, and dated in the author's own opening line to the year 437 **[A: primary text, Knysh tr., p. 2]**.
  - **Contradiction left standing:** English Wikipedia's article on the Risala says it was "written in 1046." A further muddle in the same article pairs 1046 CE with "465 AH," which is al-Qushayri's death year, not 1046. That looks like a source error rather than a live scholarly dispute, but it is recorded rather than silently repaired.
- Contents **[A: Wikipedia, "Al-Risala al-Qushayriyya"; Knysh, p. xxiv]**: part one, 83 biographies in rough chronological order from **Ibrahim b. Adham (d. 162/778)** to **Ahmad ibn Ata al-Rudhbari (d. 369/980)**; part two, 27 technical terms; part three, 44 stations and states.
  - *Correction recorded:* the pool gave al-Rudhbari's death as "369/960," but 369 AH is 979-980 CE, and the entry's own gloss ("six years before al-Qushayri's own birth") only works with 980. Self-contradictory; corrected to 980.
  - *Gap:* the pool's claim that al-Sulami's *Tabaqat al-Sufiyya* contains 103 biographies **could not be verified**; the figure usually cited in the scholarship is 105. The comparison is kept out of the journey file.

## 6. The Saljuq years, the vizier, and the citadel

- Nishapur falls under **Saljuq control in 429/1038 [A: Knysh, p. xxii]**, after which al-Qushayri is drawn into the Hanafi versus Shafi'i contest for the city.
- **436/1045**: he issues a manifesto asserting himself as spokesman of the Shafi'i-Ash'ari party **[R: Knysh, p. xxii]**, in the same year as the Risala.
- **1053**: the Saljuq vizier **Amid al-Mulk al-Kunduri** institutes public cursing of Ash'arites from the pulpits of Nishapur **[A: Wikipedia, "Al-Kunduri"; corroborated by Grokipedia and by the University of Tehran *Journal of the History of Islamic Civilization*]**.
- **Arrested and imprisoned for a week in the citadel of Nishapur [A: Knysh, p. xxii; TDV, which specifies the city's ancient fortress and dates the arrest to c. 446/1054; Wikipedia's Risala article, which says al-Kunduri "publicly denounced and jailed Ash'ari scholars, including al-Qushayri"]**.
  - *Correction recorded:* the pool's geography lens tagged this [R] and framed it as an unresolved conflict because the English Wikipedia article on his life omits an imprisonment. An omission in one encyclopedia article is not a contradiction of three other sources that state it. Retagged [A]; the "encyclopedia conflict" framing is dropped.
- **Released in 446/1054** after his followers threaten a general rising of Nishapur's Shafi'ites **[A: Knysh, p. xxii]**; TDV has armed supporters freeing him.
- **448/1056**: invited by the Abbasid caliph **al-Qa'im** to hold hadith sessions in the caliphal palace at Baghdad **[A: Knysh, p. xxii; Wikipedia; TDV]**.
  - *Correction recorded:* the geography lens called the year unconfirmed while its own chronology lens gave 448/1056. The year is confirmed; the hedge was wrong.
- **On return he cannot re-enter Nishapur** and goes to **Tus [A: Knysh, p. xxii]**.
  - **Contradiction left standing:** Knysh says he settles at Tus; TDV says only that he visited Tus and other Khurasani cities in these years. "Settles" is stronger than the non-Knysh evidence carries, and the journey file says so.
  - *Correction recorded:* Tus was pinned at 36.3283 / 59.6046, which is 3.5 km from central Mashhad and 18.8 km from Tus. Fixed to 36.4792 / 59.5097 per Wikipedia.
- **455/1063**: **Nizam al-Mulk** reverses al-Kunduri's policy and restores the balance between the factions **[A: Knysh, p. xxii]**; al-Qushayri, seventy-nine by the lunar count, returns to Nishapur and stays until death **[A]**.

## 7. Household, disciples, death

- **Children:** *contradiction, partly resolved.* The pool's chronology claimed six sons by Fatima and three more by a second wife, nine in all. **No accessible source supports nine.** English Wikipedia: "six sons and several daughters between Fatima and his second wife," i.e. six sons **total**. TDV: Fatima bore six sons and one daughter. The "three further sons" is uncorroborated and reads as a misparse. The journey file states six sons and at least one daughter by Fatima, further children by a second wife of unrecorded number, and names the disagreement.
- **Few direct Sufi disciples**, despite the renown; he keeps a lodge in Nishapur for those he has. Knysh attributes the scarcity of successors to a temperament "academic and methodical rather than charismatic and inspirational" **[R: Knysh, p. xxii]**. That is Knysh's judgment, not a documented fact, and is tagged accordingly.
- The exception: **Abu 'Ali al-Fadl b. Muhammad al-Farmadhi (d. 477/1084)**, later teacher of **Abu Hamid al-Ghazali (d. 505/1111) [A: Knysh, p. xxii]**. Wikipedia relays the scholar Shaykh Amin's judgment that al-Ghazali's *Ihya Ulum al-Din* builds on the Risala's blueprint **[R, at one remove]**.
- **Death: Sunday, 16 Rabi al-Akhir 465 AH = 30 December 1072 (Julian) [A: Wikipedia; TDV]**.
  - **Contradiction, and this one is resolvable.** imamghazali.org gives "Sunday morning, the 16th of Rabi' al-Awwal, 465 AH (30 November 1072)." TDV gives 16 Rabi **al-Thani** (Rabi II / al-Akhir). Tabular conversion: 16 Rabi I 465 = 29 November 1072, a Thursday; 16 Rabi II 465 = 29 December 1072, and 30 December 1072 Julian was a Sunday. The tradition specifies Sunday morning, so only the December date is self-consistent. The devotional site has the month wrong. Recorded with the working, not asserted by authority.
- **Buried near al-Madrasa al-Qushayriyya, beside al-Daqqaq [A: Wikipedia; imamghazali.org]**. The exact spot within Nishapur is not preserved.

## 8. Afterlife

- **1220**: a manuscript copy reaches **North Africa [A: Wikipedia, "Al-Risala al-Qushayriyya"]**. The source names no city; the atlas pin marks the region and says so.
- **1221**: Nishapur destroyed over ten days by the Mongols avenging Genghis Khan's slain son-in-law, the population killed and skulls piled in pyramids **[A: Wikipedia, "Nishapur"]**.
  - **Gap, explicitly:** no source reached states whether al-Qushayri's grave or the Qushayriyya madrasa survived. Do not treat the tomb as a confirmed loss; treat it as unknown.
- Repeated earthquakes across the 12th and 13th centuries compound the damage **[A: Wikipedia, "Nishapur"]**. The buried old city is excavated by the **Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1935-1940 and 1947-48 [A]**; **no report names his grave among the finds [gap]**.
- By contrast, Attar's tomb receives a mausoleum from **Ali-Shir Nava'i** in the 16th century and a renovation under **Reza Shah in 1940 [A: Wikipedia, "Attar of Nishapur"]**. **No comparable monument for al-Qushayri is documented in anything reached.** Whether any modern marker exists at his grave is unknown.
- Translations **[A: Wikipedia, "Al-Risala al-Qushayriyya"]**: oldest surviving is a medieval Persian rendering by **Abu Ali al-Usmani**, later critically edited by **Badi al-Zaman Furuzanfar** (place and date of that edition not given in the sources reached). English: partial, **von Schlegell, 1990**; **Harris and Bakhtiar, 1997/2002**; first complete annotated English, **Knysh, 2007**. Turkish **Uludag, 1978** (13th printing by 2025), German **Gramlich, 1989**, French **Fontenay, 2016**.

## 9. Interlock with the existing atlas

- `abu_yazid_al_bistami.journey.json` already **cites Qushayri by name** ("Encyclopaedia Iranica, citing Qushayri p. 38") for Bistami's letters. The atlas has been leaning on his reporting without giving him a file **[A]**.
- Shared canonical pins inherited byte-identical: **Nishapur city 36.2064 / 58.7958** (as `attar.journey.json` fixes it), **Kaaba 21.4225 / 39.8262** (hallaj, rabia, bistami), **Medina 24.4672 / 39.6111**, **Kufa 32.0286 / 44.4009** (bistami's pilgrim-road stop), **Baghdad 33.3152 / 44.3661** (junayd, bistami), **Mongol-sack Nishapur 36.2131 / 58.7975** and **Shadiyakh 36.1671 / 58.807** (attar).
- Mutual gaze in the campa: **junayd_al_baghdadi** (the transmission chain that reaches al-Daqqaq), **abu_yazid_al_bistami** and **ibrahim_ibn_adham** (the pilgrim road, and the Epistle's first biography), **hallaj** and **rabia** (the Meccan courtyard and the Risala's notices), **attar**, **rumi**, **baha_ud_din_walad** (the same Nishapur ground, two generations later), **genghis_khan** (1221).
- QUEUE.md, line 185, lists him between Junayd al-Baghdadi and Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani as author of "the foundational manual systematizing early Sufi biography and doctrine" **[A]**. A grep of all `*.journey.json` confirms no existing file takes him as subject **[A]**.

## 10. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **The village of his birth** within Ustuwa: not named anywhere reached.
2. **The year of the hajj**: two lenses of the same pool differ by a decade; no source fixes it.
3. **The marriage year to Fatima**: no source gives one.
4. **Al-Daqqaq's death year**: "around 405/1015 or somewhat later" is as close as Knysh will come.
5. **The number of children by the second wife**: sources disagree and none is decisive.
6. **The site of al-Madrasa al-Qushayriyya** within Nishapur's old town: not preserved, not located.
7. **The tomb's form, inscription, condition, and survival after 1221**: entirely unknown from accessible material.
8. **Place and date of Furuzanfar's edition** of the Persian Usmani translation: not given.
9. **Al-Sulami's biography count** in the *Tabaqat*: unverified (103 vs the usual 105).
