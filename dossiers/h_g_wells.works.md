## Works by Herbert George Wells

### Novels and book-length fictions

- **1895:** *The Time Machine*. A scientific romance developed from the earlier, unfinished serial *The Chronic Argonauts*. [1][2]
- **1895:** *The Wonderful Visit*. A satirical fantasy novel about an angel displaced into Victorian England. [1]
- **1896:** *The Island of Doctor Moreau*. A scientific romance and ethical fable. [1]
- **1896:** *The Wheels of Chance*. A comic bicycling novel. [1]
- **1897:** *The Invisible Man*. A scientific romance. [1]
- **1898:** *The War of the Worlds*. A scientific romance of invasion and imperial reversal. [1]
- **1899; revised 1910:** *When the Sleeper Wakes* / *The Sleeper Awakes*. A future-society novel substantially revised and retitled by Wells; the two forms are manifestations of one continuing obra, not simple reprints. [1]
- **1900:** *Love and Mr Lewisham*. A realist novel of education, work, and marriage. [1]
- **1901:** *The First Men in the Moon*. A lunar scientific romance. [1]
- **1902:** *The Sea Lady*. A fantasy and social satire. [1]
- **1904:** *The Food of the Gods and How It Came to Earth*. A scientific romance about uncontrolled biological enlargement. [1]
- **1905:** *Kipps*. A comic realist novel. [1]
- **1905:** *A Modern Utopia*. A hybrid philosophical dialogue, travel narrative, and speculative novel; it resists a clean division between fiction and social project. [1][3]
- **1906:** *In the Days of the Comet*. A scientific romance and utopian transformation narrative. [1]
- **1908:** *The War in the Air*. A future-war novel. [1]
- **1909:** *Tono-Bungay*. A realist novel whose commercial satire expands into a diagnosis of English society. [1]
- **1909:** *Ann Veronica*. A novel of women’s independence, education, and sexual politics. [1]
- **1910:** *The History of Mr Polly*. A comic realist novel. [1]
- **1911:** *The New Machiavelli*. A political novel with strong autobiographical elements. [1]
- **1912:** *Marriage*. A social novel. [1]
- **1913:** *The Passionate Friends*. A novel of intimacy, marriage, and social purpose. [1]
- **1914:** *The Wife of Sir Isaac Harman*. A social novel about marriage and women’s autonomy. [1]
- **1914:** *The World Set Free*. A scientific romance that imagines atomic warfare and world government. [1]
- **1915:** *Bealby*. A comic novel. [1]
- **1915:** *Boon*. A satirical composite of fiction, dialogue, parody, and criticism, issued under the persona “Reginald Bliss”; its pseudonymous framing makes authorship part of the object. [1]
- **1915:** *The Research Magnificent*. A novel of intellectual and moral aspiration. [1]
- **1916:** *Mr Britling Sees It Through*. A wartime novel and national self-examination. [1]
- **1917:** *The Soul of a Bishop*. A religious and social novel. [1]
- **1918:** *Joan and Peter*. A long educational novel and generational chronicle. [1]
- **1919:** *The Undying Fire*. A theological novel recasting the Book of Job. [1]
- **1922:** *The Secret Places of the Heart*. A psychological novel. [1]
- **1923:** *Men Like Gods*. A utopian scientific romance. [1]
- **1924:** *The Dream*. A future-framed historical and social novel. [1]
- **1925:** *Christina Alberta’s Father*. A psychological and social novel. [1]
- **1926:** *The World of William Clissold*. A six-book novel of ideas whose essayistic form resists a strict fiction/nonfiction boundary. [1]
- **1927:** *Meanwhile*. A political novel subtitled “The Picture of a Lady.” [1]
- **1928:** *Mr. Blettsworthy on Rampole Island*. A satirical fantasy novel. [1]
- **1930:** *The Autocracy of Mr. Parham*. A political satire. [1]
- **1932:** *The Bulpington of Blup*. A realist novel about self-invention and fantasy. [1]
- **1933:** *The Shape of Things to Come*. A speculative future history presented as an edited record; it is simultaneously fiction, forecast, and world-state project. [1]
- **1937:** *Brynhild*. A comic novel. [1]
- **1937:** *Star Begotten*. A speculative novel about possible Martian influence. [1]
- **1938:** *Apropos of Dolores*. A novel. [1]
- **1939:** *The Holy Terror*. A political novel presented as the life of a dictator. [1]
- **1940:** *Babes in the Darkling Wood*. A novel of wartime youth and disorientation. [1]
- **1941:** *You Can’t Be Too Careful*. A satirical novel. [1]

### Film projects, scenarios, and screen works

- **1929:** *The King Who Was a King: The Book of a Film*. A written film scenario for a motion picture that was never made; the unrealized film remains a project, while the published scenario is an obra. [1]
- **1935–1936:** *Things to Come*. A screenplay and completed film developed from *The Shape of Things to Come* and *The Work, Wealth and Happiness of Mankind*; its combination of text, design, performance, music, and moving image approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk, although Wells did not control every element. [1]
- **1936:** *The Man Who Could Work Miracles*. A screenplay and completed film derived from Wells’s earlier story. [1]
- **1936:** *The New Faust*. An unproduced screen adaptation of *The Story of the Late Mr. Elvesham*, published as a scenario; it survives as a project rather than a finished film. [1]

### Forecasts, social analysis, and world-history constructions

- **1901:** *Anticipations of the Reactions of Mechanical and Scientific Progress upon Human Life and Thought*. A systematic exercise in social forecasting. [1][3]
- **1902:** *The Discovery of the Future*. A lecture-essay defending disciplined inquiry into possible futures. [1]
- **1903:** *Mankind in the Making*. A work of social analysis and educational prescription. [1][3]
- **1903:** *The English House of the Future*. A collaborative symposium contribution; Wells made only part of the resulting text. [1]
- **1903:** *Scepticism of the Instrument*. An epistemological essay. [1]
- **1906:** *The Future in America*. A travel book and social diagnosis. [1][3]
- **1906:** *The So-Called Science of Sociology*. A polemical sociological paper. [1]
- **1912:** *Great Thoughts from H. G. Wells*. A selection of extracts: a publishing manifestation assembled from earlier texts rather than a wholly new Wells obra. [1]
- **1912:** *Thoughts from H. G. Wells*. Another extract compilation whose status is closer to an editorial manifestation than an independent work. [1]
- **1916:** *What Is Coming?*. A book of wartime forecast and reconstruction. [1]
- **1920:** *The Outline of History*. A synthetic world history repeatedly revised by Wells, with acknowledged research assistance; its successive forms belong to an evolving historical project. [1]
- **1921:** *The New Teaching of History*. A defense and methodological supplement to *The Outline of History*. [1]
- **1921:** *The Grisly Folk*. A speculative essay on prehistoric humanity, sometimes collected with fiction although it is not straightforwardly a story. [1]
- **1922:** *A Short History of the World*. A compressed, independently written world history, later revised. [1]

- **1927:** *Democracy Under Revision*. A political essay. [1]

- **1931:** *The Work, Wealth and Happiness of Mankind*. An encyclopedic survey of human organization and a constructive social project. [1]
- **1938:** *Foretelling the Future*. A retrospective essay on prediction. [1]

### Philosophy, religion, education, play, and protocols

- **1897:** *Certain Personal Matters*. A collection of personal and humorous essays. [1]
- **1908:** *First and Last Things*. A philosophical confession and proposed rule of life. [1][3]
- **1911:** *Floor Games*. An illustrated protocol for imaginative children’s play using miniature worlds. [1][3]
- **1913:** *Little Wars*. A rule system and protocol for miniature warfare; it is also a playable designed object, not simply a book to be read. [1][3]
- **1917:** *God the Invisible King*. A personal theology and religious declaration. [1]
- **1917:** *Introduction to Nocturne*. An introductory essay attached to Frank Swinnerton’s novel; it resists treatment as a freestanding Wells work because its original function is paratextual. [1]
- **1930:** *Divorce as I See It*. A social and personal argument. [1]
- **1930:** *Points of View*. A collection of essays and addresses. [1]
- **1936:** *The Anatomy of Frustration*. A pseudoscientific and philosophical analysis presented through the fictive researcher “William Burroughs Steele”; it deliberately troubles the boundary between treatise and fiction. [1]
- **1938:** *World Brain*. A collection of essays proposing a globally organized, continually updated system of knowledge; beyond the book, “World Brain” is an unrealized information project. [1]
- **1939:** *Travels of a Republican Radical in Search of Hot Water*. A travel book and political-personal meditation. [1]
- **1945:** *The Happy Turning*. A brief dream vision and spiritual-philosophical testament. [1]

### Biography, autobiography, and self-archive

- **1920:** *Frank Swinnerton*. A collaborative biographical book with Arnold Bennett and Grant Overton; Wells authored only one component. [1]
- **1924:** *The Story of a Great Schoolmaster*. A biography of Frederick William Sanderson and an argument about education. [1]
- **1934:** *Experiment in Autobiography*. A two-volume autobiography and intellectual self-analysis. [1]
- **1984, posthumous:** *H. G. Wells in Love: Postscript to An Experiment in Autobiography*. A private autobiographical manuscript published after Wells’s death and edited by G. P. Wells; the writing is Wells’s, but the titled public book is a posthumous editorial manifestation. [1]

### Late and posthumous editorial manifestations

- **1931:** *Selections from the Early Prose Works of H. G. Wells*. A retrospective selection; its contents are earlier obras, while the selection is a distinct editorial object. [1]
- **1946:** *The Time Machine: An Invention and Other Stories*. A lifetime collection combining the novel with fourteen stories; it is a rearrangement of existing works. [1]
- **1975:** *H. G. Wells: Early Writings in Science and Science Fiction*. A posthumous scholarly collection of early fiction and essays; Wells made the component texts but not this editorial object. [1]
- **1984:** *The Man with the Nose and Other Uncollected Stories of H. G. Wells*. A posthumous recovery collection; its book-form is editorial rather than authorial. [1]
- **Later posthumous selections and omnibus collections:** These are manifestations assembled from Wells’s existing texts, not additional works made by him; consequently editions such as *Selected Stories*, *Tales of the Weird and Supernatural*, and similarly retitled anthologies are not enumerated as new obras. [1]
