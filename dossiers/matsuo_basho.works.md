## Works by Matsuo Bashō

### Hokku: an untitled poetic corpus

- **1662–1694:** *Approximately 980 extant hokku*. This is an obra without an authorial collected title: short poems transmitted through manuscripts, poem sheets, correspondence, travel prose, and anthologies rather than a single book made by Bashō. “Haiku” is the customary modern label, but in Bashō’s time these were *hokku*—potentially independent poems and also opening verses for linked compositions; the exact total, texts, and dates vary among complete editions, and some attributions remain doubtful. ([University of California Press](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/basho-2/epub-pdf); [Barnhill, textual note](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780791484654_A40634984/preview-9780791484654_A40634984.pdf))

### Authored and edited haikai books

- **1672:** *Kai Ōi* (貝おほひ, *The Seashell Game*). Bashō’s first independently published book, conceived as a judged competition between paired hokku; it is simultaneously anthology, criticism, comic performance, and manifesto for his early haikai practice. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/basho); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Matsuo-Basho))

- **1680:** *Tōsei Montei Dokugin Nijū Kasen* (桃青門弟独吟廿歌仙, *Twenty Solo Kasen by Tōsei’s Disciples*). A collection assembled under Bashō’s name and direction in which disciples each undertake a thirty-six-link sequence; it is better understood as a pedagogical group project than as a book authored solely by him. ([Yamanashi Prefectural University Bashō database](https://www2.yamanashi-ken.ac.jp/~itoyo/basho/relateddocuments.htm); [Kōhon Bashō Zenshū catalogue](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BN02920424))

- **1683:** *Minashiguri* (虚栗, *Shriveled Chestnuts*). A haikai anthology compiled by Takarai Kikaku, containing Bashō’s poems and his preface; it is frequently attached to Bashō’s bibliography, but the volume itself is Kikaku’s compilation, not Bashō’s sole obra. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/basho); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/literature-and-arts/asian-literature-biographies/basho))

### Travel haibun and diaries

- **1684–1687:** *Nozarashi kikō* (野ざらし紀行, *Journal of Bleached Bones in a Field* or *Travelogue of Weather-Beaten Bones*). A travel haibun made from prose, headnotes, and hokku after Bashō’s 1684–85 journey; the received work was revised later, so journey date and textual completion date do not coincide. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/); [Nozarashi Kikō overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nozarashi_Kik%C5%8D))

- **1687:** *Kashima mōde* / *Kashima kikō* (鹿島詣, *A Visit to Kashima Shrine*). A short, prosimetric pilgrimage account incorporating poems by Bashō and his companions; its plural voices make it more than an individual travel diary. ([Smithsonian catalogue of Barnhill’s collected prose](https://www.si.edu/object/siris_sil_865593); [Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1687–1688; arranged posthumously:** *Oi no kobumi* (笈の小文, *The Knapsack Notebook*). An unfinished or unfinalized travel project combining aesthetic reflection, narrative fragments, and hokku; Bashō’s disciples imposed the surviving organization after his death, so it resists treatment as a completed authorial book. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/); [Yamanashi Prefectural University Bashō database](https://www2.yamanashi-ken.ac.jp/~itoyo/basho/relateddocuments.htm))

- **1688:** *Sarashina kikō* (更科紀行, *Sarashina Travelogue* or *Journey to Sarashina*). A compact haibun account of a journey to view the autumn moon at Mount Obasute, joining itinerary, literary memory, prose, and verse. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/); [Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/basho))

- **1689–1694; first printed 1702:** *Oku no hosomichi* (おくのほそ道, *The Narrow Road to the Deep North/Interior*). A deliberately reconstructed literary journey in prose and hokku, revised for roughly five years rather than a documentary transcript of the 1689 trip; its manuscript completion and posthumous first printing must therefore be dated separately. ([Digital Literary Map of Japan](https://literarymaps.nijl.ac.jp/en/resources/); [Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/); [Oku no Hosomichi overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oku_no_Hosomichi))

- **1691:** *Saga nikki* (嵯峨日記, *Saga Diary*). A dated retreat diary mixing daily record, quotations, hokku, and the presence of disciples; unlike the travel narratives, its diary structure preserves the contingency of successive days. ([Smithsonian catalogue of Barnhill’s collected prose](https://www.si.edu/object/siris_sil_865593); [Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

### Short haibun, inscriptions, encomia, and poetic documents

- **1680:** *Shiba no to* (柴の戸, *The Brushwood Gate*). A short haibun or dedicatory prose piece; it survives as an individually named text, not as an independent book. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1681:** *Tsukiwabisai* (月侘斎). A brief haikai-prose composition whose title names a poetic persona or dwelling; its small scale makes “document” or “haibun” more accurate than “book.” ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1681:** *Bōsha no kan* (茅舎の感, *Thoughts in a Thatched Hut*). A short reflective haibun associated with Bashō’s hut life. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1681:** *Kan’ya no ji* (寒夜の辞, *Words on a Cold Night*). A brief occasional prose-and-poetry text rather than a separately published work. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1683:** *Natsuno no gazan* (夏野の画讃, *Inscription on a Picture of a Summer Field*). A *gazan*—writing made for or upon an image—so the meaningful object is the conjunction of calligraphy, poem, and picture, not a detachable literary “book.” ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1685:** *Jitoku no shin* (自得の箴, *Admonition on Self-Attainment*). A compact didactic text: closer to a maxim or personal protocol than narrative prose. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1686:** *Kakiho no ume* (垣穂の梅, *Plum at the Hedge*). A short named haibun preserved in the prose corpus. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1686:** *Shizan no hisago* (四山の瓢, *Shizan’s Gourd*). An occasional prose composition centered on a material object; text and inscribed or presented thing form a small composite obra. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1686:** *Kasa no ki* (笠の記, *Record of a Traveling Hat*). A short object-centered record or encomium rather than a conventional essay. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1686:** *Yukimaruge* (雪丸げ, *Rolling a Snowball*). An occasional haibun whose transmitted identity includes its prose and associated verse. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1686:** *Fukagawa no yuki no yo* (深川の雪の夜, *A Snowy Night at Fukagawa*). A brief situational haibun, not a separately issued volume. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1688:** *Jūhachirō no ki* (十八楼の記, *Record of the Eighteen-Story Tower*). A place-writing or commemorative prose piece with poetic function. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1688:** *Ubune* (鵜舟, *Cormorant-Fishing Boat*). A short haibun arising from an observed performance and transmitted among Bashō’s occasional prose. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1688:** *Bashō-an jūsan’ya* (芭蕉庵十三夜, *The Thirteenth Night at Bashō-an*). An occasional moon-viewing text combining social event and literary manifestation. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1688:** *Fukagawa hachihin* (深川八貧, *Eight Poor Men of Fukagawa*). A communal haikai-prose performance whose identity depends upon Bashō’s circle as well as Bashō himself. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1689:** *Kamiko no ki* (紙衾の記, *Record of a Paper Quilt*). A short prose inscription or object-record, making the quilt and its text difficult to separate conceptually. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1689:** *Akechi ga tsuma no hanashi* (明智が妻の話, *The Story of Akechi’s Wife*). A short exemplary narrative included among Bashō’s haibun. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1690:** *Sharaku-dō no ki* (洒落堂の記, *Record of Sharaku Hall*). A commemorative account of a named dwelling, functioning partly as literary dedication. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1690:** *Genjūan no ki* (幻住庵の記, *Record of the Phantom Dwelling*). A sustained autobiographical and philosophical haibun about residence, travel, and poetic vocation; scholarship commonly treats it as an essay, although its prose-and-hokku construction resists that single-medium label. ([Reference Guide to World Literature](https://www.cur.ac.rw/mis/main/library/documents/book_file/digital-6476117c1434c2.34132592.pdf); [Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1690:** *Shijō no kawara suzumi* (四条の河原涼み, *Taking the Cool at the Shijō Riverbank*). A short occasional prose-poem tied to a public place and seasonal practice. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1690:** *Unchiku jigazō no san* (雲竹自画像の讃, *Inscription on Unchiku’s Self-Portrait*). A portrait inscription: a relational visual-verbal manifestation rather than a freestanding literary work. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1691:** *Katata izayoi no ben* (堅田十六夜の弁, *Account of the Sixteenth-Night Moon at Katata*). An occasional moon-viewing haibun. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1691:** *Shimada no shigure* (島田の時雨, *Winter Rain at Shimada*). A brief named poetic-prose composition. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1691:** *Yuki no kareobana* (雪の枯尾花, *Withered Miscanthus in Snow*). A short haibun or image-text preserved in the prose corpus, not to be confused with later memorial publications using related language. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1692:** *Seikyo no ben* (栖去の弁). A short occasional prose text; translations and romanization of its title vary, and it has no stable independent English title. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1692:** *Bashō o utsusu kotoba* (芭蕉を移す詞, *Words on Transplanting the Banana Tree*). A commemorative text made around a physical act and place; the event, tree, dwelling, and writing jointly constitute its obra. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1692:** *Tsukue no mei* (机の銘, *Inscription for a Desk*). A *mei*, or inscribed motto: language intended to inhabit an object rather than describe one from a distance. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1693:** *Kyoroku ribetsu no kotoba* / *Saimon no ji* (許六離別の詞／柴門の辞, *Words on Parting from Kyoroku*). A farewell address with variant traditional titles; it is both personal communication and a statement of poetic transmission. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1693:** *Heikan no setsu* (閉関の説, *Account of Closing the Gate*). A withdrawal text announcing Bashō’s temporary seclusion; because the declaration performs the withdrawal it describes, it approaches a manifesto or speech act. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1694:** *Gaikotsu no ezan* (骸骨の絵讃, *Inscription on a Picture of a Skeleton*). A visual-verbal inscription whose full object includes image, calligraphy, and poetic response. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1694:** *Aki no asane* (秋の朝寝, *Sleeping Late on an Autumn Morning*). A late short haibun or occasional prose-poem. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

### Collaborative linked verse and Bashō-school anthologies

- **1665–1694:** *Extant haikai linked-verse sequences*. This is a collaborative corpus—not a body of poems Bashō can own alone—comprising hundred-verse sequences, fifty-link works, thirty-six-link *kasen*, and shorter forms in which he supplied only some links while also directing the session. The standard critical edition requires three volumes for the linked verse, and individual sequences are usually identified by their opening words rather than authorial titles. ([Kobe University bibliographic listing of Kōhon Bashō Zenshū](https://www.office.kobe-u.ac.jp/ksui-yamaguchiseishi/seishi%27s%20collection.pdf); [Kōhon Bashō Zenshū catalogue](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BN02920424))

- **1684:** *Fuyu no hi* (冬の日, *Winter Days*). Five collaborative *kasen* by the Owari poets with Bashō participating and guiding the new style; Yamamoto Kakei compiled the book, so it is a Bashō-school Gesamtkunstwerk of session, sequence, selection, and print rather than Bashō’s sole-authored collection. ([Eisei Bunko Museum](https://www.eiseibunko.com/english/pdf/2021Autumn_list_English.pdf); [Babel Press description of the Seven Anthologies](https://www.babel.co.jp/babelpress/product/book227/))

- **1686:** *Haru no hi* (春の日, *Spring Days*). A Bashō-school collection compiled by Kakei, containing collaborative linked verse and hokku; Bashō’s presence is authorial and supervisory, not exclusive. ([Yamanashi Prefectural University Bashō database](https://www2.yamanashi-ken.ac.jp/~itoyo/basho/relateddocuments.htm); [Babel Press](https://www.babel.co.jp/babelpress/product/book227/))

- **1689:** *Arano* / *Kōya* (曠野／阿羅野, *The Wasteland*). A school anthology compiled by Kakei with Bashō’s contributions and authority behind it; both Japanese title forms occur in catalogues. ([Yamanashi Prefectural University Bashō database](https://www2.yamanashi-ken.ac.jp/~itoyo/basho/relateddocuments.htm); [Babel Press](https://www.babel.co.jp/babelpress/product/book227/))

- **1690:** *Hisago* (ひさご, *The Gourd*). A collaborative Bashō-school anthology containing hokku and linked verse; its collective production resists attribution to Bashō as a single “author.” ([Yamanashi Prefectural University Bashō database](https://www2.yamanashi-ken.ac.jp/~itoyo/basho/relateddocuments.htm); [Babel Press](https://www.babel.co.jp/babelpress/product/book227/))

- **1691:** *Sarumino* (猿蓑, *The Monkey’s Raincoat*). An anthology compiled by Mukai Kyorai and Nozawa Bonchō under Bashō’s supervision, containing roughly four hundred hokku and four *kasen*; it is a deliberately ordered manifestation of the school’s mature practice, not simply “a book by Bashō.” ([Sarumino bibliographic account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarumino); [Babel Press](https://www.babel.co.jp/babelpress/product/book227/))

- **1694:** *Sumidawara* (炭俵, *The Charcoal Sack*). A collective anthology of Bashō-school verse associated with the late “lightness” style; Bashō contributed and exerted poetic direction, but the anthology’s many voices prevent sole authorship. ([Yamanashi Prefectural University Bashō database](https://www2.yamanashi-ken.ac.jp/~itoyo/basho/relateddocuments.htm); [Babel Press](https://www.babel.co.jp/babelpress/product/book227/))

- **1698, posthumous:** *Zoku Sarumino* (続猿蓑, *The Continuation of The Monkey’s Raincoat*). A posthumously issued Bashō-school anthology containing work by Bashō but assembled after his death; it is a manifestation of his circle and legacy, not a finished book made by him, despite its canonical place among the “Seven Anthologies.” ([Yamanashi Prefectural University Bashō database](https://www2.yamanashi-ken.ac.jp/~itoyo/basho/relateddocuments.htm); [Babel Press](https://www.babel.co.jp/babelpress/product/book227/))

### Letters, poetic instruction, and final dispositions

- **1681–1694:** *Extant correspondence*. The critical collected edition prints 171 autograph letters and separately marks thirty supplementary or reference letters; these are made texts—often carrying poems, criticism, travel news, and practical instructions—but not an author-designed book, and doubtful or non-autograph items must not be silently merged with the secure corpus. ([Kadokawa, Kōhon Bashō Zenshū, vol. 8](https://store.kadokawa.co.jp/shop/g/g321302000227/); [Bashō letters index](https://www2.yamanashi-ken.ac.jp/~itoyo/basho/letter/index.htm))

- **c. 1682–1694:** *Poetic teachings preserved in letters and disciples’ records*. These remarks form a transmitted body of critical practice rather than a stable authorial treatise: some are Bashō’s written instructions, while others are pupils’ recollections, and the latter resist classification as texts made directly by him. The critical edition accordingly separates the *hairon* material from verse, prose, and letters. ([Osaka University of Education bibliography](https://www.lib.osaka-kyoiku.ac.jp/?action=common_download_main&upload_id=2098); [Kōhon Bashō Zenshū catalogue](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BN02920424))

- **1694:** *Letter of testament to Matsuo Hanzaemon*. A written final disposition included in the collected prose tradition; it is a practical legal-personal document rather than imaginative literature, but it remains something Bashō made. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

- **1694:** *Three dictated testaments recorded by Shikō*. These final instructions survive through another person’s hand, making them collaborative documentary manifestations: Bashō supplied the spoken intention, while Kagami Shikō supplied the writing and transmission. ([Shinchōsha collected prose contents](https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/620872/))

### Calligraphy, poem sheets, and haiga

- **c. 1670s–1694:** *Extant autograph poem sheets, inscriptions, and calligraphic manuscripts*. These are physical works as well as textual witnesses: a hokku written on a *tanzaku*, scroll, letter, or presentation sheet is not merely an edition of an abstract poem but a singular act of brushwork. Survival and authentication are object-by-object matters, so this corpus has no secure single total. ([Eisei Bunko Museum exhibition list](https://www.eiseibunko.com/english/pdf/2021Autumn_list_English.pdf); [Kadokawa autograph-letter census](https://store.kadokawa.co.jp/shop/g/g321302000227/))

- **c. 1680s–1694:** *Haiga and pictorial inscriptions attributed to Bashō*. In these works sketch, calligraphy, and hokku refuse separation into image and text; at least some surviving examples have been identified as both drawn and written by Bashō, while other attributions require authentication and should not be treated as an uncontested catalogue. ([Oral Tradition, vol. 20.2](https://journal.oraltradition.org/wp-content/uploads/files/articles/20ii/20_2_complete.pdf); [Eisei Bunko Museum](https://www.eiseibunko.com/english/pdf/2021Autumn_list_English.pdf))
