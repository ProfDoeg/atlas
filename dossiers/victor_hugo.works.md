## Works by Victor-Marie Hugo

### Juvenilia and early projects

- **1816–1817:** *Irtamène*. An unfinished verse tragedy from Hugo’s adolescence; the surviving draft is a project rather than a completed drama. [Massin]
- **1817:** *Athélie, ou les Scandinaves*. An early tragedy or dramatic sketch, preserved among the juvenile works; its unfinished state resists treatment as a performed play. [Massin]
- **1818–1819:** *Inez de Castro*. A youthful melodrama, substantially completed but not staged or published in Hugo’s lifetime. [Massin]
- **1819–1821:** *A.Q.C.H.E.B.* An enigmatic juvenile textual project whose abbreviated title has no securely established expansion; it survives as manuscript material rather than a finished publication. [Massin]
- **1820:** *Bug-Jargal*. A novella about the Haitian Revolution, later radically expanded into the 1826 novel; the two states are related but distinct textual objects. [Massin]
- **1821:** *Le Télégraphe*. A short-lived literary periodical made with Hugo’s brothers; Hugo’s individual contributions cannot always be cleanly separated from the collective enterprise. [Groupe Hugo chronology]

### Novels and fictional narratives

- **1823:** *Han d’Islande*. A Gothic historical novel, revised in later editions. [Massin]
- **1826:** *Bug-Jargal*. A novel-length reconstruction of the 1820 novella, not merely its reprinting. [Massin]
- **1829:** *Le Dernier Jour d’un condamné*. A fictional first-person account that also acts as an abolitionist intervention against capital punishment; it lies between novel, argument and manifesto. [Massin]
- **1831:** *Notre-Dame de Paris. 1482*. A historical novel; the expanded 1832 state restored chapters absent from the first edition, so “the work” has more than one authorial form. [Massin]
- **1834:** *Claude Gueux*. A narrative founded on a criminal case and made to operate as an argument against social injustice and the death penalty. [Massin]
- **1845–1862:** *Les Misérables*. A vast social novel begun as *Les Misères*, interrupted, and transformed during exile; the long project history is part of the obra. [Massin]
- **1866:** *Les Travailleurs de la mer*. A novel of labour, machinery, the sea and exile, conceived together with Hugo’s Channel Islands writing and drawings. [Massin]
- **1869:** *L’Homme qui rit*. A historical and philosophical novel whose grotesque protagonist also generated related drawings. [Massin]
- **1874:** *Quatrevingt-treize*. Hugo’s final completed novel, treating the Revolution and the Vendée war. [Massin]

### Poetry collections and poetic cycles

- **1822:** *Odes et poésies diverses*. Hugo’s first book-length poetry collection; later collections absorbed and rearranged much of it. [BnF poetry]
- **1824:** *Nouvelles Odes*. A second collection subsequently reworked into *Odes et Ballades*. [Massin]
- **1826–1828:** *Odes et Ballades*. An evolving collection rather than a single immutable text; its 1828 form is the fullest authorial arrangement. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1829:** *Les Orientales*. A poetry collection responding to Greece, the eastern Mediterranean and contemporary Orientalism. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1831:** *Les Feuilles d’automne*. A lyric collection organized around domestic life, memory and political reflection. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1835:** *Les Chants du crépuscule*. A lyric and political collection shaped by the unsettled aftermath of the July Revolution. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1837:** *Les Voix intérieures*. A collection joining intimate elegy, public meditation and poetic self-examination. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1840:** *Les Rayons et les Ombres*. A poetry collection that also formulates Hugo’s idea of the poet’s public function. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1853:** *Les Châtiments*. A satirical and prophetic poetic attack on Napoleon III; as an exile publication it functions partly as a political manifesto. [BnF poetry]
- **1856:** *Les Contemplations*. A two-part lyric architecture—“formerly” and “today”—constructed retrospectively around bereavement, memory and exile; some printed poem dates are authorial staging rather than reliable composition dates. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1854–1884; unfinished:** *Dieu*. An unfinished metaphysical epic project, posthumously arranged and published in 1891; editorial ordering cannot be equated without qualification with a final authorial text. [BnF poetry]
- **1854–1860; unfinished:** *La Fin de Satan*. An unfinished epic and theological project, posthumously published in 1886; it was conceived with *Dieu* and *La Légende des siècles* as part of a larger poetic architecture. [BnF poetry]
- **1855–1856; unfinished:** *Solitudines coeli*. A manuscript poetic sequence associated with the evolving *Dieu* project; it is a working constellation, not an independently finalized book. [Massin]
- **1857–1858; unfinished:** *L’Esprit humain*. A projected component or alternative organization of the metaphysical epic material later associated with *Dieu*. [Massin]
- **1859, 1877 and 1883:** *La Légende des siècles*. An epic cycle issued in a first series, a second series and a complementary series; it is one expanding obra manifested in three authorial publications. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1865:** *Les Chansons des rues et des bois*. A collection of playful, erotic, pastoral and philosophical verse. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1872:** *L’Année terrible*. A poetic chronicle of the Franco-Prussian War, the siege, the Commune and Hugo’s personal bereavements. [BnF poetry]
- **1877:** *L’Art d’être grand-père*. A poetry collection focused on childhood, family and political futurity. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1878:** *Le Pape*. A book-length philosophical poem attacking clerical authority while imagining a transformed Christianity. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1879:** *La Pitié suprême*. A philosophical poem making mercy the basis of ethical and political judgment. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1880:** *Religions et religion*. A philosophical poem distinguishing institutional religions from Hugo’s conception of universal religion. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1880:** *L’Âne*. A satirical philosophical poem in which animal speech exposes institutional and intellectual pretension. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1881:** *Les Quatre Vents de l’esprit*. A four-part poetic summa—satirical, dramatic, lyric and epic—and Hugo’s last major poetry collection published during his lifetime. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **Various dates; collected 1888 and 1893:** *Toute la lyre*. A posthumously constructed collection of dispersed and unpublished poems; because executors selected and organized it, the collection is not wholly an authorially completed obra. [BnF poetry]
- **1870–1871; collected 1898:** *Les Années funestes*. Poems concerning war and political catastrophe, assembled posthumously from manuscripts. [BnF poetry]
- **Various dates; collected 1902:** *Dernière Gerbe*. A posthumous editorial gathering of poems rather than a collection Hugo finalized under that title. [BnF poetry]
- **Various dates; collected posthumously:** *Océan, poésie*. A modern editorial designation for uncollected manuscript verse; it is an archive-like body of work, not a book Hugo named and completed. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]

### Drama, opera and theatre projects

- **1827:** *Cromwell*. A vast historical verse drama, effectively unstageable in its original scale; its preface became a manifesto of Romantic drama. [Massin]
- **1828:** *Amy Robsart*. A prose drama adapted indirectly from Walter Scott and initially staged under another name; Hugo’s authorship was concealed before later acknowledgment. [Massin]
- **1829:** *Marion de Lorme*. A verse drama prohibited before its eventual 1831 premiere. [Massin]
- **1830:** *Hernani*. A verse drama whose premiere became a public manifestation of French theatrical Romanticism. [Massin]
- **1832:** *Le Roi s’amuse*. A verse drama suppressed after its first performance; the play and the censorship struggle became inseparable political acts. [Massin]
- **1833:** *Lucrèce Borgia*. A prose drama written for the popular theatre. [Massin]
- **1833:** *Marie Tudor*. A prose historical drama. [Massin]
- **1835:** *Angelo, tyran de Padoue*. A prose drama of political and sexual power. [Massin]
- **1836:** *La Esmeralda*. An opera libretto adapted by Hugo from *Notre-Dame de Paris*, with music by Louise Bertin; the libretto is Hugo’s text, while the opera as performed is a collaboration. [Massin]
- **1838:** *Ruy Blas*. A verse drama written for the Théâtre de la Renaissance. [Massin]
- **1839; unfinished:** *Les Jumeaux*. An abandoned verse drama about the Man in the Iron Mask, first published posthumously; incompletion is intrinsic to its status. [Massin]
- **1843:** *Les Burgraves*. A verse drama whose hostile reception helped end Hugo’s sustained engagement with public theatre. [Massin]
- **1854:** *La Forêt mouillée*. A short verse comedy later placed among the posthumously published plays called *Théâtre en liberté*. [Massin]
- **1865:** *La Grand’Mère*. A verse comedy-drama written in exile and not staged in Hugo’s lifetime. [Massin]
- **1866:** *Mille francs de récompense*. A prose social comedy-drama whose unpublished lifetime status allowed Hugo unusual theatrical freedom. [Massin]
- **1866:** *L’Intervention*. A short social comedy in prose. [Massin]
- **1867:** *Mangeront-ils ?*. A verse comedy combining fairy tale, satire and political allegory. [Massin]
- **1867–1868:** *Sur la lisière d’un bois*. A short dramatic fantasy, preserved among the exile theatre. [Comédie-Française]
- **1869:** *L’Épée*. A verse drama or dramatic parable about power and legitimacy. [Massin]
- **1869:** *Les Deux Trouvailles de Gallus*. A two-part verse drama comprising *Margarita* and *Esca*. [Massin]
- **1869; unfinished:** *Welf, castellan d’Osbor*. An incomplete dramatic project preserved among Hugo’s later theatre manuscripts. [Massin]
- **1869; unfinished:** *Le Château du diable*. A fragmentary dramatic project; it resists classification as a completed play. [Massin]
- **1869; unfinished:** *Les Gueux*. An unfinished dramatic project surviving in Hugo’s theatrical papers. [Massin]
- **1869; unfinished:** *La Guerre aux démolisseurs* or associated dramatic scenario. The title and boundaries vary in editorial treatments, so it is safer understood as manuscript theatre material than as a finalized play. [Massin]
- **1869; published 1882:** *Torquemada*. A large verse drama on religious fanaticism, completed in exile but published only near the end of Hugo’s life. [Massin]
- **1854–1869; collected 1886:** *Théâtre en liberté*. A posthumous editorial umbrella for the exile plays, not a single play or a collection demonstrably finalized by Hugo under that form. [Massin]

### Criticism, philosophy, travel and historical prose

- **1820s–1830s:** *Articles divers*. Reviews, literary criticism and polemical journalism later gathered in collected editions; this is a dispersed body of textual acts rather than one titled work. [Massin]
- **1834:** *Littérature et philosophie mêlées*. A deliberately mixed collection of criticism, essays, earlier articles and reflective prose. [Massin]
- **1834:** *Étude sur Mirabeau*. A historical-political essay incorporated into *Littérature et philosophie mêlées*. [Massin]
- **1834:** *Journal des idées, des opinions et des lectures d’un jeune Jacobite de 1819*. An intellectual notebook retrospectively published within *Littérature et philosophie mêlées*; it occupies the border between diary, archive and composed essay. [Massin]
- **1834:** *Sur Walter Scott*. Literary criticism included in *Littérature et philosophie mêlées*. [Massin]
- **1834:** *Guerre aux démolisseurs*. A manifesto-like essay against the destruction of historic architecture, developed from an earlier 1825 article. [Massin]
- **1839–1840:** *Voyages de 1839 et 1840*. Travel notebooks combining observation, letters, sketches and preparatory matter for *Le Rhin*; they are working documents as well as travel writing. [Massin]
- **1842:** *Le Rhin: lettres à un ami*. A composite of travel letters, history, architectural observation and political argument; its concluding section exceeds the category of travel book. [Massin]
- **1843:** *Voyage aux Pyrénées*. A travel manuscript published only posthumously and later editorially grouped with other journeys. [Massin]
- **1846:** *Le Retour de l’Empereur*. A prose account or meditation on the return of Napoleon’s remains. [Massin]
- **1840s:** *De ce que j’apprends chaque jour*. A notebook-like sequence of observations and reflections; its openness and private manuscript state resist calling it a finished work. [Massin]
- **1840s:** *Choses de la Bible*. Notes and prose meditations on scripture, surviving as an unfinished dossier rather than an authorially issued book. [Massin]
- **1852:** *Napoléon le Petit*. A political pamphlet whose denunciation of Louis-Napoleon is an act of opposition, not merely a description. [Massin]
- **1852; published 1877–1878:** *Histoire d’un crime*. Hugo’s eyewitness history of the 1851 coup, written as political testimony and published in two volumes decades later. [Massin]
- **1850s:** *La Vision de Dante*. A prose or mixed-form visionary fragment preserved in the exile papers. [Massin]
- **1850s–1860s:** *La Révolution, le verso de la page*. An unfinished philosophical-historical project whose surviving pieces were organized by later editors. [Massin]
- **1860:** *William Shakespeare*—initial project. Begun as a preface to François-Victor Hugo’s Shakespeare translation, it expanded into an independent book on genius, art and history. [Massin]
- **1864:** *William Shakespeare*. A critical-philosophical book that operates as Hugo’s manifesto for art, literary history and democratic genius. [Massin]
- **1860s:** *Utilité du beau*. An unfinished aesthetic essay or project, posthumously recovered from manuscript. [Massin]
- **1860s:** *Promontorium somnii*. A meditation on imagination, dream and artistic creation, left unpublished and not fully finalized by Hugo. [Massin]
- **1860s:** *Philosophie: commencement d’un livre*. The beginning of an unrealized philosophical book; the title itself records project status rather than completion. [Massin]
- **1860s:** *Les Choses de l’infini*. A cluster of philosophical prose manuscripts, not a securely finished book. [Massin]
- **1860s:** *Contemplation suprême*. A philosophical text or project preserved in manuscript and published through editorial reconstruction. [Massin]
- **1860s:** *La Mer et le Vent*. A prose meditation associated with the Channel Islands and *Les Travailleurs de la mer*. [Massin]
- **1866; later revised:** *L’Archipel de la Manche*. A geographical, historical and autobiographical essay first connected with *Les Travailleurs de la mer* and later published separately. [Massin]
- **1867:** *Paris*. An extended introduction to the Paris guide issued for the Exposition universelle; it is an independent civic and historical essay within a collective publication. [Massin]
- **1874:** *Mes fils*. A memorial and political family text devoted to Charles and François-Victor Hugo. [Massin]
- **1875:** *Le Droit et la Loi*. A political-philosophical essay distinguishing ideal right from enacted law. [Massin]
- **1875:** *Ce que c’est que l’exil*. An autobiographical and political account that turns exile into a public ethical position. [Massin]
- **1875–1876:** *Actes et paroles: Avant l’exil; Pendant l’exil; Depuis l’exil*. An authorially organized archive of speeches, letters, declarations and interventions; it is simultaneously a collection and a record of political acts. [Massin]
- **1878:** *Paris et Rome*. An anticlerical political essay or manifesto defending secular republican authority. [Massin]
- **1887–1900:** *Choses vues*. Posthumously arranged notebooks of witnessed events, portraits and recollections; the familiar title denotes an editorial construction from manuscripts rather than a book completed by Hugo. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1890:** *Alpes et Pyrénées*. A posthumous editorial travel collection assembled from earlier journeys. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1892:** *France et Belgique*. A posthumous travel collection assembled from notebooks and journey texts. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1892:** *Voyages et excursions*. A posthumously constituted group of travel writings; its boundaries depend on the edition. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **1901:** *Post-Scriptum de ma vie*. A posthumous assemblage of philosophical prose; the title and organization belong substantially to Hugo’s literary executors. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **Various dates; collected posthumously:** *Océan, prose*. A modern editorial body of fragments, notes and uncollected prose rather than a bounded work Hugo completed under that title. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **Various dates; collected posthumously:** *Feuilles paginées*. Manuscript leaves whose pagination suggests provisional sequences without establishing a final book; they resist being treated as one completed work. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]
- **Various dates:** *Portefeuilles* and *Dossiers*. Notes, fragments, plans and documentary deposits printed throughout the Massin edition; these are evidence of making and abandoned projects, not automatically autonomous works. [Massin]

### Manifestos, speeches, declarations and public textual acts

- **1827:** *Préface de Cromwell*. A theoretical preface that acted as a manifesto for Romantic drama and the conjunction of sublime and grotesque. [Massin]
- **1832:** *Préface du Dernier Jour d’un condamné*. An expanded abolitionist argument attached to the novel and intended to intervene directly in the death-penalty debate. [Massin]
- **1841:** *Discours de réception à l’Académie française*. A ceremonial speech that also states a literary and political genealogy. [Massin]
- **1848:** *Discours sur les ateliers nationaux*. A parliamentary speech and political act concerning work, poverty and the revolutionary crisis. [Maisons Victor Hugo]
- **1848:** *Discours contre la peine de mort*. A parliamentary intervention intended to change law and public conduct, properly a manifesto-like act as well as a speech. [Maisons Victor Hugo]
- **1848:** *Discours sur la liberté de la presse*. A parliamentary defense of press freedom. [Maisons Victor Hugo]
- **1849:** *Discours sur la misère*. A parliamentary intervention asserting that extreme poverty could and should be abolished. [Massin]
- **1849:** *Discours au Congrès de la paix*. An internationalist political address envisioning a federated Europe. [Massin]
- **1850:** *Discours sur la liberté de l’enseignement*. A political speech opposing clerical domination of education. [Massin]
- **1851:** *Appels and proclamations against the coup d’État*. Emergency public texts intended to organize resistance; their status is closer to performative political acts than to literary works. [Massin]
- **1859:** *Appel aux États-Unis d’Amérique*. A public antislavery intervention associated with the John Brown case. [Massin]
- **1860s–1880s:** *Appeals against executions and for amnesty*. A recurring body of open letters, petitions and declarations concerning capital punishment, political prisoners and the Communards; it has no single fixed title or final boundary. [Massin]
- **1876:** *Pour la Serbie*. A public appeal responding to atrocities in the Balkans and calling for European action. [Massin]
- **1878:** *Discours d’ouverture du Congrès littéraire international*. A programmatic speech on authorship and literary property. [Massin]
- **1879:** *Discours sur l’Afrique*. A banquet speech advocating European colonization in terms now inseparable from nineteenth-century imperial ideology; its historical importance does not remove that resistance to celebratory classification. [Massin]
- **1881:** *Discours pour le quatre-vingtième anniversaire*. A public self-summation joining literary, republican and humanitarian claims. [Massin]

### Diaries, correspondence and collaborative manifestations

- **1810s–1885:** *Correspondance*. Thousands of letters constitute a made textual body but not one authorially bounded work; published selections and editions organize it differently. [Massin]
- **1820s–1885:** *Carnets, agendas and notebooks*. Working records containing accounts, appointments, drafts, names, drawings and observations; they resist separation into “work” and documentary residue. [Massin]
- **1833–1883:** *Letters to Juliette Drouet*. One side of an enormous reciprocal correspondence; each letter is a textual act, while the familiar corpus is an archival and editorial construction. [Groupe Hugo chronology]
- **1853–1855:** *Procès-verbaux des tables parlantes*. Records of the Jersey séances made by several participants. Hugo took part and influenced the proceedings, but the supposed voices, scribes and collective setting make sole authorship untenable. [Massin]
- **1853–1855; published posthumously:** *Le Livre des Tables*. An editorially constituted selection from séance records; it is better classified as a collective manifestation than as an ordinary book authored by Hugo. [Groupe Hugo bibliography]

### Graphic obra: drawings, ink works, prints and hybrid pages

- **c. 1816–1885:** *Œuvre graphique*. A corpus now known to exceed 3,500 drawings and related works, while the complete catalogue raisonné remains unfinished; consequently no honest closed list of every sheet yet exists. Massin’s collected edition organizes it as people and animals, monsters and spirits, architecture, landscape, marine subjects, décor, stencils, folds and inkblots. [Massin; Graphic-art catalogue study]
- **1830s:** *Caricatures of family, friends and contemporaries*. Pen drawings made largely for private circulation rather than exhibition; they form a practice more than a titled series. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1834–1843:** *Travel-journal drawings*. Pencil records of buildings, landscapes and architectural details, often recopied in ink; they are simultaneously documents, gifts and autonomous images. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **c. 1847:** *Le Burg à la croix*. A large imaginary-architecture drawing produced as Hugo’s graphic practice reached maturity. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **c. 1847–1850:** *Le Champignon*. A visionary drawing of architecture or landscape whose unstable scale resists literal description. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **c. 1847–1850:** *Gallia*. A large allegorical graphic work. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **c. 1847–1850:** *La Ville morte*. An imaginary cityscape in mixed graphic media. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **c. 1847–1850:** *Vue de Paris*. A large urban vision; the title does not make it straightforward topographical documentation. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **c. 1847–1850:** *Paysage aux trois arbres*. A landscape drawing belonging to Hugo’s mature ink-and-wash practice. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1850:** *Le Burg à l’ange*. An imaginary architectural landscape made with the mixed and transfer-like procedures characteristic of Hugo’s mature work. [Maisons Victor Hugo collections]
- **1850:** *Les Deux Châteaux*. An architectural fantasy incorporating scraping and other experimental handling. [Maisons Victor Hugo collections]
- **1850s:** *Le Phare*. A lighthouse image or group of related images in which architecture becomes visionary emblem rather than straightforward illustration. [Massin]
- **1854:** *Ecce*. A death-penalty image from the exile period; it acts visually as abolitionist testimony. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1854:** *Ecce Lex*. A drawing of an executed body whose Latin title turns the image into an accusation against the law. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1850s:** *Les Pendus*. A group of drawings of hanged figures rather than one securely delimited composition; titles and dates vary among sheets and catalogues. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1856:** *Burg*. An ink work on a wooden support; its material and procedure place it between drawing, painting and decorative object. [Maisons Victor Hugo collections]
- **1860s:** *Eddystone Lighthouse*. A drawing associated imaginatively with *Les Travailleurs de la mer*, but not merely an illustration subordinate to the novel. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1860s:** *Casquets Lighthouse*. A marine architectural drawing associated with *L’Homme qui rit*. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1866:** *La Durande*. A drawing connected with the ship in *Les Travailleurs de la mer* and part of Hugo’s movement between verbal and graphic invention. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1870s:** *Le Poème de la sorcière*. A sequence of grotesque faces forming a visual narrative against cruel and unreasoning justice; it is a series rather than one sheet. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1850s–1870s:** *Souvenirs*. Decorated and sometimes painted frames made as gifts and for the Billiard Room at Hauteville House; frame, inscription and image form hybrid objects. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1850s–1870s:** *Cartes de visite and calligraphic greeting drawings*. Small works built around Hugo’s name, initials and address; they combine drawing, writing, gift and social act. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1850s–1870s:** *Taches*. Inkblots and transferred stains, some developed into images and some left unresolved. Whether every surviving blot is a work, a test or the beginning of a work is genuinely uncertain. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1850s–1870s:** *Pochoirs*. Stencilled works made with cut paper, lace, leaves and other matrices; their method crosses drawing, print and decorative design. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1850s–1870s:** *Pliages*. Folded-paper and symmetrical ink experiments; they are graphic procedures and manifestations as much as conventionally titled works. [Massin]
- **1850s–1870s:** *Painted frames*. Frames designed or painted by Hugo, often for other images; calling them merely accessories misses their status as made visual objects. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **Undated:** *Untitled ink tests and working marks*. The surviving corpus includes sheets that may be technical trials, prompts for future images or completed abstractions; their resistance to being called works is part of the catalogue problem. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]

### Architecture, interiors, furniture and total environments

- **1832–1833:** *Playing-card house for the Hugo children*. A miniature house with kitchen, library and billiard room, made from folded and cut playing cards; it is a domestic object and architectural model rather than a text. [Le Monde, “Victor Hugo’s interior architecture”]
- **1832–1848:** *Interior arrangements at 6 place des Vosges*. A changing domestic display of medievalizing furniture, armour, textiles and works by artist friends; because it was dispersed, it survives as a partially documented decorative project rather than an intact work. [Maisons Victor Hugo; Le Monde]
- **1856–1870:** *Hauteville House*. Hugo directed the architecture, interiors, furniture, inscriptions, lighting and symbolic program of his Guernsey home, working with craftsmen and family members. It is best understood as a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk, not solely as a building “by” Hugo. [Maisons Victor Hugo; Le Monde]
- **1856–1859:** *Red Room, Hauteville House*. A room-scale composition of colour, furniture, tapestry, ceramics, carving and reused objects. [Maisons Victor Hugo]
- **1856–1859:** *Blue Room, Hauteville House*. A coordinated interior whose fabric, furniture and colour make the room itself the obra. [Maisons Victor Hugo]
- **1856–1859:** *Oak Gallery, Hauteville House*. An architectural-interior assemblage using carved wood and repurposed fittings, including material from carriage trunks. [Le Monde]
- **1856–1859:** *Tapestry Room, Hauteville House*. A total interior joining textiles, furniture and symbolic display. [Maisons Victor Hugo]
- **1856–1859:** *Billiard Room, Hauteville House*. A display environment incorporating Hugo’s “Souvenirs” and painted frames. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1856–1862:** *Look-out, Hauteville House*. A glazed rooftop writing room designed as both belvedere and workplace; architecture, furniture, seascape and writing practice form one environment. [Maisons Victor Hugo; Le Monde]
- **1856–1870:** *Furniture and decorative objects for Hauteville House*. Cabinets, benches, tables, screens, frames and composite pieces were designed through sketches and assembled by craftsmen; attribution therefore ranges from Hugo’s hand to Hugo’s design and direction. [Maisons Victor Hugo drawings]
- **1856–1870:** *Vive Ama bench*. A monumental carved bench assembled within Hugo’s Guernsey decorative vocabulary; its execution was collaborative even where the design is attributed to Hugo. [Le Monde]
- **1856–1870:** *Standing writing desk*. A secretary adapted into a standing workstation in the Look-out; it is improvised furniture and part of the larger house-work. [Le Monde]
- **1856–1870:** *Painted Chinese-screen assemblages*. Imported painted blinds were reframed and extended with Hugo’s own painted additions, making hybrid works whose makers include anonymous Chinese artisans, Hugo and local craftsmen. [Le Monde]
- **1856–1870:** *Decorative inscriptions and monograms at Hauteville House*. Carved, painted and assembled words, initials and mottos act simultaneously as text, ornament and architectural program. [Le Monde]
- **1859–1864:** *La Fallue interiors*. Rooms and furniture devised for Juliette Drouet’s Guernsey residence; the ensemble has been altered and dispersed, so its status is reconstructive rather than intact. [Maisons Victor Hugo]
- **1864–1870:** *Hauteville Fairy/Hauteville II interiors*. Hugo designed or directed interiors and furniture for Juliette Drouet’s later residence. The surviving objects are collaborative decorative works, while the lost ensemble was the larger obra. [Maisons Victor Hugo]
- **1860s:** *Fold-away table for Juliette Drouet*. A wall-integrated drop-leaf table designed for Drouet’s house; craftsmen executed Hugo’s conception. [Le Monde]
- **1870s–1880s:** *Later Paris interiors*. Hugo resumed decorative arrangement after exile, but these changing domestic ensembles are less completely preserved than Hauteville House and resist delimitation as single finished works. [Le Monde]
