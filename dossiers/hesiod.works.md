## Works by Hesiod of Ascra

### Substantially surviving poems generally accepted as Hesiod’s

- **Early seventh century BC, approximately:** *Theogony*. An archaic Greek hexameter poem that orders the origins and genealogies of the gods and the establishment of Zeus’s rule. Modern scholarship generally accepts it as Hesiodic, although some ancient critics rejected it and the transmitted poem may contain later additions; its first-person “Hesiod” may be either a historical poet or a traditional poetic persona ([Clay and Vergados](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811074/83484/excerpt/9781107483484_excerpt.pdf); [Koning](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780190209049_A35486247/preview-9780190209049_A35486247.pdf)).
- **Early seventh century BC, approximately:** *Works and Days*. A didactic hexameter poem addressed to Perses, combining instruction about justice, labor, farming, seafaring, seasonal signs, and auspicious days. It is generally accepted as Hesiodic, but its apparent autobiography cannot safely be treated as unmediated historical testimony, and individual passages have been suspected of interpolation ([Clay and Vergados](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811074/83484/excerpt/9781107483484_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. I](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674996229)).

### Substantially surviving poem of the Hesiodic corpus, but not by Hesiod

- **About 575 BC:** *Shield of Heracles*. A hexameter heroic narrative about Heracles’ combat with Cycnus, dominated by an elaborate description of Heracles’ shield; its opening reuses material belonging to the *Catalogue of Women*. Although transmitted under Hesiod’s name and included in the standard corpus, linguistic, stylistic, and historical evidence makes Hesiodic authorship highly improbable ([Koning](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780190209049_A35486247/preview-9780190209049_A35486247.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).

### Fragmentary genealogical and heroic poems attributed to Hesiod

- **Date uncertain, probably archaic:** *Catalogue of Women* (also *Ehoiai*). A five-book genealogical hexameter poem connecting heroic families through mortal women who bore children to gods, apparently extending the world of the *Theogony* down to the generation of Helen’s suitors. It survives only in papyri, quotations, and testimonia; whether Hesiod composed it remains disputed, and even its relationship to the ending of the *Theogony* is uncertain ([Clay and Vergados](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811074/83484/excerpt/9781107483484_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Great Ehoiai*. A fragmentary genealogical epic, perhaps an enlarged form of the *Catalogue of Women*, although the evidence does not establish whether it was an independent poem, an alternative recension, or merely a bibliographical title. Attribution to Hesiod is traditional rather than secure ([Koning](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780190209049_A35486247/preview-9780190209049_A35486247.pdf); [Merkelbach and West](https://archive.org/details/fragmentahesiode0000hesi)).
- **Date unknown:** *Wedding of Ceyx*. A lost or fragmentarily attested hexameter narrative concerning the wedding of Ceyx; ancient testimony placed it among Hesiodic poems, but its authorship and original extent cannot be recovered ([Clay and Vergados](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811074/83484/excerpt/9781107483484_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Melampodia*. A fragmentary genealogical and heroic poem centered on Melampus and other legendary seers. It belongs to the ancient Hesiodic corpus, but neither its authorship nor its complete narrative design is known ([Clay and Vergados](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811074/83484/excerpt/9781107483484_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Aegimius*. A fragmentary heroic epic, apparently involving Aegimius, the Dorians, Heracles, and conflict with the Lapiths. Ancient sources variously associated the poem with Hesiod and with Cercops, so it resists attribution to a single maker ([Clay and Vergados](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811074/83484/excerpt/9781107483484_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Descent of Theseus and Peirithous into Hades*. A scarcely attested katabasis narrative reported by Pausanias among material associated with Hesiod. It may not have been an independent Hesiodic poem at all: the surviving evidence overlaps with traditions assigned to the lost epic *Minyas* ([Pausanias 9.31.5](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Paus.%209.31.5); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Epithalamium for Peleus and Thetis*. An alleged wedding song or hexameter wedding narrative known only through extremely limited ancient attribution. Its independent existence, title, boundaries, and Hesiodic authorship are all uncertain, so it is better treated as a reported item in the corpus than as an securely recoverable work ([Merkelbach and West](https://archive.org/details/fragmentahesiode0000hesi); [survey of the ancient attributions](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/21/75/90/28/2021759028/2021759028.pdf)).

### Fragmentary instructional and specialist poems attributed to Hesiod

- **Date unknown:** *Precepts of Chiron*. A didactic hexameter poem in which Chiron instructs the young Achilles in religious, ethical, and practical conduct—a protocol voiced inside heroic myth. Aristophanes of Byzantium was already said to have rejected Hesiod’s authorship, and only fragments and reports survive ([Stamatopoulou](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/62990/excerpt/9781107162990_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Great Works*. A fragmentary didactic poem whose title suggests a longer counterpart or expanded form of *Works and Days*. It may instead have been an umbrella compilation incorporating several instructional poems, so its resistance to being treated as a discrete work is part of its bibliographical status ([Stamatopoulou](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/62990/excerpt/9781107162990_excerpt.pdf); [Merkelbach and West](https://archive.org/details/fragmentahesiode0000hesi)).
- **Date unknown:** *Astronomy* (also reported as *Astrology* or *Book about Stars*). A fragmentary hexameter catalogue of constellations, their forms, positions, movements, and possibly their myths, useful for activities such as farming and navigation. It may have been an independent instructional poem or a section of an expanded *Works and Days* ([Stamatopoulou](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/62990/excerpt/9781107162990_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Ornithomancy* (*Bird Divination*). An instructional poem or appended section explaining bird omens, reportedly performed or transmitted as a continuation of *Works and Days*. Ancient scholarship already declared it spurious, and no surviving fragment can be assigned to it with certainty; even its status as an independent text is therefore doubtful ([Stamatopoulou](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/62990/excerpt/9781107162990_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Idaean Dactyls*. A poorly attested poem containing origin stories about metallurgy and the mythical Idaean Dactyls. Only testimonia or minimal fragments survive, and attribution to Hesiod cannot be tested ([Stamatopoulou](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/62990/excerpt/9781107162990_excerpt.pdf); [Most, vol. II](https://books.google.com/books?id=CgGbIKguHwsC)).
- **Date unknown:** *Potters*. A lost instructional or occupational poem named in the later catalogue of writings circulating under Hesiod’s name. Virtually nothing establishes its form, contents, date, or authorship, so the title records an ancient attribution rather than a securely identifiable made object ([Koning](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780190209049_A35486247/preview-9780190209049_A35486247.pdf); [Suda, η 583](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-cgi-bin/search.cgi?search_method=QUERY&login=guest&enlogin=guest&user_list=η,583&db=REAL)).

### Reported occasional work of doubtful existence or authorship

- **Date unknown:** *Dirge for Batrachus*. The *Suda* attributes to Hesiod a lament for a beloved named Batrachus, but no text survives and the biographical setting is otherwise unknown. It resists being called a recoverable work: what exists is a late lexicographical notice of a title, not independently verifiable evidence of Hesiod’s act of composition ([Suda, η 583](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-cgi-bin/search.cgi?search_method=QUERY&login=guest&enlogin=guest&user_list=η,583&db=REAL); [Koning](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780190209049_A35486247/preview-9780190209049_A35486247.pdf)).
- **Date unknown:** *On Preserved Foods*. A poem about preserved or salted foods was attributed to Hesiod in a single report preserved by Athenaeus, but that same testimony rejects the attribution. It is included here as a disputed ancient claim, not as something that can responsibly be said to have been made by Hesiod ([Stamatopoulou](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811071/62990/excerpt/9781107162990_excerpt.pdf); [Athenaeus 3.116b](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Ath.+3.116b)).
