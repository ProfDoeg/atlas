## Works by Jules-Félix Coutan

### Academic works, studies, and Roman submissions

- **1872:** *Ajax bravant les dieux et foudroyé*. Plaster figure made for the Prix de Rome competition; Coutan won the grand prix with it. The surviving work belongs to the École nationale supérieure des beaux-arts. ([INHA/AGORHA](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/8611eef3-6a5c-4c8f-a233-bd111046a46f))
- **1874–April 1875:** *Œdipe et le Sphinx*. Plaster bas-relief, the first-year Rome submission completed a year late; it was shown at the 1876 Salon, but its present location is unknown. ([INHA/AGORHA](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/3c0490c8-1a1b-445a-bea4-ceed4a739e6f?database=80))
- **1874–April 1875:** *Le Rémouleur, copie d’après l’antique*. Marble academic copy and first-year Rome submission, executed during Coutan’s second year; it survives at the École des beaux-arts. As a prescribed copy, its status is closer to an exercise than an autonomous invention. ([INHA/AGORHA](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/eb99e28e-6086-42dc-9936-c8fdd6b50a08))
- **1874–April 1875:** *Éros*. Plaster statue sent from Rome in Coutan’s second year and awarded a first-class medal at the 1876 Salon; the original state deposit is now untraced, while a marble, a damaged bronze, and reductions survive. ([INHA/AGORHA](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/b8fd1418-d444-46bf-a974-342bb3e56435), [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/eros-5952))
- **1875–1876:** *L’Agriculture*. Bas-relief made as a Rome submission; it belongs to the documented sequence of Coutan’s envois, although the surviving object and its present location are not securely established. ([INHA/AGORHA artist dossier](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/8611eef3-6a5c-4c8f-a233-bd111046a46f))
- **1876:** *Tête d’étude*. Required Rome project recorded as “non exécutée.” It is an unrealized project, not a surviving sculpture. ([INHA/AGORHA artist dossier](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/8611eef3-6a5c-4c8f-a233-bd111046a46f?database=80))
- **1876:** *Esquisse de bas-relief*. Required Rome project recorded as “non exécutée”; no made object is known, so it resists inclusion as an obra except as documented intention. ([INHA/AGORHA artist dossier](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/8611eef3-6a5c-4c8f-a233-bd111046a46f?database=80))
- **1876–1877:** *Saint Christophe portant le Christ enfant*. Monumental group conceived in plaster and marble as Coutan’s final Rome submission; the work was delayed, and the plaster is documented at the Jardin Massey in Tarbes while the marble’s history is less certain. ([INHA/AGORHA](https://agorha.inha.fr/recherche?affichage=cartouche&facets=&fieldSort=&filters=&noticeType=&page=22&pageSize=50&selectionId=PHzq1YMBCO-h5TLib3Z_&sort=&terms=&type=simple&withAttachment=false))
- **1879:** *Jupiter et Léda*. Signed plaster high-relief, probably deposited with the Académie des beaux-arts around 1879–1880; the absence of a deposit record makes that early provenance uncertain. ([Académie des beaux-arts](https://www.academiedesbeauxarts.fr/sites/default/files/inline-files/Notice-Jules-Coutan.pdf))

### Independent statues, reliefs, and editions

- **1875 or later:** *Cupidon debout* / *Standing Cupid*. Bronze reduction derived from Coutan’s Roman-period treatment of Éros and issued in more than one size by commercial founders; individual casts are manifestations of the model rather than separate compositions. ([Sotheby’s catalogue note](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2007/19th-century-furniture-sculpture-ceramics-and-works-of-art-including-property-formerly-from-the-palacio-ferreyra-argentina-n08305/lot.274.html), [INHA/AGORHA](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/b8fd1418-d444-46bf-a974-342bb3e56435))
- **1882:** *La Porteuse de pain*. Social-realist standing figure, known through the Petit Palais plaster model and formerly through a bronze erected near the Tour Saint-Jacques; the public bronze was melted down under the Vichy regime. ([Paris Musées collection search](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/recherche/auteur/Coutan%2C%20Jules%20F%C3%A9lix))
- **1885:** *Sergent d’armes du XIVe siècle*. Historical-costume statue made for the Paris Hôtel de Ville, with a model preserved by the Petit Palais; nineteenth-century catalogues record the composition in plaster before its public translation. ([Paris Musées](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/recherche/auteur/Coutan%2C%20Jules%20F%C3%A9lix), [1889 official catalogue](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0e/Catalogue_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral_officiel%3B_beaux-arts%3B_Exposition_centennale_de_l%27art_fran%C3%A7ais_%281789-1889%29_%28IA_cataloguegeneral00expo%29.pdf))
- **c. 1887:** *La Paix armée*. Allegorical statue conceived in plaster and cast in bronze for a Paris public garden; sources variously call it a statue, column, or monument and disagree between the place d’Anvers and parc Montsouris, reflecting relocations or confusion between model and installation. ([Paris Musées](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/recherche/auteur/Coutan%2C%20Jules%20F%C3%A9lix), [1889 official catalogue](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0e/Catalogue_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral_officiel%3B_beaux-arts%3B_Exposition_centennale_de_l%27art_fran%C3%A7ais_%281789-1889%29_%28IA_cataloguegeneral00expo%29.pdf))
- **1889:** *La Ville de Paris*. Seated allegorical figure belonging to the *Fontaine du Progrès* ensemble; a small plaster model survives at the Petit Palais. It is both an individual sculpture and a component of a larger Gesamtkunstwerk-like fountain. ([Paris Musées](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/ru/node/227864))
- **1890:** *Buste de Jean-Charles Alphand*. Marble portrait presented by the personnel of the 1889 Exposition’s works department; a related bronze was installed on Alphand’s tomb at Père-Lachaise. ([Paris Musées](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/de/node/227531), [Inventaire général des richesses d’art de la France](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6a/Inventaire_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral_des_richesses_d%27art_de_la_France_%28IA_inventairegenera331fran%29.pdf))
- **1891:** *Le Manuscrit*, also called *La Calligraphie*. Marble architectural allegory made for the Bibliothèque nationale’s Richelieu complex; the two titles describe the same figure in different sources and should not automatically be counted as separate works. ([Musée d’Orsay artist dossier](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/jules-coutan-9615), [INHA/AGORHA artist dossier](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark%3A/54721/8611eef3-6a5c-4c8f-a233-bd111046a46f))
- **1893:** *L’Union du Travail et de la Paix*. Allegorical group presented to the engineer Louis Lemoine by his employees; reductions are documented, but the extent and dating of the edition are not securely established. ([Musée d’Orsay biographical bibliography](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/jules-coutan-9615))
- **late 19th–early 20th century:** *La Renommée*. Bronze high-relief of a winged female figure riding a horse; no precise creation date is established, and the known cast entered the Musée des beaux-arts de Pau in 1930. ([POP/Joconde](https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/joconde/00980001412))
- **undated:** *Femme nue enjambant une souche d’arbre*. Unfinished or study-like sculptural figure preserved at the Petit Palais; its generic museum title and absent date make it resist treatment as a fully fixed, independently titled work. ([Paris Musées collection search](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/en/recherche/type/oeuvre/ET/auteur/Coutan%2C%20Jules%20F%C3%A9lix))
- **undated:** *Femme nue tenant une draperie*. Sculptural study preserved at the Petit Palais. Its descriptive title is supplied by the collection rather than necessarily by Coutan. ([Paris Musées collection search](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/en/recherche/type/oeuvre/ET/auteur/Coutan%2C%20Jules%20F%C3%A9lix))

### Architectural sculpture and exposition ensembles

- **c. 1879–1885:** *Voltaire*. Architectural statue commissioned for the reconstructed Hôtel de Ville in Paris; the exact execution date is not fixed in the consulted sources. ([Wikisculpture repertory](https://wikisculpture.fr/index.php/bios-sculpteur/215-coutan-jules))
- **1889:** *Fontaine du Progrès*, also described as *La France portant le flambeau de la civilisation*. Monumental fountain ensemble for the Exposition universelle, combining a central Genius or France figure with seated allegories including *La Ville de Paris*. The fountain was an ephemeral exposition Gesamtkunstwerk, although models of components survive. ([1889 official catalogue](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0e/Catalogue_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral_officiel%3B_beaux-arts%3B_Exposition_centennale_de_l%27art_fran%C3%A7ais_%281789-1889%29_%28IA_cataloguegeneral00expo%29.pdf), [Paris Musées](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/ru/node/227864))
- **1898:** *Dix cariatides de la salle Favart*. Ten supporting figures integrated into the tiers of boxes in the third Opéra-Comique; they are architectural sculpture and a serial ensemble, not ten securely differentiated titled works. ([Opéra-Comique historical guide](https://www.opera-comique.com/sites/default/files/2022-05/oc_guide_historique_2018_web.pdf))
- **c. 1898–1900:** *La France de la Renaissance*. Monumental allegorical figure at the upstream, left-bank pylon of the Pont Alexandre-III; a preparatory model survives at the Petit Palais. ([Paris Musées collection search](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/recherche/auteur/Coutan%2C%20Jules%20F%C3%A9lix))
- **c. 1898–1900:** *Lion*. Monumental animal sculpture associated with Coutan’s contribution to the Pont Alexandre-III. The consulted repertories identify it independently, though its conceptual relation to *La France de la Renaissance* makes the boundary between component and separate obra uncertain. ([Wikisculpture repertory](https://wikisculpture.fr/index.php/bios-sculpteur/215-coutan-jules))
- **1900:** *Portique monumental du pavillon de la Manufacture nationale de Sèvres*. Polychrome architectural and ceramic ensemble designed with architect Charles-Auguste Risler for the Exposition universelle; it incorporates reliefs, ornament, and a central female medallion and is now installed in square Félix-Desruelles. Its inseparability of sculpture, ceramics, color, and architecture makes “Gesamtkunstwerk” more accurate than “statue.” ([Wikimedia documentation](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3APortique_monumental_Jules-F%C3%A9lix_Coutan_004.JPG), [Paris Musées collection search](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/recherche/auteur/Coutan%2C%20Jules%20F%C3%A9lix))
- **1893–1901:** *Les Races humaines*, also called *Chasseurs d’aigles* or *Les Dénicheurs d’aigles*. Monumental high-relief commissioned by the state in 1893: the plaster model is dated 1900 and the bronze was installed on the Muséum national d’histoire naturelle in 1901. The divergent titles identify one composition in different institutional contexts, not three works. ([À nos grands hommes](https://anosgrandshommes.musee-orsay.fr/index.php/Detail/objects/32109/view/pdf/export_format/_pdf_ca_objects_summary), [POP/Joconde](https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/joconde/000SC013503))
- **1906:** *La Science*. Architectural bas-relief on the former viaduc de Passy, now the Pont de Bir-Hakeim; it forms a pendant with *Le Travail*. ([Wikisculpture repertory](https://wikisculpture.fr/index.php/bios-sculpteur/215-coutan-jules))
- **1906:** *Le Travail*. Architectural bas-relief on the Pont de Bir-Hakeim and pendant to *La Science*. ([Wikisculpture repertory](https://wikisculpture.fr/index.php/bios-sculpteur/215-coutan-jules))
- **1907–1914:** *The Glory of Commerce*. Colossal architectural group—Mercury flanked by Minerva and Hercules—designed for the 42nd Street façade of Grand Central Terminal, New York, and fabricated in sections by American contractors. Coutan supplied the sculptural creation, but the installed manifestation is necessarily collaborative. ([Musée d’Orsay artist dossier](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/jules-coutan-9615))
- **by 1914:** *La Clémence*. Monumental architectural allegory for the Palais de Justice in Paris; the repertory dates the installed work to 1914, though the design may precede that date. ([Wikisculpture repertory](https://wikisculpture.fr/index.php/bios-sculpteur/215-coutan-jules))

### Funerary works and memorial monuments

- **1885–1890:** *Monument funéraire de la famille Herbette*. Collaborative funerary monument at Montparnasse Cemetery, made with Léon Longepied, medalist Oscar Roty, and architect Poussin; Coutan’s marble figure was exhibited at the 1890 Salon as a statue intended for Mme Louis Herbette’s tomb. Authorship therefore belongs to an ensemble, not to Coutan alone. ([Nouvelles Archives de l’art français](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/60/Nouvelles_archives_de_l%E2%80%99art_fran%C3%A7ais%2C_3e_s%C3%A9rie%2C_tome_13.pdf), [Salon documentation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/73/Explication_des_ouvrages_de_peinture_et_dessins%2C_sculpture%2C_architecture_et_gravure%2C_des_artistes_vivans_%28IA_explicationdesou1891soci%29.pdf))
- **1895:** *Monument commémoratif aux soldats de la Vienne morts pendant la guerre de 1870–1871*. Multi-figure public memorial in Poitiers, known also through a plaster model at the Musée Sainte-Croix; later intervention damaged the bronze patina, so its present manifestation is not materially identical with Coutan’s original finish. ([Wikisculpture repertory](https://wikisculpture.fr/index.php/bios-sculpteur/215-coutan-jules))
- **undated, after 1891:** *Buste de Jean-Charles Alphand pour son tombeau*. Bronze funerary portrait at Père-Lachaise, related to but materially distinct from the 1890 marble presentation bust. ([Inventaire général des richesses d’art de la France](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6a/Inventaire_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral_des_richesses_d%27art_de_la_France_%28IA_inventairegenera331fran%29.pdf))
- **undated:** *Buste du baron Haussmann pour son tombeau*. Funerary portrait at Père-Lachaise attributed to Coutan in modern repertories; the consulted sources do not supply a secure execution date. ([French Sculpture Census biographical reference](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/jules-coutan-9615))
- **c. 1904 or later:** *Figures angéliques du tombeau de José C. Paz*. Funerary sculptures for the Paz family mausoleum in La Recoleta Cemetery, Buenos Aires. Sources agree on Coutan’s authorship but do not establish a single secure execution date or fixed collective title. ([Buenos Aires funerary documentation](https://nuclea.cnea.gob.ar/bitstreams/9320e829-03f1-4f59-9e4e-64df3764baad/download))
- **1908:** *Mausolée de Nicolás Avellaneda*. Funerary sculptural ensemble in La Recoleta Cemetery, Buenos Aires; it is a tomb architecture–sculpture complex rather than a single freestanding statue. ([Monument scholarship](https://9dejulio.gov.ar/pdf/museo/Monumento%20conmemorativo%20y%20espacio%20publico%20en%20Iberoamerica.pdf))
- **after 1907:** *Mausolée du général Luis María Campos*. Funerary sculptural ensemble in La Recoleta Cemetery, Buenos Aires. The consulted scholarly survey assigns it to Coutan but does not give a precise completion date. ([Monument scholarship](https://9dejulio.gov.ar/pdf/museo/Monumento%20conmemorativo%20y%20espacio%20publico%20en%20Iberoamerica.pdf))
- **early 20th century:** *Monument à Benjamín Vicuña Mackenna*. Public commemorative monument in Santiago de Chile; a precise execution or inauguration date was not securely recoverable from the consulted corpus and is therefore not supplied. ([Monument scholarship](https://9dejulio.gov.ar/pdf/museo/Monumento%20conmemorativo%20y%20espacio%20publico%20en%20Iberoamerica.pdf))
- **1907 competition:** *Projet de monument à Bartolomé Mitre*. Competition design for an equestrian monument in Buenos Aires; Coutan was a finalist, but the commission went to Davide Calandra and Edoardo Rubino. This is an unrealized project, and no completed monument by Coutan should be inferred from it. ([Monument scholarship](https://9dejulio.gov.ar/pdf/museo/Monumento%20conmemorativo%20y%20espacio%20publico%20en%20Iberoamerica.pdf))
- **1914:** *Monument à Carlos Pellegrini*. Carrara-marble and bronze public monument in Buenos Aires, combining Pellegrini’s figure with allegories of Justice, Law, the Republic, Industry, and Commerce; it was inaugurated in September 1914. ([City of Buenos Aires](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/node/49555), [Buenos Aires heritage study](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/sites/default/files/media/document/2015/02/05/17c0fd41d6c46f78f40c5a558bcc34bdea586d29.pdf))
