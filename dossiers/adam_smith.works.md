## Works by Adam Smith

### Books: sustained authored obras

- **1759–1790:** *The Theory of Moral Sentiments*. A treatise in moral philosophy, first published in 1759 and repeatedly remade by Smith across six lifetime editions (1761, 1767, 1774, 1781, and the substantially recast 1790 edition); these revisions are successive manifestations of one evolving obra, not mere reprints. [Glasgow Edition overview](https://special.lib.gla.ac.uk/explore/adamsmith300/explorelearn/ideas/keyworks/); [edition history](https://iep.utm.edu/smith/)

- **1776–1789:** *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations*. A systematic inquiry into political economy, issued in five lifetime editions; Smith materially revised it, especially for the third edition of 1784, so its editions form an evolving obra. [Glasgow Edition](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Glasgow_Edition_of_the_Works_and_Cor.html?id=3RoEAAAAYAAJ); [Cambridge Companion excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805217/79241/excerpt/9780521779241_excerpt.pdf)

### Essays, reviews, editorial texts, and public letters

- **1748:** *Preface to William Hamilton’s Poems on Several Occasions*. An unsigned editorial preface written for a collection Smith helped bring into print; the volume, rather than the preface alone, is partly an editorial obra made through selection and presentation of another poet’s work. [Glasgow Edition text and editorial introduction](https://www.free-ebooks.net/academic-science/The-Glasgow-Edition-of-the-Works-of-Adam-Smith/html/17)

- **1755:** *A Review of Johnson’s Dictionary*. An anonymous critical review in the first *Edinburgh Review*, assessing Samuel Johnson’s lexicographical method rather than merely reporting the book’s appearance. Its attribution to Smith is accepted by the editors of the Glasgow Edition. [University of Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/library/files/special/exhibns/scottish/adamsmith.html); [Glasgow Edition contents](https://iep.utm.edu/smith/)

- **1756:** *A Letter to the Authors of the Edinburgh Review*. An anonymous survey and critical intervention concerning contemporary European literature, including the French *Encyclopédie* and Rousseau’s *Discourse on Inequality*. Though styled as a letter, it was made for public print and functions as an essay. [Glasgow Edition contents](https://iep.utm.edu/smith/); [Cambridge Companion excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805217/79241/excerpt/9780521779241_excerpt.pdf)

- **1758:** *Dedication to William Hamilton’s Poems on Several Occasions*. A dedicatory paratext for the second edition of Hamilton’s poems. It is unsigned and its authorship is not certain, but the Glasgow editors include it as probably Smith’s because of the circumstances of the edition. [Glasgow Edition editorial discussion](https://www.free-ebooks.net/academic-science/The-Glasgow-Edition-of-the-Works-of-Adam-Smith/html/17)

- **1761:** *Considerations concerning the First Formation of Languages, and the Different Genius of Original and Compounded Languages*. A speculative history of language, first published independently in *The Philological Miscellany* and later attached to the third and subsequent editions of *The Theory of Moral Sentiments*. It is both a separate essay and, in those manifestations, an architectural part of a larger book. [Publication history](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecaf.12550); [Glasgow Edition placement](https://iep.utm.edu/smith/)

- **1776:** *Letter from Adam Smith, LL.D. to William Strahan, Esq.* Often called Smith’s account of the life, illness, and character of David Hume, this private letter was deliberately published and thereby became a public memorial and controversial ethical intervention. It is a letter acting as an obituary rather than a conventional book. [Glasgow *Correspondence*](https://pratclif.com/liberty/smith/Smith_0141-07_EBk_v4.pdf)

- **before 1758; published 1795:** *The Principles which Lead and Direct Philosophical Enquiries; Illustrated by the History of Astronomy*. A long philosophical history of scientific explanation, probably substantially completed before 1758 but withheld during Smith’s life and published by his literary executors in *Essays on Philosophical Subjects*. Its date of composition is approximate, and the posthumous printed text is a manifestation of a surviving manuscript rather than a publication completed by Smith. [Glasgow Edition contents](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Glasgow_Edition_of_the_Works_and_Cor.html?id=48IO3cLjaWcC); [dating discussion](https://economics.studio/economic-scientists-history/writings-108478.html)

- **date uncertain; published 1795:** *The Principles which Lead and Direct Philosophical Enquiries; Illustrated by the History of the Ancient Physics*. An unfinished historical-philosophical essay surviving among the manuscripts Smith allowed his executors to preserve. It resists treatment as a completed work: its posthumous publication does not settle how finished Smith considered it. [Glasgow Edition contents](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Glasgow_Edition_of_the_Works_and_Cor.html?id=48IO3cLjaWcC)

- **date uncertain; published 1795:** *The Principles which Lead and Direct Philosophical Enquiries; Illustrated by the History of the Ancient Logics and Metaphysics*. An unfinished companion history, preserved and printed posthumously. It belongs to Smith’s larger project of a connected history of knowledge but is only a surviving fragment of that project. [Glasgow Edition contents](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Glasgow_Edition_of_the_Works_and_Cor.html?id=48IO3cLjaWcC)

- **date uncertain; published 1795:** *Of the External Senses*. A philosophical essay or draft on perception, issued after Smith’s death from the manuscripts retained by his executors. Its state does not warrant silently treating it as a finished treatise. [Glasgow Edition contents](https://iep.utm.edu/smith/)

- **date uncertain; published 1795:** *Of the Nature of that Imitation which takes place in what are called the Imitative Arts*. An aesthetics essay spanning painting, sculpture, and related arts. It is a surviving component of Smith’s projected history of the liberal sciences and elegant arts, not a completed version of that larger project. [Glasgow Edition description](https://about.libertyfund.org/collections/the-glasgow-edition-of-the-works-of-adam-smith/); [contents](https://iep.utm.edu/smith/)

- **date uncertain; published 1795:** *Of the Affinity between Music, Dancing, and Poetry*. An aesthetics essay treating relations among media; because its subject refuses a clean separation of sound, movement, and verse, it approaches a small theoretical Gesamtkunstwerk even though its own medium is prose. [Glasgow Edition contents](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Glasgow_Edition_of_the_Works_and_Cor.html?id=48IO3cLjaWcC)

- **before March 1783; published 1795:** *Of the Affinity between certain English and Italian Verses*. An unfinished paper on poetic form which Smith was still planning to perfect in 1783. The surviving text therefore records a project in progress rather than a work he declared complete. [Smith’s 1783 letter concerning the paper](https://pratclif.com/liberty/smith/Smith_0141-07_EBk_v4.pdf); [Glasgow Edition contents](https://iep.utm.edu/smith/)

### Lectures and student-made manifestations

- **1748–1751:** *Edinburgh lectures on rhetoric, belles-lettres, and related philosophical subjects*. A course of public lectures and thus a performed intellectual obra, known from biographical evidence but not preserved as a complete authorial text. It resists ordinary bibliography because the event—not a surviving manuscript—is the primary thing Smith made. [Cambridge Companion excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805217/79241/excerpt/9780521779241_excerpt.pdf)

- **1762–1763:** *Lectures on Rhetoric and Belles Lettres*. A Glasgow lecture course surviving in a student’s report, not in a book manuscript written by Smith; the Glasgow Edition prints that report as evidence of Smith’s performed teaching. Authorship is consequently layered: Smith made and delivered the course, while an unidentified student made the extant verbal record. [Glasgow Edition catalogue](https://special.lib.gla.ac.uk/explore/adamsmith300/explorelearn/ideas/keyworks/); [edition description](https://iep.utm.edu/smith/)

- **1762–1763:** *Lectures on Jurisprudence: Report of 1762–3*. A student’s notes from Smith’s Glasgow course on justice, police, revenue, and arms. It is an incomplete manifestation of an oral course rather than a finished Smith-authored book. [Glasgow Edition description](https://obnb.uk/p5618357-lectures-on-jurisprudence)

- **1766:** *Lectures on Jurisprudence: Report dated 1766*. A second, independent student report of Smith’s jurisprudence teaching. Its date refers to the manuscript report, while the lectures it records may derive from an earlier session; it cannot simply be merged with the 1762–63 report because the two witnesses differ. [Glasgow Edition description](https://obnb.uk/p5618357-lectures-on-jurisprudence)

### Drafts and fragments

- **c. 1763:** *Early Draft of Part of The Wealth of Nations*. An untitled manuscript draft containing an early formulation of material later remade for *The Wealth of Nations*. Its modern title is editorial, and it is evidence of a developing project rather than an independent book Smith named. [Contents of *Lectures on Jurisprudence*](https://obnb.uk/p5618357-lectures-on-jurisprudence)

- **date uncertain:** *Fragment on the Division of Labour*. One of two separately surviving manuscript fragments associated with the formation of *The Wealth of Nations*. It lacks the completeness and fixed authorial title of an autonomous work. [Contents of *Lectures on Jurisprudence*](https://obnb.uk/p5618357-lectures-on-jurisprudence)

- **date uncertain:** *Second Fragment on the Division of Labour*. A distinct surviving fragment printed alongside the early draft and jurisprudence reports in the Glasgow Edition. Like its companion, it is better understood as workshop material from the *Wealth of Nations* project than as a finished obra. [Contents of *Lectures on Jurisprudence*](https://obnb.uk/p5618357-lectures-on-jurisprudence)

### Memoranda, correspondence, and administrative writing

- **1740–1790, with conjecturally dated items:** *Surviving correspondence of Adam Smith*. A dispersed documentary corpus of letters rather than one designed work; the Glasgow Edition arranges both letters by Smith and letters addressed to him, including damaged, excerpted, undated, and conjecturally dated witnesses. Only the outgoing letters are things Smith made, and even their survival is accidental rather than authorially curated. [Glasgow *Correspondence*, editorial principles and numbered letters](https://pratclif.com/liberty/smith/Smith_0141-07_EBk_v4.pdf)

- **February 1778:** *Smith’s Thoughts on the State of the Contest with America*. An unsigned policy memorandum advocating possible responses to the American conflict. The title is Alexander Wedderburn’s contemporary endorsement, and attribution rests on biographical, textual, and doctrinal evidence rather than a Smith signature, so it remains an attributed document rather than an uncontested authored publication. [Glasgow *Correspondence*, Appendix B](https://pratclif.com/liberty/smith/Smith_0141-07_EBk_v4.pdf)

- **1778–1790:** *Custom-house documents*. Minutes, reports, queries, and other administrative instruments made or signed by Smith as a Scottish customs commissioner, often collectively with fellow commissioners. These are institutional acts and protocols rather than literary works; signature does not always establish that Smith personally composed their wording. [Glasgow *Correspondence*, Appendix D](https://pratclif.com/liberty/smith/Smith_0141-07_EBk_v4.pdf)

### Unfinished and destroyed projects

- **announced 1759–1790; destroyed 1790:** *A theory and history of law and government*. Smith repeatedly announced a projected account of jurisprudence, government, and the historical development of law but never published it. Shortly before his death he ordered manuscripts destroyed, so the project—not a recoverable finished text—is the work; the lecture reports preserve related thinking but are not the lost treatise itself. [Cambridge Companion excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805217/79241/excerpt/9780521779241_excerpt.pdf); [University of Glasgow overview](https://special.lib.gla.ac.uk/explore/adamsmith300/explorelearn/ideas/keyworks/)

- **date uncertain; manuscripts destroyed 1790:** *Connected history of the liberal sciences and elegant arts*. A large projected history known through Dugald Stewart’s account and through the essays Smith permitted to survive. It resists being called a completed work because most of the underlying manuscripts were among the sixteen manuscript volumes destroyed at Smith’s direction. [Discussion of the project and destroyed manuscripts](https://s3-euw1-ap-pe-df-pch-content-store-p.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/9780203092750/54425c81-7f33-4151-8d91-280539daf6b1/preview.pdf)

- **before July 1790:** *Destroyed manuscript corpus*. Sixteen folio volumes were burned shortly before Smith’s death at his instruction. Their contents and degrees of completion cannot now be securely enumerated, so they are not sixteen nameable works; what survives bibliographically is the deliberate act of refusing their manifestation as works. [Account of the destroyed manuscripts and larger projects](https://s3-euw1-ap-pe-df-pch-content-store.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/9780203092750/54425c81-7f33-4151-8d91-280539daf6b1/preview.pdf)
