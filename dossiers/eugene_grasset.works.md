## Works by Eugène Samuel Grasset

### Gesamtkunstwerke, interiors, furniture, and architectural decoration

- **1869–1870:** *Décorations du Théâtre de Lausanne*. Painted and sculpted theatrical décor produced before Grasset settled in Paris; surviving components and a complete inventory have not been established. [Murray-Robertson 1981; Lepdor 2011]
- **1877–1880:** *Décoration et ameublement de l’hôtel particulier de Charles Gillot, rue Madame*. A Gesamtkunstwerk joining carved furniture, wall treatment, textiles, lighting, and architectural woodwork; Fulgraff executed much of the cabinetmaking from Grasset’s designs. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1877–1880:** *Salle à manger de Charles Gillot*. The surviving ensemble includes a monumental buffet, table, chairs, fireplace, and architectural fittings; treating it simply as “furniture” obscures Grasset’s design of the room as a unified environment. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1877–1880:** *Buffet de la salle à manger Gillot*. A carved oak-and-walnut cabinet designed by Grasset and executed by Fulgraff, populated by fantastic animals and figures derived from popular and medieval imagery. [Lepdor 2011; Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1877–1885:** *Chaises de la salle à manger Gillot*. A model of high-backed carved oak chair executed by Fulgraff in multiple examples; the number and dates of surviving examples vary among collection and sale records. [Marc Maison](https://www.marcmaison.fr/mobilier-francais-19-siecle/sieges-fauteuils-salon/grasset-%E2%80%93-fulgraff-serie-de-6-chaises-en-chene-sculpte-paris-1880%E2%80%931885)
- **1877–1880:** *Lit de l’hôtel Gillot*. A lost bed known through documentation of the commission; its disappearance makes it a documented design rather than an extant object. [Murray-Robertson 1981; Lepdor 2011]
- **1877–1880:** *Cheminée monumentale de l’hôtel Gillot*. An architectural furnishing within the Gillot ensemble, not an autonomous sculpture. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1881–1883:** *Décor du cabaret Le Chat Noir*. An interior project including an exterior cat sign, a fireplace, chandeliers, and ironwork; because the cabaret changed premises and was later dismantled, the boundaries and survival of Grasset’s contribution are uncertain. [Lemoisne 1900; Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1881–1883:** *Enseigne du Chat Noir*. A black cat reclining against a golden sun between large wrought-iron lanterns, designed as an architectural sign rather than as a printed logo. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1880s:** *Façade de l’hôtel Dumas, Paris*. An architectural-decoration project attributed to Grasset in the monographic literature; published accounts do not provide a secure single date or a complete separation of his work from that of the architect and craftsmen. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1890–1895:** *Décoration de l’église Saint-Étienne de Briare*. A Gesamtkunstwerk of cartoons for façade mosaics, pavement mosaics, and related decoration, manufactured in Briare and executed collaboratively rather than physically made by Grasset alone. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1890:** *Fronton de l’église Saint-Étienne de Briare*. A mosaic architectural composition made from Grasset’s cartoon. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1894:** *La Mosaïste*. A figurative mosaic in Briare enamels; the title names both a represented craftswoman and the manufactured object. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1895:** *Sols en mosaïque de l’église Saint-Étienne de Briare*. An extensive group of pavement designs rather than one readily bounded picture; virtually all the church’s mosaic floors were produced from Grasset’s cartoons. [Lepdor 2011]
- **c. 1900–1907:** *Décors en lave émaillée de la Samaritaine*. Façade ornament designed for Frantz Jourdain’s department-store building and realized industrially in enamelled lava; authorship is therefore design authorship shared with architect and fabricators. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1905:** *Salle à manger de Marcelle Seure*. A walnut ensemble designed for Charles Gillot’s daughter, comprising a large table, six chairs, a buffet, a serving cabinet, and two wall consoles. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1905:** *Grande table de la salle à manger Seure*. A component of the Seure room, meaningful both as an individual furnishing and as part of the larger ensemble. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1905:** *Six chaises de la salle à manger Seure*. A set designed as part of the room rather than six unrelated works. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1905:** *Buffet de la salle à manger Seure*. Walnut cabinet furniture belonging to the coordinated Seure ensemble. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1905:** *Desserte de la salle à manger Seure*. A serving cabinet designed for the same interior. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1905:** *Deux consoles d’applique de la salle à manger Seure*. A paired element of the Seure room; the pair, not each console separately, is the design unit recorded by the literature. [Lepdor 2011]

### Illustrated books and editorial Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1880:** *Les Fêtes chrétiennes*. Illustration cycle for Abbé Drioux’s book; Grasset made the images and ornament, not the authored religious text. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1882–1883:** *Le Petit Nab*. Illustrations and book decoration for René Delorme, writing as Saint-Juirs; sources differ between 1882 and 1883 because design, printing, and publication were not simultaneous. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1883:** *Histoire des quatre fils Aymon, très nobles et très vaillants chevaliers*. A book Gesamtkunstwerk in which Grasset coordinated illustrations, borders, page architecture, and colour reproduction around an existing medieval narrative; Charles Gillot printed the colour gillotage and Charles Marcilly bound the deluxe object. [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artists/13287-eugene-grasset)
- *Jean des Figues*. An illustration project repeatedly singled out in the contemporary Grasset literature; it survives as drawings rather than as a securely identified completed publication, so its resistance to “book” status is essential. [Lemoisne 1900](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eug%C3%A8ne_Grasset_et_son_oeuvre_%28IA_eugenegrassetets00lemo_0%29.pdf)
- *Esclarmonde* / *La Chasse d’Esclarmonde*. A cycle of fantastic drawings associated with a literary subject; contemporary catalogues describe the images, but the project’s intended final publication remains unclear. [Lemoisne 1900](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eug%C3%A8ne_Grasset_et_son_oeuvre_%28IA_eugenegrassetets00lemo_0%29.pdf)
- *La Légende de saint Nicolas*. A group of narrative illustrations documented in the 1894 exhibition literature; no securely dated autonomous book by Grasset corresponds to the title. [Lemoisne 1900](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eug%C3%A8ne_Grasset_et_son_oeuvre_%28IA_eugenegrassetets00lemo_0%29.pdf)
- **1896:** *La Plante et ses applications ornementales*. A teaching publication directed by Grasset and illustrated substantially through designs by his pupils; it is a protocol of ornamental transformation and a collective classroom production, not wholly an autograph picture-book. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1897–1904:** *Nouveau Larousse illustré*. Cover, title, lettering, and emblematic graphic work for the multi-volume dictionary; the dictionary’s text is not Grasset’s work. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1900:** *Le Procurateur de Judée*. Fourteen compositions for Anatole France’s story, engraved by Ernest Florian and published by Édouard Pelletan; Grasset made the illustration cycle and book decoration, not the literary text. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/rechercher.do?index=TOUS3&numNotice=12079800&typeNotice=p)
- **1901:** *Almanach du bibliophile pour l’année 1901*. A decorated annual produced for Édouard Pelletan; its identity lies in Grasset’s editorial ornament and illustrations rather than in authorship of all its verbal contents. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1905:** *Petit Larousse illustré*. Graphic and emblematic design work for the dictionary, including the institutional visual language derived from the Larousse sowing emblem; Grasset did not author the lexicon. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1905–1907:** *Méthode de composition ornementale*. A two-volume protocol based on Grasset’s teaching, explaining geometric and ornamental construction rather than merely displaying finished decoration; bibliographies variously cite 1905, 1907, or the two-volume span. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=TOUS3&listeAffinages=FacLocal_Lcl2CRdjTol&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=12079800&triResultParPage=5&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=p)
- **1909:** *Balthasar*. Illustrations for Anatole France’s text; the 1909 volume is a manifestation of earlier designs and must not be mistaken for a prose work authored by Grasset. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=TOUS3&listeAffinages=FacLocal_Lcl2CRdjTol&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=12079800&triResultParPage=5&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=p)
- **1910:** *Larousse pour tous*. Graphic work for the encyclopedic dictionary; it belongs to Grasset’s continuing Larousse identity programme rather than to his authored theoretical writings. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1911:** *Les Résurrections italiennes*. Thirteen compositions accompanying Henry Bérenger’s text, published by Pelletan. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=TOUS3&listeAffinages=FacLocal_Lcl2CRdjTol&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=12079800&triResultParPage=5&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=p)
- **1917:** *La Marseillaise, chant national*. A woven illustrated edition whose images were designed by Grasset and mechanically translated into textile by Cumin and Masson; it is simultaneously book, fabric, and manufactured manifestation. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=TOUS3&listeAffinages=FacLocal_Lcl2CRdjTol&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=12079800&triResultParPage=5&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=p)
- **Unpublished; edited posthumously:** *Compositions végétales*. An unfinished or unpublished teaching treatise preserved in notes and manuscripts; as a project and protocol it remains a work even though Grasset never fixed it as a published book. [Musée d’Orsay archives](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/sites/default/files/2022-06/Fonds_Archives_MO_Pr%C3%A9sentation_20220607.pdf)

### Type, emblems, and graphic identities

- **c. 1890:** *“Je sème à tout vent” — emblème Larousse*. Grasset reformulated Émile-Auguste Reiber’s earlier dandelion device; it is therefore a consequential redesign, not an uncontested wholly original invention. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1897–1900:** *Caractère Grasset*. A typeface adapted by Grasset from Nicolas Jenson’s fifteenth-century roman, patented by Georges Peignot in 1897 and cut by Henri Parmentier; Grasset authored the model, while the foundry determined its manufactured typographic manifestations. [Lepdor 2011]
- **c. 1899:** *Le Trèfle à quatre feuilles de la marque Georges Richard*. A commercial emblem for the cycle and automobile manufacturer, appearing both independently and within Grasset’s advertisements. [MCBA press-image dossier](https://www.musees.vd.ch/fileadmin/groups/16/images/expositions/Grasset/Dossier%20image%20presse%20new.pdf)
- **1903–1904:** *Type Grasset pour les timbres-poste*. A project commissioned to replace France’s “type Sage” but rejected for metropolitan use; adapted manifestations appeared on Indochinese stamps and postal stationery in 1904, so the unrealized French project and colonial issue should not be conflated. [Lepdor 2011]

### Posters and public graphic works

- **1885–1886:** *Les Fêtes de Paris — Gala du Théâtre de l’Opéra*. A colour advertising poster for a public entertainment. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1887:** *Librairie romantique E. Monnier*. A commercial poster for the publisher-bookseller E. Monnier, printed by J. Bognard. [Lemoisne 1900](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eug%C3%A8ne_Grasset_et_son_oeuvre_%28IA_eugenegrassetets00lemo_0%29.pdf)
- **1890:** *Théâtre national de l’Odéon, saison 1890–1891*. A theatrical-season poster. [Lemoisne 1900](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eug%C3%A8ne_Grasset_et_son_oeuvre_%28IA_eugenegrassetets00lemo_0%29.pdf)
- **c. 1891:** *À la Place Clichy*. A department-store poster; impressions and later reductions are often catalogued under different dates, although they derive from the same design. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1892:** *Encre L. Marquet, la meilleure de toutes les encres*. A commercial poster printed by G. de Malherbe; later impressions sometimes bear dates such as 1895 that describe the impression rather than the design. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/artists/2301-eugene-grasset)
- **1892:** *Chocolat Mexicain Masson Paris*. A commercial poster printed by G. de Malherbe; museum records also use the shorter title *Dépôt de chocolat Masson*. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1892–1893:** *Exposition des artistes décorateurs, Grafton Gallery, Londres*. An exhibition poster whose date is variously assigned to the 1892 design or 1893 event. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1893:** *La Valkyrie — poème et musique de Richard Wagner*. A music-publication or performance poster issued for P. Schott. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1893:** *Exposición Internacional de Madrid 1893–94 / Ne Plus Ultra, Reino de España*. A poster for the Madrid exhibition; repositories use variant French, Spanish, and promotional titles for the same design. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1894:** *Jeanne d’Arc — Sarah Bernhardt*. A theatrical poster made in two documented versions; the two variants should not be silently collapsed into a single physical object. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1894:** *Exposition Eugène Grasset — Salon des Cent*. An exhibition poster that also appeared as the cover of the associated issue of *La Plume*, making poster and periodical cover distinct manifestations of one design. [Salon des Cent](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_des_Cent)
- **1895:** *Histoire de France*. A promotional poster using a historical female figure; its exact commissioning publication is inconsistently described in later image catalogues. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1895–1897:** *A New Life of Napoleon / The Century Magazine*. An American magazine poster; sources distinguish the illustration’s initial commission from later poster printings. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1897:** *Nouveau Larousse illustré en six volumes*. A poster advertising the dictionary and simultaneously consolidating its graphic identity. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1897:** *Exposition Alexandre Falguière, sculpteur*. Exhibition poster derived from Grasset’s allegory *La Sculpture*; the image also appeared as a frontispiece and independent chromolithograph. [MCBA](https://www.mcba.ch/en/collection/la-sculpture-sculpture/)
- **1898:** *Harper’s Magazine*. An American periodical poster; different printings and language states survive. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- **1899:** *Cycles et automobiles — marque Georges Richard*. A commercial poster incorporating the four-leaf-clover identity devised for the manufacturer. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1900:** *L’Andalousie au temps des Maures — Exposition de 1900*. An exhibition poster evoking medieval Islamic Spain. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1902:** *Imprimerie G. de Malherbe*. A poster advertising the printer with whom Grasset repeatedly collaborated. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1904:** *Société des artistes décorateurs*. An exhibition or institutional poster for the society. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **c. 1900–1905:** *Abricotine, délicieuse liqueur*. A commercial poster whose design date is not securely fixed; museum and market records commonly range from about 1900 to 1905. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1905:** *Suzy Deguez dans ses danses d’art*. A performance poster. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- *Chemins de fer du Sud de l’Algérie*. A travel or railway poster known from surviving impressions; a dependable design date was not established in the consulted catalogues. [MAD Paris acquisitions report](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/mad_ra23.pdf)
- *Le Pion d’Espagnol*. A graphic poster documented in the Musée des Arts décoratifs collection; the consulted record does not supply a secure date. [MAD Paris acquisitions report](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/mad_ra23.pdf)
- *L’Illustration*. A periodical poster documented in the Musée des Arts décoratifs collection; multiple print states make a single undifferentiated date unsafe. [MAD Paris acquisitions report](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/mad_ra23.pdf)

### Stained glass: cartoons, projects, and collaborative manifestations

- **1893:** *Le Siège d’Orléans*. A cartoon for the projected Jeanne d’Arc windows at Orléans; as an unrealized architectural project, its intention rather than installation is the work. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1893:** *Vaucouleurs — Jeanne d’Arc part avec Jean de Metz*. A cartoon from the projected Jeanne d’Arc cycle. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1893:** *Chinon — Jeanne se présente à Charles VII au milieu de sa cour*. A cartoon from the projected Jeanne d’Arc cycle. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1893:** *Jeanne d’Arc dans sa prison*. A cartoon from the projected Jeanne d’Arc cycle. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1893:** *Entrée de Jeanne d’Arc à Orléans*. A historical composition associated in contemporary writing with Grasset’s Jeanne d’Arc imagery; its relation to the individually catalogued Orléans cartoons is not completely resolved. [Lemoisne 1900]
- **1893–1894:** *Verrières de la vie de Jeanne d’Arc pour la cathédrale Sainte-Croix d’Orléans*. An unrealized cycle project encompassing several surviving cartoons; it should not be described as installed stained glass. [Lemoisne 1900; Musée d’Orsay]
- **1894:** *La Matinée de printemps* / *Le Printemps*. A stained-glass manifestation executed by Félix Gaudin from Grasset’s cartoon; museum and exhibition sources use both titles. [MAD Paris](https://madparis.fr/~period/rubrique1.html)
- **1894:** *La Vie de saint Joseph*. A stained-glass cycle for the church of Sainte-Madeleine at Troyes, designed by Grasset and executed in collaboration with Félix Gaudin. [Lepdor 2011]
- *Saint Hubert*. A stained-glass design or window for Lyon; the consulted summary sources do not establish a firm date or identify every surviving component. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- *Saint Michel*. A stained-glass composition associated with Châlons; it must be distinguished from the related *Jeanne d’Arc et saint Michel* design named in contemporary criticism. [Lemoisne 1900]
- *Jeanne d’Arc*. A stained-glass composition associated with Châlons; surviving records do not always distinguish cartoon, window, and later replica. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- *La Musique*. A stained-glass composition associated with Châlons. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- *L’Automne*. A stained-glass composition associated with Châlons and conceived as a seasonal counterpart to *Le Printemps*. [Murray-Robertson 1981]
- *Tige de Jessé* / *Arbre de Jessé*. A stained-glass composition praised in contemporary criticism; title forms vary and a dependable date was not recovered. [Lemoisne 1900]
- *Vitraux du temple de L’Abergement*. A group of Protestant-church windows in Vaud designed by Grasset and executed by specialist glassworkers; available summaries do not supply a secure complete list of individual titles. [Lepdor 2011]

### Tapestry, textiles, wallpaper, and ceramics

- *Barbe-Bleue*. A tapestry or tapestry cartoon representing the Bluebeard legend, documented in the 1894 exhibition; whether contemporary references name the textile manifestation or Grasset’s model is not always clear. [Lemoisne 1900]
- **c. 1900:** *La Marseillaise*. A textile or tapestry design distinct from, though related to, the woven illustrated edition published in 1917. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1900:** *Fête de printemps*. A tapestry or large textile panel shown at the Exposition universelle; Otto Alfred Briffod later adapted the design for the Lausanne building *La France*, and that adaptation is not an autograph Grasset work. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1870s–1900s:** *Modèles de papiers peints*. A body of industrial pattern designs rather than a securely enumerated series of autonomous works; the principal monographs attest the practice but do not provide a complete titled census. [Murray-Robertson 1981; Lepdor 2011]
- **1870s–1900s:** *Modèles d’étoffes et de broderies*. A dispersed body of designs recorded by early exhibition catalogues; many sheets lack fixed titles or dates and resist reduction to “works” in the conventional singular sense. [Lemoisne 1900]
- **1870s–1900s:** *Modèles de faïence et de céramique*. Designs made for manufacture by others; the early literature confirms the body of work but does not identify every model by title. [Lemoisne 1900; Lepdor 2011]

### Jewellery and metalwork

- **c. 1898–1900:** *Apparitions*. An enamelled brooch designed by Grasset and executed within the jewellery trade, commonly associated with Henri Vever; design authorship and manufacture are collaborative rather than interchangeable. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **c. 1900:** *Peigne “Assyrienne”*. A hair comb designed by Grasset and realized as jewellery, now associated with the Petit Palais collection. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1900:** *Broche émaillée aux deux visages*. An enamelled brooch joining two profile heads; published descriptions do not consistently identify the executing jeweller. [Lepdor 2011]
- **c. 1900:** *Projet de broche émaillée*. A gouache design whose status is a jewellery project; without evidence of execution it should not be silently promoted into a finished brooch. [Lepdor 2011]
- *Projet de boucle de ceinture aux deux paons*. A jewellery or metalwork design; no secure date or executed example was established in the consulted catalogue literature. [Lepdor 2011]
- *Projet de pendentif*. A gouache model for jewellery rather than the manufactured pendant itself. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/344024)
- **1890s–1900s:** *Modèles de bijoux pour Henri Vever*. A body of jewellery projects and executed manifestations shown under Vever’s name; the division between Grasset’s drawings, workshop adaptation, and final fabrication must be retained. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1870s–1900s:** *Modèles de ferronnerie*. A dispersed design practice documented in early exhibition criticism but not exhaustively itemized or titled. [Lemoisne 1900]

### Historical compositions and autonomous studies

- *Bataille de Grandson*. A historical composition named in contemporary criticism; the source prints “Granson,” but the normalized place-name is Grandson, and no secure date was supplied. [Lemoisne 1900]
- *Siège de Paris par Henri IV*. A historical composition documented in the early monographic literature. [Lemoisne 1900]
- *François Ier armé chevalier*. A historical composition documented by contemporary critics; its intended reproductive or architectural destination remains uncertain. [Lemoisne 1900]
- **1886:** *Autoportrait*. A self-portrait reported in later catalogues; its precise medium and present location are not consistently stated. [Lepdor 2011]
- **1892:** *Trois femmes et trois loups*. A drawing sometimes dated later in reproductive sources; 1892 is used in parts of the catalogue literature, but the dating is not unanimous. [Lepdor 2011]
- *Portrait de jeune fille dans un médaillon*. An undated lithograph known in a hand-coloured impression; the descriptive museum title does not prove that Grasset assigned it a fixed title. [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artists/13287-eugene-grasset)
- **1890–1903:** *Fleurs de rose de Noël ou ellébore*. A botanical study in the Musée d’Orsay teaching archive; the broad date range reflects archival attribution, not a known year of execution. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1890–1903:** *Feuilles de rose de Noël ou ellébore*. A botanical study belonging to Grasset’s pedagogical and ornamental research. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1890–1903:** *Raies enchevêtrées*. An ornamental animal study; it is closer to a reusable teaching model than to an autonomous exhibition picture. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1890–1903:** *Raies schématisées*. An analytical ornamental study of rays. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1890–1903:** *Raies blanches et bouclées de dos*. An animal and pattern study preserved as teaching material. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1904–1913:** *Têtes de cheval*. An animal study assigned a broad archival date range. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- **1904–1913:** *Un bœuf*. An animal study produced in the context of Grasset’s later teaching and ornamental research. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/ressources/repertoire-artistes-personnalites/eugene-grasset-13977)
- *Le Travail, par l’Industrie et le Commerce, enrichit l’Humanité*. A large allegorical composition frequently named as a principal work; published summaries differ about its exact medium and date, so neither is supplied here. [Lepdor 2011]
