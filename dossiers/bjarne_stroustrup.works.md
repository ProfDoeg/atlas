## Works by Bjarne Stroustrup

### Programming languages, implementations, and continuing projects

- **1979–1983:** *C with Classes*. An experimental programming language—C augmented with classes and other Simula-inspired facilities—and the immediate ancestor of C++; it was a project and working tool rather than a standardized language. Stroustrup wrote its original source-to-source preprocessor. ([history](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf), [FAQ](https://www.stroustrup.com/bs_faq.html))

- **1979–present:** *C++*. A programming language and evolving technical ecosystem designed and originally implemented by Stroustrup; later versions are collective products of users, implementers, and the ISO committee, so present-day C++ cannot be attributed to him alone. The name was adopted in 1983, the first commercial release appeared in 1985, and standardization made it a continuing communal obra. ([C++ page](https://www.stroustrup.com/C++.html), [history](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf))

- **c. 1982–1993:** *Cfront*. The original C++ compiler, translating C++ into C. Stroustrup wrote and maintained the earliest versions, but later releases were substantially collaborative; consequently the whole implementation series resists treatment as his unaided work. ([FAQ](https://www.stroustrup.com/bs_faq.html), [history](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf))

- **1989–present:** *The ISO C++ standardization project*. A long-running collective project in which Stroustrup helped create the ANSI/ISO effort and led its Evolution Working Group from 1990 to 2004. The resulting standards are institutional protocols, not individually authored works by Stroustrup. ([biography](https://www.stroustrup.com/bio.html), [committee papers](https://www.stroustrup.com/WG21.html))

- **2014–present:** *C++ Core Guidelines*. An open, continuously revised protocol for type-safe, resource-safe modern C++, begun by Stroustrup and developed with Herb Sutter and many community contributors. It is deliberately unfinished and cannot properly be classified as a fixed Stroustrup text. ([project](https://github.com/isocpp/CppCoreGuidelines), [historical account](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl20main-p5-p-bfc9cd4--final.pdf))

### Books and book-length texts

- **1979:** *Communication and Control in Distributed Computer Systems*. Stroustrup’s University of Cambridge Ph.D. thesis: a book-length scholarly text on distributed systems and the earliest book-form item in the standard bibliographic record. ([historical bibliography](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf), [DBLP](https://dblp.org/pid/s/BjarneStroustrup.html))

- **1986:** *The C++ Programming Language*. The first book-form definition and exposition of C++; Stroustrup’s bibliography gives 1986, while his biographical résumé also describes the first edition as 1985, reflecting publication/release dating rather than two different obras. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html), [biography](https://www.stroustrup.com/bio.html))

- **1990:** *The Annotated C++ Reference Manual*. A jointly authored reference book by Margaret A. Ellis and Stroustrup, commonly called “the ARM”; it functioned as both description and de facto specification during early standardization. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **1991:** *The C++ Programming Language, Second Edition*. A substantially revised manifestation of the 1986 book, documenting the evolved language rather than constituting a wholly unrelated title. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **1994:** *The Design and Evolution of C++*. A historical and argumentative monograph explaining the language’s design, constraints, and development; it is neither a reference manual nor a standard. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **1997:** *The C++ Programming Language, Third Edition*. A rewritten manifestation aligned with standard-era C++, bibliographically treated as an edition of the continuing book obra. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **2000:** *The C++ Programming Language, Special Edition*. An expanded hardcover manifestation of the third edition, not a separately conceived book despite its distinct subtitle and pagination. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **2008:** *Programming: Principles and Practice Using C++*. A beginner-to-professional programming textbook, conceived as instruction in programming principles rather than merely a C++ reference. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **2013:** *The C++ Programming Language, Fourth Edition*. A major revised manifestation covering C++11 and its standard library. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **2013:** *A Tour of C++*. A compact book for experienced programmers; it is an overview rather than an abridged edition of *The C++ Programming Language*. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **2014:** *Programming: Principles and Practice Using C++, Second Edition*. A revised manifestation of the 2008 textbook. ([publications](https://www.stroustrup.com/papers.html))

- **2018:** *A Tour of C++, Second Edition*. A substantially revised manifestation updated for C++17. Stroustrup’s books page accidentally lists this edition twice; that duplication is not evidence of two works. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **2022:** *A Tour of C++, Third Edition*. A revised manifestation updated for C++20. ([books](https://www.stroustrup.com/books.html))

- **2024:** *Programming: Principles and Practice Using C++, Third Edition*. The third manifestation of the instructional obra, published after the last modification date shown on Stroustrup’s separate books page but recorded in his main bibliography. ([publications](https://www.stroustrup.com/papers.html))

### Research papers, technical essays, and historical accounts

- **1978:** *On Unifying Module Interfaces*. A research paper proposing a common interface for procedures, monitors, and processes. ([paper](https://www.stroustrup.com/unifying_interfaces_1978.pdf))

- **1979:** *An Inter-Module Communication System for a Distributed Computer System*. A conference paper derived from Stroustrup’s distributed-systems research and subsequently reprinted. ([publications](https://www.stroustrup.com/papers.html))

- **1980:** *Classes: An Abstract Data Type Facility for the C Language*. A technical report and later journal paper describing the central mechanism of C with Classes; it is simultaneously research report and language-design document. ([historical bibliography](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf))

- **1980:** *A Set of C Classes for Co-routine Style Programming*. A technical report describing a concrete class library; a later 1987 publication was co-authored with Jonathan Shopiro and should be treated as a revised manifestation. ([historical bibliography](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf))

- **1980:** *Specification of Software Systems for a Range of Execution Environments*. A distributed-computing workshop paper. ([publications](https://www.stroustrup.com/papers.html))

- **1981:** *Long Return: A Technique for Improving the Efficiency of Inter-Module Communication*. A research article on distributed-system implementation. ([publications](https://www.stroustrup.com/papers.html))

- **1981:** *Extensions of the C Language Type Concept*. An internal Bell Labs memorandum and language-design precursor; its restricted institutional form makes it less a public work than an archived design artifact. ([historical bibliography](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf))

- **1982–1983:** *Adding Classes to C: An Exercise in Language Evolution*. First an internal Bell Labs document, then a 1983 journal article; these are manifestations of one language-design paper. ([historical bibliography](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf))

- **1983–1984:** *The C++ Reference Manual*. Written in 1983, issued as Bell Labs report CSTR-108 in January 1984, and revised that November. It is a protocol-like language specification rather than a teaching book. ([historical bibliography](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf))

- **1984:** *Data Abstraction in C*. A Bell Labs Technical Journal paper, written in 1983, on the language mechanisms that became C++. ([historical bibliography](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf))

A repeatedly revised essay appearing in conference and magazine forms; the 1991 European Software Festival version is another manifestation rather than an entirely independent work. ### Standards proposals and design papers as a corpus

- **1990s–2026:** *C++ standards-committee papers*. This is a large, continuing corpus of individually titled proposals, alternatives, wording papers, and discussion documents rather than one stable obra. Stroustrup explicitly calls his own list incomplete and warns that many proposals were rejected, superseded, or mutated before adoption; consequently neither that list nor this atlas can honestly claim bibliographic completeness, and proposal authorship must not be confused with authorship of the resulting ISO standard. ([Stroustrup’s committee bibliography](https://www.stroustrup.com/WG21.html), [WG21 document index](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/docs/papers/))
