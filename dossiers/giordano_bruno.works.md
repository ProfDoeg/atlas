## Works by Giordano Bruno

### Italian drama and philosophical dialogues

- **1582:** *Candelaio*. A prose comedy or anti-comedy, framed by dedicatory and introductory pieces; its interwoven farces make it a theatrical obra rather than a philosophical treatise. [AGB; OC]
- **1584:** *La cena de le Ceneri*. An Italian philosophical dialogue that stages a London supper-debate about Copernican astronomy, the moving Earth, and Bruno’s own cosmology. [AGB; OC]
- **1584:** *De la causa, principio et uno*. A five-dialogue metaphysical work on cause, principle, matter, form, and unity. [AGB; OC]
- **1584:** *De l’infinito, universo et mondi*. A five-dialogue cosmological obra arguing for an infinite universe and innumerable worlds. [AGB; OC]
- **1584:** *Spaccio de la bestia trionfante*. A mythic philosophical dialogue in which the constellations are morally reformed; it functions partly as a manifesto for ethical and religious renovation. [AGB; OC]
- **1585:** *Cabala del cavallo pegaseo, con l’aggiunta dell’Asino cillenico*. A satirical philosophical dialogue followed by a shorter dramatic dialogue; the printed object deliberately mixes paradox, encomium of ignorance, cabala, and comedy. Whether the appended *Asino cillenico* is counted as an independent work or part of the *Cabala* varies by edition. [AGB; OC]
- **1585:** *De gli eroici furori*. A cycle of dialogues, poems, emblems, and commentaries about the intellect’s pursuit of the infinite. Its refusal to separate verse, image-program, philosophy, and staged conversation makes it Bruno’s clearest Gesamtkunstwerk. [AGB; OC]

### Arts of memory and Lullian protocols

- **1582:** *De umbris idearum*. A Latin philosophical and mnemonic work whose rotating lettered wheels and image systems are not merely illustrations but working components of an ars memoriae. [AGB; BOL; OM]
- **1582:** *Ars memoriae*. A protocol for constructing and operating an artificial memory, printed with *De umbris idearum* but textually distinguishable from it. [AGB; BOL; OM]
- **1582:** *Cantus Circaeus ad eam memoriae praxim ordinatus*. A dialogue involving Circe followed by a practical memory art; it combines literary manifestation, magical transformation, and mnemonic protocol. [AGB; BOL; OM]
- **1582:** *De compendiosa architectura et complemento artis Lullii*. A compact reconstruction and claimed completion of Ramon Llull’s combinatorial art, equipped with operative figures. [AGB; OM]
- **1583:** *Ars reminiscendi in phantastico campo exarandi*. A mnemonic protocol printed in the volume commonly identified by its “thirty seals”; its bibliographical boundaries with the following *Explicatio* are not always represented consistently. [AGB; BOL; OM]
- **1583:** *Explicatio triginta sigillorum*. An exposition of thirty mnemonic “seals,” combining diagrams, conceptual devices, and instructions for mental operation. [AGB; BOL; OM]
- **1583:** *Sigillus sigillorum*. A theoretical culmination of the “seals” project, concerned with cognition and universal methods of knowing rather than memory alone. [AGB; BOL; OM]
- **1586–1587:** *Animadversiones circa lampadem lullianam*. Notes or lectures revising a Lullian “lamp”; it survives through manuscript transmission and resists classification as a finished book. [AGB; BOL]
- **1587:** *De lampade combinatoria Lulliana*. A combinatorial protocol that turns Lullian wheels and tables into an instrument for inquiry. [AGB; BOL]
- **1587:** *De progressu et lampade venatoria logicorum*. A “hunting lamp” for discovering terms and arguments, conceived as an operative logical instrument rather than descriptive philosophy. [AGB; BOL]
- **1588:** *De specierum scrutinio et lampade combinatoria Raymundi Lullii*. A revised combinatorial art for examining concepts or “species”; although related to the earlier *Lampas combinatoria*, the bibliography treats it as a distinct publication. [AGB; BOL]
- **1591:** *De imaginum, signorum et idearum compositione*. Bruno’s last published mnemonic work, a protocol for composing images, signs, and ideas across elaborate spatial and combinatorial structures. [AGB; BOL; OM]

### Mathematical, physical, and cosmological works

- **1586:** *Figuratio Aristotelici Physici auditus*. A diagrammatic synopsis of Aristotle’s *Physics*; its figures organize the teaching, so it is simultaneously commentary and visual instrument. [AGB; BOL]
- **1586:** *Mordentius*. The first of four short Latin dialogues concerning Fabrizio Mordente’s proportional compass, initially printed with *De Mordentii circino*. [AGB; BOL; AQ]
- **1586:** *De Mordentii circino*. A mathematical dialogue interpreting Mordente’s compass and drawing philosophical consequences from it. [AGB; BOL; AQ]
- **1586:** *Idiota triumphans*. A polemical dialogue arising from Bruno’s quarrel with Mordente; it was printed with *De somnii interpretatione*. [AGB; AQ]
- **1586:** *De somnii interpretatione*. A satirical dialogue presenting the controversy through the interpretation of a dream. [AGB; AQ]
- **1588:** *Libri physicorum Aristotelis explanati*. Lecture-based explanations of Aristotle’s physical works; the surviving text is a teaching manuscript rather than a publication completed by Bruno. [AGB; BOL]
- **1588:** *Articuli centum et sexaginta adversus huius tempestatis mathematicos atque philosophos*. One hundred and sixty theses against contemporary mathematicians and philosophers, offered as a public intellectual challenge. [AGB; BOL]
- **1591:** *De triplici minimo et mensura*. A Latin philosophical poem with prose apparatus and diagrams on the minimum in metaphysics, physics, and geometry. Its verse, figures, and demonstrations form a composite scientific obra. [AGB; BOL]
- **1591:** *De monade, numero et figura*. A Latin poem with diagrams and commentary concerning monad, number, geometry, and symbolic figures. [AGB; BOL]
- **1591:** *De innumerabilibus, immenso et infigurabili, seu de universo et mundis*. An eight-book Latin cosmological poem expanding Bruno’s project of an infinite universe and innumerable worlds. [AGB; BOL]
- **1591–1592:** *Praelectiones geometricae*. Geometrical teaching materials developing the mathematics of *De triplici minimo*; they remained in manuscript until Giovanni Aquilecchia published them in 1964. [AGB; PGA]
- **1591–1592:** *Ars deformationum*. A companion geometrical protocol explaining transformations or “deformations” of figures; it too survives as lecture material discovered and edited in the twentieth century. [AGB; PGA]

### Disputations, theses, and ceremonial speech-acts

- **1586:** *Centum et viginti articuli de natura et mundo adversus Peripateticos*. One hundred and twenty anti-Aristotelian theses prepared for public disputation in Paris. As propositions meant to provoke and govern an event, they are closer to a manifesto and performance score than to a conventional treatise. [AGB; BOL]
- **1588:** *Camoeracensis acrotismus, seu rationes articulorum physicorum adversus Peripateticos*. A developed defence of the Paris theses, recast for publication after the attempted disputation. [AGB; BOL]
- **1588:** *Oratio valedictoria*. Bruno’s farewell address to the University of Wittenberg; it is a ceremonial speech-act of gratitude, self-positioning, and intellectual allegiance. [AGB; BOL]
- **1589:** *Oratio consolatoria*. A memorial oration for Duke Julius of Brunswick-Lüneburg, intended to console the court and publicly fashion the dead ruler’s image. [AGB; BOL]
- **1587; published 1612:** *Artificium perorandi*. A rhetoric course based largely on Aristotelian teaching, transmitted by students and published posthumously by Johann Heinrich Alsted. The surviving text is therefore a mediated record of Bruno’s instruction, not an author-supervised book. [AGB; BOL]
- **1591; published 1595 and enlarged in 1609:** *Summa terminorum metaphysicorum*. A philosophical lexicon derived from Bruno’s Zurich lectures and edited by his pupil Raphael Egli; its wording and organization are consequently mediated rather than securely author-final. [AGB; BOL; STM]
- **1591; published 1609:** *Praxis descensus seu applicatio entis*. An application of the metaphysical terms preserved with the later *Summa*. It is attributed to Bruno through Egli’s manuscript transmission, but cannot be treated as an independently published, author-supervised work. [BOL; STM]

### Magical, natural-philosophical, and medical manuscripts

- **1587–1588:** *Lampas triginta statuarum*. A vast combinatorial “lamp” organized through thirty conceptual statues. Surviving in manuscript, it merges ontology, theology, mnemonic imagery, and an operative art; it may correspond to the *Lampas cabalistica* announced by Bruno. [AGB; BOL; OMg]
- **1589–1590:** *De magia*. A dictated or working treatise defining kinds of magic and the natural principles on which magical action was thought to depend. It was not published by Bruno and retains the character of workshop material. [AGB; BOL; OMg]
- **1589–1590:** *De magia mathematica*. An unfinished collection of procedures involving words, diagrams, seals, and figures; its compilation-like state resists treatment as a completed treatise. [AGB; BOL; OMg]
- **1589–1590:** *De rerum principiis, elementis et causis*. A manuscript natural-philosophical and magical treatise on principles, elements, causes, spirits, and correspondences. [AGB; BOL; OMg]
- **1589–1591:** *Medicina Lulliana*. A manuscript project applying Lullian combination to medicine; it is a medical-intellectual protocol, not evidence of a completed clinical practice. [AGB; BOL; OMg]
- **circa 1590–1591:** *Theses de magia*. A compact series of theses related to *De magia* but transmitted as a distinct text. Its exact relation to the longer treatise—summary, alternate redaction, or teaching outline—remains editorially problematic. [AGB; BOL; OMg]
- **circa 1590–1591:** *De vinculis in genere*. A theory and protocol of “bonds” operating through imagination, desire, persuasion, and relation. The surviving text belongs to an incompletely realized multi-part project and should not be mistaken for a finished authorial publication. [AGB; BOL; OMg]

### Lost, reported, or incompletely realized projects

- **1568–1571?:** *Arca di Noè*. A youthful work known only through later testimony and now lost; neither its date nor its completed status is secure. [CHR]
- **1576?:** *Gli pensier gai*. A reported poem or poetic project, unpublished and lost. Its title and existence depend on biographical testimony rather than a surviving object. [CHR]
- **1576?:** *Tronco d’acqua viva*. Another reported, unpublished poetic composition; no text survives, so it resists verification as a completed work. [CHR]
- **1576–1581:** Lectures on Sacrobosco’s sphere. A teaching activity, possibly accompanied by a commentary or *De sphaera* text; no securely identifiable manuscript survives, and it should not automatically be converted into a lost “book.” [CHR; IEP]
- **1576–1581:** Lectures on Aristotle’s *De anima*. A course delivered at Toulouse; evidence establishes the teaching project more securely than any finished written work. [CHR]
- **1577?:** *De’ segni de’ tempi*. A small Italian work which Bruno said he had printed in Venice to raise money, possibly on astronomical or meteorological signs; no copy is known, and even its subject remains inferential. [CHR; IEP]
- **1579:** *Censure contro il De la Faye*. Bruno’s objections to Antoine de La Faye at Geneva, apparently printed and then suppressed or lost. The surviving disciplinary record confirms the controversy more clearly than the precise form of the text. [CHR]
- **1579–1581?:** *Clavis magna*. A lost or unrealized “Great Key” to the Lullian and mnemonic art, repeatedly associated with Bruno’s later combinatorial works. Because no complete witness survives, scholars disagree over whether it was one finished book, an evolving project, or an umbrella title. [CHR; OM]
- **1581–1582?:** *De’ predicamenti di Dio*. A lost work or lecture project on divine predicates. Its date, form, and relationship to the later *Summa terminorum metaphysicorum* remain uncertain. [CHR; STM]
- **by 1582:** *Purgatorio de l’Inferno*. A work mentioned by Bruno but otherwise lost; it may have been completed, projected, or merely advertised, and nothing beyond the title securely defines it. [CHR]
- **1586:** *Arbor philosophorum*. A lost “tree of philosophers,” probably a classificatory or combinatorial project; its medium and degree of completion cannot now be established. [CHR]
- **1587:** Lectures on Aristotle’s *Organon*. A Wittenberg teaching project now lost. The evidence supports lectures, but not necessarily a separately authored book. [CHR]
- **1589–1590:** *Delle sette arti liberali*. A lost Italian project on the seven liberal arts; whether it was completed is unknown. [CHR]
- **1589–1590:** *Delle sette arti inventive*. A lost companion project on seven arts of invention; its relation to Bruno’s Lullian “lamps” is plausible but not textually recoverable. [CHR]
- **by 1591:** *De rerum imaginibus*. A lost work on images cited in the late bibliography. It may have belonged to the same broad project as *De imaginum, signorum et idearum compositione*, but identity between them cannot be assumed. [CHR]
- **by 1591:** *Templum Mnemosynes*. A lost “Temple of Mnemosyne,” apparently a mnemonic architecture or project. With only its title recoverable, it is safer to call it a reported project than a completed work. [CHR]
- **by 1591:** *De multiplici mundi vita*. A lost work or projected treatise on the manifold life of the world; completion and date are uncertain. [CHR]
- **by 1591:** *De naturae gestibus*. A lost or projected work on the operations or “gestures” of nature. No surviving text permits a firmer classification. [CHR]
- **by 1591:** *De principiis veri*. A lost project on the principles of truth; its title is recorded, but its boundaries and completion are unknown. [CHR]
- **by 1591:** *De astrologia*. A lost astrological work or project. Its inclusion in retrospective lists does not establish that Bruno completed it. [CHR]
- **before May 1592?:** *De sigillis Hermetis et Ptolomaei et aliorum*. A lost work on seals attributed to Bruno in later bibliographical discussion; the attribution itself is uncertain, so it cannot be entered without qualification as an accepted obra. [PGA; CHR]
