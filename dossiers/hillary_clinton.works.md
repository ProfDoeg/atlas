## Works by Hillary Diane Rodham Clinton

### Academic and legal texts

- **1969:** *“There Is Only the Fight…”: An Analysis of the Alinsky Model*. Undergraduate honors thesis on Saul Alinsky, deposited at Wellesley College; it is an academic obra rather than a published book. Its restricted availability during Bill Clinton’s presidency generated controversy, but Wellesley identifies Clinton as its author. [Bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_Hillary_Clinton#Scholarly_articles_and_chapters)
- **1973:** *Children Under the Law*. Legal article in the *Harvard Educational Review* 43, no. 4, pp. 487–514; reprinted in *The Rights of Children* in 1974. [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.17763/haer.43.4.e14676283875773k)
- **1977:** *Children’s Policies: Abandonment and Neglect*. Legal-policy article in the *Yale Law Journal* 86, no. 7, pp. 1522–1531. [JSTOR record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/795794)
- **1979:** *Children’s Rights: A Legal Perspective*. Chapter in Patricia A. Vardin and Ilene N. Brody’s edited collection *Children’s Rights: Contemporary Perspectives*, pp. 21–36. [Bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_Hillary_Clinton#Scholarly_articles_and_chapters)
- **2004:** *Remembering Burke Marshall*. Memorial essay in the *Yale Law Journal* 113, no. 4, pp. 787–790. [Yale Law School repository](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/handle/20.500.13051/9573)
- **2010:** *Leading Through Civilian Power: Redefining American Diplomacy and Development*. Foreign-policy essay published under Clinton’s name while she was secretary of state; its institutional setting makes it both an argument and an official-policy manifestation. [Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/articles/north-america/2010-11-01/leading-through-civilian-power)
- **2011:** *America’s Pacific Century*. Strategic-policy essay in *Foreign Policy*; like other cabinet-level texts, it represents both Clinton’s position and the collective work of a government writing and policy staff. [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2011/10/11/americas-pacific-century/)
- **2020:** *A National Security Reckoning: How Washington Should Think About Power*. Foreign-policy essay proposing a revised conception of American power. [Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/articles/united-states/2020-10-09/hillary-clinton-national-security-reckoning)

### Books and book-length obras

- **1996:** *It Takes a Village: And Other Lessons Children Teach Us*. Policy book about children, families, and communal responsibility. Its individual authorship is disputed: Clinton claimed responsibility for the final text, while writer-editor Barbara Feinman helped organize and draft it but received no printed credit; the book should therefore be understood as a collaborative obra published under Clinton’s name. [White House account](https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1996/01/1996-01-11-background-information-on-first-ladys-book.html) [contemporary discussion](https://time.com/archive/6728437/the-ghost-and-mrs-clinton/)
- **1998:** *Dear Socks, Dear Buddy: Kids’ Letters to the First Pets*. Illustrated selection of children’s letters to the Clinton White House, connected by Clinton’s commentary; it is partly authored book and partly curated archive. [Official bibliography](https://www.hillaryclinton.com/about/)
- **2000:** *An Invitation to the White House: At Home with History*. Illustrated account of ceremonial, domestic, artistic, and preservation work in the White House; a book whose subject is itself a collaborative institutional Gesamtkunstwerk. [Official bibliography](https://www.hillaryclinton.com/about/)
- **2003:** *Living History*. Political autobiography published under Clinton’s name. The narrated “I” was produced with Lissa Muscatine, Maryanne Vollers, Ruby Shamir, editors, and researchers, so scholarship treats it as an explicitly collective autobiographical construction rather than unassisted writing. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/alh/article/24/3/523/103133)
- **2006:** *It Takes a Village*, tenth-anniversary edition. A revised manifestation of the 1996 book with a new introduction, not an entirely new obra. [Bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_Hillary_Clinton#Books)
- **2014:** *Hard Choices*. Memoir of Clinton’s years as secretary of state, produced with a writing team including Dan Schwerin, Ethan Gelber, and Ted Widmer; attribution to Clinton coexists with acknowledged collaborative composition. [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/lifestyle/style/who-wrote-that-political-memoir-no-who-actually-wrote-it/2014/06/09/8e89ccae-f00a-11e3-9ebc-2ee6f81ed217_story.html)
- **2016:** *Stronger Together: A Blueprint for America’s Future*. Campaign manifesto credited jointly to Hillary Rodham Clinton and Tim Kaine. It presents the Clinton–Kaine campaign’s collective platform, so it acts politically as much as it describes policy and resists attribution to either named candidate alone. [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Stronger-Together/Hillary-Rodham-Clinton/9781501161735)
- **2017:** *It Takes a Village*. Picture-book re-creation of the earlier work, illustrated by Marla Frazee; its altered audience, text, and visual form make it more than a reprint but not wholly independent of the 1996 obra. [Bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_Hillary_Clinton#Books)
- **2017:** *What Happened*. Campaign memoir and political argument about the 2016 presidential election. It is published as Clinton’s personal account, while acknowledgments identify the advisers and writers involved in shaping it. [Simon & Schuster author catalogue](https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/Hillary-Rodham-Clinton/1487327)
- **2019:** *The Book of Gutsy Women: Favorite Stories of Courage and Resilience*. Jointly authored with Chelsea Clinton; a collection of biographical portraits, personal reflections, and exemplary narratives. [Official bibliography](https://www.hillaryclinton.com/about/)
- **2020:** *Grandma’s Gardens*. Children’s picture book jointly written with Chelsea Clinton and illustrated by Carme Lemniscates; the visual object is consequently a three-person collaboration. [Official bibliography](https://www.hillaryclinton.com/about/)
- **2021:** *State of Terror*. Political thriller jointly written with novelist Louise Penny. It is Clinton’s first published novel but emphatically a co-created fiction, combining Penny’s novelistic practice with Clinton’s experience of government. [Simon & Schuster author catalogue](https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/Hillary-Rodham-Clinton/1487327)
- **2024:** *Something Lost, Something Gained: Reflections on Life, Love, and Liberty*. Collection of autobiographical and political essays published under Clinton’s name. [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/Hillary-Rodham-Clinton/1487327)

### Manifestos, speeches, and performed texts

- **1969:** *Wellesley College commencement address*. An extemporaneously revised student speech responding to Senator Edward Brooke; the performed intervention, rather than any later transcript, is the primary obra. [LIFE/TIME account](https://time.com/3502935/life-with-hillary-portraits-of-a-wellesley-grad-1969/)
- **1995:** *Remarks to the United Nations Fourth World Conference on Women*. The Beijing address commonly known as “Women’s Rights Are Human Rights” is a speech-manifesto: an official, collectively prepared text intended to alter political language and action. A version appeared in *Women’s Studies Quarterly* in 1996. [UN Women](https://www.unwomen.org/en/digital-library/videos/2015/12/hillary-clinton-remarks-at-the-un-fourth-world-conference-on-women)
- **2011:** *Remarks in Recognition of International Human Rights Day*. Delivered in Geneva and commonly identified by its declaration that gay rights are human rights; it is an official diplomatic manifesto whose named speaker and institutional authorship cannot be cleanly separated. [U.S. State Department archive](https://2009-2017.state.gov/secretary/20092013clinton/rm/2011/12/178368.htm)
- **1992–present:** *Public speeches, testimony, debate performances, and official remarks*. This is a very large, dispersed rhetorical corpus rather than one fixed work: individual performances were routinely made with speechwriters, policy staff, campaign organizations, and government departments. No complete scholarly collected-works edition exists, so claiming an exhaustive itemization would manufacture completeness; the corpus is catalogued and studied as a body of adaptive public rhetoric. [Michigan State University Press study](https://msupress.org/9781611864673/hillary-clintons-career-in-speeches/)

### Newspaper and magazine texts

- **1995–2000:** *Talking It Over*. Syndicated newspaper column published under Clinton’s name through Creators Syndicate. It is a serial body of work rather than a single text, and the surviving collection—not the partial lists in general bibliographies—is the appropriate unit; staff assistance means that individual authorship should not be assumed to equal solitary composition. [Bibliographic record and collection link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography_of_Hillary_Clinton#Columns_by_Clinton)

### Policy projects and collective institutional works

- **1993–1994:** *Health Security Act project*. Clinton chaired the presidential task force that developed the proposed national health-care plan. The project was unfinished as legislation—it never became law—and the bill, working papers, and political campaign were collective governmental products rather than a single text authored by Clinton.
- **1997:** *Vital Voices Democracy Initiative*. Government initiative launched by Clinton and Secretary of State Madeleine Albright to support women’s political and economic participation; its continuation as Vital Voices Global Partnership makes it an institution and ongoing project, not a bounded personal work.
- **2016:** *The 2016 Clinton–Kaine policy platform*. A large collective body of campaign protocols and manifestos supervised and publicly adopted by Clinton but researched and written by campaign policy teams. *Stronger Together* is its principal book manifestation, not proof of solitary authorship.

### Audio, television, film, and stage works

- **2020–present:** *You and Me Both with Hillary Clinton*. Interview podcast hosted by Clinton and made with iHeartMedia. It is a serial conversational work whose guests, producers, and editors share responsibility for each episode. [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/you-and-me-both-with-hillary-clinton/id1531768983)
- **2020:** *HiddenLight Productions*. Production company founded by Hillary Clinton, Chelsea Clinton, and Sam Branson. It is an ongoing creative project and institutional obra, not a single film attributable to Clinton. [HiddenLight](https://www.hiddenlight.com/)
- **2022:** *Gutsy*. Eight-part television documentary series hosted and executive-produced by Hillary and Chelsea Clinton, adapted from *The Book of Gutsy Women*. Its interviews, travel, performance, and documentary construction make it a multimedia collaborative manifestation rather than another edition of the book. [Official biography](https://www.hillaryclinton.com/about/)
- **2022:** *In Her Hands*. Documentary about Afghan mayor Zarifa Ghafari, executive-produced by Hillary and Chelsea Clinton through HiddenLight. Clinton’s producer credit denotes enabling and shaping the production, not authorship of its images or direction. [HiddenLight work catalogue](https://www.hiddenlight.com/our-work/)
- **2024:** *Suffs*. Broadway production of Shaina Taub’s musical, produced by a team including Hillary Rodham Clinton and Malala Yousafzai. Clinton helped bring this manifestation to Broadway but did not write its book, music, or lyrics; calling the musical itself “by Clinton” would erase Taub’s authorship. [Internet Broadway Database](https://www.ibdb.com/broadway-production/suffs-538249) [Shubert Organization](https://shubert.nyc/press/suffs-to-open-on-broadway-this-spring/)

### Things that resist treatment as works

- **1970s–present:** *Letters, memoranda, legal files, government records, emails, and social-media posts*. These are made texts and archival traces, but most were produced as correspondence, administration, or communication rather than offered as obras. Their survival in archives or publication through disclosure does not retroactively make each item a literary work, and no complete scholarly bibliography treats them as such.
- **1992–2016:** *Campaigns for public office*. Each campaign combined speeches, visual design, slogans, advertising, platforms, software, rallies, and organized labor. They may be read as collaborative political Gesamtkunstwerke, but not as works authored by Clinton alone.
- **1993–2013:** *Official policies and diplomatic initiatives*. Clinton helped make policies as first lady, senator, and secretary of state, but statutes, treaties, departmental reviews, and programs belong to collective institutions. Individual attribution generally cannot be established merely from her sponsorship or public advocacy.
