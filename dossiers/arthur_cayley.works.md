## Works by Arthur Cayley

### Mathematical obra

- **1841–1895:** *Mathematical papers, nos. 1–966*. This is Cayley’s principal obra: 966 separately titled papers, notes, memoirs, translations, corrections, encyclopedia articles, addresses, and short communications, canonically enumerated in *The Collected Mathematical Papers of Arthur Cayley*, vols. I–XIII. The corpus is not perfectly synonymous with “everything Cayley wrote”: some controversial pieces and duplicate translations survive there only as titles, while editorial notes were added during republication; Cayley supervised vols. I–VII and part of VIII, after which A. R. Forsyth completed the project. The individual titles and original publication details are recorded in the supplementary title-list and index rather than reproduced here as 966 arbitrarily reclassified “books.” [Cayley’s preface](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511703676.001); [supplementary index](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ); [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/the-collected-mathematical-papers/6BF83A792BB96E0B3809C3766BEF9C79); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

### Treatise

- **1876; revised edition 1895:** *An Elementary Treatise on Elliptic Functions*. A book-length mathematical treatise and Cayley’s only substantial authored mathematics book, founded on Legendre and Jacobi but containing new material. The second edition was in press when Cayley died, so its final manifestation is partly posthumous; Forsyth placed the treatise after the 966 papers as item 967 of the collected edition. [Collected Papers, no. 967](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511703799.082); [MacTutor’s account of the treatise](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Extras/Cayley_elliptic/).

### Protocol

- **1894:** *The Principles of Book-Keeping by Double Entry*. A twenty-page, sixpenny instructional booklet: properly a protocol or practical primer, not a research paper and not part of Cayley’s mathematical treatise. Its existence complicates the recurrent statement that Cayley wrote “only one book,” which means one substantial mathematical book. [Digitized first edition](https://books.google.com/books?id=8fUuAQAAIAAJ); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

### Contribution to another author’s obra

- **1890:** *Chapter VI* in Peter Guthrie Tait’s *An Elementary Treatise on Quaternions*, third edition. A separately authored contribution embedded in Tait’s book rather than a freestanding Cayley book; the chapter concerns the application of quaternions to geometry. Its status resists a simple “work” label because the containing obra is Tait’s, while this component is Cayley’s. [MacTutor biography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Cayley/).

- **1863; enlarged editions 1879:** Contributions to George Salmon’s *A Treatise on the Higher Plane Curves*. Cayley’s assistance was substantial enough for the standard biographical bibliography to say that his part “was not negligible,” especially in the later editions, but the exact textual boundary between Salmon’s and Cayley’s work is not fixed as an independently titled object; it should therefore be recorded as a collaborative contribution, not silently converted into a Cayley-authored book. [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

### Reference-work manifestations

- *Curve*. A partly authored *Encyclopædia Britannica* article. It belongs to the numbered mathematical corpus, but Cayley’s contribution is only part of the composite article, so the article as a whole resists attribution to him alone. [Supplementary title-list and index](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

- *Determinant*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* reference article and a compact expository manifestation of Cayley’s determinant theory, included among the writings identified by the collected-work index. [Supplementary title-list and index](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

- *Equation*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* reference article, distinct in function from Cayley’s research memoirs even though it is incorporated into the canonical corpus. [Supplementary title-list and index](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

- *Gauss*. A biographical *Encyclopædia Britannica* article rather than a mathematical research paper. [Supplementary title-list and index](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

- *Monge*. A biographical *Encyclopædia Britannica* article rather than a mathematical research paper. [Supplementary title-list and index](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

- *Numbers, Partition of*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* reference article on partitions, related to but not identical with Cayley’s separately published research papers on the subject. [Supplementary title-list and index](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

- *Surface*. A partly authored *Encyclopædia Britannica* article. As with *Curve*, Cayley made only part of the composite text, so undivided authorship should not be assigned to him. [Supplementary title-list and index](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ); [*Dictionary of Scientific Biography* bibliography](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Cayley.pdf).

### Editorial project and collected manifestation

- **1889–1898:** *The Collected Mathematical Papers of Arthur Cayley*. A fourteen-volume editorial project and material manifestation of Cayley’s oeuvre: thirteen volumes containing consecutively numbered items 1–967, followed by a supplementary volume of titles, classifications, and indexes. Cayley undertook and supervised the project through seven volumes and part of the eighth; it remained unfinished at his death and was completed by A. R. Forsyth, so the whole set cannot be treated as Cayley’s unaided creation. [Cayley’s preface](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511703676.001); [contemporary account in *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/057217a0); [supplementary volume](https://books.google.com/books?id=1ZBQAAAAYAAJ).
