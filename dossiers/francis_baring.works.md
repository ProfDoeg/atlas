## Works by Sir Francis Baring

### Institutional obra

- **1762–1804:** *John & Francis Baring & Co.* A merchant house established jointly by Francis, John, and Charles Baring on 25 December 1762; Francis remained a partner until 1804 and was the principal force in its early development. It is an institutional obra rather than a bounded personal work, and its collaborative, continuing life—later as Baring Brothers—resists exclusive attribution to him. ([Baring Archive timeline](https://baringarchive.org.uk/history/timeline/); [Baring Archive partnership list](https://baringarchive.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/the_baring_archive_hc1.pdf))

### Signed economic and political pamphlets

- **1786:** *The Principle of the Commutation-Act Established by Facts*. A policy pamphlet defending the Commutation Act and its reduction of tea duties; the contemporary imprint names Francis Baring, and the work appears in the standard biographical checklist. ([Google Books bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Principle_of_the_Commutation_Act_Est.html?id=SUVpAAAAcAAJ); [Dictionary of National Biography](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography%2C_1885-1900/Baring,_Francis_%281740-1810%29))

- **1797:** *Observations on the Establishment of the Bank of England, and on the Paper Circulation of the Country*. An argumentative monetary-policy pamphlet, not a protocol: it analyzes the Bank, paper currency, and limits on note issue. It was followed by a separate sequel later that year and should not be conflated with it or with editions binding the two together. ([Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/nhbyt7db); [Cambridge, *The Age of Paper* bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/age-of-paper/bibliography/5785815DE744D05B970E4609B0BC8D0A))

- **1797:** *Further Observations on the Establishment of the Bank of England, and on the Paper Circulation of the Country*. A distinct sequel responding to the continuing controversy over specie payments and paper circulation, rather than merely a revised edition of the preceding pamphlet. ([Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/hf9623gr); [Cambridge, *The Age of Paper* bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/age-of-paper/bibliography/5785815DE744D05B970E4609B0BC8D0A))

- **1801:** *Observations on the Publication of Walter Boyd, Esq., M.P.* A signed polemical pamphlet answering Boyd’s argument about the suspension of cash payments, prices, and paper currency. Some surviving volumes append Boyd’s *Letter*, but that appended text is Boyd’s work, not Baring’s. ([Cambridge, *The Age of Paper* bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/age-of-paper/bibliography/5785815DE744D05B970E4609B0BC8D0A); [CiNii bibliographic description](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA13963438))

### Anonymous or disputed attributions

- **1788:** *A Hasty Sketch of the Conduct of the Commissioners for the Affairs of India: With a Concise State of the Case Relative to the Four Regiments; and of the Pretended Declaratory Bill Presented to the Hon. the House of Commons*. An anonymous political intervention concerning East India Company government. Goldsmiths’-Kress cataloguing attributes it to Baring, but the title page does not name him and the older *Dictionary of National Biography* checklist omits it; it therefore remains an attributed, not securely signed, obra. ([Goldsmiths’-Kress catalogue record reproduced by Gale](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/1005000R.pdf); [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/qdkv5d9n); [Dictionary of National Biography](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography%2C_1885-1900/Baring,_Francis_%281740-1810%29))

- **1801:** *A Twelve-Penny Answer to a Three Shillings and Six-Penny Pamphlet, Intituled A Letter on the Influence of the Stoppage of Issues in Specie at the Bank of England, on the Prices of Provisions, and Other Commodities*. An anonymous inexpensive polemic against Walter Boyd, attributed to Baring in the Goldsmiths’ Library catalogue. Later scholarship questions that attribution: an advertisement indicates that the author had not seen Baring’s signed *Observations* before writing, so agreement with Baring’s position is not proof that he made the text. ([Goldsmiths’-Kress catalogue record reproduced by Gale](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/1005000R.pdf); [Arie Arnon, *The Early Round of the Bullionist Debate, 1800–1802*](https://in.bgu.ac.il/en/humsos/Econ/Workingpapers/0714.pdf))

- **1801:** *A Second Twelve-Penny Answer to a New (and Five Shillings) Edition of a Three Shillings and Six-Penny Pamphlet, Intituled “A Letter on the Influence of the Stoppage of Issues in Specie at the Bank of England, on the Prices of Provisions and Other Commodities; with Additional Notes and a Preface”*. An anonymous continuation of the preceding Boyd controversy, likewise catalogued under Baring; one surviving copy is reportedly inscribed “From the Author,” but that inscription does not name the author. Modern scholarship explicitly marks the attribution uncertain, so this text resists admission to the secure oeuvre. ([Goldsmiths’-Kress catalogue record reproduced by Gale](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/1005000R.pdf); [Musashi University collection description](https://www.musashi.ac.jp/library/shoukai/collection/england/bd0rj20000000ab2-att/bd0rj20000000v76.pdf); [Arie Arnon, *The Early Round of the Bullionist Debate, 1800–1802*](https://in.bgu.ac.il/en/humsos/Econ/Workingpapers/0714.pdf))
