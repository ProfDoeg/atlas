## Works by Bernardo O'Higgins Riquelme

### Manuscripts, correspondence, and records

- **1798–1842:** *Correspondence*. A body of diplomatic, military, political, agricultural, and personal letters rather than a single work; the surviving corpus is incomplete, and some letters issued under O’Higgins’s name were drafted by secretaries. Ernesto de la Cruz’s two-volume *Epistolario de don Bernardo O’Higgins* and the documentary *Archivo de don Bernardo O’Higgins* are the standard collected sources, not works authored by their editors. ([Bibliografía O’Higginiana](https://revistaderechopublico.uchile.cl/index.php/RDPU/article/download/36374/38013/125131); [Archivo de don Bernardo O’Higgins](https://biblioteca.anh.org.ar/cgi-bin/koha/opac-detail.pl?biblionumber=20721))

- **3–31 July 1816:** *Diario llevado en Mendoza mientras San Martín se hallaba en Córdoba*. A manuscript diary or working record, not a retrospective memoir; Memoria Chilena catalogues it as a journal kept by O’Higgins during San Martín’s absence. ([Memoria Chilena](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-propertyvalue-147721.html))

- **1817–1823:** *Official correspondence, decrees, orders, military reports, and administrative memoranda of the Supreme Directorate*. This is a collective governmental documentary production dispersed through the *Archivo de don Bernardo O’Higgins*: O’Higgins authorized or signed much of it, but signature and institutional responsibility do not establish that he personally composed every text. ([Index to the 35-volume Archivo](https://es.scribd.com/doc/7253774/Indice-Del-Archivo-de-Don-Bernardo-O-Higgins); [thematic index](https://es.scribd.com/document/884112538/Apendice-Edit-UCatolica-1965-Compressed))

- **8 October 1842:** *Testamento*. A notarized legal protocol made before Jerónimo Villafuerte, distinct from the subsequently named “political testament”; it disposed of O’Higgins’s property and entrusted private obligations to his family. The document is a collaborative juridical instrument rather than an autonomously authored literary text. ([Ricardo Donoso study and chronology](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371109813_Bernardo_O%27Higgins_y_la_conformacion_del_orden_republicano_1810-1830))

- **1842, traditionally dated 17 October:** *Testamento político*. A posthumously circulated list of national projects—ports, roads, railways, settlement near Talcahuano, colonization, education, naval infrastructure, and southern territorial policy—attributed to O’Higgins near the end of his life. Its familiar title is editorial rather than demonstrably his own, and it should not be confused with the notarized testament of 8 October. ([discussion of its date and contents](https://www.cooperativa.cl/noticias/sociedad/historia/chile/a-198-anos-del-acta-de-independencia-la-discusion-continua-talca-o/2016-02-11/173310.html))

### Proclamations and manifestos

- **17 February 1817:** *Proclama de O’Higgins al asumir el mando supremo del Estado*. A manifesto in the operative sense: it announced his acceptance of supreme authority and called for national unity. It bears the signatures of O’Higgins and secretary Miguel Zañartu, so its precise compositional authorship is institutional rather than securely individual. ([Ley Chile](https://www.leychile.cl/navegar?idNorma=1122444))

- **1 April 1817:** *Letters announcing the new Chilean government to the Prince Regent of Britain, the President of the United States, the Emperor of Russia, and other foreign rulers*. A coordinated diplomatic manifestation rather than independent literary works; surviving copies are catalogued among O’Higgins’s papers, but secretarial drafting is probable. ([Memoria Chilena bibliography](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-562.html))

- **1 January–12 February 1818:** *Proclamación de la Independencia de Chile*. A state act signed and promulgated by O’Higgins, not a text securely written by him: the surviving official wording was repeatedly revised and is attributed principally to Miguel Zañartu, while Bernardo de Vera y Pintado wrote the accompanying manifesto. The dates and places of the declaration, signature, and public proclamation remain subjects of historical dispute. ([Memoria Chilena](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-96486.html); [contemporary printed text](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/archivos2/pdfs/MC0059058.pdf))

- **1818:** *Manifiesto que hace a las naciones el Director Supremo de Chile de los motivos que justifican su revolución y la declaración de su Independencia*. A diplomatic manifesto issued in O’Higgins’s governmental voice, but composed by Bernardo de Vera y Pintado; it therefore resists inclusion as O’Higgins’s personally authored work and belongs here as a commissioned state act. ([Memoria Chilena, “Documentos oficiales”](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/609/w3-article-96058.html); [account of authorship](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-96486.html))

- **19 April 1819:** *Proclama a los soldados españoles destinados a la expedición contra Buenos Aires*. A printed act of political persuasion addressed to enemy troops and issued under O’Higgins’s name; the documentary record does not establish whether its wording is entirely his or secretarial. ([Memoria Chilena bibliography](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-562.html))

- **19 September 1819:** *Proclama a los ciudadanos instándolos a que defiendan sus libertades*. A mobilizing manifesto associated with O’Higgins’s government and preserved in the collected documentary record. Its administrative transmission is secure, but individual compositional authorship is not. ([Memoria Chilena document](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/archivos2/pdfs/MC0039066.pdf))

- **31 August 1820:** *Manifiesto del Capitán General de Ejército don Bernardo O’Higgins a los pueblos que dirige*. A printed political self-account issued at the end of the revolution’s tenth year; it is both governmental manifesto and public defense of his administration. ([Memoria Chilena bibliography](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-562.html))

- **1820:** *Proclama al pueblo peruano*. A multilingual manifesto prepared for distribution by the Liberating Expedition, including a Quechua version. O’Higgins commissioned and sent it, but the surviving evidence indicates that Juan Egaña probably composed the text; it is therefore his political project, not securely his writing. ([Archivo Nacional de Chile](https://www.archivonacional.gob.cl/celebrando-la-independencia-de-peru))

- **After February 1820:** *El Supremo Director de Chile a los hijos de Valdivia*. A proclamation of incorporation addressed to Valdivians after the patriot capture of the city; its surviving print has no fully fixed publication date. It is an official manifestation under O’Higgins’s authority rather than demonstrably an autograph composition. ([Memoria Chilena bibliography](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-562.html))

- **28 January 1823:** *Abdicación del Director Supremo*. An oral and subsequently textualized political act by which O’Higgins surrendered power to avert civil conflict. The familiar “farewell” exists through accounts and documentary transmission, so it should not be treated uncritically as a stable, author-prepared literary text. ([Memoria Chilena](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-562.html))

- **1826:** *El Capitán General de Ejército don Bernardo O’Higgins a sus conciudadanos en Chile*. A proclamation from exile defending his intentions and possible return; Congress received a copy on 4 August 1826. It acts as political intervention rather than detached autobiography. ([Sessions of the Chilean Congress](https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Sesiones_de_los_Cuerpos_Lejislativos_de_la_Rep%C3%BAblica_de_Chile/1826/Sesi%C3%B3n_del_Congreso_Nacional%2C_en_4_de_agosto_de_1826_%281%29))

### Protocols and constitutional acts

- **27 February 1817:** *Reglamento provisorio de sueldos de oficiales y prest de la tropa del Ejército de Chile*. A military pay protocol signed by O’Higgins and secretary José Ignacio Zenteno; its function is instructional and administrative, and the signature does not prove sole verbal authorship. ([*Impresos chilenos, 1776–1818*, item 204](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/archivos2/pdfs/MC0042547.pdf))

- **22 March 1817:** *Prohibición de usar escudos de armas e insignias de nobleza*. A decree that operated simultaneously as protocol and republican manifesto, ordering the removal of public signs of hereditary nobility. It was issued by O’Higgins and countersigned by Miguel Zañartu. ([Ley Chile](https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?f=1817-03-22&i=1063090))

- **1818:** *Proyecto de Constitución Provisoria para el Estado de Chile*. A constitutional project presented, approved, and promulgated under O’Higgins, but not properly a sole-authored work: a commission prepared the text, and O’Higgins’s role was executive, political, and signatory. ([BCN biographical record](https://www.bcn.cl/historiapolitica/resenas_parlamentarias/wiki/Bernardo%20O%27Higgins%20Riquelme))

- **1822:** *Convocatoria con que se reunió la Honorable Convención*. An executive convocation printed with the 1822 Constitution and explicitly attributed to O’Higgins; it is an institutional speech-act that brought the convention into being. ([Memoria Chilena catalogue](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-8977.html))

- **1822:** *Mensaje del Poder Ejecutivo* and *Otro mensaje del Poder Ejecutivo*. Two executive addresses printed with the 1822 Constitution and attributed to O’Higgins. They are governmental messages, probably produced with secretarial assistance, rather than chapters of the constitution itself. ([Memoria Chilena catalogue](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-8977.html))

- **23–30 October 1822:** *Constitución Política del Estado de Chile*. A constitutional manifestation sanctioned and promulgated by O’Higgins, not a text he can be credited with writing alone; the convention and governmental advisers participated in its making. Contemporary opposition alleged that minister José Antonio Rodríguez Aldea shaped it to prolong O’Higgins’s authority. ([Memoria Chilena](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-8977.html); [BCN biographical record](https://www.bcn.cl/historiapolitica/resenas_parlamentarias/wiki/Bernardo%20O%27Higgins%20Riquelme))

### Drawings and spatial projects

- **1818:** *Croquis de La Cañada*. A hand-drawn plan for converting Santiago’s former watercourse and boundary into a planted public promenade. Unlike many “works of O’Higgins’s government,” this surviving plan is catalogued directly under O’Higgins’s authorship. ([Memoria Chilena and Archivo Nacional, MAP 368](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-86758.html))

- **1818 onward:** *Paseo de la Cañada / Alameda de las Delicias*. An urban project derived from O’Higgins’s croquis and executed through governmental labor and later alterations. It is an obra in the civic and spatial sense, but neither its construction nor its subsequent form was his individual handiwork. ([catalogued croquis](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-86758.html))

- **1818:** *Templo Votivo de Maipú*. A vowed commemorative church-project arising from the independence campaign. O’Higgins initiated the project, but the building now identified with it was completed in a radically later architectural form; the intention and institutional foundation, rather than the present structure, are his obra. ([BCN history of the law](https://www.bcn.cl/historiadelaley/historia-de-la-ley/vista-expandida/4298/))

### Institutions, organizations, and collective projects

- **1817:** *Academia Militar*. A state institution created by O’Higgins’s government to train officers, later understood as the origin of Chile’s Military School. It is an institutional obra, not a personally manufactured object. ([BCN biographical record](https://www.bcn.cl/historiapolitica/resenas_parlamentarias/wiki/Bernardo%20O%27Higgins%20Riquelme))

- **1817:** *Legión de Mérito de Chile*. A republican order intended to replace inherited rank with publicly recognized service. Its making was juridical and ceremonial and depended on the state apparatus, although O’Higgins was its chief and principal sponsor. ([Memoria Chilena](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-562.html))

- **1817:** *Transitional flag of Chile*. O’Higgins adopted the blue-white-red transitional flag as a state emblem on 28 May. Adoption is not design authorship; claims that he personally “created the flag” collapse executive authorization, anonymous or disputed design, manufacture, and later standardization into a single act. ([BCN historical discussion](https://www.bcn.cl/laborparlamentaria/participacion?idParticipacion=742336))

- **1817:** *Present Chilean national flag*. A state emblem established during O’Higgins’s government, but its individual designer remains disputed and minister José Ignacio Zenteno had the direct institutional role in delivering the definitive design. It should therefore be classified as a governmental manifestation associated with O’Higgins, not securely as his artwork. ([BCN historical discussion](https://www.bcn.cl/laborparlamentaria/participacion?idParticipacion=742336))

- **1817–1818:** *Escuadra Nacional*. The organized Chilean naval force assembled under O’Higgins’s direction. It is a collective military and logistical project—ships, crews, finance, command, and policy—rather than a bounded artifact made by one person. ([BCN account](https://www.bcn.cl/laborparlamentaria/participacion?idParticipacion=1616125))

- **1818:** *Academia de Jóvenes Guardias Marinas*. A naval-training institution founded by O’Higgins’s government and subsequently identified as the origin of Chile’s Naval School. Its authorship is institutional, even though its creation formed part of O’Higgins’s naval project. ([BCN biographical record](https://www.bcn.cl/historiapolitica/resenas_parlamentarias/wiki/Bernardo%20O%27Higgins%20Riquelme))

- **1818–1820:** *Ejército Libertador del Perú*. A transnational military project financed and organized in Chile, commanded in the field by José de San Martín, and supported by the fleet under Thomas Cochrane. O’Higgins made its realization a central state project, but operational and intellectual authorship was necessarily shared. ([BCN biographical record](https://www.bcn.cl/historiapolitica/resenas_parlamentarias/wiki/Bernardo%20O%27Higgins%20Riquelme); [Archivo Nacional](https://www.archivonacional.gob.cl/celebrando-la-independencia-de-peru))

- **1819:** *Reopened Instituto Nacional*. A restoration of an existing educational institution, not an original foundation and therefore resistant to the simple claim that O’Higgins “created” it. The governmental act of reopening and sustaining it is the relevant obra. ([BCN history of the law](https://www.bcn.cl/historiadelaley/historia-de-la-ley/vista-expandida/4298/))

- **1820:** *Reopened Biblioteca Nacional*. Like the Instituto Nacional, this was the revival of an institution founded during the Patria Vieja, not its first creation. O’Higgins’s work was administrative reconstitution. ([BCN history of the law](https://www.bcn.cl/historiadelaley/historia-de-la-ley/vista-expandida/4298/))

- **1820:** *Almacenes francos de Valparaíso*. A commercial and customs institution established under O’Higgins to encourage maritime trade. It is an economic protocol embodied in warehouses and regulations, not an individually authored building. ([BCN history of the law](https://www.bcn.cl/historiadelaley/historia-de-la-ley/vista-expandida/4298/))

- **1817–1823:** *Primary-school program and introduction of Lancasterian instruction*. An educational project implemented through decrees, teachers, municipalities, and schools. It has no single stable title or object and resists treatment as one authored “work,” but contemporary summaries consistently identify it as a major undertaking of O’Higgins’s government. ([BCN history of the law](https://www.bcn.cl/historiadelaley/historia-de-la-ley/vista-expandida/4298/); [BCN biographical record](https://www.bcn.cl/historiapolitica/resenas_parlamentarias/wiki/Bernardo%20O%27Higgins%20Riquelme))
