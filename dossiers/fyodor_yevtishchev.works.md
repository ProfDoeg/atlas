## Works by Fyodor Adrianovich Yevtishchev

### Embodied and co-produced performances

- **1873:** *Hommes-chiens* (“Dog-Men”). A touring father-and-child exhibition made with Adrian Yevtishchev: bodily display rather than a fixed dramatic work, and one in which the three-year-old Fyodor’s creative agency cannot be established. Contemporary documentation calls them “two individuals exhibited,” so this manifestation resists attribution as Fyodor’s obra; the same source makes his conventional 1868 birth year disputable by describing him as three in November 1873. ([Bertillon, 1873](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Des_deux_individus_exhib%C3%A9s_sous_le_nom_d%E2%80%99Hommes-chiens))

- **From 1884; ending uncertain, no later than 1904:** *Jo-Jo, the Dog-Faced Boy* (later *Jo-Jo, the Dog-Faced Man* and *the Human Skye Terrier*). A recurring embodied performance-persona combining Yevtishchev’s self-presentation, Russian cavalry costume, growling or barking, audience interaction, publicity images, and a fictitious capture narrative; it is best classified as a multimedia performance assemblage, not a text or wholly autonomous work. Yevtishchev performed its physical and vocal component, but Barnum and other showmen devised its names, framing story, advertising, and staging, making authorship collaborative and coercively commercial rather than securely his alone; sources agree on the 1884 beginning but differ over whether his life ended in 1903 or 1904, and do not establish a final performance date. ([D’Alessandro, 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/theatre-survey/article/abs/athome-humbugs-freaks-and-fakes-in-the-nineteenthcentury-parlor-museum/707CDF195E5F22392D9AD79BCE4A3644); [Dillman, 2017](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/6908471k))

### Sources

- Jacques Bertillon, [“Des deux individus exhibés sous le nom d’Hommes-chiens,” *La Nature*, no. 25 (22 November 1873), pp. 385–387](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Des_deux_individus_exhib%C3%A9s_sous_le_nom_d%E2%80%99Hommes-chiens). Contemporary account of the joint exhibition.
- Michael D’Alessandro, [“At-Home Humbugs: Freaks and Fakes in the Nineteenth-Century Parlor Museum,” *Theatre Survey* 63, no. 1 (2022), pp. 3–33](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0040557421000557). Scholarly performance history identifying the act’s beginning, costume, billing, and principal actions.
- Sarah Beltran Dillman, [“‘Freaking’ the Archive: Archiving Possibilities with the Victorian Freak Show” (University of California, Riverside, 2017)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/6908471k). Scholarly archival study treating Jo-Jo as a construction produced through interlocking textual, visual, oral, and staged media.
- Jane R. Goodall, [*Performance and Evolution in the Age of Darwin: Out of the Natural Order* (Routledge, 2002)](https://books.google.com/books?id=FyKCAgAAQBAJ). Standard scholarly history used to situate bodily exhibition as performance rather than bibliography.
- No catalogue raisonné, collected-works edition, discography, or scholarly bibliography of works by Yevtishchev is known. The consulted performance histories attribute no authored books, pamphlets, scripts, compositions, photographs, or other independently made objects to him; surviving life-story pamphlets, advertisements, posters, and portraits therefore are not listed as his works.
