## Works by Strabo of Amaseia

### Historical obra

- **Probably begun in the 30s BC; completed after 37 BC, perhaps about 29 BC:** *Historika Hypomnēmata* (*Historical Commentaries*, also translated *Historical Sketches* or *Historical Notes*). A large Greek universal history, now lost except for about nineteen fragments and testimonia, catalogued as FGrHist/BNJ 91; Strabo designed its later portion as a continuation of Polybius. Ancient evidence permits either forty-seven books in all—four introductory books followed by forty-three “after Polybius”—or a total of forty-three, so both its extent and completion date remain disputed.

### Geographical obra

- **Date disputed; final revisions no earlier than AD 23:** *Geōgraphika* (*Geography*). A seventeen-book prose synthesis of physical geography, peoples, political history, literature, and Strabo’s own travel observations, conceived as the companion or sequel to his historical work rather than as an atlas of drawn maps. It survives nearly complete, although the end of Book 7 and smaller passages are lost; scholars variously propose prolonged composition from the Augustan period, an early edition followed by revision, or composition principally under Tiberius around AD 17–23/24. The latest securely dated event the text mentions is the death of the client king Juba II, usually placed in AD 23, which anchors the latest-composition camp. Strabo's own confirmed travels were real but limited — Asia Minor, a stretch of the Tyrrhenian Italian coast, a voyage past the Cycladic island of Gyaros, and an extended journey up the Nile with the Roman prefect Aelius Gallus as far as the first cataract at Syene, probably in 25 or 24 BC, which supplied much of his material on Egypt in Book 17; for most of the work's other regions he compiles and argues with predecessors such as Eratosthenes, Posidonius, and historians of Alexander's campaigns including Onesicritus. The earliest manuscripts covering Books 1–9 date only from the tenth century, with a complete thirteenth-century manuscript supplying the rest; a fragmentary fifth-century palimpsest is the earliest direct textual evidence. The work circulated far less in the Latin West than in the Greek East — major Roman compilers such as Pliny the Elder show little clear sign of using it, and the more cartographic tradition of Ptolemy eventually overshadowed it — until its Western rediscovery through a Latin translation and the first printed Greek edition (Aldus Manutius, Venice, 1516). (Wikipedia, "Geographica"; strabo.ca)

### Possible work or preparatory project

- **Probably begun in the 30s BC, if independent at all:** *A work on Alexander the Great* (no securely transmitted independent title). Strabo refers to material concerning Alexander, and Duane W. Roller reconstructs an early biographical project whose contents were subsequently absorbed into the *Geography*, especially Books 11, 15, and 16. It resists classification as a separate obra: the traditional scholarly inventory recognizes only the *Historika Hypomnēmata* and *Geography*, while an older interpretation assigns the Alexander material to the introductory books of the historical work; no independent manuscript or securely identified fragment proves that a distinct book was completed.
