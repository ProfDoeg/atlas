## Works by Ángel della Valle

### Paintings of rural life, travel, and work

- **1881:** *Caballos de San Marcos*. Oil painting of horses at San Marcos; the date and title appear in later surveys, but the accessible catalogue records do not disclose its present location.[1]
- **1887:** *En la pampa*. Equestrian oil painting made or first exhibited during Della Valle’s Florentine period; it was an early approach to the imagery later developed in *La vuelta del malón*.[2]
- **1887:** *La banda lisa*. Painting of a rural or Indigenous subject, exhibited in Buenos Aires among works sent from Florence; its present location is not established in the consulted sources.[2]
- **1888:** *Tren de la Pampa*. Landscape-and-transport painting; the title also occurs as *El tren de la Pampa*, and the consulted sources do not establish its present location.[1]
- **1889:** *Anochecer en la laguna* (also recorded as *Atardecer en la laguna*). Oil painting of a lagoon landscape; the variation in title should not be silently normalized.[1][3]
- **1893:** *La estación de Lomas de Zamora*. Oil painting of the Ferrocarril del Sud station and its surroundings: simultaneously landscape, record of travel infrastructure, and scene of modern provincial life.[3][4]
- **1893:** *Corrida de sortija* (also *Corrida de sortija en día patrio* and *La carrera de sortijas*). Monumental oil painting of the gaucho equestrian contest; the differing published titles appear to designate the same composition, but that identity is not made explicit in every source.[3][5]
- **1895:** *Al borde del monte*. Oil painting on panel depicting riders or livestock beside woodland.[3]
- **ca. 1900:** *Apartando*. Rural oil painting of mounted workers separating cattle; the approximate date comes from later surveys rather than an inscription documented in the accessible record.[1]
- **ca. 1900:** *Incendio en la Pampa*. Panoramic oil painting of a grassland fire, generally dated around 1900.[1][6]
- **Undated:** *El juego del pato*. Large oil painting of the mounted rural game; neither the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes nor the 1990 catalogue information visible in the consulted sources supplies a secure date.[7]
- **Undated:** *Paisanos*. Oil painting of rural figures; the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes deliberately leaves it undated.[8]
- **Undated:** *Un potrero* (also *La tranquera*). Small oil painting on panel of a fenced pasture; a later museum publication proposes “ca.” without a recoverable year, while the collection record gives no date.[9][10]
- **Undated:** *Indios con tropilla y bueyes*. Oil painting of Indigenous riders moving animals; the consulted reproduction catalogue supplies neither a secure date nor provenance.[11]
- **Undated:** *Patrulla en la Pampa*. Rural or military-patrol painting known through later historical surveys; date and present location remain unspecified.[6]
- **Undated:** *Enlazando*. Rural-action painting known by title through later surveys; no secure date or present location was found.[6]
- **Undated:** *Domando*. Painting of horse-breaking; the accessible survey names it but supplies no fixed date or location.[6]
- **Undated:** *Jueces de carrera*. Painting concerned with rural horse racing; the consulted survey does not establish its date or present location.[6]
- **Undated:** *El rodeo*. Rural livestock painting recorded by the Museo Sívori’s online catalogue, but the accessible scholarly and museum notices do not provide a defensible date.[12]

### Historical, literary, mythological, and religious paintings

- **ca. 1882:** *Monje tocando el violín*. Watercolour of a violin-playing monk, catalogued as no. 41 in the 1990 catalogue raisonné; “ca. 1882” is the catalogue tradition rather than a visibly inscribed date.[13]
- **ca. 1889:** *Desnudo*. Oil painting of a nude, reproduced without title in *Martín Fierro* in 1904 and dated approximately 1889 by the catalogue raisonné; its publication title is therefore descriptive rather than necessarily authorial.[14]
- **1890:** *La diosa del Amor*. Mythological oil painting; its title and date are consistently reported in later surveys, although the consulted sources do not identify a current institutional owner.[1]
- **1892:** *La vuelta del malón*. Monumental oil painting conceived for the World’s Columbian Exposition in Chicago; it is a history painting and political image, not a neutral record of an Indigenous raid.[2][15]
- **1892:** *La vuelta del malón* (“Malón chico”). Reduced oil version given to Pedro Lagleyze after the Chicago journey; frequently called a sketch, although the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes explicitly distinguishes it as a later gift-version.[2][16]
- **1892 or later:** *Indio con cruz*. Oil study or autonomous fragment extracted from *La vuelta del malón*; its status resists a clean division between preparatory study, repetition, and independent work.[2][17]
- **1892 or later:** *El guerrero y la cautiva*. Oil study or autonomous repetition of the principal pair in *La vuelta del malón*; the consulted sources identify the fragment but do not supply a secure individual date.[2]
- **1892 or later:** *La cautiva*. Painting catalogued as no. 106 in the 1990 catalogue raisonné; the title may refer to one of the isolated post-*Malón* compositions, but the accessible documentation does not prove that identity.[18]
- **Undated:** *Paso de los Andes*. Historical-painting project surviving as a sketch in the Museo Histórico General San Martín, Mendoza, and catalogued as no. 120; the sketch, rather than a documented finished canvas, is the extant obra.[19]
- **Undated:** *Prometeo*. Mythological painting named in later surveys; no secure date, dimensions, or present location could be established from the consulted sources.[6]

### Portraits and figure studies

- **Undated:** *Autorretrato*. Painted self-portrait; published sources reproduce or name it without supplying a secure date.[1]
- **Undated:** *La gitanita*. Oil portrait traditionally identified as Josefina Palma and catalogued as no. 82; an auction catalogue’s dating “circa 1860” is impossible for an artist born in 1852 and must be treated as an error, not silently retained.[20]
- **Undated:** *Retrato de Josefina Monty de Lagleyze*. Portrait incorporated into the border of the tapestry-like *Cacería de jabalí con perros*; it is simultaneously a likeness and part of a larger decorative ensemble.[21]

### Interiors and representations of the artist’s workplace

- **ca. 1900:** *El taller*. Oil painting of Della Valle’s studio, with *La vuelta del malón*, *La terraza*, and another painting visible inside it; catalogued as no. 207. It is both an interior and a self-curated image of a body of work, although the painter himself is not necessarily represented.[22]
- **Undated:** *La terraza*. Interior or domestic painting known from the catalogue raisonné and from its representation inside *El taller*; the catalogue describes it and *El taller* as exceptional within Della Valle’s production.[22]

### Decorative works and architectural representations

- **Undated:** *Cacería de jabalí con perros*. Oil on tapestry cloth, with an ornamental floral border, the initials “PL,” and portraits of Josefina Monty de Lagleyze; probably part of the Lagleyze house decorative cycle, but “probably” is essential because the ensemble attribution is not conclusive.[21]
- **Undated:** *Casa de Gobierno*. Unsigned tempera on paper attributed to Della Valle and reproduced lithographically; because it lacks a signature and is known through the art market, its attribution should remain qualified.[23]
- **Undated:** *Congreso Nacional*. Tempera on paper attributed to Della Valle; an architectural representation rather than a book or autonomous architectural project, and its signature status is not stated in the accessible notice.[23]
