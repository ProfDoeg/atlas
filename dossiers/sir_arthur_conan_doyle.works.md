## Works by Sir Arthur Ignatius Conan Doyle

### Fictional prose: novels and novellas

- **1887:** *A Study in Scarlet*. Sherlock Holmes novel, first published in *Beeton’s Christmas Annual*; the book publication followed in 1888.
- **1888:** *The Mystery of Cloomber*. Supernatural and imperial-adventure novel.
- **1889:** *Micah Clarke*. Historical novel about the Monmouth Rebellion.
- **1890:** *The Firm of Girdlestone*. Commercial and domestic melodrama written substantially earlier than its publication.
- **1890:** *The Sign of Four*. Sherlock Holmes novel, first published as *The Sign of the Four* in *Lippincott’s Monthly Magazine*; the title varies among early manifestations.
- **1891:** *The White Company*. Historical adventure novel set during the Hundred Years’ War.
- **1891:** *The Doings of Raffles Haw*. Scientific romance about an inventor capable of transmuting matter into gold.
- **1892:** *The Great Shadow*. Historical novella concerning the Napoleonic era and Waterloo.
- **1892:** *Beyond the City*. Domestic novella about suburban life, marriage, and women’s independence.
- **1893:** *The Refugees*. Historical novel about Louis XIV’s France and French Protestant migration.
- **1894:** *The Parasite*. Supernatural novella about mesmerism and coercive control.
- **1895:** *The Stark Munro Letters*. Epistolary, substantially autobiographical novel about a young doctor.
- **1896:** *Rodney Stone*. Historical novel combining Regency society with prize-fighting.
- **1897:** *Uncle Bernac*. Napoleonic historical novel.
- **1898:** *The Tragedy of the Korosko*. Imperial adventure novel, also issued as *A Desert Drama*.
- **1899:** *A Duet, with an Occasional Chorus*. Domestic novel about marriage.
- **1900:** *Hilda Wade: XII. The Episode of the Dead Man Who Spoke*. Doyle completed Grant Allen’s serial novel after Allen’s death; only its final episode is Doyle’s work, so the whole novel resists attribution to him.
- **1902:** *The Hound of the Baskervilles*. Sherlock Holmes novel serialized in 1901–02 and situated before Holmes’s apparent death in “The Final Problem.”
- **1906:** *Sir Nigel*. Historical novel and narrative prequel to *The White Company*.
- **1912:** *The Lost World*. Professor Challenger scientific-romance novel.
- **1913:** *The Poison Belt*. Apocalyptic Professor Challenger novella.
- **1915:** *The Valley of Fear*. Sherlock Holmes novel serialized in 1914–15.
- **1926:** *The Land of Mist*. Professor Challenger novel that turns the series toward Spiritualism.
- **1927–1928:** *The Maracot Deep*. Undersea scientific romance serialized before its 1929 book manifestation.
- **Written 1883; published 2011:** *The Narrative of John Smith*. Unfinished novel whose surviving manuscript is a reconstruction begun after the original manuscript was lost in the post; its incompleteness is intrinsic to the project.

### Short fiction: named cycles and first-authorized collections

- **ca. 1864–1865:** *The Story of a Bengal Tiger*. Childhood fiction, unpublished in Doyle’s lifetime; its status is that of a surviving juvenile manuscript rather than a completed public work.
- **ca. 1870–1875:** *The Judkin’s Tragedy*. Juvenile story associated with Doyle’s manuscript periodical *The Wasp*.
- **1878:** *The Haunted Grange of Goresthorpe*. Early story rejected during Doyle’s lifetime and first published in 2000.
- **1879–1930:** *Uncollected and periodical short fiction*. An obra comprising approximately 259 fictional texts in the specialist bibliography, including juvenile manuscripts, magazine stories, collaborations, and posthumously recovered pieces; treating it as one “book” would conceal materially different texts and publication states.
- **1887–1927:** *The Sherlock Holmes canon*. A cross-publication fictional corpus of four novels and fifty-six short stories. “Canon” is a retrospective bibliographical category rather than a single object Doyle issued under that title.
- **1892:** *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes*. First authorized Holmes short-story collection.
- **1892:** *The Gully of Bluemansdyke and Other Stories*. Short-story collection.
- **1892:** *The Great Shadow and Other Napoleonic Tales*. Collection built around historical fiction; later manifestations altered its contents.
- **1894:** *The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes*. Holmes collection whose contents and ordering differ among early British and American manifestations.
- **1894:** *Round the Red Lamp: Being Facts and Fancies of Medical Life*. Medical-story collection.
- **1896:** *The Exploits of Brigadier Gerard*. First collection of Gerard stories.
- **1898:** *The Man from Archangel and Other Tales of Adventure*. Short-story collection.
- **1900:** *The Green Flag and Other Stories of War and Sport*. Short-story collection organized around military and sporting subjects.
- **1903:** *Adventures of Gerard*. Second principal collection of Brigadier Gerard stories.
- **1905:** *The Return of Sherlock Holmes*. Holmes short-story collection.
- **1908:** *Round the Fire Stories*. Collection of mystery, horror, and adventure stories.
- **1911:** *The Last Galley: Impressions and Tales*. Collection mixing tales with impressionistic prose.
- **1917:** *His Last Bow: Some Reminiscences of Sherlock Holmes*. Holmes collection, including the wartime title story.
- **1918:** *Danger! and Other Stories*. Short-story collection whose title story imagines submarine warfare against Britain.
- **1922:** *Tales of the Ring and Camp*. Thematic selection from earlier stories.
- **1922:** *Tales of Pirates and Blue Water*. Thematic selection of maritime and pirate fiction.
- **1922:** *Tales of Terror and Mystery*. Thematic selection of horror and mystery fiction.
- **1922:** *Tales of Twilight and the Unseen*. Thematic selection of supernatural fiction.
- **1922:** *Tales of Adventure and Medical Life*. Thematic selection combining adventure and medical stories.
- **1922:** *Tales of Long Ago*. Thematic selection of historical stories.
- **1927:** *The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes*. Final Holmes short-story collection; early British and American manifestations differ in arrangement.
- **1929:** *The Maracot Deep and Other Stories*. Collection joining the title scientific romance to shorter fiction.

### Collaborative fiction and serial experiments

- **1892:** *The Fate of Fenella*. Collaborative serial novel written successively by numerous authors; Doyle made one installment, not the whole obra.
- **1900:** *Hilda Wade: XII. The Episode of the Dead Man Who Spoke*. Completion of Grant Allen’s unfinished novel; Doyle’s episode is both an autonomous contribution and part of another author’s project.

### Drama, libretti, and dramatic projects

- **Undated:** *Admiral Denver*. Seven-page dramatic draft; an unfinished project rather than a performed play.
- **Undated:** *The Criminal*. Twelve-page dramatic draft.
- **Undated:** *The Black Hawk*. Nine-page dramatic draft.
- **Undated:** *The Flights of Dr Curtius*. Fourteen-page synopsis; an unrealized dramatic project.
- **Undated:** *The Waterloo Play*. Fifteen-page manuscript distinct from *A Story of Waterloo*; its fragmentary form resists treatment as a completed play.
- **1888:** *Angels of Darkness*. Three-act drama derived from *A Study in Scarlet*; neither published nor performed in Doyle’s lifetime.
- **1893:** *Jane Annie; or, The Good Conduct Prize*. Comic-opera libretto co-written with J. M. Barrie, with music by Ernest Ford; a collaborative theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk rather than Doyle’s text alone.
- **1893:** *Foreign Policy*. Play.
- **1894:** *A Story of Waterloo*. One-act stage play featuring the veteran Corporal Gregory Brewster.
- **1899:** *Sherlock Holmes*. Stage play associated with both Doyle and William Gillette. Gillette extensively reshaped Doyle’s earlier dramatic material, so sole authorship should not be assigned silently.
- **1899:** *Halves*. One-act play, also known as *Brothers*.
- **1902:** *A Duet*. Dramatic duologue adapted from Doyle’s novel.
- **1903:** *The Adventures of Gerard*. Stage adaptation of the Brigadier Gerard fiction.
- **1909:** *The Fires of Fate*. Stage adaptation of *The Tragedy of the Korosko*.
- **1909:** *The House of Temperley*. Boxing drama developed under the earlier working title *Sir Charles Tregellis* and related to *Rodney Stone*.
- **1910:** *The Lift*. Unperformed play.
- **1910:** *A Pot of Caviare*. One-act play.
- **1910:** *The Speckled Band: An Adventure of Sherlock Holmes*. Stage adaptation first developed under the title *The Stonor Case*.
- **1913:** *Mrs. Thompson*. Neither published nor performed; a surviving dramatic project.
- **1914:** *Dawn*. Play co-written with Percival Wilde.
- **1921:** *The Crown Diamond: An Evening with Sherlock Holmes*. One-act Holmes play; Doyle subsequently reworked its plot as “The Adventure of the Mazarin Stone.”
- **1922:** *The Journey*. Dramatic dialogue published in Doyle’s collected poems, making it a text that crosses the categories of verse book and theatre.

### Poetry and verse collections

- **1870–1922:** *Individual poems*. An obra of approximately 133 separately identified poems in the specialist bibliography, ranging from juvenile manuscript verse to war poetry and occasional verse; many lack an independent book manifestation.
- **1898:** *Songs of Action*. First poetry collection.
- **1911:** *Songs of the Road*. Poetry collection.
- **1919:** *The Guards Came Through and Other Poems*. Collection dominated by wartime and patriotic verse.
- **1922:** *The Poems of Arthur Conan Doyle: Collected Edition*. Doyle’s principal collected-poems manifestation; “collected” does not mean that every surviving juvenile or occasional poem was included.

### Medicine, photography, sport, and scientific argument

- **1879:** *Gelseminum as a Poison*. Medical letter reporting Doyle’s self-experimentation.
- **1880:** *Log of the S.S. Hope*. Shipboard report or diary from Doyle’s Arctic voyage; not publicly issued until 2012.
- **1881:** *After Cormorants with a Camera*. Photographic article.
- **1882:** *Notes on a Case of Leucocythaemia*. Medical case report.
- **1882:** *On the Slave Coast with a Camera*. Photographic travel article.
- **1882:** *Up an African River with a Camera*. Photographic travel article.
- **1882:** *Dry Plates on a Wet Moor*. Photographic article.
- **1890:** *The Great Keinplatz Experiment*. Fiction rather than a scientific protocol despite its experimental title.
- **1890:** *The Physiological Action of Gelsemium*. Medical doctoral thesis; an academic research work.
- **1891:** *The Use of Imagination in Crime*. Essay joining criminological speculation to literary practice.
- **1893:** *The Great Brown-Pericord Motor*. Technological fantasy rather than a practicable engineering project.
- **1894:** *An Alpine Pass on “Ski”*. Sporting and travel essay describing Doyle’s early skiing in Switzerland.
- **1909:** *The Crime of the Congo*. Documentary polemic and political intervention concerning atrocities in the Congo Free State.
- **1910:** *The Romance of Medicine*. Medical-historical essay.
- **1912:** *The Case of Oscar Slater*. Judicial polemic intended to reopen a wrongful-conviction case; as an intervention seeking action, it approaches a manifesto.
- **1913:** *The Case of Mr. George Edalji*. Doyle’s writings on Edalji form a campaigning dossier rather than one stable literary work; bibliography and periodical history divide it among articles and letters.
- **1914:** *Civilian National Reserve*. Public-defense proposal functioning partly as a protocol for voluntary organization.
- **1914:** *The Treatment of Our Prisoners*. Wartime documentary and polemical pamphlet.
- **1917:** *An Outing with the Fleet*. Naval reportage.
- **1918:** *Life After Death*. Spiritualist argument first delivered through public advocacy and subsequently issued as a book.
- **1928:** *What Does Spiritualism Actually Teach and Stand For?*. Expository and proselytizing pamphlet.

### War history, public affairs, autobiography, and travel

- **1900:** *The Great Boer War*. Historical account repeatedly revised as the war continued; consequently it is a changing textual project rather than one invariant text.
- **1900:** *The Great Boer War: A British Officer’s Account of the War in South Africa*. Related manifestations and abridgments should not be mistaken for separately authored obras.
- **1902:** *The War in South Africa: Its Cause and Conduct*. Political defense of British conduct, conceived as an internationally distributed intervention.
- **1907:** *Through the Magic Door*. Essays on books and reading.
- **1907:** *The Story of Mr. George Edalji*. Investigative campaign text advocating redress for Edalji; its practical purpose makes it more than detached description.
- **1909:** *The Crime of the Congo*. Illustrated humanitarian campaign book; its photographs and documentary apparatus make the printed object more than prose alone.
- **1911:** *The Great Boer War*. Later revised manifestation of the continuing historical project, not a newly composed work.
- **1914:** *The German War: Some Sidelights and Reflections*. Collection of wartime essays.
- **1914:** *To Arms!*. Recruiting pamphlet that acts upon its readers and therefore functions as a manifesto.
- **1915:** *The Western Wanderings of a British Officer*. Travel and wartime observation.
- **1915:** *The Outlook on the War*. Wartime lecture or pamphlet manifestation.
- **1915:** *The German War: The Channel Tunnel*. Strategic advocacy.
- **1916:** *A Visit to Three Fronts*. War reportage based on visits to the British, Italian, and French fronts.
- **1916–1920:** *The British Campaign in France and Flanders*. Six-volume military history issued while events and evidence were still developing; the project is serial, revised, and not reducible to a single publication date.
- **1917:** *The British Campaign in France and Flanders, 1914*. First retrospective segment of the larger war-history project.
- **1918:** *The British Campaign in France and Flanders, 1915*. Continuation of the multi-volume history.
- **1918:** *The British Campaign in France and Flanders, 1916*. Continuation of the multi-volume history.
- **1919:** *The British Campaign in France and Flanders, 1917*. Continuation of the multi-volume history.
- **1919:** *The British Campaign in France and Flanders, January to July 1918*. Continuation of the multi-volume history.
- **1920:** *The British Campaign in France and Flanders, July to November 1918*. Concluding volume of the original project.
- **1923–1924:** *Memories and Adventures*. Autobiography serialized before its 1924 book publication.
- **1924:** *Memories and Adventures*. Book manifestation of Doyle’s autobiography.
- **1928:** *The British Campaigns in Europe, 1914–1918*. Condensed and revised recasting of the earlier six-volume history, not merely a reprint.
- **1929:** *Our African Winter*. Travel narrative of Doyle’s African spiritualist lecture tour.

### Spiritualist obras, manifestos, and evidential projects

- **1918:** *The New Revelation*. Spiritualist manifesto presenting Doyle’s religious conversion as public doctrine.
- **1919:** *The Vital Message*. Spiritualist manifesto and doctrinal sequel to *The New Revelation*.
- **1919:** *The Case for Spirit Photography*. Evidential polemic defending psychic photography.
- **1920:** *The Wanderings of a Spiritualist*. Travel narrative and missionary account of Doyle’s Australasian tour.
- **1920:** *Spiritualism and Rationalism*. Published debate with Joseph McCabe; the object records an adversarial event and is not Doyle’s text alone.
- **1921:** *The Evidence for Fairies*. Illustrated evidential argument for the Cottingley photographs. Subsequent exposure of the photographs as staged makes Doyle’s interpretation discredited, but not nonexistent.
- **1921:** *The Coming of the Fairies*. Expanded book-length investigation and advocacy concerning the Cottingley photographs.
- **1922:** *The Case for Spirit Photography*. Book or pamphlet manifestations collect a broader controversy; editions and constituent papers vary.
- **1922:** *Spiritualism—Some Straight Questions and Direct Answers*. Catechetical pamphlet functioning as both instruction and advocacy.
- **1922:** *The Spiritualist’s Reader*. Anthological religious project; editorial selection, rather than sole composition, is Doyle’s principal act of making.
- **1923:** *Our American Adventure*. Travel and spiritualist mission narrative.
- **1923:** *The Case of Mrs. Cantlon*. Psychic-case pamphlet.
- **1924:** *Our Second American Adventure*. Travel and spiritualist mission narrative.
- **1924:** *Pheneas Speaks*. A record of purported communications received through mediumship. Doyle framed and edited the object, but its claimed spirit authorship makes it resist ordinary authorship categories.
- **1924:** *Psychic Experiences*. Doyle’s contribution to the collaborative volume *Survival*; the whole book is not solely his.
- **1925:** *The Early Christian Church and Modern Spiritualism*. Historical-doctrinal pamphlet.
- **1925:** *My Religion*. Spiritualist profession of faith; some bibliographies also identify a collaborative or composite publication context, so manifestation and authorship require care.
- **1926:** *The History of Spiritualism*. Two-volume historical and apologetic work.
- **1926:** *The Land of Mist*. Although a novel, it also operates as spiritualist advocacy; it deliberately refuses a clean division between fiction and manifesto.
- **1927:** *The Psychic Question as I See It*. Essay contributed to the composite volume *The Case For and Against Psychical Belief*.
- **1927:** *The Case of the “Highgate Ghost”*. Psychic-investigation pamphlet.
- **1927:** *The Edge of the Unknown*. Spiritualist essays later collected in book form.
- **1928:** *A Word of Warning*. Spiritualist pamphlet.
- **1928:** *The Roman Catholic Church: A Rejoinder*. Religious polemic.
- **1928:** *The British Spiritualists’ Lyceum Manual*. Doyle contributed to the institutional project, but the manual is a collective protocol rather than his sole obra.
- **1929:** *If I Were a Preacher*. Religious essay separately published and also appearing in collaborative contexts.
- **1930:** *The Edge of the Unknown*. Final book-length collection of psychic and spiritualist arguments.

### Political and legal campaign texts

- **1906–1907:** *The George Edalji campaign writings*. Articles, letters, interviews, and evidential arguments seeking reversal of Edalji’s conviction. They form a practical intervention dispersed across newspapers rather than a single stable work.
- **1909:** *The Crime of the Congo*. Human-rights campaign object combining prose, testimony, and images.
- **1912–1928:** *The Oscar Slater campaign writings*. A dispersed dossier of book, articles, letters, petitions, and public statements aimed at overturning Slater’s conviction; calling it one literary work would erase its procedural character.
- **1913:** *Divorce and the Church*. Collaborative reform pamphlet with Lord Hugh Cecil; Doyle’s titled contribution was “Reform of the Law Needed.”
- **1914:** *In Quest of Truth*. Collaborative debate or polemical publication with Captain H. Stansbury.
- **1914–1918:** *Wartime recruiting and civil-defense writings*. A body of articles, letters, pamphlets, and proposals intended to change public behavior; many function as manifestos or protocols rather than reflective essays.
- **1920s:** *Spiritualist organizational manifestos and election interventions*. A dispersed body of public letters and declarations advocating institutional and political recognition for Spiritualism.

### Journalism, essays, published letters, prefaces, interviews, and lectures

- **1873–1930:** *Essays, pamphlets, articles, and published letters*. The specialist catalogue identifies more than 1,100 items. This is a heterogeneous documentary corpus—not one genre and not safely reducible to “books”—including medical reports, criticism, campaign letters, war commentary, Spiritualist apologetics, sporting prose, and posthumously printed texts.
- **1888–1930:** *Recorded interviews*. The specialist catalogue lists more than 100 interview texts. Authorship is intrinsically divided between Doyle’s speech and reporters’ selection, paraphrase, titling, and transcription, so these resist classification as works made by Doyle alone.
- **1883–1930:** *Lectures and speeches*. More than 300 occasions are catalogued. A lecture is an event or performance; surviving reports, notes, and repeated deliveries are manifestations, not necessarily distinct authored texts.
- **1890–1930:** *Prefaces, introductions, forewords, dedications, and appreciations*. Approximately sixty separately catalogued contributions to Doyle’s and other writers’ books. These are paratexts: genuine made texts, but not independent books.
- **1903:** *Arthur Conan Doyle Author’s Edition prefaces*. Eight autobiographical prefaces prepared for the collected edition; they belong both to individual volumes and to the edition as a larger editorial obra.
- **1916–1919:** *Prefaces to The British Campaign in France and Flanders*. Five separately made paratexts within the evolving war-history project.

### Letters, diaries, manuscripts, drawings, and other non-book objects

- **1875:** *The Feldkirchian Gazette*. Juvenile manuscript periodical containing prose, verse, and drawings; its composite handwritten form makes it closer to a miniature Gesamtkunstwerk than to an ordinary authored book.
- **Throughout his life:** *Private and professional correspondence*. More than 1,100 letters are separately recorded by the specialist catalogue. Letters are situated acts addressed to particular recipients, and later publication does not retroactively turn them into books.
- **Throughout his life:** *Surviving literary manuscripts*. The specialist catalogue identifies more than 130 manuscript objects. Drafts, fair copies, fragments, notes, and publication texts are manifestations of projects and should not automatically be counted as additional works.
- **Throughout his life:** *Drawings and paintings*. More than eighty visual objects are catalogued, including juvenile illustration, sketches, and designs. They are obras in graphic media, not texts in the narrow bibliographical sense.
- **Throughout his life:** *Inscribed books and presentation material*. Dozens of inscriptions survive, but most resist being called autonomous works: they are situated marks added to other people’s manufactured objects.
- **Undated and various dates:** *Miscellaneous handwritten writings*. Notes, lists, captions, and documentary fragments survive; where neither stable title nor completed intention exists, they are evidence of making rather than securely bounded works.
- **1928:** *Sir Arthur Conan Doyle*. Fox Movietone sound-film appearance in which Doyle speaks about Sherlock Holmes and Spiritualism. The performance is Doyle’s, while the audiovisual manifestation is a collaborative industrial production.
- **1930:** *Spiritualist address filmed at Windlesham*. Recorded speech or performance made shortly before Doyle’s death; the surviving film is simultaneously a technological record and an authored act of public advocacy.

### Editorial and collected-work projects

- **1903:** *Arthur Conan Doyle Author’s Edition*. Multi-volume collected edition supervised and prefaced by Doyle. It is an editorial obra, not a newly written set of constituent works.
- **1914:** *The Works of A. Conan Doyle*. Collected edition whose material largely re-manifests earlier works.
- **1928–1929:** *Sherlock Holmes—The Complete Short Stories*. Collected manifestation rather than a new fictional composition.
- **1929:** *The Conan Doyle Stories*. Retrospective collected-fiction volume.
- **1929:** *Sherlock Holmes—The Complete Long Stories*. Collected manifestation of the four Holmes novels.
- **1930:** *The Crowborough Edition of the Works of Sir Arthur Conan Doyle*. Author-associated collected edition issued around the time of his death; its bibliographical unity does not make the reprinted contents new works.
- **1930:** *The Complete Sherlock Holmes: The A. Conan Doyle Memorial Edition*. Posthumous memorial manifestation; Doyle made the constituent fiction but not, in the ordinary sense, the completed memorial object.
