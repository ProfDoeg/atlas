## Works by Sir Edward Coley Burne-Jones

### Juvenilia and works on paper

- **c. 1840–1852:** *Juvenile drawings and caricatures*. Childhood copies, historical scenes, portraits, animals, and humorous drawings survive; many lack individual titles or secure dates and resist treatment as a closed series.
- **1853–1898:** *Sketchbooks, studies, cartoons, and tracings*. Thousands of figure, drapery, plant, animal, architectural, and compositional studies constitute the working archive behind Burne-Jones’s paintings and designs. The online catalogue raisonné records sheets separately, but divisions between autonomous drawing, reused design, workshop instrument, and fragment are often unstable.
- **1856:** *The Waxen Image*. A pen-and-ink narrative drawing illustrating the medieval story of the enchanted image.
- **1857:** *Clara von Bork*. A watercolour derived from Wilhelm Meinhold’s Gothic romance *Sidonia the Sorceress*.
- **1857:** *Sidonia von Bork 1560*. A watercolour companion to *Clara von Bork*.
- **1857:** *The Knight’s Farewell*. A watercolour-and-gouache picture whose medieval subject and minute execution place it between manuscript illumination and easel painting.
- **1857–1858:** *The Prioress’s Tale*. A watercolour based on Chaucer.
- **1858:** *The Going of the Rose*. A watercolour associated with the artist’s early Rossettian period.
- **1858:** *The Backgammon Players*. A watercolour of two richly dressed women at play.
- **1858:** *The Annunciation and the Adoration of the Magi*. A watercolour conceived with the compact organization of an illuminated manuscript.
- **1858–1861:** *Cupid and Psyche drawings*. A project for illustrating William Morris’s retelling of the story; designs later migrated into wood engraving, stained glass, and painted decoration, so the “work” is the transmissible design as much as any single object.
- **1860:** *Buondelmonte’s Wedding*. A watercolour based on Florentine history.
- **1861:** *The Nativity*. A watercolour organized like a devotional altarpiece.
- **1861:** *The Annunciation*. A watercolour, distinct from later painted and stained-glass treatments of the same subject.
- **1861:** *The Madness of Sir Tristram*. A watercolour inspired by Malory.
- **1862:** *Morgan le Fay*. A watercolour of the Arthurian enchantress.
- **1863:** *The Merciful Knight*. A watercolour based on the legend of St John Gualbert.
- **1865:** *Green Summer*. A watercolour group portrait translated into an invented pastoral world.
- **1865:** *The Lament*. A watercolour whose subject is deliberately indeterminate.
- **1866:** *The Princess Tied to the Tree*. A watercolour treatment of Perseus and Andromeda and an early manifestation of the later Perseus project.
- **1868:** *Astrologia*. A watercolour personification of astrology.
- **1868:** *Temperantia*. A watercolour personification of Temperance.
- **1868:** *The Hours*. A watercolour procession of personified hours.
- **1870:** *Love Disguised as Reason*. A watercolour allegory.
- **1870:** *Phyllis and Demophoön*. A watercolour whose exhibition caused controversy over nudity and contributed to Burne-Jones’s resignation from the Old Water-Colour Society; the title and composition were subsequently revised.
- **1871:** *Le Chant d’Amour* / *The Love Song*. A large watercolour version of a composition Burne-Jones also developed as an oil painting.
- **1873:** *Love among the Ruins*. A large watercolour, damaged in 1893 and restored by Burne-Jones in 1898; a distinct later oil version followed.
- **1874:** *The Wine of Circe*. A second version of the composition first exhibited in 1869.
- **1875:** *The Mirror of Venus*. A watercolour version of a composition also realized in oil.
- **1882–1898:** *The Flower Book*. A project of thirty-eight circular watercolours inspired by flower names rather than botanical illustration. Left unpublished at Burne-Jones’s death, it is simultaneously a finished sequence and an incompletely realized book project.
- **1885–1895:** *British Museum sketchbook*. A sustained private sequence of designs, inventions, and compositional notes bequeathed by Burne-Jones to the museum; it is a working object rather than a single finished artwork.

### Easel pictures and serial pictorial projects

- **1858–1861:** *The Briar Rose, first series*. Three early watercolours—*The Prince Enters the Briar Wood*, *The King and His Court*, and *The Sleeping Princess*—forming the first manifestation of Burne-Jones’s Sleeping Beauty project.
- **1860:** *The Blessed Damozel*. A watercolour based on Dante Gabriel Rossetti’s poem.
- **1861:** *The Heart of the Rose*. A watercolour associated with the *Roman de la Rose*.
- **1862–1863:** *The Nativity Triptych*. A three-part painted devotional ensemble comprising the Nativity and attendant scenes.
- **1863:** *The Wheel of Fortune*. An early watercolour version of a composition Burne-Jones repeatedly reformulated.
- **1864:** *The Princess Sabra Led to the Dragon*. A watercolour from the legend of St George.
- **1865–1867:** *The Legend of St George and the Dragon*. A cycle of seven oil paintings commissioned for the dining room of Myles Birket Foster. The pictures were subsequently dispersed, making the project more readily apprehended through reconstructions than as an intact decorative environment.
- **1868–1877:** *The Days of Creation*. A sequence of six watercolours in which successive angels embody the days of Creation; a seventh design represents divine rest.
- **1868–1877:** *The Beguiling of Merlin*. A large oil painting developed over nearly a decade from Malory’s account of Merlin and Nimue.
- **1868–1878:** *Le Chant d’Amour* / *The Love Song*. An oil painting begun years before its first exhibition; its long gestation complicates any single-date classification.
- **1869:** *The Wine of Circe*. The first exhibited watercolour version of Circe awaiting the sailors of Odysseus.
- **1869–1873:** *Laus Veneris*. A large watercolour based on the Tannhäuser legend.
- **1869–1874:** *The Hesperides*. A large watercolour of the guardians of the golden apples.
- **1870–1874:** *Pan and Psyche*. A large watercolour based on William Morris’s version of the story.
- **1871–1880:** *The Golden Stairs*. A large oil painting of an unnamed procession; Burne-Jones avoided supplying a fixed narrative, and the picture resists conversion into an illustrated “text.”
- **1871–1883:** *The Wheel of Fortune*. A monumental oil version of the allegory.
- **1872–1877:** *The Mirror of Venus*. An oil painting of Venus and her companions looking into a pool.
- **1872–1881:** *The Mill*. A large oil painting combining dancing figures, musicians, water, and architecture without a determinate narrative.
- **1875–1884:** *King Cophetua and the Beggar Maid*. A large oil painting based on the ballad of the African king who marries a beggar.
- **1875–1888:** *The Perseus Series*. A projected cycle of ten subjects commissioned for Arthur Balfour’s London house. Burne-Jones produced a complete set of gouache designs, cartoons, studies, and several large oil paintings, but never completed the intended painted ensemble; the project, rather than any single surviving set, is therefore the obra.
- **1875–1888:** *The Call of Perseus*. The opening Perseus composition, surviving in the project’s design sequence rather than as one of the completed large oils.
- **1875–1888:** *Perseus and the Graiae*. A Perseus-cycle composition developed through studies and a finished design.
- **1875–1888:** *Perseus and the Sea Nymphs*. A Perseus-cycle composition represented by design and preparatory material.
- **1875–1888:** *The Finding of Medusa*. A Perseus-cycle composition represented in the design sequence.
- **1875–1888:** *The Death of Medusa*. A Perseus-cycle composition for which extensive studies survive, although the projected large painting remained incomplete.
- **1875–1888:** *The Birth of Pegasus and Chrysaor*. A Perseus-cycle subject preserved chiefly through the project’s designs.
- **1884–1885:** *The Rock of Doom*. A completed large oil from the Perseus cycle.
- **1885–1888:** *The Doom Fulfilled*. A completed large oil from the Perseus cycle.
- **1886–1887:** *The Baleful Head*. A completed large oil from the Perseus cycle.
- **1877:** *The Annunciation*. A large oil painting setting the biblical scene within a compressed architectural space.
- **1878:** *An Angel Playing a Flageolet*. A small oil-and-gold picture whose icon-like form also reflects Burne-Jones’s decorative design practice.
- **1879–1885:** *The Depths of the Sea*. An oil painting of a mermaid carrying a drowned sailor.
- **1880:** *The Tree of Forgiveness*. An oil treatment of Phyllis and Demophoön, reformulating the controversial 1870 watercolour.
- **1881–1898:** *The Last Sleep of Arthur in Avalon*. A monumental oil painting repeatedly enlarged and revised but unfinished at Burne-Jones’s death. Its incompletion is integral to its status as a lifelong Arthurian project.
- **1884:** *Flora*. An oil personification adapted from a figure Burne-Jones had designed for decorative work.
- **1884:** *Pomona*. An oil personification similarly crossing between easel painting and decorative design.
- **1884–1890:** *The Legend of the Briar Rose, second series*. Four monumental oils—*The Briar Wood*, *The Council Chamber*, *The Garden Court*, and *The Rose Bower*. Later installed at Buscot Park with additional painted connecting panels, the ensemble approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than an ordinary picture series.
- **1886–1887:** *The Garden of Pan*. An oil painting of an Arcadian encounter with Pan.
- **1887:** *The Arming of Perseus*. An independent oil treatment related to, but not identical with, the commissioned Perseus cycle.
- **1888:** *The Bath of Venus*. An oil painting returning to the artist’s Venus imagery.
- **1888:** *The Heart of the Rose*. A large oil from the *Romaunt of the Rose* theme.
- **1889:** *The Love that Leads the Pilgrim*. An oil composition derived from the *Roman de la Rose*.
- **1889–1890:** *The Star of Bethlehem*. A monumental watercolour commissioned for Birmingham’s municipal collection; Burne-Jones also adapted the composition for tapestry.
- **1890:** *Sponsa de Libano* / *The Bride of Lebanon*. An oil painting derived from the Song of Songs and from an earlier stained-glass design.
- **1890–1893:** *The Golden Knight*. An oil painting left unfinished.
- **1893:** *Vespertina Quies*. An oil painting whose Latin title suggests, but does not narratively determine, “evening rest.”
- **1893–1894:** *Love among the Ruins*. The oil version of the 1873 watercolour composition.
- **1894:** *The Prioress’s Tale*. A late oil painting based on Chaucer, distinct from the early watercolour.
- **1895:** *The Dream of Launcelot at the Chapel of the Holy Grail*. An oil painting related to Burne-Jones’s Grail tapestry designs.
- **1895–1896:** *Portrait of Baronne Madeleine Deslandes*. An oil portrait; the sitter’s surname is frequently, but incorrectly, printed “Desandes.”
- **1896–1897:** *Love and the Pilgrim*. A large oil painting from the *Romaunt of the Rose* theme and the last major picture Burne-Jones completed.
- **1896–1898:** *The Wizard* / *The Magician* / *The Necromancer*. An unfinished oil known by several posthumous titles; Burne-Jones reportedly referred to it descriptively as his maiden-and-necromancer picture, so no single modern title is wholly secure.

### Portraits

- **1850s–1890s:** *Portrait drawings and caricatures*. A large, open body of likenesses of relatives, friends, patrons, artists, and public figures. Many were informal gifts or private jokes and resist classification as commissioned “works.”
- **1863:** *Portrait of Georgiana Burne-Jones*. A painted likeness of the artist’s wife.
- **1868:** *Portrait of Maria Zambaco*. A portrait drawing of Burne-Jones’s model and collaborator.
- **1870:** *Portrait of Maria Zambaco*. A painted portrait, distinct from the many studies of her used in imaginative compositions.
- **1879:** *Portrait of Katie Lewis*. A painted child portrait.
- **1883:** *Portrait of Frances Graham*. A painted portrait of the artist’s close friend and patron.
- **1884:** *Portrait of Dorothy Drew*. A portrait drawing of Gladstone’s granddaughter.
- **1886:** *Portrait of Lady Windsor*. A painted portrait.
- **1887:** *Portrait of Margaret Burne-Jones*. A portrait of the artist’s daughter.
- **1893:** *Portrait of Philip Comyns Carr*. A painted child portrait.
- **1890s:** *Portrait of Lady Elizabeth Lewis*. A late portrait study known in more than one version and pose.

### Murals and architectural decoration

- **1857–1859:** *Oxford Union murals*. Burne-Jones contributed Arthurian scenes to the collaborative decoration of the Oxford Union debating hall, principally *Nimue Brings Sir Peleus to Ettard’s Arms*. The rapidly deteriorating wall paintings were an experimental collective project rather than conventional portable pictures.
- **1859–1860:** *Red House decoration*. Painted furniture, wall decoration, stained glass, and other designs made with William Morris, Dante Gabriel Rossetti, Elizabeth Siddal, Ford Madox Brown, and others. Authorship is distributed, and surviving fragments do not reconstruct a single securely bounded work.
- **1861–1863:** *St Frideswide, Christ Church Cathedral, Oxford*. A painted decorative scheme narrating the saint’s legend.
- **1862–1863:** *The Nativity triptych and related church decoration*. A devotional ensemble whose panel-painting format deliberately evokes medieval church furniture.
- **1863–1864:** *The Backgammon Players cabinet*. Painted decoration for furniture manufactured by Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co.; design, carpentry, and object are collaborative rather than identical acts of authorship.
- **1867:** *The Adoration of the Magi*, St Michael and All Angels, Brighton. A mural executed in association with Morris’s firm.
- **1876–1877:** *The Story of Troy*, The Hill, Witley. A decorative cycle designed for the music room of Alexander Ionides’s house; the surviving and dispersed elements make the original room an incompletely recoverable Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1881:** *The Grey Ladies*. A painted decorative composition of spectral female figures, left unfinished and later separated from its architectural setting.
- **1881–1894:** *St Paul’s Within-the-Walls mosaics, Rome*. Burne-Jones designed the extensive apse and chancel imagery executed in mosaic by other makers. The finished church interior is a collaborative manifestation of his designs, not an object fabricated by his hand.
- **1891–1902:** *The Ascension and related mosaics, St Philip’s Cathedral, Birmingham*. Burne-Jones designed the chancel mosaics, which were translated and installed by workshop specialists; parts were completed after his death.

### Stained-glass projects

- **1857–1898:** *Stained-glass designs for domestic, institutional, and ecclesiastical settings*. The catalogue raisonné and A. C. Sewter’s specialist catalogue enumerate Burne-Jones’s cartoons and their numerous Morris-firm realizations individually. Because cartoons were repeatedly resized, recoloured, recombined, and installed at different dates, neither “one cartoon” nor “one window” consistently defines the work.
- **1861:** *The Legend of St George windows*, Harden Grange, Bingley. An early domestic stained-glass narrative designed for Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co.
- **1862:** *The Good Women windows*, Red House. Female figures derived from Chaucerian and classical heroines; related designs were also translated into painted tiles.
- **1862:** *The New Jerusalem*, St Michael and All Angels, Lyndhurst. A multi-figure east window also known as *The Courts of Heaven*.
- **1862:** *Answers to Prayer*, St Michael and All Angels, Lyndhurst. A stained-glass window combining several biblical episodes.
- **1864:** *The Last Judgment*, St Michael and All Angels, Lyndhurst. A large stained-glass composition produced by Morris’s firm from Burne-Jones’s design.
- **1866:** *The Worship of the Lamb*, All Saints, Selsley. A major multi-light stained-glass scheme.
- **1868:** *The Last Judgment*, St Philip’s, Birmingham. A large west window; later conservation and wartime history belong to the manifestations, not to the original design alone.
- **1869:** *The Good Shepherd*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A stained-glass composition for Philip Webb’s church.
- **1873:** *The Tree of Jesse*, Christ Church Cathedral, Oxford. A large genealogical stained-glass window.
- **1874:** *The Song of Solomon windows*, St Helen’s, Darley Dale. A sequence of figural and narrative lights based on the biblical poem.
- **1874–1878:** *The Angels of Creation*. Stained-glass versions and variants of the Creation angels, adapted from Burne-Jones’s watercolour sequence.
- **1878:** *The Last Judgment*, St Philip’s, Birmingham. The east-window project, distinct from the cathedral’s earlier west window.
- **1880:** *The Annunciation*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A stained-glass treatment of a subject Burne-Jones repeatedly used in other media.
- **1881:** *The Pelican in Her Piety*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A stained-glass emblematic composition.
- **1882:** *The Good Shepherd*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A later window within the church’s extensive Morris & Co. glazing.
- **1885–1886:** *The Legend of St Frideswide*, Christ Church Cathedral, Oxford. A stained-glass narrative cycle.
- **1886:** *The Last Judgment*, St Mary the Virgin, Easthampstead. A large east window.
- **1887:** *The Adoration of the Magi*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A stained-glass composition later adapted for tapestry and the Birmingham watercolour *The Star of Bethlehem*.
- **1889:** *The Ascension*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A stained-glass window produced from Burne-Jones’s design.
- **1890:** *The Nativity*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A late window within the church’s larger decorative programme.
- **1891:** *The Days of Creation*, Manchester College, Oxford. A stained-glass sequence adapted from the earlier watercolours.
- **1891–1896:** *The Last Judgment*, St Philip’s, Birmingham. Burne-Jones’s south-transept window, completing a group of his major designs in the church.
- **1893:** *The Tree of Jesse*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A late stained-glass genealogy.
- **1896:** *The Crucifixion*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A late window designed for Morris & Co.
- **1896:** *The Ascension*, St Martin’s, Brampton. A companion late design.
- **Various dates:** *Apostles, saints, angels, virtues, sibyls, musicians, biblical figures, and secular heroines*. Hundreds of reusable cartoons supplied Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co. and Morris & Co. with a repertory installed in Britain and abroad. This is a body of design work rather than a single fixed series, and workshop repetition prevents a simple one-design/one-object census.

### Tapestry and embroidery designs

- **1870s–1890s:** *Figurative embroidery designs*. Burne-Jones supplied figures while William Morris and other collaborators supplied ornament, colour planning, and execution. Authorship belongs to the design project collectively rather than to every surviving stitched manifestation.
- **1881–1886:** *The Story of Troy*. A projected tapestry cycle developed through cartoons and studies; not all intended scenes were woven, so its primary status is project rather than completed ensemble.
- **1885:** *Pomona*. A tapestry figure designed by Burne-Jones with floral ornament by William Morris and woven by Morris & Co.
- **1885:** *Flora*. A companion tapestry design combining Burne-Jones’s figure with Morris workshop ornament.
- **1885:** *The Woodpecker*. A tapestry whose figure was designed by Burne-Jones and whose surrounding ornament and verse were supplied by William Morris.
- **1886:** *The Arming and Departure of the Knights*. An early large Arthurian tapestry design that anticipated the later Holy Grail cycle.
- **1886:** *Angeli Laudantes*. A tapestry of music-making angels designed by Burne-Jones and realized collaboratively by Morris & Co.
- **1887:** *The Adoration of the Magi*. A tapestry designed from Burne-Jones’s composition, with ornamental work by Morris and John Henry Dearle; later weavings are manifestations of the design rather than new Burne-Jones inventions.
- **1888:** *The Star of Bethlehem*. A tapestry adaptation of *The Adoration of the Magi* composition.
- **1890–1894:** *The Quest of the Holy Grail tapestries*. A cycle designed for Stanmore Hall by Burne-Jones, William Morris, and John Henry Dearle and woven at Merton Abbey. Burne-Jones devised the figures and narrative compositions, Morris and Dearle much of the ornament, and workshop weavers made the objects: the ensemble is a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1890–1894:** *The Knights of the Round Table Summoned to the Quest by the Strange Damsel*. The opening narrative tapestry of the Holy Grail cycle.
- **1890–1894:** *The Arming and Departure of the Knights*. The second major narrative in the Holy Grail cycle.
- **1890–1894:** *The Failure of Sir Launcelot to Enter the Chapel of the Holy Grail*. A narrative tapestry in the cycle.
- **1890–1894:** *The Failure of Sir Gawaine*. A narrative tapestry in the cycle.
- **1890–1894:** *The Ship*. A Holy Grail tapestry showing the chosen knights embarked upon their quest.
- **1890–1894:** *The Attainment: The Vision of the Holy Grail to Sir Galahad, Sir Bors, and Sir Percival*. The cycle’s culminating narrative tapestry.
- **1890–1894:** *Verdure panels for the Holy Grail series*. Armorial and landscape hangings completing the room scheme; their status is environmental and collaborative rather than that of independent figure pictures.

### Ceramic, furniture, metalwork, and miscellaneous designs

- **1861–1862:** *The Legend of Good Women tiles*. Designs of Chaucerian and classical heroines made for painted ceramic tiles; spellings and titles vary among cartoons, tiles, and later catalogues.
- **1861–1862:** *Cinderella tiles*. A six-tile narrative sequence designed for Morris’s firm.
- **1861–1862:** *Beauty and the Beast tiles*. A ceramic narrative sequence.
- **1861–1862:** *The Labours of the Months tiles*. Calendar designs, some by Burne-Jones and some involving other designers; individual attribution must therefore be retained rather than assigning the whole set indiscriminately to him.
- **1860s:** *Tristram and Isoude furniture panels*. Arthurian and romance subjects painted or designed for cabinets and other furniture made by Morris’s firm.
- **1860s–1890s:** *Painted furniture designs*. Cabinets, pianos, sideboards, and panels bearing Burne-Jones figures were normally collective productions involving separate makers and decorators.
- **1871:** *The Graham Piano*. A decorated piano made for William Graham, with painted imagery by Burne-Jones and an instrument made by a specialist manufacturer; it is both functioning object and collaborative decorative artwork.
- **1870s–1890s:** *Jewellery and metalwork designs*. A comparatively small body of designs for brooches, pendants, church plate, and decorative fittings, often executed by other craftspeople and sometimes known only through drawings.
- **1880s:** *The Prioress’s Tale cabinet*. Decorated furniture incorporating Burne-Jones’s Chaucerian imagery.
- **1890s:** *The Graham Piano lid and related Orpheus designs*. Musical and mythological decoration in which image, instrument, furniture, and performance context resist separation.

### Book illustration, illumination, and graphic publication

- **1858:** *The Defence of Guenevere and Other Poems*. Burne-Jones contributed a design to the visual conception of William Morris’s first book of poems, although the volume is not wholly his creation.
- **1862:** *The Summer Snow and Other Fairy Tales*. Designs prepared for a projected illustrated book by William Morris; the publication did not materialize as intended, so the surviving drawings document an unrealized book project.
- **1862:** *King Sigurd the Crusader*. An illustration for the periodical *Good Words*.
- **1863:** *The Dalziels’ Bible Gallery*. Burne-Jones designed biblical illustrations for the Dalziel brothers’ projected publication. Publication was delayed and radically altered, making this another project whose drawings and blocks have a clearer identity than the intended book.
- **1865:** *The Parable of the Sower*. A wood-engraved book illustration after Burne-Jones’s design.
- **1868–1870:** *The Earthly Paradise illustrations*. Numerous designs for William Morris’s poem-cycle, only some of which were translated into wood engraving; the intended fully illustrated edition remained unrealized.
- **1870:** *A Book of Verse*. Burne-Jones supplied illustrations to William Morris’s illuminated manuscript made for Georgiana Burne-Jones; the manuscript is a collaborative book-object rather than a printed edition.
- **1872:** *Love Is Enough*. Illustrative and ornamental contributions were contemplated for Morris’s book, but the projected extensive illustration was not realized; surviving designs belong to the project rather than to the published volume alone.
- **1870s:** *Rubáiyát of Omar Khayyám manuscripts*. Burne-Jones supplied figure miniatures for illuminated manuscripts written and decorated by William Morris. Individual copies differ, and the manuscripts are collaborative Gesamtkunstwerke.
- **1891–1896:** *The Works of Geoffrey Chaucer*. Burne-Jones designed the eighty-seven illustrations for the Kelmscott Press volume, translated into wood engraving by William Harcourt Hooper; William Morris designed the type, borders, initials, and page architecture. The “Kelmscott Chaucer” is therefore a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk, not simply an illustrated book by either man alone.
- **1894:** *The Tale of Beowulf*. Burne-Jones designed an illustration for the Kelmscott Press edition, but it was not used in the published book.
- **1897:** *The Story of Sigurd the Volsung and the Fall of the Niblungs*. Burne-Jones produced designs associated with the Kelmscott Press edition; the degree to which individual images reached publication varies.
- **1898:** *The Beginning of the World*. A posthumously issued portfolio of twenty-five wood engravings after Burne-Jones’s biblical designs. The printed manifestation appeared after his death and was selected and mediated by others, although the underlying designs were his.

### Theatre, pageantry, and ephemeral invention

- **Various dates:** *Costume and stage designs*. Drawings for dress, theatrical tableaux, and amateur performances survive, but many were situational inventions rather than stable, titled works.
- **1884:** *The Masque of Cupid*. Designs for a projected masque connected with Burne-Jones’s *Cupid and Psyche* imagery; the unrealized performance makes the project more securely identifiable than any complete manifestation.
- **1887:** *The Golden Legend tableaux*. Designs associated with staged or ceremonial presentations; attribution and the boundary between costume study, pageant direction, and autonomous drawing vary by sheet.
- **1890s:** *The Chariot of Love*. A vast, unfinished decorative painting conceived as a procession of Love. Numerous studies survive, but the intended final object was never completed, so the project itself is the work.

### Sources

- [Burne-Jones Catalogue Raisonné](https://www.eb-j.org/browse). The principal work-level source consulted: an evolving online catalogue covering paintings, drawings, sketchbooks, stained glass, mosaics, tapestries, embroidery, tiles, furniture, book illustration, juvenilia, copies, workshop realizations, and unfinished projects.
