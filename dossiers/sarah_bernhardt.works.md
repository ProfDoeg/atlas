## Works by Sarah Bernhardt

### Authored texts: memoir, fiction, travel writing, and theatrical protocol

- **1878:** *Dans les nuages: Impressions d’une chaise*. A first-person account of Bernhardt’s balloon ascent, combining travel narrative, comic observation, and autobiographical performance; it is a literary obra rather than a technical account of aeronautics. [Gallica’s Bernhardt bibliography](https://gallica.bnf.fr/selections/fr/html/litteratures/classiques-de-la-litterature-francaise-acces-par-ordre-alphabetique-dauteur-0)

- **1907:** *Ma double vie: Mémoires de Sarah Bernhardt*. An autobiography whose self-dramatization makes it both memoir and an extension of Bernhardt’s public persona; the English *My Double Life* and *Memories of My Life* are manifestations and adaptations of this obra, not additional works. [Bibliothèque nationale de France](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k9602592p), [French Ministry of Culture](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **1920:** *Petite Idole*. A novel about theatrical celebrity, published in English as *The Idol of Paris*; the translation is a later manifestation of the same fiction. [EBSCO biographical bibliography](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/sarah-bernhardt/)

- **c. 1920–1921:** *Joli Sosie*. A novel; catalogues disagree or remain imprecise about its publication year, so the broader range is retained. [BnF authority bibliography](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=AUT3&listeAffinages=FacEnLigne_gallica%3BFacFonctAuteur_0070&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=11891564&triResultParPage=0&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=), [Gallica](https://gallica.bnf.fr/selections/fr/html/litteratures/classiques-de-la-litterature-francaise-acces-par-ordre-alphabetique-dauteur-0)

- **1923, posthumous:** *L’Art du théâtre: la voix, le geste, la prononciation*. A protocol of acting—part treatise, part transmission of embodied practice—issued after Bernhardt’s death; its posthumous editorial form means that the published book should not be assumed to reproduce an authorially finalized manuscript without mediation. [BnF authority bibliography](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=AUT3&listeAffinages=FacNatDoc_a%3BFacLocal_Lcl1CRdj&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=11891564&triResultParPage=0&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=), [bibliographic record cited by the standard biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Bernhardt#Bibliography)

### Plays and dramatic adaptations

- **1888:** *L’Aveu*. A one-act prose drama written by Bernhardt and produced at the Odéon; it is an authored play, distinct from the roles she created in plays by others. [BnF authority bibliography](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=AUT3&listeAffinages=FacEnLigne_gallica%3BFacFonctAuteur_0070&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=11891564&triResultParPage=0&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=), [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/3125-bernhardt-sarah-rosine-bernard)

- **1907:** *Adrienne Lecouvreur*. A six-act dramatic reworking associated with Bernhardt; sources describe it as her new version of the Lecouvreur story, but its relationship to the earlier Scribe–Legouvé play makes “adaptation” safer than wholly independent authorship. [Bernhardt works bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Bernhardt#Books_by_Bernhardt)

- **1909; published 1911:** *Un cœur d’homme*. A four-act play first staged at the Théâtre des Arts on 22 December 1909; 1911 is the publication date often attached to the printed text, not necessarily the work’s date of creation. [BnF authority bibliography](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=AUT3&listeAffinages=FacEnLigne_gallica%3BFacFonctAuteur_0070&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=11891564&triResultParPage=0&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=)

- **1916:** *Du théâtre au champ d’honneur*. A one-act wartime play created by Bernhardt; its purpose is overtly interventionist and patriotic, so it approaches a manifesto enacted as theatre rather than merely describing the war. [BnF catalogue record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/changerPage.do?afficheRegroup=false&affinageActif=false&critereRecherche=&index=AUT3&listeAffinages=&motRecherche=&nbPage=3&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=11891564&pageEnCours=2&triResultParPage=0&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&trouverDans=&typeNotice=)

### Sculpture: established works and surviving manifestations

- **c. 1869:** *Portrait medallion of Bernhardt’s aunt*—variously identified as Aunt Rosine or “Aunt Breeck.” Her reported first modeled work; the object is unlocated and even the sitter’s name varies in retrospective accounts, so its status rests on documentary testimony. [Waters, *Artists of the Nineteenth Century and Their Works*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d8/Artists_of_the_nineteenth_century_and_their_works._A_handbook_containing_two_thousand_and_fifty_biographical_sketches_%28IA_cu31924102145087%29.pdf), [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/)

- **c. 1874:** *Bust of Mlle Emmy de \*\*\**. A portrait bust described by Bernhardt but now unlocated; the sitter’s suppressed surname is part of the historical record and should not be silently completed. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1874:** *Portrait de Mlle B. G. …*. A portrait sculpture exhibited at the Salon of 1874; the abbreviated catalogue title does not permit a secure identification of its sitter. [Mason exhibition appendix](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1874:** *Buste de Mlle Hocquigny*. A marble portrait bust, recorded in a private collection; the surname also appears in variant modern spellings. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf), [documented market record](https://www.mutualart.com/Artist/Sarah-Bernhardt/1B1566E7F1D5FFD8)

- **c. 1874–1876:** *Bust of Adolphe de Rothschild*. A repeatedly attempted portrait project which Bernhardt said she destroyed after three unsatisfactory versions; it survives, if at all, as an abandoned project rather than a finished obra. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1875:** *Portrait de Mlle R. Bernhardt* / *Bust of Régina Bernhardt*. A portrait of Bernhardt’s sister exhibited at the 1875 Salon; the original is unlocated, while later exhibition references may describe the same work or another manifestation. [Mason exhibition appendix](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1875:** *Bust of Miss Moulton*. A child portrait modeled from life and recorded by Bernhardt; Mason located it in a private collection. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1875:** *Hermione*—formerly or alternatively unidentified. A fantastical female bust signed and dated 1875; it was absent from the documented oeuvre and was only tentatively identified from its imagery with Hermione in Racine’s *Andromaque*, so both title and subject remain attributional proposals. [Sotheby’s catalogue](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2018/19th-20th-century-sculpture-l18230/lot.68.html)

- **1876:** *Portrait de M. D. …*. A portrait bust exhibited at the Salon of 1876; the abbreviated sitter remains unidentified. [Mason exhibition appendix](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1876:** *Après la tempête*. A sculptural group of a Breton woman holding a drowned child, known through a full-size plaster and a marble manifestation; the plaster is lost or unlocated, while the marble survives at the National Museum of Women in the Arts. [National Museum of Women in the Arts](https://nmwa.org/art/collection/apres-la-tempete/), [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/)

- **1876:** *Self-portrait as Berthe in La Fille de Roland*. A terracotta self-portrait in role: simultaneously portrait, performance residue, and sculpture rather than a neutral likeness. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **c. 1876–1877:** *Buste de Henry de la Pommeraye*. A portrait bust documented in accounts of Bernhardt’s sculpture, but not securely located. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **c. 1876–1878:** *Buste de Georges Clairin*. A portrait of the painter and Bernhardt’s close collaborator; its present location and precise date remain uncertain. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **c. 1876–1878:** *Buste de William Busnach*. A portrait bust of the dramatist, documented retrospectively but now unlocated. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1877:** *Le Fou et la Mort*. A literary sculptural group derived from Victor Hugo’s *Le Roi s’amuse*, representing Triboulet with his daughter’s skull; bronze and terracotta manifestations survive in Dijon and Mont-de-Marsan. [French Ministry of Culture](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **1877–1900, unfinished:** *Médée*. A life-size statue visible unfinished in an 1877 studio view and again, still apparently incomplete, in a photograph published in 1900; it is best classified as a long-lived project, not silently promoted to a completed sculpture. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1877–1878:** *Buste d’Émile de Girardin*. A portrait bust produced in full-size and reduced terracotta and bronze manifestations; different casts are manifestations of one modeled obra, not separate inventions. [Musée d’Orsay](https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/emile-de-girardin-68087), [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/)

- **1878:** *Buste de Louise Abbéma*. A marble portrait bust of the painter, with other material manifestations reported; the marble in the Musée d’Orsay is the principal surviving version. [Mason thesis record](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/), [French Ministry of Culture](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **1878–1879:** *Self-portrait bust*. A conventional self-portrait photographed in Bernhardt’s studio and possibly sold to Grand Duke Constantine of Russia; the claimed sale and identification are not independently confirmed, and the bust is unlocated. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1878–1879:** *Le Chant*. A colossal allegorical figure made for Charles Garnier’s façade of the Opéra de Monte-Carlo; as architectural sculpture it is part of a larger ensemble and resists separation into an autonomous gallery object. [Christie’s catalogue](https://www.christies.com/lot/sarah-bernhardt-1844-1923-le-chant-6538705/), [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/)

- **1879:** *Bellone enfant*. A child bust exhibited in London; Mason cautions that it may be the work later called *Mars enfant*, so the two titles may not denote two distinct sculptures. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/3/uk-bl_ethos_441203.pdf)

- **c. 1879–1880:** *Primavera*. A sculpture exhibited during Bernhardt’s 1880 American exhibition; its present location is not established. [Mason exhibition appendix](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **c. 1880:** *Statuette de Sarah Bernhardt*. A full-length self-representation offered during Bernhardt’s American exhibition; it converts her performed public identity into a saleable sculptural object and is now unlocated. [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1880:** *Ophélie*. A bas-relief of Shakespeare’s drowned heroine made in plaster, bronze, and possibly as many as three marble copies; uncertain edition and copy counts make those objects manifestations of one modeled work rather than securely enumerable separate obras. [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf), [Sotheby’s catalogue](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2017/erotic-passion-desire-l17322/lot.1.html)

- **1880:** *Encrier fantastique* / *Autoportrait en chimère*. A functional inkwell, symbolic self-portrait, and sculpture in one—effectively a small Gesamtkunstwerk refusing the division between decorative art, portraiture, theatrical persona, and utility. Plaster, bronze, and reduced casts survive, but the size of the bronze edition is uncertain. [Paris Musées](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/petit-palais/oeuvres/encrier-fantastique-autoportrait-en-chimere), [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1881:** *Portrait de Coquelin cadet*. A portrait bust exhibited at the Salon of 1881; surviving correspondence shows that sittings had been interrupted, but the exhibited work was treated as complete. [Mason exhibition appendix and discussion](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **c. 1880s:** *Buste de Blanche Baretta*. A portrait bust recorded among Bernhardt’s principal sculptures but presently unlocated; its date is not secure enough to narrow further. [Pierre Berton, *Sarah Bernhardt as I Knew Her*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59511/pg59511-images.html)

- **c. 1880s:** *Buste de Maurice Bernhardt*. A portrait of Bernhardt’s son, documented in early biographical inventories but unlocated and insecurely dated. [Pierre Berton, *Sarah Bernhardt as I Knew Her*](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59511/pg59511-images.html)

- **1885:** *Mars enfant*. A bust of the child-god wearing a chimera-crested helmet, exhibited in marble and later issued in polychrome ceramic manifestations with Edmond Lachenal; it may be identical to the earlier *Bellone enfant*. [Christie’s catalogue](https://onlineonly.christies.com/s/maitres-anciens-peintures-sculptures-dessins-orfevrerie-online/sarah-bernhardt-1844-1923-et-edmond-lachenal-1855-1948-101/277809), [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/)

- **1888:** *Henriette*. A marble sculpture exhibited at the Salon; its relationship to an earlier work of the same or a related title is uncertain. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/3/uk-bl_ethos_441203.pdf)

- **c. 1888–1889, destroyed:** *Love and Death* / *un amour et la mort*. A clay group which Graham Robertson said he helped Bernhardt destroy; another studio account may describe the same object, but no surviving work confirms either title. It resists classification as a finished work and is best retained as a destroyed project. [Mason, *Making Love/Making Work*](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **c. 1889:** *Portrait funéraire de Jacques Damala*. A marble funerary portrait of Bernhardt’s husband, now at the Metropolitan Museum of Art; it is both intimate memorial and sculptural portrait. [Metropolitan Museum of Art collection search](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search?q=Sarah%20Bernhardt%20Damala), [French Ministry of Culture overview](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **c. 1891–1897:** *Buste de Simone Bernhardt* / *Tête d’enfant*. A child portrait probably representing Bernhardt’s granddaughter Simone; the identification of the “child’s head” exhibited in 1897 and Chicago’s 1893 “bust of a child” with this work remains probable, not certain. [Mason exhibition appendix](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1897; bronze 1900:** *Buste de Victorien Sardou*. A plaster portrait exhibited at the 1897 Salon and subsequently manifested in bronze; the Petit Palais preserves a bronze version. [Mason exhibition appendix](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf), [French Ministry of Culture](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **c. 1900:** *Algues*. A decorative bronze formed from marine life collected or molded at Belle-Île; its union of found natural form, casting, and applied art makes “sculpture” accurate but incomplete. [French Ministry of Culture](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **c. 1900:** *Poisson*. One of Bernhardt’s marine sculptures shown at the 1900 Exposition universelle; surviving documentation does not establish how many differently titled fish objects belonged to the series. [Mason exhibition appendix](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **c. 1900:** *Lion de mer*. A marine decorative sculpture reportedly acquired by the comtesse de Béarn; its precise form and present location are uncertain. [French Ministry of Culture overview](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **1900:** *Dague aux algues*. A sculpture-dagger shown at the Exposition universelle: functional object, weapon, jewelry-like ornament, and sculpture are inseparable, making it another small Gesamtkunstwerk. [French Ministry of Culture](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah), [Paris Musées](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/petit-palais/oeuvres/dague)

- **1900:** *Buste d’Edmond Rostand*. Mason identifies this as Bernhardt’s last known completed sculpture; the original is unlocated, and a modern stone reconstruction from photographs is another artist’s work, not a surviving Bernhardt original. [Mason catalogue study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **1904 or earlier:** *Bust of a Child*. A sandstone sculpture made with or translated into ceramic by Edmond Lachenal and exhibited at the 1904 St Louis exposition; it may overlap with *Mars enfant* or a portrait of Simone, and the exhibition title does not settle the identity. [Official St Louis exposition catalogue](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/Official_catalogue_of_exhibitors._Universal_exposition%2C_St._Louis%2C_U.S.A._1904_%28IA_officialcatalogu00loui_1%29.pdf)

- **after 1914, unfinished and uncertain:** *Tomb figures of Maurice, Simone, and Lysiane*. Cornelia Otis Skinner reported that the wheelchair-bound Bernhardt began modeling family figures for her tomb, but supplied neither date nor source; none decorates the actual tomb. These should be retained as uncertain projects, not accepted finished works. [Mason’s critical catalogue discussion](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

### Painting and drawing

- **1875:** *Portrait or study of a woman*—fixed title unknown. An oil dated 1875 has appeared in documented sales, but the available record does not establish an authorial title; imposing one would convert a description into a false title. [Drouot catalogue](https://www.gazette-drouot.com/telechargement/catalogue?venteId=8763)

- **1878:** *La Jeune Fille et la Mort*. A large Salon painting joining theatrical allegory and easel painting; sources differ between “young girl” and “young woman” in translation, but these are descriptions of the same canvas rather than separate works. Its present location is not established. [French Ministry of Culture overview](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah), [Pierre Berton](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59511/pg59511-images.html)

- **c. 1878–1879:** *La Marchande des palmes*. A large painting documented in photographs of Bernhardt’s studio; it has sometimes been confused in captions with sculpture visible nearby, illustrating how her studio practice resists clean separation by medium. [Mason image catalogue](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/3/uk-bl_ethos_441203.pdf)

- **1880:** *Original Sketch*—no stable title recovered. A drawing printed in the authorized souvenir catalogue for Bernhardt’s American art exhibition; without the catalogue image or an authorial caption, “Original Sketch” is a catalogue description, not a secure title. [Contemporary catalogue discussed in Mason](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/4710/1/uk_bl_ethos_441203_vol.2.pdf)

- **c. 1880s–1900s:** *Belle-Île landscapes and seascapes*. A body of paintings rather than one securely enumerable obra: Bernhardt painted multiple coastal views, but no catalogue raisonné establishes a complete title-by-title corpus, and many remain privately held or unlocated. The resistance to enumeration is a condition of the evidence, not evidence that the paintings did not exist. [Petit Palais exhibition dossier](https://www.petitpalais.paris.fr/sites/default/files/content/press-kits/dp_sarahbernhardt_en.pdf), [French Ministry of Culture overview](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **c. 1885:** *Autoportrait*. An oil self-portrait known through the art market; “1885” is generally approximate rather than securely documented by an exhibition record. [Artnet artist record](https://www.artnet.fr/artistes/sarah-bernhardt/)

- **1910:** *Autoportrait*. An oil self-portrait now in the Fondation Bemberg, Toulouse; it is a distinct painted obra from the earlier sculptural self-portraits and chimera-inkwell. [French Ministry of Culture overview](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

- **1922:** *Nature morte aux pêches*. A late still-life painting preserved by the Musée Carnavalet. [French Ministry of Culture overview](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/musees/Les-musees-en-France/les-collections-des-musees-de-france/decouvrir-les-collections/les-femmes-artistes-sortent-de-leur-reserve/icones/Bernhardt-Sarah)

### Recorded voice: performance manifestations

- **1880:** *Menlo Park phonograph recording*. An experimental recording made during Bernhardt’s visit to Edison; it is a vanished or inaccessible performance trace rather than an authored verbal text, and surviving descriptions do not establish its contents securely. [Henry Ford collection record](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/269585)

- **1902:** *La Chanson d’Eviradnus*. A Pathé cylinder of Bernhardt reciting Victor Hugo: the poem is Hugo’s obra, while Bernhardt made the vocal performance embodied in this recording. [Marie-Madeleine Mervant-Roux, “Peut-on entendre Sarah Bernhardt?”](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-societes-et-representations-2013-1-page-165?lang=en)

- **1902:** *Le Lac*. A Pathé cylinder of a text by Maurice Bernhardt; Bernhardt’s creation is the recorded interpretation, not the underlying text. A later Gramophone manifestation was also issued. [Mervant-Roux discography](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-societes-et-representations-2013-1-page-165?lang=en), [BnF record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb37888774s)

- **1902:** *Phèdre*—excerpt. A Pathé cylinder of Racine’s tragedy; Bernhardt’s vocal performance survives as a mechanically reproducible manifestation of an otherwise ephemeral role. [Mervant-Roux discography](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-societes-et-representations-2013-1-page-165?lang=en)

### Film performances and collaborative moving-image manifestations

- **1900:** *Le Duel d’Hamlet*. A filmed fragment of Bernhardt’s stage performance, created for the Phono-Cinéma-Théâtre; the accompanying sound did not survive, so the extant film is an incomplete manifestation of an intended sound-and-image ensemble. Bernhardt performed but did not solely author or direct the film. [Mervant-Roux](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-societes-et-representations-2013-1-page-165?lang=en), [Victoria Duckett context](https://www.press.uillinois.edu/books/?id=p080375)

- **1908:** *La Tosca*. A short filmed performance based on Sardou’s play; it is a collaborative screen object rather than a Bernhardt-authored drama. [David W. Menefee, *Sarah Bernhardt in the Theatre of Films and Sound Recordings*](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

- **1911:** *La Dame aux camélias* / *Camille*. A screen manifestation of Bernhardt’s Marguerite Gautier; Bernhardt created the starring performance, while the play and film were made collaboratively by others. [BFI record](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/ee2ea96c-4321-53c6-a13c-9771f27190a6/la-dame-aux-camelias), [Menefee filmography](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

- **1912:** *Adrienne Lecouvreur*. A film performance connected to Bernhardt’s own dramatic reworking of the subject, but still a collaborative film rather than a wholly authored cinematic obra. [Menefee filmography](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

- **1912:** *Les Amours de la reine Élisabeth* / *Queen Elizabeth*. A feature-length screen vehicle built around Bernhardt’s performance; its international versions are distributions or manifestations, not separate works by her. [Menefee filmography](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

- **1912:** *Sarah Bernhardt à Belle-Île*. A nonfiction film of Bernhardt in her chosen landscape; it lies between portrait, publicity, travel image, and self-presentation, and should not be treated simply as a fictional acting role. [Menefee filmography](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

- **1913:** *Jeanne Doré*. A filmed stage melodrama centered on Bernhardt’s performance. [Menefee filmography](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

- **1915:** *Mères françaises*. A wartime propaganda film in which Bernhardt played a French nurse or mother figure; the performance acts politically and therefore approaches a cinematic manifesto, though its authorship is collective. [Victoria Duckett’s account summarized in Bernhardt’s film history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Bernhardt#Motion_pictures)

- **1915:** *Ceux de chez nous*. Sacha Guitry’s filmed portrait includes Bernhardt as herself; her contribution is a performed self-manifestation, not authorship of the documentary. [Menefee filmography](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

- **1921:** *Daniel*. A film known incompletely, including a documented death scene; Bernhardt’s performance survives principally through fragmentary evidence. [Menefee filmography](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

- **1923, unfinished:** *La Voyante*. Bernhardt began or prepared this film but died during production; the project was completed using other arrangements, so her intended performance is an unfinished project rather than a complete Bernhardt manifestation. [Menefee filmography](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html)

### The performed and directed theatre as a resistant body of work

- **1862–1922:** *Sarah Bernhardt’s stage performances*. Menefee’s performance chronology records the roles, tours, revivals, and changing versions, but these cannot honestly be relabeled as hundreds of authored “works by Sarah Bernhardt”: the plays belong to their dramatists, while her interpretations were ephemeral creations renewed at each performance. Collectively they form her principal obra—a body of embodied work rather than a shelf of texts. [David W. Menefee, *Sarah Bernhardt in the Theatre of Films and Sound Recordings*](https://www.kriso.lt/sarah-bernhardt-theatre-films-sound-recordings-db-9780786438822.html), [Comédie-Française](https://comedie-francaise.fr/artistes/sarah-bernhardt)

- **1893–1899:** *Direction of the Théâtre de la Renaissance*. Bernhardt’s production, casting, visual supervision, acting, and institutional direction formed a collaborative theatrical project; individual plays were generally written by others, so the theatre’s repertoire should not be converted into an author bibliography. [Petit Palais exhibition dossier](https://www.petitpalais.paris.fr/sites/default/files/content/press-kits/dp_sarahbernhardt_en.pdf)

- **1899–1923:** *Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt*. Her renamed and reshaped theatre joined repertory, star persona, costumes, sets, touring, publicity, and management: a sustained theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk and institution rather than one bounded work. Authorship was necessarily distributed among dramatists, designers, actors, craftspeople, and Bernhardt as director-star. [Comédie-Française](https://comedie-francaise.fr/artistes/sarah-bernhardt), [Petit Palais exhibition dossier](https://www.petitpalais.paris.fr/sites/default/files/content/press-kits/dp_sarahbernhardt_en.pdf)
