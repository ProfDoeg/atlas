## Works by Elizebeth Smith Friedman

### Cryptanalytic protocols, systems, and operational corpora

- **1917:** *Methods for the Reconstruction of Primary Alphabets*. Riverbank Publication no. 21, coauthored with William F. Friedman, is a technical protocol for reconstructing substitution-cipher alphabets rather than a general book about cryptography; the joint authorship is established, although the precise division of labor is not. ([NSA](https://www.nsa.gov/History/Cryptologic-History/Historical-Figures/Historical-Figures-View/Article/1623028/elizebeth-s-friedman/))

- **1917–1919:** *Riverbank codebreaking and code-construction work*. This was a collaborative body of cryptanalytic solutions, secure systems, and instructional work made for United States agencies during the First World War; surviving accounts do not reliably separate Elizebeth’s individual products from William Friedman’s or the Riverbank Cipher Department’s collective production. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1927–1930:** *Reports on cryptanalytic work*. An archival corpus of reports, intelligence summaries, intercepted messages, decipherments, code notes, and memoranda made during Friedman’s work against liquor smugglers; it resists treatment as a single titled work because the finding aid describes operational files rather than a publication. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1930:** *Proposal for a Coast Guard cryptanalytic unit*. An institutional project and administrative protocol that helped turn a small codebreaking operation into the unit Friedman subsequently recruited, trained, and directed; the archival description supplies no fuller fixed title. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1931–1932:** *Decipherments*. Two annual archival corpora of solved illicit communications. These are made texts, but not autonomous “works” in the ordinary bibliographical sense: their form and meaning belong to continuing intelligence operations. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1931–1939:** *Cryptanalytic case files*. Friedman produced decipherments, notes, memoranda, reports, exhibits, and expert evidence for the *I’m Alone*, Havana-smuggling, Holmwood, Ezra, Lew Kim Yuen, Gordon Lim, and Ching Chun Hee cases; the archive sometimes identifies individual documents, but it does not establish that every item in each case file was written by her. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1934:** *Informal Journal of Conexco Smuggling Activities*. An operational journal preserved under this archival title; it records an investigation rather than presenting itself as a finished literary work. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1939–1945:** *Axis clandestine-communications solutions*. A collaborative cryptanalytic corpus encompassing German and other clandestine traffic, including machine and manual systems; Friedman led the technical work, but individual solutions belonged to Unit 387/OP-20-GU and cannot responsibly be converted into a list of individually authored works. The unit reportedly solved more than 8,500 messages in 1940–45. ([National Coast Guard Museum](https://nationalcoastguardmuseum.org/articles/behind-the-codes/))

- **1940–1945:** *History of Coast Guard Unit #387 (Cryptanalytic Unit), 1940–1945*. This classified technical history is sometimes catalogued under Leonard T. Jones and Elizebeth Friedman, but contemporary archival descriptions call it anonymous, while Friedman herself recalled that Commander Jones was writing such a study. It should therefore not be silently claimed as her obra, even though it documents work she directed and may embody contributions from her and her team. ([NSA oral-history notes](https://media.defense.gov/2021/Jul/15/2002763461/-1/-1/0/NSA-OH-1976-22-EFRIEDMAN.PDF); [official historical source description](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D-PURL-gpo52785/pdf/GOVPUB-D-PURL-gpo52785.pdf))

### Books, articles, and essays

- **1957:** *The Shakespearean Ciphers Examined: An Analysis of Cryptographic Systems Used as Evidence That Some Author Other Than William Shakespeare Wrote the Plays Commonly Attributed to Him*. A jointly authored scholarly book by Elizebeth and William Friedman, developed from their prize-winning manuscript and made to test—and reject—claimed cryptographic proofs of alternative Shakespearean authorship. The archive does not divide responsibility chapter by chapter. ([Folger Shakespeare Library](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/52477); [Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1957:** *Why Shakespeare Does for Me*. An article or personal essay preserved under this title in Friedman’s papers; the finding aid records its existence but does not identify its original periodical or publication status. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1959:** *Acrostics, Anagrams, and Chaucer*. A jointly authored scholarly article by Elizebeth and William Friedman, published in *Philological Quarterly* 38, pp. 1–20; it is both critical essay and cryptographic demonstration, including a deliberately constructed anagrammatic message. ([UTSA Chaucer bibliography](https://chaucer.lib.utsa.edu/omeka/items/browse?collection=8&page=14&sort_field=Item+Type+Metadata%2CDate); [Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

### Unfinished projects and manuscripts

- **circa 1940:** *Manuscript detailing early career*. An unpublished autobiographical manuscript identified by description rather than a fixed title. Its incompleteness and archival status make it closer to testimony-in-progress than to a finished memoir. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1959:** *Foreword to an uncompleted work*. A surviving foreword whose intended larger work was never completed; neither the finding aid nor the surviving description supplies a secure title, so the project cannot be named more precisely. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **date not fixed:** *Book on Codes and Ciphers*. An unfinished manuscript by Friedman surviving in five chapters. It is a project rather than a completed book, and its archival descriptive title should not be confused with William Friedman’s separately published encyclopedia article “Codes and Ciphers.” ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1919–1967:** *Alphabet Book*. A long-duration project represented by research materials in two parts and typed portions of a manuscript. No evidence in the finding aid establishes a final title, finished state, or publication. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1966:** *Draft of ESF Memoirs*. An unpublished memoir draft. It remains an unfinished autobiographical project rather than a stable, author-approved book. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

### Research projects without completed works

- **1920s–1960s:** *Voynich Manuscript investigation*. A decades-long collaborative project with William Friedman, represented by notes, correspondence, and research materials rather than a completed decipherment. The project resisted becoming a successful “solution”; the Friedmans’ provisional conclusion instead appears indirectly through the cryptographic construction in “Acrostics, Anagrams, and Chaucer.” ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf); [Folger exhibition documentation](https://folgerpedia.folger.edu/Decoding_the_Renaissance_exhibition_material))

- **date not fixed:** *Beale Treasure investigation*. A personal cryptanalytic research project preserved as “Beale Treasure Material”; the archive does not identify a completed report or defensible solution. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **date not fixed:** *Maya and Inca writing investigations*. Research into ancient writing systems preserved principally as notes and collected materials. The finding aid does not establish that these investigations became completed, separately titled works. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **date not fixed:** *Pearl Harbor attack investigation*. A documentary research project comprising extracts and copied correspondence; its archival arrangement does not support calling the assembled file a finished authored work. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1960s–1970s:** *William F. Friedman library and bibliography*. A curatorial and bibliographical project through which Elizebeth helped assemble, describe, and prepare the Friedmans’ cryptological library for transfer to the George C. Marshall Foundation. This is an obra of arrangement and preservation rather than a conventionally authored text. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf); [NSA biographical record](https://www.nsa.gov/History/Cryptologic-History/Historical-Figures/Historical-Figures-View/Article/1623028/elizebeth-s-friedman/))

### Oral, occasional, and private texts

- **1934:** *NBC interview*. A radio manifestation co-created with an interviewer, not a solely authored text; the archive preserves it as part of Friedman’s media record. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **date not fixed:** *Poems*. An archival group of poems, apparently private or unpublished. Because the finding aid supplies neither individual titles nor dates, they must remain one incompletely described corpus rather than invented separate entries. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **date not fixed:** *Cryptographic Messages*. A group of private or occasional coded compositions. They hover between protocol, puzzle, and social act, and the finding aid does not enumerate or title the individual messages. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **date not fixed:** *Funnies*. Humorous pieces preserved under Friedman’s initials in the family papers. The archival label establishes a corpus but not stable individual titles, dates, or publication status. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1973:** *Oral-history interview with Forest C. Pogue*. Recorded autobiographical testimony made jointly by speaker and interviewer; it is a historical source and a performed manifestation rather than a conventionally authored book. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1975:** *Taped interviews of March 25, April 1, and April 22*. Three separately transcribed oral-history manifestations in which Friedman shaped a retrospective account of her life and work. Their dialogic production resists exclusive authorship. ([Marshall Foundation finding aid](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Friedman_Elizabeth.pdf))

- **1976:** *NSA oral-history interview*. A recorded institutional interview later issued as NSA document OH-1976-22. It is co-produced testimony, and its annotations explicitly preserve uncertainty over the authorship of the Unit 387 history. ([National Security Agency](https://media.defense.gov/2021/Jul/15/2002763461/-1/-1/0/NSA-OH-1976-22-EFRIEDMAN.PDF))
