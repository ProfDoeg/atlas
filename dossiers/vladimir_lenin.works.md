## Works by Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov

### Books and book-length studies

- **1893:** *New Economic Developments in Peasant Life*. An economic study written under the pseudonym V. Ilyin; its surviving manuscript is incomplete, so the obra resists treatment as a finished book.
- **1894:** *What the “Friends of the People” Are and How They Fight the Social-Democrats*. A polemical study originally issued clandestinely in three mimeographed parts; the second part has not survived.
- **1896–1899:** *The Development of Capitalism in Russia: The Process of the Formation of a Home Market for Large-Scale Industry*. A systematic economic monograph, published in 1899 under the name Vladimir Ilyin.
- **1897:** *The Tasks of the Russian Social-Democrats*. A programmatic pamphlet written in exile.
- **1897:** *The New Factory Law*. A pamphlet explaining and attacking the law of June 2, 1897.
- **1899:** *A Protest by Russian Social-Democrats*. A manifesto drafted by Lenin and adopted by seventeen exiled Marxists; it is a collective political act as well as an authored text.
- **1899:** *A Draft Programme of Our Party*. A project for a party programme rather than an adopted programme.
- **1899:** *A Retrograde Trend in Russian Social-Democracy*. An unfinished polemical article, first published posthumously.
- **1901:** *The Persecutors of the Zemstvo and the Hannibals of Liberalism*. A political pamphlet.
- **1901–1902:** *What Is to Be Done? Burning Questions of Our Movement*. A book-length intervention on revolutionary organization, written in 1901 and published in 1902.
- **1902:** *Letter to a Comrade on Our Organisational Tasks*. An instructional political letter that circulated as a pamphlet; in function it approaches a protocol.
- **1902–1903:** *To the Rural Poor*. A popular explanatory pamphlet about the Social-Democratic programme and the peasant question.
- **1904:** *One Step Forward, Two Steps Back: The Crisis in Our Party*. A book-length polemic and analysis of the Russian Social-Democratic Labour Party’s Second Congress.
- **1904:** *The Zemstvo Campaign and Iskra’s Plan*. A tactical pamphlet.
- **1905:** *Two Tactics of Social-Democracy in the Democratic Revolution*. A programmatic book-length polemic.
- **1905:** *The Agrarian Programme of the Liberals*. A pamphlet on liberal land policy.
- **1906:** *The Victory of the Cadets and the Tasks of the Workers’ Party*. A political pamphlet.
- **1906:** *The Revision of the Agrarian Programme of the Workers’ Party*. A programmatic pamphlet.
- **1906:** *Report on the Unity Congress of the R.S.D.L.P.*. A delegate’s report expanded into a pamphlet.
- **1907:** *The Agrarian Programme of Social-Democracy in the First Russian Revolution, 1905–1907*. A major study written in 1907; publication was interrupted when the printer’s sheets were confiscated, and the complete work appeared only after Lenin’s death.
- **1907:** *The St. Petersburg Elections and the Hypocrisy of the Thirty-One Mensheviks*. An electoral polemic.
- **1907:** *The Social-Democratic Election Campaign in St. Petersburg*. A pamphlet on electoral tactics.
- **1907:** *The Election Results in St. Petersburg*. A pamphlet analyzing voting returns.
- **1907:** *The Crisis of Menshevism*. A factional pamphlet.
- **1907:** *Preface to the Russian Translation of Karl Marx’s Letters to Dr. Kugelmann*. A substantial interpretive essay attached to another author’s correspondence rather than an independent book.
- **1908:** *Marxism and Revisionism*. A theoretical essay written for the twenty-fifth anniversary of Marx’s death.
- **1908:** *Materialism and Empirio-Criticism: Critical Comments on a Reactionary Philosophy*. A book-length philosophical polemic, published in 1909 under the pseudonym V. Ilyin.
- **1910:** *Notes of a Publicist*. A factional-political study.
- **1913:** *The Three Sources and Three Component Parts of Marxism*. A concise expository article rather than a treatise.
- **1913:** *Karl Marx: A Brief Biographical Sketch with an Exposition of Marxism*. An encyclopaedia article written in 1914 for the *Granat Encyclopaedic Dictionary*; its familiar English title represents the later independent presentation of that commissioned reference work.
- **1914:** *The Right of Nations to Self-Determination*. A theoretical and polemical study published serially.
- **1915:** *Socialism and War: The Attitude of the R.S.D.L.P. Towards the War*. A pamphlet written jointly with Grigory Zinoviev; it must not be treated as Lenin’s sole-authored obra.
- **1915:** *The Collapse of the Second International*. A book-length political article issued in the journal *Kommunist*.
- **1915:** *The Revolutionary Proletariat and the Right of Nations to Self-Determination*. A programmatic thesis-like article.
- **1916:** *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism: A Popular Outline*. A book written principally in the first half of 1916 and first published in 1917; the title of its earliest edition differed slightly from the now-standard title.
- **1916:** *The Discussion on Self-Determination Summed Up*. A theoretical polemic.
- **1916:** *A Caricature of Marxism and Imperialist Economism*. A long polemic, unfinished in the sense that its projected final section was not written.
- **1917:** *Letters on Tactics*. A pamphlet comprising two political letters.
- **1917:** *The Tasks of the Proletariat in Our Revolution*. A draft platform or project for a proletarian party; it was written in April but published only in September 1917.
- **1917:** *Political Parties in Russia and the Tasks of the Proletariat*. A comparative political pamphlet.
- **1917:** *Lessons of the Revolution*. A historical-political essay issued as a pamphlet.
- **1917:** *The Impending Catastrophe and How to Combat It*. A programme of emergency economic measures; as an intervention directed toward action, it has affinities with both manifesto and protocol.
- **1917:** *Can the Bolsheviks Retain State Power?*. A pamphlet arguing for the practical possibility of Bolshevik government.
- **1917:** *The State and Revolution: The Marxist Theory of the State and the Tasks of the Proletariat in the Revolution*. A book written in hiding. Its projected seventh chapter was never written, making the published obra an explicitly unfinished project.
- **1918:** *The Immediate Tasks of the Soviet Government*. A programmatic essay on administration, labour discipline, and economic reconstruction.
- **1918:** *“Left-Wing” Childishness and the Petty-Bourgeois Mentality*. A pamphlet-length polemic.
- **1918:** *The Proletarian Revolution and the Renegade Kautsky*. A book-length polemic written after the October Revolution.
- **1919:** *A Great Beginning*. A pamphlet interpreting communist subbotniks as a new form of labour.
- **1919:** *Economics and Politics in the Era of the Dictatorship of the Proletariat*. A theoretical article intended for the second anniversary of Soviet power.
- **1920:** *“Left-Wing” Communism—an Infantile Disorder*. A book written for delegates to the Communist International’s Second Congress.
- **1920:** *The Tax in Kind*. A pamphlet explaining the transition toward the New Economic Policy, written and published in 1921 despite the conventional association with Lenin’s 1920–21 policy turn.
- **1922:** *On the Significance of Militant Materialism*. A programmatic philosophical article.
- **1923:** *How We Should Reorganise the Workers’ and Peasants’ Inspection*. A late policy article dictated during Lenin’s illness.
- **1923:** *Better Fewer, but Better*. Lenin’s last published article, a project for reorganizing the Soviet administrative apparatus.

### Manifestos, programmes, theses, declarations, and political protocols

- **1895:** *Draft and Explanation of a Programme for the Social-Democratic Party*. An unfinished programme project composed in prison; its explanatory portion is incomplete.
- **1900:** *Draft of a Declaration of the Editorial Board of Iskra and Zarya*. A collective publishing manifesto drafted by Lenin.
- **1902:** *Draft Programme of the Russian Social-Democratic Labour Party*. A programme project prepared with Georgy Plekhanov; its authorship is therefore collaborative and internally negotiated.
- **1903:** *Draft Rules of the R.S.D.L.P.*. An organizational protocol proposed to the party congress.
- **1904:** *Statement and Documents on the Break of the Central Institutions with the Party*. A documentary factional intervention.
- **1905:** *A Militant Agreement for the Uprising*. A tactical proposal approaching a protocol for coordinated revolutionary action.
- **1905:** *Resolution on the Armed Uprising*. A draft resolution: an authored proposal whose status depended upon collective adoption.
- **1905:** *Draft Resolutions for the Third Congress of the R.S.D.L.P.*. A suite of proposed acts rather than a single descriptive essay.
- **1905:** *The First of May*. A proclamation or manifesto addressed to workers.
- **1906:** *An Appeal to the Party by Delegates to the Unity Congress Who Belonged to the Former “Bolshevik” Group*. A collectively issued manifesto; Lenin participated in drafting it, so sole authorship should not be assumed.
- **1907:** *Draft Resolutions for the Fifth Congress of the R.S.D.L.P.*. A suite of proposed party decisions.
- **1907:** *Theses for a Report on the Attitude of the Social-Democratic Party Towards the State Duma*. Speaking notes that also formulate a tactical programme.
- **1912:** *The Tasks of Revolutionary Social-Democracy in the European War*. A set of theses drafted after the outbreak of war.
- **1914:** *The War and Russian Social-Democracy*. A manifesto of the R.S.D.L.P. Central Committee written by Lenin; it is institutionally collective even where his drafting hand is identifiable.
- **1915:** *Conference of the R.S.D.L.P. Groups Abroad*. Lenin drafted resolutions and associated materials for this collective party event.
- **1915:** *Draft Resolution of the Left-Wing Delegates at the International Socialist Conference at Zimmerwald*. A proposed international anti-war declaration that was not adopted in Lenin’s preferred form.
- **1915:** *The Voice of an Honest French Socialist*. A manifesto-like appeal built around an open letter.
- **1916:** *The Socialist Revolution and the Right of Nations to Self-Determination: Theses*. A joint thesis document associated with Lenin and Zinoviev; editorial presentation varies, and it should not automatically be assigned to Lenin alone.
- **1917:** *The Tasks of the Proletariat in the Present Revolution* (“April Theses”). Ten theses published in *Pravda*; “April Theses” is the conventional later name, while the text itself functioned as a project for redirecting Bolshevik policy.
- **1917:** *Draft Theses for an Appeal to the International Socialist Commission and All Socialist Parties*. An unadopted project for an international appeal.
- **1917:** *Resolution on the War*. A draft party resolution associated with the April Conference.
- **1917:** *Resolution on the Agrarian Question*. A proposed party protocol for revolutionary land policy.
- **1917:** *Resolution on the National Question*. A proposed party declaration on national self-determination.
- **1917:** *The Tasks of the Revolution*. A manifesto-like programme addressed to the revolutionary crisis.
- **1917:** *The Crisis Has Matured*. An urgent insurrectionary intervention assembled in several sections; part of it was withheld at the time.
- **1917:** *Advice of an Onlooker*. A tactical protocol for insurrection, presented as a letter.
- **1917:** *Letter to the Members of the Bolshevik Party*. A manifesto for immediate seizure of power.
- **1917:** *Letter to the Members of the Central Committee*. A last-minute operational appeal for insurrection.
- **1917:** *Draft Decree on Peace*. The project that became the Second All-Russia Congress of Soviets’ Decree on Peace; authorship of the draft is Lenin’s, while enactment made it a collective state act.
- **1917:** *Draft Decree on the Land*. A governmental protocol assembled partly from peasant mandates and enacted collectively; Lenin’s authorship lies in selection, formulation, and sponsorship rather than invention of every provision.
- **1917:** *Draft Regulations on Workers’ Control*. A protocol for workplace supervision that passed through collective revision.
- **1917:** *Declaration of Rights of the Working and Exploited People*. A constitutional manifesto drafted by Lenin and adopted, with institutional authority, by Soviet bodies.
- **1918:** *The Socialist Fatherland Is in Danger!*. An emergency decree-manifesto generally attributed to Lenin and adopted by the Council of People’s Commissars; the institutional act and the individual draft should be distinguished.
- **1918:** *Six Theses on the Immediate Tasks of the Soviet Government*. A compressed policy protocol.
- **1918:** *Theses on the Present Political Situation*. A project for party policy during civil war.
- **1918:** *Draft Programme of the R.C.P.(B.)*. A programme project substantially drafted by Lenin but collectively revised and adopted.
- **1919:** *Draft Programme of the R.C.P.(B.)*. Lenin’s renewed programme draft for the Eighth Party Congress; the adopted programme is a collective party document.
- **1919:** *Theses and Report on Bourgeois Democracy and the Dictatorship of the Proletariat*. A founding manifesto of the Communist International combining propositions with an oral report.
- **1919:** *Great Initiative*. A programmatic interpretation of voluntary labour whose English title is also rendered *A Great Beginning*.
- **1920:** *Preliminary Draft Theses on the National and Colonial Questions*. A project submitted to the Communist International’s Second Congress and subsequently amended through collective deliberation.
- **1920:** *Preliminary Draft Theses on the Agrarian Question*. A proposed international protocol for communist agrarian policy.
- **1920:** *Twenty-One Conditions of Admission to the Communist International*. Lenin participated decisively in drafting this protocol, but the final conditions were the work of a commission and congress and should not be catalogued as his uncontested sole composition.
- **1921:** *Rough Draft of Theses Concerning the Peasants*. A preliminary policy project connected with the transition to the New Economic Policy.
- **1921:** *Draft Resolution on the Role and Tasks of the Trade Unions*. A party-policy protocol.
- **1921:** *Preliminary Draft Resolution of the Tenth Congress of the R.C.P.(B.) on Party Unity*. The project behind the congress resolution commonly called the ban on factions; adoption made the final act collective.
- **1921:** *Preliminary Draft Resolution on the Syndicalist and Anarchist Deviation in Our Party*. A proposed disciplinary and ideological protocol.
- **1922:** *Draft Resolution on the Report of the R.C.P.(B.) Delegation in the Comintern*. A proposed institutional act.
- **1922:** *On the Formation of the U.S.S.R.*. Notes and proposals concerning the constitutional relationship among Soviet republics; these are interventions in a collective state-making project, not a self-contained constitution authored by Lenin.
- **1922:** *On the Question of the Nationalities or of “Autonomisation”*. A dictated memorandum criticizing Stalin’s autonomisation scheme; it remained unpublished during Lenin’s lifetime.

### Speeches, reports, lectures, and oral manifestations

- **1895–1923:** *Speeches, reports, interventions, and remarks preserved in the Collected Works*. This is an editorially constituted corpus of hundreds of oral manifestations at party congresses, Soviet congresses, trade-union meetings, rallies, conferences, and governmental sessions. Many survive only through shorthand reports, newspaper summaries, secretarial records, or later editorial reconstructions and therefore resist identification with fixed authorial texts.
- **1905:** *Report on the Third Congress of the R.S.D.L.P.*. An oral political report surviving as an edited text.
- **1906:** *Report on the Unity Congress of the R.S.D.L.P.*. A sequence of reports and interventions whose pamphlet version is a related but not identical made object.
- **1907:** *Speeches at the Fifth Congress of the R.S.D.L.P.*. A corpus of oral interventions rather than one work.
- **1917:** *Speech on the Tasks of the Soviet Government*. An oral intervention whose surviving form depends upon contemporary reporting.
- **1917:** *Reports on Peace and on the Land at the Second All-Russia Congress of Soviets*. Foundational governmental performances linked to, but distinct from, the decrees adopted by the congress.
- **1918:** *Report on War and Peace to the Seventh Congress of the R.C.P.(B.)*. An oral defense of the Brest-Litovsk settlement.
- **1918:** *Report on the Review of the Programme and on Changing the Name of the Party*. A congress report proposing institutional transformation.
- **1918:** *Speech at a Meeting in the Michelson Works*. Lenin’s last speech before Fanny Kaplan’s assassination attempt; the extant text is reportorial.
- **1919:** *Opening Address and Reports to the First Congress of the Communist International*. Performative contributions to the creation of a new international organization.
- **1919:** *Lecture on the State*. An oral pedagogical manifestation preserved from a stenographic record.
- **1920:** *Report on the International Situation and the Fundamental Tasks of the Communist International*. A congress performance linked to the theses debated by delegates.
- **1920:** *Speech on the Terms of Admission into the Communist International*. An oral defense of an institutional protocol.
- **1920:** *The Tasks of the Youth Leagues*. A speech delivered to the Third All-Russia Congress of the Russian Young Communist League and subsequently stabilized as a printed text.
- **1921:** *Report on the Political Work of the Central Committee*. A major oral report to the Tenth Party Congress.
- **1921:** *Report on the Substitution of a Tax in Kind for the Surplus-Grain Appropriation System*. A policy speech connected with the institutional inauguration of the New Economic Policy.
- **1921:** *Speeches on Party Unity and the Anarcho-Syndicalist Deviation*. Performative arguments attached to resolutions adopted collectively.
- **1922:** *Political Report of the Central Committee to the Eleventh Congress of the R.C.P.(B.)*. Lenin’s final party-congress report.
- **1922:** *Five Years of the Russian Revolution and the Prospects of the World Revolution*. A report to the Fourth Congress of the Communist International and Lenin’s last major public speech.

### Letters, telegrams, memoranda, and administrative acts

- **1893–1922:** *Letters to Relatives*. An editorial corpus of private communications, not a single intended obra; the English *Collected Works* organizes it as Volume 37.
- **1895–1923:** *Political and organizational correspondence*. Thousands of letters, notes, telegrams, telephone messages, directives, endorsements, questionnaires, and memoranda occupy Volumes 34–36 and 43–45 and are interspersed throughout the chronological volumes. Some are jointly signed, some are secretarial transcriptions, some survive only in drafts, and many resist the category of autonomous “work.”
- **1900–1903:** *Correspondence concerning Iskra*. A distributed project made through letters about financing, printing, transport, contributors, editorial relations, and clandestine organization.
- **1903–1904:** *Letters concerning the split in the R.S.D.L.P.*. Political acts within an organizational struggle; the corpus has no single fixed title supplied by Lenin.
- **1908–1910:** *Correspondence concerning the Bolshevik Centre and the Capri school*. A documentary field of contested party administration rather than one authored work.
- **1912–1914:** *Directives and correspondence with Pravda*. A collaborative newspaper-making practice dispersed across letters, editorial suggestions, financial instructions, and articles.
- **1914–1917:** *War-period correspondence*. Letters and coded organizational communications connecting émigré Bolshevik groups, Russian activists, and the international socialist left.
- **1917:** *Letters on the political crisis of September–October*. A group including *The Bolsheviks Must Assume Power*, *Marxism and Insurrection*, *The Crisis Has Matured*, and operational letters on the uprising; it is a sequence of interventions, not a book.
- **1918–1922:** *Council of People’s Commissars directives and correspondence*. Administrative acts produced by Lenin as chairman within a governmental apparatus; attribution must distinguish his drafts and signatures from collectively adopted decisions and texts prepared by secretaries or agencies.
- **1918–1920:** *Civil-war telegrams and directives*. Operational communications concerning food supply, transport, military defense, repression, and local administration. Their status is frequently that of executive act or protocol rather than literary work, and exact responsibility may be shared with the institutions issuing them.
- **1921–1922:** *New Economic Policy memoranda and directives*. A dispersed administrative project covering taxation, concessions, trade, finance, cooperatives, and state industry.
- **1922:** *Letter to the Congress*. A set of dictated notes dated December 23–26, 1922, with an addendum on Stalin dated January 4, 1923; later convention calls it “Lenin’s Testament,” but that title was not Lenin’s, and the object is a cluster of confidential recommendations rather than a testament in the legal sense.
- **1922:** *On Giving Legislative Functions to the State Planning Commission*. A dictated memorandum associated editorially with the late congressional letters.
- **1922:** *The Question of Nationalities or “Autonomisation”*. A dictated memorandum dated December 30–31; it forms part of Lenin’s late struggle over the Georgian affair and Soviet federalism.
- **1923:** *Letter to Stalin concerning the insult to Nadezhda Krupskaya*. A dictated personal-political ultimatum; its existence and date are established in the collected correspondence, while its character resists classification as a public work.
- **1923:** *Letter to Trotsky on defending the Georgian case*. A dictated request for political representation; it belongs to an unfinished intervention because illness prevented Lenin from carrying the project through personally.
- **1923:** *Notes to Mdivani, Makharadze, and others*. Brief dictated messages expressing support in the Georgian dispute; they are fragments of political action rather than independent essays.

### Notebooks, excerpts, marginalia, and research projects

- **1895–1916:** *Philosophical Notebooks*. An editorial obra formed from reading excerpts, summaries, marginal comments, diagrams, and fragments, chiefly from 1914–16 but including earlier material. Lenin did not design or title the English collected volume as a finished book.
- **1914–1915:** *Conspectus of Hegel’s The Science of Logic*. A working notebook of extracts and comments, not a completed philosophical treatise.
- **1915:** *Conspectus of Hegel’s Lectures on the History of Philosophy*. Research notes whose later editorial presentation should not be confused with authorial publication.
- **1915:** *On the Question of Dialectics*. A compact philosophical fragment found among the notebooks; whether it is treated as an autonomous essay or a notebook entry is editorially contingent.
- **1915–1916:** *Plan of Hegelian Dialectics*. A schematic intellectual project rather than a finished text.
- **1915–1916:** *Conspectus of Aristotle’s Metaphysics*. Reading notes and annotations.
- **1916:** *Notebooks on Imperialism*. A collection of 148 notebooks in the Russian fifth edition and 148 constituent notebook groupings as described in the English volume’s editorial apparatus; Volume 39 labels the body *Notebooks on Imperialism*. It is the research project behind *Imperialism*, not an alternative edition of that book.
- **1900–1916:** *Notebooks on the Agrarian Question*. Sixty-six notebooks of extracts, statistics, plans, and commentary assembled editorially in Volume 40. They document a long research project and were not made by Lenin as a publishable unified book.
- **1896–1917:** *Notebooks, plans, and preparatory materials collected in Volume 41*. An editorial corpus of drafts, calculations, bibliographical notes, and unfinished projects rather than one authored obra.
- **1917–1923:** *Notebooks, plans, and preparatory materials collected in Volume 42*. A heterogeneous editorial corpus whose items range from speech outlines to statistical notes and governmental memoranda.
- **1916:** *Plan for a Book on Imperialism*. A project embedded in the imperialism notebooks; portions were realized in the published book, but the preparatory plan and the final work are not identical.
- **1917:** *Plans and Materials for The State and Revolution*. A documentary project including the so-called “Blue Notebook,” bibliographical preparation, quotations, and structural plans; the notebook is not itself the published book.
- **1918–1923:** *Reading notes, lists, calculations, and marginal instructions produced in government*. Ephemeral working objects preserved selectively by editors. Their survival is uneven, and the collected editions do not justify claiming that every such manifestation has been recovered.

### Newspapers, editorial enterprises, organizations, and revolutionary state-making

- **1900–1903:** *Iskra*. A clandestine newspaper and transnational organizational project co-founded and co-edited by Lenin. It is a collaborative obra whose identity exceeds the articles Lenin individually wrote for it.
- **1901–1902:** *Zarya*. A Marxist theoretical journal made collectively by the *Iskra* organization, with Lenin as an editor and contributor.
- **1904–1905:** *Vperyod*. A Bolshevik newspaper founded and directed by Lenin as an organizational instrument.
- **1905:** *Proletary*. A Bolshevik newspaper edited by Lenin; the periodical as a whole is collaborative and cannot be reduced to his signed or unsigned contributions.
- **1906:** *Volna*. A legal Bolshevik newspaper to which Lenin contributed and whose political direction he helped shape.
- **1906:** *Vperyod* and *Ekho*. Short-lived legal newspaper projects in which Lenin participated editorially.
- **1906–1907:** *Proletary* (Russian legal and émigré phases). A continuing Bolshevik editorial project under Lenin’s direction, distinct in production history from any single text it carried.
- **1910–1912:** *Sotsial-Demokrat*. The R.S.D.L.P. central organ, edited collectively with Lenin’s participation amid sustained factional conflict.
- **1912–1914:** *Pravda*. A mass workers’ daily whose creation was collective; Lenin supplied political direction and extensive correspondence and articles but did not singly make the newspaper.
- **1913–1914:** *Prosveshcheniye*. A legal Bolshevik theoretical journal shaped by Lenin as contributor and adviser.
- **1915–1916:** *Sotsial-Demokrat* and *Kommunist*. Émigré anti-war publishing projects; *Kommunist* was collaboratively edited and ended amid disagreements among its editors.
- **1917:** *Pravda* under revolutionary conditions. An editorial and political project in which Lenin’s April return produced a contested redirection rather than an uncontested single-author creation.
- **1903–1912:** *The Bolshevik faction*. An organizational project rather than a text or solely personal obra. Lenin was its principal strategist, but it was made through collective action and cannot properly be catalogued as his exclusive creation.
- **1912:** *The Prague Conference party organization*. An institutional reconstitution promoted by Lenin and others; its status as the founding moment of a separate Bolshevik party is historically and terminologically disputed.
- **1917:** *The October insurrection*. A collective revolutionary action for which Lenin argued, planned, and exerted decisive political pressure. It strongly resists classification as an individual “work”: authorship belongs to organizations and participants, not to Lenin alone.
- **1917–1924:** *The Soviet government*. Lenin helped create and led the Council of People’s Commissars, but the state is neither his authored text nor his individual obra; it is a collective, coercive, institutional construction.
- **1918:** *The Russian Communist Party programme-revision project*. A collectively produced ideological and organizational project in which Lenin supplied drafts, reports, amendments, and polemics.
- **1919:** *The Communist International*. An international institution created collectively, with Lenin as a principal initiator, theorist, and drafter. Calling it simply “Lenin’s creation” would erase the agency of parties and delegates who constituted it.
- **1920–1921:** *The electrification project and GOELRO*. Lenin championed, commissioned, and politically interpreted the project but did not author its technical plan; the plan itself belongs to the State Commission for the Electrification of Russia.
- **1921:** *The New Economic Policy*. A policy formation advocated and explained by Lenin but made through party and state decisions, economic pressures, and implementation by many actors. It is better classified as a collective governmental project than an individually authored work.
- **1922:** *The Union of Soviet Socialist Republics*. Lenin intervened in the constitutional project and opposed Stalin’s initial autonomisation scheme, but illness limited his role and the resulting union was a collective and contested institutional construction.

### Sound and moving-image manifestations

- **1919–1921:** *Gramophone speeches*. Lenin recorded a small group of political speeches, including addresses concerning Soviet power, the Third International, the middle peasantry, labour discipline, pogroms against Jews, and work for transport. These are performed sound objects rather than books, and discographic titles and recording dates differ slightly among catalogues and reissues.
- **1918–1922:** *Filmed appearances of Lenin*. Newsreel fragments record Lenin at public ceremonies, congresses, and gatherings. They are manifestations of his bodily and political performance, but the films were made by camera operators and production organizations, not authored by Lenin.
- **1919:** *What Is Soviet Power?*. A recorded speech issued on gramophone disc; the recording is a distinct performed object from printed transcriptions bearing the same title.
- **1919:** *How the Working People Can Be Saved from the Oppression of the Landowners and Capitalists Forever*. A gramophone address whose identity includes Lenin’s recorded voice and the manufactured disc.
- **1919:** *The Third Communist International*. A recorded political address.
- **1919:** *Appeal to the Red Army*. A recorded exhortation, functioning as oral manifesto.
- **1919:** *In Memory of Chairman of the All-Russia Central Executive Committee, Comrade Y. M. Sverdlov*. A memorial speech preserved as sound.
- **1919:** *On the Middle Peasants*. A recorded policy address.
- **1919:** *On Labour Discipline*. A recorded protocol-like exhortation concerning work and production.
- **1919:** *On Combating Anti-Jewish Pogroms*. A gramophone manifesto against antisemitic violence.
- **1920:** *The Work of the People’s Commissariat for Education*. A recorded address on education; discographic sources may assign variant dates or titles to particular pressings.
- **1921:** *On the Tax in Kind*. A recorded explanation associated with the New Economic Policy and distinct from the pamphlet of similar title.

### Editorially constituted remainder and limits of completeness

- **1893–1923:** *The approximately 4,500 documents represented by the Russian and English collected-works traditions*. This is the nearest standard scholarly basis for “everything Lenin made,” but it is an editorial corpus, not a single Lenin-created obra. It includes articles, drafts, letters, telegrams, speeches, resolutions, notes, marginalia, and institutional documents while omitting or withholding other material.
- **1893–1923:** *Unsigned and pseudonymous journalism attributed to Lenin*. Attribution rests on editorial research, archival evidence, style, correspondence, or newspaper records and is not equally secure for every item; the collected editions sometimes present editorial attribution as settled where later scholars may remain cautious.
- **1893–1923:** *Lost works and missing portions*. These include the lost second part of *What the “Friends of the People” Are*, unlocated letters, unpublished or destroyed clandestine material, speeches without adequate transcripts, and planned sections never composed. They cannot honestly be supplied with invented titles or texts.
- **1893–1923:** *Collective documents drafted, edited, signed, or adopted by Lenin*. The collected editions frequently include party and state documents because Lenin drafted or participated in them. Their presence does not establish sole authorship, and the exact division of labour is sometimes unknown.
- **1893–1923:** *Posthumously titled fragments*. Editors supplied titles to numerous notes, drafts, plans, and letters that Lenin left untitled. Such titles identify archival objects conveniently but must not be mistaken for names Lenin gave finished works.
- **1893–1923:** *Documents excluded from the Soviet collected editions*. The English forty-five-volume edition translates the fourth Russian edition and carries the selection, dating, attribution, ideological framing, and archival restrictions of its institutional context. It therefore cannot be called a complete neutral catalogue raisonné.
- **1893–1923:** *Documents added or restored in the fifth Russian Complete Collected Works*. The fifty-five-volume fifth Russian edition expands the corpus but likewise reflects Soviet editorial policy and does not eliminate all omissions, disputed attributions, or inaccessible records.
- **Undated and incompletely dated:** *Notes, plans, address lists, calculations, annotations, and fragments*. Editors often assign ranges or conjectural dates from handwriting, paper, content, or archival sequence. Where no secure date or authorial title exists, the object remains an undated working trace rather than a falsely completed “work.”
