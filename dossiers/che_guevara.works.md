## Works by Ernesto "Che" Guevara

### Travel diaries and chronicles

- **1950:** *Notas del viaje por el interior de Argentina*. A private travel notebook rather than a finished book; only selections survive in publication, so its boundaries and title are not entirely fixed.
- **1951–1952:** *Notas de viaje* (*Diarios de motocicleta*). A cycle of retrospective travel chronicles made from Guevara’s first Latin American diary and photographs; the familiar book title belongs to its posthumous edition.
- **1953:** *Machu Picchu: enigma de piedra en América*. A travel article published in the Panamanian magazine *Siete* on 12 December 1953.
- **1953–1956:** *Otra vez: diario del segundo viaje por Latinoamérica*. Guevara’s own title for an intimate, unfinished diary not intended for publication; it follows his second continental journey through Guatemala and Mexico.

### Revolutionary diaries and testimonial obras

- **1956–1958:** *Diario de un combatiente: Sierra Maestra–Santa Clara*. Guevara’s campaign notebooks from the Cuban insurrection, later used as raw material for *Pasajes*; surviving gaps mean that even the critical edition is not a literally complete daily record.
- **1959–1964:** *Pasajes de la guerra revolucionaria*. A deliberately shaped testimonial obra made from campaign memories and earlier notebooks, first appearing as separately published episodes and then as a book in 1963.
- **1965:** *Pasajes de la guerra revolucionaria: Congo*. A critical memoir of the failed Congo expedition, revised by Guevara after leaving Africa but withheld during his lifetime; it is both campaign history and an unfinished political reckoning.
- **1966–1967:** *Diario en Bolivia*. A field diary extending from 7 November 1966 to 7 October 1967. It was confiscated, copied and politically contested before its 1968 publication; it records the project of an insurgency rather than constituting a book prepared for readers.

### Military protocols and revolutionary theory

- **1959:** *¿Qué es un guerrillero?* An explanatory article defining the guerrilla as both combatant and social reformer.
- **1959:** *La guerra de guerrillas y la población campesina*. An essay connecting armed struggle to rural social conditions.
- **1960–1961:** *La guerra de guerrillas*. A book-length protocol derived from the Cuban campaign: military instruction, political theory and retrospective rationalization at once.
- **1960:** *Consejos al combatiente*. A seven-part instructional series comprising “Moral y disciplina de los combatientes revolucionarios,” “Disciplina de fuego en el combate,” “Solidaridad en el combate,” “Aprovechamiento del terreno,” “El contraataque,” “Las ametralladoras en el combate defensivo” and “Artillería de bolsillo.”
- **1961:** *Cuba: ¿excepción histórica o vanguardia en la lucha anticolonialista?* A strategic essay arguing about whether Cuba supplied a repeatable revolutionary model.
- **1962:** *Influencia de la Revolución Cubana en la América Latina*. A political analysis of Cuba’s continental effects.
- **1962:** *Táctica y estrategia de la revolución latinoamericana*. A strategic essay written in October–November 1962 but published only after Guevara’s death.
- **1963:** *La guerra de guerrillas: un método*. A manifesto-like argument for armed struggle, explicitly distinct from the earlier manual bearing a similar title.

### Political economy and the project of socialist construction

- **1959–1965:** *Sistema presupuestario de financiamiento*. Not one bounded text but an administrative-economic project devised through Guevara’s work at Cuba’s National Bank and Ministry of Industries. Its obra consists in institutions, regulations, accounting practices, speeches and polemical essays, and therefore resists classification as a book.
- **1961:** *Cuba no admite que se separe la economía de la política*. Guevara’s principal address to the Inter-American Economic and Social Council at Punta del Este; simultaneously diplomatic intervention, development proposal and anti-imperialist manifesto.
- **1961:** *Discusión colectiva; decisión y responsabilidad únicas*. An administrative protocol defining collective deliberation and individual executive responsibility.
- **1962:** *Tareas industriales de la Revolución en los años venideros*. A programmatic essay on industrial development.
- **1962:** *El cuadro, columna vertebral de la Revolución*. A didactic-political essay defining the revolutionary cadre as an institutional and ethical type.
- **1963:** *Contra el burocratismo*. A diagnosis of bureaucratic failure that also proposes organizational remedies.
- **1963:** *Consideraciones sobre los costos de producción como base del análisis económico de las empresas sujetas al sistema presupuestario*. A technical-economic essay within the larger financing-system project.
- **1963:** *Sobre el concepto de valor*. A contribution to Cuba’s dispute over value, planning and commodity categories under socialism.
- **1963–1964:** *El gran debate sobre la economía en Cuba*. A distributed polemical obra rather than a book made by Guevara alone: he initiated and sustained the debate, but later volumes bearing this title also contain writings by his opponents and were assembled by editors.
- **1964:** *Sobre el sistema presupuestario de financiamiento*. Guevara’s principal theoretical statement on his alternative model of socialist management.
- **1964:** *La banca, el crédito y el socialismo*. A polemical intervention in the economic debate.
- **1964:** *La planificación socialista, su significado*. An essay treating planning as a political and social relation rather than merely a technical procedure.
- **1964:** *Cuba, su economía, su comercio exterior, su significado en el mundo actual*. A synthetic account of Cuba’s economy and international position.
- **1965–1966:** *Apuntes críticos a la economía política*. Notes and commentaries intended as the conceptual foundation for a future manual. The contemplated book was never completed, so the project—not the posthumous editorial container—is the work.
- **1965–1966:** *Marx y Engels: una síntesis biográfica*. Unfinished biographical sketches belonging to the *Apuntes críticos* project; whether Guevara intended an independent work cannot be established.

### Political and philosophical essays

- **1954:** *Yo vi caer a Jacobo Árbenz*. An eyewitness political chronicle of the Guatemalan coup, given to Mario Dalmau but published only posthumously.
- **1960:** *Notas para el estudio de la ideología de la Revolución Cubana*. An essay locating the revolution’s ideas in lived practice rather than in a prior doctrinal program.
- **1961:** *Comentarios sobre la Revolución Cubana*. An account written for a Soviet collection of historical and ethnographic essays.
- **1963:** *Primeras impresiones al conocer a Fidel*. A brief autobiographical-political recollection.
- **1965:** *El socialismo y el hombre en Cuba*. An open letter to Carlos Quijano published in *Marcha*; part theoretical essay and part manifesto for the ethical creation of a “new human being.”
- **1965–1967:** *Apuntes filosóficos*. A project assembled from reading notebooks, copied passages and comments made in Africa, Prague and Bolivia, together with earlier philosophical notebooks. It aimed toward a popular Marxist manual but never became a finished work and contains substantial excerpts from other authors rather than Guevara’s words alone.
- **1967:** *Mensaje a los pueblos del mundo a través de la Tricontinental*. A manifesto intended to produce coordinated anti-imperialist action—often known by its call for multiple Vietnams—rather than a merely descriptive essay.

### Journalism and serial manifestations

- **1959:** *Una revolución que comienza*. A three-part chronicle published by the Brazilian magazine *O Cruzeiro*, later absorbed into *Pasajes de la guerra revolucionaria*.
- **1959:** *América vista desde el balcón afroasiático*. A political travel essay arising from Guevara’s diplomatic tour.
- **1959:** *La República Árabe Unida: un ejemplo*. One of a sequence of diplomatic travel reports.
- **1959:** *La India, país de grandes contrastes*. A diplomatic travel chronicle.
- **1959:** *Japón se repone de la tragedia atómica*. A diplomatic travel chronicle reflecting on postwar Japan.
- **1959:** *Indonesia y la unidad sólida de su pueblo*. A diplomatic travel chronicle.
- **1959:** *Intercambio comercial y amistad con Ceilán y Pakistán*. A diplomatic-economic travel report.
- **1959:** *Yugoslavia, un pueblo que lucha por sus ideales*. A diplomatic travel chronicle.
- **1960:** *El Francotirador*. A sequence of eighteen satirical political columns published pseudonymously in *Verde Olivo*. The series is a journalistic persona and serial manifestation, not a single continuously argued book; the collected bibliography identifies the individual installments but does not establish that Guevara conceived them as an independent obra.

### Oratory, diplomacy and recorded speech

- **1958–1967:** *Speeches and interviews*. A large oral body of work encompassing Radio Rebelde bulletins, workplace talks, television appearances, press conferences, diplomatic addresses and international speeches. The nine-volume *Escritos y discursos* and its source bibliography enumerate hundreds of occasions; many titles are later editorial descriptions, so treating each transient utterance as a deliberately titled “work” would manufacture an oeuvre Guevara did not name.
- **11 December 1964:** *Address to the United Nations General Assembly*. A diplomatic speech and performative declaration of Cuba’s international position.
- **24 February 1965:** *Speech at the Second Economic Seminar of Afro-Asian Solidarity, Algiers*. A diplomatic-economic manifesto criticizing unequal relations between socialist and underdeveloped countries.
- **August 1961:** *Punta del Este interventions*. The principal address, press conference and replies together form a diplomatic performance; posthumous books titled *Punta del Este* are editorial dossiers rather than a single book written by Guevara.

### Correspondence and manifestos in letter form

- **1940s–1967:** *Correspondence*. Hundreds of personal, administrative and political letters constitute a dispersed epistolary body rather than one authored book. The archive remains incompletely classified, and published collections are selections made by later editors.
- **1960:** *Letter to Ernesto Sábato*. A political-intellectual letter addressing the revolution and the writer’s responsibility.
- **1962:** *Letter to Anna Louise Strong*. A political letter reflecting on Cuba and international socialism.
- **1964:** *Letter to Charles Bettelheim*. An intervention in the economic debate conducted in epistolary form.
- **1965:** *Farewell letter to Fidel Castro*. A private-political instrument relinquishing offices and Cuban citizenship in preparation for internationalist combat; publication turned it into a manifesto, although that was not its original material setting.
- **1965:** *Farewell letter to his parents*. A private testament written before his departure on an internationalist mission.
- **1965:** *Farewell letter to his children*. A familial testament whose public circulation was posthumous.
- **1965:** *Letter to Carlos Quijano*. The epistolary form in which *El socialismo y el hombre en Cuba* first appeared; it is the same work, not a second title.
- **1966:** *Letter to Hildita*. A private family letter from the period between the Congo and Bolivian campaigns.

### Poems, stories and literary projects

- **Youth–1956:** *Juvenile poems*. The personal archive preserves poems from Guevara’s youth, including *Canto a Fidel* (written in Mexico in 1956). They form a small, incompletely published literary corpus rather than a securely delimited poetry collection.
- **1950s:** *Stories, chronicles and literary reviews*. The archive records an assortment of youthful narratives and reviews. Their survival is evidence of literary making, but no complete scholarly catalogue of separately titled items has been publicly issued.
- **1956:** *Canto a Fidel*. A poem written before the *Granma* expedition and first published in Cuba in 1959; it is a literary-political address, not a speech or prose manifesto.

### Prefaces and paratexts

- **1960:** *Preface to Alberto Bayo’s Mi aporte a la Revolución cubana*. A commissioned paratext framing another author’s military memoir.
- **1961:** *Preface to Gaspar Jorge García Galló’s Biografía del tabaco habano*. A paratext to a work on Cuba’s tobacco economy.
- **1963:** *Preface to El partido marxista-leninista*. A political paratext.
- **1964:** *Preface to Geología de Cuba*. An institutional-scientific paratext.
- **1964:** *Preface to Võ Nguyên Giáp’s Guerra del pueblo, ejército del pueblo*. A framing text connecting Vietnamese and Cuban revolutionary experience.

### Photographic obra

- **1950–1967:** *Photographic archive*. Hundreds of negatives and prints made during Guevara’s journeys, political work and campaigns constitute a visual obra in several formats and techniques. *Che, fotógrafo* is the title of a 2001 curatorial catalogue, not a title Guevara gave the body of images.
- **1951–1952:** *Photographs of the first Latin American journey*. Travel photographs made alongside *Notas de viaje*; image and writing operate together without quite becoming a planned Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1959–1964:** *Diplomatic and industrial photographs*. Images made on international tours and during Cuba’s early revolutionary period; they resist division into discrete titled works because the archive preserves them principally as a photographic collection.

### Editorial and communications projects

- **1957–1958:** *El Cubano Libre*. A Rebel Army newspaper revived and directed in Guevara’s column. It is a collective publication rather than his sole work, though he wrote for it and helped make its material and political form.
- **1958:** *Radio Rebelde*. Guevara established the clandestine station at La Mesa, while its continuing broadcasts were collectively produced. It is best classified as a communications project and institution, not as an authored text.
- **1962–1964:** *Nuestra Industria*. The Ministry of Industries’ technical-economic journals provided a forum Guevara helped create and repeatedly used. The journals are collective institutional manifestations, while his signed articles remain distinct works.
- **1959–1965:** *Voluntary-labour campaign*. A social and pedagogical project promoted and repeatedly enacted by Guevara to join political consciousness to productive work. It made practices, rituals and images rather than a bounded object and therefore resists being called an individual artwork.

### Collected works and posthumous editorial objects

- **1970:** *Obras escogidas, 1956–1967*. A two-volume posthumous selection by Casa de las Américas; it transmits Guevara’s works but is an editor-made collection, not a new work made by Guevara.
- **1970:** *Che en la Revolución cubana*. A seven-volume posthumous corpus; its organization is editorial.
- **1977:** *Escritos y discursos*. A nine-volume collected-works edition published by Editorial de Ciencias Sociales. It is the principal broad printed corpus for his Cuban-period articles, speeches, interviews and letters, but it remains a selection rather than a catalogue raisonné.
- **1997–present:** *Proyecto Editorial Che Guevara*. More than thirty thematic and critical volumes issued by the Centro de Estudios Che Guevara with Ocean Press/Ocean Sur. Titles such as *Che Guevara presente*, *América Latina*, *Retos de la transición socialista en Cuba* and *El gran debate* are valuable editorial constructions, not additional works authored under those titles by Guevara.
