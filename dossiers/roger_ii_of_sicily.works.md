## Works by Roger II de Hauteville

### Royal and legal creations

- **1130:** *Kingdom of Sicily*. A political obra created when Roger united Sicily and the Norman territories of southern Italy under a new royal title. It is not a discrete object but an institutional creation—sustained through law, administration, ceremony, architecture, and multilingual government—and therefore resists classification as a conventional “work.” ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Roger-II); [Hubert Houben, *Roger II of Sicily*](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805216/52087/excerpt/9780521652087_excerpt.pdf))

- **c. 1140s:** *Leges regis Rogerii*, conventionally called the *Assizes of Ariano*. A legislative protocol defining royal authority, public order, offices, religion, and criminal law. Genuine Rogerian legislation survives in two substantially different later textual witnesses, but scholars dispute whether it was promulgated as a single code at Ariano in 1140; the title *Assizes of Ariano* is itself retrospective. ([Treccani, “Assise di Ariano”](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/assise-di-ariano_%28Federiciana%29/); [G. A. Loud, “The Laws of King Roger II”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.21995805); [Revue du Centre Michel de L’Hospital](https://polen.uca.fr/revue-cmh/index.php?id=907))

- **1140:** *Ariano monetary ordinance and coinage reform*. A regulatory and material protocol that reorganized the kingdom’s currency and introduced a unified silver coin, the *ducalis*, alongside revised gold, billon, and copper issues. Falco of Benevento explicitly associates a monetary edict—not necessarily the entire surviving law collection—with Roger’s assembly at Ariano. ([Revue du Centre Michel de L’Hospital](https://polen.uca.fr/revue-cmh/index.php?id=907); [Andrea Saccocci et al., *Italian Coins in the British Museum*, vol. 1](https://air.unimi.it/bitstream/2434/821348/2/Italian%20Coins%20in%20the%20British%20Museum%201-pdf.pdf))

### Diplomatic and administrative texts

- **1105–1154:** *Roger II’s Latin diplomas*. A chancery obra comprising privileges, grants, confirmations, judgments, and mandates issued in Roger’s name first as count and duke and then as king. Carlrichard Brühl’s critical corpus distinguishes authentic, doubtful, forged, and lost acts; its appendices identify ninety-one specifically attested lost diplomas, while Brühl estimated that the chancery’s total losses may have been far greater. ([Carlrichard Brühl, ed., *Rogerii II regis diplomata Latina*](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1990_num_33_129_2458_t1_0070_0000_2); [Cambridge discussion of the diplomatic corpus](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805216/52087/excerpt/9780521652087_excerpt.pdf))

- **1105–1154:** *Greek and Arabic royal acts*. A multilingual chancery corpus issued under Roger’s authority, not a personally handwritten oeuvre. Its boundaries remain incomplete: the Greek documents may represent roughly half the chancery’s production, while no critical collected edition comparable to Brühl’s Latin volume has yet secured the entire Greek-and-Arabic corpus. ([Cambridge discussion of Roger’s chancery](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805216/52087/excerpt/9780521652087_excerpt.pdf); [G. A. Loud and Alex Metcalfe, eds., *The Society of Norman Italy*](https://mamlikshistory.blogspot.com/2023/10/download-pdf-medieval-mediterranean.html))

### Geography as commissioned Gesamtkunstwerk

- **c. 1138–1154:** *Nuzhat al-mushtāq fī ikhtirāq al-āfāq* (“The Delight of One Who Wishes to Traverse the Regions of the World”), commonly called the *Book of Roger*. A geographic project commissioned by Roger and authored by al-Idrisi, combining descriptive text with seventy regional maps. It belongs to Roger’s making as patron and organizer, not as author; the surviving manuscript tradition is later and does not preserve Roger’s presentation copy intact. ([Terra Chartarum](https://www.terra-chartarum.org/collection/idrisi/); [Encyclopaedia Britannica, “al-Sharīf al-Idrīsī”](https://www.britannica.com/biography/al-Sharif-al-Idrisi))

- **1154:** *World map associated with the Book of Roger*, usually called the *Tabula Rogeriana*. A cartographic manifestation within al-Idrisi’s commissioned geographic project, conventionally represented today by reconstructions assembled from its sectional maps. It should not be confused with a single securely surviving original sheet drawn for Roger. ([Terra Chartarum](https://www.terra-chartarum.org/collection/idrisi/); [Bodleian Libraries, al-Idrisi manuscript record](https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/objects/48117e74-4e3d-4f38-9ee4-b148eb15fdd1/))

- **before 1154; lost:** *Silver planisphere*. Al-Idrisi describes a large silver disk bearing a map of the world, made for Roger as part of the same geographic project. The object itself is lost, so its dimensions, appearance, and exact relation to the surviving textual maps depend upon al-Idrisi’s account rather than material evidence. ([Encyclopaedia Britannica, “al-Sharīf al-Idrīsī”](https://www.britannica.com/biography/al-Sharif-al-Idrisi); [Terra Chartarum](https://www.terra-chartarum.org/collection/idrisi/))

### Architectural foundations and total works

- **founded 1131; unfinished at Roger’s death in 1154:** *Cathedral of Cefalù*. A royal foundation conceived as cathedral, dynastic burial church, and monumental statement of kingship. Its architecture, mosaics, liturgical furnishings, and intended royal tombs form a Gesamtkunstwerk, but construction proceeded in distinct phases and substantial parts postdate Roger, making “Roger’s cathedral” a project rather than a wholly completed personal work. ([Treccani, “La Sicilia normanna: Cefalù, Palermo, Monreale”](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/la-sicilia-normanna-cefalu-palermo-monreale_%28Storia-della-civilt%C3%A0-europea-a-cura-di-Umberto-Eco%29/); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1487.pdf); [“Rogerius structor”](https://ojs.unica.it/index.php/abside/article/view/6581))

- **begun c. 1132; consecrated 1140; decoration continuing thereafter:** *Cappella Palatina, Palermo*. Roger’s palace chapel is an architectural and ceremonial Gesamtkunstwerk integrating Latin basilican planning, Byzantine mosaics, an Islamicizing painted *muqarnas* ceiling, marble revetment, inscriptions, and royal ritual. Its parts were produced by many workshops and at different dates, so Roger is properly its founder and commissioning patron, not its individual artisan. ([William Tronzo, *The Cultures of His Kingdom*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1pzk0j7); [University of Palermo study](https://iris.unipa.it/handle/10447/73780); [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1487/))

- **1130s–1150s:** *Decorative program of the Cappella Palatina*. A coordinated manifestation comprising mosaics, carved and painted wooden ceiling, marble pavement and wall revetment, inscriptions, and furnishings. It can be analytically separated from the building, but doing so obscures its function as a courtly environment; dating individual campaigns and assigning responsibility between Roger, clerics, administrators, and workshops remain subjects of scholarship. ([William Tronzo, *The Cultures of His Kingdom*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1pzk0j7); [Ernst Kitzinger, *The Mosaics of the Cappella Palatina in Palermo*](https://archive.org/details/mosaicsofcappell0000kitz); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1487.pdf))

- **1131–1148:** *Mosaic program of Cefalù Cathedral*. A royal commission centered on the apse image of Christ Pantokrator and its attendant sacred hierarchy. The surviving inscription dates the principal sanctuary mosaics to 1148, but the extent of Roger’s planned program and the reason work stopped before filling the church remain uncertain. ([Treccani, “La Sicilia normanna”](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/la-sicilia-normanna-cefalu-palermo-monreale_%28Storia-della-civilt%C3%A0-europea-a-cura-di-Umberto-Eco%29/); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1487.pdf))

- **c. 1132:** *Monastery and church of San Giovanni degli Eremiti, Palermo*. A Latin monastic foundation established by Roger near the royal palace, incorporating or replacing earlier structures on the site. The familiar red-domed complex has undergone extensive later alteration and restoration, so the present monument is not an untouched Rogerian object. ([UNESCO, Arab-Norman Palermo](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1487/); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1487.pdf))

- **1120s–1130s:** *Archimandritate of the Holy Saviour, Messina*. A royal monastic project that gathered Greek-rite monasteries under the authority of San Salvatore and made the institution a central instrument of Roger’s ecclesiastical government. Its classification as an obra lies in institutional foundation and endowment rather than in a single surviving building; the original complex was repeatedly transformed and ultimately destroyed. ([Hubert Houben, *Roger II of Sicily*](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805216/52087/excerpt/9780521652087_excerpt.pdf); [Paul Oldfield, *Sanctity and Pilgrimage in Medieval Southern Italy*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139018116))

- **before 1154:** *Royal Palace complex, Palermo, Rogerian enlargement*. Roger transformed the existing fortified palace into the seat and ceremonial apparatus of the new kingdom, including the Cappella Palatina and spaces associated with the royal administration and workshops. Because the palace incorporates Islamic, Norman, and later rebuilding, no secure catalogue allows every surviving room or tower to be assigned personally to Roger. ([UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1487.pdf); [UNESCO, Arab-Norman Palermo](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1487/))

### Royal-workshop objects and regalia

- **1133–1134:** *Mantle of Roger II*, traditionally the “Coronation Mantle.” A monumental silk, gold, pearl, enamel, and gemstone textile made in the Palermo royal workshop and dated by its Arabic inscription. Since Roger was crowned in 1130, it was not made for that coronation; its original ceremonial function is uncertain, and “coronation mantle” describes its later use in the Holy Roman imperial regalia. ([Kunsthistorisches Museum Wien](https://www.khm.at/de/objektdb/detail/100435/); [Discover Islamic Art](https://islamicart.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?cp=&id=object%3BEPM%3Bat%3BMus22%3B44%3Ben); [Smarthistory](https://smarthistory.org/coronation-mantle/))

- **1145:** *Pair of porphyry sarcophagi for Cefalù Cathedral*. Roger ordered two porphyry tombs with canopies for the dynastic burial setting he intended at Cefalù. The project was not fulfilled as planned: the tombs were later transferred to Palermo, reused within the Hohenstaufen royal necropolis, and cannot now be mapped onto Roger’s original intentions without dispute. ([Treccani, “La Sicilia normanna”](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/la-sicilia-normanna-cefalu-palermo-monreale_%28Storia-della-civilt%C3%A0-europea-a-cura-di-Umberto-Eco%29/); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1487.pdf))

### Coinage as serial obra

- **1105–1127:** *Comital coinage of Roger II*. A changing body of gold *tarì* and copper issues struck in Roger’s name as count of Sicily and Calabria. Individual dies and varieties are workshop products rather than objects personally designed by Roger, but the series is a royal manifestation traceable through the modern die-and-type catalogue. ([Andrea Saccocci et al., *Italian Coins in the British Museum*, vol. 1](https://air.unimi.it/bitstream/2434/821348/2/Italian%20Coins%20in%20the%20British%20Museum%201-pdf.pdf))

- **1127–1130:** *Ducal coinage of Roger II*. Coins issued after Roger acquired the mainland duchy, carrying titles and imagery appropriate to his expanded authority. The catalogue treats this as a distinct phase rather than as editions or reprints of a single coin. ([Andrea Saccocci et al., *Italian Coins in the British Museum*, vol. 1](https://air.unimi.it/bitstream/2434/821348/2/Italian%20Coins%20in%20the%20British%20Museum%201-pdf.pdf))

- **1130–1140:** *Early royal coinage*. A multilingual and multimetallic serial obra—gold *tarì*, copper *follari*, and related denominations—through which Roger’s new kingship appeared in Arabic, Greek, and Latin forms. Attribution and sequencing depend on mints, legends, overstrikes, and die study rather than securely recorded issue dates. ([Andrea Saccocci et al., *Italian Coins in the British Museum*, vol. 1](https://air.unimi.it/bitstream/2434/821348/2/Italian%20Coins%20in%20the%20British%20Museum%201-pdf.pdf))

- **1140–1154:** *Reformed royal coinage, including the silver ducalis*. The material implementation of Roger’s monetary reform; the *ducalis* depicts Roger with his son Roger, duke of Apulia, while other denominations circulated without royal portraiture. It is best classified as a controlled series and governing protocol rather than as one artwork. ([Andrea Saccocci et al., *Italian Coins in the British Museum*, vol. 1](https://air.unimi.it/bitstream/2434/821348/2/Italian%20Coins%20in%20the%20British%20Museum%201-pdf.pdf))
