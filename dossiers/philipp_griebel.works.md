## Works by Philipp Griebel of Gräfenroda

### Terracotta animal sculpture

- **1874–1893:** Untitled animal heads and animal figures. Griebel’s principal obra was a serially reproducible body of painted terracotta sculpture for houses and gardens, developed from his training as an animal-head modeller; documented subjects include deer, foxes, large hunting dogs, storks, and other woodland animals. No surviving scholarly catalogue enumerates every model, and later factory stock incorporates models acquired from other makers, so individual attribution is often impossible. ([Bengen, pp. 67–73](https://www.etta-bengen.de/media/Welt.pdf); [Zwergstatt history](https://zwergstatt-graefenroda.de/geschichte/); [Zwergstatt manufactory account](https://zwergstatt-graefenroda.de/en/manufactory/))

- **c. 1890:** *Fuchs auf einem Kaninchenbau* (*Fox on a Rabbit Warren*). A modelled terracotta animal group and the only individually titled object that the standard historical survey explicitly illustrates and attributes to Philipp Griebel himself. ([Bengen, pp. 70–71](https://www.etta-bengen.de/media/Welt.pdf))

### Fairy-tale figures

- **1874–1893:** Untitled terracotta fairy-tale figures. These formed part of the factory’s early production alongside animals and animal heads, but the consulted historical survey supplies neither model titles nor a secure item-by-item attribution to Griebel rather than his workshop; they therefore resist treatment as a catalogue of autonomous works. ([Bengen, p. 46](https://www.etta-bengen.de/media/Welt.pdf))

### Garden gnomes

- **c. 1880–1893:** Untitled early Gräfenroda gnome models. These were hollow-moulded, fired, and painted terracotta garden figures, conceived for serial production rather than as unique sculptures. The surviving evidence does not establish a complete list of Griebel’s models, and the present manufactory cautions that similarities among makers and the later accumulation of competitors’ moulds make attribution difficult. ([Zwergstatt history](https://zwergstatt-graefenroda.de/geschichte/); [Zwergstatt manufactory account](https://zwergstatt-graefenroda.de/en/manufactory/))

- **c. 1890:** Untitled “first” Griebel garden gnome. The Griebel museum tradition credits Philipp Griebel with introducing his first gnome around 1890, reportedly inspired by local miners; early examples represented serious or amiably smiling, weathered, bearded older men rather than later caricatures. This object resists secure work status: no unique specimen, fixed title, or model number is identified, and priority is disputed between Griebel and August Heissner. ([Bengen, pp. 67–72](https://www.etta-bengen.de/media/Welt.pdf))

### Workshop project

- **1874–1893:** *Terrakottafiguren-Fabrik Philipp Griebel*. A continuing project and collective obra rather than a single object: the workshop founded by Griebel to model and serially manufacture terracotta figures, exhibiting animal figures at the Leipzig Trade Fair by 1876 and later adding gnomes. Products bearing the continuing firm name cannot automatically be counted as Philipp Griebel’s personal works, because his descendants operated the factory after his death and its historical mould stock also absorbed models by other manufacturers. ([Zwergstatt history](https://zwergstatt-graefenroda.de/geschichte/); [Bengen, pp. 69–73](https://www.etta-bengen.de/media/Welt.pdf); [Museum Grimma](https://museum-grimma.de/gartenzwerge-aus-graefenroda/))
