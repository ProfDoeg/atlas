## Works by Charles Robert Darwin

### Voyage books, scientific monographs, and edited works

- **1829–1832:** *Records of Captured Insects*. Short entomological records supplied to James Francis Stephens’s *Illustrations of British Entomology*; contributions rather than a freestanding book. [Darwin Online, F1968](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **[1835]:** *Extracts from Letters Addressed to Professor Henslow*. A privately printed assemblage of Darwin’s geological and natural-history letters from the voyage; Henslow selected the extracts, so the pamphlet is both Darwin’s writing and another person’s editorial construction. [Darwin Online, F1](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1838–1843:** *The Zoology of the Voyage of H.M.S. Beagle*. A five-part collaborative zoological publication edited and superintended by Darwin. Owen, Waterhouse, Gould, Jenyns, and Bell wrote the principal taxonomic sections; Darwin supplied introductions and observations, making the whole an editorial project rather than a solely authored book. [Darwin Online, F8–F9](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1839:** *Journal and Remarks, 1832–1836*. Darwin’s contribution to volume 3 of FitzRoy’s *Narrative of the Surveying Voyages*—simultaneously travel narrative, scientific journal, and component of a collaborative expedition record. The separately issued *Journal of Researches* is the same textual obra in another bibliographical manifestation, not a different journey. [Darwin Online, F10.3–F11](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1842:** *The Structure and Distribution of Coral Reefs*. A geological monograph and the first published component of Darwin’s projected geology of the *Beagle* voyage. [Darwin Online, F271](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1844:** *Geological Observations on the Volcanic Islands Visited during the Voyage of H.M.S. Beagle*. The second monograph in the voyage-geology project. [Darwin Online, F272](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1846:** *Geological Observations on South America*. The third monograph in the voyage-geology project; later collected editions recombined it with the volcanic-islands volume but did not create a new underlying work. [Darwin Online, F273–F276](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1851:** *A Monograph of the Sub-class Cirripedia: The Lepadidae; or, Pedunculated Cirripedes*. The first volume of Darwin’s taxonomic obra on living barnacles. [Darwin Online, F339.1](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1851:** *A Monograph on the Fossil Lepadidae, or, Pedunculated Cirripedes of Great Britain*. A palaeontological monograph issued by the Palaeontographical Society and distinct from the corresponding study of living forms. [Darwin Online, F342.1](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1854:** *A Monograph of the Sub-class Cirripedia: The Balanidae (or Sessile Cirripedes), the Verrucidae, etc.* The second volume of the living-barnacle obra. [Darwin Online, F339.2](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1854:** *A Monograph on the Fossil Balanidae and Verrucidae of Great Britain*. The second fossil-barnacle monograph. [Darwin Online, F342.2](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1858:** *On the Tendency of Species to Form Varieties; and on the Perpetuation of Varieties and Species by Natural Means of Selection*. A composite scientific communication assembled by Charles Lyell and Joseph Hooker from writings by Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace. Its authorship is deliberately plural, and neither Darwin nor Wallace attended the reading. [Darwin Online, F350](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1859:** *On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection, or the Preservation of Favoured Races in the Struggle for Life*. A scientific argument presented by Darwin as an abstract of a much larger project. Six authorized English editions substantially revised the text, but they are manifestations and revisions of the same obra rather than six independent works. [Darwin Online, F373 and later editions](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1862:** *On the Various Contrivances by Which British and Foreign Orchids Are Fertilised by Insects*. An experimental botanical monograph; the substantially revised 1877 title, *The Various Contrivances by Which Orchids Are Fertilised by Insects*, remains its second edition rather than a separate work. [Darwin Online, F800–F803](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1862:** *Recollections of Professor Henslow*. Darwin’s biographical contribution to Leonard Jenyns’s *Memoir of the Rev. John Stevens Henslow*, not a separately authored biography. [Darwin Online, F830](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1865:** *On the Movements and Habits of Climbing Plants*. A journal monograph later transformed into a revised book under the title *The Movements and Habits of Climbing Plants*; these are successive manifestations of one botanical study. [Darwin Online, F834a–F839](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1868:** *The Variation of Animals and Plants under Domestication*. A two-volume experimental and synthetic treatise, also the principal completed portion of Darwin’s planned larger species work; it introduced his provisional hypothesis of pangenesis. [Darwin Online, F877](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1871:** *The Descent of Man, and Selection in Relation to Sex*. A two-volume treatise applying evolutionary explanation to human descent and elaborating sexual selection. Darwin extensively recast it for the one-volume second edition of 1874, but that remains a revised manifestation of the same obra. [Darwin Online, F937–F948](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1872:** *The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals*. A comparative work combining argument, observation, illustration, and photography; its mixed evidentiary media make it more than a purely verbal text. [Darwin Online, F1142](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1875:** *Insectivorous Plants*. An experimental botanical monograph on plant movement, digestion, and response. [Darwin Online, F1217](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1876:** *The Effects of Cross and Self Fertilisation in the Vegetable Kingdom*. An experimental monograph built from long-running plant trials and numerical comparisons. [Darwin Online, F1249](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1877:** *The Different Forms of Flowers on Plants of the Same Species*. A botanical synthesis of Darwin’s work on dimorphism, trimorphism, and heterostyly. [Darwin Online, F1277](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1879:** *Preliminary Notice* to Ernst Krause’s *Erasmus Darwin*. A substantial biographical study of Darwin’s grandfather joined to Krause’s scientific essay; the composite volume resists attribution as either Darwin’s book alone or a mere preface. [Darwin Online, F1319](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1880:** *Prefatory Notice* to August Weismann’s *Studies in the Theory of Descent*. A framing essay attached to another scientist’s translated work. [Darwin Online, F1414](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1880:** *The Power of Movement in Plants*. An experimental monograph jointly credited to Charles Darwin and Francis Darwin; treating it as Charles’s work alone would erase its declared collaboration. [Darwin Online, F1325](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1881:** *The Formation of Vegetable Mould, through the Action of Worms, with Observations on Their Habits*. An experimental and geological monograph on earthworms as agents of landscape change. [Darwin Online, F1357](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1882:** *Notes on Instinct*. Manuscript material incorporated after Darwin’s death into George Romanes’s *Animal Intelligence*; it survives as a contribution shaped by another author’s book rather than an autonomous publication. [Darwin Online, F1416](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1883:** *Prefatory Notice* to Hermann Müller’s *The Fertilisation of Flowers*. A posthumously published introductory text, completed as part of Darwin’s support for Müller’s work. [Darwin Online, F1432](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1883:** *A Posthumous Essay on Instinct*. A manuscript essay published by George Romanes in *Mental Evolution in Animals*. An uncorrected Linnean Society proof also survives, so its status lies between Darwin’s manuscript obra and Romanes’s posthumous editorial manifestation. [Darwin Online, F1434 and F3534](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

### Protocols, questionnaires, and texts intended to act

- **[1839]:** *Questions about the Breeding of Animals*. A privately printed questionnaire: a protocol for distributed observation rather than a descriptive treatise. Its date is conventionally supplied in brackets because the original sheet is undated. [Darwin Online, F262](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1841:** *Queries Respecting the Human Race, to Be Addressed to Travellers and Others*. A collective British Association protocol produced by a committee that included Darwin; individual wording cannot always be securely apportioned to him. [Darwin Online, F1975](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1849:** *Geology*. Darwin’s instructional chapter in John Herschel’s *Manual of Scientific Enquiry*, written as a field protocol for travellers and naval officers. [Darwin Online, F325](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1849:** *On the Use of the Microscope on Board Ship*. An instructional appendix in the same manual; its object is practical action under shipboard conditions. [Darwin Online, F1822](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1867:** *Queries about Expression*. A circulated questionnaire designed to gather observations across cultures. Later journal and anthropological-handbook printings are manifestations of this protocol, not independent works. [Darwin Online, F873–F876](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1877:** *To Members of the Down Friendly Club*. A printed address governing the affairs of a local mutual-aid society; it acts institutionally and is closer to a manifesto or administrative intervention than to a scientific publication. [Darwin Online, F1303](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#books)

- **1863–1882:** *Appeals, declarations, memorials, and testimonials*. A body of public-action texts concerning steel animal traps, vivisection, war, university tests, scientific institutions, giant-tortoise protection, Niagara Falls, Russian Jews, and individual appointments. Many were jointly signed, so they are acts in which Darwin participated rather than works attributable to him alone. [Darwin Online, “Articles & Printed Letters”](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

### Short scientific papers, notices, reviews, and printed letters

- **1836–1882:** *Shorter Publications*. An obra comprising Darwin’s journal articles, society papers, reviews, notes, queries, newspaper interventions, and printed letters—ranging from South American geology and seed dispersal to orchid fertilisation, animal behaviour, plant movement, and inheritance. Individual offprints, translations, and repeated newspaper printings are manifestations; the scholarly collected edition identifies the distinct shorter publications without mistaking every printing for a new work. [Darwin Online, *Charles Darwin’s Shorter Publications 1829–1883*, F2043](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1836:** *A Letter, Containing Remarks on the Moral State of Tahiti, New Zealand, &c.* A jointly attributed letter by Robert FitzRoy and Darwin; the precise division of authorship is not securely recoverable. [Darwin Online, F1640](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1836:** *Geological Notes Made during a Survey of the East and West Coasts of South America*. Darwin’s first substantial published geological paper from the voyage. [Darwin Online, F1642](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1837:** *Observations of Proofs of Recent Elevation on the Coast of Chili*. A Geological Society paper interpreting coastal evidence gathered during the voyage. [Darwin Online, F1645](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1837:** *A Sketch of the Deposits Containing Extinct Mammalia in the Neighbourhood of the Plata*. A geological-palaeontological paper based on Darwin’s South American observations and collections. [Darwin Online, F1646](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1837:** *On Certain Areas of Elevation and Subsidence in the Pacific and Indian Oceans*. A paper formulating the tectonic framework behind Darwin’s coral-reef theory. [Darwin Online, F1647](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1840:** *On the Connexion of Certain Volcanic Phenomena in South America*. A geological paper linking volcanism, mountain building, and continental elevation. [Darwin Online, F1656](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1840:** *On the Formation of Mould*. The first published formulation of the earthworm research Darwin would expand into his final book four decades later. [Darwin Online, F1655](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1844:** *Observations on the Structure and Propagation of the Genus Sagitta*. A zoological paper derived from voyage observations. [Darwin Online, F1664](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1844:** *Brief Descriptions of Several Terrestrial Planariae, and of Some Remarkable Marine Species*. A descriptive zoological paper incorporating observations of behaviour. [Darwin Online, F1669](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1857:** *On the Action of Sea-Water on the Germination of Seeds*. An experimental paper addressing possible mechanisms of geographical dispersal. [Darwin Online, F1694](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1862:** *On the Two Forms, or Dimorphic Condition, in the Species of Primula*. A botanical paper establishing the reproductive significance of floral dimorphism. [Darwin Online, F1717](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1863:** *On the Existence of Two Forms, and on Their Reciprocal Sexual Relation, in Several Species of the Genus Linum*. A continuation of the heterostyly project. [Darwin Online, F1723](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1863:** *On the So-called “Auditory-sac” of Cirripedes*. A corrective anatomical paper arising from Darwin’s barnacle research. [Darwin Online, F1722](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1863:** *Review of Contributions to an Insect Fauna of the Amazon Valley*. Darwin’s review of Henry Walter Bates’s work; it is criticism and advocacy as well as scientific exposition. [Darwin Online, F1725](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1865:** *Three Forms of Lythrum salicaria*. An experimental paper on trimorphism, later absorbed into *The Different Forms of Flowers*. [Darwin Online, “Articles & Printed Letters”](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1868:** *On the Character and Hybrid-like Nature of the Offspring from the Illegitimate Unions of Dimorphic and Trimorphic Plants*. A technical paper extending Darwin’s experimental work on heterostyly. [Darwin Online, F1742](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1869:** *Notes on the Fertilization of Orchids*. A shorter continuation of the orchid-fertilisation project. [Darwin Online, F1748](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1871:** *Pangenesis*. A reply defending and clarifying Darwin’s provisional theory of heredity. [Darwin Online, F1751](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1877:** *A Biographical Sketch of an Infant*. A scientific article developed from observations of Darwin’s son William made decades earlier; it is both family record and contribution to developmental psychology. [Darwin Online, F1779](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

- **1882:** *The Action of Carbonate of Ammonia on Chlorophyll-bodies*. Darwin’s final scientific paper, published in the year of his death. [Darwin Online, F1801](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#articles)

### Voyage notebooks, diaries, and specimen records

- **1826:** *Edinburgh Diary*. A student diary; it resists being called a public “work,” but survives as a deliberately maintained personal record. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR129](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1827:** *Observations on the Ova of Flustra*. A student paper read to Edinburgh’s Plinian Society. The society did not publish its proceedings and the original manuscript has not survived intact, so the work is known through later records and an early notebook rather than a definitive text. [Darwin Online, F1583a](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#publishedmanuscripts)

- **1827:** *Observations on the “Eggs” of a Marine Leech*. Another Plinian Society paper; Darwin’s identification was mistaken, and the original paper is lost, making this an incompletely recoverable scientific act rather than a stable textual object. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1831–1836:** *Beagle Field Notebooks*. Fifteen interlinked field notebooks containing words, figures, maps, and sketches. Their conventional place-name titles are later labels and are not always verbatim or contemporaneous, so the set is better understood as a working apparatus than as fifteen finished books. [Darwin Online, introduction to the field notebooks](https://darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/Chancellor_fieldNotebooks.html)

- **1831–1836:** *Beagle Specimen Notebooks*. Three records for animals and dried plants, three for specimens preserved in spirits, and four geological specimen books. These are cataloguing instruments tied to physical collections and resist separation into “text” and “object.” [Darwin Online, “Darwin’s Papers & Manuscripts”](https://darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/vanWyhe_Manuscripts.html)

- **1832–1836:** *Zoological Diary*. An extensive manuscript record of animals, plants, fungi, localities, and behaviour from the voyage, distinct from the personal diary and later published narrative. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR30–31](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1832–1836:** *Geological Diary*. Darwin’s longest voyage manuscript: a multi-volume observational record from which the later geological publications were developed. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR32–38](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1831–1836:** *Beagle Diary*. A personal and travel diary later mined and rewritten for *Journal and Remarks*; manuscript diary and published travel book are related but not identical works. [Darwin Online, “Darwin’s Papers & Manuscripts”](https://darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/vanWyhe_Manuscripts.html)

### Evolutionary, philosophical, and experimental manuscript projects

- **1836–1837:** *Red Notebook*. A post-voyage notebook bridging geological reflection and Darwin’s earliest evolutionary theorizing. Its colour-name is archival convention rather than an authorial title. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1837–1839:** *Notebooks B, C, D, and E on Transmutation of Species*. Four sequential working notebooks in which Darwin developed his species theory. They are projects of inquiry, not polished treatises, and their letter names are archival identifiers. [Barrett, *Early Writings of Charles Darwin*](https://darwin-online.org.uk/converted/published/1974_Barrett_F1582/1974_Barrett_F1582.html)

- **1838–1839:** *Notebooks M and N on Man, Mind, and Materialism*. Working notebooks on psychology, human origins, morality, and materialism; their heterogeneous entries resist classification as a single argument. [Barrett, *Early Writings of Charles Darwin*](https://darwin-online.org.uk/converted/published/1974_Barrett_F1582/1974_Barrett_F1582.html)

- **1838–1840:** *Old and Useless Notes about the Moral Sense & Some Metaphysical Points*. Darwin’s own dismissive label for a continuing philosophical notebook. Its resistance to “work” status is explicit in its title, although it preserves a coherent project concerning mind and morality. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR91.4–55](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1838:** *Glen Roy Notebook*. A geological field notebook devoted to the Scottish parallel roads; it combines observations and diagrams rather than forming a finished paper. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR130](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1838:** *St Helena Model Notebook*. A working notebook concerning Darwin’s model of geological elevation and subsidence, produced after the voyage despite its place-name label. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1839–1844:** *Questions & Experiments Notebook*. A research protocol in continuing manuscript form: proposed tests, questions, observations, and results rather than a publishable linear text. [Darwin Online, notebook catalogue](https://test.darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/vanWyhe_notebooks.html)

- **1842:** *Pencil Sketch of the Species Theory*. The first compact exposition of Darwin’s evolutionary theory, written privately and left unpublished in his lifetime. It is a completed sketch but also the provisional beginning of a larger project. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR6](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1844:** *Essay on Species Theory*. A substantially enlarged private essay, preserved in draft and fair-copy forms and published only posthumously. The surviving textual plurality prevents any single manuscript from being treated unproblematically as “the” work. [Darwin Online, F1556 and CUL-DAR6](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#publishedmanuscripts)

- **1855–1867:** *Experiment Book*. A continuing laboratory and garden record shared in places with members of Darwin’s household. It is an experimental apparatus in manuscript form rather than a finished literary work. [Darwin Online, notebook catalogue](https://test.darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/vanWyhe_notebooks.html)

- **1856–1858:** *Natural Selection*. Darwin’s unfinished “big species book,” abandoned when Wallace’s manuscript precipitated the 1858 joint communication and the writing of *Origin*. The surviving chapters constitute a major project even though Darwin never completed or published it as planned. [Darwin Online, F1583 and manuscript catalogue](https://darwin-online.org.uk/contents.html#publishedmanuscripts)

- **1838–1860:** *Books Read / Books to Be Read*. Three overlapping reading notebooks. They are records and research infrastructure rather than authored works in the ordinary sense, but they materially shaped Darwin’s projects. [Darwin Online, notebook catalogue](https://test.darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/vanWyhe_notebooks.html)

- **1839–1856:** *Notebook of Observations on the Darwin Children*. A longitudinal family observation record that later supplied *A Biographical Sketch of an Infant* and material on expression. It resists separation into private document, dataset, and scientific project. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR210.11.37](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1855–1859:** *Catalogue of Down Specimens Notebook*. A manuscript inventory connecting labels, specimens, experiments, and place; it functions as a catalogue rather than a discursive text. [Darwin Online, notebook catalogue](https://test.darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/vanWyhe_notebooks.html)

### Autobiographical and memorial writings

- **1838:** *Life. Written August 1838*. An unfinished autobiographical fragment covering Darwin’s early life. It was not published in his lifetime and has no more elaborate authorial title. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR91.56–63](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1838–1881:** *Journal*. A retrospective and continuing chronology of major events in Darwin’s life, begun in 1838. It is neither the *Beagle Diary* nor a conventional intimate diary, but a personal reference record. [Darwin Online, notebook catalogue](https://test.darwin-online.org.uk/EditorialIntroductions/vanWyhe_notebooks.html)

- **1851:** *Our Poor Child, Annie*. A private memorial of Anne Elizabeth Darwin. It resists classification as a formal work: it is an act of mourning preserved among family papers and published only posthumously. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR210.13.40](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1858:** *Our Poor Baby*. A private remembrance of Charles Waring Darwin, likewise a memorial document rather than a work prepared for readers. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR210.13.42](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1866–1875:** *A Sketch of the Principal Events in My Life*. A group of short autobiographical memoranda, not a single polished narrative. [Darwin Online manuscript catalogue, CUL-DAR91.102–113](https://darwin-online.org.uk/manuscripts.html)

- **1876–1881:** *Recollections of the Development of My Mind and Character*. Darwin’s autobiography, written principally between 28 May and 3 August 1876, with later changes. Francis Darwin’s 1887 publication omitted family-sensitive passages; Nora Barlow’s 1958 edition restored the omissions, so neither early print manifestation simply equals the manuscript. [Freeman, *Charles Darwin: A Companion*](https://darwin-online.org.uk/content/contentblock?basepage=1&hitpage=221&itemID=A27&viewtype=text)

### Correspondence as an obra that resists being a work

- **1821–1882:** *Correspondence of Charles Darwin*. Thousands of surviving letters constitute a distributed scientific, familial, administrative, and social practice rather than a work Darwin designed as a whole. Individual letters later printed in newspapers or books sometimes became publications, but the correspondence corpus itself resists classification as a single authored text; the completed scholarly edition contains both letters by Darwin and letters addressed to him. [Darwin Correspondence Project](https://www.darwinproject.ac.uk/)
