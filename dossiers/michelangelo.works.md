## Works by Michelangelo di Lodovico Buonarroti Simoni

### Sculpture and sculptural projects

- **c. 1489–1490:** *Head of a Faun*. Marble sculpture described by early biographers but now lost; its appearance and even its status as an independent finished work cannot be verified (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper, sculpture catalogue).
- **c. 1490–1492:** *Madonna of the Stairs*. Marble relief in *rilievo schiacciato*, generally accepted as Michelangelo’s earliest surviving sculpture (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S1).
- **c. 1491–1492:** *Battle of the Centaurs*. Unfinished marble relief, conceived as an autonomous classical-historical composition rather than as architectural decoration (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S2).
- **1492:** *Snowman for Piero de’ Medici*. An ephemeral sculpture reportedly made from snow; it survives only in textual testimony and resists the ordinary idea of a durable artwork.
- **c. 1492–1493:** *Crucifix for Santo Spirito*. Polychromed wooden devotional sculpture; now generally accepted as Michelangelo’s, although the attribution was established only after its modern rediscovery (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S3).
- **c. 1492–1494:** *Hercules*. Large marble statue made in Florence and later sent to France; lost, and known through documentary and secondary visual evidence (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S4).
- **1494–1495:** *Saint Petronius*. Marble statuette for the Arca di San Domenico, Bologna; an element within an older, multi-author funerary Gesamtkunstwerk (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S6).
- **1494–1495:** *Saint Proculus*. Marble statuette for the Arca di San Domenico, Bologna; damaged and subsequently restored (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S6).
- **1494–1495:** *Angel with a Candlestick*. Marble kneeling angel for the Arca di San Domenico, Bologna; one component of the collaborative monument (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S6).
- **c. 1495–1496:** *Young Saint John the Baptist*. Lost or unidentified marble statue formerly associated with Lorenzo di Pierfrancesco de’ Medici; proposed identifications remain disputed.
- **1496:** *Sleeping Cupid*. Artificially aged marble sold as an antique; lost. It was simultaneously sculpture, imitation, and commercial deception, which makes its intended identity part of the obra (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S5).
- **1496–1497:** *Bacchus*. Marble statue with a satyr, made for Cardinal Raffaele Riario but rejected by him; substantially intact though damaged (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S7).
- **c. 1497:** *Cupid*. Marble figure now known chiefly through descriptions; its identity and relation to other lost Cupids are uncertain.
- **1498–1499:** *Vatican Pietà*. Signed marble group made for Cardinal Jean Bilhères de Lagraulas and installed in St Peter’s; a completed funerary-devotional sculpture (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S8).
- **1501–1504:** *David*. Colossal marble statue carved from a long-abandoned block; originally a cathedral project, it became a Florentine civic monument before being moved indoors (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S10).
- **1501–1504:** *Madonna and Child (Bruges Madonna)*. Marble devotional group, apparently diverted from an Italian commission and sold to Bruges (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S9).
- **1501–1504:** *Saint Peter*. Marble statuette for the Piccolomini altar in Siena Cathedral; part of a commission that Michelangelo left incomplete (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S9).
- **1501–1504:** *Saint Paul*. Marble statuette for the Piccolomini altar, Siena (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S9).
- **1501–1504:** *Saint Pius*. Marble statuette for the Piccolomini altar, Siena (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S9).
- **1501–1504:** *Saint Gregory*. Marble statuette for the Piccolomini altar, Siena (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S9).
- **c. 1502–1508:** *David of Rohan*. Colossal bronze statue commissioned for Florentine civic display and later sent to France; lost, and sometimes called the *Bronze David*.
- **c. 1503–1504:** *Pitti Tondo*. Unfinished marble relief of the Virgin, Child, and infant Saint John; the visible incompletion is integral to its present identity (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S11).
- **c. 1504–1506:** *Taddei Tondo*. Unfinished marble relief of the Virgin and Child with the infant Saint John (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S12).
- **1505–1545:** *Tomb of Pope Julius II*. A repeatedly contracted, radically reduced funerary project: first conceived as a vast freestanding monument, it ultimately became a wall tomb in San Pietro in Vincoli. The changing project—not merely the installed monument—is the obra (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **1505–1506:** *Moses*. Monumental marble figure eventually installed at the centre of the reduced Tomb of Julius II; substantially reworked by Michelangelo in later decades (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **c. 1513–1516:** *Dying Slave*. Marble figure for an abandoned phase of the Tomb of Julius II; highly finished but never installed in the tomb (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **c. 1513–1516:** *Rebellious Slave*. Marble figure for the same abandoned tomb scheme; unfinished in places and never installed (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **c. 1519–1534:** *Young Slave*. Unfinished marble figure associated with a later design for the Julius tomb (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **c. 1519–1534:** *Bearded Slave*. Unfinished marble figure associated with the Julius tomb (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **c. 1519–1534:** *Atlas Slave*. Unfinished marble figure associated with the Julius tomb (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **c. 1519–1534:** *Awakening Slave*. Unfinished marble figure associated with the Julius tomb (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **c. 1527–1530:** *Genius of Victory*. Marble group usually connected with the Julius tomb, although its exact intended position and date remain debated (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **1542–1545:** *Rachel*. Marble figure installed on the completed Tomb of Julius II; assistants participated in the monument’s execution (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **1542–1545:** *Leah*. Marble figure installed on the completed Tomb of Julius II; workshop participation complicates the idea of single authorship (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S15).
- **1505–1506:** *Saint Matthew*. Unfinished marble apostle begun for Florence Cathedral; the sole material beginning of a projected twelve-statue series (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S13).
- **1506–1508:** *Bronze Portrait of Pope Julius II*. Monumental seated statue made for San Petronio, Bologna; destroyed in 1511 and recast as artillery, so the obra survives through documents and descriptions (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S14).
- **1514–1516:** *Risen Christ, first version*. Abandoned marble version of the Minerva *Christ*, rejected after a dark vein appeared in the face; later completed or altered by another hand (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S16).
- **1519–1521:** *Risen Christ (Christ Carrying the Cross)*. Marble devotional statue in Santa Maria sopra Minerva; assistants completed details, and a later bronze covering materially altered the nude figure (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S16).
- **1520–1534:** *Medici Chapel sculptural ensemble*. A funerary Gesamtkunstwerk integrating architecture, tombs, statuary, and planned painting; left incomplete when Michelangelo permanently departed Florence (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S17).
- **1524–1534:** *Giuliano de’ Medici, Duke of Nemours*. Idealized seated marble figure for the Medici Chapel rather than a literal portrait (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S17).
- **1524–1534:** *Night*. Reclining marble personification on Giuliano’s tomb; finished to differing degrees across its surface (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S17).
- **1524–1534:** *Day*. Reclining marble personification on Giuliano’s tomb; conspicuously unfinished, especially in the head (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S17).
- **1524–1534:** *Lorenzo de’ Medici, Duke of Urbino*. Idealized seated marble figure for the Medici Chapel (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S17).
- **1524–1534:** *Dawn*. Reclining marble personification on Lorenzo’s tomb (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S17).
- **1524–1534:** *Dusk*. Reclining marble personification on Lorenzo’s tomb; areas remain unfinished (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S17).
- **c. 1521–1534:** *Medici Madonna*. Unfinished marble group intended for the Medici Chapel’s altar wall (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S17).
- **c. 1524–1534:** *River Gods for the Medici Chapel*. Projected reclining figures known principally through drawings and a surviving full-size clay or earth model; they were never realized in marble, so the project rather than a completed sculpture is the work.
- **c. 1530:** *Crouching Boy*. Small unfinished marble figure, often associated with the Medici Chapel, although its intended function is uncertain (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S18).
- **c. 1530–1532:** *Apollo-David*. Unfinished marble statue whose subject is disputed: inventories and attributes permit identification as either Apollo or David (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S19).
- **c. 1539–1540:** *Brutus*. Unfinished marble bust conceived as an all’antica political portrait; its precise political meaning remains debated (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S20).
- **c. 1547–1555:** *Florentine Pietà (Bandini Pietà)*. Marble group intended for Michelangelo’s own tomb; he attacked and abandoned it, and Tiberio Calcagni subsequently repaired and completed parts of it (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S21).
- **16th century, attribution disputed:** *Palestrina Pietà*. Unfinished marble group traditionally attributed to Michelangelo but rejected or heavily qualified by many modern scholars; workshop or later authorship remains possible.
- **c. 1552–1564:** *Rondanini Pietà*. Repeatedly recarved marble group on which Michelangelo worked until shortly before his death; neither a conventional finished sculpture nor merely an abandoned block, but a continuing project (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper S22).
- **Date and authorship disputed:** *Crucifix at Montserrat*. Small wooden crucifix attributed by some scholars to Michelangelo; the attribution is not secure and should not be silently treated as canonical.

### Paintings and painted projects

- **c. 1487–1488:** *The Torment of Saint Anthony*. Tempera-and-oil panel after an engraving by Martin Schongauer; attribution to the young Michelangelo has gained significant support but is not universally accepted (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P1).
- **c. 1496–1497:** *Madonna and Child with Saint John and Angels (Manchester Madonna)*. Unfinished panel painting; generally attributed substantially to Michelangelo, though the extent of assistants’ work and its precise date remain debated (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P2).
- **c. 1500–1501:** *The Entombment*. Unfinished tempera panel whose attribution to Michelangelo is widely accepted but has a history of dispute (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P3).
- **c. 1504–1506:** *Doni Tondo (Holy Family)*. Completed circular panel painting in its elaborate original frame, whose design is also associated with Michelangelo (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P4).
- **1504–1506:** *Battle of Cascina*. Unexecuted fresco project for the Palazzo Vecchio; Michelangelo produced a celebrated full-size cartoon, now lost, known through copies and related studies. The project and cartoon—not a surviving mural—constitute the obra.
- **1508–1512:** *Sistine Chapel Ceiling*. Monumental fresco programme comprising the Genesis narratives, prophets, sibyls, ancestors of Christ, ignudi, and fictive architecture. It is a single painted architectural system—a Gesamtkunstwerk—rather than merely a collection of detachable scenes (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5; Vatican Museums).
- **1508–1512:** *The Drunkenness of Noah*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **1508–1512:** *The Flood*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **1508–1512:** *The Sacrifice of Noah*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **1508–1512:** *The Fall and Expulsion from Eden*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **1508–1512:** *The Creation of Eve*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **1508–1512:** *The Creation of Adam*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **1508–1512:** *The Separation of Land and Water*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **1508–1512:** *The Creation of the Sun, Moon, and Plants*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **1508–1512:** *The Separation of Light from Darkness*. Fresco within the Sistine Ceiling programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P5.1).
- **c. 1530:** *Leda and the Swan*. Tempera painting made for Alfonso I d’Este but never delivered; the original is lost and its composition survives through copies and a preparatory head study.
- **c. 1531–1534:** *Venus and Cupid*. Full-size cartoon supplied to Jacopo Pontormo, who executed the surviving painting. Michelangelo made the design, not the painted panel; authorship therefore belongs to a collaborative translation between media.
- **1536–1541:** *The Last Judgment*. Monumental fresco on the Sistine Chapel’s altar wall; later censorship, overpainting, restoration, and the destruction of earlier decoration make its present surface historically composite (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P6; Vatican Museums).
- **1542–1545:** *The Conversion of Saul*. Fresco in the Pauline Chapel, Vatican; a completed late mural within a larger chapel programme (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P7).
- **1545–1550:** *The Crucifixion of Saint Peter*. Fresco in the Pauline Chapel and Michelangelo’s last major painted composition (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper P7).

### Architecture and architectural projects

- **1505 onward:** *Architectural designs for the Tomb of Julius II*. Successive schemes transformed a freestanding, multi-storey mausoleum into the reduced wall monument completed in 1545; architecture and sculpture cannot meaningfully be separated in this project.
- **1513–1514:** *Chapel of Leo X at Castel Sant’Angelo*. Architectural and decorative project, subsequently altered or lost; its reconstruction depends on documentary evidence (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A1).
- **1516–1520:** *Façade of San Lorenzo*. Monumental church-façade project supported by models, drawings, quarrying, and contracts but cancelled before construction. It is an unfinished project, not a building (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A2; Ackerman).
- **c. 1517:** *Windows of the Palazzo Medici Riccardi*. Architectural interventions traditionally connected with Michelangelo, including the ground-floor *finestre inginocchiate*; execution involved masons and later alterations.
- **1520–1534:** *New Sacristy (Medici Chapel), San Lorenzo*. Built funerary chapel integrating structure, wall articulation, sculpture, and projected painting; completed and altered by others after Michelangelo left Florence (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A3; Ackerman).
- **1523–1534:** *Laurentian Library reading room*. Architectural interior designed by Michelangelo and executed with workshop and successor participation (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A4; Ackerman).
- **1524–1559:** *Laurentian Library vestibule and staircase*. A spatial and sculptural architectural project; the staircase was realized after Michelangelo sent a model from Rome, with material and details mediated by others (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A4; Ackerman).
- **1524–1534:** *Ricetto and ancillary designs for the Laurentian Library*. Doors, windows, ceiling, floor, desks, and fittings form an integrated design system rather than independent buildings (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A4).
- **1528–1529:** *Fortifications of Florence*. Defensive project comprising surveys, drawings, bastions, and construction during the siege; much was destroyed or absorbed into later works, and the surviving sheets function as operational protocols as well as designs (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A5; Ackerman).
- **1531–1532:** *Tribune for the Relics, San Lorenzo*. Architectural installation for displaying Medici relics; realized with assistance and later modifications (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A6).
- **1538–1564:** *Piazza del Campidoglio*. Urban Gesamtkunstwerk joining trapezoidal space, façades, paving, stairs, sculpture, and civic ceremony; only portions were built in Michelangelo’s lifetime, with later architects interpreting his project (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A8; Ackerman).
- **1538–1564:** *Palazzo Senatorio redesign*. New façade, double stair, and urban focus for the Campidoglio; executed and modified over a long period by other architects (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A8).
- **1538–1564:** *Palazzo dei Conservatori redesign*. Giant-order palace façade for the Campidoglio; only begun during Michelangelo’s lifetime and completed by successors (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A8).
- **1538–1564:** *Palazzo Nuovo project*. Mirroring palace proposed to complete the Campidoglio ensemble; constructed after Michelangelo’s death and therefore best classified as a posthumously realized project (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A8).
- **1538–1564:** *Cordonata Capitolina*. Ceremonial ramp-stair linking the city to the Campidoglio; constructed from Michelangelo’s project with later completion (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A8).
- **1546–1549:** *Palazzo Farnese completion*. Michelangelo redesigned the upper façade, cornice, courtyard storey, and axial relation to the garden; the palace remains a multi-author building begun by Antonio da Sangallo the Younger (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A9; Ackerman).
- **1546–1564:** *St Peter’s Basilica*. Michelangelo radically simplified and rebuilt the inherited basilica project, especially its eastern massing, piers, apses, and dome system. The present church is a multi-generational obra: its later dome and nave are not wholly his construction (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A10; Ackerman).
- **1546–1564:** *Dome of St Peter’s*. Architectural project developed through drawings, structural work, and a large wooden model; the dome was completed after Michelangelo’s death with consequential alterations by Giacomo della Porta and Domenico Fontana.
- **c. 1547–1550:** *Sforza Chapel, Santa Maria Maggiore*. Chapel project begun under Michelangelo’s direction and completed by others; attribution of particular executed details is consequently uneven (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A11; Ackerman).
- **1550–1559:** *San Giovanni dei Fiorentini*. Multiple centralized-church designs and models for an unbuilt project; these competing solutions are intentions made visible, not a lost completed building (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A12; Ackerman).
- **1559–1560:** *Porta Pia*. Monumental urban gate designed for Pius IV and built with substantial deviations and later additions; its two faces belong to different building campaigns (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A13; Ackerman).
- **1560–1564:** *Monument to Julius III*. Unexecuted architectural-funerary designs whose precise reconstruction remains uncertain (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper architecture catalogue).
- **1561–1564:** *Santa Maria degli Angeli*. Conversion of part of the Baths of Diocletian into a church; later rebuilding transformed its appearance, although Michelangelo’s fundamental spatial reuse survives (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper A14; Ackerman).
- **1562–1564:** *Porta San Giovanni and associated Roman gate studies*. Designs associated with Michelangelo’s final architectural activity, but the extent of his responsibility for particular executed forms is disputed.
- **1563–1564:** *Tomb of Cardinal Giovanni Girolamo Morone*. Projected monument known through documentation and designs; not completed as an autograph architectural-sculptural work.

### Drawings, models, and works made for translation by others

- **c. 1487–1564:** *Surviving corpus of drawings*. Several hundred accepted or debated sheets encompass figure studies, anatomical studies, cartoons, architectural plans, fortification surveys, poems with drawings, and presentation sheets. Individual attribution, dating, sheet division, and the distinction between recto and verso works remain active catalogue problems; Charles de Tolnay’s *Corpus dei disegni di Michelangelo* and the Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper graphic catalogue enumerate them rather than treating “the drawings” as one stylistically uniform work.
- **c. 1504–1506:** *Cartoon for the Battle of Cascina*. Monumental full-size drawing, later cut up and lost; copies preserve portions of its composition, but they are manifestations by other hands rather than the cartoon itself.
- **1508–1512:** *Cartoons and working drawings for the Sistine Ceiling*. Operational drawings used to transfer forms into fresco; many were consumed, altered, perforated, or discarded in use, so they resist the status of autonomous display works.
- **c. 1520–1534:** *Architectural and figure drawings for the Medici Chapel*. A developing project distributed across numerous sheets rather than one definitive blueprint.
- **1528–1529:** *Fortification drawings*. Plans and surveys made to direct Florence’s defence; these sheets are simultaneously drawings, engineering protocols, and instruments of government.
- **c. 1532:** *The Rape of Ganymede*. Presentation drawing made for Tommaso de’ Cavalieri; versions and copies complicate identification of the prime autograph sheet (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper graphic catalogue).
- **c. 1532:** *The Punishment of Tityus*. Presentation drawing for Tommaso de’ Cavalieri, subsequently altered or reused on its reverse (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper graphic catalogue).
- **c. 1533:** *The Fall of Phaeton*. Presentation composition surviving in several developed versions; it is a serially reconsidered image rather than a single fixed work (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper graphic catalogue).
- **c. 1533:** *Children’s Bacchanal*. Highly finished presentation drawing for Tommaso de’ Cavalieri (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper graphic catalogue).
- **c. 1533–1534:** *The Dream (Il Sogno)*. Elaborate presentation drawing; its allegorical meaning remains disputed (Zöllner–Thoenes–Pöpper graphic catalogue).
- **c. 1534–1541:** *Drawings for Vittoria Colonna*. Devotional presentation works including variants of the *Pietà*, *Crucifixion*, and *Christ and the Woman of Samaria*; several compositions survive through copies, making autograph status a recurrent problem.
- **c. 1534–1541:** *Pietà for Vittoria Colonna*. Presentation drawing or design known in an autograph sheet and related copies; a private devotional manifestation rather than a preparatory study for an identified sculpture.
- **c. 1538–1541:** *Crucifixion for Vittoria Colonna*. Presentation design surviving through versions whose autograph status has been debated.
- **c. 1530s:** *Cleopatra*. Presentation drawing later found to carry another drawing on its reverse; the sheet’s two-sided history complicates the notion of one self-contained image (Casa Buonarroti catalogue).
- **c. 1530s:** *Zenobia*. Ideal-head presentation drawing traditionally identified by this title; the subject identification is not certain.
- **c. 1540–1564:** *Late Crucifixion drawings*. A series of intensely revised devotional sheets rather than preparatory studies for one securely identifiable finished object.
- **Various dates:** *Full-size architectural and sculptural models*. Michelangelo used clay, wax, wood, and other perishable materials to define projects including the San Lorenzo façade, Medici Chapel, Laurentian staircase, and St Peter’s dome. Some survive only in documents; workshop execution and later alteration often make individual authorship difficult to delimit.

### Poetry and deliberately composed texts

- **c. 1503–1560:** *Rime*. A corpus of more than three hundred lyric and fragmentary compositions—sonnets, madrigals, epigrams, quatrains, and unfinished drafts—canonically ordered in Enzo Noè Girardi’s critical edition. Michelangelo did not authorize a finished printed book: the corpus is an unstable body of manuscripts, revisions, fragments, and poems embedded in letters or drawings rather than a single work titled *Rime*.
- **c. 1503–1504:** *Poems concerning the David and Florentine politics*. Short occasional compositions preserved among the *Rime*; dates and boundaries of individual fragments are not always certain (Girardi).
- **c. 1510:** *Sonnet on painting the Sistine Ceiling*. Comic-complaint poem accompanied by a self-caricature; it is simultaneously poem, drawing, letter-like communication, and record of bodily labour (Girardi; Casa Buonarroti).
- **1520s–1530s:** *Poems for Tommaso de’ Cavalieri*. A changing group of love poems, not an authorially fixed cycle; manuscript variants and later editorial alteration make the “sequence” unstable (Girardi).
- **1530s–1540s:** *Poems for Vittoria Colonna*. Spiritual and affectionate lyrics exchanged within a relationship of manuscript circulation; they were not assembled by Michelangelo as a definitive book (Girardi).
- **1546:** *Proposed selection of eighty-nine poems*. A manuscript collection prepared by Luigi del Riccio, Donato Giannotti, and other associates with Michelangelo’s involvement but never published in his lifetime. It is an unfinished editorial project and collective textual object rather than a completed authorial volume.
- **c. 1540s–1560:** *Late devotional poems*. Sonnets and madrigals concerned with death, salvation, artistic failure, and divine love; their grouping as a late spiritual sequence is editorial rather than Michelangelo’s fixed arrangement (Girardi).

### Letters, records, and working texts

- **1496–1564:** *Carteggio diretto*. Michelangelo’s surviving outgoing correspondence, critically edited by Giovanni Poggi, Paola Barocchi, and Renzo Ristori. The letters are made texts but generally resist classification as literary works: they negotiate money, marble, family affairs, commissions, politics, friendship, and workshop operations.
- **Various dates:** *Ricordi*. Notes, memoranda, accounts, household records, and workshop records collected in the critical edition *I ricordi di Michelangelo*. These are documentary acts and memory aids rather than a unified authored book.
- **Various dates:** *Contracts and contractual drafts*. Legal and operational texts concerning sculpture, architecture, assistants, quarrying, and payment, collected in *I contratti di Michelangelo*. They function as protocols that authorize projects and define obligations; many concern works that changed or were never completed.
- **Various dates:** *Marble orders, quarry instructions, and transport memoranda*. Practical texts directing the extraction and movement of stone. Their authorship may be scribal or collaborative even when they embody Michelangelo’s instructions.
- **Various dates:** *Architectural measurements and building instructions*. Text-and-diagram documents used to direct distant construction, especially after Michelangelo settled in Rome; they are protocols whose intended manifestation was work by other hands.
- **c. 1518:** *Illustrated provisioning list*. A household shopping list supplemented with drawings of the requested foods or vessels, traditionally explained as assistance for a servant who could not read. It is neither autonomous artwork nor literary composition, but its resistance to that distinction is precisely part of its interest.
