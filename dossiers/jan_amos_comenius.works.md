## Works by Jan Amos Komenský

### Lexicographical, encyclopedic, and cartographic projects

- **1612–1656:** *Linguae Bohemicae thesaurus*. A projected comprehensive Czech lexicon, grammar, and treasury of idioms and proverbs. It remained unfinished, and the accumulated manuscript was reportedly destroyed in the Leszno fire of 1656.
- **1612:** *Problemata miscellanea*. An early philosophical disputation or collection of problems, now lost; older bibliographies sometimes call it *Sylloge quaestionum controversarum*.
- **1614–1627:** *Theatrum universitatis rerum*. An encyclopedic “theatre of all things,” begun as *Amphitheatrum universitatis* and repeatedly enlarged. It survives only incompletely and resists classification as a finished work: it was an evolving project for ordering universal knowledge.
- **1618–1627:** *Moraviae nova et post omnes priores accuratissima delineatio*. An engraved map of Moravia, first printed in 1627: a cartographic obra rather than a book, subsequently recopied and reissued by other mapmakers.
- **1620–1622:** *De antiquitatibus Moraviae*. A projected history of Moravian antiquities, now lost.
- **c. 1620–1630:** *De origine baronum a Žerotín*. A genealogical-historical work on the Žerotín family; its extent and survival are uncertain.
- **1631:** *Concordantiae Bibliorum*. A biblical concordance, reportedly destroyed at Leszno in 1656.
- **1649:** *Index plenus vocum Germanicarum*. A German word-index or lexical instrument.
- **c. 1650:** *Syntagma rerum, conceptuum et verborum*. A projected alignment of things, concepts, and words; it belongs to the pansophic language project rather than to ordinary lexicography.
- **before 1656:** *Materiarum pansophicarum sylva*. A large treasury of definitions and axioms gathered over many years, destroyed at Leszno; the lost compilation was working material for pansophy, not necessarily a finished work.
- **before 1656:** *Sapientia bis et ter oculata*. A collection or treatise on analogical insight, likewise reported destroyed at Leszno.
- **1654–1658:** *Lexicon atriale Latino-Latinum*. An advanced Latin dictionary designed for the “Atrium” stage of Comenius’s graded language course.
- **1650s–1670:** *Liber librorum, seu Bibliotheca portatilis, hoc est Lexicon reale pansophicum*. An unfinished “real” or thing-based pansophic dictionary. It resists being called a completed text: surviving material represents a lifelong organizational project.

### Didactic systems and school protocols

- **1628–1632:** *Didaktika česká*. The Czech form of Comenius’s general theory and protocol of education; unpublished in his lifetime.
- **1633–1638:** *Didactica magna*. A Latin reconstruction and expansion of the Czech *Didaktika*, setting out a universal system for teaching “everyone everything”; printed in *Opera didactica omnia* in 1657.
- **1628–1632:** *Informatorium školy mateřské*. A protocol for education during the first six years of life, addressed principally to parents and caregivers.
- **1633:** *Informatorium der Mutterschul*. Comenius’s German adaptation of the preschool protocol, not merely an unrelated translation.
- **before 1657:** *Schola infantiae*. The Latin recasting of the *Informatorium*, incorporated into *Opera didactica omnia*.
- **1628:** *Scholae vernaculae delineatio*. A design for the vernacular school and its curriculum; it is a school project rather than a narrative description.
- **1628:** *Vernaculae scholae classis sex libelli*. Six projected class-books for vernacular schooling; their status is fragmentary or lost.
- **1632:** *Navržení krátké o obnovení škol v Království českém*. A reform memorandum outlining how Bohemian schools should be restored.
- **1635:** *Leges illustris gymnasii Lesnensis*. Operational statutes for the Leszno gymnasium—a protocol regulating an institution.
- **1637:** *Faber fortunae, sive Ars consulendi sibi ipsi*. A practical guide to prudent self-direction, later incorporated into the didactic corpus.
- **1644–1648:** *Methodus linguarum novissima*. A systematic theory and protocol for language teaching, built upon Comenius’s general didactic principles.
- **1645:** *Regulae vitae*. A compact set of rules for a wise, peaceful, active, and ethically ordered life.
- **1650–1651:** *Schola pansophica*. The design for a pansophic school at Sárospatak; simultaneously an institutional project, curriculum, and public proposal.
- **1650–1651:** *Primitiae laborum scholasticorum*. A collection presenting the first fruits of the Sárospatak school experiment.
- **1651–1652:** *Laborum scholasticorum continuatio*. A continuation documenting and promoting the same educational experiment.
- **1651:** *De reperta ad autores Latinos prompte legendos et clare intelligendos facili brevique via*. A design for a three-class Latin school and a practical route to reading authors.
- **1651:** *Umění kazatelské*. A Czech handbook of preaching; its dating and textual transmission are less secure than those of the major didactic works.
- **1652:** *De ratione studii* by Joachim Fortius Ringelbergius and Erasmus, edited by Comenius. An editorial-pedagogical assemblage with Comenius’s introduction, not wholly his authored text.
- **1653:** *Fortius redivivus, sive De pellenda scholis ignavia*. A manifesto against idleness and disorder in schools.
- **1653:** *Praecepta morum*. A school manual of moral precepts.
- **1653:** *Leges scholae bene ordinatae*. Rules for the government of a well-ordered school: explicitly a protocol.
- **1654:** *Laborum scholasticorum Patakini obitorum coronis*. Comenius’s farewell address and retrospective conclusion to the Sárospatak project.
- **1657:** *Opera didactica omnia*. A four-part authorial assemblage of earlier and newly written educational works. It is an obra and system-building Gesamtkunstwerk of pedagogy rather than a single newly composed treatise.
- **1657:** *Vita gyrus, sive De occasionum varietate*. An autobiographical account of the circumstances that shaped Comenius’s educational work, written as part of the framing apparatus of *Opera didactica omnia*.
- **1657:** *Ventilabrum sapientiae*. A brief didactic-pansophic text, conceived as a “winnowing fan” for discriminating knowledge.
- **1657:** *E scholasticis labyrinthibus exitus in planum*. A manifesto promising a way out of the labyrinth of contemporary schooling.
- **1657:** *Latium redivivum*. A proposal for restoring practical command of Latin.
- **1657:** *Typographeum vivum*. A didactic metaphor and program treating the school as a living printing-house.
- **1657:** *Paradisus juventuti Christianae reducendus*. A reform proposal for restoring a Christian “paradise” to youth.
- **1657:** *Traditio lampadis*. A valedictory manifesto about transmitting the lamp of learning.
- **1657:** *Paralipomena didactica*. Supplementary didactic observations appended to Comenius’s educational system.
- **1657:** *Didacticae idea ex arcanis aeternis*. A compressed theological formulation of the principles underlying didactics.

### Language courses, dictionaries, and illustrated learning objects

- **1614–1616:** *Grammaticae facilioris praecepta*. An early Latin grammar designed to simplify instruction.
- **1631:** *Grammatica Latina legibus vernaculae concinnata*. A Latin grammar organized according to the structures of the learner’s vernacular.
- **1628–1631:** *Janua linguarum reserata*. A textbook that teaches language and a classified account of the world simultaneously. Its numerous authorial revisions make it a continuing project rather than one fixed text.
- **1633:** *Januae linguarum reseratae vestibulum*. An elementary “vestibule” preceding the *Janua* in the graded language course.
- **1637:** *De sermonis Latini studio*. A didactic dissertation defining four stages—Vestibulum, Janua, Palatium, and Thesaurus—for learning Latin.
- **1640s:** *Januae linguarum novissimae clavis*. A grammatical “key” associated with the revised *Janua*.
- **1640s:** *Annotationes super Grammaticam novam janualem*. Notes on the new Janual grammar.
- **1640s:** *De Lexico januali Latino-Germanico*. A design or discussion of the Latin-German *Janua* dictionary.
- **1640s:** *De Atrio Latinitatis*. A proposal for the advanced “Atrium” stage of Latin learning.
- **1649–1650s:** *Lexicon januale Latino-Germanicum*. A Latin-German dictionary serving the *Janua* course.
- **1651–1652:** *Eruditionis scholasticae pars III: Atrium*. The advanced stage of the language curriculum, combining rhetoric, elegant expression, and knowledge of things.
- **1652:** *Ars ornatoria, sive Grammatica elegans*. A handbook of stylistic ornament attached to the *Atrium*.
- **1652:** *Latinae linguae atrium*. The principal advanced Latin textbook of the Sárospatak course.
- **1657:** *Parvulis parvulus, omnibus omnia*. A supplement to the *Vestibulum* made from short sentences employing basic Latin vocabulary.
- **1657:** *Pro Latinitate Januae linguarum apologia*. A defense of the Latinity and teaching practice of the *Janua*.
- **1658:** *Orbis sensualium pictus*. An illustrated, multilingual textbook joining words, images, sounds, things, and actions. It is the clearest Comenian Gesamtkunstwerk: neither pictures nor prose are sufficient without the other.
- **1658:** *Lucidarium*. A related illustrated teaching project or sample sometimes associated with the development of *Orbis pictus*; its independent extent and status are uncertain.
- **1650s:** *Stammwörter der deutschen Sprache*. A lexical aid organized around German root words.
- **1650s:** *Comenianae linguarum methodi synopsis*. A synopsis of Comenius’s language-teaching method.

### School theatre and performative pedagogy

- **1638:** *Diogenes Cynicus redivivus*. A philosophical school play in which instruction becomes staged action.
- **1638–1641:** *Abrahamus patriarcha*. A biblical school drama performed publicly in 1641 and printed later.
- **1654:** *Schola ludus, seu Encyclopaedia viva*. A cycle of school plays dramatizing the classified world of the *Janua*. It is a pedagogical Gesamtkunstwerk in which encyclopedia, theatre, speech, gesture, and collective performance coincide.

### Pansophic and universal-reform projects

- **1634–1637:** *Conatuum Comenianorum praeludia*. A prematurely printed prospectus of Comenius’s pansophic endeavors; its Oxford publication occurred without his full control.
- **1638:** *Conatuum pansophicorum dilucidatio*. A clarification and defense of the pansophic project addressed to its critics.
- **1639:** *Prodromus pansophiae*. The “forerunner” of pansophy, substantially overlapping the *Praeludia* tradition; whether particular printings constitute a distinct work or an editorial manifestation requires edition-specific judgment.
- **c. 1640:** *Janua rerum reserata*. A “gate of things” or first philosophy intended to open the order of reality itself; revised repeatedly and published posthumously.
- **1641–1643:** *Pansophiae diatyposis*. A set of architectural projections for a future pansophic work, including *Templi sapientiae ichnographia*, *Templi pansophici delineatio orthographica*, and scenographic sections. These are plans and models of a work still to be made.
- **1641:** *Via lucis*. A universal-reform manifesto proposing the dissemination of intellectual light among all peoples, including international institutions of learning and communication.
- **1645–1670:** *De rerum humanarum emendatione consultatio catholica*. Comenius’s unfinished seven-part “General Consultation” on reforming human affairs. It is his largest project and a textual Gesamtkunstwerk, joining philosophy, education, language reform, religion, politics, and institutional design.
- **1645–1670:** *Panegersia*. Part I of the *Consultatio*, a manifesto summoning humanity to universal reform.
- **1645–1662:** *Panaugia*. Part II of the *Consultatio*, a theory of universal illumination.
- **1645–1670:** *Pansophia*. Part III of the *Consultatio*, an unfinished universal ordering of knowledge and reality.
- **1645–1670:** *Pampaedia*. Part IV of the *Consultatio*, a protocol for educating every human being throughout the whole of life.
- **1645–1670:** *Panglottia*. Part V of the *Consultatio*, a project for reforming languages and constructing universal communication.
- **1645–1670:** *Panorthosia*. Part VI of the *Consultatio*, a program for reforming philosophy, religion, and political society.
- **1645–1670:** *Pannuthesia*. Part VII of the *Consultatio*, a final universal exhortation; it remained incomplete.
- **1645:** *Pansophiae dogmaticae delineatio*. A systematic sketch of dogmatic pansophy.
- **1640s:** *Janua rerum sive Totius pansophiae seminarium*. A pansophic “seedbed” and precursor to the larger universal system.
- **1640s:** *Pansophiae Christianae liber III*. A surviving component or developmental state of Christian pansophy, not securely separable from the changing pansophic project.
- **1640s:** *Ad excitanda publice veritatis et pacis studia elaborandorum operum catalogus*. A catalogue of works to be written for truth and peace: unusually, the work is a protocol for making further works.
- **1640s:** *Consultationis brevissima delineatio*. A short outline of the projected *Consultatio*.
- **1640s:** *Studii pansophici in Anglia fautoribus*. An address to English supporters of pansophic study.
- **1654:** *Gentis felicitas*. A political-pansophic “mirror” describing how a people may recognize and attain collective happiness.
- **c. 1655–1656:** *Evigila Polonia*. A political exhortation or manifesto urging Poland to awaken.
- **1665–1670:** *Clamores Eliae*. A manuscript notebook of reforming appeals, memoranda, schemes, and prayers. It resists the status of a finished work: the critical edition preserves an open-ended workshop of late thought.
- **1667:** *Angelus pacis*. A peace manifesto addressed to negotiators during the Anglo-Dutch conflict and calling European powers to reconciliation.
- **1668:** *Unum necessarium*. A late spiritual-philosophical testament on distinguishing the one necessary thing from the world’s distractions.

### Natural philosophy and arguments over knowledge

- **1615:** *De angelis*. A student disputation on angels; its survival and precise form are uncertain.
- **1632–1633:** *Physicae ad lumen divinum reformatae synopsis*. A natural philosophy reconstructed under the authority of Scripture and divine light.
- **1632:** *Astronomia ad lumen physicum reformanda*. A projected reform of astronomy; its status is fragmentary.
- **1659:** *Disquisitiones de caloris et frigoris natura*. Investigations of heat and cold intended as a forerunner to a revised natural philosophy.
- **1659:** *Cartesius cum sua naturali philosophia a mechanicis eversus*. A polemical critique of Cartesian natural philosophy.
- **1667:** *Petrus Serarius: Responsio ad exercitationem paradoxam*. An anti-Cartesian response published under the name Petrus Serarius; Comenius’s authorship or editorial responsibility has been debated and should not be treated as uncontested.

### Spiritual works, consolations, and prayers

- **1617:** *Retuňk proti Antikristu a svodům jeho*. A spiritual warning or “rescue” against Antichrist and religious deception; older bibliographies also associate this period with *Pauperum oppressorum clamores in caelum*.
- **c. 1620:** *Retuňk duchovní věrným a mnohými pokušeními zemdleným křesťanům*. A consolatory spiritual tract for Christians exhausted by persecution.
- **1622:** *Přemyšlování o dokonalosti křesťanské*. A meditation on Christian perfection.
- **1622:** *Nedobytedlný hrad jméno Hospodinovo*. A devotional consolation presenting God’s name as an impregnable refuge.
- **1623–1624:** *Truchlivý*. A dialogic lament over the oppression of church and country, issued in successive parts and later continued.
- **1624:** *O sirobě*. A meditation on orphanhood and abandonment.
- **1624:** *Přes boží*. A devotional work whose title and textual identity are transmitted uncertainly.
- **1625:** *Centrum securitatis*. A spiritual-philosophical dialogue locating true security in God rather than in the world.
- **1630–1631:** *Praxis pietatis, to jest O cvičení se v pobožnosti pravé*. Comenius’s Czech adaptation of Lewis Bayly’s devotional manual. It is a translation-transformation, not an independently invented source work.
- **1650:** *Kšaft umírající matky Jednoty bratrské*. A personified testament in which the dying Unity of Brethren distributes its spiritual inheritance; it acts as ecclesial manifesto and consolation.
- **1653:** *Animae sanctae aeterna regna cum triumpho ingredientis beatum satellitium*. A consolatory funeral text addressed to Laurens de Geer after his father’s death.
- **1656:** *Enoch*. A devotional sermon or treatise on continual walking with God.
- **1656:** *Matuzalém*. A funeral sermon on the gift of long life.
- **1663:** *Renuntiatio mundi*. A devotional renunciation of the world.
- **1669:** *De zelo sine scientia et charitate admonitio fraterna*. A fraternal admonition to Samuel Maresius against zeal lacking knowledge and charity.
- **1669–1670:** *O horlivosti bez umění a lásky napomenutí bratrské*. The Czech manifestation of the admonition on zeal; its relationship to the Latin work is adaptive rather than simply bibliographical duplication.
- **1670:** *Continuatio admonitionis fraternae*. A continuation of the dispute with Maresius and an autobiographical defense, unfinished or issued only at the end of Comenius’s life.

### Biblical condensations, translations, and aids

- **1620–1623:** *Manuálník aneb Jádro celé Biblí svaté*. A vast Czech condensation of the Bible intended as a portable scriptural core.
- **1626:** *Žalmové Davidovi*. Czech renderings of selected Psalms; the exact extent of Comenius’s contribution within later printings is not always secure.
- **1631–1632:** *Harmonia evangelistarum*. A harmonization of the Gospel narratives.
- **1632:** *Historia o umučení, smrti, pohřbu i vzkříšení Ježíše Krista*. A Czech composite Passion and Resurrection narrative assembled from the Evangelists.
- **1632:** *Harmonia historie o vzkříšení Páně*. A harmonized account of the Resurrection.
- **1658:** *Janua sive Introductorium in Biblia sacra*. An introductory gateway to the Bible.
- **1658:** *Novi Testamenti epitome*. A typographically differentiated epitome of the New Testament, using print design to distinguish things, words, phrases, and propositions.
- **1660s:** *Theatrum Scripturae*. A projected “theatre” of Scripture, conceived as the biblical counterpart to the encyclopedic *Theatrum universitatis rerum*; it remained incomplete.
- **1660s:** *Bibliorum Turcicorum dedicatio*. A dedication associated with the projected or produced Turkish Bible.
- **1660s:** *De Turcica Novi Testamenti versione Anglica annotatio*. Notes concerning an English-supported Turkish translation of the New Testament.

### Hymnals, songs, and editorial music projects

- **1634:** *Rada k opravě kancionálu*. Recommendations for revising the Brethren’s hymnal—an editorial protocol rather than a songbook.
- **1659:** *Kancionál*. A Czech Brethren hymnal compiled and edited by Comenius, containing inherited, adapted, translated, and original material. Attribution of individual hymns cannot automatically be transferred from the collection to Comenius.
- **1661:** Preface to *Kirchen-, Haus- und Herzens-Musica*. Comenius’s contribution to a German hymnal he helped edit; the collection as a whole is collaborative and cannot be called solely his work.
- **dates uncertain:** Individual spiritual songs and occasional poems. The critical corpus includes short verse works, but the boundaries between original composition, translation, adaptation, and traditional Brethren material remain work-specific.

### Ecclesiology, church order, and confessional protocols

- **1632:** *Historia fratrum Bohemorum*. A history of the Bohemian Brethren; the work is lost or survives only indirectly.
- **1632:** *O základu a trvání církve pravé a falešné*. An ecclesiological tract distinguishing true and false churches.
- **1632:** *Otázky některé o Jednotě bratří českých*. A question-and-answer defense or explanation of the Unity of Brethren.
- **1635–1636:** *Na spis proti Jednotě bratrské od Samuela Martinia sepsaný ohlášení*. A polemical defense of the Unity against Samuel Martinius.
- **1637:** *Cesta pokoje*. An irenic manifesto proposing concord and love as the way to preserve the church.
- **1643:** *Irenica quaedam scripta pro pace Ecclesiae*. A group of writings for ecclesiastical peace; the title may designate an assemblage rather than one unitary composition.
- **1643:** *Calendarium ecclesiasticum*. An ecclesiastical calendar.
- **1646:** *Christianismus reconciliabilis reconciliatore Christo*. An argument that Christians can be reconciled through Christ.
- **1648:** *Independentia, aeternarum confusionum origo*. A polemic submitted to the English national synod against ecclesiastical independency.
- **1649:** *Manuductio in viam pacis ecclesiasticae*. A guide toward ecclesiastical peace.
- **1660:** *De bono unitatis et ordinis, disciplinaeque et obedientiae*. A composite ecclesiological obra on the value of unity, order, discipline, and obedience.
- **1660:** *Ecclesiae Anglicanae salutem in Domino*. A formal address to the Church of England.
- **1660:** *Ecclesiae Slavonicae brevis historiola*. A short history of the Slavic church.
- **1660:** *Ratio disciplinae ordinisque ecclesiastici in Unitate Fratrum Bohemorum*. A descriptive and normative protocol of the church order of the Bohemian Brethren.
- **1660:** *Ad rationem ordinis et disciplinae annotata quaedam*. Supplementary observations on that order and discipline.
- **1660:** *Paraenesis ad ecclesias, nominatim Anglicanam*. An exhortation to the churches, especially the English church.
- **1661:** *Katechismus pro mládež českou Jednoty bratrské*. A catechetical teaching protocol for the Brethren’s Czech youth.
- **1661:** *Die uralte christliche katholische Religion*. A German confessional-catechetical manifestation related to Comenius’s defense of the Brethren’s faith.
- **1662:** *Confessio, aneb Počet z víry a učení i náboženství Jednoty bratří českých*. A confession of the Brethren’s faith, doctrine, and worship; its textual history includes inherited corporate material as well as Comenius’s editorial authorship.
- **date uncertain:** *Navržení o pravdivém všech věřících s Kristem sjednocení*. A proposal concerning the true union of believers with Christ.

### Theological controversies and irenic interventions

- **1629:** *De veris et falsis prophetis*. A Czech tract on true and false prophets, later translated or adapted for the prophetic publications.
- **1637:** *De quaestione, utrum Dominus Jesus propria virtute a mortuis resurrexerit, responsum*. A response to the Socinian Melchior Schäffer on Christ’s resurrection.
- **1639:** *A dextris et sinistris*. A polemical defense of Christ’s divinity and humanity.
- **1640:** *De Christianorum uno Deo, Patre, Filio, Spiritu*. An anti-Socinian statement of Trinitarian faith.
- **1644:** *Judicium de judicio Valeriani Magni … sive Absurditatum echo*. A polemical judgment on Valerian Magni’s rule of faith.
- **1645:** *Judicium Ulrici Neufeldii de fidei catholicae regula*. A further intervention in the controversy over the Catholic rule of faith; Comenius used “Ulricus Neufeldius” pseudonymously.
- **1640s:** *De dissidentium in rebus fidei Christianorum reconciliatione hypomnemata quaedam*. Memoranda on reconciling Christians divided in matters of faith.
- **1640s:** *Excerpta ex Paraclesi Erasmi et Melanchthonis hypotheseos*. An editorial compilation of passages from Erasmus and Melanchthon.
- **1640s:** *De legitima in ecclesiis evangelicis pastorum missione dissertatio*. A dissertation on the legitimate calling of Protestant pastors.
- **1640s:** *De colloquii Thorunensis apparatu cogitationes quaedam*. Reflections concerning preparations for the Colloquy of Thorn.
- **1640s:** *De controversia circa ritum acceptionis in Sacra coena panis et calicis judicium*. A judgment on the disputed manner of receiving bread and wine in the Eucharist.
- **1659:** *Vindicatio famae et conscientiae Johannis Comenii a calumniis Nicolai Arnoldi*. An autobiographical and theological defense against Nicolaus Arnoldus.
- **1660:** *De irenico irenicorum admonitio*. An admonition responding to Socinian offers of religious peace.
- **1661:** *Theologia naturalis, sive Liber creaturarum*. Comenius’s abridged and stylistically recast edition of Raymond of Sabunde. It is an adaptation of another author’s work, not wholly an original treatise.
- **1661:** *De iterato Sociniano irenico iterata ad Christianos admonitio*. A renewed warning concerning Socinian irenicism.
- **1661:** *Socinismi speculum*. A synoptic polemical “mirror” of Socinian belief and unbelief.
- **1662:** *Admonitio tertia*. A two-part warning addressed first to Daniel Zwicker and then to Christians generally.
- **1662:** *A dextris et sinistris* (revised publication). A later manifestation of the earlier anti-Socinian work rather than an entirely new composition.

### Sermons, funeral addresses, and occasional orations

- **1630:** *Svědectví podlé povědomosti a pravdy dané paní Esteře Sádovské*. A German funeral address for Ester Sádovská.
- **1630s:** *Exhortatio ad juventutem in synodo Ostrorogensi*. An exhortation to young people delivered in a synodal setting.
- **1636:** *Kázání XXI: O tajemstvích smrti, vzkříšení a na nebe vstoupení Krista*. A cycle of twenty-one sermons on Christ’s death, resurrection, and ascension.
- **1636:** *Spiegel guter Obrigkeit*. A German funeral oration for Count Rafał Leszczyński, shaped as a “mirror” of good government.
- **1630s–1640s:** *Concio synodalis de ministris*. A synodal sermon concerning ministers.
- **1649:** *Kázání pohřební nad knězem Pavlem Fabriciem*. A Czech funeral sermon for the minister Pavel Fabricius.
- **1649:** *O vymítání ďábelství*. A sermon concerning the expulsion of mute and other demons.
- **1655:** *Boj s Bohem modlitbami*. A sermon and prayer composed amid danger at Leszno, treating prayer as struggle with God.
- **1655:** *Modlitba kající*. A penitential prayer associated with the same crisis.
- **1656:** *Kázání vánoční*. A Christmas sermon.
- **date uncertain:** Concept for a sermon at the ordination of David Prüfer. A surviving working draft, not a securely finished publication.

### Histories, testimony, and political interventions

- **1632:** *Historia persecutionum Ecclesiae Bohemicae*. A history and political indictment of the persecution of the Bohemian church, compiled from corporate and historical materials; parts of its authorship and source-work are collaborative.
- **1632:** *Haggaeus redivivus*. A prophetic-political exhortation to rulers, ministers, and peoples to rebuild church and commonwealth.
- **1632–1633:** *Moudrost starých Čechů za zrcadlo vystavená potomkům*. A collection of Czech proverbs fashioned as a moral-historical mirror for descendants.
- **1649:** *Johannis Lasitii Historiae de origine et rebus gestis Fratrum Bohemicorum liber VIII*. Comenius edited and published the eighth book of Jan Łasicki’s history; Łasicki, not Comenius, is the principal author.
- **1655–1656:** *Panegyricus Carolo Gustavo*. A political panegyric to Charles X Gustav of Sweden. Its celebratory action makes it closer to a manifesto of allegiance and hope than to neutral description.
- **1656:** *Lesnae excidium*. A historical account of the destruction of Leszno.
- **1656:** *Carolo Gustavo votiva acclamatio*. A votive political acclamation addressed to Charles X Gustav.
- **1660s:** *Sermo secretus Nathanis ad Davidem*. A confidential political admonition traditionally associated with Prince Sigismund Rákóczi; its dating and precise intended recipient require caution.
- **1667:** *Angelus pacis*. Besides being a peace manifesto, it is a direct diplomatic intervention addressed to the Breda peace congress.

### Prophetic publications and contested collaborative works

- **1625:** *Vidění a zjevení Kryštofa Kottera*. Comenius’s edited Czech presentation of Christoph Kotter’s visions. The visions are Kotter’s claimed revelations; Comenius made their textual and public manifestation but did not claim to originate them.
- **1627–1629:** *Vidění panny Kristiny Poniatovské*. An edited account of Krystyna Poniatowska’s visions. Its mixed authorship must be retained: visionary testimony, scribal mediation, translation, and Comenius’s editorial framing coexist.
- **1654–1657:** *Lux in tenebris*. A Latin prophetic compilation containing revelations attributed to Kotter, Poniatowska, and Mikuláš Drabík, translated, arranged, defended, and published by Comenius. It is a collaborative manifestation, not a body of prophecies authored by him.
- **1650s:** *De principis Transylvaniae ruina*. A prophetic-political text concerning the fall of the Transylvanian prince, transmitted within the revelation complex.
- **1659:** *Historia revelationum Christophori Kotteri, Christinae Poniatoviae, Nicolai Drabicii*. Comenius’s history of the revelations and the events surrounding their publication.
- **1659–1663:** *Revelationum divinarum epitome*. An abridgment of the prophetic corpus.
- **1660s:** *Continuatio historiae revelationum Nicolai Drabicii*. A continuation concerning Drabík’s revelations; authorial layers include Drabík’s material and Comenius’s mediation.
- **1664–1667:** *Lux e tenebris novis radiis aucta*. A greatly enlarged prophetic compilation. Its disastrous political predictions and collaborative authorship make it particularly resistant to being treated as a conventional authored book.
- **1660s:** *Drabicianarum visionum continuatio*. A continuation of Drabík’s visions edited or transmitted by Comenius.
- **1660s:** *Voluminis prophetici dimissio*. A dismissal or valedictory framing text for the prophetic volume.
- **1660s:** *Visiones nocturnae Stephani Melisch*. An edited Latin presentation of Stephan Melisch’s nocturnal visions.
- **1660s:** *Nächtliche Gesichte Stephani Melisch*. The German manifestation of Melisch’s visions.
- **1660s:** *Fortsetzung der nächtlichen Gesichte Stephani Melisch*. A continuation of the German visionary record.
- **date uncertain:** *Boj Michala a anjelů jeho s drakem a anjely jeho*. A prophetic-apocalyptic text preserved within the corpus; its exact authorship and editorial formation are not secure.

### Public-health, pastoral, and mission protocols

- **1631:** *Kurzer Bericht von der Pestilenz*. A German practical account of plague and appropriate responses.
- **1632:** *Zpráva kratičká o morním nakažení*. A Czech plague manual related to the German work: a protocol for bodily, civic, and spiritual conduct during epidemic disease.
- **1650s:** *Instructio profecturis in Lithuaniam*. Instructions for persons departing for Lithuania.
- **1660:** *Truchlivý hlas pastýřů*. A pastoral lament addressed by frightened shepherds to their scattered and endangered flocks.
- **date uncertain:** *Antidotum apostasiae*. A pastoral “antidote” against apostasy.
- **date uncertain:** *Palinodia apostasiae*. A model recantation or reversal of apostasy.
- **date uncertain:** *Přemyšlování o duchovní radosti věrných a pravých křesťanů*. A meditation on the spiritual joy of faithful Christians.
- **date uncertain:** *Spicilegium didacticum*. A didactic gleaning or miscellany whose precise date and degree of completion remain uncertain.
- **date uncertain:** *Centum dialogi pansophiae*. A projected or fragmentary set of one hundred pansophic dialogues.

### Correspondence and administrative writing

- **1628–1670:** *Epistulae / correspondence*. Roughly six hundred surviving letters form a distributed practical obra: pastoral direction, publishing instructions, diplomacy, fundraising, intellectual collaboration, and institutional government. Individual letters are documents rather than independently titled works, and the critical *Opera omnia* correspondence series is still being published.
- **throughout his ministry:** Synodal records, dedications, prefaces, recommendations, school memoranda, and official declarations. The critical edition treats many as textual items, but their boundaries and authorship can be corporate or scribal; they should not be inflated into independently authored “books.”
