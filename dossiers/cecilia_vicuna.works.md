## Works by Cecilia Vicuña

### Precarios, objects, and works meant to disappear

- **1966–present:** *Precarios / Basuritas*. An open-ended obra of tiny sculptures assembled from feathers, sticks, stones, thread, shells, and street debris. Many manifestations were deliberately surrendered to wind or tide, so the surviving photographs and later re-creations are not identical with the vanished works.
- **1966:** *Casa Espiral*. An ephemeral spiral construction at Con Con, made for the tide to erase; it resists treatment as a stable object because disappearance was part of its making.
- **1966:** *Robot jardín*. A mixed-media object made from wool and tin.
- **1966:** *Culo e’ canasta*. A mixed-media object made from a basket core and paint.
- **1966:** *Cono Rojo*. A small mixed-media construction in cement and wood.
- **1966:** *Casa e’ palo*. A mixed-media construction in wood and paint.
- **1967:** *Guardián*. A precario made at Con Con and erased by the tide; what persists is its documented manifestation rather than an extant sculpture.
- **1973–1974:** *Journal of Objects for the Chilean Resistance*. A political project in which Vicuña made an object from London street debris each day—about 400 objects in all—along with twelve books. Because much of it was precarious or dispersed, the Journal is at once an obra, archive, daily protocol, and incomplete surviving body.
- **1973–1974:** *Red Silk*. Typewriting on silk belonging to the *Journal of Objects for the Chilean Resistance*.
- **1973–1974:** *Libro Tul*. A Polaroid-and-cloth object/book belonging to the *Journal of Objects for the Chilean Resistance*.
- **1973–1974:** *Cajita e’ tierra*. A cardboard, earth, wire, and paint object belonging to the *Journal of Objects for the Chilean Resistance*.
- **1974:** *Paño e’ sangre / Venda / Bandage*. A precario from the *Journal of Objects for the Chilean Resistance*; its alternative titles are retained because the artist’s archive gives both.
- **1982:** *Cementerio*. A precario/object.
- **1984:** *Mica*. A precario/object.
- **1986:** *Cruz de Hilo*. A thread construction that operates as both object and minimal spatial weaving.
- **1986:** *Canasta Espiral*. A precario/object structured around a spiral basket form.
- **1987:** *Mummy’s Skirt*. A precario/object.
- **1987:** *Shaman Herido*. A precario/object.
- **1989:** *Jazmín*. A precario/object.
- **1990:** *Arco Arrayán*. A precario/object.
- **1990:** *Origen del Tejido*. An object that figures the origin of weaving rather than merely representing a textile.
- **1990:** *Poncho Huiro*. A precario made with seaweed; its unstable organic material makes it an event of decay as much as an object.
- **1990:** *Cacho Cabra*. A precario/object.
- **1994:** *Ofrenda*. A precarious offering rather than an autonomous, permanent sculpture.

### Quipus and spatial weavings

- **c. 1966:** *El quipu que no recuerda nada / The Quipu That Remembers Nothing*. An empty cord identified by Vicuña as her first precario. It is an anti-record—a quipu whose announced content is the absence of memory.
- **1972:** *El Hilo Azul*. A blue thread woven through Vicuña’s bedroom at Con Con; it was remade in 2000 as *La Pieza del Hilo Azul*, so original action and later installation should not be silently treated as the same object.
- **1974:** *London Quipu*. A quipu installation within the *Journal of Objects for the Chilean Resistance* exhibition in London.
- **1989:** *Quipu in the Gutter*. A street quipu on Beach Street, New York; a spatial intervention whose urban situation belongs to the work.
- **2000:** *Quipu Semiyo*. A site-specific quipu made for *Semiya* in Santiago, joining seed, textile, sound, and political memory.
- **2002:** *Dis solving Threads of Water and Light*. A collaborative exhibition-installation with César Paternosto at the Drawing Center; authorship is shared rather than exclusively Vicuña’s.
- **2002:** *Springs*. A quipu/spatial weaving installed at the Hedda Sterne Studio.
- **2006:** *Quipu Menstrual*. A red-fiber environmental and performative quipu first made at El Plomo Glacier; institutional manifestations followed, so it is better understood as a recurring project than one fixed sculpture.
- **2006:** *Skyscraper Quipu*. An urban quipu made in New York.
- **2006:** *Cable Telefónico Quipu*. A quipu installation made from or in relation to telephone cable for *Multiplicación* at the Museo de Arte Contemporáneo, Santiago.
- **2010:** *Truck Quipu*. A mobile quipu manifestation documented in *Kon Kon*.
- **2012:** *Quipu Austral*. An installation of long streams of unspun dyed wool and Vicuña’s recorded voice, made for the 18th Biennale of Sydney; it is a quipoem and Gesamtkunstwerk rather than textile alone.
- **2012:** *Quipu Aural*. A quipu installation at Galería Patricia Ready whose title makes listening integral to the textile form.
- **2013:** *Quipu Austral*. A later manifestation at FRAC Lorraine; the repeated title does not necessarily denote the unchanged 2012 installation.
- **2014:** *Quipu Rojo*. A red quipu installation associated with the *Artists for Democracy* archive exhibition.
- **2014:** *Quipu de Lamentos*. A quipu of lament made for the same archival-exhibition context.
- **2018:** *Disappeared Quipu*. A monumental immersive installation linking hanging fibers, projected images, sound, and erased Indigenous systems of knowledge; a Gesamtkunstwerk explicitly concerned with disappearance.
- **2022:** *Quipu del Exterminio / Extermination Quipu*. A site-specific quipu incorporating wool, plant fiber, hair, metal, wood, shells, seeds, bone, clay, plaster, plastic, and pastel.
- **2022:** *Brain Forest Quipu*. A monumental Tate Modern commission made from found and organic materials, sound, and collective participation; its material instability and collaborative knots make it a project and manifestation, not simply a textile object.

### Installations, environments, and collective projects

- **1971:** *Otoño / Autumn*. Vicuña’s first institutional installation, simultaneously conducted in the street and the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, Santiago. Its crossing of public action, found matter, and exhibition space resists separation into installation and performance.
- **1971:** *Día de la Semilla*. A proposal made to President Salvador Allende for a day of collecting and planting seeds across roofs, terraces, cities, and fields. It remained unrealized, but as an ecological-political intention it is properly a project, not a missing object.
- **1974–2014:** *Artists for Democracy*. A collective political and archival project initiated in London; later exhibitions reorganized its surviving records, so no single display exhausts the work.
- **1977:** *Homage to Vietnam*. An installation and political solidarity work.
- **1990:** *Precario / Precarious*. An installation whose fragile component objects continued the precario project in an institutional setting.
- **1994:** *Hilumbres Allqa*. A spatial weaving/installation structured around the union of black and white.
- **1994:** *Ceq’e Fragments*. An installation invoking the Andean ceque system through fragments rather than reconstructing it as a complete map.
- **1996:** *Wiñay Rutusqa*. A site-responsive installation or spatial weaving.
- **1997:** *A Net of Holes*. An installation in which absence and interval are constitutive materials.
- **1999:** *Cloud-Net*. A multimedia installation and “poem in space,” inseparable from the related performance and publication.
- **2000:** *Semiya*. A site-specific installation at Galería Gabriela Mistral joining seed, weaving, quipu, performance, and film; a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than one medium.
- **2001:** *Canoa de Luz*. A light installation organized around the canoe form.
- **2002:** *Dis Solving*. A site-specific installation component of Vicuña and César Paternosto’s shared Drawing Center project.
- **2009:** *Painted Ideas*. An installation/exhibition work bringing text and painted proposition together.
- **2009:** *Disolución*. A mixed-media action and installation at Con Con, continuing the precarios through another deliberately unstable manifestation.
- **2011–2012:** *Water Weaving*. A site-specific performance-installation that treats water, fiber, movement, and place as one woven field.
- **2015:** *Semiya / Frø Quipu*. A Danish reconstruction or new manifestation of the 2000 seed-quipoem; its status is iterative rather than that of a replica with a single immutable original.
- **2022:** *NAUfraga*. An installation combining the quipu and precario traditions; its title and suspended debris turn shipwreck into both material condition and linguistic event.

### Paintings, screens, and palabrarmas

- **1969:** *Leopardo de Nieve*. Oil on canvas.
- **1970:** *Retrato Doble*. A large oil painting now in the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, Santiago.
- **1971:** *Sueño (Los indios matan al papa)*. Oil on canvas; the expanded parenthetical title is documented alongside the shorter *Sueño*.
- **1971:** *Janis Joe (Janis Joplin and Joe Cocker)*. A monumental oil painting joining the two musicians in one image.
- **1971:** *Biombo casita para pensar qué situación real me conviene*. A twelve-panel, double-sided painted screen with mirrors, wood, hinges, and silk ribbons; it is both painting and inhabitable thinking-device.
- **1972:** *Karl Marx*. Oil on canvas.
- **1973:** *Violeta Parra o Violenta Vid*. An oil portrait belonging to Vicuña’s conception of revolutionary heroes.
- **1973:** *Frente Cultural*. An oil painting presenting culture as a collective, circular revolutionary organism.
- **1973:** *Martillo y Repollo*. Oil on canvas.
- **1973:** *Ángel de la menstruación / Manraja*. An oil self-portrait; both titles have been attached to the painting, so the variation should be preserved rather than normalized away.
- **1974/2016:** *Pa la brarma / Palabrarma*. A word-image from the *Palabrarmas* project, later realized as a screenprint; the double date distinguishes conception from edition.
- **1974/2016:** *Eman si pasión*. A *palabrarma* later issued as a screenprint.
- **1974/2016:** *Dar ver (Anteojos para ver la verdad)*. A screenprinted word-object with sticks, functioning as linguistic instrument rather than conventional print alone.
- **1974/2020 and 1974/2022:** *Ver dad, Dar ver*. An early *palabrarma* subsequently manifested in multiple appliquéd-nylon editions; the two later realizations are distinct objects of one continuing proposition.
- **1974/2020:** *Sud ame rica*. A *palabrarma* realized in oil pastel on burlap.
- **1974/2022:** *Men tira*. A *palabrarma* remade as appliquéd nylon.
- **1975/2022:** *Emancipación participación*. A *palabrarma* realized as an appliquéd-nylon banner.
- **1975/2022:** *Árbol de manos*. A *palabrarma* realized as an appliquéd-nylon banner.
- **1975–1978:** *Palabrarmas*. An open body of drawings and word-actions that divide and recombine language into political images. Individual sheets are not consistently titled in the published checklist, so untitled examples should not be supplied with invented names.
- **1976/2022:** *Pala abra*. A *palabrarma* later realized as an appliquéd-nylon banner.
- **1978/2020:** *Unir a todo el pueblo contra la dictadura*. A political graphic later issued as a silkscreen.
- **1979:** *Gabriela Mistral*. Oil on canvas.
- **2021:** *Anteojos para ver el futuro a través del arcoíris*. Marker and watercolor on paper glasses—a wearable protocol for seeing as much as a drawing.
- **2021:** *Tara’s Tears (A Prayer for the World)*. Oil on canvas whose subtitle identifies it as prayer as well as picture.
- **2022:** *Tres espirales*. Oil on canvas.
- **2022:** *Liderezas*. Oil on canvas portraying Indigenous women leaders.
- **2022:** *Ángel torturado*. Oil on canvas.

### Performances, poetic acts, and ritual manifestations

- **1966/1994:** *Guante / The Glove*. A poetic action begun on Santiago buses and later reperformed; the glove is used as an animated mythic and weaving sign, not treated as a costume prop alone.
- **1970:** *Tied Face (Life and Death)*. A bodily action in Santiago using binding as both image and event.
- **1971:** *Otoño / Autumn*. A performance-installation spanning museum and street; repeated here because its refusal of a single medium is essential rather than accidental duplication.
- **1979:** *Vaso de Leche*. A Bogotá public action using a glass of milk to address hunger and political responsibility; it survives through later filmic reconstruction.
- **1980:** *Sol y Dar y Dad, Una Palabra Bailada*. A danced *palabrarma* and filmed performance.
- **1981:** *Parti si pasión*. A New York mixed-media performance based on the word’s visual and sonic divisions.
- **1981:** *Amarrando Bogotá*. A public mixed-media action in Bogotá.
- **1981:** *Antivero*. A mixed-media action in Colchagua.
- **1983:** *Precario / Precarious*. A performance associated with the launch of the book of the same name; book, action, and objects are related manifestations rather than synonyms.
- **1994:** *Hilacha*. A thread-based performance in New York.
- **1997:** *Allqa (Union of Black & White)*. A ritual weaving-performance concerned with joining opposites.
- **1998:** *cloud-net*. A New York performance connected to the installation and later publication.
- **2006:** *Living Quipu*. A performance in which bodies become cords and knots; it makes the quipu a collective, moving manifestation.
- **2006:** *Quipu Menstrual, La Moneda*. A performance/institutional manifestation of the larger *Quipu Menstrual* project.
- **2008:** *mtChondrial Eve (Mother of Threads)*. A vocal and textile performance invoking maternal ancestry.
- **2008:** *Paracas*. A performance in which Vicuña projected her film of that title across her body and textile surfaces.
- **2009:** *Heritage of Tears (A Musical Tribute to Neruda)*. A live vocal-poetic performance responding to Neruda in a collaborative concert context.
- **2009:** *Apu Miski Yawar*. A live musical improvisation with Irene and Vojtech Havel; authorship of the event is collaborative.
- **2009:** *The Shadow of Sorrow*. A collaborative live improvisation and performance.
- **2009:** *Disolución*. A Con Con action among ephemeral constructions; performance, film, and precario cannot be cleanly separated.
- **2011:** *Kon Kon pi*. A performance/action related to the film *Kon Kon*.
- **2012:** *Fiber of Prayer / Fiber of Gold*. A fiber-and-voice ritual performance.
- **2015:** *Cantos del Agua*. A vocal and ecological performance centered on water.
- **2015:** *Sangre magallánica / Al sur de los ecos*. A sound, voice, and ritual performance whose paired title is preserved in the artist’s archive.
- **2022:** *Ex-termination Living Quipu*. A one-time Guggenheim rotunda performance commissioned in relation to *Quipu del Exterminio*; performers, fiber, movement, and sound made a temporary living quipu.

### Sound works and recordings

- **2002:** *Instan*. A poetry-and-sound obra existing both as a book and in performed/recorded forms; its oral, visual, and typographic manifestations are not interchangeable editions of a purely verbal text.
- **2012/2015:** *Kuntur Ko*. A voice-and-sound composition first documented in 2012 and issued by Hueso Records in 2015; the differing dates refer to work and release rather than necessarily competing attributions.
- **2016:** *Red Coil*. A vocal and sonic work organized through breath, improvisation, and the recurring image of red thread.

### Editorial and translation projects

- **1988:** *The Cardboard House*. An English edition of Martín Adán’s work made within the Palabra Sur editorial project; Vicuña’s role is editorial rather than original authorship of the underlying text.
- **1988:** *The Selected Poems of Rosario Castellanos*. An edited English-language selection; a curatorial and editorial creation, not a poetry book authored by Vicuña.
- **1988:** *A Plan for Escape*. An English edition of Adolfo Bioy Casares’s novel produced within the Palabra Sur series; Vicuña’s contribution is editorial.
- **1988:** *Altazor*. An English edition of Vicente Huidobro’s poem in the Palabra Sur series; the underlying obra is Huidobro’s.
- **1988:** *Ül: Four Mapuche Poets*. An edited and translated anthology of Mapuche poetry; authorship is collective and Vicuña’s making lies in selection, translation, and framing.
- **2009:** *The Oxford Book of Latin American Poetry*. A five-century anthology co-edited with Ernesto Livon-Grosman; it is a jointly made editorial atlas, not a sole-authored book of Vicuña’s poems.

Retrospective catalogue portal used to check the span and explicitly unfinished “1964–” framing of the oeuvre.

- [Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum, “Cecilia Vicuña: Spin Spin Triangulene”](https://www.guggenheim.org/press-release/cecilia-vicuna-spin-spin-triangulene-on-view-at-the-guggenheim). Museum account of the exhibition and *Ex-termination Living Quipu* commission.
- [Memoria Chilena, “Cecilia Vicuña (1948–)”](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-3330.html). National-library scholarly chronology used to check the dating of *Kon Kon* and other career landmarks.
- No complete catalogue raisonné of Vicuña’s continually developing oeuvre was located. Consequently, “everything” here means every separately named obra found in the artist’s standard bibliography, selected filmography, official medium chronologies, and the cited retrospective/checklist sources—not every unnamed *precario*, untitled *palabrarma*, performance improvisation, drawing, edition, or lost manifestation.
