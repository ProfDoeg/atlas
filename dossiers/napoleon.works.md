## Works by Napoleon Bonaparte

### Juvenilia, fiction, and philosophical experiments

- **1786:** *Parallèle entre l’amour de la patrie et l’amour de la gloire*. A youthful philosophical essay, preserved among Napoleon’s manuscripts; it belongs to the juvenilia rather than to his later political œuvre. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/))
- **1788:** *Mémoire sur la manière de disposer les pièces de canon pour le jet des bombes*. A technical artillery memorandum: a protocol-like text intended to improve practice, not a literary work. ([Jean Tulard, ed., *Œuvres littéraires et écrits militaires*](https://bibliotheques-numeriques.defense.gouv.fr/cdem/document/50cd19ea-54df-4dc2-8ae7-2c8936bce28e))
- **1788–1789:** *Notes de lecture et extraits historiques, politiques, géographiques, religieux et militaires*. A large manuscript corpus assembled from Napoleon’s reading. These notebooks resist classification as original “works”: they are simultaneously study aids, compilations, and the workshop from which later projects emerged. ([Jean Tulard’s collected edition](https://www.napoleon.org/magazine/livres/napoleon-ier-oeuvres-litteraires-et-ecrits-militaires/); [Arthur Chuquet, *La jeunesse de Napoléon*](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/ABC/Chuquet/Nap_Jeun/T2/Nap_Jeun_2_06.htm))
- **1789:** *Le Comte d’Essex*. An unfinished historical or “English” novella, substantially dependent on Napoleon’s reading; its derivative character makes it an exercise in literary appropriation as much as an autonomous fiction. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/))
- **1789:** *Le Masque prophète*. A short prose tale from the Auxonne period, preserved among the youthful manuscripts. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/))
- **1789:** *Nouvelle Corse*. An unfinished fictional or historical narrative about Corsica; “Nouvelle” is both part of its conventional title and an unstable description of its genre. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/); [Frédéric Masson](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/MNO/Masson/Jeunesse/Jeun_130.htm))
- **1789:** *Projet de constitution de la Calotte*. A satirical constitutional project for a mock society: simultaneously jeu d’esprit, institutional fiction, and protocol. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/))
- **c. 1789:** *Réfutation de Roustan*. A polemical manuscript defending Rousseau against the Protestant minister Antoine-Jacques Roustan; its exact composition developed through Napoleon’s reading notes rather than as a separately published book. ([Arthur Chuquet](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/ABC/Chuquet/Nap_Jeun/T2/Nap_Jeun_2_06.htm))
- **c. 1789:** *Parallèle entre Jésus-Christ et Apollonius de Tyane*. A comparative religious fragment or essay known through the youthful manuscripts; it is an intellectual exercise, not a finished treatise. ([Arthur Chuquet](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/ABC/Chuquet/Nap_Jeun/T2/Nap_Jeun_2_06.htm))
- **1791:** *Discours sur les vérités et les sentiments qu’il importe le plus d’inculquer aux hommes pour leur bonheur*. Usually called the *Discours de Lyon*, this was Napoleon’s unsuccessful submission to an Academy of Lyon essay competition; surviving copies include one collated and corrected by him. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/))
- **1791:** *Lettre à Matteo Buttafoco*. A published political manifesto attacking the Corsican deputy Buttafoco and intervening in the conflict over Paoli and revolutionary Corsica. Although cast as a letter, it was made to act publicly as a pamphlet. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/))
- **1795:** *Clisson et Eugénie*. An unfinished autobiographical novella surviving in dispersed manuscript fragments. Its modern title and sequence are editorial reconstructions, so no single surviving manuscript constitutes a finished work. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/magazine/livres/clisson-et-eugenie/))
- **1796:** *Dialogue sur l’amour*. A brief philosophical dialogue or conversation-piece, conventionally treated as a literary work although it remains closer to a manuscript meditation than to a completed book. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/))

### Political manifestos, proclamations, and public voice

- **1793:** *Le Souper de Beaucaire*. A political dialogue defending the Republican government’s position during the federalist revolt; published at Avignon in 1793, it is both literary dialogue and manifesto. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/essai-sur-les-idees-de-jeunesse-de-bonaparte/))
- **1796–1815:** *Proclamations, addresses, orders of the day, and speeches*. A large body of texts addressed to armies, representative bodies, conquered populations, and the French public. Many were dictated, drafted with secretaries, or editorially mediated; the corpus is therefore Napoleon’s public voice and an instrument of rule, but not uniformly autograph authorship. ([Jean Tulard’s collected edition](https://www.napoleon.org/magazine/livres/napoleon-ier-oeuvres-litteraires-et-ecrits-militaires/); [Napoleonica archives](https://www.napoleonica.org/fr/collections/proclamation/))
- **1796–1815:** *Bulletins of the armies and the Grande Armée*. A serial body of military narratives and propaganda issued under Napoleon’s authority. They make events politically by announcing victories, losses, and interpretations; individual drafting was collaborative, so attribution to Napoleon varies from direct dictation to institutional authorization. ([Jean Tulard’s collected edition](https://www.napoleon.org/magazine/livres/napoleon-ier-oeuvres-litteraires-et-ecrits-militaires/))
- **1804:** *Texts concerning the proclamation of the hereditary Empire*. A collective constitutional and ceremonial assemblage of projects, sénatus-consultes, decrees, speeches, and proclamations. It resists treatment as a personally authored work: Napoleon was its central political maker, but assemblies, ministers, jurists, and plebiscitary procedures produced its documentary form. ([Napoleonica documentary collection](https://www.napoleonica.org/collection/8848))

### Correspondence and administrative writing

- **1784–1821:** *Correspondance générale de Napoléon Bonaparte*. An œuvre-corpus of approximately 40,500 known or identified letters, orders, notes, reports, and instructions, now critically edited in fifteen volumes. Many documents were dictated and written out by secretaries; signatures, drafts, copies, and later transmissions carry different degrees of direct authorship, and the editors explicitly continue to identify supplements and corrections. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/); [Napoleonica corpus](https://www.napoleonica.org/en/collections/correspondance/); [Fayard, vol. XV](https://www.fayard.fr/livre/correspondance-generale-tome-15-9782213706177/))
- **1796–1815:** *Military plans, operational memoranda, reconnaissances, and strategic instructions*. A dispersed body of working texts—including plans for Corsican and Mediterranean defenses, the attack on Toulon, operations in Italy, artillery organization, and later campaigns. These are protocols and projects embedded in collective military action rather than self-contained books; some survive only through secretarial copies or later editorial groupings. ([Jean Tulard’s collected edition](https://bibliotheques-numeriques.defense.gouv.fr/cdem/document/50cd19ea-54df-4dc2-8ae7-2c8936bce28e); [review of Tulard’s edition](https://www.persee.fr/doc/jds_0021-8103_1969_num_4_1_1206?pageId=T1_248))
- **1797–1815:** *Campaign narratives and historical memoranda composed in office*. Napoleon repeatedly ordered, dictated, corrected, or supplied materials for accounts of the Army of Italy and subsequent campaigns. Their boundaries and authorship are unstable because staff officers and secretaries assembled much of the surviving prose. ([Jean Tulard’s collected edition](https://www.napoleon.org/magazine/livres/napoleon-ier-oeuvres-litteraires-et-ecrits-militaires/))
- **1799–1815:** *Decrees, decisions, regulations, and minutes of government*. This immense administrative corpus was made through the Consulate and Empire’s councils and bureaux, usually promulgated in Napoleon’s name. It should not be collapsed into personal authorship: he proposed, debated, dictated, amended, approved, or signed different documents to different degrees. ([Conseil d’État](https://www.conseil-etat.fr/actualites/que-reste-t-il-du-conseil-d-etat-napoleonien-par-bruno-lasserre-vice-president-du-conseil-d-etat); [*Correspondance générale*](https://www.napoleonica.org/en/collections/correspondance/))

### Collective institutional projects

- **1800–1815:** *The Napoleonic Conseil d’État and its administrative system*. An institutional obra rather than a text: Napoleon presided over and used the Conseil as the central workshop for legislation, administration, ministers, prefects, and senior officials. It cannot be assigned to him alone, since councillors and bureaux supplied its continuing intellectual and documentary labor. ([Conseil d’État](https://www.conseil-etat.fr/actualites/que-reste-t-il-du-conseil-d-etat-napoleonien-par-bruno-lasserre-vice-president-du-conseil-d-etat))
- **1804–1810:** *The Napoleonic codes*. The Civil Code and the later procedural, commercial, and penal codes form a collective legal project enacted under Napoleon’s government. Napoleon chaired and intervened in debates, but Cambacérès, Portalis, other jurists, the Conseil d’État, and legislative bodies were makers too; calling the *Code Napoléon* his authored book would erase that collaboration. ([Conseil d’État](https://www.conseil-etat.fr/actualites/que-reste-t-il-du-conseil-d-etat-napoleonien-par-bruno-lasserre-vice-president-du-conseil-d-etat); [Conseil d’État on Portalis](https://www.conseil-etat.fr/qui-sommes-nous/le-conseil-d-etat/histoire-et-patrimoine/les-grandes-figures-de-la-juridiction-administrative/jean-etienne-marie-portalis))
- **1804–1815:** *The imperial image-program*. A Gesamtkunstwerk of ceremonies, monuments, paintings, medals, architecture, print, heraldry, costume, and controlled publicity designed to make Napoleon’s rule visible. He commissioned and supervised it but did not execute its constituent objects; architects, artists, administrators, and printers retain their own authorship. ([Annie Jourdan’s project summarized in *Le Monde*](https://www.lemonde.fr/culture/article/2021/04/29/napoleon-son-descendant-des-lettres-une-satire-nos-choix-de-lecture_6078422_3246.html))

### Exile writings and clandestine interventions

- **1815–1821:** *Dictées de Sainte-Hélène*. A composite body of dictated histories, memoirs, military studies, book criticisms, political recollections, and revisions made with Las Cases, Gourgaud, Montholon, Bertrand, and others. “Napoleon’s memoirs” is an inadequate singular title: the manuscripts arose through oral dictation, secretarial inscription, Napoleon’s corrections, companion-editors, and posthumous publication. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/une-ecriture-si-singuliere-le-cas-napoleon/); [Napoleonica study of the memoirs](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-napoleonica-la-revue-2012-1-page-52?lang=fr))
- **1815–1821:** *Accounts of Toulon, the Italian campaigns, Egypt and Syria, Brumaire, the Consulate, and the campaign of 1815*. These interconnected historical dictations became principal components of the posthumous *Mémoires pour servir à l’histoire de France sous Napoléon*. Their published division into volumes and titles was substantially the work of Gourgaud, Montholon, and publishers, although the manuscripts were dictated and corrected by Napoleon. ([Napoleonica study](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-napoleonica-la-revue-2012-1-page-52?lang=fr); [Bibliothèque militaire numérique](https://bibliotheques-numeriques.defense.gouv.fr/mediatheque-en/document/04235421-9b31-4543-980f-8086d7bbca1a?cote=D2e+680+%286%29&portal=358353))
- **1815–1821:** *Studies of the wars of Julius Caesar, Turenne, and Frederick II*. Historical-military analyses dictated at Saint Helena, accompanied by related notes on eighteenth-century war, army reorganization, and field fortification. They are strategic criticism and surrogate campaigns conducted through historiography. ([Napoleonica study](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-napoleonica-la-revue-2012-1-page-52?lang=fr))
- **1817–1818:** *Lettres du Cap*. An anonymously published political intervention smuggled from Saint Helena and directed at contemporary European opinion. Its clandestine publication makes it a manifesto disguised as correspondence. ([*Le Monde* on the critical edition](https://www.lemonde.fr/culture/article/2021/04/29/napoleon-son-descendant-des-lettres-une-satire-nos-choix-de-lecture_6078422_3246.html))
- **1817–1818:** *Lettres d’un capitaine de bateau*. Another anonymous Saint Helena publication intended to influence contemporary debate over Napoleon’s captivity and possible removal. It is political action by textual indirection, not private correspondence. ([*Le Monde*](https://www.lemonde.fr/culture/article/2021/04/29/napoleon-son-descendant-des-lettres-une-satire-nos-choix-de-lecture_6078422_3246.html))
- **1817–1818:** *Manuscrit de l’île d’Elbe*. A clandestine political text issued anonymously in Britain. The title is a publication mask rather than a transparent statement of provenance, and the text belongs to Napoleon’s campaign to act upon European opinion from captivity. ([*Le Monde*](https://www.lemonde.fr/culture/article/2021/04/29/napoleon-son-descendant-des-lettres-une-satire-nos-choix-de-lecture_6078422_3246.html))
- **1822–1825, posthumous:** *Mémoires pour servir à l’histoire de France sous Napoléon, écrits à Sainte-Hélène sous sa dictée*. An eight-volume posthumous manifestation of the Saint Helena dictations, published by Montholon and Gourgaud from manuscripts corrected by Napoleon. The edition is not simply a book “written by Napoleon”: voice, inscription, revision, selection, arrangement, and publication belong to different makers. ([Bibliothèque militaire numérique](https://bibliotheques-numeriques.defense.gouv.fr/mediatheque-en/document/04235421-9b31-4543-980f-8086d7bbca1a?cote=D2e+680+%286%29&portal=358353); [Napoleonica study](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-napoleonica-la-revue-2012-1-page-52?lang=fr))
- **1823, posthumous:** *Mémorial de Sainte-Hélène*. This is Emmanuel de Las Cases’s journal and literary construction from conversations with Napoleon, with some possibly dictated material. It resists inclusion among Napoleon’s works and is listed here precisely as a disputed boundary-object: Napoleon is speaker, subject, and participant, but Las Cases is the author of the book. ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/four-for-the-gospel-makers-the-eye-witness-memoirs-of-napoleon-on-st-helena))

### Testamentary work

- **15–29 April 1821:** *Testament, codicils, inventories, letters, and instructions to the executors*. A multipart legal and political ensemble comprising the principal will, codicils, schedules of possessions and bequests, associated letters, and instructions. Parts were dictated, recopied, signed, sealed, or written wholly in Napoleon’s hand; even the number of codicils varies between descriptions because drafts and associated instruments are counted differently. ([Fondation Napoléon, document and transcription](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/document-commente-le-testament-de-napoleon-ier/); [Fondation Napoléon, facsimile edition](https://www.napoleon.org/magazine/livres/le-testament))

### Posthumous manifestations that are not his works

- **1840:** *Retour des cendres*. The removal of Napoleon’s remains from Saint Helena and their transfer to the Invalides was a state ceremony and posthumous political manifestation. Napoleon had expressed a desired burial place in his testament, but he did not make the 1840 event; its resistance to inclusion as his work is essential to its classification. ([Napoleon’s testament](https://www.napoleon.org/histoire-des-2-empires/articles/document-commente-le-testament-de-napoleon-ier/))
- **1858–1869:** *Correspondance de Napoléon Ier*. The Second Empire’s thirty-two-volume publication was an official posthumous construction rather than a work made by Napoleon: editors selected, normalized, omitted, and sometimes altered documents. It has been superseded for scholarly purposes by the Fondation Napoléon’s fifteen-volume *Correspondance générale*. ([Napoleonica, editorial history and bibliography](https://www.napoleonica.org/en/collections/correspondance/context); [Fondation Napoléon](https://fondationnapoleon.org/en/activities-and-services/telling-history/correspondence-napoleon/))
