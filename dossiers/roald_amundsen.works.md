## Works by Roald Engebreth Gravning Amundsen

### Expeditionary projects

- **1896:** *Crossing of the Hardangervidda*. An unsuccessful winter crossing attempted with his brother Leon; Amundsen subsequently made it into a newspaper-letter series, so the journey and its textual manifestation are distinct works. ([Museene i Akershus](https://amundsen.mia.no/content-type/artikler/); [Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1903–1906:** *Gjøa expedition through the Northwest Passage*. Amundsen’s first independently led expedition was simultaneously a navigational project, an ethnographic encounter and a programme of geomagnetic observation; it completed the first transit of the passage by one vessel. Sources sometimes date the enterprise 1903–1907 because the published account and Amundsen’s European homecoming followed later. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen); [Fram Museum](https://frammuseum.no/en/polar-history/explorers/roald-amundsen-explorer/))

- **1908–1912:** *Fram expedition: projected Arctic drift, redirected South Pole expedition*. This project changed its object after Cook and Peary announced North Pole claims: the publicly proposed Arctic drift became a secretly redirected Antarctic expedition, whose five-person polar party reached the South Pole on 14 December 1911. Its shifting intention is integral to the work rather than a preliminary mistake. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1918–1925:** *Maud expedition*. Conceived as a scientific drift across the Arctic Ocean, it produced substantial geophysical and ethnographic research and completed the Northeast Passage, but never achieved Amundsen’s intended transpolar drift. It therefore resists classification as either simply completed or failed: the voyage succeeded, while its governing project remained unrealized. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1922–1923:** *Attempted polar flight from Alaska*. A project to fly north from Wainwright while *Maud* entered the ice; damaged aircraft, financial trouble and unsuccessful trial flights prevented the intended polar journey. The unrealized flight—not a later narrative imposed on it—is the work’s project-form. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen); [Museene i Akershus](https://amundsen.mia.no/resource/1925-mot-88-grader-nord-klaer/))

- **1925:** *Amundsen–Ellsworth flight toward the North Pole in N24 and N25*. A two-aircraft expedition which reached approximately 87°44′ N, made an emergency landing and survived only after the party fashioned a runway on the ice. As an obra it joins aviation, survival engineering, observation, publicity and a collaboratively authored book. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen); [Museene i Akershus](https://amundsen.mia.no/resource/1925-mot-88-grader-nord-klaer/))

- **1926:** *Norge expedition across the Polar Sea*. A collaborative expedition led by Amundsen with Lincoln Ellsworth and airship commander Umberto Nobile, flying from Svalbard across the North Pole to Alaska and observing no substantial land in the remaining unmapped region. Its authorship was publicly contested afterward by Amundsen and Nobile, so it cannot be assigned to Amundsen alone. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen); [Nasjonalbiblioteket](https://www.nb.no/historier-fra-samlingen/dramatikk-pa-tusen-meters-hoyde/))

- **1928:** *Search for the survivors of the Italia*. Amundsen joined a French–Norwegian aerial rescue project after Nobile’s airship disaster; the flying boat *Latham 47* disappeared on 18 June with all six aboard. This unfinished project is also Amundsen’s final obra, although it left no completed account and its precise end cannot be reconstructed. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

### Expedition narratives and autobiography

- **1907:** *Nordvestpassagen: Beretning om Gjøa-ekspeditionen 1903–1907*. Amundsen’s book-length account of the *Gjøa* expedition and Northwest Passage; translations, including the two-volume English *The North-West Passage*, are manifestations of this work rather than additional works. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen); [Bokselskap](https://www.bokselskap.no/forfattere/amundsen))

- **1912:** *Sydpolen: Den norske sydpolsfærd med Fram 1910–1912*. A two-volume expedition narrative: Amundsen’s principal textual obra of the South Pole project, but also a documentary container for other participants’ activities and scientific contributions. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1921:** *Nordostpassagen: Maudfærden langs Asiens kyst 1918–1920; H. U. Sverdrups ophold blandt tsjuktsjerne; Godfred Hansens depotekspedition 1919–1920*. A composite expedition book rather than a unitary solo narrative: its title expressly incorporates Harald Ulrik Sverdrup’s ethnographic work and Godfred Hansen’s depot journey alongside Amundsen’s account. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1925:** *Gjennem luften til 88° nord: Amundsen–Ellsworths polflyvning 1925*. A collaboratively made report containing separately attributed contributions by Amundsen, Hjalmar Riiser-Larsen, Leif Dietrichson, Fredrik Ramm and Jacob Bjerknes; calling it simply an “Amundsen book” erases that structure. ([Digitized first edition, Project Runeberg](https://arkivkopia.se/bok/runeberg-nord88); [Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1926:** *Den første flukt over Polhavet*. A collaborative narrative credited to Amundsen and Lincoln Ellsworth, with contributions by other technical participants including Hjalmar Riiser-Larsen, Finn Malmgren and members of the airship’s engineering staff. It is the textual manifestation of the *Norge* expedition, not proof of sole authorship of the expedition itself. ([Nasjonalbiblioteket](https://www.nb.no/historier-fra-samlingen/dramatikk-pa-tusen-meters-hoyde/); [Cappelen Damm](https://cappelendamm.no/_den-forste-flukt-over-polhavet-roald-amundsen-lincoln-ellsworth-9788202705114))

- **1927:** *Mitt liv som polarforsker*. Amundsen’s autobiography, organized chiefly around his explorations and disputes rather than a comprehensive private life; the English *My Life as an Explorer* is a manifestation, not another obra. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1928–1930:** *Roald Amundsens Opdagelsesreiser*. A four-volume memorial collected edition of the expedition narratives. Because publication extended beyond Amundsen’s death and rearranged already existing texts, it is best classified as a posthumous editorial manifestation rather than a new work made by him. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

### Journals, letters and working records

- **1897–1899:** *Belgica diary*. Amundsen’s contemporaneous private record of his first Antarctic expedition, later published as *Roald Amundsen’s Belgica Diary*; it was not originally made as a public expedition book. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen); [Fram Museum](https://frammuseum.no/en/polar-history/explorers/roald-amundsen-explorer/))

- **1900–1907:** *Diaries from the Northwest Passage period*. A sequence of working and personal diaries extending beyond the formal 1903–1906 voyage, not one continuously conceived literary work; their later edited publication is a separate manifestation. ([Fram Museum](https://frammuseum.no/en/product/roald-amundsens-dagboker-fra-nordvestpassasjen-1900-1907-2/))

- **1910–1912:** *South Pole expedition diaries*. Manuscript field records associated with *Fram*, Framheim and the polar journey. They resist treatment as a single titled work: the surviving material belongs to an archive that also contains other participants’ diaries, letters and clippings. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1918–1925:** *Maud and aerial-expedition diaries*. Working records of the Northeast Passage, Arctic-drift and aviation projects rather than polished narratives; individual notebooks have different spans and should not be collapsed into an invented single title. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen); [Nasjonalbiblioteket](https://www.nb.no/historier-fra-samlingen/dramatikk-pa-tusen-meters-hoyde/))

- **1926:** *Diary from the airship Norge*. A terse observational log made during the transpolar flight, documenting terrain, ice and conditions more than personal experience. The 2026 source edition *Dagbok fra luftskipet «Norge» i 1926* is a posthumous manifestation edited by Anne Melgård, not a newly discovered book authored in 2026. ([Nasjonalbiblioteket](https://www.nb.no/historier-fra-samlingen/dramatikk-pa-tusen-meters-hoyde/))

- **Throughout his adult life:** *Correspondence and business archive*. Thousands of situational texts—letters, telegrams, accounts, contracts and planning papers—survive in Amundsen’s private archive, while about one hundred letters to Herman Gade are held at Harvard and further groups remain elsewhere. This is a documentary body of work without a single title or determinate count, and it resists being called one “work.” ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

### Manifestos and protocols

- **1908:** *Plan for a trans-Arctic drift*. Presented to the Norwegian Geographical Society, this was a project-statement that sought support for repeating and extending Nansen’s drift in *Fram*. It functioned as both manifesto and operational proposal, although Amundsen later redirected the expedition south. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1910:** *Telegram notifying Scott that Fram was proceeding to Antarctica*. A very short performative text: it did not narrate the changed expedition but enacted its disclosure to Amundsen’s rival. It therefore fits “manifesto” more closely than book, letter or report, although its conventional archival description is telegram. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **Expedition years:** *Equipment lists, depot plans, navigational instructions and field procedures*. These manuscript protocols governed clothing, food, aircraft loads, routes, observations and survival practice. They survive as multiple records rather than one fixed-titled work; an example is the equipment list for each 1925 flying boat. ([Museene i Akershus](https://amundsen.mia.no/resource/1925-mot-88-grader-nord-fellesutstyr-per-flybat/); [Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

### Photography, lantern images and film

- **Expedition years:** *Roald Amundsen photographic corpus*. Approximately 3,500 expedition-related photographs are associated with Amundsen in the National Library’s photographic database. The corpus combines pictures he took, commissioned, collected or used in lectures, so attribution of every exposure to his hand would be false; it is a working visual archive and body of manifestations rather than 3,500 securely authored individual obras. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen); [Museene i Akershus](https://amundsen.mia.no/samlinger/))

- **After 1903–1906:** *Gjøa expedition lecture-slide collection*. Hand-coloured glass slides assembled for Amundsen’s lectures, including photographs credited to him; the set joins expedition documentation, performance apparatus and later domestic display. It is consequently closer to a visual lecture-work than to either a book or a simple photograph album. ([Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1910–1912:** *South Pole expedition film material*. Seven surviving sections of nitrate footage document preparations, the voyage, Antarctic animals, Framheim and the polar party’s departure and return. The surviving material is incomplete and fragmented, with negatives and first- and second-generation prints carrying several titles; later Norwegian, English and German lecture versions are manifestations assembled from it, not securely one original finished film. ([UNESCO Memory of the World nomination](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/norway_amundsen.pdf); [Nasjonalbiblioteket](https://www.nb.no/dokumentarv-i-nasjonalbiblioteket/roald-amundsens-sydpolfilm-i-1911/))

- **1925:** *Roald Amundsen–Lincoln Ellsworth flyveekspedisjon 1925*. Moving-image documentation of the N24/N25 expedition. Surviving archival presentation treats it as expedition footage, so Amundsen’s role is properly that of expedition-maker and subject; sole cinematographic authorship should not be inferred. ([Museene i Akershus](https://amundsen.mia.no/en/resource/roald-amundsens-life-briefly/); [Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/Roald_Amundsen))

- **1926:** *The Polar Flight with Norge*. Film documentation of the airship expedition, another collaborative visual manifestation whose makers include camera operators and editors as well as the expedition’s leaders. Amundsen made the journey and helped produce its public image, but the film cannot safely be attributed to him alone. ([Nasjonalbiblioteket](https://www.nb.no/historier-fra-samlingen/dramatikk-pa-tusen-meters-hoyde/); [Museene i Akershus](https://amundsen.mia.no/samlinger/))
