## Works by Margaret Alice Murray

### Archaeological field projects and their reports

- **1894–1896:** *Copies and drawings for Koptos*. Archaeological documentation—inscriptional copies and plates—made for Flinders Petrie’s excavation report; because the report appeared under Petrie’s name, Murray’s contribution resists treatment as a separately authored work. [Sheppard](https://shareok.org/items/18cdef06-7ee7-4a12-93fd-80486edbac45)

- **1902–1903:** *The Osireion excavation at Abydos*. Murray’s first excavation project, undertaken within Petrie’s Egyptian Research Account; its material manifestation is the 1904 report *The Osireion at Abydos*. [Sheppard](https://shareok.org/items/18cdef06-7ee7-4a12-93fd-80486edbac45)

- **1904:** *The Osireion at Abydos*. An excavation report, with specialist contributions by J. G. Milne and W. E. Crum; it is a composite archaeological obra rather than a single-author narrative book. [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL497371W/The_Osireion_at_Abydos)

- **1903–1904:** *Saqqara mastaba excavation*. A field project directed by Murray in the Old Kingdom cemetery; its publication became a long, discontinuous project completed in two parts in 1905 and 1937. [Sheppard](https://shareok.org/items/18cdef06-7ee7-4a12-93fd-80486edbac45)

- **1905:** *Saqqara Mastabas, Part I; and Gurob*. A composite excavation volume: Murray made the Saqqara report, while the separately paginated Gurob report was L. Loat’s work; drawings were supplied by several collaborators. It should not be treated as though Murray authored every component. [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Murray%2C+Margaret+Alice)

- **1908:** *Index of Names and Titles of the Old Kingdom*. A research instrument—effectively a concordance or protocol for retrieving personal names and offices—not a continuous historical narrative. [Griffith Institute/Artefacts of Excavation](https://egyptartefacts.griffith.ox.ac.uk/people/margaret-murray/)

- **1911:** *Historical Studies*. A collective archaeological volume containing work by Murray alongside E. B. Knobel, W. W. Midgley, J. G. Milne, and W. M. Flinders Petrie; Murray’s authorship attaches to parts, not to the whole volume without qualification. [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Murray%2C+Margaret+Alice)

- **1915:** *Riqqeh and Memphis VI*. A collaborative excavation report by Reginald Engelbach, Murray, Hilda Petrie, and Flinders Petrie; it is a distributed archaeological obra whose individual sections and plates have distinct makers. [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Murray%2C+Margaret+Alice)

- **1920:** *Lahun*. A collaborative site report by Flinders Petrie, Guy Brunton, and Murray; Murray is a contributor rather than sole maker. [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Murray%2C+Margaret+Alice)

- **1921–1923:** *Excavations in Malta*. A field project at Borġ in-Nadur and associated sites, directed by Murray and published incrementally; excavation, recording, artefact study, and report together form the obra. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1923:** *Excavations in Malta, Part I*. The first manifestation of Murray’s Malta project: an archaeological report rather than a general book. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1925:** *Excavations in Malta, Part II*. The second report from the Malta project. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1929:** *Excavations in Malta, Part III*. The concluding published report of the Malta excavation sequence. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1930–1931:** *Cambridge excavations in Minorca*. Murray’s project at the megalithic sites of Trapucó and Sa Torreta; its several reports are manifestations of one field enterprise as well as separately citable texts. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1932:** *Cambridge Excavations in Minorca: Trapucó, Part I*. The first excavation report from Trapucó. [Arqueólogas](https://www.arqueologas.es/murray-margaret/)

- **1933:** *Excavaciones en Menorca: Trapucó*. A Spanish-language journal presentation of the Trapucó work; it is a distinct publication but also a secondary manifestation of the larger Minorca project. [Arqueólogas](https://www.arqueologas.es/murray-margaret/)

- **1934:** *Cambridge Excavations in Minorca: Sa Torreta*. The site report for Murray’s excavation at Sa Torreta. [Arqueólogas](https://www.arqueologas.es/murray-margaret/)

- **1934:** *Corpus of the Bronze-Age Pottery of Malta*. A typological corpus made with Horace Beck and Themistocles Zammit; as a classified body of artefacts and descriptions, it functions as a research instrument rather than merely a book. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1937:** *Saqqara Mastabas, Part II*. The delayed completion of the Saqqara publication project, with chapters by Kurt Sethe and drawings by several hands; the 1905–1937 span belongs to the obra even though the volumes appeared thirty-two years apart. [ARCE Library](https://library.arce.org/portal/Author/Home?author=Murray%2C+Margaret+Alice%2C&lng=en)

- **1937:** *Petra excavation*. A small archaeological project in the Nabataean city; its public guide and technical report appeared separately in 1939 and 1940. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1938:** *Cambridge Excavations in Minorca: Trapucó, Part II*. The final report in Murray’s Minorca series. [Arqueólogas](https://www.arqueologas.es/murray-margaret/)

- **1938:** *Work at Tell el-Ajjul*. Field participation with Petrie and Ernest Mackay; it resists classification as an independently authored Murray work because her labour entered a collective excavation and its reports. [Griffith Institute/Artefacts of Excavation](https://egyptartefacts.griffith.ox.ac.uk/people/margaret-murray/)

- **1940:** *A Street in Petra*. A technical archaeological report made with J. C. Ellis from the 1937 excavation; attribution is collaborative. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1952:** *Seven Memphite Tomb Chapels*. An archaeological corpus made with Hilda Petrie, comprising a short descriptive text and twenty-seven plates; its images and records are as much the work as its prose. [ARCE Library](https://library.arce.org/portal/Author/Home?author=Murray%2C+Margaret+Alice%2C&lng=en)

- **1952:** *Ancient Gaza V*. A collective excavation report associated with Ernest Mackay and Murray; it is not securely describable as Murray’s sole work. [Egyptology Forum series list](https://www.egyptologyforum.org/EEFSeries.html)

### Museum catalogues and object studies

- **1899:** *Catalogue of the Egyptian Antiquities in the National Museum of Antiquities, Edinburgh*. A museum catalogue and classification system, published in the *Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland*; it is an ordering of a collection as well as a text. [Society of Antiquaries of Scotland](https://journals.socantscot.org/index.php/psas/article/view/6752)

- **1903:** *Guide to the Collection of Egyptian Antiquities*. A public museum guide derived from Murray’s work on the Edinburgh collection; bibliographies sometimes blur its relationship to the longer 1899 catalogue, but they are distinct manifestations. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300)

- **1910:** *The Tomb of the Two Brothers*. A museum monograph joining excavation history, artefact description, anatomy, and textual study; it grew from Murray’s public unwrapping and investigation of the Manchester Museum mummies and is therefore partly the record of a performed scientific spectacle. [Griffith Institute/Artefacts of Excavation](https://egyptartefacts.griffith.ox.ac.uk/people/margaret-murray/)

- **1930:** *Egyptian Sculpture*. An illustrated handbook and historical survey intended to teach readers how to see Egyptian sculpture. [University of Memphis Egyptology bibliography](https://www.memphis.edu/egypt/resources/online_biblio/bibliography_mar2015k-m.html)

- **1934–1940:** *Catalogue of the Egyptian antiquities at Girton College*. A museum-ordering project rather than a clearly bounded publication; Murray catalogued the collection, but the resulting intellectual arrangement resists being called a separately published work. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

### Grammars, readers, and other protocols

- **1905:** *Elementary Egyptian Grammar*. A teaching protocol for learning hieroglyphic Egyptian, designed from Murray’s UCL instruction rather than as a descriptive history of the language. [Griffith Institute/Artefacts of Excavation](https://egyptartefacts.griffith.ox.ac.uk/people/margaret-murray/)

- **1911:** *Elementary Coptic (Sahidic) Grammar*. A pedagogical grammar and instructional protocol; a second edition appeared in 1927, but that edition is a later manifestation rather than a new work. [Griffith Institute/Artefacts of Excavation](https://egyptartefacts.griffith.ox.ac.uk/people/margaret-murray/)

- **1933:** *A Coptic Reading Book, with Glossary, for the Use of Beginners*. A teaching reader made with Dorothy Pilcher; selection, sequencing, and glossary turn source texts into a pedagogical protocol. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300)

### Egyptological essays, retellings, and surveys

- **1895:** *The Descent of Property in the Early Periods of Egyptian History*. Murray’s first published research article, encouraged by Petrie and printed in the *Proceedings of the Society of Biblical Archaeology*. [Sheppard](https://shareok.org/items/18cdef06-7ee7-4a12-93fd-80486edbac45)

- **1904:** *A Roman Terra-Cotta Figure of an Apis Bull from Memphis*. A brief object notice rather than a monograph. [Archaeology Data Service](https://archaeologydataservice.ac.uk/library/browse/issue.xhtml?recordId=1097203)

- **1913:** *Ancient Egyptian Legends*. A collection of English retellings, not an edition of a single ancient text; Murray is the maker of the modern narrative forms, while the underlying stories belong to ancient textual traditions. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300)

- **1917:** *Some Fresh Inscriptions*. An epigraphic article in *Ancient Egypt*, presenting newly recorded material. [Oriental Institute bibliography](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/saoc55.pdf)

- **1931:** *Egyptian Temples*. An illustrated architectural and religious survey for a broad readership. [University of Memphis Egyptology bibliography](https://www.memphis.edu/egypt/resources/online_biblio/bibliography_mar2015k-m.html)

- **1939:** *Petra, the Rock City of Edom*. A public guide to Petra, distinct from the technical excavation report; it translates archaeological space into an itinerary for visitors. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1949:** *Ancient Egyptian Religious Poetry*. A translated and arranged anthology; the ancient poems are not Murray’s creations, but the English renderings, selection, and anthology-form are her obra. [University of Memphis Egyptology bibliography](https://www.memphis.edu/egypt/resources/online_biblio/bibliography_mar2015k-m.html)

- **1949:** *The Splendour That Was Egypt: A General Survey of Egyptian Culture and Civilisation*. A synthetic popular history shaped by Murray’s diffusionist interpretation of Egyptian influence; later revised and reissued editions are manifestations of this work, not additional works. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

### Witchcraft, folklore, and comparative religion

- **1917:** *Organisations of Witches in Great Britain*. A *Folklore* article and the first substantial public formulation of Murray’s witch-cult hypothesis; it acts almost as a manifesto for the research programme developed in her later books. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300)

- **1921:** *The Witch-Cult in Western Europe: A Study in Anthropology*. A historical-anthropological thesis claiming that early-modern witch trials preserved evidence of an organized pre-Christian religion. Its existence is secure, but its central attribution of testimony to a surviving cult was rejected by later historians and is not accepted scholarship. [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1925:** *Egyptian Elements in the Grail Romance*. A comparative-folklore essay proposing Egyptian survivals in the Grail tradition; it belongs to Murray’s broader diffusionist project. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300)

- **1929:** *Witchcraft*. An encyclopaedia article commissioned for the *Encyclopaedia Britannica* and used in successive editions for decades; because it presented Murray’s disputed hypothesis as reference knowledge, it functioned less as neutral description than as an authoritative manifestation of her programme. [Sheppard](https://shareok.org/items/18cdef06-7ee7-4a12-93fd-80486edbac45)

- **1931 or 1933:** *The God of the Witches*. A comparative-religion book extending the witch-cult hypothesis around a male horned deity. Bibliographies disagree over whether to date the first appearance 1931 or 1933, and one later scholarly list gives 1930; that disagreement should not be silently normalized. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300) [Whitehouse](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1469627/1/259-1582-1-PB.pdf)

- **1932:** *Maltese Folk-Tales*. A collection made with L. Galea from orally circulating narratives; authorship is consequently layered among narrators, collector, translator, and editor rather than wholly Murray’s. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300)

- **1954:** *The Divine King in England: A Study in Anthropology*. The final book-length extension of Murray’s witch-cult and sacred-kingship project, reading selected English royal deaths as ritual sacrifice; its argument remains disputed and unsupported by the historical consensus. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300)

- **1963:** *The Genesis of Religion*. A late comparative study proposing an evolutionary account of religious forms; published in Murray’s centenary year. [Griffith Institute/Artefacts of Excavation](https://egyptartefacts.griffith.ox.ac.uk/people/margaret-murray/)

### Editorial, pedagogical, and institutional obra

- **1914–1935:** *Editorial work for Ancient Egypt*. Murray served as the journal’s de facto editor during Petrie’s absences, while also writing articles and many, often unsigned or lightly attributed, reviews. This is a body of editorial labour whose boundaries and individual authorship cannot now be made fully discrete, so it resists enumeration as a set of securely titled Murray works. [Sheppard](https://shareok.org/items/18cdef06-7ee7-4a12-93fd-80486edbac45)

- **1898–1935:** *The UCL Egyptology curriculum*. A sustained pedagogical project comprising language, history, and religion courses, examinations, and a diploma programme. It was made through teaching and institutional design rather than fixed in a single text, and therefore belongs to Murray’s obra without being reducible to a publication. [Imhausen](https://dissertationreviews.org/margaret-murrays-egyptological-career/)

- **1900s–1950s:** *Public mummy unwrappings and Egyptological lectures*. Performative scholarly manifestations combining demonstration, interpretation, and public education; surviving reports document the events, but the events themselves—not merely their scripts—were the made things. [Sheppard](https://shareok.org/items/18cdef06-7ee7-4a12-93fd-80486edbac45)

### Autobiography

- **1963:** *My First Hundred Years*. Murray’s memoir, made at the end of her centenary year; it is a retrospective self-construction as well as a record of her life and work. [Bonser](https://doi.org/10.1080/0015587X.1961.9717300)
