## Works by Thathanka Iyotake

### Pictographic autobiographies and ledger drawings

- **c. 1870:** *Pictographic record of war deeds*. A now-lost project in which Thathanka Iyotake reportedly pictured his martial achievements; an extant set of fifty-five Fort Buford drawings was long described as Four Horns’s copies of forty of these images plus fifteen by Jumping Bull. Candace Greene’s reassessment concludes that the copyist’s identity and cultural affiliation are unsupported and that, although the pictures concern the two men’s deeds, they cannot securely document Thathanka Iyotake’s hand or even prove the traditional account of a single original “autobiography.” ([Stirling](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/23667); [Greene](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/mar/article/view/31653))

- **1882:** *Smith pictographic autobiography*. An obra of twenty-two signed pencil-and-watercolor ledger drawings made while Thathanka Iyotake was imprisoned at Fort Randall, each picturing a selected war deed; his orally supplied explanations were written down separately by Lieutenant Wallace Tear. The National Anthropological Archives regards these drawings as his work, although the present volume is partially disbound and was later altered by archival lamination. ([NAA MS 1929-b](https://www.si.edu/object/archives/sova-naa-ms1929b); [Stirling](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/23667))

- **1882:** *Pettinger pictographic record*. Thirteen unsigned drawings published by M. W. Stirling as a second Fort Randall series, closely resembling the signed Smith drawings in style, paper, and color; Stirling thought missing leaves likely because its narrative sequences have gaps. The Buffalo Bill Center now catalogues fourteen pictographs in the associated Pratt–Evans–Pettinger–Anderson group and calls the attribution to Thathanka Iyotake traditional rather than certain, so neither authorship nor completeness should be silently treated as settled. ([Stirling](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/23667); [Buffalo Bill Center](https://centerofthewest.org/2021/08/06/points-west-unexpected-journey/))

### Songs, prayers, and ceremonial compositions

The titles below are descriptive English titles or first-line titles assigned in modern transmission, not titles demonstrably fixed by the composer. The principal corpus is Robert P. Higheagle’s 1929 *Twenty-Five Songs Made by Sitting Bull*, reconstructed nearly forty years after Thathanka Iyotake’s death from One Bull, White Bull, and other elderly associates. It preserves words and contextual testimony but not a complete authorial notation of the melodies; consequently these are orally transmitted manifestations whose attribution is testimonial rather than autograph.

- **shortly after June 25, 1876:** *Fearless I Live*. A personal or war song subsequently adopted as a Lakota parade or honoring song, according to No Flesh’s testimony to Higheagle. ([Higheagle, pp. 4–5](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **1883:** *The Black Hills Belong to Me*. A political travel song reportedly sung while Thathanka Iyotake journeyed to Washington concerning the Black Hills; its dating follows the transmitted occasion rather than a contemporary manuscript. ([Higheagle, p. 4](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before June 25, 1876:** *A War Chief I Have Been*. A courage or resistance song said by Dewey Beard to have been sung before the Battle of the Little Bighorn. ([Higheagle, pp. 4–5](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Song for Fair Weather*. A Sun Dance ceremonial song reportedly composed at the request of ceremonial leaders; One Bull identified both words and melody as Thathanka Iyotake’s. ([Higheagle, p. 5](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** Untitled leadership-procession song, beginning in translation “Ye Tribes behold me.” White Bull said Thathanka Iyotake sang it when ceremonially installed as head chief; that office and occasion are themselves matters of contested historical interpretation. ([Higheagle, pp. 5–6](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *At Day Break I Roam*. A Buffalo Society song associated with seeking the pipe and blessing or traversing the land. ([Higheagle, p. 6](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Everywhere on Earth I Roam*. A Fox Society initiation song; Eagle Thunder specifically testified that Thathanka Iyotake composed it. ([Higheagle, p. 6](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **June 1876:** Untitled vision-seeking prayer for the people’s survival. Higheagle presents it as a chant performed during Thathanka Iyotake’s pre-battle *haŋbléčheyapi*; it is simultaneously prayer, song, and ritual action rather than a separable literary text. ([Higheagle, p. 7](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** Untitled Fox Society appeal to young men, beginning in translation “Ye young men, help me.” One Bull remembered it as a song Thathanka Iyotake frequently performed at society gatherings. ([Higheagle, p. 7](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Lullaby*. A domestic song said by White Bull to have been sung by Thathanka Iyotake while carrying his children or grandchildren. ([Higheagle, pp. 7–8](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Hard Times Will Be Coming*. An eagle song transmitted by One Bull, cast as the bird’s speech; the modern title is interpretive rather than securely original. ([Higheagle, p. 8](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** Untitled Creator’s song for the sweat lodge, beginning in translation “This earth the Creator I am.” One Bull transmitted it as ceremonial speech voiced by the Great Spirit; it resists separation into “song,” “prayer,” and “protocol.” ([Higheagle, pp. 8–9](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Pretty Door*. A mother song dedicated to Tatiyopa Wakan Waste, identified in Higheagle’s translation as Thathanka Iyotake’s mother. ([Higheagle, p. 9](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Strong Heart Society Song*. A warrior-society composition explicitly attributed by One Bull to Thathanka Iyotake as a Strong Heart member. ([Higheagle, pp. 9–10](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **summer 1876:** *Thunderbirds in the Clouds*. A rain-making ceremonial song reportedly made and sung during heat and drought; its words are presented as the speech of Thunderbirds. ([Higheagle, p. 10](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Four Corners*. A sacred-directions song which, according to One Bull, became Jumping Bull’s dream song for Thathanka Iyotake’s shield. ([Higheagle, pp. 10–11](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** Untitled Fox Society memorial song concerning Red Boulder. One Bull described Thathanka Iyotake as its singer and recalled its later reuse in a 1919 victory gathering, illustrating how the composition continued as communal practice rather than a fixed artifact. ([Higheagle, p. 11](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Song of the Bobolink*. A bird song said to have been made while Thathanka Iyotake watched a bobolink with the young White Bull. ([Higheagle, pp. 11–12](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *My Horse*. A song addressed to a horse before battle or buffalo hunting, transmitted by One Bull as Thathanka Iyotake’s composition. ([Higheagle, p. 12](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Crow Society Song*. A ceremonial society song attributed to Thathanka Iyotake by One Bull. ([Higheagle, p. 12](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **winter, 1870s:** Untitled prayer in time of famine. A chanted offering-prayer reportedly made at Wood Mountain during the first exceptionally hard Canadian winter; as transmitted, it is a ritual act rather than merely a verbal composition. ([Higheagle, pp. 12–13](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Pretty Bird You Have Seen Me*. A dream or vision song addressed to a woodpecker credited with warning Thathanka Iyotake of a bear. ([Higheagle, pp. 13–14](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** *Grandfather Pity Us*. A ceremonial sweat-lodge song transmitted by One Bull and explicitly described by Higheagle as made by Thathanka Iyotake. ([Higheagle, p. 14](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **c. age fifteen:** *Wolf Song*. A song dedicated to the wolf nation and connected in One Bull’s account with Thathanka Iyotake aiding a wounded wolf; the biographical date is traditional and cannot be converted securely into a calendar year. ([Higheagle, pp. 14–15](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890:** Untitled eagle song associated with a vision near Sylvan Lake. Higheagle’s compilation presents it as foreknowledge of Thathanka Iyotake’s death and the eagle’s succession to leadership; its boundaries as an independent song are less clear than those of the numbered modern selections. ([Higheagle, pp. 15–16](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

### Oratory, testimony, and orally mediated narratives

- **before 1890; recorded 1929:** *A Crow Indian Spied in a Buffalo Horn Spoon*. An autobiographical oral narrative headed “By Chief Sitting Bull” and “as told to One Bull,” then translated and written down by Higheagle. It is therefore not an autograph literary text but a layered manifestation involving speaker, family transmitter, translator, and editor. ([Higheagle, pp. 17–20](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **before 1890; recorded 1929:** *Migration of Birds*. A traditional or personal story identified by Higheagle as “Sitting Bull[’s] story” and translated from family testimony. The surviving artifact is Higheagle’s posthumous translation, so treating it as a stable authored prose work would overstate what the record establishes. ([Higheagle’s covering letter, pp. 2–3](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/61bb963a90576f5d8de985f6/t/6849f7826b7d203b943509a1/1749677954708/Twenty-Five%2BSongs%2BMade%2Bby%2BSitting%2BBull.pdf))

- **c. 1860s–1890:** *Speeches, councils, interviews, and reported sayings*. This is a body of performed oratory rather than a book: Mark Diedrich’s collected edition assembles surviving speeches and fragments from newspapers, interpreters, government records, interviews, and reminiscences. The wording is frequently mediated by translation, stenography, paraphrase, hostile journalism, or recollection, so the corpus preserves manifestations of Thathanka Iyotake’s speech but cannot be treated as a set of authorially finalized texts. ([Diedrich](https://books.google.com/books/about/Sitting_Bull.html?id=h7p1AAAAMAAJ))
