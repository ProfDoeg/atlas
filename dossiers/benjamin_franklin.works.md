## Works by Benjamin Franklin

### Collected textual obra

- **1722–1790:** *The writings and correspondence of Benjamin Franklin*. A vast textual obra—letters, memoranda, journalism, essays, speeches, scientific reports, diplomatic papers, and private notes—rather than a single work; about 30,000 Franklin-related documents survive, and the definitive *Papers of Benjamin Franklin* remains the controlling scholarly edition. Individual letters are not relisted below unless they became distinct works or projects. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/info_date.jsp) [NARA](https://www.archives.gov/nhprc/dataset-founders-online)

### Serial personae, journalism, and almanacs

- **1722:** *Silence Dogood*. A fourteen-part pseudonymous essay series printed in the *New-England Courant*, complete with an accompanying poem in the seventh number. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1722–1723:** *New-England Courant contributions*. A group of attributed or proposed-attribution newspaper pieces—including “Rules for the New-England Courant,” “On Titles of Honour,” “High Tide in Boston,” “Timothy Wagstaff,” and “Abigail Twitterfield”—whose boundaries depend on attribution scholarship rather than signed authorship. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1728–1729:** *The Busy-Body*. A pseudonymous newspaper-essay project; Franklin wrote numbers 1–5 and 8, while the larger series was collaborative, so it resists treatment as a wholly Franklin-authored work. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1729–1748:** *The Pennsylvania Gazette under Franklin*. A newspaper and continuing editorial obra that Franklin owned, printed, edited, and filled with signed and anonymous material; it is not one authored text, and attribution of individual unsigned items remains uneven. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/) [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/founding-fathers-pennsylvania)

- **1732–1758:** *Poor Richard’s Almanack* / *Poor Richard Improved*. An annual almanac issued under the persona Richard Saunders, combining calendars, practical information, prefaces, borrowed and reformulated maxims, jokes, and short essays; its sayings are not all original compositions. The issue styled 1733 was printed late in 1732, and Franklin ceased writing it after the issue for 1758. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/) [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/founding-fathers-pennsylvania)

### Pamphlets, essays, satires, and protocols

- **1725:** *A Dissertation on Liberty and Necessity, Pleasure and Pain*. A philosophical pamphlet written and printed by Franklin in London; he later repudiated it and attempted to suppress the edition. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1726:** *Plan of Conduct*. A private ethical protocol for Franklin’s own life, not originally a public literary work. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **c. 1728:** *Articles of Belief and Acts of Religion*. A private creed and devotional protocol assembled for Franklin’s own use rather than for a church or publication. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1728:** *Epitaph*. Franklin’s mock epitaph comparing his body to the binding of a book; several versions and dates circulated, making the familiar wording less fixed than its later monumental reproductions suggest. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1729:** *A Modest Enquiry into the Nature and Necessity of a Paper-Currency*. An economic and political pamphlet advocating an expanded Pennsylvania paper currency. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1729:** *The Printer to the Reader*. An editorial manifesto defining the public responsibilities and hazards of a newspaper printer. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1730s:** *Apology for Printers*. A manifesto-like defense of printing competing opinions without making the printer answerable for every opinion printed. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1730s:** *Rules and Maxims for Promoting Matrimonial Happiness*. A satirical conduct protocol whose title promises instruction while its literary action complicates that category. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1730s:** *A Witch Trial at Mount Holly*. A fabricated newspaper report and satire; because it was presented as news, it is also a media hoax rather than merely a descriptive essay. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1731:** *On the Providence of God in the Government of the World*. A religious discourse or lay sermon; Franklin apparently composed it for oral delivery, so it occupies the border between manuscript and performed text. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1731:** *Proposals and Queries to be Asked the Junto*. A protocol for collective inquiry and mutual improvement, inseparable from the associational project it organized. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1730s:** *The Drinker’s Dictionary*. A comic lexicon of expressions for drunkenness, making a catalogue rather than a continuous argument. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1730s:** *Rules for a Club Formerly Established in Philadelphia*. A constitution-like protocol for voluntary association. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1743:** *A Proposal for Promoting Useful Knowledge among the British Plantations in America*. A manifesto and institutional protocol proposing the learned network that became the American Philosophical Society. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/) [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/join-in-voluntary-associations-in-america/about-this-exhibition/making-associations-in-early-america/)

- **1747:** *The Speech of Miss Polly Baker*. A satirical courtroom speech published anonymously; long attributed to Franklin and included in his canon, but its authorship has been contested and should not be treated as certain. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1747:** *Plain Truth; or, Serious Considerations on the Present State of the City of Philadelphia and Province of Pennsylvania*. A militia manifesto intended to produce organized action, not merely to describe the colony’s defenses. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1747–1748:** *Devices and Mottoes of the Associators*. Emblematic designs and verbal devices for Pennsylvania militia companies; a small Gesamtkunstwerk of image, motto, material flag, and political organization rather than a conventional essay. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1748:** *Advice to a Young Tradesman*. A conduct essay or economic protocol addressed to an aspiring tradesman. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1749:** *Proposals Relating to the Education of Youth in Pensilvania*. A prospectus and institutional manifesto that helped call the Philadelphia academy into existence. [Penn Libraries](https://www.library.upenn.edu/exhibits/revolution-penn/foundation-and-fracture) [Penn](https://home.www.upenn.edu/about/history)

- **1749:** *Idea of the English School*. A curricular protocol for English-language education, associated with Franklin’s academy project. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1749:** *Rules Proper to be Observed in Trade*. A compact commercial conduct protocol. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1749:** *Rules for Making Oneself a Disagreeable Companion*. A satirical anti-protocol: instructions whose literal observance would produce the opposite of desirable conduct. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1751:** *Appeal for the Hospital*. A public fund-raising text that acts on its readers and belongs to the Pennsylvania Hospital project. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/) [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/join-in-voluntary-associations-in-america/about-this-exhibition/making-associations-in-early-america/)

- **1751:** *Observations Concerning the Increase of Mankind, Peopling of Countries, &c.* A demographic and political essay, initially circulated privately and later published without Franklin’s authorization. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1754:** *A Dialogue between X, Y, and Z, Concerning the Present State of Affairs in Pennsylvania*. A political dialogue advocating defensive organization. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1755:** *A Parable Against Persecution*. An anti-intolerance parable presented in scriptural style; Franklin adapted an earlier source, and later reprinting encouraged mistaken belief that it came from the Bible. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1755:** *A Parable on Brotherly Love*. A religious-political parable rather than a doctrinal tract. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1757:** *The Way to Wealth*. The preface to the final Franklin-written issue of *Poor Richard Improved*, constructed as Father Abraham’s speech from almanac maxims; it became a separately circulating work only through later publication. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1759:** *An Historical Review of the Constitution and Government of Pennsylvania*. A political history traditionally associated with Franklin but probably written principally by Richard Jackson from materials and arguments supplied by Franklin; sole authorship is disputed. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1760:** *The Interest of Great Britain Considered, with Regard to Her Colonies, and the Acquisitions of Canada and Guadaloupe*. A geopolitical pamphlet arguing that Britain should retain Canada; commonly called the “Canada Pamphlet.” [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1764:** *A Narrative of the Late Massacres, in Lancaster County, of a Number of Indians, Friends of the Province, by Persons Unknown*. A polemical narrative condemning the Paxton massacres. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1766:** *Examination before the House of Commons*. A performed political testimony preserved as a printed question-and-answer text; it was jointly produced by Franklin’s answers, his interrogators, and parliamentary reporting, so it resists simple classification as a solely authored essay. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1768:** *Causes of the American Discontents before 1768*. A newspaper essay explaining colonial grievances to a British readership. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1768:** *Of Lightning, and the Method (Now Used in America) of Securing Buildings and Persons from Its Mischievous Effects*. A public protocol for lightning protection as well as an account of the underlying science. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1768:** *A Scheme for a New Alphabet and a Reformed Mode of Spelling*. A linguistic project embodied in a phonetic alphabet and reform proposal; it remained experimental rather than becoming a generally adopted writing system. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1770:** *A Conversation on Slavery*. A dialogue opposing slavery and the slave trade. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1770–1771:** *The Cravenstreet Gazette*. A mock domestic newspaper produced within Franklin’s London household; its form turns private social play into simulated journalism. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1773:** *Rules by Which a Great Empire May Be Reduced to a Small One*. A satirical anti-protocol directing imperial ministers toward policies that would destroy their empire. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1773:** *An Edict by the King of Prussia*. A political satire using a fictional royal edict to expose Britain’s claims over its American colonies. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1774:** *Public Statement on the Hutchinson Letters*. A declaration assuming responsibility for transmitting the Hutchinson correspondence; as a public intervention in a political crisis, it acts more like a manifesto than a neutral report. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1776:** *The Sale of the Hessians*. A satire cast as a commercial or governmental transaction concerning German troops hired by Britain. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1777:** *Model of a Letter of Recommendation*. A comic template that parodies the protocol of indiscriminate recommendation writing. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1777:** *The Twelve Commandments*. A social satire framed as an expanded commandment list. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1779:** *The Ephemera*. A bagatelle—a brief literary-philosophical fable written for Madame Brillon—whose miniature scale and intimate occasion resist the status of a formal treatise. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1779:** *The Morals of Chess*. An ethical essay and protocol of play that treats behavior at the chessboard as civic and moral practice. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1779:** *The Whistle*. A moral bagatelle built from an autobiographical anecdote and addressed to Madame Brillon. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1779:** *Proposed New Version of the Bible*. A biblical parody and satire, not a sustained translation project. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1779:** *The Elysian Fields*. A literary bagatelle associated with Franklin’s intimate French circle. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1779–1780:** *Drinking Songs*. Social verse and performance pieces; their identity lies partly in convivial use, not only in the surviving words. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1780:** *Dialogue between the Gout and Mr. Franklin*. A comic dramatic dialogue personifying Franklin’s illness. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1780:** *The Handsome and the Deformed Leg*. A moral allegory or bagatelle about habits of attention. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1781:** *To the Royal Academy of *****.* A satirical mock proposal concerning bodily odors, often given the modern editorial title “Fart Proudly”; that phrase is not Franklin’s title. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1782:** *Supplement to the Boston Independent Chronicle*. A counterfeit newspaper supplement fabricated in Franklin’s press at Passy to publicize alleged British-allied atrocities; it is simultaneously satire, propaganda, typographic object, and media hoax. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1782:** *Articles for a Treaty of Peace with Madame Brillon*. A private social bagatelle that borrows diplomatic form for playful interpersonal negotiation. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1782:** *Remarks Concerning the Savages of North America*. An essay reversing European assumptions about civility and Indigenous peoples. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1782:** *Information to Those Who Would Remove to America*. A practical emigrant advisory and corrective to European fantasies about American patronage and advancement. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1784:** *An Economical Project*. A satirical-practical proposal for conserving candle wax through earlier waking, now associated with daylight-saving arguments but not a scheme for changing clocks. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1784:** *Loose Thoughts on a Universal Fluid*. A speculative scientific essay rather than a demonstrated theory. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1785:** *A Petition of the Left Hand*. A personification and educational satire framed as a petition—thus a text that performs a plea rather than merely describing one. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1786:** *The Art of Procuring Pleasant Dreams*. A practical health-and-sleep protocol with comic and philosophical elements. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1787:** *On Sending Felons to America*. A satire proposing reciprocal shipment of rattlesnakes to Britain. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1788:** *A Comparison of the Conduct of the Ancient Jews and of the Anti-Federalists in the United States of America*. A pseudonymous political satire published as “A.Z.” [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1789:** *An Account of the Supremest Court of Judicature in Pennsylvania, viz. The Court of the Press*. A satire on unaccountable newspaper power. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1789:** *An Address to the Public from the Pennsylvania Society for Promoting the Abolition of Slavery*. A collectively issued abolitionist manifesto signed in Franklin’s presidential capacity; editorial responsibility is associated with him, but it should not be treated as uncomplicated sole authorship. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1789:** *Plan for Improving the Condition of the Free Blacks*. A social and educational protocol produced within the Pennsylvania abolition society; institutional authorship complicates its attribution to Franklin alone. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1790:** *Sidi Mehemet Ibrahim on the Slave Trade*. Franklin’s last published satire, exposing proslavery arguments by transferring them to an imagined North African speaker. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

### Scientific texts, maps, and experimental projects

- **1746–1754:** *Electrical experiments and letters*. An experimental-scientific project conducted with collaborators and communicated chiefly through letters to Peter Collinson; it generated Franklin’s one-fluid theory, terminology such as positive and negative charge, and practical investigations rather than a single original book. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/) [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/ben-franklin-lives-in-your-smartphone-180979317/)

- **1751–1774:** *Experiments and Observations on Electricity, Made at Philadelphia in America*. A repeatedly enlarged book assembled in London from Franklin’s letters and papers, initially through Peter Collinson’s agency; its five English editions are successive states of an evolving scientific obra, not five independent works. [Yale](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300006506/the-papers-of-benjamin-franklin-vol-1/) [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1752:** *The kite experiment*. An experimental protocol and reported event demonstrating that storm clouds were electrically charged; William Franklin assisted, and the famous later iconography should not be confused with the surviving written account. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/) [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/ben-franklin-lives-in-your-smartphone-180979317/)

- **1750s:** *Course of Experiments*. A protocol or syllabus for electrical demonstrations rather than a narrative scientific treatise. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1769:** *A Chart of the Gulf Stream*. A scientific map produced from maritime observations, especially information supplied by Timothy Folger; Franklin published and promoted the chart, but its knowledge and execution were collaborative. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1785:** *Maritime Observations*. A body of practical nautical proposals and observations, including ship design, sea anchors, oil on water, navigation, and safety; it is better understood as a composite technical project than as a single discovery. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

### Graphic and typographic works

- **1730s–1760s:** *Nature-printed paper currency designs*. Anti-counterfeiting currency ornaments made by transferring the fine vein patterns of leaves into printing plates; individual notes were public financial objects made with partners and authorities, not solely authored artworks. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1754:** *Join, or Die*. A woodcut political emblem showing a segmented snake, printed with Franklin’s Albany-union advocacy; its status is simultaneously image, slogan, newspaper device, and manifesto. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1775–1776:** *Continental currency devices*. Emblems and mottos devised or adapted by Franklin for congressional paper money; their manufacture and authorization were collective, while attribution of particular devices varies. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1776:** *Design for the Great Seal of the United States*. A proposed emblem using the Israelites’ passage through the Red Sea; Franklin devised it as a member of a three-person committee, but the adopted Great Seal is not his design. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1776–1785:** *Passy press productions*. Diplomatic printing made at Franklin’s residence near Paris—passports, broadsides, propaganda, bagatelles, and official forms—constituting a workshop obra rather than a single authored text; assistants and compositors participated materially. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

### Political plans, treaties, constitutions, and public acts

- **1754:** *Albany Plan of Union*. A proposed intercolonial governmental structure and constitutional project; Franklin was its principal architect, but it emerged from committee deliberation and was rejected by both colonial assemblies and the Crown. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/) [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/founding-fathers-pennsylvania)

- **1754:** *Reasons and Motives for the Albany Plan of Union*. An argumentative companion to the plan, explaining its proposed machinery and political purpose. [LOA I](https://www.loa.org/books/234-silence-dogood-the-busy-body-amp-early-writings/)

- **1775:** *Articles of Confederation and Perpetual Union*. Franklin’s proposed national constitution, distinct from the later Articles adopted by Congress; the project was unfinished in the sense that his version was not enacted. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1776:** *Declaration of Independence*. A collective manifesto drafted principally by Thomas Jefferson and revised by the Committee of Five and Congress; Franklin contributed editorial changes and signed it, but did not author the whole document. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration)

- **1776:** *Plan of Treaties*. A congressional diplomatic protocol prepared by a committee including Franklin and used to guide negotiations with France; collective authorship prevents its treatment as Franklin’s private work. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1778:** *Treaty of Amity and Commerce* and *Treaty of Alliance with France*. Diplomatic instruments negotiated and signed by Franklin with fellow commissioners and French representatives; these are collective juridical acts, not authored books. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1783:** *Definitive Treaty of Peace between the United States and Great Britain*. The Treaty of Paris, negotiated and signed by Franklin, John Adams, John Jay, and the British commissioner; Franklin helped make the political settlement but cannot be assigned sole authorship. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1787:** *Speeches and motions at the Constitutional Convention*. A group of performed political interventions—including speeches on salaries, representation, prayers, and the closing appeal for unanimity—usually read aloud by another delegate because Franklin’s health limited his delivery. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/)

- **1787:** *Constitution of the United States*. A collective constitutional project debated and signed by Franklin; he made proposals and urged assent but was neither sole drafter nor sole author. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution)

- **1790:** *Petition from the Pennsylvania Society for the Abolition of Slavery*. A collective petition to Congress signed by Franklin as society president; it is a performative political text whose action was the request itself, not a private Franklin composition. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/founding-fathers-pennsylvania)

### Civic and institutional projects

- **c. 1727:** *The Junto*. A mutual-improvement and discussion club organized by Franklin; as a continuing social protocol embodied in meetings, questions, and members, it resists classification as a discrete work. [Penn Archives](https://archives.upenn.edu/exhibits/penn-people/biography/benjamin-franklin/)

- **1731:** *Library Company of Philadelphia*. A subscription-library project founded collectively from the Junto, with Franklin drafting or promoting its organizing instruments; the institution, not merely its charter, is the made thing. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/join-in-voluntary-associations-in-america/about-this-exhibition/making-associations-in-early-america/)

- **1736:** *Union Fire Company*. A voluntary firefighting association organized with other Philadelphians; it is a civic protocol enacted by people, equipment, dues, and recurring practice. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/join-in-voluntary-associations-in-america/about-this-exhibition/making-associations-in-early-america/) [City of Philadelphia](https://www.phila.gov/media/20190213125332/Historic-District-Old-City.pdf)

- **1743:** *American Philosophical Society*. A learned-society project initiated through Franklin’s proposal and made collectively; its later continuity exceeds his individual authorship. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/join-in-voluntary-associations-in-america/about-this-exhibition/making-associations-in-early-america/)

- **1749–1755:** *Academy and College of Philadelphia*. Franklin’s educational project, realized with twenty-four trustees and eventually ancestral to the University of Pennsylvania; the institution is a collective obra, not simply the pamphlet that proposed it. [Penn Libraries](https://www.library.upenn.edu/exhibits/revolution-penn/foundation-and-fracture) [Penn](https://home.www.upenn.edu/about/history)

- **1751:** *Pennsylvania Hospital*. A civic-medical institution promoted by Franklin with Thomas Bond and other subscribers; Franklin’s fund-raising and legislative strategy were formative, but the hospital was collectively made. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/join-in-voluntary-associations-in-america/about-this-exhibition/making-associations-in-early-america/)

- **1752:** *Philadelphia Contributionship for the Insurance of Houses from Loss by Fire*. A mutual fire-insurance institution founded with associates; it made risk management into an enduring civic protocol. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/join-in-voluntary-associations-in-america/about-this-exhibition/making-associations-in-early-america/)

- **1750s–1775:** *Colonial postal network reforms*. A communications project carried out with William Hunter and others: surveyed routes, milestones, accounting changes, and faster schedules. It was an administrative system rather than an invention attributable to Franklin alone. [City of Philadelphia](https://www.phila.gov/media/20190213125332/Historic-District-Old-City.pdf)

- **1775:** *United States Post Office under the Continental Congress*. Franklin became the first postmaster general and adapted the colonial network for revolutionary government; the institution was congressionally created and collectively operated, so it is not his sole work. [Smithsonian](https://invention.si.edu/invention-stories/benjamin-franklins-inventions)

- **1787:** *Pennsylvania Society for Promoting the Abolition of Slavery under Franklin’s presidency*. An abolitionist institutional project that Franklin led but did not originate alone; its petitions, address, and educational plan are manifestations of collective action. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/founding-fathers-pennsylvania)

### Instruments, machines, and material inventions

- **c. 1717:** *Swimming paddles*. Hand-worn wooden paddles devised in Franklin’s youth to increase propulsion; surviving displays are reproductions based on his later description, not securely identified original objects. [Smithsonian](https://invention.si.edu/invention-stories/benjamin-franklins-inventions)

- **1741–1742:** *Pennsylvania fireplace*. An iron heating apparatus now called the Franklin stove, accompanied by a printed explanatory account; later “Franklin stoves” incorporate alterations by other makers, so the generic name should not erase the changing object. [Smithsonian](https://invention.si.edu/invention-stories/benjamin-franklins-inventions)

- **1740s–1750s:** *Electrical battery*. An arrangement of linked Leyden jars for storing charge; Franklin coined the battery analogy and developed experimental configurations, although the device emerged within an international electrical culture. [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/ben-franklin-lives-in-your-smartphone-180979317/)

- **1740s–1750s:** *Electrical bells and sentry-box apparatus*. Experimental devices for detecting atmospheric electricity; these were instruments in an inquiry rather than consumer inventions with a single fixed model. [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/)

- **1749–1752:** *Lightning rod*. A building-protection system derived from Franklin’s electrical theory; pointed versus blunt terminals later became controversial, and European experimenters independently tested related proposals. [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/ben-franklin-lives-in-your-smartphone-180979317/)

- **1752:** *Flexible urinary catheter*. A flexible silver catheter commissioned for Franklin’s brother John and made by a silversmith from Franklin’s design; it modified an existing medical instrument rather than inventing catheterization itself. [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/benjamin-franklin-nations-first-newsman-180984281/)

- **1750s:** *Three-wheel carriage odometer*. A wheel-driven distance-measuring device used in postal-route surveying; Franklin adapted established odometer principles, so “invention” is less exact than practical redesign. [Penn](https://home.www.upenn.edu/about/history)

- **1750s–1760s:** *Improved street lamp*. A four-pane lamp with vents intended to reduce soot and improve airflow; it was a redesign of street lighting rather than the invention of the street lamp. [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/benjamin-franklin-nations-first-newsman-180984281/)

- **1761–1762:** *Glass armonica*. A musical instrument of nested, tuned glass bowls rotated by a foot treadle, developed with London glassblower Charles James; it is both machine and performance medium, and thus approaches a small Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a text. [Smithsonian](https://invention.si.edu/invention-stories/benjamin-franklins-inventions)

- **Eighteenth century, date uncertain:** *Library chair and steps*. A chair convertible into a stepladder, traditionally credited to Franklin; attribution is widespread, but exact dating and the relationship of surviving examples to his hand are uncertain. [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/benjamin-franklin-nations-first-newsman-180984281/)

- **Eighteenth century, date uncertain:** *Rocking chair with fan*. A combined chair-and-fan mechanism traditionally attributed to Franklin; it survives chiefly as a credited design rather than a securely documented, uniquely dated original. [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/benjamin-franklin-nations-first-newsman-180984281/)

- **Eighteenth century, date uncertain:** *Mechanical long arm*. A reaching device for taking books or other objects from high shelves; Franklin later described such an instrument, but surviving forms should not automatically be treated as objects made by his own hands. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/) [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/benjamin-franklin-nations-first-newsman-180984281/)

- **c. 1784–1785:** *Double spectacles*. Eyeglasses now called bifocals, combining portions of near- and distance-vision lenses; Franklin documented his use and design, but priority has been disputed and surviving examples are generally later objects based on the design. [Smithsonian](https://invention.si.edu/invention-stories/benjamin-franklins-inventions) [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/ben-franklin-lives-in-your-smartphone-180979317/)

### Life-writing

- **1771–1790:** *Autobiography*. An untitled, intermittently composed manuscript in four parts, addressed initially to Franklin’s son and left unfinished, ending decades before Franklin’s death. “The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin” is an editorial title, and its posthumous publications represent editions of the manuscript rather than new works by Franklin. [LOA II](https://www.loa.org/books/233-autobiography-poor-richard-amp-later-writings/) [PBF](https://franklinpapers.org/info_date.jsp)
