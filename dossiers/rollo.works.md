## Works by Rollo

### Political obra

- **c. 911:** The Rouen-based Norman principality. A collective political creation made by Rollo, his followers, Charles the Simple, and the existing Frankish population—not a solitary “foundation” in the modern sense. Its conventional date derives from Dudo of Saint-Quentin; no contemporary treaty survives, while a royal charter of 918 confirms an earlier grant to “Rollo and his companions” for the kingdom’s protection, and the original territory and terms remain disputed ([Sanmark](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-church-history/article/locations-of-religious-encounter-the-scandinavian-diaspora-in-the-viking-age/BAFC0B02570D26FBCEDCBD4DD8543B3A); [Hicks](https://www.perlego.com/book/916218/a-short-history-of-the-normans-pdf); [Hagger](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/norman-rule-in-normandy-9111144/8235B7C17991047F308DBC4350A89A26/listing)).

- **traditionally 911:** The settlement commonly called the Treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte. This was an enacted political protocol—land and recognition in exchange for fidelity, conversion, and defense—rather than a surviving authored text. The place, date, ceremonies, and precise obligations come principally from Dudo, writing about a century later, so the familiar “treaty” resists treatment as a securely recoverable document or a work by Rollo alone ([Sanmark](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-church-history/article/locations-of-religious-encounter-the-scandinavian-diaspora-in-the-viking-age/BAFC0B02570D26FBCEDCBD4DD8543B3A); [Hagger](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/norman-rule-in-normandy-9111144/8235B7C17991047F308DBC4350A89A26/listing); [Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp)).

- **c. 911–c. 928/933:** A territorial and military regime in the lower Seine. Rollo and his associates converted a precarious royal grant and Scandinavian occupation into a transmissible rulership centered on Rouen; calling it the “Duchy of Normandy” at this stage is retrospective, since Rollo’s contemporary title and the principality’s boundaries are uncertain ([Hicks](https://www.perlego.com/book/916218/a-short-history-of-the-normans-pdf); [Hagger](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/24844); [Lifshitz, ORB](https://the-orb.arlima.net/encyclop/high/normandy/normhist/n10th2-3.html)).

- **c. 911–c. 928/933:** Landholding arrangements for Rollo’s companions and followers. Dudo describes Rollo distributing land after his baptism, but no individual act issued by Rollo survives; modern reconstruction treats these arrangements as a collective, mostly oral political project rather than a catalogue of securely identifiable grants ([Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp); [*The Estates of the Norman Dukes*](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A9ae937a7-29b5-4847-a8b0-f0e2eaef60cf/files/r1z40kt888)).

### Attributed ecclesiastical endowments

- **traditionally 912:** A grant to the cathedral church of Saint Mary at Rouen. Dudo presents it as the first of seven daily baptismal gifts; the act is an attributed endowment, not a surviving charter, and its details cannot be independently verified ([Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp); [Cross](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1417574/1/Cross%20phd%20thesis%20final.pdf)).

- **traditionally 912:** A grant to the cathedral church of Saint Mary at Bayeux. This is the second gift in Dudo’s programmatic sequence; its form, property, and date are not preserved in a contemporary instrument ([Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp); [Cross](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1417574/1/Cross%20phd%20thesis%20final.pdf)).

- **traditionally 912:** A grant to the cathedral church of Saint Mary at Évreux. Dudo alone supplies its place in the seven-day sequence, so it should be catalogued as an attributed benefaction rather than an extant documentary work ([Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp); [Cross](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1417574/1/Cross%20phd%20thesis%20final.pdf)).

- **traditionally 912:** A grant to Mont-Saint-Michel. Dudo attributes the fourth baptismal gift to the monastery, but modern territorial research judges Mont-Saint-Michel unlikely to have been under Rollo’s influence; the attribution is therefore especially doubtful ([Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp); [*The Estates of the Norman Dukes*](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A9ae937a7-29b5-4847-a8b0-f0e2eaef60cf/files/r1z40kt888)).

- **traditionally 912:** A grant to Saint-Ouen at Rouen. Dudo identifies this as the fifth baptismal gift, but no act by Rollo survives to establish its contents or confirm the narrative’s date ([Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp); [Cross](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1417574/1/Cross%20phd%20thesis%20final.pdf)).

- **traditionally 912:** A grant to Jumièges. Dudo assigns the sixth gift to Saints Peter and Aichard at Jumièges, but the monastery probably remained deserted until its refoundation under Rollo’s son William Longsword; the supposed Rollo endowment is thus disputed and was not the physical rebuilding of the abbey ([Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp); [*The Estates of the Norman Dukes*](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A9ae937a7-29b5-4847-a8b0-f0e2eaef60cf/files/r1z40kt888)).

- **traditionally 912:** A grant of Berneval and its appurtenances to Saint-Denis. This seventh gift is known from Dudo’s retrospective narrative and functions there as a manifestation of Rollo’s new Christian legitimacy; no corresponding original charter survives ([Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp); [Cross](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1417574/1/Cross%20phd%20thesis%20final.pdf)).

### Attributed restorations and infrastructure

- **after c. 911:** Repairs to churches, urban walls, and fortifications in Rollo’s territory. Later Norman historiography credits him generally with rebuilding ruined churches and strengthening cities, but it names no securely identifiable structures and supplies no contemporary building record; this is an attributed program of rulership, not a catalogue of surviving architectural works ([Dudo tradition summarized by Fordham](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/843bertin.asp); [Cross](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1417574/1/Cross%20phd%20thesis%20final.pdf)).

### Law and public order

- **after c. 911:** The so-called Laws of Rollo. No law code, protocol, or text authored or promulgated by Rollo survives. Dudo portrays him as establishing severe public order, while the idea of a coherent foundational “constitution” was substantially fashioned by much later jurists; it therefore resists classification as an actual work by Rollo ([Davy](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/sports-and-leisure/d34a795b-2341-583b-88eb-5242ebb94f97/the-laws-of-rollo-as-a-primitive-constitution-for-normandy-writing-and-rewriting-legal-history-in-france-during-the-seventeenth-and-eighteenth-centuries); [Dudo, trans. Lifshitz](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dudu-stquentin-gesta-trans-lifshitz.asp)).

### Dynastic creation

- **early tenth century onward:** The Rollonid or Norman ruling house. Rollo’s descendants became a durable dynasty, but a family is a biological, marital, and political creation involving Poppa and later generations, not an authored object by Rollo alone; treating it as his “work” reproduces the legitimizing genealogy constructed by later Norman writers ([Henry Project](https://fasg.org/projects/henryproject/data/rollo000.htm); [Pohl](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7722/j.ctt9qdm20); [Lifshitz](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304418194007543)).

### No surviving authored text

- No securely attributable charter, treaty-text, law code, inscription, literary composition, or other authored text by Rollo is known to survive. The recoverable corpus consists instead of a political formation and actions attributed chiefly by Dudo of Saint-Quentin, whose court history was written roughly a century later to memorialize and legitimize Rollo’s descendants ([Sanmark](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-church-history/article/locations-of-religious-encounter-the-scandinavian-diaspora-in-the-viking-age/BAFC0B02570D26FBCEDCBD4DD8543B3A); [Pohl](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7722/j.ctt9qdm20); [Lifshitz](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304418194007543)).
