## Works by Laurence Douglas "Larry" Fink

### Institutional projects and financial systems

- **1988–:** *BlackRock*. An asset-management firm co-founded by Fink with seven partners; it is a collective institutional project rather than a solely authored obra. BlackRock’s own history dates its founding to 1988 and names all eight founders, so attributing the company to Fink alone would be misleading. ([BlackRock history](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/about-us/who-we-are/history))

- **Late 1980s–:** *Aladdin*. BlackRock’s evolving portfolio- and risk-management technology platform, begun as an internal system around the firm’s founding and later made the basis of BlackRock Solutions. It is a collective, continuously revised technical system—not a fixed text or an individual invention securely attributable to Fink—although he helped establish the risk-management project from which it emerged. ([BlackRock history](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/about-us/who-we-are/history), [BlackRock corporate history](https://careers.blackrock.com/who-we-are), [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364742/000119312517116538/d339979ddefa14a.htm))

### Scholarly and policy texts

- **1996:** *The Role of Pension Funds and Other Investors in Securitized Debt Markets*. A chapter by Laurence D. Fink in *The Future of the Financial System*, edited by Malcolm Edey; it is a policy and market-analysis text, not a book by Fink. ([MIT Press chapter PDF](https://direct.mit.edu/books/edited-volume/chapter-pdf/2296677/9780262276986_caj.pdf))

- **2018:** *Trends in Global Asset Management: The Rise of Index Investing*. A co-authored policy article by Fink and Barbara G. Novick in the Banque de France’s *Financial Stability Review*, no. 22. Its authorship is explicitly shared, and the object is an analytical article rather than a manifesto or company letter. ([Banque de France issue](https://publications.banque-france.fr/sites/default/files/medias/documents/financial_stability_review_22.pdf), [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bfr/fisrev/2018225.html))

- **2025:** *Larry Fink and Rob Goldstein on How Tokenisation Could Transform Finance*. A jointly authored invited essay for *The Economist* arguing that tokenization can connect traditional and digital markets; it is an advocacy essay, approaching a manifesto, but does not prescribe a formal protocol. The dating follows the original reprint, notwithstanding BlackRock pages that continued promoting it in 2026. ([authorized article reprint](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/literature/article-reprint/larry-fink-rob-goldstein-economist-op-ed-tokenization.pdf))

### Annual letters to chief executives

- **2012:** *Larry Fink’s 2012 Letter to CEOs*. The first item in BlackRock’s annual “Dear CEO” sequence: an open corporate-governance intervention addressed to leaders of companies in which BlackRock invested. It is more than commentary—an institutional manifesto backed by BlackRock’s shareholder power—although it is not a binding protocol. ([scholarly history and bibliography of the series](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2046147X231224830), [contemporary-series analysis](https://www.eco-business.com/news/esg-through-the-eyes-of-the-worlds-largest-money-manager/))

- **2014:** *Larry Fink’s 2014 Letter to CEOs*. An open governance letter in the same interventionist series; BlackRock’s surviving archive lists no 2013 counterpart, so no 2013 work is supplied merely to complete the annual pattern. ([BlackRock letter archive as preserved in the 2019 PDF](https://aips.online/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Larry-Fink-letter-to-CEOs-2019-BlackRock.pdf), [GlobeScan series analysis](https://globescan.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/GlobeScan_Analysis_Larry_Fink_Annual_Letters_to_CEOs_Jan2020.pdf))

- **2015:** *Larry Fink’s 2015 Letter to CEOs*. An open letter urging corporate leaders to resist short-termism and articulate long-term strategy; it functions as a governance manifesto rather than a neutral shareholder communication. ([Cambridge bibliography identifying the BlackRock original](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn%3Acambridge.org%3Aid%3Aarticle%3AS1469356924000211/resource/name/S1469356924000211sup001.pdf), [GlobeScan series analysis](https://globescan.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/GlobeScan_Analysis_Larry_Fink_Annual_Letters_to_CEOs_Jan2020.pdf))

- **2016:** *Larry Fink’s 2016 Letter to CEOs*. An open letter centered on long-term value creation and strategic reporting. Its addressees and BlackRock’s voting position give the text performative force, but it remains persuasion rather than a formally enforceable protocol. ([preserved BlackRock PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/63c02a64bd85a17c688a16d9/t/63d56ae9784862156906a29e/1674930921399/Larry%2BFink%27s%2B2016%2BLetter%2Bto%2BCEOs.pdf), [scholarly bibliography](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-021-09603-x))

- **2017:** *Larry Fink’s 2017 Letter to CEOs*. An annual open letter on long-term corporate strategy and the relationship between companies and their investors. It belongs to a serial institutional project and resists treatment as an autonomous literary work. ([scholarly bibliography](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-021-09603-x), [BlackRock archive reproduced with the 2019 letter](https://aips.online/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Larry-Fink-letter-to-CEOs-2019-BlackRock.pdf))

- **2018:** *A Sense of Purpose*. An open letter asserting that companies must combine financial performance with a positive social contribution. Because it seeks to change corporate conduct through the leverage of a major shareholder, it is properly described as a corporate-governance manifesto. ([BlackRock text preserved by NYU Stern](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/Index_Entry_Columbia.pdf), [BlackRock archive citation](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/literature/whitepaper/blackrock-the-debate-on-differentiated-voting-rights.pdf))

- **2019:** *Purpose & Profit*. An open letter arguing that corporate purpose and long-term profitability are interdependent. It is simultaneously a public argument and an instrument of investment stewardship, rather than merely a personal essay. ([preserved BlackRock letter](https://aips.online/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Larry-Fink-letter-to-CEOs-2019-BlackRock.pdf))

- **2020:** *A Fundamental Reshaping of Finance*. An open letter announcing that climate risk is investment risk and presenting sustainability as a central consideration in capital allocation. Its combination of declaration and promised institutional action makes it a manifesto with protocol-like consequences, though the text itself is not a regulation. ([BlackRock documentation identifying the letter](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/literature/publication/our-response-to-the-securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-consultation-on-business-responsibility-and-sustainability-reporting-formats.pdf), [BlackRock stewardship commentary](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/literature/whitepaper/viewpoint-asset-managers-of-scale-give-voice-to-investors-and-support-the-economy-december-2020.pdf))

- **2021:** *Larry Fink’s 2021 Letter to CEOs*. An open letter joining climate-transition demands to corporate purpose and long-term value. It continues the manifesto-like annual project, while its practical effects depended on BlackRock’s separate investment and voting decisions. ([Harvard Law School Forum publication](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/01/30/letter-to-ceos/))

- **2022:** *The Power of Capitalism*. An open letter defending stakeholder capitalism and addressing changes in work, climate transition, and corporate purpose. BlackRock labels it Fink’s annual letter, but its institutional production and intended market effects make uncomplicated single-author “work” status debatable. ([archived BlackRock publication](https://archive.li/YeNSw), [contemporary report](https://www.axios.com/2022/01/18/larry-fink-to-ceos-treat-your-workers-well))

### Combined annual letters to investors

- **2023:** *Larry Fink’s 2023 Annual Chairman’s Letter to Investors*. The eleventh text in the annual sequence, but a changed object: Fink combined what had previously been separate communications to CEOs and BlackRock shareholders. It is therefore both corporate report and public intervention, and its title is descriptive rather than a securely established independent title. ([Harvard Law School Forum account of the merger](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2023/03/18/larry-finks-annual-letters-to-investors/), [GlobeScan series analysis](https://globescan.com/2023/03/30/analysis-larry-finks-annual-letter-ceos-2023/))

- **2024:** *Larry Fink’s 2024 Annual Chairman’s Letter to Investors*. A combined shareholder report and extended argument about capital markets, retirement, infrastructure, energy, and BlackRock’s business. It resists a single category: part annual corporate accounting, part policy essay, part institutional manifesto. ([BlackRock text](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/investor-relations/2024-larry-fink-chairmans-letter))

- **2025:** *Larry Fink’s 2025 Annual Chairman’s Letter to Investors*. A combined corporate report and essay organized around the expansion and democratization of capital markets. Although signed by Fink, it incorporates company performance, institutional research, and credited historical assistance, so “sole-authored personal work” would overstate the available attribution. ([BlackRock text](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/investor-relations/2025-larry-fink-annual-chairmans-letter))

- **2026:** *Growing with Your Country: Thoughts from a Long-Term Optimist*. A combined annual report, policy argument, and public letter advocating broader participation in long-term investment. Fink explicitly describes it as both part of his duty to shareholders and clients and as a letter intended to begin conversations; it is therefore an institutional manifestation with manifesto-like intent, not simply an essay. ([BlackRock text](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/investor-relations/larry-fink-annual-chairmans-letter), [official PDF](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/literature/presentation/larry-fink-annual-chairmans-letter.pdf))
