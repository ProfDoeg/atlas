## Works by Fidel Alejandro Castro Ruz

### Academic work

- **c. 1950:** *La letra de cambio en el derecho internacional*. A university law thesis attributed to Castro; it survives chiefly as a bibliographical reference rather than as an independently established published text, so its precise date and textual completeness remain uncertain. [[source](https://libreriatemis.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/17-000-0011.pdf)]

### Early articles, legal texts, manifestos, letters, interviews, and broadcasts

- **1947–1 January 1959:** *Documentary corpus of the revolutionary struggle*. This is an obra dispersed across newspapers, mimeographed sheets, correspondence, radio, interviews, legal filings, military orders, communiqués, and later transcriptions—not a single authored book. Bonachea and Valdés’s scholarly bibliography identifies 74 surviving items but explicitly warns that it is incomplete: many early speeches were never transcribed, while others appeared in newspapers the bibliographers could not consult. [[bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)] [[collected-works description](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262520270/revolutionary-struggle-19471958-volume-1/)]

- **1947:** *Declaraciones del Directorio Universitario*. A collective political declaration calling on Cuban youth to oppose President Ramón Grau San Martín’s reelection; Castro’s individual share cannot be separated securely from that of the student directorate. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **1948:** *Primeros pasos del movimiento latinoamericano contra el coloniaje europeo en este continente*. A position paper presented to the University of Havana’s student federation; its familiar title comes from its later publication in *Bohemia* in 1957. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **1949:** *¡Ciudadano, esconde tu kilo!*. A rally speech opposing increased bus fares, known through its newspaper publication rather than an authorial manuscript. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **December 1951:** *Carta a Amador García*. A political Christmas letter to an Ortodoxo senator, subsequently printed in *Bohemia*. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **13 March 1952:** *¡Revolución no, zarpazo!*. A mimeographed denunciation of Batista’s coup: an intervention that acts politically and therefore approaches a manifesto rather than an essay. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **24 March 1952:** *Al Tribunal de Urgencia*. A legal brief demanding prosecution of the participants in Batista’s coup; it is simultaneously a juridical instrument and a political act. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **16 August 1952:** *Yo acuso*. A mimeographed political indictment of the Batista government. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **16 August 1952:** *Recuento crítico del P.P.C.*. A polemical historical assessment of the Partido del Pueblo Cubano and its response to Batista, issued in the mimeographed paper *El Acusador*. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **1953:** *Asaltado y destruido el estudio del escultor Fidalgo*. Castro’s first identified article in the national magazine *Bohemia*, documenting police destruction of an opposition sculptor’s studio. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

### Manifestos, pleas, and political compacts

- **26 July 1953:** *Este movimiento triunfará*. A mobilizing speech addressed to the Moncada attackers; the surviving text was published eleven years later, making the exact fidelity of its wording difficult to establish. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **26 July 1953:** *Manifiesto a la nación* (also called *Manifiesto del Moncada*). A programmatic manifesto read by Castro before the Moncada operation but drafted by the poet and militant Raúl Gómez García; it therefore belongs to Castro’s performed political project, not securely to his authored texts.

- **16 October 1953:** *La historia me absolverá*. A courtroom self-defense transformed in prison into a clandestinely circulated manifesto and political program. No trial transcript survives, so the printed work cannot safely be treated as a verbatim record of the plea: its relation to the spoken defense is explicitly qualified as alleged in the standard bibliography. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **26 September 1953:** *Carta al Tribunal de Urgencia*. A prison letter protesting official efforts to prevent Castro from conducting his defense. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **8 August 1955:** *Manifiesto núm. 1 del 26 de Julio al pueblo de Cuba*. A manifesto issued from Mexico defining revolutionary objectives and attempting to produce organized political action rather than merely describe it. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **10 December 1955:** *Manifiesto núm. 2 del 26 de Julio al pueblo de Cuba*. A second manifesto reporting Castro’s work among Cuban exiles and defining the character of the July 26 Movement. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **August 1956:** *Pacto de México* (also *Carta de México*). A political compact jointly signed by Castro and José Antonio Echeverría for the July 26 Movement and the Directorio Revolucionario; it is a negotiated collective protocol, not Castro’s sole-authored work. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **12 July 1957:** *Al pueblo de Cuba* (commonly *Manifiesto de la Sierra Maestra*). A collective manifesto by Castro, Raúl Chibás, and Felipe Pazos proposing opposition unity and a post-Batista political settlement; sole attribution to Castro erases its negotiated authorship. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **14 December 1957:** *Carta a la Junta Cubana de Liberación*. A long political letter rejecting agreements made by opposition groups in exile; in its public function it operates like a counter-manifesto. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

### Military protocols, proclamations, and communiqués

- **1956–1958:** *Rebel correspondence, orders, battlefield communiqués, and Radio Rebelde broadcasts*. This dispersed operational corpus includes instructions to Celestino Rodríguez, Ernesto Guevara, Camilo Cienfuegos, Juan Almeida, José Quevedo, and other commanders, as well as accounts of Uvero, Jigüe, El Cerro, Imías, Guisa, and Baire. These texts resist classification as literature: some are protocols that directly moved troops, conferred rank, ordered ambushes, or announced military and political facts. [[enumerated bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)] [[collected edition](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262520270/revolutionary-struggle-19471958-volume-1/)]

- **15 September 1958:** *Proclama a los soldados de Batista*. A proclamation addressed to the opposing army and intended to alter its conduct. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **31 December 1958:** *Proclama al pueblo de Santiago*. A proclamation announcing the encirclement of Santiago de Cuba. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **Night of 31 December 1958–1 January 1959:** *Orders to the rebel commands in Camagüey and Las Villas*. Military protocols directing the columns to continue fighting rather than accept a political-military substitution for Batista. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

- **1 January 1959:** *Order for the advance on Santiago and Havana and proclamation of the general strike*. A hybrid military order and performative political text intended simultaneously to move forces, call civilians into action, and prevent a successor junta from consolidating power. [[source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/making-of-a-revolutionary-a-fidel-castro-bibliography-19471958/A239243B276BD609D291C827D6FF96C1)]

### Governmental oratory and declarations

- **1959–2006:** *Speeches, appearances, interviews, press conferences, statements, and official interventions*. This enormous oral-textual obra is Castro’s principal made body of work, but it has no universally accepted definitive boundary: spoken events, broadcasts, official transcripts, later corrections, excerpts, translations, and editorially titled pamphlets are not identical objects. The documentary databases and selected editions are therefore finding aids rather than a completed catalogue raisonné. [[UCLA archive description](https://guides.library.ucla.edu/latin-america/cuba)] [[selected-works description](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262520270/revolutionary-struggle-19471958-volume-1/)]

- **2 September 1960:** *Primera Declaración de La Habana*. A mass political declaration adopted by the assembled crowd after Castro presented it; it should not be reduced to a conventional sole-authored essay because its claimed authority rests on collective acclamation.

- **30 June 1961:** *Palabras a los intelectuales*. A spoken policy intervention produced through meetings between the revolutionary government and Cuban writers and artists. Its status lies between speech, cultural-policy manifesto, and an exercise of governmental power.

- **4 February 1962:** *Segunda Declaración de La Habana*. A revolutionary manifesto read by Castro and approved by mass acclamation; it acts in the name of an assembled political collectivity, so “by Castro” describes its principal voice but not uncomplicated private authorship.

### Book-length conversations and spoken collaborative works

- **1985:** *Fidel y la religión: conversaciones con Frei Betto*. A book-length dialogue with Frei Betto about religion, Christianity, Marxism, and revolution; it is a jointly produced oral work subsequently shaped as a book, not a conventional manuscript authored by Castro alone. [[publication description](https://www.contextolatinoamericano.com/files/Contexto%20Latinoamericano_07.pdf)]

- **1985:** *Nada podrá detener la marcha de la historia*. An extended interview granted to Jeffrey M. Elliot and Mervyn M. Dymally on Cuban, international, economic, and historical questions; the questioners and editors participate in the object’s form. [[record](https://www.elib.gov.ph/results.php?f=author&q=Castro%2C+Fidel.)]

- **1987:** *Un encuentro con Fidel* / *An Encounter with Fidel*. A filmed and transcribed interview with Gianni Minà; it is simultaneously conversation, audiovisual performance, and edited book. [[record](https://books.google.com/books/about/An_Encounter_with_Fidel.html?id=t5NqAAAAMAAJ)]

- **1992:** *Un grano de maíz*. A book-length interview with Tomás Borge. Its dialogic construction makes Castro a speaker and co-maker rather than a conventional sole author.

- **2006:** *Cien horas con Fidel*; subsequently issued in English as *My Life: A Spoken Autobiography*. Ignacio Ramonet constructed the book from more than one hundred hours of interviews conducted across several years; it is therefore a collaborative spoken autobiography, not an independently written memoir, and the editions are not necessarily textually identical. [[source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Life%3A_A_Spoken_Autobiography)]

### Late authored books

- **2010:** *La victoria estratégica*. An authored historical-military reconstruction of the defeat of Batista’s 1958 summer offensive, incorporating Castro’s narration with letters, battlefield reports, testimony, maps, photographs, and documents preserved by Celia Sánchez. Its composite documentary form makes it more than a continuous memoir. [[publisher](https://www.oceansur.com/catalogo/titulos/la-victoria-estrategica)] [[publication record](https://books.google.com/books/about/La_victoria_estrat%C3%A9gica_La_contraofensi.html?id=BjZW4ytzex8C)]

- **2010–2011:** *La contraofensiva estratégica: De la Sierra Maestra a Santiago de Cuba*. A companion historical-military reconstruction following the rebel advance after the summer campaign; the 2011 Ocean Sur version was revised and augmented by Castro, so its first and revised states should not be silently conflated. [[publisher](https://www.oceansur.com/catalogo/titulos/la-contraofensiva-estrategica)] [[combined-edition record](https://books.google.com/books/about/La_victoria_estrat%C3%A9gica_La_contraofensi.html?id=BjZW4ytzex8C)]

### Serial obra

- **2007–2016:** *Reflexiones del Comandante en Jefe* / *Reflexiones del compañero Fidel*. A serial body of signed newspaper and online columns begun after Castro’s illness and withdrawal from daily government. The individual pieces address politics, war, science, ecology, sport, health, and current events; bound volumes titled *Reflexiones* are editorial containers for this evolving corpus, not separate works each time the same columns are regrouped or translated. [[selected bibliography](https://www.centrocultural.coop/blogs/utopia/2017/07/07/bibliografia-seleccionada-fidel-castro-1926-2016)]

### Projects and collective constructions that resist individual authorship

- **1953–1959:** *The revolutionary seizure of power*. Castro planned, organized, commanded, narrated, and symbolically authored this political-military project, but it is not defensibly a personal “work”: thousands of named and unnamed participants made it, while contingency and opposition also determined its form.

- **1959–2008:** *The Cuban revolutionary state under Castro*. This can be treated as a political Gesamtkunstwerk—institutions, laws, campaigns, rituals, speeches, images, education, medicine, coercive structures, and foreign policy were deliberately joined—but not as Castro’s sole creation. Calling it his obra records the exceptional concentration of leadership while risking the erasure of collective agency, institutional authorship, resistance, and victims.
