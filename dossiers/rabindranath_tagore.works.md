## Works by Rabindranath Tagore

### Poems and books of poems

- **1877:** *Banaphul*. An early narrative poem; its date and treatment as an independent work vary because it first appeared serially. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1878:** *Kabi-Kahini*. A narrative poem and Tagore’s first published book; the book, rather than every later revision of its text, is the obra named here. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1881:** *Bhagna Hriday*. A verse drama or dramatic poem, demonstrating the instability of any firm boundary between Tagore’s poetry and theatre. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1882:** *Sandhya Sangit*. A collection of “evening songs”: poems, though its title invokes music. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1883:** *Prabhat Sangit*. A companion collection of “morning songs.” [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1884:** *Chhabi O Gan*. A collection of poems and songs, deliberately crossing the boundary between spoken lyric and musical composition. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1884:** *Shaishab Sangit*. A collection of early poems later discontinued as a separate volume and consequently preserved in the *Achalita Sangraha*. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1884:** *Bhanusimha Thakurer Padabali*. Vaishnava-style lyrics written under the invented persona “Bhanusimha”; both literary masquerade and song-cycle. Its constituent poems began appearing earlier, so 1884 is the collection date, not a uniform composition date. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1886:** *Kari O Komal*. A poetry collection whose title is musical—“Sharps and Flats”—without making the whole volume a score. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1890:** *Manasi*. A collection of lyric, dramatic, satirical, and reflective poems rather than one continuous text. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1894:** *Sonar Tari*. A poetry collection, commonly rendered in English as *The Golden Boat*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1896:** *Chitra*. A poetry collection, not to be confused with Tagore’s later English play *Chitra* or the dance-drama version of *Chitrangada*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1896:** *Chaitali*. A collection of poems associated with the late-summer season. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1899:** *Kanika*. A collection of short, often epigrammatic poems. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1900:** *Kalpana*. A poetry collection. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1900:** *Katha*. A collection of narrative and legendary poems. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1900:** *Kahini*. A collection of narrative poems subsequently rearranged with related material. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1901:** *Naivedya*. A sequence of devotional poems or “offerings,” later one of the principal source-books for English *Gitanjali*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1903:** *Smaran*. A memorial sequence made after the death of Tagore’s wife; it first appeared in book form within the collected *Kavya-Grantha*. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1903:** *Shishu*. Poems of childhood, first issued in book form within *Kavya-Grantha* and later partly remade in English as *The Crescent Moon*. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1906:** *Kheya*. A poetry collection whose organizing image is a ferry or crossing. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1910:** *Gitanjali*. A Bengali collection of lyric poems and songs; it is not identical to the 1912 English *Gitanjali (Song Offerings)*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1914:** *Gitimalya*. A collection of songs and poems and one of the source-books Tagore mined for his English self-translations. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1914:** *Gitali*. A collection of song-lyrics and poems. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1914:** *Utsarga*. A collection of dedicatory poems; individual pieces had differing publication histories. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1916:** *Balaka*. A poetry collection organized around movement, flight, and modernity. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1925:** *Purabi*. A poetry collection closely associated with Tagore’s South American journey and Victoria Ocampo. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1926:** *Lekhan*. A collection of short poems and inscriptions, many originating as handwritten gifts; the inscriptions resist a clean distinction between verbal work, calligraphy, and social gesture. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1929:** *Mahua*. A poetry collection. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1932:** *Parishesh*. A poetry collection whose title signifies remainder or conclusion, though it did not conclude Tagore’s poetry. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1932:** *Punascha*. A collection central to Tagore’s experiments with Bengali prose-poetry. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1935:** *Shesh Saptak*. A collection of prose-poems and free verse. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1936:** *Patraput*. A collection of late prose-poems. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1936:** *Shyamali*. A collection of late poems; its title is also attached to the mud house Tagore helped conceive at Santiniketan, so “the work” exists in two media. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1937:** *Khapchhara*. A collection of comic, nonsensical, and irregular verse, frequently joined to drawings. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1937:** *Chhara O Chhabi*. A word-and-image book of rhymes and pictures: a small-scale Gesamtkunstwerk rather than an exclusively verbal collection. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1938:** *Prantik*. A collection of late poems shaped by Tagore’s near-fatal illness. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1939:** *Sejuti*. A poetry collection. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1940:** *Nabajatak*. A late poetry collection. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1940:** *Sanai*. A late poetry collection whose title names a wind instrument. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1941:** *Rogashajyay*. Poems made during Tagore’s final illness. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1941:** *Arogya*. A late collection reflecting on illness, recovery, and the body. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1941:** *Janmadine*. A final-period collection organized around the poet’s birthday. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1941, posthumous:** *Shesh Lekha*. Final poems assembled after Tagore’s death; the collection is editorially constituted and therefore not wholly a book he himself completed. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)

### Plays, musical dramas, dance-dramas, and festival works

- **1881:** *Valmiki-Pratibha*. A musical drama combining authored songs, borrowed or adapted melodies, acting, and spectacle; it is closer to a Gesamtkunstwerk than to a freestanding script. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1881:** *Rudrachanda*. A verse drama. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1882:** *Kal-Mrigaya*. A musical drama, subsequently absorbed in revised form into *Valmiki-Pratibha*. Its partial loss of independence is part of its status as a work. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1884:** *Prakritir Pratishodh*. A dramatic poem or verse play, commonly translated as *Nature’s Revenge*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1884:** *Nalini*. A prose drama. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1888:** *Mayar Khela*. A musical drama later radically remade as a dance-drama; the two manifestations should not be treated as one invariant text. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1889:** *Raja O Rani*. A verse drama later reworked as *Tapati*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1890:** *Visarjan*. A drama adapted from Tagore’s novel *Rajarshi*, not merely a stage edition of it. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1892:** *Chitrangada*. A lyrical verse drama based on a *Mahabharata* episode; Tagore later transformed it into a dance-drama. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1892:** *Goray Galad*. A social comedy later revised under the title *Shesh Raksha*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1893:** *Malini*. A verse drama, first published in book form in the 1896 *Kavya Granthavali*. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1908:** *Sharadotsav*. An autumn festival-play joining drama, ritual occasion, music, and season. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1908:** *Mukut*. A short play, also associated with writing for younger performers. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1909:** *Prayaschitta*. A drama later reworked as *Muktadhara* and, through another line of revision, *Paritran*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1910:** *Raja*. A symbolic drama remade by Tagore in English as *The King of the Dark Chamber*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1911:** *Achalayatan*. A drama about institutional rigidity, later shortened and revised as *Guru*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1912:** *Dakghar*. A prose play, self-translated and internationally performed as *The Post Office*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1912:** *Phalguni*. A spring festival-play combining songs, symbolic drama, and communal performance; its English manifestation is *The Cycle of Spring*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1917:** *Guru*. A shortened re-creation of *Achalayatan*, not simply an alternative title. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1922:** *Muktadhara*. A symbolic prose drama about machinery, water, and political power. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1923:** *Basanta*. A season-play or musical festival work. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1924:** *Raktakarabi*. A symbolic drama usually translated as *Red Oleanders*; manuscript and printed versions preserve substantial revision. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1925:** *Grihapravesh*. A short domestic drama. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1925:** *Shodhbodh*. A social comedy. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1926:** *Natir Puja*. A dance-inflected drama of religious and political resistance, later directed by Tagore as a film. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1926:** *Ritu-Utsav*. A composite book and performance-cycle comprising season-dramas and song-sequences, including *Shesh Barshan*, *Sharadotsav*, *Basanta*, *Sundar*, and *Phalguni*. It is an editorially assembled Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single homogeneous play. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1929:** *Tapati*. A substantial re-creation of *Raja O Rani*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1929:** *Shesh Raksha*. A revision of the early comedy *Goray Galad*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1930:** *Nabin*. A festival drama or song-sequence concerned with renewal. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1931:** *Shapmochan*. A dance-drama assembled from narration, song, mime, and dance rather than a conventional spoken script. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1933:** *Tasher Desh*. A musical dance-drama and political fantasy, usually translated as *The Land of Cards*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1933:** *Chandalika*. A dance-drama about caste, desire, and spiritual coercion, developed through more than one textual and performance form. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1934:** *Shrabangular*. A rainy-season musical performance work. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1936:** *Chitrangada*. The dance-drama remaking of the 1892 verse play: music, choreography, costume, and text are constitutive, so it should not be reduced to a new edition of the earlier drama. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1938:** *Chandalika*. A revised full dance-drama manifestation, distinct from the preceding dramatic treatment. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1939:** *Shyama*. A dance-drama developed from *Parishodh* and made from poetry, music, movement, costume, and stage image. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)

### Novels and novellas

- **1883:** *Bou-Thakuranir Hat*. A historical-social novel and Tagore’s first published novel. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1887:** *Rajarshi*. A historical novel later transformed into the drama *Visarjan*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1903:** *Chokher Bali*. A serialized social novel; book and serial forms are not textually identical. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1906:** *Noukadubi*. A novel of mistaken identity, marriage, and social obligation, later self-represented in English as *The Wreck*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1910:** *Gora*. A long political, social, and religious novel; its serialization preceded the book. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1916:** *Ghare-Baire*. A political and domestic novel, self-revised in English as *The Home and the World*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1929:** *Jogajog*. A novel of marriage, capital, and family power; its serial and book histories complicate any single fixed date. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1929:** *Shesher Kabita*. A self-reflexive novel about literary reputation and love, notwithstanding the title *The Last Poem*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1933:** *Dui Bon*. A short novel, usually translated as *Two Sisters*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1934:** *Malancha*. A short novel, commonly translated as *The Garden*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1934:** *Char Adhyay*. A political novel in four chapters about revolutionary nationalism. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)

### Short fiction, prose-poems, and composite narrative books

- **1877:** *Bhikharini*. Tagore’s first published short story; it is an individual periodical text rather than originally a book. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1877–1878, unfinished:** *Karuna*. An early serialized novel left incomplete; its unfinished project is the work, not a hypothetical completed novel. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1884:** *Ghater Katha*. A short story also circulated under an associated title; translation titles multiply the apparent works without creating new Bengali originals. [Bichitra bibliography guide](https://busybee.jdvu.ac.in/bibliography/bibliography_guide.php)
- **1891–1895 and later:** *Sadhana* stories. The major body of short fiction written for the periodical *Sadhana* includes such separately named works as *Postmaster*, *Kabuliwala*, *Kshudhita Pashan*, *Shasti*, and *Jibita O Mrita*; it is a productive phase, not a single authored book. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1891–1895:** *Galpa-Dashak*. An early ten-story collection; later collected-story arrangements superseded but did not erase this historical book-object. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1900:** *Galpaguchchha*. Tagore’s evolving collected short stories, issued in parts and repeatedly enlarged; the later Visva-Bharati form covers all his published stories, so it is a corpus and editorial construction as well as a book. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1918:** *Palataka*. A collection of narrative poems and verse tales whose genre falls between poetry and short fiction. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1922:** *Lipika*. Brief prose works variously called prose-poems, sketches, parables, or stories; their resistance to stable genre is essential to the book. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1940:** *Tin Sangi*. A collection of three late stories concerned with science, technology, and modern relationships. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **Posthumously collected:** *Galpaguchchha*’s uncollected and variant stories. No claim that every story formed an authorially fixed final corpus is possible: periodical versions, book revisions, retitlings, and posthumous arrangements survive. [Bichitra bibliography guide](https://busybee.jdvu.ac.in/bibliography/bibliography_guide.php)

### Songs and musical compositions

- **c. 1875–1941:** *Rabindra Sangit*. The body of approximately 2,200 songs for which Tagore made words, melodies, or both; variant counts reflect doubtful attributions, repeated texts, revised tunes, and differing rules about what constitutes one song. It is a body of work, not one book. [Gitabitan](https://gitabitan.io/infographics/)
- **1885:** *Rabi-Chhaya*. An early printed song collection; publication did not fix every melody, and some tunes were supplied or preserved by collaborators. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1905:** *Amar Sonar Bangla*. A patriotic song later adopted, in part, as Bangladesh’s national anthem; it is simultaneously lyric, musical composition, political manifestation, and later state symbol. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1911:** *Bharat Bhagya Bidhata*. A five-stanza song whose first stanza later became India’s national anthem under the title *Jana Gana Mana*; anthem and complete song are related but not identical objects. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1910s–1930s:** *Gitabitan*. Tagore’s own developing project to order his songs, eventually by thematic divisions; later editors completed and expanded the printed corpus, which includes most rather than demonstrably every song. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1931 onward:** *Swarabitan*. The notated song corpus initiated under Tagore and continued editorially after him; its sixty-four published volumes contain notation for only part of the song body, and notations often record collaborators’ transcriptions rather than autograph scores. [Visva-Bharati Granthana Vibhaga](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/home/gvvbpub/)
- **Various dates:** Individually titled and first-line-titled songs outside stable authorial collections. These resist treatment as separately titled “books”: Bengali song catalogues commonly identify them by opening line, while melodies, notators, dates, and even attribution may remain disputed. [Bichitra bibliography guide](https://busybee.jdvu.ac.in/bibliography/bibliography_guide.php)

### Essays, lectures, manifestos, and protocols

- **1883:** *Bibidha Prasanga*. A collection of miscellaneous critical and reflective prose. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1885:** *Alochana*. A collection of literary and social discussions. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1885:** *Rammohan Ray*. A biographical-critical pamphlet on the reformer; closer to a public intervention than a neutral biography. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1888:** *Samalochana*. A collection of literary criticism. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1891:** *Europe-Jatrir Diary*. A travel diary and observational prose work; its parts and later arrangements do not yield one wholly stable text. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1907:** *Prachin Sahitya*. Essays on classical Indian literature. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1907:** *Adhunik Sahitya*. Essays on modern literature. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1907:** *Lok-Sahitya*. Essays on folk and children’s literature, with collecting and classificatory dimensions. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1908:** *Raja Praja*. Political essays on ruler and subject. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1908:** *Swadesh*. Essays on country, society, and self-strengthening; several pieces function as political manifestos rather than detached descriptions. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1908:** *Samaj*. Essays proposing forms of social organization; at points this is a protocol for constructive work as much as a collection of reflections. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1908:** *Path O Patheya*. A political essay or address on ends and means. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1909:** *Dharma*. Essays and addresses on religion. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1909:** *Shabdatattva*. Studies in Bengali language and philology, later revised and enlarged under another title. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1911:** *Jivansmriti*. Autobiographical recollections, not a comprehensive chronological autobiography. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1912:** *Dharmashiksha*. Religious and educational discourses. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1917:** *Nationalism*. English lectures delivered abroad; a public political intervention whose force is closer to manifesto than descriptive treatise. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1917:** *Personality*. English lectures on art, consciousness, education, and the self. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1919:** *The Centre of Indian Culture*. An educational address and institutional manifesto. [Visva-Bharati English works](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/ENGLISH.html)
- **1922:** *Creative Unity*. English essays and lectures gathered into a philosophical book. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1924:** *Shikshar Milan*. An essay on the meeting of educational cultures. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1925:** *Talks in China*. Lectures delivered during Tagore’s 1924 Chinese journey and subsequently made into a book. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1928:** *Pather Sanchay*. Travel writing assembled from journeys and observations. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1929:** *Russiar Chithi*. Letters from the Soviet Union that function together as travel report and political argument. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1930:** *The Religion of Man*. The Hibbert Lectures remade as an English philosophical book. [Visva-Bharati English works](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/ENGLISH.html)
- **1931:** *Manusher Dharma*. Bengali lectures on human religion; related to but not merely a translation of *The Religion of Man*. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1931:** *Parichay*. Essays and addresses whose bibliographical boundaries vary across collected editions. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1934:** *Chhanda*. A technical study of Bengali metre. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1934:** *Bangla Bhasha-Parichay*. A study and teaching-text on the Bengali language. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1937:** *Visva-Parichay*. A popular-science book dedicated to Satyendranath Bose; it makes scientific cosmology into literary exposition. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1941:** *Sabhyatar Sankat*. Tagore’s final public address, commonly translated as *Crisis in Civilization*; it acts as a political and ethical testament rather than merely describing a crisis. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)

### Textbooks and educational protocols

- **1896:** *Sanskrita Shiksha*. A two-part Sanskrit textbook. Its protocol-like purpose—directing how language should be learned—is more important than treating it as literary prose. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1901:** *Bangla Kriyapader Talika*. A pedagogical list of Bengali verb forms, resisting classification as a literary work but plainly part of Tagore’s educational making. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1904:** *Ingraji Sopan*. An English-language primer or instructional protocol. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1908:** *Ingraji Shrutishiksha*. A textbook for learning English through listening and dictation. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1909:** *Ingraji Sahajshiksha*. An elementary English textbook. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1912:** *Pathasanchay*. A school reader or educational anthology, not a wholly new body of imaginative writing. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1930:** *Sahaj Path*. A Bengali primer integrating graded verbal instruction with image and rhythm; Nandalal Bose’s illustrations make the realized book collaborative, not Tagore’s work alone. [Visva-Bharati publications](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/home/gvvbpub/)

### Letters, diaries, and autobiographical formations

- **1879–1880:** *Yurop-Prabasir Patra*. Letters from Tagore’s first European stay; first periodical communications later became a shaped travel book. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1881:** *Yurop-Jatrir Diary*. A journey diary with an unstable history of serial and collected publication. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1887–1895:** *Chhinnapatra* correspondence. Letters chiefly written from the family estates and later selected and arranged as *Chhinnapatra*; the book is an editorial formation, not the complete correspondence. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1912:** *Chhinnapatra*. Tagore’s selected Bengali letter-book, later represented in English through *Glimpses of Bengal*. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1928–1930:** *Bhanusimher Patravali*. Letters written to Ranu Adhikari under the revived “Bhanusimha” persona; private correspondence made into a named literary sequence. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1926–1931:** *Pathe O Pather Prante*. Travel letters later collected as a named correspondence-book. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1938:** *Patradhara*. A composite letter collection comprising *Chhinnapatra*, *Bhanusimher Patravali*, and *Pathe O Pather Prante*; it is a later container, not another set of letters. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1940:** *Chhelebela*. Childhood reminiscences written late in life; memoir rather than contemporaneous diary. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **Throughout life; posthumously collected:** Tagore’s correspondence. Thousands of letters survive in recipient-defined and editorial collections; there is no single authorially closed “complete letters,” and Bichitra expressly excludes most letters from its otherwise comprehensive literary corpus. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)

### English remakings and self-translations

- **1912:** *Gitanjali (Song Offerings)*. An English sequence of 103 prose-poem translations selected from several Bengali books, only fifty-one from Bengali *Gitanjali*; it is a new composite obra, not the English edition of one Bengali book. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **1913:** *The Gardener*. English self-translations selected from numerous Bengali poems and songs and arranged as a new collection. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1913:** *The Crescent Moon*. English remakings of Bengali child-poems, principally from *Shishu*. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1913:** *Sadhana: The Realisation of Life*. English lectures and essays made into a philosophical book. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1913:** *Chitra*. Tagore’s English transformation of *Chitrangada*, not the 1896 Bengali poetry collection of the same romanized title. [Visva-Bharati English works](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/ENGLISH.html)
- **1914:** *The King of the Dark Chamber*. An English re-creation of *Raja*. [Visva-Bharati English works](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/ENGLISH.html)
- **1914:** *The Post Office*. Tagore’s English manifestation of *Dakghar*, subsequently altered in performance and publication. [Visva-Bharati English works](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/ENGLISH.html)
- **1916:** *Fruit-Gathering*. An English poetry collection assembled from several Bengali sources. [Visva-Bharati English works](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/ENGLISH.html)
- **1917:** *The Cycle of Spring*. An English performance-text derived from *Phalguni*, translated with assistance from C. F. Andrews and Nishikanta Sen. Its collaborative translation complicates sole authorship. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1918:** *Lover’s Gift and Crossing*. Two English poetic sequences published together, assembled through self-translation. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1918:** *The Parrot’s Training*. An English satirical fable, issued with illustrations by Abanindranath Tagore; the book-object is collaborative. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1919:** *The Home and the World*. English version of *Ghare-Baire*, translated by Surendranath Tagore and revised by Rabindranath; sole authorship of the English wording should therefore not be assumed. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1921:** *The Fugitive*. English poems and plays gathered from several Bengali sources. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1921:** *The Wreck*. English version of *Noukadubi*; its translated and revised form is a manifestation rather than a second original novel. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1924:** *Gora*. The English manifestation of the Bengali novel, produced through translation and Tagore’s revision rather than by unmediated self-translation alone. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1924:** *The Curse at Farewell*. Edward Thompson’s translation of *Karna-Kunti Sambad*; Tagore made the Bengali dramatic poem, but not every word of this English object. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1925:** *Red Oleanders*. The English manifestation of *Raktakarabi*, substantially connected to Tagore’s own revision but not safely reducible to a transparent duplicate of the Bengali play. [Nobel bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1913/tagore/bibliography/)
- **1928:** *Fireflies*. A collection of aphoristic English miniatures, many derived from Bengali *Lekhan* inscriptions; simultaneously poems, calligraphic gifts, and acts of translation. [Visva-Bharati English works](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/ENGLISH.html)
- **1935:** *The Golden Boat*. An English poetry selection rather than a complete translation of the Bengali *Sonar Tari*. [Visva-Bharati English works](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/ENGLISH.html)

### Images, calligraphy, and book-image works

- **c. 1924–1941:** Untitled manuscript doodles and erasure-forms. Shapes grew from crossings-out in manuscripts into autonomous creatures and designs; they resist being called illustrations because they often precede or exceed any represented text. [Visva-Bharati, “Tagore’s Paintings”](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/)
- **c. 1928–1941:** Paintings and drawings. A corpus of more than two thousand works—many untitled and many undated—comprising faces, figures, imaginary creatures, landscapes, flowers, and abstracted forms. The corpus, catalogued in *Rabindra Chitravali*, cannot honestly be converted into invented individual titles or exact dates. [Rabindra Chitravali catalogue description](https://ngmaindia.gov.in/)
- **1930:** Paris exhibition of paintings. Tagore’s first major public exhibition of visual work was a curated manifestation rather than a single object; exhibition selection and display helped produce “Tagore the painter” as a public obra. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/rabindranath-tagore-poet-and-painter)
- **1930–1931:** International painting exhibitions. Groups of works shown across Europe, Britain, the Soviet Union, and the United States changed with venue, so no one checklist should be mistaken for a fixed touring work. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/rabindranath-tagore-poet-and-painter)
- **1937:** *Chhara O Chhabi*. A collaborative field of written rhyme, handwriting, page design, and Tagore’s images; the separable “text” is only one component of the made thing. [Bichitra](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)
- **Various dates:** Painted and calligraphic manuscripts, letters, and inscriptions. Many are simultaneously writing-support, visual composition, and gift; they resist classification as either textual witness or autonomous artwork. [Bichitra manuscript collection](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/)

### Film and recorded performance

- **1932:** *Natir Puja*. A Bengali film directed by Tagore from his own stage work; only a fragment is known to survive, so the surviving object is not the complete film he made. [National Film Archive of India](https://nfai.nfdcindia.com/)
- **1930s:** Recorded recitations and songs in Tagore’s voice. These gramophone and film recordings are performances—new sonic manifestations—not simply copies of printed poems or notation; catalogues differ on takes, dates, and surviving issues. [British Library Sounds](https://sounds.bl.uk/)

### Institutions, environments, and sustained social projects

- **1901 onward:** *Brahmacharyashrama/Patha Bhavana at Santiniketan*. An experimental school conceived by Tagore: simultaneously institution, pedagogical protocol, built environment, and unfinished social project rather than a “text.” [Visva-Bharati history](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/History.html)
- **1918–1921 onward:** *Visva-Bharati*. An international university project founded and shaped by Tagore; its legal institution, curriculum, ceremonies, buildings, and ideal of intercultural meeting together form the obra, though countless collaborators made its physical manifestations. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/)
- **1922 onward:** *Sriniketan*. A project of rural reconstruction—agriculture, health, crafts, cooperative organization, and education—initiated by Tagore with Leonard Elmhirst and many others. Its collaborative and continuing nature resists the language of a solely authored work. [Visva-Bharati, Sriniketan](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/Sriniketan.html)
- **1922 onward:** *Silpa Bhavana/Silpa-Sadan*. A rural craft and vocational project within Sriniketan; a protocol for making and economic life as much as an institution. [Visva-Bharati, Sriniketan](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/Sriniketan.html)
- **1924 onward:** *Shiksha-Satra*. A school for rural children established within the Sriniketan project; its educational practice, not a single founding document, is the made thing. [Visva-Bharati, Sriniketan](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/Sriniketan.html)
- **1920s–1930s:** Santiniketan seasonal festivals. *Vasanta Utsav*, *Varsha Mangal*, *Vriksharopana*, *Halakarshana*, and related observances combined songs, poems, dance, planting, costume, procession, and place. Their repeated, variable realization makes them protocols and manifestations rather than fixed works. [Visva-Bharati festivals](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/)
- **1935–1936:** *Shyamali*. Tagore’s experimental mud dwelling at Uttarayana, made with Surendranath Kar, Nandalal Bose, and craftspeople; authorship is collaborative, and the building shares its name with a poetry book. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/)
- **Through 1941:** Uttarayana house-and-garden ensemble. Tagore participated in conceiving spaces including *Konark*, *Udayan*, *Shyamali*, *Punascha*, and *Udichi*, but architects, artists, builders, and patrons materially co-authored them; attributing the buildings to Tagore alone would be false. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/)

### Collected and editorial obras

- **1896:** *Kavya Granthavali*. Tagore’s first major collected poetical edition, including poems, songs, verse dramas, translations, and the first book appearances of *Malini* and *Chaitali*. It is an authorial reordering of prior works, not merely a neutral container. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1903–1904:** *Kavya-Grantha*, nine volumes. Edited by Mohit Chandra Sen with Tagore’s involvement, this rearranged poems by newly imposed categories and first issued *Smaran* and *Shishu* in book form; its authorship is partly editorial. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1904:** *Rabindra Granthavali*. A collected volume of stories, novels, plays, essays, and songs, notably excluding poems; its category-making is itself bibliographical information. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1907–1909:** *Gadya-Granthavali*, sixteen volumes. A collected prose edition containing essays, dramatic sketches, comedies, and a novel; “prose” here is an editorial container rather than a genre. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1915–1916:** *Kavyagrantha*, ten volumes. A collected “poetical” edition that also contains songs, verse drama, and some prose drama, demonstrating that Tagore’s own collected forms resisted later genre boundaries. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1931–1932:** *Gitabitan*, first arrangement. A collected song-text project initially organized chronologically or by prior book, later reorganized thematically; there is no single timeless *Gitabitan*. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1939–1965:** *Rabindra-Rachanavali*, twenty-seven volumes. The standard Visva-Bharati collected works began during Tagore’s lifetime but was completed after his death; it includes most lifetime book publications and some posthumous material while excluding most letters and many songs. It is therefore authoritative but not literally everything. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **1940–1941:** *Rabindra-Rachanavali: Achalita Sangraha*, two volumes. Companion volumes preserving discontinued early books and textbooks; they document works Tagore had allowed to fall out of circulation rather than proving his final endorsement of every text. [Visva-Bharati](https://www.visvabharati.ac.in/COLLECTEDWORKS.html)
- **Posthumous:** *Rabindra Chitravali*. The catalogue raisonné of Tagore’s paintings held in major Indian collections; it constructs a numbered visual corpus from works that are frequently untitled or undated and whose attribution and dating cannot always be made exact. [National Gallery of Modern Art](https://ngmaindia.gov.in/)
