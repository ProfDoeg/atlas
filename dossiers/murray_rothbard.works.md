## Works by Murray Newton Rothbard

### Treatises and monographs published during his lifetime

- **1962:** *Man, Economy, and State: A Treatise on Economic Principles*. A two-volume treatise in Austrian economics, conceived as a systematic reconstruction and extension of Ludwig von Mises’s economics; material removed from the original manuscript later became *Power and Market*.
- **1962:** *The Panic of 1819: Reactions and Policies*. A revised manifestation of Rothbard’s 1956 Columbia University doctoral dissertation, presenting an economic and political history of the first major depression in the United States.
- **1962–1963:** *Money, Free and Unfree / Moneda, libre y controlada / What Has Government Done to Our Money?* One monetary essay passing through a privately circulated manuscript, a Spanish publication, and an English booklet; the different titles are manifestations of substantially the same work rather than three independent obras.
- **1963:** *America’s Great Depression*. A historical-economic monograph applying Austrian business-cycle theory to the 1929 crash and the Hoover administration; later editions added new prefatory matter but are not separate works.
- **1969:** *Economic Depressions: Their Cause and Cure*. A popular explanatory lecture-text or pamphlet rather than a full treatise; its precise status shifts among recorded lecture, booklet, and subsequently reprinted essay.
- **1970:** *Power and Market: Government and the Economy*. A treatise on intervention, taxation, and the state, made from material originally intended for *Man, Economy, and State* but separated by its publisher; the 2004 “Scholar’s Edition” restored the two works to Rothbard’s intended architecture.
- **1972:** *Education: Free and Compulsory*. A historical and polemical monograph written earlier for the William Volker Fund and eventually issued as a book; its composition and publication dates therefore differ.
- **1973:** *For a New Liberty: The Libertarian Manifesto*. Explicitly a manifesto: a programmatic text meant to advance and organize a libertarian political position, not merely describe one.
- **1973:** *The Essential von Mises*. An intellectual-biographical introduction to Ludwig von Mises, first made as a concise pamphlet and later incorporated into larger biographical manifestations.
- **1974:** *The Case for a 100 Percent Gold Dollar*. A monetary-policy monograph whose core first appeared as a 1962 book chapter; the standalone book is a later manifestation of that text.
- **1974:** *Egalitarianism as a Revolt Against Nature and Other Essays*. An authorial essay collection joining political, historical, and philosophical interventions previously made in several contexts.
- **1975–1979:** *Conceived in Liberty*, volumes I–IV. A multivolume project of narrative American colonial and Revolutionary history: *A New Land, A New People*; *“Salutary Neglect”: The American Colonies in the First Half of the Eighteenth Century*; *Advance to Revolution, 1760–1775*; and *The Revolutionary War, 1775–1784*. The project remained materially incomplete in print during Rothbard’s life because its fifth volume was not then published.
- **1976:** *Ludwig von Mises: Scholar, Creator, Hero*. A brief intellectual biography and commemorative appraisal, related to but not identical with *The Essential von Mises*.
- **1979:** *Individualism and the Philosophy of the Social Sciences*. A small collection bringing together Rothbard’s methodological essays “The Mantle of Science” and “Praxeology as the Method of the Social Sciences”; it is a collected manifestation, not a wholly new continuous treatise.
- **1982:** *The Ethics of Liberty*. A systematic work of political philosophy and natural-rights theory, designed as the ethical counterpart to Rothbard’s economics.
- **1983:** *The Mystery of Banking*. A monetary and banking treatise written for a comparatively broad readership while retaining a systematic theoretical and historical argument.
- **1984:** *Wall Street, Banks, and American Foreign Policy*. A historical-political monograph first issued in the journal *World Market Perspective* and subsequently as a standalone booklet.
- **1985:** *What Has Government Done to Our Money? and The Case for a 100 Percent Gold Dollar*. A combined manifestation of two earlier monetary works, not a third independent composition.
- **1985:** *The Case for the Gold Standard*. A policy statement prepared from Rothbard’s testimony and associated material for the United States Gold Commission; its documentary origin makes it partly a governmental intervention and partly a pamphlet.
- **1988:** *The Ludwig von Mises Institute’s Report to the Gold Commission*. A policy document associated with Rothbard and other Institute contributors; it resists treatment as a solely authored work because institutional and collaborative authorship are involved.
- **1994:** *The Case Against the Fed*. A polemical history and critique of central banking, made as a concise intervention for a general audience.
- **1995:** *An Austrian Perspective on the History of Economic Thought, Volume I: Economic Thought Before Adam Smith*. The first half of Rothbard’s expansive history-of-thought project, published at the end of his life.
- **1995:** *An Austrian Perspective on the History of Economic Thought, Volume II: Classical Economics*. The second published half of that project; Rothbard’s larger intended history was left unfinished.
- **1995:** *Making Economic Sense*. A collection of short economic commentaries, largely drawn from Rothbard’s recurring “The Free Market” writings; it is a curated manifestation of earlier pieces as well as a book.

### Posthumously realized book projects and manuscripts

- **1971; published 2007:** *The Betrayal of the American Right*. A memoir-history of the American Old Right, completed in manuscript but unpublished during Rothbard’s lifetime; the dating of the work and the dating of its first book manifestation must therefore remain distinct.
- **1970s–1995; published 2002:** *A History of Money and Banking in the United States: The Colonial Era to World War II*. A posthumous book constructed by editor Joseph T. Salerno from several Rothbard manuscripts and essays. Its parts are Rothbard’s, but the unified volume is an editorial manifestation he did not himself make in that form.
- **1960s; published 2004:** *Man, Economy, and State with Power and Market: Scholar’s Edition*. The first published restoration of *Power and Market* to the larger treatise for which Rothbard wrote it; this realizes an authorial design but remains a posthumous editorial manifestation.
- **1971; published 2007:** *The Betrayal of the American Right*. The posthumous edition manifests a completed Rothbard manuscript, with editorial framing added after his death.
- **1950s–1960s; published 2009:** *Murray N. Rothbard vs. the Philosophers: Unpublished Writings on Hayek, Mises, Strauss, and Polanyi*. A posthumous collection edited by Roberta A. Modugno from reports and manuscripts, some originally made as private assessments rather than public works.
- **1950s–1960s; published 2010:** *Strictly Confidential: The Private Volker Fund Memos of Murray N. Rothbard*. A posthumous documentary collection edited by David Gordon. The constituent memoranda are works of commissioned private evaluation, while the book itself is an editorial construction.
- **1959; published 2015:** *Science, Technology, and Government*. A Volker Fund manuscript arguing against government direction of scientific research; long unpublished, it survives simultaneously as a commissioned report, an unrealized publication project, and a posthumous book.
- **1960s; published 2016:** *Never a Dull Moment: A Libertarian Look at the Sixties*. A posthumous collection edited by Justin Raimondo, chiefly assembling Rothbard’s *Pine Tree Features Service* columns; the constituent columns are Rothbard’s works, while their book sequence is Raimondo’s.
- **written principally in the 1970s; published 2017:** *The Progressive Era*. An unfinished historical project reconstructed and edited by Patrick Newman from Rothbard’s manuscript materials. Its incompleteness is constitutive: the published volume is neither simply a finished Rothbard book nor merely somebody else’s work.
- **written as part of the *Conceived in Liberty* project; published 2019:** *Conceived in Liberty, Volume V: The New Republic, 1784–1791*. A lost or inaccessible continuation reconstructed by Patrick Newman from manuscript materials. It realizes Rothbard’s intended fifth volume posthumously, but its final textual form is editorially mediated.

### Periodicals and continuing editorial obras

- **1965–1968:** *Left and Right: A Journal of Libertarian Thought*. A journal founded and edited by Rothbard with Leonard P. Liggio and others, and a major vehicle for his essays, reviews, unsigned editorial matter, and movement strategy. The periodical is a collaborative obra; attribution of every unsigned item to Rothbard cannot safely be assumed.
- **1969–1984:** *The Libertarian Forum*. A political newsletter edited principally by Rothbard, initially with Karl Hess, containing a very large body of signed, unsigned, and pseudonymous commentary. It approaches a continuing manifesto or political performance more than a conventional book, while the uncertain authorship of some unsigned pieces prevents a definitive item-by-item attribution.
- **1977–1978:** *Libertarian Review*. Rothbard served as editor and made numerous essays, reviews, and political interventions for the magazine; the magazine itself is collaborative and should not be treated as his sole work.
- **1982–1995:** *The Free Market* commentaries. A continuing corpus of short economic and political essays made for the Ludwig von Mises Institute’s newsletter. Later selections became *Making Economic Sense*, but the serial corpus is larger than that book.
- **1990–1995:** *Rothbard-Rockwell Report*. A monthly political newsletter made with Llewellyn H. Rockwell Jr., combining analysis, polemic, satire, movement news, and recurring departments. Individual signed pieces are Rothbard texts; the complete periodical is a collaborative editorial obra.
- **1990–1995:** *The Irrepressible Rothbard* essays. This title properly belongs to a posthumous selection from the *Rothbard-Rockwell Report*, published in 2000, rather than to a discrete manuscript Rothbard made.
- **1949–1995:** Reviews, newspaper columns, encyclopedia entries, journal articles, letters to editors, introductions, prefaces, obituaries, interviews, speeches, and investment commentaries. These comprise well over a thousand separately titled or bibliographically distinct texts in the comprehensive chronology; they resist honest compression into invented “books,” while reprints, revised versions, anonymous matter, and interviews often make the boundary of an individual authored work disputable.

### Collected manifestations made after his death

- **1997:** *The Logic of Action One: Method, Money, and the Austrian School*. A posthumous thematic collection of previously made essays; it is a new editorial manifestation but not a newly written Rothbard work.
- **1997:** *The Logic of Action Two: Applications and Criticism from the Austrian School*. A companion posthumous collection, including published essays and material derived from private memoranda.
- **2000:** *The Irrepressible Rothbard: The Rothbard-Rockwell Report Essays of Murray N. Rothbard*. A posthumous selection from the newsletter corpus; its selection and book form are editorial.
- **2006:** *The Complete Libertarian Forum, 1969–1984*. A collected facsimile manifestation of the newsletter. It preserves Rothbard’s continuing journalistic obra but also includes collaborative and not-always-certainly-attributed material.
- **2007/2011:** *Economic Controversies*. A large posthumous compilation of Rothbard’s economic essays and reviews; the compilation is not evidence that every constituent text was newly made at the date printed on the volume.
- **2010:** *The Complete Libertarian Forum*. A further collected edition of the same periodical corpus, not a distinct authored work.
- **2011:** *The Bastiat Collection*. A compilation combining Rothbard’s writing on Frédéric Bastiat with works by Bastiat and other editorial matter; it resists classification as a Rothbard-authored book.
- **2016:** *The Rothbard Reader*. A posthumous anthology edited by Joseph T. Salerno and Matthew McCaffrey. Its extracts are manifestations of earlier works, and the anthology’s architecture belongs to its editors.
- **2019:** *Rothbard A to Z*. A posthumous alphabetical compilation assembled by Edward W. Fuller and edited by David Gordon; it is an indexical rearrangement of Rothbard’s language rather than an original continuous Rothbard text.

### Unpublished, private, pseudonymous, and attribution-resistant texts

- **early 1940s:** *Young Murray Rothbard: An Autobiography*. An unpublished youthful autobiographical manuscript; its title and approximate date are supplied by the archival/bibliographical record.
- **1947:** *Spotlight on Keynesian Economics*. An unpublished essay subsequently made public by the Ludwig von Mises Institute in 2008; its delayed appearance distinguishes composition from publication.
- **1950:** *The Economics of War*. An unpublished essay first published online in 2020, long after Rothbard’s death.
- **1954:** *The Real Aggressor*. A political article published under the pseudonym “Aubrey Herbert”; the bibliography attributes it to Rothbard, but the pseudonymous manifestation should remain visible.
- **1954–1956:** Articles signed “Aubrey Herbert.” A pseudonymous corpus of political commentary whose constituent items are attributed bibliographically to Rothbard; because pseudonymity was part of how these texts appeared, replacing it silently with his legal name would erase information about the works.
- **1950s–1960s:** William Volker Fund reviews and memoranda. Privately commissioned evaluations of books, scholars, and intellectual movements, often marked confidential and not written as public literature. Later editors transformed selections into publishable books, but the original objects were institutional reports.
- **1946–1995:** Correspondence. Thousands of letters and notes survive in archives and private collections, only a selection of which has been bibliographically described. A letter is a made text, but the corpus resists enumeration as a closed oeuvre because the archival record is dispersed and acknowledged to be incomplete.
- **undated and incompletely catalogued:** Manuscript notes, marginalia, outlines, teaching materials, and unpublished fragments in the Rothbard papers and materials recovered from his apartment. These resist being called finished works, and no complete scholarly catalogue raisonné presently establishes their number, titles, or dates.
- **various dates:** Interviews and transcribed lectures. These are collaborative manifestations involving Rothbard’s speech, an interviewer or audience, recording, and later transcription or editing; bibliographies sometimes list them among writings even though they are not straightforwardly authored texts.
- **various dates:** Unsigned editorials in *Left and Right*, *The Libertarian Forum*, and the *Rothbard-Rockwell Report*. Some have been attributed to Rothbard through editorial context, but comprehensive sole authorship is not established; they therefore cannot responsibly be converted into an undisputed list of individual Rothbard works.
