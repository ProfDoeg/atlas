## Works by John Augustus Roebling

### Communities, surveys, and public works

- **1828:** *Suspension-bridge projects for Freienohl and Finnentrop*. Unrealized project designs made while Roebling was engaged in Prussian road planning; the Finnentrop design was apparently rejected by the Oberbaudeputation on security grounds. The surviving evidence describes several designs rather than one finished bridge, so this is a project corpus, not a single structure. [Kahlow](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-2-1755-1776-kahlow.pdf)

- **1831–1837:** *Saxonburg*. A planned immigrant farming settlement in Butler County, Pennsylvania, founded with his brother Carl and other German settlers. It is best understood as a collective social and spatial project rather than a work attributable to Roebling alone. [Haw](https://academic.oup.com/book/33664); [RPI biographical note](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/agents/people/146)

- **1837–1841:** *Pennsylvania canal and railroad surveys*. A body of professional survey work that included canal projects and a route across the Allegheny Mountains for the proposed continuous railroad between Harrisburg and Pittsburgh. These are distributed maps, profiles, calculations, and located routes rather than a single named obra, and the work was undertaken within larger state engineering organizations. [NPS](https://home.nps.gov/upde/learn/historyculture/john-roebling.htm); [ASCE](https://www.asce.org/about-civil-engineering/history-and-heritage/notable-civil-engineers/john-augustus-roebling); [RPI biographical note](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/agents/people/146)

### Built bridges and aqueducts

- **1844–1845:** *Allegheny Aqueduct*. A seven-span wire-cable suspension aqueduct carrying the Pennsylvania Main Line Canal across the Allegheny River at Pittsburgh; Roebling designed and built it, establishing the structural system he would continue to develop. It was subsequently demolished. [Rutgers Roebling Family Collection](https://archives.libraries.rutgers.edu/repositories/11/resources/908); [RPI Roebling Collection](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/resources/116)

- **1846:** *Monongahela Suspension Bridge*. A road bridge at Smithfield Street, Pittsburgh, commonly called the Smithfield Street Bridge; Roebling designed and built the replacement or reconstruction after fire damaged the preceding bridge. It was itself replaced in the 1880s, so the present Smithfield Street Bridge is not his structure. [RPI biographical note](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/agents/people/146); [RPI project-record description](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/20858)

- **1847–1848:** *Delaware Aqueduct*. One of four suspension aqueducts for the Delaware and Hudson Canal, carrying the canal over the Delaware River between Lackawaxen, Pennsylvania, and Minisink Ford, New York. Converted into a road bridge and repeatedly restored, it survives substantially enough to remain a manifestation of Roebling’s design, though not in its original canal function. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/upde/learn/historyculture/roeblingbridge.htm); [Rutgers](https://archives.libraries.rutgers.edu/repositories/11/resources/908)

- **1847–1848:** *Lackawaxen Aqueduct*. A two-span suspension aqueduct carrying the Delaware and Hudson Canal over the Lackawaxen River; it no longer survives. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/upde/learn/historyculture/dhcanal.htm); [Rutgers](https://archives.libraries.rutgers.edu/repositories/11/resources/908)

- **1848–1849:** *Neversink Aqueduct*. A suspension aqueduct carrying the Delaware and Hudson Canal over the Neversink River at Cuddebackville, New York. Only remnants survive. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/upde/learn/historyculture/dhcanal.htm); [Rutgers](https://archives.libraries.rutgers.edu/repositories/11/resources/908)

- **1848–1849:** *Rondout, or High Falls, Aqueduct*. The fourth Delaware and Hudson Canal suspension aqueduct, built at High Falls, New York; remains of the structure survive. Sources alternate between “Rondout” and “High Falls,” so neither name should be mistaken for a wholly separate work. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/upde/learn/historyculture/dhcanal.htm); [Rutgers](https://archives.libraries.rutgers.edu/repositories/11/resources/908)

- **1851–1855:** *Niagara Railway Suspension Bridge*. A double-deck stayed suspension bridge over the Niagara River, designed to carry both railway trains and road traffic. Roebling completed an undertaking begun with Charles Ellet’s temporary bridge and reused some earlier material, so it is a major Roebling obra without being creation ex nihilo; it was later replaced. [Roebling, *Final Report*](https://books.google.com/books?id=Di6x5SMUdl4C); [RPI biographical note](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/agents/people/146)

- **1857–1860:** *Allegheny River Suspension Bridge*. Also called the Sixth Street Bridge, this four-span Pittsburgh road bridge was designed and constructed by John Roebling with assistance from Washington Roebling. It was later demolished and replaced. [Rutgers](https://archives.libraries.rutgers.edu/repositories/11/resources/908); [RPI biographical note](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/agents/people/146)

- **1856–1867:** *Covington and Cincinnati Bridge*. A road suspension bridge over the Ohio River, now the John A. Roebling Suspension Bridge; its construction was interrupted by financial difficulties and the Civil War. Later reconstruction substantially changed its deck and stiffening system, but its towers, anchorages, and original cables survive. [Roebling’s 1867 report as documented by HAER](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/nj/nj1700/nj1749/data/nj1749data.pdf); [Rutgers](https://archives.libraries.rutgers.edu/repositories/11/resources/908)

### Unrealized and incomplete bridge projects

- **1845:** *Wheeling Suspension Bridge proposal*. Plans and estimates for an Ohio River suspension bridge submitted in competition with Charles Ellet Jr.; Ellet received the commission. It is a project—an intended bridge embodied in drawings and calculations—not the Wheeling bridge that was built. [Structure Magazine](https://www.structuremag.org/article/wheeling-suspension-bridge/)

- **1846:** *Report and Plan for a Wire Suspension Bridge, Proposed to Be Erected over the Ohio River at Cincinnati*. The initial design and advocacy document for the later Covington and Cincinnati Bridge. It operates simultaneously as engineering project, illustrated report, and public argument for making the bridge. [Structure Magazine](https://www.structuremag.org/article/covington-cincinnati-bridge/)

- **1850s:** *Tripartite Bridge*. An unrealized scheme for a bridge system joining the areas divided by Pittsburgh’s three rivers. The archival catalogue calls it a “creative solution” but does not fix a single canonical title or date; its surviving drawings, estimates, and notes therefore resist treatment as one completed work. [RPI Roebling Collection](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/resources/116); [RPI proposal-series description](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/24573)

- **1853–1857:** *Kentucky River Railroad Bridge*. A projected 1,224-foot railway suspension bridge for the Lexington and Danville Railroad: towers and anchorages were begun and material obtained, but the railroad company’s insolvency stopped construction. The abandoned suspension bridge is a project and fragmentary built work; the later bridge completed on the site was not Roebling’s design. [RPI biographical note](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/agents/people/146); [Buonopane](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-1-441-460-buonopane.pdf)

- **1857–1867:** *East River Bridge studies*. Preliminary drawings and advocacy for connecting New York and Brooklyn began at least a decade before Roebling received the commission. These studies became the Brooklyn Bridge project but should not be confused with the final constructed object. [RPI biographical note](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/agents/people/146); [Structure Magazine](https://www.structuremag.org/article/brooklyn-bridge-part-1/)

- **1860s:** *St. Louis Mississippi River Bridge proposal*. An unrealized suspension-bridge design submitted for the St. Louis crossing; James B. Eads’s arch-bridge proposal was selected instead. Roebling subsequently incorporated the design and its calculations into *Long and Short Span Railway Bridges*. [RPI proposal-series description](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/24573); [NPS historic-structure report](https://npshistory.com/publications/upde/hsr-hds-delaware-aqueduct.pdf)

- **1867–1869; completed 1883:** *East River Bridge, now the Brooklyn Bridge*. Roebling made the governing conception, preliminary plans, estimates, and much of the initial design, but died before construction had materially advanced. Washington Roebling completed and modified the plans and directed construction, with Emily Warren Roebling and a large engineering organization playing indispensable roles; the finished bridge is consequently both John Roebling’s posthumous project and a collective obra, not a structure he personally built. [RPI Roebling Collection](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/resources/116); [RPI biographical note](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/agents/people/146); [Structure Magazine](https://www.structuremag.org/article/brooklyn-bridge-part-1/)

- **1868–1870:** *Waco Suspension Bridge contribution*. Thomas M. Griffith was the named civil engineer, while Roebling’s firm supplied cables and metalwork and John Roebling apparently advised on cables and stays before his death. Because the extent of that design advice is not recoverable with certainty, the Waco bridge resists attribution as a Roebling work and belongs here only as a disputed collaborative or consultancy project. [Buonopane](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-1-441-460-buonopane.pdf); [Texas State Historical Association](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/waco-suspension-bridge); [ASCE](https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/article/2021/12/completed-in-1869-the-waco-suspension-bridge-is-now-being-restored/)

### Inventions, machines, and industrial systems

- **1841 onward:** *American wire-rope manufacturing system*. A body of experimental machinery, manufacturing practice, and commercial production begun on Roebling’s Saxonburg ropewalk and transferred to Trenton in 1848–1849. Roebling did not invent wire rope as a universal material, but he developed a distinct tension-controlled manufacturing method and effectively established its American industry; the obra is therefore a technical and industrial system, not one object. [NPS](https://home.nps.gov/upde/learn/historyculture/john-roebling.htm); [Haw](https://academic.oup.com/book/33664); [RPI Roebling Collection](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/resources/116)

- **1842:** *Method of and Machine for Manufacturing Wire Ropes* (U.S. Patent 2,720). A patented process and machine for maintaining uniform wire tension, laying strands, and wrapping completed rope; as a patent it is both a legal protocol and the specification of a physical invention. A required patent model survives at the Smithsonian. [Patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2720A/en); [Smithsonian patent model](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/ne/collections/object/nmah_1403809)

- **1847:** *Apparatus for Passing Suspension-Wires for Bridges Across Rivers* (U.S. Patent 4,945). A patented traveling-sheave apparatus and aerial-spinning method for forming suspension cables in place across a river or ravine. It is simultaneously machine, construction protocol, and enabling component of Roebling’s later bridge practice. [Patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4945A/en); [Smithsonian study](https://repository.si.edu/bitstreams/713ff1e2-878d-4887-a687-44200b6f85a3/download)

- **1849:** *Improvement in Tops for Wire Ropes* (U.S. Patent 6,082). A patented grooved “top” for arranging multiple wires into compact strands during rope manufacture. The patent drawing calls it a wire-rope machine, illustrating how this made thing resists separation into tool, method, model, and text. [Patent](https://patents.glgoo.top/patent/US6082A/en); [Smithsonian patent-model catalogue](https://repository.si.edu/bitstream/handle/10088/9698/scht-0054_Vol2.pdf)

- **1848–1869:** *Trenton wire-rope works*. The manufacturing enterprise later continued as John A. Roebling’s Sons Company, including ropewalks and wire-rope machines designed by Roebling. It is a changing industrial complex and body of production rather than a single fixed-title work; later factories and products made after his death are not attributable to him. [RPI Roebling Collection](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/resources/116); [Smithsonian finding aid](https://sirismm.si.edu/EADpdfs/NMAH.AC.0981.pdf)

### Published technical and public texts

- **1830:** *Bericht über die Reise von Mühlhausen nach Bamberg, über Nürnberg und zurück von Johann August Röbling*. A travel report surviving in transcript; its documentary status is closer to an unpublished manuscript report than to a formally issued book. [Kahlow](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-2-1755-1776-kahlow.pdf)

- **1830:** *Allgemeine Ansicht der Vereinigten Staaten von Nord-Amerika für Auswanderer, nebst Plan zu einer gemeinschaftlichen Ansiedelung daselbst*. An emigration pamphlet and colonization proposal issued under the collective attribution “several Germans” seeking fellow participants. Roebling’s participation is documented in the scholarship, but sole authorship should not be assigned to him. [Kahlow](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-2-1755-1776-kahlow.pdf)

- **1832:** *Tagebuch meiner Reise von Mühlhausen in Thüringen über Bremen nach den Vereinigten Staaten von Nordamerika im Jahre 1831, geschrieben für meine Freunde*. A published travel diary recording Roebling’s emigration; later annotated editions are manifestations of this text, not additional works. [Kahlow](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-2-1755-1776-kahlow.pdf)

- **1841:** *Some Remarks on Suspension Bridges, and on the Comparative Merits of Cable and Chain Bridges*. A two-part technical article in the *American Railroad Journal and Mechanics’ Magazine*, arguing for wire cables and articulating principles that Roebling subsequently embodied in structures. [Kahlow](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-2-1755-1776-kahlow.pdf)

- **1843:** *American Manufacture of Wire Rope for Inclined Planes, Standing Rigging, Tillers, etc.* A technical and commercial article describing Roebling’s wire-rope production and applications. It is partly exposition and partly an industrial manifesto intended to establish confidence in a new American product. [Kahlow](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-2-1755-1776-kahlow.pdf)

- **1847:** *The Great Central Railroad from Philadelphia to St. Louis*. A pamphlet advocating a transcontinental-scale east–west railway connection. Its work is prospective and political as well as descriptive: it attempts to bring an infrastructure project into being. [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/browse?c=x&key=Railroads+--+United+States+--+Surveying&type=lcsubc); [American Culture Series index](https://pq-static-content.proquest.com/collateral/media2/documents/ACS_cum.pdf)

- **1855:** *Final Report of John A. Roebling, Civil Engineer, to the Presidents and Directors of the Niagara Falls Suspension and Niagara Falls International Bridge Companies*. A technical report on the completed Niagara bridge, combining construction history, structural explanation, and professional accounting. [Digitized report](https://books.google.com/books?id=Di6x5SMUdl4C); [Kahlow](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-2-1755-1776-kahlow.pdf)

- **1855:** *Memoir of the Niagara Falls and International Suspension Bridge*. A separately identified technical memoir associated with the Niagara project. Bibliographic sources distinguish it from the *Final Report*, although closely related contents and variant cataloguing make the boundary between the two texts uncertain. [American Culture Series index](https://pq-static-content.proquest.com/collateral/media2/documents/ACS_cum.pdf)

- **1867:** *Report to the President and Board of Directors of the Covington & Cincinnati Bridge Company*. Roebling’s final engineering report on the Ohio River bridge, dated April 1, 1867; it describes both the completed structure and the reasoning behind its design. [HAER contextual essay](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/nj/nj1700/nj1749/data/nj1749data.pdf); [Structure Magazine](https://www.structuremag.org/article/covington-cincinnati-bridge/)

- **1867:** *Report of John A. Roebling, C.E., to the President and Directors of the New York Bridge Company, on the Proposed East River Bridge*. A 48-page feasibility report, estimate, and design statement dated September 1; it is also a manifesto for the bridge’s civic and monumental agency. It appeared first through contemporary newspaper publication and was subsequently reprinted, but those manifestations are not separate works. [Structure Magazine](https://www.structuremag.org/article/brooklyn-bridge-part-1/); [documentary notes](https://erenow.org/common/the-great-bridge-the-epic-story-of-the-building-of-the-brooklyn-bridge/27.php)

- **1869:** *Long and Short Span Railway Bridges*. A large-format technical treatise with plates, presenting Roebling’s parabolic-truss theory and designs for railway, Ohio River, Mississippi River, and short-span bridges. It gathers and systematizes projects rather than merely describing one built object. [ETH-Bibliothek digital copy](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/13719990); [NPS historic-structure report](https://npshistory.com/publications/upde/hsr-hds-delaware-aqueduct.pdf)

### Manuscripts, notebooks, and writings that resist work-status

- **1824–1869:** *Engineering and field notebooks*. Approximately 151 pocket and design notebooks in the joint Roebling archive contain sketches, observations, calculations, estimates, correspondence copies, and records of multiple projects. Because individual volumes mix subjects and the archive also contains Washington Roebling’s work, this is a documentary corpus—not 151 securely attributable, independently titled obras. [RPI Roebling Collection](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/resources/116)

- **1820s:** *Remarks on Bridge Construction*. A manuscript study developed from Roebling’s Bauakademie lecture notes, reading, travel, and early bridge projects. It is an intellectual workshop rather than a finished published treatise, and the surviving form and precise date are not fixed. [Kahlow](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/269143627_Johann_August_Robling_1806-1869_-_Early_Projects_in_Context)

- **1852:** *Two notebooks on the “water cure”*. Untitled manuscript notebooks recording Roebling’s hydropathic regimen and beliefs. They resist being called a public work: they are private protocols whose later relevance comes partly from the treatment he chose after his fatal injury. [McCullough documentary notes](https://erenow.org/common/the-great-bridge-the-epic-story-of-the-building-of-the-brooklyn-bridge/27.php)

- **Undated, chiefly mid-nineteenth century:** *Notes on spiritualism and philosophy*. An archival group of manuscript reflections, not a securely bounded or titled publication. The finding aid establishes their existence but not a complete internal list, so inventing individual titles or dates would misrepresent the material. [RPI Roebling Collection](https://guides.archives.rpi.edu/repositories/2/resources/116)

- **Undated:** *The Harmonies of Creation*. A philosophical manuscript attributed to Roebling in scholarship concerning his intellectual and racial thought. Its date, publication status, and textual completeness are not securely established in the sources consulted, so it should be treated as an archival manuscript rather than a finished book. [University of Kentucky study](https://files.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/232565998.pdf)

- **Undated:** *The Diversity of the Human Races*. A paper delivered by Roebling and cited in scholarship, apparently unpublished as an independent work. Its title is documented, but its date and surviving textual state remain uncertain. [University of Kentucky study](https://files.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/232565998.pdf)
