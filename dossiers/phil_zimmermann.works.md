## Works by Philip R. Zimmermann

### Software systems

- **1991–present:** *Pretty Good Privacy (PGP)*. An encryption and digital-signature software system created and first released by Zimmermann in 1991; versions after 1.0 became a collaborative obra developed by volunteers and later companies under changing ownership, so “PGP” names a software lineage rather than one stable object. ([RFC 1991](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc1991/))
- **1995–1997:** *PGPfone*. Secure-telephone and early voice-over-IP software made with Will Price, accompanied by its own protocol and owner’s manual; it is discontinued and should not be confused with PGP merely because it inherited the name. ([PGPfone Owner’s Manual](https://philzimmermann.com/docs/pgpfone10b7.pdf), [MIT PGPfone archive](https://web.mit.edu/network/pgpfone/))
- **2006–2009:** *Zfone*. A software implementation and demonstration vehicle for ZRTP that transparently encrypted calls made through existing VoIP applications. It remained publicly described as beta software and is now discontinued, making it partly a finished application and partly an experimental project. ([RFC 6189 references](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc6189/), [IETF author profile](https://datatracker.ietf.org/person/prz%40acm.org))

### Protocols, formats, and designed vocabularies

- **1986:** *A Proposed Standard Format for RSA Cryptosystems*. A technical proposal for representing data used by RSA-based systems, published in *Computer* 19(9), pp. 21–34; it is a protocol proposal rather than an application. ([DBLP record](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/computer/Zimmermann86))
- **1991–1996:** *PGP message format*. The packet, signature, encryption, compression, and ASCII-armoring format embodied in PGP and subsequently documented by Derek Atkins, William Stallings, and Zimmermann as RFC 1991. The RFC is explicitly Informational, not an Internet standard, and was later obsoleted by OpenPGP specifications written by others. ([RFC 1991](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc1991/))
- **1995–1996:** *PGPfone alphabet*. A paired vocabulary for speaking authentication values over a voice channel, designed in connection with PGPfone and studied with Patrick Juola in “Whole-Word Phonetic Distances and the PGPfone Alphabet.” Authorship is collaborative, and the alphabet resists classification as an ordinary text: its words are selected components of a security interface. ([DBLP, ICSLP 1996](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/interspeech/JuolaZ96))
- **2005–2011:** *ZRTP: Media Path Key Agreement for Unicast Secure RTP*. A peer-to-peer key-agreement protocol designed principally by Zimmermann and standardized with Alan Johnston and Jon Callas as RFC 6189. It is simultaneously an instructive protocol text and a design realized in multiple independent products; RFC 6189 records the three co-authors rather than sole authorship. ([RFC 6189](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc6189/))
- **2020–2021:** *Post-Quantum WireGuard*. A research protocol and paper proposing a hybrid post-quantum extension to WireGuard, co-created with Andreas Hülsing, Kai-Chun Ning, Peter Schwabe, and Florian Weber; the ePrint appeared in 2020 and the peer-reviewed manifestation in 2021. It should not be mistaken for WireGuard itself or credited solely to Zimmermann. ([IEEE Symposium program](https://www.ieee-security.org/TC/SP2021/program-papers.html), [DBLP record](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/sp/HulsingNSWZ21), [Cryptology ePrint 2020/379](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/379))

### Books, manuals, and source-code artifacts

- **1991–1994:** *PGP User’s Guide*. Documentation distributed with early PGP releases, including separate general and “Special Topics” volumes in some versions. Because it evolved with the software, it is a versioned protocol-manual rather than a single fixed book; portions later reappeared in Zimmermann’s 1995 guide and online essays. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html), [RFC 1991](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc1991/))
- **1995:** *The Official PGP User’s Guide*. A book-length user manual and political explanation of PGP published by MIT Press; Zimmermann’s own bibliography cautions that it is technically out of date. ([Zimmermann’s cryptography bibliography](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/bibliography/bibliography.html), [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262740173/the-official-pgp-users-guide/))
- **1995:** *PGP Source Code and Internals*. A printed book containing the complete source of PGP 2.6.2 together with explanatory material. It is both a book and a manifestation of executable software, deliberately issued in print amid the dispute over treating cryptographic source code as export-controlled material. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html), [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262240392/pgp-source-code-and-internals/))
- **1995–1996:** *PGPfone Owner’s Manual*. The operational and technical manual for PGPfone. Its surviving dated manifestations differ with software release, so the title denotes evolving documentation rather than one invariant text. ([Zimmermann-hosted manual](https://philzimmermann.com/docs/pgpfone10b7.pdf))

### Articles and research papers

- **1996:** *Whole-Word Phonetic Distances and the PGPfone Alphabet*. A conference paper co-authored with Patrick Juola that evaluates the vocabulary used for spoken authentication in PGPfone. ([DBLP record](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/interspeech/JuolaZ96))
- **1998:** *Cryptography for the Internet*. An introductory tutorial article published in *Scientific American*, explaining public-key cryptography and PGP for a general readership. ([Zimmermann’s bibliography](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/bibliography/bibliography.html), [author-hosted article](https://philzimmermann.com/docs/SciAmPRZ.pdf))
- **2009:** *The Evolution of PGP’s Web of Trust*. An essay published as a chapter in *Beautiful Security* and subsequently made available under a Creative Commons licence; it is a retrospective technical history, not the original creation of the trust model itself. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html))
- **2021:** *Post-Quantum WireGuard*. The peer-reviewed paper co-authored with Andreas Hülsing, Kai-Chun Ning, Peter Schwabe, and Florian Weber, published in the IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy proceedings. ([DBLP record](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/sp/HulsingNSWZ21))

### Manifestos, testimony, and public interventions

- **1991; revised 1999:** *Why I Wrote PGP*. A political preface embedded in the PGP documentation and later revised: not merely a description of software, but a manifesto arguing that deploying strong cryptography is itself an act in defense of privacy. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html))
- **1993:** *Testimony of Philip Zimmermann to the Subcommittee on Economic Policy, Trade, and Environment*. Congressional testimony concerning cryptography and export control; as sworn policy intervention it acts within a legislative proceeding and is not adequately described as an essay. ([Federation of American Scientists transcript](https://irp.fas.org/congress/1993_hr/931012-zimmermann.htm))
- **1995:** *Author’s Preface to PGP Source Code and Internals*. A prefatory manifesto explaining the decision to manifest source code as a printed book during the federal export investigation. It belongs to the book but also circulated independently on Zimmermann’s site. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html))
- **1995:** *Declaration of Philip R. Zimmermann in Support of Plaintiffs*. A legal declaration submitted in the constitutional challenge to cryptographic export controls; it is an act within litigation rather than a conventional publication. Its omission from Zimmermann’s selected online essays means the surviving public record should not be treated as a complete collected edition. ([EPIC cryptography archive](https://archive.epic.org/crypto/export_controls/bernstein_case/))
- **1997:** *Beware of Snake Oil*. A cautionary essay originating in the PGP User’s Guide and updated in 1997, intended to teach readers how to recognize misleading cryptographic products. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html))
- **1997:** *Testimony to the United States Senate*. Testimony delivered to a Senate subcommittee in June 1997 on encryption policy and privacy; its institutional setting makes it both text and political act. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html))
- **2001:** *No Regrets About Developing PGP*. A public response to claims that widely available encryption aided terrorism; it functions as a defense of the original PGP project rather than technical documentation. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html))
- **2001:** *PGP Marks 10th Anniversary*. A retrospective statement marking ten years since PGP’s first release. The author’s index associates it with 5 June 2001; copies or indexes that render “2021” alongside the tenth anniversary reflect an evident site-labeling error, not a plausible date for the event. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html))
- **2021:** *PGP Marks 30th Anniversary*. A retrospective statement on PGP’s thirty-year history and continuing political purpose. ([Zimmermann’s essays index](https://www.mit.edu/~prz/EN/essays/essays.html))

### Collaborative enterprises and works of uncertain individual authorship

- **1996–1997:** *PGP Inc.*. A company founded by Zimmermann to continue development and commercialization of PGP. It is an organized project and institutional vehicle, not a text or solely authored obra; its products incorporated work by many engineers before Network Associates acquired it. ([Internet Hall of Fame profile](https://www.internethalloffame.org/inductee/philip-zimmermann/))
- **2002–2010:** *PGP Corporation*. A successor company co-founded by former PGP personnel, with Zimmermann serving as adviser rather than sole maker. It belongs in the production history of PGP but resists attribution as his individual work. ([Internet Hall of Fame profile](https://www.internethalloffame.org/inductee/philip-zimmermann/))
- **2012–present:** *Silent Circle*. An encrypted-communications enterprise co-founded by Zimmermann, Jon Callas, and Mike Janke. Its services—including Silent Phone—and its later corporate manifestations are collaborative products; available bibliographies do not justify assigning each of them to Zimmermann personally. ([Silent Circle company history](https://www.silentcircle.com/about/), [Internet Hall of Fame profile](https://www.internethalloffame.org/inductee/philip-zimmermann/))
- **2014:** *Blackphone*. A privacy-oriented smartphone project produced jointly by Silent Circle and Geeksphone. Zimmermann helped formulate and publicly represent the project, but the hardware, operating system, and bundled applications were collective corporate creations, so calling the phone his individual work would overstate the attribution. ([Blackphone announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/silent-circle-unveils-worlds-first-enterprise-privacy-platform-300043293.html))
