## Works by Immanuel Kant

### Books, treatises, and philosophical projects

- **1747 [published 1749]:** *Thoughts on the True Estimation of Living Forces*. Kant’s first book, a natural-philosophical intervention in the dispute over *vis viva*; its title page bears 1747, although copies appeared in 1749. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1755:** *Universal Natural History and Theory of the Heavens*. An anonymously published cosmological book proposing a mechanical history of the formation of celestial systems; the publisher’s bankruptcy sharply limited its original circulation. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1755:** *A Succinct Exposition of Some Meditations on Fire* (*On Fire*). A Latin master’s thesis submitted to Königsberg University, not printed until 1839; its status consequently lies between academic act, manuscript, and posthumously manifested treatise. [Akademie overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1755:** *New Elucidation of the First Principles of Metaphysical Cognition*. A Latin disputation whose public defense qualified Kant to lecture as a *Privatdozent*. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1756:** *History and Natural Description of the Most Remarkable Occurrences Associated with the Earthquake Which Shook a Great Part of the Earth at the End of 1755*. A scientific pamphlet responding to the Lisbon earthquake. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1756:** *The Employment in Natural Philosophy of Metaphysics Combined with Geometry, of Which Sample One Contains the Physical Monadology*. A Latin academic disputation and professorial-application text, publicly defended at Königsberg. [Akademie edition, vol. 1](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1762:** *The False Subtlety of the Four Syllogistic Figures*. A logical essay issued as a lecture announcement. [Chronological bibliography](https://users.manchester.edu/facstaff/ssnaragon/kant/Helps/KantsWritings2.htm)
- **1763:** *The Only Possible Argument in Support of a Demonstration of the Existence of God*. A book-length metaphysical and natural-theological argument. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1763:** *Attempt to Introduce the Concept of Negative Magnitudes into Philosophy*. A short book transferring a mathematical concept into philosophical analysis. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1764:** *Observations on the Feeling of the Beautiful and Sublime*. A book combining aesthetics, moral psychology, anthropology, and national and gender typologies. Kant’s heavily annotated personal copy later became a distinct manuscript object. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1764:** *Inquiry Concerning the Distinctness of the Principles of Natural Theology and Morality*. A prize essay submitted in 1762 and published with Moses Mendelssohn’s winning response; Kant’s text received second place. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1766:** *Dreams of a Spirit-Seer Elucidated by Dreams of Metaphysics*. An anonymously published book that is simultaneously satire, report on Swedenborg, and critique of speculative metaphysics. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1770:** *On the Form and Principles of the Sensible and the Intelligible World*. Kant’s Latin inaugural dissertation for the chair of logic and metaphysics, presented and publicly defended as an academic act. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1781; substantially revised 1787:** *Critique of Pure Reason*. A philosophical book with two authorial editions whose extensive differences are conventionally preserved through separate A/B pagination; the second edition is a revision, not simply a reprint or separate obra. [Oxford Handbook source guide](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/58802/chapter/489163504)
- **1783:** *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics That Will Be Able to Present Itself as a Science*. A book conceived as a shorter analytic route into the critical project, but not merely a synopsis of the first *Critique*. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1785:** *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*. A compact systematic book seeking the supreme principle of morality and preparing, rather than completing, a future metaphysics of morals. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1786:** *Metaphysical Foundations of Natural Science*. A systematic book applying transcendental principles to the concept of matter and corporeal nature. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1788:** *Critique of Practical Reason*. The second critical book, devoted to practical reason, freedom, and moral law. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1789:** *First Introduction to the Critique of the Power of Judgment*. A completed but withheld introduction replaced before publication; part appeared in 1794 and the full text only in 1914, so it is both an abandoned component and a finished text. [Akademie edition, vol. 20](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1790:** *Critique of the Power of Judgment*. The third critical book, joining accounts of aesthetic judgment, natural purposiveness, and teleology. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1790:** *On a Discovery According to Which Any New Critique of Pure Reason Is Rendered Superfluous by an Earlier One*. A polemical book against Johann August Eberhard’s claim that Leibnizian philosophy had anticipated Kant’s critique. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1793; second edition 1794:** *Religion within the Bounds of Bare Reason*. A four-part philosophical work assembled partly from an earlier essay on radical evil and published amid censorship conflict; the second edition contains authorial changes. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1793–1794, unfinished; published 1804:** *What Real Progress Has Metaphysics Made in Germany Since the Time of Leibniz and Wolff?*. An unfinished response to a Berlin Academy prize question, assembled posthumously by Friedrich Theodor Rink from multiple manuscripts; it resists treatment as a single finished book. [Akademie edition, vol. 20](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1795; expanded 1796:** *Toward Perpetual Peace: A Philosophical Project*. A deliberately designated project cast in the form of a peace treaty, complete with preliminary and definitive articles; its protocol-like and performative structure matters as much as its argumentative content. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1797:** *The Metaphysics of Morals, Part I: Metaphysical First Principles of the Doctrine of Right*. The juridical half of a systematic work, initially issued separately. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1797:** *The Metaphysics of Morals, Part II: Metaphysical First Principles of the Doctrine of Virtue*. The ethical half completing the bipartite *Metaphysics of Morals*. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1798:** *Explanatory Remarks on the Metaphysical First Principles of the Doctrine of Right*. A corrective pamphlet answering criticism of the *Doctrine of Right*, subsequently incorporated into later presentations of that work. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1798:** *The Conflict of the Faculties*. A three-part book on the relations of theology, law, medicine, and philosophy; its third part incorporates the separately conceived open letter on the mind’s power over illness. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1798:** *Anthropology from a Pragmatic Point of View*. A book prepared by Kant from the anthropology course he had taught repeatedly since 1772–73, rather than a student transcript of those lectures. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1798:** *On Turning Out Books: Two Letters to Friedrich Nicolai*. A small polemical book presented as two public letters. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1796–1803, unfinished:** *Opus postumum*. The conventional editorial title for a large, repeatedly rewritten manuscript project on the transition from the metaphysical foundations of natural science to physics. It is not a book completed by Kant: the surviving fascicles, drafts, and self-instructions resist reduction to one settled text, and even their editorial ordering remains problematic. [Berlin-Brandenburg Academy](https://kant.bbaw.de/de/akademieausgabe) [Akademie edition, vols. 21–22](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)

### Essays, newspaper pieces, and open letters

- **1754:** *Investigation of the Question Whether the Earth’s Rotation on Its Axis, by Which It Brings About the Alternation of Day and Night, Has Undergone Any Change Since Its Origin*. A newspaper essay on terrestrial rotation. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1754:** *The Question Whether the Earth Is Aging, Considered Physically*. A natural-historical essay published serially in six newspaper installments. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1756:** *On the Causes of the Terrestrial Convulsions Occasioned by the Calamity That Befell the Western Countries of Europe toward the End of Last Year*. The first of Kant’s three writings prompted by the Lisbon earthquake, published in a Königsberg newspaper. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1756:** *Further Consideration of the Terrestrial Convulsions That Have Been Perceived for Some Time*. The third earthquake piece, likewise a newspaper article; the intervening pamphlet is listed among the treatises above. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1760:** *Thoughts on the Premature Death of Johann Friedrich von Funk, in a Letter to His Mother*. An open consolatory letter produced for a particular death, not a systematic philosophical book. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1764:** *Remarks on the Adventurer Jan Pawlikowicz Zdomozyrskich Komarnicki*. An anonymously published newspaper piece occasioned by a traveling religious eccentric; the Akademie edition relegated it to editorial apparatus rather than treating it consistently as an independent work. [Akademie overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1764:** *Essay on the Maladies of the Head*. An anonymously published, five-installment newspaper essay mixing satire with a taxonomy of mental disturbance. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1768:** *Concerning the Ultimate Ground of the Differentiation of Directions in Space*. A newspaper essay using incongruent counterparts to investigate the nature of space. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1775; revised 1777:** *On the Different Races of Human Beings*. An essay embedded in a physical-geography lecture announcement and later revised; its racial taxonomy is historically influential and scientifically discredited. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1776 and 1777:** *Two Essays Concerning Basedow’s Philanthropinum*. Two anonymous newspaper appeals supporting the experimental Dessau school; as solicitations for material support, they act as advocacy texts rather than merely describing education. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1777:** *Concerning Sensory Illusion and Poetic Fiction*. Remarks delivered in Latin at Johann Gottlieb Kreutzfeld’s academic disputation and first published in 1910; the surviving text records an oral academic intervention rather than a publication Kant prepared. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1782:** *Notice of Lambert’s Correspondence*. A short anonymous newspaper notice concerning the publication of Johann Heinrich Lambert’s letters. [Chronological bibliography](https://users.manchester.edu/facstaff/ssnaragon/kant/Helps/KantsWritings2.htm)
- **1782:** *Introduction to a Report to Physicians*. A brief prefatory notice attached to a medical report reprinted in a Königsberg newspaper. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1784:** *Idea for a Universal History with a Cosmopolitan Aim*. A journal essay presenting a conjectural plan for interpreting human history. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1784:** *An Answer to the Question: What Is Enlightenment?*. A public journal intervention defining enlightenment and arguing for the public use of reason; in that sense it also has a manifesto-like function. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1785:** *On the Volcanoes of the Moon*. A short astronomical essay in the *Berlinische Monatsschrift*. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1785:** *On the Wrongfulness of Unauthorized Publication of Books*. A journal essay on authorship, publishing, and reprinting that treats a book as an author’s address to a public rather than merely a transferable physical object. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1785:** *Determination of the Concept of a Human Race*. An anthropological essay extending Kant’s racial classification; its historical status should not be confused with valid modern biology. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1786:** *Conjectural Beginning of Human History*. A speculative historical essay based on a philosophical reading of the Genesis narrative. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1786:** *What Does It Mean to Orient Oneself in Thinking?*. A journal essay intervening in the pantheism controversy and addressing rational orientation in the absence of objective knowledge. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1788:** *On the Use of Teleological Principles in Philosophy*. A journal essay on teleology, human variation, and natural history. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1786 or 1788; published 1881:** *On Philosophers’ Medicine of the Body*. A Latin rectorial address whose date is disputed because Kant completed rectorial terms in both years; the spoken event and posthumously printed text are related but not identical manifestations. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1790:** *On the Propensity to Fanaticism and the Means against It*. A two-page anonymous contribution to a book about Cagliostro; the Akademie edition places it among correspondence even though it was published during Kant’s lifetime. [Akademie overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1791:** *On the Miscarriage of All Philosophical Trials in Theodicy*. A journal essay arguing against speculative theodicy and for an “authentic” moral interpretation. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1792:** *On Radical Evil in Human Nature*. A journal essay later reused, with revisions, as the first part of *Religion within the Bounds of Bare Reason*; it is both an independent publication and a component of the later book. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1793:** *On the Common Saying: That May Be Correct in Theory, but It Is of No Use in Practice*. A tripartite political and moral essay addressing private ethics, public law, and international relations. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1794:** *On the Influence of the Moon on the Weather*. A short scientific essay concerning a popular meteorological claim. [Chronological bibliography](https://users.manchester.edu/facstaff/ssnaragon/kant/Helps/KantsWritings2.htm)
- **1794:** *The End of All Things*. A philosophical and theological journal essay on eschatology and moral judgment. [Chronological bibliography](https://users.manchester.edu/facstaff/ssnaragon/kant/Helps/KantsWritings2.htm)
- **1796:** *On a Recently Prominent Superior Tone in Philosophy*. A polemical journal essay directed against claims to philosophical insight grounded in elevated feeling or intellectual intuition. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1796:** *On the Organ of the Soul*. A contribution sent as part of a letter to Samuel Thomas Sömmerring and printed in Sömmerring’s book; the Akademie edition places it in the correspondence, although it functioned publicly as an afterword. [Akademie correspondence overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1796:** *Settlement of a Mathematical Dispute Resting on Misunderstanding*. A one-page journal intervention rather than a treatise. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1796:** *Announcement of the Near Conclusion of a Treaty for Eternal Peace in Philosophy*. A satirical-philosophical essay extending the treaty metaphor of *Perpetual Peace*. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1797:** *On a Supposed Right to Lie from Philanthropy*. A journal essay answering Benjamin Constant on truthfulness and political right. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1798:** *On the Power of the Mind to Master Its Morbid Feelings by Sheer Resolution*. An open letter to physician C. W. F. Hufeland, intended for a medical journal and also incorporated into *The Conflict of the Faculties*; it therefore has two authorially connected manifestations. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)

### Reviews, prefaces, afterwords, and attributed collaborations

- **1764:** *Review of Silberschlag’s Theory of the Fireball That Appeared on 23 July 1762*. An anonymous newspaper review; it appears twice in parts of the Akademie edition, an editorial duplication rather than two works. [Akademie overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1771:** *Review of Peter Moscati, On the Essential Bodily Difference between the Structure of Animals and Humans*. An anonymous newspaper review of a comparative-anatomical work. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1783:** *Review of Johann Schultz’s Attempt at a Moral Doctrine for All Human Beings*. A review engaging a proposed popular system of ethics. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1785:** *Reviews of Herder’s Ideas for the Philosophy of the History of Humanity*. Three anonymous review installments published in January, March, and November; collectively they form one sustained critical engagement but appeared as three periodical objects. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1786:** *Review of Gottlieb Hufeland’s Essay on the Principle of Natural Right*. A periodical review concerning the foundations of jurisprudence. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1786:** *Remarks on Ludwig Heinrich Jakob’s Examination of Mendelssohn’s Morning Hours*. A preface contributed to another author’s book, not a freestanding book by Kant. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1788:** *Kraus’s Review of Ulrich’s Eleutheriology*. The published review is by Christian Jacob Kraus but partly based on an extant draft supplied by Kant; it therefore resists attribution as an exclusively Kantian work. [Akademie edition, vol. 8](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1790:** *Schultz’s Review of Eberhard’s Philosophical Magazine*. The published review is Johann Schultz’s, made partly from Kant’s surviving draft; Kant’s contribution exists, but the public text is collaborative and should not silently be reassigned to him. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1796:** *Remarks on Sömmerring’s On the Organ of the Soul*. Kant’s separately recognizable contribution to Sömmerring’s volume; it is also transmitted through Kant’s correspondence. [Akademie correspondence overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1800:** *Preface to Reinhold Bernhard Jachmann’s Examination of the Kantian Philosophy of Religion*. A brief authorial preface to another writer’s book, composed partly to support a project Kant had encouraged. [Akademie edition, vol. 8](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1800:** *Afterword to Christian Gottlieb Mielcke’s Lithuanian–German and German–Lithuanian Dictionary*. A short contribution to a lexicographical work, concerned with language and the cultural significance of Lithuanian. [Akademie edition, vol. 8](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)

- **1759:** *An Attempt at Some Reflections on Optimism*. An essay issued to announce Kant’s winter-semester lectures. [Akademie edition, vol. 2](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)

### Public declarations and performative notices

- **1790:** *On the Announcement of an Unauthorized Edition of Immanuel Kant’s Minor Writings*. A brief public notice contesting an unauthorized edition; it acts to deny authorization rather than merely reporting a fact. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1792:** *On the Author of the Essay toward a Critique of All Revelation*. A public declaration identifying Fichte as that book’s author and thereby materially assisting his career. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1793:** *To the Booksellers*. Kant’s second performative notice against pirated editions of his writings. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1797:** *Declaration concerning Hippel’s Authorship*. A public correction denying that Kant had written works anonymously published by Theodor Gottlieb von Hippel. [Akademie correspondence overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1797:** *Declaration against Schlettwein*. Kant’s public contribution to a controversy initiated by Johann August Schlettwein. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1799:** *Declaration concerning Fichte’s Doctrine of Science*. A public disavowal of Fichte’s claim that the *Wissenschaftslehre* completed or represented Kant’s philosophy. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)
- **1801:** *Public Notice concerning Vollmer’s Unauthorized Edition of Immanuel Kant’s Physical Geography*. A declaration rejecting Gottfried Vollmer’s publication as unauthorized; it directly complicates what can count as Kant’s own “physical geography.” [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)

### Lectures manifested through other people’s texts

- **1755–1796:** *Kant’s university lectures*. Kant repeatedly made oral courses in logic, metaphysics, ethics, natural law, natural theology, mathematics, theoretical physics, physical geography, anthropology, philosophical encyclopedia, and pedagogy. Most surviving “lectures” are student manuscripts, so they are manifestations of Kant’s teaching but not texts authored verbatim by him; their reliability and dating vary, and the Akademie edition treats them as a distinct fourth division. [Berlin-Brandenburg Academy lecture edition](https://kant.bbaw.de/de/akademieausgabe/vorlesungen)
- **1800:** *Logic: A Handbook for Lectures*. A text assembled by Gottlob Benjamin Jäsche at Kant’s request from lecture materials, but without Kant’s active supervision; it is authorized yet not securely a verbatim Kant-authored book. [Akademie edition, vol. 9](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1802:** *Physical Geography*. Friedrich Theodor Rink’s compilation, advertised as edited and partly revised at Kant’s request from Kantian manuscript materials; its editorial mediation prevents simple identification with either Kant’s spoken course or a finished authorial manuscript. [Akademie edition, vol. 9](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1803:** *On Pedagogy*. A lecture compilation edited by Rink; the provenance and arrangement of its underlying material are especially uncertain, so it resists classification as a book straightforwardly written by Kant. [Akademie edition, vol. 9](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1817 and later posthumous publications:** *Lectures on Philosophical Theology and other separately edited lecture transcripts*. These are student-note traditions published after Kant’s death, not authorially fixed works; the Akademie lecture division preserves multiple recensions rather than pretending that each subject had one definitive Kantian text. [Berlin-Brandenburg Academy lecture edition](https://kant.bbaw.de/de/akademieausgabe/vorlesungen)

### Correspondence and documentary acts

- **1747–1803:** *Correspondence*. Hundreds of letters to and from Kant survive in the Akademie edition, with additions discovered after its original correspondence volumes; individual letters are made objects, but the “correspondence” as a whole is an editorial corpus rather than a single obra. [Akademie edition, vols. 10–13](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **Dates various:** *Official correspondence*. Selected memoranda and letters produced through Kant’s university offices survive as administrative acts as well as texts. The Akademie edition’s selection is not equivalent to a complete authorial work. [Akademie correspondence overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **Dates various:** *Handwritten declarations, receipts, explanations, and wills*. These documentary acts survive in the correspondence division; they resist the category “work” because their primary purpose was legal, financial, or practical action. [Akademie correspondence overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **Dates various:** *Memorial verses for deceased colleagues*. Brief occasional compositions preserved by the Akademie edition; they are authored verbal artifacts but not philosophical works. [Akademie correspondence overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **Dates various:** *Album leaves* (*Stammbuchblätter*). Short inscriptions made in other people’s friendship albums; each is a situated manuscript act, while their grouping is editorial. [Akademie correspondence overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **12 June 1755:** *Latin speech at Kant’s master’s-degree ceremony*. A spoken academic performance reported in the chronology but now lost; no fixed title or recoverable text survives, so it cannot responsibly be promoted into a named written work. [Akademie chronology](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Texts/KantChronology.pdf)

### Manuscript remains, annotations, and unfinished formations

- **1764–1765 and later annotations:** *Remarks in Kant’s Copy of Observations on the Feeling of the Beautiful and Sublime*. A sustained body of handwritten reflections in Kant’s personal copy, supplemented by related loose sheets. The physical book was lost during the Second World War, but photographs survive; the remarks are neither a finished sequel nor merely incidental marginalia. [Akademie edition, vol. 20](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **Approximately 1753–1804:** *Reflections* (*Reflexionen*). Thousands of notes on anthropology, logic, metaphysics, moral philosophy, law, religion, mathematics, physics, chemistry, and physical geography, written in books, on interleaves, and on loose sheets. The Akademie editors numbered 8,112 units, but those numbers and thematic groupings are editorial constructions; the corpus resists being called either one work or 8,112 finished works. [Akademie Nachlass overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **Dates various:** *Annotations in Kant’s textbooks*. Marginal and interleaved notes in works by Baumgarten, Meier, Achenwall, and Eberhard form a material layer of Kant’s thinking attached to books made by other authors. They are Kantian inscriptions, not independent books, and sometimes depend so closely on the printed passage that the Akademie editors call them “explanations” rather than reflections. [Akademie Nachlass overview](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1770s–1780s:** *Course notes for anthropology*. Kant’s own working notes for delivering lectures, distinct from students’ transcripts; they are pedagogical protocols and evolving working materials rather than a completed treatise. [Akademie edition, vol. 15](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1754:** *Fragment on the Academy Prize Question*. A surviving fragment connected with the prize question of 1754, not a finished submission. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1755:** *Sketch of a Preface to Universal Natural History and Theory of the Heavens*. An unused prefatory draft, preserved as a stage in the book’s making rather than an independent publication. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1781:** *Loose Sheets and Marginalia for the Critique of Pure Reason*. Draft material and annotations in Kant’s copy of the first edition, documenting revision without constituting another completed *Critique*. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1783:** *Draft for the Prolegomena*. Preparatory manuscript material for the published book, not an autonomous work. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1788:** *Draft for the Critique of Practical Reason*. A surviving preparatory fragment whose identity depends on the completed book. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1788:** *Draft for On the Use of Teleological Principles in Philosophy*. Preparatory material for the published essay. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1788:** *Draft for the Review of Ulrich*. Kant’s contribution to what was ultimately published as Kraus’s review, crucial evidence for the work’s divided authorship. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1790:** *Draft for the Review of Eberhard’s Magazine, including “On Kästner’s Essays”*. Manuscript material used by Johann Schultz for the published review; the draft is Kant’s, while the final review is not his alone. [Akademie edition, vol. 20](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1791:** *Draft for On the Miscarriage of All Philosophical Trials in Theodicy*. A surviving preparatory fragment for the published essay. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1793:** *Drafts for Religion within the Bounds of Bare Reason*. Multiple stages of the book’s composition, not a separate unified work. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1793:** *Drafts for On the Common Saying*. Preparatory materials for the published essay. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1793–1794:** *Loose Sheets for What Real Progress Has Metaphysics Made?*. Materials related to the unfinished prize-essay project and evidence against treating Rink’s posthumous edition as a fully settled authorial text. [Akademie edition, vol. 20](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1793–1794:** *Sketches of a Preface for the Philosophy of Religion*. Unfinished prefatory alternatives associated with Kant’s philosophy-of-religion project. [Akademie edition, vol. 20](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1794:** *Drafts for On the Influence of the Moon on the Weather*. Preparatory sheets for the published scientific essay. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1794:** *Draft for The End of All Things*. Preparatory material for the published essay. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1795:** *Drafts for Toward Perpetual Peace*. Extensive preparatory material for the published philosophical project. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1796:** *Draft for On a Recently Prominent Superior Tone in Philosophy*. A surviving preliminary stage of the polemical essay. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1796:** *Drafts for Settlement of a Mathematical Dispute*. Manuscript stages behind the one-page published notice. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1796–1797:** *Drafts for The Metaphysics of Morals*. A large manuscript complex for the two-part published work, not a separate third version. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1797:** *Remarks on the Doctrine of Right*. Kant’s comments on Friedrich Bouterwek’s review of the *Doctrine of Right*, preserved together with the review that prompted them. [Akademie edition, vol. 20](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1797–1798:** *Drafts for The Conflict of the Faculties*. Preparatory manuscripts for the published three-part book. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
- **1800:** *Draft for the Preface to Jachmann’s Examination of the Kantian Philosophy of Religion*. The manuscript stage of Kant’s published prefatory contribution. [Akademie edition, vol. 23](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Kant/Helps/AcadEd.htm)
