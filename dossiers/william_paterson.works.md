## Works by William Paterson

### Institutional and colonial projects

- **1684–1700:** *Darien project*. A project for an entrepôt colony on the Isthmus of Darién, intended to connect Atlantic and Pacific commerce under principles of religious and commercial toleration. Paterson conceived it by 1684, sought European sponsorship, helped organize the expedition, and continued proposing its revival after the settlement failed; the colony itself was a collective and contested creation, not solely his obra. ([Bannister, vol. I](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson01.pdf); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1691–1694:** *Scheme for a national bank / Bank of England*. An institutional project realized collectively as the Bank of England: Paterson was its “chief projector,” negotiated with government and merchants, and became a founding director, but neither the chartered corporation nor its subsequent history is his individual work. The surviving 1691 papers include letters outlining the scheme. ([Bank of England Archive](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=13A84%2F5%2F1&pos=2&src=CalmView.Catalog); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1691:** *Plan of the Hampstead Water Works Company*. A commercial-infrastructural project for supplying north London with water; Paterson also obtained permission in 1693 to lay pipes serving Southwark. Bannister counts the plan among his works, but the resulting enterprise was collective rather than an individually authored object. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1695:** *First draft of the Act establishing the Company of Scotland Trading to Africa and the Indies*. A legislative protocol and corporate design, framed by Paterson but enacted and subsequently administered by others. It enabled the Darien undertaking and therefore belongs simultaneously to his writing, institutional work, and colonial project. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1695–1698:** *Company of Scotland subscription and expedition scheme*. A financial and logistical project through which Paterson recruited English, Scottish, and overseas capital and helped arrange the expedition’s supplies. Its realization was collaborative, and Paterson was denied control of the colonial government, so it resists treatment as a self-contained individual work. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1703:** *Public library of trade and finance*. A library-project comprising books, pamphlets, schemes, histories, voyages, and information about laws and customs; whether Paterson fully established it, rather than merely proposing and assembling it, remains uncertain. Its surviving manifestation is the manuscript catalogue in British Library Harley MS 4684. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)); [Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1706–1707:** *Financial and commercial articles of the Treaty of Union*. A collective legislative project in which Paterson reportedly had a “great share”; he also helped calculate Scotland’s financial Equivalent. The enacted articles cannot responsibly be called his sole obra. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1701–1717:** *Scheme for redeeming and consolidating the national debt*. A sustained fiscal project expressed through memoranda, petitions, calculations, and dialogue-form publications rather than one stable text. It influenced the sinking-fund settlement of 1717, although the origin and credit for that measure have long been disputed. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

### Published treatises, dialogues, and pamphlets

- **1694:** *A Brief Account of the Intended Bank of England*. An anonymous explanatory and promotional pamphlet: part description, part manifesto for Paterson’s proposed public bank. Bannister attributes it to Paterson and reproduces it from the copy in the King’s Library. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1694:** *A second tract on the Bank of England*. Bannister’s chronological bibliography records two bank tracts for this year but names only *A Brief Account* in its summary; the second item therefore has no safely recoverable title here and should not be conflated with later editions or similarly titled catalogue entries. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1695:** *Conferences on the Public Debts. By the Wednesday’s Club in Friday Street*. An anonymously issued financial dialogue or reported-conversation fiction. Bannister treated its speakers and proceedings as literary devices, but also noted bibliographical confusion over whether the work really appeared in 1695; its identity and date therefore remain problematic. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1696:** *Tracts on coin and the stoppage of the Bank of England*. Two monetary interventions, including a letter sent to John Locke, arguing for full-value coin and payment of bank paper on demand. Bannister admits that the prose is unlike Paterson’s authenticated writing and includes the letters principally because their doctrines match his; attribution is probable in that edition, not secure. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1700:** *Proposals and Reasons for Constituting a Council of Trade in Scotland*. A protocol for national commercial administration and social-economic development, dated in its preface 31 December 1700 and published anonymously. It was long attributed to John Law; Bannister and later biographers assign it to Paterson, so the historical attribution should remain visible. ([Bannister, vol. I](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson01.pdf); [Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1701:** *A Letter to a Member of the Late Parliament, Concerning the Debts of the Nation*. An anonymous fiscal pamphlet addressing national indebtedness and forming part of Paterson’s larger debt-redemption project. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)); [Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1701:** *England’s Great Concern, in the Perpetual Settlement of a Commission of Accounts; with a Discovery of Some Notable Frauds Committed in Collecting the Supplies*. A reforming manifesto and administrative protocol advocating permanent independent audit. Some bibliographies date the printed pamphlet 1702, while Bannister’s chronological list places the audit tract in 1701; the dating is therefore not uniform. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)); [Bannister, vol. I](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson01.pdf))

- **1701:** *Memoir upon Expeditions against the Spanish West Indies*. A strategic memorandum advocating military-commercial expeditions and freer intercourse with Spanish America; it is a colonial and geopolitical project rendered as a state paper. Bannister published it as the second edition of a manuscript work. ([Bannister, vol. I](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson01.pdf))

- **1701:** *Proposal to Plant a Colony in Darién, to Protect the Indians against Spain, and to Open the Trade of South America to All Nations*. A colonial manifesto and operational proposal preserved in British Museum Additional MS 12437. Saxe Bannister first printed it much later under the editorial title *Central America* (1857), so that later title is a manifestation, not Paterson’s original title. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1702:** *Paper on Taxation*. A fiscal memorandum counted separately in Bannister’s bibliography; no fixed title is supplied there, so a descriptive title is preferable to an invented one. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1705:** *The Occasion of Scotland’s Decay in Trade, with a Proper Expedient for Recovery Thereof, and the Increasing Our Wealth*. An anonymous polemical pamphlet answering John Law’s monetary proposals and opposing inconvertible paper money. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1705:** *An Essay Concerning Inland and Foreign, Publick and Private Trade; Together with Some Overtures How a Company or National Trade May Be Constituted in Scotland*. An economic essay and corporate protocol, likewise written in response to John Law’s proposals. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1706:** *An Enquiry into the Reasonableness and Consequences of an Union with Scotland*. Published through the fictional personae “Lewis Medway” and “Lawrence Phillips,” this is a dialogue-like political argument for legislative union, not a transparent authorial treatise. Bannister republishes it as the *Dialogues upon the Union of England and Scotland, at the Wednesday’s Club in Friday Street*. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)); [Bannister, vol. I](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson01.pdf))

- **1717:** *An Enquiry into the State of the Union of Great Britain, and the Past and Present State of the Trade and Publick Revenues Thereof*. An anonymous, dialogue-form enquiry attributed to the Wednesday’s Club and reportedly written at Robert Walpole’s request. The bibliographical record describes the club’s deliberations as written down by Paterson; whether the club was real or a fictional framing device has been debated. ([Grub Street Project, ESTC T84446](https://www.grubstreetproject.net/publications/T84446/); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

### Manuscripts, evidence, reports, and working papers

- **c. 1690:** *Portions of a tract on the government of the West Indies*. A fragmentary political-colonial treatise. Its incompleteness is part of its identity: it survives as portions rather than as a recoverable finished work. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1691:** *Letters outlining the national-bank scheme*. Project letters, including Paterson’s letter of 21 October to William Blathwayt and another dated 23 October, which act as proposals seeking official support rather than as conventional literary works. ([Bank of England Archive](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=13A84%2F5%2F1&pos=2&src=CalmView.Catalog))

- **1692:** *Evidence before the House of Commons on public loans*. Oral testimony and financial proposals recorded through parliamentary procedure. It resists classification as an authored text because its surviving form is an institutional record of an intervention. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1695:** *Letters on Darién*. A body of project correspondence rather than one bounded work, associated with organizing the Company of Scotland and the proposed colony. Bannister catalogues the letters collectively and does not supply a complete item-level title list. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1699:** *Report on the Disasters of Darién*. A report to the Company’s directors, drawn up after Paterson returned to Edinburgh and dated 19 December. It is an administrative reckoning with a failed collective project rather than a publication conceived as an independent work. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1700:** *Paper on the Revival of the Darién Colony*. A continuation-project proposing that the abandoned settlement be renewed; Bannister gives no fixed title in his bibliography. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1703:** *A Catalogue of Books … Collected by William Paterson, Esq.* A manuscript catalogue and surviving manifestation of Paterson’s projected public library of commerce and finance, now Harley MS 4684. The catalogue is both a bibliographical text and the trace of an incompletely documented institution. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)); [Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1706:** *Letters on the Union*. A body of political correspondence supporting and explaining Anglo-Scottish union; Bannister treats it collectively, so it should not be converted into a fictitious single titled work. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1706:** *Paper on the Revenue of England and Scotland*. A comparative fiscal calculation connected with the Union and the Scottish Equivalent; no fixed title is supplied by Bannister. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1708:** *Two Treatises Relating to the Union*. A manuscript compilation or transcription associated with Paterson and dated 1708; the surviving copy bears the only known contemporary portrait of him. Its relation to the separately published Union enquiry requires caution, and “two treatises” should not be silently collapsed into one work. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1709–1715:** *Papers concerning Paterson’s Darién indemnity*. Petitions, statements, calculations, and memorials asserting his financial claims against the Company of Scotland and the Equivalent. This is a documentary dossier generated by a legal-political process, not a single authored work. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf); [DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)))

- **1709:** *Letters to Lord Treasurer Godolphin on taxation and public affairs*. Ministerial advice belonging to Paterson’s sustained fiscal project; the collected edition records them as a group rather than as a titled work. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1710:** *Paper on Toleration*. An untitled or not securely titled policy paper classified descriptively by Bannister. It extends the principles of religious toleration associated with Paterson’s colonial and constitutional schemes. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1714–1715:** *Memorial to George I*. An autobiographical-policy memorial presenting Paterson’s experience and proposals concerning trade, taxation, public revenue, and the Hanoverian succession. Its date is given as 8 March 1714–15 under the contemporary calendar. ([DNB](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paterson,_William_(1658-1715)_(DNB00)); [Bannister, vol. I](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson01.pdf))

- **1716:** *Paper on Redeeming the National Debt*. A fiscal proposal and immediate precursor to the debt discussions incorporated into the 1717 *Enquiry*; Bannister supplies a descriptive designation rather than a fixed title. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

### Attributions that resist inclusion as works

- **1696:** *Letter to Locke on coin and Bank credit*. Bannister considered the doctrines compatible with Paterson’s but acknowledged that the style is more terse than his authenticated prose and that no conclusive external evidence identifies the writer. It is therefore an attributed text, not an uncontested obra. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))

- **1717:** *Fair Payment No Spunge*. Sometimes assigned to Paterson because it supports arguments related to his debt-redemption scheme, but Bannister concludes that Daniel Defoe wrote it. It should be recorded as a rejected attribution, not silently added to Paterson’s works. ([Bannister, vol. III](https://electricscotland.com/history/other/williampaterson03.pdf))
