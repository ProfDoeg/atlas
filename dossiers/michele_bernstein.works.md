## Works by Michèle Bernstein

### Lettrist texts, psychogeographies, and collective interventions

- **6 July 1954:** *Tout s’explique*. A very short polemical text signed “Michèle-Ivich Bernstein” in *Potlatch* no. 3; the collected run of the bulletin is the primary bibliographical witness. [*Potlatch*, collected edition](https://www.larevuedesressources.org/IMG/pdf/Potlatch.pdf)

- **17–31 August 1954:** *La Dérive au kilomètre*. A psychogeographical proposal in the triple issue 9–11 of *Potlatch*, arguing for taxis as instruments of dérive; it is less a descriptive essay than a protocol for producing disorientation. [*Potlatch*, pp. 38–39](https://www.larevuedesressources.org/IMG/pdf/Potlatch.pdf), [English bibliographical record](https://notbored.org/bernstein.html)

- **30 November 1954:** *La Ligne générale*. A collective programmatic declaration by Bernstein, Mohamed Dahou, Véra and Gil J Wolman in *Potlatch* no. 14. It approaches a manifesto: it announces experiments in behaviour, architecture, urbanism and communication intended to make situations rather than autonomous artworks. [Collected text and attribution](https://notbored.org/general-line.html)

- **22 December 1954:** *La Fleur de l’âge*. A signed polemical article in *Potlatch* no. 15 attacking the cultural marketing of youth; the scholarly discussion of Bernstein’s theatrical references confirms the attribution. [*Potlatch*, pp. 56–57](https://www.larevuedesressources.org/IMG/pdf/Potlatch.pdf), [Sorbonne Université study](https://sup.sorbonne-universite.fr/commerce-file/3695/download)

- **26 January 1955:** *Le Square des Missions étrangères*. A compact psychogeographical description in *Potlatch* no. 16: simultaneously a text about a place and a proposal for other uses of it. [*Potlatch*, pp. 67–68](https://www.larevuedesressources.org/IMG/pdf/Potlatch.pdf), [bibliographical notice](https://notbored.org/bernstein.html)

- **1955:** *Pas de discussion*. A short contribution preserved in collected editions of *Potlatch*. Its status is modest and unstable—periodical intervention rather than freestanding obra—and bibliographies do not consistently distinguish it from the bulletin’s collectively written matter. [Edition bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mich%C3%A8le_Bernstein)

- **13 October 1955:** *Intervention lettriste: protestation auprès de la rédaction du Times*. A collective open letter signed by Bernstein, Guy Debord and Gil J Wolman against the planned destruction of London’s Limehouse district; as an intervention addressed to an institution, it acts more like a manifesto than an essay. [*Potlatch*, pp. 121–23](https://www.larevuedesressources.org/IMG/pdf/Potlatch.pdf), [attribution record](https://notbored.org/bernstein.html)

- **13 October 1955:** *Lettre à M. André Chêneboit, à la rédaction du Monde*. A collectively signed polemical letter by Bernstein, Debord, “Juan Fernandez” and Jacques Fillon. It belongs to the Lettrist International’s practice of public rupture and cannot usefully be classified as an independent literary work. [*Potlatch*, pp. 124–25](https://www.larevuedesressources.org/IMG/pdf/Potlatch.pdf)

- **26 September 1955:** *Projet d’embellissements rationnels de la ville de Paris*. A collective urban project reported in *Potlatch* no. 23; Bernstein’s specifically attributed proposal was to partially destroy churches so that the remaining ruins no longer disclosed their original purpose. This is expressly a project—an unrealized instruction for transforming Paris—not a building or conventional text-work. [*Potlatch*, pp. 125–27](https://www.larevuedesressources.org/IMG/pdf/Potlatch.pdf)

- **1954–1957:** *Signed declarations, letters, editorial notices, and collectively written matter in Potlatch*. Bernstein also signed or helped prepare numerous short collective interventions that the bulletin attributes to the Internationale lettriste or to changing groups of its members. The collected edition sometimes records only that an entire issue was “rédigé par” Bernstein and others, so individual authorship cannot responsibly be assigned sentence by sentence; this corpus resists division into a secure list of personal works. [Complete collected run of *Potlatch*](https://www.larevuedesressources.org/IMG/pdf/Potlatch.pdf)

### Situationist essays, manifestos, and editorial work

- **June 1958:** *Pas d’indulgences inutiles*. A signed polemical essay in *Internationale situationniste* no. 1, directed against supposed allies whose practice failed the group’s revolutionary standard. Its force is performative and disciplinary, placing it close to a manifesto. [Situationist text bibliography](https://notbored.org/bernstein.html), [chronological archive](https://www.notbored.org/SI-texts.html)

- **1958; published in book form 1960:** *Éloge de Pinot-Gallizio*. An exhibition and catalogue essay first prepared for Gallizio’s industrial-painting exhibition in Turin and later placed at the opening of the Situationist monograph *Pinot Gallizio*. Sources differ between 1958, 1959 and 1960 because they date respectively the writing, an exhibition/catalogue manifestation, and the Bibliothèque d’Alexandrie book; these dates should not be silently collapsed. [Situationist bibliography](https://www.notbored.org/SI-bibliography.html), [University of Edinburgh study](https://era.ed.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/1842/8227/McGowan2011%20text.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2)

- **1960:** *Le Long Voyage*. Bernstein’s essay accompanied Gaston Bachelard’s text and reproductions of twenty-one tapestries by Asger Jorn and Pierre Wemaëre in a small Bibliothèque d’Alexandrie publication made for the Paris exhibition of their monumental tapestry. The title can misleadingly suggest that Bernstein made the tapestry; her obra here is the catalogue essay and its framing of another collaborative artwork. [Yale Beinecke profile](https://www.postwarcultureatbeinecke.org/mich%C3%A8le-bernstein), [scholarly history of the tapestry and publication](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S1021-14972020000100014&script=sci_arttext)

- **April 1962:** *Sunset Boulevard*. A signed critical essay in *Internationale situationniste* no. 7 on Alain Resnais, the nouveau roman and the recuperation of modernist experiment by spectacle. It is criticism functioning as Situationist polemic rather than a film or screenplay. [Journal text and publication data](https://contextxxi.org/sunset-boulevard-3388.html), [bibliographical attribution](https://www.notbored.org/sunset-boulevard.html)

- **2/3 September 1964:** *Note sur l’Internationale situationniste* / *The Situationist International*. Bernstein wrote the French explanatory text; the *Times Literary Supplement* published an anonymous English translation in shortened and altered form. The original and its manifestation are therefore not identical, and even sources disagree by one day on the issue date; the complete French version was first published much later, in *Rien n’est fini, tout commence* (2014). [Complete-version publication history](https://notbored.org/Note-on-SI.pdf), [published English version and attribution note](https://notbored.org/the-SI.html)

- **1957–1967:** *Internationale situationniste*. Bernstein was a founder of the organization and served on the journal’s editorial committee until her resignation. The twelve-issue review was collective and largely unsigned: it is reasonable to regard her editorial decade as participation in a collective obra—or even a political-literary Gesamtkunstwerk—but not to assign every unsigned article to her. [Yale Beinecke profile](https://www.postwarcultureatbeinecke.org/mich%C3%A8le-bernstein), [research guide to the integral journal edition](https://www.situationistresearch.net/resources)

### Novels and anti-novels

- **1960:** *Tous les chevaux du roi*. A novel, roman à clef and deliberate détournement of the fashionable manner associated with Françoise Sagan; it also converts the lives of Bernstein, Debord and their circle into fiction. Bernstein later called the novels “jokes,” so its resistance to solemn work-status is part of the obra rather than grounds for excluding it. [Ken Knabb’s Situationist bibliography](https://www.bopsecrets.org/SI/bibliog.htm), [scholarly study of Bernstein’s fiction](https://editions-allia.com/files/note_4729_pdf.pdf)

- **1961:** *La Nuit*. A second novel telling substantially the same story as *Tous les chevaux du roi* through the procedures and stylistic conventions of the nouveau roman. It is not a sequel but a second formal manifestation of the narrative experiment. [Ken Knabb’s Situationist bibliography](https://www.bopsecrets.org/SI/bibliog.htm), [publisher/author bibliography](https://edition-nautilus.de/autorinnen/bernstein-michele/)

### Literary criticism and later occasional texts

- **1982–1996:** *Literary criticism for Libération*. A fourteen-year body of signed reviews and feuilletons rather than one fixed work; no collected or scholarly item-level bibliography has been published, and surviving web indexes are incomplete, so inventing an exhaustive title list would confuse a newspaper archive with a bibliography. Identifiable manifestations include reviews of Jacques Bellefroid’s *Le Voleur de temps* and *Voyage de noces*, Hervé Guibert’s *Le Paradis* (14 January 1993), and “Christophe Bourdin, le soleil ni le sida…” (August 1994). [Bellefroid review archive](https://www.bellefroid.fr/revue.html), [Guibert reception study](https://mouvement-transitions.fr/index.php/hospitalites/republications-traductions-inedits/sommaire-des-articles-deja-publies/441-hospitalites-le-phenomene-guibert.html), [Bourdin reference](https://www.aides.org/actualite/lactu-remaides-le-fil-beau-pleurer)

- **2014:** *Une note sur Henri Lefebvre et Denise Cheype*. A late historical and corrective note concerning Lefebvre, Cheype and the Situationists. It is an occasional intervention in the historiography of the group, not a freestanding book. [Bernstein text index](https://notbored.org/bernstein.html)

- **2018:** *Eau chaude à tous les étages*. A new preface to the expanded edition of Walter Lewino and Jo Schnapp’s *L’Imagination au pouvoir*. It is a retrospective framing text marking the fiftieth anniversary of May 1968, not authorship of the photographic book it introduces. [Bibliographical description](https://autonomies.org/2022/05/michele-bernstein-the-situationists-and-may-68/)

### Editorial, archival, and otherwise resistant material

- **1950s–1960s:** *Poems, correspondence, and photographs in the Michèle Bernstein Papers*. Yale describes the archive as containing intimate poems, extensive correspondence and photographs, but does not supply fixed titles or dates for every item. Much of this material was not made as public “work”; it is nevertheless evidence of things Bernstein wrote or made, and its resistance to work-status and item-level cataloguing should be preserved. [Yale Beinecke collection profile](https://www.postwarcultureatbeinecke.org/mich%C3%A8le-bernstein), [collection finding aid](https://ead-pdfs.library.yale.edu/11499.pdf)

- **1958:** *Artistic direction of the cabaret La Méthode*. Bernstein briefly served as artistic director of Jacques Florencie’s Paris cabaret. This was a programming and organizational project rather than a surviving authored text, and available sources do not provide a catalogue of individual performances or programmes attributable to her. [Biographical account](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mich%C3%A8le_Bernstein)
