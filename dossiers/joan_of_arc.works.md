## Works by Joan of Arc

### Projects and vows

- **c. 1424–1425:** *The vow of virginity*. A private religious commitment, reported by Joan during her 1431 trial, rather than a written text or independently surviving object; its precise date and original formulation cannot be recovered from the record. ([Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

- **1428–1429:** *The mission to relieve Orléans and conduct the dauphin to Reims*. A religious and political project articulated through audiences, prophecies, letters, travel, and military action—not a single “work” in the usual sense. The documentary record establishes the project, but Joan’s claims about its divine origin belong to testimony rather than independently verifiable authorship. ([Taylor](https://www.manchesterhive.com/display/9781526112798/9781526112798.xml); [Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

### Dictated letters, manifestos, and protocols

- **late February–March 1429:** *Letter to Joan’s parents*. A lost dictated letter known only from Joan’s trial testimony; she said that she wrote after leaving home without permission, but neither its exact date nor its words survive. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **late February–early March 1429:** *Letter to the dauphin*. A lost petition or credential sent from Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois requesting admission to Charles’s presence; it survives only as a reference in the trial record. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php); [Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

- **after 6 March 1429:** *Letter to the clergy of Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois concerning a buried sword*. A lost instructional message asking clerics to locate a sword behind or beneath the altar; its existence rests on Joan’s interrogation, so it is both a protocol and an indirectly attested text. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php); [Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

- **22 March–26 April 1429:** *First surviving summons to the English*. A dictated manifesto addressed to the English king, regent, commanders, and soldiers, commanding withdrawal from France; no original survives, and its several manuscript versions differ sufficiently that both its precise date and division into one or several letters are disputed. ([Trial manuscript tradition](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letter_Mar1429.html); [Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **before 5 May 1429:** *Second summons to the English*. A lost manifesto inferred because Joan reportedly called the message of 5 May her third summons; it may have been the letter carried by the herald Guyenne, but its date, wording, and separate identity remain uncertain. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **30 April 1429:** *Repeated ultimatum to the English at Orléans*. A lost or indirectly reported summons, remembered in nullification-trial testimony; some catalogues treat it as the otherwise missing second summons, so it should not confidently be counted twice. ([Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html))

- **5 May 1429:** *Third summons to the English at Les Tourelles*. A short manifesto reportedly dictated to Jean Pasquerel and delivered by an arrow; only Pasquerel’s much later recollection preserves its substance, not the document itself. ([Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html); [Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **June 1429:** *First letter to Philip the Good, duke of Burgundy*. A lost diplomatic appeal asking Philip to attend Charles VII’s coronation; its existence is inferred from Joan’s second letter to the duke, which says that a herald had carried an earlier message about three weeks before. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **25 June 1429:** *First letter to the people of Tournai*. A victory announcement and political exhortation dictated at Gien. Its text survives in a copy; the municipal original was reportedly destroyed with Tournai’s archives in 1940. ([Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html); [Besnier](https://doi.org/10.3406/rnord.1958.2274))

- **4 July 1429:** *Letter to the people of Troyes*. A summons asking Troyes to recognize Charles VII, preserved only in a seventeenth-century copy by Jean Rogier. A diplomatic study by Willem van den Eerenbeemt rejected it as spurious, while later inventories continue to include it; authorship therefore remains disputed. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php); [Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html))

- **17 July 1429:** *Second letter to Philip the Good, duke of Burgundy*. A diplomatic appeal for peace, dictated at Reims on the day of Charles VII’s coronation; this is one of the five letters surviving as original documents recognized in Pierre Duparc’s study. ([Duparc](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bsnaf_0081-1181_1991_num_1989_1_9458))

- **5 August 1429:** *First letter to the people of Reims*. A political bulletin dictated near Provins, announcing the Burgundian truce and urging steadfastness; an original survives, although older editions sometimes misdate it 6 August or omit its place of issue. ([Duparc](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bsnaf_0081-1181_1991_num_1989_1_9458); [Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **before 16 August 1429:** *Letter reported in the records of Compiègne*. An otherwise unidentified and lost message; neither recipient nor contents can securely be reconstructed. ([Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html))

- **22 August 1429:** *Reply to Jean IV, count of Armagnac*. A dictated response refusing, pending divine guidance, to decide between rival papal claimants. It survives as a copy incorporated into the articles of Joan’s prosecution rather than as her original document. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php); [Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

- **26 August 1429:** *Second letter to the people of Tournai*. A lost news letter delivered from Compiègne by Tassart du Tielt and known from a municipal account; the record proves delivery but does not preserve the text. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **29 August–14 September 1429:** *Letter carried from the royal army during the Paris campaign*. A lost letter mentioned in Compiègne’s records; it may be identical with the third Tournai letter delivered on 14 September, so its status as a separate work is uncertain. ([Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html))

- **14 September 1429:** *Third letter to the people of Tournai*. A lost letter brought from Saint-Denis alongside one from Charles VII, attested in Tournai’s municipal accounts but otherwise undescribed. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **22 September 1429:** *Second letter to the people of Troyes*. A lost campaign report describing Joan’s wounding during the attack on Paris; its reading before Troyes’s council on 2 October is recorded in the municipal register. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **7 November 1429:** *Letter with Charles d’Albret to the people of Clermont*. A jointly issued requisition requesting supplies for the siege of La Charité-sur-Loire. Only a municipal reference survives, making it a collaboratively authored protocol rather than an autonomous literary work. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **9 November 1429:** *Letter to the people of Riom*. A requisition for military supplies, traditionally counted among the five surviving originals and bearing one of three supposed autograph signatures. Eerenbeemt argued that Riom’s citizens fabricated it from d’Albret’s parallel letter; Duparc’s later count retained it among the originals, so authenticity and the autograph status of its signature remain disputed. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php); [Duparc](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bsnaf_0081-1181_1991_num_1989_1_9458); [British Library](https://www.bl.uk/files/v5dwkion/production/be98fffef5c925bd6d709be814da80da2b94a4f3.pdf/immunity-from-seizure-medieval-women-august-2024.pdf?dl=))

- **c. 20 November 1429:** *Letter to Charles VII concerning Catherine de La Rochelle*. A lost warning in which Joan reportedly opposed another visionary’s claims; its existence and approximate date derive from Joan’s trial testimony rather than a surviving document. ([Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html); [Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

- **late December 1429–early January 1430:** *Letter or letters to the officials of Tours on behalf of Hauves Poulnoir*. A lost request that the banner painter be granted money for his daughter Héliote’s marriage, known through several administrative entries. The entries may refer to one letter or multiple letters, so completeness cannot be fixed. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php); [Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html))

- **1429, date unknown:** *Letter to the king of Navarre*. A lost letter known only from an old inventory of the Parlement’s archives and already untraceable when Jules Quicherat edited the documents; nothing secure is known about its purpose. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **16 March 1430:** *Second letter to the people of Reims*. A reassurance dictated at Sully in anticipation of a possible siege; an original and Jean Rogier’s copy survive. Its “Jehanne” subscription is traditionally accepted as autograph, although Eerenbeemt argued that a secretary traced it in imitation of chancery practice. ([Duparc](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bsnaf_0081-1181_1991_num_1989_1_9458); [Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **23 March 1430:** *Letter to the Hussites of Bohemia*. A crusading ultimatum preserved in a Latin copy associated with Jean Pasquerel. Its date has also appeared as 3 or 5 March, and Eerenbeemt rejected Joan’s authorship on stylistic and diplomatic grounds; it is best treated as a contested manifestation produced in her name. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php); [Williamson’s documentary inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html))

- **28 March 1430:** *Third letter to the people of Reims*. A political reassurance dictated at Sully, preserved both in an apparent original and in Rogier’s copy. Duparc counted it among the five originals, but Eerenbeemt considered both text and supposed autograph signature highly suspect. ([Duparc](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bsnaf_0081-1181_1991_num_1989_1_9458); [Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **between 6 March 1429 and 23 May 1430:** *Letter to the bishop of Senlis concerning the return of a horse*. A lost administrative message about a hackney Joan had bought from the bishop; only her trial testimony supplies its existence, and no narrower date is justified. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php); [Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

- **August–September 1430:** *Fourth letter to the people of Tournai*. A lost request for money sent while Joan was imprisoned at Beaurevoir and recorded in a municipal account. Because neither document nor wording survives, it resists classification as anything more specific than an attested prison petition. ([Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

### Designed and commissioned objects

- **April 1429:** *Large standard*. A collaborative obra commissioned by Joan and materially made and painted by Hauves Poulnoir of Tours: white cloth or boucassin, fleurs-de-lis, sacred figures, names, and silk fringe are described in accounts and testimony. The object is lost, its two faces are inconsistently described, and later reconstructions should not be mistaken for the original. ([Maison Jeanne d’Arc](https://www.jeannedarc.com.fr/maison/maisonexpoldetandard.htm); [Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

- **April 1429:** *Small standard or pennon*. The royal account pays Poulnoir for a “small” as well as a large standard; another source associates Joan’s pennon with an Annunciation. Joan commissioned and apparently specified the ensemble but did not herself execute its clothwork or painting. ([Maison Jeanne d’Arc](https://www.jeannedarc.com.fr/maison/maisonexpoldetandard.htm); [Orléans heritage guide](https://www.orleans-metropole.fr/fileadmin/orleans/MEDIA/kiosque/ville_art_histoire/vdh_jeannedarc_GB.pdf))

- **April 1429:** *Clerical banner depicting the Crucifixion*. A devotional-processional object made for priests accompanying the army at Blois, reportedly at Joan’s request and under Jean Pasquerel’s direction. Its attribution to Joan is therefore conceptual or commissional, not artisanal, and the banner itself does not survive. ([Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq); [Maison Jeanne d’Arc](https://www.jeannedarc.com.fr/maison/maisonexpoldetandard.htm))

### Signatures and coerced juridical manifestations

- **9 November 1429–28 March 1430:** *The three “Jehanne” subscriptions*. Marks attached to the Riom letter and the two later Reims letters, traditionally presented as Joan’s only surviving handwriting. Their autograph status is disputed: the British Library describes the Riom example as autograph, whereas Eerenbeemt argued that all three were written or traced by secretaries, and he also challenged two of the underlying documents. ([British Library](https://www.bl.uk/files/v5dwkion/production/be98fffef5c925bd6d709be814da80da2b94a4f3.pdf/immunity-from-seizure-medieval-women-august-2024.pdf?dl=); [Eerenbeemt](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005195301_01/_bij005195301_01_0005.php))

- **24 May 1431:** *Abjuration at Saint-Ouen*. A coerced juridical manifesto attributed to Joan and reportedly marked by her, but it strongly resists being called her work: witnesses disagreed about the form and length of the document she accepted, while the official trial record preserves a substantially longer text. Authorship, informed consent, exact wording, and even the nature of her mark are all disputed. ([Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))

### Recorded speech rather than authored objects

- **21 February–30 May 1431:** *Answers at the Rouen trial*. A large corpus of oral replies, declarations, refusals, appeals, and narrative recollections recorded and reorganized by clerical notaries in French minutes and a formal Latin record. These are indispensable manifestations of Joan’s speech but not a book she wrote: the surviving text is institutionally mediated, translated, selectively reported, and partly reconstructed. ([Hobbins](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvjz82qq))
