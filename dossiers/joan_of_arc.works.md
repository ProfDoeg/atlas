## Works by Joan of Arc

### Dictated letters and manifestos

- **March 1429:** *Letter to Charles, the Dauphin*. A dictated letter sent from Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois announcing Joan’s approach and requesting admission to the royal court. The text is lost and survives only as a reference in the Rouen record. [Williamson’s inventory of the letters](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **March 1429:** *Letter to her parents*. A dictated personal letter, probably made at Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois or Chinon; its text is lost, and its existence is known from Joan’s Rouen testimony. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **22 March 1429:** *Letter to the English*. A dictated manifesto or ultimatum addressed to the English king, Bedford, and the commanders before Orléans: it seeks to effect surrender rather than merely communicate information. No original survives; materially different copies appear in the condemnation record and several chronicles, and one transmission divides it into four letters. [Manuscripts and variants](https://www.archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letter_Mar1429.html)

- **Late March–early April 1429:** *Letter to the clergy of Sainte-Catherine-de-Fierbois*. A lost dictated request connected with the search for the sword said to lie behind the church altar. It is known from Joan’s testimony rather than from a surviving document. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **30 April 1429:** *Second letter to the English at Orléans*. A repeated ultimatum dictated after Joan entered Orléans. The document is lost; the nullification witnesses preserve only descriptions or remembered fragments, so its exact wording cannot be recovered. [Documentary notice](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letter_April_30_1429.html)

- **5 May 1429:** *Letter shot into Les Tourelles*. A short battlefield manifesto dictated for delivery by arrow to the English garrison. It is no longer extant and is known principally through the later recollection of Joan’s confessor Jean Pasquerel, making its wording uncertain. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **June 1429:** *Invitation to Philip the Good for Charles VII’s coronation*. A dictated appeal asking the duke of Burgundy to attend the coronation and reconcile with Charles. The letter is lost but is explicitly recalled in Joan’s surviving letter of 17 July. [Letter of 17 July and documentary notes](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letter_july_17_1429.html)

- **25 June 1429:** *Letter to the people of Tournai*. A dictated proclamation reporting the Loire victories and encouraging loyalty to Charles VII. Its original remained at Tournai until its destruction during the Second World War, so it now survives through earlier reproductions. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **4 July 1429:** *Letter to the people of Troyes*. A dictated summons urging Troyes to receive Charles VII. No original is known; the text survives as a copy entered in Jean Rogier’s register. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **17 July 1429:** *Letter to Philip the Good, duke of Burgundy*. A dictated diplomatic manifesto asking the duke to make peace and attend Charles VII’s coronation. This is one of five letters surviving as original documentary objects, but it is not signed by Joan. [Pierre Duparc, “À propos des lettres de Jeanne d’Arc”](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458)

- **5 August 1429:** *Letter to the people of Reims*. A dictated political letter announcing the Burgundian truce and urging the city to remain loyal. The original survives but bears no signature. [Duparc](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458)

- **Before 16 August 1429:** *Letter recorded at Compiègne*. A municipal archival reference establishes that Joan sent a letter, but neither recipient nor contents can now be securely identified. It therefore resists treatment as a recoverable text. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **22 August 1429:** *Letter to Jean IV, count of Armagnac*. A dictated response to Armagnac’s request that Joan decide between rival papal claimants. No original survives; the text is preserved within the hostile Rouen record, which used it as evidence against her. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **Before 14 September 1429:** *Letter recorded at Compiègne, apparently from Saint-Denis*. A lost letter known only through an archival notice; its recipient and subject are unknown. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **22 September 1429:** *Letter to the people of Troyes*. A dictated report concerning the unsuccessful attack on Paris. The text is lost and only a brief entry in Troyes’s municipal registers remains. [Documentary notice](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letter_sept_22_1429.html)

- **7 November 1429:** *Letter to the people of Clermont*. A lost requisition for powder, missiles, and other supplies for the siege of La Charité-sur-Loire. Its existence is documented by a municipal record; a similar letter issued by Joan’s commander, Charles d’Albret, should not be confused with hers. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **9 November 1429:** *Letter to the people of Riom*. A dictated protocol of military supply, requesting matériel for the La Charité campaign. The original survives and carries the earliest of three surviving “Jehanne” signatures; the hesitant letterforms are commonly treated as autograph, although the broader autograph status of Joan’s signatures has been debated. [British Library exhibition catalogue](https://www.bl.uk/files/v5dwkion/production/be98fffef5c925bd6d709be814da80da2b94a4f3.pdf/immunity-from-seizure-medieval-women-august-2024.pdf?dl=), [Duparc](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458), [discussion of a disputed signature](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhef_0300-9505_1921_num_7_35_2178)

- **About 20 November 1429:** *Letter to Charles VII concerning Catherine de La Rochelle*. A lost dictated assessment in which Joan opposed another claimant to prophetic authority. Its existence and approximate subject are recoverable only from the Rouen interrogations. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **1429, date unknown:** *Letter to the king of Navarre*. A lost letter known from an index to the archives of the Parlement collected by Le Nain. No content or firmer date should be supplied. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **Early January 1430:** *Letters to the municipal officers of Tours concerning Héliote Poulnoir’s marriage*. A group of dictated requests that Tours pay 100 écus toward the wedding of the daughter of Hauves Poulnoir, maker of Joan’s flags. The letters are lost but are attested by three municipal-account entries; their number and individual wording are not recoverable. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **16 March 1430:** *Letter to the people of Reims*. A dictated assurance that Joan would assist Reims if it were besieged. The original survives with a “Jehanne” signature generally regarded as hers, though it belongs to the small and contested corpus of alleged autograph marks. [Duparc](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458), [surviving-letter inventory](https://www.jeanne-darc.info/biography/letters/)

- **23 March 1430:** *Letter to the Hussites of Bohemia*. A manifesto threatening armed intervention unless its addressees returned to Catholic orthodoxy. Only a copy survives, associated with Jean Pasquerel; its authenticity and even Joan’s responsibility for its formulation have been disputed. [Williamson’s inventory](https://archive.joan-of-arc.org/joanofarc_letters.html)

- **28 March 1430:** *Letter to the people of Reims*. Joan’s last known dictated letter, reporting recent developments and encouraging the city’s resistance. The original survives with the third extant “Jehanne” signature, subject to the same palaeographical caution as the other claimed autographs. [Duparc](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458), [surviving-letter inventory](https://www.jeanne-darc.info/biography/letters/)

### Commissioned visual and ceremonial objects

- **March–April 1429:** *Great standard or banner*. A textile image-object commissioned at Tours from the painter Hauves Poulnoir, with a white field, sacred figures, fleurs-de-lis, and the names of Jesus and Mary; Joan supplied or approved the iconographic program but did not physically paint or sew it. The original is lost, and descriptions in the trial, chronicles, and nullification testimony disagree enough that modern reconstructions remain conjectural. [Orléans historical dossier](https://www.orleans-metropole.fr/fileadmin/orleans/MEDIA/kiosque/ville_art_histoire/vdh_jeannedarc.pdf), [Rouen trial description and later evidence](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythes_de_Jeanne_d%27Arc)

- **March–April 1429:** *Pennon*. A smaller military ensign commissioned alongside the great standard at Tours. Its original is lost, and witnesses sometimes used *standard*, *banner*, and *pennon* inconsistently, so its imagery and even its distinction from other surviving descriptions cannot be reconstructed with certainty. [Orléans historical dossier](https://www.orleans-metropole.fr/fileadmin/orleans/MEDIA/kiosque/ville_art_histoire/vdh_jeannedarc.pdf), [study of the ensigns](https://www.jeannedomremy.fr/TextesDivers/Adrien%20-%20Harmand%20-%20Jeanne%20d%20Arc%20-%20Ses%20costumes%20son%20armure.pdf)

- **1429:** *Small banner for her priests*. A devotional-processional object reportedly carried by the clergy accompanying Joan’s army. It is known from testimony rather than a surviving object, and the sources do not establish its exact appearance or whether it was distinct from every “small standard” mentioned elsewhere.

- **1429:** *Heraldic seal*. Joan used a seal as part of the production and authorization of her correspondence; surviving originals retain evidence of sealing, although no securely identified matrix survives. Because scribes wrote the letters and officials could apply seals, this is better understood as an administrative identity-object made for and used by Joan than as an autograph work. [Duparc’s diplomatic study](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458)

### Examination and lost record at Poitiers

- **March–April 1429:** *Answers in the Poitiers examination (“Book of Poitiers”)*. This was oral testimony produced during a lengthy ecclesiastical examination ordered by Charles VII to test Joan’s faith, conduct, and claimed mission; it helped clear the way for her deployment to Orléans. The official record and the so-called Poitiers Conclusions do not survive, although Joan repeatedly invoked a “book” kept at Poitiers during the Rouen proceedings; the surviving “Conclusions” printed by later editors are a related summary tradition, not the lost transcript itself. [Examination and loss of the record](https://www.jeanne-darc.info/trials-index/the-examination-at-poitiers/), [Daniel Hobbins, *The Trial of Joan of Arc*, including the “Poitiers Conclusions”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvjz82qq), [Charles T. Wood study cited in the scholarly literature](https://erenow.org/biographies/joan-of-arc-a-history/17.php)

### Recorded speech in the preparatory Rouen proceedings

These are mediated creations rather than writings by Joan: she spoke in French under imprisonment and interrogation, notaries made minutes, and an official Latin redaction transformed those exchanges into a judicial record. The existence of her answers is secure, but their surviving verbal form belongs jointly—and unequally—to Joan, her interrogators, notaries, editors, and translators.

- **21 February 1431:** *Answers at the first public interrogation*. Oral testimony in the castle chapel concerning identity, oath, religion, and childhood; Joan resisted an unlimited oath. What survives is the court’s record, not an autograph text. [Kelly’s analytical outline](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [session sequence](https://ste-jeannedarc.fr/condamnation/proces_index.html)

- **22 February 1431:** *Answers at the second public interrogation*. Testimony in the castle’s audience chamber, including the beginnings of her account of the voices. Its wording is mediated by the notarial process and subsequent Latin redaction. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html)

- **24 February 1431:** *Answers at the third public interrogation*. Oral answers concerning the oath, voices, and Joan’s claimed mission. They resist classification as a freely made work because they were produced under coercive jurisdiction. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html)

- **27 February 1431:** *Answers at the fourth public interrogation*. Testimony concerning the voices, her entry into France, the sword, and the banner. These answers are also principal evidence for objects now lost, but remain prosecutorially framed speech. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html)

- **1 March 1431:** *Answers at the fifth public interrogation*. Testimony concerning letters, signatures, saints, rings, prophecy, and the attempted escape from Beaurevoir. Joan distinguished letters she had signed from others she had not, but the record cannot by itself authenticate every surviving signature. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [Duparc](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458)

- **3 March 1431:** *Answers at the sixth public interrogation*. The last of the initial public examinations, treating her standard, followers, clothing, and religious claims. The court then ordered extracts made from answers it considered inadequate, underscoring that the transmitted unit was shaped by the prosecution. [English trial edition](https://www.history.ucsb.edu/wp-content/uploads/Trial-of-Joan-of-Arc.pdf), [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html)

- **10 March 1431:** *Answers at the first prison interrogation*. Non-public testimony in Joan’s cell about her departure from home, journey to Chinon, and dealings with Charles VII. It survives only through court notaries working inside a coercive process. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **12 March 1431, morning:** *Answers at the second prison interrogation*. Oral responses concerning the “sign” given to Charles VII and related matters; Joan withheld details she regarded as secret. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **12 March 1431, afternoon:** *Answers at the third prison interrogation*. A separate examination continuing questions about the sign, angels, and the royal mission. Later prosecutorial summaries should not be treated as transparent transcriptions of what she said. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **13 March 1431:** *Answers at the fourth prison interrogation*. Testimony further developing the crown-and-angel account. The record is a judicially produced manifestation of her speech, not a narrative she independently composed. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **14 March 1431, morning:** *Answers at the fifth prison interrogation*. Oral answers about the voices, obedience to the Church, clothing, and captivity. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **14 March 1431, afternoon:** *Answers at the sixth prison interrogation*. A distinct continuation of the cell examination, preserved through notarial minutes and the official redaction. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **15 March 1431:** *Answers at the seventh prison interrogation*. Testimony particularly concerned with submission to the Church and Joan’s reliance on divine command. Her distinctions were subsequently compressed in the accusation. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **17 March 1431, morning:** *Answers at the eighth prison interrogation*. Oral testimony about the voices, saints, clothing, and signs. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **17 March 1431, afternoon:** *Answers at the ninth prison interrogation*. A separate examination concerning her ring, standard, armor, and religious practices. The hostile allegation that these objects were magical belongs to the prosecution, not to Joan’s authorship. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/), [discussion of the ring accusation](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anneaux_de_Jeanne_d%27Arc)

- **24 March 1431:** *Review and correction of the recorded interrogations*. Joan was read portions of the compiled French record and asked whether they were accurate. Her confirmations and corrections are a limited form of authorization, not proof that she authored the scribal transcript as a whole. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **25 March 1431:** *Answers following the prison exhortation*. A further non-public confrontation concerning submission and ecclesiastical counsel, situated between the preparatory interrogations and the formal ordinary trial. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

### Answers in the ordinary trial

- **26 March 1431:** *Response at the opening of the ordinary trial*. Joan responded when the court changed from an ex officio investigation to a formal prosecution and introduced Jean d’Estivet’s indictment. The procedural document itself was the prosecutors’ work; only Joan’s recorded response belongs to her mediated speech. [Trial sequence and procedural analysis](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [trial text](https://famous-trials.com/the-trial-of-joan-of-arc-1431/2364-trial-transcript-reading-of-70-articles-of-accusations-and-joan-s)

- **27 March 1431:** *Answers to Articles 1–30 of the Seventy Articles*. Joan answered the prosecutor’s charges article by article in French. The Seventy Articles were drafted by d’Estivet and therefore are not her work; the answers are hers only in the qualified sense of compelled, notarially mediated oral production. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [trial text](https://famous-trials.com/the-trial-of-joan-of-arc-1431/2364-trial-transcript-reading-of-70-articles-of-accusations-and-joan-s)

- **28 March 1431:** *Answers to the remaining Seventy Articles*. A second day of responses to the formal indictment. Differences between extracts, minutes, and the completed record show why neither the accusation nor the surviving replies should be treated as Joan’s independent composition. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [trial text and textual note](https://famous-trials.com/the-trial-of-joan-of-arc-1431/2364-trial-transcript-reading-of-70-articles-of-accusations-and-joan-s)

- **31 March 1431:** *Supplementary answers on the Church*. Delayed responses addressing obedience to the Church militant and related questions. They completed the principal answer-taking phase of the ordinary trial. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html)

- **5–12 April 1431:** *The Twelve Articles*. This reduced digest of alleged propositions was constructed by Cauchon’s circle from the Seventy Articles and Joan’s earlier answers, then circulated to consultants without being read to her. It is therefore not a work by Joan, although it became the most consequential textual manifestation made out of her speech; its omissions and tendentious paraphrases are essential to its classification. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [Daniel Hobbins’s trial edition](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvjz82qq)

### Admonitions, coercion, abjuration, and relapse

- **18 April 1431:** *Replies to the charitable exhortation in prison*. While seriously ill, Joan was urged to submit to the Church and was questioned about receiving the sacraments. This was constrained oral response, preserved by the same court seeking her submission. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [proceedings index](https://ste-jeannedarc.fr/condamnation/proces_index.html)

- **2 May 1431:** *Replies at the public admonition*. After a theological sermon in the castle, Joan again defended her revelations and refused the demanded submission. The admonition was composed by her judges; her replies constitute the mediated object attributable to her. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [contemporary-record summary](https://law2.umkc.edu/faculty/projects/ftrials/JoanArcAccount.htm)

- **9 May 1431:** *Replies in the torture chamber*. Joan answered while instruments of torture were displayed and their use threatened. The judges ultimately decided against torture, but the speech cannot be separated from the coercive setting in which it was produced. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [proceedings index](https://ste-jeannedarc.fr/condamnation/proces_index.html)

- **23 May 1431:** *Replies to the final private admonition*. Joan was confronted in the castle with the University of Paris determinations and again required to submit. This was the last substantial admonitory exchange before Saint-Ouen. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html)

- **24 May 1431:** *Abjuration at Saint-Ouen*. A manifesto in the strict performative sense: the cedula did not merely describe belief but purported to renounce error and alter Joan’s legal status, substituting perpetual imprisonment for immediate delivery to secular execution. The surviving long Latin/French forms, the much shorter paper reportedly read and signed or marked at the scaffold, Joan’s comprehension, and even the manner of signature are disputed; it cannot safely be treated as a text freely composed—or perhaps even knowingly adopted—by her. [Kelly’s analysis of the scaffold proceedings](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [proceedings index](https://ste-jeannedarc.fr/condamnation/proces_index.html)

- **24 May 1431, afternoon:** *Responses upon receiving the sentence of perpetual imprisonment*. Joan’s acceptance and the court’s directions followed the abjuration. They are part of its performative aftermath rather than a separately authored written work. [Dated session sequence](https://www.isdistribution.com/work/jeanne-darc/)

- **28 May 1431:** *Answers in the relapse examination*. Questioned in her cell after resuming male clothing, Joan spoke again about her dress, voices, and the abjuration. The court treated these answers as proof of relapse, but the circumstances of her clothing change and the exact form of her retraction were contested in the later nullification testimony. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [proceedings index](https://ste-jeannedarc.fr/condamnation/proces_index.html)

- **29 May 1431:** *Statements reported to the assessors before the relapse judgment*. The judges presented accounts of Joan’s 28 May answers while assessors deliberated; this is a report made from her speech rather than a new text demonstrably spoken or approved by her. Several assessors sought another admonition, which the court did not provide. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html)

- **30 May 1431:** *Final responses and declarations before execution*. At the last prison and scaffold proceedings Joan prayed, confessed, asked forgiveness, and made declarations about her voices and conduct. These survive in the condemnation record and later eyewitness testimony with differing emphases; they are testimonial manifestations assembled by others, not a stable final authored text. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html), [proceedings index](https://ste-jeannedarc.fr/condamnation/proces_index.html)

### Signatures and marks

- **9 November 1429:** *“Jehanne” on the Riom letter*. A six-letter subscription on an original dictated letter, generally accepted as the earliest surviving signature written by Joan herself. Its uncertain execution supports autograph attribution but also demonstrates why the signature must be distinguished from the scribal body of the letter. [British Library exhibition catalogue](https://www.bl.uk/files/v5dwkion/production/be98fffef5c925bd6d709be814da80da2b94a4f3.pdf/immunity-from-seizure-medieval-women-august-2024.pdf?dl=)

- **16 March 1430:** *“Jehanne” on the Reims letter*. The second surviving claimed autograph subscription. Its authenticity has generally been accepted in modern diplomatic study, but older controversy over the Reims originals and their private provenance makes an unqualified attribution inappropriate. [Duparc](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458), [“Une signature suspecte de Jeanne d’Arc”](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhef_0300-9505_1921_num_7_35_2178)

- **28 March 1430:** *“Jehanne” on the final Reims letter*. The third and last surviving claimed autograph signature. As with the other two, it is a mark made on a dictated scribal object, not evidence that Joan wrote the letter’s main text. [Duparc](https://doi.org/10.3406/bsnaf.1991.9458), [surviving-letter inventory](https://www.jeanne-darc.info/biography/letters/)

- **24 May 1431:** *Mark or signature on the abjuration*. Trial and later witness traditions disagree about whether Joan wrote her name, made a cross, was guided in making a mark, or signed a short document different from the long abjuration preserved in the record. This disputed bodily act is legally central but cannot be catalogued as a secure autograph. [Kelly](https://joanofarctrials.com/chapter-outline.html)
