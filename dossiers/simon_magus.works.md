## Works by Simon of Gitta

### Attributed revelation

- **Undated; probably second century CE:** *Great Declaration* (*Apophasis Megalē*, also *Great Announcement*). A fragmentary cosmological and exegetical treatise preserved only through extensive hostile excerpts in the early-third-century *Refutation of All Heresies*; although its compiler presents its teachings as Simon’s, most modern scholars reject authorship by the historical first-century Simon, while April DeConick and M. David Litwa have reopened the question. It is best classified as a Simonian revelation rather than a securely attributable obra by Simon. ([Refutation 6](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050106.htm); [Litwa, pp. 1–8](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780567712967_A48541146/preview-9780567712967_A48541146.pdf))

### Lost writings reported under Simon’s name

- **Undated; attested only in later tradition:** *The Four Quarters of the World*. A wholly lost Simonian cosmological book known only by title; the evidence does not establish either its date or composition by Simon himself, so even its status as Simon’s obra remains doubtful. ([Haar](https://books.google.com/books?id=GU8gAAAAQBAJ); [Mead’s survey of the older evidence](https://www.gnosis.org/library/grs-mead/grsm_simon_magus.htm))

- **Undated; attested only in later tradition:** *Refutatorii Sermones* (“Refutatory Discourses,” sometimes rendered *Sermons of the Refuter* or *Antirrhetica*). A lost collection of polemical propositions or replies attributed to “Simon,” reportedly including criticism of the creator depicted in Genesis; its original language, title, extent, date, and personal authorship are all uncertain. ([Haar](https://books.google.com/books?id=GU8gAAAAQBAJ); [Mead’s survey of the surviving report](https://www.gnosis.org/library/grs-mead/grsm_simon_magus.htm))

### Performative and religious obra

- **Before the composition of Acts; conventionally placed in the mid-first century CE:** an unnamed practice of public wonder-working in Samaria. Acts says only that Simon practised *mageia* and astonished an audience: it identifies no individual trick, apparatus, protocol, or repeatable performance. This is therefore evidence for a performed body of work, but one that resists division into separate works and whose allegedly supernatural character cannot be historically verified. ([Acts 8:9–24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=acts+8%3A9-24&version=NRSVUE); [Litwa, pp. 2–5](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780567712967_A48541146/preview-9780567712967_A48541146.pdf))

- **First century CE, exact date unknown:** an unnamed proclamation or self-presentation associated with “the Great Power.” Acts reports that Simon represented himself as someone great and that his audience called him the divine Power known as “Great,” but it is unclear whether this designation was Simon’s own manifesto, his followers’ acclamation, or the narrator’s construction. It survives not as a fixed text but as a disputed religious manifestation. ([Acts 8:9–11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=acts+8%3A9-11&version=NRSVUE); [Litwa, pp. 5–8](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780567712967_A48541146/preview-9780567712967_A48541146.pdf))

- **First–second centuries CE, formation and authorship uncertain:** the Simonian teaching tradition. Irenaeus and later heresiologists organize a mythic system around Simon and Helen, but modern scholarship warns against projecting this developed second-century religion back onto the historical Simon. It is a collective, evolving obra—if it is a work at all—not a securely recoverable system made by one author. ([Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.23](https://logoslibrary.org/irenaeus/heresies/123.html); [Litwa, pp. 1–8](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780567712967_A48541146/preview-9780567712967_A48541146.pdf))

### Things expressly not attributable to Simon

- **Second century CE or later:** images of Simon as Zeus and Helen as Athena. Irenaeus reports that Simonians possessed and worshipped such images, but attributes their making and use to the followers, not to Simon; they therefore belong to his posthumous cult’s material culture rather than to Simon’s own obra. ([Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.23.4](https://logoslibrary.org/irenaeus/heresies/123.html))

- **Second–fourth centuries CE in surviving literary form:** flights, animated statues, transformations, resurrections, and other spectacular feats narrated in the *Acts of Peter* and Pseudo-Clementine literature. These are literary manifestations created by later Christian storytellers, not independently attested works by the historical Simon, and should not be catalogued as his productions. ([Litwa, pp. 1–8](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780567712967_A48541146/preview-9780567712967_A48541146.pdf))
