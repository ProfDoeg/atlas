## Works by Novalis

### Early poems and narrative experiments

- **1788–1793:** *Gedichte aus der Schul- und Studentenzeit*. A corpus of juvenile lyric poems—among them *Bei dem Falkenstein*, *Der Falkenstein am Harz*, *Cäsar Joseph*, *An Friedrich Wilhelm [II.]*, *An meine Freunde*, *Die Kahnfahrt*, *Das süßeste Leben*, *Badelied*, *Der Eislauf*, *Der Abend*, *Geschichte der Poesie*, *Auferstehung* and *Elegie beim Grabe eines Jünglings*. This is an editorially defined body of manuscripts, not a collection Novalis constituted or published. [HKA I; Schulz]
- **1788–1791:** *Fabeln und andere Prosastücke*. The critical edition groups the juvenile prose pieces *An dem ersten Ostertag*, *Der Tod Siphors*, *Giasar und Azora*, *Geschichte der Theoclea, einer schönen Griechin in Korinth*, *Fabeln*, *Urtöne meiner Empfindung* and *Spaziergang nach Gosegk* under this editorial heading; their status ranges from exercises and tales to sketches rather than finished books. [HKA I]
- **1789:** *Juvenile verse narratives and translations*. The surviving early papers include verse narratives, adaptations and translation exercises. They resist itemization as independent obras because many have no authorially fixed titles or final forms. [HKA VI.1–2; Novalis-Stiftung]
- **1791:** *Klagen eines Jünglings*. A poem and Novalis’s first known print publication, signed “v. H.” in Wieland’s *Neuer Teutscher Merkur*. [HKA I; Novalis-Stiftung]
- **1791–1793:** *Stammbucheintragungen*. Short inscriptions made in other people’s friendship albums; these are social manuscript acts rather than autonomous literary works, although the critical edition preserves them as a distinct corpus. [HKA VI.1–2]
- **1794–1795:** *Gedichte aus der Zeit der Begegnung mit Sophie von Kühn*. An editorial group containing *Walzer*, *An Adolph Selmnitz*, *Am Sonnabend Abend*, *Zu Sophiens Geburtstag*, *Lied beim Punsch* and *Anfang*. Several are occasional or sociable poems, not components of an author-designed cycle. [HKA I; Schulz]
- **1794–1797:** *Vermischte Gedichte aus Weißenfels und Tennstedt-Grüningen*. Manuscript occasional poems and songs, including *Vergiß mein nicht!*, *Bey der Wiederkunft einer geliebten Freundin*, *An Carolinen [Just]*, *M. und S.*, *Antwort an Carolinen [Just]*, an entry in the Grüningen church book and a poem for the garden purchase of 29 April 1797. The collective title and arrangement are editorial. [HKA I]

### Philosophical study-books and fragmentary projects

- **1795–1796:** *Fichte-Studien*. A sustained set of notebook analyses responding to Fichte’s *Wissenschaftslehre*; “Fichte Studies” is an editorial designation for a developing philosophical practice, not a treatise prepared for publication. Its internal grouping and exact chronology remain matters of editorial reconstruction. [HKA II; Kneller]
- **1796:** *Des Dichters Reich*. A poetic-philosophical fragment or draft preserved among the early studies; it never acquired the stability of a finished essay. [HKA II]
- **1796–1797:** *Fragmente und Studien bis 1797*. A heterogeneous manuscript body of philosophical, literary, scientific and political notes. It resists being called one work: the unity is supplied chiefly by chronology and the modern edition. [HKA II; Schulz]
- **1797:** *Hemsterhuis-Studien*. Reading notes, translations and philosophical transformations arising from Novalis’s engagement with Frans Hemsterhuis; an intellectual workshop rather than a completed book. [HKA II; SEP]
- **1797:** *Kant-Studien*. Notes on Kant’s theoretical and practical philosophy, intertwined in the manuscripts with the Hemsterhuis material. The familiar title is editorial, and the dossier was not published by Novalis. [HKA II; Wood]
- **1797–1798:** *Vermischte Bemerkungen*. A manuscript sequence of aphorisms from which *Blüthenstaub* was selected; because publication involved selection and alteration, the manuscript complex and the printed manifestation are not identical. [HKA II; Stoljar]
- **1798:** *Blüthenstaub*. A collection of aphoristic fragments published under the name “Novalis” in the first number of the *Athenäum*. Friedrich Schlegel selected, reordered and altered the material and contributed several fragments, so the printed work is a collaborative editorial manifestation with partially mixed authorship. [HKA II; Stoljar]
- **1797–1798:** *Logologische Fragmente I*. A retrospectively delimited group of notes about language, signs and philosophical exposition, not an authorially completed essay. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Logologische Fragmente II*. A second editorial group of language-philosophical notes; its numbering and separation from adjacent manuscript materials are modern editorial decisions. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Poësie*. Notes on poetry and poetics gathered under a manuscript or editorial rubric rather than shaped into a finished theoretical work. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Poëticismen*. Aphorisms and experimental propositions about poetic making; a fragment dossier whose boundaries vary with editions. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Vermischte Fragmente I–III*. Three editorial groupings of otherwise miscellaneous aphorisms and notes. They should not be mistaken for three books designed by Novalis. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Fragmente und Denkaufgaben*. Notes formulated partly as problems or prompts for further thought: closer to a research protocol than to a completed theoretical obra. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Anekdoten*. Brief narrative or exemplary pieces preserved within the notebooks; their collective identity is editorial. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Über Goethe*. Notes and propositions on Goethe, especially his scientific and literary practice; an unfinished critical dossier rather than a settled essay. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Studien zur bildenden Kunst*. Notes on visual art gathered by editors from the study manuscripts. They are evidence of a cross-media aesthetic project but not themselves a visual artwork or a finished treatise. [HKA II]
- **1798:** *Teplitzer Fragmente*. Notes made during the Teplitz journey, ranging across philology, science and poetry. The geographical title names a manuscript occasion, not an author-published collection. [HKA II; Schulz]
- **1798:** *Kritik der Athenaeumsfragmente*. Critical annotations on the *Athenäum* fragments, inseparable from the collaborative discourse of the Jena circle and never constituted as an independent publication. [HKA II]
- **1798:** *Titel der Fragmente*. Projected headings or organizational labels for fragmentary writing; this is metatextual planning—the design of possible works—rather than a completed work itself. [HKA II]
- **1797–1798:** *Fragmente und Studien 1797–1798*. The critical edition’s chronological remainder of philosophical, poetological and scientific notes. Its existence as a single “thing” is editorial and archival rather than authorial. [HKA II; Schulz]
- **1798–1799:** *Dialogen*. Short philosophical dialogues or dialogue drafts. The plural title covers separate experiments, and their posthumous presentation as a unit should not be confused with an authorially finished book. [HKA II]
- **1798–1799:** *Monolog*. A compact language-philosophical prose piece about language’s autonomous activity; it is more nearly a finished essay than the surrounding notebook fragments, though it remained unpublished in Novalis’s lifetime. [HKA II; Stoljar]
- **1799:** *Randbemerkungen zu Friedrich Schlegels Ideen*. Marginal responses to Schlegel’s *Ideen*; materially and intellectually parasitic on another text, these are annotations and collaborative interventions rather than a freestanding book. [HKA II]
- **1799–1800:** *Fragmente und Studien 1799–1800*. Novalis’s late notes on religion, medicine, illness, poetry, philosophy and the projected continuation of his literary works. The heading is an editorial container, and many entries are germs of projects rather than obras in a conventional sense. [HKA III; Schulz]

### Political and religious interventions

- **1798:** *Glauben und Liebe oder Der König und die Königin*. A sequence of political fragments published in the *Jahrbücher der Preußischen Monarchie*. It acts as a public intervention in monarchy, citizenship and political symbolism and therefore approaches a manifesto, although the periodical suppressed the projected continuation. [HKA II; Beiser]
- **1798:** *Politische Aphorismen* (also called *Glauben und Liebe III*). Political fragments associated with the suppressed continuation of *Glauben und Liebe*. Their title and separation are editorial, and they were not issued by Novalis as an independent work. [HKA II]
- **October–November 1799:** *Europa*. A historical-religious oration advocating a spiritually renewed Europe, read to the Jena circle but withheld after its reception there. It was first printed in full only in 1826 under the editorial title *Die Christenheit oder Europa*, so that familiar title is not securely Novalis’s own. [HKA III; Beiser; Novalis-Stiftung]

### Encyclopedic and scientific projects

- **1798–1799:** *Freiberger naturwissenschaftliche Studien*. Study notebooks from the Bergakademie covering mineralogy, geology, chemistry, physics, mathematics, medicine and natural philosophy. They are a research archive and intellectual laboratory, not a single completed scientific treatise. [HKA III; Schulz]
- **1798–1799:** *Das Allgemeine Brouillon*. An unfinished plan and accumulation of materials for an encyclopedia or “scientific Bible” joining the arts and sciences. It is best classified as a project: the intention and combinatory procedure are the work, while the familiar title is drawn from the manuscript and later editorial practice. [HKA III; Wood]
- **1798–1800:** *Mathematical, medical, chemical and physical notes*. Numerous calculations, excerpts, diagrams and experimental propositions survive outside or across the named study complexes. Treating each mark as an autonomous work would falsify their notebook function; collectively they are manifestations of Novalis’s encyclopedic research practice. [HKA III and V; HKA VI.3]

### Poems, hymns and lyric constellations

- **1798–1799:** *Gedichte aus der Freiberger Zeit*. An editorial group comprising *Der Fremdling*, *Kenne dich selbst*, *Letzte Liebe*, the 1798 distichs, *Blumen*, *Hymne*, *An die Fundgrube Auguste*, *Der müde Fremdling ist verschwunden* and *Wohin ziehst du mich*. These poems were not all conceived as one cycle. [HKA I; Schulz]
- **1798:** *Der Fremdling*. A separately published poem; it also belongs to the later editorial grouping of the Freiberg poems. [HKA I; Novalis-Stiftung]
- **1798:** *Blumen*. A short published poetic sequence or pair of poems, printed in the *Jahrbücher der Preußischen Monarchie*. It is distinct from the prose fragments published that year. [HKA I; Deutsche Biographie]
- **1799–1800:** *Hymnen an die Nacht*. A six-part Gesamtkunstwerk of prose, rhythmic prose and verse, existing in a handwritten version and in a substantially revised periodical manifestation published in the *Athenäum* in August 1800. Neither version can simply be treated as an interchangeable edition of one fixed text. [HKA I; Novalis-Stiftung; Göttingen]
- **1799–1800:** *Geistliche Lieder*. Fifteen religious poems intended in part as new congregational songs, but without music by Novalis. The familiar cycle was assembled and titled posthumously by editors; several poems also belonged to plans for *Heinrich von Ofterdingen*, so its unity as an obra is disputed. [HKA I; Schulz]
- **1799–1800:** *Späte Gedichte*. An editorial corpus including *Das Gedicht*, *An Tieck*, *Es färbte sich die Wiese grün*, *Der Himmel war umzogen*, *An Dora*, *An Julien*, *Alle Menschen seh ich leben* and *An Karl von Hardenberg*. It is a chronological grouping, not a collection authorized by Novalis. [HKA I; Schulz]
- **1799–1800:** *Wenn nicht mehr Zahlen und Figuren*. A poem surviving within the planning materials for *Heinrich von Ofterdingen*; its frequent publication as an independent lyric is a later manifestation rather than its original material status. [HKA I]
- **1800:** *Das Lied der Toten*. A poem connected with plans for the second part of *Heinrich von Ofterdingen*, surviving in more than one textual state. Its independent title does not erase its status as material for an unfinished novel-project. [HKA I; Novalis-Stiftung]
- **1800:** *Die Vermählung der Jahrszeiten*. A dramatic-lyric or operatic scene associated with the continuation of *Heinrich von Ofterdingen*. It crosses poem, dramatic tableau and projected musical theatre, making it closer to a small Gesamtkunstwerk-project than to a freestanding lyric. [HKA I; Novalis-Stiftung]

### Narrative projects

- **1798–1799:** *Die Lehrlinge zu Sais*. An unfinished natural-philosophical novel or prose cycle, comprising *Der Lehrling* and the incomplete *Die Natur*. Its embedded tale of Hyazinth and Rosenblütchen, dialogues and hymnic passages deliberately blur narrative, philosophy and scientific inquiry. [HKA I; Schulz]
- **1798–1799:** *Materialien zu den Lehrlingen zu Sais*. Plans headed or later identified as *Der Lehrling zu Sais*, *Die Naturlehre*, *Jesus der Held* and *Verwandlung des Tempels zu Sais*. These are project remains, not four completed narratives. [HKA I; Schulz]
- **1799–1800:** *Heinrich von Ofterdingen*. An unfinished novel-project: Novalis completed the first part, *Die Erwartung*, but only a fragment and plans survive for the second, *Die Erfüllung*. The 1802 posthumous book was produced by Friedrich Schlegel and Ludwig Tieck, who also supplied information about the unwritten continuation; it is therefore an editorial manifestation of an incomplete obra. [HKA I; Deutsche Biographie]
- **1799–1800:** *Klingsohrs Märchen*. An extensive fairy tale embedded in *Heinrich von Ofterdingen*, not originally an independent book. Its mixture of narrative, allegory, poetry and cosmology makes it a miniature Gesamtkunstwerk within the novel. [HKA I]
- **1799–1800:** *Gedichte aus Heinrich von Ofterdingen*. Seventeen embedded or projected poems, including the dedication, miners’ songs, songs of the singer and hermit, Klingsohr’s wine song, the songs of Fabel, *Astralis* and the pilgrim’s song. The critical edition indexes them as poems, but materially they belong to the novel and its continuation papers. [HKA I; Schulz]
- **1799–1800:** *Paralipomena zu Heinrich von Ofterdingen*. Preparatory notes, studies for *Klingsohrs Märchen*, the “Berlin papers,” projected episodes and reports preserved in notebooks and letters. This is an archive of a project rather than a finished sequel. [HKA I]
- **1799–1800:** *Drei Novellenentwürfe*. Three prose-fiction outlines printed by the critical editors among the narrative remains. Their customary collective label is editorial, and none reached completed-novella form. [HKA I]
- **1799–1800:** *Dichterische Pläne*. A manuscript complex including novel studies, *Briefe und Geschichten an Julie* and other poetic notes. These are prospective structures—projects in the strict sense—not realized works. [HKA I]

### Journals, correspondence and life-writing

- **April–July 1797:** *Journal nach dem Tode Sophie von Kühns*. A private diary combining mourning, self-observation, religious discipline, dreams and an intended experiment in approaching Sophie through death. It is a protocol for living and remembering rather than a literary work prepared for readers. [HKA IV; Donehower]
- **1797–1800:** *Tagebücher und persönliche Aufzeichnungen*. Later diaries and dated notes concerning health, work, family, reading and poetic projects. Editors necessarily impose boundaries on this material, which resists classification as either oeuvre or mere document. [HKA IV; Samuel]
- **1797–1798:** *Klarisse*. A brief prose portrait associated with Sophie von Kühn and preserved among the personal writings; its title and independent-work status derive substantially from editorial transmission. [HKA IV; Stoljar]
- **1790s–1801:** *Briefe*. Novalis’s surviving correspondence with family, Sophie von Kühn, Friedrich and August Wilhelm Schlegel, Ludwig Tieck, Caroline and Rahel Just, and others forms a large epistolary corpus. Individual letters are acts within relationships rather than chapters of an authored book, although many contain poems, criticism and plans inseparable from the literary projects. [HKA IV; Briefe der Romantik]
- **1791–1801:** *Dedications, album leaves, memoranda and other life-documents*. Short social and administrative inscriptions survive alongside the letters. Their resistance to the category “work” is material information: they are traces of actions, obligations and relationships rather than autonomous texts. [HKA IV and VI]

### Professional, legal, mining and saltworks writings

- **1794–1797:** *Juristische und administrative Schriften aus der Tätigkeit bei Kreisamtmann Just*. Drafts, memoranda, reports and official documents made during Novalis’s legal-administrative service. Some survive only as working papers or documents written within an office, making individual authorship and final responsibility difficult to assign. [HKA VI.3]
- **1798–1801:** *Technische Aufzeichnungen aus Bergakademie und Salinenverwaltung*. Calculations, observations, mine and saltworks records, excerpts and practical memoranda. These are protocols and working instruments, not literary books, but they are things Hardenberg made in his professional capacity. [HKA VI.3; Walter]
- **1799–1800:** *Braunkohlen-Erkundungsberichte*. Reports and field records produced during inspections of central German lignite deposits for the Saxon saltworks. They combine geological observation, economic planning and administrative action; exact authorship can sometimes be distributed across office drafts, copies and fair versions. [HKA VI.3; Walter]
- **1800:** *Bericht über die Holzerdenlage der hiesigen Gegend*. A substantial professional report on local lignite resources and their possible use in salt production. It is a technical-administrative obra whose manuscript transmission belongs to the “Salinenschriften,” not to Novalis’s literary publications. [HKA VI.3; Walter]
- **1799–1801:** *Salinenschriften*. The modern collective name for reports, drafts, calculations, physical and chemical essays, and administrative documents connected with the Saxon saltworks. The corpus was long thought lost and later recovered in Kraków; it is not one book, and some documents’ status as sole-authored Novalis texts requires case-by-case editorial judgment. [HKA VI.3; Perlentaucher; Walter]
