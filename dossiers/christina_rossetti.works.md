## Works by Christina Georgina Rossetti

### Poetic corpus and author-shaped collections

- **1842–1894:** *Poetic corpus*. Rossetti composed more than 1,100 poems, over 900 of which appeared during her lifetime; Rebecca W. Crump’s three-volume variorum edition is the standard complete scholarly inventory. Because many lyrics are untitled or share generic titles such as “Song,” this corpus resists reduction to a reliable title-only list: Crump identifies its things through titles, first lines, manuscript states, sequences, and publication histories.
- **27 April 1842:** *To My Mother on the Anniversary of Her Birth*. Rossetti’s earliest securely dated surviving poem; it belongs to the manuscript record later incorporated into Crump’s variorum corpus.
- **1847:** *Verses: Dedicated to Her Mother*. A privately printed collection of early poems, produced by her grandfather Gaetano Polidori; it is both a youthful obra and the first material manifestation of her poetic project.
- **1850:** *Poems published as “Ellen Alleyn” in The Germ*. A small periodical constellation rather than a book: Rossetti used this pseudonym for seven poems in the Pre-Raphaelite journal. The constituent poems remain individual works within Crump’s corpus.
- **1862:** *Goblin Market and Other Poems*. Rossetti’s first commercial poetry collection and a deliberately arranged book-object, with two illustrations and binding design by Dante Gabriel Rossetti; the verbal obra is Christina’s, while the illustrated volume is collaborative.
- **1866:** *The Prince’s Progress and Other Poems*. A second author-arranged collection, again materially shaped by Dante Gabriel Rossetti’s title-page, frontispiece, and binding designs.
- **1872; enlarged 1893:** *Sing-Song: A Nursery Rhyme Book*. A designed sequence of nursery rhymes rather than a miscellaneous anthology, made in conjunction with Arthur Hughes’s illustrations. The enlarged edition is an author-supervised new manifestation containing additional poems, not simply a reprint.
- **1875; revised 1879:** *Goblin Market, The Prince’s Progress, and Other Poems*. Rossetti’s combined and revised collected volume; it is principally a new manifestation and authorial reordering of earlier poems, although its revisions give it textual authority beyond that of an ordinary reprint.
- **1881:** *A Pageant and Other Poems*. An author-arranged collection combining lyrics, narratives, devotional poems, sequences, and “The Months: A Pageant,” a poem made as a miniature performance or masque.
- **1881:** *The Months: A Pageant*. A verse pageant for twelve personified months and attendant figures; it is simultaneously poem, dramatic script, and implied performance.
- **1881:** *Monna Innominata: A Sonnet of Sonnets*. A fourteen-sonnet counter-project to the male-authored Petrarchan sequence, presented within *A Pageant and Other Poems* but structured as an autonomous poetic obra.
- **1881:** *Later Life: A Double Sonnet of Sonnets*. A twenty-eight-sonnet devotional sequence; its architecture, rather than merely its inclusion in a collection, makes it a distinct composite work.
- **1881:** *The Thread of Life*. A three-sonnet sequence functioning as a single meditation while retaining three lyric components.
- **1893:** *Verses*. A large, author-arranged collection of devotional poetry drawn principally from *Called to Be Saints*, *Time Flies*, and *The Face of the Deep*. It is a deliberate poetic extraction and recomposition of material first embedded in prose books.
- **1893:** *Some Feasts and Fasts*. A calendrical devotional sequence within *Verses*; its parts concern saints’ days and church festivals but collectively form an ordered obra.
- **1893:** *Gifts and Graces*. A devotional poetic sequence within *Verses* organized around spiritual gifts and virtues.
- **1893:** *The World: Self-Destruction*. A devotional sequence within *Verses*, made as a progression rather than as an accidental gathering of lyrics.
- **1893:** *Divers Worlds: Time and Eternity*. A devotional sequence within *Verses* setting temporal existence against eternity.
- **1893:** *New Jerusalem and Its Citizens*. A devotional sequence within *Verses* concerned with heaven and its inhabitants.
- **1893:** *Songs for Strangers and Pilgrims*. A devotional sequence within *Verses* whose organizing identity is Christian pilgrimage.
- **1896:** *New Poems by Christina Rossetti: Hitherto Unpublished or Uncollected*. A posthumous manifestation edited by William Michael Rossetti from manuscripts and scattered publications. The poems are Christina’s works, but the selection, titles in some cases, ordering, and book as a whole are her brother’s editorial construction rather than an author-completed collection.
- **1904:** *The Poetical Works of Christina Georgina Rossetti*. William Michael Rossetti’s posthumous collected edition. It manifests her poetic corpus but is not itself an author-made obra and was superseded for textual purposes by Crump’s variorum edition.
- **1979–1990:** *The Complete Poems of Christina Rossetti: A Variorum Edition*. Crump’s definitive three-volume scholarly manifestation of the corpus, including published, privately printed, and unpublished poems and the subsequently discovered 375-page *Verses* manuscript. It is indispensable evidence for what Rossetti made but is an editor’s work, not Rossetti’s own collection.

### Long and structurally independent poems

- **1858:** *From House to Home*. A sustained visionary and devotional poem, later collected in *Goblin Market and Other Poems*.
- **27 April 1859:** *Goblin Market*. A long narrative poem about temptation, consumption, sisterhood, and restoration. The manuscript date is secure, but scholars dispute how literally it records the poem’s completion rather than its conception.
- **1862:** *The Convent Threshold*. A dramatic and devotional narrative poem first collected in *Goblin Market and Other Poems*.
- **1866:** *The Prince’s Progress*. A narrative poem written in dialogue with quest and fairy-tale forms; its delayed prince and dead princess reverse the expected completion of romance.
- **1866:** *The Iniquity of the Fathers upon the Children*. A long dramatic narrative about illegitimacy and social judgment, collected in *The Prince’s Progress and Other Poems*.
- **1866:** *Jessie Cameron*. A narrative ballad whose conclusion remains deliberately unresolved.
- **1866:** *The Lowest Room*. A dialogic poem about ambition, gender, and renunciation.
- **1881:** *A Royal Princess*. A political dramatic monologue concerned with famine, privilege, and revolutionary violence.
- **1881:** *An Old-World Thicket*. A visionary nature poem that moves from sensuous landscape to an explicitly eschatological vision.

### Fiction and mixed prose-and-verse works

- **1849–1850; first published 1897:** *Maude: A Story for Girls*. A novella interspersed with poems and closely concerned with female authorship. Rossetti completed it when young but withheld it; the posthumous text was edited by William Michael Rossetti, so its first public manifestation was not author-supervised.
- **1852–1870; collected 1870:** *Commonplace*. A novella or long domestic story and the principal narrative in *Commonplace and Other Short Stories*.
- **1850s–1860s; collected 1870:** *The Lost Titian*. A short story about an imaginary lost painting; Rossetti explicitly denied that it recorded an actual art-historical tradition.
- **1850s–1860s; collected 1870:** *Nick*. A short story; its slight scale makes it easy to lose when the containing volume is treated as a single “book.”
- **1850s–1860s; collected 1870:** *Hero*. A short story and independent constituent of *Commonplace and Other Short Stories*.
- **1850s–1860s; collected 1870:** *Vanna’s Twins*. A short story set within Rossetti’s recurrent Anglo-Italian imaginative world.
- **1850s–1860s; collected 1870:** *A Safe Investment*. A short story and separate fictional obra.
- **before 1870:** *Pros and Cons*. A purpose-written short story; Rossetti stated that it had a particular didactic object.
- **completed by 1870:** *The Waves of This Troublesome World*. A short story written for a particular didactic purpose and acknowledged by Rossetti as potentially “too childish.”
- **1870:** *Commonplace and Other Short Stories*. The author-published container joining eight previously composed narratives. It is a collection-manifestation as well as a made arrangement, not a ninth story.
- **1874:** *Speaking Likenesses*. A framed children’s fantasy comprising three cautionary dreamlike tales associated with Flora, Edith, and Maggie. Because Arthur Hughes’s illustrations participate in its structure, the published book approaches a verbal-visual Gesamtkunstwerk, though authorship of image and text remains divided.
- **1874:** *Flora’s tale in Speaking Likenesses*. The first of the book’s three embedded moral fantasies, concerning a disastrous birthday feast; the original volume supplies no stable independent title.
- **1874:** *Edith’s tale in Speaking Likenesses*. The second embedded fantasy, concerning failed fire-making; it has no fixed independent title and is most accurately described rather than falsely titled.
- **1874:** *Maggie’s tale in Speaking Likenesses*. The third embedded fantasy, a dangerous forest journey ending in promised safety; it likewise resists treatment as a separately titled story.

### Devotional protocols and meditative books

- **1874:** *Annus Domini: A Prayer for Each Day of the Year, Founded on a Text of Holy Scripture*. A calendrical protocol of 366 short prayers, each attached to scripture. It is both a devotional book and an instruction for repeated daily practice.
- **1879:** *Seek and Find: A Double Series of Short Studies of the Benedicite*. A devotional and exegetical study organized in two series around the biblical canticle; its method joins natural observation to scriptural meditation.
- **1881:** *Called to Be Saints: The Minor Festivals Devotionally Studied*. A devotional calendar of saints and lesser church festivals, combining prose meditation and verse.
- **1883:** *Letter and Spirit: Notes on the Commandments*. A devotional commentary that reads the Ten Commandments through the two commandments of love; “notes” understates its status as an extended theological obra.
- **1885:** *Time Flies: A Reading Diary*. A day-by-day book of prose reflections and poems. Although called a diary, it is not simply a private journal: it is a composed calendrical reading protocol intended for publication.
- **1892:** *The Face of the Deep: A Devotional Commentary on the Apocalypse*. A verse-by-verse prose commentary on Revelation interspersed with poems. Its fusion of exegesis, prayer, lyric, and daily spiritual practice resists the separation of “prose book” from poetic collection.

### Essays, biographies, and scholarly projects

- **1857–1863:** *Biographical entries for the Imperial Dictionary of Universal Biography*. A dispersed body of signed and attributed reference articles on Italian literary, religious, and political figures. The complete extent of her contribution is bibliographically difficult to isolate, so this body of work resists being converted into a confidently exhaustive title-list.
- **1857–1863:** *Leopardi*. A concise critical-biographical dictionary article on Giacomo Leopardi and one of the securely identified constituents of Rossetti’s reference-writing project.
- **1857–1863:** *Petrarca, Francesco*. A critical-biographical dictionary article on Petrarch, including Rossetti’s engagement with the historical identity of Laura.
- **1867:** *Dante, an English Classic*. A literary-critical essay published in the *Churchman’s Shilling Magazine and Family Treasury*.
- **1882–1884, unfinished/withdrawn:** *Project to trace allusions to Dante, Petrarch, and Boccaccio in Spenser*. Rossetti undertook this scholarly annotation for Alexander B. Grosart’s edition of Spenser but withdrew because of ill health. It is a project rather than a completed obra, and the surviving contribution should not be inflated into a finished book.
- **1884:** *Dante: The Poet Illustrated out of the Poem*. A literary-critical essay published in *The Century*, interpreting Dante through his own writings.
- **1866:** *Outlines for Illuminating: “Consider.” A Poem*. A separately issued text designed as a pattern for manuscript illumination. It sits between poem, craft prompt, and protocol: the verbal text invites another maker to complete its material manifestation.

### Editorial, excerpting, and commemorative manifestations

- **1893:** *The Rossetti Birthday Book*. A calendar anthology of extracts compiled from Christina Rossetti’s writings by her mother, Frances Rossetti. Its words derive from Christina’s oeuvre, but the selection and birthday-book structure are not Christina’s obra.
- **1896:** *New Poems* as an editorial book-object. William Michael Rossetti assembled this volume after Christina’s death; it should therefore be cited as evidence for individual poems without silently attributing its editorial architecture to her.
- **1897:** *Maude* as a published book-object. The narrative is Christina’s early work, while its posthumous editing, portrait frontispiece, and publication are later manifestations overseen by William Michael Rossetti.
- **1898–2004:** *Collected letters as edited volumes*. Rossetti made the individual letters as acts of correspondence, not as a single intended literary work. The later *Family Letters* and four-volume *Collected Letters* are scholarly manifestations of that archive and should not be mistaken for author-designed books.
- **1840s–1894:** *Surviving correspondence*. Thousands of practical, familial, literary, and devotional communications survive unevenly. They resist classification as a unified “work”: each letter is an action addressed to a particular recipient, while the corpus exists only through preservation and later editing.

### Works made partly by others or not made by Rossetti

- **1848–1877:** *Portraits for which Rossetti sat*. Christina Rossetti acted as model for works by Dante Gabriel Rossetti and others, including *The Girlhood of Mary Virgin*. These are manifestations of her appearance but not works she made, and therefore do not belong to her oeuvre except as collaborations of pose and presence.
- **1862 and 1866:** *Illustrations and bindings for Goblin Market and The Prince’s Progress*. Dante Gabriel Rossetti made the visual designs. The resulting books are collaborative objects, but attribution of the pictures to Christina would be false.
- **1872 and 1874:** *Illustrations for Sing-Song and Speaking Likenesses*. Arthur Hughes made the images. Christina created the verbal structures and participated in producing the books, but the drawings are Hughes’s works.
- **after 1894:** *Musical settings of Rossetti’s poems*. Composers transformed her poems into hymns, songs, and carols, notably settings of “In the Bleak Midwinter.” The underlying texts are Rossetti’s; the musical works are creations by the composers and are not silently absorbed into her oeuvre.
