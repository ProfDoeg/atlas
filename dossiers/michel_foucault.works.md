## Works by Paul-Michel Foucault

### Authored books and book-length interventions

- **1954:** *Maladie mentale et personnalité*. A monograph on mental illness whose theoretical framework Foucault later rejected and substantially rewrote; it should not be conflated with the revised book of 1962.

- **1961; revised 1972:** *Folie et déraison: Histoire de la folie à l’âge classique*. Foucault’s principal doctoral thesis and first large archaeological history; the 1972 *Histoire de la folie à l’âge classique* is a materially revised manifestation, while the 1964 abridgment and foreign translations are editions rather than separate obras.

- **1962:** *Maladie mentale et psychologie*. A radical rewriting of *Maladie mentale et personnalité*, not merely its second edition, although publishers and bibliographies have treated the relationship inconsistently.

- **1963:** *Naissance de la clinique: Une archéologie du regard médical*. A monograph reconstructing the historical formation of clinical perception and medical discourse.

- **1963:** *Raymond Roussel*. A literary-critical monograph that also functions as an experiment in language, death, repetition, and authorship; its English title, *Death and the Labyrinth*, names a translation, not another work.

- **1966:** *Les Mots et les Choses: Une archéologie des sciences humaines*. An archaeological history of the epistemic arrangements within which the modern human sciences became possible.

- **1969:** *L’Archéologie du savoir*. A methodological book that revises and formalizes procedures employed in Foucault’s earlier histories without establishing a permanent protocol he continued to obey unchanged.

- **1970–1971:** *L’Ordre du discours*. Foucault’s inaugural lecture at the Collège de France, delivered on 2 December 1970 and published as a book in 1971; it is simultaneously an institutional speech-act, a manifesto for a research chair, and a compact methodological essay.

- **1975:** *Surveiller et punir: Naissance de la prison*. A genealogical history of punishment and disciplinary power, designed as a book rather than assembled from the contemporaneous prison-activist documents.

- **1976:** *Histoire de la sexualité I: La Volonté de savoir*. The first installment of an announced six-volume project; the advertised sequence was abandoned, so this is both a completed obra and the surviving entrance to an unrealized project.

- **1984:** *Histoire de la sexualité II: L’Usage des plaisirs*. A completed monograph produced after Foucault recast the history-of-sexuality project around ancient practices of subjectivation.

- **1984:** *Histoire de la sexualité III: Le Souci de soi*. The companion volume to *L’Usage des plaisirs*, published shortly before Foucault’s death.

- **c. 1980–1982; published 2018:** *Histoire de la sexualité IV: Les Aveux de la chair*. A substantially completed manuscript on early Christianity, edited posthumously. Its status is disputed because Foucault requested that there be no posthumous publications and did not authorize this final editorial manifestation.

- **1978, unfinished; published 2025:** *Les Hermaphrodites*. An incomplete, originally untitled manuscript connected with the abandoned architecture of *Histoire de la sexualité*; its supplied title and conversion into a book are editorial acts, so it resists identification as a finished work authorized by Foucault.

### Documentary books and collaborative investigations

- **1973:** *Moi, Pierre Rivière, ayant égorgé ma mère, ma sœur et mon frère…*. An archival dossier collectively assembled by Foucault and a seminar group around a nineteenth-century parricide memoir, medical reports, and judicial records. Foucault presented and partly authored the object but did not author most of its documents.

- **1976:** *Les Machines à guérir: Aux origines de l’hôpital moderne*. A collective historical dossier by Foucault, Blandine Barret-Kriegel, Anne Thalamy, François Beguin, and Bruno Fortier; attribution to Foucault alone would erase how it was made.

- **1978:** *Herculine Barbin, dite Alexina B.*. An editorial assemblage of Barbin’s memoir, medical and legal documents, and Foucault’s framing materials. Its documentary subject is not Foucault’s creation, and the volume therefore resists being called simply “a book by Foucault.”

- **1980:** *L’Impossible Prison: Recherches sur le système pénitentiaire au XIXe siècle*. A collective historical discussion edited by Michelle Perrot around Jacques-Guy Petit’s research, with Foucault’s interventions; it is a collaborative manifestation rather than a sole-authored monograph.

- **1982:** *Le Désordre des familles: Lettres de cachet des Archives de la Bastille*. A documentary montage made with Arlette Farge from eighteenth-century archival petitions. The historical voices belong to petitioners and officials; Farge and Foucault selected, ordered, and interpreted them.

### Courses at the Collège de France

- **1970–1971:** *Leçons sur la volonté de savoir*. A lecture course, preserved through preparatory manuscripts and auditors’ evidence and constituted posthumously as a critical edition; it was not a book completed by Foucault.

- **1971–1972:** *Théories et institutions pénales*. A course tracing punitive institutions and political repression; its published form is an editorial reconstruction from Foucault’s working materials.

- **1972–1973:** *La Société punitive*. A course on the formation of the carceral system and capitalist discipline, distinct from though preparatory to *Surveiller et punir*.

- **1973–1974:** *Le Pouvoir psychiatrique*. A spoken course on psychiatric power, surviving through recordings and notes; the audio event and the posthumous printed edition are different manifestations.

- **1974–1975:** *Les Anormaux*. A course on expertise, monstrosity, correction, and abnormality, posthumously edited from recordings and preparatory material.

- **1975–1976:** *« Il faut défendre la société »*. A course on war as a grid for historical and political analysis, race struggle, state racism, and biopower; the title reproduces a recurrent proposition rather than a lifetime-published book title.

- **1976–1977:** No annual course was delivered because Foucault took a sabbatical. This absence matters: there is no missing course that should be supplied merely to complete the sequence.

- **1977–1978:** *Sécurité, territoire, population*. A lecture course in which an inquiry into security mechanisms develops into a history of governmentality.

- **1978–1979:** *Naissance de la biopolitique*. A lecture course chiefly investigating liberal and neoliberal governmental reason; its conventional title can misleadingly suggest a completed treatise on biopolitics.

- **1979–1980:** *Du gouvernement des vivants*. A course on government through truth, confession, and early Christian practices.

- **1980–1981:** *Subjectivité et vérité*. A course on subjectivation, sexual conduct, and ancient arts of living.

- **1981–1982:** *L’Herméneutique du sujet*. A course on care of the self, conversion, askēsis, and truth; the familiar book is a posthumous transcript-based manifestation.

- **1982–1983:** *Le Gouvernement de soi et des autres*. A course on Kant, enlightenment, political parrēsia, and the government of self and others.

- **1983–1984:** *Le Courage de la vérité: Le Gouvernement de soi et des autres II*. Foucault’s final course, concerning parrēsia and Cynic life; illness interrupted its wider development into a finished publication.

### Courses and lecture projects outside the Collège de France

- **1951:** *La magie – Le fait social total*. Preparatory notes for an École normale supérieure lecture on ethnology, published only from the archive; they are working material rather than an authorized independent work.

- **1952–1954:** *Binswanger et l’analyse existentielle*. A course-and-manuscript complex on existential analysis, issued posthumously in 2021. The modern title gathers materials Foucault did not fashion into a lifetime book.

- **1953–1954:** *Phénoménologie et psychologie*. Early lectures reconstructed from manuscripts and student evidence and published in 2021; their editorial book-form should not be projected backward onto the teaching event.

- **1954–1955:** *Problèmes de l’anthropologie / La Question anthropologique*. An École normale lecture project known partly through Jacques Lagrange’s student notes and related manuscripts; *La Question anthropologique* is the title of its 2022 editorial manifestation.

- **1964:** *La Sexualité*. A course delivered at Clermont-Ferrand, preserved in manuscript and published posthumously together with related Vincennes material.

- **1969:** *Le Discours de la sexualité*. A Vincennes course and associated working texts, now paired editorially with *La Sexualité*; it is not one of the originally advertised volumes of *Histoire de la sexualité*.

- **1966:** *Le Discours philosophique*. A completed but unpublished manuscript examining philosophy’s relation to truth, language, history, and its speaking subject; its 2023 book-form is posthumous and unauthorized by Foucault.

- **1950s–1970s; published 2024:** *Nietzsche: Cours, conférences et travaux*. An editorial constellation of courses, conferences, and manuscripts from different occasions, including Vincennes, Buffalo, Montréal, and Brazil. It is a body of work around Nietzsche, not a single book Foucault composed under this title.

- **1973:** *La vérité et les formes juridiques*. Five lectures delivered at the Pontifical Catholic University of Rio de Janeiro; their first publication in Portuguese and later French collection are textual manifestations of spoken events.

- **1974:** *La médecine sociale / La naissance de la médecine sociale*. A sequence of lectures in Rio de Janeiro on medicalization, public health, and social medicine, later printed separately rather than designed as a book.

- **1975:** *The Psychiatrization of Society and Antipsychiatry*. A São Paulo lecture series known from recordings and archival traces; its unstable translated title and incomplete publication history make it particularly resistant to treatment as a fixed text.

- **1980:** *Truth and Subjectivity*. Two Howison Lectures at Berkeley; their argument substantially overlaps the Dartmouth lectures, making separate-work status arguable rather than self-evident.

- **1980:** *L’Origine de l’herméneutique de soi*. Two English-language Dartmouth lectures, originally titled *Truth and Subjectivity* and *Christianity and Confession*, later constituted as a French critical edition.

- **1981:** *Mal faire, dire vrai: Fonction de l’aveu en justice*. A Louvain lecture course on confession and juridical truth, published from recordings and related materials rather than from an authored book manuscript.

- **1982:** *Dire vrai sur soi-même*. A Toronto lecture cycle on Greco-Roman askēsis and Christian self-examination, posthumously constituted as a book.

- **1983:** *La Parrêsia*. A lecture delivered at Grenoble whose posthumous edition places it before the Berkeley cycle; it is a related but distinct spoken intervention.

- **1983:** *Discours et vérité*. Six Berkeley lectures on parrēsia. Foucault delivered them in English, and the familiar French text is a posthumous translation and critical reconstruction.

### Essays, criticism, interviews, broadcasts, and public speech

- **1954:** *Introduction* to Ludwig Binswanger’s *Le Rêve et l’existence*. A long philosophical introduction to Foucault’s French translation, substantial enough to function as an early essay but materially attached to another author’s book.

- **1954–1988; collected 1994:** *Dits et écrits*. A posthumous four-volume editorial manifestation containing 364 numbered articles, prefaces, reviews, interviews, lectures, discussions, and other public texts. It is not one work Foucault made, while its constituents are not interchangeable “essays”; its editors included material previously made public and excluded much private or unpublished archival writing.

- **1961–1983; collected 2024:** *Entretiens radiophoniques*. A posthumous audio/text collection of radio appearances. Each broadcast was an event involving interviewers, producers, and institutions, while the collection itself was made by later editors.

- **1966:** *Le Corps utopique*. A France Culture radio lecture: a vocal and broadcast manifestation, subsequently transcribed and published, not originally a book.

- **1966:** *Les Hétérotopies*. A companion radio lecture whose later textual life led to the essay commonly called *Des espaces autres*; the broadcast, 1967 lecture, 1984 publication, and later book editions should not be silently collapsed into one object.

- **1969:** *Qu’est-ce qu’un auteur?*. A lecture and ensuing discussion before the Société française de philosophie. The oral event, printed proceedings, and revised versions are distinct manifestations of a text that questions precisely the unity implied by attaching works to an author’s proper name.

- **1971:** *Nietzsche, la généalogie, l’histoire*. An essay made for a memorial volume for Jean Hyppolite; later anthology appearances are editions, not new works.

- **1971:** *Human Nature: Justice versus Power*. A televised debate with Noam Chomsky. It is a jointly produced audiovisual event rather than a sole-authored Foucault text, although transcripts circulate as such.

- **1972:** *Les intellectuels et le pouvoir*. A conversation with Gilles Deleuze; authorship is dialogical, and the printed transcript is a manifestation of the exchange.

- **1977:** *La vie des hommes infâmes*. A programmatic essay introducing an intended anthology of archival lives. The anthology-project remained unrealized, so the project—not a nonexistent finished collection—is the work.

- **1978:** *Reportages d’idées* and the Iran dispatches. A journalistic-philosophical project undertaken for *Corriere della sera*, including Foucault’s contested reports on the Iranian uprising. The larger “reporting of ideas” series was only partly realized, and its political interpretation remains disputed.

- **1978:** *Inutile de se soulever?*. A newspaper intervention reflecting on revolt after the Iranian Revolution; it acts within a live political controversy and is closer to a public manifesto than to detached historical description.

- **1980:** *Le philosophe masqué*. An interview originally published without Foucault’s name; the deliberate anonymity makes resistance to ordinary authorial attribution part of the object itself.

- **1983–1984:** *Qu’est-ce que les Lumières?*. A cluster of lectures and essays rather than one stable text: Foucault repeatedly reworked Kant’s question in different settings, and French and English publications preserve non-identical versions.

- **1984:** *Face aux gouvernements, les droits de l’homme*. A brief public intervention associated with humanitarian action. It operates as a manifesto addressed to governments, not merely as commentary about rights.

### Activist protocols and collective political instruments

- **1971:** *Manifeste du Groupe d’information sur les prisons*. A founding declaration associated with Foucault, Jean-Marie Domenach, and Pierre Vidal-Naquet. It is a collective manifesto that helped create a practice of inquiry rather than a sole-authored philosophical essay.

- **1971–1972:** *Intolérable*, nos. 1–4. Four Groupe d’information sur les prisons inquiry-booklets concerning prisons, courts, asylum conditions, and prisoner testimony. They are collective instruments and documentary manifestations; assigning their anonymous or jointly produced contents individually to Foucault is not warranted.

- **1971–1972:** Groupe d’information sur les prisons questionnaires and inquiry protocols. These texts instructed prisoners and families to produce their own knowledge of confinement; they are protocols in the strict sense, but their collective and sometimes anonymous authorship prevents a definitive Foucault-only list.

- **1973:** *Groupe d’information sur les prisons: Archives d’une lutte, 1970–1972*. The archive and political practice preceded its later documentary editions. The GIP itself may be understood as a collective project or social apparatus, not as an obra owned by Foucault.

### Projects, unfinished objects, and archival remainder

- **1966–1970:** The Manet book project. Foucault planned a substantial study of Manet and delivered related lectures but never completed the announced book; surviving lectures and notes are evidence of a project, not authorization to invent a lost title or finished obra.

- **1976 onward:** The original six-volume *Histoire de la sexualité* project. Advertised volumes included *La Chair et le corps*, *La Croisade des enfants*, *La Femme, la mère et l’hystérique*, *Les Pervers*, and *Populations et races*. These were projected titles, not five completed missing books, and Foucault abandoned this architecture.

- **c. 1979–1984:** Research on parrēsia, truth-telling, and philosophical life. The late courses and lectures form a recognizable project, but Foucault’s death prevented its synthesis into a book; calling the project itself an obra preserves that incompletion.

- **1950s–1984:** Fonds Michel Foucault, BnF NAF 28730. The archive contains lecture drafts, reading notes, cards, manuscripts, and unfinished investigations. It is a material body of work but not a single authored text, and neither the archive catalogue nor any published bibliography establishes that every surviving leaf was intended as a work.

- **Posthumous editorial collections:** *La Grande Étrangère*, *Folie, langage, littérature*, *Généalogies de la sexualité*, and comparable thematic volumes. These are useful manifestations made by editors from lectures, interviews, and manuscripts, not additional lifetime obras; their components belong under their original occasions wherever those can be identified.

- **Indeterminate dates:** Petitions, open letters, signatures, translations, private recordings, correspondence, and attributed remarks. The principal chronological bibliography expressly excludes or only selectively records these classes, while collective authorship is often unresolved; consequently no responsible claim to a literally complete item-by-item inventory can be made from the standard published corpus.
