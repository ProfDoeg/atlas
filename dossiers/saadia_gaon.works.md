## Works by Saadia Gaon

### Lexicography, grammar, and mnemonic poetry

- **c. 902 or 912–913:** *Sefer ha-Agron* (“Book of the Collection”). A Hebrew lexicon and rhyming dictionary intended especially for poets; Saadia later produced a revised, Arabic-framed recension titled *Kitāb uṣūl al-shiʿr al-ʿibrānī*. The date varies because the sources disagree over whether he was born in 882 or 892, while agreeing that he made it at about twenty. [Malter, pp. 137–38, 306–14](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown:** *Kitāb faṣīḥ lughat al-ʿibrāniyyīn* / *Kutub al-Lugha* (“Book[s] of Eloquent Hebrew,” later *Sefer Ẓaḥut ha-Lashon*). A twelve-part grammatical project covering letters, vowels, inflection, conjugation, and related problems; it survives only in fragments, so its exact title and whether the twelve parts constituted one work or a series remain uncertain. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 138–40, 314–25](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown:** *Tafsīr al-Sabʿīn Lafẓa al-Mufrada* / *Pitron Shivʿim Millim* (“Explanation of Seventy Isolated Words”). A philological and polemical glossary—actually treating roughly ninety rare biblical words—whose appeal to Mishnaic Hebrew also acts as an argument for the necessity of oral tradition. The transmitted text may be a detached fragment of a larger work. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 140–41, 325–29](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown:** *Shir shel ha-Otiyyot* (“Poem of the Letters”). A cycle of mnemonic Hebrew couplets encoding the number of occurrences of each letter in the Bible; it resists separation into “poem” and “reference tool,” since its verse is itself a protocol for remembering Masoretic statistics. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

### Biblical translation and commentary

- **Before 942; composed in stages:** *Tafsīr al-Tawrāt* (“Interpretation of the Torah”). A Judeo-Arabic translation-paraphrase of the Pentateuch accompanied, in its learned form, by introductions and extensive commentary; its popular and scholarly recensions cannot always be cleanly separated in the surviving witnesses. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 141–46, 316–25](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Before 942; completeness disputed:** *Arabic translation and commentary on the Prophets*. This was part of Saadia’s larger Bible project; substantial material survives for Isaiah, while evidence for other prophetic books is fragmentary or indirect. The claim that he translated the entire Bible is plausible but cannot be verified from the surviving manuscripts. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 141–46, 316–25](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Before 942:** *Tafsīr Kitāb Ishāʿyā* (“Commentary on Isaiah”). A Judeo-Arabic translation and exegetical commentary, surviving incompletely. It is a component of the Bible project but circulated sufficiently independently to count as a distinct textual object. [Malter, pp. 142–45, 316–25](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Before 942:** *Kitāb al-Tasābīḥ* (“Book of Praises”: translation and commentary on Psalms). A Judeo-Arabic translation with an extensive introduction and commentary; the work survives substantially but not without textual losses. [Malter, pp. 142–45, 316–25](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Before 942:** *Translation and commentary on Proverbs*. A Judeo-Arabic exegetical work surviving in manuscript witnesses and fragments; its precise original title is not securely known. [Malter, pp. 142–45, 316–25](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Before 942:** *Translation and commentary on Job*. A Judeo-Arabic work whose introduction develops a sustained account of providence, suffering, and divine justice; it is therefore simultaneously exegesis and philosophical argument. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Before 942:** *Translation and commentary on Daniel*. A Judeo-Arabic exegetical work whose introduction attacks astrology and divination and distinguishes them from prophecy; it survives substantially. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Before 942; surviving unevenly:** *Translations and commentaries on the Five Scrolls*. Saadia’s treatments of Song of Songs, Ruth, Lamentations, Ecclesiastes, and Esther belong to the wider Bible project, but their textual survival and attribution are uneven; Esther’s commentary is also known as *Kitāb al-Īnās bi-l-Jalwa* (“Book of Consolation in Exile”). [Malter, pp. 141–46, 316–25](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; attribution traditional:** *Arabic translation and introduction to Megillat Antiochus*. A translation of the post-biblical Hanukkah narrative rather than part of the Hebrew Bible proper; its attribution to Saadia is transmitted in later witnesses and is less secure than that of his principal biblical commentaries. [Malter, pp. 394–403](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; attribution disputed:** *Commentary on the Ten Commandments*. A midrashic-exegetical composition transmitted under Saadia’s name; modern cataloguing and editions retain the attribution cautiously rather than treating it as certain. [Malter, pp. 394–403](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

### Philosophy and cosmology

- **931:** *Tafsīr Kitāb al-Mabādiʾ* (“Commentary on the Book of Principles,” i.e. *Sefer Yetzirah*). An Arabic philosophical commentary that also transmits Saadia’s distinctive recension of *Sefer Yetzirah*; it was still being completed in 931. It is commentary, textual edition, and rationalizing cosmology at once. [Georges Vajda, “Le Commentaire de Saadia sur le Séfer Yeçîra”](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rjuiv_0484-8616_1941_num_106_6_1247); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **933:** *Kitāb al-Amānāt wa-l-Iʿtiqādāt* (“Book of Trustworthy Doctrines and Beliefs,” later *Sefer Emunot ve-Deʿot*). A systematic work of Jewish philosophical theology and polemic, completed in 933 and surviving essentially intact. It is not merely descriptive philosophy: it is an apologetic project meant to stabilize belief and counter rival doctrines. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 174–260, 355–80](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

### Halakhic monographs and protocols

- **Date unknown:** *Kitāb al-Mawārīth* / *Sefer ha-Yerushot* (“Book of Inheritance”). A systematic Judeo-Arabic legal protocol governing succession; it is the most extensively preserved of Saadia’s halakhic monographs, although the surviving form may be an abridgment lacking the original’s proofs. [British Library, Or 9860](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-003391636); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Book on Gifts*. A halakhic monograph specifying how gifted property is legally acquired; only citations and fragments identify the work. [Malter, pp. 157–67, 341–51](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Book on Purchase and Commercial Transactions*. A systematic legal monograph on transfers by sale, known principally through references and fragments; its original Arabic title is not securely recoverable. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 157–67, 341–51](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Sefer ha-ʿEdut ve-ha-Shetarot* (“Book of Testimony and Legal Instruments”). A juridical protocol concerning evidence, documents, and deeds, surviving through excerpts and Genizah fragments; older scholarship sometimes shortened its designation to *Sefer ha-Shetarot*. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Book on Defilement and Purity*. A halakhic monograph or compendium on ritual impurity and purification; citations leave uncertain whether it was one independent work or part of a larger purity project. [Malter, pp. 341–51](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Treatise on the Laws of Ritual Slaughter*. A practical legal protocol on *sheḥitah*, preserved only in part. References sometimes associate it with a companion treatment of prohibited or defective animals. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Treatise on Terefot*. A halakhic protocol concerning animals rendered impermissible by mortal defects; its independence from the slaughter treatise is not entirely clear. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Treatise on Niddah*. A halakhic work on menstrual impurity and related law, known as part of Saadia’s wider purity corpus rather than from a complete manuscript. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Treatise on Forbidden Unions*. A legal monograph on incest and prohibited sexual relationships; its transmitted title and boundaries are uncertain. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown; fragmentary:** *Treatise on Festivals and the New Moon*. A protocol of festival law and calendrical proclamation; it may overlap with, but is not necessarily identical to, Saadia’s polemical calendar writings. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown:** *Commentary on the Thirteen Hermeneutical Rules of Rabbi Ishmael*. A methodological guide to rabbinic interpretation, preserved chiefly in a medieval Hebrew translation attributed to Naḥum ha-Maʿaravi rather than in Saadia’s Arabic original. [Malter, pp. 157–67, 341–51](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; title and extent uncertain:** *Introduction to the Talmud* / *Kelale ha-Talmud*. A methodological teaching text known from quotations and later notices; whether those notices all refer to one independent book is disputed. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Before 942; individually various:** *Responsa*. A dispersed body of legal rulings made in answer to questions from individuals and communities; approximately fifty have been collected under Saadia’s name, but individual authenticity remains to be critically established. The corpus resists treatment as a single authored “book,” because its units were occasioned communications later gathered by editors. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

### Liturgy and liturgical poetry

- **Date unknown, before 942:** *Kitāb Jāmiʿ al-Ṣalawāt wa-l-Tasābīḥ* (“Collection of All Prayers and Praises,” commonly *Siddur Rav Saadia Gaon*). A year-round prayer book combining prescribed prayers, Judeo-Arabic rubrics and legal explanations, and poetry; it is therefore both anthology and protocol for liturgical action, not simply a book authored line by line by Saadia. Its surviving manuscript is incomplete, and modern editions add poems from other witnesses. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 146–57, 329–41](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Before his gaonate, probably before 928:** *Azharot*. A Hebrew liturgical poem enumerating the 613 commandments for Shavuot; its acrostic identifies Saadia as *alluf*, supporting composition before he became gaon. Later publication as a “Book of Commandments” can obscure that the made thing was originally a performative poem. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Yad Izhak Ben-Zvi edition](https://books.ybz.org.il/en/product/the-book-of-commandments-of-saadia-gaon/)

- **Date unknown:** *Bakkashot*. A body of Hebrew supplicatory poems rather than one fixed book; medieval praise and Genizah discoveries establish the corpus, but its exact membership remains textually fluid. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown:** *Hoshanot*. A cycle or corpus of processional liturgical poems for Sukkot, especially Hoshana Rabbah; manuscript transmission does not furnish a single stable authorial collection. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown:** *Thirteen penitential poems for Yom Kippur*. A performative liturgical cycle preserved especially in Yemenite usage; the individual poems belong to worship rather than to a securely bounded authorial volume. [Malter, pp. 146–57, 329–41](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown:** *Barekhi Nafshi* (“Bless, My Soul”). A philosophical hymn for the Day of Atonement, joining speculative theology to liturgical performance. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown:** *Im lefi beḥirkha* (“If by Your Choice”). A Hebrew *tokheḥah*, or poem of moral reproof, articulating ideas also found in *Beliefs and Opinions*. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Date unknown; corpus incompletely delimited:** *Other piyyutim*. Genizah manuscripts attribute to Saadia additional *kerovot*, *seliḥot*, *kinot*, hymns, and philosophical poems. This is a body of work rather than a single obra, and individual attributions require manuscript-level scrutiny. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

### Calendar, chronology, and communal intervention

- **921–922:** *Letters concerning the Ben Meir calendar controversy*. A series of interventions addressed to Ben Meir, Babylonian leaders, and communities to prevent rival festival dates; these texts act rather than merely describe, functioning as communal directives or manifestos. Their number, authorship shares, and surviving wording are not entirely secure because the controversy’s dossier includes collaborative and later-copied documents. [Malter, pp. 69–88, 409–19](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **922–923:** *Sefer ha-Moʿadim* (“Book of Festivals”). A Hebrew calendrical polemic commissioned amid the dispute with Aaron ben Meir; it defended the Babylonian reckoning and recorded the controversy. Only fragments survive. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 168–71, 351–53](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown:** *Kitāb al-ʿIbbūr* / *Seder ha-ʿIbbur* (“Book/Order of Intercalation”). A calendrical protocol explaining calculation and intercalation; references and Genizah fragments do not establish its complete original form. [Malter, pp. 168–71, 351–53](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; known from a posthumous list:** *Iqāmat al-ʿIbbūr*. A calendrical work named in the *fihrist* of Saadia’s writings compiled by his sons; it is otherwise unattested, so its relation to *Kitāb al-ʿIbbūr* cannot presently be determined. [Cambridge University Library, T-S 10G5.7](https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/departments/taylor-schechter-genizah-research-unit/fragment-month/fotm-2023/fragment-6)

- **Date unknown:** *Kitāb al-Taʾrīkh* (“Book of Chronology”). A seven-part universal chronology from Creation to Saadia’s own time. Once transmitted anonymously and consequently disputed, its attribution gained support from an explicitly ascribed opening fragment; the final section nevertheless contains later alterations or additions. [Malter, pp. 171–73, 353–55](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

### Polemics and apologetic manifestos

- **905, according to one early chronology; identification uncertain:** *Responsum or refutation addressed to Ananites*. An early anti-Karaite polemic associated with Anan ben David’s followers; the surviving notices do not establish whether it is identical with the later-cited *Kitāb al-Radd ʿalā ʿAnān*. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 380–94](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Before 926:** *Three anti-Karaite “Books of Refutation”*. Later sources distinguish three works called *Kitāb al-Radd*, directed against Karaite authorities or doctrines; they survive only through citations, and the opponents and boundaries of the individual books cannot always be recovered securely. At least one is conventionally identified as the refutation of Anan. [Malter, pp. 380–94](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **926:** *Kitāb al-Tamyīz* / *Sefer ha-Hakkarah* (“Book of Discernment”). Saadia’s largest anti-Karaite polemic, a manifesto for Rabbanite interpretive authority; the work itself is lost apart from fragments and quotations in later authors. [Malter, pp. 380–88](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown:** *Refutation of Ḥiwi al-Balkhi*. A polemical answer to Ḥiwi’s rationalist objections to Scripture, reconstructed from Genizah fragments and later citations; its original title and complete extent are unknown. [Malter, pp. 388–90](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; existence uncertain:** *Polemic against Ben Zuṭa*. Saadia’s Torah commentary mentions a controversy with Ben Zuṭa, but the evidence does not prove that a separate work ever existed. This resists being called an obra at all: it may represent only an episode or passage within another text. [Malter, pp. 380–94](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **Date unknown; lost:** *Kitāb al-Sharāʾiʿ* (“Book of Religious Laws”). A polemical or theological treatment of the commandments known through bibliographical notices; its contents and relationship to Saadia’s halakhic works remain uncertain. [Malter, pp. 380–94](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

- **932; revised c. 935–936:** *Sefer ha-Galui* / Arabic *Kitāb al-Ṭarīd*. An autobiographical-apologetic manifesto defending Saadia during his conflict with Exilarch David ben Zakkai and Aaron ibn Sargado. The first version was ornate Hebrew; the later manifestation added an Arabic introduction and commentary and perhaps revised the Hebrew text, so “the work” is a layered project rather than one stable document. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon); [Malter, pp. 390–94](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)

### Letters and institutional texts

- **During his gaonate, 928–942:** *Two circular letters to the Jews of Egypt*. Communal letters seeking support for the Sura academy and asserting Saadia’s leadership; the second is structured as an extended admonition. They are institutional acts rather than detached literary compositions. [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/saadiah-ben-joseph-gaon)

- **Before 942; dates various:** *Surviving personal, scholarly, and communal correspondence*. Genizah fragments and quotations preserve additional letters, including exchanges about calendar calculation, biblical interpretation, and academy affairs. This is an open documentary corpus, not a fixed work, and attribution must be assessed item by item. [Malter, pp. 69–134, 394–419](https://archive.org/download/saadiagaonhis00malt/saadiagaonhis00malt.pdf)
