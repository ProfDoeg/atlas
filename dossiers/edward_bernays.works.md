## Works by Edward Louis Bernays

### Books and edited obras

- **1917:** *The Broadway Anthology*. A jointly made literary anthology credited to Bernays, Samuel Hoffenstein, Walter J. Kingsley, and Murdock Pemberton; Bernays was therefore co-compiler rather than sole author. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1923:** *Crystallizing Public Opinion*. A theoretical and practical book defining the emerging work of the public-relations counsel. Some later references date it 1924, apparently following a later printing; the standard bibliography and the Library of Congress book file identify the first publication as 1923. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf), [Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1927:** *An Outline of Careers: A Practical Guide to Achievement by Thirty-Eight Eminent Americans*. An edited career handbook and composite obra: Bernays assembled the contributions, wrote the introduction, and supplied its chapter on public relations. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf))

- **1928:** *Propaganda*. A book of theory and professional protocol, describing how organized persuasion can shape politics, commerce, education, art, and social life. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf), [Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1939:** *Careers for Men: A Practical Guide to Opportunity in Business, Written by Thirty-Eight Successful Americans*. A revised manifestation of the 1927 career anthology, not an entirely independent new work; Bernays remained its editor and a contributor. ([Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1940:** *Speak Up for Democracy: What You Can Do—A Practical Plan of Action for Every American Citizen*. A book-length civic protocol intended to produce democratic advocacy rather than merely describe it. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf), [Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1945:** *Take Your Place at the Peace Table: What You Can Do to Win a Lasting United Nations Peace*. A handbook and manifesto for citizen action in support of a postwar international organization; sources variously name International Press or Gerent Press as publisher. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf), [Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1952:** *Public Relations*. A book-length synthesis of Bernays’s history, theory, ethics, and professional procedures, distinct from his shorter 1945 vocational monograph of the same title. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf), [Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1955:** *The Engineering of Consent*. An edited, multi-author professional manual derived from Bernays’s 1947 essay; Bernays made the volume as editor and contributor, but did not author every chapter. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf), [Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1961:** *Your Future in Public Relations*. A vocational guide explaining the profession, its qualifications, and possible careers. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf), [Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1965:** *Biography of an Idea: Memoirs of Public Relations Counsel Edward L. Bernays*. An autobiography and retrospective construction of a professional life; its extensive client histories are evidence but also self-authored claims about Bernays’s own importance. The archive preserves a corrected final draft extending to at least 1,746 manuscript pages. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1970:** *The Case for Reappraisal of U.S. Overseas Information Policies and Programs*. An edited policy intervention rather than a solely authored monograph. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1979:** *Your Future in a Public Relations Career*. A revised successor to the 1961 vocational guide; its changed title and archival draft file identify a new manifestation, although much of the project continues the earlier book. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf), [Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1986:** *The Later Years: Public Relations Insights, 1956–1986*. A retrospective collection continuing the autobiographical and professional project beyond *Biography of an Idea*. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf), [Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

### Pamphlets, surveys, reports, and protocols

- **1927:** *A Public Relations Counsel*. A privately printed professional statement or pamphlet; it should not be confused with the periodical article “A Public Relations Counsel States His Views,” although the two belong to the same explanatory project. ([Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1927:** *The Verdict of Public Opinion on Propaganda*. A 49-page compilation of letters and responses, made from the reception of Bernays’s article “A Public Relations Counsel States His Views”; it is thus a documentary assemblage rather than a conventional authored book. ([Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Verdict_of_Public_Opinion_on_Propaga.html?id=ytTScQAACAAJ))

- **1937:** *Universities—Pathfinders in Public Opinion*. A 38-page survey made collaboratively with Doris E. Fleischman Bernays, documenting university teaching and research concerning public opinion and public relations. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf), [Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1940:** *Fighting the Fifth Column in the Americas: An Analysis and a Program*. A pamphlet that moves from diagnosis to an explicit anti-subversion program and therefore functions partly as protocol and manifesto. ([Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1941:** *Morale: The First Line of Defense*. An article or pamphlet-scale intervention on wartime morale, not a book; bibliographies identify its manifestation in the May 1941 *Infantry Journal*. ([U.S. Army Heritage bibliography](https://www.armyheritage.org/wp-content/uploads/ref-bibs/subjects/Morale/Morale%20Overview.pdf))

- **1942:** *The Future of Private Enterprise in the Post-War World*. A pamphlet project promoting a place for private enterprise after the war; the archive preserves associated correspondence rather than a conventional “book file.” ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/exist_collections/ead3pdf/mss/2003/ms003016.pdf))

- **1943:** *Democratic Leadership in Total War*. A wartime pamphlet or published address, best understood as a programmatic intervention rather than a monograph. ([Library of Congress biographical bibliography](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467))

- **1944:** *Psychological Blueprint for the Peace—Canada, U.S.A.* A planning document that treats postwar opinion as something to be designed; “blueprint” accurately signals a protocol or project rather than descriptive scholarship. ([Library of Congress biographical bibliography](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467))

- **1945:** *Public Relations*. A 23-page vocational monograph describing the profession; it is a separate made object from the much larger 1952 book with the same title. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf))

- **1945:** *Pamphlets Issued in the Public Interest*. A jointly issued pamphlet series by Edward and Doris Fleischman Bernays. The collective title names a serial project whose individual pieces are not consistently enumerated in the standard bibliographies, so it resists reduction to one stable text. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1946:** *Human Relations: The Way to Labor-Management Adjustments*. A published conference paper presented at Pennsylvania State College; it is an applied labor-relations protocol, not a general book. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1947:** *Your Career in Public Relations*. A vocational pamphlet or career text. Its publication details are incomplete in the consulted scholarly bibliography, so no publisher or format should be invented. ([Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1947:** *East Orange and the East-West Freeway: A Study with Recommendations*. A commissioned civic report: research joined to recommendations intended to influence an infrastructure decision. Its status is closer to a consultancy project embodied in a report than to a literary work. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1950:** *What the British Think of Us: A Study of British Hostility to America and Americans and Its Motivation, with Recommendations for Improving Anglo-American Relations*. A survey and policy report made with Doris E. Fleischman Bernays; its recommendations make it an operational project as well as a text. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467))

- **1950:** *How Business Can Sell the American Way of Life to the American People*. A programmatic pamphlet treating political-economic belief as the object of a public-relations campaign. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1958:** *The American Press and the Public: A Study and Recommendations*. A research report coupling survey evidence with recommendations; its object is institutional conduct rather than literary expression. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1972:** *The Future of Public Relations*. A printed manifestation of a talk delivered to the Rotary Club of New York on February 10, 1972. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1934:** *Preface to A Reference Guide to the Study of Public Opinion*. A prefatory contribution to Harwood L. Childs’s reference work, not authorship of the whole volume. ([Hallahan bibliography](https://hallahan.info/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/research-bernayspublishing.pdf))

- **1934:** *Public Relations*. A published conference address in George Bijur’s *Choosing a Career*, distinct from the 1927 chapter and the two later works of the same name. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf))

- **1935:** *Molding Public Opinion*. An article in the *Annals of the American Academy of Political and Social Science*, also appearing in a trade-periodical manifestation later that year. ### Public-relations projects and Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1913:** *Damaged Goods publicity campaign*. A project to protect and promote Eugène Brieux’s play about syphilis against censorship and police interference. Bernays made neither the play nor its text; what he made was a coalition, controversy, and publicity environment around it. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1915–1916:** *Ballets Russes American-tour publicity*. A publicity project joining performance, fashion, visual imagery, celebrity, and news; because the persuasive object existed across several media, it approaches a commercial Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single text. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1917–1918:** *Committee on Public Information work*. Bernays contributed to the United States’ wartime information apparatus and its Latin American operations. This was collaborative government propaganda, so neither the total campaign nor its numerous artifacts can be attributed to him alone. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1919:** *Employment campaign for returning servicemen*. A War Department public-relations project addressing the reintegration of veterans. It survives as an institutional undertaking, not as one stable authored text. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467))

- **1920s:** *Beech-Nut bacon-and-eggs breakfast campaign*. A commercial persuasion project using a physician survey and third-party authority to promote a “hearty” breakfast. Its familiar capsule story derives substantially from Bernays’s own retrospective accounts, so exact claims about his sole creation of an American breakfast custom should be treated cautiously. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1920s–1950s:** *Ivory Soap public-relations program*. A long-running Procter & Gamble project involving soap-sculpture competitions, schools, museums, community groups, and press coverage. It resists classification as a single campaign because it was a repeatable social and promotional apparatus spanning objects, events, institutions, and publicity. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/), [Museum of Public Relations](https://www.prmuseum.org/pioneer-edward-bernays))

- **1925:** *American participation in the Exposition internationale des arts décoratifs et industriels modernes*. Bernays served as assistant commissioner and worked on the American presence at the Paris exposition. The result was an institutional exhibition project, not a solely authored artwork or text. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1929:** *Torches of Freedom*. An American Tobacco publicity event in which women smoked during New York’s Easter parade under an emancipation slogan. It was a staged manifestation designed to become news; attribution to Bernays is secure, but the participating women, staff, photographers, journalists, and client also made the event. ([American Historical Association](https://www.historians.org/perspectives-article/creating-circumstances-edward-bernays-psychoanalysis-and-the-making-of-american-consumer-culture-march-2017/), [Museum of Public Relations](https://www.prmuseum.org/educator-exchange-classroom-guide-bernays-and-the-origins-of-moder-pr))

- **1929:** *Light’s Golden Jubilee*. A worldwide commemoration of the fiftieth anniversary of Edison’s electric lamp, incorporating ceremonies, broadcasts, printed matter, political participation, a commemorative stamp, and a coordinated moment of extinguishing and relighting. It is Bernays’s clearest Gesamtkunstwerk: a designed event distributed through technologies, institutions, bodies, and media, though General Electric and many collaborators shared its production. ([Library of Congress essay](https://lcweb2.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/LIGHTS-GOLDEN-JUBILEE.pdf), [Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1930s:** *Green Ball*. A charity ball whose fashion, décor, elite participation, retail displays, and press coverage were coordinated around green to support the promotion of Lucky Strike packaging. It was an event-based commercial Gesamtkunstwerk, not a written work. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1930s:** *American Tobacco anti-spitting campaign*. A public-health-themed publicity project whose relationship to cigarette promotion makes it difficult to call an autonomous social-service work. The campaign survives through publicity artifacts and client records rather than one fixed text. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1930–1932:** *President’s Emergency Committee for Employment public-relations program*. Bernays initiated and directed the committee’s public-relations section before expansion by a larger staff. It was collective government work, not a campaign he can be said to have made alone. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf))

- **1939–1940:** *Speak Up for Democracy campaign*. A civic project extending beyond the book into conferences, articles, publicity, instructional resources, and organized local action. The book was its protocol; the campaign was the larger obra. ([Faxon bibliography](https://soilandhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/publicrelationse00bernrich.pdf))

- **1940:** *New York City mayoral-election work*. A political-public-relations project documented in the archive. The finding aid identifies the election as a topic but does not support assigning every associated electoral artifact to Bernays personally. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1940s–1950s:** *United Brewers Industrial Foundation program*. A sustained industry public-relations project rather than one discrete campaign; the surviving obra comprises surveys, reports, releases, correspondence, and public-facing interventions. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1940s–1960s:** *United Fruit Company public-relations program*. A long-running corporate and geopolitical project that portrayed the company’s interests and, during the Guatemalan crisis, encouraged anti-communist interpretations favorable to it. Bernays made publicity structures and media circumstances, not the 1954 coup itself; claims that assign him authorship of the coup exceed the archival attribution. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/), [FRAME Propaganda Archive](https://propagandaarchive.org/about/bernays-propaganda/))

- **1940s–1950s:** *Book Publishers Research Institute program*. A research and promotional project for the publishing industry, prominently represented by surveys and reports in the archive. Its many component documents do not resolve into a single stable title. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467/search))

- **1940s–1950s:** *Brotherhood of Railroad Trainmen public-relations program*. An institutional campaign represented by correspondence, reports, surveys, releases, and background files. It is best classified as a body of consultancy work rather than a single authored obra. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467/search))

- **1950s:** *Jawaharlal Nehru American-goodwill project*. A public-relations effort concerning India’s American reputation following its neutrality during the Korean War. The archive documents Bernays’s involvement but does not justify treating the broader diplomatic outcome as his creation. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/))

- **1960s–1970s:** *Campaign to license public-relations practitioners*. A professional-regulation project involving model legislation, lobbying, correspondence, and public argument. It remained only partly realized, but as an intention sustained through action it is a project and work even without successful enactment. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467/search), [Museum of Public Relations](https://www.prmuseum.org/pioneer-edward-bernays))

- **1960s–1990s:** *Citizens’ Emergency Committee to Save Memorial Drive*. An environmental public-relations campaign opposing expansion that threatened the park-like character of Memorial Drive in Cambridge. This is collaborative civic action and therefore resists being called Bernays’s work alone. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467/search))

- **1968–1990s:** *Edward L. Bernays Foundation programs*. An institutional obra supporting public-relations education and such initiatives as the Anglo-American relations contest. The foundation’s activities are manifestations of Bernays’s project, but works made by grantees or contestants are not thereby works by Bernays. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467/search))

- **1913–1992:** *Client, institution, and organization work*. The larger professional œuvre comprises projects for Allied Chemical & Dye, the American Nurses’ Association, American Psychological Association, American Tobacco, Bank of America, Cartier, CBS, General Motors, Jacques Seligmann, Mack Trucks, Philco, Procter & Gamble, United Fruit, the U.S. Information Agency, the U.S. Sugar Beet Association, Ward Baking, and many other clients. This corpus resists enumeration as individual “works”: the Library of Congress describes 227,000 items, and its archival series is arranged by client rather than as a catalogue raisonné of campaigns. ([Library of Congress collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78012534/), [Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467))

### Periodical, archival, and unfinished projects

- **1922–1936:** *Contact*. A professional newsletter represented in the archive by bound correspondence and texts. It is a serial project rather than one text, and the finding aid does not assign every surviving item’s authorship solely to Bernays. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/exist_collections/ead3pdf/mss/2003/ms003016.pdf))

- **Undated:** *I Take My Pen in Hand*. An unpublished manuscript preserved under this title. Its incomplete publication status makes it a documented project, not a finished public manifestation. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **Undated:** *Public Relations Counsel: A Personal History*. An unpublished autobiographical manuscript. It belongs to the memoir project but cannot safely be treated as a published independent book. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **Undated:** *Untitled manuscripts*. The archive preserves at least four folders under this description. These are made texts, but because neither fixed titles nor dates are supplied, inventing individual works from the folder count would confuse archival units with authored obras. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms003016/ms003016.pdf))

- **1925 and undated:** *Poetry*. The archive records poetry files but supplies no poem titles or reliable item-level authorship statement. The material therefore resists classification as a list of named works. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/exist_collections/ead3pdf/mss/2003/ms003016.pdf))

- **1912–1992:** *Speeches, articles, editorials, forewords, introductions, letters to editors, and fragments*. This archival body of work extends far beyond the separately published titles above. The Library of Congress arranges much of it chronologically or by document type, not as an item-level bibliography; “everything” at the level of every draft, speech version, release, and fragment therefore cannot be converted honestly into one bullet per intellectual work without manuscript examination. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2467))
