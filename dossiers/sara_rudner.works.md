## Works by Sara Rudner

### Choreographic obras and performance projects

- **1972:** *Eight Lanes, Four Approaches*. A twelve-minute dance made collaboratively with Douglas Dunn; Rudner supplied the title. Dunn’s chronology dates its Barnard College presentation to May 1972, while the *Dictionnaire de la danse* gives 1971, so the date is disputed. [Douglas Dunn + Dancers](https://www.douglasdunndance.com/chronology); [Larousse, *Dictionnaire de la danse*](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/372)

- **1975:** *Dancing-on-View*. A five-hour quartet with Wendy Perron, Risa Jaroslow, and Wendy Rogers, conceived as continuous dancing that spectators could enter and leave; it is better classified as durational performance and protocol than as a fixed theatrical dance. The original was performed only once, though a two-hour adaptation appeared at Oberlin later that year and subsequent manifestations substantially reworked it. [Pew Center for Arts & Heritage, *Capturing the Tone, Celebrating the Work*](https://www.pewcenterarts.org/sites/default/files/capturing_the_tone_0.pdf); [Dance Magazine](https://dancemagazine.com/the_2009_dance_magazine_awards/)

- **1975 or undated:** *Five Hour Quartet*. The Festival d’Automne biography lists this separately from *Dancing on View*, but available accounts call the latter a five-hour quartet; without a programme establishing two distinct objects, its status as an independent work remains uncertain. [Festival d’Automne biography](https://www.festival-automne.com/storage/medias/Publish__archive_pdf__FAP_1997_DA_02_DDP.pdf)

- **1976:** *Dancing May’s Dances*. A group dance documented by Rudner’s programme and Wendy Rogers’s extensive performance notes; the surviving score shows a structured sequence containing individual dancers’ materials rather than a literary “text.” [Pew Center for Arts & Heritage, *Capturing the Tone, Celebrating the Work*](https://www.pewcenterarts.org/sites/default/files/capturing_the_tone_0.pdf)

- **1976–1977:** *Some “Yes” and More*. A long solo named in the Festival d’Automne’s retrospective list of Rudner’s works. The consulted source supplies neither a premiere date nor enough evidence to determine whether its changing performance was treated as a fixed choreography. [Festival d’Automne biography](https://www.festival-automne.com/storage/medias/Publish__archive_pdf__FAP_1997_DA_02_DDP.pdf)

- **1977:** *33 Dances on Her 33rd Birthday* (also shortened to *33 Dances*). A single-event cycle of thirty-three solos performed by Rudner in a loft on her thirty-third birthday; its exhaustive, occasion-bound manifestation resists separation into thirty-three independently titled works. [Wendy Perron, “Sara Rudner on Early Tharp”](https://wendyperron.com/sara-rudner-on-early-tharp/); [Dance Magazine](https://dancemagazine.com/the_2009_dance_magazine_awards/)

- **1977:** *November Duets/Molly’s Suite* (also billed as *November Duets*). A roughly twenty-five-minute collaborative duet by Rudner and Wendy Rogers, with Peggy Stern improvising live at the piano; its cue sheet organizes seven sections while leaving movement and music capable of varying in performance. [Pew Center for Arts & Heritage, *Capturing the Tone, Celebrating the Work*](https://www.pewcenterarts.org/sites/default/files/capturing_the_tone_0.pdf)

- **1977:** *As Is/Layers*. A group choreography for the Sara Rudner Performance Ensemble, with music by Joan La Barbara and design by Robert Kushner; NYPL’s record joins the titles with a slash, whereas a 1978 Paris programme calls the work simply *Layers*, so the relation between the two names is not fully settled. [New York Public Library](https://old-digitalcollections.nypl.org/items/e4f76dd0-aa8b-0133-ef8b-60f81dd2b63c); [Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/s/79122-Layers)

- **By 1997, date otherwise unestablished:** *As Is As Solo*. A long solo listed retrospectively alongside *Some “Yes” and More* and *33 Dances*. Its title suggests a solo extraction or transformation of *As Is*, but the consulted source does not establish that relationship, and it should not be silently treated as the same obra. [Festival d’Automne biography](https://www.festival-automne.com/storage/medias/Publish__archive_pdf__FAP_1997_DA_02_DDP.pdf)

- **1981:** *Dancing for an Hour or So*. An extended ensemble dance and marathon-like performance. A contemporary announcement calls the premiere *Dancing “For An Hour Or 50”*, while the *Dictionnaire de la danse* prints *Dancing for an Hour or So*; whether the first wording was a typographical corruption or an alternate title remains unresolved. [Larousse, *Dictionnaire de la danse*](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/372); [SUNY Purchase contemporary notice](https://dspace.sunyconnect.suny.edu/bitstreams/a0219bf6-8ee0-4700-8835-fe7d579200d2/download)

- **By 1997, individual dates unestablished:** *Eight Solos*. A project built through Rudner’s individual work with dancers to develop their physical and emotional possibilities. The collective title denotes a choreographic project or cycle; the consulted source does not name the eight constituent solos. [Festival d’Automne biography](https://www.festival-automne.com/storage/medias/Publish__archive_pdf__FAP_1997_DA_02_DDP.pdf)

- **1993:** *Inside Out*. A choreography named and dated by the *Dictionnaire de la danse*, later described as emerging from Rudner’s individualized work with dancers. It should not be confused with Linda Sastradipradja’s 2012 *Inside/Out*, made in collaboration with Rudner. [Larousse, *Dictionnaire de la danse*](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/372); [Festival d’Automne biography](https://www.festival-automne.com/storage/medias/Publish__archive_pdf__FAP_1997_DA_02_DDP.pdf)

- **Date unestablished:** *Dancing Parttime*. A Rudner choreography preserved on video in the Jerome Robbins Dance Division and identified by NYPL in a retrospective programme; the publicly available notice gives no creation date or fuller production history. [Dance/NYC and New York Public Library for the Performing Arts](https://www.dance.nyc/for-audiences/community-calendar/view/Sara-Rudners-Radiant-Life-in-Dance/2026-04-16/)

- **1999:** *Dancing-on-View* (1999 manifestation). A revival or reconstruction containing material influenced by Russell Dumas, including rhythmically organized running circles. Participants explicitly reported that they possessed no usable movement record from 1975, making this a new manifestation of the project rather than an exact restoration. [Pew Center for Arts & Heritage, *Capturing the Tone, Celebrating the Work*](https://www.pewcenterarts.org/sites/default/files/capturing_the_tone_0.pdf)

- **2007:** *Dancing-on-View (Preview/Hindsight)*. A large, intergenerational dance installation and marathon for sixteen women at the Baryshnikov Arts Center, organized in “non-theatrical time and space.” Its status lies between revival, new choreography, and continuing project. [Sarah Lawrence College](https://www.sarahlawrence.edu/news-events/archived/2006-2007-slc-news-archive.pdf); [Alastair Macaulay](https://crimson-tangerine-2ty2.squarespace.com/all-essays/movement-with-and-against-music-but-bracingly)

- **2007:** *This Dancing Life*. A four-hour dance marathon set on twenty-one performers from New York and Irish Modern Dance Theatre, with spectators invited to come and go. Its open duration and transferable score make it a protocol enacted by a community as much as a bounded dance work. [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/an-invite-to-a-dancing-life-1.954712)

- **2017:** Untitled trio findings with Jodi Melnick and Vicky Shick. A public showing of recent collaborative research rather than a securely titled, declared work; its resistance to being catalogued as an autonomous obra is part of its status. [Cathy Weis Projects](https://cathyweis.org/calendar/april-30-2017-sara-rudner-and-sally-silvers/)

- **2019:** *Dancing-on-View* (Sarah Lawrence mini-version). A manifestation for twenty-four students made for Rudner’s retirement season. The institutional account explicitly calls it a “mini-version,” not a separate newly titled work. [Sarah Lawrence College](https://www.sarahlawrence.edu/news-events/news/2019-06-19-sara-rudner%2C-dance-faculty-member-and-former-dance-program-director%2C-retires-after-20-years-nb.html)

### Media works and technological Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1978:** *Dance Frame*. A seven-minute 16-mm dance film directed by Doris Chase, scored by Joan La Barbara, and choreographed by Rudner; authorship is therefore distributed across film direction, choreography, performance, and music. [Museum of Modern Art](https://www.moma.org/calendar/events/6003)

- **1978:** *Three Video Variations* / *Doris Chase Dance Series with Sara Rudner*. A half-hour television Gesamtkunstwerk created by Chase and Rudner specifically for the television frame, rather than a stage dance subsequently recorded. The archive describes three variations but publicly identifies only one, *Theme*, which derived from Rudner’s theatrical *As Is*; the other two titles are not supplied and should not be invented. [American Archive of Public Broadcasting](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip_526-sj19k4737p)

- **1983 onward:** *HeartBeat* (also *Heartbeat* and *Heartbeats*). Christopher Janney’s interactive performance system converts the dancer’s amplified heartbeat into the rhythmic basis for improvisation; Rudner premiered it at Boston’s Institute of Contemporary Art and supplied choreographic direction. Because Janney is identified as creator-composer and Rudner as choreographic collaborator and original performer, it is a shared technological Gesamtkunstwerk, not solely a Rudner choreography. [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/1998/artheart-0513); [Janney Sound](https://www.janneysound.com/project/heartbeat/)

- **1998:** *HeartBeat: mb*. The Mikhail Baryshnikov manifestation of the *HeartBeat* system, with Janney as creator-composer, Rudner providing choreographic direction, and Baryshnikov generating improvisational movement. Authorship is deliberately plural, and the work is better understood as a protocol for live electrocardio-choreography than as a fixed solo. [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/1998/artheart-0513)

### Choreography within opera productions

- **1998; revived 2004:** *Beatrice and Benedict*. Choreography made for the Santa Fe Opera production; the 2004 appearance was a return of the production rather than evidence of a separately titled choreographic obra. [Santa Fe Opera](https://www.santafeopera.org/bio/sara-rudner/)

- **2001:** *Wozzeck*. Choreography within the Santa Fe Opera staging, subordinate to and inseparable from the larger operatic production. [Santa Fe Opera](https://www.santafeopera.org/bio/sara-rudner/)

- **2001:** *Die ägyptische Helena* (*The Egyptian Helen*). Choreography within the Santa Fe Opera production; it is a contribution to a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than authorship of the opera itself. [Santa Fe Opera](https://www.santafeopera.org/bio/sara-rudner/)

- **2004:** *Don Giovanni*. Choreography within the Santa Fe Opera production. [Santa Fe Opera](https://www.santafeopera.org/bio/sara-rudner/)

- **2005:** *Turandot*. Choreography within the Santa Fe Opera production. [Santa Fe Opera](https://www.santafeopera.org/bio/sara-rudner/)

- **2006:** *Salome*. Choreography within the Santa Fe Opera production. [Santa Fe Opera](https://www.santafeopera.org/bio/sara-rudner/)

- **Dates and titles not established in the consulted sources:** Paris Opera, Salzburg Festival, and Public Theater choreographies. Institutional biographies confirm that Rudner made choreography for productions at these organizations, but they do not identify every production; these contributions therefore cannot responsibly be expanded into invented title-and-date entries. [Sarah Lawrence College](https://www.sarahlawrence.edu/news-events/news/2019-06-19-sara-rudner%2C-dance-faculty-member-and-former-dance-program-director%2C-retires-after-20-years-nb.html); [Bennington College](https://www.bennington.edu/bennington-network/outsized-impact/sara-rudner)

### Long-duration institutional project

- **1975/1976–1982:** *Sara Rudner Performance Ensemble*. The company itself was an obra and enabling project: a small, principally female performance collective through which Rudner developed dances, marathons, and unconventional modes of presentation. Sources disagree over whether it began in 1975 or 1976, while agreeing that it ended in 1982. [Festival d’Automne biography](https://www.festival-automne.com/storage/medias/Publish__archive_pdf__FAP_1997_DA_02_DDP.pdf); [Sarah Lawrence College](https://www.sarahlawrence.edu/news-events/news/2019-06-19-sara-rudner%2C-dance-faculty-member-and-former-dance-program-director%2C-retires-after-20-years-nb.html); [Dance Magazine](https://dancemagazine.com/the_2009_dance_magazine_awards/)
