## Works by Helena Petrovna Blavatsky

### Synthetic and expository obras

- **1877:** *Isis Unveiled: A Master-Key to the Mysteries of Ancient and Modern Science and Theology*. A two-volume syncretic treatise—“Science” and “Theology”—and Blavatsky’s first book-length obra; its extensive unacknowledged borrowing and the degree of assistance supplied by collaborators remain subjects of dispute. ([*Collected Writings*, vol. I](https://www.theosophy.world/sites/default/files/ebooks/Collected_Writings_Volume_I_%281874-1878%29.pdf); [Blavatsky writings](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Blavatsky_writings))
- **1888:** *The Secret Doctrine: The Synthesis of Science, Religion, and Philosophy*. A two-volume cosmological and anthropological Gesamtkunstwerk combining commentary, comparative religion, diagrams, purportedly archaic “stanzas,” and polemic; Blavatsky described it as transmission and synthesis rather than wholly personal authorship. ([The Secret Doctrine](https://theosophy.wiki/en/The_Secret_Doctrine_%28book%29))
- **1889:** *The Key to Theosophy*. A question-and-answer exposition devised as an introductory map of Theosophical teaching and institutional practice. ([Blavatsky writings](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Blavatsky_writings))
- **1889:** *The Voice of the Silence*. A devotional-philosophical sequence presented as translated extracts from the purportedly Tibetan *Book of the Golden Precepts*; because no recoverable source text is known, its status oscillates among translation, adaptation, and original creation. ([The writings of H. P. Blavatsky](https://tforward.theosophy.wiki/the-writings-of-h-p-blavatsky))
- **1890:** *Gems from the East: A Birthday Book of Precepts and Axioms*. An illustrated calendar-anthology assigning a maxim to every day; it is principally an act of selection and arrangement, and individual sayings should not automatically be attributed to Blavatsky. ([Blavatsky writings](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Blavatsky_writings))
- **1890–1891:** *Transactions of the Blavatsky Lodge*. Two published reports of question-and-answer meetings on *The Secret Doctrine*; the obra is partly oral teaching, partly collective transcription and editing, rather than a conventional authored book. ([Blavatsky writings](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Blavatsky_writings))
- **unfinished at her death; 1892:** *The Theosophical Glossary*. A posthumously edited reference work of 2,797 entries assembled from Blavatsky’s writing and numerous source books, with contributions from W. Wynn Westcott; Boris de Zirkoff’s analysis identifies only 469 entries as wholly or partly written by her, so the printed book resists attribution as a unitary Blavatsky obra. ([The Theosophical Glossary](https://theosophy.wiki/en/The_Theosophical_Glossary_%28book%29))
- **unfinished at her death; 1897:** *The Secret Doctrine, Volume III*. A posthumous construction made by Annie Besant and G. R. S. Mead from manuscript fragments; it belongs to Blavatsky’s unfinished *Secret Doctrine* project, but not securely to the two-volume obra she authorized in 1888. ([Blavatsky writings](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Blavatsky_writings); [The Secret Doctrine](https://theosophy.wiki/en/The_Secret_Doctrine_%28book%29))
- **1888–1889; published 2014:** *The Secret Doctrine Würzburg Manuscript*. An earlier, substantially different manuscript state of the larger project, valuable as a genetic document rather than as a book completed or published by Blavatsky. ([Blavatsky writings](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Blavatsky_writings))

### Travel narratives and ethnographic-literary works

- **1879–1886; collected 1892:** *From the Caves and Jungles of Hindostan*. A Russian-language serial published under the persona “Radda-Bai,” combining travel narrative, dialogue, folklore, occult tale, and polemic; the posthumous book is a collection of installments, not the form in which the obra first appeared. ([The writings of H. P. Blavatsky](https://tforward.theosophy.wiki/the-writings-of-h-p-blavatsky); [Bibliography of H. P. Blavatsky](https://theohistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/thc-jean-paul_guignette-bibliography_of_hp_blavatsky-1987.pdf))
- **1880–1881:** *The Durbar in Lahore*. A Russian travel reportage about the imperial assemblage and northern India, shaped as literary observation rather than neutral documentary record. ([IAPSOP edition](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1881__blavatsky___the_durbar_in_lahore.pdf))
- **1883; collected in English 1893:** *The People of the Blue Mountains*. A sequence about the Nilgiri Hills and Toda people, blending ethnographic observation, travel writing, anecdote, and occult interpretation; its later book title designates a collected form produced after Blavatsky’s death. ([H. P. Blavatsky](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/H._P._Blavatsky); [Bibliography of H. P. Blavatsky](https://theohistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/thc-jean-paul_guignette-bibliography_of_hp_blavatsky-1987.pdf))

### Occult fiction and literary manifestations

- **c. 1876–1877:** *Can the Double Murder?* An occult short story later absorbed into the expanding corpus called *Nightmare Tales*. ([*Nightmare Tales* contents](https://www.universalfreemasonry.org/en/library/nightmare-tales/contents))
- **c. 1876–1877:** *An Unsolved Mystery*. An occult short story subsequently included in expanded editions of *Nightmare Tales*. ([*Nightmare Tales* contents](https://www.universalfreemasonry.org/en/library/nightmare-tales/contents))
- **June 1888:** *Karmic Visions*. A visionary prose narrative that moves between fiction, allegory, and doctrinal exemplum. ([*Nightmare Tales* contents](https://www.universalfreemasonry.org/en/library/nightmare-tales/contents))
- **April 1890:** *The Legend of the Blue Lotus*. A literary legend functioning simultaneously as occult fiction and moral allegory. ([*Nightmare Tales* contents](https://www.universalfreemasonry.org/en/library/nightmare-tales/contents))
- **1885; substantially revised c. 1890–1891:** *A Bewitched Life*. An occult tale whose late English form is an extensive rewriting, not simply a reprint of the earlier story. ([The Theosophical Library](https://theosophylib.com/hpb/nightmare-tales/))
- **begun in the 1870s; revised c. 1890–1891:** *The Luminous Shield*. An occult tale recovered and reworked from Blavatsky’s earlier American writing. ([The Theosophical Library](https://theosophylib.com/hpb/nightmare-tales/))
- **begun in the 1870s; revised c. 1890–1891:** *The Cave of the Echoes*. An occult tale with an early American textual history and a late revised form. ([The Theosophical Library](https://theosophylib.com/hpb/nightmare-tales/))
- **c. 1890–1891:** *From the Polar Lands*. An unfinished adaptation of a Russian story by Blavatsky’s sister Vera Zhelikhovskaya; it resists classification as wholly original fiction and was posthumously completed for publication. ([The Theosophical Library](https://theosophylib.com/hpb/nightmare-tales/))
- **first published 1880; revised c. 1890–1891:** *The Ensouled Violin*. An occult musician-tale extensively reworked near the end of Blavatsky’s life. ([The Theosophical Library](https://theosophylib.com/hpb/nightmare-tales/))
- **1892:** *Nightmare Tales*. A posthumous collection rather than a single newly made text; its original five-story form and later expanded contents gather works written and revised at different times. ([Theosophical Publishing Society](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Theosophical_Publishing_Society_%28London%29); [Project Gutenberg record](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/44559))

### Protocols, manifestos, and restricted teaching documents

- **14 December 1888:** *Preliminary Memorandum of the Esoteric Section*. A protocol defining membership, discipline, confidentiality, and the relation of the new school to the Theosophical Society. ([Historical Introduction to the Esoteric Papers](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/caldwell_history_inner_Group.pdf))
- **January–February 1889:** *Esoteric Instruction No. I*. A privately circulated teaching protocol correlating sound, colour, number, planets, metals, and human principles; it was made for initiated use, not for an open readership. ([Theosophical Society in America](https://www.theosophical.org/publications/quest-magazine/esoteric-instructions))
- **March–April 1889:** *Esoteric Instruction No. II*. A continuation and clarification of the first restricted instruction. ([Historical Introduction to the Esoteric Papers](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/caldwell_history_inner_Group.pdf))
- **October 1889:** *Preliminary Explanations to Esoteric Instruction No. III*. A supplementary protocol issued to prepare and correct the reception of the third instruction. ([Historical Introduction to the Esoteric Papers](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/caldwell_history_inner_Group.pdf))
- **December 1889–January 1890:** *Esoteric Instruction No. III*. The third restricted instructional text, completing the numbered sequence Blavatsky herself issued. ([Historical Introduction to the Esoteric Papers](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/caldwell_history_inner_Group.pdf))
- **September–October 1890:** *Book of Rules*. An enlarged rewriting of the *Preliminary Memorandum* into an operative constitution for the Esoteric Section; it is a protocol that attempts to produce conduct and institutional form. ([Historical Introduction to the Esoteric Papers](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/caldwell_history_inner_Group.pdf))
- **1890–1891; first collected 1985:** *Inner Group Teachings*. Oral instruction, diagrams, answers, and practices preserved in pupils’ notes; the surviving text is a mediated manifestation of teaching sessions rather than an authorially finished manuscript. ([Core bibliography](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/corelistofbooksbetter.pdf))
- **1888–1891:** *Esoteric Section notices, letters, pledges, and supplementary papers*. A dispersed body of operative documents, some published and others still unpublished; it resists treatment as one fixed work, yet collectively constituted Blavatsky’s instructional project. ([Historical Introduction to the Esoteric Papers](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/caldwell_history_inner_Group.pdf))

### Periodicals and editorial Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1879–1885:** *The Theosophist*. A monthly periodical founded, published, edited, and heavily written by Blavatsky; because its obra consists of selection, juxtaposition, correspondence, controversy, and collective authorship across successive issues, it is closer to an editorial Gesamtkunstwerk than to a book. ([The Theosophist](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/The_Theosophist))
- **1887–1891:** *Lucifer*. A London monthly founded and edited by Blavatsky, initially with Mabel Collins and later with Annie Besant; it combined original essays, fiction, translations, reviews, correspondence, and polemical interventions. ([Lucifer](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Lucifer_%28periodical%29); [IAPSOP archive](https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/lucifer/index.html))
- **1874–1891:** *Articles, editorials, open letters, reviews, answers to correspondents, notes, and marginalia collected in the* Collected Writings. This is not one text and cannot honestly be reduced to a short title list: Boris de Zirkoff’s fourteen textual volumes assemble hundreds of individually dated or tentatively dated pieces, including anonymous and pseudonymous items whose attribution sometimes rests on editorial evidence. ([Collected Writings overview](https://www.blavatskytrust.org.uk/html/nf_bt5.htm); [Core bibliography](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/corelistofbooksbetter.pdf))
- **1879–1891:** *Theosophical journalism in Russian, English, and French*. A multilingual body of contributions scattered across Theosophical and general periodicals; the corpus exceeds Blavatsky’s book-length output and survives in variant, translated, revised, unsigned, and posthumously collected states, so it resists being counted as a stable set of “books.” ([The writings of H. P. Blavatsky](https://tforward.theosophy.wiki/the-writings-of-h-p-blavatsky))

### Correspondence, notebooks, and graphic works

- **1861–1891:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of private, administrative, polemical, and instructional letters form a dispersed epistolary obra rather than a book made by Blavatsky; the published collections are incomplete editorial selections, and the projected scholarly edition has not recovered every letter. ([Core bibliography](https://www.blavatskyarchives.com/corelistofbooksbetter.pdf))
- **1874–1891:** *Scrapbooks*. Thirty-two archival volumes of press cuttings, pasted documents, doodles, drawings, corrections, and often caustic manuscript annotations; they are composite material objects and working archives, not simply texts. ([Theosophical Society Archives](https://convention.ts-adyar.org/speaker/archives))
- **undated and variously dated:** *Sketchbook*. A manuscript-art object containing ink and pencil drawings, sketches, doodles, captions, and autobiographical notes; its dispersed components resist reduction to separately titled “works.” ([*Collected Writings*, vol. I](https://www.theosophy.world/sites/default/files/ebooks/Collected_Writings_Volume_I_%281874-1878%29.pdf))
- **1840s–1891:** *Drawings, caricatures, landscapes, portraits, and emblematic designs*. Blavatsky produced graphic work throughout her life, especially in her sketchbook and scrapbooks; individual sheets are often untitled or uncertainly dated, and at least one early family painting is only tentatively attributed to her. ([H. P. Blavatsky](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/H._P._Blavatsky); [Blavatsky photographs and portraits](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Blavatsky_photographs_and_portraits))

### Institutional projects

- **1875:** *The Theosophical Society*. A continuing institutional obra co-founded with Henry Steel Olcott, William Quan Judge, and others; it cannot be assigned to Blavatsky alone, and its objects and organization changed after its formation. ([Theosophical Society](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Theosophical_Society))
- **1887:** *Blavatsky Lodge*. A London teaching and discussion body co-created by fourteen members; the surviving lodge is an institutional project rather than a personal text, and even the authority attached to its founding deed is not entirely clear. ([Blavatsky Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blavatsky_Lodge))
- **1888:** *Esoteric Section of the Theosophical Society*. A restricted school formed under Blavatsky’s direct authority; its obra comprised a social body, graded access, pledges, protocols, diagrams, correspondence, and oral teaching. ([Esoteric Instructions](https://www.theosophical.org/publications/quest-magazine/esoteric-instructions); [foundation document](https://blavatskyarchives.com/blavatsky_esoteric_papers.pdf))
- **1890:** *Inner Group of the Esoteric Section*. A twelve-member teaching circle—six women and six men—whose meetings generated diagrams, practices, oral instruction, and pupil transcripts; it is best classified as a pedagogical project rather than a publication. ([Esoteric Instructions](https://www.theosophical.org/publications/quest-magazine/esoteric-instructions))

### Disputed manifestations and resisted attributions

- **c. 1880–1885:** *The Mahatma Letters associated with Blavatsky, A. P. Sinnett, and A. O. Hume*. These letters were presented as communications from “K.H.,” “M.,” and other adepts, not as works by Blavatsky; Richard Hodgson attributed many to her as disguised writing, while Vernon Harrison later found that conclusion unsupported by the handwriting evidence. They must therefore remain a disputed manifestation, not be silently entered either among her works or outside them. ([Society for Psychical Research overview](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/hodgson-report-theosophy/); [Vernon Harrison’s affidavit](https://www.theosociety.org/pasadena/hpb-spr/hpbspr-a.htm))
- **1870s–1880s:** *Occult phenomena, “precipitated” notes, apported objects, and séance manifestations attributed to Blavatsky*. Witnesses and Blavatsky’s followers treated these as manifestations rather than artifacts she conventionally made; critics treated some or all as fabricated performances. They resist inclusion as stable works because their authorship, mechanism, material history, and sometimes existence are contested. ([1885 Hodgson investigation](https://open-data.spr.ac.uk/sites/default/files/ebook/article/hodgson_report_theosophy-298.pdf); [Harrison’s re-examination](https://www.theosociety.org/pasadena/hpb-spr/hpb-spr1.htm))
