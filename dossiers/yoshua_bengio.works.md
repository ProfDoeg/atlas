## Works by Yoshua Bengio

### Scholarly corpus

- **1988–present:** *Scientific publications of Yoshua Bengio*. A still-growing collective œuvre of journal articles, conference papers, chapters, preprints, reports, and proceedings—nearly four hundred items by mid-2020 and substantially more thereafter. Because most are coauthored and some exist as both preprints and published versions, the œuvre resists being counted as a set of wholly independent, singly authored works; the [2020 curriculum vitae](https://yoshuabengio.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/CV_Yoshua_Bengio_July2020-3.pdf) and the continuing [DBLP bibliography](https://dblp.org/pid/56/953.html) are the item-level catalogues.

### Dissertation and books

- **1991:** *Artificial Neural Networks and Their Application to Sequence Recognition*. Bengio’s McGill doctoral dissertation: a research text on neural-network treatment of sequential data, subsequently developed through articles and his 1996 monograph. [McGill catalogue record](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/concern/theses/x633f134h)

- **1996:** *Neural Networks for Speech and Sequence Recognition*. A singly authored research monograph, not merely a collection of earlier papers, on learning dependencies in speech and other sequences. [Bengio’s bibliography, item 388](https://yoshuabengio.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/CV_Yoshua_Bengio_July2020-3.pdf)

- **2007:** *Large-Scale Kernel Machines*. An edited research volume made with Léon Bottou and Olivier Chapelle; Bengio’s role is editorial and authorial within a multi-author object rather than sole authorship of the whole. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262532934/large-scale-kernel-machines/)

- **2009:** *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 22*. Conference proceedings edited with Dale Schuurmans, John Lafferty, Chris Williams, and Aron Culotta: an infrastructural scholarly object containing the work of many authors. [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2009)

- **2009:** *Learning Deep Architectures for AI*. A book-length scholarly synthesis issued as a *Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning* monograph; although often catalogued as a book, it is also formally a journal-length review. [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1561/2200000006)

- **2011:** *Optimization for Machine Learning*. An edited collection made with Suvrit Sra and Sebastian Nowozin, assembling specialist chapters rather than presenting one continuous Bengio-authored argument. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262016469/optimization-for-machine-learning/)

- **2016:** *Deep Learning*. A coauthored textbook by Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, and Aaron Courville, designed simultaneously as a pedagogical book and an openly accessible web text. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262035613/deep-learning/) [Authors’ site](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/)

- **2021:** *Art in the Age of Machine Learning*. Sofian Audry’s monograph, to which Bengio contributed a foreword; it should not be converted into a Bengio-authored book merely because publisher catalogues place it on his author page. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262046183/art-in-the-age-of-machine-learning/)

### Foundational research texts

- **1994:** *Learning Long-Term Dependencies with Gradient Descent Is Difficult*. A coauthored journal article with Patrice Simard and Paolo Frasconi that experimentally and analytically characterized the vanishing-gradient problem in recurrent networks. It is a diagnosis rather than the invention of recurrent neural networks themselves. [IEEE record](https://doi.org/10.1109/72.279181)

- **2000/2003:** *A Neural Probabilistic Language Model*. First presented in conference form and then published in an expanded journal version, this coauthored text made learned distributed word representations and next-word prediction parts of one trainable neural language model; the paired dates denote related manifestations, not two unrelated works. [Journal version](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v3/bengio03a.html)

- **2006:** *Greedy Layer-Wise Training of Deep Networks*. A coauthored conference paper with Pascal Lamblin, Dan Popovici, and Hugo Larochelle proposing layerwise pretraining for deep neural networks. It belongs to the broader, multi-laboratory revival of deep learning and should not be mistaken for a single-person invention. [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2006/hash/5da713a690c067105aeb2fae32403405-Abstract.html)

- **2007:** *Scaling Learning Algorithms towards AI*. A synthetic book chapter that framed representation learning and deep architectures as a long-term research project; its object is partly programmatic rather than merely descriptive. [Bengio’s publication text](https://www.iro.umontreal.ca/~lisa/pointeurs/Chap_bengio.pdf)

- **2009:** *Curriculum Learning*. A coauthored conference paper with Jérôme Louradour, Ronan Collobert, and Jason Weston that formalized training examples presented in an intentionally ordered curriculum. [ICML record](https://doi.org/10.1145/1553374.1553380)

- **2010:** *Understanding the Difficulty of Training Deep Feedforward Neural Networks*. A coauthored conference paper with Xavier Glorot that introduced the initialization commonly called “Glorot” or “Xavier” initialization; both names can obscure its joint authorship. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/glorot10a.html)

- **2011:** *Deep Sparse Rectifier Neural Networks*. A coauthored conference paper with Xavier Glorot and Antoine Bordes establishing rectified linear units as effective components of deep networks; it did not originate the rectifier function itself. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/glorot11a.html)

- **2012:** *Practical Recommendations for Gradient-Based Training of Deep Architectures*. A singly authored protocol-like chapter: it instructs practitioners how to train deep networks and therefore acts as a practical manual as much as a research survey. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5533)

- **2012/2013:** *Representation Learning: A Review and New Perspectives*. A review by Bengio, Aaron Courville, and Pascal Vincent, circulated as a 2012 preprint and published in 2013; it consolidates a field rather than claiming one new algorithm. [IEEE record](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2013.50)

- **2013:** *Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation*. A technical preprint proposing gradient estimators, including the straight-through estimator. Its preprint status is part of the work’s identity, even though its methods became widely reused. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3432)

- **2013:** *Deep Generative Stochastic Networks Trainable by Backprop*. A coauthored conference paper presenting generative stochastic networks as an alternative route to training generative models. [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/bengio14.html)

- **2014/2015:** *Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate*. A paper by Dzmitry Bahdanau, Kyunghyun Cho, and Bengio, circulated in 2014 and presented at ICLR in 2015, introducing a learned alignment or “attention” mechanism for neural translation. The split date reflects preprint and conference manifestations. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)

- **2014:** *Generative Adversarial Nets*. A multi-author conference paper led by Ian Goodfellow that introduced generative adversarial networks; Bengio is a coauthor, not the sole maker, and later retitlings using “Networks” are bibliographic variants rather than a new work. [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2014/hash/f033ed80deb0234979a61f95710dbe25-Abstract.html)

- **2014:** *Learning Phrase Representations Using RNN Encoder–Decoder for Statistical Machine Translation*. A coauthored paper by Kyunghyun Cho and colleagues, including Bengio, that introduced the gated recurrent unit and an encoder–decoder architecture. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/D14-1179/)

- **2015:** *Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks*. A coauthored conference paper with Samy Bengio, Oriol Vinyals, and Navdeep Jaitly proposing a training schedule between observed and generated sequence histories. [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/hash/e995f98d56967d946471af29d7bf99f1-Abstract.html)

- **2015:** *Towards Biologically Plausible Deep Learning*. A coauthored preprint exploring target propagation as a possible alternative to ordinary backpropagation; it is explicitly an exploratory project, not evidence that biological brains implement the proposed mechanism. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04156)

- **2015/2016:** *Theano: A Python Framework for Fast Computation of Mathematical Expressions*. A collective software paper documenting Theano, written by the Theano Development Team including Bengio. It records a communal software œuvre and does not support attributing the codebase to Bengio alone. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.02688)

- **2017:** *The Consciousness Prior*. A workshop paper proposing a sparse, high-level latent representation selected by attention. It is best understood as a speculative research project or hypothesis, not a completed theory of consciousness. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08568)

- **2017/2018:** *Graph Attention Networks*. A paper by Petar Veličković and collaborators, including Bengio, introducing attention-based aggregation for graph-structured data; circulated in 2017 and presented at ICLR in 2018. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rJXMpikCZ)

- **2019:** *A Meta-Transfer Objective for Learning to Disentangle Causal Mechanisms*. A coauthored conference paper treating causal representation learning as a transfer problem. It belongs to Bengio’s continuing research project on causality rather than constituting a finished causal theory. [ICLR/OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ryxWIgBFPS)

- **2019:** *From System 1 Deep Learning to System 2 Deep Learning*. A NeurIPS keynote and slide-based research programme rather than a conventional paper: it argues for neural systems capable of deliberate, compositional reasoning. Its resistance to classification as a stable text is part of its status. [NeurIPS lecture record](https://nips.cc/virtual/2019/invited-talk/15479)

- **2021:** *GFlowNet Foundations*. A multi-author preprint developing generative flow networks for sampling compositional objects in proportion to reward. It systematizes a continuing project whose terminology and formalism were also developed across several earlier papers. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09266)

- **2021:** *Flow Network Based Generative Models for Non-Iterative Diverse Candidate Generation*. A coauthored NeurIPS paper presenting the initial GFlowNet construction; its relationship to *GFlowNet Foundations* is that of an originating technical manifestation to a later synthesis. [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/e614f646836aaed9f89ce58e837e2310-Abstract.html)

- **2023:** *Machine Learning for Combinatorial Optimization: A Methodological Tour d’Horizon*. A survey with Andrea Lodi and Antoine Prouvost, begun as a 2018 preprint and published in 2021 rather than a single-date invention; it maps a methodological field. [European Journal of Operational Research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2020.07.063)

- **2023:** *Managing AI Risks in an Era of Rapid Progress*. A many-author policy paper combining scientific assessment with recommendations for governance. Because it is designed to change institutional conduct, it approaches a manifesto while retaining the form of a scholarly position paper. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17688)

- **2024:** *Managing Extreme AI Risks amid Rapid Progress*. A collectively authored *Science* article arguing for technical research and public governance of catastrophic AI risks; it is both analysis and intervention. [Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adn0117)

- **2025–present:** *Scientist AI*. Bengio’s safe-by-design research project for a non-agentic predictive system intended to understand and explain rather than autonomously pursue goals. It remains a project—not yet a finished machine or settled theory—and its implementations and formal texts are continuing manifestations. [Bengio research statement](https://yoshuabengio.org/en/research) [LawZero research programme](https://lawzero.org/en/research)

- **2026:** *Safety from Honesty in a Disinterested AI Predictor*. A multi-author formal statement of the Scientist AI programme’s proposed safety argument. Its June/July 2026 dating varies between Bengio’s site and LawZero’s displayed publication date, so the discrepancy should not be silently normalized. [Bengio research page](https://yoshuabengio.org/en/research) [LawZero](https://lawzero.org/en/research)

### Software, laboratories, and institutional projects

- **1993–present:** *The Université de Montréal learning laboratory that became Mila*. Bengio founded the research laboratory in 1993; it later expanded across Montréal institutions and acquired non-profit status in 2018. Mila is an institutional œuvre made by a large community, not a personal work wholly authored or owned by Bengio. [Mila institutional history](https://mila.quebec/en/about/about-mila)

- **2007–2017:** *Theano*. An open-source numerical-computation library initiated in Bengio’s Montréal research group and maintained by a development community. Bengio helped create the environment and coauthored its scholarly descriptions, but the software itself resists individual attribution. [Theano retrospective](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.02688) [Mila discontinuation notice](https://mila.quebec/en/article/theano-past-present-and-future-after-v1-0)

- **2016–2020:** *Element AI*. A commercial artificial-intelligence company co-founded by Bengio with Jean-François Gagné, Jean-Sébastien Cournoyer, Nicolas Chapados, Philippe Beaudoin, and Anne Martel. It was an institutional and entrepreneurial project rather than a text, and its independent form ended when ServiceNow acquired it in 2020. [ServiceNow acquisition announcement](https://www.servicenow.com/company/media/press-room/servicenow-to-acquire-element-ai.html)

- **2025–present:** *LawZero*. A non-profit AI-safety research organization founded by Bengio and incubated at Mila to pursue Scientist AI. It is simultaneously an institution, a research programme, and a vehicle for unfinished future works. [LawZero](https://lawzero.org/) [Mila impact report](https://mila.quebec/en/about/impact-reports/impact-report-2024-2025)

### Declarations, reports, and public interventions

- **2018:** *Montréal Declaration for a Responsible Development of Artificial Intelligence*. A collectively produced declaration developed through scholarly and public consultation, with Bengio participating in its responsible-AI group and signing it. It is a manifesto in the strong sense—a text meant to guide conduct—but not a work that can responsibly be assigned to Bengio alone. [Declaration history and team](https://montrealdeclaration-responsibleai.com/about/)

- **2024:** *International Scientific Report on the Safety of Advanced AI: Interim Report*. A consensus assessment prepared by an international expert group chaired by Bengio. His chairmanship makes him a directing maker of the report, but its evidence and wording are explicitly collective. [UK government publication](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/international-scientific-report-on-the-safety-of-advanced-ai)

- **2024:** *The Manhattan Declaration on Inclusive Global Scientific Understanding of Artificial Intelligence*. A manifesto co-sponsored by Bengio and Alondra Nelson and signed by nineteen other researchers and policy experts, setting out principles for global scientific cooperation and AI governance. [Mila account and signatory list](https://mila.quebec/en/news/milas-yoshua-bengio-alondra-nelson-and-many-other-ai-experts-put-forward-the-manhattan)

- **2025:** *International AI Safety Report 2025*. An international scientific assessment chaired by Bengio and written with ninety-six experts; it synthesizes evidence rather than reporting a single experiment. It is an institutional consensus object, not a singly authored Bengio book. [Report site](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/) [UK government publication](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/international-ai-safety-report-2025)

- **2026:** *International AI Safety Report 2026*. The second full report in the series, again led by Bengio as chair and made collectively by an international expert group. It is a periodically renewed assessment whose authority rests on process and synthesis rather than individual authorship. [Report site](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/) [Bengio publication record](https://yoshuabengio.org/en/research)
