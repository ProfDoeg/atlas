## Works by Abu Yazid Tayfur ibn Isa al-Bistami

### Oral obra: sayings and teachings

- **Before 234/848 or 261/875:** Untitled corpus of sayings attributed to Bāyazīd. This is an oral obra—roughly five hundred reported aphorisms, answers, homilies, counsels, prayers, autobiographical recollections, and descriptions of mystical states—not a book written or assembled by him: the sources explicitly say that he wrote nothing. It survives through competing family and disciple transmission lines, reportedly translated in part from Persian into Arabic by al-Junayd, and scattered through much later compilations; consequently, the wording, individual attributions, and completeness of the corpus cannot be guaranteed ([Böwering, “Besṭāmī, Bāyazīd”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bestami-bastami-bayazid-abu-yazid-tayfur-b/); [Keeler, *Bāyazīd*](https://brill.com/display/title/69454)).

### Manifestations: ecstatic utterances

- **Before 234/848 or 261/875:** Untitled *shaṭaḥāt* (ecstatic or theopathic utterances). These short, paradoxical first-person declarations are manifestations as much as compositions: tradition presents them as speech produced in mystical ecstasy, while later transmitters, critics, and commentators made them into a recognizable textual group. No authorial collection or stable sequence exists, and individual reports remain vulnerable to translation, expansion, and false attribution ([Böwering](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bestami-bastami-bayazid-abu-yazid-tayfur-b/); Hellmut Ritter, “Die Aussprüche des Bāyezīd Bisṭāmī,” bibliographically identified in the [*Encyclopaedia Iranica* article on Ritter](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/hellmut-ritter/); [Keeler](https://brill.com/display/title/69454), ch. 16).

### Attributed journey narratives

- **Before 234/848 or 261/875; recorded later:** Untitled accounts conventionally called Bāyazīd’s *Miʿrāj* (“ascension”). These are first-person narratives of spiritual peregrination, flight, and approach to God, but they resist classification as a single work: several short and long forms survive, and it is uncertain how much originated with Bāyazīd rather than with transmitters and redactors ([Keeler](https://brill.com/display/title/69454), chs. 14–15).

- **Attributed to Bāyazīd before 234/848 or 261/875; compiled by 395/1005:** *Ruʾyā Abī Yazīd fī qaṣd Allāh taʿālā wa-bayān ṣifatihi* (“The vision of Abū Yazīd in seeking God Most High and describing His attributes”). This is the elaborate Arabic ascension narrative embedded as chapter nine of *al-Qaṣd ilā Allāh*, a compilation ascribed to an otherwise unidentified Abū al-Qāsim and formerly, but erroneously, to al-Junayd. It is a later textual manifestation of a Bāyazīd tradition, not securely an obra made by Bāyazīd himself; even sources sympathetic to the attribution describe the extant version as highly embellished ([Böwering](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bestami-bastami-bayazid-abu-yazid-tayfur-b/); [R. A. Nicholson, “An Early Arabic Version of the Miʿrāj of Abū Yazīd al-Bisṭāmī”](https://www.digizeitschriften.de/download/pdf/864009399_0002/LOG_0036.pdf); [Keeler](https://brill.com/display/title/69454), ch. 15).

### Reported correspondence

- **Before 234/848 or 261/875:** Untitled exchanges with Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh al-Rāzī. Later sources preserve questions sent by Yaḥyā and replies attributed to Bāyazīd, concerning mystical love and insatiability for God; these are reported epistolary acts rather than surviving letters, and their original wording, number, and physical existence cannot be established ([Böwering](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bestami-bastami-bayazid-abu-yazid-tayfur-b/); [Keeler](https://brill.com/display/title/69454)).

- **Before 234/848 or 261/875:** Other reported messages and replies. Biographical traditions say that Bāyazīd communicated by messenger with Shaqīq al-Balkhī, answered an emissary from Dhū al-Nūn al-Miṣrī, and gave advice through correspondence, but no independently transmitted letter-texts survive; these therefore resist inclusion as discrete works and belong more securely to the history of attributed speech acts ([Böwering](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bestami-bastami-bayazid-abu-yazid-tayfur-b/)).

### Works sometimes mistaken for his

- **Compiled in the eleventh century:** *Kitāb al-Nūr min kalimāt Abī Ṭayfūr* (“The Book of Light from the Words of Abū Ṭayfūr”). This is not a work by Bāyazīd but Abū al-Faḍl Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī al-Sahlajī’s posthumous biographical and sayings collection, completed roughly two centuries after Bāyazīd’s death; it is the fullest principal witness to the attributed corpus, not the corpus’s authorial form ([Böwering](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bestami-bastami-bayazid-abu-yazid-tayfur-b/); [Keeler](https://brill.com/display/title/69454)).

- **Date unknown; existence uncertain:** *Manāqib-i Bāyazīd Bisṭāmī* (“Virtues/Life of Bāyazīd Bisṭāmī”). This allegedly lost Persian hagiography is attributed in later bibliography to Yūsuf ibn Muḥammad, not to Bāyazīd; whether al-Sahlajī used it is itself uncertain, so it is neither a surviving work nor evidence that Bāyazīd authored a book ([Böwering](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bestami-bastami-bayazid-abu-yazid-tayfur-b/)).
