## Works by William R. Bradshaw

### Speculative fiction

- **1892 (copyright 1891):** *The Goddess of Atvatabar: Being the History of the Discovery of the Interior World and Conquest of Atvatabar*. An illustrated hollow-Earth romance and utopian adventure, introduced by Julian Hawthorne; the first issue had a non-pictorial binding, while a second used artwork by Cyrus Durand Chapman. Wright’s standard bibliography of American fiction records it as no. 648, and the *Encyclopedia of Science Fiction* identifies it as Bradshaw’s sole book-length speculative work.

### Essays on domestic decoration

- **October–November 1898 and January 1899:** *The House: The Salon*. A multipart essay in *The Art Amateur* on the salon as a designed domestic interior. The interrupted publication makes it one serial text rather than three independent works.
- **February 1899:** *Carpets and Rugs*. An article in *The Art Amateur* treating floor coverings as elements of household decoration.

### Travel and prospecting narrative

- **April 1910:** *Hunting for Gold at Porcupine Lake*. An article published in *Field and Stream*, apparently a first-person or journalistic prospecting narrative rather than fiction; no evidence located establishes it as part of a larger work.

### Activist and organizational writing

- **8 June 1920:** *Letter Two, New York Anti-Vivisection Society*. An organizational letter concerning vivisection, jointly attributed in the surviving archival record to William R. Bradshaw, Diana Belais, and William H. Woglom. It resists classification as an autonomous “work”: it is administrative correspondence—an intervention made in the course of activism—and the repository-supplied title may not be its original title.

### Editorial projects

- **Before 1890; exact dates unverified:** *Literary Life*. A periodical edited by Bradshaw. His contribution is best understood as an editorial project or collective production, not as authorship of every text printed in the magazine; the available biographical sources do not establish a complete run under his editorship.
- **1890–1896:** *The Decorator and Furnisher*. A periodical edited by Bradshaw during this span. This, too, is a continuing editorial project and collaborative manifestation rather than a single authored text.
- **Date and role not securely established:** *Field and Stream*. Bradshaw was associated editorially with the magazine, but the consulted evidence does not define his title or tenure precisely enough to claim the magazine itself as his work.

### Disputed or insufficiently substantiated attribution

- **1914?:** *Naturopathy, the Medicine of the Future*. Some modern summaries call this a work by Bradshaw “with Benedict Lust,” but the attribution was not corroborated in the specialist Bradshaw bibliography or in a verifiable contemporary imprint. Benedict Lust had already used “medicine of the future” as a description of naturopathy, so this may conflate a slogan or periodical contribution with a separately authored book; it should not be treated as a secure Bradshaw obra without examination of the alleged 1914 original.
