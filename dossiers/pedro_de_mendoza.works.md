## Works by Pedro de Mendoza

### Documentary protocols

- **21 May 1534:** *Capitulación con la Corona para la conquista y población del Río de la Plata*. A negotiated project-charter and protocol, not Mendoza’s sole-authored text: Charles V granted offices and privileges while Mendoza assumed responsibility for financing, staffing and conducting an expedition. The surviving text’s territorial wording has generated materially different interpretations, including a disputed transcription of “hacia” as “hasta” the Strait of Magellan. ([Patrimonio Argentino](https://patrimonioargentino.cultura.gob.ar/index.php/Detail/collections/6811); [PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/autoridad/177824))

- **21 August 1535:** *Testamento de Pedro de Mendoza*. A testamentary protocol made immediately before departure, ordering Mendoza’s property and the succession to his American enterprise; it therefore operates simultaneously as a private will and as institutional planning for an unfinished project. A later codicil reportedly replaced his dead brother Diego de Mendoza and nephew Pedro Benavides with Juan de Ayolas as successor. ([Papeles rioplatenses](https://www.tesisenred.net/bitstream/handle/10803/671132/ARANO_TESIS.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1))

- **20 April 1537:** *Poder otorgado a Francisco Ruiz Galán*. A juridical instrument authorizing Ruiz Galán to govern as Mendoza’s lieutenant during his absence, rather than a descriptive composition. The Museo Mitre catalogue records the surviving witness as a six-leaf manuscript copy derived from the Archivo General de Indias. ([Museo Mitre, *Documentos del Archivo Colonial*](https://museomitre.cultura.gob.ar/media/uploads/site-10/multimedia/documentos_del_archivo_colonial.pdf))

- **20–21 April 1537:** *Instrucción que Pedro de Mendoza dejó a Juan de Ayolas*. A protocol of government, exploration and negotiation intended to act after Mendoza’s departure; scholarship dates it to 1537, while one documentary discussion specifies 21 April. What survives is a copy or traslado, reportedly made from a paper found in Mendoza’s desk when his possessions were inventoried, so its material form is not the lost original. ([Patrimonio Argentino](https://patrimonioargentino.cultura.gob.ar/index.php/Detail/collections/6814); [Museo Mitre](https://museomitre.cultura.gob.ar/media/uploads/site-10/multimedia/documentos_del_archivo_colonial.pdf); [Loreley El Jaber](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/5447237.pdf))

### Expeditionary project

- **1534–1537:** *Empresa, armada y gobernación del Río de la Plata*. A large, privately financed imperial-colonial project comprising ships, people, supplies, conquest, settlement and delegated journeys—not a single object and not safely reducible to a voyage. Its scale is disputed: a contemporary roster made in May 1535 names 738 men before later additions, whereas chroniclers and historians supplied estimates ranging from roughly 800 to 2,650; its ultimate failure and the suffering it produced are part of the obra’s status, not incidental defects. ([Revista del Museo de La Plata](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/96687/Documento_completo.pdf?sequence=1); [Universidad de Almería](https://www2.ual.es/ideimand/pedro-de-mendoza-adelantado-del-rio-de-la-plata-h-1499-1537/); [Carlos Rossi Elgue](https://www.bn.gov.ar/micrositios/admin_assets/issues/files/ead9d3c8d65ff8d12f30f8869783af58.pdf))

### Settlements and territorial manifestations

- **Early February 1536:** *Puerto de Nuestra Señora Santa María del Buen Aire*. A precarious fortified port and settlement established under Mendoza’s command, conventionally called the “first foundation” of Buenos Aires. Sources disagree between 2 and 3 February, and no foundation act comparable to Juan de Garay’s 1580 instrument is known; whether it constituted a formally founded city, rather than an encampment or port, remains disputed. It was abandoned and destroyed in 1541, so the present city is not an uninterrupted material work by Mendoza. ([Carlos Rossi Elgue](https://www.bn.gov.ar/micrositios/admin_assets/issues/files/ead9d3c8d65ff8d12f30f8869783af58.pdf); [Museo Mitre](https://museomitre.cultura.gob.ar/media/uploads/site-10/multimedia/documentos_del_archivo_colonial.pdf))

- **1536:** *Corpus Christi*. A refounded or renamed upriver stronghold within Mendoza’s enterprise, materially made by Juan de Ayolas and his company under delegated command. Its attribution therefore resists the formula “work by Mendoza”: it belongs to his colonial project, but not securely to his personal execution, and sources differ over its precise relationship to Sebastián Caboto’s earlier Sancti Spiritus and to Buena Esperanza. ([Ruy Díaz de Guzmán, *Historia argentina*](https://cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/historia-argentina-del-descubrimiento-poblacion-y-conquista-de-las-provincias-del-rio-de-la-plata--0/html/ff57d7e8-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_13.html); [Museo Mitre](https://museomitre.cultura.gob.ar/media/uploads/site-10/multimedia/documentos_del_archivo_colonial.pdf))

- **1536–1537:** *Exploraciones del Paraná and the search for the route to the “Sierra de la Plata”*. A delegated, open-ended project made through instructions, vessels and expeditions led chiefly by Ayolas and later relief parties. It resists treatment as Mendoza’s individual work because intention and authority were his, while its journeys, discoveries and physical labor belonged to numerous agents. ([Ruy Díaz de Guzmán, *Historia argentina*](https://cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/historia-argentina-del-descubrimiento-poblacion-y-conquista-de-las-provincias-del-rio-de-la-plata--0/html/ff57d7e8-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_13.html); [Carlos Rossi Elgue](https://www.bn.gov.ar/micrositios/admin_assets/issues/files/ead9d3c8d65ff8d12f30f8869783af58.pdf))

### Posthumous manifestation—not a work by Mendoza

- **23 June 1937:** *Monumento a Don Pedro de Mendoza*. A commemorative Gesamtkunstwerk in Parque Lezama—bronze figure, travertine-clad architectural screen, reliefs and symbolic fountain—made by sculptor Juan Carlos Oliva Navarro and inaugurated on the four-hundredth anniversary of Mendoza’s death. It is a later manifestation of the Mendoza foundation narrative, emphatically not something Mendoza made; its imagery converts a contested, impermanent settlement into a monumental founding act. ([Moderna Buenos Aires](https://w.modernabuenosaires.org/obras/20s-a-70s/monumento-a-don-pedro-de-mendoza); [Argentina.gob.ar](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/escultura-pedro-de-mendoza))
