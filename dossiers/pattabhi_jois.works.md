## Works by Krishna Pattabhi Jois

### Pedagogical system and institution

- **From the late 1930s; continually revised:** *Ashtanga Vinyasa Yoga*. A transmitted and codified bodily protocol joining fixed āsana sequences to counted movement, breath, gaze, and bandha; scholarship credits Jois with developing or codifying the modern system while emphasizing its debts to T. Krishnamacharya and earlier physical-culture and haṭhayoga traditions. Its claimed derivation from an ancient *Yoga Koruṇṭa* cannot be verified, and the sequences changed during Jois’s lifetime, so this resists treatment as a single, finished obra. ([University of Virginia, *Ashtanga Yoga: A Curated Guide*](https://texts.mandala.library.virginia.edu/text/ashtanga-yoga-curated-guide); [Sidnell, “Technique and the Threat of Deethicalization”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/signs-and-society/article/technique-and-the-threat-of-deethicalization/825E2F2C1F2AB8E162B1DE8DA685C9FD))

- **1948–2009:** *Ashtanga Yoga Research Institute* (later Shri K. Pattabhi Jois Ashtanga Yoga Institute). A school and long-duration pedagogical project founded in Jois’s Mysore home, not a text; its daily teaching, authorization, and lineage practices were part of the work. Sources consistently give 1948, although some lineage accounts give 1942, a discrepancy that should remain visible. ([University of Virginia, *Ashtanga Yoga: A Curated Guide*](https://texts.mandala.library.virginia.edu/book_pubreader/63181); [Sidnell, “Technique and the Threat of Deethicalization”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/signs-and-society/article/technique-and-the-threat-of-deethicalization/825E2F2C1F2AB8E162B1DE8DA685C9FD); [Institut d’Ashtanga Yoga biography, giving 1942](https://yoga-ashtanga.net/en/sri-k-pattabhi-jois-transmission/))

### Authored books and protocols

- **Written 1958–1961; published 1962:** *Yoga Mala*. Jois’s principal authored obra: a Kannada treatise and illustrated instructional protocol combining yoga philosophy with detailed directions for Sūrya Namaskāra and the primary āsana sequence. The English translation appeared in 1999 and added photographs made decades later, so those later visual elements belong to the translated edition rather than wholly to the 1962 object. ([Sidnell, “Technique and the Threat of Deethicalization”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/signs-and-society/article/technique-and-the-threat-of-deethicalization/825E2F2C1F2AB8E162B1DE8DA685C9FD); [North Point Press](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780865477513/yogamala/); [*Journal of Yoga Studies*, vol. 2](https://journalofyogastudies.org/index.php/JoYS/issue/download/2019.V2/Full%20Issue%20%7C%202019%20%E2%80%A2%20Volume%202))

- **1973:** *Yoga—Diploma Course: Syllabus and Examination*. A typed protocol listing instructions for four series—Primary, Intermediate, Advanced A, and Advanced B—given by Jois to Nancy Gilgoff in Mysore. It survives as a privately transmitted teaching document rather than a conventionally published book, and its witness-specific character makes completeness uncertain. ([Suzanne Newcombe and Karen O’Brien-Kop, eds., *Routledge Handbook of Yoga and Meditation Studies*, bibliography](https://s3-euw1-ap-pe-df-pch-content-store-p.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/9781003080121/90845b1c-7777-44b9-aef9-a3148657cc30/googleScholarPdf.pdf))

- **2005:** *Sūryanamaskāra*. A short illustrated instructional book or composite protocol issued by the Ashtanga Yoga Research Institute/Ashtanga Yoga New York, explaining the two sun-salutation sequences and their ritual and practical setting. Its contents also include editorial matter, an interview, and a traditional *Āditya Hṛdayam*, so the physical book is not wholly an individually authored Jois text. ([*Routledge Handbook of Yoga and Meditation Studies*, bibliography](https://s3-euw1-ap-pe-df-pch-content-store-p.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/9781003080121/90845b1c-7777-44b9-aef9-a3148657cc30/googleScholarPdf.pdf); [Andrew Hillam, bibliographical description](https://andrewhillam.wordpress.com/2017/04/17/what-is-suryanamaskara/))

### Manifesto and occasional texts

- **1995:** *Ashtanga vs. Power Yoga*. A letter published as “From Our Readers” in *Yoga Journal*, issue 125, p. 6; it functions as a manifesto defending Jois’s system against its rebranding as “Power Yoga.” The attribution is not straightforward: linguistic analysis notes that Jois’s limited English makes sole composition unlikely, although the letter appeared under his name and asserted his position. ([Sidnell, “Technique and the Threat of Deethicalization,” especially note 1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/signs-and-society/article/technique-and-the-threat-of-deethicalization/825E2F2C1F2AB8E162B1DE8DA685C9FD); [*Routledge Handbook of Yoga and Meditation Studies*, bibliography](https://s3-euw1-ap-pe-df-pch-content-store-p.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/9781003080121/90845b1c-7777-44b9-aef9-a3148657cc30/googleScholarPdf.pdf))

- **Spring 2003:** *An Informal Public Talk on Traditional Yoga*. A published transcript of oral teaching in *Nāmarūpa*, pp. 9–12: a manifestation of a talk rather than prose demonstrably composed for publication, and therefore resistant to classification as an authored article. ([Sidnell, “Ethical Practice and Techniques of the Self at a Yoga School in Southern India,” bibliography](https://rai.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1467-8322.12361?scrollTo=references))

- **2004:** *3 Gurus, 48 Questions*. Jois’s portion of R. Alexander Medin’s comparative interview with Jois, B. K. S. Iyengar, and T. K. V. Desikachar, published in *Nāmarūpa* 3.2, pp. 6–18. It is an elicited oral manifestation jointly shaped by interviewer, transcription, translation, and editing, not an independently authored Jois work. ([*Routledge Handbook of Yoga and Meditation Studies*, bibliography](https://s3-euw1-ap-pe-df-pch-content-store-p.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/9781003080121/90845b1c-7777-44b9-aef9-a3148657cc30/googleScholarPdf.pdf); [*Nāmarūpa*, issue description](https://namarupa.org/product/issue-19/))

### Recorded teaching manifestations

- **11 June 1989:** *Ashtanga Yoga, Series I, II, III & IV*. Four filmed teaching demonstrations in which Jois directs the sequences; Tom Sewell made the recording. These are records of Jois’s embodied protocol rather than films authored by him, and their title appears to describe an archival grouping rather than a securely documented original release. ([Institut d’Ashtanga Yoga, filmography](https://yoga-ashtanga.net/en/dvd-videos-traditional-yoga/))

- **1993; released on VHS by 1996:** *Ashtanga Yoga, 1st and 2nd Series*. A two-part instructional video manifestation produced by Yoga Works, with six practitioners executing the sequences while Jois supplies the Sanskrit names and vinyasa count. Scholarship dates the filming to 1993, while bibliographies commonly date the commercial tapes to 1996; Jois is the recorded instructor, not the sole maker of the audiovisual object. ([Sidnell, “Technique and the Threat of Deethicalization”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/signs-and-society/article/technique-and-the-threat-of-deethicalization/825E2F2C1F2AB8E162B1DE8DA685C9FD); [Gregor Maehle, *Ashtanga Yoga: Practice and Philosophy*, bibliography](https://irp.cdn-website.com/4a1b0e63/files/uploaded/Gregor%20Maehle-Ashtanga%20Yoga-%20Practice%20and%20Philosophy.pdf))
