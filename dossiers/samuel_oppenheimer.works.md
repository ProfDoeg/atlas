## Works by Samuel Oppenheimer

### Commercial and logistical obras

- **c. 1660–1703:** Imperial military-supply and credit network. This was an enterprise rather than a titled work: Oppenheimer assembled a trans-European system of lenders, contractors, grain suppliers, river transport, military magazines, and commodity credit, initially for the Palatinate and from 1672 for Emperor Leopold I; its dependence on his personal credit became apparent when it nearly collapsed at his death. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd119272229.html#ndbcontent))

- **1683–1699:** Supply system for the relief of Vienna and the reconquest of Hungary. An operational project within the larger network, it provisioned soldiers and horses, moved grain down the Danube with eight ships and fifty-two crewmen, and financed campaigns and the Peace of Karlowitz; it resists classification as a discrete “work” because its surviving form is a dispersed body of transactions rather than a single object. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd119272229.html#ndbcontent))

- **1701–1703:** Joint financing and provisioning of the imperial army in the War of the Spanish Succession. Made with Samson Wertheimer after Oppenheimer became *Oberkriegsfaktor*, this was a collaborative financial-logistical project, not a text or individually bounded composition. ([Jewish Encyclopedia, “Wertheimer, Samson”](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/14868-wertheimer-samson); [Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd119272229.html#ndbcontent))

### Communal institutions and built works

- **1674–1703:** Re-establishment of a Jewish community in Vienna. This was a continuing institutional project enabled by Oppenheimer’s residence privilege and extended household after the expulsion of 1670; it has no fixed title or completion date and should not be mistaken for a building alone. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd119272229.html#ndbcontent))

- **1686 or 1696:** Jewish cemetery in the Rossau, now the Seegasse cemetery. Oppenheimer acquired or recovered the burial ground, returned it to use, and replaced its destroyed wooden enclosure with a stone wall; institutional sources give 1686 for the purchase, while the Austrian National Library dates acquisition and rebuilding to 1696, so the date remains unsettled. ([Israelitische Kultusgemeinde Wien](https://www.ikg-wien.at/en/about-the-jewish-community-of-vienna); [Austrian National Library](https://www.onb.ac.at/mehr/blogs/detail/juedisches-erbe-in-wien-der-friedhof-in-der-rossau))

- **1698:** Jewish hospital or *Versorgungshaus* in the Rossau. A charitable institution and built obra erected near the cemetery for Vienna’s Jewish population; later rebuilding and replacement mean that “Oppenheimer’s hospital” denotes both his foundation and a building that did not survive in its original form. ([Austrian National Library](https://www.onb.ac.at/mehr/blogs/detail/juedisches-erbe-in-wien-der-friedhof-in-der-rossau); [University of Vienna archive record](https://scopeq.cc.univie.ac.at/Query/detail.aspx?ID=231083))

### Collections

- **late seventeenth century–1703:** Hebrew manuscript and book collection. This was a collecting project rather than an authored text: manuscripts obtained with Prince Eugene’s assistance formed part of the nucleus inherited and greatly enlarged by Oppenheimer’s nephew David. Older reference literature calls Samuel the library’s founder, whereas modern scholarship identifies David as the maker of the famous Oppenheim library and treats Samuel’s collection or gift as its starting stock; the boundaries and contents of Samuel’s own collection are therefore not securely reconstructable. ([Jewish Encyclopedia, “Book-Collectors”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/3553-book-collectors); [Neue Deutsche Biographie, “Oppenheimer, David”](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd138991979.html#ndbcontent))

### Sponsored texts

- **1699:** *Ḥavvot Ya’ir* (*Villages of Jair*), by Yair Ḥayim Bacharach. A printed collection of responsa whose publication Oppenheimer helped sponsor jointly with Samson Wertheimer; Oppenheimer’s name in connection with the edition records patronage, not authorship, and the text itself is Bacharach’s obra. ([Posen Library](https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/havvot-yair-villages-yair-curriculum-study); [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Wertheimer, Samson”](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/14868-wertheimer-samson))

### Political-textual intervention

- **1700:** Campaign against Johann Andreas Eisenmenger’s *Entdecktes Judenthum*. This was a project directed at the circulation of another person’s text: Oppenheimer used money and influence at court and among the Jesuits to secure an imperial prohibition. The resulting edict was an operative text—a protocol or manifesto in the broad sense—but it was issued by the emperor and cannot be listed as Oppenheimer’s authored work. ([Samuel Oppenheimer biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd119272229.html#ndbcontent); [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Wertheimer, Samson”](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/14868-wertheimer-samson))

### Authored writings

- **No securely identified authored corpus:** The standard scholarly biography and Max Grunwald’s archival monograph document Oppenheimer as banker, military supplier, patron, collector, and institutional founder, but do not establish a bibliography of books, treatises, or other titled writings by him. Surviving contracts, accounts, petitions, and correspondence are evidence of his enterprises; without item-level attribution they resist treatment as individually authored obras. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd119272229.html#ndbcontent); [Grunwald](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Samuel_Oppenheimer_und_sein_Kreis_(ein_Kapitel_aus_der_Finanzgeschichte_%C3%96sterreichs)_(IA_samueloppenheime00grun).pdf))
