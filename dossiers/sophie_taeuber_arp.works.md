## Works by Sophie Taeuber-Arp

### Catalogue status and limits of the obra

- **c. 1903–1943:** *Complete catalogued oeuvre*. The continuously revised Sophie Taeuber-Arp Research Project database presently records 1,025 objects: applied-art objects, textiles, beadwork, works on paper, paintings, reliefs, sculpture, models, photographs, and collaborations. This corpus is the nearest available catalogue raisonné, but it explicitly remains provisional; most titles and many dates were assigned after Taeuber-Arp’s death, chiefly by Hugo Weber, and therefore should not be mistaken for the artist’s own fixed nomenclature. ([STARP, “About the Database”](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/catalogue/ueber-den-werkkatalog/))

### Textiles, beadwork, and usable objects

- **c. 1903:** *[Dish towel]*. An early woven or embroidered household textile; the bracketed title is cataloguer-supplied, and its status oscillates between exercise, useful object, and obra. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **c. 1913:** *Box with Hand Embroidery*. A decorated container combining embroidery and a usable object; the catalogue records the title as one used during the artist’s lifetime. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **c. 1913:** *[Child’s Bonnet]*. A wearable textile object, not simply a design on paper; its bracketed title is not Taeuber-Arp’s own. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **c. 1914:** *Cushion with Silk Embroidery (Black Cloth)*. An embroidered furnishing whose geometric surface is inseparable from its domestic function. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **c. 1914:** *Bag in Bead Weaving*. A functional bead-woven object and abstract composition at once. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **c. 1914:** *Painted Wooden Box (for an Eau-de-Cologne Bottle)*. A painted container made for a particular use, resisting the later division between sculpture, decoration, and product design. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1915–1916:** *Vertical-Horizontal Composition*. An embroidered or woven geometric composition; several distinct works share this catalogue title, so it names neither one unique object nor a formally declared series by the artist. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/); [MoMA exhibition checklist](https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2021-11-16_Taeuber-Arp-Checklist-for-MoMA-by-Gallery_compressed.pdf))

- **c. 1915:** *[Tablecloth]*. A designed domestic textile whose use-value is part of the work rather than incidental to it. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1916:** *Cushion Panel*. A wool-and-cotton cross-stitch embroidery designed and executed by Taeuber-Arp; it is simultaneously a textile component, abstract image, and model of the teaching practice she developed in Zurich. ([Museum für Gestaltung Zürich](https://www.eguide.ch/de/objekt/ohne-titel-5/))

- **c. 1918:** *Beaded Bag*. A glass- and metal-bead purse: wearable applied art whose grid is also an autonomous abstract composition. ([MoMA exhibition checklist](https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2021-11-16_Taeuber-Arp-Checklist-for-MoMA-by-Gallery_compressed.pdf))

- **c. 1918:** *Geometric Forms (Beaded Bag)*. A second surviving bead-woven bag, catalogued separately from *Beaded Bag* despite their shared object type and approximate date. ([MoMA exhibition checklist](https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2021-11-16_Taeuber-Arp-Checklist-for-MoMA-by-Gallery_compressed.pdf))

- **1910s–1920s:** *Untitled embroidery, lace, weaving, textile, beadwork, costume, and furniture designs*. This is a body of hundreds of designs and executed objects rather than one securely bounded work; many lack artist-given titles or firm dates, and the evolving catalogue treats each surviving sheet or object separately. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/); [Kunstmuseum Basel](https://kunstmuseumbasel.ch/en/exhibitions/2021/sophie-taeuber-arp))

### Dance and performative manifestations

- **29 March 1917:** *Dance at the Opening of Galerie Dada*. An embodied manifestation performed to Hugo Ball’s poetry in a costume associated with Jean Arp and a mask often associated with Marcel Janco. Photographs and later captions have also placed the image at the Cabaret Voltaire in 1916; current research regards the Galerie Dada performance as the only securely documented identification. ([Stiftung Arp research publication](https://stiftungarp.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/STA_Stipendiatenzeitschrift_210x275_RZ_digital_Doppelseiten.pdf); [Art Journal](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00043249.2014.918806))

- **27 June 1917:** *Closing Lecture Evening of the Laban School, Zurich*. A documented public dance manifestation; no stable individual choreography title survives, so it should not be converted into a titled object retrospectively. ([Stiftung Arp research publication](https://stiftungarp.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/STA_Stipendiatenzeitschrift_210x275_RZ_digital_Doppelseiten.pdf))

- **15–24 August 1917:** *Dance at the Ordo Templi Orientis Congress, Monte Verità*. A documented performance appearance, but the precise date, choreography, and title within the congress remain unsettled. ([Stiftung Arp research publication](https://stiftungarp.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/STA_Stipendiatenzeitschrift_210x275_RZ_digital_Doppelseiten.pdf))

- **1916–1919:** *Other Dada and Laban dances*. Accounts attest further dancing, rehearsals, and participation, including the 1919 Dada soirée associated with *Noir Kakadu*, but the boundary between Taeuber-Arp’s choreography, interpretation, private practice, and collaborative event is too uncertain to make these into discrete authored works. ([Stiftung Arp research publication](https://stiftungarp.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/STA_Stipendiatenzeitschrift_210x275_RZ_digital_Doppelseiten.pdf); [Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/overview/dadaism%26gt))

### Marionettes, stage designs, and a theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1918:** *König Hirsch (The Stag King)*. A theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk for Werner Wolff and René Morax’s adaptation of Carlo Gozzi: Taeuber-Arp designed a set of seventeen articulated marionettes and associated scenery and drawings. The production is collaborative, while the surviving turned-and-painted figures are individually catalogued objects. ([Museum für Gestaltung Zürich](https://www.eguide.ch/de/objekt/ohne-titel-5/); [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/5777))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: King Deramo]*. A turned, joined, painted, and costumed wooden marionette; the bracketed character-title is cataloguer-supplied. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/konig-hirsch-deramo/))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: Tartaglia]*. A painted wooden marionette incorporating brass, silk, and beads. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/knig-hirsch-tartaglia-original-und-reproduktion/))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: Truffaldino]*. An articulated figure made from painted wood, feathers, wire, wool, thread, and glass beads. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/knig-hirsch-truffaldino-original-und-reproduktion/))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: Leander]*. A turned and painted wooden marionette with textile elements; its title is not documented as Taeuber-Arp’s own. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/knig-hirsch-leandro-original-und-reproduktion/))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: Dr. Komplex]*. A painted, articulated wooden marionette with brass fittings. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/knig-hirsch-dr-komplex-original-und-reproduktion/))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: Freudanalytikus]*. A painted wooden and brass marionette whose satirical character belongs to the adaptation’s Dada and psychoanalytic reworking. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/knig-hirsch-freudanalytikus-ot-original-und-reproduktion/))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: Stag]*. A painted wooden and brass animal marionette, distinct from the human characters. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/konig-hirsch-hirsch-original-und-reproduktion/))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: Tree]*. A mobile scenic figure made from painted wood, metal, textile, and feathers; it resists the distinction between puppet, prop, sculpture, and scenery. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/knig-hirsch-baum-original-und-reproduktion/))

- **1918:** *[König Hirsch: Study for the Marionette Tartaglia]*. A preparatory pencil drawing on tracing paper: part of the theatrical project, but also catalogued as an independent work on paper. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/knig-hirsch-studie-fr-eine-marionette-minister-tartaglia/))

- **1918:** *König Hirsch designs for the remaining figures, costumes, and sets*. The catalogue preserves additional sheets and surviving marionettes from the seventeen-figure ensemble; some titles are retrospective descriptions, and later replicas must not be confused with Taeuber-Arp’s 1918 originals. ([STARP, “About the Database”](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/catalogue/ueber-den-werkkatalog/); [MoMA catalogue](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2261_300062660.pdf))

### Sculptural heads and reliefs

- **1918:** *Untitled*. A turned and painted wooden object commonly discussed with Taeuber-Arp’s Dada heads; the absence of a secure artist-given title is material to its status. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/5777))

- **1920:** *Head*. A turned and painted wooden sculpture, often called a “Dada Head”; it draws on hat stands and applied woodturning, blurring portrait, utilitarian prototype, sculpture, and performed object. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/5777))

- **1920:** *Dada Head*. A polychrome turned-wood head distinct from the MoMA *Head*; “Dada Head” is now conventional, but the catalogue warns generally that most titles were imposed posthumously. ([STARP, “About the Database”](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/catalogue/ueber-den-werkkatalog/))

- **1927:** *Composition Aubette*. Two distinct oil-on-card reliefs share this title and catalogue year; both have unknown present whereabouts, and their titles connect them to the Strasbourg interior project without making them fragments of the installed rooms. ([STARP, CR 1927/17](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/composition-aubette-2/); [STARP, CR 1927/18](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/composition-aubette-4/))

- **1927:** *[Composition for the Aubette]*. A gouache relief assembled from cut and pasted board; it is both an autonomous object and a project-model related to the interior commission. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/composition-for-the-aubette/))

- **1920s–1940s:** *Painted wood reliefs, free-standing sculpture, and architectural models*. This is a dispersed body of individually catalogued objects, sometimes made in more than one version; later posthumous re-creations commissioned or produced by Jean Arp complicate what can securely be called an original Taeuber-Arp object. ([STARP, “About the Database”](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/catalogue/ueber-den-werkkatalog/); [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/5777))

### Architecture, interiors, furniture, murals, and stained glass

- **1921:** *Furniture for Villa Suhaglia, St. Moritz*. An interior-furnishing project rather than a single portable artwork; surviving documentation does not establish that every component was executed solely by Taeuber-Arp. ([MoMA exhibition publication](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/5206))

- **1926:** *André Horn Apartment, Strasbourg*. Taeuber-Arp designed the apartment’s interior and decoration; the installed work was subsequently destroyed, so the obra survives principally through plans, photographs, and related objects. ([MoMA, “Immersive Art Projects”](https://mobile-api.moma.org/magazine/articles/674))

- **1926:** *Bar-Dancing, Hôtel Hannong, Strasbourg*. An integrated interior of walls, fittings, and furniture, now destroyed. It is best classified as an architectural project or Gesamtkunstwerk rather than as mural painting alone. ([MoMA, “Immersive Art Projects”](https://mobile-api.moma.org/magazine/articles/674))

- **1927–1928:** *Stained-Glass Windows for the André Horn Apartment*. Geometric architectural glass conceived for a specific interior; the windows belong to the spatial commission while also being separately designed objects. ([Kunstmuseum Basel exhibition booklet](https://kunstmuseumbasel.ch/en/file/3899/f25c9b50/KMB_STA_Booklet_EN.pdf))

- **1926–1928:** *L’Aubette, Strasbourg*. A collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk by Taeuber-Arp, Jean Arp, and Theo van Doesburg, turning an eighteenth-century building into an entertainment complex. Taeuber-Arp was responsible especially for the Five-O’Clock tearoom and Aubette-Bar and contributed architectural coordination, murals, stained glass, furniture, and graphic unity; the original interiors were later covered or destroyed and are now only partially reconstructed. ([MoMA, “Immersive Art Projects”](https://mobile-api.moma.org/magazine/articles/674); [Musées de Strasbourg](https://www.musees.strasbourg.eu/documents/d/musees/dp-aubette-1928))

- **1927–1930:** *Heimendinger House, Strasbourg*. An interior-design and furnishing project documented in Taeuber-Arp’s presentation album; authorship attaches to the ensemble, while individual components and interventions were collaborative or executed by tradespeople. ([Arp Museum](https://arpmuseum.org/museum/unser-haus/news/arpinsight-auf-der-reise-durch-den-kosmos-arp-mit-kuratorin-astrid-von-asten-die-innenarchitektin-sophie-taeuber-arp.html))

- **1927–1929:** *House-Studio of Jean Arp and Sophie Taeuber-Arp, Clamart*. A three-level house and pair of studios built from Taeuber-Arp’s plans. It is architecture, furniture, workplace, and lived Gesamtkunstwerk; later additions to the property are not hers. ([Fondation Arp](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_collection.html); [Clamart municipal heritage service](https://patrimoine.clamart.fr/monuments/16))

- **1928–1930:** *Galerie Goemans, Paris*. An interior and furnishing commission whose original installation no longer survives intact; it should be understood as a spatial project rather than reduced to its documentary photographs. ([MoMA exhibition publication](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/5206))

- **1930:** *Ernest Rott Apartment, Strasbourg*. An interior-design commission incorporating modern furniture and architectural color. Surviving documentation is incomplete, making its exact boundaries uncertain. ([MoMA exhibition checklist](https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2021-11-16_Taeuber-Arp-Checklist-for-MoMA-by-Gallery_compressed.pdf))

- **1930:** *Werner Apartment, Paris*. A domestic interior project; not every furnishing visible in documentary photographs can automatically be attributed to Taeuber-Arp. ([MoMA exhibition checklist](https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2021-11-16_Taeuber-Arp-Checklist-for-MoMA-by-Gallery_compressed.pdf))

- **1926–1930:** *Drawings, plans, axonometric views, presentation photographs, and furniture designs for the Strasbourg and Paris interiors*. These sheets are simultaneously project documents and catalogued works. Their existence does not prove that every proposed element was built. ([STARP](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/catalogue/ueber-den-werkkatalog/); [MoMA](https://mobile-api.moma.org/magazine/articles/674))

### Paintings, drawings, collages, and prints

- **1905:** *[Snowberry]*. An early representational study whose title is supplied by cataloguers rather than securely documented as Taeuber-Arp’s. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1906:** *[Portrait of Mrs Sophie Taeuber-Krüsi]*. An early portrait, catalogued under a descriptive bracketed title. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1909:** *[The Park at Hofleben]*. A landscape or nature study; both identification and title come through later cataloguing. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **c. 1909:** *Designs after Nature*. A group or portfolio designation for studies rather than a single unequivocally bounded picture. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1910:** *Still Life*. Two separately catalogued works share this generic title and date. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1910:** *[Untitled (Nasturtiums)]*. A floral work whose descriptive subtitle was added for identification. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1913:** *[Untitled (Embroidery Design)]*. A work on paper made as a protocol for execution in another medium, although the design is now catalogued as an object in its own right. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1917:** *Vertical, Horizontal, Square, Rectangular*. An abstract composition now known through a posthumous descriptive title; the exact dating rests on the catalogue’s broader reconstruction of an often-undated oeuvre. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/5777))

- **1915–1925:** *[Untitled (Birds)]*. Several distinct decorative or textile-related designs share this bracketed description; they should not be collapsed into one unique titled work. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1915–1925:** *[Untitled (Boat)]*. A design with a boat motif; both the wide date range and descriptive title register unresolved evidence. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/))

- **1916–1918:** *Duo-collages and orthogonal compositions with Jean Arp*. Collaborative works in which authorship is deliberately shared and sometimes materially impossible to divide. Individual sheets belong to both artists’ oeuvres rather than requiring attribution to one hand. ([Fondation Arp biography](https://fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbio.html); [STARP, “About the Database”](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/catalogue/ueber-den-werkkatalog/))

- **1926:** *Five Extended Figures*. A gouache, metallic-paint, and pencil composition on paper, translating bodily extension into an abstract geometric vocabulary. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/5206))

- **1930:** *Composition*. An abstract painting from Taeuber-Arp’s Paris period; the generic title is posthumous rather than evidence that she conceived it as a uniquely named picture. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/5777))

- **1933:** *Cover for transition, no. 22*. A magazine cover: graphic design made for circulation, not an illustrated “book” and not simply a reproduction of an independent painting. ([MoMA catalogue bibliography](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2261_300062660.pdf))

- **1934:** *Échelonnement désaxé*. An abstract work organized through displaced or off-axis progression; its French title derives from the posthumous catalogue tradition unless contrary lifetime documentation is found. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/5777))

- **1935:** *Plate for 23 Gravures*. A print made for a multi-artist portfolio, catalogued both as a graphic work and through its larger printed context. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/5777))

- **1937:** *Der gestiefelte Stern*. A project for a book of Jean Arp’s poems with Taeuber-Arp’s illustrations, prepared with Jan Tschichold but left unpublished because of the war; the corrected proofs were issued only posthumously in 1978. The intended book is therefore a real unfinished project, while the 1978 object is not a lifetime edition. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))

- **1939:** *Gradation*. An abstract painting from a series of staggered wave-like forms; “Gradation” designates both this individual work and a compositional procedure recurring in related works. ([Kunstmuseum Basel exhibition booklet](https://kunstmuseumbasel.ch/en/file/3899/f25c9b50/KMB_STA_Booklet_EN.pdf))

- **1939:** *Muscheln und Schirme*. A privately printed book of Jean Arp’s poems with drawings by Taeuber-Arp and typography by Jan Tschichold. Her made contribution is the graphic sequence and its relation to the printed object, not the poems. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))

- **1941:** *Poèmes sans prénoms*. A privately printed collection of Jean Arp’s poems containing three drawings by Taeuber-Arp; it is a collaborative printed obra with distinct verbal and visual authorship. ([MoMA catalogue bibliography](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2261_300062660.pdf))

- **1941–1942:** *Duo-drawings with Jean Arp*. Collaborative line drawings made principally during wartime displacement in southern France. The name *duo-dessin* appeared in print only in 1946, so it is a retrospective category rather than necessarily the collaborators’ original title for every sheet. ([MoMA Magazine](https://mobile-api.moma.org/magazine/articles/665))

- **1942:** *Lithograph for 10 Origin*. A print contributed to the Zurich *Allianz* publication; it is one component of a collective printed work. ([MoMA catalogue bibliography](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2261_300062660.pdf))

- **1942–1943:** *Last nine drawings*. A group of late line drawings selected and published in *Les Derniers 9 Dessins de Sophie Taeuber-Arp* in 1943. The collective title belongs to the posthumous publication and should not be mistaken for a series title fixed by Taeuber-Arp. ([MoMA catalogue bibliography](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2261_300062660.pdf))

- **1943:** *1924, 1925, 1926, 1943*. A book project pairing poems by Jean Arp with one drawing by Taeuber-Arp; its 1944 publication was posthumous, so the printed edition is not an object she saw completed. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))

- **1943:** *Rire de coquille*. A Jean Arp poetry booklet containing Taeuber-Arp’s visual contribution, published posthumously in 1944. Her authorship pertains to the drawing or design, not to the poems. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))

- **1946:** *Le siège de l’air*. A posthumous collection of Jean Arp’s poems incorporating eight duo-drawings by Arp and Taeuber-Arp. The drawings are collaborative lifetime works; the 1946 compilation and its title are not Taeuber-Arp’s completed book. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))

- **1910s–1943:** *Remaining drawings, gouaches, watercolours, paintings, collages, prints, photographs, and designs in the catalogue raisonné*. These are  individually inventoried in STARP rather than safely compressible into a finite list of artist-named works: many are untitled, several repeat the same descriptive title, and most dates were reconstructed after her death. ([STARP catalogue](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/catalogue/); [STARP, “About the Database”](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/english/catalogue/ueber-den-werkkatalog/))

### Protocols, essays, poems, and editorial works

- **1922:** *Bemerkungen über den Unterricht im ornamentalen Entwerfen*. An essay on teaching ornamental design, published in the Swiss professional and domestic-science teachers’ association bulletin; it describes and argues for a pedagogy rather than functioning as a manifesto. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))

- **1927:** *Anleitung zum Unterricht im Zeichnen für textile Berufe*. A pedagogical protocol co-authored with Blanche Gauchat and issued by Zurich’s Gewerbeschule. It instructs teachers in systematic design for textile professions and therefore is not merely a book “about” Taeuber-Arp’s art. ([STARP bibliography](https://sophietaeuberarp.org/taeuber-arp-sophie-und-gauchat-blanche-zeichnen-fur-textile-berufe/); [digitized original](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/26060529.pdf))

- **1937–1939:** *Plastique/Plastic, nos. 1–5*. A trilingual Paris–New York periodical edited, administered, distributed, laid out, and graphically designed chiefly by Taeuber-Arp, with César Domela, George L. K. Morris, Jean Arp, and others contributing. It is an editorial project and collective platform—arguably manifesto-like in what it did for threatened abstract art—rather than a single-authored text. ([Arp Museum](https://arpmuseum.org/ausstellungen/wechselausstellungen/vorschau/kosmos-arp-erweiterte-label/sophie-taeuber-arp-plastique-vol-1-5.html); [Kunstmuseum Basel exhibition booklet](https://kunstmuseumbasel.ch/en/file/3899/f25c9b50/KMB_STA_Booklet_EN.pdf))

- **10 June 1939:** *Le Rêve*. A short text dated at Ascona and first published in Georg Schmidt’s 1948 monograph; its posthumous publication makes the surviving text a lifetime manuscript but the printed form an editorial manifestation after her death. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))

- **undated; published 1960:** *… et le voyage continua …*. A short literary text published posthumously in *Zweiklang*; the available bibliography does not establish a secure composition date, so none should be supplied. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))

- **1937; published 1960:** *Regula zur Konfirmation 1937*. A short occasional text written for Regula’s confirmation and issued posthumously in *Zweiklang*. It resists classification as a public literary work because its original status was private and personal. ([Fondation Arp bibliography](https://www.fondationarp.org/en_taeuberbiblio.html))
