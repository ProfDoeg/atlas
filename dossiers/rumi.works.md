## Works by Jalal al-Din Rumi

### Poetic obras

- **After 1244–1273:** *Dīvān-e Šams-e Tabrīzī* (also *Dīvān-e Kabīr* or *Kolliyāt-e Šams*). A multilingual lyric corpus—principally ghazals, stanzaic poems, and quatrains—rather than a book conceived as one project; its customary title reflects Rumi’s poetic identification with Shams, while its present extent and arrangement are products of manuscript transmission and modern critical editing. Attribution remains permeable: the early anthology *Nozhat al-majāles* proves that at least eighty quatrains printed in Foruzanfar’s edition belong to earlier poets, and further quatrains have also been challenged. ([Franklin D. Lewis](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Rumi-Past-and-Present-East-and-West/Franklin-D-Lewis/9781851685493); [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Nozhat al-majāles”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/nozhat-al-majales/))

- **c. 1258/1261–1273:** *Maṯnawī-ye maʿnavī* (“Spiritual Couplets”). An approximately 26,000-verse didactic and narrative poem in six books, orally composed or dictated for and written down by Ḥosām-al-Din Čalabi; its movement among story, commentary, direct address, and performed recitation makes it simultaneously poem, teaching practice, and collaborative textual manifestation. Rumi died with its final story unfinished; the alleged seventh book transmitted later is not accepted as part of his six-book obra, and its authorship remains unresolved. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Rumi’s Teachings”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/rumi-jalal-al-din-teachings/); [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Classical Persian Literature”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iran-viii2-classical-persian-literature/); [Association for Iranian Studies](https://www.associationforiranianstudies.org/content/hidden-source-knowledge-uncovering-%E2%80%9Cbook-seven%E2%80%9D-r%C5%ABm%C4%AB%E2%80%99s-mathnawi-through-anqaraw%C4%AB%E2%80%99s-commentar))

### Recorded oral manifestations

- **Mid- to late 1250s, probably; compilation date uncertain:** *Fīhi mā fīh* (“In It Is What Is in It”; also called the *Discourses*). Seventy-one teaching sessions preserved from students’ notes, not a continuous prose book written by Rumi: the speech is his manifestation, but the selection, wording, chapter units, and written object are collaborative and possibly posthumous. Its earliest substantial witnesses date from 1317 and 1350, so calling Rumi its sole “author” conceals the work performed by auditors and compilers. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Rumi’s Teachings”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/rumi-jalal-al-din-teachings/))

- **Date uncertain, before 1273:** *Majāles-e sabʿa* (“Seven Sessions” or “Seven Sermons”). A cycle of seven formal sermons, mixing Arabic homiletic openings with predominantly Persian exposition and verse; these are records of performed preaching rather than a demonstrably author-written book, and neither their individual dates nor the date and agency of their compilation are securely established. The collection belongs to the critical prose corpus edited by Tawfiq Sobḥāni, but its mediated form resists treatment as a single, deliberately composed obra. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica, bibliography to “Rumi’s Teachings”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/rumi-jalal-al-din-teachings/); [Dar al-Masnavi, “The Sermons”](https://www.dar-al-masnavi.org/sermons.html))

### Correspondence and interventions

- **Dates uncertain, before 1273:** *Maktūbāt-e Mawlānā Jalāl-al-Din Rumi* (“The Letters of Rumi”). The standard Sobḥāni edition collects 150 separately occasioned letters—to disciples, relatives, officials, and other contemporaries—many functioning as social interventions or petitions rather than literary exposition; the collection is therefore an editorial aggregate, not one project by Rumi. The oldest manuscript used for the edition dates from 1352, and the letters generally lack secure individual dates. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica, bibliography to “Rumi’s Teachings”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/rumi-jalal-al-din-teachings/); [Dar al-Masnavi, “The Letters”](https://dar-al-masnavi.org/letters.html))
