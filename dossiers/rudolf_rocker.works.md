## Works by Rudolf Rocker

### Manifestos, declarations, and organizational protocols

- **1919:** *Keine Kriegswaffen mehr!* An anti-militarist pamphlet and manifesto addressed to workers after the First World War. [1][2]
- **1920:** *Prinzipienerklärung des Syndikalismus*. The programmatic declaration adopted by the Free Workers’ Union of Germany (FAUD); it is both Rocker’s text and an organization’s collectively authorized manifesto. [1][3]
- **1920:** *Zur Geschichte der parlamentarischen Tätigkeit in der modernen Arbeiterbewegung*. A political-historical pamphlet arguing against parliamentary socialism; Yiddish editions called it *Di parlamentarishe tetigkayt in der arbayter bevegung—ratensistem oder diktatur?* [1][4]
- **1920:** *Sozialdemokratie und Anarchismus*. A polemical pamphlet distinguishing anarchism from German social democracy. [1][2]
- **1921:** *Der Bankrott des russischen Staatskommunismus*. A polemical analysis and anti-Bolshevik manifesto occasioned by the suppression of anarchists and the Kronstadt revolt. Later titles and combinations with writings by Emma Goldman are editions, not additional obras. [1][2]
- **1922:** *Prinzipienerklärung der Internationalen Arbeiter-Assoziation*. The founding declaration of the reconstituted International Workers’ Association: a manifesto and organizational protocol drafted by Rocker but adopted as the association’s collective program. [1][3]
- **1923:** *Grundlagen des revolutionären Syndikalismus*. A concise theoretical and strategic statement of revolutionary syndicalism. [1][2]
- **1924:** *Der Syndikalismus und seine Aufgaben*. A programmatic lecture or essay subsequently circulated as a separately titled text. [5]
- **1925:** *Marx und Anarchismus*. A polemical historical essay, first documented in a Yiddish publication from Mexico; its later German and English forms should not be counted as new works. [1][4]
- **1926:** *Die moderne Arbeiterbewegung und ihre Ziele*. A published address on the aims of the labor movement; the surviving early edition is Yiddish. [4]
- **1927:** *Die Rationalisierung der Wirtschaft und die Arbeiterklasse*. A pamphlet analyzing industrial rationalization and its consequences for workers. [1][2]
- **1929:** *Die Bedeutung des Klassenkampfes*. A theoretical pamphlet, first published in Yiddish, reconsidering class struggle within libertarian socialism. [1][2]
- **1934:** *Socialismo constructivo*. A programmatic essay or pamphlet proposing constructive socialist organization rather than party-state rule. [1][2]
- **1936:** *The Truth about Spain*. An intervention in the Spanish Civil War: part report, part international propaganda manifesto supporting the Spanish anarchist movement. [6]
- **1937:** *The Tragedy of Spain*. A longer political analysis of the Spanish Revolution and Civil War, distinct from the shorter *The Truth about Spain*. [6]
- **1945:** *La influencia de las ideas absolutistas en el socialismo*. A critique of authoritarian inheritances within socialist thought; the later German *Absolutistische Gedankengänge im Sozialismus* is the same obra in another linguistic manifestation. Some bibliographies date the Spanish publication 1942 rather than 1945. [1][2][7]
- **1947:** *Zur Betrachtung der Lage in Deutschland: Die Möglichkeiten einer freiheitlichen Bewegung*. A postwar assessment and strategic memorandum on whether an anarchist and syndicalist movement could be rebuilt in Germany. The expanded title used in a 1978 German edition is not a separate work. [1][2]

### Historical, theoretical, and literary studies

- **1902:** *Mikhail Bakunin: A biografishe skitse*. A short biographical and political study of Bakunin, written and first printed in Yiddish; a 1905 Białystok printing is another edition. [4]
- **1912:** *Ziel und Bedeutung kooperativer Organisationen*. A Yiddish study of cooperative organization; the German title used by bibliographers is descriptive of the original work and should not be mistaken for proof of a German first edition. [1][2]
- **1924:** *Johann Most: Das Leben eines Rebellen*. A documentary political biography that also functions as a history of the German-language anarchist movement. [1][2]
- **1928:** *Die Sechs*. A literary-philosophical composition developed from Rocker’s lectures on six figures—Hamlet, Don Quixote, Heinrich von Ofterdingen, Medardus, Faust, and Don Juan. The 1938 English *The Six* is a translated manifestation, not another obra. [1][8]
- **c. 1925–1937:** *Nationalism and Culture*. Rocker’s large historical-philosophical project on the conflict between political power and culture, conceived as a shorter study in the mid-1920s and enlarged over roughly twelve years. Portions appeared in Spanish as *Las raíces de la autoridad*, *Teología política*, and *Economía sin capitalismo* before the complete English book; *Die Entscheidung des Abendlandes* and *Nationalismus und Kultur* are German manifestations of the same evolving obra, whose postwar text contains revisions. [1][2][9]
- **1938:** *Anarcho-Syndicalism: Theory and Practice*. A systematic exposition of anarcho-syndicalist history, aims, and methods, commissioned for an English-reading public. It is simultaneously historical survey, theory, and practical orientation rather than a neutral description. [6][10]
- **1938–1949:** *Anarchism and Anarcho-Syndicalism*. A compact history of anarchist ideas and syndicalist practice, originating as an encyclopedia contribution and subsequently issued independently; its publication forms vary in title and date but do not constitute separate works. [6][11]
- **1946–1950:** *Max Nettlau: El Heródoto de la anarquía*. A biographical and intellectual study of the anarchist historian Max Nettlau. Sources variously associate it with completion or an early Spanish appearance in 1946 and a Mexican book publication in 1950; the German *Max Nettlau: Leben und Werk des Historikers vergessener sozialer Bewegungen* is a later manifestation. [1][2][12]
- **1949:** *Pioneers of American Freedom: Origin of Liberal and Radical Thought in America*. A linked series of intellectual portraits constituting a history of American liberal, radical, and anarchist traditions. [1][6]
- **date not securely established:** *Heinrich Heine: Ein deutscher Dichter als Prophet*. A literary-political essay preserved in Rocker’s corpus and published separately only posthumously; the 2006 publication date is not a date of composition. [1][3]
- **various dates:** *Artistas y rebeldes*. A Spanish-language gathering of Rocker’s literary and biographical essays rather than a securely bounded single composition; it resists treatment as one original work because its identity depends upon editorial selection. [13]

### Autobiographical project

- **during or after 1914–1918; published 1927:** *Hinter Stacheldraht und Gitter*. A memoir of Rocker’s wartime internment in Britain, first published in a two-volume Yiddish translation as *Hinter drotn un gittern*. The unavailable or unpublished German source and the translated first publication make its textual status unusually complex. [4][6]
- **completed by the 1950s:** *Memoiren eines deutschen Anarchisten*. Rocker’s extensive German typescript autobiography is the overarching project; it remained unpublished as a whole during his lifetime, so the work is better understood as a manuscript project with several partial linguistic manifestations. [1][14]
- **1947:** *La juventud de un rebelde*. The first autobiographical volume, covering Rocker’s youth and political formation. It appeared first in Spanish; *Di yugnt fun a rebel*, *Aus den Memoiren eines deutschen Anarchisten*, and the 2023 *Die Jugend eines Rebellen* are translated, abridged, selected, or posthumously edited manifestations whose boundaries are not identical. [3][6]
- **1949:** *En la borrasca: Años de destierro*. The second autobiographical volume, covering exile and the London years. The Yiddish *In shturem: Goles-yorn* and the substantially abridged English *The London Years* are manifestations of this volume rather than independent memoirs. [4][6]
- **1952:** *Revolución y regresión*. The third autobiographical volume, carrying the memoir into revolution, reaction, exile, and Rocker’s later life. A Yiddish translation appeared posthumously in two volumes; the complete German text was not published during Rocker’s lifetime. [4][6]

### Translation obras

- **1930:** *Das letzte Gefecht*. Rocker’s German translation of Émile Pataud and Émile Pouget’s syndicalist novel *Comment nous ferons la révolution*. It is a made linguistic object by Rocker, but not an authored narrative by him. [15]
- **1931:** *Caleb Williams oder Die Dinge wie sie sind*. Rocker’s German translation of William Godwin’s novel *Things as They Are; or, The Adventures of Caleb Williams*. Its obra-status is shared: Godwin made the novel, while Rocker made this German verbal manifestation. [15]
- **dates not exhaustively recoverable:** *Translations for the Yiddish anarchist press*. Rocker translated or adapted numerous works from German, French, and English for Yiddish-speaking readers. Becker’s bibliography records this activity, but much of it appeared serially, anonymously, or without stable title-level attribution; the corpus therefore resists honest enumeration as a set of securely attributable independent works. [1][14]

### Editorial and publishing projects

- **1898–1914:** *Arbeter Fraynd*. A Yiddish anarchist newspaper shaped and, from 1899, edited by Rocker. It is a collective serial project rather than a singly authored work; Rocker’s editorials and unsigned contributions cannot all be separated with certainty from the paper’s institutional voice. [14][16]
- **1900–1903 and 1905–1908:** *Germinal*. A Yiddish literary and cultural periodical founded and edited by Rocker, with interruptions. It is an editorial Gesamtkunstwerk of translation, literature, criticism, typography, and political education rather than one authored text. [14][16]
- **1919–1933:** *Der Syndikalist*. Rocker served as a principal writer and responsible editor for the FAUD newspaper. The serial itself was collective, while Rocker’s large body of signed and unsigned journalism within it remains incompletely separable into discrete obras. [3][14]
- **1929–1933:** *Gilde freiheitlicher Bücherfreunde*. A publishing project co-founded by Rocker to produce libertarian literature, including works by Alexander Berkman, William Godwin, Erich Mühsam, and John Henry Mackay. It is an institutional obra rather than a text authored by Rocker. [15][17]
- **1949–1953:** *Die Freie Gesellschaft*. Rocker was a regular contributor and intellectual guide to this postwar German libertarian-socialist monthly, but not the sole maker of the periodical. His articles form a dispersed journalistic corpus later sampled in *Aufsatzsammlung*, not a single fixed work. [2][3]
- **1892–1958:** *Journalistic and occasional writings*. Becker’s work-bibliography records a much larger field of articles, reviews, introductions, commemorations, speeches, and open letters across German-, Yiddish-, Spanish-, English-, and French-language periodicals. Many are manifestations of lectures, later chapters, translations, or unsigned editorial labor; calling each securely independent would manufacture distinctions the surviving record does not support. [1][14]

### Collected and posthumous manifestations—not new obras

- **1974:** *Aus den Memoiren eines deutschen Anarchisten*. A posthumously edited and abridged German selection from the autobiographical project, not a new work made in 1974. [1][3]
- **1980:** *Aufsatzsammlung*, volumes 1–2. Posthumous collections of writings from 1919–1933 and 1949–1953; these volumes are editorial containers rather than two newly authored works. [1][3]
- **1999:** *Nationalismus und Kultur (Die Entscheidung des Abendlandes)*. Heiner Becker’s corrected and supplemented German edition of Rocker’s major work; bibliographically crucial, but an edition rather than a new Rocker obra. [1]
- **2023:** *Die Jugend eines Rebellen*. The first extensively edited German publication of the first memoir volume, prepared from the posthumous textual record; its date belongs to this manifestation, not to Rocker’s composition. [3][18]
- **2025:** *Im Sturm der Zeiten*. A posthumously edited German publication corresponding to the second autobiographical volume; it is a new manifestation of an older manuscript work. [3]
