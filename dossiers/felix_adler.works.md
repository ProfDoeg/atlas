## Works by Felix Adler

### Books and sustained philosophical works

- **1877:** *Creed and Deed: A Series of Discourses*. A book assembled from Adler’s Ethical Culture addresses; later impressions dated 1880 and after are editions, not new works. The American Ethical Union notes that some lectures were abridged or condensed for publication. [Singerman; AEU]

- **1892:** *The Moral Instruction of Children*. A pedagogical treatise, issued as volume 21 of the *International Education Series*, that converts Adler’s educational philosophy into guidance for moral instruction. Some later references give 1898 or 1909 because they describe later editions. [Encyclopedia.com; Online Books Page]

- **1903:** *Life and Destiny; or, Thoughts from the Ethical Lectures of Felix Adler*. A collection of brief passages selected from Adler’s lectures rather than a continuously composed book; its editorial extraction makes it partly a manifestation of his oral work. [AEU; Online Books Page]

- **1905:** *Marriage and Divorce*. A book made from lectures on marriage; the 1915 version adds a third lecture and is an enlarged manifestation, not an independent work. [Wikipedia works list]

- **1905:** *The Religion of Duty*. A thematic compilation of Adler’s addresses and reflections on ethical religion rather than a single continuous argument. [AEU; Wikipedia works list]

- **1905:** *The Essentials of Spirituality*. A short philosophical-religious book identifying spirituality with morality carried into the whole of life. [Online Books Page; Wikipedia works list]

- **1915:** *The World Crisis and Its Meaning*. A book interpreting the First World War as an ethical and institutional crisis. The 1916 printing found in catalogues is an edition of the same work. [Online Books Page; Encyclopedia.com]

- **1918:** *An Ethical Philosophy of Life, Presented in Its Main Outlines*. Adler’s principal systematic work: part intellectual autobiography, part philosophical system, and part application to family, vocation, state, and international society. [PhilPapers; Wikipedia works list]

- **1920; later edition 1930:** *Incompatibility in Marriage*. A book-length treatment of marital incompatibility. Sources disagree because the works list records a 1920 publication while a digitized-library record describes a 1930 Appleton edition; the latter should not automatically be counted as a separate work. [Wikipedia works list; Online Books Page]

- **1920 or 1922:** *The Punishment of Children*. A practical educational treatise in the American Home Series. Bibliographic records variously date Abingdon editions to 1920 and 1922, so the first-publication date is not securely resolved here. [Online Books Page; Wikipedia works list]

- **1923 lectures; 1924 publication:** *The Reconstruction of the Spiritual Ideal: Hibbert Lectures Delivered in Manchester College, Oxford, May 1923*. A lecture-cycle turned book, reconstructing spirituality without conventional dogma. Some lists date it 1923 or 1925, but those dates refer respectively to its delivery or differing publication records. [Encyclopedia.com; AEU]

- **Published 1946:** *Our Part in This World*. A posthumous book edited by Horace L. Friess from Adler’s materials. Its title and book-form are editorial creations after Adler’s death, so it resists treatment as a book made wholly by Adler even though its constituent thought and language are his. [Wikipedia works list; Online Books Page]

### Separately printed lectures, addresses, and manifestos

- **1874:** *The Judaism of the Future*. Adler’s Temple Emanu-El sermon, a programmatic address that functioned as a manifesto for an ethical religion beyond traditional theism; its status is primarily that of a delivered event, although versions of its text survive. [Wikipedia biography]

- **September 1876:** *The Evolution of Hebrew Religion*. A periodical article in *Popular Science Monthly*, applying historical-developmental analysis to religion. [Wikisource author record]

- **1878:** *Conscience*. A printed lecture delivered for the benefit of the free kindergarten; both an ethical argument and a fundraising intervention. [Singerman]

- **1879:** *Atheism*. A separately printed lecture associated with the Society for Ethical Culture. [Online Books Page]

- **1879; printed circa 1880:** *The Recent Persecution of the Jews*. An address delivered on 28 December 1879 and issued as a pamphlet; the imprint itself is uncertainly dated. [Singerman]

- **1880:** *Memorial Words, Spoken over the Remains of Joseph Seligman*. A funerary address, not a book, printed as an eleven-page memorial object. [Singerman]

- **1880; printed circa 1881:** *The Anti-Jewish Agitation in Germany; Larger Tolerance*. Two addresses delivered on 19 and 26 December 1880 and subsequently joined in one pamphlet; the printing date is uncertain. [Singerman; National Library of Israel]

- **1883:** *The Need of a New Moral Movement in Religion*. A discourse delivered in New York and Chicago and printed to benefit the free kindergarten. It operates as a manifesto for Ethical Culture as well as a lecture. [Singerman; Online Books Page]

- **1884:** *An Address in Memory of Julius Hallgarten*. A short memorial address; its separate printing survives, but the available bibliography does not supply a fuller fixed title. [Singerman]

- **1885:** *The Freedom of Public Worship*. A separately printed Ethical Culture address delivered on 8 March 1885. [Singerman]

- **1885:** *Reformed Judaism*. A separately printed lecture delivered before the Society for Ethical Culture on 22 November 1885. [Singerman]

- **1887:** *Henry Ward Beecher*. A memorial or evaluative lecture delivered on 3 April 1887 and printed as an eighteen-page pamphlet. [Singerman]

- **1892:** *Die ethischen Gesellschaften*. A lecture delivered in Berlin on 3 July 1892 and published in German; it is a manifestation of Adler’s international advocacy of ethical societies. [National Library of Israel]

- **1893:** *The Sabbath and the World’s Fair*. A printed lecture delivered on 29 January 1893, addressing a particular public controversy rather than proposing a general philosophical system. [Singerman]

- **1893:** *A Protest against the Russian Treaty*. A printed address delivered on 26 March 1893; its title identifies it as an intervention intended to act politically, and therefore as a manifesto or protest-text rather than merely a description. [Singerman]

- **1894:** *Prayer and Worship*. A work named in the scholarly biographical literature, apparently derived from Adler’s discourse on religious practice; readily accessible bibliographies do not establish its precise publication form securely. [Encyclopedia.com]

- **1896:** *International Ethical Congress*. A separately issued address or congress text; the terse surviving catalogue title does not clarify whether Adler authored the whole object or only the contribution attributed to him. [Online Books Page]

- **1896–1897:** *The Ethics of Marriage*. A six-lecture Lowell Institute series. The series is an oral obra in its own right and also supplied material subsequently manifested in *Marriage and Divorce*. [Wikipedia biography]

- **1897:** *How Far Does the Ethical Society Take the Place of a Church?* A separately printed address defining the institutional and religious function of Ethical Culture. [Online Books Page]

- **1898:** *The Ethical Culture Society as the Meeting Ground of Gentiles and Jews*. An address published in the fifth series of *Ethical Addresses*; it is an essay within a collective serial object, not a standalone book. [Singerman]

- **1901:** *The Character and Achievements of Paul the Apostle of the Gentiles*. A separately printed historical-religious lecture. [Online Books Page]

- **1902:** *The Distinctive Aims of the Ethical Culture School*. Four addresses to the school’s teachers, functioning together as an educational manifesto and working protocol. [Online Books Page]

- **1904:** *The Negro Problem in the United States, with Special Reference to Mr. Du Bois’s Book “The Souls of Black Folk”*. A separately printed intervention in contemporary racial debate. The historical title is Adler’s; retaining it for identification does not endorse its terminology. [Online Books Page]

- **1904:** *New Statement of the Aim of the Ethical Culture Societies*. A concise institutional manifesto redefining the societies’ common purpose. [Online Books Page]

- **1904:** *Immortality: A Modern Scientist’s Answer to the Questions Whence and Whither?* A separately issued lecture. Its subtitle and catalogue attribution should not be taken to prove that Adler himself claimed professional standing as a scientist. [Online Books Page]

- **1910:** *Dedication of the Meeting House of the Society for Ethical Culture of New York*. A ceremonial address made for a particular building and occasion; it is both text and performed institutional act. [Online Books Page]

- **1910:** *Conservation of the Human Assets of the Nation*. National Child Labor Committee pamphlet no. 125, a reform argument tied to Adler’s work as the committee’s chairman. [Mikulaninec bibliography]

- **1912:** *Child Labor and Education*. A separately printed reform address or pamphlet issued by the National Child Labor Committee. [Online Books Page]

- **1919:** *The Moral Prerequisites of a League of Nations*. A political-ethical address arguing that international organization required moral conditions, not merely machinery. [Online Books Page]

- **1921:** *The Revival of Anti-Semitism*. A pamphlet issued by the American Ethical Union in response to renewed antisemitic agitation. [Online Books Page]

- **1923:** *A World Morally Out of Joint*. An essay or address issued by the American Ethical Union and also encountered within its periodical culture; it should not be inflated into a book. [Online Books Page]

- **1926:** *Some Characteristics of the American Ethical Movement*. Adler’s chapter in the collective anniversary volume *The Fiftieth Anniversary of the Ethical Movement, 1876–1926*. The containing volume is not solely his work. [Wikipedia works list]

### Contributions within collective works

- **1876:** *Jews*; *Jewish History*; *Jewish Literature*. Three signed or attributed encyclopedia articles prepared for Johnson’s *Universal Cyclopaedia*. Their existence is reported by the contemporary *Jewish Encyclopedia*, but the exact boundaries and headings should be checked against the cyclopaedia itself before treating variant headings as additional works. [Jewish Encyclopedia]

- **1908:** *The Basis of the Anti-Child Labor Movement in the Idea of American Civilization*. Adler’s contribution to *Child Labor and Social Progress*, the proceedings of the National Child Labor Committee’s fourth annual meeting; the larger volume is collective. [Mikulaninec bibliography]

- **1910:** *Annual Address of the Chairman of the National Child Labor Committee*. A conference address printed in the *Annals of the American Academy of Political and Social Science*; its office-bearing, recurring character makes it an institutional act as much as an essay. [Mikulaninec bibliography]

- **1911:** *Child Labor: A Menace to Civilization*. A conference paper published with the proceedings titled *Uniform Child Labor Codes*. [Mikulaninec bibliography]

- **1911:** *Principles Which Should Govern the Making of Bequests for Philanthropic Purposes*. A section contributed to Daniel S. Remsen’s edited collective work *Post-mortem Use of Wealth, Including a Consideration of Ante-mortem Gifts*. [Wikipedia works list]

### Educational, religious, and social creations

- **1876–1933:** *Ethical Culture movement*. Adler’s central life-project: an organized ethical religion grounded in moral action rather than assent to a creed. It is not reducible to one society, text, or finished institution and therefore belongs more naturally to the category project—or even social Gesamtkunstwerk—than “work.” [Encyclopedia.com; New York Society for Ethical Culture]

- **1876–1877:** *New York Society for Ethical Culture*. A religious-ethical association initiated through Adler’s 15 May 1876 address and incorporated in February 1877 with collaborators including Joseph Seligman. Because it was collectively constituted and continuously remade by its members, Adler founded it but did not author it as a solitary obra. [Wikipedia biography; New York Society for Ethical Culture]

- **1877:** *District Nursing Department*. A Society project organizing trained nurses to visit the sick poor without religious proselytizing, later understood as a precursor of the Visiting Nurse Service. It is a social-service protocol and institution, collaboratively realized from Adler’s program. [New York Society for Ethical Culture]

- **1878–1880:** *Free kindergarten and Workingman’s School*. The free kindergarten begun for working families developed into the Workingman’s School in 1880. It embodied Adler’s educational philosophy in curriculum, meals, clothing, manual training, and institutional practice rather than in a single text. [New York Society for Ethical Culture; Encyclopedia.com]

- **1879:** *Social Service Board*. The social-service arm of the New York Society for Ethical Culture. Its collective institutional authorship means it should be credited to the Society under Adler’s leadership, not treated as his private creation. [New York Society for Ethical Culture]

- **1880–1933:** *Ethical Culture School*. The Workingman’s School’s later manifestation, reorganized under this name in 1895; Adler served as rector until his death. The school is a continuing educational obra whose many teachers and pupils were co-makers. [New York Society for Ethical Culture; Wikipedia biography]

- **1883:** *Society for the Study of Child Nature*. An early child-study organization attributed to Adler in scholarly biography. Later Society histories assign a related Mother’s Society to the Society’s women in 1888, so the names, dates, and institutional continuity should not be silently collapsed. [Encyclopedia.com; New York Society for Ethical Culture]

- **1885–1887:** *Tenement House Building Company and its model tenements*. Adler and collaborators created the company in 1885; by 1887 it had completed six affordable model buildings on Manhattan’s Lower East Side. The company and buildings are collective social architecture, not texts, and contemporary critics disputed whether model construction was preferable to regulation. [Wikipedia biography]

- **1888:** *The Ethical Record*. A periodical founded within the Ethical Culture movement and associated with Adler. It is a serial platform—an ongoing collective manifestation—not a text authored wholly by him. [Encyclopedia.com]

- **1890:** *International Journal of Ethics*. A scholarly journal founded by Adler, subsequently continued by others. Founding the communicative institution is his project; the journal’s individual numbers and articles are not therefore all his works. [Encyclopedia.com; EBSCO]

- **1891:** *Summer School of Applied Ethics*. An educational project founded by Adler to connect ethical inquiry with practice. Like his other schools, it resists classification as a self-contained work because its substance lay in recurring teaching and collaboration. [Encyclopedia.com]

- **1904–1921:** *National Child Labor Committee*. Adler was its founding chairman, not its sole creator; his work consisted in helping organize and guide a durable reform institution whose documents and campaigns had collective authorship. [Encyclopedia.com; Wikipedia biography]

- **1928:** *Fieldston campus*. Adler launched the new campus of the Ethical Culture School in Riverdale. The campus and school constitute an educational environment, but the buildings themselves should not be attributed to Adler as architect. [Encyclopedia.com; New York Society for Ethical Culture]

### Unpublished and posthumously constituted corpora

- **Childhood–1933:** *Diaries, notebooks, correspondence, poems, essays, lectures, articles, book manuscripts, course notes, and miscellaneous jottings*. Columbia’s Felix Adler Papers preserve roughly 25,000 items, including both published and unpublished writing across Adler’s lifetime. This is an archival corpus rather than a single titled work, and many items have no stable title or date. [Columbia finding aid]

- **1902–1933:** *Columbia lectures on political and social ethics*. A nearly complete run of course notes survives in Adler’s papers. The sequence is a pedagogical body of work, but individual headings may name occasions, topics, or archival folders rather than independently intended works. [Columbia finding aid]

- **1876–1933:** *Ethical Culture Sunday lectures and lecture series*. Adler delivered weekly and special addresses for decades; Columbia separately inventories single addresses and lecture series, including many unpublished manuscripts. No scholarly collected-works edition fixes every lecture as a discrete work, so presenting every archival heading as a completed, authorially titled text would manufacture certainty the archive does not supply. [Columbia finding aid]

- **Undated and variously dated:** *Unpublished book manuscripts and projected writings*. The archive explicitly contains unpublished books as well as fragments and notes. These are projects: incompletion is part of their status, and the finding aid does not justify inventing unified titles or dates for them. [Columbia finding aid]

### Exclusions and attribution cautions

- **1885:** *Tiryns: The Prehistoric Palace of the Kings of Tiryns*. This archaeological book is sometimes retrieved under “Felix Adler,” but its prefatory “Professor F. Adler” was the German architect and archaeologist Friedrich Adler, not Felix Adler (1851–1933). It is therefore not included among his works. [Cambridge University Press]

- **1926:** *Aspects of Ethical Religion*. This is a festschrift made by Adler’s colleagues in his honor, not a work by Adler. Its subject and occasion do not establish authorship. [Wikipedia further reading]

- **1926:** *The Fiftieth Anniversary of the Ethical Movement, 1876–1926*. The volume is collective; only Adler’s identified contribution belongs under his authorship. Catalogue records that place the whole book under his name conflate contribution with creation. [Wikipedia works list; Online Books Page]
