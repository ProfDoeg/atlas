## Works by Simone Lucie Ernestine Marie Bertrand de Beauvoir

### Early notebooks and unfinished projects

- **1926–1930:** *Cahiers de jeunesse*. A sequence of student journals—not originally conceived as a book—preserved in manuscript and published posthumously; the surviving notebooks include the separately catalogued *4e cahier*, *Carnet 6*, and *Carnet 7*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **c. 1928:** *Notes pour un roman*. Fragmentary handwritten notes for an unrealized novel: a project rather than a completed literary work, tentatively dated by its scholarly editors. ([University of Illinois Press](https://www.press.uillinois.edu/wordpress/the-beauvoir-series-qa/))
- **1928–1929:** *Le Concept chez Leibniz*. Beauvoir’s higher-education thesis, an academic philosophical text rather than a commercial publication; its circulation and surviving textual status are limited.
- **1939–1941:** *Journal de guerre*. A wartime diary manuscript, subsequently edited and published posthumously; it is a dated record and writing workshop rather than a retrospective memoir. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Novels and novellas

- **1935–1937; published 1979:** *Quand prime le spirituel*. A linked collection of five early novellas—*Marcelle*, *Chantal*, *Lisa*, *Anne*, and *Marguerite*—rejected by publishers before the war and withheld until late in Beauvoir’s life. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1943:** *L’Invitée*. A novel, also functioning as an existential investigation through fiction; later translations and editions are manifestations of the same obra. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1945:** *Le Sang des autres*. A novel about freedom, responsibility, resistance, and political violence. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1946:** *Tous les hommes sont mortels*. A philosophical novel organized around the impossible condition of immortality. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1954:** *Les Mandarins*. A large postwar novel and roman à clef, awarded the Prix Goncourt; its relation to lived persons does not reduce it to memoir or documentary testimony. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1954; published 2020:** *Les Inséparables*. A short autobiographical novel about Beauvoir’s childhood friendship with Élisabeth “Zaza” Mabille; Beauvoir declined to publish the completed manuscript, so its status lies between finished obra and deliberately suspended project. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1965–1967; published posthumously:** *Malentendu à Moscou*. A novella preserved as a ninety-six-page typescript, apparently intended for but removed from *La Femme rompue*; sources variously date it 1965 or 1966–1967, and it first appeared posthumously before its later stand-alone edition. ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/illinois-scholarship-online/book/30537/chapter-abstract/257749734), [BnF](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb471083440))
- **1966:** *Les Belles Images*. A novel whose satirical treatment of consumer culture and bourgeois language makes social discourse part of its formal material. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1967:** *La Femme rompue*. A triptych of novellas—*L’Âge de discrétion*, *Monologue*, and *La Femme rompue*—using distinct first-person forms; it is one designed collection as well as three separable narratives. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Theatre

- **1945:** *Les Bouches inutiles*. Beauvoir’s only completed and produced play, first staged in Paris and published the same year; it dramatizes political necessity, exclusion, and collective responsibility. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Philosophical, political, and social books

- **1944:** *Pyrrhus et Cinéas*. Beauvoir’s first book-length philosophical essay, examining action, freedom, ends, and relations with other people. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1947:** *Pour une morale de l’ambiguïté*. A systematic philosophical essay on existential freedom and ethical responsibility, though Beauvoir later criticized its abstraction from concrete social conditions. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1948:** *L’Existentialisme et la sagesse des nations*. A book manifestation collecting four previously published essays rather than a wholly new continuous work. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1949:** *Le Deuxième Sexe*. A two-volume philosophical, historical, literary, and social inquiry—*Les Faits et les mythes* and *L’Expérience vécue*—whose mixed method resists classification as a single-discipline treatise. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1951–1952:** *Faut-il brûler Sade ?* A long philosophical-critical essay first serialized in two parts in *Les Temps modernes* and subsequently issued with other writing; its stand-alone books are later manifestations of that essay. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1955:** *Privilèges*. A collection joining *Faut-il brûler Sade ?*, *La Pensée de droite, aujourd’hui*, and *Merleau-Ponty et le pseudo-sartrisme*; the volume is an authored arrangement of independently circulating essays.
- **1957:** *La Longue Marche: essai sur la Chine*. A political travel study produced after Beauvoir’s 1955 visit to the People’s Republic of China; it combines reportage, argument, and an historically situated—and contested—assessment of Maoist society. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1962:** *Djamila Boupacha*. A documentary-political book made with attorney Gisèle Halimi around Boupacha’s torture and prosecution; Halimi supplied the legal narrative and Beauvoir the preface and public intervention, so it should not be silently treated as Beauvoir’s sole-authored book. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1970:** *La Vieillesse*. A large interdisciplinary study of ageing as lived experience and social oppression, combining history, ethnography, philosophy, and political critique. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Travel writing, memoir, and life writing

- **1948:** *L’Amérique au jour le jour*. A chronological travel diary shaped into a book from Beauvoir’s 1947 journey through the United States; it combines reportage, political interpretation, and personal record. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1958:** *Mémoires d’une jeune fille rangée*. The first major volume of Beauvoir’s memoir project, covering childhood and intellectual formation. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1960:** *La Force de l’âge*. The second memoir volume, narrating her life from 1929 through the Second World War. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1963:** *La Force des choses*. The third memoir volume, extending the account through postwar literary and political life; some translations divided it into two books, but those are editorial manifestations, not two original works. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1964:** *Une mort très douce*. A memoir of the illness and death of Beauvoir’s mother, also an inquiry into embodiment, care, medicine, and dying. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1972:** *Tout compte fait*. A fourth retrospective memoir volume; its thematic construction complicates the idea that it is merely the next chronological instalment. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1981:** *La Cérémonie des adieux*. A memoir of Jean-Paul Sartre’s final decade, published together with Beauvoir’s separately structured 1974 interviews with him. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1974; published 1981:** *Entretiens avec Jean-Paul Sartre, août–septembre 1974*. A sequence of recorded interviews made jointly by speaker and questioner; Beauvoir created its questions and documentary structure, while Sartre supplied the principal testimony. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Essays, reviews, lectures, and public interventions

- **1945:** *La Phénoménologie de la perception de Maurice Merleau-Ponty*. A philosophical review essay published in *Les Temps modernes*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1945:** *Idéalisme moral et réalisme politique*. A political-philosophical essay later incorporated into *L’Existentialisme et la sagesse des nations*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1945:** *L’Existentialisme et la sagesse des nations*. An essay defending existentialism against common moral objections; it later gave its title to a collection. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1945–1946:** *Jean-Paul Sartre: Strictly Personal* / *Jean-Paul Sartre*. A biographical-critical magazine article whose surviving French manuscript is untitled and undated; the English publication title is therefore a manifestation title, not securely Beauvoir’s original one. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1945:** *Quatre jours à Madrid*. A newspaper report based on Beauvoir’s Spanish journey, published in *Combat*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1946:** *Œil pour œil*. An essay on punishment, vengeance, and responsibility, first published in *Les Temps modernes*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1946:** *Littérature et métaphysique*. An essay arguing for fiction as a mode of metaphysical disclosure rather than an ornamental vehicle for pre-existing doctrine. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1947:** *An Existentialist Looks at Americans*. An English-language magazine essay interpreting American society; its published form passed through translation and editorial mediation. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1947:** *Qu’est-ce que l’existentialisme ?* A short explanatory essay published in *France-Amérique*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1947:** *Femininity, the Trap*. A magazine essay first published in English translation in *Vogue*; the translator is unidentified, so the exact relation of the printed wording to Beauvoir’s source text remains uncertain. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1949:** Review of Claude Lévi-Strauss’s *Les Structures élémentaires de la parenté*. An untitled or generically titled critical review in *Les Temps modernes*, later translated under an editorial English title. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1950:** *It’s About Time Women Put a New Face on Love*. A magazine essay published in English in *Flair* through an unidentified translator; its title and exact verbal form are therefore partly editorial. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1955:** *Merleau-Ponty et le pseudo-sartrisme*. A sustained polemical-philosophical essay responding to Maurice Merleau-Ponty. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1955:** *La Pensée de droite, aujourd’hui*. A two-part analysis of contemporary conservative ideology, later collected in *Privilèges*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1959:** *Brigitte Bardot and the Lolita Syndrome*. An essay first published in English translation; a later French text was reconstructed by back-translation, so no securely identical French original survives in publication. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1961:** *La Condition féminine*. An essay on women’s social situation, later gathered among Beauvoir’s feminist writings. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1965:** *Que peut la littérature ?* Beauvoir’s contribution to a public debate on literature’s efficacy; it is both an oral intervention and a subsequently printed text. ([University of Illinois Press](https://files.press.uillinois.edu/books/catalogs/pdf/UIP.F11Catalog.pdf))
- **1965:** *What Love Is—and Isn’t*. A magazine essay published through an unidentified English translator, making the English text a mediated manifestation. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1966:** *Situation de la femme d’aujourd’hui*. A lecture delivered in Tokyo and printed later; the oral event and edited text are related but non-identical manifestations. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1966:** *La Femme et la création*. A Japanese lecture on women’s material situation and creative activity, subsequently reconstructed in print. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1966; published 1979:** *Mon expérience d’écrivain*. A lecture on Beauvoir’s experience of authorship; its public delivery and later transcription constitute distinct manifestations. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1969:** *Amour et politique*. A short political-feminist article in *Le Nouvel Observateur*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1971:** *En France aujourd’hui on peut tuer impunément*. A newspaper intervention denouncing impunity for political violence. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1972:** *La Femme révoltée*. An interview conducted by Alice Schwarzer; it is a dialogic creation and edited journalistic object, not straightforward sole-authored prose. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1972:** *Réponse à quelques femmes et à un homme*. A public reply in *Le Nouvel Observateur*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1972:** *L’Avortement des pauvres*. A short political article connecting abortion prohibition with economic inequality. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1972:** *Déposition de Simone de Beauvoir au procès de Bobigny*. Courtroom testimony: an enacted legal intervention subsequently fixed as a printed text, and thus closer to testimony than to an independently composed essay. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1973:** *La Syrie et les prisonniers*. A political newspaper article concerning prisoners in Syria. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1973:** *Le Sexisme quotidien*. A brief programmatic text introducing the “everyday sexism” column in *Les Temps modernes*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1975:** *Solidaire d’Israël: un soutien critique*. A political essay expressing critical solidarity rather than unconditional endorsement. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1976:** *Quand toutes les femmes du monde…*. A short feminist intervention in *Le Nouvel Observateur*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1976:** *Mon point de vue: une affaire scandaleuse*. A polemical column in *Marie Claire*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1979:** *De l’urgence d’une loi antisexiste*. A newspaper argument for legislation against sexist expression and representation. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1979:** *Discours d’introduction*. An opening address to the International Committee for Women’s Rights: an event-text surviving through transcription and translation. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1983:** *La Femme, la pub et la haine*. A newspaper intervention on misogyny in advertising. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Prefaces and introductions

- **1959:** *Préface à Le “planning” familial*. A preface to Marie-Andrée Lagroua Weill-Hallé’s advocacy of family planning. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1960:** *Préface à La Grand’Peur d’aimer*. A preface to Lagroua Weill-Hallé’s book on contraception and reproductive life. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1962:** *Préface à Djamila Boupacha*. Beauvoir’s framing political text for the documentary volume assembled with Gisèle Halimi. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1964:** *Préface à La Bâtarde*. A substantial introduction that helped present Violette Leduc’s autobiographical book to the public. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1964:** *Préface à The Sexually Responsive Woman*. A short prefatory text for Phyllis and Eberhard Kronhausen’s study, apparently first published in English translation. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1966:** *Préface à Treblinka*. A preface to Jean-François Steiner’s documentary-literary account of the extermination camp revolt. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1973:** *Préface à Avortement: une loi en procès—L’affaire de Bobigny*. A political-legal framing text for the published transcript of the Bobigny proceedings. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1974:** *Préface à Divorce en France*. A feminist preface to Claire Cayron’s study. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1974:** *Présentation de Les femmes s’entêtent*. An introduction to the feminist special issue of *Les Temps modernes*, functioning as editorial framing and collective political speech. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1976:** *Préface à Regards féminins*. A preface to Anne Ophir’s work on women’s perspectives. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1977:** *Préface à Histoires du MLF*. A historical-political preface to Annie de Pisan and Anne Tristan’s account of the French women’s liberation movement. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1977:** *Foreword to History: A Novel*. A preface to an English edition of Elsa Morante’s *La Storia*; the first printing shortened it, while a fuller version appeared later, so the work has competing textual manifestations. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1981:** *Avant-propos à Chroniques d’une imposture*. A polemical foreword concerning disputes over the name and legacy of the Mouvement de libération des femmes. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1985:** *Préface à Shoah*. A preface to Claude Lanzmann’s published film text; it responds to a cinematic Gesamtkunstwerk rather than treating the film as merely a written document. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1986:** *Préface à Mihloud*. Beauvoir’s final listed preface, written for the French publication of an anonymous testimony. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Manifestos, collective texts, and editorial projects

- **1945–1986:** *Les Temps modernes*. An ongoing editorial and political project co-founded with Jean-Paul Sartre, Maurice Merleau-Ponty, and others; Beauvoir’s editorial labor helped make the journal, but the journal as a whole resists attribution as her individual obra. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/))
- **1971:** *Manifeste des 343*. A manifesto in the strict sense: a declaration designed to perform collective civil disobedience by publicly avowing illegal abortions. Beauvoir is widely credited with drafting it and certainly signed it, but it was organized and enacted collectively, so sole authorship should not erase the 342 other signatories or the women’s movement that produced the action. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/), [INA](https://www.ina.fr/ina-eclaire-actu/5-avril-1971-le-manifeste-des-343))
- **1974:** *Manifeste de la Ligue du droit des femmes*. A collective programmatic text issued by the organization Beauvoir presided over; bibliographies associate it with her, but its institutional voice resists uncomplicated sole attribution. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Film and audiovisual work

- **1974:** *Promenade au pays de la vieillesse*. A television documentary made collaboratively by Marianne Ahrne, Beauvoir, Pépo Angel, and Bertrand Hurault, with Ahrne directing; Beauvoir is a participant and co-maker, not sole author, and the later transcript is only one manifestation of the audiovisual work. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Correspondence

- **1930–1963; published 1990:** *Lettres à Sartre*. A large corpus of letters to Jean-Paul Sartre, edited posthumously by Sylvie Le Bon de Beauvoir; the published selection is an editorial construction from a broader correspondence, not a book Beauvoir herself designed. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **1940–1964; published 2004:** *Correspondance croisée: Simone de Beauvoir–Jacques-Laurent Bost*. A two-voiced correspondence whose individual letters are Beauvoir’s and Bost’s but whose paired sequence is a posthumous editorial creation.
- **1947–1964; published 1997–1998:** *Lettres à Nelson Algren* / *A Transatlantic Love Affair*. Beauvoir’s English-language letters to Nelson Algren, selected and edited posthumously; the French and English publications do not constitute new letters, and editorial selection prevents the volume from being identical with the complete historical correspondence. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))

### Posthumous collected manifestations

- **2004:** *Philosophical Writings*. A scholarly collected-works volume assembling published, archival, and newly translated philosophical texts; it is an indispensable manifestation of Beauvoir’s corpus but not a new work composed by her in 2004. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **2006 and 2019:** *Diary of a Philosophy Student*, volumes 1–2. An English scholarly edition of the early notebooks, divided into publication volumes by editors rather than by Beauvoir. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **2011:** *“The Useless Mouths” and Other Literary Writings*. A scholarly collection containing the play, short literary criticism, *Malentendu à Moscou*, and the fragmentary *Notes pour un roman*; it manifests several works and projects rather than constituting a newly authored Beauvoir obra. ([University of Illinois Press](https://files.press.uillinois.edu/books/catalogs/pdf/UIP.F11Catalog.pdf))
- **2012:** *Political Writings*. A posthumous scholarly collection of Beauvoir’s political essays, speeches, prefaces, and interventions; its organization and English textual form belong partly to its editors and translators. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/beauvoir/#Bib))
- **2015:** *Feminist Writings*. A posthumous scholarly collection recovering essays, lectures, interviews, and prefaces across Beauvoir’s career; it is a corpus-making editorial manifestation, not a single work she planned. ([University of Illinois Press](https://www.press.uillinois.edu/books/?id=c039003))
