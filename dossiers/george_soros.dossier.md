# George Soros — Research Dossier

**Subject:** George Soros (born Schwartz György; later Soros György)  
**Coverage:** 12 August 1930–20 August 2026  
**Status at cutoff:** Living  
**Method:** Chronological, documentary reconstruction. Amounts are nominal unless otherwise stated. “Soros” means George Soros personally; “SFM” means Soros Fund Management; “OSF” means the Open Society Foundations and predecessor/coordinated foundations. Those entities overlap but are not interchangeable. A grant to an organization is not, without further evidence, proof that Soros directed every act of the grantee. Allegations are identified as such and separated from charges, judgments, and convictions.

The surname has several layers. “Schwartz” was recognizably German-Jewish. Tivadar changed it to “Soros” as antisemitism intensified. In Hungarian, *soros* can mean “next in line” or “successor”; in Esperanto, *soros* can be read as the future tense of “to soar”—“will soar.” The palindrome suited Tivadar’s fondness for language. The interpretation that it was simply a later business rebranding is unsupported: the change occurred when George was six.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and ancestral setting

- **Father: Tivadar Soros**, born **Tivadar Schwartz** on 7 April 1893 at Nyírbakta, Hungary; also known in Esperanto as **Teodoro Ŝvarc** and sometimes associated with the pen name **Teo Melas**. He came from an Orthodox Jewish family but became an assimilated, secular Budapest lawyer and property/building manager. Drafted into the Austro-Hungarian army in the First World War, he was captured by Russia, sent to a Siberian prison camp, escaped, and made his way home through revolutionary Russia. He edited the Esperanto literary review *Literatura Mondo* and wrote the Esperanto memoir *Maskerado ĉirkaŭ la morto* (1965), later translated as *Masquerade: Dancing around Death in Nazi-Occupied Hungary*. George repeatedly identified Tivadar’s improvisation, appetite for risk, and response to the German occupation as his chief practical education.
- **Mother: Erzsébet “Elizabeth” Szűcs Soros.** She came from a comparatively prosperous Jewish family connected to a silk business. The household was culturally Jewish but not observant. In George’s account she was a member of the Hemlock Society late in life and considered but declined assisted suicide while terminally ill. Her exact death date is not securely established in the sources used here.
- **Brother: Paul Soros** (Hungarian: Pál Soros), born in 1926, four years older than George. He left communist Hungary, became an engineer and port-design entrepreneur in the United States, and with his wife Daisy created a fellowship for new Americans. He died in 2013.

There is a notable date conflict concerning Tivadar’s death. The German Resistance Memorial Center and biographical accounts place it in **February 1968 in New York**. In Soros’s 1994 “Reflections on Death in America,” he wrote that his father “died at home in 1963.” No consulted source reconciles the discrepancy. The 1968 date is better corroborated; the 1963 statement is retained as an autobiographical error or unresolved publishing anomaly, not silently corrected.

1. **Annaliese Witschak** — a German-born non-Jewish war orphan. Married Soros in 1960; divorced in 1983. Their children are:
   - **Robert Daniel Soros** (born 1963), investor and former SFM deputy chairman/president;
   - **Andrea Soros Colombel** (born 1965), philanthropist;
   - **Jonathan Tivadar Soros** (born 1970), lawyer, investor, political-reform donor, and former SFM executive.
2. **Susan Weber Soros** — art historian and founder of the Bard Graduate Center. Married in 1983; divorced in 2005. Their children are:

   - **Gregory James Soros** (born 1988), artist.
3. **Tamiko Bolton** — education and health-care consultant. The couple met in 2008, announced their engagement in 2012, and married on **21 September 2013** at Soros’s Bedford, New York, estate. Federal judge Kimba Wood conducted a small non-denominational ceremony; the larger reception drew figures from finance, public life, and philanthropy. It was Soros’s third marriage and Bolton’s second.

Soros also had a relationship with Brazilian actor **Adriana Ferreyr**. Its contentious end produced civil litigation discussed below. No court finding established her assault allegation.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1930–1947

### Budapest before occupation

Soros grew up in an upper-middle-class, assimilated Jewish household in Budapest. His father cultivated Esperanto and treated conventional status and rules skeptically; George later portrayed him as affectionate but emotionally reserved, and as most capable when normal institutions collapsed. The family’s name change in 1936 was a precautionary act during the growth of Hungarian antisemitism.

Hungary entered an alliance with the Axis, adopted increasingly severe anti-Jewish laws, and sent many Jewish men into forced-labor units before German troops occupied the country. Soros’s childhood therefore moved from relative urban security into state exclusion and mortal danger.

### German occupation and false identity, 1944–1945

Germany occupied Hungary on **19 March 1944**. Soros was thirteen. The facts reconstructable from Tivadar’s memoir, George’s accounts, and later interviews are:

- Jewish schoolchildren were organized through the Budapest Jewish Council. George briefly carried notices or summonses. Tivadar examined the papers, concluded that compliance could expose recipients to deportation, told George to warn them not to report, and removed him from the work.
- Tivadar obtained forged identity papers and dispersed the immediate family among separate protectors. George lived as the Christian **Sándor Kiss**, represented as the godson of a Hungarian Ministry of Agriculture official, usually identified as **Baumbach**. The official’s Jewish wife was herself in hiding.
- The official took George on one trip to inventory an estate belonging to **Mór Kornfeld**, a Jewish industrialist who had escaped or been permitted to leave Hungary. Property was being inventoried for state control. George was a fourteen-year-old dependent and spectator; no document shows him identifying Jews, deciding confiscations, taking property for himself, or serving a Nazi unit.
- George and his mother also received protection from **Elza Brandeisz**, a Lutheran dance teacher later honored by Yad Vashem as Righteous Among the Nations. The family survived the siege of Budapest.
- Tivadar used money, forged documents, shifting stories, and Christian protectors to save his wife, sons, and other people. The exact number helped varies across retellings and cannot be fixed from the consulted record.

This period later became the nucleus both of Soros’s self-understanding and of the false “Nazi collaborator” story. In interviews he said 1944 was in one sense the happiest or most exciting year of his life because he saw his father master an otherwise hopeless situation and learned that danger could be confronted. The statement referred to agency and paternal example, not pleasure in the persecution of Jews.

### Communist takeover and departure

After the Soviet capture of Budapest, Soros resumed school but did not wish to remain under the emerging communist system. In 1947, aged seventeen, he obtained permission to attend an Esperanto youth congress in Switzerland, traveled west, and continued to London. This was emigration and self-exile, not deportation. Paul left Hungary separately. Their parents remained until after the 1956 Hungarian uprising and later joined their sons in the West.

## Education and Formation, 1947–1956

In London Soros supported himself through low-paid work, including railway portering, waiting in a nightclub, and sales work. He enrolled at the **London School of Economics** and graduated in 1951; he completed further graduate work in philosophy in 1954. Accounts differ in the shorthand used for his qualifications, but agree on the institution, field, and central intellectual influence.

That influence was **Karl Popper**, the Austrian-British philosopher and author of *The Open Society and Its Enemies*. Popper opposed historicism—the belief that history follows discoverable laws toward an inevitable political destination—and argued for fallibilism, criticism, and piecemeal reform. Soros attended Popper’s lectures and later sought his assessment of philosophical work. Popper was formative but not a trading mentor or patron who placed Soros in finance.

Soros later described an extended manuscript called **“The Burden of Consciousness,”** worked on mainly in 1963–66, as a failed philosophical project. The manuscript was not published as a conventional book. Its concern with the gap between thought and reality developed into his theory of reflexivity.

He initially hoped for an academic or writing career. Unable to establish one, he wrote to merchant banks and joined finance. This failure was consequential: he continued to consider himself a philosopher operating through markets and philanthropy, even as professional philosophers and economists often treated his theoretical claims skeptically.

## Early Career, 1954–1973

### London and move to New York

- **1954–1956 — Singer & Friedlander, London.** Soros worked in entry-level merchant-banking and arbitrage functions.
- **1956 — Migration to the United States.** He moved to New York, expecting to make enough money to return to philosophy.
- **1956–1959 — F. M. Mayer.** He traded and analyzed European securities and developed expertise in cross-border arbitrage, including securities that traded in different forms in European and American markets.
- **1959–1963 — Wertheim & Co.** He was an analyst of European securities. During this period he became frustrated by shrinking arbitrage opportunities and concentrated on philosophy.
- **1963–1973 — Arnhold & S. Bleichroeder.** He became vice president and built an investment following. **Henry H. Arnhold** was an important institutional sponsor. Soros managed the **First Eagle Fund** from 1967 and created the offshore **Double Eagle Fund** in 1969. **Jim Rogers**, whom Soros recruited, became his principal analytical partner.

The Double Eagle began with approximately **$4 million**, including about **$250,000** of Soros’s own money, and used an offshore structure in Curaçao/the Netherlands Antilles. In 1970 Soros created **Soros Fund Management** as the management company. In 1973, with roughly $12 million under management, he left Arnhold & S. Bleichroeder and operated independently. The fund was later renamed the **Quantum Fund**.

## Chronological Life History

### 1973–1980: building the Soros–Rogers partnership

Soros and Rogers ran a concentrated, research-intensive global portfolio. They combined company analysis with macroeconomic positions in currencies, commodities, bonds, and equities, using leverage and selling short. The fund’s ability to move across national markets distinguished it from conventional long-only management. Its investors were largely wealthy non-U.S. individuals and institutions because of the offshore structure.

Returns were exceptional, though historical figures vary according to start date, fees, and the series used. Contemporary profiles report that the fund multiplied many times over in the 1970s. Soros accumulated enough personal capital to contemplate leaving investment management. Rogers departed in 1980 after disputes over direction and Soros’s desire to reduce his own day-to-day role. Their later relationship was distant; Rogers remained publicly critical of some of Soros’s politics and publicity.

### 1979–1986: first philanthropy and first regulatory matters

In **1979** Soros began what became a separate career in political philanthropy. He funded scholarships for Black students at the University of Cape Town under apartheid—eventually helping approximately eighty students—and supported Eastern European dissidents studying abroad.

The same year, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission brought a civil market-manipulation case over Computer Sciences Corporation. That case and a 1986 commodities-limit penalty are detailed under controversies.

In **1981**, after the fund had grown to roughly $400 million, Quantum lost about 22 percent and faced redemptions that reduced assets to around $200 million. Soros suffered an intense personal and professional strain, separated from his first wife, and began reorganizing the enterprise. The loss was a major failure but not the end of the fund.

Soros and the Hungarian Academy of Sciences created a foundation in **1984**. It distributed scholarships, supported independent cultural groups, and supplied photocopiers to libraries, universities, and hospitals. Photocopying was politically consequential in a state that restricted duplication and circulation of uncensored material. In **1986**, Soros established the China Fund for Reform and Opening in cooperation with a Chinese reform institute; it ended amid security-service suspicion and the 1989 crackdown.

### 1987–1991: market loss, Eastern Europe, and institutional expansion

Before the October 1987 stock-market crash Soros’s published investment diary expected a U.S. advance and greater danger in Japan. The opposite short-run pattern hurt Quantum, which reportedly lost roughly $300 million during the crash, although the fund finished the year positive. Soros later treated the episode as evidence that his own hypotheses were fallible, not as vindication.

**Stanley Druckenmiller** joined in 1988 and became chief portfolio strategist. **Scott Bessent** later established and ran the London office. The organization increasingly combined Soros’s macro theses with autonomous managers.

Soros created or supported:

- the **Stefan Batory Foundation** in Poland (1988);
- new foundations in Soviet and East European states from 1989;
- the **Central European University** (CEU), opening in 1991 in Prague and Budapest;
- the **International Renaissance Foundation** in Ukraine (1990);
- the **International Science Foundation** (1992), to which he committed roughly $200 million to provide emergency grants, equipment, and connectivity to scientists in the former Soviet Union.

He called this activity “political philanthropy”: funding independent capacity, information, education, and legal institutions rather than supplying ordinary relief alone. Local boards usually made grants, but Soros set overall purposes, selected senior leaders, and could intervene in major strategic choices.

### 1992: Black Wednesday

Britain belonged to the European Exchange Rate Mechanism (ERM), obliging the pound to remain within a band against other currencies, especially the German mark. German interest rates after reunification, British recession, inflation differences, and doubts about the pound’s parity made the commitment vulnerable.

Quantum accumulated a short position in sterling reported at about **$10 billion**, financed in part by borrowing pounds and buying marks and other assets. On **16 September 1992**, the British government spent reserves and announced sharp interest-rate increases, then suspended sterling from the ERM. Quantum’s profit is usually reported as approximately **$1 billion** for the immediate trade and between **$1.5 billion and $2 billion** over the broader position and related trades.

The event produced the label “The Man Who Broke the Bank of England.” The core trade and profit are documented. The literal claim is exaggerated: numerous banks and funds sold sterling, the policy inconsistency predated Soros’s position, and no private trader set the parity. The episode made him a public figure and symbol of mobile capital. The eventual official estimate of the British Treasury’s exchange-account loss was about £3.3 billion; that sum was not paid to Soros alone.

### 1993–1998: global foundation network, large gains, and Russia/Asia losses

The **Open Society Institute** was created in New York in 1993 to coordinate the widening foundation network. Aryeh Neier, a former executive director of the ACLU and Human Rights Watch, became a principal builder and served as president from 1993 to 2012.

During the Balkan wars Soros-funded bodies supported Sarajevo’s utilities, media, and humanitarian services. In the United States, Soros began sustained work on drug-policy reform, palliative care, immigration, civil liberties, and criminal justice. His **Project on Death in America** (1994–2003) spent about $45 million to expand palliative-care training and public discussion. In 1994 he also established the Lindesmith Center, later part of the Drug Policy Alliance.

Market setbacks accompanied the expansion:

- In February **1994**, a wrong-way yen position reportedly cost the fund about $600 million in a day.
- In **1997**, Quantum suffered a one-day loss reported near $2 billion in the global market break.
- In Russia, Soros joined a consortium that paid approximately **$1.875 billion** for 25 percent plus one share of the telecommunications holding **Svyazinvest**; his portion was about **$980 million**. A 1997 essay on his own website contains an obvious “$980 billion” typographical error, contradicted by the total transaction and contemporaneous reporting. He acknowledged tension between his investment and philanthropic roles. The 1998 Russian default and devaluation contributed to losses reported as high as $2 billion. He sold the Svyazinvest stake in 2004 for approximately $625 million and called it the worst investment of his career.

During the **1997 Asian financial crisis**, Malaysian prime minister Mahathir Mohamad accused Soros and other speculators of attacking the ringgit and destabilizing the region. Quantum did hold Asian currency positions, and hedge funds were active participants. Soros denied causing the crisis. An NBER study by Stephen Brown, William Goetzmann, and James Park found no empirical evidence that hedge funds as a group precipitated the crisis and found their positions too small or inconsistent to support the simple causation story. The accusation remained politically influential despite the unresolved opacity of individual positions.

### 1999–2001: technology-bubble reversal and retreat from outside capital

Quantum initially bet against Internet stocks and lost, then went long near the top of the technology boom. In 2000, losses of several billion dollars and departures by Druckenmiller and other senior managers forced a restructuring. Assets reportedly fell from roughly $10 billion to about $4 billion. Soros converted Quantum into the more conservative **Quantum Endowment Fund**. This period weakened the image of unique market infallibility, though long-run returns remained extraordinary.

The 11 September 2001 attacks and the George W. Bush administration’s response moved Soros into more direct U.S. partisan activity. He opposed the Iraq War and argued that the “war on terror” and doctrines of preventive war damaged open society.

### 2002–2010: conviction in France, U.S. political spending, and the financial crisis

On 20 December 2002 a Paris court convicted Soros of insider trading in Société Générale shares bought in 1988. French appeals and the European Court of Human Rights record are set out below. The conviction is the clearest criminal/legal finding against him and should not be blurred into unrelated accusations.

Soros spent roughly **$27 million** through independent groups and committees in the 2003–04 U.S. election cycle in an unsuccessful effort to defeat President George W. Bush. Beneficiaries included America Coming Together, the Center for American Progress Action Fund, and MoveOn-related activity. He helped form the **Democracy Alliance** in 2005, a network through which wealthy progressive donors coordinated long-term support for policy, advocacy, and organizing institutions. Its private membership and influence became recurring subjects of criticism.

In 2007 Soros publicly criticized U.S. and Israeli policy, the occupation, and AIPAC’s influence. He affirmed Israel’s right to exist and argued for negotiated coexistence, but his grants to Palestinian and Israeli rights groups—some sharply critical of the Israeli government—generated accusations that he was hostile to Israel. Those criticisms concern real grants and positions; claims that he secretly controls Jewish or Israeli politics are a different, unsupported category.

During the 2007–09 financial crisis, Soros returned to more active trading and published a revised theory of market reflexivity. Quantum reportedly gained in 2007–09 while many funds failed. He advocated stronger financial regulation, bank recapitalization, and limits on unregulated credit. The fund also made failed or poorly timed investments, including a pre-collapse position in Lehman Brothers. No evidence establishes the recurring claim that Soros “engineered” the 2008 crash.

### 2011–2017: family office, criminal-justice politics, and transfer to OSF

In **2011** SFM returned approximately $1 billion to outside investors and converted fully into a family office. The decision avoided the new registration and disclosure regime for advisers with outside clients under Dodd–Frank. Avoidance through a lawful change in business model is documented; evasion of an applicable registration duty was not charged.

The firm continued to produce large gains; industry estimates credited Quantum with approximately $40 billion in cumulative profits from 1973 through the mid-2010s. Such estimates are not audited public accounts and should not be confused with Soros’s personal net worth.

From 2014, Soros-supported state and local political committees spent on district-attorney races, backing candidates who advocated reduced incarceration, bail reform, diversion, and police accountability. The candidates’ records and the relationship between prosecution policy and crime became contentious. The financial support is documented. Claims that Soros personally instructed prosecutors in individual cases are not established by campaign contributions.

In **2015**, Russia’s Prosecutor General designated the Open Society Foundations and the Open Society Institute Assistance Foundation “undesirable organizations,” effectively banning their work. The Russian state characterized them as threats to constitutional order and security; OSF said the action suppressed independent civil society.

In **2017**, Soros transferred approximately **$18 billion** to OSF. The transfer reduced published estimates of his personal fortune from more than $20 billion to roughly $8 billion and made OSF one of the world’s largest private philanthropic networks. Cumulative giving exceeded **$32 billion**, although a portion remained invested in an endowment rather than already disbursed to final recipients.

### 2017–2022: Hungary confrontation, violence directed at Soros, and institutional succession

Viktor Orbán’s government made Soros the central figure of campaigns against migration, foreign-funded NGOs, and CEU. Orbán himself had received a Soros-funded scholarship to study at Oxford in 1989–90 and had worked in the reform milieu supported by the foundation. Government posters and a “national consultation” alleged a secret “Soros plan” to settle migrants in Hungary. Soros had openly proposed an EU asylum program, including lawful annual admissions and substantial EU financing; the government combined parts of those published proposals with claims he did not make and represented the result as a covert command plan.

Legislation directed at foreign universities and civil-society organizations led OSF to move its Budapest international office to Berlin in 2018 and CEU to move its U.S.-accredited degree programs to Vienna in 2019. The Court of Justice of the European Union ruled in 2020 that Hungary’s higher-education restrictions violated EU law and international commitments, and in 2021 that the “Stop Soros” asylum-assistance law also breached EU law.

On **22 October 2018**, a pipe bomb was found in a mailbox at Soros’s Katonah/Bedford-area residence. He was not present. It was the first discovered in a series mailed by **Cesar Sayoc** to prominent critics of Donald Trump. Sayoc pleaded guilty to 65 felonies in 2019 and received a twenty-year federal sentence. This was documented political violence directed at Soros; it was not a hoax staged by him, as some online accounts alleged.

In 2020 OSF announced a **$220 million** commitment to organizations led by Black communities and to racial-justice work. This transparent funding later became one factual kernel for claims that Soros paid individual rioters or controlled every Black Lives Matter protest. No consulted grant record, criminal case, or financial tracing establishes payment to individual protesters for violence.

Alex Soros was elected OSF chair in **December 2022**. The transition was publicly disclosed in June 2023.

### 2023–2026: handover and present position

In June **2023**, George Soros confirmed that Alex would control the roughly $25 billion family and philanthropic network, contrary to his earlier statement that no child would automatically inherit it. Alex also entered the investment committee and took responsibility for much U.S. political strategy. OSF then restructured, planned staff reductions of about 40 percent, and reduced or reoriented some European grantmaking. Grantees criticized abruptness and uncertainty; OSF said it was concentrating resources under a new operating model.

On **4 January 2025**, President Joe Biden awarded Soros the **Presidential Medal of Freedom**. Alex accepted it on his father’s behalf. The award citation emphasized democracy, human rights, education, and social justice. Critics, especially Republican politicians and conservative media, objected on the basis of Soros’s partisan donations, prosecutorial politics, and financial record.

In August 2025 President Donald Trump publicly demanded that George and Alex Soros be charged under the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act, alleging support for violent protests. In September, news organizations reported that a Justice Department official had directed several U.S. attorney’s offices to prepare possible investigations of OSF under theories including racketeering, fraud, arson, and material support for terrorism. OSF denied funding terrorism or violence and described the initiative as politically motivated. **At the 20 August 2026 cutoff, the consulted public record disclosed no indictment, formal criminal charge, conviction, or court finding against George Soros or OSF arising from those 2025 demands.** A reported directive or investigative proposal is not a charge.

OSF announced in May 2026 a five-year, $300 million U.S. democracy and economic-security initiative. George remained the network’s founder but not its operating chair. Public sources did not disclose a specific incapacitating illness. His absence from the 2025 medal ceremony should not be converted into an undocumented diagnosis.

### Governance distinctions

The phrase “Soros network” can conceal materially different kinds of control:

- **Personal capital:** assets owned by George Soros or family trusts.
- **SFM-managed capital:** family-office assets and institutional assets managed under formal mandates. A trade in an SFM regulatory filing after George reduced active management is not necessarily his personal decision.
- **OSF endowment:** irrevocably donated charitable capital, not personal spending money, although SFM has served as investment manager.
- **National foundations and grantees:** separate organizations with boards, employees, and legal duties. Soros chose broad strategy and funded them; local boards and recipients often made operational decisions.
- **Political committees:** U.S. electoral vehicles governed by campaign-finance law and separate from tax-exempt charitable grantmaking.

Claims about his wealth or influence should specify which layer is meant.

## Financial Record, Wealth, Property, and Business Relationships

### Investment method and performance

Soros’s investment process began with a hypothesis about how participants misunderstood a market. He then looked for a feedback loop in which rising prices strengthened the apparent fundamentals, attracted more buyers, and made the original misconception temporarily self-validating. He called the eventual reversal a boom/bust sequence. Trading positions were tests: discomfort or losses could signal that the thesis was wrong, and he was known for reversing positions rather than defending them indefinitely.

His record cannot be reproduced from complete public audited statements. Published summaries generally credit the Quantum funds with average annual returns near 30 percent across the 1970s–1990s, with different figures depending on fees and dates. Industry estimates placed cumulative profits around $40 billion by the mid-2010s. These figures coexist with large losses in 1981, 1987, 1994, 1997–98, and 2000.

## Political, Religious, Philosophical, and Intellectual Development

### Fallibility and reflexivity

Soros’s mature framework has four recurring elements:

1. **Fallibility:** participants possess incomplete and biased understandings.
2. **Reflexivity:** in social systems, beliefs can alter the reality they attempt to describe. Credit expansion, asset prices, collateral values, and political expectations can reinforce one another.
3. **Open society:** institutions should permit criticism, pluralism, replacement of rulers, and correction of error.
4. **Human uncertainty:** social science cannot predict reflexive systems with the precision of physics because the observers are participants.

He applied the same vocabulary to markets and politics. A market boom could validate a false premise temporarily; propaganda could similarly alter behavior and institutions. Critics in economics argued that his formulations lacked falsifiable formal models or simply redescribed feedback and bounded rationality. The 2008 crisis increased academic interest but did not produce consensus that reflexivity was a complete theory.

### From anti-totalitarian liberalism to partisan intervention

The Holocaust and communist rule gave Soros an aversion to racial states, closed ideology, and one-party power. Popper supplied the name “open society.” During the Cold War he funded non-state information and education rather than a political party. After 1989 he expected democratic capitalism to consolidate more easily than it did and later criticized both post-communist corruption and Western neglect.

By the late 1990s he argued that laissez-faire ideology—his term was **“market fundamentalism”**—could damage social cohesion as authoritarian planning did in a different way. He supported regulated markets, progressive taxation, international institutions, European integration, human rights, and welfare protections. He remained a capitalist investor and did not advocate state ownership of the economy.

After 2001 his activity became openly partisan in the United States. He opposed George W. Bush, the Iraq War, Donald Trump, and Republican restrictions on voting, immigration, and abortion. He supported Democratic candidates and progressive organizations, though he also criticized Barack Obama for insufficiently forceful reform and backed causes that were not always Democratic priorities.

### Drug policy, criminal justice, and civil liberties

Soros argued that prohibition produced incarceration, illicit markets, and avoidable disease. He funded needle exchange, medical-marijuana initiatives, sentencing reform, and later marijuana legalization. Opponents associated these policies with disorder and drug use; his published position was regulation, harm reduction, and treatment rather than encouragement of addiction.

District-attorney spending extended this philosophy into electoral politics. Soros-supported committees backed prosecutors who promised bail reform, reduced prosecution of low-level offenses, and review of police misconduct. The results varied by jurisdiction. Aggregate claims that such prosecutors either caused a national crime wave or uniquely solved mass incarceration exceed the causal evidence.

### Religion and Jewish identity

The family was secular. Soros has not presented his philanthropy as a religious mission. He has at times kept distance from organized Jewish communal institutions, which made his criticism of Israeli policy particularly contentious. In 2007 he wrote that Israel’s security required a political settlement and criticized the U.S.–Israel policy consensus and AIPAC. His position was neither theological anti-Judaism nor rejection of Israel’s existence; it was a secular, liberal critique that opponents inside and outside Jewish politics disputed sharply.

### Europe, migration, Russia, China, and Ukraine

- **Europe:** Soros treated the European Union as an incomplete open-society project requiring fiscal capacity, democratic legitimacy, and defense against nationalism. He supported the euro while criticizing its architecture.
- **Migration:** In 2015–16 he proposed an EU-wide asylum system, legal admissions, front-line support, and substantial common financing. These proposals were public. He did not publish the Hungarian government’s claimed plan for compulsory permanent settlement in every member state or personal cash payments to migrants.
- **Russia:** He supported post-Soviet education, science, media, and civil society, invested privately, lost heavily, and later became a critic of Vladimir Putin. Russian authorities banned OSF in 2015. Scholarship on the 1990s shows substantial Soros resources but also considerable discretion by Russian reformers; “Soros imposed the curriculum” is too simple.
- **China:** He initially supported reform and later treated the Chinese party-state’s use of artificial intelligence and surveillance as a principal threat to open society. The failed 1986–89 fund produced recurring, unproven CIA stories.
- **Ukraine:** The International Renaissance Foundation dates to 1990. Soros supported civic organizations, legal and economic reform, and later defense of Ukrainian sovereignty. Russian narratives portray the activity as command of a Western coup; documented grants establish influence and support, not operational authorship of mass protest or government decisions.

### Société Générale case in more detail

The underlying episode was the 1988 attempt by financier **Georges Pébereau** and associates to assemble a bloc in recently privatized Société Générale. Soros was told of interest in a raid, declined direct participation, and bought shares. French investigators began examining the trades around 1990. The legal controversy was unusually prolonged because France’s insider-trading doctrine was still developing and regulators themselves had earlier expressed uncertainty about whether information about an inchoate raid was sufficiently precise and confidential.

The Strasbourg majority did not decide whether Soros was morally culpable or re-try the facts. It decided whether, as an experienced institutional investor receiving professional advice, he could reasonably foresee that the conduct risked criminal liability. The narrow 4–3 decision and dissent are part of the record. Calling the conviction nonexistent is false; describing it as a money-laundering conviction or a U.S. conviction is also false.

### Political finance and democratic-accountability questions

Soros’s U.S. political spending is legally significant even when lawful. He used federal independent-expenditure committees, super PACs, nonprofit advocacy, state committees, and donor networks. In 2022 he placed **$125 million** into Democracy PAC for future cycles. Public totals differ because some databases count the transfer into a committee while others count only its later expenditures, and because charitable OSF grants cannot legally be treated as candidate contributions.

Supporters describe the spending as transparent participation under the same rules used by conservative megadonors. Critics argue that one person can reshape prosecutorial elections and nonprofit agendas. Both propositions can coexist: much of the spending is disclosed and lawful, and its concentration raises questions of private power. Neither establishes bribery without a quid pro quo or other evidence.

### Conflicts between investment and philanthropy

The Russia record created a genuine conflict-of-role problem. Soros’s foundations funded reform and civil society while his investment vehicles joined privatization transactions. He disclosed the conflict in “My Investments in Russia” and argued that the investment was commercial and the foundation independent. Critics contended that access, information, and reputation were impossible to separate. No consulted court found that OSF grants procured Svyazinvest for him, but the appearance and structural risk were real.

The same caution applies to OSF support for investigative journalism and governance organizations whose work affected companies or states in which SFM had interests. A shared policy position or grant is not proof that an investigation was commissioned to produce a trading result. Where specific litigation alleged such conduct—BSGR—the claim ended with prejudice and no liability finding.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### 1. “Soros was a Nazi collaborator who confiscated Jewish property”

**Story.** Soros allegedly survived by working for the Nazis, identifying Jews, or confiscating their property and later admitted feeling no remorse.

**Appearance and circulation.** The story draws on a **20 December 1998 CBS *60 Minutes*** interview with Steve Kroft. Kroft described the Ministry official as helping confiscate Jewish property and asked whether Soros felt guilt. Soros said he had no role in the confiscation, compared himself to a child spectator, and said the property would have been taken whether he was present or not. A 2006 statement by Representative Mark Souder reprinted and amplified the exchange in the Congressional Record. Glenn Beck, Alex Jones, politicians including Louie Gohmert, and viral social-media posts later collapsed “was present once” into “confiscated property.” A widely circulated photograph of an adult Nazi guard was falsely labeled Soros; it actually depicted Auschwitz guard **Oskar Gröning**.

**Evidence.** Soros was a Jewish child of thirteen/fourteen using forged Christian papers. He briefly delivered Jewish Council notices, but Tivadar stopped him and warned recipients. He accompanied his protector on an inventory visit. No evidence makes him a German or Hungarian Nazi official, beneficiary, or decision-maker. His age and concealed Jewish identity are independently incompatible with the viral SS story.

**Reputational effect.** The claim transforms the source of Soros’s open-society narrative into a charge of betrayal. It has been especially effective because the edited interview language sounds incriminating when the age, false identity, and lack of agency are removed.

### 2. “The happiest year of his life was the Holocaust”

**Story.** Soros supposedly said he enjoyed the murder of Jews.

**Source and evidence.** He did call 1944 an exhilarating or “happiest” year in accounts of his father’s resourcefulness, explaining that they confronted mortal danger and often prevailed. He did not describe deportations or deaths as joyful. The quotation is genuine; the object attributed to the happiness is changed in hostile retellings.

**Effect.** It supplies an emotionally powerful bridge between the collaborator myth and later claims of psychopathy.

### 3. “The man who single-handedly broke the Bank of England”

**Story.** One trader defeated Britain and removed it from the ERM.

**Origin.** British and international press after 16 September 1992. Soros accepted the publicity and used the phrase in later public identity.

**Evidence.** Quantum’s approximately $10 billion sterling short and enormous profit are real. So are trades by many other institutions, Bundesbank–British policy conflict, reserve pressure, recession, and an unsustainable parity. Soros was the most visible and perhaps largest individual fund manager, not the sole causal actor.

**Effect.** The legend established his genius mythology, attracted investors and scrutiny, and made him a symbol of finance overpowering democratic governments.

### 4. “Soros caused the Asian financial crisis”

**Story.** Quantum attacked the Thai baht and Malaysian ringgit, causing regional collapse.

**Origin.** Most prominently Mahathir Mohamad in 1997, amid capital flight and political pressure.

**Evidence.** Hedge funds traded Asian currencies; complete position books are not public. The best contemporaneous empirical study located found no evidence that hedge funds as a class caused the crisis and noted that positions were not large or consistently short enough to support the accusation. Domestic dollar debts, fragile banking systems, fixed exchange rates, and contagion were major documented causes.

**Effect.** The story made Soros a durable villain in parts of Asia and a shorthand for unaccountable Western capital, regardless of later partial reconciliation with Mahathir.

### 5. “Soros secretly authored color revolutions and coups”

**Story.** Soros, usually with the CIA, supposedly selected governments in Georgia, Ukraine, Serbia, and other states by financing protests.

**Origin and circulation.** Russian, Serbian, Georgian, Hungarian, and other government or nationalist media; later transnational conspiracy outlets. The “color revolution” label joined older intelligence claims to visible grants for election monitoring, independent media, youth groups, and legal aid.

**Evidence for an underlying claim.** OSF did fund civil society and media in countries that later experienced mass protest and electoral transitions. Soros openly favored democratic opposition to authoritarian governments. In Georgia he acknowledged foundation contributions to civic infrastructure around the Rose Revolution.

**Evidence against the maximal story.** Local movements had large memberships, different funders, internal conflicts, and political causes. Grant support does not show that Soros ordered turnout, controlled leaders, or determined outcomes. No consulted declassified record identifies him as a CIA operational controller. Academic work treats foreign assistance as one influence among domestic elite splits, fraud, organization, and public mobilization.

**Effect.** Governments used the story to stigmatize independent organizations as foreign agents; supporters sometimes created a counter-hagiography in which Soros “brought democracy,” likewise minimizing local agency.

### 6. “The Soros Plan” and open borders

**Story.** Soros secretly ordered Brussels to import at least one million Muslim migrants annually, force quotas on Hungary, dismantle borders, and pay each migrant a large personal allowance.

**Appearance.** Orbán government campaigns, posters, speeches, and the 2017 Hungarian national consultation.

**Evidence.** Soros openly published a 2015 EU asylum proposal calling initially for at least one million lawful asylum admissions annually and €15,000 per asylum seeker for each of the first two years, paid to states to cover housing, health, and education. In 2016 he revised proposals as conditions changed, emphasized secure external borders and legal routes, and argued for EU borrowing. The Hungarian consultation converted administrative funding into a personal cash benefit, represented proposals as binding secret control, and attributed additional claims to him.

**Effect.** The campaign helped consolidate Orbán’s migration politics and furnished a portable symbol—“Soros”—for opposition to the EU, NGOs, Muslims, and liberal cosmopolitanism.

### 7. “He pays protesters, Antifa, Black Lives Matter, and migrant caravans”

**Story.** Demonstrators are employees or mercenaries on a Soros payroll; sometimes buses, placards, or travel are alleged to be proof.

**Origin.** Recurs after protests in the United States and Europe. A 2018 claim by Representative Matt Gaetz helped spread a migrant-caravan version; 2020 racial-justice protests produced new variants.

**Evidence.** OSF openly funds immigrant-rights, legal-aid, organizing, and racial-justice organizations and announced $220 million after the 2020 protests. Some funded organizations pay staff, arrange lawful transport, or support demonstrations. No identified financial record shows Soros paying the caravan’s individual travelers or hiring people to commit riot, arson, or assault. “A grantee participated” and “the donor secretly ordered every act” are distinct propositions.

**Effect.** The claim denies participants independent motives and converts disclosed institutional funding into a universal explanation for dissent. It also underlay the 2025 political demands for RICO and terrorism investigations.

**Story.** Soros controls election technology and can alter votes.

**Origin.** U.S. election rumors, especially in 2016 and after 2020. A real personnel overlap amplified it: **Mark Malloch-Brown** had leadership roles connected to both the Smartmatic parent-company orbit and OSF at different times.

**Story.** Soros is a public face for the Rothschild family or a single secret director of banks, media, courts, migration, and governments.

**Effect.** The narrative fuses verifiable wealth and grants with limitless agency. Not every criticism of Soros is antisemitic; the specific secret-Jewish-finance motifs have a documented antisemitic genealogy.

**Story.** Soros supposedly declared himself divine or entitled to rule the world.

**Evidence.** Soros described grandiosity as a psychological tendency he learned to control and linked it to both ambition and danger. He did not found a religion or claim supernatural powers. The language is authentic self-analysis, not a literal creed.

### 11. Premature death and arrest stories

Reuters accidentally published a prewritten Soros obituary on **18 April 2013**, containing placeholder language and erroneous statements. Sympathetic institutional histories sometimes imply that photocopiers, scholarships, and grants caused the democratic transitions of 1989 or later revolutions. Communist economic failure, Soviet policy, organized dissidents, churches, workers, national movements, and millions of voters were independent causal forces. Treating Soros as the sole benefactor repeats the agency error of hostile mythology in positive form.

Portions of its argument survive indirectly in later lectures on reflexivity. It was not issued as a lost commercial book, despite occasional catalog-like references online.

## Books and Major Works About George Soros

### Biographies, profiles, and institutional histories

- **Robert Slater, _Soros: The Life, Times & Trading Secrets of the World’s Greatest Investor_** (1996), revised in later editions as **_Soros: The Life, Ideas, and Impact of the World’s Most Influential Investor_** (2009). Accessible financial biography; heavily dependent on interviews and published accounts.
- **Michael T. Kaufman, _Soros: The Life and Times of a Messianic Billionaire_** (Alfred A. Knopf, 2002; Vintage paperback, 2003). The fullest conventional biography, written by a journalist who knew the family and used interviews and Tivadar’s memoir. Its “messianic” framing became part of later debate.

- **Emily Tamkin, _The Influence of Soros: Politics, Power, and the Struggle for an Open Society_** (Harper, 2020). A study of the person, network, symbolism, and antisemitic mythology rather than a cradle-to-present biography.
- **Peter L. W. Osnos, ed., _George Soros: A Life in Full_** (Harvard Business Review Press, 2022). Multi-author interpretive portrait; edited tribute/critical appreciation rather than an independent single-author biography.
- **Sebastian Mallaby, _More Money Than God: Hedge Funds and the Making of a New Elite_** (Penguin Press, 2010). Important comparative history of hedge funds with substantial reporting on Soros, Rogers, Druckenmiller, and Quantum.

- **“The Almighty Soros? Education Reform and Foreign Influence in Post-Soviet Russia”** (*Contemporary European History*, 2026). Archival/academic treatment of Russian education work and later foreign-influence narratives.

- **Connie Bruck, “The World According to Soros,”** *The New Yorker* (23 January 1995). Major profile covering the fund, personality, Russia, regulatory history, and philosophical ambition.

## Death, Later Life, and Present Status

George Soros was alive on **20 August 2026**. No death date or cause exists. Leadership transition should not be reported as death: Alex Soros became OSF chair in December 2022 and public successor in June 2023, while professional managers run SFM. George’s 2025 Presidential Medal of Freedom was accepted by Alex on his behalf; public sources did not give a medical explanation.

His late-life activity shifted from trading and operational management to essays, institutional succession, and symbolic political importance. The family’s capital and influence continue through separate channels: SFM, OSF, Democracy PAC and allied committees, the children’s own foundations and ventures, and CEU.

## Reputation, Historical Reinterpretation, and Legacy

Because Soros is living, this is a contemporary legacy rather than posthumous reputation.

### Finance

His established financial legacy is the demonstration that a flexible offshore fund could trade equities, bonds, commodities, and currencies globally and make concentrated macro bets against government policy. Black Wednesday remains the defining example. The losses of 1981, 1987, Russia, and the technology bubble qualify the later legend of infallibility. Reflexivity influenced practitioners more than mainstream formal economics, though the global financial crisis renewed scholarly interest.

### Philanthropy and institutions

OSF created one of the largest privately funded transnational civil-society infrastructures: universities, legal aid, scholarships, media, public health, Roma rights, drug reform, election observation, and human-rights advocacy. Some institutions survived long after initial grants; others contracted when priorities changed. The network also illustrates a central question of modern philanthropy: private wealth can protect unpopular minorities and institutions from governments, while simultaneously setting public agendas without electoral authorization.

### Politics

Soros helped normalize billionaire-scale independent spending on U.S. ideological infrastructure and local prosecutorial elections. Conservative donors and organizations built parallel networks; each side cites the other to justify escalation. His transition from cross-partisan democracy support abroad to explicit Democratic opposition at home made it easier to interpret all OSF grants as party operations, even where legal entities and purposes differ.

### Symbol

Soros became a globally portable adversary-image because several real attributes fit older stories: he is Jewish, foreign-born, extremely wealthy, a currency speculator, a migration advocate, and the donor to a geographically dispersed network. Propaganda does not invent those attributes; it converts influence into omnipotence and association into command. Conversely, hagiography converts grants into sole authorship of democratic change. A documentary assessment requires holding the middle ground of evidence: enormous financial and agenda-setting power, large and traceable accomplishments, major trading failures, one sustained insider-trading conviction, other regulatory matters, political partisanship, and no substantiation for the Nazi, intelligence-master, voting-machine, or paid-rioter narratives.
