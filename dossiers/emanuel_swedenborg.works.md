## Works by Emanuel Swedenborg

### Poems, occasional texts, and literary works

- **1700:** *Verses for a Wedding (“Post Nubila Phoebus”)*. A Swedish occasional poem of ten stanzas, written for the marriage of Johannes Kolmodin and Beata Hesselius.
- **1700:** *Verses to Notman*. A four-line Swedish dedicatory poem printed in Christiernus Notman’s graduation essay.
- **1701:** *Verses to Bredberg*. A Latin dedicatory poem for a university dissertation.
- **1703:** *Verses to Eric Benzelius, Sr.*. A Latin commemorative poem addressed to the theologian and archbishop.
- **1707:** *The Select Sentences of Publilius Syrus Explained*. Swedenborg’s Latin university dissertation: an edition and moral commentary on the Roman mime-writer’s maxims.
- **1708:** *The Northern Muse at Play*. A collection of Latin verse; its poems include mythological, occasional, and personal pieces rather than forming a single narrative work.
- **1710:** *Stenbock’s Victory*. A Swedish occasional poem celebrating Magnus Stenbock’s victory at Helsingborg.
- **1710:** *Verses to Unge*. A Latin dedicatory poem.
- **1714:** *To Sophia Brenner*. A Latin poem addressed to the Swedish poet.
- **1715:** *The Fable of the Love of Urania and Her Transformation into an Earthly Worshipper*. A Latin mythological poem, later revised and reissued.
- **1717:** *First Epigram for Daedalus Hyperboreus*. A four-line Latin poem functioning as a device or motto for Swedenborg’s technological periodical.
- **1717:** *Second Epigram for Daedalus Hyperboreus*. A second four-line Latin poem for the same periodical.
- **1722?:** *Augustinus*. A surviving manuscript fragment consisting of four Latin verse lines and a dedication; its date is uncertain.
- **1722:** *A Celebration of the Name-Day of Frederick*. A fourteen-line Latin court poem.
- **1740:** *Verses in Celebration of Printing*. A ten-line Latin poem written for the tricentenary of movable-type printing.
- **1744–1745:** *The Worship and Love of God*. An unfinished philosophical-theological creation narrative in two published parts; a projected third part was never completed, so the obra is also an abandoned project.

### Mechanical projects, designs, and instruments

- **1714:** *Submarine Ship*. An engineering project described in correspondence rather than a securely documented constructed vessel; it therefore resists classification as a surviving machine.
- **1714:** *Universal Musical Instrument*. A proposed keyboard mechanism intended to imitate several instruments; known as a project, not as an extant object.
- **1714–1716:** *Air Pumps*. Designs and notes for improved pneumatic pumps, some surviving only in manuscript descriptions.
- **1714–1716:** *Air Guns*. Mechanical designs for weapons operated by compressed air.
- **1714–1716:** *Water Clock*. A design for a timekeeping mechanism regulated by water.
- **1714–1716:** *Another Hearing Aid*. A speaking- or hearing-tube design; the bibliography records the text describing the device, not a surviving instrument.
- **1716:** *Mechanical Carriage*. A proposed self-moving or mechanically assisted carriage.
- **1716:** *Machine for Raising Weights*. A mechanical project presented through drawings and explanatory prose.
- **1716:** *Sluice*. A hydraulic-engineering design associated with Christopher Polhem’s technological programme.
- **1716:** *Flying Machine*. An illustrated proposal for a human-powered aircraft. It is an engineering project rather than evidence that a flyable machine was completed.
- **1716:** *Model of a Flying Machine*. A related model or projected model is mentioned in Swedenborg’s papers, but its construction and survival are not securely established.
- **1716:** *Polhem’s Tap*. A bilingual description and illustration of Christopher Polhem’s device; Swedenborg made the publication and exposition, but the mechanism itself is Polhem’s.
- **1716–1717:** *Speaking-Tube*. A description of an acoustic communication device.
- **1717:** *Air Pump*. A published technical description of a pneumatic apparatus.
- **1717:** *Machine for Drawing Files*. A description of Polhem’s mechanized file-cutting equipment; attribution of the machine must be distinguished from Swedenborg’s authorship of its exposition.
- **1717:** *Machine for Testing the Strength of Materials*. An experimental apparatus described in *Daedalus Hyperboreus*.
- **1717:** *Machine for Raising Ore*. A mining-engineering description produced in connection with Swedenborg’s work in the College of Mines.
- **1717:** *Machine for Raising Water*. A hydraulic project or description, not a securely surviving physical object.
- **1717:** *Machine for Transporting Boats Overland*. A civil-engineering project connected with moving vessels between waterways.
- **1718:** *New Method of Constructing Docks*. A proposal in hydraulic and naval engineering.
- **1763:** *Inlaying Marble*. A published craft protocol explaining how to produce decorative inlays in marble slabs for tables and architectural ornament.

### Periodical and its scientific articles

- **1716–1718:** *Daedalus Hyperboreus*. A six-number scientific and technological periodical edited, substantially written, and financed by Swedenborg. It is a composite obra—part journal, part workshop record, part vehicle for Christopher Polhem’s inventions—and not simply a single-authored book.
- **1716:** *Method of Calculating the Ratio of Water Flow*. A hydrostatics article in *Daedalus Hyperboreus*.
- **1716:** *Observations on Polhem’s Dry Dock*. A technical report on dock construction and operation.
- **1716:** *Experiment with Round Shot and Paper*. A short experimental report.
- **1716:** *Experiment on the Resistance of Water and Snow*. A ballistics and resistance experiment.
- **1716:** *Method of Finding Longitudes*. A proposed astronomical navigation method using the Moon.
- **1716:** *First Way to Find Longitude*. A companion navigation protocol.
- **1716:** *Finding Longitudes by Stars*. A second companion method based on stellar observations.
- **1717:** *Calculating Volumes*. Mathematical tables for cubes, cylinders, and spheres.
- **1717:** *Analytical Demonstration*. A mathematical article presenting an algebraic method of demonstration.
- **1717:** *Tinwork*. A technological account of tin manufacture.
- **1717:** *Winter Experiments*. Reports of physical experiments made under winter conditions.
- **1717:** *Universal Conversion of Carolins*. A numerical table for converting Swedish coinage.
- **1718:** *Algebra*. A mathematical article or instructional text.
- **1718:** *New Method of Reckoning*. A computational protocol.
- **1718:** *Observations on Fire and Furnaces*. An experimental and metallurgical article.
- **1718:** *Derotation of the Earth*. A cosmological argument about changes in the Earth’s motion; surviving drafts show that the published article was one manifestation of a longer investigation.

### Scientific, mathematical, and philosophical books

- **1718:** *Rules of Technology*. A compact technical handbook setting out principles for mechanical construction.
- **1718:** *Attempt to Find the Longitude of Places by Means of the Moon*. A navigation treatise; Swedenborg repeatedly revised and republished the project in different languages and forms.
- **1719:** *Proposal for the Division of Money and Measures*. A programme for decimalizing Swedish currency, weights, and measures—simultaneously a mathematical text and a proposed state protocol.
- **1721:** *A Forerunner on the Principles of Natural Things*. A short natural-philosophical treatise developing a particulate account of matter.
- **1721:** *New Observations and Discoveries Respecting Iron and Fire*. An experimental metallurgical work.
- **1721:** *New Method of Finding Terrestrial Longitudes*. A Latin version and development of Swedenborg’s longitude project.
- **1722:** *Miscellaneous Observations on Natural Things*. A collection of geological, mineralogical, chemical, and physical observations published in successive parts; its heterogeneous notebook-like form is integral to the work.
- **1722:** *Swedish Currency and Finance*. A proposal concerning coinage and monetary reform.
- **1729:** *On the Height of Water and the Strong Tides in the Primeval World*. A geological treatise arguing from marine remains and changes in sea level.
- **1734:** *Philosophical and Metallurgical Works, Volume I: Principia*. A large cosmological and natural-philosophical system explaining matter, motion, magnetism, and the formation of the universe.
- **1734:** *Philosophical and Metallurgical Works, Volume II: Iron*. A documentary and technical survey of iron mining, smelting, and manufacture.
- **1734:** *Philosophical and Metallurgical Works, Volume III: Copper and Brass*. A corresponding metallurgical survey of copper and its alloys.
- **1734:** *The Infinite and the Final Cause of Creation*. A philosophical inquiry into infinity, creation, and the interaction of soul and body.
- **1740:** *Memoir of Charles XII*. An untitled recollection incorporated into Göran Nordberg’s history; it may have been written as early as 1732.
- **1740:** *Dynamics of the Soul’s Domain, Volume I*. An anatomical, physiological, and philosophical study of blood, arteries, veins, and the heart, with an introduction to rational psychology; traditionally translated under the misleading title *Economy of the Animal Kingdom*.
- **1741:** *Dynamics of the Soul’s Domain, Volume II*. A continuation on the brain, cortical substance, and soul; together the volumes form an attempted passage from anatomy to psychology.
- **1744:** *The Soul’s Domain, Volume I*. The first published portion of an anatomical-philosophical investigation traditionally called *The Animal Kingdom*.
- **1744:** *The Soul’s Domain, Volume II*. The second volume, continuing Swedenborg’s search for the soul through bodily structure and function.
- **1745:** *The Soul’s Domain, Volume III*. A third volume issued separately; the larger projected anatomical system remained incomplete.

### Scientific and philosophical manuscripts

- **1715–1717:** *Drafts and Prospectus for Daedalus Hyperboreus*. Manuscript plans, presentation copies, and preliminary versions belonging to the periodical project; they are documentary stages rather than independent finished books.
- **1715–1717:** *Notes on Tremulation*. An unfinished investigation of vibration as a basis of sensation, sound, and natural action.
- **1716–1717:** *Draft on Small Vibrations*. A related physical manuscript, surviving in more than one attempt.
- **1716:** *Draft on Finding Longitude*. Preparatory material for the published longitude writings.
- **1717:** *Memorandum on Establishing Saltworks in Sweden*. An economic and industrial proposal submitted in manuscript.
- **1717:** *Draft on Derotation of the Earth*. A thirty-eight-page precursor to Swedenborg’s published cosmological article.
- **1717:** *Draft on Instituting an Observatory*. An untitled proposal for an astronomical institution.
- **1717–1718:** *Stereometry*. Mathematical manuscript material on measuring solids.
- **1717–1718:** *Weights*. Tables or calculations concerning systems of weight.
- **1721:** *Draft of Miscellaneous Observations*. An eighteen-page precursor to the published collection.
- **1722:** *Draft on Magnets*. Experimental and theoretical notes later feeding into the *Principia*.
- **1720s:** *On Sulphur and Pyrites*. An unpublished chemical and mineralogical treatise.
- **1720s:** *On Vitriol*. An unpublished chemical investigation.
- **1720s:** *Addenda to the Principia*. Manuscript natural-philosophical material supplementing the developing cosmology.
- **1720s–1730s:** *Wolff’s Ontology and the Principia*. Notes engaging Christian Wolff’s philosophy in relation to Swedenborg’s system.
- **1730s:** *Abstract of the Principia*. Swedenborg’s synopsis of his natural philosophy.
- **1730s:** *The Mechanism of the Soul and Body*. An unfinished philosophical investigation associated with *The Infinite*.
- **1736–1740:** *Journal of Travels*. A travel diary recording European journeys, mines, manufactures, art, scholarship, and daily experience; it is a documentary assemblage rather than a designed publication.
- **1736–1740:** *Notebook of Philosophical and Scientific Extracts*. Reading notes and excerpts; because much of it copies other writers, it resists treatment as an authored work in the ordinary sense.
- **1739–1740:** *Drafts for Dynamics of the Soul’s Domain*. Preparatory anatomical and philosophical manuscripts, including material not carried into print.
- **1741–1744:** *The Brain*. An extensive, unfinished anatomical manuscript on the cerebrum, cerebellum, medulla, and nervous system.
- **1741–1744:** *The Fiber*. An anatomical-philosophical manuscript treating the nerve fibre as a fundamental organic form.
- **1741–1744:** *The Senses*. An unfinished physiological study of sensory organs and perception.
- **1741–1744:** *Generation*. A manuscript investigation of reproduction and embryology.
- **1741–1744:** *Action*. An unfinished study of bodily action and the relation between intention, nerves, and muscles.
- **1741–1744:** *The Soul*. An unfinished rational-psychological treatise intended as a culmination of Swedenborg’s anatomical project.
- **1741–1744:** *Rational Psychology*. Draft material seeking to infer the nature of the soul from anatomy and experience.
- **1741–1744:** *Anatomy of the Body*. A projected comprehensive anatomical work represented by surviving drafts rather than a completed obra.
- **1741–1744:** *Animal Spirit*. Manuscript material on the subtle fluid Swedenborg believed mediated between soul and body.
- **1743–1744:** *Swammerdam’s Book of Nature*. Extracts and commentary based on Jan Swammerdam; its dependence on another author’s work makes it principally a research notebook.
- **1743–1744:** *Journal of Dreams*. A private Swedish-Dutch diary recording dreams, religious crisis, and changing vocation. It survives incompletely and was not fashioned for publication.
- **1744–1745:** *Drafts for The Worship and Love of God*. Preliminary and continuation material for the published creation narrative, including the unrealized third part.

### Mining, finance, politics, and public memoranda

- **1717:** *Memorandum on Saltworks*. A practical project for domestic salt production.
- **1722:** *Letter to Frederick I on Working Copper*. A technical-administrative proposal addressed to the king.
- **1722:** *Paper in Reply to Conrad Quensel*. A learned defence read before the Swedish Royal Academy of Sciences and subsequently printed.
- **1720s–1740s:** *Official Reports for the College of Mines*. A body of assessments, minutes, mine inspections, and technical memoranda produced in office. It is better classified as administrative labour than as a single authored work, and the surviving documentary boundaries are not always stable.
- **1760:** *Memorandum on the Exchange Rate*. A six-page intervention in Sweden’s monetary crisis.
- **1760:** *Memorandum Recommending the Abolition of Paper Money*. A one-page policy proposal advocating currency of intrinsic value.
- **1760:** *Additional Memorandum on the Exchange Rate*. A further five-page financial intervention.
- **1761:** *Memorandum against Exporting Copper*. An economic-policy paper on the Swedish metal trade.
- **1761:** *Memorandum Defending the Swedish Constitution*. A defence of constitutional monarchy against Anders Nordencrantz.
- **1761:** *Memorandum Defending Political Freedom*. A short political proposal on preserving Sweden’s liberty.
- **1763:** *Presentation Draft of Inlaying Marble*. The manuscript prepared for the published craft article; it is a stage of that work rather than an autonomous book.
- **1766:** *Swedish Money*. A renewed printed intervention in currency policy.
- **1766:** *Additional Considerations on Swedish Finance*. A companion monetary tract.
- **1766:** *The Dearth and Scarcity in Sweden*. An economic pamphlet examining shortage and public policy.

### Biblical research apparatus and private records

- **1745–1746:** *Biblical History of Creation*. An early exegetical manuscript marking Swedenborg’s transition from natural philosophy to biblical theology.
- **1745–1747:** *The Word Explained*. An extensive but unfinished commentary on Genesis, Exodus, and portions of the historical and prophetic books. Also called *Adversaria*, it combines exegesis, spiritual experiences, and working notes and was never prepared by Swedenborg for publication.
- **1745–1747:** *First Draft Concordance of Prophetic Material in the Bible*. A manuscript index serving as research infrastructure rather than discursive prose.
- **1746–1747:** *Concordance of Proper Nouns in the Bible*. A 278-page lexical and thematic index.
- **1747–early 1748:** *Second Draft Concordance of Prophetic Material in the Bible*. A 673-page reworked concordance.
- **1747–1752:** *Marginal Notes in the Schmidt and van der Hooght Bibles*. Handwritten annotations and underlinings in Swedenborg’s own Bibles. They resist separation from the physical copies carrying them, and the two annotated books differ greatly in density.
- **1748:** *Concordance of the Four Gospels*. A 486-page manuscript index.
- **1748:** *Greek Orthodox Religion*. A three-page descriptive theological memorandum.
- **1747–1765:** *Spiritual Experiences*. A long, private, intermittently maintained record traditionally called the *Spiritual Diary*. It is a notebook sequence, not a book Swedenborg authorized for publication, and later editions have imposed titles and divisions upon difficult manuscripts.
- **1747–1765:** *Minor Spiritual Diary*. A smaller related record; its relation to the principal diary is editorially complex.
- **1748–1756:** *Rough Copy of Secrets of Heaven*. The extensive manuscript underlying the published work.
- **1748–1756:** *Indexes to Secrets of Heaven*. Swedenborg’s working indexes and concordances to his own exposition; these are tools made for navigating another obra.
- **1752:** *Swedenborg’s Almanac for 1752*. A printed almanac transformed by his handwritten appointments and notes; the base object was not made by him, but the annotated object is a personal documentary manifestation.
- **1750s:** *Swedenborg Family Genealogy*. A genealogical compilation rather than a theological treatise.

### Published theological works

- **1749–1756:** *Secrets of Heaven*. An eight-volume, 4,559-page exposition of Genesis and Exodus interwoven with reports of the spiritual world. Published anonymously, it is at once biblical commentary, visionary report, and the foundation of Swedenborg’s mature theological project.
- **1758:** *Heaven and Hell*. An anonymous systematic account of heaven, the world of spirits, and hell, presented as drawn from things “heard and seen.”
- **1758:** *The New Jerusalem and Its Heavenly Teaching*. A compact, systematically arranged digest of Swedenborg’s theology, largely directing readers back to *Secrets of Heaven*.
- **1758:** *The Last Judgment and Babylon Destroyed*. A theological manifesto announcing that the Last Judgment had already occurred in the spiritual world.
- **1758:** *The White Horse*. A short biblical exposition on Revelation 19 and the spiritual sense of Scripture.
- **1758:** *Other Planets*. A visionary cosmography reporting conversations with spirits attributed to the Moon, the planets, and worlds beyond the solar system.
- **1763:** *The Lord*. One of four doctrinal treatises for the “New Jerusalem,” concerning Christ and the divine-human.
- **1763:** *Sacred Scripture*. A doctrinal treatise on the Bible’s literal and spiritual senses.
- **1763:** *Life*. A practical doctrinal protocol arguing that religion consists in living according to divine commandments.
- **1763:** *Faith*. A short doctrinal treatise redefining saving faith in relation to charity and life.
- **1763:** *Continuation on the Last Judgment*. A supplement to the 1758 work.
- **1763:** *Continuation on the Spiritual World*. Published with the preceding supplement, this is a further report on communities and conditions in the spiritual world.
- **1763:** *Divine Love and Wisdom*. A philosophical theology of divine substance, spiritual reality, degrees, creation, and the human form.
- **1764:** *Divine Providence*. A systematic account of divine government, human freedom, permission of evil, and salvation.
- **1766:** *Apocalypse Revealed*. A two-volume verse-by-verse exposition of Revelation, incorporating staged “memorable relations” as visionary-philosophical narratives.
- **1768:** *Marriage Love*. A theological, ethical, and visionary treatment of marriage, sexuality, chastity, and their opposites; traditionally called *Conjugial Love*.
- **1769:** *A Brief Exposition of the Teaching of the New Church*. A polemical synopsis contrasting Swedenborg’s theology with established Protestant doctrine.
- **1769:** *Soul-Body Interaction*. A short philosophical-theological account of influx and the relation of mind and body, presented in dialogue with contemporary philosophical systems.
- **1770–1771:** *True Christianity*. Swedenborg’s two-volume summa of the theology of the New Church. Its original title makes an institutional and eschatological claim, so the work acts partly as manifesto as well as exposition.
- **1771:** *The Word’s Earthly and Spiritual Meanings*. A brief printed theological thesis circulated in successive editions; bibliographers distinguish the printings, but they are manifestations of one short work rather than three different intellectual projects.
- **1771:** *Theorem*. A single-sheet or very short printed doctrinal formulation associated with Swedenborg’s last publications.
- **1771:** *Answer to a Friend*. A printed reply to a theological inquiry.
- **1771:** *Answer to Oetinger’s Reply*. A polemical response in Swedenborg’s exchange with Friedrich Christoph Oetinger.

### Unpublished theological works and abandoned projects

- **1757–1759:** *Apocalypse Explained*. A massive, unfinished exposition of Revelation. Swedenborg abandoned the sequential commentary before completing the book and later made *Apocalypse Revealed* instead; posthumous editors turned the manuscript into a multi-volume work.
- **1759–1760:** *Draft of The Lord*. A surviving-copy manuscript preparatory to the 1763 publication; the original is lost.
- **before or during early 1760:** *Commentary on the Athanasian Creed*. An incomplete eighty-four-page theological commentary surviving through copies rather than Swedenborg’s original.
- **1761–1763:** *The Word of the Lord from Experience*. A manuscript study of Scripture’s spiritual meaning, traditionally titled *De Verbo*.
- **1763:** *Draft of Sacred Scripture*. Preparatory material for the published doctrinal treatise.
- **1763:** *Summaries of the Internal Sense of the Prophets and Psalms*. A concise exegetical survey commonly published posthumously as *Prophets and Psalms*.
- **1763–1764:** *Divine Love*. An unpublished theological manuscript later embedded by editors within editions of *Apocalypse Explained*.
- **1763–1764:** *Divine Wisdom*. A related manuscript, likewise not authorized as a separate publication by Swedenborg.
- **1764:** *The Spiritual World*. An unpublished continuation or supplement concerning the Last Judgment and spiritual communities.
- **1764:** *Summaries Explaining the Book of Revelation*. Working summaries associated with Swedenborg’s successive Apocalypse projects.
- **1764–1766:** *Index to Apocalypse Revealed*. A research and retrieval instrument for the published commentary.
- **1765–1766:** *Scriptural Confirmations*. A large collection of biblical passages assembled to support New Church doctrines; it is evidentiary apparatus rather than a continuous treatise.
- **1766–1767:** *Charity*. An unfinished doctrinal manuscript on charity and its forms in different occupations.
- **1766–1767:** *Marriage*. Notes and draft material associated with *Marriage Love*, traditionally published as *De Conjugio*.
- **1767–1768:** *Indexes to Marriage Love*. Swedenborg’s working indexes to the published treatise.
- **1768–1769:** *Ecclesiastical History of the New Church*. A short historical-theological sketch, unfinished and unpublished in his lifetime.
- **1768–1769:** *Justification and Good Works*. A polemical theological manuscript.
- **1768–1769:** *Conversation with Calvin*. A visionary dialogue or “memorable relation,” not a transcript independently verifiable as an historical conversation.
- **1768–1769:** *Conversations with Angels*. Short visionary narratives or doctrinal dialogues.
- **1768–1769:** *Five Memorable Relations*. A group of visionary-philosophical narratives; editorial transmission has sometimes made the group look more autonomous than Swedenborg’s manuscript arrangement warrants.
- **1768–1769:** *God the Savior*. A short doctrinal manuscript.
- **1768–1769:** *Jesus Christ*. A related Christological manuscript.
- **1768–1769:** *Gad and Asher*. A short exegetical piece on biblical tribal names.
- **1768–1769:** *Miracles and Signs*. A scriptural-theological memorandum.
- **1768–1769:** *Precepts of the Decalogue*. Notes on the Ten Commandments.
- **1768–1769:** *Index to the Formula of Concord*. An analytical index to a Lutheran confessional text; it is research apparatus based on another work.
- **1769:** *Canons of the New Church*. An unfinished systematic theology formulated in numbered propositions.
- **1769–1770:** *Various Theological Works*. Several short manuscripts—including summaries, doctrinal sketches, and visionary relations—whose later conventional titles and boundaries were supplied by editors rather than fixed by Swedenborg.
- **1770:** *Specimen and Sketch of the New Church*. A summary doctrinal project, surviving as a short manuscript.
- **1770:** *Appendix to The White Horse*. A supplement never issued by Swedenborg.
- **1770–1771:** *Additions to True Christianity*. Draft additions and supplementary passages for the published summa.
- **1771:** *Answers to Nine Questions*. Swedenborg’s responses to questions submitted by Thomas Hartley.
- **1771:** *Answers to Three Questions*. A separate short interrogatory response.
- **1771:** *Invitation to the New Church*. A late, unfinished manifesto inviting reception of the new theology.
- **1771:** *Coda to True Christianity*. Traditionally mistranslated as *The Coronis* or “Crown”; the Latin *coronis* here means an epilogue or coda. Swedenborg drafted and outlined it but did not complete the projected work.
- **around 1771:** *On the End of the Age, the Second Coming of the Lord, and the New Church*. A one-page outline associated with the unfinished coda project.
- **around 1771:** *The Abomination of Desolation*. A fragmentary late theological project.
- **1771–1772:** *Appendix to True Christianity*. Surviving outlines and fragments for an intended supplement; it never became a completed book.

### Letters and correspondence

- **1709–1772:** *Correspondence*. Hundreds of letters to relatives, scholars, officials, clergy, printers, and readers survive wholly or partly. This is a documentary corpus rather than a single obra; some originals are lost and known only through copies, summaries, or printed transcriptions, and older editions occasionally misdate or misattribute individual letters.

### Uncertain attributions and works that may not exist

- **date unknown:** *Youth’s Honor*. A work once assigned to Swedenborg, but the attribution is uncertain; it should not be silently admitted to the oeuvre.
- **date unknown:** *Reflections on the Creation*. A purported Swedenborg text whose authorship is disputed.
- **1710s?:** *Description of a Machine*. Although a copy survives in Swedenborg’s handwriting, another copy is in Christopher Polhem’s hand and the Swedish Royal Library catalogs the text as Polhem’s; it was probably copied, not composed, by Swedenborg.
- **date unknown:** *Welfare of a Country*. A Swedish prose work of uncertain authorship.
- **date unknown:** *Aims of a Literary Society*. Attribution to Swedenborg is uncertain.
- **date unknown:** *Verses to Julin*. A poem traditionally associated with Swedenborg but not securely attributable to him.
- **projected, 1740s:** *Transactions on the Soul*. A title or plan announced within the anatomical programme but not realized as a completed independent work.
- **projected, 1745:** *The Worship and Love of God, Part III*. An intended continuation that was never completed; as intention it belongs to the project, but no finished text exists.
- **projected, 1760s:** *A Full Treatment of the New Church*. A prospective work inferred from Swedenborg’s plans and cross-references; no securely identifiable completed manuscript survives.
- **projected, 1771–1772:** *Complete Coda or Appendix to True Christianity*. Swedenborg left outlines and fragments, but the comprehensive concluding work promised by the project was never made.
- **various dates:** *Lost papers reported by early editors*. Early catalogues mention several manuscripts no longer extant or known only through descriptions. Their former existence may be probable, but fabricated English titles and reconstructed contents should not be mistaken for authorially fixed works.
