## Works by Wenceslao "Wences" Casares

### Entrepreneurial obras and collective ventures

- **1994:** *Internet Argentina S.A.* An internet-service provider launched by Casares and commonly described as Argentina’s first; it is an entrepreneurial obra—a company and operating network—rather than a personally authored text. ([Stanford Technology Ventures Program](https://stvp.stanford.edu/people/wences-casares))
- **1997:** *Patagon*. An online brokerage that expanded into internet banking across Latin America, Europe, and the United States before its acquisition by Banco Santander. Sources variously call Casares its founder or identify additional founding partners, so it should be understood as a collective corporate project, not a solely authored creation. ([Stanford Technology Ventures Program](https://stvp.stanford.edu/people/wences-casares), [Endeavor](https://endeavormiami.org/2023/01/23/transforming-economies-through-high-impact-entrepreneurship/), [MECK](https://www.meckltd.com/Meck_Ltd/Main.html))
- **2002:** *Banco Lemon* (also styled *Lemon Bank*). A Brazilian retail bank and financial-inclusion project directed toward underbanked customers. Stanford attributes its founding to Casares, while MECK’s account emphasizes the founding and development work of Meyer Malka, Michael Esrubilsky, and their partners; authorship is therefore collective and inconsistently summarized. ([Stanford Technology Ventures Program](https://stvp.stanford.edu/people/wences-casares), [MECK](https://www.meckltd.com/Meck_Ltd/Team.html))
- **2002:** *Wanako Games*. A Chile-based video-game development studio co-created with Esteban Sosnik, Tiburcio de la Cárcova, and others; accounts disagree over the complete roster of founders. The studio is the relevant obra here: its individual games were collaborative studio productions and cannot responsibly be assigned to Casares as their personal maker. ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/chilean-startup-focuses-on-games-for-social-good/), [La Tercera](https://www.latercera.com/revista-que-pasa/16-1465-9-los-wanakos-despiertan-en-atakama/))
- **date not established:** *MECK, Ltd.* A private investment partnership of which Casares was a founding partner. It resists treatment as a discrete “work”: it was an organizational vehicle through which several partners developed and held enterprises, and the consulted institutional profiles do not establish a founding date. ([Stanford Technology Ventures Program](https://stvp.stanford.edu/people/wences-casares), [MECK](https://www.meckltd.com/Meck_Ltd/Team.html))
- **2007:** *Bling Nation*. A mobile-payment company and platform founded by Casares with partners, including co-CEO Meyer Malka. Its made object was simultaneously a company, payment infrastructure, and proposed consumer practice, rather than a conventional authored work. ([Stanford Technology Ventures Program](https://stvp.stanford.edu/people/wences-casares), [MECK](https://www.meckltd.com/Meck_Ltd/Team.html))
- **2012:** *Lemon Wallet*. A mobile application for digitizing and storing wallet contents, launched publicly in mid-2012 by Lemon, Inc.; Casares was its founder and CEO. LifeLock acquired the company in 2013 and incorporated its technology into LifeLock Wallet, so the obra survives partly as absorbed software rather than an independent continuing product. ([LifeLock SEC acquisition release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1383871/000119312513470740/d643875dex991.htm), [LifeLock investor presentation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1383871/000119312513470740/d643875dex992.htm))
- **2013–:** *Xapo*. A Bitcoin wallet, vault, and custody platform founded by Casares to protect savings held in Bitcoin. Xapo dates its foundation to 2013, whereas Coinbase describes its security operations as active since 2014; the discrepancy appears to distinguish foundation from public operation rather than two separate works. ([Xapo Bank](https://www.xapobank.com/en/company/about-us), [Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/en-sg/blog/coinbase-custody-acquires-xapos-institutional-business-becoming-the-worlds))
- **2020–2022:** *Xapo Bank*. Xapo’s transformation into a Gibraltar-regulated private bank bridging Bitcoin and conventional banking. This is best treated as a second manifestation of the Xapo project, not an entirely independent creation: Xapo’s institutional custody business had been sold to Coinbase in 2019, and the remaining organization pivoted toward retail banking. ([Xapo Bank](https://www.xapobank.com/en/company/about-us), [Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/en-sg/blog/coinbase-custody-acquires-xapos-institutional-business-becoming-the-worlds))

### Civic project

- **date not established:** *Fundación Síntesis*. A nonprofit project established by Casares to encourage a new generation of Latin American social and political leaders. The available institutional biographies document its existence and intention but supply neither a secure founding date nor enough evidence to delimit its individual programs as separate works. ([Stanford Technology Ventures Program](https://stvp.stanford.edu/people/wences-casares), [MECK](https://www.meckltd.com/Meck_Ltd/Team.html))

### Essays and protocols of ownership

- **2014:** *What Is Bitcoin?* An explanatory web essay introducing Bitcoin as a form of digital money. It is a text, but also an act of advocacy intended to make a novel monetary system intelligible; the surviving bibliographic index dates it 21 November 2014. ([The Case for Bitcoin Library](https://casebitcoin.com/what-is-bitcoin))
- **2017:** *Why Own Bitcoin*. A short prescriptive essay combining a case for Bitcoin ownership with rules for limiting exposure. Because it tells readers both why and how cautiously to act, it functions partly as manifesto and partly as investment protocol. ([The Case for Bitcoin Library](https://casebitcoin.com/why-own-bitcoin), [Endeavor Catalyst](https://medium.com/endeavor-catalyst/endeavor-catalysts-21-predictions-for-2021-c6ab4eb41ccf))
- **2019:** *The Case for a Small Allocation to Bitcoin*. An extended investment essay arguing for a limited Bitcoin allocation while explicitly treating Bitcoin as an experiment capable of failure. Sources disagree on the publication date—1 March 2019 in bibliographic mirrors and 11 April on one surviving republication—so the year is secure but the precise original posting date is not. ([The Case for Bitcoin Library](https://casebitcoin.com/the-case-for-a-small-allocation-to-bitcoin), [WORDS Bitcoin Journal mirror](https://bitcoinwords.github.io/the-case-for-a-small-allocation-to-bitcoin))
