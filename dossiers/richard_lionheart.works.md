## Works by Richard I of England

### Surviving lyric compositions

- **1193:** *Ja nus hons pris ne diroit sa raison* (also transmitted as *Ja nus hons pris ne dira sa raison*; BdT 420.2; Linker 241.2). A captivity song—sometimes classified as a *rotrouenge*, though that genre-label is disputed—made to lament Richard’s imprisonment and press his territories and associates to raise his ransom. It survives in markedly variant French and Occitanized manuscript forms and with melodies; scholarship identifies the original language as western French, but the surviving music cannot securely be claimed as Richard’s own composition. ([Arlima repertory](https://www.arlima.net/qt/richard_coeur_de_lion/poemes_lyriques.html); [Milonia’s critical edition](https://hdl.handle.net/11573/1016639); [Harvey 2022, pp. 110–11](https://www.lt.unina.it/Harvey-2022S.pdf))

- **Early summer–September 1197:** *Daufin, je·us voill deresnier* (also *Dalfin, je’us voill deresnier*; BdT 420.1; Linker 241.1). A political *sirventes* addressed to Dalfi d’Alvernhe and Gui II: both lyric obra and manifesto-like intervention, since its accusations and challenges attempt to alter its recipients’ conduct. It is a contrafactum using the form and melody of a song by Peire Vidal, so Richard’s securely attributable making is principally the new verbal composition; older datings range from 1194 to before 1199, while the historical allusions in Dalfi’s reply support the narrower 1197 date. ([Arlima repertory](https://www.arlima.net/qt/richard_coeur_de_lion/poemes_lyriques.html); [Harvey 2022, especially pp. 111–15](https://www.lt.unina.it/Harvey-2022S.pdf))

### Attested but lost composition

- **1192:** Untitled *sirventes* answering Henry of Burgundy. Ambroise’s *Estoire de la Guerre Sainte* reports that, after Henry circulated a hostile song when Richard declined to advance on Jerusalem from Ramla, Richard answered by making a *sirventes*; no text or title survives. Its status is therefore an attested compositional act rather than an extant work: even its precise form and contents are recoverable only from the chronicler’s description. ([Harvey 2022, p. 111 n. 4](https://www.lt.unina.it/Harvey-2022S.pdf))
