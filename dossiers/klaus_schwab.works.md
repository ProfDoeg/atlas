## Works by Klaus Martin Schwab

### Dissertations and early economic studies

- **1965:** *Der längerfristige Exportkredit als betriebswirtschaftliches Problem des Maschinenbaues (untersucht am Beispiel der Bundesrepublik Deutschland).* An engineering-economics dissertation submitted at ETH Zürich and subsequently issued as a monograph; it studies long-term export credit in the German mechanical-engineering industry. Schwab’s official bibliography treats the dissertation and published monograph as one work, not two editions of distinct works ([Schwab curriculum vitae](https://schwabacademy.org/files/ProfessorKlausSchwab.pdf)).

- **1966:** *Der Exportkredit: Hinweise für den deutschen Exporteur.* A practical guide for German exporters. It appears in the standard bibliographical list of Schwab’s early German publications, though his later official CV does not enumerate it individually ([German biographical bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Schwab#Ver%C3%B6ffentlichungen)).

- **1966:** *Öffentliche Investitionen und wirtschaftliches Wachstum.* Schwab’s economics dissertation at the University of Fribourg, published in Ravensburg; it is simultaneously an academic thesis and a monographic economic study ([bibliographic record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Offentliche-Investitionen-und-wirtschaftliches-Wachstum/oclc/30115614)).

- **1971:** *Moderne Unternehmensführung im Maschinenbau.* A management study coauthored with Hein Kroos. It is also the first sustained textual formulation of Schwab’s stakeholder model of the enterprise, but priority claims for “stakeholder theory” more generally would overstate what this particular text made ([digitized WEF copy](https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_KSC_CompanyStrategy_Presentation_2014_DE.pdf); [publication record](https://books.google.se/books/about/Moderne_Unternehmensf%C3%BChrung_im_Maschine.html?id=iSTQygAACAAJ)).

- **1976:** *Chancenmanagement.* A short management monograph about governing through opportunities and change rather than merely reacting to crises ([bibliographic record](https://search.worldcat.org/de/title/2877344); [physical description](https://www.finna.fi/Record/vaari.1755474)).

### Books, edited collections, and extended arguments

- **1995:** *Overcoming Indifference: Ten Key Challenges in Today’s Changing World.* An edited collection, not a solely authored treatise: Schwab selected and organized contributions by political, scientific, business, and intellectual figures around ten global problems ([NYU Press](https://nyupress.org/9780814780367/overcoming-indifference/); [WEF institutional history](https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_First40Years_Book_2010.pdf)).

- **2001–2002:** *The Global Information Technology Report 2001–2002: Readiness for the Networked World.* A collaborative benchmark report credited to Geoffrey S. Kirkman, Peter K. Cornelius, Jeffrey D. Sachs, and Klaus Schwab. It is an institutional research object—part statistical instrument, part policy argument—rather than an ordinary single-author book.

- **2016:** *The Fourth Industrial Revolution.* A programmatic book naming and interpreting the convergence of digital, physical, and biological technologies. It functions partly as diagnosis and partly as a manifesto for institutional action, although Schwab did not originate every technology or every prior use of the phrase “fourth industrial revolution” ([Schwab Academy bibliography](https://schwabacademy.org/); [official CV](https://schwabacademy.org/files/ProfessorKlausSchwab.pdf)).

- **2018:** *Shaping the Future of the Fourth Industrial Revolution: A Guide to Building a Better World.* A practical and normative sequel coauthored with Nicholas Davis and drawing on contributions from approximately 200 specialists; its hybrid construction makes it closer to an orchestrated policy field guide than an autonomous single-author work ([Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/598250/shaping-the-future-of-the-fourth-industrial-revolution-by-klaus-schwab-founder-and-executive-chairman-world-economic-forum-with-nicholas-davis/audio/)).

- **2020:** *COVID-19: The Great Reset.* A rapidly produced intervention coauthored with Thierry Malleret. It interprets the pandemic as an occasion for economic and institutional transformation; it is an argumentative book and call to action, not evidence that its authors created or controlled the pandemic ([WEF account of the subsequent series](https://www.weforum.org/press/2022/01/klaus-schwab-releases-the-great-narrative-as-sequel-to-the-great-reset/)).

- **2021:** *Stakeholder Capitalism: A Global Economy that Works for Progress, People and Planet.* A book coauthored with Peter Vanham that consolidates Schwab’s decades-long stakeholder project into a comparative economic argument and policy program ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/media/klaus-schwab-releases-stakeholder-capitalism-making-the-case-for-a-global-economy-that-works-for-progress-people-and-planet); [Wiley](https://www.wiley-vch.de/de/fachgebiete/finanzen-wirtschaft-recht/stakeholder-capitalism-978-1-119-75613-2)).

- **2022:** *The Great Narrative: For a Better Future.* A second collaborative book with Thierry Malleret, derived partly from conversations with other thinkers. The publisher calls it the second installment of the “Great Reset” series and a call to action, so its identity lies between authored synthesis, curated conversation, and manifesto ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/press/2022/01/klaus-schwab-releases-the-great-narrative-as-sequel-to-the-great-reset/)).

### The Intelligent Age series

- **2025:** *Thriving and Leading in the Intelligent Age: Mastering Change with Purpose.* The foundational volume of a five-book series and a practical guide to personal adaptation and leadership amid artificial intelligence and related technological change ([Schwab Academy](https://schwabacademy.org/books)).

- **2025:** *Longevity and Retirement in the Intelligent Age: Opening New Horizons in Later Life.* The second series volume, combining longevity, work, lifelong learning, and retirement into a practical social framework. Bibliographic records disagree over the exact 2025 release date and identify more than one ISBN or physical issue, but these are manifestations of the same work ([Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Longevity_and_Retirement_in_the_Intellig.html?id=CC2cEQAAQBAJ); [Schwab Academy series](https://schwabacademy.org/books)).

- **2026:** *Restoring Truth and Trust: An Agenda for the Intelligent Age.* A governance-oriented volume that acts as an agenda or manifesto for rebuilding institutional legitimacy rather than merely describing declining trust ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=GPatEQAAQBAJ); [Schwab Academy series](https://schwabacademy.org/books)).

- **2026:** *Universities, Professors, and Students in the Intelligent Age: A Call for Systems Change.* A higher-education reform book whose subtitle identifies it explicitly as an intervention and call to action ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Universities_Professors_and_Students_in.html?id=BSjKEQAAQBAJ); [Schwab Academy series](https://schwabacademy.org/books)).

- **2026:** *The Competitive Enterprise in the Intelligent Age: Mastering Strategy, Speed, and Survival.* A practical corporate-leadership playbook issued as the fifth volume in the series ([Schwab Academy](https://schwabacademy.org/books/the-competitive-enterprise-in-the-intelligent-age-mastering-strategy-speed-and-survival)).

- **2025–2026:** *The Intelligent Age Series.* A declared five-volume body of work as well as five separable books. The series is therefore an obra at a larger scale: its publisher asks that the volumes be read as a single framework viewed through different institutional domains ([Schwab Academy](https://schwabacademy.org/books)).

### Reports and serial research objects

- **1979–:** *The Global Competitiveness Report.* An annual or periodically reconfigured benchmark project credited by Schwab’s official CV to him “since 1979.” Individual volumes were produced with changing coauthors, editors, economists, and institutional teams, so the serial report is properly a collaborative institutional obra rather than dozens of solely authored Schwab books ([official CV](https://schwabacademy.org/files/ProfessorKlausSchwab.pdf)).

- **2001–:** *The Global Information Technology Report.* A recurring comparative measurement project launched through the World Economic Forum and partner institutions. Schwab participated in and was credited on its inaugural volume, but later installments have changing editorial responsibility; the series resists attribution to him as an individual work.

- **Numerous dates:** *Articles, forewords, speeches, and contributions.* This corpus resists a complete work-by-work entry: Schwab’s official CV acknowledges “numerous articles” but supplies no collected-works list, authoritative article bibliography, or completeness statement. Consequently, treating database search results as a catalogue raisonné would manufacture completeness where none exists ([official CV](https://schwabacademy.org/files/ProfessorKlausSchwab.pdf)).

### Manifestos and conceptual protocols

- **1973:** *Davos Manifesto: A Code of Ethics for Business Leaders.* A protocol-like declaration setting out corporate responsibilities to multiple stakeholders. It was issued and signed in the collective setting of the European Management Forum; Schwab convened and promoted it, but the available institutional source does not justify treating every word as his sole authorship ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/focus/the-davos-manifesto/)).

- **2019:** *Davos Manifesto 2020: The Universal Purpose of a Company in the Fourth Industrial Revolution.* A revised corporate manifesto unveiled by Schwab for the Forum’s fiftieth-anniversary meeting. It acts by prescribing duties and institutional norms, but its official status is that of a World Economic Forum declaration, not an uncontested personal text ([World Economic Forum historical page](https://www.weforum.org/focus/the-davos-manifesto/); [contemporary publication](https://time.com/5741935/world-economic-forum-davos-manifesto/)).

- **1971–:** *Stakeholder capitalism.* A conceptual project asserting that an enterprise has responsibilities to employees, customers, suppliers, communities, governments, and shareholders. It has no single stable textual boundary: it appears in the 1971 Kroos–Schwab book, the Davos manifestos, institutional practices, articles, and the 2021 book, making it a continuing project rather than one discrete work ([World Economic Forum profile](https://www.weforum.org/about/klaus-schwab/)).

- **2015–:** *The Fourth Industrial Revolution.* Beyond the 2016 book, this is a conceptual and institutional project joining technological interpretation to programs of governance. The expression and many component ideas predate Schwab’s deployment of them, so “made by Schwab” applies to his particular synthesis and promotion, not to the historical transformation itself ([World Economic Forum profile](https://www.weforum.org/about/klaus-schwab/)).

- **2020–2022:** *The Great Reset.* A public program linking post-pandemic recovery to sustainability, resilience, and stakeholder governance, manifested in a launch event, institutional initiatives, and two Schwab–Malleret books. Because it is simultaneously slogan, Forum program, and coauthored textual series, it resists classification as either one book or Schwab’s exclusively personal creation ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/press/2022/01/klaus-schwab-releases-the-great-narrative-as-sequel-to-the-great-reset/)).

### Institutions, communities, and convening projects

- **1971:** *European Management Symposium.* An event-project organized in Davos by Klaus Schwab with assistance from Hilde Schwab and institutional supporters. Planned initially as a symposium, it became the recurring convening from which the European Management Forum and ultimately the World Economic Forum developed ([WEF institutional history](https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_First40Years_Book_2010.pdf)).

- **1971–2025:** *World Economic Forum.* An institution founded by Schwab, initially as the European Management Forum, and developed under his leadership as a permanent platform for cooperation among business, government, academia, and civil society. It is an organizational obra made by many people over decades, not a personally authored object ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/about/klaus-schwab/); [institutional history](https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_A_Partner_in_Shaping_History.pdf)).

- **1998–:** *Schwab Foundation for Social Entrepreneurship.* A foundation co-created by Klaus and Hilde Schwab to identify, connect, and support social innovators. The cofounding attribution matters: it is not Klaus Schwab’s work alone ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/about/klaus-schwab/)).

- **2004–:** *Forum of Young Global Leaders.* A five-year leadership community founded by Schwab using funds from his Dan David Prize. It is an institutional protocol for selecting, connecting, and developing successive cohorts rather than a static organization or text ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/about/klaus-schwab/); [institutional account](https://www.weforum.org/stories/leadership/young-global-leaders-ygl-radio-davos/)).

- **2011–:** *Global Shapers Community.* A worldwide network of locally organized youth hubs founded by Schwab. Its continuing form depends on participants’ self-directed projects, so the network is better understood as a generative platform than as a completed personal work ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/about/klaus-schwab/); [WEF historical account](https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_A_Partner_in_Shaping_History.pdf)).

- **2025–:** *Schwab Academy.* An educational, publishing, and community platform founded after Schwab’s departure from Forum leadership. It accompanies the *Intelligent Age* books with learning and certification programs and thus approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk of books, instruction, digital tools, and organized community rather than remaining merely a publishing imprint ([Schwab Academy](https://schwabacademy.org/)).
