## Works by Chiara di Favarone di Offreduccio

### Letters of spiritual counsel

- **1234:** *First Letter to Agnes of Prague*. A letter of vocational counsel sent after Agnes entered her Prague monastery, probably after 11 June; it is also a performative intervention in Agnes’s struggle to embrace Franciscan poverty. Clare’s authorship is not seriously disputed. ([Epistolae](https://epistolae.ctl.columbia.edu/letter/560.html); [Mueller](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/0-271-02893-9.html))

- **1235–early 1238:** *Second Letter to Agnes of Prague*. A letter of spiritual direction urging Agnes to persist in her chosen form of poverty; the proposed date range is approximate, with some scholarship placing it near 1235. ([Epistolae](https://epistolae.unisi.it/letter/563.html); [Mooney](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.9783/9780812292923-008/html))

- **1237–1238:** *Third Letter to Agnes of Prague*. A contemplative letter and instrument of counsel, conventionally dated to 1238 but sometimes more cautiously placed between April 1237 and April 1238. ([Mueller text and dating](https://ldysinger.com/%40texts2/1225_clare/02_clare-txt1.htm); [Mooney](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.9783/9780812292923-008/html))

- **1253:** *Fourth Letter to Agnes of Prague*. Clare’s final surviving letter to Agnes, composed near the end of Clare’s life; it is at once personal farewell, contemplative instruction, and confirmation of the relationship created through the preceding letters. ([Epistolae](https://epistolae.ctl.columbia.edu/letter/572.html); [Mueller](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/0-271-02893-9.html))

### Protocol and constitutional obra

- **before 9 August 1253:** *Form of Life of the Order of Poor Sisters* (*Forma vitae*, commonly called the *Rule of Clare*). A protocol for communal life and a juridical instrument intended to secure the sisters’ practice of poverty; Pope Innocent IV confirmed it on 9 August 1253. It is also a composite obra, incorporating and revising earlier Franciscan and ecclesiastical materials: its authenticity is generally accepted, but “authorship” here means compilation, adaptation, legislation, and the textual crystallization of a long communal project rather than wholly original composition. ([Mueller, *A Companion to Clare of Assisi*](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004193437/9789004193437_webready_content_text.pdf); [Journal of Ecclesiastical History](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022046907004137); [Epistolae](https://epistolae.ctl.columbia.edu/woman/74.html))

### Manifesto, testament, and blessing

- **probably late 1240s–1253:** *Testament*. A retrospective spiritual testament and manifesto for the sisters’ corporate identity, poverty, and attachment to Francis’s founding intentions. Its date is not fixed, and its authenticity has been repeatedly disputed: many editors accept it as Clare’s, while Werner Maleczek and others have argued for a later origin; the earliest surviving textual witness belongs to a dossier whose compilation may date from 1263–1266. ([Vaiani, survey of the authenticity debate](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/vaianic2.htm); [Società internazionale di Studi francescani](https://www.sisf-assisi.it/elenco-delle-pubblicazioni-della-sisf.php?idC=15); [Mooney](https://dokumen.pub/clare-of-assisi-and-the-thirteenth-century-church-religious-women-rules-and-resistance-9780812292923.html))

- **undated, traditionally placed near 1253:** *Blessing*. A performative text: it does not merely describe a blessing but enacts one over Clare’s sisters and their successors. Its attribution has been disputed, and it survives in divergent recensions addressed respectively to Agnes, Ermentrude, or the sisters collectively; the earliest known witness is a Middle High German version from about 1350, while surviving Latin witnesses are later. It therefore resists identification as one stable authorial text. ([Franciscan Journey, textual-history notes](https://jdpofs.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/TOTAL-Supplemental-Reading-Franciscan-Journey.pdf); [Hahn, discussion of disputed attribution](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10023/20329/Thesis-Michael-Hahn-complete-version.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3))

### Disputed transmitted composition

- **undated; known only through a sixteenth-century witness:** *Letter to Ermentrude of Bruges*. The extant text is traditionally included in Clare’s collected writings, but it is probably a later conflation or juxtaposition of two otherwise lost letters that Clare may genuinely have sent. It consequently resists being called a single surviving work by Clare: the underlying correspondence may be hers, while the present textual object probably is not. ([Epistolae](https://epistolae.ctl.columbia.edu/letter/577.html); [Františkánské prameny](https://prameny.kapucini.cz/list-ermentrude/))
