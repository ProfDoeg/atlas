## Works by Hilde Holger

### Choreographic obras: Vienna

- **1923:** *Bourrée*. Solo choreography to music by Johann Sebastian Bach, presented at Holger’s first solo evening in the Vienna Secession. [LW]
- **1923:** *Eine Seifenblase*. Solo choreography to music by Claude Debussy, presented at the Vienna Secession. [LW]
- **1923:** *Die Forelle*. Solo choreography to music by Franz Schubert, presented at the Vienna Secession; *Forelle* and the separately catalogued English title *Trout* may be duplicate records rather than distinct works. [LW]
- **1923:** *Humoreske*. Solo choreography to music by Max Reger, presented at the Vienna Secession. [LW]
- **1923:** *Le Martyre de Saint Sébastien*. Solo choreography to music by Claude Debussy, presented at the Vienna Secession; the catalogue also records a 1929 work under its English title, whose relation to this version is unclear. [LW]
- **1923:** *Reiter im Sturm*. Solo choreography to music by Siegfried Frederick Nadel, presented at the Vienna Secession. [LW]
- **1923:** *Sarabande*. Solo choreography to music by Johann Sebastian Bach, presented at the Vienna Secession. [LW]
- **1923:** *Vogel als Prophet*. Solo choreography to music by Franz Schubert, presented at the Vienna Secession. [LW]
- **1926:** *Funeral March for a Canary*. Choreographic work to music by Lord Berners. [LW]
- **1926:** *Das mechanische Ballett* (*Mechanical Ballet*). Choreographic obra associated in its later reconstruction with music by Ludwig Hirschfeld-Mack; Holger reconstructed it more than once, so the surviving manifestations are not identical with an untouched 1926 original. [LW; K]
- **1926:** *Wacht auf!* (*Wake Up!*). Political choreography incorporating a movement chorus; it belongs to Holger’s work with lay ensembles for the communist Rote Hilfe. [W; K]
- **1926:** Movement choruses for Rote Hilfe. Several ensemble dances with nonprofessional participants are documented collectively but not individually titled; they resist enumeration as discrete, recoverable works. [K]
- **1927:** *Four Pictures from the Time of the Paris Commune*. Political choreographic cycle; the English title is supplied by the scholarly account, while detailed constituent titles are not given. [W]
- **1929:** *Chaconne & Variations*. Choreography to music by George Frideric Handel. [LW]
- **1929:** *Englischer Schäfertanz*. Choreography to music by Percy Grainger. [LW]
- **1929:** *Hebräischer Tanz*. Jewish-themed solo choreography to music by Alexander Veprik. [LW; W]
- **1929:** *Lebenswende*. Choreography to music by Karel Boleslav Jirák. [LW]
- **1929:** *Marsch*. Choreography to music by Sergei Prokofiev. [LW]
- **1929:** *The Martyrdom of Saint Sebastian*. Choreography to music by Debussy; the standard list does not establish whether this is a revision or merely a duplicate English record of the 1923 *Le Martyre de Saint Sébastien*. [LW]
- **1929:** *Mutter Erde*. Choreography to music by Heinz Graupner. [LW]
- **1929:** *Sarabande und Bourrée*. Choreography to music by Bach; its relationship to the two 1923 solos is not specified. [LW]
- **1929:** *Tanz nach rumänischen Motiven*. Choreography to music by Béla Bartók; the published list’s spelling, *Rumaischene*, appears corrupt. [LW]
- **1931:** *Ikarus*. Solo choreography to music by Heinz Graupner; reconstructed for Thomas Kampe in 1991 through Holger’s memory, improvisational tasks, and Bodenwieser-derived movement material. [K]
- **1931:** *Javanische Impression*. Choreography to music by Heinz Graupner. [LW]
- **1933:** *Die Orchidee* (*The Orchid*). Group choreography inspired by nature, documented in photographs and costume designs; some lists date its later London manifestation to 1954, making the latter either a revival or reworking. [K; LW]
- **1933:** *Kabbalistischer Tanz*. Jewish-themed choreography to music attributed in the list to Vittorio Rieti. [LW; W]
- **1933:** *Die Stadt*. Choreographic or dance-film manifestation associated with composer Marcel Rubin; the surviving filmography does not clearly identify the production form or Holger’s exact credited role. [LW]
- **1936:** *Ahasver*. Jewish-themed choreography to music by Marcel Rubin, presented at the Volkshochschule in Vienna. [LW; K]
- **1936:** *Barbarasong*. Choreography to music by Kurt Weill, presented at the Volkshochschule in Vienna. [LW]
- **1936:** *Engel der Verkündigung*. Choreography to music by Handel, presented at the Volkshochschule in Vienna. [LW]
- **1937:** *Flämischer Bilderbogen*. Choreography after Bruegel, presented at the Volksbildungshaus in Vienna; the catalogue’s *Bilderboden* is likely a transcription error, and the 1988 *Flemish Picture Sheet* may be its reconstruction. [LW]
- **1937:** *Golem*. Jewish-themed solo choreography to music attributed to Wilckens, presented at the Volksbildungshaus; later reconstructed by Holger in 1988 and for Liz Aggiss in 1992. [LW; K]
- **1937:** *Mystischer Kreis*. Choreography to music attributed to Rieti, presented at the Volksbildungshaus. [LW]
- **1937:** *Orientalischer Tanz*. Choreography to music by Heinz Graupner, presented at the Volksbildungshaus. [LW]
- **1937:** *Passacaglia*. Choreography to music by Handel, presented at the Volksbildungshaus. [LW]
- **1937:** *Tango*. Choreography to music by Ralph Benatzky, presented at the Volksbildungshaus; it is a dance work, not a literary text or musical composition by Holger. [LW]
- **1938–1939:** Untitled clandestine dances and a new solo programme. Holger continued teaching and staging private performances after the Nazi prohibition on her public work and prepared a programme for emigration, but the sources do not supply a recoverable list of titles; these manifestations resist treatment as a fixed oeuvre. [K]

### Reconstructions and composite manifestations

- **1979:** *Inspirations*. Film of reconstructed Holger dances commissioned in connection with the Austrian Theatre Museum’s *Dance of the Twentieth Century* exhibition and made with Marilyn Halford and the Arts Council of Great Britain; it is a recorded manifestation of choreography, not wholly a new dance obra or a film solely authored by Holger. [A; LW]
- **1992:** *Vier Tänze*. Reconstruction programme comprising *Die Forelle*, *Le Martyre de Saint Sébastien*, *Mechanisches Ballett*, and *Golem*, transmitted by Holger to Liz Aggiss and accompanied by Billy Cowie; it simultaneously preserves and alters the earlier solos. [K]
- **1996:** Holger’s ninetieth-birthday programme. A Southbank Centre event of group dances, talks, and workshops directed by Holger; it is better classified as a pedagogical-performance manifestation than as one titled obra. [K]
- **1996:** *Hilde*. An Amici Dance Theatre work directed by Wolfgang Stange in which Holger appeared as herself; it concerns and includes her but is not properly attributable to her as sole maker. [K]

### Pedagogical and social projects

- **1926–1938:** *Neue Schule für Bewegungskunst*. Holger’s Vienna school, company, and children’s group constituted a sustained artistic-pedagogical project in which training, improvisation, and choreography were inseparable. [D; K]
- **1940s:** *School of Art for Modern Movement* / *Hilde Holger Dance Studio*. Holger’s Bombay school was conceived as both professional institution and intimate performance space; its shifting recorded names make it a project rather than a single stable object. [K; D]
- **From 1948:** *Hilde Holger School of Contemporary Dance*. Holger’s London school and, later, Camden basement studio were long-duration pedagogical obras whose classes continually generated improvisation and choreography. [D; K]
- **From 1948:** *Hilde Holger Modern Ballet Group*. London performance collective that staged work in churches, parks, public spaces, and theatres; the group is a continuing project rather than a single choreography. [K]
- **1950s–2001:** Holger’s movement-training system. A developing protocol of technical preparation, spatial work, circular group organization, improvisation, and collective composition; it survives incompletely through pupils and exercises and therefore resists the status of a fixed, authored text. [K]
- **1960s onward:** Integrated dance and dance-movement therapy practice. Prompted partly by creative work with her son Darius, Holger developed an inclusive method for dancers with and without disabilities; this is a body of practice and social project, not one bounded work. [D; K]
- **Postwar years:** Lecture-demonstrations for the Workers’ Educational Association. A recurring hybrid of teaching, speech, and performance intended to create an audience for modern dance; individual titles and dates are not comprehensively documented. [K]

### Writings, lectures, and working texts

- **8 November 1939:** *Dance in Europe*. Unpublished lecture delivered in Bombay and preserved in the Hilde Holger Archive; it is both an expository text and a manifesto-like statement of modern dance’s relation to the other arts. [K]
- **1940s:** Articles on Western modern dance. Holger published several pieces in Bombay on its history, aesthetics, and educational value, but the consulted scholarly account does not provide a complete title-level bibliography; this body of writing cannot responsibly be expanded into invented entries. [K]
- **1947:** Untitled article in the Bombay *Evening News*. An essay presenting modern dance as an integration of body, intellect, emotion, and education; the scholarly source identifies the newspaper and year but not a fixed title. [K]
- **After 1948, undated:** *Dancing in India*. Unpublished manuscript in the Hilde Holger Archive reflecting on performance and dance study in India; its date remains approximate and must not be regularized. [K]
- **Undated:** Lecture notes and manuscripts. The archive records a larger body of Holger’s lecture notes and original manuscripts, but without a public item-level scholarly inventory they resist honest enumeration as separately titled works. [A]

### Designs, costumes, and visual works

- **Dates not comprehensively catalogued:** Stage costumes, masks, costume designs, percussion instruments, props, and a large painted backdrop. The archive preserves these performance objects, but its public description neither attributes every item exclusively to Holger nor supplies a catalogue raisonné; collaborators including Erni Kniepert, Magda Nachman, Liz Pisk, Kate Berl, and Hedi Schick designed costumes for her productions. [A; D]
- **Dates not comprehensively catalogued:** Works on paper and paintings. The archive identifies artworks by Holger as a class but does not publish a sufficient item-level catalogue of titles, dates, and attributions; treating the class as one work would be misleading, while inventing individual entries would be worse. [A]
