## Works by Émile Jaques-Dalcroze

### Pedagogical project and institutions

- **1892–1910:** *Rhythmique / méthode Jaques-Dalcroze*. A continually revised pedagogical project joining bodily movement, ear training, improvisation, and musical rhythm; it resists classification as a single work because it survives as practice, protocol, repertoire, and institution rather than one fixed text. [IJD; Bablet]
- **1910–1914:** *Bildungsanstalt Jaques-Dalcroze, Hellerau*. A school and experimental performance project established with Wolf and Harald Dohrn; its teaching, festivals, and collaboration with Adolphe Appia made it a genuine Gesamtkunstwerk, although Jaques-Dalcroze did not design its building. [IJD; Fonds]
- **1915 onward:** *Institut Jaques-Dalcroze, Geneva*. An institution founded to continue and transmit the method; it is better understood as an enduring collective project than as an individually authored obra. [IJD]

### Stage works, festivals, and musico-scenic creations

- **1883:** *La Soubrette*. Opéra-comique to a libretto by Philippe Monnier. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1883:** *Riquet à la Houppe*. Operetta to a libretto by Y. Plessis. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1888:** *Par les bois*, op. 6. Dramatic piece in three tableaux, with text by Philippe Monnier. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1890:** *L’Écolier*. Stage piece in two acts, with text by Philippe Monnier. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1892–1893:** *La Veillée*, op. 36. Secular cantata or “suite lyrique” for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, on words by Jaques-Dalcroze and Jeanne Thoiry; sources differ between the composition/publication date 1892 and first performance in 1893. [Dénes/Tchamkerten; Brice]
- **1893:** *Le Violon maudit*. Opéra-comique with a libretto by A. Puthier and Jaques-Dalcroze. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1894:** *Janie*. “Idylle musicale” in three acts, with text by Philippe Godet. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1896:** *Poème alpestre*. Large festival work in three acts for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, with text by Daniel Baud-Bovy; it was commissioned for the Swiss National Exhibition and combines civic spectacle, poetry, music, and mass performance. [Brice; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1895–1899:** *Sancho Pança*. Comédie lyrique to a libretto by Y. Plessis; the overture is dated 1895, catalogues variously date the full work 1897 or associate it with its 1899 Strasbourg production. [BnF; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1898:** *Respect pour nous*. Operetta with a libretto by Jaques-Dalcroze. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1900:** *Le Jeu du feuillu*, op. 43. Spring festival or “suite de chansons de mai” for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, with text by Jaques-Dalcroze; it is both a score and a communal spectacle. [BnF; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1903:** *Festival vaudois*, op. 55. Civic festival music for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, written for the centenary of Vaud’s entry into the Swiss Confederation; Jaques-Dalcroze also supplied the text. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1906:** *Le Bonhomme jadis*. Opéra-comique to a libretto by Franc-Nohain. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1908:** *Les Jumeaux de Bergame*. Arlequinade or opera to a libretto by Maurice Léna. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1912:** *Écho et Narcisse*. Festival work for soloists, chorus, orchestra, and rhythmic movement, with text by Jacques Chenevière; its synthesis of staged bodies and music makes it a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a cantata. [Dénes/Tchamkerten; Fonds]
- **1914:** *La Fête de juin*. Patriotic stage festival in four acts for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, with texts by Daniel Baud-Bovy and A. Malche. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1918:** *Les Premiers Souvenirs*. Festival music for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, with texts by Jacques Chenevière and Jaques-Dalcroze. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1920:** *Les Belles Vacances*. Festival music for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, with text by Pierre Girard. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1923:** *La Fête de la jeunesse et de la joie*. Festival work for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, with texts by Jacques Chenevière, Jaques-Dalcroze, and Pierre Girard. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1928:** *Notre Pays*. Festival music for soloists, chorus, and orchestra; the consulted work-list supplies no librettist. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1932:** *Le Petit Roi qui pleure*. Musical tale in three acts to Jaques-Dalcroze’s own libretto; composed or staged in 1932 but published posthumously in 1954, so composition and manifestation dates must not be conflated. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1934:** *Le Joli Jeu des saisons*. Festival music for soloists, chorus, and orchestra, with text by Jaques-Dalcroze. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]

### Musical and bodily protocols

- **1907:** *La Respiration et l’innervation musculaire*. Supplement to the method addressing breath and muscular control; simultaneously an essay and an instructional protocol. [Bablet; Method]
- **1913:** *Douze petites études mélodiques et rythmiques*. Piano studies designed as pedagogical exercises rather than autonomous concert pieces. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1913:** *Seize études plastiques*. Piano studies intended to generate or accompany expressive bodily movement; score and movement protocol are inseparable. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1918:** *La Rythmique appliquée à l’étude du piano*. Instructional method connecting rhythmic training to piano study. [Bablet; Method]
- **1920:** *Twenty Caprices and Rhythmic Studies*. Two sets of piano studies that function as exercises and compositions. [Dénes/Tchamkerten; IJD]
- **1923:** *Cinquante études-miniatures de métrique et rythmique*. Piano studies combining pedagogical protocol with miniature composition. [Dénes/Tchamkerten; Tchamkerten, Piano IV]
- **Undated:** *Dix chants polyphoniques sans paroles pour études plastiques*. Wordless vocal studies serving bodily-plastic exercises. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Trois vocalises*. Vocal exercises. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Six exercices pratiques d’intonation*. Ear-training protocol. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Six jeux rythmiques pour enfants et adolescents*. Piano-based rhythmic games; not merely pieces but instructions for participatory action. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Six petites pièces en rythmes alternés*. Piano studies in changing rhythms. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Esquisses rythmiques*. Piano exercises or studies. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Exercices de désordination*. Piano exercises deliberately separating or recombining bodily actions; the work is primarily a coordination protocol. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *La Jolie Musique*. Singing games and exercises for very young children. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Marches rythmiques*. Songs with piano for rhythmic walking; later listings distinguish accompanied and unaccompanied sets of eighty-four marches. [Dénes/Tchamkerten; Method]
- **Undated:** *Métrique et rythmique*. Two hundred piano studies. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Moderne Tonleiterschule*. Scale method made with R. Ruynemann; the collaboration prevents unqualified sole attribution. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Petites pièces de piano avec instruments à percussion*. Ensemble teaching pieces whose sounding result depends on participatory percussion. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Rythmes de chant et de danse*. Vocal-and-piano exercises linking song and movement. [Dénes/Tchamkerten]

### Method volumes, essays, memoirs, and other writings

- **1894:** *La Bonne Chanson*. Article published in the *Gazette musicale de la Suisse romande*; an aesthetic intervention rather than a musical score. [Bablet; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1903–1918:** *Méthode Jaques-Dalcroze pour le développement de l’instinct rythmique, du sens auditif et du sentiment tonal*. An evolving method in five parts, eight volumes, and supplements; publication dates and component titles vary across manifestations, so it cannot responsibly be reduced to one fixed-edition “book.” [Bablet; Method]
- **1903–1918:** *Gymnastique rythmique / La Rythmique*. First part of the method, issued in two volumes and revised or republished; a protocol for learning music through bodily action. [Bablet; Method]
- **1905:** *Vorschläge zur Reform des musikalischen Schulunterrichts*. Reform proposal for school music teaching; its prescriptive force makes it close to a manifesto. [Bablet; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Undated:** *Étude de la portée musicale / La Portée musicale*. Second part of the method, devoted to staff notation. [Bablet; Method]
- **1907:** *Les Gammes et les tonalités, le phrasé et les nuances*. Third part of the method, issued in three volumes; an instructional protocol joining tonal hearing and expression. [Bablet; Method]
- **Undated:** *Les Intervalles et les accords*. Fourth part of the method. [Bablet; Method]
- **Undated:** *L’Improvisation et l’accompagnement au piano*. Fifth part of the method; a protocol for practices that cannot be fully contained in a printed text. [Bablet; Method]
- **Undated:** *La Plastique animée*. Method volume on expressive movement, also known through later manifestations as the sixth part of the method; it treats the moving body as a musical medium. [Bablet; Method]
- **1912:** *The Eurhythmics of Jaques-Dalcroze*. English-language presentation compiled from Jaques-Dalcroze’s ideas and writings; its status as translation/collection rather than a wholly new French original should be retained. [Bablet; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1920:** *Le Rythme, la musique et l’éducation*. Collection of essays and lectures articulating the pedagogical project; revised in 1935 and translated, so those later forms are manifestations rather than separate obras. [Bablet; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1942:** *Souvenirs, notes et critiques*. Collection of memoirs, observations, and criticism. [Bablet; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1945:** *La Musique et nous: Notes de notre double vie*. Reflective prose on music and lived experience. [Bablet; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **1948:** *Notes bariolées*. Late collection of miscellaneous reflections. [Bablet; Dénes/Tchamkerten]
- **Dates not fully enumerated:** *Articles, lectures, radio texts, course notes, interviews, and unpublished writings*. The archival fonds and Misolette Bablet’s catalogue establish a much larger textual corpus, but many items lack stable independent-work status: they may be drafts, lecture manifestations, broadcasts, or documentary residues. Listing invented titles or treating every archival leaf as an obra would falsify that resistance. [Fonds; Bablet]
