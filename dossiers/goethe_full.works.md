## Works by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe

### Dramatic works, libretti, theatrical projects, and performance texts

- **1765–1767:** *Die Laune des Verliebten*. Pastoral comedy in verse; Goethe repeatedly revised this early dramatic work.
- **1768–1769; revised 1783:** *Die Mitschuldigen*. Comedy surviving in substantially different versions; no single text exhausts the work.
- **c. 1769:** *Concerto dramatico*. Early dramatic fragment.
- **1771; printed 1773:** *Geschichte Gottfriedens von Berlichingen mit der eisernen Hand*. First prose conception of the drama later published as *Götz von Berlichingen*.
- **1773:** *Götz von Berlichingen mit der eisernen Hand*. Historical drama; Goethe later made a separate stage adaptation, so the printed drama and performance version are distinct objects.
- **1773:** *Jahrmarktsfest zu Plundersweilern*. Carnival play or satirical fairground performance, later revised.
- **1773:** *Satyros oder Der vergötterte Waldteufel*. Satirical drama.
- **1773:** *Götter, Helden und Wieland*. Dramatic farce directed against Wieland’s treatment of classical mythology.
- **1773:** *Prometheus*. Dramatic fragment; distinct from the lyric customarily known by the same title.
- **1773:** *Mahomet*. Fragmentary drama rather than a completed tragedy.
- **1773:** *Hanswursts Hochzeit*. Coarse comic project surviving as a dramatic fragment.
- **c. 1773–1774:** *Der ewige Jude*. Fragmentary satirical-poetic project.
- **1774:** *Clavigo*. Five-act tragedy.
- **1774:** *Götz von Berlichingen* (stage version). Theatrical recasting of the 1773 drama, not merely a later printing.
- **1774–1775:** *Faust* (the early complex conventionally called the *Urfaust*). A manuscript constellation reconstructed editorially; “Urfaust” was not Goethe’s title and resists treatment as a single authorized work.
- **1775:** *Erwin und Elmire*. Singspiel libretto, subsequently remade in a second version.
- **1775:** *Claudine von Villa Bella*. Singspiel, later thoroughly revised.
- **1775:** *Stella. Ein Schauspiel für Liebende*. Drama first ending in a three-person union; Goethe replaced that ending with a tragic one in the later stage version.
- **1775:** *Prolog zu den neuesten Offenbarungen Gottes*. Theatrical prologue.
- **1776:** *Die Geschwister*. One-act domestic drama.
- **1776–1777:** *Lila*. Festival play or singspiel-like theatrical entertainment, existing in several stages of revision.
- **1777:** *Der Triumph der Empfindsamkeit*. Satirical festival play with music and spectacle.
- **1777–1778:** *Das Jahrmarktsfest zu Plundersweilern* (revised version). A new performance form of the earlier fairground play.
- **1778:** *Jery und Bätely*. Singspiel libretto.
- **1778:** *Die Fischerin*. Singspiel and open-air performance work incorporating lyric and music.
- **1779; prose version, 1787 verse version:** *Iphigenie auf Tauris*. Drama whose prose and verse forms are materially distinct manifestations.
- **1779–1788:** *Egmont*. Tragedy with songs and incidental music; its long genesis and theatrical-musical form resist classification as only a written drama.
- **1779–1783:** *Elpenor*. Unfinished tragedy.
- **1780–1781:** *Die Vögel*. Fragmentary adaptation of Aristophanes.
- **1780–1789; printed 1790:** *Torquato Tasso*. Verse drama.
- **1781:** *Das göttliche Kind*. Festival or occasional dramatic text.
- **1781:** *Die Wahl des Herkules*. Festival play.
- **1782–1783:** *Scherz, List und Rache*. Comic opera libretto.
- **1783:** *Die Mitschuldigen* (final revision). Goethe’s mature recasting of his early comedy.
- **1783:** *Erwin und Elmire* (second version). Revised singspiel rather than a simple reissue.
- **1784:** *Der Zauberflöte zweiter Teil*. Unfinished projected sequel to Mozart and Schikaneder’s opera; a project whose incompletion is part of its identity.
- **1784–1785:** *Die ungleichen Hausgenossen*. Operatic or dramatic fragment.
- **1785–1786:** *Nausikaa*. Tragic project preserved in plans and fragments; the intention, not a finished play, constitutes the work.
- **1786–1788:** *Claudine von Villa Bella* (second version). Extensive revision of the earlier singspiel.
- **1787–1788:** *Die vereitelten Ränke*. Operatic project surviving principally through its songs.
- **1788–1790; printed 1790:** *Faust. Ein Fragment*. Goethe’s first authorized printed manifestation of the *Faust* project.
- **1791:** *Der Groß-Cophta*. Comedy responding to the affair of the Diamond Necklace.
- **1791–1793:** *Die Aufgeregten*. Unfinished political drama about revolutionary unrest.
- **1792:** *Der Bürgergeneral*. Political comedy.
- **1793:** *Das Mädchen von Oberkirch*. Unfinished dramatic project.
- **1797:** *Die natürliche Tochter* project. Planned tragic trilogy prompted by the French Revolution; only its first drama was completed.
- **1797–1800:** *Paläophron und Neoterpe*. Festival play staging a dispute between old and new art.
- **1798:** *Zu Wallensteins Lager*. Theatrical prologue associated with Schiller’s drama.
- **1798:** *Was wir bringen*. Festival prelude made for the reopening of the Weimar theatre.
- **1799:** *Mahomet* (adaptation of Voltaire). Dramatic translation and theatrical refashioning.
- **1799–1800:** *Tancred* (adaptation of Voltaire). Dramatic translation made for performance.
- **1800:** *Epilog zu Schillers Glocke*. Occasional performance poem that functions as theatrical commemoration.
- **1802:** *Die natürliche Tochter*. Tragedy and the sole completed part of the projected trilogy.
- **1804–1805:** *Die theatralischen Abenteuer*. Adaptation project after a French theatrical source.
- **1805–1806:** *Rameaus Neffe*. Translation of Diderot’s dialogic satire; simultaneously translation, dramatic dialogue, and critical intervention.
- **1806:** *Nachspiel zu Ifflands Hagestolzen*. Theatrical afterpiece.
- **1807–1808:** *Pandora*. Dramatic festival poem left formally incomplete.
- **1808:** *Faust. Der Tragödie erster Teil*. Authorized first part of the mature tragedy; it incorporates and transforms material from the preceding *Faust* manifestations.
- **1810:** *Des Epimenides Erwachen*. Patriotic festival play.
- **1814–1815:** *Der Schutzgeist*. Festival-play project surviving incompletely.
- **1816:** *Proserpina*. Dramatic monodrama, reshaped from earlier material and intended for performance with music.
- **1817:** *Finale zu Johann von Paris*. Supplementary performance text for Boieldieu’s opera.
- **1825:** *Die Bestohlenen*. Adaptation of a French comedy.
- **c. 1826:** *Feradeddin und Kolaila*. Dramatic fragment.
- **1827:** *Epilog zu Schillers Glocke* (late performance version). A renewed commemorative staging rather than merely the republication of the earlier poem.
- **1770s–1832:** *Theaterreden, Prologe, Epiloge und Ansprachen*. A corpus of performance acts made for particular productions, court festivals, memorials, and institutional occasions; many resist independence from the events they activated.
- **1770s–1810s:** *Maskenzüge*. Courtly processions combining scenario, costume, verse, movement, music, and ephemeral spectacle: genuine *Gesamtkunstwerke* whose surviving texts record only part of the made thing.
- **c. 1774–1831; published 1832:** *Faust. Der Tragödie zweiter Teil*. Final, posthumously issued manifestation of Goethe’s lifelong dramatic project; completed for publication but not staged in his lifetime.

### Novels, tales, novellas, and narrative projects

- **1774; revised 1787:** *Die Leiden des jungen Werthers*. Epistolary novel existing in two authorially significant versions.
- **1774:** *Anekdote zu Werthers Leiden*. Short satirical narrative related to, but not part of, the novel’s fictional correspondence.
- **1777–1785; first printed 1911:** *Wilhelm Meisters theatralische Sendung*. Early novel manuscript abandoned and later transformed into *Wilhelm Meisters Lehrjahre*; it survives as an unfinished project rather than an authorized lifetime publication.
- **1786:** *Die guten Weiber*. Hybrid dialogue, tale, and essay.
- **1791–1795:** *Unterhaltungen deutscher Ausgewanderten*. Framed cycle of stories, including the independent-seeming yet structurally embedded *Märchen*.
- **1794–1796:** *Wilhelm Meisters Lehrjahre*. Bildungsroman developed from, but not identical with, the earlier *Theatralische Sendung*.
- **1795:** *Das Märchen*. Symbolic fairy tale first functioning as the conclusion of *Unterhaltungen deutscher Ausgewanderten*.
- **1795–1796:** *Reise der Söhne Megaprazons*. Fragmentary satirical travel narrative.
- **1807–1809:** *Die Wahlverwandtschaften*. Novel employing the language of chemical affinity as narrative and ethical structure.
- **1790s–1828:** *Wilhelm Meisters Wanderjahre oder Die Entsagenden*. Archive-like novel and long-running project, first issued in 1821 and radically expanded in 1829; its inserted tales, aphorisms, letters, and institutional schemes resist the unity of an ordinary novel.
- **1797–1827:** *Novelle*. Prose narrative begun under the working idea of a hunting story and completed decades later.
- **1823:** *Der Mann von fünfzig Jahren*. Novella embedded in *Wilhelm Meisters Wanderjahre* but also published separately.
- **1820s:** *Der Hausball*. Unfinished prose narrative.
- **1820s:** *Sankt Joseph der Zweite*. Narrative fragment.

### Verse collections, cycles, epics, and major poems

- **1756–1765:** *Knabengedichte*. Editorial corpus of Goethe’s childhood and school verse; it is not a single authorially designed book.
- **1767:** *Annette*. Manuscript lyric collection associated with Käthchen Schönkopf.
- **1769:** *Neue Lieder*. Goethe’s first printed collection of poems, issued with musical settings by Bernhard Theodor Breitkopf.
- **1770–1771:** *Sesenheimer Lieder*. Editorially constituted lyric group associated with Friederike Brion; its boundaries and titles are not wholly authorial.
- **1771:** *Zum Shakespeares Tag*. Celebratory address whose heightened rhetoric places it between manifesto, speech, and prose poem.
- **1772–1775:** *Frankfurter Hymnen*. Editorial constellation including such poems as *Wandrers Sturmlied*, *Prometheus*, *Ganymed* and *Mahomets Gesang*; not a collection Goethe issued under that title.
- **1774:** *Prometheus*. Hymnic poem; related to but distinct from the dramatic fragment.
- **1774:** *Ganymed*. Lyric hymn.
- **1774:** *Mahomets Gesang*. Lyric dialogue and river allegory.
- **1774:** *Der König in Thule*. Ballad later incorporated into *Faust*.
- **1774:** *Der Bauer an seinen durchlauchtigen Tyrannen*. Political poem, sometimes transmitted without secure contextual information.
- **1774:** *Heidenröslein*. Song-like lyric whose composition is generally associated with the Sesenheim period, although its first printing came later.
- **1775:** *Willkommen und Abschied*. Lyric existing in materially revised textual forms.
- **1775:** *Auf dem See*. Travel lyric.
- **1775:** *Adler und Taube*. Fable poem.
- **1775–1776:** *Urworte. Orphisch* (first conceptual beginnings; completed much later). Philosophical cycle ultimately published in expanded form in 1820; its dating belongs to a prolonged project rather than one act of composition.
- **1776:** *Wandrers Nachtlied*. Lyric beginning “Der du von dem Himmel bist”; distinct from the later poem often given the same generic title.
- **1777:** *Harzreise im Winter*. Journey poem.
- **1778:** *An den Mond*. Lyric surviving in substantially different early and later versions.
- **1780:** *Ein Gleiches* (commonly called the second *Wandrers Nachtlied*). Inscription-poem first written on the wall of a mountain hut; the original physical inscription, later copies, and printed lyric are different manifestations.
- **1782–1783:** *Der Erlkönig*. Ballad first made for *Die Fischerin* and later detached as an autonomous poem.
- **1782–1783:** *Gesang der Geister über den Wassern*. Philosophical lyric in several versions.
- **1783:** *Grenzen der Menschheit*. Philosophical hymn.
- **1783:** *Das Göttliche*. Philosophical hymn.
- **1784–1786:** *Die Geheimnisse*. Unfinished narrative-symbolic poem.
- **1787–1788; published 1795:** *Römische Elegien*. Elegiac cycle; Goethe withheld several sexually or politically pointed poems from the first publication, so the cycle’s historical and posthumous boundaries differ.
- **1790; published 1795:** *Venezianische Epigramme*. Epigrammatic travel cycle, with differences between manuscript corpus and authorized selection.
- **1792–1793:** *Reineke Fuchs*. Verse epic adapted from the European Reynard tradition.
- **1795–1796:** *Xenien*. Epigrammatic collaboration with Friedrich Schiller; individual authorship is often uncertain or joint and should not be silently assigned to Goethe.
- **1796–1797:** *Hermann und Dorothea*. Epic poem in hexameters.
- **1797:** *Balladenjahr* poems. Collaborative-context corpus produced during Goethe and Schiller’s “ballad year,” including *Der Zauberlehrling*, *Die Braut von Korinth*, *Der Gott und die Bajadere*, *Der Schatzgräber* and *Legende*; this is an editorial-historical grouping, not a book Goethe made under that title.
- **1797:** *Der Zauberlehrling*. Ballad.
- **1797:** *Die Braut von Korinth*. Ballad engaging classical and Christian worlds.
- **1797:** *Der Gott und die Bajadere*. Ballad.
- **1797:** *Der Schatzgräber*. Ballad.
- **1797:** *Die neue Melusine*. Verse-tale project later reworked as prose within *Wilhelm Meisters Wanderjahre*.
- **1798–1800:** *Achilleis*. Unfinished epic intended to continue the action of Homer’s *Iliad*.
- **1799:** *Die erste Walpurgisnacht*. Choral ballad conceived for musical realization.
- **1800:** *Amyntas*. Elegiac poem.
- **1803:** *Epilog zu Schillers Glocke*. Memorial poem made to act within a theatrical commemoration.
- **1808:** *Sonette*. Lyric cycle or grouped series prompted partly by Goethe’s exchange with Wilhelmine Herzlieb.
- **1813:** *Cantate zur Feier des Reformationsjubiläums*. Occasional cantata text intended for music and ceremony.
- **1814–1819; enlarged 1827:** *West-östlicher Divan*. Lyric, critical, translational, and autobiographical constellation inspired by Persian poetry; with its prose *Noten und Abhandlungen*, it approaches a literary *Gesamtkunstwerk* rather than a simple poetry collection.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch des Sängers*. Opening book of the *West-östlicher Divan*.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch Hafis*. Divan book staging poetic encounter with Hafez.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch der Liebe*. Divan book of love poetry.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch der Betrachtungen*. Reflective Divan book.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch des Unmuts*. Satirical and polemical Divan book.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch der Sprüche*. Aphoristic Divan book.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch des Timur*. Historically inflected Divan book.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch Suleika*. Dialogic love-poetry sequence associated with Marianne von Willemer; several poems are hers, and editions must not attribute the entire book silently to Goethe.
- **1814–1815:** *Das Schenkenbuch*. Divan book organized around wine, inspiration, and conviviality.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch der Parabeln*. Parabolic Divan book.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch des Parsen*. Divan book engaging Zoroastrian themes.
- **1814–1815:** *Buch des Paradieses*. Concluding Divan book.
- **1816:** *Bei Betrachtung von Schillers Schädel*. Philosophical occasional poem; the traditional title and circumstances of its object have generated documentary discussion.
- **1817:** *Urworte. Orphisch*. Five-part philosophical poem later expanded by an additional stanza.
- **1820:** *Marienbader Elegie*. Common modern designation for the central poem of the later *Trilogie der Leidenschaft*; the familiar title is editorial rather than Goethe’s original standalone title.
- **1821:** *Howards Ehrengedächtnis*. Poetic cycle responding to Luke Howard’s cloud classification, combining scientific nomenclature and lyric manifestation.
- **1823–1827:** *Trilogie der Leidenschaft*. Late lyric grouping comprising *An Werther*, the so-called *Marienbader Elegie*, and *Aussöhnung*.
- **1827:** *Chinesisch-deutsche Jahres- und Tageszeiten*. Late cycle of poems engaging Chinese poetic models.
- **1827–1832:** *Zahme Xenien*. Large, porous late corpus of epigrams, aphoristic verse, and occasional poems; it resists delimitation as a single finished work.
- **1756–1832:** *Gedichte*. The complete lyric corpus occupies multiple volumes in the Weimar and Frankfurt editions and includes songs, hymns, ballads, elegies, sonnets, epigrams, inscriptions, translations, occasional verse, untitled leaves, fragments, variants, and doubtful attributions. Treating every editorially separated piece as a stable “work” would conceal rather than solve the corpus’s textual problems.

### Autobiography, journeys, diaries, and documentary self-construction

- **1775–1832:** *Tagebücher*. Continuous but uneven manuscript record preserved and edited across sixteen Weimar-edition volumes; individual entries are documents rather than securely independent works.
- **1779:** *Briefe aus der Schweiz* (first journey). Epistolary travel account shaped from the Swiss journey.
- **1797:** *Briefe aus der Schweiz* (second part). Retrospective literary recasting rather than an unmediated travel diary.
- **1811–1833:** *Aus meinem Leben. Dichtung und Wahrheit*. Autobiographical project in four parts; the fourth appeared posthumously, and the title itself announces the work’s resistance to a simple documentary status.
- **1816–1817; continuation 1829:** *Italienische Reise*. Retrospectively composed travel obra made from letters, diaries, notes, and later revision; it is not the untouched record of 1786–1788.
- **1822:** *Campagne in Frankreich 1792*. Retrospective autobiographical and historical narrative.
- **1822:** *Belagerung von Mainz*. Account of the 1793 siege, produced long after the event.
- **1817–1830:** *Tag- und Jahres-Hefte als Ergänzung meiner sonstigen Bekenntnisse*. Annalistic autobiographical corpus covering 1749–1822; documentary record and crafted self-narrative overlap.
- **1814:** *Sankt-Rochus-Fest zu Bingen*. Travel, festival, ethnographic, and autobiographical sketch.
- **1814–1815:** *Im Rheingau Herbsttage*. Journey and cultural-observation sequence.
- **1814–1815:** *Kunst und Altertum am Rhein und Main*. Travel-based art-historical observations.
- **1795–1796:** *Vorbereitung zur zweiten Reise nach Italien*. Notes and project materials for a journey that did not become the intended second Italian expedition; the preparation itself is the surviving work.
- **1764–1832:** *Briefe*. A corpus of thousands of situated communications occupying fifty volumes of the Weimar edition; each letter is an act and document, while the corpus as a whole is not a book Goethe designed.
- **1799–1832:** *Briefwechsel mit Carl Friedrich Zelter*. Long collaborative correspondence; authorship is necessarily divided between the correspondents.
- **1794–1805:** *Briefwechsel mit Friedrich Schiller*. Collaborative intellectual and practical record; it cannot be classified as Goethe’s sole-authored obra.
- **1823–1832:** *Gespräche*. Goethe’s spoken statements survive principally through records made by Eckermann and other interlocutors. They are manifestations attributed to Goethe, not texts wholly written or controlled by him.

### Translation, adaptation, and editorial remaking

- **1767:** *Die Höllenfahrt Jesu Christi*. Translation or adaptation from an English source, preserved among Goethe’s early writings.
- **1772–1773:** *Die Gesänge von Selma*. German rendering from Macpherson’s purported Ossian; its source was itself a famous literary fabrication.
- **1773:** *Das Hohe Lied Salomonis*. Translation and poetic re-creation of the biblical Song of Songs.
- **1773:** *Der Lügner*. Adaptation of Corneille’s *Le Menteur*.
- **1773–1774:** *Cäsar*. Translation fragments from Shakespeare’s *Julius Caesar*.
- **1780s:** *Chöre aus Racines Athalie*. Translated choral passages intended for German theatrical use.
- **1796–1803:** *Leben des Benvenuto Cellini*. Translation and extensive literary mediation of Cellini’s autobiography; Goethe’s object is neither wholly original work nor neutral protocol.
- **1799:** *Mahomet* after Voltaire. Translation made as a performable German drama.
- **1799–1800:** *Tancred* after Voltaire. Translation and stage adaptation.
- **1805:** *Rameaus Neffe* by Denis Diderot. German translation published before the complete French original; Goethe’s edition was also an intervention in European literary transmission.
- **1800s–1820s:** *Serbian, Modern Greek, Persian, Italian, French, English, and other poetic translations and imitations*. Dispersed corpus included in the collected editions; boundaries between translation, adaptation, and original “Nachdichtung” are deliberately unstable.

### Natural science: investigations, demonstrations, and theoretical works

- **1784; published 1820/1831 in revised contexts:** *Dem Menschen wie den Tieren ist ein Zwischenknochen der oberen Kinnlade zuzuschreiben*. Anatomical manuscript arguing for the human intermaxillary bone; priority, publication history, and the degree of “discovery” have been disputed.
- **1784–1785:** *Studien zum Zwischenkieferknochen*. Anatomical drawings, preparations, correspondence, and observations forming an investigative project rather than one autonomous paper.
- **1787–1790; printed 1790:** *Versuch die Metamorphose der Pflanzen zu erklären*. Botanical treatise proposing transformation of plant organs.
- **1791:** *Beiträge zur Optik*, first part. Experimental protocol and polemical intervention concerning color phenomena.
- **1792:** *Beiträge zur Optik*, second part. Continuation of the experimental color project.
- **1798:** *Versuch über die Metamorphose der Tiere*. Morphological poem or didactic scientific verse, joining natural philosophy and poetic form.
- **1807:** *Die Farbenlehre. Entwurf einer Farbenlehre*. Preliminary formulation of the larger color project.
- **1810:** *Zur Farbenlehre*. Three-part scientific *obra* comprising a didactic exposition, a polemic against Newtonian optics, and a historical archive; its experiments, diagrams, color plates, and argumentative prose make it more than a conventional treatise.
- **1810:** *Erklärung der zur Goethes Farbenlehre gehörigen Tafeln*. Explanatory protocol for the visual plates accompanying the color theory.
- **1817–1824:** *Zur Morphologie*. Serial publication and collected research project joining botany, comparative anatomy, autobiography of discovery, and natural philosophy.
- **1817:** *Geschichte meines botanischen Studiums*. Scientific autobiography within the morphological project.
- **1817:** *Schicksal der Druckschrift*. Account of the reception and transmission of the plant-metamorphosis essay.
- **1819–1820:** *Bildung und Umbildung organischer Naturen*. Morphological essays on organic formation and transformation.
- **1820:** *Die Metamorphose der Pflanzen* (elegiac poem). Poetic manifestation of Goethe’s botanical thinking, distinct from the 1790 treatise.
- **1820:** *Zur Naturwissenschaft überhaupt, besonders zur Morphologie*. Periodical project for the publication of Goethe’s scientific writings and observations.
- **1820–1832:** *Hefte zur Morphologie*. Successive fascicles containing old and new studies, documentary evidence, correspondence, and controversy; a research archive rather than a single linear book.
- **1821:** *Wolkenlehre* writings. Meteorological observations, diagrams, and reflections prompted by Luke Howard’s cloud classification.
- **1823:** *Der Versuch als Vermittler von Objekt und Subjekt*. Methodological essay composed earlier and published within the scientific corpus.
- **1823:** *Problem und Erwiderung*. Methodological-natural-philosophical text.
- **1824:** *Principes de philosophie zoologique*. Essay responding to debates in French comparative anatomy.
- **1829–1832:** *Schriften zum Pariser Akademiestreit*. Late interventions concerning the dispute between Cuvier and Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire.
- **1770s–1832:** *Mineralogische und geologische Schriften*. Corpus of essays, notes, classifications, collection catalogues, and field observations; many items are working documents rather than finished works.
- **1770s–1832:** *Meteorologische Schriften und Tabellen*. Observational and theoretical corpus involving instruments, measurements, cloud forms, and atmospheric change.
- **1770s–1832:** *Anatomische und zoologische Zeichnungen*. Visual research objects in which scientific thought is made through drawing rather than merely illustrated after the fact.
- **1770s–1832:** *Naturwissenschaftliche Sammlungen*. Goethe assembled and ordered minerals, fossils, osteological specimens, plants, optical apparatus, and experimental materials. These collections are made epistemic environments, although their partly collaborative and changing arrangement resists attribution as discrete authored “works.”

### Art, architecture, literature, and cultural criticism

- **1772:** *Von deutscher Baukunst*. Essay on Strasbourg Cathedral and Erwin von Steinbach; criticism functioning as a manifesto for a new valuation of Gothic architecture.
- **1772–1773:** *Frankfurter gelehrte Anzeigen*. Reviews and polemical contributions, many anonymous; the attribution of individual pieces is sometimes disputed.
- **1773:** *Zum Shakespeares Tag*. Speech-manifesto declaring Shakespeare a model of dramatic freedom.
- **1774–1778:** *Physiognomische Fragmente* contributions. Textual and visual contributions to Lavater’s collaborative publication; the precise boundary of Goethe’s authorship is not always recoverable.
- **1776:** *Aus Goethes Brieftasche*. Short critical and cultural pieces.
- **1785:** *Bürgers Homer*. Literary-critical essay.
- **1788–1790:** *Venedig 1790*. Notes and essays on Venetian art and culture.
- **1795:** *Literarischer Sansculottismus*. Polemical essay on literary culture.
- **1795–1797:** *Die Horen* contributions. Poems, prose, criticism, and collaborative editorial work for Schiller’s journal; not every unsigned item is securely attributable.
- **1795–1797:** *Propyläen* project. Art journal conceived with Heinrich Meyer and others as a programmatic intervention in artistic culture.
- **1798:** *Einleitung in die Propyläen*. Programmatic art-theoretical manifesto for the journal.
- **1798:** *Über Laokoon*. Art-critical essay.
- **1798:** *Über Wahrheit und Wahrscheinlichkeit der Kunstwerke*. Aesthetic dialogue.
- **1798–1799:** *Der Sammler und die Seinigen*. Dialogic art-theoretical work.
- **1799:** *Frauenrollen auf dem römischen Theater durch Männer gespielt*. Essay on theatrical convention.
- **1799–1805:** *Weimarer Preisaufgaben*. Goethe and Heinrich Meyer’s institutional project of competition subjects, judgments, and exhibitions; collaboratively authored and performative in effect.
- **1805:** *Winckelmann und sein Jahrhundert*. Biographical-critical memorial volume edited and substantially written by Goethe, but incorporating other contributors.
- **1805–1806:** *Philipp Hackert*. Biography and editorial reconstruction of the painter’s life from Hackert’s papers.
- **1806–1816:** *Beiträge zur Jenaischen Allgemeinen Literatur-Zeitung*. Review corpus, often anonymous or editorially attributed.
- **1816–1832:** *Über Kunst und Altertum*. Six-volume periodical and curatorial project containing criticism, translations, documentary notices, and interventions by Goethe and collaborators; authorship varies item by item.
- **1816:** *Ruysdael als Dichter*. Essay interpreting landscape painting through poetic categories.
- **1816–1817:** *Altdeutsche Gemälde in Leipzig*. Art-historical observations.
- **1817:** *Über Kunst und Altertum in den Rhein- und Maingegenden*. Regional art-historical and preservation writing.
- **1818:** *Antik und Modern*. Comparative aesthetic essay.
- **1818–1820:** *Philostrats Gemälde*. Translation, commentary, and imaginative reconstruction of described ancient paintings.
- **1819:** *Leonardo da Vincis Abendmahl*. Art-historical essay.
- **1820:** *Der Triumphzug von Mantegna*. Study of Mantegna’s pictorial cycle.
- **1820s:** *Pompeji, Herculanum und Stabiä*. Archaeological and art-critical writings.
- **1820s:** *Über neuere Malerei und graphische Künste*. Corpus of criticism rather than one stable essay.
- **1827:** *Zur Weltliteratur*. The phrase names a dispersed critical project developed through essays, reviews, introductions, letters, and conversations; it resists being called a single work.
- **1828:** *Le Tasse, drame historique de M. Alexandre Duval*. Late theatrical criticism.
- **1829:** *Einleitung zu Carlyles Leben Schillers*. Critical introduction and act of international literary mediation.
- **1770s–1832:** *Maximen und Reflexionen*. Posthumously constituted corpus assembled from manuscripts, notebooks, publications, and reported statements; its familiar title does not designate one book Goethe completed.
- **1770s–1832:** *Schriften zur Literatur*. Large corpus of reviews, commemorations, introductions, notices, polemics, and fragments; anonymity and collaborative journal production make some attributions provisional.
- **1770s–1832:** *Schriften zur bildenden Kunst und Baukunst*. Corpus spanning criticism, collecting, preservation, exhibition practice, architecture, painting, sculpture, medals, and craft.

### Drawings, designs, and other visual objects

- **c. 1765–1832:** *Corpus der Goethezeichnungen*. More than two thousand attributed drawings and related sheets survive, including landscapes, architecture, geological and anatomical studies, stage designs, diagrams, figure compositions, copies, and sketches. It is not one work, and the catalogue records uncertain attributions, workshop contexts, copies, and sheets on which image and writing cannot be separated.
- **1770s–1780s:** *Landschaftszeichnungen aus Thüringen, dem Harz und der Schweiz*. Travel-based visual corpus; many sheets lack fixed authorial titles or exact dates.
- **1786–1788:** *Italienische Zeichnungen*. Architectural, landscape, antiquarian, and figure studies produced during the Italian journey; they function as observation, artistic training, travel record, and collection.
- **1786–1788:** *Bühnen- und Figurenentwürfe zu eigenen Dramen*. Visual thinking for theatrical works, including *Faust* and other stage projects; these are neither simply illustrations nor autonomous pictures.
- **1790s–1830s:** *Farbenlehre diagrams and color plates*. Experimental and demonstrative visual objects integral to the color project.
- **1790s–1830s:** *Morphological and anatomical drawings*. Research drawings in which comparison and transformation are argued visually.
- **Undated or uncertain:** *Drawings attributed to Goethe*. The scholarly corpus retains doubtful and rejected attributions as evidence; these sheets resist inclusion as Goethe’s works without qualification.

### Institutional, political, and administrative making

- **1776–1786:** *Weimar governmental memoranda and protocols*. Administrative writings on mining, roads, finance, military affairs, and commissions. These texts act upon institutions and material conditions and are therefore protocols or official acts, not literary descriptions.
- **1777–1813:** *Ilmenau mining project*. A long institutional and technological project involving inspection, reports, finance, engineering, ceremonies, and eventual failure; no single document is the work.
- **1779–1786:** *War Commission papers*. Orders, reports, and administrative decisions produced in office, often within collective bureaucratic authorship.
- **1782–1786:** *Finance and road-building papers*. Official protocols and memoranda whose effects were practical rather than primarily literary.
- **1791–1817:** *Direction of the Weimar Court Theatre*. Repertory planning, casting, adaptation, rehearsal rules, correspondence, stage practice, and institutional design together form a sustained theatrical project; performances were collaborative manifestations, not Goethe’s sole-authored objects.
- **1798:** *Regeln für Schauspieler*. Instructional theatre text: a protocol for bodily and vocal performance rather than a descriptive essay.
- **1790s–1810s:** *Weimar art exhibitions and prize competitions*. Collaborative institutional works made with Heinrich Meyer and others.
- **1803–1832:** *Supervision of the University of Jena and scientific collections*. Administrative and curatorial project documented through official writings; authorship of institutional outcomes is necessarily shared.
- **1815–1832:** *Preservation, museum, library, and collection memoranda*. Protocols acting on cultural objects and institutions.
- **1760s–1832:** *Juristische, amtliche und politische Schriften*. The complete corpus includes examination theses, legal opinions, decrees, reports, petitions, speeches, wills, and drafts; many are anonymous in function, collaboratively produced, or inseparable from bureaucratic files.
- **1770 and later:** *Positiones juris*. Latin legal theses associated with Goethe’s Strasbourg legal examination; their conventional authorship and academic production context do not map neatly onto modern sole authorship.
- **1770s–1832:** *Testamente*. Successive legal instruments rather than literary works; each acts by disposing of property and papers.

### Collections, editions, and total-work projects

- **1775–1832:** *Goethes Schriften / Werke* editions. Goethe repeatedly selected, revised, regrouped, and republished his writings; each collected edition is a curatorial obra and cannot be reduced to a neutral container.
- **1787–1790:** *Goethe’s Schriften* (Göschen edition). First major authorized collected edition, incorporating revised versions of earlier works.
- **1806–1810:** *Goethe’s Werke* (Cotta edition). Collected-works project with renewed ordering and revision.
- **1815–1819:** *Goethe’s Werke* (twenty-volume edition). Authorially supervised reconfiguration of the oeuvre.
- **1827–1830:** *Goethe’s Werke. Vollständige Ausgabe letzter Hand*. Final authorized collected-works project in forty volumes, followed by posthumous supplementary volumes; “Ausgabe letzter Hand” does not mean every text received a genuinely final authorial state.
- **1820s–1832:** *Archive and Nachlass project*. Goethe deliberately ordered manuscripts, correspondence, collections, and publication plans for posterity. The archive is a made body of work, although its later arrangement also reflects heirs and editors.
