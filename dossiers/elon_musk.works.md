## Works by Elon Reeve Musk

### Software and digital creations

- **1984:** *Blastar*. A short BASIC space-shooter program published as source code in the South African magazine *PC and Office Technology* under the twelve-year-old Musk’s name; surviving reconstructions are later implementations, not the original executable. [Ashlee Vance’s biography reproduces the listing; CNBC describes its publication and sale.](https://www.cnbc.com/2015/06/13/elon-musks-first-computer-game-blastar.html)

- **1995–1999:** *Global Link Information Network / Zip2*. An online business directory integrating maps, directions, and newspaper content, programmed initially by Musk and developed thereafter by a company and its employees. It is therefore a collaborative software-platform obra, not something Musk can accurately be said to have made alone. [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Elon-Musk) and [Time’s business chronology](https://time.com/6170834/elon-musk-business-timeline-twitter/) document its history.

- **1999–2000:** *X.com*. An online financial-services company and website co-founded by Musk, Harris Fricker, Christopher Payne, and Ed Ho; its merger with Confinity produced the company later renamed PayPal. PayPal as subsequently developed resists attribution to Musk as a personal creation. [Time](https://time.com/6170834/elon-musk-business-timeline-twitter/) and [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Elon-Musk) trace the corporate succession.

- **2023–:** *X*. The continuing transformation and rebranding of the acquired Twitter platform under Musk’s ownership. This is partly a corporate redesign and partly an ongoing political-media project, not a new platform personally authored by Musk; Twitter’s prior code, users, and institutional history remain inside it. [The company announced the name and visual rebranding in 2023.](https://blog.x.com/en_us/topics/company/2023/building-the-future-of-x)

- **2023–:** *Grok*. A family of conversational generative-AI systems produced by xAI under Musk’s direction. The models are collective technical creations rather than Musk-authored software, although Musk founded the producing institution and publicly framed their intended character. [xAI’s launch announcement names the company’s technical team](https://x.ai/news/announcing-xai), and its subsequent announcements document the Grok family.

- **2025–:** *Grokipedia*. An AI-generated online encyclopedia launched by xAI as an alternative to Wikipedia. It resists ordinary authorship: Musk initiated and promoted the project, while machine-generated entries, source materials, and an evolving platform constitute the manifested object. [A comparative scholarly study documents its launch, derivation, and citation practices.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09685)

### Manifestos, plans, protocols, and technical texts

- **2006:** *The Secret Tesla Motors Master Plan (just between you and me)*. A signed corporate manifesto: it does not merely describe Tesla but prescribes a sequence of products and uses the text to justify the company’s existence. [Tesla’s official master-plan archive dates and credits it to Musk.](https://www.tesla.com/master-plans)

- **2013:** *Hyperloop Alpha*. An open technical concept paper for passenger capsules moving through low-pressure tubes. Musk published it under his name but acknowledged work by Tesla and SpaceX engineers, so it is best treated as a Musk-led collaborative project specification rather than an individually engineered machine. [The U.S. Department of Transportation repository catalogues the paper under Musk’s name.](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/34655)

- **2014:** *All Our Patent Are Belong to You*. A manifesto and conditional patent-use protocol declaring that Tesla would not sue parties using its technology in good faith; the deliberately ungrammatical title invokes an Internet meme. [Tesla preserves the signed post and its operative promise.](https://www.tesla.com/blog/all-our-patent-are-belong-you)

- **2016:** *Master Plan, Part Deux*. A signed sequel combining vehicle, solar-energy, autonomous-driving, and shared-fleet intentions. Its promised products make it a project-text whose completion remains uneven and disputed, rather than a retrospective account of accomplished work. [Tesla dates and credits the text to Musk.](https://www.tesla.com/master-plan-part-deux)

- **2016 presentation; 2017 publication:** *Making Humans a Multi-Planetary Species*. A journal article adapted from Musk’s presentation to the 67th International Astronautical Congress, proposing a transport architecture and self-sustaining settlement on Mars. Its publication history makes it simultaneously a conference performance, technical prospectus, and unfinished civilizational project. [*New Space*, DOI 10.1089/space.2017.29009.emu](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/space.2017.29009.emu)

- **2017 presentation; 2018 publication:** *Making Life Multi-Planetary*. An abridged, edited transcript of Musk’s 68th International Astronautical Congress presentation, updating the proposed launch system later associated with Starship. Its status is closer to a published presentation than a conventionally composed research paper. [*New Space*, DOI 10.1089/space.2018.29013.emu](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/space.2018.29013.emu)

- **2022:** *Elon Musk’s Best and Final Offer to Acquire Twitter*. A securities filing and performative corporate text: the letter helped initiate the transaction it described. It is neither literature nor a technical protocol, but a legally consequential manifesto of acquisition. [The text is preserved in Twitter’s Schedule 14D-9 filing with the SEC.](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1418091/000119312522117720/d310843dsc14d9.htm)

- **2022–2023:** *The Twitter Files*. A Musk-initiated release of selected internal Twitter records through outside writers. It resists designation as a Musk work: he commissioned access and shaped the project, but Matt Taibbi, Bari Weiss, Michael Shellenberger, and others wrote the published threads, while the selection and completeness of the underlying archive were disputed. [The Columbia Journalism Review describes the project’s production and selection problems.](https://www.cjr.org/the_media_today/twitter_files_musk_taibbi.php)

- **2023:** *Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter*. A manifesto calling for a six-month pause in training certain advanced AI systems, signed by Musk alongside many others. He is a signatory, not the sole author, so its presence here records participation in a collective performative text rather than individual authorship. [Future of Life Institute preserves the letter and signatory context.](https://futureoflife.org/open-letter/pause-giant-ai-experiments/)

- **2023:** *Master Plan Part 3*. A quantified decarbonization plan issued by Tesla. Unlike the first two plans, it is credited to “Tesla,” not to Musk personally; it belongs here only as a corporate project made under his leadership, and assigning him individual authorship would be unwarranted. [Tesla’s official PDF identifies the institutional author.](https://www.tesla.com/ns_videos/Tesla-Master-Plan-Part-3.pdf)

- **2009–present:** *Tweets / posts by @elonmusk*. A vast, mutable corpus of short public texts whose components include announcements, jokes, arguments, polls, political interventions, and market-moving utterances. The corpus resists treatment as a closed collected work because posts can be deleted, replies depend on surrounding conversations, and no scholarly critical edition establishes a complete text; the Library of Congress’s former archive of public tweets ended with 2017 rather than supplying such an edition. [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2017/12/update-on-the-twitter-archive-at-the-library-of-congress-2/)

### Patent-defined inventions and industrial designs

- **1996 filing; 1999 grant:** *Interactive Network Directory Service with Integrated Maps and Directions* (US 5,944,769). A patented Zip2-era web service integrating a business directory, maps, searches, and directions, jointly attributed to Musk and Maurice J. Fitzgerald II. The patent record establishes legal inventorship, not that either inventor alone wrote every part of the implementation. [USPTO-derived inventor index](https://patents.justia.com/patent/5944769)

- **1998 filing; 2000 grant:** *Method and Apparatus for an Accelerated Radius Search* (US 6,108,650). A jointly patented method for expanding a geographic search radius according to result density, naming Musk, King-Sun Wai, and Leo Chan as inventors. [USPTO-derived inventor index](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6108650)

- **1999 filing; 2000 grant:** *Interactive Network Directory Service with Integrated Maps and Directions* (US 6,148,260). A later patent in the same Zip2 invention family, again jointly credited to Musk and Fitzgerald; it should not be mistaken for a wholly separate consumer product. [USPTO-derived inventor index](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6148260)

- **1997 filing; 2001 grant:** *System and Method for Initiating a Telephone Call Utilizing Internet Initiation* (US 6,185,194). A Zip2 patent for beginning a public-telephone-network call from an Internet display, jointly naming Musk and Aleksandar Dukic. [USPTO-derived inventor index](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6185194)

- **2001 filing; 2002 publication:** *Bi-directional Facsimile Mechanism Using the Internet* (US 2002/0018236 A1). A patent application for transmitting and retrieving faxes through web addresses, jointly naming Musk and Almir Grbic. The inventor index records an application rather than a granted U.S. patent. [USPTO-derived inventor index](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/elon-musk)

- **2011 priority; 2013 grant:** *Funnel Shaped Charge Inlet* (US 8,579,635). A functional Tesla charging-port invention jointly naming Musk, Joshua Willard Ferguson, Daryl Zalan, and Christopher Hugo Van Dyke. [Google Patents reproduces the patent record and inventor list.](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8579635B2/en)

- **2012 filing; 2013 grant:** *Vehicle Door* (US D678,154). An ornamental design patent associated with the Model X falcon-wing door, jointly naming Musk, Franz von Holzhausen, and Bernard Lee. A design patent establishes co-inventorship of the claimed appearance, not sole authorship of the complete automobile. [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD678154S1/en)

- **2012 filing; 2013 grant:** *Vehicle* (US D683,268). An ornamental vehicle design, jointly naming Musk, Franz von Holzhausen, Bernard Lee, and David Tadashi Imai. Its official title is only “Vehicle”; identifying it more precisely from its commercial context would add a title the patent does not supply. [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD683268S1/en)

- **2012 filing; 2015 grant:** *Vehicle Charge Inlet* (US D724,031). An ornamental charging-inlet design jointly naming Musk, Ferguson, Zalan, and Van Dyke; it is related to, but legally distinct from, the functional funnel-shaped-inlet patent. [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD724031S1/en)

- **2019 filing; 2023 grant, with continuations:** *Autonomous and User Controlled Vehicle Summon to a Target* (US 11,567,514; US 12,164,310; US 2025/0068166 A1). A Tesla patent family covering navigation of a vehicle toward a remotely specified target; Musk is one of eighteen named inventors. Treating the whole system as his individual invention would erase the patent’s unusually extensive joint attribution. [The USPTO-derived inventor index groups the grants and continuation application.](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/elon-musk)

### Companies and institutional projects

- **2002–:** *Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX)*. A company founded by Musk as the institutional vehicle for reducing launch costs and pursuing settlement beyond Earth. It is a continuing collective obra encompassing thousands of workers and many spacecraft; Musk’s founder and chief-engineer roles do not make every SpaceX artifact his personal work. [NASA’s Falcon 9 data sheet records the company’s 2002 establishment by Musk.](https://sma.nasa.gov/LaunchVehicle/assets/spacex-falcon-9-data-sheet.pdf)

- **2002–:** *Musk Foundation*. A private grant-making foundation established by Musk. Its institutional existence is a creation, but its sparse disclosure and dependence on Musk’s personal giving make it difficult to classify as a coherent body of philanthropic work. [ProPublica’s Nonprofit Explorer reproduces its IRS filings.](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/770587507)

- **2004–:** *Tesla under Musk’s product direction*. Musk did not found Tesla in the ordinary historical sense: Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning incorporated it in 2003, and Musk joined as lead investor and chairman in 2004. A 2009 legal settlement permitted several people, including Musk, to call themselves co-founders; Tesla’s vehicles and energy systems therefore belong here as collaborative corporate works, not as individually authored Musk objects. [Tesla’s corporate history](https://www.tesla.com/elon-musk) and [the Eberhard litigation settlement reported by CNET](https://www.cnet.com/roadshow/news/tesla-motors-founders-now-there-are-five/) document the contested designation.

- **2006–2016:** *SolarCity*. A solar-energy company conceived with Musk’s encouragement and capital but founded and operated by his cousins Lyndon and Peter Rive; Musk chaired it before Tesla acquired it. It should not be called a Musk-made company without this qualification. [Time’s chronology distinguishes patronage from founding.](https://time.com/6170834/elon-musk-business-timeline-twitter/)

- **2015–:** *OpenAI*. A nonprofit AI research project co-founded by Musk, Sam Altman, and a larger founding group; Musk left its board in 2018. Later institutional forms and products such as ChatGPT cannot be attributed to him as creations merely because he participated in the founding. [OpenAI’s 2015 announcement names its founding leadership and researchers.](https://openai.com/index/introducing-openai/)

- **2016–:** *Neuralink*. A neurotechnology company co-founded by Musk and a group of scientists and engineers to develop implantable brain–computer interfaces. Its devices and clinical work are collective technical products, while the exact allocation of the original founders’ contributions is not captured by the company name. [The peer-reviewed Neuralink systems paper lists Musk and the development team as authors.](https://www.jmir.org/2019/10/e16194/)

- **2016–:** *The Boring Company*. A tunnelling and transport enterprise begun as a SpaceX-associated project and later separated into a company. Its tunnels are corporate engineering works; the repeatedly revised Hyperloop and urban-transport promises remain projects rather than completed works. [The company’s official history](https://www.boringcompany.com/about) and [Time’s chronology](https://time.com/6170834/elon-musk-business-timeline-twitter/) document the distinction.

- **2023–:** *xAI*. An artificial-intelligence company founded by Musk. It is an institutional project rather than a personally authored AI system: the launch notice names a team with prior work at DeepMind, OpenAI, Google Research, Microsoft Research, Tesla, and the University of Toronto. [xAI’s founding announcement](https://x.ai/news/announcing-xai)

- **2024–:** *America PAC*. A political-action organization created and financed principally by Musk to influence U.S. elections. It is an operative political apparatus rather than an artwork or conventional text, though its advertisements, petitions, canvassing systems, and payments function collectively as a performative political project. [Federal Election Commission committee records](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00879510/)

### Vehicles, machines, and infrastructures made through teams

- **2006–2008:** *Falcon 1*. SpaceX’s first orbital launch vehicle and the first privately developed liquid-fuel rocket to reach orbit. Musk founded and led the producing organization and served as chief engineer, but the rocket is the work of a large engineering team; calling it solely “by Musk” would mistake leadership for individual fabrication. [SpaceX’s official Falcon 1 flight page](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/falcon-1/)

- **2008–2012:** *Tesla Roadster*. A battery-electric sports car developed by Tesla from a Lotus-derived platform. Musk influenced specifications and received a 2008 Global Green product-design award with Tesla designer Franz von Holzhausen, but Martin Eberhard, JB Straubel, Lotus, AC Propulsion, and Tesla’s engineering staff were also fundamental; personal authorship is disputed. [Tesla’s corporate account](https://www.tesla.com/roadster) and [the founders settlement](https://www.cnet.com/roadshow/news/tesla-motors-founders-now-there-are-five/) establish the collaborative context.

- **2008–:** *Falcon 9*. A family of orbital launch vehicles developed by SpaceX under Musk’s technical and executive leadership, including successively reusable first stages. It is a continuing engineered lineage rather than one stable object. [NASA’s vehicle data sheet](https://sma.nasa.gov/LaunchVehicle/assets/spacex-falcon-9-data-sheet.pdf)

- **2010–:** *Dragon*. A family of SpaceX cargo and crew spacecraft. Musk directed the company that made it, but NASA, SpaceX employees, contractors, and successive design teams share its production; it therefore belongs to Musk’s institutional oeuvre only with explicit resistance to sole authorship. [NASA’s Commercial Crew overview documents Dragon’s collaborative development.](https://www.nasa.gov/exploration-systems-development-mission-directorate/commercial-space/commercial-crew-program/)

- **2012–:** *Model S*. A Tesla electric sedan produced while Musk was chief executive and product architect; the vehicle and door design patents legally credit multiple designers. It is a corporate Gesamtkunstwerk of industrial design, software, manufacturing, charging infrastructure, and branding—not a personally made object. [Tesla’s Model S history](https://www.tesla.com/models) and [US D683,268](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD683268S1/en) support the product and joint-design attribution.

- **2012–:** *Supercharger network*. A coupled infrastructure of charging hardware, software, sites, and operating agreements initiated by Tesla under Musk. Because no single bounded artifact constitutes it, it is better classified as an expanding infrastructural project than as one invention. [Tesla’s official Supercharger history and description](https://www.tesla.com/supercharger)

- **2015–:** *Powerwall, Powerpack, and Megapack*. Tesla’s stationary-energy-storage product families, unveiled or promoted by Musk but engineered and manufactured collectively. Their coupling of industrial objects, software, electrical grids, and energy policy makes the family closer to an infrastructural project than a personal invention. [Tesla’s official energy-storage pages](https://www.tesla.com/megapack)

- **2015–:** *Model X*. A Tesla electric sport-utility vehicle distinguished by falcon-wing doors. Musk is a named co-inventor on related ornamental design patents, but the finished vehicle incorporates the work of Tesla’s broader design and engineering organization. [Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/modelx) and [US D678,154](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD678154S1/en)

- **2016–:** *Autopilot / Full Self-Driving*. An evolving collection of Tesla driver-assistance hardware, neural-network software, training data, and marketing claims developed under Musk’s direction. The name “Full Self-Driving” resists description as a completed autonomous-driving work because deployed versions have remained supervised systems, and regulators and litigants have disputed representations of their capability. [Tesla’s current support description](https://www.tesla.com/support/autopilot) and [NHTSA’s Tesla investigation materials](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/automated-vehicles-safety) provide the operative distinction.

- **2017–:** *Model 3*. A mass-market Tesla electric sedan developed under Musk’s product leadership. Like the other Tesla vehicles, it is a collective industrial creation whose “author” is more accurately the company and its workforce. [Tesla’s official product history](https://www.tesla.com/model3)

- **2018–:** *Starlink*. A SpaceX-designed and operated satellite constellation, user-terminal system, launch program, and communications service. Its distributed and continuously replaced components make it an infrastructure or Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single stable machine. [The Federal Communications Commission documents SpaceX’s licensed constellation.](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-authorizes-spacex-provide-broadband-satellite-services)

- **2019–:** *Starship and Super Heavy*. A reusable spacecraft-and-launch-vehicle system developed iteratively by SpaceX under Musk’s leadership. It remains a project as well as a succession of manifested prototypes: failures, redesigns, test articles, launch towers, and ground systems are constitutive parts of the work. [The FAA’s Starship environmental and licensing record](https://www.faa.gov/space/stakeholder_engagement/spacex_starship) and [SpaceX’s vehicle description](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/starship/) document that continuing status.

- **2019–:** *Cybertruck*. A Tesla electric pickup whose angular stainless-steel body was publicly associated with Musk’s design direction. The manufactured vehicle remains a team product, and no consulted patent catalogue supports calling Musk its sole designer. [Tesla’s official Cybertruck page](https://www.tesla.com/cybertruck)

- **2021–:** *Tesla Bot / Optimus*. A proposed and subsequently prototyped general-purpose humanoid robot. It remains an unfinished corporate project whose staged manifestations should not be confused with the fully capable labor system described in Musk’s presentations. [Tesla’s official AI page identifies Optimus as a development program.](https://www.tesla.com/AI)

- **2023–:** *Cybercab / Robotaxi project*. A projected autonomous transport vehicle and service announced through Tesla and shown in prototype form. Until the advertised autonomous network exists, the intention itself—not a completed transport system—is the principal work. [Tesla’s “We, Robot” presentation materials describe the proposed system.](https://www.tesla.com/we-robot)

### Music and performance

- **2019:** *RIP Harambe*. A novelty rap recording released under the fictive label Emo G Records and credited to Musk as performer. The production involved BloodPop and Caroline Polachek, so it is a collaborative sound recording rather than a solitary composition. [*The Guardian* documents the release and collaborators.](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2019/mar/31/elon-musk-rap-song-rip-harambe-emo-g-records)

- **2020:** *Don’t Doubt Ur Vibe*. An electronic dance recording for which Musk claimed both writing and vocal performance, released through Emo G Records. It is a song and recorded performance—not a book or manifesto—and its musical production remains collaborative. [*Rolling Stone* reported the credits and release.](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/elon-musk-dont-doubt-ur-vibe-song-946980/)
