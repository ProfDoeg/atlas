## Works by Mary Magdalene

### Historically reported speech-act

- **c. AD 30:** *Resurrection announcement*. The Gospel traditions portray Mary Magdalene as carrying news of Jesus’s resurrection to the disciples; this is an oral act of witness—almost a manifesto because it acts upon its hearers—not a surviving authored text. The accounts differ substantially, and historical criticism cannot recover her original wording or establish the event precisely ([Lupieri](https://ecommons.luc.edu/theology_facpubs/138/); [Lindars](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/first-resurrection-appearance-and-the-ending-of-marks-gospel/52F96AC9AFC9660277D5C25D59C5E099)).

### Surviving obra

- **No securely attributable surviving obra:** No extant book, letter, protocol, material artwork, or other independently identifiable work can responsibly be assigned to the historical Mary Magdalene. The sources preserve representations of her speech and actions, not objects demonstrably composed or made by her ([Ehrman](https://academic.oup.com/book/50204); [Lupieri](https://ecommons.luc.edu/theology_facpubs/138/)).

### Texts bearing Mary’s authority but not made by her

- **First half of the second century, disputed:** *Gospel of Mary*. An anonymous Christian revelatory dialogue—not a biography and not a text written by Mary—whose incomplete surviving witnesses place a character called Mary in possession of special teaching. Her identification as Mary Magdalene is widespread but not explicit in the work; the original Greek composition is usually dated to the second century, while the principal Coptic manuscript is fourth- or fifth-century ([Tuckett](https://academic.oup.com/book/34836); [NASSCAL](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-mary/)).

- **Before AD 375; possibly fictive or lost:** *Greater Questions of Mary*. A purported revelatory dialogue known only through Epiphanius’s hostile report; no manuscript survives, its date and actual existence as an independent text are uncertain, and even the Mary of its title is not unambiguously identified. It therefore resists classification as a recoverable work and cannot be attributed to Mary Magdalene ([NASSCAL](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/questions-of-mary/); [Brownsmith, Lied, and Kartzow](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09518207221137070)).

- **Before AD 375; title only:** *Lesser Questions of Mary*. Epiphanius mentions a second work under this title but supplies no recoverable contents; its independence, subject, date, and identification of “Mary” remain uncertain. It is an ancient attribution or bibliographical shadow, not evidence of something made by the historical Magdalene ([NASSCAL](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/questions-of-mary/); [Brownsmith, Lied, and Kartzow](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09518207221137070)).

### Claimed corporeal manifestations—not works

- **By 1024–1050; ceremonially reauthenticated 1265–1267:** *The Vézelay relics*. Human bones claimed as Mary Magdalene’s remains constituted a cultic manifestation rather than an obra she made; the claim originated centuries after her lifetime, and the relics’ identity is historically unverified. Vézelay’s dossier and solemn translation promoted and defended that attribution ([Scott](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/714579); [Saxer dossier review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/scrip_0036-9772_1977_num_31_2_2845_t1_0352_0000_2)).

- **9 December 1279:** *The Saint-Maximin relics*. Bones discovered in a marble sarcophagus at Saint-Maximin-la-Sainte-Baume were identified as Mary Magdalene’s body and used to challenge Vézelay’s claim. They are disputed remains—a posthumously attributed manifestation, not a made work—and scholarship describes the 1279–1280 “invention” as constructed in direct rivalry with Vézelay’s earlier authentication ([Saxer](https://www.persee.fr/doc/prohi_0033-1856_1977_num_27_109_3437?pageId=T1_259); [discovery account](https://www.persee.fr/doc/prohi_0033-1856_1977_num_27_109_3437?pageId=T1_260)).
