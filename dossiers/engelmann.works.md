## Works by Karl Max Engelmann

### Collective life-reform projects

- **1884:** *Pythagoräer-Bund* (also called *Bruder-Bund* and *Internationaler Bund für konsequente Humanität*). A life-reform association or social project co-founded in Berlin by Engelmann, Johannes Friedrich Guttzeit, Robert Springer, and Otto Rabe; its program joined vegetarianism to reforms of clothing, housing, habits, and communal life. The sources disagree about its formal name, so it resists treatment as a single, fixed-title institution.
- **Before 1900–after 1900:** *Vegetarian pension on Monte Trinità, above Locarno*. A household, business, and lived reform project operated by Engelmann and his wife rather than a conventionally bounded work. Ida Hofmann records it as the place where the future Monte Verità settlers gathered in 1900; later scholarship cautiously credits Engelmann with directing them toward Monte Monescia.

### Political and cultural studies

- **1905:** *Das Germanentum und sein Verfall: Eine rassenpolitische Studie*. A 489-page racial-political study published by Friedrich Funcke in Stuttgart. Contemporary and later descriptions identify it as a racialist interpretation of cultural history influenced by Gobineau; the title page names the author simply as “Max Engelmann,” while Monte Verità scholarship attributes it to Karl Max Engelmann.
- **1917–1918:** *Der Uranische Staat als Pflichterfüllung des Stärkeren*. A speculative constitution and manifesto for an authoritarian, elitist life-reform state. Hermann Müller dates its composition to 1917, while his bibliography records the Monte Trinità publication as 1918; both dates are therefore retained.

### Pamphlets and lectures

- **Date not established:** *Die Revolution der Zukunft: Ein erhebender Fernblick für alle unzufriedenen Denker der Gegenwart*. A 35-page political-utopian pamphlet issued in Berlin by Julius Bohne and surviving as a “second thousand” inexpensive edition. Its catalogue record supplies neither the original publication date nor evidence connecting the named “Max Engelmann” conclusively with Karl Max Engelmann, so the attribution remains provisional.
- **Before 1918:** Untitled or presently unidentified vegetarian promotional writings. Müller states that Engelmann produced additional vegetarian advocacy pamphlets but gives no titles, dates, or enumeration. This is therefore evidence of a body of writing, not a recoverable list of individual works, and it resists classification as a discrete obra.

### Disputed attribution

- **1886:** *Die Vegetarier*. Literaturport’s biographical work-list attributes this title to Max Engelmann, but the contemporary *Börsenblatt* identifies the author as “M. Enckmann” and describes the object as a 116-page *Posse mit Gesang*—a musical farce—published by Julius Bohne. Without evidence that “Enckmann” was an error or pseudonym for Engelmann, the work cannot securely be included in his oeuvre.

### Sources

- Hermann Müller, [*Bruder-Bund und Pythagoräer-Bund*](https://www.gusto-graeser.de/doc/pdf/gusto-graeser.de_20180606_1885-bruderbund-wb.pdf), especially pp. 1–5. This specialist historical study supplies the principal work-list and documents the 1884 association, Engelmann’s pension, the unidentified vegetarian writings, *Das Germanentum und sein Verfall*, and *Der Uranische Staat*.
- Literaturport, [“Max Engelmann”](https://www.literaturport.de/literaturlandschaft/autoren-berlinbrandenburg/autor/max-engelmann/). A biographical author record identifying Engelmann as a teacher and writer and offering a short “Werkauswahl”; consulted particularly for the disputed attribution of *Die Vegetarier*.
- *Börsenblatt für den deutschen Buchhandel*, 1 October 1886, [digitized issue](https://digital.slub-dresden.de/data/kitodo/Brsfded_39946221X-18861001/Brsfded_39946221X-18861001_tif/jpegs/Brsfded_39946221X-18861001.pdf). Contemporary publication notice identifying *Die Vegetarier* as a musical farce by “M. Enckmann.”
- CiNii Books, [*Die Revolution der Zukunft: Ein erhebender Fernblick für alle unzufriedenen Denker der Gegenwart*](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA56196159). Bibliographic description of the undated surviving edition; used only to establish the object’s title, form, extent, publisher, and uncertain date.
- Online Books Page, University of Pennsylvania, [entry for *Das Germanentum und sein Verfall*](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/browse?c=x&key=Germany+--+Civilization+--+Middle+Ages&type=lcsubc). Digital-text index confirming the 1905 Stuttgart publication and attribution to Max Engelmann.
- Historiallinen Aikakauskirja, [contemporary review of *Das Germanentum und sein Verfall*](https://journal.fi/haik/article/download/173418/115926/425238). Contemporary bibliographical and critical evidence for the title, subtitle, date, extent, and racial-political character of the study.
- Ida Hofmann-Oedenkoven, [*Monte Verità: Wahrheit ohne Dichtung*](https://www.gusto-graeser.info/Links/HofmannMV-Word.pdf), pp. 15–16. First-person evidence for the Engelmann household’s vegetarian pension and its role in the journey that led to Monte Verità.
