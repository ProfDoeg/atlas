## Works by Sergio Demian Lerner

### Cryptographic protocols and technical papers

- **2009–2010:** *MPF: A New Family of Practical and Secure Mental Poker Protocols*. A computer-science thesis and protocol family for peer-to-peer poker without a trusted dealer; it is both a scholarly text and an intended working system. [[UBA record](https://repositoriosdigitales.mincyt.gob.ar/vufind/Record/BDUBAFCEN_ea3af81f8ec1e067aeb156da6153468b)]

- **2011–2014:** *AppeCoin: Practical Anonymous Peer-to-Peer e-Cash System*. A preliminary divisible electronic-cash protocol, first revised in 2011 and subsequently issued as revision 0.28 in 2014; its revision history makes it a continuing project rather than a single fixed publication. [[paper](https://bitslog.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/appecoin28.pdf)]

- **2012:** *MAVE: New Lightweight Digital Signature Protocol for Massive Bulk Verifications*. A protocol paper proposing decentralized, high-volume signature verification. [[paper](https://bitslog.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/mave1.pdf)]

- **2012:** *MAVEPAY: A New Lightweight Payment Scheme for Peer-to-Peer Currency Networks*. A payment protocol derived from MAVE, intended to reduce the storage and verification burden of blockchain payments. [[author’s bibliography](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/User:Sergio_Demian_Lerner)]

- **2012:** *Destination Address Anonymization in Bitcoin*. A protocol proposal for concealing destination addresses in customer–merchant Bitcoin transactions. [[author’s bibliography](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/User:Sergio_Demian_Lerner)]

- **2014:** *The MinCen Protocol*. A cryptocurrency protocol intended to permit very short confirmation intervals while normally avoiding stale blocks. [[paper](https://bitslog.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/mincen_protocol-3.pdf)]

- **2014:** *Proof of Unique Blockchain Storage*. A protocol proposal—later described in the literature as an antecedent of proof of replication—for showing that a node possesses a distinct, locally transformed copy of public data. [[original article](https://bitslog.com/2014/11/03/proof-of-local-blockchain-storage/)]

- **2015:** *Proof of Unique Blockchain Storage Revised*. A substantial redesign based on what Lerner called an “asymmetric-time function”; it is best treated as a revised protocol rather than an independent invention with an unrelated title. [[Bitslog 2015 index](https://bitslog.com/2015/)]

- **2015:** *DECOR+ / DECOR++*. A family of consensus rules designed to reduce the incentive for selfish mining by changing the treatment of competing blocks. Names and versions vary across Lerner’s articles and later Rootstock documentation, so it resists treatment as one immutable paper. [[Bitslog 2015 index](https://bitslog.com/2015/)]

- **2016:** *Drivechains, Sidechains and Hybrid 2-Way Peg Designs*. A design study of mechanisms for moving assets between Bitcoin and sidechains; later scholarship cites it as an online protocol text rather than a conventionally published paper. [[bibliographic citation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/963.pdf)]

- **2022:** *Simplified State Storage Rent for EVM Blockchains*. With Federico Jinich, Diego Masini, and Shreemoy Mishra, a protocol proposal for distributing state-storage charges among transaction senders. [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13670)]

- **2023:** *Flyover: A Repayment Protocol for Fast Bitcoin Transfers over Federated Pegs*. With Javier Álvarez Cid-Fuentes and Diego Angel Masini, a repayment protocol in which liquidity providers accelerate transfers without weakening the underlying peg’s security model. [[IACR ePrint](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/086.pdf)]

- **2025:** *ESSPI: ECDSA/Schnorr Signed Program Input for BitVMX*. With Martin Jonas and Ariel Futoransky, a protocol for supplying authenticated program inputs to BitVMX; the paper introduces changes to the virtual CPU, fraud search, transaction graph, and data-availability mechanism. [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02772)]

- **2025:** *TOOP: A Transfer of Ownership Protocol over Bitcoin*. With Ariel Futoransky, Fadi Barbàra, Ramses Fernandez, and Gabriel Larotonda, a cryptographic protocol for transferring ownership of off-chain objects through Bitcoin. [[IACR record via DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/iacr/FutoranskyBFLL25)]

- **2025:** *FLEX: Capital-Efficient Optimistic Bridges with On-Demand Security Bonds for Bitcoin*. With Ariel Futoransky, a bridge-dispute protocol whose security bonds are supplied when a dispute arises rather than remaining permanently locked. [[DBLP bibliography](https://dblp.org/pid/322/3530)]

- **2025:** *WISCH: Efficient Data Signing via Correlated Signatures*. With Ariel Futoransky, Ramses Fernandez, Emilio Garcia, and Gabriel Larotonda, a protocol correlating signatures across schemes or messages to reduce on-chain verification costs. The IACR preprint appeared in 2025 and a workshop manifestation followed in 2026. [[bibliographic record](https://researchr.org/alias/ramses-fernandez)] [[WTSC 2026 program](https://fc26.ifca.ai/wtsc/program.html)]

- **2025:** *BATTLE: Bonded Adversarial TournamenT with Logarithmic Escalation*. With Ariel Futoransky, an abstract dispute-tournament protocol for handling many adversarial challengers with logarithmic escalation. It is distinct from the later Bitcoin-specific BATTLE construction. [[DBLP bibliography](https://dblp.org/pid/322/3530)]

- **2025:** *BATTLE for Bitcoin: Capital-Efficient Optimistic Bridges with Large Committees*. With Ariel Futoransky, a Bitcoin manifestation of the BATTLE design using BitVM-style components, pre-signed transaction graphs, and reusable rewards. [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06468)]

- **2025:** *OTS-PC: OTS-Based Payment Channels for the Lightning Network*. With Ariel Futoransky, a bidirectional Lightning payment-channel protocol built around one-time signatures on state sequence numbers. [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04021)]

- **2026:** *TRAPGC-DV: Trapdoor Garbled Circuit Designated Verification of Groth16 on Bitcoin*. With Ariel Futoransky, Fadi Barbàra, Ramses Fernandez, and Gabriel Larotonda, a protocol paper on designated verification of Groth16 proofs through trapdoor garbled circuits. [[DBLP bibliography](https://dblp.org/pid/322/3530)]

- **2026:** *APoW: Auditable Proof-of-Work Against Block Withholding Attacks*. A proof-of-work construction that makes portions of miners’ earlier nonce searches auditable; Lerner presents both a consensus-changing form and a more limited pool-level use. [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02496)]

### Cryptocurrencies, blockchains, and executable systems

- **2013:** *QixCoin*. A proof-of-concept cryptocurrency with a Turing-complete smart-contract system, derived from Lerner’s earlier peer-to-peer-poker project. It remained an experimental project rather than a durable public currency, and later accounts differ over whether to call it a platform, prototype, or cryptocurrency. [[IOV Labs profile](https://innovation.iovlabs.org/team/sergio-demian-lerner.html)] [[contemporaneous retrospective](https://regional-front.cointelegraph.com/interview/meet-the-bitcoin-foundations-newest-core-security-auditor-sergio-demian-lerner)]

- **2014:** *Redesigned Bitcoin Block Header*. A proposed replacement for Bitcoin’s mining header intended to address anticipated SHA-256 mining attacks and expand the nonce and timestamp fields. It is a design proposal, not an adopted Bitcoin artifact. [[article](https://bitslog.com/2014/03/18/the-re-design-of-the-bitcoin-block-header/)]

- **2015:** *DagCoin: A Cryptocurrency Without Blocks*. A cryptocurrency design in which transactions form a directed acyclic graph rather than being collected into conventional blocks. It was published as a design and draft paper, not established as a lasting production network. [[article index](https://bitslog.com/2015/)] [[draft](https://bitslog.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/dagcoin-v41.pdf)]

- **2015–2018:** *Rootstock / RSK*. A Bitcoin sidechain and smart-contract platform co-created through Rootstock Labs; Lerner authored its foundational white papers and designed substantial parts of its architecture, but the running network is necessarily a collective work and should not be attributed to him alone. [[original white-paper overview](https://rootstock.io/static/163f032d63d561e4c2fd7befe01d3e4e/rsk_white_paper-original.pdf)] [[project history](https://innovation.iovlabs.org/team/sergio-demian-lerner.html)]

- **2015:** *Ethash mining-algorithm contribution*. A component of Ethereum’s proof-of-work design credited to Lerner by his institutional biography. This is not a separately titled work, and Ethereum’s Ethash as a whole is a collective creation. [[IOV Labs profile](https://innovation.iovlabs.org/team/sergio-demian-lerner.html)]

- **2016:** *ASICBoost*. With Timo Hanke, a method for reducing repeated work in SHA-256 mining hardware. The name covers an optimization, associated patent claims, and a company, so it is not adequately described as a paper alone. [[IOV Labs profile](https://innovation.iovlabs.org/team/sergio-demian-lerner.html)]

- **2016–2026:** *RSK Improvement Proposals*. A continuing body of protocol specifications for Rootstock, including proposals that were adopted, deferred, rejected, withdrawn, or superseded. The repository—not a static collected edition—is the closest thing to a catalogue raisonné; Lerner stated that he wrote 27 proposals comprising 111 technical specifications during the pandemic alone, so this corpus resists reduction to a fixed “works list.” [[RSKIP index](https://github.com/rsksmart/RSKIPs)] [[author’s account](https://bitslog.com/2021/06/10/2021/)]

- **2022:** *RSK: A Bitcoin Sidechain with Stateful Smart-Contracts*. With Javier Álvarez Cid-Fuentes, Julian Len, Ramsès Fernàndez-València, Patricio Gallardo, Nicolás Vescovo, Raúl Laprida, Shreemoy Mishra, Federico Jinich, and Diego Masini, a retrospective technical specification of the already operating Rootstock system. It is a paper about—and a formal manifestation of—a collective protocol, not the network itself. [[DBLP record](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/iacr/LernerCLFGVLMJM22)]

- **2024–ongoing:** *BitVMX*. With Ramon Amela, Shreemoy Mishra, Martin Jonas, and Javier Álvarez Cid-Fuentes, a virtual CPU and challenge–response framework for optimistically verifying arbitrary computation through Bitcoin Script. It is simultaneously a paper, open technical framework, and continuing implementation project. [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06842)]

- **2025–ongoing:** *Union: A Trust-Minimized Bridge for Rootstock*. With Ramon Amela, Shreemoy Mishra, and Javier Álvarez Cid-Fuentes, a BitVMX-based bridge intended to move BTC between Bitcoin and Rootstock under a one-honest-participant assumption. The cited 2025 paper specifies a project whose implementation remained in progress. [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07435)]

### Security disclosures and forensic creations

- **2012:** *Bitmessage v1.0: Completely Broken Crypto*. A public cryptographic-security analysis of the original Bitmessage protocol. Its status lies between article and vulnerability disclosure: the text’s purpose was to act on a deployed design, not merely describe it. [[author’s bibliography](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/User:Sergio_Demian_Lerner)]

- **2012–2013:** *Bitcoin Core vulnerability disclosures*. Nine responsibly reported vulnerabilities are credited to Lerner; his surviving self-bibliography specifically names CVE-2012-3789, CVE-2012-4682, CVE-2012-4683, CVE-2012-4684, CVE-2013-2272, CVE-2013-2292, and CVE-2013-2293. These are discoveries and disclosure acts rather than authored “works,” and the available summary does not give nine independently stable titles. [[author’s bibliography](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/User:Sergio_Demian_Lerner)] [[institutional summary](https://innovation.iovlabs.org/team/sergio-demian-lerner.html)]

- **2013:** *The Bitcoin Transaction Fetch Memory Exhaustion Attack (TFMEA)*. A disclosure of a denial-of-service weakness in Bitcoin clients; it is an intervention in a software system rather than a freestanding protocol. [[author’s bibliography](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/User:Sergio_Demian_Lerner)]

- **2013:** *Get Your Peer Public Addresses*. A disclosure showing how peer-address information could weaken Bitcoin’s pseudonymity. [[author’s bibliography](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/User:Sergio_Demian_Lerner)]

- **2013:** *Vulnerability in Bouncy Castle ECDSA Signature Verification*. A disclosure of malformed-signature behavior capable of terminating applications using the Bouncy Castle cryptographic library. [[author’s bibliography](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/User:Sergio_Demian_Lerner)]

- **2013–2020:** *Patoshi research*. A continuing forensic body of graphs, software-assisted analyses, articles, and hypotheses concerning the dominant miner visible in Bitcoin’s earliest blocks. “Patoshi” names Lerner’s inferred mining pattern, not an identified person; the association with Satoshi Nakamoto remains disputed, and Lerner repeatedly presents the analysis as probabilistic and reproducible rather than certain. [[2013 archive](https://bitslog.com/2013/04/)] [[2019 reassessment](https://bitslog.com/2019/04/16/the-return-of-the-deniers-and-the-revenge-of-patoshi/)] [[2020 follow-up](https://bitslog.com/2020/06/22/a-new-mystery-in-patoshi-timestamps/)]

- **2014:** *The Bitcoin Freeze on Transaction Attack (FRONT)*. A security-analysis article describing a transaction-level attack on Bitcoin. Like a vulnerability report, it resists classification as an ordinary scholarly work because its intended effect was defensive action. [[Bitslog archive](https://bitslog.com/page/3/)]

### Patent texts

- **2009:** *Method and Apparatus for Efficient and Secure Creating, Transferring, and Revealing of Messages over a Network*. A patent specification for real-time cryptographically secured card play over a network, closely related to MPF. The United States and PCT/European filings are legal manifestations of the same invention, not separate obras. [[author’s patent list](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/User:Sergio_Demian_Lerner)]

- **2013–2014:** *Method and Apparatus for Secure Interaction with a Computer Service Provider*. With Victor Suarez Rovere, a patent application for authenticating interaction with a service provider through a controlled computing environment. Google Patents labels the PCT case “ceased,” while cautioning that its legal-status field is not a legal conclusion. [[Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2014145417A4/en)]
