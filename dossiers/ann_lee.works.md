## Works by Ann Lee

### Oral manifesto and protocol

- **c. 1770–1784:** *The testimony of Mother Ann*. An orally delivered manifesto and evolving body of doctrine centred on celibacy, confession of sins, pacifism, communal discipline, and salvation from sinful conduct. Lee recorded none of these teachings herself: they survive through followers’ recollections collected decades later, so neither their wording nor their division into individual “texts” can securely be treated as hers ([1816/1888 *Testimonies*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/dc/Books_from_the_Library_of_Congress_%28IA_testimoniesoflif00bish_0%29.pdf); [Zaborowski](https://openworks.wooster.edu/independentstudy/4067/)).

- **c. 1770–1784:** teachings on explicit confession. A repeatedly administered oral protocol requiring believers to disclose and renounce their sins before designated witnesses; the later compilers say Lee and the elders taught it continually, but do not preserve a single authorial text or inaugural date ([*Testimonies*, ch. XXVIII](https://passtheword.org/shaker-manuscripts/Testimonies/tstmnyx2.htm)).

- **c. 1770–1784:** sayings, exhortations, rebukes, parables, prayers, prophecies, visions, and revelations attributed to Mother Ann. This is a dispersed oral corpus rather than a set of separately titled works. Its principal record, *Testimonies of the Life, Character, Revelations and Doctrines of Mother Ann Lee, and the Elders with Her*, was collected from “eye and ear witnesses” by Rufus Bishop in 1812, revised by Seth Youngs Wells, and published in 1816; it is therefore a posthumous collective testimony, not a book authored by Lee ([Shaker Museum account](https://shakerml.wordpress.com/2017/03/29/the-testimonies-of-the-life-character-revelations-and-doctrines-of-our-ever-blessed-mother-ann-lee-and-the-elders-with-her/); [bibliographic record and scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Books_from_the_Library_of_Congress_(IA_testimoniesoflif00bish_0).pdf)).

### Song and embodied worship

- **1783, traditionally dated:** *Mother Ann’s Song*. A short wordless sacred song or dance tune attributed to Lee in later Shaker musical transmission. Its familiar title is an archival rubric rather than a title demonstrably supplied by Lee, and the surviving source is later than the alleged composition; the attribution and exact date should therefore be treated as traditional, not autographically established ([Boston Camerata, manuscript-source note](https://bostoncamerata.org/originalSite/cd/titles/shakercd.htm); [Roger Lee Hall, *A Shaker Music Miscellany* contents](https://www.americanmusicpreservation.com/shakermusicmiscellanytunes.htm)).

- **c. 1770–1784:** untitled songs, chants, dances, and bodily “gifts” used in worship. Contemporary recollection makes singing, shaking, marching, and dancing inseparable from Lee’s ministry, but no dependable catalogue assigns individual surviving pieces or fixed choreographies to her. These manifestations resist being called discrete works: they were collective, improvised religious actions rather than stable musical or choreographic texts ([Boston Camerata, *The American Vocalist*: Shaker songs](https://www.bostoncamerata.org/bucfdiaSite/bcbook-shakersongs.html); [*Testimonies*, “Prophecies, Visions and Revelations”](https://www.passtheword.org/SHAKER-MANUSCRIPTS/Testimonies/tstmnyx1.htm)).

### Communal and religious project

- **1774–1784:** the American Shaker gathering and settlement at Niskayuna/Watervliet. A collective religious and social project led by Lee rather than a sole-authored object: settlement, missionary journeys, celibate communal practice, worship, labor, and oral teaching together formed the intended work. It can be understood as a Gesamtkunstwerk only cautiously, because later institutional Shakerism systematized practices that were still fluid during Lee’s lifetime ([National Park Service biography](https://www.nps.gov/people/ann-lee.htm); [*Testimonies*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/dc/Books_from_the_Library_of_Congress_%28IA_testimoniesoflif00bish_0%29.pdf)).

- **1780–1783:** missionary “opening of the gospel” in New York and New England. A performed evangelical project carried out collectively with William Lee, James Whittaker, and other elders through journeys, meetings, preaching, healing claims, song, and bodily worship. It produced converts and communities rather than an authored travel narrative or sermon collection; the surviving account is retrospective and sometimes apologetic ([*Testimonies*, table of contents and narrative](https://books.google.com/books/about/Testimonies_of_the_Life_Character_Revela.html?id=mqKPE72MIFYC); [historical assessment of the recollection-based source](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=osu148666166298673&disposition=attachment)).

### Works not securely attributable

- **1816:** *Testimonies of the Life, Character, Revelations and Doctrines of Mother Ann Lee, and the Elders with Her*. Not a work made by Lee, despite catalogues that sometimes place it under her name: Rufus Bishop collected it from living witnesses in 1812, Seth Youngs Wells revised it, and the Shaker church published it thirty-two years after her death ([Shaker Museum](https://shakerml.wordpress.com/2017/03/29/the-testimonies-of-the-life-character-revelations-and-doctrines-of-our-ever-blessed-mother-ann-lee-and-the-elders-with-her/); [1888 edition metadata](https://books.google.com/books/about/Testimonies_of_the_Life_Character_Revela.html?id=mqKPE72MIFYC)).

- **1827:** *Testimonies Concerning the Character and Ministry of Mother Ann Lee*. Not Lee’s work but Seth Youngs Wells’s later compilation about her. Its presence in bibliographies under Lee’s name records her subjecthood and posthumous authority, not her authorship ([bibliographic discussion](https://openworks.wooster.edu/independentstudy/4067/)).

- **No securely documented item:** autograph books, letters, diaries, treatises, printed sermons, drawings, buildings, furniture, or other individually designed material objects. The collected testimony and subsequent scholarship explicitly place Lee’s teachings in oral transmission; absence from this list therefore reflects the evidentiary record, not an assumption that religious speech, movement, and community-making were not creative acts ([Zaborowski](https://openworks.wooster.edu/independentstudy/4067/); [*Testimonies* bibliographic history](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Books_from_the_Library_of_Congress_(IA_testimoniesoflif00bish_0).pdf)).
