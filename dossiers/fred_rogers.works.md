## Works by Fred McFeely Rogers

### Television Gesamtkunstwerke and series

- **1954–1961:** *The Children’s Corner*. Live children’s television series co-created with host Josie Carey; Rogers composed music, wrote and produced material, operated and voiced puppets, and developed figures later absorbed into the Neighborhood of Make-Believe. Because it was collaborative and much of it was broadcast live, neither sole authorship nor a complete surviving work can be claimed. [1][2]
- **1963–1966:** *Misterogers*. Fifteen-minute CBC television series created and hosted by Rogers, combining direct address, music, puppetry, and the Neighbourhood of Make-Believe; surviving records are incomplete, and sources differ by a year over whether broadcasts began in 1962 or 1963. [1][3]
- **1966–1967:** *Misterogers’ Neighborhood*. Regional WQED adaptation of the Canadian program, incorporating Canadian kinescopes into newly produced material; it is both an intermediary work and an unfinished transformation into the national series. [1][3]
- **1968–1976; 1979–2001:** *Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood*. A 895-episode television Gesamtkunstwerk created, written, hosted, executive-produced, performed and musically composed by Rogers, but materially realized by a large continuing company of directors, musicians, actors, designers and technicians. Individual installments usually have production numbers rather than stable authorial titles, so the series—not a fabricated list of episode “titles”—is the defensible obra. [1][2][4]
- **1977–1982:** *Christmastime with Mister Rogers*. Annual television special or rebroadcast package assembled from Neighborhood material; it is better treated as a seasonal manifestation of the larger television work than as six wholly independent Rogers creations. [1]
- **1978–1980:** *Old Friends … New Friends*. Twenty-part interview-documentary series created and hosted by Rogers during the production hiatus of *Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood*, concerning adult lives and searches for meaning. Most episodes are not presently in ordinary circulation, so the work survives publicly only in fragments. [1][5]
- **1994:** *Fred Rogers’ Heroes*. Television documentary special created and hosted by Rogers, presenting people whose service to others embodied his idea of heroism. [1]
- **1997:** *Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood: Then & Now*. Retrospective television special built from the series and Rogers’s commentary; a self-reflexive manifestation of the Neighborhood rather than an ordinary episode. [1]
- **2001:** *Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood*, program 1765. The final newly produced visit and intentional closure of the series, taped before the production ended in December 2000 and broadcast in 2001. It remains part of the serial obra rather than a separately titled film. [1][4]
- **2002:** *Fred Rogers: America’s Favorite Neighbor*. PBS retrospective hosted by Rogers and Michael Keaton; Rogers participated in making and presenting it, but it is not solely authored by him. [1]

### Continuing project and institution

- **1971–2003:** *Family Communications, Inc.* Rogers’s nonprofit production organization: an institutional project made to sustain children’s media, publications and public service rather than an “artwork” in the narrow sense. Its collective products cannot properly be assigned to Rogers alone merely because he founded and led it. [4]
- **1954–2003:** *The Neighborhood of Make-Believe*. A continuing fictional world made across broadcasts, scripts, songs, puppets, scenery, books and recordings; it resists separation into a single text and is best understood as a transmedia creation within Rogers’s larger Gesamtkunstwerk. [2][4]
- **1954–2001:** Daniel Striped Tiger, King Friday XIII, Queen Sara Saturday, X the Owl, Henrietta Pussycat, Lady Elaine Fairchilde and Rogers’s other puppet personae. These are recurring performed creations rather than freestanding literary works; dates of individual characters’ first appearances vary, and fabrication of the physical puppets was collaborative. [2][4]

### Children’s books and picture-book works

- **1954:** *Our Small World*. Children’s book made with Josie Carey and illustrated by Norb Nathanson; a companion creation of *The Children’s Corner*. [1]
- **1973:** *The Elves, the Shoemaker, & the Shoemaker’s Wife*. Illustrated children’s story published by Rogers’s Small World Enterprises. [1]
- **1973:** *The Matter of the Mittens*. Children’s book derived from the characters and dramatic world of Rogers’s television work. [1]
- **1973:** *Speedy Delivery*. Illustrated children’s book centered on the Mr. McFeely persona and the Neighborhood’s delivery motif. [1]
- **1974:** *Henrietta Meets Someone New*. Picture book using Henrietta Pussycat and the Neighborhood of Make-Believe. [1]
- **1974:** *Mister Rogers Talks About*. Children’s explanatory book; the broad title names a packaged educational text rather than a single fictional narrative. [1]
- **1974:** *Time to Be Friends*. Illustrated children’s book about friendship. [1]
- **1975:** *Everyone Is Special*. Illustrated children’s book extending one of Rogers’s recurrent broadcast themes. [1]
- **1975:** *Tell Me, Mister Rogers*. Question-and-answer children’s book shaped as direct communication between Rogers and young readers. [1]
- **1976:** *The Costume Party*. Illustrated Neighborhood story. [1]
- **1986:** *Planet Purple*. Illustrated electronic-book/software title issued for the Texas Instruments learning system; neither simply a printed book nor simply software. [1]
- **1987:** *If We Were All the Same*. Illustrated children’s book about difference and individuality. [1]
- **1987:** *A Trolley Visit to Make-Believe*. Picture-book manifestation of the television journey between Rogers’s house and Make-Believe. [1]
- **1987:** *Wishes Don’t Make Things Come True*. Illustrated children’s book distinguishing fantasy from action. [1]
- **1988:** *No One Can Ever Take Your Place*. Illustrated children’s book on individuality and belonging. [1]
- **1988:** *When Monsters Seem Real*. Illustrated children’s book for managing frightening fantasy. [1]
- **1988:** *You Can Never Go Down the Drain*. Illustrated children’s book developed from Rogers’s song and broadcast reassurance; it is therefore also a cross-media manifestation. [1]
- **2000:** *The Giving Box*. Illustrated book-and-activity object designed to establish a family practice of charitable giving; its box and ritual instructions make it partly a protocol rather than only a text. [1]

### Let’s Talk About It: family discussion protocols

- **1977:** *Going to the Hospital*. Family Communications booklet for talking with children about hospitalization; it is distinct from the 1988 *First Experiences* book of the same title. [1]
- **1977:** *Having an Operation*. Explanatory family booklet intended to prepare a child for surgery. [1]
- **1977:** *So Many Things to See!*. Explanatory booklet in the early *Let’s Talk About It* group. [1]
- **1977:** *Wearing a Cast*. Explanatory family booklet about injury and medical treatment. [1]
- **1993:** *Adoption*. Illustrated discussion book intended to help adults and children talk about adoption. [1]
- **1994:** *Divorce*. Illustrated discussion book intended to help families name and discuss separation and divorce. [1]
- **2000:** *Extraordinary Friends*. Illustrated discussion book about children with disabilities and relationships across difference. [1]
- **2001:** *Stepfamilies*. Illustrated discussion book about forming and living in a stepfamily. [1]

### Books and practical texts for adults

- **1983:** *Mister Rogers Talks to Parents*. Parenting book translating Rogers’s child-development practice into guidance for adults. [1]
- **1986:** *Mister Rogers’ Playbook*. Activity book made with Barry Head and illustrated by Jamie Adams; its games and exercises make it a protocol for shared adult-child play. [1]
- **1987:** *Mister Rogers Talks with Families About Divorce*. Parenting and family-discussion guide made with Clare O’Brien. [1]
- **1988:** *Mister Rogers’ How Families Grow*. Parenting and activity book made with Barry Head and Jim Prokell. [1]
- **1994:** *You Are Special: Words of Wisdom from America’s Most Beloved Neighbor*. Thematic collection of Rogers’s reflections; it is an authorized compilation rather than a continuous newly composed argument. [1]
- **1996:** *Dear Mister Rogers*. Selected correspondence and responses; the object is collaborative in a literal sense because children’s letters constitute part of its text. [1]
- **2001:** *Mister Rogers’ Playtime*. Adult-child activity book and practical play protocol. [1]
- **2002:** *The Mister Rogers Parenting Book*. Parenting reference work assembled around Rogers’s guidance on emotional and developmental subjects. [1]
- **2002:** *You Are Special: Neighborly Wisdom from Mister Rogers*. Short thematic collection; its relationship to the 1994 book of nearly the same name is that of a repackaged selection, not necessarily a wholly new obra. [1]
- **2003:** *The World According to Mister Rogers: Important Things to Remember*. Collection of sayings and short reflections prepared for publication around the time of Rogers’s death; because the book is editorially assembled, the individual words are Rogers’s but the compilation should not be mistaken for a continuous manuscript authored as such. [1]

### Songbooks

- **1960:** *Tomorrow on the Children’s Corner*. Songbook made with Josie Carey and illustrated by Mal Wittman, preserving musical material from the television series in print. [1]
- **1970:** *Mister Rogers’ Songbook*. Printed collection of Rogers compositions, prepared with Johnny Costa and illustrated by Steven Kellogg. [1]

### Albums and recorded musical works

- **1958:** *Around the Children’s Corner*. Album made with Josie Carey from songs, stories and characters of the television program. [1]
- **1959:** *Tomorrow on the Children’s Corner*. Recorded album made with Josie Carey; it is distinct from, though closely related to, the 1960 printed songbook. [1]
- **1964:** *King Friday XIII Celebrates*. Children’s narrative-and-song recording centered on Rogers’s puppet king. [1]
- **1967:** *Won’t You Be My Neighbor?*. Album of Rogers’s songs and performances associated with the emerging Neighborhood format. [1]
- **1968:** *Let’s Be Together Today*. Children’s album of songs composed and performed by Rogers. [1]
- **1969:** *Josephine the Short-Neck Giraffe*. Recorded musical story or children’s opera written and performed within Rogers’s imaginative world. [1]
- **1969:** *You Are Special*. Album of Rogers’s children’s songs. [1]
- **1970:** *A Place of Our Own*. Album combining song, dramatic character and Neighborhood themes. [1]
- **1972:** *Come On and Wake Up*. Children’s music album. [1]
- **1992:** *Growing*. Album of songs addressing childhood development; some catalogues style the title *You’re Growing*, so the title is not entirely stable across manifestations. [1][6]
- **1992:** *Bedtime*. Album of songs and spoken material for the transition to sleep. [1][6]
- **1994:** *Won’t You Be My Neighbor?*. Cassette-and-book package; a new mixed-media manifestation of Rogers’s musical repertoire rather than an entirely new composition. [1]
- **1997:** *Coming and Going*. Album addressing arrivals, departures and separation. [1][6]

### Songs, scores and musical dramas without a complete public catalogue

- **1954–2001:** *Music for The Children’s Corner, Misterogers and Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood*. A body of hundreds of songs, transitions, dramatic cues and operatic sequences composed across nearly five decades. The Fred Rogers Archive states that Rogers composed all the music for *Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood*, but no complete authoritative public thematic catalogue was found; naming only the commercially issued albums avoids inventing a supposedly complete song list. [2]
- **1960s–1980s:** the Make-Believe operas. Rogers wrote and staged recurring television operas—including *Josephine the Short-Neck Giraffe*, *Spoon Mountain*, *All in the Laundry*, *Windstorm in Bubbleland*, *Potato Bugs and Cows*, *Key to Otherland* and *A Granddad for Daniel*—as compound works of song, puppetry, studio performance and serial fiction. Dates and even counts vary with whether revisions and restagings are treated as new operas, so this corpus resists a falsely precise work-list. [4][7]
- **1967:** *Won’t You Be My Neighbor?*. Theme song for Rogers’s television visits; simultaneously a composition, ritual opening and invitation that acts upon the viewer, making it closer to a small musical manifesto than incidental music. [2][6]
- **1967:** *It’s Such a Good Feeling*. Closing song and repeated leave-taking protocol for the television program. [2][6]
- **1968:** *What Do You Do with the Mad That You Feel?*. Song about converting anger into non-destructive action; Rogers also performed it during his 1969 Senate testimony, where it functioned as argument and public advocacy as well as music. [8]
- **1969:** *Tree, Tree, Tree*. Rogers composition later issued independently and in posthumous compilations; those later releases are manifestations, not new works made by Rogers. [2][6]
- **1970:** *Sometimes People Are Good*. Rogers composition about contradictory human behavior. [2][6]
- **1970:** *Everybody’s Fancy*. Rogers composition addressing bodily difference and self-acceptance. [2][6]
- **1970:** *You Can Never Go Down the Drain*. Rogers composition later remade as a picture book. [2][6]
- **1970:** *You Are Special*. Rogers composition whose title and idea recur across albums and books; these manifestations should not be collapsed into one bibliographic object. [1][2]
- **1970:** *There Are Many Ways (to Say I Love You)*. Rogers composition presenting care as action rather than mere declaration. [2][6]
- **1970:** *Please Don’t Think It’s Funny*. Rogers composition about fear and the injury caused by ridicule. [2][6]
- **1970:** *I Like to Be Told*. Rogers composition about a child’s need for truthful preparation. [2][6]
- **1970:** *Then Your Heart Is Full of Love*. Rogers composition linking music, feeling and communication. [2][6]

### Public-address texts that act

- **1969:** statement to the United States Senate Subcommittee on Communications. Televised advocacy for continued public-broadcasting support; the statement is a manifesto in the strict sense that it sought and helped produce a material governmental decision, rather than merely describing Rogers’s television practice. [8]
- **1984:** testimony in *Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios*. Supreme Court testimony defending household videotaping of his programs; a juridical intervention and protocol of intended use rather than an entertainment work. [9]
- **1997:** Television Academy Hall of Fame/lifetime-achievement address. Ceremonial speech structured around remembered care and gratitude; it became a public text through broadcast and recording rather than through an authorial book. [10]
- **2001:** Middlebury College commencement address. Ceremonial spoken text asking listeners to recognize those who helped them become themselves; its primary object was the live address, with transcripts and videos as later manifestations. [11]
- **2002:** Dartmouth College commencement address. Rogers’s final commencement address, a ceremonial and ethical exhortation whose circulating video and transcript are manifestations of the event. [12]
- **2002:** Presidential Medal of Freedom response. Brief ceremonial public statement; it resists elevation into a freestanding “work,” but belongs to the record of texts Rogers deliberately made for public action. [13]

### Exhibition project

- **1998:** *Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood* children’s-museum exhibit. Interactive exhibition created by Rogers and Family Communications for the Children’s Museum of Pittsburgh, later circulated to other museums; it is an environmental and educational project, not a book or television episode, and its authorship is institutional and collaborative. [1]

### Posthumous manifestations—not independent works made by Rogers

- **2005:** *Life’s Journeys According to Mister Rogers*. Posthumous editorial compilation from Rogers’s words; a manifestation of existing material, not a book he completed after death. [1]
- **2005:** *The Mister Rogers Parenting Resource Book*. Posthumous reorganization of parenting material; the compilation’s title and arrangement belong to its editors and publisher as well as to Rogers’s underlying words. [1]
- **2005:** *Good Weather or Not*. Posthumously published children’s book credited jointly to Rogers and Hedda Bluestone Sharapan; its publication date cannot be treated as a composition date. [1]
- **2006:** *Josephine the Short-Neck Giraffe*. Posthumous book manifestation of Rogers’s earlier musical story, not a newly made 2006 Rogers work. [1]
- **2019:** *A Beautiful Day in the Neighborhood: The Poetry of Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood*. Illustrated editorial selection of song texts by Rogers; the book as object was made after his death, while its constituent compositions belong to the earlier television oeuvre. [1]
- **2019:** *Many Ways to Say I Love You: Wisdom for Parents and Children*. Posthumous compilation and reissue of Rogers’s parenting reflections, not an independently completed late manuscript. [1]
- **2019:** *It’s Such a Good Feeling: The Best of Mister Rogers*. Posthumous historical compilation assembled by Omnivore Recordings from earlier performances; Rogers made the recordings and compositions, but not this album’s selection and sequence. [1][6]
- **2023:** *Back in the Neighborhood: The Best of Mister Rogers, Volume 2*. Posthumous compilation of earlier recordings; a new discographic manifestation made by later producers, not by Rogers himself. [6]
