## Works by Marcel Proust

### Composite book-objects and collections

- **1896:** *Les Plaisirs et les Jours*. A deliberately heterogeneous book-object—stories, prose poems, portraits, and verse—made with Madeleine Lemaire’s illustrations, Reynaldo Hahn’s four piano pieces, and Anatole France’s preface; it approaches a collaborative *Gesamtkunstwerk* rather than a novel by Proust alone. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [1896 edition and contents](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Les_Plaisirs_et_les_Jours))

- **1919; enlarged 1921:** *Pastiches et mélanges*. An authored collection of previously published pastiches, criticism, commemorations, and essays, including the multi-voiced *Affaire Lemoine* sequence; its contents pre-existed the book and overlap with Proust’s Ruskin prefaces. ([1921 edition and contents](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Pastiches_et_M%C3%A9langes); [Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

### Translations, commentaries, and critical book-objects

- **1904:** *La Bible d’Amiens*. Proust’s French translation of John Ruskin’s book, accompanied by an extensive preface, notes, and supplementary material that amount to an independent critical creation; his mother Jeanne Proust and Marie Nordlinger helped him overcome his limited English, so “translation by Proust” conceals collaborative mediation. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [Kolb-Proust bibliography](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/proust/biblio/))

- **1906:** *Sésame et les Lys*. Translation of two Ruskin lectures with notes and the substantial preface later known as *Sur la lecture* or *Journées de lecture*; it is simultaneously translation, commentary, and an essay on reading. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [Kolb-Proust bibliography](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/proust/biblio/))

### Abandoned and unfinished narrative projects

- **1895–1899, approximately:** *Jean Santeuil*. An abandoned novel project surviving in discontinuous manuscript sections; the title, sequence, and apparent unity of the “novel” were imposed by Bernard de Fallois for the 1952 publication, so the printed work resists identification with a finished book made by Proust. ([Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm); [Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust))

- **1907–1908:** *Les Soixante-Quinze Feuillets*. An early narrative project underlying *À la recherche du temps perdu*, preserved on seventy-six rather than seventy-five folios and recovered from Bernard de Fallois’s papers in 2018; its familiar title is an archival/editorial designation, not a title fixed by Proust. ([Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/marcel-proust-les-soixante-quinze-feuillets-gallimard-rattraper-le-temps-perdu); [Le Monde](https://www.lemonde.fr/livres/article/2021/04/08/les-soixante-quinze-feuillets-marcel-proust-en-petites-pelotes_6075982_3260.html))

- **1908–1909, with related material before and after:** *Dossier du Contre Sainte-Beuve*. An unfinished, unstable project combining criticism of Sainte-Beuve, autobiographical conversation, essay, and developing fiction. *Contre Sainte-Beuve* as published in 1954 was an editorial construction by Bernard de Fallois; the 2022 Pléiade therefore replaces that artificial unity with a genetic “dossier.” ([2022 Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm); [Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust))

### The novel-project and its manifestations

- **c. 1908–1922:** *À la recherche du temps perdu*. A single continually expanding novel-project, left incompletely revised at Proust’s death; its seven customary volumes are publication units and textual manifestations rather than seven wholly independent novels. The manuscripts, notebooks, typescripts, proofs, and added *paperolles* form a material “work within the work.” ([Tadié Pléiade description](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm); [BnF-manuscript account](https://www.lemonde.fr/livres/article/2022/11/18/a-la-recherche-de-proust-cent-ans-apres_6150382_3260.html))

- **1913:** *Du côté de chez Swann*. First published manifestation of the novel-project, containing *Combray*, *Un amour de Swann*, and *Noms de pays : le nom*. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust))

- **1919:** *À l’ombre des jeunes filles en fleurs*. Second published manifestation, extensively enlarged through corrected proofs and *paperolles*; it received the Prix Goncourt. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [BnF-manuscript account](https://www.lemonde.fr/livres/article/2022/11/18/a-la-recherche-de-proust-cent-ans-apres_6150382_3260.html))

- **1920–1921:** *Le Côté de Guermantes*. Third manifestation, issued in two separately published parts. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

- **1921–1922:** *Sodome et Gomorrhe*. Fourth manifestation; an initial section appeared with *Le Côté de Guermantes II*, followed by two volumes, making its original bibliographic boundaries unusually complicated. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

- **written and revised through 1922; published 1923:** *La Prisonnière*. Posthumous fifth manifestation, prepared for publication after Proust’s death; it was comparatively advanced but had not received an authorially final text. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

- **written and revised through 1922; published 1925:** *Albertine disparue* / *La Fugitive*. Posthumous sixth manifestation, surviving in incompatible states and under competing titles; a late, drastically shortened typescript discovered in 1986 makes completeness and authorial finality irreducibly disputed. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

- **written through 1922; published 1927:** *Le Temps retrouvé*. Posthumous final manifestation; its broad architecture was established early, but substantial passages remained unrevised when Proust died. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust); [Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

### Independently published short fiction and recovered creations

- **1896:** *L’Indifférent*. A short story published in *La Vie contemporaine*, omitted from *Les Plaisirs et les Jours*, forgotten, and rediscovered in the twentieth century; it is a separate periodical creation rather than an original component of the 1896 book. ([Kolb-Proust bibliographic file](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/fr/guide/bibliof/); [Pléiade description](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *Pauline de S.* An unpublished youthful story apparently withheld while Proust assembled *Les Plaisirs et les Jours*; its date is not securely fixed. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651); [University of Strasbourg](https://recherche.unistra.fr/actualites-recherche/actualites-de-la-recherche/du-cote-des-inedits-retrouves-de-proust.html))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *Le Mystérieux Correspondant*. A youthful short story recovered from the Fallois archive; its posthumous title also became the editor’s title for the containing volume. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *Souvenir d’un capitaine*. A youthful short narrative excluded from Proust’s first collection and first printed posthumously. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *Jacques Lefelde (L’Étranger)*. A recovered youthful narrative whose double title reflects manuscript or editorial uncertainty rather than a securely fixed published title. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *Aux Enfers*. A recovered dialogue of the dead, one of several formally distinct experiments that Proust declined to include in his first book. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651); [University of Strasbourg](https://recherche.unistra.fr/actualites-recherche/actualites-de-la-recherche/du-cote-des-inedits-retrouves-de-proust.html))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *Après la 8e symphonie de Beethoven*. A recovered prose response to music, poised between short fiction, reverie, and criticism. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *La Conscience de l’aimer*. A recovered youthful short story; no secure composition date was supplied by Proust. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *Le Don des fées*. A recovered fairy tale, demonstrating that the rejected manuscripts were not a single uniform story-cycle. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651); [University of Strasbourg](https://recherche.unistra.fr/actualites-recherche/actualites-de-la-recherche/du-cote-des-inedits-retrouves-de-proust.html))

- **1890s; published 2019:** *« C’est ainsi qu’il avait aimé. »* A recovered narrative identified by its opening or manuscript wording rather than by a demonstrably final authorial title. ([2019 collection contents](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=63651))

### Essays, criticism, journalism, pastiche, and public interventions

- **1882–1889:** *Écrits de jeunesse*. School compositions, early literary attempts, and other juvenilia now treated by the 2022 Pléiade as a chronological corpus, not as a book Proust designed. ([2022 Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

- **1890–1893:** *Écrits de lycée, de revue et de salon*. Essays, reviews, literary pieces, and contributions to such collective ventures as *Le Banquet*; this is an editorial corpus rather than one authorial obra. ([2022 Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm); [Kolb-Proust bibliographic file](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/fr/guide/bibliof/))

- **1894–1899:** *Essais, articles, portraits, poems, and occasional texts*. A dispersed body of periodical creations, including art and literary criticism; the writings were individual interventions before later editors gathered them. ([2022 Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm); [Kolb-Proust bibliographic file](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/fr/guide/bibliof/))

- **c. 1895:** *Chardin et Rembrandt*. An unfinished or unpublished art-critical essay surviving in manuscript; the now-current title joins material concerning two painters and should not be mistaken for an authorially issued book. ([2022 Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

- **1900–1911:** *Ruskinian, artistic, political, memorial, and literary writings*. A dispersed corpus of essays, prefaces, newspaper articles, and open interventions—including Proust’s writings on threatened churches, the Dreyfus affair, salons, artists, and writers—established comprehensively in the 2022 Pléiade rather than made as one book. ([2022 Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm); [Kolb-Proust bibliographic file](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/fr/guide/bibliof/))

- **1908–1909:** *L’Affaire Lemoine*. A sequence of pastiches recasting one contemporary fraud in the manners of Balzac, Flaubert, Sainte-Beuve, Henri de Régnier, the Goncourts, Michelet, Émile Faguet, Renan, and Saint-Simon; it is one generative project manifested as multiple counterfeit authorial voices. ([1921 contents](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Pastiches_et_M%C3%A9langes); [Larousse](https://www.larousse.fr/encyclopedie/oeuvre/Pastiches_et_M%C3%A9langes/181683))

- **1912–1918:** *Essays and articles of the early Recherche period*. Reviews, interviews, wartime writings, prepublications, and public statements collected chronologically by the 2022 Pléiade; the grouping is editorial, while the constituent texts are separate manifestations. ([2022 Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm); [Kolb-Proust bibliographic file](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/fr/guide/bibliof/))

- **1918–1922:** *Late essays, interviews, prefaces, open letters, and prepublications*. A corpus of occasional and public writing produced alongside the later volumes of the novel; it includes excerpts from the novel-project, which are manifestations rather than additional works. ([2022 Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm); [Kolb-Proust bibliographic file](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/fr/guide/bibliof/))

- **1936:** *Chroniques*. A posthumous editorial collection of Proust’s previously dispersed journalism and criticism. It is useful as a bibliographic container but resists being called a work Proust made, because he neither chose its contents nor gave it this form. ([Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust))

### Personal protocols, correspondence, and working archive

- **c. 1886 and c. 1890:** *Responses to confession albums (“Questionnaire de Proust”)*. Two principal sets of answers to a pre-existing social protocol, not questionnaires invented by Proust; their later eponymous title transformed private performances into a quasi-work. ([Kolb-Proust bibliography](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/kolbproust/proust/biblio/); [questionnaire history](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questionnaire_de_Proust))

- **c. 1880–1922:** *Correspondance*. Thousands of letters forming a vast epistolary practice rather than a bounded work; Philip Kolb’s twenty-one-volume chronological edition is a scholarly reconstruction, and later claims of “complete correspondence” remain qualified because letters continue to surface and some groups have been omitted. ([Corr-Proust bibliography](https://proust.elan-numerique.fr/guide/bibliography); [Pléiade catalogue account](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/PROUST.nG.htm))

- **principally 1908–1922:** *Carnets, Cahiers 1–75, notebooks, typescripts, proofs, and paperolles*. The material writing apparatus from which *Contre Sainte-Beuve* and the *Recherche* emerged: not seventy-five autonomous books, but neither disposable residue nor merely preparatory matter. Genetic scholarship treats this mobile, cross-referenced archive as a corpus whose boundaries and internal “texts” resist ordinary work-cataloguing. ([ITEM critical edition](https://www.item.ens.fr/edition-des-cahiers/); [Société des Amis de Marcel Proust](https://www.amisdeproust.fr/ressources/oeuvres-de-proust))
