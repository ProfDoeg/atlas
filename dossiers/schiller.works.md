## Works by Friedrich Schiller

### Dramatic works

- **1776–1777:** *Die Christen*. Lost school drama; its title and subject survive only through testimony, so the obra itself is no longer recoverable. [NA 43]
- **1779:** *Der Student von Nassau*. Lost dramatic project known from documentary evidence rather than a surviving text. [NA 43]
- **1777–1781:** *Die Räuber*. Five-act tragedy, first printed in 1781 and first staged in 1782; Schiller produced substantially different reading and Mannheim stage versions. [NA 3]
- **1782–1783:** *Die Verschwörung des Fiesko zu Genua*. Republican tragedy whose manuscript, first printing, and Mannheim stage form represent materially different versions rather than mere reprints. [NA 4]
- **1782–1784:** *Luise Millerin / Kabale und Liebe*. Bourgeois tragedy conceived as *Luise Millerin* and published under Iffland’s suggested title *Kabale und Liebe*. [NA 5]
- **c. 1780–1782:** *Semele*. Short mythological drama from Schiller’s early period; complete enough to be performed, although not published by him as an independent work. [NA 5]
- **1784–1785:** *Der versöhnte Menschenfeind*. Unfinished comedy, also transmitted under the character-name title *Der Menschenfeind*; it resists classification as a finished play. [NA 5]
- **1787:** *Körners Vormittag*. Comic occasional scene made for Christian Gottfried Körner’s birthday; a private theatrical object rather than a conventional public drama. [NA 5]
- **1783–1787; revised to 1805:** *Don Karlos, Infant von Spanien*. Historical tragedy existing as *Thalia* fragments, a five-act reading version, Hamburg and Riga stage versions, and a final 1805 edition; these forms cannot be reduced to one stable text. [NA 6–7]
- **1796–1799:** *Wallenstein*. Dramatic Gesamtkunstwerk-like trilogy comprising a theatrical prologue and two tragedies, repeatedly reshaped between performance and print. [NA 8]
- **1798:** *Wallensteins Lager*. Verse prologue or first part of *Wallenstein*, designed as a large ensemble spectacle. [NA 8]
- **1798–1799:** *Die Piccolomini*. Dramatic middle panel of *Wallenstein*, ending without independent resolution. [NA 8]
- **1799:** *Wallensteins Tod*. Concluding tragedy of the *Wallenstein* complex. [NA 8]
- **1799–1800:** *Maria Stuart*. Five-act historical tragedy about the last days of Mary Stuart; completed, staged, and printed in 1800. [NA 9.1]
- **1800–1801:** *Die Jungfrau von Orleans*. “Romantic tragedy” based on Joan of Arc but deliberately departing from historical record. [NA 9.2]
- **1801–1803:** *Die Braut von Messina oder Die feindlichen Brüder*. Tragedy with choruses, conceived as an experiment in joining modern theatre to Greek tragic form. [NA 10]
- **1803–1804:** *Wilhelm Tell*. Historical drama whose communal action, landscape, political argument, and private revenge plots make it more than a single-hero tragedy. [NA 10]
- **1804:** *Die Huldigung der Künste*. Festival play or theatrical homage made for the arrival of Maria Pavlovna in Weimar; an occasional manifestation combining declamation, allegorical figures, music, and staging. [NA 10]
- **1804–1805:** *Demetrius*. Unfinished historical tragedy left as scenes, drafts, and extensive plans; the project, rather than a completed play, is the surviving obra. [NA 11]

### Dramatic projects and fragments

- **c. 1782–1783:** *Die Braut in Trauer*. Dramatic fragment or plan; no finished play exists. [NA 12]
- **1788–1791:** *Die Malteser*. Large tragic project about the siege of Malta, surviving through plans and fragmentary scenes composed at different times. [NA 12]
- **c. 1799–1800:** *Die Polizei*. Unfinished dramatic project concerned with state surveillance and intrigue. [NA 12]
- **c. 1800:** *Die Kinder des Hauses*. Unfinished historical-drama project. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801:** *Agrippina*. Roman historical-tragedy project preserved in notes and sketches rather than a completed text. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1804:** *Warbeck*. Projected tragedy about the English pretender Perkin Warbeck; incomplete and multiply planned. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1802:** *Rosamund oder Die Braut der Hölle*. Dramatic project surviving chiefly as plans. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1804:** *Die Gräfin von Flandern*. Historical-drama project; fragmentary. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1804:** *Themistokles*. Projected tragedy on the Athenian statesman, never completed. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1804:** *Das Schiff*. Dramatic project known from a plan or fragment, not a finished stage text. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1804:** *Die Flibustier*. Projected drama about Caribbean freebooters; its unstable surviving title and notes belong to a project, not a completed work. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1804:** *Seestück*. Fragmentary maritime dramatic project. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1804:** *Elfriede*. Unfinished dramatic project. [NA 12]
- **c. 1801–1804:** *Die Prinzessin von Celle*. Historical-tragedy project concerning Sophia Dorothea of Celle; unfinished. [NA 12]
- **c. 1799–1804:** *Entwurf eines Lustspiels im Geschmack von Goethes Bürgergeneral*. Sketch for a comedy modeled on Goethe’s *Der Bürgergeneral*; the intention survives, but no play was completed. [NA 12]

### Stage adaptations, translations, and theatrical remakings

- **1788:** *Egmont*. Stage adaptation of Goethe’s drama; Schiller cut and rearranged the work for performance, so it is neither an original drama nor merely a reprint. [NA 13]
- **1800:** *Macbeth*. German stage adaptation of Shakespeare, made through existing translations but extensively altered for the Weimar theatre. [NA 13]
- **1801:** *Nathan der Weise*. Stage adaptation of Lessing’s drama. [NA 13]
- **1801:** *Turandot, Prinzessin von China*. Free theatrical adaptation of Carlo Gozzi’s fiaba, mediated by an earlier German translation. [NA 14]
- **1801:** *Die Rätsel der Turandot*. Riddle texts made for *Turandot*; dependent theatrical components rather than a separate conventional play. [NA 14]
- **1802:** *Iphigenie auf Tauris*. Projected or partial stage adaptation of Goethe’s drama; its surviving state is incomplete. [NA 14]
- **1803:** *Stella*. Stage adaptation of Goethe’s drama, principally a theatrical intervention rather than a new independent play. [NA 14]
- **1804:** *Othello*. Stage adaptation of Shakespeare based substantially on Johann Heinrich Voß’s translation but reorganized and altered by Schiller. [NA 14]
- **c. 1779–1780:** *Iphigenie in Aulis*. Translation from Euripides; a classical-language exercise that became a German dramatic text. [NA 15.1]
- **c. 1780:** *Die Phönizierinnen*. Translation from Euripides, surviving incompletely. [NA 15.1]
- **c. 1780:** *Der rasende Herkules*. Translation from Seneca’s *Hercules furens*, associated with Schiller’s academy studies. [NA 15.1]
- **c. 1780:** *Die Troerinnen*. Translation from Seneca; fragmentary or incomplete. [NA 15.1]
- **1781:** *Dido*. Translation or adaptation from Virgil’s *Aeneid*, Book IV, in verse. [NA 15.1]
- **1785:** *Merkwürdiges Beispiel einer weiblichen Rache*. Translation and autonomous extraction of an episode from Diderot’s *Jacques le fataliste*; it functions as a German novella even though its substance is translated. [NA 15.2]
- **1785:** *Der Parasit oder Die Kunst, sein Glück zu machen*. Translation and adaptation of Louis-Benoît Picard’s comedy *Médiocre et rampant*. [NA 15.2]
- **1803:** *Der Neffe als Onkel*. Adaptation of Picard’s comedy *Encore des Ménechmes*. [NA 15.2]
- **1803:** *Phädra*. Verse translation of Racine’s tragedy *Phèdre*, made for the Weimar stage. [NA 15.2]

### Narrative prose

- **1786:** *Verbrecher aus Infamie, eine wahre Geschichte*. First form of the psychological crime narrative later revised as *Der Verbrecher aus verlorener Ehre*. [NA 16]
- **1792:** *Der Verbrecher aus verlorener Ehre*. Revised manifestation of *Verbrecher aus Infamie*; the altered title and narration make it more than a simple reprint. [NA 16]
- **1786:** *Eine großmütige Handlung, aus der neuesten Geschichte*. Short exemplary historical narrative. [NA 16]
- **1787–1789:** *Der Geisterseher. Aus den Papieren des Grafen von O\*\*\**. Unfinished serial novel combining memoir, political conspiracy, religious imposture, and supernatural suspense; Schiller abandoned the project. [NA 16]
- **1788:** *Spiel des Schicksals*. Historical-anecdotal narrative. [NA 16]
- **1789:** *Herzog von Alba bei einem Frühstück auf dem Schlosse zu Rudolstadt, im Jahr 1547*. Historical dialogue or anecdotal scene, poised between historiography and fiction. [NA 16]
- **1795:** *Eine merkwürdige Belagerung von Antwerpen in den Jahren 1584 und 1585*. Historical narrative made from material related to Schiller’s Netherlands history. [NA 16]
- **c. 1780:** *Avanturen des neuen Telemachs*. Juvenile satirical manuscript containing prose and Schiller’s drawings; a mixed-media private object rather than a conventionally published book. Its attribution and exact dating have required editorial reconstruction. [NA 43]

### Historical works

- **1788:** *Geschichte des Abfalls der vereinigten Niederlande von der spanischen Regierung*. Historical monograph planned on a larger scale but completed and published only through its first book; incompleteness belongs to the project’s history. [NA 17]
- **1788:** *Einleitung zu der Geschichte des Abfalls der vereinigten Niederlande*. Programmatic historical introduction, both part of and separable from the larger Netherlands project. [NA 17]
- **1788:** *Von der niederländischen Gesetzgebung*. Historical excursus or component study. [NA 17]
- **1788:** *Die Regierung der Margaretha von Parma*. Historical section belonging to the Netherlands history. [NA 17]
- **1788:** *Der Bildersturm*. Historical section on the iconoclastic revolt. [NA 17]
- **1788:** *Der Geusenbund*. Historical section on the noble confederacy. [NA 17]
- **1788:** *Graf Egmont und Graf Horn*. Historical biographical section. [NA 17]
- **1789:** *Was heißt und zu welchem Ende studiert man Universalgeschichte?* Inaugural lecture and manifesto for universal history as an intellectual practice. [NA 17]
- **1789:** *Übersicht des Zustands von Europa zur Zeit des ersten Kreuzzugs*. Universal-historical study. [NA 17]
- **1790–1792:** *Geschichte des Dreißigjährigen Kriegs*. Multi-part historical narrative first produced for a larger publishing enterprise and subsequently issued as a book. [NA 18]
- **1792:** *Über Völkerwanderung, Kreuzzüge und Mittelalter*. Historical essay assembled around large-scale movements in European history. [NA 18]
- **1792:** *Die Gesetzgebung des Lykurgus und Solon*. Comparative historical-political essay derived from Schiller’s lectures. [NA 18]
- **1792:** *Die Sendung Moses*. Speculative history of religion and political culture, drawing heavily on contemporary Masonic and Enlightenment sources. [NA 18]
- **1792:** *Etwas über die erste Menschengesellschaft nach dem Leitfaden der mosaischen Urkunde*. Conjectural historical-anthropological essay. [NA 18]
- **1789–1793:** *Historische Kalenderbeiträge*. A corpus of short historical narratives, portraits, anecdotes, and chronological articles made for popular almanacs; editorial boundaries between independent obra and commissioned contribution are not always firm. [NA 19.1]
- **1790s:** *Historische Vorlesungen und Entwürfe*. Lecture manuscripts, plans, excerpts, and unfinished historical studies; these are projects and working texts rather than a single completed history. [NA 19.1]

### Medical and academic writings

- **1779:** *Philosophie der Physiologie*. Rejected medical dissertation or dissertation project; the surviving text joins speculative philosophy to physiology and was not accepted for the degree. [NA 20; NA 43]
- **1780:** *Versuch über den Zusammenhang der tierischen Natur des Menschen mit seiner geistigen*. Accepted medical dissertation, treating bodily and mental life as an interacting whole. [NA 20]
- **1780:** *De discrimine febrium inflammatoriarum et putridarum*. Latin medical dissertation on distinguishing inflammatory from putrid fevers; long omitted or misidentified in parts of the transmission and restored to the corpus by later scholarship. [NA 22; NA 43]
- **1780:** *Beobachtungen bei der Leichenöffnung des Eleven Hiller*. Anatomical report; an institutional medical document rather than literary prose. [NA 22]
- **1780:** *Über die Krankheit des Eleven Grammont*. Clinical case report. [NA 22]
- **1779:** *Bericht an Herzog Karl Eugen über die Mitschüler und sich selbst*. Academy report composed under institutional compulsion; authorship is Schiller’s, but its status as a freely conceived “work” is doubtful. [NA 22]

### Philosophical, aesthetic, and poetic-theoretical writings

- **1784:** *Was kann eine gute stehende Schaubühne eigentlich wirken?* Lecture later known as *Die Schaubühne als eine moralische Anstalt betrachtet*; an argument for theatre as a civic and moral institution. [NA 20]
- **1788:** *Über den Grund des Vergnügens an tragischen Gegenständen*. Aesthetic essay on tragic pleasure. [NA 20]
- **1792:** *Über die tragische Kunst*. Treatise on the means and ends of tragedy. [NA 20]
- **1792:** *Vom Erhabenen*. Essay on sublimity; its relation to the later *Über das Erhabene* is that of a distinct treatment, not merely an alternative title. [NA 20]
- **1793:** *Über Anmut und Würde*. Philosophical-aesthetic treatise relating bodily appearance, freedom, moral action, grace, and dignity. [NA 20]
- **1793:** *Kallias oder Über die Schönheit*. Philosophical project transmitted chiefly through letters to Körner; it resists being called a finished treatise because Schiller never completed the intended work. [NA 20]
- **1793–1794:** *Augustenburger Briefe*. Early epistolary formulation of Schiller’s aesthetic-education project; partly lost after a fire and subsequently remade into the *Ästhetische Briefe*. [NA 20]
- **1794:** *Über das Pathetische*. Aesthetic essay on suffering, representation, and moral freedom. [NA 20]
- **1795:** *Briefe über die ästhetische Erziehung des Menschen*. Twenty-seven-letter philosophical work; epistolary in form but functioning as a systematic aesthetic and political manifesto. [NA 20]
- **1795:** *Über die notwendigen Grenzen beim Gebrauch schöner Formen*. Aesthetic essay on form, truth, and philosophical exposition. [NA 21]
- **1795:** *Über die Gefahr ästhetischer Sitten*. Short philosophical intervention on the limits of aesthetic cultivation. [NA 21]
- **1795–1796:** *Über naive und sentimentalische Dichtung*. Multi-part essay and poetic typology, first appearing serially before being revised as a larger treatise. [NA 20]
- **1797:** *Über das Erhabene*. Later essay on sublimity and freedom; publication was posthumous, and its precise date of composition has been debated. [NA 21]
- **1797:** *Gedanken über den Gebrauch des Gemeinen und Niedrigen in der Kunst*. Aesthetic essay distinguishing legitimate representation from degrading effect. [NA 21]
- **1798:** *Über das Erhabene in der Kunst*. Title sometimes imposed in editorial traditions on material related to Schiller’s sublime essays; the textual boundaries and nomenclature require caution. [NA 21]
- **1795:** *Über die ästhetische Erziehung des Menschen in einer Reihe von Briefen: Vorstufen und Paralipomena*. Drafts and discarded passages belonging to the project rather than an independent finished treatise. [NA 20–21]
- **1795–1800:** *Philosophische Briefe und Entwürfe*. A corpus of plans, notes, tabular schemes, and unfinished arguments; it is evidence of continuing projects, not one authorially constituted book. [NA 20–21]

### Editorial and periodical projects

- **1782:** *Anthologie auf das Jahr 1782*. Author-edited poetry anthology, published anonymously and containing Schiller’s own poems alongside attributed and pseudonymous matter; it is an editorial obra as well as a container of individual texts. [NA 1; NA 22]
- **1782:** *Wirtembergisches Repertorium der Literatur*. Short-lived critical periodical co-edited by Schiller; individual unsigned notices have posed attribution problems. [NA 22]
- **1785–1786:** *Rheinische Thalia*. Periodical conceived and edited by Schiller, combining his own drama, fiction, essays, and translations with contributions by others. [NA 22]
- **1786–1791:** *Thalia*. Continuation and transformation of the *Rheinische Thalia* into a literary journal; it is an editorial project, not wholly an authored text. [NA 22]
- **1792–1793:** *Neue Thalia*. Renewed literary periodical edited by Schiller. [NA 22]
- **1795–1797:** *Die Horen*. Monthly journal founded and edited by Schiller with Goethe and a wide network of contributors; its collective authorship makes the periodical itself a collaborative manifestation rather than solely Schiller’s text. [NA 22]
- **1796–1800:** *Musen-Almanach*. Annual poetry publication edited by Schiller; it became the vehicle for ballads, epigrams, the *Xenien*, and work by many other writers. [NA 1–2; NA 22]
- **1800–1805:** *Prachtausgabe der Gedichte*. Planned authorial collected edition of Schiller’s poetry; unfinished at his death, it survives as an ordering and revision project rather than a completed publication. [NA 2.1]

### Correspondence and documentary writing

- **1772–1805:** *Briefe Schillers*. Corpus of Schiller’s surviving letters, drafts, enclosures, notes, petitions, and business communications; the Nationalausgabe prints them chronologically across ten volumes. This is a body of made texts, not one book, and lost letters are known from replies or references. [NA 23–32; NA 43]
- **1794–1805:** *Briefwechsel zwischen Schiller und Goethe*. Collaborative correspondence later constituted editorially as a major work; individual letters are Schiller’s or Goethe’s, while the “exchange” as a closed book is posthumous. [NA 27–32]
- **1784–1805:** *Briefwechsel mit Christian Gottfried Körner*. Intellectual and personal correspondence containing drafts and philosophical projects, notably *Kallias*; its unity as a work is retrospective. [NA 23–32]
- **1793–1794:** *Briefe an den Augustenburger Kreis*. Correspondence from which the aesthetic-education project emerged; some originals were destroyed, making the surviving corpus incomplete. [NA 26–27]
- **1772–1805:** *Petitionen, contracts, receipts, memoranda, and official notes*. Documentary corpus included or registered by the Nationalausgabe; these are authored acts and records, but many resist the honorific category “work.” [NA 23–32; NA 43]
