## Works by Messer Simone

### No securely attributable obra

- **No known date:** *No surviving or documented work*. Messer Simone di Geri de’ Bardi is known from civic, military, property, and notarial records, not as the maker of a text, artwork, building, or other identifiable obra. The archival documents concerning him—including records of offices held, guarantees, property transactions, and military activity—are evidence about his life, not works authored by him; accordingly, no scholarly works catalogue or collected-works bibliography exists for him.

### Documentary traces, not works

- **1288–1315; possibly 1329:** *Civic and notarial records concerning Simone de’ Bardi*. These records attest appointments, political and military activity, and transactions, but they were produced by municipal or notarial institutions and therefore resist classification as Simone’s works. The possible 1329 appointment of a *dominus Symon de Bardis* remains uncertain because that name may designate another member of the Bardi family.

### Sources

- Antonietta D’Addario, “[Bardi, Simone](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/simone-bardi_%28Enciclopedia-Dantesca%29/),” *Enciclopedia Dantesca*, Istituto dell’Enciclopedia Italiana, 1970. This biographical authority inventories the documentary evidence for Simone’s life but attributes no literary, artistic, architectural, or other work to him.
- Isidoro Del Lungo, [*La donna fiorentina del buon tempo antico*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/57940/57940-h/57940-h.htm), Florence, 1906. Del Lungo distinguishes Messer Simone di Geri from other contemporary Bardi named Simone and discusses the archival evidence identifying him as Beatrice Portinari’s husband; it supplies no catalogue of works by him.
