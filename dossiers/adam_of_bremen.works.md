## Works by Adam of Bremen

### Documentary text of disputed authorship

- **11 June 1069:** Charter of Archbishop Adalbert granting tithes to the canoness Gisela in exchange for donated estates. This administrative instrument names “Adam, master of the schools” as the person who wrote and subscribed it, but both his identification with the historian and the nature of his contribution remain disputed: *scripsi* may mean that he drafted the text rather than physically engrossed it, and the surviving document is probably not in his hand. ([Hamburgisches Urkundenbuch, no. 101](https://books.google.com/books?id=GhdKAAAAcAAJ&pg=PA96); [Scior](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050048314_A21684082/preview-9783050048314_A21684082.pdf); [Riemer](https://hghl.org/files/hghl.de/Homepage/pdf/Riemer.Erben%20der%20Billunger.JbMvM98%282020%29S.113-148.pdf))

### Historiographical obra

- **c. 1072–1076, revised to c. 1081 or possibly 1085:** *Gesta Hammaburgensis ecclesiae pontificum* (“Deeds of the Bishops of the Hamburg Church”). Adam’s only securely identified obra is a composite Latin history in four books, framed by a dedicatory prologue and a verse epilogue to Archbishop Liemar: Books I–III combine institutional history with a biography-like account of Archbishop Adalbert, while Book IV, *Descriptio insularum aquilonis*, is a geographical and ethnographic survey of northern Europe. The author’s manuscript and earliest redactions are lost; consequently the exact date, completeness, wording, and boundary between Adam’s work and subsequent intervention cannot be recovered with certainty. ([Schmeidler’s critical edition](https://www.dmgh.de/mgh_ss_rer_germ_2/index.htm); [Medieval Nordic Literature in Latin](https://medieval.wiki.uib.no/index.php?title=Adam_Bremensis); [Bartusik, Biskup, and Morawiec](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781000610383_A43003981/preview-9781000610383_A43003981.pdf))

### Revision project and contested accretions

- **c. 1075/1076–c. 1081, possibly continuing until 1085:** *Scholia to the Gesta Hammaburgensis ecclesiae pontificum*. These marginal additions, corrections, explanations, and short excursuses constitute an ongoing revision project rather than an independent work: many probably originated with Adam, but others were added, altered, incorporated into the main text, or removed by later scribes. Schmeidler attributed roughly 113 scholia to Adam and reconstructed successive authorial states, but that reconstruction—including the supposed dedication copy and the attribution of individual scholia—has been challenged; the collective scholia therefore resist classification as wholly Adam’s obra. ([Medieval Nordic Literature in Latin](https://medieval.wiki.uib.no/index.php?title=Adam_Bremensis); [Damm, “Ad insulas Baltici”](https://bastion.eurekamag.com/research/097/000/097000901.php); [Bartusik, Biskup, and Morawiec](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781000610383_A43003981/preview-9781000610383_A43003981.pdf))
