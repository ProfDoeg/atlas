## Works by Henryk Erlich

### Political essays and pamphlets

- **1934:** *In kamf farn revolutsyonern sotsyalizm* (*In the Struggle for Revolutionary Socialism*). A 104-page Yiddish political treatise defining Erlich’s democratic, anti-Bolshevik socialism; an English translation by Haim Kantorovitch and Anna Bercowitz appeared the same year as *The Struggle for Revolutionary Socialism* and is an edition of the same obra, not another work. The standard *Leksikon fun der nayer yidisher literatur* lists it among Erlich’s three works in book form. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
- **1935:** *Der iker fun bundizm* (*The Essence of Bundism*). A sixteen-page programmatic pamphlet explaining Bundism; its brevity and political purpose place it between an essay and a statement of doctrine. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
- **1935:** *Der “Forverts” un der “Bund”* (*The Forverts and the Bund*). A forty-eight-page polemical pamphlet concerning the relationship or dispute between the New York Yiddish newspaper *Forverts* and the Bund. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)

### Manifesto

- **1937:** *Manifest fun “Bund,” tsu di yidishe arbetndike masn in Poyln* (*Manifesto of the Bund to the Jewish Working Masses in Poland*). A twenty-one-page manifesto reportedly circulated in more than 100,000 copies: a text intended to mobilize its readers, not merely describe a position. The literary lexicon places it with Erlich’s publications but says that “the Bund” published it and does not establish sole authorship; it should therefore be treated as a collective party text associated with Erlich, not securely as his individual obra. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)

### Journalism and editorial projects

- **1905–1906:** *Nasze Hasło* (*Our Watchword*). An illegal Polish-language Bundist periodical edited collectively by Erlich, Bronisław Grosser, and others. It is an editorial-political project rather than a singly authored work, and the standard biographical account does not enumerate Erlich’s individual unsigned contributions. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
- **1913–1914:** *Di Tsayt* (*The Times*). The Bundist organ whose founding editorial board included Erlich, Dovid Zaslovski, Beynish Mikhalevitsh, and later Yitskhok Rafes. Erlich helped make the newspaper as a continuing collective manifestation; calling the whole periodical his “work” would overstate individual authorship. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
- **1912–1917:** Contributions to *Den’* (*The Day*). A body of Russian-language journalism rather than one fixed obra; the consulted lexicon confirms Erlich as a contributor but supplies neither a complete article bibliography nor secure titles and dates for the individual pieces. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
- **1914–1917:** *Evreiskie vesti* (*Jewish News*). A Russian-language monthly published by Erlich and political comrades while *Di Tsayt* was suppressed during the First World War. This was a collective publishing project, and the source does not isolate Erlich’s individual articles. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
- **1917–1918:** *Rabochaia gazeta* (*Workers’ Gazette*). The central Menshevik newspaper on whose editorial board Erlich served from November 1917. His role belongs to the making of a serial political forum, but the newspaper as a whole resists attribution to him as an individual work. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
- **circa 1919–1920:** *Głos Bundu* (*Voice of the Bund*). A weekly that Erlich published and edited for a time; confiscation of several issues led to two convictions later extinguished by amnesty. It is best classified as an editorial project, not as a book or a wholly individual composition. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
- **1921–1939:** Articles and editorials in *Folkstsaytung / Unzer Folkstsaytung* (*People’s Newspaper / Our People’s Newspaper*). Erlich contributed from 1921, later became editor, and wrote political journalism almost daily; this large, dispersed body of work shaped the Polish Bund’s public position but has no complete item-level bibliography in the standard lexicon. A posthumous anthology preserves only selected writings, so treating its selection as “everything” would be misleading. [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik); [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article/513)

### Prison document

- **1939–1941:** History and account of the Polish Bund written under NKVD interrogation. This long, comprehensive institutional history was compelled from Erlich in Soviet prison and supplied detailed information about the Bund’s organizations. It has no securely established authorial title in the consulted scholarship and resists being called a voluntary “work”: it is simultaneously testimony, coerced deposition, historical narrative, and secret-police evidence. [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article/513); [Gertrud Pickhan, “That Incredible History of the Polish Bund Written in a Soviet Prison”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/jews-in-early-modern-poland/that-incredible-history-of-the-polish-bund-written-in-a-soviet-prison-the-nkvd-files-on-henryk-erlich-and-wiktor-alter/23FF39AEFF505A6EAE20FA97C3FE035E)

### Anti-fascist committee project and protocol

- **September–October 1941:** Project for an international Jewish anti-Hitler committee. Devised jointly with Wiktor Alter after their release from prison, this was an organizational project intended to unite Jewish socialist and workers’ bodies internationally against Nazism. Soviet authorities instead established a more tightly controlled Jewish Anti-Fascist Committee; Erlich and Alter’s version therefore survives chiefly as an unrealized project, not a completed institution. [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article/513); [Jewish Telegraphic Agency](https://www.jta.org/archive/soviet-government-asked-alter-and-erlich-to-lead-jewish-anti-nazi-campaign)
- **October 1941:** Letter and organizational plan addressed to Joseph Stalin concerning a Jewish anti-Hitler committee. Co-authored with Wiktor Alter, this is both a petition and a protocol: it proposes the committee’s structure, leadership, and field of action. Surviving documentation attributes the plan jointly to the two men, so individual authorship cannot be separated. [Electronic Library of Historical Documents](https://www.docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/297368-iz-pisma-g-erliha-i-v-altera-i-v-stalinu-o-sozdanii-evreyskogo-antigitlerovskogo-komiteta-oktyabr-1941-g); [*The Case of Henryk Erlich and Victor Alter*](https://www.bibliotecaginobianco.it/fliplibri/ERLALT/)
- **October 1941:** Letter to Lavrentii Beria accompanying the anti-Hitler committee proposal. Jointly signed by Erlich and Alter, this short administrative text reports completion of the plan and asks Beria to accelerate official approval. It is not an independent literary work but an operative document belonging to the larger committee project. [*The Case of Henryk Erlich and Victor Alter*](https://www.bibliotecaginobianco.it/fliplibri/ERLALT/)

### Posthumous collected manifestation

- **1951:** *Fun Henrik Erlikhs pen* (*From Henryk Erlich’s Pen*). A posthumously selected body of Erlich’s writings forming one section of *Henrik Erlikh un Viktor Alter: zeyer lebn, kamf un martirer-toyt*. It is a later editorial manifestation of earlier texts—not a new work made by Erlich—and makes no claim to completeness. [Yiddish Book Center](https://www.yiddishbookcenter.org/collections/yiddish-books/spb-nybc200521/erlich-henryk-alter-victor-henrik-erlikh-un-viktor-alter); [Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2252/Erlikh-Henrik)
