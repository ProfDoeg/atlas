## Works by Vaslav Nijinsky

### Choreographic obras

- **1912:** *L’Après-midi d’un faune*. Ballet choreography to music by Claude Debussy, premiered at the Théâtre du Châtelet on 29 May 1912; its radically planar movement makes it a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk with Léon Bakst’s décor and costumes, rather than an autonomous “text.” Nijinsky’s choreography survives unusually well through photographs, production evidence, and his later notated score. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/ballets-russes/exhibition-items.html#obj28); [Guest and Jeschke](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA22612503.amp))
- **1913:** *Jeux*. Ballet choreography to Debussy’s score, premiered on 15 May 1913; it is an embodied work whose original movement was not comprehensively notated and is therefore only incompletely recoverable. Modern stagings advertised as reconstructions necessarily combine evidence with conjecture. ([Korean Citation Index study of Nijinsky’s four ballets](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002655359); [Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/nijinsky-vaslav-1889-1950))
- **1913:** *Le Sacre du printemps*. Ballet choreography to Igor Stravinsky’s music, with scenario and designs by Nicholas Roerich, premiered on 29 May 1913. Nijinsky’s choreographic manuscript does not survive; Marie Rambert’s annotations and other fragmentary evidence support reconstructions, but scholars stress that the precise original movements cannot now be recovered, so reconstructed versions are not simply the original obra returned intact. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/bronislava-nijinska/articles-and-essays/le-sacre-du-printemps/); [mdwPress](https://www.mdw.ac.at/mdwpress/mdwp004-005/))
- **1913, unfinished:** *Sarabande*. An unrealized choreographic project begun for music by Johann Sebastian Bach and left as fragmentary notational “seeds”; because it never reached performance or a complete score, it is a project rather than a finished ballet. Its proposed reconstruction depends upon interpretation of sparse evidence. ([arts•meme report on the Hodson–Fullington–Turocy research workshop](https://artsmeme.com/2017/07/05/seeking-nijinskys-unrealized-sarabande-ballet-turocy/))
- **1916:** *Till Eulenspiegel*. Comic-dramatic ballet choreography to Richard Strauss’s tone poem, first staged at New York’s Manhattan Opera House in 1916, with scenery and costumes by Robert Edmond Jones. The original choreography was subsequently lost; later productions, including Patrice Bart’s 1994 staging, are reconstructions or reimaginings and should not be mistaken for uninterrupted transmission of Nijinsky’s choreography. ([Bibliothèque nationale de France](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/rechercher.do?index=TIT3&numNotice=13334945); [Korean Citation Index study](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002655359))

### Choreographic protocol and scores

- **1910s:** *Nijinsky’s system of dance notation*. A protocol for inscribing bodily movement, developed from but substantially altering the Stepanov system Nijinsky had learned in St Petersburg. It is both an unfinished general project and a working technology embodied in surviving experiments and scores, rather than a single securely bounded text. ([Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/blog/adventures-choreographic-notation-and-tale-two-ballet-rebels-or-nijinskynijinska); [British Library, Add MS 47215](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-002103433))
- **1913:** *Choreographic score for L’Après-midi d’un faune* (preliminary score). An experimental manuscript witness to Nijinsky’s notation project, distinct from both the performed 1912 ballet and the completed 1915 score; the Morgan Library reports this score among the Nijinsky materials in the Bronislava Nijinska Collection. ([Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/blog/adventures-choreographic-notation-and-tale-two-ballet-rebels-or-nijinskynijinska))
- **August–September 1915:** *Choreographic score of L’Après-midi d’un faune*. A 28-folio autograph score using groups of three staves, with Russian notes about action, dancers, scenery, costumes, and properties. The Library of Congress describes related images under the date 1912, while the completed manuscript now in the British Library is explicitly dated 1915; these records should not be silently collapsed into one date. ([British Library, Add MS 47215](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-002103433); [Library of Congress item record](https://www.loc.gov/item/2023861927/))

### Unfixed performances and manifestations

- **19 January 1919:** *“Wedding with God” / final Suvretta House performance*. An improvised solo manifestation in St Moritz involving prolonged stillness, spoken declarations, lengths of black and white cloth arranged as a cross, and an enacted vision of war. It resists identification as a fixed work: the familiar title derives from Nijinsky’s description of the occasion, and the event survives through witness accounts rather than a score or stable choreography. ([El País account drawing on Lucy Moore’s biography](https://elpais.com/cultura/2026-04-25/reencuentro-con-nijinsky-en-el-liceo.html); [MoMA artist chronology](https://www.moma.org/artists/4305-vaslav-nijinsky))

### Drawings and visual obra

- **ca. 1916:** *Untitled*. Ink on colored paper; an abstract drawing rather than a theatrical design, now in the Museum of Modern Art. Its title and approximate date are cataloguing descriptions, not necessarily names or dates assigned by Nijinsky. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/36007))
- **ca. 1917–1918:** *Untitled (Figures)*. Drawing now in the Museum of Modern Art; the parenthetical title is descriptive and the dating approximate. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/audio/playlist/286/3708))
- **ca. 1916–1919:** *Untitled abstract drawings*. A dispersed body of ink, pencil, and crayon works dominated by eyes, faces, arcs, intersecting curves, and repeated geometric or figural forms. Many sheets have no authorial title or secure individual date, and no complete catalogue raisonné was located; it is therefore more accurate to name the corpus as an obra than to manufacture a list of supposedly discrete titled works. ([MoMA artist catalogue](https://www.moma.org/artists/4305-vaslav-nijinsky); [MoMA exhibition labels](https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/6_Kirstein_ExtendedLabels.pdf))

### Diary, letters, and poems

- **19 January–4 March 1919:** *Diary*. A sustained manuscript composition written in three school notebooks during approximately six weeks in St Moritz. *The Diary of Vaslav Nijinsky* is an editorial title: Romola Nijinsky’s first published version rearranged, abridged, and normalized the manuscript, whereas Joan Acocella’s unexpurgated edition, translated by Kyril FitzLyon, sought to restore its order and extent. ([Joan Acocella, “Secrets of Nijinsky”](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1999/01/14/secrets-of-nijinsky/); [University of Illinois Press/BiblioVault](https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9780252073625))
- **1919:** *Fourth notebook: letters and poems*. A companion manuscript containing sixteen addressed or projected letters as well as poems, rather than simply a fourth continuous section of the diary. Romola Nijinsky selected six letters, heavily edited them, inserted them into her diary edition, and omitted the rest; the notebook was first included as a whole in the 1999 unexpurgated edition, so the familiar published “Diary” is itself an editorially constructed obra. ([Joan Acocella, “Secrets of Nijinsky”](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1999/01/14/secrets-of-nijinsky/); [University of Siena study](https://usiena-air.unisi.it/handle/11365/7938))
