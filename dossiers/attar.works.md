## Works by Farid al-Din Attar

### Narrative and didactic verse obras

- *Asrār-nāma* (“Book of Secrets”). A Persian mystical maṯnawī arranged as twenty-two discourses illustrated by anecdotes; it lacks the continuous frame narrative found in several of Attar’s other long poems. It belongs to the generally accepted corpus, although its date is unknown. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- **1177, possibly:** *Manṭeq al-ṭayr* (“The Speech/Conference of the Birds”; also *Maqāmāt-e ṭoyūr*). A framed mystical journey-poem in maṯnawī verse, in which the birds seek the Simorgh through seven valleys. A verse dates its completion to 573 AH/1177, but that verse is absent from some manuscripts, so the date is not conclusive. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Moṣībat-nāma* (“Book of Affliction”). A mystical journey-poem in maṯnawī verse: a spiritual wayfarer questions forty orders of celestial, earthly, and psychological being before being directed inward. Its composition date is not securely known. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Elāhī-nāma* (“Divine Book”). A didactic mystical maṯnawī whose frame presents a king answering the desires of his six sons through embedded stories. Šafiʿi Kadkani argues that this poem was originally called *Ḵosrow-nāma*, but concrete manuscript evidence for that earlier title has not been found. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/); [O’Malley, “Ḵosrow-nāma”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/%E1%B8%B5osrow-nama/)

### Lyric and quatrain compilations

- *Dīvān*. An authorial body or collection rather than a single continuous text, consisting chiefly of ghazals with a smaller group of qaṣīdas; Attar separated most of his quatrains into the *Moḵtār-nāma*. Individual poems and the collection’s transmitted boundaries remain subject to textual criticism. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Moḵtār-nāma* (“Book of Selection”). A deliberately organized florilegium of rubāʿī quatrains in fifty thematic chapters, made by selecting and rearranging material from Attar’s larger lyric store. The received corpus contains approximately 2,088 quatrains, but another 191 appear in some manuscripts, and the authenticity of every individual quatrain is unresolved. [Meneghini Correale, “Moḵtār-nāma”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/moktar-nama/)

### Prose hagiographic obra

- *Taḏkerat al-awlīāʾ* (“Memorial of the Saints”). A Persian prose collection of biographies, sayings, and exemplary narratives concerning early Sufis. The original recension ending with Ḥallāj is accepted as Attar’s; biographies of later saints appended in the expanded transmission are not his, so the familiar long text resists treatment as a wholly authorial work. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/); [Lewis, “Farid al-Din ʿAttar”](https://www.iis.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2006/11/attar_logo-610964304.pdf)

### Destroyed or lost self-attested projects

- *Jawāher-nāma* (“Book of Jewels”). A completed or substantially realized text that Attar says he destroyed with his own hand; no recoverable text survives, and it must not be confused with *Jawhar al-ḏāt*, a later work attributed to another “Attar.” Its form and contents cannot now be securely classified. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Šarḥ al-qalb* (“Explication of the Heart”). Another work Attar reports destroying himself. Only the title and self-attestation remain, making it simultaneously a lost obra and evidence of an abandoned or repudiated project. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Kašf al-asrār* (“Unveiling of Secrets”). A work named in the introduction to *Taḏkerat al-awlīāʾ* but otherwise lost or unidentified. Nothing secure is known about its medium, form, completion, or relation to *Asrār-nāma*, so even its status as a finished text remains resistant to classification. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Maʿrefat al-nafs* (“Knowledge of the Self”). A second otherwise unknown work named in the introduction to *Taḏkerat al-awlīāʾ*. Its title survives, but its contents, form, and completeness do not. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)

### Contested obra

- *Ḵosrow-nāma* (also *Gol o Hormoz*, *Gol o Ḵosrow*, or *Ḵosrow o Gol*). A courtly verse romance rather than a Sufi didactic poem, transmitted in an early manuscript dated 1297 and traditionally accepted by Ritter, Foruzānfar, and Nafisi as Attar’s work. Šafiʿi Kadkani and other scholars reject the attribution as a forgery or the work of another poet named Attar, while Austin O’Malley argues that the manuscript history and style leave the traditional attribution viable; it therefore cannot be silently admitted to or excluded from Attar’s oeuvre. [O’Malley, “Ḵosrow-nāma”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/%E1%B8%B5osrow-nama/); [O’Malley, “An Unexpected Romance”](https://www.middleeastmedievalists.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/UW-27.pdf)
- *Meʿrāj-nāma*. A very short maṯnawī on the Prophet’s ascension, accepted by Ritter but possibly only a detached section from a larger poem rather than an independent obra. Its attribution and textual independence are not secure enough for inclusion in the core corpus. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Jomjoma-nāma*. A short maṯnawī about Jesus restoring a dead king’s skull to speech; Ritter accepted it, but its fragment-like form suggests that it may be an extracted illustrative story rather than a separately made work. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Bolbol-nāma* (“Book of the Nightingale”). A short allegorical maṯnawī in which the nightingale answers before Solomon for its love songs to the rose. Ritter included it among Attar’s possible works, but Nafisi rejected it on stylistic grounds. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Pand-nāma* (“Book of Counsel”). A short versified moral protocol or conduct manual long circulated under Attar’s name. Nafisi defended the attribution, but the text does not name Attar, has no known witness before the fifteenth century, and differs sharply from his characteristic language and ideas; most specialists therefore exclude it. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)

### Apocrypha recorded by the scholarly bibliography

- *Oštor-nāma* (“Book of the Camel”). A maṯnawī transmitted under Attar’s name but excluded from his oeuvre on stylistic grounds; the actual author remains unidentified. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Jawhar al-ḏāt* or *Jawāher al-ḏāt* (“Essence/Jewels of the Essence”). A substantial poem by an unidentified later poet using the name Attar, not the destroyed *Jawāher-nāma* attested by Farid al-Din Attar himself. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Ḥallāj-nāma*. A poem assigned by older bibliography to Attar but rejected because its style belongs to a different authorial group. It is not safely describable as something Farid al-Din Attar made. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Manṣūr-nāma*. Another poem from the later pseudo-Attar corpus, stylistically incompatible with the secure works. Its historical existence is clear, but its creator is not Farid al-Din Attar on the prevailing scholarly judgment. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Bīsar-nāma*. A work traditionally attributed to Attar but rejected on stylistic grounds and left without an identified author. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Maẓhar al-ʿajāʾeb* (“Manifestation of Wonders”). A poem from a Shiʿi-oriented pseudo-Attar group; specialist bibliography excludes it from Farid al-Din Attar’s oeuvre. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Lesān al-ḡayb* (“Tongue of the Unseen”). A work attributed to Attar in the later transmission but assigned by textual scholarship to the apocryphal corpus. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Ḵayyāṭ-nāma* (“Book of the Tailor”). A title catalogued among works once attributed to Attar but judged indisputably apocryphal by Ritter. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Waṣlat-nāma* (“Book of Union”). A transmitted attribution rather than an accepted Attarian obra; Ritter classified it as indisputably apocryphal. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Kanz al-asrār* (“Treasure of Secrets”). A work circulated under Attar’s name but classified as indisputably apocryphal; it must not be conflated with the authentic *Asrār-nāma*. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Meftāḥ al-fotūḥ* (“Key to the Openings”). A falsely attributed work placed by Ritter outside Attar’s oeuvre. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Waṣīyat-nāma* (“Testament”). A purported testament or counsel text attributed to Attar but judged indisputably apocryphal in the standard critical bibliography. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
- *Kanz al-ḥaqāʾeq* (“Treasure of Truths”). A title preserved within the attributed corpus but rejected by Ritter as apocryphal; its presence in that corpus records reception, not something demonstrably made by Farid al-Din Attar. [Reinert, “ʿAṭṭār, Farīd-al-Dīn”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/attar-farid-al-din-poet/)
